Fully nonlinear logistic equations with sanctuary
Isabeau Birindelli, Giulio Galise, Fabiana Leoni

Abstract
. For the fully nonlinear stationary logistic equation ℱ ⁡ ( x , D 2 ​ u ) + μ ​ u = k ⁡ ( x ) ​ u p \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=k(x)u^{p} with p > 1 p>1 and k ⁡ ( x ) ≥ 0 k(x)\geq 0 , in a bounded domain with Dirichlet boundary condition, we determine, in terms of μ \mu , the existence and uniqueness or the nonexistence of a positive solution. Furthermore, we study the asymptotic behavior of the solutions when μ \mu approaches the boundary points of the existence range.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega
be a bounded domain of
\mathbb{R}^{N}
and
p>1
. Let
k(x)
be a bounded continuous function in
\Omega
such that
0\leq k(x)\leq k_{1}
.
In this paper we study existence, nonexistence and asymptotic for the following problem

(1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=k(x)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
u>0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

where
\mathcal{F}
is a uniformly elliptic operator, positively homogenous of degree 1.

This equation has been extensively studied when
\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u):=\Delta u
, beginning with the famous paper of Kazdan and Warner
[
11
]
who studied it because it is related to “
the
problem of describing the set of scalar curvature functions associated with Riemannian metrics on a given connected, but not necessarily orientable, manifold of dimension
n\geq 3
”.
The scalar curvature is the function
k(x)
which, in that paper, is supposed to be positive.

Here, as far as the function
k\in C(\Omega)
is concerned, we will consider two cases: either it satisfies

(K1)

\exists\,k_{0}>0\ \mbox{such that}\ k(x)\geq k_{0}>0\ \,\forall x\in\Omega

or

(K2)

\begin{cases}\exists\,\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega\ \mbox{such that}\ k(x)=0\ \,\forall x\in\overline{\Omega_{0}}\,,\\
\displaystyle\inf_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{1}}k>0\ \ \forall\,\Omega_{1}\subsetneqq\Omega\ \mbox{such that}\ \Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}.\end{cases}

In the case
\mathcal{F}=\Delta
, the hypothesis (
K2
) corresponds to the equation studied by Ouyang
[
12
,
13
]
and Del Pino and Felmer in
[
7
,
8
]
. See also Du and Huang
[
9
]
for the study of the asymptotic behavior of solutions.

In general, the parabolic problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}u_{t}-\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)=\mu u-k(x)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
u>0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

can be interpreted as the logistic equation in population dynamics. In that model
u(t,x)
represents the density of the population,
\mu u
is its natural growth, while

-k(x)u^{p}
is a logistic term that counters the natural growth of the population (lack of food, enemy, etc…). The set
\Omega_{0}
where
k(x)=0
is like an oasis, where the population
has no enemies and it can follow its natural growth. The second order term
\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)
represents the spatial diffusion of the species. When it is the Laplacian the diffusion is linear and homogenous; here, we focus on a
nonlinear diffusion case. For more details, we refer to e.g.
[
5
]
or
[
1
]
. We consider here the stationary case, a first step in order to study the parabolic problem in a future work.

We shall see that, as in most works concerning the logistic equation, the principal eigenvalue plays a crucial role, acting as the threshold which separates the existence from the
nonexistence regime.

For variational operators the principal eigenvalue is defined through the well known Rayleigh’s quotient. On the other hand, following the approach of Berestycki, Nirenberg, Varadhan
[
2
]
, the notion of principal eigenvalue can be extended to a vast family of fully nonlinear operators, by using its characterization through the validity of the Maximum Principle. More precisely, we will use the definition of principal eigenvalue given by

\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega):=\sup\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{such that}\ \exists\,\psi>0\ \mbox{and}\ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\psi)+\mu\psi\leq 0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\}.

The precise conditions on the class of operators that we consider and on the principal eigenvalue will be given in the next section, but they are the standard ones used in the theory of viscosity solutions. It is important to remark that in the whole paper, when considering solutions, subsolutions or supersolutions, we always mean them in the viscosity sense.

We will always suppose that the operator
\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\cdot)
satisfies hypothesis (
F1
), (
F2
) and (
F3
) given in subsection
2.1
, and that both
\Omega
and
\Omega_{0}
are
C^{2}
bounded domains.

Our main results are contained in the following three theorems.

The first one sharply relates the existence of solutions of (
1
) with the values of
\mu
compared with the principal eigenvalue.

Theorem 1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (
F2
) and (
F3
).

(1)

If
k(x)
satisfies (
K1
), then there exists a unique solution of (
1
) if and only if
\mu>\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If
k(x)
satisfies (
K2
), then there exists a unique solution of (
1
) if and only if
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)<\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
.

The next theorems are concerned with the behavior of the solution for
\mu
converging to the boundary points of the existence range. We denote by
\phi_{\Omega}
the positive solution of (
7
) such that
\left\|\phi_{\Omega}\right\|_{\infty}=1
.

Theorem 2
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (
F2
) and (
F3
).
Assume that the function
k
satisfies either assumption (
K1
) or (
K2
), and let
u_{\mu}
be the solution of (
1
) for
\mu
in the interval of existence of a solution.
Then, as
\mu\rightarrow\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
from above, one has, uniformly in
\Omega
,

(2)

u_{\mu}\rightarrow 0

and

(3)

v_{\mu}=\frac{u_{\mu}}{\|u_{\mu}\|_{\infty}}\rightarrow\phi_{\Omega}\,.

Theorem 3
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (
F2
) and (
F3
).
Under assumption (
K2
), if
u_{\mu}
is the solution of (
1
) for
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)<\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
, then, as
\mu\rightarrow\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
from below, one has

u_{\mu}\rightarrow+\infty\ \hbox{uniformly in}\ \overline{\Omega_{0}}\,,

\frac{u_{\mu}}{\|u_{\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}}\rightarrow\phi_{\Omega_{0}}\ \hbox{uniformly in}\ \overline{\Omega_{0}}

and

u_{\mu}\to\underline{U}\quad\hbox{ locally uniformly in $\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{0}}$,}

where
\underline{U}
is the
minimal solution of

(4)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})u=k(x)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
u>0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
u=+\infty&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{0}.\end{array}\right.

We wish to emphasize that the approach taken here, based on the comparison principle and the construction of barriers, provides new proofs even for the classical case

\mathcal{F}=\Delta
and naturally explains the role of the principal eigenvalue in the existence results.
Of particular interest is the construction of a supersolution in Lemma
15
.

In the next section we state the precise conditions on the operator, we recall the definition and main properties of the principal eigenvalue and we provide some key tools such as the maximum principle and the comparison principle.
In Section 3 we prove Theorem
1
and the existence of the minimal and maximal blow-up solutions of problem (
4
).
Finally, in Section 4 we prove the asymptotic results.

2.
Comparison principles and preliminaries

2.1.
Hypotheses and notations

In the whole paper the operator
\mathcal{F}\in C(\Omega\times\mathcal{S}_{N})
will be uniformly elliptic, i.e.

\exists\Lambda\geq\lambda>0
such that

(F1)

\lambda\tr P\leq\mathcal{F}(x,X+P)-\mathcal{F}(x,X)\leq\Lambda\tr P

for all
x\in\Omega
and any
X,P\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
,
P\geq 0
,
\mathcal{S}_{N}
being the set of
N
-squared symmetric matrices.

Furthermore we will suppose:

(F2)

\mathcal{F}(x,tX)=t\mathcal{F}(x,X)\qquad\forall t\geq 0,\,X\in\mathcal{S}_{N}

and there exists a modulus of continuity
\omega
such that

(F3)

\mathcal{F}(x,X)-\mathcal{F}(y,Y)\leq\omega(\alpha|x-y|^{2}+|x-y|)

whenever
\alpha>0
,
x,y\in\Omega
,
X,Y\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
and

-3\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X&0\\
0&-Y\end{array}\right)\leq 3\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{array}\right).

Of course this condition is void if the operator does not depend explicitly on the variable
x
.

In the following we will often use the distance function. Let us fix some notations.
We will use

d_{\Omega}(x)
to indicate the distance function to the boundary of
\Omega
and, when no ambiguities arise, we will just write
d(x)
. For
\delta>0
, let us set
\Omega_{\delta}:=\{x\in\overline{\Omega},\;:\,d_{\Omega}(x)<\delta\}
. Recall that if the
\Omega
is
C^{2}
so is
d_{\Omega}
in
\Omega_{\delta}
for
\delta
sufficiently small and that
|\nabla d|=1
.

Another key role is played by the extremal Pucci operators
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and
\mathcal{M}^{-}
. Precisely, for
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
given, the Pucci’s supremum operator is defined as

(5)

\mathcal{M}^{+}(M)=\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}\geq 0}e_{i}+\lambda\,\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}\,,

where
e_{1},\ldots,e_{N}
are the eigenvalues of the matrix
M
belonging to the space
\mathcal{S}_{N}
of the
N
-squared symmetric matrices. Equivalently,
\mathcal{M}^{+}
can be defined as

\mathcal{M}^{+}(M)=\sup_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}{\rm tr}(AM)

where
{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\Lambda}=\left\{A\in{\mathcal{S}}_{N}\,:\,\lambda\,I_{N}\leq A\leq\Lambda\,I_{N}\right\}
,
I_{N}
being the identity matrix.

Symmetrically, Pucci’s infimum operator is defined as

(6)

\mathcal{M}^{-}(M)=\mathcal{M}^{-}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(M)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}\geq 0}\mu_{i}+\Lambda\,\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}=\inf_{A\in{\mathcal{A}}_{\lambda,\Lambda}}{\rm tr}(AM)\,.

2.2.
Principal eigenvalues

As mentioned in the introduction, we will use the principal eigenvalue à la Berestycki, Nirenberg Varadhan i.e.

\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega):=\sup\{\mu\in\mathbb{R}\ \mbox{such that}\ \exists\psi>0\ \mbox{and}\ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\psi)+\mu\psi\leq 0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega\}.

Of course the functions
\psi
considered in the definition of
\lambda_{1}^{+}
are meant to be supersolutions in the viscosity sense.

The reason
\lambda_{1}^{+}
is called an “eigenvalue” is that
it is possible to prove that there exists a function
\phi_{\Omega}
, positive in
\Omega
, called eigenfunction solution of

(7)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\phi_{\Omega})+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)\phi_{\Omega}=0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
\phi_{\Omega}=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

see
[
3
,
14
]
. The homogeneity condition (
F2
) is crucial for the existence of the eigenfunction
\phi_{\Omega}
and it implies that, without loss of generality,
we can and will always choose
\phi_{\Omega}
such that
\|\phi_{\Omega}\|_{\infty}=1
.

We here recall the results concerning the relationship between the maximum principle and the “principal eigenvalue”.

Proposition 4
.

If
\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
and
u
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u\geq 0\ \mbox{in }\ \Omega\ \mbox{and}\ u\leq 0\ \mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\,,

then
u\leq 0
.
If
u\geq 0
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)u\geq 0\ \mbox{in }\ \Omega\ \mbox{and}\ u\leq 0\ \mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\,,

then there exists
\tau
such that
u(x)=\tau\phi_{1}(x)

The proof goes back to
[
2
]
in the linear case and we refer to
[
3
,
14
]
for the fully nonlinear case. Let us remark that in
[
14
]
the operator is supposed to be convex.
The second result of Proposition
4
is a consequence of
[
14
, Theorem 4.1]
.
In that Proposition, the hypothesis that the operator be convex is not needed. Indeed, the proof holds true also for non convex operators since the convexity of the operator is used only in formula (4.1) of that paper; but in formula (4.1), the left hand side can be substituted by the maximal Pucci operator
\mathcal{M}^{+}
and then one can use the convexity of
\mathcal{M}^{+}
.

We now recall the following comparison principle, proved in Theorem 3.6 in
[
3
]
.

Proposition 5
.

Let
f,g\in C(\Omega)
be such that
f\leq 0
and
f\leq g
in
\Omega
. If
\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
and
v
and
u
are respectively super and subsolution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)+\mu v\leq f,\ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u\geq g\,\mbox{in}\ \Omega

such that
u\geq 0
in
\Omega
and
v\geq u
on
\partial\Omega
, then
u\geq v
in
\Omega
if

•

either
f<0
in
\Omega

•

or
g(\bar{x})>0
for any
\bar{x}
such that
f(\bar{x})=0
.

A consequence of Proposition
5
is

Corollary 6
.

Let
v
and
u
be respectively super and subsolution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)+\mu v\leq 0,\ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u\geq 0\,\mbox{in}\ \Omega.

Suppose that
\mu>0
and
\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
.
If
v\geq u>0
on
\partial\Omega
then
v\geq u
in
\Omega
.

Proof.

Consider
\epsilon>0
small enough and
\gamma_{\varepsilon}
such that on
\partial\Omega
,
u_{\varepsilon}=u-\varepsilon>v_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}=\frac{v}{1+\gamma_{\varepsilon}}
.
On the other hand,
u_{\varepsilon}
and
v_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}
satisfy respectively

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{\varepsilon})+\mu u_{\varepsilon}\leq-\mu\varepsilon<0\quad\,\text{in $\Omega$}

and

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}})+\mu v_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}\geq 0\quad\,\text{in $\Omega$}.

Hence we are in the hypothesis of Proposition
5
and

u_{\varepsilon}\geq v_{\gamma_{\varepsilon}}
in
\Omega
. Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
we get the required result.
∎

2.3.
Comparison principles

The main goal of this subsection is to prove the following comparison principle.

Proposition 7
.

Let
u_{1}>0
and
u_{2}\geq 0
in
\Omega
be satisfying

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})+\mu u_{1}\leq k(x)u_{1}^{p},\ \ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{2})+\mu u_{2}\geq k(x)u_{2}^{p},\ \mbox{in }\Omega

with
k(x)\geq 0
not identically zero.

If
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
on
\partial\Omega
then
u_{1}\geq u_{2}
in
\Omega
.

In order to do so we will use a few classical results that will be useful throughout the paper.
The first concerns difference of sub and supersolutions (see e.g.
[
4
]
and
[
10
]
) for which we will recall the proof in our setting,
the other is the famous Alexandrov-Bakelman-Pucci estimate for fully non linear operators see e.g.
[
4
]
, whose proof is not recalled here, and its consequence i.e. the maximum principle in small domain. The last ingredient for the proof of Proposition
7
is a strong comparison principle which is certainly expected and somehow known but, for completeness sake and convenience of the reader, we will give its proof.

Proposition 8
.

Let
u\in USC(\Omega)
and
v\in LSC(\Omega)
be respectively viscosity subsolution and supersolution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)=f(x,v)\quad\;\text{in $\Omega$}

and

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)=g(x,v)\quad\;\text{in $\Omega$, }

where
f,g\in C(\Omega\times\mathbb{R})
and
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (
F3
). Then the difference
w=u-v
is a
viscosity solution of the maximal differential inequality

\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}w)\geq f(x,u)-g(x,v)\quad\;\text{in $\Omega$.}

Proof.

Let
x_{0}\in\Omega
and let
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
be such that

(u-v)(x_{0})=\varphi(x_{0})\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad(u-v)(x)<\varphi(x)\quad\forall x\in\Omega\backslash\left\{x_{0}\right\}.

Fix
r>0
sufficiently small such that
\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\subset\Omega
. For
\alpha>0
, let
x_{\alpha},y_{\alpha}\in\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}
be such that

(8)

\max_{\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}\times\overline{B_{r}(x_{0})}}\left((u-\varphi)(x)-v(y)-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x-y|^{2}\right)=(u-\varphi)(x_{\alpha})-v(y_{\alpha})-\frac{\alpha}{2}|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|^{2}.

In view of
[
6
, Lemma 3.1]
one has
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|^{2}=0
and, using the strict inequality in (
8
), we may further assume, up to extracting a subsequence, that
\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}x_{\alpha}=\displaystyle\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}y_{\alpha}=x_{0}
. Moreover

\begin{split}v(x_{0})\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to+\infty}v(y_{\alpha})&\leq\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}v(y_{\alpha})\\
&\leq\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}(u-\varphi)(x_{\alpha})\leq(u-\varphi)(x_{0})=v(x_{0})\\
v(x_{0})\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to+\infty}v(y_{\alpha})&\leq\liminf_{\alpha\to+\infty}(u-\varphi)(x_{\alpha})\\
&\leq\limsup_{\alpha\to+\infty}(u-\varphi)(x_{\alpha})\leq(u-\varphi)(x_{0})=v(x_{0}).\end{split}

The above inequalities imply that

(9)

\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}v(y_{\alpha})=v(x_{0})\;\quad\text{and}\;\quad\lim_{\alpha\to+\infty}u(x_{\alpha})=u(x_{0}).

By
[
6
, Theorem 3.2]
, there exist
X_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{S}_{N}
such that

-3\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&0\\
0&I\end{array}\right)\leq\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}X_{\alpha}&0\\
0&-Y_{\alpha}\end{array}\right)\leq 3\alpha\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}I&-I\\
-I&I\end{array}\right)

and

\left(\alpha(x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha})+D\varphi(x_{\alpha}),X_{\alpha}+D^{2}\varphi(x_{\alpha})\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,+}u(x_{\alpha})\;,\;\;\left(\alpha(x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}),Y_{\alpha}\right)\in\overline{J}^{2,-}v(x_{\alpha}).

Using the sub and supersolution properties of
u
and
v
and (
F1
),(
F3
), we obtain

(10)

\begin{split}f(x_{\alpha},u(x_{\alpha}))-g(y_{\alpha},v(y_{\alpha}))&\leq F(x_{\alpha},X_{\alpha}+D^{2}\varphi(x_{\alpha}))-F(y_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha})\\
&\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{\alpha}))+F(x_{\alpha},X_{\alpha})-F(y_{\alpha},Y_{\alpha})\\
&\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{\alpha}))+\omega(\alpha|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|^{2}+|x_{\alpha}-y_{\alpha}|).\end{split}

By letting
\alpha\to+\infty
and using (
9
), we conclude that

f(x_{0},u(x_{0}))-g(x_{0},v(x_{0}))\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0})).

∎

We now recall the Alexandroff-Bakelman-Pucci estimate for viscosity subsolutions, see e.g.
[
4

.

Theorem 9
.

Suppose that
u
is a viscosity solution of

{\mathcal{M}^{+}}(D^{2}u)\geq f\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,

then there exists
C=C(N,\lambda,\Lambda,\rm{diam}\,\Omega)
such that

\sup_{\Omega}u\leq\sup_{\partial\Omega}u+C\|f^{-}\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}.

Corollary 10
.

Let
D>0
. For any domain
\Omega
such that
{\rm{diam}}\,\Omega\leq D
, and any given
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, there exists
\delta=\delta(N,\lambda,\Lambda,\mu,D)>0
such that if
|\Omega|\leq\delta
and
v
is solution of

\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}v)+\mu v\geq 0\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\ v\leq 0\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega

then
v\leq 0
in
\Omega
.

This is a well-known fact, we provide the proof because it is brief and may help the forgetful reader.

Proof.

If
\mu\leq 0
it follows from the standard maximum principle for proper operators with uniformly elliptic principal part which is independent of

. So we can suppose that
\mu>0
Suppose by contradiction that
\sup_{\Omega}v^{+}>0
. Since

{\mathcal{M}^{+}}(D^{2}v)\geq-\mu v^{+}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,

by Theorem

we get that

\sup_{\Omega}v\leq C\|\mu v^{+}\|_{L^{N}(\Omega)}\leq C\mu\delta^{\frac{1}{N}}\sup_{\Omega}v^{+}.

Hence if

we get a contradiction. Hence the choice of

depends only on

,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
D
and
\mu
.
∎

Let us now provide a strong comparison principle:

Proposition 11
.

Let
u_{1}\geq 0
and
u_{2}\geq 0
in
\Omega

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})+\mu u_{1}\leq k(x)u_{1}^{p},\ \ \mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{2})+\mu u_{2}\geq k(x)u_{2}^{p},\ \mbox{in }\Omega

.

If
u_{1}\geq u_{2}

\Omega

u_{1}>u_{2}
in
\Omega
or
u_{1}\equiv u_{2}
.

Proof.

By contradiction, we can find
x_{0},x_{1}\in\Omega
with
u_{1}(x_{1})=u_{2}(x_{1})
,
u_{1}(x_{0})>u_{2}(x_{o})
and a ball
B_{R}(x_{0})\subset\Omega
such that

(11)

u_{1}(x)>u_{2}(x)\quad\forall x\in\overline{B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}.

and
x_{1}\in B_{R}(x_{0})
. We introduce
w(x)=\delta(e^{-c|x-x_{0}|}-e^{-cR})
with

and
\delta\in(0,1)

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}(u_{2}+w))-k(x)(u_{2}+w)^{p}>\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})-k(x)u^{p}_{1}\quad\,\text{in $B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})$}

u_{2}+w\leq u_{1}\quad\,\text{on $\partial\left(B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})\right)$.}

It is easy to see that for

\lambda c^{2}-\frac{2\Lambda(N-1)c}{R}-\sup_{B_{R}(x_{o})\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}(k(x){(u_{2}(x)+1)}^{p-1})>1,

we get, in

\begin{split}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}(u_{2}+w))&-k(x)(u_{2}+w)^{p}\geq\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{2})+{\mathcal{M}}^{-}(D^{2}w)-k(x)(u_{2}+w)^{p}\\
&\geq-\mu u_{2}+{\mathcal{M}}^{-}(D^{2}w)+k(x)(u^{p}_{2}-(u_{2}+w)^{p})\\
&\geq-\mu u_{2}+\delta e^{-c|x-x_{0}|}\left(\lambda c^{2}-\frac{2\Lambda(N-1)c}{R}-\sup_{B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})}(k(x){(u_{2}+1)}^{p-1})\right)\\
&>-\mu u_{2}+\delta e^{-cR},\end{split}

while

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})-k(x)u_{1}^{p}\leq-\mu u_{1}\leq-\mu u_{2}\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Clearly
u_{2}+w\leq u_{1}
on
\partial B_{R}(x_{0})
. Moreover, using (

u_{1}(x)\geq u_{2}(x)+w(x).

This is a contradiction, since
x_{1}\in B_{R}(x_{0})\setminus B_{\frac{R}{2}}(x_{0})
.
∎

3.
Existence and nonexistence of solutions.

Part of Theorem
1
, concerns the nonexistence of positive solutions for
\mu\leq\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
and
\mu\not\in(\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0}))
. This result follows quite immediately from the definition of principal eigenvalues and their properties.

Proposition 14
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (
F2
) and (
F3
).

(1)

If
k\in C(\Omega)
satisfies (
K1
), then there are no positive solutions of (
1
) if
\mu\leq\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
.

(2)

If
k\in C(\Omega)
satisfies (

), then there are no positive solutions of (
1
) if
\mu\not\in(\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega),\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0}))

Proof.

The nonexistence of solutions of (
1
) for
\mu\leq\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
under hypothesis (
K1
) or (
K2
) relies only on the fact that
k
is nonnegative and not identically zero.

If
\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
, since
u
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u\geq 0\ \mbox{in }\ \Omega\ \mbox{and}\ u\leq 0\ \mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega,

by Proposition
4
we infer that
u\leq 0
. Hence for any
k(x)\geq 0
, if
\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
there can be no positive solutions of (
1
).

If
\mu=\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
, again, using Proposition
4
there exists
\tau
such that
u(x)=\tau\phi_{1}(x)
where
\phi_{1}
is the eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
. But this is a contradiction since
k(x)\not\equiv 0
.

As far as the nonexistence for
\mu\geq\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
, we first note that the case
\mu>\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
comes from the definition of
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
.
Indeed, if there exists a solution
u
of (
1
) for
\mu>\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
, then in
\Omega_{0}
,
u>0
and

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=0.

But, this implies by definition that
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})\geq\mu
.

If
\mu=\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
, we suppose by contradiction that there exists
u>0
solution of (
1
). This implies that there exists
\delta>0
such that
u\geq\delta>0
in
\Omega_{0}
. Let
M=\max_{\overline{\Omega_{0}}}u
and
\varepsilon>0
such that
\varepsilon<\frac{\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})\delta}{2M-\delta}
. This implies that

satisfies in

This contradicts the definition of
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
and it ends the proof of the non existence results.
∎

Next, we are going to concentrate on
the existence part of Theorem
1
. We begin with the proof of the existence of a supersolution.

Lemma 15
.

Given

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{+})+\mu w^{+}=k(x)(w^{+})^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\

Let

Proposition 16
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{F}
satisfies (
F1
), (

) and (

(1)

If

satisfies (

) and

(2)

k\in C(\Omega)
satisfies (

\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)<\mu<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})

1
).

Proof.

We begin by constructing a subsolution for (
1

k(x)\leq k_{1}
and let

0<\alpha\leq\left(\frac{\mu-\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)}{k_{1}}\right)^{\frac{1}{p-1}}.

With this choice of

is a subsolution of (

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{-})+\mu w^{-}=(\mu-\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega))w^{-}\geq k(x)(w^{-})^{p}\ \mbox{in}\ \Omega,\;\,w^{-}=0\ \mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega.

Remark
w^{-}
is a subsolution in both cases (
K1
) and (
K2

We now proceed with the case where
k
satisfies hypothesis (
K1
), so that
k_{0}\leq k(x)\leq k_{1}

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{+})+\mu w^{+}=\mu C=k_{0}C^{p}\leq k(x)(w^{+})^{p}.

When

Let

and we choose

for any

Now, under either of the hypothesis on

Let
u_{0}=w^{+}
, and

be the solution of the following Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{n+1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{n+1}=k(x)u_{n}^{p}-\mu_{0}u_{n}&\text{in $\Omega$}\\
u_{n+1}=0&\text{on $\partial\Omega$.}\end{array}\right.

Observe that

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{1}=f(x,w^{+})\geq\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{+})+(\mu-\mu_{0})w^{+}\quad\text{in $\Omega$}

Furthermore

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{1}=f(x,w^{+})\leq f(x,w^{-})\leq\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{-})+(\mu-\mu_{0})w^{-}\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Since the operator is proper, from the boundary condition we get

w^{-}\leq u_{1}\leq u_{0}\leq C_{1}.

Suppose, for induction sake that, for some

, we have
w^{-}<u_{n}\leq u_{n-1}\leq w^{+}
. Then

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{n+1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{n+1}=f(x,u_{n}(x))\geq f(x,u_{n-1}(x))=\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{n})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{n}\quad\text{in $\Omega$},

and

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{n+1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{n+1}=f(x,u_{n}(x))\leq f(x,w^{-})\leq\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w^{-})+(\mu-\mu_{0})w^{-}\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Again, the comparison principle in
\Omega
implies that

Since the sequence

is bounded, by standard

estimates, see e.g.
[

, the functions of

are also equi-Hölder continuous. Hence, under either hypothesis (
K1
) or hypothesis (
K2
), the monotone bounded sequence
u_{n}
converges to
\bar{u}\geq\alpha\phi_{\Omega}>0
solution of
(
1
).
Due to the Corollary
13
(in the particular case
\phi\equiv 0
on
\partial\Omega
and then
\psi_{\phi}\equiv 0
in
\overline{\Omega}
) the boundary condition is satisfied by

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

It follows from Propositions
14
and

.

In the following propositions, we establish the existence and uniqueness of positive solutions to the Dirichlet problem (
1
) posed in
\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0}
, with general continuous boundary condition on
\partial\Omega_{0}
.

Proposition 17
.

Let
k\in C(\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0})
and let
\phi\in C(\partial\Omega_{0})
be such that
k>0
in
\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}
and
\phi\geq 0
in
\partial\Omega_{0}
not identically zero. Then, for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a unique positive solution
u\in C(\overline{\Omega})
of

(26)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=k(x)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
u=\phi&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{0}\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Step 1.
For
\delta>0
, we shall prove that there exists
u_{\delta}
positive solution of

(27)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=(k(x)+\delta)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
u=\phi&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{0}.\end{array}\right.

We begin by constructing a super-solution. Let
\mu^{\star}>\max\{\mu,\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)\}

We are in the hypothesis of (1) in Proposition
16
, hence there exists
w
solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w)+\mu^{\star}w=(k(x)+\delta)w^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
w=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Now take
M>1
such that
Mw^{+}\geq\phi
on
\partial\Omega_{0}
. It is easy to see that
w^{+}=Mw
is a supersolution of (
27
).
On the other hand
w^{-}\equiv 0
is a subsolution.

We now proceed to construct a sequence such that
u_{0}=w^{+}
and recursively
u_{n+1}
is the solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{n+1})+(\mu-\mu_{0})u_{n+1}=(k(x)+\delta)u_{n}^{p}-\mu_{0}u_{n}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
u_{n+1}=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
u_{n+1}=\phi&\mbox{on}\ \partial\Omega_{0},\end{array}\right.

with
\mu_{0}
chosen large enough that
f(x,t)=(k(x)+\delta)t^{p}-\mu_{0}t
is decreasing in
(0,\max_{\overline{\Omega}}w^{+})
and
\mu_{0}>\mu
.

Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
16
, it is easy to see that the sequence is monotone decreasing and that uniform boundary estimates of Corollary
13
hold.
Hence the sequence
u_{n}
converges to the desired solution. We leave the details to the interested reader.

Step 2.
We want to prove that
u_{\delta}
converges, as
\delta\to 0
, to
u_{0}
a solution of (
26
). For this, we first note that, for any
\delta>0
, we have that

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{\delta})+\mu u_{\delta}={(k(x)+\delta)}u_{\delta}^{p}\geq k(x)u_{\delta}^{p}\quad\text{in $\Omega$}.

Hence, using Corollary
13
in
\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{0}
, we have that

(28)

u_{\delta}(x)\leq\psi_{\phi}(x)+Cd(x),

where
\psi_{\phi}
is the solution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\psi_{\phi})=0\;\;\;\text{in $\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}$}\,,\;\;\psi_{\phi}=0\;\;\;\text{on $\partial\Omega$}\,\;\;\psi_{\phi}=\phi\;\;\;\text{on $\partial\Omega_{0}$}.

Moreover,
u_{\delta}
is monotone with respect to
\delta
. Indeed, if
\delta_{1}<\delta_{2}
, then

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{\delta_{1}})+\mu u_{\delta_{1}}\leq(k(x)+\delta_{2})u^{p}_{\delta_{1}}\quad\,\text{in $\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{0}$}.

Combining Propositions
11
-
7
we obtain
u_{\delta_{2}}<u_{\delta_{1}}
in
\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{0}
.
So, if we fix
\bar{\delta}>0
, using (
28
), we deduce that for any
\delta\leq\bar{\delta}
it holds

u_{\bar{\delta}}\leq u_{\delta}\leq\psi_{\phi}+Cd\quad\,\text{in $\overline{\Omega}\backslash\Omega_{0}$}.

So
(u_{\delta})_{\delta}
is equibounded and, by
C^{0,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}
estimates, it converges to
u_{0}
solution of (
26
). ∎

We can now prove the existence of blow-up solutions.

Theorem 18
.

Let
k\in C(\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega}_{0})
be such that
\displaystyle\inf_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{1}}k>0
whenever
\Omega_{1}\subsetneqq\Omega
such that
\Omega_{0}\subset\subset\Omega_{1}
. Then, for any
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist
\overline{U}_{\mu}\geq\underline{U}_{\mu}>0
respectively the smallest and the largest solution of

(29)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=k(x)u^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
u=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
u=+\infty&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{0},\end{array}\right.

i.e. such that any solution
u
of (
29
) satisfies

\underline{U}_{\mu}\leq u\leq\overline{U}_{\mu}.

Proof.

Let
\delta>0
be sufficiently small that there exists (see the proof of Theorem 2.4 in
[
9
]
)
k^{\star}\in C^{2}(\overline{\Omega}_{0,\delta})
such that, for some function
f
,

k^{\star}(x):=f(d_{\Omega_{0}}(x))\leq k(x).

Let
\gamma>\frac{3}{p-1}
so that
w(x)=k^{\star}(x)^{-\gamma}
satisfies, for some
C_{0}
depending only on the ellipticity condition of
\mathcal{F}
,
\mu
and the
C^{2}
norm of
k^{\star}
, the inequality

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w)+\mu w\leq C_{0}k^{\star}(x)^{-\gamma-2}\quad\;\forall x\in\Omega_{0,\delta}.

Reducing
\delta
if necessary, me may further assume that
\Omega_{\delta}\cap\Omega_{0,\delta}=\emptyset
and that

C_{0}k^{\star}(x)^{-\gamma-2}\leq k(x)w^{p}(x)
for any
x\in\Omega_{0,\delta}
. Hence

(30)

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w)+\mu w\leq k(x)w^{p}(x)\quad\;\forall x\in\Omega_{0,\delta}.

Let
u_{n}
be the sequence of solutions of (
26
) with
\phi(x)=n
. Pick
\varepsilon
such that

(31)

0<\varepsilon<\min\left\{\min_{\left\{d_{\Omega}(x)=\delta\right\}}u_{1}\,,\,\min_{\left\{d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)\leq\delta\right\}}w\right\}

and define

\psi(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\varepsilon&\text{if $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)\geq\delta$}\\
\varepsilon+\alpha(\delta-d_{\Omega_{0}}(x))^{3}&\text{if $\frac{\delta}{2}\leq d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)<\delta$,}\end{array}\right.

where

\alpha=\frac{\max_{\left\{d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)=\frac{\delta}{2}\right\}}w-\varepsilon}{{(\delta/2)^{3}}}

is chosen in such a way

(32)

\psi(x)\geq w(x)\quad\;\text{if $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)=\frac{\delta}{2}$.}

Note that
\psi\in C^{2}(\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}})
and that

(33)

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}\psi)+\mu\psi\leq C\quad\;\forall x\in\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}

for some constant
C=C(\varepsilon,\alpha,\delta,\mu)
.

Let

W(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}w(x)&\text{if $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)<\frac{\delta}{2}$}\\
\min\left\{w(x),\psi(x)\right\}&\text{if $\frac{\delta}{2}\leq d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)<\delta$}\\
\psi(x)&\text{if $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)\geq\delta$ and $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)\geq\delta$}\\
\min\left\{\psi(x),u_{1}(x)\right\}&\text{if $d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)<\delta$}.\end{array}\right.

In view of (
31
)-(
32
) the function
W
is continuous in
\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega_{0}}
. We claim that for

M^{p-1}\geq\max\left\{1\,,\,\frac{C}{\varepsilon^{p}\displaystyle\inf_{\Omega\backslash\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}}k}\right\},

the function
W^{+}(x)=MW(x)
is a supersolution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u)+\mu u=k(x)u^{p}\quad\;\text{in $\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega_{0}}$.}

By the choice of
M
, it is easy to realize that the functions
Mw
,
M\psi
and
Mu_{1}
are supersolutions of the above equation respectively in
\Omega_{0,\delta}
,
\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}}
and
\Omega\backslash{\overline{\Omega}_{0}}
.
Hence for
x\notin\partial\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}\cup\partial\Omega_{0,\delta}\cup\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, the function
W^{+}
satisfies, in the viscosity sense,
the inequality
\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u(x))+\mu u(x)\leq k(x)u^{p}(x)
.
To check the supersolution property in the remaining cases, let
\varphi\in C^{1}(\Omega\backslash\overline{\Omega_{0}})
touching from below
W^{+}
at
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}\cup\partial\Omega_{0,\delta}\cup\partial\Omega_{\delta}
. If
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{0,\frac{\delta}{2}}
, from (
32
) we infer that
\varphi
is in fact a test function for
Mw
and the required inequality holds.
If instead
x_{0}\in\partial\Omega_{0,\delta}\cup\partial\Omega_{\delta}
, then
W^{+}\equiv M\psi
in a neighborhood on
x_{0}
because of (
31
), and the claim is proved.

Hence by Proposition
7
the sequence
u_{n}
is monotone increasing and bounded above by
W^{+}
and it converges to
\underline{U}_{\mu}
solution of (
29
). Furthermore it is easy to see that
\underline{U}_{\mu}
is the smallest of all blow up solutions of (
29
).

In order to construct the largest of blow up solutions, consider a sequence of domains
\Omega_{n}
such that for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\Omega_{n+1}\subset\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega

and
\Omega_{0}=\cap_{n}\Omega_{n}
. Let
v_{n}
be a solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v_{n})+\mu v_{n}=k(x)v_{n}^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{n}\\
v_{n}=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
v_{n}=+\infty&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{n}.\end{array}\right.

Then
v_{n}
is a monotone decreasing sequence of solutions converging to
\overline{U}_{\mu}
a solution of (

).
∎

Asymptotic results

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By the monotonicity proved in Corollary
12
, it is enough to study the asymptotic behaviour of the sequence
\{u_{n}=u_{\mu_{n}}\}
, where
\{\mu_{n}\}
is a monotone decreasing sequence converging to
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
. The sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is monotone decreasing and uniformly bounded, hence it converges locally uniformly to

u_{0}\geq 0
nonnegative solution of

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}u_{0})+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)u_{0}=k(x)u_{0}^{p}\geq 0\;\;\text{in $\Omega$,}\;u\leq 0\;\;\text{on $\partial\Omega$}.

By Proposition
4
,
u_{0}\equiv\tau\phi_{1}
for some
\tau\geq 0
. Since
k\not\equiv 0
, then necessarily
\tau=0
and
u_{0}\equiv 0
.

The above argument shows that
u_{\mu}\to 0
uniformly in
\Omega
as
\mu\to\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)
. Observing further that
the functions
v_{\mu}=\frac{u_{\mu}}{\|u_{\mu}\|_{\infty}}
satisfy

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v_{\mu})+\mu v_{\mu}=\|u_{\mu}\|_{\infty}^{p-1}k(x)v_{\mu}^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
v_{\mu}>0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
v_{\mu}=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega,\end{array}\right.

standard
C^{0,\alpha}_{\text{loc}}
elliptic estimates imply that, locally uniformly in
\Omega
,
v_{\mu}\to v
, where
v
is a nonnegative solution of

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)v=0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\\
v=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega.\end{array}\right.

Moreover, denoting by
x_{\mu}
the maximum point of
v_{\mu}
, that is
v_{\mu}(x_{\mu})=1
, by Corollary
13
we also infer that there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\mu
(since
\mu
is bounded in this argument), such that
1=v_{\mu}(x_{\mu})\leq Cd(x_{\mu})
. Thus
(x_{\mu})_{\mu}
is bounded away from the boundary of
\Omega
and the uniform local convergence of
(u_{\mu})_{\mu}
ensures that
\left\|v\right\|=1
. By Proposition
4
, we conclude that
v=\phi_{\Omega}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3
.

We follow the proof of Du and Huang
[
9
]
. For
n\geq 1
, let us introduce the set

\Omega_{n}=\left\{x\in\Omega\ :d_{\Omega_{0}}(x)<\frac{1}{n}\right\}

which will be assumed, without loss of generality, to be contained in
\Omega
. We further set
\mu_{n}=\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{n})
. Then,
\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega)<\mu_{n}<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
and
\mu_{n}\to\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
. As in the proof of Theorem
2
, in order to study the asymptotic behavior of
u_{\mu}
as
\mu\to\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})
, it is enough to analyze the monotone increasing sequence
u_{n}=u_{\mu_{n}}
.

Step 1
: For any compact subset
K\subset\Omega_{0}
, one has

u_{n}\to+\infty\quad\hbox{ uniformly in }K\,.

Let us set

\alpha\,:=\inf_{\Omega_{0}}u_{1}\,,\qquad\beta\,:=\min_{K}\phi_{\Omega_{0}}\,.

Then, both
\alpha
and
\beta
are positive, and
u_{n}\geq\alpha
in
\Omega_{0}
for all
n\geq 1
. For
M>0
fixed arbitrarily large, we can select a compact set
K^{*}
, with
K\subset K^{*}\subset\Omega_{0}
such that

\phi_{\Omega_{0}}\leq\frac{\alpha\beta}{2M}\qquad\hbox{ on }\partial K^{*}\,.

Let us further denote by
\phi_{n}
the eigenfunction
\phi_{\Omega_{n}}
. We observe that
\phi_{n}\to\phi_{\Omega_{0}}
uniformly in
K^{*}
. Then, for
n
sufficiently large, we have

\phi_{n}<\frac{\alpha\beta}{M}\quad\hbox{ on }\partial K^{*}\,,\quad\phi_{n}>\frac{\beta}{2}\quad\hbox{ in }K\,.

Moreover, both
u_{n}
and
\frac{M}{\beta}\phi_{n}
are solutions of the equation

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)+\mu_{n}v=0\quad\mbox{in int}(K^{*})

and
\frac{M}{\beta}\phi_{n}\leq\alpha\leq u_{n}
on
\partial K^{*}
. Since
\mu_{n}<\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})<\lambda^{+}_{1}({\rm int}(K^{*}))
, by Corollary
6
we deduce that
u_{n}\geq\frac{M}{\beta}\phi_{n}
in
K^{*}
. Hence,
u_{n}\geq\frac{M}{2}
in
K
and, by the arbitrariness of
M>0
, we obtain the conclusion of Step 1.

Step 2
:
\inf_{\Omega_{0}}u_{n}\to+\infty
.

By the minimum principle, for all
n\geq 1
there exists a minimum point
x_{n}\in\partial\Omega_{0}
for
u_{n}
in
\overline{\Omega_{0}}
. Let us assume, by contradiction, that the conclusion of Step 2 is false. Then, up to a subsequence still denoted with
n
, we have that
\{u_{n}(x_{n})\}
is a bounded sequence.

By the uniform interior ball property for
\Omega_{0}
, there exists
R>0
and, for each
n\geq 1
, a point
y_{n}\in\Omega_{0}
such that
\overline{B_{R}(y_{n})}\subset\overline{\Omega_{0}}
and
\overline{B_{r}(y_{n})}\cap\partial\Omega_{0}=\{x_{n}\}
.

For
\sigma\,,\ c_{n}>0
to be suitably fixed, let us consider the function

v_{n}(x)=u_{n}(x_{n})+c_{n}\left(e^{-\sigma|x-y_{n}|^{2}}-e^{-\sigma R^{2}}\right)\,.

A direct computation shows that, for
\sigma>0
sufficiently large independently of
c_{n}
,
v_{n}
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v_{n})+\mu_{n}v_{n}\geq 0\quad\hbox{ in }B_{R}(y_{n})\setminus B_{R/2}(y_{n})\,.

Moreover, on
\partial B_{R}(y_{n})
we have
v_{n}(x)=u_{n}(x_{n})\leq u_{n}(x)
, as well as
v_{n}(x)\leq u_{n}(x)
for
x\in\partial B_{R/2}(y_{n})
by choosing

c_{n}=\frac{\inf_{B_{R/2}(y_{n})}u_{n}-u_{n}(x_{n})}{e^{-\sigma R^{2}/4}-e^{-\sigma R^{2}}}\,.

We note that, by Step 2 and the assumption on the boundedness of
\{u_{n}(x_{n})\}
, we have that
c_{n}\to+\infty
.

Corollary
6
implies that
u_{n}\geq v_{n}
in
B_{R}(y_{n})\setminus B_{R/2}(y_{n})
, and
u_{n}(x_{n})=v_{n}(x_{n})
. Hence, setting
\nu_{n}=\frac{y_{n}-x_{n}}{|y_{n}-x_{n}|}
, we obtain

(34)

\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\geq\frac{\partial v_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})=c_{n}\left(2\sigma Re^{-\sigma R^{2}}\right)\to+\infty\,.

On the other hand, we can consider the solution
w_{n}
of the problem

\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}w_{n})+\mu_{n}w_{n}=k(x)w_{n}^{p}&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
w_{n}>0&\mbox{in }\ \Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega}_{0}\\
w_{n}=0&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega\\
w_{n}=u_{n}(x_{n})&\mbox{on }\ \partial\Omega_{0}\end{array}\right.

By the comparison principle given in Proposition
11
, we deduce that
u_{n}\geq w_{n}
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
and, obviously,
u_{n}(x_{n})=w_{n}(x_{n})
. Hence, we also have

\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\leq\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\,.

Since
\{u_{n}(x_{n})\}
is bounded, the maximum principle implies that
\{w_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded in
\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega_{0}
. By standard elliptic estimates, we then obtain that
\{w_{n}\}
is bounded in
C^{1}(\overline{\Omega}\setminus\Omega_{0})
, hence there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

\frac{\partial u_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\leq\frac{\partial w_{n}}{\partial\nu_{n}}(x_{n})\leq C_{0}\,,

which is a contradiction to (
34
).

Step 3
:
v_{n}\,:=u_{n}/\|u_{n}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}\to\phi_{\Omega_{0}}
uniformly in
\overline{\Omega}_{0}
.

The functions
v_{n}
satisfy

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v_{n})+\mu_{n}v_{n}=0\,,\quad v_{n}>0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega_{0}

and they are uniformly bounded. By standard
C^{1}
elliptic estimates, they are uniformly bounded in
C^{1}(\Omega_{0})
, hence there exists
v\in C(\overline{\Omega_{0}})
such that, up to a subsequence,
v_{n}\to v
uniformly in
\overline{\Omega_{0}}
. As a consequence,
v
satisfies

\mathcal{F}(x,D^{2}v)+\lambda_{1}^{+}(\Omega_{0})v=0\,,\quad v\geq 0\quad\hbox{in }\Omega_{0}\,.

Since
\left\|v\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}=1
, the strong maximum principle implies that
v>0
in
\Omega_{0}
. Hence, both
v
and
\phi_{\Omega_{0}}
are positive solutions of the same equation in
\Omega_{0}
. By Proposition
4
, we deduce that there exists
\tau>0
such that
v=\tau\,\phi_{\Omega_{0}}
. Finally, since
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}=\|\phi_{\Omega_{0}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{0})}=1
, it follows that
\tau=1
.

Step 4
:
u_{n}\to\underline{U}
locally uniformly in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
.

By Proposition
4
, we have that
u_{n}\leq\underline{U}
in
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
. Hence, the monotone increasing sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is locally uniformly bounded in
\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{0}}
. Thus, by using also Step 2, we obtain that
u_{n}\to V
locally uniformly in
\Omega\setminus\overline{\Omega_{0}}
, where
V
is a solution of problem (
4
). By minimality of
\underline{U}
, we then conclude
V\equiv\underline{U}
.
∎
