Integrated analysis of physicochemical properties, microbiome, and flavor profiles for differentiating two aroma grades of sauce-flavor Daqu
Authors: Yuanmin Huang, Fan Chen, Zhihai Yu, Xiaofang Sheng, Shangyu Wen, Xuan Zhang, Weiyuan Tang, Mingzheng Huang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Aroma characteristics are critical indicators for evaluating sauce-flavor Daqu quality. This study systematically compared physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, microbiomes, and flavor profiles of first-grade (GF) and second-grade (GS) aroma Daqu. GF had higher total acidity, amino nitrogen content, acid protease activity, a lower pH, and was correlated with enrichment of bacteria potentially associated with flavor precursor production such as Kroppenstedtia guangzhouensis and Kroppenstedtia eburnea. GS showed higher liquefying/cellulase activities and pH, and was associated with dominance by hydrolytic fungi such as Paecilomyces variotii and off-odor-related Oceanobacillus. HS-SPME-GC–MS combined with VIP and OAV analyses identified 11 differential volatile compounds. Aldehydes were strongly correlated with positive aroma grading and may serve as potential indicators associated with grade differentiation, while GS accumulated dimethyl trisulfide correlating with off-odors. The findings reveal the relationships between multi-omics characteristics and aroma grade differentiation of Daqu, and provide theoretical support for Daqu quality evaluation and production regulation.

Introduction

As one of the four major flavor styles of traditional Chinese Baijiu, sauce-flavor Baijiu boasts the most complex flavor profile among Baijiu variants (Qin et al., 2024). It is brewed via a solid-state fermentation process using high-temperature Daqu as the core saccharifying and fermenting agent (Luo et al., 2025), with its unique flavor originating from intricate microbial metabolic activities and enzymatic catalysis within Daqu. Daqu harbors abundant microbial communities and enzyme systems: microbes secrete hydrolases to decompose macromolecules in raw materials into small molecules, which serve as nutrients and precursors for volatile flavor compound synthesis (Chen, Peng, et al., 2025; Gong et al., 2025). Given that Daqu accounts for 50% of fermentation grains in sauce-flavor Baijiu production (Tang et al., 2024), the quality of Daqu directly determines the flavor quality and grade of the final Baijiu product. In actual industrial production, sauce-flavor Daqu is routinely graded according to comprehensive quality indexes, and aroma characteristics act as the primary criterion for quality classification. Accurate discrimination of aroma grades is important for rational Daqu blending, standardized fermentation management, and stable production of high-quality sauce-flavor Baijiu, since inconsistent aroma properties of Daqu can result in unstable flavor and variable product quality. Therefore, aroma characteristics represent one of the core indicators for Daqu grading and quality control. However, current Daqu quality evaluation primarily relies on sensory assessment and simplistic physicochemical analyses (Xing et al., 2025). While sensory evaluation remains the indispensable industrial standard for quality grading, its inherent subjectivity poses challenges for standardization, quality control and stable production of the sauce-flavor Baijiu industry (Tang et al., 2024). Therefore, discovering objective molecular markers to assist and quantify the existing sensory evaluation system is of great significance.

Identifying key microbial taxa and metabolites associated with Daqu quality has therefore become a research priority (Chen, Peng, et al., 2025; Niu et al., 2025). With advances in modern molecular biology, high-throughput sequencing (Yu, Huang, et al., 2024), metagenomics (Hou et al., 2024; Yang, Yuan, et al., 2025) and untargeted metabolomics (Dai et al., 2024) have enabled systematic analysis of Daqu microbial composition and metabolic function evolution. Previous studies have characterized microbial markers for different Daqu types: Kroppenstedtia and Bacillus distinguish yellow/white Daqu, while Oceanobacillus and Scopulibacillus characterize black Daqu (Chen, Peng, et al., 2025). Staphylococcus gallinarum, Brevibacterium intestinavium, and Kroppenstedtia eburnea are markers for high-temperature Daqu (Yu, Yao, et al., 2025), and Staphylococcus gallinarum, Kroppenstedtia sp. and Saccharopolyspora sp. differentiate high-temperature Daqu from medium-temperature Daqu (Wang et al., 2024). Metabolomic studies have revealed that yellow and black Daqu are enriched in amino acids and fatty acids (Chen, Peng, et al., 2025), with amino acids identified as potential quality markers (Xing et al., 2025); trimethylpyrazine, 2,3-butanediol, and methyl hexanoate vary across Daqu grades (Yu, Liu, et al., 2025); and pyrazine compounds dominate high-temperature Daqu metabolites (Tang, Li, Li, et al., 2025). However, almost all existing studies have focused on Daqu classification based on color or production temperature, rather than on actual sensory aroma grades. Reliable microbial and metabolic markers that can accurately distinguish Daqu with different aroma characteristics are still lacking. In addition, the formation mechanism of aroma differences and the intrinsic relationship between microbial communities and aroma-related metabolites in sauce-flavor Daqu of different aroma grades remain unclear.

Aroma characteristics are the most critical sensory quality of Daqu, governing its quality alongside appearance and contour (Tang et al., 2024). Previous studies have identified 26 compounds associated with Daqu's unique “Qu-aroma” (e.g., isovaleric acid, 1-hexanol, isovaleraldehyde, γ-nonalactone, and 4-vinylguaiacol) (Yang et al., 2024), and 29 key aroma-active compounds across grades, with butyl acrylate and (2E)-2-octenal contributing floral/fruity notes and linalool/phenylacetaldehyde imparting sweetness (Tang et al., 2024). Acetic acid, isovaleric acid, phenylethanol, acetophenone, and guaiacol distinguish Daqu with and without Douchi aroma (He et al., 2025),while low-temperature/humid production conditions induce pungent ammonia-like odors by trimethylamine (Tang, Li, Shan, et al., 2025). Despite the established role of aroma in Daqu quality evaluation, most current grading systems rely on color rather than aroma, with limited research on aroma-driven grading and its underlying mechanisms.

Based on the above observations, this study proposes a chemical hypothesis: variations in the aroma grade of sauce-flavor Daqu are likely associated with differences in the composition and content of volatile aroma-active compounds, which may be closely related to the structure and metabolic characteristics of the indigenous microbial community. Specifically, we hypothesize that: (1) Sauce-flavor Daqu of different aroma grades exhibit distinct microbial community structures, and specific dominant taxa may be closely related to their corresponding metabolic potentials. (2) These differential microbial taxa may participate in the biosynthesis or biotransformation of flavor precursors, thereby potentially contributing to the formation of characteristic volatile aroma compounds. (3) The relative contents of these aroma-active compounds and their potential synergistic effects may be important factors underlying the sensory aroma grade of Daqu, and could serve as candidate chemical markers for Daqu aroma grade differentiation.

To address this gap, the present study focuses on sauce-flavor Daqu with distinct aroma grades. Integrating metagenomic sequencing, headspace solid-phase microextraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS), and physicochemical analyses, we systematically characterized differences in microbial community structure, volatile compound profiles, and physicochemical properties between aroma grades. To ensure analytical rigor and reliable identification of volatile compounds, the present study used a combination of NIST library matching, linear retention indices (LRI) validation, and verification with authentic standards for key odorants wherever possible, to improve the accuracy and credibility of compound identification. This multi-dimensional investigation aims to reveal the discriminative characteristics of aroma-grade sauce-flavor Daqu, establish a theoretical framework for understanding aroma-grade differentiation, and provide scientific support for standardized quality evaluation and production regulation of sauce-flavor Daqu.

Materials and methods

Sample collection

Samples were collected from a sauce-flavor Baijiu enterprise in Moutai Town, Guizhou Province, China. The raw material used for Daqu preparation was predominantly wheat. Briefly, the Daqu preparation process involved crushing the wheat, mixing it with appropriate amounts of water and mother Daqu, shaping the mixture into blocks, and allowing it to undergo high-temperature solid-state fermentation before storage (Zhu et al., 2022). All samples were produced under consistent natural environmental conditions with a standardized fermentation cycle of 45 days, and originated from the same production batch to minimize confounding variations from raw materials, production technology, and fermentation environment. A total of 20 Daqu blocks were randomly collected from different positions in the same storage warehouse, including 10 first-grade Daqu (GF) and 10 s-grade Daqu (GS).

Daqu grade classification was conducted by a 16-member (4 males and 12 females, aged 20 to 30 years) expert panel consisting of production and research personnel specializing in Daqu from sauce-flavor Baijiu enterprises. All panelists had extensive industrial experience in quality evaluation, and no additional training was necessary. The two aroma grades were classified according to the sensory evaluation criteria established based primarily on overall aroma profile rather than visual appearance (GF and GS showed no obvious differences in external morphology), and the final grade was subject to the unanimous conclusion of the full panel. Detailed grading criteria are as follows. First-grade Daqu (GF): Normal and typical sauce-flavor aroma, with noticeable Douchi-like aroma and roasted aroma, showing the characteristic basic aroma of high-quality sauce-flavor Daqu. Second-grade Daqu (GS): Retained the basic characteristic aroma of sauce-flavor Daqu, but exhibited obvious offensive odors, including moldy odor, ammonia-like odor, acetic acid odor, and other off-flavors. Representative photographs of GF and GS samples are provided in Fig. S1 to show their appearance.

Each Daqu block was sampled using the quartering method, with 1.0 kg aliquots weighed, ground, and homogenized thoroughly. Homogenized samples were divided into two portions: 10 g aliquots were sealed in sterile cryovials and stored at −80 °C for volatile compound analysis and metagenomic sequencing. The remaining samples were placed in self-sealing bags and stored at −20 °C for physicochemical property determination.

Analysis of physicochemical and enzymatic properties

Physicochemical indicators of Daqu, including moisture content, pH value, acidity, amino nitrogen content, and starch content, were determined in accordance with the national standard General Analysis Methods for Daqu (QB/T 4257-2011). Meanwhile, the activities of three key hydrolytic enzymes (saccharifying enzyme, liquefying enzyme, and esterifying enzyme) were also quantified following the same standard protocol.

Additionally, the reducing sugar content of the samples was assayed via the 3,5-dinitrosalicylic acid (DNS) method (Luo et al., 2022). For protease activity determination, the protocol established by Fan et al. (2015) was adopted as a reference. Specially, cellulase activity was measured using the carboxymethyl cellulose sodium-DNS (CMCNa-DNS) method (Liu, Li, et al., 2024), whereas hemicellulase activity was quantified by the DNS method using xylan as the substrate (Zhang et al., 2024)—consistent with the reducing sugar assay principle but optimized for hemicellulose hydrolysis products.

Metagenomic sequencing

Metagenomic DNA extraction, metagenomic library construction, and subsequent bioinformatics analyses were performed following previously established methods (Gan et al., 2025). Total microbial DNA was extracted from samples using the OMEGA Mag-Bind Soil DNA Kit (Cat. No. M5635-02) (Omega Bio-Tek, Norcross, GA, USA) in strict accordance with the manufacturer's standard operating procedures.

DNA quantity was quantified using a Qubit™ 4 Fluorometer (WiFi-enabled model: Q33238) equipped with Q32856 tubes and the Q33231 assay kit (Invitrogen, Carlsbad, CA, USA), while DNA quality was evaluated via agarose gel electrophoresis. Extracted microbial DNA was used to construct 400-bp insert metagenomic shotgun libraries with Illumina TruSeq Nano DNA LT Kit, followed by sequencing on an Illumina NovaSeq X Plus (USA) with 150 bp paired-end (PE150) reads.

After quality filtering of raw sequencing data, the clean reads were taxonomically classified with Kaiju software; the reference database was the NCBI non-redundant protein (NR) database with an e-value cutoff of 1e−5. Open reading frames (ORFs) in assembled contigs were predicted with MetaGeneMark, and non-redundant genes were functionally annotated against KEGG and CAZy databases using mmseqs2 algorithm. The raw metagenomic sequencing data have been deposited in the NCBI Sequence Read Archive (SRA) under the BioProject accession number: PRJNA1443083.

Flavor detection

Sensory analysis

Sensory analysis of the aroma profile of Daqu was performed by a panel of 10 assessors with extensive sensory evaluation experience (4 males and 6 females, aged 20 to 30 years). Prior to formal evaluation, the panel generated a comprehensive list of sensory attribute descriptors for Daqu aroma via free listing. Combined with relevant published literature and practical industrial production experience, the panel processed the collected descriptors by merging synonyms and removing invalid and redundant terms. For sensory assessment, 20 g of Daqu sample was placed in a 50 mL beaker for aroma evaluation. A series of n-butanol standard solutions with gradient concentrations were used as reference samples for aroma intensity calibration. The intensity of each aroma attribute was rated on a 10-point scale (0 = none and 10 = strong), and the mean score for each descriptor was calculated across all panel members. The experiments involved in this study were conducted with the full knowledge and consent of all participants, and no ethical approval was required for this research. All olfactory tests were completed within safe dosage ranges, and no participants suffered physical or psychological discomfort. Meanwhile, the rights and privacy of all sensory panelists were strictly protected throughout the trial, and no personal identifiable information was disclosed. The experimental Daqu samples were derived from common food-grade cereal raw materials such as wheat.

Electronic nose analysis of aroma profiles

A portable PEN3 electronic nose system, equipped with ten sensors sensitive to diverse volatile compounds (Hou et al., 2024), was used for aroma detection. The measurement protocol followed the method described by Wang et al. (2024).

Identification of volatile compounds via HS-SPME-GC–MS

Headspace solid-phase microextraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) was employed for volatile compound analysis. A 1 cm-50/30 μm DVB/CAR/PDMS SPME fiber was used for extraction. Briefly, 0.5 g of sample was placed in a 20 mL headspace vial, supplemented with 10 μL cyclohexanone (internal standard) and 8 mL saturated NaCl solution, then sealed with a PTFE septum. The vial was equilibrated at 40 °C for 15 min, followed by headspace extraction at the same temperature for 30 min. The fiber was desorbed in the GC injection port for 2 min under splitless mode.

GC and MS operating conditions were consistent with those reported by Sheng et al. (2024). Volatile compounds were separated on a high-polarity InertCap WAX capillary column (60 m × 0.25 mm i.d., 0.25 μm film thickness). Qualitative analysis was performed by matching mass spectra to the NIST 17 standard library with a similarity threshold of ≥80. Under identical chromatographic conditions, linear retention indices (LRI) were calculated by analyzing a series of n-alkanes (C7–C30). Only compounds showing an LRI difference of less than 10 compared with literature values were retained for qualitative identification. Cyclohexanone was used as the internal standard. The internal standard stock solution was prepared at 1520 μg/L, and 10 μL was added to each sample, giving a final concentration of 1900 μg/L. Semi-quantitative analysis of volatile compounds was conducted using the internal standard method, with the formula as follows (Zhang, Wang, et al., 2025):ρx=AxAs×ρs×Vmwhere ρx is the mass concentration of the unknown volatile compound (μg/kg); Ax is the peak area of the unknown volatile compound; As is the peak area of the internal standard; ρs is the mass concentration of the internal standard (μg/L). V is the volume of the extraction solvent (L); m is the mass of the Daqu sample (kg).

Accurate quantification of key differential compounds and OAV analysis

The differential volatile compounds screened via semi-quantitative analysis were accurately quantified using the calibration curve method, and their flavor contribution was evaluated via the odor activity value (OAV). A total of 13 compounds were precisely quantified, including isobutyraldehyde, 2-methylbutyraldehyde, isovaleraldehyde, hexanal, 3-methyl-1-butanol, 3-octanone, dimethyl trisulfide, 1-nonanal, ethyl nonanoate, linalool, 1-octanol, 2-undecanone, and guaiacol. The detailed procedures are as follows:

An ethanol-water solution was used as the solvent to dissolve the target compounds. The standard stock solution of each target compound with a known concentration was serially diluted to prepare standard working solutions with 7 concentration levels. 8 mL of ultrapure water and the internal standard solution were added to each working solution, followed by vortex mixing for subsequent detection (He et al., 2025).

The above standard working solutions were analyzed by GC–MS under the exact same conditions as those used for sample determination. The calibration curve of each target compound was plotted with the peak area ratio of the target analyte to the internal standard as the ordinate (Y-axis), and the concentration ratio of the target analyte to the internal standard as the abscissa (X-axis). The final mass concentration of the target compound in the sample was calculated based on the corresponding calibration curve.

The contribution of each volatile compound to the overall flavor of the sample was characterized by the OAV, which was calculated with the formula: OAV = C/OT. Where C is the mass concentration of the target compound in the sample, and OT is the odor threshold of the corresponding compound in water. Compounds with an OAV ≥ 1 are generally considered to have the potential to contribute to the overall aroma profile of the sample.

Statistical analysis

Intergroup differences were analyzed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test in R statistical software, and the Benjamini-Hochberg (BH) method was used for FDR correction for multiple comparisons, with the significance threshold set as adjusted p < 0.05. For microbial community analysis, α- and β-diversity analyses were performed to characterize variations in microbial composition and function. Microbial communities comparisons across samples were based on the Bray-Curtis distance metric, with results visualized via principal coordinate analysis (PCoA).

Microbial analysis results were plotted using R packages (ggplot2, ggtree), while violin plots of physicochemical indices and enzyme activities were generated in GraphPad Prism (Version 10.4.1). For flavor compound data, evaluation and correlation analysis were conducted using the free online platform Metware Tools (https://cloud.metware.cn).

Before PCA and OPLS-DA analysis, all relative content data of volatile compounds were preprocessed by UV standardization (Unit Variance Scaling) to eliminate the influence of different content orders of magnitude of compounds on the model. Spearman correlation analysis was performed between dominant genera and physicochemical variables, volatile compounds, and the BH method was used for FDR correction for multiple comparisons. The threshold for significant correlation was set as adjusted p < 0.05 and correlation coefficient |r| ≥ 0.8.

Results

Physicochemical and enzyme activity characteristics

Daqu quality is closely associated with its biochemical properties. Physicochemical analysis revealed that GS had a significantly higher pH than GF (p = 0.000004, 95% CI = −0.35, −0.18), whereas GF exhibited significantly higher total acidity (p = 0.003, 95% CI = 0.05, 0.22) and amino nitrogen (p = 0.006, 95% CI = 0.16, 0.85) content compared to GS. No significant difference was detected between GS and GF in moisture content (p = 0.962, 95% CI = −0.38, 0.39), reducing sugar (p = 0.768, 95% CI = −0.78, 1.06) content, or starch (p = 0.299, 95% CI = −7.97, 2.61) content (Fig. 1a–f). Enzyme activity analysis indicated that GS exhibited extremely significantly higher liquefying (p = 0.000057, 95% CI = −0.73, −0.33) and cellulase activities (p = 0.004, 95% CI = −0.36, −0.08) than GF (Fig. 1g–l). Saccharifying enzyme activity (p = 0.239, 95% CI = −520.16, 139.46) was apparently higher in GS, whereas hemicellulose (p = 0.613, 95% CI = −0.91, 0.55), acid protease (p = 0.146, 95% CI = −9.55, 58.87), and esterifying enzyme activities (p = 0.865, 95% CI = −12.88, 15.17) were apparently higher in GF, though none of these four indicators showed significant differences.Fig. 1Differences in physicochemical properties (a-f) and enzyme activities (g-l) between two grades of Daqu (GF: first-grade Daqu; GS: second-grade Daqu). p indicates statistical significance.Fig. 1

Microbial composition

Metagenomic sequencing was used to characterize the microbial community composition of two grades of sauce-flavor Daqu. To further elaborate microbial community structural characteristics, systematic analysis of microbial composition was performed at three taxonomic levels, namely, phylum, genus, and species. Across all samples, 182 phyla, 4280 genera, and 18,111 species were identified in total.

At the phylum level, Firmicutes was the dominant bacterial phylum in both grades of Daqu, with a higher relative abundance in GS (Fig. 2a). For fungi, Ascomycota was more abundant in GF (Fig. 2d).Fig. 2Composition of microbial communities in different grades of Daqu. Composition of microbial communities in different grades of Daqu. Bacterial community composition at the phylum (a), genus (b), and species (c) levels; fungal community composition at the phylum (d), genus (e), and species (f) levels.

At the genus level, the bacterial genera in Daqu were dominated by Kroppenstedtia, Desmospora, Saccharopolyspora, and Bacillus, of which Kroppenstedtia, Desmospora, and Saccharopolyspora were more prevalent in GF (Fig. 2b). Fungal genera were dominated by Aspergillus, Paecilomyces, and Lichtheimia; Paecilomyces had a higher relative abundance in GF, while Aspergillus was more dominant in GS (Fig. 2e).

At the species level, the dominant bacterial species were Kroppenstedtia guangzhouensis, Kroppenstedtia eburnea, Desmospora sp. 8437, and Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula, all of which were more dominant in GF (Fig. 2c). Dominant fungal species included Paecilomyces variotii, Rasamsonia emersonii, Lichtheimia corymbifera, and Aspergillus chevalieri. Specifically, GF exhibited significantly higher relative abundances of P. variotii and R. emersonii compared to GS (Fig. 2f).

Microbial diversity analysis

To characterize the complexity and inter-sample differences of microbial communities in Daqu (beyond compositional characteristics described in Section 3.2), α-diversity analysis was performed using metagenomic sequencing data (Fig. 3a). Results showed that GF exhibited higher α-diversity indices than GS, but with varying statistical significance: the Shannon index was significantly higher in GF than in GS (p = 0.021), the Simpson index was also significantly elevated in GF (p = 0.021), while the Chao1 and ACE indices showed numerical increases in GF but no significant differences (p = 0.15 for both). This indicates that GF had significantly greater microbial diversity, with a non-significant trend toward higher richness.Fig. 3Microbial diversity (a); principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) (b); LEfSe analysis cladogram (c) and horizontal bar chart (d).Fig. 3

To further assess differences in microbial community structure, principal coordinate analysis (PCoA) was performed based on the Bray-Curtis distance algorithm for visualization (Fig. 3b). PCoA results demonstrated significant separation between the two Daqu grades.

Subsequently, LEfSe analysis was used to identify Daqu grade-specific microbial taxa associated with a screening threshold of a LDA score > 3.5 (Fig. 3c–d). At the phylum/class level, GF had higher abundances of Actinomycetia (class) and Firmicutes (phylum), while GS was more enriched in phylum Ascomycota and Mucoromycota. At the genus level, GF had higher abundances of Kroppenstedtia, Desmospora, and Saccharopolyspora, whereas Aspergillus, Paecilomyces, and Lichtheimia were more abundant in GS. At the species level, GF exhibited higher abundances of Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula, Kroppenstedtia guangzhouensis, Kroppenstedtia eburnea, and Desmospora sp. 8437, while GS was more enriched in Paecilomyces variotii, Lichtheimia corymbifera, and Aspergillus chevalieri.

Additionally, the microbial community in Fig. S2 exhibits a distinct phylogenetic framework, with four major phyla (Firmicutes, Mucoromycota, Ascomycota, and Actinobacteria) as the core taxonomic backbone.

Dominant taxa cluster into key genera, each with multiple representative species: Aspergillus (including A. fumigatus, A. chevalieri), Kroppenstedtia (e.g., K. eburnea, K. guangzhouensis), Virgibacillus (e.g., V. oceani, V. dakarensis), and Melghirimyces (including M. algeriensis, M. thermohalophilus). Notable additional taxa include Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula, Paecilomyces variotii, and Lichtheimia corymbifera. Its network of positive and negative correlations forms a regulated coexistence structure linked to the functional performance of Daqu.

Annotations of carbohydrate-active enzymes (CAZy)

The CAZy database was used to annotate carbohydrate metabolic potential encoded in the metagenomes of Daqu-related microorganisms (Fig. 4b). Among the six major CAZy functional categories, Glycoside Hydrolases (GH) and Glycosyl Transferases (GT) were the most abundant, whereas Polysaccharide Lyases (PL) were the least abundant. The abundant GH-related genes in Daqu microorganisms indicate a strong genetic potential for converting grain-derived polysaccharides into fermentable sugars. GF exhibited higher GH abundance, with elevated relative abundances of GH128, GH25, GH106, GH73, GH115, GH154, GH92, GH117, GH70, and GH170, suggesting greater carbohydrate degradation potential. In contrast, GS showed relatively higher GT abundance, indicating stronger genetic potential related to glycosyl transfer and polysaccharide biosynthesis.

Functional β-diversity PCoA analysis (Fig. 4d) confirmed significant differences in CAZy profiles between the two Daqu grades. Meanwhile, metagenome-encoded CAZy families showed distinct enrichment patterns (Fig. 4f and h): 32 CAZy families (e.g., CBM50, GT51, GH2, GH73, GH25, GH13, GH92, CE14, GH115, and GH24) were enriched in GF, whereas only a few families (CE8, CBM24, GH17, GT59, GT91, and GH13_22) were enriched in GS.

Flavor analysis

Sensory evaluation was conducted on GF and GS, with eight sensory indicators included: material aroma, sweet, sauce aroma, douchi aroma, floral or fruit, fragrant and crispy, fermentation aroma, and off-flavor. GF exhibited better performance than GS in multiple aroma indicators (Fig. 5). It gained higher scores in the attributes of material aroma, sweet and sauce aroma. Headspace solid-phase microextraction-gas chromatography–mass spectrometry (HS-SPME-GC–MS) was employed to identify volatile compounds, with 58 volatile flavor compounds detected (Supplementary Table 3). These included esters (12), alcohols (13), acids (1), aldehydes (10), ketones (6), pyrazines (3), and other categories (13). Among all volatile components, esters and alcohols were the most abundant classes, representing the predominant volatile components in Daqu. At the compound level, phenethyl alcohol, ethyl palmitate, ethyl linoleate, and tetramethylpyrazine were present at relatively high concentrations in both GF and GS, suggesting that they represented the main volatile components shared by the two aroma grades. In addition, benzaldehyde, trimethylpyrazine, ethyl nonanoate, pyrrole-2-carboxaldehyde, and 2-acetyl pyrrole were also detected at relatively high levels in one or both grades, contributing to the complex aroma matrix of high-temperature Daqu. These compounds together formed the basic volatile profile of high-temperature Daqu, whereas the subsequent multivariate and OAV analyses were used to further identify compounds associated with aroma-grade differentiation. To further identify the differences between GF and GS, a supervised OPLS-DA model was established. The model effectively distinguished the volatile organic compounds in the two types of Daqu (Fig. 7b), with R2X = 0.567, R2Y = 0.986, and Q2 = 0.911, all above 0.5, indicating high reliability and predictive accuracy (Fig. S6) (The OPLS-DA model was validated using a leave-one-out cross-validation (LOOCV) scheme). After 200 permutation tests, the regression line of Q2 intersected the vertical axis below zero, suggesting no obvious overfitting within the current dataset (Fig. 7c).

The VIP value reflects the relative importance of variables in classification. Variables with VIP > 1 are generally considered potential differential metabolites between groups (Li et al., 2024). According to the methods in the references (He et al., 2025), by screening the variables with VIP > 1 and p < 0.05, and combining with further significance test (fold change ≥1.5 or ≤ 0.67), 13 volatile organic compounds with significant differences were finally determined (Fig. 7d).

In order to further analyze the aroma differences of Daqu, OAV of the above compounds was calculated. OAV represents the ratio of compound content to threshold, which is often used to quantify the contribution of compounds to sample aroma. A compound with an OAV ≥ 1 is generally considered to have the potential to contribute to the overall aroma profile of the sample (Chen, Han, et al., 2024). It should also be noted that Daqu's complex solid-state matrix may affect compound release and sensory perception, meaning that actual aroma contributions may slightly differ from OAV-based estimations. As shown in Table 1, among the 13 differential volatile compounds, 11 showed OAVs greater than 1. In GS Daqu, linalool, dimethyl trisulfide, and guaiacol exhibited much higher OAVs than other compounds and served as the dominant characteristic aroma contributors; in addition, 2-undecanone was detected only in GS Daqu. In GF Daqu, 1-nonanal showed the highest OAV, and the OAVs of branched-chain and aliphatic aldehydes including 2-methylbutyraldehyde, isovaleraldehyde, isobutyraldehyde, 3-methyl-1-butanol, and hexanal were significantly higher than those in GS Daqu; 1-octanol was detected exclusively in GF Daqu. 2-methylbutyraldehyde, isovaleraldehyde, isobutyraldehyde, 3-methyl-1-butanol, hexanal, 3-octanone, and ethyl nonanoate were detected in both samples with higher OAVs in GF Daqu. Overall, GF and GS Daqu displayed distinct differences in the composition and aroma contribution intensity of volatile compounds. These results indicated that the 11 volatile organic compounds with high OAVs contributed to the overall aroma of Daqu, which might be the main source of the aroma in high-temperature Daqu and the key compounds closely associated with the differences in aroma grades among Daqu samples. Discussion

Daqu quality is closely associated with the flavor and quality of sauce-flavor Baijiu, with its aromatic profile serving as the core quality determinant (He et al., 2025). Here, we systematically compared two Daqu grades (GF: first-grade; GS: second grade, classified by aromatic profiles) in terms of physicochemical properties, microbial community structure, functional traits, and flavor profiles. By integrating key findings with relevant literature, we discussed potential aroma differentiation patterns and quality-related associations of sauce-flavor Daqu, laying a critical theoretical foundation for standardized production.

Physicochemical properties and enzyme activities: key factors associated with aroma formation

GF's significantly higher amino nitrogen content and total acidity underscore its superior material basis for flavor synthesis, as amino acids act as pivotal precursors for umami and aromatic substances in Baijiu (Huang et al., 2021). Specifically, under the high-temperature conditions of sauce-flavor Daqu manufacturing, these abundant free amino acids efficiently participate in the Maillard reaction with reducing sugars, generating nitrogen-containing heterocyclic compounds (e.g., pyrazines) that directly contribute to the characteristic roasted and Douchi-like aromas of sauce-flavor Baijiu (Yu, Zhang, et al., 2024). This advantage may be associated with a positive feedback loop: GF's elevated acid protease activity accelerates protein hydrolysis into amino acids, which not only supply microbial nitrogen sources (Zhu, Song, et al., 2023) but also serve as precursors for organic acid biosynthesis (Liu, Li, et al., 2024), contributing to sustained acid accumulation. As a key ecological filter, higher acidity is thought to be associated with the restriction of miscellaneous bacteria and the maintenance of the microecosystem (Ma et al., 2022), which is supported by Mantel tests linking fungal community structure to acidity.

In contrast, GS's significantly higher liquefying and cellulase activities reflect a metabolic bias toward macromolecule degradation, a trait attributed to its enrichment of hydrolytic specialist taxa (e.g., Aspergillus chevalieri, Lichtheimia corymbifera) that possess the genetic potential to secrete thermostable amylases and cellulases (De Andrade Silva et al., 2020; Zuo et al., 2020). However, this high hydrolytic activity does not translate to superior quality, as GF's balanced enzyme profile—characterized by moderate hydrolytic activity and lower saccharifying power—is theoretically considered to be more suitable for the multi-round fermentation requirements of sauce-flavor Baijiu (Tang et al., 2024), though dynamic brewing trials are required to fully validate this hypothesis. Low saccharifying power avoids excessive starch degradation in single fermentation, reserving substrates for gradual flavor accumulation, while GF's higher esterifying enzyme activity is beneficial for ester synthesis (Yang, Lu, et al., 2025), further enhancing aroma complexity.

Notably, pH, total acidity, amino nitrogen, and key enzyme activities—indices with significant grade differences—exhibit complex correlations with both microbial genera and flavor compounds. These correlations suggest their potential role in microbial community assembly and flavor formation, highlighting the synergistic associations of “process-microorganism-enzyme activity” in Daqu quality differentiation (Mendonça et al., 2023).

Microbial community structure: potential factors associated with functional and flavor divergence

At the phylum level, Firmicutes and Ascomycota dominate both grades, reflecting the conserved selective pressure of high-temperature fermentation (Chen, Peng, et al., 2025; Zhang et al., 2023). However, grade-specific functional differentiation emerges at the genus/species level: GF is enriched in potential functional taxa (e.g., Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula, Kroppenstedtia guangzhouensis, Desmospora sp. 8437) potentially associated with key roles in flavor precursor synthesis and secondary metabolism, while GS accumulates hydrolytic specialists (e.g., Paecilomyces variotii, Lichtheimia ramosa) (Xu et al., 2024; Zhu, Song, et al., 2023).

GF's higher microbial α-diversity and complex inter-species interaction networks (e.g., Rasamsonia emersonii–Kroppenstedtia co-occurrence) enhance microecosystem stability and functional complementarity (Liu et al., 2023). For instance, macromolecule degradation associated with Kroppenstedtia potentially provides metabolic substrates for secondary metabolite synthesis linked to S. rectivirgula-driven secondary metabolite synthesis (Tang, Li, Shan, et al., 2025), reinforcing GF's flavor advantages. In contrast, GS's overgrowth of Oceanobacillus may alter niche balance by competing for nitrogen sources (Zhu, Song, et al., 2023), which is closely linked to the accumulation of undesirable metabolites. These findings suggest that microbial community composition and interaction complexity—rather than mere taxonomic abundance—are key factors associated with Daqu quality, with functional taxa enrichment contributing to superior flavor outcomes.

Functional annotations: metabolic potential associated with quality differences

KEGG and CAZy functional analyses reveal metabolic specialization between grades. GF enrichment in Xenobiotics Biodegradation and Metabolism (KEGG) may indicate a greater genetic potential to transform diverse exogenous substances, which may be associated with the greater diversity of flavor-related metabolites observed in GF(Yang, Tie, et al., 2025), consistent with GF's higher total volatile content. This reflects a more robust metabolic potential for aroma synthesis.

CAZy profiles highlight grade-specific metabolic biases: GF's higher Glycoside Hydrolase (GH) and Carbohydrate-Binding Module (CBM) abundances, particularly CBM50, indicate a greater genetic potential for the hydrolysis of starch, cellulose, and N-acetylglucosamine-containing polysaccharides, which may be associated with reducing sugar availability and flavor precursor accumulation (Liu, Chen, et al., 2024; Yu et al., 2022). In contrast, GS's higher Glycosyl Transferase (GT) abundance suggests a tendency toward functional polysaccharide synthesis, potentially diverting substrates from direct flavor enhancement (Yu et al., 2022). These results suggest that metabolic pathway specialization—rather than overall metabolic intensity— is a key factor associated with grade differences, with GF's metabolic network showing greater potential for flavor precursor generation.

Flavor compounds: key indicators of quality gradation

Sensory evaluation results showed that GF possessed more desirable sensory quality and better matched the typical sensory attributes of authentic sauce-aroma Daqu. HS-SPME-GC–MS and electronic nose analyses further indicated grade-specific flavor signatures. In this study, esters and alcohols were the predominant volatile classes in both GF and GS, with ethyl palmitate, ethyl linoleate, phenethyl alcohol, and tetramethylpyrazine detected at relatively high levels, indicating that these compounds constituted the basic volatile background of high-temperature Daqu. Previous studies have also reported that Daqu aroma is closely associated with “Qu-aroma”-related compounds, floral/fruity compounds, and pyrazine-derived sauce-like or roasted aroma characteristics (Tang et al., 2024; Tang, Li, Li, et al., 2025; Yang et al., 2024). GF's higher total volatile content and balanced ester-alcohol-aldehyde ratio, coupled with enrichment in aldehydes (e.g., hexanal, isovaleraldehyde), are considered to contribute to its superior sensory quality (Wang et al., 2022; Yang et al., 2024). Among them, 1-hexanol, isovaleraldehyde, γ-nonanolactone and 4-vinylguaiacol have been identified as key aroma-active volatile organic compounds, which are suggested to contribute to the “Qu aroma” of high-quality Daqu (Yang et al., 2024).

In contrast, GS's enrichment in undesirable sulfur compounds (e.g., dimethyl trisulfide [DMTS]) —with OAVs exceeding sensory thresholds— is suggested to contribute to pickle-like and camphor-like off-odors (Tang et al., 2024; Wang et al., 2020). Although DMTS can impart roasted/meaty notes at low concentrations (Song et al., 2021), its excess in GS undermines flavor quality, a phenomenon that may be associated with Oceanobacillus enrichment. The identification of 11 core differential compounds (VIP > 1 and OAV > 1) further suggests aldehydes may serve as candidate indicators of aroma grade differences, providing potential markers for Daqu quality evaluation.

Microbial-metabolite association characterization

pH and moisture content act as key environmental factors associated with microbial community assembly (e.g., GF's well-ventilated storage favors aerobic functional taxa, while GS's humid conditions promote hydrolytic specialists) (De Andrade Silva et al., 2020; Xu et al., 2024).

This study identified a significant positive correlation between the genus Kroppenstedtia and branched-chain aldehydes, including isobutyraldehyde, isovaleraldehyde, and 2-methylbutyraldehyde. This association may be partly related to its reported proteolytic capacity and the Ehrlich pathway. Members of Kroppenstedtia are reported to possess strong extracellular protease activity, which potentially facilitates the hydrolysis of substrate proteins into free amino acids. Additionally, their metabolic potential may be associated with branched-chain amino acid accumulation (BCAAs, e.g., leucine, isoleucine), which serve as key flavor precursors (Guan et al., 2024). These BCAAs first undergo transamination to form α-keto acids, which are then rapidly decarboxylated by α-keto acid decarboxylase to generate the corresponding branched-chain aldehydes (Hazelwood et al., 2008; Ye et al., 2020).

Rasamsonia showed a significant positive correlation with 1-nonanal and hexanal, two classic volatile degradation products of abundant unsaturated fatty acids. Specifically, hexanal is primarily produced via enzymatic (LOX/HPL) or autoxidative cleavage of linoleic acid, while 1-nonanal is the major oxidative degradation product of oleic acid (Schwab et al., 2008; Song et al., 2022). A plausible mechanism underlying this correlation is that Rasamsonia may be involved in lipid metabolism through lipase- and lyase-related activities (Fan et al., 2020). It is highly likely that Rasamsonia utilizes lipoxygenase (LOX) and subsequent cleavage enzymes to channel acyl-CoA precursors into oxidative cleavage pathways, thereby potentially contributing to the generation of these aldehydic volatiles (Pennerman et al., 2022).

Linalool exhibited the strongest correlation with microorganisms. The enriched Firmicutes and filamentous fungi form a functional consortium with the genetic potential to secrete robust extracellular β-glucosidases (Sidar et al., 2024; Su et al., 2021). These enzymes have the capacity to cleave the β-1,4-glycosidic bonds of odorless linalool precursors, potentially releasing free, aroma-active linalool into the Daqu matrix and enhancing its floral aroma profile (Sarry & Gunata, 2004). Notably, linalool had a higher odor activity value (OAV) in GS, which may explain the stronger floral sensory properties of microbial GS.

Oceanobacillus was significantly positively correlated with dimethyl trisulfide. Previous studies have demonstrated a strong positive association between Oceanobacillus enrichment and the accumulation of volatile sulfur compounds in fermentation systems, as this genus encodes genes associated with the degradation of sulfur-containing amino acids (Chen, Zhu, et al., 2024). Therefore, the accumulation of sulfides in GS Daqu is strongly associated with the enrichment of specific taxa, which may be related to the putative demethiolation of precursor amino acids by these enriched taxa.

Besides the positive correlations discussed above, significant negative associations were also observed in the microbe-metabolite network. Bacillus exhibited a negative correlation with 3-methyl-1-butanol, 2-methylbutyraldehyde, isovaleraldehyde and isobutyraldehyde, all of which are typical branched-chain flavor metabolites generated via the Ehrlich pathway (Hazelwood et al., 2008). This negative correlation may arise from substrate preference partitioning: Bacillus tends to prioritize carbohydrate metabolism and rapid proliferation during the early fermentation stage, competing for ecological niches with amino acid-fermenting taxa (e.g., Kroppenstedtia) that are associated with the Ehrlich pathway, thereby potentially contributing to lower accumulation of branched-chain aldehydes and alcohols (Hazelwood et al., 2008; Zhu et al., 2026). Collectively, these negative correlations highlight the complex competition and interactions within the microbial community, which were collectively associated with the final volatile profile of Daqu.

Implications

This study suggests that sauce-flavor Daqu grade differences are closely associated with the synergistic interactions among physicochemical properties, microbiome assembly, and metabolic function. Key insights include: (1) functional taxa enrichment and interaction complexity are more critical than taxonomic abundance for quality; (2) metabolic pathway specialization (e.g., GH/CBM vs. GT) may be associated with flavor precursor generation; (3) environmental parameters (e.g., ventilation, humidity) are suggested to indirectly influence quality via microbial community shaping.

The distinctive flavor characteristics of Baijiu are predominantly attributed to the solid-state fermentation system employing Daqu as the primary starter (Chen, Ni, et al., 2025; Gao et al., 2025). As a pivotal saccharification and fermentation starter, Daqu functions not only as a vital precursor for flavor components but also contributes the indispensable microbial consortium and enzymatic system involved in the fermentation process (Chen, Ni, et al., 2025; Xu et al., 2026). The characteristic aldehydes of GF screened in this study are important components of the “Qu aroma” of sauce-flavor Baijiu; while the sulfur-containing off-flavor compounds enriched in GS will remain in the Baijiu fermentation process, leading to off-flavors in the finished wine and reducing the quality of Baijiu (Wang et al., 2020). Therefore, the accurate distinction of Daqu aroma grade is of great practical significance to ensure the quality of finished sauce-flavor Baijiu.

Conclusions and future directions

Multivariate analysis revealed differences in microbial community structure, physicochemical properties, enzyme activities, and flavor profiles between two sauce-flavor Daqu grades (GF: first-grade; GS: second-grade) under the conditions of this study.

GF exhibited superior comprehensive quality, with higher total acidity, amino nitrogen content, and a lower pH, coupled with enrichment of bacteria potentially related to flavor formation (e.g., Kroppenstedtia, Saccharopolyspora). Within the studied samples, these taxa were positively correlated with indicators of robust metabolic potential, accompanying the accumulation of volatile flavor compounds (esters, alcohols, aldehydes, ketones) and higher aroma complexity. In contrast, GS featured higher pH, elevated liquefying/cellulase activities, and dominance of hydrolytic fungi (e.g., Aspergillus, Lichtheimia), with a lower total volatile content and enrichment of sulfur-containing compounds (e.g., dimethyl trisulfide) associated with off-odors.

Volatile compounds with significant differences were screened based on VIP value, p-value, and fold change (FC) criteria. Combined with odor activity value (OAV) analysis, 11 differential compounds were tentatively recognized as candidate indicators for distinguishing grade-specific flavor characteristics in the analyzed samples.

This study investigated multi-dimensional associations among Daqu quality, microbial functional potentials, and metabolites under the specific processing conditions, indicating that grade differences are closely correlated with distinct variations in physicochemical properties, functional microbiome assembly, and metabolic profiles. The findings provide a preliminary foundation for understanding aroma differentiation patterns and offer valuable references for the quality optimization and process management of sauce-flavor Daqu.

However, certain limitations should be acknowledged. First, samples were obtained from only one batch at a single distillery; thus, the generalizability of the conclusions requires further validation using multi-batch, multi-region and multi-distillery datasets with larger sample sizes. Meanwhile, this study only evaluated the aroma activity potential of compounds through OAV calculation, without GC-O, aroma recombination and sensory verification experiments, and the actual sensory contribution of the screened key compounds needs to be further confirmed. In addition, although the OPLS-DA model showed a high Q2 value, this result was obtained from a relatively limited sample set and LOOCV-based validation; therefore, the model performance may be optimistic and specific to the present dataset. Independent validation using larger multi-batch datasets is still required before the model can be generalized.

To address these limitations and advance the field, future research should focus on: (1) Directionally isolating functional taxa (e.g., Kroppenstedtia eburnea, Saccharopolyspora rectivirgula) from GF to develop microbial starters, enhancing quality stability; (2) Optimizing fermentation and storage parameters to guide functional microbiome assembly and reduce off-flavors (e.g., DMTS); (3) Integrating metatranscriptomics and metabolomics to further explore the putative formation pathways of core aldehydes, and exploring off-odor masking/inhibition strategies (e.g., caffeine addition) (Zhu, Chen, et al., 2023); (4) Developing rapid Daqu quality detection based on electronic nose technology, leveraging its ability to distinguish aroma grades (Zhang, Ran, et al., 2025). (5) Investigating the dynamic microbial and metabolic interactions during Daqu fermentation, while carrying out the aforementioned large-scale verifications and rigorous sensory assessments. Collectively, these efforts will provide robust technical support for the standardized and directional production of high-quality sauce-flavor Daqu.
