FLT3L-secreting cDC1 in situ vaccination enhances antitumor immunity and synergizes with PD-1 blockade in murine non-small cell lung cancer
Authors: Jensen Abascal, Camelia Dumitras, Linh M Tran, William Crosson, Bitta Kahangi, Michael Oh, Austin Rennels, Raymond J Lim, Hong Jiang, Diana Reyimjan, Nalani J Coleman, Edgar Perez-Reyes, Samantha Chin, Kostyantyn Krysan, Steven M Dubinett, Bin Liu, Ramin Salehi-Rad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundNon-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) frequently evades immune surveillance through defective antigen presentation and a suppressive tumor microenvironment (TME), limiting the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB). Conventional type 1 dendritic cells (cDC1s) are essential for initiating antitumor CD8+ T-cell responses; however, their abundance and function are often diminished in NSCLC, contributing to poor outcomes and resistance to immunotherapy. We hypothesized that in situ vaccination (ISV) using gene-modified cDC1s engineered to secrete FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (FLT3L) would enhance cDC1 function within the TME, promote antitumor immunity, and improve responses to ICB.MethodsSyngeneic murine models of NSCLC (KrasG12D/P53-/-/Lkb1−/−; KrasG12D/P53-/-; and KrasG12D) with varying tumor mutational burden, along with the MC38 model, were used to assess the therapeutic efficacy of FLT3L-cDC1 ISV. Flow cytometry and multiplex immunofluorescence were used to evaluate immune mechanisms of response. To assess translational relevance, immune and tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) signatures were analyzed in The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) NSCLC datasets, with TLS signatures refined using a retrained xCell2 framework incorporating curated TLS and high endothelial venule (HEV) microdissection datasets.ResultsFLT3L-cDC1 ISV remodeled the TME across multiple NSCLC models, inducing T lymphocyte infiltration and expanding cytolytic CD8+ T cells. FLT3L-cDC1 ISV was associated with increased formation of immature TLS with primary follicle-like features within the TME. TCGA analyses revealed that FLT3L expression correlates with activated DC, T cell, and B cell signatures, as well as HEV-enriched TLS-associated programs. Combination with PD-1 blockade further enhanced the antitumor immunity of FLT3L-cDC1 ISV, resulting in robust local and systemic T-cell activation and the expansion of activated CCR7+PD-L1+ cDC1s and stem-like TCF1+PD-1+ CD8+ progenitors within the TME. In an LKB1-deficient NSCLC model, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV plus PD-1 blockade induced complete and durable regression in 85% of tumors, leading to long-lasting systemic tumor-specific immune memory, consistent with effective tumor vaccination.ConclusionsFLT3L-cDC1 ISV represents a rational cytokine-enhanced cellular immunotherapy designed to overcome immunosuppression and restore DC function within the TME, thereby promoting tumor-specific adaptive immune responses and enhancing responsiveness to ICB.

WHAT IS ALREADY KNOWN ON THIS TOPIC

Absence or dysfunction of the T cell compartment and immunosuppression within the tumor microenvironment (TME) are central mechanisms of resistance to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) in non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC). Prior studies have shown that in situ vaccination (ISV) with monocyte-derived dendritic cells (DCs) is feasible in patients with NSCLC; however, clinical efficacy has been limited, underscoring the need for strategies that enhance DC function and improve responsiveness to immunotherapy. Conventional type 1 dendritic cells (cDC1s) are specialized antigen-presenting cells critical for cross-presentation of tumor antigens and activation of cytotoxic CD8+ T cell responses, yet their abundance and function are frequently diminished in NSCLC. FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (FLT3L) is a key regulator of DC development and expansion, but clinically feasible approaches for sustained, localized delivery of cDC1s and FLT3L within the TME remain limited.

WHAT THIS STUDY ADDS

This strategy overcomes resistance to ICB in LKB1-deficient NSCLC, leading to durable tumor regression and systemic tumor-specific immune memory. Integration of human The Cancer Genome Atlas datasets further supports the association between FLT3L expression and antitumor immune programs, including activated DC, T cell, and high endothelial venule-enriched tertiary lymphoid structure signatures in NSCLC.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the leading cause of cancer death worldwide, with non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) accounting for approximately 85% of cases.1 2 Immune checkpoint inhibitors (ICIs) targeting programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) or programmed death-ligand (PD-L1) have transformed NSCLC therapy.3 4 Although ICIs induce durable responses in some patients, most fail to respond or develop resistance.5 Clinical benefit from PD-1/PD-L1 blockade correlates with high clonal tumor mutational burden (TMB), abundant CD8+ T cell infiltration, and interferon (IFN)-γ/C-X-C Motif Chemokine Ligand 9 (CXCL9) transcriptional signatures, highlighting the central role of effective antitumor T cell immunity.6–12 Nevertheless, even in the presence of immunogenic neoantigens, NSCLCs frequently evade immune surveillance through defective antigen presentation and a suppressive tumor microenvironment (TME).2 13–19

Dendritic cells (DCs) are essential mediators of antitumor immunity, bridging innate and adaptive responses through professional antigen presentation and cytokine production.20 In situ vaccination (ISV) using intratumoral DC injection is an antigen-agnostic approach that leverages the full repertoire of tumor antigens to elicit broad T cell-mediated immunity.21 22 We previously demonstrated that ISV with gene-modified monocyte-derived DCs secreting C-C motif chemokine ligand 21 (CCL21-DC) is feasible and safe in patients with advanced NSCLC.23–25 Despite immunologic evidence of tumor-specific T cell activation in a subset of patients, objective responses were not achieved with CCL21-DC monotherapy or in combination with pembrolizumab, highlighting the need for next-generation DC vaccines with enhanced efficacy.

Conventional type 1 DCs (cDC1s) are dependent on the transcription factors BATF3, IRF8 and ID2 and possess a superior capacity for tumor-antigen cross-presentation and CD8+ T cell activation compared with cDC2s and inflammatory monocyte-derived DCs.26 27 Tumor-residing cDC1s also produce CXCL9/10 to recruit effector T cells into the TME28 and amplify adaptive immunity through an IFN-γ/interleukin (IL)-12 feedback loop.20 29 The critical role of cDC1s in tumor immunology is further supported by studies showing that Batf3⁻/⁻ mice lacking cDC1s exhibit impaired antitumor responses and resistance to ICI therapy.30–32 In humans, higher cDC1 density within the TME correlates with improved prognosis and responsiveness to ICI.16 33 34

FMS-like tyrosine kinase 3 ligand (FLT3L) is a central regulator of DC differentiation and expansion via signaling through its receptor, FLT3R.35 In human cancers, high intratumoral FLT3L expression correlates with increased DC infiltration and improved responses to PD-1 blockade.34 Preclinical and early clinical studies combining intratumoral FLT3L with radiotherapy and TLR3/CD40 agonists have shown local cDC1 enrichment, enhanced CD8+ T cell activation, and tumor regression.36 37 However, repeated intratumoral administration is clinically impractical in lung cancer, and systemic FLT3L delivery only modestly expands tumor-resident DCs.37 38

In this study, we report an ISV strategy using FLT3L-secreting cDC1s (FLT3L-cDC1) designed to locally enhance DC function within the TME while minimizing the need for repeated cytokine administration. FLT3L-cDC1 ISV increases intratumoral FLT3L levels and promotes robust T lymphocyte infiltration and tertiary lymphoid structure (TLS) formation in murine NSCLC. Analysis of The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) NSCLC dataset revealed that FLT3L expression correlates with TLS-associated gene signatures enriched for high endothelial venule (HEV) programs in NSCLC. When combined with PD-1 blockade, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV induces durable tumor regression and systemic tumor-specific immune memory in a murine model of NSCLC, suggesting that this strategy may be a viable approach for ICI-refractory NSCLC.

Results

Flt3l gene-modified cDC1s (FLT3L-cDC1) secrete FLT3L and maintain antigen-presenting function

Murine CD103+ cDC1s were derived from bone marrow precursors cultured in FLT3L-supplemented DC differentiation media,39 yielding a predominantly CD103+Clec9A+ population (figure 1A). cDC1s were activated overnight with poly(I:C) on day 14 (D14) and transduced on D15 with a lentivirus encoding FLT3L (FLT3L-cDC1) or control lentivirus generated from the same backbone (green fluorescent protein (GFP)-cDC1). FLT3L-cDC1s secreted 9 ng/mL of FLT3L per 1×106 cells 24 hours after transduction, whereas FLT3L was undetectable in GFP-cDC1s supernatants (figure 1B). Transduction did not alter DC phenotype or activation induced by poly(I:C), as determined by CD80/CD86 expression (online supplemental figure S1A). FLT3L-cDC1s exhibited viability (figure 1C) and phagocytic activity (online supplemental figure S1B) comparable to untransduced or GFP transduced controls. FLT3L-cDC1s retained antigen-presenting capacity, effectively priming OVA-specific CD8+ T cells in vitro (figure 1D). Cytokine profiling revealed that FLT3L expression alone did not affect cytokine secretion, with poly(I:C) stimulation driving robust induction of IP-10 (CXCL10) and MIG (CXCL9), which are essential for sustained antitumor adaptive immune responses (online supplemental figure S1C).28

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV induces antitumor responses in murine models

The therapeutic efficacy of FLT3L-cDC1 ISV was evaluated across multiple syngeneic murine tumor models, including NSCLC models that recapitulate features of human disease, such as elevated TMB and common co-occurring driver mutations.40 Poly(I:C)-activated cDC1s transduced with either GFP or FLT3L were administered intratumorally (3×106 cells per injection) on D6, D8, and D10 following tumor implantation (figure 1E). In the LKR13-3M NSCLC model (KrasG12D; TMB=3.8/Mb),40 FLT3L-cDC1 ISV, but not GFP-cDC1, significantly inhibited tumor growth relative to vehicle control (figure 1F). Tumor FLT3L concentrations were significantly increased 48 hours after FLT3L-cDC1 ISV, confirming localized cytokine delivery (online supplemental figure S1D). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV also elicited antitumor effects in the 1969B-KP-3M (KrasG12D Tp53−/−; TMB=22/Mb) and 1940A-KPL-3M (KrasG12D Tp53−/− Lkb1−/−; TMB=7.2/Mb) NSCLC models40 (figure 1G,H), as well as in the MC38 colon adenocarcinoma model (figure 1I). In contrast, recombinant murine FLT3L (rFLT3L; 100 ng/injection, approximately 10-fold higher than the amount secreted by 1×106 FLT3L-cDC1s), administered intratumorally or intraperitoneally on the same schedule, failed to inhibit tumor growth (figure 1H). These data demonstrate that therapeutic efficacy requires both cDC1 delivery and localized FLT3L expression and support FLT3L-cDC1 as an effective ISV across multiple tumor models.

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV enhances cDC1 trafficking to the TdLN and promotes T cell infiltration into the TME

To assess whether FLT3L expression influences trafficking of vaccine cDC1s in vivo, CellTracker Red (CTR)-labeled cDC1s were injected intratumorally into 1969B-KP-3M tumor-bearing mice and tumors and tumor-draining lymph nodes (TdLN) were subsequently analyzed by flow cytometry (figure 2A, online supplemental figure S2A–C). While intratumoral retention of CTR+cDC1 vaccines was similar between groups, FLT3L-cDC1s exhibited significantly increased migration to the TdLN at 48 hours (figure 2B and online supplemental figure S3A), suggesting enhanced trafficking and potential for improved T cell priming. This effect was specific to autologous FLT3L-cDC1s, as endogenous cDCs remained unchanged in both the tumor and TdLN (online supplemental figure S3B,C). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV also increased neutrophils within the TME compared with control treatment, while frequencies of monocytes, inflammatory monocyte-derived cells (MDC), and tumor-associated macrophages remained unchanged (online supplemental figure S3D).

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV markedly augmented intratumoral T cell infiltration, inducing a >30-fold and >60-fold increase in CD8+ and conventional CD4+ T cells, respectively, compared with GFP-cDC1 and approximately 1,000-fold and 60-fold increases relative to vehicle controls (figure 2C). This expansion encompassed naïve, central memory (TCM), and effector memory (TEM) subsets, with the most pronounced increases in naïve and TCM compartments (figure 2D,E). Although FoxP3+CD4+ regulatory T cells (Treg) were also increased, the CD8+/Treg ratio was significantly elevated only in FLT3L-cDC1-treated tumors (online supplemental figure S3E). The increase in T cell tumor infiltration is unlikely to result from direct FLT3L signaling, as T cells lack FLT3 receptor expression, and recombinant FLT3L failed to promote T cell migration in transwell assays (online supplemental figure S3F). Similar results were observed in the LKR13-3M NSCLC model, where FLT3L-cDC1 ISV promoted robust expansion of CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, particularly within the naïve subset (online supplemental figure S4A,B).

FLT3L expression correlates with T cell and activated DC signatures in human NSCLC

Immune infiltration profiles inferred by xCell41 revealed strong associations between FLT3L expression and multiple immune cell populations in TCGA lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) and lung squamous cell carcinoma (LUSC) cohorts (figures 2F,G and online supplemental figure S5). FLT3L positively correlated with DCs, B cells, and T cells in both LUAD and LUSC, consistent with previous reports.42 43 The strongest correlations were observed with activated DCs, naïve CD4+ T cells, CD8+ TCM, and B cells (figures 2F,G and online supplemental figure S5). In contrast, FLT3L expression was inversely correlated with Th2 CD4+ T cells in both LUAD and LUSC. Given that both naïve T cells and activated DCs express CCR7 and CD62L, we evaluated whether FLT3L expression correlated with these markers. FLT3L correlated strongly (rho >0.45) with CCR7 and SELL (encoding CD62L) in LUAD and LUSC (figure 2H,I).

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV enhances cytolytic T cell infiltration in the TME

To assess the spatial distribution and functional state of intratumoral immune cells, multiplex immunofluorescence (MIF) was performed on tumors collected on D12 and D14 following FLT3L-cDC1 ISV (figure 3A), time points selected to permit evaluation of adaptive immune responses. Formalin-fixed paraffin-embedded tumor sections were stained with a T cell-focused MIF panel (PanCK (epithelial), DAPI (nuclear), CD4, CD8, Foxp3 (Treg), Ki67 (proliferation), and GzmB (effector function)), and T cell densities were quantified at the tumor border (±150 µm from the PanCK-defined edge) and tumor core (≥600 µm inward) (figure 3B).44 While FLT3L-cDC1 ISV did not generate sustained changes at the tumor border (online supplemental figure S6A,B), it induced robust CD8+ T cell infiltration into the tumor core, accompanied by marked increases in intratumoral granzyme B (GzmB)+ cytolytic and Ki67+ proliferating cytolytic CD8+ T cells (figure 3C–D). Conventional CD4+ T cells exhibited only transient increases within the tumor core, without corresponding changes in Tregs (online supplemental figure S7A–C). In contrast, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV promoted expansion of proliferating and cytolytic CD8+ T cells within the tumor core over time (figure 3E). These data show that FLT3L-cDC1 ISV drives sustained intratumoral infiltration and expansion of cytolytic CD8+ T cells, supporting its potential to enhance adaptive antitumor immunity.

FLT3L expression correlates with HEV-enriched TLS signatures in human NSCLC

The strong correlation between FLT3L expression and gene signatures associated with B cells, including memory B cells, class-switched B cells, and plasma cells, as well as T cells and activated cDCs (figures 2F,G and online supplemental figure S5) prompted us to investigate its potential association with TLS formation, which has been linked to improved overall survival in patients with NSCLC.33 45 46 Because immune signatures inferred from bulk TCGA data lack spatial resolution, composite TLS scores may be elevated even when immune cells are not colocalized and instead diffusely distributed. HEVs, which mediate the recruitment of T cells and CCR7+ activated DCs into secondary lymphoid structures, are essential for TLS development.47 To capture TLS-associated organization, we derived TLS signatures by jointly retraining transcriptomic profiles from laser capture microdissected HEVs within TLS regions compared with non-HEV vessels outside TLS in tumors,48 together with TME immune programs.49 FLT3L expression showed a strong positive association with the HEV-enriched TLS signature in both LUAD and LUSC (figure 4A,B). Within the HEV-enriched TLS subgroup in LUAD, higher HEV signature expression (top quartile) was associated with significantly improved progression-free survival (PFS) compared with the HEV-low group (bottom quartile) (figure 4C), consistent with prior studies reporting the favorable prognostic value of HEVs in patients with NSCLC.50

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV is associated with increased immature TLSs with primary follicle features within the TME

To assess whether FLT3L-cDC1 ISV induces TLS formation, D12 sections from the 1969B-KP-3M tumors (figure 3A) were analyzed using a 6-plex MIF panel (PanCK (epithelial), DAPI (nuclear), B220 (B cell), CD11c (cDC), CD3 (T cell), CD31 (endothelial)) to identify key TLS components (figure 4D).45 46 At this time point, only immature TLSs with primary follicle-like features were identified, with no mature secondary follicle-like TLSs observed (figure 4D). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV was associated with increased immature TLS formation, with TLSs detected in approximately 70% of analyzed sections (figure 4E). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV significantly increased both the number of immature TLSs and the total TLS area per section (figure 4F). These data suggest that intratumoral FLT3L may promote TLS formation.

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV synergizes with PD-1 blockade in murine NSCLC

To determine whether FLT3L-cDC1 ISV could improve response to PD-1 blockade, we evaluated the combination in the 1940A-KPL-3M NSCLC model, harboring Lkb1 inactivation known to drive an immunosuppressive TME and limit anti-PD-1 immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) response (figure 5A).17 51–53 FLT3L-cDC1 synergized with anti-PD-1 therapy, by Bliss independence analysis, resulting in significant tumor suppression and complete tumor regression in approximately 85% of mice (figure 5B).54 Although GFP-cDC1 also enhanced anti-PD-1 responses, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV yielded a substantially higher rate of complete responses (85% vs 40%) (figure 5B).

PD-1 blockade augments FLT3L-cDC1 ISV-induced cDC trafficking and expansion of PD-1+TCF1+CD8+ effector T cells in TdLNs

To investigate the mechanisms underlying the synergy between FLT3L-cDC1 ISV and PD-1 blockade, we profiled immune populations in KPL-3M tumors and TdLNs on D14, a time point selected to assess adaptive responses while minimizing confounding effects of tumor size (online supplemental figure S8A). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV increased cDC1 and cDC2 abundance in the TdLNs by approximately 3-fold and 2-fold, respectively, and these effects were further enhanced by PD-1 blockade (figure 5C). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV also enriched effector T cells in TdLNs (online supplemental figure S8B), increasing GzmB+CD8+ TEM cells and IFN-γ+CD8+ T cells by approximately 3-fold and 2-fold, respectively (figure 5D,E). PD-1 blockade further amplified this response, resulting in nearly a 9-fold increase in cytolytic GzmB+CD8+ TEM cells relative to controls (figure 5D). Notably, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV in combination with PD-1 blockade expanded the pool of stem-like PD-1+TCF1+CD8+ T cells, a population known to sustain durable responses to immunotherapy55 56 (figure 5F). These findings indicate that PD-1 blockade augments FLT3L-cDC1-mediated cDC trafficking and enriches progenitor-like CD8+ effector T cells in TdLNs, suggesting improved priming and sustained antitumor T cell activation.

PD-1 blockade amplifies FLT3L-cDC1-driven Th1 and cytolytic T cell activation in the TME

Within the TME, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV did not alter overall cDC frequencies; however, the addition of PD-1 blockade increased intratumoral cDC1 and cDC2 abundance, including activated PD-L1+CCR7+ cDC1s (figure 5G). FLT3L-cDC1 monotherapy selectively reduced neutrophils, whereas combination-treated tumors showed trends toward increased monocytes, macrophages, and MDCs (online supplemental figure S8C,D). FLT3L-cDC1 ISV induced an approximately 2-fold increase in effector CD4+ T cells, including IFN-γ+CD4+ T cells indicative of Th1 polarization, and PD-1 blockade further amplified this response to more than a 6-fold increase relative to controls (figure 5H). Combination therapy, but not FLT3L-cDC1 monotherapy, also robustly enriched effector CD8+ T cells, resulting in an elevated CD8+/Treg ratio (figure 5I and online supplemental figure S8E). At this later time point, naïve and TCM cells were no longer the dominant populations, with T cells largely exhibiting effector phenotypes (figure 5I and online supplemental figure S8F,G). Notably, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV combined with PD-1 blockade elicited >8-fold increases in stem-like PD-1+TCF1+ CD8+ T cells, cytolytic GzmB+ CD8+ TEM cells, and IFN-γ+CD8+ T cells, indicating potent effector activation (figure 5J–L). These findings demonstrate that PD-1 blockade sustains and amplifies FLT3L-cDC1-driven activation of cDC1s, Th1 cells, and cytolytic CD8+ T cells within the TME, consistent with known IFN-γ-dependent cDC1–T cell cross-talk that reinforces cDC1 survival and CD8+ T cell expansion.57

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV combined with PD-1 blockade enhances systemic effector T cells

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV and combination therapy with anti-PD-1 increased splenic effector CD8+ and CD4+ T cells, including GzmB+ CD8+ TEM cells, without a corresponding increase in Tregs, with the largest expansion observed in the combination group (figure 6A–C). Notably, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV monotherapy expanded splenic IFN-γ+ CD4+ and CD8+ T cells, suggesting activation of a broader pool of tumor-reactive lymphocytes (figure 6D,E). This response was markedly enhanced by PD-1 blockade (figure 6D,E), consistent with the synergistic antitumor effects observed in vivo.

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV combined with PD-1 blockade induces durable, systemic tumor-specific immune memory

To determine whether curative combination therapy induces long-term protective immunity, mice that achieved complete regression of KPL-3M tumors following FLT3L-cDC1 ISV plus anti-PD-1 treatment were rechallenged with KPL-3M cells 5 months after cure, alongside age-matched naïve controls. Bioluminescence imaging confirmed successful tumor engraftment in all mice (figure 6F). Previously cured mice rapidly rejected KPL-3M tumors, whereas naïve controls succumbed to tumors (figure 6F,G). This memory response was tumor-specific, as cured mice displayed no protection against the unrelated syngeneic MyC-CaP prostate tumor (figure 6H).

Discussion

In this study, we demonstrate that engineering cDC1s to secrete FLT3L markedly enhances their ISV antitumor efficacy in murine NSCLC. FLT3L-cDC1 ISV drives robust intratumoral cytolytic T cell infiltration and promotes TLS formation within the tumors. These findings align with our TCGA analyses showing that FLT3L expression strongly correlates with activated DCs, T cells, and B cells and HEV-enriched TLS gene signatures in human NSCLC. Importantly, FLT3L-cDC1 ISV overcomes the immunosuppressive microenvironment characteristic of ICB-resistant LKB1-deficient NSCLC, enabling complete tumor regression and durable, tumor-specific immune memory.

FLT3L-cDC1 ISV rapidly expanded intratumoral T cells within 48 hours, particularly in the naïve and TCM subsets. Although tumor-residing DCs have been shown to facilitate naïve T cell entry into tissues,58 the greatest expansion in our study was observed following FLT3L-cDC1 ISV, with only modest increases after administration of control cDC1s, suggesting that FLT3L plays a dominant role in driving this early response. Consistent with these observations, analysis of TCGA NSCLC datasets demonstrated strong correlations between FLT3L expression and naïve/TCM-associated gene signatures as well as CCR7 and CD62L (SELL) expression in both LUAD and LUSC. Because T cells neither express FLT3R nor migrate toward FLT3L in vitro, these effects are unlikely to reflect direct FLT3L-driven chemotaxis. Given the rapid kinetics at 48 hours, this increase is also unlikely to reflect de novo T cell priming. Instead, our findings suggest that FLT3L-cDC1 ISV rapidly remodels the tumor milieu, likely through DC-dependent mechanisms, as supported by the lack of efficacy of rFLT3L, to facilitate recruitment or retention of lymphocytes, including naïve and TCM subpopulations typically restricted to secondary lymphoid organs.

A key observation from this study is the association between FLT3L activity and TLS formation, which has been correlated with improved survival in NSCLC.33 47 FLT3L-cDC1 ISV, but not GFP-cDC1, was associated with increased formation of immature TLS exhibiting primary follicle-like features. This observation is supported by our TCGA analyses, which demonstrate that FLT3L expression strongly correlates with B cell, T cell, and activated cDC signatures, as well as refined TLS-associated gene programs enriched for HEV-associated pathways in both LUAD and LUSC. Recent work has shown that HEVs mediate the entry of stem-like TCF1+PD-1+ CD8+ T cells into tumors and predict enhanced ICB responsiveness,50 aligning with our observation that FLT3L-cDC1 ISV augments PD-1 blockade. FLT3R has also been linked to DC infiltration and TLS formation in hepatocellular carcinoma, supporting the broader relevance of this axis across solid tumors.43 However, our study was not designed to determine whether TLS formation is required for mediating responses to immunotherapy and therefore does not establish causality. Further delineation of pathways underlying the association between FLT3L-cDC1 ISV and immature TLS formation may identify opportunities to enhance TLS maturation and therapeutic efficacy.

The experimental framework used here differs fundamentally from previous investigations employing intratumoral cDC1 vaccination.59 60 Zhou et al pre-pulsed activated cDC1s with OVA protein or tumor lysate in B16-OVA melanoma and K7M3 osteosarcoma models,59 whereas our antigen-agnostic ISV approach uses poly(I:C)-activated cDC1s delivered intratumorally, relying on their in situ uptake of endogenous tumor antigens and eliminating the need for antigen pulsing. More recently, Ferris et al demonstrated that ISV with autologous cDC1s can drive the rejection of 1956 mOVA fibrosarcoma tumors by direct antigen presentation in Irf8+32–/– mice lacking endogenous cDC1s.60 In contrast, control cDC1 ISV in our murine studies failed to induce tumor control in most NSCLC models, reflecting the difficulty of generating effective antitumor immunity in antigenically heterogeneous tumors without exogenous antigen loading or additional immune amplification. These findings underscore the limitations of OVA-based, highly immunogenic systems for translational studies and highlight the importance of evaluating DC-based vaccines in models that better reflect the complexity of human disease.40

Our study builds on emerging evidence supporting the utility of FLT3L in enhancing antitumor immunity. Preclinical studies have shown that daily intratumoral FLT3L administration combined with radiotherapy and dual TLR3/CD40 stimulation activates tumor-specific T cells and overcomes ICB resistance.36 Systemic delivery of FLT3L-based cellular platforms, such as FLT3L/IL-12–engineered DC precursors and FLT3L-secreting CAR T cells, have likewise improved immunotherapy responses by boosting cDC1-dependent and IFN-γ-dependent T cell activation and promoting epitope spreading.61–63 A recent clinical trial further demonstrated that nine consecutive intratumoral doses of FLT3L with the TLR3 ligand poly(I:C) and radiotherapy in patients with non-Hodgkin’s lymphoma expanded endogenous cDCs and increased CD8+ T cell responses, resulting in tumor regression in some patients.37 However, daily intratumoral dosing is not feasible in patients with lung cancer, and systemic FLT3L, while expanding peripheral cDCs, elicits limited cDC accumulation in solid tumors likely due to known barriers that restrict immune infiltration into the TME.37 38 Our approach addresses these limitations: gene-modification of cDC1s enables localized delivery of FLT3L within the tumor, reducing the need for repeated cytokine administration while simultaneously augmenting cDC1 function. Importantly, we have previously demonstrated the clinical feasibility and safety of DC-based ISV in patients with NSCLC, supporting the translational relevance of this strategy.23

Despite the diversity of NSCLC murine models used, spanning multiple driver mutations, TME profiles, TMB levels, and degrees of clonal heterogeneity, our study has several limitations. First, syngeneic subcutaneous models do not fully recapitulate human tumorigenesis or the dynamic processes of immunosurveillance; however, orthotopic lung tumor models are not compatible with repeated intratumoral ISV delivery. Second, in vitro generated cDC1s may not fully recapitulate the phenotype and function of endogenous cDC1s. Finally, our analyses of human TCGA and related datasets are correlative and require validation at the protein and spatial levels in human tumors. In addition, while the multigene, HEV-enriched TLS-associated signature aims to capture coordinated immune organization, it does not resolve all mature TLS states.

In summary, we show that gene modification of cDC1s enables feasible and localized delivery of FLT3L to the TME, enhancing their antitumor function. FLT3L-cDC1 ISV triggers robust adaptive immune responses, is associated with TLS formation and overcomes immunotherapy resistance in murine NSCLC.

Methods

Generation of GFP/FLT3L-cDC1

CD103+ cDC1s were generated using a previously described protocol with minor modifications.39 Bone marrow cells were cultured in cDC1 medium (Roswell Park Memorial Institute (RPMI-1640)+10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin+50 µM 2-mercaptoethanol (Gibco)+300 ng/mL murine FLT3L (PeproTech)+5 ng/mL murine Granulocyte-Macrophage Colony-Stimulating Factor(GM-CSF; PeproTech)). A total of 1.5×107 cells were seeded into 10 cm non-tissue culture-treated Petri dishes (Bioland) in 10 mL of medium and maintained at 37°C in 5% CO₂. On D5, 5 mL of fresh cDC1 medium was added. On D9, loosely adherent cells were harvested by pipetting and reseeded at 3.0×105 cells/mL into fresh 10 cm dishes in 10 mL cDC1 medium. On D12, cultures were supplemented with 3 µg of murine FLT3L and 50 ng of murine GM-CSF. On D14, 500 µg poly(I:C) HMW (InvivoGen) was added. After 16 hours (D15), loosely adherent cDC1s were collected. For transduction, cDC1s were resuspended in 1.0×106 cells/mL of lentivirus in non-tissue culture-treated 6-well plates (Falcon) and spinfected at 800 g for 2 hours at 32°C with the vector control lentivirus (pHIV-eGFP; Addgene #21373) or a lentivirus encoding murine FLT3L (pHIV-FLT3L). The pHIV-FLT3L construct was generated by cloning the murine FLT3L coding sequence into pHIV-eGFP using XbaI and BamHI (NEB) restriction enzymes and was validated by sequencing. For in vivo experiments, transduced cDC1s were pelleted at 1,500 rpm for 5 min, washed with phosphate-buffered saline (PBS), and injected intratumorally in 50 µL of PBS with calcium and magnesium.

TexasRed-Dextran uptake assay

The TexasRed-Dextran assay was performed as previously described for FITC-Dextran uptake.64 Briefly, TexasRed-Dextran powder was resuspended at 25 mg/mL in Milli-Q water and diluted 1:20 into temperature-conditioned Fluorescence-Activated Cell Sorting (FACS) tubes containing 5.0×105 control or transduced cDC1s in 200 µL of cDC1 media. Tubes were incubated at either 4°C or 37°C for 15 min. Following incubation, cells were pelleted at 1,500 rpm for 5 min at room temperature, washed with PBS supplemented with 2% FBS, and analyzed by flow cytometry using a NovoCyte Quanteon.

cDC1 survival in vitro

Transduced or control cDC1s were plated at 1.0×10⁶ cells/mL in 0.25 mL of cDC1 media per well in 24-well plates (Nest Biotech). From D0 to D6, cells were harvested daily from three wells per condition and stained with trypan blue for viability assessment. Average viable cell counts per well were calculated and plotted.

cDC1 and OT-1 T cell co-culture assay

Control or transduced cDC1s derived from C57BL/6J mice were incubated with or without 20 µg/mL OVA-1 SIINFEKL peptide (InvivoGen) in cDC1 media for 60 min at 37°C. Cells were pelleted at 1,500 rpm for 5 min at room temperature and washed with RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin. CD8+ T cells were isolated from the spleens of OT-I transgenic mice (JAX strain C57BL/6-Tg(TcraTcrb)1100Mjb/J) using the MojoSort Mouse CD8 T Cell Isolation Kit (BioLegend). Isolated OT-I CD8+ T cells were washed with warm PBS+2% FBS, resuspended at 1.0×106 cells/mL, and labeled with 1 µM CellTrace Violet proliferation dye (Thermo Fisher) for 20 min at 37°C according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Labeled OT-I CD8+ T cells (1.0×105) were co-cultured with cDC1s (5.0×105) in U-bottom, tissue culture-treated 96-well plates (Corning) in 200 µL RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin. Proliferation of OT-I CD8+ T cells was assessed after 72 hours by flow cytometry using a NovoCyte Quanteon.

Transwell migration assay

Naïve murine splenocytes were isolated and cultured at 5.0×106 cells/mL in RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin in the presence of 5 µg/mL anti-CD3 (Bio X Cell) and 2 µg/mL anti-CD28 (Bio X Cell) for 48 hours. Cells were collected, washed, and resuspended at 2.5×105 in 100 µL RPMI-1640 supplemented with 2% FBS and 1% penicillin-streptomycin, then loaded into the 3 µm upper insert of a 24-well transwell tissue culture-treated plate (Costar) in triplicate per condition. The lower chamber contained 400 µL RPMI-1640 supplemented with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin only (untreated), or with the addition of recombinant CXCL9 (100 ng/mL; PeproTech) or FLT3L (1 µg/mL; PeproTech). Plates were incubated for 3 hours at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere with 5% CO₂. Following incubation, cells from the lower chamber were collected, and the underside of the insert was washed to recover adherent migrated cells. Cells were pelleted at 3,000 rpm for 5 min, resuspended in 100 µL RPMI-1640 with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin, and plated in duplicate (50 µL/well) into white flat-bottom 96-well plates (Greiner). 50 µL/well of CellTiter-Glo reagent (Promega) was added, and viable cell abundance was measured according to the manufacturer’s protocol. Mean viable cell counts were calculated per condition and fold change relative to control cell count was graphed.

Cell lines

The murine LKR13-K-3M, 1969B-KP-3M, and 1940A-KPL-3M NSCLC cell lines were previously described.40 MyC-CaP cells were obtained from the American Type Culture Collection, and the MC38 colon adenocarcinoma line was provided by Dr. Antoni Ribas (UCLA). All cell lines were cultured in RPMI-1640 (K-3M, KP-3M, and KPL-3M) (Corning) or DMEM (MyC-CaP and MC38) (Gibco), supplemented with 10% FBS+1% penicillin-streptomycin, at 37°C in a humidified atmosphere containing 5% CO₂. Cell lines were tested annually for authentication (DNA IQ and PowerPlex 1.2 systems, Promega) and routinely confirmed to be free of Mycoplasma contamination (MycoAlert, Lonza).

In vivo studies

FVB/NCrl and 129-E mice were obtained from Charles River Laboratories, and C57BL/6J mice were purchased from Jackson Laboratories. All mice were housed in pathogen-free facilities and all animal experiments were approved by the Animal Research Committee at UCLA. Tumor cells (100 µL in PBS containing calcium and magnesium (Corning)) were implanted subcutaneously into 7–9 weeks old mice at doses indicated in figure legends. cDC1s were derived from the same genetic background as the tumor-bearing mice. Tumor length and width were measured with calipers every 2–3 days, and tumor volume was calculated using the formula: 0.4×length×width². Tumor weights were obtained at the time of euthanasia. For efficacy and immunology studies, mice bearing ~50 mm³ tumors were randomized and treated with intratumoral PBS (with calcium and magnesium) vehicle control, GFP-cDC1s (3.0×106 cells), FLT3L-cDC1s (3.0×106 cells), intraperitoneal anti-PD-1 antibody (100 µg, Bio X Cell, clone RMP1-14), intraperitoneal or intratumoral recombinant murine FLT3L (100 ng, PeproTech), or combinations as indicated in figure legends. To ensure comparable baseline tumor sizes, outlier tumors identified by Grubbs’ test were excluded prior to randomization. Mice were euthanized when tumors reached 1,500 mm³ in tumor rechallenge studies. For in vivo bioluminescence imaging, mice were injected intraperitoneally with 150 mg/kg D-luciferin (GoldBio) and imaged after 10 min using the IVIS Spectrum system. For trafficking studies, poly(I:C)-activated, transduced cDC1s were labeled with CTR CMTPX (2.5 µM; Invitrogen) in PBS supplemented with 2% FBS for 20 min at 37°C before intratumoral injection. To minimize potential confounders, mice from different treatment groups were randomly distributed across cages. Investigators were blinded to group allocation during treatment administration and tumor measurements. Outcome assessment and data analysis were not formally blinded.

ELISA and Luminex

Supernatants from cDC1 cultures were collected from wells containing 1.0×106 cells rested for 24 hours post-transduction. ELISA assays (R&D Systems) were performed according to the manufacturer’s instructions, and absorbance was measured using a Bio-Rad plate reader. Luminex assays (Millipore) were conducted following kit protocols (Milliplex Mouse Cytokine/Chemokine Panel MCYTMAG70PMX32BK) and analyzed on an LX200 system (Luminex). Cytokine values are reported as log₁₀ (1 + (Median Fluorescence Intensity (MFI) − background)).

Phenotyping by flow cytometry

Tissues were processed into single-cell suspensions as previously described.64 Surface staining was performed for 20 min at 4°C in PBS supplemented with 2% FBS. Intracellular staining (ICS) for Ki67, GzmB, and FoxP3 were carried out using the FOXP3 Transcription Factor Staining Kit (eBioscience) according to the manufacturer’s instructions. For cytokine production analysis, single-cell suspensions were stimulated for 4 hours at 37°C using the Cell Stimulation Cocktail (eBioscience) and ICS was performed using the intracellular fixation and permeabilization kit (eBioscience). Data were acquired on a NovoCyte Quanteon Flow Cytometer (Agilent) and analyzed with NovoExpress (Agilent) and FlowJo (TreeStar) software. The list of the flow antibodies is provided in online supplemental table S1.

Multiplex immunofluorescence

MIF was performed using the same protocol as previously described.44 For spatial analysis, the tumor border was defined as the region extending 150 µm outward and inward from the PanCK+ tumor edge, and the tumor core as a region ≥600 µm inward from the edge (figure 3B). Multiple core regions (n=2–4 per tumor) were averaged to represent cellular density within the larger core space. For each region, average cell density per mm² of tissue was quantified to avoid confounding effects from areas overlapping with glass rather than tissue. For TLS analysis, distinct TLS structures both within the tumor and proximal to the tumor border were defined as: structures showcasing distinct cellular density and organization from adjacent tissue that must contain CD3+ cells, B220+ cells, CD11c+ cells, and CD31+ vascularization. TLS counts were quantified and the combined tissue area (μm2) of all TLSs per section was summed and quantified. The list of MIF antibodies is provided in online supplemental table S1.

TCGA transcriptomic analysis

Immune profiles were derived from RNA sequencing (RNA-seq) data from TCGA LUAD (n=510) and LUSC (n=498) databases. RNA-seq expression matrices were downloaded from UCSC Xena. Cell type enrichment analysis was performed using the xCell.41 Correlations between FLT3L expression and immune signatures were assessed using Spearman correlation coefficients (R basic package).

TLS gene signature derivation

TCGA RNA-seq expression matrices were obtained from UCSC Xena, while curated immune cell and TLS signature genes were sourced from TME Compendium dataset (https://science.bostongene.com/kassandra/)49 and GEO (GSE196703),48 respectively. TLS and immune cell gene signatures were jointly retrained using the xCell2 framework,65 incorporating the TME Compendium dataset49 and transcriptomic profiles from a prior study in which gene expression was derived from laser capture microdissected HEVs in TLS regions compared with non-HEV vessels outside TLS in tumors,48 thereby generating TLS-associated signatures from histologically annotated tumor regions. This joint retraining was performed to minimize cross-talk between immune subsets and TLS-associated programs. To validate the performance of the retrained signatures in an external cohort, immune cell profiles in TCGA were estimated using xCell2 with both the original and retrained signatures, demonstrating strong concordance for overlapping cell types (Spearman ρ>0.8) in both LUAD and LUSC cohorts. TLS-associated signatures were subsequently evaluated for associations with PFS. Retrained and original reference gene sets are detailed in online supplemental table S2 and S3.

Statistical analysis and reproducibility

Experiments were performed at least two times unless otherwise specified, and data from a representative experiment are shown. Statistical analyses were conducted using GraphPad Prism (V.10), with a significance threshold of p<0.05. Data are presented as mean±SEM unless noted otherwise. Group comparisons were made using two-tailed unpaired t-tests or one-way ANOVA with Bonferroni correction for multiple comparisons. Survival analyses were evaluated using the log-rank (Mantel-Cox) test.

Bliss independence model synergy calculation

Tumor growth inhibition (TGI) was calculated using the following formula: TGI = (1 − (average mass treated / average mass control)). The expected effect of the combination therapy, Eexp, was calculated assuming drug independence using the Bliss independence model: Eexp = TGIanti-PD-1 + TGIFLT3L-cDC1 – (TGIanti-PD-1 x TGIFLT3L-cDC1).54 The observed effect of the combination therapy, Eobs, calculated as the TGIcombo was found to be greater than the Eexp (0.9818>0.7906) indicating synergistic efficacy.
