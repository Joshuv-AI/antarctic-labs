Upper Tail Large Deviations for Extremal Eigenvalues of the Real, Complex and Symplectic Elliptic Ginibre Matrices
Sung-Soo Byun, Yong-Woo Lee, Seungjoon Oh

Abstract
. We consider the elliptic Ginibre ensembles in the real, complex and symplectic symmetry classes. As the matrix size tends to infinity, we derive the asymptotic behaviour of the upper tail large deviation probabilities for both the spectral radius and the rightmost eigenvalue. More generally, we obtain asymptotic formulas for the probability that an eigenvalue is found in a prescribed region outside the support of the elliptic law, thereby providing a unified framework in which the results for the spectral radius and the rightmost eigenvalue appear as special cases. The key ingredient of our analysis is the precise asymptotic behaviour of the associated one-point functions, which is of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

Exact analytic results have played a central role in the development of random matrix theory. For the classical ensembles, particularly those with Gaussian entries, the presence of rich algebraic and integrable structures permits explicit finite-dimensional formulas for a broad class of observables, including correlation functions and various deviation probabilities; see
[
59
]
for an account of this aspect of the literature up until around 2010. Over the past decades, these structures have given rise to striking exact identities, from determinantal and Pfaffian formulas to characterisations in terms of Painlevé equations. Remarkably, new exact formulas and structural features continue to appear, revealing additional layers of integrability even in models long regarded as classical; see e.g.
[
62
,
63
]
and references therein.

A notable example in which such exact identities play a decisive role is the study of extremal eigenvalue statistics.
In the classical Gaussian invariant ensembles, namely the Gaussian orthogonal, unitary, and symplectic ensembles (GOE, GUE, and GSE)
[
59
, Chapter 1]
, the joint probability density function of the eigenvalues
\{x_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
on the real line is proportional to

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|x_{j}-x_{k}|^{\beta}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-\frac{\beta N}{4}x_{j}^{2}}\,dx_{j},

(1.1)

where the Dyson index takes the values
\beta=1,2,4
corresponding to GOE, GUE, and GSE, respectively. It is by now classical that the empirical spectral measure associated with (
1.1
) converges to Wigner’s semicircle law

\frac{\sqrt{4-x^{2}}}{2\pi}\mathbbm{1}_{[-2,2]}(x)\,dx,

(1.2)

as
N\to\infty.
At the macroscopic scale, it is well known that the largest and smallest eigenvalues converge almost surely to the right and left endpoints of the semicircle law, respectively.
At the level of fluctuations, the typical edge behaviour is governed by the Tracy-Widom distributions, which have become a cornerstone of modern probability theory and of universality phenomena in random matrix theory.

A natural next step is to investigate atypical events, the large deviation behaviour of the extremal eigenvalues.
In this context, a fundamental question concerns the large-
N
asymptotics of the probability

\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}x_{j}\geq s\Big]\qquad\textup{for }s>2,

(1.3)

where the restriction
s>2
reflects the fact that
2
is the right edge of the semicircle law (
1.2
).
As expected for the upper tail event, this probability decays exponentially fast as
N\to\infty
.
It was shown in
[
82
]
(see also
[
16
, Theorem 6.2]
for the case
\beta=1
) that for
s>2
,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}x_{j}\geq s\Big]=-\Phi_{1}(s),

(1.4)

where the rate function is given by

Beyond the Hermitian setting, the fundamental models in non-Hermitian random matrix theory are the Ginibre ensembles in the real, complex, and symplectic symmetry classes, denoted by GinOE, GinUE, and GinSE, respectively.
These ensembles consist of random matrices with independent and identically distributed Gaussian entries, real in the GinOE case, complex in the GinUE case, and quaternionic in the GinSE case; see
[
28
]
for a recent review.
Unlike the Hermitian case, where the joint probability densities of the GOE, GUE, and GSE admit the unified
\beta
-ensemble form (
1.1
), no such unified structure exists for the Ginibre ensembles.
For instance, the joint probability density of the complex eigenvalues
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
of the GinUE is proportional to

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}e^{-N|z_{j}|^{2}}d^{2}z_{j},

(1.6)

whereas its GinSE counterpart is proportional to

\prod_{1\leq j<k\leq N}|z_{j}-z_{k}|^{2}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{k}|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{N}|z_{j}-\bar{z}_{j}|^{2}e^{-2N|z_{j}|^{2}}d^{2}z_{j}.

(1.7)

The situation for the GinOE is more involved.
In this case, the eigenvalue distribution is no longer absolutely continuous
with respect to the two-dimensional Lebesgue measure, due to the non-trivial probability of having purely real eigenvalues; see Figure
1
(A). Nevertheless, upon conditioning on the number of real and complex eigenvalues, the corresponding joint density admits an explicit representation; see
[
28
, Section 7.1]
.

Despite the distinct integrable structures appearing
in their joint eigenvalue densities, all three Ginibre ensembles share the same limiting empirical distribution, namely the circular law

\frac{1}{\pi}\mathbbm{1}_{\mathbb{D}}(z)\,d^{2}z,\qquad\mathbb{D}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\leq 1\}.

(1.8)

As before, it is natural to investigate the statistics of extremal eigenvalues in this non-Hermitian setting.
Among the most fundamental observables are the
spectral radius
and the
rightmost eigenvalue
given by

\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}|z_{j}|,\qquad\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}\operatorname{Re}z_{j},

(1.9)

respectively.
By definition, the spectral radius quantifies radial deviations from the circular law and determines whether eigenvalues exit the limiting support.
In contrast, the rightmost eigenvalue plays a crucial role in continuous-time dynamical systems, as stability is determined by the sign of its real part.
A paradigmatic example is May’s work
[
83
]
on ecosystem stability, where large ecological networks are modelled by random interaction matrices.
In this setting, stability of the equilibrium requires that all eigenvalues lie in the left half-plane.
Therefore, estimating the probability of atypical fluctuations that push eigenvalues beyond this stability boundary is of fundamental importance, both in random matrix theory and in applications to complex systems; see also
[
17
,
71
]
.

For the Ginibre ensembles, as a non-Hermitian counterpart of
(
1.3
), one asks for the asymptotic behaviour of the probabilities

\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}|z_{j}|\geq s\Big],\qquad\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}\operatorname{Re}z_{j}\geq s\Big]\qquad\textup{for }s>1,

(1.10)

where the restriction
s>1

(

For the GinUE, the asymptotic behaviour of the spectral radius was obtained in

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}|z_{j}|\geq s\Big]=-\Phi_{0}(s),

(1.11)

where
\beta=2
and

\Phi_{0}(s):=\frac{1}{2}(s^{2}-2\log s-1).

(1.12)

As before, we introduce the subscript
0
for later convenience.
A key advantage of considering the GinUE and the spectral radius is that the
problem preserves radial symmetry. As a consequence, the determinantal
structure allows one to express this probability in terms of a single
summation involving the incomplete Gamma function, for which a detailed
asymptotic analysis can be carried out; see

In contrast, for the GinOE and GinSE, as well as for the rightmost eigenvalue, the radial symmetry is broken so that one needs an alternative approach.
For the GinOE and GinUE, it was shown in the recent work of Xu and Zeng

[

that

\displaystyle\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}|z_{j}|\geq s\Big]=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}\operatorname{Re}z_{j}\geq s\Big]=-\Phi_{0}(s),

(1.13)

where
\beta=1
and

In contrast to the extensive body of work on large deviation probabilities
for Hermitian random matrix ensembles discussed above,
the corresponding theory in the non-Hermitian setting remains comparatively underdeveloped, despite the growing interest in non-Hermitian random matrix theory in recent years.
In particular, the study of upper tail large deviation probabilities for general non-Hermitian ensembles extending the Ginibre models still remains largely open.
Indeed, even for the Ginibre ensembles themselves, the corresponding result is still unavailable in the symplectic case.

In this work, we contribute to this direction by employing a unified framework of exact finite-
N
analysis to derive upper tail large deviation probabilities for the elliptic Ginibre ensembles in all three symmetry classes.
Before presenting our results in detail in the next section, we summarise our main findings as follows.

•

For the elliptic Ginibre matrices in all three symmetry classes, we derive in Theorem
2.1
the upper tail large deviation probabilities for both the spectral radius and the rightmost eigenvalue.
Our results interpolate between the Hermitian and the non-Hermitian results, (
1.4
) and (
1.13
).

•

In Theorem
2.2
, which extends Theorem
2.1
, we establish the large deviation probabilities for the event that an eigenvalue is located in a prescribed domain outside the typical limiting spectrum.

•

As an intermediate step towards the proof of Theorem
2.2
, in Propositions
4.2
,

4.3
, and
4.4
, we establish the precise asymptotic behaviour of the one-point functions of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles outside the limiting spectrum, which are of independent interest.

2.
Main results

We begin by introducing our model, the elliptic Ginibre ensembles. Let
\mathcal{G}
denote the Ginibre ensemble, the
N\times N
random matrix with independent centred Gaussian entries of variance
1/N
. We consider the real, complex, and quaternionic cases, corresponding to the GinOE, GinUE, and GinSE, respectively. In the quaternionic case, we adopt the standard
2\times 2
complex matrix representation; see
[
28
]
for further details.

By construction, the elliptic Ginibre ensembles interpolate between the
Ginibre ensembles and their Hermitian counterparts.
They are defined as a convex linear combination of the Hermitian and
anti-Hermitian parts of a Ginibre matrix: for a given non-Hermiticity parameter
\tau\in[0,1)
,

\mathcal{X}_{\tau}:=\frac{\sqrt{1+\tau}}{2}(\mathcal{G}+\mathcal{G}^{\dagger})+\frac{\sqrt{1-\tau}}{2}(\mathcal{G}-\mathcal{G}^{\dagger}).

(2.1)

Depending on the underlying symmetry class of the Ginibre matrix,
we denote the corresponding ensembles by eGinOE, eGinUE, and eGinSE.
We refer to
[
28
, Sections 2.3, 7.9 and 10.5]
for comprehensive reviews on these ensembles. Note that, by definition, the extremal cases
\tau=0
and
\tau\to 1
recover the Ginibre ensembles and the corresponding Gaussian invariant ensembles, respectively.

The joint probability density functions of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles take forms analogous to that of their Ginibre counterparts. For instance, in the cases of the eGinUE and eGinSE, the joint densities are given by (
1.6
) and (
1.7
), respectively, with the Gaussian term
|z|^{2}
replaced by the corresponding external potential:

V(z):=\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big(|z|^{2}-\tau\operatorname{Re}z^{2}\Big).

(2.2)

Note that when
\tau=0
, the potential
V
reduces to the quadratic potential
|z|^{2}
corresponding to the Ginibre ensembles. On the other hand, as
\tau\to 1
, it formally recovers the Gaussian potential in (
1.1
), since

\lim_{\tau\to 1}V(x+iy)=\begin{cases}x^{2}&\textup{if }y=0,\\
+\infty&\textup{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(2.3)

Here, the value
+\infty
reflects the fact that, in this limit, the probability of observing eigenvalues off the real axis vanishes, which is consistent with the matrix model becoming Hermitian.

Turning to the averaged macroscopic behaviour of the eigenvalues
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
, as
N\to\infty
, the empirical spectral distribution converges weakly to

\frac{1}{\pi(1-\tau^{2})}\,\mathbbm{1}_{E}(z)\,d^{2}z,\qquad E:=\Big\{(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}:\Big(\frac{x}{1+\tau}\Big)^{2}+\Big(\frac{y}{1-\tau}\Big)^{2}\leq 1\Big\},

(2.4)

which is known as the elliptic law; see Figure
1
.

Theorem 2.1

(
Upper tail large deviation probabilities for spectral radius and rightmost eigenvalue
)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In the eGinOE case, we further assume that
N
is even. Suppose that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}|z_{j}|\geq s\Big]=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\max_{j=1,\ldots,N}\operatorname{Re}z_{j}\geq s\Big]=-\Phi_{\tau}(s),

(2.7)

where

\Phi_{\tau}(s):=\frac{1}{2}\bigg(\frac{1}{1+\tau}-\frac{1}{2\tau}\bigg)s^{2}+\frac{s\sqrt{s^{2}-4\tau}}{4\tau}-\log\bigg(\frac{s+\sqrt{s^{2}-4\tau}}{2}\bigg).

(2.8)

Here,
\beta
is given by (
2.6
).

One can observe that the rate function (
2.8
) reduces to (
1.5
) and (
1.12
) when
\tau=0
and
\tau=1
, respectively.
Consequently, for
\beta=1,2
, Theorem
2.1
interpolates between the results of
[
82
]
(
\tau=1
) and
[
92
]
(
\tau=0
).
On the other hand, we note that for
\beta=4
, our result is new even in the GinSE case (
\tau=0

It is straightforward to verify that
\Phi_{\tau}(s)\geq 0
for

Moreover, the function

In addition, note that for

This behaviour is in contrast with the case

.

This evaluation follows from (

Theorem 2.2

(

)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed, and let
N\in\mathbb{N}
. In the eGinOE case, we further assume that

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{\beta N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\exists\,1\leq j\leq N\ \textup{such that }z_{j}\in U\Big]=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U}\Omega(z),

\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{P}\Big[\exists\,1\leq j\leq N\ \textup{such that }z_{j}\in U\Big]=-\min\Big\{\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U\setminus\mathbb{R}}\Omega(z),\frac{1}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U\cap\mathbb{R}}\Omega(z)\Big\}.

.
The latter is recovered by taking

Remark 3

(Rate function as a solution to an obstacle problem)
.

We now discuss the potential-theoretic interpretation of the rate

For this, we briefly recall some basic notions from logarithmic
potential theory
[
85

Let
Q:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{R}
be a general external potential
satisfying standard growth and regularity assumptions.
The equilibrium measure
\sigma_{Q}
is defined as the unique minimiser
of the weighted logarithmic energy functional

I_{Q}[\mu]:=\int_{\mathbb{C}^{2}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\mu(z)\,d\mu(w)+\int_{\mathbb{C}}Q(z)\,d\mu(z).

(2.18)

Under suitable regularity conditions on
Q
, the equilibrium measure
takes the form

d\sigma_{Q}(z)=\Delta Q(z)\,\mathbbm{1}_{S_{Q}}(z)\,\frac{d^{2}z}{\pi},\qquad\Delta=\partial\bar{\partial},

(2.19)

where the compact set
S_{Q}
, known as the
droplet
, is the support of

\sigma_{Q}
.
In particular, when the potential
Q
is given by
V
in
(
2.2
), the associated equilibrium measure coincides
with the elliptic law (
2.4
).

The
obstacle function

\check{Q}
associated with a potential

Q
is defined as the maximal subharmonic function satisfying

\check{Q}(z)\leq Q(z)\quad\text{on }\mathbb{C},\qquad\check{Q}(z)\leq\log|z|^{2}+O(1)\quad\text{as }|z|\to\infty.

(2.20)

Equivalently, it admits the representation

\check{Q}(z)=\gamma-2\,U_{Q}(z),\qquad U_{Q}(z):=\int_{\mathbb{C}}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,d\sigma_{Q}(w),

(2.21)

where
\gamma
is a unique constant satisfying
2U_{Q}+Q=\gamma
on
S_{Q}
and
2U_{Q}+Q\geq\gamma
on
\mathbb{C}
quasi-everywhere.

For the elliptic law (
2.4
), by using

[
23
, Lemma 2.4]
and
[
39
, Lemma 3.3 with
c=0
]
, one can show that

\displaystyle\int_{E}\log\frac{1}{|z-w|}\,\frac{d^{2}w}{(1-\tau^{2})\pi}=\frac{1}{2}-\operatorname{Re}\bigg[\frac{z(z-\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau})}{4\tau}\bigg]-\log\bigg|\frac{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau}}{2}\bigg|.

(2.22)

Then by the representation (
2.21
), for the potential
V
defined in (
2.2
), we have

\check{V}(z)=\operatorname{Re}\bigg[\frac{2z}{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau}}\bigg]+2\log\bigg|\frac{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau}}{2}\bigg|.

(2.23)

Combining this with (
2.13
), it follows that
\Omega
admits the expression

\Omega(z)=V(z)-\check{V}(z).

(2.24)

Therefore, one observes that the large deviation rate function admits a natural interpretation in terms of the obstacle function associated with the underlying potential.

2.3.
Asymptotics of the one-point functions outside the droplet

The key ingredient in the proof of Theorem
2.2
is the asymptotic behaviour of the one-point function of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles.

For the eGinUE and eGinSE, we denote their one-point functions by
R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}
and
R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}
, respectively. These are characterised by

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(z_{j})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}z,\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{j=1}^{N}f(z_{j})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)\,d^{2}z,

(2.25)

for any suitable test function
f:\mathbb{C}\to\mathbb{C}
.
For the eGinOE, we denote by
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}
and
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}
the real and complex one-point functions, which are characterised by

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{z_{j}\in\mathbb{R}}f(z_{j})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)\,dx,\qquad\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{z_{j}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{R}}f(z_{j})\bigg]=\int_{\mathbb{C}}f(z)R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}(z)\,d^{2}z

(2.26)

for suitable test functions.

In Propositions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.4
below, we establish that as
N\to\infty
,
the one-point functions exhibit exponential asymptotics
outside the droplet
E
; see also Figures
5
,
6
and
7
.
More precisely, for
z\in E^{c}
,

R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)=\exp\Big(-N\Omega(z)+o(N)\Big),\qquad R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)=\exp\Big(-2N\Omega(z)+o(N)\Big)

(2.27)

for the eGinUE and eGinSE, respectively, where
\Omega
is given by (
2.13
).
For the eGinOE, we have, for
x\in\mathbb{R}
with
|x|>1+\tau
, and for
z\in E^{c}\setminus\mathbb{R}
,

R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)=\exp\Big(-\frac{N}{2}\Omega(x)+o(N)\Big),\qquad R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}(z)=\exp\Big(-N\Omega(z)+o(N)\Big).

(2.28)

These asymptotic behaviours explain why the function
\Omega
naturally appears in the rate function in Theorem
2.2
.

We stress here that the asymptotic behaviour of
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)
was previously derived in
[
17
,
70
]
; see also
[
33
, Remarks 3.4 and 3.10]
.
In Propositions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.4
, we derive not only the leading-order asymptotics (
2.27
) and (
2.28
), but also the precise expansion up to and including the constant-order term.

We also emphasise that in the eGinOE case, the large deviation rate function originates from
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}
, rather than from
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}
.
From a probabilistic point of view, this indicates that when the large deviation event occurs, it is typically a real eigenvalue that enters the unexpected domain
U
(in Theorem
2.2
), rather than a complex eigenvalue; see
[
92
]
for a similar discussion.

Remark 4

(Generalisation to random normal matrix models)
.

For the symmetry class corresponding to the GinUE (
\beta=2
),
the results presented above can be formulated in a more general framework,
namely in the setting where the quadratic potential
|z|^{2}
in
(
1.6
) is replaced by a general external potential
Q(z)

satisfying suitable regularity and growth assumptions.
Such models are known as random normal matrix models or determinantal Coulomb gases.

Owing to the determinantal structure and the representation in terms of planar orthogonal polynomials, one may derive that the associated one-point function
R_{N,Q}
governs the asymptotic behaviour of the form

R_{N,Q}(z)=\exp\Big(-N\big(Q(z)-\check{Q}(z)\big)+o(N)\Big),

(2.29)

for
z
outside the droplet, where
\check{Q}
denotes the obstacle function defined in (
2.21
).

For a radially symmetric potential
Q(z)=Q(|z|)
, the obstacle function
can be computed explicitly using Jensen’s formula; see e.g.

[
85
, Theorem 6.1 in Section IV.6]
and
[
37
, Eq. (2.4)]
.
In particular, when
Q(z)=|z|^{2}
, we have
\check{Q}(z)=2\log|z|+1
and hence the asymptotic behaviour (
2.29
) is consistent with the known result in the literature
[
40
, Proposition 1 with
\Gamma=2
]
.

The upper bound of the form (
2.29
) is indeed known under the name of the exterior estimate; see e.g.
[
14
, Lemma 3.3]
and
[
13
, Section 4.1.1]
.
On the other hand, it is expected that the corresponding lower bound can be derived from recent development of the theory of planar orthogonal polynomials
[
72
]
.
Together, these results suggest that for the random normal matrix model,
Theorem
2.2
is expected to hold with
\Omega
replaced by
Q(z)-\check{Q}(z)
, which is consistent with our formulation (
2.24
).

Nevertheless, we do not pursue this direction further in the present work. Indeed, the above formulation relies crucially on the determinantal structure and therefore applies only to the case
\beta=2
.
For the other symmetry classes (
\beta=1,4
), a comparable theory has not yet been developed. Instead, our focus here is on the elliptic Ginibre ensembles, where we are able to obtain a comprehensive understanding across all three symmetry classes.

Plan of the paper

The rest of this paper is organised as follows. In Section
3
, we collect several exact identities for the one-point functions of the three symmetry classes of elliptic Ginibre ensembles. In Section
4
, we combine these identities with asymptotic properties of the relevant orthogonal polynomials to derive the large-
N
behaviour of the one-point functions; see Propositions
4.2
,
4.3
, and
4.4
. Finally, these results are used to establish our main theorems, Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

3.
Exact formulas for finite-
N

As mentioned above, the availability of exact finite-
N
formulas plays a crucial role in enabling a precise asymptotic analysis.
For the elliptic Ginibre ensembles, substantial progress has been made in the exact computation of correlation functions, in particular through the underlying determinantal and Pfaffian integrable structures.

The finite-
N
analysis we discuss here proceeds in two main steps. The first step is to obtain explicit representations of the one-point functions.
Such representations have been developed using the determinantal and Pfaffian structures of the ensembles, together with the orthogonal and skew-orthogonal polynomial formalism; see in particular
[
19
,
67
]
and
[
74
]
for the integrable structures of the Pfaffian point processes associated with the eGinOE and eGinSE, respectively.
Within these frameworks, the one-point functions can be expressed explicitly in terms of large summations involving Hermite polynomials.
However, these summation formulas are not immediately amenable to asymptotic analysis, as their large summation structure obscures the dominant contributions.
To overcome this difficulty, in the second step, we adopt the approach developed in
[
79
,
26
,
67
,
32
]
, which avoids a direct analysis of the large summations. Instead, it exploits an appropriate operator framework to compute the one-point functions in a more tractable form.

3.1.
Planar orthogonal and skew-orthogonal polynomials

To introduce the integrable structures underlying the eigenvalue distributions of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles, we begin by fixing some notation. Recall that the external potential
V
is given by (
2.2
). We then define the associated weight functions

\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z):=e^{-NV(z)},\qquad\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z):=e^{-2NV(z)},

(3.1)

corresponding to the eGinUE and eGinSE, respectively.
For the eGinOE, the weight function is slightly more involved and takes the form

\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}(z):=e^{\frac{N}{1+\tau}((\operatorname{Im}z)^{2}-(\operatorname{Re}z)^{2})}\operatorname{erfc}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{2N}{1-\tau^{2}}}\ \operatorname{Im}z\Big),

(3.2)

for
\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0
, and defined by the symmetry
z\mapsto\overline{z}
for
\operatorname{Im}z<0
, where

\operatorname{erfc}(z)=\frac{2}{\sqrt{\pi}}\int_{z}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}\,dt

is the complementary error function.

In addition to the weight functions, key ingredients in the description of the one-point functions are the families of associated planar orthogonal and skew-orthogonal polynomials. For the elliptic Ginibre ensembles, these polynomials can be expressed in terms of the Hermite polynomials

H_{n}(x):=(-1)^{n}e^{x^{2}}\frac{d^{n}}{dx^{n}}e^{-x^{2}}.

(3.3)

For a positive integer
N
and a non-negative integer
j
, it is convenient to introduce the monic polynomials

\phi_{N,j}(z):=\Big(\frac{\tau}{2N}\Big)^{j/2}H_{j}\Big(\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\tau}}z\Big).

(3.4)

With this notation in place, we first define the orthogonal polynomials and their corresponding norms associated with the eGinUE:

p_{N,j}^{\mathbb{C}}(z):=\phi_{N,j}(z),\qquad h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{C}}:=\frac{j!}{N^{j+1}}\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}.

(3.5)

For the eGinSE and eGinOE, the situation is more involved, as the relevant families form skew-orthogonal polynomials, whose construction requires a separate analysis.
For the eGinSE, we define

p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{H}}(z):=\phi_{2N,2j+1}(z),\qquad p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{H}}(z):=\sum_{l=0}^{j}\frac{(2j)!!}{(2l)!!}\frac{1}{(2N)^{j-l}}\phi_{2N,2l}(z).

(3.6)

It was shown in
[
74
]
(see also
[
3
]
) that these form a family of skew-orthogonal polynomials (we omit the precise definition here), with skew-norm

h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{H}}:=\frac{(2j+1)!}{(2N)^{2j+2}}2(1-\tau)\pi\sqrt{1-\tau^{2}}.

(3.7)

Finally, for the eGinOE, the skew-orthogonal polynomials are given in
[
67
]
by

p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{R}}(z):=\phi_{N,2j}(z),\qquad p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{R}}(z):=\phi_{N,2j+1}(z)-\frac{2j}{N}\phi_{N,2j-1}(z),

(3.8)

with skew-norm

h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{R}}:=\frac{(2j)!}{N^{2j+3/2}}\sqrt{2\pi}(1+\tau).

(3.9)

These families of polynomials introduced above serve as the building blocks for expressing the one-point functions of the eGinUE, eGinSE, and eGinOE.

3.2.
Exact identities of the eGinUE correlation functions

We begin with the exact formulas for the eGinUE case. Using (
3.5
), it is convenient to introduce

K_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w):=\sum_{j=0}^{M-1}\frac{p_{N,j}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)p_{N,j}^{\mathbb{C}}(w)}{h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{C}}}.

(3.10)

It is well known that the eigenvalues of the eGinUE form a determinantal point process with (unweighted) correlation kernel

K_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w):=K_{N,N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w).

(3.11)

Consequently, the one-point function
R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)
admits the representation

R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)=\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)K_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{z}),

(3.12)

where
\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}
is given by (
3.1
). Such a determinantal formula has been used to derive several interesting properties of the eGinUE, including two-point observables; see e.g.
[
86
]
.

The main idea due to Lee and Riser in
[
79
, Proposition 2.3]
for the asymptotic analysis of the correlation kernel is the following: upon applying a suitable differential operator, the kernel can be rewritten in terms of only the last few orthogonal polynomials of highest degree. We recall the precise statement.

Lemma 3.1

(cf. Proposition 2.3 in
[
79
]
)
.

For any positive integers
N
and
M
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}\Big[K_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{z})\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\Big]=\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{1}{h_{N,M-1}^{\mathbb{C}}}\Big(\tau p_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{p_{N,M-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}-p_{N,M-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{p_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}\Big)\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z).

(3.13)

In particular, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial z}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)=\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{1}{h_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}}\Big(\tau p_{N,N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{p_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}-p_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\overline{p_{N,N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}\Big)\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z).

(3.14)

This lemma provides an effective tool for analysing the large-
N
asymptotic behaviour of the correlation kernel across various regimes (see also
[
2
]
for an alternative approach). Moreover, its applicability extends beyond the unitary case: as we will discuss in the following subsections, an analogous strategy can also be implemented for the other symmetry classes by exploiting suitable connection formulas.

3.3.
Exact identities of the eGinSE correlation functions

In contrast to the eGinUE whose eigenvalues form a determinantal point process, the eigenvalues of the eGinSE constitute a Pfaffian point process. The associated pre-kernel is given by

\displaystyle\kappa_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z,w):=\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}\frac{p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{H}}(w)-p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{H}}(w)}{h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{H}}},

(3.15)

where
p_{j}^{\mathbb{H}}
and
h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{H}}
are defined in (
3.6
) and (
3.7
), respectively.
Consequently, the one-point function
R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)
admits the representation

R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)=(\overline{z}-z)\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)\kappa_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z,\overline{z}),

(3.16)

where the weight function
\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)
is given by (
3.1
).

Observe that, since the polynomials
p_{N,j}^{\mathbb{H}}
are expressed as finite linear combinations of Hermite polynomials, the pre-kernel (
3.15
) admits a representation as a double summation. This additional layer of summation creates a further difficulty in the asymptotic analysis.
To overcome this obstacle, the key idea developed in
[
26
, Proposition 1.1]
is to apply a suitable differential operator, thereby establishing a connection formula with the eGinUE kernel.

Lemma 3.2

(cf. Proposition 1.1 and Lemma 3.2 in
[
26
]
)
.

For any positive integer
N
, we have

\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial z}-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}z\Big)\kappa_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z,w)=\frac{N}{(1-\tau^{2})}\Big(K_{2N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)-E_{N}(z,w)\Big),

(3.17)

where
K_{2N}
is given by (
3.11
) with
N\mapsto 2N
and

E_{N}(z,w):=\frac{1}{h_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}}p_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)p_{N,2N-2}^{\mathbb{H}}(w).

(3.18)

Moreover, we have

\Big(\frac{\partial}{\partial w}-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}w\Big)E_{N}(z,w)=-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\frac{1}{h_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}}p_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)p_{2N,2N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(w).

(3.19)

Combined with Lemma
3.1
, this reduction yields a representation that is amenable to asymptotic analysis. This strategy has been applied to derive various scaling limits of the eGinSE correlation functions; see
[
26
,
27
]
.

3.4.
Exact identities of the eGinOE correlation functions

Finally, we turn to the integrable structure of the eGinOE correlation functions.
The eigenvalues of the eGinOE form a Pfaffian point process. However, since the spectrum consists of both real and complex eigenvalues, the one-point functions for the real and complex parts must be treated separately.
In addition, the structure of the Pfaffian kernel depends on the parity of the dimension
N
. In what follows, we restrict our attention to the case where
N
is even.

The correlation functions of classical asymmetric random matrices with real entries can be expressed as a rank-one perturbation of their complex counterparts; see
[
67
,
35
]
.
For the one-point function of the real eigenvalues, it was shown in
[
67
]
that

R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)=\sqrt{\frac{1-\tau^{2}}{2\pi N}}\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}(x)K_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(x,x)+\frac{1}{h_{N,N-2}^{\mathbb{R}}}\sqrt{\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}(x)}\phi_{N,N-1}(x)\int_{0}^{x}du\sqrt{\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}(u)}\phi_{N,N-2}(u),

(3.20)

where
\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
K_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)
are defined in (
3.2
) and (
3.10
), respectively.
Using this, various asymptotic properties of the real eigenvalues such as the limiting density and scaling limits were derived in
[
67
,
32
,
33
]
.

For the one-point function of the complex eigenvalues, it was also shown in
[
67
]
(for even
N
) that

R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}(z)=\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{R}}(z)S_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(\overline{z},z)

(3.21)

for
\operatorname{Im}z>0
, and by the symmetry
z\mapsto\overline{z}
for
\operatorname{Im}z<0
, where

\displaystyle S_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(z,w):=i\sum_{j=0}^{N/2-1}\frac{p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{R}}(z)p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{R}}(w)-p_{N,2j+1}^{\mathbb{R}}(w)p_{N,2j}^{\mathbb{R}}(z)}{h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{R}}}.

(3.22)

Here,
p_{N,j}^{\mathbb{R}}
and
h_{N,j}^{\mathbb{R}}
are given by (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), respectively.

The following lemma is obtained in
[
5
]
showing that
R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}
likewise admits a representation as a finite-rank perturbation of its complex counterpart.

Lemma 3.3

(cf. Eq. (5.18) in
[
5
]
)
.

For even
N
, we have

S_{N}^{\mathrm{c}}(z,w)=i\sqrt{\frac{\pi N}{2(1-\tau^{2})}}\bigg((z-w)K_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,w)-\tau\frac{p_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)p_{N,N-2}^{\mathbb{C}}(w)-p_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(w)p_{N,N-2}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}{h_{N,N-2}^{\mathbb{C}}}\bigg),

(3.23)

where
K_{N,M}^{\mathbb{C}}
is given in (
3.10
).

4.
Large-
N
asymptotic analysis

This section is devoted to the asymptotic behaviour of the one-point functions of the eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre ensembles outside the droplet, as well as to the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and

2.2
.

For this analysis, we make use of asymptotic expansions of the Hermite polynomials, which we now recall.
For this purpose, we define

\psi(z):=\frac{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau}}{2},

(4.1)

where the square root is taken with the principal branch on
\mathbb{C}\setminus[-2\sqrt{\tau},2\sqrt{\tau}]
. This function is the inverse of the Joukowsky transform. Namely, it is a conformal map from the exterior of the unit disc onto the exterior of the ellipse
E
.
Next, we define

g(z):=\frac{z}{2\psi(z)}+\log\psi(z).

(4.2)

Then the function
\Omega
in (
2.13
) can be expressed as

\Omega(z)=V(z)-2\operatorname{Re}g(z),

(4.3)

where
V
is given by (
2.2
).

Asymptotic expansions of the Hermite polynomials are well known in the
literature. We utilise the expansions given in
[
55
,
79
]
.
Recall that the monic polynomial
\phi_{N,j}
is given by (
3.4
).

Lemma 4.1

(cf. Lemma 2.5 in
[
79
]
)
.

Let
r
be a fixed integer and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E
. Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

\phi_{N,N+r}(z)=\sqrt{h_{N,N+r}^{\mathbb{C}}}\Big(\frac{N}{2\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}\Big)^{\frac{1}{4}}\psi(z)^{r}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}\,e^{Ng(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big),

(4.4)

where the error term is uniform within a set bounded away from
E
.

4.1.
Asymptotics of the one-point functions

We now present the asymptotic behaviours of the one-point functions in the following Propositions
4.2
,
4.3
and
4.4
for each eGinUE, eGinOE and eGinSE, respectively.
We begin with the eGinUE.

Proposition 4.2

(
Asymptotics of the one-point function for the eGinUE
)
.

Let
\tau\in[0,1)
be fixed. Let
N
be a positive integer and
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus E
.
Then as
N\to\infty
, we have

R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)=\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|}{|\psi(z)|^{2}}\frac{\operatorname{Re}\psi(z)}{\operatorname{Re}[\psi(z)-\psi(z)^{-1}]}e^{-N\Omega(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big),

(4.5)

where
\psi
and
\Omega
are defined in (
4.1
) and (
2.13
).
The error term is uniform within a set bounded away from
E
.

See Figure
5
for a numerical confirmation of Proposition
4.2
.

.

(b)

.

Proof of Proposition

.

We may assume that
\operatorname{Re}z\geq 0
, since otherwise the conclusion follows by the symmetry
z\mapsto-\overline{z}
.
Let us write
x=\operatorname{Re}z
and
y=\operatorname{Im}z
. By combining Lemmas
3.1
and
4.1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\frac{1}{h_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}}\operatorname{Re}\Big[\phi_{N,N}(z)\overline{\phi_{N,N-1}(z)}\Big]

\displaystyle=-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\operatorname{Re}\psi(z)}{|\psi(z)|^{2}}e^{-N\Omega(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big).

Applying Laplace’s method, we obtain

\displaystyle R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(u+iy)\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\operatorname{Re}\psi(z)}{|\psi(z)|^{2}}\frac{1}{N\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Omega(z)}e^{-N\Omega(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big).

We note that

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Omega(z)=\frac{2}{1+\tau}\operatorname{Re}[\psi(z)-\psi(z)^{-1}],

(4.6)

which yields the desired conclusion.
∎

Proof of Proposition
4.3
.

By symmetries, we may assume
\operatorname{Re}z,\operatorname{Im}z\geq 0

3.3

(4.9)

For the first term,
K_{N,N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(z,\overline{z})

3.1
and
4.1

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Big[K_{N,N-1}(z,\overline{z})\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\Big]

Then, applying Laplace’s method and writing
z=x+iy
for
x,y\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle y\,K_{N,N-1}(z,\overline{z})

\displaystyle=-\frac{y}{\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)}\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}\Big[K_{N,N-1}(u+iy,u+iy)\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(u+iy)\Big]\,du

\displaystyle=\frac{y}{1+\tau}\sqrt{\frac{2N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\operatorname{Re}\psi(z)}{|\psi(z)|^{4}\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Omega(z)}e^{2N\operatorname{Re}g(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big).

\tau\frac{\operatorname{Im}[\phi_{N,N-1}(z)\phi_{N,N-2}(\overline{z})]}{h_{N,N-2}^{\mathbb{C}}}=\tau\sqrt{\frac{N}{2\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\operatorname{Im}\psi(z)}{|\psi(z)|^{4}}e^{2N\operatorname{Re}g(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big).

Then as

R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)=\frac{\operatorname{Im}z}{1-\tau}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|}{|\psi(z)^{2}-1|^{2}}\frac{\operatorname{Im}[\psi(z)^{2}]}{\operatorname{Re}[\psi(z)-\psi(z)^{-1}]}e^{-2N\Omega(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big),

(4.10)

where

and

) and (

Proof of Proposition
4.4
.

Let
x=\operatorname{Re}z
and
y=\operatorname{Im}z
. By symmetry, we may assume
x\geq 0
.
Using Lemma
3.2
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial x}R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{N}{1-\tau^{2}}(\overline{z}-z)\omega_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)\Big(\overline{E_{N}(z,\overline{z})}-E_{N}(z,\overline{z})\Big).

This reduces the problem to computing the asymptotics of
E_{N}(z,\overline{z})
.
Again by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\frac{\partial}{\partial w}\Big[E_{N}(z,w)e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}w^{2}}\Big]=-\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\frac{1}{h_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}}p_{2N,2N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)p_{2N,2N-1}^{\mathbb{C}}(w)e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}w^{2}}.

We note that
E_{N}(z,w)e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}w^{2}}
is an entire function that converges to
0
as
\operatorname{Re}w\to+\infty
.
Hence, by the method of steepest descent, we obtain

\displaystyle E_{N}(z,w)e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}w^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{w}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial\eta}\Big[E_{N}(z,\eta)e^{-\frac{N}{1+\tau}\eta^{2}}\Big]\,d\eta

\displaystyle=\frac{2N}{1+\tau}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}e^{2Ng(z)}\int_{w}^{\infty}\frac{\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(\eta)}}{\psi(\eta)}e^{2N(g(\eta)-\frac{1}{1+\tau}\eta^{2})}d\eta\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big)

remains bounded.
Rearranging the terms yields

E_{N}(z,w)=\sqrt{\frac{N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(z)}\frac{\sqrt{\psi^{\prime}(w)}}{\psi(w)^{2}-1}e^{2N(g(z)+g(w))}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big).

\displaystyle R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(z)

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{\partial}{\partial u}R_{N}^{\mathbb{H}}(u+iy)\,du

\displaystyle=-\int_{x}^{\infty}\frac{4Ny}{1-\tau^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(u+iy)|\operatorname{Im}[\psi(u+iy)^{2}]}{|\psi(u+iy)^{2}-1|^{2}}e^{-2N\Omega(u+iy)}du\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big)

\displaystyle=\frac{4N\operatorname{Im}z}{1-\tau^{2}}\sqrt{\frac{N}{\pi^{3}(1-\tau^{2})}}\frac{|\psi^{\prime}(z)|\operatorname{Im}[\psi(z)^{2}]}{|\psi(z)^{2}-1|^{2}}\frac{1}{2N\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Omega(z)}e^{-2N\Omega(z)}\Big(1+O\Big(\frac{1}{N}\Big)\Big),

4.2.
Proof of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2

Using the asymptotic behaviour established in the previous section, we now prove Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.
We first show the following.

Lemma 4.5
.

For
s>1+\tau
, let
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\geq s\}
or
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}z\geq s\}
. Then we have

\inf_{z\in U}\Omega(z)=\Omega(s).

(4.11)

Proof.

Let
z=x+iy=re^{i\theta}
. Also denote

u:=\psi(z)=\frac{z+\sqrt{z^{2}-4\tau}}{2},\qquad z=u+\frac{\tau}{u}.

Then, we have

x=\operatorname{Re}\Big(u+\frac{\tau}{u}\Big)=\frac{(|u|^{2}+\tau)\operatorname{Re}u}{|u|^{2}},\qquad y=\operatorname{Im}\Big(u+\frac{\tau}{u}\Big)=\frac{(|u|^{2}-\tau)\operatorname{Im}u}{|u|^{2}}

(4.12)

and

\Omega(z)=\frac{1}{1-\tau^{2}}\Big(|z|^{2}-\tau\operatorname{Re}z^{2}\Big)-\operatorname{Re}\Big(\frac{z}{u}+2\log u\Big).

First fix
y\in\mathbb{R}
and suppose
x>x_{0}>1+\tau
. Differentiating
\Omega
with respect to
x
yields

\frac{\partial}{\partial x}\Omega(z)=\frac{2x}{1+\tau}-2\operatorname{Re}\frac{1}{u}=\frac{2x}{1+\tau}\frac{|u|^{2}-1}{|u|^{2}+\tau},

where we have used (
4.12
).
Since
\psi:\mathbb{C}\setminus E\to\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
is conformal and
x>1+\tau
, we have
|u|>1
. Hence

\inf_{x>x_{0}}\Omega(x+iy)=\Omega(x_{0}+iy),\qquad y\in\mathbb{R}.

(4.13)

By symmetry, it also holds that

\inf_{x<-x_{0}}\Omega(x+iy)=\Omega(-x_{0}+iy).

Next fix
x>1+\tau
and differentiate
\Omega
with respect to
y
:

\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Omega(z)=\frac{2y}{1-\tau}+2\operatorname{Im}\frac{1}{u}=\frac{2y}{1-\tau}\frac{|u|^{2}-1}{|u|^{2}-\tau},

where we have used (
4.12
).
Since
|u|>1
, it follows that

\inf_{y\in\mathbb{R}}\Omega(x+iy)=\Omega(x),\qquad x>1+\tau.

(4.14)

On the other hand, for fixed
r>1+\tau
, we compute

\frac{\partial}{\partial\theta}\Omega(z)=\Big(-y\frac{\partial}{\partial x}+x\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\Big)\Omega(z)=\frac{2\tau r^{2}\sin 2\theta}{1-\tau^{2}}\frac{|u|^{4}-1}{|u|^{4}-\tau^{2}}.

Thus, we have

\inf_{\theta\in[0,2\pi)}\Omega(re^{i\theta})=\Omega(r)=\Omega(-r),\qquad r>1+\tau.

(4.15)

For
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}z\geq s\}
, it follows from (
4.13
) and (
4.14
) that
\inf_{z\in U}\Omega(z)=\Omega(s)
.
For
U=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|\geq s\}
, using (
4.13
) and (
4.14
), together with symmetry to cover the left half-plane
\{x+iy:x<0\}
, we obtain

\inf_{z\in U}\Omega(z)=\inf_{|z|=s}\Omega(z).

Then (
4.15
) yields the desired result, completing the proof.
∎

Deriving the leading-order asymptotics of the upper tail from the asymptotics of the one-point function is standard in random matrix theory; see e.g.
[
59
, Section 14.6.4]
.
Our argument follows a slight modification of the approach in
[
92
]
.

Proof of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
.

Note that Theorem
2.1
is an immediate consequence of Theorem
2.2
, Lemma
4.5
, and (
2.16
).
Therefore, it suffices to prove Theorem
2.2
.

Let
\{z_{j}\}_{j=1}^{N}
denote the eigenvalues of the elliptic Ginibre ensemble.
Given a measurable set
U\subset\mathbb{C}
, we have the bounds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\Big[z_{j}\in U\Big]\leq\mathbb{P}\Big[\exists\,1\leq j\leq N\ \textup{such that }z_{j}\in U\Big]\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\Big[z_{j}\in U\Big].

(4.16)

By the definitions (
2.25
) and (
2.26
), the upper and lower bounds can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}\Big[z_{j}\in U\Big]=\mathbb{E}\Big[\sum_{j=1}^{N}\mathbbm{1}_{U}(z_{j})\Big]=\begin{cases}\int_{U\cap\mathbb{R}}R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)\,dx+\int_{U\setminus\mathbb{R}}R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}(z)\,d^{2}z,&\text{for the eGinOE},\\
\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{H}}(z)\,d^{2}z,&\text{for the eGinU/SE}.\end{cases}

(4.17)

Now suppose that
U
is bounded away from
E
.
Then, as
N\to\infty
, we claim that

\displaystyle\int_{U\cap\mathbb{R}}R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{r}}(x)\,dx=\exp\bigg[-\frac{N}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{x\in U\cap\mathbb{R}}\Omega(x)+o(N)\bigg],\qquad\int_{U\setminus\mathbb{R}}R_{N}^{\mathbb{R},\mathrm{c}}(z)\,d^{2}z=\exp\bigg[-N\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U\setminus\mathbb{R}}\Omega(z)+o(N)\bigg]

for the eGinOE, and

\displaystyle\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}/\mathbb{H}}(z)\,d^{2}z=\exp\bigg[-\frac{\beta N}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U}\Omega(z)+o(N)\bigg]

for the eGinU/SE, where
\beta
is defined in (
2.6
).
Then Theorem
2.2
follows directly from this claim together with (
4.16
) and (
4.17
).

It remains to prove the claim. We give the details only for the eGinUE.
The cases of the eGinSE and eGinOE follow by the same arguments.
Let
\mu
denote the Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{C}
.
Since the claim is trivial when
\mu(U)=0
, we assume that
\mu(U)\neq 0
.
Let
a:=\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U}\Omega(z)
and, for
\epsilon>0
, set

U_{\epsilon}:=U\cap\Omega^{-1}[a,a+\epsilon).

Choose
\epsilon_{1}>0
arbitrarily and let
\delta:=\mu(U_{\epsilon_{1}})>0
.
Next choose a decreasing sequence
\epsilon_{N}>0
such that
\epsilon_{N}\to 0
and
\mu(U_{\epsilon_{N}})>\delta/N
.

By construction,
U_{\epsilon_{1}}
is bounded away from both
E
and infinity.
Since
\psi
is the Joukowsky transform from
\mathbb{C}\setminus E
to
\mathbb{C}\setminus\mathbb{D}
, Proposition
4.2
implies that there exists a constant
c>0
such that

R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\geq c\sqrt{N}e^{-N\Omega(z)}

for all
z\in U_{\epsilon_{1}}
. Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}z

\displaystyle\geq\int_{U_{\epsilon_{N}}}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}z\geq\mu(U_{\epsilon_{N}})c\sqrt{N}e^{-N(a+\epsilon_{N})}d^{2}z>\frac{\delta c}{\sqrt{N}}e^{-N(a+\epsilon_{N})},

which gives rise to the lower bound

\displaystyle\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}x\geq\exp\bigg[-\frac{\beta N}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U}\Omega(z)+o(N)\bigg].

To prove the upper bound, choose
r\in(1+\tau,\infty)
sufficiently large so that
\Omega(r)>a
and
\mu(U\cap B_{r})>0
, where
B_{r}
denotes the ball of radius
r
in
\mathbb{C}
centred at the origin.
Again by Proposition
4.2
, there exists a constant
c^{\prime}>0
such that

R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\leq c^{\prime}\sqrt{N}e^{-N\Omega(z)}

for all
z\in U\cap B_{r}
.
Then, we have

\displaystyle\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}z

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\int_{U}e^{-N\Omega(z)}\,d^{2}z\leq c^{\prime}\bigg[\int_{U\cap B_{r}}+\int_{\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r}}\bigg]e^{-N\Omega(z)}\,d^{2}z

\displaystyle\leq c^{\prime}\bigg[\pi r^{2}e^{-Na}+2\pi\int_{r}^{\infty}e^{-N\Omega(x)}\,dx\bigg]\leq c^{\prime\prime}e^{-Na}

for some constant
c^{\prime\prime}>0
.
In the third inequality, we used that
\Omega(|z|)\leq\Omega(z)
for all
z\in\mathbb{C}\setminus B_{r}
, as shown in the proof of Lemma
4.5
.

Combining the above, as
N\to\infty
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{U}R_{N}^{\mathbb{C}}(z)\,d^{2}x\leq\exp\bigg[-\frac{\beta N}{2}\operatorname*{ess\,inf}_{z\in U}\Omega(z)+o(N)\bigg].

This proves the claim for the eGinUE and hence completes the proof of Theorem
2.2
.
∎
