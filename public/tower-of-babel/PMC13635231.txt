A Freshwater Fish Dataset for Visual Recognition with Manually Localized ROIs and SAM-Derived Instance Masks
Authors: Shakib Absar, Iftekhar Ahmed, Md Istiaque Khalique, Mohammad Shorfuzzaman, Md Mahfuzur Rahman, Asrar U Haque, Md Saidur Rahman Kohinoor
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe SylFishBD dataset addresses the critical need for automated identification of local fish species in Bangladesh’s dynamic aquatic ecosystems and bustling fish markets. It comprises 9,075 high-resolution images of 9 prevalent freshwater species, captured under uncontrolled, real-world conditions over 7 months to capture seasonal variations in appearance, freshness, and market presentation. Unlike prior datasets limited to controlled studio settings, SylFishBD faithfully replicates the complexity of operational fish markets: diverse lighting (natural daylight to fluorescent), varied viewpoints (top-down, angled, close-up), cluttered backgrounds (ice, trays, water, scales), and natural occlusions (vendor hands, overlapping fish). Each image contains exactly one clearly centered fish instance, annotated with a tight bounding box and a high-precision binary segmentation mask generated using the Segment Anything Model (SAM). All images are standardized to 500 × 500 pixels, organized hierarchically by species, and accompanied by comprehensive metadata, enabling seamless integration into machine learning pipelines. The dataset supports a wide range of computer vision tasks, including classification, object detection, instance segmentation, and morphological analysis, without requiring additional preprocessing. By bridging the gap between laboratory-based datasets and authentic market environments, SylFishBD serves as a robust, publicly available benchmark for developing deployable models in real-world aquaculture, trade transparency, price monitoring, and regulatory oversight. It empowers researchers and practitioners to advance automated species recognition, freshness assessment, and fair market practices in one of the world’s most active fish-producing regions.

Background & Summary

Bangladesh, a vital South Asian nation, boasts one of the world’s most extensive networks of rivers, wetlands, canals, and ponds, making it a global hotspot for aquatic biodiversity. Approximately 70% of its fish production comes from inland freshwater sources, with the remaining contributions from marine (sea) and brackish water systems, positioning Bangladesh as a key player in the global fisheries sector. Freshwater fish, often colloquially referred to as freshwater fish in local dialects, serve as a fundamental dietary staple for the population, providing essential proteins, vitamins, and minerals to over 170 million people. Beyond nutrition, the fisheries sector is a cornerstone of Bangladesh’s economy, generating significant revenue through domestic trade, exports (particularly of species like hilsa and shrimp), and employment. It supports an estimated 17–18 million livelihoods, including full-time fishers, part-time workers, traders, processors, and transporters, with women playing a crucial role in post-harvest activities. The sector contributes about 3–4% to the national GDP and accounts for over 60% of animal protein intake, highlighting its socio-economic importance. However, challenges such as overfishing, habitat degradation due to climate change (e.g., rising sea levels and salinity intrusion), pollution from industrial effluents, and unsustainable aquaculture practices threaten this vital resource. In comparison to marine fisheries, freshwater systems offer higher accessibility for small-scale operations but face greater vulnerability to environmental changes. Marine fisheries, focused on the Bay of Bengal, emphasize species suited for export and industrial processing, but they are prone to issues like illegal, unreported, and unregulated (IUU) fishing and cyclones. Existing datasets, such as BengalDeltaFish1, BD-freshwater-fish2, BanFish3, BDFreshFish4, and Fish-Pak5, have attempted to address fish identification but fall short due to their limitations. Most contain limited images collections or rely on controlled capture settings, such as uniform backgrounds or fixed lighting, which do not reflect the real-world complexity of market environments. In contrast, datasets from Western countries often focus on seawater fish species, which dominate their aquatic research due to their coastal ecosystems and commercial fishing industries. This leaves a gap in robust datasets for freshwater and sweet water fish, particularly in South-East Asia. The dataset DeepFish6 is primarily marine-focused, providing a large number of underwater images across diverse marine habitats with annotations for segmentation, classification, and size estimation, which is valuable for computer vision model benchmarking but less tailored for freshwater market environments. Additionally, the global freshwater fish trophic interactions dataset7 compiles extensive ecological data covering dietary and trophic relationships among numerous freshwater species worldwide, enriching ecological and biodiversity studies beyond visual identification.

The SylFishBD dataset addresses this gap with a restricted yet robust collection of 9,075 annotated images of nine common freshwater fish species, collected over 6–7 months from Sylhet’s markets. Each image, captured under varied lighting, angles, and backgrounds, includes segmentation masks, which outline the fish to support advanced computer vision tasks, such as automated species identification. This makes SylFishBD particularly valuable for South-East Asian aquatic research, where freshwater ecosystems are critical to biodiversity and food security, and it complements the seawater-focused datasets prevalent in Western research. The dataset’s real-world conditions and comprehensive annotations make it ideal for building machine learning models that can assist vendors, consumers, and researchers in accurately identifying fish species, reducing market errors, and supporting sustainable fisheries management. By providing a publicly accessible resource, SylFishBD empowers stakeholders across Bangladesh and South-East Asia to develop practical, technology-driven solutions for aquatic biodiversity challenges. Methods

The SylFishBD dataset comprises 9,075 images of nine prevalent fish species from local fish markets in Sylhet, Bangladesh, designed to advance automated fish species identification for applications in price monitoring, market transparency, and consumer education. Unlike prior datasets with limited images and controlled settings, SylFishBD captures real-world variability, including diverse backgrounds, lighting, and angles, collected over 7 months to account for seasonal changes. The dataset is organized into a Folder, with each images annotated for species classification. images are stored in JPEG format, with annotations provided in JSON files compatible with common deep learning frameworks.

Data Collection

The SylFishBD dataset was collected from local fish markets in Sylhet, Bangladesh over 6–7 months, capturing seasonal variation and diverse real-world conditions. Images were taken using smartphones (e.g., Samsung Galaxy S21, iPhone 12) under uncontrolled lighting, angles, and backgrounds to reflect practical market scenarios. Species were identified by an experienced fish vendor and verified using standard taxonomic references. A subset of 500 images was re-evaluated by a second expert, achieving 99.4% agreement, indicating high labeling reliability.

The dataset is currently limited to Sylhet, and variations in fish appearance across other regions of Bangladesh may affect model generalizability. Future work will include data from additional regions. All images were systematically organized to ensure data integrity and support annotation in Table 4.

Data Preprocessing

The raw images in the SylFishBD dataset were captured in real-world fish markets under highly variable conditions differing lighting, camera angles, resolutions, and file formats. To establish a consistent foundation for annotation and segmentation, a uniform preprocessing pipeline was applied to all 9,075 images. Each image was resized to a fixed resolution of 500 × 500 pixels using bilinear interpolation, with aspect ratio preserved via letterboxing (black padding added symmetrically to shorter dimensions). This approach prevented geometric distortion of fish morphology while maintaining key diagnostic features such as fin structure, scale alignment, body curvature, and eye positioning critical for accurate species identification.

This preprocessing directly enabled:

Bounding Box Annotation: A fixed canvas size reduced bounding box placement variability and improved inter-annotator agreement.

The complete preprocessing pipeline, implemented in Python using OpenCV and PIL was developed specifically for this work, ensuring complete reproducibility from raw market photographs to the final annotated and masked dataset, though the code is not publicly released.

Bounding Box Annotation

The SylFishBD dataset was annotated using the LabelImg tool, which supports bounding box annotations for object detection and classification tasks. Each image was carefully labeled to identify the fish species, with bounding boxes drawn around individual fish instances to specify their location and class. Annotations are stored in JSON format, following the structure shown in the provided Table 6, which includes fields such as “label,” “points” (bounding box coordinates), “imagePath,” “imageHeight,” and “imageWidth, mainly saved in COCO format for flexibility. This format ensures compatibility with popular deep learning frameworks like TensorFlow and PyTorch. The annotation process was conducted by trained personnel and cross-verified to ensure high accuracy, making the dataset suitable for developing and evaluating machine learning models for fish species identification in real-world market settings.

Segmentation Mask Generation using SAM

To elevate the SylFishBD dataset into a multi-task benchmark for advanced computer vision applications-including instance segmentation with high-precision pixel-level binary segmentation masks were generated for all 9,075 images using the Segment Anything Model (SAM)8. Unlike traditional annotation methods requiring labor-intensive manual polygon labeling, SAM enables automatic mask generation through prompt engineering, demonstrating strong zero-shot transfer capabilities across diverse visual domains8. As a foundation model trained on 11 million images, SAM demonstrates remarkable zero-shot instance segmentation capabilities when prompted with coarse annotations such as bounding boxes9,10. The segmentation pipeline details in Table 7 leveraged the existing bounding box annotations as prompts to SAM, enabling zero-shot, prompt-guided segmentation of individual fish instances under highly challenging real-world conditions: dense market clutter, partial occlusions, water reflections, vendor interference, overlapping specimens, and variable lighting. This approach ensures tight, object-specific masks that accurately delineate fine anatomical details such as fins, eyes, scales, and body contours. The Post-processing steps included:

Experimental Design

The SylFishBD dataset was specifically curated to enable automated fish species identification in real-world local fish markets of Bangladesh, addressing critical challenges such as species mislabeling, price manipulation, and lack of consumer transparency. A total of 9,075 high-quality images containing 9,075 annotated fish instances across nine prevalent freshwater species were collected from five distinct fish markets in Sylhet, Bangladesh (coordinates: 24.8911°N, 91.8700°E) over a 6–7 month period to capture seasonal variations in fish morphology, coloration, and market presentation (e.g., fresh vs. iced, live vs. processed).

This strict one-fish-per-image policy, combined with comprehensive annotation and mask generation, ensures that SylFishBD is a clean, unambiguous, and highly challenging benchmark for real-world fish species recognition. The dataset faithfully reflects the true complexity of operational fish markets, enabling robust training and evaluation of vision models for practical deployment, such as mobile apps for consumer education, automated pricing systems, or regulatory market monitoring tools. Baseline Model Performance

Object detection

Evaluation of object detection performance on the SylFishBD dataset revealed exceptional accuracy across both YOLO architectures (Table 15). The YOLOv5s model achieved a precision of 0.990, recall of 0.993, mAP@0.5 of 0.994, and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.883. The YOLOv8s architecture demonstrated comparable performance with a mAP@0.5 of 0.994, and mAP@0.5:0.95 of 0.887. Notably, YOLOv8s exhibited marginally superior localization accuracy (higher mAP@0.5:0.95) despite requiring 22% more parameters (11.13M vs. 9.13M), suggesting improved bounding box regression capabilities. Both models maintained consistently high F1-scores (>0.99) across all nine fish species, indicating robust detection performance with minimal false positives or missed detections.

Semantic segmentation

Segmentation performance was evaluated using U-Net and DeepLabV3+ architectures, both demonstrating high-fidelity pixel-level classification (Table 15). DeepLabV3+ achieved superior performance with a mean IoU of 0.9375 and Dice coefficient of 0.9510, likely benefiting from its atrous spatial pyramid pooling and encoder-decoder structure. U-Net achieved competitive results with a mean IoU of 0.9301 and Dice coefficient of 0.9428. The sub-1% performance gap between architectures suggests that the high-contrast aquaculture imaging conditions facilitate robust segmentation regardless of network topology. Both models achieved per-class IoU scores exceeding 0.92 for all species, with ilish and boal exhibiting marginally lower scores (IoU ~ 0.93) potentially attributable to their elongated body morphologies.
