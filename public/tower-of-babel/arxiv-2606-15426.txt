Inexact Warped Resolvent iterations
Raul T. Marcavillaca 1, Fernando Roldán 2

Abstract
. In this paper we aim to solve structured monotone inclusions using inexact warped resolvents evaluated under a relative-error criterion. The resulting algorithms admit a geometric interpretation as relaxed projection methods onto dynamically generated cuts, extending classical projection–proximal and hybrid extragradient proximal frameworks to nonlinear warped resolvent. Under mild assumptions, we establish weak convergence of the iterates. We further derive strong convergence results by incorporating projection steps onto intersections of halfspaces via Haugazeau-type scheme, as well as linear convergence under a metric subregularity assumption. The proposed algorithm provides a unified framework for incorporating inexact resolvent computations into several classical schemes arising in monotone operator theory and primal-dual optimization, such as Tseng’s forward-backward-forward splitting, forward-backward-half-forward, Chambolle–Pock, and Condat–Vũ. Finally, we present applications in saddle-point and structured convex minimization problems. Numerical experiments on synthetic saddle-point instances and computed tomography reconstruction demonstrate the computational advantages of the proposed methods. Keywords. Operator splitting, warped resolvents, monotone inclusions, projection methods, relative-error methods, convex optimization. 2020 Mathematics Subject Classification. 47H05, 65K05, 65K15, 90C25.

Problem 1.1
.

Let
{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
be a real Hilbert space, let
\bm{A}\colon{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to 2^{{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}}
be a maximally monotone operator, and let
\bm{C}\colon{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
be a cocoercive operator. The problem is to

(1.1)

\text{find }\bm{x}\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\text{ such that }0\in\bm{A}\bm{x}+\bm{C}\bm{x},

under the assumption that the solution set, denoted by
\mathcal{S}
, is nonempty.

Problem
1.1
covers a large class of structured optimization models, variational systems and inclusion problems with complex structures.
For instance, given real Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
, the problem

(1.2)

\textnormal{find }x\in\mathcal{H}\textnormal{ such that }0\in Ax+L^{\ast}BLx+Cx+Dx,

where
A\colon\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
and
B\colon\mathcal{G}\to 2^{\mathcal{G}}
are maximally monotone operators,
L\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
is a bounded linear operator,
C\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a cocoercive operator, and
D\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is a Lipschitz continuous operator, can be reformulated as an instance of Problem
1.1
through suitable primal-dual embeddings in the space
{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}=\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}
(see for instance
[
50

Operator splitting methods for solving (
1.1

[
26
,
51
]
, which, for
x_{0}\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
and
\gamma\in]0,2\beta[
, where
\beta\in\left]0,+\infty\right[

\bm{C}
, iterates as follows:

(1.3)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad x_{n+1}=J_{\gamma\bm{A}}(x_{n}-\gamma\bm{C}x_{n}).

The sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by FB converges weakly to a point in
\mathcal{S}
. In recent years, variants of FB including variable metrics and
warped resolvents
(also called nonlinear-forward-backward algorithms) have attracted considerable attention due to their flexibility and their ability to exploit the structure of large-scale optimization problems
[

,

,

,

,

,

\bm{M}:{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}

J_{\bm{A}}^{\bm{M}}=(\bm{M}+\bm{A})^{-1}\circ\bm{M}
and it was introduced in
[

,

. Note that

is the standard resolvent. When
\bm{M}=\nabla f
, where

[

For instance, given a nonnegative sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

[
33

(1.4)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}w_{n}=J_{\bm{A}+\bm{C}}^{\bm{M}}x_{n}\\
w_{n}^{*}=\bm{M}x_{n}-\bm{M}w_{n}-\bm{C}w_{n}\\
t_{n}^{*}=w_{n}^{*}+\bm{C}x_{n}\\
\delta_{n}={\left\langle{{x_{n}-w_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{*}}}\right\rangle}-\frac{1}{4\beta}\|w_{n}-x_{n}\|^{2}\\
d_{n}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\delta_{n}}{\|t^{*}_{n}\|^{2}}t^{*}_{n},&\textnormal{ if }\delta_{n}>0;\\
0,&\textnormal{ otherwise }\end{cases}\\
x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\lambda_{n}d_{n}.\end{array}\right.

Under mild assumptions on
\bm{M}
and
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
also converges weakly to a point in
\mathcal{S}
. A long list of popular splitting methods can be recovered from warped resolvent iterations, such as FB, forward-backward-forward (FBF)
[

,

]
, forward-backward-half-forward (FBHF)
[

,

]
, Chambolle–Pock
[

,

]
, Condat–Vũ
[

,

,

, forward-reflected-backward (FRB)
[

, Douglas–Rachford
[

[

.

From a practical viewpoint, exact evaluations of proximal or resolvent operators are often computationally expensive or even impossible. Consequently, inexact proximal frameworks and relative-error criteria have become fundamental tools in modern optimization and monotone operator theory; see, for instance,
[
1
,
2
,
36
,
57
]
. In particular, the hybrid projection-proximal point method and the hybrid extragradient-proximal point method of Solodov and Svaiter
[
57
,
59
]
use approximate proximal information to construct separating hyperplanes or extragradient directions. Recently, an inexact version of the degenerate proximal point algorithm
[
10
]
was proposed in
[
2
]
and, as a consequence, inexact versions of Chambolle–Pock and the Davis–Yin method
[
35
]
were derived. For example, given
x\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
,
p=J_{\bm{A}}x
can be approximated by solving the system

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v\in\bm{A}p\\
e=v+x-p,\\
\end{cases}

where
e\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
is the error of the approximation. Note that, if
e=0
, then

and
p=J_{\bm{A}}x
.

In this paper, we introduce a relative-error warped resolvent framework based on geometrically generated separating halfspaces. The proposed methods admit a natural interpretation as relaxed projection schemes onto dynamically generated cuts, extending classical projection-proximal and hybrid extragradient proximal methodologies
[
57
,
58
,
60
]
to the nonlinear warped resolvent setting. Our setting incorporates variable metrics through two positive definite operators
\bm{P}
and
\bm{S}
, thereby providing substantial flexibility and allowing us to encompass several important algorithms from the literature within a unified geometric perspective. Our convergence analysis first establishes Fejér monotonicity, summability properties, and weak convergence of the generated iterates under mild assumptions. Moreover, by an adequate choice of the relaxation sequence
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, we also provide a warped resolvent algorithm with explicit step-sizes which has a simpler structure, which facilitates its numerical implementation. We further obtain strong convergence by incorporating projection steps onto intersections of separating halfspaces, in the spirit of Haugazeau-type methods. In addition, linear convergence is derived under a metric subregularity assumption. As a consequence, we derive inexact versions of several well-known splitting schemes including FB, FBF, FBHF, Chambolle–Pock, Condat–Vũ, and FPDHF. These methods allow for inexact evaluations of the backward steps under relative-error criteria. We provide applications in saddle-point problems and structured convex minimization. In addition, numerical implementations on large-scale synthetic saddle-point instances and computed tomography reconstruction are presented to illustrate the numerical advantages and practical performance of the proposed algorithms.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section we collect notation, definitions, and basic auxiliary results used throughout the paper. Let
\mathbb{R}
denote the set of real numbers. Throughout the paper,
\mathcal{H}
and
\mathcal{G}
denote real Hilbert spaces endowed with inner product
{\left\langle{{\cdot}\mid{\cdot}}\right\rangle}
and induced norm
\|\cdot\|
. We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
the set of bounded linear self-adjoint positive definite operators on
\mathcal{H}
. For
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, we define the norm
\|x\|_{P}:=\sqrt{{\left\langle{{x}\mid{Px}}\right\rangle}}
. Note that
(\mathcal{H},{\left\langle{{\cdot}\mid{\cdot}}\right\rangle}_{P})
is a real Hilbert space and we have

(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{2})\quad{\left\langle{{x}\mid{y}}\right\rangle}\leq\|x\|_{P^{-1}}\|y\|_{P}.

Given
\rho>0
and
\bar{x}\in\mathcal{H}
, we denote

B_{S}(\bar{x},\rho)=\{x\in\mathcal{H}:\|x-\bar{x}\|_{S}\leq\rho\}
. In addition, we denote
\lambda_{\min}(P)
and
\lambda_{\max}(P)
as the positive constants such that

(2.1)

(\forall x\in\mathcal{H})\quad\lambda_{\min}(P)\|x\|^{2}\leq\|x\|_{P}^{2}\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)\|x\|^{2}.

Let
C\subset\mathcal{H}
be a nonempty closed convex set and let
S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
. The distance from
x\in\mathcal{H}
to
C
with respect to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{S}
is defined by

d_{S}(x,C):=\inf_{y\in C}\|x-y\|_{S}.

Since
C
is closed and convex, the distance is attained at the metric projection

P_{C}^{S}(x):=\arg\min_{y\in C}\|x-y\|_{S},

and therefore

d_{S}(x,C)=\|x-P_{C}^{S}x\|_{S}.

The power set of
\mathcal{H}
is denoted by
2^{\mathcal{H}}
. For a set-valued operator
A:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
, its graph is defined by

\textnormal{gra}\,A:=\{(x,u)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\mid u\in Ax\}.

An operator
A:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
is said to be
monotone
if

(\forall((x,u),(y,v))\in(\textnormal{gra}\,A)^{2})\quad{\left\langle{{u-v}\mid{x-y}}\right\rangle}\geq 0.

Additionally,
A
is maximally monotone if it is monotone and its graph is
maximal in the sense of
inclusions among the graphs of monotone operators.
Given
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
, the operator
A
is said to be
\sigma
-strongly monotone with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{P}
if
\sigma>0
and

(\forall((x,u),(y,v))\in(\textnormal{gra}\,A)^{2})\quad{\left\langle{{u-v}\mid{x-y}}\right\rangle}\geq\sigma\|x-y\|_{P}^{2}.

We say that
A
is injective if for all
(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{2}
such that
Ax\cap Ay\neq\emptyset
implies that
x=y
. The standard resolvent of
A
is given by
J_{A}=(\textnormal{Id}+A)^{-1}
. An extension of the classical resolvent is given by the
warped resolvent

[
19
]
. Let
D\subset\mathcal{H}
be nonempty, let
U:D\to\mathcal{H}
, and let
M:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
be such that

\textnormal{ran}\,U\subset\textnormal{ran}\,(U+M)\quad\text{and}\quad U+M\text{ is injective}.

The
warped resolvent
of
M
with kernel
U
is defined by
J_{M}^{U}:=(U+M)^{-1}\circ U
. Equivalently, for every
x\in D
, the point
p=J_{M}^{U}x
is characterized by

Ux-Up\in Mp.

We refer the reader to
[
19
,
33
]
for further properties and illustrative examples.
Let
S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
,
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
, and
\beta>0
.
The operator
T:\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{H}
is
\beta
-cocoercive with respect to
S
if

(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{2})\quad{\left\langle{{Tx-Ty}\mid{x-y}}\right\rangle}\geq\beta\|Tx-Ty\|_{S^{-1}}^{2},

and it is
\beta
-Lipschitz with respect to
S
if

(\forall(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}^{2})\quad\|Tx-Ty\|_{S^{-1}}\leq L\|x-y\|_{S}.

In the case where
S=\textnormal{Id}
, we simply say that the operator is
\beta
-cocoercive (or
\beta
-Lipschitz, respectively).

Let
f:\mathcal{H}\to(-\infty,+\infty]
be an extended real-valued function. The domain of
f
is defined by
\textnormal{dom}\,f=\{x\in\mathcal{H}\mid f(x)<+\infty\}
. Recall that
f
is said to be proper if
\textnormal{dom}\,f\neq\emptyset
, and convex (respectively, lower semicontinuous (l.s.c.)) if
\textnormal{epi}\,f
is a convex (respectively, closed) subset of
\mathcal{H}\times\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
the class of all proper, convex, and lower semicontinuous functions on
\mathcal{H}
. The subdifferential of
f
is the set-valued operator
\partial f:\mathcal{H}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}}
defined by

\partial f(x)=\left\{v\in\mathcal{H}\;\middle|\;f(y)\geq f(x)+{\left\langle{{y-x}\mid{v}}\right\rangle}\quad\forall y\in\mathcal{H}\right\}.

If
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, then
\partial f
is a maximally monotone operator and
(\partial f)^{-1}=\partial f^{*}
, where
f^{*}\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
denotes the Fenchel conjugate of
f
. Furthermore, if
f
is differentiable and its gradient
\nabla f
is
\beta
-Lipschitz continuous, it is
\frac{1}{\beta}
-cocoercive
[
5
, Corollary 18.17]
.

We conclude this section with a standard projection formula that will be used in the convergence analysis of the proposed algorithms. It follows from the usual projection formula onto a closed affine halfspace applied to the Hilbert space

(\mathcal{H},{\left\langle{{\cdot}\mid{\cdot}}\right\rangle}_{S})
; see for instance
[
5
, Example 29.20]
.

Lemma 2.1

(General Projection onto Affine Halfspace)
.

Let
\varphi:\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{R}
be a nonconstant affine function with
\nabla\varphi\neq 0
.
Let
H=\{z\in\mathcal{H}:\varphi(z)\leq 0\}
be a halfspace, and let
\|\cdot\|_{S}
be a norm induced by some
S\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{H})
.
Then the projection of
x\in\mathcal{H}
onto
H
is given by:

P^{S}_{H}(x)=x-\frac{\max\{0,\varphi(x)\}}{\|\nabla\varphi\|_{S^{-1}}^{2}}S^{-1}\nabla\varphi.

3.
Inexact Warped Resolvent iterations

In this section we introduce the inexact warped resolvent algorithm and prove its weak convergence to a solution to Problem
1.1
.

In the context of Problem
1.1
, let
\bm{M}\colon{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
and suppose that
(\bm{M}+\bm{C}+\bm{A})^{-1}
is well-defined. Given
x\in\bm{\mathcal{H}}
, the warped resolvent step of the algorithm in (
1.4
) is given by

(3.1)

x_{+}=\bm{J}_{\bm{A}+\bm{C}}^{\bm{M}}(x)=(\bm{M}+\bm{C}+\bm{A})^{-1}(\bm{M}x).

This step can be equivalently characterized by the inclusion-equation system

(3.2)

\displaystyle\textnormal{find }\quad(y,x_{+})\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}^{2}\quad\textnormal{ such that }\quad\begin{cases}v\in\bm{A}x_{+}\\[2.84526pt]
v+\bm{M}x_{+}-\bm{M}x+\bm{C}x_{+}=0.\end{cases}

Motivated by this formulation, and the relative-error framework introduced in
[
57
,
60
]
, we define the following notion of approximate solution to (
3.2
).

Definition 3.1
.

A pair
(w,v)\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\times{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
is called a
\sigma
-approximate solution
of the system (
3.2
) if
\sigma\in[0,1[
and

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}v\in\bm{A}w\\
e=v+\bm{M}w-\bm{M}x+\bm{C}w,\\
\|e\|_{\bm{P}^{-1}}\leq\sigma\|w-x\|_{\bm{P}}.\end{cases}

When
\sigma=0
, condition (
3.3
) reduces to the exact system (
3.2
), and hence to exact warped resolvent step (
3.1
). In this sense, the residual
e
is controlled
relative
to the displacement
w-x
, in the spirit of relative-error proximal methods. This notion of approximate solution naturally leads to the inexact warped resolvent algorithm. Before presenting the algorithm, we introduce the standing assumptions ensuring its well-definedness.

Assumption 3.2
.

In the context of Problem
1.1
consider the following assumptions.

(1)

Let
\bm{S}\in\mathcal{P}({\bm{\mathcal{H}}})
and
\bm{P}\in\mathcal{P}({\bm{\mathcal{H}}})
.

(2)

Suppose that
\bm{C}
is
\beta
-cocoercive with respect to
\bm{P}
for
\beta\in\left]0,+\infty\right[
.

(3)

Let
(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
[\underline{\lambda},\overline{\lambda}]\subset\penalty\ ]0,2[
.

(4)

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\bm{M}_{n}\colon{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
be an operator such that
\textnormal{ran}\,\bm{M}_{n}\subset\textnormal{ran}\,(\bm{M}_{n}+\bm{A}+\bm{C})
.

The next algorithm is the inexact warped resolvent version of the algorithm in
[
33
, Eq. (4.34)]
.

Algorithm 3.3
.

In the context of Problem
1.1
and Assumption
3.2
. Let
x_{0}\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
and consider the following recurrence.

(3.4)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{find }(w_{n},v_{n})\in\textnormal{gra}\,\bm{A}\textnormal{ such that }\\
\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}w_{n}^{*}=\bm{M}_{n}x_{n}-\bm{M}_{n}w_{n}-\bm{C}w_{n}\\
e_{n}=v_{n}-w_{n}^{*}\\
\|e_{n}\|_{\bm{P}^{-1}}\leq\sigma\|w_{n}-x_{n}\|_{\bm{P}}\end{array}\right.\\
t_{n}^{*}=v_{n}+\bm{C}x_{n}\\
\delta_{n}={\left\langle{{x_{n}-w_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{*}}}\right\rangle}-\frac{1}{4\beta}\|w_{n}-x_{n}\|^{2}_{{\bm{P}}}\\
d_{n}=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\delta_{n}}{\|t^{*}_{n}\|^{2}_{\bm{S}^{-1}}}\bm{S}^{-1}t^{*}_{n},&\textnormal{ if }\delta_{n}>0;\\
0,&\textnormal{ otherwise }\end{cases}\\
x_{n+1}=x_{n}-\lambda_{n}d_{n}.\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Remark 3.4
.

(1)

The condition
\textnormal{ran}\,\bm{M}_{n}\subset\textnormal{ran}\,(\bm{M}_{n}+\bm{A}+\bm{C})
in Assumption
3.2
.
4
guarantees that the system in (
3.3
) admits an exact solution. This ensures that the iterations of Algorithm
3.3
are well-defined. Unlike the frameworks in
[
19
,
33
]
, we do not require the operator
\bm{M}_{n}+\bm{A}+\bm{C}
to be injective.

(2)

In the case that
\bm{S}={\bf Id}
and
\sigma=0
, Algorithm
3.3
coincides with the algorithm in
[
33
, Eq. (4.34)]
with
q_{n}\equiv x_{n}
. Moreover, it reduces to the algorithm proposed in
[
19
]
if, additionally, we set
\bm{C}=0
. On the other hand, if
\sigma=0
and we set
\bm{M}_{n}=\widetilde{\bm{M}}_{n}-\bm{C}
, where
\widetilde{\bm{M}}_{n}\colon{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}\to{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
is a strongly monotone operator, Algorithm
3.3
reduces to the algorithm studied in
[
39
]
.

(3)

Algorithm
3.3
admits the following geometric interpretation as a projection method onto a sequence of separating half-spaces. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define the half-space

(3.5)

\bm{H}_{n}=\big\{{z\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}}\penalty\ \big|\penalty\ {{\left\langle{{z-w_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{*}}}\right\rangle}\leq\frac{1}{4\beta}\|w_{n}-x_{n}\|^{2}_{\bm{P}}}\big\}.

Then, the update
x_{n+1}
coincides with a relaxed projection of
x_{n}
onto
\bm{H}_{n}
with respect to the metric induced by
\bm{S}
. Moreover, the construction of
(w_{n},v_{n})
together with the relative error condition, ensures that the solution set
\mathcal{S}
is contained in
\bm{H}_{n}
. Hence
\bm{H}_{n}
separates the current iterate
x_{n}
from
\mathcal{S}
whenever
\delta_{n}>0
, as shown below. Consequently, Algorithm
3.3
can be viewed as a cutting-plane method that generates a sequence of outer approximations
(\bm{H}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
of the solution set and performs (relaxed) projections onto these half-spaces. This perspective is closely related to the geometric framework of projection methods for monotone inclusions; see, e.g.,
[
32
,
33
,
58
]
.

Theorem 3.5
.

In the context of Problem
1.1
and Assumption
3.2
. Let
x_{0}\in{\bm{\mathcal{H}}}
and consider the sequence
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
generated by Algorithm
3.3
. Then the following hold:

(1)

\textnormal{zer}(\bm{A}+\bm{C})\subset\bm{H}_{n}
, where
\bm{H}_{n}
is defined as in (
3.5
).

(2)

For each
x\in\textnormal{zer}(\bm{A}+\bm{C})
, the sequence
(\|x_{n}-x\|_{\bm{S}})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges.

(3)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\|d_{n}\|^{2}_{\bm{S}}<+\infty
and hence
d_{n}\to 0
;

(4)

Assume, in addition, that one of the following conditions is satisfied:

(a)

w_{n}-x_{n}\to 0
and
\bm{M}_{n}w_{n}-\bm{M}_{n}x_{n}\to 0
;

(b)

\bm{M}_{n}
is
\alpha
-strongly monotone with respect to
{\bm{P}}
for
\alpha\in\penalty\ ]1/(4\beta)+\sigma,+\infty[
and
\zeta
-Lipschitz continuous for
\zeta\in\left]0,+\infty\right[
.

Then,
(x_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
converges weakly to a point in
\mathcal{S}
.

Proof.

Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows from (
3.4
) and Lemma
2.1
that

(3.6)

x_{n+1}=x_{n}+\lambda_{n}(P_{\bm{H}_{n}}^{\bm{S}}(x_{n})-x_{n}).

Moreover, for any
z\in\mathcal{S}
, we have
-\bm{C}z\in\bm{A}z
and from (
3.4
) that
v_{n}\in\bm{A}w_{n}
. Therefore, by the monotonicity of
\bm{A}
we deduce that

(3.7)

{\left\langle{{v_{n}+\bm{C}z}\mid{w_{n}-z}}\right\rangle}\geq 0.

Furthermore, by (
3.7
), the cocoercivity of
\bm{C}
with respect to
{\bm{P}}
, and combining Cauchy–Schwarz and Young’s inequality, we deduce

\displaystyle{\left\langle{{z-w_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{*}}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{{v_{n}+\bm{C}x_{n}}\mid{z-w_{n}}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{{v_{n}+\bm{C}x_{n}+\bm{C}z-\bm{C}z}\mid{z-w_{n}}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\sqrt{\gamma\tau}\|L\|}\left(\|z\|^{2}+\frac{\tau}{\gamma}\|v\|^{2}\right).

In addition, for every

\displaystyle\|(x,u)\|^{2}_{\bm{S}}

\displaystyle={\left\langle{{(x-\gamma L^{*}u,\gamma u/\tau-\gamma Lx)}\mid{(x,u)}}\right\rangle}

\displaystyle=\|x\|^{2}-2\gamma{\left\langle{{Lx}\mid{u}}\right\rangle}+\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\|u\|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\|x\|^{2}-\sqrt{\gamma\tau}\|L\|\|x\|^{2}-\frac{\gamma\sqrt{\gamma}\|L\|}{\sqrt{\tau}}\|u\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\|u\|^{2}

(4.21)

\displaystyle=(1-\sqrt{\gamma\tau}\|L\|)\left(\|x\|^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{\tau}\|u\|^{2}\right).

Therefore, by (

), the condition on the error in Algorithm

5.
Applications and numerical experiments

In this section, we provide practical applications and numerical experiments to show the advantages of incorporating approximations in the computation of the resolvent. We present two classes of problems: saddle-point and convex optimization problems. While both formulations can be mathematically linked via Fenchel-Rockafellar duality, they represent fundamentally different modeling paradigms that necessitate separate treatment. The saddle-point formulation is native to adversarial and equilibrium-seeking settings, such as zero-sum games, robust optimization, and generative adversarial networks. In these contexts, the dual variable is not an auxiliary construct, but a primary decision entity (e.g., a competing player or an adversary) endowed with its own structural constraints. All numerical experiments were implemented in MATLAB on a desktop computer equipped with an Intel Core i7-14700K processor (3.4/5.6 GHz), 64 GB of RAM, and running Windows 11 Pro 64-bit. The code is available in this
repository
.

5.1.
Saddle-Point Problems

In this subsection, we consider the numerical solution of the following saddle-point problem.

Problem 5.1
.

Let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
,
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{G})
,
L\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
be a bounded linear operator. The problem is to

(5.1)

\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}\max_{y\in\mathcal{G}}f(x)+{\left\langle{{Lx}\mid{y}}\right\rangle}-g(y),

under the assumption that its solution set is nonempty.

This problem encompasses several applications such as zero-sum games
[
66
]
, robust optimization
[
6
]
, generalized lasso problems
[
61
,
62
]
, and generative adversarial networks
[
38
,
49
]
, among others. Problem
5.1
is equivalent to

(5.2)

\text{find}\quad(x,y)\in\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\quad\text{ such that }\quad(0,0)\in A(x,y)+D(x,y),

where

\displaystyle A\colon\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\to 2^{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}}\colon(x,y)\mapsto\partial f(x)\times\partial g(y)

\displaystyle D\colon\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}\to{\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{G}}\colon(x,y)\mapsto(L^{*}y,-Lx).

We have that
A
is maximally monotone and
D
is monotone and
\|L\|
-Lipschitz, thus, Problem
5.1
can be solved by the FBF algorithm and its inexact resolvent versions, namely, Algorithm
4.2
(IFBF
inexact FBF
) and Algorithm
4.4
(EIFBF
explicit inexact FBF
) for
C=0
. Particularly, Algorithm
4.2
reduces to

(5.3)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{ find }(z_{n}^{1},y_{n}^{1})\in\textnormal{gra}\,\partial f\textnormal{ and }(z_{n}^{2},y_{n}^{2})\in\textnormal{gra}\,\partial g\textnormal{ such that }\\
\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}(z_{n}^{1,*},z_{n}^{2,*})=(x_{n}^{1}/\gamma-L^{*}x^{2}_{n}-z_{n}^{1}/\gamma,x_{n}^{2}/\gamma+Lx^{1}_{n}-z_{n}^{2}/\gamma)\\
(e_{n}^{1},e_{n}^{2})=(y_{n}^{1}-z_{n}^{1,*},y_{n}^{2}-z_{n}^{2,*})\\
\|e_{n}^{1}\|^{2}+\|e_{n}^{2}\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}(\|z_{n}^{1}-x_{n}^{1}\|^{2}+\|z_{n}^{2}-x_{n}^{2}\|^{2}),\end{array}\right.\\
(t_{n}^{1,*},t_{n}^{2,*})=(y_{n}^{1}+L^{*}z_{n}^{2},y_{n}^{2}-Lz_{n}^{1})\\
\delta_{n}={\left\langle{{x_{n}^{1}-z_{n}^{1}}\mid{t_{n}^{1,*}}}\right\rangle}+{\left\langle{{x_{n}^{2}-z_{n}^{2}}\mid{t_{n}^{2,*}}}\right\rangle}\\
(d_{n}^{1},d_{n}^{2})=\begin{cases}\dfrac{\delta_{n}}{\|(t_{n}^{1,*},t_{n}^{2,*})\|^{2}}(t_{n}^{1,*},t_{n}^{2,*}),&\textnormal{ if }\delta_{n}>0;\\
0,&\textnormal{ otherwise }\end{cases}\\
(x_{n+1}^{1},x_{n+1}^{2})=(x_{n}^{1},x_{n}^{2})-\lambda_{n}(d_{n}^{1},d_{n}^{2}).\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Similarly, it is possible to derive the explicit version of Algorithm
4.4
, which is omitted here for the sake of conciseness. To exhibit the advantages of allowing approximations in the computation of the resolvent, we consider the following numerical example.

5.1.1.
Numerical implementation

To numerically compare Algorithm
4.2

\begin{array}[]{l}(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{if }\quad\|r^{k}\|\leq\sigma\|p^{k}-x_{n}^{1}\|\quad\textnormal{ return}\\[5.69054pt]

r^{k+1}=r^{k}-\alpha_{k}\mathcal{M}d^{k}\\

5.2.
Convex Optimization Problems

We focus on the following convex optimization problem.

Problem 5.2
.

Let
L\colon\mathcal{H}\to\mathcal{G}
be a
bounded linear operator, let
f\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
,
h\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{H})
, and
g\in\Gamma_{0}(\mathcal{G})
. Suppose that
h
is differentiable with a
(1/\beta)
-Lipschitz continuous
gradient. The problem is to

(5.5)

\min_{x\in\mathcal{H}}f(x)+g(Lx)+h(x)

under the assumption that its solution set is nonempty.

This optimization problem encompasses applications in data science
[
29
]
, machine learning
[
9
]
, image processing

[
8
,
14
,
21
,
22
]
, among others.
If
0\in\text{\rm sri}\,(\textnormal{dom}\,g-L(\textnormal{dom}\,f))
, by considering
A=\partial f
,
B=\partial g
,
C=\nabla h
, and
D=0
, Problem
5.2
is a particular instance of Problem
4.1

[
5
, Theorem 27.2]
. Therefore, this problem can be solved by the Condat–Vũ algorithm which needs to evaluate
\text{\rm prox}\,_{\tau f}
and
\text{\rm prox}\,_{\sigma g^{*}}
, where
(\tau,\sigma)\in\left]0,+\infty\right[
. Similarly to previous subsection, in the case that
\text{\rm prox}\,_{\tau f}
has a high computational cost, it is desirable to consider an approximation of it to decrease the computational time.
In this scenario, considering the operators
A
,
B
,
C
, and
D
defined above, Algorithm
4.8
with exact resolvent on
B
(see (
4.16
)) reduces to the following sequence:

(5.6)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{ find }(p_{n},y_{n})\in\textnormal{gra}\,\partial f\textnormal{ such that }\\
\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}p_{n}^{*}=x_{n}/\gamma-\nabla h(x_{n})-L^{*}u_{n}-p_{n}/\gamma\\
q_{n}=\text{\rm prox}\,_{\tau g^{*}}\left(u_{n}+\tau L(2p_{n}-x_{n}-\gamma(y_{n}-p_{n}^{*}))\right)\\
\|y_{n}-p_{n}^{*}\|\leq\sigma\|(p_{n}-x_{n},q_{n}-u_{n})\|_{\bm{S}},\end{array}\right.\\
(a_{n},b_{n})=(y_{n}+Cx_{n}+L^{*}u_{n},(u_{n}-q_{n})/\gamma)\\
(t_{n}^{1},t_{n}^{2})=\bm{S}(a_{n},b_{n})\\
\delta_{n}={\left\langle{{x_{n}-p_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{1}}}\right\rangle}+{\left\langle{{u_{n}-q_{n}}\mid{t_{n}^{2}}}\right\rangle}-\frac{1}{4\widehat{\beta}}\|(x_{n}-p_{n},u_{n}-q_{n})\|^{2}_{{\bm{S}}}\\

0,&\textnormal{ otherwise }\end{cases}\\
(x_{n+1},u_{n+1})=(x_{n},u_{n})-\lambda_{n}d_{n},\end{array}\right.\end{array}

where

is defined as in (

4.11
with exact resolvent on

(see (

)) reduces to

(5.7)

(\forall n\in\mathbb{N})\quad\begin{array}[]{l}\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{ find }(p_{n},y_{n})\in\textnormal{gra}\,\partial f\\

5.2.1.
Computed Tomography Reconstruction

A particular instance of Problem
5.2
is the image reconstruction problem arising, for example, in Computed Tomography (CT)
[
43
]
. In particular, let
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{R}^{N}
,
\mathcal{G}=\mathbb{R}^{M}
, and consider
c\in\mathbb{R}^{M}
a noisy tomographic projection of an image
\overline{x}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
. The objective is to reconstruct
\overline{x}
from the observation
c
. We assume that

c=T(\overline{x})+\epsilon

where
T\in\mathbb{R}^{M\times N}
is the discretized Radon projector and
\epsilon
represents Gaussian noise. An approach to recover
\overline{x}
is to solve the following optimization problem:

(5.8)

\min_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}F(x):=\frac{1}{2}\|Tx-c\|_{2}^{2}+\lambda_{1}H_{\delta}(Wx)+\lambda_{2}\|\nabla x\|_{1}

where, for a given
\delta>0

H_{\delta}
is the Huber function defined by

(5.9)

(\forall x=(x_{i})_{1\leq i\leq N}\in\mathbb{R}^{N})\quad H_{\delta}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\phi_{\delta}(x_{i})\ \textnormal{ and }\ (\forall\eta\in\mathbb{R})\quad\phi_{\delta}(\eta)=\begin{cases}|\eta|-\frac{\delta}{2},&\text{ if }|\eta|>\delta,\\
\frac{\eta^{2}}{2\delta},&\text{ otherwise},\end{cases}

W\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times N}
is an orthonormal wavelet transform,
\nabla
is the discrete gradient with Neumann boundary conditions, and
(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2})\in\left]0,+\infty\right[^{2}

\lambda_{1}H_{\delta}\circ W
and
\lambda_{2}\|\nabla\cdot\|_{1}
promote sparsity of the image to be recovered while
\frac{1}{2}\|T(\cdot)-c\|_{2}^{2}
acts as the data fidelity term. We have that
H_{\delta}
is differentiable and its gradient is
(1/\delta)
-Lipschitz continuous. Explicit formulas for
\nabla H_{\delta}
and
\text{\rm prox}\,_{H_{\delta}}
can be found in
[
17
]
and, by the orthogonality of

\text{\rm prox}\,_{H_{\delta}\circ W}

[

24

44

46

55

Since

\sigma=0

\partial f=T^{*}T-T^{*}c
in (

\gamma(y_{n}-p_{n}^{*})=(\textnormal{Id}+\gamma T^{*}T)p_{n}-x_{n}+\gamma(\nabla h(x_{n})+L^{*}u_{n})-\gamma T^{*}c.

(5.10)

\begin{array}[]{l}(\forall k\in\mathbb{N})\quad\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\textnormal{if }\quad\|r^{k}\|\leq\sigma\sqrt{(1-\gamma\tau\|L\|^{2})}\|p^{k}-x_{n}\|\quad\textnormal{ return}\\[5.69054pt]

\textnormal{otherwise}\\[5.69054pt]
\left\lfloor\begin{array}[]{l}\alpha_{k}=\dfrac{\|r^{k}\|^{2}}{(d^{k})^{\top}{\mathcal{M}d^{k}}}\\

6.
Conclusion

In this article, we have proposed an inexact warped resolvent framework for structured monotone inclusions, based on relative-error evaluations and separating halfspace constructions. This geometric viewpoint provides a unified interpretation of several splitting schemes and allows for inexact backward computations in both standard and primal–dual settings. The proposed methods are shown to converge weakly under mild assumptions, strong convergence via Haugazeau-type projection steps, and linear convergence under metric subregularity. Numerical experiments on saddle-point problems and computed tomography reconstruction illustrate the flexibility of the framework and its practical potential in significantly reducing computational costs for large-scale problems.
