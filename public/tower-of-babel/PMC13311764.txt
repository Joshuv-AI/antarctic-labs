Introduction

Kidney failure is a major health problem, with a global prevalence of 823 per million population treated with maintenance haemodialysis (HD).1 The high economic burden and the elevated mortality of the condition often overshadow the effects on quality of life in kidney failure. Indeed, cognitive impairment is common in people with kidney failure undergoing maintenance HD. It affects up to 40%–50% of patients across domains including attention, executive function, memory and visuospatial abilities.2 In the large observational study ‘Cognitive HD’, assessing HD patients, mild cognitive impairment was extremely common (up to 80% of participants). The impairment was observed across multiple domains, including complex attention, executive function, language, learning/memory and perceptual-motor abilities.2

This burden is exacerbated by vascular injury, chronic inflammation and uraemia, contributing to poor quality of life and increased mortality.3 Changes in brain water handling accompanying kidney failure might also be responsible for the altered cognitive functions.4 Despite its high prevalence and impact, effective interventions for cognitive dysfunction in this population remain limited.

The international, multicentre, prospective, randomised, controlled study comparing high-dose haemodiafiltration (HDF) versus conventional high flux HD (CONVINCE, n=1360) found that HDF positively impacts cognitive function.5,8 It is also known that other non-pharmacological strategies such as cognitive training may enhance cognitive reserve and improve functioning.9 Emerging evidence suggests that immersive virtual reality (VR)-based interventions may be effective and engaging for cognitive training in other high-risk populations, such as older adults with mild cognitive impairment.10 11 However, the feasibility, acceptability and safety of such interventions in people receiving HD for kidney replacement therapy are limited. Recently, a randomised study on 54 HD patients showed that VR headsets improved patient comfort (the study was not aimed at analysing the effect on cognition),12 particularly during arteriovenous fistula cannulation.13 14 Similarly, VR headsets were also tested in these patients 1 day before the session (n=47) with improvement in pruritus and anxiety.15 A prospective study on VR used as a method to assess cognitive performance in HD is ongoing.16 VR has also been used as ‘art therapy’ in HD patients to improve emotional state.17

VIRTUAL is a randomised controlled trial designed to address this evidence gap by evaluating whether immersive VR-based cognitive training is acceptable and feasible in adults receiving HD. The primary objective of this pilot trial is to evaluate the feasibility and acceptability of immersive VR cognitive training during HD sessions. Key questions include the willingness of clinicians to offer and patients to engage with the intervention during dialysis, the tolerability of VR equipment, adherence over a 12-week period, and the viability of cognitive outcome assessments in this setting. This feasibility study will inform the design and conduct of a larger definitive randomised controlled trial to assess clinical efficacy. Publication of this feasibility protocol is important to inform the design, acceptability and implementation challenges of VR interventions during HD, an area with limited prior methodological evidence.

Trial design

Experimental intervention

Participants randomised to the intervention arm will receive VR-based cognitive training using the Enhance VR platform (Virtuleap, Lisbon, Portugal) via Meta Quest 3S head-mounted displays (Meta Platforms, California, USA). The hardware features a Snapdragon XR2 Gen2 processor, 8 GB RAM, 128 GB internal memory, and a resolution of 1832×1920 pixels per eye, with a refresh rate of 90–120 Hz and motion controllers.

Each session will last no more than 30 min and will consist exclusively of exercises requiring unilateral hand movements; this ensures that the arm with the arteriovenous fistula remains still during the intervention (see table 1). A trained clinician will supervise every session to ensure safety and protocol adherence. All site personnel involved in intervention delivery will receive standardised training before participant enrolment. Intervention delivery will be supervised by trained clinical staff following a predefined session manual.

Study assessments

Baseline cognitive function will be characterised using the MoCA, together with patient-reported outcome measures including EQ-5D-5L, BDI and GAD-7. Neuropsychological assessments will be performed before the start of the dialysis session or during the first hour of treatment, according to local logistical feasibility, to minimise the potential effects of intradialytic fatigue, hypotension or postdialysis exhaustion on cognitive performance.

Harms and adverse events

Adverse events will be actively monitored at each intervention session by trained dialysis staff using a predefined checklist. Events of interest include nausea, vomiting, vertigo, visual disturbance, headset discomfort and vascular access complications (eg, dislodgement or bleeding).

Serious adverse events (eg, hospitalisation, life-threatening events) will be recorded and reported within 24 hours to the coordinating investigator. All adverse events will be summarised descriptively by study group.

Sample size

A total sample of 60 participants (30 per group) was chosen in line with recommendations for pilot and feasibility randomised trials, which suggest 24–35 participants per arm to estimate recruitment, adherence, and retention parameters with adequate precision.

Recruitment

Recruitment will occur over a 12-week period, with dialysis centre staff identifying and approaching eligible patients. Informed consent will be obtained by trained investigators.

Randomisation and allocation concealment

Participants will be randomly allocated in a 1:1 ratio to VR cognitive training plus standard care or standard care alone.

The allocation sequence was generated using a computer-based random number generator with a fixed seed (20251028) to ensure reproducibility. Randomisation uses stratified permuted blocks with variable block sizes of 4 and 6 and is stratified by study site.

The allocation list was prepared centrally by an independent statistician not involved in participant recruitment, intervention delivery or outcome assessment, prior to recruitment and implemented through a secure web-based system. Investigators enrolling participants do not have access to the allocation sequence. Treatment assignment is revealed only after participant enrolment and baseline assessment to ensure allocation concealment.

Participant identifiers follow a predefined numbering convention (SITE-### incremental format; eg, SITE1-001) to maintain tracking and balance across sites.

Blinding

The trial is open-label with respect to participants and intervention staff. Outcome assessors and data analysts will remain blinded to group allocation throughout data analysis.

Data collection and management

All data will be entered into a REDCap (www.project-redcap.org) database. Site staff will maintain detailed logs of intervention delivery and adherence.

Data security and participant confidentiality will be maintained in compliance with General Data Protection Regulation (GDPR) and local regulations. Site staff will maintain detailed logs of intervention delivery and adherence, including completed sessions, session duration, task completion, interruptions, temporary pauses and early terminations. Periodic central review of intervention logs will be conducted to assess consistency of protocol implementation across sites and to better characterise usability and tolerability of the VR intervention during dialysis.

Statistical methods

All analyses will follow the intention-to-treat principle. Feasibility outcomes will be summarised using descriptive statistics with 95% CIs.

Predefined thresholds for feasibility include:

A recruitment rate ≥33% that indicates trial feasibility.

A recruitment rate ≥33% that indicates trial feasibility.

An intervention adherence ≥80% that supports protocol viability.

An intervention adherence ≥80% that supports protocol viability.

A participant retention rate ≥90% at end of treatment that suggests follow-up acceptability.

A participant retention rate ≥90% at end of treatment that suggests follow-up acceptability.

Feasibility thresholds were defined a priori based on published methodological recommendations for pilot trials and pragmatic considerations related to the dialysis population. These thresholds will not be used in isolation but as part of a traffic-light system to guide decisions regarding progression to a definitive trial, informing ‘go’, ‘amend’ or ‘stop’ decisions based on observed practical challenges and participant feedback.

Missing data will be summarised descriptively by treatment group and outcome category, including the extent and reasons for missing assessments whenever available. No formal imputation procedures are planned given the feasibility nature of the trial.

No formal hypothesis testing or interim analyses are planned. Exploratory comparisons of outcome completion rates and adverse events will be conducted descriptively.
