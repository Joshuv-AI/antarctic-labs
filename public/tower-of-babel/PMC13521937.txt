Potentially functional variants of Wnt signaling pathway genes predict risk of primary lung cancer in Chinese Han population
Authors: Xiaohui Chen, Qiuping Xu, Linlin Huang, Wencong Hong, Tingshan Xu, Liying Yu, Yiming Zeng, Fei He, Yuan Xu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wnt signaling is a highly conserved pathway that regulates cell proliferation, differentiation, apoptosis and stem cell self-renewal, which has implications for embryonic development and tumorigenesis. We investigated the potential role of candidate single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) of Wnt signaling pathway genes with computational evidence of regulatory potential on lung cancer in Chinese Han patients. The case–control study included 1,143 diagnosed primary lung cancer patients and 1,172 healthy controls. It was identified that Wnt2 rs4730775, Axin1 rs1981492 and Dvl2 rs222851 were associated with increased risk of lung cancer (adjusted Odds Ratio [ORadj] = 1.227, 95% confidence interval [CI] = 1.020–1.477; ORadj = 1.460, 95% CI = 1.019–2.090; ORadj = 1.477, 95% CI = 1.200–1.819, respectively). Dvl2 rs222851 was associated with a noticeably increased risk of lung cancer using dominant model by multi-classification logistic regression analysis (ORadj = 1.437, 95% CI = 1.178–1.754). Meanwhile, it enhanced the predictive ability of lung cancer incidence with the area under the Receiver Operating Characteristic Curve (AUROC) increased to 0.742 (95% CI = 0.721–0.762). Additionally, the crossover analysis revealed that Dvl2 rs222851 risk genotypes along with cooking oil fume exposure increased lung cancer risk by 1.514-fold (ORadj = 2.514, 95% CI = 1.647–3.839). There was positive multiplicative interaction between rs222851 and cooking oil fume exposure (ORadj = 1.174, 95% CI = 1.055–1.306). Our findings indicated that the genetic mutation of Wnt2 rs4730775, Axin1 rs1981492 and Dvl2 rs222851 on Wnt signaling pathway could promote the occurrence of lung cancer in Chinese Han population. Additionally, the Dvl2 rs222851 variant was significantly associated with exposure to cooking oil fumes.

Introduction

Lung cancer (LC) remains the leading cause of cancer-related mortality worldwide, accounting for approximately 1.8 million deaths annually1. In recent years, remarkable progress has been achieved in the field of targeted anticancer drugs and immunotherapy for lung cancer, but early screening remains challenge. Most patients diagnosed with lung cancer typically present advanced-stage disease due to rapid progression and metastasis. Continuous advances in biomedical technology have led to the discovery of biomarkers for tumor diagnosis, treatment, prognosis and risk assessment. Exploring the susceptibility markers of lung cancer has far-reaching significance for improving the prevention and treatment level.

Four decades of research since the discovery of the first Wnt gene has identified that the Wnt pathway is central to a host of developmental and disease-related processes2,3. Wnt signaling pathway is a highly conserved signaling pathway, which plays an important role in stem cell development and tissue homeostasis regulation4. It is mainly activated in the process of embryonic development, which precisely regulates the growth and development of the body. Meanwhile, it not only maintains the stable state of stem cells, but also regulate the self-renewal and proliferation of stem cells5. Genetic mutation and dysregulation of Wnt signaling pathway are closely related to cancer stem cells6. Wnt signaling pathway can be divided into classical pathway and non-classical pathway. At present, most studies suggested that classical pathway can regulate cell proliferation and activation, which is closely related to the occurrence and development of a variety of tumors7. It had been reported that the mutations in Wnt pathway components were related to many human cancers, including lung cancer8, gastric cancer9, colorectal cancer10, breast cancer11 and liver cancer12.

Single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) constitute more than 90% of the genetic variation of human genome13. SNP, related genes of Wnt signaling pathway, can cause the variation of signaling pathway regulation ability, and then drive stem cells to transform into tumor stem cells, and continuously activate the signaling pathway related to self-renewal and proliferation of tumor stem cells, so as to differentiate and produce tumor cells, maintain tumor growth and promote tumor cell proliferation14. Therefore, we speculate that SNPs of related genes on this signaling pathway might regulate the expression of key genes and their downstream genes, which might lead to stem cell dysfunction and change the susceptibility of lung cancer. Similarly, the susceptibility to lung cancer was different among the people with the same genotype. To a certain degree, this difference might be due to the influence of genetic factors on environmental risk factors. Therefore, exploring the interaction between gene and environment may be a good way to understand the cause of cancer. This study discusses the relationship between Wnt signal pathway related gene SNPs and lung cancer susceptibility, and investigates the interaction between gene and environment, to provide theoretical support for the improvement of lung cancer prevention and control strategies.

Materials and methods

Study subjects

Subjects were consecutively recruited from three hospitals (First Affiliated Hospital of Fujian Medical University, Fujian Medical University Union Hospital, and Fuzhou General Hospital of Nanjing Military Command) between January 2006 and December 2012. We enrolled 1,143 patients diagnosed primary lung cancer (histologically or cytologically confirmed primary lung cancer, newly diagnosed, no prior anticancer treatment) and 1,172 healthy controls. Additionally, healthy individuals from the community, matched for gender and age (± 3 years), were included, all of whom had resided in Fujian for more than 10 years. This study was approved by the Institutional Review Board of Fujian Medical University (Fuzhou, China) and was performed according to the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. All participants provided informed written consent.

Data and sample collections

We collected epidemiological data through person-to-person interviews using a standardized questionnaire, including general demographic characteristics, body mass index (BMI), smoking, drinking tea, fruits intake, exposure to cooking fumes and pollution of residential areas. Certainly, all variables were clearly defined. Smoking was defined as accumulated smoking greater than 100 cigarettes. Drinking tea was drinking at least 1 cup per week for more than half a year. Fruits intake included < 3 times/week and ≥ 3 times/week. Cooking oil fume exposure was assessed by trained interviewers using a standardized, in-person questionnaire. Exposure was defined as self-reported regular cooking activities (stir-frying, deep-frying, or pan-frying) for ≥ 1 year at a frequency of ≥ 3 times per week, regardless of ventilation hood use.

A 5 mL pre-treatment blood sample was collected from each individual for genomic DNA extraction. The sample was taken in the laboratory using the Oragene DNA self-collection kit (DNA Genotek, OraSure Technologies, Inc., Ottawa, ON, Canada).

Selection and genotyping of SNPs

Based on data in Genebank (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/Genebank), dbSNP (http://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/SNP), and the International HapMap database (http://www.hapmap.org), we selected SNPs to explore the association between SNPs in Wnt signaling and the susceptibility of primary lung cancer. SNPs were selected using a tagSNP strategy applying: (1) MAF ≥ 0.10 in East Asian (CHB/JPT) HapMap populations; (2) r2 > 0.8 for adequate LD block coverage; (3) cross-referencing with RegulomeDB and GTEx to prioritize variants with evidence of regulatory function. Exclusion criteria: MAF < 0.10; r2 < 0.8; monomorphic in pilot sample; located in repetitive regions incompatible with Sequenom MALDI-TOF assay design. SNPs genotyping was conducted using matrix-assisted laser desorption and ionization time-of-flflight mass spectrometry (MALDITOF-MS) with the Sequenom platform according to the manufacturer’s iPLEX Application Guide (Sequenom Inc, San Diego, CA, USA). We analyzed results using the MassArrayTyper 4.0 Software. After processed all test samples, we selected 10% of samples at random and reran for quality control purposes that genotyping call rates were greater than 90%.

Statistical methods

Differences in general demographic characteristics distributions between the case and control groups were established by the Chi-squared test (χ2). The genotypes distribution was analyzed to determine the distribution of SNP accorded with Hardy–Weinberg’s law of genetic balance. A binary unconditional logistic regression model was conducted to analyze the association of each site with lung cancer under the co-dominant, dominant, and recessive genetic models. We applied the Benjamini-Hochberg FDR correction to all 15 P-values (5 SNPs×3 genetic models). The stepwise logistic regression model was used to confirm independent predictive factors of the lung cancer prognosis. The Odds Ratio (OR), adjusted OR (ORadj), and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. We have performed comprehensive in silico annotation using RegulomeDB v2 (GRCh38) and GTEx Analysis Release V10. In addition, the multiplication interaction between the SNPs and epidemiologic factors was analyzed by logistic regression model. The Receiver Operating Characteristic (ROC) curve was applied to evaluate the prediction ability of the disease prediction model, while the DeLong test was used to compare the performance of ROC curves. The crossover analysis was performed to investigate the interaction of SNPs and epidemiologic factors.

All statistical tests were two-sided and statistical significance was set at P < 0.05, carried out by SPSS software version 22.0 (SPSS Inc., Chicago, IL, USA) and Stata software version 14.0 (Stata Corp, College Station, TX, USA).

Association between single SNP and susceptibility of lung cancer

The allele distribution of each SNP in the control group was in accordance with Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium, indicating that the recruited subjects were representative of the general population. Associations were investigated in different genetic effect models (co-dominant, dominant, and recessive) of SNPs and lung cancers (Table 2). The genotypes and allele distributions of rs4730775 (dominant), rs1981492 (recessive) and rs222851 (co-dominant and dominant) were nominally significant prior to multiple testing correction between the case and control groups. After adjustments for general demographic characteristics and environmental factors, the individuals with Wnt2 rs4730775 CT + TT genotype had a high risk of lung cancer compared to those with CC genotype (ORadj = 1.227, 95% CI = 1.02–1.477) in the dominant genetic model. Relative to the GG + AG genotype, the Axin1 rs1981492 AA genotype was remarkably associated with an increased lung cancer risk in the recessive genetic model (ORadj = 1.460, 95% CI = 1.019–2.090). In the co-dominant model, individuals carrying Dvl2 rs222851 AG genotypes have a 47.7% increased risk of lung cancer, compared to individuals carrying the AA genotype (ORadj = 1.477, 95% CI = 1.200–1.819). The results of the dominant model also showed individuals who carrying AG + GG genotypes have a significantly higher risk of lung cancer than those with AA genotype (ORadj = 1.417, 95% CI = 1.163–1.727). After FDR correction, associations remained significant (q < 0.05): rs222851 dominant (q = 0.007) and rs222851 co-dominant (q = 0.015).

Predictive power of rs222851

The multi-classification logistic regression analysis showed that gender, nation, education, BMI, smoking, drinking tea, fruit intake, cooking fumes, polluting enterprises were independent factors of lung cancer (Table 3 Model 1). In environment-gene model (Model 2), the statistical analysis displayed a significantly increased risk for lung cancer in individuals carrying the rs222851 SNP under the dominant genetic model (OR = 1.437, 95% CI = 1.178–1.754). Besides, the multi-classification logistic regression result indicated in the environment-gene model whatever the genetic model, there was no statistically significant association between rs4730775 and rs1981492 and primary lung cancer.

Combined and interaction effects of rs222851 and environmental factors

The relationship between SNP rs222851 and lung cancer risk was further explored through stratified analyses in environment subgroups. As shown in Table 4, we observed that SNP rs222851 C > G had more significant influence on lung cancer in subjects without cooking oil fume exposure (ORadj = 2.347, 95% CI = 1.440–3.828, P = 0.026).

Discussion

Wnt signaling pathway is involved in virtually every aspect of embryonic development and also controls homeostatic self-renewal in a number of tissues15–17. In many tissues, activation of Wnt signalling has also been associated with cancer. The tightly regulated self-renewal mediated by Wnt signalling in stem and progenitor cells is subverted in cancer cells to allow malignant proliferation18,19. In the present study, we have investigated the role of potentially functional single nucleotide polymorphisms of Wnt signaling related genes on lung cancer. It was identified that the genetic mutation of Wnt2 rs4730775, Axin1 rs1981492 and Dvl2 rs222851 were association with a significantly increased risk of lung cancer in Chinese Han populations after adjustments for general demographic characteristics and environmental factors. Following multi-classification logistic regression analysis, we found individuals carrying rs222851 SNPs had a notably increased risk of developing lung cancer using the dominant genetic model. Additionally, the ROC curve indicated Dvl2 rs222851 could improve the prediction ability of lung cancer. Further analyses suggested that there might be interaction between rs222851 and cooking oil fume exposure in the development of lung cancer. Our in silico analysis demonstrates that rs222851 is a RegulomeDB Rank 1f variant overlapping 702 regulatory peaks, and a significant cis-eQTL in lung tissue (GTEx V10) for CTDNEP1, ACADVL, ELP5, and ENSG00000279641 among the European population. This confirms rs222851 as an active regulatory variant capable of modulating gene expression in lung tissue among other ethnic groups.

Wnt2 gene, located on human chromosome 7q31, is an important member of the Wnt protein family20. It has been reported that Wnt2 is over-expressed in many types of tumors and has the ability to activate the Wnt signaling pathway in certain tumor cells, including lung cancer21, hepatocellular carcinoma22, cervical carcinoma23 and colorectal cancer24. These researches revealed that Wnt2 was involved in tumorigenesis and development, thereby playing an important regulatory role in tumor progression. In this study, we found that Wnt2 rs4730775 was related to the susceptibility of lung cancer, and the mutation genotype of wnt2 rs4730775 increased the risk of lung cancer in the dominant genetic model. However, in the further multi-classification logistic regression analysis, there was no statistically significance about rs4730775. SNP rs4730775 is located in the 3 ‘untranslated region of Wnt2, which may have functional effects, influencing the expression level of Wnt2. Previous studies also indicated rs4730775 polymorphism was related to the susceptibility of liver cancer22 and strabismus25. Nevertheless, further direct experiments are needed to verify the function of rs4730775 whether it has a role in tumorigenesis of lung cancer.

Axin is an important negative regulator in Wnt signaling pathway which forms intracellular protein complex with GSK3 β and APC, and inhibits Wnt signaling by promoting β - Catenin degradation26. Axin family has two members, Axin1 and Axin2, which has been identified as an effective tumor suppressor gene27. It has been found that Axin gene mutation and genetic polymorphism are closely related to many tumor disease11,28. The association between Axin2 polymorphism and cancer risk has been examined in several case-control studies from different areas29–33. Axin2 rs2240308 polymorphism significantly and race-specifically correlates with decreased cancer risk. The research of Axin2 rs2240308 was relatively extensive while SNP associated with Axin1 was not clear. The study found that compared to the GG + AG genotype, AA genotype of Axin1 rs1981492 increased the risk of lung cancer in the recessive genetic model. This is the first report of an association between the locus and lung cancer. Extensive research has illuminated the prevalence of Axin1 mutations or dysfunction across a broad spectrum of malignancies34. Furthermore, certain splice variants of AXIN1 may exert oncogenic effects35,36. The rs1981492 polymorphism may modulate lung cancer susceptibility through functional alterations of Axin1. When identified during genetic susceptibility assessments, the presence of this genetic variant may warrant consideration of enhanced surveillance measures, including implementation of targeted lung cancer screening protocols.

Dvl2 serves as a pivotal regulator in Wnt signaling transduction, functioning as a molecular scaffold that propagates receptor-mediated signals to downstream target gene. This evolutionarily conserved protein, mapped to chromosome 17p13.2, demonstrates significant oncogenic potential through its pathological overexpression in malignant tissues37,38. Upon Wnt activation, DVL2 recruits AXIN away from the destruction complex, permitting β-catenin to accumulate and translocate to the nucleus to drive transcription of oncogenes including MYC and CCND117. Knocking down Dvl2 can inhibit Wnt-3a signal transduction and MMP activity, thus reducing the behavior of malignant cells39. DVL2 overexpression has been reported in cisplatin-resistant NSCLC cells, where it enhances Wnt/β-catenin signaling and promotes tumor cell survival, providing direct functional relevance in lung carcinogenesis40. Furthermore, it has been found that genetic polymorphisms in Dvl2 are associated with susceptibility, advanced TNM stage or tumor location in Colorectal Cancer37. However, there have been few reports regarding associations between Dvl2 polymorphisms and lung cancer. Our analysis demonstrated that the rs222851 polymorphism (AG genotype) confers a statistically significant elevation in lung cancer susceptibility, suggesting an association between rs222851 allelic variants and oncogenic risk. We presume that the rs222851 polymorphism in Dvl2 may exert functional consequences through transcriptional regulation of Dvl2 expression, potentially modulating oncogenesis susceptibility via dysregulation of the Wnt signaling axis. This proposed mechanism warrants rigorous validation through functional genomics approaches, complemented by cohort analyses to establish clinical correlations.

Lung cancer remains a major global health challenge. Emerging evidence underscores the growing burden of lung cancer among never-smokers, that environmental pollutants, indoor air contaminants, and genetic predispositions contribute to its multi-factorial etiology41,42. Cooking oil fumes, as an indoor air contaminant, contain a variety of carcinogens—such as benzo[a]pyrene, polycyclic aromatic hydrocarbons, and formaldehyde—that generate reactive oxygen species (ROS) in lung epithelial cells43,44. The previous study44 has shown that cooking oil fume could induce DNA damage, influence DNA cross-links and thus is an independent risk factor for lung cancer. It has been reported that the combination of CYP1A1, GSTM1 null genotype and cooking oil fumes significantly increased the risk of female lung cancer45. ROS-mediated oxidative stress inhibits GSK-3β activity, thereby reducing β-catenin phosphorylation and promoting its cytoplasmic accumulation—a mechanism that converges with DVL2-mediated Wnt pathway activation.Therefore, the stratified analysis and crossover analysis ware conducted to suggest that rs222851 risk genotype and cooking oil fume exposure have synergistic effect on lung cancer. We propose that carriers of the rs222851 G allele, with altered DVL2 regulatory activity, may experience synergistic Wnt/β-catenin hyperactivation when exposed to cooking oil fume-derived ROS. This biologically explains the positive multiplicative interaction. However, it was just a statistical estimation, further studies clarified their biological mechanism are required.

The pathogenesis and progression of lung cancer represent a multi-factorial process mediated by dynamic gene-environment interdependence46. Heterogeneous susceptibility to lung carcinogenesis among populations with distinct germline genetic variations persists even under equivalent cumulative exposure to environmental carcinogens, highlighting the critical role of host genetic determinants in modifying individual vulnerability through complex gene-environment interactions that influence disease initiation and progression. In this study, we detected not only the positive associations between the polymorphisms and lung cancer risk, but also the interaction of them with environmental risk factors on the susceptibility to lung cancer, which is a highlight of our article.

However, several deficiencies should be considered. First, the selection and memory biases are unavoidable in case-control study. Besides, the exposure assessment of cooking oil fume was obtained by subjective report, which has limited accuracy. Besides, although our study was restricted to self-identified Chinese Han individuals recruited from a single province, principal component analysis (PCA) and genomic inflation factor (λ) calculation were not performed to formally exclude cryptic population stratification. This represents a methodological limitation, and future studies should incorporate such approaches. In addition, the role of SNPs in the development of lung cancer should be confirmed in different ethnicities, and further detailed mechanisms need to be explored in the future studies.

Conclusions

Our study revealed that rs4730775, rs1981492 and rs222851 were associated with a significantly increased risk of lung cancer, and rs222851 variant was an independent factor of lung cancer, which elevated the prediction ability of lung cancer. Additionally, the significant interactions were observed between the rs222851 and cooking oil fume exposure on lung cancer risk.
