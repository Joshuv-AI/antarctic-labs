K-GMRF: Kinetic Gauss-Markov Random Field for First-Principles Covariance Tracking on Lie Groups
ZhiMing Li

Abstract
Tracking non-stationary covariance matrices is fundamental to vision yet hindered by existing estimators that either neglect manifold constraints or rely on first-order updates, incurring inevitable phase lag during rapid evolution. We propose K-GMRF, an online, training-free framework for covariance tracking that reformulates the problem as forced rigid-body motion on Lie groups. Derived from the Euler–Poincaré equations, our method interprets observations as torques driving a latent angular velocity, propagated via a structure-preserving symplectic integrator. We theoretically prove that this second-order dynamics achieves zero steady-state error under constant rotation, strictly superior to the proportional lag of first-order baselines. Validation across three domains demonstrates robust tracking fidelity: (i) on synthetic ellipses, K-GMRF reduces angular error by 30 × \times ( 15.6 ∘ → 0.51 ∘ 15.6^{\circ}\to 0.51^{\circ} ) compared to Riemannian EMA while maintaining stability at high speeds; (ii) on SO(3) stabilization with 20% dropout, it decreases geodesic error from 29.4 ∘ 29.4^{\circ} to 9.9 ∘ 9.9^{\circ} ; and (iii) on OTB motion-blur sequences, it improves IoU from 0.55 to 0.74 on BlurCar2 with a 96% success rate. As a fully differentiable symplectic module, K-GMRF provides a plug-and-play geometric prior for data-constrained scenarios and an interpretable layer within modern deep architectures.

Introduction

Covariance matrices encode second-order statistics fundamental to computer vision: region covariance descriptors
30
capture joint feature distributions for detection and tracking; diffusion tensors model anisotropic tissue structure in medical imaging
22
; and sample covariances underpin texture classification, action recognition, and visual attention
17
. A recurring challenge is
tracking non-stationary covariances
—estimating how the underlying positive-definite matrix evolves over time from noisy observations.

The symmetric positive-definite (SPD) constraint precludes naive Euclidean operations. Linearly averaging SPD matrices produces the “swelling effect”
22
, motivating Riemannian approaches
10
;
16
;
6
. Yet most methods rely on first-order updates (EMA or gradient descent) that suffer inherent phase lag when the target rotates at constant angular velocity—a structural limitation of overdamped dynamics.

Kalman filtering on Lie groups
5
;
27
;
7
introduces second-order dynamics via a velocity state, enabling prediction during dropout. However, these approaches linearize the manifold and apply standard Kalman updates, sacrificing intrinsic geometry. The connection to the underlying likelihood remains implicit.

We propose the
Kinetic Gauss-Markov Random Field
(K-GMRF), reformulating covariance tracking as forced rigid-body motion on the isospectral manifold
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
. We derive the update equations from the Euler–Poincaré formalism
19
;
14
, proving that observations act as natural-gradient torques
1
on the Lie algebra. The resulting symplectic integrator
12
preserves manifold structure while enabling zero-lag tracking and inertial coasting through occlusions.

Contributions.

•

Mechanics-based formulation.
We derive K-GMRF from Euler–Poincaré equations, proving the
whitened commutator torque

\tau=S^{-1}[C,M]S^{-1}
equals the natural gradient of the Wishart likelihood on
\so(d)
(Theorem
1
).

•

Zero-lag with quantified bounds.
We prove K-GMRF achieves zero steady-state error in the stability domain
\mathcal{D}=\{(\eta,\gamma):0<\gamma<2,\,\eta<2(2-\gamma)/\kappa_{\max}\}
(Theorem
2
), while any first-order method incurs lag
\geq|\Omega^{*}|/4
(Theorem
3
). The Lyapunov energy contracts as
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t+1}]\leq(1-\kappa)\mathcal{E}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
with
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}=O(\sigma^{2}/(m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}))
.

•

Rate-optimal tracking bound.
The time-averaged risk satisfies
\mathcal{R}_{T}\leq C_{1}\sigma^{2}/(m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2})+C_{2}V_{\Omega}/(\gamma T)+C_{3}d_{0}^{2}(1-\kappa)^{T}/T
(Theorem
5
), matching the minimax lower bound in leading terms (Theorem
6
). The phase transition occurs at
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\to 0
.

•

Empirical validation.
We validate on synthetic and real benchmarks:
30\times
error reduction on SPD(2) ellipse tracking,
3\times
improvement on SO(3) stabilization with 20% dropout, and 0.74 IoU (vs. 0.55) on OTB motion-blur sequences.

Positioning.
Rather than competing with deep trackers in rich-data regimes, K-GMRF targets
data-constrained
and
interpretability-critical
applications—medical imaging, scientific discovery, or online adaptation without pre-training. Its derivation from first principles yields a
differentiable symplectic layer
that injects physical consistency into deep networks: the Kick-Drift-Measure integrator can be unrolled as a recurrent module, enabling end-to-end training while preserving manifold structure by construction.

Related Work

Covariance descriptors in visual tracking.

The seminal work of Tuzel
et al
.

30
introduced region covariance descriptors for object detection and tracking, encoding spatial and photometric statistics in a compact SPD matrix. Subsequent extensions integrated covariance descriptors with particle filtering
25
and clustering-based model update
24
. Porikli
et al
.

23
pioneered the use of Lie algebra for incremental covariance updates, establishing an early connection between Riemannian geometry and tracking. However, these methods rely on first-order averaging that ignores the curvature of the SPD manifold and lacks predictive capability during occlusions.

Riemannian geometry on SPD manifolds.

Pennec
et al
.

22
established the foundational Riemannian framework for tensor computing, demonstrating the “swelling effect” of Euclidean averaging on SPD matrices. This motivated a rich body of work on Riemannian operations for SPD matrices, including Fréchet means
22
, Log-Euclidean mappings
22
, and affine-invariant metrics
17
. Recent advances have embedded Riemannian geometry into deep networks: SPDNet
16
introduced bilinear mappings and eigenvalue rectification; Brooks
et al
.

6
developed Riemannian batch normalization; and U-SPDNet
31
addressed information degradation through skip connections. Chen
et al
.

8
further proposed local geometric mechanisms for SPD networks. These methods achieve impressive results in classification but do not address the temporal dynamics required for online tracking of non-stationary covariances.

Filtering on Lie groups.

State estimation on Lie groups has received significant attention in robotics. Solà
et al
.

28
provided a comprehensive tutorial on Lie theory for state estimation, while Bourmaud
et al
.

5
developed discrete extended Kalman filters on Lie groups. Brossard
et al
.

7
extended the unscented Kalman filter to Lie groups with applications to visual-inertial odometry. The invariant extended Kalman filter (IEKF)
3
exploits symmetry to achieve improved convergence guarantees. Sjøberg and Egeland
27
proposed Lie algebraic UKF for pose estimation. These methods maintain velocity/acceleration states, enabling prediction during sensor dropout. However, they typically linearize the manifold into tangent space and apply standard Kalman updates, sacrificing the intrinsic Riemannian structure. Furthermore, the innovation term lacks a principled connection to the likelihood function on the manifold.

Hamiltonian mechanics and symplectic integration.

Geometric numerical integration
12
has demonstrated the superiority of structure-preserving algorithms for long-horizon simulation of Hamiltonian systems. Marsden and West
20
established variational integrators that exactly preserve symplectic structure. In machine learning, Greydanus
et al
.

11
introduced Hamiltonian neural networks that learn the Hamiltonian from data; SympNets
18
architecturally encode symplecticity for improved generalization. For rigid body control, the Euler–Poincaré reduction
14
;
19
provides a principled framework to derive equations of motion on Lie groups. Wiśniewski and Kulczycki
32
applied this framework to externally forced rigid body motion. Our work bridges this mechanical formalism to statistical estimation, deriving the observation-induced torque from the Wishart likelihood’s natural gradient.

Natural gradient and information geometry.

Amari
1
introduced the natural gradient, which preconditions gradient descent with the Fisher information matrix to achieve reparametrization invariance. Martens
21
provided modern perspectives connecting natural gradient to second-order optimization. In our framework, the “whitened commutator torque” arising from the Euler–Poincaré equations is shown to be precisely the natural gradient of the Wishart log-likelihood projected onto the Lie algebra (Theorem
1
). This establishes a principled bridge between information geometry and Hamiltonian mechanics for covariance tracking.

EMA-based model update in tracking.

Exponential moving average (EMA) remains the dominant paradigm for template update in visual tracking. Huang and Zhou
15
analyzed EMA from an optimization perspective, proposing regularization and reinitialization to mitigate drift. However, EMA is fundamentally a first-order method: it cannot anticipate target motion and exhibits inherent phase lag when tracking rotating targets (Theorem
3
). Our K-GMRF addresses this by introducing momentum on the Lie algebra, enabling zero-lag tracking (Theorem
2
) and inertial coasting during observation dropout.

Kinetic Gauss-Markov Random Field

3.1
Geometric Setup and Observation Model

Definition 1

(Isospectral Orbit)
.

Given
\Lambda=\diag(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d}
with
\lambda_{1}>\cdots>\lambda_{d}>0
, the isospectral orbit is

\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}:=\{Q\Lambda Q^{\top}:Q\in\SO(d)\}\subset\mathbb{S}_{++}^{d}.

(1)

The tangent space at
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
is
T_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}=\{[\Omega,M]:\Omega\in\so(d)\}
, where
[\cdot,\cdot]
denotes the matrix commutator.

Definition 2

(Whitened Spectral Gap)
.

The identifiability parameter for rotation recovery under observation noise
\sigma^{2}
is

\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}:=\min_{i\neq j}\left|\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2}}\right|.

(2)

Definition 3

(Whitened Commutator Torque)
.

For state
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, observation
C\in\mathbb{S}_{+}^{d}
, and whitened covariance
S:=M+\sigma^{2}I
:

\tau(M,C):=S^{-1}[C,M]S^{-1}\in\so(d).

(3)

Assumption 1

(Wishart Observation Model
2
)
.

The observation
C_{t}
is generated by
mC_{t}\sim\mathcal{W}_{d}(m,M_{t}^{*}+\sigma^{2}I)
, where
M_{t}^{*}
is the latent state. Thus
\mathbb{E}[C_{t}\mid M_{t}^{*}]=M_{t}^{*}+\sigma^{2}I
.

Remark 1

(Why Gauss-Markov Random Field?)
.

In Lie algebra coordinates
(\xi_{t},\omega_{t})\in\so(d)\times\so(d)
, where
\xi_{t}=\log_{M^{*}}(M_{t})
is the configuration and
\omega_{t}
is angular velocity, the constant-velocity prior

\textstyle E_{\mathrm{prior}}=\sum_{t}\|\xi_{t}-\omega_{t}\|^{2}+\|\omega_{t+1}-\omega_{t}\|^{2}

(4)

induces a
block-tridiagonal precision matrix
—the hallmark of a Gauss-Markov Random Field
26
. K-GMRF thus generalizes classical first-order GMRF (random walk prior on position) to a second-order (constant-velocity prior)

Inertia inverse operator.
Given eigendecomposition
M=U\Lambda U^{\top}
, define
d_{i}:=\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2}
. For torque
\tau\in\so(d)
, let
\tilde{\tau}:=U^{\top}\tau U
. The inertia inverse is computed element-wise:

\left[\mathcal{I}_{M}^{-1}(\tau)\right]_{ij}=\frac{d_{i}d_{j}}{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}+\epsilon}\cdot\tilde{\tau}_{ij},\quad i\neq j,

(5)

where
\epsilon>0
is a regularization term preventing singularity when eigenvalues coincide.

Computational complexity.
Each iteration requires
O(d^{3})
for the eigen decomposition yet Cayley–Neumann integration reduces this to
O(Kd^{2})
cost per frame

3.3
Torque as Natural Gradient Projection

Theorem 1

(Geometric Consistency
1
;
22
)
.

Under the affine-invariant Riemannian metric (AIRM) on
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, the torque (
3
) equals the natural gradient of the Wishart negative log-likelihood projected onto
\so(d)
:

\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V(M;C)=\left[\mathcal{I}_{M}^{-1}\left(\tfrac{m}{2}\tau(M,C)\right),M\right],

(6)

where
V(M;C)=\frac{m}{2}(\log\det S+\tr(S^{-1}C))
and
\mathcal{I}_{M}:\so(d)\to\so(d)
is the inertia operator defined by
\langle\mathcal{I}_{M}(\Omega_{1}),\Omega_{2}\rangle_{F}=g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}([\Omega_{1},M],[\Omega_{2},M])
.

Proof.
See Supplementary Material A.3.
\square

Lemma 1

(Unbiasedness and Concentration)
.

Under Assumption
1
, the torque satisfies:

(i)
Conditional unbiasedness
:
\mathbb{E}[\tau(M,C_{t})\mid M_{t}^{*}]=\tau(M,M_{t}^{*}+\sigma^{2}I)
, with
\tau(M^{*},S^{*})=0
.

(ii)
Variance bound
:
\mathbb{E}[\|\tau-\mathbb{E}[\tau]\|_{F}^{2}]\leq K_{\tau}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})/m
, where
K_{\tau}
depends only on the spectral structure.

Proof.
See Supplementary Material A.4.
\square

Lemma 2

(Local Strong Convexity
22
)
.

On orbit neighborhoods, the Hessian of expected negative log-likelihood satisfies

\mathrm{Hess}_{\mathcal{O}}\bar{V}(M^{*})\succeq\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})\cdot I,\quad\text{where }\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})>0\iff\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}>0.

(7)

The curvature
\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})\to 0
as
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\to 0
, characterizing the phase transition boundary.

Proof.
See Supplementary Material A.5.
\square

Proposition 1

(Structure Preservation
20
;
12
)
.

The undamped K-GMRF (
\gamma=0
) is a first-order Lie group variational integrator:

(i)
Orbit invariance
:
M_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}\Rightarrow M_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
for all
t\geq 0
.

(ii)
Symplecticity

3.4
Zero Steady-State Error under Constant Rotation

The central theoretical contribution establishes
zero steady-state error
for the second-order K-GMRF dynamics, in contrast to the
inevitable lag
of first-order methods.

Theorem 2

(Zero-Lag Tracking)
.

Consider noiseless observations
C_{t}=M_{t}^{*}
and constant angular velocity
\Omega_{t}^{*}\equiv\Omega^{*}
. Define the stability domain

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{(\eta,\gamma):0<\gamma<2,\quad 0<\eta<\frac{2(2-\gamma)}{\kappa_{\max}}\right\},

(8)

where
\kappa_{\max}=\max\mathrm{eig}(\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K})
and
\mathcal{K}
is the stiffness operator from Lemma
2
. Then for
(\eta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{D}
:

\lim_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{*})=0\quad\textit{(zero steady-state error)}.

(9)

Convergence is locally exponential with rate determined by the spectral radius of the linearized error dynamics.

Proof.
The proof proceeds by linearizing the error dynamics around the equilibrium trajectory
(M_{t}^{*},\Omega^{*})
. The characteristic polynomial of the resulting discrete damped harmonic oscillator yields the Schur stability conditions defining
\mathcal{D}
. See Supplementary Material B.1 for complete details.
\square

Theorem 3

(Inevitable Lag of First-Order Methods)
.

Consider any first-order method (exponential moving average, Riemannian gradient descent) without explicit velocity state, evolving as
M_{t+1}=\exp(-\eta\mathcal{A}^{-1}\tau_{t})M_{t}\exp(\cdot)^{\top}
. Under the same constant-rotation setting as Theorem
2
, the steady-state error satisfies

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{*})\geq\frac{|\Omega^{*}|_{F}}{2\eta\bar{\kappa}},

(10)

where
\bar{\kappa}=\max\mathrm{eig}(\mathcal{A})
and stability requires
\eta\bar{\kappa}<2
. Combining these constraints:

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{*})\geq\frac{|\Omega^{*}|_{F}}{4}.

(11)

Zero steady-state error is structurally unachievable for any stable first-order method.

Proof.
The first-order dynamics require nonzero position error to generate corrective torque. Balancing the driving term against stability constraints yields the stated lower bound. See Supplementary Material B.2.
\square

3.5
Stochastic Stability and Tracking Error Bound

Theorem 4

(Energy Contraction to Noise Ball
9
)
.

Define the Lyapunov energy
\mathcal{E}_{t}:=T(u_{t})+\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}
, where
u_{t}:=\Omega_{t}-\Omega^{*}
is the velocity error and
\xi_{t}:=\log_{M^{*}}(M_{t})
is the configuration error. Under
(\eta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{D}
and Assumption
1
, there exist constants
\kappa\in(0,1)
and

\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}:=\frac{C_{0}\sigma^{2}}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}

(12)

such that the conditional expectation satisfies

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq(1-\kappa)\mathcal{E}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}.

(13)

Iterating yields
\sup_{t}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}/\kappa
(convergence to noise ball).

Proof.
The proof combines Lemmas
1
–
2
with a discrete Lyapunov argument. The contraction rate
\kappa
depends on
(\eta,\gamma,\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})
. See Supplementary Material B.3.
\square

Theorem 5

(Master Theorem: Time-Averaged Tracking Risk)
.

Let
V_{\Omega}:=\sum_{t=1}^{T-1}|\Omega_{t+1}^{*}-\Omega_{t}^{*}|_{F}
denote the total variation of target angular velocity. Under Assumption
1
and
(\eta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{D}
, the time-averaged risk

\mathcal{R}_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}\big[d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{*})^{2}\big]

(14)

satisfies the following bound:

\boxed{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{R}_{T}\;\leq\;&\underbrace{\frac{C_{1}\sigma^{2}}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}}_{\text{statistical}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{C_{2}V_{\Omega}}{\gamma T}}_{\text{nonstationarity}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{C_{3}d_{0}^{2}(1-\kappa)^{T}}{T}}_{\text{transient}}\end{aligned}}

(15)

where
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
depend on
(\Lambda,\sigma^{2},\eta,\gamma)
but not on
(m,T,V_{\Omega})
.

Phase transition.
When
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\to 0
, the statistical error diverges. When
(\eta,\gamma)\notin\mathcal{D}
, the transient term fails to decay.

Proof.
The bound combines Theorem
4
with a perturbation analysis for time-varying
\Omega_{t}^{*}
. The nonstationarity term arises from Lemma C (target variation perturbation) in the Supplementary Material. See Supplementary Material B.4.
\square

Generalization to Noncommutative Rotations and Optimality

Corollary 1

(Extension to
d\geq 3
: Noncommutative Lie Algebra)
.

For
d\geq 3
, the Lie algebra
\so(d)
is noncommutative, introducing Baker–Campbell–Hausdorff (BCH)
13
remainder terms in the error dynamics. Theorems
2
–
5
remain valid with the following modifications:

1.

The energy contraction (
13
) acquires an additional term:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq(1-\kappa^{\prime})\mathcal{E}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{BCH}}|\Omega^{*}|^{2}\mathcal{E}_{t},

(16)

where
C_{\mathrm{BCH}}=O(1)
is a universal constant from the BCH expansion.

2.

The stability domain
\mathcal{D}
contracts by a factor depending on
|\Omega^{*}|
to absorb the BCH perturbation.

3.

The master bound (
15
) holds with modified constants
C_{1}^{\prime}=C_{1}(1+O(d|\Omega^{*}|^{2}))
.

Critically, the phase transition is still governed by
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
; noncommutativity affects only the constants.

Proof.
The proof uses Lemma 9.1 (BCH remainder bound
O(r^{3})
), Lemma 9.2 (adjoint Lipschitz), and Lemma 9.3 (multi-plane rotation decomposition) to control higher-order terms. See Supplementary Material C.1.

Theorem 6

(Minimax Lower Bound and Rate Optimality
29
)
.

Consider the class of fixed-gain online filters
\{\phi_{t}\}
over the problem family
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2},m,V_{\Omega})
. The minimax risk satisfies

\inf_{\{\phi_{t}\}}\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}_{T}\geq c_{1}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}+c_{2}\frac{V_{\Omega}}{T}

(17)

for universal constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
. Comparing with Theorem
5
, K-GMRF achieves the minimax rate in both the statistical (
1/m
) and nonstationarity (
V_{\Omega}/T
) terms.

Proof.
The lower bound follows from a two-point testing argument combined with Fano’s inequality applied to the orbit distance. The
1/m
term arises from Fisher information of the Wishart model; the
V_{\Omega}/T
term from a random walk adversary. See Supplementary Material C.2.
\square

Appendix A
Geometry and Statistical Setup

This section establishes the geometric framework and observation model for analyzing statistical tracking on rotation groups. We fix the state space dimension
d\geq 2
and denote by
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{++}
the manifold of
d\times d
symmetric positive definite matrices. The Lie algebra of skew-symmetric matrices is denoted
\mathfrak{so}(d):=\{\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\Omega^{\top}=-\Omega\}
, and the commutator is written as
[A,B]:=AB-BA
.

A.1
Conjugate Orbit Manifold and Tangent Space Geometry

The state evolution is constrained to an isospectral manifold with fixed eigenvalues.

Definition 4

(Conjugate Orbit Manifold)
.

Given a strictly ordered eigenvalue matrix
\Lambda=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}_{++}
with
\lambda_{1}>\cdots>\lambda_{d}>0
, we define the conjugate orbit as

\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}:=\{M=Q\Lambda Q^{\top}:Q\in SO(d)\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d}_{++}.

(18)

Its homogeneous space representation is
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}\simeq SO(d)/\mathrm{Stab}(\Lambda)
, where the stabilizer subgroup is

\mathrm{Stab}(\Lambda):=\{Q\in SO(d):Q\Lambda Q^{\top}=\Lambda\}.

(19)

Due to the non-degeneracy of the spectrum,
\mathrm{Stab}(\Lambda)
is the finite group of diagonal sign matrices.

Lemma 3

(Tangent Space via Commutator)
.

For any
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, the tangent space admits the representation

T_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}=\{[\Omega,M]:\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(d)\}\subset\mathbb{S}^{d},

(20)

and the map
\mathrm{ad}_{M}^{*}:\mathfrak{so}(d)\to T_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
,
\Omega\mapsto[\Omega,M]
, is a linear isomorphism when
\Lambda
has simple spectrum.

Proof.

Consider a curve
M(t)=Q(t)\Lambda Q(t)^{\top}
on
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
. Differentiating yields

\dot{M}=\dot{Q}\Lambda Q^{\top}+Q\Lambda\dot{Q}^{\top}.

(21)

Define
\Omega:=\dot{Q}Q^{\top}\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
. Then

\dot{M}=\Omega(Q\Lambda Q^{\top})-(Q\Lambda Q^{\top})\Omega=[\Omega,M].

(22)

Thus every tangent vector can be written as
[\Omega,M]
. The reverse inclusion follows directly from the above construction.
∎

A.2
Whitening Matrix and Whitened Torque

To introduce a metric adapted to the noise structure, we first define whitened quantities.

Definition 5

(Whitening Matrix and Torque)
.

Given observation noise variance
\sigma^{2}>0
:

1.

For any state
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, define the
whitening matrix
as
S:=M+\sigma^{2}I\in\mathbb{S}^{d}_{++}
. Note that
S
and
M
share eigenvectors, so
[M,S]=0
.

2.

For any observation matrix
C\in\mathbb{S}^{d}_{+}
, define the
whitened torque
as

\tau(M,C):=S^{-1}[C,M]S^{-1}\in\mathfrak{so}(d).

(23)

Since
S^{-1}
commutes with
M
, the torque can equivalently be written as
\tau=[S^{-1}CS^{-1},M]
.

Definition 6

(Noise-Adapted Riemannian Metric)
.

We equip
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
with the affine-invariant Riemannian metric (AIRM) induced by the whitening matrix
S
. For
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
and tangent vectors
U,V\in T_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
:

g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}(U,V):=\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}US^{-1}V).

(24)

This metric is the restriction of the AIRM on
\mathbb{S}^{d}_{++}
(with base point
S
) to the orbit. This geometric choice is essential for canceling the covariance structure of observation noise.

A.3
Statistical Observation Model

We adopt a rank-
m
Wishart observation model that encompasses both single-sample (
m=1
) and mini-batch (
m>1
) covariance estimation.

Assumption 2

(Rank-
m
Wishart Observation)
.

Let the true state be
M^{\star}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
and define the true whitened covariance
S^{\star}:=M^{\star}+\sigma^{2}I
. At each time step, we observe
m
independent samples
v_{j}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,S^{\star})
. The sample covariance matrix is

C:=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}.

(25)

This is equivalent to
mC\sim\mathcal{W}_{d}(m,S^{\star})
, a Wishart distribution with
m
degrees of freedom and scale matrix
S^{\star}
. The first moment satisfies
\mathbb{E}[C\mid M^{\star}]=S^{\star}
.

Definition 7

(Negative Log-Likelihood)
.

Omitting terms independent of
M
, the negative log-likelihood (NLL) of observation
C
given state
M
is

V(M;C):=\frac{m}{2}\left(\log\det S+\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}C)\right),\quad\text{where }S=M+\sigma^{2}I.

(26)

Definition 8

(Whitened Spectral Gap)
.

Define the
whitened spectral gap

\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
as the minimum distance between reciprocals of whitened eigenvalues:

\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}:=\min_{i\neq j}\left|\frac{\lambda_{i}}{\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2}}-\frac{\lambda_{j}}{\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2}}\right|=\min_{i\neq j}\frac{\sigma^{2}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}.

(27)

This quantity controls the identifiability of the rotation direction from noisy observations.

Appendix B
Proof of Theorem
1
(Geometric Consistency)

This section proves that the algebraic torque
\tau
defined in Definition
5
is precisely the dual representation of the natural gradient of the negative log-likelihood
V(M;C)
on the orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
with respect to the metric
g^{\mathcal{O}}
.

Lemma 4

(Commutator-Trace Adjoint Identity)
.

For any
A,M\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
and
\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
, the following identity holds:

\langle A,[\Omega,M]\rangle_{F}=\langle[M,A],\Omega\rangle_{F},

(28)

where
\langle X,Y\rangle_{F}=\mathrm{tr}(X^{\top}Y)
denotes the Frobenius inner product.

Proof.

By the cyclic property of the trace,

\displaystyle\langle A,[\Omega,M]\rangle_{F}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A(\Omega M-M\Omega))

(29)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(A\Omega M)-\mathrm{tr}(AM\Omega)

(30)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}(MA\Omega)-\mathrm{tr}(AM\Omega)

(31)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{tr}((MA-AM)\Omega)=\langle[M,A],\Omega\rangle_{F}.

(32)

∎

Theorem 7

(Geometric Consistency)
.

Let
J_{M}:\mathfrak{so}(d)\to\mathfrak{so}(d)
be the inertia operator induced by the metric
g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}
, defined via

\langle J_{M}(\Omega_{1}),\Omega_{2}\rangle_{F}:=g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}([\Omega_{1},M],[\Omega_{2},M]).

(33)

Then the Riemannian gradient of
V(M;C)
on
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
satisfies

\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V(M;C)=\left[J_{M}^{-1}\left(\frac{m}{2}\tau(M,C)\right),M\right].

(34)

In other words, in the sense of Lie algebra duality, the whitened torque
\tau
is the projection of the natural gradient.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in four steps.

Step 1: Compute the Euclidean gradient.
We first compute the unconstrained matrix gradient of
V
with respect to
M
. Using the differential identities
d(\log\det S)=\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}dS)
and
d(\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}C))=-\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}(dS)S^{-1}C)
, together with
dS=dM
, we obtain

dV=\frac{m}{2}\mathrm{tr}\left((S^{-1}-S^{-1}CS^{-1})dM\right).

(35)

Thus the Euclidean gradient is
\nabla_{M}V=\frac{m}{2}(S^{-1}-S^{-1}CS^{-1})\in\mathbb{S}^{d}
.

Step 2: Restrict to the tangent space.
Consider the directional derivative along a tangent vector
\delta M=[\Omega,M]\in T_{M}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
. By Lemma
4
,

dV([\Omega,M])=\langle\nabla_{M}V,[\Omega,M]\rangle_{F}=\langle[M,\nabla_{M}V],\Omega\rangle_{F}.

(36)

Step 3: Compute the commutator
[M,\nabla_{M}V]
.
We have

[M,\nabla_{M}V]=\frac{m}{2}\left([M,S^{-1}]-[M,S^{-1}CS^{-1}]\right).

(37)

Since
S
is a polynomial function of
M
(namely
S=M+\sigma^{2}I
), they commute, hence
[M,S^{-1}]=0
. For the second term, using the commutativity of
S^{-1}
and
M
:

-[M,S^{-1}CS^{-1}]=-S^{-1}[M,C]S^{-1}=S^{-1}[C,M]S^{-1}=\tau(M,C).

(38)

Therefore the directional derivative becomes

dV([\Omega,M])=\left\langle\frac{m}{2}\tau(M,C),\Omega\right\rangle_{F}.

(39)

Step 4: Identify the Riemannian gradient.
By definition, the Riemannian gradient
\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V
is the unique tangent vector satisfying

g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}(\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V,[\Omega,M])=dV([\Omega,M]),\quad\forall\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(d).

(40)

Write
\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V=[\Omega^{\sharp},M]
. Substituting into the definition of
g_{M}^{\mathcal{O}}
and
J_{M}
:

\langle J_{M}(\Omega^{\sharp}),\Omega\rangle_{F}=\left\langle\frac{m}{2}\tau(M,C),\Omega\right\rangle_{F}.

(41)

Since this holds for all
\Omega
, we have
J_{M}(\Omega^{\sharp})=\frac{m}{2}\tau(M,C)
, which gives
\Omega^{\sharp}=J_{M}^{-1}(\frac{m}{2}\tau)
. Substituting back yields the stated result.
∎

Appendix C
Statistical Concentration (Lemma
1
)

This section establishes the statistical properties of the whitened torque
\tau(M,C)
, proving that it is an unbiased estimator of the latent geometric deviation with variance converging at rate
O(1/m)
.

Lemma 5

(Wishart Second Moment Bound)
.

If
C
satisfies Assumption
2
, then for any deterministic matrices
A,B
:

\mathbb{E}\|A(C-S^{\star})B\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m}\|A\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|B\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\left((\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\star})^{2}+\|S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}\right).

(42)

Proof.

Let
W:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}v_{j}v_{j}^{\top}
, so
W\sim\mathcal{W}_{d}(m,S^{\star})
and
C=W/m
. The Wishart covariance identity gives

\mathrm{Cov}(W_{ij},W_{kl})=m(S^{\star}_{ik}S^{\star}_{jl}+S^{\star}_{il}S^{\star}_{jk}),

(43)

hence

\mathrm{Cov}(C_{ij},C_{kl})=\frac{1}{m}(S^{\star}_{ik}S^{\star}_{jl}+S^{\star}_{il}S^{\star}_{jk}).

(44)

Thus

\mathbb{E}\|C-S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i,j}\mathrm{Var}(C_{ij})=\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i,j}(S^{\star}_{ii}S^{\star}_{jj}+(S^{\star}_{ij})^{2})=\frac{1}{m}((\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\star})^{2}+\|S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}).

(45)

The stated bound follows from sub-multiplicativity of norms.
∎

Theorem 8

(Statistical Concentration)
.

Under Assumption
2
, for any fixed
M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
:

(1) Conditional unbiasedness:

\mathbb{E}[\tau(M,C)\mid M^{\star}]=S^{-1}[S^{\star},M]S^{-1}.

(46)

In particular, when
M=M^{\star}
, we have
\mathbb{E}[\tau(M^{\star},C)]=0
.

(2) Variance bound:
There exists a constant
K(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\left\|\tau(M,C)-\mathbb{E}[\tau]\right\|_{F}^{2}\mid M^{\star}\right]\leq\frac{K(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})}{m}.

(47)

The explicit constant is
K=4\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{4}\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}(\|S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}+(\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\star})^{2})
.

Proof.

(1) Unbiasedness:
By linearity of expectation and
\mathbb{E}[C\mid M^{\star}]=S^{\star}
:

\mathbb{E}[\tau]=\mathbb{E}[S^{-1}[C,M]S^{-1}]=[S^{-1}S^{\star}S^{-1},M]=S^{-1}[S^{\star},M]S^{-1},

(48)

where we used
[S^{-1},M]=0
to factor out
S^{-1}
. When
M=M^{\star}
, we have
S=S^{\star}
. Since
S^{\star}=M^{\star}+\sigma^{2}I
, clearly
[S^{\star},M^{\star}]=0
, so the expectation vanishes.

(2) Variance bound:
Let
\bar{\tau}=\mathbb{E}[\tau]
. The deviation is

\tau-\bar{\tau}=S^{-1}[C-S^{\star},M]S^{-1}.

(49)

We need to bound
\|\tau-\bar{\tau}\|_{F}^{2}
. Using the commutator norm inequality
\|[X,Y]\|_{F}\leq 2\|X\|_{F}\|Y\|_{\mathrm{op}}
:

\displaystyle\|\tau-\bar{\tau}\|_{F}

\displaystyle=\|S^{-1}(C-S^{\star})MS^{-1}-S^{-1}M(C-S^{\star})S^{-1}\|_{F}

(50)

\displaystyle\leq\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|[C-S^{\star},M]\|_{F}

(51)

\displaystyle\leq 2\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|C-S^{\star}\|_{F}.

(52)

Squaring and taking expectation, using Lemma
5
with
A=B=I
:

\mathbb{E}\|\tau-\bar{\tau}\|_{F}^{2}\leq 4\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{4}\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\mathbb{E}\|C-S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}=\frac{4\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{4}\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}}{m}((\mathrm{tr}\,S^{\star})^{2}+\|S^{\star}\|_{F}^{2}).

(53)

On the fixed orbit
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, we have
\|M\|_{\mathrm{op}}=\lambda_{1}
and
\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}=(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{-1}
, which are constants. Thus the bound is
O(1/m)
.
∎

Appendix D
Identifiability and Spectral Gap (Lemma
2
)

This section analyzes the curvature of the objective function, establishing the quantitative connection between identifiability and the spectral gap.

Definition 9

(Population Risk)
.

Given true state
M^{\star}
, define the population risk as

\overline{V}(M):=\mathbb{E}[V(M;C)\mid M^{\star}]=\frac{m}{2}\left(\log\det S+\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}S^{\star})\right)+\mathrm{const},

(54)

where
S=M+\sigma^{2}I
and
S^{\star}=M^{\star}+\sigma^{2}I
. On
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
,
\log\det S
is constant, so the directional curvature of
\overline{V}
is entirely determined by
\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}S^{\star})
.

Theorem 9

(Local Identifiability and Strong Convexity)
.

If
\Lambda
has simple spectrum (all
\lambda_{i}
distinct), then
\overline{V}(M)
is locally strongly convex at
M^{\star}
. Specifically, along any geodesic
M(t)=e^{t\Omega}M^{\star}e^{-t\Omega}
for
\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
, the second derivative satisfies

\frac{d^{2}}{dt^{2}}\overline{V}(M(t))\Big|_{t=0}\geq\mu_{\mathrm{id}}\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2},

(55)

where the strong convexity constant is controlled by the whitened spectral gap:

\mu_{\mathrm{id}}\geq\frac{m}{4}\cdot\frac{(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}.

(56)

Thus the problem is locally identifiable if and only if
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}>0
.

Proof.

Step 1: Simplify the population risk.
Since
\mathbb{E}[C]=S^{\star}
, omitting constants:

\overline{V}(M)=\frac{m}{2}\mathrm{tr}(S^{-1}S^{\star}).

(57)

Without loss of generality, assume
M^{\star}=\Lambda
, so
S^{\star}=D:=\Lambda+\sigma^{2}I
is diagonal. Consider the perturbation
M(t)=e^{t\Omega}\Lambda e^{-t\Omega}
, which gives
S(t)=e^{t\Omega}De^{-t\Omega}
and
S(t)^{-1}=e^{t\Omega}D^{-1}e^{-t\Omega}
.

Step 2: Second-order variation.
Substituting into
\overline{V}
:

\overline{V}(t)=\frac{m}{2}\mathrm{tr}(e^{t\Omega}D^{-1}e^{-t\Omega}D).

(58)

Using the expansion
e^{t\Omega}=I+t\Omega+\frac{t^{2}}{2}\Omega^{2}+O(t^{3})
:

e^{t\Omega}D^{-1}e^{-t\Omega}=D^{-1}+t[\Omega,D^{-1}]+\frac{t^{2}}{2}[\Omega,[\Omega,D^{-1}]]+O(t^{3}).

(59)

Since
\mathrm{tr}([\Omega,D^{-1}]D)=\mathrm{tr}(\Omega D^{-1}D-D^{-1}\Omega D)=\mathrm{tr}(\Omega)-\mathrm{tr}(D^{-1}\Omega D)=0
by cyclicity and the fact that
D
is diagonal, the first-order term vanishes (confirming
M^{\star}
is a critical point). The second-order coefficient is

\frac{d^{2}\overline{V}}{dt^{2}}\Big|_{t=0}=\frac{m}{4}\mathrm{tr}\left([\Omega,[\Omega,D^{-1}]]D\right).

(60)

Step 3: Hessian eigenvalue analysis.
Using the identity
\mathrm{tr}([\Omega,X]Y)=-\mathrm{tr}(X[\Omega,Y])
with
X=[\Omega,D^{-1}]
and
Y=D
:

\mathrm{tr}\left([\Omega,[\Omega,D^{-1}]]D\right)=-\mathrm{tr}\left([\Omega,D^{-1}][\Omega,D]\right).

(61)

For diagonal
D=\mathrm{diag}(d_{k})
where
d_{k}=\lambda_{k}+\sigma^{2}
, we have
[\Omega,D]_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}(d_{j}-d_{i})
. Similarly,
[\Omega,D^{-1}]_{ij}=\Omega_{ij}(d_{j}^{-1}-d_{i}^{-1})=\Omega_{ij}\frac{d_{i}-d_{j}}{d_{i}d_{j}}
. Computing the trace:

\displaystyle-\sum_{i,j}[\Omega,D^{-1}]_{ji}[\Omega,D]_{ij}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{i,j}\left(\Omega_{ji}\frac{d_{j}-d_{i}}{d_{i}d_{j}}\right)\left(\Omega_{ij}(d_{j}-d_{i})\right)

(62)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i,j}\Omega_{ij}^{2}\frac{(d_{i}-d_{j})^{2}}{d_{i}d_{j}},

(63)

where we used
\Omega_{ji}=-\Omega_{ij}
and thus
\Omega_{ji}\Omega_{ij}=-\Omega_{ij}^{2}
. The sum is nonzero only for
i\neq j
:

\frac{d^{2}\overline{V}}{dt^{2}}=\frac{m}{4}\sum_{i\neq j}\Omega_{ij}^{2}\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}.

(64)

Step 4: Lower bound.
Define
w_{ij}:=\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}
. We seek
w_{\min}=\min_{i\neq j}w_{ij}
. From the definition of
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
:

\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}\leq\left(\frac{\sigma^{2}|\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j}|}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}\right)^{2}=\frac{\sigma^{4}}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}\cdot w_{ij}.

(65)

Therefore,

w_{ij}\geq\frac{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}{\sigma^{4}}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}\geq\frac{(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}.

(66)

Since
\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}=\sum_{i\neq j}\Omega_{ij}^{2}
, we obtain

\frac{d^{2}\overline{V}}{dt^{2}}\geq\frac{m}{4}\left(\frac{(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{2}}{\sigma^{4}}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}\right)\|\Omega\|_{F}^{2}.

(67)

∎

Corollary 2

(Degenerate Spectral Gap)
.

If there exist
i\neq j
such that
\lambda_{i}=\lambda_{j}
, then
w_{\min}=0
, and there exists
\Omega\neq 0
such that
M(t)=e^{t\Omega}M^{\star}e^{t\Omega^{\top}}\equiv M^{\star}
. Thus
\overline{V}
has zero curvature in that direction, and local strong convexity fails.

Appendix E
Deterministic Skeleton Analysis

This appendix analyzes the noiseless setting
\sigma=0
with perfect observations
C_{t}\equiv M_{t}^{\star}
. The true target rotates at constant angular velocity
\Omega^{\star}\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
:

M_{t+1}^{\star}=\exp(\Omega^{\star})M_{t}^{\star}\exp(\Omega^{\star})^{\top}.

(68)

We compare two classes of deterministic dynamics: the second-order K-GMRF (Kick-Drift) which maintains angular velocity state, and the first-order overdamped methods (EMA/gradient flow) which lack explicit velocity state.

E.1
Notation and Linearization Setup

Let
\Lambda
be the fixed spectrum and
\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}=\{Q\Lambda Q^{\top}:Q\in SO(d)\}
. Write
M_{t}=Q_{t}\Lambda Q_{t}^{\top}
and
M_{t}^{\star}=Q_{t}^{\star}\Lambda Q_{t}^{\star\top}
. Define the relative rotation and its logarithm:

R_{t}:=Q_{t}Q_{t}^{\star\top}\in SO(d),\qquad\xi_{t}:=\log(R_{t})\in\mathfrak{so}(d).

(69)

This is equivalent to
\xi_{t}=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M_{t})
(uniqueness guaranteed by simple spectrum in a local neighborhood). For small
|\xi_{t}|_{F}
:

M_{t}=\exp(\xi_{t})M_{t}^{\star}\exp(\xi_{t})^{\top},\quad d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})=|\xi_{t}|_{F}+o(|\xi_{t}|_{F}).

(70)

First-order expansion of torque and stiffness operator.
In the noiseless case
\sigma=0
, the deterministic torque takes the unwhitened form
\tau_{t}=[C_{t},M_{t}]=[M_{t}^{\star},M_{t}]\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
. Substituting
M_{t}=\exp(\xi_{t})M_{t}^{\star}\exp(\xi_{t})^{\top}
and expanding to first order in
\xi_{t}
:

M_{t}=M_{t}^{\star}+[\xi_{t},M_{t}^{\star}]+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}),

(71)

hence

\tau_{t}=[M_{t}^{\star},M_{t}]=[M_{t}^{\star},[\xi_{t},M_{t}^{\star}]]+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}).

(72)

Define the linear stiffness operator
\mathcal{K}_{t}:\mathfrak{so}(d)\to\mathfrak{so}(d)
by

\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi:=-[M_{t}^{\star},[\xi,M_{t}^{\star}]].

(73)

Then the first-order approximation reads
\tau_{t}=-\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi_{t}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2})
.

The operator
\mathcal{K}_{t}
is positive semi-definite and satisfies

\langle\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle_{F}=|[\xi,M_{t}^{\star}]|_{F}^{2}\geq 0.

(74)

Under the whitened spectral gap condition
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}>0
, Theorem
9
implies the existence of
\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})>0
such that

\langle\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle_{F}\geq\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})|\xi|_{F}^{2}

(75)

for sufficiently small
|\xi|_{F}
, with
\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})\to 0
if and only if
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\to 0
.

E.2
Proof of Proposition
1
(Structure Preservation)

Theorem 10

(Structure Preservation: Symplecticity and Orbit Invariance)
.

Let
\gamma=0
and consider deterministic torque
\tau_{t}
with the discrete update

\Omega_{t+1}=\Omega_{t}+\eta\mathcal{I}^{-1}\tau_{t},\qquad M_{t+1}=\exp(\Omega_{t+1})M_{t}\exp(\Omega_{t+1})^{\top}.

(76)

Then:

(1) Orbit invariance:
If
M_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, then
M_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
for all
t\geq 0
.

(2) Symplecticity:
On the phase space
T^{*}SO(d)
(or its equivalent left-trivialized representation), the Kick-Drift map is symplectic. Equivalently, it is a first-order instance of a Lie group variational integrator consistent with undamped Euler-Poincaré/rigid body Hamiltonian dynamics, with local truncation error
O(\eta^{2})
.

Proof.

(1) Orbit invariance.
Since
\Omega_{t+1}\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
, we have
A_{t+1}:=\exp(\Omega_{t+1})\in SO(d)
. If
M_{t}=Q_{t}\Lambda Q_{t}^{\top}
, then

M_{t+1}=A_{t+1}Q_{t}\Lambda Q_{t}^{\top}A_{t+1}^{\top}=(A_{t+1}Q_{t})\Lambda(A_{t+1}Q_{t})^{\top}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}.

(77)

The result follows by induction.

(2) Symplecticity.
On
SO(d)
, introduce the momentum variable
\Pi=\mathcal{I}\Omega\in\mathfrak{so}(d)^{*}\simeq\mathfrak{so}(d)
(identified via the Frobenius pairing). Consider the Hamiltonian

H_{t}(Q,\Pi):=T(\Pi)+V_{t}(Q\Lambda Q^{\top}),\qquad T(\Pi)=\frac{1}{2}\langle\Pi,\mathcal{I}^{-1}\Pi\rangle_{F},

(78)

where
V_{t}
is the observation potential (negative log-likelihood or its noiseless limit). By Theorem
7
, the orbit gradient satisfies

\mathrm{grad}_{\mathcal{O}}V_{t}(M)=\left[\mathcal{I}_{M}^{-1}\left(\frac{m}{2}\tau(M,C_{t})\right),M\right],

(79)

showing that
\tau
is precisely the dual representation of the potential energy’s variation with respect to Lie algebra directions (i.e., the “generalized torque”).

Split the Hamiltonian as
H_{t}=T+V_{t}
. Under the canonical symplectic form
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
on
T^{*}SO(d)
, the following sub-flows are both symplectic:

Kick sub-flow (potential only):
Fix
Q
and update momentum:

\Pi_{t+1}=\Pi_{t}+\eta\tau_{t}.

(80)

This is the time-
\eta
map of Hamiltonian
H_{V}=V_{t}
. Since
H_{V}
contains no momentum,
Q
remains fixed while
\Pi
translates by
-\partial_{Q}V_{t}
; the translation amount is given by
\tau_{t}
. Hamiltonian flow preserves
\omega_{\mathrm{can}}
, so Kick is symplectic.

Drift sub-flow (kinetic only):
Fix
\Pi
and advance configuration by group exponential:

Q_{t+1}=\exp(\eta\mathcal{I}^{-1}\Pi_{t+1})Q_{t}.

(81)

This is the time-
\eta
map of Hamiltonian
H_{T}=T
. Since
H_{T}
contains no
Q
,
\Pi
remains constant while
Q
advances along the left-trivialized geodesic. Drift is therefore symplectic.

Since both Kick and Drift are symplectic, their composition
F=F_{\mathrm{Drift}}\circ F_{\mathrm{Kick}}
is also symplectic:

F^{*}\omega_{\mathrm{can}}=\omega_{\mathrm{can}}.

(82)

Furthermore, Kick-Drift is a first-order splitting (symplectic Euler) discretization, yielding local truncation error
O(\eta^{2})
.
∎

E.3
Proof of Theorem
2
(Zero-Lag Tracking)

Theorem 11

(Zero-Lag Tracking: Linearization to Damped Harmonic Oscillator)
.

Under noiseless observations
C_{t}=M_{t}^{\star}
with constant angular velocity
\Omega^{\star}
satisfying
M_{t+1}^{\star}=\exp(\Omega^{\star})M_{t}^{\star}\exp(\Omega^{\star})^{\top}
, there exists a nonempty parameter domain
\mathcal{D}\subset(\eta,\gamma)
such that the error dynamics of the second-order K-GMRF is Lyapunov stable at the equilibrium trajectory
(M_{t},\Omega_{t})=(M_{t}^{\star},\Omega^{\star})
. For sufficiently small initial error, local exponential convergence holds with zero steady-state error:
d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})\to 0
.

Proof.

For
(M_{t}^{\star},\Omega^{\star})
to be an equilibrium trajectory, the damping term must act on the
velocity error
rather than the absolute velocity. Define the velocity error
u_{t}:=\Omega_{t}-\Omega^{\star}
and consider the second-order discrete skeleton (Kick-Drift):

u_{t+1}=(1-\gamma)u_{t}+\eta\mathcal{I}^{-1}\tau_{t},\qquad M_{t+1}=\exp(\Omega^{\star}+u_{t+1})M_{t}\exp(\Omega^{\star}+u_{t+1})^{\top}.

(83)

Under this formulation,
u\equiv 0
and
\xi\equiv 0
correspond to
\Omega\equiv\Omega^{\star}
and
M\equiv M^{\star}
, which is indeed a fixed trajectory.

Step 1: First-order form of error kinematics.
From

R_{t+1}=Q_{t+1}Q_{t+1}^{\star\top}=\exp(\Omega^{\star}+u_{t+1})Q_{t}Q_{t}^{\star\top}\exp(-\Omega^{\star})=\exp(\Omega^{\star}+u_{t+1})R_{t}\exp(-\Omega^{\star}),

(84)

with
\xi_{t}=\log(R_{t})
small, the Baker-Campbell-Hausdorff (BCH) formula gives the first-order approximation:

\xi_{t+1}=\xi_{t}+u_{t+1}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}|u_{t+1}|_{F}).

(85)

Step 2: Torque linearization.
From Section
E.1
:

\tau_{t}=[M_{t}^{\star},M_{t}]=-\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi_{t}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}),\qquad\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi:=-[M_{t}^{\star},[\xi,M_{t}^{\star}]],

(86)

and under the spectral gap condition, there exists
\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})>0
such that
\langle\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi,\xi\rangle_{F}\geq\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})|\xi|_{F}^{2}
.

Step 3: Damped harmonic oscillator difference equation.
Substituting the linearization into the Kick update:

u_{t+1}=(1-\gamma)u_{t}-\eta\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi_{t}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}).

(87)

Using the kinematics
u_{t}=\xi_{t}-\xi_{t-1}+o(|\xi|)
(from
\xi_{t}=\xi_{t-1}+u_{t}+o(|\xi|)
), eliminating
u
yields the leading-order difference equation:

\xi_{t+1}-\xi_{t}=(1-\gamma)(\xi_{t}-\xi_{t-1})-\eta\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{t}\xi_{t}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}).

(88)

Ignoring higher-order terms and treating
\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{t}
as a positive-definite operator with time-invariant spectrum under the conjugate equivariance of
M_{t}^{\star}
, the scalar eigenmode
e_{t}
satisfies:

e_{t+1}-e_{t}=(1-\gamma)(e_{t}-e_{t-1})-\eta\kappa e_{t},

(89)

where
\kappa>0
is an eigenvalue of
\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}
. This is a discrete damped harmonic oscillator with characteristic polynomial:

r^{2}-(2-\gamma-\eta\kappa)r+(1-\gamma)=0.

(90)

Step 4: Stability domain and exponential convergence.
The Schur stability criterion for this second-order polynomial gives the necessary and sufficient conditions: both roots lie inside the unit circle if and only if

0<\gamma<2,\qquad 0<\eta\kappa<2(2-\gamma).

(91)

Let
\kappa_{\max}
be the largest eigenvalue of
\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}
(bounded above by the local Lipschitz constant from Theorem
9
). Define

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{(\eta,\gamma):0<\gamma<2,\quad 0<\eta<\frac{2(2-\gamma)}{\kappa_{\max}}\right\}.

(92)

This ensures simultaneous stability of all modes. For sufficiently small initial error (ensuring linearization validity):

\xi_{t}\to 0,\qquad u_{t}=\xi_{t}-\xi_{t-1}\to 0,

(93)

hence

d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})=|\xi_{t}|_{F}+o(|\xi_{t}|_{F})\to 0,

(94)

and
\Omega_{t}=\Omega^{\star}+u_{t}\to\Omega^{\star}
. Zero-lag tracking is achieved.
∎

Remark 2

(On the damping formulation)
.

If damping were applied directly as Rayleigh-type
-\gamma\mathcal{I}\Omega
(contracting absolute angular velocity), then for
\Omega^{\star}\neq 0
, the pair
(M_{t},\Omega_{t})=(M_{t}^{\star},\Omega^{\star})
would not satisfy the equilibrium condition: at
M=M^{\star}
the torque vanishes, but the damping term is nonzero, causing
\Omega
to decay. Maintaining nonzero angular velocity would require persistent external torque, hence nonzero configuration error. This scenario leads to inevitable phase error, contradicting the zero steady-state error claim.

E.4
Proof of Theorem
3
(Inevitable Lag of First-Order Methods)

Theorem 12

(Inevitable Lag of EMA: Structural Limitation of First-Order Methods)
.

Consider any first-order (overdamped) orbit gradient flow or equivalent EMA-type update without explicit velocity state. Under constant angular velocity rotation, the steady-state error satisfies

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})\geq c\cdot|\Omega^{\star}|_{F},

(95)

where
c>0
depends on the step size and smoothing strength. Zero steady-state error is unachievable while maintaining stability.

Proof.

Consider the typical first-order overdamped form: update
M
along the negative orbit gradient (a geometric version of EMA):

M_{t+1}=\exp(-\eta G(M_{t},C_{t}))M_{t}\exp(-\eta G(M_{t},C_{t}))^{\top},

(96)

where
G(M_{t},C_{t})\in\mathfrak{so}(d)
is the gradient direction generator. By Theorem
7
,
G
is linearly related to
\tau(M_{t},C_{t})
(via the inertia operator
\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}
), hence in a neighborhood of
M_{t}\approx M_{t}^{\star}
, its first-order approximation is of the same order as
\tau_{t}
.

Define the error
\xi_{t}=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M_{t})
. Since the target rotates by
\Omega^{\star}
each step while the algorithm carries no velocity state, the error must include a “target drift” term at first order. Ignoring noncommutative higher-order terms:

\xi_{t+1}=\xi_{t}+\Omega^{\star}-\eta\mathcal{A}_{t}\xi_{t}+O(|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}),

(97)

where the linear operator
\mathcal{A}_{t}\simeq\mathcal{I}^{-1}\mathcal{K}_{t}
satisfies
\mathcal{A}_{t}\succeq\underline{\kappa}I
(for
\underline{\kappa}>0
) and
\|\mathcal{A}_{t}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\bar{\kappa}
under the spectral gap condition.

Ignoring higher-order terms and taking the time-invariant approximation
\mathcal{A}_{t}\equiv\mathcal{A}
, we obtain the linear system:

\xi_{t+1}=(I-\eta\mathcal{A})\xi_{t}+\Omega^{\star}.

(98)

For stability, we require
\rho(I-\eta\mathcal{A})<1
; in particular, along the largest eigenvalue direction:

0<\eta\bar{\kappa}<2.

(99)

When stable, the steady-state solution exists and is unique:

\xi_{\infty}=(\eta\mathcal{A})^{-1}\Omega^{\star}.

(100)

When
\Omega^{\star}\neq 0
, we have
\xi_{\infty}\neq 0
, so the steady-state error is strictly nonzero. Furthermore, using
\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\bar{\kappa}
:

|\xi_{\infty}|_{F}=|(\eta\mathcal{A})^{-1}\Omega^{\star}|_{F}\geq\frac{|\Omega^{\star}|_{F}}{\eta\|\mathcal{A}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}\geq\frac{|\Omega^{\star}|_{F}}{\eta\bar{\kappa}}.

(101)

Combining with the stability constraint
\eta\bar{\kappa}<2
:

|\xi_{\infty}|_{F}\geq\frac{|\Omega^{\star}|_{F}}{2}.

(102)

Since
d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})=|\xi_{t}|_{F}+o(|\xi_{t}|_{F})
, there exists a constant
c>0
(taking
c=1/2
in normalized units) such that

\liminf_{t\to\infty}d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})\geq c|\Omega^{\star}|_{F}.

(103)

This shows that first-order overdamped/EMA methods must maintain nonzero phase error to generate gradient “driving force” that counteracts the constant angular velocity input. Zero steady-state error is structurally unachievable under stability constraints.
∎

Appendix F
Stochastic Stability Analysis

This section establishes stochastic stability bounds by combining the deterministic skeleton analysis with statistical concentration properties of the torque.

F.1
Auxiliary Lemmas

We first establish three auxiliary lemmas that bridge the geometric, statistical, and nonstationarity aspects into a unified energy framework.

Lemma 6

(Energy Equivalence)
.

Suppose
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}>0
(simple spectrum, whitened identifiability) and consider a neighborhood
\mathcal{B}_{r}(M_{t}^{\star})=\{M\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}:d_{\mathcal{O}}(M,M_{t}^{\star})\leq r\}
where the logarithmic map
\xi=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M)
is unique and smooth. Define

u_{t}:=\Omega_{t}-\Omega_{t}^{\star},\qquad\xi_{t}:=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M_{t}).

(104)

For any positive-definite inertia operator
\mathcal{I}\succ 0
on
\mathfrak{so}(d)
, let

T(u)=\frac{1}{2}\langle u,\mathcal{I}u\rangle_{F},\qquad\widetilde{V}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star}):=\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}.

(105)

Then there exist constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
(\Lambda,\sigma^{2},\mathcal{I},r)
such that for all
M_{t}\in\mathcal{B}_{r}(M_{t}^{\star})
:

c_{1}(|u_{t}|_{F}^{2}+|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2})\leq\mathcal{E}_{t}:=T(u_{t})+\widetilde{V}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})\leq c_{2}(|u_{t}|_{F}^{2}+|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}).

(106)

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{I}\succ 0
, there exist spectral bounds
\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{I})\leq\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I})
such that

\frac{\lambda_{\min}(\mathcal{I})}{2}|u|_{F}^{2}\leq T(u)\leq\frac{\lambda_{\max}(\mathcal{I})}{2}|u|_{F}^{2}.

(107)

In the neighborhood where
r
is small enough for the logarithmic map to be unique,
\widetilde{V}=\frac{1}{2}|\xi|^{2}
is locally equivalent to
d_{\mathcal{O}}^{2}
(since
\xi
is the group logarithm coordinate and
d_{\mathcal{O}}(M,M^{\star})=|\xi|_{F}
in this representation). Combining these bounds yields the result.
∎

Lemma 7

(Lipschitz Continuity of Torque)
.

Fix
C
and
\sigma^{2}>0
. For any
M_{1},M_{2}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
:

|\tau(M_{1},C)-\tau(M_{2},C)|_{F}\leq L_{\tau}(C)\cdot d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{1},M_{2}),

(108)

where

L_{\tau}(C)\lesssim\|C\|_{F}\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{3}\lesssim\|C\|_{F}(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{-3}.

(109)

Proof.

Recall
S(M)=M+\sigma^{2}I
and
\tau(M,C)=S(M)^{-1}[C,M]S(M)^{-1}
. Expand the difference:

\displaystyle\tau(M_{1},C)-\tau(M_{2},C)

\displaystyle=(S_{1}^{-1}-S_{2}^{-1})[C,M_{1}]S_{1}^{-1}+S_{2}^{-1}([C,M_{1}]-[C,M_{2}])S_{1}^{-1}

(110)

\displaystyle\quad+S_{2}^{-1}[C,M_{2}](S_{1}^{-1}-S_{2}^{-1}).

(111)

Using the identity
S_{1}^{-1}-S_{2}^{-1}=S_{1}^{-1}(S_{2}-S_{1})S_{2}^{-1}=S_{1}^{-1}(M_{2}-M_{1})S_{2}^{-1}
, and noting that

|[C,M_{1}]-[C,M_{2}]|_{F}=|[C,M_{1}-M_{2}]|_{F}\leq 2\|C\|_{\mathrm{op}}|M_{1}-M_{2}|_{F},

(112)

combined with
\|S^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq(\lambda_{d}+\sigma^{2})^{-1}
and the local equivalence
|M_{1}-M_{2}|_{F}\lesssim d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{1},M_{2})
on the compact homogeneous manifold, we obtain the stated Lipschitz form.
∎

Lemma 8

(Target Variation Energy Perturbation)
.

Let the energy be
\mathcal{E}_{t}=T(u_{t})+\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|^{2}
as in Lemma
6
. When the reference point changes from
(M_{t}^{\star},\Omega_{t}^{\star})
to
(M_{t+1}^{\star},\Omega_{t+1}^{\star})
(with the same estimate
(M_{t},\Omega_{t})
fixed), there exists a constant
C
such that

\left|\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(t+1)}-\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(t)}\right|\leq C|\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}-\Omega_{t}^{\star}|_{F}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(t)}},

(113)

where
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(t)}
denotes the energy defined with respect to
(M_{t}^{\star},\Omega_{t}^{\star})
, and
\mathcal{E}_{t}^{(t+1)}
similarly.

Proof.

Velocity error term:

u_{t}=\Omega_{t}-\Omega_{t}^{\star}
changes to
u_{t}^{\prime}=\Omega_{t}-\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}=u_{t}-(\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}-\Omega_{t}^{\star})
, hence

|T(u_{t}^{\prime})-T(u_{t})|\leq\|\mathcal{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}|u_{t}||\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|+\frac{\|\mathcal{I}\|_{\mathrm{op}}}{2}|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|^{2}\lesssim|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|\sqrt{T(u_{t})}+|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|^{2}.

(114)

Configuration error term:
The change
M_{t}^{\star}\mapsto M_{t+1}^{\star}
is a small rotation
\exp(\Omega_{t}^{\star})
. In the small neighborhood, the logarithmic map is Lipschitz with respect to the base point, so
|\xi_{t}^{\prime}-\xi_{t}|\lesssim|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|
, and thus

\left|\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}^{\prime}|^{2}-\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|^{2}\right|\lesssim|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}||\xi_{t}|+|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|^{2}.

(115)

Combining and using
|\xi_{t}|+|u_{t}|\lesssim\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}}
(from Lemma
6
) yields the perturbation bound of the form
C|\Delta\Omega^{\star}|\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}}
.
∎

F.2
Proof of Theorem
4
(Energy Contraction to Noise Ball)

Theorem 13

(Stochastic Stability: Expected Energy Contraction to Noise Ball)
.

Under the following assumptions:

(1) Observation:
mC_{t}\sim\mathcal{W}_{d}(m,S^{\star})
with
S^{\star}=M^{\star}+\sigma^{2}I
, and
\{C_{t}\}
conditionally independent.

(2) Identifiability:
\Lambda
has simple spectrum,
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}>0
.

(3) Locality: Initial condition
M_{0}
satisfies
d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{0},M^{\star})\leq r
where
\xi=\log_{M^{\star}}(M)
is unique; parameter choices ensure iterates remain in this neighborhood.

(4) Algorithm: K-GMRF with

\tau_{t}=S_{t}^{-1}[C_{t},M_{t}]S_{t}^{-1},\quad u_{t+1}=(1-\gamma)u_{t}+\eta\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}(\tau_{t}),\quad M_{t+1}=\exp(\Omega_{t+1})M_{t}\exp(\Omega_{t+1})^{\top},

(116)

where
u_{t}=\Omega_{t}-\Omega^{\star}
and the “frozen target” setting means
\Omega_{t}^{\star}\equiv\Omega^{\star}
.

(5) Stability domain: There exists
L_{H}<\infty
(local Jacobian/effective stiffness upper bound) such that

0<\gamma<2,\qquad 0<\eta<\frac{2(2-\gamma)}{L_{H}}.

(117)

Define

\xi_{t}:=\log_{M^{\star}}(M_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{t}:=T(u_{t})+\widetilde{V}(M_{t},M^{\star})=\frac{1}{2}\langle u_{t},\mathcal{I}u_{t}\rangle_{F}+\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|_{F}^{2}.

(118)

Then there exist constants
\kappa=\kappa(\eta,\gamma,L_{H})\in(0,1)
and

\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}=\frac{\eta^{2}}{m}C_{\mathrm{noise}}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})

(119)

such that for all
t
:

\boxed{\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq(1-\kappa)\mathcal{E}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}.}

(120)

Consequently,
\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]\leq\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}/\kappa
.

Proof.

The proof proceeds in four steps.

Step 1: Decompose torque into mean force and martingale noise.
Let

g_{t}:=\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}(\tau_{t}),\quad\bar{\tau}_{t}:=\mathbb{E}[\tau_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=\tau(M_{t},S^{\star}),\quad\tilde{\tau}_{t}:=\tau_{t}-\bar{\tau}_{t}.

(121)

By Theorem
8
(unbiasedness),
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{\tau}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
, and (variance bound):

\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{\tau}_{t}|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\frac{K_{\tau}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})}{m}.

(122)

Thus
g_{t}=\bar{g}_{t}+\tilde{g}_{t}
where
\bar{g}_{t}:=\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}(\bar{\tau}_{t})
and
\tilde{g}_{t}:=\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}(\tilde{\tau}_{t})
, with

\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{g}_{t}|_{F}^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\|\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\frac{K_{\tau}}{m}.

(123)

Step 2: Local error dynamics (first-order recursion for
\xi
).
By Proposition
1
(structure preservation),
M_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda}
, so in the small neighborhood:

M_{t}=\exp(\xi_{t})M^{\star}\exp(\xi_{t})^{\top}.

(124)

Since
M_{t+1}=\exp(\Omega_{t+1})M_{t}\exp(\Omega_{t+1})^{\top}=\exp(\Omega_{t+1})\exp(\xi_{t})M^{\star}\exp(\xi_{t})^{\top}\exp(\Omega_{t+1})^{\top}
, with
|\xi_{t}|,|\Omega_{t+1}|
small, BCH expansion gives (for some constant
c_{\mathrm{BCH}}
):

\xi_{t+1}=\xi_{t}+u_{t+1}+r_{t+1}^{(1)},\qquad|r_{t+1}^{(1)}|\leq c_{\mathrm{BCH}}(|\xi_{t}|_{F}|u_{t+1}|_{F}+|u_{t+1}|^{2}).

(125)

Step 3: Deterministic part gives contraction (strong convexity/effective stiffness).
Consider the “mean system” (noise removed):
u_{t+1}=(1-\gamma)u_{t}+\eta\bar{g}_{t}
. By Theorem
7
,
\bar{g}_{t}
is the normalized natural gradient generator on the orbit; near
M^{\star}
, Theorem
9
gives orbit-direction strong convexity, so the local linearization is equivalent to a “stiffness” operator
H\succ 0
:

\bar{g}_{t}=-H\xi_{t}+r_{t}^{(2)},\qquad\langle\xi_{t},H\xi_{t}\rangle\geq\mu|\xi_{t}|^{2},\qquad\|H\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq L_{H},

(126)

with
|r_{t}^{(2)}|\leq c|\xi_{t}|^{2}
(second-order remainder).

Combining, in the small neighborhood the system is approximately (linear dominant):

\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{t+1}\\
u_{t+1}\end{pmatrix}=A\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{t}\\
u_{t}\end{pmatrix}+\eta B\tilde{g}_{t}+\text{(higher order)},

(127)

where

A=\begin{pmatrix}I-\eta H&(1-\gamma)I\\
-\eta H&(1-\gamma)I\end{pmatrix},\qquad B=\begin{pmatrix}I\\
I\end{pmatrix}.

(128)

Stability domain role:
For each eigenvalue
\kappa_{i}\in(0,L_{H}]
of
H
, the scalar mode satisfies the characteristic polynomial from Section
E.3
:

r^{2}-(2-\gamma-\eta\kappa_{i})r+(1-\gamma)=0.

(129)

Under
0<\gamma<2
and
0<\eta\kappa_{i}<2(2-\gamma)
, roots lie inside the unit circle, so
A
is Schur stable. This condition for all
\kappa_{i}\leq L_{H}
is equivalent to
0<\eta<2(2-\gamma)/L_{H}
.

Step 4: Construct discrete Lyapunov and obtain expected contraction plus noise term.
Since
A
is Schur stable, there exists a unique symmetric positive-definite matrix
P\succ 0
solving the discrete Lyapunov equation:

P-A^{\top}PA=I.

(130)

Define the quadratic Lyapunov function
\mathcal{L}_{t}:=z_{t}^{\top}Pz_{t}
where
z_{t}=(\xi_{t},u_{t})^{\top}
. Ignoring higher-order terms (or absorbing them into smaller
\eta,r
) and using
\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=0
:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]=z_{t}^{\top}A^{\top}PAz_{t}+\eta^{2}\mathbb{E}[\tilde{g}_{t}^{\top}B^{\top}PB\tilde{g}_{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\text{(higher order)}.

(131)

By the Lyapunov equation,
z_{t}^{\top}A^{\top}PAz_{t}=z_{t}^{\top}(P-I)z_{t}=\mathcal{L}_{t}-|z_{t}|^{2}
. Thus:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\mathcal{L}_{t}-|z_{t}|^{2}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{\max}(B^{\top}PB)\mathbb{E}[|\tilde{g}_{t}|^{2}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]+\text{(higher order)}.

(132)

Using the variance bound from Step 1:

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq\mathcal{L}_{t}-|z_{t}|^{2}+\eta^{2}\lambda_{\max}(B^{\top}PB)\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\frac{K_{\tau}}{m}+\text{(higher order)}.

(133)

Finally, using the spectral bounds
\lambda_{\min}(P)|z_{t}|^{2}\leq\mathcal{L}_{t}\leq\lambda_{\max}(P)|z_{t}|^{2}
to convert
-|z_{t}|^{2}
to
-\kappa\mathcal{L}_{t}
:

-|z_{t}|^{2}\leq-\frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}(P)}\mathcal{L}_{t}.

(134)

Setting

\kappa:=\frac{1}{\lambda_{\max}(P)}\in(0,1),\qquad\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}:=\eta^{2}\lambda_{\max}(B^{\top}PB)\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\frac{K_{\tau}}{m},

(135)

we obtain
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{L}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq(1-\kappa)\mathcal{L}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
. By Lemma
6
(
\mathcal{L}_{t}
equivalent to
\mathcal{E}_{t}
), the result follows with constants absorbed.
∎

F.3
Proof of Theorem
5
(Master Theorem: Time-Averaged Tracking Risk)

Theorem 14

(Tracking Error Upper Bound: Three-Term Decomposition with Phase Transition)
.

Under the following assumptions:

(1) The true angular velocity satisfies the bounded total variation condition:

V_{\Omega}:=\sum_{t=0}^{T-1}|\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}-\Omega_{t}^{\star}|_{F}\leq V_{\max}.

(136)

(2) Observations are rank-
m
Wishart satisfying Theorem
8
.

(3) Damped K-GMRF with
(\eta,\gamma)\in\mathcal{D}
(stability domain of Theorem
13
), and initial conditions in a local neighborhood where
\xi_{t}=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M_{t})
is defined throughout.

(4) Define the time-averaged risk:

\mathcal{R}_{T}:=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})^{2}].

(137)

Then there exist explicit functions
\Phi
(constants depending on
d,\Lambda,\sigma^{2},\eta,\gamma
) such that:

\boxed{\mathcal{R}_{T}\leq\underbrace{\frac{C_{3}d_{0}^{2}(1-\kappa)^{T}}{T}}_{\Phi_{\mathrm{trans}}}+\underbrace{\frac{C_{1}\sigma^{2}}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}}_{\Phi_{\mathrm{stat}}}+\underbrace{\frac{C_{2}V_{\Omega}}{\gamma T}}_{\Phi_{\mathrm{ns}}}}

(138)

with phase transition boundaries:

\bullet
If
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\downarrow 0
, then
C_{1}
diverges (loss of identifiability).

\bullet
If
(\eta,\gamma)\notin\mathcal{D}
, then
\kappa\leq 0
(contraction failure/divergence).

The order estimates are:
C_{1}=\Theta(1/\kappa)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
,
C_{2}=\Theta(1/\kappa)\cdot\mathrm{poly}(d,\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
,
C_{3}=\Theta(1)
.

Proof.

Step 1: Write Theorem
13
as “moving reference + external perturbation” version.
Let

u_{t}=\Omega_{t}-\Omega_{t}^{\star},\qquad\xi_{t}=\log_{M_{t}^{\star}}(M_{t}),\qquad\mathcal{E}_{t}=T(u_{t})+\frac{1}{2}|\xi_{t}|^{2}.

(139)

When
\Omega_{t}^{\star}
is no longer constant, the
u_{t+1}
recursion acquires an additional term (“acceleration perturbation”). In the analysis framework where damping acts on velocity error (consistent with Section
E.3
):

u_{t+1}=(1-\gamma)u_{t}+\eta\mathcal{I}_{M_{t}}^{-1}(\tau_{t})-(\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}-\Omega_{t}^{\star}).

(140)

The first-order contraction inequality of Theorem
13
is interrupted by a “perturbation term.” Using Lemma
8
(reference change energy perturbation):

\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq(1-\kappa)\mathcal{E}_{t}+\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}+C_{\Omega}|\Omega_{t+1}^{\star}-\Omega_{t}^{\star}|_{F}\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}},

(141)

where
\kappa,\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
are of the same order as in Theorem
13
, and
C_{\Omega}
depends only on
(\Lambda,\sigma^{2},\mathcal{I},r)
.

Step 2: Establish linear recursion for
\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}}
(to obtain linear
V_{\Omega}
).
Let
e_{t}:=\sqrt{\mathcal{E}_{t}}\geq 0
. Using the inequality

\sqrt{(1-\kappa)e_{t}^{2}+a_{t}e_{t}+b}\leq\sqrt{1-\kappa}\,e_{t}+\frac{a_{t}}{2\sqrt{1-\kappa}}+\sqrt{b}\quad(a_{t}\geq 0,b\geq 0),

(142)

with
a_{t}=C_{\Omega}|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|
and
b=\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
:

\mathbb{E}[e_{t+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\leq q\,e_{t}+\alpha|\Delta\Omega_{t}^{\star}|+\beta,

(143)

where

q:=\sqrt{1-\kappa}\in(0,1),\qquad\alpha:=\frac{C_{\Omega}}{2\sqrt{1-\kappa}},\qquad\beta:=\sqrt{\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}}.

(144)

Iterating and taking full expectation:

\mathbb{E}[e_{t}]\leq q^{t}e_{0}+\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q^{t-1-s}(\alpha|\Delta\Omega_{s}^{\star}|+\beta).

(145)

Summing over
t=1,\ldots,T
and exchanging summation order:

\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[e_{t}]\leq e_{0}\sum_{t=1}^{T}q^{t}+\alpha\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}|\Delta\Omega_{s}^{\star}|\sum_{t=s+1}^{T}q^{t-1-s}+\beta\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q^{t-1-s}.

(146)

Using geometric series bounds:

\sum_{t=s+1}^{T}q^{t-1-s}\leq\frac{1}{1-q},\qquad\sum_{t=1}^{T}\sum_{s=0}^{t-1}q^{t-1-s}\leq\frac{T}{1-q}.

(147)

Thus:

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[e_{t}]\leq\underbrace{\frac{q(1-q^{T})}{T(1-q)}e_{0}}_{\text{transient}}+\underbrace{\frac{\alpha}{T(1-q)}\sum_{s=0}^{T-1}|\Delta\Omega_{s}^{\star}|}_{\text{nonstationarity}}+\underbrace{\frac{\beta}{1-q}}_{\text{statistical noise}}.

(148)

Since
1-q=1-\sqrt{1-\kappa}\asymp\kappa
, the second term gives

\frac{\alpha}{T(1-q)}V_{\Omega}=O\left(\frac{V_{\Omega}}{\kappa T}\right).

(149)

In damped systems,
\kappa
is typically of order
\gamma
(especially away from stability boundary,
\kappa\gtrsim c\gamma
), so this can be written as
O(V_{\Omega}/(\gamma T))
.

Step 3: Return from
e_{t}
to
\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]
and squared distance.
By Jensen’s inequality and
(a+b+c)^{2}\leq 3(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2})
:

\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t}]=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[e_{t}^{2}]\lesssim\underbrace{\frac{e_{0}^{2}q^{2T}}{T}}_{\text{transient}}+\underbrace{\frac{\beta^{2}}{(1-q)^{2}}}_{\text{statistical}}+\underbrace{\frac{V_{\Omega}}{T}\cdot\frac{1}{(1-q)^{2}}}_{\text{nonstationarity (linear }V_{\Omega}\text{)}},

(150)

where
\beta^{2}=\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
and
(1-q)^{2}\asymp\kappa^{2}
. Absorbing constants into
C_{1},C_{2},C_{3}
yields the three-term form.

Finally, using Lemma
6
(
\mathcal{E}_{t}\gtrsim|\xi_{t}|^{2}
) and
|\xi_{t}|=d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})
(in local logarithm coordinates):

\mathcal{R}_{T}=\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{t},M_{t}^{\star})^{2}]\lesssim\frac{1}{T}\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mathbb{E}[\mathcal{E}_{t}],

(151)

yielding the stated upper bound.
∎

Remark 3

(Why the statistical term diverges as
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{-2}
)
.

The
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{-2}
divergence arises from two facts in the established proof chain:

(1)
Identifiability degradation:
Theorem
9
shows that as
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}\to 0
, the orbit-direction strong convexity constant
\mu(\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}})\to 0
. This means the potential energy/score information about the rotation direction weakens.

(2)
Noise amplification through preconditioning:
The algorithm drive is
\mathcal{I}_{M}^{-1}(\tau_{t})
. When the spectral gap shrinks,
\mathcal{I}_{M}
becomes “soft” in the corresponding mode, and its inverse amplifies random torque noise. This amplification rate is related to the difference of
(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})^{-1}
, i.e.,
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
. Thus
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
necessarily contains a negative power of
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
, causing the steady-state error to diverge.

Whether
\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}
enters through the contraction constant
\kappa
(via
\mu
) or the noise term
\sigma_{\mathrm{eff}}^{2}
(via
\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}\|
), the final result exhibits the “phase transition explosion”
\propto\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{-2}
.

F.4
Proof of Theorem
6
(Minimax Lower Bound)

We establish the information-theoretic lower bound via two constructions: a static subfamily (Le Cam two-point method) for the statistical term, and a change-point subfamily for the nonstationarity term.

Theorem 15

(Minimax Lower Bound)
.

Consider the class of fixed-gain online filters
\{\phi_{\theta}\}
with time-invariant parameters
\theta
, operating over the problem family
\mathcal{P}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2},m,V_{\Omega})
. The minimax risk satisfies

\inf_{\{\phi_{\theta}\}}\sup_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{R}_{T}\geq c_{1}\frac{\sigma^{2}}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}+c_{2}\frac{V_{\Omega}}{T}

(152)

for universal constants
c_{1},c_{2}>0
depending only on
(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
.

Proof.

Part I: Statistical lower bound via Le Cam’s method.
We construct a two-point testing problem on the static subfamily where
M_{t}^{\star}\equiv M^{\star}
for all
t
.

Fix
M_{0}=\Lambda
and select the “softest” direction
(i,j)
that minimizes

w_{ij}:=\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}.

(153)

Define
M_{1}=R(\varepsilon)\Lambda R(\varepsilon)^{\top}
where
R(\varepsilon)=\exp(\varepsilon E^{ij})
with
E^{ij}
the canonical skew-symmetric basis element satisfying
|E^{ij}|_{F}=\sqrt{2}
.

Under a single rank-
m
Wishart observation
C\sim\mathcal{W}_{d}(M+\sigma^{2}I,m)
, the KL divergence between the two hypotheses satisfies

D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{M_{0}}\|P_{M_{1}})\asymp m\varepsilon^{2}\cdot\frac{(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}}{(\lambda_{i}+\sigma^{2})(\lambda_{j}+\sigma^{2})}=m\varepsilon^{2}w_{ij}.

(154)

Since
w_{ij}\asymp\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2})
, we have
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\asymp m\varepsilon^{2}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}
.

Applying Le Cam’s lemma with Pinsker’s inequality: for any estimator
\hat{M}
,

\inf_{\hat{M}}\sup_{k\in\{0,1\}}\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{\mathcal{O}}(\hat{M},M_{k})^{2}]\gtrsim d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{0},M_{1})^{2}(1-\mathrm{TV}(P_{0},P_{1})).

(155)

Choosing
\varepsilon
such that
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\leq c_{0}
(constant) ensures
\mathrm{TV}\leq 1/2
. Since
d_{\mathcal{O}}(M_{0},M_{1})^{2}\asymp\varepsilon^{2}
in the local logarithm coordinates, we obtain

\inf_{\hat{M}}\sup_{k}\mathbb{E}_{k}[d_{\mathcal{O}}(\hat{M},M_{k})^{2}]\gtrsim\frac{1}{m\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2}}\cdot\mathrm{poly}(\Lambda,\sigma^{2}).

(156)

Part II: Nonstationarity lower bound via change-point detection.
We construct two trajectory sequences that differ only at one time point.

Fix
t_{0}\in\{1,\ldots,T-1\}
. Define:

•

Sequence A: Apply rotation
\exp(\delta E^{ij})
at time
t_{0}
, identity otherwise.

•

Sequence B: Apply rotation
\exp(\delta E^{ij})
at time
t_{0}+1
, identity otherwise.

Both sequences have total variation
V_{\Omega}=\delta\sqrt{2}
.

The observation distributions differ only at one time step, so
D_{\mathrm{KL}}(P_{A}\|P_{B})=O(m\delta^{2}\Delta_{\mathrm{wh}}^{2})
. By the same Le Cam argument, any estimator incurs error
\gtrsim\delta^{2}
with constant probability when
D_{\mathrm{KL}}\leq c_{0}
.

Averaging over the unknown change-point location
t_{0}
(uniform prior on
\{1,\ldots,T-1\}
), the time-averaged risk satisfies

\mathcal{R}_{T}\gtrsim\frac{1}{T}\cdot\delta^{2}=\frac{V_{\Omega}^{2}}{2T}.

(157)

In the regime where
V_{\Omega}
is small (the tracking-relevant regime), this gives the
V_{\Omega}/T
scaling.

Combining both bounds.
Taking the supremum over both subfamilies yields the stated lower bound. Comparing with Theorem
5
, K-GMRF achieves the minimax rate in both the statistical (
1/m
) and nonstationarity (
V_{\Omega}/T
) terms.
∎

Remark 4

(Restriction to fixed-gain filters)
.

The lower bound applies to fixed-gain online filters where the update rule
\hat{M}_{t}=\phi_{\theta}(\hat{M}_{t-1},C_{t})
uses time-invariant parameters
\theta
. This class includes K-GMRF, Riemannian EMA, Kalman filters, and other practical tracking algorithms. If full-history batch estimators
\hat{M}_{t}=\phi_{t}(C_{1:t})
were permitted, the static term could be improved to
O(1/(mT))
by temporal aggregation, but this violates the online/causal constraint central to tracking applications.

H.5
Practical Tuning Guidelines

This section provides practitioners with heuristics for selecting K-GMRF hyperparameters
(\eta,\gamma)
.

H.5.1
Stability Domain

From Theorem
4
, the stability domain is:

\mathcal{D}=\{(\eta,\gamma):0<\gamma<2,\;\eta<2(2-\gamma)/\kappa_{\max}\},

(161)

where
\kappa_{\max}=\|\mathcal{I}^{-1}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\cdot L_{\tau}
depends on the inertia tensor and torque Lipschitz constant.

Practical rule of thumb:

1.

Start with
\gamma=0.95
(high damping, conservative).

2.

Set
\eta=0.05
(small step size).

3.

If the tracker
oscillates
: increase
\gamma
toward
1.0
.

4.

If the tracker
lags
: decrease
\gamma
toward
0.8
.

5.

If the tracker
diverges
: halve
\eta
.
