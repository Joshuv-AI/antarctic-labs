Normalized solutions to mass supercritical Schrödinger equations with radial potentials
Pablo Carrillo, Louis Jeanjean

Abstract
We study the stationary nonlinear Schrödinger equation − Δ ​ u + V ⁡ ( x ) ​ u + λ ​ u = | u | q − 2 ​ u , u ∈ H 1 ​ ( ℝ N ) , N ≥ 2 , -\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=|u|^{q-2}u,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\quad N\geq 2, (0.1) where V ∈ L ∞ ​ ( ℝ N ) V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N}) is a radial potential. In the L 2 L^{2} -supercritical regime, we show the existence of an explicit μ 0 > 0 \mu_{0}>0 such that, for any μ ∈ ( 0 , μ 0 ) \mu\in(0,\mu_{0}) , Equation ( 0.1 ) admits two solutions having L 2 L^{2} norm μ \mu . The potential V V is not assumed to have a sign, nor a specific behavior at infinity and only a low regularity is required. Our proof relies on the use of Morse type information, on some spectral arguments, and on a blow-up analysis developed in a radial setting.

Introduction

In this paper we are interested in the existence of solutions to the nonlinear Schrödinger equation with potential in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}

-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=|u|^{q-2}u,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

(1.1)

where it is assumed, all along, that
N\geq 2
,
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{*}.

We recall that
2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
, if
N\geq 3
and
2^{*}=+\infty
, if
N=2
.

Equations of the form of (
1.1
) appear from the study of time dependent nonlinear Schrödinger equations

-i\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\varphi(x,t)=\Delta_{x}\varphi(x,t)+f(x,\varphi(x,t)),

when searching for standing wave solutions of the form

\varphi(x,t)=e^{i\lambda t}u(x),

where
u
is a real function. Here we consider the case of a separable potential - power non-linearity

f(x,z)=V(x)+|z|^{q-2}z.

A notable direction in the study of Equation (
1.1
) consists in fixing the
L^{2}
norm of the desired solution, which physically is interpreted as searching for solutions where the mass of the particle is fixed. In this case, solutions to Equation (
1.1
) may be searched through variational methods and in fact arise as critical points of the
C^{2}
energy
functional

E(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}-\frac{1}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}

(1.2)

subject to the mass constraint

H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})=\{u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\colon\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\mu\},

(1.3)

where
\mu>0
is the desired norm. Note that the parameter
\lambda
, present in Equation (
1.1
), comes out here as a Lagrange multiplier and thus is actually an unknown of the problem.

Whenever
2<q<2+\frac{4}{N}
, it can be readily seen, using the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality (
2.1
), that the energy functional defined in (
1.2
) is bounded from below and coercive on
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, so solutions can be looked for via a global minimization approach. This case is known as the mass subcritical regime and after, some earlier works,
[
43
,
44
]
, its treatment started essentially with the works of P.-L. Lions
[
34
,
35
]
. This case is still under investigation and we would like to point out
[
24
,
26
]
for recent sharp results.

Whenever
2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{*}
we are in the mass supercritical regime and it may be seen by testing function that the Energy functional is unbounded from below in
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. As a result, one needs to look for other types of critical points such as local minima or critical points at some minimax level, typically lying at a mountain pass level. In this latter situation it is now well known that a central difficulty is the possible unboundedness of the Palais-Smale sequences (PS sequences for short) for
E
constrained to
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. Under the assumption that the problem is autonomous, namely that
V
is a constant, but considering more general nonlinearities this case we first handled in
[
27
]
. A key idea to tackling this difficulty was the introduction of a natural constraint related to the Pohozaev identity which furthermore allowed to solve additional compactness issues. Since then, many results on autonomous equations or systems have been obtained, still making use of such type of identity, see for example
[
3
,
6
,
7
,
11
,
22
,
25
]
. For more general nonlinearities we may refer the reader to

[
30
,
41
]
and for results concerning the Sobolev critical case
see
[
29
,
42
,
45
,
46
]
.

Turning back to cases where
V
is not a constant let us mention the case of trapping potentials, i.e.

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=+\infty.

In the study of Schrödinger systems like in
[
37
]
, this condition allowed to regain some compactness. Let us also mention
[
8
,
31
]
dealing with cases where the potential is partially confining.

Recently the case of non-trapping potentials has begun to be studied in the mass supercritical regime and specifically the case where
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0.
As already anticipated, one of the main issues is recovering enough compactness for the Palais Smale sequences. Inspired by the techniques used in the autonomous cases, most strategies are still based on the used of a Pohozaev type identity which now reads as

\frac{1}{q}\|u\|_{q}^{q}=\frac{2}{N(q-2)}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}-\frac{1}{q-2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}-\frac{2}{N(q-2)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u\nabla u\cdot x.

(1.4)

However, the presence of the additional last term makes the analysis more delicate and requires further assumptions on certain norms of
V(x)
and of

W(x):=|x|V(x)
.

To be more specific, in
[
4
]
the authors considered the case of non-negative potentials and either
V\in L^{\frac{N}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
or
V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. In the first case a solution to Equation (
1.1
) was obtained for every
\mu>0
under the assumption that
\|V\|_{\frac{N}{2}}
and
\|W\|_{N}
are below a certain explicit constant; in the latter case they required
\|V\|_{\infty}
and
\|W\|_{\infty}
to be smaller certain thresholds depending on the mass
\mu>0
. These bounds allowed, somehow, to recover the estimates obtained in the autonomous cases. This paper was later complemented by
[
36
]
, where existence results involving non-positive potentials with
V\in L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for all
s\in[\frac{N}{2},+\infty]
were derived with similar smallness conditions (depending on the mass constraint
\mu>0
for all
s\neq\frac{N}{2}
) on the norms of both
V
and
W
. In both papers the assumption that the potential has a sign allows to benefit from a comparison between the considered problem and the associated problem at infinity.

Alternatively, the authors of
[
5
]
derived a procedure which involved studying the equation on large but
bounded
domains, then passing to the limit to recover
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. Whilst also making a Pohozaev identity come into play, this time the required estimates for the potential terms involved additional assumptions on the derivatives of the potential. It is also worth mentioning that in order to recover compactness they present a novel and interesting condition on the derivative of the potential at infinity.

Actually, in all the above mentioned papers, a Pohozaev identity of the type of (
1.4
) plays a crucial role at some stage of the proof. In order to be able to deduce information from it, it seems necessary to impose bounds on
W(x)=|x|V(x)
which leads to imposing a certain speed of decay of the potential
V
. Sometimes requiring additional regularity on
V
is also necessary.

At this point let us observe that the set of solutions to Equation (
1.1
) under the mass constraint (
1.3
), remains unchanged if we replace the potential
V(x)
by
V(x)+d
, where
d\in\mathbb{R}
is arbitrary.
Thus, the assumption
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
may be seen as the existence of a limit at infinity for the potential
V(x)
. In the above-mentioned papers, setting this limit to
0
allowed for simplification and uniformization when presenting the results. Therefore, the required conditions on
V
, such as a sign or a decay condition at infinity, should be understood as conditions on
(V-\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x))
and accordingly for
W(x)
.

Let us also mention that there do exist other approaches that do not require the use of a Pohozaev identity nor conditions on
V
at infinity. For example in
[
38
]
the authors instead use a Lyapunov-Schmidt reduction technique to derive existence results. While this manages to deal with the problem of a priori estimates and loss of compactness at infinity, it comes at the cost of having to assume the non-degeneracy of the potential at a given point and also it only provides a perturbation result in the sense that if a solution is obtained for small values of
\mu>0
there is no explicit bound saying how small
\mu>0
must be.

Following the above discussion, in this paper we develop, under the assumption that the potential is radial, an approach to find non-perturbation results which avoids the use of a global Pohozaev identity as (
1.4
) and the need of higher orders of regularity of the potential or of norm bounds on
W(x)=V(x)|x|
. In fact, we do not need the potential
V
to have a limit at infinity either.

In order to state our results we introduce some notations. We consider the self-adjoint operator

-\Delta+V\colon H^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\subset L^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to L^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

and we denote by
\sigma(-\Delta+V)
its spectrum and by
\lambda_{1}(V)
the infimum of its spectrum. Namely

\lambda_{1}(V):=\inf_{u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}}\frac{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+V(x)|u|^{2}}{\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{2}}.

Additionally, we denote the essential spectrum of
-\Delta+V
and its infimum respectively by
\sigma_{ess}(-\Delta+V)
and
\lambda_{ess}(V)
.

The assumptions on the potential that will be used in the paper are the following:

(V0)

V
is a radial function;

(V1)

V\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
;

(V2)

The infimum of the spectrum
\lambda_{1}(V)
is an eigenvalue.

Let
\underline{V}:=\mathrm{essinf}_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)
. As already observed, adding some constant to
V
makes no difference regarding sets of solutions. In particular the quantity

\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)\leq 0

is invariant under such operation. Having this in mind we may now state our main existence results.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
(V0)-(V2)
holds. Then, there exists an explicit value
\mu_{0}>0
depending only on
N,q
and
(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V))
such that, for all
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0}),
there exists a positive radial solution to Equation (
1.1
) under the mass constraint (
1.3
) lying at a mountain pass level of
E
restricted to
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
.
Moreover, the associated Lagrange multiplier satisfies
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}(V).

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
(V0)-(V2)
holds. Let
\mu_{0}>0
be as in Theorem
1.1
. For any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0}),
there exists a positive radial solution to Equation (
1.1
) under the mass constraint (
1.3
) which is a local minimizer of
E
restricted to
H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, whose energy level is strictly below the one of the solution obtained in Theorem
1.1
. Moreover, its associated Lagrange multiplier also satisfies
\lambda>-\lambda_{1}(V).

Note that combining Theorem

1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain for any
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
the existence of two solutions for Equation
(
1.1
)
whose
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
norm is exactly
\mu
.

Before going any further, we would like to make some comments on our assumptions.

Remark 1.3
.

The threshold mass value
\mu_{0}>0
is explicit and is given in (
3.2
). Roughly speaking, it appears when proving that, for
\mu>0
sufficiently small, the functional
E
restricted to
{H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
admits a geometry of local minimum. As we shall succeed proving that a local minimum exists this will prove Theorem
1.2
. Since we are in a mass supercritical case, the existence of a geometry of local minima implies that
E
has a geometry of mountain pass (a MP geometry for short). As it will be clear from our proofs, the limitation
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
is only used to prove these geometries and not to handle compactness issues.

Remark 1.4
.

Assumption
(V1)
implies that solutions to Equation
(
1.1
)
are in
C^{1,\alpha}_{\mathrm{\textup{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap W^{2,\infty}_{\mathrm{\textup{loc}}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}).
Such regularity result is explicitly used in Lemma
4.1
below and moreover is sufficient to use strong maximum principles
or a comparison principle for Schrödinger operators in the proof of Theorem
4.6
.

Remark 1.5
.

Assumption
(V2)
holds if, for example, there exists a

\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
such that

\frac{\int|\nabla\varphi_{0}|^{2}+V\varphi_{0}^{2}}{\int|\varphi_{0}|^{2}}\leq\lambda_{ess}(V).

Indeed, if the inequality is strict then
\lambda_{1}(V)<\lambda_{ess}(V)
and in that case
[
40
, Th XIII.48]
yields that
\lambda_{1}(V)
is achieved by a positive eigenfunction. Otherwise we have that
\lambda_{1}(V)=\lambda_{ess}(V)
and such
\varphi_{0}\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
is a minimizer for
\lambda_{1}(V)
.

Remark 1.6
.

If one assumes that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
, then
\lambda_{ess}(V)=0
. Indeed it is straightforward to check that the operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}\colon H^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle\to L^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})

\displaystyle\psi

\displaystyle\mapsto V\psi,

is relatively
(-\Delta)
-compact (the definition can be found in
[
23
, Definition 14.1]
) and then, see
[
23
, Theorem 14.6]
, that
\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta+V)=\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta)=[0,+\infty).
However this is only a sufficient condition and
\lambda_{ess}(V)=0
may be true without the existence of a limit for
V
at infinity.

Remark 1.7
.

If one assumes that
\lim_{|x|\to\infty}V(x)=0
, then
\lambda_{1}(V)\leq 0
and for Assumption
(V2)
to hold the negative part of
V
must be non-trivial. In particular, for
N\geq 3
the Cwikel-Lieb-Rozenblum theorem tells us that, to have
\lambda_{1}(V)<0
, a necessary condition is for
\|V_{-}\|_{\frac{N}{2}}
to be above a certain positive threshold, see
[
19
, Theorem 4.31]
. In the case
N=2
, however, where having
\|V_{-}\|_{\infty}\neq 0
is a sufficient for the existence of a negative eigenvalue.

Remark 1.8
.

The assumption that the potential
V
is radial allows to work in the subspace of radial functions
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. As such, we benefit from the compact inclusion of
L^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
into
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
for any
s\in(2,2^{*})
, property which is helpful to control the loss of mass at infinity. However, several of our results still hold without this restriction. In turn the fact that we work in
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
will require new arguments in the blow-up analysis that we will perform in order to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Turning now to the proof of Theorem
1.1
, in order to obtain the existence of the required a critical point, it appears necessary to show that, at least one PS sequence for
E
constrained to
{H_{\mu}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}
at the MP level, is bounded. To do so, we shall borrow a general strategy which was developed in a series of works
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
devoted to the existence of prescribed
L^{2}
norm solutions for Schrödinger equations set on metric graphs. The common feature with our problem being the lack of a useful Pohozaev identity due to the impossibility to use scaling on graphs. As a first step we consider, for
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=\rho|u|^{q-2}u,\quad u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\\
\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\mu,\end{cases}

(1.5)

with
N\geq 2
,
V\in L^{\infty}
, and
2+\frac{4}{N}<q<2^{*}
, whose associated energy
functional is now

E_{\rho}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)u^{2}-\frac{\rho}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}

(1.6)

constrained to

H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}):=\{u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\colon\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\mu\}.

The underlying idea is to use the monotonicity trick, in terms of the formulation given in
[
28
]
,
in order to obtain, for almost every
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, a bounded PS sequence at the corresponding MP level. Actually we shall directly make use of
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
, recalled here in Proposition
2.7
, which yields the existence of a bounded PS sequence with additional Morse type information. We will denote the Morse index of a function
u
as
\morse(u)
, for a precise definition refer to Definition
2.1
. This information will be centrally used in a blow-up analysis.

Note that to ensure that the limit of this PS sequence belongs to the mass constraint, we must prove its strong convergence. This is one of the central difficulties in the search of solutions having a prescribed
L^{2}
norm and, in general, the strategy resides in first proving that the Lagrange multiplier associated with the weak limit of the PS sequence, which we will call
\lambda_{\rho}
, satisfies an appropriate
strict inequality
.
In addition to checking the boundedness of (some particular) PS sequences, it is also at this point that the Pohozaev identity proved particularly useful in many cases. Here, we shall use spectral arguments to show that
\lambda_{\rho}>-\lambda_{1}(V)
.
Having this information and using that, thanks to the radial nature of the potential, we can work in the subspace of radially symmetry functions we then deduce the strong convergence and obtain the following result.

Theorem 1.9
.

Assume that
(V0)-(V2)
holds and let
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
where
\mu_{0}>0
is defined in Theorem
1.1
. Then, for almost every
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, there exists a positive radial solution
u_{\rho}\in H^{1}_{\mu}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
to Problem (
1.5
). This solution lies at a MP level, denoted by
c_{\rho}
, which is defined in (
3.3
). It has an associated Lagrange multiplier strictly above
-\lambda_{1}(V)
and its Morse index
\morse(u_{\rho})
is less than
2
.

To derive Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.9
we choose a sequence
\{\rho_{n}\}\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]
with
\rho_{n}\to 1^{-}
for which Theorem
1.9
provides a sequence
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}\subset H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of solutions to (
1.5
) where
\rho=\rho_{n}
.
It is readily seen that if such sequence converges (strongly), its limit will be a solution as searched for in Theorem
1.1
.
Exploiting the analysis performed in the derivation of Theorem
1.9
, this convergence issue can be reduced first to showing the boundedness of
\{u_{\rho_{n}}\}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and then further to proving the boundedness of the associated sequence of Lagrange multipliers
\{\lambda_{\rho_{n}}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
. This latter condition will be the one proven through a blow-up analysis. Actually, it will be a consequence of more
general result of independent interest:

Theorem 1.10
.

Let
(V0)-(V1)
hold and
\{(\rho_{n},\lambda_{n},u_{n})\}\subset[\frac{1}{2},1]\times\mathbb{R}\times H_{\mathrm{rad}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a sequence of solutions to

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=\rho u^{q-1}\\
u>0,\end{cases}

(1.7)

such that
\rho_{n}\to 1^{-}
.
Suppose moreover there exist constants
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}>0
and
c_{1}\in\mathbb{R}
such that

\mu_{1}\leq\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\mu_{2}\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad E_{\rho_{n}}(u_{n})\leq c_{1}

(1.8)

where
E_{\rho_{n}}
is the associated energy functional defined in (
1.6
).
Suppose finally that the Morse index of the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
is uniformly bounded, i.e. there exists
m^{*}<+\infty
such that,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\morse(u_{n})\leq m^{*}.

Then,

\sup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\lambda_{n}<+\infty.

To prove Theorem
1.10
we shall proceed by contradiction and assume that
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
. Under this assumption it is readily seen, see Lemma
4.1
, that the sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
is necessarily unbounded and we develop a blow-up analysis that will be used to find a contradiction. This blow-up analysis, performed in Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
, see also Corollary
4.10
, leads to the conclusion that, passing to the subsequence, a blow-up phenomenon is only possible either at the origin or at a finite number of radii. In addition, the solutions exponentially decay away from those blow-up points. Moreover, in order to obtain these intermediate results one may remark that Assumption (
1.8
) has not been yet utilised. In fact, it only plays a role at the end of the proof of Theorem
1.10
to conclude that no blow-up may happen.

Compared to the blow-up analysis that is also performed in
[
12
,
14
,
15
]
, the fact that we work here in the subspace of radially symmetric functions presents new challenges.
As
\lambda_{n}\to+\infty
, it is rather standard to have solutions of equations of the type of (
1.7
) concentrating at a singular point, leading to the formation of the so called spike-layer solutions, or simply spikes. In the case of radial functions it is clear that spikes may only form at the origin. However, still in a radial context, if a sequence of local maximum does not converge to the origin what we actually obtain are solutions which concentrate around a sphere, phenomenon which is much less understood. First attempts to study concentration on higher dimensional sets can be found in
[
1
]
and
[
16
]
where conditions on the potential were derived in order to show the existence of radial solutions concentrating around spheres and curves for the related equation

-\epsilon\Delta u+V(x)u=u^{p},\quad u>0,\quad p>1,

in
\mathbb{R}^{N}
as
\epsilon\to 0.
When it comes to solutions with (uniformly) bounded Morse index, the number of concentration sets usually remains bounded. This phenomenon is highlighted in
[
17
]
, see also
[
18
,
39
]
for a related result for spikes. In
[
17
]
, under this additional condition, the authors consider the problem

\begin{cases}-\Delta u+\lambda V(x)u=u^{q}\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\\
u>0,\quad\mbox{in }\Omega\\
u=0,\quad\mbox{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

(1.9)

where
q>1
,
V
is smooth, and
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an annulus. In particular they explicit necessary conditions on an auxiliary potential for a blow-up to take place at a given radius. Let us also mention the work
[
33
]
where (
1.9
) is considered in the special case
V\equiv 1
. There the focus is, as in our case, on the existence of multiplicity of normalized solutions. In our setting, even if we do benefit from some ideas introduced in
[
17
]
, the fact that Equation (
1.1
) is set on the full space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
induces new features. Indeed, we must also consider the possibility that sequences of blow-up points escape to infinity or, on the contrary, accumulate around the origin. In that later case, one needs to distinguish two rates of accumulation leading to the analysis of distinct limit problems.
Having proved Theorems
4.5
and
4.6
, the fact that the additional assumption (
1.8
) implies the boundedness of
\{\lambda_{n}\}
is derived making use of a one-dimensional Pohozaev type identity, see Lemma
4.11
.

2
Preliminaries

For
s\in(2,2^{*}),
we denote by
C_{N,s}
the optimal constant in the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, i.e. the optimal constant only depending on
N
and
s
such that

\|u\|_{s}^{s}\leq C_{N,s}\|u\|_{2}^{\theta_{s}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\xi_{s}}

(2.1)

for all
u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, where

\theta_{s}:=\frac{2s-N(s-2)}{2}\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\xi_{s}:=\frac{N(s-2)}{2}.

We now introduce the definition of the Morse index used in the statement of Theorems
1.9
and
1.10
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
N\geq 2
and let
u\in H_{\mathrm{\textup{loc}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
be a radial weak solution to

-\Delta u+V(x)u+\lambda u=\rho|u|^{q-2}u,

where
V\in L_{\mathrm{rad}}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
\lambda,\rho\in\mathbb{R}
and
2<q<2^{*}
.
The
Morse index
of
u
, denoted
\morse(u)
, is the maximum
integer
m
such that there exists a subspace

\mathcal{V}\subset H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
of dimension
m
with the property that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{0\}

there holds

\displaystyle Q_{V}^{N}(\varphi;u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|{\nabla\varphi}|^{2}+(V(x)+\lambda-\rho(q-1)|u|^{q-2})|\varphi^{2}|dx<0.

(2.2)

In the case that
V\equiv 0
we will use the notation
Q_{0}^{N}(\varphi;u)
.

In our proofs, we will have to consider limit problems posed on the real line. In that case we shall make use of the following definition of the Morse index.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
u\in H_{\mathrm{\textup{loc}}}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
be a weak solution of

-u^{\prime\prime}+V(r)u+\lambda u=\rho|u|^{q-2}u.

The
Morse index
of
u
, still denoted
\morse(u)
, is the maximum
integer
m
such that there exists a subspace

\mathcal{V}\subset H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
of dimension
m
with the property that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{V}\setminus\{0\}

there holds

\displaystyle Q_{V}^{1}(\varphi;u)

\displaystyle:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}|{\varphi^{\prime}}|^{2}+(V(x)+\lambda-\rho(q-1)|u|^{q-2})|\varphi^{2}|dx<0.

(2.3)

As before, in the case that
V\equiv 0
we will use
Q_{0}^{1}(\varphi;u)
.

We now turn to the abstract result from critical point theory
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
. In order to introduce it we need to put ourselves into some context. Let
\left(E,\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle\right)
and
\left(H,(\cdot,\cdot)\right)
be two
infinite-dimensional
Hilbert spaces and assume that
E\hookrightarrow H\hookrightarrow E^{\prime}
,
with continuous injections. For simplicity, we assume that the continuous injection
E\hookrightarrow H
has norm at most
1
and identify
E
with its image in
H
.
Set

\begin{cases}\|u\|^{2}=\langle u,u\rangle,\\
|u|^{2}=(u,u),\end{cases}\quad u\in E,

and define for
\mu>0
the set

S_{\mu}=\{u\in E\big|\,|u|^{2}=\mu^{2}\}.

In the context of this paper, we shall have
E=H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
,
H=L^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and

S_{\mu}=H_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}^{1}=\{u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}:\|u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}=\mu\}.

Clearly,
S_{\mu}
is a smooth submanifold of
E
of codimension
1
. Furthermore its tangent space at a given point
u\in S_{\mu}
can be considered as the closed subspace of codimension
1
of
E
given by:

T_{u}S_{\mu}=\{v\in E\big|\,(u,v)=0\}.

In the following definition, we denote by
\|\cdot\|_{*}
and
\|\cdot\|_{**}
, respectively, the operator norm of
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R})
and of
\mathcal{L}(E,\mathcal{L}(E,\mathbb{R}))
.

Definition 2.3
.

Let
\phi:E\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
. We say that
\phi^{\prime}
and
\phi^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets if for any
R>0
one can find
M=M(R)>0
such that, for any
u_{1},u_{2}\in B(0,R)
:

||\phi^{\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime}(u_{2})||_{*}\leq M||u_{1}-u_{2}||^{\alpha},\quad||\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{1})-\phi^{\prime\prime}(u_{2})||_{**}\leq M||u_{1}-u_{2}||^{\alpha}.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in E
, we define the continuous bilinear map:

D^{2}\phi(u)=\phi^{\prime\prime}(u)-\frac{\phi^{\prime}(u)\cdot u}{|u|^{2}}(\cdot,\cdot).

Definition 2.5
.

Let
\phi
be a
C^{2}
-functional on
E
. For any
u\in S_{\mu}
and
\theta>0
, we define
the
approximate Morse index
by

\tilde{m}_{\theta}(u)=\sup\left\{\dim\,L\left|\begin{array}[]{l}\ L\text{ is a subspace of $T_{u}S_{\mu}$ such that: }D^{2}\phi(u)[\varphi,\varphi]<-\theta\|\varphi\|^{2},\quad{\forall\varphi\in L\setminus\{0\}}\end{array}\right.\right\}.

If
u
is a critical point for the constrained functional
\phi|_{S_{\mu}}
and
\theta=0
, we say that this is the
Morse index of
u
as constrained critical point
.

Remark 2.6
.

Suppose
u
is a critical point of the functional
\phi|_{S_{\mu}}.
Then
D^{2}\phi(u)
restricted to
T_{u}S_{\mu}
coincides with the constrained Hessian of
\phi_{S_{\mu}}
at
u
.

We now recall
[
13
, Theorem 1.5]
.

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
I\subset(0,+\infty)
be an interval and consider a family of
C^{2}
functionals
\Phi_{\rho}\colon E\to\mathbb{R}
of the form

\Phi_{\rho}(u)=A(u)-\rho B(u),\quad\quad\rho\in I,

where
B(u)\geq 0
for all
u\in E
, and

either
A(u)\to+\infty
or
B(u)\to+\infty
as
u\in E
and
\|u\|\to+\infty
.

(2.4)

Suppose moreover that

\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}
and
\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets in the sense of Definition
2.3
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
.
Finally, suppose there exist
w_{0},w_{1}\in S_{\mu}
(independent of
\rho
) such that, setting

\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],S_{\mu})\colon\gamma(0)=w_{0},\gamma(1)=w_{1}\},

we have

c_{\rho}=\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{t\in[0,1]}\Phi_{\rho}(\gamma(t))>\max\big\{\Phi_{\rho}(w_{0}),\Phi_{\rho}(w_{1})\big\},\quad\forall\rho\in I.

Then, for almost every
\rho\in I
, there exist sequences
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
and
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
such that, as
n\to+\infty
,

(i)

\Phi_{\rho}(u_{n})\to c_{\rho}
;

(ii)

\|\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime}|_{S_{\mu}}(u_{n})\|\to 0
;

(iii)

\{u_{n}\}
is bounded in
E
;

(iv)

\tilde{m}_{\zeta_{n}}(u_{n})\leq 1
.

Remark 2.8
.

If the sequence
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
provided by
Proposition
2.7
converges to some
u_{\rho}\in S_{\mu}
, then
in view of points (i)–(ii),

u_{\rho}
is a critical point of

\Phi_{\rho}|_{S_{\mu}}
at level
c_{\rho}
. Let us show that the
Morse index of
u_{\rho}
, as a
constrained critical point, satisfies

\tilde{m}_{0}(u_{\rho})\leq 1
. Assume by contradiction
that this is not the case. Then, in view of Definition
2.5
, we may assume that there exists a subspace

W_{0}\subset T_{u_{\rho}}S_{\mu}
with
\dim W_{0}=2
such that

D^{2}\Phi_{\rho}(u_{\rho})[w,w]<0\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

Since
W_{0}
is of finite dimension, its unit sphere is
compact and therefore there exists
\theta>0
such that

D^{2}\Phi_{\rho}(u_{\rho})[w,w]<-\theta\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

Now,
using that

\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime}
and
\Phi_{\rho}^{\prime\prime}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on
bounded sets for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
, it directly follows that
there exists
\delta>0
small enough such that for any
v\in S_{\mu}

satisfying
\|v-u_{\rho}\|\leq\delta
there holds

D^{2}\Phi_{\rho}(v)[w,w]<-\frac{\theta}{2}\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

In particular, for
n
large enough we have
\|u_{n}-u_{\rho}\|\leq\delta
and
\zeta_{n}<\theta/2
(for a given sequence
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
),
so the previous inequality implies

D^{2}\Phi_{\rho}(u_{n})[w,w]<-\frac{\theta}{2}\|w\|^{2}<-\zeta_{n}\|w\|^{2}\quad\text{for all }w\in W_{0}\setminus\{0\}.

From Proposition
2.7
(iv), the above directly implies that
\dim W_{0}\leq 1
reaching the desired contradiction.

Remark 2.9
.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.7
(ii)–(iii), we deduce in a standard way, see
[
13
, Remark 1.3]
or
[
9
, Lemma 3]
, that

\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})+\displaystyle\lambda_{n}(u_{n},\cdot)\to 0\quad\text{ in }E^{\prime}\text{ as }n\to+\infty

where we have set

\lambda_{n}:=-\displaystyle\frac{1}{\mu}\Phi^{\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[u_{n}].

(2.5)

We call the sequence
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
defined in
(
2.5
) the sequence of
almost Lagrange
multipliers
.

We finish the section with a result that will allow to derive information on the sequence of almost Lagrange multipliers. It is a direct extension of
[
14
, Lemma 2.7]
, see also
[
12
, Lemma 2.5]
.

Lemma 2.10
.

Let
\{u_{n}\}\subset S_{\mu}
,
\{\lambda_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}

and
\{\zeta_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{+}
with
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
.
Assume
that, for a given
M\in\mathbb{N}
, the following conditions hold:

(i)

For large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
, all subspaces

W_{n}\subset E
with the property

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}<-\zeta_{n}\|\varphi\|^{2},\qquad\text{for all }\,\varphi\in W_{n}\setminus\{0\},

(2.6)

satisfy that
\dim(W_{n})\leq M
.

(ii)

There exist
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, a subspace
Y
of

E
with
\dim(Y)\geq M+1
and
a>0
such that, for large enough

n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda|\varphi|^{2}\leq-a\|\varphi\|^{2},\qquad\text{for all }\,\varphi\in Y.

(2.7)

Then
\lambda_{n}>\lambda
for all large enough
n\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, if (
2.7
) holds for any

\lambda<\lambda_{0}
, for some
\lambda_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
, then

\displaystyle\liminf_{n\to\infty}\lambda_{n}\geq\lambda_{0}.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
\lambda_{n}\leq\lambda
along a
subsequence still denoted
\{\lambda_{n}\}
. Keep
denoting
\{u_{n}\}
and
\{\zeta_{n}\}
the corresponding subsequences.
From (
2.7
) we have,

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}\leq\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda|\varphi|^{2}\leq-a\|\varphi\|^{2}\qquad\text{for all }\varphi\in Y\setminus\{0\}.

Now, since
\zeta_{n}\to 0^{+}
, there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that:
\forall n\geq n_{0}
,

\zeta_{n}<a
. Thus, for an arbitrary
n\geq n_{0}
, we obtain

\Phi^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}(u_{n})[\varphi,\varphi]+\lambda_{n}|\varphi|^{2}\leq-\zeta_{n}\|\varphi\|^{2},\qquad\text{for all }\,\varphi\in Y\setminus\{0\},

which is in contradiction with (
2.6
) since
\dim(Y)\geq M+1
.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.9
, Theorem
1.2
and conditional proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In this section we first give the proof of Theorem
1.9
and of Theorem
1.2
. Next, assuming that Theorem
1.10
holds we give the proof of Theorem
1.1
.

In the proof of Theorem
1.9
, Proposition
2.7
will be a key ingredient. We recall that the family of functionals
E_{\rho}\colon H_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\to\mathbb{R}
, with
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
, is defined by

E_{\rho}(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|u|^{2}-\frac{\rho}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}.

(3.1)

Clearly
E_{\rho}
is of the form
E_{\rho}(u)=A(u)-\rho B(u)
and (
2.4
) holds. It can also be checked, in a standard way, that
E^{\prime}_{\rho}
and
E^{\prime\prime}_{\rho}
are
\alpha
-Hölder continuous on bounded sets in the sense of Definition
2.3
for some
\alpha\in(0,1]
. To show that
E_{\rho}
enters the framework of Proposition
2.7
, it remains to show that it has a mountain pass geometry on
H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
uniformly with respect to
\rho\in[\frac{1}{2},1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume that
(V0)-(V2)
hold. For every
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
, where
\mu_{0}>0
is the unique positive solution to

\mu_{0}^{-\frac{4q-2N(q-2)}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{2q}{N(q-2)C_{N,q}}\right)^{\frac{4}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{N(q-2)-4}{2N(q-2)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mu_{0}^{2}\big(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)\big)=0,

(3.2)

there exist
u_{0},u_{1}\in H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
independent of
\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right]
such that

c_{\rho}:=\inf\limits_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\,\max\limits_{t\in[0,1]}E_{\rho}(\gamma(t))>\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{1}(V)>\max\{E_{\rho}(u_{0}),E_{\rho}(u_{1})\},\qquad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right],

(3.3)

where

\Gamma:=\{\gamma\in C([0,1],H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}))\big|\penalty\ \gamma\penalty\ \mbox{is continuous},\penalty\ \gamma(0)=u_{0},\gamma(1)=u_{1}\}.

Proof.

Fix an arbitrary
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
. In order to prove the lemma it suffices to find a bounded open set
\Sigma_{\mu}\subset H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
and functions
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
and
u_{1}\notin\bar{\Sigma}_{\mu}
such that

\max\{E_{\rho}(u_{0}),E_{\rho}(u_{1})\}<\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{1}(V)\leq\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)<\inf_{u\in\partial\Sigma_{\mu}}E_{\rho}(u),\qquad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

In this direction, we may find a lower bound of
E_{\rho}
using (
2.1
):

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(u)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\underline{V}-\frac{\rho}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|u|^{q}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla u\|^{2}_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\underline{V}-\frac{C_{N,q}}{q}\mu^{\theta_{q}}\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{\xi_{q}}.

(3.4)

Aiming to prove (
3.3
) we consider the real valued function

f_{\mu}(t)=\frac{1}{2}t^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V))-\frac{C_{N,q}}{q}\mu^{\theta_{q}}t^{\xi_{q}},\quad t>0.

This function has a unique global maximum point
t_{\mu}\in\mathbb{R}
satisfying

0<t_{\mu}^{\xi_{q}-2}=\frac{q}{\xi_{q}C_{N,q}\mu^{\theta_{q}}},

and

\begin{split}f_{\mu}(t_{\mu})&=t_{\mu}^{2}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{C_{N,q}}{q}\mu^{\theta_{q}}t_{\mu}^{\xi_{q}-2}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V))=\left(\frac{q}{\xi_{q}C_{N,q}\mu^{\theta_{q}}}\right)^{\frac{2}{\xi_{q}-2}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{\xi_{q}}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\big(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)\big)\\
&=\mu^{-\frac{4q-2N(q-2)}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{2q}{N(q-2)C_{N,q}}\right)^{\frac{4}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{N(q-2)-4}{2N(q-2)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\big(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)\big).\end{split}

(3.5)

Note also that, for any
t>0
, since
\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)\leq 0
we have

f_{\mu_{1}}(t)\geq f_{\mu_{2}}(t),\quad\mbox{if}\quad 0<\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}.

(3.6)

Using the estimates (
3.4
), (
3.5
) and the definition of
f_{\mu}
we obtain

\begin{split}\inf&\Big\{E_{\rho}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)\colon u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|u\|_{2}=\mu,\ \|\nabla u\|_{2}=t_{\mu}\Big\}\\
&\geq\mu^{-\frac{4q-2N(q-2)}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{2q}{N(q-2)C_{N,q}}\right)^{\frac{4}{N(q-2)-4}}\left(\frac{N(q-2)-4}{2N(q-2)}\right)+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}(\underline{V}-\lambda_{ess}(V)).\end{split}

(3.7)

We set

\Sigma_{\mu}:=\Big\{u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\colon\|u\|_{2}=\mu,\ \|\nabla u\|_{2}<t_{\mu}\Big\}

(3.8)

and observe, from the definition of
\mu_{0}>0
and (
3.7
), that

\inf\Big\{E_{\rho}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)\colon u\in\partial\Sigma_{\mu}\Big\}>0,\quad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(3.9)

We now turn to searching appropriate functions
u_{0}
and
u_{1}
. For
u_{0}\in H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
we shall take

u_{0}:=\frac{\varphi_{0}}{||\varphi_{0}||_{2}}\,\mu

where
\varphi_{0}\geq 0
is a minimizer associated to
\lambda_{1}(V)
(whose existence is insured by condition
(V2)
). Clearly

E_{\rho}(u_{0})-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{1}(V)=-\frac{1}{q}\|u_{0}\|_{q}^{q}<0,\quad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(3.10)

Let us now prove that
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. Setting

t_{0}=\displaystyle\frac{t_{\mu}}{\|\nabla u_{0}\|_{2}}
if we show that
t_{0}>1
this will implies that
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
. One the one hand

\|\nabla[t_{0}u_{0}]\|_{2}=t_{\mu}
and

E_{\mu}(t_{0}u_{0})-\frac{1}{2}(t_{0}\mu)^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)<0,\quad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(3.11)

On the other hand, taking into account (
3.6
), we have

\inf\Big\{E_{\rho}(u)-\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{ess}(V)\colon u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),\ \|u\|_{2}<\mu,\ \|\nabla u\|_{2}=t_{\mu}\Big\}>0.

(3.12)

Combining (
3.11
) and (
3.12
) we deduce that
t_{0}\mu>\mu
, namely that
t_{0}>1.
This proves that
u_{0}\in\Sigma_{\mu}
.

Now, for an arbitrary non negative function
\varphi\in H^{1}_{\mu,\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\backslash\{0\}
we define, for
t>0
, the function
\varphi_{t}(x):=t^{\frac{N}{2}}\varphi(tx)
. Observe that, for all
t>0
,
\|\varphi_{t}\|_{2}=\mu
and

\displaystyle E_{\rho}(\varphi_{t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi_{t}|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}V(x)|\varphi_{t}^{2}|-\frac{\rho}{q}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi_{t}|^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{t^{2}}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\frac{1}{2}||V||_{\infty}\,\mu^{2}-\frac{\rho}{q}t^{\frac{N(q-2)}{2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\varphi|^{q}.

(3.13)

Noting that
\frac{N(q-2)}{2}>2
we see, from (
3.13
), that
E_{\rho}(\varphi_{t})\to-\infty
as
t\to+\infty
. Thus we may take
t_{1}>0
sufficiently large so that
u_{1}:=\varphi_{t_{1}}\notin\overline{\Sigma}_{\mu}
and

E_{\rho}({u_{1}})\leq E_{\rho}(u_{0}),\qquad\forall\rho\in\left[\frac{1}{2},1\right].

(3.14)

In view of the definition of
\Sigma_{\mu}
in (
3.8
), of (
3.9
), (
3.10
) and (
3.14
), the lemma is now proved.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

From the proof of Lemma
3.1
we directly deduce, under assumptions
(V0)-(V2)
, that for all
\mu\in(0,\mu_{0})
,

\delta_{\mu}:=\inf\Big\{E_{\rho}(u)\colon u\in\Sigma_{\mu}\Big\}<\frac{1}{2}\mu^{2}\lambda_{1}(V)<\inf_{u\in\partial\Sigma_{\mu}}E_{\rho}(u)

). In particular

We now establish a series of results which will be useful in deriving information on the Lagrange multipliers associated to the limit function of the Palais-Smale sequences provided by the application of Proposition
2.7
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Assume that
(V0)-(V1)
hold. For every
\lambda<-\lambda_{{\rm ess}}(V)
and every
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
there exists a subspace of infinite dimension
Y
of
H^{1}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
such that for all
\varphi\in Y\backslash\{0\}

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla\varphi|^{2}+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(V(x)+\lambda-(q-1)\rho|u|^{q-2})\varphi^{2}<0.

Proof.

Since
\lambda_{{\rm ess}}(V)=\inf\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta_{\mathrm{rad}}+V)
, for every
0<\epsilon<-\lambda-\lambda_{\rm ess}
there exists
\lambda_{\epsilon}\in\sigma_{\rm ess}(-\Delta_{\mathrm{rad}}+V)
such that

\lambda_{\epsilon}<\lambda_{\rm ess}(V)+\epsilon.

Fix one such
\epsilon
. Using Weyl’s criterion, see
[
23
, Theorem 7.2]
, we obtain the existence of a sequence
\{\varphi_{n}\}
of functions in
H^{2}_{\mathrm{rad}}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
, such that
\|\varphi_{n}\|_{2}=1,

Lemma 3.4

there exists a radial function

satisfying i) and ii). Passing to subsequences, without loss of generality we may rename the sequences so that they are ordered, i.e.
a_{n}^{i}\leq a_{n}^{j}
if
i\leq j.

Our procedure to proving the result will be divided into two main steps. First, we will suppose that a given sequence
\{a_{n}^{i}
} satisfies
a_{n}^{i}\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\to+\infty
and obtain a contradiction. To do so, we implement the sequence
\{u_{n}\}
into (
4.40
) with a carefully chosen sequence of intervals
I_{n}
containing the selected sequence
\{a_{n}^{i}\}
.
Once we have discarded the possibility that
a_{n}^{i}\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\to+\infty
, the second step will then discard the possibility that
\{a_{n}^{i}\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\}
remains bounded. Note already that from Remark

we know that if such sequence exists then it is unique.

Before delving into the procedure an observation is in order. From (
3.35
) and (

), we obtain that, for

sufficiently large,

\left(\frac{q-2}{2q}\right)\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}^{2}\leq E_{\rho_{n}}(u_{n})+\frac{\lambda_{n}\mu_{2}^{2}}{q}-\left(\frac{q-2}{2q}\right)\underline{V}\mu_{2}^{2}\leq\frac{3\mu_{2}^{2}}{q}\lambda_{n}

or, equivalently

\|\nabla u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\leq\mu_{2}\sqrt{\frac{6\lambda_{n}}{q-2}}.

(4.44)

Step 1: discarding the possibility (
4.5
)
.

Here we work under the assumption that

\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}\to+\infty,

(4.45)

for some given

.
For
i\leq j\leq M,
let

\overline{k}_{j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}^{j}}{a_{n}^{i}}

(note that, passing to subsequences, such limits exist) and let

\alpha=\max\Big\{i\leq j\leq M\colon\overline{k}_{j}\neq+\infty\Big\}.

We set
\overline{K}=\overline{k}_{\alpha}
. Recall that the sequences are ordered and so
\overline{K}\in[1,+\infty)
.

Also let, for
1\leq j\leq i
,

\underline{k}_{j}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}^{j}}{a_{n}^{i}}

and

\beta=\min\Big\{1\leq j\leq i,\colon\underline{k}_{j}>0\Big\}.

We set
\underline{K}=\underline{k}_{\beta}
. Note that
\underline{K}\in(0,1].

Finally, define

I_{n}=\Big[\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i},\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}\Big]\supset[a_{n}^{i}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}},a_{n}^{i}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}].

(4.46)

Also let

J_{i}=\{j\in\{\beta,\dots,\alpha\}\}.

(4.47)

As anticipated, our aim is to substitute
[a,b]:=I_{n},u=u_{n}
into (
4.40
) after multiplication by
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}
. To do so we will analyze each of the terms separately. Let us focus first on the boundary terms. We shall prove that there can all be neglected. In this aim some estimates are in order.

There holds, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, that
a_{n}^{\alpha}\leq\frac{5}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}
and so

\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{j}\geq\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }i\leq j\leq\alpha.

(4.48)

Also, if
\alpha<M
, we have, by definition of
\alpha
, that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}^{\alpha+1}}{a_{n}^{i}}=+\infty

and so for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large
a_{n}^{\alpha+1}\geq\frac{7}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}
.
Then,

a_{n}^{j}-\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}\geq a_{n}^{\alpha+1}-\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}\geq\frac{1}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }\alpha+1\leq j\leq M.

(4.49)

Note that if
\alpha=M
, then
(
4.48
)
directly holds for
i\leq j\leq M.
Globally thus, in view of (
4.48
) and (
4.49
),

\Big|\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{j}\Big|\geq\frac{1}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }i\leq j\leq M

(4.50)

and (
4.50
) obviously extends to all
j\in\{1,\cdots,M\}.
The estimate (
4.50
) along with (
4.23
) and (

) enable to deduce that

\displaystyle\left|\left.\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}u_{n}u^{\prime}_{n}\right|_{r=\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\left(\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{q-2}+{\frac{1}{q-2}+\frac{1}{2}}}\right)\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}|\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{j}|}\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{0}\cdot\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\frac{\overline{K}}{4}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}}\right)^{2}\to 0,

where the convergence to
0
follows from (
4.45
). Similarly

\begin{split}\left.\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\frac{u_{n}^{2}}{r}\right|_{r=\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}&=\frac{\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}{\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\frac{\overline{K}}{4}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}}\right)^{2}\to 0;\\
\left.\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\lambda_{n}u_{n}^{2}r\right|_{r=\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}&\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i})\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\frac{\overline{K}}{4}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}}\right)^{2}\to 0;\\
\left.\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}u_{n}^{q}r\right|_{r=\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}&\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i})\epsilon^{2}e^{q\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\frac{\overline{K}}{4}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}}\right)^{q}\to 0;\\
\left.\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}(u_{n}^{\prime})^{2}r\right|_{r=\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}&\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i})\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\frac{\overline{K}}{4}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{i}}\right)^{2}\to 0.\end{split}

(4.51)

To tackle the boundary terms at
r=\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}
observe that, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large,

\frac{3}{4}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}\leq a_{n}^{\beta}

and so

a_{n}^{j}-\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}\geq a_{n}^{\beta}-\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}\geq\frac{1}{4}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }\beta\leq j\leq i.

(4.52)

Also, if
\beta>1
, we have, by definition of
\beta
, that

\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{a_{n}^{\beta-1}}{a_{n}^{i}}=0

and so, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large,
a_{n}^{\beta-1}\leq\frac{1}{4}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}
.
Then,

\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{j}\geq\frac{3}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{\beta-1}\geq\frac{1}{4}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }1\leq j\leq\beta-1.

(4.53)

Note that if
\beta=1
, then
(
4.52
)
directly holds for
1\leq j\leq i.
Globally thus we get from (
4.52
) and (
4.53
) that

\Big|\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}-a_{n}^{j}\Big|\geq\frac{1}{4}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i},\quad\mbox{for }1\leq j\leq i.

(4.54)

Also (
4.54
) obviously extends to all
j\in\{1,\cdots,M\}.
In view of (
4.54
) and still using (
4.45
) we can, just like for the upper bounds, show that each of the boundary terms at
r=\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}
goes to
0
as
n\to\infty
.

We now turn to the integral terms in (
4.40
). First let us show that the one involving the product
u_{n}u_{n}^{\prime}
also converges to
0
. Using (
4.43
), (
4.44
) and taking (
1.8
) into account we get, for some constant
C>0
,

\displaystyle\left|\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\int_{\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i}}^{\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}}V(r)ru_{n}u_{n}^{\prime}dr\right|

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\frac{\|V\|_{\infty}}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i})^{N-2}}\|u_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{N})}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\frac{\|V\|_{\infty}}{|S^{N-1}|}\frac{1}{(\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i})^{N-2}}\mu_{2}\left(\mu_{2}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\sqrt{\frac{6}{q-2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\,\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{2}{q-2}}(a_{n}^{i})^{2-N}\to 0.

Indeed if
N=2
(or if
a_{n}^{i}\not\to 0
), we immediately deduce that the right hand side tends to
0
. If
N\geq 3
, we use that (
4.45
) gives, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large, that
a_{n}^{i}\geq\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then taking into account that
q<2^{*}=\frac{2N}{N-2}
the conclusion also holds.

Regarding the rest of the integral terms in (
4.40
), we will split them between what happens close and far from the points of maxima, that is, we make the decomposition

I_{n}=[\frac{1}{2}\underline{K}a_{n}^{i},\frac{3}{2}\overline{K}a_{n}^{i}]=\left(I_{n}\cap\bigcup_{j\in{J}_{i}}(a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}},a_{n}^{j}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\right)\cup(I_{n}\cap\mathcal{A}_{R,n})

and proceed to study each of the terms individually. Define, for
j\in{J}_{i}
(refer to (
4.47
)) and similarly to (
4.8
), the functions (for simplicity in the calculations below we omit the dependence in
j
since it does not play a role)

v_{n}(s):=v_{n}^{j}(s)=\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{q-2}}u_{n}\left(\frac{s}{\sqrt{\lambda_{n}}}+a_{n}^{j}\right).

(4.55)

Then, close to the points of maxima for all
j\in{J}_{i}
we have, as
n\to+\infty
,

\begin{split}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}&\int_{a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{a_{n}^{j}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}u_{n}^{q}(r)dr=\int_{-R}^{R}v_{n}^{q}(s)ds\to\int_{-R}^{R}\phi^{q};\\
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}&\int_{a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{a_{n}^{j}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\lambda_{n}u_{n}^{2}(r)dr=\int_{-R}^{R}v_{n}^{2}(s)ds\to\int_{-R}^{R}\phi^{2};\\
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}&\int_{a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{a_{n}^{j}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}|V(r)u_{n}^{2}(r)|dr\leq\lambda_{n}^{-1}\|V\|_{\infty}\int_{-R}^{R}v_{n}^{2}(s)ds\to 0;\\
\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}&\int_{a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{a_{n}^{j}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}\left|\frac{u_{n}^{2}(r)}{r^{2}}\right|dr\leq\frac{\lambda_{n}^{-1}}{(a_{n}^{j}-R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})^{2}}\int_{-R}^{R}v_{n}^{2}(s)ds=\frac{1}{(\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}a_{n}^{j}-R)^{2}}\int_{-R}^{R}v_{n}^{2}(s)ds\to 0\\
\end{split}

where
\phi
is the unique solution to (
4.9
). We now turn to the estimates in
{I_{n}}\cap\mathcal{A}_{R,n}
. For some constant

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R,n}\cap I_{n}}u_{n}^{q}(r)dr

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}\epsilon^{q}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{q}{q-2}}e^{q\gamma R}\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R,n}}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{M}e^{-\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}|r-a_{n}^{j}|}\right)^{q}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}\epsilon^{q}e^{q\gamma R}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{\complement_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}(A(a_{n}^{i},R\lambda_{n}^{-{1}/{2}}))}e^{-q\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}|r-a_{n}^{j}|}dr

\displaystyle\leq C\epsilon^{q}e^{q\gamma R}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\int_{R}^{+\infty}e^{-q\gamma y}dy

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon_{1}(R),

where

can be made arbitrarily small by taking

Similarly,

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{A}_{R,n}}\lambda_{n}u_{n}^{2}(r)dr\leq\epsilon_{2}(R),

\displaystyle\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{q}{q-2}}

Also, since on

In all, multiplying (

and furthermore taking the limit as

\displaystyle=|{J}_{i}|\cdot\left|\left(N-\frac{3}{2}-\frac{1}{q}\right)\left(\frac{2q}{q+2}\right)+\left(2-N\right)\right|\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}

\displaystyle={|{J}_{i}|\cdot\left(\frac{(N-1)(q-2)}{q+2}\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\phi^{2}}.

This forces

so, having chosen an arbitrary

such that

Step 2: Discarding possibility (
4.11
).
From the previous step we have the existence of a unique sequence
\{a_{n}\}
of blow-up local maxima satisfying
a_{n}\lambda_{n}^{1/2}\to 0
, and, for
n
sufficiently large,

u_{n}(a_{n})=\max\{u_{n}(x)\colon x\in B(0,a_{n}+R_{n}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})\}

for some sequence

. In order to now discard this final possibility we will now focus on estimating

\mathscr{I}:=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2}{q-2}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B(0,a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}|u_{n}|^{2}dx

in two different ways. First, fix
\epsilon>0
and take
n\geq N_{\epsilon}
and
R\geq R_{\epsilon}
as in Theorem
4.6
. Using the fact that the there is only one sequence of maxima, if
n
is sufficiently large then (
4.23
) becomes

u_{n}(r)\leq\epsilon\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{q-2}}e^{\gamma R}e^{-\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}|r-a_{n}|}\quad\text{for }\quad r\geq a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}.

Now, since
a_{n}\to 0
, we may assume that
a_{n}<1
for
n\in\mathbb{N}
sufficiently large and thus,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2}{q-2}}\epsilon^{2}\lambda_{n}^{\frac{2}{q-2}}e^{2\gamma R}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}\backslash B(0,a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}e^{-2\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(|x|-a_{n}|)}dx

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}}\epsilon^{2}e^{s\gamma R}\int_{a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{+\infty}r^{N-1}e^{-2\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}(r-a_{n})}dr

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}}\epsilon^{2}e^{2\gamma R}\int_{R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}}}^{+\infty}(y+a_{n})^{N-1}\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{N-1}{2}}e^{-2\gamma\lambda_{n}^{\frac{1}{2}}y}dy

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon^{2}e^{s\gamma R}\int_{R}^{+\infty}(z+1)^{N-1}e^{-2\gamma z}dz

so (
4
) remains bounded as
n\to\infty
with
R
fixed. On the other hand,

\displaystyle\mathscr{I}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2}{q-2}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}u_{n}^{2}dx-\int_{B(0,a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}u_{n}^{2}dx\right)

\displaystyle\geq\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2}{q-2}}\mu_{1}^{2}-\lambda_{n}^{\frac{N}{2}-\frac{2}{q-2}}\int_{B(0,a_{n}+R\lambda_{n}^{-\frac{1}{2}})}u_{n}^{2}dx\to+\infty

as

and

is fixed.
The divergence of the term (

)
follows directly from (
4.17
), used with

.
In all, taking the limit as
{n\to\infty}
and keeping

fixed results in a contradiction between (

) and (

). This implies that the sequence of Lagrange multipliers
\{\lambda_{n}\}
must, in all cases, remain bounded since otherwise there would exist sequences of blow point which, as we have seen, lead to a contradiction. The proof is now finished.
