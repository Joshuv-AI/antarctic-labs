Public Executive Summary
The Ohio State University NEXTCAR project was a multi-phase, multi-year research,
development, implementation and demonstration effort focused on developing and validating
advanced vehicle and powertrain control strategies that dramatically improve the energy
efficiency of connected and automated vehicles (CAVs). The project, which ran from 2016 to
2025, successfully integrated two key areas of innovation: advanced powertrain management
and intelligent vehicle motion control, ultimately showcasing the success of its technologies in a
fully functional and efficient connected and automated test vehicle.

1. Contribution to Understanding
This research fundamentally advanced the understanding and application of holistic energy
management in connected and automated vehicles by developing a powertrain-agnostic
framework that coordinates propulsion system control across internal combustion engine (ICE),
hybrid electric (HEV), battery electric (BEV), and plug-in hybrid electric (PHEV) architectures,
with driver assistance and vehicle automation functions. The team demonstrated the
synergistic benefits of combining powertrain optimization (including Dynamic Skip Fire, which
intelligently shuts off engine cylinders, as well as optimization of electrified propulsion
functions like regenerative braking and transmission gear shifting) with predictive, connectivity-
enabled vehicle speed controls. Among these approaches, intelligent speed planning
optimization, implemented through Eco-Approach and Departure for intersections and Eco-
Adaptive Cruise Control for traffic following, proved to be the primary drivers of the energy
efficiency gains that were ultimately achieved. Building upon that foundation, the integration of
advanced powertrain management strategies further improved adaptability and energy-
efficient performance under real-world driving variability. This project demonstrated
conclusively that optimal vehicle energy efficiency is achieved when vehicle automation and
powertrain systems are fully integrated and operate in coordination based on real-time traffic
and infrastructure data.

2. Technical Effectiveness and Economic Feasibility
The project successfully developed and demonstrated the performance and robustness of the
connectivity-enabled predictive control algorithms under a range of real-world conditions. The
final project demonstration was conducted at the Transportation Research Center, Inc., a
vehicle testing facility in East Liberty, Ohio, in May 2025. The project framework’s technical
effectiveness was demonstrated through the consistent energy-efficiency gains enabled by
intelligent Eco-driving strategies tailored to powertrain characteristics. In one example, the
vehicle control logic identified cases, such as with turbocharged engines, where more assertive
acceleration improved the overall vehicle energy and fuel efficiency. The project technical
approach was validated across two distinct vehicle platforms in Phase I and Phase II, confirming
robustness and adaptability.

3. Public Benefit
The Ohio State University NEXTCAR project provides significant public benefit by advancing
innovation and the transformation of the automotive transportation sector. Its primary
outcome is the significant improvement of vehicle energy efficiency and the corresponding
reduction of energy consumption and individual vehicle operating costs. Overall, the research
and development activity conducted in this project establishes a foundation for a smarter
transportation ecosystem, positioning the deployment of connected and automated vehicle
(CAV) vehicle technologies as a key step in advancing national priorities in energy efficiency,
cost reduction, and technological innovation.

Project Activities
The project focused on minimizing the energy use of connected and automated vehicles by
integrating intelligent vehicle speed planning control with advanced powertrain management.
The central hypothesis was that coordinating these systems using data from vehicle
connectivity (V2I/V2V) to anticipate upcoming driving conditions would yield synergistic energy
savings beyond what isolated and driving-agnostic optimizations can achieve.
 To demonstrate the hypothesis, the OSU team developed and validated two primary control
algorithms: an Eco-Approach and Departure (Eco-AND) which optimizes vehicle speed near
signalized intersections with Signal Phase and Timing (SPaT) data broadcast via V2I
communications, and predictive Eco-Adaptive Cruise Control (Eco-ACC), which benefits of
upcoming route and traffic information from GPS and connectivity services. The integration
and validation of these technologies was successfully demonstrated in a test vehicle operating
under real-world driving conditions across a range of extra-urban scenarios. The experimental
result confirmed significant and verifiable energy efficiency improvements in vehicle energy
consumption.

A subset of the experimental verification tests was repeated during the final Field Day event at
the American Center for Mobility (ACM), independently confirming the energy-efficiency
benefits of the developed control strategies

 To accommodate the requirements of the final technical evaluation, the project schedule was
extended by six months, from November 2024 to June 2025. This modification, with a
corresponding budget restructuring, ensured sufficient time to complete the final integration,
calibration, and conduct rigorous testing necessary for the comprehensive validation of the fully
integrated control system.

 H. Networks/Collaborations Fostered
 The project established a successful, high-impact collaboration between the lead academic
 institution, The Ohio State University (OSU), BorgWarner Inc., a major Tier 1 global
 automotive supplier, and the Transportation Research Center, Inc. (TRC), an independent
 testing facility.
 The Ohio State University gratefully recognizes Stellantis as a key unfunded collaborator in
 the NEXTCAR Phase II program. Stellantis provided extensive technical support and access
 to the Chrysler Pacifica Hybrid platform, including detailed powertrain data and engineering
 expertise, which were essential to the successful integration, calibration, and experimental
 validation of the advanced control technologies developed under this project.
 Finally, the team actively collaborated with the American Center for Mobility (ACM) for the
 final demonstration and evaluation of the connected vehicle technologies.
