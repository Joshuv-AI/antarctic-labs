1
Introduction and reductions

Consider the linear Schrödinger equation on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
,
n\geq 1,
given by

(1)

\begin{cases}i\partial_{t}u(x,t)-\Delta_{x}u(x,t)=0,\\
u(x,0)=f(x).\end{cases}

Its solution can be formally expressed as

(2)

e^{it\Delta}f\left(x\right)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}e^{2\pi ix\cdot\xi}e^{4\pi^{2}it|\xi|^{2}}\widehat{f}(\xi)d\xi.

The question of pointwise convergence was first proposed by Carleson in 1980
[
3
]
. He asked for the values of
s>0
for which

(3)

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f\left(x\right)=f(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

holds true for all functions
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Carleson
[
3
]
proved this convergence when
n=1
and
s\geq\frac{1}{4}
. Shortly after, Dahlberg and Kenig
[
6
]
showed that this condition was necessary, as (
3
) fails whenever
s<\frac{1}{4}
.

Over the years, many researchers have contributed to this problem, including Carbery, Cowling, Sjölin, Vega, Moyua, Vargas, Tao, and Bourgain, among others. A major breakthrough came in higher dimensions. In 2016, Bourgain
[
1
]
proved the necessity of the condition
s\geq\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
. On the sufficiency side, Du, Guth and Li
[
7
]
solved the case
n=2
with
s>\frac{1}{3}
in 2017, and in 2019, Du and Zhang
[
8
]
proved the sharp result for general
n\geq 3
with
s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
.

We consider a variation of this question by approaching the initial datum along a curve. Fix
0<\alpha\leq 1
and
D\geq 1.
Define the family of curves

(4)

\Gamma_{D}^{\alpha}:=\left\{\gamma:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{n}:\text{for all }t,t^{\prime}\in[0,1],\;|\gamma(t)-\gamma(t^{\prime})|\leq D|t-t^{\prime}|^{\alpha}\right\}.

The problem of convergence along such curves was first studied by Cho, Lee, and Vargas
[
4
]
. In dimension
n=1
, they proved that for
\alpha
-Hölder curves with
\alpha\in(0,1]
, the limit
\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f(x+\gamma(t))=f(x)
holds a.e. for
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s>\max\left\{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha,\frac{1}{4}\right\}
, which they also showed to be sharp up to the endpoint. Later, in 2021, Li and Wang
[
14
]
extended this result to dimension
n=2
for the range
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
, proving convergence for
s>\frac{3}{8}
. More recently, in 2023, Cao and Miao
[
2
]
gave a proof for general dimension
n
, for curves with Hölder index
\frac{1}{2}\leq\alpha\leq 1
, and for
s>\frac{n}{2(n+1)}
. Another simpler proof of this fact was given by the first author in
[
11
]
.

In this paper, we are interested in the more delicate regime
\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
, where the curves become highly tangential. Specifically, we ask what sufficient conditions on the regularity of
f
ensure that

(5)

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\gamma(t)\right)=f(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Remark 1.1
.

A change of variables shows that it is enough to consider the case
D=1
. From now on we assume
D=1

and will denote
\Gamma_{1}^{\alpha}
by
\Gamma^{\alpha}.

A maximal bound is a sufficient condition to guarantee the above convergence. Let
B_{r}^{n}(x_{0})
denote the ball of radius
r>0
centered at
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Assume that there exists a positive constant
C
such that, for a
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}
,

(6)

\left\|\sup_{0<t<1}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\gamma(t)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{1}(0)\right)}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{r}\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\right)}

holds for all

f

\in H^{r}(\mathbb{R}^{n}).

Then, (
5
) holds for every
f\in H^{s},
and all
s>r
.

There is a well known estimate.

Remark 1.2
.

Estimate (
6
) holds for any
r>\frac{n}{2}
.

We are going to reduce (
6
) to the bound below. We proceed as in
[
8
]
.
By Littlewood-Paley decomposition, the time localization lemma (e.g. Lemma 3.1 in S. Lee
[
10
]
) and parabolic rescaling, (
6
) can be reduced to the following estimate.
Assume that there exists
s>0
and a constant
C_{s}
satisfying that, for a
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\alpha}
,

(7)

\left\|\sup_{0<t<R}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+R\gamma\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B^{n}_{R}(0)\right)}\leq C_{s}R^{s}\|f\|_{2}

holds for all
R\geq 1
and all
f
with
\operatorname{supp}\widehat{f}\subset A(1)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|\xi|\sim 1\right\}
. Then, (
6
) holds for any
r>s
and all
f\in H^{r}
.

As shown by counterexamples found in
[
1
]
and
[
4
]
,

(8)

s>\max\left\{{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}},{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\right\}

is a necessary condition for (
7
) to hold.

We are interested in finding the infimum of the values
s
that satisfy
(
7
).
In the following section, we discuss a theorem that provides the critical value for
s
when we restrict to a certain family of curves.

2
Main results

Let
R>>1
.
Let
\alpha_{j}>0
for

. Denote

Further suppose that

for

Theorem 2.1
.

Let

\lim_{t\to 0}e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\gamma_{0}(t)\right)=f(x),\hskip 10.00002pt\text{a.e.}\hskip 10.00002ptx\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

for all
f\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
s>s_{0}(n,\alpha)
.

Denote
\theta(t)=\theta_{R}(t)=R\gamma_{0}\left(\frac{t}{R^{2}}\right)
for

. That is, consider

(12)

\theta(t)=\left(R^{1-2\alpha_{1}}t^{\alpha_{1}},...,R^{1-2\alpha_{n}}t^{\alpha_{n}}\right).

By the considerations in Section
1
, the following theorem implies the above
Theorem

2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
n\geq 1
and
0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a positive constant

such that the following holds. For all

,

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t<R}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\theta(t)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B_{R}(0),dx\right)}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{s_{0}(n,\alpha)+\varepsilon}\|f\|_{2},

where
s_{0}(n,\alpha)=\max\left\{{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}},{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\right\}
.

We are going to use the following result from
[
8
]
. We denote
B_{r}^{n+1}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})
the
(n+1)-
dimensional ball of radius r centered at
(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})

(Corollary 1.7 in

)
.

Let

, there exists a constant

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
n\geq 1

0<\alpha<\frac{1}{2}

\varepsilon>0

C

C_{\varepsilon}
and such that the following holds. For all

We first prove (

Proposition 3.2
.

Suppose that

\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset B^{n}_{1}(0)

If

|t-s|\leq 8,
then,

\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x)\right|\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|,

\widehat{f_{\mathfrak{l}}}(\xi)=e^{2\pi i\mathfrak{l}\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)
.
Hence, by Hölder’s inequality, for
p\geq 1
,

(21)

\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x)\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|^{p}.

Therefore, if
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq 4
and
|t-t^{\prime}|\leq 4
, then, for all

(22)

\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x)\right|^{p}\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t^{\prime}+1}\int_{B_{1}(x^{\prime})}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|^{p}dyds,

and,

(23)

\int_{t}^{t+1}\int_{B_{1}(x)}\left|e^{ir\Delta}f(u)\right|^{p}dudr\leq\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{t^{\prime}}^{t^{\prime}+1}\int_{B_{1}(x^{\prime})}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|^{p}dyds.

Proof.

f
with
\text{supp}\widehat{f}\subset B^{n}_{1}(0)

. Define

to be the set of integers inside the

\displaystyle\sup_{0<t<1}\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x+\theta(t))\right|

\displaystyle=\max_{k\in N(\theta)}\sup_{t\in\theta^{-1}(B_{1}(k))\cap[0,1]}\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x+\theta(t))\right|.

For each
k

as an element in
\theta^{-1}(B_{1}(k))\cap[0,1]
such that

(25)

\displaystyle\leq 2\max_{k\in N(\theta)}\left|e^{it_{k}\Delta}f(x+\theta(t_{k}))\right|.

Since
|t_{k}|\leq 1
and
|x+\theta(t_{k})-(x+k)|\leq 1
, by

3.2

(26)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max_{k\in N(\theta)}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{1}(x+k)}|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)|^{2}dyds\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Now take the squared
2-
norm of the above,

(27)

\displaystyle\left\|\sup_{0<t<1}\left|e^{it\Delta}f(x+\theta(t))\right|\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{R}(0),dx)}^{2}

(28)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}(0)}\left(\max_{k\in N(\theta)}\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{B_{1}(x+k)}|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)|^{2}dyds\right)dx.

Majorize the maximum over

(29)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{\mathfrak{l}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{l}}(y)|^{2}\left(\sum_{k\in N(\theta)}\int_{B_{R}(0)}\chi_{B_{1}(x+k)}(y)dx\right)dyds.

), this is

(49)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|j|\leq R\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{(1+|\mathfrak{k}|)^{n+1}}|e^{it_{j}\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{k}}(j+\theta(t_{j}))|^{2}\right).

By the above inequality and Proposition
3.2
, denoting
\widehat{f_{\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{l}}}=e^{2\pi i\mathfrak{l}\xi}e^{2\pi i\mathfrak{k}\xi}\widehat{f}(\xi)
,
we deduce that

(50)

\displaystyle\phantom{=}\left\|\sup_{1<t<R}\left|e^{it\Delta}f\left(x+\theta(t)\right)\right|\right\|_{L^{2}\left(B_{R}(0),dx\right)}^{2}

(51)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|j|\leq R\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{[(1+\mathfrak{k})(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)]^{n+1}}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+1}\int_{B_{1}(j)}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{l}}(z+\theta(t_{j}))\right|^{2}dzds\right),

Change
y=z-\theta(t_{j})
in the inner integral, and it becomes

(52)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|j|\leq R\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{[(1+\mathfrak{k})(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)]^{n+1}}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j}+1}\int_{B_{1}(j+\theta(t_{j}))}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|^{2}dyds\right).

By definition of
Q_{j}
, the above sum is

(53)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\\
|j|\leq R\end{subarray}}\left(\sum_{\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{[(1+\mathfrak{k})(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)]^{n+1}}\int_{Q_{j}}\left|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{l}}(y)\right|^{2}dyds\right).

Now, for each
i
, choose
\lambda,\eta
such that
Q_{i}\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}
. Then, by Fubini’s theorem, the definition of
X_{\lambda,\eta}
, and Remark
3.3
,

(54)

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{\mathfrak{l},\mathfrak{k}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}}\frac{1}{[(1+\mathfrak{k})(1+|\mathfrak{l}|)]^{n+1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\sim 1\end{subarray}}^{R}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\eta\sim 1\end{subarray}}^{R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}}\eta\int_{X_{\lambda,\eta}}|e^{is\Delta}f_{\mathfrak{k},\mathfrak{l}}(y)|^{2}dyds.

Notice that the innermost sum has only the term for
\eta=1
whenever
\lambda\geq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
.
We can apply Lemma
3.4
and obtain (
19
). We are done.
∎

Let us now turn to the proof of Lemma
3.4
. Note that by Theorem
2.4
it can be reduced to the following.

Proposition 3.6
.

For all
\varepsilon>0,
there exists
C_{\varepsilon}>0
such that

(55)

\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\left(y\right)\right\|_{L^{2}\left(X_{\lambda,\eta}\cap(B^{n}_{\lambda}(k)\times[0,2\lambda]),dydt\right)}\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\eta^{-\frac{1}{2}}R^{s_{0}(n,\alpha)}\|f\|_{2},

for all

1\leq\lambda\leq R
,

1\leq\eta\lesssim R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}

and
k\in\lambda\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

3.1
Proof of the last reduction: Proposition
3.6

In this section, we want to use Theorem
2.3
to prove Proposition
3.6
.
Before that, we need to prove three facts related to the density factor
\phi
that arises from Theorem
2.3
.

The density factor
\phi
always has the following three properties. First, fix
1\leq\lambda\leq R
and observe that
for any

X^{\prime}\subset B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
, a union of lattice unit cubes in
B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
, we have that

(56)

\phi_{X^{\prime},n,\lambda}\leq\phi_{X^{\prime},n,R},

by definition (
14
).

Second, we want to remark that translations of the set
X^{\prime}
do not change the estimate. That is, for any choice of
X^{\prime}\subset B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
and any
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B^{n}_{R}(0)
,

(57)

\phi_{X^{\prime}-(k,0),n,R}\leq\phi_{X^{\prime},n,2R}.

This can be shown as follows. Fix
(y,\tau)\in B^{n+1}_{R}(0)
. Then

(58)

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X^{\prime}-(k,0):Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\right\}

(59)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X^{\prime}:Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y+k,\tau)\right\}.

However, calling
y+k=y^{\prime}
, we have
(y^{\prime},\tau)\in B_{2R}^{n+1}(0)
. Thus the above is

(60)

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X^{\prime}:Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y^{\prime},\tau)\right\}.

Taking a maximum as in (
14
) completes the proof of (
57
).

The third property is the Lemma below.

Lemma 3.7
.

For all
1\leq\lambda\leq R
, all
\eta\geq 1

and all
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
we have that, for
X_{\lambda,\eta}
defined as above,

(61)

\phi_{X_{\lambda,\eta},n,R}\lesssim\eta^{-1}\max\{1,R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\}.

Proof.

Fix
\lambda,\eta
and
f
.
We are going to bound the amount of unit cubes within
X_{\lambda,\eta}
that can fit inside an arbitrary ball of radius
r\leq R
.
Notice that, in order to estimate (
61
), it is enough to consider
r\leq\lambda
. Indeed, for
\lambda\leq r,
covering
B_{r}^{n+1}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\cap X_{\lambda,\eta}
with about
\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right)^{n}
balls of radius
\lambda
, we see that

(62)

\#\left\{Q_{k}\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}:Q_{k}\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(x^{\prime},t^{\prime})\right\}\leq\left(\frac{r}{\lambda}\right)^{n}\sup\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\\
\lambda\leq\tau\leq 2\lambda\end{subarray}}\#\left\{Q_{k}\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}\cap B_{\lambda}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\right\}.

Take
(y,\tau)
where
\lambda\leq\tau\leq 2\lambda
and
0<r\leq\lambda
such that
B_{r}(y,\tau)\subset B_{4\lambda}^{n+1}(0)
.
Then

(63)

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}:Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\right\}\leq

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}\#\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:Q_{j}\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\cap\left(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times[\lambda,2\lambda]\right)\right\}.

The set above is a subset of

(64)

\left\{j\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:(j+\theta(t),t)\in B^{n+1}_{r+1}\left(y,\tau\right)\text{, for some }t\in[0,R]\right\}.

The cardinal of this set is bounded by the
n
-volume of

(65)

\displaystyle\,\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x+\theta(t),t)\in B_{r+1}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\text{, for some }t\in[0,R]\right\}

(66)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:(x+\theta(t),t)\in B_{r+1}^{n}(y)\times[\tau-r,\tau+r]\text{, for some }t\in[0,R]\right\}

(67)

\displaystyle\subset

\displaystyle\,B_{r+1}^{n}(y)-\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r].

Whenever
\text{length}(\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r])<r
, the above set has a volume comparable to
r^{n}
. Otherwise, the above set is a tubular
r
-neighborhood of
\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r]
. Therefore, the
n
-volume of the aforementioned set is, up to dimensional constants, lesser than or equal to
r^{n-1}\text{length}\left(\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r]\right)
. From these facts and (
63
), we have that

(68)

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}:Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\right\}\lesssim\eta^{-1}\max\left\{r^{n},r^{n-1}\text{length}\left(\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r]\right)\right\}

Let us calculate the value of the length appearing above. Remember we are denoting
\alpha=\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n\end{subarray}}\{\alpha_{j}\}.
By definition (
12
) of our curve, we have that

(69)

\displaystyle\text{length}\left(\theta[\tau-r,\tau+r]\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau-r}^{\tau+r}|\theta^{\prime}(s)|ds\simeq\int_{\tau-r}^{\tau+r}R^{1-2\alpha}s^{\alpha-1}ds\lesssim 2rR^{1-2\alpha}\tau^{\alpha-1}

Recall
(
68
) and use the above to deduce that

(70)

\displaystyle\#\left\{Q\subset X_{\lambda,\eta}:Q\subset B_{r}^{n+1}(y,\tau)\right\}\lesssim\eta^{-1}r^{n}\max\left\{1,R^{1-2\alpha}\tau^{\alpha-1}\right\}.

Therefore, by definition (
14
), since
\tau\simeq\lambda
,

(71)

\phi_{X_{\lambda,\eta},n,R}\lesssim\eta^{-1}\max\{1,R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\},

and we are done.
∎

We now turn to the proof of Proposition
3.6

Proof.

Fix

1\leq\lambda\leq R

and
1\leq\eta\lesssim R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\cap B^{n+1}_{R}(0)
. Remember that
X_{\lambda,\eta}\subset B_{R}^{n+1}(0)
by definition.
Apply
Theorem

2.3
, at scale
\lambda

instead of
R
,
to bound

(72)

\displaystyle\phantom{leq}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(X_{\lambda,\eta}\cap B^{n+1}_{\lambda}(k,0)\right)}

(73)

\displaystyle=\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|e^{it{\color[rgb]{0,0.85,0.85}}\Delta}[f(\cdot-k)]\right\|_{L^{2}\left((X_{\lambda,\eta}-(k,0))\cap B^{n+1}_{\lambda}(0)\right)}

(74)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\phi_{\left(X_{\lambda,\eta}-(k,0)\right)\cap B^{n+1}_{\lambda}(0),n,\lambda}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f(\cdot-k)\|_{2}.

By
(
56
) and (
57
),
this is

(75)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}(\phi_{X_{\lambda,\eta},n,2R})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}.

By Lemma
3.7
the above is

(76)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}(\eta^{-1}\max\{1,R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\})^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}

Observe that, whenever
R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}\leq\lambda\leq R
, we have
\eta\lesssim R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\leq 1
.

(77)

\displaystyle=C_{\varepsilon}\lambda^{\varepsilon}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}

(78)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2},

and (
55
) is established in this case.

Otherwise, if
1\leq\lambda\leq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
,
by (
76
),

(79)

\displaystyle\phantom{leq}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\|e^{it\Delta}f\right\|_{L^{2}\left(X_{\lambda,\eta}\cap B^{n+1}_{\lambda}(k,0)\right)}

(80)

\displaystyle\lesssim C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\eta^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n+1}}\left(R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}.

Since
\eta\lesssim R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1},
this is

(81)

\displaystyle\leq C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}\left(R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{n+1}}\left(R^{1-2\alpha}\lambda^{\alpha-1}\right)^{\frac{1}{n+1}}\lambda^{\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}

(82)

\displaystyle=C_{\varepsilon}R^{\varepsilon}R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}}\|f\|_{2}.

Denote the constant above by
C_{R,\lambda}=R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}\lambda^{\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}}
. We distinguish two cases.

In the first case, if
\alpha<\frac{1}{n+1}
, then
\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}<0,
thus
C_{R,\lambda}\leq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}\leq R^{s_{0}(n,\alpha)}
and we are done.

In the second case, if
\alpha\geq\frac{1}{n+1}
, since
\lambda\leq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
,

(83)

\displaystyle C_{R,\lambda}

\displaystyle\leq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{2}}R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\left(\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\right)}

(84)

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\left(\frac{1-\alpha}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}-\frac{1}{2(n+1)}\right)}

(85)

\displaystyle=R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}\cdot\frac{n}{2(n+1)}}\leq R^{s_{0}(n,\alpha)}.

This gives (
55
) for
1\leq\lambda\leq R^{\frac{1-2\alpha}{1-\alpha}}
.
∎
