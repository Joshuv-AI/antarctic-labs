Complete Relational Description of Spin in a Quantum Background
Hannah Troger, Ofek Bengyat, Thomas D. Galley, Marios Christodoulou

Abstract
The standard description of the state of a spin in quantum mechanics presupposes externally fixed directions—a classical background. Can a spin be fully described instead in relation to other quantum mechanical systems? Poulin suggested twenty years ago group averaging over rotations the joint state of a fundamental spin and a reference spin with large angular momentum which, however, yields a classical bit in a probabilistic mixture. We revisit this idea and show that when the quantum reference system is augmented to two large spins, the standard quantum mechanical description of a spin is recovered in the limit of large quantum numbers for the reference system.



I
Introduction

The standard quantization of a spin makes reference to a fixed external classical frame, which defines directions along which the spin may be measured. This frame could be imagined to be physically realized, for instance, by the walls of the laboratory. More generally, this ‘classical background’ corresponds to a space geometry, such as a Euclidean space.

However, if all physical systems obey quantum mechanics, including spacetime, the general description of a quantum system—of a spin in particular—should be in relation to other quantum systems. With a view towards a quantum theory of spacetime, such ‘quantum frames of reference’ have been considered since the late 80s

We had to overcome two difficulties. At first glance, it seems intuitive that a spin can be fully described in relation to any other physical system that ‘points’ towards a direction. But, this is only sufficient for a classical spin. For a quantum spin, it is not enough to relationally encode that the joint state is a (mostly) aligned or a (mostly) anti-aligned state. It is also necessary to encode complementarity: the system of reference needs to be a composite system, with two angular momenta that do not commute. This leads to the second difficulty, which is technical: the resulting Clebsch–Gordan coefficients are somewhat complicated.

The calculation presented in this work builds on an idea by Poulin from 2007
31
. They considered the joint state of a spin-1/2 system with a second, larger spin, removing the fiducial information of the coordinate axes by averaging the density matrix over rotations. However, this yields a mixed state for the spin, no different than the state of a classical probabilistic bit (see Section
II
). We will show that augmenting the quantum reference frame with a second large spin and following a similar procedure, yields a relational state that includes information on the coherence of the spin-1/2 system. The encoding becomes approximately exact and the state of the spin-1/2 becomes approximately pure when quantum numbers for the reference are taken large (see Section
III
). This is our main result, sketched in Figure
1

IV.2

IV.3

II
Spin in relation to one reference spin

Poulin discussed the following strategy to extract a relational description of a quantum spin
31
. Start from the standard quantum mechanical description of a joint system consisting of a spin-1/2, which is the system of intrest, and a reference spin. The dependence on classical externally defined directions is removed by (incoherently, see below) group averaging the joint state over rotations. Finally, take the limit of large angular momentum for the reference spin.

Let us illustrate the above concretely. Consider the spin-
1/2
state

\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}}=\alpha\ket{\uparrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}+\beta\ket{\downarrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}.

(1)

The
z
-axis can be physically modeled as a second quantum system
\mathcal{G}
with angular momentum
\hat{J}^{\mathcal{G}}
, prepared in its highest magnetic moment state
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
. That is, we have that
\hat{J}^{\mathcal{G}}\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}=\hat{M}_{z}^{\mathcal{G}}\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}=G\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
, where
\hat{M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
is the magnetic moment of
\mathcal{G}
in the
z
-direction. The state
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
is chosen because it is the ‘most’ semi-classical among the states
\ket{G,M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
, in the sense that it saturates the Heisenberg uncertainty relation. Denoting as
\Delta M_{x}
the variance of the expectation value of
\hat{M}_{x}
on
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
, and similarly for
\Delta M_{y}
, we have that
\Delta M_{x}\Delta M_{y}=\frac{\hbar}{2}
, see e.g
28
. Intuitively, the uncertainty cone around the most probable direction of the spin is minimal.

A spin-1/2 system
\mathcal{S}
alongside a large spin system
\mathcal{G}

II
. Adding another large spin system
\mathcal{H}
to the reference, yields a relational state which approaches an exact encoding of the qubit
\mathcal{S}
as the quantum numbers of the reference grow, see Section
III
. Note that analogous states may be written for
\rho_{j^{\mathcal{SH}}}
.

.

The spin system
\mathcal{S}
and the reference system
\mathcal{G}
do not interact with each other. In the total angular momentum eigenbasis, their joint state is

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle=\left(\alpha\ket{\uparrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}+\beta\ket{\downarrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}\right)\otimes\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}

(2)

\displaystyle=\alpha\ket{G+\tfrac{1}{2},G+\tfrac{1}{2}}_{z}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2G+1}}\ket{G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}}_{z}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}

\displaystyle+\frac{\beta\sqrt{2G}}{\sqrt{2G+1}}\ket{G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}}_{z}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}

The prefactors are the Clebsch-Gordan (CG) coefficients for coupling a spin-
1/2
with a spin
G
.

The dependence on the external
z
-axis can be removed by group averaging the density matrix
\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
over rotations
\omega\in SO(3)
:

\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}})=\int_{SO(3)}\!\!\!\text{d}\omega\;R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}(\omega)\;\rho\;R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}(\omega)^{\dagger},

(3)

where
\text{d}\omega
denotes the Haar measure on
SO(3)
. The above group averaging may be called
incoherent
because it acts diagonally on the density matrix
\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
. However, we will see that this form of group averaging does
not
mean that the spin cannot have quantum coherence; rather, it removes coherence between sectors of different
total
angular momentum (see the calculation in the next Section and the discussion in Section
IV.1
).

The representation
R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}(\omega)
of the rotation group acts on the systems
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{G}
as

R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}(\omega)=D^{1/2}(\omega)\otimes D^{G}(\omega),

(4)

where
D^{j}(\omega)
is the Wigner rotation matrix in the spin-
j
representation. The resulting state is

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}})=

\displaystyle\left(|\alpha|^{2}+\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}\right)|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2}|\otimes\frac{\mathbb{1}_{2G+1}}{2G+1}

(5)

\displaystyle+\frac{2G|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G-\tfrac{1}{2}|\otimes\frac{\mathbb{1}_{2G}}{2G},

where
|G\pm\frac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G\pm\frac{1}{2}|
are projectors on the corresponding total angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
eigenspace. The above state is diagonal in
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
, due to the diagonal group averaging. The sector of the magnetic component along
z
is now maximally mixed: the information for
\hat{M}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
, which is defined with respect to the fiducial background
z
-axis, has been erased. Tracing out the magnetic moment sector, we get the state

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}})=

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+\frac{2G|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G-\tfrac{1}{2}|.

(6)

Let us pause to make a comment of conceptual relevance. As expected, the above state makes no reference to a system of axis: the two states
G\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
correspond to the two systems being aligned or anti-aligned, a relational statement. However, it raises the question: how to interpret the fact that the joint system has
total
angular momentum
G\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
? This seems to imply that both systems are rotating with respect to some other, background system. This implicit presence of background can be also removed via the use of a coherent average, see Section
IV.2
.

Let us now inspect the limit when the quantum number of the reference is large. When
G\gg 1
the second term in (
II
) is negligible, and the state is approximately

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}})\approx|\alpha|^{2}|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2}|+|\beta|^{2}|G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G-\tfrac{1}{2}|.

(7)

This state is the probabilistic mixture of a classical spin with probability
|\alpha|^{2}
of being in the spin-up state and probability
|\beta|^{2}
of being in the spin-down state. Note that the above result can be seen directly from (
2
) and the fact that (
5
) projects on eigenspaces of the total angular momentum of
\mathcal{G}
. We have presented the intermediate steps in this simpler setting, because in the following Section they become necessary.

To understand the apparent loss of coherence, it is instructive to consider as a starting point (instead of the pure state (
1
)) the probabilistic mixture

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathcal{S}}=|\alpha|^{2}|\uparrow\rangle\!\langle\uparrow|^{\mathcal{S}}+|\beta|^{2}|\downarrow\rangle\!\langle\downarrow|^{\mathcal{S}}.

(8)

The joint state of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{G}
becomes

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=\;

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}|\uparrow\rangle\!\langle\uparrow|^{\mathcal{S}}|G,G\rangle\!\langle G,G|^{\mathcal{G}}

(9)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle|\beta|^{2}|\downarrow\rangle\!\langle\downarrow|^{\mathcal{S}}|G,G\rangle\!\langle G,G|^{\mathcal{G}}.

In the total angular momentum eigenbasis, it reads

\displaystyle\mu^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=\;

\displaystyle|\alpha|^{2}|G+\tfrac{1}{2},G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2},G+\tfrac{1}{2}|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}_{z}

(10)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}_{z}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{2G|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}_{z}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2G}\,|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}_{z}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2G}\,|\beta|^{2}}{2G+1}|G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}_{z}.

Note that the diagonal of
\mu^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
is the same as that of the density matrix
\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=|{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}\rangle\!\langle{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}|
where
\ket{\psi^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}
is the pure joint state (
2
). Therefore, the incoherent group average applied on
\mu^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
will yield the same result as on
\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
, as it only keeps the diagonal terms in the total angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
. Then, tracing out the magnetic component sector, again returns the relational state (
7
).

The above implies that with a choice of reference system consisting of a single large spin, only the magnitude of the spin amplitudes can be encoded in the relational state, not their relative phase. The apparent loss of coherence of the target spin-1/2 is
not
due to averaging incoherently over total system rotations. As the next section shows, when the reference system is composite and made of
two
large spins, the same procedure produces a complete relational encoding of the pure spin state.

III
Spin in relation to two reference spins

We now augment the reference system with a second spin system
\mathcal{H}
, which is in a state orthogonal to
\mathcal{G}
with respect to the reference system of axis. That is, the point of departure is the joint state of the spin-
1/2
system
\mathcal{S}
with the two spins
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
, taken to be in the states
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
and
\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\left(\alpha\ket{\uparrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}+\beta\ket{\downarrow}^{\mathcal{S}}_{z}\right)\otimes\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}.

(11)

Since we have a composite reference system
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}
, in order to use recoupling theory we need to make an arbitrary choice on whether to first couple the spin system
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{G}
or
\mathcal{H}
. That is, on whether to first consider the recoupling basis over angular momenta
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
or
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{H}}
. We choose to first couple
\mathcal{S}
to
\mathcal{G}
, and then
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}
to
\mathcal{H}
. The result for when
\mathcal{S}
is first coupled to
\mathcal{H}
can also be surmised at the end of the calculation. The combinatorial coefficients are significantly more involved than the case presented in the previous Section where only one reference large spin was used. Below, we give the main steps of the calculation and refer to Appendix
A
for details.

III.1
Basis Change

We start by changing basis to the eigenbasis of angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
and magnetic moment
m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
of the joint system of
\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{G}
. This is change of basis is of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}},m^{\mathcal{S}}}\right\}\otimes\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{G}},m^{\mathcal{G}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\otimes\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{H}},m^{\mathcal{H}}}\right\}

(12)

\displaystyle\rightarrow\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},j^{\mathcal{S}},j^{\mathcal{G}}}\right\}

\displaystyle\otimes\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{H}},m^{\mathcal{H}}}\right\}.

For brevity, since the eigenvalues
j^{\mathcal{S}}=S
and
j^{\mathcal{G}}=G
are constant, we omit the corresponding state factors
\otimes\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}}=S}
and
\otimes\ket{j^{\mathcal{G}}=S}
. That is, we use the shorthand notation

\displaystyle\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}\equiv\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}\otimes\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}}=S}\otimes\ket{j^{\mathcal{G}}=G}.

(13)

On this basis, the state
\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
is given by

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\Big(

\displaystyle\alpha\ket{G+\tfrac{1}{2},G+\tfrac{1}{2}}

(14)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2G+1}}\ket{G+\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\beta\,\sqrt{2G}}{\sqrt{2G+1}}\ket{G-\tfrac{1}{2},G-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Big)\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}.

Now, we change basis again to the eigenbasis that has as eigenvalues the angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
of the joint system of
\mathcal{S}
,
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
. This is of the form

\displaystyle\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}\right\}\otimes\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{H}},m^{\mathcal{H}}}\right\}\rightarrow\left\{\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}},m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}},j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}\right\}.

(15)

As with the constant
j^{\mathcal{S}}=S
and
j^{\mathcal{G}}=G
factors, we have also suppressed
\otimes\ket{j^{\mathcal{H}}=H}
from the ket-bra notation. To implement this basis change we rewrite the state
\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
in the
z
-basis:

\displaystyle\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}=\frac{1}{2^{H}}\sum_{h\,=-H}^{H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}}\ket{H,h}^{\mathcal{H}}_{z}.

(16)

Inserting this into (
14
) and defining the CG coefficients

\displaystyle C^{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}m^{\mathcal{H}}}:=\braket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}|j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}j^{\mathcal{H}}m^{\mathcal{H}}},

(17)

the state
\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
takes the form

\displaystyle\ket{\psi}^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\sum_{J=G-1/2+h}^{G+1/2+H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}}

(18)

\displaystyle\Bigg[

\displaystyle\alpha\,C^{J,G+1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G+1/2,h}\ket{J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}+h,G+\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle\frac{\beta}{\sqrt{2G+1}}C^{J,G-1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G-1/2,h}\ket{J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}+h,G+\tfrac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle+\,

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2G}\,\beta}{\sqrt{2G+1}}C^{J,G-1/2+h}_{G-1/2,G-1/2,h}\ket{J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}+h,G-\tfrac{1}{2}}\Bigg],

see Appendix
A.1
for intermediate steps. We use the convention that
C^{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}m^{\mathcal{H}}}=0
when
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}<m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
or
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}>j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}+H
.

III.2
Group Averaging

To obtain the relational state, which makes no reference to a fixed system of axis,
we now calculate the incoherent group average of
the density matrix
\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
(given explicitly in Appendix
A.2
), corresponding to the state (
18
). In analogy to (
3
), the incoherent group averaging operation is defined as

\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}})=\int_{SO(3)}\!\!\!\text{d}\omega\;R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}(\omega)\;\rho^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}\;R^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}(\omega)^{\dagger}.

(19)

After tracing out the identity on the magnetic moment sector (compare to (
5
) and (
II
) in the previous Section), we get

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})=

\displaystyle\sum_{J=G-1/2-H}^{G+1/2+H}\Bigg[

(20)

\displaystyle\frac{\left|\alpha\right|^{2}}{2}\,S^{(1)}_{JGH}|J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2G}\alpha\bar{\beta}}{\sqrt{2G+1}}S^{(2)}_{JGH}|J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{G\,\left|{\beta}\right|^{2}}{2G+1}\,S^{(3)}_{JGH}|J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2G}\,\left|{\beta}\right|^{2}}{2G+1}\,S^{(4)}_{JGH}|J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G-\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha\bar{\beta}}{\sqrt{2G+1}}S^{(5)}_{JGH}|J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}|

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\frac{\left|{\beta}\right|^{2}}{2(2G+1)}\,S^{(6)}_{JGH}|J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle\!\langle J,G+\tfrac{1}{2}|\Bigg]+h.c.

Recall that these operators are projectors of the form
|j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}},j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}\rangle\!\langle j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}},j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}|
. The coefficients
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
are real numbers satisfying
-1\leq S^{(i)}_{JGH}\leq 1
. They depend on the total angular momenta
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=J
and on the constant quantum numbers
j^{\mathcal{G}}=G
and
j^{\mathcal{H}}=H
. The coefficients
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
are defined in Appendix
A.2
.

Due to the use of the incoherent group average, we expect that the total angular momentum
J
is in a probabilistic mixture. As we see in a moment, while the state (
20
) is clearly not pure, nevertheless, this relational description of the spin-1/2
\mathcal{S}
with respect to
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
has encoded information on the coherence of
\mathcal{S}
. We now turn to studying the scaling behavior of the CG coefficients
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
in the regime of large and equal reference spins
G
and
H
.

III.3
Large reference spins limit

We now take
G=H\gg 1
. The idea is that the usual state of a spin-1/2 should be recovered from the state (
20
), since
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
would behave approximately as two classical gyroscopes pointing in orthogonal directions: the physical realization of two Cartesian axis.

We verified this through a numerical investigation, which is given in detail in Appendix
A.3
. The main points are as follows. The coefficients
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
are all bounded from below by
-1
and from above by
1
. Then, by inspection of (
20
), when
G\gg 1
, the matrix elements corresponding to
S^{(4)}_{JGH}
,
S^{(5)}_{JGH}
and
S^{(6)}_{JGH}
will be negligible due to the pre-factors falling as
1/\sqrt{G}
,
1/\sqrt{G}
and
1/G
respectively. The matrix elements corresponding to
S^{(1)}_{JGH}
,
S^{(2)}_{JGH}
and
S^{(3)}_{JGH}
have pre-factors that fast become constant when
G\gg 1
. Therefore, only these three terms are not negligible.

Then, the state
\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{C}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})
becomes approximately:

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathcal{E}}^{C}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})

\displaystyle=\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}}\bra{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}}

(21)

where

\ket{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}}=\alpha\ket{G+1/2}+\beta\ket{G-1/2}.

(22)

This is our main result. As expected, the density matrix
\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}
, defined in Appendix
A
and depicted in Fig.
2
, is diagonal in the total angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=J
. It is highly peaked on
J=\sqrt{2G}
, the classical magnitude when adding two orthogonal vectors of equal magnitude
G
. That is, as
G=H
grows it approaches the pure state
\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}\approx\ket{J=\sqrt{2G}}\bra{J=\sqrt{2G}}
. It holds classical information regarding the total angular momentum of the total system
\mathcal{SGH}
. The spin state also approaches the aligned/anti-aligned pure state (
22
), defined relationally with respect to the
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=G\pm\frac{1}{2}
eigenstates. Analogously, had we followed the above procedure but first coupled
\mathcal{S}
with
\mathcal{H}
, we would have arrived at a similar result to (
21
), with the spin-1/2 state being instead

\ket{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{H}}}}=\frac{\alpha+\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{H+1/2}+\frac{\alpha-\beta}{\sqrt{2}}\ket{H-1/2}.

(23)

We have shown that the composite system
\mathcal{GH}

IV
Comments

IV.1
The classical limit

We saw in Section
II
that when the reference system consists of only one large spin, the relational state for the spin-1/2 is a probabilistic mixture, see (
7
). This makes it seem like an effective projective measurement was done, thereby ‘collapsing’ the state
31
.

However, we are only manipulating the states through the (re)coupling theory of
SU(2)
, there is no interaction coupling: no measurement can be taking place. Instead, the quantum system used as a reference could not capture the information about the quantum coherence of the spin-1/2. For this, a second spin pointing in a different direction is needed. In fact, while it was convenient for calculation purposes to take two spins that become orthogonal in the classical limit, a composite reference system of any two non-parallel large spins would also suffice (see Section
IV.3
).

When two large spins are used as a composite reference system, the spin-1/2 can be described relationally with invariant (under rotations) quantum numbers. To extract information about
\mathcal{S}
, measurements can be done on the final density matrix. Concretely, a (tomographically) complete set of observables to describe
\mathcal{S}
are the following Pauli-type spin operators:

\displaystyle\Sigma_{1}

\displaystyle=|G+1/2\rangle\!\langle G-1/2|+|G-1/2\rangle\!\langle G+1/2|

(24)

\displaystyle\Sigma_{2}

\displaystyle=-i|G+1/2\rangle\!\langle G-1/2|+i|G-1/2\rangle\!\langle G+1/2|

\displaystyle\Sigma_{3}

\displaystyle=|G+1/2\rangle\!\langle G+1/2|+|G-1/2\rangle\!\langle G-1/2|.

The observables
\Sigma_{i}
are defined with respect to the joint angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{SG}}=G\pm 1/2
eigenstates of the subsystem
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}
, which make no reference to a system of axis. As before, a similar definition would hold with respect to
j^{\mathcal{SH}}=H\pm 1/2
if we had first coupled
\mathcal{H}
to
\mathcal{S}
.

Let us now discuss the formal limit
G=H\rightarrow\infty
. A technical point here is that for the limit of (
20
) to make sense, the bounded pre-factors
-1\leq S^{(i)}_{JGH}\leq 1
must converge. We give numerical evidence which strongly implies that this holds in Appendix
A.3
. Now, while the angular momenta
G
and
H
are infinite, the relational description remains meaningful: the Hilbert space is still
\mathbb{C}^{2}
. The two states
\ket{G\pm 1/2}
become
orthogonal
, as is expected from two classical gyroscopes.

Intuitively, the cone of uncertainty around the most probable direction that a reference spin is pointing towards shrinks to zero. In particular, (
21
) implies that the joint state becomes a pure state both for the fundamental spin and the composite reference system. For the latter, the state corresponds to a total angular momentum of
\sqrt{2}G
, the length of the vector sum of the two orthogonal gyroscopes
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
.

In other words, although each gyroscope is treated fully quantum mechanically throughout, the limit
G,H\to\infty
makes the spin states with distinct orientations effectively orthogonal, so that the reference systems have sharply distinguishable directions. Note that this holds in the limit whenever
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
point in directions differing by arbitrarily small angles. Therefore, as shown in Section
IV.3
, we expect that any two non-parallel reference spins suffice to fully resolve the spin-1/2 state in the classical limit.

The above can be compared with the description of spin in a quantum reference frame considered in
23
. In that work, a reference system was considered consisting of three orthogonal spins with infinite angular momentum from the outset, mimicking a Cartesian system of three axis. The reference system is not considered classical in that work in the sense that it can be imagined in a quantum superposition, but it does not encode any non-trivial commutation relations. Here, we have started from two spins with finite angular momentum, used recoupling theory, and showed that in the limit of infinite reference spins full information on the target spin is encoded.

In summary, in the classical limit the states
\ket{G\pm 1/2}
can be thought of abstractly as two states
\ket{A}
and
\ket{AA}
, for aligned and anti-aligned, defining a qubit. The physical interpretation of the system in terms of relational quantities, is well defined and coincides with the usual description of a spin with respect to two classical orthogonal directions.

IV.2
Coherent vs incoherent average

Let us now examine how the calculation changes when using the coherent, rather than the incoherent, group average, and discuss the conceptual differences between the two approaches. Assuming a group
\Omega
with Haar measure
\text{d}\omega
, the coherent group average projects a vector
\ket{\psi}
on its invariant component:

\ket{\psi}\mapsto\int_{\Omega}U(g)\ket{\psi}\;\text{d}\omega.

(25)

As an operation on density matrices, it reads

|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\mapsto\left(\int_{\Omega}U(\omega)\ket{\psi}\;\text{d}\omega\right)\left(\int_{\Omega}\bra{\psi}U^{\dagger}(\omega^{\prime})\,\;\text{d}\omega^{\prime}\right).

(26)

This is different from the incoherent average that we have used, which corresponds to the diagonal group integration:

|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|\mapsto\int_{\Omega}U(\omega)|\psi\rangle\!\langle\psi|U^{\dagger}(\omega)\;\text{d}\omega.

(27)

While the coherent average fixes the total angular momentum
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
to zero, the incoherent average does not in general fix
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
. In this sense, the coherent average can be interpreted as treating the total angular momentum of the system as fiducial, a gauge effect which is to be done away with by projecting to the gauge invariant subspace. Indeed, in order to attribute any physical significance to the total angular momentum of the system, it would need to be rotating with respect to something else. Therefore, in order to have a description that does not implicitly assume the existence of anything else other than the spin
and the two reference spins, the coherent average must be used.

The relational state resulting from the coherent average can be surmised from the results we have already arrived at through the incoherent average. Projecting (
18
) onto
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=0
fixes
h=-j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
,
so that
m^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}+h=0
. For large
G
, the second term in (
18
), which goes as
\beta/\sqrt{2G+1}
, is negligible.
The first and third terms have coefficients
\alpha
and
(\sqrt{2G}/\sqrt{2G+1})\,\beta\to\beta
respectively. Each contains a CG coefficient of the form

C^{0,\,0}_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},\,j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}},\,-j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}
with
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}=G\pm\tfrac{1}{2}
.
Since
G\pm\tfrac{1}{2}\approx G
for large
G
, the two CG coefficients can be taken equal. Likewise, both terms have
h\approx-G
in the binomial weights, and can also be taken approximately equal. Therefore, denoting with
P^{0}
the projection on the invariant subspace, the coherent average yields that
P^{0}|\psi\rangle^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
is proportional to

\displaystyle\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=0}\otimes\Big(\alpha\big|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\big\rangle+\beta\big|G-\tfrac{1}{2}\big\rangle\Big).

(28)

We can compare this to (
21
), the result of the incoherent average. As we have seen, when
G=H\gg 1
, it reads approximately

\ket{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\sqrt{2}G}\otimes\Big(\alpha\,\big|G+\tfrac{1}{2}\big\rangle\;+\;\beta\,\big|G-\tfrac{1}{2}\big\rangle\Big)

(29)

Notice that if we were to take the formal limit of large
G

before
projecting the state (
18
) onto
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=0
, we would have arrived at a
different
result than (
28
). The reason is that the leading term in the sum over
h
and
J
is
\ket{H,0}
, see (
18
), and this has zero projection in the
z
-direction. Inspection of (
16
)
shows that the only components that survive the projection to the
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=0
sector are those
with a large negative z-component. Their coefficients are far smaller than that of the term
\ket{H,0}
, which most closely resembles a classical gyroscope, but this plays no role since after the projection to this sector the state is again normalized.

To summarize, the incoherent group averaging diagonalizes over the total angular momentum of the entire system: it destroys coherence between the total angular momentum sectors of the entire system
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}\mathcal{S}
. This treats as classical the
total
angular momentum of the system. However, as (
21
), (
22
) and (
23
) demonstrate, this does not imply that the coherence on
subsystems
is erased: within a block of definite total angular momentum, we have coherent superpositions of the angular momenta states of the subsystems
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{S}
and
\mathcal{H}\mathcal{S}
. As seen from (
28
) and (
29
), using the coherent instead of the incoherent average would simply have the effect that the total angular momentum is fixed to zero, instead of being in a probabilistic mixture that is highly peaked on the classically expected value.

IV.3
Relation to reference frame quantization

We now discuss the relation of our construction to what is known as reference frame quantization. The idea here is as follows: given a symmetry group
\Omega
with Haar measure
\text{d}\omega
, a target system
\mathcal{H}_{S}
and a reference system
\mathcal{H}_{R}
transforming under
U_{S}(\omega)
and
U_{R}(\omega)
respectively with
\omega\in\Omega
, the system
S
is mapped onto the
\Omega
-invariant subsystem of
\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
. More explicitly, the aim is to find maps from states
\rho_{S}
and effects (observables)
E_{S}

\displaystyle\rho_{S}

\displaystyle\mapsto\rho_{\mathrm{inv}},

(30)

\displaystyle E_{S}

\displaystyle\mapsto E_{\mathrm{inv}},

where
\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathcal{I}^{\Omega}(\rho_{\mathrm{inv}})
and
E_{\mathrm{inv}}=\mathcal{I}^{\Omega}(E_{\mathrm{inv}})
are
\Omega
-invariant operators on
\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
, and
\mathcal{I}^{\Omega}(\cdot)=\int_{\Omega}(U_{S}(\omega)\otimes U_{R}(\omega))(\cdot)(U_{S}^{\dagger}(\omega)\otimes U_{R}^{\dagger}(\omega))\;\text{d}\omega
. Moreover, we are interested in the notion of an
exact encoding
of
\mathcal{H}_{S}
in the G-invariant subsystems of
\mathcal{H}_{S}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{R}
. This is the case when
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{S}E_{S})=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}E_{\mathrm{inv}})
, possibly in some limit.

A general procedure for implementing
an exact encoding for compact groups
\Omega
is proposed in
5
. The reference systems are taken to be
\mathcal{H}_{R}\simeq L^{2}(\Omega)
acted on by the left regular representation:
U_{R}(\chi)\ket{\omega}=\ket{\chi\omega}
where
\braket{\omega|\chi}=\delta_{\omega,\chi}
for
\omega,\chi\in\Omega
. The encoding is then:

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\mapsto\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{\Omega}U_{S}(\omega)\rho_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(\omega)\otimes|\omega\rangle\!\langle\omega|_{R}\;\text{d}\omega,

(31)

\displaystyle E_{S}\mapsto E_{\mathrm{inv}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}U_{S}(\omega)\rho_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(\omega)\otimes|\omega\rangle\!\langle\omega|_{R}\;\text{d}\omega,

where
N
is some normalisation constant
N\mathbb{1}=\int|g\rangle\!\langle g|\ \text{d}g
(for a rigorous treatment of this integral see e.g.
21
).

Taking
Omega=\mathrm{SO}(3)
or
\mathrm{SU}(2)
, the procedure sketched above is different from the one that we have presented, as we have made use of reference frames that are not isomorphic to
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
. A precise group representation theoretic characterisation of the
\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}
reference system used in this work, and how it contrasts to
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
reference frames, is given in Appendix
B.2
.

We find it difficult to imagine how to physically model the quantum reference system as living in
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
. Considering instead a composite reference system made of two quantum spins, as we have done in this work, the interpretation of the relational state becomes straightforward. Furthermore, the description is quantitative, the formulas we have arrived at are explicit and could be used to calculate numerically e.g. the amount of coherence in the spin-1/2 sector for given
G
and
H
.

On the other hand, using techniques inspired from the formal apparatus developed in
5
, we can make an abstract but general statement regarding the case of a system of interest with many degrees of freedom. We have established here that a pair of gyroscopes
\mathcal{G},\mathcal{H}
can fully resolve a single spin-1/2 state in the classical limit
G,H\to\infty
. We now ask, does this result extends to qudits, which carry spin
\frac{d-1}{2}
representations of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
? Indeed, we show in Appendix
B.3
that for any compact group
G
and coherent state system
\{\ket{\psi(\omega)}|\omega\in\Omega\}
where
\ket{\psi(\omega)}\not\sim\ket{\psi(\chi)}
for
\chi\neq\omega
, if there is a well defined limit
\braket{\psi(\omega)|\psi(\chi)}\to\delta_{\omega,\chi}
, in this limit we have an exact encoding:
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}E_{\mathrm{inv}})\to\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{S}E_{S})
. This holds true for the type of states we have used to model the reference system
\mathcal{GH}
, of the form
U(\omega)\ket{G,G}
. They can be chosen to form an overcomplete coherent state basis and so that for any two different
\omega
they become orthogonal in the limit of large quantum number
G
, see e.g.
28
for details.

Therefore, the general moral of the calculation carries through to target systems of many degrees of freedom: a reference quantum system with two non-commuting operators satisfying the algebra of angular momenta increasingly resolves the quantum state of a target system that transforms under
SU(2)
as the quantum numbers of the reference grow larger, and does so exactly in the limit.

V
Conclusion

We showed how a spin-
1/2
can be fully described in relation to another quantum system. The point of departure was to write in the standard quantum mechanical formalism the joint state of a qubit
\mathcal{S}
with two other quantum mechanical spins
\mathcal{G}
and
\mathcal{H}
that are pointing in orthogonal directions and have arbitrary angular momenta. We calculated the state after the incoherent group average over
\mathrm{SO}(3)
, which removes any reference to external fixed axes. The directional information that remains is relational, encoding quantum amplitudes on the degree that
\mathcal{S}
is aligned or anti-aligned with the reference angular momenta. We demonstrated that when the quantum numbers of the reference systems are large, the usual state of the qubit is recovered, thereby demonstrating that the encoding becomes exact as the classical limit for the reference is approached.

We clarified in this context some points of contention in the contemporary discussion on quantum reference frames regarding when it is appropriate to use the incoherent or the coherent average. The incoherent group averaging does
not
result in loss of coherence of the target system—here the spin-1/2. It is the coherence between sectors of different
total
angular momentum that is lost; the coherence between the angular momenta of subsystems is encoded. Only the latter information is what is relevant if what is sought after is to remove any reference to a background. The incoherent average retains information about the coherence of subsystems, but yields a non-zero total angular momentum for the entire system. This implies that a background system is needed, in order to physically interpret the total rotation of the joint system.

Whether to use the incoherent versus the coherent average depends on whether the total angular momentum of the system is to be treated as physical or not. For a completely background independent relational description, the coherent average should be used. This also would be appropriate, for instance, if the operators satisfying the algebra of angular moment do not physically correspond to rotations but to areas, as happens in the theory of spin-networks forming the kinematics of loop quantum gravity. Indeed, in that theory, the coherent average is used (in conjunction with a closure constraint) to define quantum polyhedral geometries, see e.g.
34
;
6
. We have seen that the result for the coherent average procedure in the limit of large quantum numbers for the reference can be extracted from the relational state resulting from the incoherent average. In this limit, the only difference between the two procedures is that the coherent average forces a zero total angular momentum while the incoherent average fixes it to the classically expected value.

Finally, we have shown that the general conclusion of our case study for a spin-1/2 target system, carries through for arbitrarily large target systems that transform under
SU(2)
: a quantum reference system with two operators that do not commute and satisfy the algebra of angular momenta, will yield an exact encoding of the target system in the limit of large quantum numbers for the reference. Regarding future steps, it is of particular interest to study how well the encoding works for more general reference states (entangled, mixed or non-coherent spin states), thereby quantifying how imperfections in the quantum reference frame degrade the recovered coherence.

\displaystyle S^{(4)}_{JGH}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\binom{2H}{H+h}\bar{C}^{J,G-1/2+h}_{G-1/2,\,G-1/2,h}C^{J,G-1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G-1/2,h}

\displaystyle S^{(5)}_{JGH}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}\binom{2H}{H+h+1}}C^{J,G+1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G+1/2,h}\bar{C}^{J,G+1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G-1/2,h+1}

\displaystyle S^{(6)}_{JGH}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\binom{2H}{H+h}|C^{J,G-1/2+h}_{G+1/2,G-1/2,h}|^{2}.

A.3
Large Gyroscope Limit

Note that even though the Clebsch-Gordan coefficient are all real numbers, we keep the complex conjugate notation for bookkeeping purposes. The Clebsch-Gordan coefficients are real numbers between -1 and 1 since they are inner products of normalized vectors. This implies that the sums
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
defined above are also bounded by -1 from below and 1 from above:

\displaystyle\left|S^{(i)}_{JGH}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\binom{2H}{H+h}=\frac{1}{4^{H}}\cdot 2^{2H}=1.

(39)

Note that for
i=2,5
, the binomial coefficient is replaced with
\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}\binom{2H}{H+h+1}}
, but this does not influence the limit, as can be confirmed numerically.

When
G=H\to\infty
, the matrix elements in
\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})
are of the form
a^{(i)}_{G}S^{(i)}_{JGH}
. For
i=4,5,6
, we have that
a^{(i)}_{G}\to 0
. Since
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
are bounded, the corresponding matrix element vanishes, that is,
a^{(4,5,6)}_{G}S^{(4,5,6)}_{JGH}\to 0
.

For the remaining matrix elements
i=1,2,3
, we denote
S^{(1,2,3)}_{JGH}\to S^{(1,2,3)}_{J\infty}
. We work under the assumption that this limit exists. We give numerical evidence that this is the case at the end of this Appendix. The incoherently group averaged state in the limit
G=H\to\infty
is therefore given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})\to\mathcal{E}^{\lim}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})=\sum_{J=1/2}^{\infty}

\displaystyle\Bigg[\frac{|\alpha|^{2}}{2}\,S^{(1)}_{J\infty}|J,G+1/2\rangle\!\langle J;\,G+1/2|

\displaystyle+\frac{|\beta|^{2}}{2}\,S^{(2)}_{J\infty}|J,G-1/2\rangle\!\langle J,G-1/2|

\displaystyle+\alpha\bar{\beta}\,S^{(3)}_{J\infty}|J,G+1/2\rangle\!\langle J,G-1/2|\Bigg]+h.c.

The letter
G
remains here not as a variable, but only as part of the conventional label for the two different eigenstates of the operator
j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}
. Furthermore, looking at the expressions for
S^{(i)}_{JGH}
one notices the numerically verifiable fact that for all

,
S^{(1)}_{J\infty}=S^{(2)}_{J\infty}=S^{(3)}_{J\infty}
(see Fig.

S_{J\infty}
. We can therefore further compactify the expression for the limit physical state to the form (

),

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}^{\lim}(\rho^{\mathcal{SGH}})

\displaystyle=\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}\otimes\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}=\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}\otimes\ket{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}}\bra{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}},

(40)

\displaystyle\ket{\psi_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}}}}

\displaystyle=\alpha\ket{G+1/2}+\beta\ket{G-1/2}.

with the constant, numeric density matrix

\displaystyle\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}=\sum_{J=1/2}^{\infty}S_{J\infty}|J\rangle\!\langle J|,

(41)

describing the total angular momentum of the entire system
\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}
. Its values
\bra{J}\rho_{j^{\mathcal{S}\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}}\ket{J}=S_{J\infty}

.

Appendix B
Coherent state characterisation of quantum reference frames

In this appendix we contrast the reference frame constructed out of gyroscopes used in the present work to the
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
reference frame used in the reference frame quantization procedure of
5
. We show that these are two different group coherent state systems
28
, even though both allow for a full encoding of a qubit in the relative degrees of freedom between the reference frame and spin 1/2 particle. The
L^{2}(G)
reference frame is ideal, in the sense that it allows for a perfect encoding of the system of interest in the
G
-invariant algebra of the composite of reference and system. Examples of reference frame quantization in the literature which explore the relation between the size of the reference frame and the accuracy of the encoding, as done in the present work, include
24
;
35
;
22
.

B.1
Ideal
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
coherent state systems

For a compact group
G
the space of square integrable functions
L^{2}(G)
(for a given choice of measure) transforms under the left regular representation as:

\displaystyle U_{L}(g)\ket{g^{\prime}}=\ket{gg^{\prime}}.

(42)

Using the Peter-Weyl theorem we can decompose
L^{2}(G)
under the left regular representation of
G
as:

\displaystyle L^{2}(G)\simeq\bigoplus_{\lambda\in\hat{G}}V_{\lambda}\otimes W_{\lambda},

(43)

where
\hat{G}
is the set of irreducible representations of
G
,
V_{\lambda}
carries the irreducible representation
U_{\lambda}
of
G
, and the
multiplicity
space
W_{\lambda}
carries
\dim(W_{\lambda})
copies of the trivial representation. Moreover
\dim(V_{\lambda})=\dim(W_{\lambda})
. In the case of
G=\mathrm{SO}(3)
the irreps are labeled by integers
\lambda
and hence parametertising a rotation
\Omega\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
by three real parameters
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
gives:

\displaystyle U_{L}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\simeq\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}D^{j}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2j+1}.

(44)

The states
\ket{g}
have support in every
j\in\mathbb{N}
and hence the coherent state system transforms under
\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}D^{j}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\otimes\mathbb{1}_{2j+1}
.

As shall be proven in the next section a key difference with the
\mathrm{SO}(3)
coherent state system constructed in the present work is that the
L^{2}(\mathrm{SO}(3))
coherent state system also carries a unitary representation of the right regular action, which in this case is the right regular representation:

\displaystyle U_{R}(g)\ket{g^{\prime}}=\ket{g^{\prime}g^{-1}},

(45)

where once more using the Peter-Weyl theorem:

\displaystyle U_{R}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma)\simeq\bigoplus_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\mathbb{1}_{2j+1}\otimes\bar{D}^{j}(\alpha,\beta,\gamma).

(46)

B.2
Orthogonal gyroscope coherent state systems

B.2.1
Representation theoretic characterisation

The quantum reference frame of Sec.
III
is obtained from the reference state
\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
acted on by the representation
D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)
:
\ket{\psi(\Omega)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
,
\Omega\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
.

Using
\mathrm{SU}(2)
representation theory we can decompose the representation as follows:

\displaystyle V^{G}\otimes V^{H}

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}V^{j},

(47)

\displaystyle D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)

\displaystyle\simeq\bigoplus_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}D^{j}(\Omega).

We now determine the support of the reference state
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
on the subspaces
V^{j}
carrying representations
D^{j}
.
Using standard angular momentum coupling rules we have:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}

\displaystyle=\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\left(\frac{1}{2^{H}}\sum_{h\,=-H}^{H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}}\ket{H,h}^{\mathcal{H}}_{z}\right)

(48)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{2^{H}}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}}\sum_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}C^{j,G+h}_{G,G;H,h}\ket{j,G+h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{H}}\sum_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}\sum_{h=-H}^{H}\sqrt{\binom{2H}{H+h}}C^{j,G+h}_{G,G;H,h}\ket{j,G+h}.

For every
j\in\{|G-H|,...,G+H\}
one has a
h\in\{-H,...,H\}
such that
j=G+h
, hence for every
j
there is a coefficient
C^{j,G+h}_{G,G;H,h}=C^{j,j}_{G,G;H,h}\neq 0
. This shows that
\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
has support in every irrep
D^{j}
.

Since
\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}
has support in every irreducible representation
D^{j}(R)
for
j\in\{|G-H|,...,G+H\}
the coherent state system
\{\ket{\psi(\Omega)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}|\Omega\in\mathrm{SO}(3)\}
transforms under the representation
D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}D^{j}(\Omega)
of
\mathrm{SO}(3)
.

To confirm that it is a
\mathrm{SO}(3)
coherent state system we need to show that:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(\Omega)}=e^{i\phi}\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime})}\implies\Omega=\Omega^{\prime},\forall\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathrm{SO}(3).

(49)

Equivalently we need to show that the stabilizer group of the ray
\ket{\psi(I)}
is trivial. It is sufficient to show that the stabilizer group of the vector
\ket{\psi(I)}
is trivial.

The stabilizer of
\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}
is the
{\rm U}(1)
subgroup
{\rm Stab}(\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z})=D^{G}(e^{iZt}),t\in[0,2\pi)
and the stabilizer of
\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
is the
{\rm U(1)}
subgroup
{\rm Stab}(\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x})=D^{G}(e^{iXt}),t\in[0,2\pi)
. The stabilizer group
{\rm Stab}(\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x})
of
\ket{\psi(I)}^{\mathcal{G}\mathcal{H}}=\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z}\otimes\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x}
, is given by
{\rm Stab}(\ket{G,G}^{\mathcal{G}}_{z})\cap{\rm Stab}(\ket{H,H}^{\mathcal{H}}_{x})=D^{G}(I)\otimes D^{H}(I)
.

B.2.2
Left-right regular action

The
\mathrm{SO}(3)
coherent state system
\ket{\psi(\Omega)}
carries a left regular action (which is not the left regular representation however):

\displaystyle D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime})}=\ket{\psi(\Omega\Omega^{\prime})},\forall\Omega,\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathrm{SO}(3).

(50)

We now show that the right regular action:

\displaystyle\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime})}\mapsto\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime}\Omega^{-1})},

(51)

is not unitary.

Let us assume there exists a unitary representation
C:\Omega\mapsto C(\Omega)
such that
C(\Omega)\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime})}=\ket{\psi(\Omega^{\prime}\Omega^{-1})}
. We now show that this leads to a contradiction.

By construction this representation commutes with
D(\Omega)=D^{G}(\Omega)\otimes D^{H}(\Omega)
. By Schur’s lemma this implies that:

\displaystyle C(\Omega)\simeq\bigoplus_{j=|G-H|}^{G+H}c_{j}(\Omega)\mathbb{1}^{j},c_{j}(\Omega)\in\mathbb{C}\ \forall j\in\{|G-H,...,G+H\}.

(52)

By unitarity
c_{j}(\Omega)=e^{i\theta_{j}(\Omega)}
for all
\forall j\in\{|G-H,...,G+H\},\Omega\in\mathrm{SO}(3)
.

Eq. (
51
) implies that
C(\Omega)\ket{\psi(I)}=\ket{\psi(\Omega^{-1}}
. Define
\Pi_{j}
the projector onto
V_{j}
, the carrier space of the irreducible representation
D_{j}
. Then
\Pi_{j}(C(\Omega)\ket{\psi(I)})=c_{j}(\Omega)\Pi_{j}(\ket{\psi(I)})
. However by assumption
D(\Omega^{-1})\ket{\psi(I)}=C(\Omega)\ket{\psi(I)}
which implies
\Pi_{j}(D(\Omega^{-1})\ket{\psi(I)})=D^{j}(\Omega^{-1})\Pi_{j}\ket{\psi(I)}=c_{j}(\Omega)\Pi_{j}(\ket{\psi(I)})
. This implies the one dimensional subspace spanned by
\Pi_{j}(\ket{\psi(I)})
is invariant under
D^{j}(\Omega)
which contradicts the fact that
D^{j}
is irreducible and of dimension strictly greater than 1 for
j\neq 0
.

B.3
Reference frame quantization for generic coherent state system

We now consider a reference frame
\mathcal{H}_{R}
consisting of some coherent state system for
G
:
\{\ket{\psi(g)}|g\in G\}
which is not ideal:
|\!\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(g^{\prime})}\!|^{2}\neq 0
for
g\neq g^{\prime}
. We moreover assume that:

\displaystyle\int|\psi(g)\rangle\!\langle\psi(g)|\;\text{d}g=N\mathbb{1},\quad N\in\mathbb{R}.

(53)

The encoding maps are:

\displaystyle\rho_{S}\mapsto\tilde{\rho}_{\mathrm{inv}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{Nd_{R}}\int_{G}U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes|\psi(g)\rangle\!\langle\psi(g)|_{R}\;\text{d}g,

(54)

\displaystyle E_{S}\mapsto\tilde{E}_{\mathrm{inv}}:=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\int_{G}U_{S}(g)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes|\psi(g)\rangle\!\langle\psi(g)|_{R}\;\text{d}g,

where
d_{R}=\dim(\mathcal{H}_{R})
.

The probabilities for the encoded states and effects are:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}E_{\mathrm{inv}})=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\mathrm{Tr}\left[\int_{G}U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(g)\otimes|\psi(g)\rangle\!\langle\psi(g)|_{R}\;\text{d}g\quad\int_{G}U_{S}(h)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(h)\otimes|\psi(h)\rangle\!\langle\psi(h)|_{R}\;\text{d}h)\right]

(55)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(g^{-1}h)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(h)\otimes\ket{\psi(g)}\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(h)}\bra{\psi(h)}_{R}\right]\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}h,

we carry out the change of integration variable:
g^{-1}h\to k
and
g\to g
:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\otimes\ket{\psi(g)}\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(gk)}\bra{\psi(gk)}_{R}\right]\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}k,

(56)

since
\ket{\psi(h)}_{R}
form an overcomplete basis we use
\mathrm{Tr}_{R}(\rho_{RS})=\frac{1}{N}\int\bra{\psi(h)}_{R}\rho_{RS}\ket{\psi(h)}_{R}\;\text{d}h
:

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\right]\times\frac{1}{N}\int_{G}\bra{\psi(h)}\;\ket{\psi(g)}\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(gk)}\bra{\psi(gk)}_{R}\;\ket{\psi(h)}\;\text{d}h\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}k

(57)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\right]\times\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(gk)}\bra{\psi(gk)}\frac{1}{N}\left[\int_{G}\ket{\psi(h)}\bra{\psi(h)}\;\text{d}h\right]\;\ket{\psi(g)}\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}k

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\right]\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(gk)}\braket{\psi(gk)|\psi(g)}\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}k,

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{d_{R}N^{2}}\int_{G}\int_{G}|\!\braket{\psi(e)|\psi(k)}\!|^{2}\;\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\right]\;\text{d}g\;\text{d}k,

where
e\in G
is the identity element. Using cyclicity of trace,
\mathrm{Tr}\left[U_{S}(g)\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(gk)\right]=\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(k)\right]
. Moreover, by tracing (
53
) we get that
\int_{G}\text{d}g=d_{R}N
, leading to

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\int_{G}|\!\braket{\psi(e)|\psi(k)}\!|^{2}\;\mathrm{Tr}\left[\rho_{S}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(k)\right]\;\text{d}k

(58)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{Tr}\left(\rho_{S}\;\int_{G}\text{d}k\;\frac{|\!\braket{\psi(e)|\psi(k)}\!|^{2}}{N}U_{S}(k)E_{S}U_{S}^{\dagger}(k)\right).

This is a trace of
\rho_{S}
multiplying the effect
E_{S}
averaged over all the coherent states of the system with their weights
\frac{|\!\braket{\psi(e)|\psi(k)}\!|^{2}}{N},\;\int_{G}\text{d}k\;\frac{|\!\braket{\psi(e)|\psi(k)}\!|^{2}}{N}=1
being their overlap with the coherent state corresponding to the identity element of the group.

We note that in the case where the reference frame is ideal:
|\!\braket{\psi(g)|\psi(g^{\prime})}\!|^{2}=0
for
g\neq g^{\prime}
and
N=1
, we obtain an exact encoding:
\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{S}E_{S})=\mathrm{Tr}(\rho_{\mathrm{inv}}E_{\mathrm{inv}})
.
