Kirby diagrams for an infinite family of exotic ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} ’s
Siddharth Shrivastava

Abstract
. Eli, Hom, and Lidman showed that the manifolds produced by attaching the simplest positive Casson handle C ​ H + CH^{+} to a slice disc complement of the ribbon knot T 2 , n ​ # ​ T 2 , − n T_{2,n}\#T_{2,-n} for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and odd, and removing the boundary, form a countably infinite family of exotic ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} ’s. They provided a Kirby diagram for the case n = 3 n=3 . In this short note, we extend this for n ≥ 3 n\geq 3 and odd, and provide Kirby diagrams for two such families of exotic ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} ’s, which are then shown to be equivalent. We then generalise these diagrams to a family of exotic ℝ 4 \mathbb{R}^{4} ’s built using ribbon disc complements of the pretzel knots P ⁡ ( n , − n , 2 ​ k ) P(n,-n,2k) .



1.
Introduction

Exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s are smooth manifolds that are homeomorphic but not diffeomorphic to the standard
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. This is a phenomenon exclusive to dimension
4
, because for all other
n\neq 4
, any smooth manifold homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is also diffeomorphic to the standard
\mathbb{R}^{n}

[
12
]
. It is known that uncountably many exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s exist
[
13
]
. One way to construct exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s involves attaching a Casson handle to a ribbon disc complement and removing the boundary, giving what are called
ribbon

\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s (see
[
3
]
and
[
8
, Chapter 14]
).

Eli, Hom, and Lidman
[
4
]
provided a way to distinguish exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s that are in the form of
slice

\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s, which are built by attaching a Casson handle to a
slice
disc complement and removing the boundary, by using Gadgil’s end Floer homology
[
5
]
. This allowed them to produce a countably infinite family of exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s. Specifically, they showed that the manifolds
\mathcal{R}_{n}
, obtained by attaching the simplest positive Casson handle
CH^{+}
to a slice disc complement of the ribbon knot
T_{2,n}\#T_{2,-n}
(for
n\geq 3
and odd) and removing the boundary, are pairwise nondiffeomorphic exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s
[
4
, Corollary 1.2]
. This result does not depend on the choice of slice disc. That is, we can construct each
\mathcal{R}_{n}
by using any slice disc for the knot
T_{2,n}\#T_{2,-n}
, and the resulting family of manifolds
\{\mathcal{R}_{n}\}_{n=3,5,7,...}
will be a family of pairwise nondiffeomorphic exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s. This is due to the fact that the end Floer homology does not depend on the choice of slice disc, but only on the slice knot.

To obtain a more concrete understanding of the above families of exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s, it can be useful to represent them using Kirby diagrams. Eli, Hom, and Lidman provided a Kirby diagram for one such choice of
\mathcal{R}_{3}
, built using a ribbon disc complement for
T_{2,3}\#T_{2,-3}

[
4
, Figure 1]
. In this short note, we first extend this for
n\geq 3
and odd, and provide the Kirby diagrams for two such families of exotic
\mathbb{R}^{4}
’s.
