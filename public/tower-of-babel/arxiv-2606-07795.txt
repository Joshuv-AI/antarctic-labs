Abstract
. We study the problem of incremental view maintenance (IVM) under inserts to K K -databases, where K K is a commutative semiring without additive inverse. The key observation put forward in this paper is that the complexity of the IVM problem depends fundamentally on the underlying semiring. We introduce a class of conjunctive queries called p p -hierarchical and show that for any p p -hierarchical query with fractional hypertree width 𝖿𝗁𝗍𝗐 \mathsf{fhtw} and any insert-only update sequence of length N N to an initially empty K K -database over an arbitrary semiring K K without additive inverse, we can construct a data structure that can be updated in amortized 𝒪 ⁡ ( N 𝖿𝗁𝗍𝗐 − 1 ) \mathcal{O}(N^{\mathsf{fhtw}-1}) time and can support constant delay enumeration of the query result. In particular, the amortized update time for any α \alpha -acyclic p p -hierarchical query is constant. We also give conditional lower bounds showing that any conjunctive query without self-joins that is not p p -hierarchical cannot be maintained with amortized constant update time and constant enumeration delay under inserts to K K -databases. Here, K K can be the natural semiring and its generalizations to the provenance and covariance semirings or any idempotent and strictly ordered semiring such as the tropical semiring. When put together, our upper and lower bounds imply a dichotomy for the insert-only maintenance of conjunctive queries without self-joins and the aforementioned semirings: A query can be maintained with amortized constant update time and constant enumeration delay if and only if it is α \alpha -acyclic p p -hierarchical. Our dichotomy is sandwiched between two known maintenance dichotomies, both for the Boolean semiring. On the one hand, tractable maintenance under inserts-only can only be achieved for the free-connex α \alpha -acyclic queries, which form a strict superset of the α \alpha -acyclic p p -hierarchical queries. On the other hand, tractable maintenance under both inserts and deletes can only be achieved for the q q -hierarchical queries, which form a strict subset of the α \alpha -acyclic p p -hierarchical queries.

Further works on semiring-annotated databases.

The theoretical foundations of
K
-databases
(
11
)
have been extensively explored across various classical database problems. Early works established the decidability and complexity bounds for the containment and equivalence of conjunctive queries under provenance semiring semantics
(
12

(
10

(
22

Yet whereas IVM performs incremental computation under inserts to the input database, semi-naïve evaluation generates inserts to the query result which is used as input at the next iteration of a recursive fixed-point loop. Recent work
(
19
)
introduced an extension of recursive Datalog queries to
K
-databases and studied under which conditions on the semiring
K
the semi-naïve evaluation of recursive queries over
K
-databases terminates. It does not consider the time complexity to process each insert and does not provide a characterization of queries with tractable maintenance under inserts. We note that by unfolding the linear recursion for reachability a fixed number of steps we obtain path queries over binary
K
-relations. These queries are
\alpha
-acyclic, yet they are not p-hierarchical and not even free-connex. Therefore, they are not tractable for the semirings considered in this paper, not even for the Boolean semiring.

Data Updates

We model database
updates
as a sequence of single-tuple inserts. When a tuple
\mathbf{x}
is inserted into a relation
R
having schema
\mathbf{X}
, we represent this operation via a delta relation
\delta R:\mathsf{Dom}(\mathbf{X})\to K
. The state of the relation post-update is simply its union with the delta:
R\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{def}}}{{=}}R\cup\delta R
. To propagate updates through joins, we apply standard delta rules. For a join of two views
V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})
and
V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
, the change is evaluated as
\delta(V_{1}\cdot V_{2})=(\delta V_{1}\cdot V_{2})\cup(V_{1}\cdot\delta V_{2})\cup(\delta V_{1}\cdot\delta V_{2})
. For multi-way joins, this rule is applied recursively. In our insert-only framework, where a single physical relation is modified at any given step (say,
V_{1}
),
\delta V_{2}
is strictly empty. This simplifies the propagation to
\delta(V_{1}\cdot V_{2})=\delta V_{1}\cdot V_{2}
.

Updates are defined for joins of relations using the classical delta rule:
\delta(V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}))=(\delta V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}))\cup(V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot\delta V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}))\cup(\delta V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot\delta V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}))
. This generalizes to a join of arbitrary relations by taking
V_{2}
to be the join of relations and applying recursively the delta rule to
\delta V_{2}
. If only
V_{1}
is changed, then
\delta V_{2}=\emptyset
and
\delta(V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2}))=\delta V_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot V_{2}(\mathbf{Z}_{2})
. Furthermore, delta operations distribute seamlessly over variable marginalization, meaning
\delta(\sum_{\mathbf{Y}}V)=\sum_{\mathbf{Y}}\delta V
. Finally, recall our assumption regarding distinct relation symbols for self-joins; an insert into a physical database relation triggers independent delta updates for each of its logical copies in the query.

3.
P-Hierarchical Queries

We start by introducing the prior, related class of q-hierarchical queries, which can be maintained with
\mathcal{O}(1)
update time and constant delay enumeration under insert-delete sequences over the Boolean semiring.

Definition 1 (q-hierarchical).

(
(
6
, Def. 3.1)
)
A conjunctive query
Q
is
q-hierarchical
if for any two variables
X,Y\in\mathsf{vars}(Q)
the following is satisfied:

(1)

[hierarchical property]
\mathsf{at}(X)\cap\mathsf{at}(Y)=\emptyset
or
\mathsf{at}(X)\subseteq\mathsf{at}(Y)
or
\mathsf{at}(Y)\subseteq\mathsf{at}(X)
, and

(2)

[q property]      if
\mathsf{at}(X)\subsetneq\mathsf{at}(Y)
and
X\in\mathsf{Free}(Q)
, then
Y\in\mathsf{Free}(Q)
.

Our main contribution is the syntactic characterization of all tractable conjunctive queries that can be maintained in amortized constant time and allow constant delay enumeration under updates over a variety of semirings. This is the class of
p-hierarchical
(short for piecewise-hierarchical) queries. In subsequent sections we prove that acyclic p-hierarchical queries are exactly the conjunctive queries that can be maintained under inserts in amortized constant time and allow constant delay enumeration for commonly used semirings such as the natural and the tropical semiring. The key insight behind this class is that under insert-only sequences, the free variables no longer need to satisfy the strict non-overlapping hierarchies required by q-hierarchical queries under insert-delete sequences. Instead, the hierarchical constraints apply exclusively to how bound variables interact with each other and with the free variables.

Definition 2 (p-hierarchical Query).

A conjunctive query
Q
is p-hierarchical if the following is satisfied:

(1)

[bound-bound interaction]
\forall X,Y\in\mathsf{Bound}(Q)
:
\mathsf{at}(X)\subseteq\mathsf{at}(Y)
or
\mathsf{at}(Y)\subseteq\mathsf{at}(X)
or
\mathsf{at}(X)\cap\mathsf{at}(Y)=\emptyset
; and

(2)

[bound-free interaction]
\forall X\in\mathsf{Bound}(Q)
,
Y\in\mathsf{Free}(Q)
:
\mathsf{at}(X)\subseteq\mathsf{at}(Y)
or
\mathsf{at}(X)\cap\mathsf{at}(Y)=\emptyset
.

The class of
\alpha
-acyclic p-hierarchical queries is strictly sandwiched between the classes of q-hierarchical queries
(
6
)
and of free-connex

(

, as it strictly includes the former and is included in the latter.

4.
Maintaining P-Hierarchical Queries

In this section, we present our maintenance approach for p-hierarchical queries. We begin with a high-level intuition, which is to maintain different parts of the query over different semirings.
We then introduce the notion of variable orders
(
16
)
, which are used to decompose the query into q-hierarchical subqueries that can be maintained efficiently over the original semiring, and then derive maintenance plans for these q-hierarchical subqueries. We demonstrate this technique using the query from Ex.
4
as a running example. Then we show how the join of these subqueries can be maintained over the Boolean semiring while still recovering the payloads over the original semiring. Finally, we establish the correctness and complexity of our approach.

4.1.
Intuition behind our Approach

The intuition behind our maintenance approach for p-hierarchical queries relies on the decomposition of p-hierarchical queries into a full conjunctive query over q-hierarchical sub-queries, as established in Prop.
5
. Our algorithm uses two ingredients: an approach to maintain efficiently q-hierarchical queries over any semiring and another approach to maintain full conjunctive queries restricted to the Boolean semiring. It relies on prior work that already introduced the two ingredients. Prior work
(
6
;
14
;
24
;
29
)
introduced approaches that can maintain tractably q-hierarchical queries under both inserts and deletes using a factorized representation of the query result or a conceptually similar hierarchy of materialized views. We use a similar construction for the first ingredient. Further prior work
(
2
)
introduced an approach to maintain full conjunctive queries under inserts to
\mathbb{B}
-databases using again a hierarchy of materialized views. We use this latter approach as our second ingredient.

Our algorithm composes the maintenance strategies for the two ingredients to obtain one maintenance strategy for any p-hierarchical query as one hierarchy of materialized views. Remarkably, this suffices to guarantee the correctness of both the update and the enumeration steps. Inserts are propagated bottom-up and update each view from the leaf representing the updated relation through the hierarchy to the root view. Yet at the border between the two maintenance strategies, the payloads from the original semiring are cast as payloads in the Boolean semiring. This ensures that different payloads for the same tuple become indistinguishable under the Boolean semiring. Only the addition of a new tuple is propagated further beyond this border. This reduces the amount of insert propagations and brings an asymptotic complexity gain when compared to the approach that keeps the same semiring throughout the entire hierarchy of materialized views. This reduction in update propagations comes with a loss: The payload in the original semiring is not propagated beyond the border for existing tuples. This means that at enumeration time, the tuples in the views above the border, whose join make up the tuples in the query result, do not carry the correct payload. To retrieve the correct payload for a tuple in the query result, we need to construct it on the fly using lookups in the views right below the border, so in the root views for each of the q-hierarchical query. Since their number is only linear in the number of q-hierarchical subqueries (and independent of the size of the input data), this takes constant time. Furthermore, the prefix hierarchy made up of the views above the border supports the enumeration of all tuples in the query result with constant delay, following prior work
(
2
)
.

4.5.
Augmenting View Trees

In the previous section, we showed how to decompose a p-hierarchical query
Q
into q-hierarchical subqueries, and how to construct a forest of view trees
\mathcal{T}
such that
Q
is the join of the root views of these view trees. Now consider the query that results from this decomposition,
Q(\mathbf{X})=\prod_{V(\mathbf{Y})\in\mathsf{roots}(\mathcal{T})}V(\mathbf{Y})
. We can augment each view tree by adding an indicator projection
\mathbbm{1}_{V(\mathbf{Y})}(\mathbf{Y})
atop each root view
V(\mathbf{Y})
. Joining these indicator projections yields the join query
Q^{\prime}
that can be maintained over the Boolean semiring using prior work
(
2
)
, as long as we restrict ourselves to the insert-only setting. In particular, all tuples in
Q^{\prime}
have non-zero payload if and only if they have non-zero payload in
Q
. Thus, when we enumerate a tuple
t
from
Q^{\prime}
with a non-zero payload, we can recover its payload in
Q
by simply projecting each
t
onto the schema of each indicator projection, and looking up its payload in the view below it in the forest of augmented view trees. Multiplying these payloads and replacing each payload of a tuple in
Q^{\prime}
with the respective product recovers the correct payloads of the tuples in
Q
, as if the entire query was maintained over the same semiring. We are now ready to present the proof of the main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Consider the forest of view trees
\mathcal{T}

\mathcal{T}

Consider a single-tuple insert to
\mathcal{D}
. Because
\mathcal{T}
has disjoint atoms (Lemma

Now we will show how to use the result of

Thus, when we begin with an initially empty database

and receive a stream of

(which takes constant time) and we update the query

Now we will show that

. We will show

is a valid tree decomposition for

Now we will show that for every variable

and

\displaystyle\mathsf{fhtw}(Q^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\min_{(T_{1},\chi)\in\mathsf{TD}(Q(\mathbf{F}))}0pt(T_{1},\chi)

5.
Lower Bounds for Non-P-Hierarchical Queries

In this section, we prove Theorem
2
that establishes lower bounds for the maintenance of queries without self-joins under inserts over different semirings. In particular, we show that non-
p
-hierarchical queries over
K
-databases, where
K
is the natural or tropical semiring, cannot be maintained with constant update time and constant enumeration delay under widely held conjectures. We then sketch the extension of the proof for further semirings in
\mathbb{K}
.

Our lower bounds rely on the hardness of the Online Matrix-Vector Multiplication (OMv) problem. We start by introducing the OMv problem.

Definition 1 (Online Matrix-Vector Multiplication (OMv)
(
13
)
).

We are given an
n\times n
Boolean matrix
\mathbf{M}
and receive
n
Boolean column vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
of size
n
, one by one; after seeing each vector
\mathbf{v}_{i}
, we output the product
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{i}
before we see the next vector.

It is strongly believed that the OMv problem cannot be solved in subcubic time.

Conjecture 2 (OMv conjecture, Theorem 2.2
(
13
)
).

For any
\gamma>0
, there is no algorithm that solves this problem in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3-\gamma})

Although, we base our results on the hardness of the OMv conjecture, we introduce the following additional conjecture which is a consequence of the OMv conjecture
(
13
)
since it is a more suitable starting point for some of our reductions.

Definition 3 (Online Vector-Matrix-Vector Multiplication (OuMv)
(
13
)
).

We are given an
n\times n
Boolean matrix
\mathbf{M}
and receive
n
pairs of Boolean column vectors
(\mathbf{u}_{1},\mathbf{v}_{1}),\dots,(\mathbf{u}_{n},\mathbf{v}_{n})
of size
n
, one by one; after seeing each pair of vectors
(\mathbf{u}_{i},\mathbf{v}_{i})
, we output the product
\mathbf{u}_{i}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{i}
before we see the next pair.

Conjecture 4 (OuMv conjecture, Theorem 2.4
(
13
)
).

For any
\gamma>0
, there is no algorithm that solves this problem in time
\mathcal{O}(n^{3-\gamma})

5.1.
Intuition behind our Lower Bound Proofs

The OMv and OuMv conjectures are widely used to prove conditional lower bounds
(
6
;
15
;
13
;
17
)
. In all subsequent lower bound proofs, we reduce the OMv or OuMv problem to the IVM problem of a query
Q
over a
K
-database of size
N=\Theta(n^{2})
. That is, if there were an algorithm maintaining a non-p-hierarchical query with
\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma})
enumeration delay and
\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma})
amortized update time, for any
\gamma>0
, one could use this algorithm to solve the OMv problem in subcubic time.

Preprocessing time.

To expose the matrix
\mathbf{M}
and the sequence of vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
from the OMv problem to our IVM oracle for
Q
, we need to (1) populate an
\mathbb{N}
-database on which
Q
is maintained and (2) extract from the result of
Q
the sequence of result vectors
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{n}
. The first task requires inserts to the database, while the second task requires enumeration of the query result. As we show in the following proofs, the cost of these two tasks, if they were to use
O(n^{1-2\gamma})
amortized update time and enumeration delay, for some
\gamma>0
, already contradict the OMv conjecture as they would yield an
\mathcal{O}(n^{3-2\gamma})
algorithm for the OMv problem. Therefore, an
\mathcal{O}(n^{3-2\gamma})=\mathcal{O}(N^{3/2-\gamma})
preprocessing time still contradicts the OMv conjecture. For our next reductions, however, we have no preprocessing time as we start with the empty database.

Simulating Deletes using Inserts.

Prior lower bound proofs based on reductions from OMv/OuMv were for the insert-delete setting, e.g.,
(
6
;
15
;
17
)
, whereas our proofs are for the more restricted insert-only setting. For the insert-delete setting, the OMv problem is encoded dynamically into the database: at each step, as a new vector
\mathbf{v}_{k}
arrives, the reduction deletes the contents of the relation representing the previous vector and inserts the new one. Because our framework strictly prohibits deletions, we must encode the OMv steps differently. We bypass the need for deletions by continually inserting the exact same tuples into the database, but with carefully engineered payloads. However, because older tuples are not deleted, the relation’s overall state becomes corrupted by prior insertions. To extract the correct answer for the current step, we must logically filter out this prior data using different algebraic mechanisms depending on the underlying semiring.

The Natural Semiring: Accumulation and Differencing.

In the natural semiring, payloads accumulate additively. At each step
k
, we insert tuples corresponding to the indices
i
such that
\mathbf{v}_{k}[i]=1
with a payload of
1
. Because previous insertions cannot be deleted, the target relation represents the cumulative sum of all vectors seen so far,
\mathbf{t}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\mathbf{v}_{i}
. Consequently, enumerating the query computes the matrix-vector product over this cumulative state, yielding
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{t}_{k}
. We bypass the lack of deletions by taking the algebraic difference between successive steps: subtracting the evaluated vector of step
k-1
from step
k
perfectly isolates the result for the current step,
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{k}
.

The Tropical Semiring: Dominance via Minimization.

In the tropical semiring, where the operators are minimum and addition, we cannot use subtraction to isolate the current step. Instead, we simulate state replacement by encoding “freshness” directly into the payloads. At each step
k
, we insert the indices
i
such that
\mathbf{v}_{k}[i]=1
with a strictly decreasing, negative payload
\Delta_{k}
(where
\Delta_{k}<\Delta_{k-1}
). Because the semiring’s summation acts as a minimum operator, the lowest-cost path through the joins will always be dominated by the most recently inserted tuples. Stale tuples from earlier steps retain larger payloads and are naturally filtered out by the minimization. By simply checking if the query’s output exactly matches the expected combined payload of fresh tuples (for instance,
2\Delta_{k}
), we can determine if a valid OMv intersection exists at the current step, rendering the older data effectively invisible.

5.2.
The Natural Semiring

We first show the lower bound for the natural semiring
(\mathbb{N},+,\times)
.

Let
Q
be any conjunctive query without self-joins that is not p-hierarchical. We reduce the OMv problem to the incremental maintenance of
Q
. The query
Q
must violate one of the two conditions from Def.
2
. We next analyze each of these two cases in isolation.

Case 1

Assume

violates the bound-bound interaction, that is,

is of the form
Q(\mathbf{F})=\sum_{\mathbf{B}}R(X,\mathbf{Z}_{R})\cdot S(X,Y,\mathbf{Z}_{s})\cdot T(Y,\mathbf{Z}_{T})\cdot U_{1}(\mathbf{Z}_{1})\cdot\ldots\cdot U_{m}(\mathbf{Z}_{m})

,

, and

Database construction.

We simplify the query

by fixing the variables in

that is not in the domains of

and

to also represent a tuple consisting entirely of this constant, with arity determined by a relation’s schema. To neutralize the effect of the relations

on the joins on

as follows:

•

If

.

•

If

or

for all

, where

is the value for

or

.

•

If

, we insert

for all

, where

are the values for

Note that
1
is the multiplicative identity of the natural semiring.
By construction,

and

are equivalent for the above-constructed database with arbitrary

-relations

,

, and

.

Let
\mathbf{M}
be the
n\times n
Boolean matrix in the OMv problem. We initialize the database by encoding
\mathbf{M}
into the
\mathbb{N}
-relation

: for every
\mathbf{M}_{ij}=1
, we insert

.
The OMv problem also gives a sequence of vectors
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}
. At step

, we must compute the vector
\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{k}
. Upon receiving
\mathbf{v}_{k}
, we make the following inserts into relation

. For every index

such that

. For all other indices

A key constraint of our reduction is that we cannot delete from the database relations. Yet we can achieve the effect of deletes followed by inserts by inspecting the difference in the query answer before and after inserts. This is explained next.
Since we cannot delete the previous entries in
T
and because the natural semiring accumulates payloads, the vector represented by

at step

is the cumulative vector
\mathbf{t}_{k}=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\mathbf{v}_{m}
. This sum is over the natural semiring, not the Boolean semiring. Even though,

are Boolean vectors, we treat them as integer vectors and recover the Boolean result out of the query answer. The database implicitly holds the state
\mathbf{u}_{k}=\mathbf{M}\mathbf{t}_{k}
.

Recovering the result for the OMv problem from the query result.

To extract the
n
-dimensional vector
\mathbf{u}_{k}
from the scalar result of

, we execute a sequence of
2n
query enumerations and inserts. For each index

:

(1)

We enumerate the current scalar output

.

(2)

We insert
R(i)\mapsto 1
.

(3)

We enumerate the new scalar output

.

Let
R^{\prime}(i)
be the value of

before we inserted
R(i)\mapsto 1
. Inserting

increases the scalar query answer by exactly the value given by the dot product between the

-th row of

and the vector

:

\displaystyle Q_{\text{after}}^{(i)}-Q_{\text{before}}^{(i)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{x\in[n]\setminus\left\{i\right\},y}R(x)\cdot S(x,y)\cdot T(y)+\sum_{y}R(i)\cdot S(i,y)\cdot T(y)\right)

\displaystyle-\left(\sum_{x\in[n]\setminus\left\{i\right\},y}R(x)\cdot S(x,y)\cdot T(y)+\sum_{y}R^{\prime}(i)\cdot S(i,y)\cdot T(y)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y}\underbrace{(R(i)-R^{\prime}(i))}_{1}\cdot S(i,y)\cdot T(y)=\sum_{y}S(i,y)\cdot T(y)=\mathbf{M}_{i}\mathbf{t}_{k}=\mathbf{u}_{k}[i]

.

After

from the previous step

To recover the Boolean vector

.

Time analysis.

There is no preprocessing as we start with the empty database.
At each step

, as we keep the distinct tuples in each relation; on inserting a tuple

In each of the

Assume there exists an algorithm that maintains

\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{3}{2}-\gamma})=\mathcal{O}(n^{3-2\gamma})

Time analysis.

Assume there exists an algorithm that maintains

and
\mathcal{O}(N^{\frac{1}{2}-\gamma})=\mathcal{O}(n^{1-2\gamma})

\mathcal{O}(N)
inserts, for some

5.3.
The Tropical Semiring

We show the lower bound for the min tropical semiring
\mathbb{T}=(\mathbb{R}\cup\{+\infty\},\min,+)

Let

be any conjunctive query without self-joins that is not p-hierarchical.
There are two ways in which

Case 1

We assume that

Database construction.

Similar to the previous reductions, we simplify the query

For conciseness, we overload

•

If

•

If

Note that

By construction,

Let
\mathbf{M}
be the

be the sequence of vector pairs at step

\mathbb{T}

•

If

.

•

If
\mathbf{M}_{jk}=0

.

Additionally, we initialize

and

with payload

At each step

Recovering the result for the OMv problem from the query result.

Let

•

Case 1:
\mathbf{u}_{i}^{\top}\mathbf{M}\mathbf{v}_{i}=1

Thus, there exist indices

For any pair

Time analysis.

The database size is

n

Generalization to Idempotent Strictly Ordered Semirings.

We can generalize our previous lower bound beyond the specific tropical semiring, by relying on the algebraic properties of idempotency and strictly ordered semirings. To simulate an insert-only sequence, we must be able to inject new payloads that “dominate” old payloads. That is, the sum (in the semiring) of the new payload with the existing payload yields the new payload, so we can effectively forget the existing payload. This is the same effect as deleting a tuple
t
with the existing payload and inserting
t
with the new payload. This can be achieved if the semiring is idempotent. Next, since we may want to use new payloads for each insert, we require that the domain of the semiring contains an infinite strictly increasing chain of elements. Finally, we need to be able to observe if a multiplication contains exclusively new payloads, so the multiplication needs to be strictly monotonic over this chain.

As established
(
11
;
9
)
, any idempotent semiring
(K,+,\cdot)
induces a
natural order

\preceq_{K}
defined by:

a\preceq_{K}b\iff a+b=b
. For the tropical semiring, the natural order
a\preceq_{K}b
is the reverse order
a\geq b
on
\mathbb{Z}\cup\left\{\infty\right\}
, since
a\min b=b
whenever
a\geq b
. For the Boolean semiring, the natural order on the domain
\{\textit{false},\textit{true}\}
is
\textit{false}\preceq_{K}\textit{true}
, since
\textit{false}\vee\textit{true}=\textit{true}
.
The
+
semiring operator computes the least upper bound of its operands, effectively allowing the query evaluation to ignore old payloads and only retain the dominant new payloads. To formalize the infinite chain of strictly increasing elements that is preserved by multiplication, we define a specific class of semirings.

Definition 6 (Idempotent Strictly Ordered (ISO) Semirings).

A semiring
(K,+,\cdot)
is
idempotent strictly ordered
if it is idempotent with a natural order
\preceq_{K}
(
a\preceq_{K}b\iff a+b=b
) and it satisfies conditions:

(1)

There exists a countably infinite sequence of elements
(c_{i})_{i}\in\mathbb{N}
in
K
such that for all
i\geq 1
,

c_{i}\preceq_{K}c_{i+1}\text{ and }c_{i}\neq c_{i+1.}

(2)

The multiplication operator (
\cdot
) is strictly monotonic over this chain. For any elements in the chain where
a\prec_{K}b
, it holds that
a\cdot c\prec_{K}b\cdot c
for any
c\neq 0_{K}
, where
0_{K}
denotes the addition identity of the semiring.

The combination of these properties guarantees that the multiplication of exclusively fresh payloads (
c_{i}\cdot c_{i}
) strictly dominates multiplications, where one of the payloads precedes the fresh ones (e.g.,
c_{i-1}\cdot c_{i}
). The tropical semiring
(\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\},\min,+)
belongs to this class by utilizing the chain
c_{i}=-i\cdot C
(for
C\geq 2
). In this semiring, decreasing payloads map to strictly greater elements in the min-induced natural partial order, and scalar addition strictly preserves these differences.

We are now ready to prove the lower bound for idempotent strictly ordered semirings. The lower bounds hold for any idempotent strictly ordered semiring by applying the exact reduction presented for the tropical semiring, under the following homomorphic mapping of the payload assignments and operators:

•

Identities:
The absorbing payload
\infty
maps to the addition identity
0_{K}
(the minimum element under
\preceq_{K}
). The neutral payload
0
maps to the multiplicative identity
1_{K}
.

•

Payloads:
The decreasing integers
\Delta_{i}
map directly to the strictly increasing chain elements
c_{i}
.

•

Operators:
The (
\min
) aggregation maps to the semiring addition (
+
), which computes the supremum. The scalar addition evaluation maps to the semiring multiplication (
\cdot
).

Under this mapping, the fresh product evaluating to
\Delta_{i}+\Delta_{i}=2\Delta_{i}
in the tropical reduction maps to
c_{i}\cdot c_{i}
in the idempotent strictly ordered semiring. A stale product evaluates to an element bounded by
c_{i-1}\cdot c_{i}
. By Def.
6
, strict monotonicity guarantees
c_{i-1}\cdot c_{i}\prec_{K}c_{i}\cdot c_{i}
. Consequently, the semiring addition operator (
+
) filters out all stale products by acting as a supremum, yielding exactly
c_{i}\cdot c_{i}
if and only if the corresponding Boolean matrix-vector product is
1
. The contradiction of the complexity bounds remains identical.

6.
Conclusion

In this paper, we study the role of semirings on the incremental view maintenance problem for conjunctive queries. We show that a query can be maintained under single-tuple inserts with amortized constant update time and constant enumeration delay over
K
-databases if and only if the query is
\alpha
-acyclic p-hierarchical. Here,
K
is the natural or tropical semiring, or generalizations of them. The "only if" result is conditional on the OMv conjecture. We also give a maintenance procedure for arbitrary (so not only
\alpha
-acyclic) p-hierarchical queries and show that the amortized time is
\mathcal{O}(N^{\mathsf{fhtw}-1})
, where
N
is the length of the sequence of inserts and
\mathsf{fhtw}
is the fractional hypertree width of the Boolean version of the query.

There are several immediate lines of future work worth exploring:

•

Our understanding of optimality for IVM remains severely limited, as it has to be built on lower bounds. Recent work
(
30
)
introduced new conditional lower bounds for IVM beyond those conditioned on the OMv problem. A natural further step is to define classes of queries whose maintenance complexity matches the new lower bounds for various semirings.

•

One challenge brought by our maintenance algorithm for p-hierarchical queries concerns the enumeration of the query result: Whereas the entire query result can be enumerated with constant delay after each insert, it remains open whether such a constant-delay enumeration is possible for the delta of the query result after each insert.

•

The seminal result on q-hierarchical queries
(
6
)
supports both inserts and deletes to the standard database under set semantics, but does not consider the generalization to
K
-databases. A question is whether such a generalization is immediate. Prior work
(
24
;
16
)
showed this to be the case for the
\mathbb{Z}
-ring and the covariance ring, but the case of arbitrary rings (semirings with additive inverse) remains open.

for the bag
\chi_{i}(t)
that does not use edges
e\notin\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})
.

By the definition of a p-hierarchical decomposition, any external atom
e\notin\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})
contains no bound variables of
Q_{i}
. Therefore, its contribution to the bag
\chi_{i}(t)
is restricted entirely to the free variables
e\cap\mathsf{vars}(Q_{i})\subseteq\mathsf{Free}(Q_{i})
. Because
Q_{i}
is a free-dominated subquery, every atom in
Q_{i}
contains all of
\mathsf{Free}(Q_{i})
. So, we can move that weight to any other edge that contains all the free variables. Pick an arbitrary edge
e^{*}\in\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})
such that
e^{*}
covers
\mathbf{F}_{i}
. We define
\rho^{\prime}
exclusively over
\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})
:

\rho^{\prime}(e)=\begin{cases}\rho(e)+\sum_{e^{\prime}\notin\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})}\rho(e^{\prime})&\text{if }e=e^{*}\\
\rho(e)&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

For any bound variable
X\in\mathsf{Bound}(Q_{i})
, its coverage is preserved because it was only ever covered by atoms in
\mathsf{at}(Q_{i})
. For any free variable
Y\in\mathsf{Free}(Q_{i})
, the exact weight lost by discarding external atoms is transferred to
e^{*}
, which contains
Y
. Thus,
\rho^{\prime}
is a valid edge cover for
\chi_{i}^{\prime}(t)
with
\sum\rho^{\prime}_{i}(e)\leq w
, so we conclude that
\mathsf{fhtw}(Q_{i})\leq\mathsf{fhtw}(Q)
.

Furthermore, the TD
(T_{i},\chi_{i})
must contain a bag with
\chi_{i}(t)\supseteq\mathbf{F}_{i}
. Using this node, we can attach the subtree
T_{i}
to
T^{\prime}
by adding an edge between this node
t\in\mathsf{nodes}(T_{i})
to a node
t^{\prime}\in\mathsf{nodes}(T^{\prime})
with
\chi^{\prime}(t^{\prime})\supseteq F_{i}
while preserving the connectedness property of TDs for the free variables. The connectedness for the bound variables is guaranteed by each individual TD
T_{i}
. We do the same for all TDs to obtain a new TD
(T^{*},\chi^{*})
for
Q
.

The subtree
T^{\prime}
ensures the free-connex property and since every atom of
Q
belongs to some subquery
Q_{i}
, it must be covered by some bag in
T_{i}
, so it is also covered by some bag in
T^{*}
. Finally, since every bag in
T^{\prime}
and
T_{i}
has a fractional edge cover of at most
w
, the width of
(T^{*},\chi^{*})
is at most
w
. Therefore,
(T^{*},\chi^{*})
is a free-connex TD for
Q
with
0pt(T^{*},\chi^{*})=w
.
∎
