Abstract
. We study some interesting hypersurfaces that naturally arise when studying the period map on the moduli space of hypersurfaces, in the context of Sung Gi Park’s recent work on studying the GIT moduli space of hypersurfaces via the minimal exponent. We compute the Hodge structure on the singular cohomology and the intersection cohomology of these hypersurfaces, and also show the K K -polystability of certain mildly singular degenerate hypersurfaces among them. In particular, the following hypersurface is K K -polystable for l ≥ 2 l\geq 2 : { x 11 ⋯ x 1 ​ d + … + x l ​ d ⋯ x l ​ d = 0 } ⊂ ℙ l ​ d − 1 . \{x_{11}\cdots x_{1d}+\ldots+x_{ld}\cdots x_{ld}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}.

The following hypersurfaces are important examples arising in this picture:

The most degenerate case is given by the equation

(1)

X_{l,d}=\{x_{11}\cdots x_{1d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}.

Note that the Hodge structure of the middle cohomology of a smooth hypersurface

h^{l-1,ld-l-1}(Y)=h^{ld-l-1,l-1}(Y)=1,\quad\text{and}\quad h^{i,ld-2-i}(Y)=h^{ld-2-i,i}(Y)=0\text{ for }0\leq i<l-1.

The Hodge-theoretic behavior of this degeneration is exactly the same as the maximally unipotent degeneration of Calabi–Yau hypersurfaces to the toric boundary, for example:

\mathcal{X}=\left\{([x_{1}:\ldots:x_{d}],t):x_{1}\cdots x_{d}+t(x_{1}^{d}+\ldots+x_{d}^{d})=0\right\}\subset\mathbb{P}_{\mathbb{D}}^{d-1}.

We introduce a notation. For a hypersurface
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N+1}
, we will consider the singular cohomology modulo the hyperplane classes:

\overline{H}^{i}(X,\mathbf{Q})\coloneqq\begin{cases}H^{i}(X,\mathbf{Q})/\langle h^{i/2}\rangle&i\text{ is even}\\
H^{i}(X,\mathbf{Q})&i\text{ is odd}.\end{cases}

Here,
h\in H^{2}(X,\mathbf{Q})
is the restriction of the hyperplane class from the projective space.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+1}
be a hypersurface given by the homogeneous polynomial
f(x)
of degree
d
. Consider the hypersurface

Y=\{f(x)+y_{11}\cdots y_{1d}+\ldots+y_{l1}\cdots y_{ld}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+1+ld}.

The singular cohomology of
X
and
Y
is related in the following manner:

\overline{H}^{m+ld+i}(Y)\simeq\bigoplus_{0\leq i_{0}}\overline{H}^{m+i_{0}}(X)(-l-i+i_{0})^{\oplus a_{i-i_{0}}}.

In particular, if
X
is smooth, we have

\overline{H}^{m+ld+i}(Y)\simeq H^{m}_{\prim}(X)(-l-i)^{\oplus a_{i}}.

Here, the multiplicities

are defined as

a_{i}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\ldots+i_{l}=i\\
0\leq i_{j}\leq d-2\end{subarray}}{d-1\choose i_{1}}\cdots{d-1\choose i_{l}}.

These numbers are non-zero for
0\leq i\leq l(d-2)
.

Remark 1.2
.

We also remark that the short exact sequence of mixed Hodge structures

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Hodge modules

We use the language of mixed Hodge modules
[
30
,
31
]
in a very mild manner. Since we do not need the full theory of mixed Hodge modules, throughout the paper, one could understand them as certain constructible complexes with additional data that allow us to keep track of the Hodge structures and weights. A mixed Hodge module has an underlying
\mathbf{Q}
-complex which is a perverse sheaf. There is a six functor formalism and various functors defined in the category of mixed Hodge modules which are compatible with the corresponding functors defined in the level of constructible complexes.

Over a point, the category of mixed Hodge modules is the abelian category of mixed Hodge structures. Therefore, whenever a natural operation on the level of constructible complexes spits out a vector space, the corresponding operation in the level of mixed Hodge modules outputs a Hodge structure.

Saito’s theory provides objects
\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[\dim X]
and
\IC_{X}^{H}
on
X
whose underlying constructible sheaves are the constant sheaf
\mathbf{Q}_{X}[\dim X]
and the intersection complex
\IC_{X}
, respectively. We remark that in general, the object
\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[\dim X]
lives in the derived category of mixed Hodge modules. However, throughout this paper, this will always be a genuine mixed Hodge module since
X
has hypersurface singularities. If we push forward these objects to a point and take cohomology, we obtain the Hodge structures of the singular cohomology and the intersection cohomology of
X
. By
[
31
]
, the highest graded piece of the weight filtration is
\gr_{\dim X}^{W}\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[\dim X]\simeq\IC_{X}^{H}
(again, we assume that
X
has hypersurface singularities). In particular, we have the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}_{X}\to\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[\dim X]\to\IC_{X}^{H}\to 0.

We call
\mathcal{K}_{X}
the RHM defect object of
X
following the terminology in
[
27
]
.

2.2.
Nearby and vanishing cycles

Consider a polynomial function
f\colon\mathbf{C}^{m+1}\to\mathbf{C}
with central fibre
X=f^{-1}(0)_{\mathrm{red}}
. Then the nearby and vanishing cycles provide functors between the category of mixed Hodge modules

\psi_{f}\colon\MHM(\mathbf{C}^{m+1})\to\MHM(X),\qquad\varphi_{f}\colon\MHM(\mathbf{C}^{m+1})\to\MHM(X).

This is compatible with the corresponding (shifted) functors constructed in the level of constructible complexes. Throughout this paper, we will only need the nearby and vanishing cycles of the constant mixed Hodge module
\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{C}^{m+1}}^{H}[m+1]
. Therefore, we will use a slight abuse of notation:

\psi_{f}\coloneqq\psi_{f}\mathbf{Q}_{\mathbf{C}^{m+1}}^{H}[m+1]

and analogously for
\varphi_{f}
. We denote by
\psi_{f,1}
and
\varphi_{f,1}
for the unipotent parts of
\psi_{f}
and
\varphi_{f}
, respectively.

There are canonical morphisms of mixed Hodge modules

\mathrm{can}\colon\psi_{f,1}\to\varphi_{f,1},\qquad\mathrm{Var}\colon\varphi_{f,1}\to\psi_{f,1}(-1).

The compositions
\mathrm{can}(-1)\circ\mathrm{Var}\colon\varphi_{f,1}\to\varphi_{f,1}(-1)
and
\mathrm{Var}\circ\mathrm{can}\colon\psi_{f,1}\to\psi_{f,1}(-1)
are the nilpotent operator
N
induced by the logarithm of the monodromy. The monodromy weight filtration of
N
agrees with the weight filtration on
\psi_{f,1}
and
\varphi_{f,1}
with central weight
m
and
m+1
, respectively.

The morphism
\mathrm{can}
is surjective, and
\mathrm{Var}
is injective. We have a short exact sequence of mixed Hodge modules:

0\to\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[m]\to\psi_{f,1}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\varphi_{f,1}\to 0.

We show a small lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

As in the set-up above, we have an isomorphism

\mathcal{K}_{X}(-1)\simeq\ker(N\colon\varphi_{f,1}\to\varphi_{f,1}(-1)).

Proof.

Temporarily, we will use the notation
N_{\psi}
and
N_{\varphi}
for the nilpotent operator acting on
\psi
and
\varphi
respectively, in order to avoid confusion. By the surjectivity of
\mathrm{can}
and the injectivity of
\mathrm{Var}
, the trivial Hodge module
\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[m]
is identified with
\ker N_{\psi}
. Since
\IC_{X}^{H}=\gr_{ld-1}^{W}\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[ld-1]
, we see that

\mathcal{K}_{X}\simeq\ker N_{\psi}\cap\im N_{\psi}(1).

We have a short exact sequence

0\to\ker N_{\psi}\hookrightarrow\ker N_{\psi}^{2}\xrightarrow{N_{\psi}}\ker N_{\psi}(-1)\cap\im N_{\psi}\to 0.

Therefore, it is enough to show that
\mathrm{can}(\ker N_{\psi}^{2})=\ker N_{\varphi}
. This follows from the description

N_{\psi}^{2}\colon\psi_{f,1}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{can}}\varphi_{f,1}\xrightarrow{N_{\varphi}}\varphi_{f,1}(-1)\xrightarrow{\mathrm{Var}(-1)}\psi_{f,1}(-2),

the surjectivity of
\mathrm{can}
and the injectivity of
\mathrm{Var}
.
∎

Proposition 2.2

.

We note that the statement in
[

2.3.
Affine Milnor fibration

We recall a preliminary fact on the vanishing cycles associated to homogeneous polynomials. Let
f\colon\mathbf{C}^{m}\to\mathbf{C}
be a homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
. Then
M_{f}=f^{-1}(1)
is called the
affine Milnor fiber
. This is homotopically equivalent to the Milnor fiber at zero. The group of
d
-th roots of unity
\mu_{d}
acts on
M_{f}
by multiplication and this induces the monodromy transformation

T\colon M_{f}\to M_{f},\qquad(z_{1},\ldots,z_{m})\mapsto(e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}z_{1},\ldots,e^{\frac{2\pi i}{d}}z_{m}).

We have the following relationship between the stalks of
\varphi_{f}
at zero and the cohomology of the affine Milnor fiber
M_{f}
.

.

In the set-up described above, we have an isomorphism between the stalks of the vanishing cycles and the reduced cohomology of the affine Milnor fiber, compatible with the action of the monodromy:

Example 2.5

(Simple normal crossings)
.

Let

2.4.
Singular cohomology of a hypersurface

We compile a series of well-known facts.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+1}
be a hypersurface of degree
d
given by the homogeneous equation
f
. We identify
f
with the polynomial function
f\colon\mathbf{C}^{m+2}\to\mathbf{C}
. Let

and
M_{f}=\{f=1\}\subset\mathbf{C}^{m+2}
. Then we have a sequence of isomorphisms

\displaystyle\overline{H}^{m+i}(X,\mathbf{Q})\simeq\widetilde{H}^{m+1-i}(U,\mathbf{Q})^{\vee}(-m-1)\simeq\left(\widetilde{H}^{m+1-i}(M_{f},\mathbf{Q})^{\mu_{d}}\right)^{\vee}(-m-1)\simeq\left((\mathcal{H}^{-i}\iota_{0}^{*}\varphi_{f})^{T}\right)^{\vee}(-m-1).

Proof.

The first isomorphism follows from the long exact sequence of cohomology associated to the triple
(X,\mathbb{P}^{m+1},U)
. The second isomorphism follows since
M_{f}\to U
is a
\mu_{d}
-étale quotient where the action is given by multiplication by
d
-th roots of unity. The last isomorphism follows from Proposition
2.4
.
∎

2.5.
Valuative criterion for
K
-stability

We briefly discuss the valuative criterion for detecting
K
-(semi)stability, developed by Fujita–Li
[
13
,
20
]
and the equivariant version by
[
37
]
detecting
K
-polystability. We introduce relevant invariants and refer the details to
[
5
]
. Let
X
be a Fano variety and
v
be a divisorial valuation on
X
. For
v=r\ord_{E}
for a divisor on
X
given by
E\subset Y\xrightarrow{\mu}X
, we define the volume as follows:

\vol(-K_{X};v\geq\alpha)=\vol(-\mu^{*}K_{X}-r\alpha E)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{(\dim X)!}{N^{\dim X}}\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{F}_{v}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(X,-NK_{X}),

where the real-indexed filtration
\mathcal{F}
on
H^{0}(X,-NK_{X})
is defined as

\mathcal{F}_{v}^{t}H^{0}(X,-NK_{X})=\{s\in H^{0}(X,-NK_{X}):v(s)\geq t\}.

The
\delta
-invariant is given by

\delta(X)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X}(v)}{S_{X}(v)},

where the infimum runs over all divisorial valuations on
X
. The quantity
A_{X}(v)
is the log discrepancy, and
S_{X}(v)
is defined by

S_{X}(v)=\frac{1}{(-K_{X})^{n}}\int_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\vol(X,-K_{X};v\geq\alpha)d\alpha.

The valuative criterion for
K
-stability says the following:

Theorem 2.7

(
[
13
,
20
,
5
,
22
]
)
.

A Fano variety
X
is
K
-stable (resp.
K
-semi-stable) if and only if
\delta(X)>1
(resp.
\geq 1
).

For the equivariant version of the valuative criterion, suppose we have a reductive algebraic group
G\subset\mathrm{Aut}(X)
acting on a Fano variety
X
. Then we define

\delta_{G}(X)=\inf_{v}\frac{A_{X}(v)}{S_{X}(v)}

where the infimum runs over
G
-invariant
divisorial valuations over
X
. Then we have

Theorem 2.8

(
[
37
]
*Corollary 4.14)
.

For
X
and
G
as above, if
\delta_{G}(X)>1
, then
X
is
K
-polystable.

4.1.
Vanishing cycles
\varphi_{F}

We first give a description for the graded pieces of
\varphi_{g}

g=z_{1}\cdots z_{d}\colon\mathbf{C}^{d}\to\mathbf{C}

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
g\colon\mathbf{C}^{d}\to\mathbf{C}
be as above. We have an isomorphism

\bigoplus_{w=2}^{2(d-1)}\gr_{w}^{W}\varphi_{f}\simeq\bigoplus_{t=2}^{d}\bigoplus_{|J|=t}\bigoplus_{m=0}^{t}\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-m-1)

so that nilpotent operator
N\colon\gr_{w}^{W}\varphi_{f}\to\gr_{w-2}^{W}\varphi_{f}(-1)
is given by the identity

\id:\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-m-1)\to\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-m)(-1)

for

on each summand and zero otherwise. Here,
L_{J}
is the codimension
|J|
linear subspace given by the equation
\{z_{j}=0:j\in J\}
.

Proof.

Follows from
[
29
]
*§11, which verifies this in the level of mixed
\mathbf{Q}
-Hodge complexes.
∎

Now, we carry out the calculation of the weight graded pieces of
\varphi_{F}
. Before that, we fix some notation.

Definition 4.2
.

For
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{l})
with integers
2\leq t_{i}\leq d
, we define

V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\begin{subarray}{c}m_{1}+\ldots+m_{l}=m\\
0\leq m_{i}\leq t_{i}-2\end{subarray}}\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{1})}\otimes\ldots\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{l})}.

Here
\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{i})}
are just one-dimensional vector spaces and the superscripts are there just for the purpose of bookkeeping the indices. We define the primitive part as

V_{\mathbf{t},\prim}^{(m)}\coloneqq\ker V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}\to V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m-1)},

where the map is given by

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq i\leq l:m_{i}\geq 1\end{subarray}}\sum_{m_{1},\ldots,m_{l}}\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{1})}\otimes\ldots\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{l})}\xrightarrow{1}\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{1})}\otimes\ldots\otimes\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{i}-1)}\otimes\ldots\mathbf{Q}^{(m_{l})}.

We give a small remark which follows from standard
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}
-representation theory.

Lemma 4.3
.

V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}\to V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m-1)}
is surjective if and only if
m\leq\left\lceil\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}\right\rceil-l
and is injective if and only if
m\geq\left\lfloor\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}\right\rfloor-l+1
. In particular,
V_{\mathbf{t},\prim}^{(m)}
is non-zero for
0\leq m\leq\left\lfloor\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}\right\rfloor-l
where
|\mathbf{t}|=t_{1}+\ldots+t_{l}
.

We record the dimensions of these vector spaces.

Lemma 4.4
.

\sum_{m}\dim V_{\mathbf{t},\prim}^{(m)}q^{m}=\tau_{<\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}-l+1}\left((1-q)\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{q^{t_{i}-1}-1}{q-1}\right)\in\mathbf{Z}[q].

The truncation
\tau_{<\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}-l+1}
of a polynomial is defined as

\tau_{<\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}-l+1}\left(\sum_{m\geq 0}\alpha_{m}q^{m}\right)=\sum_{m<\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}-l+1}\alpha_{m}q^{m}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\sum_{m}\dim V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}q^{m}=\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{q^{t_{i}-1}-1}{q-1}\in\mathbf{Z}[q].

The assertion then follows from Lemma
4.3
.
∎

We give the description for the weight graded pieces of
\varphi_{F}
.

Lemma 4.5
.

We have

\bigoplus_{w=2l}^{2l(d-1)}\gr_{w}^{W}\varphi_{F}\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathbf{t}}\bigoplus_{|J_{i}|=t_{i}}\bigoplus_{0\leq m_{i}\leq t_{i}-2}\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-|m|-l)\simeq\bigoplus_{\mathbf{t}}\bigoplus_{|J_{i}|=t_{i}}\bigoplus_{m=0}^{|\mathbf{t}|-2l}\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-m-l)\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}.

Here,
\mathbf{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{l})
runs over integers
2\leq t_{i}\leq d
and
J=(J_{1},\ldots,J_{l})
runs over all subsets
J_{i}\subset\{1,\ldots,d\}
with size
t_{i}
. The linear subspace
L_{J}
is given by
\{z_{ij}=0:\text{for all }j\in J_{i},1\leq i\leq l\}
. The nilpotent operator
N\colon\gr_{w}^{W}\varphi_{F}\to\gr_{w-2}^{W}\varphi_{F}(-1)
is given by

\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-m-l)\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}\to\IC_{L_{J}}^{H}(-(m-1)-l)(-1)\otimes_{\mathbf{Q}}V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m-1)}

for

and the linear map
V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m)}\to V_{\mathbf{t}}^{(m-1)}
as in Definition
4.2

Proof.

Follows directly from Lemma

Proposition 4.7

We have the following description of the RHM defect object

4.3.
Intersection cohomology

Proposition 4.8
.

The intersection cohomology
\IH^{\bullet}(X)
is of Hodge–Tate type. Furthermore, we have the following formula for the generating series of the intersection Betti numbers.

\sum\dim\IH^{2i}(X)q^{i}=\frac{q^{ld-1}-1}{q-1}+(q^{d-1}-(q-1)^{d-1})^{l}q^{l-1}+\sum_{\nu}{l\choose\nu_{2},\nu_{3},\ldots,\nu_{d}}{d\choose 2}^{\nu_{2}}\cdots{d\choose d}^{\nu_{d}}g_{\nu}(q)\in\mathbf{Z}[q],

where

g_{\nu}(q)=(-1)^{\|\nu\|}\frac{q^{ld-\|\nu\|}-1}{q-1}\cdot\tau_{<\|\nu\|/2}\left((1-q)\cdot q^{l-1}\prod_{k=2}^{d}\left(\frac{q^{k-1}-1}{q-1}\right)^{\nu_{k}}\right).

and the sum ranges over
\nu=(\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{d})\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
such that
\nu_{2}+\ldots+\nu_{d}=l
and
\nu_{d}\neq l
. Here,
\|\nu\|=\sum_{k=2}^{d}k\nu_{k}
.

We first compute the contribution of the defect object in the Grothendieck group of mixed Hodge structures
K_{0}(\mathrm{MHS})
.

Lemma 4.9
.

We have

(-1)^{ld-1}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[\mathcal{H}^{i}a_{*}\mathcal{K}_{X}]=\sum_{\nu}{l\choose\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{d}}{d\choose 2}^{\nu_{2}}\cdots{d\choose d}^{\nu_{d}}g_{\nu}(u)\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHS}),

where
g_{\nu}(u)
is defined as in Proposition
4.8
. Here,
u=\mathbf{Q}(-1)\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHS})
. The sum ranges over
\nu=(\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{d})\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d-1}
such that
\nu_{2}+\ldots+\nu_{d}=l
and
\nu_{d}\neq l
.

Proof.

The alternating sum of cohomology gives a homomorphism

\chi\colon K_{0}(\MHM(X))\to K_{0}(\mathrm{MHS}),\qquad[\mathcal{M}]\mapsto\sum(-1)^{i}[\mathcal{H}^{i}a_{*}\mathcal{M}]

from the Grothendieck group of mixed Hodge modules on
X
to the Grothendieck group of mixed Hodge structures. Therefore, it is enough to calculate the image of
\bigoplus_{w}\gr_{w}\mathcal{K}_{X}
under this homomorphism. From Proposition
4.7
, we have

\chi(\mathcal{K}_{X})=\sum_{\mathbf{t}\neq(d,\ldots,d)}\bigoplus_{|J_{i}|=t_{i}}\bigoplus_{m\geq 0}\chi\left(\IC_{\mathbb{P}(L_{J})}^{H}(-m-l+1)\right)\cdot\dim V_{\mathbf{t},\prim}^{(m)}.

Note that
\dim L_{J}=ld-|J|
, therefore

\chi\left(\IC_{\mathbb{P}(L_{J})}^{H}(-m-l+1)\right)=(-1)^{ld-|J|-1}u^{m+l-1}\frac{u^{ld-|J|}-1}{u-1}.

Let
\nu_{k}=\#\{i:t_{i}=k\}
. For
\nu=(\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{d})
such that
\nu_{2}+\ldots+\nu_{d}=l
, there are

{l\choose\nu_{2},\ldots,\nu_{d}}{d\choose 2}^{\nu_{2}}\cdots{d\choose d}^{\nu_{d}}

many possibilities for
J=(J_{1},\ldots,J_{l})
having such value of

. Then the assertion follows by applying Lemma
4.4
and noting that

\sum_{m\geq 0}u^{m+l-1}\dim V_{\mathbf{t},\prim}^{(m)}=\tau_{<\frac{|\mathbf{t}|}{2}}\left(u^{l-1}(1-u)\prod_{i=1}^{l}\frac{u^{t_{i}-1}-1}{u-1}\right)=\tau_{<\frac{\|\nu\|}{2}}\left(u^{l-1}(1-u)\prod_{k=2}^{d}\left(\frac{u^{k-1}-1}{u-1}\right)^{\nu_{k}}\right),

and
|J|=|\mathbf{t}|=\|\nu\|
.
∎

By calculating the contribution of the singular cohomology, we conclude this section by proving Proposition
4.8
.

Proof of Proposition
4.8
.

From the short exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{K}_{X}\to\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[ld-2]\to\IC_{X}^{H}\to 0,

we have

\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[\IH^{i}(X)]=(-1)^{ld-1}\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[\mathcal{H}^{i}a_{*}\mathcal{K}_{X}]+\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}(X)]\in K_{0}(\mathrm{MHS}).

The first object is calculated in Lemma

. We compute the second term using Theorem

\displaystyle\sum_{i}(-1)^{i}[H^{i}(X)]

\displaystyle=\frac{u^{ld-1}-1}{u-1}+(-1)^{ld-2}u^{l-1}(a_{0}-a_{1}u+\ldots+(-1)^{l(d-2)}a_{l(d-2)}u^{l(d-2)})

In this section, we show that the hypersurface

in Theorem

5.1.
Automorphism group of
X

We return to the setup where
X
is the projective hypersurface given by the equation
x_{11}\cdots x_{1d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}=0
. From now on,
X
will always be this hypersurface. Let
G_{\sigma}=\mathfrak{S}_{d}^{l}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{l}
where the action
\mathfrak{S}_{l}\to\Aut(\mathfrak{S}_{d}^{l})
is given by permuting the entries. This is naturally a subgroup of
\mathfrak{S}_{ld}
. The element
((\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}),\mu)\in G_{\sigma}
acts on the set
\{(i,j):1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq d\}
by

((\tau_{1},\ldots,\tau_{l}),\mu)(i,j)=(\mu(i),\tau_{i}(j)).

We let

\widetilde{T}=\left\{\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{11},\ldots,\lambda_{ld})\in\mathrm{GL}_{ld}(\mathbf{C}):\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{1j}=\ldots=\prod_{j=1}^{d}\lambda_{lj}\right\}\subset\mathrm{GL}_{ld}(\mathbf{C}).

We have an action
G_{\sigma}\to\mathrm{Aut}(\widetilde{T})
by permuting the variables
\lambda_{ij}
in the way above, and the semi-direct product
\widetilde{G}=\widetilde{T}\rtimes G_{\sigma}
is a subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}_{ld}(\mathbf{C})
. The center
Z(\widetilde{G})
is
\mathbf{C}^{*}\cdot\id
and we let
G=\widetilde{G}/Z(G)
. Note that
\widetilde{G}
acts faithfully on the affine cone
C(X)
of
X
and
G
acts faithfully on
X
. In fact,
G
is actually the automorphism group of
X
. We note that
G
is reductive since there is a compact Zariski dense subgroup given by a semidirect product of a finite group and a real torus.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
G
and
X
as above. Then
\Aut(X)=G
.

Proof.

The Grothendieck–Lefschetz theorem
[
14
]
*Exposé XII, Corollaire 3.6, tells us that all the automorphisms of
X
are linear. By Proposition
4.7
, the support of
\gr_{2(l-1)}^{W}\mathbf{Q}_{X}^{H}[\dim X]
is the union of all coordinate points in
\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}
. This locus should be fixed by an automorphism of
X
. Therefore, the automorphisms are compositions of permutation matrices and diagonal matrices. It is easy to show that among those, the matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{ld}(\mathbf{C})
fixing
x_{11}\cdots x_{1d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}
modulo scalar multiples are exactly the elements in
\widetilde{G}
. The assertion for
\Aut(X)
follows since
G=\widetilde{G}/\mathbf{C}^{*}\cdot\id
.
∎

We fix some notations to encode the torus action. Consider the free abelian group
\mathbf{Z}^{ld}
with basis elements
\{e_{ij}:1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq d\}
. Let
\epsilon_{i}=e_{i1}+\ldots+e_{id}
for
1\leq i\leq l
and consider the subgroup
L\subset\mathbf{Z}^{ld}
generated by
\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{2},\ldots,\epsilon_{1}-\epsilon_{l}
. It is easy to see that
L
is saturated in
\mathbf{Z}^{ld}
; hence the quotient
M\coloneq\mathbf{Z}^{ld}/L
is a free abelian group. We view
e_{ij}
and
\epsilon=\epsilon_{1}=\ldots=\epsilon_{l}
as elements in
M
. Note that the torus
\Spec\mathbf{C}[M]
is naturally isomorphic to
\widetilde{T}
where the character
e_{ij}
corresponds to the coordinate
\lambda_{ij}
of
\widetilde{T}
. We let
\sigma^{\vee}\subset M
be the cone spanned by the vectors
e_{ij}\in M
. The dual here is a purely symbolic notation motivated by toric geometry. There is a well-defined morphism
\deg\colon M\to\mathbf{Z}
by assigning
\deg(e_{ij})=1
for all
e_{ij}
. This corresponds to the one-parameter subgroup given by the diagonal
\mathbf{C}^{*}\cdot\id
of
\widetilde{T}
.

Remark 5.2
.

Some of the notations and the arguments in this section are motivated by those in toric geometry and in the theory of
T
-varieties
[
4
]
. However, we decided not to use the terminology therein for the readers who are unfamiliar with
T
-varieties and to keep the necessary ingredients as minimal as possible.

5.2.
Structure of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}

We describe the structure of the cone
\sigma^{\vee}
.

Proposition 5.3
.

Let
\sigma^{\vee}
be as in the previous section. Then the number of facets of
\sigma^{\vee}
is
d^{l}
. For each
\vec{j}=(j_{1},\ldots,j_{l})\in\{1,\ldots,d\}^{l}
, we have a corresponding facet
\tau_{\vec{j}}
generated by
\{e_{ij}:1\leq i\leq l,j\neq j_{i}\}
.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\{\epsilon\}\cup\{e_{ij}:1\leq i\leq l,j\neq j_{i}\}
spans
M
integrally. Since
\dim M=ld-l+1
, this is a
\mathbf{Z}
-basis for
M
. Therefore, there is a linear functional
\varphi_{\vec{j}}
such that
\varphi_{\vec{j}}(\epsilon)=1
and
\varphi_{\vec{j}}(e_{ij})=0
for
1\leq i\leq l
and
j\neq j_{i}
. This implies that
\varphi_{\vec{j}}(e_{ij_{i}})=\varphi_{\vec{j}}(\epsilon-\sum_{j\neq j_{i}}e_{ij})=1
. Therefore,
\varphi_{\vec{j}}
pairs non-negatively with
\sigma^{\vee}
and zero on
\tau_{\vec{j}}
. This shows that
\tau_{\vec{j}}
is a facet of
\sigma^{\vee}
. In order to show that these are all the facets, it is enough to show that
\sigma^{\vee}=\bigcap_{\vec{j}}\{\varphi_{\vec{j}}\geq 0\}
. The inclusion
\subseteq
is obvious. For the other inclusion, let
\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}e_{ij}
be an element in the right-hand side. From
\varphi_{\vec{j}}(\sum a_{ij}e_{ij})=\sum_{i=1}^{l}a_{ij_{i}}
, we get

From the description of the facets, we have a chamber decomposition of

It is clear that

and takes the value zero exactly on the boundary of the cone

.

.

We define
\widetilde{\tau}_{\vec{j}}
and

We prove two small lemmas that will be used later.

Lemma 5.5
.

For

, there are unique integers

We can assume that

∎

Lemma 5.6
.

For
u\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
such that
\deg(u)=N(l-1)d
, we have

. There is a constant

depending only on

\left|\#\Big\{u\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M:\varphi(u)=m,\deg(u)=Nd(l-1)\Big\}-d^{ld-1}\frac{(N(l-1)-m)^{l(d-1)-1}}{(l(d-1)-1)!}\right|\leq C(l,d)N^{l(d-1)-2}

for all
0\leq m\leq(l-1)N

is large enough.

Proof.

The first assertion is clear by applying the degree to Lemma
5.5
. Let
u\in\sigma^{\vee}\cap M
such that

and
\deg(u)=Nd(l-1)
. Without loss of generality, suppose that
\vec{j}=(d,d,\ldots,d)
and

. The

can be uniquely expressed as

1\leq j\leq d-1\end{subarray}}a_{ij}\epsilon_{ij}.

Since

\sum_{1\leq i\leq l,1\leq j\leq d-1}a_{ij}=d(N(l-1)-m)

and the contribution from the overlap of

is uniformly bounded by a constant multiple of

Lemma 5.7

Let

Clear from the description.

Proposition 5.8
.

We have a one-to-one correspondence between

-invariant valuations

on
X
,
\widetilde{G}
-invariant valuations
\widetilde{v}
on

centered at the origin up to scaling, and
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
-invariant valuations

on
\mathbb{P}^{l-2}

\mathfrak{S}_{l}
-action on

is given by the standard representation. Moreover, we have the following properties:

(1)

v
and
v_{0}
are restrictions of
\widetilde{v}
under the following field inclusions:

\mathbf{C}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2})\simeq\mathrm{Frac}(R)^{\widetilde{T}}\subset\mathbf{C}(X)\simeq\mathrm{Frac}(R)^{\mathbf{C}^{*}\cdot\id}\subset\mathrm{Frac}(R).

Here,
\mathrm{Frac}(R)^{\widetilde{T}}

(2)

If

G
-invariant valuation on

, then the center

(3)

If
v_{0}
is a
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
-invariant valuation on
\mathbb{P}^{l-2}

is not contained in the union of hyperplanes
\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}\{z_{i}=0\}
.

(4)

If
\widetilde{v}
is a
\widetilde{G}
-invariant valuation on

centered at the origin and

(5)

If

is a
\mathfrak{S}_{l}
-invariant valuation on

is determined by property (4) and the following description on the homogeneous parts:

for

, where

.

(6)

Similarly, if
v
is a
G
-invariant valuation on
X
, then the
\widetilde{v}
is determined by property (4) and the following analogous description:

\widetilde{v}(f_{m})=m+v(f/x_{11}^{m}),

for

(7)

v

is divisorial.

Proof.

The one-to-one correspondence follows from (1), (5), and (6) where the scaling for
\widetilde{v}
is determined by fixing
\widetilde{v}(x_{11})=1
. Part (1) is clear. We note that the inclusion

For (2), consider a
G
-invariant valuation

on

intersects one of the open charts

. Since the finite group

We prove (4). Let

We show (5). Note that

For a

Proposition 5.10
.

If

and

is the

for a divisor

We show a lemma first.

Lemma 5.11
.

Let
v_{0}
be a divisorial valuation on
\mathbb{P}^{l-2}
and
\alpha>0
be a real number. For
\epsilon>0
, we have

\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{F}_{v_{0}}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(i))}{N^{l-2}}-\frac{1}{(l-2)!}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}\left(\frac{i}{N}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha\right)\right|\leq\epsilon

for all
0\leq i\leq(l-1)N
, if
N
is large enough.

Proof.

Note that
f(t)=\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(t\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)
is a continuous increasing function on
[0,l-1]
. Let
0=t_{0}<\ldots<t_{m}=l-1
such that
|f(t_{k+1})-f(t_{k})|<\epsilon/2
. For
N
big enough, we have

\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{F}_{v_{0}}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(\left\lfloor t_{k}N\right\rfloor)}{N^{l-2}}-\frac{1}{(l-2)!}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(t_{k}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)\right|<\epsilon/2,

for all
k=0,\ldots,m
. Then for
0\leq i\leq(l-1)N
, pick
k
such that
\left\lfloor Nt_{k}\right\rfloor\leq i<\left\lfloor Nt_{k+1}\right\rfloor
. We see that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{F}_{v_{0}}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(i))}{N^{l-2}}\right|\geq\left|\frac{\dim_{\mathbf{C}}\mathcal{F}_{v_{0}}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(\left\lfloor t_{k}N\right\rfloor)}{N^{l-2}}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{(l-2)!}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(t_{k}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\geq\frac{1}{(l-2)!}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}\left(\frac{i}{N}\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha\right)-\left(1+\frac{1}{(l-2)!}\right)\frac{\epsilon}{2}.

A similar inequality for the other direction using

deduces the lemma.
∎

We show Proposition

.

Proof.

We recall the notation
R=\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]/(F)
the homogeneous coordinate ring of
X
, and the decomposition by homogeneous parts

H^{0}(X,-NK_{X})=R_{N(l-1)d}=\bigoplus_{\deg(u)=N(l-1)d}\Gamma_{u}.

Let
v
be a divisorial valuation on
X
. Under this identification, we have

\mathcal{F}_{v}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(X,-NK_{X})\simeq\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{v}}^{N\alpha+N(l-1)d}R_{N(l-1)d},

where

is the valuation on

\Gamma_{u}\cap\mathcal{F}_{\widetilde{v}}^{N\alpha+N(l-1)d}R_{N(l-1)d}\simeq\mathcal{F}_{v_{0}}^{N\alpha}H^{0}(\mathbb{P}^{l-2},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(\varphi(u))),

on

∎

\vol(X,-K_{X})=d^{ld-1}(l-1)^{ld-2}.
Hence,

S_{X}(v)={ld-2\choose l-1}\frac{1}{(l-1)^{ld-3}}\int_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\int_{x=0}^{l-1}(l-1-x)^{l(d-1)-1}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(x\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)dxd\alpha.

Note that

\int_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(x\cdot\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)d\alpha=x^{l-1}\int_{\alpha=0}^{\infty}\vol_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(1),v_{0}\geq\alpha)d\alpha=\frac{x^{l-1}}{l-1}S_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(v_{0}).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle S_{X}(v_{X})

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(v_{0})\cdot{ld-2\choose l-1}\frac{1}{(l-1)^{ld-2}}\int_{x=0}^{l-1}(l-1-x)^{l(d-1)-1}x^{l-1}dx

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(v_{0})(l-1){ld-2\choose l-1}\int_{0}^{1}(1-x)^{l(d-1)-1}x^{l-1}dx

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(v_{0})(l-1){ld-2\choose l-1}\frac{(l(d-1)-1)!(l-1)!}{(ld-1)!}=S_{\mathbb{P}^{l-2}}(v_{0})\frac{l-1}{ld-1}.

The last integral is commonly known as the Euler integral of the first kind.
∎

5.6.
Local structure of the
K
-moduli space

Here, we analyze the local structure of the
K
-moduli space near the hypersurface
X
. The space of first order deformations of
X
is
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\Ext(\Omega_{X}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})
and the deformation of
X
is unobstructed. By the Luna étale slice theorem for algebraic stacks
[
3
]
, the local structure of the
K
-moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{K}
is given by the following Cartesian square:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{[}\mathbb{T}^{1}/\!\Aut(X){]}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{M}^{K}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{T}^{1}/\!/\!\Aut(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces M^{K},}

where the horizontal maps are étale.

We describe
\mathbb{T}^{1}
and the action of
\Aut(X)
on it. The following proposition says that the formula describing the infinitesimal deformations of smooth hypersurfaces works almost verbatim. The computation is standard but we decided to include it for readers convenience.

Proposition 5.13
.

We have an isomorphism

\mathbb{T}^{1}\simeq\frac{\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]_{d}}{\mathrm{span}\langle x^{\epsilon_{i}-e_{ij}+e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}:1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq l,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq d\rangle}.

The torus
T\subset\Aut(X)
acts as

\lambda\cdot g(x)\mapsto\frac{g(\lambda\cdot x)}{\lambda_{11}\cdots\lambda_{1d}}

for
\lambda=(\lambda_{11},\ldots,\lambda_{ld})\in T
and
g\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
.

Proof.

From the conormal sequence

0\to\mathcal{O}_{X}(-X)\to\Omega_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}^{1}|_{X}\to\Omega_{X}^{1}\to 0,

we get an exact sequence

0\to\Hom_{\mathcal{O}_{X}}(\Omega_{X}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{X})\to H^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X})\xrightarrow{\delta}H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))\to\mathbb{T}^{1}\to H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X}).

Consider the exact sequence

0\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}(-X)\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}\to\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X}\to 0

and note that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}(-d)
has no cohomology since
ld+1-d\geq 2
. This follows from Borel–Weil–Bott calculation, for example, see
[
6
]
*§A.1. Therefore, we have an isomorphism
H^{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}})\to H^{i}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X})
. Therefore, we see that

H^{1}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X})=0,\quad H^{0}(\mathcal{T}_{\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}}|_{X})\simeq\mathrm{span}\langle x_{ij}\partial_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\rangle\Big/\sum_{i,j}x_{ij}\partial_{ij},\quad H^{0}(\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))=R_{d}/\langle F\rangle

The map
\delta
is given by

x_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\partial_{ij}\mapsto x_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}\partial_{ij}F=x^{\epsilon_{i}-e_{ij}+e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}}.

Then the assertion for
\mathbb{T}^{1}
follows. The torus action on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
can be seen from the torus action on
H^{0}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X}(X))
.
∎

We note that the family
\mathcal{X}
over
\mathbb{T}^{1}

\mathcal{X}=\left\{(x,G)\in\mathbb{P}^{ld-1}\times\mathbb{T}^{1}:(F+G)(x)=0\right\}\to\mathbb{T}^{1}

for a chosen inclusion
\mathbb{T}^{1}\hookrightarrow\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]_{d}
restricts to the miniversal deformation of
X
at the origin.

We now analyze the stability of the action of
\Aut(X)
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Note that
\Aut(X)=T\rtimes G_{\sigma}
where
G_{\sigma}=\mathfrak{S}_{d}^{l}\rtimes\mathfrak{S}_{l}
is a finite group. Therefore, the stability of a vector
v\in\mathbb{T}^{1}
is completely characterized by the action of the torus
T
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
. When a torus acts diagonally on a vector space, the characterization of polystable points is well known. For example, see
[
11
]
or
[
35
]
*Proposition 6.15.

Proposition 5.14
.

Let
V
be a vector space and
T
be a torus that acts diagonally on
V
. Let
M=\Hom(T,\mathbf{C}^{*})
be the group of characters and consider the decomposition

V=\bigoplus_{u\in M}V_{u}.

For
v\in V
, we write
v=\sum_{u\in M}v_{u}
such that
v_{u}\in V_{u}
. We consider the polytope

\Phi(v)=\mathrm{conv}\{u:v_{u}\neq 0\},

the convex hull of the support of the weights of
v
. The vector
v
is polystable if and only if
0\in\mathrm{Relint}\Phi(v)
.

We describe the eigenspace decomposition of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
with respect to the action of the torus
T\subset\Aut(X)
. Note that the character group of
T
can be identified canonically with
M_{0}=\{u\in M:\deg(u)=0\}
.

Lemma 5.15
.

Let
\Psi=\{u\in M_{0}:u+\epsilon\in\sigma^{\vee}\}
and

\Phi=\Psi\setminus\{e_{ij}-e_{i^{\prime}j^{\prime}}:1\leq i,i^{\prime}\leq l,1\leq j,j^{\prime}\leq d\}.

We have
\mathbb{T}^{1}=\bigoplus_{u\in\Phi}\mathbb{T}^{1}_{u}
with
\dim\mathbb{T}^{1}_{u}=1
.

Proof.

From Proposition
5.13
, some collection of degree
d
monomials from a basis of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
diagonalizing the torus action. The degree
d
monomials have weights in
\Psi
due to the factor of
\lambda_{11}^{-1}\cdots\lambda_{1d}^{-1}
in the action of
T
on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Note that every eigenspace of
\mathbf{C}[x_{ij}]_{d}
has dimension 1 except
\mathrm{span}\langle x^{\epsilon_{1}},\ldots,x^{\epsilon_{l}}\rangle
. However, these vectors become trivial on
\mathbb{T}^{1}
. Note that the support of the eigenspace decomposition
\Phi
is also clear from the description of
\mathbb{T}^{1}
in Proposition
5.13
.
∎

For example, it is easy to show that the hypersurface given by the equation
f(x_{11},\ldots,x_{td})+x_{t+1,1}\cdots x_{t+1,d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}=0
is
K
-polystable for
f
general, just by looking at the support of the monomials appearing in this polynomial.

Proof.

Let
M^{\leq t}\subset M
be the subgroup generated by
e_{ij}
for
1\leq i\leq t
and
1\leq j\leq d
. Define
M_{0}^{\leq t}=M_{0}\cap M^{\leq t}
and
\Phi_{0}^{\leq t}=\Phi\cap M_{0}^{\leq t}
, where
\Phi
is as in Lemma
5.15
. The deformation to the hypersurface

f(x_{11},\ldots,x_{td})+x_{t+1,1}\cdots x_{t+1,d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}=0

is given by the general linear combination of monomials in
\Phi_{0}^{\leq t}
. It is easy to see that
\Phi_{0}^{\leq t}
spans
M_{0}^{\leq t}
and
\sum_{u\in\Phi_{0}^{\leq t}}u=0
. This tells us that
0\in\mathrm{Relint}(\mathrm{conv}\Phi_{0}^{\leq t})
. Therefore

\{f(x_{11},\ldots,x_{td})+x_{t+1,1}\cdots x_{t+1,d}+\ldots+x_{l1}\cdots x_{ld}=0\}

is
K
-polystable for
f
general.
∎

6.
GIT stability

We finish the paper by proving Proposition
1.4
. The proof is an easy consequence of Luna’s criterion and the Hilbert–Mumford numerical criterion. We first state these criteria for readers’ convenience.

Proposition 6.1

(Hilbert–Mumford numerical criterion
[
26
]
)
.

Let
w=(w_{0},\ldots,w_{n})\in\mathbf{Q}^{n+1}
be a rational root system. For a polynomial
f=\sum_{\alpha\in\mathbf{Z}_{\geq 0}^{n+1}}c_{\alpha}x^{\alpha}\in\mathbf{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}]
, the weight
\mathrm{wt}_{w}(f)
is given by the following formula:

\mathrm{wt}_{w}(f)=\min\{w_{0}\alpha_{0}+\ldots+w_{n}\alpha_{n}:c_{\alpha}\neq 0\}.

Let
X
be the hypersurface in
\mathbb{P}^{n}
given by a homogeneous equation
f(x_{0},\ldots,x_{n})=0
. Then
X
is GIT stable (resp.​ GIT semi-stable) if and only if for all
w\in\mathbf{Q}^{n+1}
and all linear change of coordinates
g\in SL(n+1)
, we have
\mathrm{wt}_{w}(f\circ g)<\frac{d}{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{i}
(resp.​
\leq
). Furthermore,
X
is GIT-polystable if it is GIT-semistable and if all the monomials in
f\circ g
have the same weight if the inequality in the criterion is an equality.

Luna’s criterion allows us to efficiently test the Hilbert–Mumford criterion when the hypersurface has extra symmetry. As in the set-up above, assume that
f
the fixed by a reductive subgroup
H
of
SL(n+1)
. Then in order to check GIT semi(resp.​ poly)-stability, it is enough to check the numerical criterion only for
g\in C_{SL(n+1)}(H)
, the centralizer of
H
, rather than the whole group
SL(n+1)
(see
[
35
]
*pg. 221-222).

Proof of Proposition
1.4
.

Let
f(x)\in\mathbf{C}[x_{0},\ldots,x_{m+1}]
be a polynomial such that
X=\{f(x)=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{m+1}
is GIT-semistable, and
Y
be the corresponding hypersurface given by the equation
F(y,x)=y_{1}\cdots y_{d}+f(x)=0
in
\mathbb{P}^{m+1+d}
. Note that the autormorphism group of
Y
contains a torus
\{(t_{1},\ldots,t_{d})\in(\mathbf{C}^{*})^{d}:t_{1}\cdots t_{d}=1\}
, where the action is given by the multiplication on the
y
-coordinates. The centralizer of this torus in
SL(d+m+2)
is

H=\left\{\begin{bmatrix}\Lambda&0\\
0&g\end{bmatrix}:\Lambda\in(\mathbf{C}^{*})^{d},g\in GL(m+2),\det(\Lambda)\det(g)=1\right\}.

By applying Luna’s criterion, it is enough to check the Hilbert–Mumford numerical criterion after conjugating
F
with the element in
H
. Let
\widetilde{g}=\mathrm{diag}(\Lambda,g)\in H
and pick a weight system
(w_{1},\ldots,w_{d},v_{0},\ldots,v_{n})\in\mathbf{Q}^{m+d+2}
. Then we have

\mathrm{wt}_{(w,v)}(F\circ\widetilde{g})=\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i},\mathrm{wt}_{v}(f\circ g)\right\}\leq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i},\frac{d}{m+2}\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}v_{j}\right\}\leq\frac{d}{m+d+2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}+\sum_{j=1}^{m+1}v_{j}\right).

The first inequality follows from the GIT semistability of
X
. The above inequality is an equality if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}=\frac{d}{m+2}\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}v_{j}=\mathrm{wt}_{v}(f\circ g).

Therefore, the analogous statement for polystability also holds.
∎
