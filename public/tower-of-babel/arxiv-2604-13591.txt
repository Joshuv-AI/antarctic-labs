1.
Introduction

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open bounded domain with smooth boundary
\partial\Omega
. Let
2^{*}_{Q}\coloneq\frac{2Q}{Q-2}
be the critical Sobolev exponent associated with the Sobolev inequality in
\mathbb{G}
, where
Q
denotes the homogeneous dimension. For
\lambda>0
, we study the following critical problem

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\lambda u+u^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}+f(\xi)\quad&&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0\quad&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
u_{+}\coloneq\max\{u,0\}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
denotes the sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
. Our study is motivated by the classical problem introduced by Ambrosetti and Prodi
[
1
]
, which describes how the solvability and multiplicity of solutions depend on the interaction between the nonlinearity and the spectrum of the underlying operator. Precisely, they studied the boundary value problem

(1.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta u=g(u)+f(x),&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
f\in C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
, for some
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
g(0)=0
,
g^{\prime\prime}(s)>0
for all
s\in\mathbb{R}
. Defining

g^{-}=\lim_{s\to-\infty}g(s)/s
,
g^{+}=\lim_{s\to+\infty}g(s)/s
and assuming
0<g^{-}<\mu_{1}<g^{+}<\mu_{2}
, where
\mu_{k}
be the
k
-th eigenvalue of
-\Delta
with with Dirichlet boundary conditions, they proved the existence of a closed, connected
C^{1}
-manifold
M\subset C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
of codimension one such that
C^{0,\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})\setminus M
consists of two components. For
f
lies in one component the problem admits no solution, in the other exactly two solutions, while for
f\in M
it admits a unique solution. This result emphasizes the role of the interaction between the asymptotic behavior of the nonlinearity and the spectrum of
-\Delta
. Motivated by this perspective, several authors have studied Ambrosetti-Prodi type problems with unilateral superlinear growth, in particular
g^{-}\in(0,\mu_{1})
and
g^{+}=+\infty
. A prototype example is

(1.3)

\left\{\begin{aligned} &-\Delta u=\lambda u+u_{+}^{p}+f(x)\quad&&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
&u=0\quad&&\text{ on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
1<p<\frac{N+2}{N-2}
, if
N\geq 3
,
1<p<\infty
if
N=2
, and
u_{+}=\max\{u,0\}
. For
\lambda<\mu_{1}
, the nonlinearity
g(s)=(s_{+})^{p}+\lambda s
crosses the first eigenvalue, yielding a superlinear Ambrosetti-Prodi problem. This case was studied by de Figueiredo
[
17
]
, assuming
f\in C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
of the form
f=t\phi_{1}+h
, where
\phi_{1}
is the positive normalized eigenfunction associated to
\mu_{1}
,
h\in C^{\alpha}(\bar{\Omega})
satisfies
\int_{\Omega}h\phi_{1}\,dx=0
, and
t\in\mathbb{R}
. When
\lambda>\mu_{1}
, the nonlinearity does not cross the first eigenvalue, this case was investigated by Ruf and Srikanth
[
42
]
. Assuming
\lambda\neq\mu_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
and the same structure of the term
f
, they proved the existence of a constant
T(h)
such that, for
t>T(h)
, problem (
1.3
) admits at least two solutions. Their approach relies on the generalized Mountain Pass Theorem or linking theorem. Subsequently, Deng
[
16
]
considered a superlinear elliptic problem involving the critical growth
u^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}
, together with a perturbation
g(x,u_{+})
of subcritical growth, and proved the existence of multiple solutions. Later, de Figueiredo and Jianfu
[
18
]
investigated a critical Ambrosetti-Prodi type problem of the form

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta u&=\lambda u+u_{+}^{2^{*}-1}+f(x)\quad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{aligned}\right.

where
f=t\varphi_{1}+h
as before, but with
h\in L^{r}(\Omega)
for some
r>N
.
They showed that the problem admits a negative solution for large values of
|t|
, with the sign of

depending on whether
\lambda
lies below or above the first eigenvalue
\mu_{1}
. When
\lambda
is not an eigenvalue and the dimension is sufficiently large, the existence of a second solution was established via the linking theorem. In the resonant case
\lambda=\mu_{1}
, they proved that a solution exists provided

is sufficiently small in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. Moreover, they established bifurcation results from any eigenvalue. Ambrosetti-Prodi type problems involving different operators have also been studied in
[
2
,
12
,
15
,
36
,
37
,
40
,
43
,
46
]
and the references therein.

Despite these developments in the Euclidean setting, to the best of our knowledge Ambrosetti-Prodi type problems, both in the subcritical and critical cases, have not been previously studied in the framework of Carnot groups. In particular, no results appear to be available for problem (
1.1
), which involves the sub-Laplacian and unilateral critical growth. The purpose of the present work is to fill this gap. Before stating our main results, we briefly review some related developments on semilinear equations in Carnot groups.

1.1.
Sub-Laplacians and Semilinear Problems on Carnot Groups

Let
\mathbb{G}
be a Carnot group and
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
denote a sub-Laplacian on
\mathbb{G}
(see Section
2
for precise definitions). The operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a second-order differential operator which is degenerate elliptic. The lack of uniform ellipticity leads to substantial analytical difficulties and prevents the direct use of many classical tools from elliptic theory. A crucial feature distinguishing sub-Laplacians on Carnot groups from general degenerate elliptic operators is that
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
can be written as a sum of squares of left-invariant vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s hypoellipticity condition
[
24
]
. This structure yields some regularity properties and plays a fundamental role in the analysis of partial differential equations on Carnot groups. In this direction, Folland
[
19
]
developed a functional-analytic framework for sub-Laplacians and established the existence of a global fundamental solution. Shortly thereafter, Rothschild and Stein
[
41
]
clarified the role of sums of squares of vector fields in the study of second-order partial differential equations, initiating a systematic study of PDEs on Carnot groups. In the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
, which is the simplest nontrivial Carnot group, the associated critical problem
has been extensively studied. In a series of seminal works, Jerison and Lee
[
25
,
26
,
27
]
obtained sharp Sobolev inequalities and explicit extremal functions, extending the classical results of Aubin and Talenti. The importance of these results for critical nonlinear equations was emphasized by Brézis and Nirenberg
[
10
]
and the corresponding Brézis-Nirenberg type result on
\mathbb{H}^{n}
was proved in
[
13
]
. Further related results on the Heisenberg group are available in
[
3
,
4
,
11
,
14
,
20
,
23
,
28
,
29
,
31
,
32
,
33
,
38
,
44
,
45
]
. For general Carnot groups, explicit minimizers for the critical Sobolev inequality are not known. However, the best Sobolev constant is attained
[
22
]
, and the asymptotic behavior of minimizing sequences has been described in detail, see
[
7
]
. Using these estimates, Loiudice
[
30
]
established a Brézis-Nirenberg type result for critical equations on Carnot groups. Further related results on critical problems in Carnot groups can be found in
[
5
,
8
,
22
,
23
]
. After reviewing the relevant works on the Carnot group, we are now ready to discuss the assumptions and main results of this paper.

1.3.
Our approach

The proofs of Theorem
1.1
-
1.3
are based on variational methods applied to the energy functional associated with problem (
1.1
), defined on
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. We first establish the existence of a nonpositive solution
u_{t}
of problem (
1.1
). We observe that for any nonpositive solution the nonlinear term vanishes, and the equation reduces to a linear problem. The existence of such a solution follows from the Fredholm alternative and uniform bounds, for sufficiently large values of the parameter
t
, with the sign depending on the position of
\lambda
relative to the first eigenvalue. To obtain a second solution, we consider perturbations around the first solution
u_{t}
. This leads to a new variational formulation whose critical points correspond to weak solutions of problem (
1.1
). For
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, the associated energy functional exhibits a mountain-pass geometry. For
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
, with
\lambda
not an eigenvalue, we use a linking argument based on the spectral decomposition of the underlying space into finite and infinite-dimensional subspaces. Using suitable spectral inequalities to verify the geometric conditions required by the linking theorem. Due to the critical growth, the Palais-Smale condition does not hold globally in both cases. Therefore, a key step is to show that the minimax level avoids the non-compactness levels associated with concentrating sequences
u_{\varepsilon}
. In the Carnot group setting, this is particularly delicate because the explicit form of the concentrating functions is not known. We overcome this difficulty by exploiting their asymptotic behavior, which is sufficient to derive the required energy estimates. Another difficulty comes from the presence of the first solution
u_{t}
in the critical term, which introduces additional lower-order terms in the energy estimates. To handle this issue, we localize the critical nonlinearity on suitable subsets of
\Omega
where the concentrating functions are sufficiently large. On these sets, the critical term dominates the perturbation induced by

u_{t}
, allowing us to control the interaction terms. With this approach, we show that Palais-Smale sequences at the minimax level are bounded and converge to nontrivial limits. As a result, we obtain the existence of a second solution for homogeneous dimension
Q>6
, both in the mountain-pass case and in the linking case.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
deals with the resonant case
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
. Under suitable smallness and sign assumptions on the function
f
, we show that the associated energy functional is bounded below on an appropriate constraint. A direct minimization argument then yields a nontrivial critical point, which corresponds to a weak solution
u_{t}
of problem (
1.1
).

The proof of Theorem
1.3
is based on a bifurcation analysis near higher eigenvalues of the sub-Laplacian. Starting from the branch of nonpositive solutions obtained in Theorem
1.1
, we write solutions of (
1.1
) in the form
u=u_{t}(\lambda)+v
, which leads to an equivalent equation for the perturbation
v
. Using the spectral decomposition of the underlying space and a classical bifurcation theorem by Rabinowitz, we show that each eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
,
k>1
, is a bifurcation point. Finally, an energy estimate shows that the bifurcating solutions can only occur for
\lambda<\lambda_{k}
, which determines the direction of bifurcation.

The main features of this work can be summarized as follows.

•

To the best of our knowledge, Ambrosetti-Prodi type problems have not been previously investigated in the framework of Carnot groups, including the Heisenberg group. This paper provides the first existence, multiplicity, and bifurcation results in this non-Euclidean setting.

•

The analysis is carried out for the sub-Laplacian on Carnot groups, where the operator is degenerate elliptic and many classical tools from elliptic theory are no longer available. The approach relies instead on subelliptic variational methods and the functional framework of Folland-Stein Sobolev spaces.

•

The presence of critical Sobolev growth is treated by adapting Brézis-Nirenberg type compactness arguments to the Carnot group setting. In contrast with the Euclidean case and the Heisenberg group, explicit extremal functions for the critical Sobolev inequality are not known in general Carnot groups, which requires a refined analysis.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we recall basic notions on Carnot groups, the sub-Laplacian, and the functional framework, including spectral properties and Sobolev-type embeddings. Section
3
establishes the existence of a nonpositive solution and a second solution to problem (
1.1
), using mountain-pass or linking arguments, with the critical growth handled via Brézis-Nirenberg-type estimates adapted to the Carnot group setting. Section
4
deals with the resonant case
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
, where existence is proved via a direct minimization argument under suitable smallness and sign conditions on
f
. Finally, in Section
5
, we prove the bifurcation near higher eigenvalues.

2.
Preliminaries

The purpose of this section is to recall basic definitions and properties of Carnot groups that will be used throughout the paper. We follow the approach based on homogeneous Lie group structures, which is equivalent to the classical theory of stratified Lie groups.

2.1.
Carnot groups and Folland-Stein spaces

Let
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ)
be a Lie group, where
\circ
denotes the group law.
Assume that
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ)
is endowed with a family of Lie group automorphisms

\{\delta_{\mu}\}_{\mu>0}
, called dilations, of the form

(2.1)

\delta_{\mu}(\xi^{(1)},\xi^{(2)},\ldots,\xi^{(r)})=\bigl(\mu\,\xi^{(1)},\,\mu^{2}\xi^{(2)},\,\ldots,\,\mu^{r}\xi^{(r)}\bigr),

where
\xi^{(i)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,r
and
N_{1}+\cdots+N_{r}=N
. Let
\mathfrak{g}
be the Lie algebra of
(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ)
, i.e., the space of all left-invariant vector fields. Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{N_{1}}
be the left-invariant vector fields on
\mathbb{G}
satisfying
X_{j}(0)=\partial/\partial\xi_{j}^{(1)}\big|_{0}
for
j=1,\dots,N_{1}
. We assume that these vector fields generate the whole Lie algebra under Lie brackets, i.e.

\operatorname{Lie}\{X_{1},\dots,X_{N_{1}}\}=\mathfrak{g}.

Under this assumption, we refer to

\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},\circ,\delta_{\mu})

as a (homogeneous) Carnot group of step
r
, with
N_{1}
generators. An equivalent description of a Carnot group is given in terms of the stratification of its Lie algebra. Precisely, a Carnot group can equivalently be defined as a connected, simply connected Lie group whose Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
admits a stratification, i.e., a direct sum decomposition

(2.2)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}

\displaystyle=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{r},\quad\text{ satisfying }

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &[V_{1},V_{j}]=V_{j+1},&&\text{for }1\leq j\leq r-1,\\
&[V_{1},V_{r}]=\{0\}.\end{aligned}\right.

Here
V_{1}
is called the horizontal layer, and
V_{r}\neq\{0\}
.
The formulations (
2.1
) and (
2.2
) are equivalent. In particular,
every stratified group admits homogeneous dilations of the form (
2.1
), and conversely, every homogeneous Carnot group is isomorphic to a stratified group (see
[
7
]
). The homogeneous dimension of
\mathbb{G}
is defined by

Q\coloneq\sum_{j=1}^{r}j\,N_{j}.
The sub-Laplacian associated with
\mathbb{G}
is the second-order differential operator

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N_{1}}X_{i}^{2}.

A basic example of a Carnot group is the abelian group
\mathbb{G}=(\mathbb{R}^{N},+)
. In this case, the stratification is trivial, the homogeneous dimension as
Q=N
, and the associated sub-Laplacian coincides with the usual constant-coefficient elliptic operator on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
. A fundamental non-commutative example is the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}^{2n+1},\circ)
, whose homogeneous dimension is
Q=2n+2
. The group operation is given by
\xi\circ\xi^{\prime}=\left(x+x^{\prime},\ y+y^{\prime},\ t+t^{\prime}+2\bigl(x^{\prime}\cdot y-x\cdot y^{\prime}\bigr)\right),
for all
\xi=(x,y,t),\xi^{\prime}=(x^{\prime},y^{\prime},t^{\prime})\in\mathbb{R}^{2n+1}
, where
x,y,x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
t,t^{\prime}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Now, we collect some basic analytic facts that will be used throughout the paper. Since the horizontal vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{N_{1}}
generate the entire Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
under Lie brackets, they satisfy Hörmander’s condition. Hence, the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a hypoelliptic operator. Moreover,
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
satisfies Bony’s maximum principle (see
[
9
]
). The vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{N_{1}}
are homogeneous of degree one with respect to the family of dilations
\{\delta_{\mu}\}_{\mu>0}
introduced in
(
2.1
). Moreover, the adjoint operator of
X_{i}
is
-X_{i}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{G})
, and therefore the sub-Laplacian
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a self-adjoint operator in divergence form. The Lebesgue measure on
\mathbb{R}^{N}
coincides with the Haar measure of
\mathbb{G}
(In the present work, all integrals are understood with respect to the Haar measure). In particular, it is left-invariant under group translations, and it satisfies the scaling property
|\delta_{\mu}(E)|=\mu^{Q}\,|E|
for every measurable
E\subset\mathbb{R}^{N}
. For a smooth function
u:\mathbb{G}\to\mathbb{R}
, we denote by

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\coloneq(X_{1}u,\dots,X_{N_{1}}u)

the horizontal (or sub-elliptic) gradient associated with the operator
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
.
Both
\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
and
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
are left-translation invariant and they are homogeneous of degree one and two respectively, with respect to
\delta_{\mu}
. We recall that a function
\rho:\mathbb{G}\to[0,\infty)
is called a homogeneous (quasi)norm if

•

\rho(\xi)=0
iff
\xi=0
;

•

\rho(\delta_{\mu}\xi)=\mu\ \rho(\xi)
for all
\mu>0
;

•

\rho(\xi\circ\eta)\leq C\bigl(\rho(\xi)+\rho(\eta)\bigr)
for some
C\geq 1
.

Such norms on every Carnot group always exist. A celebrated result of Folland
[
19
]
ensures that
there is a homogeneous norm
|\cdot|_{\mathbb{G}}
and a constant
C_{Q}>0
such that

\Gamma_{\xi}(\eta)\coloneq\frac{C_{Q}}{|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{G}}^{\ Q-2}},\qquad Q\geq 3,

is a fundamental solution of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
with pole at
\xi
. Homogeneous norms induce left-invariant distances of the form
d(\eta,\xi)=|\xi^{-1}\circ\eta|_{\mathbb{G}},
all of which are equivalent and generate the Euclidean topology on
\mathbb{G}
. Open balls with respect to the homogeneous distance
d
are denoted by
B_{r}(\xi)\coloneq\{\eta\in\mathbb{G}:d(\eta,\xi)<r\}
.

We now fix the notation and the function spaces for the problem (
1.1
). Let
2^{*}_{Q}\coloneq\frac{2Q}{Q-2}
denote the critical Sobolev exponent in the setting of Carnot groups. The corresponding
critical power in the nonlinearity is
2_{Q}^{*}-1=\frac{Q+2}{Q-2}
, which plays the same
role as the exponent
\frac{N+2}{N-2}
in the classical semilinear Poisson equation in
\mathbb{R}^{N},(N\geq 3)
. A fundamental role in the functional analysis on Carnot groups is played by the following
Sobolev-type inequality (see, for instance,
[
19
]
)

(2.3)

\|u\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\quad\text{for all }u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega),

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
is open and for some
C>0
. The optimal constant in (
2.3
) is explicitly known in some special cases, such as the Heisenberg group
\mathbb{H}^{n}
(see
[
26
]
).
Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open set, the Folland-Stein Sobolev space associated with a Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
is given by

S^{1}(\Omega)=\bigl\{u\in L^{2_{Q}^{*}}(\Omega)\ :\ \nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\in L^{2}(\Omega)\bigr\},

and it is endowed with the norm

(2.4)

\|u\|_{S^{1}(\Omega)}\coloneq\|u\|_{L^{2_{Q}^{*}(\Omega)}}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

We denote by
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
the closure
C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega)
with respect to the norm (
2.4
). By the Sobolev inequality (
2.3
), the norm (
2.4
) is equivalent on
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
to the norm induced by the inner product

\langle u,v\rangle_{S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)}\coloneq\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\rangle\,d\xi.

As a consequence,
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is a Hilbert space. We emphasize that, for general unbounded
domains
\Omega
, the space
S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is not embedded into
L^{2}(\Omega)
. A function
u\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
is said to be a weak solution of the
problem (
1.1
) if

(2.5)

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u,\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\varphi\rangle\,d\xi=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\ \varphi\ d\xi+\int_{\Omega}u^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}\varphi\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}f\ \varphi\ d\xi\quad\text{for all }\varphi\in S_{0}^{1}(\Omega).

Every classical solution of (
1.1
) satisfies (
2.5
), since the formal adjoint of

\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
is
-\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}
. Finally, we point out that the exponent
2^{*}_{Q}
is critical for the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
. Indeed, even when
\Omega
is bounded, the continuous embedding

S_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)

fails to be compact. This lack of compactness produces the main difficulty in the study of critical problems on Carnot groups.

We recall that if
Q\leq 3
, then the group
\mathbb{G}
reduces to the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
equipped with the usual additive structure. Therefore, throughout this paper we assume that the homogeneous dimension satisfies
Q>3
.

2.2.
Spectral properties of the sub-Laplacian on Carnot groups

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{G}
be an open
and a bounded domain. We consider the Dirichlet eigenvalue problem associated with the sub-Laplacian

\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
:

\left\{\begin{aligned} -\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u&=\lambda u\quad&&\text{in }\Omega,\\
u&=0\quad&&\text{on }\partial\Omega.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
\Omega
is bounded, the embedding

S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\Omega)
is compact. As a consequence, the operator
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
endowed with
Dirichlet boundary conditions is self-adjoint on
L^{2}(\Omega)
and has a compact resolvent. Therefore, its spectrum consists of a sequence of real eigenvalues

0<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k}\leq\lambda_{k+1}\leq\cdots,\qquad\lambda_{k}\to+\infty\quad\text{as }k\to\infty,

each having finite multiplicity. The first eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}
admits the variational characterization

\lambda_{1}=\min_{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}.

The minimum is achieved by a function
e_{1}\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
,
which can be chosen to be strictly positive in
\Omega
.
The positivity and the simplicity of
\lambda_{1}

follow from Bony’s maximum principle
[
9
]
for degenerate operators. More generally, for each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the
(k+1)
-th eigenvalue can be
characterized by the min-max principle as

(2.6)

\lambda_{k+1}=\min_{u\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}}{\|u\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}},

where

\mathcal{S}^{+}\coloneq\bigl\{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\langle u,e_{j}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0,\quad j=1,\dots,k\bigr\}.

For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists an eigenfunction

e_{k+1}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
associated with
\lambda_{k+1}
,
which attains the minimum in (
2.6
). Finally, the family
\{e_{k}\}_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
forms an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(\Omega)
and an orthogonal basis of
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Eigenfunctions of the Dirichlet sub-Laplacian are of class
C^{\infty}(\Omega)
,
while their regularity up to
\partial\Omega
depends on the geometry of the boundary.

2.3.
Some asymptotic estimates

Garofalo and Vassilev
[
22
]
extended the classical concentration-compactness principle of Lions to the framework of Carnot groups. In particular, they showed that the optimal constant in the critical Sobolev embedding

S_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{G})\hookrightarrow L^{2_{Q}^{*}}(\mathbb{G})

can be characterized by the variational problem

(2.7)

S_{\mathbb{G}}\coloneq\inf_{u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{G})\setminus\{0\}}\frac{\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}^{2}}{\|u\|_{L^{2_{Q}^{*}}(\mathbb{G})}^{2}}.

Moreover, every minimizing sequence for (
2.7
) is relatively compact in
S_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{G})
, up to the action of left translations and homogeneous dilations on
\mathbb{G}
.
As a consequence, the infimum in (
2.7
) is achieved. The attainability of
S_{\mathbb{G}}
has direct implications for the associated critical problem. In particular, the equation

(2.8)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=u^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{G}

admits a nontrivial, non-negative solution

u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{G}).
By Bony’s maximum principle
[
9
]
, every non-negative solution of (
2.8
) is positive. Bonfiglioli and Uguzzoni in
[
8
]
obtained asymptotic estimates at infinity of u in terms of the fundamental solution of
\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
. More precisely, they proved that there exists
M>0
such that

u(\xi)\leq M\min\{1,d(\xi)^{2-Q}\}.

Moreover, there exists
\alpha\in(0,1)
such that every positive solution
u
of (
2.8
) belongs to
\Gamma^{\alpha}(\mathbb{G})
. Here,
\Gamma^{\alpha}(\mathbb{G})
denotes the Lipschitz space adapted to the homogeneous structure of the Carnot group
\mathbb{G}
, introduced by Folland
[
19
]
. These
spaces are defined using the group law and homogeneous norm
d
on
\mathbb{G}
.
In addition, Loiudice
[
30
]
proved that if

u\in S_{0}^{1}(\mathbb{G})
is a positive solution of (
2.8
), then there
exists a constant
C>0
such that

u(\xi)\approx\frac{C}{d(\xi)^{Q-2}}\quad\text{as }d(\xi)\to\infty.

Now, let
U
be a fixed minimizer of (
2.7
) and for
\varepsilon>0
, consider the rescaled family

U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)=\varepsilon^{\frac{2-Q}{2}}\,U\left(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}\xi\right).

Up to multiplicative constants, the functions
U_{\varepsilon}
are solutions of

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=u^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{G}.

Moreover, they satisfy

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}U_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{G})}^{2}=\|U_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\mathbb{G})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2},\qquad\forall\,\varepsilon>0.

Let
R>0
be such that
B_{d}(0,R)\subset\Omega
(we may assume
0\in\Omega
).
Take
\varphi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(B_{d}(0,R))
with
0\leq\varphi\leq 1
and

\varphi\equiv 1
in
B_{d}(0,R/2)
. Set

u_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\coloneq\varphi(\xi)\ U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\ \text{ for }\ \xi\in\mathbb{G}.

We now derive some estimates for the family
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}_{\varepsilon>0}
,
which will play a crucial role in showing that the minimax level of the associated energy functional lies below a suitable threshold. To this end, we recall the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the functions
u_{\varepsilon}
satisfy

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2}),

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2_{Q}^{*}}(\Omega)}^{2_{Q}^{*}}

\displaystyle=S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q}),

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\begin{cases}C_{1}\,\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2}),&\text{if }Q>4,\\
C_{1}\,\varepsilon^{2}|\log\varepsilon|+O(\varepsilon^{2}),&\text{if }Q=4,\end{cases}

where
C_{1}>0
is a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

For the proof, see
[
30
, Lemma 3.3]
. Using the functions
u_{\varepsilon}
and the asymptotic estimates in Lemma
2.1
, we derive further estimates for
u_{\varepsilon}
.

Lemma 2.2
.

For some positive constants
C_{2}
and
C_{3}
and
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}},\quad\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\leq C_{3}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}.

Proof.

We recall the polar coordinates formula for radial functions. For
0\leq r_{1}<r_{2}
and any measurable function
f:[r_{1},r_{2}]\to\mathbb{R}
,

(2.9)

\int_{B_{d}(0,r_{2})\setminus B_{d}(0,r_{1})}f(d(\xi))\ d\xi=Q\ |B_{d}(0,1)|\int_{r_{1}}^{r_{2}}f(\rho)\ \rho^{Q-1}\ d\rho,

whenever at least one of the integrals exists. Now, using the properties of
\varphi
, we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\xi)\ U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\ d\xi\leq\int_{B_{d}(0,R)}U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)\ d\xi=\varepsilon^{\frac{2-Q}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R)}U(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}\xi)\ d\xi.

By the change of variables
\eta=\delta_{1/\varepsilon}\xi
and choosing
r_{0}<R/\varepsilon
, the above inequality reduces to evaluating integrals of functions
that depend only on the homogeneous distance
d(\cdot)
, i.e.

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R/\varepsilon)}U(\eta)\ d\eta

\displaystyle=\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,r_{0})}U(\eta)\ d\eta+\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R/\varepsilon)\setminus B_{d}(0,r_{0})}U(\eta)\ d\eta

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}C_{0}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R/\varepsilon)\setminus B_{d}(0,r_{0})}\frac{d\eta}{d(\eta)^{Q-2}},

where
C_{0},C>0
. Then applying (
2.9
) gives

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\left[1+\int_{r_{0}}^{R/\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\rho^{Q-2}}\rho^{Q-1}\ d\rho\right]=C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\left[1+\int_{r_{0}}^{R/\varepsilon}\rho\ d\rho\right].

By integrating the above equation, we get

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\leq C_{2}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}.

Similarly, we can compute

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}\leq\int_{B_{d}(0,R)}U_{\varepsilon}(\xi)^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\ d\xi=\varepsilon^{-\frac{Q+2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R)}U(\delta_{1/\varepsilon}\xi)^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\ d\xi=\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R/\varepsilon)}U(\eta)^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\ d\eta.

By using Theorem
3.2
and (
2.9
), we have

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,r_{0})}U(\eta)^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\ d\eta+C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\int_{B_{d}(0,R/\varepsilon)\setminus B_{d}(0,r_{0})}\frac{d\eta}{d(\eta)^{Q+2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\left[1+\int_{r_{0}}^{R/\varepsilon}\frac{1}{\rho^{Q+2}}\rho^{Q-1}\ d\rho\right]=C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\left[1+\int_{r_{0}}^{R/\varepsilon}\rho^{-3}\ d\rho\right].

A direct computation yields

\|u_{\varepsilon}\|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}+C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}\leq C_{3}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}},

for some
C_{3}>0
.
∎

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

3.1.
Existence of a Nonpositive Solution

First, we show that problem (
1.1
) admits a nonpositive solution.
If
u\leq 0
in
\Omega
, then
u_{+}=0
and
u
necessarily satisfies

(3.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\lambda u+te_{1}+h,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

Consider first the auxiliary problem

(3.2)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\lambda u+h,&\text{ in }\Omega,\\
u=0,&\text{ on }\partial\Omega.\end{cases}

If
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, problem (
3.2
) admits a unique solution.
If
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda\neq\lambda_{k}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
, existence and uniqueness follow from the Fredholm alternative.
If
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
, a solution exists provided

h\in\ker(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}-\lambda I)^{\perp}
, and it is unique in this orthogonal complement. Let
u_{0}\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
denote a solution of (
3.2
). Moreover, since
h\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have
u_{0}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
. Assume
\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}
and let
u_{t}
be a solution of (
3.1
). Setting
w\coloneq u_{t}-u_{0}
, we obtain

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}w=\lambda w+te_{1}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\qquad w=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Since
e_{1}
is an eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{1}
, we have
w=\frac{t}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda}e_{1},

and therefore
u_{t}=u_{0}+\frac{t}{\lambda_{1}-\lambda}e_{1}.

Using the positivity of
e_{1}
and the boundedness of
u_{0}
,
we choose
t<0
if
\lambda<\lambda_{1}
and
t>0
if
\lambda>\lambda_{1}
,
with
|t|
sufficiently large, so that
u_{t}\leq 0
in
\Omega
.
Hence, (
1.1
) admits a nonpositive solution.

3.2.
Existence of a second solution

Now, we aim to identify a second solution of equation (
1.1
) in the form
u=v+u_{t}
, with
v
satisfying

(3.3)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v=\lambda v+(v+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}\quad\text{in }\Omega,\quad v=0\ \text{on}\ \partial\Omega.

To achieve this, we search for a critical point of the associated functional

J:S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R},

defined by

J(v)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\ d\xi-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\ d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi.

Let
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1})
for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
(The case
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
is treated by the Mountain Pass Theorem as a special case this). We proceed to verify that
J
satisfies the geometric conditions required by the linking theorem
[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
. For this, we consider the orthogonal decomposition

S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)=\mathcal{S}^{-}\oplus\mathcal{S}^{+},

where

\mathcal{S}^{-}\coloneq\mathrm{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\},\quad\mathcal{S}^{+}\coloneq(\mathcal{S}^{-})^{\perp}=\left\{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\langle u,e_{j}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}=0,\ \forall j=1,\dots,k\right\}.

Note that for
0<\lambda<\lambda_{1}
, we choose
\mathcal{S}^{-}=\emptyset
and
\mathcal{S}^{+}=S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
Using the spectral properties of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
, we have the following inequalities:

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq\lambda_{k+1}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{S}^{+},\qquad\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\lambda_{k}\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\forall v\in\mathcal{S}^{-}.

Next, we define the sets used to verify the linking geometry:

S_{\rho}\coloneq\partial B_{d}(0,\rho)\cap\mathcal{S}^{+},

Q\coloneq\left\{v=w+sz:w\in\mathcal{S}^{-},\ \|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r,\ 0\leq s\leq R\right\},

where
z\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, and the constants
0<\rho<R
and
r>0
. In order to select a suitable
z\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
and control the functional
J
on
Q
, we consider the orthogonal projections of
u_{\varepsilon}
as follows:

Let
\pi_{+}
and
\pi_{-}
denote the orthogonal projections from
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
onto
\mathcal{S}^{+}
and
\mathcal{S}^{-}
, respectively. Using these projections, we have the following estimates:

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
such that for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon>0
, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}},

\displaystyle\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\right)\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{Q-2},

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}-|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))|^{2}\right)\,d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{Q-2}.

Proof.

We write
u_{\varepsilon}=\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})
. Since
\mathcal{S}^{-}
is finite-dimensional,
\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})
can be expressed as
\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}e_{i}
, where
\beta_{i}=\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}e_{i}\,d\xi
. Since the first eigenfunction associated with
\lambda_{1}
is positive, we have
\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}\not\equiv 0
.
By the equivalence of norms in finite-dimensional spaces and Lemma
2.2
, we have

\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\beta_{i}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\left(\int_{\Omega}u_{\varepsilon}e_{i}\ d\xi\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\|e_{i}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{Q-2},

and
\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{(Q-2)/2}.

From the triangle inequality, Lemma
2.2
, the
L^{1}
and
L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}
estimates follow immediately

\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}\leq C\varepsilon^{(Q-2)/2},\quad\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\leq C\varepsilon^{(Q-2)/2}.

Next, using the integral representation

|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{d}{d\theta}|u_{\varepsilon}-\theta\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\theta

and standard inequalities for powers of sums, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\left(|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\right)d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2^{*}_{Q}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left|u_{\varepsilon}-\theta\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\left|\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}\right|\ d\xi\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq 2^{*}_{Q}(2^{*}_{Q}-1)\int_{0}^{1}\int_{\Omega}\left(|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}+\theta^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}|\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\right)|\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}|\ d\xi\ d\theta

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}+\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\right)\leq C\varepsilon^{Q-2}.

Finally, by orthogonality of the projections,

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}=\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon}))\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C\varepsilon^{Q-2},

which proves the gradient estimate.
∎

For a fixed constant
K>0
, define

A_{\varepsilon,K}\coloneq\left\{\xi\in\Omega:\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}(\xi))>K\right\}.

We first observe that
A_{\varepsilon,K}
contains a small ball centered at the origin.
Indeed, by the definition of
u_{\varepsilon}
and the estimate on
\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}
, we have

\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}(0)=u_{\varepsilon}(0)-\pi_{-}u_{\varepsilon}(0)\geq C\varepsilon^{-(Q-2)/2}-C\varepsilon^{(Q-2)/2}.

Hence
\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}(0)\to+\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
.
Since both
u_{\varepsilon}
and
\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})
are continuous,
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})
is continuous as well.
Therefore, there exists
\delta>0
, independent of
\varepsilon
, such that

B_{d}(0,\delta)\subset A_{\varepsilon,K}.

Lemma 3.2
.

As
\varepsilon\to 0
, the following estimates hold:

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}|\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2}),

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}|\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\,d\xi+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}),

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}|\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}|\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|\,d\xi+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}).

Proof.

Writing
u_{\varepsilon}=\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})
, for any
p\geq 1
we have

\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}\,d\xi=\int_{\Omega}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\xi+\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}\left(|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)d\xi-\int_{\Omega\setminus A_{\varepsilon,K}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\xi.

Using the identity

|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}=-p\int_{0}^{1}|u_{\varepsilon}-t\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{p-2}(u_{\varepsilon}-t\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon}))\,\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dt,

together with Hölder’s inequality and Lemma
3.1
, we obtain

\left|\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K}}\left(|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{p}-|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\right)d\xi\right|=\begin{cases}O(\varepsilon^{Q-2}),&p=2^{*}_{Q},\\[4.0pt]
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}),&p=2^{*}_{Q}-1,\\[4.0pt]
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}),&p=1.\end{cases}

Moreover, since
B_{d}(0,\delta)\subset A_{\varepsilon,K}
, it follows that

\int_{\Omega\setminus A_{\varepsilon,K}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\xi\leq\varepsilon^{Q-\frac{(Q-2)}{2}p}\int_{d(\eta)>\delta/\varepsilon}|U(\eta)|^{p}\,d\eta,

which yields

\int_{\Omega\setminus A_{\varepsilon,K}}|u_{\varepsilon}|^{p}\,d\xi=\begin{cases}O(\varepsilon^{Q}),&p=2^{*}_{Q},\\[4.0pt]
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}}),&p=2^{*}_{Q}-1,\\[4.0pt]
O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}),&p=1.\end{cases}

Combining the above estimates proves Lemma
3.2
.
∎

Before constructing the linking structure, we first verify that the functional
J
is bounded from below on small spheres in
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. The following lemma ensures that
J
attains a positive level on such a sphere, which will serve as one part of the linking geometry.

Lemma 3.3
.

There exist constants
\rho>0
and
\kappa>0
such that

J(v)\geq\kappa,\quad\forall\,v\in S_{\rho}\coloneq\partial B_{d}(0,\rho)\cap\mathcal{S}^{+}.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
. Since
u_{t}\leq 0
and
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1})
, we have

J(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{\lambda}{2}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi.

Applying the spectral inequality
\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,d\xi\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{k+1}}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi
for
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, we get

J(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi.

Further, the Sobolev inequality on the Carnot group gives

\int_{\Omega}(v)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi\leq S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-\frac{Q}{Q-2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi\right)^{2^{*}_{Q}/2},

so that

J(v)\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-\frac{Q}{Q-2}}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}.

Define the auxiliary function

\Phi(\rho)\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\rho^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-\frac{Q}{Q-2}}\rho^{2^{*}_{Q}},\quad\rho\geq 0.

Observe that
\Phi
is positive near zero, vanishes at zero, and attains its maximum at

\rho\coloneq S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}>0.

Denote this maximum by

\kappa\coloneq\Phi(\rho)=\frac{S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}}{Q}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}>0.

Thus, for every
v\in S_{\rho}
, we conclude

J(v)\geq\kappa,

which proves the lemma.
∎

To establish the linking geometry, we show that the functional
J
remains strictly below the level
\kappa
on the boundary of
Q
. To this end, we choose
z\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
as follows. Let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the bubble introduced earlier and
\pi_{+}
the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. Set

z\coloneq\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}),

so that
z\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
. With this choice, the critical term dominates along large values of the parameter
s
, yielding a uniform upper bound for
J
on
\partial Q
.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exist constants
r_{0}>0
,
R_{0}>0
, and
\varepsilon_{0}>0
such that for all
r\geq r_{0}
,
R\geq R_{0}
, and
0<\varepsilon\leq\varepsilon_{0}
, we have

J(v)<\kappa,\quad\forall v\in\partial Q,

where
\kappa>0
is defined in Lemma
3.3
.

Proof.

We split the boundary
\partial Q
into three parts for clarity:

Q_{1}\coloneq\{w\in\mathcal{S}^{-}:\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq r\},\quad Q_{2}\coloneq\{w+s\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}):w\in\mathcal{S}^{-},\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|=r,0\leq s\leq R\},

Q_{3}\coloneq\{w+R\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}):w\in\mathcal{S}^{-},\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|\leq r\}.

Case 1:
v\in Q_{1}
.

In this case,
v
lies purely in
\mathcal{S}^{-}
. By the characterization of eigenvalues
\lambda_{k}
and the fact that
\lambda\in(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k+1})
, we have

J(v)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi\leq 0.

Thus, for all
v\in Q_{1}
,
J(v)<\kappa
, and this case is complete.

Case 2:
v\in Q_{2}
.

Let
\delta^{2}_{0}\coloneq\sup_{0<\varepsilon\leq 1}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
.
If
s<s_{0}\coloneq\sqrt{2\kappa}/\delta_{0}
, then the quadratic part dominates and we immediately get

\displaystyle J(v)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\right)r^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\|(w+s\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})(u_{\varepsilon})u_{\varepsilon}+u_{t})_{+}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\frac{s^{2}\delta^{2}_{0}}{2}<\kappa.

For
s\geq s_{0}
, we estimate the nonlinear term by localizing on a subset of
\Omega
where
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})
is sufficiently large.
Let
w\in\mathcal{S}^{-}
. Since
\mathcal{S}^{-}
is finite dimensional and
u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, there exists a constant
K_{0}
, independent of
s
and
\varepsilon
such that

K_{0}\coloneq\sup\left\|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<\infty.

Since
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})(0)\to\infty
as
\varepsilon\to 0
, there exists
\bar{\varepsilon}>0
such that, for
0<\varepsilon\leq\bar{\varepsilon}
, the set

A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}\coloneq\{\xi\in\Omega:\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}(\xi))>K_{0}\}

has a positive measure for all sufficiently small
\varepsilon
. We estimate the nonlinear term on
A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}
. Since
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}>0
on
A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}
, by the Fundamental Theorem of Calculus, we have

\displaystyle\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right)^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi-\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi-\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi

\displaystyle=2^{*}_{Q}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left(\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}+\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\left(\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}+\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right)-|\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right)\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\ d\xi\ d\tau.

Using the mean value theorem, there exists
\theta=\theta(\xi)\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}+\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\left(\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}+\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right)-\left|\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})

\displaystyle=(2^{*}_{Q}-1)\ \left|\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\theta\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left|\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right)^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi-\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi-\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{1}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left|\tau\ \pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\theta\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\left|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|\ d\xi\ d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left(\left|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|+\left|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right|\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\right)\ d\xi.

Applying this inequality and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right)_{+}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}+\left\|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}

\displaystyle\quad-C\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}\left(|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|+|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|\left|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\right)\ d\xi

\displaystyle\geq\left\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}+\left\|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\quad-C\Big(\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}+\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}\Big).

Then, by applying Lemmas
2.1
,
3.1
,
3.2
and using (
3.4
), we deduce that, for
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small,

\displaystyle J(v)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\lambda\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}\left[\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}+\left\|\frac{w+u_{t}}{s}\right\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}}-C\Big(\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}+\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{1}(A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}})}\Big)\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)

\displaystyle\quad+Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}}\left(\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}})+\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{1}(\Omega)}+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\left(S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}\left(S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q})+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)+Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}}\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q+2}{2}})\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\frac{s^{2}}{2}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}

\displaystyle\coloneq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s).

Using the estimates from (
3.5
), this becomes

J(v)\leq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)r^{2}+\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}).

Since
\frac{1}{2}(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}})r^{2}\to-\infty
as
r\to\infty
, there exists
r>0
such that
J(v)<0
for
v\in Q_{2}
.

Case 3:
v\in Q_{3}
.

Here
v=w+R\pi_{+}u_{\varepsilon}
with
w\in\mathcal{S}^{-}\cap\bar{B}_{d}(0,r)
.
Since
\mathcal{S}^{-}\cap\bar{B}_{d}(0,r)
is finite-dimensional and
u_{t}\in L^{\infty}
, there exists
K>0
such that
\|w+u_{t}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq K
.
Choose
0<\varepsilon_{0}<\bar{\varepsilon}
small enough so that
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}(0))>2K
for
0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
. Then, for sufficiently large
R>R_{0}
, the set

\Big\{\xi\in\Omega:\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}(\xi))+\frac{w(\xi)+u_{t}(\xi)}{R}>1\Big\}

has a positive measure. Again, similar to Case 2, the dominant term is
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})
, ensuring

J(v)<0\leq\kappa,\quad\forall v\in Q_{3}.

Combining the three cases, we conclude that there exist
\varepsilon_{0}
and
R_{0}>0
such that if
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}
and
R>R_{0}
, we have
J(v)<\kappa
, for all
v\in\partial Q
. This establishes the desired linking geometry.
∎

We consider the function

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)\coloneq\frac{s^{2}}{2}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}},\qquad s>0.

Since
2^{*}_{Q}>2
, we have
\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)\to-\infty
as
s\to+\infty
and

\Phi_{\varepsilon}(0)=0
, hence
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
attains a maximum at some

s_{\varepsilon}>0
. A direct computation yields

\Phi_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}(s)=sS_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-s^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+2^{*}_{Q}Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}.

The critical point
s_{\varepsilon}
satisfies

s_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}=\frac{S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}}{S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-2^{*}_{Q}C\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}}=1+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right),

which implies

s_{\varepsilon}=1+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right).

Evaluating
\Phi_{\varepsilon}
at
s_{\varepsilon}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s_{\varepsilon})=\frac{s_{\varepsilon}^{2}}{2}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-\frac{s_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+Cs_{\varepsilon}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\right)S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right)=\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right),

where we used
\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}=\frac{1}{Q}
.
Therefore,

(3.5)

\max_{s>0}\Phi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right).

We now prove that the energy of the functional
J
on the entire linking
set
{Q}
is strictly below the critical Sobolev level.
This estimate will play a crucial role in the compactness analysis
at the minimax level.

Lemma 3.5
.

Assume that
Q>6
. Then

\max_{v\in{Q}}J(v)<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}},

where
S_{\mathbb{G}}
denotes the best Sobolev constant.

Proof.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be fixed sufficiently small so that all estimates established in the previous lemma hold.
Take
v=w+s\,\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\in Q
, where
w\in\mathcal{S}^{-}
and
s\geq 0
. From the definition of
J
, we write

\displaystyle J(v)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w|^{2}-\lambda w^{2}\big)\,d\xi+\frac{s^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-\lambda(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\big)\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}\big(w+s\,\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})+u_{t}\big)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi.

Case 1:

s<s_{0}
, where
s_{0}
is defined in Lemma
3.4
. Using the boundedness of
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, we obtain

J(v)\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\delta_{0}^{2}<\kappa<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}.

Case 2:
Assume that
s\geq s_{0}
.
If
s\geq s_{0}
, using (
3.4
) and arguing as in Lemmas
2.1
and
3.2
,
we obtain

(3.6)

J(w+s\,\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))\leq\frac{s^{2}}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-\lambda(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\big)\,d\xi-\frac{s^{2^{*}_{Q}}}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi+Cs^{2^{*}_{Q}}\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}.

Proceeding as in (
3.5
), we deduce from (
3.6
) that

J(w+s\,\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))\leq\frac{1}{Q}\left(\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})|^{2}-\lambda(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2}\big)\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}\left(\int_{A_{\varepsilon,K_{0}}}(\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi\right)^{-\frac{Q-2}{2}}+O\!\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right).

Using the estimates from Lemmas
2.1
,
3.1
and
3.2
on
\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})
, we deduce

\displaystyle J(w+s\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon}))

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Q}\frac{\left(S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})-\lambda C\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)^{\frac{Q}{2}}}{\left(S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}+O(\varepsilon^{Q})+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}}+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}-\frac{1}{2}\lambda O(\varepsilon^{2})+O\left(\varepsilon^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\right),

where we have used
\|\pi_{+}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\|u_{\varepsilon}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}-\|\pi_{-}(u_{\varepsilon})\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\geq C\varepsilon^{2}+O(\varepsilon^{Q-2})
, for
Q>6
. Finally, if
Q>6
, i.e.
2<\tfrac{Q-2}{2}
, the result follows by choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small.
∎

As a consequence of Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.5
, the functional
J
satisfies the geometric assumptions of the
linking theorem without the Palais-Smale condition

[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
.
Therefore, there exists a sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(3.7)

\displaystyle J(v_{n})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}|^{2}-\lambda v_{n}^{2}\big)\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi=c+o(1),

(3.8)

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),\phi\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi-\lambda v_{n}\phi)\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}\phi\,d\xi=\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

for all
\phi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, where the minimax level
c
is given by

c\coloneq\inf_{\gamma\in\Gamma}\max_{v\in Q}J(\gamma(v)),

with
\Gamma=\{\gamma\in C({Q},S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)):\gamma(v)=v,\text{ if }v\in\partial Q
}.
Moreover, by Lemma
3.5
, the level
c
satisfies

c<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}.

Lemma 3.6
.

The sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

First we compute

\displaystyle J(v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}-1}_{+}v_{n}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}(v_{n}+u_{t})\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi.

Since
u_{t}\leq 0
and
(v_{n}+u_{t})_{+}\geq 0
, the second integral on the right-hand
side is nonnegative. Hence,

\displaystyle J(v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi

(3.9)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi.

Combining (
3.7
), (
3.8
), and (
3.9
), we obtain

(3.10)

\frac{1}{Q}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi\leq J(v_{n})-\frac{1}{2}\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\leq c+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+o(1),

where
\varepsilon_{n}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.
Next, we decompose

v_{n}=v_{n}^{(1)}+v_{n}^{(2)},\quad v_{n}^{(1)}\in\mathcal{S}^{+},
where
v_{n}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}.

Taking
v_{n}^{(1)}
as a test function in (
3.8
) and using the variational
characterization of
\lambda_{k+1}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}^{(1)}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\lambda\|v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}v_{n}^{(1)}\,d\xi

(3.11)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}v_{n}^{(1)}\,d\xi.

Using (
3.8
), (
3.10
), and (
3.11
), together with Hölder’s and Young’s inequalities, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}v_{n}^{(1)}\,d\xi+\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\|v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\left(\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{2^{*}_{Q}-1}{2^{*}_{Q}}}+\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\|v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left(\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi\right)^{\frac{2(2^{*}_{Q}-1)}{2^{*}_{Q}}}+\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left(c\ Q+\frac{\varepsilon_{n}\ Q}{2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}+o(1)\right)^{\frac{2(2^{*}_{Q}-1)}{2^{*}_{Q}}}

\displaystyle\quad+\varepsilon_{n}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}^{\prime}+C\varepsilon_{n}\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{\frac{Q+2}{Q}}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Choosing
\varepsilon>0
such that

0<\varepsilon C<1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}},

we deduce

(3.12)

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq C+\varepsilon_{n}C\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{(Q+2)/Q}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(1)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

By an analogous argument applied to
v_{n}^{(2)}\in\mathcal{S}^{-}
, we obtain

(3.13)

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq\bar{C}+\varepsilon_{n}C\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{(Q+2)/Q}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}^{(2)}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\right).

Adding (
3.12
) and (
3.13
), we conclude that the sequence

\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
∎

We now establish the compactness of Palais-Smale sequences below the critical level.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
\{v_{n}\}\subset S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
be a Palais-Smale sequence for
J
at level

c<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}
. Then, up to a subsequence,

v_{n}\to v
strongly in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
3.6
, the sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Hence, up to a subsequence, there exists
v\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
such that

(3.14)

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v\ \text{in }S^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\qquad v_{n}\to v\ \text{in }L^{q}(\Omega),\ 2\leq q<2_{Q}^{*},\qquad v_{n}\to v\ \text{a.e. in }\Omega.

Since
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, by the continuous embedding
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2_{Q}^{*}}(\Omega)
we deduce that the sequence

\{(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}\}
is bounded in

L^{\frac{2_{Q}^{*}}{2_{Q}^{*}-1}}(\Omega)
.
Consequently, up to a subsequence, there holds

(3.15)

(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}\rightharpoonup(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}\quad\text{in }L^{\frac{2_{Q}^{*}}{2_{Q}^{*}-1}}(\Omega).

Passing to the limit in (
3.8
), we obtain
J^{\prime}(v)=0
, that is,
v
is a weak solution of

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v=\lambda v+(v+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}.

Since
u_{t}\leq 0
, we observe that

\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}v\ d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}(v+u_{t}-u_{t})\ d\xi\geq\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi.

Recalling that
J^{\prime}(v)=0
, we conclude that

J(v)=\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2_{Q}^{*}}\right)\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi=\frac{1}{Q}\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi\geq 0.

Set
w_{n}\coloneq v_{n}-v
. By the Brezis-Lieb lemma, we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi+o(1),

(3.17)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+o(1).

Therefore, combining (
3.14
), (
3.16
) and (
3.17
) we obtain

(3.18)

J(v_{n})=J(v)+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\ d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi+o(1).

It is easy to see that the following identity holds

(3.19)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}v\ d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\ d\xi.

From (
3.14
) (
3.16
) and (
3.19
), we compute

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}|^{2}-\lambda v_{n}^{2}\big)\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}v_{n}\,d\xi+o(1)

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi-\lambda\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi+o(1).

Using
J^{\prime}(v)=0
and (
3.19
), we rewrite the terms involving
v
to obtain

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\quad-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi+o(1).

By the Brézis-Lieb lemma, we have

\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(v+u_{t})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi+o(1).

Substituting this identity into the previous expression and using (
3.15
), (
3.17
) yields

\displaystyle\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi+o(1).

Since
\langle J^{\prime}(v_{n}),v_{n}\rangle_{S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)}\to 0
and

\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}-1}_{+}u_{t}\,d\xi\to 0\quad\text{as }n\to\infty,

we conclude that

(3.20)

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}(w_{n})|^{2}\,d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2_{Q}^{*}}_{+}\,d\xi+o(1).

We define

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi=a\geq 0.

Case 1:
If
a=0
. In this case,
v_{n}\to v
strongly in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and hence

c=J(v).

Case 2:
If
a>0
. From (
3.20
) and the Sobolev inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi\Big)^{2/2^{*}_{Q}}\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}(w_{n})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi\Big)^{2/2^{*}_{Q}}=S_{\mathbb{G}}\Big(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\ d\xi+o(1)\Big)^{2/2^{*}_{Q}},

which implies

(3.21)

a\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

Combining (
3.18
), (
3.20
),(
3.21
) and recall that
J(v)\geq 0
, we get

c+o(1)=J(v_{n})=J(v)+\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\frac{a}{Q}\geq\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

This contradicts Lemma
3.5
. Therefore,
a=0
.
Finally, we conclude that

v_{n}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }S^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

As shown in Lemmas
3.3
,
3.4
, and
3.5
, the functional

J
satisfies the linking geometry of
[
35
, Theorem 5.1]
.
Hence, there exists a Palais-Smale sequence
\{v_{n}\}\subset S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
at some
level
c<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}
.
By Lemma
3.6
, the sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and by
Lemma
3.7
, up to a subsequence,

v_{n}\to v\quad\text{strongly in }S^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Therefore,
v
is a critical point of
J
and satisfies (
3.3
). Moreover,
v\not\equiv 0
. Indeed, if
v=0
, then the strong convergence implies

c=\lim_{n\to\infty}J(v_{n})=J(0)=0,

which contradicts
c>0
. Hence,
v
is nontrivial.
Finally, setting
u=v+u_{t}
, we obtain a nontrivial weak solution of
(
1.1
). This completes the proof.
∎

4.
The proof of Theorem
1.2

We consider the resonant problem associated with the first eigenvalue

(4.1)

\begin{cases}-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u=\lambda_{1}u+u^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}+f(\xi)&\text{in }\Omega,\\[5.69054pt]
u=0&\text{on }\partial\Omega,\end{cases}

where
\lambda_{1}
denotes the first eigenvalue of the sub-Laplacian
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}

on
\Omega
with homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions.
In the resonant case
\lambda=\lambda_{1}
, a necessary condition for the
solvability of (
4.1
) is

(4.2)

\int_{\Omega}f(\xi)\,e_{1}(\xi)\,d\xi<0,

where
e_{1}
is the positive
L^{2}
-normalized eigenfunction corresponding to

\lambda_{1}
. Throughout this section, we further assume that
f
is sufficiently small in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
Let
\mathcal{S}^{-}\coloneq\operatorname{span}\{e_{1}\}
and

\mathcal{S}^{+}\coloneq(\mathcal{S}^{-})^{\perp}
in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Then every

u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
admits a unique decomposition of the form

u=te_{1}+v,\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\quad v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}.

With respect to this decomposition, the energy functional

J:S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{R}
associated with (
4.1
) can be written as

J(u)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi-\lambda_{1}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v+te_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}f(\xi)\,(v+te_{1})\,d\xi,

where
t=\int_{\Omega}ue_{1}\,d\xi
.

We first study the behavior of the functional along the resonant direction associated with the first eigenfunction. This allows us to reduce the problem to a constrained variational problem on the orthogonal complement.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every fixed
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, the functional
J
is bounded above
when restricted to the one-dimensional subspace
\mathcal{S}^{-}
.

Proof.

Fix
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
and define

h(t)\coloneq J(v+te_{1}),\qquad t\in\mathbb{R}.

We first observe that for
t<0
the positive part satisfies

(v+te_{1})_{+}\leq v_{+}
, and therefore

h(t)\leq\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\bigl(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}-\lambda_{1}v^{2}\bigr)\,d\xi+\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\,\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Hence
h
is bounded above on
(-\infty,0]
.
Next, we consider the behavior of
h(t)
as
t\to+\infty
.
We claim that

(4.3)

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\left(\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v+te_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}f(\xi)\,(v+te_{1})\,d\xi\right)=+\infty.

It follows that

h(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to+\infty
.
To prove (
4.3
), set

K\coloneq\sup_{\xi\in\Omega}e_{1}(\xi).

Since
e_{1}
is continuous and positive in
\Omega
, there exists an open set

\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
of positive measure such that

e_{1}(\xi)>\frac{K}{2}\qquad\text{for all }\xi\in\Omega_{0}.

By Lusin’s theorem, for any
\varepsilon>0
(choose, for instance,

\varepsilon=|\Omega_{0}|/2
), there exists a continuous function

g:\Omega_{0}\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\big|\bigl\{\xi\in\Omega_{0}:g(\xi)\neq v(\xi)\bigr\}\big|<\varepsilon.

Consequently, the set

H\coloneq\bigl\{\xi\in\Omega_{0}:g(\xi)=v(\xi)\bigr\}

has a positive measure. Since
g
is continuous on
\Omega_{0}
, it is bounded on
H
. Define

M\coloneq\sup_{\xi\in H}|v(\xi)|.

Then, for all
\xi\in H
and for every
t\geq t_{0}\coloneq 4M/K
, we have

e_{1}(\xi)+\frac{v(\xi)}{t}\geq\frac{K}{2}-\frac{M}{t}\geq\frac{K}{4}.

It follows that

\left(e_{1}+\frac{v}{t}\right)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\geq\left(\frac{K}{4}\right)^{2^{*}_{Q}}\quad\text{a.e. in }H,

and hence there exists
\delta>0
such that

\int_{\Omega}\left(e_{1}+\frac{v}{t}\right)_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi\geq\delta\qquad\text{for all }t\geq t_{0}.

Indeed,

\int_{\Omega}(v+te_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi=t^{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}\left(e_{1}+\frac{v}{t}\right)^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\,d\xi\geq\delta\,t^{2^{*}_{Q}},

for all
t\geq t_{0}
, which proves (
4.3
). Since
h
is continuous and bounded above on
(-\infty,0]
and satisfies

h(t)\to-\infty
as
t\to+\infty
, it follows that
J(v+te_{1})
is bounded above
with respect to
t\in\mathbb{R}
.
Consequently, the functional
J
is bounded above on
\mathcal{S}^{-}
.
∎

By Lemma
4.1
, the map

t\longmapsto J(v+te_{1})

is bounded above on
\mathbb{R}
and satisfies

h(t)\to-\infty\quad\text{as }t\to+\infty.

Hence
h
attains a global maximum on
\mathbb{R}
.
We now characterize this
maximizer and its dependence on
v
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For every
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, there exists a unique real number
t(v)
such that

J(v+t(v)e_{1})=\max_{t\in\mathbb{R}}J(v+te_{1}).

Moreover, the mapping

v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\longmapsto t(v)\in\mathbb{R}

is of class
C^{1}
.

Proof.

Let
t_{0}
be a global maximizer of the function
h(t)=J(v+te_{1})
. Since
h
is differentiable, we have

(4.4)

h^{\prime}(t_{0})=-\int_{\Omega}(v+t_{0}e_{1})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}e_{1}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\,d\xi=0.

Moreover, a direct computation shows that

h^{\prime\prime}(t)=-\int_{\Omega}(v+te_{1})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}e_{1}^{2}\,d\xi\leq 0,

and therefore
h
is concave on
\mathbb{R}
. Thus, the set of maxima is a closed interval. We now show that this interval reduces to a single point.

Assume by contradiction that the maximizer is not unique. Then
h
is constant
on an interval of maximizers, which implies that

h^{\prime\prime}(t_{0})=0
at any maximizer
t_{0}
. From the expression of
h^{\prime\prime}
, this yields
(v+t_{0}e_{1})_{+}=0
a. e. in
\Omega
. Substituting this information
into (
4.4
), we obtain

\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\,d\xi=0,

which contradicts assumption (
4.2
). Hence, the maximizer is unique, and we
denote it by
t(v)
. Since
h^{\prime}(t(v))=0
and
h^{\prime\prime}(t(v))<0
, the Implicit Function Theorem ensures that
the mapping
v\mapsto t(v)
is of class
C^{1}
.
∎

For the special case
v=0
, the maximizer
t(0)
of
h(t)=J(te_{1})
satisfies

\int_{\Omega}(t(0)e_{1})_{+}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}e_{1}\,d\xi+\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\,d\xi=0.

Since
f
satisfies condition (
4.2
), we have
t(0)>0
. Indeed, we can solve for
t(0)
explicitly as

t(0)=\left(\frac{-\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\,d\xi}{\int_{\Omega}e_{1}^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi}\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}-1}}.

Using the map
v\mapsto t(v)
, we define the functional

F:\mathcal{S}^{+}\longrightarrow\mathbb{R},\qquad F(v)\coloneq J(v+t(v)e_{1}),

whose minimization over a suitable ball in
\mathcal{S}^{+}
will lead to a solution of the original problem. At
v=0
, the functional takes the value

F(0)=J(t(0)e_{1})=-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(t(0)e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi-t(0)\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\,d\xi=\frac{2^{*}_{Q}-1}{2^{*}_{Q}}t(0)\int_{\Omega}(-fe_{1})\,d\xi>0.

Putting the value of
t(0)
in the above, we get

(4.5)

F(0)=\frac{Q+2}{2Q}\frac{\displaystyle\left(-\int_{\Omega}f\ e_{1}\ d\xi\right)^{\frac{2^{*}_{Q}}{2^{*}_{Q}-1}}}{\left(\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}e_{1}^{2^{*}_{Q}}d\xi\right)^{\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}-1}}}.

Next, for general
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
, the functional can be written as

F(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}-\lambda_{1}v^{2})\ d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v+t(v)e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}f(v+t(v)e_{1})\ d\xi.

The purpose of introducing the functional
F
is to obtain a critical point of
J
by minimizing
F
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
. To this end, we show that, under suitable smallness assumptions on the function
f
, the functional
F
admits a local minimum in the interior of a ball of
\mathcal{S}^{+}
.
Let

(4.6)

\displaystyle K_{1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\lambda_{2}^{-\frac{Q}{4}}}{Q+2}\ S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}\left(\frac{Q}{Q+2}\right)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}(\lambda_{2}-\lambda_{1})^{\frac{Q+2}{4}},

(4.7)

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\min\left\{\left(\frac{2}{Q+2}\right)^{\frac{Q+2}{2Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q+2}{4}},\,\left(\frac{1}{Q+2}\right)^{\frac{Q+2}{2Q}}\|e_{1}\|_{2^{*}_{Q}}\left[\frac{Q}{Q+2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\right)S_{\mathbb{G}}\right]^{\frac{Q+2}{4}}\right\}.

In addition to condition (
4.2
), we assume that
f
satisfies

(4.8)

\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq K_{1},\qquad-\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\ d\xi<K_{2}.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume that conditions (
4.2
) and (
4.8
) are satisfied.
Then there exists a constant
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

F(v)\geq\alpha_{0}>F(0),

for every
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
with
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sigma_{0}
, where

\sigma_{0}=\left[\frac{Q}{Q+2}\left(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\right)\right]^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}.

Proof.

Let
v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
. Since
t(v)
maximizes
t\mapsto J(v+te_{1})
, we have

F(v)=J(v+t(v)e_{1})\geq J(v).

Using the spectral inequality

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi\geq\lambda_{2}\int_{\Omega}v^{2}\,d\xi,\qquad\forall v\in\mathcal{S}^{+},

we obtain

\displaystyle F(v)

\displaystyle\geq J(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}-\lambda_{1}v^{2}\big)\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}fv\,d\xi

(4.9)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi-\|f\|_{2}\|v\|_{2}.

By the Sobolev inequality

\|v\|_{L^{2^{*}_{Q}}(\Omega)}\leq S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-\frac{1}{2}}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)},

and the setting

\sigma\coloneq\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}
, we deduce from
(
4.9
) that

F(v)\geq\Phi(\sigma)

where

\Phi(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)\sigma^{2}-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{-\frac{Q}{Q-2}}\sigma^{2^{*}_{Q}}-\|f\|_{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1/2}\sigma,\qquad\sigma\geq 0.

A direct computation shows that
\Phi
attains its maximum at

\sigma_{0}=\left[\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)\right]^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}},

Evaluating
\Phi
at
\sigma_{0}
yields

\Phi(\sigma_{0})=\sigma_{0}\left[\frac{2}{Q+2}\Big(\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{Q+2}{4}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}-\|f\|_{2}\lambda_{2}^{-1/2}\right].

By the smallness assumption
\|f\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq K_{1}
in (
4.8
) and using (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\Phi(\sigma_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{0}\left[\frac{2}{Q+2}\Big(\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{Q+2}{4}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}-K_{1}\lambda_{2}^{-1/2}\right]

\displaystyle\geq\sigma_{0}\left[\frac{1}{Q+2}\Big(\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big)^{\frac{Q-2}{4}}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{Q+2}{4}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{4}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{Q+2}\Big(\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big)^{\frac{Q-2}{2}}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)^{\frac{Q}{2}}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{\frac{Q}{2}}\coloneq\alpha_{0}>0.

From (
4.9
), we obtain

F(v)\geq\alpha_{0}\quad\text{ for all }v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\text{ with }\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sigma_{0}.

Using the explicit expression of

F(0)
in (
4.5
) together with the second smallness condition in (
4.8
), we obtain

\displaystyle F(0)\leq\frac{1}{2Q}\left[\frac{Q}{Q+2}\Big(1-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{2}}\Big)S_{\mathbb{G}}\right]^{\frac{Q}{2}}

Consequently, under assumptions (
4.2
) and (
4.8
), there exists a constant
\alpha_{0}>0
such that

F(v)\geq\alpha_{0}>F(0),\qquad\forall\,v\in\mathcal{S}^{+}\text{ with }\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sigma_{0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

From assumption (
4.8
) we also obtain the estimate

(4.10)

F(0)<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

In view of Lemma
4.3
, the functional
F
exhibits a local geometric
structure on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
, which motivates the following constrained
minimization problem:

(4.11)

m\coloneq\min\bigl\{F(v):v\in B_{d}(0,\sigma_{0})\bigr\}.

Lemma 4.4
.

The minimization problem (
4.11
) admits a nontrivial solution

v_{0}\in B_{d}(0,\sigma_{0})
. Consequently, problem (
4.1
)
possesses a weak solution.

Proof.

From (
4.10
), we have

(4.12)

m<\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

Let
\{v_{n}\}\subset B_{d}(0,\sigma_{0})
be a minimizing sequence for (
4.11
). Since
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma_{0}
, up to a subsequence, there exists
v_{0}\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that

(4.13)

v_{n}\rightharpoonup v_{0}\text{ weakly in }S^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\quad v_{n}\to v_{0}\text{ in }L^{q}(\Omega),\ 2\leq q<2^{*}_{Q},\quad v_{n}\to v_{0}\text{ a.e. in }\Omega.

By weak lower semicontinuity,

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{0}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq\sigma_{0}.

Using Ekeland’s variational principle, we may assume that

F(v_{n})\to m,\qquad F^{\prime}(v_{n})\to 0\text{ as }n\to\infty.

This implies

(4.14)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}|^{2}-\lambda_{1}v_{n}^{2}\big)\ d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+t(v_{n})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}f(v_{n}+t(v_{n})e_{1})\ d\xi

\displaystyle=m+o(1),

(4.15)

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v_{n}\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi-\lambda_{1}v_{n}\ \phi\big)\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+t(v_{n})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}\phi\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}f\ \phi\ d\xi

\displaystyle=o(1),

for all
\phi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Passing to the limit in (
4.15
) and using (
4.13
) together with Lemma
4.2
, we find that
v_{0}
satisfies

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v_{0}=\lambda_{1}v_{0}+(v_{0}+t(v_{0})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}+f

in the weak sense, along with

\int_{\Omega}\big((v_{0}+t(v_{0})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}e_{1}+fe_{1}\big)\,d\xi=0.

It remains to show
v_{0}\neq 0
.
We first claim that

(4.16)

\lim_{n\to\infty}t(v_{n})=t(v_{0}).

Indeed, if not, suppose
\lim_{n}t(v_{n})=t_{1}\neq t(v_{0})
. Then by (
4.4
),

\int_{\Omega}(v_{n}+t(v_{n})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}e_{1}\ d\xi=-\int_{\Omega}fe_{1}\ d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}+t(v_{0})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}e_{1}\ d\xi,

which would yield

\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}+t_{1}e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}e_{1}\ d\xi=\int_{\Omega}(v_{0}+t(v_{0})e_{1})^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}e_{1}\ d\xi,

a contradiction. Thus (
4.16
) holds. Let
w_{n}\coloneq v_{n}-v_{0}
. Using the Brézis-Lieb lemma in (
4.14
) and (
4.15
), we obtain

(4.17)

\displaystyle F(v_{n})

\displaystyle=F(v_{0})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi-\frac{1}{2^{*}_{Q}}\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi=m+o(1),

(4.18)

\displaystyle F^{\prime}(v_{n})v_{n}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi-\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi=o(1).

Let
b\coloneq\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi\geq 0
. If
b=0
, then
v_{n}\to v_{0}
strongly and
v_{0}\neq 0
follows. Suppose
b>0
. By the Sobolev inequality and (
4.18
), we have

b=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w_{n}|^{2}\,d\xi\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\int_{\Omega}|w_{n}|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\,d\xi\right)^{2/2^{*}_{Q}}\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}b^{2/2^{*}_{Q}}.

That is

(4.19)

b\geq S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}.

Combining (
4.12
), (
4.17
) and (
4.19
), we obtain

m=\lim_{n\to\infty}F(v_{n})\geq F(v_{0})+\frac{1}{Q}b\geq F(v_{0})+\frac{1}{Q}S_{\mathbb{G}}^{Q/2}>F(v_{0})+m,

It follows that
F(v_{0})<0
. Since
F(0)>0
, this implies that
v_{0}\neq 0
. Finally, Lemma
4.3
ensures that
F(v)\geq\alpha_{0}>0
on the boundary
\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=\sigma_{0}
, so
v_{0}\in B_{d}(0,\sigma_{0})
. Hence
v_{0}
is a nontrivial minimizer of
F
and yields a weak solution of (
4.1
).
∎

5.
Proof of Theorem
1.3

In this section, we study the bifurcation of the set of solutions of (
1.1
). Let
u_{t}(\lambda)=u_{t}
denote the nonpositive solution obtained in Section
3
.
If
f=te_{1}+h
with
h\in\ker(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}-\lambda I)^{\perp}
, then
u_{t}(\lambda)
is well defined for all
\lambda\neq\lambda_{1}
. In the case
\lambda=\lambda_{k}
,
k\neq 1
, the set of solutions of (
1.1
) bifurcating from
(\lambda_{k},u_{t}(\lambda_{k}))
is equivalent to the set of solutions of (
3.3
) bifurcating from
(\lambda_{k},0)
.
Let

\mathcal{S}^{-}=\operatorname{span}\{e_{1},\dots,e_{k}\},\qquad\mathcal{S}^{+}=(\mathcal{S}^{-})^{\perp}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Every eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}
of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
gives rise to a bifurcation point
(\lambda_{k},0)
of (
3.3
). Consequently, Theorem
1.3
follows.

Proof.

The conclusion follows from an abstract bifurcation theorem due to Böhme
[
6
]
and Marino
[
34
]
, see also in
[
39
, Theorem 11.4]
. We consider a function
g:\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
is defined by

g(\lambda,s)=\psi(s)(s+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}+(1-\psi(s)),

with
\psi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfy

\psi(s)=1\quad\text{for }|s|\leq 1,\qquad\psi(s)=0\quad\text{for }|s|\geq 2,\qquad 0\leq\psi(s)\leq 1\ \ \forall s.

Then
g\in C^{1}
, and for bounded
\lambda
, we have
g(\lambda,s)=o(|s|)
as
s\to 0
.
Set

\Phi(v)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}G(\lambda,v)\ d\xi,\qquad\text{ where }G(\lambda,v)=\int_{0}^{v}g(\lambda,t)\ dt,

and
v\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.
It is standard to check that
\Phi\in C^{2}
.
A critical point
u
of
\Phi
restricted to the manifold

\mathcal{N}\coloneq\left\{u\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega):\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\ d\xi=r^{2}\right\}

is a weak solution of

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}u-g(\lambda,u)=\mu u

for some Lagrange multiplier
\mu
. Define the bilinear form

(Av,\phi)=\int_{\Omega}\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\cdot\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\ d\xi,\qquad B(v)\phi=\int_{\Omega}g(\lambda,v)\phi\ d\xi,

for
\phi\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Let
2<q<2^{*}_{Q}
and define
\Omega_{0}\coloneq\{\xi\in\Omega:|v(\xi)|\geq 2\}
for
v\in S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
|v|^{q}\geq 2^{q}
on
\Omega_{0}
, it follows that

\int_{\Omega}|v|^{q}\,d\xi\geq 2^{q}|\Omega_{0}|,\qquad\text{hence}\qquad|\Omega_{0}|\leq 2^{-q}\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q}.

Hence,

|B(v)\phi|\leq\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}}|v|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}|\phi|\ d\xi+\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\phi|\ d\xi.

On
\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}
, Hölder’s inequality together with the Sobolev embedding

S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(\Omega)
yields

\int_{\Omega\setminus\Omega_{0}}|v|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}|\phi|\,d\xi\leq\|v\|^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{L^{2(2^{*}_{Q}-1)}(\Omega)}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

On
\Omega_{0}
, using Hölder’s inequality and the estimate on
|\Omega_{0}|
, we have

\int_{\Omega_{0}}|\phi|\,d\xi\leq|\Omega_{0}|^{1/2}\|\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{q}(\Omega)}^{q/2}\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}.

Combining the above estimates and using again the Sobolev embedding,
we conclude that

|B(v)\phi|\leq C\left(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}+\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{q/2}\right)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}\phi\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=o(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})\quad\text{as }\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\to 0.

This shows
\|B(v)\|=o(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
. Therefore, by in
[
39
, Theorem 11.4]
, each eigenvalue of
-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}
is a bifurcation point of

(5.1)

-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}v-g(\lambda,v)=\lambda v.

Since
g(\lambda,v)=o(\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)})
and
\lambda
is bounded, it follows from (
5.1
) that

\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\leq C\|v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}=Cr.

Finally, regularity arguments imply that if
r
is sufficiently small, then

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}<1,\qquad g(\lambda,v)=(v+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Next, we show that the bifurcation branch bends locally to the left.

Proposition 5.2
.

If
(\lambda,v(\lambda))
, with
v(\lambda)\neq 0
, is a solution of (
3.3
) such that

\lambda\to\lambda_{k},\quad k\neq 1,\qquad v(\lambda)\to 0,

then
\lambda<\lambda_{k}
. Consequently, if
h\in\ker(-\Delta_{\mathbb{G}}-\lambda_{k}I)^{\perp}
and
(\lambda,u(\lambda))
, with
u(\lambda)\neq 0
, is a solution of (
1.1
) such that

\lambda\to\lambda_{k},\quad k\neq 1,\qquad u(\lambda)\to u_{t}(\lambda_{k}),

then necessarily
\lambda<\lambda_{k}
.

Proof.

Let
u=v+w
be a solution of (
3.3
), where
v\in\mathcal{S}^{-}
and
w\in\mathcal{S}^{+}
.
Multiplying (
3.3
) by
w-v
and integrating by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\big(|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w|^{2}-|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\big)\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(\lambda u+(u+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+})(w-v)\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}\Big[\lambda(w^{2}-v^{2})+(v+w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}(w-v)\Big]\ d\xi.

This implies

(5.2)

\Big(1-\tfrac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\Big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w|^{2}\ d\xi-\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\int_{\Omega}|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v|^{2}\ d\xi\leq\int_{\Omega}(v+w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}(w-v)\ d\xi.

By the convexity of the function
s\mapsto(s+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}-1}_{+}
and since
u_{t}
is non negative, we estimate

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}(v+w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}(w-v)\ d\xi

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}(v+w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}-1}_{+}(2w-u)\ d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(2w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi-\int_{\Omega}(u+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}(2w+u_{t}(\lambda))^{2^{*}_{Q}}_{+}\ d\xi\leq\int_{\Omega}|2w|^{2^{*}_{Q}}\ d\xi

(5.3)

\displaystyle\leq C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}}.

Combining (
5.2
) and (
5
), we obtain

\Bigg(\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\Big)-C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\Bigg)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}-\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}\Big)\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}v\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leq 0.

Suppose, by contradiction, that
\lambda\geq\lambda_{k}
.
Since
\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k}}-1\geq 0
and
u=v+w\neq 0
, we must have
w\neq 0
.
Hence,

(5.4)

\Big(1-\frac{\lambda}{\lambda_{k+1}}\Big)\leq C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}w\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}\leq C\|\nabla_{\mathbb{G}}u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2^{*}_{Q}-2}.

Letting
\lambda\to\lambda_{k}
and
u\to 0
in
S^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, the left-hand side
of (
5.4
) converges to

1-\frac{\lambda_{k}}{\lambda_{k+1}}>0
, while the right-hand side tends to zero, which is impossible. Therefore, we must have
\lambda<\lambda_{k}
.
∎
