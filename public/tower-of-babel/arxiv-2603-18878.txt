Chain recurrent shifts on trees
Andrew Mortensen, David Walmsley

Abstract
We characterize when a weighted backward shift is chain recurrent on the ℓ p \ell^{p} , 1 ≤ p < ∞ 1\leq p<\infty and c 0 c_{0} spaces of a directed tree.

Introduction

For a map
f:X\to X
on a metric space
X
with metric

\delta
-pseudotrajectory
of
f
is a finite or infinite sequence
(x_{j})_{i<j<k}
in
X

-\infty\leq i<k\leq\infty
and

d(f(x_{j}),x_{j+1})\leq\delta\text{ \hskip 7.22743ptfor all \hskip 7.22743pt}i<j<k-1.

A finite
\delta
-pseudotrajectory of the form
(x_{j})_{j=0}^{k}
is also called a
\delta
-chain
for
f
from
x_{0}
to
x_{k}
, with
k
its length. The map
f
is called
chain recurrent
(resp.
chain transitive
) if for every
x\in X
(resp.
x,y\in X
) and every
\delta>0
, there is a
\delta
-chain for
f
from
x
to itself (resp. from
x
to
y
).
Furthermore,
f
is called

if for every
x,y\in X
and every
\delta>0
, there exists
k_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
k\geq k_{0}
, there is a
\delta
-chain for
f
from
x
to

with length
k
. For a map

, let

be its set of chain recurrent points, specifically

-chain for
f
from

to itself

. Then
f
is chain recurrent if and only if
CR(f)=X
. Moreover, we say that
f
has the
positive shadowing property
if for every
\epsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
such that every
\delta
-pseudotrajectory
(x_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of

is
\epsilon

f
, that is, there exists
x\in X
such that

for all

. If

is bijective, then the shadowing property is defined by replacing the set
\mathbb{N}_{0}
by the set

The concepts of pseudotrajectories, shadowing, and chain recurrence trace their roots back to the foundational research of Conley

[

16

.

Only recently have the notions of shadowing and chain recurrence been investigated in the setting of linear dynamics

, and many interesting results have appeared. For example, the first examples of non-hyperbolic operators with the shadowing property were exhibited in

The example of

2
General background and definitions

2.1
Directed trees

As in
[
13
,
11
,
12
,
15
]
, a
directed tree

(V,E)
is a connected directed graph with a countable vertex set
V
and a set of directed edges
E\subset V\times V\setminus\{(v,v):v\in V\}
such that:

•

V
has no cycles;

•

each vertex
v\in V
has at most one
parent
, that is, a unique vertex denoted by
\parent(v)\in V
such that
(\parent(v),v)\in E
;

•

there is at most one vertex with no parent, called the
root
and denoted by
root
when it exists.

Any vertex whose parent is
v
is called a
child
of
v
, and the set of children of
v
is denoted by
\Child(v)
. Inductively, we define
\Child^{0}(v)=\{v\}
, and for
n\geq 1
,
\Child^{n}(v)
is the union of the sets of children of each vertex in
\Child^{n-1}(v)
. A vertex with no children is called a
leaf
. If the tree has a root, we call
\Child^{n}(\verb|root|)
the
n
th generation
of the tree,
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, and denote the
n
th generation by
\Gen_{n}
. Observe that the generations of a rooted tree partition the vertex set. Following
[
11
]
, for an unrooted tree, we fix a vertex
v_{0}\in V
and define, for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the
n
th generation
with respect to
v_{0}
as

\displaystyle\Gen_{n}=\Gen_{n}(v_{0})=\{v\in V:\exists m\geq\max(-n,0)\text{ such that }\parent^{n+m}(v)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})\}.

It is not difficult to see that the generations of an unrooted tree also partition the vertex set. We prove this with a quick lemma.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed leafless tree, and fix
v_{0}\in V
so as to enumerate the generations of the tree. If
k<n
, then

\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v)=\Gen_{n}\text{ and }\Gen_{k}=\bigcup_{w\in\Gen_{n}}\{\parent^{n-k}(w)\}.

Consequently,
|\Gen_{k}|\leq|\Gen_{n}|
whenever
k<n
.

Proof.

To show
\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v)\subseteq\Gen_{n}
, let
u\in\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v).
Then there exists
v\in\Gen_{k}
for which
v=\parent^{n-k}(u)
. Since
v\in\Gen_{k}
, there exists an integer
m\geq\max(-k,0)
for which
\parent^{m+k}(v)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})
. Substituting
v=\parent^{n-k}(u)
into the previous expression and simplifying yields
\parent^{m+n}(u)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})
, and since
m\geq\max(-k,0)\geq\max(-n,0)
, we have that
u\in\Gen_{n}
.

To show reverse containment
\Gen_{n}\subseteq\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v)
, let
u\in\Gen_{n}
, which implies the existence of an integer
m\geq\max(-n,0)
such that
\parent^{m+n}(u)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})
. Let
v=\parent^{n-k}(u)
and
l=m+\max(-k,0)
. Then
l\geq\max(-k,0)
, and we compute that

\displaystyle\parent^{l+k}(v)=\parent^{l+n}(u)=\parent^{l-m}(\parent^{m+n}(u))=\parent^{l-m}(\parent^{m}(v_{0}))=\parent^{l}(v_{0}).

Hence
v\in\Gen_{k}
, and
v=\parent^{n-k}(u)
implies
u\in\Child^{n-k}(v)
, which establishes the reverse containment and shows
\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v)=\Gen_{n}
. The set equality
\Gen_{k}=\bigcup_{w\in\Gen_{n}}\{\parent^{n-k}(w)\}
can be proved with a similar argument.

Since the tree is leafless, each vertex has at least one child, and we can deduce from the
\bigcup_{v\in\Gen_{k}}\Child^{n-k}(v)=\Gen_{n}
that
|\Gen_{k}|\leq|\Gen_{n}|
whenever
k<n
.
∎

Every vertex in an unrooted tree has a unique parent, and thus the above lemma shows that for any
v_{0}\in V
, the generations with respect to
v_{0}
partition the vertex set of an unrooted tree. The above lemma also allows us to more easily imagine our trees to be directed from left to right.

We say an unrooted tree has a
free left end
if there is some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
such that
\Gen_{k}
is a singleton for all
k\leq n
. There is a simple criterion to check if a tree has a free left end.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted directed leafless tree, and fix
v_{0}\in V
so as to enumerate the generations of the tree. Then
V
has a free left end if and only if there exists
n\in\mathbb{Z}
for which
|\Gen_{n}|<\infty
.

Proof.

The (
\Rightarrow
) direction is obvious. For the (
\Leftarrow
) direction, suppose
|\Gen_{n}|<\infty
for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
. If
|\Gen_{n}|=1
, then for every
k<n
, Lemma
2.1
implies
|\Gen_{k}|\leq|\Gen_{n}|=1
, which shows
V
has a free left end. So suppose
1<|\Gen_{n}|<\infty
. It suffices to prove the existence of an integer
k<n
such that
|\Gen_{k}|<|\Gen_{n}|
.

Since
|\Gen_{n}|>1
, there exist vertices
u,v\in\Gen_{n}
with
u\neq v
and integers
l\geq\max(-n,0)
and
m\geq\max(-n,0)
such that

\displaystyle\parent^{m+n}(v)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})\text{ and }\parent^{l+n}(u)=\parent^{l}(v_{0}).

Without loss of generality, we may assume
m\geq l
. Then
\parent^{m+n}(u)=\parent^{m}(v_{0})
as well, since

\displaystyle\parent^{m+n}(u)=\parent^{m-l}(\parent^{l+n}(u))=\parent^{m-l}(\parent^{l}(v_{0}))=\parent^{m}(v_{0}).

It is impossible for
m
to equal
-n
, since that would imply, by the above equations, that
u=\parent^{m}(v_{0})=v
. Then since
m\geq\max(-n,0)
, we can deduce that
m>-n
.

Since
\parent^{m}(v_{0})\in\Gen_{-m}
, we have

\displaystyle\{u,v\}\subseteq\Child^{n+m}(\parent^{m}(v_{0}))\subseteq\Gen_{n},

where the last set containment follows from Lemma
2.1
. Thus the mapping
g:\Gen_{n}\to\Gen_{-m}
given by
g(w)=\parent^{n+m}(w)
is not an injective mapping, since
u,v\in\Gen_{n}
and
g(u)=g(v)
. But the map
g
is surjective by Lemma
2.1
, and the existence of a non-injective surjection between the finite sets
\Gen_{n}
and
\Gen_{-m}
shows
|\Gen_{-m}|<|\Gen_{n}|
.
∎

2.2
Sequence spaces and weighted shifts on directed trees

Let
\mathbb{K}
denote either
\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
and let
V
be an arbitrary finite or countable set. We denote by
\mathbb{K}^{V}
the space of all real or complex sequences
f=(f(v))_{v\in V}
over
V
, and endow
\mathbb{K}^{V}
with the product topology. The canonical unit sequences
are denoted by
e_{v}=\chi_{\{v\}}
,
v\in V
; that is,

\displaystyle e_{v}(u):=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }u=v\\
0&\text{if }u\neq v\\
\end{cases}

A subspace of
\mathbb{K}^{V}
is called a Banach sequence space over
V
if it is endowed with a Banach space topology for which the canonical embedding into
\mathbb{K}^{V}
is continuous.

Our focus is on the following Banach sequence spaces over
V
:

\displaystyle\ell^{p}(V)=\left\{f\in\mathbb{K}^{V}:\|f\|_{p}\coloneq\sum_{v\in V}|f(v)|^{p}<\infty\right\},\,1\leq p<\infty,

with the typical modification for
\ell^{\infty}(V)
, and

\displaystyle c_{0}(V)=\left\{f\in\mathbb{K}^{V}:\forall\varepsilon>0,\ \exists F\subset V\text{ finite},\,\forall v\in V\setminus F,\ |f(v)|<\varepsilon\right\},

which is endowed with the canonical norm of
\ell^{\infty}(V)
.

Now suppose
(V,E)
is a directed tree and we have chosen a sequence
\lambda\coloneq(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}
of non-zero scalars called
weights
. Then the associated
weighted backward shift

B_{\lambda}
on
V
is formally defined by

\displaystyle[B_{\lambda}(f)](v)\coloneq\sum_{u\in\Child(v)}\lambda_{u}f(u),\text{ for each }f=(f(v))_{v\in V}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}\text{ and each }v\in V.

As a consequence of the closed graph theorem,
B_{\lambda}
is continuous on a Banach sequence space
X
once
B_{\lambda}:X\to X
is well defined. Characterizing conditions for the continuity of
B_{\lambda}
on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq p<\infty
and on
c_{0}(V)
can be found in
[
11
, Proposition 2.3]
.

As in
[
11
]
, for simplicity in discussions of repeated shifts
B_{\lambda}^{n}
, for
v\in V
and
u\in\Child^{n}(v)
with
n\geq 1
, we define
\lambda(v\to u)\coloneq\lambda_{\parent^{(n-1)}(u)}\dots\lambda_{\parent(u)}\lambda_{u}
. Thus
\lambda(v\to u)
is the product of weights
\lambda_{w}
for each vertex
w
along the unique path from
v
to
u
. Repeated applications of
B_{\lambda}
yields that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle[B_{\lambda}^{n}(f)](v)=\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}\lambda(v\to u)f(u),\text{ for each }f=(f(v))_{v\in V}\in\mathbb{K}^{V}\text{ and each }v\in V.

(1)

When
n=0
, we interpret
B_{\lambda}^{n}
as the identity operator. Hence by defining
\lambda(v\to v)\coloneq 1
for each
v\in V
and recalling
\Child^{0}(v)=\{v\}
, the above formula holds when
n=0
, as well.

3
Characterization of recurrent backward shifts on trees

We need a lemma to prepare for our main result.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
V
be a finite or countable set and let
X
be
c_{0}
or one of the
\ell^{p}(V)
spaces. The following statements hold for every linear operator
T:X\to X
.

(a)

Let
(f_{l})_{l=0}^{n}
be a finite sequence in
X
. Then
(f_{l})_{l=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-chain for
T
if and only if the finite sequence
g_{l}\coloneq f_{l}-T(f_{l-1})
,
1\leq l\leq n
, fulfills that
f_{n}=T^{n}(f_{0})+\sum_{l=1}^{n}T^{n-l}(g_{l})
and
\|g_{l}\|<\delta
for every
1\leq l\leq n
.

(b)

T
is chain recurrent if and only if for every canonical basis vector
e_{v}\in X
and every
\delta>0
, there is a
\delta
-chain for
T
from
e_{v}
to
0
and a
\delta
-chain for
T
from
0
to
e_{v}
.

Proof.

The (
\Rightarrow
) direction of statement (a) is an immediate consequence of
[
15
, Lemma 2.2(a)]
and its proof. For the
(\Leftarrow
) direction,
(f_{l})_{l=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-chain for
T
because for every
1\leq l\leq n
, the relationship
g_{l}=f_{l}-T(f_{l-1})
implies
\|f_{l}-T(f_{l-1})\|=\|g_{l}\|<\delta.

The (
\Rightarrow
) direction of statement (b) follows immediately from the fact that chain recurrence and chain transitivity are equivalent notions for the linear operator
T
. For the (
\Leftarrow
) direction, since
T(0)=0
, we can concatenate a
\delta
-chain from
e_{v}
to
0
with another
\delta
-chain from
0
to
e_{v}
to create a
\delta
-chain from
e_{v}
to
e_{v}
. Hence every basis vector
e_{v}
is a chain recurrent vector for
T
. Since the set of chain recurrent vectors for
T
is a closed subspace of
X
by
[
6
, Lemma 8 and Proposition 26]
, the set of chain recurrent vectors must contain
\overline{\Span\{e_{v}:v\in V\}}=X
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The results cited in the above proof hold in more general settings than just Banach sequence spaces. Consequently, Lemma
3.1
holds in more generality than stated. For example, both statements hold for any Fréchet sequence space
X
in which the canonical vectors
(e_{v})_{v\in V}
form an unconditional basis. Since we do not need this level of generality for our purposes, we omit the details.

For
1<p<\infty
, let
p^{*}
be the unique number in
(1,\infty)
for which
1/p+1/p^{*}=1
. Since the unrooted case is more involved, we present it first.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted leafless directed tree and
\lambda=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
a weight. We have the following:

(a)

Let
X=\ell^{1}(V)
, and let
B_{\lambda}
be bounded on
X
. Then
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent if and only if

(i)

\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\sup_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\rightarrow u)|=\infty
, and

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sup_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|=\infty
.

(b)

Let
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, and let
B_{\lambda}
be bounded on
X
. Then
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent if and only if

(i)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}=\infty
, and

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}=\infty
.

(c)

Let
X=c_{0}(V)
, and let
B_{\lambda}
be bounded on
X
. Then
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent if and only if

(i)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|=\infty
, and

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|=\infty
.

Proof.

We do the proof of (b) involving
X=\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
, since the other cases are similar and slightly easier.

(
\Rightarrow
) Suppose
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent. Let
v\in V
and
t>1
. Since
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent, there must be a 1-chain from
te_{v}
to itself of the form

\displaystyle te_{v}=f_{0},f_{1},...,f_{n}=te_{v}.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exist vectors
g_{l}\in\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq l\leq n
, such that
||g_{l}||_{p}<1
for every
l=1,2,...,n
and,

\displaystyle te_{v}=B_{\lambda}^{n}(te_{v})+\sum_{l=1}^{n}B_{\lambda}^{n-l}(g_{l}).

Evaluating both sides of the previous at expression at vertex
v
, using (
1
) for the right-hand side, gives us

\displaystyle t=0+\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}\lambda(v\to u)g_{l}(u).

The triangle inequality then yields

\displaystyle t\leq\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)g_{l}(u)|,

and by viewing the double sum as an
\ell^{1}
-norm, we can apply Hölder’s Inequality to obtain:

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}|g_{l}(u)|^{p}\bigg)^{1/p}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}\|g_{l}\|_{p}

\displaystyle<\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{l}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}.

Since
t>1
can be arbitrarily large, we must have
\big(\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}\big)^{1/p^{*}}=\infty
, which implies
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}=\infty
, and since
\Child^{0}(v)=\{v\}
, we must have
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}=\infty
. This verifies the first characterizing condition listed in the theorem.

The other condition is derived similarly. Since
B_{\lambda}
is chain transitive, there must be a
1
-chain from
-te_{v}
to
0
of the form

\displaystyle-te_{v}=p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}=0.

By Lemma
3.1
, there exist vectors
q_{j}\in\ell^{p}(V)
,
1\leq j\leq m
, such that
||q_{j}||_{p}<1
for every
j=1,2,...,m
, and,

\displaystyle 0=B_{\lambda}^{m}(p_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{\lambda}^{m-j}(q_{j}).

Since the sequence
B_{\lambda}^{m}(p_{0})=-t\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}
, evaluating both sides of the previous expression at the vertex
\parent^{m}(v)
and rearranging yields

\displaystyle t\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)q_{j}(u).

Thus the triangle inequality yields

\displaystyle t\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}q_{j}(u)\bigg|.

Similar to earlier, Hölder’s Inequality implies

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}},

from which the second characterizing condition in the theorem follows, since
t>1
can be arbitrarily large and the exponent
1/p^{*}
does not affect the limit being
\infty
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Assume that

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}=\infty\text{ and }\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(v\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}=\infty.

By Lemma
3.1
, it suffices to show that for every
\delta>0
and every
v\in V
, there is a
\delta
-chain from
0_{X}
to
e_{v}
and a
\delta
-chain from
e_{v}
to
0_{X}
. We first find a
\delta
-chain from
0_{X}
to
e_{v}
of the form

0_{X}=f_{0},f_{1},\ldots,f_{n}=e_{v}.

To begin, we claim that

\displaystyle\sup\bigg\{\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)\sigma(u)|:\sigma\in\ell^{p}(V),\|\sigma\|_{p}\leq 1\bigg\}=\infty.

(2)

Towards a contradiction, suppose this wasn’t the case. Then the linear functional
h:\ell^{p}(V)\to\mathbb{K}
defined by
h(\sigma)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}\lambda(v\to u)\sigma(u)
would be continuous since its operator norm

\displaystyle\|h\|\coloneq\sup\{|h(\sigma)|:\|\sigma\|_{p}\leq 1\}

is finite. Then
h
belongs to the dual space of
\ell^{p}(V)
, and since the dual space of
\ell^{p}(V)
is isometrically isomorphic to
\ell^{p^{*}}(V)
, we must have
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)|^{p^{*}}<\infty
, which contradicts our assumption that that sum is
\infty
.

As a consequence of (
2
), there exists a vector
\sigma\in\ell^{p}(V)
with
\|\sigma\|_{p}\leq 1
such that

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n}(v)}|\lambda(v\to u)\sigma(u)|>\frac{1}{\delta}-1.

Since rotating any value of
\sigma(u)
does not change the norm of
\sigma
, by redefining
\sigma
if necessary so as to rotate each value
\sigma(u)
to make
\lambda(v\to u)\sigma(u)
a non-negative real number, we may choose a positive integer
n
large enough so that the number

t:=\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{l}(v)}\lambda(v\to u)\sigma(u)>\frac{1}{\delta}.

For
l\in\{1,\ldots,n\}
, we introduce the vectors

\displaystyle g_{l}:=\frac{1}{t}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}\sigma(u)e_{u}.

Then
\|g_{l}\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{t}\|\sigma\|_{p}<\delta
for every
1\leq l\leq n
, so by setting
f_{0}\coloneq 0_{X}
and
f_{l}\coloneq B_{\lambda}(f_{l-1})+g_{l}
for each
1\leq l\leq n
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
(f_{l})_{l=0}^{n}
is a
\delta
-chain from
0_{X}
to
f_{n}
, and furthermore

\displaystyle f_{n}=B_{\lambda}^{n}(f_{0})+\sum_{l=1}^{n}B^{n-l}_{\lambda}(g_{l}).

To establish that
f_{0},\ldots,f_{n}
is a
\delta
-chain from
0_{X}
to
e_{v}
, what remains to show is
f_{n}=e_{v}
.

Observe that
B_{\lambda}(f_{0})=0_{X}
and for any vertex
u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)
, we have
B_{\lambda}^{n-l}(e_{u})=\lambda(v\to u)e_{v}
. Thus our equation for
f_{n}
becomes

\displaystyle f_{n}=\bigg(\sum_{l=1}^{n}\sum_{u\in\Child^{n-l}(v)}\frac{1}{t}\sigma(u)\lambda(v\to u)e_{v}\bigg)=\frac{1}{t}e_{v}\bigg(\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{l}(v)}\sigma(u)\lambda(v\to u)\bigg)=\frac{t}{t}e_{v}=e_{v},

as desired.

Our last task is to find a
\delta
-chain from
0
to
e_{v}
of the form

p_{0},p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}.

We claim there there exists
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
\gamma\in\ell^{p}(V)
with
\|\gamma\|_{p}\leq 1
for which

\displaystyle s\coloneq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\gamma(u)>\frac{1}{\delta}.

(3)

To establish (
3
), it suffices to show there exists
\gamma\in\ell^{p}(V)
with
\|\gamma\|_{p}\leq 1
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{n}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\gamma(u)\bigg|>\frac{1}{\delta},

(4)

since we can rotate each value of
\gamma
, if necessary, to ensure the sum in (
3
) involves only non-negative numbers.

To see that (
4
) holds, consider the family of linear functionals
T_{m}:\ell^{p}(V)\to\mathbb{C}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, defined by

\displaystyle T_{m}(\sigma)=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\sigma(u).

If there exists some
m
for which
T_{m}
is not continuous, then the operator norm
\|T_{m}\|
must be
\infty
, which immediately implies the existence of
\gamma\in\ell^{p}(V)
with
\|\gamma\|_{p}\leq 1
for which (
4
) holds.

Now suppose each
T_{m}
is continuous, which implies
T_{m}\in(\ell^{p}(V))^{*}
. Since the dual space of
\ell^{p}(V)
is isometrically isomorphic to
\ell^{p^{*}}(V)
, the operator norm of each
T_{m}
must be

\displaystyle\|T_{m}\|

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}

\displaystyle=\bigg(\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}\sum_{u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\bigg|^{p^{*}}\bigg)^{1/p^{*}}.

Then by assumption,
\|T_{m}\|\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
. Hence the family
(T_{m})_{m}
is not uniformly bounded, so by the uniform boundedness principle, there must exist some
\gamma\in\ell^{p}(V)
with
\|\gamma\|_{p}\leq 1
such that
\sup_{m}\|T_{m}\gamma\|=\infty
, which implies
\sup_{m}\|T_{m}\gamma\|^{p^{*}}=\infty
as well. This finishes the proof that (
4
) holds, which also establishes (
3
).

For
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
, we introduce the vectors

\displaystyle q_{j}:=-\frac{1}{s}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\gamma(u)e_{u}.

Then
\|q_{j}\|_{p}\leq\frac{1}{s}\|\gamma\|_{p}<\delta
for every
1\leq j\leq m
, so by setting
p_{0}\coloneq e_{v}
and
p_{j}\coloneq B_{\lambda}(p_{j-1})+q_{j}
, Lemma
3.1
implies that
(p_{j})_{j=0}^{m}
is a
\delta
-chain from
p_{0}=e_{v}
to
p_{m}
, and

\displaystyle p_{m}=B_{\lambda}^{m}(p_{0})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}B_{\lambda}^{m-j}(q_{j}).

What remains to check is that
p_{m}=0_{X}
.

Observe that
B_{\lambda}^{m}(p_{0})=\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}
and for any vertex
u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))
, we have
B_{\lambda}^{m-j}(e_{u})=\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}
. Thus our equation for
p_{m}
becomes

\displaystyle p_{m}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}-\frac{1}{s}\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)\gamma(u)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}

\displaystyle=\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}\bigg(1-\frac{1}{s}\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{m}\sum_{u\in\Child^{m-j}(\parent^{m}(v))}\frac{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)}\gamma(u)\bigg)\bigg)

\displaystyle=\lambda(\parent^{m}(v)\to v)e_{\parent^{m}(v)}(1-\frac{s}{s})

\displaystyle=0_{X},

which finishes the proof that
p_{0},\ldots,p_{m}
is a
\delta
-chain from
e_{v}
to
0_{X}
.
∎

Example 3.5
.

The simplest weighted shift to consider is when all the weights are the same, which is to consider a scalar mulitple of the unweighted backward shift
B
. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
be non-zero. Then the weight sequence defined by
\lambda_{v}=\lambda
for each
v\in V
corresponds to the
Rolewicz operator

\lambda B
, named after the important work of Rolewicz in
[
18
]
. As shown in
[
11
, Example 2.5]
, any Rolewicz operator is bounded on
\ell^{1}(V)
, while it is bounded on
\ell^{p}(V)
,
1<p<\infty
or on
c_{0}(V)
if and only if
\sup_{v\in V}|\Child(v)|<\infty
.

The conditions in Theorem
3.3
can be simplified when considering a Rolewicz operator. In this case, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, we have

•

u\in\Child^{n}(v)\Rightarrow|\lambda(v\to u)|=|\lambda|^{n}
;

•

u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))\Rightarrow\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|=\frac{|\lambda|^{j}}{|\lambda|^{n}}=|\lambda|^{j-n}
.

As a consequence, we have the following two results.

Corollary 3.6
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted leafless directed tree with no leaves. Let
\lambda\in\mathbb{K}
be non-zero and
\lambda B
the corresponding Rolewicz operator. We have the following:

(a)

The operator
\lambda B
is chain recurrent on
\ell^{1}(V)
if and only if
|\lambda|=1
.

(b)

Let
X=\ell^{p}(V),1<p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and let
\lambda B
be bounded on
X
. Then
\lambda B
is chain recurrent on
X
if and only if

(i)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\Child^{n}(v)||\lambda|^{np^{*}}=\infty
, and

(ii)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n}|\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))||\lambda|^{(j-n)p^{*}}=\infty
.

If, additionally, the tree has a free left end, then condition (ii) is equivalent to demanding
|\lambda|\leq 1
.

Here,
p^{*}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

Proof.

Simplifying the conditions in Theorem
3.3
(a) shows that
\lambda B
is chain recurrent on
\ell^{1}(V)
if and only if
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}|\lambda|^{n}=\infty
and
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|\lambda|^{j-n}=\infty
, which happens if and only if
|\lambda|=1
.

To prove (b), let
X=\ell^{p}(V),1<p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
. Simplifying the conditions in Theorem
3.3
(parts (b) and (c)) show that
\lambda B
is chain recurrent if and only if conditions (i) and (ii) above hold. If the tree has a free left end, then there are vertices
v
for which
\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))=\{\parent^{n-j}(v)\}
for every

. For such a vertex

, we have
\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))||\lambda|^{(j-n)p^{*}}=\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}|\lambda|^{(j-n)p^{*}}
, which approaches
\infty
if and only if
|\lambda|\leq 1
.
∎

Example 3.8
.

Let
(V,E)
be a directed tree and fix
v_{0}
so as to enumerate the generations of the tree, picking
v_{0}=\verb|root|
in the rooted case and picking an arbitrary vertex
v_{0}\in V
otherwise. A weight
\lambda=(\lambda_{v})_{v\in V}
is called
symmetric
if any two vertices belonging to the same generation have identical corresponding weights. For
v\in\Gen_{n}
, we can then write
\lambda_{n}=\lambda_{v}
. We say that a directed tree
(V,E)
is
symmetric
if any two vertices belonging to the same generation have the same number of children. For
v\in\Gen_{n}
of a symmetric tree, we set
\gamma_{n}=|\Child^{n}(v)|
.

The class of symmetric weighted backward shifts on symmetric trees form another notable class of examples, since they generalize the classical weighted backward shifts on
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{Z}
. Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted leafless directed symmetric tree, and fix
v_{0}
to enumerate the generations of the tree. Then for some
v\in\Gen_{m}
, the following computations will help us simplify the conditions in Theorem
3.3
.

•

u\in\Child^{n}(v)\Rightarrow|\lambda(v\to u)|=\prod_{i=m+1}^{m+n}|\lambda_{i}|
;

•

u\in\Child^{j}(\parent^{n}(v))\Rightarrow\bigg|\frac{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to u)}{\lambda(\parent^{n}(v)\to v)}\bigg|=\frac{|\lambda_{m-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{m-n+j}|}{|\lambda_{m-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}=\prod_{i=-n+j+1}^{0}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m+i}|}
.

We need a simple lemma as well.

Lemma 3.9
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a (two-tailed) sequence of positive terms. Then the following are equivalent.

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}=\infty
;

(ii)

for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=m}^{m+n-1}a_{i}=\infty
.

Furthermore, the following are equivalent as well.

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{-i}=\infty
;

(iv)

for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=m-n+1}^{m}a_{i}=\infty
.

Proof.

We only prove (i)
\Leftrightarrow
(ii) since the proof of (iii)
\Leftrightarrow
(iv) is essentially the same. For an integer
k>1
, the
k
th partial sums of the series in (i) and (ii) satisfy

\displaystyle\text{for }m>1,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{k}\prod_{i=m}^{m+n-1}a_{i}=\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdots a_{m-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{m+k-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}-\frac{1}{a_{1}\cdots a_{m-1}}\sum_{n=1}^{m-1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\text{for }m<1,

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{k}\prod_{i=m}^{m+n-1}a_{i}=\sum_{n=1}^{-m+1}\prod_{i=m}^{m+n-1}a_{i}+\prod_{i=m}^{0}a_{i}\sum_{n=1}^{k-m+1}\prod_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}.

from which the equivalence of (i) and (ii) can be seen.
∎

We use this lemma and the two facts preceding it to prove the following two results.

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
(V,E)
be an unrooted leafless symmetric directed tree and
\lambda=(\lambda_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
be a symmetric weight on
V
. We have the following:

(a)

A continuous symmetric shift
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent on
\ell^{1}(V)
if and only if both

(i)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{i}|=\infty
, and

(ii)

\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}|\lambda_{-i}|=\infty
.

(b)

Let
X=\ell^{p}(V),1<p<\infty
, or
X=c_{0}(V)
, and let
B_{\lambda}
be bounded on
X
. Then
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent on
X
if and only if

(i)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i-1}|\lambda_{i}|^{p^{*}}=\infty
, and

(ii)

for each
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\gamma_{m-n}\cdots\gamma_{m-n+j-1}}{|\lambda_{m-n+j+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}\bigg)=\infty
.

If, additionally, the tree has a free left end, then condition (ii) is equivalent to demanding

(iii)

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{-i}|}=\infty
.

Here,
p^{*}=1
for
X=c_{0}(V)
.

Proof.

In the case of
\ell^{1}(V)
in part (a), after simplifying the two conditions in Theorem 3.3(a) simplify to, we see that
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent if and only if for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(i’)

\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\prod_{i=m+1}^{m+n}|\lambda_{i}|=\infty
, and

(ii’)

\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\prod_{i=m-n+j+1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{i}|}=\infty
.

Since
\lim_{n\to\infty}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\prod_{i=m-n+j+1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{i}|}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=m-n+1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{i}|}
, Lemma
3.9
shows conditions (i’) and (ii’) are equivalent to (i) and (ii) in the statement of Corollary
3.10
(a).

For case (b), let
v\in V
and suppose
v\in\Gen_{m}
, where
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. By the symmetry of the tree, we have
|\Child^{n}(v)|=\gamma_{m}\cdots\gamma_{m+n-1}
for
n\geq 1
. Consequently, we see from Theorem
3.3
, after some simplification based on the remarks before Lemma
3.9
, that
B_{\lambda}
is chain recurrent on
X
if and only if for every
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, both

(i’)

\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=m+1}^{m+n}\gamma_{i-1}|\lambda_{i}|^{p^{*}}=\infty
, and

(ii)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{\gamma_{m-n}\cdots\gamma_{m-n+j-1}}{|\lambda_{m-n+j+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}\bigg)=\infty
.

By Lemma
3.9
, condition (i’) is equivalent to
\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\gamma_{i-1}|\lambda_{i}|^{p^{*}}=\infty
, while (ii) matches what we claimed.

Finally, if the tree has a free left end, then for any
m\in\mathbb{Z}
, the products
\gamma_{m-n}\cdots\gamma_{m-n+j-1}
are uniformly bounded above for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
1\leq j\leq n
, and they are uniformly bounded below by
1
. Thus condition (ii) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\bigg(\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m-n+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}+\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{m-n+j+1}\cdots\lambda_{m}|}\bigg)=\infty,

which is equivalent to
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=m-n-1}^{m}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{i}|}=\infty
, which by Lemma
3.9
is equivalent to
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\prod_{i=0}^{n}\frac{1}{|\lambda_{-i}|}=\infty
.
∎
