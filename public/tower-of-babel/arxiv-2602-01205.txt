Long-time asymptotics of (1,3)-sign solitary waves for the damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation
Kenjiro Ishizuka

Abstract
. We consider the damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation ∂ t 2 u − Δ ​ u + 2 ​ α ​ ∂ t u + u − | u | p − 1 ​ u = 0 , \displaystyle\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+2\alpha\partial_{t}u+u-|u|^{p-1}u=0,\ ( t , x ) ∈ ℝ × ℝ d , \displaystyle(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}, where α > 0 \alpha>0 , 2 ≤ d ≤ 5 2\leq d\leq 5 and energy sub-critical exponents p > 2 p>2 . In this paper, we prove that any solution that is asymptotic to a superposition of four solitons with exactly one soliton of opposite sign evolves so that the three like-signed solitons spread out in an equilateral-triangle configuration centered at the oppositely signed soliton.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Setting of the problem

We consider the following damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{aligned} &\partial_{t}^{2}u-\Delta u+2\alpha\partial_{t}u+u-f(u)=0,&(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d},\\
&\left(u(0,x),{\partial}_{t}u(0,x)\right)=\left(u_{0}(x),u_{1}(x)\right)\in\mathcal{H},\end{aligned}\right.

(DNKG)

where
2\leq d\leq 5
,
\mathcal{H}=H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
f(u)=|u|^{p-1}u
, with a power
p
in the energy sub-critical range, namely

\displaystyle 2<p<\infty\ \mbox{for}\ d=2\ \mbox{and}\ 2<p<\frac{d+2}{d-2}\ \mbox{for}\ d=3,4,5.

It follows from
[
4
]
that the Cauchy problem for (
DNKG
) is locally well-posed in the energy space
\mathcal{H}
. Moreover, defining the energy of a solution
\vec{u}=(u,{\partial}_{t}u)
by

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}(t))=\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{u}(t)\|_{\mathcal{H}}^{2}-\frac{1}{p+1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{p+1}}^{p+1},

we have

\displaystyle E(\vec{u}(t_{2}))-E(\vec{u}(t_{1}))=-2\alpha\int_{t_{1}}^{t_{2}}\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}dt.

(1.1)

In addition, the equation (
DNKG
) enjoys several invariances:

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} u(t,x)&\mapsto-u(t,x),\\
u(t,x)&\mapsto u(t,x+y),\\
u(t,x)&\mapsto u(t+s,x),\end{aligned}

(1.2)

where
y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
s\in\mathbb{R}
.

1.2.
Long-time asymptotics of multi-soliton solutions

In this paper, we are interested in the dynamics of multi-soliton solutions related to the ground state
Q
, which is the unique positive, radial
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
solution of

\displaystyle-\Delta Q+Q-f(Q)=0.

(1.3)

(See
[
31
]
). The ground state generates the stationary solution
(Q,0)
. By (
1.2
), the function
(-Q,0)
as well as any translate
(Q(\cdot+y),0)
is also a solution of (
DNKG
). There have been intensive studies on global behavior of general (large) solutions for nonlinear dispersive equations, where the guiding principle is the
soliton resolution conjecture
, which claims that generic global solutions are asymptotic to superposition of solitons for large time. For (
DNKG
), Feireisl
[
16
]
proved the soliton resolution along a time sequence: for any global bounded solution
\vec{u}
of (
DNKG
), there exist a time sequence
t_{n}\to\infty
and a set of stationary solutions
\{\varphi_{k}\}_{k=1}^{K}
(
K\geq 0
), and sequences
\{c_{k,n}\}_{k=1,2,\ldots,K,n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\|u(t_{n})-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\varphi_{k}(\cdot-c_{k,n})\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t_{n})\|_{L^{2}}\right)=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\left(\min_{j\neq k}|c_{j,n}-c_{k,n}|\right)=\infty.

In particular, in the
d=1
case, since the stationary solutions of (
DNKG
) consist only of translates of
\pm Q
, Côte, Martel, and Yuan
[
8
]
proved that if
\vec{u}
is a global solution of (
DNKG
), there exist a sign
\sigma=\pm 1
,
K\geq 0
, and functions
z_{k}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|u(t)-\sigma\sum_{k=1}^{K}(-1)^{k}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(z_{k+1}(t)-z_{k}(t)\right)

\displaystyle=\infty.

Prior to the present result, Côte, Martel, Yuan, and Zhao
[
9
]
proved in general space dimension that any two-soliton superposition built from
Q
must consist of solitons of opposite signs, and that the solitons move so that their centers evolve along a fixed straight line. In particular, this result makes the interaction forces acting on the solitons explicit, and suggests that like-signed solitons attract whereas oppositely signed solitons repel. Using this force law,
[
8
]
showed that in one space dimension, in a multi-soliton superposition the signs of neighboring solitons must be opposite.

On the other hand, when the space dimension is at least two, two types of difficulties arise. The first is that, in dimensions
d\geq 2
, there exist many different kinds of stationary solutions (here “different” means inequivalent even after allowing for spatial translations and sign changes). The second is that soliton configurations depend on relative directions as well as distances. When considering a superposition of copies of
Q
, each soliton feels an attractive or repulsive interaction from other nearby solitons. In
[
8
]
, thanks to the restriction to one space dimension, the centers of the solitons in such a superposition lie on a single line, so it suffices to examine the interactions with adjacent solitons. By contrast, in dimensions
d\geq 2
, three or more solitons can be simultaneously close, and one must analyze not only the distances but also the relative directions. Despite these difficulties, there has been notable recent progress on multi-soliton solutions that can be described as superpositions of copies of
Q
. As mentioned above, Côte, Martel, Yuan, and Zhao
[
9
]
showed that, in general space dimension, a two-soliton superposition experiences essentially no “rotational” (i.e., tangential) interaction, and that the inter-soliton force is attractive for like signs and repulsive for opposite signs. Subsequently, the author
[
20
]
showed that three-soliton superpositions are necessarily collinear. Indeed, it is known that any solution asymptotic to a superposition of three solitons must contain exactly one soliton whose sign is opposite to that of the other two (see
[
6
]
). In this case, unless the three solitons are collinear, the oppositely signed soliton is pushed outward by repulsion from the other two; consequently, the attraction between the like-signed solitons dominates the repulsion between oppositely signed solitons, and the configuration cannot sustain a three-soliton state. We also note that, as a constructive approach, Côte and Du
[
6
]
imposed symmetry conditions and constructed solutions whose soliton centers form highly symmetric configurations, such as an equilateral triangle or a regular tetrahedron.

1.3.
Main result

First, we define solutions that behave as the superposition of ground states.

Definition 1.1
.

A solution
\vec{u}
of (
DNKG
) is called a
K
-solitary waves
for
K\in\mathbb{N}
if there exist
\sigma\in\{-1,1\}^{K}
and
z:[0,\infty)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{K}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|u(t)-\sum_{k=1}^{K}\sigma_{k}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)&=0,\\
\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\min_{j\neq k}|z_{j}(t)-z_{k}(t)|\right)&=\infty.\end{aligned}

(1.4)

Remark 1.1
.

In space dimension
d=1
, thanks to the damping term
\alpha
, Definition
1.1
remains equivalent to the original definition even if it is replaced with convergence in the sense of time sequences
t_{n}\to\infty
. On the other hand, in space dimension
d\geq 2
, even if a solution converges along a sequence of times, it is not clear whether it remains uniformly bounded in
\mathcal{H}
for all
t
. Hence, the equivalence of the two notions is not known. In this paper, we are interested in the long-time behavior of the positions of solitons. Therefore, we adopt Definition
1.1
.

Remark 1.2
.

Hereafter, unless stated otherwise, when we discuss
K
-solitary waves we implicitly assume that it is a solution of (
DNKG
).

In particular, in this paper we focus on 4-solitary waves in which exactly one solitary wave has the opposite sign from the other three, that is, superpositions consisting of three solitons of one sign and one soliton of the other sign. Accordingly, in order to distinguish not only the number of solitary waves but also whether each component is a copy of
Q
or of
-Q
, we introduce the notion of
(m,n)
-sign solitary waves as follows.

Definition 1.2
.

A solution
\vec{u}
of (
DNKG
) is called a
(m,n)
-sign solitary waves
for
m,n\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
if there exist
z_{+}:[0,\infty)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{m}
and
z_{-}:[0,\infty)\to(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\|u(t)-\sum_{k=1}^{m}Q(\cdot-z_{+,k}(t))+\sum_{k=1}^{n}Q(\cdot-z_{-,k}(t))\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\right)&=0,\\
\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\min_{(\circ,j)\neq(\bullet,k)}|z_{\circ,j}(t)-z_{\bullet,k}(t)|\right)&=\infty.\end{aligned}

(1.5)

Remark 1.3
.

Since (
DNKG
) is invariant under the sign change
u\mapsto-u
, any property established for
(m,n)
-sign solitary waves solutions immediately yields the corresponding property for
(n,m)
-sign solitary waves solutions. It was also proved in
[
6
]
that no
(m,0)
-sign or
(0,m)
-sign solitary waves solutions exist.

Remark 1.4
.

Hereafter, unless stated otherwise, when we discuss
(m,n)
-sign solitary waves we implicitly assume that it is a solution of (
DNKG
).

In this paper, we are interested in
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves solutions. By imposing suitable symmetries, Côte and Du
[
6
]
constructed a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves for which the center of the
+Q
-soliton converges to a point, while the centers of the remaining three solitons spread out so as to form an expanding equilateral triangle. In the present work, we prove that this geometric behavior does not rely on any symmetry assumption: if a solution is a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves, then the center of the
+Q
-soliton converges, and the other three soliton centers diverge in an equilateral-triangle configuration with the
+Q
-soliton as the center of similarity.
Before stating our main theorem, we define spreading out in an equilateral-triangle configuration by introducing the following terminology.

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\in S^{d-1}
. We say that
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
form an
equilateral triple
if

\displaystyle\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}=0.

Remark 1.5
.

If
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\in S^{d-1}
form an equilateral triple, then they lie in a common
two-dimensional subspace of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
(indeed,
\omega_{3}=-(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})
).
Moreover,
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle|\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}|=|\omega_{2}-\omega_{3}|=|\omega_{3}-\omega_{1}|.

Then, our main result is as follows:

Theorem 1.1
.

If a solution
\vec{u}
of (
DNKG
) is a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves, there exist
z_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, an equilateral triple
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\in S^{d-1}
, and functions
z_{k}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
k=1,2,3
such that for all
t>2
,

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)-\left(Q(\cdot-z_{\infty})-\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-1},

(1.6)

and for
k=1,2,3
,

\displaystyle z_{k}(t)=z_{\infty}+\left(\log{t}-\frac{d-1}{2}\log{(\log{t})}+c_{0}\right)\omega_{k}+O\left(\frac{\log{(\log{t})}}{\log{t}}\right),

(1.7)

where
c_{0}
is a constant depending only on
d,\ \alpha
, and
p
.

Remark 1.6
.

If
\vec{u}
is a
(3,1)
-sign solitary waves, then
-\vec{u}
is a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves by (
1.2
). Therefore, by Theorem
1.1
, the long-time dynamics of the soliton centers for a
(3,1)
-sign solitary waves are the same as those for a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves.

Remark 1.7
.

When
d=1
,
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves solutions do not exist. This is because a
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves corresponds to a solution that spreads in three distinct directions; however, in the case
d=1
, there are only two possible directions (positive direction and negative direction).

Remark 1.8
.

To completely characterize the long-time dynamics of four solitary waves solutions, it is also necessary to understand the long-time behavior of
(2,2)
-sign solitary waves solutions.
In contrast to the
(1,3)
-sign case, the
(2,2)
-sign configuration has no distinguished (reference) soliton—there is no unique soliton whose sign differs from all the others—and this makes the asymptotic analysis more delicate.

1.4.
Difficulties and Ideas for the Main Result

Determining the long-time behavior of a four-soliton configuration is more difficult than in the case of a superposition of three or fewer solitons. Indeed, since each soliton is acted on by forces generated by the other three solitons, one cannot rule out a priori the possibility that the soliton centers exhibit complicated long-time motions. For instance, in the three-soliton case,
[
20
]
shows that it suffices to first prove that the two distances from the oppositely signed soliton to the other two become comparable. In that regime, if the three centers are not collinear, then the oppositely signed soliton is pushed outward by the repulsive forces exerted by the other two, and the apex angle of the corresponding isosceles triangle decreases. By contrast, in the
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves setting considered here, the forces act from three different directions, so that even the force exerted on the oppositely signed soliton by two of the solitons may be canceled by the force coming from the remaining soliton.
Because the inter-soliton forces are so delicate, it is conceivable a priori that, for
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves, the three like-signed solitons might spread out in a non-equilateral configuration—for instance, forming a triangle with angles
50^{\circ}
,
60^{\circ}
, and
70^{\circ}
—rather than an equilateral one. In this paper, to study the motion of the soliton centers, we exploit the fact that the mutual distances between solitons evolve in a way that is coupled to the geometry of the configuration, in particular to the angles formed by the centers.
More precisely, taking the oppositely signed soliton as a reference, we consider the three directions along which the other solitons separate, and introduce the associated unit direction vectors; the angles between these directions are encoded by the inner products of these unit vectors.
We then derive an effective system of ordinary differential equations for these angular variables and reduce it to a closed ODE system that can be written solely in terms of the three pairwise inner products. The resulting dynamical system can be analyzed by an elementary phase-portrait analysis.
A key idea behind this reduction is a separation of time scales: the angular variables evolve much more slowly than the radial distances, so that, to leading order, one may treat the distances as evolving as if the angles were frozen.

1.5.
Previous results

First, we introduce results related to the soliton resolution conjecture. The soliton resolution conjecture has been studied for the energy-critical wave equation. We refer to
[
14
,
27
]
, in which the conjecture was proven for all radial solutions in space dimension
d\geq 3
. Historically, we refer to
[

,
12
,
14
,
15
]
, where the conjecture has been proved in space dimension
d=3,5,7,\ldots
or
d=4,6
using the method of channels. In recent years, Jendrej and Lawrie
[
27
]
proved the conjecture in space dimension
d\geq 4
using a novel argument based on the analysis of collision intervals. Similar results regarding the conjecture have been obtained for cases with a potential
[
28
,
32
]
, with a damping term
[
18
]
, as well as for wave maps
[
13
,
25
]
, for the heat equation
[
2
]
, all under some rotational symmetry. In recent years, in order to gain a finer understanding of the long-time dynamics, there has been growing interest in the analysis of intermediate (or transient) states that arise before the final soliton-resolution regime as
t\to+\infty
. For energy-critical wave equations, see
[
38
,
39
]
, and for the heat equation, see
[
3
]
.

On the other hand, the conjecture remains widely open for the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation, namely the
\alpha=0
case. Nakanishi and Schlag
[
37
]
proved the conjecture as long as the energy of the solution is not much larger than the ground state energy in space dimension
d=3
.
For the damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation case, the energy decay (
1.1
) makes the analysis simpler than the undamped case. Historically, Keller
[
29
]
constructed stable and unstable manifolds in a neighborhood of any stationary solution of (
DNKG
), and Feireisl
[
16
]
proved the conjecture along a time sequence. Burq, Raugel and Schlag
[
4
]
proved the conjecture in the full limit

for all radial solutions. In particular, Côte, Martel and Yuan
[
8
]
proved the conjecture for
d=1
without size restriction and radial assumption for a non-integrable equation. In a result without any size or symmetry restriction, Kim and Kwon
[
30
]
proved the conjecture for Calogero-Moser derivative nonlinear Schödinger equation. On the other hand, in the case of general dimensions
d\geq 2
, it remains unclear how solitary waves are arranged and how they move. One reason is that, in general dimensions, it is not known whether solutions remain bounded in
the energy space
\mathcal{H}
; another is that (
DNKG
) admits a wide variety of stationary solutions.

In general space dimension, Côte, Martel, Yuan, and Zhao
[
9
]
proved that 2-solitary waves with same sign do not exist. Furthermore, they constructed a Lipschitz manifold in the energy space with codimension 2 of those solutions asymptotics to 2-solitary waves with the opposite signs. Subsequently, the author and Nakanishi
[
21

provided a complete classification of solutions into 5 types of global behavior for all initial data in a small neighborhood of each superposition of two ground states with the opposite signs. Additionally, results concerning an excited state of (
1.3

[
11
]

[
6

A crucial aspect of analyzing multi-solitary waves is understanding the movement of their centers caused by interactions between solitary waves. In particular, a 2-solitary waves with the same sign does not exist because the interaction between them generates an attractive force. Moreover, the author
[
20
]
proved that, in the case of
3
-solitary waves, the three solitons become collinear, with only the middle soliton having the opposite sign. The long-time dynamics force a collinear configuration in which exactly one soliton has the opposite sign and this oppositely signed soliton lies between the other two. In
[
19

, the author also constructed 2-solitary waves with the same signs for the damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation with a repulsive delta potential in
d=1

In addition, several studies have been conducted on multi-solitary waves for the nonlinear dispersive equations. For the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation, Côte and Muñoz
[
10
]
constructed multi-solitary waves based on the ground state. Furthermore, Côte and Martel
[
7
]
extended the work of
[
10
]
by constructing multi-solitary waves based on the stationary solution of the nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation. Notably, multi-solitary waves usually behave asymptotically as the solitary waves moving at distinct constant speeds. More recently, Aryan
[
1
]
has constructed a 2-solitary wave where the distance between each solitary wave is
2(1+O(1))\log{t}
as
t\to\infty
. There are several results regarding multi-solitary waves with logarithmic distance; see
e.g.

[
17
,
33
,
35
,
36
]
. Notably, for the damped nonlinear Klein-Gordon equation, multi-solitary waves of (
DNKG
) cannot move at a constant speed by the damping
\alpha
. A distinctive feature of the present work is that it provides a complete rigidity description for the long-time dynamics of multi-soliton solutions. Most of the aforementioned results on multi-solitons have focused primarily on construction and existence. Among them, the results in
[
6
]
and
[
34
]
concern regular polygonal configurations for dispersive equations. In
[
6
]
, the existence of such a regular polygon configuration is obtained by imposing symmetry assumptions on the solutions. In
[
34

In both works, symmetry is imposed in order to simplify the interactions between solitons, since without symmetry the interactions become significantly more involved. To the best of my knowledge, the present paper is the first to show, in the context of dispersive equations, that the centers of multi-solitons are forced to form an equilateral triangle.

Remark 1.9
.

By a “bubble solution”, we mean a stationary solution (or standing wave) obtained from a fixed stationary profile by applying the scaling symmetry. In this paper, we refer to such solutions as “solitons”. For instance, in the context of soliton resolution for the energy-critical wave equation, the solitons that arise are precisely bubble solutions. Moreover, by a “multi-bubble solution”, we mean a solution that is asymptotic to a superposition of bubbles. We emphasize that the term “multi-bubble solution” is not restricted to the case where all bubbles have the same scale, as in
[
34
]
. In many contexts, it also includes superpositions of bubble solutions associated with different scales. Indeed,
[
22
,
23
,
24
,
25
,
26
]
study two-bubble solutions with different scales.

2.1.
Basic properties of the ground state

Since
Q
is radial, there exists
q:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
such that

\displaystyle Q(x)=q(|x|).

Furthermore,
q
satisfies

\displaystyle q^{\prime\prime}+\frac{d-1}{r}q^{\prime}-q+q^{p}=0.

(2.1)

In addition, there exists a constant
c_{q}
depending only on
d
and
p
such that

\displaystyle|q(r)-c_{q}r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}e^{-r}|+|q^{\prime}(r)+c_{q}r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}e^{-r}|\lesssim r^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-r}.

(2.2)

Next, in order to make the leading interaction term more explicit, we introduce the vector field

H:\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\{0\}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
defined by

\displaystyle H(z)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\nabla Q^{p}(x))\,Q(x-z)\,dx.

(2.3)

Moreover, we define
g:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
as

\displaystyle H(z)=\frac{z}{|z|}\,g(|z|).

We note that this definition is well-defined. Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle g(r+h)-g(r)=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\partial}_{1}(Q^{p})(x)\Bigl(Q\bigl(x-(r+h)e_{1}\bigr)-Q(x-re_{1})\Bigr)\,dx.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(r)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}{\partial}_{1}(Q^{p})(x)\,{\partial}_{1}Q(x-re_{1})\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q^{p}(x)\,{\partial}_{1}^{2}Q(x-re_{1})\,dx.

In addition, by direct computation,

\displaystyle{\partial}_{1}^{2}Q(x-re_{1})=\frac{\sum_{k=2}^{d}x_{k}^{2}}{|x-re_{1}|^{3}}q^{\prime}(|x-re_{1}|)+\frac{(x_{1}-r)^{2}}{|x-re_{1}|^{2}}q^{\prime\prime}(|x-re_{1}|).

Using (
2.1
), we obtain

\displaystyle|g(r)-c_{g}r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}e^{-r}|+|g^{\prime}(r)+c_{g}r^{-\frac{d-1}{2}}e^{-r}|\lesssim r^{-\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-r},

(2.4)

where

\displaystyle c_{g}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}Q^{p}(x)e^{-x_{1}}\,dx.

Moreover, by (
2.4
), we have for
R\gg 1
and
|a|\ll R

\displaystyle\left|g(R+a)-e^{-a}g(R)\right|\lesssim\frac{g(R)}{R},

(2.5)

and we have for
|a|\ll 1
and
1\ll R

\displaystyle\left|g(R+a)-(1-a)g(R)\right|\lesssim g(R)\left(a^{2}+\frac{|a|}{R}\right).

(2.6)

We will discuss further properties of
H

4.
Estimates on interactions between like-signed solitons

In this section and what follows, we analyze the behavior of the soliton centers. In the
(1,3)
-sign configuration, there are six pairwise interactions, which makes the analysis of the long-time soliton dynamics more delicate. We therefore begin by comparing the distances between solitons of the same sign and those between solitons of opposite signs. To this end, we first introduce several definitions. First, we define
\tilde{D}
and
\hat{D}
as

\displaystyle\tilde{D}=\min{(\rho_{12},\rho_{23},\rho_{13})},\ \hat{D}=\min{(\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3})}.

(4.1)

Then, we have

\displaystyle D=\min{(\tilde{D},\hat{D})}.

(4.2)

The goal of this section is to prove the following proposition concerning
\tilde{D}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Then, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(t)-D(t)>D^{\frac{1}{5}}(t).

(4.3)

4.1.
Basic properties of
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves

First, we collect the basic properties concerning
\tilde{D}
and
\hat{D}
. By (
2.16
), we have
t\gg 1

\displaystyle V=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{23})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})>-\frac{1}{100}\mathcal{F}(D).

(4.4)

In particular, by (
4.4
) when
\tilde{D}=D
holds we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\tilde{D})<\frac{100}{33}\mathcal{F}(\hat{D}),

and we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\hat{D})>\frac{33}{100}\mathcal{F}(D).

(4.5)

By (
4.5
), there exists a constant
C_{\circ}>0
such that for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\hat{D}-C_{\circ}<\tilde{D}.

(4.6)

In this section, we need to analyze delicate variations in the inter-soliton distances. To this end, we establish the following lemma concerning
D
and
\mathcal{F}(D)

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
0<\epsilon<1
. Then for any
T\gg 1
, there exists
T^{\prime}>T
such that the following hold.

(1)

For
T\leq t\leq T^{\prime}

\displaystyle\left|D(t)-D(T)\right|\leq 2D^{\epsilon}(T).

(4.7)

(2)

Moreover, there exists

depending on

such that

\displaystyle c_{\bullet}D^{\epsilon}(T)\leq\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds\leq\frac{1}{c_{\bullet}}D^{\epsilon}(T).

(4.8)

Proof.

Fix
\epsilon>0
. First, by Lemma
3.3
, there exists
C>1
depending on
\vec{u}
such that for
t\geq T

\displaystyle|\dot{D}|\leq C\mathcal{F}(D).

(4.9)

By Lemma
2.3
, the function

is not in

there exists

such that

\displaystyle\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds}{D(s)}=\frac{1}{C}D^{\epsilon-1}(T).

We show that, for this choice of
T^{\prime}
, (
4.7
), and (
4.8
) hold.

First, we prove (
4.7
). For any
T\leq t\leq T^{\prime}
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\log{D(t)}-\log{D(T)}\right|&=\left|\int_{T}^{t}\frac{\dot{D}(s)ds}{D(s)}\right|\\
&\leq\int_{T}^{t}\frac{|\dot{D}(s)|ds}{D(s)}\\
&\leq C\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds}{D(s)}=D^{\epsilon-1}(T).\end{aligned}

In particular, noting that

, the above implies that for any

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}D(T)\leq D(t)\leq 2D(T).

\displaystyle D^{\epsilon-1}(T)

\displaystyle=C\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds}{D(s)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2C}{D(T)}\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds,

\displaystyle D^{\epsilon-1}(T)

\displaystyle=C\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\frac{\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds}{D(s)}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{C}{2D(T)}\int_{T}^{T^{\prime}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds,

which yields (

Before proceeding to the analysis of distances, we record estimates on the angles of triangles with large side lengths.

Lemma 4.3
.

We assume
M\gg 1
. Then,
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2},\varsigma_{3}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfy the following.

(1)

When
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2},\varsigma_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left|\ |\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}|-M\ \right|

\displaystyle\leq M^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\left|\ |\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|-M\ \right|

\displaystyle\leq M^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|-M

\displaystyle\geq-M^{\frac{99}{100}},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{1}}{|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{1}|}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\leq 5M^{-\frac{1}{100}},

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{2}}{|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{2}|}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-5M^{-\frac{1}{100}},

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-5M^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

(2)

When
\varsigma_{1},\varsigma_{2},\varsigma_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\left|\ |\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}|-M\ \right|

\displaystyle\leq M^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|-M

\displaystyle\geq-M^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|-M

\displaystyle\geq-M^{\frac{99}{100}},

we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|}-\frac{1}{2}

\displaystyle\geq-5M^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

Remark 4.1
.

The lemma depends only on the configuration of three points and is therefore essentially two-dimensional. Thus, to gain geometric intuition, one may restrict attention to a planar triangle, in which the claim is most easily visualized.

Proof.

We define

\displaystyle\Lambda_{1}=|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{2}|,\ \Lambda_{2}=|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{1}|,\ \Lambda_{3}=|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1}|.

First, we prove (1). By assumption, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{1}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{1}}{|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{1}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{2}^{2}+\Lambda_{3}^{2}-\Lambda_{1}^{2}}{2\Lambda_{2}\Lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2(M+M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}-(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}{2(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{M^{2}+7M^{\frac{199}{100}}}{2(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1+\frac{7}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}}}{2\left(1-\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}}\right)^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{5}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}}.

Furthermore, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{2}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{2}}{|\varsigma_{3}-\varsigma_{2}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{2}+\Lambda_{3}^{2}-\Lambda_{2}^{2}}{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{2}+(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}-(M+M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{1}}{2\Lambda_{3}}-\frac{4M^{\frac{199}{100}}}{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{3}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{M-M^{\frac{99}{100}}}{2(M+M^{\frac{99}{100}})}-\frac{4M^{\frac{199}{100}}}{2(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}}.

By the same calculation, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|}\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}},

and therefore we prove (1).

Next we prove (2). By the direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{1}-\varsigma_{3}|}\cdot\frac{\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}}{|\varsigma_{2}-\varsigma_{3}|}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Lambda_{1}^{2}+\Lambda_{2}^{2}-\Lambda_{3}^{2}}{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}-(M+M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}{2\Lambda_{1}\Lambda_{2}}

\displaystyle\geq 1-\frac{(M+M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}{2(M-M^{\frac{99}{100}})^{2}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{(1+\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}})^{2}}{2(1-\frac{1}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}})^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}-\frac{5}{M^{\frac{1}{100}}}.

Therefore we prove (2).
∎

and

\displaystyle\quad\max{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}-\tilde{D}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\max{(\rho_{12}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{13}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{23}(t^{\prime}))}-\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{7}{10}}(t^{\prime}).

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\frac{7}{10}}(t^{\prime}).

In particular, by (
4.25
) and
D\gg 1
, we have for
t^{\prime}\leq t\leq T_{3}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)<D^{\frac{9}{10}}(t),\\
\max{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}<\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{9}{10}}(t).\end{aligned}

(4.28)

Furthermore, since we have

\displaystyle\quad\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{23}(t^{\prime})}{3}+D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})-D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{23}(t^{\prime})}{3}+D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime})}{3}-D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{\prime})>0,

we define
T_{4}\in[t^{\prime},T_{3}]
by

\displaystyle T_{4}=\sup{\{t\in[t^{\prime},T_{3}]\ \mbox{such that \eqref{bsass} holds on}[t^{\prime},t]\}}.

(4.29)

We assume
T_{4}<T_{3}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\hat{D}(T_{4})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{4})+\rho_{13}(T_{4})+\rho_{23}(T_{4})}{3}+D^{\frac{2}{3}}(T_{4})=0.

Then, by (
4.28
), we may apply Lemma
4.4
. Therefore, by (
4.21
) and
|\frac{d}{dt}D^{\frac{2}{3}}|\lesssim D^{-\frac{1}{3}}\mathcal{F}(D)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\hat{D}-\frac{\rho_{12}+\rho_{23}+\rho_{13}}{3}+D^{\frac{2}{3}}\right)(T_{4})>0.

Therefore, we have
T_{4}=T_{3}
. Then, by Lemma
4.4
and (
4.26
), there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\left(\hat{D}(T_{3})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})+\rho_{23}(T_{3})}{3}\right)-\left(\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{23}(t^{\prime})}{3}\right)\\
&\geq c\int_{t^{\prime}}^{T_{3}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds\\
&\geq cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{7}{10}}(t^{\prime}).\end{aligned}

(4.30)

On the other hand, since we have (
4.22
), (
4.30
),

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})+\rho_{23}(T_{3})}{3}

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{D}(T_{3}),

and

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{23}(t^{\prime})}{3}

\displaystyle<\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\hat{D}(T_{3})-\tilde{D}(T_{3})+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\hat{D}(T_{3})-\tilde{D}(T_{3})+\left(\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})-\hat{D}(t^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\hat{D}(T_{3})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})+\rho_{23}(T_{3})}{3}\right)-\left(\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{23}(t^{\prime})}{3}\right)

\displaystyle\quad-D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{7}{10}}(t^{\prime})-D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime}).

Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(T_{3})-\hat{D}(T_{3})\leq-cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{7}{10}}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t^{\prime}),

which contradicts (
4.6
). Therefore we complete the proof.

∎

4.3.
Distance analysis II

In the previous subsection, we showed that at least one of
\rho_{12},\ \rho_{23},\ \rho_{13}
stays away from
D
, so that the corresponding inter-soliton interaction has essentially no effect.
Next, we show that the interaction associated with the
second
farthest distance among these three is also negligible.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves . Furthermore, we assume that there exist
1\ll T_{1}<T_{2}\leq\infty
such that we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)<D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t),

(4.31)

\displaystyle\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}<\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t).

(4.32)

Then, we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\left(\hat{D}(t)-\frac{\min{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}+\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}}{2}\right)\\
&-\left(\hat{D}(T_{1})-\frac{\min{(\rho_{12}(T_{1}),\rho_{13}(T_{1}),\rho_{23}(T_{1}))}+\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(T_{1}),\rho_{13}(T_{1}),\rho_{23}(T_{1}))}}{2}\right)\\
&\gtrsim\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds.\end{aligned}

(4.33)

Proof.

By symmetry, we may assume that we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\rho_{12}(t)

\displaystyle<\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t),

\displaystyle\rho_{13}(t)

\displaystyle<\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t).

In this case, by Lemma
4.5
, we have
\rho_{23}(t)-D(t)\gtrsim D^{\frac{2}{3}}(t)
for
T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}
. Moreover, since
\rho_{12},\ \rho_{23},\ \rho_{13}
are continuous and (
4.32
) holds on
[T_{1},T_{2})
, we may assume that we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\rho_{23}(t)=\max{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}.

Then, by (
3.11
) and (
3.13
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho_{1}}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})c_{12}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})c_{13}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})u_{12}\cdot u_{1}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})u_{13}\cdot u_{1}+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})c_{12}+2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})c_{23}

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{21})u_{21}\cdot u_{2}+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})c_{13}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})c_{23}+2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{31})u_{31}\cdot u_{3}+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle=-2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})(u_{12}\cdot u_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})(u_{1}\cdot u_{21})

\displaystyle\quad-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})(u_{12}\cdot u_{13})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{13}

\displaystyle=-2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})(u_{31}\cdot u_{1})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})(u_{13}\cdot u_{3})

\displaystyle\quad-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})(u_{13}\cdot u_{12})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

In particular, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}&\leq-\frac{\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})}{2}+\frac{u_{1}\cdot u_{21}+u_{1}\cdot u_{31}}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})\\
&\quad+\frac{u_{12}\cdot u_{2}}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\frac{u_{13}\cdot u_{3}}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).\end{aligned}

(4.34)

By (
4.6
), (
4.31
), and (
4.32
), we have

\displaystyle|\rho_{12}-D|

\displaystyle\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\rho_{13}-D|

\displaystyle\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\rho_{1}-D

\displaystyle\geq-D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}-D

\displaystyle\geq-D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\rho_{3}-D

\displaystyle\geq-D^{\frac{99}{100}}.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.3
we have

\displaystyle c_{12}-\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}},\ c_{13}-\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

(4.35)

Here, we assume that
\rho_{1}=\hat{D}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle|\rho_{12}-D|\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\rho_{13}-D|\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle|\rho_{1}-D|\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

and therefore by Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\displaystyle u_{2}\cdot u_{12}-\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}},\ u_{1}\cdot u_{12}+\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}},

\displaystyle u_{3}\cdot u_{13}-\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}},\ u_{1}\cdot u_{13}+\frac{1}{2}\geq-5D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

Thus, by (
4.35
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{1}

\displaystyle\geq 2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})\right)-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})\right)+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

(4.36)

By (
4.34
) and (
4.36
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{1}-\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}\geq\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right)\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).

(4.37)

Next, suppose that
\rho_{2}=\hat{D}
. We have

\displaystyle|u_{2}+u_{3}-u_{1}|^{2}=3+2c_{23}-2c_{12}-2c_{13}\geq 0.

(4.38)

By (
4.35
) and (
4.38
), we have

\displaystyle c_{23}+\frac{1}{2}\gtrsim-D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

(4.39)

Furthermore, since
\rho_{2}
satisfies
|\rho_{2}-D|\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}}
, we obtain

\displaystyle u_{21}\cdot u_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\gtrsim-D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

(4.40)

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho_{2}}\geq 2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})-\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

(4.41)

Next, we estimate
\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}
. We first treat the terms involving
\rho_{1}
.
If
\rho_{1}
satisfies
\rho_{1}-D>D^{\frac{99}{100}}
, then we have
\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})\lesssim D^{-\frac{1}{100}}\mathcal{F}(D)
; hence, together with (
4.40
) we have

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})\right)+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

On the other hand, if
\rho_{1}
satisfies
\rho_{1}-D\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}}
, then by Lemma
4.3
we have

\displaystyle u_{1}\cdot u_{21}-\frac{1}{2}\lesssim D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{13})\right)+\frac{3}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

Thus, in either case, combining the above estimate with (
4.41
) yields

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{2}-\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{7}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})-\frac{1}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right)

\displaystyle\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).

If
\rho_{3}=\hat{D}
, the same argument as in the case where
\rho_{2}
attains the minimum gives

\displaystyle\dot{\rho_{3}}-\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).

Therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{D}}-\frac{\dot{\rho}_{12}+\dot{\rho}_{13}}{2}\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).

(4.42)

Integrating (
4.42
) on
[T_{1},t]
completes the proof.
∎

Using this lemma, we next show that the
second
distance is also sufficiently far from
D
.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Furthermore, we assume that we have for
T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)<D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t).

(4.43)

Then, we have for
T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}

\displaystyle\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}\geq\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t).

(4.44)

Proof.

We assume that there exists
T_{1}\leq t^{\prime}\leq T_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{13}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{23}(t^{\prime}))}<\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime}).

(4.45)

In this case, by symmetry and Lemma
4.5
and continuity, we may assume that

\rho_{23}>\tilde{D}+D^{\frac{2}{3}}
holds for
T_{1}\leq t\leq T_{2}
. Then, we introduce the following bootstrap estimate

\displaystyle\hat{D}(t)-\frac{\rho_{12}(t)+\rho_{13}(t)}{2}+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t)\geq 0.

(4.46)

By Lemma
4.2
, there exists
T_{3}
such that we have for
t^{\prime}\leq t\leq T_{3}

\displaystyle|D(t)-D(t^{\prime})|\leq 2D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime}),

and we have

\displaystyle c_{\bullet}D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime})\leq\int_{t^{\prime}}^{T_{3}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds\leq\frac{1}{c_{\bullet}}D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime}).

Then, by Lemma
3.3
, (
4.6
), (
4.43
), and (
4.45
), we have for
t^{\prime}\leq t\leq T_{3}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})-\hat{D}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t),

\displaystyle\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}-\tilde{D}(t)

\displaystyle\lesssim\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{13}(t^{\prime}),\rho_{23}(t^{\prime}))}-\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\lesssim D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t).

In particular, we have for
t^{\prime}\leq t\leq T_{3}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)&<D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t),\\
\mbox{{mid}}{(\rho_{12}(t),\rho_{13}(t),\rho_{23}(t))}&<\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{2}}(t).\end{aligned}

(4.47)

Furthermore, since we have

\displaystyle\quad\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})}{2}+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})-D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})}{2}+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime})}{2}-D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t^{\prime})>0,

we define
T_{4}\in[t^{\prime},T_{3}]
by

\displaystyle T_{4}=\sup{\{t\in[t^{\prime},T_{3}]\ \mbox{such that \eqref{bsass2} holds on}\ [t^{\prime},t]\}}.

(4.48)

We assume that
T_{4}<T_{3}
. Then, we have

\displaystyle\hat{D}(T_{4})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{4})+\rho_{13}(T_{4})}{2}+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(T_{4})=0.

Then, by (
4.47
), we may apply Lemma
4.6
. Therefore, by (
4.42
) and
|\frac{d}{dt}D^{\frac{1}{3}}|\lesssim D^{-\frac{2}{3}}\mathcal{F}(D)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\hat{D}-\frac{\rho_{12}+\rho_{13}}{2}+D^{\frac{1}{3}}\right)(T_{4})>0.

Therefore, we have
T_{4}=T_{3}
. Then, by Lemma
4.6
, there exists
c>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} &\quad\left(\hat{D}(T_{3})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})}{2}\right)-\left(\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})}{2}\right)\\
&\geq c\int_{t^{\prime}}^{T_{3}}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds\\
&\geq cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime}).\end{aligned}

(4.49)

Furthermore, by (
4.43
), (
4.45
), (
4.49
), and

\displaystyle\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})}{2}\geq\tilde{D}(T_{3}),

we obtain

\displaystyle\quad\hat{D}(T_{3})-\tilde{D}(T_{3})+D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\hat{D}(T_{3})-\tilde{D}(T_{3})+\left(\tilde{D}(t^{\prime})-\hat{D}(t^{\prime})\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(\hat{D}(T_{3})-\frac{\rho_{12}(T_{3})+\rho_{13}(T_{3})}{2}\right)-\left(\hat{D}(t^{\prime})-\frac{\rho_{12}(t^{\prime})+\rho_{13}(t^{\prime})}{2}\right)-D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime})-D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime}).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(T_{3})-\hat{D}(T_{3})

\displaystyle\leq-cc_{\bullet}D^{\frac{2}{5}}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t^{\prime})+D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t^{\prime}),

which contradicts (
4.6
). Therefore we complete the proof.
∎

4.4.
Distance analysis III

From the discussion so far, we have shown that among
\rho_{12},\ \rho_{23},\ \rho_{13}
, it cannot happen that two or more of them are close to
D
.
Therefore, as a final step, we prove that
\tilde{D}
is sufficiently larger than
\hat{D}
.
To this end, somewhat ad hoc, we define a constant
c_{\heartsuit}
as

\displaystyle e^{c_{\heartsuit}}=\frac{4+\sqrt{3}}{4}.

(4.50)

Furthermore, we define
D_{mod}
as

\displaystyle D_{mod}(t)=\min{(\rho_{1}(t),\rho_{2}(t),\rho_{3}(t)+c_{\heartsuit})}.

(4.51)

Remark 4.2
.

At first glance, the above definition of
c_{\heartsuit}
may look like an unrelated numerical choice.
However, keeping in mind the algebraic relations satisfied by
c_{12},\ c_{23},\ c_{13}
and seeking a condition that allows an argument similar to Lemma
4.4
and Lemma
4.6
, one naturally arrives at the constant
c_{\heartsuit}
.

Lemma 4.8
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves . Furthermore, we assume that there exist
1\ll T_{1}<T_{2}\leq\infty
such that we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\tilde{D}(t)-\hat{D}(t)<D^{\frac{1}{4}}(t).

(4.52)

Then, we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle\left(D_{mod}(t)-\tilde{D}(t)\right)-\left(D_{mod}(T_{1})-\tilde{D}(T_{1})\right)\gtrsim\int_{T_{1}}^{t}\mathcal{F}(D(s))ds.

(4.53)

Proof.

By symmetry, we may assume that
\tilde{D}(T_{1})=\rho_{12}(T_{1})
.
In this case, by Lemma
4.7
and continuity, we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}
.

\displaystyle\rho_{12}(t)=\tilde{D}(t),\ \rho_{13}(t)>\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t),\ \rho_{23}(t)>\tilde{D}(t)+D^{\frac{1}{3}}(t).

Then we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho_{1}}

\displaystyle=2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})c_{12}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})c_{13}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})u_{12}\cdot u_{1}+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{2}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})c_{12}+2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})c_{23}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})u_{21}\cdot u_{2}+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{3}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})c_{13}+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})c_{23}+2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle=-2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{12})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})(u_{12}\cdot u_{2})+\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})(u_{1}\cdot u_{21})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

We note that by definition we have
|D_{mod}-\hat{D}|\leq c_{\heartsuit}
.
First, we assume that
D_{mod}=\rho_{1}
.
Since we have
\rho_{1}\leq\rho_{3}+c_{\heartsuit}
, by (
2.11
) we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})-e^{c_{\heartsuit}}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{F}(D)}{D}.

(4.54)

Since we have for
T_{1}\leq t<T_{2}

\displaystyle|\rho_{1}-D|+|\rho_{12}-D|\leq D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}-D\geq-D^{\frac{99}{100}},

by Lemma
4.3
we obtain

\displaystyle c_{12}-\frac{1}{2}\gtrsim-D^{-\frac{1}{100}},\ u_{1}\cdot u_{21}-\frac{1}{2}\lesssim D^{-\frac{1}{100}}.

(4.55)

Furthermore, if we have
\rho_{2}>D+D^{\frac{99}{100}}
, then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}(\rho_{2})\lesssim\frac{\mathcal{F}(D)}{D},

and therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{1}-\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

On the other hand, if we have
\rho_{2}<D+D^{\frac{99}{100}}
, then by Lemma
4.3
we have

\displaystyle\left|c_{12}-\frac{1}{2}\right|+\left|u_{12}\cdot u_{2}-\frac{1}{2}\right|+\left|u_{1}\cdot u_{21}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\lesssim D^{-\frac{1}{100}},

and therefore we obtain

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{1}-\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

Hence, in either case,

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{1}-\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})-\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right).

(4.56)

By (
4.55
) and (
4.56
), we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{\rho}_{1}-\dot{\rho}_{12}&\geq\frac{3}{2}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})-\frac{4+\sqrt{3}}{4}\mathcal{F}(\rho_{1})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right)\\
&=\frac{2-\sqrt{3}}{4}\mathcal{F}(D_{mod})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right)\\
&\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).\end{aligned}

(4.57)

The case
D_{mod}=\rho_{2}
can be treated by the same argument as above. Next, we assume that
D_{mod}=\rho_{3}+c_{\heartsuit}
. When
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle>D+D^{\frac{99}{100}},

\displaystyle\rho_{2}

\displaystyle>D+D^{\frac{99}{100}},

we have

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{3}-\dot{\rho}_{12}\geq 2\mathcal{F}(\rho_{3})+o\left(\mathcal{F}(D)\right)\gtrsim\mathcal{F}(D).

Next, if
\rho_{1}
and
\rho_{2}
satisfy

\displaystyle\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{a}_{1}&=\left(-2a_{1}+\frac{1}{2}a_{2}+\frac{1}{2}a_{3}+d_{12}+d_{13}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right),\\
\dot{a}_{2}&=\left(\frac{1}{2}a_{1}-2a_{2}+\frac{1}{2}a_{3}+d_{12}+d_{23}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right),\\
\dot{a}_{3}&=\left(\frac{1}{2}a_{1}+\frac{1}{2}a_{2}-2a_{3}+d_{13}+d_{23}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.8)

Next, we derive differential equations for
d_{12},d_{13},d_{23}
from (
5.4
). By

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}-\frac{1}{L}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{|a|}{L^{2}}

and (
6.7
),
d_{12}=c_{12}+\frac{1}{2}
satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\left\{(1-c_{12}^{2})(2-a_{1}-a_{2})+(1-a_{3})(1-c_{12})(c_{13}+c_{23})\right\}

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{7}}\right).

Furthermore, by direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\quad(1-c_{12}^{2})(2-a_{1}-a_{2})+(1-a_{3})(1-c_{12})(c_{13}+c_{23})

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{3}{4}+d_{12}-d_{12}^{2}\right)(2-a_{1}-a_{2})+(1-a_{3})\left(\frac{3}{2}-d_{12}\right)(-1+d_{13}+d_{23})

\displaystyle=3d_{12}+\frac{3}{2}d_{13}+\frac{3}{2}d_{23}-\frac{3}{4}a_{1}-\frac{3}{4}a_{2}+\frac{3}{2}a_{3}+O(|a|^{2}+|d|^{2}).

From symmetry, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{d}_{12}&=\left(3d_{12}+\frac{3}{2}d_{13}+\frac{3}{2}d_{23}-\frac{3}{4}a_{1}-\frac{3}{4}a_{2}+\frac{3}{2}a_{3}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{7}}\right),\\
\dot{d}_{13}&=\left(\frac{3}{2}d_{12}+3d_{13}+\frac{3}{2}d_{23}-\frac{3}{4}a_{1}+\frac{3}{2}a_{2}-\frac{3}{4}a_{3}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{7}}\right),\\
\dot{d}_{23}&=\left(\frac{3}{2}d_{12}+\frac{3}{2}d_{13}+3d_{23}+\frac{3}{2}a_{1}-\frac{3}{4}a_{2}-\frac{3}{4}a_{3}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\\
&\quad+O\left(\frac{|a|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{7}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.9)

In particular, we have

\displaystyle|\dot{d}_{12}|+|\dot{d}_{13}|+|\dot{d}_{23}|\lesssim\frac{|a|+|d|}{t\log{t}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{7}}.

(6.10)

Here, we define
\xi_{1},\xi_{2},\xi_{3}
as

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \xi_{1}&=a_{1}-\frac{1}{5}(4d_{12}+4d_{13}+2d_{23}),\\
\xi_{2}&=a_{2}-\frac{1}{5}(4d_{12}+2d_{13}+4d_{23}),\\
\xi_{3}&=a_{3}-\frac{1}{5}(2d_{12}+4d_{13}+4d_{23}),\\
|\xi|&=\sqrt{\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}}.\end{aligned}

(6.11)

Then, by direct computation, we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |\xi|+|d|&\sim|a|+|d|,\\
|\xi|^{2}+|d|^{2}&\sim|a|^{2}+|d|^{2}.\end{aligned}

(6.12)

By (
6.8
), (
6.11
), and (
6.12
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{\xi}_{1}&=\left(-2\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{3}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)+O\left(\frac{|\xi|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right),\\
\dot{\xi}_{2}&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}-2\xi_{2}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{3}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)+O\left(\frac{|\xi|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right),\\
\dot{\xi}_{3}&=\left(\frac{1}{2}\xi_{1}+\frac{1}{2}\xi_{2}-2\xi_{3}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)+O\left(\frac{|\xi|+|d|}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.13)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\xi|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left(-4\xi_{1}^{2}-4\xi_{2}^{2}-4\xi_{3}^{2}+2\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+2\xi_{2}\xi_{3}+2\xi_{3}\xi_{1}\right)\mathcal{F}(L)

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(\frac{|\xi|^{2}+|d|^{2}}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right).

Since we have

\displaystyle\xi_{1}^{2}+\xi_{2}^{2}+\xi_{3}^{2}\geq\xi_{1}\xi_{2}+\xi_{2}\xi_{3}+\xi_{3}\xi_{1},

there exists
c_{1}>0
such that we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|\xi|^{2}\leq-\frac{c_{1}}{t}|\xi|^{2}+\frac{1}{c_{1}}\left(\frac{|d|^{2}}{t(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{6}}\right).

(6.14)

By Gronwall’s inequality and (
6.10
), we obtain

\displaystyle|\xi|^{2}\lesssim\frac{|d|^{2}}{(\log{t})^{\frac{1}{2}}}+\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{6}},

which implies (
6.6
).

Step 2: We prove (
6.5
).

By (
6.6
) and (
6.9
), there exists
c_{2}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \left|\dot{d}_{12}-\left(\frac{12}{5}d_{12}+\frac{9}{5}d_{13}+\frac{9}{5}d_{23}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\right|&\leq c_{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{\frac{5}{4}}}|d|+\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}\right),\\
\left|\dot{d}_{13}-\left(\frac{9}{5}d_{12}+\frac{12}{5}d_{13}+\frac{9}{5}d_{23}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\right|&\leq c_{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{\frac{5}{4}}}|d|+\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}\right),\\
\left|\dot{d}_{23}-\left(\frac{9}{5}d_{12}+\frac{9}{5}d_{13}+\frac{12}{5}d_{23}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\right|&\leq c_{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{\frac{5}{4}}}|d|+\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.15)

Since we have

\displaystyle 0\leq|u_{1}+u_{2}+u_{3}|^{2}=3+2c_{12}+2c_{13}+2c_{23},

we obtain

\displaystyle d_{12}+d_{13}+d_{23}\geq 0.

(6.16)

Furthermore, by Lemma
5.8
, we have

\displaystyle\max{(d_{12},d_{13},d_{23})}\sim|d|.

(6.17)

Here, we assume that there exists
t^{\prime}\gg 1
such that

\displaystyle\max{(d_{12}(t^{\prime}),d_{13}(t^{\prime}),d_{23}(t^{\prime}))}>\frac{100c_{2}}{L^{3}(t^{\prime})}.

(6.18)

Then, we introduce the following bootstrap estimate

\displaystyle\max{(d_{12}(t),d_{13}(t),d_{23}(t))}\geq\frac{10c_{2}}{L^{3}(t)}.

(6.19)

We define
T_{1}\in[t^{\prime},\infty]
by

\displaystyle T_{1}=\sup{\{t\in[t^{\prime},\infty)\ \mbox{such that \eqref{dbs} holds on}[t^{\prime},t]\}}.

(6.20)

Furthermore, we assume that
T_{1}<\infty
. Then, we assume

\displaystyle d_{12}(T_{1})=\max{(d_{12}(T_{1}),d_{13}(T_{1}),d_{23}(T_{1}))}.

Then, by (
6.15
) and (
6.17
), we have at
t=T_{1}

\displaystyle\dot{d}_{12}

\displaystyle\geq\left(\frac{12}{5}d_{12}+\frac{9}{5}d_{13}+\frac{9}{5}d_{23}\right)\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}-c_{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{\frac{5}{4}}}|d|+\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{3}{5}\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}d_{12}-c_{2}\left(\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{\frac{5}{4}}}|d|+\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}d_{12}-c_{2}\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{4}}

\displaystyle>0.

Since
L^{-3}
is decreasing, (
6.19
) holds for all
t
with
0<t-T_{1}\ll 1
, which contradicts the definition of
T_{1}
. Hence
T_{1}=\infty
. In this case,
\max{(d_{12},d_{13},d_{23})}
is bounded and monotone increasing, so it converges to a nonzero limit; this contradicts (
6.4
). Therefore, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\max{(d_{12},d_{13},d_{23})}\lesssim\frac{1}{L^{3}}.

(6.21)

By (
6.6
), (
6.17
) and (
6.21
), we obtain (
6.5
). This completes the proof.

∎

6.2.
Long-time dynamics of
(1,3)
-sign solitary waves

In the previous subsection, we refine the estimates for
\rho_{1},\rho_{2},\rho_{3}
. Using these improved bounds, we derive a more precise system of ODEs for
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Then,
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{z}_{0}&=-\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}(Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3})+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),\\
\dot{z}_{1}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}Z_{1}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),\\
\dot{z}_{2}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}Z_{2}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),\\
\dot{z}_{3}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}Z_{3}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.22)

Proof.

Applying (
2.12
) and Lemma
6.2
to Lemma
5.1
, we obtain for
k=1,2,3

\displaystyle\left|\mathcal{F}(\rho_{k})-\mathcal{F}(L)\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}},\ \left|\frac{1}{\rho_{k}}-\frac{1}{L}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{5}}.

Substituting these bounds into the equations in Lemma
5.1
yields (
6.22
).
∎

Here, we analyze the long-time behavior of
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
.
We first estimate the barycenter of
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
using Lemma
6.3
.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Then, there exists
z_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z_{0}(t)+z_{1}(t)+z_{2}(t)+z_{3}(t)}{4}-z_{g}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{2}}.

(6.23)

Proof.

By Lemma
6.3
, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\dot{z}_{0}+\dot{z}_{1}+\dot{z}_{2}+\dot{z}_{3}}{4}\right|\lesssim\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}.

Since the right-hand side is integrable, there exists
z_{g}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\frac{z_{0}+z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}}{4}
converges to
z_{g}
as
t\to\infty
. In particular, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left|\frac{z_{0}(t)+z_{1}(t)+z_{2}(t)+z_{3}(t)}{4}-z_{g}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s(\log{s})^{3}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain (
6.23
).
∎

Next, we prove the convergence of
z_{0}
. The idea, as in
[
20
, Lemma 4.8]
, is to argue that if
Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3}
did not converge, then its magnitude would grow and eventually become much larger than
\rho_{12},\rho_{13},\rho_{23}
, leading to a contradiction.

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Then, there exists
z_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle|z_{0}(t)-z_{\infty}|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{2}}.

(6.24)

Proof.

First, rewriting Lemma
6.3
in terms of
Z_{1},Z_{2},Z_{3}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \dot{Z}_{1}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}(2Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3})+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),\\
\dot{Z}_{2}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}(Z_{1}+2Z_{2}+Z_{3})+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),\\
\dot{Z}_{3}&=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}(Z_{1}+Z_{2}+2Z_{3})+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.25)

Moreover, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{12}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\rho_{12}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{13}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\rho_{13}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{23}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\rho_{23}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).

Here, we define
\mathcal{R}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}=\rho_{12}+\rho_{13}+\rho_{23}.

(6.26)

Then, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{R}}=\frac{\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\mathcal{R}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).

(6.27)

Furthermore, we define
W=Z_{1}+Z_{2}+Z_{3}
and
\mathcal{W}=|W|
. Then,
W
satisfies

\displaystyle\dot{W}=\frac{4\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}W+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).

Therefore, there exists
C_{1}>1
such that we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left|\dot{\mathcal{W}}-\frac{4\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\mathcal{W}\right|\leq\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{F}(L)}{L^{3}}.

(6.28)

Here, we assume that there exists
t^{\prime}>0
such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t^{\prime})>\frac{C_{1}}{L^{2}(t^{\prime})}.

(6.29)

Then, we introduce the following bootstrap estimate

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}(t)>\frac{C_{1}}{L^{2}(t)}.

(6.30)

We define
T_{1}\in[t^{\prime},\infty]
by

\displaystyle T_{1}=\sup{\{t\in[t^{\prime},\infty)\ \mbox{such that \eqref{Wbs} holds on}\ [t^{\prime},t]\}}.

(6.31)

Furthermore, we assume that
T_{1}<\infty
. Then, we have
\mathcal{W}(T_{1})=\frac{C_{1}}{L^{2}(T_{1})}
. Furthermore, by (
6.28
), we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{W}}(T_{1})\geq\frac{4\mathcal{F}(L(T_{1}))}{L(T_{1})}\mathcal{W}(T_{1})-\frac{C_{1}\mathcal{F}(L(T_{1}))}{L^{3}(T_{1})}>0.

Moreover, since
\frac{C_{1}}{L^{2}}
is monotone decreasing, the estimate (
6.30
) also holds for
0<t-T_{1}\ll 1
, which contradicts the maximality of
T_{1}
.
Hence
T_{1}=\infty
. Then, since
\mathcal{W}
is monotone increasing for
t>t^{\prime}
, if
\mathcal{W}(t)
did not tend to
+\infty
as
t\to\infty
, then
\mathcal{W}
would converge.
In that case, for
1\ll T_{2}
we would have

\displaystyle 1\gtrsim\mathcal{W}(e^{T_{2}})-\mathcal{W}(T_{2})\gtrsim\int_{T_{2}}^{e^{T_{2}}}\frac{dt}{t\log t},

which is impossible. Therefore,
\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{W}(t)=\infty
. Here we define
\mathcal{X}
as

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}=\frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{W}}.

(6.32)

Then, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\mathcal{X}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\mathcal{W}^{2}}\left(\dot{\mathcal{R}}\mathcal{W}-\dot{\mathcal{W}}\mathcal{R}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\frac{\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{W}}+O\left(\frac{\mathcal{W}+\mathcal{R}}{\mathcal{W}^{2}t(\log{t})^{3}}\right)

\displaystyle=-\frac{3\mathcal{F}(L)}{L}\mathcal{X}+O\left(\frac{1+\mathcal{X}}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),

which implies

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\mathcal{X}(t)=0.

(6.33)

On the other hand, by Proposition
4.1
, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}

\displaystyle=|z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}-3z_{0}|

\displaystyle=\left|(z_{1}-z_{2})+2(z_{2}-z_{3})+3(z_{3}-z_{1})+3(z_{1}-z_{0})\right|

\displaystyle\leq 3\mathcal{R}+3\rho_{1}

\displaystyle\leq 7\mathcal{R},

which contradicts (
6.33
). Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}=|z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}-3z_{0}|\lesssim\frac{1}{L^{2}}\sim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{2}}.

Then, defining
z_{\infty}=z_{g}
, by (
6.23
) we obtain (
6.24
).

∎

6.3.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

We now prove Theorem
1.1
. First, we establish the following lemma.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
\vec{u}
be a (1,3)-sign solitary waves. Then, there exist
z_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and equilateral triple
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\in S^{d-1}
such that we have for
k=1,2,3
and
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left|z_{k}(t)-z_{\infty}-\omega_{k}\left(\log{t}-\frac{d-1}{2}\log{(\log{t})}+c_{\star}\right)\right|\lesssim\frac{\log{(\log{t})}}{\log{t}}.

(6.34)

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
and Lemma
6.5
, we have for
k=1,2,3

\displaystyle\dot{Z}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{k})u_{k}+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right),

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}_{k}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{F}(\rho_{k})+O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{3}}\right).

Moreover, we have

\displaystyle\dot{u}_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\dot{Z}_{k}-\dot{\rho}_{k}u_{k}}{\rho_{k}}

\displaystyle=O\left(\frac{1}{t(\log{t})^{4}}\right).

Therefore, there exists
\omega_{k}\in S^{d-1}
such that
\lim_{t\to\infty}u_{k}(t)=\omega_{k}
. Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|u_{k}(t)-\omega_{k}\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{t}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{s(\log{s})^{4}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{3}}.

By Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.5
and (
5.12
), we obtain for
k=1,2,3

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} z_{k}(t)&=z_{0}(t)+\rho_{k}(t)u_{k}(t)\\
&=z_{\infty}+\rho_{k}(t)\omega_{k}+O\left(\frac{1}{(\log{t})^{2}}\right)\\
&=z_{\infty}+\omega_{k}\left(\log{t}-\frac{d-1}{2}\log{(\log{t})}+c_{\star}\right)+O\left(\frac{\log{(\log{t})}}{\log{t}}\right).\end{aligned}

(6.35)

In particular, by Lemma
6.4
,
\frac{z_{0}+z_{1}+z_{2}+z_{3}}{4}
is bounded. On the other hand, by Lemma
6.5
and (
6.35
), we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\frac{z_{0}(t)+z_{1}(t)+z_{2}(t)+z_{3}(t)}{4}

\displaystyle=z_{\infty}+\frac{\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}}{4}\left(\log{t}-\frac{d-1}{2}\log{(\log{t})}+c_{\star}\right)

\displaystyle\quad+O\left(\frac{\log{(\log{t})}}{\log{t}}\right).

Therefore,
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
satisfy

\displaystyle\omega_{1}+\omega_{2}+\omega_{3}=0,

which implies
\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}
form an equilateral triple. Therefore we complete the proof.

∎

Finally, we prove Theorem
1.1
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By Lemma
2.1
, there exist
C^{1}
functions
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}:[0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left\|u(t)-\left(Q(\cdot-z_{0}(t))-\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}=0,

\displaystyle\lim_{t\to\infty}\left(\min_{0\leq j<k\leq 3}|z_{k}(t)-z_{j}(t)|\right)=\infty,

and we have for
1\leq l\leq d
,
0\leq i\leq 3
, and
t\gg 1
,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Bigl\{{\partial}_{t}u(t)+2\alpha\Bigl(u(t)-Q(\cdot-z_{0}(t))+\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\Bigr)\Bigr\}\,{\partial}_{l}Q(\cdot-z_{i}(t))=0.

In this case, we apply the preceding analysis to this choice of
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
.
First, by Proposition
4.1
, (
2.19
) holds true. Therefore, by Lemma
2.4
and Lemma
5.2
, we have for
t\gg 1

\displaystyle\left\|u(t)-\left(Q(\cdot-z_{0}(t))-\sum_{k=1}^{3}Q(\cdot-z_{k}(t))\right)\right\|_{H^{1}}+\|{\partial}_{t}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim t^{-1},

which is (
1.6
). Choosing
c_{0}=c_{\star}
, the asymptotic behaviors of
z_{0},z_{1},z_{2},z_{3}
satisfy (
1.7
) by Lemma
6.6
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎
