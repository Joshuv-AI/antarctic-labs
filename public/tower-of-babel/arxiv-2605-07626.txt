Weighted Distributions of Complex Multiplication Orders in Ordinary Isogeny Classes
Mohammed El Baraka & Siham Ezzouak

Abstract
We develop a global arithmetic framework for the study of endomorphism rings inside ordinary elliptic isogeny classes over finite fields. Let p p be a prime and let ℐ ⁡ ( t , p ) \mathcal{I}(t,p) be an ordinary isogeny class over 𝔽 p \mathbb{F}_{p} with Frobenius trace t t , so that the discriminant Δ = t 2 − 4 ​ p \Delta=t^{2}-4p factors as Δ = v 2 ​ D K \Delta=v^{2}D_{K} . In this setting, the possible endomorphism rings are precisely the quadratic orders 𝒪 f = ℤ + f ​ 𝒪 K \mathcal{O}_{f}=\mathbb{Z}+f\mathcal{O}_{K} with f | v f\mid v . Building on Deuring’s correspondence, we express the distribution of these orders in terms of weighted class numbers h ∗ ​ ( D ) = h ⁡ ( D ) / w ⁡ ( D ) h^{\ast}(D)=h(D)/w(D) , thereby obtaining explicit formulas for global distributions across the entire isogeny class. This approach overcomes the limitations of the classical local viewpoint, in which the endomorphism ring is constant along each level of an ℓ \ell -isogeny volcano. In particular, we introduce weighted exact and cumulative distributions of endomorphism rings, which induce canonical laws for the ℓ \ell -adic valuation of conductors and recover the vertical stratification of ℓ \ell -volcanoes in an averaged sense. On the global side, varying the prime p p , we relate the existence of curves with a prescribed CM order 𝒪 D \mathcal{O}_{D} to splitting conditions in the associated ring class field L D L_{D} . These results establish a unified perspective linking Deuring theory, isogeny graph geometry, and class field theory, and provide a natural framework for quantitative and algorithmic studies of isogeny classes.

Introduction

Let
q=p^{n}
be a prime power and let
\ell\neq p
be a prime.
The
\ell
-isogeny graph
G_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is the graph whose vertices are the

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism classes of elliptic curves over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, and where two vertices
are joined by an edge if there exists an
\ell
-isogeny between them defined over

\mathbb{F}_{q}
. For ordinary elliptic curves, the connected components of
G_{\ell}(\mathbb{F}_{q})

exhibit a highly structured geometry known as
\ell
-isogeny volcanoes
,
which play a central role in computational number theory and in isogeny-based
cryptography
[
16
,
15
,
19
]
.

Arithmetic structure and limitations of the local viewpoint.

It is well known that if
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
is ordinary, then its endomorphism ring

\End(E)
is an order in an imaginary quadratic field
K
. More precisely,

\End(E)
lies between
\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{E}]
and the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and is
therefore of the form
\mathcal{O}_{f}=\mathbb{Z}+f\mathcal{O}_{K}
for a uniquely determined
conductor
f\geq 1
(see
[
3
,
21
,
18
]
).

In the
\ell
-isogeny graph, the
\ell
-adic valuation of this conductor controls
the vertical position of a vertex inside its volcano component: descending edges
correspond to multiplication of the conductor by
\ell
, while ascending edges
correspond to division by
\ell
. As a consequence, each volcano component is
stratified into levels on which the endomorphism ring is constant.

However, this
local, componentwise
viewpoint has an intrinsic limitation:
within a fixed volcano component, the endomorphism ring is constant on each level,
and any notion of “exact density” is therefore trivial, being supported on a single
level. This makes it impossible to extract meaningful statistical information
purely from the geometry of individual components.

Global distributions and weighted counting.

The main idea of this paper is to move beyond this limitation by introducing a

global arithmetic viewpoint
, where distributions are defined across the
entire ordinary isogeny class rather than within a single volcano component.

Fix a prime
p
and an ordinary isogeny class
\mathcal{I}(t,p)
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
Writing the Frobenius discriminant as

\Delta=t^{2}-4p=v^{2}D_{K},

the possible endomorphism rings in
\mathcal{I}(t,p)
are precisely the quadratic
orders
\mathcal{O}_{f}
with
f\mid v
.

A key feature of our approach is that we work with
weighted counts
,
expressed in terms of the quantities

h^{\ast}(D)=\frac{h(D)}{w(D)},

which naturally arise in Deuring’s correspondence. This avoids the well-known
pathologies caused by automorphisms (notably for
j=0
and
1728
), and yields
arithmetically canonical distributions.

Within this framework, we obtain explicit formulas for the global distribution of
endomorphism rings inside
\mathcal{I}(t,p)
, leading to two fundamental invariants:

•

a
weighted exact distribution
, describing the contribution of each order

\mathcal{O}_{f}
;

•

a
weighted cumulative distribution
, encoding the conductor filtration.

These distributions induce, for each prime
\ell\neq p
, a canonical global law for
the
\ell
-adic valuation of the conductor, which recovers the vertical structure
of
\ell
-isogeny volcanoes when averaged over all components.

Horizontal variation and class field theory.

On the global side, when the prime
p
varies, the existence of curves with
endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}_{D}
is governed by class field theory.
More precisely, for
p\nmid D
, the condition that there exists an elliptic curve

E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{D}
is equivalent to the complete splitting
of
p
in the ring class field
L_{D}
of
\mathcal{O}_{D}
, or equivalently to the
complete splitting of the Hilbert class polynomial
H_{D}
modulo
p

[
2
,
18
]
.

By the Chebotarev density theorem, this yields a natural density

\frac{1}{[L_{D}:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{1}{2h(D)}

for such primes. This provides a
horizontal distribution law
that complements
the vertical conductor distribution inside a fixed isogeny class.

Main contributions.

The contributions of this paper can be summarised as follows:

•

we introduce a global framework for studying endomorphism rings inside
ordinary isogeny classes via weighted distributions;

•

we derive explicit formulas for these distributions in terms of weighted
class numbers, providing a non-trivial refinement of Deuring’s correspondence;

•

we establish a canonical connection between these global distributions and
the vertical structure of
\ell
-isogeny volcanoes;

•

we relate the occurrence of prescribed CM orders across varying primes to
Chebotarev densities in ring class fields.

Perspective.

These results highlight a fundamental duality between:

•

vertical, conductor-driven distributions inside a fixed isogeny class;

•

horizontal, Chebotarev-governed distributions as the base field varies.

This unified viewpoint provides a natural arithmetic framework for understanding
the structure of isogeny graphs and suggests further connections with both
computational and cryptographic applications.

2.1
Ordinary isogeny classes and quadratic orders

Let
q=p^{n}
with
p
prime, and let
E/\mathbb{F}_{q}
be an elliptic curve with Frobenius
endomorphism
\pi=\pi_{E}
. Its characteristic polynomial is

X^{2}-tX+q,\qquad t=\tr(\pi),\qquad\#E(\mathbb{F}_{q})=q+1-t,

and its discriminant is

\Delta_{\pi}:=t^{2}-4q<0.

If
E
is ordinary, then
K:=\mathbb{Q}(\pi)
is an imaginary quadratic field and one has

\mathbb{Z}[\pi]\subseteq\End(E)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K},

where
\mathcal{O}_{K}
is the maximal order of
K

(see
[
21
,
18
]
).

Every order
\mathcal{O}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K}
can be written uniquely as

\mathcal{O}_{f}=\mathbb{Z}+f\,\mathcal{O}_{K},\qquad f=[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}_{f}]\geq 1,

and its discriminant satisfies

\disc(\mathcal{O}_{f})=f^{2}D_{K},

where
D_{K}
is the fundamental discriminant of
K

(see
[
2
, Ch. 7]
).

Lemma 2.1

(Inclusion of quadratic orders)
.

For
f,f^{\prime}\geq 1
, one has

\mathcal{O}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{f^{\prime}}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad f^{\prime}\mid f.

Proof.

If
f^{\prime}\mid f
, write
f=f^{\prime}m
and obtain

\mathcal{O}_{f}=\mathbb{Z}+f\mathcal{O}_{K}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}+f^{\prime}\mathcal{O}_{K}=\mathcal{O}_{f^{\prime}}
.
Conversely, if
\mathcal{O}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{f^{\prime}}
, then

[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}_{f^{\prime}}]\mid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}_{f}]
, hence
f^{\prime}\mid f
.
∎

Remark 2.2

(Terminology)
.

Over finite fields, every ordinary elliptic curve has complex multiplication in the
broad sense that
\End(E)
is an order in an imaginary quadratic field.
Throughout this paper, the statement “
E
has CM by
\mathcal{O}
” means the precise condition

\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}
.

2.2
Existence of curves with prescribed endomorphism ring

Fix an imaginary quadratic order
\mathcal{O}_{f}
of discriminant
D=f^{2}D_{K}<0
.
Let

\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\{\,[E]:E/\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{ ordinary with }\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{f}\,\}

denote the set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism classes with prescribed endomorphism ring.

The following result is a consequence of Deuring’s theory and Waterhouse’s classification

[
3
,
21
]
.

Proposition 2.3

(Deuring–Waterhouse criterion)
.

Let
q=p^{n}
with
p\nmid D
. Then
\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
is non-empty if and only if
there exist integers
t,v
such that

4q=t^{2}-v^{2}D,\qquad t\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}.

Remark 2.4
.

When non-empty, the size of
\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{f}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
depends on the arithmetic of
q

and the factorisation of the Hilbert class polynomial
H_{D}
modulo
p
.
In particular, over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and under complete splitting of
H_{D}
, one obtains
h(D)

distinct
j
-invariants.

2.3
\ell
-isogenies and the volcano stratification

Fix a prime
\ell\neq p
. Consider the
\ell
-isogeny graph restricted to an ordinary
isogeny class over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
: vertices are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism classes of ordinary elliptic curves,
and edges correspond to
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rational cyclic
\ell
-isogenies.

The structure of connected components is governed by the
\ell
-adic valuation of the
conductor and the splitting behaviour of
\ell
in
K
.

Let
D=\disc(\End(E))
and denote by
\big(\frac{D}{\ell}\big)
the Kronecker symbol.
The following structure theorem is due to Kohel and subsequent refinements

[
16
,
15
,
19
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Volcano structure)
.

Assume the connected component contains no curves with
j=0
or
1728
.
Then it has the structure of an
\ell
-volcano: its vertices are partitioned into levels

V_{0},\dots,V_{d}
such that

1.

all vertices in
V_{i}
have the same endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}_{i}
, and

v_{\ell}([\mathcal{O}_{K}:\mathcal{O}_{i}])=i;

2.

edges are of three types:

•

horizontal edges preserve
\End(E)
;

•

descending edges multiply the conductor by
\ell
;

•

ascending edges divide the conductor by
\ell
;

and each vertex with
i>0
has a unique ascending neighbour;

3.

the number of outgoing
\ell
-isogenies depends only on whether
\ell

divides the conductor:

•

if
\ell\nmid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\End(E)]
, then there are

1+\big(\frac{D}{\ell}\big)
horizontal edges and

\ell-\big(\frac{D}{\ell}\big)
descending edges;

•

if
\ell\mid[\mathcal{O}_{K}:\End(E)]
, then there is exactly one ascending edge,
no horizontal edges, and
\ell
descending edges.

Definition 2.6

(
\ell
-volcano)
.

An
\ell
-volcano is a connected graph whose vertices are partitioned into levels

V_{0},\dots,V_{d}
such that:

•

each vertex in
V_{i}
(
i>0
) has exactly one neighbour in
V_{i-1}
;

•

edges either stay within a level or connect consecutive levels;

•

vertices in
V_{i}
for
i<d
have degree
\ell+1
.

The level
V_{0}
is called the surface and
V_{d}
the floor.

2.4
Notation

In the sequel, we work inside a fixed ordinary isogeny class over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
For a given
\ell
, we consider the decomposition

V=\bigsqcup_{i=0}^{d}V_{i},

where all curves in
V_{i}
have endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}_{i}
.

In Section
3
, we introduce global (weighted)
distributions of endomorphism rings across the entire isogeny class.

3
Order distribution inside one ordinary isogeny class over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

In this section we fix a
single ordinary
isogeny class over a prime field

\mathbb{F}_{p}
and describe how endomorphism rings are distributed across the entire
isogeny class. In contrast with the local (componentwise) volcano picture,
this global viewpoint yields genuinely non-trivial distribution laws.

3.1
The ordinary isogeny class and the conductor parameter

Let
p
be a prime and let
t\in\mathbb{Z}
satisfy

t^{2}<4p\qquad\text{and}\qquad p\nmid t.

Then the isogeny class
\mathcal{I}(t,p)
of elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{F}_{p}
with

\tr(\pi_{E})=t
is ordinary
[
21
]
.

Set

\Delta:=t^{2}-4p<0,\qquad K:=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{\Delta}).

Write uniquely

\Delta=v^{2}D_{K},

(3.1)

where
D_{K}<0
is the fundamental discriminant of
K
and
v\geq 1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
denote the maximal order of
K
. For each divisor
f\mid v
,
define the quadratic order

\mathcal{O}_{f}:=\mathbb{Z}+f\,\mathcal{O}_{K},\qquad D_{f}:=\disc(\mathcal{O}_{f})=f^{2}D_{K}.

For every
E\in\mathcal{I}(t,p)
one has

\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{E}]\subseteq\End(E)\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{K},

and
\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{f}
for a unique divisor
f\mid v

(Deuring
[
3
]
, Waterhouse
[
21
]
).

3.2
Weighted class numbers and Deuring decomposition

Let

w(D):=\#\mathcal{O}_{D}^{\times}

denote the number of units in the quadratic order of discriminant
D
, and define
the
weighted class number

h^{\ast}(D):=\frac{h(D)}{w(D)}.

The following theorem expresses the decomposition of the isogeny class in terms
of weighted class numbers.

Theorem 3.1

(Weighted Deuring decomposition)
.

Let
p
be prime and
t
satisfy
t^{2}<4p
and
p\nmid t
, and write

\Delta=t^{2}-4p=v^{2}D_{K}
. Then:

1.

For every
E\in\mathcal{I}(t,p)
one has
\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{f}
for a unique

f\mid v
.

2.

The isogeny class admits the decomposition

H(\Delta)=\sum_{f\mid v}h^{\ast}(D_{f}),

(3.2)

where
H(\Delta)
is the Hurwitz class number.

3.

More precisely, the contribution of curves with endomorphism ring

\mathcal{O}_{f}
is given by the weighted count
h^{\ast}(D_{f})
.

Proof.

This follows from Deuring’s correspondence between ordinary isogeny classes
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and equivalence classes of positive definite binary quadratic forms
of discriminant
\Delta
(see
[
3
,
21
,
2
]
).
The decomposition according to endomorphism rings corresponds to partitioning
these classes according to their associated orders.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

The quantities
h^{\ast}(D_{f})
should be interpreted as
weighted counts
,
reflecting the contribution of automorphism groups. In particular, they do not
always correspond to literal cardinalities of sets of isomorphism classes.

3.3
Global weighted densities

The previous decomposition naturally defines global distributions inside the
isogeny class.

Definition 3.3

(Weighted exact density)
.

For
f\mid v
, define

\Delta^{=,\mathrm{wt}}_{f}(t,p):=\frac{h^{\ast}(D_{f})}{\sum_{g\mid v}h^{\ast}(D_{g})}.

Definition 3.4

(Weighted containment density)
.

For
f\mid v
, define

\Delta^{\supseteq,\mathrm{wt}}_{f}(t,p):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}g\mid v\\
f\mid g\end{subarray}}\Delta^{=,\mathrm{wt}}_{g}(t,p).

Proposition 3.5

(Explicit global distributions)
.

For each
f\mid v
,

\Delta^{=,\mathrm{wt}}_{f}(t,p)=\frac{h^{\ast}(D_{f})}{\sum_{g\mid v}h^{\ast}(D_{g})},\qquad\Delta^{\supseteq,\mathrm{wt}}_{f}(t,p)=\frac{\sum_{g\mid v,\ f\mid g}h^{\ast}(D_{g})}{\sum_{g\mid v}h^{\ast}(D_{g})}.

Proof.

This follows directly from the decomposition (
3.2
) and the inclusion
relation
\mathcal{O}_{f}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{g}\iff f\mid g
.
∎

3.4
Vertical
\ell
-adic distribution

Fix a prime
\ell\neq p
. The
\ell
-adic valuation of the conductor
controls the position of a curve inside its
\ell
-volcano component.

Define the
level mass

M_{i}(t,p;\ell):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\mid v\\
v_{\ell}(f)=i\end{subarray}}h^{\ast}(D_{f}).

Corollary 3.6

(Global
\ell
-adic distribution)
.

For every
i\geq 0
,

\Pr\big[v_{\ell}(\mathrm{cond}(\End(E)))=i\big]=\frac{M_{i}(t,p;\ell)}{\sum_{g\mid v}h^{\ast}(D_{g})}.

Remark 3.7
.

This distribution provides a canonical global description of the vertical
structure of
\ell
-isogeny volcanoes when averaged over all components.

4
Varying the prime: ring class fields and Chebotarev densities

Fix an imaginary quadratic discriminant
D<0
and let

\mathcal{O}_{D}=\mathbb{Z}+f\,\mathcal{O}_{K}

be the quadratic order of discriminant
D=f^{2}D_{K}
in the imaginary quadratic field

K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{D_{K}})
.

Let
H_{D}(X)\in\mathbb{Z}[X]
denote the Hilbert class polynomial of discriminant
D
, and let

L_{D}
be the associated
ring class field
of
\mathcal{O}_{D}
, i.e. the maximal
abelian extension of
K
corresponding to the ideal class group
\Cl(\mathcal{O}_{D})
.

One has

[L_{D}:K]=h(D),\qquad[L_{D}:\mathbb{Q}]=2h(D),

(4.1)

and there is a canonical isomorphism

\Gal(L_{D}/K)\simeq\Cl(\mathcal{O}_{D})

via the Artin reciprocity map (see
[
2
, Ch. 9–11]
).

4.1
CM existence over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
and splitting of
H_{D}\bmod p

For a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
p
, define

\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{D}}(\mathbb{F}_{q}):=\{\,j(E)\in\mathbb{F}_{q}:E/\mathbb{F}_{q}\text{ ordinary with }\End(E)\simeq\mathcal{O}_{D}\,\}.

We recall the classical equivalence relating CM existence, Hilbert class polynomials,
and splitting in the ring class field.

Theorem 4.1

(CM existence and splitting criterion)
.

Let
p
be a prime such that
p\nmid D
. The following are equivalent:

1.

\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{D}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\varnothing
;

2.

the polynomial
H_{D}(X)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
;

3.

H_{D}(X)
splits completely into linear factors in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[X]
;

4.

p
splits completely in the ring class field
L_{D}/\mathbb{Q}
.

Moreover, when these conditions hold, the polynomial
H_{D}
has exactly
h(D)
distinct
roots in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
, corresponding to
h(D)
distinct
j
-invariants.

Proof.

Assume
p\nmid D
. By Deuring’s reduction theory, an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{F}_{p}

has endomorphism ring
\mathcal{O}_{D}
if and only if the Frobenius at
p
acts trivially
on the set of CM
j
-invariants of discriminant
D
.

By class field theory, this is equivalent to
p
splitting completely in the ring
class field
L_{D}
. The Galois action on the roots of
H_{D}
is identified with

\Gal(L_{D}/K)
, and the factorisation of
H_{D}\bmod p
is determined by the Frobenius
conjugacy class.

In particular,
H_{D}
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
if and only if Frobenius acts trivially,
which is equivalent to complete splitting. In this case, all
h(D)
roots lie in
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.
See
[
3
,
21
,
2
,
18
]
.
∎

Remark 4.2

(Isomorphism classes)
.

The set
\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{D}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})
parametrises
j
-invariants rather than
isomorphism classes of curves. Different curves with the same
j
-invariant may occur
as quadratic twists, but this does not affect the counting of CM points.

Remark 4.3

(Prime powers)
.

Let
q=p^{n}
with
p\nmid D
. Then
\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{D}}(\mathbb{F}_{q})\neq\varnothing

if and only if the Frobenius element at
p
in
\Gal(L_{D}/\mathbb{Q})
has order dividing
n
.
Equivalently,
H_{D}(X)
has a root in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{n}}
.

4.2
Chebotarev density

We now describe the asymptotic frequency of primes
p
for which CM by
\mathcal{O}_{D}

occurs over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Theorem 4.4

(Chebotarev density for CM primes)
.

Let
D<0
and let
L_{D}/\mathbb{Q}
be the ring class field of
\mathcal{O}_{D}
.
Then the set

\mathcal{P}_{D}:=\{\,p\text{ prime}:p\nmid D\text{ and }\Ell_{\mathcal{O}_{D}}(\mathbb{F}_{p})\neq\varnothing\,\}

has natural density

\dens(\mathcal{P}_{D})=\frac{1}{[L_{D}:\mathbb{Q}]}=\frac{1}{2h(D)}.

(4.2)

Proof.

By Theorem
4.1
, the set
\mathcal{P}_{D}
consists exactly of primes
that split completely in
L_{D}/\mathbb{Q}
(excluding finitely many primes dividing
D
).

By the Chebotarev density theorem, primes whose Frobenius element lies in the identity
conjugacy class have density
1/[L_{D}:\mathbb{Q}]
. Using (
4.1
) gives
(
4.2
). See
[
17
]
.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Effective form)
.

Effective versions of Chebotarev yield, for

\pi_{D}(x):=\#\{\,p\leq x:\ p\in\mathcal{P}_{D}\,\},

an asymptotic of the form

\pi_{D}(x)=\frac{\Li(x)}{2h(D)}+O\!\left(x\,e^{-c\sqrt{\log x}}\right),

for some explicit constant
c>0
depending on
L_{D}
.

5
Conclusion

In this work, we have provided a unified arithmetic framework for the study of
endomorphism rings within ordinary elliptic isogeny classes over finite fields.
Building on the Deuring–Waterhouse classification, we showed that the possible
endomorphism rings
\End(E)
are precisely the quadratic orders lying between

\mathbb{Z}[\pi_{E}]
and the maximal order
\mathcal{O}_{K}
, and are naturally indexed by
the divisors of the conductor.

Our main contribution is to move beyond the local, componentwise perspective of

\ell
-isogeny volcanoes and to introduce a genuinely global viewpoint. By
expressing the distribution of endomorphism rings in terms of weighted class
numbers, we obtained explicit formulas for global densities across the entire
isogeny class. These distributions provide a canonical probabilistic description
of conductor values and, consequently, of the vertical structure of
\ell
-volcanoes
when averaged over all components.

On the horizontal axis, varying the prime
p
, we related the occurrence of a
fixed CM order
\mathcal{O}_{D}
to splitting conditions in the associated ring
class field
L_{D}
. This yields a precise asymptotic frequency governed by the
Chebotarev density theorem, namely a natural density of
1/(2h(D))
for primes
admitting CM by
\mathcal{O}_{D}
. This establishes a clear bridge between the
arithmetic of class fields and the statistical behaviour of isogeny classes.

Taken together, these results highlight two complementary distribution laws:
a vertical, conductor-driven structure inside a fixed isogeny class, and a
horizontal, Chebotarev-governed distribution as the base field varies. This
duality provides a coherent framework for understanding the arithmetic and
combinatorial properties of isogeny graphs.

Beyond their intrinsic theoretical interest, these results have potential
applications in computational number theory and in isogeny-based cryptography,
where understanding the distribution of endomorphism rings is crucial for
algorithmic navigation, parameter selection, and security analysis.

Several directions for future work naturally arise. One may seek to refine these
distributions at the level of individual volcano components, to obtain
non-weighted counting results under additional arithmetic constraints, or to
extend the analysis to higher-dimensional abelian varieties and more general
isogeny graphs.
