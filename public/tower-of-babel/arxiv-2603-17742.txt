Inflation with the Gauss–Bonnet term in the Palatini formulation
and Syksy Räsänen

Abstract
We consider the Gauss–Bonnet term coupled to the inflaton in the Palatini formulation of gravity. Unlike in the metric formulation, the Gauss–Bonnet term is not always a total derivative. We solve for the connection and insert it into the action, exactly for the spatially flat FLRW spacetime, and using the gradient approximation and order reduction for a general spacetime. We consider three cases: when the connection is unconstrained, and when non-metricity or torsion is put to zero. In all cases, the leading order change to the inflaton kinetic has the same form as that generated by the Chern–Simons term, but a negative sign. The modification of the gravitational wave sector also has the same form as in the Chern–Simons case but with a negative sign, except possibly for zero torsion, depending on the coupling and the potential. Within the range of validity of our approximations, differences from the metric formulation are small unless the kinetic term flips sign or is close to doing so.

2.1
Geometrical quantities

2.1.1
Nonmetricity, torsion, and curvature

In the Palatini formulation the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
and the connection
\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}
are independent variables. We decompose the connection, defined with the covariant derivative as
\nabla_{\beta}A^{\alpha}=\partial_{\beta}A^{\alpha}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\beta\gamma}A^{\gamma}
,
\nabla_{\beta}A_{\alpha}=\partial_{\beta}A_{\alpha}-\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\beta\alpha}A_{\gamma}
, as

\displaystyle\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathring{\Gamma}^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}+L^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha\beta}=\mathring{\Gamma}^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}+J^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha\beta}+K^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha\beta}\ ,

(2.1)

where
\mathring{\Gamma}_{\alpha\beta}^{\gamma}
is the Levi–Civita connection of the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}
and
L^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha\beta}
is the distortion tensor. We denote quantities defined with the Levi–Civita connection with
\mathring{}
. In the second equality we have decomposed
L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
into the disformation
J_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
and the contortion
K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
, defined as

\displaystyle J_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left(Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-Q_{\gamma\alpha\beta}-Q_{\beta\alpha\gamma}\right)\ ,\quad K_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\equiv\frac{1}{2}(T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}+T_{\gamma\alpha\beta}+T_{\beta\alpha\gamma})\ ,

(2.2)

where
Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
and
T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
are the non-metricity and the torsion, respectively, defined as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}\equiv\nabla_{\alpha}g_{\beta\gamma}=-2L_{(\gamma|\alpha|\beta)}=-2J_{(\gamma|\alpha|\beta)}\ ,\quad T^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle 2\Gamma^{\alpha}_{[\beta\gamma]}=2L^{\alpha}{}_{[\beta\gamma]}=2K^{\alpha}{}_{[\beta\gamma]}\ .

(2.3)

We have
Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=Q_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}
,
J_{\alpha\beta\gamma}=J_{\alpha(\beta\gamma)}
, and
K^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}{}^{\gamma}=K^{[\alpha}{}_{\beta}{}^{\gamma]}
.

We decompose the Riemann tensor into the Levi–Civita part and the distortion part as

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}

\displaystyle\equiv

\displaystyle\partial_{\gamma}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta\beta}-\partial_{\delta}\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\gamma\beta}+\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\gamma\mu}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\delta\beta}-\Gamma^{\alpha}_{\delta\mu}\Gamma^{\mu}_{\gamma\beta}=\mathring{R}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}+2\mathring{\nabla}_{[\gamma}L^{\alpha}{}_{\delta]\beta}+2L^{\alpha}{}_{[\gamma|\mu|}L^{\mu}{}_{\delta]\beta}\ .

(2.4)

There are three independent first contractions of the Riemann tensor. We need only two: the Ricci tensor
R_{\alpha\beta}\equiv R^{\gamma}{}_{\alpha\gamma\beta}
and the co-Ricci tensor
\hat{R}^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\equiv g^{\mu\nu}R^{\alpha}{}_{\mu\nu\beta}
. Note that neither is symmetric. When non-metricity vanishes, we have
\hat{R}_{\alpha\beta}=-R_{\alpha\beta}
. The unique full contraction of the Riemann tensor is the Ricci scalar
R\equiv g^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta}
. The Einstein tensor (defined by the variation of the Einstein–Hilbert action with respect to the metric) is
G_{\alpha\beta}\equiv R_{(\alpha\beta)}-\frac{1}{2}g_{\alpha\beta}R
.

2.1.2
Projective transformation

The projective transformation of the connection is defined as
[
86
]

\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}\to\Gamma^{\gamma}_{\alpha\beta}+\delta^{\gamma}{}_{\beta}A_{\alpha}\ ,

(2.5)

where
A_{\alpha}
is an arbitrary vector. Under this transformation non-metricity and torsion change as

\displaystyle Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\to Q_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-2g_{\beta\gamma}A_{\alpha}

\displaystyle T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}

\displaystyle\to T_{\alpha\beta\gamma}-2g_{\alpha}{}_{[\beta}A_{\gamma]}\ .

(2.6)

The Riemann tensor transforms as

\displaystyle R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}\to R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}+2\delta^{\alpha}{}_{\beta}\partial_{[\gamma}A_{\delta]}\ .

(2.7)

If the theory is invariant under the projective transformation and either non-metricity or torsion appears only in the form (
2.1.2
), it can be removed by a projective transformation. If both non-metricity and torsion have the form (
2.1.2
), they can be completely exchanged for each other. (This is the case in
f(R)
gravity and in the simplest Higgs inflation model
[
87
,
88
]
.) In a FLRW spacetime, due to symmetry the torsion tensor can only have terms proportional to
g_{\alpha[\beta}u_{\gamma]}
and
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}u^{\delta}
, where
u^{\alpha}
is the four-velocity orthogonal to the hypersurface of symmetry and
\epsilon_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}
is the Levi–Civita tensor. The first term has the form (
2.1.2
) and the second violates parity, so if the theory is projectively invariant and parity-invariant, it is always possible to eliminate the torsion in a FLRW spacetime. In contrast, non-metricity in a FLRW spacetime is not limited to the form (
2.1.2
), so it cannot always be transformed away.

is not a total derivative for arbitrary non-metricity

.

2.2
Action and equations of motion

2.2.1
Action

We consider a scalar field coupled to the Gauss–Bonnet term, with the action (in units where the reduced Planck mass is unity)

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=\int\mathrm{d}^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\left[\frac{1}{2}R-\frac{1}{2}K(\varphi)X-V(\varphi)-E(\varphi)\mathcal{G}\right]\ ,

(2.10)

where we have included the non-minimal kinetic function
K(\varphi)
and the direct Gauss–Bonnet or Euler coupling
E(\varphi)
; we denote
X_{\alpha\beta}\equiv\partial_{\alpha}\varphi\partial_{\beta}\varphi
,
X\equiv X^{\alpha}{}_{\alpha}
. In the action, the Riemann tensor
R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
depends only on the connection, not on the metric. The action is projectively invariant. Varying the Gauss–Bonnet contribution with respect to
R^{\alpha}{}_{\beta\gamma\delta}
gives
-6E\Sigma^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}
. A direct coupling to the Ricci scalar of the form
F(\varphi)R
could be eliminated with the conformal transformation of the metric
g_{\alpha\beta}\to F^{-1}g_{\alpha\beta}
, which would shift
K\to K/F
,
V\to V/F^{2}
,
E\to E
, so we can neglect such coupling without loss of generality.

3.1.2
Metric action

Inserting the spatially flat FLRW metric ansatz (

3.2
Gradient approximation

3.2.1
The expansion scheme

As in the Chern–Simons case
[
77
]
, to find the equations for the perturbations, we solve the
L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
equation of motion (
2.2.3
) in the gradient approximation and insert the solution back into the action to obtain a theory that depends only on
g_{\alpha\beta}
and
\varphi
. By the gradient approximation we mean that
\partial_{\alpha}\varphi
is small, both for time and spatial derivatives. This expansion is well suited to slow-roll inflation in the super-Hubble regime. One difference from the Chern–Simons case is that the Gauss–Bonnet term is not always a total derivative. The first term on the right-hand side of (
2.2.3
) is proportional to
\partial_{\alpha}E=E^{\prime}\partial_{\alpha}\varphi
analogously to the Chern–Simons case, but the second term is proportional to
E
, and has no Chern–Simons counterpart. However, we have seen that for the spatially flat FLRW background the solution depends only on
\partial_{\alpha}E
. Therefore, in the gradient approximation even the terms proportional to
E
are suppressed by
\partial_{\alpha}E
, as we know that they vanish when
\partial_{\alpha}E=0
. So
\partial_{\alpha}E
works as an expansion parameter controlling the validity of our effective field theory, but unlike in the FLRW case, we can also have terms of the form
E\partial_{\alpha}E
, which are not parametrically smaller than
\partial_{\alpha}E
. The requirement that
\partial_{\alpha}E
is small could be satisfied with a small
E^{\prime}
or
\partial_{\alpha}\varphi
: in the gradient approximation
\partial_{\alpha}\varphi
is small, so
E^{\prime}
can be large.

Solving for the connection and inserting it back into the action to obtain a theory only for the metric and the scalar field results in higher order derivative terms that are not of the Horndeski nor DHOST form, so as a fundamental theory it would likely have ghosts
[
95
,
96
,
97
]
. (Going to higher orders in the solution for
L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
would generate higher order derivatives with no upper limit, so the stability properties are not clear.) But we take even the original action (
2.10
) to be an effective theory, and apply an order reduction procedure to eliminate the higher derivatives in the action
[
98
,
99
,
100
,
101
,
102
,
19
,
60
,
20
]
. As in
[
77
]
, we split the Levi–Civita Riemann tensors in the action into Ricci and Weyl parts using (
2.16
). We then replace the Ricci tensor in the higher derivative terms (but not in the Levi–Civita Gauss–Bonnet term, which does not lead to higher order equations of motion) by using the lowest order (in the gradient expansion) metric equation of motion (
2.11
) with the energy-momentum tensor (
2.12
), which simply reads
\mathring{R}_{\alpha\beta}=Vg_{\alpha\beta}
. In the action (
2.10
) there is only one term linear in
L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
that is not a boundary term, namely the second term after the last equality in (
2.8
), multiplied by
-E
. Partial integration shifts the derivative to
E
, so even for this term we need to know
L_{\alpha\beta\gamma}
only to linear order in
\partial_{\alpha}E
to get the action to second order in
\partial_{\alpha}E
.

We consider the three cases when no constraints are imposed on the connection and when either non-metricity or torsion is taken to be zero. Let us start from the case when the connection is unconstrained.

4
Conclusion

We have studied the Gauss–Bonnet term directly coupled to the inflaton in the Palatini formulation, following our work on the Chern–Simons term in the Palatini formulation
[
77
]
. In contrast to the metric formulation, the Gauss–Bonnet term is not always a total derivative if non-metricity is non-zero, and in general it leads to extra degrees of freedom, some of which may be unstable. We consider three cases: when the connection is unconstrained, and when either non-metricity or torsion is put to zero a priori. We solve the connection equation of motion exactly in the spatially flat FLRW spacetime and insert it back into the action. The result is the same in all three cases. The leading order correction to the inflaton kinetic term has the same form as in the Chern–Simons case, but the sign is negative, although the full kinetic term is bounded from below. (In the Chern–Simons case the prefactor is positive in the unconstrained and the zero non-metricity case, and zero in the zero torsion case.) So the correction can only have a big impact if the kinetic term flips sign or is close to doing so, which could enable novel behaviour. The correction to the kinetic term is small in slow-roll inflation, but could play a role during preheating
[
79
,
82
,
83
]
.

We then consider perturbations. We treat the action as an effective theory and use the gradient approximation, as well as order reduction to eliminate higher derivatives. As with the Chern–Simons term, the unconstrained and the zero non-metricity case turn out to be identical at leading order, and the Gauss–Bonnet term is a total derivative, unlike in the zero torsion case. In all cases, the leading order Palatini corrections to the gravitational wave action turn out to have exactly the same form as with the Chern–Simons term, just with a different prefactor. In the Chern–Simons case, the corrections cured a metric formulation gradient instability, here they destabilise the theory further, except in the zero torsion case possibly somewhere in field space, as the prefactor is field dependent and can have either sign. However, because of the gradient approximation, the new gradient terms must be subleading, so the theory is stable within the range of validity of the approximation, as in the Chern–Simons case.

The Gauss-Bonnet term alone in the Palatini formulation in the unconstrained case has 9 degrees of freedom in the region of solution space where the constraint matrix has maximum rank
[
76
]
. It remains to be determined how many degrees of freedom there are, and whether any of them are ghosts, when the Einstein–Hilbert term and direct coupling to a scalar field are included, and when torsion is put to zero. If all degrees of freedom turn out to be healthy, the theory can be studied without resort to gradient approximation and order reduction to check whether the results found here have wider validity. Our analysis of the spatially flat FLRW spacetime is exact, and shows that in this case there are no new degrees of freedom.
