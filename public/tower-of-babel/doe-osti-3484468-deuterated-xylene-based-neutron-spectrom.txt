 Conclusions

 • Deuterated xylene effectively differentiates between charged particle interactions
 and gamma ray interactions
 − Allows for accurate neutron spectrometry
 • Deuterated xylene can unfold measured 241AmBe spectra well
 − Can also unfold D-T, D-D, 252Cf (not shown in this slideshow)
 − 241AmBe, 252Cf spectra representative of most unknown nuclear material
 (fission sources and (𝛼𝛼, n)
 • Unfolded counts are absolute; the unfolding algorithm gives a rough idea of how
 strong your source is based on the measurement time and distance away from
 the source

 Extra B – PSD coefficient determination

 • Creating a good PSD cut is essential for
 spectrum unfolding
 − Too strict and you lose a lot of possible
 neutron pulses, which will skew the
 unfolded spectrum
 − Too loose and you include too many
 gamma pulses, which will introduce noise
 • Need to find a way to determine a cut that
 maximizes the number of neutron pulses and
 minimizes the number of gamma pulses

 Extra B – PSD coefficient determination

 • Solution: Break the entire TTR versus total integral heatmap into slices, and find
 the TTR that gives the minimum neutron-gamma overlap for each slice
 − Slice the tail versus total heatmap into chunks
 − Calculate the TTR for all pulses in each chunk
 − Fit the a two-term Gaussian to each TTR histogram
 − Find the TTR that minimizes the overlapping area between the gamma and
 neutron Gaussians
 − Fit a curve to the optimal TTRs
