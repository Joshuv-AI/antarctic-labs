Introducing the Pregnancy and Lactation Section
Authors: Kelly K. Park, Nanette B. Silverberg
Published: 2026

Pregnancy and the peripartum period, including lactation, are pivotal times in women’s lives and are marked by significant physiological changes that can impact skin health. As of 2021, there were 1.9 billion women of reproductive age (15-49 years), and worldwide, as of 2024, there were 2.25 live births per woman.1,2 Globally, exclusive breastfeeding rates have increased by 10 percentage points over the past decade, reaching 48%.3 This is in addition to those who elect to exclusively pump or do a combination of breastmilk and formula. The World Health Organization recommends breastfeeding for at least 2 years.4 Breastfeeding has health benefits to the mother and the child, including but not limited to reduced childhood infections and malocclusion, whereas nursing mothers experience long-term risk reduction in breast cancer and improved birth spacing. A recent meta-analysis projected potential prevention of 823,000 annual deaths in children less than 5 years and 20,000 annual deaths from breast cancer.5

These statistics underscore the significant number of women who would benefit from specialized dermatological care during pregnancy and lactation. Furthermore, the absence of data and clear messaging for pregnant and lactating women results in poor health outcomes. A study looking at women with rheumatologic disease demonstrates that there are consequences to a lack of clear guidance on healthcare during pregnancy and lactation, and these result in health impairments for the patient and impairments in the doctor–patient relationship.6

Therefore, it is with pleasure that we announce that starting in January 2026 the International Journal of Women’s Dermatology will feature a new section entitled “Pregnancy and Lactation.” Its purpose is to highlight the unique dermatological challenges and emerging research during this special time in women’s lives. This is a practice gap in dermatology, as it is a critical yet underrepresented area in dermatology training, research, and practice.

The concept for this section was developed by Dr. Kelly K. Park and brought to the attention of the IJWD’s Editor-in-Chief, Dr. Nanette B. Silverberg, who supported the proposal. The idea for the section was presented at the IJWD Editorial Board Meeting at the 2025 American Academy of Dermatology and was met with enthusiasm. Dr. Silverberg graciously overcame administrative hurdles to officially include the section in the journal. The mission of this section is to create a dedicated section; we hope the IJWD will become a leading source of information and support for dermatologists involving the skin and maternal health, while ultimately improving patient outcomes. Knowledge of pregnancy and lactation outcomes is power for the dermatologic patient and their children, and for the dermatologist.

In this issue, to inaugurate the section, Dr. Park has co-authored: “Dermatologic Issues Arising in Lactation: A Review” to bring to the attention of dermatologists the possible concerns of postpartum and lactating women. This manuscript was written in a multidisciplinary fashion with Dr. Katrina Mitchell, a well-known breast surgical oncologist and breastfeeding medicine specialist.
