Abstract
. In joint work with Lacini and Sheridan, we proved that the first and second secant varieties of a smooth projective complex variety embedded by the complete linear system of a sufficiently positive line bundle are projectively normal. The purpose of this paper is to establish effective results on how positive the embedding line bundle must be for this result to hold. We also provide effective conditions under which the defining ideal of the first secant variety is generated by cubics, and furthermore, generated by 3 × 3 3\times 3 -minors of a matrix of linear forms. The latter result gives an effective version of a theorem of Agostini and the second author.

Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n

Note that

.
It is a natural problem to study what kinds of singularities secant varieties possess. After the pioneering work on secant varieties of algebraic curves by Bertram
[
3
]
and Vermeire
[

]

[

,

]
). It was proven that the

-th secant variety

has normal Du Bois singularities when

by Chou–Song
[
10
, Theorem 1.2]
and Ullery
[

, Corollary C]
,

[

The primary aim of this paper is to make these results effective for

and

. For this purpose, we consider a line bundle on

of the form

with

If

is
(2k+1)
-very ample, then
\operatorname{Sing}(\Sigma_{k})=\Sigma_{k-1}

except when
(X,L)=(\mathbb{P}^{1},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{P}^{1}}(2k+1))
, in which case,
\Sigma_{k}=\mathbb{P}^{2k+1}

[
8
, Corollary E]
. Since a sharp effective result has already been established for the case of algebraic curves
[
14
]
, we henceforth assume that

.
It is known that

has normal Du Bois singularities when

(see
[

and

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
. For an integer

, let

be a line bundle on
X
with
H
very ample and
B
nef. When either

and

or

and

(1)

\Sigma_{k}=\Sigma_{k}(X,L)
has normal Du Bois singularities.

(2)

\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}H^{0}(X,L)=\mathbb{P}^{r}
is projectively normal.

(3)

H^{i}(\Sigma_{k},\mathcal{O}_{\Sigma_{k}}(\ell))=0
for
i>0
and
\ell>0
.

(4)

\Sigma_{k}
is Cohen–Macaulay if and only if
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for
1\leq i\leq n-1
. In this case,
\Sigma_{k}\subseteq\mathbb{P}^{r}

(5)

\Sigma_{k}
has rational singularities if and only if
H^{i}(X,\mathcal{O}_{X})=0
for
1\leq i\leq n
.

There has been a great deal of work on equations defining secant varieties (see e.g.,

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

. For an integer

, let

-minors of a matrix of linear forms when

More precisely, if

-minors of the

\operatorname{Cat}(L_{1},L_{2}):=\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{1}\beta_{1}&\alpha_{1}\beta_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{1}\beta_{b}\\
\alpha_{2}\beta_{1}&\alpha_{2}\beta_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{2}\beta_{b}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\alpha_{a}\beta_{1}&\alpha_{a}\beta_{2}&\cdots&\alpha_{a}\beta_{b}\\

where

is a basis of

, and

is a basis of

(see
[

-minors of

]

(see also the discussion right after

, so we assume that

. An effective result for

to be determinantally presented had been known in the case of Veronese embeddings

]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume that
n\geq 2
. Let
L_{1}:=\omega_{X}\otimes H^{m_{1}}\otimes B_{1}

This can be derived from cohomology vanishing for line bundles on nested Hilbert schemes of points, to which Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing theorem is applied. Since we are finally working with

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect necessary facts on Hilbert schemes of points and secant varieties. We closely follow the notations in
[
8
]
and
[
9
]
, and we refer to those papers and the references therein for further details. Throughout the paper, let
X
be a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n
, and
L
be a line bundle on
X
.

2.1.
Hilbert schemes of points

The
Hilbert scheme
of
k
points on
X
is set-theoretically

X^{[k]}:=\{\xi\subseteq X\mid\dim\xi=0,\operatorname{length}(\xi)=k\}.

In this paper, we only deal with the case where
k\leq 4
. In this case,
X^{[k]}
is an irreducible projective variety of dimension
kn
, and furthermore,
X^{[k]}
is smooth if and only if
n\leq 2
or
k\leq 3
(see
[
6
]
). There is the
universal family

\mathcal{Z}_{k}:=\{(x,\xi)\in X\times X^{[k]}\mid x\in\xi\}\subseteq X\times X^{[k]}

over
X^{[k]}
with projection maps
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{k}\to X
and
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon\mathcal{Z}_{k}\to X^{[k]}
. If
n\leq 2
or
k\leq 3
, then
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
has rational singularities (see
[
8
, §1.2.4]
). Let

E_{k,L}:=\textrm{pr}_{2,*}\textrm{pr}_{1}^{*}L

be the
tautological bundle
associated to
L
, which is a rank
k
vector bundle on
X^{[k]}
since
\textrm{pr}_{2}
is a finite flat morphism of degree
k
. Notice that
H^{0}(X^{[k]},E_{k,L})=H^{0}(X,L)
and the fiber of
E_{k,L}
over
\xi\in X^{[k]}
can be identified with
H^{0}(\xi,L|_{\xi})
. In particular,
E_{k,L}
is globally generated when
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample, i.e.,
H^{0}(X,L)\to H^{0}(\xi,L|_{\xi})
is surjective for every
\xi\in X^{[k]}
. In this case, the evaluation map
H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{[k]}}\to E_{k,L}
is surjective, so its kernel
M_{k,L}
is a vector bundle on
X^{[k]}
. We have a short exact sequence

0\longrightarrow M_{k,L}\longrightarrow H^{0}(X,L)\otimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{[k]}}\longrightarrow E_{k,L}\longrightarrow 0.

When
k=1
, we simply write
M_{L}:=M_{1,L}
. If
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon X\times X^{[k]}\to X^{[k]}
is the second projection, then
M_{k,L}=\operatorname{pr}_{2,*}\mathscr{I}_{\mathcal{Z}_{k}}\otimes(L\boxtimes\mathcal{O}_{X^{[k]}})
.
We now make a few comments on which line bundles on
X
are
(k-1)
-very ample. It is well-known that the line bundle
\omega_{X}\otimes H^{m}\otimes B
on
X
with
H
very ample and
B
nef is globally generated as soon as
m\geq n+1
. By
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
, if
m\geq n+k
, then
\omega_{X}\otimes H^{m}\otimes B
is
(k-1)
-very ample. We will frequently use this fact in Sections
3
and
4
.

Now, we consider the Hilbert–Chow morphism
h_{k}\colon X^{[k]}\to X^{(k)}
. The
k
-th symmetric product
X^{(k)}
is obtained by the quotient of the ordinary
k
-th product
X^{k}
by the symmetric group action
\mathfrak{S}_{k}
permuting the components. The line bundle
L^{\boxtimes k}
on
X^{k}
descends to a line bundle
S_{k,L}
on
X^{(k)}
. We define the line bundles

T_{k,L}:=h_{k}^{*}S_{k,L},~N_{k,L}:=T_{k,L}(-\delta_{k}),~\text{and}~A_{k,L}:=T_{k,L}(-2\delta_{k})=N_{k,L}(-\delta_{k})

on
X^{[k]}
, where
\delta_{k}
is a divisor on
X^{[k]}
with
\mathcal{O}_{X^{[k]}}(-\delta_{k})=\det E_{k,\mathcal{O}_{X}}
. If
k=1
, then
\delta_{1}=0
and
E_{1,L}=T_{1,L}=N_{1,L}=A_{1,L}=L
. Note that
N_{k,L}=\det E_{k,L}
. Thus
N_{k,L}
is globally generated when
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample. By
[
16
, Theorem 1.1]
,
H^{k-1}
is
(k-1)
-very ample for a very ample line bundle
H
on
X
, so
N_{k,H^{k-1}}
is globally generated. On the other hand, if a line bundle
B
on
X
is nef (resp. ample), then the line bundle
S_{k,B}
on
X^{(k)}
is nef (resp. ample) so that the line bundle
T_{k,B}=h_{k}^{*}S_{k,B}
is nef (resp. nef and big).
We will frequently use these facts in Sections
3
and
4
.
We have
H^{0}(X^{[k]},T_{k,L})=S^{k}H^{0}(X,L)
and
H^{0}(X^{[k]},N_{k,L})=\wedge^{k}H^{0}(X,L)
. We refer to
[
8
, §2.3.2]
for basic properties of these line bundles on
X^{[k]}
. When
k=2,3
, we can compute the canonical line bundle
\omega_{X^{[k]}}=T_{k,\omega_{X}}((n-2)\delta_{k})
(see
[
8
, §1.1.7]
).

2.2.
Nested Hilbert schemes of points

For positive integers
k_{1}<\cdots<k_{\ell}
, the
nested Hilbert scheme
parametrizing nested sequence of zero-dimensional subschemes of length
k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}
on
X
is set-theoretically

X^{[k_{1},\ldots,k_{\ell}]}:=\{(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{\ell})\in X^{[k_{1}]}\times\cdots\times X^{[k_{\ell}]}\mid\xi_{1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\xi_{\ell}\}\subseteq X^{[k_{1}]}\times\cdots\times X^{[k_{\ell}]}.

In this paper, we only deal with the nested Hilbert schemes
X^{[k-1,k]}
for
k=2,3,4
,
X^{[1,2,3]}
, and
X^{[1,2,3,4]}
. They are all irreducible projective varieties. Furthermore,
X^{[k-1,k]}
is smooth for
k=2,3
(see
[
6
]
), and the remaining
X^{[3,4]}
,
X^{[1,2,3]}
, and
X^{[1,2,3,4]}
have Goresntein canonical singularities (see
[
7
, Theorem 1.2]
,
[
8
, Lemma 4.20]
, and
[
7
, Corollary 4.3]
, respectively).

Assume that
k=2,3,4
, and consider the nested Hilbert scheme
X^{[k-1,k]}
. There are projection maps

\rho_{k-1,k}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\longrightarrow X^{[k]}~~\text{ and }~~\tau_{k-1,k}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\longrightarrow X^{[k-1]},

and there is a residual morphism

\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\longrightarrow X,~~(\eta,\xi)\longmapsto(\mathscr{I}_{\xi},\mathscr{I}_{\eta}).

Note that
\rho_{k-1,k}
is generically finite and factors thorough the universal family
\mathcal{Z}_{k}
over
X^{[k]}
. On the other hand,
X^{[k-1,k]}
can be obtained by the blow-up

\textrm{bl}_{k-1,k}\colon X^{[k-1,k]}\longrightarrow X\times X^{[k-1]}

along the universal family
\mathcal{Z}_{k-1}
with exceptional divisor
F_{k-1}
. Then
\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}=\textrm{pr}_{1}\circ\textrm{bl}_{k-1,k}
and
\tau_{k-1,k}=\textrm{pr}_{2}\circ\textrm{bl}_{k-1,k}
, where
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon X\times X^{[k-1]}\to X
and
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon X\times X^{[k-1]}\to X^{[k-1]}
are projection maps. Notice that
\mathcal{Z}_{2}=X^{[1,2]}
is obtained by the blow-up of
X\times X
along the diagonal
\Delta\subseteq X\times X
. In particular,
\rho_{2}
is finite. If
L
is
(k-1)
-very ample, then there are short exact sequences

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}^{*}L(-F_{k-1})\longrightarrow\rho_{k-1,k}^{*}E_{k,L}\longrightarrow\tau_{k-1,k}^{*}E_{k-1,L}\longrightarrow 0

\displaystyle 0\longrightarrow\rho_{k-1,k}^{*}M_{k,L}\longrightarrow\tau_{k-1,k}^{*}M_{k-1,L}\longrightarrow\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}^{*}L(-F_{k-1}).

We have
\rho_{k-1,k}^{*}T_{k,L}=\tau_{k-1,k}^{*}T_{k-1,L}\otimes\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}^{*}L
and
\rho_{k-1}^{*}\delta_{k}=\tau_{k-1}^{*}\delta_{k-1}+F_{k-1}
. We can also compute the canonical line bundle

\omega_{X^{[k-1,k]}}=\rho_{k-1,k}^{*}\omega_{X^{[k]}}(F_{k-1})=(\tau_{k-1,k}^{*}\omega_{X^{[k-1]}}\otimes\textrm{res}_{k-1,k}^{*}\omega_{X})((n-1)F_{k-1})=\textrm{bl}_{k-1,k}^{*}(\omega_{X}\boxtimes\omega_{X^{[k-1]}})((n-1)F_{k-1}).

For the nested Hilbert scheme
X^{[1,2,3]}
, there are projection maps

\rho_{1,2,3}\colon X^{[1,2,3]}\longrightarrow X^{[3]},~\tau_{1,2,3}\colon X^{[1,2,3]}\longrightarrow X^{[2]},~\text{ and }~\rho_{1,2,3}^{\prime}\colon X^{[1,2,3]}\longrightarrow X^{[2,3]},

and there is a residual morphism

\textrm{res}_{1,2,3}\colon X^{[1,2,3]}\longrightarrow X,~~(x,\eta,\xi)\longmapsto(\mathscr{I}_{\xi},\mathscr{I}_{\eta}).

Note that
\rho_{1,2,3}
is generically finite and
\rho_{1,2,3}^{\prime}
is finite. We have
\rho_{1,2,3}=\rho_{2,3}\circ\rho_{1,2,3}^{\prime}
. On the other hand,
X^{[1,2,3]}
can be obtained by the blow-up

\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}\colon X^{[1,2,3]}\longrightarrow X\times X^{[1,2]}

along the universal family

\mathcal{W}_{1,2}:=\{(x,(y,\xi))\in X\times X^{[1,2]}\mid x,y\in\xi\}\subseteq X\times X^{[1,2]}

over
X^{[1,2]}
with exceptional divisor
F_{1,2}
. Then
\textrm{res}_{1,2,3}=\textrm{pr}_{1}\circ\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}
and
\tau_{1,2,3}=\textrm{pr}_{2}\circ\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}
, where
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon X\times X^{[1,2]}\to X
and
\textrm{pr}_{2}\colon X\times X^{[1,2]}\to X^{[1,2]}
are projection maps. Note that
\mathcal{W}_{1,2}
has two irreducible components. We have
\rho_{1,2,3}^{*}T_{k,L}=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}^{*}(L\boxtimes\rho_{1,2}^{*}T_{2,L})
and
\rho_{1,2,3}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[3]}}(\delta_{3})=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\boxtimes\rho_{1,2}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[2]}}(\delta_{2}))(F_{1,2})
. We can also compute the canonical line bundle

\omega_{X^{[1,2,3]}}=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3}^{*}(\omega_{X}\boxtimes\omega_{X^{[1,2]}})((n-1)F_{1,2})
.

For the nested Hilbert scheme
X^{[1,2,3,4]}
, there is the projection map
\rho_{1,2,3,4}\colon X^{[1,2,3,4]}\to X^{[4]}
, which is generically finite. As before,
X^{[1,2,3,4]}
can be obtained by the blow-up

\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3,4}\colon X^{[1,2,3,4]}\longrightarrow X\times X^{[1,2,3]}

along the universal family

\mathcal{W}_{1,2,3}:=\{(x,(y,\eta,\xi))\in X\times X^{[1,2,3]}\mid x\in\xi,y\in\eta,\eta\subseteq\xi\}\subseteq X\times X^{[1,2,3]}

over
X^{[1,2,3]}
with exceptional divisor
F_{1,2,3}
. Note that
\mathcal{W}_{1,2,3}
has three irreducible components. We have
\rho_{1,2,3,4}^{*}T_{k,L}=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3,4}^{*}(L\boxtimes\rho_{1,2,3}^{*}T_{3,L})
and
\rho_{1,2,3,4}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[4]}}(\delta_{4})=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3,4}^{*}(\mathcal{O}_{X}\boxtimes\rho_{1,2,3}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{[3]}}(\delta_{3}))(F_{1,2,3})
. We can also compute the canonical line bundle

\omega_{X^{[1,2,3,4]}}=\textrm{bl}_{1,2,3,4}^{*}(\omega_{X}\boxtimes\omega_{X^{[1,2,3]}})((n-1)F_{1,2,3})
.

2.3.
Secant varieties

From now on, we assume that
k=2
or
3
so that
X^{[k]}
and
X^{[k-1,k]}
are smooth. Let

B^{k}=B^{k}(L):=\mathbb{P}(E_{k+1,L})~\text{ with canonical projection }~\pi_{k}\colon B^{k}\longrightarrow X^{[k]}.

We further assume that
L
is
(2k-1)
-very ample. Since
E_{k,L}
is globally generated, it follows that the tautological line bundle
\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1)
is base point free. Note that
H^{0}(B^{k},\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1))=H^{0}(X^{[k]},E_{k,L})=H^{0}(X,L)
. Thus the complete linear system
\lvert\mathcal{O}_{B^{k}}(1)\rvert
gives a morhpism

3.
Cohomology Vanishing results on nested Hilbert schemes of points

In this section, we establish various cohomology vanishing statements, which are the main ingredients of the proofs of the main theorems. Recall that
X
is a smooth projective complex variety of dimension
n

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
k\geq 1
and
1\leq i\leq k
be integers, and
L_{i}:=\omega_{X}\otimes H^{m_{i}}\otimes B_{i}
be a line bundle on
X
with
H
very ample and
B_{i}
nef for an integer
m_{i}\geq 0
. If
a\geq 1
,
m_{1}\geq(k-1)(a+n-1)+1
, and
m_{i}\geq a+n
for
2\leq i\leq k
, then

H^{i}(X^{k},\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{1,k}}^{a}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L_{k}))=0~~\text{ for $i>0$},

where
\Delta_{1,i}:=\{(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\in X^{k}\mid x_{1}=x_{i}\}
is a pairwise diagonal for
2\leq i\leq k
. In particular, if
m_{1}\geq(k-1)n+1
and
m_{i}\geq n+1
for
2\leq i\leq k
, then

H^{i}(X,M_{L_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{L_{k}}\otimes L_{1})=0~~\text{ for $i>0$}.

Proof.

This is well-known, but we include the proof for the reader’s convenience.
Let
b\colon Y\to X^{k}
be the blow-up of
X^{k}
along
\Delta_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{1,k}
with exceptional divisors
E_{1,2},\ldots,E_{1,k}
such that
b(E_{1,i})=\Delta_{1,i}
for
2\leq i\leq k
.
It is known (see e.g.,
[
2
, Corollary 3.5]
) that

\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{1,k}}=\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}}\cdots\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,k}}=\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,k}}.

As
L_{2},\ldots,L_{k}
are globally generated and
H^{i}(X,L_{2})=\cdots=H^{i}(X,L_{k})=0
for
i>0
, we find

R^{i}\textrm{pr}_{1,*}\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{1,k}}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L_{k})=\begin{cases}M_{L_{2}}\otimes\cdots\otimes M_{L_{k}}\otimes L_{1}&\text{for $i=0$}\\
0&\text{for $i>0$},\end{cases}

where
\textrm{pr}_{1}\colon X^{k}\to X
is the projection given by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\mapsto x_{1}
(see e.g.,
[
2
, Theorem 3.6]
).
Thus the first assertion implies the second assertion.
One can also easily check that
Y
can be obtained by the successive blow-ups of along the strict transforms of
\Delta_{1,2},\ldots,\Delta_{1,k}
in any order. In particular,
Y
is smooth, and for
2\leq i\leq k
, the line bundle
D_{1,i}:=b^{*}\textrm{pr}_{1,i}^{*}(H\boxtimes H)(-E_{1,i})
on
Y
is nef, where
\textrm{pr}_{1,i}\colon X^{k}\to X^{2}
is the projection map given by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\mapsto(x_{1},x_{i})
. Set
E:=E_{1,2}+\cdots+E_{1,k}
and
D:=D_{1,2}\otimes\cdots\otimes D_{1,k}
.
Note that

H^{i}(X^{k},\mathscr{I}_{\Delta_{1,2}\cup\cdots\cup\Delta_{1,k}}^{a}\otimes(L_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L_{k}))=H^{i}(Y,b^{*}(L_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L_{k})(-aE))~~\text{ for $i>0$}.

We may write

b^{*}(L_{1}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L_{k})(-aE)=\omega_{Y}\otimes\underbrace{b^{*}(H^{m_{1}-(k-1)(a+n-1)}\boxtimes H^{m_{2}-(a+n-1)}\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes H^{m_{k}-(a+n-1)})}_{\text{nef and big}}\otimes\underbrace{D^{a+n-1}}_{\text{nef}}.

Thus the first assertion follows from Kawamata–Viehweg vanishing.
∎
