1. Public Executive Summary
This project was undertaken to advance the technical readiness and commercial viability of a next-generation,
industrialized concrete floating foundation for offshore wind turbines called VolturnUS +. The design objective
is to deliver a platform that is lower cost, faster to manufacture, simpler to deploy, and optimized for domestic
supply chains and local workforce participation. To enable project financing and commercial adoption, an at-sea
demonstration under representative operating conditions was required and therefore this project aimed to deploy
a ¼-scale VolturnUS+ prototype offshore the Coast of Maine

Led by the University of Maine, the project was executed in collaboration with more than 31 contractors—
predominantly Maine-based companies, strengthening regional industrial capacity. Funding support was
competitively awarded by the U.S. Department of Energy ARPA-e.. UMaine successfully designed, fabricated,
launched, and commissioned a ¼-scale floating wind turbine system representing a 20-MW commercial
platform. The deployed unit stands approximately 110 ft above the waterline, spans 52 ft in width, weighs over
375 short tons, and supports a 225-kW grid-connected turbine. It is stationed ~1.5 km offshore of Castine,
Maine, in ~70 ft water depth. Site metocean conditions provide a high-probability operating environment
representative of full-scale commercial deployments. Both the floating system and its construction methodology
were engineered to be directly scalable to utility-scale production.

All required Local, State, and Federal permits were acquired. System design followed the American Bureau of
Shipping Guide for Floating Wind Turbines. Fabrication planning covered foundation construction, launch
logistics, turbine integration, tow-out, mooring installation, and dynamic cable deployment using methods
representative of commercial practice.

The foundation was constructed in Trenton, Maine using movable sliding formwork to mirror full-scale
production workflows, including cold-weather concrete placement. The build sequence comprised: slab casting;
vertical wall construction; integration of a cast-in steel transition piece; top-slab placement using stay-in-place
steel and foam forms; welded mooring interfaces; and post-tensioning for enhanced structural capacity and
watertightness. The unit was built on an elevated platform to enable transport via self-propelled modular
transporters (SPMTs)—a requirement for commercial manufacturing. Final marine coatings were applied to
meet U.S. Coast Guard requirements.

On March 30, the completed foundation was transferred to a tidal launch ramp and successfully launched at
high tide, then staged on temporary mooring with support from Maine Maritime Academy. The structure was
subsequently towed ~60 nautical miles to Mack Point in Searsport, Maine for turbine integration, with all
marine notifications and permits satisfied. At Mack Point, the tower, nacelle, and blades were installed via
heavy-lift crane while the foundation remained afloat. Electrical and mechanical commissioning were
completed over a two-day integration window.

Four pre-laid mooring lines—each comprising chain segments, long synthetic nylon rope sections, and ground
chain to drag-embedment anchors—were installed offshore Castine. The hybrid rope-chain architecture reduces
seabed footprint by approximately 2–3× relative to all-chain systems while maintaining station-keeping
performance. The floating unit was towed to site and connected to the preinstalled moorings.

 2

A seabed export cable was installed from shore to the platform and terminated via a buoyancy-supported lazy-
wave configuration. This geometry accommodates platform motions and mitigates fatigue loading, consistent
with full-scale floating wind practice. The turbine is fully grid connected.

The project achieved a full-system offshore demonstration of a scalable concrete floating wind platform and
validated industrialized construction, logistics, and installation methodologies suitable for commercial
deployment. The effort de-risked technology adoption, strengthened Maine’s offshore wind supply chain, and
demonstrated a replicable pathway to cost-effective floating wind infrastructure with high local economic
participation.

Although the project successfully completed the installation of the prototype, the ARPA-e ATLANTIS program
was discontinued and funding terminated.

3. Accomplishments and Objectives
This award allowed the University of Maine to demonstrate a number of key objectives. The focus of this
project was on demonstrating an intermediate-scale version (1 to 4 scale) of the VolturnUS + Floating Offshore
Wind Turbine platform concept in a real ocean environment with an operating turbine off the coast of Maine for
one year. The VolturnUS+ hull utilizes a novel motion mitigation technology resulting in significant reductions
in platform complexity, size and costs as compared to a traditional floating foundation.

4. Project Activities
The project focused on demonstrating an intermediate-scale (1:4) VolturnUS+ floating offshore wind turbine
platform in a real ocean environment off the coast of Maine with an operating, grid-connected turbine over a
one-year period to de-risk the technology and accelerate commercialization. The core technical approach
centered on validating a novel motion-mitigation hull design that materially reduces platform complexity,
footprint, and cost relative to conventional floating foundations, while executing full-system deployment, at-sea
operations, and performance monitoring to reach Technology Readiness Level (TRL) 6. Parallel workstreams
advanced full-scale readiness through pre-FEED engineering, constructability planning, cost modeling, and
techno-economic analysis aligned with ARPA-E metric-space methods and ATLANTIS cost targets, alongside
independent third-party qualification and structured industry engagement. Results to date include successful
intermediate-scale construction and offshore stationing with grid connection achieved through coordination of
27 partners; delivery of multiple full-scale designs and constructability reviews generating strong developer
interest in the U.S. and Europe; near-completion of an independent New Technology Qualification review with
the American Bureau of Shipping; and substantial commercialization progress via a four-developer advisory
board, recurring industry forums, and active market development for future deployments.
