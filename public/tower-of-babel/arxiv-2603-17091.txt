1.
Introduction

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism. Denote by
\mathcal{P}(X)
the set of Borel probability measures on
X
with the weak
∗
-topology; by
\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)
its subset of
T
-invariant measures; by
\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)\subset\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)
the set of ergodic elements; and, given
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(X)
,
\operatorname{{supp}}(\mu)
stands for its support.

Measure-theoretic and topological entropy are classical invariants in the theory of dynamical systems and are related by the classical variational principle

h_{top}(T)\,\,\,=\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,h_{\mu}(T)\,\,\,=\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,h_{\mu}(T)

(1)

which sometimes provides a natural way to choose important elements from
{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
. Entropy-dimensional concepts have been recently developed in order to estimate the complexity of infinite entropy systems from a better suited viewpoint. The
metric mean dimension
, introduced by E. Lindenstrauss and B. Weiss in
[
9
]
, is both a dynamical analogue of the box dimension and a dimensional analogue of the topological entropy. In what follows, we denote the upper and lower metric mean dimensions by
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)
and
\mathrm{\underline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)
, respectively, thus emphasizing their dependence on the fixed metric
d
on the space
X
where the dynamics
T
acts. These concepts vanish if the topological entropy of
T
is finite; if, otherwise,
T
has infinite entropy, they quantify the speed at which the entropy at scale
\varepsilon
approaches
+\infty
as the scale goes to zero. The choice of the metric
d
has impact precisely on
such rate of convergence.

In
[
10
]
, E. Lindenstrauss and M. Tsukamoto established a connection between the upper metric mean dimension and the rate distortion function
\mathcal{R}_{\mu}
.
More precisely, they showed that under the tame growth of covering numbers

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Meanwhile, in
[
6
]
, Y. Gutman and A. Śpiewak proved a corresponding relation between the upper metric mean dimension and the Rényi entropy, namely

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\,\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,h_{\mu}(P)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,

where the infimum is taken over the finite Borel partitions
P
of
X
with diameter at most
\varepsilon
and
h_{\mu}(P)
stands for the entropy of
\mu
with respect to
P
. Moreover, Problem
3
in
[
6
]
asked whether the upper metric mean dimension could be expressed in terms of the Brin-Katok local entropy. An affirmative answer to this problem was given by R. Shi in
[
15
]
:

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,.

Apart from the ones mentioned above, many other distinct notions of measure-theoretic metric mean dimension were shown to exist and be inspired by some version of entropy, such as Katok metric entropy (cf.
[
15
]
), Shapira entropy (cf.
[
15
]
) and Pfister-Sullivan entropy (cf.
[
21
]
), just to mention a few. A unified framework was provided by E. Chen, R. Yang and X. Zhou in
[
3
]
, where they consider the following family of entropy-like functions of a measure
\mu
at a given scale
\varepsilon
(see the precise definitions in
[
3
]
and Section
3
):

{\mathcal{D}}\,\,=\,\,\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} &\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta),\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta),\,\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon),\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon),\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon),\,\,\,h_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta),\,\,\\
&PS_{\mu}(T,\varepsilon),\,\,\,{\mathcal{R}}_{\mu,\,L^{\infty}}(\varepsilon),\,\,\,\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}h_{\mu}(P),\,\,\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\mu}(T,d,\varepsilon)\end{aligned}\Bigg\}.

(2)

The authors prove that, for any ergodic probability measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)
, the limits

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

(3)

do not depend on the choice of
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
(so they coincide with the upper and lower
L^{\infty}
-rate distortion dimensions, respectively) and that, for every
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(4)

We stress that, in equality (
4
), the maximization over the space of probability measures is done for a fixed scale, which only afterwards is made to go to zero. A recurring question in the literature
asks whether we may exchange the order of
\limsup_{\varepsilon}
and
\sup_{\mu}
in (
4
). An affirmative answer to this question would suggest natural definitions of measure-theoretic metric mean dimension, namely

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T,F)(\mu)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

and provide a classical variational principle for the metric mean dimension in terms of
F
; moreover, if
\sup_{\mu}
could be replaced by
\max_{\mu}
, this principle would single out a measure which captures the complexities of the system over all scales
\varepsilon
.
It is known that this order can be changed under the marker property (cf.
[
11
]
), but there are relevant systems that do not satisfy this condition, as shown in
[
16
,
18
]
. The following examples shed some light on this problem.

Example 1.1
.

[
10
, Section VIII]

Consider the set
A=\{0\}\cup\{1/n\colon n\in\mathbb{N}\}
,
X=A^{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the metric

d(x,y)\,=\,\sum_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}\,2^{-|n|}\,|x_{n}-y_{n}|

and
T\colon X\to X
the standard shift. Then
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)=1/2
(cf.
[
20
,
7
]
) and

\forall\,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)\quad\quad\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,=\,0.

So,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,>\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Example 1.2
.

[
17
, Theorem 1.4]

In
[
17
]
Tsukamoto constructs a homeomorphism
T
on a compact metric space
(X,d)
with the following properties:

\bullet
For any ergodic measure
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
we have
\,\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\nu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,=\,0.

\bullet
There exists
\eta\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
such that

\,\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\eta}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,=\,+\infty.

Consequently, one has
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)=+\infty
and

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,>\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Yet, it is still true for this example that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(5)

Why do they behave differently with respect to the change in the order in which
\limsup_{\varepsilon}
and
\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}
appear? It turns out that a major role in this issue is played by the behavior of the measure-theoretic
\varepsilon
-entropy under ergodic decompositions. On the one hand, if the upper metric mean dimension is finite, then the upper rate distortion dimension

\overline{R}(X,d,T,\mu)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\mathcal{R}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

is a convex function of
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)
with respect to its ergodic decomposition (cf.
[
17
, Theorem 1.1]
). Thus, in Example
1.1
, the change in order between
\limsup_{\varepsilon}
and
\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}
produces a strict inequality. On the other hand, the system described in Example
1.2
has
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)=+\infty
and the convexity no longer holds. This is the reason why (
5
) is possible.

The purpose of this paper is to explain that we can understand this issue more clearly by comparing the maps
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
with a new concept that we introduce in the next section.

2.
Main results

After recalling the problem of quantization of measures, we motivate the questions we address and describe our main contributions.

2.1.
Quantization

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space. Quantization of measures, as addressed in
[
5
]
, consists in approximating, at arbitrarily small resolutions, a given measure by another with finite support. Let
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
stand for either a Wasserstein distance or the Lévy-Prokhorov distance on the space of Borel probability measures
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
(see the precise definitions in Subsection
3.5
).

Definition 2.1
.

Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
and
\varepsilon>0
, the
quantization number
Q_{\mu,D}(\varepsilon)
of
\mu
with respect to
D
at a scale
\varepsilon
is the least positive integer
N
such that there exists
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
with
D(\mu,\nu)<\varepsilon
and
\nu
is supported on a set of cardinality
N
. The
upper
and
lower quantization dimensions of
\mu
are defined as

\overline{\dim}_{Q}(\mu,D)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\log Q_{\mu,D}(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\epsilon)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{\dim}_{Q}(\mu,D)\,=\,\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\log Q_{\mu,D}(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\epsilon)}.

It is known (cf.
[
1
, Proposition 3.4]
) that the quantization number of
\mu
at scale
\varepsilon
is bounded from above by the
\varepsilon
-covering number of the space (see Subsection
3.3
). Moreover, the quantization dimensions are related to the upper and lower box-counting dimensions of the space by the following variational principles (see
[
1
, Proof of Theorem 3.9]
):

\overline{\dim}_{B}(X,d)\,\,=\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}(X)}\,\overline{\dim}_{Q}(\mu,D)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{\dim}_{B}(X,d)\,\,=\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}(X)}\,\,\underline{\dim}_{Q}(\mu,D).

(6)

2.2.
Dynamical quantization

We now address the corresponding dynamical objects. Let
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map. Given
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we denote by
D_{n}
the Wasserstein or the Lévy-Prokhorov distance induced in
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
by the Bowen metric
d_{n}
in
X
(defined in (
14
)).

Definition 2.2
.

Given
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
,
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
and
\varepsilon>0
, the
upper
and
lower dynamical quantization numbers of
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}(X)
at scale
\varepsilon
are given by

\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{\mu,D_{n}}(\varepsilon)\quad\text{and}\quad\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,=\,\liminf_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\log Q_{\mu,D_{n}}(\varepsilon).

The following relation between the metric entropy and the dynamical quantization numbers of an ergodic measure was proved in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.3
.

[
1
, Appendix A.3]

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map. Given
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)
, then

h_{\mu}(T)\,\,=\,\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,\,=\,\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon).

We observe that, applying
[
1
, Proposition 3.4 & (1.1)]
to the space
(X,d_{n})
, we obtain

\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,h_{\varepsilon}(X,d,T)\qquad\forall\,\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}(X),

(7)

where

h_{\varepsilon}(X,d,T)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log\,S(X,n,\varepsilon)

and
S(X,n,\varepsilon)
is the maximal cardinality of the
\varepsilon
-separated subsets of
X
(see Subsection
3.2
). Combining (
7
) with Theorem
2.3
and the classical variational principle (
1
), we get

\displaystyle h_{top}(T)

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)

(8)

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\,\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\lim_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)\mathrlap{.}

Definition 2.4
.

The
upper
and
lower mean quantization dimensions of a measure
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
with respect to the metric
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
are given, respectively, by

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,D,\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,D,\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

We will show (cf. Lemmas
6.2
and
6.5
) that, if
\mu
is ergodic, then the mean quantization dimensions of
\mu
do not change if one replaces
\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)
by
\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)
in their definitions. Also, by (
7
), we have

\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,D,\mu)\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T)\qquad\forall\,\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X).

(9)

Our first result states that the dynamical quantization numbers can be included in the set
{\mathcal{D}}
, that is, they satisfy the properties (
3
) and (
4
). Moreover, the mean quantization dimension satisfies a classical variational principle, where maximization is taken over the space of invariant probability measures, which can be seen both as a dimensional analogue of (
1
) and as a dynamical analogue of (
6
). Furthermore, measures of maximal mean quantization dimension always exist.

Theorem A
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space,
T\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism and
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
. Then:

(a)
For every
\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)
and
F\,\in\,{\mathcal{D}}
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,D,\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,D,\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(b)

[Variational principle I]

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T)

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\mathrlap{.}

(c)

[Variational principle II]

\displaystyle\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\,\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,T,D,\mu)

(10)

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,D,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\mathrlap{.}

We observe that, in general, the maximum in (
10
) cannot be replaced by the supremum over the space of ergodic measures. Indeed, we discuss in Section
7
an example of a compact metric space
(X_{0},d_{0})
and a homeomorphism
T_{0}\colon X_{0}\to X_{0}
for which

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X_{0},d_{0},T_{0})\,\,>\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T_{0}}(X_{0})}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

By Theorem
A
(a)
, for any
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
we have

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X_{0},d_{0},T_{0})\,\,>\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T_{0}}(X_{0})}\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X_{0},d_{0},T_{0},D,\mu)\,\,=\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T_{0}}(X_{0})}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

2.3.
Main property

The fundamental aspect that enables us to prove (
10
) is that the quantization numbers of an invariant measure are, up to normalization of scales by the weights appearing in its ergodic decomposition, at least the quantization numbers of its ergodic components. We express this idea using the following property.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
{\mathcal{K}}
be a convex subset of
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
. A map
F\colon{\mathcal{K}}\times(0,+\infty)\,\to[0,+\infty]
is said to satisfy the
scaled monotonicity under measure domination
(s.m.m.d. for short) in
{\mathcal{K}}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that, for every
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}
with
\mu\geq t\,\nu
for some
t\in(0,1)
, one has

F(\mu,t\,\varepsilon)\,\geq\,F(\nu,C\,\varepsilon)\quad\forall\,\varepsilon>0.

Theorem B
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
{\mathcal{K}}
be a convex subset of
{\mathcal{P}}(X)
. If
F\colon{\mathcal{K}}\times(0,+\infty)\,\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies the s.m.m.d. property in
{\mathcal{K}}
, then

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(11)

We observe that the s.m.m.d. property is essentially incompatible with convexity. For instance, assume that map
F(\cdot,\varepsilon)
is convex in
{\mathcal{K}}
for every
\varepsilon>0
and satisfies the s.m.m.d. property. Then, for any
\mu,\nu\in{\mathcal{K}}
, we have

F(\nu,C\varepsilon)\,\,\leq\,\,F\big((\mu+\nu)/2,\varepsilon/2\big)\,\,\leq\,\,\big[F(\mu,\varepsilon/2)\,+\,F(\nu,\varepsilon/2)\big]/2.

Hence,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\nu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\leq\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

and we conclude that the map

\mu\,\mapsto\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

is constant in
{\mathcal{K}}
.

The previous result provides a generalization of item (c) of Theorem
A
that also answers the following question.

Question 2.6
.

Under what assumptions on
F\colon{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)\times(0,+\infty)\,\to[0,+\infty]
can we ensure that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,=\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{P}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,?

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism. If
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
satisfies the s.m.m.d. property in
{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
, then

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(12)

We note that, for the previous reasult to be valid, it is not enough to assume that the s.m.m.d. property is satisfied in
{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(P)
. Actually, the upper Brin-Katok
\varepsilon
-entropy satisfies the s.m.m.d. property in
{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(P)
(due to the inequalities in
[
3
, Lemma 3.1]
, Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
), but we show in Subsection
7.2
that it does not comply with the variational principle (
12
).

2.4.
Variational principles for maps in
{\mathcal{D}}

Since all maps
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
, as well as dynamical quantization numbers, have the same growth rate at ergodic measures, it is worthwhile to investigate how each individual
F
acts on invariant measures, as this is the main key to obtaining the variational principle (
10
). In spite of the common probabilistic and geometric hybrid nature of these concepts, some of them satisfy the s.m.m.d. property (as happens with Katok
\varepsilon
-entropy and Shapira
\varepsilon
-entropy; cf. Remark
7.1
), while others are convex (like Brin-Katok
\varepsilon
-entropy; cf. Lemma
4.7
). This is the content of the next result.

Theorem C
.

The following assertions hold:

(a)

Let
(X,d)
be compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a homeomorphism. Then, for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,LP,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,LP,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Consequently, if

F\in\big\{\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon),\,\,\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)\big\}
, then

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(b)

There exists a compact metric space
(X_{0},d_{0})
and a homeomorphism
T_{0}\colon X_{0}\to X_{0}
such that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X_{0},d_{0},T_{0})\,\,\,\,>\,\,\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T_{0}}(X_{0})}\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

for every

F\,\,\in\,\,\Bigg\{\begin{aligned} \,\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,&\delta)\,,\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,,\,\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,,\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,,\,\,\,\\
&\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)\,,\,\,\,\,\,\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}h_{\mu}(P)\,\,\,\end{aligned}\Bigg\}.

2.5.
Ergodic equilibrium states

The previous discussion suggests another question, previously addressed in
[
3
]
.

Question 2.8
.

Under what conditions on the space
(X,d)
, on the map
T\colon X\to X
, or else on the measure-theoretic
\varepsilon
-entropy map
F
can we guarantee that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,?

Admittedly, we are not aware of the existence of any map
F
that complies with such a variational principle. The next example describes a setting where this principle is valid generically.

Example 2.9
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact connected smooth boundaryless manifold with dimension
\mathrm{dim}X\geq 2
, endowed with a Riemannian metric
d
. A Borel probability measure
\lambda
on
X
is said to be
good
if
\lambda(\{x\})=0
for every
x\in X
and
\lambda(U)>0
for every non-empty open subset
U
of
X
. Denote by
\mathrm{Homeo}_{\lambda}(X)
the space of homeomorphisms
T\colon X\to X
that preserve
\lambda
. This is a complete metric space if one considers in
\mathrm{Homeo}_{\lambda}(X)
the topology induced by the metric

\rho(T_{1},T_{2})\,=\,\max\big\{d\big(T_{1}(x),T_{2}(x)\big),\,d\big(T_{1}^{-1}(x),T_{2}^{-1}(x)\big)\big\}.

G. Lacerda and S. Romaña proved in
[
8
, Theorems A & B]
that a
C^{0}
-generic homeomorphism in
T\in\mathrm{Homeo}_{\lambda}(X)
satisfies

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\,\dim X\,\,=\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\lambda}^{K}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

(13)

Consider the residual subset
\mathfrak{R}
of
\mathrm{Homeo}_{\lambda}(X)
such that, if
T\in\mathfrak{R}
, then
\lambda
is ergodic (cf.
[
12
]
) and (
13
) holds. Combining this information with item
(a)
of Theorem
A
, we conclude that, for every
T\in\mathfrak{R}
,
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
and
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,T,D,\mu)\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

with
\lambda
as a maximizing measure.

Example 2.10
.

Consider the set
A=[0,1]
with the Euclidean metric
|\cdot|
, the space
X=A^{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the metric

d(x,y)\,=\,\sum_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}\,2^{-|n|}\,|x_{n}-y_{n}|

and the standard shift
\sigma\colon X\to X
. Then
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)=1
(cf.
[
10
]
). Let
\lambda=\mathrm{Leb}^{\mathbb{Z}}
be the product measure on
[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}
of the Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]
. Then
\lambda
is
\sigma
-invariant and ergodic; and, due to
[
10
, E. Example]
and Theorem
A
, for
D\in\{W_{p}\colon p\in[1,+\infty)\}\cup\{LP\}
and every
F\in{\mathcal{D}}
,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\lambda,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,\sigma,D,\lambda).

Therefore,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,\sigma,D,\mu).

The previous example can be generalized. A compact metric space
(A,\rho)
is Ahlfors regular if there exists a Borel probability measure
\nu\in{\mathcal{P}}(A)
satisfying

\exists\,\varepsilon_{0},\,C_{1},\,C_{2}>0\,\colon\quad C_{1}\,\varepsilon^{\mathrm{dim_{B}(A,\,\rho)}}\,\,\leq\,\,\nu(B(a,\varepsilon))\,\,\leq\,\,C_{2}\,\varepsilon^{\mathrm{dim_{B}(A,\,\rho)}}\quad\quad\forall a\in A,\,\,\forall 0<\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}

where
B(a,\varepsilon)
stands for the open ball in
A
centered at
a
with radius
\varepsilon
with respect to the metric
\rho
. For instance, a finite set with the normalized counting measure or the Euclidean cube with the Lebesgue measure are Ahlfors regular. Consider a Ahlfors regular space
(A,\rho,\nu)
, the shift map on the space

X=A^{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the metric
d
and the product measure
\lambda=\nu^{\mathbb{Z}}\in{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)
. Given

F\in{\mathcal{D}}
, we already know that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,\geq\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

On the other hand, for any
x\in X
,
\varepsilon\in(0,\varepsilon_{0})
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\,\subset\,\big\{y\in X\colon\,\,y_{j}\in B(x_{j},\varepsilon)\quad\forall\,0\leq j<n\big\}

so,

\lambda\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)\,\,\leq\,\,\prod_{0\,\leq\,j\,<n}\,\,\nu\big(B(x_{j},\varepsilon)\big)\,\,\leq\,\,C_{2}^{n}\,\cdot\,\varepsilon^{n\,\mathrm{dim_{B}(A,\,\rho)}}.

These estimates yield to (see Definition
4.5
)

\overline{h}_{\lambda}^{BK}(\varepsilon)\,\geq\,-\log C_{2}+\mathrm{dim_{B}(A,\,\rho)}\,\cdot\,\log\,(1/\varepsilon).

Therefore,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\lambda}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\geq\,\,\mathrm{dim_{B}(A,\,\rho)}\,\,=\,\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma).

Thus,

\displaystyle\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\lambda,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\,

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\,\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,\sigma,D,\lambda)\,\,\,=\,\,

\displaystyle\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{Q}}(X,d,\sigma,D,\mu).

Remark 2.11
.

Regarding the lower Brin-Katok entropy
\underline{h}_{\lambda}^{BK}(\varepsilon)
, R. Yang proved in
[
23
, Theorem 3.9]
that, if the Hausdorff and the upper box dimension of the alphabet
(A,\rho)
coincide, then

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(A^{\mathbb{Z}},d,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(A^{\mathbb{Z}})}\,\,\frac{\underline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(A^{\mathbb{Z}})}\,\,\frac{\underline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

For instance, if
(A,\rho)
is Ahlfors regular then this condition is satisfied. Furthermore, the previous reasoning implies

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(A^{\mathbb{Z}},d,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\underline{h}_{\lambda}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{\sigma}(A^{\mathbb{Z}})}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\underline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

2.6.
Organization of the paper

In Sections
3
and
4
we define the main concepts we use and gather some of their properties. After proving Theorems
A
and
B
in the ensuing sections,
we pave the way for proving Theorem
C
by thoroughly
discussing an example in Section
7
.

3.
Basic definitions

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map.

3.1.
Bowen dynamical metrics

Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the dynamical metric
d_{n}\colon X\times X\,\to\,[0,+\infty)
, determined by the distance
d
in
X
and the map
T
, is defined by

d_{n}(x,z)=\max\,\Big\{d(x,z),\,d(T(x),T(z)),\,\dots,\,d(T^{n-1}(x),T^{n-1}(z))\Big\}.

(14)

It is easy to check that
d_{n}
is indeed a metric and that it generates the same topology as
d
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and a point
x\in X
, the
(n,\varepsilon)
-ball centered at
x
with radius
\varepsilon
with respect to the metric
d_{n}
(also called Bowen ball) is the set

\mathit{B}_{n}(x,\varepsilon)=\{y\in X\colon\,d_{n}(x,y)\,<\,\varepsilon\}.

(15)

3.2.
Separated sets, spanning sets, open covers, closed covers

Given
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the next three quantities count the number of orbit segments of length
n
that are distinguishable at scale
\varepsilon
.

One says that a set
E\subset X
is
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated if any two distinct points in
E
are at least
\varepsilon
apart in the metric
d_{n}
, that is,
d_{n}(x,z)>\varepsilon
for every
x\neq z\in E
. We denote by
S(X,n,\varepsilon)
the maximal cardinality of the
(n,\varepsilon)
-separated subsets of
X
. When
n=1
, we simplify this notation by saying that the set
E
is
\varepsilon
-separated, and denoting the maximal cardinality of the
\varepsilon
-separated subsets of
X
by
S(X,d,\varepsilon)
. We note that, for every
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

S(X,n,\varepsilon)\,=\,S(X,d_{n},\varepsilon).

A set
Y\subset X
is said to be
(n,\varepsilon)
-spanning if for any
x\in X
there exists
z\in Y
such that
d_{n}(x,z)<\varepsilon
. We denote by
R(X,n,\varepsilon)
the minimal cardinality of the
(n,\varepsilon)
-spanning subsets of
X
. Again, when
n=1
, we refer to
\varepsilon
-spanning sets, with minimal cardinality denoted
R(X,d,\varepsilon)
, and one has

R(X,n,\varepsilon)\,=\,R(X,d_{n},\varepsilon)\quad\forall\,\varepsilon>0\,\,\,\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)
be the minimum cardinality of any cover of
X
by open balls with radius
\varepsilon
in the metric
d_{n}
. When
n=1
, we refer to
\mathcal{N}(X,d,\varepsilon)
, so

\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)\,=\,\mathcal{N}(X,d_{n},\varepsilon)\quad\forall\,\varepsilon>0\,\,\,\forall\,n\in\mathbb{N}.

In what follows, if we need to use closed balls instead of open ones, we change the notation to
\mathcal{C}(X,d,\varepsilon)
. We note that, given
\varepsilon>0
,

\mathcal{N}(X,d,2\varepsilon)\,\leq\,\mathcal{C}(X,d,\varepsilon)\,\leq\,\mathcal{N}(X,d,\varepsilon).

(16)

Due to the compactness of
X
, the values of
S(X,n,\varepsilon)
,
R(X,n,\varepsilon)
,
\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)
and
\mathcal{C}(X,n,\varepsilon)
are finite for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
. In addition, the previous three quantities are related by the following inequalities (cf.
[
2
, Lemma 2.5.1]
and
[
22
, §7]
):

\mathcal{N}(X,n,2\varepsilon)\,\leq\,R(X,n,\varepsilon)\,\leq\,S(X,n,\varepsilon)\,\leq\,R(X,n,\varepsilon/2)\,\leq\,\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon/2).

(17)

Moreover,

\mathcal{N}(X,n+m,\varepsilon)\,\leq\,\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)\,.\,\mathcal{N}(X,m,\varepsilon)

(18)

so, the sequence
\big(\log\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)\big)_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
is sub-additive.

3.3.
Box-counting dimension

Given
\varepsilon>0
, recall that
\mathcal{N}(X,d,\varepsilon)
stands for the smallest number of open balls in the metric
d
with radius
\varepsilon
that cover
X
. We observe that, for every
\varepsilon>0
, one has

S(X,d,2\varepsilon)\,\leq\,\mathcal{N}(X,d,\varepsilon)\,\leq\,S(X,d,\varepsilon/2).

(19)

The upper box-counting dimension of
(X,d)
is defined by

\mathrm{\overline{dim}_{B}}(X,d)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\log\mathcal{N}(X,d,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

The lower box-counting dimension
\mathrm{\underline{dim}_{B}}(X,d)
is defined by taking
\liminf
instead of
\limsup
. Notice that, due to (
19
), the upper box-counting dimension may be computed using
\varepsilon
-separated sets, that is,

\mathrm{\overline{dim}_{B}}(X,d)\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\log S(X,d,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

(and similarly for the lower version).

3.4.
Metric mean dimension

The
upper and lower metric mean dimensions
are labels for dynamical systems introduced by E. Lindenstrauss and B. Weiss in
[
9
]
to quantify the complexity of infinite entropy systems. We denote them by
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)
and
\mathrm{\underline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)
, respectively, to emphasize their dependence on the fixed metric
d
of the space
X
where the dynamics
T
acts. They are defined by

\displaystyle\mathrm{\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}}\big(X,d,T\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\mathrm{\underline{mdim}_{M}}\big(X,d,T\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Due to (
17
), the metric mean dimension may be computed using
R(X,n,\varepsilon)
or
\mathcal{N}(X,n,\varepsilon)
instead of
S(X,n,\varepsilon)
.

In what follows, we simplify the notation by denoting

h_{\varepsilon}(X,d,T)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log\,\mathcal{Z}(X,n,\varepsilon)

(20)

where
\mathcal{Z}
is chosen from the set
\{\mathcal{N},R,S\}
according to the context.

3.5.
W_{p}
and
LP
metrics on
\mathcal{P}(X)

It is known that the space
\mathcal{P}(X)
of the Borel probability measures on
X
is nonempty; and it is compact if endowed with the weak
∗
-topology. Moreover, there are metrics on
\mathcal{P}(X)
inducing this topology, the classic ones being the
Wasserstein distances
and the
Lévy-Prokhorov distance
. The former are defined by

\mathrm{W_{p}}(\mu,\nu)=\inf_{\pi\,\in\,\Pi(\mu,\nu)}\,\left(\int_{X\times X}\,\big(d(x,y)\big)^{p}\;d\pi(x,y)\right)^{1/p}

where
p\in[1,+\infty)
and
\Pi(\mu,\nu)
denotes the set of probability measures on the product space
X\times X
with marginals
\mu
and
\nu
(see
[
19
]
and references therein for more details). The latter is defined by

LP(\mu,\nu)=\inf\,\Big\{\varepsilon>0\colon\,\,\forall\,E\subset X\quad\forall\,\,\text{$\varepsilon$-neighborhood $V_{\varepsilon}(E)$ of $E$ one has}

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\nu(E)\leq\mu(V_{\varepsilon}(E))+\varepsilon\quad\text{ and }\quad\mu(E)\leq\nu(V_{\varepsilon}(E))+\varepsilon\Big\}.

These distances are compared by (cf.
[
1
, p. 11]
)

\displaystyle W_{q}

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,W_{p}\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,\operatorname{{diam}}(X,d)^{1-\frac{q}{p}}\,W_{q}^{\frac{q}{p}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\,1\leq q\leq p

(21)

\displaystyle LP^{1+\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,W_{p}\,\,\,\leq\,\,\bigl(1+\operatorname{{diam}}(X,d)^{p}\bigr)^{\frac{1}{p}}\,LP^{\frac{1}{p}}\qquad

\displaystyle\forall\,p\in[1,+\infty).

We refer the reader to
[
4
,
19
]
, where more information can be found on these metrics.

4.
Measure-theoretic
\varepsilon
-entropies

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space,
T\colon X\to X
a homeomorphism and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
. Recall from Subsection
3.1
the definitions of dynamical metric
d_{n}
and
(n,\varepsilon)
-ball
B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)
.

4.1.
(\varepsilon,\delta)-
Katok entropy

For
0<\delta<1
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\varepsilon>0
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}(n,\varepsilon,\delta)
the minimum number of open
(n,\varepsilon)
-balls with radius
\varepsilon
in the dynamical metric
d_{n}
needed to cover any set of
\mu
-measure strictly bigger than
1-\delta
.

Definition 4.1
.

The
upper
and
lower
(\varepsilon,\delta)-
Katok entropy of
\mu
are given, respectively, by

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\log\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}(n,\varepsilon,\delta)}{n}\qquad\text{ and }\qquad\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,=\,\liminf_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\log\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}(n,\varepsilon,\delta)}{n}.

(22)

The
upper
and
lower Katok entropy of
\mu
at scale
\varepsilon
are defined by

\displaystyle\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\,\,\lim_{\delta\,\to\,0^{+}}\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\delta\,\to\,0^{+}}\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)

(23)

\displaystyle\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon)

\displaystyle=\,\,\lim_{\delta\,\to\,0^{+}}\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\sup_{\delta\,\to\,0^{+}}\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\mathrlap{.}

Remark 4.2
.

In the literature, we can find an alternative definition of Katok entropy, whose values for ergodic measures are as above but the extension to invariant probabilities is done by integrating over their ergodic decompositions. In particular, the resulting entropy-like map is convex. Thus the corresponding measure-theoretic metric mean dimension does not satisfy a classical variational principle (cf. Proposition
7.2
).

4.2.
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-Shapira entropy

Given a finite open cover
{\mathcal{U}}
of
X
, the diameter of
{\mathcal{U}}
, which we denote by
\operatorname{{diam}}({\mathcal{U}},d)
, is given by
\max_{U\,\in\,{\mathcal{U}}}\,\operatorname{{diam}}(U,d)
, where the diameter
\operatorname{{diam}}(A,d)
of a set
A\subset X
is the supremum of the distances between pairs of points in
A
. In what follows,
\mathrm{Leb}({\mathcal{U}})
stands for the Lebesgue number of
{\mathcal{U}}
, that is, the largest
r>0
such that every open ball of radius
r
is contained in some element of
{\mathcal{U}}
.

Take
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
. For
0<\delta<1
and a finite open cover
{\mathcal{U}}
of
X
, we denote by
\mathcal{N}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)
the minimum number of elements of
{\mathcal{U}}
whose union has a
\mu
-measure strictly greater than
1-\delta
. Set

\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\log\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}}^{n},\delta)}{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)\,=\,\liminf_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\log\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}}^{n},\delta)}{n}

where
{\mathcal{U}}^{n}\,=\,\bigvee_{j=0}^{n-1}\,T^{-j}\big({\mathcal{U}}\big)
.

Shapira proved in
[
14
]
that, if
\mu
is ergodic, then the above limits exist and are independent of
\delta
. However, since we are interested in all invariant measures and their interplay with
\delta
, we keep it in the notation.

Definition 4.3
.

The
upper
and
lower
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-Shapira entropies of
\mu
are given by

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,=\,\inf_{\operatorname{{diam}}({\mathcal{U}},d)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)\qquad\text{\qquad}\underline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,=\,\inf_{\operatorname{{diam}}({\mathcal{U}},d)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\underline{h}^{S}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}},\delta).

(24)

The next lemma describes a connection between Katok and Shapira entropies.

Lemma 4.4
.

For every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\delta<1
,

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta/4).

and

\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta/4).

Proof.

We prove the inequalities in the first line; a similar reasoning shows the others. Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
,
\varepsilon>0
and
0<\delta<1
, fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
{\mathcal{U}}
such that
\operatorname{{diam}}({\mathcal{U}},d)\leq\varepsilon
. For each nonempty
U\in{\mathcal{U}}^{n}
, take
x_{U}\in U
. Then
U\subset B_{n}(x_{U},\varepsilon)
and

{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(n,\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\min\big\{{\mathcal{F}}\subset{\mathcal{U}}^{n}\,\colon\,\mu\big(\cup_{U\in{\mathcal{F}}}B_{n}(x_{U},\varepsilon)\big)>1-\delta\big\}\,\,\leq\,\,{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}}^{n},\delta).

Thus,

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\leq\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)

and the first inequality follows by taking the infimum over
{\mathcal{U}}.

Regarding the remaining inequalities, let
{\mathcal{U}}
be a cover of
X
satisfying
\operatorname{{diam}}({\mathcal{U}},d)\leq\varepsilon
and
\mathrm{Leb}({\mathcal{U}})\geq\varepsilon/4
(which always exists, cf.
[
6
, Lemma 3.4]
). Since every Bowen ball
B_{n}(x,\varepsilon/4)
is contained in some element of
{\mathcal{U}}^{n}
, we have
{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}({\mathcal{U}}^{n},\delta)\leq{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(n,\varepsilon/4,\delta)
. Thus,

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{S}({\mathcal{U}},\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon/4,\delta/4).

∎

4.3.
Brin-Katok
\varepsilon
-entropy

Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
, this notion of complexity is based on the average decay rate of the size, measured by
\mu
, of a dynamical ball in
X
.

Definition 4.5
.

For
x\in X
and
\varepsilon>0
, the
upper
and
lower Brin-Katok entropies of
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
at
x
with respect to the scale
\varepsilon
are given by

\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,-\,\frac{\log\,\mu\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)}{n}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,=\,\liminf_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,-\,\frac{\log\,\mu\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)}{n}

and the
upper
and
lower Brin-Katok entropies of
\mu
at scale
\varepsilon
are given, respectively, by

\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)\,=\,\int\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,d\mu(x)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,=\,\int\underline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,d\mu(x).

The following two properties will be useful later on.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
and
\varepsilon>0
. Then

\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,\,\leq\,\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon/2)\qquad\text{at }\,\mu\text{-a.e. }x\,\in\,X.

In particular,

\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)\,\,\leq\,\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon/2).

Proof.

To simplify the notation, set

h_{\varepsilon/2}\,=\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon/2).

For each
\gamma>0
, let

A_{\gamma}\,=\,\{x\,\in\,X\,\colon\,\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,>\,h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma\}.

Claim:

\quad\mu(A_{\gamma})\,=\,0
for every
\gamma>0.

Proof of the Claim:

Fix
\gamma>0
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N},
let

\displaystyle A_{\gamma}^{(n)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\big\{x\,\in\,X\,\colon\,-\frac{1}{n}\,\log\mu\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)\,>\,h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma\big\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\,\big\{x\,\in\,X\,\colon\,\mu\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)\,<\,e^{-n(h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma)}\big\}.

Take
n_{\gamma}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

S(X,n,\varepsilon/2)\,\,\leq\,\,e^{n(\,h_{\varepsilon/2}\,\,+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,)}\qquad\forall\,n\,\geq\,n_{\gamma}.

Hence,

A_{\gamma}=\bigcap_{N\,\geq\,n_{\gamma}}\,\bigcup_{n\,\geq\,N}\,A_{\gamma}^{(n)}.

Fix
N\,\geq\,n_{\gamma}
. For each
n\,\geq\,N
, let
E_{n}
be a maximal
(n,\varepsilon/2)
-separated subset of
X.
Then

A_{\gamma}^{(n)}\,\subset\,\bigcup_{y\,\in\,E_{n}}\,B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)\qquad\quad\text{and}\quad\qquad\#E_{n}\,\,\leq\,\,e^{n\,(\,h_{\varepsilon/2}\,\,+\,\frac{\gamma}{2}\,)}.

For each
x\in A_{\gamma}^{(n)}
, take
y\,\in\,E_{n}
satisfying
x\,\in\,B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)
. Then
B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)\,\subset\,B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)
, which implies that
\mu\big(B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)\big)\,<\,e^{-n(h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma)}
. Define

E^{\prime}_{n}\,\,=\,\,\Big\{y\,\in\,E_{n}\,\colon\,\mu\big(B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)\big)\,<\,e^{-n(h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma)}\Big\}.

Then
A_{\gamma}^{(n)}\,\subset\,\bigcup_{y\,\in\,E^{\prime}_{n}}\,B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)
and we get

\mu\big(A_{\gamma}^{(n)}\big)\,\,\leq\,\,\sum_{y\,\in\,E^{\prime}_{n}}\,\mu\big(B_{n}(y,\varepsilon/2)\big)\,\,\leq\,\,\#E^{\prime}_{n}\,\,\,e^{-n(h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma)}\,\,\leq\,\,\#E_{n}\,\,\,e^{-n(h_{\varepsilon/2}\,+\,\gamma)}\,\,\leq\,\,e^{\frac{-n\gamma}{2}}.

Thus,

\mu\big(A_{\gamma}\big)\,\,\leq\,\,\mu\Big(\bigcup_{n\,\geq\,N}\,A_{\gamma}^{(n)}\Big)\,\,\leq\,\,\sum_{n\,\geq\,N}\,e^{\frac{-n\gamma}{2}}\,\,=\,\,\frac{e^{\frac{-N\gamma}{2}}}{1\,-\,e^{\frac{-\gamma}{2}}}\qquad\forall\,N\,\geq\,n_{\gamma}.

We finish the proof of the claim by taking
N\,\to\,+\infty
.
∎

Now, Lemma
4.6
is a straightforward consequence of the claim since, given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
and
\varepsilon>0
,

X\setminus\Big\{x\in X\colon\,\,\overline{h}^{BK}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,x)\,\leq\,\limsup_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,\log S(X,n,\varepsilon/2)\Big\}\,\,\subseteq\bigcup_{\gamma\,\in\,\mathbb{Q}\,\cap\,(0,+\infty)}A_{\gamma}.

∎

Lemma 4.7
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, the maps

\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)\,\,\,\longmapsto\,\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)\,\,\,\longmapsto\,\,\,\underline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)

are convex.

Proof.

We prove the assertion for the upper Brin-Katok entropy; the reasoning concerning the other map is similar. Fix
\varepsilon>0
. Let
(\mu_{k})_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of invariant probability measures and
(t_{k})_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of positive real numbers such that
\sum_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,t_{k}\,=\,1
. Consider
\mu\,=\,\sum_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,t_{k}\,\mu_{k}\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X).
For each
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\mu\,\geq\,t_{k}\,\mu_{k}
. Therefore, for all
x\in X
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\log\,\mu\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)\,\,\geq\,\,\log\,\mu_{k}\big(B_{n}(x,\varepsilon)\big)\,+\,\log\,t_{k}

and so

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon,x)\,\,\leq\,\,\overline{h}_{\mu_{k}}^{BK}(\varepsilon,x)\qquad\forall\,x\in X.

We finish the proof by integrating both sides of the previous inequality with respect to
\mu_{k}
, multiplying both sides by
t_{k}
and adding up over
k\in\mathbb{N}.

∎

4.4.
Rényi entropy

Denote by
P
any finite Borel measurable partition of
X
, by
\operatorname{{diam}}(P)
its diameter, defined by
\max_{A\,\in\,P}\,\operatorname{{diam}}A
and by
P^{n}
the refined partition
\bigvee_{j=0}^{n-1}\,T^{-j}(P)
. Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
, the measure-theoretic entropy of
\mu
with respect to
P
is defined by

h_{\mu}(P)\,=\,\lim_{n\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{1}{n}\,H_{\mu}(P^{n})

where
H_{\mu}(Q)\,=\,-\sum_{\mathrm{Q}\,\in\,Q}\,\mu(\mathrm{Q})\,\log\mu(\mathrm{Q})
stands for the Shannon entropy.

Definition 4.8
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, the
Rényi
\varepsilon
-entropy of
\mu
at scale
\varepsilon
is defined by

\inf_{\operatorname{{diam}}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,h_{\mu}(P)

where the infimum is taken over all finite Borel measurable partitions of X with diameter at most
\varepsilon
.

We refer the reader to
[
6
, Section 3]
for more information on this concept.

5.
Proof of Theorem
B

Assume that
F\colon{\mathcal{K}}\times(0,+\infty)\,\to[0,+\infty]
satisfies the s.m.m.d. property and let
C>0
be the constant provided by Definition
2.5
.

Clearly, for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{K}}
, one has

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\geq\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

and so

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\geq\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

(25)

Regarding the converse inequality, set

\mathcal{L}\,=\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

and let
(\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})_{\ell\,\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of scales converging to zero such that

\mathcal{L}\,=\,\lim_{\ell\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})}.

Taking a subsequence if necessary, we can assume that

\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell}\,<\,2^{-\ell}\quad\quad\forall\,\ell\in\mathbb{N}.

Fix a sequence
(t_{\ell})_{\ell\,\in\mathbb{N}}
of positive real numbers with polynomial decay to zero as
\ell
goes to
+\infty
and such that
\sum_{\ell\,\in\mathbb{N}}\,t_{\ell}\,=\,1
. For example, we may choose
t_{\ell}\,=\,6/(\pi\,\ell)^{2}
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, since
\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell}\,<\,2^{-\ell}
for every
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
, the sequence
\varepsilon_{\ell}\,=t_{\ell}\,\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell}/C
satisfies

\lim_{\ell\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{\ell})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})}\,=\,1

and so

\mathcal{L}\,\,=\,\,\lim_{\ell\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon^{\prime}_{\ell})}\,\,=\,\,\lim_{\ell\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,C\,\varepsilon_{\ell}\,/t_{\ell})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{\ell})}.

Case 1
:
\mathcal{L}<+\infty
.

For every
N\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
L(N)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,F(\mu,C\,\varepsilon_{\ell}\,/t_{\ell})\,\,>\,\,\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\Big)\,\cdot\,\mathcal{L}\,\cdot\,\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{\ell})\qquad\forall\,\ell\geq L(N).

(26)

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

L(N+1)\,>\,L(N)\quad\quad\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}.

Consider the subsequence
(\varepsilon_{L(N)})_{N\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
of
(\varepsilon_{\ell})_{\ell\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
and a sequence of measures
(\mu_{N})_{N\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
in
{\mathcal{K}}
such that

F\big(\mu_{N},C\,\varepsilon_{L(N)}\,/t_{L(N)}\big)\,\,>\,\,\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\Big)\,\cdot\,\mathcal{L}\,\cdot\,\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{L(N)})\qquad\forall N\in\mathbb{N}.

(27)

Let
\mu_{0}\in{\mathcal{K}}
be defined by

\mu_{0}\,=\,\beta\,\cdot\sum_{N\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,t_{L(N)}\,\mu_{N}

where
\beta
is a normalization constant, which is greater than or equal to
1
since each natural number appears at most once in the sequence
\big({L(N)}\big)_{N\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\mu_{0}\,\geq\,t_{L(N)}\,\mu_{N}\quad\quad\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}

so, by the s.m.m.d. property in
{\mathcal{K}}
and the inequality (
27
), we get

F\big(\mu_{0},\varepsilon_{L(N)}\big)\,\,\geq\,\,F\big(\mu_{N},C\,\varepsilon_{L(N)}\,/t_{L(N)}\big)\,\,>\,\,\Big(1-\frac{1}{N}\Big)\,\cdot\,\mathcal{L}\,\cdot\,\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{L(N)})\qquad\forall\,N\in\mathbb{N}.

By dividing the previous estimates by
\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{L(N)})
and taking
N\,\to\,+\infty
, we obtain

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu_{0},\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\geq\,\,\limsup_{N\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{F(\mu_{0},\varepsilon_{L(N)})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon_{L(N)})}\,\,\geq\,\,\mathcal{L}.

(28)

Bringing together (
25
) and (
28
), we conclude that

\mathcal{L}\,\,=\,\max_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{K}}}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

Case 2
:
\mathcal{L}=+\infty
.

The proof in this case proceeds in a similar way after replacing
\big(1-\frac{1}{N}\big)\,\cdot\,\mathcal{L}
on the right-hand side of (
26
) by
N
.

Proof of Theorem

This section is divided into two parts, corresponding to the choice of either the Wasserstein or the Lévy-Prokhorov distance to metrize the weak

topology.

6.1.
W_{p}
-mean quantization dimension

Definition
2.1
admits the following reformulation when
D
is a
p
-Wasserstein metric for some
p\in[1,+\infty)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

[
1
, Proposition 3.2]
The quantization number
Q_{\mu,W_{p}}(\varepsilon)
for the metric
W_{p}
is the minimal cardinality of a set
F\subset X
satisfying

\int d(x,F)^{p}\,d\mu(x)\,\,\leq\,\,\varepsilon^{p}

where
d(x,F)\,=\,\min_{y\,\in\,F}\,d(x,y).

The next lemma relates the Katok entropies to the dynamical quantization numbers with respect to the Wasserstein distances.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
(X,d)
be a compact metric space and
T\colon X\to X
be a continuous map. For every
\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)
and
p\,\in[1,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2\varepsilon,3/4)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{1},\varepsilon)\,\,\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{p},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2^{-1/p}\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2\varepsilon,3/4)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{1},\varepsilon)\,\,\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{p},\varepsilon)

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\,\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2^{-1/p}\varepsilon).

Proof.

We shall prove the statement for the upper limits, starting at the first inequality. Given
n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
F\subset X
be such that

\#F\,=\,Q_{\mu,W_{1,n}(\varepsilon)}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int d_{n}(x,F)\,d\mu(x)\,\,\leq\,\,\varepsilon.

By Markov’s inequality (cf.
[
13
]
),

\mu\Big(d_{n}(\cdot\,,\,F)\,\geq\,2\,\varepsilon\Big)\,\,\leq\,\,\frac{1}{2\,\varepsilon}\,\int d_{n}(x,F)\,d\mu(x)\,\,\leq\,\,\frac{1}{2}.

Thus,

\mu\Big(\bigcup_{y\,\in\,F}\,B_{n}(y,2\,\varepsilon)\Big)\,\,\geq\,\,1/2\,\,>\,\,1\,-\,3/4

and so

Q_{\mu,W_{1,n}(\varepsilon)}\,\,\geq\,\,\mathcal{N}_{\mu}(n,2\,\varepsilon,3/4).

We conclude the argument by applying logarithm to both sides of this inequality, dividing by
n
and taking
n\to+\infty.

The second inequality is immediate since the map
p\,\mapsto\,W_{p}
is non-decreasing (cf.
[
19
, p. 210]
) or (
21
)). Regarding the third inequality, fix

0\,<\,\delta\,<\,\frac{1}{2}\,\Big(\frac{\varepsilon}{\operatorname{{diam}}(X,d)}\Big)^{p}

and

s\,>\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2^{-1/p}\varepsilon,\delta).

Then, for sufficiently large
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

{\mathcal{N}}_{\mu}(n,2^{-1/p}\varepsilon,\delta)\,<\,e^{n\,s}.

Take
F\subset X
such that

\mu\Big(\bigcup_{y\,\in\,F}\,B_{n}(y,2^{-1/p}\,\varepsilon)\Big)\,\,\,>\,\,\,1-\delta\qquad\text{and}\qquad\#F\,<\,e^{n\,s}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\int d_{n}(\cdot\,,\,F)^{p}\,d\mu\,\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\int_{\displaystyle\bigcup_{y\,\in\,F}\,B_{n}(y,2^{-1/p}\,\varepsilon)}\,\,d_{n}(\cdot\,,\,F)^{p}\,d\mu\,\,+\,\,\int_{\displaystyle X\setminus\bigcup_{y\,\in\,F}\,B_{n}(y,2^{-1/p}\,\varepsilon)}\,\,d_{n}(\cdot\,,\,F)^{p}\,d\mu

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{2}\,\,\mu\Big(\bigcup_{y\,\in\,F}\,B_{n}(y,2^{-1/p}\,\varepsilon)\Big)\,\,+\,\,\delta\,\cdot\,\operatorname{{diam}}(X,d_{n})^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\frac{\varepsilon^{p}}{2}\,\,+\,\,\delta\,\cdot\,\operatorname{{diam}}(X,d)^{p}\,\,\,\,\,\leq\,\,\,\,\,\varepsilon^{p}.

Thus,

Q_{\mu,W_{p,n}(\varepsilon)}\,\,\leq\,\,\#F\,\,<\,\,e^{ns}

and so

\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{p},\varepsilon)\,\,\leq\,\,s.

To finish the proof, we are left to make
s\,\to\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2^{-1/p}\varepsilon,\delta)
and take the limit as
\delta\to 0^{+}
.
∎

By (
3
), (
4
) and the fact that

\Big\{\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2\varepsilon,3/4),\,\,\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon),\,\,\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(2\varepsilon,3/4),\,\,\underline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon)\Big\}\,\subset\,{\mathcal{D}}

it is easy to see that Lemma
6.2
yields:

(a)
For every
\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{E}}_{T}(X)
,
F\,\in\,{\mathcal{D}}
and
p\,\in[1,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,W_{p},\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,W_{p},\mu)

\displaystyle\,=\,

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

and, for every
p\,\in[1,+\infty)
,

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T)

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\overline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{p},\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,

\displaystyle=\,\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,\mathcal{E}_{T}(X)}\,\frac{\underline{Q}_{\mu}(T,W_{p},\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\mathrlap{.}

Combining the previous equalities with (

(b)
For every

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{M}(X,d,T)

Proof of Theorem

Since the

Combining this information with Lemma

\displaystyle\overline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,LP,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,=\,\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\underline{\mathrm{mdim}}_{Q}(X,d,T,LP,\mu)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\,\,\,\,=\,\,\,\liminf_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{S}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Remark 7.1
.

By definition, for a fixed
\delta>0
, the
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-Katok entropy satisfies

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)\,\,\geq\,\,\overline{h}_{\nu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta/t)\,\,\geq\,\,\overline{h}_{\nu}^{K}(\varepsilon/t,\delta/t)

whenever
\mu\geq t\,\nu
for some

. In particular,
\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\varepsilon)
has the s.m.m.d. property, and the map
\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon)\,=\,\lim_{\delta\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon,\delta)
satisfies the following stronger version of that property:

\overline{h}_{\mu}^{K}(\varepsilon)\,\,\geq\,\,\overline{h}_{\nu}^{K}(\varepsilon)\,\,\geq\,\,\overline{h}_{\nu}^{K}(\varepsilon/t).

The corresponding statements for the Shapira entropies also hold.

7.1.
Example

Consider the interval
[0,1]
with the Euclidean distance and the space
[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}
endowed with the following distance, which is compatible with the product topology:

d\big((x_{n})_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}},(y_{n})_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}\big)\,\,=\,\,\sum_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}\,\frac{|x_{n}\,-\,y_{n}|}{2^{|n|}}.

It is known (cf.
[
9
]
) that the shift map

\sigma\big((x_{n})_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}\big)\,\,=\,\,(x_{n+1})_{n\,\in\,\mathbb{Z}}

satisfies

\mathrm{mdim}_{M}([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}},d,\sigma)\,\,=\,\,1.

We refer to a closed shift-invariant subset
Y\subset[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}
as a
subshift
.

Fix a sequence
a\,=\,(a_{k})_{k\,\geq\,0}
of real numbers and a sequence
b\,=\,(b_{k})_{k\,\geq\,1}
of positive integers that meet the following conditions:

\begin{split}&\text{(C1) }\hskip 8.5359pt0=a_{0}<a_{1}<...<a_{k}\to 1.\hskip 227.62204pt\\
&\text{(C2)}\hskip 8.5359pt(a_{k}-a_{k-1})_{k}\text{ decreases to zero.}\\
&\text{(C3) }\hskip 8.5359pt(b_{k})_{k}\text{ is strictly increasing.}\end{split}

To simplify the notation, we write

\varepsilon_{k}\,\,=\,\,\frac{|a_{k}\,-\,a_{k-1}|}{b_{k}}

(31)

and, for each
k\in\mathbb{N},

J_{k}\,\,=\,\,\Big\{a_{k-1}\,+\,j\,\varepsilon_{k}\,\,\,\colon\,\,j\,\in\,\big\{0,...,b_{k}-1\big\}\Big\}.

Then,

J_{i}\,\cap\,J_{j}\,=\,\emptyset\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,i\neq j\qquad\text{and}\qquad|x-y|\,\geq\,\varepsilon_{k}\,\,\,\,\,\,\forall\,x,y\,\in\,J_{k}.

Denote
X_{k}\,=\,\big(J_{k}\big)^{\mathbb{Z}}
and the subshift

X\,\,=\,\,\bigcup_{k=1}^{+\infty}\,X_{k}\,\,\cup\,\,\big\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\big\}\,\,\subset\,\,[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}.

By construction, every ergodic measure of the system
(X,d,\sigma)
is either the Dirac mass
\delta_{1^{\mathbb{Z}}}
or gives full mass to
X_{k}
, for some
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus, by the ergodic decomposition theorem, we may write any invariant probability measure
\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
as

\mu\,\,=\,\,\sum_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,t_{k}\,\mu_{k}\,\,+\,\,t_{0}\,\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}},

(32)

where
t_{0}\,=\,\mu(\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\})
and

\mu_{k}\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X),\qquad\mu_{k}(X_{k})\,=\,1,\qquad t_{k}\,=\,\mu(X_{k})\qquad\quad\forall\,k\in\mathbb{N}.

We recall that, given
k\in\mathbb{N}
, the topological entropy of the shift map restricted to
X_{k}
is
\log b_{k}
. So, the topological entropy of
(X,\sigma)
is infinite and there exists an invariant probability measure with maximal entropy, but it is not ergodic.

7.2.
Brin-Katok and Rényi entropies do not satisfy the variational principle (
12
)

In what follows, we assume that the parameters are chosen so that

\mathrm{mdim}_{M}(X,d,\sigma)\,=\,1\quad\quad\text{and}\quad\quad\lim_{k\,\to\,+\infty}\,\,\frac{\log\varepsilon_{k}}{\log\varepsilon_{k+1}}\,\,=\,\,1.

This is the case, for instance, if we take
a_{k}\,=\,\sum_{n=1}^{k}6/\pi n^{2}
and
b_{k}\,=\,3^{k}
.

Proposition 7.2
.

Consider the system
(X,d,\sigma)
and a map

F\colon(\mu,\varepsilon)\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)\times(0,+\infty)\mapsto F(\mu,\varepsilon).

Assume that:

(i)

The map

\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)\,\longmapsto\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

is convex.

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
and
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
,

F(\mu,\varepsilon)\,\,\leq\,\,h_{\varepsilon}(\operatorname{{supp}}(\mu),\,d|_{\operatorname{{supp}}(\mu)},\,\sigma|_{\operatorname{{supp}}(\mu)}).

Then

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,\leq\,\,\mu(\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\})\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\qquad\forall\,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X).

(33)

Proof.

Take
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
and consider its decomposition (
32
), say

\mu\,\,=\,\,\sum_{k\,\in\,\mathbb{N}}\,t_{k}\,\mu_{k}\,\,+\,\,t_{0}\,\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}}.

Fix
K\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu,\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{K}\,t_{k}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu_{k},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,+\,\,\sum_{k\,>\,K}\,t_{k}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\mu_{k},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\qquad\,+\,\,t_{0}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\leq\,\,\sum_{k\,=\,1}^{K}\,t_{k}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\log b_{K}}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,+\,\,\sum_{k\,>\,K}\,t_{k}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{h_{\varepsilon}(X,d,\sigma)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle\qquad\,+\,\,\,t_{0}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}

\displaystyle=\,\,\mu\big(\bigcup_{k\,>\,K}J_{k}\big)\,\mathrm{mdim}_{M}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,+\,\,t_{0}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{F(\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}},\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}.

Making
K\to+\infty
, we get (
33
) since

\lim_{K\,\to\,+\infty}\,\mu\big(\bigcup_{k\,>\,K}J_{k}\big)\,=\,0.

∎

Taking into account that

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\delta_{\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}}}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,=\,0

and summoning the properties of
\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}
described in Lemmas
4.6
and
4.7
, Proposition
7.2
yields:

Corollary 7.3
.

For the system
(X,d,\sigma)
we have,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,0\qquad\forall\,\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X).

Therefore, as
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)=1
,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,>\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\overline{h}_{\mu}^{BK}(\varepsilon)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

We also have a similar statement for the Rényi entropy.

Corollary 7.4
.

For every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\,h_{\mu}(P)}{\log(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,0.

Therefore, as
\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)=1
,

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,\sigma)\,\,>\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)}\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\,h_{\mu}(P)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

Proof.

We are left to show that the Rényi entropy satisfies the assumptions of Proposition
7.2
. We start by proving that the map

\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)\,\longmapsto\,\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\,\frac{\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\,h_{\mu}(P)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}

(34)

is convex. The next property was first established for ergodic measures in
[
6
]
and then extended to invariant probability measures in
[
23
]
.

Claim:

[
6
,
23
]

For every
\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}\big([0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}\big)
,

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\inf_{\mathrm{diam}(P)\,\leq\,\varepsilon}\,\,h_{\mu}(P)}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,\limsup_{m\,\to\,+\infty}\,\frac{h_{\mu}(P_{m})}{\log\,m}

(35)

where
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

P_{m}\,=\,\pi_{0}^{-1}\Big\{\Big[\frac{i}{m},\frac{i+1}{m}\Big)\,\colon\,i=0,...,m-1\Big\}

and
\pi_{0}\colon[0,1]^{\mathbb{Z}}\to[0,1]
is the projection on the
0
-coordinate.

In particular, the partitions considered on the limit at the right-hand side of (
35
) do not depend on the measure. Since, for each
m\in\mathbb{N}
, the map
\mu\mapsto h_{\mu}(P_{m})
is affine (cf.
[
22
, Proof of Theorem 8.1]
), and the
\limsup
of affine functions is convex, we conclude that the map (
34
) is convex.

The second assumption of Proposition
7.2
was established in
[
6
, Section 3]
.
∎

7.3.
(\varepsilon,\delta)-
Katok/Shapira entropy with fixed
\delta

Fix
\delta_{0}\in(0,1)
. Given
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
, there exists
K=K(\mu,\delta_{0})
such that
\mu\big(\bigcup_{k\,>\,K}J_{k}\big)<\delta_{0}
. Since
\sigma
restricted to
\bigcup_{k\,>\,K}J_{k}\cup\{1^{\mathbb{Z}}\}
has finite topological entropy, one has

\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}\,\,=\,\,0.

Therefore, as this equality is valid for every
\mu\in{\mathcal{P}}_{\sigma}(X)
, we conclude that

\mathrm{\overline{mdim}_{M}}(X,d,T)\,\,>\,\,\sup_{\mu\,\in\,{\mathcal{P}}_{T}(X)}\,\limsup_{\varepsilon\,\to\,0^{+}}\,\frac{\overline{h}^{K}_{\mu}(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})}{\log\,(1/\varepsilon)}.

By Lemma
4.4
, the above inequality also holds for the
(\varepsilon,\delta_{0})
-Shapira entropy.
