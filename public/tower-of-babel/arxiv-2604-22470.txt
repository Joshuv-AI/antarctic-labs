Error of discretization of Caputo fractional derivative in weighted spaces
Łukasz Płociniczak, Hubert Woszczek

Abstract
We establish uniform error bounds of the L1 discretization of the Caputo fractional derivative of the function from the weighted Sobolev space with weight belonging to the Mucknenhoupt class. We present how our framework works for several examples of weight, which belong to the Muckenhoupt class. As and application, we show the convergence of the L1 scheme for the Fractional ODE. Finally, we verify the theoretical results with numerical illustrations.



Keywords:
Caputo derivative; L1 scheme; weighted Sobolev spaces; Muckenhoupt weights; uniform error bounds

MSC:
65D25, 26A33, 46E35, 65M15

1
Introduction

The Caputo derivative is defined for sufficiently smooth functions by

D^{\alpha}y(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{y^{\prime}(s)}{(t-s)^{\alpha}}ds\ ,\quad 0<\alpha<1.

(2)

Equivalence between formulations can be recovered by integrating by parts. The most prominent methods used to discretize the Caputo derivative are derived or generalized from Convolution Quadrature (utilizing the convolutional form of the Caputo derivative,
[
5
]
), Grünwald-Letnikov (a generalization of finite differences,
[
2
]
), or the L1 scheme (based on piecewise linear approximation,
[
8
]
). The latter can be expressed as

\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n}):=\frac{\tau^{-\alpha}}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\left(y(t_{n})-b_{n-1}y(0)-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(b_{n-i-1}-b_{n-i})y(t_{i})\right),

(3)

where
b_{i}=(i+1)^{1-\alpha}-i^{1-\alpha}
. Through this scheme, the Caputo derivative is approximated on the discrete grid
t_{n}=n\tau\leq T
(with
t_{0}=0
), where
\tau>0
is the time step and
T>0
is the maximal time.

The L1 method has many favorable properties that make it versatile in the design of numerical methods for various applications. Due to its convenient properties, the L1 method was analyzed in various functional settings. The historically first proof of the accuracy of the discretization was obtained for functions belonging to space
C^{2}([0,T])
resulting in order
2-\alpha
(see, for example,
[
4
]
, Theorem 4.1). In this setting, it is also possible to find the explicit asymptotic form of the best error constant
\overset{\sim}{C}_{\alpha,n}\sim-\zeta(\alpha-1)/\Gamma(2-\alpha)
, where
\zeta
is the Riemann zeta function (see
[
7
]
). Recently, the trunction error for the function belonging to the Hölder space
C^{k,\beta}([0,T])
, with
k=0,1
and
0\leq\beta\leq 1
with
k+\beta>\alpha
was analyzed in
[
1
]
. The resulting order was equal to
k+\alpha-\beta
. One can also use graded meshes to maintain an optimal convergence rate of
2-\alpha
, simultaneously relaxing the functional setting to space
C^{\alpha}([0,T])
, with
y^{(m)}\sim t^{\alpha-m}
for
m=0,1,2
(see
[
9
,
3
]
). In this paper, we establish uniform bounds on the discretization error of the L1 method for functions belonging to a weighted Sobolev space
W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
. To formally define this functional setting, we first introduce the weighted space
L^{p}
.

Definition 1
.

Let
\omega:(0,T)\to[0,\infty)
be a locally integrable weight function and
1\leq p<\infty
. The weighted space
L^{p}
, denoted
L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T)
, is equipped with the norm

\|y\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}|y(t)|^{p}\,\omega(t)dt\right)^{1/p}.

(4)

Building upon this, we define the corresponding weighted Sobolev space.

Definition 2
.

The weighted Sobolev space
W^{s,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
is defined as

W^{s,p}_{\omega}(0,T):=\left\{y\in L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T):y^{\prime},y^{\prime\prime},\ldots,y^{(s)}\in L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T)\right\},

(5)

endowed with the norm

\|y\|_{W^{s,p}_{\omega}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{T}\left(\sum_{k=0}^{s}|y^{(k)}(t)|^{p}\right)\omega(t)dt\right)^{1/p}.

(6)

Finally, we require the weight function to satisfy a specific integrability condition, encapsulated by the Muckenhoupt class.

Definition 3
.

For
1<p<\infty
, a weight
\omega
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
if the following condition holds

[\omega]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{J\subset(0,T)}\left(\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}\omega(t)dt\right)\left(\frac{1}{|J|}\int_{J}\omega(t)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}dt\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

(7)

where the supremum is taken over all sub-intervals
J
of
(0,T)
.

It is a well-known phenomenon in the theory of fractional calculus that solutions to time-fractional differential equations typically exhibit a weak singularity at the initial time
t=0
, even when the source term and the initial data are smooth. For example, the solution often contains terms
y(t)\sim t^{\alpha}
near the origin. While the function itself remains continuous, its derivatives become singular. Due to this singular behavior, the second derivative
y^{\prime\prime}(t)
frequently fails to be integrable over an open interval, which means that the exact solution
y
does not belong to the standard Sobolev space
W^{2,p}(0,T)
or the space of smooth functions
C^{2}[0,T]
. Consequently, traditional error analyses that rely on the assumption
y\in C^{2}[0,T]
or
y\in W^{2,p}(0,T)
are practically restrictive and often inconsistent with the true regularity of fractional differential equations. To resolve this limitation, it is necessary to introduce a weight function
\omega(t)
that controls the singularity near the origin. Thanks to that, we can guarantee that
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
. The Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
class is specifically chosen here because it provides the precise harmonic analysis framework required to bound the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operators in weighted spaces. Utilizing
A_{p}
weights allows us to perform a robust numerical analysis that is entirely consistent with the physically realistic, non-smooth behavior of fractional-order models.

2
Main results

We start with the following proposition, which justifies the well-posedness of the Caputo derivative in our spaces.

Proposition 1

(Well-Definedness of the Caputo Derivative)
.

Let
0<\alpha<1
and
1<p<\infty
. Assume that the weight
\omega
belongs to the Muckenhoupt class
A_{p}
on
(0,T)
. Then, the Caputo fractional derivative operator
D^{\alpha}
is a bounded linear operator from
W^{1,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
to
L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T)
. That is, there exists a constant
C>0
such that for all
y\in W^{1,p}_{\omega}(0,T)

\|D^{\alpha}y\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\|y\|_{W^{1,p}_{\omega}}.

(8)

Proof.

It is well-known that the Caputo derivative can be expressed as the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral of order
1-\alpha
applied to the first derivative
y^{\prime}
. Let
\beta=1-\alpha
. We denote the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator by

I^{\beta}f(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-s)^{\beta-1}f(s)ds.

(9)

Thus, we can write
D^{\alpha}y(t)=I^{1-\alpha}(y^{\prime})(t)
. To prove the proposition, it suffices to show that the operator
I^{1-\alpha}
is bounded on the weighted space
L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T)
. It is a classical result in harmonic analysis (see, e.g.,
[
6
]
) that the Riemann-Liouville fractional integral operator
I^{\beta}
is bounded on
L^{p}_{\omega}(0,T)
provided that the weight
\omega
satisfies the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
condition. Therefore, we have

\|I^{1-\alpha}y^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}},

(10)

where
C
is a constant depending on
p
,
\alpha
, and the
A_{p}
characteristic of
\omega
. Since
\|y^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq\|y\|_{W^{1,p}_{\omega}}
by the definition of the Sobolev norm, we conclude

\|D^{\alpha}y\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}=\|I^{1-\alpha}y^{\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}\leq C\|y\|_{W^{1,p}_{\omega}},

(11)

and the proof is complete.
∎

Next, we state the main result.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\omega\in A_{p}
and
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
. The uniform error of the L1 scheme satisfies

|D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})|\leq C\tau^{2-\alpha-1/p}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}},

(12)

where

\Lambda(\omega,\tau)=\sup_{0\leq j\leq N-1}\tau^{-1/q}W_{j}=\sup_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\omega(s)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}ds\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

(13)

Proof.

We consider a uniform grid
t_{n}=n\tau
for
n=0,\dots,N
, with step size
\tau=T/N
. The L1 scheme is given by replacing
y^{\prime}(s)
with the backward difference quotient
u_{j}
:

\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}u_{j}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}ds,

(14)

where
u_{j}=\frac{y(t_{j+1})-y(t_{j})}{\tau}
. Let us define the seminorm

[y]_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}:=\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}}.

(15)

Since
[y]_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}\leq\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}
, we will derive the bounds in terms of this seminorm. Let us denote
R_{n}(y):=|D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})|
. Subtracting (
14
) from the exact derivative, we have

R_{n}(y)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(1-\alpha)}\left|\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j})ds\right|.

(16)

Since
y^{\prime}
is an absolutely continuous function, using the Taylor series expansion with the integral form of the remainder, for all
s\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})
, we have

|y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j}|=\left|y^{\prime}(s)-\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}y^{\prime}(\xi)d\xi\right|=\left|\tau^{-1}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\int_{\xi}^{s}y^{\prime\prime}(\eta)d\eta d\xi\right|\leq\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|y^{\prime\prime}(\eta)|d\eta.

(17)

Let
Y_{j}:=\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|y^{\prime\prime}(\eta)|d\eta
. Thus,
\sup_{s\in(t_{j},t_{j+1})}|y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j}|\leq Y_{j}
. Since the only singularity occurs in the term
j=n-1
with
n\geq 1
, this term has to be estimated separately. By direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(y^{\prime}(s)-u_{n-1})ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}|y^{\prime}(s)-u_{n-1}|ds

\displaystyle\leq Y_{n-1}\int_{t_{n-1}}^{t_{n}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}ds=\frac{\tau^{1-\alpha}}{1-\alpha}Y_{n-1}.

(18)

We estimate the remaining part as follows. Since
\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j})ds=0
, and
(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha}
are constant, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j})ds\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\left[(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}-(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha}\right](y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j})ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}-(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha}||y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j}|ds.

(19)

Let
k(s)=(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}
. From the Mean Value Theorem, we have
|k(s)-k(t_{j+1})|=|k^{\prime}(\xi)||s-t_{j+1}|
for some
\xi\in[t_{j},t_{j+1}]
. Since
k^{\prime}(s)=\alpha(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha-1}
, we have
\max_{\xi\in[t_{j},t_{j+1}]}k^{\prime}(\xi)=\alpha(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}
and
|k(s)-k(t_{j+1})|\leq\tau\alpha(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}
. Hence, we have

\displaystyle\left|\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}(y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j})ds\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|(t_{n}-s)^{-\alpha}-(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha}||y^{\prime}(s)-u_{j}|ds

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\tau^{2}(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}Y_{j}.

(20)

From Hölder’s inequality with conjugates
p,q
(
1/p+1/q=1
), we have

\displaystyle Y_{j}

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|y^{\prime\prime}(s)|\omega(s)^{1/p}\omega(s)^{-1/p}ds

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}|y^{\prime\prime}(s)|^{p}\omega(s)ds\right)^{1/p}\left(\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\omega(s)^{-q/p}ds\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle=\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}\|\omega^{-1/p}\|_{L^{q}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}.

(21)

Let us denote
W_{j}:=\|\omega^{-1/p}\|_{L^{q}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}
. Plugging all these calculations into (
16
), we have

R_{n}(y)\leq C\left[\tau^{1-\alpha}W_{n-1}\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}(t_{n-1},t_{n})}+\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\tau^{2}(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}W_{j}\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}\right].

(22)

Applying Hölder’s inequality to the sum in the above inequality, we have

\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}A_{j}B_{j}\leq\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}A_{j}^{q}\right)^{1/q}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}B_{j}^{p}\right)^{1/p},

(23)

where
A_{j}=\tau^{2}(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}W_{j}
and
B_{j}=\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}
. Note that
(\sum B_{j}^{p})^{1/p}=\|y^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{p}_{\omega}(0,t_{n})}\leq\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}
. Now, let us analyze the term:

\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}A_{j}^{q}\right)^{1/q}=\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}\left[\tau^{2}(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-\alpha-1}W_{j}\right]^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

(24)

To do this, we introduce

\Lambda(\omega,\tau):=\sup_{0\leq j\leq N-1}\tau^{-1/q}W_{j}=\sup_{j}\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\omega(s)^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}ds\right)^{\frac{p-1}{p}}.

(25)

Then
W_{j}\leq\tau^{1/q}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}A_{j}^{q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\tau^{1/q}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{n-2}(t_{n}-t_{j+1})^{-(\alpha+1)q}\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\tau^{1/q}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\left(\frac{1}{\tau}\int_{0}^{t_{n}-\tau}(t_{n}-s)^{-(\alpha+1)q}ds\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq\tau^{2}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{t_{n}-\tau}(t_{n}-s)^{-(\alpha+1)q}ds\right)^{1/q}

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\tau^{2-(\alpha+1)+1/q}=C_{2}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\tau^{2-\alpha-1/p}.

(26)

This concludes the proof.
∎

Corollary 1
.

Let
\omega(t)=t^{\mu}
. If
y\in W^{2,p}_{t^{\mu}}(0,T)
and
0\leq\mu<p-1
, the discretization error is bounded by

|D^{\alpha}y-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y|\leq C\tau^{2-\alpha-\frac{1+\mu}{p}}\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{t^{\mu}}}.

(27)

Proof.

We calculate
\Lambda(\omega,\tau)
for
\omega(t)=t^{\mu}
. We must evaluate
W_{j}=\|t^{-\mu/p}\|_{L^{q}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}
. For the first interval
[0,\tau]
, we have

W_{0}=\left(\int_{0}^{\tau}t^{-\frac{\mu q}{p}}dt\right)^{1/q}.

(28)

Convergence requires
\frac{\mu q}{p}<1\iff\mu<\frac{p}{q}=p-1
. Evaluating the integral, we have

W_{0}=\left(\frac{\tau^{1-\mu q/p}}{1-\mu q/p}\right)^{1/q}=\left(\frac{p}{p-\mu q}\right)^{1/q}\tau^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\mu}{p}}.

(29)

Since
t^{-\mu q/p}
is a strictly decreasing function of
t>0
, the supremum is achieved at
j=0
. Thus, we have

\Lambda(t^{\mu},\tau)=\left(\frac{p}{p-\mu q}\right)^{1/q}\tau^{-1/q}\tau^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\mu}{p}}=\left(\frac{p}{p-\mu q}\right)^{1/q}\tau^{-\frac{\mu}{p}}.

(30)

Finally, we substitute
\Lambda(t^{\mu},\tau)
into the bound obtained in Theorem
1
, which yields the desired result.
∎

Corollary 2
.

Let the Jacobi weight be given by
\omega(t)=t^{\mu}(T-t)^{\gamma}
. If
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
with
0\leq\mu<p-1
and
0\leq\gamma<p-1
, the discretization error is bounded by

|D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})|\leq C\tau^{2-\alpha-\frac{1+\nu}{p}}\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}},

(31)

where
\nu=\max(\mu,\gamma)
and
C
is a constant independent of
\tau
.

Proof.

We need to estimate
\Lambda(\omega,\tau)
for the Jacobi weight
\omega(t)=t^{\mu}(T-t)^{\gamma}
. We must evaluate the integrals

W_{j}^{q}=\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}s^{-\frac{\mu q}{p}}(T-s)^{-\frac{\gamma q}{p}}ds

(32)

for
j=0,\dots,N-1
. Assume, without loss of generality, that
\tau\leq T/2
. We divide the grid intervals into three cases: the left boundary (
j=0
), the right boundary (
j=N-1
), and the interior intervals (
1\leq j\leq N-2
). For the left boundary
j=0
, we have
s\in[0,\tau]
. Thus,
(T-s)\geq T/2
. This allows us to bound the integral as follows

W_{0}^{q}\leq(T/2)^{-\frac{\gamma q}{p}}\int_{0}^{\tau}s^{-\frac{\mu q}{p}}ds=C_{1}\tau^{1-\frac{\mu q}{p}},

(33)

which converges since
\mu<p-1\iff\frac{\mu q}{p}<1
.

For the right boundary
j=N-1
, we have
s\in[T-\tau,T]
, which implies
s\geq T/2
. Similarly, we obtain

W_{N-1}^{q}\leq(T/2)^{-\frac{\mu q}{p}}\int_{T-\tau}^{T}(T-s)^{-\frac{\gamma q}{p}}ds=C_{2}\tau^{1-\frac{\gamma q}{p}},

(34)

which converges since
\gamma<p-1
. For the interior intervals (
1\leq j\leq N-2
), both
s
and
T-s
are bounded away from zero by at least
\tau
, ensuring that the singularity is avoided, and
W_{j}^{q}\leq C_{3}\tau
.

Since we are concerned with the limit as
\tau\to 0
, and the fractional exponents satisfy
1-\frac{\mu q}{p}<1
and
1-\frac{\gamma q}{p}<1
, the boundary terms dominate the interior terms. Taking the
q
-th root, we obtain that for all
j
,

W_{j}\leq C\max\left(\tau^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\mu}{p}},\tau^{\frac{1}{q}-\frac{\gamma}{p}}\right)=C\tau^{\frac{1}{q}}\tau^{-\frac{\max(\mu,\gamma)}{p}}.

(35)

Letting
\nu=\max(\mu,\gamma)
, we calculate the supremum

\Lambda(\omega,\tau)=\sup_{j}\tau^{-1/q}W_{j}\leq C\tau^{-\frac{\nu}{p}}.

(36)

Substituting this bound into Theorem
1
yields the final estimate.
∎

Corollary 3
.

Let the weight be given by
\omega(t)=\left(\ln\frac{eT}{t}\right)^{-\mu}
with
\mu>0
. If
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
, the discretization error is bounded by

|D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})|\leq C\tau^{2-\alpha-1/p}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}},

(37)

where
C
is a constant independent of
\tau
.

Proof.

We must evaluate
\Lambda(\omega,\tau)
for the logarithmic weight
\omega(t)=\left(\ln\frac{eT}{t}\right)^{-\mu}
. To do this, we calculate
W_{j}=\|\omega^{-1/p}\|_{L^{q}(t_{j},t_{j+1})}
, which requires evaluating the integrals

W_{j}^{q}=\int_{t_{j}}^{t_{j+1}}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{s}\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}ds.

(38)

Because the integrand
\left(\ln\frac{eT}{s}\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}
is a strictly decreasing function of
s
in the interval
(0,T]
, the supremum of these integrals over any subinterval of length
\tau
occurs at the first interval
[0,\tau]
. Thus, we have

W_{0}^{q}=\int_{0}^{\tau}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{s}\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}ds.

(39)

To evaluate this, we use the substitution
s=\tau x
, which gives
ds=\tau dx
. The integral becomes

W_{0}^{q}=\tau\int_{0}^{1}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau x}\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}dx=\tau\int_{0}^{1}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}-\ln x\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}dx.

(40)

Let
\beta=\frac{\mu q}{p}>0
. Using the standard algebraic inequality
(A+B)^{\beta}\leq C_{\beta}(A^{\beta}+B^{\beta})
for positive
A
and
B
, we can bound the integral as follows

W_{0}^{q}\leq C_{\beta}\tau\int_{0}^{1}\left[\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\beta}+(-\ln x)^{\beta}\right]dx.

(41)

Note that the integral of the second term is the Gamma function,
\int_{0}^{1}(-\ln x)^{\beta}dx=\Gamma(\beta+1)
, which is a finite constant. Therefore, we obtain

W_{0}^{q}\leq C_{\beta}\tau\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\beta}+C_{\beta}\tau\Gamma(\beta+1).

(42)

For sufficiently small step sizes
\tau
, the term containing the logarithm dominates. Hence, there exists a constant
C>0
such that:

W_{0}^{q}\leq C\tau\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{\mu q}{p}}.

(43)

Taking the
q
-th root yields

W_{0}\leq C^{1/q}\tau^{1/q}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}.

(44)

Finally, we compute the supremum term

\Lambda(\omega,\tau)=\sup_{j}\tau^{-1/q}W_{j}=\tau^{-1/q}W_{0}\leq\tilde{C}\left(\ln\frac{eT}{\tau}\right)^{\frac{\mu}{p}}.

(45)

Substituting this bound for
\Lambda(\omega,\tau)
into the general error estimate in Theorem
1
yields the claim.
∎

3
Application to a Fractional Differential Equation

To demonstrate the versatility of our truncation error bound, we apply the L1 scheme to a standard linear fractional initial value problem

D^{\alpha}y(t)+\lambda y(t)=f(t),\quad t\in(0,T],

(46)

with the initial condition
y(0)=y_{0}
, where
\lambda\geq 0
and
f
is a source term assumed to be continuous. The corresponding numerical scheme is obtained by evaluating (
46
) at
t_{n}
and replacing the Caputo derivative with the L1 approximation
\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}
. That is,

\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}Y_{n}+\lambda Y_{n}=f(t_{n}),\quad n=1,2,\dots,N,

(47)

with
Y_{0}=y_{0}
. Here,
Y_{n}
denotes the numerical approximation of
y(t_{n})
. Set
e_{n}=y(t_{n})-Y_{n}
to represent the global discretization error.

Combining the discrete stability of the L1 scheme with our main result, we obtain the following global convergence bound.

Theorem 2

(Convergence for the Fractional ODE)
.

Suppose that the exact solution to (
46
) satisfies
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
with
\omega\in A_{p}
. Then, there exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
\tau
, such that the global error satisfies

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|e_{n}|\leq C\tau^{2-\alpha-1/p}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}.

(48)

Proof.

Evaluating the exact equation (
46
) at
t_{n}
gives

D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})+\lambda y(t_{n})=f(t_{n}).

(49)

Now, we subtract the numerical scheme (
47
) from (
49
) to obtain

(D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}Y_{n})+\lambda e_{n}=0.

(50)

By adding and subtracting
\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})
, and using the linearity of the operator
\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}
, we further have

\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}e_{n}+\lambda e_{n}=-R_{n}(y),

(51)

where
R_{n}(y)=D^{\alpha}y(t_{n})-\delta_{\tau}^{\alpha}y(t_{n})
is the truncation error analyzed in Theorem
1
. Because
\lambda\geq 0
, it is a known property of the L1 scheme (see, e.g.,
[
4
]
) that the discrete operator is unconditionally stable. Consequently, the global error is governed by the maximum truncation error through the discrete fractional Grönwall inequality, which gives

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|e_{n}|\leq\frac{t_{N}^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|R_{n}(y)|.

(52)

Applying the uniform bound for
R_{n}(y)
obtained in Theorem
1
, and noting that
t_{N}=T
, we deduce

\max_{1\leq n\leq N}|e_{n}|\leq\frac{T^{\alpha}}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}C\tau^{2-\alpha-1/p}\Lambda(\omega,\tau)\|y\|_{W^{2,p}_{\omega}}.

(53)

Setting
C=\frac{T^{\alpha}C}{\Gamma(1+\alpha)}
concludes the proof.
∎

5
Conclusion

In this paper, we have presented a rigorous discretization error analysis for the L1 approximation of the Caputo fractional derivative. By conducting our analysis within the framework of Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
weighted Sobolev spaces,
W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
, we successfully circumvented the unrealistic assumption of full
C^{2}[0,T]
or
W^{2,p}(0,T)
regularity that is frequently used in the classical literature. Our results provide sharp, uniform truncation error bounds that explicitly incorporate the weight function, allowing the numerical analysis to gracefully handle the weak singularities naturally inherent to time-fractional differential equations. Furthermore, we demonstrated the flexibility of this theoretical framework by explicitly deriving convergence rates for various physically relevant singularity profiles—including initial algebraic, terminal algebraic, Jacobi, and logarithmic weights—and by establishing a global convergence bound for a representative fractional initial value problem.

Although the current framework provides robust estimates for solutions whose singular behavior can be controlled to ensure
y\in W^{2,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
, exact solutions to fractional partial differential equations frequently possess intermediate regularity. Future work will focus on extending these weighted error estimates to functions in fractional-order Sobolev spaces
W^{s,p}_{\omega}(0,T)
with
1<s<2
.
