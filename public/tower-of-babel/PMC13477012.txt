Organelle-centered resistosome signaling in plant immunity
Authors: Qi Wang, Yunsheng Yang, Libo Zhang, Ming Chang
Published: 2026

The hidden contribution of intracellular calcium stores

Calcium (Ca2+) signaling is one of the earliest and most conserved hallmarks of plant immune activation (Wang et al., 2024). During pattern-triggered immunity, pathogen perception induces rapid and transient increases in cytosolic Ca2+, whereas effector-triggered immunity (ETI) is typically associated with stronger and more sustained Ca2+ signatures that are tightly linked to hypersensitive cell death (Zhang et al., 2024). The recent discovery that activated nucleotide-binding leucine-rich repeat (NLR) resistosomes function as Ca2+-permeable channels at the plasma membrane (PM) has established a prevailing model in which extracellular Ca2+ influx serves as a central driver of immune activation (Wang et al., 2019; Bi et al., 2021).

However, this PM-centered framework leaves an important question unresolved: how are the unusually sustained and spatially complex Ca2+ signatures associated with ETI generated and maintained? Plant cells contain extensive intracellular Ca2+ stores, including the vacuole, the endoplasmic reticulum (ER), chloroplasts, and mitochondria, all of which maintain Ca2+ concentrations substantially higher than the Ca2+ concentration in the cytosol (Wang et al., 2024). Previous studies have suggested that these organelles actively shape immune-associated Ca2+ dynamics and downstream defense outputs (Wang et al., 2024). Yet, despite long-standing speculation about the contribution of intracellular Ca2+ stores to plant immunity, direct mechanistic links between organellar Ca2+ mobilization and NLR activation have remained largely elusive.

A recent study now provides compelling evidence that helper NLR resistosomes can directly mobilize intracellular Ca2+ pools (Ibrahim et al., 2026). These findings establish a direct mechanistic link between organellar Ca2+ stores and resistosome signaling during ETI. Structural diversification enables membrane specialization of helper NLRs

A central conceptual advance of this study is the demonstration that structural diversification of helper NLRs underlies their membrane specialization and compartment-specific immune functions (Figure 1; Ibrahim et al., 2026). In the prevailing sensor–helper model of ETI, upstream sensor NLRs perceive pathogen effectors, whereas downstream helper NLRs execute immune signaling through resistosome formation (He et al., 2025). Previous studies have shown that canonical coiled-coil (CC)-type NLR (CC-NLR) helpers such as NLR-REQUIRED FOR CELL DEATH (NRC) proteins and the RPW8-like coiled-coil (CCR)-type NLR (CCR-NLR) helper ACTIVATED DISEASE RESISTANCE 1 (ADR1) form PM-associated resistosomes that function as Ca2+-permeable channels. By contrast, NRG1 has now been shown to employ a distinct organelle-centered strategy (Jacob et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024; Ibrahim et al., 2026).

Using AlphaFold 3-based structural modeling, the authors found that CCR-NLR resistosomes contain an unusually extended N-terminal funnel architecture compared with that of canonical CC-NLRs such as NRC4 (Ibrahim et al., 2026). In particular, the elongated α4 helix of NbNRG1 was identified as a key determinant of organellar membrane targeting, especially its targeting of the chloroplast envelope. Whereas single-point mutants within this extended helix showed partial relocalization to the PM, the K126E/L129E double mutant localized predominantly to the PM and exhibited strongly reduced immune activity, highlighting the importance of correct membrane targeting for helper NLR function (Ibrahim et al., 2026).

Importantly, this membrane specialization is supported not only by structural modeling but also by functional relocalization experiments. Nanobody-mediated trapping of the canonical helper CC-NLR NRC4 at the chloroplast envelope abolished the hypersensitive response and failed to deplete stromal Ca2+, whereas chloroplast-trapped NRG1 retained the ability to trigger both the hypersensitive response and chloroplast Ca2+ efflux (Ibrahim et al., 2026). These findings demonstrate that resistosomes are functionally adapted to specific membrane environments rather than functioning as universally interchangeable channels across different membranes.

Remarkably, chloroplast targeting is conserved among NRG1-like CCR-NLRs spanning approximately 360 million years of land plant evolution, including an NRG1-like CCR-NLR from a monilophyte lineage that predates the divergence of the NRG1 and ADR1 subfamilies (Ibrahim et al., 2026). Although these localization analyses relied on heterologous expression in N. benthamiana, the conserved targeting pattern suggests that organelle-centered immune signaling is an ancient feature of helper NLR evolution rather than a species-specific phenomenon (Ibrahim et al., 2026).

These findings raise the intriguing possibility that helper NLRs have evolved structurally specialized membrane-targeting modules that enable immune signaling across distinct cellular compartments. Rather than functioning solely as generic PM-localized immune pores, different classes of resistosomes may localize to specific membrane systems to mobilize Ca2+ from distinct extracellular or intracellular stores. Such membrane specialization could provide an elegant mechanism for diversifying immune outputs and expanding the spatial organization of ETI signaling within plant cells (Figure 1).

Compartmentalized resistosome signaling expands immune execution strategies

The discovery of NbNRG1-mediated chloroplast Ca2+ signaling expands the current view of resistosomes from PM-associated immune pores to compartment-specialized signaling modules (Ibrahim et al., 2026). Previous studies established that canonical CC-NLR resistosomes, including the singleton receptor HOPZ-ACTIVATED RESISTANCE 1 and helper NRC proteins, assemble at the PM to mediate extracellular Ca2+ influx during immune activation (Bi et al., 2021; Liu et al., 2024). By contrast, activated NbNRG1 associates with the chloroplast envelope and promotes the release of Ca2+ from chloroplast stores into the cytosol (Ibrahim et al., 2026). Activated NbNRG1 also forms punctate structures associated with multiple intracellular membrane systems, including the chloroplast envelope, the ER, and mitochondria, although chloroplast-associated Ca2+ signaling is currently the best-characterized example (Ibrahim et al., 2026). Together, these findings support a multicompartment model of resistosome signaling, in which distinct NLR resistosomes mobilize Ca2+ from different stores to shape immune-associated Ca2+ signatures in plant cells (Figure 1).

Such compartmentalized immune organization may provide several important advantages to plant defense. First, the use of multiple Ca2+ sources could enhance the robustness and persistence of ETI-associated Ca2+ signaling, particularly when pathogen effectors suppress PM Ca2+ influx pathways by targeting immune-associated Ca2+-permeable channels such as cyclic nucleotide-gated channels (Lee et al., 2024). Sustained elevations of cytosolic Ca2+ are a hallmark of ETI (Wang et al., 2024), yet such long-lasting Ca2+ signatures may not rely exclusively on extracellular Ca2+ influx. Instead, intracellular Ca2+ stores within the vacuole, the ER, chloroplasts, and mitochondria may collectively contribute to the amplitude and duration of immune Ca2+ signaling (Wang et al., 2024).

Second, organelle-localized resistosomes may directly couple Ca2+ release to organelle-specific immune functions. Chloroplasts are central hubs for salicylic acid biosynthesis, reactive oxygen species (ROS) production, stromule formation, and retrograde signaling during plant immunity (Rui et al., 2025). Consequently, chloroplast-derived Ca2+ signals may not only amplify cytosolic immune signaling but also coordinate local immune responses within chloroplasts. This spatial coupling between organelle-derived Ca2+ signals and organellar functions adds another layer of regulation to immune signaling.

Finally, compartmentalized resistosome signaling may increase the evolutionary flexibility of plant immune systems. Pathogen effectors frequently target PM-associated immune receptors and ion channels to suppress defense signaling (Lee et al., 2024). The emergence of organelle-associated resistosomes may therefore provide alternative immune signaling routes that are potentially less vulnerable to pathogen interference. Such diversification of immune execution strategies could enhance the resilience of plant innate immunity during host–pathogen coevolution.

Outstanding questions in compartmentalized Ca2+ immunity

The discovery that helper NLR resistosomes can mobilize Ca2+ from intracellular stores opens an important new avenue for understanding the spatial organization of plant immune signaling. Nevertheless, several fundamental questions remain unresolved.

First, how does NbNRG1 mediate chloroplast Ca2+ efflux across the double-membrane chloroplast envelope? Current evidence demonstrates that activated NbNRG1 associates with the chloroplast envelope and promotes stromal Ca2+ release into the cytosol (Ibrahim et al., 2026). However, it remains unclear whether NbNRG1 forms a functional trans-envelope pore spanning both chloroplast membranes or acts in concert with additional host components. Resolving the structural basis of chloroplast Ca2+ export will be essential for understanding how organellar resistosomes mediate ion transport.

Second, how are chloroplast-derived Ca2+ signals integrated with other chloroplast immune outputs? Chloroplasts not only serve as intracellular Ca2+ stores but also function as central hubs for ROS production, salicylic acid biosynthesis, stromule formation, and retrograde signaling during immunity (Rui et al., 2025). Whether NbNRG1-triggered Ca2+ efflux directly coordinates these pathways remains unknown. Of particular interest is how chloroplast Ca2+ signals interact with ROS signaling and how organelle-derived Ca2+ signals are transmitted to the nucleus to drive transcriptional reprogramming during ETI.

Third, is organellar specialization a general property of helper NLRs? In addition to NRG1 proteins, the ADR1 family constitutes the second major branch of CCR-NLR helper proteins (Kourelis and Adachi, 2022). Whether ADR1 resistosomes also localize to specific intracellular membranes and mobilize Ca2+ from distinct organellar stores remains unknown. Notably, a recent study showed that ADR1-L1 can assemble with the ENHANCED DISEASE SUSCEPTIBILITY 1–PHYTOALEXIN DEFICIENT 4 complex into highly organized, ring-like resistosome clusters at the PM, forming a cell death-associated signaling hub (Ge et al., 2026). Whether organelle-associated NRG1 puncta similarly form higher-order supramolecular immune assemblies remains unexplored.

Fourth, have pathogens evolved effectors that specifically target organellar resistosome signaling? Numerous pathogen effectors suppress PM-associated immune receptors and Ca2+-permeable channels (Lee et al., 2024). The discovery of organelle-associated resistosomes raises the possibility that chloroplasts and other organelles may also serve as important battlegrounds during host–pathogen coevolution. Identifying effectors that interfere with organellar resistosome assembly, membrane targeting, or channel activity could reveal new mechanisms of immune suppression.

Finally, how do intracellular Ca2+ sources collectively shape ETI-associated Ca2+ signatures? Sustained cytosolic Ca2+ elevation during ETI may not arise from a single source (Wang et al., 2024). Chloroplasts, mitochondria, the vacuole, the ER, and extracellular Ca2+ pools may each contribute distinct temporal and spatial components to immune Ca2+ signaling. Understanding how these Ca2+ sources are spatially and temporally coordinated will be essential for establishing a systems-level framework for compartmentalized Ca2+ signaling in plant immunity.
