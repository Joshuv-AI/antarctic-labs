C1ORF112 drives lung adenocarcinoma progression through e2f8-mediated transcriptional activation
Authors: Jingsi Wang, Guixue Yang, Xufeng Deng, Xiaobing Liu, Dan Chen, Quanxing Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundLung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) remains a leading cause of cancer mortality, with poor prognosis driven by metastasis and therapeutic resistance. Chromosome 1 open reading frame 112 (C1ORF112) encodes a nuclear protein involved in DNA replication and repair, but its role in LUAD is unclear. To systematically investigate the clinical relevance, functional impact, and transcriptional regulation of C1ORF112 in LUAD through integrative bioinformatic and experimental approaches.MethodsC1ORF112 expression and prognostic significance were analyzed using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. Single-cell RNA sequencing defined cellular localization and communication patterns. Functional enrichment, protein-protein interaction (PPI), and transcription factor prediction analyses were performed. In vitro assays and xenograft models assessed biological functions and transcriptional regulation by E2F8.ResultsC1ORF112 was significantly upregulated in LUAD and correlated with poor overall survival (OS). Single-cell analysis showed elevated expression in malignant cells, fibroblasts, endothelial cells, and mast cells, accompanied by increased communication with M2 macrophages and exhausted CD8+ T cells. Overexpression of C1ORF112 inhibited apoptosis and promoted proliferation in LUAD cells, while xenografts exhibited accelerated tumor growth and increased Ki67 expression. Mechanistically, E2F8 directly bound the C1ORF112 promoter, enhancing its transcription and inhibiting downstream p53 signaling.ConclusionsOur integrative analysis suggests that C1ORF112 exhibits oncogenic properties in LUAD by promoting tumor survival, proliferation, and potentially remodeling the tumor microenvironment. The transcriptional activation by E2F8 highlights the E2F8-C1ORF112 axis as a candidate prognostic biomarker and therapeutic vulnerability, though prospective clinical validation and drug sensitivity studies are warranted.Keywords: Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), chromosome 1 open reading frame 112 (C1ORF112), E2F8, biomarker, transcriptional regulation

Highlight box

Key findings

• E2F8-driven chromosome 1 open reading frame 112 (C1ORF112) upregulation suppresses p53, promoting proliferation and apoptosis resistance in lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD), while single-cell data reveal enhanced crosstalk with M2 macrophages and exhausted CD8+ T cells.

What is known and what is new?

• C1ORF112 (FIRRM) is a DNA repair protein overexpressed in gastric/colorectal cancers, but its expression, regulation, and functional role in LUAD remain completely uncharacterized.

• First integrative multi-omics (TCGA/GEO) and experimental validation (in vitro/vivo) identify C1ORF112 as a LUAD oncogene, E2F8 as its direct transcriptional activator, and map its immune microenvironment remodeling.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The E2F8-C1ORF112 axis represents a novel prognostic biomarker and therapeutic vulnerability, warranting prospective clinical validation and high-throughput screening for small-molecule inhibitors.

Introduction

Lung adenocarcinoma (LUAD) is the most prevalent histological subtype of non-small cell lung cancer, accounting for nearly 40% of all lung cancer diagnoses worldwide (1,2). Despite advances in early detection and targeted therapies, the 5-year overall survival (OS) rate for LUAD remains below 20% due to high metastatic potential and therapeutic resistance (3,4). The global incidence of LUAD continues to rise, particularly among non-smokers and younger populations, underscoring an urgent need to better understand its molecular underpinnings and to identify novel biomarkers and therapeutic targets that can improve clinical outcomes (5,6).

Chromosome 1 open reading frame 112 (C1ORF112), also recently designated as FIRRM (FancJ-interacting residue repositioning mutant), encodes a highly conserved nuclear protein essential for maintaining genomic integrity (7). Under physiological conditions, C1ORF112 forms a constitutive complex with FANCJ and C12orf48 to facilitate the resolution of DNA interstrand crosslinks (ICLs) (7,8). Moreover, C1ORF112 has been identified as a synthetic lethal partner with the DNA translocase PICH, emphasizing its indispensable role in managing DNA under-replication and ensuring proper chromosome segregation during mitosis (9). Recent studies have also demonstrated that C1ORF112 promotes DNA end resection, a pivotal step in homologous recombination, by recruiting the exonuclease EXO1 to DNA damage sites (7,9). While limited evidence suggests its involvement in specific malignancies, its precise role in LUAD and its potential as a therapeutic target remain largely unexplored (7,8). Recent studies have demonstrated that C1ORF112 interacts with key cell-cycle regulators and participates in homologous recombination through associations with EXO1, RAD54L, and other DNA repair factors (7,9). Dysregulated C1ORF112 expression has been observed in multiple malignancies, including gastric and colorectal cancer, where its overexpression correlates with enhanced proliferation, genomic instability, and poor clinical outcomes (10-12). Despite these insights, the specific contribution of C1ORF112 to LUAD pathogenesis has not been characterized. The regulatory networks controlling C1ORF112 transcription, its impact on tumor microenvironment interactions, and its potential as a predictive biomarker or therapeutic vulnerability remain unexplored. Addressing these gaps will be essential to understanding whether C1ORF112 can serve as a novel driver of genomic instability and treatment resistance in LUAD.

In this study, we comprehensively evaluated C1ORF112 expression and prognostic significance in LUAD using The Cancer Genome Atlas (TCGA) and multiple Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) datasets. Integrating bulk and single-cell transcriptomic analyses, we investigated the oncogenic pathways and cell-cell communication networks associated with C1ORF112. Furthermore, we elucidated upstream transcriptional regulation by E2F8 and assessed the biological impact of C1ORF112 overexpression on apoptosis, proliferation, and tumor growth in vitro and in vivo. Our findings reveal C1ORF112 as a novel oncogenic driver and potential therapeutic target in LUAD. We present this article in accordance with the MDAR and ARRIVE reporting checklists (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-1-0085/rc).

Methods

Data sources

Transcriptomic profiles from RNA sequencing, along with associated clinical annotations for LUAD tumors and matched normal lung tissues, were sourced from the University of California, Santa Cruz (UCSC) Xena repository (https://xena.ucsc.edu/, last accessed March 2025), which aggregates data from TCGA. Complementary microarray expression profiles were downloaded from the GEO database. Specifically, GSE19188 (encompassing 91 LUAD and 65 normal specimens) and GSE40791 (including 94 LUAD and 100 normal specimens) served as independent validation sets for evaluating C1ORF112 transcript levels. Additionally, GSE72094 (n=398) and GSE13213 (n=117) datasets were utilized to investigate the prognostic implications of C1ORF112 expression in LUAD cohorts. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments.

Prognostic analysis

The optimal threshold for stratifying C1ORF112 expression in survival assessments was identified via the “surv_cutpoint” algorithm within the survminer R package, with the “minprop” threshold configured to 0.3. Kaplan-Meier estimator plots were constructed to delineate OS disparities between cohorts exhibiting elevated versus diminished C1ORF112 expression, with intergroup variances assessed through the log-rank test. Statistical significance was ascribed to two-tailed P values below 0.05.

Functional enrichment analysis

Enrichment profiling for Gene Ontology (GO) terms, Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways, and gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was conducted employing the clusterProfiler package in R. Differentially expressed genes were prioritized by their log2 fold-change magnitudes derived from TCGA-LUAD datasets, with reference gene sets curated from the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB). Enriched pathways were deemed noteworthy if they demonstrated adjusted P values under 0.05 and a false discovery rate (FDR) below 0.25, as adjusted via the Benjamini-Hochberg procedure.

Interactome construction and transcription factor prediction interactome

The protein-protein interaction (PPI) landscape encompassing C1ORF112 was assembled using the STRING online resource, while associations between C1ORF112 and its interactors were quantified through queries in the Tumor Immune Estimation Resource (TIMER) database. CHIP-Atlas and Gene Transcription Regulation Database (GTRD) databases were employed to identify potential transcription factors (TFs) binding to the C1ORF112 locus. Genes with |R2| >0.7 and P<0.05 in TCGA-LUAD were defined as candidate upstream TFs.

Single-cell transcriptomics and intercellular signaling assessment

Single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) profiles were procured from the GSE127465 accession in GEO. Cells with <200 or >5,000 detected genes and those with high mitochondrial or erythrocyte gene expression were excluded. Post-normalization, the 2,000 most variably expressed genes were selected for principal component analysis (PCA), with subsequent t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding (t-SNE) projection leveraging the leading 20 principal components. All analyses were executed within the Seurat framework (version 4.3.0), encompassing data normalization, variable gene identification, scaling, PCA, Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP)/t-SNE dimensionality reduction, and clustering. Cell types were annotated according to the Tumor Immune Single-cell Hub (TISCH) database.

Intercellular communication networks were inferred and analyzed using CellChat, which estimates ligand-receptor interactions and signaling pathway activities. Significant pathways and interaction pairs were visualized.

Cell culture and plasmid transfection

A549 cells were propagated in Dulbecco’s Modified Eagle Medium (DMEM) containing high glucose levels and augmented with 10% fetal bovine serum, maintained at 37 ℃ under 5% CO2 in a humidified incubator. At 70–90% confluency, cells underwent transient transfection with plasmid DNA facilitated by Lipofectamine 2000 reagent. DNA and Lipofectamine components were independently suspended in serum-free DMEM, allowed to equilibrate for 5 minutes, combined, and complexed for 20 minutes at ambient temperature. The resulting transfection mixtures were applied to cells for 5 hours, after which the medium was refreshed, and cultures were incubated for a further 48 hours.

Western blotting

Cellular extracts were prepared by lysis in EBC buffer fortified with Phenylmethanesulfonyl fluoride (PMSF) and a cocktail of protease inhibitors. Samples were denatured by heating to 100 ℃ for 10 minutes. Equivalent protein quantities were separated via Sodium dodecyl sulfate-polyacrylamide gel electrophoresis (SDS-PAGE) and electrotransferred onto Polyvinylidene fluoride (PVDF) membranes. Following blockade of nonspecific binding sites, membranes were probed with primary antibodies, then with horseradish peroxidase (HRP)-linked secondary antibodies, and immunoreactive bands were detected through enhanced chemiluminescence.

Flow cytometry

Forty-eight hours post-transfection, A549 cells were harvested, fixed in 70% ethanol at 4 ℃ overnight, rinsed, and incubated with Propidium iodide (PI)/RNase A staining solution for 30 minutes at room temperature in the absence of light. DNA content-based cell cycle phase distributions were quantified using flow cytometry.

Dual-luciferase reporter assay

Transfected cells were lysed in 300 µL of passive lysis buffer, and 20 µL lysate was used to sequentially measure firefly and Renilla luciferase activities using respective substrates. Bioluminescent signals were captured across a 350–700 nm spectral range.

Vivo tumorigenesis model

Six-week-old male BALB/c athymic nude mice received subcutaneous inoculations of 2×106 A549 cells engineered for stable C1ORF112 overexpression or vector-only controls, suspended in 100 µL of sterile saline. Tumor dimensions (length and width) were serially monitored. Volumes were computed using the formula: volume = length × (width)2/2. Experiments were performed under a project license (No. AMUWEC20255472) granted by the Laboratory Animal Welfare and Ethics Committee of the Third Military Medical University, in compliance with the institutional guidelines for the care and use of animals.

Tissue immunohistochemical staining

Xenograft tumors were immersion-fixed in 4% paraformaldehyde, embedded in paraffin, and sectioned at 4 µm thickness. Slides underwent dewaxing, hydration, quenching with 3% hydrogen peroxide, and blockade with 5% bovine serum albumin. Primary antibodies were applied overnight at 4 ℃, followed by HRP-conjugated secondary reagents and 3,3’-diaminobenzidine (DAB) chromogenic development. Nuclei were counterstained with hematoxylin. The extent of positive immunoreactivity was systematically quantified.

Statistical analysis

Computational bioinformatic evaluations were executed utilizing R software (version 4.2.2). Quantitative experimental outcomes were processed with GraphPad Prism (version 8.0.2). Intergroup comparisons involving two independent samples were performed via the Student’s t-test or one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), as appropriate. Differential expression profiles between neoplastic and non-neoplastic tissues were examined employing the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Associative relationships were quantified through Pearson correlation coefficients. Disparities in Kaplan-Meier OS trajectories were appraised using the log-rank test. A bilateral P value threshold of less than 0.05 was adopted to denote statistical significance.

Results

C1ORF112 is upregulated and associated with poor prognosis in LUAD

C1ORF112 expression was significantly elevated in LUAD tissues compared to normal tissues in the TCGA-LUAD dataset, which was validated in GSE19188 and GSE40791 (Figure 1A). High C1ORF112 expression was associated with shorter OS in TCGA and two GEO cohorts (Figure 1B-1D).

GSEA revealed that C1ORF112 was enriched in E2F_TARGETS, G2M_CHECKPOINT, MYC_TARGETS_V2, MITOTIC_SPINDLE, and P53_PATHWAY gene sets (Figure 1E). GO analysis indicated enrichment in DNA replication-related processes (e.g., DNA replication initiation, nuclear DNA replication) (Figure 1F). KEGG analysis further implicated pathways such as cell cycle, cytokine-cytokine receptor interaction, hematopoietic cell lineage, p53 signaling, and ECM-receptor interaction (Figure 1G). The PPI network showed interactions between C1ORF112 and ten proteins, including EXO1, RACGAP1, KIF14, KIF15, PARPBP, NCAPG, NCAPH, CENPF, RAD54L, and FIGNL1, with strong expression correlations observed (Figure 1H-1J).

Cellular localization and communication patterns of C1ORF112

To investigate the expression pattern of C1ORF112 at the cellular level in LUAD, we analyzed scRNA-seq data (GSE127465) and identified 13 major cell populations, including B cells, T cells, dendritic cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, macrophages, malignant cells, mast cells, monocytes, natural killer (NK) cells, and plasma cells (Figure 2A). Compared with normal tissues, C1ORF112 expression was reduced in effector immune cells but elevated in malignant cells, mast cells, endothelial cells, and fibroblasts (Figure 2B).

(D,E) Interaction number and strength when malignant cells act as signal senders (D) or receivers (E). (F) Major sender and receiver cell types among malignant cells with high or low C1ORF112 expression. CellChat analysis revealed that C1ORF112-high malignant cells exhibited enhanced intercellular communication, particularly as signal senders toward M2 macrophages, CD8+ exhausted T cells, and monocytes, and as receivers from fibroblasts (Figure 2C-2F). Furthermore, C1ORF112 high-expressing malignant cells were associated with multiple signaling pathways, including EGF, TENASCIN, growth differentiation factor (GDF), DESMOSOME, heparan sulfate proteoglycan (HSPG), CD96, UGRP1, SEMA3, EPHB, and AGRN (Figure 2G). To address the relationship between C1ORF112-mediated microenvironmental changes and the E2F8-p53 signaling axis, we further deepened our single-cell analysis by stratifying malignant cells into C1ORF112-positive (C1ORF112+) and C1ORF112-negative (C1ORF112−) subpopulations. Intercellular communication analysis revealed that the enhanced interactions with M2 macrophages, fibroblasts, and exhausted CD8+ T cells were strictly dependent on C1ORF112 expression and were mediated by specific differential ligand-receptor pairs (Figure S1A-S1C). Furthermore, KEGG pathway enrichment of upregulated genes in C1ORF112+ malignant cells confirmed the activation of key oncogenic signaling pathways (Figure S1D). Importantly, transcription factor activity analysis using DoRothEA demonstrated that E2F8 activity was significantly elevated in C1ORF112+ malignant cells compared to their C1ORF112- counterparts (Figure S1E).

Overexpression of C1ORF112 inhibits apoptosis in LUAD cells

To investigate the biological function of C1ORF112 in LUAD, we overexpressed C1ORF112 in A549 cells. Successful transfection was verified through Western blotting (Figure 3A). Subsequent flow cytometry assays revealed that C1ORF112 overexpression markedly reduced the apoptotic rate in A549 cells relative to those transfected with empty vector (Figure 3B; P<0.05).

C1ORF112 overexpression suppresses apoptosis in LUAD cells. (A) Western blot verification of successful overexpression of C1ORF112 in A549 cells. (B) Flow cytometry analysis of apoptosis in A549 cells. *, P<0.05; ***, P<0.001. LUAD, lung adenocarcinoma.

C1ORF112 is transcriptionally regulated by E2F8 and promotes LUAD cell proliferation

Using CHIP-Atlas and GTRD databases, combined with correlation analysis in TCGA-LUAD, we identified three potential upstream TFs of C1ORF112: E2F8, EZH2, and LIN9 (Figure 4A).

(C) Western blot verification of successful E2F8 overexpression in A549 cells. (D) Dual-luciferase reporter assay confirming direct binding between E2F8 and the C1ORF112 promoter. (E) C1ORF112 expression levels in A549 cells overexpressing E2F8. (F) P53 expression levels in A549 cells overexpressing C1ORF112. ns, not significant; **, P<0.01; ***, P<0.001; ****, P<0.0001. LUAD, lung adenocarcinoma; TFs, transcription factors.

Because GSEA revealed that C1ORF112 is enriched in E2F target gene sets (Figure 1E), we focused on E2F8, whose function remains poorly understood. Correlation analysis showed a significant positive correlation between E2F8 and C1ORF112 expression (P<0.0001; Figure 4B). Overexpression of E2F8 in A549 cells was successfully verified by Western blot (P=0.003; Figure 4C). To elucidate the precise binding mechanism of E2F8 on the C1ORF112 promoter, we generated a series of truncated C1ORF112 promoter reporter constructs (spanning −200 to 2,000 bp) and performed detailed dual-luciferase assays. While the full-length promoter (0–2,000 bp) exhibited the highest transcriptional activity, the truncated fragment spanning 500–1,000 bp retained the most prominent luciferase activity among the segments. A secondary peak of activity was observed in the 1,500–2,000 bp region. Conversely, deletions of these specific regions substantially abolished the reporter activity. These refined experimental verifications indicate that E2F8 does not bind uniformly across the promoter but specifically targets distinct core response elements located predominantly within the 500–1,000 and 1,500–2,000 bp regions to transcriptionally activate C1ORF112 (P<0.001; Figure 4D and Figure S2). Consistently, C1ORF112 expression was upregulated in cells overexpressing E2F8 (P<0.0001; Figure 4E).

Furthermore, enrichment analysis indicated that C1ORF112 is involved in cell cycle and p53 signaling pathways (Figure 1E-1G). To further explore downstream regulation, we examined p53 expression in A549 cells overexpressing E2F8. The results showed that the p53 signaling pathway was significantly suppressed in E2F8-overexpressing cells (P<0.001; Figure 4F).

C1ORF112 enhances tumorigenesis of LUAD in vivo

We further evaluated the oncogenic effect of C1ORF112 in vivo. A549 cells stably expressing C1ORF112 or harboring control vector were implanted subcutaneously into BALB/c nude mice, with tumor progression tracked over a 4-week period.

Xenografts from C1ORF112-overexpressing A549 cells exhibited substantially greater tumor volumes and masses than those from control cells (Figure 5A,5B). Immunohistochemical analysis further showed increased Ki67 expression in C1ORF112-overexpressing tumors, indicating enhanced proliferative activity (Figure 5C; P<0.05).

Discussion

Our study demonstrates that C1ORF112 is markedly upregulated in LUAD tissues and correlates with poor OS. The scRNA-seq reveals cell-type-specific expression and altered cell-cell communication in C1ORF112-high malignant cells. Functional assays show that C1ORF112 overexpression suppresses apoptosis and promotes proliferation in A549 cells, while E2F8 directly regulates C1ORF112 expression and inhibits downstream p53 signaling. In vivo, C1ORF112 enhances tumor growth and Ki67 expression.

The bulk transcriptomic analyses confirm that C1ORF112 mRNA levels are significantly elevated in TCGA-LUAD and two independent GEO cohorts, with high expression predicting shorter OS in LUAD patients. Pan-cancer analysis further establishes C1ORF112 overexpression as an unfavorable prognostic marker in LUAD and other malignancies, including lower-grade glioma and gastric cancer, underscoring its broad oncogenic relevance (10,12).

The scRNA-seq of LUAD tissues uncovers a complex microenvironment comprising 13 distinct cell populations. C1ORF112 expression is notably elevated in malignant epithelial cells, endothelial cells, fibroblasts, and mast cells, whereas effector immune populations—including cytotoxic CD8+ T cells and NK cells—exhibit reduced expression. This differential distribution suggests that C1ORF112 supports tumor cell autonomy and vascular remodeling while dampening anti-tumor immunity (13-15). CellChat analysis reveals that C1ORF112-high malignant cells intensify outbound signaling to M2-polarized macrophages and exhausted CD8+ T cells. These interactions reinforce an immunosuppressive niche by promoting macrophage M2 polarization and T cell dysfunction, consistent with reports that tumor-derived transforming growth factor beta (TGF-β) and programmed death-ligand 1 (PD-L1) signals facilitate immune escape in LUAD (16-18). Concurrently, C1ORF112-high cells receive enhanced paracrine signals from cancer-associated fibroblasts through CXCL12-CXCR4 and FGF2-FGFR1 axes, indicating a reciprocal crosstalk that bolsters tumor growth and stromal support. Collectively, these findings position C1ORF112 as a central modulator of cell-cell communication in the LUAD microenvironment, orchestrating pro-tumorigenic signaling loops that enhance immune evasion, stromal support, and angiogenesis. Targeting C1ORF112-mediated communication pathways may therefore disrupt these malignant circuits and restore anti-tumor immunity.

Functionally, C1ORF112 overexpression suppressed apoptosis and promoted proliferation of LUAD cells in vitro, as well as accelerated tumor growth in vivo.

Overexpression of C1ORF112 in vivo and vitro significantly reduces apoptotic rates and enhances proliferation, demonstrating its direct role in tumor cell survival. Mechanistically, C1ORF112 interacts with replication protein complexes—such as Minichromosome maintenance (MCM) helicase and DNA polymerase δ—to stabilize replication forks and accelerate S-phase entry, thereby bypassing DNA damage checkpoints (19,20). Our PPI analysis further identifies C1ORF112 as a hub protein interacting with key repair factors, including EXO1, RAD54L, and PARPBP. This is highly consistent with the physiological function of C1ORF112 in promoting homologous recombination and stabilizing the replisome (7,9). In LUAD, the marked upregulation of C1ORF112 likely represents a pathological adaptation, where malignant cells hijack these endogenous repair mechanisms to sustain rapid proliferation despite oncogene-induced replication stress (8,9). Elevated C1ORF112 levels suppress intrinsic apoptotic signaling by stabilizing anti-apoptotic BCL-2 family members and diminishing caspase-3 activation (21,22), consistent with reduced Annexin V/PI staining and prior evidence that C1ORF112 knockdown induces cell cycle arrest and compromises cancer cell viability. The anti-apoptotic function of C1ORF112 underscores its potential as a therapeutic target.

We further identified E2F8 as a key transcriptional activator of C1ORF112. E2F8 is an atypical member of the E2F family that regulates cell cycle genes by forming homo- or heteromeric complexes, regulating S-phase and G2/M checkpoint genes—including CCNE1, CDK2, and PLK1—while repressing CDK inhibitors p21 Cip1 and p27 Kip1 (23,24). In our study, dual-luciferase assays confirm that E2F8 directly binds the C1ORF112 promoter as well as significantly increasing its transcription. Moreover, the E2F8-C1ORF112 axis directly suppresses downstream p53 signaling. By dampening p53 signaling, this axis inhibits intrinsic apoptosis and accelerates proliferation. Furthermore, the attenuation of the p53 pathway likely alters the tumor secretome, facilitating the recruitment of M2 macrophages and exhaustion of CD8+ T cells, thereby linking intracellular oncogenic signaling to the immunosuppressive microenvironment. This intricate interplay between C1ORF112 and E2F8 underlies a synergistic mechanism driving LUAD progression through coordinated replication support, apoptosis inhibition, and nuanced p53 pathway modulation.

In recent years, significant advances in the molecular characterization of LUAD have led to the identification of actionable genomic alterations—most notably mutations in EGFR, ALK rearrangements, ROS1 fusions, and MET exon 14 skipping—which have transformed the therapeutic landscape through targeted inhibitors and markedly improved patient outcomes (25,26). Concurrently, the advent of immune checkpoint inhibitors targeting PD-1/PD-L1 has further extended survival in subsets of patients, although durable responses are observed in only a fraction of cases and primary or acquired resistance remains a formidable challenge (27-29). Large-scale transcriptomic and proteomic profiling efforts have uncovered additional oncogenic drivers and tumor microenvironment signatures, highlighting the heterogeneity of tumor-immune interactions and the critical role of stromal components in modulating therapy response (30-32). Despite these strides, OS for advanced-stage disease remains suboptimal, underscoring the necessity to elucidate novel biomarkers and regulatory networks—such as the E2F8-C1ORF112 axis described herein—that may underlie therapeutic resistance and provide new avenues for precision intervention.

The study has several limitations. First, its retrospective design and exclusive use of TCGA and GEO public datasets may introduce sampling bias, heterogeneity in data processing, and incomplete clinical annotation, which could limit generalizability. Second, the nude mouse xenograft model lacks adaptive immunity, preventing evaluation of C1ORF112’s impact on T cell or NK cell-mediated responses in an immunocompetent setting. Finally, the mechanistic link between C1ORF112 and immune or stromal cell modulation remains correlative; deeper exploration of downstream effectors and in vivo immune profiling is required.

Building upon our current findings, several avenues for future research are warranted. First, considering the predicted interaction between C1ORF112-high malignant cells and immunosuppressive populations such as M2 macrophages and exhausted CD8+ T cells, subsequent studies should employ immunocompetent syngeneic mouse models and spatial transcriptomics to causally validate these microenvironmental dynamics in vivo (33). Second, given the essential role of the E2F8-C1ORF112 axis in stabilizing replication forks and facilitating DNA repair, targeting C1ORF112 may induce synthetic lethality when combined with poly (ADP-ribose) polymerase (PARP) inhibitors or other DNA-damaging therapies, representing a promising translational direction (34). Finally, well-designed prospective clinical cohorts are required to definitively validate the prognostic utility of C1ORF112 and to explore its potential as a novel predictive biomarker for the efficacy of immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapies in LUAD (35).

Conclusions

In summary, our preclinical and bioinformatic findings suggest that C1ORF112 exhibits oncogenic potential in LUAD by supporting tumor cell survival, proliferation, and pro-tumorigenic microenvironmental interactions. Transcriptional activation by E2F8 and suppression of the p53 signaling cascade underline its multifaceted involvement in LUAD pathogenesis. While our study highlights the E2F8-C1ORF112 axis as a promising candidate for targeted intervention, these conclusions are primarily based on public datasets and in vitro/in vivo models. Future studies incorporating large-scale patient-derived clinical cohorts and specific drug sensitivity assays are essential to definitively establish its clinical utility as a prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target.
