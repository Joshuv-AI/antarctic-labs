Counterexamples to the Lorentzian Calderón problem
Lauri Oksanen, Miika Sarkkinen

Abstract
. We show that two non-isometric, smooth, globally hyperbolic Lorentzian metrics can have the same hyperbolic Dirichlet-to-Neumann map on an infinite cylinder with timelike boundary.



1.
Introduction

Positive results on the Lorentzian Calderón problem have been obtained recently
[
SY18
,
AFO22
,
AFO25
,
ORS24
,
YZ25
,
Yi25
,
GO25
]
. These results impose rather stringent geometric conditions. Partly motivating such conditions, we show that having data on an infinite cylinder with timelike boundary is not enough for the problem to be uniquely solvable. Our counterexamples are similar to earlier examples in the context of inverse scattering for moving obstacles
[
Ste91
]
in the sense that they are based on finite speed of propagation. We refer to
[
LO22
]
for examples with data on only a part of a cylinder, and to
[
Esk19
]
for non-smooth, cloaking type examples. Cloaking has been extensively studied in the context of the classical (Riemannian) version of Calderón problem, see the review
[
GKLU09
]
. We mention also two studies of spacetime cloaking in physics literature
[
MFKB10
,
FFOG12
]
.

1.1.
Statement of results

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a noncompact, connected smooth manifold of
1+n,n\geq 1
. Let
g
be a time-orientable Lorentzian metric on
\mathcal{M}
. We call
(\mathcal{M},g)
with such properties a
spacetime
. We assume that the spacetime is globally hyperbolic, which means that
[
BS07
]

(1)

\mathcal{M}
contains no closed causal curves, and

(2)

the causal diamonds
J_{g}^{-}(p)\cap J_{g}^{+}(q)
are compact for any
p,q\in\mathcal{M}
.

We note that for dimension
1+n\geq 3
, which is the dimension we are interested in on physical grounds, the first condition can be dropped from the definition
[
HM19
]
. Global hyperbolicity can also be equivalently characterized by saying that
\mathcal{M}
contains a Cauchy hypersurface, that is, a subset
\Sigma\subset\mathcal{M}
that each inextendible timelike curve crosses exactly once
[
Ger70
, Thm. 11]
. Moreover, results due to Bernal and Sánchez
[
BS03
,
BS05
]
guarantee that a globally hyperbolic manifold can be foliated by smooth spacelike Cauchy hypersurfaces given by the level sets of a temporal function
\tau\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M})
, that is, a function whose differential
d\tau
is timelike. Such a function is called a
Cauchy temporal function
. For any inextendible causal curve
\gamma:\mathbb{R}\to\mathcal{M}
it holds that
\lvert\tau(\gamma(s))\rvert\to\infty
as
s\to\pm\infty
. Moreover, we have a global representation for the metric
g
in terms of
\tau
:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime with a Cauchy temporal function
\tau
. Then
(\mathcal{M},g)
is isometric to the smooth product manifold
\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
with the metric

-\kappa d\tau^{2}+g_{\tau},

where
\kappa\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma)
is strictly positive, and
g_{\tau}
is a Riemannian metric on
\Sigma
that depends on
\tau
.

Globally hyperbolic spacetimes form a natural setting for inverse problems for the wave equation, as global hyperbolicity implies that the Cauchy problem for the wave equation is well-posed and solutions thereof have a finite propagation speed
[
BGP07
, Thms. 3.2.11, 3.2.12]
. The Lorentzian Calderón problem is an inverse boundary value problem for hyperbolic partial differential equations on Lorentzian manifolds, where one seeks to determine the underlying geometry of a spacetime from measurements at the boundary. This problem can be viewed as a Lorentzian analogue of the classical Calderón problem in the Riemannian setting, but exhibits fundamentally different features due to the causal structure of the wave equation.

Let us now give a precise statement of the problem. We say that
M\subset\mathcal{M}
is a
cylinder
if it is a submanifold of the same dimension with
\mathcal{M}
and has a smooth timelike boundary
\partial M
. Consider the wave equation on such
M

(1.1)

\Box_{g}u=0,\quad u\rvert_{\partial M}=\varphi,\quad u\rvert_{\tau\ll 0}=0,

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\partial M)
, and
\Box_{g}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator defined by

\Box_{g}u=-\lvert g\rvert^{-1/2}\partial_{j}(\lvert g\rvert^{1/2}g^{jk}\partial_{k}u).

Here the Einstein summation convention was implied. Then we define the hyperbolic Dirichlet-to-Neumann (DN) map

\Lambda_{g}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\partial M)\to C^{\infty}(\partial M),\quad\Lambda_{g}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}\varphi=\partial_{\nu}u\rvert_{\partial M},

where
\nu
is the unit inwards pointing normal vector to
\partial M
. In the Lorentzian Calderón problem, the data is given by the hyperbolic DN map, and the inverse problem is to recover the metric
g
, up to boundary-fixing diffeomorphisms.
In this paper, we give negative answers to the following uniqueness question for the Lorentzian Calderón problem.

Question 1.1
.

Let
g
and
g^{\prime}
be globally hyperblic Lorentzian metrics on
\mathcal{M}
, and let
M
be a cylinder with respect to both the metrics. Suppose that
\Lambda_{g}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}=\Lambda_{g^{\prime}}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}
. Are
g
and
g^{\prime}
isometric by a boundary-fixing diffeomorphism
F:M\to M
,
F\rvert_{\partial M}=\textnormal{id}
?

We will provide examples of smooth Lorentzian manifolds
(M,g)
obtained by choosing a cylinder inside a globally hyperbolic spacetime
(\mathcal{M},g)
, such that uniqueness in the Lorentzian Calderón problem fails therein. These include a cylinder in Minkowski spacetime given by a hyperboloid, Schwarzschild black hole and white hole spacetimes, and certain type of cosmological spacetimes. We show that these spacetimes can be locally perturbed in such a fashion that the DN map does not detect the resulting change in the geometry.

The counterexamples are based on the existence of a nonempty open set
U\subset M
that is unreachable from
\partial M
, meaning

(1.2)

\displaystyle J_{g}^{-}(U)\cap\partial M=\varnothing\quad\text{or}\quad J_{g}^{+}(U)\cap\partial M=\varnothing.

To state this common basis as a proposition, consider first the problem

(1.3)

\Box_{g}u=f,\quad\supp u\subset J_{g}^{+}(\supp f),

where
f\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{M}\backslash M)
. We may then define the
source-to-solution map

L_{g}:C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathcal{M}\backslash M)\to C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}\backslash M),\quad f\mapsto u\rvert_{\mathcal{M}\backslash M}.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime with a Cauchy temporal function
\tau
, and let
M\subset\mathcal{M}
be a cylinder.
Suppose there exists a nonempty open set
U\subset M
satisfying (
1.2
). Then there exists a globally hyperbolic metric
g^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{M}
such that
\tau
is a Cauchy temporal function with respect to
g^{\prime}
,
M
is a cylinder with respect to
g^{\prime}
, and that
L_{g}=L_{g^{\prime}}
but
g
is not isometric to
g^{\prime}
.

Moreover, to obtain a negative answer to Question
1.1
, we combine Proposition
1.1
with

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
g
and
g^{\prime}
be globally hyperblic Lorentzian metrics on
\mathcal{M}
, and let
\tau
be a Cauchy temporal function and
M
a cylinder with respect to both the metrics. Suppose
L_{g}=L_{g^{\prime}}
. Then
\Lambda_{g}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}=\Lambda_{g^{\prime}}^{\textnormal{Hyp}}
.

We will prove Propositions
1.1
and
1.2
in Section
3
.

1.2.
Some basic notions of the Lorentzian causality theory

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a Lorentzian manifold with metric of signature
(-,+,\dots,+)
. A vector
v\in T_{p}\mathcal{M}
is called
timelike
,
null
(or
lightlike
), or
spacelike
if
g(v,v)<0
,
g(v,v)=0
with
v\neq 0
, or
g(v,v)\geq 0
with equality only if
v=0
, respectively. A vector is called
causal
if it is either timelike or null.

The manifold
(\mathcal{M},g)
is said to be
time-oriented
if there exists a smooth timelike vector field
T
on
\mathcal{M}
. In this case, a causal vector
v\in T_{p}\mathcal{M}
is said to be
future-pointing
if
g(v,T)<0
, and
past-pointing
if
g(v,T)>0
.

A piecewise smooth curve
\gamma:I\to\mathcal{M}
is said to be
timelike
,
null
, or
causal
if its tangent vector
\dot{\gamma}(t)
is timelike, null, or causal for all
t\in I
. It is
future-directed
(resp.
past-directed
) if
\dot{\gamma}(t)
is future-pointing (resp. past-pointing) for all
t
.

We write
p\ll q
for
p,q\in\mathcal{M}
if there exists a future-directed timelike curve from
p
to
q
,
p<q
if there exists a future-directed causal curve from
p
to
q
, and
p\leq q
if
p<q
or
p=q
. The
chronological
and
causal future
of
p
are

I_{g}^{+}(p):=\{q\in\mathcal{M}\;:\;p\ll q\},\quad J_{g}^{+}(p):=\{q\in\mathcal{M}\;:\;p\leq q\},

and the
chronological

I^{-}(p)
and
causal past

J_{g}^{-}(p)
are defined analogously. Further, for a set
A\subset\mathcal{M}
,

\displaystyle I_{g}^{\pm}(A)=\bigcup_{p\in A}I_{g}^{+}(p),\quad J_{g}^{\pm}(A)=\bigcup_{p\in A}J_{g}^{+}(p).

One has
I^{\pm}(A)\subset J^{\pm}(A)
, and
I^{\pm}(A)
are open sets for any
A\subset\mathcal{M}
, while
J^{\pm}(p)
need not be closed in general
[
O’N83
, Lem. 14.2]
. If
(\mathcal{M},g)
is globally hyperbolic, then
J^{\pm}(K)
are closed for compact
K\subset\mathcal{M}

[
HM19
]
.

A causal (or timelike) curve
\gamma:I\to\mathcal{M}
is said to be
future inextendible
if it has no future endpoint, i.e., there is no point
p\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\gamma(t)\to p
as
t\to\sup I
. Similarly,
\gamma
is
past inextendible
if it has no past endpoint, i.e., there is no point
p\in\mathcal{M}
such that
\gamma(t)\to p
as
t\to\inf I
. The curve
\gamma
is called
inextendible
if it is both future and past inextendible.
Equivalently, a causal curve is inextendible if it cannot be extended to a strictly larger interval, which in this formulation is expressed by the absence of endpoints in
\mathcal{M}
.

2.
Counterexamples

2.1.
Minkowski hyperboloid

The simplest Lorentzian manifold is the Minkowski space of special relativity, that is, the manifold
\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
equipped with the metric

g=-dt^{2}+\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j},

where
(t,x)
are the natural coordinates on
\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
. The Minkowski space is the paradigm case of a globally hyperbolic manifold where the level surfaces of
t
give a smooth foliation by Cauchy hypersurfaces. Up to an affine reparametrization, a future-directed null geodesic of
g
can be written in the form

(2.1)

\gamma(s)=(t(s),x(s))=(t_{0}+s,x_{0}+sv),\quad(t_{0},x_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n},

where
v\in S^{n-1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Now take a domain
M\subset\mathbb{R}^{1+n}
defined by

M=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n}:\lvert x\rvert^{2}-a\lvert x\rvert-t^{2}\leq 0\},\quad a>0,

and compute the differential of the function
f(t,x)=\lvert x\rvert^{2}-a\lvert x\rvert-t^{2}
:

df=-2tdt+2\delta_{ij}x^{j}dx^{i}-a\lvert x\rvert^{-1}\delta_{ij}x^{j}dx^{i}.

Observe that the boundary
\partial M=\{f=0\}
is timelike; indeed, we have

g(df,df)\rvert_{\partial M}=4(-t^{2}+\lvert x\rvert^{2}-a\lvert x\rvert)\rvert_{\partial M}+a^{2}=a^{2}>0.

Consider then the the diamond-shaped domain

(2.2)

U=\{(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}^{1+n}:\lvert t\rvert+\lvert x\rvert<a/2\}.

A null geodesic
\gamma
given by (
2.1
) with
(t_{0},x_{0})\in U
stays in the interior of
M
. Indeed,

\displaystyle\lvert x(s)\rvert\leq|x_{0}-t_{0}v|+|t_{0}v+sv|=|x_{0}-t_{0}v|+|t(s)|<a/2+|t(s)|,

and

\displaystyle\lvert x(s)\rvert^{2}-a\lvert x(s)\rvert-|t(s)|^{2}<\lvert x(s)\rvert^{2}-a\lvert x(s)\rvert-(a/2-\lvert x(s)\rvert)^{2}=-a^{2}/4<0.

Consequently,
U\cap(J^{-}(\partial M)\cup J^{+}(\partial M))=\varnothing
, and by Propositions
1.1
and
1.2
we have a counterexample to Question
1.1
.

2.2.
Black and white hole spacetimes

The unique spherically symmetric solution to the vacuum Einstein equation is the Schwarzschild metric, expressed in coordinates as

(2.3)

g=-(1-r_{S}/r)dt^{2}+(1-r_{S}/r)^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}g_{S^{2}},\quad t\in\mathbb{R},\,r\in(0,r_{S})\cup(r_{S},\infty),

where
r_{S}>0
is a constant called the Schwarzschild radius and
g_{S^{2}}
is the unit two-sphere metric. The Schwarzschild spacetime has a curvature singularity at
r=0
, whereas the singularity at
r=r_{S}
is merely a coordinate artefact. Introducing the null coordinates

(2.4)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=-\left(\frac{r}{r_{S}}-1\right)^{1/2}e^{(r-t)/2r_{S}},

(2.5)

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{r}{r_{S}}-1\right)^{1/2}e^{(r+t)/2r_{S}},

the metric becomes

(2.6)

g=-\frac{4r_{S}^{3}}{r}e^{-r/r_{S}}dudv+r^{2}g_{S^{2}}.

It is convenient to further transform to a coordinate system where we have only timelike and spacelike coordinates, namely the Kruskal coordinates
T,R
defined by
T=(v+u)/2
and
R=(v-u)/2
.
The metric now reads

g=\frac{4r_{S}^{3}}{r}e^{-r/r_{S}}(-dT^{2}+dR^{2})+r^{2}g_{S^{2}}.

The coordinate
r
is determined implicitly from

T^{2}-R^{2}=\left(1-\frac{r}{r_{S}}\right)e^{r/r_{S}},

and coordinates
T,R
have ranges
R\in(-\infty,\infty),T^{2}<R^{2}+1
. The Kruskal coordinates cover the whole maximally extended Schwarzschild spacetime, also known as the Kruskal spacetime
\mathcal{K}

[
O’N83
, Cor. 13.37]
. The spacetime
\mathcal{K}
contains both a black hole region
\mathcal{B}=\{r<r_{S},T>0\}
where no causal curve can escape (by
[
O’N83
, Prop. 13.30]
), and a white hole region
\mathcal{W}=\{r<r_{S},T<0\}
that no causal curve can enter (due to time reversal symmetry of the metric). The Kruskal spacetime is globally hyperbolic. Indeed, the level set
\{T=0\}
is a Cauchy hypersurface
[
O’N83
, pp. 418-419]
.

Consider the submanifold
M=\{p\in\mathcal{K}:r(p)<r_{0}\}
for some
r_{0}>r_{S}
. From the representation (
2.3
) of the metric, it is clear that
g(dr,dr)\rvert_{r=r_{0}}>0
so that the boundary
\partial M=\{r=r_{0}\}

\partial M

On the other hand, the white hole region

2.3.
Universe with a Big Bounce

Consider a Friedman–Lemaître–Robertson–Walker (FLRW) metric of the form

(2.7)

g=-dt^{2}+\cosh^{2}(Ht)g_{\mbox{\scriptsize E}},\quad t\in(-\infty,\infty),

g_{E}=\delta_{ij}dx^{i}dx^{j}

H>0

R>\pi/H

\partial M
is timelike. Consider then a maximal null geodesic
\gamma:[0,l]\to M
with
l\in[0,\infty]

\gamma(0)\in\partial M
, and
\dot{\gamma}(0)
pointing inside. To study light rays in
M

3.
Proofs

We now prove Proposition
1.1
, starting with three lemmas. Then we turn to the proof of Proposition
1.2
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a time-oriented Lorentzian manifold and let
U\subset\mathcal{M}
be open. Then
J_{g}^{\pm}(U)=I_{g}^{\pm}(U)
.

Proof.

Let us prove the claim for the causal future; the past version is analogous.
Let
q\in J_{g}^{+}(U)
. Then there is
p\in U
with
p\leq q
. As
U
is open, there is
\tilde{p}\in U\cap I^{-}(p)
. Now
\tilde{p}\ll p\leq q
, and
[
O’N83
, Cor. 14.1]
yields
\tilde{p}\ll q
. Hence
q\in I_{g}^{+}(U)
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
U\subset\mathcal{M}
be open. Suppose that
g
and
g^{\prime}
are time-oriented Lorentzian metrics on
\mathcal{M}
and that the metrics and the time-orientations coincide in
\mathcal{M}\backslash U
. Then
J^{\pm}_{g}(U)=J^{\pm}_{g^{\prime}}(U)
.

Proof.

Let us prove the claim for the causal future; the past version is analogous.
Let
q\in J^{+}_{g}(U)
. By Lemma
3.1
there is a curve
\gamma:[a,b]\to\mathcal{M}
that is future-directed and timelike with respect to
g
, and
\gamma(a)\in U
and
\gamma(b)=q
. Write

\displaystyle c=\sup\{s\in(a,b):\gamma(s)\in U\}.

Then
\gamma\rvert_{[c,b]}
is future-directed and timelike with respect to
g^{\prime}
.
By continuity, there is
0<\delta\leq c-a
such that
\gamma\rvert_{[c-\delta,b]}
is future-directed and timelike with respect to
g^{\prime}
.
Due to the definition of
c
as a supremum, and the openness of
U
, there is some
s\in[c-\delta,b]
such that
\gamma(s)\in U
. Hence
q\in I^{+}_{g^{\prime}}(U)
.
The opposite inclusion is analogous.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be globally hyperbolic with a Cauchy temporal function
\tau
, and let
K\subset\mathcal{M}
be compact. Then
J_{g}^{+}(K)\cap\{\tau\leq\tau_{0}\}
and
J_{g}^{-}(K)\cap\{\tau\geq\tau_{0}\}
are compact for any
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{R}
.

Proof.

Let us prove the claim for the causal future; the past version is analogous.
Denote the closure of causal future pointing tangent vectors on
K
as

\mathcal{C}=\{(p,v)\in T\mathcal{M}:p\in K,\,g(v,v)\leq 0,\,v\,\,\textnormal{future pointing or $v=0$}\}.

Let now
q\in J_{g}^{+}(K)
. Then there exists
p\in K
with
p\leq q
, and by global hyperbolicity and
[
O’N83
, Prop. 14.19]
there is a causal geodesic from
p
to
q
. This implies that
\exp(\mathcal{C})=J_{g}^{+}(K)
. Introducing an auxiliary Riemannian metric
\zeta
on
\mathcal{M}
, consider the unit sphere bundle

SK_{\zeta}=\{(p,v)\in T\mathcal{M}:p\in K,\,\zeta(v,v)=1\},

which is compact for compact
K
. Write
\mathcal{C}_{1}=\mathcal{C}\cap SK_{\zeta}
, which also is compact as
\mathcal{C}
is closed. Let
\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C}_{1}\times[0,\infty)
be the domain such that for each
(p,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
the geodesic starting from
(p,v)
and defined on
\mathcal{D}\cap(\{(p,v)\}\times[0,\infty))
is future inextendible. Let then the map
\Phi:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{M}
be defined by
\Phi(p,v;s)=\exp_{p}sv
, and let
\lambda:\mathcal{C}_{1}\to[0,\infty)
be the function such that

\tau(\Phi(p,v;\lambda(p,v)))=\tau_{0}.

Since
\tau
is a Cauchy temporal function and
\alpha:s\mapsto\Phi(x,v;s)
is a causal curve for each
(p,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
, we have
\partial_{s}\tau(\alpha(s))>0
. From the implicit function theorem it then follows that
\lambda
is in particular continuous. Thus, there exists the maximum
L=\max_{(p,v)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}}\lambda(p,v)
and
J_{g}^{+}(K)\cap\{\tau\leq\tau_{0}\}\subset\Phi(\mathcal{C}_{1}\times[0,L])
, which is compact. The claim then follows from the fact that
J_{g}^{+}(K)\cap\{\tau\leq\tau_{0}\}
is closed.
∎

Proof of Proposition

.

We express

in the form provided by Theorem

.
Let

be a coordinate chart with coordinates
(\tau,x_{1},...,x_{n})
, and let

be a constant curvature Lorentzian metric in

given in local coordinates by

where

.

1

The metric

.

Then let

, and that
\{\chi=1\}

Let us show that

\displaystyle g^{\prime}(d\tau,d\tau)=1/((1-\chi)g_{\tau\tau}+\chi h_{\tau\tau})<0.

We let

\partial_{s}\tau(\gamma(s))=d\tau(\dot{\gamma}(s))>0

\displaystyle\gamma(\tau)=(\tau,x(\tau)).

\displaystyle 0>g^{\prime}(\dot{\gamma}(\tau),\dot{\gamma}(\tau))=(1-\chi)g_{\tau\tau}+\chi h_{\tau\tau}+G^{(\tau)}(\dot{x}(\tau),\dot{x}(\tau)).

exactly once.

We have shown that
\tau
is a Cauchy temporal function on

, which then is globally hyperbolic. Observe that

in view of (

). Thus

is timelike with respect to

.

Next, we prove that
g
and

are not isometric for a suitable choice of

. Let

be the scalar curvature associated with

. Denote by
\textnormal{Cr}(S_{g})
the set of critical values of

. By the Morse–Sard theorem,

; in particular,

Remark 1
.

By the method of proof of Proposition
1.1
, we see that
g^{\prime}
is not necessarily a small perturbation of

, in the sense that the curvature of

To prove Proposition
1.2
, we prove first a lemma.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
(\mathcal{M},g)
be a globally hyperbolic spacetime with a Cauchy temporal function
\tau
, and let
M\subset\mathcal{M}
be a cylinder. Let

and write

\displaystyle M_{1}=M\cap\{\tau\leq T\},\quad M_{2}=(\mathcal{M}\setminus M)\cap\{\tau\leq T\},\quad\Gamma=\partial M\cap\{\tau\leq T\}.

Consider the map

,

, where

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box_{g}v=f&\text{in $\mathcal{M}$}\\
v|_{\tau\ll 0}=0.\end{cases}

Let

for all

\displaystyle\begin{cases}\Box_{g}u_{j}=0&\text{in $M_{j}$, $j=1,2$,}\\

\partial_{\nu}u_{1}|_{\Gamma}-\partial_{\nu}u_{2}|_{\Gamma}=h\\

This problem has a unique solution as can be seen by iterating
[
Wil92
, Thm. 5.4]
in local coordinates. Applying this theorem with
s>0
large (and therefore
r<0
large in absolute value), we see that

,

, are well-defined on

for

. The theorem also guarantees enough regularity for

in the normal direction, so that the integration by parts below is justified.
Define

\displaystyle(w,f)_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times C_{0}^{\infty}(M_{1}\cup M_{2})}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1,2}(u_{j},\Box_{g}v)_{\mathcal{D}^{\prime}\times C_{0}^{\infty}(M_{j})}=(h,v)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle=(h,Af)_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0.

Proof of Proposition
1.2
.

We will apply Lemma
3.4
to
g
and
\tau

for the solution of (

.
