mRNA vaccine design targeting Merkel cell polyomavirus for immunotherapy of Merkel cell carcinoma
Authors: Akmal Zubair, Faisal Ahmad, Muhammad Yaqoob Shahani, Naila Afghan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe pathogenesis of Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) is characterized by ubiquitous and most likely silent childhood infection that may cause Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC). This mRNA vaccine includes the capsid proteins VP1 and VP2, as well as the Large T antigen and small T antigen, which are highly immunogenic. This has led to the development of mRNA vaccines against these T and Capsid peptides. Different computational methods was used to identify the epitopes of helper CD4 + lymphocytes and CD8 + lymphocytes. All of selected epitopes was analyzed for their toxicity, allergenicity, and immunogenicity. The MD simulation was carried out in an orthorhombic TIP3P water box with a buffer region of 10 Å, and Na+/Cl- counter ions at a physiological amount of salt (150 mM) were added to neutralize the system. Once the NVT and NPT aggregates were equilibrated, a 100 ns manufacturing run at 310 K and 1 atm was performed. Our vaccine has 19 epitopes in the vaccine construct, including HTLs and CTLs. The vaccine was found to have an increased hydrophilicity, and the average hydropathicity score was − 8.95. The Ramachandran plot revealed potential stability, 94.6% of the amino acid units were found in the allowed region. The vaccine showed potentially high affinity with the TLR3 receptor as the docking score was − 318.56 KJ/mol, and the confidence score was 0.9668. After codon optimization, there was a knowing improvement in the expression in E. coli vectors that produced vaccines, as indicated by the increase in GC content to 53.41. MM-GBSA analysis showed that there was a uniform binding affinity of TLR3 between − 1500 and − 2000 kcal/mol. Our vaccine construct against MCPyV showed potential immune responses and should be advanced to in vitro and in vivo clinical experiments.Keywords: Merkel cell polyomavirus, Vaccine, mRNA, Bioinformatics, CancerSubject terms: Biochemistry, Biotechnology, Cancer, Immunology

Introduction

Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) is a circular, double-stranded, non-enveloped DNA virus with a size of 5.4 kb, from the family of Polyomaviridae1,2. The virus was initially discovered in Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC) tissues and is considered a significant oncogenic virus in human beings1. When the host is immunosuppressed or aged, the virus may be incorporated into the native genome and induce malignant transformation, but in general, MCPyV infection is very common among the population, and the majority of them are asymptomatic3,4.

Merkel cell cancer is a neuroendocrine skin tumor that is rare but is highly severe and increasingly reported worldwide5. It is characterized by high recurrence, early metastasis, and poor prognosis, especially in elderly or immunocompromised patients3,6. Almost all MCC cases are associated with the MCPyV infection, where the introduction of the genome of the virus and the synthesis of oncogenic proteins cause tumorigenesis2,7. Even though immunotherapies, including immune checkpoint inhibitors, have been shown to increase therapy efficacy, this approach is not effective in all patients; preventive and therapeutic vaccine-based measures should be implemented5,8. A non-coding administrative section separates the early and late portions of the MCPyV genome. Tumor antigens like large T antigen (LT) and small T antigen (sT), which interfere with the control of the host cell cycle, particularly with cancer suppressor proteins like p53 and retinoblastoma protein, are expressed in the early area and are crucial for viral replication and carcinogenesis9,10. The VP1 and VP2 structural peptides are coded by the late portion, implicated in viral capsid assembly, attachment to host cells, and viral penetration11,12. These viral proteins are promising targets for vaccine development since they facilitate a viral infection and immunogenicity2.

Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) is one of the most common human polyomaviruses, and serological surveys found that almost 60 to 80% of the world’s adult population is infected with MCPyV during childhood13. Persistent infection and integration of the viral genome into the host may be involved in the development of Merkel cell carcinoma (MCC), especially in elderly and immunocompromised patients, although the infection is usually asymptomatic in healthy people. The prevalence of MCC has risen over the past decades and is higher in North America, Europe, and Australia, and is related to aging, exposure to ultraviolet radiation, and immunosuppression1. Despite its relative rarity, MCC has an aggressive clinical course, comprising rapid progression, early metastasis, and poor survival, thus imposing a significant clinical burden. Considering the correlation between the presence of MCPyV and MCC, it has become even more important to find effective immunotherapeutic and vaccine-based approaches to the targeting of MCPyV-associated oncogenic proteins14.

The recent developments in immunoinformatics and computational biology have drastically transformed vaccine development. In silico methods allow rapid identification of antigenic epitopes, predicting the immune response, and testing the safety and stability of vaccines before being experimentally validated15,16. In particular, the multi-epitope vaccine design can incorporate numerous B-cell and T-cell epitopes for various viral proteins, which increases the immune coverage and minimizes the chances of immune escape17,18. These methods have already been used for a number of viral pathogens and are also under consideration in the case of oncolytic viruses7,19.

A promising platform is messenger RNA (mRNA) vaccines, which are safe, quicker to manufacture, and can produce both humoral and cellular immunity20,21. In contrast to the traditional vaccines, mRNA vaccines do not include live pathogens and can be designed accordingly to produce a specific set of antigenic determinants, which makes them extremely versatile and efficient20. Moreover, the technology of mRNA vaccination has proven to be highly successful in recent years, which justifies its use in the creation of vaccines against viral diseases and cancer-related antigens21,22.

In-silico vaccine design based on bioinformatics is a revolutionary approach to vaccine design, replacing the traditional vaccine trial-and-error with a very specific and rationalized vaccine design. Reverse vaccinology is computational screening of entire viral or bacterial genomes to identify the most immunogenic epitopes, which can then only be targeted for a strong immune response23. This electronic pre-screening is massively efficient, and years of lab work can be condensed into a few weeks, and toxic/allergenic sequences can be filtered prior to a single vial being made. Furthermore, it is very cost-effective since it lowers the cost of pathogen cultivation and the use of large animals for testing24. Bioinformatics can prevent the appearance of a potentially hazardous and universally ineffective final construct in silico by predicting global population coverage with algorithms of HLA binding.

The current research aimed to synthesize an mRNA vaccine for the MCPyV using immunoinformatics. The antigenic sequences of the capsid proteins VP1 and VP2, and small T antigen and large T antigen, were used in the prediction of epitopes and the construction of vaccines. The vaccine that was designed was also evaluated using structural modeling, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulation, simulation of immune response, and codon optimization to determine its stability, immunogenicity, and ability to be expressed. The paper provides a computational framework for the design of a possible vaccine against Merkel cell carcinoma that is associated with MCPyV.

Materials and methodology

Recovery of target proteins sequences

The database National Center for Biotechnology Information (NCBI) is used for the retrieval of target protein sequences in the format of FASTA. The proteins of Merkel Cell Polyomavirus (MCPYV), including capsid proteins VP1 (APA21079.1) and VP2 (APA21078.1), as well as large T antigen (CAW30990.1), small T antigen (YP_009111422.1), are retrieved25. The VaxiJen v2.0 platform was then used to further assess these for immunogenicity as shown in Fig. 1. The antigenicity of the retrieved proteins and predicted epitopes was analyzed with the help of VaxiJen v2.0 server (https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/vaxijen/VaxiJen/VaxiJen.html), which adopts the virus model with a threshold value of 0.4. Protein and epitope with antigenicity score > 0.4 were taken as antigenic and selected for further downstream analysis. The allergenicity was assessed based on allergenicity prediction software (AllerTOP v2.1) https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test/ and the default prediction parameters were used for toxicity profiling (ToxinPred2) https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred2/. The water solubility of the selected epitopes was also assessed to guarantee their suitability for the construction and expression of vaccines26.

Prediction of CTLs epitopes

The NetMHCpan 4.0 https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/NetMHCpan-4.0/27 and NetCTL v1.2 https://services.healthtech.dtu.dk/services/NetCTL-1.2/28 have been employed to quantify the CD8 + T− cell reactivity to pathogens, where the CTL antigenic determinant may begin29. NetCTL v1.2 is a prediction tool in immunoinformatics that predicts cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes. It is a composite comprising three elements, namely: TAP passage effectiveness, TAP proteasomal C-terminal breakage prediction, and MHC class I interaction prediction. The predicted cytotoxic T lymphocyte (CTL) epitopes were obtained by performing prediction analysis on selected MCPyV proteins on NetCTL v1.2 and NetMHCpan 4.0 servers. The binding affinity and the immunogenic potential of the 9-mer peptide sequences were predicted for different HLA class I alleles (HLA-A01:01, HLA-A30:02, HLA-A32:01, HLA-A68:01, HLA-A68:02, HLA-B51:01, and HLA-B*58:01). For combined prediction of proteasomal cleavage, TAP transport efficiency, and MHC-I binding affinity, the default threshold value was set at 0.75 in NetCTL v1.2. To further validate peptide–MHC class I interactions, NetMHCpan 4.0 was used. A final selection for epitopes with high binding affinity, high antigenicity, non-allergenicity, non-toxicity, and good water solubility was made for the final construction of the vaccine. VaxiJen v2.0 with the threshold of 0.4 was used for antigenicity, AllerTOP v2.0 for allergenicity, and ToxinPred2 for toxicity.

Prediction of HTL epitopes

IEDB is applied to establish the reactive regions that connect to MHC class-II and can elicit humoral and cellular immune reactions30 and the NetMHCpan 4.0 software27. For the decision of the mRNA vaccine, most of the HTL epitopes from the two frameworks were selected for the construction of the vaccine. The IEDB MHC-II binding prediction tool and NetMHCIIpan 4.0 server were used to predict helper T lymphocyte (HTL) epitopes. For the identification of MHC-II binding peptides, the recommended prediction method of the IEDB was used. 15-mer peptide sequences were tested against the HLA-DR alleles, HLA-DRB107:01, HLA-DRB301:01, HLA-DRB302:02, and HLA-DRB501:01. The epitopes with high binding affinity and low percentile rank values were selected for further analyses. These predicted HTL epitopes were then assessed for antigenicity, allergenicity, toxicity, and water solubility using the VaxiJen v2.0, AllerTOP v2.0, ToxinPred2, and peptide solubility analysis. The final vaccine construct was only composed of highly antigenic, non-allergenic, non-toxic, and water-soluble epitopes31.

mRNA vaccine construction via in-silico

Merkel Cell Polyomavirus (MCPYV) mRNA vaccine was developed by combining the selected HTL and CTL epitopes with numerous additional co-translational residues. The original purpose of the 5′ cap was to aid in the mRNA vaccine construct’s mRNA decoding, nucleus-to-cytoplasm trafficking, dissociation, and stability32. The Kozak region promotes accurate translation at the start codon, while the 5(UTR) is recognized to guarantee the stability of the mRNA and aid in its effective translation. For boosting the immunological response, the Human Beta Defensive 1 (HBD1) was applied as an adjuvant33. Adjuvant and HTL antigen sites were linked by GPGPG adaptors, HTL and LBL epitopes were linked by EAAK spacers, and CTL and LBL antigen sites were linked by linkers known as AAY. Human β-defensin-1 (HBD-1) was included in the vaccine construct as an adjuvant to boost the immune response of the vaccine by activating innate immunity and antigen presentation. Different linkers (EAAAK) were employed to obtain the structural stability and proper epitope separation. GPGPG, AAY). Structural rigidity between the adjuvant and the epitopes was introduced via the EAAAK linker, and epitope processing and presentation to HTLs were enhanced with GPGPG linkers. Moreover, AAY linkers facilitated efficient CTL epitope processing and MHC-I presentation, leading to enhanced activation of the cytotoxic T-cells and thereby, vaccine immunogenicity The model of vaccine includes MHC I-targeting domain (MITD), signaling peptide, and Tissue plasminogen activator (tPA). MITD carries CTL epitopes to the MHC-I partition of the endoplasmic reticulum, whereas tPA encourages epitope flow. To increase the stability and translational effectiveness of mRNA, the poly-A tail and 3′ untranslated region were inserted34.

Projection of allergenicity, antigenicity, and toxicity of vaccine model

The immunogenicity, hypersensitivity, and toxic nature of the mRNA vaccination model were evaluated using appropriate bioinformatics tools. VaxiJen 2.0 was used to estimate antigenicity35. The AllerTop 2.0 program was used to assess the vaccine construct’s potential allergenicity, the AllerTop 2.0 tool https://www.ddg-pharmfac.net/allertop_test36. ToxinPred 2 https://webs.iiitd.edu.in/raghava/toxinpred2/ was used to quantify the vaccine construct’s toxicity to determine its safety37.

Prediction of physicochemical properties of the vaccine construct

The vaccine’s physical and chemical aspects were examined using the ExPASy ProtParam analysis https://web.expasy.org/protparam/ system traits38. Web users may see projected numbers for the building’s length, molecular mass, aliphatic proportion, potential isoelectric point (pI), presumed half-life, and grand average hydrophobicity (GRAVY). By using the Protein-Sol program to analyze the solubility of the vaccine design, we were able to quantify the hydrophilic characteristics of the amino acid residues and their capacity to repel water39.

Anticipation of the secondary structure

The SOPMA tool https://npsa.lyon.inserm.fr/cgi-bin/npsa_automat.pl?page=/NPSA/npsa_sopma.html36 and PSIPRED server https://bioinf.cs.ucl.ac.uk/psipred/37,38 were used to quantify the amino acids that form the additional configuration of the immunogen design, which consists of coil, α-helix, and β-sheet. Protein secondary structure may be predicted using SOPMA and PSIPRED. Based on multiple sequence alignment and amino acid propensities, the statistical methods of SOPMA may be used to estimate the structure of eight alpha helices, eight beta sheets, and coils. PSIPRED employs a neural network to provide predictions and is based on PSI-BLAST to generate evolutionary profiles (PSSM). While PSIPRED is more sophisticated and significantly more accurate, SOPMA is simpler and more accurate. Because of its machine-learning-based methodology, PSIPRED is frequently employed.

Prediction, improvement, and validation of tertiary structures

The AlphaFold 3 tool https://alphafoldserver.com/ is applied to project the three-dimensional structure of the vaccine model39. The Ramachandran illustration created by ProCheck determined that most of the amino acids were in energetically accessible areas. AlphaFold 3 far surpasses earlier AI-based prediction methods and homology modeling in predicting the tertiary structure of proteins, interactions between proteins, and conformational flexibility. Instead of relying on experimentally solved templates extensively, AlphaFold 3 uses deep learning models that are trained on vast structural databases, which makes it very reliable to predict structures even when the template for a protein is found only a limited number40.

Molecular docking between the vaccine construct and TLR3

The vaccine construct’s molecular interaction with TLR3. Toll-like receptors (TLRs) are key mediators of inflammation, causing pathways in the digestive tract that connect cellular and adaptive immunity and regulate immunological responses to pathogen-derived ligands41. Consequently, using the HDOCK http://hdock.phys.hust.edu.cn/ server, the tertiary design of the vaccine was docked with TLR-3. TLR3 plays a crucial role in the innate immune system by recognizing and activating downstream antiviral signalling pathways, resulting in the production of type I interferons and pro-inflammatory cytokines. The activation of TLR3 has been shown to induce the expression of antigen-related molecules and trigger innate and adaptive inflammatory responses to viral infections. The finding of strong interaction between the designed mRNA vaccine and TLR3 in the present study implies that the vaccine construct may be able to effectively activate immune signaling pathways. Therefore, targeting specifically TLR3 could result in increased immunogenicity and cytokine production, and increase cellular and humoral immune response to Merkel cell virus infection42,43.

Normal mode analysis

It is anticipated that iMOD https://imods.iqf.csic.es/, an internal coordinate system normal mode computing technology, will be used for the computational modeling of the vaccine40. By doing Normal mode analysis (NMA) in internal (dihedral) coordinates, this gadget unexpectedly replicates the operational movements of large compounds in biological networks. Our method creates reasonable transformation routes between two similar configurations, even for huge polymers. Normal Mode Analysis (NMA) of the vaccine construct was done using the server iMODS to analyze structural flexibility, stability and collective motions of the construct. The server computes deformability, eigenvalues, covariance matrices and elastic network models, which helps to understand the dynamics of the complex and its conformational mobility34,41.

Codon optimization and in silico cloning

The codons of vaccines were optimized by applying the K12 form of E. coli strain manufacturing network and the Java Codon Adaptation software (JCat) service (http://www.jcat.de/Start.jsp). Using GC contents and the codon adaptation index (CAI), the service assesses the vaccines’ expression levels. GC concentrations between 30 and 70% are acceptable, and a score of 0.8 is rather decent, even though an ideal CAI score would be 1.044. EcoR53k1 and hpa1 restriction sites were used to introduce into pET28a(+) E. coli vector the vaccine-refined gene sequences. In the last, applying the SnapGene tool (https://www.snapgene.com/), the refined sequences of the vaccine were transmitted to the vector pET-28a(+).

Immune response simulation

The typical peptide sequence of the vaccine may be investigated for antigenicity using immunological response mapping. The immunological reaction to the vaccine was simulated employing the C-immunosim platform. The C-ImmSim server https://kraken.iac.rm.cnr.it/C-IMMSIM/ was used to simulate the immune response with default settings. The three doses of the vaccine were given at time steps 1, 84, and 168 (4 weeks apart). The simulation was run for 1050 time steps, with each time step representing 8 h of real biological time. Throughout the simulation, default immune environment parameters, such as HLA selection, dynamics of lymphocytes, cytokine profile, and antigen processing parameters, were maintained. The antigen clearance and immunoglobulin production, B-cell and T-cell dynamics and cytokine responses were analyzed to assess the immunogenic properties of the vaccine construct, as was the generation of memory cells45.

mRNA structure prediction

The RNAfold online tool http://rna.tbi.univie.ac.at/ was used to estimate the vaccine’s secondary configuration46. The tool offered thermodynamically calculated minimum free energy (MFE) and query mRNA structures during the anticipation47. Before this experiment, the conversion of DNA into RNA would be performed to exchange the JCat refined genetic sequences into RNA sequences. Then, using the tool called RNAfold would be useful in projecting the secondary framework of the vaccine depending on these RNA sequences46.

MD simulation and MMGBSA analysis

The system was allowed to stabilize through energy optimization using the steepest descent technique until the highest force dropped to less than 10 kJ/mol, indicating system stability. The LINCS technique was used to limit bond lengths, and the Particle Mesh Ewald (PME) method was applied to compute wide-ranging electric-force–related bonding involving both van der Waals and Coulombic interactions, with a cutoff length of 0.9 nm48. Two processes were included in the equilibration process: first, the system temperature was stabilised using an NVT ensemble simulation at a constant temperature of 300 K and a constant volume of 100 ps42,43.

A 300 ps NPT ensemble simulation at 300 K and 1 bar of pressure preceded this, allowing the system density to equilibrate while maintaining constant particle number, pressure, and temperature. To remove edge effects, all three spatial directions were periodicized with regard to borders49. During the NPT stage, the system volume was allowed to fluctuate dynamically. Finally, trajectories were examined using integrated GROMACS tools during a 100 ns production MD simulation.

The complex’s binding free energy was projected through the MM/GBSA (Molecular Mechanics/Generalized Born Surface Area) method using gmx_MMPbSA, a standard apparatus for MM/PBSA and MM/GBSA computations done with GROMACS. The electrostatic, polar, non-polar, and van der Waals solvation energy terms were computed using snapshots obtained from the MD trajectory50,51. The entire binding free energy was determined by adding these contributions. The interaction strength and robustness of the complex during the simulation time may be accurately and efficiently estimated using this technique52.

Construction of mRNA vaccine for MCPyV

About 17 epitopes were used to construct the mRNA vaccine against MCPyV. The epitopes contain about 8 HTLs and 9 CD8 + lymphocytes (CTL), and they were among the seventeen antigenic determinants found in the vaccine prototype. Key structural and regulatory elements, including the 5′ cap, 5′ untranslated region (UTR), Kozak sequence, and the tissue plasminogen activator (tPA) signal peptide, were included in the vaccine design to ensure correct mRNA and peptide production and immunological vulnerability. For the connection with HTL epitopes and to boost immunological activation, β-defensin 1 was introduced as an immune-regulator, and this was done with the help of GPGPG links. HTL and CTLs were included using spacers. As illustrated in Fig. 2, this intentional design was intended to guarantee stability, substantial expression, and strong immune system activation.

Discussion

Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV) is a well-characterized cancer-causing virus that causes a skin malignancy that occurs globally. It is a fact that more developments have been made in immunotherapy, but no efficient vaccine is working against MCPyV, representing the need for new defense approaches. Because of this, the present study used a computational technique to create an mRNA vaccine against MCPyV using the virus’s key proteins, which include the capsid proteins VP1 and VP2, big T antigenic site, and small T antigenic site.

The design of multi-epitope vaccines has become a popular topic because it allows the simultaneous production of both humoral and cellular immunity targeted at multiple antigenic determinants17,18. There was a total of 17 epitopes (9 CTL and 8 HTL epitopes) used in this study due to their antigenicity, non-toxicity, and non-allergenicity. The epitopes that were chosen exhibited desirable antigenic potential, meaning that they were effective in the stimulation of immune responses. Other epitope-based designs have been effective in designing vaccines against several viral pathogens16,19.

Introduction of the proper linkers and adjuvants is vital in the process of improving vaccine immunogenicity. In the current construct β-defensin is employed as an adjuvant to improve immune activation with GPGPG linkers being utilized to preserve the structure and appropriate presentation of the epitope44. Past reports have indicated that β-defensin may serve as an immune modulator, by facilitating the activation of dendritic cells and increasing antigen presentation45,46. Effective translation and stability of the mRNA vaccine construct are further enhanced by the incorporation of regulatory elements such as the Kozak sequence, 5 cap, and untranslated regions.

The physicochemical searches showed that the vaccine construct designed has good characteristics such as stability, hydrophilicity, and appropriate molecular weight. The value of instability index is less than 40, meaning that it is a stable construct, whereas the value of GRAVY is negative, indicating that it is hydrophilic, which can facilitate its interaction with the immune cells and inhibit the antigen presentation47. Additionally, the vaccine design has a high antigenicity score, which suggests that it would probably trigger a robust immune response48.

Structural studies further confirmed the vaccine construct’s suitability and stability. The secondary structure has been dominated by random coils and α-helices, suggesting that epitopes are more flexible and easier for the immune system to process49,52. It is claimed that random coil areas are often antigenic and surface-exposed. Furthermore, the modelled structure appears to be stable and structurally solid based on the tertiary structure validation results, which include high Ramachandran plot statistics and refinement scores53.

Toll-like receptor 3 (TLR-3) molecular docking was applied to ascertain how the vaccine design interacted with the innate immunological system. The detection of viral RNA and the start of innate immune responses, which in turn set off adaptive immunological responses, depend heavily on TLR-354. The study’s strong docking score suggests that the vaccine design and TLR-3 consistently interact, which is likely to be successful in activating downstream immune signalling pathways. Computational docking has been widely used to predict receptor-ligand interactions and the immunostimulatory potential of vaccination candidates55,56.

The stability of the vaccine-receptor combination was further reinforced by molecular dynamics modelling57. The complex may undergo the conformational changes necessary to bind the complex in a stable way, and the resultant eigenvalue showed the appropriate level of structural flexibility. Increased flexibility and stability of molecular interactions are often indicated by lower eigenvalues. These results indicate the possibility of the vaccine construct having stable interactions with immune receptors at physiological conditions58.

The results of the immunological simulation demonstrated that the intended vaccine construct may stimulate macrophages, dendritic cells, natural killer cells, CD4 + T cells, CD8 + T cells, and B cells, among other immune system components. Effective antiviral immunity requires the activation of both cellular and antibody-mediated reactions. The vaccine’s capability to trigger a broad immune reaction highlights its potential efficacy in treating MCPyV infection59,60.

The vaccine design will be successfully produced in the E. coli system, according to a codon optimisation study, which showed a codon adaptation index (CAI) of 1.0 and GC content within the ideal range. Another strategy that is highly adopted to achieve protein expression and increase the efficiency of translation in heterologous systems is codon optimization. Such findings suggest that the vaccine construct designed can be produced on a large scale61–63.

Moreover, RNA secondary structure analysis showed positive values of minimum free energy, which showed that the mRNA construct was able to fold. Stable mRNA forms are needed to maintain transcripts and ensure efficient translation after delivery. The free energies were found to be low, indicating that the designed mRNA vaccine may have structural stability, which is a vital factor in its functionality43,64.

Although these are encouraging results, it should be noted that the complexity of biological systems cannot be fully captured by computational predictions. Therefore, research should be done both in vitro and in vivo to confirm the safety, immunogenicity, and protective effectiveness of the suggested vaccine design65.

The study’s shortcomings

Although it has some significant drawbacks, computer-based vaccine design is also beneficial. It cannot accurately recreate the intricacy of real immunological responses, and its predictions are heavily reliant on the quality of the given data. This recommends that vaccine elements that show assurance in computational systems could not perform well in actual biological systems in terms of stability, expression, and immunogenicity. Large-scale laboratory testing and clinical trials are still necessary as a result.

Strengths of the study

The transition of computational vaccine to preclinical and clinical advancement is a key step that speeds up the process and increases its specificity. Long before they begin any laboratory work, researchers may learn a great deal about the attractiveness of the antigens they want to target, the steadiness of a protein they are working with, and which epitopes are more likely to trigger an immunological response, thanks to computational techniques. In order to maximize the vaccine’s effectiveness and reduce its hazards, an in-silico vaccine model may also be applied to comprehend the immunological pathways that the vaccination must stimulate. The vaccine design may be readily altered to accommodate new variations or other similar diseases after laboratory testing confirms these predictions. This adaptability offers computationally directed vaccinations with a robust reaction to the fast development of infectious illnesses66,67.

Conclusion

Our mRNA vaccine was able to prevent infection by the Merkel cell polyomavirus (MCPyV). The stability and antigenicity of the vaccine were confirmed by structural studies following the identification of epitopes and with the help of bioinformatics-based methodologies. Strong binding between the vaccine and Toll-like receptors (TLRs) is important to initiate immunological response, according to docking tests. Molecular dynamics simulations demonstrated the stability and flexibility of the vaccine compounds. The E. coli vectors performed better because to the codon optimisation. Following vaccination, B-cell and T-cell responses were maintained, and the innate immune system was activated.

The secondary structure analysis predicts the stability of mRNA configurations, which is the foundation for the vaccine’s efficacy. Generally speaking, our vaccine may be used to prevent MCPyV infections. More in vitro, in vivo, and clinical research is needed to determine its effectiveness, safety, and potential in treating MCPyV infection and malignancies. This thorough investigation clarifies the characteristics of the vaccine and lays the groundwork for future research by eliminating the requirement for clinical trials and laboratory testing to ascertain the practical efficiency of these mRNA vaccines.
