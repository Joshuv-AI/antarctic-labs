Structural equation modeling of the association between factors related to medication adherence, perceived social support, and patients’ resilience to hypertension: a cross-sectional study
Authors: Simin Tahmasbi, Amir Faraji, Azam Alavi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundHypertension affects over a billion people in the world, despite this prevalence, poor adherence to antihypertensive medication remains the main problem in low-income regions. Social support and resilience might influence adherence to antihypertensive medication.This cross-sectional study investigated the associations among perceived social support, resilience, and antihypertensive medication adherence in hypertensive patients in Izeh County, Iran.MethodsThis descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study was conducted, from April to December 2024 among adults with hypertension selected through a two-stage cluster sampling method from urban and rural health centers in Izeh County. Of the 600 initially enrolled participants (350 urban, 250 rural), 70 were excluded due to more than two missing items on the MMAS-8 questionnaire, leaving 530 participants for analysis.Data were collected using self-administered validated questionnaires under researcher supervision at urban and rural health centers, including the Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8), the Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (MSPSS), and the Resilience Questionnaire.No resilience subscales were removed due to intellectual property restrictions, and potential conceptual overlap was statistically controlled. Analyses were conducted using SPSS 24 and AMOS 20, applying regression, path analysis, and structural equation modeling (SEM) to assess direct and indirect pathways.ResultsPerceived social support showed a significant positive effect on resilience (β = 0.43, p < 0.001) and an indirect effect on medication adherence (β = 0.10, p < 0.001). Resilience had a significant direct effect on medication adherence (β = 0.19, p = 0.002). The direct effect of social support on medication adherence was not statistically significant (p = 0.070). Among social support dimensions, family support was the strongest (M = 15.82, SD = 2.82). The SEM model demonstrated The model demonstrated an acceptable fit to the observed data (RMSEA = 0.054; CFI = 0.91).ConclusionThese findings bring out the imperative of social support and resilience to medication adherence among hypertensive patients. It is important to strengthen the social networks and psychological resilience to improve adherence to antihypertensive medication and overall disease management,Psychosocial strategies should thus be incorporated into future interventions to improve adherence to antihypertensive medication within resource-limited settings.Keywords: Hypertension, Medication adherence, Social support, Psychological resilience, Structural equation modeling

Introduction

Hypertension is one of the major global health threats, affecting both developing and developed countries almost equally [1]. The global burden of hypertension is estimated to affect over 1 billion people worldwide, with little evidence of decline in these figures [2]. In a 2018 study in Iran, nearly 6.6 million Iranians were diagnosed with high blood pressure [3]. Nonetheless, treatment, management, and prevalence of hypertension vary by geographical location and ethnicity [4]. While hypertension rates have declined in high-income countries, low-income countries experience the opposite trend [5, 6]. Non-adherence to antihypertensive medications remains a major contributor to poor blood pressure management and increased cardiovascular complications [7]. Psychosocial factors including social support, coping capacity, and resilience play a critical role in disease management.

Hypertension is one of the most common chronic conditions globally, affecting over one billion people [8]. Non-adherence to antihypertensive medications remains a major contributor to poor blood pressure management and increased cardiovascular complications [9]. Psychosocial factors including social support, coping capacity, and resilience play a critical role in disease management [10].

Medication adherence is a critical determinant of successful management in chronic diseases such as hypertension [11]. Effective disease management not only reduces morbidity and mortality but also improves quality of life [12]. Non-adherence to antihypertensive medication whether intentional or unintentional remains a prevalent issue, contributing to disease progression and increased healthcare costs [13, 14]. Psychosocial factors, such as social support, depression, stress, and family dynamics, have been shown to influence hypertension outcomes and self-care behaviors [15, 16]. Managing hypertension requires strict adherence to antihypertensive medications, regular blood pressure monitoring, lifestyle modification, and family involvement [17].

Social support, as defined by the American Psychological Association, refers to the emotional, informational, and practical assistance received from one’s social network. This support can originate from family, friends, colleagues, or groups, and plays a crucial role in coping with psychological, social, and biological stressors [18]. It not only enhances adherence to antihypertensive medications and regular intake schedules but also provides emotional and psychological security, helping patients manage daily stressors associated with chronic illness. This support reduces non-adherence caused by mental and emotional exhaustion rather than forgetfulness [19].

Patient-related barriers to adherence include personal factors, social and economic factors, condition-related factors, and healthcare system-related factors [20]. Given the high prevalence of hypertension and the influence of cultural and socioeconomic factors on adherence, it is necessary to explore how these factors interact in Iranian populations [21]. Despite substantial evidence on the general influence of social support on chronic disease management, the specific pathways through which social support affects medication adherence, particularly via psychological resilience, remain unclear. Studies in recent years suggest that resilience may act as a psychological buffer enhancing adherence, yet this mediating mechanism has not been adequately explored, especially within Iranian cultural and socioeconomic contexts [10, 22].

Despite growing evidence regarding the role of psychosocial factors in chronic disease management, limited studies have simultaneously examined the associations among perceived social support, resilience, medication adherence, and blood pressure control within a comprehensive theoretical framework [23–25]. Most previous studies have focused primarily on direct associations between social support and adherence, while the potential mediating role of resilience has received comparatively little attention, particularly in non-Western and middle-income settings such as Iran [26–28]. Moreover, only a few studies have applied Structural Equation Modeling (SEM) to investigate these associations simultaneously through both direct and indirect pathways [29–31].

To address this gap, the present study was informed by Social Cognitive Theory and the Stress and Coping Model, which propose that supportive social associations may strengthen psychological resilience and coping capacity in individuals with chronic illness [32–34]. Enhanced resilience may subsequently improve adherence to prescribed antihypertensive management and self-management behaviors, ultimately contributing to better blood pressure control. SEM provides an appropriate analytical framework for evaluating these complex psychosocial associations and examining both direct and mediated effects among latent constructs [7, 8, 29].

Therefore, the present study aimed to investigate the associations among perceived social support, resilience, medication adherence, and blood pressure control among patients with hypertension using SEM.

Although previous studies have examined associations between psychosocial factors and hypertension outcomes, limited evidence exists regarding the simultaneous direct and indirect relationships among social support, resilience, medication adherence, and blood pressure control using structural equation modeling, particularly in Iranian populations.Therefore, there is a distinct gap in the literature: the combined direct and indirect effects of social support and resilience on medication adherence among hypertensive patients especially in non-Western, low- and middle-income settings remain underexplored.

To what extent does social support directly and indirectly (through resilience) affect medication adherence in adults with hypertension?

Based on the conceptual framework and previous evidence, the following hypotheses were:

Study design and setting

This descriptive-analytical cross-sectional study was conducted in Izeh County, Iran, from April to December 2024.The target population included all adults (≥ 30 years: The age threshold of ≥ 30 years was selected because the prevalence of hypertension increases markedly from early adulthood onward, and including younger age groups could introduce heterogeneity and reduce the interpretability of self-management behaviors.) diagnosed with hypertension residing in urban and rural areas of the county.

Urban and rural residence was classified based on the official definitions provided by the Statistical Center of Iran. Urban areas refer to settlements with municipality administration, while rural areas include villages and settlements located outside municipal jurisdictions and administered through rural councils (Dehyari).

Exclusion criteria

Participants who were unwilling to participate or unable to complete the questionnaire, or those with incomplete or invalid responses that could affect data quality were excluded.

The two-stage cluster sampling method was used to recruit participants from urban and rural areas of Izeh County.

Stage 1 (selection of health centers)

In the first stage, urban and rural health centers were considered as clusters. A number of health centers were randomly selected from the list of active health centers in Izeh County.

Stage 2 (selection of participants)

In the second stage, individuals diagnosed with hypertension who were registered in the selected health centers were identified through health records. Eligible participants were selected proportionally from each center until the required sample size was achieved, resulting in the recruitment of 350 participants from urban areas and 250 participants from rural areas.

Exclusions and Final Sample: Seventy participants were excluded due to > 2 missing MMAS-8 items, in accordance with the predefined criterion for handling missing data. The final analyzed sample included 530 participants (300 urban, 230 rural), representing an 11.7% reduction fully explained by missing-item exclusions.

Homogeneity of the study population

Although participants were recruited from both urban and rural areas, all participants belonged to the same geographic region (Izeh County) and were covered by the same primary health care system, which follows standardized protocols for the diagnosis and management of hypertension.

Furthermore, identical inclusion criteria, sampling procedures, and data collection tools were applied to both urban and rural participants, which enhanced the comparability of the two groups.Therefore, the study population was considered relatively homogeneous in terms of health service access, diagnostic criteria, and study procedures.

Ethical considerations

Ethical considerations were observed in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. Informed consent was obtained from all participants, the necessary permissions were obtained from relevant authorities for sampling, and the confidentiality of participants’ personal information was ensured. The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Islamic Azad University of Shahrekord on 27 July 2024 and was registered in the National System of Bioethics with the ethical code IR.IAU.SHK.REC.1403.333.

Data collection

Data were collected using self-administered questionnaires under researcher supervision to clarify any questions. The following validated Persian instruments were used.

Multidimensional Scale of Perceived Social Support (MSPSS)

A 12-item scale assessing perceived support from family, friends, and significant others. Cronbach’s alpha in this study: 0.90 (total scale), > 0.70 (subscales). The validity and reliability of the Persian version were confirmed by Salimi et al. [36].

Morisky Medication Adherence Scale (MMAS-8)

An 8-item scale evaluating adherence to adherence to antihypertensive medication.

Dichotomous items [1–7] scored 1 = No, 0 = Yes, except Item 5 (reverse-coded). Item 8 is a 5-point Likert item transformed to 0–1. Missing data ≤ 2 items were handled by median substitution; >2 items were excluded. Cronbach’s alpha in this study: 0.76.

The MMAS-8 was selected due to its widespread international use, availability of a validated Persian version, comparability with prior Iranian and international studies, and suitability for community-based hypertension research. Although newer adherence instruments exist, MMAS-8 remains one of the most commonly applied tools in SEM-based adherence studies, allowing comparison with existing literature. The validity and reliability of the Persian version of the MMAS-8 have been confirmed among Iranian patient [37].

Resilience questionnaire

A 29-item instrument across five subscales (adaptability, self-management, rational empowerment, medication adherence, spirituality). Factor loadings of all items, including the “medication adherence” subscale, were > 0.40 and significant (p < 0.05), indicating statistical independence from MMAS-8. Cronbach’s alpha > 0.85 for all components.

The validity and reliability of the Persian version of this scale have been reported in Iranian populations [38].

The researcher collected demographic variables, including age, sex, marital status, education level, economic status, place of residence, and family medical history, using a researcher-designed demographic questionnaire.

Reliability and validity of the instruments were assessed using Cronbach’s alpha, composite reliability (CR), factor loadings, and average variance extracted (AVE). Cronbach’s alpha was used to assess internal consistency, with values above 0.70 considered acceptable. Composite reliability values greater than 0.70 and factor loadings above 0.40 were considered satisfactory. AVE was used to evaluate convergent validity. In addition, first-order confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) was performed, and standardized factor loadings were examined for all constructs. All factor loadings were statistically significant at the 95% confidence level. The composite reliability of the scale was 0.75 and the average variance extracted was 0.31.

License to use the MMAS-8 scale was obtained. Furthermore, we confirm that all necessary permissions and licenses for the use of all scales and questionnaires utilized in this study have been secured.

Data analysis

Data were analyzed using SPSS version 24 and AMOS version 20. Descriptive statistics, including mean and standard deviation, were used to summarize the study variables. Regression analysis, path analysis, and structural equation modeling (SEM) were performed to examine direct and indirect associations among the study variables.

Before conducting SEM, the assumptions of the analysis were assessed. Normality was evaluated using skewness and kurtosis values, which were within acceptable ranges. Sample size adequacy was considered appropriate with respect to the complexity of the proposed model and the number of estimated parameters. Multicollinearity was examined using tolerance and variance inflation factor (VIF), and no evidence of problematic multicollinearity was observed. Linearity among variables was assessed using scatterplots and correlation patterns, which supported the use of SEM.

In the SEM, social support and resilience were modeled as second-order latent variables, while medication adherence (MMAS-8 total score) was treated as an observed variable. Model fit was evaluated using standard fit indices, including the root mean square error of approximation (RMSEA), comparative fit index (CFI), incremental fit index (IFI), goodness-of-fit index (GFI), adjusted goodness-of-fit index (AGFI), normed fit index (NFI), and standardized root mean square residual (SRMR). Indirect effects were examined using bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples and 95% bias-corrected confidence intervals.

SEM Details

Assumptions of SEM

Prior to conducting structural equation modeling (SEM), the assumptions of the analysis were examined. Normality was assessed using skewness and kurtosis indices as well as multivariate normality obtained from AMOS output [39]. The sample size was considered adequate for SEM based on commonly recommended criteria suggesting a minimum sample size and an appropriate ratio of cases to estimated parameters [29, 40]. Multicollinearity among the predictor variables was evaluated using variance inflation factor (VIF) and tolerance statistics. The results showed acceptable values (Tolerance = 0.852; VIF = 1.174), indicating that multicollinearity was not a concern in this study. In addition, linearity among the study variables was examined through inspection of scatterplots of the bivariate associations, which suggested approximately linear associations.

Model specification

In the SEM analysis, social support and resilience were modeled as second-order latent variables, while medication adherence (MMAS-8 total score) was treated as an observed variable. The measurement model was first evaluated using confirmatory factor analysis (CFA), factor loadings, composite reliability, and convergent validity indices.

Model estimation

Subsequently, the structural model was estimated in AMOS using maximum likelihood estimation. Direct and indirect associations among variables were examined.

Indirect effects testing

Bootstrapping with 1,000 resamples and 95% bias-corrected confidence intervals was applied to test the significance of indirect effects [40].

Model fit evaluation

Model fit was evaluated using multiple indices, including RMSEA (< 0.08), CFI (≥ 0.90), IFI (≥ 0.90), GFI (> 0.90), AGFI (> 0.90), NFI (> 0.90), and SRMR (< 0.05) [41, 42].

Reporting of coefficients

Standardized coefficients (β) were reported in the text, whereas unstandardized coefficients (B) were presented in the tables to enhance transparency and reproducibility.

Final model selection

The final structural model was selected based on both theoretical considerations and empirical model fit. The final model demonstrated acceptable fit indices (RMSEA = 0.054, CFI = 0.91) and statistically significant structural pathways consistent with the hypothesized conceptual framework.

Direct, indirect, and total effects were estimated using structural equation modeling. Indirect effects were tested using bootstrap analysis with 1,000 resamples, and total effects were calculated as the sum of direct and indirect effects.

Discussion

The present study examined the associations among perceived social support, resilience, and medication adherence in patients with hypertension using structural equation modeling (SEM). Overall, the findings indicated that the proposed conceptual model demonstrated an acceptable fit to the data, suggesting that the empirical evidence supports the hypothesized associations among the study variables. The model fit indices indicated that the structural model provided a satisfactory representation of the observed data and supported the theoretical framework underlying the study. Direct and indirect effects of the variables were assessed using SEM, with the total effect calculated as the sum of direct and indirect contributions [43].

Demographic differences in medication adherence, perceived social support, and resilience

Demographic differences in medication adherence, perceived social support, and resilience were explored to provide a broader understanding of the psychosocial context of hypertension management. The results indicated that medication adherence did not significantly differ across most demographic variables, including place of residence, marital status, education level, living arrangement, and family history. However, a significant difference was observed according to sex, with female participants reporting slightly higher adherence scores. These findings are consistent with previous evidence suggesting that gender differences in medication adherence are often modest or inconsistent, although women may sometimes demonstrate higher adherence due to greater health awareness and more frequent interactions with healthcare services [8, 24].

The borderline association observed between socioeconomic status and medication adherence in the present study suggests that financial resources and access to healthcare services may influence adherence behaviors. Previous research has similarly highlighted the role of social and economic determinants in shaping chronic disease management and adherence to antihypertensive medication [18, 44].

With regard to perceived social support, significant differences were observed according to marital status, living arrangement, and socioeconomic status. Participants who were married or living with family members reported higher levels of perceived social support compared with those living alone. This finding aligns with the broader literature indicating that family associations and close social networks provide emotional, informational, and instrumental resources that facilitate coping with chronic diseases and improve health-related behaviors [27, 44].

Individuals with higher socioeconomic status also reported greater perceived social support, which may reflect broader social networks and better access to supportive resources. Similarly, resilience differed significantly according to socioeconomic status, with higher resilience observed among participants with greater socioeconomic resources. These findings are consistent with previous theoretical and empirical work suggesting that socioeconomic advantages can strengthen coping capacity, psychological flexibility, and adaptive responses to chronic illness [28, 45].

Direct effects of social support on resilience of HTN patients

The findings of this study demonstrated that perceived social support had a significant direct effect on resilience among patients with hypertension. This finding is consistent with previous research suggesting that supportive social associations enhance individuals’ ability to cope with stress and adapt to chronic health conditions. Namvar et al. reported that social support significantly predicts resilience among hypertensive patients, with hope acting as a mediating mechanism that strengthens individuals’ psychological resources and coping abilities [22]. Similarly, Ma et al. found that social support is positively associated with factors such as sense of coherence, physical literacy, and self-efficacy, which collectively contribute to better management of hypertension [10].

However, not all studies have reported a straightforward associations between social support and resilience. For example, Li et al. found that although both social support and resilience independently contributed to improved mental health during the COVID-19 pandemic, their interaction was not consistently significant. In some cases, high social support appeared to buffer the negative effects of low resilience, suggesting a moderating rather than purely synergistic associations [46].

Evidence from Iranian contexts also supports the importance of social support in strengthening resilience among patients with chronic conditions. Peer-support interventions and family-based supportive environments have been shown to enhance coping strategies and promote psychological adjustment among patients dealing with chronic illnesses, including hypertension [47, 48].

These findings are also consistent with theoretical perspectives emphasizing the interaction between social environments and psychological resources in health behavior. Social Cognitive Theory and stress–coping models suggest that individuals’ health behaviors are shaped not only by personal abilities but also by social contexts that provide emotional encouragement, informational guidance, and practical assistance [33, 49].

Nevertheless, a substantial body of evidence indicates that social support plays a critical role in improving adherence to antihypertensive medication. A meta-analysis by Magrin et al. demonstrated that functional social support such as emotional encouragement, practical assistance, and disease-related guidance is more strongly associated with adherence to antihypertensive medication than structural support alone [50]. Similarly, Shahin et al. confirmed a positive associations between perceived social support and medication adherence among patients with hypertension [51]. Studies conducted in various cultural contexts, including China, the United States, and Saudi Arabia, have also reported that high-quality and contextually appropriate social support is associated with improved adherence to antihypertensive medication regimens [52–54].

Direct effects of resilience on adherence of HTN patients

The results of this study indicated that resilience had a significant direct effect on medication adherence among patients with hypertension. Resilience represents an individual’s capacity to adapt to adversity, manage stress, and maintain positive health behaviors despite challenges associated with chronic illness [26, 45].

Empirical evidence supports the role of resilience in promoting adherence to antihypertensive medication recommendations. For example, Boima et al. reported that higher resilience among hypertensive patients was associated with more adaptive coping strategies, which subsequently improved adherence behaviors [55]. Similarly, Li et al. found that resilience mediated the associations between perceived autonomy support and medication adherence among older adults with hypertension, suggesting that resilience strengthens individuals’ capacity to translate supportive environments into consistent management behaviors [56].Related psychological constructs, including self-efficacy, adaptive coping, and illness acceptance, have also been identified as important facilitators of medication adherence [56–58]. These constructs overlap conceptually with resilience, as they reflect individuals’ ability to regulate health behaviors, overcome barriers, and maintain long-term engagement with treatment regimens.

Evidence from Iranian studies also supports this interpretation. Ghaderi Nasab et al. reported that factors such as disease acceptance, self-efficacy, and adaptive coping play a central role in facilitating medication adherence among patients with hypertension [57].Additionally, health literacy and illness perception have been shown to influence adherence behaviors and are closely linked to resilience-related psychological processes [59].

Indirect effect of social support on medication adherence through resilience of HTN patients

The findings of the present study suggest that resilience may also indirectly influence medication adherence by shaping how individuals perceive, seek, and utilize social support resources. Resilient individuals may be more capable of mobilizing available support systems, interpreting supportive interactions positively, and translating social encouragement into effective health behaviors.

Meta-analytic evidence indicates that functional social support, rather than mere availability of social associations, is strongly associated with adherence behaviors [50]. In addition, Shen et al. found that higher levels of social support were associated with improved medication literacy among older adults with hypertension, which in turn promoted more appropriate medication use [60].

The literature highlights several factors that may explain variability in the association between social support and medication adherence, including differences in the type of support provided (practical versus emotional), cultural expectations regarding family involvement, individual health literacy, and broader healthcare system characteristics. Within this context, resilience may function as a foundational psychological resource that enables patients to effectively mobilize and utilize social support [50, 60].

The present findings suggest that social support may influence medication adherence primarily through resilience, which is consistent with prior evidence indicating the importance of psychosocial resources in hypertension management [1] Although the direct effect of social support on medication adherence was not statistically significant, the significant indirect pathway indicates that resilience may function as an important psychological mechanism linking perceived social support to adherence behaviors in patients with hypertension [2].

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting the findings of this study. First, the cross-sectional design limits the ability to establish causal associations among perceived social support, resilience, and medication adherence. Longitudinal or interventional studies are therefore needed to clarify the temporal and causal pathways among these variables. Second, all data were collected using self-reported questionnaires, which may introduce recall bias and social desirability bias.

Third, although validated instruments were used, one subscale of the resilience questionnaire is labeled “medication adherence,” which may conceptually overlap with the outcome variable measured by the MMAS-8. Although confirmatory factor analysis demonstrated statistical separability between resilience and medication adherence constructs, some degree of conceptual proximity cannot be fully excluded. Therefore, the mediation findings should be interpreted with caution, and resilience should be understood as a broader psychological resource rather than a purely behavioral construct.

Fourth, missing data for a small number of MMAS-8 items were handled using median substitution, which may have slightly reduced variability in adherence scores. Fifth, the relatively large number of questionnaire items may have contributed to respondent fatigue, potentially affecting response accuracy; however, acceptable reliability coefficients and normality indices suggest that this effect was limited.

Finally, selection bias should be considered because participants were recruited from health centers and may represent individuals who are more actively engaged with healthcare services. In addition, the study sample was drawn from a single county in Iran, which may limit the generalizability of the findings to other populations or healthcare settings. Despite these limitations, the present study provides important insights into the psychosocial pathways linking social support, resilience, and medication adherence among patients with hypertension.

Conclusion

This study demonstrated that perceived social support significantly enhances resilience, and resilience in turn positively influences medication adherence among patients with hypertension. Although social support did not show a significant direct effect on adherence, a significant indirect effect was observed through resilience. These findings highlight the mediating role of resilience in translating social support into improved medication adherence and emphasize the importance of psychosocial mechanisms in hypertension management.
