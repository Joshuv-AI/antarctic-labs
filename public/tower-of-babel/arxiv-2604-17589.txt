Pointwise character bounds for SU ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SU}(3)
Yunfeng Zhang

Abstract
. We present a basic pointwise bound for the irreducible characters of SU ⁡ ( 3 ) \mathrm{SU}(3) and, as an application, derive new L p L^{p} bounds for these characters. Our approach is based on the descent of characters to singular sets and the cancellation in this formula.



1.
Introduction

Let
\mathrm{SU}(n)
denote the special unitary group of degree
n
.
We have the following basic pointwise bound for the irreducible characters of
\mathrm{SU}(2)
:

(1.1)

\displaystyle\left|\frac{\sin((n+1)\theta)}{\sin\theta}\right|\leq\min\{n+1,|\sin\theta|^{-1}\},\qquad n\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0},\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}.

The goal of this paper is to prove an analogous pointwise bound for the irreducible characters of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
and, as an application, derive new
L^{p}
bounds for these characters.

Bounds for characters, both pointwise and in
L^{p}
norms, are central to harmonic analysis on compact groups. For example, they arise in the study of summability of Fourier series
[
4
,
19
,
9
,
10
]
, lacunary sets
[
3
,
7
,
10
]
, and the singularity of central measures
[
12
,
11
]
. As characters form a distinguished class of spherical functions on compact symmetric spaces, their analytic behavior also serves as a model for more general spherical functions
[
5
,
16
]
. Finally, since characters are eigenfunctions of the Laplace operator on compact Lie groups, their
L^{p}
bounds shed light on the general problem of Laplacian eigenfunction bounds on manifolds
[
18
,
21
]
.

Throughout the paper, let
G
denote
\mathrm{SU}(3)
. Let
T
be a maximal torus of
G
. Let
\mathfrak{t}
be the Lie algebra of
T
, and
\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the real dual of
\mathfrak{t}
. Let
\Phi^{+}\subset i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be a positive root system for
G
.
For example, we may pick
\Phi^{+}=\{\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},-\alpha_{0}\}
; see Figure
1
. Let
\Lambda^{+}\subset i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
be the set of dominant weights. For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, let
\chi_{\mu}:G\to\mathbb{C}
be the corresponding irreducible character of
G
.
Let

\chi(\mu,H):=\chi_{\mu}(\exp H),\qquad\mu\in\Lambda^{+},\ H\in\mathfrak{t}.

Let
\rho
be the half sum of positive roots. We state our main theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
and
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle|\chi(\mu,H)|\leq C\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\min\left\{|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha\rangle|,\left|\sin\left(\frac{\alpha(H)}{2i}\right)\right|^{-1}\right\}.

For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, let
d_{\mu}=\chi(\mu,0)
be the dimension of the associated irreducible representation. Using the Weyl dimension formula
[
20
]

(1.3)

\displaystyle d_{\mu}=\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\frac{\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\rho,\alpha\rangle},

we may rewrite the inequality (
1.2
) as

(1.4)

\displaystyle|\chi(\mu,H)|\leq Cd_{\mu}\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\left(1+\left|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha\rangle\sin\left(\frac{\alpha(H)}{2i}\right)\right|\right)^{-1}.

The above inequality suggests a natural extension to spherical functions on symmetric spaces of compact type:

Conjecture 1.2
.

Let
\psi(\mu,H)
be the spherical function on an irreducible symmetric space
X
of compact type, with spectral parameter
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}\subset i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
and spatial parameter
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, normalized such that
\psi(\mu,0)=1
.
Let
W
denote the Weyl group, and let
\Phi^{+}
be the positive restricted root system for
X
, with the root multiplicity function denoted by
\Phi^{+}\ni\alpha\mapsto m(\alpha)\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
. Then

(1.5)

\displaystyle|\psi(\mu,H)|\leq C\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\left(1+\left|\langle s\mu,\alpha\rangle\sin\left(i\alpha(H)\right)\right|\right)^{-\frac{m(\alpha)}{2}}

holds for some uniform constant
C
.

The bound (
1.5
) is naturally expected from the heuristics of stationary phase. Write the symmetric space
X
as the standard quotient
U/K
, where

U
is the maximal compact subgroup of a complex semisimple Lie group
G_{\mathbb{C}}
.
Clerc gave an integral formula for the spherical function (Theorem 2.2 of
[
5
]
), namely,

\psi(\mu,H)=\int_{K_{\exp H}}e^{\mu(\mathcal{H}((\exp H)k))}\ dk,

where
\mathcal{H}
denotes the complexified Iwasawa projection which is multi-valued and holomorphic on an open dense subset
\omega
of
G_{\mathbb{C}}
,
and
K_{\exp H}=\{k\in K:(\exp H)k\in\omega\}
is an open dense subset of
K
.
Assuming that both the spectral parameter
\mu
and and the spatial parameter
H
are regular,
Clerc found that the critical points of the phase function
k\mapsto\mu(\mathcal{H}((\exp H)k))
are indexed by the Weyl group
W
, and for each
s\in W
, the eigenvalues of the corresponding Hessian are exactly of absolute values

\left|\langle s\mu,\alpha\rangle\sin\left(i\alpha(H)\right)\right|
, with
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
counted with their multiplicities. Thus (
1.5
) is the expected bound from stationary phase. However, proving (
1.5
) via stationary phase would require establishing the expected normal forms of the phase function that are locally uniform in both
\mu
and
H
which can be singular, and this seems to be a formidable task for now
[
8
,
1
,
16
,
15
]
.

What was known about Conjecture
1.2
?
Assume that
\mu
remains in a cone away from the Weyl chamber walls—more precisely, assume that there exists a constant
c>0
with
|\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle|>c|\mu|
for all
\alpha\in\Phi^{+}
. Then the bound (
1.5
) reduces to

(1.6)

\displaystyle|\psi(\mu,H)|\leq C\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\left(1+|\mu|\cdot\left|\sin\left(i\alpha(H)\right)\right|\right)^{-\frac{m(\alpha)}{2}}.

Using stationary phase,
Clerc established (
1.6
) for
H
lying in a compact set contained in the open set of regular points (Theorem 3.4 of
[
5
]
), while Marshall established (
1.6
) for
H
lying in a small neighborhood of the origin (Theorem 1.6 of
[
16
]
).
Our
Theorem
1.1
seems to be the first result establishing the full pointwise bound (
1.5
) for spherical functions on a high-rank compact symmetric space.

Our proof relies on explicit character formulas, namely the Weyl character formula and its consequences for the descent of characters to singular sets (
2.3
). The Weyl group symmetry of the characters plays an essential role in the proof.
The parabolic subgroups of the Weyl group and their cosets dictate the descent of characters to singular sets (
2.3
). A key observation is that an appropriate grouping of terms in the descent formula, indexed by cosets, produces the cancellation needed to obtain the desired bounds; see (
3.6
) and (
4.6
). Additionally, the Weyl group symmetry of both the characters and the bound (
2.2
), in both
\mu
and
H
, allows one to reduce the proof to representative configurations, thereby simplifying the analysis. The framework developed here—based on the Weyl group symmetry, parabolic subgroups, and cancellation in the descent formula—is expected to extend to more general compact Lie groups, and we defer the treatment of the general case to future work.

We now discuss several consequences of Theorem
1.1
.
For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, let
\overline{\mu}
and
\underline{\mu}
denote the maximum and minimum of

\{\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
, respectively. Note that the second largest element of

\{\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}

is always comparable to
\overline{\mu}
(in fact, it is at least
\tfrac{1}{2}\overline{\mu}
). It follows from the Weyl dimension formula (
1.3
) that

d_{\mu}\approx\overline{\mu}^{2}\underline{\mu}.

For
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, let
c(H)
denote
the product of the two largest elements of

\{|\sin(\alpha(H)/2i)|:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
. Then
\exp H
is non-central in
G
if and only if
c(H)\neq 0
.

As an immediate corollary of (
1.4
), we have:

Theorem 1.3
.

For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
and
H\in\mathfrak{t}
such that
\exp H
is non-central in
G
, we have

(1.7)

\displaystyle\frac{|\chi(\mu,H)|}{d_{\mu}}\leq Cc(H)^{-1}(\overline{\mu}\underline{\mu})^{-1}.

Proof.

Suppose the two largest elements of

\{|\sin(\alpha(H)/2i)|:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}

are attained at the positive roots
\alpha^{1},\alpha^{2}
. Then
(
1.4
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{|\chi(\mu,H)|}{d_{\mu}}\leq C\sum_{s\in W}c(H)^{-1}(|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{1}\rangle|\cdot|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{2}\rangle|)^{-1}.

As
\{|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha\rangle|:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}=\{\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle:\alpha\in\Phi^{+}\}
, at least one of

|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{1}\rangle|
and
|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{2}\rangle|

is comparable to
\overline{\mu}
, while the other is bounded below by
\underline{\mu}
. Hence

|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{1}\rangle|\cdot|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha^{2}\rangle|\geq\overline{\mu}\,\underline{\mu}
. The result follows.
∎

Bounds such as (
1.7
) have been valuable in understanding the singularity of central measures on compact Lie groups
[
12
,
11
]
. In particular, it was shown in Theorem 1.1 of
[
11
]
that

(1.8)

\displaystyle\frac{|\chi(\mu,H)|}{d_{\mu}}\leq Cc(H)^{-1}d_{\mu}^{-1/2},

which was used to characterize
L^{2}
powers of orbital measures.
Noting that
d_{\mu}\approx\overline{\mu}^{2}\underline{\mu}
, our bound (
1.7
) improves upon (
1.8
) by a factor of
\underline{\mu}^{-1/2}
.

As another application of Theorem
1.1
, we prove the following
L^{p}
bound for irreducible characters of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, we have

(1.9)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C\cdot\begin{cases}1,&0<p<\frac{8}{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\log^{3/8}(2+\underline{\mu}),&p=\frac{8}{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\underline{\mu}^{\,3-\frac{8}{p}},&\frac{8}{3}<p<3,\\[5.69054pt]
\underline{\mu}^{1/3}\log^{1/3}(2+\overline{\mu}/\underline{\mu}),&p=3,\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{\mu}^{\,1-\frac{3}{p}}\underline{\mu}^{\,2-\frac{5}{p}},&3<p<5,\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{\mu}^{2/5}\underline{\mu}\,\log^{1/5}(2+\overline{\mu}/\underline{\mu}),&p=5,\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{\mu}^{\,2-\frac{8}{p}}\underline{\mu},&p>5.\end{cases}

In
[
22
]
, we established

(1.10)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq C\cdot\begin{cases}1,&2\leq p<\frac{8}{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\log^{3/8}(2+\overline{\mu}),&p=\frac{8}{3},\\[5.69054pt]
\overline{\mu}^{\,3-\frac{8}{p}},&p>\frac{8}{3}.\end{cases}

In the “regular regime”
\overline{\mu}\approx\underline{\mu}
, the new bound (
1.9
) matches the bound (
1.10
), which is expected to be sharp in this regime and was verified to be so for
\mu=N\rho
(
N\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 1}
)
[
22
]
.
However, in the “singular regime”
\overline{\mu}\gg\underline{\mu}
, an inspection shows that (
1.9
) improves upon (
1.10
).
We expect (
1.9
) to be sharp for all spectral parameters
\mu
and all
p>0
.

Using
d_{\mu}\approx\overline{\mu}^{2}\underline{\mu}
, one readily obtains the following corollary of Theorem
1.4
:

Theorem 1.5
.

For
\mu\in\Lambda^{+}
, we have

(1.11)

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq Cd_{\mu}^{\,1-\frac{8}{3p}},\qquad p>\frac{8}{3}.

The above bound improves upon Corollary 2.4 of
[
7
]
, which established (
1.11
) up to an arbitrarily small loss in the power of
d_{\mu}
.

Finally, we note another interesting consequence of Theorem
1.4
:

Theorem 1.6
.

For any sequence
\mu_{n}\in\Lambda^{+}
with
\overline{\mu_{n}}\to\infty
and

\underline{\mu_{n}}\leq C
, we have
d_{\mu_{n}}\to\infty
while

\|\chi_{\mu_{n}}\|_{L^{p}(G)}\leq K(p,C)<\infty
for all
n
and all
p<3
.

This recovers a result of
[
17
]
, where such sequences were shown to exist for
\mathrm{SU}(n)
and later generalized to all classical compact Lie groups in
[
10
]
, with applications to lacunary sets. In contrast, our result applies uniformly to all sequences in
\Lambda^{+}
satisfying the conditions in the above theorem, going beyond the particular constructions in
[
17
,
10
]
.

Remark 1.7
.

There is a parallel theory for spherical functions on symmetric spaces of noncompact type. In fact, our method of proving Theorem
1.1
applies equally well to establishing a similar pointwise bound for spherical functions on
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{SU}(3)
which is the noncompact dual of
\mathrm{SU}(3)
. Namely, let
\varphi(\lambda,H)
be the spherical function on
\mathrm{SL}(3,\mathbb{C})/\mathrm{SU}(3)
with spectral parameter
\lambda\in\mathfrak{a}^{*}
and spatial parameter
H\in\mathfrak{a}
, normalized such that
\varphi(\lambda,0)=1
.
Let

\varphi_{0}(H)=\varphi(0,H)
.
Then

(1.12)

\displaystyle|\varphi(\lambda,H)|\leq C\varphi_{0}(H)\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\left(1+|\langle s\lambda,\alpha\rangle\langle\alpha,H\rangle|\right)^{-1}

holds for some uniform constant
C
. We note that in a recent preprint
[
2
]
, the same estimate (
1.12
) was established for spherical functions on general complex semisimple groups, by different methods.

Remark 1.8
.

It might be interesting to compare our bounds (
1.4
) and (
1.7
) with character bounds for finite groups. In
[
14
]
, it was established that there exists an absolute constant
c>0
such that for all finite quasisimple groups
G
of Lie type, irreducible characters
\chi
of
G
, and elements
g\in G
, it holds

|\chi(g)|\leq\chi(1)^{1-c\frac{\log|g^{G}|}{\log|G|}}.

Here
g^{G}
denote the conjugacy class of
g
. All these bounds reflect the principle that elements with larger conjugacy classes (i.e., less singular elements) exhibit stronger oscillation and consequently smaller character values.

2.
Preliminaries

We use the invariant inner product on the Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}
to identify

i\mathfrak{t}^{*}\cong\mathfrak{t}.

More precisely, for each
\alpha\in i\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, there exists a unique element

H_{\alpha}\in\mathfrak{t}
such that

\alpha(H)=i\,\langle H_{\alpha},H\rangle,\qquad H\in\mathfrak{t}.

Via this identification, we will suppress the distinction between

\alpha
and
H_{\alpha}
, and simply regard
\alpha
as an element of

\mathfrak{t}
.
Thus we may write

\alpha(H)=i\,\langle\alpha,H\rangle,\qquad H\in\mathfrak{t}.

Let

\Lambda=\left\{\mu\in\mathfrak{t}:\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}\right\}

be the weight lattice.
Let

\Lambda_{\text{reg}}=\left\{\mu\in\mathfrak{t}:\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha\rangle}{\langle\alpha,\alpha\rangle}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\neq 0}\text{ for }\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}\right\}

be the set of regular weights. Whatever the choice of the positive root system and thus of the set
\Lambda^{+}
of dominant weights,
we have

\bigsqcup_{s\in W}s(\Lambda^{+}+\rho)=\Lambda_{\text{reg}}.

To smooth the application of the Weyl group symmetry of the characters,
we write

\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\chi(\mu-\rho,H).

Then the Weyl character formula reads
[
20
]

\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\frac{\sum_{s\in W}(\det s)e^{i\langle s\mu,H\rangle}}{\sum_{s\in W}(\det s)e^{i\langle s\rho,H\rangle}},\qquad\mu\in\Lambda_{\text{reg}},\ H\in\mathfrak{t}.

The denominator may be factored so that we have

(2.1)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\frac{\sum_{s\in W}(\det s)e^{i\langle s\mu,H\rangle}}{\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha,H\rangle}{2}\right)}.

To make notation lighter, for all
\alpha
in the root system
\Phi
and
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, we denote

\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|:=\left|\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha,H\rangle}{2}\right)\right|.

Let
\widetilde{\Phi}=\{\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\}
be the collection of extended simple roots for
\mathrm{SU}(3)
(see Figure
1
).
We may rewrite the bound (
1.2
) using
\widetilde{\Phi}
instead of positive roots as

(2.2)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)|\leq C\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}}\min\left\{|\langle s\mu,\alpha\rangle|,\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|^{-1}\right\}.

Explicitly, the Weyl group
W
for
\mathrm{SU}(3)
equals
\{e,s_{\alpha_{0}},s_{\alpha_{1}},s_{\alpha_{2}},s_{\alpha_{1}}s_{\alpha_{0}},s_{\alpha_{0}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\}
. We will need the following lemma, which expresses the Weyl group as the product of a parabolic subgroup and a set of coset representatives.

\alpha_{2}
\alpha_{0}

Lemma 2.1

(parabolic subgroups of the Weyl group and their cosets)
.

Let
s_{\alpha}
denote the reflection across the line through the origin orthogonal to
\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}
. Let
e
denote the identity element of
W
.
For
j=0,1,2
, let
W^{j}=\{e,s_{\alpha_{j}}\}
, and
let
W_{j}=\{e,s_{\alpha_{j+1}},s_{\alpha_{j+1}}s_{\alpha_{j}}\}
, with
\alpha_{3}
understood to be
\alpha_{0}
.
Then
W=W^{j}W_{j}
for
j=0,1,2
.

Proof.

This can certainly be checked easily by hand. We refer to Section 1.10 of
[
13
]
for a general statement.
∎

For
j=0,1,2
and
X\in\mathfrak{t}
,
let

X^{j}=\mathrm{Proj}_{\alpha_{j}}X=\frac{\langle X,\alpha_{j}\rangle}{|\alpha_{j}|^{2}}\alpha_{j},

X_{j}=X-X^{j}.

Then clearly from the definition of the weight lattice
\Lambda
we have:

Lemma 2.2

(weight projection)
.

For all
\mu\in\Lambda
,
\mu^{j}\in\mathbb{Z}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}
.

For
j=0,1,2
and
X,\nu\in\mathbb{R}\alpha_{j}
, let

\widetilde{\chi}^{j}(\nu,X)=\frac{\sin(\langle\nu,X\rangle)}{\sin(\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},X\rangle}{2})}.

We have the following key “descent formula” (in the terminology of
[
6
]
for spherical functions on complex groups), which decomposes irreducible characters into sums of lower-rank characters, thereby clarifying their behavior near the alcove walls
\{H\in\mathfrak{t}:\langle\alpha,H\rangle=2\pi k\}
,
\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}
,
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. In the proof of Theorem
1.1
, a key observation is that this decomposition exhibits subtle cancellation among certain terms (see (
3.6
) and (
4.6
)).

Lemma 2.3

(character descent)
.

We have

(2.3)

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\frac{1}{\prod_{k\neq j}2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{k},H\rangle}{2}\right)}\sum_{s_{j}\in W_{j}}(\det s_{j})e^{i\langle s_{j}\mu,H_{j}\rangle}\widetilde{\chi}^{j}((s_{j}\mu)^{j},H^{j}).

Proof.

A general version for compact Lie groups was established in Lemma 2.4 of
[
22
]
. We include the proof here for completeness.
Applying Lemma
2.1
, we may rewrite the Weyl character formula (
2.1
) as

\displaystyle\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\frac{1}{\prod_{k\neq j}2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{k},H\rangle}{2}\right)}\sum_{s_{j}\in W_{j}}(\det s_{j})\frac{e^{i\langle s_{j}\mu,H\rangle}-e^{i\langle s_{\alpha_{j}}s_{j}\mu,H\rangle}}{2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},H\rangle}{2}\right)}.

Write
H=H_{j}+H^{j}
and noting that
s_{\alpha_{j}}H_{j}=H_{j}
while
s_{\alpha_{j}}H^{j}=-H^{j}
, the above formula becomes

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\prod_{k\neq j}2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{k},H\rangle}{2}\right)}\sum_{s_{j}\in W_{j}}(\det s_{j})e^{i\langle s_{j}\mu,H_{j}\rangle}\frac{e^{i\langle s_{j}\mu,H^{j}\rangle}-e^{-i\langle s_{j}\mu,H^{j}\rangle}}{2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},H\rangle}{2}\right)}.

Noting that

\displaystyle\frac{e^{i\langle s_{j}\mu,H^{j}\rangle}-e^{-i\langle s_{j}\mu,H^{j}\rangle}}{2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},H\rangle}{2}\right)}=\frac{e^{i\langle(s_{j}\mu)^{j},H^{j}\rangle}-e^{-i\langle(s_{j}\mu)^{j},H^{j}\rangle}}{2i\sin\left(\frac{\langle\alpha_{j},H\rangle}{2}\right)}=\widetilde{\chi}^{j}((s_{j}\mu)^{j},H^{j}),

the proof is finished.
∎

We need the following character bound for
\mathrm{SU}(2)
:

Lemma 2.4

(rank-one bound)
.

For
j=0,1,2
,
X\in\mathbb{R}\alpha_{j}
, and
\nu\in\mathbb{Z}\cdot\frac{1}{2}\alpha_{j}
,

|\widetilde{\chi}^{j}(\nu,X)|\leq\min\{2|\nu|/|\alpha_{j}|,\|\alpha_{j}(X)\|^{-1}\}.

Proof.

This is the same as (
1.1
), the proof of which is elementary and omitted.
∎

Combing Lemmas
2.2
and
2.4
, we obtain

(2.4)

\displaystyle|\widetilde{\chi}^{j}(\mu^{j},X)|\leq\min\{2|\mu^{j}|/|\alpha_{j}|,\|\alpha_{j}(X)\|^{-1}\},\qquad\text{for all }\mu\in\Lambda.

We now reduce the desired bound (
2.2
) to suitable cases for the spectral and spatial parameters, by using the Weyl group symmetry of this bound.

First, we have the well-known Weyl group symmetry of the characters

\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,sH),\qquad\text{for all }s\in W.

Thus both sides of the inequality (
2.2
) are invariant under the symmetry

H\mapsto sH,\qquad s\in W.

Noting that
W
acts by permuting the projective roots (i.e., roots modulo sign),
in proving (
2.2
) we may assume without loss of generality that

(2.5)

\displaystyle\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|\leq\|\langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\|\leq\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|.

Second, it is also well known that

\widetilde{\chi}(\mu,H)=(\det s)\widetilde{\chi}(s\mu,H),\qquad\text{for all }s\in W.

Thus both sides of the inequality (
2.2
) are also invariant under the symmetry

\mu\mapsto s\mu,\qquad s\in W.

Then we may also assume without loss of generality that

(2.6)

\displaystyle|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\leq|\langle\mu,\alpha_{2}\rangle|\leq|\langle\mu,\alpha_{0}\rangle|.

This implies that

(2.7)

{\delta_{1}}
that
{\delta_{1}}-\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}k_{1}
is always an even number, the above identity implies that

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(-\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},H_{1}\rangle+\frac{{\delta_{1}}\pi}{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sin\left(-\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle+\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},H^{1}\rangle+\frac{{\delta_{1}}\pi}{2}\right)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\sin\left(\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle+\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{2|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}f_{1}(H)\right)\right|.

Noting that
\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle/|\alpha_{1}|^{2}\in\frac{1}{2}\mathbb{Z}
, in the evaluation of the above quantity we may assume that

\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\in[-\pi,\pi]
and thus
|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle|\approx\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|
.
Then

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(-\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},H_{1}\rangle+\frac{{\delta_{1}}\pi}{2}\right)\right|\lesssim|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\cdot\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|+|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\cdot|f_{1}(H)|.

Using (
4.3
) and the assumption that

\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|\leq\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|
,
we get

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{1},H_{0}\rangle+\frac{{\delta_{1}}\pi}{2}\right)\right|\lesssim|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\cdot\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|.

We have verified (
4.7
) and thus finished the proof.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Assume that
|\mu|^{-1}\leq\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|\leq\|\langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\|\leq\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|
and
|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\lesssim|\mu|\approx|\langle\mu,\alpha_{2}\rangle|\approx|\langle\mu,\alpha_{0}\rangle|
. Then

|J^{1}_{2}|\lesssim\frac{1}{\|\langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\|\cdot\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|}|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that

(4.9)

\displaystyle\left|(-1)^{\delta_{1}}\widetilde{\chi}^{1}({(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1}},H^{1})-\widetilde{\chi}^{1}({(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1}},H^{1})\right|\lesssim|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|.

We have

\displaystyle\left|(-1)^{\delta_{1}}\widetilde{\chi}^{1}({(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1}},H^{1})-\widetilde{\chi}^{1}({(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1}},H^{1})\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|}\left|\sin(\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle+{\delta_{1}}\pi)-\sin(\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle)\right|

(4.10)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2}{\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|}\left|\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}(\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle+{\delta_{1}}\pi-\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle)\right)\right|.

Now compute

\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle=\frac{\langle s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle,

\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle=\frac{\langle s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle,

which together imply, using (
4.8
) and (
4.2
), that

\displaystyle\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle-\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle

\displaystyle=-2\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{0},\alpha_{1}\rangle\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}2\pi k_{1}+\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}f_{1}(H).

Using the fact that
\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}k_{1}+{\delta_{1}}
is always an even number, we then have

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}(\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle+{\delta_{1}}\pi-\langle(s_{\alpha_{2}}s_{\alpha_{1}}\mu)^{1},H^{1}\rangle)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(\frac{\pi}{2}\left(\frac{2\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}k_{1}+{\delta_{1}}\right)-\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{2|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}f_{1}(H)\right)\right|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left|\sin\left(\frac{\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle}{2|\alpha_{1}|^{2}}f_{1}(H)\right)\right|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\cdot|f_{1}(H)|

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle|\langle\mu,\alpha_{1}\rangle|\cdot\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|.

Here in the last inequality we used (
4.3
).
Applying the above estimate to (
4
), we obtain the desired estimate (
4.9
) and conclude the proof.
∎

5.
L^{p}
bounds for characters

Let
A=\{H\in\mathfrak{t}:\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\geq 0,\ \langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\geq 0,\ -\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\leq 2\pi\}
be a Weyl alcove.
Applying the Weyl integration formula
[
20
]
along with (
1.4
), we have

\displaystyle\|\chi_{\mu}\|_{L^{p}(G)}^{p}

\displaystyle=4\int_{A}|\chi(\mu,H)|^{p}\prod_{\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}}\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|^{2}\,dH

(5.1)

\displaystyle\lesssim d_{\mu}^{p}\int_{A}\sum_{s\in W}\prod_{\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}}\frac{\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|^{2}}{(1+|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha\rangle|\cdot\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|)^{p}}\,dH.

Observe that the above integrand is invariant under the extended affine Weyl group: it is invariant not only under the Weyl group action
H\mapsto sH
(
s\in W
), but also under translations by the coweight lattice

\{H\in\mathfrak{t}:\langle\alpha,H\rangle\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\}.

Consequently, without loss of generality, to obtain an upper bound for the above integral, we may replace the alcove
A
by the neighborhood

A_{0}=\left\{H\in\mathfrak{t}:\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\geq 0,\ \langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\geq 0,\ -\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\leq\tfrac{4\pi}{3}\right\}

of
H=0
in
A
, since the translates of
A_{0}
under the extended affine Weyl group already cover
A
(Figure
2
).

Figure 2.
Translates of
A_{0}
under the extended affine Weyl group;
A_{0}
is the shaded region.

For
s\in W
, let

\widetilde{a}=|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha_{1}\rangle|,\quad\widetilde{b}=|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha_{2}\rangle|,\quad\widetilde{c}=|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha_{0}\rangle|.

For
H\in\mathfrak{t}
, we introduce the coordinates

t_{1}=\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle,\quad t_{2}=\langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle.

Then
A_{0}=\left\{t_{1},t_{2}\geq 0,\ t_{1}+t_{2}\leq\frac{4\pi}{3}\right\}
, and for
H\in A_{0}
,

t_{1}\approx\|\langle\alpha_{1},H\rangle\|,\quad t_{2}\approx\|\langle\alpha_{2},H\rangle\|,\quad t_{1}+t_{2}\approx\|\langle\alpha_{0},H\rangle\|.

We aim to bound

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}(s):=

\displaystyle\int_{A_{0}}\prod_{\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}}\frac{\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|^{2}}{(1+|\langle s(\mu+\rho),\alpha\rangle|\cdot\|\langle\alpha,H\rangle\|)^{p}}\,dH

(5.3)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{A_{0}}\frac{t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{2}(t_{1}+t_{2})^{2}}{(1+\widetilde{a}t_{1})^{p}(1+\widetilde{b}t_{2})^{p}(1+\widetilde{c}(t_{1}+t_{2}))^{p}}\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2}.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be the relabeling of the set

\{|\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle|:\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}\}
such that
a\geq b\geq c
. Then for all
s\in W
, we have

\mathcal{I}(s)\lesssim\begin{cases}a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p},&0<p<\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p}\log(2+c),&p=\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{2p-8},&\frac{8}{3}<p<3,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-3}c^{-2}\log\left(2+\frac{a}{c}\right),&p=3,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{p-5},&3<p<5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5}\log\left(2+\frac{b}{c}\right),&p=5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5},&p>5.\end{cases}

Proof.

As
s
varies over
W
, the triple
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b},\widetilde{c})
runs over all permutations of the fixed set

\{|\langle\mu+\rho,\alpha\rangle|:\alpha\in\widetilde{\Phi}\}
. Observe that the integral in (
5.3
) is maximized when

(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b},\widetilde{c})=(a,b,c)\quad\text{or}\quad(b,a,c).

Thus it suffices to get the desired estimate for the integral in (
5.3
) when
(\widetilde{a},\widetilde{b},\widetilde{c})=(a,b,c)
.

Splitting
A_{0}
into
t_{1}\geq t_{2}
and
t_{1}\leq t_{2}
, we may further assume

t_{1}\leq t_{2},

since this region yields the larger contribution under the assumption
a\geq b
. Then
t_{1}+t_{2}\approx t_{2}
.

Let
M=\frac{4\pi}{3}
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}=\mathcal{I}(s)

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{0\leq t_{1}\leq t_{2}\leq M}\frac{t_{1}^{2}t_{2}^{4}}{(1+at_{1})^{p}(1+bt_{2})^{p}(1+ct_{2})^{p}}\,dt_{1}\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{M}\frac{t_{2}^{4}}{(1+bt_{2})^{p}(1+ct_{2})^{p}}\left(\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{(1+at_{1})^{p}}\,dt_{1}\right)\,dt_{2}.

The case of
0<p<3
.
We have

\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{(1+at_{1})^{p}}\,dt_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}t_{2}^{3},&0\leq t_{2}\leq a^{-1},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}t_{2}^{3-p},&t_{2}\geq a^{-1}.\end{cases}

Recall that
a\geq b\geq c>0
. We split the
t_{2}
-integral at

t_{2}=a^{-1},b^{-1},c^{-1}
to obtain

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}t_{2}^{7}\,dt_{2}+a^{-p}\int_{a^{-1}}^{b^{-1}}t_{2}^{7-p}\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle+a^{-p}b^{-p}\int_{b^{-1}}^{c^{-1}}t_{2}^{7-2p}\,dt_{2}+a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p}\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{7-3p}\,dt_{2}.

For the first three terms, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}t_{2}^{7}\,dt_{2}+a^{-p}\int_{a^{-1}}^{b^{-1}}t_{2}^{7-p}\,dt_{2}+a^{-p}b^{-p}\int_{b^{-1}}^{c^{-1}}t_{2}^{7-2p}\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{-8}+a^{-p}b^{p-8}+a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{2p-8}.

For the last term, we have

a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p}\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{7-3p}\,dt_{2}\lesssim\begin{cases}a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p},&0<p<\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p}\log(2+c),&p=\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{2p-8},&\frac{8}{3}<p<3.\end{cases}

Using
a\geq b\geq c
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}\lesssim\begin{cases}a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p},&0<p<\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{-p}\log(2+c),&p=\frac{8}{3},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-p}b^{-p}c^{2p-8},&\frac{8}{3}<p<3.\end{cases}

The case of
p=3
.
We have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{(1+at_{1})^{3}}\,dt_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}t_{2}^{3},&0\leq t_{2}\leq a^{-1},\\[4.0pt]
a^{-3}\log(2+at_{2}),&t_{2}\geq a^{-1}.\end{cases}

Then we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}t_{2}^{7}\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}\int_{a^{-1}}^{b^{-1}}t_{2}^{4}\log(2+at_{2})\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle+a^{-3}b^{-3}\int_{b^{-1}}^{c^{-1}}t_{2}\log(2+at_{2})\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}b^{-3}c^{-3}\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{-2}\log(2+at_{2})\,dt_{2}.

The last integral above may be estimated by

\displaystyle\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{-2}\log(2+at_{2})\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{-2}\left[\log\left(2+\frac{a}{c}\right)+\log(ct_{2})\right]\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim c\log\left(2+\frac{a}{c}\right),

so that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{-8}+a^{-3}b^{-5}\log\left(2+\frac{a}{b}\right)+a^{-3}b^{-3}c^{-2}\log\left(2+\frac{a}{c}\right).

Since
a\geq b\geq c
, the last term dominates, and hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\lesssim a^{-3}b^{-3}c^{-2}\log\!\left(2+\frac{a}{c}\right).

The case of
p>3
.
We have

\int_{0}^{t_{2}}\frac{t_{1}^{2}}{(1+at_{1})^{p}}\,dt_{1}\lesssim\begin{cases}t_{2}^{3},&0\leq t_{2}\leq a^{-1},\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3},&t_{2}\geq a^{-1}.\end{cases}

Hence

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}t_{2}^{7}\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}\int_{a^{-1}}^{b^{-1}}t_{2}^{4}\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle+a^{-3}b^{-p}\int_{b^{-1}}^{c^{-1}}t_{2}^{4-p}\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{-p}\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{4-2p}\,dt_{2}.

For the third term, we have

a^{-3}b^{-p}\int_{b^{-1}}^{c^{-1}}t_{2}^{4-p}\,dt_{2}\lesssim\begin{cases}a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{p-5},&3<p<5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5}\log\left(2+\frac{b}{c}\right),&p=5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5},&p>5.\end{cases}

For the remaining terms, we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{a^{-1}}t_{2}^{7}\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}\int_{a^{-1}}^{b^{-1}}t_{2}^{4}\,dt_{2}+a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{-p}\int_{c^{-1}}^{M}t_{2}^{4-2p}\,dt_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle a^{-8}+a^{-3}b^{-5}+a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{p-5}.

Using
a\geq b\geq c
, we obtain

\mathcal{I}\lesssim\begin{cases}a^{-3}b^{-p}c^{p-5},&3<p<5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5}\log\left(2+\frac{b}{c}\right),&p=5,\\[2.84526pt]
a^{-3}b^{-5},&p>5.\end{cases}

The proof is completed.
∎
