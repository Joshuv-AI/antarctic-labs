 Systems Using Planned Reservoirs
• The areas that new reservoirs are planned, shown as R1
  and R2 on the right, are in a flat area that do not have
  much elevation gain. The low point of Reservoir R2,
  where the intake/outtake would need to be, is only about
  20 meters above lake Havasu’s normal water elevation.
  The low point of R1 is about 65 meters above Lake
  Havasu’s normal water elevation.
• This would mean that a PSH system formed by connecting
  reservoir R2 with Lake Havasu would be strictly infeasible
  due to lack of hydraulic head. R1 could have enough head
  to be feasible, however at over 3 km horizontal distance
  from the lake would not have feasible L:H ratio (over 50)

 Open-Loop Systems
• There are locations within the Chemehuevi
  reservation boundaries that would have a head
  height of at least 60 meters and an L:H ratio of
  16 or below with Lake Havasu.
• These locations as identified by NLR’s geospatial
  algorithm are shown as red dots on the right.
• Most potential locations are found in the south
  central part of the reservation, where the
  highest elevations can be found. A small
  number of small systems (<15 MW capacity)
  can be found in the very northwest corner.
• All but one 6 MW system is within an area
  designated as a “nature preserve”

 Closed-Loop Systems
• Because most of the elevation difference
  available on Chemehuevi land is found within
  the mountainous areas in the south, the
  potential for a “closed-loop” reservoir using
  two new reservoirs is very similar to systems
  that would use Lake Havasu as the lower
  reservoir.
• For instance, in the potential system shown
  on the right the lower reservoir is only about
  40 meters above Lake Havasu, but is still 80
  meters below the upper reservoir while
  being three times closer than lake Havasu.

 Closed-Loop Systems
• A closed loop system would require
  building two new reservoirs instead of
  one, but would have a number of
  potential advantages:
 • A smaller distance between
 reservoirs means less land
 disturbance for the conveyance
 system between the two reservoirs.
 • Water rights would be used to fill
 reservoir but not to operate.
 • Potential for a significantly easier
 and shorter licensing process due to
 not having to use a USBR owned
 asset as part of the system.

 Takeaways

• There is low elevation difference available within the areas where water uses are
  planned, and the areas with available elevation difference are remote from
  planned water uses. This makes PSH using the planned reservoir locations or
  hydropower between reservoirs and end uses of the water impractical.
• There is potential for PSH, even up to hundreds of MW of capacity, that could be
  formed either by using lake Havasu directly as the lower reservoir, or with a closed
  loop system using two new reservoirs.
• However, such a system would require building an upper reservoir in an area that is
  currently planned as a nature preserve.
• Therefore, the feasibility of PSH on Chemehuevi land (particularly one large enough
  to act as the primary energy storage for the planned 6 km2 solar farm) is
  dependent on whether it is deemed acceptable for this area to be used.

Final Results
Presentation

 Best Systems: Open-Loop Systems
• After preparing the best potential systems given our last
 discussion, it became clear that the very best large
 systems (100 MW+) are the open-loop systems that pair
 directly with Lake Havasu, whereas systems with smaller
 capacity are better opportunities for closed-loop systems
 with two new reservoirs.
• There is one specific location (34.415,-114.36) that the
 algorithm thinks is ideal to build a reservoir to pair with
 Lake Havasu for large scale energy storage. Reservoirs
 formed by different maximum dam heights from 50 to 100
 meters give maximum generation capacities between 121
 to 528 MW at ten hours of storage.
• The largest systems are the cheapest, but costs may be
 underestimated for tall dam heights over such rough
 terrain. The reservoir formed by a 70 meter maximum
 height dam is shown on the right which may be more
 realistic and can still provide 215 MW at maximum
 generation.
                                                                  NREL | 12

 Best Systems: Closed-Loop Systems
• For smaller systems, our algorithm thinks closed-loop
 systems where two reservoirs can be placed far closer
 together (even with somewhat lower head) are better
 options. However, options that are even close to being as
 cost competitive with the larger systems on a $/kW basis
 are limited. Ultimately, three systems are the best options
 at sizes of 90, 51, and 28 MW max generating power.
• In my opinion, the largest site at 90 MW is close enough in
 size to the open-loop systems that it may make sense to
 pursue one of the open loop systems instead. Our
 algorithm believes the smallest system to be quite a bit
 more expensive on a $/kW basis. Therefore, the 51 MW
 system (shown on right) may be the most promising site
 for a smaller system. For this system, the upper reservoir
 location is the same as identified for the open-loop site
 (34.415,-114.36) and the lower reservoir is located at
    (34.416, -11435)

 Other Options Examined
• Tanked Systems: Pre-fab water tanks can be cost effective way of storing very small
  amount of water relative to open reservoirs, on the scale of tens of acre-feet, but at
  the head heights available even very small systems would require 100’s of acre feet
  of water. For reference, for a recent analysis we did on the island of Molokai in
  Hawaii for a 7 MW tanked PSH system the preferred system has a nominal gross
  head of over 1,900 feet, or over 3 times what is available here. Not likely a viable
  option on the Chemehuevi reservation.
• Distribution Generation using PSH: The viable PSH locations are located too far
  away from current and planned uses and do not have a high enough elevation
  difference from them to make electrical generation between the PSH systems and
  end uses to make sense. PSH systems should be thought of as separate
  infrastructure projects from other reservoirs/water conveyance systems for other
  uses on the reservation.
