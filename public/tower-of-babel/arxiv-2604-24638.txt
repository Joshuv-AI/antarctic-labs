k k -step nilpotent symplectic Lie algebras associated with graphs
Josefina Barrionuevo, Paulo Tirao and Sonia Vera

Abstract
. We construct families of k k -step nilpotent symplectic Lie algebras associated with graphs, extending the construction given in [ PT ] for the 2-step case. We also show that, under mild conditions on the nilpotency type, there exist symplectic Lie algebras of that type.

Introduction

Symplectic geometry is a central area of mathematics, with deep connections to physics—most notably as the geometric language of classical mechanics—as well as to differential geometry and topology. Its algebraic counterpart is given by the study of
symplectic Lie algebras
, which arise as the infinitesimal counterparts of symplectic symmetries on Lie groups. These structures provide a rich framework with important connections to representation theory, Poisson geometry, and mathematical physics.

Following the seminal work of Benson and Gordon
[
BG
]
, particular attention has been devoted to nilmanifolds, that is, compact quotients of nilpotent Lie groups. Later, Dani and Mainkar
[
DM
]
studied the family of
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebras associated with simple graphs, together with the corresponding nilmanifolds, drawing particular attention to this class. In particular,
[
PT
]
determines which of these graph-associated Lie algebras admit a symplectic form.

The general problem of deciding which nilpotent Lie algebras admit a symplectic form has been studied by many authors. Most available results concern 2-step nilpotent Lie algebras, low-dimensional cases, and filiform Lie algebras. In contrast, examples of
k
-step nilpotent symplectic Lie algebras remain scarce for
k\geq 3
.

Recently,
[
ABDGH
]
characterized nilpotent Lie algebras with a codimension-one abelian ideal admitting a symplectic structure. This family includes
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras for every
k
.

Given a simple graph
G=(V,E)
, one can associate to it a
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
. If
G
is the complete graph on
m
vertices, then
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
is the free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
m
generators. It was shown in
[
D
]
that this Lie algebra admits a symplectic form if and only if
k=2
and
m=3
.

In this paper, we extend the construction introduced in
[
PT
]
for 2-step nilpotent symplectic Lie algebras
associated with graphs to the
k
-step case.
The nilpotent Lie algebras we construct are all naturally graded.

All Lie algebras in this paper are finite-dimensional over a a field
\mathbb{K}
of characteristic zero.

1.
Some basic preliminaries

1.1.
Symplectic Lie algebras

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra of dimension
2n
.
A symplectic form on
\mathfrak{g}
is a closed
2
-form
\omega\in\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
such that
\omega^{n}\neq 0
.
If
\{x_{1},\dots,x_{2n}\}
is a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and
\{x^{*}_{1},\dots,x^{*}_{2n}\}
is its dual basis, then a closed
2
-form

\omega=\sum_{i<j}a_{i,j}\,x^{*}_{i}\wedge x^{*}_{j}

is symplectic if and only if

\omega^{n}=c\,x^{*}_{1}\wedge x^{*}_{2}\wedge\cdots\wedge x^{*}_{2n},

for some
c\neq 0
.

Remark 1.1
.

If the indices satisfy

\{i_{k}:\,k=1,\dots,n\}\cup\{j_{k}:\,k=1,\dots,n\}=\{1,\dots,2n\},

then

(1.1)

\omega=\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}\,x^{*}_{i_{k}}\wedge x^{*}_{j_{k}}

satisfies
\omega^{n}\neq 0
, provided that
a_{k}\neq 0
for all
k=1,\dots,n
.

Recall that
\omega
is closed if
d(\omega)=0
, where
d:\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\Lambda^{3}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the linear map defined by

d(x^{*}_{i}\wedge x^{*}_{j})=d^{1}(x^{*}_{i})\wedge x^{*}_{j}-x^{*}_{i}\wedge d^{1}(x^{*}_{j}),

and
d^{1}:\mathfrak{g}^{*}\to\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}^{*}
is the dual map of the bracket

[\;,\;]:\Lambda^{2}\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}.

A Lie algebra admitting a symplectic form is called a symplectic Lie algebra.

1.2.
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras

The lower central series of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is the descending filtration of ideals

\mathfrak{g}^{1}\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq\mathfrak{g}^{k}\supseteq\cdots

defined recursively by

\mathfrak{g}^{1}=\mathfrak{g}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}^{i}],\quad\text{for }i\geq 1.

Note that it satisfies

[\mathfrak{g}^{i},\mathfrak{g}^{j}]\subseteq\mathfrak{g}^{i+j},

for all
i,j\geq 1
.

The associated graded Lie algebra is

\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{g})=\bigoplus_{i\geq 1}\mathfrak{g}^{i}/\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}.

Its Lie bracket is defined by

[\overline{x},\overline{y}]=\overline{[x,y]},

for
x\in\mathfrak{g}^{i}
and
y\in\mathfrak{g}^{j}
, where
\overline{x}
and
\overline{y}
denote the classes of
x
and
y
in

\mathfrak{g}^{i}/\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}
and
\mathfrak{g}^{j}/\mathfrak{g}^{j+1}
, respectively, and
\overline{[x,y]}
denotes the class of
[x,y]
in
\mathfrak{g}^{i+j}/\mathfrak{g}^{i+j+1}
.

The Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is said to be
k
-step nilpotent if
\mathfrak{g}^{k+1}=0
and
\mathfrak{g}^{k}\neq 0
. It is said to be
naturally graded
if
\mathfrak{g}\simeq\operatorname{gr}(\mathfrak{g})
.

A Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a graded decomposition

(1.2)

\mathfrak{g}=V_{1}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k},

such that
[V_{i},V_{j}]\subseteq V_{i+j}
, is at most
k
-step nilpotent. In general, such a Lie algebra may admit different graded decompositions.

If, in addition,

(1.3)

\mathfrak{g}^{i}=V_{i}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k},

then
\mathfrak{g}
is
k
-step nilpotent and naturally graded. Such a decomposition is called a
Carnot grading
. It is unique up to isomorphism.

Remark 1.2
.

Having a Carnot grading and being naturally graded are equivalent notions.

1.3.
(Di)graphs

A (simple) graph is a pair
G=(V,E)
, where
E\subseteq\mathcal{P}^{2}(V)
.

A directed graph, or
digraph
, is a graph in which each edge is assigned a direction; that is, each edge has an origin and a terminus. Thus, a digraph is a pair
G=(V,E)
, where
E\subseteq V\times V
and, whenever
(u,v)\in E
, one has
(v,u)\notin E
.

By forgetting the directions, every digraph naturally determines a graph.

A
directed path
in a digraph from a source vertex
u
to a target vertex
v
is a finite sequence of distinct vertices
v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{k}
such that:

•

v_{1}=u
and
v_{k}=v
;

•

(v_{i},v_{i+1})\in E
for all
i\in\{1,\dots,k-1\}
.

Thus, in a directed path, one must always follow the orientation of the edges.

In this paper, we will repeatedly use the following two families of digraphs:

(1)

A tree directed from the leaves to the root.

Given a tree and a choice of a root
v
, there is a unique way to orient its edges so that, for every vertex
u\neq v
, there is a directed path from
u
to
v
.

(2)

A unicyclic graph directed centripetally.

Given a unicyclic graph (that is, a graph with exactly one cycle), one may view it as a cycle with a tree attached to some vertices of the cycle.
Each such tree is oriented as in (1) by taking its cycle vertex as the root, and the cycle itself may be given either of its two possible cyclic orientations.

1.4.
2-step nilpotent Lie algebras associated with graphs and 2-forms

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, with
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
.
The associated 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra is the quotient

\mathfrak{g}(2,G)=L_{(2)}(m)/I,

where
L_{(2)}(m)
is the free 2-step nilpotent Lie algebra and
I
is the ideal
generated by
\{[v_{i},v_{j}]:\;\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\not\in E\}
.

A basis of
\mathfrak{g}(2,G)
is

\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}\cup\{[v_{i},v_{j}]:\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E,i<j\},

and its corresponding dual basis of

is

\{v_{1}^{*},\dots,v_{m}^{*}\}\cup\{[v_{i},v_{j}]^{*}:\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\in E,i<j\}.

Note that, for these basis vectors,

and
d^{1}([v_{i},v_{j}]^{*})=v_{i}^{*}\wedge v_{j}^{*}
,
and hence the
2
-forms

(1.4)

v_{i}^{*}\wedge v_{j}^{*}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\sigma_{i,j}=v_{i}^{*}\wedge[v_{i},v_{j}]^{*}

3.
The family of
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)

Given a digraph
G
and a function
\kappa:E\to\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
, we define a nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
.
This algebra is a quotient of the
k

\mathfrak{g}(k,G)

where

3.1.
The
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra associated with a graph

Let
G=(V,E)
be a graph, with vertex set
V=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
. For
k\geq 2
, the
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra associated with
G
is the quotient

\mathfrak{g}(k,G)=L_{(k)}(m)/I,

where
L_{(k)}(m)
is the free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra on
m
generators, and
I
is the ideal generated by

\{[v_{i},v_{j}]:\{v_{i},v_{j}\}\notin E\}.

The free
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra is naturally graded:

L_{(k)}(m)=L_{1}\oplus L_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{k},

and
I
is a homogeneous ideal:

I=I_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{k},

where
I_{j}=I\cap L_{j}\subseteq L_{j}
. Hence
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
is naturally graded:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{g}(k,G)

\displaystyle=L_{(k)}(m)/I

\displaystyle=(L_{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{k})/(I_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus I_{k})

\displaystyle\cong L_{1}\oplus L_{2}/I_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus L_{k}/I_{k}

\displaystyle=V_{1}\oplus V_{2}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k}.

Moreover, this decomposition is a Carnot grading, that is,

\mathfrak{g}(k,G)^{i}=V_{i}\oplus V_{i+1}\oplus\cdots\oplus V_{k},\qquad\text{for all }1\leq i\leq k.

Remark 3.1
.

If the graph
G
has connected components
G_{1},\dots,G_{\ell}
, then

\mathfrak{g}(k,G)\simeq\mathfrak{g}(k,G_{1})\oplus\cdots\oplus\mathfrak{g}(k,G_{\ell}).

In particular, each isolated vertex of
G
corresponds to a
1
-dimensional abelian direct summand of
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
.

Proposition 3.7
.

c_{r}\neq 0
only if

\mathtt{m}(b_{r})=\mathtt{m}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}(b_{t}).

Therefore, it is enough to prove that this equality never holds for
b_{r}=v_{j}
or for
b_{r}=w_{i,j}^{l}
, with
1\leq l\leq\kappa_{i,j}
.

(1)

Suppose that

\mathtt{m}(v_{j})=\mathtt{m}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}(b_{t}).

Then

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\mathtt{m}_{j}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}_{j}(b_{t}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathtt{m}_{h}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}_{h}(b_{t}),\quad\text{for all }h\neq j.

This implies that
b_{s}=0
or
b_{t}=0
, a contradiction.

(2)

Suppose that

\mathtt{m}(w_{i,j}^{1})=\mathtt{m}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}(b_{t}).

Then

\displaystyle 1

\displaystyle=\mathtt{m}_{i}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}_{i}(b_{t}),

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\mathtt{m}_{h}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}_{h}(b_{t}),\quad\text{for all }h\neq i.

Again, this implies that
b_{s}=0
or
b_{t}=0
, a contradiction.

(3)

Suppose that

\mathtt{m}(w_{i,j}^{l})=\mathtt{m}(b_{s})+\mathtt{m}(b_{t})

for some
2\leq l\leq\kappa_{i,j}
. By Lemma
3.6
, we have
b_{s}=v_{j}
or
b_{s}=w_{i,j}^{l-1}
. In either case,

b_{s}\in\{v_{j}\}\cup\{w_{i,j}^{r}:1\leq r\leq\kappa_{i,j}\},

a contradiction.

∎

Definition 3.8
.

A
chain digraph
is a pair
(\kappa,G)
, where
G=(V,E)
is a digraph and
\kappa:E\rightarrow\mathbb{N}_{\geq 2}
is a function.
We denote its value on the edge
(v_{i},v_{j})\in E
by
\kappa_{i,j}
and its maximum value by
k
.

Definition 3.9
.

Given a chain digraph
(\kappa,G)
,
its associated Lie algebra
(\kappa,G)
is the quotient Lie algebra

\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)=\mathfrak{g}(k,G)/I_{\kappa},

where

is the ideal of
\mathfrak{g}(k,G)
given by

I_{\kappa}=\left(\bigcap_{\begin{subarray}{c}(v_{i},v_{j})\in E\end{subarray}}I_{i,j}^{\kappa_{i,j}}\right)\cap\left(V_{3}\oplus\dots\oplus V_{k}\right).

Proposition 3.10
.

Given a chain digraph
(\kappa,G)
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is
k
-step nilpotent.
A basis for
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is

B_{1}\cup\bigcup_{l=2}^{k}B_{l}^{\kappa},

where
B_{1}=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{m}\}
and

B_{l}^{\kappa}=\big\{w_{i,j}^{l}:(v_{i},v_{j})\in E\text{ and }l\leq\kappa_{i,j}\big\}.

The Lie bracket of
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is determined by the nonzero brackets

[w_{i,j}^{l},v_{j}]=w_{i,j}^{l+1},\qquad\text{if }(v_{i},v_{j})\in E\text{ and }1\leq l\leq\kappa_{i,j}-1.

Moreover,
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is a naturally graded Lie algebra, and a Carnot grading is

\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)=\langle B_{1}\rangle\oplus\langle B_{2}^{\kappa}\rangle\oplus\dots\oplus\langle B_{k}^{\kappa}\rangle.

From now on, given a chain digraph
(\kappa,G)
, the basis given in the previous proposition will be called

\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
. The
basis elements corresponding to the edge

are

and

, for
2\leq l\leq\kappa_{i,j}
.

Corollary 3.11
.

It holds that

d(v_{i}^{*})=0\quad\text{and}\quad d({w_{i,j}^{l}}^{*})={w_{i,j}^{l-1}}^{*}\wedge v_{j}^{*},\text{ for all $2\leq l\leq\kappa_{i,j}$}.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
(\kappa,G)
be a chain digraph. If
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is symplectic, then
\dim(\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G))
is even and
G
has no more edges than vertices.

Proof.

It is well known that any Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with a nondegenerated closed
2

\dim(Z(\mathfrak{g}))\leq\dim(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})

In our case, the set
S=\{w_{i,j}^{\kappa_{i,j}}:(v_{i},v_{j})\in E\}
is contained in

and
\dim(\langle S\rangle)=|E|
. Also,
\dim(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{g}^{\prime})=|V|
.

Therefore,
|E|\leq|V|
and the dimension of
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is even.
∎

5.
Symplectic Lie algebras of prescribed nilpotency type

Given a finite-dimensional
k
-step nilpotent Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, its
nilpotency type
is the
k+1
-tuple

(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k+1})
of dimensions of the successive quotients of the lower central series, i.e.,

a_{i}=\dim(\mathfrak{g}^{i}/\mathfrak{g}^{i+1}).

Observe that
a_{k+1}=0
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Given a chain digraph
(\kappa,G)
, the type
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k+1})
of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
satisfies

|\kappa^{-1}(\{l\})|=a_{l}-a_{l+1},

for all
2\leq l\leq k
.

Proof.

It follows from Proposition
3.10
that

a_{l}=|\bigcup_{i=l}^{k}B_{i}^{\kappa}|-|\bigcup_{i=l+1}^{k}B_{i}^{\kappa}|=|B_{l}^{\kappa}|.

Hence, for each
2\leq l\leq k-1
,

\displaystyle a_{l}-a_{l+1}

\displaystyle=|B_{l}^{\kappa}|-|B_{l+1}^{\kappa}|

\displaystyle=|\{w_{i,j}^{l}:(v_{i},v_{j})\in E,\;l\leq\kappa_{i,j}\}|-|\{w_{i,j}^{l+1}:(v_{i},v_{j})\in E,\;l+1\leq\kappa_{i,j}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{(v_{i},v_{j})\in E:\,l\leq\kappa_{i,j}\}|-|\{(v_{i},v_{j})\in E:\,l+1\leq\kappa_{i,j}\}|

\displaystyle=|\{(v_{i},v_{j})\in E:\,l=\kappa_{i,j}\}|

\displaystyle=|\kappa^{-1}(\{l\})|.

For
l=k
,

a_{k}-a_{k+1}=a_{k}=|B_{k}^{\kappa}|=|\kappa^{-1}(\{k\})|.

∎

Theorem 5.2
.

Given a
k+1
-tuple of natural numbers
(a_{1},a_{2},\dots,a_{k},0)
, let

If

, and

-step nilpotent Lie algebra of type

Let

.

(2)

Construct some boxes as follows:

(b)

In

\mathcal{F}
and if
(\kappa,G)\in\mathcal{F}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0)}
, then
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is symplectic.

Let
(\kappa,G)
be a chain digraph that can be formed following the previous steps. From Steps (1) and (5), the maximum value of
\kappa
is
k
, so
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is
k
-step nilpotent. It remains to prove that its type is
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0)
.

Let
(n_{1},\dots,n_{k},0)
be the type of
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
, from Lemma
5.1
and Step (1), we have

\displaystyle n_{i}-n_{i+1}

\displaystyle=|\kappa^{-1}(\{i\})|

\displaystyle=\{\text{edges with $i-2$ over points}\}

\displaystyle=a_{i}-a_{i+1},

for all
2\leq i\leq k
.
In particular, it satisfies
n_{k}=a_{k}
. So, inductively, we have
n_{i}=a_{i}
for all
2\leq i\leq k
.

It only remains to prove that
n_{1}=a_{1}
. Observe that
n_{1}
is the number of vertices in the graph
G
. Also,

G=A\cup B\cup C,

where
A=(V_{A},E_{A})
is the disjoint union of the graphs in the boxes
A_{i}
,
B=(V_{B},E_{B})
is the union of the graphs in the boxes
B_{i}
, and
C
is the graph in the box
C=(V_{C},E_{C})
.
Since
A
and
B
are forests and
C
is a union of unicyclics graphs, we have that

\displaystyle|V_{A}|

\displaystyle=|E_{A}|+|\{\text{connected components of }A\}|;

\displaystyle|V_{B}|

\displaystyle=|E_{B}|+|\{\text{connected components of }B\}|;

\displaystyle|V_{C}|

\displaystyle=|E_{C}|.

From step (2), we have

\displaystyle|V_{A}|

\displaystyle=|E_{A}|+S;

\displaystyle|V_{B}|

\displaystyle=|E_{B}|+T;

\displaystyle|V_{C}|

\displaystyle=|E_{C}|.

So,

\displaystyle|V_{G}|

\displaystyle=|V_{A}|+|V_{B}|+|V_{C}|

\displaystyle=|E_{A}|+S+|E_{B}|+T+|E_{C}|

\displaystyle=|E_{A}|+S+|E_{B}|+a_{1}-a_{2}-S+|E_{C}|

\displaystyle=|E_{A}|+|E_{B}|+|E_{C}|+a_{1}-a_{2}.

We already have proved that
a_{2}=n_{2}
, and we also know that
n_{2}=|E_{G}|=|E_{A}|+|E_{B}|+|E_{C}|
. Thus,

|V_{G}|=n_{2}+a_{1}-a_{2}=a_{1}.

Therefore, for every chain digraph
(\kappa,G)\in\mathcal{F}_{(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0)}
, the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}(\kappa,G)
is symplectic and its type is
(a_{1},\dots,a_{k},0)
.
∎
