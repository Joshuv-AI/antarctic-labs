Dataset on informal recyclable waste aggregators (‘Đồng nát’) in Hanoi, Vietnam
Authors: Thai Huyen Nguyen, Tien Tam Nguyen, Kyoko Take
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis article presents a multidisciplinary dataset documenting informal recyclable-waste collection activities carried out by Informal Recyclable Waste Aggregators (IRWA) in Hanoi, the capital of Vietnam. IRWA constitute a key component of “đồng nát” [1–3], a long-standing and highly adaptive informal recycling system that has existed for decades, well before the establishment of modern waste-management infrastructures, and continues to play a crucial role in urban recycling in cities such as Hanoi [4].The dataset was produced using combined data-acquisition methods, including socio-economic surveys, structured interviews, photographic documentation, and systematic recording of recyclable materials collected, traded, and transported [5]. Complementary spatial observations were conducted to document architectural contexts, street configurations, and landscape characteristics associated with informal collection activities in different urban settings of Hanoi [6].The dataset integrates anonymized survey tables, photographic archives, coded interview transcripts, and descriptive spatial notes organized into standardized metadata categories. Variables capture demographic information, collection practices, material types and quantities, economic transactions, mobility patterns described qualitatively, and characteristics of collection environments such as alley typologies, public-space interfaces, and street-level activity patterns.The dataset is formatted for reuse in areas such as socio-economic profiling of informal labor, urban landscape analysis, material-flow documentation, circular-economy modelling, and the development of policy frameworks related to waste management and Extended Producer Responsibility (EPR) integration in Southeast Asian cities [7].Keywords: Informal sector, Informal recyclable waste aggregator, Urban analysis, Interdisciplinary approach

Background

The initial surveys were conducted within the framework of the JEAI Recycurbs Viet research project (2017–2020) and were subsequently updated through additional field investigations [[3], [4],6,10]. An integrated approach combining spatial and artistic perspectives with sociological methods was applied to document the scale, functions, and operational patterns of informal recyclable waste collection networks in Hanoi and, more broadly, in Vietnam [[1], [4]].

The dataset adopts an interdisciplinary scope, including sociological, historical, geographical, and landscape-related information [5]. In addition to findings reported in the research book “ĐỒNG NÁT (Scavengers and scavenging) in Hanoi” [2], this dataset provides updated records for Hanoi in the context of recent changes in urban governance classifications and adjustments to urban–rural spatial boundaries. The data are organized in a structured format to support further analysis and reuse in subsequent studies of informal recycling networks [5].

Data Description

The article describes a dataset compiled from the repository associated with field surveys and sociological interviews conducted among IRWAs in Hanoi between 2016 and 2024. The dataset constitutes the raw output of these surveys, from which the analytical outputs presented in this article (maps, tables, and charts) were subsequently derived. It covers the territory of Hanoi, with a total surveyed area of 3359.8 km² and a population of 8.7176 million inhabitants at the time of data collection.1

The dataset comprises 975 individual IRWAs, each represented as a single observation, with 14 variables organized into four main categories: spatial classification (urban or rural, based on the 2025 administrative reorganization of Hanoi), socio-demographic characteristics of facility owners (gender, age, hometown, and residence), temporal characteristics (establishment period), and physical and architectural attributes (ground area, storage capacity, mezzanine availability, and building materials).

However, several operationally relevant variables are not included in the dataset. Data on facility throughput, such as the volumes of recyclable materials handled and the number of workers, could not be systematically obtained, as IRWA owners were generally reluctant to disclose economic and operational details during field surveys. Similarly, contextual indicators at the commune or ward level, including population density and waste generation figures, are not provided, as these data have not yet been updated to reflect the new administrative boundaries introduced by the 2025 reorganization.

Before 1 July 2025, Hanoi comprised 13 urban districts and 17 rural districts within the metropolitan structure, encompassing a total of 579 commune-and-ward-level administrative units. From 1 July 2025 onward, Hanoi implemented the national policy on administrative reorganization and the establishment of a two-tier local government structure introduced by the Vietnamese Government. Under this reform, the administrative system was reduced to two levels only: the provincial/city level and the commune/ward level. In practice, the district level was entirely abolished, and new communes and wards were formed through the merger of existing administrative territories. Following this reform, Hanoi now comprises 126 commune-and-ward-level administrative units, including 51 wards and 75 communes [8].

In the dataset referenced in this article, the data will therefore be updated according to two areas: urban and rural. In this classification, wards are considered urban areas, while communes are considered rural areas. The number of IRWAs recorded in the urban and rural areas respectively is 539 and 436 (Fig. 1).

Spatial distribution of recorded IRWAs in urban and rural areas of Hanoi.

The number and scale of these IRWAs have never been comprehensively surveyed. As per the surveyed IRWA owners' requests, personal information and locations are not published.

At survey points comprising IRWAs, sociological information regarding the gender, age, and hometown of the IRWA owners will be concurrently collected with spatial data, including area, surface, architectural characteristics, and nature of the construction works, as well as the surrounding landscape.

The dataset summarizes recorded IRWAs in Hanoi by area and period of establishment. Data are classified into three spatial categories within Hanoi, including the whole municipality (Hanoi total), urban and rural areas. Two types of information are provided for each category: absolute numbers and percentages.

The dataset includes 570 recorded IRWAs for which the corresponding survey information was successfully obtained, including 338 in urban areas and 232 in rural areas. This dataset provides a comprehensive and structured overview of the sociodemographic characteristics and physical conditions of IRWAs in Hanoi. By systematically documenting information on gender, age range, residence, hometown, ground area, storage capacity, mezzanine availability, and building materials, the dataset enables a multi-dimensional description of IRWAs across both urban and rural areas within the municipality.

The standardized tabular format and disaggregation by spatial category ensure clarity and comparability across variables. The inclusion of both absolute values and percentages facilitates reuse of the data for descriptive statistics, spatial analysis, and policy-oriented studies related to informal waste management systems and urban–rural differences [7].

All data are organized in a consistent structure, enabling traceability and secondary analysis. The dataset can serve as a reference source for researchers, planners, and policymakers interested in informal waste activities, small-scale facilities, and infrastructure conditions in rapidly urbanizing contexts.

Experimental Design, Materials and Methods

The procedures for data collection and aggregation used for the IRWA dataset in Hanoi follow the same overall methodology applied by the research team in previous surveys conducted in several cities across Vietnam [[5], [6]].

However, the Hanoi survey incorporates additional components aimed at documenting the architectural and spatial characteristics of IRWAs, as well as estimating their operational capacity. Specifically, during or immediately after the face-to-face interview with the IRWA owner, a member of the survey team conducted an on-site architectural and spatial assessment focusing on building materials, ground area, storage spaces, the presence of a mezzanine, and other relevant functional spatial configurations [5].

In the standardized questionnaire, page 1 is dedicated to the survey form, while preliminary spatial observations were recorded on page 2 through on-site measurements and hand-drawn sketches (Fig. 12). Photographic documentation was also used to complement these records. The collected materials were subsequently used by the surveyor to produce facade and plan drawings that accurately represent the architectural and spatial configuration of each IRWA, including corresponding dimensions (Fig. 8, Fig. 9, Fig. 10, Fig. 11).

In addition, the IRWA dataset presented in this article has been updated to reflect the recent reorganization of administrative units and the establishment of a two-tier local government structure [8]. Under this administrative reform, the district level is no longer recognized as an official tier of governance. Consequently, the research team chose to represent urban–rural distinctions based on the administrative status of wards and communes. In this classification, all ward-level units are considered urban areas, while commune-level units are categorized as rural areas. This logic was applied consistently in preparing the analysis-ready dataset [9] and in producing spatial visualizations using QGIS 3.38 Grenoble.

Limitations

Data on informal waste collectors and IRWAs frequently experience fluctuations and changes due to the flexible nature of the network. It should be noted that the data presented within the scope of this article were collected, updated, and processed the last in October 2024. Since then, there may have been certain fluctuations and changes.
