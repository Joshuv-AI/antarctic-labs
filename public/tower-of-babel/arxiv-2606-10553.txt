Kernel theorems for rigidly-compactly generated ∞ \infty -categories
Giovanni Rossanigo

Abstract
. We prove two representability results for rigidly-compactly generated ∞ \infty -categories and functors between them. The first one represents contravariant linear functionals out of a category of perfect objects with values in a category of (pseudo)-coherent objects in terms of (pseudo)-coherent objects. The second one represents covariant functionals out of coherent objects with values in a category of coherent objects in terms of perfect objects. The techniques used belong to the realm of “functional analysis” of presentable stable categories and ultimately depend on the interaction between three notion of finiteness, namely compactness, dualizability and coherence. These results apply to 𝔼 ∞ \mathbbm{E}_{\infty} -ring spectra, quasi-proper maps of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes and certain spectral algebraic spaces. We also reformulate Grothendieck duality in terms of internal left adjoints.

1.
Introduction

Many of the stable categories arising in homotopy theory and derived algebraic geometry are
rigidly-compactly generated
: they are compactly generated stable categories with a compatible symmetric monoidal structure such that the monoidal unit is compact and compact objects are dualizable.
Typical examples include the
\infty
-category of spectra
Sp
, module categories over connective
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-rings, and the derived
\infty
-category
\text{QCoh}(X)
of quasi-coherent sheaves on a quasi-compact quasi-separated scheme
X
.
In such contexts it is fruitful to regard compact objects as the

“finite”
part of the theory, while various
t
-structures single out further finiteness conditions (namely, almost compactness and bounded almost compactness in homotopy theory and pseudo-coherence and coherence in algebraic geometry).

A fundamental input of rigidly-compactly generated categories is that
colimit-preserving
linear functors are controlled by
kernels
.
To be concrete, let
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
denote the
\infty
-category of small idempotent-complete stable
\infty
-categories and equip it with the symmetric monoidal structure constructed by Blumberg, Gepner, and Tabuada in
[
8
]
and recall that a commutative algebra
A\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
is rigid if and only if every object is dualizable.
Then the main
“kernel theorem”
asserts that for every pair
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
of small idempotent-complete
\mathcal{A}
-modules, the
\mathcal{A}
-linear Yoneda embedding identifies
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{op}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{N})
with
\mathcal{A}
-linear exact functors
\mathcal{M}\to\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N})
; here
Ind
denotes the ind-completion.
The goal of this paper is to establish two
finitary
kernel theorems that refine the above equivalence when the source is restricted to
“finite objects in the
t
-structure”
.

These finite objects are defined for
tor-finite categories
and the finitary kernel theorems are statements about functors between them.
To be concrete, a tor-finite category is a rigidly-compactly generated category
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
equipped with a
t
-structure generated by a set of compact objects
S_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
.
This
t
-structure is required to be accessible, compatible with filtered colimits, right and left complete, and since for this data there exists a notion of
finite tor-dimension
one requires that compact objects are of finite tor-dimension, so that tensoring with compacts has uniformly bounded
t
-exactness properties.
On the side of morphisms, a functor between tor-finite categories is just a rigid functor which is right
t
-exact and has a right
t
-exact up to a shift right adjoint.

As said before, in a tor-finite category
\mathcal{A}
one has well-behaved notions of
pseudo-coherence
and
coherence
.
We denote by
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
and
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A})
the corresponding full
subcategories.
Pseudo-coherent objects are defined to be the connective objects in the
t
-structure whose coconnective truncation defines a compact object in the coconnective part, and coherent objects are simply the bounded pseudo-coherent objects.
Under mild assumptions, these objects relate to Neeman (bounded) pseudo-compact objects studied in
[
23
, Definition 0.20]
.

As the Grothendieck–Neeman duality applies to rigid functors that have a compact object-preserving right adjoint (for us the
quasi-perfect
functors), our finitary kernel theorems apply to functors of tor-finite categories that have a pseudo-coherent object-preserving right adjoint, that is, to
quasi-proper
functors in the terminology (which we adopt) of Lipman-Neeman
[
18
, Page 3]
.
In particular, we prove the first finitary kernel theorem, which asserts that, for a quasi-proper functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
, the exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functors on compact objects with pseudo-coherent (respectively coherent) values are represented by pseudo-coherent (respectively coherent) kernels in
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 1.0.1

(Functors out of
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}
)
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a quasi-proper functor and assume that
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
is a finite generating set.
Assume furthermore that the family
(\text{ev}_{s})_{s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}
detects coconnective objects.
Then there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})),\qquad\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A}))

induced by the
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding.

In the statment, the clause
“
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
is a finite generating set”
forces certain projective classes generated by
S_{\text{fin}}
and
S_{\mathcal{B}}
to agree, even if the
t
-structure geneated by them do not agree.
It constitutes the main ingredient of the proof and, in applications, it ultimately reduces to produce a finite collection of compact connective generators (which are not required to compactly generate the given
t
-structure).

A complementary problem is to represent exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functors defined on coherent objects of
\mathcal{B}
.
Here the appropriate representing objects are no longer coherent kernels, but rather compact kernels, and one must use the dual
A^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding.
The obstruction is that
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})
is usually not generated, as a stable category, by compact objects alone.
We therefore introduce a descent condition designed to reduce the representability question to a setting where coherence and compactness coincide.
Concretely, we define morphisms
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{R}
of
universal descent
to a
regular
tor-finite category
\mathcal{R}
.
Regularity is easy to explain, since it simply reduces to
\mathcal{R}^{\omega}=\text{Coh}(\mathcal{R})
.
Universal descent, instead, is a technical assumption that produces an explicit finite-stage retract
of the identity, allowing one to descend many properties from the target to the source.
In particular, it allows us to prove the following finitary kernel theorem, which asserts that, for a quasi-proper functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
, the exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functors on coherent objects with coherent values are represented by compact kernels.

Theorem 1.0.2

(Functors out of
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})
)
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be quasi-proper and assume that
\mathcal{B}
admits a morphism of universal descent
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{R}
to a regular category such that there exists a finite generating set
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{R}}
for which
(\text{ev}_{r})_{r\in S_{\mathcal{R}}}
detects coconnective objects on
\mathcal{A}
.
Then there exists an equivalence of categories

(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}(\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B}),\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A}))

induced by the dual
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding.

These formal results have applications which the reader can find in
Section 5
.
The first one is in the realm of
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-rings.

Corollary 1.0.3
.

Let
f:A\to B
be a map between connective
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-rings and assume that
\mathcal{B}
is an almost compact
\mathcal{A}
-module.
Then there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(B)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(A)}(\text{Perf}(B)^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(A)),\qquad\text{Coh}(B)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(A)}(\text{Perf}(B)^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(A))

induced by the
\text{Perf}(A)
-linear Yoneda embedding.

The second application is to schemes, and it provides the following generalization of
[
23
, Example 0.7]
.
To state it, let us denote by
\text{Perf}(-)\subseteq D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(-)\subseteq D^{-}_{\text{coh}}(-)
the stable categories of perfect, bounded with coherent cohomology and bounded above with coherent cohomology complexes, respectively. Then:

Corollary 1.0.4
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a proper map and assume that
Y
is noetherian.
Then there are equivalences of categories

D^{-}_{\text{coh}}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},D^{-}_{\text{coh}}(Y)),\qquad D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(Y)).

induced by the
\text{Perf}(Y)
-Yoneda embedding.
If furthermore
X
admits a quasi-perfect regular alteration, then there is an equivalence of categories

\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}}\to\Fun_{\text{Perf}(Y)}^{\text{ex}}(D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(X),D^{b}_{\text{coh}}(Y))

induced by the dual
\text{Perf}(Y)
-Yoneda embedding.

A more general result may be found in
Section 5
.

We also prove a representability result for quasi-proper morphisms between arbitrary quasi-compact quasi-separeted schemes.
Still in the realm of algebraic geometry, we also prove a representability result for spectral algebraic spaces.

Corollary 1.0.5
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of finite cohomological dimension of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces which is proper and locally almost of finite presentation.
Assume also that
Y
is noetherian.
Then there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(Y)),\qquad\text{Coh}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(Y)).

induced by the
\text{Perf}(Y)
-Yoneda embedding.

2.1.
Remarks on commutative algebra in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}

We begin with a list of observations.
Most of the material appears in
[
20
, Section 4.8]
, in
[
4
, Section 4]
, in
[
14
, Chapter I.1]
and in
[
15
, Section 4]
.

Remark 2.1.1
.

Recall that a category
\mathcal{C}
is presentable if it is accessible (that is, there exists some small regular cardinal
\kappa
such that
\mathcal{C}\simeq\text{Ind}_{\kappa}(\mathcal{D})
is the ind-completion of a small category
\mathcal{D}
) and admits small colimits.
Let
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}
denote the category of presentable categories and colimit-preserving functors (or equivalently, left adjoints).
Lurie constructed a symmetric monoidal structure on
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}
characterized by the following universal property: given two presentable categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, their Lurie tensor product is a presentable category
\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D}
with a functor
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D}
such that for every presentable category
\mathcal{E}
, precomposition with it induces an equivalence

\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})\to\Fun^{\text{L},\text{L}}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}).

Here the superscript
\text{L},\text{L}
denotes the full subcategory spanned by those functors
\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{E}
which preserve colimits separately in each variable.
It follows from this universal property that the symmetric monoidal structure is also closed. To be precise, for every pair of presentable categories
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{D}
, there exists a natural equivalence

\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{C}\otimes\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})\simeq\Fun^{\text{L},\text{L}}(\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E})\simeq\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{C},\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{D},\mathcal{E}))

in
\mathcal{E}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}
, so that
\Fun^{\text{L}}(-,-)
exhibits the internal hom.
Since
\Fun^{\text{L}}(\text{Spc},\mathcal{C})\simeq\mathcal{C}
, it follows that the category of spaces
Spc
is the neutral element for the Lurie tensor product.

We will need a stable version of this observation.

Remark 2.1.2
.

Let
Sp
denote the category of spectra, defined as the colimit
\text{Sp}=\colim(\text{Spc}_{*}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\text{Spc}_{*}\xrightarrow{\Sigma}\dots)
in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}
, or dually, as the limit
\text{Sp}\simeq\lim(\dots\xrightarrow{\Omega}\text{Spc}_{*}\xrightarrow{\Omega}\text{Spc}_{*})
in
\text{Pr}^{\text{R}}
, the category of presentable categories and right adjoints.
Here
\text{Spc}_{*}
denotes the category of pointed spaces, whereas
\Sigma
and
\Omega
denote the suspension and loop functors, respectively.
Adjoining a point to a space and then
“infinitely suspending it”
produces a functor
\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:\text{Spc}\to\text{Sp}
.
It follows that a presentable category
\mathcal{C}
is stable if and only if the canonical map
\text{id}_{\mathcal{C}}\otimes\Sigma^{\infty}_{+}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}\otimes\text{Sp}
is an equivalence.
In particular, since
Sp
is a stable category, the inverse of the equivalence
\text{Sp}\to\text{Sp}\otimes\text{Sp}
makes
Sp
into a commutative algebra of
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}
.
It follows that the category of stable presentable categories and left adjoints can be realized as
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}\simeq\text{Mod}_{\text{Sp}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}})
and that taking spectrum objects
-\otimes\text{Sp}:\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}\to\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
defines a symmetric monoidal functor.

Remark 2.1.3
.

Let
\text{Cat}^{\text{st}}
denote the category of small stable categories and exact functors.
Let
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
denote the full subcategory of
\text{Cat}^{\text{st}}
spanned by the idempotent-complete stable categories.
Then the ind-completion functor
\text{Ind}:\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}\to\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
induces an equivalence between
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
and the subcategory
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
of
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
spanned by the compactly generated stable categories and by the left adjoint functors preserving compact objects.
The inverse is given by taking compact objects
(-)^{\omega}:\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
.
Now the symmetric monoidal structure on
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
restricts to a symmetric monoidal structure on
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
.
In particular,
[
4
, Proposition 4.4]
implies that the above equivalence can enhanced to a symmetric monoidal equivalence.
More precisely, the tensor product of
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
is given by a small idempotent-complete stable category
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}
which is the recipient of the universal functor
\mathcal{A}\times\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
which is exact in each variable.
In other words, there exists an equivalence

\Fun^{\text{ex}}(\mathcal{A}\otimes\otimes\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}(\mathcal{A},\Fun^{\text{ex}}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}))

of categories for all
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C}\in\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
.

We also remind the relevant features of commutative algebra objects and modules.

Remark 2.1.4
.

It follows by construction that a commutative algebra object in

Let
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})

and regard it as a symmetric monoidal category with the induced relative tensor product

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}}(-,-)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Fun^{\text{ex}}(-,-)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Fun_{\mathcal{A}}(-,-)}}

where

denotes the category of
\mathcal{A}
-linear functors. Objects of

\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})

2.2.
The linear Yoneda embedding

Following
[
15
, Section 4.2]
we now recall the construction of the enriched Yoneda embedding.

Construction 2.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be a commutative algebra and let
\mathcal{M}\in\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be an
\mathcal{A}
-module. Let
m\in\mathcal{M}
and consider the action functor
-\otimes m:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{M}
.
By definition this functor preserves finite colimits and hence it admits an ind-right adjoint
\hom_{\mathcal{M}}(m,-):\mathcal{M}\to\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
.
The functoriality of these cosntruction produces a functor

\text{\char 72\relax}_{\mathcal{A}}:\mathcal{M}\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{op}},\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A}))

which is given on objects by
m\mapsto\hom_{\mathcal{M}}(-,m)
.

In general the above functor is not fully-faithful; dualizability is exactly what is needed.

Definition 2.2.2
.

A commutative algebra in
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
is called
rigid
if every object in it is dualizable.
We let
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
denote the full subcategory of
\text{CAlg}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
spanned by the rigid commutative algebras.

We recall the following folkore result.

Lemma 2.2.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be a rigid commutative algebra.

(1)

Taking duals defines a symmetric monoidal equivalence
(-)^{\vee}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\text{op}}
.

(2)

Let
\mathcal{M}\in\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a presentable
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-module. Then the action of
\mathcal{A}
on
\mathcal{M}
restricts to the full subcategory
\mathcal{M}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
of compact objects.

Proof.

Consider point
(1)
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is rigid, every object
a\in A
admits a (chosen) dual
a^{\vee}
together with evaluation and coevaluation maps
\text{ev}_{a}:a\otimes a^{\vee}\to\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}
and
\text{coev}_{a}:\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\to a^{\vee}\otimes a
satisfying the triangle identities.
Define the functor
(-)^{\vee}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}^{\text{op}}
by taking duals. Since for dualizable objects there are canonical equivalences
(a\otimes b)^{\vee}\simeq b^{\vee}\otimes a^{\vee}\simeq a^{\vee}\otimes b^{\vee}
, the functor
(-)^{\vee}
is symmetric monoidal.
Moreover,
(-)^{\vee}
is adjoint to itself: the unit
\eta_{a}:a\to(a^{\vee})^{\vee}
and counit
\varepsilon_{a}:(a^{\vee})^{\vee}\to a
are the usual double-dual maps.
Dualizability implies
\eta_{a}
(equivalently
\varepsilon_{a}
) is an equivalence for every
a
, hence
(-)^{\vee}
is an equivalence of symmetric monoidal categories.

Consider point
(2)
.
Fix
a\in\mathcal{A}
. The action endofunctor
a\otimes-:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{M}
preserves colimits (because the
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-action does), hence is a left adjoint.
Since
a
is dualizable in
\mathcal{A}
, the functor
a\otimes-
has right adjoint
a^{\vee}\otimes-
.
In particular,
a^{\vee}\otimes-
preserves filtered colimits (indeed it preserves all colimits), so
a\otimes-
preserves compact objects.
In particular, the
\mathcal{A}
-action restricts to
\mathcal{M}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{M}
.
∎

The following result has been proved many times in the literature.
We recall the proof for completeness.

Lemma 2.2.4
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be a rigid algebra and let
\mathcal{M}\in\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be an
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-compactly generated module.
Then
\mathcal{M}
is dualizable, and the dual can be identified with
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\simeq\text{Ind}((\mathcal{M}^{\omega})^{\text{op}})
.

Proof.

Set
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}=\text{Ind}((\mathcal{M}^{\omega})^{\text{op}})
.
First of all, point
(2)
of
2.2.3
implies that the
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-action on
\mathcal{M}
restricts to an
\mathcal{A}
-action on the small idempotent-complete stable category
\mathcal{M}^{\omega}
.
Hence
(\mathcal{M}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}
is canonically an
\mathcal{A}
-module as well, and therefore
\text{Ind}((\mathcal{M}^{\omega})^{\text{op}})
inherits a canonical
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-module structure.
In particular,
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\in\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
To prove dualizability of
\mathcal{M}
with dual
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}
, it suffices to construct, for every module
\mathcal{B}\in\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
, a natural equivalence

\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{B}\simeq\Fun^{\text{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B}),

where
\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}
denotes the relative Lurie tensor product.
Because
\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{st}})
is closed symmetric monoidal, for every
\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{B}
there is a natural equivalence

(1)

\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee},\;\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})).

Since taking opposites is compatible with
\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})
-linearity, there is a natural equivalence

\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}}.

Plugging this into (
1
) and using the closed structure again (and currying-uncurrying) gives a chain of natural equivalences

\displaystyle\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{B},\mathcal{C})

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee},\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}},\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{C}).

Yoneda then implies that
\mathcal{M}^{\vee}\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\mathcal{B}\simeq\Fun^{\mathrm{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(M^{\vee},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}}
.
By using
2.1.5
twice it follows that

\Fun^{\text{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M}^{\vee},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}}\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}}((\mathcal{M}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{B}^{\text{op}})^{\text{op}}\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}}((\mathcal{M}^{\omega}),\mathcal{B})\simeq\Fun^{\text{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\mathcal{M},\mathcal{B})

thus concluding the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.2.5
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be a rigid algebra and let
\mathcal{M},\mathcal{N}\in\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
be two
\mathcal{A}
-modules. Then there exists a natural equivalence

\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{op}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{N})\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M},\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N}))

in
\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.

Proof.

There are equivalences

\displaystyle\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{op}}\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M}^{\text{op}})\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\simeq\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M})^{\vee}\otimes_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N})

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun^{\text{L}}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Ind}(\mathcal{M}),\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{M},\text{Ind}(\mathcal{N})).

Indeed the first one follows since
\text{Ind}:\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})\to\text{Mod}_{\text{Ind}(\mathcal{A})}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
is symmetric monoidal, the second one by
2.2.4
since
\mathcal{A}
is rigid, the third one by definition of internal hom and the last one by
2.1.5
.
∎

2.3.
Grothendieck duality

A central feature of rigidly-compactly generated categories and of functors between them is that Grothendieck–Neeman duality admits a clean axiomatic formulation in this setting.
One such formulation is due to Balmer, Dell’Ambrogio, and Sanders
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
.
We will now recast this duality in the language of
(\infty,2)
-categories, which we will henceforth refer to simply as
2
-categories.

Remark 2.3.1
.

To work
2
-categorically with presentable stable categories, one uses the standard
Cat
-enriched enhancement: for
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
the mapping category is

\underline{\Hom}_{2\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})=\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}),

and composition is induced by composition of functors.
More intrinsically (in the sense of complete Segal objects, see
[
14
, Chapter I.1, 6.1.8]
), one may package this as an
2
-category
2\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
whose underlying
1
-category
\iota_{1}2\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
is the usual symmetric monoidal
1
-category of presentable stable categories and colimit-preserving functors, and whose hom-
1
-categories are
\Fun^{\text{L}}(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B})
.
In particular, adjunctions internal in this
2
-category coincide with ordinary adjunctions of functors.
Finally, the Lurie tensor product of presentable categories upgrades (functorially) to this
2
-categorical enhancement, yielding a symmetric monoidal
2
-category structure on
2\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
.

Every
2
-category supports the notion of
“internal left adjoint”
, bootstrapped from the usual notion of adjunction of categories.
We now specialize it to modules in
2\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
.

Definition 2.3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra.
Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a map in
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{B}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
. We will say that
f^{\text{L}}
is an
internal left adjoint
if its right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
preserves colimits and the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{B}\otimes\mathcal{N}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{M}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{N}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{id}_{\mathcal{B}}\otimes f^{\text{L}}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{act}_{\mathcal{M}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\text{act}_{\mathcal{N}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{f^{\text{L}}}}

is horizontally right adjointable.

In other terms, the functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is an internal left adjoint if and only if the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
preserves colimits and the canonical right-lax
\mathcal{B}
-module structure
\text{act}_{\mathcal{M}}(b,f^{\text{R}}(n))\to f^{\text{R}}(\text{act}_{\mathcal{M}}(b,n))
is an equivalence for every
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
n\in\mathcal{N}
.

We now procede towards Grothendieck–Neeman duality.

Remark 2.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra and let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be an internal left adjoint. Then
f^{\text{R}}
is a
\mathcal{B}
-module map. Furthermore,
f^{\text{R}}
admits a right adjoint
f^{(1)}
which has the structure of a right-lax
\mathcal{B}
-module structure. This lax structure can be constructed explicitly as

\text{act}_{\mathcal{N}}(b,f^{(1)}(m))\to f^{(1)}f^{\text{R}}(\text{act}_{\mathcal{N}}(b,f^{(1)}(m)))\simeq f^{(1)}(\text{act}_{\mathcal{M}}(b,f^{\text{R}}f^{(1)}(m)))\to f^{(1)}(\text{act}_{\mathcal{M}}(b,m))

for
b\in\mathcal{B}
and
m\in\mathcal{M}
. Here the first map and last map are given by the unit and counit of
f^{\text{R}}\dashv f^{(1)}
, respectively.

Remark 2.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{M}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra and let
\mathcal{N}\in\text{CAlg}_{\mathcal{M}/}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be an algebra object under
\mathcal{M}
.
Under the equivalence
\text{CAlg}_{\mathcal{M}/}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})\simeq\text{CAlg}(\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{M}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}))
the category
\mathcal{N}
inherits the structure of a
\mathcal{M}
-module. In particular, if
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is the structure map, then the
\mathcal{M}
-module structure
\mathcal{M}\times\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}
on
\mathcal{N}
is given by
(m,n)\mapsto f^{\text{L}}(m)\otimes_{\mathcal{N}}n
.

Remark 2.3.5
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a map of commutative algebras in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
which is an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules. Then the functor
f^{\text{R}}
is colimit preserving and
\mathcal{M}
-linear and the right adjoint
f^{(1)}
has a right lax
\mathcal{M}
-module structure.
Consider now the equivalences

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathcal{M}}(f^{\text{R}}f^{(1)}(x)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}y,x\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}y)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{M}}(f^{\text{R}}(f^{(1)}(x)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}f^{\text{L}}(y)),x\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}y)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{N}}(f^{(1)}(x)\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}f^{\text{L}}(y),f^{(1)}(x\otimes_{\mathcal{N}}y))

where the first one follows by linearity of
f^{\text{R}}
and the second one by adjunction. If
\varepsilon:f^{\text{R}}f^{(1)}\to\text{id}_{\mathcal{M}}
is the counit of
f^{\text{R}}\dashv f^{(1)}
, then the object
\varepsilon_{x}\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}\text{id}_{y}
produces a canonical morphism

f^{(1)}(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{N}}f^{\text{L}}(-)\to f^{(1)}(-\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}-).

The Grothendieck-Neeman duality is about understanding this morphism.

Definition 2.3.6
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a map of commutative algebras in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
which is an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules. We will say that
Grothendieck–Neeman duality holds for

f^{\text{L}}
if the canonical map
f^{(1)}(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{N}}f^{\text{L}}(-)\to f^{(1)}(-\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}-)
is an equivalence.

We have then the following
2
-categorification of Grothendieck-Neeman duality.

Theorem 2.3.7
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a map of commutative algebras in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
which is an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules. Then the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
is an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules if and only if Grothendieck–Neeman duality holds.

Proof.

Assume that
f^{\text{R}}
is an internal left adjoint. Then
f^{(1)}
is
\mathcal{M}
-linear. Since the lax structure on
f^{(1)}
is given by the canonical map
f^{(1)}(-)\otimes_{\mathcal{N}}f^{\text{L}}(-)\to f^{(1)}(-\otimes_{\mathcal{M}}-)
the claim follows.
Conversely, if this map is an equivalence, to show that
f^{\text{R}}
is an internal left adjoint it suffices to show that
f^{(1)}
preserves colimits.
Since
f^{\text{L}}
is colimit preserving, evaluating at the unit in the first argument shows that
f^{(1)}
is colimit preserving.
∎

The following observations are a translation of this language for rigidly-compactly generated categories.

Remark 2.3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra and assume that
\mathcal{B}
is generated by the dualizable objects.
Then by
[
24
, Corollary 3.8]
every right-lax
\mathcal{B}
-linear functor is
\mathcal{B}
-linear.
Therefore a
\mathcal{B}
-module map
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
is an internal left adjoint if and only if its right adjoint is colimit preserving.

Example 2.3.9
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a morphism of commutative algebras in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
.
Assume that
\mathcal{M}
and
\mathcal{N}
are rigidly-compactly generated.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
, being symmetric monoidal, preserves dualizable objects, thus it preserves compact objects.
But every functor preserving compact objects between compactly-generated categories has a filtered colimit preserving right adjoint.
In the stable case this implies that the right adjoint preserves colimits.
Hence
2.3.8
implies that
f^{\text{L}}
is an internal left adjoint.
To spell it out: every functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
is an internal left adjoint in
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{M}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
.

Grothendieck–Neeman duality asks the functor
f^{\text{R}}
to be an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules.
Once again,
2.3.8
implies that
f^{\text{R}}
is an internal left adjoint if and only if the right adjoint
f^{(1)}
preserves colimits (and thus it has a further right adjoint).
Since
f^{(1)}
is exact, this is equivalent to
f^{(1)}
preserving filtered colimits, which in turn is equivalent to
f^{\text{R}}
preserving compact objects.
This is Neeman’s criterion in
[
3
, Theorem 1.7]
.
Notice that if this is the case, the functor
f^{\text{L}}
admits a left adjoint.

Definition 2.3.10
.

Let
p^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
be a map of commutative algebras in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
which is an internal left adjoint of
\mathcal{M}
-modules. The object
\omega_{p}=p^{(1)}(\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{M}})
is called the
dualizing object of

p^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{M}\to\mathcal{N}
.

3.
Pseudo-coherent and coherent objects

3.1.
Right-complete presentable
t
-structures

Recall that a
t
-structure on a presentable stable category is accessible if the connective aisle is presentable, and it is compatible with filtered colimits if the coconnective aisle is closed under filtered colimits.

Definition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category equipped with a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})
. We will say that the
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})
is
right-complete presentable
if it is accessible, compatible with filtered colimits and right complete.
We will furthermore say that the
t
-structure is
excellent
if it is also left-complete.

Lemma 3.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category with accessible
t
-structure. Then the following are equivalent.

(1)

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
is compatible with filtered colimits.

(2)

The functor
i_{\leq 0}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\to\mathcal{C}
preserves filtered colimits.

(3)

The functor
i_{\leq 0}\tau_{\leq 0}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
preserves filtered colimits.

(4)

The functor
i_{\geq 0}\tau_{\geq 0}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
preserves filtered colimits.

(5)

The functor
\tau_{\geq 0}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
preserves filtered colimits.

These equivalent conditions imply the following.

(6)

The functor
i_{\geq 0}:\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\to\mathcal{C}
preserves compact objects.

(7)

The functor
\tau_{\leq 0}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
preserves compact objects.

Furthermore,

(a)

If
\mathcal{C}
is compactly-generated, then so is
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
(with compact objects being retracts of objects of the form
\tau_{\leq 0}c
for
c\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
). In this case, the above conditions are equivalent to
\tau_{\leq 0}
preserving compact objects.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
are compactly-generated, then the above conditions are equivalent to
i_{\geq 0}
preserving compact objects.

Proof.

The equivalence
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
is by definition.
The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(3)
follows since
\tau_{\leq 0}
is a left adjoint whereas the converse
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
follows since the
t
-structure is accessible. A similar argument (coupled with the fact that also
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is presentable) shows the equivalence
(4)\Leftrightarrow(5)
.
To conclude, note that
(2)\Leftrightarrow(5)
follows by considering the cofibre sequence of functors
\tau_{\geq 0}\to id\to\tau_{\leq-1}\simeq\Sigma^{-1}\tau_{\leq 0}
on
\mathcal{C}
.
Assume now the equivalent conditions
(1)
-
(5)
.
The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(7)
follows since a right adjoint preserving filtered colimits has a left adjoint preserving compact objects. The same argument imples
(5)\Rightarrow(6)
.
Consider the
“furthermore”
part. Point (a) follows since a left Bousfield localization of a compactly generated category is again compactly generated. For point (b), if
\mathcal{C}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
are compactly generated, then
(6)
implies
(5)
since a left adjoint that preserves compact objects between compactly-generated categories has a right adjoint that preserves filtered colimits.
∎

We also need to incorporate a monoidal structure.

Definition 3.1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})

t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})

We will say that the

\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}

In other terms,
t

\otimes

Remark 3.1.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category.
Then

\mathcal{C}

t

Assume now that
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})

If
S

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})

Consider

Definition 3.3.1

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category equipped with an accessible

We will say that an object

Remark 3.3.2
.

The nomenclature we have chosen here comes from algebraic geometry.
Pseudo-coherent objects were first introduced by Illusie in
[
6
]
via a slightly different definition.
There, pseudo-coherent complexes on a scheme are defined as complexes which, locally, are (quasi-)isomorphic to bounded above complexes which admit projective resolutions by finitely generated projectives.
If the scheme appears to be noetherian, then pseudo-coherent complexes are precisely the bounded-above complexes whose cohomology is coherent.
A similar statement can be deduced from
Theorem 3.4.7
and from
Section 5
.

Remark 3.3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable category equipped with a
t
-structure
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0})

is

In particular,
\tau_{\leq k}\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}=\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n+k}

The first result of this section shows some basic properties of these subcategories.

Lemma 3.3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category equipped with an accessible
t
-structure.
Then
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
are idempotent-complete stable subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

To show that
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
are stable subcategories of
\mathcal{C}
it suffices to prove that they are closed under finite colimits and desuspensions. This follows immediately since almost compact objects are closed under finite colimits and desuspensions (because compact objects are), together with the stability of connective and bounded objects.
A similar argument shows also that
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
are closed under retracts, since compact objects are. It follows that
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})
and
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
are idempotent-complete, since a stable thick subcategory of a presentable category is.
∎

D(n+1)
in
S
, being
S
closed under extensions.
Furthermore, the equivalence
\text{cofib}(D(n+1)\to x)\simeq\Sigma^{n+1}\text{fib}(P\to c(n))
shows that the map
D(n+1)\to x
induces an isomorphism on
\pi_{i}
for
0\leq i\leq n-2
. It follows by
3.3.10
that the canonical map
\colim_{n\in N}D(n)\to x
is an equivalence. Since, by construction, the cofiber of each map
D(n)\to D(n+1)
is an
n
-fold suspension of an object of
S
, the claim follows.
∎

Remark 3.3.12
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be as in the assumptions. Denote by
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{\geq 0}
the full subcategory of the connective half
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
spanned by the compact objects.
Then
3.1.2
implies that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}_{\geq 0}
.
It follows that if

There is another notion of finiteness that we should explore.

Definition 3.3.13
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable symmetric monoidal category equipped with a
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
.
Let
a\leq b\in\mathbbm{Z}
.
We will say that an object
x\in\mathcal{C}
has
tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
if
x\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
is right
t
-exact up to
-a
and left
t
-exact up to
b
.
We will say that an object has
finite tor-dimension
if it has tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
for some
a\leq b\in\mathbbm{Z}
.
We let
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
spanned by the objects of finite tor-dimension.

In other words,
x\in\mathcal{C}
has tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
if tensoring with
x
restricts to functors
x\otimes-:\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\to\mathcal{C}_{\geq a}
and
x\otimes-:\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\to\mathcal{C}_{\leq b}
.

The next observation connects our definition of finite tor-amplitude with the classical one.

Remark 3.3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable symmetric monoidal category equipped with a
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
. Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
and let
n\in\mathbbm{Z}
. Consider the following properties.

(1)

For every
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
the object
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
is
n
-connective.
Equivalently,
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
.

(2)

For every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and for every
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq m}
the object
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
is
n
-connective.

(3)

For every
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
the object
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
is
n
-connective.

Clearly
(1)\Rightarrow(2)\Rightarrow(3)
.
The implication
(3)\Rightarrow(2)
follows by induction.
Indeed, if
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq m}
, then tensoring the cofibre sequence
\Sigma^{k}\pi_{k}(y)\to\tau_{\leq k}y\to\tau_{\leq k-1}y
with
x
produces a cofibre sequence
x\otimes\Sigma^{k}\pi_{k}(y)\to x\otimes\tau_{\leq k}y\to x\otimes\tau_{\leq k-1}y
.
The first term belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\geq n+k}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
, and the claim follows since the connective half of a
t
-structure is closed under extensions.
The implication
(2)\Rightarrow(1)
is not true in general.
It holds for instance if the monoidal unit is (connective and) eventually coconnective.
More generally, it holds if
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq a}
is eventually connective: indeed it follows by choosing
m
with
a+m+1\geq n
and using the triangle
\tau_{\geq m+1}\to y\to\tau_{\leq m}y
with
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.

Lemma 3.3.15
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable symmetric monoidal category equipped with a
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
. Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
.

(1)

If
x
has tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
, then
\pi_{n}(x\otimes y)\simeq 0
for every
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
and
n\notin[a,b]
.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
is presentable, that
\otimes
preserves colimits in each variable and that the
t
-structure is right-complete presentable. Assume also the monoidal unit is eventually coconnective (or that
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq A}
).

(2)

If
x
is such that
\pi_{n}(x\otimes y)\simeq 0
for every
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
and
n\notin[a,b]
, then
x
has tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
.

Proof.

Assume that
x
has tor-amplitude in
[a,b]
and let
y\in\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
be discrete. Then
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq A}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq b}
so that
\pi_{n}(x\otimes y)\simeq 0
for
n\notin[a,b]
. This proves
(1)
.

Consider
(2)
. Since the monoidal unit is connective and eventually coconnective (or since
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq A}
),
3.3.14
coupled with the assumption of homotopy groups implies that tensoring with
x
produces a functor
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\to\mathcal{C}_{\geq a}
. To show that
x\otimes-
is left
t
-exact up to
b
, consider
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
.
Since the
t
-structure is right complete, the canonical map
\colim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tau_{\geq-n}y\to y
is an equivalence.
Since
\tau_{\leq 0}
is a left adjoint, the above map produces an equivalence
\colim_{n\in\mathbb{N}}\tau_{\leq 0}\tau_{\geq-n}y\to y
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
is comatible with filtered colimits it suffices to show that
x\otimes y\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq b}
for
y\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq-n}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
.
This can be proved by induction, similarly to
3.3.14
, on the cofibre sequence
\tau_{\geq-k}y\to\tau_{\geq-k-1}y\to\Sigma^{-k-1}\pi_{-k-1}y
for
0\leq k\leq n
.
∎

We now study the categorical properties of objects of finite tor-amplitude.

Lemma 3.3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable symmetric monoidal category equipped with a
t
-structure. Then
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
is a stable subcategory, closed under retracts. In other words, it is a thick subcategory.
If
\mathcal{C}
is also presentable, then
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
is idempotent-complete.

Proof.

It suffices to show that
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
is pointed (which is trivial), stable under cofibres and desuspensions. Closure under desuspensions follows since
\otimes
is exact in each variable separately.
Closure under cofibres follows since the connective half is closed under cofibres, and the same happens to coconnective halves, after a possible shift.
Finally, since the connective and coconnective half of a
t
-structure are closed under retracts, the claim follows for
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
.
The last claim is a general fact of stable thick subcategories of presentable stable categories.
∎

Objects of finite tor-amplitude control many
“geometric”
properties.
Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra with a right-complete presentable
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
.
Then we may define
\text{Perf}(\mathcal{C})
to be the intersection
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
, and call its objects
perfect objects
. The next result is an instance of
“perfect object controlling the geoemtry”
.

Lemma 3.3.17
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be a commutative algebra with a right-complete presentable
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
.
Assume that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
.

(1)

Then
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.

(2)

If the unit of the monoidal structure is eventually coconnective, then
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})
.

Proof.

This is an application of
3.3.7
. Indeed point
(1)
follows since the monoidal unit is connective so that every compact object is eventually connective, whereas point
(2)
follows since the unit
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}
is eventually coconnective (and the usual argument by induction shows that every compact object is eventually coconnective).
∎

We can now analyze the behaviour of pseudo-coherent and coherent objects under tensor products.

Lemma 3.3.18
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be a rigid commutative algebra equipped with a right-complete presentable
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
.
Assume that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
. Then
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
and
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})
are
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
-modules.

Proof.

Since
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{C}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
, it follows that
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
. In particular,
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
.
Let
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
be pseudo-coherent and let
c\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
be compact. It follows that
x\otimes c
is in
\mathcal{C}^{-}
, being the
t
-structure compatible with
\otimes
.
To be precise, if
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq N}
and
c\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq M}
then
c\otimes x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq NM}
.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
and consider a filtered diagram
y:I\to\tau_{\leq n}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq NM})
. Then

\displaystyle\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\tau_{\leq n}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq NM})}(\tau_{\leq n}(c\otimes x),y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c\otimes x,y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,c^{\vee}\otimes y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,\colim_{i\in I}c^{\vee}\otimes y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(x,c^{\vee}\otimes(\colim_{i\in I}y_{i}))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(c\otimes x,\colim_{i\in I}y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\tau_{\leq n}(\mathcal{C}_{\geq NM})}(\tau_{\leq n}(c\otimes x),\colim_{i\in I}y_{i}).

Here the first equivalence follows by fully-faithfullness and the second one by rigidity.
For the third one note that, since
c^{\vee}\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
is of finite tor-dimension, the objects
c^{\vee}\otimes y_{i}
are in some truncation of a shift of the connective half, so that pseudo-coherence of
x
allows to commute the colimit.
The fourth equivalence follows since
\otimes
commutes with small colimits in each variable, the fifth one by rigidity and the last one by fully-faithfullness.
The case of coherent objects follows then from above plus the fact that tensoring with a compact object (hence a tor-finite object) preserves bounded objects.
∎

We summarize (again) the situation.

Remark 3.3.19
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})

(1)

The collection

(2)

(3)

The

.

Then

.
Regarding pseudo-coherence, point

imply that

.

3.4.
Coherent categories

Even if pseudo-coherent and coherent objects behaved well categorically, it is not entirely clear how to compute them explicitly.
The goal of this section is to fill this gap by showing that, under some coherence assumption,
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
can be computed in terms of the homotopy groups of the
t
-structure.

Definition 3.4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with a right complete presentable
t
-structure. We will say that the
t
-structure is
coherent
if it restricts to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.

Before exploiting all the properties of coherent
t
-structures we point out a few discrepancies between our definition and the one already available in the literature.

Warning 3.4.2
.

Our definition of coherent category is
not
the same of Ben-Zvi, Nadler and Preygel
[
5
, Definition 6.2.7]
.
Lemma 6.2.5 in loc. shows how to compute pseudo-coherent objects in terms of their homotopy groups, proving the same statement of
Theorem 3.4.7
.
The author believes that this claim is actually false. Indeed, if
R
is a connective
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-ring spectrum with
\pi_{0}(R)
coherent, then
\text{Mod}_{R}
is coherent in the sense of Definition 6.2.7.
In particular, since
R\in(\text{Mod}_{R})^{\omega}\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{\text{Mod}_{R}})
, Lemma 6.2.5 implies that every
\pi_{n}(R)
is finitely presented as a
\pi_{0}(R)
-module. This is true if and only if
R
is coherent in the sense of
[
20
, Definition 7.2.4.16]
.

Warning 3.4.3
.

Furthermore, our definition is, in general, different form the Canonaco-Neeman-Stellari
[
11
, Definition 10.1.1]
.
It agrees under the assumptions of
3.5.11
.

We now study the heart of a coherent
t
-structure.

Lemma 3.4.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with a right-complete presentable
t
-structure.
Then the following conditions are equivalent.

(1)

The
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
restricts to one on
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
, that is the
t
-structure is coherent.

(2)

The inclusion
i_{\leq 0}^{\leq 1}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\leq 1}
preserves compact objects.

(3)

Desuspending
\Sigma^{-1}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\to\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
preserves compact objects.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
. Then in this case
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}=\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}=(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}
.
In particular,

(4)

The subcategory of compact objects in the heart
(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
is abelian.

Proof.

Consider
(1)\Leftrightarrow(2)
.
First of all, recall that the
t
-structure restricts to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if for every
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
the truncation
\tau_{\leq n}x\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
is again pseudo-coherent.
Since
\tau_{\leq m}\circ\tau_{\leq n}\simeq\tau_{\leq\text{min}(n,m)}
, the claim then follows by noting that
\tau_{\leq n}x\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
is compact if and only if
\tau_{\leq m}x\in\mathcal{C}_{\leq m}
is compact for all
m\geq n
and
i_{\leq m}^{\leq n}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq m}\to\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
preserves compact objects for
m\geq n
.
The equivalence
(2)\Leftrightarrow(3)
follows since
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 1}=\Sigma^{1}\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
.
Assume now the equivalent conditions
(1)
-
(2)
and
(3)
.
First of all, note that the
t
-structure also restricts to
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})
, thus producing hearts
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}
and
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}
so that the equality
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}=\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}
is essentially by definition.
To prove that
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}=(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}
, note that
(\subseteq)
is always true and that
(\supseteq)
follows since
i_{\leq 0}^{\leq 1}
preserves compact objects.
Finally, since the heart of a
t
-structure is always abelian, the above equalities prove
(4)
.
∎

Recall that a
locally coherent abelian
1
-category
is a compactly generated Grothendieck abelian
1
-category whose compact objects form an abelian category. Examples include modules over a coherent ring and quasi-coherent sheaves over a coherent scheme.

Lemma 3.4.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with a coherent
t
-structure such that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
.
Then
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
is a locally coherent abelian
1
-category.

Proof.

Since
(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}
forms an abelian subcategory of
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
by
3.4.4
, it suffices to show that
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
is compactly generated.
Since
\mathcal{C}
is compactly generated,
3.1.2
implies that
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
is compactly generated.
Since
3.4.4
implies that
i_{\leq 0}^{\leq 1}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\leq 1}
preserves compact objects,
[
19
, Corollary 5.5.7.3]
implies that
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}\simeq\mathcal{C}_{\leq 1}/\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}
is compactly generated.
∎

We may wonder if condition
(4)
of
3.4.4
is equivalent to the other coniditions.
This is not always the case as we need the following.

Lemma 3.4.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with a right-complete presentable
t
-structure such that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
.
Consider the following condition for
n\geq 0
.

(\text{HC})_{n}

The category
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
is compactly generated and the compact objects are closed under finite limits.

Then the
t
-structure is coherent if and only if
(\text{HC})_{n}
holds for every
n\geq 0
.

Proof.

Assume that the
t
-structure is coherent.
Fix
n\geq 0
and consider the truncation
\tau_{\geq 0}:\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}\to\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
with kernel
\mathcal{C}_{\leq-1}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is compactly generated by
3.1.2
and since the inclusion
\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}_{\leq 1}
preserves compacts, an application of
[
19
, Corollary 5.5.7.3]
implies that
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
is compactly generated.
To show that the comapct objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
are closed under finite limits, since
\tau_{\geq 0}
is right exact, it suffices to show that the compact objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
are closed under finite limits. This follows by
3.4.4
.

Conversely, assume that
(\text{HC})_{n}
holds for every
n\geq 0
.
To show that the
t
-structure is coherent it suffices to show that the it restricts to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.
Let
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
and assume without loss of generality that
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.
Fix
k\in\mathbbm{Z}
.
Then
\tau_{\leq k}x
is clearly connective and almost compact in
\tau_{\leq k}\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
, thus
\tau_{\leq k}x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.
For the other truncation, consider the fibre sequence
\tau_{\geq 1}x\to x\to\pi_{0}(x)
.
Apply the truncation
\tau_{\leq n}
for every
n\geq 0
to produce a fibre sequence
\tau_{\leq n}\tau_{\geq 1}x\to\tau_{\leq n}x\to\pi_{0}(x)
.
Since all the terms belong to
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
, it follows that
\tau_{\leq n}\tau_{\geq 1}x\simeq\text{fib}(\tau_{\leq n}x\to\pi_{0}(x))
in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
.
Since
x
and
\pi_{0}(x)
belong to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
, the first one by assumption and the second one being
\pi_{0}(x)\simeq\tau_{\leq 0}x
the truncation of a pseudo-coherent object, it follows by assumption
(\text{HC})_{n}
that
\tau_{\leq n}\tau_{\geq 1}x
is compact in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\leq n}
.
Since this holds for every
n\geq 0
, it follows that
\tau_{\geq 1}x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.
In particular, the
t
-structure restricts to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
.
∎

As we will see, even though condition
(\text{HC})_{n}
seems very hard to check in practice, many interesting categories satisfy it.

Then we have the following.

Theorem 3.4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category with a coherent
t
-structure.
Assume that the
t
-structure is left separated and
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{-}
.
Assume also that:

(S)

There exists a generating subcategory
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
which is closed under finite colimits and extensions and consists of pseudo-coherent connective objects of
\mathcal{C}
.

Then:

(1)

The heart
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})^{\heartsuit}=\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
consists precisely of the compact objects of
\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit}
.

(2)

An object

if and only if
\pi_{n}x\in(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}
and
\pi_{n}x=0
for

(3)

x\in\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})
if and only if
\pi_{n}x\in(\mathcal{C}^{\heartsuit})^{\omega}
and
\pi_{n}x=0
for all but finitely many

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
is the left

\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C})

Point

3.4.4
.
Since point
(3)
follows immediately from point

by noticing that coherent objects are defined to be bounded above, it suffices to show
(2)

Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
Since both conditions imply that

is eventually connective, it is safe to assume that
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is connective.
Suppose first that
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is pseudo-coherent and connective.
Since
x
is connective,
\pi_{n}(x)\simeq 0
for
n<0
.
Since the
t
-structure restricts to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
by
3.4.4
, it follows that

The cofibre sequence

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}

By desuspending and applying point

Assume now that

is connective and that

for every

By

.
As in the proof of

mapping to

is a
n

S

Equivalently, it suﬀices to produce a cofibre sequence

for each

such that

is in

Set, by convention,

Define

via the pullback square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D(n+1)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{n}P}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces x}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Sigma^{n}c(n)}}

Pseudo-compact objects

We now introduce Neeman’s pseudo-compact objects.

Definition 3.5.1

Let

n
-connective.

spanned by the pseudo-compact objects.
We also denote by
\mathcal{C}^{b}_{c}
the intersection
\mathcal{C}^{-}_{c}\cap\mathcal{C}^{b}
,

Let
\mathcal{C}
be compactly generated and equipped with a compact generator

and
\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(G,\mathcal{C}_{\geq N})=0

Warning 3.5.2

A priori there is no reason to expect

Lemma 3.5.3

Let

(2)

In the following it will be useful to work with an equivalence class of
t
-structures rather than a single one.

Remark 3.5.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a stable category with a pair of
t
–structures
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}^{1},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}^{1})
and
(\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}^{2},\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}^{2})
.
We will say that the
t
–structures are
equivalent
if there exists an integer
A\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\geq A}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}^{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\geq-A}^{1}
.
Equivalently, if
\mathcal{C}_{\leq-A}^{1}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\leq 0}^{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\leq A}^{1}
.
Being equivalent is an equivalence relation on the (possibly large or even very large) set of
t
-structures on a stable category.
It is a common belief that all the meaningful constructions associated to a
t
-structure should only depend on the equivalence class.
For example, the full subcategories
\mathcal{C}^{-},\mathcal{C}^{+}
and
\mathcal{C}^{b}
are insensitive to equivalent
t
-structures.

Notice, however, that not every property of
t
-structures is preserved by passing to equivalence classes.
For example:

(1)

Being accessible is not preserved.

(2)

Being compatible with filtered colimits is not preserved.

(3)

Being compactly generated is not preserved.

(4)

The heart of the
t
-structure depends on a representative.

On the other hand, being right or left complete is instead preserved by the passing to the quotient.

Remark 3.5.5
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category, and assume that
\mathcal{C}
admits a single compact generator.
Then this compact generator determines an equivalence class of
t
-structures.
In particular,
[
23
, Lemma 0.13]
and
[
23
, Example 0.17]
imply that this equivalence class is insensitive to compact generators, in the sense that two different compact generators generate the same equivalence class of
t
-structure.
We will refer to this equivalence class as the
preferred equivalence class
.

The next observation is a useful criterion to produce left complete
t
-structures.

Remark 3.5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with a compact generator
G\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
such that
\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(G,\Sigma^{i}G)=0
for
i>>0
.
Then
[
10
, Lemma 3.6]
implies every
t
-structure in the preferred equivalence class is left and right complete.

Definition 3.5.7
.

[
23
, Definition 8.3]

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}

\mathcal{C}

Remark 3.5.8

Proposition 3.5.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{L,\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category equipped with right-complete presentable

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
comes equipped with a compact generator
G\in\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
such that the
t
-structure is in the preferred equivalence class and such that
\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(G,\Sigma^{i}G)=0
for
i>>0
.
Assume also that:

(S)

The subcategory
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is a generating subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
.

Then
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})=\mathcal{C}^{-}_{c}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
First of all, since
\mathcal{C}
has an eventually connective compact generator, every compact object is bounded below.
In particular, every object in
\mathcal{C}^{-}_{c}
is an extension of bounded below objects, and hence bounded below. Since every object in
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})
is bounded below, it is safe to assume that
x\in\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
is
0
-connective.

Assume first that
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
. The proof of
3.3.11
showed that

can be written as a sequential colimit

where each

is compact and connective.
Fix now
n>0
. Since
D(n+1)
is in
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\geq 0}
and the

-structure is compatible with filtered colimits, point
(6)
of
3.1.2
implies that
D(n+1)
is compact in
\mathcal{C}
.
In particular, the map
D(n+1)\to x
is a morphism from a compact object to

, and it is a
\pi_{i}
-equivalence for every

, hence its cofibre lies in
\mathcal{C}_{\geq n}
, thus showing

.

Assume now that

. We will prove that

is pseudo-coherent by showing that it can be written as a sequential colimit

of

.
Then the

will conclude.
Now we can easily modify the proof of point

of

4.
Dualities

We now take the first steps towards the two main results of this paper.

4.1.
Tor-finite categories

We start by axiomatizing the categories which are
“tor-finite”
.

Construction 4.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be a rigidly-compactly generated stable category.
We let
\text{Acc}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
denote the (possibly very very large) set of small collection of compact objects
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
for which the inclusion
S\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
is symmetric monoidal.
We regard
\text{Acc}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
as a poset ordered by inclusion.
Then every rigid functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
determines a functor
f:\text{Acc}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})\to\text{Acc}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{D})
.
In particular, the assignment
\mathcal{C}\mapsto\text{Acc}^{\otimes}(\mathcal{C})
may be regarded as a covariant functor
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})\to\widehat{\widehat{\text{Cat}}}
to very very large categories.
The cocartesian unstraightening produces then a cocartesian fibration
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}\to\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
Objects of
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}
may be identified with a pair
(\mathcal{C},S_{\mathcal{C}})
consisting of a rigidly-compactly generated stable category
\mathcal{C}
and a symmetric monoidal embedding
S_{\mathcal{C}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
; morphisms in
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}
are then rigid functors
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
such that
f^{\text{L}}(S_{\mathcal{C}})\subseteq S_{\mathcal{D}}
.

Remark 4.1.2
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},S_{\mathcal{C}})
be in
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}
.
Accordingly to
3.1.4
, picking a small skeleton of
S_{\mathcal{C}}
produces an accessible
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
.
The construction of this
t
-structure is insensitive to the choice of the skeleton generating it.
In particular, the symmetric monoidal embedding
S_{\mathcal{C}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
shows that the
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
is compatible with
\otimes
.
It follows that
(\mathcal{C},S_{\mathcal{C}})
may be identified with a rigidly-compactly generated stable category
\mathcal{C}
together with an accessible
t
-structure on
\mathcal{C}
which is compatible with
\otimes
.
In the following we will always tacitly assume this type of construction.

Remark 4.1.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{C},S_{\mathcal{C}})
be in
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}
and assume that
S_{\mathcal{C}}
generates, meaning that there exists a small skeleton of generators inside
S_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Then
3.1.6
implies that the accessible
t
-structure generated by
S_{\mathcal{C}}
is right-complete and compatible with filtered colimits.
In other words it is right-complete presentable and compatible with
\otimes
.

Definition 4.1.4
.

We let
\text{Pr}^{\text{tor}}
denote the non-wide non-full subcategory of
\text{Pr}^{S,\otimes}
on those
(\mathcal{C},S_{\mathcal{C}})
such that
S_{\mathcal{C}}
generates and the resulting the
t
-structure is left complete and
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
and on those functors whose right adjoint is right
t
-exact up to a shift.
We will refer to objects and functors in
\text{Pr}^{\text{tor}}
as
tor-finite categories
and
functors of tor-finite categories
, respectively.

Remark 4.1.5
.

To spell it out:

(1)

An object in
\text{Pr}^{\text{tor}}
consists of a rigidly-compactly generated category
\mathcal{C}
together with a
t
-structure generated by a collection of compact objects
S_{\mathcal{C}}
.
The
t
-structure is accessible, compatible with
\otimes
, compatible with filtered colimits, left-complete and right-complete.
Furthermore,
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
.

(2)

A functor in
\text{Pr}^{\text{tor}}
consists of a rigid functor
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{D}
which is right
t
-exact and such that its right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}:\mathcal{D}\to\mathcal{C}
is left
t
-exact and right
t
-exact up to a shift.
Notice that
f^{\text{L}}
is an internal left adjoint in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
, so that
f^{\text{R}}
preserves all colimits.

Furthermore, since the monoidal unit is connective,
3.3.17
implies that
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\cap\text{Tor}(\mathcal{C})
.
More importantly,
3.3.6
and
3.3.18
imply that
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{C}),\,\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{C})\in\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{C}^{\omega}}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})
are small idempotent-complete stable modules over
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
.

The next observation links tor-finite categories to the theory of approximation of Neeman.

Remark 4.1.6
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category and let
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be a small collection of compact object that generates.
Then for every connective object
x\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
there exists a cofibre sequence
s\to x\to c
where
s
is a sum of objects of
S_{\mathcal{B}}
and
c\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 1}
is
1
-connective.

Indeed since
S_{\mathcal{B}}
is generating there exists a
\pi_{0}
-epimorphism
s\to x
with
s
a sum of objects of
S_{\mathcal{B}}
. The long exact sequence in homotopy associated to the cofibre sequence
s\to x\to c
shows that not only
c\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
, but
c\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 1}
since
\pi_{0}c\simeq 0
.

Remark 4.1.7
.

Let
\mathcal{B}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category and let
S\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be a small collection of compact object that generates.
In other terms, the smallest localizing subcategory
\text{Loc}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
containing
S
is all of
\mathcal{B}
.
Then
S

thickly generates the compact objects
, in the sense that
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}=\text{Thick}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
, where
\text{Thick}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
denotes the smallest idempotent-complete stable subcategory containing
S
.
Indeed, let
\text{Loc}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)\subseteq\mathcal{B}
be the localizing subcategory generated by
S
.
Since
S
consists of compact objects, the compact objects of
\text{Loc}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
are exactly the thick closure of
S
, that is
(\text{Loc}_{\mathcal{B}}(S))^{\omega}=\text{Thick}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
.
In particular, the equality
\text{Loc}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)=\mathcal{B}
, yields
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}=\text{Thick}_{\mathcal{B}}(S)
.

4.2.
Quasi-perfect and quasi-proper functors

The goal of this section is to study the behaviour of (pseudo-)coherent object under morphisms of commutative algebras.

Lemma 4.2.1
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be an internal left adjoint in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
and assume that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are equipped with accessible and compatible with filtered colimits
t
-structures such that
f^{\text{L}}
is right
t
-exact and
f^{\text{R}}
is right
t
-exact up to a shift.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
restricts to a functor
f^{\text{L}}:\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})\to\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
be pseudo-coherent, and assume without loss of generality, that
x\in\mathcal{A}_{\geq 0}
. Since
f^{\text{L}}
is right
t
-exact,
f^{\text{L}}(x)
is in
\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
. Let
y_{i}:I\to\tau_{\leq n}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0})
be a filtered diagram. Then:

\displaystyle\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\tau_{\leq n}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0})}(\tau_{\leq n}f^{\text{L}}(x),y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}}(\tau_{\leq n}f^{\text{L}}(x),y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(\tau_{\leq n}f^{\text{L}}(x),y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{B}}(f^{\text{L}}\tau_{\leq n}(x),y_{i})

\displaystyle\simeq\colim_{i\in I}\Hom_{\mathcal{A}}(\tau_{\leq n}(x),f^{\text{R}}y_{i})

Here the first and second equivalence follows by fully-faithfullness, the third one by
[
19
, Proposition 5.5.6.28]
and the last one by adjunction.
Since
f^{\text{R}}
is left
t
-exact and right
t
-exact up to a shift
N\in\mathbb{N}
, it follows that
f^{\text{R}}(y_{i})\in\tau_{\leq n}\mathcal{A}_{\geq-N}
.
Thus the claim will be proved (after translating back through the preceding equivalences) if the inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{\geq 0}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\geq-N}
preserves compact objects. This follows by
3.1.2
, being the
t
-structure compatible with filtered colimits.
∎

The situation is much easier with pseudo-compact objects.

Lemma 4.2.2
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
Assume that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{B}
are equipped with
t
-structures such that
f^{\text{L}}
is right
t
-exact.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
preserves pseudo-compact objects.

Proof.

If
x\in\mathcal{A}^{-}_{c}
, then for every
m>0
there exists a cofibre sequence
b\to x\to e
where
b\in\mathcal{A}_{c}
is compact and
e\in\mathcal{A}_{\geq m}
. Exactness of
f^{\text{L}}
produces then the cofibre sequence
f^{\text{L}}(b)\to f^{\text{L}}(x)\to f^{\text{L}}(e)
, which exhibits
f^{\text{L}}(x)
as a pseudo-compact object of
\mathcal{B}
since
f^{L}
preserves compacts being rigid.
∎

We are more interested in understanding when the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
preserves pseudo-coherent objects.
Note that, when
f^{\text{R}}
sends pseudo-coherent to pseudo-coherent, then it also preserves coherent objects, being left
t
-exact.
Anyway, let us give a name to these functors.

Definition 4.2.3
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
We will say that
f^{\text{L}}
is
quasi-proper
if the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
preserves pseudo-coherent objects.

The nomenclature comes from algebraic geometry.
Indeed, a map
f:X\to Y
between quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes is called quasi-proper if the (derived) pushforward
f_{*}
preserves pseudo-coherent complexes.
We suggest Lipman and Neeman’s article
[
18
]
for a nice review of all the categorical properties of the (derived) pushforward.
For us what matters is that they also review the notion of quasi-perfect maps, that is those maps of quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes whose (derived) pushforward preserves perfect complexes.
This notion has an immediate generalization in our context.

Definition 4.2.4
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
We will say that
f^{\text{L}}
is
quasi-perfect
if the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{A}
preserves compact objects.

Remark 4.2.5
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-perfect if and only the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
is an internal left adjoint in
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
by
2.3.9
. In particular, the Grothendieck-Neeman duality, that is
Theorem 2.3.7
, applies.

The main result of the above mentioned article is
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
. It says that a quasi-compact quasi-separated map
f:X\to Y
between quasi-compact quasi-separated schemes is quasi-perfect if and only if it is quasi-proper and the twisted inverse image
f^{(1)}
is
t
-bounded (or, equivalently, that
f
is quasi-proper and of finite tor-dimension). We can prove a (partial) analogue of this statement in the abstract setting we are developing.

Lemma 4.2.6
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-proper if and only if
f^{\text{R}}
carries compact objects to pseudo-coherent objects.

Proof.

The implication
(\Rightarrow)
is trivial since
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
in a tor-finite category.
Consider
(\Leftarrow)
.
Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
be the error of right
t
-exactness of
f^{\text{R}}
so that
f^{\text{R}}(\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0})\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{\geq-N}
.
Let
x\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
be pseudo-coherent.
After a shift, it is safe to assume that
x\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
is connective.
In paritcular,
3.3.11
implies that
x
can be written as a sequential colimit
x\simeq\colim_{n\in N}D(n)
where the cofibre of each map
D(n-1)\to D(n)
is a
n
-fold suspension of an object of
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}_{\geq 0}=\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\cap\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
, thus of a compact and connective object of
\mathcal{B}
.
Since
f^{\text{L}}
is an internal left adjoint, the functor
f^{\text{R}}
preserves colimits, thus
f^{\text{R}}(x)\simeq f^{\text{R}}(\colim_{n\in N}D(n))\simeq\colim f^{\text{R}}(D(n))
.
Now the cofibre of each
f^{\text{R}}(D(n-1))\to f^{\text{R}}(D(n))
is given by the
n
-fold suspension of an object of
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathcal{A}_{\geq-N}
since
f^{\text{R}}
carries compact objects to pseudo-coherent objects.
By the
\infty
-Dold-Kan correspondence, this sequential colimit corresponds to a simplicial object whose pieces are pseudo-coherent and
(-N)
-connective. Thus, after a shift,
3.3.8
implies that
f^{\text{R}}(x)
is pseudo-coherent.
∎

Lemma 4.2.7
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories which is left
t
-exact up to a shift.
Then
f^{\text{R}}
restricts to a functor
\text{Tor}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{Tor}(\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\text{Tor}(\mathcal{B})
. To show that tensoring with
f^{\text{R}}(x)
defines a left
t
-exact and right
t
-exact functor up to a shift it suffices to notice that
f^{\text{R}}(x)\otimes-\simeq f^{\text{R}}(x\otimes f^{\text{L}}(-))
is a composition of left
t
-exact and right
t
-exact functors up to a shift, since
f^{\text{L}}
is left
t
-exact up to a shift.
∎

In particular, we deduce the following result.

Corollary 4.2.8
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.

(1)

If
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-perfect, then it is quasi-proper.

Assume that
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}=\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})\cap\text{Tor}(\mathcal{A})
.

(2)

If
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-proper and left
t
-exact up to a shift, then it is quasi-perfect.

Proof.

For point
(1)
, if
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-perfect, then its right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
preserves compact objects. Then the second condition of
4.2.6
applies.
For point
(2)
, since
f^{\text{R}}
preserves pseudo-coherent objects and since
f^{\text{L}}
is left
t
-exact up to a shift,
4.2.7
implies that
f^{\text{R}}
restricts to a functor
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\cap\text{Tor}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})\cap\text{Tor}(\mathcal{A})=\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
. Thus
f^{\text{R}}
preserves compact objects, hence
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-perfect.
∎

4.3.
Functors out of
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Then
f^{\text{L}}
restricts to a functor between compact objects and exhibits
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
as a small idempotent-complete stable commutative algebra under
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
. In particular, we may regard
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
as an
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-module in
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
.
Thus an application of
2.2.5
produces an equivalence

\mathcal{B}\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})

in
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{A}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
.
The equivalence is explicitly given by
x\mapsto\mathcal{B}(-,x)
where
\mathcal{B}(-,-)=f^{\text{R}}\underline{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{B}}(-,-)
is the linear Yoneda.
Our goal is now to study the above Yoneda embedding when the source is a
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
-module
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, such as
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\subseteq\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
.
In general, by restricting the source to a
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
-module
\mathcal{B}_{0}\subseteq\mathcal{B}
, the linear Yoneda embedding will still be fully-faithful.
However, it will cease to be an equivalence, since not every exact and
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
arises from an object of
\mathcal{B}_{0}
.
For this reason, it is helpful to approach the problem from the other side.
Instead of restricting the source of the linear Yoneda embedding, it is easier to restrict its target, and then identify its kernel.

We now study the case of (pseudo-)coherent objects in tor-finite categories.

Lemma 4.3.1
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a quasi-proper functor.
Then the
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding produces functors

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})),\qquad\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A}))

which are fully-faithful.

Proof.

Indeed, being
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
a small idempotent-complete stable
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
-module, the tensor product of
\mathcal{B}
restricts to a functor
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\times\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\to\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
.
Since
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-proper, the right adjoint
f^{\text{R}}
sends
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
.
It follows that the first functor is fully-faithful, being the restriction of a fully-faithful functor.
The claim for coherent objects follows since coherent objects are the coconnective pseudo-coherent objects, and
f^{\text{R}}
is left
t
-exact.
∎

We begin with some simple observations.

Remark 4.3.2
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Then for every
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
the evaluation at
b
admits a left adjoint

b_{!}:\mathcal{A}\rightleftarrows\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}):\text{ev}_{b}

and a right adjoint.
Indeed, since limits and colimits in the functor category are computed pointwise and are preserved by
\text{ev}_{b}
, the usual yoga with Kan-extension implies that
\text{ev}_{b}
has adjoints on both sides.

Remark 4.3.3
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
The counit
\varepsilon_{b}:b_{!}\circ\text{ev}_{b}\to\text{id}
evaluated at an exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor
F:(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
and at an object
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
admits a section given by the unit
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathcal{A}}\to\mathcal{B}(b,b)
as a consequence of the triangle identities.
In particular,
\text{ev}_{b}(\varepsilon_{b}(F)):\mathcal{B}(b,b)\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}F(b)\to F(b)
is a split epimorphism.

Remark 4.3.4
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Denote by
F_{\geq 0}
the smallest full subcategory of
\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
closed under small colimits and extensions and spanned by
b_{!}(a)
for
a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}
and
b\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Regard it as a
t
-structure via
3.1.4
.
Since
f^{\text{L}}(S_{\mathcal{A}})\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
and since the monoidal unit of
\mathcal{A}
is connective, the equivalence
\mathcal{B}\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
extends to a
t
-exact equivalence.
It follows that the
t
-structure generated by
F_{\geq 0}
inherits all the properties enjoyed by the
t
-structure on
\mathcal{B}
.
Moreover,
b_{!}
is right
t
-exact and
\text{ev}_{b}
is left
t
-exact for every
b\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
(and hence for every
b\in\mathcal{B}_{\geq 0}
).

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories. The previous observation implies that the
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda restricts to equivalence

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{PCoh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})),\qquad\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{Coh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}))

between the pseudo-coherent and coherent objects.
What is left now to show is that
PCoh
and
Coh
can
“enter into the second member of the functor category”
.
There are three ingredients.
The first one is the following.

Lemma 4.3.5
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories.
Let
a\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
and
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
.
Then
b_{!}(a)
defines an exact and
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
which is pseudo-coherent.
If
f^{\text{L}}
is left
t
-exact up to a shift, the same holds if
a\in\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A})
is coherent.

Proof.

By definition
b_{!}(a)\simeq a\otimes_{\mathcal{A}}\mathcal{B}(-,b)\simeq\mathcal{B}(-,f^{\text{L}}(a)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}b)
since
(-)\in\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
is compact (hence dualizable).
Since
a\in\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
and
b\in\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
it follows that
f^{\text{L}}(a)\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}b
is in
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})
by
4.2.1
.
The equivalence
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\simeq\text{PCoh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}))
of
4.3.4
implies then that
b_{!}(a)
is pseudo-coherent.
The coherent case is analogus.
∎

The second ingredient is a particular comonad on the functor category.

Remark 4.3.6
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and let
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be generating.
Then there exists an adjunction

L:\mathcal{A}^{S_{\mathcal{B}}}\rightleftarrows\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}):R

where the left adjoint
L
is given by
L((a_{s})_{s\in S})=\bigoplus_{s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}s_{!}(a_{s})
and the right adjoint is given by
R(F)=(\text{ev}_{s}(F))_{s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}
.
Let
T
be the induced comonad and let
T^{\bullet}:\Delta^{\text{op}}\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
denote the associated simplicial object.

The next result explains our interest in the comonad
T
.

Lemma 4.3.7
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and let
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be generating.
Let
F\in\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
.
Then
F\simeq|T^{\bullet+1}F|
.

Proof.

By the Barr-Beck theorem it is sufficient to check that
R
is conservative and that it preserves geometric realization of
R
-split simplicial objects.
Since colimits in
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
are computed pointwise, each evaluation
\text{ev}_{s}
preserves colimits, so that
R
does that too.
For conservativity, let
\alpha:G\to G^{\prime}
be natural transformations and let
\mathcal{U}
denote the full subcategory of
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
spanned by those
x
such that
\alpha_{x}:G(x)\to G^{\prime}(x)
is an equivalence.
Since
G
and
G^{\prime}
are exact functors,
\mathcal{U}
is closed under shifts, cofibers, and retracts.
If
\alpha_{s}
is an equivalence for all
s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
, then
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
, and the thickness of
S_{\mathcal{B}}
explained in
4.1.7
implies
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}=\mathcal{U}
.
That is,
R
is conservative.
∎

The final ingredient needed for the main result of this section are projective classes.

Remark 4.3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category.
Recall that a
projective class

(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
on
\mathcal{C}
consists of a class of objects
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{C}
and an ideal of morphisms
\mathcal{I}
of
\mathcal{C}
such that:

(1)

The ideal
\mathcal{I}
is orthogonal to
\mathcal{P}
, in the sense that
(x\to y)\in\mathcal{I}
if and only if
\pi_{0}\hom(p,x)\to\pi_{0}\hom(p,y)
is zero for all
p\in\mathcal{P}
.

(2)

There are enough projectives, in the sense that for every
x\in\mathcal{C}
there exists a cofibre sequence
p\to x\to y
where
p\in\mathcal{P}
and
(x\to y)\in\mathcal{I}
.

Projective classes are
“over-determined”
.
Indeed, given a class of objects
\mathcal{P}
of
\mathcal{C}
, let
\mathcal{P}\text{-null}
denote the class of morphisms
x\to y
in
\mathcal{C}
such that for every
p\in\mathcal{P}
the induced map
\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(p,x)\to\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(p,y)
is the zero homomorphism.
Then
\mathcal{P}\text{-null}
is an ideal and the pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}{\text{-null}})
is a projective class if and only if there are enough projectives.

The next result shows how to construct projective classes.

Proposition 4.3.9
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category, and let
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be an essentially small collection of objects.
Choose a small skeleton
\{s_{i}\}_{i\in I}
in
S
and let
\mathcal{P}=\text{Thick}_{1}(\mathbb{Z}S)
be the smallest subcategory of
\mathcal{C}
closed under small coproducts, retracts and shifts that contains
S
.
Then the pair
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{P}\text{-null})
is a projective class on
\mathcal{C}
.

Proof.

To show that there are enough projectives, fix
x\in\mathcal{C}
.
Since
I
is small and since
\mathcal{C}
is locally small, the disjoint union
J_{x}:=\amalg_{(i,n)\in I\times\mathbb{Z}}\pi_{0}\Hom_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{n}s_{i},x)
is a set.
Define
p_{x}:=\amalg_{(i,n,\alpha)\in J_{x}}\Sigma^{n}s_{i}\in\mathcal{P}
and let
\varepsilon:p_{x}\to x
be the morphism whose component on the summand indexed by
(i,n,\alpha)
is the chosen map
\alpha:\Sigma^{n}s_{i}\to x
.
Let
y_{x}
be the cofibre of
\varepsilon
.
Applying
\hom_{\mathcal{C}}(\Sigma^{n}s_{i},-)
yields an exact sequence on
\pi_{0}
,

\pi_{0}\hom(\Sigma^{n}s_{i},p_{x})\to\pi_{0}\hom(\Sigma^{n}s_{i},x)\to\pi_{0}\hom(\Sigma^{n}s_{i},y_{x}).

Since the first arrow is surjective by construction, the second map is zero for all
(i,n)
. Since
\hom_{\mathcal{C}}(-,x)
sends coproducts in the first variable to products, and retracts to retracts, vanishing (or surjectivity) for each
\Sigma^{n}s_{i}
implies the same for every object obtained from them by shifts, coproducts, and retracts.
Therefore
x\to y_{x}
lies in
\mathcal{P}\text{-null}
.
∎

We will apply the previous construction to a specific essentially small collection of objects.

Remark 4.3.10
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
be a compactly generated stable category and let
S\subseteq\mathcal{C}
be an essentially small collection of objects that thickly generates the compact objects, in that
\text{thick}(S)=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
.
Then
4.3.9
applied to
\text{thick}(S)=\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
determines a projective class whose projective objects are given by retracts of arbitrary coproducts of compact objects.
We will refer to this projective class as the
projective class of compact objects
.
Notice that this projective class depends only on the compact objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
In other terms, if
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
are two essentially small collections of objects that thickly generate the compact objects, then

\mathcal{P}_{1}:=\text{Thick}_{1}(\text{thick}(S_{1}))=\text{Thick}_{1}(\mathcal{C}^{\omega})=\text{Thick}_{1}(\text{thick}(S_{2}))=:\mathcal{P}_{2}

define the same projective class.

Remark 4.3.11
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}}
be a presentable stable category.
Let
(\mathcal{P},\mathcal{I})
be a projective class on
\mathcal{C}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}=\mathcal{P}
and inductively define
\mathcal{P}_{n}
to be the class of all retracts of objects
y
that sit in a cofibre sequence
x\to y\to p
with
x\in\mathcal{P}_{n-1}
and

.
Define also
\mathcal{I}^{n}
by the
n
-fold composites of
\mathcal{I}
.
Then
[

, Theorem 1.1]
implies that
(\mathcal{P}_{n},\mathcal{I}^{n})
is a projective class.

In the setting of tor-finite categories there is a natural projective class.

Notation 4.3.12
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and let
S_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
and
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be generating.
We will denote by
S_{!}
the full subcategory of
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
spanned by
s_{!}(a)
for
s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
and
a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Then
S_{!}
thickly generates the compact objects of
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
.
In particular, by picking a small skeleton,
4.3.10
equips
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
with the projective class generated by compact objects.
We will denote it by
(\mathcal{P}_{!},\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})
.
Notice that this projective class, under the equivalence

, corresponds to the projective class of compact objects of
\mathcal{B}
.

The following observation is the crucial ingredient in the proof of the main result of this section, since it allows us to move from infinite data to finite data.

Remark 4.3.13
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and let
S_{\mathcal{A}}\subseteq\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
and
S_{\mathcal{B}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
be generating.
Assume now that there exists a finite subcategory
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
consisting of compact generators of
\mathcal{B}
.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}
be the smallest full subcategory closed under small coproducts and retracts containing
\text{thick}(\{s_{!}(a)\text{ for }s\in S_{\text{fin}},a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}\})
.
Then
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}=\mathcal{P}_{!}
by
4.3.10
since the thick closure of the
s_{!}(a)
for
s\in S_{\text{fin}}
and
a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}
agrees with the compact objects.

Lemma 4.3.14
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and let
F:(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
be an exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor.
Then the cofibre of the counit
TF\to F
is
\mathcal{P}_{!}
-null.

Proof.

Consider the cofibre sequence
TF\to F\to D
of the counit
TF\to F
.
Then for every
a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}
and
s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
there is a fibre sequence

\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(s_{!}(a),TF)\to\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(s_{!}(a),F)\to\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(s_{!}(a),D)

in
Sp
which, by adjunction, is equivalent to

\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(a,(TF)(s))\to\hom_{\mathcal{A}}(a,F(s))\to\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(s_{!}(a),D).

By
4.3.3
the component of the counit has a section, so that the first map is split surjective.
In particular, the second map must be zero (as it follows by the universal property of pullbacks in a stable category), and hence zero on all homotopy groups.

Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{\Fun}^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})^{\omega}
be the full subcategory spanned by those compact objects
P
such that

\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(P,TF)\to\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(P,F)

is surjective on all homotopy groups.
The subcategory
\mathcal{U}
is thick.
Indeed, this follows from the long exact sequences on homotopy groups associated to cofibre sequences in the variable
p
.
By the previous paragraph,
\mathcal{U}
contains the generators
s_{!}(a)
for
s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
and
a\in S_{\mathcal{A}}
.
Since these objects thickly generate
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})^{\omega}
, it follows that
\mathcal{U}=\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})^{\omega}
.
Therefore, for every
P\in\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})^{\omega}
, the map

\pi_{0}\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(P,F)\to\pi_{0}\hom_{\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})}(P,D)

is zero.
Since
\mathcal{P}_{!}=\text{Thick}_{1}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})^{\omega})
is obtained from compact objects by small coproducts, retracts and shifts, the same holds for every
P\in\mathcal{P}_{!}
. Thus
F\to D
is
\mathcal{P}_{!}
-null.
∎

Lemma 4.3.15
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a functor of tor-finite categories and assume that
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
is a finite generating set.
Let
F:(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
be an exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor.
Then for every integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a cofibre sequence
F_{n}\to F\to D_{n}
where
F_{n}
is pseudo-coherent and
(F\to D_{n})\in(\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})^{n}
.

Proof.

Since
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
is a finite generating set,
4.3.13
implies that
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}=\mathcal{P}_{!}
and
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}\text{-null}=\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null}
.
Let
T_{\text{fin}}=\bigoplus_{s\in S_{\text{fin}}}s_{!}\circ\text{ev}_{s}
be the comonad of
4.3.6
restricted to
S_{\text{fin}}
.

The proof now goes by induction.
For the base step, consider the cofibre sequence
T_{\text{fin}}F\to F\to D_{1}
.
Since for every
s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}
the object
\text{ev}_{s}(F)
belongs to
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
,
4.3.5
implies that
s_{!}(\text{ev}_{s}(F))
is pseudo-coherent.
Since
S_{\text{fin}}
is finite, the object
T_{\text{fin}}F=\bigoplus_{s\in S_{\text{fin}}}s_{!}(\text{ev}_{s}(F))
is again pseudo-coherent.
To check that the map
F\to D_{1}
is
\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null}
it suffices to check that it is
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}\text{-null}
, but this follows by adapting proof of
4.3.14
: the cofibre of the counit
T_{\text{fin}}F\to F
is
\mathcal{P}_{\text{fin}}\text{-null}
, hence
\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null}
.
This proves the base case.
Assume therefore that there exists a cofibre sequence
F_{n}\to F\to D_{n}
where
F_{n}
is pseudo-coherent and
(F\to D_{n})\in(\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})^{n}
.
Since
F_{n}
and
F
factor through
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
, the same happens for
D_{n}
.
In particular, the cofibre sequence
T_{\text{fin}}D_{n}\to D_{n}\to D_{n+1}
is such that
T_{\text{fin}}D_{n}
is pseudo-coherent and
D_{n}\to D_{n+1}
is in
\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null}
.
Consider then the composite
F\to D_{n}\to D_{n+1}
, which lies in
(\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})^{n+1}
, and compute its fibre
F_{n+1}\to F\to D_{n+1}
.
The octahedral axiom produces then a cofibre sequence
F_{n}\to F_{n+1}\to T_{\text{fin}}D_{n}
, which shows that
F_{n+1}
is pseudo-coherent, proving the claim.
∎

We can finally prove the main result.

Theorem 4.3.16

(Functors out of
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}
)
.

Let
f^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{B}
be a quasi-proper functor and assume that
S_{\text{fin}}\subseteq S_{\mathcal{B}}
is a finite generating set.
Assume furthermore that the family
(\text{ev}_{s})_{s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}
detects coconnective objects.
Then there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})),\qquad\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A}))

induced by the
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding.

Proof.

By construction there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{PCoh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})),\qquad\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})\to\text{Coh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}))

induced by the
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear Yoneda embedding.
In particular, the claim will be proved if

\text{PCoh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}))\simeq\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A}))

are equivalent subcategories of
\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A})
.
The inclusion
(\subseteq)
follows by
4.3.1
since
f^{\text{L}}
is quasi-proper.
For
(\supseteq)
, let
F:(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
be an exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor and assume that it factors through
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
.
By
4.3.15
for every integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a cofibre sequence
F_{n}\to F\to D_{n}
where
F_{n}
is pseudo-coherent and
F\to D_{n}
is in
(\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})^{n}
.
In particular, there are integers
i_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}
such that each
\Sigma^{i_{n}}F_{n}
is connective.
Thus
3.3.11
implies that every
\Sigma^{i_{n}}F_{n}
may be written as a sequential colimit
\Sigma^{i_{n}}F_{n}\simeq\colim_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B^{n}(i)
where each
B^{n}(i)
is in
S_{\mathcal{B}}
.
Hence every
F_{n}
can be written as a sequential colimit of objects in
\mathcal{P}_{!}
, thus showing that
F_{n}\in(\mathcal{P}_{!})_{2}
, being it realized as the cofibre of two terms in
\mathcal{P}_{!}
.
Similarly, since
F
is the geometric realization of
T^{\bullet+1}F
, it follows that
F\in(\mathcal{P}_{!})_{4}
.
Since in the cofibre sequence
F_{4}\to F\to D_{4}
the last map is in
(\mathcal{P}_{!}\text{-null})^{4}
, it follows that
F
must be a direct summand of
F_{4}
, and since pseudo-coherent objects are thick,
F\in\text{PCoh}(\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\mathcal{A}))
is pseudo-coherent.
In particular, the restricted Yoneda

\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{B})\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\mathcal{A}^{\omega}}((\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A}))

is an equivalence.
For the coherent version, it suffices to show that an exact
\mathcal{A}^{\omega}
-linear functor
F:(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\text{op}}\to\mathcal{A}
factors through
\text{Coh}(\mathcal{A})
if and only if it factors through
\text{PCoh}(\mathcal{A})
and it is eventually coconnective.
Since the family
(\text{ev}_{s})_{s\in S_{\mathcal{B}}}
detects coconnective objects, the second equivalence follows.
∎

4.4.
Morphism of universal descent

We now introduce the theory of universal descent associated to a functor of tor-finite categories.

Notation 4.4.1
.

We will denote by
\Delta
the simplex category and by
\Delta_{\leq n}
the full subcategory of
\Delta
spanned by those objects
[m]\in\Delta
such that
m\leq n
.
We will also denote by
\Delta^{+}
the augmented simplex category, and we will denote by
[\text{-}1]
the augmented object.
Let
\mathcal{D}
be a category.
An
augmented cosimplicial object
in
\mathcal{D}
is a functor
\mathcal{C}^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\mathcal{D}
.

Notation 4.4.2
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be a functor in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
We define the following augmented cosimplicial objects.

(1)

First of all, we can regard
\mathcal{C}
as
\mathcal{B}
-module in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
.
In particular, we can form the Cech nerve of
h^{\text{L}}
inside
\text{Mod}_{\mathcal{B}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
Objectwise, this augmented cosimplicial object is given by
[n]\mapsto\mathcal{C}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\dots\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}\mathcal{C}
, where the tensor product is taken
(n+1)
-times if
n\geq 0
.
If
n=\texttt{-}1
we set
[\texttt{-}1]\mapsto\mathcal{B}
.
The augmentation map is given by
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
.
We will generally regard it as a cosimplicial object in
\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
and we will denote it by
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
.

(2)

Since
h^{\text{L}}
is rigid, it restricts to a functor
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\to\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
.
Since
\mathcal{C}^{\omega}
is a
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
-module in
\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
, the same construction as before produces an augmented cosimplicial object
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
.

Since the Ind-completion furnishes a symmetric monoidal equivalence
\text{Ind}:\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}\to\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}}
which extends to modules, the two augmented cosimplicial objects contain the same amount of data. However, it is easier to impose assumptions on the second augmented cosimplicial object.

Definition 4.4.3
.

Let
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
be an augmented cosimplicial object.
We will say that
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}
satisfies the
Beck–Chevalley condition
if for any morphism
\alpha:[m]\to[n]
in
\Delta^{+}
the square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{m}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{m+1}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{n}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{n+1}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d^{0}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces d^{0}}

is horizontally right adjointable, that is, the horizontal maps admit right adjoints and the canonical morphism
\alpha\circ d^{0}_{*}\to d^{0}_{*}\circ\alpha
is an equivalence.

The following result is an instance of the claim made above.

Lemma 4.4.4
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be a functor in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
, and
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
the associated augmented cosimplicial object.
If

Proof.

Apply the definition to

to deduce the existence of a right adjoint

By Ind-completing it follows that

Remark 4.4.5
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be a functor in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
Let
H:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
be the induced monad.
Then there exists a filtration
\dots\to\varphi_{n}\to\dots\to\varphi_{1}\to\varphi_{0}
of small colimit (and finite limit) preserving functors
\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
constructed as follows.
For every integer
n\geq 0
let
\varphi_{n}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{B}
be the
n
-th partial totalization
\text{Tot}_{n}(H^{\bullet})
of the cosimplicial object determined by
H
.
In other terms,

\varphi_{n}\simeq\lim_{\Delta_{\leq n}}(h^{\text{R}}h^{\text{L}}\rightrightarrows h^{\text{R}}h^{\text{L}}h^{\text{R}}h^{\text{L}}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\textstyle{\rightarrow}\\[-2.58334pt]
\textstyle{\rightarrow}\\[-2.58334pt]
\textstyle{\rightarrow}\end{subarray}}\dots).

The natural transformations
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi_{n-1}
are induced by restricting the limit along the inclusion
\Delta_{\leq n-1}\hookrightarrow\Delta_{\leq n}
and, intuitively, they forget the top degree
n
.

In particular, a careful analysis of the natural transformations involved, together with the triangle identities, shows the following result.

Lemma 4.4.6
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be a functor in
\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
and let

be augmentation ideal.
For every integer
n\geq 1
the cofibre of
\varphi_{n}\to\varphi_{n-1}
is equivalent to
H^{n}(I)
.

Proof.

This is standard Dold-Kan correspondence business.
Indeed, for a cosimplicial object
x^{\bullet}
the cofibre of
\text{Tot}_{n}(x^{\bullet})\to\text{Tot}_{n-1}(x^{\bullet})
is equivalent to the
n
-suspension of the normalized piece of
x^{\bullet}
.
If the cosimplicial object comes from a monad, then this normalized piece is equivalent to
\Sigma^{-n}H^{n}(I)
.
∎

Morphisms of universal descent are defined to be those for which the filtration becomes uninteresting for larger indexes.

Definition 4.4.7
.

Let

be a functor in

is of

(1)

The associated augmented cosimplicial object
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}
satisfies the Beck–Chevalley condition.

(2)

There exists an integer

such that the identity on
\mathcal{B}
is a retract of
\varphi_{e}
.

We will call

the

Remark 4.4.8
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be of universal descent.
Since the associated augmented cosimplicial object
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
satisfies the Beck–Chevalley condition, it follows that

preserves compact objects.
In particular, the filtration

restricts to a filtration of exact functors
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}\to\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
.
Furthermore, the identity on
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
is a retract of

for some exponent

.

Our next goal is to show that for a morphism of universal descent

the category

can be reconstructed from the augmented cosimplicial object
(\mathcal{B}^{\omega})^{\bullet}:\Delta^{+}\to\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}}
.
By Ind-completing, the same result holds for

In order to prove our main result we need first a technical lemma.

Lemma 4.4.9
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be of universal descent.
Then

is conservative.

Proof.

Let
\alpha:x\to x^{\prime}
in

be a morphism such that

is an equivalence in
\mathcal{C}
.
By assumption there exists some integer

such that the identity

is a retract of
\varphi_{e}
.
Since equivalences are stable under retracts, to show that
\alpha

\varphi_{e}(\alpha)
is an equivalence.
This follows by the explicit definition of the
\varphi_{n}

∎

We then deduce the following.

Proposition 4.4.10
.

Let
h^{\text{L}}:\mathcal{B}\to\mathcal{C}
be of universal descent.
Then
\mathcal{B}^{\bullet}
is a limit diagram.

Proof.

Since

is conservative by

, by the Barr-Beck theorem it suffices to show that the category
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
admits geometric realizations of
h^{\text{L}}
-split simplicial objects, and those geometric realizations are preserved by
h^{\text{L}}
.
Let
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\Fun(\Delta,\mathcal{B}^{\omega})
be the full subcategory spanned by those cosimplicial objects which admit limit in
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
and which is preserved by
h^{\text{L}}
.
Notice that

is a thick subcategory (since
\mathcal{B}^{\omega}
is idempotent-complete).
Moreover, it contains those cosimplicial objects which admit splittings (since they admit a limit, and since applying
h^{\text{L}}
furnishes cosimplicial objects which admit splittings, hence that have a limit).
Let now
x^{\bullet}:\Delta\to\mathcal{B}
be an
h^{\text{L}}
-split cosimplicial object.
Since

by construction, the claim is proved.
∎

4.5.

\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B})

To prove the second duality result, we will deduce it from a class of tor-finite categories for which it is automatically satisfied.

Definition 4.5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a tor-finite category.
We will say that
\mathcal{R}
is
regular
if the compact objects coincide with the coherent ones, that is
\mathcal{R}^{\omega}=\text{Coh}(\mathcal{R})
.

Example 4.5.2
.

Let
A
be a connective
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-ring and consider the category of modules
\text{Mod}_{A}
.
In general, every perfect objects of
\text{Mod}_{A}
, that is, every compact object, is pseudo-coherent, thus providing an inclusion
\text{Perf}(A)\subseteq\text{PCoh}(A)
.
If
A
is also coconnective, then every perfect object is coconnective, thus proving refining the previous inclusion to
\text{Perf}(A)\subseteq\text{Coh}(A)
.
If
\text{Mod}_{A}
is regular then the other inclusion holds, and by
[
21
, Lemma 11.3.3.3]
it follows that
A
must be discrete.

For this reason, regular
\mathbbm{E}_{\infty}
-rings are defined by just asking
\text{Coh}(A)\subseteq\text{Perf}(A)
.

This implies that our definition of regular categories is not optimal, and therefore that all the arguments which we now propose suffer of the same problem.
Anyway, the our next goal is to show that for regular categories the second abstract Neeman duality is a consequence of the first one.

Let

Since

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Fun_{\mathcal{B}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}(\text{Coh}(\mathcal{R}),\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B}))}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{R}^{\omega}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Fun_{\mathcal{B}^{\omega}}^{\text{ex}}((\mathcal{R}^{\omega})^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(\mathcal{B}))}}

Let

Let

Since

\mathcal{B}(x,y)=f^{\text{R}}\underline{\text{Hom}}_{\mathcal{B}}(x,y)\simeq f^{\text{R}}(x^{\vee}\otimes_{\mathcal{B}}y)

Lemma 4.5.5

Let

Lemma 4.5.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Cat}^{\text{perf}})

5.
Examples

We now present some applications, ranging from modules to quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces, passing through schemes.

5.1.
Modules

We start by discussing a broad class of examples given by module categories.

Remark 5.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
be a symmetric monoidal category and let
x\in\text{CAlg}(\mathcal{C})
be a commutative algebra.
Let
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
denote the category of
x
-modules in
\mathcal{C}
and regard it as a symmetric monoidal category under the relative tensor product
\otimes_{x}
.
Tensoring with
x
determines a functor
x\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\to\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
which admits a conservative right adjoint
\text{res}:\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
.

We now show that module objects are stable under many constructions introduced so far.
Let us first analyze compact generation.

Lemma 5.1.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
be a rigid algebra and let
x\in\mathcal{C}
be a commutative algebra.
Then the category
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
of
x
-module objects in
\mathcal{C}
is a rigid algebra.
Moreover,
x\otimes-:\mathcal{C}\to\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
is rigid.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is presentable stable and the forgetful functor
\text{res}:\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\to\mathcal{C}
preserves and detects all limits and colimits, it follows that
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
is presentable stble.
It also follows that the relative tensor product commutes with small colimits in each variable, making
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\in\text{CAlg}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L}}_{\text{st}})
.
Regarding compact generation, it suffices to notice that the essential image of the compact generators of
\mathcal{C}
compactly generates
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
.
Finally, since tensoring with
x
is a symmetric monoidal functor, we deduce that dualizable and compact objects in
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})
coincide.
Thus
\text{Mod}_{x}(\mathcal{C})\in\text{CAlg}^{\text{rig}}(\text{Pr}^{\text{L},\omega}_{\text{st}})
.
The statement about rigidity of
x\otimes-
is then trivial.
∎

Let us now analyze the

\text{QCoh}(X)
is rigidly-compactly generated.
Its compact generation by a single object follows then by
[
21
, Corollary 9.6.3.2]
.

To sum up, we have the following.

Corollary 5.3.5
.

Let
X
be a quasi-compact and quasi-separated spectral algebraic space.
Then the category of quasi-coherent sheaves
\text{QCoh}(X)
on
X
, equipped with the standard
t
-structure, is tor-finite.
Moreover,
X
comes equipped with a compact connective generator
G
such that
\{G\}\subseteq\text{Perf}(X)_{\geq 0}
determines a finite generating family.
Finally, if
X
is noetherian, then
\text{QCoh}(X)
is coherent.

Proof.

It suffices to understand the objects of finite tor dimension, which, by construction, are designed to be the objects of finite tor dimension in the geometric sense.
Since every perfect complex is such, the claim follows.
∎

We now study morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces.

Remark 5.3.6
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces.
In this case
f
determines a symmetric monoidal and colimit preserving functor
f^{*}:\text{QCoh}(Y)\to\text{QCoh}(X)
which is also right
t
-exact.
Furthermore:

(1)

The pushforward
f_{*}
is right
t
-exact up to a shift precisely when
f
is of finite cohomological dimension.
In this case,
f^{*}
defines a functor of tor-finite categories.

(2)

Thanks to the direct image theorem,
[
21
, Theorem 5.6.02]
, any proper and locally almost of finite presentation of spectral Deligne-Mumford stacks
f:X\to Y
is such that
f_{*}:\text{QCoh}(X)\to\text{QCoh}(Y)
carries almost perfect objects to almost perfect objects.

The definitions of proper and locally almost of finite presentation morphisms are in
[
21
, Definition 5.1.2.1 and Definition 4.2.0.1]
.

Since pseudo-coherent objects are defined to coincide with Lurie’s almost perfect objects on a quasi-compact spectral Deligne-Mumford stack, we deduce the following.

Corollary 5.3.7
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of finite cohomological dimension of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces which is proper and locally almost of finite presentation.
Then
f^{*}:\text{QCoh}(Y)\to\text{QCoh}(X)
is quasi-proper.

We now deduce the following consequence of
Theorem 4.3.16
.

Corollary 5.3.8
.

Let
f:X\to Y
be a morphism of finite cohomological dimension of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces which is proper and locally almost of finite presentation.
Assume also that
Y
is noetherian.
Then there are equivalences of categories

\text{PCoh}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},\text{PCoh}(Y)),\qquad\text{Coh}(X)\to\Fun^{\text{ex}}_{\text{Perf}(Y)}(\text{Perf}(X)^{\text{op}},\text{Coh}(Y)).

induced by the
\text{Perf}(Y)
-Yoneda embedding.

Proof.

Let
G
be a compact generator of
\text{QCoh}(X)
.
It suffices to show that, for a perfect complex
G
, generating
\text{QCoh}(X)
then the Yoneda embedding
\text{QCoh}(X)(G,-):\text{QCoh}(X)\to\text{QCoh}(Y)
detects eventually coconnective objects.
By reducing to
Y
affine, the proof follows by
[
5
, Proposition 7.0.2 and Remark 7.0.3]
.
Their argument, which is for algebraic spaces, can be carried without any modification also for spectral algebraic spaces and works for morphisms of quasi-compact quasi-separated spectral algebraic spaces.
∎

Unfortunately, we do not know any application of
Theorem 4.5.7
in the realm of spectral algebraic geometry.
Actually,
4.5.2
shows that we are far from proving an honest converse to
Theorem 4.3.16
.
