Generic simplicity for self-adjoint operators under bounded potential perturbations Thanks: The first author is a postdoctoral fellow of the Research Foundation – Flanders (FWO) under the postdoctoral grant No 1210226N
MARIANNA CHATZAKOU AND BERNARD HELFFER

Abstract
. We are interested in the generic simplicity of the spectrum of self-adjoint operators under bounded potential perturbations. More precisely, given a semibounded self-adjoint operator with compact resolvent and a suitable space of real-valued bounded perturbations, we study whether all eigenvalues of the perturbed operator are simple for a generic choice of the potential. In the first part of this paper we prove an abstract criterion which ensures that the set of perturbations giving only simple eigenvalues is residual. In the second part, we apply this criterion to several geometric and analytic settings, including sub-Laplacians and maximally hypoelliptic operators on compact manifolds, Laplacians on bounded domains with different boundary conditions, and Schrödinger-type operators on non-compact spaces.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Motivation and abstract strategy

The question whether the spectrum of an operator is simple after a generic zeroth-order perturbation is a classical one. In the elliptic case on compact manifolds it is closely related to the work of Albert and Uhlenbeck on generic properties of eigenfunctions
[
1
,
2
,
3
,
4
,
48
]
. In particular, the work of Uhlenbeck
[
48
]
makes use of many techniques that are not available for all the models considered here. In that setting one usually perturbs an elliptic operator by a smooth real potential and proves that, for a residual set of such potentials, all eigenvalues are simple. Once simplicity is known, several nodal consequences can then be proved. For example, the usual variational proof of Courant’s nodal theorem gives the sharp bound for each eigenfunction when the corresponding eigenvalue is simple. This viewpoint is also useful in the sub-Riemannian setting, where Pleijel and Courant type results for sub-Laplacians are considered, see
[
17
,
15
,
5
,
18
]
.
The present paper isolates the perturbative mechanism behind generic simplicity. We do not start from an elliptic operator, and we do not use smoothness of eigenfunctions in the proof of the abstract theorem. The base operator
L
is only assumed to be self-adjoint on
L^{2}(X)
, semibounded from below, and with compact resolvent. The perturbations are real bounded multiplication operators. More precisely, they are taken from an admissible real Fréchet space
\mathcal{Q}
, see Definition
2.1
, continuously embedded in
L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
. For
q\in\mathcal{Q}
we write

L_{q}=L+M_{q},

and we ask whether the set of
q
’s for which all eigenvalues of
L_{q}
are simple is residual in
\mathcal{Q}
.

The answer is affirmative under a finite-dimensional splitting condition on the eigenspaces. Particularly, if
\lambda
is a multiple eigenvalue of
L_{q}
and
E_{q}=\ker(L_{q}-\lambda I)
, we assume that there is a perturbation
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
such that the finite-dimensional operator
G_{\sigma}:=(P_{E_{q}}M_{\sigma})|_{E_{q}}
, where
P_{E_{q}}:L^{2}(X)\rightarrow E_{q}
is the orthogonal projection with respect to the
L^{2}(X)
inner product, and
M_{\sigma}
is the multiplication by
\sigma
, satisfies the hypothesis

(H
q,λ,σ
)

G_{\sigma}:=(P_{E_{q}}M_{\sigma})|_{E_{q}}:E_{q}\to E_{q},\qquad G_{\sigma}\notin\mathbb{R}I_{E_{q}}.

The point of the paper is that if the condition
C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{Q}
is satisfied, then no unique-continuation argument is needed to find such a
\sigma
. In particular, this permits to include the case of sub-Laplacians with
C^{\infty}(M)
potentials. The perturbative part of the proof is close to the idea of Albert
[
3
]
based on Kato’s analytic perturbation theory
[
31
]
. Finally, we point out that Uhlenbeck’s work
[
48
]
also considers properties of eigenfunctions which do
not fit into the current framework.

1.2.
Relation with known results

Our initial goal was to extend the elliptic results of
[
3
]
. In particular, we cover the cases of smooth perturbations on compact manifolds, bounded perturbations on possibly nonsmooth bounded domains, and bounded smooth perturbations in non-compact examples for which the unperturbed operator already has compact resolvent. A further point of the present approach, in contrast with the classical elliptic perturbation arguments of Albert
[
3
]
, is that the finite-dimensional splitting argument is carried out directly in the complex Hilbert space
L^{2}(X,\mathbb{C})
, and does not require the eigenspaces of the operators under consideration to be spanned by real-valued eigenfunctions. About the assumption on the compact resolvent, we note that this is part of the abstract theorem: bounded potentials preserve the compactness of the resolvent, but in general, they do not create it; see for instance the case of the harmonic oscillator.

The recent work of Yang and Guo
[
49
]
studies a degenerate elliptic Dirichlet problem of the form

-\operatorname{div}(w\nabla u)=\lambda u,

where the weight is positive inside the domain and degenerates on part of the boundary. Their paper proves a Courant nodal-domain theorem in that setting and also obtains a residual simplicity statement for bounded potentials. We note that the Dirichlet realization of
-\operatorname{div}(w\nabla)
, under their compact-embedding assumption on the weighted form domain, is a self-adjoint semibounded operator with compact resolvent; the perturbations
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\Omega)
are precisely bounded multiplication perturbations. Thus their residual simplicity theorem fits into the present framework with
X=\Omega
,
L=-\operatorname{div}(w\nabla)
, and
\mathcal{Q}=L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. Our proof separates the abstract spectral perturbation argument from the operator-specific questions, such as the description of the form domain, compactness, regularity, and unique continuation. Regarding the study of the nodal domains of the aforesaid operator, the authors of
[
49
]
make use of the fact that nodal domains are linked to the variational theory of
spectral minimal partitions as studied in the work of Helffer,
Hoffmann-Ostenhof and Terracini
[
22
]
.

An important class of operators to which our abstract theorem applies is that of sub-Riemannian Laplacians
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
. For such operators, associated with vector fields satisfying Hörmander’s condition, hypoellipticity and subelliptic estimates go back to Hörmander
[
29
]
, while optimal estimates were considered by Rothschild–Stein
[
42
]
. Related spectral control questions for degenerate subelliptic models were recently studied by Harakeh and Hillairet for Baouendi–Grushin type operators in
[
21
]
. In the sub-Riemannian context, the proof of the Pleijel-type bounds of Frank and Helffer uses nilpotent approximation, Weyl asymptotics and Faber–Krahn inequalities
[
17
]
in the equi-regular case of rank
2
. Our paper uses their restriction theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
in one application to Courant’s theorem for sub-Laplacians, proving in particular that the set of potentials
q\in C^{\infty}
for which the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}+q
satisfies Courant’s nodal theorem is residual.

Another class of examples comes from polynomials of vector fields, which are maximally hypoelliptic operators. A characterisation of these operators was conjectured by Helffer and Nourrigat in 1979, see
[
23
,
24
]
and finally was proven in 2022 by Androulidakis, Mohsen and Yuncken
[
6
]
. Although it is not necessary, we follow their presentation here which involves a calculus adapted to filtered foliation (see also the recent abstract
C^{*}
-algebraic approach of Mohsen
[
37
]
).

Several further classes of self-adjoint Schrödinger and magnetic Schrödinger operators on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with compact resolvent are furnished by the classical criteria and examples in
[
45
,
25
,
30
,
32
,
43
,
20
,
28
]
.
Whenever these results give a self-adjoint, semibounded operator
L
with compact resolvent, our abstract theorem applies to
L+M_{q}
for bounded real perturbations
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, for instance when
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{Q}\subset L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{d},\mathbb{R})
.

In
[
48
]
, K. Uhlenbeck also considers metric perturbations for Laplacians, and we hope to extend this
result to the sub-Riemannian case
[
12
]
.

1.3.
Main theorem and applications

In the sequel we denote by
L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset H\rightarrow H
, where
H
is a Hilbert space, the symmetric operator with domain
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
. We say that
L
is semibounded (from below) if

\left\langle Lu,u\right\rangle\geq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert^{2}\,,

where
C\in\mathbb{R}
is some constant. In this case we simply write
L\geq C
.

More precisely, in the sequel we assume that for every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
in some suitable function space
\mathcal{Q}
, the operator
L_{q}:=L+M_{q}
, where
M_{q}
stands for the multiplication operator by
q
, is
self-adjoint, semibounded, and has compact resolvent. We denote by

\lambda_{1}(q)\leq\lambda_{2}(q)\leq\cdots

the eigenvalues of
L_{q}
, counted with multiplicity and arranged in
non-decreasing order.

The main theorem is Theorem
2.13
. It states that, if
L
is self-adjoint, semibounded, has compact resolvent, and the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies Hypothesis (
H_{q,\lambda,\sigma})
for all
q,\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
,
\lambda\in\sigma(L_{q})
, then

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{Q}}=\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\text{ all eigenvalues of }L+q\text{ are simple}\}

is residual in
\mathcal{Q}
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a Fréchet space, it is completely metrizable; hence it is a Baire space by the Baire category theorem for complete metric spaces, and the residual set is dense.

We also discuss the finite-dimensional splitting criterion when perturbations are required to be supported in a fixed open set
\omega\subset X^{\circ}
. In that case, the criterion is the non-vanishing of the restrictions of the eigenspace to
\omega
, and a sufficient condition is the weak unique-continuation property from
\omega
. This localisation is not needed for the global residual theorem when all compactly supported perturbations are available, but it is useful when one wants to prescribe where the perturbation is made.

The applications are collected in Section
3
. On compact manifolds without boundary we treat sub-Laplacians and maximally hypoelliptic operators. For sub-Laplacians, generic simplicity implies a residual form of Courant’s nodal theorem for the perturbed operator. On bounded domains we include Dirichlet and Neumann Laplacians on Lipschitz domains, Robin Laplacians on arbitrary open sets of finite measure in the sense of Arendt and Warma
[
7
]
, and magnetic Schrödinger realizations. We also include the case of an operator which is sums of squares of Hörmander vector fields with continuous uniformly
elliptic coefficient matrices, and the case of weighted divergence-form elliptic operators on bounded
domains. On non-compact spaces we include harmonic and anharmonic oscillators and magnetic Schrödinger operators, where the base operator has compact resolvent.

2.
Abstract framework

Throughout this section,
X
is a smooth manifold, possibly with boundary, endowed with a fixed positive smooth density
d\mu
, and

L^{2}(X):=L^{2}(X,\mathbb{C};d\mu).

When
X
has boundary,
X^{\circ}
denotes its interior.

2.1.
Conditions and preliminary results

Definition 2.1

(Admissible perturbation space)
.

An admissible perturbation space on
X
is a real Fréchet space

(\mathcal{Q},\tau_{\mathcal{Q}})
together with a continuous injective linear map

\iota:\mathcal{Q}\longrightarrow L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}).

By abuse of notation, we identify
q\in\mathcal{Q}
with its image

\iota(q)\in L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
.
All topological notions on
\mathcal{Q}
are understood with
respect to the Fréchet topology
\tau_{\mathcal{Q}}
.

Hypothesis 2.2
.

Let
L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)
be self-adjoint, let

\mathcal{Q}
be an admissible perturbation space, and for
q\in\mathcal{Q}

write

L_{q}:=L+M_{q}.

For
q\in\mathcal{Q}
and for an eigenvalue
\lambda
of
L_{q}
, we set

E_{q,\lambda}:=\operatorname{Ker}(L_{q}-\lambda I).

We say that the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies Hypothesis
(H)
if,
for every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
and every eigenvalue
\lambda
such
that
\dim E_{q,\lambda}\geq 2
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
such that the operator
G_{\sigma}
satisfies
{(\rm H}_{q,\lambda,\sigma})
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E\subset L^{2}(X)
with
\dim E\geq 2
. Then, there exists
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})
such that the operator

\forall c\in\mathbb{R}\qquad G_{\sigma}:=(P_{E}M_{\sigma})|_{E}:E\to E\neq cI_{E}.

Proof.

Assume by contradiction that for every
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})
one has
G_{\sigma}=c_{\sigma}I_{E}

for some
c_{\sigma}\in\mathbb{R}
.
Choose orthonormal vectors
\varphi,\psi\in E
.
Then, for every
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})
, we have

0=\langle G_{\sigma}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle M_{\sigma}\varphi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\int_{X}\sigma\,\varphi\,\overline{\psi}\,d\mu.

Since by the Cauchy–Schwarz
\varphi\,\overline{\psi}\in L^{1}(X,\mathbb{C})
, by the above it follows that

\varphi\,\overline{\psi}=0\qquad\text{a.e. on }X.

On the other hand, because
G_{\sigma}=c_{\sigma}I_{E}
, we have

\langle G_{\sigma}\varphi,\varphi\rangle_{L^{2}}=\langle G_{\sigma}\psi,\psi\rangle_{L^{2}}\qquad\text{for every }\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R}),

implying that

\int_{X}\sigma\bigl(|\varphi|^{2}-|\psi|^{2}\bigr)\,d\mu=0\qquad\text{for every }\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R}),

and so
|\varphi|^{2}=|\psi|^{2}
a.e. on
X
. Combining the above we get
\varphi,\psi=0
a.e. on
X
, which contradicts
\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(X)}=\|\psi\|_{L^{2}(X)}=1
, finishing the proof.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

If
C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{Q}
, then the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})

satisfies hypothesis
(H)
.

Proof.

Follows by
Proposition
2.3
applied to
E=\operatorname{Ker}(L_{q}-\lambda I)
.

∎

Remark 2.5
.

Let
\omega\subset X^{\circ}
be a non-empty open set and set

Q_{\omega}:=C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R}).

For a finite-dimensional subspace
E\subset L^{2}(X)
, define

E_{\omega}:=\{u|_{\omega}:\ u\in E\}\subset L^{2}(\omega).

Then, if
\dim E\geq 2
,

\exists\sigma\in Q_{\omega}\quad\text{such that}\quad(P_{E}M_{\sigma})|_{E}\notin\mathbb{R}I_{E}

if and only if

E_{\omega}\neq\{0\}.

Indeed, if
E_{\omega}=\{0\}
, then clearly

(P_{E}M_{\sigma})|_{E}=0\in\mathbb{R}I_{E}.

Conversely, assume that
E_{\omega}\neq\{0\}
, and suppose that

(P_{E}M_{\sigma})|_{E}\in\mathbb{R}I_{E}\qquad\text{for every }\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R}).

Choose
u\in E
with
u|_{\omega}\neq 0
and
\|u\|_{L^{2}(X)}=1
, and choose

v\in E\cap u^{\perp}
with
\|v\|_{L^{2}(X)}=1
. Repeating the proof of
Proposition
2.3
, with test functions supported in
\omega
, gives

u\overline{v}=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad|u|^{2}=|v|^{2}\quad\text{a.e. on }\omega.

Hence
u=v=0
a.e. on
\omega
, contradicting
u|_{\omega}\neq 0
.
Therefore there exists
\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R})
such that

(P_{E}M_{\sigma})|_{E}\notin\mathbb{R}I_{E}.

In particular, for an eigenspace

E_{q}=\ker(L_{q}-\lambda I),

set

E_{q,\omega}:=\{u|_{\omega}:u\in E_{q}\}\subset L^{2}(\omega).

If, in addition,
C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R})\subset\mathcal{Q}
, then the preceding
criterion shows that the perturbation
\sigma
in Hypothesis (H) can be
chosen with support in
\omega
provided

E_{q,\omega}\neq\{0\}.

A simple sufficient condition for this is the weak unique-continuation
property from
\omega
:

u\in\ker(L_{q}-\lambda I),\qquad u=0\text{ a.e. on }\omega\quad\Longrightarrow\quad u=0\text{ a.e. on }X.

Indeed, if
R_{\omega}:E_{q}\to L^{2}(\omega)
is the restriction map

R_{\omega}u=u|_{\omega},

then
(\mathrm{UCP}_{\omega})
says precisely that

\ker R_{\omega}=\{0\}.

Thus
R_{\omega}
is injective. Hence, if
E_{q}\neq\{0\}
, then

E_{q,\omega}=R_{\omega}(E_{q})\neq\{0\}.

Consequently, when
\dim E_{q}\geq 2
, there exists

\sigma\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\omega,\mathbb{R})

such that

(P_{E_{q}}M_{\sigma})|_{E_{q}}\notin\mathbb{R}I_{E_{q}}.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
H
be a complex Hilbert space, and let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset H\to H

be self-adjoint, semibounded, and with compact resolvent. Let
B\in\mathcal{B}(H)
be bounded and self-adjoint. Then
L+B
is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
, semibounded, and has compact resolvent.

Proof.

Obviously
L+B
has domain
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
and is symmetric. By
[
31
, Chapter V, §4, Theorem 4.3]

L+B
is also self-adjoint on
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
. Now since
L\geq-CI
, for some
C\in\mathbb{R}
and
B\in\mathcal{B}(H)
, we also get
L+B\geq-(C+\left\lVert B\right\rVert)I
; i.e.,
L+B
is also semibounded. Now, to prove that
L+B
has compact resolvent, choose
z<-(C+\|B\|)
. Then
z\in\rho(L+B)
, and also
z\in\rho(L)
. We may therefore apply the resolvent identity:

(L+B-z)^{-1}-(L-z)^{-1}=-(L+B-z)^{-1}B(L-z)^{-1}.

Since
L
has compact resolvent, the operator
(L-z)^{-1}
is compact. Moreover the operators
B,(L+B-z)^{-1}
are bounded, and so the right-hand side of the above equality is a compact operator. Hence, the operator
(L+B-z)^{-1}
is also compact, and this finishes the proof.
∎

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\rightarrow L^{2}(X)
be self-adjoint, semibounded, and with compact resolvent. Let
\mathcal{Q}
be an admissible perturbation space. Then for every
p,q\in\mathcal{Q}
and every
k\geq 1
,

|\lambda_{k}(p)-\lambda_{k}(q)|\leq\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}.

Proof.

To simplify the notation we will denote by
Q_{A}
the closed quadratic form of an operator
A
. Since
p,q\in\mathcal{Q}
are bounded, we have
\operatorname{Dom}(Q_{L})=\operatorname{Dom}(Q_{L_{p}})=\operatorname{Dom}(Q_{L_{q}})
. For
u\in\operatorname{Dom}(Q_{L})
with
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}=1
one has

Q_{L_{p}}(u)=Q_{L}(u)+\int_{X}p|u|^{2}\,d\mu,\qquad Q_{L_{q}}(u)=Q_{L}(u)+\int_{X}q|u|^{2}\,d\mu.

Hence

|Q_{L_{p}}(u)-Q_{L_{q}}(u)|=\left|\int_{X}(p-q)|u|^{2}\,d\mu\right|\leq\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)},

and so

Q_{L_{q}}(u)-\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq Q_{L_{p}}(u)\leq Q_{L_{q}}(u)+\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}.

The assumptions on
L
allow us to apply the min–max principle (see e.g
[
41
, Theorem XIII.1]
) and get

\lambda_{k}(q)-\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\leq\lambda_{k}(p)\leq\lambda_{k}(q)+\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}},

which proves the claim.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

be self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent. Let
p,\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}
, and for
t\in\mathbb{R}
, define
T(t):=L+M_{p}+tM_{\sigma}
.
Let
\lambda
be an eigenvalue of
T(0)=L+M_{p}
of multiplicity

m:=\dim E,\qquad E:=\ker(T(0)-\lambda I).

Then there exist
t_{0}>0
, real-analytic functions
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\geq 1},\mu_{j}:(-t_{0},t_{0})\to\mathbb{R}
and
\{\nu_{j}\}_{j\geq 1},\nu_{j}:(-t_{0},t_{0})\to L^{2}(X,d\mu)

such that:

(1)

for every fixed
|t|<t_{0}
, one has
T(t)\nu_{j}(t)=\mu_{j}(t)\nu_{j}(t)
for all
j\geq 1

and the family
\{\nu_{j}(t)\}_{j\geq 1}
forms a complete orthonormal basis of

L^{2}(X)
;

(2)

after (possible) relabelling, one has
\mu_{j}(0)=\lambda
for
j=1,\dots,m,

and the set
\{\nu_{1}(0),\dots,\nu_{m}(0)\}
forms an orthonormal basis of
E
;

(3)

for every
j=1,\dots,m
, one has
G_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0)=\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)\,\nu_{j}(0).

In particular, the numbers
\mu_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\mu_{m}^{\prime}(0)

are precisely the eigenvalues of
G_{\sigma}
, counted with multiplicity.

Proof.

Consider the complexified family
\widetilde{T}(z):=L+M_{p}+zM_{\sigma},z\in\mathbb{C}.

Since
p,\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}\subset L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
, the multiplication operators

M_{p}
and
M_{\sigma}
are bounded on
L^{2}(X)
. Hence the family

z\mapsto\widetilde{T}(z)
has constant domain
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
, and for every

u\in\operatorname{Dom}(L)
the map

z\longmapsto\widetilde{T}(z)u=(L+M_{p})u+zM_{\sigma}u

is entire. Therefore
\widetilde{T}
is a holomorphic family of type (A) in the sense of
[
31
, Chapter VII, §2]
.
Moreover,
\widetilde{T}(z)^{*}=\widetilde{T}(\overline{z})
, and in particular, for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
T(t)=\widetilde{T}(t)

is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
, since
M_{p}+tM_{\sigma}
is bounded and self-adjoint. We next show that
\widetilde{T}(z)
has compact resolvent for every
z\in\mathbb{C}
.
Set
B_{z}:=M_{p}+zM_{\sigma}
. Then
B_{z}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(X))
. Since
L
is self-adjoint,
i\tau\in\rho(L)
for every
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, and
\|(L-i\tau)^{-1}\|\leq|\tau|^{-1}.

Hence choosing
\tau\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
such that
|\tau|>\|B_{z}\|
we obtain

\|B_{z}(L-i\tau)^{-1}\|<1,

and therefore
\widetilde{T}(z)-i\tau

is invertible, with

(\widetilde{T}(z)-i\tau)^{-1}=(L-i\tau)^{-1}\bigl(I+B_{z}(L-i\tau)^{-1}\bigr)^{-1}.

Since
(L-i\tau)^{-1}
is compact, it follows that

(\widetilde{T}(z)-i\tau)^{-1}
is compact. Now fix
t_{0}>0
, and set
I_{0}:=(-t_{0},t_{0})
. An application of

[
31
, Chapter VII, Theorem 3.9]
yields scalar-valued functions
\widetilde{\lambda}_{1},\widetilde{\lambda}_{2},\dots,

and
L^{2}(X)
-valued functions
\widetilde{\phi}_{1},\widetilde{\phi}_{2},\dots,

all holomorphic on
I_{0}
, such that for every

t\in I_{0}
,

T(t)\widetilde{\phi}_{n}(t)=\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(t)\widetilde{\phi}_{n}(t),\qquad\|\widetilde{\phi}_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(X)}=1,\qquad\langle\widetilde{\phi}_{n}(t),\widetilde{\phi}_{k}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\delta_{nk},

and the family
\{\widetilde{\phi}_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}
is a complete orthonormal basis of

L^{2}(X)
. Moreover, the list
\{\widetilde{\lambda}_{n}(t)\}_{n\geq 1}
exhausts the spectrum of

T(t)
, counted with multiplicity.
Since
\lambda
has multiplicity
m
for
T(0)
, after relabelling the family if
necessary, we may assume that

\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}(0)=\lambda\qquad\text{for }j=1,\dots,m,

and

\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}(0)\neq\lambda\qquad\text{for }j\geq m+1.

For every
j\geq 1
, define
\mu_{j}(t):=\widetilde{\lambda}_{j}(t),
and
\nu_{j}(t):=\widetilde{\phi}_{j}(t),
for
t\in I_{0}.

Then the functions
\mu_{j},\nu_{j}
are real-analytic on
I_{0}
and satisfy

T(t)\nu_{j}(t)=\mu_{j}(t)\nu_{j}(t),\qquad\|\nu_{j}(t)\|_{L^{2}(X)}=1,\qquad\langle\nu_{j}(t),\nu_{k}(t)\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\delta_{jk},

and the family
\{\nu_{j}(t)\}_{j\geq 1}
is a complete orthonormal basis of
L^{2}(X)
for every

t\in I_{0}
. Moreover,

\mu_{j}(0)=\lambda\qquad\text{for }j=1,\dots,m.

At
t=0
, the vectors
\nu_{1}(0),\dots,\nu_{m}(0)
are orthonormal eigenvectors of

T(0)
corresponding to the eigenvalue
\lambda
. Since
\lambda
has
multiplicity
m
, they form an orthonormal basis of
E
.
It remains to prove (3).
Fix
j\in\{1,\dots,m\}
, and let
e\in E
be arbitrary. Taking the inner product of

T(t)\nu_{j}(t)=\mu_{j}(t)\nu_{j}(t)

with
e
, we get

\langle T(t)\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\mu_{j}(t)\,\langle\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}.

Since
T(t)=T(0)+tM_{\sigma},

this becomes

\langle T(0)\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}+t\,\langle M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\mu_{j}(t)\,\langle\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}.

Now
e\in E
means
T(0)e=\lambda e,

and since
T(0)
is self-adjoint, we have

\langle T(0)\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\langle\nu_{j}(t),T(0)e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\lambda\,\langle\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}.

Hence

t\,\langle M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=(\mu_{j}(t)-\lambda)\,\langle\nu_{j}(t),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}.

For
t\neq 0
, divide by
t
and let
t\to 0
. Since
\mu_{j}

is real-analytic,

\frac{\mu_{j}(t)-\lambda}{t}\longrightarrow\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0).

Since
\nu_{j}
is
L^{2}(X)
-valued real-analytic, we have
\nu_{j}(t)\longrightarrow\nu_{j}(0)
and
M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(t)\longrightarrow M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0)
in
L^{2}(X)
.
Therefore

\langle M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)\,\langle\nu_{j}(0),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}\qquad\text{for every }e\in E.

Since
\nu_{j}(0)\in E
, this is equivalent to

\langle P_{E}M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}=\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)\,\langle\nu_{j}(0),e\rangle_{L^{2}(X)}\qquad\text{for every }e\in E.

Hence

P_{E}M_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0)=\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)\,\nu_{j}(0),

that is,

G_{\sigma}\nu_{j}(0)=\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)\,\nu_{j}(0).

Thus each
\nu_{j}(0)
,
j=1,\dots,m
, is an eigenvector of
G_{\sigma}
with
eigenvalue
\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)
. Since
\nu_{1}(0),\dots,\nu_{m}(0)

is an orthonormal basis of
E
, the numbers
\mu_{1}^{\prime}(0),\dots,\mu_{m}^{\prime}(0)

are exactly the eigenvalues of
G_{\sigma}
, counted with multiplicity.
∎

Lemma 2.9
.

Let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

be self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent, and let
q\in\mathcal{Q}
. For
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we also define the sets

A_{n}:=\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\lambda_{1}(q),\dots,\lambda_{n}(q)\text{ are simple}\},\qquad A_{0}:=\mathcal{Q}.

Then for every
n\geq 1
, the set
A_{n}
is open in
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.6
, every
L_{q}
is self-adjoint on
\operatorname{Dom}(L)
, bounded from below, and has compact resolvent. Hence its spectrum consists of a non-decreasing sequence of real eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}(q)\leq\lambda_{2}(q)\leq\cdots\nearrow+\infty,

counted with multiplicity.
We claim that for each
n\geq 1
the set
A_{n}
is open in
\tau_{\mathcal{Q}}
. Indeed, let
p\in A_{n}
. Then, by definition of
A_{n}
, we have

\lambda_{1}(p)<\lambda_{2}(p)<\cdots<\lambda_{n}(p)<\lambda_{n+1}(p).

Set
\delta:=2^{-1}\min_{1\leq j\leq n}\bigl(\lambda_{j+1}(p)-\lambda_{j}(p)\bigr)>0.

If
q\in\mathcal{Q}
satisfies
\left\lVert p-q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}<\delta,

then Lemma
2.7
, yields

|\lambda_{j}(p)-\lambda_{j}(q)|<\delta\qquad\text{for }1\leq j\leq n+1.

Hence, for every
j=1,\dots,n
, we have

\displaystyle\lambda_{j+1}(q)-\lambda_{j}(q)

\displaystyle=\bigl(\lambda_{j+1}(p)-\lambda_{j}(p)\bigr)+\bigl(\lambda_{j+1}(q)-\lambda_{j+1}(p)\bigr)-\bigl(\lambda_{j}(q)-\lambda_{j}(p)\bigr)

\displaystyle>\bigl(\lambda_{j+1}(p)-\lambda_{j}(p)\bigr)-2\delta\geq 0.

Since the first inequality is strict, we obtain

\lambda_{1}(q)<\lambda_{2}(q)<\cdots<\lambda_{n}(q)<\lambda_{n+1}(q).

Thus
q\in A_{n}
, and because the inclusion
\mathcal{Q}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})
is continuous, we have shown that
A_{n}
is open in
\tau_{\mathcal{Q}}
.
∎

Lemma 2.10
.

Let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

be self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent. Assume that the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies the hypothesis
(H)
. Fix
n\geq 0
, and let

p\in A_{n}
. Suppose that the eigenvalue

\lambda:=\lambda_{n+1}(p)

of
L_{p}
has multiplicity
m:=\dim\operatorname{Ker}(L_{p}-\lambda I)>1.

Then for every neighborhood
U
of
p
in
\mathcal{Q}
, there exists

q\in U\cap A_{n}

such that the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n+1}(q)
of
L_{q}

is strictly smaller than the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\lambda_{n+1}(p)
of
L_{p}
.

Proof.

Since
\lambda=\lambda_{n+1}(p)
has multiplicity
m
, the ordered eigenvalues of
L_{p}

satisfy

\lambda_{n+1}(p)=\lambda_{n+2}(p)=\cdots=\lambda_{n+m}(p)=\lambda.

Moreover, since
p\in A_{n}
, the first
n
eigenvalues are simple, and hence for all
j\in\{1,\cdots,n\}
we have
\lambda_{j}(p)<\lambda
.
Also, by definition of the multiplicity
m
, we know
\lambda<\lambda_{n+m+1}(p).

Choose
\rho>0
such that

0<\rho<\frac{1}{3}\min\Bigl(\{\lambda-\lambda_{j}(p):1\leq j\leq n\}\cup\{\lambda_{n+m+1}(p)-\lambda\}\Bigr),\qquad n\geq 1.

and if
n=0
, choose

0<\rho<\frac{1}{3}\bigl(\lambda_{m+1}(p)-\lambda\bigr).

If we set
I:=(\lambda-\rho,\lambda+\rho)
, then we see that
I
contains no point of
\sigma(L_{p})
other than
\lambda
.
Let

E:=\operatorname{Ker}(L_{p}-\lambda I).

Since
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies (H), there exists
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}

such that
G_{\sigma}
is not a scalar multiple of the identity.
Now let

g_{1}\leq g_{2}\leq\cdots\leq g_{m}

be the eigenvalues of
G_{\sigma}
, counted with multiplicity. Since
G_{\sigma}\notin\mathbb{R}I_{E}
, not all
g_{i}
are equal; hence there exists an integer
1\leq r<m
such that

g_{1}=\cdots=g_{r}<g_{r+1}\leq\cdots\leq g_{m}.

Consider the perturbed family
T(t):=L_{p}+tM_{\sigma}.

By Lemma
2.8
, after shrinking to a sufficiently small interval
(-t_{0},t_{0})

there exist real-analytic functions

\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{m}(t)

such that

\mu_{j}(0)=\lambda,\qquad\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0)=g_{j},\qquad j=1,\dots,m,

and these are precisely the eigenvalues of

T(t)
lying in the interval
I
, counted with multiplicity, i.e., there are at least
m
eigenvalues of
T(t)
,
t\neq 0
, inside
I
. Now, for every pair
1\leq j\leq r<k\leq m
, we have

\mu_{k}(t)-\mu_{j}(t)=(\mu_{k}^{\prime}(0)-\mu_{j}^{\prime}(0))t+o(t)=(g_{k}-g_{1})t+o(t).

Setting

c:=\min_{k>r}(g_{k}-g_{1})>0,

we see that there exists
t_{1}>0
such that for every

0<t<t_{1}

and every such pair
(j,k)
, one has

\mu_{k}(t)-\mu_{j}(t)>\frac{c}{2}\,t>0.

Hence, for fixed
0<t<t_{1}
,

\mu_{j}(t)<\mu_{k}(t)\qquad\text{whenever }1\leq j\leq r<k\leq m,

and therefore, for fixed
0<t<t_{1}
, the smallest value among
\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{m}(t)

can only be attained among
\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{r}(t).

Equivalently, if

(2.1)

\nu(t):=\min\{\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{m}(t)\}

and

(2.2)

J(t):=\{\,j\in\{1,\dots,m\}:\mu_{j}(t)=\nu(t)\,\},

then

J(t)\subset\{1,\dots,r\},\qquad\text{hence}\qquad\#J(t)\leq r.

Since
U
is a neighborhood of
p
in
\mathcal{Q}
, and since the map

t\longmapsto p+t\sigma

is continuous from
\mathbb{R}
to
\mathcal{Q}
, there exists
t_{2}>0
such that

p+t\sigma\in U\qquad\text{whenever }|t|<t_{2}.

Since, by Lemma
2.9
, the set
A_{n}
is open, there exists
t_{3}>0
such that

p+t\sigma\in A_{n}\qquad\text{whenever }|t|<t_{3}.

If
n\geq 1
, set

\delta_{1}:=(\lambda-\rho)-\lambda_{n}(p)>0,\qquad\delta_{2}:=\lambda_{n+m+1}(p)-(\lambda+\rho)>0.

By Lemma
2.7
, we have

|\lambda_{k}(p+t\sigma)-\lambda_{k}(p)|\leq\|t\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}=|t|\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}\qquad\text{for all }k\geq 1.

Since
\sigma\in\mathcal{Q}\subset L^{\infty}(X)
, and
\sigma\neq 0
, we may choose

t_{4}:=\frac{1}{2\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}}\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}>0.

Then for every
|t|<t_{4}
and for
n\geq 1
we have

\lambda_{n}(p+t\sigma)<\lambda-\rho,\qquad\lambda_{n+m+1}(p+t\sigma)>\lambda+\rho.

Indeed, if
n\geq 1
, then for
t_{4}
as above we have

|t|\,\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}<\delta_{1},\delta_{2}

implying that

\lambda_{n}(p+t\sigma)\leq\lambda_{n}(p)+|\lambda_{n}(p+t\sigma)-\lambda_{n}(p)|<\lambda_{n}(p)+\delta_{1}=\lambda-\rho,

and also that

\lambda_{n+m+1}(p+t\sigma)\geq\lambda_{n+m+1}(p)-|\lambda_{n+m+1}(p+t\sigma)-\lambda_{n+m+1}(p)|>\lambda_{n+m+1}(p)-\delta_{2}=\lambda+\rho.

If
n=0
, only the second inequality is needed, and one may take

t_{4}:=\frac{\delta_{2}}{2\|\sigma\|_{L^{\infty}(X)}}.

Now fix

0<t<t_{\min}:=\min\{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4}\},

and set

q:=p+t\sigma.

Since
t<t_{2},t_{3}
, we have

q\in U\cap A_{n}\,,

and since
t<t_{4}
,

\lambda_{n}(q)<\lambda-\rho

if
n\geq 1
, and

\lambda_{n+m+1}(q)>\lambda+\rho.

Moreover, for
t<t_{0}
, the interval

I:=(\lambda-\rho,\lambda+\rho)

contains at least the
m
eigenvalues

\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{m}(t)

of the operator

T(t)=L_{p}+tM_{\sigma}=L_{q},

counted with multiplicity. Actually, we will show that
I
contains exactly
m
eigenvalues of
L_{q}
: it is easy to see that there are exactly
n
eigenvalues of
L_{q}
below the interval
I
.
Indeed, if
n\geq 1
, then
\lambda_{n}(q)<\lambda-\rho
, so there are at least
n

eigenvalues below
I
; on the other hand, if there were more than
n
eigenvalues below
I
,
then together with the (at least)
m
eigenvalues lying in
I
this would give at least
n+m+1

eigenvalues strictly below
\lambda+\rho
, contradicting

\lambda_{n+m+1}(q)>\lambda+\rho.

Hence there are exactly
n
eigenvalues of
L_{q}
below
I
, and since also
\lambda_{n+m+1}(q)\notin I
, we see that there are exactly
m
eigenvalues of
L_{q}
inside
I
. The case
n=0
is simpler.
Therefore the eigenvalues of
L_{q}
lying in
I
are precisely

\lambda_{n+1}(q),\lambda_{n+2}(q),\dots,\lambda_{n+m}(q),

counted with multiplicity, and so, for a fixed
0<t<t_{{\rm min}}
, one has
\{\lambda_{n+1}(q),\cdots,\lambda_{n+m}(q)\}=\{\mu_{1}(t),\cdots,\mu_{m}(t)\}
as multisets. In particular,

\lambda_{n+1}(q)

is the smallest eigenvalue of
L_{q}
lying in
I
.
Now, for a fixed
0<t<t_{{\rm min}}
, consider
\nu(t)
as in (
2.1
).
Since

\mu_{j}(t)<\mu_{k}(t)\qquad\text{for all }1\leq j\leq r<k\leq m,

the minimum
\nu(t)
can only be attained among

\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{r}(t).

Since the multiset of eigenvalues of
L_{q}
lying in
I
is exactly

\{\mu_{1}(t),\dots,\mu_{m}(t)\},

the multiplicity of the eigenvalue
\nu(t)
of
L+q
is exactly
\#J(t)
where
J(t)
is as in (
2.2
).
But
\nu(t)
is the smallest eigenvalue of
L_{q}
in
I
, so

\nu(t)=\lambda_{n+1}(q).

Therefore

\operatorname{mult}\bigl(\lambda_{n+1}(q)\bigr)=\#J(t)\leq r<m.

Hence the multiplicity of
\lambda_{n+1}(q)
is strictly smaller than that of

\lambda_{n+1}(p)
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Lemma 2.11
.

Let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

be self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent. Assume that the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies the hypothesis
(H)
. Then, for every
n\geq 0
, the set
A_{n+1}
is dense in
A_{n}
.

Proof.

Fix
p\in A_{n}
and let
U
be a neighborhood of
p\in\mathcal{Q}
. If
\lambda_{n+1}(p)
is already simple,
then
p\in U\cap A_{n+1}
and there is nothing to prove. Otherwise, apply Lemma
2.10
to find

q_{1}\in U\cap A_{n}
such that the multiplicity of
\lambda_{n+1}(q_{1})
is strictly smaller than that of

\lambda_{n+1}(p)
. If
\lambda_{n+1}(q_{1})
is still not simple, then
q_{1}\in A_{n}
and
A_{n}
is open, so

U\cap A_{n}
is an open neighborhood of
q_{1}
; applying Lemma
2.10
again, we get

q_{2}\in U\cap A_{n}
with strictly smaller multiplicity for
\lambda_{n+1}(q_{2})
. This process terminates after finitely many steps, producing some

q\in U\cap A_{n}

for which
\lambda_{n+1}(q)
is simple. Then
q\in U\cap A_{n+1}
, which proves that
A_{n+1}
is dense in
A_{n}
.
∎

2.2.
The main abstract theorem

Theorem 2.13
.

Let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

be self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent. Assume that the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
satisfies the hypothesis
(H)
. Then the set

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{Q}}:=\Bigl\{q\in\mathcal{Q}:\text{all eigenvalues of }L+M_{q}\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
\mathcal{Q}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.9
, each
A_{n}
is open in
\mathcal{Q}
. By Lemma
2.9
, each
A_{n}
is open in
\mathcal{Q}
.
By Lemma
2.11
,
A_{n+1}
is dense in
A_{n}
for every

n\geq 0
. Since
A_{0}=\mathcal{Q}
, it follows by induction that
each
A_{n}
is open and dense in
\mathcal{Q}
. Moreover,

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{Q}}=\bigcap_{n\geq 1}A_{n}.

Hence
\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is a countable intersection of open
dense subsets of
\mathcal{Q}
, and is therefore residual in

\mathcal{Q}
. Since
\mathcal{Q}
is a Fréchet space, it is a Baire
space. Consequently, by the Baire category theorem,

\mathcal{G}_{\mathcal{Q}}
is dense in
\mathcal{Q}
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Below are geometric settings, and corresponding admissible perturbation spaces
\mathcal{Q}
for which hypothesis (H) is automatic:

•

In the case where
X=M
is a compact manifold
M
without boundary, the natural choice for the admissible perturbation space is
\mathcal{Q}=C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, endowed with its usual Fréchet topology. Clearly,

C_{c}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})=C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{Q}.

•

In the case where
X=M
is a compact manifold with boundary and interior
M^{\circ}
, the natural choice for the admissible perturbation space is
\mathcal{Q}=C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, endowed with its usual Fréchet topology. We have

C_{c}^{\infty}(M^{\circ},\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{Q}.

If
M
is realised as the closure
M=\overline{\Omega}
of a bounded regular domain
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, one may equivalently write

\mathcal{Q}=C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}),

and then

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}).

•

In the case where
X=\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a bounded domain, possibly with non-regular boundary, a basic choice for the admissible perturbation space is

\mathcal{Q}=L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}),

endowed with the norm

\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}:=\operatorname*{ess\,sup}_{x\in\Omega}|q(x)|.

We have

C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{Q}.

Thus in the non-smooth boundary setting one does not impose any regularity up to the boundary on the perturbation.

•

In the case of a non-compact domain
X\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
the basic example for
\mathcal{Q}
is the space

C_{b}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}):=\{q\in C^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}):\partial^{\alpha}q\in L^{\infty}(X)\text{ for every multi-index }\alpha\},

endowed with the seminorms

\left\lVert q\right\rVert_{m}:=\max_{|\alpha|\leq m}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}q\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X)}.

We have

C_{c}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C_{b}^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R})=\mathcal{Q}.

Therefore, in all the above settings, Proposition
2.3
shows that hypothesis (H) is automatically satisfied. In particular, in the regular boundary case one may work with perturbations that are smooth up to the boundary, whereas in the non-regular boundary examples of Section
3.2
one works with bounded perturbations in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
. In particular, if the operator

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset L^{2}(X)\to L^{2}(X)

is self-adjoint, semibounded and with compact resolvent, then the abstract Theorem
2.13
applies to the pair
(L,\mathcal{Q})
.

3.
Applications

In this section we apply the abstract theorem, Theorem
2.13
, to several classes of operators on compact manifolds without boundary, compact manifolds with boundary, bounded domains, and non-compact manifolds.

3.1.
On compact manifolds without boundary

Here the underlying setting is a compact manifold
M
without boundary and the space of admissible perturbations is
\mathcal{Q}=C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

3.1.1.
Sub-Laplacians and Courant’s nodal theorem

Recall that the sub-Laplacian
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
on
M
is defined by

\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}:=\sum_{i=1}^{m}X_{i}^{*}X_{i}\,,

where
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M)
are smooth vector fields on
M
, and
X^{*}
stands the formal adjoint of
X\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M)
defined by

\left\langle X^{*}u,v\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}=\left\langle u,Xv\right\rangle_{L^{2}(M)}\qquad\text{for all }u,v\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}).

One then sees that
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
satisfies

(\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}u,u)_{L^{2}(M)}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\left\lVert X_{i}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}^{2},\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})\,.

The vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M)
satisfy the so-called Hörmander’s condition introduced in
[
29
]
. Let
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}
denote the smallest number of iterated commutators of the vector fields
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
that is needed to generate
T_{x}M
at any
x\in M
. Hörmander proved that the operator
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
is hypoelliptic
[
29
, Theorem 1.1]
and also provided a sub-elliptic estimate with loss. Later on, Rothschild and Stein
[
42
, Theorem 17 and estimate (17.20), p. 311]
proved the following optimal sub-elliptic estimate: there exists
C>0
such that for every
u\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
,

(3.1)

\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{2/k}(M)}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}+C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\,.

To be precise, the sub-elliptic estimate (
3.1
) appears in
[
42
]
in a local form. However, it is not difficult to globalise the argument on the whole
M
, see e.g.
[
33
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Moreover, since
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
is symmetric and nonnegative,
hypoellipticity of
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}+I
and compactness of
M
imply that
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
is essentially self-adjoint;
see
[
40
, Theorem X.26]
. Hence it admits a self-adjoint extension with

\mathcal{L_{\rm sub}}:\operatorname{Dom}(\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub})\subset L^{2}(M)\longrightarrow L^{2}(M)\,.

We keep the notation
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
for its self-adjoint extension, and we note that
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
has compact resolvent, and there exists an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions

(\varphi_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of
L^{2}(M)
and a nondecreasing sequence of real eigenvalues
(\lambda_{j})_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
, multiplicity counted, such that

\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}\varphi_{j}=\lambda_{j}\varphi_{j},\qquad(\varphi_{i},\varphi_{j})_{L^{2}(M)}=\delta_{ij},\qquad 0=\lambda_{0}<\lambda_{1}\leq\lambda_{2}\leq\cdots\to+\infty\,.

Moreover, each
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
is smooth. Indeed, since
\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(M)
and so also
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}\varphi_{j}\in L^{2}(M)
, a repeated use of the sub-elliptic estimate (
3.1
) shows that
\varphi_{j}\in H^{s}(M)
for arbitrary large
s
, and so
\varphi_{j}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Similar arguments show that if
q\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
, and
u\in L^{2}(M)
satisfies
(\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}+q)u=\lambda u
for
some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, then
u\in C^{\infty}(M)
, i.e. the eigenfunctions of
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}+q
are smooth.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
be the sub-Laplacian associated with the vector fields

X_{1},\dots,X_{m}
. Then the set

\mathcal{C}_{\rm sub}:=\Bigl\{q\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\text{ every eigenfunction of }\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}+M_{q}\text{ satisfies Courant's nodal theorem}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

The proof follows from Theorem
2.13
and
[
15
, Theorem 1]
which says that for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
, any eigenfunction of
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
with eigenvalue

\lambda_{k}
has at most
k+{\rm mult}(\lambda_{k})-1
nodal domains, where
{\rm mult}(\lambda_{k})
denotes the multiplicity of
\lambda_{k}
. Let us recall that the key point is to verify in the sub-Riemannian case the so-called restriction lemma, see
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
and references therein.
∎

3.1.2.
Maximally hypoelliptic differential operators

We fix vector fields

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M)

satisfying Hörmander’s bracket-generating condition. We denote by

\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
the bracket filtration generated by these vector
fields in the sense of
[
6
, Definition 1.3]
. Thus

\mathcal{F}^{0}:=0,

and, for
j\geq 1
, we have

\mathcal{F}^{j}:=\operatorname{span}_{C^{\infty}(M)}\left\{[X_{i_{1}},[X_{i_{2}},\ldots,[X_{i_{\ell-1}},X_{i_{\ell}}]\ldots]]:1\leq\ell\leq j\right\},

with the convention that brackets of length
1
are just the vector fields

X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}
. In particular,

\mathcal{F}^{1}=\operatorname{span}_{C^{\infty}(M)}\{X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}\}.

There exists
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\mathcal{F}^{N}=\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M).

Moreover,

[\mathcal{F}^{i},\mathcal{F}^{j}]\subset\mathcal{F}^{i+j},\qquad i,j\geq 0,

so
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}=(\mathcal{F}^{1},\ldots,\mathcal{F}^{N})
is a filtered
foliation.

Although the operator is written as a polynomial in the first-layer vector
fields
X_{j}\in\mathcal{F}^{1}
, the associated nilpotent model is determined
by the full bracket filtration
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
.

The intrinsic definition of
Helffer–Nourrigat cone defined here in the more modern and rather technical way as in
[
6
]
can be found in its initial form in
[
23
, p.13]
.

Definition 3.2

(Helffer–Nourrigat cone)
.

For
x\in M
, we set

I_{x}:=\{f\in C^{\infty}(M):f(x)=0\},\qquad\mathcal{F}_{x}^{j}:=\mathcal{F}^{j}/I_{x}\mathcal{F}^{j},

and define

\mathfrak{g}_{x}:=\bigoplus_{j=1}^{N}\mathcal{F}_{x}^{j}/\mathcal{F}_{x}^{j-1}.

Then
\mathfrak{g}_{x}
is a graded nilpotent Lie algebra, and we denote by
G_{x}
the associated simply connected
graded nilpotent Lie group. Following
[
6
, § 1.4]
, if
X\in\mathcal{X}^{\infty}(M)
we denote by
\widetilde{X}\in\mathcal{X}^{\infty}(M\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
the vector field
\widetilde{X}(x,t)=(X(x),0)
. The adiabatic foliation associated to
\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
is

a\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}=t\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{1}+t^{2}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{2}+\cdots+t^{N}\widetilde{\mathcal{F}}^{N},

and the Helffer–Nourrigat cone at
x
is

T_{x}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}:=\left\{\xi\in\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\ast}:(x,\xi,0)\in\overline{T^{\ast}M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}^{\ast}}^{\,(a\mathcal{F}^{\bullet})^{\ast}}\right\}.

Via Kirillov’s orbit method, more precisely through the canonical identification

G_{x}\backslash\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\ast}\simeq\widehat{G_{x}},

the image

T_{x}^{\ast}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}/G_{x}\subset G_{x}\backslash\mathfrak{g}_{x}^{\ast}

may be regarded as the corresponding closed dilation-invariant subset of the unitary dual

\widehat{G_{x}}
.

Characterisation 3.3

(Maximal hypoellipticity in the compact case,
[
6
, Theorem C]
)
.

Let
D:C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})\rightarrow C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
be a non-commutative polynomial of the form
D=\sum_{|I|\leq k}a_{I}X_{I},
where
a_{I}\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
. Then
D
is called a maximally hypoelliptic operator if one of the following equivalent conditions is satisfied:

•

for every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R},D:H_{X}^{s+k}(M)\rightarrow H_{X}^{s}(M)
is invertible modulo compact operators;

•

for every (or for some)
s\in\mathbb{R}
and for every distribution
u:Du\in H_{X}^{s}(M)\Rightarrow u\in H_{X}^{s+k}(M)
;

•

for every
x\in M
and every non-trivial
\pi\in T_{x}^{*}\mathcal{F}^{\bullet}
, the principal symbol
\sigma_{k}(D,x,\pi)
is injective on smooth vectors
C^{\infty}(\pi)
.

Recall that the Sobolev space
H_{X}^{s}
, for
s\in\mathbb{N}
, is defined as

H_{X}^{s}(M):=\bigl\{\,u\in L^{2}(M)\}\,:X_{I}u\in L^{2}(M)\,\,\text{for all }\,\,|I|\leq s\,\bigr\}.

The definition extends to any
s\in\mathbb{R}
by interpolation (
s>0
) or duality (
s<0
).

Proposition 3.4
.

Let
D
satisfy Characterisation
3.3
. Assume moreover that
D
is formally self-adjoint on
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{C})
and bounded from below. Then, the set

\mathcal{G}_{D}:=\Bigl\{q\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\text{ all eigenvalues of }D+q\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

Since
D
is maximally hypoelliptic,
[
6
, Theorem 3.43(a)]
gives injectivity of its principal symbol. Since
D
is formally symmetric, the same applies to the formal adjoint, and
[
6
, Theorem 3.44(b)]
implies that
D
is
*
-maximally hypoelliptic. Hence
[
6
, Corollary 3.45(a)]
implies that
D
is essentially self-adjoint
on
L^{2}(M)
. We keep the same notation for its self-adjoint extension. Since
D
is bounded from below, choose
z<\inf\sigma(D)
. Then
z\in\rho(D)
is an element of the resolvent set of
D
, and so the operator

D-z:\operatorname{Dom}(D)\subset L^{2}(M)\to L^{2}(M)

is bijective. We now show that

(3.2)

(D-z)^{-1}:L^{2}(M)\to\widetilde{H}^{k}(M)

is bounded. Let
f\in L^{2}(M)=H_{X}^{0}(M)
, and set
u:=(D-z)^{-1}f.
Then
(D-z)u=f\in H_{X}^{0}(M).

Since the operator
D-z
trivially has the same principal symbol
as
D
it is also maximally hypoelliptic by

[
6
, Theorem 3.43(a)]
, and so by Characterisation
3.3

u\in H_{X}^{k}(M).

Thus
(D-z)^{-1}
is well-defined as a map

L^{2}(M)\to H_{X}^{k}(M).

To prove boundedness, let
f_{n}\to f
in
L^{2}(M)
, and assume that

(D-z)^{-1}f_{n}\to u\qquad\text{in }H_{X}^{k}(M).

Set
u_{n}:=(D-z)^{-1}f_{n}.
Since the inclusion
H_{X}^{k}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(M)
is continuous, we also have
u_{n}\to u
in
L^{2}(M)
.
Moreover,

(D-z)u_{n}=f_{n}\to f\qquad\text{in }L^{2}(M).

Because
D-z
is closed (being self-adjoint), it follows that

u\in\operatorname{Dom}(D)\qquad\text{and}\qquad(D-z)u=f.

Thus
u=(D-z)^{-1}f
, and the graph of (
3.2
) is closed. By the closed graph theorem

(D-z)^{-1}
is bounded.
Now by
[
29
]
the inclusion
H_{X}^{k}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(M)
is compact. A combination of the latter together with the fact that the operator (
3.2
) is bounded, implies that
D
has compact resolvent. The conclusion then follows by Theorem
2.13
, and the proof is complete.
∎

3.1.3.
Sums of squares with continuous coefficients

The next example shows that, for Hörmander-type divergence-form operators with uniformly elliptic coefficients, the abstract generic-simplicity theorem applies and one also obtains Courant’s nodal domain bound for the generic potentials.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{m}\in\mathfrak{X}^{\infty}(M)
be a Hörmander system of
smooth vector fields on a compact manifold
M
without boundary, and let

A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq m}\in C^{0}(M;\operatorname{Sym}_{m}(\mathbb{R})).

Assume that
A
is uniformly elliptic in the
X
-directions in the sense of

[
47
, p. 352]
; namely, there exist constants
0<c\leq C
such that

c|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}a_{ij}(x)\xi_{i}\xi_{j}\leq C|\xi|^{2},\qquad x\in M,\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{m}.

On
H_{X}^{1}(M)
we define the form

\mathfrak{q}_{L_{A}}(u,v):=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}\int_{M}a_{ij}(x)(X_{j}u)\overline{X_{i}v}\,d\mu.

We denote by

L_{A}:\operatorname{Dom}(L_{A})\subset L^{2}(M)\longrightarrow L^{2}(M)

the self-adjoint operator associated with
\mathfrak{q}_{L_{A}}
. Formally,

L_{A}=\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}X_{i}^{*}a_{ij}(x)X_{j}.

Then the set

\mathcal{C}_{A}:=\Bigl\{q\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\text{ real-valued eigenfunctions of }L_{A}+M_{q}\text{ satisfy Courant's theorem}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

By the Hörmander condition, the embedding

H_{X}^{1}(M)\hookrightarrow L^{2}(M)

is compact. The uniform ellipticity of
A
gives

c\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|X_{j}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2}\leq\mathfrak{q}_{L_{A}}(u,u)\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{m}\|X_{j}u\|_{L^{2}(M)}^{2},

so that the form norm is equivalent to the norm of
H_{X}^{1}(M)
.
By Proposition
4.3
and the above,
L_{A}
has compact resolvent, and so by
Theorem
2.13
we get that

\mathcal{G}_{A}:=\bigl\{q\in C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R}):\hbox{ all eigenvalues of }L_{A}+M_{q}\hbox{ are simple}\bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
. Next we prove that

\mathcal{G}_{A}\subset\mathcal{C}_{A}.

Let
q\in\mathcal{G}_{A}
, and let
u
be a real-valued eigenfunction of

L_{A}+M_{q}
associated with the
k
-th eigenvalue
\lambda_{k}(q)
. Thus

u\in\operatorname{Dom}(L_{A})\subset H_{X}^{1}(M)

and

(L_{A}+M_{q})u=\lambda_{k}(q)u\qquad\text{in }L^{2}(M).

Then, by the definition of
L_{A}
through the closed form,

\sum_{i,j=1}^{m}X_{i}^{*}\bigl(a_{ij}X_{j}u\bigr)-(\lambda_{k}(q)-q)u=0\,,

in the weak sense on
M
.
By the Harnack inequality and the Harnack-to-continuity implication
of Chanillo–Wheeden
[
11
, Theorem 5.3]
, see also Lu’s
Hörmander-vector-field framework
[
35
]
and his observation

[
36
, Remark 4]
, weak solutions are continuous; hence
u\in C^{0}(M)
. See also
[
16
, Theorem 3.3]
for the extension of the latter results. Thus, for real-valued eigenfunctions, the nodal domains are well-defined as the
connected components of

M\setminus u^{-1}(\{0\}).

Finally, with the use of the restriction theorem
[
17
, Theorem 2.2]
, and reasoning as before for the case of
\mathcal{L}_{\rm sub}
, we complete the proof.
∎

3.2.
On compact manifolds with boundary and bounded domains

In this section we include examples of operators on non-smooth domains where Theorem
2.13
applies verbatim. We start with an example of a class of elliptic operators of second order where the set of bounded potentials for which the corresponding eigenfunctions satisfy Courant’s nodal theorem is residual.

3.2.1.
Second order elliptic operators with Hölder coefficients

We consider the low-regularity elliptic setting studied by
Alessandrini
[
5
]
. The point of this formulation is that the
coefficients are only Hölder continuous. In dimension
n\geq 3
, this is
below the regularity needed for the
proof of Courant’s theorem that is using unique continuation.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, be a bounded domain.
Let
0<\alpha<1
, and assume that

A=(a_{ij})_{1\leq i,j\leq n}\in C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega;\operatorname{Sym}_{n}(\mathbb{R}))

is real symmetric and uniformly elliptic. Thus there exist constants

0<\kappa\leq K
such that

\kappa|\xi|^{2}\leq A(x)\xi\cdot\xi\leq K|\xi|^{2},\qquad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\quad\text{for a.e. }x\in\Omega.

Let
b,\rho\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
, with
\rho\geq\rho_{0}>0
a.e. in
\Omega
.
Set

L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega):=L^{2}(\Omega,\rho dx).

Let

L_{\operatorname{ell}}:\operatorname{Dom}(L_{\operatorname{ell}})\subset L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega)\longrightarrow L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega)

be the self-adjoint operator associated with the form

\mathfrak{a}_{0}(u,v):=\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}b\,u\overline{v}\,dx,\qquad\operatorname{Dom}(\mathfrak{a}_{0})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Equivalently, in the weak sense,

L_{\operatorname{ell}}u=\rho^{-1}\bigl(-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)+bu\bigr).

Then the set

\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ell}}:=\Bigl\{V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}):\text{ real-valued eigenfunctions of }L_{\operatorname{ell}}+M_{V}\text{ satisfy Courant's theorem}\Bigr\}

is residual in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.

Proof.

For
V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
, the operator

L_{\operatorname{ell}}+M_{V}
is associated in
L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega)
with the
form

\mathfrak{a}_{V}(u,v):=\mathfrak{a}_{0}(u,v)+\int_{\Omega}Vu\overline{v}\,\rho dx,\qquad\operatorname{Dom}(\mathfrak{a}_{V})=H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Equivalently,

\mathfrak{a}_{V}(u,v)=\int_{\Omega}A\nabla u\cdot\overline{\nabla v}\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(b+\rho V)u\overline{v}\,dx.

The assumptions on
A
,
b
, and
\rho
imply that

\mathfrak{a}_{0}
is closed and semibounded from below. Indeed, after adding
a sufficiently large multiple of the
L^{2}_{\rho}
-norm, the form norm is
equivalent to the usual
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
-norm. Since
\Omega
is bounded,
the embedding

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\hookrightarrow L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega)

is compact. Hence
L_{\operatorname{ell}}
has compact resolvent. An application of Theorem
2.13
gives that the set

\mathcal{G}_{\operatorname{ell}}:=\Bigl\{V\in L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}):\text{ all eigenvalues of }L_{\operatorname{ell}}+M_{V}\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R})
.
Note that, although the abstract theorem was stated using a fixed smooth density, it can also be applied directly in the weighted space

L^{2}_{\rho}(\Omega)
. Next, we prove that

\mathcal{G}_{\operatorname{ell}}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\operatorname{ell}}.

Let
V\in\mathcal{G}_{\operatorname{ell}}
, and let
u
be a real-valued
eigenfunction of
L_{\operatorname{ell}}+M_{V}
. Assume that
u
is
associated with the
k
-th eigenvalue, i.e., we have

(L_{\operatorname{ell}}+M_{V})u=\lambda_{k}(V)u,

where the eigenvalues are counted with multiplicity.
Then
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, and

\mathfrak{a}_{V}(u,\varphi)=\lambda_{k}(V)\int_{\Omega}u\overline{\varphi}\,\rho dx,\qquad\varphi\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

Equivalently,

-\operatorname{div}(A\nabla u)+d_{\lambda,V}u=0\qquad\text{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(\Omega),

where
d_{\lambda,V}:=b+\rho V-\lambda_{k}(V)\rho\in L^{\infty}(\Omega).
Thus
u
solves a divergence-form uniformly elliptic equation with bounded
zeroth-order coefficient. By the De Giorgi–Nash–Moser interior Hölder
regularity theorem, see for instance Gilbarg–Trudinger

[
19
, Theorem 8.24]
, there exists

\beta\in(0,1)
such that

u\in C^{0,\beta}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\Omega),

and in particular, the nodal domains of
u
are well-defined as
open connected components of
\Omega\setminus u^{-1}(\{0\})
.
By the nodal-domain restriction argument of Pleijel, in the low-regularity
form used by Alessandrini, see
[
39
, Theorem 1]
and

[
5
, Proposition 2.1 and Remark 2.2]
, we see that
u
has at most

k
nodal domains, and this completes the proof.
∎

3.2.2.
Boundary realisations of the Laplacian and of the magnetic Schrödinger operators

Example 3.7

(Dirichlet Laplacian on bounded Lipschitz domains)
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}

Example 3.9

(Robin Laplacian on an arbitrary open set of finite measure)
.

Let
n\geq 2
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary open set with
finite Lebesgue measure. Let
\Gamma:=\partial\Omega
, and let
\sigma
denote the

(n-1)

\Gamma
. Following
[
7

\Gamma_{\sigma}:=\{z\in\Gamma:\exists r>0\text{ such that }\sigma(\Gamma\cap B(z,r))<\infty\},

and let

be the maximal
\sigma
-admissible set, that is,

is
\sigma
-admissible and

\operatorname{Cap}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\Gamma_{\sigma}\setminus S)=0,

\operatorname{Cap}_{\Omega}
stands for the relative capacity with respect to

Let
\beta\in L^{\infty}(S,\sigma)

[

, p. 17–18]
. Since

[

[

is

, and by

[

.

In the below examples on the Neumann and Dirichlet realisation of the magnetic Schrödinger operator in an open bounded regular domain

we have collected the necessary properties of the aforesaid operators from

for the application of Theorem

.

Example 3.11

(Neumann realisation of the magnetic Schrödinger operator)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded set, let

A=(A_{1},\dots,A_{n})
be a
C^{\infty}
vector field on
\overline{\Omega}
and
V\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})

and fix
h>0
. Let

P_{h,A,V,\Omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(hD_{x_{j}}-A_{j})^{2}+V(x)

be the magnetic Schrödinger operator,
and denote by

P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega},

its Neumann realization. On the boundary
\partial\Omega
we impose the magnetic-Neumann boundary condition

\vec{n}\cdot(h\nabla-iA)u=0\quad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

\vec{n}
denotes the normal vector to

\partial\Omega
. The Neumann realization
P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is the Friedrichs extension attached to the
quadratic form

Q^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}(u):=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(|\nabla_{h,A}u|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2}\Bigr)\,dx,\qquad u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{C}),

with
\nabla_{h,A}=h\nabla-iA
. Since
\Omega
is regular and bounded, the form domain of the operator
P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is

V^{N}(\Omega)=H^{1}(\Omega),

and the operator domain is

D(P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega})=\Bigl\{u\in H^{2}(\Omega):\vec{n}\cdot(h\nabla-iA)u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega\Bigr\},

The operator
P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is self-adjoint and we have

\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(|\nabla_{h,A}u|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2}\Bigr)\,dx\geq-C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall u\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{C}),

and so also
P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is bounded from below. Moreover, in
[
26
, p. 8]
it is shown that
P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
has compact resolvent. Therefore, by Theorem
2.13
the set

\mathcal{G}_{N}^{\mathrm{mag}}:=\Bigl\{V\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}):\text{all eigenvalues of }P^{N}_{h,A,V,\Omega}\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})
.

Example 3.12

(Dirichlet realisation of the magnetic Schrödinger operator)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a bounded set. For
P_{h,A,V,\Omega}
as in Example
3.11
we define its Dirichlet realisation as the Friedrichs extension attached to the
quadratic form

P^{D}_{h,A,V,\Omega}:=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(|\nabla_{h,A}u|^{2}+V(x)|u(x)|^{2}\Bigr)\,dx,\qquad u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega;\mathbb{C}).

Its form domain is
V^{D}(\Omega)=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega),
while the operator domain is
D(P^{D}_{h,A,V_{q},\Omega})=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)\cap H^{2}(\Omega)
.
The operator
P^{D}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is self-adjoint and we have

\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(|\nabla_{h,A}u|^{2}+V_{q}(x)|u(x)|^{2}\Bigr)\,dx\geq-C\|u\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\qquad\forall u\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\Omega).

So
P^{D}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
is bounded from below. Moreover, in
[
26
, p. 8]
it is shown that
P^{D}_{h,A,V,\Omega}
has compact resolvent. Therefore, by Theorem
2.13
the set

\mathcal{G}_{D}^{\mathrm{mag}}:=\Bigl\{V\in C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R}):\text{all eigenvalues of }P^{D}_{h,A,V,\Omega}\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C^{\infty}(\overline{\Omega},\mathbb{R})
.

3.3.
On non-compact manifolds

Example 3.13

(Harmonic and anharmonic oscillators)
.

For
k\in\mathbb{N}^{*}

we define

\mathcal{P}_{2k}:=\big\{p:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}\,\text{ polynomial satisfying }\liminf_{|x|\rightarrow\infty}\frac{p(x)}{|x|^{2k}}>0\big\}.

Let
q\in\mathcal{P}_{2\ell}
and
p\in\mathcal{P}_{2k}
be real-valued. Then there exists
q_{0},p_{0}>0
such that

q(\xi)+q_{0}\geq 1,\qquad p(x)+p_{0}\geq 1.

Let
T_{0}
be the positive self-adjoint realization on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
of

T_{0}=q(D)+p(x)+q_{0}+p_{0}.

By
[
13
, Corollary 5.4(b)]
, one has
T_{0}^{-1}\in S_{r}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}))
for some values of
r>0
(depending on
k,\ell
),
and hence
T_{0}^{-1}
is compact. Therefore
T_{0}
has compact resolvent. Set

T:=T_{0}-(p_{0}+q_{0})I.

Then
T
is self-adjoint, bounded from below, and has compact resolvent.
Additionally, by Theorem
2.13
, the set

\mathcal{G}_{\mathrm{anh}}:=\Bigl\{V\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}):\text{all eigenvalues of }T+V\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.

Example 3.14

(Magnetic Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
)
.

Let

P_{h,A,V,\mathbb{R}^{n}}=\sum_{j=1}^{n}(hD_{x_{j}}-A_{j})^{2}+V(x),

be as in Example
3.11
with
\Omega=\mathbb{R}^{n}
. It is clear that, if
V\geq-C
, the operator is semi-bounded, that the form domain
is explicitly given by

\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\Bigl\{u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\nabla_{h,A}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\ (V+C_{0})^{1/2}u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\Bigr\},

and that
P_{h,A,V,\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is essentially self-adjoint. We denote by
L_{\rm mag}
its self-adjoint realisation. If we also assume further that the embedding

\mathcal{V}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

is compact, then
L_{\rm mag}
has compact resolvent. We refer to
[
20
,
28
,
30
,
32
]
,
[
25
, Theorem 1.1, Corollary 1.2]
for general criteria.
Consequently,

\mathcal{G}_{\rm mag,pol}:=\Bigl\{q\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R}):\text{all eigenvalues of }L_{\rm mag}+M_{q}\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2},\mathbb{R})
. In particular, if
V(x)\to+\infty
, then Persson’s lemma gives
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(P_{h,A,V,\mathbb{R}^{n}})=\varnothing
, and so
P_{h,A,V,\mathbb{R}^{n}}
has compact resolvent. By Theorem
2.13
the set

\mathcal{G}_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}^{\mathrm{mag}}:=\Bigl\{q\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R}):\text{all eigenvalues of }P_{h,A,V,\mathbb{R}^{n}}+q\text{ are simple}\Bigr\}

is residual in
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n},\mathbb{R})
.

The sufficient condition
V(x)\to+\infty
in Example
3.14

is not necessary. Let us recall two typical examples.
First, let
L_{\rm S}
be the Friedrichs realisation of

L_{\rm S}:=-\Delta_{x,y}+x^{2}y^{2}.

The potential
x^{2}y^{2}
does not tend to
+\infty
as

|(x,y)|\to\infty
, since it vanishes on the coordinate axes. Nevertheless,
this operator has compact resolvent; this is one of the examples first
considered by Simon in
[
45
]
.

Second, let
L_{\rm mag}
be the Friedrichs realisation of

L_{\rm mag}:=\left(-i\partial x-y(x^{2}+y^{2})\right)^{2}+\left(-i\partial y+x(x^{2}+y^{2})\right)^{2}.

Writing
r^{2}=x^{2}+y^{2}
, and using the convention
D+A
, this corresponds to

A=(-yr^{2},xr^{2})
. Its magnetic potential is therefore

B=\partial_{x}A_{2}-\partial_{y}A_{1}=4r^{2},

so
B\to+\infty
as
r\to\infty
.

4.
Additional remarks on unique continuation and compact resolvent

The next remark records some settings in which the weak unique-continuation property holds, so that Remark
2.12
applies. It also emphasises that the stability of the unique continuation under compactly supported zeroth-order perturbations is a feature of the
second-order elliptic operators and should not be assumed for non-elliptic
sums of squares.

Remark 4.1
.

Let
\omega\subset X^{\circ}
be open, and assume that

\omega\cap X_{\alpha}\neq\varnothing\qquad\text{for every connected component }X_{\alpha}\text{ of }X^{\circ}.

When
X^{\circ}
is connected, this simply means that
\omega\neq\varnothing
. Under condition
(C_{\omega})
, the property
(\mathrm{UCP}_{\omega})
holds for the second-order elliptic examples considered in
Examples
3.7
,
3.8
, and
3.9
. This is a consequence of Aronszajn’s
unique-continuation theorem; see
[
8
, Remark 3, p. 248]
.
The boundary condition plays no role in this interior argument. The same conclusion holds for the harmonic/anharmonic operators of
Example
3.13
when
q(D)
is second-order
elliptic. It also holds for the magnetic Schrödinger operators in
Examples
3.14
,
3.11
, and
3.12
, after viewing the complex equation as a real

2\times 2
elliptic system; see the system version
[
8
, Remark 3, p. 248]
of the unique-continuation theorem. In all these cases, adding a potential in

L^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}
only changes the lower-order terms, and so it does not
affect the unique-continuation argument. By contrast, for the non-elliptic examples of Section
3.1
one should not
infer
(\mathrm{UCP}_{\omega})
unless an independent weak
unique-continuation theorem is available for the particular operator under
consideration; Bahouri
[
10
, (1.13), p. 140]
shows
that for

P_{0}=\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\bigl(\partial_{x_{2}}+x_{1}\partial_{x_{3}}\bigr)^{2}

there exist a neighbourhood
V
, functions
a,u\in C^{\infty}(V)
, with

u\not\equiv 0
, such that

(P_{0}+a)u=0\quad\text{in }V,

and
u
vanishes on a non-empty open subset of
V
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
L
be a self-adjoint operator on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
,
and assume that

L\geq\gamma

for some
\gamma\in\mathbb{R}
. Let

\mathcal{Q}(L):=D\bigl((L+s)^{1/2}\bigr),\qquad s>-\gamma,

be the form domain of
L
, endowed with its form norm. Then
L
has
compact resolvent if and only if the canonical embedding

\mathcal{Q}(L)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}

is compact.

Proof.

Follows from
[
41
, Theorem XIII.64, p. 245]
; see also
[
34
, Corollary 1.5]
.
∎

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth manifold, possibly non-compact, endowed with a positive
smooth density, and set

\mathcal{H}:=L^{2}(X).

Let
\mathcal{Q}
be an admissible perturbation space, and let

L:\operatorname{Dom}(L)\subset\mathcal{H}\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}

be self-adjoint and semibounded. The following assertions are equivalent:

(1)

L
has compact resolvent;

(2)

L_{q_{0}}=L+M_{q_{0}}
has compact resolvent for some
q_{0}\in\mathcal{Q}
;

(3)

L_{q}=L+M_{q}
has compact resolvent for every
q\in\mathcal{Q}
;

(4)

The canonical embedding

\mathcal{Q}(L)\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}

is compact, where
\mathcal{Q}(L)
is the form domain of
L
.

Assume moreover that

C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})\subset Q.
If one, hence all, of the above equivalent conditions
holds, then the set
\mathcal{G}_{Q}
is residual in
Q
.

Proof.

Assume first that
L
has compact resolvent. Let
q\in Q
. Since

q\in Q\subset L^{\infty}(X,\mathbb{R}),

the operator
M_{q}
is bounded and self-adjoint on

\mathcal{H}
. Hence, by Lemma
2.6
,
L_{q}
is also
self-adjoint, bounded from below, and has compact resolvent. Thus
1.

\Rightarrow

3.
, and
3.

\Rightarrow

2.
is immediate. Conversely, assume that
2.
holds. Since
L
is semibounded and
M_{q_{0}}
is
bounded,
L_{q_{0}}
is also bounded from below. Moreover, we can write

L=L_{q_{0}}-M_{q_{0}},

and since
-M_{q_{0}}
is bounded and self-adjoint on
\mathcal{H}
, another
application of Lemma
2.6
shows that
L
has compact resolvent. Therefore
2.

\Rightarrow

1.
The equivalence between
1.
and
4.
follows from Proposition
4.2
. Finally, under the extra assumption that
C_{c}^{\infty}(X^{\circ},\mathbb{R})\subset Q
, the conclusion follows from Corollary
2.4
and Theorem
2.13
.
∎

We point out that the compact-resolvent assumption of the abstract Theorem
2.13
is not essential
for the generic-simplicity mechanism itself; if one restricts attention to a
spectral region where the spectrum is discrete, consists of isolated
eigenvalues of finite multiplicity, and is stable under the admissible
perturbations, the same Baire-category argument applies. The following example on the spectrum of the Coulomb Hamiltonian, see e.g.
[
46
]
, illustrates this point.

Example 4.4
.

Let
\gamma>0
, and let
L_{\gamma}
be the Friedrichs extension on

L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
associated with

-\Delta-\frac{\gamma}{|x|}.

Let

Q:=\left\{q\in C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R}):q(x)\to 0\text{ as }|x|\to\infty\right\},

endowed with the topology induced by
C_{b}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})
.
Then
Q
is an admissible perturbation space and

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})\subset Q.

For
q\in Q
, set

L_{\gamma,q}:=L_{\gamma}+M_{q}.

Then the set

G_{\gamma}:=\left\{q\in Q:\text{ every eigenvalue of }L_{\gamma,q}\text{ in }(-\infty,0)\text{ is simple}\right\}

is residual in
Q
.

Indeed, for
q\in Q
, write

L_{\gamma,q}=-\Delta+a_{\gamma,q}(x),

where
a_{\gamma,q}(x):=-\frac{\gamma}{|x|}+q(x).

Since
a_{\gamma,q}(x)\to 0
as
|x|\to\infty
and
a_{\gamma,q}\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{3}),
by Persson’s

[
38
, Theorem 2.1, Proposition 3.1]
, the bottom of the essential spectrum is
0
, and hence for every
\alpha<0
the
spectrum of
L_{\gamma,q}

is finite and consists of isolated eigenvalues of finite multiplicity.
In particular, for every
N\geq 1
, the interval

(-\infty,-1/N]:=(-\infty,\alpha_{N}]

contains only finitely many eigenvalues of
L_{\gamma,q}
, counted with
multiplicity. Define

G_{\gamma,N}:=\left\{q\in Q:\text{ every eigenvalue of }L_{\gamma,q}\text{ in }(-\infty,\alpha_{N}]\text{ is simple}\right\}.

Then

G_{\gamma}=\bigcap_{N\geq 1}G_{\gamma,N},

and it remains to prove that each
G_{\gamma,N}
is open and dense in
Q
. We first prove openness. Fix
p\in G_{\gamma,N}
. Since the spectrum
below
0
is discrete, we may choose

\beta\in(\alpha_{N},0)\quad\text{such that}\quad\sigma(L_{\gamma,p})\cap(\alpha_{N},\beta]=\varnothing.

The spectral part of
L_{\gamma,p}
below
\beta
is finite, and the
eigenvalues in
(-\infty,\alpha_{N}]
are simple. By the same min–max
estimate as in Lemma
2.7
, applied to this finite spectral part below

\beta
, these eigenvalues remain separated for all
q
sufficiently
close to
p
in
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
. Moreover, by choosing
q

sufficiently close to
p
, no spectral point lying above
\beta
can
enter the interval
(-\infty,\alpha_{N}]
. Hence

q\in G_{\gamma,N}.

Since the inclusion

Q\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})

is continuous,
G_{\gamma,N}
is open in
Q
.

We now prove density. Fix
p\in Q
, let
U
be a neighborhood of

p
in
Q
, and choose

\beta\in(\alpha_{N},0)\quad\text{such that}\quad\beta\notin\sigma(L_{\gamma,p}).

The spectral part of
L_{\gamma,p}
in
(-\infty,\beta)
is finite. Since

C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3},\mathbb{R})\subset Q,

Corollary
2.4
gives Hypothesis (H) for every multiple eigenspace of

L_{\gamma,p}
. We can therefore repeat the argument of
Lemmas
2.10
and
2.11
on the finite spectral part below
\beta
. The
only change is that Lemma
2.8
is used only in its local form: if

\lambda<\beta
is an isolated eigenvalue of finite multiplicity, then
the eigenvalues near
\lambda
arising from
\lambda
admit real-analytic
branches under bounded potential perturbations; see
Kato
[
31
, Chapter VII, §3.2, p. 387]
.

Thus, by the same argument as in Lemma
2.10
, the multiplicity
of a multiple eigenvalue below
\beta
can be reduced by an arbitrarily
small perturbation in
Q
. Since there are only finitely many
eigenvalues below
\beta
, repeating this finitely many times gives
some

q\in U

such that every eigenvalue of
L_{\gamma,q}
below
\beta
is simple.
Since

(-\infty,\alpha_{N}]\subset(-\infty,\beta),

we have

q\in U\cap G_{\gamma,N},

and so
G_{\gamma,N}
is dense in
Q
.

Summarising,
G_{\gamma,N}
is open and dense in
Q
for every
N\geq 1
.
Since
Q
is a Fréchet space, it is a Baire space. Hence

G_{\gamma}=\bigcap_{N\geq 1}G_{\gamma,N}

is residual in
Q
.

The Coulomb term is only a model case: it may be replaced by any real-valued
V\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})+(L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3}))_{\varepsilon}
, i.e. by a potential for which there exist
V_{n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
with
V-V_{n}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\|V-V_{n}\|_{\infty}\to 0
.
For such
V
, the operator
V(-\Delta+1)^{-1}
is compact, see e.g.
[
41
, Example 6, p. 117]
, hence
\sigma_{\mathrm{ess}}(L_{V})=[0,\infty)
, where
L_{V}
is the self-adjoint realization of
-\Delta+V
; setting
L_{V,q}:=L_{V}+M_{q}
,
q\in Q
, the same conclusion holds.
