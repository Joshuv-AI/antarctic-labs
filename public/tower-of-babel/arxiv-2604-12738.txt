Introduction

The notion of a Frobenius manifold appears in relation to various topics in algebraic and analytic geometry, such and quantum cohomology, deformation of meromorphic connections,
unfolding of singularities and others. Of a particular interest for us here will be the formal Frobenius manifolds, that is a Frobenius
structure on the formal completion of a manifold at a point. In this situation there are two equivalent descriptions, either as an algebra over a certain cyclic operad
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
,
or alternatively as a genus
0
cohomological field theory. Let us briefly recall these correspondences, for a detailed discussion of it, as well as general theory of Frobenius
manifolds we refer to
[
Du
]
and
[
Ma
]
.

0.1
.

Let
X
be a super-manifold, equipped with a flat structure, i.e. a subsheaf of flat vector fields
T^{f}_{X}\hookrightarrow T_{X}
, as well as an even symmetric non-degenerate form
g\colon S^{2}(T_{X})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
.
A Frobenius structure on
X
is, roughly speaking, a commutative and associative binary
\mathcal{O}_{X}
-linear operation
\circ\colon S^{2}(T_{X})\to T_{X}
, which in flat coordinates is described
by an even potential function
\Phi
, namely for
x,y,z\in T^{f}_{X}
we have
g(x\circ y,z)=xyz(\Phi)
, where on the right side vector fields act as derivations on functions.

If
X
is a formal super-manifold, then it can be identified with the completion of a super vector space
V
at zero, equipped with a symmetric non-degenerate pairing

g\colon S^{2}(V)\to\mathbf{k}
. The potential
\Phi
is an element of
\widehat{S}^{\bullet}(V^{*})
, and its homogeneous components then can be thought of as multilinear operations on
V
.
Specifically, we have a collection of symmetric operations
m_{n}\colon S^{n}(V)\to V
for
n\geqslant 2
, such that the maps
Y_{n+1}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n+1})=g(m_{n}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}),v_{n+1})

are
S_{n+1}
-invariant, and the potential
\Phi=\sum(Y_{n}/n!)
, where
Y_{n}
is considered as an element of
S^{n}(V^{*})
.
This collection of operations
\{m_{n}\}
equips
(V,g)
with a structure of an algebra over the cyclic operad

\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
.

0.2
.

On the other hand this structure also admits a more algebro-geometric interpretation. Consider the moduli spaces
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
of stable curves of genus zero with
n
marked points.
The entire collection
\{\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n},n\geqslant 3\}
forms a cyclic operad with values in algebraic manifolds, where the operadic composition is given by attaching curves along
marked points. Specifically, let
C\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}
with marked points
\{x_{0},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and similarly
C_{i}\in\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k_{i}+1}
with marked points

\{x^{i}_{0},\ldots,x^{i}_{k_{i}}\}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
, then the map

\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n+1}\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k_{1}+1}\times\cdots\times\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,k_{n}+1}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,(1+\sum k_{i})}

at the level of points is given by first taking the disjoint union of curves
C,C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}
and then identifying points
x_{i}
and
x^{i}_{0}
for all
1\leqslant i\leqslant n
.

At the level of cohomology this induces a structure of a cyclic co-operad consisting of spaces
H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
. A cohomological field theory (of genus
0
) on a space

(V,g)
is a co-action of this co-operad. To see the relation of this notion and the previous
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
-algebras let us recall the structure of the cohomology groups
of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
, which is particularly easy to understand (unlike the case of higher genera) (
[
Ke
]
).

The combinatorial type of a stable curve
C
of genus
0
is given by a stable tree, where vertices correspond to the irreducible components of
C
, tails to the marked points
and two vertices are connected by an edge if and only if the corresponding irreducible components of
C
intersect. The cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
is purely even
and can be read entirely from the stratification of the moduli spaces by the combinatorial type of the stable curve. First, consider the case of
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
, which is
isomorphic to the projective line
\mathbb{P}^{1}
. The open strata
\mathcal{M}_{0,4}
is isomorphic to
\mathbb{P}^{1}-\{0,1,\infty\}
, and its combinatorial type is the corolla tree with a single vertex
and four tails. The three points forming the boundary divisor correspond to the trees with two vertices, one internal edge, and the tails labeled in one of three possible ways

(1,2\mid 3,4)
,
(1,3\mid 2,4)
and
(1,4\mid 2,3)
.

D_{(1,2\mid 3,4)}\quad\quad=
1
2
3
4

In the cohomology ring
H^{\bullet}(\mathbb{P}^{1})
(and in fact in the Chow ring
A(\mathbb{P}^{1})
) the classes of divisors corresponding to these three points coincide, i.e.

(0.2.1)

D_{(1,2\mid 3,4)}=D_{(1,3\mid 2,4)}=D_{(1,4\mid 2,3)}.

Now, interpreting tails
(1,2,3)
as inputs, tail
4
as the output and assigning to each
vertex the binary operation
m_{2}
it is clear that these relations express the associativity of
m_{2}
.

Now the relations (
0.2.1
) are in a sense fundamental, and imply all other relations in the cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
for all
n
. Consider
the stable forgetful maps
p_{ijkl}\colon\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}\to\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,4}
, that forget all marked points except four points marked by
i,j,k,l
and then stabilize the resulting
curve. The preimage of the strata
D_{(1,2\mid 3,4)}
is the closure of the union of strata corresponding to trees with two vertices, one internal edge and the tails attached to
the vertices in all possible ways, such that tails
i,j
are on one side and tails
k,l
are on the other. Keel has shown that the cohomology
H^{\bullet}(\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n})
as an algebra
is generated by classes of strata corresponding to all two-vertex trees modulo the preimage of relations (
0.2.1
) along
p_{ijkl}
for all quadruples
\{i,j,k,l\}
.
Interpreting these relations from the operadic point of view by assigning to a vertex with
n+1
attached flags the operation
m_{n}
we recover the higher associativity relations
in the operad
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
, thus establishing equivalence of the structures of a cyclic
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
-algebra and a cohomological field theory.

0.3
.

In his book on Frobenius manifolds (
[
Ma
]
) Yuri Manin asks what would be the analog of this picture if one starts with the
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-operad instead of the
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
.
In this paper we outline the first steps towards answering this question. First, in section
1
we give a definition of an
L
-manifold, which is the global geometric notion extending
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebras,
similar to how Frobenius manifold is the global version of the
\mathrm{Com}_{\infty}
-algebra. Here we have to work with the
curved
cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
algebras in order to have the
same pattern as in the Frobenius setting. Then in section
2
we give several basic examples of
L
-manifolds, including explicit description in the cases of low odd dimension.
Then in sections
3
and
4
we define spaces
H^{\bullet}_{S}
which we use to define a version of cohomological field theory equivalent to the notion of
a curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra.

Of a particular interest here is that combinatorially all the constructions go parallel to the case of Frobenius manifolds and moduli spaces of stable curves. This leads us to
formulate two questions.

Conjecture 0.4
.

There exist a family of spaces (or rather Artin stacks)
\mathcal{L}_{0,n}
forming a cyclic operad, and equipped with the “forgetful” maps

p\colon\mathcal{L}_{0,n}\to\mathcal{L}_{0,m}
, for
m\leqslant n
, as well as the “section” maps
x_{i}\colon\mathcal{L}_{0,n}\to\mathcal{L}_{0,n+1}
for
1\leqslant i\leqslant n+1
, satisfying the obvious relations (as for
\overline{\mathcal{M}}_{0,n}
),
such that the spaces
H^{\bullet}_{\underline{\mathbf{n}}}\simeq H^{\bullet}(\mathcal{L}_{0,n})
.

Question 0.5
.

If such spaces
\mathcal{L}_{0,n}
exist, are they moduli spaces of something? And if the answer is positive, is there a natural higher genus version of these space
\mathcal{L}_{g,n}
, and what is
the corresponding higher genus notion of
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra?

Main definitions

A super-manifold
X
is a pair
(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X})
, where
X_{0}=(X_{0},\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}})
is a classical smooth manifold in one of the usual categories, like
C^{\infty}
, complex-analytic, algebraic manifolds, etc.
And
\mathcal{O}_{X}=(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bar{0}}\oplus(\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bar{1}}
is a
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded sheaf of algebras over
X_{0}
, satisfying the following conditions.

•

The quotient of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
by the ideal generated by odd elements
\mathcal{O}_{X}/((\mathcal{O}_{X})_{\bar{1}})
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}
.

•

Locally on
X_{0}
(in appropriate topology)
\mathcal{O}_{X}\simeq\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Lambda^{\bullet}(V)
for some free
\mathcal{O}_{X_{0}}
-module
V
, such that the generating elements from
V
are placed in odd degree.

We denote
T_{X}
the tangent bundle on
X
, in other words the
\mathbb{Z}/2
-graded sheaf of even and odd self-derivations of
\mathcal{O}_{X}
. And consider an even (super-)antisymmetric bilinear
form
\omega\colon\Lambda^{2}(T_{X})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
, which induces an isomorphism
\omega^{\prime}\colon T_{X}\to T_{X}^{*}
. If we denote by
\Pi T_{X}
the parity shifted tangent bundle, then giving
form
\omega
is equivalent to giving an even symmetric bilinear form
g\colon S^{2}(\Pi T_{X})\to\mathcal{O}_{X}
, inducing an isomorphism
g^{\prime}\colon\Pi T_{X}\to\Pi T_{X}^{*}
.

A flat affine structure on a super-manifold
X
is a subsheaf
T_{X}^{f}\hookrightarrow T_{X}
, such that it locally spans the tangent bundle, i.e.
T_{X}\simeq T_{X}^{f}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathcal{O}_{X}
, and for any two sections

v,w\in T_{X}^{f}
, called flat vector fields, the Lie bracket
[v,w]=0
. Notice that in the
C^{\infty}
and complex-analytic settings we can find local coordinates
x_{i}
on
X
, such that
the partial derivatives
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial x_{i}
form a local basis of
T_{X}^{f}
. If we choose a covering of
X
by charts equipped with such local coordinates, the transition maps between
charts are given by affine linear transformations.

If the bilinear form
\omega
is compatible with the flat affine structure on
X
, then it is necessarily closed, and therefore is a symplectic form on
X
.

Definition 1.1
.

a)

A pre-
L
-manifold is a symplectic super-manifold
(X,\omega)
equipped with the affine flat structure
T_{X}^{f}
compatible with the symplectic form, and an odd vector field
Q\in\Gamma(X,\Pi T_{X})
.

b)

A local potential is a local odd function
\Phi
on
X
, such that the odd 1-form
\sigma=\omega^{\prime}(Q)=d\Phi
. In other words
Q
is an odd Hamiltonian vector field with Hamiltonian
\Phi
:

\omega(X,Q)=X\Phi.

A pre-
L
-manifold is said to be potential if it admits a global potential
\Phi
.

c)

A potential pre-
L
-manifold is called an
L
-manifold if it satisfies the Lie condition (as will be explained later)

(1.1.1)

d(\omega(Q,Q))=0.

Let
(V,\omega)
be a finite dimensional symplectic super vector space, then a
formal

L
-manifold structure on
(V,\omega)
is an
L
-manifold, with the underlying
space
X
being the completion of
V
at zero, so that the algebra of functions

\mathcal{O}_{X}=\widehat{S^{\bullet}}(V^{*})\simeq\mathbf{k}[[x_{1},\ldots,x_{m}]]\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Lambda[\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}],

and the symplectic structure is induced by
\omega
after identifying the tangent sheaf with
\mathcal{O}_{X}\mathop{\otimes}\limits V
.

Proposition 1.2
.

If
(X,\omega,Q)
is an
L
-manifold, then the super-commutator
[Q,Q]=0
.

Proof:
Denote by
\{-,-\}
the Poisson bracket on
\mathcal{O}_{X}
induced by the symplectic form
\omega
. Then for any function
f\in\mathcal{O}_{X}
we have

Qf=\{\Phi,f\}
and therefore

[Q,Q]f=2Q^{2}f=2\{\Phi,\{\Phi,f\}\}=\{\{\Phi,\Phi\},f\}.

On the other hand
\{\Phi,\Phi\}=Q\Phi=\omega(Q,Q)
, which according to the Lie condition (
1.1.1
) is a constant function. Hence the Poisson bracket
on the right hand side vanishes and we conclude that
[Q,Q]=0
.

\square

1.3
Differential equation for
\Phi
..

Consider a system of local flat coordinates
\{x_{i}\}
and let
\{\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial x_{i}\}
be the corresponding basis of flat vector fields, determined by condition that

df=\sum dx_{i}(\partial_{i}f).

To simplify notation we will also write for iterated derivatives
f_{i_{1}\ldots i_{k}}:=\partial_{i_{1}}\ldots\partial_{i_{k}}f
.
We denote
\omega_{ij}=\omega(\partial_{i},\partial_{j})
and let
\omega^{ij}
be the inverse matrix. These matrices satisfy the following symmetry relations

\omega_{ij}=-(-1)^{\bar{x}_{i}\bar{x}_{j}}\omega_{ji},\quad\quad\omega^{ij}=(-1)^{(\bar{x}_{i}+1)(\bar{x}_{j}+1)}\omega^{ji}.

Since by assumption form
\omega
is compatible with the flat structure, the coefficients of these matrices are elements of the ground ring.
If the latter is purely even (for instance if we are working over the field
\mathbf{k}
) then
\omega_{ij}
is non-zero only if
\bar{x}_{i}=\bar{x}_{j}
,
in which case the symmetry condition for
\omega^{ij}
coincides with that for
\omega_{ij}
. To simplify the signs we will assume that we are
working over a purely even base ring.

Using this notation the potentiality condition
b)
reads

Q=\sum\omega^{ji}\Phi_{i}\partial_{j}.

And the condition (
1.1.1
) is equivalent to the system of non-linear differential equations for the potential
\Phi
:

(1.3.1)

\sum_{i,j}\omega^{ji}\Phi_{ki}\Phi_{j}=0.

1.4
Curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra..

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a finite dimensional super vector space, equipped with an even symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form
g\colon S^{2}(V)\to\mathbf{k}
. Consider a collection of
antisymmetric multilinear operations
\ell_{n}\colon\Lambda^{n}(V)\to\Pi^{n}V
, for
n\geqslant 0
, where
\Pi
stands for the parity change operator. In other words
operation
\ell_{n}
has arity
n
and parity
(n\ \mathrm{mod}\ 2)
. In particular
\ell_{0}
is an element of
V_{\bar{0}}
, which will be referred to as the
curvature
,
and
\ell_{1}
is an odd endomorphism of
V
, which will be called a
differential
. Let us also denote by
\underline{\mathbf{n}}
the set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
.

Definition 1.5
.

A curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
V
, is a collection
\{\ell_{n}\}
, satisfying the following conditions.

a)

Cyclic invariance: operations
Y_{n+1}\colon V^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits(n+1)}\to\mathbf{k}
defined as
Y_{n+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n+1})=g(\ell_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}),x_{n+1})
are
\mathbb{Z}/(n+1)
-invariant (in the super sense), i.e.

Y_{n+1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x_{n+1})=(-1)^{n+\bar{x}_{1}(\bar{x}_{2}+\cdots+\bar{x}_{n+1})}Y_{n+1}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1},x_{1}).

b)

Higher Jacobi relations: for all
n\geqslant 0
we have

(1.5.1)

\sum_{\underline{\mathbf{n}}=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}}(-1)^{|S_{1}||S_{2}|}\varepsilon(S_{1},S_{2})\ell_{|S_{2}|+1}(\ell_{|S_{1}|}(x_{i}\mid i\in S_{1}),x_{j}\mid j\in S_{2})=0.

Here the sum is taken over all partitions of
\underline{\mathbf{n}}
into two subsets (possibly empty), and
\varepsilon(S_{1},S_{2})
is the sign of the permutation of inputs
\{x_{i}\}
,
by the shuffle of
\underline{\mathbf{n}}
obtained by first arranging all the elements of
S_{1}
in the increasing order followed by all the
elements of
S_{2}
, also in the increasing order. Notice, that the sign here also involves the degrees of the inputs
x_{i}
, for instance, sign
of the transposition of the two adjacent elements
(i,i+1)
is
(-1)^{1+\bar{x}_{i}\bar{x}_{i+1}}
.

For convenience we will write
c:=\ell_{0}
for the curvature element,
d:=\ell_{1}
for the differential and
[x,y]=\ell_{2}(x,y)
for the binary operation.
The first few identities (
1.5.1
) for small
n
are as follows:

a)

for
n=0
we have
d(c)=0
,

b)

for
n=1
we have
d^{2}(x)+[c,x]=0
,

c)

for
n=2
we have
d[x,y]-[dx,y]-(-1)^{\bar{x}}[x,dy]+\ell_{3}(c,x,y)=0
,

d)

for
n=3
we have

d\ell_{3}(x,y,z)+[[x,y],z]-(-1)^{\bar{y}\bar{z}}[[x,z],y]+(-1)^{\bar{x}(\bar{y}+\bar{z})}[[y,z],x]+\\
\ell_{3}(dx,y,z)+(-1)^{\bar{x}}\ell_{3}(x,dy,z)+(-1)^{\bar{x}+\bar{y}}\ell_{3}(x,y,dz)+\ell_{4}(c,x,y,z)=0.

In particular, if we forget the pairing
g
on
V
and put curvature
c=0
, we recover the standard notion of a homotopy Lie algebra.

Proposition 1.6
.

Let
(V,\omega)
be a finite dimensional symplectic super vector space. Then there is a bijection between the set of formal
L
-manifold structures on
(V,\omega)

and the set of curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-structures on
(\Pi V,g=\omega^{\Pi})
. It sends a collection of operations
\{\ell_{n}\}
to the potential

\Phi=\sum_{n\geqslant 1}{Y_{n}\over n!},

where
Y_{n}
is considered as an element of
\Lambda^{n}(\Pi V^{*})\simeq S^{n}(V^{*})
.

Proof:
Let
\{x_{i}\}
be a system of flat coordinates, and as before denote by
\{\partial_{i}\}
the corresponding basis of flat vector fields. Furthermore, we identify the space
\Pi V

with the fiber over zero of the parity shifted tangent bundle, and denote by
e_{i}=\partial_{i}^{\Pi}|_{0}
the corresponding basis vectors of
\Pi V
.

For any super vector space
W
we will fix an explicit isomorphism
\Lambda^{n}(W)\simeq S^{n}(\Pi W)
by sending

w_{1}\wedge w_{2}\wedge\ldots\wedge w_{n}\mapsto(-1)^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}(i-1)\bar{w}_{i}}w_{1}^{\Pi}w_{2}^{\Pi}\ldots w_{n}^{\Pi}.

Let us denote the sign on the right hand side by
\lambda(w_{1},\ldots,w_{n})
.

Looking at the linear terms of
\Phi
we find that
(c,e_{i})=\Phi_{i}(0)
, and on the other hand if we write
c=\sum C^{j}e_{j}
we have

(C^{j}e_{j},e_{i})=(-1)^{\bar{x}_{i}+1}\omega_{ji}.

Therefore
C^{j}=\sum\Phi_{i}(0)\omega^{ij}
, since
\Phi_{i}(0)
can only be non-zero if
\bar{x}_{i}=1
.
Similarly, if we write
D_{i}^{k}
for the matrix coefficients of the differential
d=\ell_{1}
, i.e.

d(e_{i})=\sum_{k}D_{i}^{k}e_{k},

then by looking at the quadratic terms of
\Phi
we find on one hand

(d(e_{i}),e_{j})=(-1)^{\bar{x}_{j}+1}\Phi_{ij}(0),

and on the other hand

(d(e_{i}),e_{j})=\sum_{k}(-1)^{\bar{x}_{j}+1}D_{i}^{k}\omega_{kj}.

Therefore, multiplying on the right side by
\omega^{jk}
we see that
D_{i}^{k}=\Phi_{ij}(0)\omega^{jk}
. And so the first of relations (
1.5.1
)
d(c)=0
is
equivalent to vanishing of the system (
1.3.1
) at the origin.

We will now show that the rest of the higher Jacobi conditions are equivalent to vanishing of the iterated derivatives of the differential equations (
1.3.1
) at the origin.
Therefore, since
\Phi
is a formal series, this will imply that the equations hold.

It may be helpful to represent the formulas here graphically, using planar trees.

1.7
.

Recall that a ribbon graph is a collection
(V,F,\delta,\sigma,j)
, where
V
is the set of vertices,
F
is the set of flags (or half-edges),
\delta\colon F\to V
is the incidence
map which attaches a flag to a specific vertex,
\sigma
is a collection of cyclic orders
\sigma_{v}
on the set
F_{v}:=\delta^{-1}(v)
for each vertex
v\in V
, and
j\colon F\to F
is
an involution on the set of flags which prescribes gluing of pairs of flags into edges. A planar tree is a ribbon graph without cycles.

Let
T
be a connected planar tree, a tail of
T
is a flag fixed by the involution
j
. An orientation of
T
is a map
or\colon F\to\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}
, such that all two-element
orbits of
j
map surjectively onto
\{\mathrm{in},\mathrm{out}\}
. If a tree has at least one tail, we will choose one of them and orient it outward, which in turn
induces an orientation on the entire tree, by requiring that every set
F_{v}
has exactly one outward flag. Denote by
\mathrm{Tail}(T)
the set of tails of
T
and by
E(T)
the set of
internal edges of
T
.

We will decorate the tree by assigning to every flag
f\in F
an element
m(f)
in the basis of flat vector fields. More precisely, if it is a tail we will decorate it
by
dx_{m(f)}
, and if it is an internal edge
(i\to j)
we decorate it by
\omega^{ij}
. By assigning to every vertex of
T
a function we obtain an element of

(T^{*}_{V})^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathrm{Tail}(T)}
, by reading inputs, say in a counterclockwise order, starting from the outward tail.

For example the following tree

\Phi_{ki}
\Phi_{j}
\omega^{ji}
dx_{k}

represents the form:
\sum dx_{k}(\omega^{ji}\Phi_{ki}\Phi_{j})
.

Differentiating this expression with respect to
x_{m}
corresponds to adding an additional inward flag to either one of the vertices and then taking sum of the
resulting trees. Thus in this example we obtain

(1.7.1)

\Phi_{mki}
\Phi_{j}
\omega^{ji}
dx_{k}
dx_{m}
+(-1)^{\bar{x}_{m}(1+\bar{x}_{k})}
\Phi_{ki}
\Phi_{jm}
\omega^{ji}
dx_{k}
dx_{m}

Arguing as before, if we write
\ell_{n}(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{n}})=B_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}^{k}e_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle(\ell_{n}(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{n}}),e_{j})

\displaystyle=\lambda(e_{i_{1}},\ldots,e_{i_{n}},e_{j})\Phi_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n},j}(0)

\displaystyle=(-1)^{\bar{e}_{j}}B_{i_{1}\ldots i_{n}}^{k}\omega_{kj}.

Let us evaluate form in (
1.7.1
) at zero. Notice that for non-zero terms we can cyclically shift indices of derivatives of
\Phi
without changing sign.
So for the first tree we have
\Phi_{imk}(0)=(-1)^{\bar{x}_{m}+\bar{x}_{k}}B_{im}^{l}\omega_{lk}
and since
\bar{x}_{m}=\bar{x}_{k}
, multiplying by
\omega^{kl}e_{l}

we obtain the commutator
[c,e_{m}]
. Similarly for the second tree we obtain
d^{2}(e_{m})
, which together gives the second of the Jacobi identities (
1.5.1
).

Iterating this process of adding more new tails labeled by elements of a set
S
, it is clear that the resulting trees will be indexed by partitions of
S
into two disjoint
subsets
S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
and by direct examination we see that we end up with all the relations in (
1.5.1
).

\square

Remark 1.8
.

Alternatively, the bijection between formal
L
-manifolds and curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebras can be described as follows. Let
V
be a formal
L
-manifold with
a potential
\Phi
, then the odd 1-form
d\Phi
is a map
T_{V}\to\Pi\mathcal{O}_{V}
, which is completely determined by its value on the flat vector fields, that is a map

V\to\Pi\mathcal{O}_{V}\simeq\Pi S^{\bullet}(V^{*})
. Using isomorphism between
V
and
V^{*}
induced by the symplectic form we obtain

V^{*}\to\Pi S^{\bullet}(V^{*}),

which in turn induces an odd derivation of the symmetric algebra. The Lie condition (
1.1.1
) ensures that this map is square-zero, and gives
an alternative definition of the
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra structure on
\Pi V
as a differential in the Chevalley-Eilenberg complex.

Definition 1.9
.

We say that an
L
-manifold
(X,\omega,Q)
is isotropic if
\omega(Q,Q)=0
.

1.10
Euler vector field..

Let
E
be an even vector field on an
L
-manifold
X
, which is conformal, that is
\mathrm{Lie}_{E}(\omega)=D\omega
for some constant
D
, or equivalently

E(\omega(V,W))-\omega([E,V],W)-\omega(V,[E,W])=D\omega(V,W).

We say that a conformal vector field
E
is Euler if in addition it preserves the flat structure and satisfies condition

[Q,E]=d_{Q}Q

for some constant
d_{Q}
.

Since by definition of an
L
-manifold the pairing
\omega(Q,Q)
is a constant function we have

0=E(\omega(Q,Q))=D\omega(Q,Q)+\omega([E,Q],Q)+\omega(Q,[E,Q])=(D-2d_{Q})\omega(Q,Q).

In other words an
L
-manifold
X
with an Euler vector field
E
has to be isotropic unless
D=2d_{Q}
.

In terms of the potential
\Phi
the Euler vector field conditions imply that up to a constant
\Phi
is an eigenvector of
E
:

(1.10.1)

E\Phi=(D-d_{Q})\Phi+\mathrm{const}.

Indeed, differentiating along a vector field
X
, we have
X(E\Phi)=E(X\Phi)-[E,X]\Phi
. On the other hand, by definition of potential
X\Phi=\omega(X,Q)
,
and hence

X(E\Phi)=E(\omega(X,Q))-\omega([E,X],Q)=\\
D\omega(X,Q)+\omega([E,X],Q)+\omega(X,[E,Q])-\omega([E,X],Q)=(D-d_{Q})\omega(X,Q),

which implies (
1.10.1
).

2
Examples

We begin with two extreme examples. First, if
X
is a purely even manifold, then the ring of functions
\mathcal{O}_{X}
is purely even and the potential

\Phi
, being an odd function, has to be zero. In other words a purely even manifold doesn’t admit a non-trivial
L
-manifold structure.

On the other hand, if
X
is a purely odd manifold, then it is formal, and its ring of function is a finite dimensional exterior algebra in odd variables. Hence,
by proposition
1.6
, it is given by a purely even curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with only even operations
\ell_{n}
,
n\geqslant 0
.

2.1
Flat vector field
Q
..

If
X
is a pre-
L
-manifold, such that odd vector field
Q
is a flat, then both the potentiality condition and

\omega(Q,Q)=\mathrm{const}
are automatically satisfied. Specifically, in this case the potential is given by a linear function in odd flat coordinates

\Phi=\sum a_{i}\xi_{i}
. In terms of the
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra structure, the shifted tangent bundle
\Pi T_{X}
is equipped with a locally constant even section
c
, i.e. the curvature,
and the rest of the
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
operations are trivial.

2.2
Lie super-algebra..

For any Lie super-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
of dimension
(m,n)
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric invariant bilinear form we can construct an
L
-manifold with
the underlying superspace
X=\mathbb{A}^{n,m}\simeq\Pi\mathfrak{g}
, by proceeding as in proposition
1.6
. Since all the operations
\ell_{n}
with
n\geqslant 3

vanish, the
L
-manifold structure in the formal neighborhood of zero extends to the entire affine space. The potential in the coordinates dual to
\mathfrak{g}

is given by an odd cubic (homogeneous) polynomial. Of course at other points of
X
the fibers of
\Pi T_{X}
in general may have nonzero curvature and differential,
but no higher operations
\ell_{n}
with
n\geqslant 3
.

Let us work out in more detail the case of super-algebra
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)=\mathfrak{gl}(V)
. Let
\{v_{i}\}
be a basis of
V
, and write
e_{ij}=v_{i}\mathop{\otimes}\limits v_{j}^{*}
for
the corresponding basis of
\mathfrak{gl}(m,n)
. And consider the bilinear form

(e_{ij},e_{ji})=(-1)^{\bar{v}_{i}},

which satisfies all of the above assumptions. Denote by
x_{ji}
the flat coordinate on
X
dual to basis element
e_{ij}
, in other words we have
\partial/\partial x_{ji}=\partial_{ij}=e_{ij}^{\Pi}
.
Then the potential is given by

\Phi=\sum_{(i,j,k)}(-1)^{\bar{v}_{i}+\bar{v}_{j}+\bar{v}_{k}}x_{ik}x_{kj}x_{ji}.

Here the sum is taken over all triples
(i,j,k)
up to cyclic shift. Notice that since the degree
\bar{x}_{ii}=1
, the terms with
i=j=k
do not contribute to the sum.

Proposition 2.3
.

The
L
-manifold
X
associated to a Lie super-algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is isotropic.

Proof:
Let
\{e_{i}\}
be a basis of
\mathfrak{g}
and denote by
\{x_{i}\}
the corresponding system of flat coordinates on
X
, such that
\partial_{i}=\partial/\partial x_{i}=e_{i}^{\Pi}
. Consider an even vector field

E=\sum_{i}x_{i}\partial_{i}.

Clearly, we have
Ex_{i}=x_{i}
and
[\partial_{i},E]=\partial_{i}
for all
i
, and therefore
E
is an Euler vector field with
D=2
and
d_{Q}=-1
. Since
D\neq 2d_{Q}
this implies,
as was shown in paragraph
1.10
, that
\omega(Q,Q)=0
.

\square

Remark 2.4
.

Similar argument can be used to show that if
\mathfrak{g}
is a cyclic curved
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
algebra, such that all operations
\ell_{n}
vanish except for a single
n>0
, then

E
as above is again an Euler vector field on the associated
L
-manifold
X
, with
D=2
and
d_{Q}=(1-n)
. Therefore it is once again isotropic.

It would be interesting to characterize isotropic (formal)
L
-manifolds in terms of the corresponding
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra structures.

2.5
L
-manifolds of dimension
(n,1)
..

Consider a super-manifold
X
of dimension
(n,1)
, and denote by
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
a system of flat even coordinates on
X
and by
\xi
a flat odd coordinate. By rescaling
\xi

if necessary we may assume that
\omega(\partial_{\xi},\partial_{\xi})=1
. Any odd function
\Phi
can be written as
\Phi=\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\xi
, therefore all of the derivatives

\Phi_{x_{i}}
have factor
\xi
. The Lie condition (
1.1.1
) can be written as

\varphi(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})^{2}+\sum_{i,j}\omega^{ji}\Phi_{x_{i}}\Phi_{x_{j}}=\mathrm{const}.

The sum in this expression contains factor
\xi^{2}
and therefore vanishes, which implies that
\varphi
is a constant function. Hence in this case the vector field
Q
is flat and we
are in the situation discussed in paragraph
2.1
.

2.6
L
-manifolds of dimension
(n,2)
..

Consider a super-manifold
X
of dimension
(n,2)
and, as before, let
(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
be a system of flat even coordinates on
X
, and
(\xi_{1},\xi_{2})
a pair of flat odd coordinates.
By applying a linear transformation with constant coefficients to the odd coordinates we can assume that the restriction of the form
\omega
to flat odd vector fields is given by matrix

\omega\mid_{\left<\partial_{\xi_{1}},\partial_{\xi_{2}}\right>}\ =\ \begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

We will also denote by
\overline{\omega}
the restriction of form
\omega
to the even manifold
X_{0}
, and let
\{-,-\}
be the Poisson bracket corresponding to
\overline{\omega}
.

The potential
\Phi
can be written as

\Phi=\varphi_{1}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\xi_{1}+\varphi_{2}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\xi_{2},

for some even functions
\varphi_{1}
and
\varphi_{2}
. By separating derivatives with respect to even and odd variables, the Lie condition
\omega(Q,Q)=C
for some constant
C

is equivalent to the following two conditions.

\varphi_{1}\varphi_{2}=C,

\{\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}\}=0.

If
C=0
then one of the factors
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
is zero (at least locally if we are working in the
C^{\infty}
setting) and the second condition is obviously satisfied.
Therefore the potential has the form
\Phi=\varphi\xi_{1}
, for an arbitrary even function
\varphi
.

If
C\neq 0
then
\varphi_{2}=C/\varphi_{1}
and again, since
\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}
are functionally dependent, the second condition is satisfied. Therefore we have

\Phi=\varphi\xi_{1}+{C\xi_{2}\over\varphi}

for an arbitrary even function
\varphi
.

3
Cohomological field theory

In this section we will give a description of the curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebras in the flavor of cohomological field theory. We define graded algebras
H^{\bullet}_{S}
for any
finite set
S
combinatorially, and show that a coaction of this collection
\{H^{\bullet}_{S}\}
on a super vector space
V
is equivalent to giving a structure of a curved cyclic

\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra.

It would be interesting to describe the spaces
H^{\bullet}_{S}
as the cohomology of some moduli spaces (or more likely Artin stacks), similar to the relation between the formal
Frobenius manifolds and cohomological field theories with spaces of (co)operations being the cohomology of the moduli of stable curves
\mathcal{M}_{0,S}
.

3.1
.

Let
S
be a finite set (possibly empty) and
\sigma
be a partition of
S
into two disjoint subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}
. Such partitions are in bijection with trees with two
vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
, one internal edge and tails labeled by elements of
S
. Specifically, given such a tree we put
S_{i}
to be the set of tails attached to vertex
v_{i}
, for
i=1,2
.

For any tree
\tau
with tails labeled by
S
we consider a symbol
m(\tau)
of degree
|E(\tau)|
.
Fix a vertex
v\in\tau
with at least one adjacent flag, and a flag
f\in F_{v}
, and let
F_{v}^{\circ}=F_{v}-\{f\}
be the set of all the remaining adjacent flags.
Then for every partition of
F_{v}^{\circ}=F_{1}\sqcup F_{2}
(either
F_{1}
or
F_{2}
can be empty)
we can construct a new tree
\tau^{\prime}=\tau^{\prime}(\tau,v,f,F_{1},F_{2})

obtained from
\tau
by adding a new vertex
v^{\prime}
, connecting it to
v
by an edge
e
and attaching all flags from
F_{1}
to
v^{\prime}
and all flags from
F_{2}
to
v
.

For any such triple
(\tau,v\in\tau,f\in F_{v})
we can consider a linear combination

(3.1.1)

R(\tau,v,f):=\sum_{F_{v}^{\circ}=F_{1}\sqcup F_{2}}(-1)^{|F_{1}||F_{2}|}\varepsilon(F_{1},F_{2})m(\tau^{\prime}(\tau,v,f,F_{1},F_{2})).

Definition 3.2
.

The space
H^{n}_{S}
is the quotient of the linear span of symbols
m(\tau)
for all trees
\tau
with
n
internal edges and tails labeled by
S
, modulo subspace spanned by elements
R(\tau^{\prime},v,f)

for all trees
\tau^{\prime}
with
(n-1)
internal edges, vertices
v\in\tau^{\prime}
and flags
f\in F_{v}
.

For any partition
\sigma
of the set of tails
S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
we can define a boundary map
\varphi_{\sigma}\colon H^{\bullet}_{S}\to H^{\bullet}_{S_{1}^{+}}\mathop{\otimes}\limits H^{\bullet}_{S_{2}^{+}}
, where

S_{i}^{+}
denotes the disjoint union of
S_{i}
and one additional element. Explicitly, if a tree
\tau
doesn’t contain an edge which induces partition of tails into subsets
S_{1}
and
S_{2}

then
\varphi_{\sigma}(m(\tau))=0
. Otherwise, we cut
\tau
along this edge into two trees
\tau^{\prime}
and
\tau^{\prime\prime}
and put
\varphi_{\sigma}(m(\tau))=m(\tau^{\prime})\mathop{\otimes}\limits m(\tau^{\prime\prime})
.

3.3
.

For a vector space
V
we can consider a cohomological field theory on
V
consisting of a collection of even maps

I_{n}\colon(\Pi V)^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits n}\to\Pi H^{\bullet}_{n},

which satisfy the following two conditions.

a)

I_{n}
is
S_{n}
invariant, where
S_{n}
acts on the left by permuting the factors and on the right by relabeling tails of trees.

b)

Are compatible with the boundary maps in the sense that for any partition
\sigma
of
S
into
S_{1}
,
S_{2}

\varphi_{\sigma}(I_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}))=\varepsilon(\sigma)(I_{S_{1}^{+}}\mathop{\otimes}\limits I_{S_{2}^{+}})\left(\bigotimes_{S_{1}}x_{i}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Delta\mathop{\otimes}\limits\bigotimes_{S_{2}}x_{j}\right),

where
\Delta
denotes the Casimir element in
V\mathop{\otimes}\limits V
.

By directly comparing the definitions of spaces
H^{\bullet}_{S}
and curved cyclic
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
-algebra (
1.5
) we arrive at the following statement.

Proposition 3.4
.

There is a bijection between the set of cyclic curved
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
structures and the set of cohomological field theories on
V
.

\square

Explicitly, let
\{\ell_{n}\}
be the collection of
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
operations and
Y_{n}
are as defined in
1.5
. Then we put

I_{S}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\sum_{\tau}Y_{\tau}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})m(\tau),

where

Y_{\tau}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=\left(\bigotimes_{v\in\tau}Y_{|F_{v}|}\right)\left(\bigotimes_{S}x_{i}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\Delta^{\mathop{\otimes}\limits E(\tau)}\right).

Here on the right side the Casimir elements
\Delta
are indexed by the internal edges of
\tau
, and the inputs
x_{i}
as well as factors of the Casimir elements are fed
into operations
Y_{n}
according to the combinatorics of the tree.

4
Weighted graph complex

The constructions of the previous section can be seen as the tree part (genus zero part) of a version of the graph complex. We begin by recalling relevant notions
to fix the notations.

Consider a graph
G=(V,F,\delta,j)
(we keep the same notation as in paragraph
1.7
, without the cyclic order of flags around each vertex).
An orientation of
G
is a generator of the highest exterior power of the free abelian group generated by all internal edges and tails of
G
.

\mathfrak{or}\in\bigwedge\mathbb{Z}^{E(G)\cup\mathrm{Tail}(G)}.

The purpose of the orientation is to take care of the signs of graphs obtained by contracting or inserting edges.

A weighted modular graph is a graph
G
equipped with the additional data consisting of
g_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geqslant 0}
and
w_{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
for each vertex
v\in V
. We will
refer to
g_{v}
as the genus of the vertex
v
, and
w_{v}
as its weight. The purpose of numbers
g_{v}
is to keep track of the total genus of the graph as we
contract its edges, and the purpose of
w_{v}
is to keep track of the multiplicities of vertices. In particular we define the total genus and total weight of a
weighted modular graph as

g(G)=\sum_{v\in V}g_{v}+\mathop{\mathrm{dim}}\nolimits H^{1}(|G|),\quad\quad w(G)=\sum_{v\in V}w_{v},

where
|G|
denotes the geometric realization of
G
.

4.1
.

Denote by
C_{n}
the free abelian group generated by pairs
(G,\mathfrak{or})
, where
G
is (a representative of) an isomorphism class of connected weighted modular graphs with
n
tails
and
\mathfrak{or}
is its orientation, modulo relations
(G,-\mathfrak{or})=-(G,\mathfrak{or})
. We consider a grading of
C_{n}
by the number of internal edges of
G
and denote by

C_{n}^{p}
the corresponding component of degree
p
. Furthermore, it is graded by the total weight and the total genus of
G
and we will write
C_{g,n,w}^{p}
when
it is necessary to specify this additional grading.

Consider a differential on
C_{n}
of degree
+1
defined by expanding a vertex of a graph into a new edge in all possible ways.

d(G,\mathfrak{or})=\sum(\widetilde{G},e\wedge\mathfrak{or}),

where the sum is taken over all weighted modular graphs
\widetilde{G}
with an edge
e
connecting some vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
, such that

a)

by removing the edge
e
and identifying vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
we obtain graph
G
;

b)

let
\bar{v}
be the common image of vertices
v
and
v^{\prime}
then
w_{\bar{v}}=w_{v}+w_{v^{\prime}}
and

g_{\bar{v}}=\begin{cases}g_{v}+g_{v^{\prime}},\quad\textrm{if }v\neq v^{\prime}\\
g_{v}+1,\quad\textrm{if }v=v^{\prime};\end{cases}

c)

for all other vertices
u
of
G
the weight and genus are the same as in
\widetilde{G}
.

Clearly this differential is compatible with grading of
C_{n}^{\bullet}
by both weight and genus. Let us restrict to the subcomplex
C^{\bullet}_{0,n,w}
of genus zero,
which is spanned by trees, such that genus of every vertex is zero.

Proposition 4.2
.

For all
n\geqslant 1
we have isomorphisms

H^{p}_{\underline{\mathbf{n}}}\ \simeq\ \bigoplus_{w\geqslant 1}H^{p}(C^{\bullet}_{0,n,w}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathbf{k}).

Moreover, for each
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
the cohomology of
C^{\bullet}_{0,n,w}
is concentrated in degree
p=w-1
, and is spanned by trees
with weights of all vertices equal to
1
.

Proof:
Consider a tree
\tau
with
p
vertices of total weight
p+1
, so that there is exactly one vertex
v
of weight
2
, while the rest of them are of weight
1
. Notice that
there is at least one flag attached to
v
, due to the assumption that
n\geqslant 1
and connectedness of the tree. Applying the differential
of the graph complex
C^{\bullet}
to
\tau
we obtain a linear combination of trees which coincides with the (double of the) relation
R(\tau,v,f)
in (
3.1.1
) for any choice of flag
f\in F_{v}
.
Since the number of internal edges of a tree with
p+1
vertex is equal to
p
, we conclude that the cohomology
H^{p}(C^{\bullet}_{0,n,p+1}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathbf{k})\simeq H^{p}_{\underline{\mathbf{n}}}
.

The vanishing of all the other cohomology groups
H^{p}(C^{\bullet}_{0,n,w}\mathop{\otimes}\limits\mathbf{k})
for
p<w-1
is a consequence of the Koszulity of the operad
\mathrm{Lie}_{\infty}
.

\square

Remark 4.3
.

For

the two-vertex tree spanning

(see paragraph

At the level of
