Hilbert Space and Defect Hilbert Spaces Associated with Categorical Symmetries
Qiang Jia, Jiahua Tian

Abstract
We present a quantum mechanical approach to understanding the Hilbert space and the defect Hilbert spaces associated with line operators of BF theory combined with level- k k Chern-Simons theory. The defect Hilbert spaces are closely related to the category of ∗ * -representations of the C ∗ C^{*} -algebra of the compactly supported sections of the Fell line bundle over the conjugation action groupoid G / / Ad G G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G , and the structure of this category and the groupoid action on the objects of this category is interpreted quantum mechanically. We show that the action of the line operators on the Hilbert space of the B ​ F + k ​ C ​ S BF+kCS TQFT is given concretely by a convolution between the kernels that represent the line operators, and that the codimension- 2 2 twist and the codimension- 1 1 prequantum line bundle arise as two transgressions of the same universal level k ∈ H 4 ​ ( B ​ G , ℤ ) k\in H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{Z}) . For finite gauge group, the resulting convolution-eigenvalue formula is identified with the Verlinde formula for the (twisted) Drinfeld double D ω ​ ( G ) D^{\omega}(G) via an explicit phase-by-phase match with the known finite modular data. For compact Lie group, the convolution-kernel eigenvalues coincide in the regular sector with the semiclassical Hopf-link S S -kernel, identifying two complementary derivations of the same modular data.

2
The Conjugation Groupoid, the Untwisted Fell Line Bundle, and the Simple Basis

Before discussing the action of line operators on the Hilbert space of the pure
BF
theory, it is useful to write explicitly the simple objects of the untwisted groupoid algebra
C^{*}(G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G)
of the conjugation action groupoid
G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G
. Throughout this section, we keep the theory untwisted, namely we do
not
turn on the additional level-
k
Chern-Simons term. In particular, the relevant Fell line bundle is the trivial one. For notational simplicity, we first phrase the explicit basis formulas for finite
G
, so that all vector spaces are finite-dimensional and all convolutions are finite sums. For compact Lie
G
, finite orbit sums are replaced by
L^{2}
-sections, equivalently by direct integrals over the corresponding conjugacy classes and the precise replacement of the finite sum (
13
) will be stated below. In that setting, the same ket notation is used only as distributional shorthand.

It is important, however, to keep track of what kind of state space is being described here. For a finite group, the relevant symmetry category is
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}
, while for a compact Lie group we use its analogue
{\rm Hilb}(G)
, the category of measurable fields of Hilbert spaces over
G

39
. The vectors introduced in this section, such as
\ket{g_{i},\mu}
below, are basis vectors in a simple object
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\in\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{G})
, or in the compact Lie group setting
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\in\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
. They should be interpreted as the internal state space attached to the codimension-
2
topological line defect labeled by
([g],\rho_{g})
, not as basis vectors of the physical Hilbert space obtained by quantizing the
BF
theory on a spatial Riemann surface to be discussed later. Throughout the paper we mostly use the notation
{\rm Hilb}(G)
and
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
, which is adapted to the compact Lie group setting; in the finite group case, these specialize to
\mathrm{Vec}_{G}
and
\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{G})
, respectively.

We consider the conjugation action groupoid

\mathcal{G}:=G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G\,.

(1)

Its objects are the elements
x\in G
, and an arrow from
x
to
uxu^{-1}
is denoted by

(u,x):x\longrightarrow uxu^{-1}\,.

(2)

The source, target, and composition maps are

s(u,x)=x\,,\qquad t(u,x)=uxu^{-1}\,,

(3)

(v,uxu^{-1})\circ(u,x)=(vu,x)\,.

(4)

Following
39
, we use the Fell line bundle
\Sigma_{k}
over the conjugation groupoid
\mathcal{G}
as a geometric incarnation for the cocycle phases appearing in the twisted groupoid convolution algebra. In the untwisted case considered in this section, the Fell line bundle over
\mathcal{G}
is simply the trivial complex line bundle

\Sigma_{0}:=(G\times G)\times\mathbb{C}\longrightarrow G\times G\,,

(5)

whose fiber over an arrow
(u,x)
is a one-dimensional complex vector space

(\Sigma_{0})_{(u,x)}\cong\mathbb{C}\,.

(6)

Therefore a compactly supported section of
\Sigma_{0}
is simply a complex-valued function on the set of arrows of
\mathcal{G}
. If
\delta_{(u,x)}
denotes the delta-section supported on the single arrow
(u,x)
, then the convolution product in the untwisted groupoid algebra is

\delta_{(v,y)}*\delta_{(u,x)}=\delta_{y,uxu^{-1}}\,\delta_{(vu,x)}\,,

(7)

which is just the algebraic incarnation of the groupoid composition (
4
). Namely we must require
t(u,x)=s(v,y)
and the result is supported only on the arrow
(vu,x)
.

A representation of the trivial Fell line bundle
\Sigma_{0}
is therefore the same as a collection of vector spaces
\{E_{x}\}_{x\in G}
, one for each object of the groupoid, together with linear isomorphisms (see
48
)

T_{u}(x):E_{x}\longrightarrow E_{uxu^{-1}}

(8)

for every arrow
(u,x)
, obeying the untwisted composition rule

T_{v}(uxu^{-1})\,T_{u}(x)=T_{vu}(x)\,.

(9)

Equivalently, the operator corresponding to the basis element
\delta_{(u,x)}
of the groupoid algebra is precisely the transport map
T_{u}(x)
from the fiber over
x
to the fiber over
uxu^{-1}
. In the twisted case, these transport maps would compose only projectively, with the discrepancy controlled by the
U(1)
-valued
2
-cocycle of the Fell line bundle
57
. In the present untwisted
BF
theory, however, there is no additional phase in (
9
).

2.1
The Simple Basis from a Conjugacy Class

We now construct the simple object labeled by a conjugacy class
[g]
and an irreducible representation
\rho_{g}
of the centralizer
C_{G}(g)
. The conjugacy class of
g
is the orbit of
g
under conjugation,

[g]=\{ugu^{-1}:u\in G\}\cong G/C_{G}(g)\,.

(10)

Choose a set of representatives
\{r_{i}\}
for the left cosets in
G/C_{G}(g)
, and define

g_{i}:=r_{i}gr_{i}^{-1}\,.

(11)

Then each
g_{i}
is an element of the conjugacy class
[g]
, and every element of
[g]
appears exactly once in this way.

Let
V_{\rho_{g}}
be the representation space of
\rho_{g}
. The simple object
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}
is a
G
-equivariant Hilbert-space field over
G
supported on the conjugacy class
[g]
. Its fiber over the object of
\mathcal{G}
is

(H_{[g],\rho_{g}})_{x}=\begin{cases}V_{\rho_{g}}\,,&x\in[g]\,,\\
0\,,&x\notin[g]\,.\end{cases}

(12)

After choosing the coset representatives
r_{i}
, we may identify each nonzero fiber
(H_{[g],\rho_{g}})_{g_{i}}
with a copy of
V_{\rho_{g}}
and write

H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\cong\bigoplus_{i}V_{\rho_{g}}^{(i)}\,.

(13)

For compact Lie
G
, the above equation is replaced by the induced Hilbert field over the conjugacy class. After choosing a measurable trivialization, such a Hilbert field may be written schematically as the direct integral

H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\simeq\int_{G/C_{G}(g)}^{\oplus}V_{\rho_{g}}\,d\bar{r}

(14)

where
d\bar{r}
is the quotient measure on
G/C_{G}(g)\cong[g]
. Thus the finite label
i
in (
13
) is replaced for compact Lie
G
by a point
rC_{G}(g)\in G/C_{G}(g)
. The symbols
|rgr^{-1},\mu\rangle
should then be understood as distributional Dirac sections localized at
rgr^{-1}\in[g]
, and not as normalizable Hilbert-space vectors. Operator identities involving these kets are to be interpreted after smearing against
L^{2}
-sections. In the finite group notation, if
\{e_{\mu}\}
is a basis of
V_{\rho_{g}}
, we denote by:

\ket{g_{i},\mu}

(15)

the basis vector
e_{\mu}
sitting in the
i
-th copy
V_{\rho_{g}}^{(i)}
, or equivalently in the fiber over the object
g_{i}
. Thus the first label directly records
which representatives

g_{i}
in the orbit
[g]
we are sitting over, while the second label
\mu
records a basis vector in the representation space of the centralizer irrep
\rho_{g}
.

The reason for introducing the coset representatives is therefore purely bookkeeping: the simple object
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}
is not supported only at the single object
g
, but on the entire orbit
[g]
. The representatives
r_{i}
provide a concrete way to identify the fiber over each representative
g_{i}
with the fixed vector space
V_{\rho_{g}}
, but the basis ket itself is more naturally labeled by
g_{i}
.

The same data also determine a representation of the untwisted groupoid algebra on the underlying vector space of the simple object:

\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}:C^{*}(G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G)\longrightarrow\mathrm{End}\big(H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big)\,.

(16)

The notation emphasizes that this representation depends on the label
([g],\rho_{g})
of the simple object. Different conjugacy classes
[g]
have different supports in the object set of the groupoid, and even for fixed
[g]
, different irreducible representations
\rho_{g}
of the centralizer
C_{G}(g)
give different actions on the internal fiber
V_{\rho_{g}}
. Thus
([g],\rho_{g})
is precisely the datum that classifies which representation of the groupoid algebra we are talking about.

2.2
How Groupoid Arrows Act on the Basis

We now describe explicitly how an arrow of the conjugation groupoid acts on the basis (
15
). Since
(u,x)
has source
x
, it can only act nontrivially on the fiber over
x
. Hence, if
x\neq g_{i}
for all
i
, then
(u,x)
annihilates
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}
. If
x=g_{i}
, then the arrow

(u,g_{i}):g_{i}\longrightarrow ug_{i}u^{-1}

(17)

must map the
i
-th fiber of (
12
) to the fiber over the conjugated element
ug_{i}u^{-1}
.

To make this map explicit, note that
ur_{i}
belongs to a unique left coset of
C_{G}(g)
, so there exist a unique index
j
and a unique element
c\in C_{G}(g)
such that

ur_{i}=r_{j}c\,.

(18)

Because
c
centralizes
g
, we have

ug_{i}u^{-1}=ur_{i}gr_{i}^{-1}u^{-1}=r_{j}cgc^{-1}r_{j}^{-1}=r_{j}gr_{j}^{-1}=g_{j}\,.

(19)

Therefore the arrow
(u,g_{i})
lands in the
j
-th fiber. Its action is precisely the action of
c
through the representation
\rho_{g}
:

\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big(\delta_{(u,g_{i})}\big)\ket{g_{i},\mu}=\sum_{\nu}\big[\rho_{g}(c)\big]_{\nu\mu}\ket{g_{j},\nu}\,.

(20)

Equivalently, if we want a formula for a general arrow
(u,x)
, then

\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big(\delta_{(u,x)}\big)\ket{g_{i},\mu}=\delta_{x,g_{i}}\sum_{\nu}\big[\rho_{g}(r_{j}^{-1}ur_{i})\big]_{\nu\mu}\ket{g_{j},\nu}\,,

(21)

where
j
is determined by the condition
ug_{i}u^{-1}=g_{j}
, so that
r_{j}^{-1}ur_{i}\in C_{G}(g)
.

This formula gives the precise relation between the arrow notation and the basis notation. The first entry of the arrow transports
g_{i}
to its conjugate
g_{j}
, while the residual factor in the centralizer acts on the internal charge label
\mu
through
\rho_{g}
.

As a consistency check, one can verify directly that (
20
) reproduces the groupoid multiplication. Suppose that

ur_{i}=r_{j}c\,,\qquad vr_{j}=r_{k}d\,,\qquad c,d\in C_{G}(g)\,.

(22)

Then

vur_{i}=vr_{j}c=r_{k}dc\,,

(23)

and hence

\begin{split}\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big(\delta_{(v,g_{j})}\big)\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big(\delta_{(u,g_{i})}\big)\ket{g_{i},\mu}&=\sum_{\lambda,\nu}\big[\rho_{g}(d)\big]_{\nu\lambda}\big[\rho_{g}(c)\big]_{\lambda\mu}\ket{g_{k},\nu}\\
&=\sum_{\nu}\big[\rho_{g}(dc)\big]_{\nu\mu}\ket{g_{k},\nu}\\
&=\pi_{[g],\rho_{g}}\big(\delta_{(vu,g_{i})}\big)\ket{g_{i},\mu}\,,\end{split}

(24)

which is exactly the representation-theoretic version of the convolution law (
7
). This is the untwisted formula relevant for the pure
BF
theory. If one later turns on the level-
k
Chern-Simons twist, the same transport law is modified by the
U(1)
-phase of the corresponding Fell line bundle, and the centralizer representation
\rho_{g}
is replaced by the appropriate projective representation.

2.3
Half-Braiding and Braiding in the Basis

We now explain how the braiding in
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
is written in the notation introduced above. In the untwisted case relevant for the pure
BF
theory, the category
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
can be described in terms of
G
-equivariant Hilbert-space fields
{\rm Hilb}_{G}(G)
over
G
. The braiding is obtained by using the class representative of the first factor to transport the second factor by conjugation. In terms of the equivariant vector bundle description used above, this transport is exactly the arrow action encoded by the maps
T_{u}(x)
in (
8
).

Let
X
and
Y
be two objects of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
. Their tensor product is the convolution tensor product

(X\otimes Y)_{z}=\bigoplus_{xy=z}X_{x}\otimes Y_{y}\,.

(25)

Thus if
v_{x}\in X_{x}
and
w_{y}\in Y_{y}
, then the elementary tensor
v_{x}\otimes w_{y}
lies in the component
(X\otimes Y)_{xy}
.

To distinguish clearly the half-braiding from the full braiding, let us first introduce the one-dimensional degree-
x
object
\delta_{x}
of the
underlying
category
{\rm Hilb}(G)
, namely

(\delta_{x})_{x}=\mathbb{C}\,,\qquad(\delta_{x})_{z}=0\quad\text{for}\ z\neq x\,.

(26)

Here
\delta_{x}
is
not
the delta-section
\delta_{(e,x)}
of the groupoid algebra supported on the identity arrow at
x
, nor for generic
x
is it an object of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
itself: an honest object of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
supported on
x
must in general be supported on the full conjugacy class
[x]
. Rather, because
Y\in\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
is a centered object, it comes equipped with a half-braiding against every object of
{\rm Hilb}(G)
, in particular against the degree-
x
line
\delta_{x}
. If
1_{x}
denotes its basis vector and
w_{y}\in Y_{y}
is a homogeneous vector of degree
y
, then the half-braiding of the centered object
Y
with the degree-
x
line
\delta_{x}\in{\rm Hilb}(G)
is

c^{Y}_{x}:\delta_{x}\otimes Y\longrightarrow Y\otimes\delta_{x}\,,\qquad c^{Y}_{x}(1_{x}\otimes w_{y}):=T^{Y}_{x}(y)(w_{y})\otimes 1_{x}\,.

(27)

Here
T^{Y}_{x}(y):Y_{y}\rightarrow Y_{xyx^{-1}}
is the transport map assigned by
Y
to the arrow
(x,y):y\to xyx^{-1}
. In other words, this is not a new kind of map: it is precisely the earlier groupoid transport of (
8
) specialized to the representation
E=Y
and the arrow label
u=x
, so one may read

T^{Y}_{x}(y)=T_{u}(y)\big|_{u=x,\;E=Y}\,.

(28)

The target must be
Y_{xyx^{-1}}
because
c^{Y}_{x}
is a morphism in
{\rm Hilb}(G)
: the source
1_{x}\otimes w_{y}
has total degree
xy
, while an element of
Y_{z}\otimes\delta_{x}
has total degree
zx
, so degree preservation forces
zx=xy
, hence
z=xyx^{-1}
.

The relevant
center coherence
is the tensor-compatibility axiom for the half-braiding of the centered object
Y
:

c^{Y}_{X\otimes Z}=\big(c^{Y}_{X}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z}\big)\circ\big(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes c^{Y}_{Z}\big),\qquad c^{Y}_{\mathbf{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{Y}\,.

(29)

Specializing to
X=\delta_{u}
and
Z=\delta_{v}
, and evaluating on
1_{u}\otimes 1_{v}\otimes w_{y}
, this gives

T^{Y}_{u}(vyv^{-1})\,T^{Y}_{v}(y)=T^{Y}_{uv}(y)\,,

(30)

which is exactly the same composition law as the groupoid-representation rule (
9
), now read in the center language.

So the logic is the following. The
half
-braiding belongs to the Drinfeld-center structure of an object
Y\in\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
, and is evaluated against arbitrary objects of the underlying category
{\rm Hilb}(G)
. The
full
braiding, by contrast, is the braiding internal to
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
itself, obtained by applying this half-braiding to the underlying
{\rm Hilb}(G)
-object of the first factor.

The full braiding with a general object
X
is obtained by decomposing
X
into its homogeneous sectors

X=\bigoplus_{x\in G}X_{x}

(31)

and applying the half-braiding of
Y
with
x
on each
X_{x}
-sector. Concretely, for
v_{x}\in X_{x}
one treats
v_{x}
as carrying
x
label, so the braiding is

c_{X,Y}(v_{x}\otimes w_{y}):=T^{Y}_{x}(y)(w_{y})\otimes v_{x}\,.

(32)

Thus the full braiding in
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
is assembled from the half-braidings with the elementary degree-
x
lines in the underlying category
{\rm Hilb}(G)
. Only when
x\in Z(G)
, where
Z(G)\in G
is the center subgroup so that
[x]=\{x\}
, can such a line also be viewed as an honest object of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
supported at the single group element
x
.

The right-hand side of (
32
) indeed lies in the correct homogeneous component of
Y\otimes X
, because

T^{Y}_{x}(y)(w_{y})\otimes v_{x}\in Y_{xyx^{-1}}\otimes X_{x}\subset(Y\otimes X)_{(xyx^{-1})x}=(Y\otimes X)_{xy}\,.

(33)

Hence the braiding preserves the total grading while moving the second factor by conjugation with
x
carried by the first factor.

We now specialize to two simple objects

X=H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\,,\qquad Y=H_{[h],\rho_{h}}\,.

(34)

For the first object we keep the notation of the previous subsection

g_{i}=r_{i}gr_{i}^{-1}\,,\qquad\ket{g_{i},\mu}\in(H_{[g],\rho_{g}})_{g_{i}}\,.

(35)

For the second object we choose representatives
\{q_{k}\}
of the left cosets in
G/C_{G}(h)
and define

h_{k}:=q_{k}hq_{k}^{-1}\,,\qquad\ket{h_{k},\nu}\in(H_{[h],\rho_{h}})_{h_{k}}\,.

(36)

Then the tensor product basis vector

\ket{g_{i},\mu}\otimes\ket{h_{k},\nu}

(37)

lies in the homogeneous component of
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes H_{[h],\rho_{h}}
labeled by
g_{i}h_{k}
.

To compute its braiding, we must evaluate the action of the arrow
(g_{i},h_{k})
on the second factor. By the same argument as in (
18
)–(
20
), there exist a unique index
\ell
and a unique element
c_{ik}\in C_{G}(h)
such that

g_{i}q_{k}=q_{\ell}c_{ik}\,,\qquad c_{ik}\in C_{G}(h)\,.

(38)

Equivalently,

g_{i}h_{k}g_{i}^{-1}=h_{\ell}\,.

(39)

Therefore the transport of the second factor by the arrow
(g_{i},h_{k})
is

T^{H_{[h],\rho_{h}}}_{g_{i}}(h_{k})\ket{h_{k},\nu}=\sum_{\lambda}\big[\rho_{h}(c_{ik})\big]_{\lambda\nu}\ket{h_{\ell},\lambda}\,.

(40)

Thus the elementary half-braiding of
H_{[h],\rho_{h}}
with the object
\delta_{g_{i}}
is

c^{H_{[h],\rho_{h}}}_{g_{i}}\big(1_{g_{i}}\otimes\ket{h_{k},\nu}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\big[\rho_{h}(c_{ik})\big]_{\lambda\nu}\ket{h_{\ell},\lambda}\otimes 1_{g_{i}}\,.

(41)

The full braiding between the two simples is obtained by tensoring this half-braiding with the internal vector in the
g_{i}
-sector. More explicitly, under the decomposition

H_{[g],\rho_{g}}=\bigoplus_{i}\delta_{g_{i}}\otimes V_{\rho_{g}}^{(i)}\,,

(42)

one may regard

\ket{g_{i},\mu}=1_{g_{i}}\otimes e_{\mu}\,.

(43)

Substituting (
41
) into the general formula (
32
), we obtain the braiding between the two simples:

c_{H_{[g],\rho_{g}},H_{[h],\rho_{h}}}\big(\ket{g_{i},\mu}\otimes\ket{h_{k},\nu}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\big[\rho_{h}(c_{ik})\big]_{\lambda\nu}\ket{h_{\ell},\lambda}\otimes\ket{g_{i},\mu}\,,

(44)

where
\ell
and
c_{ik}
are determined by (
38
).

This formula should be compared directly with the action of groupoid arrows discussed in the previous subsection. The representative
g_{i}
carried by the first factor acts on the second factor exactly as the arrow
(g_{i},h_{k})
:

(g_{i},h_{k}):h_{k}\longrightarrow g_{i}h_{k}g_{i}^{-1}=h_{\ell}\,.

(45)

After this transport is performed, one flips the two tensor factors. Therefore the braiding is not, in general, simply multiplication by
\rho_{h}(g_{i})
: the element
g_{i}
does not usually belong to the centralizer
C_{G}(h)
. Instead one must first rewrite its action on the
h_{k}
-fiber in the form (
38
), and only the residual centralizer element
c_{ik}
acts through the representation
\rho_{h}
.

It is useful to isolate the commuting case. Suppose that
g_{i}
commutes with
h_{k}
. Then
g_{i}h_{k}g_{i}^{-1}=h_{k}
, so
\ell=k
, and (
38
) becomes

g_{i}q_{k}=q_{k}c_{ik}\,,\qquad c_{ik}=q_{k}^{-1}g_{i}q_{k}\in C_{G}(h)\,.

(46)

In this case the braiding reduces to

c_{H_{[g],\rho_{g}},H_{[h],\rho_{h}}}\big(\ket{g_{i},\mu}\otimes\ket{h_{k},\nu}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\big[\rho_{h}(q_{k}^{-1}g_{i}q_{k})\big]_{\lambda\nu}\ket{h_{k},\lambda}\otimes\ket{g_{i},\mu}\,.

(47)

In particular, if we choose the base representative
q_{1}=e
so that
h_{1}=h
, and if
g_{i}\in C_{G}(h)
, then

c_{H_{[g],\rho_{g}},H_{[h],\rho_{h}}}\big(\ket{g_{i},\mu}\otimes\ket{h,\nu}\big)=\sum_{\lambda}\big[\rho_{h}(g_{i})\big]_{\lambda\nu}\ket{h,\lambda}\otimes\ket{g_{i},\mu}\,.

(48)

This is the precise sense in which, in a commuting sector, the braiding looks like “the second charge transforms by
\rho_{h}(g_{i})
and then the two factors are exchanged”.

Since we are still in the pure
BF
theory, there is no additional phase in (
44
). If one later replaces the trivial Fell line bundle by a nontrivial Fell line bundle
\Sigma_{k}
, then the transport law (
40
) is multiplied by the corresponding transgression phase, so (
44
) acquires an extra
\Sigma_{k}
-dependent factor while keeping the same conjugation pattern of the basis labels.

Throughout the discussion above, the braiding was written on homogeneous basis
vectors
|g_{i},\mu\rangle
and
|h_{k},\nu\rangle
of the two simple objects
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}
and
H_{[h],\rho_{h}}
.

(
44

\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{G})

3
The Actions of Extended Operators on the Hilbert Space of the SymTFT

We now turn to the genuine Hilbert space of the untwisted
BF
theory on a spatial torus. This is a different state space from the simple objects discussed in section
2
. There the kets
\ket{g_{i},\mu}
live in
H_{[g],\rho_{g}}\in\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
and encodes codimension-
2
line-defect data in the Drinfeld center. Here, by contrast, the kets
\ket{x(a),x(b)}_{BF}
form a position basis for the physical Hilbert space assigned by quantization to the codimension-
1
spatial manifold which we choose to be
T^{2}
. The relation between the two structures is that an object of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
labels a topological line operator, and this line operator acts on the physical Hilbert space
H_{BF}(T^{2})
.

For simplicity, we will fix the 3D spacetime to be a solid torus
D^{2}\times S^{1}
with the “time-direction” being the radial direction of
D^{2}
. In this case, the spatial manifold to which one assigns the Hilbert space of the theory is topologically a torus. We will denote by
a
and
b
the
a
- and
b
-cycles of the spatial manifold
T^{2}
. The configuration space of the theory is given by a flat connection
x
on
T^{2}
specified by the holonomies
x(a),x(b)\in G
satisfying
x(a)x(b)=x(b)x(a)
, modulo gauge transformations that act on
x(a)
and
x(b)
as simultaneous conjugations. I.e., the configuration space of the theory is:

\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}=\{(x(a),x(b))\in G\times G|x(a)x(b)=x(b)x(a)\}/\sim

(57)

with
(x(a),x(b))\sim(gx(a)g^{-1},gx(b)g^{-1})
,
\forall g\in G
. We will denote by
\mathcal{A}_{0}
the configuration space before modding out gauge transformations and by
\bra{x(a),x(b)}_{BF}
(and its dual
\ket{x(a),x(b)}_{BF}
) with
(x(a),x(b))\in\mathcal{A}_{0}
a representative of a gauge-equivalent class of configuration of the theory. The Hilbert space of the theory is the space of functions on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
and is represented as
\psi(x):=\braket{x(a),x(b)|\psi}_{BF}\in H_{BF}
. When no confusion can arise, we will suppress the subscript
BF
on these physical basis states. This function must satisfy
\psi(x)=\psi(gxg^{-1})
in order to respect the structure of
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
in (
57
).

To match the notation of section
2
, we continue to label untwisted line operators by the same pair
([g],\rho_{g})
, where
g
is a chosen representative of the conjugacy class and
\rho_{g}
is an irreducible representation of
C_{G}(g)
. In section
3.1
, we will define the representation of gauge invariant line operators, which live in
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
, on
H_{BF}
by looking at their action on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. In section
3.2
, we will show that the representation we define indeed respects the structure of
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
. We show in particular that the representation of gauge invariant line operators on
H_{BF}
is simply a convolution in the space of functions on
\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0}
. In section
3.3
we will provide concrete examples.

3.1
The Gauge Invariant Line Operator

We start with the line operator
v_{g,\rho_{g}}
whose action on a representative
\bra{x}
of a physical state is as follows

\bra{x(a),x(b)}\rho(v_{g,\rho_{g}})=\mathbf{1}_{C_{G}(x(b))}(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))\bra{gx(a),x(b)}\,,

(58)

where
\mathbf{1}_{H}(x)
is a
\mathbf{1}
-function supported on
H\subset G
. Here we always assume that the line operator
v_{g,\rho_{g}}
is placed along the
b
-cycle, and we have implicitly assumed that
\rho
acts as a representation mapping the line operators into an endomorphism of the Hilbert space. To understand this action, notice that the line operator
v_{g,\rho_{g}}
is written as

v_{g,\rho_{g}}=\textrm{tr}_{\rho_{g}}\mathcal{P}\exp\left(i\oint_{b}A\right)\exp\left(i\oint_{b}(\alpha,B)\right)\,,

(59)

in the
BF
theory, where
g=e^{i\alpha}
and
\textrm{tr}_{\rho_{g}}\mathcal{P}\exp\left(i\oint_{b}A\right)
is the Wilson loop of representation
\rho_{g}
under the
C_{G}(g)
subgroup. The insertion of
\exp\left(i\oint_{b}(\alpha,B)\right)
shifts the holonomy
x(a)
along the
a
-cycle to
gx(a)
. Since
gx(a)
should commute with
x(b)
, which implies the action gives zero unless
g\in C_{G}(x(b))
. On the other hand, the Wilson loop
\textrm{tr}_{\rho_{g}}\mathcal{P}\exp\left(i\oint_{b}A\right)
depends on the holonomy along
b
-cycle, and is evaluated to be the character
\chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))
.

Unfortunately,
\rho(v_{g,\rho_{g}})
is not a genuine physical operation on
\bra{x(a),x(b)}
, since in general

\bra{x(a),x(b)}\rho(v_{g,\rho_{g}})\neq\bra{kx(a)k^{-1},kx(b)k^{-1}}\rho(v_{g,\rho_{g}})\,,

(60)

due to
\mathbf{1}_{C_{G}(kx(b)k^{-1})}(g)=\mathbf{1}_{C_{G}(x(b))}(k^{-1}gk)
being not necessarily equal to
\mathbf{1}_{C_{G}(x(b))}(g)
for arbitrary
k\in G
. In other words,
v_{g,\rho_{g}}
acts differently on different representatives of the same physical state. To resolve this issue, we define:

v_{[g],\rho_{g}}:=\int_{[g]}d\mu(g)\ v_{g,\rho_{g}}\,,\quad\textrm{with}\quad\mu(kgk^{-1})=\mu(g),\quad\forall k\in G\,,

(61)

where
\mu(g)
is a measure on the conjugacy class
[g]
, which is chosen to be invariant under the conjugation of
k\in G
.

One way to construct a conjugate-invariant measure
\mu(g)
is via the measure of the coset
[G/C_{G}(g)]
54
. Consider the coset decomposition
k=rh
with
k\in G,r\in[G/C_{G}(g)],h\in C_{G}(g)
, and decompose the measure
dk=d\lambda dh
. Begin with the operator

v_{[g],\rho_{g}}:=\int_{G}dk\ v_{kgk^{-1},\rho_{kgk^{-1}}}=\int_{[G/C_{G}(g)]}dr\int_{C_{G}(g)}dh\,v_{rgr^{-1},\rho_{rgr^{-1}}}\,,

(62)

where
\rho_{kgk^{-1}}(x):=\rho_{g}(k^{-1}xk)
. By construction,
v_{[g],\rho_{g}}
depends only on the conjugacy class of
g
. We assume
dk
and
dh
are left-invariant measure on
G
and
C_{G}(g)
, respectively, and we normalize
\int_{C_{G}(g)}dh=1
. The integral is independent on the choice of the coset representative
r
, since if we shift
r\rightarrow rh^{\prime},h^{\prime}\in C_{G}(g)
, we have
dk=d(rh^{\prime}h)=drd(h^{\prime}h)=drdh
. Moreover,
dr
is also left-invariant under the action of
G
as
dk
is left-invariant. Since the elements in
[G/C_{G}(g)]
have a one-to-one correspondence to the elements in the conjugacy class
[g]
via the map
r\rightarrow rgr^{-1}
, therefore
dr
also defines a measure
d\mu(g)
on
[g]
which is invariant under conjugation. We will write down the measure explicitly for the conjugacy class of
SU(2)
in the example section.

The action of
v_{[g],\rho_{g}}
on a representative
\bra{x(a),x(b)}
of a physical state is:

\bra{x(a),x(b)}\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))\bra{gx(a),x(b)}\,,

where

here is understood as the induced measure on the submanifold

from
[g]
. For finite
G
, the integral in (3.7) is simply the finite sum over
[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))
. For compact Lie
G
, the same notation denotes the distributional localization of the orbital integral over
[g]
to the commuting locus with

, with appropriate normalization

One can check that:

\begin{split}&\bra{hx(a)h^{-1},hx(b)h^{-1}}\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\\

=&\int_{[hgh^{-1}]\cap C_{G}(hx(b)h^{-1})}d\mu(hgh^{-1})\ \chi_{\rho_{hgh^{-1}}}(hx(b)h^{-1})\bra{hgx(a)h^{-1},hx(b)h^{-1}}\\

where in the third line we shifted

and the fourth line we used the fact that

is invariant under conjugation. Thus

is a gauge invariant line operator. We will denote by

the space of such line operators formed by tensoring

for it to be a genuine representation of

3.2
Stacking the Line Operators

We now consider stacking two line operators on the
b
-cycle to form
v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\in\mathcal{L}
acting on a representative
\bra{x}
from the right. Since
Z(\mathrm{Hilb}(G))
is braided, the two stacked line operators are canonically related by the braiding
c_{v_{[g],\rho_{g}},v_{[h],\rho_{h}}}:v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\xrightarrow{\sim}v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\otimes v_{[g],\rho_{g}}
. Thus, in the Hilbert-space representation considered below, braided commutativity should appear as the operator relation:

\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}})\cong\rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\otimes v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\,.

(65)

Namely, after applying the Hilbert-space action and identifying the two stackings through this braiding, the corresponding endomorphisms of

H_{\mathrm{BF}}
should match.

If
\rho:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\text{End}(H_{BF})
were a genuine representation of
\mathcal{L}
, it must preserve the tensor product of
\mathcal{L}
, i.e. the following must hold

\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}})=\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\ \rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}})\,,

(66)

where the RHS is set to be a multiplication from the right. As a sanity check, we must require that (
66
) respects (
65
). To see this, by (
66
) we have

\begin{split}\bra{x}\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}})&=\bra{x}\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\ \rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}})\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))\bra{gx(a),x(b)}\rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}})\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(g)\int_{[h]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(h)\\
&\qquad\chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))\chi_{\rho_{h}}(x(b))\bra{hgx(a),x(b)}\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(g)\int_{[h]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(ghg^{-1})\\
&\qquad\chi_{\rho_{g}}(x(b))\chi_{\rho_{ghg^{-1}}}(x(b))\bra{ghx(a),x(b)}\\
&=\bra{x}\rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}})\ \rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})=\bra{x}\rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\otimes v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\end{split}

(67)

where in the fourth line we shift
h
to
ghg^{-1}
and in the fifth line we used the fact that
g
is restricted to be in
C_{G}(x(b))
by the first integral
\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(x(b))}d\mu(g)
, so that
\chi_{\rho_{ghg^{-1}}}(x(b))=\chi_{\rho_{h}}(g^{-1}x(b)g)=\chi_{\rho_{h}}(x(b))
. Therefore, as long as
\rho:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\text{End}(H_{BF})
defined in (
63
) is a genuine representation of
\mathcal{L}
that preserves the tensor product in
\mathcal{L}
, the requirement of commutativity (
65
) is trivially satisfied.

Unfortunately, one cannot simply require (
66
) be true, since there are extra consistency conditions to be checked. Schematically, for
v_{[g],\rho_{g}},v_{[h],\rho_{h}}\in\mathcal{L}
, we have

v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}}=\bigoplus_{[k],\rho_{k}}N_{([g],\rho_{g}),([h],\rho_{h})}^{[k],\rho_{k}}\ v_{[k],\rho_{k}}\,,

(68)

where
[k]
and
\rho_{k}
depend implicitly on
([g],\rho_{g})
and
([h],\rho_{h})
. It is natural to expect that
[k]
runs over all conjugacy classes that appear in
[h]\cdot[g]
, the set of which coincides with those obtained from
[g]\cdot[h]
. Hence (
68
) is automatically invariant under
[g]\leftrightarrow[h]
. Given (
68
), for
\rho
to be a genuine representation of
\mathcal{L}
, we must require

\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\ \rho(v_{[h],\rho_{h}})=\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}}\otimes v_{[h],\rho_{h}})=\sum_{[k]\in[h]\cdot[g],\rho_{k}}N_{([g],\rho_{g}),([h],\rho_{h})}^{[k],\rho_{k}}\ \rho(v_{[k],\rho_{k}})\,.

(69)

One must also ask what centralizer representations
\rho_{k}
could have appeared in the direct sum. A natural construction would be to first construct the induced
G
-representations
\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(g)}^{G}\rho_{g}
and
\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(h)}^{G}\rho_{h}
, and consider the tensor product decomposition of
\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(g)}^{G}\rho_{g}\otimes\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(h)}^{G}\rho_{h}
, for which we have:

\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(g)}^{G}\rho_{g}\otimes\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(h)}^{G}\rho_{h}=\bigoplus_{[k]\in[h]\cdot[g]}\bigoplus_{\rho_{k}}{N^{\prime}}^{([k],\rho_{k})}_{([g],\rho_{g}),([h],\rho_{h})}\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(k)}^{G}\rho_{k}\,,

(70)

where the sum runs over all conjugacy classes obtained from
[h]\cdot[g]
, and all representations of each class
[k]
that come from the tensor product decomposition of
\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(g)}^{G}\rho_{g}\otimes\text{Ind}_{C_{G}(h)}^{G}\rho_{h}
with representative
k=hg
. It is natural to expect that
N^{\prime}
’s in (
70
) are proportional to the corresponding
N
’s in (
69
), which, if verified, will ensure that
\rho:\mathcal{L}\rightarrow\text{End}(H_{BF})
defined in (
63
) respects the tensor product in
\mathcal{L}
.

Let us revisit the action (
63
). For a fixed

, we define the wave function:

\psi_{v}(u)=\braket{u,v|\psi}\,,

(71)

for
v:=x(b)
,

. One can think of

\psi_{v}(kuk^{-1})=\braket{kuk^{-1},v|\psi}=\braket{kuk^{-1},kvk^{-1}|\psi}=\braket{u,v|\psi}=\psi_{v}(u),\ \forall k\in C_{G}(v)\,,

(72)

\psi_{v}
is a class function of
C_{G}(v)
, i.e.
\psi_{v}\in Cl(C_{G}(v))
. One can now write the action of
\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})
on
Cl(C_{G}(v))
as

\begin{split}(\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\psi_{v})(u)&:=\bra{u,v}\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\ket{\psi}\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)\ \psi_{v}(gu)\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)\ \left(\int_{C_{G}(v)}dh\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{h,g}\right)\ \psi_{v}(gu)\\
&=\int_{C_{G}(v)}dh\left(\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{h,g}\right)\ \psi_{v}(gu)\\
&:=\int_{C_{G}(v)}dh\ \mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}(h^{-1})\ \psi_{v}(hu)\,,\end{split}

(73)

with kernel

is defined as

\mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}(h)=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{h^{-1},g}\,,

(74)

where
\int_{C_{G}(v)}dx\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{x,h}f(x)=f(h)\,,\forall h\in C_{G}(v)
. For any
\ell\in C_{G}(v)
we have

\begin{split}\mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}(\ell h\ell^{-1})&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(g)\ \chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{\ell h^{-1}\ell^{-1},g}\\
&=\int_{[g]\cap C_{G}(v)}\ d\mu(\ell g\ell^{-1})\ \chi_{\rho_{\ell g\ell^{-1}}}(v)\ \delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{\ell h^{-1}\ell^{-1},\ell g\ell^{-1}}\\
&=\mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}(h)\,,\end{split}

(75)

where in the second line we change
g\rightarrow\ell g\ell^{-1}
, and in the last step we use
\delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{\ell h^{-1}\ell^{-1},\ell g\ell^{-1}}=\delta^{C_{G}(v)}_{h^{-1},g}
and
\chi_{\rho_{\ell g\ell^{-1}}}(v)=\chi_{\rho_{g}}(\ell^{-1}v\ell)=\chi_{\rho_{g}}(v)
. Hence
\mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}(h)
is a class function on
C_{G}(v)
and (

) is a convolution by
\mathcal{K}^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{v}
in the
v
-sector of
H_{BF}\cong L^{2}(\widetilde{\mathcal{A}}_{0})
, i.e. when

. Thus, the action of
v_{[g],\rho_{g}}
on

can be written as

(\rho(v_{[g],\rho_{g}})\psi_{v})(u)=(\mathcal{K}_{v}^{[g],\rho_{g}}*\psi_{v})(u)\,,

(76)

which is a convolution on
Cl(C_{G}(v))
.

We now look for eigenfunctions and eigenvalues of (

). Since
\mathcal{K}_{v}^{[g],\rho_{g}}
is itself a class function of

, it can be expanded as

\mathcal{K}_{v}^{[g],\rho_{g}}(x)=\sum_{R_{v}}K^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{R_{v}}\chi_{R_{v}}(x)\,,

where
\chi_{R_{v}}(x)
is the character of
R_{v}\in\text{Irrep}(C_{G}(v))
, the set of the isomorphism classes of the irreducible representations of
C_{G}(v)
. Thus the convolution (
76
) becomes

(\mathcal{K}_{v}^{[g],\rho_{g}}*\psi_{v})(u)=\sum_{R_{v}}K^{[g],\rho_{g}}_{R_{v}}(\chi_{R_{v}}*\psi_{v})(u)\,.

(78)

Recall that we have

\frac{1}{|G|}\int dg\,\chi_{\rho}(g)\chi_{\nu}(g^{-1}h)=\delta_{\rho,\nu}\frac{\chi_{\rho}(h)}{\chi_{\rho}(e)}\,,

of any group

, the convolution with the character is

\begin{split}(\mathcal{K}*\chi_{R})(x)&=\int_{G}dh\ \mathcal{K}(h^{-1})\mathop{\mathrm{tr}}\nolimits(\rho_{R}(h)\rho_{R}(x))\\

\begin{split}\rho_{R}(x)^{-1}\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}\rho_{R}(x)&=\int_{G}dh\ \mathcal{K}(h^{-1})\rho_{R}(x^{-1}hx)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h\rightarrow xhx^{-1}}}{{=}}\int_{G}dh\ \mathcal{K}(h^{-1})\rho_{R}(h)=\mathcal{A}_{\mathcal{K}}\,,\end{split}

for any

4.1

Let

Consider the mixed action on

The temporal components

6
The Transgression of Chern-Simons Level

The brief discussion in section

6.4
Summary

The relationship between the codimension-

\displaystyle k\in H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{Z})

\displaystyle\rightsquigarrow\quad\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\delta\big(\tau(k)\big)=\mathrm{tr}_{S^{1}}(k),\ \ \tau(k)\in H^{2}(G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G,U(1)),\\[4.0pt]
\mathrm{tr}_{\Sigma}(k)=c_{1}(\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma,k})\in H^{2}(\operatorname{Loc}_{G}(\Sigma),\mathbb{Z}).\end{array}\right.

(241)

Here
\delta:H^{2}(G//_{\mathrm{Ad}}G,U(1))\xrightarrow{\sim}H^{3}([G/G],\mathbb{Z})
is the isomorphism induced by the exponential sequence, so if one implicitly identifies these two cohomology groups via
\delta
, one may simply write
\tau(k)=\mathrm{tr}_{S^{1}}(k)
. The first line is the gerbe/Fell-line-bundle twist on the loop stack, written in the groupoid language used throughout this paper. The second line is the first Chern class of the prequantum line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma,k}\to\operatorname{Loc}_{G}(\Sigma)
.
Thus the two structures at different codimensions come from the same origin in the sense that they descend from the same universal level, namely
k\in H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{Z})
. However, they are clearly not the same as geometric objects: the circle transgression gives a degree-
3
gerbe-type twist whose cocycle-level description is
\sigma_{k}
, whereas the surface transgression gives a degree-
2
line bundle whose curvature is the magnetic form
k\,\omega_{AB,\Sigma}
.

Put differently, the codimension-
1
line bundle is the ordinary line-bundle “shadow” of the same higher twist that governs the categorical data at codimension-
2
. In the compact Lie-group picture, the circle transgression carries a gerbe
3
-curvature while the surface transgression carries the ordinary
2
-curvature (
237

;
57
;

2
datum
\tau(k)
and the codimension-
1
line bundle
\mathcal{L}_{\Sigma,k}
are governed by the same level
k
, while living in adjacent degrees.

7
Comparison with Known Cases

The convolution-eigenvalue formalism developed in sections
3
and
5
reconstructs, in the finite-group case, well-known modular data of the (twisted) Drinfeld double, while in the compact Lie-group case it provides explicit kernels in a regime where no closed-form modular
S
-matrix is available. In this section we make these statements precise and locate the present work within the existing literature.

The guiding physical intuition is that a line operator labeled by
([g],\rho_{g})
acting on the torus sector labeled by
([h],\rho_{h})
should produce the normalized Hopf-link amplitude between those two labels. In the finite-group notation used below, the corresponding eigenvalue is
\lambda_{\rho_{h}}^{[g],\rho_{g}}
, or
\lambda_{\rho_{h}}^{[g],\rho_{g};\Sigma_{k}}
in the twisted theory. Concretely, one may prepare the sector
([h],\rho_{h})
by inserting that line along the core of a solid torus; acting by the line
([g],\rho_{g})
along the dual cycle then produces a Hopf link. The unnormalized amplitude is therefore the modular kernel
S_{([g],\rho_{g}),([h],\rho_{h})}
, and the eigenvalue should be obtained by dividing by the vacuum amplitude in the same sector:

\lambda_{\rho_{h}}^{[g],\rho_{g}}\;=\;\left(\frac{S_{([g],\rho_{g}),([h],\rho_{h})}}{S_{([e],\mathbf{1}),([h],\rho_{h})}}\right)^{*},

(242)

with the evident replacement
S\to S^{\omega}
and
\lambda_{\rho_{h}}^{[g],\rho_{g}}\to\lambda_{\rho_{h}}^{[g],\rho_{g};\Sigma_{k}}
in the twisted case. In the regular sector of compact Lie group we have the same schematic statement. The complex conjugation records the bra/ket and orientation conventions used in our convolution action. The point of the comparisons below is therefore to test whether the explicit convolution action on
H_{BF}(T^{2})
or
H_{BF+kCS}(T^{2})
computes the same Hopf-link pairing as the modular-data description. For finite groups this principle becomes a theorem, because
\textbf{Rep}(D^{\omega}(G))
is a modular tensor category and the Verlinde diagonalization identifies fusion eigenvalues with normalized
S
-matrix columns. For compact Lie groups we use the same principle more cautiously: in the regular sector the convolution kernel and the Hopf-link path-integral kernel should agree after the appropriate normalization, whereas a full continuous Verlinde theorem would require additional harmonic analysis that lies beyond the scope of this paper.

7.3
Compact Lie Groups: Regular Sector and Match with the Hopf-Link
S
-Kernel

For compact Lie group
G
, the category
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
is no longer a finite, semisimple modular tensor category, and there is no closed-form modular
S
-matrix in the standard finite-MTC sense. Nevertheless, the convolution kernels of section
3.3
and the projective compact Lie-group kernels obtained by the twisted construction of section
5.2
are well-defined operational objects. In a recent work
40
, semi-classical
S
- and
T
-kernels for compact Lie
G
with
k\in H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{Z})
are derived from a Hopf-link / framing path integral on
S^{3}
. We show below that, in the
regular sector
where both insertion holonomies are regular elements of
G
, the eigenvalue formula derived from our convolution kernel coincides exactly with the Hopf-link
S
-kernel of
40
, up to the conventional complex conjugation of (
246
) and the standard Weyl-denominator normalization. This identifies the convolution kernel of this paper and the path-integral kernel of
40
as two computations of the same regular-sector modular kernel. Turning this kernel match into a full compact Lie-group analogue of the fusion-preservation identity (
247
) requires the continuous version of Verlinde formula, which lies beyond the scope of this paper.

To set up notation, let
G
be a non-abelian compact connected Lie group with maximal torus
T\subset G
, Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
, Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{t}=\mathrm{Lie}(T)
, positive root system
\Lambda^{+}\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}
, and Weyl group
W=N_{G}(T)/T
where
N_{G}(T)
is the normalizer of
T
. An element
g\in G
is called
regular
if its centralizer
C_{G}(g)
is a maximal torus, conjugate to
T
. We may then choose a representative
g\in T
, and
C_{G}(g)=T
. Two regular elements
g,h\in T
are conjugate in
G
iff
h=w(g):=\tilde{w}\,g\,\tilde{w}^{-1}
for some
w\in W
and lift
\tilde{w}\in N_{G}(T)
; equivalently,
W\cdot g
is the orbit of
g
in
T
under the Weyl action.

We focus on the regular sector where
C_{G}(g)=C_{G}(v)=T
are abelian, hence the irreducible representations
\rho_{g}
and
R_{v}
are one-dimensional characters of
T
. We label them by integral weights

\mu,\nu\in\operatorname{Hom}(T,U(1))\subset\mathfrak{t}^{*}.

Here
\mu
labels the charge representation
\rho_{g}=\chi_{\mu}
attached to the line operator
([g],\rho_{g})
, while
\nu
labels the centralizer representation
R_{v}=\chi_{\nu}
used to diagonalize the fixed-
v
sector. We use the same symbols for a character and for its differential on
\mathfrak{t}
. Thus, writing
g=e^{i\alpha}
,
v=e^{i\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{t}
, we have

\chi_{\mu}(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\mu(\theta)}\,,\qquad\chi_{\nu}(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\nu(\theta)}\,,\qquad\dim R_{v}=1\,.

(265)

For
x=w(g)\in W\cdot g
, the centralizer is
C_{G}(x)=\tilde{w}T\tilde{w}^{-1}=T
, and the conjugated centralizer representation
\rho_{x}
corresponds to the Weyl-translated weight, giving

\chi_{\rho_{x}}(e^{i\theta})=e^{i\mu(w^{-1}(\theta))}\,.

(266)

We assume
v\in T
as the representative. In the regular sector, the intersection
[g]\cap C_{G}(v)
appearing in the unfolded eigenvalue formula (
243
) simplifies dramatically: for
g,v
both regular, then

[g]\cap C_{G}(v)\;=\;[g]\cap T\;=\;\{w\cdot g|w\in W\}\,,

(267)

which is a finite set of
|W|
elements. Furthermore, since
C_{G}(g)=C_{G}(v)=T
are abelian, the irreducible projective representations
\rho^{\alpha_{g}}_{g}
and
R^{\alpha_{v}}_{v}
are one-dimensional characters of
T
. We label them by integral weights
n\in\mathfrak{t}^{*}
. Here
n
labels the irreducible projective representation
\rho^{\alpha_{g}}_{g;n}=\chi^{\alpha_{g}}_{g;n}
attached to the line operator
([g],\rho_{g})
, while
m
labels the irreducible projective representation
R^{\alpha_{v}}_{v;m}=\chi_{v;m}^{\alpha_{v}}
used to diagonalize the fixed-
v
sector. Thus, writing
g=e^{i\alpha}
,
v=e^{i\beta}
with
\alpha,\beta\in\mathfrak{t}
, the modular
S
-matrix (
255
) reads

S^{\omega}_{([g],\rho_{n}),([v],R_{m})}=\frac{1}{|C_{G}(v)|}\sum_{w\in W}\chi^{\alpha_{w(g)}}_{\rho_{w(g);n}}(v)^{*}\,\chi^{\alpha_{v}}_{R_{v;m}}(w(g))^{*}\,.

(268)

However this is not yet the form of
S
-matrix derived in
40
, and our task in the following section is to show that it is actually equivalent to that form and one can show that (
90
) holds at least in the regular sector.

Let us begin with
k=0
. Applying the convolution-eigenvalue formula (
84
) to the kernel (
74
), equivalently using (
81
), and then substituting the regular intersection (
267
) together with the character identifications (
265

266

\lambda^{[g],\mu}_{\nu}=\sum_{w\in W}\,\chi_{\rho_{w(g)}}(v)\,\chi_{\nu}\big(w(g)\big)=\sum_{w\in W}\,e^{i\mu(w^{-1}(\beta))}\,e^{i\nu(w(\alpha))}\,.

(269)

After the harmless relabeling

, the same formula becomes

\lambda^{[g],\mu}_{\nu}=\lambda^{[g],\mu;\Sigma_{0}}_{\nu}=\sum_{w\in W}\,e^{i\mu(w(\beta))}\,e^{i\nu(w^{-1}(\alpha))}\,.

(270)

A direct generalization to level-
k
case would be to replace the ordinary characters in (
270
) by projective characters to have

\lambda^{[g],\mu;\Sigma_{k}}_{\nu}=\sum_{w\in W}\,\chi^{\alpha_{w(g)}}_{\rho_{w(g)}}(v)\,\chi^{\alpha_{\nu}}_{\nu}\big(w(g)\big)

(271)

where
\alpha_{w(g)}
and
\alpha_{\nu}
are 2-cocycles that twist the representations of
C_{G}(w(g))
and
C_{G}(v)
, respectively. We see that this is indeed proportional to (
268

268

S
-kernel in the regular sector computed in

S^{(k)}_{([g],\mu),([h],\nu)}\;\propto\;\sum_{w\in W}\,e^{-\frac{ik}{2\pi}\,\mathrm{tr}(\alpha\,w(\beta))}\,\chi^{*}_{\mu}(e^{iw(\beta)})\,\chi^{*}_{\nu}(e^{iw^{-1}(\alpha)})

(272)

as mentioned earlier.

Comparing (
271

272

\lambda^{[g],\mu;\Sigma_{k}}_{\nu}\;\propto\;\left(\frac{S^{(k)}_{([g],\mu),([h],\nu)}}{S^{(k)}_{([e],\mathbf{1}),([h],\nu)}}\right)^{*}\,,

up to some normalization factor. At

For general

\rho_{\theta}(\varphi_{1})\rho_{\theta}(\varphi_{2})=\alpha_{\theta}(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2})\rho(\varphi_{1}+\varphi_{2})\,,

8
Conclusion and Outlook

In this paper we gave a quantum-mechanical description of both the Hilbert space of the
BF
and
BF+kCS
theories and the defect Hilbert spaces associated with topological line operators. On the codimension-
2
side, we described the simple objects of the conjugation-groupoid algebra in an explicit basis, worked out the action of groupoid arrows, and expressed the half-braiding and braiding data in concrete terms. On the codimension-
1
side, we showed that the action of line operators on the physical Hilbert space is realized by convolution kernels, first in the untwisted
BF
theory and then in the twisted theory obtained by turning on a level-
k
Chern-Simons term. In the twisted case, the Fell line bundle over the conjugation groupoid and its cocycle representative encode the projective transport of defect data as well as the projective action on the
BF+kCS
Hilbert space, while the quantization discussion identifies the corresponding prequantum line bundle on the moduli of flat fields. By formulating both the codimension-
2
twist and the codimension-
1
magnetic line bundle as transgressions of the same universal level
k\in H^{4}(BG,\mathbb{Z})
, we made precise the common origin of the categorical symmetry data and the Hilbert-space quantization data that appear throughout the paper. Finally, in section
7
we identified the resulting convolution-eigenvalue formula, in the finite-group case, with the Verlinde formula for the (twisted) Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathrm{Vec}_{G})
via an explicit phase-by-phase match with the modular data in
24
. We also consider compact Lie groups like
U(1)
and
SU(2)
, and compare to the semiclassical Hopf-link calculation of
S
-kernel of
40
. The remaining open issues, principally a complete continuous Verlinde theorem for
{\rm Hilb}_{G}(G)
and the singular-sector match with
40
, are left for future work.

There are several directions arising from the analysis above that are worth exploring in future work. First, when we match the modular data of compact Lie group to the Hopf-link calculation in section
7.3
, we mainly focus on the regular sector of Lie group through the paper, where the centralizer group is a maximal torus. It is tempting to consider the singular-sector extension of the Hopf-link match of section
7.3
, where one or both centralizers
C_{G}(g)
,
C_{G}(v)
are non-abelian subgroups. Both the convolution-kernel framework of this paper and the path-integral framework of
40
remain well-defined in this regime, but the comparison requires an analysis stratified by the centralizer structure on the kernel side and the non-regular ansatz of
(
40
, eq. (4.32))
on the path-integral side; the structural reason to expect agreement is that both formulations compute the same prequantum-line-bundle holonomy data, now over a positive-dimensional
W_{g}\backslash W/W_{h}
stratification. A second direction is the formulation of a complete continuous Verlinde theorem for
\mathcal{Z}({\rm Hilb}(G))
, which is the Lie group generalization of the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\text{Vec}_{G})
for finite
G
. Section
7.3
establishes only the eigenvalue identity (
273
); and the missing analytic ingredients are the existence and reality of the distributional fusion measure, together with control of the boundary and singular-sector contributions. A third direction is the extension to non-compact gauge groups, e.g.
SL(2,\mathbb{R})
relevant for three-dimensional gravity, where the Plancherel decomposition of the centralizers involves both continuous and discrete series and the convolution-kernel analysis becomes considerably richer than the compact Lie case treated here. Finally, the Hopf-link comparison developed in section
7.3
is the simplest case of a broader program: the convolution kernels of this paper should compute, by the same prequantum-line-bundle interpretation, the SymTFT modular invariants associated with arbitrary links and three-manifolds, including lens spaces
L(p,q)
via Heegaard splittings and other Dehn-surgery presentations. We hope to return to these questions in future work.

\chi_{\rho_{p}}^{\alpha_{p}}(v)=\vartheta_{g}(v;p\rightarrow p)\chi_{\rho_{g}}^{\alpha_{g}}(r^{-1}vr)\,,

(296)

where

\vartheta_{g}(v;p\rightarrow p)=\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(r^{-1}vr,g))}\,.

(297)

Now consider
h\in C_{G}(v)
and denote
p^{\prime}=hph^{-1}
. Since both
p,h\in C_{G}(v)
, we also have
p^{\prime}\in C_{G}(v)
. Suppose
p^{\prime}=r^{\prime}g{r^{\prime}}^{-1}
, and we choose
r^{\prime}=hr
as the representative of the coset. Then we have similarly

\chi_{\rho_{p^{\prime}}}^{\alpha_{p^{\prime}}}(v)=\vartheta_{g}(v;p^{\prime}\rightarrow p^{\prime})\chi_{\rho_{g}}^{\alpha_{g}}({r^{\prime}}^{-1}vr^{\prime})\,.

(298)

Since
h\in C_{G}(v)
, we have
r^{-1}vr={r^{\prime}}^{-1}vr^{\prime}
, compare the two we have

\frac{\chi_{\rho_{p}^{\prime}}^{\alpha_{p}^{\prime}}(v)}{\chi_{\rho_{p}}^{\alpha_{p}}(v)}=\frac{\vartheta_{g}(v;p^{\prime}\rightarrow p^{\prime})}{\vartheta_{g}(v;p\rightarrow p)}=\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(r^{-1}vr,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(r^{-1}vr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}\,,

(299)

so the goal is to prove

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(w,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(w,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}((h,v),(p,v))}{\sigma_{k}((p^{\prime},v),(h,v))}\,,

(300)

where we set
w=r^{-1}vr\in C_{G}(g)
.

To simplify the notations, let us introduce

A=(r,g),\quad B=(w,g),\quad C=(h,p),\quad D=(v,p),\quad E=(v,p^{\prime})\,,

(301)

and we also denote

AB=(rw,g)=(vr,g)=DA,\qquad CA=(hr,g),

(302)

and, since
h\in C_{G}(v)
,

CD=(hv,p)=(vh,p)=EC.

(303)

Thus we can rewrite

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(w,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(w,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}(E,CA)}{\sigma_{k}(CA,B)}\frac{\sigma_{k}(A,B)}{\sigma_{k}(D,A)}.

(304)

Apply the cocycle condition (
132
) to
C,A,B
:

\sigma_{k}(C,AB)\,\sigma_{k}(A,B)=\sigma_{k}(C,A)\,\sigma_{k}(CA,B),

(305)

Since
AB=DA
, we have

\frac{\sigma_{k}(A,B)}{\sigma_{k}(CA,B)}=\frac{\sigma_{k}(C,A)}{\sigma_{k}(C,DA)}.

(306)

Substituting into (
304
) gives

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(w,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(w,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}(E,CA)\,\sigma_{k}(C,A)}{\sigma_{k}(C,DA)\,\sigma_{k}(D,A)}.

(307)

Then apply the cocycle condition to
C,D,A
:

\sigma_{k}(C,DA)\,\sigma_{k}(D,A)=\sigma_{k}(C,D)\,\sigma_{k}(CD,A).

(308)

Using
CD=EC
, it becomes

\sigma_{k}(C,DA)\,\sigma_{k}(D,A)=\sigma_{k}(C,D)\,\sigma_{k}(EC,A).

(309)

Therefore

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(w,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(w,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}(E,CA)\,\sigma_{k}(C,A)}{\sigma_{k}(C,D)\,\sigma_{k}(EC,A)}.

(310)

Finally, apply the cocycle condition to
E,C,A
:

\sigma_{k}(E,CA)\,\sigma_{k}(C,A)=\sigma_{k}(E,C)\,\sigma_{k}(EC,A),

(311)

hence

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(hr,g))}{\sigma_{k}((hr,g),(w,g))}\frac{\sigma_{k}((r,g),(w,g))}{\sigma_{k}((v,p),(r,g))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}(E,C)}{\sigma_{k}(C,D)}=\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(h,p))}{\sigma_{k}((h,p),(v,p))}.

(312)

To proceed, notice that as a transgressed cocycle
\sigma_{k}
can be written as

\sigma_{k}((u,x),(s,z))=\omega(uszs^{-1}u^{-1},u,s)\,\omega(u,x,s)^{-1}\,\omega(u,s,z),

(313)

where
x=szs^{-1}
and
\omega\in Z^{3}(G,U(1))
is the 3-cocycle, see, for example, (5.15) in
24
. Using
vp^{\prime}v^{-1}=p^{\prime}
,
vpv^{-1}=p
,
hph^{-1}=p^{\prime}
, and

hvh^{-1}=v
, we obtain

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(h,p))

\displaystyle=\omega(p^{\prime},v,h)\,\omega(v,p^{\prime},h)^{-1}\,\omega(v,h,p),

(314)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}((h,p),(v,p))

\displaystyle=\omega(p^{\prime},h,v)\,\omega(h,p,v)^{-1}\,\omega(h,v,p).

(315)

Therefore

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(h,p))}{\sigma_{k}((h,p),(v,p))}=\frac{\omega(p^{\prime},v,h)\,\omega(v,h,p)\,\omega(h,p,v)}{\omega(v,p^{\prime},h)\,\omega(p^{\prime},h,v)\,\omega(h,v,p)}.

(316)

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}((h,v),(p,v))

\displaystyle=\omega(v,h,p)\,\omega(h,v,p)^{-1}\,\omega(h,p,v),

(317)

\displaystyle\sigma_{k}((p^{\prime},v),(h,v))

\displaystyle=\omega(v,p^{\prime},h)\,\omega(p^{\prime},v,h)^{-1}\,\omega(p^{\prime},h,v).

(318)

Hence

\frac{\sigma_{k}((h,v),(p,v))}{\sigma_{k}((p^{\prime},v),(h,v))}=\frac{\omega(p^{\prime},v,h)\,\omega(v,h,p)\,\omega(h,p,v)}{\omega(v,p^{\prime},h)\,\omega(p^{\prime},h,v)\,\omega(h,v,p)}.

(319)

Comparing (
316
) and (
319
) gives

\frac{\sigma_{k}((v,p^{\prime}),(h,p))}{\sigma_{k}((h,p),(v,p))}=\frac{\sigma_{k}((h,v),(p,v))}{\sigma_{k}((p^{\prime},v),(h,v))}.

(320)

Combining (
312
) and (
320
), we conclude

\frac{\vartheta_{g}(v;p^{\prime}\to p^{\prime})}{\vartheta_{g}(v;p\to p)}=\frac{\alpha_{v}(h,p)}{\alpha_{v}(p^{\prime},h)}\,.

(321)
