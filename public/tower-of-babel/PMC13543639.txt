Kinetics, mechanistic, toxicological and economical investigations of para-chlorophenol degradation by UV/persulfate and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ processes
Authors: Tayyeba Ali, Talha Sharif, Shabir Ahmad, Sumaira Wilayat, Muhammad Ateeq, Javed Ali Khan, Hazratullah Paktin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Para-chlorophenol (4-chlorophenol, 4-CP) is a member of the toxic chlorinated phenolic compounds. It is broadly used in preservatives, and as a precursor for some herbicides and fungicides and has been detected in natural aquatic environments. This work examined the photochemical degradation of 4-CP by ultraviolet light (UV), UV/persulfate (UV/PS) and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ (UV/PS/Fe2+) methods. Under UV light irradiation (UV-C, 254 nm), the removal efficiency of 4-CP (20 mg/L) was 44.7, 58.8 and 66.8% at pH 3, 6 and 11, respectively, after 25 min of irradiation. The addition of 0.5 mM PS to UV enhanced the removal efficiency of 4-CP to 90.2% (at pH 3) and 100% (at pH 6 and 11), at the same treatment time. However, complete removal of 4-CP occurs in UV/persulfate/Fe2+ process, employing Fe2+ = 0.3 mg/L, PS = 0.5 mM and 4-CP = 20 mg/L, at pH 3, after 25 min of treatment. Additionally, the degradation efficacy was adversely affected by humic acid (HA) and inorganic ions. Similarly, the TOC removal was 65.8 and 86.1%, for UV/PS and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ techniques, after 120 min. Moreover, the contribution of SO4•− and HO• was investigated by scavenging study. Additionally, GC-MS was used to detect the degradation products (DPs) of 4-CP. Finally, ECOSAR program was used to evaluate the toxicity of 4-CP and its DPs. Nonetheless, the present study suggests that UV/PS and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ techniques can effectively eliminate chlorophenols from aquatic environments.Graphical Abstract

Introduction

Industrialization and urbanization are essential for the modern world. The use of several forms of energy for industrialization leads in the disposal of a considerable amount of waste into the environment. This causes various issues with public health, as well as greenhouse gas emissions and water pollution [1, 2]. Water pollution has become a serious issue as a result of large amounts of domestic sewages and industrial wastes being released into the water bodies [3, 4]. Wastewater coming from various industries such as pesticide, pharmaceuticals, and petrochemical processing includes huge amounts of organic compounds like aromatic compounds, chlorinated hydrocarbons, textile dyes, and phenolic compounds [5].

Chlorophenols, a class of phenolic compounds, had been categorized as priority hazardous contaminants by the United States Environmental Protection Agency and the European Commission Decision 2455/2001/EC [6, 7]. They are widely used for a variety of purposes, including preservatives (in paint, wood and dye industries) and as precursors in several fungicides, herbicides and dyes [8]. A starting precursor for the synthesis of 2-benzyl-4-chlorophenol germicides is 4-chlorophenol. It is also utilized in the manufacturing of analgesics containing acetophenetidin. The main application for 2,4-DCP is the synthesis of 4-dichlorophenoxy acetic acid, a common pesticide that is widely utilized in agricultural production [9]. These are the building blocks for extremely hazardous chemicals known as polychlorodibenzofurans and polychlorophenoxyphenols [9]. It is widely used in the production of antibacterial and other anti-helminthic medications, including triclosan [10]. Chlorophenols (CPs) are characterized as resistant and hardly biodegradable, that represent a threat to environmental integrity and human health [11].

4-CP, a toxic chlorinated organic pollutant, continually enters to the environment, producing severe pollution and devastation [12, 13]. They have received a great deal of interest due to their toxic nature for living organisms and potential to accumulate biologically in humans as well as animals [14]. Even at low concentrations of 4-CP (3–4 mg/L), results in DNA damage, bioaccumulation, and mortality in aquatic animals [15]. The presence of 4-CP in the aquatic system at concentrations ranging from 0.005 to 20 µg/L has a chronic effect on humans [16]. Therefore, it is necessary to treat 4-CP contaminated wastewater to protect the environment from its negative effects [17].

A variety of traditional techniques are used for the elimination of chlorophenols from wastewater, including the coagulation, activated sludge process, precipitation, ion exchange, membrane filtration, and biological treatment. These traditional methods are proficient in reducing wastewater with high concentrations of organic chemicals; however, they prove inadequate for wastewater at lower pollutants concentrations. While each conventional technique possesses its own merits and drawbacks, a significant disadvantage is the generation of secondary pollutants. Thus, advance oxidation procedures arise as efficient methods for the elimination of organic contaminants from wastewater [18, 19].

Advanced oxidation processes (AOPs) are highly effective methods which can degrade organic compounds [20]. AOPs employ highly reactive oxidizing agents including hydroxyl radicals (HO•) and sulfate radicals (SO4•−) to eradicate organic contaminants from aquatic environments [21]. They are distinguished by their ease of implementation, high efficiency, environmental compatibility, and ability to oxidize a wide range of pollutants. Moreover, they have been frequently used in the degradation of refractory contaminant [22]. AOPs based on sulphate radicals or hydroxyl radicals have been demonstrated in studies for effective degradation of organic pollutants [23–29]. However, hydroxyl-radical based AOPs may be limited by their short half-life (< 1 µs). There has been increasing interest in sulfate radical-based AOPs due to an increasing variety of applications documented in the literature. SO4•− offers numerous distinct advantages, including a greater redox potential (E0 = 2.5 to 3.1 V versus standard hydrogen electrode (SHE)), an expanded working pH range (2 − 8), and a considerably extended half-life (30 to 40 µs) compared to HO• [30, 31]. Therefore, for the treatment of contaminated water, SR-AOPs are regarded to be the most promising advanced oxidation process [32].

The primary goal of this work was to explore the efficacy of sulphate radical-based advanced oxidation procedures, such as UV, UV/PS, and photo-Fenton like techniques, for the removal of 4-CP. The impact of initial 4-CP, PS, and Ferrous ion concentrations have been studied. Experiments at various pH levels were also carried out in order to determine the optimum pH for 4-CP removal. Moreover, the impact of inorganic ions on removal of 4-CP was studied. Additionally, GC–MS was used to identify the degradation products (DPs) of 4-CP. Finally, the toxicity of DPs was assessed by ECOSAR program.

Experimental

Chemicals and reagents

4-Chlorophenol, sodium persulfate (Na2S2O8), HCl and NaOH were supplied by Sigma-Aldrich, Darmstadt, Germany. Ferrous sulfate (FeSO4.7H2O), methanol, Tertiary-butyl alcohol (t-BuOH), NaHCO3, Na2CO3, NaCl, and humic acid (HA) were purchased from Merck, Darmstadt, Germany. All reagents and compounds were of analytical grade and utilized without further purification.

Experimental procedures

An aqueous solution with a 100 mg/L was prepared by mixing an appropriate amount of 4-CP with distilled water. The reactions were conducted at ambient temperature. The pH of the solution was adjusted/maintained using either 1 M HCl or 1 M NaOH. The source of irradiation was a 35-watt Philips, Holland-made UV-C lamp (254 nm). After a certain period of time, the samples were taken for quantification of 4-CP.

Analytical methods

High performance liquid chromatography (Agilent 1100 series) was used for quantification of 4-chlorophenol, detector wavelength set at 280 nm. The detail of the procedure is given somewhere else [33]. The DPs were determined by analyzing the irradiated samples using gas chromatography-mass spectrometer (GC Agilent-8890, MS Agilent-5977). The procedure is described in detail by Iqbal et al. [34]. The Shimadzu VCSH-ASI TOC analyzer was employed to quantify the total organic carbon (TOC). The toxicity of 4-CP and its DPs was assessed using the ECOSAR software. This is an appealing program utilized for assessing the chronic and acute toxicity of chemical substances. The acute toxicities were evaluated in terms of LC50 and EC50 values of 4-CP and its DPs [21].

Removal of 4-CP by UV/persulfate and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ techniques

Impact of PS

The PS dosage plays a substantial role for the formation of SO4•− and the subsequent degradation of 4-chlorophenol. To ascertain the role of PS in 4-chorophenol degradation using UV/PS process, various dosages of PS ranging from 0.2 to 1 mM were investigated at pH 6.0, keeping the [4-CP]0 constant at 20 mg/L. The degradation efficacy of 4-CP linearly increases with rise in PS concentration. The degradation of 4-CP was found to be 89.8% using 0.2 mM PS. Similarly, complete removal of 4-CP occurs employing 0.5 and 1 mM PS, after 25 min of irradiation (Fig. 2a). However, the kobs was 0.0669, 0.0876 and 0.1265 min− 1, for 0.2, 0.5 and 1 mM PS, accordingly. Impact of [4-CP]0

It is particularly crucial to explore how different initial concentrations of target contaminant affect the efficacy of a water treatment technology in eliminating them, as these contaminants vary in concentration in natural water. It will contribute to the development of viable water treatment technology for naturally contaminated water under a variety of conditions [21]. Therefore, the influence of initial 4-CP concentration on the removal efficacy of 4-CP by UV/PS and photo-Fenton like techniques were determined by conducting a series of experiments.

In UV/PS process, the degradation efficiency was 100% for 10 and 20 mg/L 4-CP, while 86.5% for 30 mg/L 4-CP, respectively, using PS = 0.5 mM and pH = 6, after 25 min of irradiation (Fig. 3a). The pseudo-first-order rate constant (kobs) was 0.1215, 0.0876, and 0.0617 min− 1 at [4-CP]0 = 10, 20, and 30 mg/L, accordingly.

However, in photo-Fenton like process, the effect of same starting concentration of 4-CP was evaluated as in UV/PS process i.e., 10, 20, and 30 mg/L (Fig. 3b). The degradation efficiency of 4-CP was 100% for 10 and 20 mg/L. However, the percent removal of 4-CP was 92.7% for 30 mg/L, after 25 min of reaction time. The other experimental conditions were kept constant at PS = 0.5 mM and pH = 3. The kobs for 10, 20 and 30 mg/L of 4-CP was 0.1645, 0.113 and 0.0704 min− 1, respectively. Since both of these processes rely on SO4•−, the decrease in 4-CP degradation with increasing 4-CP concentrations can be explained by the same reasons. Furthermore, the concentration of degradation products increases with the rising concentration of 4-CP; hence, SO4•− is likely to interact with degradation products instead of 4-CP, resulting in decline in degradation efficiency of 4-CP.

The removal rate increases with rise in 4-CP concentration. As the concentration of 4-CP increases, a greater number of 4-CP molecules are subjected to SO4•−, hence enhancing the probability of collisions between SO4•− and 4-CP molecules, leading to an increased degradation rate of 4-CP per unit time [21]. The degradation rate for 10, 20 and 30 mg/L of 4-CP was 0.625, 0.983 and 1.225 mg/L/min, for UV/persulfate technique (Table 1). Moreover, for UV/persulfate/Fe2+ technique the degradation rate was 0.708, 1.100 and 1.335 mg/L/min, respectively, for 10, 20 and 30 mg/L of 4-CP, (Table 1).

Impact of [Fe2+]0 in UV/PS/Fe2+ technique

High amounts of Fe²⁺ can precipitate, leading to additional secondary treatment and increased operational costs for large-scale water treatment systems [40]. Therefore, the impact of initial [Fe2+]0 was evaluated for 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mg/L. The % degradation of 4-CP was 94.0% for 0.1 mg/L of Fe2+ and 100% for both 0.3 and 0.5 mg/L of Fe2+, employing 4-CP = 20 mg/L and pH = 3, after 25 min of irradiation (Fig. 4). Similarly, the observed rate constant for 0.1, 0.3 and 0.5 mg/L of Fe2+ was 0.0891, 0.113 and 0.1354 min− 1, respectively. The % degradation of 4-CP increases as Fe2+ concentration rises because Fe2+ catalytically activates PS which produce SO4•− (reaction (9)) [39].

Influence of pH

One of the most vital parameters to assess the effectiveness of AOPs for pollutants degradation is the solution’s pH, which might have impact on the rate of chemical reactions by producing radicals [49]. The stability of oxidants, the production and redox potential of SO4•− and •OH, and kind of iron species in solution during the advanced oxidation process are all directly impacted by pH [50, 51]. To examine the influence of pH on the degradation efficiency of 4-CP, experiments were performed at pH 3.0, 6.0, and 11.0 for the UV/persulfate technique, and pH 3 and 6 for the UV/persulfate/Fe2+ technique. The experiment at pH 11 was not conducted in the case of UV/persulfate/Fe2+. The reason for this is that the Fe2+ converted into ferric oxyhydroxide species at alkaline pH, which are unable to efficiently activate PS [50]. The removal efficacy of 4-CP was 90.3% at pH 3, while complete removal of 4-CP occurs at pH 6 and 11, using 4-CP (20 mg/L), PS (0.5 mM), after 25 min (Fig. 5a). Similarly, as the pH increases from 3 to 6 the kobs increases from 0.0634 to 0.0876 min− 1. However, the observed rate constant increases to 0.0971 as pH increases to 11. The increase in degradation of 4-CP at alkaline pH is due to the efficient absorption of UV light in its anionic state [36]. Similarly, at alkaline pH, PS can be activated by both base and UV, which produce more SO4•− (reactions (10)–(12)) [28]. TOC analysis

The main goal of water treatment technology is to remove or reduce the harmful organic compounds in contaminated water. Therefore, it is crucial to assess the effectiveness of a specific water treatment technique in diminishing the total organic carbon (TOC) of a certain contaminant [61]. The Elimination of TOC from 4-CP solution using UV/persulfate and photo-Fenton like systems was investigated. The removal of the TOC required more time than the total removal of the 4-CP. The removal of TOC was investigated at pH 6.0 for the UV/PS technique to enhance its applicability to natural water conditions. Similarly, the removal of TOC was investigated at pH 3.0 for the UV/PS/Fe2+ method, as Fe2+ demonstrates enhanced catalytic activity in acidic conditions. The achieved TOC removal of 4-CP was 65.8 and 86.1% for the UV/PS and UV/PS/Fe2+ procedures, respectively, after 120 min (Fig. 7). The enhanced TOC removal of 4-CP in the photo-Fenton like technique is attributable to the triggering of PS via UV light and Fe2+, leading to the generation of higher number of SO4•− (reactions (7) and (9)).

Relative contribution of reactive radicals

Radical quenching experiments were conducted to evaluate the relative contributions of reactive species in the UV/PS process. Tertiary-butyl alcohol (t-BuOH) was employed as a selective HO• scavenger, whereas methanol was used to quench both SO4•− and HO• [65]. Under the experimental conditions of [4-CP]0 = 20 mg L⁻1, PS = 0.5 mM, pH = 6, the degradation efficiency of 4-CP decreased from 100 to 94.3% upon the addition of 10 mM t-BuOH after 25 min of UV irradiation (Fig. 8). This 5.7% reduction in removal efficiency can be attributed to the scavenging of HO• by t-BuOH. Similarly, the addition of 10 mM methanol reduced the degradation efficiency from 100 to 63.5% after 25 min of irradiation, corresponding to a 36.5% decrease, indicating major contribution of SO4•− in the degradation of 4-CP in UV/PS process (Fig. 8). Moreover, the contribution of SO4•− is 30.6%, i.e., HO• − SO4•− (94.3 − 63.5 = 30.6%). For comparison, direct photolysis achieved 58.8% degradation of 4-CP at 25 min. Therefore, the degradation efficiency of 4-CP in the methanol/UV/PS system was 4.7% higher (63.5–58.8 = 4.7%) than that observed under direct photolysis. This additional removal may be attributed to the contribution of singlet oxygen (1O2) or the presence of residual reactive radical species that persisted despite the addition of methanol.

The effectiveness of the UV/PS and UV/PS/Fe2+ techniques were evaluated in terms of economic cost. The total cost for UV/PS and UV/PS/Fe2+ processes were 0.7583 and 0.7971 $ m− 3. Degradation products

The assessment of the target pollutant’s removal is frequently employed to evaluate the treatment process’s efficiency; however, the generation of degradation products during this process presents significant challenges. These DPs exhibit greater resistance to breakdown and elevate the toxicity levels of treated water. Therefore, it is essential to take into account information regarding the DPs of target pollutants [67].

The breakdown of 4-chlorophenol results in the production of hydroquinone, 1,4-benzoquinone, and 4-chlorocatechol. Prior studies indicate that electron transfer and abstraction of hydrogen are presumed to be the initial steps in the SO4•− attack during the oxidation of 4-chlorophenol [68]. A sulphate radical-induced attack on a benzene ring results in the generation of carbon-centered radicals through transfer of electrons from the organic component to the sulphate radicals [69]. The hydrolysis of the radical cation generated post-sulfate elimination leads to the production of hydroxylated radical products, which subsequently react with O2 to yield the stable intermediates hydroquinone and 4-chlorocatechol. Similarly, 1,4-Benzoquinone is subsequently generated through hydrogen abstraction from hydroquinone [33]. Further, these aromatic ring DPs were oxidized and convert into open chain oxalic and acetic acid. At the end, complete mineralization of 4-CP occur [70] as shown in Scheme 1.

Toxicity investigation

The main purpose of a water treatment technology is to produce pure, clean water devoid of hazardous pollutants. Therefore, the toxicity of the treated solution should be evaluated before recommending a water treatment system for practical implementation. Herein, the toxicity of 4-CP and its degradation products towards three aquatic organisms including fish, daphnia and green algae were assessed using ECOSAR program (Table 5).

The criteria and guidelines of Chinese hazard evaluation for new chemical compounds/substances (HJ/T154-2004) were applied to assess the chronic toxicities of the compounds of interest. These criteria were as follows: ChV greater than 10 (not harmful), 1 < ChV < 10 (harmful), 0.1 < ChV < 1 (toxic), and ChV less than 0.1 (extremely toxic). On the other hand, the acute toxicities were examined in compliance with the European Union standards [71]. According to these recommendations, substances with higher acute and chronic toxicities have lower ChV, LC50, and EC50 values. Fortunately, the acute and chronic toxicities of the identified DPs was found to be less than the parent 4-CP. As a result, the UV/PS process is an efficient water treatment system when used for purification of chlorophenols containing water.

Conclusion

This study investigated the removal of 4-CP by three different processes such as sole UV, UV/persulfate, and photo-Fenton like techniques. The removal efficacy in UV alone of 4-CP was 44.7, 58.8 and 66.8% degradation of 4-CP (20 mg/L), at pH 3, 6, and 11, after 25 min of treatment. The addition of 0.5 mM PS to UV enhances the removal of 4-CP to 90.2% (pH 3) and 100% (pH 6 and 11), respectively after 25 min. Furthermore, complete removal of 4-CP occurs in photo-Fenton like process using Fe2+ = 0.3 mg/L, PS = 0.5 mM, 4-CP = 20 mg/L, at pH 3, after 25 min of irradiation. Additionally, the degradation efficacy was adversely affected by humic acid (HA) and inorganic ions. The degradation of 4-CP decreases from 100% to 94.8, 84.6, 81.8 and 70.3% in presence of chloride, carbonate, bicarbonate and HA. Similarly, the TOC removal of 4-CP was 65.8 and 86.1%, in UV/persulfate and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ techniques, after 120 min. However, the DPs were determined by GC – MS. Moreover, the contribution of SO4•− and HO• was investigated by scavenging study. Finally, ECOSAR program was used to assess the toxicity of 4-CP and its detected DPs against fish, daphnia and green algae. The developed UV/persulfate and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ processes show promising potential for the treatment of industrial and municipal wastewater, including complex effluents containing multiple organic pollutants. These processes can be applied to real water matrices, offering efficient degradation under optimized conditions. However, limitations such as sensitivity to water matrix components, the need for careful optimization of reagent dosages, and UV penetration in large-scale systems should be considered for practical implementation. Nevertheless, this work suggests that the UV/persulfate and UV/persulfate/Fe2+ processes can be efficient methods for removing of 4-CP from aquatic compartments.
