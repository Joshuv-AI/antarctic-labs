energy efficiency.

Guidelines for safe
battery operation and
prolonging lifetime

 Battery lifetime – Battery
 system safety
Operating within safe voltage limits is critical for maintaining performance and safety
• Good battery quality, battery pack design, and battery pack control system make for
  very safe systems with predictable performance
• If any of the individual batteries, the pack design, or the pack control system are bad,
  battery systems may be dangerous

Fires in electric bicycles or scooters, which have low quality battery systems, are much
more common than electric vehicle fires.

Degradation generally makes batteries less reliable, especially when performing a
demanding application.

                                                                            National Laboratory of the Rockies | 23

 Battery lifetime –
 Prolonging battery life
• Avoid keeping batteries fully charged
 ◦ Being fully charged ≈ being very hot
 ◦ Battery failure is more dangerous when batteries are fully charged
• Keep battery temperature below 40 °C
 ◦ Vehicles parked in the sun may easily reach 40 °C at air temperatures of 30 °C
 ◦ Battery systems in hot climates may not last as long as in those in temperature
 climates
• For NMC-Gr, NCA-Gr, … avoid high depth-of-discharge
 ◦ LFP-Gr doesn’t care about depth-of-discharge, every other battery type does

 System balance
Battery cells have variation in performance, and the performance of a system
made up of many cells is limited by the ‘weakest link’. This variation can be
‘real’, i.e., capacity variability, or due to SOC variability across the system even
if all cells are identical.

 Real world problems –
 Corrosion
 Anytime metals are exposed to an electrochemical potential (due to
the voltage of the battery, or when two dissimilar metals are joined),
corrosion may occur in the presence of humidity.
 Battery corrosion is a huge issue for Lead-acid batteries, as they
generate gas, and often expel acidic electrolyte onto their terminals when
this gas is vented. Lithium-ion batteries are completely sealed, and should
never vent gas or electrolyte fluid, and thus are less likely to suffer
significantly from corrosion.

Source: Volta Foundation 2022

                                                              National Laboratory of the Rockies | 31

 Real world problems –
 Corrosion
 Atmospheric humidity can still cause corrosion, as any condensation will react with atmospheric CO2 to
from small concentrations of carbonic acid, creating a weakly acidic electrolyte solution that will lead to
corrosion. This corrosion is worse if the humid air contains salt from sea spray.
 Humidity can be forced into a battery pack by natural causes, like temperature gradients (hot humid air
contacting a battery that is colder) or pressure gradients (a rapid increase in atmospheric pressure, like during
a storm). This humidity can lead to corrosion even if the battery is not cycling, because the metal is at the
same voltage as the battery even if not current is flowing. Higher voltage will accelerate any corrosion
processes, so lower voltage batteries should be more resistant to corrosion – low voltage (< 150 V) battery
systems can still provide power at high voltages (>1000 V) using DC-DC converters.
 Corrosion is prevented mainly by good design and by avoiding exposing the battery to humid external
air. For instance, liquid cooling systems will not pass external air over the batteries, anti-corrosion coatings can
be applied to exposed metals, or batteries could be heated to ensure they are hotter than the dew point to
guarantee zero condensation. If near the ocean, battery systems should be sited using local knowledge of
wind and fog patterns so that the battery system is not exposed to salty humid air.

Source: Volta Foundation 2022

Final thoughts

 Final thoughts
• Keep batteries in their ‘happy place’: Not too hot (> 55 °C) or too cold (< 0 °C), avoid
  humidity
• To extend lifetime, keep SOC low when possible
• Most system failures come from the system, not the cells: monitor variability of SOC,
  capacity, and temperature across the system to check for outliers
 ◦ Outliers can get overcharged or overdischarged, leading to catastrophic failure
• Most system failures occur during the first few years. Be especially vigilant at the
  beginning!

If battery energy storage systems are engineered well, have good software controls and
monitoring, and are maintained, systems with today’s battery technology may last 20+
years.
