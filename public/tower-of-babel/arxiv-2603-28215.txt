Ribbon categories from ind-exact algebras: simple current case


Abstract
. We give criteria for when finitely generated local modules over a commutative algebra A A in the ind-completion 𝒞 ^ \widehat{\mathcal{C}} of a braided tensor category 𝒞 \mathcal{C} inherit the structure of a (rigid, braided, ribbon) tensor category. We then apply this to simple current algebras A = ⨁ g ∈ Γ E g A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g} , where Γ \Gamma is a subgroup of invertible objects in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} . Using a description of simple A A -modules, we verify the required hypotheses for this class of algebras and deduce rigidity, braided, ribbon, and non-degeneracy properties for their finitely generated local modules. As applications, we construct examples of ribbon tensor categories from quantum supergroup categories for unrolled 𝔤 ​ 𝔩 ​ ( 1 | 1 ) \mathfrak{gl}(1|1) .



1.
Introduction

Simple currents (i.e. invertible objects) play a central role in constructions and classification problems in braided tensor categories arising in representation theory and conformal field theory. Given a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
and a subgroup
\Gamma
of its group of isomorphism classes of invertible objects, the object
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
(where
E_{g}\in\mathcal{C}
denotes an invertible object corresponding to
g\in\Gamma
) has a structure of an algebra in the ind-completion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
under an appropriate cocycle condition. We call an algebra of this type a
simple current algebra
(see Definition
2.11
for the precise definition). The goal of this paper is to give workable criteria ensuring that suitable subcategories of modules over a commutative algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
inherit the structure of ribbon tensor category from
\mathcal{C}
and to establish that commutative simple current algebras satisfy these criteria under mild assumptions.

1.1.
Main results

Throughout this paper, we work over an algebraically closed field
\Bbbk
.
The paper has two parts.
First, we set up a general framework for commutative algebras
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, where
\mathcal{C}
is a braided tensor category. A right
A
-module is said to be
finitely generated
if it is a quotient of the free
A
-module
X\otimes A
for some object
X\in\mathcal{C}
. Based on this definition, one can define the category
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
of finitely generated
A
-modules and its subcategory
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
of finitely generated local modules.

{namedtheorem}

[A]
Let
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
be a haploid commutative algebra.

(a)

If
A
is Artinian, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
are abelian categories of finite length.

(b)

If moreover
A
is ind-exact, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is tensor and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is braided tensor.

(c)

If in addition
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon and
A
is Frobenius with
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is ribbon.

See Definitions
3.3
and
3.8
for the
definitions of Artinian and ind-exact algebras, respectively. The term
Frobenius algebra is used in an extended sense as defined in
Definition
3.14
, and in particular the algebra
A

in (c) may not be a rigid object in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Our discussion of Artinian and ind-exact algebras builds upon
[
CEO24
]
and our prior work
[
SY24
]
. Part (c) is the main new contribution in this theorem: we introduce ‘infinite’ Frobenius algebras and use this property to obtain the ribbon structure of
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Second, we discuss a practical way of checking ind-exactness of algebras. Etingof and Penneys proved that in an abelian braided monoidal category with right exact tensor product, if all the simple objects are rigid, then all finite length objects are rigid
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
. In Appendix
A
, we prove a non-braided generalization of this result (Theorem
A.2
). Using this, we obtain the following result (Theorem
3.9
):

{namedtheorem}

[B]
Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tensor category and
A
an Artinian central commutative algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Then,
A
is ind-exact if and only if every simple
A
-module is rigid.

Third, and this is the main contribution of the paper: we prove that
simple current algebras
(Definition
2.11
) satisfy these hypotheses under explicit and checkable conditions, and we derive concrete consequences for their module categories. For a simple current algebra
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
, we first classify simple
A
-modules. We then verify the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
for this class: in particular,
A
is always Artinian (Proposition
4.25
) and Frobenius (Theorem
4.27
). If
A
is central commutative and char
(\Bbbk)=0
, we use the explicit description of simple
A
-modules to show that they are rigid. By Theorem
1.1
, this establishes that
A
is ind-exact. Section
4
also gives a general categorical non-degeneracy criterion for the category
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
.

{namedtheorem}

[C]
Suppose that char
(\Bbbk)=0
. Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of the group of isomorphism classes of invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}
such that
c_{E_{g},E_{g}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{g}\otimes E_{g}}
for all
g\in\Gamma
. Then the object
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
has a unique (up to isomorphism) structure of commutative simple current algebra. For the commutative simple current algebra
A
, we have:

(a)

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is a tensor category and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a braided tensor category.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon and
\theta_{E_{g}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{g}}
for all
g\in\Gamma
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is ribbon.

Now suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is Frobenius (i.e., it has enough projectives). Then,

(c)

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
are Frobenius.

(d)

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is finite if and only if
\Irr(\mathcal{C})/\Gamma
is finite, and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is braided finite if and only if
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma})/\Gamma
is finite. Here,
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
is the full subcategory

\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}:=\{X\in\mathcal{C}|c_{E_{g},X}c_{X,E_{g}}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes E_{g}}\ \forall\ g\in\Gamma\}.

(e)

If the simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
that trivially double braid with all other simples in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
are precisely
E_{g}
for
g\in\Gamma
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is non-degenerate.

One may call a ribbon tensor category with trivial Müger center a
modular tensor category
. Then Theorem
1.1
gives sufficient conditions that a commutative simple current algebra
A
must satisfy to ensure that the category
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is modular.

1.2.
VOA motivation and applications

The study of simple current extensions originated in conformal field theory with the work of Schellekens and Yankielowicz
[
SY89
]
on modular invariants generated by integer-spin simple currents. In vertex operator algebra theory, Dong, Li, and Mason
[
DLM96
]
gave an early treatment of extensions by simple currents. In the rational tensor-categorical setting, the algebra-object and local-module viewpoint was developed by Kirillov–Ostrik
[
KJO02
]
and by Fuchs, Runkel, and Schweigert
[
FRS04
]
. Later, Huang, Kirillov, and Lepowsky
[
HKJL15
]
showed that, under suitable hypotheses, extensions of a VOA
V
are equivalent to commutative associative algebra objects with trivial twist in the braided tensor category of
V
-modules. In this framework, the extended VOA
W
corresponds to an algebra object
A
in
\mathcal{C}
(or
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
), and the category of
W
-modules is described by the category of local
A
-modules; see
[
HKJL15
,
CKM24
]
. If the underlying object of the algebra is a direct sum of invertible objects, it is called a simple current extension (this is why we call an algebra of the form
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
a simple current algebra).

Our motivation comes from studying simple current extensions of logarithmic VOAs. The representation categories of such VOAs are typically not semisimple and not finite, and moreover, the simple current extensions are typically not objects of the original category, but rather lie in the ind-completion.
There are many papers which discuss rigid, ribbon structures on categories of modules over simple current extensions in specific examples; see, for example

[
AR18
,
CMY22a
,
CR22
,
CMY22b
]
.

Our results can be viewed as a rigidity inheritance statement for simple current extensions of vertex operator
algebras.
Let
V
be a VOA such that a suitable category
\mathcal{C}
of
V
-modules carries a braided tensor category structure.
Assume moreover that
\mathcal{C}
is rigid, and let
V\subset W
be a simple current extension.
In the tensor-categorical formulation, the extension corresponds to a commutative algebra object

A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, and
W
-modules are described by local
A
-modules in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

[
HKJL15
,
CKM24
]
.
Under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, we show that the category
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is rigid.
Thus
W
also admits a rigid braided tensor category of representations.
This is useful because rigidity is often one of the hardest properties to establish in vertex tensor category
theory.

1.3.
Examples

We also obtain examples of (braided, ribbon) tensor categories by applying our criteria to commutative simple current algebras in two families of non-semisimple ribbon categories: the categories of integral weight modules for
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})

[
Rup22
]
and
U^{E}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(1|1))

[
GY25
]
. In these settings, one can sum invertibles over suitable subgroups to obtain commutative simple current algebras, and our criteria produce new categories of local modules. In the unrolled quantum group setting, this specializes to the explicit lattice criterion of Creutzig–Rupert
[
CR22
]
.

Note that the weight module categories of
U^{H}_{q}(\mathfrak{g})
and
U^{E}_{q}(\mathfrak{gl}(1|1))
are relative modular categories in the sense of
[
CGPM14
]
. It would be interesting to examine whether the relative modular structure descends to the local module category obtained from our results.

1.4.
Organization

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
recalls basic terminology on tensor categories and centers. Section
3
develops a general framework for constructing (braided, ribbon) tensor categories from commutative algebras in ind-completions; the main criteria are summarized in Theorem
3.1
. Section
4
applies this framework to simple current algebras. Section
5
records concrete examples from the unrolled quantum groups of
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
. Appendix
A
contains a non-braided variant of the Etingof–Penneys
[
EP26
]
lemma used for rigidity arguments.

2.
Background

2.1.
Notations

Throughout the paper,
\Bbbk
will denote an algebraically closed field. We will write
\mathbf{vec}
to denote the category of finite-dimensional
\Bbbk
-vector spaces and
\mathbf{Vec}
to denote the category of all
\Bbbk
-vector spaces.

Given a category
\mathcal{C}
, we write
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}
to denote the opposite category of
\mathcal{C}
.
Unless otherwise noted, the monoidal product and the unit object of a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
are denoted by
\otimes:\mathcal{C}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
and
\mathbbm{1}\in\mathcal{C}
, respectively.

\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
will denote the category
\mathcal{C}
with the opposite monoidal product. For a braided category
\mathcal{C}
with braiding
c
, we use the notation
\overline{\mathcal{C}}
to denote
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{rev}}
with reversed braiding
\overline{c}_{X,Y}=c_{Y,X}^{-1}
.

Regarding the terminology related to duality, we follow the conventions used in
[
EGNO16
]
.
An object of a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is
left rigid
and
right rigid
if it has a left and a right dual object, respectively. A
rigid
object is a left and right rigid object. We say that
\mathcal{C}
is
rigid
if every object of
\mathcal{C}
is rigid. Given a left rigid object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, we denote by

(X^{*},\ \mathrm{ev}_{X}:X^{*}\otimes X\to\mathbbm{1},\ \mathrm{coev}_{X}:\mathbbm{1}\to X\otimes X^{*})

the left dual object of
X
. We call
\mathrm{ev}_{X}
and
\mathrm{coev}_{X}
the
evaluation
and the
coevaluation
for
X
, respectively. An (object-part of) a right dual object of
X
is denoted by
{}^{*}\!X
.

2.2.
Tensor categories

A
locally finite abelian category
is an essentially small
\Bbbk
-linear abelian category where every object is of finite length and every Hom space is finite-dimensional. A
tensor category
is a locally finite abelian category endowed with a structure of a rigid monoidal category such that the monoidal product is
\Bbbk
-bilinear and the unit is simple
[
EGNO16
]
. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tensor category. We record the following result for later use.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
Del02
, Proposition 1.1]
)
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is an abelian
\Bbbk
-linear monoidal category with
\End_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1})\cong\Bbbk
whose objects have finite length. If
\mathcal{C}
is rigid, then it is Hom-finite.

A tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
Frobenius
if, in addition, every simple object of
\mathcal{C}
has an injective hull
[
ACE15
]
. Several equivalent conditions for
\mathcal{C}
to be Frobenius were given in
[
SS23
]
.

2.3.
Braided tensor categories

2.3.1.
Müger center and nondegeneracy

Given a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
and a monoidal subcategory
\mathcal{D}
, the
centralizer
of
\mathcal{D}
in
\mathcal{C}
is defined as:

\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{C})=\{X\in\mathcal{C}\mid c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes Y},\,\forall\;Y\in\mathcal{D}\}.

The
Müger center
of
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}:=\mathcal{Z}_{2}(\mathcal{C}\subset\mathcal{C})
. We call
\mathcal{C}

non-degenerate
if
\mathcal{C}
is a tensor category and its Müger center is equivalent to
\mathbf{vec}
.

We will use the following criteria for nondegeneracy.

Lemma 2.2
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{char}(\Bbbk)=0
. Then a braided Frobenius tensor category
\mathcal{C}
is non-degenerate if and only if the only simple object of the Müger center of
\mathcal{C}
is
\mathbbm{1}
up to isomorphism.

Proof 2.3
.

Since
\mathcal{C}
is Frobenius, the projective cover of
\mathbbm{1}
exists in
\mathcal{C}
.
To show the ‘if’ part, we note that
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1})=0
under our assumption (the proof in the finite case
[
EGNO16
, Theorem 4.4.1]
requires that a projective cover of
\mathbbm{1}
exists in
\mathcal{C}
but does not use the finiteness of
\mathcal{C}
). If
\mathbbm{1}
is the only simple object of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
up to isomorphism, every object of
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is a finite direct sum of
\mathbbm{1}
since
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1},\mathbbm{1})=0
, and therefore
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
is equivalent to
\mathbf{vec}
. The converse is trivial.

2.3.2.
Ribbon tensor categories

A
ribbon tensor category
is a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
equipped with a
twist
, i.e. a natural automorphism
\theta=\{\theta_{X}:X\xrightarrow{\sim}X\}_{X\in\mathcal{C}}
satisfying

(1)

\theta_{X\otimes Y}=c_{Y,X}\circ c_{X,Y}\circ(\theta_{X}\otimes\theta_{Y}),\qquad\theta_{X^{*}}=(\theta_{X})^{*}

for all
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
.
One can deduce
\theta_{\mathbbm{1}}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbbm{1}}
from the former equation.

2.4.
Modules in a closed monoidal category

Given algebras
A
and
B
in a monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}
,
\mathcal{C}_{B}
and
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{B}
, the categories of left
A
-modules, right
B
-modules and
A
-
B
-bimodules in
\mathcal{C}
, respectively.

A monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
closed
if for every
X\in\mathcal{C}
the endofunctors
X\otimes-
and
-\otimes X
on
\mathcal{C}
have right adjoints. Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed monoidal category. Given
X\in\mathcal{C}
, we denote by
[X,-]
a right adjoint of
-\otimes X
. The assignment
(X,Y)\mapsto[X,Y]
extends to a functor
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathcal{C}\to\mathcal{C}
, which we call the
right internal Hom functor
. By definition, there is a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W\otimes X,Y)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(W,[X,Y])
for
W,X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
. The
left internal Hom functor
is defined in a similar manner. Since a functor admitting a right adjoint preserves colimits, the endofunctors
X\otimes(-)
and
(-)\otimes X
preserve colimits.

We assume that
\mathcal{C}
admits equalizers and coequalizers. For a right
A
-module
M
and a left
A
-module
N
in
\mathcal{C}
, their tensor product
M\otimes_{A}N
is defined to be the coequalizer of
a^{r}_{M}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{N}
and
\mathrm{id}_{M}\otimes a^{l}_{N}
, where
a^{r}_{M}:M\otimes A\to M
and
a^{l}_{N}:A\otimes N\to N
are the right and the left action of
A
on
M
and
N
, respectively. Since
\mathcal{C}
is closed, one can verify that the category
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{A}
of
A
-bimodules is a monoidal category with monoidal product
\otimes_{A}
and unit
A
. Moreover,
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{A}
is a closed monoidal category. A construction of the internal Hom functor is found in
[
SY24
, §3.2]
.

2.4.1.
Modules over a central algebra

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a closed monoidal category admitting equalizers and coequalizers. We call an algebra
A
in
\mathcal{C}

central commutative
if it is equipped with a half-braiding
\sigma
such that
(A,\sigma)
is a commutative algebra in the Drinfeld center
\mathcal{Z}(\mathcal{C})
. In this case, a right
A
-module can be viewed as a left
A
-module by the action given by
\sigma
, and the category
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is viewed as a full subcategory of
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{A}
. One can verify that
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is closed under
\otimes_{A}
, and therefore
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is a monoidal category.
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is also a closed monoidal category by the same internal Hom functor as
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{A}

[
SY24
, §3.3]
.

2.4.2.
Local modules

Assume that the closed monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is braided and
A
is a commutative algebra in
\mathcal{C}
. Then, as noted above, the category
\mathcal{C}_{A}
is monoidal with the relative tensor product
\otimes_{A}
. However, it is not braided in general. The full subcategory of
\mathcal{C}_{A}
consisting of
local
modules, that is, those
A
-modules
(M,\rho)
for which the action
\rho:M\otimes A\to M
satisfies
\rho\circ c_{A,M}\circ c_{M,A}=\rho
, is braided with the braiding inherited from
\mathcal{C}

[
Par95
]
.
When
\mathcal{C}
admits equalizers, the category
\mathcal{C}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is in fact a closed monoidal category by the same internal Hom functor as
{}_{A}\mathcal{C}_{A}

[
SY24
, §3.3]
.

2.5.
Ind-completions and their subcategories

2.5.1.
Ind-completions

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tensor category. The ind-completion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
of
\mathcal{C}
is the completion of
\mathcal{C}
under filtered colimits
[
KS06
]
. It is a Grothendieck abelian category, and the inclusion
\mathcal{C}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is exact and fully faithful. Moreover,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
admits a monoidal structure extending that of
\mathcal{C}
, and the inclusion is strong monoidal. While
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is not rigid, it is closed monoidal.
When
\mathcal{C}
is braided, the braiding of
\mathcal{C}
naturally extends to
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
When
\mathcal{C}
is moreover a ribbon category, the twist of
\mathcal{C}
naturally extends to a natural isomorphism
\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}\to\mathrm{id}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}
satisfying the first equation in (
1
).

2.5.2.
Category of modules over an algebra in the ind-completion

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a tensor category and
A
an algebra in the ind-completion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
The category
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is identified with the category of algebras over the monad
T:=-\otimes A
.
Since
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is closed monoidal, the functor
T
admits a right adjoint.
Thus
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is an abelian category such that the forgetful functor
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is exact and faithful.

2.5.3.
Various module categories

Let
A
be an algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
We recall the following three finiteness conditions for a right
A
-module.

Definition 2.4
.

A right
A
-module
M
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is said to be
finitely generated
if there exists an epimorphism
X\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow M
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
for some
X\in\mathcal{C}
.

M
is
finitely presented
if there exists an exact sequence
X\otimes A\to Y\otimes A\to M\to 0
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
for some
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
.

M
is
of finite length
if it admits a finite composition series in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
.
We denote by
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
the full subcategories of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
consisting of finitely generated, finitely presented and finite length modules, respectively.

It is obvious that the full subcategory
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is closed under quotients. However,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
need not be closed under kernels in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, so it may fail to be abelian. The full subcategory
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
of finite-length objects is a Serre subcategory of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
; in particular it is abelian.

We recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is monoidal with respect to
\otimes_{A}
when
A
is central commutative. Although we do not know whether
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
are closed under
\otimes_{A}
, one can prove:

Lemma 2.5
.

If
A
is central commutative, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is closed under
\otimes_{A}
.

Proof 2.6
.

If
M_{i}\in\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
(
i=1,2
) is a quotient of
X_{i}\otimes A
(
X_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
), then
M_{1}\otimes_{A}M_{2}
is a quotient of
(X_{1}\otimes A)\otimes_{A}(X_{2}\otimes A)\cong(X_{1}\otimes X_{2})\otimes A
and thus it is finitely generated.

From the definitions, we have
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\subseteq\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. As we show next,
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\subseteq\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
as well.

Lemma 2.7
.

Every finite length
A
-module is finitely generated.

Proof 2.8
.

Let
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
be of finite length. Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be the family of all finitely generated submodules of
M
.
We first claim that
M=\sum_{i\in I}F_{i}
.
Since
M
is an object of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, we can write
M=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}
for some family of subobjects
X_{\lambda}\subset M
with
X_{\lambda}\in\mathcal{C}
. For each
\lambda\in\Lambda
, let
W_{\lambda}
be the image of the subobject
X_{\lambda}\otimes A
under the action
M\otimes A\to M
. Then
W_{\lambda}
is a submodule of
M
, and by construction it is a quotient of the free
A
-module
X_{\lambda}\otimes A
. Hence
W_{\lambda}
is finitely generated. Moreover,
X_{\lambda}\subset W_{\lambda}
by the unit axiom. Therefore

M=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}X_{\lambda}\subset\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda}W_{\lambda}\subset\sum_{i\in I}F_{i}\subset M,

which proves the claim.

Next, note that the family
\{F_{i}\}_{i\in I}
is directed under inclusion. Indeed, if
F_{i}
and
F_{j}
are finitely generated submodules of
M
, then
F_{i}\oplus F_{j}
is also finitely generated. Hence, its quotient
F_{i}+F_{j}
is a finitely generated submodule of
M
. Thus there exists
k\in I
such that
F_{i}\subset F_{k}
and
F_{j}\subset F_{k}
.

Assume for contradiction that
M
is not finitely generated. Choose
j_{1}\in I
such that
F_{j_{1}}\neq 0
. Since
F_{j_{1}}\neq M
and
M=\sum_{i\in I}F_{i}
, there exists
i_{2}\in I
such that
F_{i_{2}}\not\subset F_{j_{1}}
. By directedness, there exists
j_{2}\in I
such that
F_{j_{1}}+F_{i_{2}}\subset F_{j_{2}}
.
Then
F_{j_{1}}\subsetneq F_{j_{2}}
. Repeating this argument, we construct a strictly increasing chain
F_{j_{1}}\subsetneq F_{j_{2}}\subsetneq F_{j_{3}}\subsetneq\cdots
of submodules of
M
, which is impossible because
M
has finite length. Hence
M
is finitely generated.

Remark 2.9
.

As
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is a locally presentable category, so is
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. In such a category, there is a general notion of finitely presented and finitely generated objects, defined by the condition that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}}(M,-)
commutes with filtered colimits and filtered colimits of monomorphisms, respectively. Our definitions of finitely generated and finitely presented modules coincide with these general notions
[
SS23
]
. Thus, the lemma above can be proven by appealing to general results on locally presentable categories, but we have given direct proofs for the convenience of readers.

2.5.4.
Local modules in ind-completion

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a braided tensor category, and let
A
be a commutative algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
By
[
CKM24
, Proposition 2.56]
(specialized to the non-super case),
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is closed under taking kernels and cokernels. Hence it is an abelian category and the inclusion functor
i:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}\hookrightarrow\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is exact.

In fact,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a coreflective subcategory of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}

[
Par95
, Theorem 3.6]
. That is,
i
admits a right adjoint
i^{R}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
. Explicitly, for
(M,\rho)\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
i^{R}(M,\rho)
is the equalizer of the maps

(2)

[A,\rho]\circ\eta_{M},[A,\rho\circ c_{A,M}\circ c_{M,A}]\circ\eta_{M}:M\to[A,M],

where
\eta_{M}:M\to[A,M\otimes A]
is the component of the unit of the adjunction. In particular,
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is closed under colimits in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and the inclusion
i
preserves colimits.

Definition 2.10
.

We define the full subcategories
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
as the intersections of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
with
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, respectively.

2.6.
Invertibles in a tensor category

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a locally finite
\Bbbk
-linear abelian monoidal category with simple unit. We recall some basic facts about invertible objects in
\mathcal{C}
. An object
X\in\mathcal{C}
is called
invertible
if there exists an object
Y\in\mathcal{C}
such that
X\otimes Y\cong\mathbbm{1}\cong Y\otimes X
. The set of isomorphism classes of invertible objects in
\mathcal{C}
forms a group under the tensor product, denoted by
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
.

2.6.1.
Group cohomology

We fix our convention for group cohomology. Let
G
be a group. For an integer
n\geq 1
, we denote by
\mathrm{C}^{n}(G)
the set of all maps from
G\times\cdots\times G
(
n
times) to
\Bbbk^{\times}
such that
f(x_{1},\cdots,x_{n})=1
whenever one of
x_{i}
is the identity element. An element of
\mathrm{C}^{n}(G)
is called a
normalized
n
-cochain
. For each
n\geq 1
, there is the
coboundary map
\partial_{n+1}:\mathrm{C}^{n}(G)\to\mathrm{C}^{n+1}(G)
. For example,

(3)

\displaystyle\partial_{2}(f)(x,y)=f(x)f(xy)^{-1}f(y),

(4)

\displaystyle\partial_{3}(g)(x,y,z)=g(x,y)g(x,yz)^{-1}g(xy,z)g(y,z)^{-1}

for
f\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(G)
,
g\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(G)
and
x,y,z\in G
. An element of
\mathrm{Ker}(\partial_{n+1})
and of
\mathrm{Im}(\partial_{n})
is called an
n
-cocycle
and an
n
-coboundary
, respectively.
We define the
n
-th cohomology group

\mathrm{H}^{n}(G)
to be the quotient group
\mathrm{Ker}(\partial_{n+1})/\mathrm{Im}(\partial_{n})
.

2.6.2.
A 3-cocycle arising from the associator

For each class
g\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
, we choose an object
E_{g}\in\mathcal{C}
representing
g
. We assume that
E_{1}
is the unit object of
\mathcal{C}
. Since
E_{1}
is simple and the endofunctor
E_{g}\otimes(-)
on
\mathcal{C}
is an equivalence,
E_{g}\cong E_{g}\otimes E_{1}
is also a simple object. Thus, by Schur’s lemma and local finiteness, the space
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(E_{g},E_{h})
for
g,h\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
is one-dimensional if
g=h
, and zero otherwise.
We further choose isomorphisms

\phi_{x,y}:E_{x}\otimes E_{y}\to E_{xy}\quad(x,y\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C}))

such that
\phi_{1,x}
and
\phi_{x,1}
are unit isomorphisms for all
x\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
. We define the map
\omega:{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})^{3}\to\Bbbk^{\times}
so that the following diagram is commutative:

(5)

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces(E_{x}\otimes E_{y})\otimes E_{z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{xy}\otimes E_{z}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{xyz}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{x}\otimes(E_{y}\otimes E_{z})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{x}\otimes E_{yz}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E_{xyz},}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\alpha_{E_{x},E_{y},E_{z}}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{x,y}\otimes\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{xy,z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\omega(x,y,z)\,\mathrm{id}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}\otimes\phi_{y,z}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\phi_{x,yz}}

for all
x,y,z\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
, where
\alpha
is the associator. As is well-known, the pentagon axiom implies that
\omega
is a 3-cocycle on the group
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
. The cocycle
\omega
depends on the choice of the isomorphisms
\phi_{x,y}
; however, its cohomology class does not.

2.6.3.
An abelian 3-cocycle arising from the braiding

We assume that the monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
has a braiding
c
. Then we also define the map
\beta:{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})^{2}\to\Bbbk^{\times}
by

(6)

\beta(x,y)\mathrm{id}_{E_{xy}}=\phi_{y,x}\circ c_{E_{x},E_{y}}\circ\phi_{x,y}^{-1}

for
x,y\in{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
.
The hexagon axioms for the braiding imply that the pair
(\omega,\beta)
forms an
abelian 3-cocycle
on
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
in the sense of Eilenberg–Mac Lane
[
EML54
]
; see also
[
JS93
]
.
The function
\mathsf{q}:{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})\to\Bbbk^{\times}
defined by
\mathsf{q}(g)=\beta(g,g)
is a quadratic form whose associated symmetric bicharacter is the double braiding scalar
b_{\mathsf{q}}(g,h)=\beta(g,h)\beta(h,g)
. This perspective is developed in
[
JS93
]
,
[
DGNO10
, §2.11, Appendix D]
and
[
EGNO16
, §8.4]
.

2.6.4.
(Commutative) algebras from invertible objects

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a locally finite
\Bbbk
-linear abelian closed monoidal category, and define
\omega
(and
\beta
) as before (when
\mathcal{C}
is braided).
We now introduce the following class of algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, which is the main object of study in this paper:

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
. A
simple current algebra
over
\Gamma
is an algebra
A
such that
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
as an object of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, the multiplication
A\otimes A\to A
restricts to an isomorphism
E_{x}\otimes E_{y}\to E_{xy}
for all elements
x,y\in\Gamma
and the unit is given by the inclusion morphism
\mathbbm{1}=E_{1}\to A
.

Lemma 2.12
.

We fix a subgroup
\Gamma
of
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
and set
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
.

(a)

The object
A
becomes a simple current algebra over
\Gamma
if and only if the restriction of the 3-cocycle
\omega
to
\Gamma\times\Gamma\times\Gamma
is a coboundary. If this is the case, for any cochain
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\Gamma)
such that
\omega|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma\times\Gamma}=\partial_{3}(\eta)
, the morphism
\mu:A\otimes A\to A
induced by

(7)

\mu_{x,y}:=\eta(x,y)\phi_{x,y}:E_{x}\otimes E_{y}\to E_{xy}\quad(x,y\in\Gamma)

makes
A
a simple current algebra over
\Gamma
.

(b)

We assume that
\mathcal{C}
is braided. Then
A
is a commutative simple current algebra if and only if
\mathsf{q}|_{\Gamma}\equiv 1
, i.e.,
\beta(g,g)=1
for all
g\in\Gamma
. If this is the case, then we have

(8)

\beta(x,y)\beta(y,x)=1,\qquad\text{for all}\;\;x,y\in\Gamma.

Parts (a) and (b) are well known; see
[
EGNO16
, §8.8]
for the pointed fusion category case,
[
DGNO10
, §2.11]
for the general statement, and
[
FRS04
, §3]
for simple current extensions.

Proof 2.13
.

(a) Given a normalized 2-cochain
\eta:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\Bbbk^{\times}
, it is straightforward to verify that the morphism
\mu:A\otimes A\to A
induced by (
7
) makes
A
a simple current algebra if and only if
\omega|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma\times\Gamma}=\partial_{3}(\eta)
. In particular, we have proved the ‘if’ part. To show the converse, we assume that
A
is a simple current algebra with multiplication
\mu
. By the definition of a simple current algebra, the multiplication must be induced by (
7
) for some map
\eta:\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\Bbbk^{\times}
. The same computation as ‘if’ part completes the proof.

(b) Suppose
A
is a simple current algebra with multiplication induced by
\eta
. The commutativity condition
\mu\circ c_{A,A}=\mu
on the
(x,y)
-component reads

(9)

\eta(y,x)\,\beta(x,y)=\eta(x,y)\qquad(x,y\in\Gamma).

Setting
x=y
and using
\eta(x,x)\neq 0
gives
\beta(x,x)=1
, i.e.,
\mathsf{q}|_{\Gamma}\equiv 1
.
Conversely, assume that
\mathsf{q}|_{\Gamma}\equiv 1
.
Then the abelian
3
-cocycle
(\omega|_{\Gamma^{3}},\beta|_{\Gamma^{2}})
has trivial associated quadratic form.
By the Eilenberg–Mac Lane isomorphism between abelian cohomology and quadratic forms (see
[
Gal26
]
for a recent reference), its class in abelian cohomology is therefore trivial.
Hence there exists a normalized
2
-cochain
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\Gamma)
such that

\partial_{3}(\eta)=\omega|_{\Gamma^{3}}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\beta(x,y)=\eta(x,y)\eta(y,x)^{-1}\quad(x,y\in\Gamma).

The first identity gives an algebra structure on
A
by part (a), while the second identity is exactly the commutativity condition (
9
). Thus
A
is a commutative algebra.
Equation (
8
) easily follows from (
9
).

The choice of
\eta
in (a) is not unique. If a simple current algebra
A_{i}
(
i=1,2
) is constructed from a cochain
\eta_{i}\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\Gamma)
satisfying
\partial_{3}(\eta_{i})=\omega|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma\times\Gamma}
, then
A_{1}\cong A_{2}
as algebras if and only if
\eta_{1}
and
\eta_{2}
are cohomologous. In other words, the set of the isomorphism classes of simple current algebras over
\Gamma
(if it is non-empty) is a torsor over
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma)
.

The choice of
\eta
in (b) is also not unique. Suppose that cochains
\eta_{1},\eta_{2}\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\Gamma)
define two commutative simple current algebra structures on the object
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
.
Then
\rho:=\eta_{1}\eta_{2}^{-1}
is a symmetric
2
-cocycle on the abelian group
\Gamma
.
Since the group
\Bbbk^{\times}
is divisible, every symmetric
2
-cocycle
\Gamma\times\Gamma\to\Bbbk^{\times}
is a coboundary.
Thus there exists
\lambda\in\mathrm{C}^{1}(\Gamma)
such that
\rho=\partial_{2}(\lambda)
.
Rescaling each summand
E_{x}
by the scalar
\lambda(x)
yields an algebra isomorphism between the two structures. In particular, the cohomology class of
\eta
in
\mathrm{H}^{2}(\Gamma)
, and hence the isomorphism class of the commutative algebra
A
, is uniquely determined.

3.
A construction of ribbon braided tensor categories

In this section, we present a framework for constructing new ribbon braided tensor categories. We begin with a braided tensor category
\mathcal{C}
and consider commutative algebras within its ind-completion
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. The full category of modules over such an algebra is typically too large to form a tensor category of the type we study, as it will include non-rigid and infinite length objects. Therefore, we must identify specific subcategories that are closed under tensor products and satisfy necessary finiteness conditions.

We establish that for
Artinian algebras
the three finiteness conditions of finitely generated, finitely presented, and finite length modules coincide (Theorem
3.6
), yielding a
\Bbbk
-linear abelian monoidal category where every object is of finite length. In Section
3.2
, we introduce ind-exact algebras (following
[
CEO24
]
) to ensure rigidity of the category of finitely generated modules. In Section
3.3
, we introduce the notion of a Frobenius algebra in a closed monoidal category by extending the commonly used definition in the rigid case (see Definition
3.14
), and show that the category of finitely generated local modules is ribbon if the algebra
A
is Artinian, ind-exact and Frobenius in our sense and the twist is the identity on
A
. The main results are summarized in Theorem
3.1
below.

Convention.
Throughout this section,
\mathcal{C}
denotes a tensor category (with additional structure imposed when needed),
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
its ind-completion, and
A
an algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{C}
is a braided tensor category and
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is a commutative algebra.
If
A
is Artinian, haploid and ind-exact, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is a tensor category and

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a braided tensor category.
If in addition,
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon,
A
is Frobenius and
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a ribbon
tensor category.

Proof 3.2
.

This theorem is a summary of results proved later in this section. The first claim follows from Theorem
3.21
, and the second claim follows from Theorem
3.25
.

3.1.
Artinian algebras

Recall the subcategories
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, and
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
of finite length, finitely generated, and finitely presented
A
-modules, respectively, from Definition
2.4
.

Ideally, one would like to work inside the full subcategory of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
consisting of objects that are simultaneously finitely generated, finitely presented, and of finite length.
This leads to the guiding problem: find algebras
A
for which these three finiteness conditions coincide.

Definition 3.3
.

We call an algebra
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

Artinian

[
CEO24
]
if every finitely generated right
A
-module is of finite length.

We first record the key property of Artinian algebras.

Lemma 3.4
.

If
A
is Artinian, every finitely generated right
A
-module is finitely presented.

Proof 3.5
.

Let
M
be a finitely generated
A
-module. By definition there exists an epimorphism

f:X\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow M
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
for some
X\in\mathcal{C}
.
Let
K:=\ker(f)
and let
k:K\hookrightarrow X\otimes A
be the kernel inclusion, so we have an exact sequence

0\to K\xrightarrow{k}X\otimes A\xrightarrow{f}M\to 0.

Since
X\otimes A
is finitely generated and
A
is Artinian,
X\otimes A
has finite length.
Hence
K
has finite length as a subobject of
X\otimes A
in the abelian category
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
.
By Lemma
2.7
,
K
is finitely generated. Thus there exists
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and an epimorphism

g:X^{\prime}\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow K
.

Then
k\circ g:X^{\prime}\otimes A\to X\otimes A
has image
K=\ker(f)
, so
f
is the cokernel of
k\circ g
.
Equivalently,

X^{\prime}\otimes A\xrightarrow{k\circ g}X\otimes A\xrightarrow{f}M\to 0

is exact, proving that
M
is finitely presented.

Our interest in Artinian algebras stems from the following theorem, which shows that being Artinian forces these finiteness notions to coincide.

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
be an Artinian algebra. Then we have
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and these categories are abelian
\Bbbk
-linear categories where objects have finite length.
If
\mathcal{C}
has enough projective objects, then so do these categories.

Proof 3.7
.

By Lemma
3.4
, it is immediate that
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Next, using Lemma
2.7
together with the Artinian property, we see that an object of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is finitely generated if and only if it is of finite length, i.e.
\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Thus, the three categories are equal. In particular,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is abelian,
\Bbbk
-linear and objects have finite length.

Assume that
\mathcal{C}
has enough projective objects.
Let
M\in\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, and choose an epimorphism
q:X\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow M
with
X\in\mathcal{C}
.
Choose an epimorphism
\pi:P\twoheadrightarrow X
in
\mathcal{C}
with
P
projective.
Then
\pi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}:P\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow X\otimes A
is an epimorphism in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, and hence so is

q\circ(\pi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}):P\otimes A\twoheadrightarrow M
.
Moreover
P\otimes A
is projective in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
: indeed, for any
A
-module
N
we have an adjunction

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}}(P\otimes A,N)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(P,U(N)),

where
U
is the forgetful functor, which is exact because kernels and cokernels in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
are computed in

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
Since
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(P,-)
is exact, it follows that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}}(P\otimes A,-)
is exact.
Thus
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
has enough projectives.

3.2.
Ind-exact algebras

We consider the following notion from
[
CEO24
, §6]
.

Definition 3.8
.

We call an algebra
A
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}

ind-exact
if the functor
-\otimes_{A}-:\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\times\mathrm{fg}\text{-}{}_{A}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is bi-exact. Here,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}{}_{A}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is the category of finitely generated left
A
-modules, which is defined in the same manner as
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
.

Theorem 3.9
.

Let
A
be an Artinian central commutative algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. The following are equivalent:

(a)

A
is ind-exact.

(b)

The category
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is rigid.

(c)

Every simple
A
-module is rigid.

Proof 3.10
.

(a)\Rightarrow(b)
: The Artinian assumption implies that
\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Thus, every object in
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is finitely presented, that is, it is the cokernel of a morphism
X\otimes A\to Y\otimes A
for some
X,Y\in\mathcal{C}
. Here
X\otimes A
and
Y\otimes A
are rigid. So,
the claim follows because rigid objects in an abelian monoidal category with biexact tensor product are closed under taking cokernels
[
Wig18
]
.

(b)\Rightarrow(a)
: Rigidity implies that the functor
-\otimes_{A}V
admits left/right adjoints given by tensoring with left/right duals of
V
in
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Thus, tensoring over
A
is bi-exact.

(b)\Rightarrow(c)
: This is immediate.

(c)\Rightarrow(b)
: Denote
\mathcal{D}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Since
A
is Artinian, Theorem
3.6
implies that
\mathcal{D}
is an abelian

\Bbbk
-linear category in which every object has finite length. In
\mathcal{D}
, every object is a quotient of a free module
X\otimes A
for some

3.3.
Frobenius algebras

In this subsection, we introduce Frobenius algebras in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and discuss their properties. We show that if
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon and
A
is a Frobenius algebra, then the category of local modules over

Recall that
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is closed monoidal and we denote the right internal Hom as
[-,-]
. It satisfies
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(L\otimes M,N)\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(L,[M,N])

Definition 3.14
.

Let

\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
with multiplication

is a

As we will see in Section

). We expect co-Frobenius Hopf algebras to provide more examples, and we will pursue this line of inquiry in future work.

Now we give properties of Frobenius algebras. Lemma

Let

(10)

for

and

.

There is a natural transformation

We define

With the above notation, we have a natural isomorphism

Proof 3.18

.

3.4.
Braided case

We also record the analogous finiteness consequences for the local module category. In this section,
\mathcal{C}
is assumed to be a braided tensor category.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
A\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
be a commutative Artinian algebra. Then we have

\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathrm{fp}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}},

and these categories are finite length, abelian,
\Bbbk
-linear, braided monoidal categories.
If, moreover,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
has enough injective objects, then so do these categories.

Proof 3.20
.

The first statement follows from Theorem
3.6
. As
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and both
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
are abelian
\Bbbk
-linear subcategories of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is an abelian
\Bbbk
-linear subcategory as well. Lastly, since
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is finite length and
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a full subcategory of
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is finite length as well.

Recall that the inclusion functor
i:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
admits a right adjoint
i^{R}
. If
M\in\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}=\mathrm{fl}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
, then
i^{R}(M)
(which is a subobject of
M
, see (
2
)) is also of finite length, hence finitely generated. Thus, the right adjoint
i^{R}:\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\to\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
restricts to a functor
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}\to\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
.

Suppose that
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
has enough injective objects. Let
M\in\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
and choose an injection
i(M)\hookrightarrow I
in
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
with
I
injective. Then,
i^{R}(I)
is injective in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
because
\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}}(-,i^{R}(I))\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}}(i(-),I)
is exact. As
M\hookrightarrow i^{R}(I)
, we see that
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
has enough injective objects as well.

Lastly, when
A
is a commutative algebra, the braided monoidal structure on
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
restricts to
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
because the tensor product of two finitely generated local modules is again finitely generated.

3.4.1.
Rigidity

Theorem 3.21
.

Let

3.4.2.
Ribbon structure on local modules

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a ribbon tensor category with braiding
c
and twist
\theta
. Then
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
is a closed braided monoidal category. Moreover, the twist induces an automorphism of the identity functor of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
that satisfies
\theta_{M\otimes N}=c_{N,M}c_{M,N}(\theta_{M}\otimes\theta_{N})
for all
M,N\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.

We denote by
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
the Müger center of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Next we will show that, in the ind-completion of a ribbon category, the internal Hom is compatible with the ribbon structure. In particular,
\theta_{[M,\mathbbm{1}]}=[\theta_{M},\mathbbm{1}]
for
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.

Lemma 3.23
.

For all objects
M\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
and
X\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
, we have
\theta_{[M,X]}=[\theta_{M},\theta_{X}]
.

Proof 3.24
.

We write
M
as
M=\varinjlim_{i\in I}M_{i}
for
M_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
. Then we have
[M,X]=\varprojlim_{i\in I}\,[M_{i},X]
.
If
W\in\mathcal{C}
, then we may, and do, identify
[W,X]
with
X\otimes W^{*}
. Hence we have

\theta_{[W,X]}=\theta_{X\otimes W^{*}}=c_{W^{*},X}c_{X,W^{*}}(\theta_{X}\otimes\theta_{W^{*}})=\theta_{X}\otimes(\theta_{W})^{*}=[\theta_{W},\theta_{X}],

where we have used the assumption
X\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}
at the third equality.
Since
M_{i}\in\mathcal{C}
for all
i
,

\theta_{[M,X]}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}\theta_{[M_{i},X]}=\varprojlim_{i\in I}\,[\theta_{M_{i}},\theta_{X}]=[\varinjlim_{i\in I}\theta_{M_{i}},\theta_{X}]=[\theta_{M},\theta_{X}].

Let
A
be a commutative Frobenius algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. Then the category
\mathcal{D}:=\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
of finitely-generated local
A

Theorem 3.25
.

If
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
and
\mathcal{D}
is rigid, then
\mathcal{D}
is a ribbon category.

We explain that this theorem can be applied to simple current algebras in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
in characteristic zero in Section
4
.
The condition
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
is analogous to triviality of the Nakayama automorphism in the finite-dimensional Frobenius case
[
FFRS06
, Proposition 2.25]
.

Proof 3.26
.

By the assumption that
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
, we can define a natural isomorphism
\theta^{A}_{M}:M\to M
for
M\in\mathcal{D}
by
\theta^{A}_{M}=\theta_{M}
in the same way as
[

, Theorem 1.17 (2)]
. With the use of the internal Hom functor, the duality functor of

is given by

.
It remains to show that
(\theta^{A}_{M})^{\dagger}=\theta^{A}_{M^{\dagger}}

, there is a natural isomorphism

4.
Representation theory of simple current algebras

Throughout this section,
\mathcal{C}
denotes a locally finite
\Bbbk
-linear abelian closed monoidal category with simple unit; additional hypotheses (rigidity, braiding, etc.) will be imposed when needed.
We also fix a subgroup
\Gamma
of the group
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
of invertible objects of
\mathcal{C}
and, for each
g\in\Gamma
, choose an object
E_{g}\in\mathcal{C}
representing
g
. In view of Lemma
2.12
(a), we assume that there is a 2-cochain
\eta\in\mathrm{C}^{2}(\Gamma)
such that
\partial_{3}(\eta)=\omega|_{\Gamma\times\Gamma\times\Gamma}
, where
\omega
is the 3-cocycle on
{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
arising from the associator, and construct a simple current algebra
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
by the 2-cochain
\eta
.
The aim of this section is to investigate the representation theory of
A
.
In this section, we verify that
A
satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem
3.1
under some assumptions on
\Gamma
and
\mathcal{C}
.
As a consequence, we obtain criteria ensuring the category of finitely-generated local modules over
A
is a ribbon (Frobenius) tensor category (Theorem
4.44
).

4.1.
Classification of simple modules

In this subsection, we give a classification of simple right modules of the algebra
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}E_{g}
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
.
To do this, we study the restriction of
A
-modules to subalgebras of
A
, which is similar in spirit to Clifford theory.

The invertible summands
E_{g}
act on simples of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
by tensor product, so any simple

-module
M
is supported on a single
\Gamma
-orbit of a simple constituent

A
to the stabilizer subgroup
S=\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
and the corresponding subalgebra
A_{X}=\bigoplus_{s\in S}E_{s}
, which captures the part of the action that preserves
X
. The associativity constraints for an
A_{X}
-action on
X
produce a canonical 2-cocycle
\xi_{X}
on
S
; this cocycle measures the obstruction to choosing the identifications
X\otimes E_{s}\simeq X
compatibly, and it forces the multiplicity space to carry a
\xi_{X}
-projective representation of
S
. Thus simple
A_{X}
-modules whose underlying object is isomorphic to
X\oplus\dotsb\oplus X
are parametrized by irreducible
\xi_{X}
-projective representations
W
of
S
, and the corresponding simple

-modules are obtained by induction
\mathbf{M}(X,W)=(W\otimes X)\otimes_{A_{X}}A

by

by the conjugate data, yielding the same induced
A

Projective representations

We recall basics on projective representations of a group.
Let

\rho(s)\,\rho(t)=\xi(s,t)\,\rho(st)\quad\text{for all }s,t\in S.

-actions. We denote by

\Bbbk

and multiplication
e_{s}\cdot e_{t}=\xi(s,t)\,e_{st}

4.1.5.
Finiteness of the number of simple modules

We give a formula of the number of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
and, in particular, determine when it is finite. Given an abelian category
\mathcal{A}
, we denote by
\Irr(\mathcal{A})
the set of isomorphism classes of simple objects of
\mathcal{A}
. The group
\Gamma
acts on
\Irr(\mathcal{C})
by
[X]\cdot g=[X\otimes E_{g}]
for
g\in\Gamma
and
[X]\in\Irr(\mathcal{C})
(this action is well-defined since
(-)\otimes E_{g}
is an autoequivalence and preserves simplicity).

Corollary 4.23
.

For brevity, we set
I_{X}=\Irr\bigl(\mathrm{Rep}(\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)^{\mathrm{op}},\,\xi_{X}^{\mathrm{op}})\bigr)
for a simple object
X\in\mathcal{C}
, where
\xi_{X}
is the
2
-cocycle determined by (
13
). Then there is a bijection

\bigsqcup_{X\in\mathcal{O}}I_{X}\to\Irr(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}),\quad[\rho]\mapsto[\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)]\quad([\rho]\in I_{X}),

where
\mathcal{O}
is the set of orbit representatives of
\Irr(\mathcal{C})/\Gamma
.

The bijection implies
\#\,\Irr(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A})=\sum_{[X]\in\mathcal{O}}\;\#I_{X}
. Since the set
I_{X}
is non-empty and finite for all simple
X\in\mathcal{C}
(since the stabilizer
\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
is finite), we have that
\Irr(\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A})
is finite if and only if
\Irr(\mathcal{C})/\Gamma
is finite.

Proof 4.24
.

We first prove that the map is surjective.
By Theorem
4.19
, every simple right
A
-module is isomorphic to
\mathbf{M}(X^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})
for some simple
X^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}
and some
[\rho^{\prime}]\in I_{X^{\prime}}
. By the definition of the action of
\Gamma
on
\Irr(\mathcal{C})
, there exists
X\in\mathcal{O}
and
g\in\Gamma
such that
X^{\prime}\cong X\otimes E_{g}
. Letting
\rho=(\rho^{\prime})^{g^{-1}}
, we have
\mathbf{M}(X^{\prime},\rho^{\prime})\cong\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)
by Theorem
4.21
. This means that the map is surjective.

To show the injectivity, we let
X,Y\in\mathcal{O}
,
[\rho]\in I_{X}
and
[\sigma]\in I_{Y}
and assume that
[\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)]=[\mathbf{M}(Y,\sigma)]
. By Theorem
4.21
, there is an element
g\in\Gamma
such that
Y\cong X\otimes E_{g}
and
\sigma\sim\rho^{g}
. Since
X
and
Y
are representatives of some orbits, we have
X=Y
and thus
g\in\mathrm{Stab}_{\Gamma}(X)
. Hence
\sigma\sim\rho^{g}\sim\rho
. This shows that the map is injective. The proof is done.

From here on,

4.2.1.
Artinian property

Proposition 4.25
.

The algebra
A
is Artinian.

Proof 4.26
.

Since a finitely generated object of
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
is a quotient of
X\otimes A
for some
X\in\mathcal{C}
, and since a quotient of a finite length object is also of finite length, it suffices to show that the free module
X\otimes A
for
X\in\mathcal{C}
is of finite length. We prove this by induction on the length of
X
as an object of
\mathcal{C}
.

The claim is trivial if
\ell(X)=0
. We assume that
\ell(X)=1
or, equivalently,
X
is a simple object. Since
X\otimes A_{X}
is a right
A_{X}
-module whose underlying object is isomorphic to a direct sum of finitely many copies of
X
, by Lemma
4.7
, there exists
\rho\in\mathrm{Rep}(S^{\mathrm{op}},\xi_{X}^{\mathrm{op}})
such that
X\otimes A_{X}\cong\mathbf{L}(X,\rho)
as right
A_{X}
-modules. There are isomorphisms

X\otimes A\cong(X\otimes A_{X})\otimes_{A_{X}}A\cong\mathbf{L}(X,\rho)\otimes_{A_{X}}A=\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)

of right
A
-modules. Thus, by Lemma
4.17
,
X\otimes A
is of finite length.

Now we assume that
\ell(X)>1
and
Y\otimes A
is of finite length for all
Y\in\mathcal{C}
with
\ell(Y)<\ell(X)
. We choose a simple subobject
X^{\prime}\subset X
and let
X^{\prime\prime}=X/X^{\prime}
so that there is a short exact sequence
0\to X^{\prime}\to X\to X^{\prime\prime}\to 0
in
\mathcal{C}
. By applying the free module functor to this sequence, we obtain a short exact sequence
0\to X^{\prime}\otimes A\to X\otimes A\to X^{\prime\prime}\otimes A\to 0
in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}
. Since
X^{\prime}\otimes A
and
X^{\prime\prime}\otimes A
are of finite length, so is

. This proves that
A
is Artinian.

4.2.2.
Frobenius algebra structure

In this subsection, we prove that
A
is Frobenius. We recall Iovanov’s result
[
Iov06
]
, which plays a key role. Let
\mathcal{A}
be a complete and cocomplete abelian category, and let
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
be an infinite family of objects of
\mathcal{A}
. Then we have two objects: The product
\Pi:=\prod_{i\in I}M_{i}
and the coproduct
\Sigma:=\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i}
. There is a canonical morphism
\Sigma\to\Pi
. When
\mathcal{A}
is the category of modules over a ring and
M_{i}\neq 0
for infinitely many
i
, the canonical morphism is never an isomorphism. However, according to Iovanov
[
Iov06
, Example 2.9]
, the canonical morphism
\Sigma\to\Pi
is an isomorphism if
\mathcal{A}
is an ind-completion of a locally finite abelian category and
\Sigma
is quasi-finite. We use this to prove:

Theorem 4.27
.

The algebra
A
is Frobenius with the Frobenius form
\lambda:A\to\mathbbm{1}
given by the projection to
E_{1}=\mathbbm{1}
.

Proof 4.28
.

For a family
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in I}
of objects of
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
, we have
[\bigoplus_{i\in I}M_{i},N]\cong\prod_{i\in I}[M_{i},N]
. Thus,

[A,\mathbbm{1}]\cong\prod_{g\in\Gamma}[g,\mathbbm{1}]\cong\prod_{g\in\Gamma}g^{-1}\cong\prod_{g\in\Gamma}g.

Unwinding the definition of the morphism
\phi:A\to[A,\mathbbm{1}]
in Definition
3.14
, one checks that under the above identifications it coincides with the
canonical morphism
\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}g\to\prod_{g\in\Gamma}g
:
indeed, writing
A=\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}g
, the composite
\lambda\mu

vanishes on
g\otimes h
unless
h\simeq g^{-1}
, in which case it is identified (up to the chosen
identifications
g\otimes g^{-1}\cong\mathbbm{1}
) with the evaluation pairing.
By adjunction, this yields a morphism
g\to[A,\mathbbm{1}]\cong\prod_{h\in\Gamma}h
whose only non-zero component is the
canonical map
g\to g
into the
h=g
factor. Summing over
g
gives the canonical morphism

\bigoplus_{g\in\Gamma}g\to\prod_{g\in\Gamma}g
.
This is an isomorphism by Iovanov’s result cited above.

4.3.3.
Ribbon structure and non-degeneracy

Let
\mathcal{G}:=\langle E_{g}\mid g\in\Gamma\rangle\subset\mathcal{C}
. By (
22
),
\mathcal{G}
is symmetric, hence
\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}=\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(\mathcal{G}\subset\mathcal{C})
. Now we can state the main result of this section.

Theorem 4.44
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a (Frobenius) braided tensor category such that condition (
21
) is satisfied.

(a)

Then,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a (Frobenius) braided tensor category.

(b)

If
\mathcal{C}
is ribbon and
\theta_{E_{g}}=\mathrm{id}_{E_{g}}
for all
g\in\Gamma
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a ribbon tensor category.

(c)

When
\mathcal{C}
has enough projectives,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a braided finite tensor category if and only if
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma})/\Gamma
is finite.

Proof 4.45
.

(a) Recall that
A
is Artinian and haploid. Moreover, by Corollary
4.33
, all simple
A
-modules are rigid. Thus, by Theorem
3.21
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a braided (Frobenius) tensor category.

(b) Note that
A
is Frobenius by Theorem
4.27
, and that
\theta_{A}=\mathrm{id}_{A}
by the assumption on twists. Since
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is rigid by part (a), Theorem
3.25
implies that it is ribbon.

(c) By part (a),
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
is a locally finite abelian category with enough projectives. Hence, it is braided finite if and only if it has finitely many simple objects. The latter is equivalent to
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma})/\Gamma
being finite by Corollary
4.40
.

Theorem 4.46
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a Frobenius braided tensor category and
char
(\Bbbk)=0
. If the simple objects in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
that trivially double braid with all other simples in
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
are precisely
E_{g}
for
g\in\Gamma
, then
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is non-degenerate.

Proof 4.47
.

As char
(\Bbbk)=0
, condition (
21
) is satisfied. Thus, Theorem
4.44
(a) implies that
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is a Frobenius tensor category. Hence, by Lemma
2.2
,
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is non-degenerate if and only if the only simple object in its Müger center is
A
. But, simple objects in
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}
are
\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)
for
X\in\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
and for such an object to lie in the Müger center, it has to double braid trivially with all other simple objects. By Lemma
4.42
, this implies that
X
trivially double braids with all simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
. By assumption of the theorem,
X\cong E_{g}
for some
g\in\Gamma
. Now, Lemma
4.13
implies that
\mathbf{M}(X,\rho)\cong A
. Thus, the Müger center of
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is trivial, i.e.
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A}
is non-degenerate.

5.
Examples from quantum (super)group categories

In this section we study two families of examples coming from unrolled quantum groups
U^{H}(\mathfrak{g})
and the unrolled quantum supergroups
\mathfrak{gl}(1|1)
.
We analyze the corresponding categories of finite-dimensional weight modules and the simple-current local-module categories they produce. We follow the same program:

(a)

identify the invertible simple objects and their tensor product law;

(b)

compute the relevant braiding and twist data on these invertibles;

(c)

choose commutative simple current algebras and extract consequences for the corresponding category of local modules.

The categorical consequences in step (c) are obtained by applying Theorem
4.44
to the explicit simple-current data.

Let
\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}=\{\bar{0},\bar{1}\}
be the additive group of order
2
. We will sometimes write
M_{S,T}
to denote the double braiding between two objects
S
and
T
.

5.1.
Unrolled quantum group categories

The categorical input follows Creutzig–Rupert
[
CR22
]
(and references therein); we recall only the data needed for applying the simple-current machinery of Section
4
. The setting for general sublattices
L^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
(Proposition
5.1
and its corollaries) is new.

Fix a simple complex Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with Cartan matrix
A=(a_{ij})
,
root lattice
Q\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, and weight lattice
P\subset\mathfrak{h}^{*}
.
Assume
q
is a primitive
\ell
-th root of unity such that ord
(q^{2})>d_{i}
for all
i
. We fix
\log q
and set
q^{z}=\exp(z\log q)
for
z\in\mathbb{C}
. We also set

(26)

r=\ell\text{ if }\ell\text{ is odd},\qquad r=\ell/2\text{ if }\ell\text{ is even}.

Let
\mathcal{C}:=\mathrm{Rep}^{\mathrm{wt}}(U_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{g}))
be the category of finite-dimensional weight modules for Rupert’s unrolled restricted quantum group, obtained from the unrolled quantum group by quotienting by the Hopf ideal generated by the prescribed powers of the root vectors. Concretely,
U_{q}^{H}(\mathfrak{g})
is generated by
E_{i},F_{i},H_{i}
together with group-like elements
K_{\gamma}
indexed by a fixed lattice between
Q
and
P
, and a weight module is a finite-dimensional module that decomposes into common
H_{i}
-eigenspaces such that, for
\gamma=\sum_{i}k_{i}\alpha_{i}
, the operator
K_{\gamma}
acts on the
\lambda
-weight space by the scalar
q^{\sum_{i}k_{i}d_{i}\lambda(H_{i})}
.
Let
\lambda(H_{i})
denote evaluation of
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
on
H_{i}
.
By Rupert
[
Rup22
]
(see also
[
CR22
]
),
\mathcal{C}
is a ribbon Frobenius tensor category with enough projectives (equivalently, projective covers), and its Müger center is trivial.

Let
M_{\lambda}
denote the simple quotient of the Verma module with highest weight
\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Then

\Irr(\mathcal{C})=\{\,M_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\,\}.

The module
M_{\lambda}
is one-dimensional, hence invertible, if and only if
E_{i}
and
F_{i}
act by
zero for all
i
, which is equivalent to
\lambda(H_{i})\in\frac{\ell}{2d_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
for all
i
. In that case we write
C_{\lambda}:=M_{\lambda}
. Hence

\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}:=\left\{\lambda\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\,\middle|\,\lambda(H_{i})\in\frac{\ell}{2d_{i}}\mathbb{Z}\,\forall\,i\right\}

is isomorphic to the additive group of invertible simple objects in
\mathcal{C}
, via
\lambda\mapsto C_{\lambda}
, and for
\lambda,\mu\in\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
one has
C_{\lambda}\otimes C_{\mu}\cong C_{\lambda+\mu}
. On invertibles one has

c_{C_{\lambda},C_{\mu}}=q^{\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle}\tau,\qquad M_{C_{\lambda},C_{\mu}}=q^{2\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle}\mathrm{id}.

where
\tau
is the braiding of
\mathbf{Vec}
and

\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle:=\sum_{i,j}d_{i}(A^{-1})_{ij}\lambda(H_{i})\mu(H_{j})

for the invariant bilinear form on
\mathfrak{h}^{*}
. Here
d_{i}=\langle\alpha_{i},\alpha_{i}\rangle/2
are the symmetrizers. The twist is
[
CR22
, Eq. (4.12)]
:

(27)

\theta_{C_{\lambda}}=q^{\langle\lambda,\lambda+2(1-r)\rho\rangle}\mathrm{id}_{C_{\lambda}},

where
\rho
is the Weyl vector and
r
is as in (
26
). Thus, for an additive subgroup

L^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
, the corresponding simple current algebra is

A_{L^{\prime}}=\bigoplus_{\lambda\in L^{\prime}}C_{\lambda}\in\widehat{\mathcal{C}}.

In the notation of Section
4
, write

\beta(\lambda,\mu)=q^{\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle},\qquad b(\lambda,\mu)=q^{2\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle},\qquad\mathsf{q}(\lambda)=q^{\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle}=\beta(\lambda,\lambda).

For an additive subgroup
L^{\prime}\subseteq\mathfrak{h}^{*}
, we write

(L^{\prime})^{*}:=\{\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\mid\langle\gamma,\lambda\rangle\in\mathbb{Z}\text{ for all }\lambda\in L^{\prime}\}

for the dual lattice with respect to
\langle-,-\rangle
.

Proposition 5.1
.

(a)

The object
A_{L^{\prime}}
is a haploid algebra in
\widehat{\mathcal{C}}
. It is commutative if and only if
\mathsf{q}|_{L^{\prime}}\equiv 1
. Equivalently,
\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle\in\ell\mathbb{Z}
for
\lambda\in L^{\prime}
.

(b)

Let

\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}:=\left\{X\in\mathcal{C}\ \middle|\ c_{C_{\lambda},X}\circ c_{X,C_{\lambda}}=\mathrm{id}_{X\otimes C_{\lambda}}\ \ \forall\,\lambda\in L^{\prime}\right\}.

Then
\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}=\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{int}}_{L^{\prime}}
, where
\mathcal{C}^{\mathrm{int}}_{L^{\prime}}
is the full subcategory of objects all of whose weights lie in
\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
.

(c)

Assume that
A_{L^{\prime}}
is commutative. Then the orbit set
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}})/L^{\prime}
, where
L^{\prime}
acts by tensoring with the invertibles
C_{\lambda}
, is identified with

\Lambda(L^{\prime}):=\frac{\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}}{L^{\prime}}.

Note that when
A_{L^{\prime}}
is commutative, equivalently
\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle\in\ell\mathbb{Z}
for all
\lambda\in L^{\prime}
, we get
L^{\prime}\subseteq\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
, so the quotient
\Lambda(L^{\prime})
is well defined.

Proof 5.2
.

(a)
On invertibles, the braiding scalar is
\beta(\lambda,\mu)=q^{\langle\lambda,\mu\rangle}
, and this is a bicharacter on
\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
. Hence the associated abelian
3
-cocycle on the pointed subcategory of invertibles is represented by
(1,\beta)
, so the ordinary associator cocycle
\omega
restricts trivially to
L^{\prime}
. By Lemma
2.12
(a), this gives an algebra structure on
A_{L^{\prime}}
for any additive subgroup
L^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
. Then Lemma
2.12
(b) implies that
A_{L^{\prime}}
is commutative if and only if
\mathsf{q}|_{L^{\prime}}\equiv 1
. Since
\mathsf{q}(\lambda)=q^{\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle}
, this is equivalent to
\langle\lambda,\lambda\rangle\in\ell\mathbb{Z}
for all
\lambda\in L^{\prime}
. That
A
is haploid follows from
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1},C_{\lambda})=0
for
\lambda\neq 0
, hence

\mathrm{Hom}_{\widehat{\mathcal{C}}}(\mathbbm{1},A_{L^{\prime}})\cong\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbbm{1},C_{0})\cong\Bbbk
.

(b)
Let
X\in\mathcal{C}
be a weight module. For a weight vector
w_{\gamma}\in X
one has

\bigl(c_{C_{\lambda},X}\circ c_{X,C_{\lambda}}\bigr)(v_{\lambda}\otimes w_{\gamma})=q^{2\langle\lambda,\gamma\rangle}(v_{\lambda}\otimes w_{\gamma}).

Hence
X\in\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}
iff
2\langle\lambda,\gamma\rangle\in\ell\mathbb{Z}
for every
\lambda\in L^{\prime}
and every weight
\gamma
of
X
, i.e. iff all weights lie in
\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
.

(c)
Since
A_{L^{\prime}}
is commutative by
(a)
, Theorems
4.19
and
4.21
together with Proposition
4.38
show that simple local
A_{L^{\prime}}
-modules are classified by pairs
(M_{\gamma},\rho)
, where
M_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}
and
\rho
is an irreducible projective representation of

\mathrm{Stab}_{L^{\prime}}(M_{\gamma}):=\{\,\eta\in L^{\prime}\mid M_{\gamma}\otimes C_{\eta}\cong M_{\gamma}\,\}^{\mathrm{op}}.

Since
M_{\gamma}\otimes C_{\eta}\cong M_{\gamma+\eta}
and
M_{\lambda}\cong M_{\mu}
only for
\lambda=\mu
, this stabilizer is trivial. Hence
\rho
is necessarily trivial, so simple local
A_{L^{\prime}}
-modules are parametrized exactly by
L^{\prime}
-orbits in
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}})
.

Moreover,
Q\subseteq\tfrac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
since
\langle\eta,\alpha_{i}\rangle=d_{i}\eta(H_{i})\in\tfrac{\ell}{2}\mathbb{Z}
for
\eta\in L^{\prime}\subseteq\Lambda_{\mathrm{inv}}
and each simple root
\alpha_{i}
; hence if
\gamma\in\tfrac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
, then every weight of
M_{\gamma}
also lies in
\tfrac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
.

As
\Irr(\mathcal{C})=\{\,M_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\mathfrak{h}^{*}\,\}
, part
(b)
gives that the simple objects of
\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}
are exactly

\{\,M_{\gamma}\mid\gamma\in\tfrac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}\,\}.

Moreover,
M_{\gamma}\otimes C_{\eta}\cong M_{\gamma+\eta}
for
\eta\in L^{\prime}
,
so two such simples lie in the same
L^{\prime}
-orbit if and only if their parameters differ by an element of
L^{\prime}
. This identifies
\Irr(\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}})/L^{\prime}
with
\frac{\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}}{L^{\prime}}=\Lambda(L^{\prime})
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
M_{\gamma}\in\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}
be simple. If
M_{\gamma}
is non-invertible, then there exists
a simple object
M_{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}}
such that

c_{M_{\alpha},M_{\gamma}}\circ c_{M_{\gamma},M_{\alpha}}\neq\mathrm{id}.

In particular, every simple object of
\mathcal{Z}_{(2)}(\mathcal{C}_{L^{\prime}})
is invertible.

Proof 5.4
.

As observed in the proof of Proposition
5.1
(c)
, one has

Q\subseteq\frac{\ell}{2}(L^{\prime})^{*}
.

Now let
v_{\gamma}
and
v_{\alpha_{i}}
be highest weight vectors of
M_{\gamma}
and
M_{\alpha_{i}}
.
Write the braiding as
\tau\circ\check{R}
with
\check{R}=\mathcal{H}\,\widetilde{R}
, where

\mathcal{H}
acts on weight vectors by
q^{\langle-,-\rangle}
and

\widetilde{R}=1+\sum_{\nu\in Q_{>0}}\sum_{s}c_{\nu,s}\,E_{\nu,s}\otimes F_{\nu,s}

is a PBW expansion with each
E_{\nu,s}
of positive root weight
\nu>0
. Since
v_{\gamma}
and

v_{\alpha_{i}}
are highest weight vectors, every nontrivial PBW term kills the first tensor factor, so

\widetilde{R}(v_{\gamma}\otimes v_{\alpha_{i}})=v_{\gamma}\otimes v_{\alpha_{i}},\qquad\widetilde{R}(v_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes v_{\gamma})=v_{\alpha_{i}}\otimes v_{\gamma}.

Therefore

\bigl(c_{M_{\alpha_{i}},M_{\gamma}}\circ c_{M_{\gamma},M_{\alpha_{i}}}\bigr)(v_{\gamma}\otimes v_{\alpha_{i}})=q^{2\langle\gamma,\alpha_{i}\rangle}(v_{\gamma}\otimes v_{\alpha_{i}})=q_{i}^{2\gamma(H_{i})}(v_{\gamma}\otimes v_{\alpha_{i}}).

If
M_{\gamma}
is non-invertible, then
\gamma(H_{i})\notin\frac{\ell}{2d_{i}}\mathbb{Z}
for some

Corollary 5.5
.

Assume that

is commutative.

(a)

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A_{L^{\prime}}}

(c)

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}^{\mathrm{loc}}_{A_{L^{\prime}}}

Proof 5.6
.

(a) By Theorem

(b), being ribbon is equivalent to

for all

.

5.2.2.
Braiding between simples

The braiding in
\mathcal{C}
is given by

c_{V,W}=\tau_{V,W}\circ\widetilde{R}_{V,W}\circ\Upsilon_{V,W},

where, for weight vectors
v\in V
of weight
\lambda
and
w\in W
of weight
\mu
, we have

\Upsilon_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=q^{-\lambda_{E}\mu_{G}-\lambda_{G}\mu_{E}}(v\otimes w),\qquad\tau_{V,W}(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{\bar{v}\,\bar{w}}(w\otimes v),

and
\widetilde{R}
is left multiplication by

1+(q-q^{-1})(X\otimes Y)(K\otimes K^{-1})
.

For the one-dimensional modules we have
X=Y=0
, hence
\widetilde{R}=\mathrm{id}
.
Thus for
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(nr/2,b)_{\bar{p}}
and
\varepsilon^{\prime}=\varepsilon(n^{\prime}r/2,b^{\prime})_{\bar{p}^{\prime}}

with basis vectors
v,v^{\prime}
, respectively, we obtain

(29)

c_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}(v\otimes v^{\prime})=(-1)^{\bar{p}\,\bar{p}^{\prime}}q^{\frac{(-nb^{\prime}-bn^{\prime})r}{2}}(v^{\prime}\otimes v).

In particular, the double braiding is scalar:

(30)

c_{\varepsilon^{\prime},\varepsilon}\circ c_{\varepsilon,\varepsilon^{\prime}}=q^{-(nb^{\prime}+n^{\prime}b)r}\,\mathrm{id}_{\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime}}=\mathrm{id}_{\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime}}.

Let
V=V(\alpha,a)_{\bar{p}}
be a
2
-dimensional quantum Kac module and let

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(nr/2,b)_{\bar{q}}
be
1
-dimensional with basis vector
w
.
Choose a homogeneous weight basis
\{v,v^{\prime}\}
of
V
satisfying (
28
).
Since
Xw=Yw=0
, the correction term in
\widetilde{R}
vanishes on both
V\otimes\varepsilon
and

\varepsilon\otimes V
, hence
\widetilde{R}=\mathrm{id}
in both cases. Therefore

\displaystyle c_{V,\varepsilon}(v\otimes w)=(-1)^{\bar{p}\,\bar{q}}\,q^{-\alpha b-a\frac{nr}{2}}\,(w\otimes v),\;\;

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,V}(w\otimes v)=(-1)^{\bar{p}\,\bar{q}}\,q^{-\frac{nr}{2}a-b\alpha}\,(v\otimes w),

\displaystyle c_{V,\varepsilon}(v^{\prime}\otimes w)=(-1)^{(\bar{p}+\bar{1})\,\bar{q}}\,q^{-\alpha b-(a-1)\frac{nr}{2}}\,(w\otimes v^{\prime}),\;\;

\displaystyle c_{\varepsilon,V}(w\otimes v^{\prime})=(-1)^{(\bar{p}+\bar{1})\,\bar{q}}\,q^{-\frac{nr}{2}(a-1)-b\alpha}\,(v^{\prime}\otimes w).

In particular, the parity signs cancel in the double braiding. Thus, the double braiding is diagonal on
the weight basis, and since
q^{r}=1
we get

(31)

c_{\varepsilon,V}\circ c_{V,\varepsilon}=q^{-2\alpha b}\,\mathrm{id}_{V\otimes\varepsilon}.

5.2.3.
Müger center of
\mathcal{C}

Theorem 5.7
.

The Müger center

.

of

5.2.4.
The group of invertibles

For

For

Finiteness of
\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}

\mathrm{fg}\text{-}\widehat{\mathcal{C}}_{A}^{\mathrm{loc}}

\Gamma=L\times\{\bar{0}\}\leq{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
with

, and let

.
The
\Gamma

-parameter modulo the

(

(

.

Proposition 5.11
.

Let
\Gamma\leq{\sf Inv}(\mathcal{C})
satisfy
\mathsf{q}|_{\Gamma}\equiv 1

, and let

.

By (

), every one-dimensional simple

\varepsilon(\frac{nr}{2},b)_{\bar{p}}
centralizes
\Gamma
, hence belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\Gamma}
.
The
\Gamma
-action on these simples is translation by

on

, so their

Appendix A
Non-braided version of Etingof-Penneys’ lemma

In Appendix
A
, by an
abelian monoidal category
, we mean an abelian category endowed with a structure of a monoidal category such that the monoidal product is additive and right exact in each variable.

A.1.
Etingof-Penneys’ Lemma

An object
X
of an abelian monoidal category
\mathcal{C}
is said to be
left flat
if the endofunctor
X\otimes(-)
on
\mathcal{C}
is exact. Similarly,
X
is said to be
right flat
if
(-)\otimes X
is exact. We say that
\mathcal{C}
has enough left (right) flat objects if every object of
\mathcal{C}
is a quotient of a left (right) flat object of
\mathcal{C}
. A
flat
object is a left and right flat object.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an abelian monoidal category, and let

0\to Y\xrightarrow{i}Z\xrightarrow{p}X\to 0
be a short exact sequence in
\mathcal{C}
.
Assume that
\mathcal{C}
has enough left flat objects and enough right flat objects.

(a)

If
X
and
Y
are left rigid and right flat, then
Z
is left rigid.

(b)

If
X
and
Z
are left rigid, then
Y
is left rigid.

(c)

If
Y
and
Z
are left rigid and
i^{*}
is an epimorphism, then
X
is left rigid.

This lemma was established by Etingof and Penneys under the assumption that
\mathcal{C}
is braided
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
. They also remarked that the use of the braiding is not essential
[
EP26
, Remark 4.4]
. One of the aims of this Appendix is to demonstrate that
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
holds in the above form in the absence of a braiding by tracing the original proof carefully.

As is well-known, a left (right) rigid object is left (right) flat (see Lemma
A.8
below). One technical difficulty in the non-braided case is that a left (right) rigid object need not be right (left) rigid. For example, given a left rigid object
Y
, a map between Yoneda
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}
groups induced by the functor
Y^{*}\otimes(-)
is considered in the proof of
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
. However, since the functor
Y^{*}\otimes(-)
is not necessarily exact in our non-braided setting, we will face some technical problems. For completeness, in §
A.4
, we discuss maps between Yoneda
\mathrm{Ext}^{1}
groups induced by an additive functor which is not necessarily exact.

Another point to note is that techniques of the Tor functor were used in the proof of
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
. Instead of justifying the use of the Tor functor in our setting, we have chosen to assume that
\mathcal{C}
has enough left flat objects and enough right flat objects, and to verify some necessary technical lemmas on flat objects in a direct way in §
A.3
.
The assumption on
\mathcal{C}
does not matter for our main application to the category of finitely generated modules over a central commutative algebra in a braided tensor category in §
3
, since every object of such a category is a quotient of a flat (in fact, rigid) object.

An important consequence of the above Lemma is:

Theorem A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be an abelian monoidal category in which every object has finite length. Assume that
\mathcal{C}
has enough left flat objects and enough right flat objects.
If every simple object of

is rigid.

.

It is obvious that the zero object of

is rigid. Let

\mathcal{C}

We prove the rigidity of

by induction on the length of

If

has length

, then

is simple and hence rigid by assumption.

Now let

have length

of length

Choose a short exact sequence

is rigid, and by assumption

is rigid.
In particular,

and

are flat (see Lemma

to this exact sequence shows that

is left rigid.
Applying Lemma

in the reversed monoidal category

A.2.
Lemmas on rigid objects

We first collect useful lemmas on rigid objects. The following lemma is found in the proof of
[
EP26
, Lemma 4.2]
:

Lemma A.4
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a monoidal category, let
L
and
R
be objects of
\mathcal{C}
, and let
\varepsilon:L\otimes R\to\mathbbm{1}
and
\eta:\mathbbm{1}\to R\otimes L
be morphisms in
\mathcal{C}
. Suppose that

\xi:=(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L}\otimes\eta)\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta:=(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ(\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})

are invertible in
\mathcal{C}
. Then we have

\varepsilon\circ(\xi^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})=\varepsilon\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L}\otimes\zeta^{-1}),\quad(\zeta^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L})\circ\eta=(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\xi^{-1})\circ\eta.

Let
\varepsilon^{\prime}
and
\eta^{\prime}
be both sides of the former and the latter equation, respectively. Then the triples
(L,\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta)
and
(L,\varepsilon,\eta^{\prime})
are left dual objects of
R
.

Proof A.5
.

By the functorial property of
\otimes
, it is easy to verify

\varepsilon\circ(\xi\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})=(\varepsilon\otimes\varepsilon)\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L}\otimes\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})=\varepsilon\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L}\otimes\zeta).

By composing
\xi^{-1}\otimes\zeta^{-1}
from the right, we obtain the first equation of the statement. The second one is proved in a similar way. Now we have

\displaystyle(\varepsilon^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{L}\otimes\eta)=(\varepsilon\otimes\mathrm{id}_{L})\circ(\xi^{-1}\otimes\eta)=\xi\circ\xi^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{L},

\displaystyle(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\varepsilon^{\prime})\circ(\eta\otimes\mathrm{id}_{R})=(\mathrm{id}_{R}\otimes\varepsilon)\circ(\eta\otimes\zeta^{-1})=\zeta\circ\zeta^{-1}=\mathrm{id}_{R},

which means that
(L,\varepsilon^{\prime},\eta)
is a left dual object of
R
.
The case of
(L,\varepsilon,\eta^{\prime})
is proved in a similar way.

Let
A
and
B
be objects of
\mathcal{C}
, and let

be a morphism in
\mathcal{C}
. To discuss the rigidity of

from that of

or its converse, we note the following lemma: Let

and

\mathcal{C}

and

be morphisms in

\varepsilon_{B}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes f)=\varepsilon_{A}\circ(f^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A}),\quad(f\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ\eta_{A}=(\mathrm{id}_{B}\otimes f^{\prime})\circ\eta_{B}.

.

In the above situation, we have

\displaystyle f\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\varepsilon_{A})\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})=(\mathrm{id}_{B}\otimes\varepsilon_{B})\circ(\eta_{B}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B})\circ f,

\displaystyle(\varepsilon_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A^{\prime}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A^{\prime}}\otimes\eta_{A})\circ f^{\prime}=f^{\prime}\circ(\varepsilon_{B}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{B^{\prime}})\circ(\mathrm{id}_{B^{\prime}}\otimes\eta_{B}).

The first equation is proved as follows:

\displaystyle f\circ(\mathrm{id}_{A}\otimes\varepsilon_{A})\circ(\eta_{A}\otimes\mathrm{id}_{A})

First, we discuss the naturality of

Z
are left rigid, and that
i^{*}:Z^{*}\to Y^{*}
is epic in
\mathcal{C}
.

We define
X^{\vee}=\mathrm{Ker}(i^{*})
and denote by
\iota:X^{\vee}\to Z^{*}
the inclusion morphism. Since
i^{*}
is epic, we have an exact sequence
0\to X^{\vee}\xrightarrow{\iota}Z^{*}\xrightarrow{i^{*}}Y^{*}\to 0
. We note that
Y^{*}
is right flat since it has a right dual object
Y
. Thus, by Lemma
A.12
, we have the following exact sequence:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes X^{\vee}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes Z^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X\otimes Y^{*}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0.}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes i^{*}}

Since
(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes i^{*})(p\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z^{*}})\mathrm{coev}_{Z}=0
, the morphism
(p\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z^{*}})\mathrm{coev}_{Z}
factors through the kernel of
\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes i^{*}
, which is equal to the image of
\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\iota
. Since
\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\iota
is monic, we have a morphism
\eta_{X}:\mathbbm{1}\to X\otimes X^{\vee}
such that

(59)

(\mathrm{id}_{X}\otimes\iota)\circ\eta_{X}=(p\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z^{*}})\circ\mathrm{coev}_{Z}.

There is also an exact sequence
X^{\vee}\otimes Y\to X^{\vee}\otimes Z\to X^{\vee}\otimes X\to 0
. By the same argument as the proof of Part (2), we have a morphism
\varepsilon_{X}:X^{\vee}\otimes X\to\mathbbm{1}
in
\mathcal{C}
satisfying the following equation:

(60)

\varepsilon_{X}\circ(\mathrm{id}_{X^{\vee}}\otimes p)=\mathrm{ev}_{Z}\circ(\iota\otimes\mathrm{id}_{Z}).

By (
59
), (
60
) and Lemma
A.6
, we conclude that
(X^{\vee},\varepsilon_{X},\eta_{X})
is a left dual object of
X
. The proof is done.
