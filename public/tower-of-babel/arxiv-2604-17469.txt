Ergodic properties of the harmonic process
Frank Redig, Berend van Tol

Abstract
In this paper we study detailed fluctuation results for a class of non-equilibrium steady states. The main example is the boundary driven harmonic model [ 10 ] . In this model, the non-equilibrium steady state (NESS) is a mixture of products of geometric distributions, of which the local parameters are in turn distributed as uniform order statistics. For such a NESS, we prove law of large numbers, central limit theorem and large deviation results for fields of a general local functions (generalizing the density field). We also obtain quantitative results on the deviation from local equilibrium.



1
Introduction

Understanding the microscopic and emergent macroscopic properties of non-equilibrium steady states (NESS) is an important challenge in mathematical statistical physics. At present only for a limited class of boundary driven models one can access microscopic details of the NESS such as the expected profile, correlation functions and large deviation rate functions. Important examples include boundary driven stochastic systems such as the (a)symmetric exclusion process
[
6
]
which is exactly solvable via the matrix ansatz solution. More recently, the harmonic model and a more general class of exactly solvable models were introduced in
[
10
,
9
]
.
In these boundary-driven generalized harmonic models, the equilibrium measures are product measures, and in the presence of boundary reservoirs the NESS can be written as a mixture of product measures with equilibrium marginals. In the simplest setting of the harmonic process on a chain
\{1,\ldots,N\}
with left (resp. right) boundary reservoir parameter
\theta_{L}
(resp.
\theta_{R}>\theta_{L}
), this NESS is a product of geometric distributions, where the parameters of the geometric distributions are themselves distributed as order statistics of independent uniforms on the interval
[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]
, see
[
4
,
12
,
3
]
. Therefore, in that setting the measure describing the mixture is explicitly given as the joint distribution of those uniform order statistics. The fact that the NESS can be written as a mixture of product measures with equilibrium marginals holds for a much larger class of models on general graphs, including, e.g., the boundary driven KMP model, see
[
12
,
5
]
. However, at present, only in the boundary driven generalized harmonic models on a chain with reservoirs at the left and right ends, the measure which describes this mixture is known in closed form. As a consequence, in those models many detailed properties of the corresponding NESS can be derived, including a large deviation principle for the density profile
[
3
]
with the rate function predicted from macroscopic fluctuation theory
[
2
,
1
]
.

In this paper we revisit the boundary driven harmonic model,
and from the representation of the NESS as a mixture of product states, we derive several detailed results about fluctuations of the density field and more general fields associated to local functions. Roughly speaking, these results follow from a combination of precise fluctuation results for product measures with slowly varying parameters, combined with precise fluctuation results for order statistics. With minor modifications, these results hold for the entire one-parameter family of boundary driven
(generalized) harmonic models and exactly solvable heat conduction models
[
8
]
. Indeed, for all these models, the NESS can be described as a mixture of product measures, where the measure which describes the mixtures is expressed in terms of generalized order statistics (Dirichlet process).

In summary, in our paper we obtain the following main results for the NESS of the boundary driven harmonic model.

1.

Strong law of large numbers for the profile of a local function.

2.

Central limit theorem for the profile of a local function.

3.

Local equilibrium and
deviations from local equilibrium for finite system size.

4.

Large deviations for the empirical profile of a local function.

The remainder of our paper is organized as follows.
First, we define the precise mixture of product states which we study (Section 2). Second, we prove fluctuation results for order statistics, i.e., the random parameters (Section 3).
We then use these fluctuation results to prove the results announced above.
More precisely, in Section 4 we prove local equilibrium (in the large system-size limit
N\to\infty
) and quantitative deviations from it for finite system size
N
, in Section 5 we prove the law of large numbers for fields of general local functions, in Section 6 we prove central limit theorems for the fluctuation fields of general local functions, and finally in Section 7 we prove large deviations for
fields of general (bounded) local functions.

2
Mixed product states

In this section we describe the mixed product states which we study, where we focus on mixtures of geometric distributions, inspired by the stationary measure of the boundary driven harmonic model

[
10
]
.
We consider particle systems on a finite chain

\{1,\ldots,N\}
with left and right boundary reservoirs. The configuration space of such a particle system is given by

\Omega_{N}={\mathbb{N}}^{\{1,\ldots,N\}}
where
{\mathbb{N}}=\{0,1,2,\ldots\}
. For
\eta\in\Omega_{N}
, and
i\in\{1,\ldots,N\}
,
\eta_{i,N}
represents the number of particles at lattice site
i
.
Let
\nu_{\theta}
denote a probability measure on
{\mathbb{N}}
, parametrized by a parameter
\theta\in[0,\infty)
. The main example considered in this paper is
the geometric distribution, which we parametrize as follows:

\nu_{\theta}(n)=\frac{1}{1+\theta}\Big(\frac{\theta}{1+\theta}\Big)^{n}.

(1)

Notice that in this parametrization, the parameter
\theta
coincides with the expectation, i.e.,

\sum_{n}n\nu_{\theta}(n)=\theta.

Then we can define associated product measures on the configuration space
\Omega_{N}
given by

\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\theta(i)}
.
Here
\theta:\{1,\ldots,N\}\to[0,\infty)
denotes what we call the profile of local parameters.
We call a measure
\mu
a mixture of product measures with geometric marginals if it is of the form

\mu(\eta)=\mathbb{E}\left(\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\Theta_{i,N}}[\eta_{i,N}]\right).

(2)

Here
\Theta_{i,N},i=1,\ldots,N
is a collection of non-negative random variables which we call the random (local) parameters.
The expectation
\mathbb{E}
in (
2
) is over these random parameters

\Theta_{i,N},i=1,\ldots,N
.
Equivalently,
\mu
is a mixture of product measures of the form
\nu_{\theta}
if it is of the form

\mu(\eta)=\int\dots\int\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\theta_{i,N}}[\eta_{i,N}]\Lambda(d\theta_{1,N}\ldots d\theta_{N,N})

(3)

where
\Lambda(d\theta_{1,N}\ldots d\theta_{N,N})
is a probability measure on
(a subset of)
[0,\infty)^{N}
, which corresponds in the notation (
2
) to the joint distribution of the random parameters
\Theta_{i,N},i=1,\ldots,N
. This measure

\Lambda(d\theta_{1,N}\ldots d\theta_{N,N})

is the measure which describes the mixture, and is called the “mixture measure”.

Motivated by the NESS in the harmonic model,
[
4
,
3
]
, in this paper we restrict to mixtures of products of geometric distributions, where the parameters are distributed as order statistics. That is, the random parameters
\{\Theta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
are defined as follows. Let
\theta_{L},\theta_{R}\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
be such that
\theta_{L}<\theta_{R}
. These parameters,
\theta_{L}
and
\theta_{R}
, correspond to the boundary parameters of the Harmonic models. We define
N
independent, uniformly distributed random variables on
[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]
:

U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{1},U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{2}\dots U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{N}\sim\text{Unif}([\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]).

(4)

The parameters
\Theta_{1,N},..,\Theta_{N,N}
are then the order statistics of these
N
uniform random variables
\{U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
, i.e., for
i\in\{1,...,N\}
,

\Theta_{i,N}:=U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{i:N},

(5)

with
U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{i:N}
the
i
-th order statistic of
U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{1},U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{2},\dots,U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{N}
. Notice that one can construct
\{U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{i:N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
from independent standard uniform random variables
U_{1},U_{2},\dots,U_{N}
via

U^{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}_{i:N}:=\theta_{L}+(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})U_{i:N}

(6)

with
U_{1},...,U_{N}\sim U([0,1])
and
U_{i:N}
the
i
-th order statistic.

We further denote by
\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}:[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
the function

\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x):=[\theta_{L}+(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})x]\mathds{1}(0\leq x\leq 1).

(7)

R
EMARK
2.1
.

In what follows, we will often prove results about the mixed measure (
3
) and the
corresponding random variable
\eta_{i,N},\Theta_{i,N},i=1,\ldots,N
.
We think of these random variables as defined on a common underlying probability space
(jointly for all values of
N
). This implies that we can speak about almost sure asymptotic results
(as
N\to\infty
).

3
Fluctuation properties of the random parameters

We start this section with a basic result on moments of uniform order statistics,
which are known in closed form (see e.g. equation 2.1 in
[
14
]
). Proposition
3.1
below states this explicit expression, which we will use occasionally throughout this text.

P
ROPOSITION
3.1
.

Let
\alpha_{1},...,\alpha_{N}\in\mathbb{R}
. We have

\mathbb{E}(U_{1:N}^{\alpha_{1}}\cdot U_{2:N}^{\alpha_{2}}\dots U_{N:N}^{\alpha_{N}})=\Gamma(N+1)\left\{\prod_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\dots\alpha_{j}+j)}{\Gamma(\alpha_{1}+\dots\alpha_{j}+j+1)}\right\}.

(8)

In particular, we have the following expression for the
k
-th moment,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, of
U_{r:N}
,
r\in\{1,\dots,N\}
.

\mathbb{E}(U_{r:N}^{k})=\frac{r(r+1)\dots(r+k-1)}{(N+1)(N+2)\dots(N+k)}.

(9)

In the rest of this section we state and prove several ergodic results for the order statistics
\{\Theta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
, which we call from now on “the random parameters” (in the spirit of section 2). More precisely, in Proposition
3.2
we prove a strong law of large numbers (SLLN) and in Proposition
3.3
the central limit theorem (CLT). These results are essentially the SLLN and CLT in chapter 19 of
[
16
]
extended to hold for local functions of the random parameters. More precisely, the SLLN and CLT in
[
16
]
hold for general L-statistics, i.e. random variables of the form

\sum_{i=1}^{N}h(\Theta_{i,N})\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right),

(10)

for some appropriate functions
\varphi
and
h
on
\mathbb{R}
. In this paper we are interested in sums of local functions of the random parameters:

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}h(\Theta_{i+1},\dots,\Theta_{i+k})\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

(11)

\displaystyle=h(\Theta_{1},\dots,\Theta_{k})\varphi\left(\frac{1}{N}\right)+\dots+h(\Theta_{N-2k+1},\dots,\Theta_{N-k})\varphi\left(\frac{N-k}{N}\right).

In later sections we use these results to prove the corresponding statements for the random variables

\{\eta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
with distribution given in (
2
).

Here and in the rest of this paper we define the operator
\tau_{i}
acting on local functions
f(x)=f(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})
as

(\tau_{i}f)[x]:=f(x_{i+1},\dots,x_{i+k})
. Let
E
be either
E={\mathbb{N}}
or
E=\mathbb{R}
. Throughout this text we call for fixed
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
the function
f:E^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
, with
N\geq k
, a local function with dependence set
\{1,2,\ldots,k\}
if
f(x)
only depends on
x_{1},\ldots,x_{k}
. That is, with slight abuse of notation,
f(x)=f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{k})
.

In subsection
3.3
we state and prove a concentration inequality for the random parameters which will later be used to prove local equilibrium for
\{\eta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
. Finally we state a large deviation theorem for the random parameters in subsection
3.4
, from which we will later again find corresponding results for the
\{\eta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
random variables.

3.1
Law of large numbers for the random parameters

P
ROPOSITION
3.2

(SLLN for the random parameters)
.

Let
\varphi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be differentiable with bounded derivative and let
h:[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be both continuous and local with dependence set
\{1,\dots,k\}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then,

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\rightarrow\int_{0}^{1}h(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))\varphi(x)dx

(12)

almost surely as
N\rightarrow\infty
. Here it is understood that for a real number
\rho\in[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]
we write
h(\rho)
to denote

h(\rho,\rho,\ldots,\rho)^{N}.

(13)

That is,
h(\rho)
is the value of
h
acting on
(\rho,\rho,\ldots,\rho)\in[\theta_{l},\theta_{R}]^{N}
.

P
ROOF
.  It is sufficient to show the result for the case where
h
is a monomial of the form
h(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdot x_{2}^{p_{2}}\dots x_{k}^{p_{k}}
,
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, since the polynomials are dense in
C([\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]^{k})
. Assume wlog that for all
x\in[0,1]:

\varphi(x)\geq 0
.

Theorem 3 in Chapter 19 of
[
16
]
gives that for
p=p_{1}+p_{2}+\dots+p_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)\rightarrow\int_{0}^{1}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))^{p}\varphi(x)dx

(14)

Furthermore, by the definition of order statistics, we have that the random parameters
\{\Theta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
, are monotone in
i
. Hence we almost surely have

\Theta_{1,N}^{p}\leq h(\Theta)=\Theta_{1,N}^{p_{1}}\Theta_{2,N}^{p_{2}}\dots\Theta_{k,N}^{p_{k}}\leq\Theta_{k,N}^{p}.

(15)

As a consequence

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)\leq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i+1,N}^{p_{1}}\Theta_{i+2,N}^{p_{2}}\dots\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p_{k}})\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

(16)

and

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i+k,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)\geq\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i+1,N}^{p_{1}}\Theta_{i+2,N}^{p_{2}}\dots\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p_{k}})\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right).

(17)

Using (
14
) it is straightforward to see that the left hand side of (
16
) and the left hand side of (
17
) both converge almost surely to the same limit:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i+k,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=k}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i+k}{N}\right)

(18)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=k}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\left[\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)+\frac{k}{N}\varphi^{\prime}\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)+O(N^{-2})\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)+O(N^{-1})\xrightarrow{a.s.}\int_{0}^{1}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))^{p}\varphi(x)dx

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)+O(N^{-1})

(19)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{a.s.}\int_{0}^{1}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))^{p}\varphi(x)dx.

We conclude that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\Theta_{i+1,N}^{p_{1}}\Theta_{i+2,N}^{p_{2}}\dots\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p_{k}})

\displaystyle\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}(\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)

(20)

\displaystyle\xrightarrow{a.s.}\int_{0}^{1}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))^{p}\varphi(x)dx=\int_{0}^{1}h(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x)\mathds{1})\varphi(x)dx.

\;\qed

3.2
Central limit theorem for the random parameters

P
ROPOSITION
3.3

(CLT for the random parameters)
.

Let
\varphi:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be be a continuous function and let
h:[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be differentiable and local with dependence set
\{1,\dots,k\}\subset{\mathbb{N}}
,
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Then

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}\Big((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]-\mathbb{E}((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta])\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})

(21)

Here,
\sigma_{T}^{2}
is given by

\sigma_{T}^{2}=(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[s\wedge t-st]\varphi(s)\varphi(t)h^{\prime}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(s))h^{\prime}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(t))dsdt.

(22)

with
h^{\prime}:[\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
defined as

h^{\prime}(\theta)=\left[\frac{\partial}{\partial t}h(t,\dots,t)\right]_{t=\theta}.

(23)

P
ROOF
.  It is sufficient to show the result for the case where
h
is a monomial of the form
h(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})=x_{1}^{p_{1}}\cdot x_{2}^{p_{2}}\dots x_{k}^{p_{k}}
,
p_{1},\dots,p_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
, since the polynomials are dense in
C([\theta_{L},\theta_{R}]^{k})
. Assume wlog that for all
x\in[0,1]:

\varphi(x)\geq 0
. We want to show that

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}\Big((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]-\mathbb{E}((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta])\Big)

(24)

is asymptotically distributed as
\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})
with

\displaystyle\sigma_{T}^{2}

\displaystyle=(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[s\wedge t-st]\varphi(s)\varphi(t)h^{\prime}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(s))h^{\prime}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(t))dsdt

(25)

\displaystyle=p^{2}\int_{0}^{1}\int_{0}^{1}[s\wedge t-st](\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(s))^{p-1}(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(t))^{p-1}\varphi(s)\varphi(t)dsdt.

and
p=p_{1}+p_{2}+\dots+p_{k}\in{\mathbb{N}}
. Theorem 1 in Chapter 19 of
[
16
]
gives that

\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{N}(\Theta_{i,N})^{p}\varphi\left(\frac{i}{N}\right)-\int_{0}^{1}h(\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}(x))\varphi(x)dx\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2}).

(26)

Furthermore, by the definition of order statistics, we have that the random parameters
\{\Theta_{i,N}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
, are monotone in
i
. Hence we almost surely have

\Theta_{1,N}^{p}\leq h(\Theta)=\Theta_{1,N}^{p_{1}}\Theta_{2,N}^{p_{2}}\dots\Theta_{k,N}^{p_{k}}\leq\Theta_{k,N}^{p}.

(27)

As a consequence (
24
) has the following lower and upper bound:

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}\Big((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]-\mathbb{E}((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta])\Big)

(28)

and

\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\geq\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=0}^{N-k}\Big((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta]-\mathbb{E}((\tau_{i}h)[\Theta])\Big)

(29)

.

We claim that

\Big|\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p})-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})\Big|=O(N^{-1})

(30)

Provided that this claim holds, we can use (
26
) to show that both the left hand side of (
29
) and the left hand side of (
28
) are asymptotically distributed as a centered normal with variance
\sigma_{T}^{2}
:

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)=

(31)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})+O(N^{-1})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})

and

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)=

(32)

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\Big(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p})+O(N^{-1})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)=

\displaystyle\lim_{N\rightarrow\infty}\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{i=k}^{N}\Big(\Theta_{i,N}^{p}-\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})+O(N^{-1})\Big)\varphi\Big(\frac{i}{N}\Big)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\text{d}}}{{=}}\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2}).

Because both the upper and the lower bound of (
24
) are asymptotically distributed as a
\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})
we can conclude that (
24
) is asymptotically distributed as a
\mathscr{N}(0,\sigma_{T}^{2})
as well.

To prove the claim in (
30
), we use Proposition
3.1
to calculate
\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N}^{p})
and
\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i+k,N}^{p})
explicitly. For
j\in\{1,\dots,N\}
.

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{j,N}^{p})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\left((\theta_{L}+(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})U_{j:N})^{p}\right)

(33)

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}\theta_{L}^{p-l}(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{l}\mathbb{E}(U_{j:N}^{l})

\displaystyle=\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}\theta_{L}^{p-l}(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{l}\frac{j(j+1)\dots(j+l-1)}{(N+1)(N+2)\dots(N+l)}.

We see that the left hand side of (
30
) becomes

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}\theta_{L}^{p-l}(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{l}\frac{(i+k)(i+k+1)\dots(i+k+l-1)-i(i+1)\dots(i+l-1)}{(N+1)(N+2)\dots(N+l)}\right|

(34)

\displaystyle=\left|\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}\theta_{L}^{p-l}(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{l}\frac{O(i^{l-1})}{(N+1)(N+2)\dots(N+l)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{l=1}^{p}\binom{p}{l}\theta_{L}^{p-l}(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})^{l}\frac{O(N^{l-1})}{(N+1)(N+2)\dots(N+l)}\right|=O(N^{-1}).

\;\qed

3.3
Concentration for the random parameters

In Section
4
we will show that the measures
\mu_{N}
satisfy
local equilibrium
. This entails that
\mu_{N}
look locally like a product measure if one takes the limit
N\rightarrow\infty
. This property is mathematically formulated in
Definition
4.1
. The reason local equilibrium holds for
(\mu_{N})_{N\geq 1}
is that the random variables
\Theta_{i,N}
concentrate sufficiently fast on their expectation as
N\rightarrow\infty
:

\Theta_{i,N}\approx\mathbb{E}(\Theta_{i,N})=\theta_{L}+(\theta_{R}-\theta_{L})\frac{i}{N+1}=\rho_{\theta_{L},\theta_{R}}\left(\frac{i}{N+1}\right).

(35)

Moreover, the random parameters concentrate
uniformly
. This is the content of Proposition
3.4
.

P
ROPOSITION
3.4
.

Let
\epsilon>0
and define
\Delta_{i,N}:=|\Theta_{i,N}-\mathbb{E}[\Theta_{i,N}]|
. Then

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\in\{1,...,N\}}\Delta_{i,N}\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}N}.

(36)

P
ROOF
.  Notice that

\mathbb{P}\left(\sup_{i\in\{1,...,N\}}\Delta_{i,N}\geq\epsilon\right)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(\Delta_{i,N}\geq\epsilon)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{1}{\epsilon^{2}}\text{Var}(\Theta_{i,N}).

(37)

For the last step we use the Markov inequality and the fact that
\mathbb{E}[\Delta_{i,N}^{2}]=\text{Var}(\Theta_{i,N})
. Since the
k^{th}
order statistic of
N
uniform random variables follows a
\text{Beta}(k,N+1-k)
distribution, we have

\text{Var}(\Theta_{i,N})=\frac{i(N+1-i)}{(N+1)^{2}(N+2)^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{N^{2}}.

(38)

Substitution in the bound above concludes the proof.

\;\qed

We define the sample paths associated to the random variables

In

4
Local equilibrium

In this section we show that the mixture measures
\mu_{N}
satisfy local equilibrium. Before we do so, we first formally introduce the notion of local equilibrium in Definition
4.1
. Intuitively, local equilibrium should be understood as follows. Typically, the interacting particle systems which we consider have homogeneous equilibrium (i.e., reversible) product measures of the form
\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\lambda_{\rho}
with parameter
\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. Then the system satisfies local equilibrium if
\mu_{N}
looks like a product measure with parameter
\rho(x)
around each macroscopic point
x
under the scaling
N\rightarrow\infty
.

D
EFINITION
4.1

(Local Equilibrium)
.

Let
\rho:[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a non-negative continuous function and let
\{\lambda\}_{\theta\geq 0}
be a family of probability measures on
{\mathbb{N}}
indexed by parameter
\theta\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. We say that the sequence of probability measures
\mu_{N}
on
{\mathbb{N}}^{N}
,
N\in{\mathbb{N}}
, is local equilibrium with density profile
\rho
and marginal
\lambda_{\rho}
if

\lim_{N\to\infty}\int(\tau_{\lfloor xN\rfloor}g(\eta))\ d\mu_{N}(\eta)=\int g(\eta)\otimes_{i=1}^{k}d\lambda_{\rho(x)}(\eta_{k})

(42)

for every integrable local function
g:{\mathbb{N}}^{N}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
with dependence set
\{1,\dots,k\}
.

In Subsection
4.1
below, we first show how local equilibrium is related to the concentration of the random parameters as proved in Proposition
3.4
. Next we prove a quantitative version of local equilibrium in terms of the self-duality polynomials associated to Harmonic processes. This is done in Subsection
4.2
.

4.1
Local equilibrium from the concentration of the random parameters

If the function
g
in Definition
4.1
is a monomial, then (
42
) reduces (
47
) to in Theorem
4.1
. Once one has proven local equilibrium for monomial

, it is straightforward to extend the result general local functions. By conditioning on the random parameters, the left hand side of (

(43)

The uniform concentration in Proposition
3.4
allows us to replace the random parameters by their expectation as is stated in Lemma
4.1
. The local equilibrium property then follows automatically, since
\mu_{N}
becomes a product measure if we replace the random parameters by something deterministic.

L
EMMA
4.1
.

For all

4.2
Quantitative local equilibrium and self-duality polynomials

If the function
g
is the

associated to the harmonic process, then we show the following quantitative version of local equilibrium:

\int(\tau_{\lfloor xN\rfloor}g(\eta))\ d\mu_{N}(\eta)-\int g(\eta)\otimes_{i=1}^{k}d\lambda_{\rho(x)}(\eta_{k})=O(1/N)

D

4.2

The function

L
EMMA
4.2
.

Conditioned on the random parameters the expectation of

P
ROOF
.  The statement of the lemma follows from straightforward computation. Let

and

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[{\eta_{1,N}\choose k}\Big|\Theta_{1,N}\right]=

Equation (

\;\qed

5
Law of large numbers for fields of local functions

In this section we will study fields associated to a general local function, and show their convergence to a deterministic limit.
The density field

6
Central limit theorem for fluctuation fields of local functions

In this section we look at the fluctuation fields, i.e., the fluctuations around the non-equilibrium steady state profile on central limit scale.
In equilibrium, the stationary state is reversible and is a product measure, and as a consequence these fluctuation fields converge to white noise.
In non-equilibrium (i.e., different reservoir parameters

\theta_{L}\not=\theta_{R}
), there is a white noise contribution (corresponding to the local equilibrium approximation of the stationary state), and a contribution coming from the fluctuation of the random parameters, which are responsible for the long-range correlations in the NESS.
As we will see below, the latter behaves as a Brownian bridge (cf. also (
3.3
)).

Let

Large deviations for fields of local functions

In
[
3
]
we proved a large deviation principle for the density field in the NESS of the generalized harmonic models, with a rate function closely related to the rate function obtained in
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
. In this section we generalize this large deviation result to the field of a local function.
The idea is that under a product measure of the form

the field of a local function

\theta

Therefore, because the profile of the

7.1

Let

unbounded functions such as

With a slide abuse of notation
we denote by

the homogeneous product measure
with geometric marginals with parameter

(cf. (

where, in turn, the large deviation free energy

F(\theta,\lambda;g)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\mathbb{E}_{\nu_{\theta}}e^{\lambda\sum_{i=1}^{N-k}\tau_{i}g}.

(114)

The existence of the limit (

[
11

The following proposition generalizes this statement for
inhomogeneous product measures with a given profile of parameters.
More precisely, consider
a continuous function
\Theta:[0,1]\to\mathbb{R}^{+}

\nu_{\Theta}=\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\Theta(i/(N+1))}

Then we have the following.

P

7.2

Notice that the precise form
\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\Theta(i/N)}

7.1
) can be relaxed.
Indeed if the product measure is of the form

\otimes_{i=1}^{N}\nu_{\theta_{N}(i)}
where
\theta_{N}(i)
is such that its associated sample path

\Theta_{N}(t):=\theta_{N}(\lfloor tN\rfloor)

converges to a limit

in the Skorokhod topology (of cadlag paths

7.1

7.1

3.5

Proposition

]
