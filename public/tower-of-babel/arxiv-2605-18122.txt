Spectral energy-loss bump and γ \gamma -ray pulsar halos Journal: High Energy Astrophysics
Kun Fang

Abstract
LHAASO J0248 + + 6021, a possible γ \gamma -ray pulsar halo associated with PSR J0248 + + 6021 (J0248), exhibits a highly curved spectrum as revealed by LHAASO and Fermi-LAT measurements. We propose a direct interpretation of this large curvature: the energy-loss bump in the parent electron spectrum has not yet significantly departed from the high-energy cutoff. This requires either that the ambient magnetic field strength B B around J0248 be lower than the typical value in the interstellar medium, or that the electron injection age be significantly shorter than the pulsar characteristic age. For the much older Geminga pulsar, the expected energy-loss bump in its γ \gamma -ray halo spectrum has shifted below 100 ​ GeV 100\ \text{GeV} , in excellent agreement with Fermi-LAT measurements. Thus, the broadband spectra of young and old pulsar halos find a unified interpretation in the picture of a time-dependent energy-loss bump. Meanwhile, the spectral measurements of LHAASO J0248 + + 6021 only constrain the combination of B B and electron injection age. The uncertainty in B B leads to an order-of-magnitude variation in the fitted diffusion coefficient. Future X-ray observations are expected to break the degeneracies.

2
Initial fit of the LHAASO J0248+6021 Spectrum

Once electrons are injected from the PWN into the ISM, their transport can be described by the diffusion-loss equation, written as

\frac{\partial N}{\partial t}=\nabla\cdot(D\nabla N)+\frac{\partial(bN)}{\partial E_{e}}+Q\ ,

(1)

where
N
is the differential number density of electrons,
E_{e}
is the electron kinetic energy,
b\equiv|dE_{e}/dt|
is the absolute energy-loss rate,
Q
is the source function, and
D
is the diffusion coefficient, which takes the form of
D(E_{e})=D_{100}(E_{e}/100\ \text{TeV})^{\delta}
. We employ
phect

(
Fang 2026
)
, a computation tool developed for pulsar halos, to solve Eq. (
1
) and obtain the pulsar halo emission. The
StdDiff_N
model is adopted in this work, which assumes spherically symmetric diffusion.

The energy-loss rate can be expressed as
b(E_{e})=b_{0}E_{e}^{2}=(b_{0,\mathrm{syc}}+b_{0,\mathrm{ics}})E_{e}^{2}
. The first term corresponds to the synchrotron loss, where
b_{0,\mathrm{syc}}
is a constant proportional to the magnetic field energy density, i.e.,
b_{0,\mathrm{syc}}\propto B^{2}
. The second term corresponds to the ICS loss, where
b_{0,\mathrm{ics}}
is energy dependent due to the Klein-Nishina (KN) effect
(
Fang et al. 2021
)
.

The source function
Q(E_{e},r,t)
can be decomposed into an energy component
q_{E}
, a spatial component
q_{r}
, and a temporal component
q_{t}
. Under the point-source assumption,
q_{r}
represents a Dirac function. The energy term, or say the electron injection spectrum, takes the form of

q_{E}(E_{e})\propto E_{e}^{-p}\ {\rm exp}\left[-\left(\frac{E_{e}}{E_{e,c}}\right)^{2}\right]\ ,

(2)

which is suggested by the relativistic shock acceleration theory
(
Dempsey and Duffy 2007
)
.

The temporal variation of electron injection from the PWN is assumed to follow that of the pulsar spin-down luminosity
\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}
, as

q_{t}(t)\propto\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}(t)=\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}\ (\tau)\frac{\left(1+\frac{t}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{-2}}{\left(1+\frac{\tau}{\tau_{0}}\right)^{-2}}=\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}(\tau)\ \tau_{c}^{2}\ (t+\tau_{0})^{-2}\ ,

(3)

where
t=0
represents the pulsar birth time,
\tau
is the true age of the pulsar, and
\tau_{0}
is the initial spin-down timescale. The current spin-down luminosity
\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}(\tau)=2.1\times 10^{35}\ \text{erg}\ \text{s}^{-1}
and characteristic age
\tau_{c}=62.4\ \text{kyr}
are given by the ATNF catalog
(
Manchester et al. 2005
)
. The validity of Eq. (
3
) depends on the pulsar braking index being
3
, which leads to the relation
\tau=\tau_{c}-\tau_{0}

(
Gaensler and Slane 2006
)
.

The electron escape process from the PWN may not begin immediately at the pulsar birth time
(
Hinton et al. 2011
)
. If we consider an injection delay of

\tau_{\text{dl}}
and define
\tilde{t}=0
as the initial escaping time, Eq. (
3
) is then rewritten as

q_{t}(\tilde{t})\propto\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}(\tau)\ \tau_{c}^{2}\ (\tilde{t}+\tau_{\text{dl}}+\tau_{0})^{-2}=\dot{E}_{\text{sd}}(\tau)\ \tau_{c}^{2}\ (\tilde{t}+\tilde{\tau}_{0})^{-2}\ ,

(4)

where we define
\tilde{\tau}_{0}\equiv\tau_{\text{dl}}+\tau_{0}
and refer to it as the effective initial spin-down timescale. The actual duration of electron injection, or say the electron injection age, is
\tau-\tau_{\text{dl}}=\tau_{c}-\tilde{\tau}_{0}
. The form of Eq. (
4
) is identical to that of Eq. (
3
), so the
phect
computation can be applied directly, with the understanding that the variable meanings have changed.

We perform a
\chi^{2}
fitting to the LHAASO and Fermi-LAT spectra of LHAASO J0248
+
6021. The
95\%
flux upper limits (ULs) in the data are treated in a relaxed manner: when the model lies above (below) an UL, it contributes a value of
4
(
0
) to the
\chi^{2}
statistic. The UL at the highest energy from LHAASO and those below
100\ \text{GeV}
from Fermi-LAT do not provide strong constraints on the model and are therefore not included in the fitting procedure.

Energy-loss bump in electron and
\gamma
-ray spectra

The left panel of Fig.
2
shows the spatially integrated parent-electron spectrum of a pulsar halo at various injection ages. For each injection age
\tilde{t}
, there is a critical energy
E_{e,\text{cr}}
satisfying
\tilde{t}=\int_{E_{e,\text{cr}}}^{\infty}dE_{e}^{\prime}/b(E_{e}^{\prime})
. All electrons injected at time zero have now descended to energies below
E_{e,\text{cr}}
. We define
E_{e}<E_{e,\text{cr}}
as the injection regime, where the spectrum remains largely unaffected by energy losses and still retains the form of the injected spectrum;
E_{e}>E_{e,\text{cr}}
as the energy
-
loss regime, where the spectrum has been significantly affected by radiative losses.

A spectral bump emerges around
E_{e,\text{cr}}
due to the squeezing effect caused by the nonlinear energy losses. In other words, higher-energy electrons injected at the earliest times accumulate just below
E_{e,\text{cr}}
at time
\tilde{t}
. The shape of the bump is affected by Eq. (
4
), for which we assume
\tilde{\tau}_{0}=10\ \text{kyr}
in this section. Note that Eq. (
1
) adopts a continuous approximation for energy losses. If the discrete nature of ICS is taken into account, the actual spectral bump may be slightly broader than that obtained here
(
John and Linden 2023
;
Xia et al. 2025
)
. However, this difference does not qualitatively affect the conclusions of this work.

The critical energy decreases as the injection age increases. In this section, we assume
B=3\ \mu\text{G}
and, for a more straightforward illustration, consider only the CMB as the seed photon field for ICS. If the KN effect is neglected,
E_{e,\text{cr}}
can be simply estimated as

E_{e,\text{cr}}\approx\frac{1}{b_{0}\tilde{t}}\ .

(5)

For
\tilde{t}=300\ \text{kyr}
,
E_{e,\text{cr}}
is estimated to be
\approx 2\ \text{TeV}
, consistent with the bump position indicated by the pink line in the left panel of Fig.
2
.

The energy-loss bump in the electron spectrum maps onto the
\gamma
-ray spectrum produced through ICS, as shown in the right panel of Fig.
2
. Initially, the
\gamma
-ray spectrum approaches that directly generated by the injection spectrum. As time increases, the energy-loss bump departs from the high-energy cutoff structure caused by injection. The shaded region in the figure represents the typical energy range for pulsar halo measurements by LHAASO and HAWC. It can be seen that, given the current parameter setting, a highly curved spectrum within the shaded region is expected only when the injection age is small.

Constrain physical parameters with the LHAASO J0248+6021 data

4.1
Spectrum fitting

We quantify the uncertainty in
b_{0}
by considering a range of magnetic field strengths. Although estimates of the temperature and energy density of the infrared photons also carry uncertainties, we regard
b_{0,\mathrm{syc}}
as the dominant source of uncertainty, since the magnetic field strength around J0248 remains unconstrained and
b_{0,\mathrm{syc}}
is highly sensitive to it.

The current electron injection age of J0248 is
\tau_{c}-\tilde{\tau}_{0}
, where
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
is a free parameter. As indicated by Eq. (
5

\gamma
-ray spectrum. Therefore, rather than treating
B
and
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
as free parameters simultaneously, we hold
B
at different values and fit for
\tilde{\tau}_{0}
. The other free parameters are
E_{e,c}
and
\eta
.

The synchrotron loss rate for

25

for

statistics of all best-fit models are close to unity, yet the best-fit curves lie slightly above the

4

4.2
Profile fitting

We fix the parameter combinations obtained from the spectral fits in the previous subsection and fit the one-dimensional surface brightness distribution (referred to as
profile
hereafter) of LHAASO J0248
+
6021 above
25\ \text{TeV}
, with
D_{100}
as the free parameter. The slope of the diffusion coefficient is assumed to be
\delta=1/3
as suggested by Kolmogorov’s theory. The fitting results are shown in the left panel of Fig.
4
. The parent electron energy for
\gamma
rays above
25\ \text{TeV}
lies in the energy-loss regime, where the characteristic diffusion distance is determined by the energy-loss timescale rather than the injection age. As the magnetic field strength governs the energy-loss timescale, a degeneracy exists between it and the diffusion coefficient when fitting the profile. Specifically, a stronger magnetic field can be compensated for by a larger diffusion coefficient to produce a similar profile: as
B
increases from
1\ \mu\text{G}
to
5\ \mu\text{G}
, the best-fit
D_{100}
increases from
2.6\times 10^{27}\ \text{cm}^{2}\ \text{s}^{-1}
to
2.3\times 10^{28}\ \text{cm}^{2}\ \text{s}^{-1}
.

Theoretically,
D_{\text{Bohm}}=\frac{1}{3}r_{L}c=\frac{1}{3}\frac{E_{e}c}{eB}
represents the lower limit of the diffusion coefficient. The right panel of Fig.
4
indicates that cases with
B\gtrsim 1\ \mu\text{G}
all satisfy this constraint. Additionally, LHAASO J0248
+
6021 has not been detected to exhibit significant asymmetry, implying that the correlation length
l_{c}
of the turbulent magnetic field could be substantially smaller than the source scale, which is approximately
30\ \text{pc}
. For a Kolmogorov-type turbulent magnetic field,
l_{c}=\left(\frac{3D}{c}\right)^{3/2}\left(\frac{eB}{E}\right)^{1/2}

(
Aloisio and Berezinsky 2004
)
. Even for
B=5\ \mu\text{G}
, the derived
l_{c}
is merely
\approx 5\ \text{pc}
, well satisfying the symmetry constraint. Therefore, the order-of-magnitude variation in the diffusion coefficient due to the uncertainty of the magnetic field cannot be further constrained by theory.

Typically, the primary uncertainty in diffusion coefficient estimates for pulsar halos arises from the distance measurement to the pulsar. Nevertheless, for J0248, the distance of
2\ \text{kpc}
given by the ATNF catalog is fairly accurate. J0248 has a very large dispersion measure with significant non
-
linear time variations, indicating that it is traversing a dense, inhomogeneous electron cloud
(
Theureau et al. 2011
)
. The only suitable environment along the line of sight is the giant HII region W5. J0248 is located near the open cluster IC 1848 within W5, for which the Gaia DR3 parallax measurement gives a distance of
2004\pm 9\ \text{pc}

(
Della Croce et al. 2025
)
. Even considering the physical scale of W5 itself, which is several tens of pc, this introduces only a
1-2\%
uncertainty in the distance estimate to J0248. Furthermore, from proper motion measurements of J0248, the transverse velocity corresponding to a distance of
2\ \text{kpc}
is
500\ \text{km}\ \text{s}^{-1}

(
Theureau et al. 2011
)
, which is also reasonable. This implies that we may be able to provide an accurate estimate of the diffusion coefficient around J0248 once the magnetic field is effectively constrained.

Cao et al. 2025
also provides the energy dependence of the extension of LHAASO J0248
+
6021. In
A
, we compare the energy dependence predicted by our model with the measurements, and find them to be consistent within the uncertainties. With improved measurement precision in the future, this will offer an opportunity to impose meaningful constraints on the energy dependence of the diffusion coefficient.

An old pulsar halo system

6
Conclusion and discussion

The parent electrons of a pulsar halo accumulate through continuous injection. Radiative energy losses shift the early-injected electrons to lower energies, forming a time-dependent energy-loss bump in the spectrum. This work proposes a direct interpretation of the highly curved
\gamma
-ray spectrum of LHAASO J0248
+
6021: its energy-loss bump has not yet significantly departed from the high-energy cutoff structure. This implies that either the ambient magnetic field strength, which affects the energy-loss rate, is lower than the typical ISM value (
3\ \mu\text{G}
), or the electron injection age is significantly smaller than the characteristic age of its central pulsar J0248. For Geminga, whose characteristic age is about six times that of J0248, the energy-loss bump in its expected pulsar halo spectrum has shifted below
100\ \text{GeV}
, showing remarkable consistency with Fermi-LAT measurements.

Fitting the LHAASO and Fermi-LAT spectra of LHAASO J0248
+
6021 constrains the combination of magnetic field strength
B
and electron injection age, while the degeneracy between them remains. The uncertainty in
B
allows the diffusion coefficient from the profile fit to vary by an order of magnitude. We expect EP X-ray observations to determine
B
, thereby providing definitive estimates of the diffusion coefficient and electron injection age.

The broadband
\gamma
-ray spectral characteristics of the Geminga halo justify a hard power-law component in the injection spectrum (
p\sim 1.0
). In addition, based on the height of the energy-loss bump measured by Fermi-LAT, we infer that the initial spin period of Geminga may be shorter than
0.021\ \text{s}
, comparable to that of the Crab pulsar.

The quantitative results of this work may be subject to the following assumptions: (1) We attribute the main uncertainty in the electron energy-loss rate to the magnetic field strength, ignoring uncertainties in the dust radiation field. (2) We assume that the pulsar spin-down is purely due to magnetic dipole radiation, i.e., a braking index of
3
. (3) We assume that the electron injection follows exactly the time evolution of the pulsar spin-down power, i.e., a constant energy conversion efficiency. These factors currently lack clear constraints but do not qualitatively affect our conclusions. Therefore, we leave further discussion to future work.
