Global convergence of W 1 , ∞ W^{1,\infty} -steepest descent for PDE constrained shape optimisation with semilinear elliptic equations in function space
Klaus Deckelnick, Philip J. Herbert, Michael Hinze

Abstract
We prove global convergence in function space for the steepest descent method in shape optimisation with semilinear elliptic partial differential equations. Steepest descent is realized in the Lipschitz topology. In addition, we prove a conditional convergence result for the resulting shapes in two space dimensions.



Keywords:
PDE constrained shape optimisation,

W^{1,\infty}
-steepest-descent, global convergence, semilinear elliptic PDE

Introduction

In this article, we continue our investigations on the convergence of the steepest descent method for shape optimisation problems, which we began in
[
DHH25
]
. To this end, we consider the general PDE constrained shape optimisation problem

\min\mathcal{J}(\Omega):=\int_{\Omega}j(x,u(x),\nabla u(x))\,dx,\,\Omega\in\mathcal{S},

(1.1)

where
j
is a real-valued function whose properties will be specified in Section
2
, and
u
now weakly solves the semilinear elliptic boundary value problem

-\Delta u+g(u)=f\text{ in }\Omega,\quad u=0\text{ on }\partial\Omega.

Furthermore,
\mathcal{S}
is a collection of admissible domains contained in a given hold-all domain
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d=2,3
. As it is in general hard to calculate a minimizer of (
1.1
) one frequently uses
a descent approach in order to produce a sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\subset\mathcal{S}

which, under appropriate conditions, converges to a stationary shape
\Omega
satisfying
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)=0
. We shall use the method of mappings and construct the sets
\Omega^{k}\in\mathcal{S}

as
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
, where
\Phi^{k}\colon\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D}
is a bi–Lipschitz mapping and
\hat{\Omega}\Subset D
is a fixed reference domain. Under suitable conditions on
j
the shape derivative

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})
is a bounded linear functional on
W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
so that
a natural choice of descent direction is given by

V^{k}\in\argmin\left\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[V]:V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}),|DV|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\right\}.

(1.2)

Here,
|DV|
denotes the spectral norm of the Jacobian
DV
. As a result,
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[V^{k}]<0
unless
\Omega^{k}
is already stationary.
The new domain
\Omega^{k+1}
is then obtained as
\Omega^{k+1}=\Phi^{k+1}(\hat{\Omega})
, where
\Phi^{k+1}=({\rm id}+t_{k}V^{k})\circ\Phi^{k}
and
t_{k}>0
is chosen via the Armijo step size rule, see Algorithm
2.1

for a more detailed description.
Our aim in this paper is to investigate the convergence of this algorithm within a function
space setting. As our main results we shall prove that

•

an Armijo stepsize for
W^{1,\infty}
-steepest descent directions of the shape functional exists, see Lemma
3.1
;

•

the algorithm is globally convergent in the sense that
\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})\|\rightarrow 0
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, see Theorem
3.2
;

•

in two space dimensions the sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has a subsequence
that converges with respect to the Hausdorff complementary metric to a stationary point of
\mathcal{J}
,
provided that
(\Phi^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is bounded in
W^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{2})
, see Theorem
3.6
.

These results build on and extend the analysis in
[
DHH25
]
, where
shape optimisation subject to the linear Poisson problem is considered and the convergence for a finite element approximation of problem (
1.1
) is examined. Theorem 3.3 in
[
DHH25
]
proves global convergence of the steepest descent method applied to the discretized problem, and, under an additional condition, the existence of a discrete stationary shape. A crucial ingredient in the corresponding proof is
[
DHH25
, Lemma 3.2]
, which guarantees the existence of the Armijo stepsize in the steepest descent algorithm. A close inspection of that result shows that it is independent of the finite element discretization parameter and therefore virtually carries over to our infinite-dimensional setting. We shall exploit this observation and extend it to the case of a semilinear state equation in order
to prove that our steepest descent method is globally convergent, see
Theorem
3.2
below.

Of course it is also interesting to examine under which conditions
the sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to a stationary shape
\Omega
. A related result
establishing the convergence of a sequence of discrete stationary shapes to a stationary shape of

\mathcal{J}
was obtained in
[
DHH25
, Theorem 4.7]
under the assumption that the mappings
(\Phi^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
satisfy

\frac{1}{M}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq|\Phi^{k}(x_{1})-\Phi^{k}(x_{2})|\leq M|x_{1}-x_{2}|\quad\mbox{ for all }x_{1},x_{2}\in D\mbox{ and all }k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}

(1.3)

for some
M>0
. We shall be able to weaken this condition in the case of two space dimensions in that we only
assume the second inequality in (
1.3
), see Theorem
3.6
.
The reason for the restriction to two dimensions is that we make strong use
of two important results due to Šverák,
[
Šve93
]
and Chambolle & Doveri,
[
CD97
]
respectively. These results establish
\gamma
–convergence for the Dirichlet and Neumann problems and are crucial for passing to the limit
in the state and adjoint equations as well as in the expression for
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)
. An important
advantage of these results is that they essentially require topological conditions on the
sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
which in our case follow from the representation
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
together
with the fact that the mappings
\Phi^{k}
are bi-Lipschitz.

For additional information on the subject of shape optimisation we refer the reader to the seminal works of Delfour and Zolésio
[
DZ11
]
, of Sokolowski and Zolésio
[
SZ92
]
, and the recent overview article
[
ADJ21
]
by Allaire, Dapogny, and Jouve, where also a comprehensive bibliography on the topic can be found.

Preliminaries

2.1
Setting of the problem

Let
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be an open, convex, and bounded hold-all domain with a Lipschitz boundary and

\mathcal{S}:=\{\Omega\subset D\,|\,\Omega\mbox{ is open }\}.

We consider the shape optimisation problem

\min_{\Omega\in\mathcal{S}}\mathcal{J}(\Omega)=\int_{\Omega}j(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\,dx,

where
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
is the unique solution of

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g(u)\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\qquad\mbox{ for all }\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.1)

Here ’
\cdot
’ denotes the Euclidean inner product of two vectors.
In what follows we assume that
f\in H^{1}(D)
,
g\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
g^{\prime}(t)\geq 0
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
and that
j\in C^{2}(D\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
satisfies

\displaystyle|j(x,u,z)|+|j_{x}(x,u,z)|+|j_{xx}(x,u,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{1}(x)+c_{1}\bigl(|u|^{q}+|z|^{2}\bigr);

(2.2)

\displaystyle|j_{u}(x,u,z)|+|j_{xu}(x,u,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{2}(x)+c_{2}\bigl(|u|^{q-1}+|z|^{2-\frac{2}{q}}\bigr);

(2.3)

\displaystyle|j_{z}(x,u,z)|+|j_{xz}(x,u,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{3}(x)+c_{3}\bigl(|u|^{\frac{q}{2}}+|z|\bigr);

(2.4)

\displaystyle|j_{uu}(x,u,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{4}(x)+c_{4}\bigl(|u|^{q-2}+|z|^{2-\frac{4}{q}}\bigr);

(2.5)

\displaystyle|j_{zz}(x,u,z)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\varphi_{5}(x)

(2.6)

for all
(x,u,z)\in D\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where a subscript
x
denotes the derivative with respect to the first variable,
u
, the second, and
z
, the third.
Here,
2\leq q<\infty
if
d=2
, and
q=6
if
d=3
. Also,
\varphi_{1},\ldots,\varphi_{5}
are non-negative with

\varphi_{1}\in L^{1}(D),\varphi_{2}\in L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(D),\varphi_{3}\in L^{2}(D),\varphi_{4}\in L^{\frac{q}{q-2}}(D)
and

\varphi_{5}\in L^{\infty}(D)
.
Note that the choice of
q
implies the continuous embedding
H^{1}_{0}(D)\hookrightarrow L^{q}(D)
, so that there exists
c>0
with

\|v\|_{L^{q}}\leq c\|v\|_{H^{1}}\qquad\mbox{ for all }v\in H^{1}_{0}(D).

(2.7)

From here onwards we omit the explicit dependencies on the spatial variable
x
wherever appropriate.
Using the techniques described in Sections 4.4 and 4.5 of
[
ADJ21
]
, one calculates for the shape derivative of
\mathcal{J}

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(j(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\Div V+j_{x}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\cdot V-j_{z}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\cdot DV^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla u\Bigr)\,dx

(2.8)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(\bigl(DV+DV^{\mathsf{T}}-\Div VI\bigr)\nabla u\cdot\nabla p-g(u)p\mbox{ div }V+{\Div}(fV)p\Bigr)\,dx

for all
V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Here,

p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is the solution of the adjoint problem

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g^{\prime}(u)p\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(j_{u}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\eta+j_{z}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\cdot\nabla\eta\bigr)\,dx\quad\mbox{ for all }\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(2.9)

Note that the assumptions (
2.2
)–(
2.4
) ensure that
all integrals that appear on the right hand side of (
2.8
) and (
2.9
) exist. At the same time these growth conditions
also guarantee uniform control on the solutions of the state
and adjoint equations as shown in

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
. Then the problems (
2.1
) and (
2.9
) admit unique solutions
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
, and
p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, respectively. Moreover, there exists a constant
c^{\star}
, such that

\|u\|_{H^{1}}+\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}+\|p\|_{H^{1}}\leq c^{\star}.

(2.10)

The constant
c^{*}
only depends on
d,D,f,j
and
g
, but
is independent of
\Omega
. Furthermore, we think of
u
and
p
as being extended by zero to
D
.

Proof.

In order to prove the existence of a solution
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
of
(
2.1
) we first consider for
M>0
the modified nonlinearity

g_{M}(t):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}g(-M),&t<-M,\\
g(t),&-M\leq t\leq M,\\
g(M),&t>M.\end{array}\right.

Note that
g_{M}
is increasing since
g^{\prime}\geq 0
.
Using the theory of monotone operators it can be shown that the problem

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{M}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{M}(u_{M})\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

has a unique solution
u_{M}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
, see
[
VM25
, Section 2.6]
for details. Let us next denote by

v\in H^{2}(D)\cap H^{1}_{0}(D)
the solution to the Poisson problem

-\Delta v=f-g(0)\;\mbox{ a.e. in }D,\quad v=0\mbox{ on }\partial D.

Since
H^{2}(D)\hookrightarrow C^{0}(\bar{D})
for
d=2,3
we infer that there exists
K\geq 0
, which only depends on
d,D,f
and
g
,
such that
|v(x)|\leq K
for all
x\in\bar{D}
. The function
w:=v+K
then satisfies

w\geq 0
in
\bar{D}
as well as

-\Delta w+g_{M}(w)=-\Delta v+g_{M}(w)=f-g_{M}(0)+g_{M}(w)\geq f\quad\mbox{ a.e. in }D

since
g_{M}
is increasing and
g_{M}(0)=g(0)
. If we multiply the above relation by

\eta\in C^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\eta\geq 0
in
\Omega
and use an approximation argument we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla w\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g_{M}(w)\eta\,dx\geq\int_{\Omega}f\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\,\eta\geq 0\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Hence,

\int_{\Omega}\nabla(u_{M}-w)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx+\int_{\Omega}(g_{M}(u_{M})-g_{M}(w))\eta\,dx\leq 0\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\eta\geq 0\mbox{ a.e. in }\Omega.

Since
w\geq 0
in
D
we have that
\eta=(u_{M}-w)^{+}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\eta\geq 0
and
therefore by the monotonicity of
g_{M}

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla(u_{M}-w)^{+}|^{2}\,dx\leq 0.

Thus,
(u_{M}-w)^{+}=0
so that
u_{M}\leq w=v+K\leq 2K
a.e. in
\Omega
. A similar argument
shows that
u_{M}\geq-2K
a.e. in
\Omega
, so that

\|u_{M}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq M

(2.11)

if we choose
M=2K
. In particular,
g_{M}(u_{M})=g(u_{M})
and
u=u_{M}
is a solution of
(
2.1
), while uniqueness is a consequence of the monotonicity of
g
. While the
L^{\infty}
–bound for
u
in (
2.10
) then follows from (
2.11
), the
H^{1}
–seminorm is controlled
by testing (
2.1
) with
\eta=u
and using that
g(u)u\geq g(0)u
a.e. in
\Omega
.

In order to prove the bound on
p
we test (
2.9
) with
\eta=p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
and use
the fact that
g^{\prime}\geq 0
, (
2.3
), (
2.4
),
Hölder’s inequality and (
2.7
) to obtain

\displaystyle\int_{D}|\nabla p|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}|\nabla p|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\left[\bigl(\varphi_{2}+c_{2}(|u|^{q-1}+|\nabla u|^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}})\bigr)|p|+\bigl(\varphi_{3}+c_{3}(|u|^{\frac{q}{2}}+|\nabla u|)\bigr)|\nabla p|\right]\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\bigl(\|\varphi_{2}\|_{L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}}+c_{2}(\|u\|_{L^{q}}^{q-1}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2(q-1)}{q}})\bigr)\|p\|_{L^{q}}

\displaystyle+\bigl(\|\varphi_{3}\|_{L^{2}}+c_{3}(\|u\|_{L^{q}}^{\frac{q}{2}}+\|\nabla u\|_{L^{2}})\bigr)\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\bigl(1+\|u\|_{H^{1}}^{q-1}\bigr)\|p\|_{H^{1}}\leq c\bigl(1+(c^{*})^{q-1}\bigr)\|p\|_{H^{1}}\leq c\bigl(1+(c^{*})^{q-1}\bigr)\|\nabla p\|_{L^{2}},

where we also made use of Poincaré’s inequality for
D
and the bound on
u
. The estimate for

\|p\|_{L^{2}}
now follows from another application of Poincaré’s inequality.
∎

2.2
Descent algorithm

Let us next translate the ideas formulated in the introduction
into a descent method in the space
W^{1,\infty}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
To do so, let us fix an open, nonempty reference domain
\hat{\Omega}\Subset D
. The open sets generated by
our descent method will be constructed via the method of mappings
in the form
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
with suitable
bi–Lipschitz mappings
\Phi^{k}:\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D}
.

Algorithm 2.1

(Steepest descent with Armijo line search)
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
be a fixed constant.

0. Let
\Omega^{0}:=\hat{\Omega},\Phi^{0}:=\id
.

For k=0,1,2,…:

1. If
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})=0
, then stop.

2. Choose
V^{k}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that

V^{k}\in\argmin\{J^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[W]\,|\,W\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|DW|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\}.

3. Choose the maximum
t_{k}\in\{\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{4},\ldots\}
such that

\mathcal{J}\bigl((\id+t_{k}V^{k})(\Omega^{k})\bigr)-\mathcal{J}(\Omega^{k})\leq\gamma t_{k}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[V^{k}].

4. Set
\Phi^{k+1}:=(\id+t_{k}V^{k})\circ\Phi^{k},\,\Omega^{k+1}:=(\id+t_{k}V^{k})(\Omega^{k})
.

In the following lemma we collect the properties of the mappings
\Phi^{k}
generated by Algorithm
2.1
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Algorithm
2.1
produces a sequence of bi–Lipschitz mappings
\Phi^{k}:\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D}
with

\Phi^{k}=\id
on
\partial D
and open sets
\Omega^{k}\subset D
with
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
.

Proof.

Let us prove these assertions by induction over
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. They are clearly
satisfied for
k=0
by step 0 of the algorithm. Let us
next assume that for some
k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
the mapping
\Phi^{k}:\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D}
is bi–Lipschitz with
\Phi^{k}=\id
on
\partial D
and
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
.
Let
V^{k}\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the direction obtained in step 2 of the algorithm (see
Theorem 2.1 and the subsequent remarks in
[
DHH24
]
for the existence of
V^{k}
) and
0\leq t_{k}\leq\frac{1}{2}
the step size chosen in step 3. Since
|DV^{k}|\leq 1
a.e. in
D
and
D
is convex we infer that

\frac{1}{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\leq|(\id+t_{k}V^{k})(x_{1})-(\id+t_{k}V^{k})(x_{2})|\leq\frac{3}{2}|x_{1}-x_{2}|\qquad\forall x_{1},x_{2}\in D,

so that
\Phi^{k+1}=(\id+t_{k}V^{k})\circ\Phi^{k}
is bi–Lipschitz and in particular injective.
Furthermore, observing that
V^{k}=0
on
\partial D
we have that
\Phi^{k+1}=\Phi^{k}=\mbox{id}
on
\partial D
.

In order to prove that
\Phi^{k+1}
is surjective we repeat an argument that was
used in the proof of Lemma 2.1 in
[
DHH25
]
. For
every
p\in D
we have that

\mbox{deg}(\Phi^{k+1},D,p)=\mbox{deg}(\mbox{id},D,p)=1,

where
deg
denotes the Brouwer degree. Therefore we infer from the existence property of the degree that there is
x\in D
with
p=\Phi^{k+1}(x)
so
that
D\subset\Phi^{k+1}(D)
. Next we claim that
D
is closed in
\Phi^{k+1}(D)
. To see this,
let
(p_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence in
D
such that
p_{n}\rightarrow p
as
n\rightarrow\infty
for some
p\in\Phi^{k+1}(D)
, say
p=\Phi^{k+1}(x)
with

x\in D
. If
p\in\partial D
, then
\Phi^{k+1}(x)=p=\Phi^{k+1}(p)
, which implies in view of the injectivity of
\Phi^{k+1}
that
x=p
,
a contradiction. Hence
p\in D
. As
D
is also open in
\Phi^{k+1}(D)
and
\Phi^{k+1}(D)
is connected we infer that
D=\Phi^{k+1}(D)
. Since
\Phi^{k+1}=\id
on
\partial D
we conclude that
\Phi^{k+1}:\bar{D}\rightarrow\bar{D}
is bijective. Therefore,

\Phi^{k+1}
is a bi–Lipschitz map from
\bar{D}
to
\bar{D}
with
\Phi^{k+1}=\id
on
\partial D
. Furthermore,

\Omega^{k+1}=(\id+t_{k}V^{k})(\Omega^{k})=(\id+t_{k}V^{k})\circ\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})=\Phi^{k+1}(\hat{\Omega})
.

Finally, the sets
\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
are open because
\Phi^{k}
is a homeomorphism and

\hat{\Omega}
is open.
∎

3
Convergence of the descent algorithm

In this Section, we now demonstrate the global convergence of the algorithm, as well as showing that in two dimensions, the sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
has a convergent subsequence provided that the sequence
(\Phi^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is bounded in
W^{1,\infty}(D;\mathbb{R}^{2})
.

3.1
Global convergence

Our aim in this section is to show that the steepest descent Algorithm
2.1
is globally convergent in the sense that

\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})\|:=\sup\{\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[W]\,|\,W\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}),|DW|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\}\rightarrow 0,\quad\mbox{ as }k\rightarrow\infty.

The corresponding result mainly relies on the following lemma,
which establishes the existence of an Armijo stepsize for a
given descent direction. Let us note that it is here, where
the growth conditions on the second partial derivatives of
j
in (
2.2
)–(
2.6
) are needed.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\gamma\in(0,1)
be a fixed constant,
\Omega\in\mathcal{S}
, and

V\in\argmin\{J^{\prime}(\Omega)[W]\,|\,W\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}),\,|DW|\leq 1\mbox{ a.e. in }D\}.

Suppose that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]\leq-\epsilon
for some
\epsilon>0
. Then there exists

0<\delta<1
which only depends on
j,f,g,D,d,\gamma
and
\epsilon
such that

\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{t})-\mathcal{J}(\Omega)\leq\gamma t\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]\qquad\mbox{ for all }0\leq t\leq\delta,\;\mbox{ where }\Omega_{t}=T_{t}(\Omega)\mbox{ and }T_{t}=\mbox{id}+tV.

Proof.

The proof follows the lines of the proof of
[
DHH25
, Lemma 3.2]
, in which
a corresponding result was obtained in a finite–dimensional setting, where
state, adjoint state and deformation field are elements of finite element spaces.
Nevertheless many arguments immediately carry over to the infinite–dimensional case and
we will direct the reader to the corresponding argument in
[
DHH25
]

when appropriate.

Let
\Omega_{t}=T_{t}(\Omega)
with
T_{t}(x)=x+tV(x)
. In view of the definition of
\mathcal{J}

we have

\mathcal{J}(\Omega_{t})=\int_{\Omega_{t}}j(\cdot,u_{t},\nabla u_{t})\,dx,

where
u_{t}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{t})
solves

\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla\eta_{t}\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}g(u_{t})\eta_{t}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{t}}f\eta_{t}\,dx\qquad\forall\eta_{t}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{t}).

Choosing
\eta_{t}:=\eta\circ T_{t}^{-1}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega_{t})
for a fixed
\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
we thus infer that

\int_{\Omega_{t}}\nabla u_{t}\cdot\nabla(\eta\circ T_{t}^{-1})\,dx+\int_{\Omega_{t}}g(u_{t})\eta\circ T_{t}^{-1}\,dx=\int_{\Omega_{t}}f\eta\circ T_{t}^{-1}\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.1)

Transforming to
\Omega
we obtain

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u_{t}\circ T_{t}\cdot\nabla(\eta\circ T_{t}^{-1})\circ T_{t}\,|\mbox{det}DT_{t}|\,dx+\int_{\Omega}g(u_{t}\circ T_{t})\eta\,|\mbox{det}DT_{t}|dx=\int_{\Omega}f\circ T_{t}\,\eta\,|\mbox{det}DT_{t}|\,dx

(3.2)

for all
\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Since
\mbox{det}DT_{t}=\mbox{det}(I+tDV)
and
|DV|\leq 1
in
\bar{D}
one easily
verifies that

\mbox{det}DT_{t}-1=t{\Div}V+r_{1},\quad\mbox{ with }|r_{1}|\leq ct^{2},

Theorem 3.2
.

2.1

let

such that

\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k_{j}})[V^{k_{j}}]=-\|\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k_{j}})\|\leq-\epsilon\quad\mbox{ for all }j\in\mathbb{N}.

that there exists
\delta>0
which is independent of

3.2
Convergence to a stationary shape in two dimensions

In what follows we restrict our analysis to the two–dimensional case and assume in addition that
the reference domain
\hat{\Omega}
has a Lipschitz boundary.
In order to examine the convergence of the sequence
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with

\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\hat{\Omega})
we require the following concepts, see
e.g.
[
HP06
]
.
Given two open sets
\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}\subset D
their Hausdorff complementary distance
is defined by

\rho_{H}^{c}(\Omega_{1},\Omega_{2}):=\max_{x\in\bar{D}}|d_{\complement\Omega_{1}}(x)-d_{\complement\Omega_{2}}(x)|,

where
d_{\complement\Omega}(x):=\inf\{|x-y|:y\in\bar{D}\setminus\Omega\}
for all
x\in D
.
We say that the sequence of open sets
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
converges to the open set
\Omega
in the sense of the Hausdorff complementary metric if
\rho_{H}^{c}(\Omega^{k},\Omega)\rightarrow 0,k\rightarrow\infty
. Since our optimisation problem is constrained by the elliptic boundary value problem (
2.1
) we shall require continuity of (
2.1
) with respect to
\Omega
in an appropriate sense. In order to formulate the corresponding concept we shall consider

H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
as a closed subspace of
H^{1}_{0}(D)
by associating with each element
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
its extension by zero
e_{0}(u)\in H^{1}_{0}(D)
.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
\Omega^{k},\Omega
be open subsets of
D
.

a) We say that
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\gamma
–converges to
\Omega
, if
e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(u)

in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
for every
f\in H^{-1}(D)
. Here,
u_{k},u
denote the solutions of

\int_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\langle f,\eta\rangle\quad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k})\quad\text{ and }\quad\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\langle f,\eta\rangle\quad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega),\,

respectively. Furthermore,
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
is the duality pairing between
H^{-1}
and
H^{1}_{0}
.

b) We say that
(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is Mosco–convergent to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
if

•

for every
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
there exists a sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
with
u_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k})
such that
e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(u)
in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
;

•

for every sequence
(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
u_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k})
and
e_{0}(u_{k})\rightharpoonup v\in H^{1}_{0}(D)
we have that
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
.

Theorem 3.3
.

(
[
HP18
, Proposition 3.5.5]
) Let
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be
a sequence of open subsets of

. Then
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\gamma

\Omega

if and only if

.

Next, for an open set
\Omega\subset D

we denote by

.

(Šverák,
[
Šve93
]

Theorem 3.5
.

(Chambolle & Doveri,
[
CD97
]

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\Phi^{k}=\id

\gamma
– converges to
\Omega
.

2.1

v\in H^{1}_{0}(D)\cap L^{\infty}(D)
such that

e_{0}(u_{k})\rightharpoonup v\text{ in }H^{1}_{0}(D),\quad e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow v\text{ in }L^{2}(D),\quad e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow v\text{ a.e. in }D.

(3.24)

In view of Theorem
3.3
we have that
(H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}))_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
Mosco–converges to
H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)

which implies that
v\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
. Our next aim is to prove that
e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow v
in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
, and that
v
solves the state equation (
2.1
) in
\Omega
. To begin, denote by
\tilde{u}_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k})
the unique solution of the linear equation

\int_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla\tilde{u}_{k}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{k}}(f-g(v))\eta\,dx\quad\forall\,\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}).

Then, the
\gamma-
convergence implies that
e_{0}(\tilde{u}_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(u)
,
in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
, where
u\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
solves

\int_{\Omega}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega}(f-g(v))\eta\,dx\quad\forall\,\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

(3.25)

The difference
\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k}
satisfies

\int_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla(\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k})\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{k}}(g(u_{k})-g(v))\eta\,dx\quad\forall\,\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}).

Plugging
\eta:=\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k}
into the above relation
and using the uniform boundedness of the sequence

(u_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we obtain

\|\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq c\|u_{k}-v\|_{L^{2}}\|\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k}\|_{L^{2}},

which implies
\|\tilde{u}_{k}-u_{k}\|_{H^{1}}\rightarrow 0
. Thus, after possibly passing
to a further subsequence,

e_{0}(u_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(u)\qquad\mbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(D)\mbox{ and a.e. in }D,

(3.26)

so that by (
3.24
)
e_{0}(u)=v
and hence it follows from (
3.25
) that
u
solves the state equation (
2.1
)
in
\Omega
.
Next we claim that

\displaystyle j_{u}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle j_{u}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\quad\mbox{ in }L^{\frac{q}{q-1}}(D),

(3.27)

\displaystyle j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\quad\mbox{ in }L^{2}(D,\mathbb{R}^{d}).

(3.28)

In order to show (
3.28
) we set
f_{k}:=|j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))-j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))|^{2}
. Clearly,
f_{k}\rightarrow 0
a.e. in
D
, while
(
2.4
) implies that

f_{k}\leq c\bigl(\varphi_{3}^{2}+|e_{0}(u_{k})|^{q}+|\nabla e_{0}(u_{k})|^{2}+|e_{0}(u)|^{q}+|\nabla e_{0}(u)|^{2}\bigr)=:r_{k}.

We have that
r_{k}\rightarrow r:=c\bigl(\varphi_{3}^{2}+2|e_{0}(u)|^{q}+2|\nabla e_{0}(u)|^{2}\bigr)
a.e. in
D
as well as
\int_{D}r_{k}\,dx\rightarrow\int_{D}r\,dx
as
k\rightarrow\infty
, so that the generalised Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem yields (
3.28
).
The relation (
3.27
) is proved in the same way.

Let us next consider the sequence of adjoint solutions
(p_{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Similar as
above we may assume that there exists a further subsequence
and
w\in H^{1}_{0}(D)
such that

e_{0}(p_{k})\rightharpoonup w\text{ in }H^{1}_{0}(D),\quad e_{0}(p_{k})\rightarrow w\text{ in }L^{2}(D),\quad e_{0}(p_{k})\rightarrow w\text{ a.e. in }D.

(3.29)

As above our aim is to prove that
e_{0}(p_{k})\rightarrow w
strongly in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
and that
w
solves the adjoint equation (
2.9
) in
\Omega
. Let

h\in H^{-1}(D)
be defined by

\langle h,\eta\rangle:=\int_{D}\bigl(j_{u}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\eta+j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\cdot\nabla\eta\bigr)\,dx

and denote by
\tilde{p}_{k}\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k})
the solution of

\int_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla\tilde{p}_{k}\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\langle h,\eta\rangle-\int_{\Omega^{k}}g^{\prime}(e_{0}(u))w\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}).

Using that
(\Omega^{k})_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}

\gamma
–converges to
\Omega
we deduce that
e_{0}(\tilde{p}_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(p)
in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
as
k\rightarrow\infty
,
where
p\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega)
is the unique solution of

\int_{\Omega}\nabla p\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\langle h,\eta\rangle-\int_{\Omega}g^{\prime}(e_{0}(u))w\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega).

On the other hand we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla(p_{k}-\tilde{p}_{k})\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx=\int_{\Omega^{k}}\bigl(g^{\prime}(e_{0}(u))w-g^{\prime}(u_{k})p_{k}\bigr)\eta\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega^{k}}\bigl(j_{u}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))-j_{u}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\bigr)\,\eta\,dx

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega^{k}}\bigl(j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))-j_{z}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\bigr)\cdot\nabla\eta\,dx\qquad\forall\eta\in H^{1}_{0}(\Omega^{k}).

Testing the above relation with
\eta=p_{k}-\tilde{p}_{k}
and
recalling (
3.26
), (
3.27
), (
3.28
) and (
3.29
) we infer that

e_{0}(p_{k})-e_{0}(\tilde{p}_{k})\rightarrow 0
in
H^{1}_{0}(D)
so that in conclusion

e_{0}(p_{k})\rightarrow e_{0}(p)\qquad\mbox{ in }H^{1}_{0}(D),

(3.30)

and thus
w=e_{0}(p)
, i.e.
p
solves the adjoint equation (
2.9
).
Next we note that
\partial\Omega^{k}=\Phi^{k}(\partial\hat{\Omega})
, so that the number of connected components of
\partial\Omega^{k}

coincides with the corresponding number of
\partial\hat{\Omega}
and
is therefore uniformly bounded. Furthermore, (
3.21
) and the fact that
\hat{\Omega}
has a Lipschitz boundary imply that

\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\Omega^{k})=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}\mathcal{H}^{1}(\Phi^{k}(\partial\hat{\Omega}))\leq M\mathcal{H}^{1}(\partial\hat{\Omega})<\infty.

Applying Theorem
3.5
with
f\equiv 1
and noting that the solution of the corresponding Neumann problem on
\Omega
is given by the indicator function
\chi_{\Omega}
we infer that

\chi_{\Omega^{k}}\rightarrow\chi_{\Omega}\mbox{ in }L^{2}(D)\mbox{ and a.e. in }D

(3.31)

after possibly passing to a further subsequence.
We have for
V\in W^{1,\infty}_{0}(D,\mathbb{R}^{2})

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[V]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega^{k}}\bigl(DV+DV^{\mathsf{T}}-\Div VI\bigr)\nabla u_{k}\cdot\nabla p_{k}\,dx+\int_{\Omega^{k}}\Div(fV)p_{k}-g(u_{k})p_{k}\Div V\,dx

(3.32)

\displaystyle+\int_{\Omega^{k}}\Bigl(j(\cdot,u_{k},\nabla u_{k})\Div V+j_{x}(\cdot,u_{k},\nabla u_{k})\cdot V-j_{z}(\cdot,u_{k},\nabla u_{k})\cdot DV^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla u_{k}\Bigr)\,dx

\displaystyle=:

\displaystyle A_{k}+B_{k}+C_{k}.

Abbreviating
A:=\int_{\Omega}\bigl(DV+DV^{\mathsf{T}}-\Div VI\bigr)\nabla u\cdot\nabla p\,dx
we have

\displaystyle|A_{k}-A|=|\int_{D}\bigl(DV+DV^{\mathsf{T}}-\Div VI\bigr)\bigl(\chi_{\Omega^{k}}\nabla e_{0}(u_{k})\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p_{k})-\chi_{\Omega}\nabla e_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p)\bigr)|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\int_{D}\chi_{\Omega^{k}}|\nabla e_{0}(u_{k})\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p_{k})-\nabla e_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p)|\,dx+C\int_{D}|\chi_{\Omega^{k}}-\chi_{\Omega}|\,|\nabla e_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p)|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigl(\|e_{0}(u_{k})-e_{0}(u)\|_{H^{1}}\|e_{0}(p_{k})\|_{H^{1}}+\|e_{0}(u)\|_{H^{1}}\|e_{0}(p_{k})-e_{0}(p)\|)_{H^{1}}\bigr)+C\int_{D}\zeta_{k}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\bigl(\|e_{0}(u_{k})-e_{0}(u)\|_{H^{1}}+\|e_{0}(p_{k})-e_{0}(p)\|)_{H^{1}}\bigr)+C\int_{D}\zeta_{k}\,dx,

where we have used Lemma
2.1
and have set
\zeta_{k}:=|\chi_{\Omega^{k}}-\chi_{\Omega}||\nabla e_{0}(u)\cdot\nabla e_{0}(p)|
. Clearly,
\zeta_{k}\leq 2|\nabla e_{0}(u)||\nabla e_{0}(p)|\in L^{1}(D)
and
\zeta_{k}\rightarrow 0
a.e. in
D
in view of (
3.31
). Thus Lebesgue’s dominated convergence theorem together with (
3.26
) and (
3.30
) implies that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}A_{k}=A
, while a similar argument
shows that
\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}B_{k}=B:=\int_{\Omega}\Div(fV)p-g(u)p\Div V\,dx
. Finally, in order to
examine the terms
C_{k}
we note that similarly to (
3.28
) one can show that

j(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))\rightarrow j(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u)),\;j_{x}(\cdot,e_{0}(u_{k}),\nabla e_{0}(u_{k}))\rightarrow j_{x}(\cdot,e_{0}(u),\nabla e_{0}(u))\;\mbox{ in }L^{1}(D),

from which we infer together with (
3.28
) that

\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}C_{k}=C:=\int_{\Omega}\Bigl(j(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\Div V+j_{x}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\cdot V-j_{z}(\cdot,u,\nabla u)\cdot DV^{\mathsf{T}}\nabla u\Bigr)\,dx.

Recalling (
3.32
) we deduce that
\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega)[V]=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}\mathcal{J}^{\prime}(\Omega^{k})[V]=0
with the help of Theorem
3.2
.
∎

4
Numerical experiment

Finally, we present a numerical example that shows, on the one hand, that the conditions of Theorem
3.6
can be verified numerically and are also fulfilled in our example, and on the other hand, that the iteration sequence of the steepest descent method in Algorithm
2.1
can potentially converge to the empty set.
Here, we consider
g=\frac{1}{2}\exp
and set the data
f(x)=1+g(u^{*}(x))
, where
u^{*}(x)=\frac{4}{{3\pi}}-\frac{\|x\|^{2}}{4}
.
We also set
j(x,u,z)=\frac{1}{2}(u-u_{d}(x))^{2}
with
u_{d}(x)=\frac{4}{\pi}-\|x\|^{2}
.
This setting is similar to that of
[
DHH25
, Experiment 1]
with a linear state equation, where the radial symmetric local minimizer
B_{\frac{4}{\sqrt{3\pi}}}(0)
and
\emptyset
of the shape functional are known. However, for the semilinear case considered here we are not aware of non-trivial exact (radially symmetric) solutions. But we expect that the solution behaviour will be similar to that in the linear case.

The numerical experiments were conducted using DUNE
[
BBD+21
]
, particularly the python bindings
[
DNK20
,
DN18
]
.
The initial grids are constructed with pygmsh
[
Sch22
]
.

In our numerical experiment Algorithm
2.1
is initialized with two different domains. As stopping criterion we use
\|J^{\prime}(\Omega_{k})\|\leq 10^{-3}

\Omega^{0}=(-0.75,0.75)^{2}

\Omega^{k}

\emptyset

of the linear case, compare Fig.
1
, bottom row.
