Abstract
. We show that S ​ U ​ ( 1 , 1 ) SU(1,1) NLFT can diverge pointwise for square-summable coefficients. As a consequence, we prove that the classical pointwise asymptotics of polynomials orthogonal on the unit circle can fail for measures in the Szegő class. We also discuss some special cases when the pointwise convergence holds.



1.
Introduction to
SU(1,1)
NLFT, statement of the problems, and main results.

1.1.
SU(1,1)
NLFT on
\mathbb{Z}
.

Let
\mathbb{T}
and
\mathbb{D}
denote the unit circle and open unit disc in
\mathbb{C}
, respectively, both centered at the origin. Let
F=\{F_{n}\}|_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\in\mathbb{D}^{\infty}
. Taking
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, we define a compactly supported sequence
F^{\langle N\rangle}
by truncation:
F^{\langle N\rangle}_{n}=F_{n}\cdot\chi_{|n|\leqslant N},n\in\mathbb{Z}
, where
\chi_{E}
denotes the characteristic function of a set
E
. Define the
2\times 2
matrix
X_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
as solution to the recursion

(1.1)

X_{n}=\Omega_{n}(F^{\langle N\rangle})\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}z&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)X_{n-1},\quad n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,

where

\Omega_{n}(F):=\frac{1}{(1-|F_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\overline{F_{n}}\\
F_{n}&1\end{array}\right),\quad X_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})=\left(\begin{matrix}z^{n}&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right),\quad n<-N\,.

Let
J:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&-1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
and notice that matrix
\Omega_{n}(F)
is
J
-unitary (i.e., belongs to
U(1,1)
). The matrix
\left(\begin{smallmatrix}z&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
is
J
-unitary for
z\in\mathbb{T}
, it is
J
-contraction if
|z|<1
, and
J
-expansion if
|z|>1
. Since

\det X_{n}=z^{n},\,n\in\mathbb{Z}

it makes sense to renormalize

(1.2)

X_{n}=:\left(\begin{matrix}z^{n}&0\\
0&1\end{matrix}\right)\widetilde{X}_{n}\,,

and then

(1.3)

\widetilde{X}_{n}=\frac{1}{(1-|F_{n}|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\overline{F_{n}}z^{-n}\\
F_{n}z^{n}&1\end{array}\right)\widetilde{X}_{n-1},

with
\widetilde{X}_{n}=I:=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
for
n<-N
. One can view
\widetilde{X}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
as Jost solution of recursion (
1.3
) with normalization
\widetilde{X}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})=I
as
n\to-\infty
. Now
\widetilde{X}_{n}\in SU(1,1)
for
z\in\mathbb{T}
. More careful study of this new recursion implies that (see p.5 in
[
33
]
) the matrix
\widetilde{X}_{n}
takes the form

\widetilde{X}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})=:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{a}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})&\mathfrak{b}^{(*)}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})&\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\end{array}\right)\,,

where

(1.4)

f^{(*)}(z):=\overline{f(\bar{z}^{-1})}.

Clearly,
f^{(*)}(z)=\overline{f(z)}
if
z\in\mathbb{T}
.
Following
[
33
]
(except that we take the transposition of the matrix product used in
[
33
]
, p.4 when defining the NLFT because we prefer to multiply the transfer matrices in the order
\ldots\cdot(\cdot)_{-N+1}\cdot(\cdot)_{-N}
rather than
(\cdot)_{-N}\cdot(\cdot)_{-N+1}\ldots
) we put forward a definition:

Definition.

For
F^{\langle N\rangle}
, define
SU(1,1)
nonlinear Fourier transform (NLFT) on
\mathbb{Z}
as the map

F^{\langle N\rangle}\mapsto\overbrace{F^{\langle N\rangle}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\end{array}\right)\,,

where
\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle}):=\mathfrak{a}_{\infty}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
and

\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})=\mathfrak{b}_{\infty}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
.

In fact, since
F^{\langle N\rangle}
has compact support, we get

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{a}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}_{n}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\end{array}\right),\quad\forall n\geqslant N\,.

The definition of NLFT can be extended to other classes of
\{F_{n}\}
that decay as
|n|\to\infty
. We will provide more references later but we start by focusing on
F\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z}),p\in[1,\infty)
. It is known
[
33
]
, p.10 that
F\in\ell^{1}(\mathbb{Z})
implies existence of two functions
\mathfrak{a}(z,F)
and
\mathfrak{b}(z,F)
, both defined on
\mathbb{T}
, such that
\|\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})-\mathfrak{a}(z,F)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}\to 0
and
\|\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})-\mathfrak{b}(z,F)\|_{A(\mathbb{T})}\to 0
when
N\to\infty
and
A(\mathbb{T})
denotes the Wiener’s algebra. Similarly, for
F\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
with
p\in(1,2)
, the nonlinear analog of the Menshov-Paley-Zygmund theorem was established (see,
[
33
]
, p.11 and
[
28
,
14
,
25
]
) which yields, in particular, the following result: if
p\in(1,2)
and
F\in\ell^{p}(\mathbb{Z})
, then the limits

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
exist for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
. For
p>2
the above limits might diverge for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
(see
[
23
]
for the discussion in the context of Jacobi matrices). The borderline case
p=2
is critical, it attracted a lot of attention in harmonic analysis and approximation theory communities but the answer was not known. We formulate the following two questions:

Q1
Z
(the strong version of
SU(1,1)
pointwise convergence of NLFT on

\mathbb{Z}
):
Let
F\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Is it true that the limits

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
exist for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
?

In folklore, the assertion that those limits do actually exist often went under the name
Nonlinear Carleson Conjecture (NCC)
(see, e.g.,
[
5
]
, p.5 for
SU(2)
setting). This problem is motivated by the study of the existence of wave operators for Jacobi matrices (see
[
18
]
). The
SU(1,1)
structure of the problem ensures that

(1.5)

\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})-\mathfrak{b}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})=1,\quad z\in\mathbb{C}

and, in particular,
|\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})|\geqslant 1
for
z\in\mathbb{T}
.
We will use the following notation

\mathfrak{r}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle}):=\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})/\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\,.

Clearly,
\mathfrak{r}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
satisfies
|\mathfrak{r}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})|<1,z\in\mathbb{T}
. We suggest the following weaker version of
\bf Q1_{\mathbb{Z}}
.

Q2
Z
(a weak version of
SU(1,1)
pointwise convergence of NLFT on

\mathbb{Z}
):
Let
F\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
. Is it true that the limit

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{r}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
exists for a.e.
z\in\mathbb{T}
?

Our first main result is the following theorem

Theorem 1.1
.

The answer to
Q1
Z
is negative. If fact, there is
F\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z})
such that the limits
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
and
\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})
diverge at every
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

Remark.
In the theorem, we can take
F
such that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits F\subset\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. Our argument does not provide an answer to
Q2
Z
.

Let
\sigma
be a probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
with the infinite support (in the sense of cardinality). Denote the monic orthogonal polynomials by
\{\Phi_{n}(z,\sigma)\}
and monic orthonormal polynomials by
\{\varphi_{n}(z,\sigma)\}
, we use OPUC for both as shorthand. That is,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{T}}\Phi_{n}(z,\sigma)z^{-j}d\sigma=0,\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}\varphi_{n}(z,\sigma)z^{-j}d\sigma=0,\quad\forall j\in\{0,\ldots,n-1\},\quad\int_{\mathbb{T}}|\varphi_{n}(z,\sigma)|^{2}d\sigma=1,

\displaystyle\deg(\Phi_{n})=\deg(\varphi_{n})=n,\quad\text{coeff}_{n}(\Phi_{n})=1,\quad\text{coeff}_{n}(\varphi_{n})>0\,,

where
\text{coeff}_{n}(Q)
denotes the
n
-th coefficient of the polynomial
Q
. For any polynomial
Q
, we let
Q^{*}:=z^{n}\overline{Q(\bar{z}^{-1})}
. Notice that such
\ast
-operation is different from the
(\ast)
-operation in (
1.4
) and it depends on
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
. In fact,
Q^{*}=z^{n}Q^{(*)}
.

Definition.

The measure
\sigma
belongs to the Szegő class (
\sigma\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
) if

\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log w\,dm>-\infty\,,

where
d\sigma=wdm+d\sigma_{s}\,,

m
is the normalized Lebesgue probability measure on
\mathbb{T}
and
\sigma_{s}
is the singular part of
\sigma
.

The existence of the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}^{*}(z,\sigma)
for
z\in\mathbb{T}
has been studied in many papers under different assumptions on measure
\sigma
and we will discuss some results in section 3. Now, we give two relevant applications of Theorem
1.1
to the OPUC theory:

Theorem 1.2
.

There is
\sigma\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
such that the limit

\lim_{n\to\infty}\varphi_{n}^{*}(z,\sigma)

diverges for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

and

Theorem 1.3
.

There is
\sigma\in\mathrm{Sz}(\mathbb{T})
and the sequence
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
such that the orthogonal series

(1.6)

\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}}\alpha_{n}\varphi_{n}(z,\sigma)

diverges for all
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

Remark.
The measure
\sigma
in the previous two theorems is actually more regular: it is absolutely continuous with continuous and positive density (see Theorem
3.1
). For
\sigma=m
, we have
\varphi_{n}=z^{n}
and the series (
1.6
) converges a.e. on
\mathbb{T}
for every
\{\alpha_{n}\}\in\ell^{2}(\mathbb{Z}^{+})
by the Carleson theorem
[
10
,
28
]
which settled the Lusin’s conjecture. The Theorem
1.3
shows that the analog of Lusin’s conjecture does not hold within the Szegő class of orthogonality measures.

1.2.
SU(1,1)
NLFT on
\mathbb{R}
.

Suppose
q\in L^{1}_{\rm loc}(\mathbb{R})
. For every
T>0
, we let
q^{\langle T\rangle}:=q\cdot\chi_{|x|<T}
.
Define
X(x,k,q^{\langle T\rangle})
as the solution to

\quad\partial_{x}{X}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}ik&\overline{q}^{\langle T\rangle}\\
q^{\langle T\rangle}&0\end{array}\right)X\quad\text{for }\,\,x\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad X(x,k,q^{\langle T\rangle})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}e^{ikx}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\quad\text{for}\quad x\leqslant-T\,,

where
k\in\mathbb{C}
. It is easy to check that
X\in U(1,1)
for
k\in\mathbb{R}
,
X
is
J
-contraction for
k\in\mathbb{C}^{+}
, and
J
-expansion for
k\in\mathbb{C}^{-}
. If
X=:\left(\begin{smallmatrix}e^{ikx}&0\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)\widetilde{X}
, then

\partial_{x}{\widetilde{X}}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\overline{q}^{\langle T\rangle}e^{-ikx}\\
q^{\langle T\rangle}e^{ikx}&0\end{array}\right)\widetilde{X}\quad\text{for}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{X}(x,k,q^{\langle T\rangle})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\quad\text{for }\quad x\leqslant-T\,.

Now,
\widetilde{X}\in SU(1,1)
for
k\in\mathbb{R}
. For
x\geqslant T
, we have

\widetilde{X}(x,k,q^{\langle T\rangle})=:\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\mathfrak{a}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})&\mathfrak{b}^{\#}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})&\mathfrak{a}^{\#}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})\end{array}\right)\,,

where we denote
f^{\#}(k):=\overline{f(\bar{k})}
for
k\in\mathbb{C}
.

Definition.

For
q^{\langle T\rangle}
, define
SU(1,1)
NLFT on
\mathbb{R}
as the map

q^{\langle T\rangle}\mapsto\overbrace{q^{\langle T\rangle}}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{a}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})\\
\mathfrak{b}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})\end{array}\right)\,.

In analogy with
\mathbb{Z}
setup, we have two questions for the critical case when
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(they both have positive answers for
q\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}),p\in[1,2)
, see
[
11
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
28
]
):

Q1
R
(the strong version of
SU(1,1)
pointwise convergence of NLFT on

\mathbb{R}
):
Let
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Is it true that the limits

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})
and
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})
exist for a.e.
k\in\mathbb{R}
?

and

Q2
R
(a weak version of
SU(1,1)
pointwise convergence of NLFT on

\mathbb{R}
):
Let
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. Is it true that the limit

\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})/\mathfrak{a}^{\#}(k,F^{\langle T\rangle})
exists for a.e.
k\in\mathbb{R}
?

The first question is motivated by the scattering theory in Schrödinger evolution
[
16
]
. Our next result is

Theorem 1.4
.

The answer to
Q1
R
is negative. In fact, there is
q\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for which the limits
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathfrak{a}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})
and
\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathfrak{b}(k,q^{\langle T\rangle})
do not exist for all
k\in\mathbb{R}
.

The structure of the paper is as follows. The second section contains the proof of Theorem
1.1
. There, we also briefly mention how the proof of Theorem
1.1
can be modified to get Theorem
1.4
. In the third section, we discuss the connection of

NLFT on
\mathbb{Z}
to the OPUC theory and prove Theorem
1.2
and Theorem
1.3
. We also explain how the general theorems on convergence of orthogonal series provide the best known results on the pointwise convergence of
SU(1,1)
NLFT on
\mathbb{Z}
.

2.
Proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.4
.

Suppose
w:\mathbb{Z}\to[1,\infty)
satisfies

\displaystyle w(-n)=w(n),

\displaystyle w(n_{1}+n_{2})\leqslant w(n_{1})w(n_{2}),

\displaystyle|n|^{-1}\log w(n)\to 0,\quad|n|\to\infty\,.

We call these
w
strong Beurling weights
[
30
]
, p.306. The standard example is
w=(1+|n|)^{\beta},\beta\geqslant 0
. One says
f\in A_{w}(\mathbb{T})
if

\|f\|_{A_{w}(\mathbb{T})}:=\|\widehat{f}\|_{\ell^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{Z})}:=\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}w(n)|\widehat{f}(n)|<\infty\,.

In the case when
F\in\ell^{1}_{w}(\mathbb{Z})
and
w
is a Beurling weight, we have
\mathfrak{b}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\to\mathfrak{b}(z,F)
and
\mathfrak{a}(z,F^{\langle N\rangle})\to\mathfrak{a}(z,F)

for
N\to\infty
and the convergence is understood in the Wiener’s algebra
A_{w}(\mathbb{T})
. That allows us to define the NLFT for
F\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
if we choose
w=(1+|n|)^{\beta},\forall\beta\geqslant 0
.

Definition.
For
F\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
the NLFT is the map

F\mapsto\overbrace{F}:=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mathfrak{a}(z,F)\\
\mathfrak{b}(z,F)\end{array}\right)\,.

If
F\in\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
, the function
\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
satisfies the following properties:

(A) We have
|\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F)|^{2}=1+|\mathfrak{b}(z,F)|^{2}
for
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

(B)
\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F)\in A(\mathbb{D})
is outer function in
\mathbb{D}
and

(2.1)

\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F)=\exp\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\log|\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(\xi,F)|dm\right)=\exp\left(\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\frac{\xi+z}{\xi-z}\log(1+|\mathfrak{b}(\xi,F)|^{2})dm\right),

for
z\in\mathbb{D}
.

(C) (nonlinear Plancherel identity
[
33
,
25
]
)

(2.2)

-\sum_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}\log(1-|F_{n}|^{2})=2\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log|\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(\xi,F)|dm=\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log(1+|\mathfrak{b}(\xi,F)|^{2})dm\,.

We need the following two results (see
[
4
]
, p. 314 in
[
30
]
, and
[

]
for

). The first one is important for solving the Ablowitz-Ladik equation (

Theorem 2.1
.

The map
F\mapsto\mathfrak{b}(z,F)
is bijective from
\mathcal{S}^{*}(\mathbb{Z})
to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.

Theorem 2.2
.

There is
\tau_{0}>0
such that
\|F\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\leqslant\tau_{0}\Rightarrow\|\mathfrak{b}(\cdot,F)\|_{A_{1}(\mathbb{T})}\lesssim\|F\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}
and
\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{A_{1}(\mathbb{T})}\leqslant\tau_{0}\Rightarrow\|F(\mathfrak{b})\|_{\ell_{1}(\mathbb{Z})}\lesssim\|\mathfrak{b}\|_{A_{1}(\mathbb{T})}

We will be using the following notation: given

F_{\to N}:(F_{\to N})_{n}=F_{n-N},\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\,,F^{\leqslant M}:=F\cdot\chi_{n\leqslant M}\,.

One has the following lemma.

Lemma 2.3
.

Suppose
F
and
G
are compactly supported sequences on
\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits F\subset(-\infty,N_{1}]
,
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits G\subset[-N_{2},\infty)

. Then,

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n\rangle})}{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}=\frac{1}{(1-|F_{n}|^{2})^{\tfrac{1}{2}}}\left(\frac{F_{n}z^{n}\mathfrak{b}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}+1\right)\,.

Since

\left|{\mathfrak{b}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}/{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}\right|\leqslant 1,\,z\in\mathbb{T}

and
F_{n}\to 0
, we get

\left|\frac{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n\rangle})}{\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,F^{\langle n-1\rangle})}-1\right|\lesssim|F_{n}|

uniformly in
z\in\mathbb{T}
if
n
is large enough. The maximum principle extends this bound to
z\in\mathbb{D}
. The estimate on the difference of the arguments follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Suppose the sequence
G
is such that
\mathop{\mathrm{supp}}\nolimits G\subset[n_{0},\infty)
and
\sum_{m+1\leqslant s\leqslant m+p}|G_{s}|\leqslant\tfrac{1}{2}
for a fixed
m
and
p\in\mathbb{N}
. Then,

(4.5)

|\mathfrak{r}(z,G^{\leqslant m+p})-\mathfrak{r}(z,G^{\leqslant m})|\lesssim\sum_{m+1\leqslant s\leqslant m+p}|G_{s}|

for
z\in\mathbb{T}
.

Proof.

We take
z\in\mathbb{T}
. For shorthand, we let
\alpha_{j}(z):=\mathfrak{a}^{(*)}(z,G^{\leqslant j})
,
\beta_{j}(z):=\mathfrak{b}(z,G^{\leqslant j})
, and
r_{j}:=\beta_{j}/\alpha_{j}
. Then, from (
1.3
), one has

\alpha_{n+1}=\frac{1}{(1-|G_{n+1}|^{2})^{\tfrac{1}{2}}}(\alpha_{n}+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\beta_{n}^{*}),\,\beta_{n+1}=\frac{1}{(1-|G_{n+1}|^{2})^{\tfrac{1}{2}}}(\beta_{n}+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\alpha_{n}^{*})\,.

That gives

\displaystyle r_{n+1}-r_{n}=\frac{r_{n}+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\alpha_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n}}{1+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\beta_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n}}-r_{n}=G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\frac{\alpha_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n}-\beta_{n}^{*}r_{n}/\alpha_{n}}{1+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\beta_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{sul}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\frac{G_{n+1}z^{n+1}}{\alpha_{n}^{2}(1+G_{n+1}z^{n+1}\beta_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n})}

and, recalling that
|\beta_{n}^{*}/\alpha_{n}|\leqslant 1,|r_{n}|<1,|\alpha_{n}|\geqslant 1
, we get

=|r_{n+1}-r_{n}|\leqslant{|G_{n+1}|}/({1-|G_{n+1}|})\,,

so (
4.5
) follows by summation and triangle inequality.
∎
