Abstract
. The well known phenomenon of exploding and vanishing gradients in deep neural networks is analyzed using multiplicative ergodic theory. The effect of adding a residual connection is explained in this context. Specifically, a characterization of Liapunov exponents due to Furstenberg and Kifer [ 8 ] is exploited in order to make a precise statement about the Liapunov spectrum and the effect of residual connections on it.

1.
Introduction

One of the phenomena that has been observed in the training of Deep Neural Networks (DNNs) is that of exploding and decaying gradients. This refers to the empirically observed fact that on application of stochastic gradient descent (SGD) for their training, the gradients in certain directions may asymptotically tend to vanish or blow up in norm. There is a considerable literature for explaining this phenomenon and also for schemes to ameliorate this effect, see

for a small sampler. Our objective here is to present a different take on this problem using multiplicative ergodic theory and Liapunov exponents. In particular, we use an exact characterization of Liapunov exponents due to Furstenberg and Kifer
[
8
]
in order to precisely pin down the effect of residual connections.

To put our work in context, we briefly recall the approach taken in some of the prior works to explain this phenomenon and highlight the basic differences vis-a-vis the present effort. We discuss only a few representative works that are closest to ours in flavour, particularly in the common feature of using multiplicative ergodic theory.

The closest to us in spirit is
[
13
]
. Indeed this work applies multiplicative ergodic theory to flag the role of Liapunov exponents in shaping the asymptotic landscape of the input-output map of a DNN. What is new in our work is the use of the exact characterization of the Liapunov exponents due to Fudenberg and Kifer
[
8
]
that allows us to go significantly further in order to pin down the Liapunov spectrum in greater detail and characterize the effect of residual connections on it in a precise mathematical fashion using projective geometry.

A similar comparison applies to
[
6
]
which, under additional structural assumptions on the DNN, argues that residual connections will ‘tame’ the Liapunov exponents in a certain sense, see
ibid.
for details. An early work in this direction is
[

Other related works include

In an alternative approach,

To summarize, the key distinction between the present work and its predecessors is the use of the exact characterization of Liapunov spectrum in multiplicative ergodic theory due to

2.
Notation and Preliminaries

We begin this section by describing up front the mapping from DNN to a discrete dynamical system which lies at the core of this work and some of the related works discussed above. This will be followed by setting up of the core notation that will be operative throughout.

2.1.
DNN as a dynamical system

Fix
d\geq 1
. As a deep neural network, we consider a concatenation of
N\gg 1
blocks of feedforward neural networks, with inputs and outputs from
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to itself. This will be our DNN. (DNNs in general allow different dimensions for each layer. See Remark
3.3
below.)

The component networks are assumed to belong to a parametrized family
f_{\beta},\beta\in\mathbb{R}^{s}
for some
s\geq 1
and are assumed to be jointly continuously differentiable in
\beta
and the input. This includes, e.g., sigmoidal networks, but not ReLU. We denote by
\beta^{N}
the vector

[\beta_{N},\beta_{N-1},\cdots,\beta_{1}]\in\mathbb{R}^{sN}

and by
f^{N}_{\beta^{N}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
the composition thereof, i.e.,

(1)

f^{N}_{\beta^{N}}:=f_{\beta_{N}}\circ f_{\beta_{N-1}}\circ\cdots\circ f_{\beta_{1}}\circ f_{\beta_{0}}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}.

Here ‘
\circ
’ denotes composition of functions, i.e.
f\circ g(x):=f(g(x))
.

There is a standard way of mapping this composition into iterates of an equivalent discrete time dynamical system. Specifically, let
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and recursively define

(2)

x_{n+1}=f_{\beta_{n}}(x_{n}),\ n\geq 0.

Then inductively, one has

f^{N}_{\beta^{N}}(x_{0})=x_{N},N\geq 1

and vice versa. Thus the two descriptions are completely equivalent.

Let
D(\beta)(x):=
the
d\times d
Jacobian matrix of
f_{\beta}
evaluated at
x
, parametrized by
\beta
. Likewise, let
D^{N}(\beta^{N})(x^{N}):=
the
d\times d
Jacobian matrix of
f^{N}
, parametrized by
\beta^{N}
and evaluated at
x^{N}:=[x_{N},\cdots,x_{0}]
. Then by the chain rule of differentiation,

(3)

D^{N}(\beta^{N})(x^{N})=\prod_{m=0}^{N}D(\beta_{N-m})(x_{N-m}).

Let
\eta_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We shall be interested in the asymptotic behaviour of

(4)

\displaystyle\eta^{N}

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{m=0}^{N}D(\beta_{N-m})(x_{N-m})\right)\eta_{0}.

(5)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle D(\beta_{N})(x_{N})\eta^{N-1}.

Equation (
5
) represents a time-inhomogeneous linear dynamical system with
\beta_{n}
’s serving as parameters. The second equality is the counterpart of the equivalence of (
1
) and (
2
) mentioned above, but for the
linearized dynamics
given by (
5
) as opposed to the original dynamics (
2
).

Summarizing, this dynamical view of DNNs is tantamount to:

(1)

viewing input as the initial condition of a nonlinear dynamics,

(2)

the layer index
m\geq 0
as a discrete time index,

(3)

the input-output map
f_{\beta_{i}}
of the
i
th layer of the feedforward network as a time-dependent map that dictates the evolution of the dynamics at time
i
from the state at time
i
to the state at time
i+1
,

(4)

\beta_{i}
’s are parameters of the next step map at time
i
,
i\geq 0
.

This equivalence allows us to apply techniques from the theory of nonlinear dynamics to DNNs as the depth of the DNN tends to infinity.

3.
Furstenberg-Kifer theorem

Multiplicative ergodic theorems go back to
[
12
]
. The basic result and the associated theory of Liapunov exponents has been extended in many directions, see
[
2
]
for a comprehensive treatment. We use here a variant due to Furstenberg and Kifer
[
8
]
which has the additional feature of giving a clean characterization of the Liapunov spectrum and the resulting direct sum decomposition of the state space, in terms of an associated Markov chain on the projective space. This needs the following additional assumption.

Assumption 1:

\int\left(\log^{+}\|A\|+\log^{+}\|A^{-1}\|\right)\mu(dA)<\infty.

Definition 3.1
.

Given a
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
and a
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1})
, we define
\mu*\nu\in\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1})
by

(6)

\int\varphi d(\mu*\nu):=\int\varphi(Ax)\mu(dA)\nu(dx)\ \ \ \ \forall\ \varphi\in C_{b}(\mathcal{M}).

Lemma 3.2
.

The set
\mathcal{S}:=\{\nu\in\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1}):\mu*\nu=\nu\}\subset\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1})
is a nonempty and compact simplex whose extreme points are mutually singular.

Proof.

This is immediate from the discussion in
[
8
]
, pp. 17-18, where the correspondence of
\mathcal{S}
with the stationary distributions of a Markov chain is established. Specifically, define the transition kernel

\kappa:P^{d-1}\to\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1})

by

\int f(y)\kappa(dy|x):=\int_{P^{d-1}}f\left(\frac{Ax}{\|Ax\|}\right)\mu(dA)\ \ \forall\ f\in C\left(P^{d-1}\right).

(Equivalently,

\kappa(B|x):=\int_{P^{d-1}}I\left\{\left(\frac{Ax}{\|Ax\|}\right)\in B\right\}\mu(dA)

for all Borel
B\subset P^{d-1}
.)

Thus
\nu
is simply an invariant measure of this transition kernel by Definition
3.1
. Since
P^{d-1}
is compact and the map
x\to\kappa(dy|x)
is seen to be continuous in
x
, it follows from standard Markov process theory
[
4
]
,
[
11
]
that the set of such probability measures
\nu
forms a nonempty compact simplex whose extreme points are mutually singular. ∎

We call
\mathcal{S}
the set of
\mu
-stationary measures in
\mathcal{P}(P^{d-1})
. We shall also need the following definitions.

(1)

For a subspace
Z
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denote by
\overline{Z}
the set of corresponding elements of
P^{d-1}
.

(2)

For
\mu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{M})
, say that a subspace
Z
of
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
\mu
-invariant
if it is invariant under
\mu
-a.s.
A\in\mathcal{M}
.

Remark 3.3
.

A priori, our assumption that all
f_{\beta_{i}}
’s map
\mathbb{R}^{d}
to itself is not restrictive. We can take the
d<\infty
to be an upper bound (assumed to exist) on the dimensionality of the input or output space of the individual networks and set the appropriate connection weights to zero if the actual dimension is lower. However, Assumption 1 above is restrictive. It is required in order to be able to use the theory of
[
8
]
. Our aim is to demonstrate a plausible mechanism for explaining the observed benefits of residual connections in a quantitative fashion, albeit for a stylized model. Since matrices satisfying Assumption 1 are dense in
\mathcal{M}
, this exercise is not entirely unreasonable. That said, it will need a lot more sophisticated mathematics (in particular, a suitable extension of Theorem

in order to push these results to full generality, which is a task for the future.

With this notation and caveats, we next state the key results from

Under stated hypotheses,

Theorem 3.4
.

(i)
There exist an integer

, a sequences of subspaces of

\{0\}\subset L_{r}\subset L_{r-1}\subset\cdots\subset L_{2}\subset L_{1}\subset L_{0}=\mathbb{R}^{d}

\gamma(\mu):=\gamma^{0}(\mu)>\gamma^{1}(\mu)>\gamma^{2}(\mu)>\cdots>\gamma^{r}(\mu)

such that, if

\lim_{N\uparrow\infty}\frac{1}{N}\log\left\|X_{N}X_{N-1}\cdots X_{1}v\right\|=\gamma^{i}(\mu).

(ii)
These

\alpha(\mu,\nu):=\int_{\mathcal{M}}\int_{P^{d-1}}\log\left(\|Au\|\right)\mu(dA)\nu(du)

as

The

4.
Effect of residual connections

In order to map our problem to this framework, we consider
\{\beta_{n}\}
i.i.d. with law (say)
\Gamma\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{s})
. We make the following additional assumption:

Assumption 2:
The limit
x_{\infty}:=\lim_{n\uparrow\infty}x_{n}
exists a.s. in (
2
).

We justify this as follows. In classifier DNNs, there are finitely many classes encoded as euclidean vectors
C=\{c_{1},\cdots,c_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(say) at the output and for
\mu
-a.s.
x_{0}
, the above limit
x_{\infty}\in C
is well defined. Fix
x_{0}=v
in the probability
1
set where this holds. Then Assumption 2 holds.

As we are considering the asymptotic regime, in what follows, we consider
x_{n}
replaced by
x_{\infty}
. Furthermore, since
x_{\infty}
is measurable with respect to the tail
\sigma
-field
\cap_{n\geq 1}\sigma(\beta_{i},i\geq n)
which is trivial by the Kolmogorov
0-1
law,
x_{\infty}
is a.s. a constant and we may take it to be a deterministic constant. Thus
D(\beta_{n})(x_{\infty}),n\geq 0,
are i.i.d. with law (say)
\upsilon
.

The ResNet architecture can be viewed as adding a forward connection, the so called ‘residual connection’, that replaces every forward block
x\mapsto f_{\beta_{i}}(x)
of the DNN by the map
x\mapsto x+f_{\beta_{i}}(x)
. We now explore how this affects the overall input-output map of the DNN in the limit as
N\uparrow\infty
, in view of the foregoing.

We do this by comparing the above maps for
x=\hat{u}
as defined earlier with
\|\hat{u}\|=c>0
(say). Since
\mu
and therefore the set
\mathcal{S}
of possible values of
\nu
is fixed, we focus on the quantity

\xi(u,A):=\log(\|Au\|)=\log\left(\frac{\|A\hat{u}\|}{\|\hat{u}\|}\right),

and explore how it changes when
A
is replaced by
I+A
.

Now we are ready to prove our main result.

Theorem 4.1
.

The Liapunov spectrum under a residual connection is a smaller perturbation of the spectrum of the identity matrix (i.e., the vector of all
1
’s) than without a residual connection.

Proof.

It is easy to check that
\Phi(\hat{u}),\Psi(\hat{u})
are independent of the
c=\|\hat{u}\|
above from their very definition. Hence we can and do take
c=1
without any loss of generality. Now consider

\hat{u}_{0}:=\hat{u},\ \hat{u}_{1}:=A\hat{u}_{0},\ \hat{u}_{2}:=(I+A)\hat{u}_{0}=\hat{u}_{0}+A\hat{u}_{0}.

Consider the two dimensional parallelopiped
B
formed by
\textbf{0},\hat{u}_{0},\hat{u}_{1}
and
\hat{u}_{2}
. Let
\hat{u}_{3}:=
the intersection of its diagonals. Then
\hat{u}_{3}
is in the relative interior of
B
. Let
\hat{u}_{i}^{\prime}:=\Phi(\hat{u}_{i}),i=0,1,3
. Then
u_{i}^{\prime}\in B\cap S^{d}
for
i=0,1,3
. It is then easy to see that
\hat{u}_{3}^{\prime}
lies in the relative interior of the arc
B\cap S^{d}
joining
\hat{u}_{0}^{\prime}
and
\hat{u}_{1}^{\prime}
. Therefore it is closer to
\hat{u}_{0}^{\prime}
than
\hat{u}_{1}^{\prime}
. This property is preserved under the map
\Psi
. That is, on mapping these vectors to the corresponding equivalence classes in
P^{d-1}
,
\Psi(\hat{u}_{3}^{\prime})
lies closer to
\Psi(\hat{u}_{0})
than
\Psi(\hat{u}_{1})
, in the metric topology of
P^{d-1}
.
Hence it follows that

\displaystyle\int_{P^{d-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\log\left(\|(I+A)u\|\right))\varphi(dA)\nu(du)\ -

\displaystyle\ \int_{P^{d-1}}\int_{\mathcal{M}}\log\left(\|Au\|\right))\varphi(dA)\nu(du)\ <\ 0.

The claim follows. ∎

This result explains the stabilizing effect of residual connections on the Liapunov exponents of the DNN in a precise quantifiable sense.
