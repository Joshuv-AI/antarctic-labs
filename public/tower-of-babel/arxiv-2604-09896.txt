Stochastic homogenization of fractional obstacle problems
Francesco Deangelis, Matteo Focardi, Caterina Ida Zeppieri

Abstract
. We prove a stochastic homogenization result for a class of nonlinear and nonlocal variational problems in domains with many small randomly distributed (bilateral) obstacles. Our model case is a Dirichlet problem for the fractional p p -Laplacian, p > 1 p>1 , where a pinning condition u = 0 u=0 is imposed on the solution in a random collection of small balls whose centers and radii are generated by a stationary marked point process . Such a general obstacle distribution allows for clustering effects to appear with positive probability. Under suitable moment conditions on the obstacle radii, we identify a critical scaling regime in which the fractional p p -capacity density of the obstacles is asymptotically additive almost surely . In turn, this key property allows us to derive an effective homogenized problem which is formally analogous to the one obtained in the periodic setting or under the assumption of well-separation for the obstacles. The analysis also extends to the case of randomly shaped obstacles and to a broad class of nonlocal interaction kernels . At the methodological level, the paper develops a streamlined proof strategy with several new ingredients, among them the use of Palm measures.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Overview

In this paper we study the effective behavior of sequences of
nonlinear
and
nonlocal
Dirichlet problems posed in domains containing a diverging number of increasingly small
randomly distributed
(bilateral) obstacles.

To set up the framework, let
\Omega
be the sample space of an underlying probability space and let
\varepsilon>0
be a small parameter. The prototypical problem we consider is

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u=f&\text{in}\;U\setminus T^{\omega}_{\varepsilon},\cr u=0&\text{on}\;\partial U\cup T^{\omega}_{\varepsilon},\end{cases}

(1.1)

where
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is open, bounded and Lipschitz,
(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}
denotes the (regional) fractional
p
-Laplacian of order
s
, with
p\in(1,\infty)
and
s\in(0,1)
. In (
1.1
) the source term
f
belongs to
L^{p^{\prime}}(U)
and, for
\omega\in\Omega
,
T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}
denotes a realization of the
random
obstacle set defined as

T_{\varepsilon}=\bigcup\{B_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho}(\varepsilon x)\colon(x,\rho)\in\supp N,\,x\in\varepsilon^{-1}U\}.

(1.2)

In (
1.2
)
\lambda_{\varepsilon}>0
is a secondary small scale satisfying
\lambda_{\varepsilon}\ll\varepsilon
, and
N
is a
marked point process
. More precisely,
N
is a random counting measure of the form

N=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{(x_{i},\rho_{i})},

where
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued random variables and
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued random variables, representing the marks associated to the points
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
Under
minimal
structural assumptions on
N
, which in particular shall be
stationary
and
ergodic
with respect to shifts in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
(see (
2.15
) and (
2.16
) for the precise definitions), in this paper we prove that, when
sp\in(0,n)
, there is a critical scaling
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
for the obstacle radii such that, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, the solutions to (
1.1
) converge
almost surely
to the solution of the effective problem

\begin{cases}(-\Delta)^{s}_{p}u+\gamma\,|u|^{p-2}u=f&\text{in}\;U,\cr u=0&\text{on}\;\partial U,\end{cases}

(1.3)

where
\gamma>0
is explicit and encodes the asymptotic geometry and distribution of the obstacles. More precisely,
\gamma
represents the averaged limit density of the fractional
p
-capacity of the random obstacle set
T_{\varepsilon}
, as we are going to explain in detail below.

1.2.
A brief literature review

The asymptotic behavior of solutions to local Dirichlet problems in periodically perforated domains (or in domains with obstacles) has been a very active research area since the seminal works of Marchenko and Khruslov
[
32
]
, Rauch and Taylor
[
36
,
37
]
, and Cioranescu and Murat
[
15
,
14
]
.
From a mathematical point of view, these problems are interesting because, when the perforations are at a critical size, the limit equation shows an extra zero-order term, originally dubbed “a strange term coming from nowhere”, which reflects the additive asymptotic behavior of the capacity of the homogenizing obstacles.

Several approaches have been developed over the years to study local problems posed in perforated domains; comprehensive treatments can be found in the monographs
[
5
,
18
,
13
,
8
,
33
,
12
]
. In particular, Dal Maso gave a complete variational solution
[
17
,
16
]
by building on the
\Gamma
‑convergence framework previously introduced by De Giorgi, Dal Maso, and Longo
[
21
]
.
In the 2000s the interest in the homogenization of obstacle problems resurfaced in the nonlocal community thanks to Caffarelli and Mellet
[
9
]
, who were the first to study these problems for the fractional Laplacian. Their method relies on the celebrated Caffarelli-Silvestre extension
[
11
]
, which allows to reduce the problem posed for the fractional Laplacian to a local problem. Indeed, thanks to
[
11
]
the solution to the nonlocal problem in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
can be interpreted as the boundary trace of the solution of a
degenerate
but local elliptic equation in the higher-dimensional positive half-space
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}
.
Caffarelli and Mellet’s proof is then based on the construction of oscillating test functions for degenerate elliptic PDEs and their homogenization result holds for “well separated” randomly shaped obstacles placed on a periodic lattice.

A genuinely nonlocal approach was later proposed by the second author by resorting to
\Gamma
-convergence techniques combined with an ergodic theorem for multiparameter stationary processes
[
27
,
28
]
. Circumventing the construction of the oscillating test functions, this variational approach applies to a broad class of nonlocal and nonlinear problems and easily extends beyond the periodic setting (see also
[
26
]
). Moreover, an explicit expression for the limiting capacity density of the obstacles is obtained in
[
27
,
28
]
, whereas it remained implicit in the extension-based works
[
9
,
10
]
. We stress, however, that the analysis in
[
27
,
28
]
strongly relies on the spatial distribution of the obstacles, in particular ruling out clustering effects. More precisely, in the works mentioned above the obstacles are required to be
well separated
, a condition that enables local constructions as in the periodic setting.

We also mention here the recent work
[
2
]
where the authors study nonlocal variational problems of convolution type in periodically perforated domains, via
\Gamma
-convergence.

On the other hand, in the local setting, Giunti, Höfer, and Velázquez
[
30
]
significantly extended homogenization results for the Poisson equation in perforated domains by considering perforations generated by a stationary and ergodic marked point process as in (
1.2
), under the assumption that both the centers and the radii exhibit short-range correlations. This framework allows the centers
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to be arbitrarily close and the radii
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
to be arbitrarily large, so that clustering of perforations can occur with probability one (cf.
[
31
,
35
]
).
Assuming finiteness of the average limit capacity density of the perforations (which, in view of the scaling properties of the elliptic capacity, amounts to a suitable moment condition on the radii) the authors prove that, for
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{n/(n-2)}
, a homogenization result analogous to those in
[
15
,
14
]
holds. Their proof is based on the construction of oscillating test functions in the spirit of Cioranescu and Murat, with the main difficulty being to ensure that the presence of clusters does not break down this construction. The key observation in
[
30
]
is that the imposed moment condition and the decorrelation assumption over large distances (formulated as a quantitative strong mixing condition) guarantee that, almost surely, clustering holes have asymptotically negligible capacity.

More recently, a nonlinear counterpart of this result was obtained under similar assumptions by Scardia, Zemas, and Zeppieri
[
39
]
via a purely variational approach, thus providing the first homogenization result of obstacle problems for nonlinear elliptic PDEs (with variational structure) without the assumption of well-separation.

1.3.
Main contributions of the paper and method of proof

In the present paper, we extend the homogenization results of
[
30
,
39
]
to the nonlocal setting. More precisely, we establish a homogenization result for a broad class of nonlocal and nonlinear obstacle problems under minimal assumptions on the geometry and size of the obstacles. Our result also provides a substantial extension, in the nonlocal framework, of the homogenization results obtained in
[
26
,
27
]
. Indeed, our approach does not rely on any separation assumption, thus accommodating genuinely irregular configurations of obstacles. Moreover, the analysis applies to a class of interaction kernels which is significantly larger than the one considered in
[
27
]
.

In contrast to
[
30
,
39
]
, we remove the short-range correlation assumption on the underlying marked point process, which is now only required to be stationary and ergodic (see also
[
6
]
) and to satisfy a suitable moment condition on the obstacle radii. Moreover, we cover also the case of randomly shaped obstacles (see also
[
38
]
).
In the same spirit as
[
27
,
39
]
, our approach is purely variational and avoids the delicate PDE techniques and regularity results employed in
[
9
]
, which are moreover specific to the fractional Laplacian. This yields a robust and unified framework for the stochastic homogenization of a broad class of nonlocal and nonlinear obstacle problems.
In addition, the paper develops a streamlined proof strategy with several methodological simplifications and new ingredients with respect to
[
30
,
39
]
(see the comments hereafter).

We now provide an overview of the main contribution of the paper and outline its proof strategy. For the sake of presentation, we focus on the model case of problem (
1.1
), where the random obstacle set
T_{\varepsilon}
consists of a union of balls with random centers and radii, as defined in (
1.2
) (see Figure
1
).

\end{overpic}

Figure 1.
In blue a realization of the random set
T_{\varepsilon}
. (In the picture the centers of the balls are generated by a Poisson point process, while the radii are i.i.d. random variables with a log-normal distribution.)

To keep the presentation intuitive, we disregard technicalities and assume only that the marked point process
N
is stationary and ergodic. We refer the reader to Section
3

for the complete list of assumptions, as well as for the treatment of the case in which ergodicity is relaxed
(cf. Remark
3.5
).

We observe that problem (
1.1
) has a variational structure. Indeed, for fixed
\varepsilon
, the unique solution to (
1.1
) coincides with the minimizer in the fractional Sobolev space
W^{s,p}_{0}(U)
of the functional

\displaystyle\int_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}-p\int_{U}fu\,\mathrm{d}{x}+\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}(u),

(1.4)

where, for every
\omega\in\Omega
,

\chi_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}(u)=\begin{cases}0&\text{if }\;u=0\,\,\text{ on }T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\cap U,\cr+\infty&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By the fundamental property of
\Gamma
-convergence, the convergence of the functionals in (
1.4
) yields the convergence of the corresponding minimizers and hence of the solutions to (
1.1
). Indeed, we note that in this setting the compactness of minimizing sequences directly follows from the Poincaré Inequality in
W^{s,p}_{0}(U)
. Moreover, since the linear term in (
1.4
) can be treated as a continuous perturbation, it actually suffices to focus on the asymptotic behavior of

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}&\text{ if }u\in W^{s,p}(U),\,{u}=0\,\,\text{ on }T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\cap U,\cr+\infty&\text{otherwise in }\,L^{p}(U).\end{cases}

(1.5)

In the main result of the paper, Theorem
3.2
, we prove that for
sp\in(0,n)
,
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{n/(n-sp)}
, and under the moment condition

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\bigg]<+\infty,

(1.6)

where
Q
is a unit cube in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the random functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}

\Gamma
-converge,
almost surely
, to the deterministic functional given by

\mathcal{F}(u)=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\gamma\int_{U}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}&\text{if }\;u\in W^{s,p}(U),\cr+\infty&\text{otherwise in }\;L^{p}(U),\end{cases}

where

\gamma=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\bigg].

(1.7)

In (
1.7
)
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})
denotes the fractional
p
-capacity, or in short the
(s,p)
-capacity, of the unit ball (see Section
2.2
for its definition). We observe that
\gamma
factorizes into the contribution of the local geometry (via the
(s,p)
-capacity of the fundamental obstacle shape) and that of the global distribution of the obstacles (via the
(n-sp)
-moment of the radii). A similar formula is obtained in

[
30
,
39
]
. Moreover, we refer the reader to Remarks
3.1
,
3.4
, and
3.5
for different characterizations of the capacitary constant
\gamma
.
While the necessity of the moment condition (
1.6
) is immediate from (
1.7
), the main contribution of the present work is to show that (
1.6
) is also sufficient to establish a homogenization result analogous to that of
[
27
]
in this more general setting.

In the same spirit as
[
30
]
, a crucial step in the proof of the homogenization theorem is a decomposition result of the random obstacle set
T_{\varepsilon}
. Roughly speaking, we decompose
T_{\varepsilon}
into the disjoint union of two sets
T_{\varepsilon}^{g}
and
T_{\varepsilon}^{b}
, where
T_{\varepsilon}^{g}
consists of “small” and
\varepsilon
-separated obstacles (the so-called
good
obstacles), while
T_{\varepsilon}^{b}
contains the remaining ones (the so-called
bad
obstacles), in particular clusters (see (
4.1
)-(
4.3
) for the precise definitions). By construction, the obstacles in
T_{\varepsilon}^{g}
are also well separated from a suitable enlargement of the bad set
T_{\varepsilon}^{b}
(see (
4.4
)).
We then show that, almost surely and for
\varepsilon
sufficiently small,

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}\cap Q)\simeq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}^{g}\cap Q)+\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}^{b}\cap Q)

and
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}^{b}\cap Q)\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
. Therefore, thanks to the
\varepsilon
-separation of the obstacles in
T_{\varepsilon}^{g}
, we obtain

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}\cap Q)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}^{g}\cap Q)=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\,\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{\varepsilon x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}.

Above we have also used the choice
\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{n/(n-sp)}
, together with the scaling property of the
(s,p)
-capacity, namely
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{\rho})=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\rho^{n-sp}
. Finally, exploiting the stationarity and ergodicity of
N
together with (
1.6
), invoking the ergodic theorem for marked point processes, we deduce that, almost surely,

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{\varepsilon}\cap Q)=\gamma.

The above heuristic argument shows that the moment condition (
1.6
) is in fact sufficient to ensure that, even in the presence of clusters, when
\lambda_{\varepsilon}
is critical, (with probability one) the
(s,p)
-capacity density of the obstacle set
T_{\varepsilon}
is finite and asymptotically additive, as in the periodic setting.

The proof of Theorem
3.2
builds on the argument outlined above, which, however, is not sufficient on its own to conclude. Indeed, both the lower and upper bound inequalities (cf. Proposition
5.1
and Proposition
5.2
, respectively) require two additional key ingredients: a nonlocal version of the so-called Joining Lemma (cf.
[
27
, Proof of Lemma 3.9]
) and a probabilistic discrete approximation of the capacitary term in the
\Gamma
-limit (cf. Proposition
4.11
).

More precisely, the Joining Lemma (originally proved in the local periodic setting by Ansini and Braides
[
4
]
) is reminiscent of a slicing and averaging argument due to De Giorgi. It allows us to identify suitable annular neighborhoods around the good obstacles where a sequence with equibounded energy can be modified so as to become locally constant without generating additional energy concentration. The resulting sequence can then be used as a competitor in the capacitary problem, and its appropriately chosen constant boundary values enable the reconstruction of the capacitary term in
\mathcal{F}
thanks to a refined version of the ergodic theorem for marked point processes that plays a crucial role (cf. Proposition
2.3
).

To carry out this program, we introduce some technical simplifications and develop some methodological novelties with respect to the approaches in
[
30
,
39
]
.
First, to make the application of the Joining Lemma possible we introduce a substantially simplified good/bad obstacles decomposition (see Section
4.1
), which yields the geometric separation properties required in the proof through a shorter argument than in
[
30
,
39
]
. In particular, instead of relying on
[
30
, Lemma 4.2]
, we select the relevant obstacles by means of a simple truncation procedure depending on a deterministic parameter, which tends to
+\infty
at the end of the argument. Second, we formulate the relevant discrete-to-continuum limit passages in terms of almost sure convergence of suitably rescaled counting measures (see (
2.18
)) associated with the underlying marked point process (see Sections
2.3.2
and
4.5
). This allows us to derive the asymptotic behavior of random sums from general ergodic principles and to recast the corresponding limit computations as convergence-to-integral statements. A key point here is that, rather than passing to the limit only at the level of the original marked point process, we work directly with the
thinned process
(see Section
2.3.4
) associated with the obstacle selection. The probabilistic analysis of this selected configuration relies on Palm measures (see Section
2.3.3
), which allow us to compute the expectation measure of the thinned process as a function of the truncation parameter. This in turn makes it possible to apply the ergodic theorem directly at the level of the selected obstacles.

While in the proof of the lower bound inequality the contribution of clusters can be neglected, the upper bound is more delicate, as one needs to show that the lower bound is optimal along a suitable recovery sequence. In particular, the recovery sequence must be constructed so as to satisfy the pinning condition also on the clustering obstacles. This construction presents difficulties which are, to some extent, analogous to those encountered in the construction of oscillating test functions, although no explicit solution of PDEs is required.
Finally, in the upper bound construction we show that the capacitary contribution is concentrated along the diagonal set in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
and arises from short-range interactions, whereas long range interactions contribute only to the nonlocal term in the
\Gamma
-limit.

To conclude this introduction we note that the
\Gamma
-convergence result described above can be extended to more general interaction kernels and to obstacles with random shapes (cf. Theorem
3.6
). Moreover, the ergodicity assumption on the marked point process can be relaxed, in which case the limiting functional features a random capacitary term (cf. Remark
3.5
).

Preliminaries and notation

Basic notation

2.2.
Fractional capacity

Throughout the paper, we assume that

,

. Let

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T):=\inf_{\{A\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,A\supset T\}}\inf\left\{|u|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:\,u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,u\geq 1\,\mathcal{L}^{n}\text{-a.e. on }A\right\},

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}
zero.

More generally, we shall deal with translation-invariant, even symmetric, homogeneous kernels

One can easily verify that

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}
, namely

c^{-1}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T)\leq\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)\leq c\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T),

(2.5)

for every
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

For fixed
T\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the existence and uniqueness of a function
u
realizing
\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)
is obtained in the
homogeneous space
\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in L^{p^{\ast}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,|u|_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}<+\infty\}
,

p^{\ast}:=\frac{np}{n-sp}
, appealing to the direct methods of the Calculus of Variations and using the strict convexity of the

\mathcal{K}
and the fact that the set
\{u\in\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,\tilde{u}\geq 1\text{ q.e. on }T\}
is
convex and strongly closed. In what follows such a function is referred to as the
\mathscr{K}
-
capacitary potential
for
T
.

For later use, it is also convenient to recall two different notions of relative fractional capacities introduced in
[
27
, Section 2.4]
.
Namely, let
0<r\leq R
, and for
T\subset B_{r}
define

\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R}):\,u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{r},\,\tilde{u}\geq 1\text{ q.e. on }T\right\},

and

C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R}):=\inf\left\{\mathcal{K}(u,\mathbb{R}^{n}):\,u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,u=0\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\overline{B}_{R},\,\tilde{u}\geq 1\text{ q.e. on }T\right\}.

In fact, the former quantity is useful in the proof of the lower-bound inequality (cf. Proposition
5.1
),
while the latter is used in the proof of the upper bound inequality (cf. Proposition
5.2
).
In particular, by the very definitions it is easy to deduce that

\max\{\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T),\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)\}\leq C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R})

(2.6)

for every
T\subset B_{r}
,
0<r<R
.

In what follows we shall prove the uniform convergence of the above defined relative capacities to the global one.
To this aim it is convenient to introduce some notation to simplify the calculations below:
for any
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable function
w
and any
\mathcal{L}^{2n}
-measurable
subset
E
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the
locality defect
of the functional
\mathcal{K}
as

\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}(w,E):=\int_{E}\mathscr{K}(x-y)|w(x)-w(y)|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}.

Hence, by definition and being
\mathscr{K}
even symmetric, for any pair of disjoint
\mathcal{L}^{n}
-measurable subsets
A
and
A^{\prime}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we have

\mathcal{K}(w,A\cup A^{\prime})=\mathcal{K}(w,A)+\mathcal{K}(w,A^{\prime})+2\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}(w,A\times A^{\prime}).

(2.7)

The next result establishes some useful properties of the relative capacities introduced above.
It extends
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
proved for the standard kernel
|\cdot|^{-(n+sp)}
to the more general class defined in (
2.2
).
The strategy of proof is that of
[
27
, Lemma 2.12]
with the additional insight that the
\mathscr{K}
-capacitary potential of the ball is vanishing at infinity, as a consequence of the regularity theory developed in
[
22
]
.
In what follows it is useful to notice that if
x\in B_{R}

\int_{B_{R}^{c}}\frac{1}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim_{n,s,p}\mathrm{dist}^{-sp}(x,\partial B_{R})\,.

(2.8)

The latter estimate is a consequence of a direct integration using polar coordinates
(see
[
27
, Lemma A.1]
).

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
\mathscr{K}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\to[0,\infty)
be
\mathcal{L}^{n}
measurable and satisfy (
2.2
).
For every
\rho>0
it holds

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\sup_{T\subseteq B_{\rho}}|C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{r})-\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)|=0.

(2.9)

Moreover,
for every
0<\rho<r<R

\sup_{T\subseteq B_{\rho}}\left(\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)-\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)\right)\lesssim_{n,s,p}\frac{r^{sp}}{(R-r)^{sp}}C_{\mathscr{K}}(B_{\rho},B_{r}).

(2.10)

In addition, if
R(r)>0
is such that
R(r)/r\to+\infty
as
r\to+\infty
, then

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\sup_{T\subseteq B_{\rho}}|\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)-\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R(r)};r)|=0\,,

(2.11)

and

\lim_{r\to+\infty}\sup_{T\subseteq B_{\rho}}\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}(\xi_{r}^{T},B_{R(r)}\times{B}_{R(r)}^{c})=0,

(2.12)

for every function
\xi_{r}^{T}
such that

\mathcal{K}(\xi_{r}^{T},\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{r})+\frac{1}{r}.

(2.13)

Proof.

Let
\rho>0
,
T\subseteq B_{\rho}
,
r>\rho
be fixed.

Let us first prove (
2.9
). To this aim let
u^{T}
,
u^{B_{\rho}}\in\dot{W}^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be the

\mathscr{K}
-capacitary potentials of
T
and
B_{\rho}
, respectively; then
0\leq u^{T}\leq u^{B_{\rho}}\leq 1

\mathcal{L}^{n}
a.e. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
by
[
27
, Lemma 2.11]
. In addition,
u^{B_{\rho}}
satisfies

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}u^{B_{\rho}}(x)=0

(2.14)

in view of the results contained in
[
25
]
.

With fixed
\delta>0
consider the Lipschitz map

\psi_{\delta}(t):=\frac{t-\delta}{1-\delta}\vee 0
,

\mathrm{Lip}(\psi_{\delta})\leq(1-\delta)^{-1}
, and set

w_{\delta}(x):=\psi_{\delta}(u^{T}(x))
. Up to
\mathcal{L}^{n}
negligible sets,
\{w_{\delta}>0\}=\{u^{T}>\delta\}\subseteq\{u^{B_{\rho}}>\delta\}\subseteq B_{R_{\delta}}
, for some
R_{\delta}\to+\infty

as
\delta\to 0^{+}
. Then
w_{\delta}\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with

\mathcal{K}(w_{\delta},\mathbb{R}^{n})\leq\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{p}}\mathcal{K}(u^{T},\mathbb{R}^{n})=\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{p}}\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T),\quad\|w_{\delta}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\frac{1}{1-\delta}\|u^{T}\|_{L^{p}(B_{R_{\delta}})}.

Moreover,
\tilde{w}_{\delta}\geq 1
q.e. on
T
, and being
\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(\cdot)
an increasing set function, we conclude that

0\leq C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,R_{\delta})-\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)\leq\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{p}}-1\right)\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)\leq\left(\frac{1}{(1-\delta)^{p}}-1\right)\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(B_{\rho}).

In conclusion, (
2.9
) follows since
(0,+\infty)\ni r\to C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{r})
is monotone decreasing.

To prove estimate (
2.10
), let
u\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
be any admissible function
for the minimum problem defining
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T,B_{R};r)
, then
u
is also admissible
for the one defining
\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T)
. Using the estimate in (
2.8
) we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)\leq\mathcal{K}(u,\mathbb{R}^{n})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:locality defect}}}{{=}}\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R})+\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R}^{c})+2\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}(u,B_{R}\times B_{R}^{c})

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle u|_{B_{r}^{c}}=0}}{{=}}\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R})+2\int_{B_{r}}|u(x)|^{p}\int_{B^{c}_{R}}\mathscr{K}(x-y)\mathrm{d}{y}\mathrm{d}{x}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{e:Kernels},\eqref{e:stima Adams}}}{{\lesssim_{n,s,p}}}\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R})+c\int_{B_{r}}\frac{|u(x)|^{p}}{\mathrm{dist}^{sp}(x,\partial B_{R})}dx

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R})+\frac{c}{(R-r)^{sp}}\int_{B_{r}}|u(x)|^{p}dx\lesssim_{n,s,p}\mathcal{K}(u,B_{R})+\frac{cr^{sp}}{(R-r)^{sp}}\mathcal{K}(u,B_{r})\,,

where in the last inequality we used the scaled version of the Poincaré Inequality on balls and again (
2.2
).
By passing to the infimum over the admissible test functions we infer

\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T)-\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)\leq\frac{cr^{sp}}{(R-r)^{sp}}\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)\leq\frac{cr^{sp}}{(R-r)^{sp}}C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{r}).

We deduce statement (
2.10
) since
C_{\mathscr{K}}(\cdot,B_{r})

is a monotone increasing set function.

The limit in (
2.11
) follows at once from formulas (
2.9
), (
2.10
), and the inequality in (
2.6
).

Finally, let
\xi_{r}^{T}
satisfy (
2.13
). Then
\xi_{r}^{T}
is admissible for the problem defining

\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)
, so that for all
r<R
we get

\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{R};r)\leq\mathcal{K}(\xi_{r}^{T},B_{R})\leq C_{\mathscr{K}}(T,B_{r})+\frac{1}{r}\,.

Therefore, recalling the definition of
\mathcal{D}_{\mathcal{K}}
, by combining (
2.9
) and (
2.11
)
we get (
2.12
).
∎

2.3.
Marked point processes

For a comprehensive treatment of marked point processes we refer the reader to the monographs
[
19
,
20
]
. Below we recall some notions and results which are relevant to the problem under examination.

2.3.1.
Basic definitions

Let
X
be a topological space. The Borel
\sigma
-algebra on
X
is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(X)
. We say that a Borel measure is boundedly finite if it is finite on every bounded Borel set.
Let
\mathcal{N}
be the set of boundedly finite integer-valued measures on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
. For any
\mu\in\mathcal{N}
, we define its ground measure
\mu_{g}
as

\mu_{g}(A)=\mu(A\times\mathbb{R}_{+}),

for every
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If
\mu_{g}
is a simple counting measure on
\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
i.e.
, if
\mu_{g}(\{x\})\in\{0,1\}
for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we write
\mu\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
.

We say that a sequence
\{\mu_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{N}

w^{\#}
-converges (“weak hash” converges) to
\mu\in\mathcal{N}
if

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}_{j}\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f\,\mathrm{d}{\mu}

for every bounded continuous function
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
vanishing outside a bounded set. This notion of convergence defines the
w^{\#}
-topology (“weak hash” topology) on
\mathcal{N}
, whose Borel
\sigma
-algebra is denoted by
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})
. The
w^{\#}
-topology is metrizable on
\mathcal{N}
(see
[
19
, A2.6.1]
).
Moreover, by
[
20
, Proposition 9.1.IV]
,
\mathcal{N}
, with the
w^{\#}
-topology, is a complete separable metric space and
\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})
is the smallest
\sigma
-algebra with respect to which the mappings
\mathcal{N}\ni\mu\mapsto\mu(B)
are measurable for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. In all that follows follows, whenever a statement holds true
\mathbb{P}
-a.e. in
\Omega
we may equivalently write
almost surely
.

A
marked point process
(m.p.p.) on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a measurable mapping
N\colon(\Omega,\mathscr{F})\rightarrow(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}))
such that
\mathbb{P}(N\in\widehat{\mathcal{N}})=1
.
Let
N
be a m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
;
for every
\omega\in\Omega
, set
N^{\omega}:=N(\omega)
. The probability law of
N
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}:=\mathbb{P}\circ N^{-1}
. Define
\widehat{\Omega}:=N^{-1}(\widehat{\mathcal{N}})
; clearly
\widehat{\Omega}\subset\Omega
and
\mathbb{P}(\widehat{\Omega})=1
.

For
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
we define the shift operator
S_{y}:\mathcal{N}\to\mathcal{N}
as

(S_{y}\mu)(B):=\mu(S_{y}^{\prime}B),

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
, where

S^{\prime}_{y}B:=\{(x+y,\rho):(x,\rho)\in B\}.

We say that
N
is stationary (with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
) if

\mathcal{P}\circ S_{y}=\mathcal{P}\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(2.15)

Moreover, we say that
N
is ergodic (with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
) if, in addition,

E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}),\quad S_{y}E=E\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\Rightarrow\,\mathcal{P}(E)\in\{0,1\}.

(2.16)

Now suppose that
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is a flow of
\mathbb{P}
-preserving transformations on
\Omega
,
i.e.
, a group of transformations
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}:\Omega\to\Omega
such that
(y,\omega)\mapsto\tau_{y}\omega
is a measurable map from
(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega,\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\mathscr{F})
to
(\Omega,\mathscr{F})
and
\mathbb{P}\circ\tau_{y}=\mathbb{P}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We say that
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is ergodic if

F\in\mathscr{F},\quad\tau_{y}F=F\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\,\Rightarrow\,\mathbb{P}(F)\in\{0,1\}.

We observe that if

N^{\tau_{y}\omega}=S_{y}N^{\omega},

(2.17)

for every
(y,\omega)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\Omega
, then (
2.15
) holds true. Moreover, if additionally
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
is ergodic, then (
2.16
) holds also true.

Following
[
20
, Chapter 9.5]
we introduce the
expectation measures
of
N
and
N_{g}
defined, respectively, as

M(B):=\mathbb{E}[N(B)],\quad M_{g}(A):=\mathbb{E}[N_{g}(A)]

for every
B\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})
and
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
\mathbb{E}
denotes the expected value with respect to the probability measure
\mathbb{P}
. Throughout this section we tacitly assume that
M
and
M_{g}
are boundedly finite.

Thanks to
[
19
, Lemma A2.7.II]
(see also the comments before
[
20
, Lemma 12.2.III]
) property (
2.15
) entails

M=\mathcal{L}^{n}\times\nu,

where
\nu(\mathbb{R}_{+})<\infty
, since
M_{g}
is boundedly finite. Therefore,

\nu
can be normalized and
M
can be rewritten as

M=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}\times\pi,

where
m_{g}:=\mathbb{E}[N_{g}(Q)]\in[0,+\infty)
and
\pi
is a probability measure on
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
. The constant
m_{g}
is referred to as the
intensity of the ground process
.

2.3.2.
Ergodic theorems

For later purposes, in this section we collect some useful consequences of the Ergodic Theorem for stationary and ergodic marked point processes
[
20
, Theorem 12.2.IV and Corollary 12.2.V]
.

We start recalling that, since
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
is a complete separable metric space, we can appeal to
[
20
, Lemma 9.1.XIII]
to deduce the existence of a sequence
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued random variables and a sequence
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
of
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued random variables such that almost surely

N=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{(x_{i},\rho_{i})}.

Moreover, for every
\varepsilon>0
, we define the rescaled measures

N_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{(\varepsilon x_{i},\rho_{i})},\quad N_{g,\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{\varepsilon x_{i}}.

(2.18)

The following result is a well known consequence of the Ergodic Theorem for stationary and ergodic point processes
[
20
, Theorem 12.2.IV and Corollary 12.2.V]
and can be found, for example, in
[
24
, Claim 7.1]
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
N
be a stationary and ergodic m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then
there exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{g,\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}{M_{g}}(x),

for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and every
f\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

An analogous result holds true for
N_{\varepsilon}
as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
N
be a stationary and ergodic m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then, for every
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\pi)
there exists
\Omega_{h}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{M}(x,\rho),

for every
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
and every
f\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

As a simple consequence, we also have the following result.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
N
be a stationary and ergodic m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then, for every
h\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{+},\pi)
there exists
\Omega_{h}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
such that

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}U)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho),

for every
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
and for every
U\in\mathcal{A}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
bounded and Lipschitz.

The corresponding proofs are postponed to Appendix
A
.

2.3.3.
Palm distributions

In this section we recall the notion of
Palm distributions
for a stationary marked point process. These are introduced through a refined version of the Campbell Theorem (see
[
20
, Theorem 13.2.III]
and the introduction to
[
20
, Section 13.4]
).

Theorem 2.5

(Refined Campbell’s Theorem)
.

Let
N
be a stationary and ergodic m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Then there exists a family
\{\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}\}_{\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{+}}
of probability measures on
(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}))
, called Palm distributions associated to the m.p.p.
N
, such that

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x,\rho,N)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{N}}f(x,\rho,S_{-x}\mu)\,\mathrm{d}{M}(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}}(\mu),

(2.19)

for every nonnegative
(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}))
-measurable function
f
.

We notice that, taking
f(x,\rho,\mu)=\tilde{f}(x,\rho,S_{x}\mu)
for a nonnegative
(\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})\otimes\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}))
-measurable function
\tilde{f}
, (
2.19
) can be equivalently written as

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x,\rho,S_{x}N)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{N}}f(x,\rho,\mu)\,\mathrm{d}{M}(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}}(\mu).

(2.20)

Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be such that
M(A\times A^{\prime})\in(0,\infty)
.
Choosing
f(x,\rho,\mu)=\mathds{1}_{A}(x)\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}(\rho)\mathds{1}_{E}(\mu)
, with
E\in\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N})
, by (
2.20
) we get

\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(E)=\frac{1}{M(A\times A^{\prime})}\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathds{1}_{A}(x)\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}(\rho)\mathds{1}_{E}(S_{x}N)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)\right].

Consider now the set of measures
\mathcal{N}_{(0,\rho)}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{N}:\mu(\{(0,\rho)\})>0\}
. Clearly, if
(x,\rho)\in\supp N
, then
(0,\rho)\in\supp S_{x}N
, and thus
\mathds{1}_{\mathcal{N}_{(0,\rho)}}(S_{x}N)=1
. The latter equality readily implies that
\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(\mathcal{N}_{(0,\rho)})=1
, hence
\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}
is concentrated on
\mathcal{N}_{(0,\rho)}
. Moreover, since
\mathcal{N}_{(0,\rho)}\subset\mathcal{N}_{0}:=\{\mu\in\mathcal{N}:\mu(\{0\}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})>0\}
we also have
\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(\mathcal{N}_{0})=1
. Similarly, since
\mathcal{P}(\widehat{\mathcal{N}})=1
, we have
\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(\widehat{\mathcal{N}})=1
.

2.3.4.
Thinned process

In this section we introduce some notion of thinning of point processes. Let
N
be a m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
;
for
\delta>0
fixed, we define the thinned process

N^{\delta}:=\sum_{i\in I^{\delta}}\delta_{(x_{i},\rho_{i})}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{Y_{\delta}}(S_{x_{i}}N)\delta_{(x_{i},\rho_{i})},

with

I^{\delta}:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:\min_{j\neq i}|x_{i}-x_{j}|\geq\delta\},\quad Y_{\delta}=\{\mu\in\mathcal{N}:\mu(B_{\delta}\times\mathbb{R}_{+})=1\}.

We observe that in the context of homogeneous Poisson point processes
N^{\delta}
is known as
first Matérn hard-core process

[
34
]
.

Now let
N
be stationary and ergodic with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Since the map
N\mapsto N^{\delta}
commutes with
S_{y}
for every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, it is immediate to deduce that
N^{\delta}
is stationary and ergodic with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. Therefore, using Theorem
2.5
, we can compute the expectation measure of
N^{\delta}
as follows.
Let
A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
; in view of the definition of
N^{\delta}
we have

\begin{split}\mathbb{E}[N^{\delta}(A\times A^{\prime})]&=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{Y_{\delta}}(S_{x_{i}}N)\mathds{1}_{A}(x_{i})\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}(\rho_{i})\right]\\
&=\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathds{1}_{A}(x)\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}(\rho)\mathds{1}_{Y_{\delta}}(S_{x}N)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)\right]\\
&=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathcal{N}}\mathds{1}_{A}(x)\mathds{1}_{A^{\prime}}(\rho)\mathds{1}_{Y_{\delta}}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}{M}(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}}(\mu)\\
&=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)\int_{A^{\prime}}\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(Y_{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho).\end{split}

(2.21)

Therefore, thanks to (
2.21
), the expectation measure
M^{\delta}
of the thinned process
N^{\delta}
can be expressed in terms of the Palm measure
\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}
as

M^{\delta}=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}\times(\mathcal{P}_{(0,\cdot)}(Y_{\delta})\pi).

Furthermore, taking
A^{\prime}=\mathbb{R}_{+}
in (
2.21
), we deduce that the expectation measure
M_{g}^{\delta}
of the ground process
N_{g}^{\delta}
is given by

M_{g}^{\delta}=m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(Y_{\delta})\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\,\mathcal{L}^{n}.

We conclude this section by observing that

\lim_{\delta\downarrow 0}\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(Y_{\delta})=1,

(2.22)

for every
\rho\in\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Indeed, we have

\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}(Y_{\delta})=\int_{\mathcal{N}_{0}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{N}}}\mathds{1}_{Y_{\delta}}(\mu)\,\mathrm{d}{\mathcal{P}_{(0,\rho)}}(\mu);

moreover, every
\mu\in\mathcal{N}_{0}\cap\widehat{\mathcal{N}}
belongs to
Y_{\delta}
for sufficiently small
\delta>0
. Hence, (
2.22
) follows by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.

3.
Statement of the main result

In this section we introduce the random nonlocal functionals we are going to study and state the main result of this work.

3.1.
Assumptions

Let
N
be a m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. As already observed,
N
can be written as a countable sum of Dirac deltas;
i.e.
,

N=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{(x_{i},\rho_{i})}\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.}

for some
\mathbb{R}^{n}
-valued random variables
\{x_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}
-valued random variables
\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.

We assume that
N
satisfies the following hypotheses:

(H1)

N
is stationary and ergodic;

(H2)

the expectation measures
M
and
M_{g}
are boundedly finite;

(H3)

m_{g}:=\mathbb{E}[N_{g}(Q)]>0
;

(H4)

\{\rho_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfy the moment condition

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\bigg]<+\infty.

(3.1)

Remark 3.1

(On the assumptions)
.

Some comments on hypotheses
(H1)
-
(H4)
are in order.

We notice that thanks to
(H1)
and
(H2)
we know that
M=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}\times\pi
, with
m_{g}<+\infty
and
\pi
probability measure on
(\mathbb{R}_{+},\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}_{+}))
, while
(H3)
ensures that the ground process
N_{g}
is nontrivial.

Moreover, in view of Theorem
2.5
, by choosing
f(x,\rho,N)=\mathds{1}_{Q}(x)\,\rho^{n-sp}
in (
2.19
), we obtain

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\bigg]=m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho).

(3.2)

Therefore, the moment condition (
3.1
) in
(H4)
can be equivalently replaced by

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)<+\infty.

(3.3)

3.2.
The functionals

Let
U\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open, bounded, and Lipschitz. For
\omega\in\widehat{\Omega}
and
\varepsilon>0
consider the set of indices

I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}:=\{i\in\mathbb{N}:x_{i}(\omega)\in\varepsilon^{-1}U\}

and the parameter

\lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\varepsilon^{\frac{n}{n-sp}}.

We define the
obstacle set

T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}
as

T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}:=\bigcup_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}T_{\varepsilon}^{i,\omega},

where

T_{\varepsilon}^{i,\omega}:=B_{\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}(\omega)}(\varepsilon x_{i}(\omega)).

Finally, we define the functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}:L^{p}(U)\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
as

\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}&\text{ if }u\in W^{s,p}(U),\,\tilde{u}=0\,\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\hbox{-}\text{q.e. on }T_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\cap U,\\
\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise. }\end{cases}

(3.4)

3.3.
The main result

We are now in a position to state the main theorem of this work, that is, an
almost sure

\Gamma
-convergence result for the random functionals
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}
.

Theorem 3.2

(
\Gamma
-convergence)
.

Let
N
be a m.p.p. on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with marks in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
satisfying
(H1)
-
(H4)
. Let
\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}
be the functionals defined as in (
3.4
). Then there exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that, for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
(\mathcal{F}_{\varepsilon}^{\omega})

\Gamma
-converges in the
L^{p}(U)
-topology, as
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, to the deterministic functional
\mathcal{F}:L^{p}(U)\longrightarrow[0,+\infty]
given by

\mathcal{F}(u):=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\int_{U\times U}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\gamma\int_{U}|u|^{p}\mathrm{d}{x}&\text{ if }u\in W^{s,p}(U),\\
\\
+\infty&\text{ otherwise, }\end{cases}

where

\gamma:=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\bigg].

Some remarks are in order.

Remark 3.3

(Periodically distributed obstacles with random radii)
.

We observe that the case of periodically distributed obstacles with random radii can be recovered as a special case of Theorem
3.2
.

To this end, let
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
be an enumeration of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. We notice that the ground process corresponding to
\{z_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
is stationary with respect to
integer
translations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, but not stationary with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
; therefore this does not satisfy
(H1)
. Hence, to produce a ground process which is both periodic and

(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
-stationary we need to consider a random translation of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
as follows. Let
\zeta
be a uniformly distributed random variable on
[0,1)^{n}
. For any
i\in\mathbb{N}
, let
x_{i}:=z_{i}+\zeta
. Then the ground process
N_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{x_{i}}
is stationary with respect to
(S_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
. To see that, we fix
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and we define the random variable
\zeta_{y}
by

\zeta_{y}:=(\zeta-y)\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb{Z}^{n},

where
(\cdot)\,\mathrm{mod}\,\mathbb{Z}^{n}
denotes the unique representative in
[0,1)^{n}
. We also define

z_{y}:=(\zeta-y)-\zeta_{y}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n},

so that
\zeta-y=\zeta_{y}+z_{y}
. Observe that
\zeta_{y}
and
\zeta
have the same probability distribution. Moreover,

S_{y}N_{g}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{z_{i}+\zeta-y}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{z_{i}+\zeta_{y}+z_{y}}=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{z_{i}+\zeta_{y}},

since clearly
\mathbb{Z}^{n}+z_{y}=\mathbb{Z}^{n}
. Hence,
S_{y}N_{g}
has the same probability distribution as
\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\delta_{z_{i}+\zeta}=N_{g}
.
Furthermore, for every
\omega\in\Omega
,
N_{g}^{\omega}
is
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-periodic, being a translated copy of
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
.

Remark 3.4

(Wald identity)
.

We observe that, under some additional assumptions on the m.p.p., the constant
\gamma
in Theorem
3.2
has a more explicit expression in the spirit of the classical Wald Identity (see e.g.
[
7
]
). Namely,
besides stationarity and ergodicity, suppose that the random variables
\rho_{i}^{n-sp}
and
\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})
are independent for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
. Moreover, assume that
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{i}^{n-sp}]=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}]
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then we have

\gamma=m_{g}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}].

Indeed, under these assumptions,
\mathbb{E}[\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})]=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}]\mathbb{E}[\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})]
for every
i\in\mathbb{N}
, and hence, since
\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})\geq 0
,
by a consequence of the Monotone Convergence Theorem
[
29
, Theorem 2.15]
, we obtain

\begin{split}\gamma&=\mathbb{E}\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}=\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})]\\
&=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}]\,\mathbb{E}\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}\mathds{1}_{Q}(x_{i})=\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}]\,\mathbb{E}[N_{g}(Q)]=m_{g}\mathbb{E}[\rho_{1}^{n-sp}].\end{split}

Remark 3.5

(Stationary nonergodic m.p.p.)
.

It is also worth mentioning here that Theorem
3.2
is stated and proven in the ergodic case for notational simplicity only. In fact, if in
(H1)
the ergodicity assumption is dropped, then Theorem
3.2
still holds up to replacing the constant
\gamma
with the random variable
\kappa
defined as follows

\kappa^{\omega}:=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\,\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\,\big|\,\mathcal{I}\bigg](\omega).

(3.5)

Here we additionally assume that the probability space
(\Omega,\mathscr{F},\mathbb{P})
where
N
is defined is endowed with a flow of
\mathbb{P}
-preserving transformations
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
(see Section
2.3.1
for the definition) satisfying (
2.17
).
Moreover, in (
3.5
),
\mathcal{I}
denotes the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
(\tau_{y})_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}}
-invariant events;
i.e.
,

\mathcal{I}:=\{E\in\mathscr{F}:\tau_{y}E=E\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\}

and
\mathbb{E}[\cdot|\mathcal{I}]
a version of the conditional expectation given
\mathcal{I}
.
This additional structure is only used to formulate the nonergodic case on the underlying sample space and is not restrictive, since one may canonically introduce a new probability space
(\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathscr{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}}):=(\mathcal{N},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{N}),\mathcal{P})
, endowed with the flow
\tilde{\tau}_{y}:=S_{y}
, together with the marked point process
\tilde{N}(\omega):=\omega
, which has the same law as
N
and satisfies all the required properties.

By definition of conditional expectation, we readily obtain that
\mathbb{E}[\kappa]=\gamma
. Then, by
(H4)
we get that
\kappa^{\omega}<+\infty
for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

Furthermore, as in the ergodic case,
\kappa
can be rewritten in terms of an integral over
\mathbb{R}_{+}
with respect to some
random
measure. Specifically, if
\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
denotes the set of boundedly finite measures on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, by
[
20
, Lemma 12.2.III]
there exists an
\mathcal{I}
-measurable random measure
\psi
on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
,
i.e.
, a measurable mapping from
(\Omega,\mathcal{I})
to
(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}},\mathcal{B}(\mathcal{M}_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}))
, such that almost surely

\mathbb{E}\left[\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)\,\middle|\,\mathcal{I}\right]=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x,\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{\psi}(\rho),

(3.6)

for all nonnegative measurable functions
f
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
Then, choosing
f(x,\rho)=\mathds{1}_{Q}(x)\rho^{n-sp}
, we get

\kappa^{\omega}=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\,\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\psi^{\omega}}(\rho),

for
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega\in\Omega
.

We conclude this remark by observing that a proof of the
\Gamma
-convergence result in the stationary case can be obtained from the proof in the ergodic case with minor changes.

For later purposes, we start by defining some suitable sets of indices together with the corresponding sets of obstacles.

with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in F_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}=0,

whenever
F_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}\subset I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}
for every
\varepsilon>0
,
R\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#F_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}=0.

(4.6)

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
be given by Lemma
4.2
.
Then, for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
and every
L\in\mathbb{N}
, in view of (
4.6
) we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{R\uparrow\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in F^{\omega}_{\varepsilon,R}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\\
&\leq\limsup_{R\uparrow\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in F^{\omega}_{\varepsilon,R}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{[0,L]}(\rho_{i}(\omega))+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{(L,\infty)}(\rho_{i}(\omega))\\
&\leq L^{n-sp}\limsup_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#F^{\omega}_{\varepsilon,R}+m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)\int_{L}^{\infty}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\\
&\leq m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)\int_{L}^{\infty}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho).\end{split}

Then, the claim follows by the arbitrariness of
L
and by the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

4.3.
Capacity of the not very good obstacles

In this subsection we show that the capacity of the union of the
not very good
obstacles is infinitesimal. To this end, we preliminarily need to prove a number of lemmas.

Lemma 4.4
.

There exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\|\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}(\omega)\|_{\ell^{q}(I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega})}=0\,,

(4.7)

for every
q\in(n-sp,\infty]
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
be the intersection of the events of probability one given by Lemma
4.2
and Lemma
4.3
, respectively, and let
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
.
Set
F_{\varepsilon}:=\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}:\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}>\varepsilon\}
.
Recalling that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{n-sp}=\varepsilon^{n}
, by the very definition of
F_{\varepsilon}
and by Lemma
4.2
we get

\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#F_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{sp}\sum_{i\in F_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}(\omega))^{n-sp}\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{sp}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}=0\,.

(4.8)

For every
q\in(n-sp,\infty)
, writing
I_{\varepsilon}=(I_{\varepsilon}\setminus F_{\varepsilon})\cup F_{\varepsilon}
, in view of the continuous embedding
\ell^{n-sp}(I_{\varepsilon})\hookrightarrow\ell^{q}(I_{\varepsilon})
(with embedding constant equal to one)
we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i})^{q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}\setminus F_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i})^{q}+\sum_{i\in F_{\varepsilon}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i})^{q}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{q-sp}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}\setminus F_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}+\Big(\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in F_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\Big)^{\frac{q}{n-sp}}\,,

(4.9)

where in the last line we used again that
\lambda_{\varepsilon}^{n-sp}=\varepsilon^{n}
.
Then (
4.7
) follows from (
4.3
),
Lemma
4.2
, and
Lemma
4.3
also invoking (
4.8
).

Finally, by taking the
q
-th root in (
4.3
), using the Minkowski Inequality, and letting
q\to\infty
, it is easy to infer that

\lambda_{\varepsilon}\max_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}\leq\varepsilon+\Big(\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in F_{\varepsilon}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\Big)^{\frac{1}{n-sp}}\,,

hence Lemma
4.3

yields the conclusion for
q=\infty
, as well.
∎

The next lemma provides us with some useful information concerning the cardinality of the of indices introduced in Subsection
4.1
which are instrumental for the sequel.

Lemma 4.5
.

There exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
:

(a)

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#(I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R,\omega})=0
;

(b)

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#(I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R,\omega}\setminus G_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega})=0
;

(c)

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#(G_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}\setminus VG_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega})=0
;

(d)

\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#NVG_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}=0
.

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
be the intersection of the events of probability one given by Lemmas
4.1
-
4.4
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
.

We start by proving statement
(a)
. By the

4.1
and by the Lipschitz regularity of
\partial U
,
[

, Proposition 1.62(b)]
implies

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#(I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R,\omega})=\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}(N^{\omega}_{g,\varepsilon}(\varepsilon^{-1}U)-N_{g,\varepsilon}^{2/R,\omega}(\varepsilon^{-1}U))\\

hence
(a)
is proven.

We now turn to the proof of
(b)
. To this end, we observe that

I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R}\setminus G_{\varepsilon,R}\subset\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}:\rho_{i}>R\}\cup\left\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R}:\mathrm{dist}(\varepsilon x_{i},\partial U)>\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right\}.

(4.10)

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}:\rho_{i}(\omega)>R\}\leq\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\mathds{1}_{(R,\infty)}(\rho_{i}(\omega))\\

Finally, since

\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#\left\{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R}:\mathrm{dist}(\varepsilon x_{i},\partial U)>\frac{\varepsilon}{R}\right\}\lesssim_{n}R^{n}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\mathcal{L}^{n}((\partial U)_{2\frac{\varepsilon}{R}})=0.

(4.12)

Therefore, the statement in
(b)
follows by gathering (
4.10
)-(

).

To prove
(c)
we start by observing that

G_{\varepsilon,R}\setminus VG_{\varepsilon,R}=\{i\in G_{\varepsilon,R}:\exists j\in I_{\varepsilon},\,j\neq i,\,B_{\frac{\varepsilon}{R}}(\varepsilon x_{i})\cap B_{2\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{j}}(\varepsilon x_{j})\neq\emptyset\}.

(4.13)

By the very definition of
G_{\varepsilon,R}
, we notice that if

and

), then necessarily

, where

,

, such that

with

and to (

)

\begin{split}&\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#E_{\varepsilon,R}^{1,\omega}\lesssim R^{n}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}(\omega))^{n}+\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#F^{\omega}_{\varepsilon,R}=0.\end{split}

Similarly, again invoking Lemma
4.4
with

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#E_{\varepsilon,R}^{2,\omega}\lesssim\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}R^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(\lambda_{\varepsilon}\rho_{i}(\omega))^{n}+\lim_{R\uparrow\infty}\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\#(I_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}\setminus I_{\varepsilon}^{2/R,\omega})=0,\end{split}

Finally, we notice that the convergence statement in

\lim_{k\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}|(u)_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(v_{k})_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}\lesssim_{n,m,R}\lim_{k\to\infty}\lVert u-v_{k}\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}=0.

Hence, without loss of generality, we can assume that
u\in C^{\infty}_{c}(U)
. Let
\eta>0
be arbitrary and fixed; since
u
is uniformly continuous, for sufficiently large

|u(x)-u(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})|<\eta
for every

and

, Thus, we obtain

\begin{split}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}|(u)_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-u(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})|^{p}\leq\eta^{p}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\#I_{j}=\eta^{p}m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)\end{split}

and the arbitrariness of

\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}|(u)_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-u(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})|^{p}=0.

(4.24)

Then, (
4.22
) follows by (
4.20
) in Lemma
4.10
also observing that, by Lemma
4.5
-
(b)
, we have

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{j}^{2/R}\setminus G_{j,R}}|u(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})|^{p}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\#(I_{j}^{2/R}\setminus G_{j,R})=0\,.

We now turn to the proof of (
4.23
).

Since
\rho_{i}\leq R
whenever
i\in G_{j,R}
, arguing as above readily gives

\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}=0.

Finally, by combining (

4.20
) in Lemma
4.21
, Lemma
4.3
, and Lemma
4.5
-
(b)

we deduce

\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{j}^{2/R}\setminus G_{j,R}}|u(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})|^{p}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}\leq\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\lim_{R\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{j}^{2/R}\setminus G_{j,R}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}=0

and hence the claim.
∎

Proof of the main result

This section is primarily devoted to the proof of the almost sure
\Gamma
-convergence result stated in Theorem

. The argument proceeds in two main steps, establishing separately the lower bound and the upper bound inequalities. At the end of this section we outline the proof of Theorem

We start proving the lower-bound inequality.

Proposition 5.1
.

There exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
\varepsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
,
u\in L^{p}(U)
, and

in

\mathcal{F}(u)\leq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(u_{j}).

(5.1)

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
be as in Proposition
4.11
and
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
.
We assume that the right-hand side in (
5.1
) is finite otherwise the liminf-inequality is trivially satisfied. Up to possibly extracting a subsequence, we can additionally assume that the liminf in (
5.1
) is actually a limit. As a consequence we immediately deduce that
\sup_{j\in\mathbb{N}}|u_{j}|_{W^{s,p}(U)}<+\infty
and
\tilde{u}_{j}=0

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}
-q.e. on
T_{j}^{\omega}\cap U
for every
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Fix
R,L\in\mathbb{N}
,
\delta>0
and define
m_{R}:=\lfloor R^{\frac{n-sp+1}{sp}}\rfloor
.
Let
\{w_{j}^{\omega}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be the sequence given by Lemma
4.9
applied with
G=G_{\varepsilon,R}^{\omega}
and

m
replaced by
m_{R}
. In view of (
4.19
) we can find a constant
c_{n,s,p,R}>0
such that

\left(1+\frac{c_{n,s,p,R}}{L}\right)\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(u_{j})\geq\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(w_{j}^{\omega}).

(5.2)

Up to a possible further subsequence, we may assume that the liminf in the right-hand side of (
5.2
) is a limit and that
w_{j}^{\omega}\to u

\mathcal{L}^{n}
-a.e. in
U
.
We observe that
\cup_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}\times B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})\subset\Delta_{\delta}
, for sufficiently large
j
. Therefore, we have

\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(w_{j}^{\omega})\geq\int_{(U\times U)\setminus\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|w_{j}^{\omega}(x)-w_{j}^{\omega}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})},

so that Fatou’s Lemma entails

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(w_{j}^{\omega})\geq\int_{(U\times U)\setminus\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}.

(5.3)

Recalling the definition of
\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
in (
4.18
) we observe that

\begin{split}&\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}\\
&\geq\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}\inf\{|w|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}:w\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\,w=0\text{ on }C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}},\,\tilde{w}=(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}\,\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\text{-q.e.}\text{ on }T_{j}^{i,\omega}\}\\
&=\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(T_{j}^{i,\omega},\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}},m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1})\\
&=\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}\lambda_{j}^{n-sp}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}(\omega)},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-1}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}},m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}).\end{split}

Since
B_{\rho_{i}(\omega)}\subset B_{R}
for every
i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}
, appealing to (
2.10
) in Lemma
2.1

we get

\sup_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}(\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}(\omega)})-\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{\rho_{i}(\omega)},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-1}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}},m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}))\\
\leq\frac{c_{n,s,p}}{(m_{R}-1)^{sp}}C_{s,p}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}}),

for some
c_{n,s,p}>0
; therefore

\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}\geq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\liminf_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\\
-\frac{c_{n,s,p}}{(m_{R}-1)^{sp}}\limsup_{j\to\infty}C_{s,p}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}})\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}.

Thus, we find

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}\\
&\geq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\rho_{i}(\omega)^{n-sp}\\
&-\lim_{R\to\infty}\frac{c_{n,s,p}}{(m_{R}-1)^{sp}}\lim_{L\to\infty}\limsup_{j\to\infty}C_{s,p}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}})\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}.\end{split}

By (
2.9
) in Lemma
2.1
and by the scaling property of the fractional capacity we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}C_{s,p}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}})=\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{R})=R^{n-sp}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1}),

hence recalling the definition of
m_{R}
we get

\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{c_{n,s,p}}{(m_{R}-1)^{sp}}C_{s,p}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}})=0.

Then, Proposition
4.11
yields

\begin{split}\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|w_{j}^{\omega}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})}\geq m_{g}\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)=\gamma\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p},\end{split}

thus

\begin{split}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(u_{j})&=\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\left(1+\frac{c_{n,s,p,R}}{L}\right)\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(u_{j})\\
&\geq\int_{(U\times U)\setminus\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|u(x)-u(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}+\gamma\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}.\end{split}

(5.4)

Finally, the conclusion follows by gathering (
5.2
)-(
5.4
) and by passing to limit as
\delta\to 0
thanks to the Dominated Convergence Theorem.
∎

In the next proposition we prove the so-called limsup-inequality.

Proposition 5.2
.

There exists
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega^{\prime})=1
such that for every
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
,
u\in L^{p}(U)
, and
\varepsilon_{j}\downarrow 0
, there exists
u_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(U)
such that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}^{\omega}(u_{j})\leq\mathcal{F}(u).

(5.5)

Proof.

Let
\Omega^{\prime}\in\mathscr{F}
be the intersection of the events of probability one given by Lemmas
4.2
,
4.4
,
4.6
, and Proposition
4.11
and let
\omega\in\Omega^{\prime}
.
To simplify the notation, in the rest of the proof we are going to omit the explicit dependence on
\omega
of all the relevant quantities.

We observe that by a standard continuity and density argument it suffices to prove (
5.5
) for target functions
u\in W^{1,\infty}(U)\cap L^{p}(U)
(cf.
[
8
, Proposition 1.28 and Remark 1.29]
).

Let
L,R\in\mathbb{N}
be fixed and let
\{w_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
be given by Lemma
4.9
applied with
G=VG_{j,R}
,
u_{j}\equiv u
, and
m=2
.
Let then
\xi_{L}\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
be such that
\xi_{L}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{L}
,
\tilde{\xi}_{L}=1

\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\text{-q.e.}
on
B_{1}
, and

|\xi_{L}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C_{s,p}(B_{1},B_{L})+\frac{1}{L}.

(5.6)

Recalling that
\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}=B_{2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})
(cf. (
4.18
)), we define

v_{j}(x):=\begin{cases}w_{j}(x)\quad&\text{if }x\in U\setminus\bigcup\limits_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}},\\
\left(1-\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right)(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}\quad&\text{if }x\in\bigcup\limits_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}.\end{cases}

For
i\in VG_{j,R}
we have
\rho_{i}\leq R
, hence
L\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}<2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}
, for sufficiently large
j\in\mathbb{N}
. In particular, this implies that
T_{j}^{i}\subset\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
for every
i\in VG_{j,R}
, thus
v_{j}=0
on
\cup_{i\in VG_{j,R}}T_{j}^{i}
.
Moreover, since
w_{j}=(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}
on
\partial\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
, we immediately deduce that
v_{j}\in W^{s,p}(U)
.

Now, let
\phi_{j,R}\in W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
be such that

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{j,R}=0\quad\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\text{-q.e.}\text{ on }\bigcup_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}B_{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}),

\displaystyle\tilde{\phi}_{j,R}=1\quad\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}\text{-q.e.}\text{ on }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\bigcup_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}B_{2\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}),

\displaystyle|\phi_{j,R}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq 2C_{s,p}\left(\bigcup_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}B_{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}),\bigcup_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}B_{2\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})\right).

(5.7)

We define
u_{j}:=v_{j}\phi_{j,R}
, then, by construction,
u_{j}=0
on
T_{j}
and
u_{j}\in W^{s,p}(U)
.

We recall property (
4.4
) and define

U_{j}:=U\setminus(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\cup D_{NVG_{j,R}})

(5.8)

with

\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}:=\bigcup_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}},

(5.9)

and

D_{NVG_{j,R}}:=\bigcup_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}B_{2\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})\cap U\,.

(5.10)

We observe that for every
L\in\mathbb{N}
we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\sup_{R\in\mathbb{N}}\lVert u_{j}-u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}=0.

(5.11)

Indeed we have

\int_{U}|u_{j}-u|^{p}\leq\int_{U_{j}}|w_{j}-u|^{p}+\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|u_{j}-u|^{p}+\sum_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}\int_{B_{2\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}|u_{j}-u|^{p}.

(5.12)

The first term in (

) approaches zero as

since
w_{j}\to u
in
L^{p}(U)
. On the other hand, using that
u_{j}=v_{j}=\left(1-\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right)(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}
on
\bigcup\limits_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
together with the very definition of

the second term in (

\begin{split}&\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|u_{j}-u|^{p}\\
&=\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{T_{j}^{i}}|u|^{p}+\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{B_{L\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})\setminus T_{j}^{i}}|u_{j}-u|^{p}+\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\setminus B_{L\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}|u-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}\\

[

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|u-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}\lesssim_{n,s,p}(2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1})^{sp}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}|u|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}\leq\varepsilon_{j}^{sp}|u|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(U)},\end{split}

which implies, by Lemma

,
that the second term in (

Regarding the third term in (

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}\int_{B_{2\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}|u_{j}-u|^{p}\lesssim_{n,p}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\sum_{i\in NVG_{j,R}}(\lambda_{j}\rho_{i})^{n}\lesssim_{n,p}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\sum_{i\in I_{j}}(\lambda_{j}\rho_{i})^{n}\,,\end{split}

hence the conclusion follows at once by Lemma
4.4
with

.

The rest of the proof is devoted to show that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{F}_{j}(u_{j})\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\vartheta,

(5.13)

where
\vartheta=\vartheta(\delta,R,L)>0
is such that

\displaystyle{\lim_{\delta\to 0}\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\vartheta=0}.

Since the computations leading to (
5.13
) are rather involved, we perform them in a number of consecutive steps.

Step 1
.

In this first step we preliminary reformulate (
5.13
).

By (
5.11
) and the Lebesgue Dominated Convergence Theorem, for every
R,\,L\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\delta>0
, we have

\lim_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(U\times U)\setminus\Delta_{\delta})=\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u,(U\times U)\setminus\Delta_{\delta}).

Therefore, we are only left to estimate the energy of
u_{j}
in a neighborhood of the diagonal
\Delta
. To this end, since
u_{j}=w_{j}
on
U_{j}
, by Lemma
4.9
we deduce that

\begin{split}&\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(U_{j}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})=\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(w_{j},(U_{j}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\\
&\leq\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(w_{j},(U\times U)\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\frac{c_{n,s,p,R}}{L}|u|_{W^{s,p}(U)}^{p}+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u,(U\times U)\cap\Delta_{\delta}),\end{split}

(5.14)

for some
c_{n,s,p,R}>0
.
Hence, by the Dominated Convergence Theorem

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(U_{j}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\vartheta\,.

(5.15)

Therefore, in view of (
5.15
), the inequality in (
5.13
) reduces to proving that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}(\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(U\times(U\setminus U_{j}))\cap\Delta_{\delta})+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},((U\setminus U_{j})\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta}))\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\vartheta.

(5.16)

To establish (
5.16
) we use the definition of
U_{j}
to get

U=\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\cup D_{NVG_{j,R}}\cup U_{j},

where the sets in the above union are pairwise disjoint and defined in (
5.8
), (
5.9
), and (
5.10
), respectively.
In particular, we have

\displaystyle U\times(U\setminus U_{j})=(U\setminus U_{j})^{2}\cup(U_{j}\times(U\setminus U_{j})),

\displaystyle(U\setminus U_{j})^{2}=(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\cup D_{NVG_{j,R}})^{2},

\displaystyle(U\setminus U_{j})\times U_{j}=(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\cup D_{NVG_{j,R}})\times U_{j}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\cdot,A\times B)=\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\cdot,B\times A)
, proving (
5.16
) amounts to showing that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\Big(\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})+2\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\\
+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},D_{NVG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\\
+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(D_{NVG_{j,R}}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\Big)\leq\mathcal{F}(u)+\vartheta.

(5.17)

In the next steps we will prove (
5.17
) by separately treating the different terms in its left-hand side.

Step 2
.

We show that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\gamma\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}+\vartheta\,.

(5.18)

We have

\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}|u_{j}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}).

(5.19)

We now start by estimating the first term on the right hand side of (
5.19
). To this end let
x,y\in\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
; by definition of
u_{j}
, we have

|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|=\lvert(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}\rvert\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)-\xi_{L}\left(\frac{y-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert

and hence, by a change of variables, estimate (
2.9
) in Lemma
2.1
,
Proposition
4.11
, and (
5.6
)

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}|u_{j}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}|\xi_{L}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}

\displaystyle\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\left(C_{s,p}(B_{1},B_{L})+\frac{1}{L}\right)\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathrm{cap}_{s,p}(B_{1})m_{g}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)+\vartheta=\gamma\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}+\vartheta.

(5.20)

Then, it remains to estimate the second term in (
5.19
). For
(x,y)\in(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}
, with
i,k\in VG_{j,R}
and
i\neq k
, we have

|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{p}\lesssim_{p}|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}\\
+\left|\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right|^{p}|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}+\left|\xi_{L}\left(\frac{y-\varepsilon_{j}x_{k}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{k}}\right)\right|^{p}|(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}.

(5.21)

Moreover, since
i,k\in VG_{j,R}\subset G_{j,R}
, by the triangle inequality, for sufficiently large
j
, we get

2\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{R}\leq\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{R}+\delta<2\delta,

and

\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|\leq|x-y|\leq\frac{3}{2}\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|.

(5.22)

Then, recalling that
u\in\lip(U)
, we obtain

|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}\lesssim_{p}\lip(u)^{p}\varepsilon_{j}^{p}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{p}

and

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\lesssim_{n,p}\lip(u)^{p}\varepsilon_{j}^{-n-(s-1)p}R^{-2n}\varepsilon_{j}^{2n}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}.\end{split}

By a counting argument (see also
[
27
, (2.7)]
), it is easy to see that for every
i\in G_{j,R}
we have

\#\left\{k\in G_{j,R}:k\neq i,\frac{h}{R}<|x_{i}-x_{k}|\leq\frac{h+1}{R}\right\}\lesssim_{n}h^{n-1}.

If
n+(s-1)p\geq 0
, we can estimate the above sum using the fact that
\sum_{h=2}^{l}h^{-(1+a)}\leq\frac{l^{-a}}{-a}
for any
l\in\mathbb{N}
and
a<0
. Namely, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}=\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in VG_{j,R}\\
k\neq i,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}\\
&=\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\sum_{h=2}^{\lfloor 2\delta R/\varepsilon_{j}\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in VG_{j,R}\\
h<R|x_{i}-x_{k}|\leq h+1\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}\\
&\lesssim_{n}R^{n+(s-1)p}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\sum_{h=2}^{\lfloor 2\delta R/\varepsilon_{j}\rfloor}h^{-(1+(s-1)p)}\lesssim_{n,s,p}R^{n+(s-1)p}\#VG_{j,R}\delta^{(1-s)p}R^{(1-s)p}\varepsilon_{j}^{(s-1)p}.\end{split}

Since
\{B_{\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{R}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})\}_{i\in VG_{j,R}}
are pairwise disjoint, we have
\#VG_{j,R}\lesssim_{n}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)(\frac{\varepsilon_{j}}{R})^{-n}
, which gives

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}\lesssim_{n,s,p}R^{2n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-n+(s-1)p}\delta^{(1-s)p}

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim_{n,s,p}\lip(u)^{p}\delta^{(1-s)p}\,.

(5.23)

If, on the other hand,
n+(s-1)p<0
, we get both

\begin{split}&\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-(s-1)p}\\
&\leq\#VG_{j,R}^{2}\left(\frac{2\delta}{\varepsilon_{j}}\right)^{-n-(s-1)p}\lesssim_{n,s,p}R^{2n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-2n}\varepsilon_{j}^{n+(s-1)p}\delta^{-n-(s-1)p}\end{split}

and

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-(u)_{C_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\lesssim_{n,s,p}\lip(u)^{p}\delta^{-n-(s-1)p}\,.

(5.24)

We now deal with the second term in (
5.21
) (the third one being analogous). By (
5.22
) a change of variables gives

\begin{split}&\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}\frac{|\xi_{L}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}(x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&=\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|\xi_{L}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}(x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}))|^{p}\left(\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}}}|x-y|^{-(n+sp)}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\right)\,\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&\lesssim_{n}R^{-n}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-n-sp}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-sp}\int_{B_{L\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}|\xi_{L}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}(x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}))|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&=R^{-n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-sp}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-sp}(\lambda_{j}\rho_{i})^{n}\int_{B_{L}}|\xi_{L}|^{p}\,,\end{split}

and, as a consequence,

\begin{split}&\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k\end{subarray}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times\tilde{B}_{j}^{k,h_{j}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|\xi_{L}(\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}(x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lVert\xi_{L}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{L})}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-sp}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i,k\in VG_{j,R}\\
i\neq k,\,\varepsilon_{j}|x_{i}-x_{k}|<2\delta\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-sp}\\
&=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lVert\xi_{L}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{L})}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-sp}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\sum_{h=2}^{\lfloor 2\delta R/\varepsilon_{j}\rfloor}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k\in VG_{j,R}\\
h<R|x_{i}-x_{k}|\leq h+1\end{subarray}}|x_{i}-x_{k}|^{-n-sp}\\
&\lesssim_{n}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lVert\xi_{L}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{L})}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-sp}R^{n+sp}\#VG_{j,R}\sum_{h=2}^{\infty}h^{-n-sp}h^{n-1}\\
&\lesssim_{n}\limsup_{j\to\infty}\lVert\xi_{L}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{L})}^{p}\lambda_{j}^{n}\varepsilon_{j}^{-sp}R^{2n+sp}\varepsilon_{j}^{-n}\sum_{h=2}^{\infty}h^{-1-sp}\lesssim\lVert\xi_{L}\rVert_{L^{p}(B_{L})}^{p}R^{2n+sp}\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{\frac{(sp)^{2}}{n-sp}}=0.\end{split}

(5.25)

Then (
5.18
) follows at once from (
5
), (
5.23
), (
5.24
), and the very last estimate.

Step 3
.

We prove that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\vartheta.

(5.26)

Since
\{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\}_{i\in VG_{j,R}}
are pairwise disjoint, we deduce

\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta})=\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}).

Moreover, for every
(x,y)\in(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}
we have

\begin{split}&u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)=\left(1-\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right)(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-w_{j}(y)\phi_{j,R}(y)\\
&=(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)+w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)+w_{j}(y)(1-\phi_{j,R}(y))\end{split}

and hence

\begin{split}|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{p}&\lesssim_{p}|w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\\
&+|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{p}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}|1-\phi_{j,R}(y)|^{p}.\end{split}

(5.27)

Then the estimate in (
2.8
) and the fact that
w_{j}\equiv(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}
on
C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}
by definition give for sufficiently large
j\in\mathbb{N}
,

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}&\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n+sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\lesssim_{n,s,p}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{B_{2^{-3h_{j}-2}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}\frac{|w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}}{\mathrm{dist}^{sp}(x,\partial\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&\lesssim_{n,s,p}\Big(\frac{2^{3h_{j}+2}R}{\varepsilon_{j}}\Big)^{sp}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{B_{2^{-3h_{j}-2}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}}(\varepsilon_{j}x_{i})}|w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&\lesssim_{n,s,p}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}|w_{j}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}\leq\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(w_{j},(U\times U)\cap\Delta_{\delta}),\end{split}

(5.28)

where in the last but one inequality we have used the scaled
Poincaré-Wirtinger Inequality
[
27
, (2.10) in Remark 2.7]
.
Note that the last term in the above inequality (
5.28
) has been already estimated in (
5.14
).

Concerning the second term in (
5.27
), since
i\in VG_{j,R}
, a change of variables yields

\begin{split}&\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}|x-y|^{-(n+sp)}\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\leq\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times(U\setminus\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}})}|x-y|^{-(n+sp)}\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\leq\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}(\lambda_{j}\rho_{i})^{n-sp}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\xi_{L},B_{2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}})).\end{split}

(5.29)

In the last inequality we have used that,
since
\rho_{i}\leq R
for any
i\in VG_{j,R}
,
2^{-3h_{j}-1}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\rho_{i}^{-1}\geq 2^{-3L-1}R^{-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}\geq L^{2}>L
for sufficiently large
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and that
\xi_{L}=0
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{L}
.
Therefore, by Lemma
4.2
and by
(
2.12
) in Lemma
2.1

we have

\displaystyle\limsup_{j\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}|x-y|^{-(n+sp)}\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\xi_{L},B_{L^{2}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{L^{2}}))\lim_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in I_{j}}\rho_{i}^{n-sp}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\xi_{L},B_{L^{2}}\times(\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{L^{2}}))m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(U)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\rho^{n-sp}\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\leq\vartheta.

(5.30)

The third term in (
5.27
) gives us

\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(w_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(w_{j},(U\times U)\cap\Delta_{\delta}),

(5.31)

which was already estimated in (
5.14
). Regarding the last term in (
5.27
), since
\phi_{j,R}=1
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus D_{NVG_{j,R}}
, by Lemma
4.6
and (
5
), we have

\begin{split}&\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|1-\phi_{j,R}(y)|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&=\limsup_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in VG_{j,R}}\int_{(\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}}\times D_{NVG_{j,R}})\cap\Delta_{\delta}}\frac{|(1-\phi_{j,R}(x))-(1-\phi_{j,R}(y))|^{p}}{|x-y|^{n-sp}}\,\mathrm{d}{x}\,\mathrm{d}{y}\\
&\leq\limsup_{j\to\infty}|\phi_{j,R}|^{p}_{W^{s,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}=0.\end{split}

(5.32)

Then (
5.26
) follows by gathering
(
5.28
),
(
5
),
(
5.31
), and
(
5.32
).

Step 4
.

We have

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},D_{NVG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\vartheta.

Since
u_{j}=u\phi_{j,R}
on

, we obtain,

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},D_{NVG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})&\lesssim_{p}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(\phi_{j,R},D_{NVG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})+\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u,D_{NVG_{j,R}}^{2}\cap\Delta_{\delta})\\

Then (

) follows by Lemma
4.6
(cf. also (

Step 5
.

We show that

\limsup_{j\to\infty}\mathcal{D}_{s,p}(u_{j},(\tilde{B}_{VG_{j,R}}\times U_{j})\cap\Delta_{\delta})\leq\vartheta\,.

For

, we have

\begin{split}&u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)=\left(1-\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right)(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-w_{j}(y)\\
&=(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}-w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)+w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y),\end{split}

and hence

|u_{j}(x)-u_{j}(y)|^{p}\lesssim_{p}|w_{j}(x)-(u)_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}}}|^{p}+\lVert u\rVert_{L^{\infty}(U)}^{p}\left\lvert\xi_{L}\left(\frac{x-\varepsilon_{j}x_{i}}{\lambda_{j}\rho_{i}}\right)\right\rvert^{p}+|w_{j}(x)-w_{j}(y)|^{p}

We then observe that the terms above give contributions which were already estimated in (

), (
5.29
), and (

), respectively.

.

) as previously noticed.

Proof of Theorem
3.6
.

We start by observing that, thanks to

, in the proof we can resort to the same sets of indices introduced in Section

and

\begin{split}&\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}\mathcal{K}(w_{j}^{\omega},B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})\geq\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(S_{j}^{i,\omega},\tilde{B}_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}},m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}R^{-1})\\

In view of assumption

,

for every

.
Therefore, arguing as in the proof of Proposition

\begin{split}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}\mathcal{K}(w_{j}^{\omega},B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})&\geq\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\mathrm{cap}_{\mathscr{K}}(S_{j}^{i,\omega})\\
&-\frac{c_{n,s,p}}{(m_{R}-1)^{sp}}C_{\mathscr{K}}(B_{R},B_{m_{R}^{-3h_{j}^{\omega}-2}\varepsilon_{j}\lambda_{j}^{-1}R^{-1}})\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}.\end{split}

Then, invoking formula (
3.10
) and
Remark
4.12
, we may conclude that

\begin{split}&\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}\mathcal{K}(w_{j}^{\omega},B_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}})\geq\lim_{R\to\infty}\lim_{L\to\infty}\liminf_{j\to\infty}\varepsilon_{j}^{n}\sum_{i\in G_{j,R}^{\omega}}|(u_{j})_{C_{j}^{i,h_{j}^{\omega}}}|^{p}\gamma_{i}(\omega)\\
&=\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\sum_{x_{i}\in Q}\gamma_{i}\bigg]=\tilde{\gamma}\lVert u\rVert_{L^{p}(U)}^{p}.\end{split}

Appendix A

For the readers’ convenience, in this appendix we prove Proposition
2.3
and Proposition
2.4
.

Proof of Proposition
2.3
.

The proof follows the ideas of
[
23
, B.1]
. We recall that a sequence
\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
of bounded Borel sets in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called a
convex averaging sequence

[
20
, Definition 12.2.I]
if
A_{j}\subset A_{j+1}
,
A_{j}
is convex for any
j\in\mathbb{N}
, and
r(A_{j})\to\infty
as
j\to\infty
, where
r(A)
is the supremum of all radii
r\geq 0
such that
A
contains a ball of radius
r
. By the Ergodic Theorem for stationary and ergodic m.p.p.
[
20
, Theorem 12.2.IV and Corollary 12.2.V]
, for any convex averaging sequence
\{A_{j}\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and any
h\in L^{1}(\pi)

\lim_{j\to\infty}\frac{1}{\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{j})}\int_{A_{j}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N}(x,\rho)=m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\quad\mathbb{P}\text{-a.e.}

We now show that for any
h\in L^{1}(\pi)
there exists
\Omega_{h}\in\mathscr{F}
with
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{h})=1
such that for any
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
, if
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i},b_{i}]
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
and
a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
for
i=1,\ldots,n
, then

\lim_{t\to+\infty}\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{(tA)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho).

(A.1)

To this end we define

F^{\omega}(t,A):=\frac{1}{t^{n}}\int_{(tA)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho),\quad G(A):=m_{g}\mathcal{L}^{n}(A)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho).

Without loss of generality, we assume that
h\geq 0
. If
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(0,b_{i}]
with
b_{i}\geq 0
, then
\{jA\}_{j\in\mathbb{N}}
is a convex averaging sequence and
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\lim_{j\to\infty}F(j,A)=G(A)
.
Moreover, for any
t\geq 0
, we have
\lfloor t\rfloor\leq t<\lfloor t\rfloor+1
and

\frac{\lfloor t\rfloor^{n}}{(\lfloor t\rfloor+1)^{n}}F(\lfloor t\rfloor,A)\leq F(t,A)\leq\frac{(\lfloor t\rfloor+1)^{n}}{\lfloor t\rfloor^{n}}F(\lfloor t\rfloor+1,A),

which implies that
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\lim_{t\to+\infty}F(t,A)=G(A)
. If (
A.1
) holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.e. for
A=A_{1}
and
A=A_{2}
then it clearly holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.e. for
A=A_{1}\setminus A_{2}
. As a consequence, exploiting the previous case, by the argument contained in
[
23
, Proof of Lemma B.1]
, it is easy to see that (
A.1
) holds
\mathbb{P}
-a.e. for
A=\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i},b_{i}]
with
a_{i}<b_{i}
. So that we can conclude by observing that
\{\prod_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i},b_{i}]:a_{i}<b_{i},\,a_{i},b_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},\,i=1,\ldots,n\}
is countable.

Now let
\Omega_{h}
be the event of probability one such that (
A.1
) holds, and let
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
. Moreover, let
f\in C_{c}^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
\delta>0
. Since
f
is uniformly continuous, there exists
\sigma>0
such that
|f(x)-f(y)|<\delta
whenever
|x-y|<\sigma
. Let
K:=\min\{k\in\mathbb{N}:\supp f\subset(-k,k]^{n}\}
. Then there are
A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}
pairwise disjoint sets of the kind described above such that
(-K,K]^{n}=\cup_{i=1}^{l}A_{i}
and
\rm{diam}(A_{i})<\sigma
for any
i=1,\ldots,l
. Then, by construction,
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\inf_{A_{i}}f\,\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}\leq f\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sup_{A_{i}}f\,\mathds{1}_{A_{i}}
and hence

\begin{split}&\sum_{i=1}^{l}\inf_{A_{i}}f\,\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}A_{i})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\\
&\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sup_{A_{i}}f\,\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}A_{i})\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho).\end{split}

Then, by the choice of
\Omega_{h}
, we get

\begin{split}&m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}-\delta(2K)^{n}\right)\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\inf_{A_{i}}f\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{i})\\
&\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}f(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\\
&\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\sum_{i=1}^{l}\sup_{A_{i}}f\mathcal{L}^{n}(A_{i})\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}f(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}+\delta(2K)^{n}\right).\end{split}

Finally, we conclude by the arbitrariness of
\delta
.
∎

Proof of Proposition
2.4
.

Let
\Omega_{h}
be the event of probability one given by Proposition
2.3
and let
\omega\in\Omega_{h}
. For
\delta>0
, let
U_{\delta}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial U)<\delta\}
and
U_{-\delta}:=\{x\in U:\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial U)>\delta\}
. By the Urysohn Lemma
[
29
, Proposition 8.18]
, there exist
\varphi_{\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
and
\varphi_{-\delta}\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{n};[0,1])
such that
\varphi_{\delta}=1
on
\overline{U}
,
\supp\varphi_{\delta}\subset U_{\delta}
,
\varphi_{-\delta}=1
on
\overline{U_{-\delta}}
,
\supp\varphi_{-\delta}\subset U
. By construction,
\varphi_{-\delta}\leq\mathds{1}_{U}\leq\varphi_{\delta}
, thus we have

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\varphi_{-\delta}(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\\
&\leq\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}U)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\varphi_{\delta}(x)h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N_{\varepsilon}^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\end{split}

while, by the choice of
\Omega_{h}
, there holds

\begin{split}&m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{-\delta})\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{-\delta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}\\
&\leq\liminf_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}U)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\leq\limsup_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}\varepsilon^{n}\int_{(\varepsilon^{-1}U)\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{N^{\omega}}(x,\rho)\\
&\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi_{\delta}(x)\,\mathrm{d}{x}\leq m_{g}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}h(\rho)\,\mathrm{d}{\pi}(\rho)\mathcal{L}^{n}(U_{\delta}).\end{split}

Since
\partial U
is Lipschitz, we can conclude by taking the limit as
\delta\to 0
.
∎
