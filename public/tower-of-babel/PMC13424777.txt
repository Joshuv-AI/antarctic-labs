Persistent Urethral Burning Without Identifiable Infection: A Case Report Highlighting Diagnostic Challenges and Integrative Symptom-Oriented Management
Authors: Xudong Hu, Dawei Gao, Shanshan Tong, Xiaojing Zheng, Dalin Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Persistent urethral burning without identifiable infection represents a diagnostically challenging condition in male urology and may lead to repeated empirical treatment with limited benefit. We report the case of a 35-year-old man who experienced isolated urethral burning for approximately six years after an initial urinary tract infection. Despite repeated evaluations and multiple conventional treatments, his symptoms persisted. Laboratory investigations, including urinalysis, urine culture, gonococcal smear examination, and prostatic fluid analysis, showed no evidence of active infection or clinically significant prostatic inflammation. Transabdominal pelvic ultrasonography revealed only mild nonspecific prostatic changes without major structural abnormalities. Given the chronic refractory symptoms and negative diagnostic findings, an integrative symptom-oriented management strategy incorporating a modified Gancao Xiexin decoction was initiated. The patient reported gradual improvement, with the visual analogue scale score decreasing from 8 at baseline to 4 after one week and to 0 after two months. Anxiety, sleep quality, bowel habits, and overall well-being also improved, and no adverse effects or symptom recurrence were reported during follow-up. This case highlights the diagnostic uncertainty associated with persistent urethral burning without identifiable infection and suggests that noninfectious pain-related mechanisms may deserve consideration in selected patients. The observed improvement should be interpreted as a temporal association rather than evidence of therapeutic efficacy, and further studies are needed to clarify the underlying mechanisms and evaluate integrative management approaches for chronic unexplained urethral pain conditions.

Introduction

Urethral burning is a common urological symptom that is most frequently associated with urinary tract infection, sexually transmitted infection, or inflammatory conditions such as prostatitis (Hoffman et al., 2025). In routine clinical practice, empirical antimicrobial therapy is often initiated based on the assumption of an underlying infectious process (Sell et al., 2021). In many patients, symptoms improve after appropriate treatment. However, a subset of individuals continue to experience persistent urethral discomfort despite repeated negative microbiological investigations and the absence of identifiable structural abnormalities (Kaur & Arunkalaivanan, 2007).

Chronic unexplained urethral burning represents a diagnostically challenging and insufficiently characterized clinical condition (Kaur & Arunkalaivanan, 2007). These patients frequently undergo repeated medical evaluations and prolonged empirical treatment with limited sustained benefit, often resulting in considerable psychological distress and reduced quality of life (Riegel et al., 2014). In some cases, the absence of objective laboratory or imaging abnormalities may further contribute to diagnostic uncertainty and delayed recognition of potential noninfectious mechanisms.

Increasing evidence suggests that persistent urogenital discomfort may involve complex neurophysiological processes beyond infection alone. Proposed mechanisms include neurogenic inflammation, autonomic nervous system dysfunction, mucosal hypersensitivity, altered sensory modulation, and central sensitization (Curatolo, 2024). Similar mechanisms have been implicated in chronic pelvic pain syndrome and other functional pain-related disorders, in which symptoms may persist despite the absence of clear structural pathology (He et al., 2024).

Because standardized management strategies remain limited for patients with chronic unexplained urethral burning, alternative symptom-oriented and multidisciplinary approaches may be considered when conventional therapies fail. Here, we report the case of a 35-year-old man with persistent urethral burning lasting approximately six years despite repeated negative investigations and unsuccessful conventional treatments. The case highlights the diagnostic challenges associated with chronic unexplained urethral symptoms and describes the clinical course following an integrative management approach incorporating traditional Chinese herbal medicine.

Case Report

A 35-year-old man with a history of hepatitis B presented with persistent urethral burning that had persisted for approximately six years. The symptoms initially began in June 2018 with dysuria accompanied by bilateral inguinal discomfort, for which he sought care at the urology department of a local hospital. At that time, routine urinalysis suggested urinary tract infection, and oral antibiotic therapy was prescribed. Following treatment, the dysuria improved; however, the patient subsequently developed a persistent burning sensation localized to the base of the urethra. Over the following years, the patient underwent repeated evaluations and received multiple empirical treatments at several medical institutions, including repeated courses of oral antibiotics, Chinese patent medicines, topical therapies, and herbal prescriptions, without sustained symptom relief. The burning sensation was persistent and recurrent, substantially affecting the patient’s quality of life and psychological well-being. Notably, the patient denied urinary frequency, urgency, gross hematuria, urethral discharge, fever, or significant perineal pain throughout most of the disease course.

Between February and May 2024, several laboratory investigations were performed at our hospital to evaluate the persistent urethral symptoms. Repeated urinalysis showed no significant pyuria or hematuria. Urine culture demonstrated no bacterial growth. Gonococcal smear examination was negative, and no microbiological evidence of sexually transmitted infection was identified. Prostatic fluid analysis revealed no significant inflammatory findings. These findings did not support active urinary tract infection, sexually transmitted infection, or clinically significant prostatic inflammation. On December 31, 2024, transabdominal pelvic ultrasonography was performed during the patient’s re-evaluation at our clinic. The examination demonstrated mild enlargement of the prostate with slight protrusion into the bladder lumen, but no major structural abnormalities or obvious obstructive lesions were observed (Supplementary Material). Taken together, the laboratory and imaging findings failed to identify a clear infectious or structural cause explaining the persistent urethral burning.

At presentation to our clinic on December 31, 2024, the patient’s primary complaint remained persistent urethral burning localized to the base of the urethra. He described the symptom as continuous and distressing, particularly during periods of psychological stress and fatigue. In addition to the urethral burning sensation, the patient reported anxiety related to the prolonged and unresolved disease course. Associated symptoms included sweating of the palms and soles, dry mouth, a sweet taste in the mouth, loose stools, mild lower abdominal discomfort, fatigue, and intermittent lower back soreness.

Physical examination revealed no obvious abnormalities of the external genitalia. No urethral discharge, skin lesions, or focal neurological deficits were identified. From a traditional Chinese medicine perspective, the patient demonstrated a red tongue with a white greasy coating and a thin, deep pulse, which was considered consistent with a pattern of spleen deficiency with damp-heat.

Given the prolonged disease course, repeated negative microbiological investigations, and limited benefit from conventional empirical therapies, an integrative symptom-oriented management strategy was initiated. An individualized herbal prescription based on a modified Gancao Xiexin decoction was prescribed. The decoction was prepared by simmering the herbal mixture for approximately 30 minutes to obtain 200 mL of medicinal liquid, which the patient was instructed to take twice daily at approximately 8:00 AM and 8:00 PM. The patient demonstrated good adherence to the treatment regimen and reported no adverse effects during therapy. After one week of treatment, the urethral burning sensation was noticeably alleviated, with the visual analogue scale (VAS) score decreasing from 8 to 4. At one-month follow-up, the symptoms had markedly improved, and the patient reported better sleep quality and reduced anxiety. After two months of treatment, the urethral burning sensation had almost completely resolved, with a VAS score of 0. Improvements were also observed in emotional well-being, lower back discomfort, and bowel habits. During follow-up three months after symptom resolution, the patient reported no recurrence of urethral burning and remained in stable physical and psychological condition.

From the patient’s perspective, the persistent unresolved symptoms over several years caused considerable frustration, anxiety, and uncertainty because repeated medical evaluations failed to identify a clear cause. Following integrative treatment, the patient reported substantial improvement in daily comfort, emotional well-being, and overall quality of life.

Discussion

Persistent urethral burning without identifiable infection represents a diagnostically challenging and insufficiently characterized clinical presentation in male urology. In routine clinical practice, urethral burning is commonly attributed to urinary tract infection, sexually transmitted infection, or chronic prostatitis (Hoffman et al., 2025). However, as demonstrated in the present case, a subset of patients may continue to experience chronic symptoms despite repeated negative microbiological investigations and the absence of major structural abnormalities (Kaur & Arunkalaivanan, 2007). Such patients frequently undergo prolonged empirical treatment, particularly repeated courses of antibiotics, with limited or unsustained benefit.

Several aspects of the present case are clinically noteworthy. First, the patient experienced isolated urethral burning for approximately six years despite repeated evaluations and multiple treatment attempts. Repeated urinalysis, urine culture, gonococcal smear examination, and prostatic fluid analysis failed to identify evidence of active infection. In addition, pelvic ultrasonography revealed only mild nonspecific prostatic changes without significant obstructive or structural pathology. The persistent symptoms despite largely negative investigations highlight the diagnostic uncertainty frequently encountered in patients with chronic unexplained urogenital discomfort (Riegel et al., 2014).

Second, the clinical presentation did not fully conform to typical infectious urethritis or classic chronic bacterial prostatitis. The patient consistently denied urethral discharge, fever, significant lower urinary tract symptoms, or severe perineal pain during most of the disease course. The absence of sustained response to antimicrobial therapy further reduced the likelihood of an ongoing infectious process (Sell et al., 2021). These findings suggest that persistent urethral burning may, in certain patients, represent a functional or noninfectious pain-related condition rather than an active microbial disease.

Increasing evidence suggests that chronic unexplained urogenital symptoms may involve complex neurophysiological mechanisms beyond infection alone. Previous studies have proposed that neurogenic inflammation, altered sensory processing, autonomic nervous system dysfunction, mucosal hypersensitivity, and central sensitization may contribute to persistent pain or burning sensations in the absence of clear structural pathology (Breser et al., 2017; He et al., 2024). Similar mechanisms have also been discussed in chronic pelvic pain syndrome and other chronic functional pain disorders (Curatolo, 2024; He et al., 2024). In the present case, the prolonged symptom duration, repeated negative investigations, coexistence of anxiety-related symptoms, and fluctuating symptom severity may support the possibility that altered pain modulation or functional dysregulation contributed to symptom persistence. This interpretation is also consistent with recent pain literature emphasizing that altered pain perception can occur even when clear nociceptive or structural drivers are difficult to identify (Martinez-Lozano et al., 2025).

In this context, the patient sought an integrative management approach after multiple conventional treatment strategies failed to provide sustained symptom relief. Rather than targeting a specific pathogen, the intervention focused on symptom-oriented management and overall functional regulation. Following treatment with a modified Gancao Xiexin decoction, the patient experienced gradual improvement in urethral burning, anxiety, sleep quality, bowel habits, and overall well-being. Importantly, the temporal association between treatment initiation and symptom improvement should be interpreted cautiously. Because this report describes a single uncontrolled clinical observation, spontaneous fluctuation of symptoms, regression to the mean, placebo effects, and psychological influences cannot be excluded.

Nevertheless, the observed improvement raises the possibility that integrative therapeutic strategies may provide symptomatic benefit in selected patients with chronic unexplained urogenital discomfort. In related chronic prostatitis/chronic pelvic pain syndrome populations, previous systematic reviews have suggested that Chinese herbal medicine or traditional Chinese medicine may improve symptom scores and quality of life, although the available evidence remains limited by methodological heterogeneity and study quality (Wang et al., 2016; Xue et al., 2019). From a translational perspective, the traditional Chinese medicine pattern identified in this case may partially overlap with contemporary concepts involving autonomic dysregulation, chronic stress responses, gut-brain interaction, and altered pain processing (He et al., 2024; Martinez-Lozano et al., 2025). Although these interpretations remain hypothetical, they may provide a conceptual bridge between traditional symptom-based approaches and modern neurophysiological models of chronic pain.

This case also highlights an important clinical concern regarding prolonged empirical therapy in patients with persistent unexplained symptoms. Repeated antibiotic exposure in the absence of microbiological evidence may contribute to unnecessary medication use, psychological frustration, and delayed recognition of potential noninfectious mechanisms (Rees et al., 2015). Earlier consideration of functional, neuropathic, or centrally mediated pain-related conditions may help reduce diagnostic uncertainty and improve patient-centered management strategies in selected cases (Kaur & Arunkalaivanan, 2007).

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this report describes a single patient, and the findings cannot be generalized to broader populations. Second, although repeated microbiological investigations and imaging studies failed to identify a clear infectious or structural cause, not all potential differential diagnoses were exhaustively excluded. Advanced investigations such as cystoscopy, urodynamic testing, pelvic magnetic resonance imaging, or formal neuropathic pain assessment were not performed. Therefore, the possibility of unrecognized functional, neuropathic, or pain-spectrum disorders cannot be completely excluded. Third, the clinical outcomes were primarily based on subjective symptom reporting, including visual analogue scale scores and self-reported psychological improvement. Validated urological symptom instruments, such as the National Institutes of Health Chronic Prostatitis Symptom Index (NIH-CPSI), were not prospectively collected (Litwin et al., 1999). In addition, no objective post-treatment reassessment was available during follow-up. Finally, because of the uncontrolled nature of the observation, a direct causal relationship between the herbal intervention and symptom resolution cannot be established.

Conclusion

This case illustrates a clinically relevant presentation of persistent urethral burning without identifiable infection or major structural abnormalities. It highlights the diagnostic uncertainty and therapeutic challenges encountered in patients with chronic unexplained urogenital symptoms. The clinical course suggests that noninfectious pain-related mechanisms and multidisciplinary, symptom-oriented management strategies may deserve consideration when conventional approaches fail. Further prospective studies are needed to clarify the underlying mechanisms and evaluate the potential role of integrative management in chronic unexplained urethral pain conditions.
