Executive Summary
The objective of this project was to perform a review of the vendors who offer aluminum 4047 filler metal.
From the evaluation criteria specified by LANL, only the Lincoln Electric Company can supply the filler metal
product controlled by the vendor from the smelting process to packaging of the filler wire spool. Two
additional companies (Hobart and AlcoTec) do specify the chemistry for the “green” rod ingot from the
smelting process procured from a 3rd party supplier. Once the green rod has been received, these companies
then control the reduction of the green rod to the final filler wire diameter, wire surface cleaning, and
packaging. Alloy 4047 has been shown to be more prone to porosity as compared to other 4000 series
aluminum filler metals. The increased propensity is believed to be a result of the higher silicon content in 4047.
Porosity in the weld is largely a result of the residual hydrogen level for the filler wire where in particular, the
surface hydrogen content is a more significant factor as compared to the bulk hydrogen level. The surface
hydrogen level is a result of hydrated oxide formation. As a result, all manufacturers recommend the used of
heated, low humidity-controlled storage for the filler wire.

Introduction
This report provides the results of a manufacturer/vendor survey conducted for aluminum 4047 filler metal
spools to be used with the Gas Metal Arc Welding Process. The subject review represents an update to an
earlier review conducted by Gray Beards Engineering in October 2024.

The purpose of the review is to provide LANL with the best option(s) regarding the metal manufacturers or
vendors that are best aligned to meet the specified requirements (below) for procurement.

LANL Evaluation Criteria:
  1. The selected filler wire manufacturer should have full control of the filler wire manufacturing process
 (i.e. ingot metal, green rod, filler wire manufacturing and packaging).
  2. LANL would desire to not issue any special requirements regarding the filler wire that would force the
 procurement of a large quantity or single heat. Rather, filler metal would be purchased as an off-the-
 shelf product in small quantities over time to maintain a fresh stock and eliminate issues as a result of
 long-term storage of aluminum.
  3. The filler wire would be purchased in 0.047” dia. on small (ideally 5 lb) spools, but no larger than 16
 lbs.

Manufacturer/Vendor Survey
The following manufacturers/vendors advertise for sale aluminum 4047 filler metal; 1) Lincoln Electric
Company, 2) Hobart Company, 3) AlcoTec (ESAB), 4 ) US Welding Corporation, 5) Weldcote, 6) Washington
Alloys, and 7 ) Aufhauser. Each company was contacted to get answers to the evaluation criteria as well as
copies of the specifications for their 4047 filler metal offerings. Table 1 below summarizes the results from the
investigation where the responses are; Y = “Yes”, N = “No”, and U = “Undetermined” (need more
information). Please note the summary Table does includes the Astrolite Company which had previously
offered aluminum 4047 filler metal during the 2024 survey. Astrolite was acquired by the Tricor Company and
has discontinued sale of 4047 filler metal.

 2

From the review, Lincoln Electric remains the only Company with full control of the 4047 filler metal
manufacturing process from the smelting operation to packaging and distribution from their North American
facility in Ontario, Canada.

Both Hobart and AlcoTec purchase the starting “green” rod from a 3rd party supplier where both companies
specify a tighter ingot chemistry allowance than that permitted by the American Welding Society (AWS)
specification A5.10. Both companies provide value-added operations in the reduction of the green rod to wire,
cleaning and packaging of the final products. In comparison, US Welding Corp., Weldcote, Washington Alloys,
and Aufhauser essentially just re-market filler wire produced by other vendors.

Lincoln Electric Company Conversation Notes
Raheel Khan
Cell: (+1) 780 919 0374
Email: Raheel_Khan@lincolnelectric.com

 1. Lincoln controls the entire manufacturing process for their 4047 filler metal process. Lincoln performs
 smelting green rod production, and wire reduction to final size at their Ontario Canada facility.
 2. Wire shaving is a key part of the process for controlling the hydrogen levels and cleanliness of the final
 filler wire. Lincoln currently uses 4 shaving operations for 4047 and this is the most shaving operations
 they have deployed for aluminum filler metals.
 3. 4000 series aluminum filler metals are more prone to porosity than other aluminum series alloys.
 Believed to be largely related to the silicon content.
 4. 4047 is more prone to porosity issues than 4043. Again, believed to be from the higher silicon content.
 5. Lincoln analyzes bulk hydrogen content using a Lecco analysis.
 6. Manufactured filler wire is stored in a climate-controlled environment until shipping.
 7. Lincoln does recommend LANL to use climate-controlled storage for the filler wire. Lincoln will try to
 provide a storage procedure used by Air Products in Florida as a guideline. The objective is to maintain
 a low humidity level and to prevent significant temperature/humidity changes which can cross the dew
 point and form surface moisture on the filler wire. This event will hydrate the surface oxide on the filler
 metal.
 8. Lincoln currently provides packaging in 300, 100, 30, and 16 lb drums or spools. Lincoln would
 consider other spool sizes if needed by LANL.
 9. Lincoln can provide hermetic sealed packaging. There process uses a vacuum environment, then seal.
 They currently do not use an argon over-pack and seal method, but more discussion on this is possible.
 10. From their experience, the larger the filler wire diameter the lower the observed hydrogen and porosity.
 This effect is attributed to a reduction in the ratio of the surface to cross sectional area of the filer metal.
 11. Lincoln has evaluated laser surface cleaning of the filler wire as a possible process improvement, but
 have found no significant benefit to warrant its implementation in the production process.

Conclusions

This report provides the results of a vendor survey conducted for aluminum 4047 filler metal to determine
which vendors best meet the evaluation criteria established by LANL. From this review, only the Lincoln
Electric Company can supply the filler metal product meeting all of the criteria. If direct control of the smelting
process is not required, then two additional companies (Hobart and AlcoTec) can provide a 4047 filler metal
product meeting the remaining criteria.

From the review, alloy 4047 is reported to be more prone to porosity as compared to other 4000 series
aluminum filler metals where the porosity is largely a result of the surface hydrogen content. As a result, all
vendors recommend the used of heated, low humidity-controlled storage for the filler wire.

Recommendations
 1. Procure sample wire spools from multiple heats of 4047 filler wire from those vendors selected from this
 evaluation.
 a. Perform an evaluation of the wire spools to evaluate the filler wire surface quality, cleanliness
 and hydrogen content (surface and bulk).
 b. Perform weld tests to compare the consistency in performance and volumetric weld quality from
 spool to spool using an established welding procedure.

 2. For procurement of 4047 filler wire, establish a procedure that minimizes the time the purchased product
 has resided on a storage shelf and has been outside a controlled storage environment.
