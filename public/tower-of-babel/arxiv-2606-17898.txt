Counterexamples to the L 1 L^{1} and L ∞ L^{\infty} boundedness of the one-dimensional wave operators
Sisi Huang and Xiaohua Yao

Abstract
. It is well established that the wave operators W ± ​ ( H , − Δ ) W_{\pm}(H,-\Delta) for the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator H = − Δ + V ⁡ ( x ) H=-\Delta+V(x) are bounded on L p ​ ( ℝ ) L^{p}(\mathbb{R}) for all 1 < p < ∞ 1<p<\infty in both generic and exceptional cases. They are also bounded on L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) and L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) in the exceptional case with lim x → − ∞ f + ​ ( 0 , x ) = 1 \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)=1 . For the remaining endpoint cases, it has long been expected that they are generally unbounded at the endpoints p = 1 , ∞ p=1,\infty due to the presence of the Hilbert transform in the low energy part, yet a rigorous proof has been missing. In this paper, we show that even for a bounded and compactly supported non-zero potential V V , the wave operators W ± ​ ( H , − Δ ) W_{\pm}(H,-\Delta) are unbounded on L 1 ​ ( ℝ ) L^{1}(\mathbb{R}) and L ∞ ​ ( ℝ ) L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}) in the generic case, as well as in the exceptional case with the condition lim x → − ∞ f + ​ ( 0 , x ) ≠ 1 \lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1 . Hence together with those known results, our counterexamples complete the picture of the L p L^{p} boundedness of one-dimensional wave operators.



1.
Introduction and main result

1.1.
Introduction

In this paper, we consider the one-dimensional Schrödinger operator

H=-\Delta+V(x),\quad\Delta=\frac{d^{2}}{dx^{2}},

where
V(x)
is a real-valued potential satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2
, with
\left<x\right>=\sqrt{1+|x|^{2}}
. Under this assumption, by the Kato-Rellich theorem, both
-\Delta
and
H
are self-adjoint operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with domain
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
(the Sobolev space of order
2
). They generate the unitary groups
e^{it\Delta}
and
e^{itH}
, respectively. Moreover, it is well known that
H
has no positive or zero eigenvalues, all eigenvalues are strictly negative, the absolutely continuous spectrum is
[0,+\infty)
, and the singular continuous spectrum is absent (cf.
[
27
,
20
]
).

The
wave operators
associated with
H
are defined as the following strong limits on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
:

W_{\pm}:=W_{\pm}(H,-\Delta):=s\mbox{-}\lim_{t\to\pm{\infty}}e^{itH}e^{it\Delta}.

(1.1)

It is known (cf.
[
1
,
21
,
17
]
) that
W_{\pm}
exist as partial isometries from
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
onto
\mathcal{H}_{ac}(H)
(the absolutely continuous spectral subspace of
H
), hence are

L^{2}
-bounded. Subsequent works such as Weder
[
28
]
, Galtbayar-Yajima
[
15
]
and D’Ancona-Fanelli
[
4
]
further established that
W_{\pm}
are
L^{p}
bounded for all
1<p<\infty
in both generic and exceptional cases (see Definition
1.1
below) under various conditions on the potential. Moreover, Weder proved that
W_{\pm}
are also bounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
if
V
is of exceptional type and
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)=1
. On the other hand, he pointed out that, in general, they
are not bounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
or
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
because the low energy part of
W_{\pm}

contains terms with the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
.

More precisely, he showed that the low energy part admits the decomposition

W_{\pm}\Psi(-\Delta)=W_{\pm,r}\pm\frac{i}{2}\chi_{1}(x)f_{+}(0,x)\mathcal{H}\Psi(-\Delta)(a_{1}+a_{2}\tau)\mp\frac{i}{2}(1-\chi_{1}(x))f_{-}(0,x)\mathcal{H}\Psi(-\Delta)(a_{1}+a_{3}\tau),

(1.2)

where
\Psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\Psi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq\mu_{0}
(
\mu_{0}>0
small),
W_{\pm,r}
are
L^{p}
bounded for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
,
\chi_{1}(x)\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
with
\chi_{1}(x)=0
for
x\leq 0
and
\chi_{1}(x)=1
for
x\geq 1
,
f_{\pm}(0,x)
are Jost functions,
(\tau f)(x):=f(-x)
, and

a_{1}=T(0)-1,\quad a_{2}=R_{1}(0),\quad a_{3}=R_{2}(0)

with
T(0)
the transmission coefficient and
R_{j}(0)
the reflection coefficients at zero energy. This decomposition offers important insight into the possible endpoint singularities, as it isolates them into terms involving the Hilbert transform, whose coefficients are explicitly expressed through the zero-energy transmission and reflection coefficients. In the exceptional case with
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)=1
, all
a_{j}
vanish and the decomposition contains no Hilbert transform, which immediately yields the
L^{1}
and
L^{\infty}
boundedness proved by Weder
[
28
]
.

However, the Hilbert transform terms persist for the remaining two cases (
the generic case, and the exceptional case with

\bm{\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1}
).
Although the Hilbert transform
\mathcal{H}
itself is unbounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, to the best of our knowledge, a rigorous proof that the composed operators in (
1.2
)
are unbounded on these two endpoint spaces is not clear.
First, the spatial multipliers
\chi_{1}(x)f_{\pm}(0,x)
generally do not commute with

\mathcal{H}
and one cannot directly infer the unboundedness
of the composite operators from that of
\mathcal{H}
alone. Second, the two Hilbert terms are summed, and a possible cancellation between
their singular parts cannot be excluded apriori.

Motivated by this, we provide a proof for these two remaining cases by a method that avoids the delicate Jost function asymptotics used in the above decomposition. We show that, even for a bounded and compactly supported non-zero potential
V
,
W_{\pm}
are unbounded on
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
in the generic case,
as well as in the exceptional case with

\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1
. Moreover, in the latter case,
they are even unbounded from
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
(Bounded Mean Oscillation space) (see Theorem
1.2
).

To prove this,
we start from the stationary representation (cf.
[
21
,
25
,
26
]
) of
W_{-}
(the case
W_{+}
is similar since
W_{+}f=\overline{W_{-}\overline{f}}
),

W_{-}=I-\frac{1}{\pi i}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda R^{+}_{V}({\lambda}^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda,

where
R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)
and
R^{+}_{V}(\lambda)
are boundary values of free and perturbed resolvents on
(0,+\infty)
, respectively. Fix a sufficiently small constant
0<\lambda_{0}\ll 1
and choose a smooth cutoff function
\chi(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
such that
\chi(\lambda)\equiv 1
on
(-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0})
and
\chi(\lambda)\equiv 0
on
(-\infty,-2\lambda_{0})\cup(2\lambda_{0},+\infty)
. We further decompose

W_{-}=I-\frac{1}{\pi i}(W^{L}_{-}+W^{H}_{-}),

where the low energy part
W^{L}_{-}
and the high energy part
W^{H}_{-}
are given by

\displaystyle W^{L}_{-}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda\chi(\lambda)R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda,

\displaystyle W^{H}_{-}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda(1-\chi(\lambda))R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda.

(1.3)

It is known that the high energy part of wave operators is
L^{p}
bounded for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
(cf.
[
28
]
, another proof the
L^{p}
boundedness of
W^{H}_{-}
also see Appendix
B
). Therefore,
in this paper we only focus on the low energy part

\bm{W^{L}_{-}}
. To this end, we first study the asymptotic behavior of
R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})
near
\lambda=0
(see Subsection
2.1
). This enables us to isolate the singular terms at the endpoints
p=1,\infty
, which exhibit a clearer structure and admit a fairly explicit integral kernel representation (see Subsection
2.2
). By analyzing these kernels and testing the behaviors of these singular operators on some specific functions, we prove their unboundedness and establish the desired result (see Section
3
).

1.2.
Main result

To illustrate our results, we introduce some definitions and notation. First recall that the
Jost functions

f_{\pm}(\lambda,x)
are the solutions to the equation

-f^{\prime\prime}(\lambda,x)+V(x)f(\lambda,x)=\lambda^{2}f(\lambda,x),\quad\lambda,x\in\mathbb{R}

satisfying the following asymptotic conditions

|f_{\pm}(\lambda,x)-e^{\pm i\lambda x}|\rightarrow 0\ \ {\rm as}\ x\rightarrow\pm\infty.

It is known (cf.
[
6
]
) that if
\left<x\right>V(x)\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, the Jost functions are uniquely defined for all
\lambda,x\in\mathbb{R}
. We denote by
W(\lambda)
their Wronskian

W(\lambda):=f_{+}(\lambda,x)f^{\prime}_{-}(\lambda,x)-f^{\prime}_{+}(\lambda,x)f_{-}(\lambda,x).

Definition 1.1
.

We say that
V
is of generic type if
W(0)\neq 0
and is of exceptional type if
W(0)=0
. We also say that zero is a resonance of
H
if the potential
V
is of exceptional type.

Furthermore, Jensen and Nenciu
[
18
]
pointed out that zero is a resonance of
H
if and only if there exists a unique (up to a constant factor) non-zero
\phi\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
such that
H\phi=0
in the distributional sense. Consequently, the trivial potential
V\equiv 0
is of exceptional type.

Moreover, recall that
f\in{\rm BMO}:={\rm BMO}(\mathbb{R})
(Bounded Mean Oscillation space) if
f\in L^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R})
and

\|f\|_{{\rm BMO}}:=\sup_{I}\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}{}|f(x)-f_{I}|dx<\infty,\quad f_{I}=\frac{1}{|I|}\int_{I}{}f(x)dx,

where the supremum takes over all bounded intervals, and
f\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:=\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
(Hardy space) if
f
is a tempered distribution and

\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}:=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{}\sup_{t>0}|(f*\varphi_{t})(x)|dx<\infty,\quad\varphi_{t}(x)=t^{-1}\varphi(x/t)

for some Schwartz function
\varphi
with
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\varphi(x)dx=1
. It is known that the dual space
(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{*}={\rm BMO}
(cf.
[
12
]
).

We write
a\lesssim b
if
a\leq cb
for some constant
c>0
with
a,b\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
. Let
\mathbb{B}(X,Y)
denote the space of all bounded linear operators from
X
to
Y
, and abbreviate
\mathbb{B}(X,X)
as
\mathbb{B}(X)
when
X=Y
. For brevity, we also abbreviate
L^{p}(\mathbb{R})
as
L^{p}
. Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
H=-\Delta+V(x)
be the Schrödinger operator on
\mathbb{R}
and
V(x)
be real-valued satisfying
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If
V
is of generic type and
\beta>7
, then
W_{\pm}\notin\mathbb{B}(L^{1})\cup\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty})
but
W_{\pm}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty},{\rm BMO})
.

(ii)

Assume in addition that
V
is compactly supported. If
V
is of exceptional type and
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1
, then
W_{\pm}\notin\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty},{\rm BMO})\cup\mathbb{B}(L^{1})
.

Remark 1.3
.

Further remarks
on Theorem
1.2
are given as follows.

•

We can explicitly construct a class of potentials satisfying the above conditions (ii). For instance, take
0<\phi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\phi(x)=b
for
x>1
and
\phi(x)=a
for
x<-1
(
b\neq a
) and let
V=\frac{\Delta\phi}{\phi}
. It is clear that
V\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
,
H\phi=0
, and thus zero is a resonance of
H
. Moreover, in this case the Jost function
f_{+}(0,x)=\frac{1}{b}\phi(x)
and satisfies
\lim\limits_{x\rightarrow-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1
.

•

Recall that
W_{\pm}\in\mathcal{B}(L^{p})
for all
1<p<\infty
in both generic and exceptional cases (cf.
[
4
,
15
,
28
]
), and Weder further proved that
W_{\pm}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{1})\cap\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty})
in the exceptional case with

\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)=1
. Together with these known results, our
counterexamples complete the picture of the
L^{p}
boundedness of
one-dimensional wave operators.

•

For both the generic case and the exceptional case with

\lim\limits_{x\to-\infty}f_{+}(0,x)\neq 1
, the wave operators are unbounded on

L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
and
L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, yet a result of Weder shows that they are bounded
from
\mathcal{H}^{1}(\mathbb{R})
to
L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
. However, the two cases differ in the boundedness from

L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
to
\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R})
. We prove that
W_{\pm}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty},{\rm BMO})
in the generic case, while
W_{\pm}\notin\mathbb{B}(L^{\infty},{\rm BMO})
in the
exceptional case.

Remark 1.4
.

(The
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators)
We remark that an important property of wave operators is the following
intertwining property

f(H)P_{ac}(H)=W_{\pm}f(-\Delta)W^{*}_{\pm}

which allows one to reduce
L^{p}-L^{q}
estimates for the perturbed operator
f(H)
to the corresponding estimates for the free operator
f(-\Delta)
:

\|f(H)P_{ac}(H)\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\leq\|W_{\pm}\|_{L^{q}\rightarrow L^{q}}\|f(-\Delta)\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{q}}\|W^{*}_{\pm}\|_{L^{p}\rightarrow L^{p}},

where
f
is any Borel function on
\mathbb{R}
. Because of such a useful feature, the
L^{p}
boundedness of wave operators has been extensively studied.
It is known that K. Yajima first studied this question in his seminal works
[
30
,
31
,
32
,
33
]

for Schrödinger operators
-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}+V
on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
d\geq 3
, where he proved that wave operators are
L^{p}
bounded for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
if zero energy is regular. Subsequently, this topic has been developed for lower dimensions, for instance,
[
34
,
19
,
9
]
for
d=2
and
[
28
,
15
,
4
,
5
,
29
]
for
d=1
and references therein.

Moreover, many studies have extended to higher-order Schrödinger operators
(-\Delta_{\mathbb{R}^{d}})^{m}+V
with
m\geq 2
. The first work
[
14
]
by Goldberg and Green established
L^{p}
boundedness for
1<p<\infty
in the regular case for
(m,d)=(2,3)
, which was subsequently extended to general case
d>2m
by Erdoğan and Green in
[
7
,
8
]
. Further developments include Mizutani, Wan and Yao’s investigation
[
23
,
24
]
of endpoint behavior and zero energy resonances for
(m,d)=(2,3)
and their complete analysis
[
22
]
of all zero resonance types in
(m,d)=(2,1)
, along with Galtbayar-Yajima’s study
[
16
]
of the
(m,d)=(2,4)
case. More recently, Erdoğan-Green-LaMaster
[
11
]
considered the case
d>4m
while Cheng, Soffer, Wu and Yao
[
2
,
3
]
covered the remaining cases
1\leq d\leq 4m
.

Organization of the paper

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section

near

(Subsection

), and establishing the

2.
The analysis on the
L^{p}
boundedness of the low energy part
W^{L}_{-}

In this section, we analyze the
L^{p}
boundedness of the low energy part
W^{L}_{-}
, which is given by

W^{L}_{-}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda\chi(\lambda)R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda,

(2.1)

where
R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)
and
R^{\pm}_{V}(\lambda)
are boundary values of free and perturbed resolvents on
(0,+\infty)
, respectively, i.e.,

R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}(-\Delta-(\lambda\pm i\varepsilon))^{-1},\quad R^{\pm}_{V}(\lambda)=\lim_{\varepsilon\downarrow 0}(H-(\lambda\pm i\varepsilon))^{-1},\quad\lambda>0.

It is known (cf.
[
21
,
1
]
) that such limits exist in the operator norm of
\mathbb{B}(L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}))
for
s>\frac{1}{2}
for both free and perturbed cases under our assumption on
V
. Here

L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}):=\{f:\left<x\right>^{s}f(x)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})\}.
Moreover, the kernel of
R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2})
is given by (see e.g.
[
18
]
)

R^{\pm}_{0}(\lambda^{2},x,y)=\frac{\pm i}{2\lambda}e^{\pm i\lambda|x-y|},\quad\lambda>0,\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}.

(2.2)

Therefore, it is crucial to investigate the asymptotic behavior of
R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})
near
\lambda=0
.

2.1.
Asymptotic behavior of
R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})
near
\lambda=0

To this end, we introduce

M(\lambda):=U+vR^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2})v,

where
U
and
v
are the multiplication operators by
U(x)
and
v(x)
, respectively. Here
U(x)={\rm sgn}~V(x)
, namely,
U(x)=1
if
V(x)\geq 0
and
U(x)=-1
if
V(x)<0
, and
v(x)=\sqrt{|V(x)|}
. Since
H
has no positive eigenvalues in the interval
(0,+\infty)
, it follows that
M(\lambda)
is invertible on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for all
\lambda>0
. We denote its inverse by
M^{-1}(\lambda)
.
Moreover, using the resolvent identity

R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})=R_{0}^{+}(\lambda^{2})-R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})VR^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2})

together with the relations
vUv=V
and
U^{2}=I
(the identity operator), we obtain

R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V=R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2})vM^{-1}(\lambda)v.

(2.3)

This indicates that we can turn to study the asymptotic behavior of
M^{-1}(\lambda)
near
\lambda=0
.

Before presenting the expansion of
M^{-1}(\lambda)
, we introduce some notation. Let
P
be the orthogonal projection onto the space spanned by
v
, i.e.,

P=\alpha\left<\cdot,v\right>v,\quad\alpha=\|V\|^{-1}_{L^{1}}.

Define
Q:=I-P
. Obviously,
Q
is the orthogonal projection onto
({\rm span}\{v\})^{\bot}
, and thus it satisfies

Qv=0,\quad\left<Qf,v\right>=0,\quad f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}).

Denote

T_{0}=U+vG_{0}v,\quad G_{0}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{2}|x-y|.

Let
S_{1}
be the orthogonal projection onto the kernel space
{\rm Ker}QT_{0}Q|_{QL^{2}(\mathbb{R})}
, that is,

S_{1}L^{2}(\mathbb{R})=\{f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}):\left<f,v\right>=0,\ QT_{0}f=0\}.

Throughout this paper, we denote by
K
the integral operator with the kernel
K(x,y)
, i.e.,

(Kf)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}{}K(x,y)f(y)dy.

Moreover, we say that an integral operator
K\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2})
is
absolutely\ bounded
if its associated absolutely value integral operator
|K|
, defined by the kernel
|K(x,y)|
, is also bounded on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
H=-\Delta+V(x)
with
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2
. Then
M^{-1}(\lambda)
has the following asymptotic expansion on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
for
0<\lambda<2\lambda_{0}
.

(i)

If
V
is of generic type and
\beta>7
, then

M^{-1}(\lambda)=QA^{0}_{0}Q+\lambda(QA^{0}_{11}+A^{0}_{12}Q)+\lambda{P_{1}}+\Gamma^{0}_{2}(\lambda).

(2.4)

(ii)

If
V
is of exceptional type and
\beta>11
, then

M^{-1}(\lambda)=-icS_{1}\lambda^{-1}+QA^{1}_{0}Q+2\alpha c(S_{1}T_{0}+T_{0}S_{1})+\lambda\left(QA^{1}_{11}+A^{1}_{12}Q\right)+\lambda{P_{1}}+\lambda P_{2}+\Gamma^{1}_{2}(\lambda).

(2.5)

Here

•

{P_{1}}=-2i\alpha P,\quad P_{2}=4i\alpha^{2}cT_{0}S_{1}T_{0}
with
c
defined in (
A.5
);

•

A^{j}_{0},A^{j}_{11},A^{j}_{12}\ (j=0,1)
are
\lambda
-independent bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
;

•

\Gamma^{j}_{2}(\lambda)
are
\lambda
-dependent bounded operators on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that all the operators appeared in the right hand sides of (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) are absolutely bounded. Moreover,
\Gamma^{j}_{2}(\lambda)
satisfies that for
s=0,1,2
,

\|(\partial^{s}_{\lambda}\Gamma^{j}_{2})(\lambda)\|_{\mathbb{B}(L^{2})}\leq C_{s}\lambda^{2-s},\quad 0<\lambda<2\lambda_{0},\quad j=0,1.

(2.6)

Remark 2.2
.

(1) It is known that such an expansion was previously considered by Jensen and Nenciu in
[
18
]
, but the explicit form of the coefficients was not provided. For our discussion of the
L^{p}
boundedness of
W^{L}_{-}
, a more precise expansion is needed. Based on their ideas, we compute these coefficients in detail in Appendix
A
.

(2) We remark that the orthogonal projections
Q
and
S_{1}
in the expansions can eliminate the singularity of free resolvent at
\lambda=0
(see Lemma
2.5
below), which plays a crucial role in establishing the
L^{p}
boundedness of
W^{L}_{-}
.
Furthermore, we point out that if
V
is of exceptional type, then
S_{1}
is a rank-one operator (see Remark
A.2
), which greatly facilitates our subsequent discussion of boundedness at the endpoints
p=1,\infty
.

2.2.
The
L^{p}
boundedness of
W^{L}_{-}

With Lemma

, we can derive the following

boundedness of
W^{L}_{-}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
H=-\Delta+V
with
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for some
\beta>2
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

If

is generic type and

W^{L}_{-}=\sum\limits_{A\in\Omega_{0}}K_{A}+K_{{P_{1}}}

(2.7)

and

W^{H}_{-}=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda(1-\chi(\lambda))R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda=W^{H}_{1}-W^{H}_{2},

where
\chi(\lambda)\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\chi(\lambda)\equiv 1
on
(-\lambda_{0},\lambda_{0})
and
\chi(\lambda)\equiv 0
on
(-\infty,-2\lambda_{0})\cup(2\lambda_{0},+\infty)
, and

\displaystyle W^{H}_{1}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda(1-\chi(\lambda))R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda,

\displaystyle W^{H}_{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda(1-\chi(\lambda))R^{+}_{0}(\lambda^{2})VR^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})V(R^{+}_{0}-R^{-}_{0})({\lambda}^{2})d\lambda.

We first prove the
L^{p}
boundedness of
W^{H}_{1}
. For this purpose, the following lemma is crucial.

Lemma B.2
.

For any
x\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}
, denote

u(x):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{i\lambda x}\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))d\lambda.

(B.1)

Then

|u(x)|\lesssim\min\{|\ln|x||,|x|^{-2}\}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}).

(B.2)

The integral (
B.1
) should be understood in the distributional sense, i.e., for any Schwartz function
\psi
,

u(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}u(x)\psi(x)dx=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))\hat{\psi}(-\lambda)d\lambda,\quad\hat{\psi}(\lambda):=\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{-i\lambda x}\psi(x)dx.

Thus
u\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
(the space of tempered distributions) because

|u(\psi)|\lesssim\big\|x\hat{\psi}(x)\big\|_{L^{\infty}}=\big\|\hat{\psi^{\prime}}\big\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq\|\psi^{\prime}\|_{L^{1}}\lesssim\sum\limits_{0\leq\alpha\leq 3}\|x^{\alpha}\psi^{\prime}(x)\|_{L^{\infty}}.

The proof of this lemma is postponed to the end of this appendix.

Proposition B.3
.

Let
H=-\Delta+V
with
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for
\beta>1
. Then
W^{H}_{1}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

By (
2.2
), the kernel of
W^{H}_{1}
is given by

W^{H}_{1}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{\pm}\int_{0}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))\int_{\mathbb{R}}V(\omega)e^{i\lambda(|x-\omega|\pm|y-\omega|)}d\omega d\lambda:=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{\pm}W^{\pm}_{1}(x,y).

Denote

f(x)=\min\{|\ln x|,x^{-2}\},\quad x>0.

Note that for any
\omega,y\in\mathbb{R}
, using the invariance of the
L^{1}
-norm and and the evenness of
f(||\cdot|\pm|y||)
, we get

\big\|f(||\cdot-\omega|\pm|y||)\big\|_{L^{1}}=\left\{\begin{aligned} &2\int_{|y|}^{+\infty}f(x)dx\leq 2\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x)dx<\infty,\quad``+"\\
&2\Big(\int_{0}^{|y|}f(|y|-x)dx+\int_{|y|}^{+\infty}f(x-|y|)dx\Big)\leq 4\int_{0}^{+\infty}f(x)dx<\infty,\quad``-".\end{aligned}\right.

This, together with Lemma
B.2
gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}}|W^{\pm}_{1}(x,y)|dx\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}\big|V(\omega)u(|x-\omega|\pm|y-\omega|)\big|d\omega dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}|V(\omega)|\cdot\big\|f(||\cdot-\omega|\pm|y-\omega||)\big\|_{L^{1}}d\omega\lesssim\|V\|_{L^{1}}<\infty.

By symmetry, the same estimate holds for
\|W^{\pm}_{1}(x,\cdot)\|_{L^{1}}
. Lemma
2.6
then yields
W^{H}_{1}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
∎

To treat
W^{H}_{2}
, we first give the following estimates of
R^{+}_{V}(\lambda)
for large
\lambda
based on
[
13
, Lemma 3.9]
.

Lemma B.4
.

For
k=0,1,2,3,\cdots
, let
H=-\Delta+V
with
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for
\beta>2(k+1)
. Then
(\partial^{k}_{\lambda}R^{+}_{V})(\lambda)\in\mathbb{B}(L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}))
is continuous for any
s>k+\frac{1}{2}
. Moreover, the following estimate holds:

\big\|(\partial^{k}_{\lambda}R^{+}_{V})(\lambda)\big\|_{\mathbb{B}(L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\lambda^{-\frac{(1+k)}{2}},\quad s>k+\frac{1}{2},\quad\lambda\geq\lambda^{2}_{0}.

Proposition B.5
.

Let
H=-\Delta+V
with
|V(x)|\lesssim\left<x\right>^{-\beta}
for
\beta>6
. Then
W^{H}_{2}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.

Proof.

It follows from (
2.2
) that

W^{H}_{2}(x,y)=-\frac{1}{4}\sum\limits_{\pm}W^{\pm}_{2}(x,y),

where
\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))
and

\displaystyle W^{\pm}_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{i\lambda(|x|\pm|y|)}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)k^{\pm}_{H}(\lambda,x,y)d\lambda,

\displaystyle k^{\pm}_{H}(\lambda,x,y)

\displaystyle=\big<R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})F^{\pm}_{y,\lambda},F^{-}_{x,\lambda}\big>,\quad F^{\pm}_{y,\lambda}(\omega)=e^{\pm i\lambda(|y-\omega|-|y|)}V(\omega).

Note that using the triangle inequality and the decay of
V
, we obtain, for any
s<\beta-\frac{1}{2}-k
,

\sup\limits_{\lambda,y}\big\|\partial^{k}_{\lambda}F^{\pm}_{y,\lambda}\big\|_{L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R})}\lesssim 1.

Moreover, Lemma
B.4
implies that for
j=0,1,2
and
s>j+\frac{1}{2}
,

\|\partial^{j}_{\lambda}\big(R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})\big)\|_{\mathbb{B}(L^{2}_{s}(\mathbb{R}),L^{2}_{-s}(\mathbb{R}))}\lesssim\lambda^{-1},\quad\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}.

Combining these two estimates and the formula

\displaystyle\big(\partial^{\ell}_{\lambda}k^{\pm}_{H}\big)(\lambda,x,y)=\sum\limits_{k=0}^{\ell}C^{k}_{\ell}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{k}C^{j}_{k}\big<\partial^{j}_{\lambda}\big(R^{+}_{V}(\lambda^{2})\big)\cdot\big(\partial^{k-j}_{\lambda}F^{\pm}_{y,\lambda}\big),\partial^{\ell-k}_{\lambda}F^{-}_{x,\lambda}\big>,

we derive, for
\beta>6
and
\ell=0,1,2
,

\sup\limits_{x,y}\big|\big(\partial^{\ell}_{\lambda}k^{\pm}_{H}\big)(\lambda,x,y)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{-1},\quad\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}.

(B.3)

It is clear that
|W^{\pm}_{2}(x,y)|\lesssim 1\lesssim\left<|x|-|y|\right>^{-2}
if
||x|\pm|y||\leq 1
. For
||x|\pm|y||\geq 1
, denote
K^{\pm}_{H}(\lambda,x,y):=\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)k^{\pm}_{H}(\lambda,x,y).
Note that for
j=0,1,2
,

\widetilde{\chi}^{(j)}(\lambda)\big|_{\lambda=\lambda_{0}}=0,\quad\big|\widetilde{\chi}^{(j)}(\lambda)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{-(j+1)},\quad\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}.

(B.4)

Integrating by parts twice to
W^{\pm}_{2}(x,y)
and using the estimates (
B.4
) and (
B.3
) yields

\displaystyle|W^{\pm}_{2}(x,y)|=\frac{1}{(|x|\pm|y|)^{2}}\Big|\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{+\infty}e^{i\lambda(|x|\pm|y|)}(\partial^{2}_{\lambda}K^{\pm}_{H})(\lambda,x,y)d\lambda\Big|\lesssim\frac{1}{(|x|\pm|y|)^{2}}\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-2}d\lambda\lesssim\left<|x|-|y|\right>^{-2}.

Therefore, by Lemma
2.6
, we conclude
W^{H}_{2}\in\mathbb{B}(L^{p})
for all
1\leq p\leq\infty
.
∎

Combining Propositions
B.3
and
B.5
, we complete the proof of Theorem
B.1
. Finally, we come to prove Lemma
B.2
.

Proof of Lemma
B.2
.

To establish (
B.2
), for any
\varepsilon>0
, we introduce

u_{\varepsilon}(x):=\int_{0}^{+\infty}e^{i\lambda x}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda)d\lambda,\quad\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)=\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda)),

where
\varphi\in C^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
satisfies
\varphi(x)=1
for
|x|\leq\frac{1}{2}
and
\varphi(x)=0
for
|x|\geq 1
.
Clearly,
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
for any
\varepsilon>0
. Moreover, we note that for any
\psi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
, the dominated convergence theorem yields

u_{\varepsilon}(\psi)=\int_{0}^{+\infty}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda)\hat{\psi}(-\lambda)d\lambda\rightarrow\int_{0}^{+\infty}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\hat{\psi}(-\lambda)d\lambda=u(\psi),\quad\varepsilon\rightarrow 0.

Next we claim the uniform estimate

\sup\limits_{\varepsilon>0}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|\lesssim\min\{|\ln|x||,|x|^{-2}\}:=f(x).

(B.5)

Once this is proved, since
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
, for any
\psi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we obtain

|u(\psi)|=\lim\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}|u_{\varepsilon}(\psi)|\leq\limsup\limits_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0}\int_{\mathbb{R}}|u_{\varepsilon}(x)\psi(x)|dx\lesssim\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)|\psi(x)|dx\lesssim\sup\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}}|\psi(x)|

which implies the desired (
B.2
).
To obtain (
B.5
), we distinguish two cases. If
0<|x|<1
, since supp
(1-\chi(\lambda))\subseteq[\lambda_{0},+\infty)
and supp
\varphi(\lambda)\subseteq\{|\lambda|\leq 1\}
, we have

u_{\varepsilon}(x)=\Big(\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{|x|^{-1}}+\int_{|x|^{-1}}^{\varepsilon^{-1}}\Big)e^{i\lambda x}\lambda^{-1}(1-\chi(\lambda))\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda)d\lambda:=u_{\varepsilon,1}(x)+u_{\varepsilon,2}(x).

For
u_{\varepsilon,1}(x)
,

|u_{\varepsilon,1}(x)|\lesssim\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{|x|^{-1}}\lambda^{-1}d\lambda\lesssim|\ln|x||.

For
u_{\varepsilon,2}(x)
, integrating by parts once yields

u_{\varepsilon,2}(x)=i\frac{e^{i\frac{x}{|x|}}}{x}\widetilde{\chi}(|x|^{-1})\varphi(\varepsilon|x|^{-1})-\frac{1}{ix}\int_{|x|^{-1}}^{\varepsilon^{-1}}e^{i\lambda x}(\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda))^{\prime}d\lambda.

Notice that by the estimate (
B.4
) and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we get

\big|(\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda))^{\prime}\big|=\big|\widetilde{\chi}^{\prime}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda)+\lambda^{-1}\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varepsilon\lambda\varphi^{\prime}(\varepsilon\lambda)\big|\lesssim\lambda^{-2},\quad\lambda\geq\lambda_{0}.

Therefore,

|u_{\varepsilon,2}(x)|\lesssim 1+\frac{1}{|x|}\int_{|x|^{-1}}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-2}dx\lesssim 1,\quad{\rm uniformly\ in\ }\varepsilon>0.

If
|x|\geq 1
, applying the integration by parts twice to
u_{\varepsilon}(x)
and using (
B.4
) and
\varphi\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R})
, we obtain

|u_{\varepsilon}(x)|=\Big|-x^{-2}\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{+\infty}e^{i\lambda x}(\widetilde{\chi}(\lambda)\varphi(\varepsilon\lambda))^{\prime\prime}d\lambda\Big|\lesssim|x|^{-2}\int_{\lambda_{0}}^{+\infty}\lambda^{-3}d\lambda\lesssim|x|^{-2}.

To sum up, we derive (
B.5
). This completes the proof.
∎
