Abstract
. We study a Schrödinger equation in the upper half-space with a nonlinear Neumann boundary interaction driven by the Bessel operator ℬ z ( a ) \mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)} , a > − 1 a>-1 . The problem arises naturally as an extension formulation for a nonlocal NLS with memory and can also be interpreted as a Schrödinger evolution with a nonlinear singular source concentrated on a codimension-one interface. We first develop a complete linear theory for the associated inhomogeneous problem with nonhomogeneous Neumann data. A central ingredient is a new Duhamel representation formula that separates bulk and boundary dynamics and identifies the precise role of the boundary propagator. Using this formula, we establish sharp Strichartz estimates adapted to the geometry of the half-space and the singular structure induced by the Bessel operator. The analysis reveals a basic dichotomy between the regimes a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 and − 1 < a < 0 -1<a<0 : in the former, bulk and boundary exhibit a unified dispersive behavior, whereas in the latter the dispersive structure becomes anomalous, requiring weighted estimates and distinct functional frameworks for the bulk and boundary contributions. As an application of the linear theory, we prove well-posedness results for the nonlinear problem in the L a 2 L_{a}^{2} -mass critical and subcritical regimes. For a ≥ 0 a\geq 0 , we obtain global well-posedness for sufficiently small critical data, together with local and global results in the subcritical case. In the anomalous range − 1 < a < 0 -1<a<0 , we establish existence and uniqueness on arbitrary finite time intervals for sufficiently small critical data, as well as local well-posedness in the subcritical regime. The results provide a unified dispersive framework for Schrödinger equations with nonlinear boundary interactions and singular extension structures associated with nonlocal-in-time dynamics.

1.
Introduction

In this paper we study dispersive properties and well-posedness for a Schrödinger equation in the upper half-space with a nonlinear Neumann boundary interaction. More precisely, given
a>-1
,
\mu\in\mathbb{C}
and
p>1
, we analyze the following Cauchy problem in the half-space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}=\mathbb{R}^{d}_{x}\times\mathbb{R}^{+}_{z}
with a nonlinear boundary interaction:

(1.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U-i(\Delta_{x}U+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U)=F(X,t),&\ \ X=(x,z)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1},\ t\in\mathbb{R},\\
\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=-\mu|U(x,0,t)|^{p-1}U(x,0,t),&\ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\in\mathbb{R},\\
U(X,0)=u_{0}(X),\end{cases}

where we have denoted with
\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}:=\partial_{zz}+\frac{a}{z}\partial_{z}

the singular Bessel operator on the half-line
z>0
.

The interest of (
1.1
) is motivated by two complementary interpretations which are further elucidated in Section
1.1
, where we also provide several references:

(i)

on the one hand, when
a=0
, extending
U
evenly across the boundary
z=0
leads to a Schrödinger evolution in the whole space with a nonlinear source concentrated on the codimension-one hypersurface
\{z=0\}
. More generally, for arbitrary
a>-1
, we obtain a nonlinear singular interaction supported on the interface coupled to a singular Bessel operator;

(ii)

on the other hand, and more fundamentally for the purposes of this work, (
1.1
) arises naturally as the extension problem associated with a nonlocal NLS with memory, in a manner reminiscent of the extension procedure of Caffarelli and Silvestre for fractional Laplacians.
In a forthcoming work we will use the theory developed here to analyze this nonlocal problem.

The present paper develops a complete dispersive framework for (
1.1
). The main difficulty is that the interaction between the boundary geometry and the Bessel operator produces two genuinely different dispersive regimes. When
a\geq 0
, the equation behaves in many respects like a classical Schrödinger evolution: the propagator obeys the natural scaling
(X,t)\to(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}t)
of the equation, bulk and boundary exhibit compatible dispersion, and the corresponding Strichartz estimates fit into a unified framework. In contrast, when
-1<a<0
, the dispersive structure becomes anomalous. The bulk propagator no longer behaves according to the expected scaling laws, weighted estimates become unavoidable, and the natural functional spaces involve sums and intersections of Lebesgue spaces. One of the principal goals of the paper is to identify and quantify this dichotomy.

Our analysis begins with the linear problem with nonhomogeneous Neumann data,

(1.2)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U-i(\Delta_{x}U+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U)=F(X,t),\\
\displaystyle\lim_{z\to 0^{+}}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=\Phi(x,t),\\
U(X,0)=u_{0}(X).\end{cases}

A central contribution of the paper is the derivation of a new Duhamel representation formula for (
1.2
). This formula separates the evolution into bulk and boundary components and identifies the precise role played by the boundary propagator. More precisely, we show that solutions can be represented as

U=\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}(u_{0})+\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)+\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi),

where the operators
\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}
and
\mathbb{D}_{a}
describe the bulk evolution, while
\Theta_{a}^{\star}
encodes the boundary interaction. This decomposition lies at the heart of the paper and provides the mechanism through which the bulk and boundary dispersive behaviors can be analyzed separately.

Building on this representation formula, we establish sharp Strichartz estimates adapted to the geometry of the problem. The estimates reveal a striking asymmetry between the bulk and boundary dynamics. In the range
a\geq 0
, the bulk theory obeys the natural scaling relation

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}+\frac{a+1}{m}=\frac{d+a+1}{2},

leading to a family of triples
(q,r,m)
of admissible exponents. In the anomalous regime
-1<a<0
, however, the relevant estimates no longer follow the scaling dictated by dimensional analysis. Instead, the bulk propagator behaves as though
a=0
, but only after introducing a nontrivial weight in the transverse variable
z
. The resulting estimates involve discrepant dispersive rates and require a substantially different functional framework.

An additional difficulty concerns traces on the boundary. Although the Strichartz estimates provide uniform control in the transverse variable
z
, such bounds do not by themselves guarantee convergence to the boundary manifold
\{z=0\}
. Since the nonlinearity in (
1.1
) acts precisely through the boundary trace
U(x,0,t)
, establishing the existence and continuity of traces becomes an essential step in the nonlinear analysis. We resolve this issue through a separate trace theorem which allows the nonlinear fixed-point argument to close in the critical spaces.

As an application of the linear theory, we prove local and global well-posedness results for (
1.1
) in the
L_{a}^{2}
-mass critical and subcritical regimes. In the range
a\geq 0
, we obtain global well-posedness for sufficiently small critical data, together with local and global results in the subcritical case. In the anomalous range
-1<a<0
, we prove existence and uniqueness on arbitrary finite time intervals for sufficiently small critical data, as well as local well-posedness in the subcritical regime.

The present work extends and substantially generalizes our previous analysis in
[
23
]
, where we studied the one-dimensional problem associated with the Bessel Schrödinger equation on the half-line. It is also related to recent investigations of nonlinear Schrödinger equations with boundary nonlinearities in the case
a=0
, especially in one spatial dimension, i.e., when
d=1
. However, the singular extension structure considered here leads to a considerably richer dispersive theory and requires new analytical tools that do not appear in the case
a=0
.

2.
Description of the work and statement of the results

In this long section we discuss the more technical aspects of the work and also state the main results. It will be helpful for the reader to become familiar right from the start with some of the relevant notation. We continue to indicate with
X=(x,z),Y=(y,\zeta)
generic points in the bulk space
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
, whereas the notation
X_{0}=(x,0),Y_{0}=(y,0)
is used for points of the boundary

\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
. Points in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
will be denoted by

(X,t),(Y,\tau)
.
As customary,
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
is the Schwartz class in the variables
(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d+1}
, whereas we denote by
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
the restrictions to the half-spaces
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
of the Schwartz functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{(x,z)}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{(x,z)}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
respectively. We indicate by
d\omega_{a}(X)=z^{a}dxdz
the invariant measure for the differential operator
\Delta_{x}+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}
in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
. We mean by this that given
U,V\in C^{\infty}_{0}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\overline{V}(\Delta_{x}U+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U)d\omega_{a}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}U\ \overline{(\Delta_{x}V+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}V)}d\omega_{a}(X).

We denote with
L^{m}_{a,z}=L^{m}(\mathbb{R}^{+},z^{a}dz)
,
1\leq m<\infty
. When
m=\infty
, we understand that
L^{\infty}_{a,z}=L^{\infty}_{z}
. In this work we routinely use mixed norm spaces
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}
,
L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
and
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
. Such classes are discussed in detail in Section
3.1
. When
m=r
we write
L^{m}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
for the class
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{m}_{x}
. For instance,
L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})=L^{2}_{a,z}L^{2}_{x}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1},d\omega_{a}(X))
. When
m=\infty
, we simply write
L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
. The notation
C^{b}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
indicates the space
C_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
of continuous bounded functions on
(0,\infty)
with values in the Banach space
L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
.

2.1.
A key Duhamel formula

To understand the problem (
1.1
) we first analyze the linear problem (
1.2
) with a nonzero Neumann condition.
Our first main result, Theorem
5.1
, shows that, given
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
,
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
and
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, then for any
-1<a<1
, the problem (
1.2
) admits a unique
mild solution
given by the following generalized Duhamel formula

(2.1)

\displaystyle U(X,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau,

where we have denoted by

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)

\displaystyle=\frac{e^{-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(t)\,\frac{(d+a+1)\pi}{4}}}{2^{\frac{2d+a+1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{e^{i\,\frac{z^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{|t|^{\frac{d+a+1}{2}}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}J_{\frac{a-1}{2}}\!\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}\right),\qquad t\neq 0,

the Schrödinger propagator with zero Neumann condition, see Section
4
. Some of the basic properties of this function are:

(1)

\overline{\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)}=\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,-t)
;

(2)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(\lambda X,\lambda Y,\lambda^{2}t)=\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)
,
\lambda>0
;

(3)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t):=\underset{\zeta\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)=\frac{e^{-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(t)\,\frac{(d+a+1)\pi}{4}}}{2^{d+a}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)}\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{|t|^{\frac{d+a+1}{2}}},\qquad t\neq 0
.

In particular, unlike what happens for
\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)
when
X,Y\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
, the boundary restriction
\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t)
showcases one single dispersive regime
|t|^{-\frac{d+a+1}{2}}
for all
a>-1
.
This fact is responsible for the surprising uniformity of boundary Strichartz estimates in Section
7
which, unlike the estimates in the bulk in Section
6
, do not distinguish between the two regimes
-1<a<0
and
0\leq a<1
.

Formula (
2.1
) plays a pervasive role in this work and it will be convenient, henceforth,
to express the mild solution (
2.1
) to the linear Cauchy problem (
1.2
) in the following form

(2.2)

U(X,t)=\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})(X,t)+\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t)+\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)(X,t),

where the operators
\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a},\mathbb{D}_{a}
(
a>-1
), and
\Theta_{a}^{\star}
(
-1<a<1
) are respectively defined by:

(2.3)

\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}(u_{0})(X,t):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y),

(2.4)

\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t):=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau,

and

(2.5)

\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(X,t):=i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau.

We emphasize that the mild solution (
2.2
) satisfies the weighted Neumann condition

\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=\Phi(x,t),\ \ \ \ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\ t\not=0,

but it is not a classical solution in general. For instance, if
f\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
and
f
is even in
z
, and if
h\in C_{0}^{\infty}[0,\infty)
, with
h(z)\equiv 1
for
0\leq z\leq 1
, then for the initial datum

(2.6)

u_{0}(X)=f(X)+\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h(z)\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}),\ \ \ \ -1<a<1,

forcing term
F=0
and Neumann datum
\Phi=0
, the solution given by (
2.2
) satisfies

\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=\begin{cases}0,\ \ \ \text{for}\ t\not=0,\\
1,\ \ \ \text{for}\ t=0.\end{cases}

2.2.
Admissible triples

We next introduce the relevant notion of admissible triple. As it will be clear from the next definition, in the problem (
1.2
) there exist two different regimes:
a\geq 0
and
-1<a<0
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
r,m\geq 2
. The triple
(q,r,m)
is called
admissible
if it satisfies:

(2.7)

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}+\frac{a+1}{m}=\frac{d+a+1}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \text{when}\ \ a\geq 0;

or

(2.8)

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}+\frac{1}{m}=\frac{d+1}{2},\ \ \ \\
\ \ \ \ \text{when}\ \ -1<a<0.

Note that the triple
(\infty,2,2)
is admissible for any
a>-1
. Concerning Definition
2.1
some comments are in order.
First, in Section
4.3
we show that
for any

a>-1
the condition (
2.7
) is necessary for the following Strichartz estimate to hold for the solution to (
1.2
) with forcing term
F=0
and zero Neumann condition
\Phi=0
:

(2.9)

||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

This follows in a standard way from the scalings
(X,t)\to(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}t)
. However, in the anomalous range
-1<a<0
the operator
\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}
does not obey these scalings. Instead, the equation (
2.8
) suggests that it behaves as if
a=0
, except that a weight appears in the relevant Strichartz estimates and the spaces
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
must be replaced by sums and intersections of Lebesgue spaces.

A second comment is that, since we want the solution (
2.2
) to be continuous and bounded up to the boundary manifold
\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
, we need to have
m=\infty
in (
2.9
). In such case,
from (
2.7
), (
2.8
) we see that admissibility of the triple
(q,r,\infty)
means

(2.10)

a\geq 0:\ \ \ \ \ \frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d+a+1}{2};\ \ \ \ \ \ \ -1<a<0:\ \ \ \ \ \frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d+1}{2}.

We next discuss these two cases separately.

Case

a\geq 0
. Since in such case we must have
d+a-1\geq 0
, two possibilities present:

(2.11)

\text{(a)}\ \ \ \ \ d+a-1>0,\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \text{or}\ \ \ \ \ \ \text{(b)}\ \ \ \ d+a-1=0.

(a)

If
d+a-1>0
and
q>2
, then if
(q,r,\infty)
is admissible, the first equation in (
2.10
) implies that it must be
2\leq r<\frac{2d}{d+a-1}
. In such situation we are able to establish unified Strichartz estimates (bulk
+
boundary). If
q=2
, then the triple
(2,r,\infty)
is admissible iff
r=\frac{2d}{d+a-1}
. The open end-point
(2,\frac{2d}{d+a-1},\infty)
is the counterpart of the Keel-Tao result for
(2,\frac{2d}{d-2})
when
d\geq 3
, see
[
41
,
56
]
, but the situation is complicated by the fact that in the
z
-variable we have
m=\infty
in (
2.9
).

(b)

If
d+a-1=0
, we must necessarily have
d=1
and
a=0
, and the first equation in (
2.10
) becomes

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=1.

This situation has been treated in the interesting recent work
[
49
]
, and our results recover theirs for all triples
(q,r,\infty)
satisfying
q>2
, or equivalently
r<\infty
. At the end-point
(2,\infty,\infty)
the estimate (
2.9
) fails in view of the Montgomery-Smith counterexample in
[
47
]
.

Case

-1<a<0
. Again, we have two possibilities:

(2.12)

\text{(c)}\ \ \ \ \ d>1,\ \ \ \ \ \text{or}\ \ \ \ \ \text{(d)}\ \ \ \ \ d=1.

(c)

If
d>1
and
q>2
, then the second equation in (
2.10
) implies that, if
(q,r,\infty)
is admissible, we must have
2\leq r<\frac{2d}{d-1}
. In such situation, our best possible Strichartz estimates contain a weight in the
z
variable and present a discrepancy between bulk and boundary.
If instead
q=2
, then for
(2,r,\infty)
to be admissible, we must have
r=\frac{2d}{d-1}
. The end-point
(2,\frac{2d}{d-1},\infty)
when
-1<a<0
is presently open.

(d)

If
d=1
, the second equation in (
2.10
) implies that
(q,r,\infty)
is admissible if

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{r}=\frac{1}{2}.

Since
q=2
is not allowed, our result cover all admissible triples
(q,r,\infty)
.

Remark 2.2
.

For future use, it will be important to keep in mind that the triple
(r,r,\infty)
is always admissible, and we have:

(2.13)

q=r=\frac{2(d+2)}{d+a+1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ q^{\prime}=r^{\prime}=\frac{2(d+2)}{d+3-a},\ \ \ \ \text{when}\ \ a\geq 0,

Organization of the paper.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
we collect preliminary material from our work
[
23
]
, including properties of the Bessel operator, the associated weighted functional framework, dispersive estimates on the half-line, and a generalized Hardy–Littlewood–Sobolev lemma that plays a key role in the anomalous regime
-1<a<0
.

In
4
we study the Schrödinger propagator associated with homogeneous Neumann conditions in the upper half-space. We establish its basic structural properties, including symmetry, scaling, unitarity, and the corresponding Duhamel formula. We also discuss the distinction between mild and classical solutions and identify the admissible scaling relations underlying the Strichartz theory.

Section
5
is devoted to the linear problem with nonhomogeneous Neumann boundary data. There we derive the fundamental representation formula (
2.1
), equivalently written as (
2.2
), which separates the bulk evolution from the boundary forcing term. This formula constitutes the backbone of the paper and is the starting point for all subsequent dispersive estimates.

In Section
6
we establish bulk Strichartz estimates for the propagator. The analysis reveals a sharp distinction between the cases
a\geq 0
and
-1<a<0
: in the former one obtains estimates compatible with the natural scaling of the equation, whereas in the latter weighted estimates and mixed sum/intersection spaces become unavoidable.

Section
7
is devoted to boundary Strichartz estimates. In contrast with the bulk theory, the boundary propagator exhibits a remarkably uniform dispersive behavior for all
a>-1
. Exploiting this structure, we prove estimates that are independent of the dichotomy governing the bulk dynamics.

The existence of traces on the boundary is addressed in Section
8
. Although the estimates of
7
provide uniform control in the transverse variable
z
, they do not by themselves guarantee convergence to the boundary. The main result of this section establishes the existence and continuity of boundary traces in the appropriate functional setting.

In Section
9
we combine the estimates obtained in the previous sections to prove Theorems 1.3 and 1.4. Finally, Section
10
is devoted to the nonlinear problem. There we establish local and global well-posedness results in the mass-critical and subcritical regimes, completing the proof of Theorems
2.8

and
2.9
. In the closing Section
11
we use our Strichartz estimates to prove a theorem a la Tomas-Stein for the restriction of the Fourier-Hankel transform to a paraboloid.

3.1.
Function spaces

Given exponents
1\leq q,r<\infty
, we will denote by
L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
the Banach space of measurable functions
\Phi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

||\Phi||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\Phi(\cdot,t)||^{q}_{L^{r}_{x}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(x,t)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{q}{r}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}<\infty.

If
1\leq r,m<\infty
we will denote by
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}
the Banach space of measurable functions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(3.15)

||f||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|f(x,z)|^{r}dx\right)^{\frac{m}{r}}d\omega_{a}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}||f(\cdot,z)||^{m}_{L^{r}_{x}}d\omega_{a}(z)\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty.

When
r=m
we write
L^{r}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
instead of
L^{r}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}
. In such case, the norm is given by

||u||_{L^{r}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}|u(x,t)|^{r}d\omega_{a}(X)\right)^{\frac{1}{r}},

where, by slightly abusing the notation, we have let

(3.16)

d\omega_{a}(X)=z^{a}dxdz=z^{a}dX.

Since the context will be clear, there will be no risk of confusion between the two measures
d\omega_{a}(z)
and
d\omega_{a}(X)
.
In a similar way, given
1\leq q,r,m<\infty
we will denote by
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
the Banach space of measurable functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(3.17)

||F||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||^{m}_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}z^{a}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty.

By standard tools one can show that, when none of the exponents equals
\infty
, the spaces
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
are respectively dense in
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}
and
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
.
When one or more of the exponents

equals
\infty
, the corresponding norms must be replaced by essential suprema. When
m=\infty
, we understand that
L^{\infty}_{a,z}=L^{\infty}_{z}
. We will also indicate with

(3.18)

C^{b}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}=C_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}\cap L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}

the space of the functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that
z\to F(\cdot,z,\cdot)
is continuous and bounded in
z\geq 0
, with values in the Banach space
L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
. Such space is endowed with the norm
\underset{z\geq 0}{\sup}||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}=||F||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}
.

In the proof of Theorem
2.9
we will need to restrict the Lebesgue spaces in (
3.17
) to a time interval
[0,T]
. For this purpose, we will use the notation
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{x}
to indicate the Banach space of measurable functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times[0,T]\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(3.19)

||F||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{x}}:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||^{m}_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{x}}z^{a}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty,

where we have let

||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{T}L^{r}_{x}}=\left(\int_{0}^{T}||F(\cdot,z,t)||^{q}_{L^{r}_{x}}dt\right)^{\frac{1}{q}},

and continue to use the notation (
3.17
) for the mixed norms on the whole line
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

It will be useful to keep in mind how the norms (
3.15
) and (
3.17
) respectively change under the following change of scale

f_{\lambda}(X)=f(\lambda X)=f(\lambda x,\lambda z),\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ F_{\lambda}(X,t)=F(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}t).

We have

(3.20)

||f_{\lambda}||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}}=\lambda^{-(\frac{d}{r}+\frac{a+1}{m})}\ ||f||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{r}_{x}},\ \ \ \ \ \ \ ||F_{\lambda}||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}=\lambda^{-(\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}+\frac{a+1}{m})}\ ||F||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}.

Finally, we use the brief notation
L^{q_{1}+q_{2}}
to indicate the
Banach space
L^{q_{1}}+L^{q_{2}}
of measurable functions
f
such that
f=f_{1}+f_{2}
, with
f_{1}\in L^{q_{1}}
and

f_{2}\in L^{q_{2}}
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{q_{1}+q_{2}}}=\inf_{f=f_{1}+f_{2},f_{1}\in L^{q_{1}},f_{2}\in L^{q_{2}}}\|f_{1}\|_{L^{q_{1}}}+\|f_{2}\|_{L^{q_{2}}}.

We also use the short notation
L^{p\cap q}
to denote the Banach space
L^{p}\cap L^{q}
, endowed with the norm

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p\cap q}}=\|f\|_{L^{p}}+\|f\|_{L^{q}}.

We recall that
(L^{p+q})^{\prime}=L^{p^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}}
. We respectively indicate by

L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}+q_{2}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
and
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}\cap q_{2}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
the space of measurable functions
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
such that

(3.21)

\displaystyle||F||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}+q_{1}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||^{m}_{L^{q_{1}+q_{2}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}z^{a}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty

\displaystyle||F||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}\cap q_{1}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle:=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,z,\cdot)||^{m}_{L^{q_{1}\cap q_{2}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}z^{a}dz\right)^{\frac{1}{m}}<\infty,

For measurable functions
U:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times[0,T]\to\overline{\mathbb{C}}
, similarly to (
3.19
) we will write
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}+q_{2}}_{T}L^{r}_{x}
and
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q_{1}\cap q_{2}}_{T}L^{r}_{x}
.

3.2.
A generalized Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev lemma

We close this section with a generalization of the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev theorem. For its proof see for instance
[
12
, Lemma 5.1]
.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{\infty}<1
,
C_{1},C_{2}>0
. Let
K:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that

\displaystyle|K(t)|\leq\begin{cases}\frac{C_{1}}{|t|^{\gamma_{1}}}\ \ \ \ \text{if }\ |t|\leq 1,\\
\frac{C_{2}}{|t|^{\gamma_{\infty}}}\ \ \ \ \text{if}\ |t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

Let
1<p<q<\infty
,
1<p_{\infty},q_{\infty}<\infty
be exponents such that

\displaystyle\gamma_{1}=1+\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p},\ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \gamma_{\infty}\geq 1+\frac{1}{q_{\infty}}-\frac{1}{p_{\infty}}.

Then one has

(3.22)

\displaystyle\|f\star K\|_{L^{q+q_{\infty}}}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p\cap p_{\infty}}}.

We will apply Lemma
3.6
with the choices
p=q^{\prime}
and
p_{\infty}=q^{\prime}_{\infty}
. This means that, given
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{\infty}
, the exponents
q,q_{\infty}
are determined by the equations

(3.23)

\frac{2}{q}=\gamma_{1},\ \ \ \ \ \ \frac{2}{q_{\infty}}=\gamma_{\infty}.

4.
The Schrödinger propagator with a zero Neumann condition

Given
a>-1
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, we next consider the following generalization of the Cauchy problem (
3.4
), (
3.5
):

(4.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U-i\left\{\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U+\Delta_{x}U\right\}=F(X,t),\ \ \ (X,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\\
\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=0,\ \ \ \ U(X,0)=u_{0}(X).\end{cases}

It is well-known (see for instance formula (2.10) in
[
8
]
for the case of the heat equation) that the fundamental solution
\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)
of the Cauchy problem (
4.1
) is given by

(4.2)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)=S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)S(x,y,t).

In (
4.2
) we have denoted by
S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)
the fundamental solution (
3.6
), whereas
S(x,y,t)
is the kernel (
3.14
).
Note that (
3.5
) implies the Neumann condition

(4.3)

\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)=0.

From (
3.6
) and (
3.14
) we have

(4.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)=\frac{e^{-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(t)\,\frac{(d+a+1)\pi}{4}}}{2^{\frac{2d+a+1}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}}\frac{e^{i\,\frac{z^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{|t|^{\frac{d+a+1}{2}}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}J_{\frac{a-1}{2}}\!\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}\right),\qquad t\neq 0.

We notice the following consequence of (
4.4
):

(4.5)

\overline{\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)}=\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,-t).

This identity is justified by the fact that
J_{\frac{a-1}{2}}(\frac{z\zeta}{2t})
is real-valued for
z,\zeta,t>0
.
Using (
3.9
), we obtain

(4.6)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t):=\underset{\zeta\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)=\frac{e^{-i\,\operatorname{sgn}(t)\,\frac{(d+a+1)\pi}{4}}}{2^{d+a}\pi^{\frac{d}{2}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)}\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{|t|^{\frac{d+a+1}{2}}},\qquad t\neq 0.

In the special case
a=0
, (
4.4
) takes a simple form, see
[
23
, Prop. 2.6]
.

Proposition 4.1
.

When
a=0
we have

\mathbb{S}_{0}(X,Y,t)=\frac{e^{-i\operatorname{sgn}(t)\frac{(d+1)\pi}{4}}}{2^{\frac{2d+3}{2}}\pi^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}+\zeta^{2}+|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}}{|t|^{\frac{d+1}{2}}}\left(e^{i\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}}+e^{-i\frac{z\zeta}{2|t|}}\right),\qquad t\neq 0.

4.1.
Mild versus classical solutions

We recall that
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
respectively indicate the restrictions to the half-spaces
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
of the Schwartz functions in
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{X}
and
\mathbb{R}^{d+1}_{X}\times\mathbb{R}_{t}
. We also respectively indicate with
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
the spaces of
C^{\infty}
functions
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\to\mathbb{C}
, and
F:\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{C}
, such that for any
N\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
one has

||f||_{N}=\underset{k+|\alpha|\leq N}{\sup}\ \ \underset{X\in\mathbb{R}^{+}}{\sup}(1+|X|^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}\left|\left(\frac{1}{z}\partial_{z}\right)^{k}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}f(X)\right|<\infty,

and similarly

||F||_{N}=\underset{k+|\alpha|+\ell\leq N}{\sup}\ \ \underset{(x,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{+}\times\mathbb{R}}{\sup}(1+|X|^{2}+t^{2})^{\frac{p}{2}}\left|\left(\frac{1}{z}\partial_{z}\right)^{k}\partial^{\alpha}_{x}\partial_{t}^{\ell}F(X,t)\right|<\infty.

One has

\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}),\ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})\subset\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}),

but the inclusions are strict. For instance, the function
u_{0}
in (
2.6
) belongs to
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, but it does not belong to
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
. The reason for this is that for any
u\in\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
we must have for
a>-1

\underset{(X,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}}{\sup}|z^{a}\partial_{z}u(X)|\ \underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\ 0,

but it is easy to see that
z^{a}\partial_{z}u_{0}(X)\to 1
as
z\to 0^{+}
.

The classes
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
can be characterized as the restrictions to the half-spaces
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
and
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R}
of Schwartz functions which are
even
in the variable
z
. Although we will not use them in the present work, we have mentioned them since they are the appropriate spaces for
classical
solutions. For the notion of mild solution see
[
50
, Definition 2.3, p.106]
.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
a>-1
. Assume that
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
.
There exists a unique mild solution to the problem (
4.1
) given by the Duhamel formula

(4.7)

\displaystyle U(x,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau.

If
u_{0}\in\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
F\in\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
, then
U
is also a classical solution.

4.2.
Conservation of mass

We define the Schrödinger propagator for the problem (
4.1
), with forcing term
F(X,t)\equiv 0
, as the operator

(4.8)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(t)u_{0}(X):=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y).

Keeping (
2.3
), (
3.16
) and (
4.2
) in mind, we see that we can write

(4.9)

\mathbb{S}_{a}(t)u_{0}(X)=S_{a}(t)(S(t)(u_{0}(\cdot,\cdot))(x))(z)=S(t)\big(S_{a}(t)(u_{0}(\cdot,\cdot))(z)\big)(x).

The following result will be important in the sequel.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
a>-1
. For every
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, and every
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we have

||\mathbb{S}_{a}(t)u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}=||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

Proof.

Using (
4.9
), Proposition
3.2
, the classical result

||S(t)\psi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=||\psi||_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})},

and Tonelli’s theorem, we easily obtain

\displaystyle||\mathbb{S}_{a}(t)u_{0}||^{2}_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|S(t)\big(S_{a}(t)(u_{0}(\cdot,\cdot))(z)\big)(x)|^{2}dxz^{a}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|S_{a}(t)(u_{0}(x,\cdot))(z)\big)|^{2}dxz^{a}dz=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|S_{a}(t)(u_{0}(x,\cdot))(z)\big)|^{2}z^{a}dzdx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|u_{0}(x,z)|^{2}z^{a}dzdx=||u_{0}||^{2}_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

∎

4.3.
Admissible triples

We next discuss the question of the admissible exponents in our Strichartz estimates. Since in the problem (
1.2
) the space variables
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z>0
play different roles, we use the mixed Lebesgue spaces
L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
with the norms (
3.17
).

Given an initial datum
u_{0}\in\Sigma(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, and forcing term
F\equiv 0
, consider the unique mild solution
U
to (
4.1
). Comparing (
4.7
) with (
2.3
), we see that
U=\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})
. We ask for which exponents
1\leq q,r,m\leq\infty
does a priori estimate (
2.9
) hold.
If for
\lambda>0
we consider the scaled function
U_{\lambda}(X,t)=U(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}t)
, then
U_{\lambda}
also solves (
4.1
), with initial datum
u_{0,\lambda}(X)=u_{0}(\lambda X)
. Inserting
U_{\lambda}
and
u_{0,\lambda}
in (
2.9
), and using (
3.20
), we reach the conclusion that

\lambda^{-(\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}+\frac{a+1}{m})}\ ||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})||_{L^{m}_{a,z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C\lambda^{-\frac{d+a+1}{2}}||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

From the arbitrariness of
\lambda>0
, we see that a necessary condition for (
2.9
) to hold for any
a>-1
is that the triple
(q,r,m)
satisfy the equation (
2.7
) in Definition
2.1
. However, as we will see, (
2.7
) is sufficient only in the range
a\geq 0
. As we have mentioned already, the propagator does not obey scalings in the anomalous range
-1<a<0
.

Before closing we note that, when
a=0
and
d=1
condition (
2.7
) reads

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{1}{r}+\frac{1}{m}=1.

This is the admissibility hypothesis (1.2) for the triple
(q,r,m)
in the paper
[
49
]
.

5.
Cauchy problem with nonzero Neumann condition

One difficulty with the problem (
1.1
) is to have a good representation formula of Duhamel type for the solution
of the linear problem with nonzero Neumann condition:

(5.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}U-i\left\{\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U+\Delta_{x}U\right\}=F(X,t),\ \ \ (X,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times(0,\infty),\\
\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)=\Phi(x,t),\ \ \ \ U(X,0)=u_{0}(X).\end{cases}

In this section we solve this question by carefully exploiting the non-admissible solution
u(z)=z^{1-a}
of the Bessel operator (we mean by this that
u
fails to satisfy (
3.5
)). Our main result is the following.

Theorem 5.1
.

Given
-1<a<1
, let
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
,
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
,
\Phi=\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. There exists a unique mild solution to the problem (
5.1
). Such function is represented by the formula

(5.2)

\displaystyle U(X,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau,

where
S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)
is as in (
3.9
).

Proof.

In a nutshell, the main idea is to reduce the Cauchy problem (
5.1
) to one with homogeneous Neumann condition such as (
4.1
). The implementation of this plan will entail some delicate steps.
Suppose that
U
solves (
5.1
). For
R>0
we fix a function
h_{R}\in C^{\infty}[0,\infty)
such that:

(5.3)

\begin{cases}h_{R}\equiv 1,\ \ \text{for}\ 0\leq z\leq R,\ \ \text{and}\ \ h_{R}\equiv 0\ \ \text{for}\ z\geq 2R,\\
|h_{R}^{(k)}(z)|\leq C(k)R^{-k},\ \ \ \forall k\in\mathbb{N}.\end{cases}

We introduce the auxiliary function

(5.4)

U_{R}(X,t):=U(X,t)-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t).

A computation shows that

\displaystyle\partial_{t}U_{R}-i\left\{\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U_{R}+\Delta_{x}U_{R}\right\}

\displaystyle=\partial_{t}U-i\left\{\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U+\Delta_{x}U\right\}-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\left[\partial_{t}\Phi-i\Delta_{x}\Phi\right]

\displaystyle-i\Phi\left[\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}h_{R}(z)+2z^{-a}h_{R}^{\prime}(z)\right],

and we have moreover

(5.5)

\begin{cases}\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U_{R}(X,t)=\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}U(X,t)-\Phi(x,t)=0,\\
\\
U_{R}(X,0)=U(X,0)-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,0)=u_{0}(X)-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,0).\end{cases}

From (
5.4
)-(
5.5
) it ensues that if
U
solves the problem (
5.1
),
then for any
R>0
the function
U_{R}
solves (
4.1
), with forcing term given by

(5.6)

F(X,t)-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\left[\partial_{t}\Phi-i\Delta_{x}\Phi\right](x,t)-ik_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t),

where we have let

k_{R}(z):=\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}h_{R}(z)+2z^{-a}h_{R}^{\prime}(z).

Note that by the support property (
5.3
) of
h_{R}
and the decay of its derivatives, we know that

(5.7)

\operatorname{supp}\ k_{R}\subset[R,2R]\ \ \ \ \ \text{and}\ \ \ \ \ \underset{z\geq 0}{\sup}\ |k_{R}(z)|\leq CR^{-(a+1)}.

From (
4.7
), (
5.6
) we thus obtain

(5.8)

\displaystyle U(X,t)-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t)=U_{R}(X,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,0)d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left[\partial_{\tau}\Phi-i\Delta_{y}\Phi\right](y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau.

Rearranging terms in (
5.8
), we obtain the following representation for
U
:

(5.9)

\displaystyle U(X,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,0)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t)

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left[\partial_{\tau}\Phi-i\Delta_{y}\Phi\right](y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

Our next step is to carefully remove the derivatives from
\Phi
in the last integral in the right-hand side of (
5.9
):

(5.10)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left[\partial_{\tau}\Phi-i\Delta_{y}\Phi\right](y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left\{\int_{0}^{t-\varepsilon}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\partial_{\tau}\Phi(y,\tau)d\tau\right\}d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Delta_{y}\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,\varepsilon)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,t-\varepsilon)d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,0)d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle-\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left\{\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\tau}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)d\tau\right\}d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{y}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau,

where the integration by parts

, along with its derivatives.
Using the fact that

), we have

(5.11)

\displaystyle-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,\varepsilon)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,t-\varepsilon)d\omega_{a}(Y)=-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t).

Moreover,

\displaystyle-\partial_{\tau}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)

\displaystyle=-i(\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}+\Delta_{y})\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)

\displaystyle=-iS(x,y,t-\tau)\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)-iS_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\Delta_{y}S(x,y,t-\tau).

Using this identity, we can convert the last two integrals in (

) in the following way

(5.12)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{t}\partial_{\tau}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)d\tau d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle+i\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Delta_{y}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\int_{0}^{t}S(x,y,t-\tau)\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)d\tau d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle=-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau.

Substituting (
5.11
), (
5.12
) in (
5.10
), we obtain

(5.13)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\left[\partial_{\tau}\Phi-i\Delta_{y}\Phi\right](y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle=-\frac{z^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(z)\Phi(x,t)+\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,0)d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau.

If we now replace (

) in the identity (

),
we finally obtain for every

\displaystyle U(X,t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)u_{0}(Y)d\omega_{a}(Y)+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)F(Y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{\zeta}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t-\tau)\zeta^{a}d\zeta\right)dyd\tau

\displaystyle-i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau.

We now make two crucial claims.

Claim 1:
For every
X\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
and

we have

(5.15)

\underset{R\to\infty}{\lim}\ \int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau=0.

Proof of Claim 1
:
By a standard change of variable, we write

(5.16)

\displaystyle I_{R}

\displaystyle:=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t-\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\Phi(y,\tau)d\omega_{a}(Y)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,\tau)\Phi(y,t-\tau)\Bigg(\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,\zeta,\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta\Bigg)dyd\tau.

Since
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, the function
\tau\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,\tau)\Phi(y,t-\tau)dy
is in
L^{1}(0,t)
. To establish (
5.15
) it thus suffices to prove that

(5.17)

\underset{\tau\in[0,t]}{\sup}\ L_{R}(\tau):=\underset{\tau\in[0,t]}{\sup}\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,\zeta,\tau)k_{R}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

If for fixed
z>0
, we set

(5.18)

A_{\tau}(\zeta):=\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}}{4\tau}}}{(2i\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}J_{\frac{a-1}{2}}(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}),

then from (

) and the support property of

, we can write

(5.19)

L_{R}(\tau)=\int_{R}^{2R}F_{\tau}(\zeta)e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta,

where we have let

(5.20)

F_{\tau}(\zeta)=A_{\tau}(\zeta)\,k_{R}(\zeta)\,\zeta^{a}.

By the asymptotic of

for large
\zeta
in
[
23
]
, we know that there exists a constant
C(a,z)>0
such that when
\zeta>>1
we have

(5.21)

|A_{\tau}(\zeta)|\leq C(a,z)\tau^{-1/2}\zeta^{-\frac{a}{2}}.

From (
5.7
), (
5.20
) and (
5.21
), we infer for
\zeta\in[R,2R]

(5.22)

|F_{\tau}(\zeta)|\leq C(a,z)\tau^{-1/2}R^{-\frac{a}{2}}R^{-(a+1)}R^{a}\cong C(a,z)\tau^{-1/2}R^{-(\frac{a}{2}+1)}.

Substituting this information in (
5.19
), we conclude

|L_{R}|\leq C(a,z)\tau^{-1/2}R^{-\frac{a}{2}},

but this estimate does not provide decay in the regime
-1<a\leq 0
.
To achieve a better decay of
L_{R}
, we integrate by parts in the oscillatory integral (
5.19
).
We thus have

\displaystyle L_{R}(\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{1}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}F_{\tau}(\zeta)\frac{d}{d\zeta}e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta=\left[F_{\tau}(\zeta)\frac{e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\right]_{\zeta=R}^{\zeta=2R}-\int_{R}^{2R}\left[\frac{F^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}-\frac{F_{\tau}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta^{2}}{2\tau}}\right]e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta.

Using (
5.7
) and (
5.21
), we obtain for large
R>>1

\left|\left[F_{\tau}(\zeta)\frac{e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\right]_{\zeta=R}^{\zeta=2R}\right|\leq C\tau^{1/2}R^{-\frac{a}{2}}R^{-(a+2)}R^{a}\cong R^{-(\frac{a}{2}+2)}\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

We next estimate

\displaystyle\left|\int_{R}^{2R}F^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)\frac{e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}d\zeta\right|\leq\frac{C\tau}{R}\int_{R}^{2R}|F^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)|d\zeta.

To estimate
|F^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)|
we set
\nu=\frac{a-1}{2}\in(-1,0)
, and let

. Then we can write

A_{\tau}(\zeta)=\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}}{4\tau}}}{(2i\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}G_{\nu}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right).

Since by
[
40

G^{\prime}_{\nu}(z)=-z^{-\nu}J_{\nu+1}(z),

we infer that, for
\zeta>>1

(5.23)

\displaystyle A^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)

\displaystyle=-\frac{e^{i\frac{z^{2}}{4\tau}}}{(2i\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\frac{z}{2\tau}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}J_{\frac{a+1}{2}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right)

\displaystyle\cong{(2\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\frac{z}{2\tau}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2\tau}\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\cong C(a,z)\tau^{a-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{-\frac{a}{2}}.

From (
5.7
), (
5.20
), (
5.21
) and (
5.23
), we now have for any
-1<a<1
and

\displaystyle|F^{\prime}_{\tau}(\zeta)|

\displaystyle\leq C(z,a)\left[R^{-\frac{a}{2}}R^{-(a+1)}R^{a-1}+R^{-\frac{a}{2}}R^{-(a+2)}R^{a}+R^{-\frac{a}{2}}R^{-(a+1)}R^{a}\right]

\displaystyle\cong R^{-(1+\frac{a}{2})}\ \longrightarrow\ 0.

Finally, we obtain from (

)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{R}^{2R}F_{\tau}(\zeta)\frac{e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}}{\frac{i\zeta^{2}}{2\tau}}d\zeta\right|\leq\frac{C\tau}{R^{2}}\int_{R}^{2R}|F_{\tau}(\zeta)|d\zeta\cong C(a,z)R^{-(\frac{a}{2}+2)}\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

These estimates prove (
5.17
), thus completing the proof of (
5.15
). This establishes Claim 1.

Claim 2:
For every
z>0
and
t>0
we have

(5.24)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta=-S_{a}(z,0,t).

Proof of Claim 2
:
Writing the Bessel operator in divergence form

\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}f=\zeta^{-a}\partial_{\zeta}\big(\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}f\big),

and by the support property of

, integrating by parts we have

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta=\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\varepsilon}^{2R}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\partial_{\zeta}\big(\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)d\zeta

\displaystyle=-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\varepsilon}^{2R}\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}\left(\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\right)\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)d\zeta,

where we have used
h_{R}(2R)=0
. We now observe that

\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\left[\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\right]_{\zeta=\varepsilon}=0.

This follows from the fact that
1-a>0
and from the zero Neumann condition (
3.7
) satisfied by

,

\underset{\zeta\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\zeta^{a}\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)=0.

We thus find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta=-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\varepsilon}^{2R}\left(h_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\partial_{\zeta}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)d\zeta

\displaystyle=-\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\left[\left(h_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\right]_{\zeta=\varepsilon}+\underset{\varepsilon\to 0^{+}}{\lim}\int_{\varepsilon}^{2R}\partial_{\zeta}\left(h_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)d\zeta.

Since for every fixed

we have
h_{R}(\zeta)\to 1
as

, we obtain the critical information that

In conclusion, we have proved that, for every

we have

(5.25)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{\zeta^{1-a}}{1-a}h_{R}(\zeta)\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta=-S_{a}(z,0,t)+H(R),

where

(5.26)

\displaystyle H(R):=\int_{R}^{2R}\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)d\zeta

Since

, and since (
3.6
) gives for

large

S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)\cong\frac{1}{t^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2t}\right)^{\frac{1-a}{2}}\left|J_{\frac{a-1}{2}}(\frac{z\zeta}{2t})\right|\cong\frac{1}{t^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\left(\frac{z\zeta}{2t}\right)^{-\frac{a}{2}}\cong t^{-\frac{1}{2}}\zeta^{-\frac{a}{2}},

we obtain from (

As for the Claim 1, this estimate provides no decay in the regime

. To remediate this aspect, we proceed as in the proof of Claim 1, and write

S_{a}(z,\zeta,t)=A_{t}(\zeta)\ e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4t}}=\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\frac{d}{d\zeta}e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}},

where

is as in (

\displaystyle H(R)

\displaystyle=\int_{R}^{2R}\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\frac{d}{d\zeta}e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta

\displaystyle=\left[\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}\right]_{\zeta=R}^{\zeta=2R}

\displaystyle-\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{d}{d\zeta}\left[\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\right]e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta

\left|\left[\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}\right]_{\zeta=R}^{\zeta=2R}\right|\leq\frac{C(a)}{R^{2+\frac{a}{2}}}\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

In a similar fashion, we infer that

\left|\int_{R}^{2R}\frac{d}{d\zeta}\left[\left(\frac{2-a}{1-a}h^{\prime}_{R}(\zeta)+\frac{\zeta h^{\prime\prime}_{R}(\zeta)}{1-a}\right)\frac{A_{t}(\zeta)}{\frac{i\zeta}{2\tau}}\right]e^{i\frac{\zeta^{2}}{4\tau}}d\zeta\right|\leq\frac{C(a)}{R^{2+\frac{a}{2}}}\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

The latter two estimates prove that

H(R)\ \underset{R\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

In view of (
5.25

With Claims 1 & 2 in hands, we now return to the identity (

∎

6.1.
The generalized Ginibre-Velo operators

For any
a>-1
we consider the operator
\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}
in (

Since according to Definition

Theorem 6.1
.

For any
a>-1
we have
\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}:L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})\to L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, and moreover

||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}u_{0}||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}=||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

We also have for any

||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}\leq||F||_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

Proof.

The former statement about
\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}
is just a reformulation of the conservation of mass in Proposition

and there is nothing to prove. The statement about

is a direct consequence of the former and of the definition (

\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(X)d\tau.

This gives for every

\displaystyle||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}\leq\int_{0}^{t}||\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}||F(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}d\tau\leq||F||_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

∎

Next, we represent the operator
\mathbb{T}_{a}:L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})\to L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
, whose adjoint is
\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For every function
F\in\mathscr{S}^{+}
, we have

(6.1)

\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(-t)(F(\cdot,t))(X)dt.

Proof.

Denote by

the inner product in

. For every
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
we obtain from (

) and (

)

\displaystyle\langle\mathbb{T}_{a}(F),u_{0}\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\langle F,\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}(u_{0})\rangle\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}F(X,t)\overline{\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}(u_{0})(X,t)}d\omega_{a}(X)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}F(X,t)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\overline{\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y,t)}\ \overline{u_{0}(Y)}d\omega_{a}(Y)d\omega_{a}(X)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}F(X,t)\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(Y,X,-t)\ \overline{u_{0}(Y)}d\omega_{a}(Y)d\omega_{a}(X)dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\overline{u_{0}(Y)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(Y,X,-t)F(X,t)d\omega_{a}(X)dt\ d\omega_{a}(Y)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\overline{u_{0}(Y)}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(-t)(F(\cdot,t))(Y)dt\ d\omega_{a}(Y)

This proves (
6.1
).

∎

We next have the following result which establishes the part of Theorem
2.3
relative to the bulk. We emphasize that, in view of the discussion of

a\geq 0
and (

) in the introduction, the hypothesis

in the statement of Theorem

Theorem 6.3
.

Let

. Suppose that the triple

be admissible and that

. For any

(6.2)

\displaystyle||U||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right].

Proof.

According to Theorem
5.1
the solution

is represented by

(6.3)

U(X,t)=\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})(X,t)+\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t),

see also (

6.2
) will follow from the following a priori inequalities:

(6.4)

||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\ ||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})},

and

(6.5)

||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\ ||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

To prove (
6.4
), we fix
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
. For any
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
, the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives

(6.6)

\displaystyle\langle\langle\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0}),F\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle u_{0},\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)\rangle\leq||u_{0}||_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}||\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}.

Next, Hölder inequality gives

(6.7)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||^{2}_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{T}_{a}(F),\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)\rangle=\langle\langle\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F),F\rangle\rangle\leq||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

We now use (
2.3
), (
6.1
) to write

(6.8)

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(X,t)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(X)d\tau.

This gives for every
z>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(6.9)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r}_{x}}d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}||S(t-\tau)(S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r}_{x}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(d,r)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau,

where in the last inequality we have used Proposition
3.5
.
Keeping (
2.3
) and (
3.12
) in mind, we have for any
z>0

\displaystyle||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq\frac{C(a)}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\ \zeta^{a}d\zeta.

Substituting this estimate in (
6.9
), we find for every
z>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(6.10)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau\zeta^{a}d\zeta

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)\ \int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\beta}(h(\zeta,\cdot))(t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta,

where we have set

(6.11)

h(\zeta,\tau)=||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

and for a function
f
on
\mathbb{R}
we have denoted by

(6.12)

I_{\beta}(f)=f\star|\cdot|^{-(1-\beta)},\ \ \ \ \ \ 0<\beta<1,

the M. Riesz operator of fractional integration on
\mathbb{R}
, in which we are letting

(6.13)

1-\beta=\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})=\frac{d+a+1}{r}-\frac{d}{r}.

By the Hardy-Littlewood-Sobolev theorem (see
[
53
]
) we know that, if
0<\beta<1
, and
q
is such that
1<q^{\prime}<\beta^{-1}
, then we have

(6.14)

I_{\beta}:L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}\ \longrightarrow\ L^{q}_{t},\ \ \ \ \text{provided that}\ \ \ \ \frac{1}{q^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{q}=1-\frac{2}{q}=\beta.

Notice that, since
a\geq 0
and
(q,r,\infty)
is admissible, by (
2.10
) we have

1-\beta=\frac{2}{q}=\frac{d+a+1}{2}-\frac{d}{r},

which is exactly
(
6.13
). Since we are assuming
2<q<\infty
, we have
0<\beta<1
, which is equivalent to

0<1-\frac{d+a+1}{2}+\frac{d}{r}<1\ \Longleftrightarrow\ \frac{2d}{d+a+1}<r<\frac{2d}{d+a-1}.

Note that, since
r\geq 2
, it is automatic that
\frac{2d}{d+a+1}<r
.

From all this and from (
6.10
) we conclude that

(6.15)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}||I_{\beta}(h(\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{t}}\zeta^{a}d\zeta

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}||h(\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}}\zeta^{a}d\zeta=C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\zeta^{a}d\zeta

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Taking the supremum in
z>0
we obtain from (
6.15
) the following crucial estimate:

(6.16)

||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Substitution of (
6.15
) in (
6.7
) gives for any
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
,

(6.17)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\ ||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Inserting (
6.17
) into (
6.6
), and taking the supremum on all
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
, completes the proof of (
6.4
).

To establish (
6.5
), we use (
2.4
) which gives for
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
:

(6.18)

\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t)=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(X)d\tau.

We next want to show that for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and
z>0
we have

(6.19)

||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}I_{\beta}(h(\zeta,\cdot))(t)\zeta^{a}d\zeta,

with
h(\zeta,\tau)
as in (
6.11
). Once (
6.19
) is proved, we proceed as in the proof of (
6.15
), (
6.16
), and obtain (
6.5
). To prove (
6.19
), we use (
4.2
) and Proposition
3.5
to find

(6.20)

||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{t}\frac{||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau.

Next, we apply (
3.12
) to find for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z>0

\displaystyle|S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(x,z)|\leq||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(x,\cdot)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}}\leq\frac{C(a)}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(x,\zeta,\tau)|\zeta^{a}d\zeta.

Applying Minkowski’s integral inequality, we obtain from this estimate

\displaystyle||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq\frac{C(a)}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\zeta^{a}d\zeta

Inserting this estimate in (
6.20
), we find

\displaystyle||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}\zeta^{a}d\zeta d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau\zeta^{a}d\zeta,

which establishes (
6.19
). This completes the proof.

∎

We next discuss the regime
-1<a<0
. We have the following.

Theorem 6.4
.

Let
-1<a<0
and suppose that the exponents
q,r
and
q_{\infty}
satisfy the conditions

(6.21)

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d+1}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \frac{2}{q_{\infty}}+\frac{d}{r}\leq\frac{d+a+1}{2}.

Moreover, we assume that
q>2
and that
q_{\infty}<\infty
. For any
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
the unique mild solution to the problem (
4.1
) satisfies the following a priori estimate of Strichartz type

(6.22)

\displaystyle||Uk||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})\left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right].

Proof.

We proceed as in the beginning of the proof of Theorem
6.3
. In view of (
6.3
), the estimate (
6.22
) will follow from the two inequalities

(6.23)

||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})\ ||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})},

and

(6.24)

||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})\ ||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

for any
u_{0}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
and
F\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
.

To establish (
6.23
), we use duality to estimate

(6.25)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||^{2}_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbb{T}_{a}(F),\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)\rangle=\langle\langle\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)k,Fk^{-1}\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\leq||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

where
k(z)
is the weight in Definition
3.3
. Using (
6.8
), we now obtain for any
X=(x,z)
and
t\in\mathbb{R}

\displaystyle\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(X,t)k(z)=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(X)k(z)d\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}S(t-\tau)(S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)k(z))(x)d\tau,

where in the second equality we have used (
4.9
). As in (
6.9
), this gives for every
z>0
and
t\in\mathbb{R}

(6.26)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)k(z)||_{L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,r)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)k(z))||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|t-\tau|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau,

where as before we have used Proposition
3.5
.
By (
3.13
) in Proposition
3.4
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
z>0
we find

\displaystyle|S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(x,z)k(z)|\leq||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\cdot,\tau))(x,\cdot)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}}

\displaystyle\leq C(a)\left\{|t-\tau|^{-\frac{a+1}{2}}+|t-\tau|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}\int_{0}^{\infty}|F(x,\zeta,\tau)|k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta.

Minkowski’s integral inequality gives

\displaystyle||S_{a}(t-\tau)(F(\cdot,\tau))(\cdot,z)k(z))||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq C(a)\left\{(|t-\tau|^{-\frac{a+1}{2}}+|t-\tau|^{-\frac{1}{2}})\right\}\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta.

Inserting this estimate in (
6.26
), gives

(6.27)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,t)k(z)||_{L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||F(\cdot,\zeta,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\tau}{|t-\tau|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}\left\{(|t-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}+|t-\tau|^{\frac{1}{2}})\right\}}k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)\ \int_{0}^{\infty}(K\star h(\zeta,\cdot))(t)k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta,

where
h(\zeta,\tau)
is as in (
6.11
), and we have set

(6.28)

K(t):=\left\{|t|^{-\frac{a+1}{2}}+|t|^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right\}|t|^{-d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}.

We now apply Lemma
3.6
with

\gamma_{1}=\frac{1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})=\frac{d+1}{2}-\frac{d}{r}=\frac{2}{q},\ \ \ \ \ \ \gamma_{\infty}=\frac{d+a+1}{2}-\frac{d}{r},

where the last equality in the expression of
\gamma_{1}
is justified by the first equation in (
6.21
).
To apply Lemma
3.6
we must know that
0<\gamma_{1},\gamma_{\infty}<1
.
Note that, since
-1<a<0
, we have
0<\frac{a+1}{2}<\frac{1}{2}
, and so
\gamma_{\infty}<\gamma_{1}
. Since
\gamma_{1}=\frac{2}{q}
, the hypothesis
q>2
implies
\gamma_{1}<1
, and therefore also
\gamma_{\infty}<1
. On the other hand, in view of the second inequality in (
6.21
), the hypothesis
q_{\infty}<\infty
implies
\gamma_{\infty}\geq\frac{2}{q_{\infty}}>0
, and therefore we also have
\gamma_{1}>0
. From

and (

), we have:

K(t)\leq C\begin{cases}|t|^{-\gamma_{1}},\ \ \ \ |t|\leq 1,\\
|t|^{-\gamma_{\infty}},\ \ \ \ |t|\geq 1.\end{cases}

By Lemma
3.6
we infer that there exists a constant
C=C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})>0
such that

(6.29)

||K\star h(\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}}\ \leq C\ ||h||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}}=C\ ||F(\cdot,\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

where in the last equality we have used (
6.11
). Inserting (
6.29
) into (
6.26
), we find for any
z>0

||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)(\cdot,z,\cdot)k(z)||_{L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta.

By taking the supremum in
z>0
, we finally obtain the following basic estimate

(6.30)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}^{\star}\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})\int_{0}^{\infty}||F(\cdot,\zeta,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}k^{-1}(\zeta)\zeta^{a}d\zeta

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Returning to (
6.25
), and using (
6.30
), we finally obtain

(6.31)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}_{a}(F)||_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}L^{1}_{a,z}}.

From (
6.6
) and (
6.31
) we find

(6.32)

\displaystyle\langle\langle\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0}),F\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\leq\ C(d,a,r,q_{\infty})||u_{0}||_{L_{a}^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}L^{1}_{a,z}}.

If in (
6.32
) we now take the supremum on all measurable
F
such
||Fk^{-1}||_{L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}L^{1}_{a,z}}\leq 1
, we finally obtain (

).

The proof of (
6.24
) can be achieved in a similar manner, by combining the arguments in the proof of (

) with those in that of (

). We leave the relevant details to the interested reader.

7.
Boundary Strichartz estimates

In this section we assume that
-1<a<1
and establish Strichartz estimates for the following problem

(7.1)

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}V-i\left\{\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}V+\Delta_{x}V\right\}=0,\ \ \ (X,t)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times(0,\infty)\\
\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}\partial_{z}V(X,t)=\Phi(x,t),\ \ \ \ V(X,0)=0,\end{cases}

which corresponds to taking
u_{0}(X)\equiv 0
,
F(X,t)\equiv 0
in problem (
5.1
). According to Theorem
5.1
, see also (
2.5
),
the mild solution of (
7.1
) is given by the integral operator

(7.2)

\displaystyle V(X,t)

\displaystyle=\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(X,t):=i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x,y,t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau,

where
S_{a}(z,0,t)
is as in (
3.9
).
One notable aspect of the results in this section is that, because of the special form (
3.9
), (
7.2
) of the boundary propagator, we will not be forced to distinguish between the regimes
1>a\geq 0
and
-1<a<0
, as we did for the Strichartz estimates in the bulk in Section
6
.
It is useful to obtain a representation of the operator
\Theta_{a}
of which
\Theta^{\star}_{a}
is the adjoint.

Proposition 7.1
.

For every
V\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
we have

(7.3)

\displaystyle\Theta_{a}(V)(y,\tau)

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,\tau-\sigma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(y,x,\tau-\sigma)V(x,z,\sigma)dxd\sigma z^{a}dz

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(Y_{0},X,\tau-\sigma)V(X,\sigma)d\omega_{a}(X)d\sigma

\displaystyle=\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\mathbb{S}_{a}(\tau-\sigma)(V(\cdot,\sigma))(Y_{0})d\sigma.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\langle\Theta_{a}(V),\Phi\rangle=\langle\langle V,\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)\rangle\rangle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}V(X^{\prime},t)\overline{\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(X^{\prime},t)}d\omega_{a}(X^{\prime})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}V(X^{\prime},t)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)}\ \overline{S(x^{\prime},y,t-\tau)}\ \overline{\Phi(y,\tau)}dyd\tau d\omega_{a}(X^{\prime})dt

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\Phi(y,\tau)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,\tau-t)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(y,x^{\prime},\tau-t)V(x^{\prime},z,t)dx^{\prime}dtz^{a}dzdyd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\overline{\Phi(y,\tau)}\left(\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,\tau-\sigma)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(y,x^{\prime},\tau-\sigma)V(x^{\prime},z,\sigma)dx^{\prime}d\sigma z^{a}dz\right)dyd\tau.

This identity proves (
7.3
).

∎

Before proving the next result, we recall two classical integrals, see, e.g.,
[
27
, formulas 3.761.4 & 3.761.9, p.420-1]
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
b>0,0<|\Re\mu|<1
. Then

(7.4)

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\mu-1}\sin(by)dy=\frac{\Gamma(\mu)}{b^{\mu}}\sin\frac{\mu\pi}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ \int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\mu-1}\cos(by)dy=\frac{\Gamma(\mu)}{b^{\mu}}\cos\frac{\mu\pi}{2}.

Therefore,

(7.5)

\int_{0}^{\infty}y^{\mu-1}e^{\pm iby}dy=\frac{\Gamma(\mu)}{b^{\mu}}e^{\pm i\frac{\mu\pi}{2}}.

With the aid of Lemma
7.2
, we obtain the following auxiliary result.

Lemma 7.3
.

For every
-1<a<1
we have

(7.6)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)\overline{S_{a}(z,0,t-\sigma)}z^{a}dz=\frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)}\,\frac{\exp\!\left(i\,\frac{(a+1)\pi}{4}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\tau-\sigma)\right)}{|\tau-\sigma|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}.

Proof.

Using (
3.9
), we find

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)\overline{S_{a}(z,0,t-\sigma)}z^{a}dz

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-2a}}{\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})^{2}(t-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iz^{2}\left[\frac{1}{4(t-\tau)}-\frac{1}{4(t-\sigma)}\right]}z^{a+1}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{-2a}}{\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})^{2}(t-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iz^{2}\left[\frac{\tau-\sigma}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}\right]}z^{a+1}\frac{dz}{z}.

Suppose
\tau-\sigma>0
. Then the change of variable
\zeta=z^{2}\frac{\tau-\sigma}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}
, gives

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iz^{2}\left[\frac{\tau-\sigma}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}\right]}z^{a+1}\frac{dz}{z}=\frac{2^{a+1}(t-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}{(\tau-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{i\zeta}\zeta^{\frac{a+1}{2}-1}d\zeta

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{a+1}(t-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}{(\tau-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})e^{i\frac{(a+1)\pi}{4}},

where in the last equality we have applied (
7.5
) in Lemma
7.2
with
\mu=\frac{a+1}{2}
. Notice that, since we are assuming
-1<a<1
, we have
0<\mu<1
. If instead
\tau-\sigma>0
, then we make the change of variable
\zeta=z^{2}\frac{\sigma-\tau}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}
, obtaining

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{iz^{2}\left[\frac{\tau-\sigma}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}\right]}z^{a+1}\frac{dz}{z}=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-iz^{2}\left[\frac{\sigma-\tau}{4(t-\tau)(t-\sigma)}\right]}z^{a+1}\frac{dz}{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{a+1}(t-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}(t-\sigma)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}{(\sigma-\tau)^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\Gamma(\frac{a+1}{2})e^{-i\frac{(a+1)\pi}{4}},

again by (
7.5
).
Combining these results, we obtain (
7.6
).

∎

We next establish the main result of this section. Before stating it, we stress that the assumption (
7.7
) coincides with the admissibility condition (
2.10
) only when
a\geq 0
.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
-1<a<1
and suppose that
q,r

satisfy

(7.7)

\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}=\frac{d+a+1}{2},

with
r\geq 2
and

q>2
. Then there exists
C(d,a,r)>0
such that for any
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
one has

(7.8)

||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}\leq C(d,a,r)\ ||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Furthermore, we also have

(7.9)

||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)\ ||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Proof.

For the proof of (
7.8
) we proceed as follows:

(7.10)

\displaystyle||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(X,t)\overline{\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(X,t)}d\omega_{a}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)\overline{S_{a}(z,0,t-\sigma)}z^{a}dz\right)S(t-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))(x)\overline{S(t-\sigma)(\Phi(\cdot,\sigma))(x)}d\tau d\sigma dx

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)\overline{S_{a}(z,0,t-\sigma)}z^{a}dz\right)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(t-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))(x)\overline{S(t-\sigma)(\Phi(\cdot,\sigma))(x)}dxd\tau d\sigma

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{1-a}}{\Gamma\!\left(\frac{a+1}{2}\right)}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\exp\!\left(i\,\frac{(a+1)\pi}{4}\,\operatorname{sgn}(\tau-\sigma)\right)}{|\tau-\sigma|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(\sigma-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))(x)\overline{\Phi(x,\sigma)}dxd\tau d\sigma,

where in the last equality we have used (
7.6
) in Lemma
7.3
along with the group property of the Schrödinger propagator
S(t)
. Assume now that
q,r
satisfy (
7.7
) with
r\geq 2
. Since
a>-1
, we have

(7.11)

\frac{2}{q}=\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})>0.

Hölder inequality gives

(7.12)

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(\sigma-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))(x)\overline{\Phi(x,\sigma)}dx\right|\leq||S(\sigma-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))||_{L^{r}_{x}}||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Using (
7.12
), we finally obtain from (
7.10
)

(7.13)

\displaystyle||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}^{2}\leq C(a)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{|\tau-\sigma|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}||S(\sigma-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))||_{L^{r}_{x}}||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\tau d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C(a,d)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{1}{|\tau-\sigma|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\frac{1}{|\tau-\sigma|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\tau d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq C(a,d)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|\tau-\sigma|^{\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\tau\right)||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\sigma,

where in the second to the last inequality we have used Proposition
3.5
.
If we let

h(\tau)=||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

and consider the fractional integration operator (
6.12
),
then from (
7.13
) we have

(7.14)

\displaystyle||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,d)\int_{0}^{t}I_{\beta}(h)(\sigma)||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\sigma,

where we have let

(7.15)

1-\beta=\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})=\frac{d+a+1}{2}-\frac{d}{r}=\frac{2}{q},

where in the last equality we have used (
7.7
). Since we are assuming
q>2
, from (
7.11
) and (
7.15
) we obtain
0<\beta<1
.
Exactly as in (
6.14
) we thus conclude that

(7.16)

\|I_{\beta}(h)||_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C(\beta,q)||h||_{L^{q^{\prime}}(\mathbb{R})}=C(d,a,r)||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Finally, applying Hölder inequality to (
7.14
), we obtain

(7.17)

\displaystyle||\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(a,d)\int_{\mathbb{R}}I_{\beta}(h)(\sigma)||\Phi(\cdot,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\sigma

\displaystyle\leq||I_{\beta}(h)||_{L^{q}_{t}}||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)||\Phi||^{2}_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

where in the last inequality we have used (
7.16
). This establishes (
7.8
).

To complete the proof of the theorem we are left with proving (
7.9
). We use one more time the duality argument. Let
V\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
, then using (
7.3
) in Proposition
7.1
, we find

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi),V\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle=\langle\Phi,\Theta_{a}(V)\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(y,\tau)\overline{\Theta_{a}(V)(y,\tau)}dyd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(y,\tau)\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\overline{\mathbb{S}_{a}(\tau-\sigma)(V(\cdot,\sigma))(Y_{0})}d\sigma dyd\tau

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\tau}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(y,\tau)S(\sigma-\tau)\left(S_{a}(\sigma-\tau)\left(\overline{V(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)}\right)(z)\right)(y)dyd\sigma d\tau

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}||S(\sigma-\tau)\left(S_{a}(\sigma-\tau)\left(\overline{V(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)}\right)(0)\right)||_{L^{r}_{x}}d\sigma d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(d,r)\int_{\mathbb{R}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\frac{1}{|\sigma-\tau|^{d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}||S_{a}(\sigma-\tau)\left(\overline{V(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)}\right)(0)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}d\sigma d\tau,

where in the last inequality we have used Proposition
3.5
.
Keeping in mind that

\displaystyle S_{a}(\sigma-\tau)\left(\overline{V(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)}\right)(0)=\int_{0}^{\infty}S_{a}(z,0,\sigma-\tau)\overline{V(\cdot,z,\sigma)}z^{a}dz,

from (
3.9
) and Minkowski integral inequality, we obtain the critical estimate

\displaystyle||S_{a}(\sigma-\tau)\left(\overline{V(\cdot,\cdot,\sigma)}\right)(0)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq\frac{C(a)}{|\sigma-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}}}\int_{0}^{\infty}||V(\cdot,z,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}z^{a}dz.

We emphasize that this is the second time in the proof where, because the point
Y_{0}=(y,0)\in\partial\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}
, we are not forced to distinguish between
-1<a<0
and
a\geq 0
(the first small miracle has occurred in the proof of (
7.10
)).
Substituting this estimate in the above, we infer

(7.18)

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi),V\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{||V(\cdot,z,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}}{|\sigma-\tau|^{\frac{a+1}{2}+d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}}d\sigma d\tau z^{a}dz

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}}||\Phi(\cdot,\tau)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}I_{\beta}(h(z,\cdot))(\tau)d\tau z^{a}dz,

where we have let

h(z,\sigma)=||V(\cdot,z,\sigma)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

and
\beta
is the same as in (
7.15
). Since as in the proof of (
7.8
), we find

||I_{\beta}(h(z,\cdot))||_{L^{q}_{t}}\leq C(\beta)\ ||h(z,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}}=||V(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

applying Hölder inequality to (
7.18
), we finally have

\displaystyle\langle\langle\Theta_{a}^{\star}(\Phi),V\rangle\rangle

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\int_{0}^{\infty}||I_{\beta}(h(z,\cdot))||_{L^{q}_{t}}z^{a}dz

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\int_{0}^{\infty}||V(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}z^{a}dz

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}||V||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Taking the supremum on all
V\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
, by the density of
\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}\times\mathbb{R})
in
L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}
and duality, we finally obtain (
7.9
).

∎

8.
Traces on the boundary

The boundary Strichartz estimate (
7.9
) in Theorem
7.4
establishes the uniform boundedness in the orthogonal variable
z>0
of the solution
U
to the problem (
1.2
). This result does not suffice, however, to guarantee the existence of a trace on the boundary, a property which plays a critical role in the analysis of the nonlinear problem (
1.1
).

In this section we close this gap. Our main result is the following.

Theorem 8.1

(Existence of traces)
.

Let
-1<a<1
and suppose that
q,r
satisfy (
7.7
), with
r\geq 2
and
q>2
. Given
\Phi\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}
,
the function
\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)
belongs to the space
C^{b}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
. By this we mean that for every
z_{0}\geq 0

(8.1)

\underset{z\to z_{0}}{\lim}\ ||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)(\cdot,z,\cdot)-\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)(\cdot,z_{0},\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}=0.

Proof.

For the definition of the space
C^{b}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}
see (
3.18
). We divide the proof into two steps, by first showing that the desired conclusion (
8.1
) is true when
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. To simplify the notation, we set
V_{z}(x,t):=\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)(x,z,t)
. In view of (
2.5
), given
z\geq 0
we have

\displaystyle V_{z}(x,t)

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{S}_{a}(X,Y_{0},t-\tau)\Phi(y,\tau)dyd\tau=i\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,0,t-\tau)S(t-\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,\tau))(x)d\tau

\displaystyle=i\int_{0}^{t}S_{a}(z,0,\tau)S(\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,t-\tau))(x)d\tau.

Keeping (
3.9
) in mind, we see that for
t>0
and every
z,z_{0}\geq 0
, we have

(8.2)

V_{z}(x,t)-V_{z_{0}}(x,t)=c(a)\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\frac{a+1}{2}}e^{i\frac{z_{0}^{2}}{4\tau}}[e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1]S(\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,t-\tau))(x)d\tau,

where
c(a)\in\mathbb{C}
is a number whose explicit value is irrelevant in what follows, and we have let

\omega(h)=h^{2}+2z_{0}h,\ \ \ \ \ \ h=z-z_{0}.

Without restriction, we can assume that
h\geq 0
. If we set

U_{h}(x,t):=V_{z}(x,t)-V_{z_{0}}(x,t),

it is clear that, to establish (
8.1
), we need to prove that

(8.3)

\underset{h\to 0}{\lim}\ ||U_{h}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}=0.

With this objective in mind, we define for
\tau\geq 0

g(\tau):=\|\Phi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}}.

Because
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
, we have
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
. This is easily seen as follows. Since
2\leq r\leq\infty
, we know
\frac{1}{2}\leq\frac{1}{r^{\prime}}\leq 1
. Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
such that
N>d\geq\frac{d}{r^{\prime}}
. Then

\displaystyle g(t)^{r^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{\left[(1+|X|^{2})^{\frac{N}{2}}|\Phi(x,t)|\right]^{r^{\prime}}}{(1+|X|^{2})^{\frac{r^{\prime}N}{2}}}dx\leq C(N,r)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{dx}{(1+|x|^{2})^{\frac{r^{\prime}N}{2}}}dx\leq C(d,N,r).

This shows that
g\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, and similar arguments prove that
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})
.

Next, applying Proposition
3.5
, for every
t>0
we obtain from (
8.2
):

(8.4)

\displaystyle||U_{h}(\cdot,t)||_{L_{x}^{r}}

\displaystyle\leq C(a)\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\frac{a+1}{2}}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|||S(\tau)(\Phi(\cdot,t-\tau))||_{L_{x}^{r}}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{t}\tau^{-\frac{a+1}{2}-d(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{r})}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|g(t-\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\int_{0}^{\infty}\tau^{-\frac{2}{q}}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|g(t-\tau)d\tau

\displaystyle=C(d,a,r)K_{h}\star g(t),

where we have let

K_{h}(\tau):=\tau^{-\frac{2}{q}}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|\,\mathbf{1}_{(0,\infty)}(\tau).

We now claim that:

(8.5)

\underset{h\to 0}{\lim}\ ||K_{h}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}=0.

To prove (
8.5
) we argue as follows:

\displaystyle||K_{h}||_{L^{1}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\omega(h)}\tau^{-2/q}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|d\tau+\int_{\omega(h)}^{\infty}\tau^{-2/q}|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|d\tau=I_{h}(\tau)+II_{h}(\tau).

To estimate
I_{h}(\tau)
we use the trivial bound

|e^{i\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}}-1|\leq 2
,
to obtain

I_{h}(\tau)\leq 2\int_{0}^{\omega(h)}\tau^{-2/q}d\tau=\frac{q}{q-2}\omega(h)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\ \underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ 0,

since by assumption
q>2
. To estimate
II_{h}(\tau)
, instead, we use the bound
|e^{i\theta}-1|\leq|\theta|
, valid for every
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, to estimate

II_{h}(\tau)\leq\int_{\omega(h)}^{\infty}\tau^{-2/q}\frac{\omega(h)}{4\tau}d\tau=\frac{\omega(h)}{4}\int_{\omega(h)}^{\infty}\tau^{-2/q-1}d\tau=\frac{q}{8}\omega(h)^{1-\frac{2}{q}}\ \underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

This proves (
8.5
).
Since
g\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
, Young’s inequality now gives

(8.6)

\|K_{h}\star g\|_{L_{t}^{1}}\leq\|K_{h}\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{1}},\qquad\|K_{h}\star g\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}}\leq\|K_{h}\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}.

From (
8.4
), (
8.5
) and (
8.6
), we infer

(8.7)

\|U_{h}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C\|K_{h}\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{1}}\ \underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ 0,\ \ \ \ \ \|U_{h}\|_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\leq C\|K_{h}\|_{L^{1}}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}}\ \underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

Finally, we have from (
8.7
):

\displaystyle\|U_{h}\|^{q}_{L_{t}^{q}L_{x}^{r}}=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|U_{h}(\cdot,t)\|^{q}_{L_{x}^{r}}dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}}\|U_{h}(\cdot,t)\|^{q-1}_{L_{x}^{r}}\|U_{h}(\cdot,t)\|_{L_{x}^{r}}dt\leq\|U_{h}\|^{q-1}_{L_{t}^{\infty}L_{x}^{r}}\|U_{h}\|_{L_{t}^{1}L_{x}^{r}}\ \underset{h\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ 0.

This proves (
8.3
) - and therefore (
8.1
) - when
\Phi\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
. Assume now that
\Phi\in L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}
and choose
\Phi_{n}\in\mathscr{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d+1})
such that

\Phi_{n}\to\Phi
in
L_{t}^{q^{\prime}}L_{x}^{r^{\prime}}
.
Denoting
V_{z}(x,t)=\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)(x,z,t)
and
V^{n}_{z}(x,t)=\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi_{n})(x,z,t)
, we have

(8.8)

||V_{z}-V_{z_{0}}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq||V_{z}-V^{n}_{z}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}+||V^{n}_{z}-V^{n}_{z_{0}}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}+||V^{n}_{z_{0}}-V_{z_{0}}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}.

By the boundary Strichartz estimate (
7.9
) there exists a constant
C(d,a,r)>0
, such that for every
z\geq 0
, one has

\displaystyle||V_{z}-V_{z}^{n}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle=||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi-\Phi_{n})(\cdot,z,\cdot)||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C(d,a,r)||\Phi-\Phi_{n}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, we thus choose
n(\varepsilon)\in\mathbb{N}
such that

2C(d,a,r)||\Phi-\Phi_{n}||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall n\geq n(\varepsilon).

We infer that for every
z,z_{0}\geq 0

(8.9)

||V_{z}-V_{z}^{n(\varepsilon)}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}+||V^{n(\varepsilon)}_{z_{0}}-V_{z_{0}}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\ \ \ \ \ \ \forall z,z_{0}\geq 0.

Using (
8.3
), we now choose
\delta(\varepsilon)>0
such that for every
z,z_{0}\geq 0
, with
|z-z_{0}|\leq\delta(\varepsilon)
, we have

(8.10)

||V^{n(\varepsilon)}_{z}-V^{n(\varepsilon)}_{z_{0}}||_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}.

Combining (
8.8
), with (
8.9
) and (
8.10
), we reach the desired conclusion (
8.1
).

∎

9.
Proof of Theorems
2.3
and
2.4

In this brief section we combine the results of Sections
5
,
6
and
7
and finally prove Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
.
We begin with recalling that, thanks to (
5.2
) in Theorem
5.1
, the mild solution
U
to the problem (
1.2
)
is represented by (
2.2
):

(9.1)

U(X,t)=\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})(X,t)+\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(X,t)+i\Theta_{a}(\Phi)(X,t).

Proof of Theorem
2.3
.

For every
t\in\mathbb{R}
we obtain from (
10.5
)

\displaystyle||U(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\Theta_{a}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F||_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right]

where in the last inequality we have used Theorem
6.1
and (
7.8
) in Theorem
7.4
. By taking the supremum in
t\in\mathbb{R}
, we obtain (
2.15
).

The estimate (
2.16
), instead, follows by combining (
10.5
) with (
6.4
), (
6.5
) and with (
7.9
) in Theorem
7.4
.

∎

We next turn to the

Proof of Theorem
2.4
.

To establish (
2.18
) we use again Theorem
6.1
, combined with (
7.8
) in Theorem
7.4
which we apply to the triple
(q_{\infty},r,\infty)
(recall that this triple satisfies the first equation in (
2.17
)):

\displaystyle||U(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\Theta_{a}(\Phi)(\cdot,t)||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)\left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F||_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right].

Passing to the supremum in

, we obtain (
2.18
).

To prove (
2.19
), we combine (
10.5
) with (

) and (
6.24
), to obtain

(9.2)

\displaystyle||U\ k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C\ \left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F\ k^{-1}||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}\cap q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right]+||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}.

To estimate the third term in the right-hand side of (

) we use the trivial observation that
0<k(z)\leq 1
, and that moreover we have:

L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}.

This gives

(9.3)

\displaystyle||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)k||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q+q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(\Phi)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}

\displaystyle\leq C(d,a,r)||\Phi||_{L^{q^{\prime}_{\infty}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}},

where in the last inequality we have used (

) in Theorem

applied to the triple
(q_{\infty},r,\infty)
. Substituting (

10.
Well-posedness

In this section we finally turn to the well-posedness in the nonlinear Cauchy problem (
1.1
). We first introduce the notion of
L^{2}_{a}
-mass critical problem. Suppose
U
solves (
1.1
) with
F=0
and, for every
\lambda>0
, consider the function

U_{\lambda}(X,t)=\lambda^{-\gamma}U(\lambda^{-1}X,\lambda^{-2}t),\ \ \ \ \gamma>0.

Clearly, for every
\lambda>0
,
U_{\lambda}
is a solution of
\partial_{t}U_{\lambda}-i(\Delta_{x}+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)})U_{\lambda}=0
. We have

\partial_{z}U_{\lambda}(X,t)=\lambda^{-(1+\gamma)}\partial_{z}U(\lambda^{-1}X,\lambda^{-2}t),

and therefore

\displaystyle z^{a}\partial_{z}U_{\lambda}(X,t)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a-(1+\gamma)}(\lambda^{-1}z)^{a}\partial_{z}U(\lambda^{-1}X,\lambda^{-2}t)

\displaystyle\ \underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\longrightarrow}\ \mu\lambda^{a-(1+\gamma)}|U(\lambda^{-1}x,0,\lambda^{-2}t)|^{p-1}U(\lambda^{-1}x,0,\lambda^{-2}t)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{a-1-\gamma+\gamma p}\mu|U_{\lambda}(x,0,t)|^{p-1}U_{\lambda}(x,0,t).

We infer that for
U_{\lambda}
to also be a solution to (
1.1
) with
F=0
, we must have

\gamma=\frac{1-a}{p-1}.

Now, the function

U_{\lambda}(X,t)=\lambda^{-\frac{1-a}{p-1}}U(\lambda^{-1}X,\lambda^{-2}t)\ \underset{t\to 0}{\longrightarrow}\ u_{0,\lambda}(X):=\lambda^{-\frac{1-a}{p-1}}u_{0}(\lambda^{-1}X).

Since we have from (
3.20
)

||u_{0,\lambda}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}=\lambda^{-\frac{1-a}{p-1}+\frac{d+a+1}{2}}||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})},

we see that in order to have constant mass, we must have

\frac{1-a}{p-1}=\frac{d+a+1}{2}.

Solving for
p
in this equation we reach the conclusion that

p=p_{c}:=1+\frac{2(1-a)}{d+a+1}.

Note that, since we want
p_{c}>1
, we must assume that
a<1
. This explains (
2.21
) in Definition
2.6
in the range
0\leq a<1
.

When instead
-1<a<0
, then because of the anomalous behavior of the Bessel propagator
e^{it\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}}
in (
3.13
) of Proposition
3.4
, the critical exponent
p_{c}
is no longer dictated by the scalings
(\lambda X,\lambda^{2}t)
. It turns out that, in such negative range of values of
a
, the critical dimension ceases to be
D=d+a+1
, and is instead
D=d+1
. Accordingly, the critical exponent for (
1.1
) is given by

p_{c}=1+\frac{2}{d+1},

which explains (
2.22
) in Definition
2.6
.
We will need the following.

Lemma 10.1
.

Let
a>-1
and suppose that (
1.1
) be
L^{2}_{a}
-mass critical, with
p=p_{c}
. If the triple
(q,r,\infty)
is admissible, and we let
\tilde{q}=pq^{\prime}
,
\tilde{r}=pr^{\prime}
, then also
(\tilde{q},\tilde{r},\infty)
is admissible.

Proof.

We discuss the case
a\geq 0
, the regime
-1<a<0
is dealt with in the same way. We must show that if
q,r
satisfy (
2.10
), then we also have

(10.1)

\frac{2}{pq^{\prime}}+\frac{d}{pr^{\prime}}=\frac{d+a+1}{2}.

The left-hand side of (
10.1
) equals

\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\left(\frac{2}{q}+\frac{d}{r}\right)=\frac{d+2}{p}-\frac{1}{p}\frac{d+a+1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}\frac{d+3-a}{2},

where in the second to the last equality we have used (
2.10
). Since by (
2.21
) in Definition
2.6
we have
p=\frac{d+3-a}{d+a+1}
, the desired conclusion (
10.1
) follows.

∎

Lemma 10.2

(Conservation of mass)
.

Let
U\in L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})
be a mild solution to (
1.1
) with
F=0
. Then

(10.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}|U(X,t)|^{2}d\omega_{a}(X)=-2\Im(\mu)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|U(x,0,t)|^{p}dx.

In particular, if
\Im(\mu)=0
, then the
mass
is constant in time.

Proof.

Omitting for simplicity the variable of integration, we have

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}|U(X,t)|^{2}d\omega_{a}(X)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\left[U_{t}\bar{U}+U\bar{U}_{t}\right]d\omega_{a}(X)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}\left[i\bar{U}(\Delta_{x}U+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}U)-iU(\Delta_{x}\bar{U}+\mathscr{B}_{z}^{(a)}\bar{U})\right]d\omega_{a}(X)

\displaystyle=i\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}}(-|\nabla_{x}U|^{2}+|\nabla_{x}U|^{2})d\omega_{a}(X)+i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}[\bar{U}(z^{a}U_{z})_{z}-U(z^{a}\bar{U}_{z})_{z}]dzdx

\displaystyle=i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}[-\bar{U}_{z}(z^{a}U_{z})+U_{z}(z^{a}\bar{U}_{z})]dzdx+i\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\mu-\bar{\mu})|U(x,0,t)|^{p}dx,

where we have used the fact that for
t\not=0

\underset{z\to 0^{+}}{\lim}z^{a}U_{z}(x,z,t)=-\mu|U(x,0,t)|^{p-1}U(x,0,t).

∎

We are ready to present the

Proof of Theorem

.

Assume that
a\geq 0
and let

, see (

). Suppose that

According to Theorems

, and keeping (

(10.3)

\mathcal{X}=C(\mathbb{R},L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1}))\cap C^{b}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x},

endowed with its natural norm

||U||_{\mathcal{X}}=||U||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||U||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}.

The couple

In the sequel, given a function

|||U|^{p-1}U(0)||_{L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}=||U(0)||_{L^{r}_{x}}^{p}.

In view of (

From (

(10.9)

\displaystyle||\Lambda_{a}(U)||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}\leq||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F||_{L^{1}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+|\mu|\ ||\Theta^{\star}_{a}(|U|^{p-1}U(0))||_{L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}

C_{1}=C_{1}(d,a,r)>0
.

Furthermore, by (
6.4

(10.10)

\displaystyle||\mathbb{T}^{\star}_{a}(u_{0})||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}+||\mathbb{D}_{a}(F)||_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq C_{2}\left[||u_{0}||_{L^{2}_{a}(\mathbb{R}_{+}^{d+1})}+||F||_{L^{1}_{a,z}L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}\right].

From (

\displaystyle||\Lambda_{a}(U)||_{\mathcal{X}}

At this point we divide the discussion into the two cases (1) and (2).

(1) The

\displaystyle|||U|^{p-1}U(0)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}=||U(\cdot,0,\cdot)||^{p}_{L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq||U||^{p}_{L^{\infty}_{z}L^{q}_{t}L^{r}_{x}}\leq||U||_{\mathcal{X}}^{p},

From the definition (

\displaystyle|||U|^{p-1}U(0)-|V|^{p-1}V(0)||_{L^{q^{\prime}}_{t}L^{r^{\prime}}_{x}}
