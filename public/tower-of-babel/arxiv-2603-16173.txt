Long time dynamics and anomalous dissipation of energy in viscous forced active scalar equations
Susan Friedlander, Anthony Suen

Abstract
. We study an abstract family of advection-diffusion equations within the framework of the fractional Laplacian. The system involves two independent diffusion parameters: one introduced via a damping operator acting on the scalar unknown and the other as the coefficient of the fractional Laplacian. We establish existence and convergence results in specific parameter regimes and limits. In particular, we demonstrate the absence of anomalous energy dissipation for long-time averaged solutions. Moreover, we investigate the long time dynamics and prove the existence of a unique global attractor. These results are then applied to two specific classes of active scalar equations in geophysical fluid dynamics, namely the surface quasigeostrophic equation and the magnetogeostrophic equation.

1.
Introduction

Active scalar equations are ubiquitous in nature, science and engineering. They are of great importance in practical applications ranging from fluid mechanics to atmospheric science, oceanography and geophysics. Various active scalar equations, such as drift-diffusion equations, surface quasi-geostrophic (SQG) equation and magnetogeostrophic (MG) equation, have been widely studied by different teams of researchers, yet due to the nonlinear and nonlocal nature of these equations, many fundamental issues in the rigorous mathematical analysis remain unsolved and highly challenging. . In particular, the map

\displaystyle\theta\mapsto\langle N^{(\kappa)}(\theta),\Psi^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle

(4.6)

is locally bounded in
L^{2}
and weakly continuous in
L^{2}
on bounded sets of
L^{p}
,
1\leq p<2
.

Proof.

The method is similar to the one given in
[
CTV14
]
, and we give the proof for
\Omega=\mathbb{T}^{3}
as an example. Suppose
\theta_{n}
is weakly converging to its weak limit
\theta
in
L^{2}
and
\theta_{n}
satisfy the assumed bound

\displaystyle\sup_{n}\|\theta_{n}\|_{L^{p}}\leq A_{p}

(4.7)

for some
A_{p}>0
. Then by integrating
\chi(\frac{x}{R})\theta^{p-1}
against
\theta_{n}
, passing to the limit in
n
and taking
R\to\infty
, we also have

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq A_{p}.

(4.8)

In view of the decomposition of the map (
4.6
), it suffices to address the map
F_{3}
since
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
satisfy the claimed properties trivially. The crucial convergence for consideration is the one that involves
u[\theta]
, which is given by

\displaystyle\langle\theta_{n}u[\theta_{n}],\nabla J_{\varepsilon}w_{k}\rangle\to\langle\theta u[\theta],\nabla J_{\varepsilon}w_{k}\rangle

(4.9)

for
k=1,2,...,N
. For
\theta\in L^{p}
and smooth compactly supported
\phi
, the key for proving (
4.9
) is to rewrite
\langle\theta u[\theta],\nabla\phi\rangle
as

\displaystyle\langle\theta u[\theta],\nabla\phi\rangle=\frac{1}{2}\int(\Lambda^{-1}\theta)(x)[\Lambda,\nabla\phi]u[\theta](x)dx,

where
[\Lambda,f]g=\Lambda(fg)-f\Lambda g
is the commutator operator. Since we have

\displaystyle\Lambda(fg)(x)-f\Lambda g(x)=C\,P.V.\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\frac{g(y)(f(x)-f(y))}{|x-y|^{4}}dy,

if
f
is compactly supported in a ball
B(0,R)
of radius
R
, then for
|x|\geq 2R
,

\displaystyle|\Lambda(fg)(x)-f\Lambda g(x)|\leq C|x|^{-4}\|f\|_{L^{2}}\|g\|_{L^{2}}.

(4.10)

Take
\phi=J_{\varepsilon}w_{k}
and choose
R>1
such that
supp(\phi)\subset B(0,R)
, for
\theta\in L^{p}
, we define
C_{\phi}(\theta)(x)
by

\displaystyle C_{\phi}(\theta)(x)=[\Lambda,\nabla\phi]u[\theta].

Then by (
1.6
) and (
4.10
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x|\geq 2R}(\Lambda^{-1}\theta_{n})(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx\Big|\leq CR^{-1}\|\theta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\Big(\|\theta_{n}\|_{L^{p}}+\|\theta_{n}\|_{L^{2}}\Big),

and hence using the bounds (
4.7
) and (
4.8
),

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{|x|\geq 2R}(\Lambda^{-1}\theta_{n})(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx\Big|\leq CR^{-1}A_{2}(A_{p}+A_{2}).

(4.11)

Now we consider the difference

\displaystyle\int(\Lambda^{-1}\theta_{n})(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx-\int(\Lambda^{-1}\theta)(x)C_{\phi}(\theta)(x)dx

(4.12)

\displaystyle=\int_{B(0,2R)}(\Lambda^{-1}(\theta_{n}-\theta))(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx+\int_{B(0,2R)}(\Lambda^{-1}(\theta))(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n}-\theta)(x)dx

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{|x|\geq 2R}(\Lambda^{-1}\theta_{n})(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx-\int_{|x|\geq 2R}(\Lambda^{-1}\theta)(x)C_{\phi}(\theta)(x)dx.

We let
\epsilon>0
be given. Since
\Lambda^{-1}\theta,C_{\phi}(\theta)\in L^{2}
, together with the bound (
4.11
), by choosing
R
large enough, the third and the fourth term on the right side of (
4.12
) can be bounded by
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
each. On the other hand, the first term on the right side of (
4.12
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{B(0,2R)}(\Lambda^{-1}(\theta_{n}-\theta))(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n})(x)dx\Big|\leq CA_{2}\|\Lambda^{-1}(\theta_{n}-\theta)\|_{L^{2}(B(0,2R)}.

Since
\chi(\frac{x}{2R})\Lambda^{-1}(\theta_{n}-\theta)
is uniformly bounded in
H^{1}
and converges weakly to
0
in
L^{2}
,
\|\Lambda^{-1}(\theta_{n}-\theta)\|_{L^{2}(B(0,2R)}
converges to zero as
n\to\infty
. Finally, since
C_{\phi}(\cdot)
is bounded linear on
L^{2}
,
C_{\phi}(\theta_{n}-\theta)
converges weakly to 0 as
n\to 0
and hence as
n\to 0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{B(0,2R)}(\Lambda^{-1}(\theta))(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n}-\theta)(x)dx=\int\chi(\frac{x}{2R})(\Lambda^{-1}(\theta))(x)C_{\phi}(\theta_{n}-\theta)(x)dx\to 0.

The above shows that as
n\to\infty
, the difference on the left side of (
4.12
) can be bounded in absolute value by
\epsilon
. Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, we conclude that

is locally bounded in
L^{2}
and weakly continuous in
L^{2}
on bounded sets of
L^{p}
,
1\leq p<2
.
∎

We give the definition of stationary statistical solution of (

) when

Definition 4.4
.

A stationary statistical solution of (

) with

We say that the stationary statistical solution satisfies the energy dissipation balance if

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(\lambda\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}-\langle S,\theta\rangle)d\mu(\theta)=0.

(4.16)

The following theorem shows the convergence of stationary statistical solution as

Theorem 4.5
.

Let

be a sequence of stationary statistical solutions of (

. Assume that there exists

and a stationary statistical

of (

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\int_{L^{2}}\Phi(\theta)d\mu^{(\kappa)}(\theta)=\int_{L^{2}}\Phi(\theta)d\mu(\theta)

Define a set

\displaystyle B=\Big\{\theta\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}:\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{\|S\|_{L^{2}}}{\lambda}\Big\}.

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle N(\theta),\Psi^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle N^{(\kappa)}(\theta),\Psi^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle d\mu^{(\kappa)}(\theta)=0.

In the next theorem, it shows that if a sequence of stationary statistical solutions of (

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
\mu^{(\kappa)}
be a sequence of stationary statistical solutions of (
1
) is supported in the set

as given above. Let
\mu
be any weak limit of
\mu^{(\kappa)}
in

\mu
is a stationary statistical solution of (

) that satisfies the energy dissipation balance (

Proof.

The proof is essentially the same as the one given in
[
CTV14
]
for the 2D case, and we only sketch some of the key steps. To prove the energy dissipation balance (
4.16
), we observe that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(\kappa\mathcal{D}-S)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}(\lambda\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}-\langle S,\theta\rangle)d\mu(\theta)

where
J_{\varepsilon}
is the standard mollifier as defined before. Assuming that for all
\varepsilon>0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(\kappa\mathcal{D}-S)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=-\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta),

(4.19)

then it suffices to show that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=0.

(4.20)

Since
\nabla\cdot u[\theta]=0
, upon integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle\nabla J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),\rho_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta],\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta),

where

\displaystyle\rho_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta],\theta)=J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta]\theta)-(J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta])(J_{\varepsilon}(\theta)).

(4.21)

Notice that for any

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\langle\nabla J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),\rho_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta],\theta)\rangle=0,

and using the boundedness of

, we obtain the point-wise bound that

\displaystyle|\langle\nabla J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),\rho_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta],\theta)\rangle|\leq C\|\theta\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}^{2}.

Therefore, by the Lebegue dominated convergence theorem, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle\nabla J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),\rho_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta],\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=0,

which implies (
4.20

4.19

\displaystyle J_{\varepsilon}(N(\theta))=J_{\varepsilon}(\kappa\mathcal{D}-S)+J_{\varepsilon}(u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\theta),

hence it is equivalent to show that

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(N(\theta))\rangle d\mu(\theta)=0.

We choose
{w_{j}}_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset C^{\infty}_{0}
an orthonormal basis of
L^{2}
. For each
\varepsilon>0
, consider a sequence of cylindrical test functionals

such that

\displaystyle\Psi_{m}(\theta)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),w_{j}\rangle^{2},

then we have

\displaystyle\langle N(\theta),\Psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),w_{j}\rangle\langle J_{\varepsilon}(N(\theta)),w_{j}\rangle.

Moreover, it can proved that

\displaystyle|\langle N(\theta),\Psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle|\leq\|J_{\varepsilon}(\theta)\|_{L^{2}}\|J_{\varepsilon}(N(\theta))\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle J_{\varepsilon}(\theta),J_{\varepsilon}(N(\theta))\rangle d\mu(\theta)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\int_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\langle N(\theta),\Psi_{m}^{\prime}(\theta)\rangle d\mu(\theta)=0,

which implies (

We recall the concept of generalized (Banach) limit that will be used for addressing the long time average of solutions

Definition 4.7
.

A generalized limit (Banach limit) is a bounded linear functional

\displaystyle{\rm Lim}_{t\to\infty}:BC([0,\infty))\to\mathbb{R}

such that

•

{\rm Lim}_{t\to\infty}(g)\geq 0,\,\forall g\in BC([0,\infty)),\,g\geq 0.

•

{\rm Lim}_{t\to\infty}(g)=\lim_{t\to\infty}g(t)
whenever the usual limit exists.

The following proposition gives the relation between the long time averages of (

Proposition 4.8

Let

\displaystyle\int_{L^{2}}\Phi(\theta)d\mu^{(\kappa)}(\theta)={\rm Lim}_{t\to\infty}\frac{1}{t}\int_{0}^{t}\Phi(\theta^{(\kappa)}(x,s))ds.

The measure is supported in the set

\displaystyle\bar{A}=\Big\{\theta:\|\theta\|_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}}\leq\frac{\|S\|_{L^{2}}}{\lambda},\,\|\theta\|_{L^{p}}\leq\bar{A}_{p},\,1\leq p\leq\infty\,\Big\}

Proof of Theorem

for

\displaystyle\pi^{\lambda}(t)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{G}^{\lambda},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

In particular,
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
is maximal among the class of bounded invariant sets in
H^{1}
.

Finally, we address the fractal dimensions for the global attractors
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
obtained in Theorem
6.14
. Given a compact set
X
, we give the following definition for fractal dimension
\text{\rm dim}_{f}(X)
, which is based on counting the number of closed balls of a fixed radius
\varepsilon
needed to cover
X
; see
[
Rob13
]
for further explanation.

Definition 6.21
.

Given a compact set
X
, let
N(X,\varepsilon)
be the minimum number of balls of radius
\varepsilon
that cover
X
. The
fractal dimension

\text{\rm dim}_{f}(X)
of
X
is given by

\displaystyle\text{\rm dim}_{f}(X):=\limsup_{\varepsilon\to 0}\frac{\log N(X,\varepsilon)}{-\log\varepsilon}.

In order to prove that
\text{\rm dim}_{f}(\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})
is finite, we need to show that the solution map
\pi^{\lambda}
given in (
6.1
) is uniform differentiable. More precisely, we have the following definition:

Definition 6.22
.

We say that
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
is
uniform differentiable
on
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
if for every
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, there exists a linear operator
D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta_{0},\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda};0<\|\theta_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon}\frac{\|\pi^{\lambda}(t)(\varphi_{0})-\pi^{\lambda}(t)(\theta_{0})-D\pi^{\lambda}(t)(\varphi_{0}-\theta_{0})\|_{H^{1}}}{\|\theta_{0}-\varphi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}}\to 0
as
\varepsilon\to 0
,

(6.57)

and

\displaystyle\sup_{\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}\sup_{\psi_{0}\in H^{1}}\frac{\|D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})(\psi_{0})\|_{H^{1}}}{\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}}<\infty
,  for all
t\geq 0
.

(6.58)

Next, Lemma
6.23
shows that
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
is indeed uniform differentiable with the associated linear operator
D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})
being given by

\displaystyle D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})[\psi_{0}]:=\psi(t),

where
\psi
is the solution of the following linearised problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\frac{d\psi}{dt}=A_{\theta}[\psi],\\
\psi(x,0)=\psi_{0}(x).\end{array}\right.

Here
\theta=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
and
A_{\theta}
is the elliptic operator given by

A_{\theta}[\psi]=A_{\theta_{0}}(t)[\psi]:=-\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}\psi-u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\psi-u[\psi]\cdot\nabla\theta.

(6.61)

Lemma 6.23
.

Let
\kappa>0
be fixed. If
\lambda
and
\gamma
satisfy either (
6.46
) or (
6.47
), then the corresponding solution map
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
is uniform differentiable on
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
. Furthermore, the linear operator
D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})
is compact.

Proof.

For
\theta_{0}
,
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, we let
\theta(t)=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
and
\varphi(t)=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\varphi_{0}
. We denote
\psi_{0}=\varphi_{0}-\theta_{0}
and define
\psi(t)=D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})[\psi_{0}]
, where
\psi(t)
satisfies (
6.2
). We also define

\displaystyle\eta(t)=\varphi(t)-\theta(t)-\psi(t)=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\varphi_{0}-\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}-D\pi^{\lambda}(t,\theta_{0})[\psi_{0}].

Then
\eta(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\eta+\lambda\mathcal{D}\eta+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}\eta+u[\eta]\cdot\nabla\theta+u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\eta=-u[w]\cdot w,\\
\eta(x,0)=0,\end{array}\right.

where
w(t)=\varphi(t)-\theta(t)=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\psi_{0}
. We take
L^{2}
-inner product of (
6.2
)
1
with
-\Delta\eta
and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\kappa\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}u[\eta]\cdot\nabla\theta\Delta\eta-\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{k}}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\eta\partial_{x_{k}}\eta+\int_{\Omega}u[w]\cdot\nabla w\Delta\eta.

(6.64)

Assume that
\lambda
and
\gamma
satisfy (
6.46
). Using (
6.51
) on
\nabla u
, the second integrand of (
6.2
) can be bounded by

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{k}}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\eta\partial_{x_{k}}\eta\Big|\leq C\|\nabla u\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

To estimate the first integrand appeared in (
6.2
), we readily have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\eta]\cdot\nabla\theta\Delta\eta\Big|=\Big|\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u[\eta]\theta)\cdot\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\nabla\eta\Big|\leq\|\Lambda^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u[\eta]\theta)\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}}.

Using (
6.48
), we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u[\eta]\theta)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u[\eta]\theta)\|_{L^{2}},

and using the product estimate (
3.5
),

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u[\eta]\theta)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\Big(\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}u[\eta]\|_{L^{4}}\|\theta\|_{L^{4}}+\|u[\eta]\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big).

Using (
5.1
), (
6.49
) and (
6.50
), the terms
\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}u[\eta]\|_{L^{4}}\|\theta\|_{L^{4}}
and
\|u[\eta]\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}
can be bounded respectively by

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}u[\eta]\|_{L^{4}}\|\theta\|_{L^{4}}\leq C\|\Lambda\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\|u[\eta]\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\eta]\cdot\nabla\theta\Delta\eta\Big|\leq C\Big(\|\Lambda\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}},

and by the similar argument,

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[w]\cdot\nabla w\Delta\eta\Big|\leq C\|\Lambda w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}}.

Dropping the non-negative term
\lambda\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}
, the identity (
6.2
) then implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\kappa\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\eta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+C\Big(\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Lambda\eta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad+C\|\Lambda w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\eta\|_{L^{2}}.

(6.65)

On the other hand, we consider the function
w=w(t)
satisfying

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}w+\lambda\mathcal{D}w+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}w+u[\varphi]\cdot\nabla w+u[w]\cdot\nabla\theta=0,\\
w(x,0)=\psi_{0}.\end{array}\right.

Taking
L^{2}
-inner product of (
6.2
)
1
with
-\Delta w
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\lambda\|w\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\kappa\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}=\int_{\Omega}u[\varphi]\cdot\nabla w\Delta w+\int_{\Omega}u[w]\cdot\nabla\theta\Delta w.

(6.68)

Similar to the previous case for
\eta
, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\varphi]\cdot\nabla w\Delta w\Big|\leq C\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla w\|^{2}_{L^{2}},

and

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[w]\cdot\nabla\theta\Delta w\Big|\leq C\Big(\|\Lambda w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda w\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}w\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence (
6.68
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\kappa\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{2}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+C\Big[\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\varphi\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\kappa}(\|\Lambda\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\varphi\|^{2}_{L^{2}})\Big]\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

(6.69)

Since
\theta_{0}
,
\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
,
\theta
and
\varphi
both satisfy the bounds (
6.2
)-(
6.3
) with

being given by (

\displaystyle\|\theta\|_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}},\qquad\|\varphi\|_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}},

(6.70)

and

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|\theta(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{1+\gamma}}d\tau\leq M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}},\qquad\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\|\varphi(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{1+\gamma}}d\tau\leq M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}},\qquad\forall T>0.

(6.71)

We apply the bounds (
6.70
) on (
6.2
) and use Grönwall’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\|w(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}),\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

(6.72)

as well as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|w(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}d\tau\leq\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

(6.73)

where
K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})
is a positive function in

. We combine (
6.2
) with (
6.70
) and (

) to get

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\kappa\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq CM_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{CM^{2}_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}}{\kappa}\|\eta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{C}{\kappa}K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})\|\psi_{0}\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\|w\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

(6.74)

Using Grönwall’s inequality on (
6.74
) and recalling the fact that
\eta(0)=0
, we conclude that

\displaystyle\|\eta(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq\tilde{K}(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})\|\psi_{0}\|^{4}_{H^{1}},

where
\tilde{K}(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}):=\exp\Big((CM_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}+\frac{CM^{2}_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}}{\kappa})t\Big)\frac{C}{\kappa}K^{2}(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})
. Hence we prove that

\displaystyle\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\left(\sup_{\theta_{0},\varphi_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda};0<\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}\leq\varepsilon}\frac{\|\eta(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{1}}}{\|\psi_{0}\|_{H^{1}}}\right)\leq\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tilde{K}(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})\varepsilon^{2}=0,

and (
6.57
) follows. The bound (
6.58
) can be proved by performing similar analysis on
\psi
.

For any
t>0
and
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, in order to show that the linear operator

is compact, it suffices to show that if
U_{1}
is the unit ball in
H^{1}
, then

. Based on previous estimates, we readily obtain

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\psi(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}d\tau\leq K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}),\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

By the mean value theorem, for
t>0
, there exists
\tau\in(0,t)
such that

\displaystyle\|\psi(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{t}K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}).

(6.75)

We take the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
6.2
)
1
with
\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi
and obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+\lambda\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{2}}+\kappa\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\gamma}}=\int_{\Omega}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\psi\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi+\int_{\Omega}u[\psi]\cdot\nabla\theta\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi.

Using the commutator estimate (
3.6
) and the bound (
6.49
), the term
\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\psi\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi\Big|
can be bounded by

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\psi\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi\Big|\leq C\Big(\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\gamma}\psi\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\psi\|_{L^{2}},

and similarly, we also have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}u[\psi]\cdot\nabla\theta\Lambda^{2+\gamma}\psi\Big|\leq C\Big(\|\Lambda\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda\psi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\gamma}\theta\|_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\Lambda^{1+\gamma}\psi\|_{L^{2}}.

Using Young’s inequality, we obtain from (
6.75
) that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\gamma}}\leq\frac{C}{\kappa}\Big(\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{1+\gamma}\theta\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\Big)\|\psi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

(6.76)

Integrating (
6.76
) from
\tau
to

, applying Grönwall’s inequality and using the bounds (

) and (

), we arrive at

\displaystyle\|\psi(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\psi(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\exp\Big(\frac{C}{\kappa}\int_{\tau}^{t}\|\theta(\cdot,s)\|^{2}_{H^{1+\gamma}}ds\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{t}K(t,M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}})\exp\Big(\frac{C}{\kappa}M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}t\Big),

(6.77)

hence

. The case when (
6.47

.
∎

Next we show that there is an
N
such that volume elements which are carried by the flow of
\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
, with
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, decay exponentially for dimensions larger than
N
. We recall the following proposition for which the proof can be found in
[
CF85
]
,
[
CF88
]
.

Proposition 6.24
.

Consider
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, and an initial orthogonal set of infinitesimal displacements
\{\psi_{1,0},\dots,\psi_{n,0}\}
for some
n\geq 1
. Suppose that
\psi_{i}
obey the following equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\psi_{i}=A_{\theta_{0}}(t)[\psi_{i}],\qquad\psi_{i}(0)=\psi_{i,0},

(6.78)

for all
i\in\{1,\dots,n\}
and
t\geq 0
, where
A_{\theta_{0}}(t)
is given by (
6.61
). Then the volume elements

\displaystyle V_{n}(t)=\|\psi_{1}(t)\wedge\dots\wedge\psi_{n}(t)\|_{H^{1}}

satsify

\displaystyle V_{n}(t)=V_{n}(0)\exp\left(\int_{0}^{t}\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(s)A_{\theta_{0}}(s))ds\right),

where the orthogonal projection
P_{n}(s)
is onto the linear span of
\{\psi_{1}(s),\dots,\psi_{n}(s)\}
in the Hilbert space
H^{1}
, and
\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(s)A_{\theta})
is defined by

\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(s)A_{\theta})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}(-\Delta\varphi_{j}(s))A_{\theta}[\varphi_{j}(s)]dx

for
n\geq 1
, with
\{\varphi_{1}(s),\dots,\varphi_{n}(s)\}
an orthornormal set spanning the linear span of
\{\psi_{1}(s),\dots,\psi_{n}(s)\}
. If we define

\displaystyle\langle P_{n}A_{\theta_{0}}\rangle:=\limsup_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(t)A_{\theta_{0}}(t))dt,

then we further obtain

\displaystyle V_{n}(t)\leq V_{n}(0)\left(t\sup_{\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}\sup_{P_{n}(0)}\langle P_{n}A_{\theta_{0}}\rangle\right),\qquad\forall t\geq 0,

(6.79)

where the supremum over
P_{n}(0)
is a supremum over all choices of initial
n
orthogonal set of infinitesimal displacements that we take around
\theta_{0}
.

Using Proposition
6.24
, we show that the
n
-dimensional volume elements actually
decay exponentially in time
for
n
is sufficiently large, which is based on the following lemma:

Lemma 6.25

(Contractivity of large dimensional volume elements)
.

Let
\kappa>0
be fixed, and assume that
\lambda
and
\gamma
satisfy (
6.46
). There exists
N
such that for any
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
and any set of initial orthogonal displacements
\{\psi_{i,0}\}_{i=1}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle P_{n}A_{\theta_{0}}\rangle<0,

(6.80)

whenever
n\geq N
. Here
N
can be chosen explicitly from (
6.83
) below. If the condition (
6.47
) is in force instead, the inequality (
6.80
) holds whenever
n\geq N
with
N
being chosen from (
6.84
) below.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in H^{1}
be arbitrary. For
\theta_{0}\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
, using the definition of
A_{\theta}
in (
6.61
) and the fact that
\nabla\cdot u[\theta]=0
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{2}\xi A_{\theta}[\xi]dx\leq-\kappa\|\xi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+\Big|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{k}}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\xi\partial_{x_{k}}\xi dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u[\xi]\cdot\nabla\theta)\Lambda^{2}\xi dx\Big|.

Using the bound (
6.50
), we readily have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{k}}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\xi\partial_{x_{k}}\xi dx\Big|\leq C\|\nabla u[\theta]\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\xi\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

And using the product estimate (
3.5
) and the bounds (
6.49
)-(
6.50
), we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u[\xi]\cdot\nabla\theta)\Lambda^{2}\xi dx\Big|\leq C\|\theta\|_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\|\Lambda\xi\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\xi\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{2}\xi A_{\theta}[\xi]dx\leq-\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\xi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}+C\Big(\|\Lambda\theta\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{\kappa}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\Big)\|\xi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}.

(6.81)

On the other hand, for
T>0
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(t)A_{\theta_{0}}(t))dt=\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}(\Lambda^{2}\varphi_{j}(t))A_{\theta}[\varphi_{j}(t)]dxdt.

(6.82)

We apply (
6.81
) on (
6.82
) and together with the bound (
6.2
) on
\theta
to obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(t)A_{\theta_{0}}(t))dt

\displaystyle\leq-\frac{\kappa}{2T}\int_{0}^{T}\text{\rm Tr}(P_{n}(t)\Lambda^{\gamma})+C(M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}+M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}^{2})n\leq-\frac{\kappa}{C}n^{1+\frac{\gamma}{d}}+C(M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}+M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}^{2})n,

where the last inequality follows from the fact that the eigenvalues
\{\lambda_{j}^{(\gamma)}\}
of
\Lambda^{\gamma}
obey the following estimate (see
[
YYY13
, Theorem 1.1]
):

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\lambda_{j}^{(\gamma)}\geq\frac{1}{C}n^{1+\frac{\gamma}{d}},

for some universal constant
C>0
which depends only on
d
and
\gamma
. We choose
N>0
such that

\displaystyle-\frac{\kappa}{C}N^{1+\frac{\gamma}{d}}+C(M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}+M_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}^{2})N<0,

(6.83)

then (
6.80
) holds whenever
n\geq N
. For the case when (
6.47
) is in force, we make use of the estimate that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{2}\xi A_{\theta}[\xi]dx\leq-\lambda\|\xi\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\Big|\int_{\Omega}\partial_{x_{k}}u[\theta]\cdot\nabla\xi\partial_{x_{k}}\xi dx\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u[\xi]\cdot\nabla\theta)\Lambda^{2}\xi dx\Big|.

Then we choose
N>0
such that

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{C}N^{1+\frac{1}{d}}+C(\bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}+\bar{M}_{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}^{2})N<0,

(6.84)

then the same result holds whenever
n\geq N
.
∎

From the results obtained in Lemma
6.23
and Lemma
6.25
, together with Proposition
6.24
, we obtain:

•

the solution map
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
is uniform differentiable on
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
;

•

the linearisation

of
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
is compact;

•

the large-dimensional volume elements which are carried by the flow of

Therefore, following the lines of the argument in

CF88
, pp. 115–130, and Chapter 14]
, we can finally conclude that

is finite. The results can be summarised in the following corollary:

Corollary 6.26

(Finite dimensionality of the attractor)
.

Let
N
be as defined in Lemma
6.25
. Then the fractal dimension of
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
is finite, and we have
\text{\rm dim}_{f}(\mathcal{G}^{\lambda})\leq N
.

Applications to magneto-geostrophic equations

7.1.
The MG equations in the class of drift-diffusion equations

We now apply our results claimed by Section
2
to the magnetogeostrophic active scalar equation with the presence of damping operator
\lambda\mathcal{D}
. Specifically, we are interested in the following active scalar equation in the domain
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times(0,\infty)=[-\pi,\pi]^{3}\times(0,\infty)
(with periodic boundary conditions):

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{t}\theta+u\cdot\nabla\theta+\lambda\mathcal{D}\theta+\kappa\Lambda^{\gamma}\theta=S,\\
u=\mathcal{M}[\theta],\theta(x,0)=\theta_{0}(x)\end{array}\right.

via a Fourier multiplier operator
\mathcal{M}
which relates
u
and
\theta
. More precisely,

\displaystyle u_{j}=\mathcal{M}_{j}[\theta]=(\widehat{\mathcal{M}_{j}}\hat{\theta})^{\vee}

(7.3)

for
j\in\{1,2,3\}
. The explicit expression for the components of
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}
as functions of the Fourier variable
k=(k_{1},k_{2},k_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}
with
k_{3}\neq 0
are given by

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{1}(k)

\displaystyle=[k_{2}k_{3}|k|^{2}-k_{1}k_{3}(k_{2}^{2}+\nu|k|^{4})]D(k)^{-1},

(7.4)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{2}(k)

\displaystyle=[-k_{1}k_{3}|k|^{2}-k_{2}k_{3}(k_{2}^{2}+\nu|k|^{4})]D(k)^{-1},

(7.5)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{3}(k)

\displaystyle=[(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2})(k_{2}^{2}+\nu|k|^{4})]D(k)^{-1},

(7.6)

where

\displaystyle D(k)=|k|^{2}k_{3}^{2}+(k_{2}^{2}+\nu|k|^{4})^{2}.

Since for self-consistency of the model, we assume that
\theta
and
u
have zero vertical mean, and we take
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}_{j}(k)=0
on
\{k_{3}=0\}
for all
j=1,2,3
. We write
\mathcal{M}_{j}=\partial_{x_{i}}T_{ij}
for convenience and take the non-dimensional viscosity
\nu=1
unless otherwise simplified. The system (
7.1
)-(
7.6
) can be rigorously obtained from the incompressible MHD via the postulates in
[
ML94
]
, and the detailed derivations can be found in
[
FV11
,
FRV12
]
. We also refer to (
7.1
) as the
fractional
MG equation when
\gamma\in(0,2]
.

To apply the results from Section
2
, it suffices to show that the linear operator
T_{ij}
satisfies the assumptions A1 to A4 given in Section
1
.

Proposition 7.1
.

We define
T_{ij}
by
\mathcal{M}_{j}=\partial_{x_{i}}T_{ij}
. Then
T_{ij}
satisfy the assumptions A1, A2, A4 in Section
1
and the assumption A3’ in Section
5
.

Proof.

The details for the proof can be found in
[
FS18
, Lemma 5.1–5.2]
and from the discussion in
[
FV11
, Section 4]
as well as
[
FS21
, Section 7]
. We omit the details here.
∎

In view of Proposition
7.1
, the abstract Theorem
2.1
, Theorem
2.2
and Theorem

can then be applied to the MG equations (

). More precisely, we have

Theorem 7.2

(Absence of anomalous dissipation of energy as
\kappa\to 0
for MG equations)
.

Let
\theta_{0},S\in L^{\infty}
, and assume that
\lambda>0
and
\gamma\in(0,2]
be fixed. For each
\kappa\geq 0
, there exists a unique solution
\theta=\theta^{(\kappa)}(x,t)
to (
7.1
) satisfying

\displaystyle\lim_{\kappa\to 0}\left(\limsup_{t\to\infty}\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla\theta^{(\kappa)}(\cdot,s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}ds\right)=0.

Theorem 7.3

(
H^{s}
-convergence as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
for MG equations)
.

Let
\kappa>0
be given as in (
7.1
), and let
\theta_{0},S\in C^{\infty}
be the initial datum and forcing term respectively with zero mean. If
\theta^{(\lambda)}
and
\theta^{(0)}
are smooth solutions to (
7.1
) for
\lambda>0
and
\lambda=0
respectively, then

\displaystyle\lim_{\lambda\rightarrow 0}\|(\theta^{(\lambda)}-\theta^{(0)})(\cdot,t)\|_{H^{s}}=0,

for all
s\geq 0
and
t\geq 0
.

Theorem 7.4

(Existence of global attractors for MG equations)
.

Let
S\in L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}
. For
\kappa>0
, let
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
be solution operator for the initial value problem (
7.1
) via (
6.1
). Then the solution map
\pi^{\lambda}(t):H^{1}\to H^{1}
associated to (
1
) possesses a unique global attractor
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
for all
\lambda\geq 0
. In particular, if
\lambda
and
\gamma
satisfy either (
6.46
) or (
6.47
), then the global attractor
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
of
\pi^{\lambda}(t)
enjoys the following properties:

•

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
is fully invariant, namely

\displaystyle\pi^{\lambda}(t)\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}=\mathcal{G}^{\lambda},\qquad\forall t\geq 0.

•

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
is maximal in the class of
H^{1}
-bounded invariant sets.

•

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
has finite fractal dimension.

In the coming subsection, we will further address the limiting properties of
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
which are related to the MG equation when
\lambda=0
.

7.2.
Behaviour of global attractors for varying
\lambda\geq 0

In the work
[
FS21
]
, when
\kappa>0
and
\lambda=0
, the authors proved the existence of a compact global attractor
\mathcal{G}^{0}
in
H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})
for the MG equations (
7.1
) with
S\in L^{\infty}\cap H^{1}
. More precisely,
\mathcal{G}^{0}
is the global attractor generated by the solution map
\pi^{0}
via

\displaystyle\pi^{0}(t):H^{1}\to H^{1},\qquad\pi^{0}(t)\theta_{0}=\theta(\cdot,t),\qquad t\geq 0,

(7.7)

where
\theta
is the solution to the MG equation with
\theta(\cdot,0)=\theta_{0}
. In this subsection, we obtain results when
\lambda
is varying, which can be summarised in the following theorem:

Theorem 7.5

(Upper semicontinuity of global attractors at
\lambda\geq 0
)
.

Let
\kappa>0
be fixed in (
7.1
). Let
\lambda_{0}\geq 0
be arbitrary, then the collection
\displaystyle\{\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\geq 0}
is
upper semicontinuous
at
\lambda_{0}
in the following sense:

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda_{0}}}\|\phi-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow\lambda_{0}
.

(7.8)

In particular, if
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
are the global attractors for the MG equations (
7.1
) as obtained by Theorem
7.4
, then
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
and
\mathcal{G}^{0}
satisfy

\displaystyle\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}}\inf_{\psi\in\mathcal{G}^{0}}\|\phi-\psi\|_{H^{1}}\rightarrow 0
as
\lambda\rightarrow 0
.

(7.9)

Remark 7.6
.

Here are some relevant remarks regarding Theorem
7.5
:

•

By the maximality of global attractors, we readily have
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}|_{\lambda=0}=\mathcal{G}^{0}
. Hence it suffices to show (
7.8
) as (
7.9
) follows immediately.

•

Although
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}
has finite fractal dimension for all
\lambda>0
and
\gamma\in(0,2]
, it is unknown whether
\mathcal{G}^{0}
has finite fractal dimension for the case when
\gamma\in(0,1)
.

•

It is also worth pointing out that the
H^{1}
-convergence result (
7.8
) is stronger than the one obtained in
[
FS21
, Theorem 7.5]
which showed the
L^{2}
-convergence for varying
\nu
in the system (
7.1
)-(
7.6
).

To obtain the convergence result claimed by Theorem
7.5
, we need to prove that

L1.

there is a compact subset
\mathbf{U}
of
H^{1}
such that
\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\subset\mathbf{U}
for every
\lambda\geq 0
; and

L2.

for
t>0
,
\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
is continuous in
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
, uniformly for
\theta_{0}
in compact subsets of
H^{1}
.

Once the conditions L1 and L2 are fufilled, we can apply the result from
[
HOR15
]
to conclude that (
7.8
) holds as well.

To show that condition L1 holds, as claimed by Lemma
6.9
, there exists a constant
R_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\geq 1
which depends on
\|S\|_{L\infty\cap H^{1}}
,
\kappa
,
\gamma
such that the set
\mathbf{U}:=B_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}
where

\displaystyle B_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}=\left\{\phi\in H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}:\|\phi\|_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq R_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\right\}

enjoys the following properties:

•

B_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}
is a compact set in
H^{1}
which depends only on
\|S\|_{L\infty\cap H^{1}}
,
\kappa
,
\gamma
;

•

\mathcal{G}^{\lambda}\subset B_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}
for all
\lambda\geq 0
.

On the other hand, in order to prove that condition L2 holds as well, we need some higher order bounds on
\theta
that are uniform in
\lambda
. The following lemma gives the necessary
H^{1}
-estimates on
\theta
which are uniform in
\lambda
.

Lemma 7.7

(Uniform
H^{1}
-bound on
\theta
)
.

We fix
\kappa>0
and define
\mathbf{K}=\{\phi\in H^{1}:\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq R_{\mathbf{K}}\}
where
R_{\mathbf{K}}>0
. For any
\gamma\in(0,2]
,
\theta_{0}\in\mathbf{K}
and
\lambda\geq 0
, if
\theta^{(\lambda)}(t)=\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
, then
\theta^{(\lambda)}(t)
satisfies

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\theta^{(\lambda)}(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\|\theta^{(\lambda)}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}^{2}d\tau\leq M_{*}(t),\qquad\forall t>0,

(7.10)

where
M_{*}(t)
is a positive function in
t
which depends only on
t
,
\kappa
,
\|S\|_{H^{1}}
and
R_{\mathbf{K}}
.

Proof.

We take
\theta=\theta^{(\lambda)}
and
u=u[\theta^{(\lambda)}]
for simplicity. Recall (
6.1
) from the proof of Lemma
6.8
that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq\Big(\frac{4C}{\kappa}\Big)^{\frac{2-2\alpha}{\gamma}}(2C_{\alpha})^{2}K_{\infty}^{1+\frac{2(1-\alpha)}{\gamma}}+\frac{8}{c_{\gamma,3}\kappa}\|S\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\kappa}{4}\|\theta\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}},

(7.11)

where
C
,
K_{\infty}
,
C_{\alpha}
,
c_{\gamma,3}
are all constant that are independent of
\lambda
. Upon applying Grönwall’s inequality on (
7.11
), the bound (
7.10
) holds for some
M_{*}(t)
which depends on the terms appeared on the right side of (
7.11
) but is independent of
\lambda
.
∎

Remark 7.8
.

Replacing
\gamma
by
2-\gamma
, for any
\gamma\in(0,2]
, we also have the following uniform bound on
\theta^{(\lambda)}
:

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq\tau\leq t}\|\theta^{(\lambda)}(\cdot,\tau)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\kappa\int_{0}^{t}\|\theta^{(\lambda)}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}^{2}d\tau\leq M_{*}(t),\qquad\forall t>0.

(7.12)

The uniform-in-
\lambda
bounds (
7.10
) and (
7.12
) are crucial for controlling the
H^{1}
-norm of
\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
; see the proof of Lemma
7.9
for details.

We are now ready to state and prove the following lemma which gives the continuity of
\pi^{\lambda}
in
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
.

Lemma 7.9
.

For each
t>0
,
\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
is continuous in
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
, uniformly for
\theta_{0}
in compact subsets of
H^{1}
.

Proof.

Given a compact set
\mathbf{K}
in
H^{1}
, we choose
R_{\mathbf{K}}>0
such that
\mathbf{K}\subset\{\phi\in H^{1}:\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq R_{\mathbf{K}}\}
. For each
\theta_{0}\in\mathbf{K}
and
\lambda_{1}
,
\lambda_{2}\geq 0
, we define

\theta^{(\lambda_{i})}(t)=\pi^{\lambda_{i}}(t)\theta_{0},\qquad i\in\{1,2\}.

We write
\phi=\theta^{(\lambda_{1})}-\theta^{(\lambda_{2})}
, then
\phi
satisfies
\phi(\cdot,0)=0
and

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\phi+(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}+u^{(\lambda_{1})}\cdot\nabla\phi+\lambda_{2}\mathcal{D}\phi+(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\mathcal{D}\theta^{\lambda_{1}}+\kappa\lambda^{\gamma}\phi=0,

(7.13)

where
u^{(\lambda_{i})}=u[\theta^{(\lambda_{i})}]
for
i=1,2
. Multiply (
7.13
) by
-\Delta\phi
and integrate,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}

\displaystyle\leq\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\Delta\phi\Big|+\Big|(\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2})\int_{\Omega}\mathcal{D}\theta^{\lambda_{1}}\cdot\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\phi\Big|+\Big|\int_{\Omega}u^{(\lambda_{1})}\cdot\nabla\phi\Delta\phi\Big|.

(7.14)

Upon integrating by part and exploiting the fact that
\nabla\cdot u^{(\lambda_{1})}=0
, the last integral of (
7.2
) vanishes. For the first term on the right side of (
7.2
), by applying Fourier transform and Parseval’s theorem, we readily obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\Delta\phi\Big|=\Big|\int_{\Omega}\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}[(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}]\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\phi\Big|.

Hence by Hölder’s inequality, we have

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{\Omega}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\Delta\phi\Big|\leq\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}[(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}]\|_{L^{2}}\|\Lambda^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}\phi\|_{L^{2}}

Using (
3.6
), we further obtain

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}[(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}]\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}[(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}]-(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+\|(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\Lambda^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{L^{2}}\Big).

To bound the term
\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\|_{L^{\infty}}
, using (
5.1
) and (
3.2
)-(
3.3
), for
q\in(3,6)
, we have

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\|_{L^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\|_{W^{1,q}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\|_{W^{2,2}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}.

Similarly, we also have
\|u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})}\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}
and hence we obtain

\displaystyle\|\Lambda^{1-\frac{\gamma}{2}}[(u^{(\lambda_{1})}-u^{(\lambda_{2})})\cdot\nabla\theta^{\lambda_{2}}]\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}\|\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{H^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}.

We deduce from (
7.2
) that

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1}}+\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\phi\|^{2}_{H^{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}}\leq C\|\phi\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\theta^{\lambda_{2}}\|_{H^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}^{2}+C|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|^{2}\|\theta^{\lambda_{1}}\|^{2}_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}.

(7.15)

By the bounds (
7.10
) and (
7.12
), the integrals
\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\theta^{\lambda_{2}}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{2-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}^{2}d\tau
and

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\theta^{\lambda_{1}}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{H^{\frac{3}{2}}}^{2}d\tau
are bounded independent of
\lambda_{1}
and
\lambda_{2}
. By applying Grönwall’s inequality on (
7.15
), we conclude that there exists a positive function
C_{*}(t)
which depends only on
t
,
\kappa
,
\|S\|_{H^{1}}
and
R_{\mathbf{K}}
such that

\displaystyle\|(\theta^{(\lambda_{1})}-\theta^{(\lambda_{2})})(\cdot,t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}=\|\phi(t)\|^{2}_{H^{1}}\leq C_{*}(t)|\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}|^{2},\qquad\forall t>0,

(7.16)

and (
7.16
) implies
\pi^{\lambda}(t)\theta_{0}
is continuous in
\lambda\in[0,\infty)
uniformly for
\theta_{0}
in
\mathbf{K}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
7.5
.

By choosing
\mathbf{U}=B_{1+\frac{\gamma}{2}}
and applying Lemma
7.9
, the conditions L1 and L2 as stated at the beginning of this subsection follow immediately and we conclude that (
7.8
) holds. We finish the proof of Theorem
7.5
.
∎
