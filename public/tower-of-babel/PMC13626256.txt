Colombiains A–C, Three New Alkaloids From the Soft Coral‐Derived Fungus Cladosporium colombiae CSGL 10M10

Jing Xie, Bing‐Bing Liu, Yang‐Ying Zheng, Jun‐Feng Huan, Xiao‐Yu Tian, Xiu‐Ping Lin, Hui‐Mei Xie, Yuan‐Yuan Cheng, Cui‐Xian Zhang

Abstract

Three new alkaloids, colombiains A–C (1–3), together with 8 known ones (4–11), were isolated from the soft coral‐derived fungus Cladosporium colombiae CSGL 10M10. Their structures were elucidated by spectroscopic interpretation, electronic circular dichroism (ECD), and single‐crystal X‐ray crystallographic methods. Compounds 6 and 11 exhibited significant cardioprotective activity against oxygen‐glucose deprivation/reoxygenation (OGD/R)‐induced injury in H9c2 myocardial cell line at 10 μM. In addition, a preliminary network pharmacology analysis of compound 6 suggested that β‐carboline alkaloids might exert protective effects through a multi‐target, multi‐pathway mode of action, providing a rationale for future mechanistic studies.

Introduction

Cladosporium, a genus of saprophytic or endophytic fungi, is widely distributed in natural environments and particularly abundant in marine ecosystems has garnered significant interest for its chemically diverse and bioactive secondary metabolites, which encompass polyketides, alkaloids, steroids, and terpenoids with antimicrobial, cytotoxic, and enzyme‐inhibitory effects [, , ]. The alkaloids reported from Cladosporium are mainly tetramic acid derivatives, diketopiperazines, and other types such as β‐carboline alkaloids []. Notably, only one β‐carboline alkaloid has been isolated from this genus [], suggesting that Cladosporium represents a promising yet underexplored source for the discovery of β‐carboline alkaloids. The skeleton of β‐carbolines is a pyrido[3,4‐b]indole framework and occurs in both terrestrial and marine organisms [, , ]. Natural and synthetic β‐carboline derivatives have shown protective effects in experimental models of cardiomyocyte injury, including oxidative‐stress and myocardial ischemia/reperfusion models [, , ]. These observations provide a rationale for evaluating Cladosporium derived metabolites in a defined cardiomyocyte‐injury assay.

As part of our continuing investigation of bioactive metabolites from marine microorganisms [, , , , ], a chemical investigation of the soft coral‐associated fungus Cladosporium colombiae CSGL 10M10 was carried out. This investigation afforded three new alkaloids, colombiains A ‐ C (1–3), together with eight known metabolites (4–11) (Figure ). The potential cardioprotective effects of the compounds were evaluated using an OGD/R‐induced injury model in H9c2 cardiomyocytes, and compounds 6 and 11 exhibited protective effects comparable to those of the positive control diazoxide. Further network pharmacology analysis indicated that compound 6 might exert its cardioprotective effects through signaling pathways including diabetic cardiomyopathy, lipid and atherosclerosis, and neurotrophin signaling.

Results and Discussion

Compound 1 was obtained as a white amorphous solid and had the molecular formula C8H12N2O3 as determined by high‐resolution electrospray ionization mass spectrometry (HRESIMS) ion peak at m/z 185.0925 [M + H]+ (calcd. for 185.0926), implying 4 degrees of unsaturation (Figure ). The Ultraviolet (UV) absorption maxima was reached at 216 nm (Figure ). The infrared (IR) spectrum displayed characteristic absorptions for carbonyl (1726 cm−1) (Figure ). The 1H nuclear magnetic resonance (NMR), 13C NMR and distortionless enhancement by polarization transfer (DEPT) spectrum displayed signals for one methyl at δ
H 1.52 (3H, d, J = 7.0 Hz, H3‐10), three methylenes at δ
H 1.68 (2H, m, H‐7), 2.44 (2H, m, H‐8), and 3.72 (2H, m, H‐6), one methine at δ
H 6.19 (1H, m, H‐3), and one formyl proton at δ
H 8.63 (1H, s, H‐1) (Table , Figures ). The 13C NMR, DEPT, and heteronuclear single‐quantum coherence (HSQC) spectra indicated 8 carbon resonances corresponding to one methyl carbon at δ
C 17.6 (CH3, C‐10), three methylene carbons at δ
C 18.6 (CH2, C‐7), 33.8 (CH2, C‐8), and 46.2 (CH2, C‐6), one aliphatic methine carbon at δ
C 48.3 (CH, C‐3), one formyl carbon at δ
C 161.5 (CH, C‐1), and two amide carbonyl carbons at δ
C 174.5 (C, C‐4) and 175.8 (C, C‐9) (Table , Figures ).

All protons were assigned to the corresponding carbons from the HSQC spectrum (Table , Figure ). Analysis of the 1H‐1H COSY spectrum (Figure ) allowed the compound 1 to be divided into two structural subunits: N‐formylalanine and 2‐pyrrolidinone (Table , Figure ). The spin system of N‐formylalanine was fully assigned on the basis of combined 1H‐1H COSY correlations of H‐3 (δ
H 6.19)/H3‐10 (δ
H 1.52) and 13C NMR data (Table , Figure ). The attachment of the N‐formyl moiety was established by the HMBC correlations of H‐3 (δ
H 6.19) to C‐1 (δ
C 161.5) and C‐4 (δ
C 174.5), and H3‐10 (δ
H 1.52) to C‐3 (δ
C 48.3) and C‐4 (δ
C 174.5). The second spin system was assigned to the 2‐pyrrolidinone subunit based on the continuous 1H‐1H COSY correlations of H2‐6 (δ
H 3.72)/H2‐7 (δ
H 1.68)/H2‐8 (δ
H 2.44), together with the 2D NMR data (Table , Figures ). In its HMBC spectrum, the correlations from H2‐6 (δ
H 3.72) to C‐9 (δ
C 175.8), H2‐7 (δ
H 1.68) to C‐9 (δ
C 175.8), and H2‐8 (δ
H 2.44) to C‐9 (δ
C 175.8) supported the assignment of 2‐pyrrolidinone (Figure ). Finally, the HMBC correlation from H2‐6 (δ
H 3.72) to C‐4 (δ
C 174.5) established the linkage between C‐4 of N‐formylalanine and the amide nitrogen of the 2‐pyrrolidinone moiety in compound 1.

Biogenetically, the stereocenter at C‐3 of 1 was derived from alanine. As naturally occurring alanine was almost exclusively in the L‐configuration [, ], the 3S configuration was tentatively assigned from a biogenetic perspective. This deduction was further supported by the calculated ECD spectrum, in which the simulated spectrum of the 3S isomer showed excellent agreement with the experimental ECD spectrum (Table , Figures , ). Based on the above evidence, the structure of 1 was determined and named as colombiain A.

Compound 2 was isolated as a white amorphous solid. Its molecular formula was assigned as C8H14N2O4 on the basis of the HRESIMS data m/z 203.1023 [M+H]+ (calcd. for 203.1032), indicating 3° of unsaturation (Figure ). The UV absorption maxima was reached at 200 nm (Figure ). The IR spectrum revealed the presence of carbonyl (1740 cm−1) (Figure ). A detailed comparison of 1H and 13C NMR data of compounds 2 and 1 revealed that they were analogues (Table , Figures ). The key distinction was that the 2‐pyrrolidinone moiety in compound 1 underwent hydrolysis to form a γ‐aminobutyric acid unit, which was supported by a mass difference of 18 Da between the two compounds and the disappearance of the HMBC correlations from H2‐6 to C‐9. To verify the absolute configuration of 2, the calculated ECD spectrum was compared with the experimental ECD curve (Table , Figures , ), which unambiguously assigned the 3S configuration to C‐3. Therefore, compound 2 was given the trivial name colombiain B.

Compound 3 was initially obtained as clear light yellow block crystals with a molecular formula of C17H10N2O4 based on its HRESIMS peak at m/z 307.0712 [M+H]+ (calcd. for 307.0719), indicating 14° of unsaturation (Figure ). The UV absorption maxima were reached at 222, 250, 299, and 395 nm (Figure ). The IR spectrum exhibited characteristic absorption bands at carbonyl (1724, 1697 cm−1) and aromatic ring (1620, 1462 cm−1) (Figure ). Comparison of the NMR data (Figures ) indicated that compound 3 was highly similar to the known compound Furan‐2‐yl(9H‐pyrido[3,4‐b]indol‐1‐yl) methanone [], suggesting that 3 was a β‐carboline derivative. The most notable difference was the presence of an additional carboxyl carbon signal in 3, which was consistent with the mass difference of 44 Da observed in their HRESIMS data. The key HMBC correlations from H‐4 (δ
H 9.16) to C‐19 (δ
C 166.8) indicated that the carbonyl group was attached to C‐3 (Figure ). However, no correlation was observed between H‐16 (δ
H 8.20) with C‐14 (δ
C 178.5), thereby the planar structure of 3 couldn't be established (Figure ). Fortunately, 3 was obtained as light yellow block crystals upon standing in MeOH at room temperature, and its structure was established by single‐crystal X‐ray diffraction using Cu Kα radiation [CCDC 2555402] (Table , Figure ). So, compound 3 was identified as colombiain C.

The eight known compounds were identified as 1‐(Furan‐2‐yl)‐9H‐pyrido[3,4‐b]indole‐3‐carboxylic acid (4) [], flazine (5) [], 1‐carbomethoxy‐β‐carboline (6) [], N‐acetyl‐β‐oxotryptamine (7) [], indole‐3‐methylethanoate (8) [], 1H‐indole‐3‐carbaldehyde (9) [], 2‐acetylpyridine (10) [], nicotinic acid (11) [] by comparing their spectroscopic data with the corresponding literature data (Tables ).

Cardiovascular diseases (CVDs) remain the leading cause of mortality worldwide, the pursuit of cardioprotective agents have long been a central focus in cardiovascular drug discovery and development []. All isolated compounds were initially evaluated for cytotoxicity against normal H9c2 cardiomyocytes at a concentration of 10 μM, and the results demonstrated that compounds 3 (87.26 ± 4.14)% and 5 (87.95 ± 3.97)% exhibited cytotoxic effects compared to the control group (Figure ). Through the further assessment of cardioprotective effects using an OGD/R‐induced injury model in H9c2 cells, compounds 6 (71.91 ± 3.44)% and 11 (72.18 ± 2.85)% significantly improved cell viability (Figure ), showing the same protective effects as the positive control diazoxide (71.52 ± 2.49)%.

In order to investigate the possible mechanism of the cardioprotective compounds, the potential targets of compound 6 were obtained by Swiss Target Prediction (Figure ). In the Venn diagram, all cardioprotection‐related targets overlapped with the targets of 6 (Figure ). A protein‐protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using the STRING database and subsequently visualized and analyzed in Cytoscape 3.10.4 (Figure ). Topological analysis of the PPI network identified PTGS2, SRC, GSK3B, MMP9, and MAOB as the core targets (Figure ). Gene Ontology (GO) functional enrichment (Figure ) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses (Figure ) indicated that the mechanism of β‐carboline alkaloids was primarily associated with neural regulation‐related pathways, while cardioprotection‐related pathways may involve the diabetic cardiomyopathy pathway [, ], the lipid and atherosclerosis pathway [, , ], and the neurotrophin signaling pathway [, ]. These network pharmacology results may provide a basis for future investigations into the cardioprotective mechanisms of β‐carboline alkaloids.

Conclusions

In conclusion, two new dipeptide derivatives colombiains A (1) and B (2), one new β‐carboline alkaloid colombiain C (3), together with three known β‐carboline analogs (4–6), three simple indole alkaloids (7–9) and two known pyridine derivatives (10–11), were isolated from the soft coral‐derived fungus Cladosporium colombiae CSGL 10M10. Notably, compounds 4 ‐ 11 were isolated from the genus Cladosporium for the first time, thereby enriching its chemical structural diversity. Bioactivity assays demonstrated that compounds 6 and 11 exhibited cardioprotective effects comparable to the positive control diazoxide. Furthermore, network pharmacology predictions indicated that β‐carboline alkaloids might exert cardioprotective effects through anti‐inflammatory, lipid‐regulating, anti‐atherosclerotic, and myocardial metabolic modulation pathways.

Experimental Section

General Experimental Procedures

Optical rotations were measured with an MCP 200 digital polarimeter (Anton Paar, Austria). UV and CD spectra were taken using a Circular Dichroism J‐810 spectrometer (JASCO, Japan). IR spectra were recorded on a Shimadzu IRAffinity‐1 Fourier‐transform infrared spectrophotometer (Shimadzu, Japan). X‐ray crystallographic data were acquired using a Rigaku Synergy Custom diffractometer with a HyPix detector (Rigaku, Japan). 1D and 2D NMR spectra were acquired on a Bruker AVANCE III HD 400 MHz NMR spectrometer (Bruker, Germany). Triple TOF 5600+ mass spectrometry system (AB Sciex, U.S.A.). QuikSep semi‐preparative high performance liquid chromatography (HPLC) instrument (Beijing Huideyi Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China). Column 1: Kromasil semi‐preparation column (21.2 × 250 mm, 10 μm, Sweden). Column 2: Kromasil column (10 × 250 mm, 5 μm, Sweden). Silica gel (200−300 mesh, Qingdao Marine Chemical Factory, Qingdao, China). ODS gel (S‐50 μm, YMC Co., Ltd., Kyoto, Japan). Methanol, ethyl acetate, petroleum ether and dichloromethane were used for column chromatography (all analytically pure, Tianjin Damao Chemical Reagent Factory, Tianjin, China). Rat cardiomyocyte H9c2 cell line (RRID: CVCL_0286) was obtained from Shanghai Institute for Biological Sciences, Chinese Academy of Science (Shanghai, China).

Fermentation, Extraction

The fungal strain Cladosporium colombiae CSGL 10M10 was isolated and purified from a soft coral collected in the Beibu Gulf region of Guangxi, P.R. China. A voucher strain (CSGL 10M10) has been deposited at the Laboratory of Marine Natural Medicine, Guangzhou University of Chinese Medicine, Guangzhou, P.R. China. The fermentation of strain CSGL 10M10 was performed on Zhen‐1 medium (400 × 1 L flasks with NaBr, 100 × 1 L flasks with NaCl) and GPY medium (50 × 1 L flasks, each with NaBr and NaCl). The Zhen‐1 medium contained maltose 4.0%, yeast extract 1.3%, sodium glutamate 1.0%, sorbitol 5.0%, tryptophan 0.05%, MgSO4·7H2O 0.03%, KH2PO4 0.05%, sea salt 3.5%. The GPY medium consisted of glucose 1.0%, yeast extract 0.2%, peptone 1.0%, sea salt 0.25%. After fermentation, the mycelia and broth were filtered and separated by gauze. The broth was concentrated at 50°C and sequentially extracted three times with EtOAc (ethyl acetate) and n‐BuOH (n‐ butanol, v:v = 1:1) in sequence. The respective organic phases were concentrated under reduced pressure to afford the EtOAc and n‐BuOH extracts of the broth. The mycelia were extracted with MeOH three times (each for 72 h). The combined MeOH extracts were dissolved in distilled water and then sequentially partitioned with PE (petroleum ether), EtOAc, and n‐BuOH (v:v = 1:1), each three times. Based on the TLC and 1H NMR, the EtOAc and n‐BuOH fractions obtained from the broth and mycelia of strain 10M10 across all tested media were combined to afford the total EtOAc extract (183.1 g) and total n‐BuOH extract (417.0 g), respectively.

Isolation and Purification

The total EtOAc extract (183.1 g) was subjected to silica gel column chromatography and elutied with a gradient of PE—EtOAc (100:0 ‐ 0:100, v/v) to yield seven fractions (Fr.e1 to Fr.e7). Similarly, the total n‐BuOH extract (417.0 g) was separated by silica gel column chromatography with CH2Cl2 ‐ CH3OH (100:0 ‐ 0:100, v/v) to afford seven fractions (Fr.n1 to Fr.n7). The TLC analysis revealed partial overlap between the polar fractions of the EtOAc extract and the nonpolar fractions of the n‐BuOH extract. Based on TLC and 1H NMR analysis, these fractions were further combined to afford eight primary fractions (Fr.1 ‐ Fr.8).
