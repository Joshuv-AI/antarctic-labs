Stability and Hopf bifurcation analysis of a fractional-order Filippov prey-predator model with prey refuge and fear effects
Authors: Muhammad Nadeem, P. Ramesh, M. Sambath, Yahya Alsayaad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study investigates the stability of a fractional-order Filippov prey-predator model that incorporates the ecological complexities of prey refuge and the fear effect. The primary research question is: How do fractional-order dynamics, combined with non-smooth Filippov switching mechanisms, influence the long-term stability and persistence of predator-prey populations? We establish the existence and uniqueness of the solution, ensuring the model’s mathematical consistency. Key results include the proof of solution non-negativity and boundedness, confirming ecological feasibility, and a detailed local and global stability analysis of equilibrium points. Our analysis reveals that the fractional order parameter θ significantly modifies stability conditions, with lower orders inducing slower convergence and prolonged transients, reflecting memory effects. Numerical simulations corroborate the theoretical findings and illustrate the stabilizing impact of prey refuge and the destabilizing potential of the fear effect. This work demonstrates that a fractional-order Filippov framework provides a more nuanced and realistic representation of ecological dynamics than its integer-order counterpart.

Introduction

The interaction between predator and prey populations has been a fundamental subject of study in ecological dynamics. Classical predator-prey models, such as the Lotka-Volterra and Filippov models, have been used to understand the stability of these ecosystems and predict the outcomes of predator-prey interactions under various conditions1–4. However, real-world ecological systems often exhibit complex behaviors that cannot always be captured by classical integer-order models. This has motivated the incorporation of fractional-order derivatives into dynamical systems, which provide a more accurate description of systems with memory effects and hereditary properties, commonly observed in biological systems5–10.

The filippov prey-predator model, which incorporates the effects of fear and prey refuge, provides a robust framework for studying the non-smooth dynamics of predator-prey interactions. Fear effects represent a behavioral response by prey to the presence of predators, leading to changes in prey dynamics. The prey refuge, on the other hand, introduces a mechanism by which prey can seek refuge in safe areas, potentially altering predator-prey interactions and enhancing the stability of the prey population.

Nowadays, many researchers are focusing on Filippov prey-predator models and are actively incorporating new terms and ecological factors to enhance their realism and complexity. Recently, Hamdallah et al.11 conducted a stability analysis of a Filippov prey-predator model incorporating fear effects and prey refuge, highlighting the role of non-smooth dynamics and ecological interactions. Balci12 investigated the impact of predation fear and its carry-over effects in a fractional-order prey-predator model with prey refuge. Jana et al.13 examined the influence of physical and behavioral prey refuge on the stability and bifurcation of a Gause-type Filippov prey-predator system. Patra et al. analyzed a Filippov model with a discontinuous harvesting policy involving prey refuge. Cortes-Garcia14 explored the stability of a Filippov Gause predator-prey model with or without cooperative hunting among predators, depending on prey density.

Liu et al.15 studied bifurcation and optimal harvesting in a discrete-time predator-prey model incorporating fear and prey refuge effects. Zhu et al.16 analyzed bifurcations in a three-dimensional Filippov pest-natural enemy system with stage structure in the prey population. Fawzy et al.17 investigated mosquito suppression using the Filippov framework via an incompatible insect technique. Zhang et al.18 examined the existence of multiple sliding segments and bifurcation phenomena in a Filippov prey Cpredator model. Yadav et al.19 studied a Filippov-type predator-prey system with selective harvesting of predators. Bhattacharyya et al.20 focused on the dynamics of a Filippov predator-prey system under stage-specific intermittent harvesting. Li21 conducted a bifurcation analysis of a Filippov predator-prey model with two thresholds, while Verma et al.22 investigated bifurcation and pattern formation in a prey Cpredator model with cooperative hunting.

Usually prey species do not exhibit constant refuge-seeking behavior; rather, they adjust their defensive strategies based on perceived risk, switching between refuge utilization and active foraging depending on the prey-to-predator ratio23,24. While recent studies have explored fear and refuge effects in predator-prey systems25,26, most assume either constant refuge availability or a smooth transition between states, thereby failing to capture the discontinuous switching behavior observed in nature. Furthermore, ecological interactions are inherently characterized by memory effects and nonlocal dynamics that classical integer-order models cannot adequately represent. Fractional-order models offer a more realistic alternative by accounting for hereditary properties27–33, yet their integration with discontinuous Filippov systems remains largely unexplored.

To address these gaps, this study presents a novel fractional-order Filippov predator-prey model that integrates fear effect with threshold-dependent prey refuge switching. The key novelty lies in the synergistic combination of three critical features: (i) a Filippov switching mechanism based on the prey-to-predator ratio, enabling realistic representation of adaptive refuge behavior; (ii) predator-induced fear effects modulating prey growth; and (iii) extension to a fractional-order framework using the Caputo derivative, capturing the memory-dependent nature of biological interactions. To the best of our knowledge, this is the first attempt to formulate and analyze a fractional-order Filippov system incorporating both fear and threshold-dependent refuge in predator-prey dynamics. Through analytical and numerical investigations, we explore equilibrium stability and bifurcation phenomena, contributing to a deeper understanding of how adaptive prey behavior, memory effects, and fear responses collectively shape ecosystem stability and species coexistence.

In this connection, we extend the Filippov prey-predator framework by incorporating fractional-order derivatives to account for memory and non-local ecological interactions. Through a comprehensive stability analysis, we demonstrate how fractional-order dynamics influence the system s stability and overall behavior. Our findings reveal that fractional-order models can more accurately represent ecological systems characterized by memory and historical dependency. Additionally, incorporating fear effects and prey refuge not only increases the model s complexity but also enhances its ecological realism. We show that fractional-order terms significantly affect the system s stability, particularly regarding equilibrium existence and the populations responses to perturbations. This work opens promising avenues for future research, especially in exploring the ecological implications of fractional-order dynamics in more complex ecosystem models. Future studies could focus on numerical simulations under varied ecological scenarios and real-world applications. The inclusion of fractional-order derivatives in ecological models holds considerable potential for advancing our understanding of complex biological systems and ensuring their long-term stability in fluctuating environments.

Problem formulation and some preliminaries

Filippov systems provide a powerful framework for modeling ecological interactions where biological processes exhibit threshold-dependent switching behavior34,35. Such systems are characterized by a discontinuous vector field across a switching manifold, allowing the representation of abrupt behavioral changes in response to environmental cues. In general, a Filippov system is constructed by defining two distinct subsystems, each valid in a different region of the phase space, separated by a switching threshold. Since the right-hand side is undefined on Σ, a unique solution can be obtained using Filippov’s convex method36, which defines a sliding vector field on the discontinuity surface. This formulation gives rise to various equilibrium types, including regular, virtual, pseudo-equilibria, and tangent points, enabling the analysis of complex bifurcation phenomena such as boundary node bifurcations, touching bifurcations, and sliding crossing bifurcations.

In our study, we adopt this Filippov framework to model prey refuge switching based on the prey-to-predator ratio \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\frac{x}{y}$$\end{document} relative to a critical threshold ζ. Specifically, when the ratio falls below ζ, prey seek refuge, activating a subsystem with refuge effects; when the ratio exceeds ζ, refuge is not utilized, and the system switches to a subsystem without refuge. To further enhance the biological realism of the model, we extend this integer-order Filippov formulation to a fractional-order setting using the Caputo fractional derivative. This extension captures hereditary properties and long-term memory effects inherent in ecological systems, which are not adequately represented by classical integer-order derivatives. Conclusion

In this study, we investigated the existence and uniqueness of the solution for the fractional-order filippov prey-predator model, incorporating the effects of fear and prey refuge. By applying fractional differential equations, we captured the memory effects and non-local interactions that are often observed in real-world ecological systems. The analytical results confirmed that the model possesses a unique solution that is both non-negative and bounded, ensuring its biological feasibility. We carried out a detailed local and global stability analysis of the system s equilibrium points. The local stability conditions revealed that the system stabilizes around the coexistence equilibrium under certain parameter constraints. The global stability analysis demonstrated that the system converges to a stable state over time, provided that the conditions on the model parameters are satisfied. The fear effect was shown to reduce the prey population growth rate, which indirectly influences the predator population. On the other hand, the prey refuge mechanism weakened the predator s effective predation rate, creating a stabilizing effect on the prey population. The combination of these two factors significantly influenced the system’s stability behavior.

Through numerical simulations, we illustrated the theoretical findings and visualized the impact of fractional-order derivatives on the system s dynamics. The simulations confirmed that fractional-order models exhibit slower convergence and prolonged transients compared to their integer-order counterparts, reflecting the influence of memory effects on ecological stability. Overall, this work demonstrates that the fractional-order filippov prey-predator model provides a more realistic representation of ecological systems by accounting for memory effects and discontinuous dynamics. The results offer valuable insights for understanding the long-term behavior of predator-prey interactions and can be applied to ecological conservation and management strategies. Future work could extend this model to incorporate multi-species interactions, seasonal variations, or stochastic effects, further enhancing its applicability to real-world ecosystems.

In summary, this study demonstrates that incorporating fractional-order dynamics into a Filippov predator-prey framework with fear and prey refuge reveals more nuanced stability conditions than classical integer-order models. A key insight is that the fractional order parameter θ acts as a bifurcation parameter (Example4, see Fig. 5): lower θ (stronger memory effects) stabilizes the system by damping oscillatory instabilities, while higher θ permits sustained cycles. This suggests that ecological memory such as delayed prey recovery or predator inertia can be a stabilizing force in itself.

From a management perspective, our results suggest that creating or maintaining prey refuge areas (e.g., cover crops, burrows) above a threshold ( m>0.67 in Example 5, see Fig. 6) can stabilize predator-prey systems even under moderate fear effects, reducing the need for direct population control. Conversely, managing predator-induced fear (e.g., through non-lethal deterrents) may be more effective than reducing predation rates alone in systems with strong memory effects (low fractional order θ). These findings provide actionable guidelines for conservation and wildlife management, while the model itself opens avenues for incorporating stochasticity and time delays in future work.

Limitations and future work

Extending integer-order models to the fractional-order domain often uncovers a richer spectrum of system behaviors. However, fractional-order formulations alone may not always capture the full complexity of the dynamics. In such cases, incorporating time delays or stochastic elements becomes essential for achieving a more realistic representation.
