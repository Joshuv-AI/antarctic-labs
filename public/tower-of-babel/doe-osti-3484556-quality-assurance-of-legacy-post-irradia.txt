 ABSTRACT
The U.S. DOE-NE’s Advanced Reactor Technologies (ART) Fast Reactor Program (FRP) and the NE-4
Advanced Fuels Campaign (AFC) have jointly undertaken the qualification of the legacy post-irradiation
examination (PIE) data held in the Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD), covering metallic fuel
experiments conducted in the Experimental Breeder Reactor II (EBR-II) and the Fast Flux Test Facility
(FFTF). In FY26 this effort reached a milestone: six major types of PIE data—contact profilometry,
isotopic gamma scan, fission gas chemistry, fission gas release, laser profilometry, and neutron
radiography—have been qualified for all available experiments in FIPD, and the U.S. nuclear industry can
now draw on them with confidence in licensing activities for metallic fuel-based advanced fast reactors.
This report documents the qualification process and the resulting status of the PIE data.

1 Introduction
 Metallic U(Pu)Zr fuel is the fuel form on which the U.S. nuclear industry has converged for its fast
reactor designs. Its development record reaches back to the 1960s, when the first U(Pu)Zr irradiations
were conducted in the Chicago Pile-5 (CP-5) and the Experimental Breeder Reactor-II (EBR-II). The
effort grew substantially between 1984 and 1994 under the Integral Fast Reactor (IFR) program, which
remains the single largest source of metallic fuel performance data in the United States.
 The IFR program fielded an extensive experimental campaign. Steady-state irradiations were
performed in EBR-II and the Fast Flux Test Facility (FFTF), while the response of irradiated fuel pins to
transient conditions was examined in the Transient Reactor Test (TREAT) facility and in out-of-pile
furnace tests at Argonne's Alpha-Gamma Hot Cell Facility (AGHCF). Together these tests established the
technical basis for qualifying metallic fuel as a viable reactor fuel, and the record they produced were
preserved in a wide variety of media: Argonne technical reports, IFR program documentation, EBR-II
operating and run reports, technical memoranda, post-irradiation examination (PIE) records, post-test
analyses, engineering drawings, qualification reports, and peer-reviewed journal and conference papers.
Measured material property data were additionally consolidated in compilations such as the IFR Metallic
Fuels Handbook.
 Recognizing that this material would be difficult to use while it remained dispersed across so many
sources, the IFR program began work in the early 1990s on the IFR Materials Information System (IMIS),
an integrated database intended to bring the entire body of information together. The abrupt cancellation
of the program in 1994 left IMIS unfinished. The task of preserving and organizing metallic fuel data was
later resumed by the U.S. DOE-NE Advanced Reactor Technologies (ART) Fast Reactor Program (FRP),
which has developed and maintained the ART Fast Reactor Databases for more than a decade. The Fuels
Irradiation & Physics Database (FIPD), which holds the EBR-II and FFTF metallic fuel irradiation and
PIE data, is one of these databases and is the subject of this report. Further information on the databases,
including how to request access, is available at the ART Fast Reactor Databases website
(https://frdb.ne.anl.gov).
 Since FY22, the DOE NE-4 Advanced Fuels Campaign (AFC) has also supported FIPD development
through the incorporation of FFTF metallic fuel experimental data into the database, and beginning in
FY24 the AFC has supported the FIPD data qualification effort as well.
In FY26, the FIPD data qualification effort reached a major milestone: the qualification of six types
of major PIE data—contact profilometry, isotopic gamma scan, fission gas chemistry, fission gas
release, laser profilometry, and neutron radiography—was completed for all available experiments
in FIPD. This report documents that accomplishment. The activities planned for the coming fiscal year
are also reported.

1.1 Motivation
 The United States holds a body of metallic fuel knowledge that no other country has accumulated,
built up over decades of irradiation testing and post-irradiation examination. That knowledge, however,
cannot be carried into a licensing application in the form in which it was originally recorded.
 The obstacle is one of pedigree rather than content. Nearly all of these measurements were made before
ASME NQA-1 defined the quality assurance controls that the U.S. Nuclear Regulatory Commission
(NRC) now expects in a licensing submission, and that apply equally when legacy measurements are used
to validate modern fuel performance codes such as BISON [1]. Until the integrity and reliability of a

 1

dataset have been established through a formal process, it cannot be used to support a safety or
performance case, however sound the underlying measurement may have been.
 The emphasis in metallic fuel data management has therefore moved away from archival preservation
and toward qualification. With support from the U.S. Department of Energy, legacy records are being
evaluated against present-day standards so that what emerges is a body of data able to withstand regulatory
scrutiny.
 This demand is concrete rather than anticipatory. Reactor vendors such as TerraPower, GE-Hitachi,
Oklo, and Advanced Reactor Concepts (ARC), together with national programs such as Nuclear Energy
Advanced Modeling and Simulation (NEAMS), depend on these data to develop and validate the fuel
performance codes used in core design and safety analysis, to establish the safety case for a particular fuel
and reactor design, and ultimately to license their reactors.

1.2 FIPD: Fuels Irradiation & Physics Database
 FIPD [2-6] archives legacy U(Pu)Zr metallic fuel data in structured form, drawn principally from
irradiations in EBR-II and FFTF. Much of its content originates in the IFR program [7], whose
experimental campaign was aimed at qualifying metallic driver fuel for sodium-cooled fast reactors. The
database also carries PIE results obtained at both reactors after that program ended [8-9].
 Its holdings fall into three categories:
 • Fuel pin fabrication data. Nominal design specifications together with the as-built record for
 each pin, covering fuel composition and U-235 enrichment, isotopic content, cladding type, the
 dimensions of the fuel slug and cladding, and smeared density.
 • Operational conditions. Calculated in-reactor histories giving the axial profiles of power,
 temperature, burnup, neutron fluence, and isotopic densities. These were derived with Argonne's
 validated physics and thermal-hydraulics codes—REBUS [10], EBRFLOW [11], RCT [12], and
          SUPERENERGY-II [13-14].
 • Post-irradiation examination (PIE) results. Measurements and analyses including profilometry,
 neutron radiography, fission gas release, and fuel and cladding chemistry, performed for the most
 part in the Hot Fuel Examination Facility (HFEF).
 The breadth of materials and conditions represented is what makes the database useful to the nuclear
industry [15-16], the national laboratories, and universities. Fuel pin compositions span U-xPu-10Zr (x =
0 to 28 wt%) and U-xZr (x = 2 to 14 wt%), clad in 316SS, D9, or HT9 [17]. The irradiations cover peak
linear powers of 10 to 19 kW/ft (33–62 kW/m), peak burnups of 0.5 to 20 at%, and peak cladding
temperatures of 490 to 660 °C.
 Alongside the extensive EBR-II records, FIPD holds PIE data and the corresponding irradiation
conditions for the FFTF experiments [2], among them the IFR-1 [18-19] and the MFF series [20-21]
experiments. A separate “Reactor-Level Documents” section gathers EBR-II operating and quality
assurance material that belongs to no single experiment, and the database is accompanied by a document
library holding raw PIE records, design specifications, and safety reports.
 Much of the metallic fuel data in FIPD is undergoing formal qualification. A dedicated Quality
Assurance Program Plan governs that effort and is the subject of the next section; the present report covers
the qualification of the PIE data.

 1.3 Quality Assurance Program Plan (QAPP)
 Quality assurance for the legacy metallic fuel data is established at the top level by a Quality Assurance
Program Plan (QAPP) written to the American Society of Mechanical Engineers (ASME) Nuclear Quality
Assurance (NQA-1) 2008/2009 Addenda Standard. Argonne submitted the plan in 2019; the U.S. NRC
reviewed and formally accepted it in 2020 [22-23], finding that it meets the requirements of Appendix B
to 10 CFR Part 50 [24]. An NRC audit two years later confirmed that the plan as implemented provides
programmatic controls sufficient for legacy data that applicants intend to use [25].
 The QAPP governs the quality of the data held in FIPD and shapes how the database continues to be
developed. It recognizes three methods by which legacy data may be qualified:

 • QA Program Equivalency: An evaluation to determine if the acquisition, processing, and
 archiving of data were performed in accordance with sound technical and administrative practices
 compliant with NRC requirements.
 • Peer Review: An independent evaluation of data to determine if the originally employed
 methodology was acceptable and if confidence is warranted in the results.
 • Data Corroboration: An analysis to determine if subject matter data can be substantiated through
 comparison with other confirmed parameter values.

 Peer review was selected for this effort after the three options were weighed against what the historical
record actually contains [26]. Program equivalency proved impractical: demonstrating it calls for detailed
contemporaneous documentation—instrument calibration logs, staff training records—that is not
consistently available for measurements made decades ago. "Confirmatory Testing," another method
recommended in the ASME NQA-1 2008 standard, was deliberately left out of the plan following the
NRC's review of the QAPP [24, 27].
 NQA-1 assigns this evaluation [27] to one or more Subject Matter Experts (SMEs) with expertise in
nuclear fuels and materials. Their responsibilities are:

 • Evaluating the original data acquisition process to determine the acceptability of its associated
 uncertainties.
 • Assessing the adequacy of the interpretations and conclusions derived from the data, considering
 the impact of any uncertainties.
 • Forming and documenting a final conclusion on the overall quality and validity of the data.
 • Establishing a formal report that documents the entire peer review activity.

 The procedure that implements this process under the QAPP, together with the reasoning behind the
choice of the peer review method, is described in the Data Qualification Process chapter.

2 Data Qualification Process
 This chapter describes the process by which the legacy data in FIPD are qualified. It covers the
development of the PIE data specifications, which supply the measurement context that the original
records lack, and the workflow that implements the peer review method required by the QAPP. The
qualification of the PIE data reported in the following chapter was carried out according to this process.

2.1 PIE Specification Development
 Legacy PIE records carry very little of the context needed to read them. Manuals and written
procedures were rarely archived alongside the measurements, so the uncertainty attached to a given value
is often nowhere stated, and interpreting a record correctly can demand the judgment of someone who
performed that kind of examination at the time. Without that context a user cannot trace how a
measurement was made, cannot interpret it with confidence, and cannot qualify it. Contact profilometry
illustrates the problem: a trace retrieved from FIPD is of little use unless the top and bottom of the fuel
pin are identified, the axes carry defined scales, and the accuracy of the measurement is stated.
 Argonne addressed this gap by writing a quality assurance supporting document—a specification—
for each type of PIE measurement. A specification sets out how the measurement was performed and the
principles that govern the interpretation of the resulting data, so that the record becomes usable
independently of the individuals who produced it. Industry users have taken a strong interest in these
documents and in the qualified data they accompany, which they rely on for licensing.
 Producing a specification is itself a controlled activity. Each document passes through peer review and
formal approval, and is additionally circulated to industry users, who test it against the way the data are
used in practice. Figure 2.1 traces that route from initial draft to publication and shows where each
specification currently stands.

2.2 PIE Data Qualification Workflow
 The workflow that puts the peer review method into practice is shown in Figure 2.2. It applies the
ASME NQA-1 2008/2009 Addenda standard, as the QAPP requires [24], to the legacy metallic fuel data
held in FIPD.

 Figure 2.2. PIE data qualification workflow.

 Qualification of a dataset begins by retrieving it from the FIPD library, where a record may exist as a
hard copy, as a native digital file, or as a previously digitized file. The supporting documentation is
assembled at the same time: the PIE data specification for that measurement type, described in the
preceding section, together with the QAPP, the instrument manuals in use at the time, experiment loading
diagrams, laboratory memoranda, and any peer-reviewed articles that draw on the data.
 A Subject Matter Expert (SME) then works through the dataset alongside this material, using the
specification as the reference against which accuracy, relevance, and completeness are judged. Whatever
the review turns up — inconsistencies, missing values, outright errors— is recorded in a Data

 5

Qualification Report (DQR). In cases where only part of a dataset can be qualified, the SME states in the
DQR the range over which the data may be used.
 The DQR is a controlled record. It identifies the data and establishes their traceability, describes any
problems found, notes what quality programs had previously been applied, sets out the evaluation
measures used, and states the qualification result. The draft goes to the program QA representative and
the program manager for formal QA review; once the SME has resolved their comments, the approved
DQR is filed in the formal records repository, which completes the internal QA process.
 What reaches the stakeholder is a qualified PIE data package of five components:
 • Data Qualification Report (DQR): The primary document that records the qualification process
 and provides essential information about the data.
 • Measurement Record: Certified copies of the original hard-copy records that have been qualified.
 • Digital Data: Since most PIE data exists in hard-copy form, it is digitized using a specialized
 software, the Fast Reactor Data Digitalization System (FRDDS) [3]. An SME subsequently
 reviews the digitized data to verify its accuracy.
 • PIE Data Specification: This document provides guidance for interpreting the PIE data and
 summarizes QA-relevant information about the measurement.
 • Other QA Documents: These are supporting references cited in the DQR and PIE specification,
 such as facility operating manuals, measurement procedures, relevant publications, and lab
 memos.
 The package is the deliverable. The code, calculations, and working notes the SME generated along
the way are retained, but held separately.
 Industry users apply their own criteria to data quality and raise their own questions about the records
and the documentation. The team answers every comment received and, where warranted, revises the
DQR or other parts of the package. The exchange continues until the industry partner is satisfied and
formally accepts the package for use in its NRC licensing work.
 Qualified metallic fuel datasets can be downloaded directly from FIPD. The complete qualified data
package, including the DQR and all supporting documents, can be obtained through a formal request to
the FIPD team.

3 PIE Data Quality Assurance
 PIE data are one of the most heavily used data types in FIPD, as they provide the direct experimental
evidence of how metallic fuel behaved under irradiation and are therefore central to fuel performance code
validation and licensing applications. This chapter summarizes the PIE data currently available in FIPD
for the EBR-II and FFTF experiments, presents the qualification status of those data, and describes the
additional independent peer review performed at INL.

3.1 PIE Data in FIPD
 The workflow that puts the peer review method into practice is shown in Figure 2.2 It applies the
ASME NQA-1 2008/2009 Addenda standard, as the QAPP requires [24], to the legacy metallic fuel data
held in FIPD.

      3.1.1 EBR-II Data
 The bulk of the EBR-II PIE data in FIPD comes from fuel pins irradiated under the IFR program. At
the fuel pin level the holdings include contact and laser profilometry, fission gas release and chemistry,
neutron radiography, isotopic gamma scans, metallography, and bow and weight measurements; at the
subassembly level they include profilometry and radiographs. A large collection of photographs of
irradiated pins and assemblies accompanies these records.
 Assembling this material has been one of the larger undertakings in the development of FIPD. Records
have been recovered from both Idaho National Laboratory (INL) and Argonne, then reviewed, processed,
and integrated into the database. Recovery continues, but most of the PIE data that has been located is
now processed and accessible in FIPD.
 Seven types of measurement account for most of the interest shown by industry practitioners and by
DOE programs such as NEAMS. Each is introduced below in terms of what the measurement captures
and what it is used to determine.
 • Contact profilometry. Contact profilometry traces record the outer diameter of the fuel pin
 cladding along its length. The resulting cladding strain is a critical indicator of fuel performance,
 as it reveals the extent of the cladding dimensional changes caused by phenomena such as creep,
 void swelling, and fuel-cladding mechanical interaction (FCMI) behavior [35].
 • Isotopic gamma scan. Isotopic and gross gamma scans measure the axial distribution of
 individual isotopes along a fuel pin. From these distributions, the fuel slug length and elongation,
 the location of sodium, and the location of cracks in the fuel slug can be interpreted [36].
 • Fission gas chemistry. Fission gas chemistry data are obtained by puncturing the fuel pin and
 analyzing the released gas with a mass spectrometer, which yields the chemical composition of the
 fission gas.
 • Fission gas release. Fission gas analysis of a punctured pin provides the plenum volume and the
 fission gas pressure [37]. Together with the fission gas chemistry data, these measurements allow
 fuel performance analysts to determine the number of moles of gas in the fuel plenum, a quantity
 that is often compared with the number of moles generated by fission events.
 • Laser profilometry. Laser profilometry traces are taken at multiple angles around the pin and
 measure the same quantity as contact profilometry—the cladding outer diameter—but with higher
 resolution, reduced noise, and a greater number of data points.

 7

 • Metallography. Metallography data consist primarily of optical microscopy (OM) images,
 complemented by a smaller subset of electron probe micro analysis (EPMA) and scanning electron
 microscopy (SEM) data. These data elucidate the microstructure of the metallic fuel, which is a
 key factor in understanding its properties, behavior, and performance under various conditions.
 Metallography is essential for inspecting fuel-cladding chemical interaction (FCCI), phase
 distribution during irradiation, and fission gas bubble development.
 • Neutron radiography. Pin neutron radiographs provide two-dimensional images of the fuel slug.
 From these images, the axial and radial swelling of the fuel and the location of cracks in the fuel
 slug can be interpreted.

3.2 PIE Data Qualification Status
 As of the end of FY26, six types of major PIE data have been qualified for all available
experiments in FIPD: contact profilometry, isotopic gamma scan, fission gas chemistry, fission gas
release, laser profilometry, and neutron radiography. With this milestone, FIPD users can now use these
six types of PIE data with confidence in licensing submittals, fuel performance code validation, and other
modeling and simulation applications, knowing that the data have been qualified in accordance with the
QAPP. Table 3.2 lists the number of qualified fuel pins for each EBR-II and FFTF experiment in FIPD,
broken down by measurement type, for a total of 3,365 qualified fuel pin measurements across 23 EBR-
II experiments and the FFTF IFR-1 and MFF series experiments.
 Users are nonetheless advised to obtain the corresponding Data Qualification Report (DQR) from the
FIPD team before relying on these data in official work. The qualification status recorded here does not
by itself convey the range over which a given dataset was found valid, the uncertainties attached to the
measurement, or the limitations and caveats identified during the review. That information resides in the
DQR, and reading it is necessary for the data to be interpreted and applied correctly.

3.3 Additional Peer Review
 INL was selected to perform an additional peer review. The INL reviewer was not involved in the
original measurements during the IFR program, which ensures an impartial perspective. However, they
possess extensive PIE experience from many years of work at the Hot Fuel Examination Facility (HFEF)
and other relevant sites.
 This combination of impartiality and deep expertise ensures a comprehensive and unbiased evaluation,
strengthening the data's credibility. This supplementary review provides a crucial opportunity to identify
any potential oversights that may have been missed during the internal qualification process,
demonstrating our commitment to maintaining the highest standards of accuracy and thoroughness.
 In FY26, the laser profilometry data of the X441A experiment (10 pins) were submitted to INL for
independent peer review, and the review was completed within the fiscal year. Table 3.3 summarizes the
PIE data packages that have undergone additional peer review from FY24 to the present.

4 Conclusions and Future Activities
 FIPD now holds most of the key data produced by the U.S. metallic fuel experiments, and it has
reached a maturity that makes it immediately useful to fuel behavior analysts, reactor designers, and the
licensing work of companies building fast reactors on U-Zr fuel. TerraPower/GE-Hitachi, Oklo Inc., and
Advanced Reactor Concepts (ARC) are all developing designs that draw on that experience, and several
are already in discussion with the regulator, which places FIPD and the other ART databases on the critical
path of their programs.
 The constraint on that use has been quality assurance rather than content. The IFR-era measurements
that make up most of the archive were not produced under NQA-1 controls, so qualification is a
prerequisite for any licensing use, and its schedule is set by the applications that depend on it.
 For the six major PIE data types, that prerequisite has now been met. As this report has described,
contact profilometry, isotopic gamma scan, fission gas chemistry, fission gas release, laser profilometry,
and neutron radiography have been qualified for every available experiment in FIPD. Users can draw on
these data with confidence in licensing submittals, in fuel performance code validation, and in modeling
and simulation more generally. The associated data qualification reports (DQRs) and their supporting
documentation can be obtained through a formal request to the FIPD team.
 The primary data qualification activity planned for FY27 is to complete the quality assurance of the
metallography data, the one remaining major PIE data type. Metallography is essential for interpreting
fuel-cladding chemical interaction, phase distribution during irradiation, and fission gas bubble
development, and its qualification will complete the quality assurance of all major PIE data types in FIPD.
