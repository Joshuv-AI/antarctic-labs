1.
Introduction

We study positive weak solutions of the following quasilinear critical equation with general nonlinearity

(1.1)

-\Delta_{p}u=g(u)\qquad\text{in }\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},

where
1<p<n
,
\Delta_{p}u=\operatorname{div}(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u)
denotes the
p
-Laplacian, and the nonlinearity
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
satisfies the structural conditions stated in Assumption
1.1
below.
Throughout the manuscript we write

p^{\ast}:=\frac{np}{n-p},\qquad q:=p^{\ast}-1,\qquad\alpha:=\frac{n-p}{p-1},\qquad a:=\frac{n-p}{p},

and

G(s):=\int_{0}^{s}g(t)\,dt.

The qualitative theory of critical elliptic equations traces back to the fundamental work on the Yamabe problem. For the semilinear case
p=2
, the complete classification of entire solutions of

(1.2)

-\Delta u=u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}},\qquad u>0,\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

was obtained by Caffarelli, Gidas and Spruck
[
3
]
. Subsequently, a simplified proof was proposed by Chen and Li
[
6
]
. Earlier contributions under stronger decay assumptions at infinity are due to Obata
[
30
]
and to Gidas, Ni and Nirenberg
[
20
]
.

Building on this Liouville-type theorem, Schoen
[
36
]
(see also
[
37
]
) discovered a striking Harnack inequality for positive solutions of
-\Delta u=u^{\frac{n+2}{n-2}}
in a ball
B_{3R}
:

(1.3)

\Bigl(\sup_{B_{R}}u\Bigr)\Bigl(\inf_{B_{2R}}u\Bigr)\leq C(n)R^{2-n}.

This estimate, together with its associated energy bound, became a cornerstone of the blow-up analysis for critical equations: it provides the basic compactness that underlies the pointwise description of singular solutions, the finite-dimensional reduction arguments, and the refined asymptotics of concentrating sequences; see
[
8
,
9
,
11
,
26
,
27
]
and the references therein. Li and Zhang
[
29
]
later extended (
1.3
) to positive solutions of equations
-\Delta u=g(u)
with a much wider class of nonlinearities, assuming only that the function
s\mapsto s^{-\frac{n+2}{n-2}}g(s)
is nonincreasing on
(0,\infty)
and admits a finite positive limit as
s\to\infty
. Schoen’s Harnack inequality has been extended to fractional equations
by Caffarelli, Jin, Sire and Xiong
[
4
]
, to integral
equations by Jin and Xiong
[
21
]
, and to conformally invariant fully nonlinear equations by Li and Li
[
24
,
25
]
. Their proof, as well as the related classification work in
[
3
,
6
]
, rests on the moving sphere (or moving plane) method, which employs the Kelvin transform to compare the values of a solution on different scales, exploiting the conformal invariance of the equation.

However, the Kelvin transform and hence the moving sphere method are not currently available when the Laplacian is replaced by the
p
-Laplacian with
p\neq 2
, since the equation is no longer conformally invariant. For
p\neq 2
, the absence of a Kelvin transform is a genuine obstruction in the global classification problem.

The purpose of this paper is to establish, for all
1<p<n
, a conditional Harnack inequality under two explicit hypotheses: the bounded-entire classification hypothesis for possible blow-up limits, and a preliminary singular-rate control on normalized necks. The main new point is a
Pohozaev-neck
argument which upgrades the preliminary neck control to the sharp outer-sphere infimum estimate needed for the sup
\times
inf Harnack inequality. This replaces the Kelvin transform and moving-sphere method available in the semilinear conformally invariant case
p=2
.
This idea is usually applied in geometric analysis to analyze the ”bubbling” phenomenon in sequences of maps; see e.g.
[
15
,
17
]
.

We assume the nonlinearity
g
satisfies the following structural conditions.

Assumption 1.1
.

The function
g:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
is continuous and satisfies the following three conditions.

(G1)

There exists a constant
\Lambda>0
such that

(1.4)

0<g(s)\leq\Lambda s^{q}\qquad\hbox{for every }s>0.

This is the global critical upper growth assumption throughout the paper.

(G2)

The function

s\longmapsto s^{-q}g(s)

is nonincreasing on
(0,\infty)
.

(G3)

There exists a constant
L\in(0,\infty)
such that

(1.5)

\lim_{s\to\infty}s^{-q}g(s)=L.

The model case is
g(s)=s^{p^{*}-1}
, the Euler–Lagrange equation for the sharp Sobolev embedding
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\hookrightarrow L^{p^{*}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

The second hypothesis concerns the possible entire blow-up limits. For every
A\in(0,\,\infty]
, define for
s>0
,

(1.6)

g_{A}(s):=\begin{cases}A^{-q}g(As),&A<\infty,\\
Ls^{q},&A=\infty.\end{cases}

Assumption 1.2

(Entire bounded critical classification)
.

Every bounded positive entire weak solution
V
of

(1.7)

-\Delta_{p}V=g_{A}(V)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

is an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble.

For every sequence
\Gamma_{j}\to\infty
and every sequence
v_{j}
arising from the doubling normalization, satisfying the normalization, boundedness, critical growth, convergence-to-bubble, and Pohozaev-sign hypotheses (defined explicitly in the statement of Lemma
3.7
), there exist constants
C_{s}>0
and
R_{s}>1
, independent of
j
, such that

(1.8)

v_{j}(y)\leq C_{s}|y|^{-a}\qquad\text{for }R_{s}\leq|y|\leq 4\Gamma_{j},

where
a=\frac{n-p}{p}
.

The second assumption above is essential for our analysis. Indeed, if it were to fail, one could then extract, after a suitable rescaling around a distant point, another globally bounded entire critical profile. In this sense, the argument constitutes the local-neck counterpart of the preliminary non-sharp decay estimates that are employed in the finite-energy critical
p
-Laplace setting. In the finite-energy regime, such preliminary estimates can be established by means of a doubling argument combined with small tail-energy bounds, as carried out in
[
42
]
.

A closely related sharp condition also appears in the recent work of Ciraolo–Gatti
[
13
]
. For the pure critical equation

-\Delta_{p}u=u^{p^{*}-1}
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, they assume that the infimum over large balls has the fundamental
p
-harmonic decay rate,
namely

\inf_{B_{R}}u\leq CR^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}},\qquad R\geq 1,

and use this hypothesis to obtain global integrability estimates and, under
boundedness or
L^{q}
-integrability with
q\geq p^{*}
, classification as a

p
-bubble. In the present paper the analogous sharp estimate is not assumed
on the original solution; rather, the Pohozaev neck lemma upgrades the
preliminary normalized neck control
v_{j}\lesssim|y|^{-(n-p)/p}
to the sharp
outer-sphere estimate

\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\lesssim\Gamma_{j}^{-\frac{n-p}{p-1}}.

Thus our neck argument can be viewed as a local blow-up analogue of the sharp infimum behavior used by Ciraolo–Gatti.

Theorem 1.3

(Harnack inequality)
.

Suppose that
g
satisfies Assumption
1.1
and that Assumption
1.2
holds. Then there exists a constant
C
, depending only on the data in the structural and compactness assumptions, such that for any
R>0
and any positive weak solution
u
of
-\Delta_{p}u=g(u)
in
B_{3R}
with
\sup_{B_{R}}u\geq 1
, we have

(1.9)

\Bigl(\sup_{B_{R}}u\Bigr)\Bigl(\inf_{B_{2R}}u\Bigr)^{p-1}\leq C\,R^{\,p-n}.

Remark 1.4
.

When
p=2
, the exponent
p-1=1
and (
1.9
) reduces precisely to the classical sup
\times
inf form (
1.3
) of Schoen and Li–Zhang.

Assumption
1.2
should be understood as a classification hypothesis for the possible entire blow-up limits. It does not claim that the original nonlinearity
g
is globally a pure power. Indeed, if for some

A\in(0,\infty]
a bounded positive entire solution
V
of

-\Delta_{p}V=g_{A}(V)\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

is classified as an Aubin–Talenti bubble, then substituting the explicit bubble
profile into the equation shows that
g_{A}(s)=L_{A}s^{q}
on the range

0<s\leq\max_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V

of that particular solution, for some constant
L_{A}>0
. This is not a global structural
conclusion on
g
.

Here an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble means a function of the form

(1.10)

U_{\lambda,x_{0},\mu}(x)=A_{n,p,\mu}\left(\frac{\lambda^{1/(p-1)}}{\lambda^{p/(p-1)}+|x-x_{0}|^{p/(p-1)}}\right)^{(n-p)/p},

where
\lambda>0
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
\mu>0
, and
A_{n,p,\mu}>0
is chosen so that

-\Delta_{p}U_{\lambda,x_{0},\mu}=\mu U_{\lambda,x_{0},\mu}^{q}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

When
\mu=L
, this is the bubble corresponding to the limiting equation

g_{\infty}(s)=Ls^{q}
. The exact value of
A_{n,p,L}
is not used. If
U
is a bubble, then there exists a positive constant
\kappa
such that

(1.11)

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{\alpha}U(x)=\kappa.

In the semilinear case
p=2
, Li and Zhang
[
29
]
proved exactly this
statement under the monotonicity condition (G2) and local boundedness of
g
; see also
[
1
,
8
]
for related classification results under some additional conditions.
For
p\neq 2
, the pure-power equation

(1.12)

-\Delta_{p}V=LV^{q}

has been classified in several ranges.
Catino–Monticelli–Roncoroni explicitly discuss the critical
p
-Laplace classification problem, including
infinite-energy solutions and additional growth hypotheses; see
[
5
]
. They introduced an integral identity method
and obtained the full classification when
n=2
or
n=3
with
3/2<p<2
.
Crucially for the present work, they also proved
[
5
, Corollary 1.5]
that
every positive
bounded
weak solution of (
1.12
)
is an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble, provided
n\leq 6
, or
n\geq 7
and
p>n/3
.
The finite-energy classification is available from the
D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
theory; see

[
16
,
38
,
42
]
. We also mention that, for positive weak solutions,
Ou
[
31
]
extended the classification to
p>(n+1)/3
for
n\geq 3
without any further assumptions,
and Vétois
[
43
]
further slightly improved the range to
p>p_{n}

with
p_{n}\in(\frac{n}{3},\frac{n+1}{3})
for
n\geq 4
.
In the recent work
[
13
]
, Ciraolo and Gatti classified all positive bounded weak solutions under the assumption that the infimum of the solution behaves properly at infinity.
Ciraolo, Figalli and Roncoroni
[
14
]
classified positive
D^{1,p}
-solutions of critical anisotropic
p
-Laplacian equations in convex cones. More recently,
[
12
]
removed the finite-energy assumption in
[
14
]
in the range
\frac{n+1}{3}<p<n
.
Saintier’s blow-up theory provides
related
H^{1}_{p}
decompositions and pointwise estimates in compact-manifold settings; see
[
33
]
.

For a general nonlinearity
g
, however, the finite-
A
blow-up equations

-\Delta_{p}V=g_{A}(V),\qquad 0<A<\infty,

need not be pure-power equations a priori. Thus Assumption
1.2

is retained as an explicit conditional hypothesis. Nevertheless, in the pure-power case
g(s)=Ls^{q}
, all the rescaled nonlinearities satisfy
g_{A}(s)=Ls^{q}
, and the above known classification results in
[
5
,
31
,
43
]
make the theorem unconditional in their respective parameter ranges. Outside these ranges, the present theorem reduces the Harnack inequality to two
separate inputs: the classification of bounded entire blow-up limits and the preliminary singular neck control in Assumption
1.2
.

Remark 1.5
.

In the limiting case
p=n
, the critical Sobolev embedding is of exponential type, governed by the Moser–Trudinger inequality. The model equation becomes the

-Liouville equation
-\Delta_{n}u=e^{u}
. For this problem the appropriate form of the Harnack inequality is replaced by a
\sup+\inf
estimate. This type of estimate was first proved for

by Shafrir
[

]
, and was subsequently extended by Brezis, Li and Shafrir
[

]
, Chen and Lin
[

,

]
, Li
[

]
, and Chen and Li

]
. For general

, the

-type inequality for solutions of

by using the classification result in

.

Remark 1.6

(Energy-bounded version)
.

Assume, in addition to the structural hypotheses, that

g(s)\equiv Ls^{q}\qquad\text{for all }s>0.

Then there is a useful unconditional variant of Theorem
1.3
if one
restricts to solutions with a scale-invariant critical energy bound and power nonlinearity
g(s)=Ls^{q}
. More precisely,
assume that Assumption
1.1
holds and let
E>0
. If
u>0
solves

-\Delta_{p}u=Lu^{q}\qquad\text{in }B_{3R},

and

\int_{B_{3R}}u^{p^{\ast}}\,dx\leq E,

then the same conclusion as in Theorem
1.3
holds, with a constant
depending also on
E
:

\left(\sup_{B_{R}}u\right)\left(\inf_{B_{2R}}u\right)^{p-1}\leq C(n,p,\Lambda,L,g,E)R^{p-n}.

Indeed, in the contradiction argument one rescales around the doubling-selected point by

v_{j}(y)=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-1}u_{j}(x_{j}+\rho_{j}y),\qquad\rho_{j}=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-p/(n-p)}.

The
L^{p^{\ast}}
-energy is invariant under this critical scaling:

\int_{B_{4\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}^{p^{\ast}}\,dy=\int_{B_{4\sigma_{j}}(x_{j})}u_{j}^{p^{\ast}}\,dx\leq E.

Consequently, via a Caccioppoli test, every entire blow-up limit
V
has finite critical energy. By the
known finite-energy classification for the critical
p
-Laplace equation in

D^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, see

[
16
,
38
,
42
]
,
the limit
V
must be an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble.
Moreover, in the finite-energy regime, the preliminary estimates (
1.8
) can be established by means of a doubling argument combined with small tail-energy bounds, as carried out in
[
42
]
.
Thus the Pohozaev-neck
lemma applies exactly as in the proof of Theorem
1.3
.

This observation also explains precisely where the general case is difficult. If the
energy bound is removed, the same blow-up procedure still produces a bounded
positive entire solution of

-\Delta_{p}V=LV^{p^{\ast}-1}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

in the pure-power case, but it need not have finite energy. Hence the finite-energy classification no longer applies. Therefore, in the present approach, removing the energy bound is equivalent to excluding non-bubble bounded entire blow-up limits of infinite energy. This is exactly the content of the bounded-entire classification hypothesis
Assumption
1.2
.

We now describe the main idea of the proof. The monotonicity of
s^{-q}g(s)
in (G2) of Assumption
1.1
implies the structural sign condition

nG(s)-asg(s)\geq 0,\qquad G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g(t)\,dt,\quad a=\frac{n-p}{p}.

Consequently the Pohozaev functional is nonnegative on balls.
However, the opposite sign appears at isolated
p
-harmonic singularities. If

w(x)=\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}+\beta+o(1),\qquad\beta>0,

then the Pohozaev functional has a strictly negative limiting value. This is the basic sign contradiction.

The neck lemma converts this sign contradiction into a quantitative decay statement,
provided a preliminary singular-rate upper control is available on the normalized neck.
Roughly speaking, the preliminary control gives a uniform annular Harnack inequality at
the first-crossing scale; after rescaling, one obtains a
p
-harmonic singular profile whose
Pohozaev sign contradicts the nonnegative sign inherited from the original equation.

The Harnack inequality then follows by contradiction: a failure of the sup
\times
inf
estimate produces a blow-up sequence, the classification hypothesis identifies the core bubble, the second assumption in Assumption
1.2
supplies the preliminary neck control, and the Pohozaev neck lemma upgrades this to the sharp fundamental-rate decay.

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 fixes notation and recalls elementary facts about the
p
-Laplacian and the Pohozaev functional. Section 3 contains the main analytic ingredients: the Kichenassamy–Véron pole theorem, the Pohozaev identity, the sign computation at isolated singularities, and the Pohozaev-neck lemma. Section 4 proves the Harnack inequality by contradiction and blow-up.

2.
Preliminaries: notations and definitions

Throughout the note,
n
denotes an integer with
n\geq 2
, and
p
denotes a real number satisfying
1<p<n.

The Euclidean norm of
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
is denoted by
|x|
. If
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
r>0
, then

B_{r}(x_{0}):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:|x-x_{0}|<r\}

is the open Euclidean ball of radius
r
and center
x_{0}
. We write

B_{r}:=B_{r}(0).

The boundary of
B_{r}(x_{0})
is denoted by
\partial B_{r}(x_{0})
, and
dS
denotes Euclidean surface measure
on a sphere. If the center is the origin,
\nu=x/|x|
denotes the outward unit normal vector on

\partial B_{r}
. If
u
is differentiable, then

u_{\nu}:=\nabla u\cdot\nu

is its normal derivative on the sphere.

We denote by

\omega_{n-1}:=\mathcal{H}^{n-1}(\mathbb{S}^{n-1})

the surface measure of the unit sphere
\mathbb{S}^{n-1}=\partial B_{1}
.
The critical Sobolev exponent for
W^{1,p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is

p^{*}:=\frac{np}{n-p}.

The exponent appearing in the critical equation is

q:=p^{*}-1=\frac{n(p-1)+p}{n-p}.

The decay exponent of the
p
-harmonic fundamental solution is

\alpha:=\frac{n-p}{p-1}.

Finally, the coefficient which appears in the Pohozaev identity is

a:=\frac{n-p}{p}.

These constants satisfy the useful identity

(2.1)

a=\frac{\alpha(p-1)}{p}.

The
p
-Laplacian is written with the sign convention

\Delta_{p}u:=\operatorname{div}\bigl(|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\bigr),

so that the equation considered below is

-\Delta_{p}u=g(u).

A positive constant denoted by
C
may change from line to line. Dependence of constants is always
recorded when it is important. For example,
C=C(n,p,\Lambda)
means that
C
depends only on the
listed quantities.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be open. A positive weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}u=g(u)\qquad\hbox{in }\Omega

is a function

u\in W^{1,p}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega),\qquad u>0\quad\hbox{a.e. in }\Omega,

such that
g(u)\in L^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\Omega)
and

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\varphi\,dx=\int_{\Omega}g(u)\varphi\,dx

for every function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{1}(\Omega)
.

For any continuous function
h:(0,\infty)\to\mathbb{R}
, we define its primitive by

H(s):=\int_{0}^{s}h(t)\,dt,

whenever the integral is finite. In particular, for the function
g
above we write

(2.2)

G(s):=\int_{0}^{s}g(t)\,dt.

3.
The pole theorem and the neck lemma

The proof uses two known or conjectural inputs: the entire classification Assumption
1.2
and the following isolated
p
-harmonic pole theorem of Kichenassamy–Véron.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
22
, Theorem 1.1, Remark 1.4]
)
.

Let
1<p<n
. Define the
p
-harmonic fundamental profile

(3.1)

\Phi(x):=|x|^{-\alpha}\qquad(x\neq 0).

Let
w
be a positive
p
-harmonic function in
B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, i.e.

-\Delta_{p}w=0\qquad\hbox{weakly in }B_{2}\setminus\{0\}.

Assume that
w/\Phi
is bounded near
0
. Then there exists
\gamma\geq 0
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
such that

w-\gamma\Phi\in L^{\infty}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(B_{2}),\quad w(x)=\gamma\Phi(x)+\beta+o(1)\qquad\text{as }x\to 0.

Moreover, when
\gamma>0
, after shrinking the punctured ball if necessary, the gradient of
w
does not vanish near the singularity, and,

\lim_{x\to 0}|x|^{\alpha+|\zeta|}D^{\zeta}(w-\gamma\Phi)(x)=0

for every multi-index
\zeta
with
|\zeta|\geq 1
, at points where the derivatives are understood classically.
Moreover,

(3.2)

-\Delta_{p}w=\gamma^{p-1}c_{n,p}\delta_{0}\qquad\hbox{in }\mathcal{D}^{\prime}(B_{2}),

where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac mass at
0
, and

c_{n,p}:=\omega_{n-1}\alpha^{p-1}.

The theorem above is the normalized version of the main isotropy theorem in

[
22
, Theorem 1.1]
. Their fundamental solution differs from (
3.1
) by a fixed
normalizing constant. More precisely, since in the present paper we use
\Phi(x)=|x|^{-\alpha}
, the coefficient of the Dirac mass becomes
\omega_{n-1}\alpha^{p-1}
. The existence of the finite limit

\beta=\lim_{x\to 0}(w-\gamma\Phi)

is obtained in their proof by the same comparison argument which gives the boundedness of the regular part. Serrin’s earlier isolated-singularity theory is the precursor of this
input; see
[
34
,
35
]
.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
w>0
be
p
-harmonic in
B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, and suppose that

w(x)=\gamma|x|^{-\alpha}+\beta+z(x)\qquad\text{as }x\to 0,

where
\gamma\geq 0
,
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, and
z(x)\to 0
as
x\to 0
.
Assume moreover that, when
\gamma>0
, the Kichenassamy–Véron weighted derivative asymptotics hold, namely

|x|^{\alpha+1}|\nabla z(x)|\to 0,\qquad|x|^{\alpha+2}|D^{2}z(x)|\to 0\qquad\text{as }x\to 0.

Then

|x|\,|\nabla z(x)|\to 0\qquad\text{as }x\to 0.

Proof.

We first treat the case
\gamma=0
. Then
w=\beta+z
is bounded near the origin. By the removable singularity theorem for bounded
p
-harmonic functions,
w
extends as a
p
-harmonic function across
0
. Hence
w\in C^{1,\theta}
in a smaller ball, for some
\theta\in(0,1)
. Therefore

|x|\,|\nabla z(x)|=|x|\,|\nabla w(x)|\leq|x|\,\|\nabla w\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\to 0.

Thus it remains to consider the case
\gamma>0
.

Set
h(x):=\gamma|x|^{-\alpha}.

Then both
w=h+\beta+z
and
h
are
p
-harmonic in

B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
. We prove the desired estimate by rescaling on fixed
annuli. Let

A_{0}:=B_{2}\setminus\overline{B}_{1/2},\qquad A_{1}:=B_{3/2}\setminus\overline{B}_{2/3}.

For
0<r<1/4
, define

H_{r}(y):=h(ry)=\gamma r^{-\alpha}|y|^{-\alpha},\qquad Z_{r}(y):=z(ry),\qquad y\in A_{0}.

Since
z(x)\to 0
, and since the weighted derivative asymptotics hold, we have

\|Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})}\to 0,

and

r^{\alpha}\|\nabla_{y}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})}+r^{\alpha}\|D_{y}^{2}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})}\to 0\qquad\text{as }r\downarrow 0.

On the other hand,

\nabla_{y}H_{r}(y)=-\alpha\gamma r^{-\alpha}|y|^{-\alpha-2}y.

Hence, on
A_{0}
,

cr^{-\alpha}\leq|\nabla_{y}H_{r}(y)|\leq Cr^{-\alpha},

where
c,C>0
depend only on
n,p,\gamma
. In particular, for all sufficiently
small
r
,

|\nabla_{y}H_{r}(y)+t\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y)|\geq cr^{-\alpha}

for every
y\in A_{0}
and every
t\in[0,1]
.

Let
\mathcal{A}(\xi):=|\xi|^{p-2}\xi.

Since
H_{r}+\beta+Z_{r}=w(ry)
and
H_{r}=h(ry)
are both
p
-harmonic with
respect to the
y
-variable, their weak equations give

\operatorname{div}_{y}\bigl(\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{y}H_{r}+\nabla_{y}Z_{r})-\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{y}H_{r})\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }A_{0}.

By the mean value formula,

\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{y}H_{r}+\nabla_{y}Z_{r})-\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{y}H_{r})=M_{r}(y)\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y),

where

M_{r}(y):=\int_{0}^{1}D\mathcal{A}(\nabla_{y}H_{r}(y)+t\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y))\,dt.

Therefore
Z_{r}
solves the linear divergence-form equation

\operatorname{div}_{y}\bigl(M_{r}(y)\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y)\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }A_{0}.

We now normalize the coefficient matrix by setting

\mathcal{B}_{r}(y):=r^{\alpha(p-2)}M_{r}(y).

Multiplying the coefficient matrix by a positive constant does not change the
equation, so

\operatorname{div}_{y}\bigl(\mathcal{B}_{r}(y)\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y)\bigr)=0\qquad\text{in }A_{0}.

Recall that

D\mathcal{A}(\xi)=|\xi|^{p-2}I+(p-2)|\xi|^{p-4}\xi\otimes\xi.

Since
|\nabla_{y}H_{r}+t\nabla_{y}Z_{r}|
is comparable to
r^{-\alpha}
on
A_{0},

the matrices
\mathcal{B}_{r}
are uniformly elliptic independent of
r
.
Moreover the matrices
\mathcal{B}_{r}
have uniformly bounded
C^{1}
-norm on
A_{0}
.
Indeed,

|D_{y}^{2}H_{r}(y)|\leq Cr^{-\alpha}\qquad\text{on }A_{0},

and

\|D_{y}^{2}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})}=o(r^{-\alpha}).

Since
D^{2}\mathcal{A}(\xi)=O(|\xi|^{p-3})
away from
\xi=0
, we get
\|\mathcal{B}_{r}\|_{C^{1}(A_{0})}\leq C

with
C
independent of
r
.
Thus, the standard interior
C^{1}
-estimate for uniformly elliptic linear divergence-form
equations with
C^{1}
coefficients therefore gives

\|\nabla_{y}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{1})}\leq C\|Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})},

where
C
is independent of
r
. Since
\|Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{0})}\to 0
, we obtain
\|\nabla_{y}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{1})}\to 0.

Finally, let
x\to 0
, put
r=|x|
, and set
y=x/|x|\in S^{n-1}\subset A_{1}
.
Then

|x|\,|\nabla z(x)|=|\nabla_{y}Z_{r}(y)|\leq\|\nabla_{y}Z_{r}\|_{L^{\infty}(A_{1})}\to 0.

This proves the corollary.
∎

3.1.
The Pohozaev identity and the structural sign

We first prove an algebraic sign coming from Assumption
1.1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g
satisfy Assumption
1.1
, and let
G
be defined by (
2.2
). Then

(3.3)

nG(s)-a\,sg(s)\geq 0\qquad\hbox{for every }s>0.

Proof.

Fix
s>0
. Since
t\mapsto t^{-q}g(t)
is nonincreasing, for every
t\in(0,s)
we have

\frac{g(t)}{t^{q}}\geq\frac{g(s)}{s^{q}}.

Therefore

G(s)=\int_{0}^{s}g(t)\,dt\geq\frac{g(s)}{s^{q}}\int_{0}^{s}t^{q}\,dt=\frac{sg(s)}{q+1}.

Since
q+1=p^{*}
, this gives

G(s)\geq\frac{sg(s)}{p^{*}}.

Multiplying by
n
and using

\frac{n}{p^{*}}=\frac{n-p}{p}=a,

we obtain (
3.3
).
∎

Proposition 3.4

(Pohozaev identity)
.

Let
f:(0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
be continuous, and define

F_{f}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f(t)\,dt.

Let
u>0
be a regular solution of

(3.4)

-\Delta_{p}u=f(u)\qquad\hbox{in }B_{r}.

Then

(3.5)

P_{u}(r)=\int_{B_{r}}\left[nF_{f}(u)-a\,uf(u)\right]dx,

where

P_{u}(r):=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left[a\,u|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{\nu}-\frac{r}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+r|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{\nu}^{2}+rF_{f}(u)\right]dS.

The same identity holds for weak solutions by the standard approximation argument.

Proof.

Set

A:=|\nabla u|^{p-2}\nabla u.

The equation (
3.4
) is

-\operatorname{div}A=f(u).

Multiplying by
x\cdot\nabla u
and integrating over
B_{r}
gives

(3.6)

\int_{B_{r}}f(u)x\cdot\nabla u\,dx=-\int_{B_{r}}(\operatorname{div}A)(x\cdot\nabla u)\,dx.

The right-hand side is

(3.7)

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{r}}(\operatorname{div}A)(x\cdot\nabla u)

\displaystyle=-\int_{\partial B_{r}}(A\cdot\nu)(x\cdot\nabla u)\,dS+\int_{B_{r}}A\cdot\nabla(x\cdot\nabla u)\,dx.

Since

A\cdot\nabla(x\cdot\nabla u)=|\nabla u|^{p}+x\cdot\nabla\left(\frac{|\nabla u|^{p}}{p}\right),

we get

(3.8)

\int_{B_{r}}A\cdot\nabla(x\cdot\nabla u)=\frac{p-n}{p}\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx+\frac{r}{p}\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dS.

On the other hand,

(3.9)

\int_{B_{r}}f(u)x\cdot\nabla u\,dx=\int_{B_{r}}x\cdot\nabla F_{f}(u)\,dx=r\int_{\partial B_{r}}F_{f}(u)\,dS-n\int_{B_{r}}F_{f}(u)\,dx.

Finally, testing the equation with
u
gives

(3.10)

\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{p}\,dx=\int_{B_{r}}uf(u)\,dx+\int_{\partial B_{r}}u|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{\nu}\,dS.

Combining (
3.6
)–(
3.10
), and using
x\cdot\nu=r
and

x\cdot\nabla u=ru_{\nu}
on
\partial B_{r}
, gives (
3.5
).
∎

Now we use the following consequence of the pole analysis, which gives a key observation in the current manuscript. The essential part is that a positive regular part of the solution forces the pole Pohozaev value to be negative.

Proposition 3.5

(Pohozaev value at a pole)
.

Let
w
be as in Theorem
3.1
. Suppose that

(3.11)

w(x)=\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}+\beta+o(1)\qquad\text{as }x\to 0,

with
\kappa>0
and
\beta>0
, and suppose that the derivative asymptotics
from Theorem
3.1
hold. Define, for
0<r<1
,

(3.12)

\mathcal{P}_{w}(r):=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left[a\,w|\nabla w|^{p-2}w_{\nu}-\frac{r}{p}|\nabla w|^{p}+r|\nabla w|^{p-2}w_{\nu}^{2}\right]dS.

Then
\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)
is independent of
r
, and

\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)=-a\,\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}\beta<0\qquad\text{for every }0<r<1.

Proof.

First we prove that
\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)
is independent of
r
. Let

0<r_{1}<r_{2}<1
. Applying the Pohozaev identity Proposition
3.4
in the annulus

B_{r_{2}}\setminus\overline{B_{r_{1}}}
, where
w
is
p
-harmonic, gives

P_{w}(r_{2})-P_{w}(r_{1})=0.

Hence
\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)=P_{w}(r)
is constant.

It remains to compute this constant. Put
h(x):=\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}.

Then on
\partial B_{r}
,

h_{\nu}=-\alpha\kappa r^{-\alpha-1},\qquad|\nabla h|=\alpha\kappa r^{-\alpha-1}.

The pure pole
h
has zero Pohozaev value. Indeed,

h=\frac{r}{\alpha}|\nabla h|\qquad\text{on }\partial B_{r},

and therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h}(r)

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left[ah|\nabla h|^{p-2}h_{\nu}-\frac{r}{p}|\nabla h|^{p}+r|\nabla h|^{p-2}h_{\nu}^{2}\right]\,dS

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left[-ah|\nabla h|^{p-1}+\frac{p-1}{p}r|\nabla h|^{p}\right]\,dS

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left[-\frac{a}{\alpha}r|\nabla h|^{p}+\frac{p-1}{p}r|\nabla h|^{p}\right]\,dS=0,

since

\frac{a}{\alpha}=\frac{p-1}{p}.

Now we consider the model function
h+\beta
. Since
\nabla(h+\beta)=\nabla h
,
only the first term in the Pohozaev functional changes. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{h+\beta}(r)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{h}(r)+a\beta\int_{\partial B_{r}}|\nabla h|^{p-2}h_{\nu}\,dS

\displaystyle=a\beta\int_{\partial B_{r}}-(\alpha\kappa r^{-\alpha-1})^{p-1}\,dS

\displaystyle=-a\beta(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}r^{n-1-(\alpha+1)(p-1)}\omega_{n-1}.

Since
(\alpha+1)(p-1)=n-1,

we obtain

\mathcal{P}_{h+\beta}(r)=-a\,\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}\beta.

We now justify the passage from the model function
h+\beta
to the actual function
w
. Write

\eta(x):=w(x)-h(x)-\beta.

By the pole expansion in Theorem
3.1
together with Corollary
3.2
,

\omega(r):=\sup_{\partial B_{r}}|\eta|+\sup_{\partial B_{r}}r|\nabla\eta|\to 0\qquad\text{as }r\to 0.

Set
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}:=\alpha\kappa r^{-\alpha-1}.

Then on
\partial B_{r}
,

\nabla h=-{\mathcal{C}}_{r}\nu,\qquad h=\kappa r^{-\alpha}=\frac{r}{\alpha}{\mathcal{C}}_{r}.

In particular, for
r
small enough,
|\nabla w|
is comparable to
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
.

Let

\mathcal{I}_{r}(u):=au|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{\nu}-\frac{r}{p}|\nabla u|^{p}+r|\nabla u|^{p-2}u_{\nu}^{2}

denote the boundary integrand in the
p
-harmonic Pohozaev functional.
We claim that

\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{r}(w)-\mathcal{I}_{r}(h+\beta)\right|\,dS\longrightarrow 0.

Indeed, since the vectors
\nabla w
and
\nabla h
both have size comparable to
{\mathcal{C}}_{r}
, the
C^{1}
-bounds for the maps

\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p-2}\xi\cdot\nu,\qquad\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p},\qquad\xi\mapsto|\xi|^{p-2}(\xi\cdot\nu)^{2}

give, uniformly on
\partial B_{r}
,

\displaystyle\bigl||\nabla w|^{p-2}w_{\nu}-|\nabla h|^{p-2}h_{\nu}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-2}|\nabla\eta|,

\displaystyle\bigl||\nabla w|^{p}-|\nabla h|^{p}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}|\nabla\eta|,

\displaystyle\bigl||\nabla w|^{p-2}w_{\nu}^{2}-|\nabla h|^{p-2}h_{\nu}^{2}\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq C{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}|\nabla\eta|.

Therefore,

\left|\mathcal{I}_{r}(w)-\mathcal{I}_{r}(h+\beta)\right|\leq C|\eta|{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}+C(h+|\beta|){\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-2}|\nabla\eta|+Cr{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}|\nabla\eta|.

Using
h=(r/\alpha){\mathcal{C}}_{r}
, this becomes

\left|\mathcal{I}_{r}(w)-\mathcal{I}_{r}(h+\beta)\right|\leq C|\eta|{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}+Cr{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-1}|\nabla\eta|+C|\beta|{\mathcal{C}}_{r}^{p-2}|\nabla\eta|.

After integration over
\partial B_{r}
, and using

(\alpha+1)(p-1)=n-1,

we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\partial B_{r}}\left|\mathcal{I}_{r}(w)-\mathcal{I}_{r}(h+\beta)\right|\,dS

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\partial B_{r}}|\eta|+C\sup_{\partial B_{r}}r|\nabla\eta|+Cr^{\alpha}\sup_{\partial B_{r}}r|\nabla\eta|

\displaystyle\leq C\omega(r)\to 0.

Hence

\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)-\mathcal{P}_{h+\beta}(r)\to 0\qquad\text{as }r\downarrow 0.

Since we have already computed

\mathcal{P}_{h+\beta}(r)=-a\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}\beta,

it follows that

\lim_{r\downarrow 0}\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)=-a\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}\beta.

Because
\mathcal{P}_{w}(r)
is independent of
r
, the same identity holds for every

0<r<1
.
The right-hand side is strictly negative since
a>0
,
\kappa>0
, and

\beta>0
.

∎

3.2.
The normalized Pohozaev neck lemma

We now prove the key local compactness result.

Remark 3.6
.

Lemma
3.7
below is a conditional neck-improvement lemma. It assumes a preliminary singular-rate upper control

v_{j}(y)\lesssim|y|^{-a},\qquad a=\frac{n-p}{p},

and upgrades it to the sharp
p
-harmonic fundamental rate

\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\lesssim\Gamma_{j}^{-\alpha},\qquad\alpha=\frac{n-p}{p-1}.

The proof does not use Kelvin inversion. The preliminary singular control gives a uniform annular Harnack inequality at the first-crossing scale; the Pohozaev sign argument then rules out slow decay at the fundamental rate.

Lemma 3.7

(Pohozaev neck improvement under preliminary singular control)
.

Let
\Gamma_{j}\to\infty
. For each
j
, let
v_{j}
be a positive weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}v_{j}=f_{j}(v_{j})\qquad\text{in }B_{4\Gamma_{j}}.

Assume the following conditions.

(N1)

Normalization and boundedness:

v_{j}(0)=1,\qquad 0<v_{j}\leq C_{0}\qquad\text{in }B_{4\Gamma_{j}}.

(N2)

Critical upper growth:

(3.13)

0\leq f_{j}(s)\leq\Lambda_{0}s^{q}\qquad\text{for all }s>0.

(N3)

Critical convergence:

(3.14)

f_{j}\to f_{\infty}\qquad\text{locally uniformly on }(0,\infty).

(N4)

Pohozaev sign: if

F_{j}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}f_{j}(t)\,dt,

then

(3.15)

nF_{j}(s)-a\,sf_{j}(s)\geq 0\qquad\text{for every }s>0.

(N5)

Bubble limit:

v_{j}\to U\qquad\text{in }C^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where
U
is an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble solving

-\Delta_{p}U=f_{\infty}(U)\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

(N6)

Preliminary singular upper control: There exist constants
C_{s}>0
and

R_{s}>1
, independent of
j
, such that

(3.16)

v_{j}(y)\leq C_{s}|y|^{-a}\qquad\text{for }R_{s}\leq|y|\leq 4\Gamma_{j}.

Then there exists a constant

C=C(n,p,C_{0},\Lambda_{0},U,C_{s},R_{s})

such that

(3.17)

\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\leq C\Gamma_{j}^{-\alpha}

for all sufficiently large
j
.

Proof.

Let
\kappa>0
be defined by

\kappa:=\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{\alpha}U(x).

Since
U
is an Aubin–Talenti bubble, its explicit profile satisfies

-\Delta_{p}U=L_{U}U^{q}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}

for some constant
L_{U}>0
. Since also

-\Delta_{p}U=f_{\infty}(U),

it follows that

f_{\infty}(U)=L_{U}U^{q}\qquad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We argue by contradiction. Suppose that (
3.17
) fails. Then, after passing to a
subsequence,

(3.18)

\Gamma_{j}^{\alpha}\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\to\infty.

For
r>0
, define the closed annulus

A_{r}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\ r/2\leq|x|\leq 2r\}

and the annular height

H_{j}(r):=r^{\alpha}\sup_{A_{r}}v_{j}.

We also write

H_{U}(r):=r^{\alpha}\sup_{A_{r}}U.

Since
U
is an Aubin–Talenti bubble and

\lim_{|x|\to\infty}|x|^{\alpha}U(x)=\kappa,

we may choose
R_{0}>2
so large that

S_{U}:=\sup_{r\geq R_{0}}H_{U}(r)<\infty.

By the local uniform convergence
v_{j}\to U
, we have

H_{j}(R_{0})\to H_{U}(R_{0}).

Hence, after increasing
j
if necessary,

H_{j}(R_{0})\leq S_{U}+1.

Set

\mathcal{D}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{1}{3}<|x|<3\right\},\qquad A_{\rm in}:=\left\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:\frac{3}{4}\leq|x|\leq\frac{3}{2}\right\}.

The preliminary singular control will imply below that, on
\mathcal{D}
, the rescaled
functions
w_{j}
solve an equation of the form

-\Delta_{p}w_{j}=b_{j}(x)w_{j}^{p-1},\qquad 0\leq b_{j}\leq B_{s},

with
B_{s}
independent of
j
. Let
C_{\rm Har}
be the Harnack-chain constant in

\mathcal{D}
for such equations, chosen so that

\sup_{A_{1}}h\leq C_{\rm Har}\inf_{A_{\rm in}}h,\qquad A_{1}:=B_{2}\setminus B_{1/2},

for every positive weak solution of

-\Delta_{p}h=b(x)h^{p-1},\qquad 0\leq b\leq B_{s},

in
\mathcal{D}
. Set
c_{H}:=C_{\rm Har}^{-1}
. Choose
K
so large that

K>\max\left\{S_{U}+1,\ 2S_{U},\ \frac{\kappa+2}{c_{H}}\right\}.

Then

H_{j}(R_{0})<K

for all sufficiently large
j
.

By the contradiction assumption (
3.18
),

H_{j}(\Gamma_{j}/2)\geq(\Gamma_{j}/2)^{\alpha}\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\to\infty.

Therefore, by the continuity of
r\mapsto H_{j}(r)
, there exists a first radius

r_{j}\in[R_{0},\Gamma_{j}/2]

such that

(3.19)

H_{j}(r_{j})=K,\qquad H_{j}(r)\leq K\quad\text{for }R_{0}\leq r\leq r_{j}.

We claim that

r_{j}\to\infty.

Indeed, if not, then after passing to a subsequence we would have

r_{j}\to r_{\ast}\in[R_{0},\infty).

Since the annuli
A_{r_{j}}
remain in a fixed compact subset of

\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
, the local uniform convergence
v_{j}\to U
, together with the continuity of
U
, gives

H_{j}(r_{j})\to H_{U}(r_{\ast}).

But
r_{\ast}\geq R_{0}
, so

H_{U}(r_{\ast})\leq S_{U}<K,

contradicting
H_{j}(r_{j})=K
in (
3.19
). Hence
r_{j}\to\infty
.

The first-crossing property implies the pointwise estimate

(3.20)

v_{j}(y)\leq CK|y|^{-\alpha}\qquad\hbox{for }2R_{0}\leq|y|\leq 2r_{j}.

Indeed, if
|y|\leq r_{j}
, choose
r=|y|
in (
3.19
); if
r_{j}<|y|\leq 2r_{j}
, use the annulus

A_{r_{j}}
.

Define the neck rescaling

(3.21)

w_{j}(x):=r_{j}^{\alpha}v_{j}(r_{j}x).

Then
w_{j}
is defined at least in
B_{2}
, and

(3.22)

\sup_{A_{1}}w_{j}=K,\qquad A_{1}=B_{2}\setminus B_{1/2}.

The equation for
w_{j}
is

(3.23)

-\Delta_{p}w_{j}=\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j}),

where

(3.24)

\widetilde{f}_{j}(s):=r_{j}^{n}f_{j}(r_{j}^{-\alpha}s).

To verify (
3.24
), note that

\nabla w_{j}(x)=r_{j}^{\alpha+1}\nabla v_{j}(r_{j}x)

and hence

-\Delta_{p}w_{j}(x)=r_{j}^{\alpha(p-1)+p}f_{j}(v_{j}(r_{j}x)).

Since
\alpha(p-1)=n-p
, this exponent is
n
.

Using (
3.13
), we get

(3.25)

0\leq\widetilde{f}_{j}(s)\leq\Lambda_{0}r_{j}^{n-\alpha q}s^{q}.

The exponent is negative, since

(3.26)

n-\alpha q=n-\frac{n-p}{p-1}\cdot\frac{n(p-1)+p}{n-p}=-\frac{p}{p-1}.

We now prove the annular Harnack estimate. Since
r_{j}\to\infty
, for all
large
j
we have
r_{j}/3\geq R_{s}
. Also, because
r_{j}\leq\Gamma_{j}/2
, one has

3r_{j}<4\Gamma_{j}
. Hence (
3.16
) applies to
r_{j}x
for
every
x\in\mathcal{D}
. Therefore

w_{j}(x)=r_{j}^{\alpha}v_{j}(r_{j}x)\leq C_{s}r_{j}^{\alpha-a}|x|^{-a}\qquad\text{for }x\in\mathcal{D}.

Since
w_{j}>0
, write

-\Delta_{p}w_{j}=b_{j}(x)w_{j}^{p-1},\qquad b_{j}(x):=\frac{\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j}(x))}{w_{j}(x)^{p-1}}.

Using (
3.25
) and the preceding estimate, we obtain on
\mathcal{D}

0\leq b_{j}(x)\leq\Lambda_{0}r_{j}^{-\frac{p}{p-1}}w_{j}(x)^{q-p+1}\leq\Lambda_{0}C_{s}^{q-p+1}|x|^{-a(q-p+1)}\leq B_{s},

because

(\alpha-a)(q-p+1)=\frac{p}{p-1}.

Thus the Harnack-chain inequality in
\mathcal{D}
gives

(3.27)

\inf_{A_{\rm in}}w_{j}\geq c_{H}\sup_{A_{1}}w_{j}=c_{H}K.

On every compact set
K_{0}\Subset B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, the first-crossing estimate
(
3.20
) gives a uniform upper bound for
w_{j}
. Therefore
(
3.25
) and (
3.26
) imply

\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j})\to 0\qquad\text{locally uniformly on compact subsets of }B_{2}\setminus\{0\}.

By the local Harnack inequality and local
C^{1,\beta}
estimates for
p
-Laplace type equations, after
passing to a subsequence,

w_{j}\to w\qquad\hbox{in }C^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(B_{2}\setminus\{0\}),

where

-\Delta_{p}w=0\qquad\hbox{in }B_{2}\setminus\{0\}.

The relevant regularity and Harnack theory for quasilinear equations goes back to Serrin, Trudinger,
Ural’tseva, Lewis, Tolksdorf, and others; see
[
34
,
40
,
23
,
41
,
32
]
.

The estimate (
3.20
) gives

0<w(x)\leq CK|x|^{-\alpha}\qquad\hbox{in }B_{2}\setminus\{0\}.

Thus, we can apply the Kichenassamy–Véron pole theorem, namely Theorem
3.1
.

We identify the coefficient of the pole. For
0<\rho<1
, integrating (
3.23
) over
B_{\rho}
yields

(3.28)

-\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla w_{j}|^{p-2}\partial_{\nu}w_{j}\,dS=\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j})\,dx.

Moreover, (
3.21
) and (
3.24
) give

(3.29)

\int_{B_{\rho}}\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j})\,dx=\int_{B_{\rho r_{j}}}f_{j}(v_{j})\,dy.

Let
R>2R_{0}
be fixed. The convergence
v_{j}\to U
and
(
3.14
) imply

\int_{B_{R}}f_{j}(v_{j})\,dy\to\int_{B_{R}}L_{U}U^{p^{*}-1}\,dy.

For
R\leq|y|\leq\rho r_{j}
, the first-crossing estimate
(
3.20
) gives

v_{j}(y)\leq CK|y|^{-\alpha}.

Therefore, by (
3.13
),

f_{j}(v_{j}(y))\leq C|y|^{-\alpha q}.

Since

\alpha q=n+\frac{p}{p-1}>n,

the right-hand side is integrable at infinity. Hence, after first letting
j\to\infty

and then
R\to\infty
,

\int_{B_{\rho r_{j}}}f_{j}(v_{j})\,dy\to\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{U}U^{p^{*}-1}\,dy.

Passing to the limit in (
3.28
), we obtain

-\int_{\partial B_{\rho}}|\nabla w|^{p-2}w_{\nu}\,dS=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{U}U^{p^{*}-1}\,dy.

The bubble tail (
1.11
) gives

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}L_{U}U^{p^{*}-1}\,dy=\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}.

Therefore the pole coefficient of
w
is exactly
\kappa
, and

w(x)=\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}+z(x),\qquad z\in L^{\infty}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(B_{2}).

We next prove that the regular part is positive. By the buffered annular Harnack
estimate (
3.27
),

\inf_{A_{\rm in}}w_{j}\geq c_{H}K.

Since
A_{\rm in}\Subset B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, the convergence

w_{j}\to w
in
C^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(B_{2}\setminus\{0\})
gives

\inf_{A_{\rm in}}w\geq c_{H}K.

In particular, because
\partial B_{1}\subset A_{\rm in}
, the choice of
K
gives

(3.30)

w\geq\kappa+2\qquad\text{on }\partial B_{1}.

For
0<\delta<\kappa
, define

(3.31)

\phi_{\delta}(x):=(\kappa-\delta)|x|^{-\alpha}+1.

Both
w
and
\phi_{\delta}
are
p
-harmonic in
B_{1}\setminus\{0\}
. By
(
3.30
),
w\geq\phi_{\delta}
on
\partial B_{1}
.
Near
0
, using (
3.2
),

w-\phi_{\delta}=\delta|x|^{-\alpha}+z(x)-1>0

for
|x|
sufficiently small. The comparison principle in
B_{1}\setminus B_{\varepsilon}
,
followed by letting
\varepsilon\downarrow 0
, gives

w\geq\phi_{\delta}\qquad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

Letting
\delta\downarrow 0
, we obtain

z(x)=w(x)-\kappa|x|^{-\alpha}\geq 1\qquad\text{in }B_{1}\setminus\{0\}.

In particular,

\beta:=\lim_{x\to 0}z(x)\geq 1,

and the regular part is positive at the pole in the sense of
Proposition
3.5
.

Since
w
is
p
-harmonic in
B_{2}\setminus\{0\}
, Proposition
3.5
gives

\mathcal{P}_{w}(1)=-a\,\omega_{n-1}(\alpha\kappa)^{p-1}\beta<0.

On the other hand, the Pohozaev sign for
w_{j}
gives the opposite inequality. Indeed, define

\widetilde{F}_{j}(s):=\int_{0}^{s}\widetilde{f}_{j}(t)\,dt.

The sign (
3.15
) is preserved by the scaling (
3.24
); hence

n\widetilde{F}_{j}(s)-a\,s\widetilde{f}_{j}(s)\geq 0.

By Proposition
3.4
,

P_{w_{j}}(1)=\int_{B_{1}}\left[n\widetilde{F}_{j}(w_{j})-a\,w_{j}\widetilde{f}_{j}(w_{j})\right]dx\geq 0.

On
\partial B_{1}
,
w_{j}\to w
in
C^{1}
, and by (
3.25
)–(
3.26
),

\widetilde{F}_{j}(w_{j})\to 0\qquad\hbox{uniformly on }\partial B_{1}.

Thus

\mathcal{P}_{w}(1)=\lim_{j\to\infty}P_{w_{j}}(1)\geq 0.

This contradicts Proposition
3.5
. Therefore (
3.18
) is impossible, and the proof of
(
3.17
) is complete.
∎

4.
The Harnack theorem

With the neck lemma in hand, we now give the proof of Theorem
1.3
, which was stated in the introduction.

Proof of Theorem
1.3
.

Assume by contradiction that the estimate fails. Then there exist
sequences
R_{j}>0
and positive weak solutions
u_{j}
of
-\Delta_{p}u_{j}=g(u_{j})

in
B_{3R_{j}}
such that

(4.1)

\sup_{B_{R_{j}}}u_{j}\geq 1,\qquad R_{j}^{\,n-p}\,M_{j}\,m_{j}^{p-1}\to+\infty,

where we set

M_{j}:=\sup_{B_{R_{j}}}u_{j},\qquad m_{j}:=\inf_{B_{2R_{j}}}u_{j}.

Define

d(x):=\operatorname{dist}(x,\partial B_{2R_{j}})
for
x\in B_{2R_{j}}
.
Choose
x_{j}\in B_{2R_{j}}
such that

(4.2)

d(x_{j})^{n-p}u_{j}(x_{j})=\max_{B_{2R_{j}}}d(x)^{n-p}u_{j}(x).

Set

\widetilde{M}_{j}:=u_{j}(x_{j}),\quad d_{j}:=d(x_{j}),\quad\sigma_{j}:=\frac{d_{j}}{8}.

Since
d(x)\geq R_{j}
for
x\in B_{R_{j}}
, (
4.2
) yields

(4.3)

\widetilde{M}_{j}\,d_{j}^{\,n-p}\geq R_{j}^{\,n-p}M_{j}.

If
y\in B_{4\sigma_{j}}(x_{j})=B_{d_{j}/2}(x_{j})
, then
d(y)\geq d_{j}/2
. Hence, by
(
4.2
),

u_{j}(y)\leq 2^{n-p}\widetilde{M}_{j}\qquad\hbox{for }y\in B_{4\sigma_{j}}(x_{j}).

Combining (
4.1
) with (
4.3
), we also obtain

(4.4)

\widetilde{M}_{j}\sigma_{j}^{n-p}m_{j}^{p-1}\to\infty.

Define the critical blow-up scale

\rho_{j}:=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-\frac{p}{n-p}},\qquad\Gamma_{j}:=\frac{\sigma_{j}}{\rho_{j}}=\sigma_{j}\,\widetilde{M}_{j}^{\frac{p}{n-p}}.

Then
\Gamma_{j}\to\infty
. Indeed,

\Gamma_{j}^{n-p}=\sigma_{j}^{n-p}\widetilde{M}_{j}^{p}=\left(\widetilde{M}_{j}\sigma_{j}^{n-p}m_{j}^{p-1}\right)\left(\frac{\widetilde{M}_{j}}{m_{j}}\right)^{p-1},

and the first factor tends to infinity by (
4.4
), while the second is at least
1
.

Now, define the rescaled functions

(4.5)

v_{j}(y):=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-1}\,u_{j}(x_{j}+\rho_{j}y),\qquad|y|<4\Gamma_{j}.

Then

v_{j}(0)=1,\quad 0<v_{j}(y)\leq 2^{n-p}\quad\text{in}\ B_{4\Gamma_{j}}.

Moreover,

(4.6)

-\Delta_{p}v_{j}=f_{j}(v_{j}),

where

f_{j}(s):=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-q}\,g(\widetilde{M}_{j}s).

The global upper bound (
1.4
) gives

f_{j}(s)\leq\Lambda s^{q}.

Passing to a subsequence we may assume that

A:=\lim_{j\to\infty}\widetilde{M}_{j}\in[2^{p-n},\infty]
. Note that
A\geq 2^{-(n-p)}>0
because
M_{j}\geq 1
and

\widetilde{M}_{j}\geq 2^{-(n-p)}M_{j}
by (
4.3
) and
d_{j}\leq 2R_{j}
. By local compactness and Harnack, after passing to a subsequence,

v_{j}\to V\qquad\hbox{in }C^{1}_{{\mathop{\mathrm{}}{\,loc\,}}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),

where

-\Delta_{p}V=g_{A}(V)\qquad\hbox{in }\mathbb{R}^{n},

with

0<V\leq 2^{n-p},\qquad V(0)=1.

Here the nonlinearity
g_{A}(s)
is defined by (
1.6
), with the constant
L
taken from (
1.5
).

By the classification hypothesis stated in Assumption
1.2
,

V
is an Aubin–Talenti
p
-bubble, and substituting its explicit form into the
equation yields
g_{A}(s)=L_{A}s^{q}
on the range
0<s\leq\max_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}V
.

Note that

f_{j}(s)\to g_{A}(s)\qquad\hbox{locally uniformly for }s>0.

The monotonicity of
s^{-q}g(s)
gives the Pohozaev sign for
f_{j}
by
Lemma
3.3
. By the second assumption in Assumption
1.2
, this normalized
blow-up neck sequence also satisfies the preliminary singular control (
3.16
). Therefore Lemma
3.7
applies and yields

(4.7)

\inf_{\partial B_{\Gamma_{j}}}v_{j}\leq C\,\Gamma_{j}^{-\alpha}.

For
|y|=\Gamma_{j}
, the point
x_{j}+\rho_{j}y\in B_{\sigma_{j}}(x_{j})\subset B_{2R_{j}}
.
Consequently,

(4.8)

v_{j}(y)=\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-1}\,u_{j}(x_{j}+\rho_{j}y)\geq\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-1}\,m_{j}.

Combining this with (
4.7
) gives

\frac{m_{j}}{\widetilde{M}_{j}}\leq C\,\Gamma_{j}^{-\alpha}.

Using
\Gamma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\,\widetilde{M}_{j}^{p/(n-p)}
and

\alpha=(n-p)/(p-1)
, we get

m_{j}\leq C\,\sigma_{j}^{-\alpha}\,\widetilde{M}_{j}^{-\frac{1}{p-1}}.

Therefore

\widetilde{M}_{j}\sigma_{j}^{n-p}\,m_{j}^{p-1}\leq C,

which contradicts (
4.4
).
The contradiction shows that the original assumption was false, and the Harnack
inequality
holds for every
R>0
and every positive weak solution
u
in
B_{3R}

with
\sup_{B_{R}}u\geq 1
. The proof is complete.
∎
