Degenerate Bifurcations and Universal Relaxation Scaling in Black Hole Thermodynamics
Bidyut Hazarika 1, Mozib Bin Awal 1, Prabwal Phukon 1 , 2

Abstract
We present a dynamical systems approach to black hole thermodynamic criticality based on bifurcation equations. We construct an effective thermodynamic landscape in which black holes relax toward equilibrium fixed points. To describe this process, we introduce a flow parameter τ \tau , interpreted as a phenomenological relaxation time, which governs the approach toward equilibrium configurations in thermodynamic state space. Near critical points, the thermodynamic flow simplifies into universal mathematical forms, which allows different black holes to be grouped into different universality classes based on their critical behavior. Our analysis further shows critical slowing down, with relaxation timescales determined entirely by the local bifurcation structure.

In the next section, we investigate the critical slowing-down behavior associated with thermodynamic bifurcations in black holes. To illustrate the generality of the framework, we consider four representative systems: Schwarzschild-AdS, RN-AdS, Euler–Heisenberg-AdS, and 6D Gauss-Bonnet-AdS black holes. These examples are specifically chosen because they exhibit different bifurcation structures and distinct numbers of fixed points and half-stable points, as summarized in Table

We show that these differences naturally lead to distinct critical slowing-down exponents governed entirely by the local bifurcation structure near the critical point.

II
Schwarzschild-AdS Black Hole and Critical Slowing Down

We begin with the bifurcation function

\dot{z}=F(z,h),

(26)

where
F(z,h)
for the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole is

F(z,h)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-4\pi hz+3z^{2}\right).

(27)

The equilibrium points of the system are obtained from

F(z,h)=0.

(28)

The critical point occurs when two equilibrium branches merge together. Mathematically, this happens when

F(z_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}\Big|_{(z_{c},h_{c})}=0.

(29)

The derivative of the bifurcation function is

\frac{\partial F}{\partial z}=-2\pi h+3z.

(30)

Setting this equal to zero gives

z_{c}=\frac{2\pi h_{c}}{3}.

(31)

Substituting this into the equilibrium equation, we obtain

1-\frac{4\pi^{2}h_{c}^{2}}{3}=0.

(32)

Hence the critical point is

h_{c}=\frac{\sqrt{3}}{2\pi},\qquad z_{c}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{3}}.

(33)

To study the behavior near the critical point, we introduce small deviations

z=z_{c}+\xi,\qquad h=h_{c}+\mu,

(34)

where

\mu=h-h_{c}

(35)

measures the distance from the critical point.

Substituting these into the bifurcation equation and expanding near the critical point, the leading-order dynamics becomes

\dot{\xi}=F_{h}|_{c}\,\mu+\frac{1}{2}F_{zz}|_{c}\,\xi^{2}.

(36)

The required derivatives are

F_{h}=-2\pi z,\qquad F_{zz}=3.

(37)

Evaluating them at the critical point,

F_{h}|_{c}=-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}},\qquad F_{zz}|_{c}=3.

(38)

Therefore,

\dot{\xi}=-\frac{2\pi}{\sqrt{3}}\mu+\frac{3}{2}\xi^{2}.

(39)

After rescaling the variables, this equation takes the universal form

\dot{\xi}=\mu+\xi^{2}.

(40)

This is the standard saddle-node bifurcation normal form.

The equilibrium solutions are obtained from

\mu+\xi^{2}=0.

(41)

Thus,

\xi_{*}\sim\mu^{1/2}.

(42)

Next, we perturb the equilibrium solution by writing

\xi=\xi_{*}+\delta.

(43)

Substituting into the bifurcation equation and keeping only linear terms in
\delta
, we obtain

\dot{\delta}=\lambda\delta,

(44)

where

\lambda=\frac{\partial\dot{\xi}}{\partial\xi}\Big|_{\xi_{*}}.

(45)

Using the normal form equation
40
,

\dot{\xi}=\mu+\xi^{2},

(46)

gives

\lambda=2\xi_{*}.

(47)

Since

\xi_{*}\sim\mu^{1/2},

(48)

the eigenvalue behaves as

\lambda\sim\mu^{1/2}.

(49)

The relaxation time is defined as

\tau=\frac{1}{|\lambda|}.

(50)

Therefore,

\tau\sim\mu^{-1/2}.

(51)

Hence, as the critical point is approached
(\mu\to 0)
, the relaxation time diverges. This is the phenomenon of critical slowing down.

III
RN-AdS Black Hole and Higher-Order Criticality

For the RN-AdS black hole, the thermodynamic flow equation is

\dot{z}=F(z,h),

(52)

with bifurcation function

F(z,h)=\frac{1}{2}\left(-1+\frac{Q^{2}}{z^{2}}+4\pi hz-3z^{2}\right).

(53)

The equilibrium configurations are obtained from

F(z,h)=0.

(54)

The critical point is determined from the degeneracy conditions

F(z_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{z}(z_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{zz}(z_{c},h_{c})=0.

(55)

To study the dynamics near criticality, we introduce

z=z_{c}+\zeta,\qquad h=h_{c}+\mu,

(56)

where
\mu
measures the distance from the critical point.

Expanding the flow equation near the critical point, the linear and quadratic terms vanish due to the degeneracy conditions. The leading-order dynamics therefore becomes

\dot{\zeta}=a\mu+b\zeta^{3}.

(57)

After suitable rescaling, this reduces to the universal cusp normal form

\dot{\zeta}=\mu+\zeta^{3}.

(58)

The equilibrium solutions satisfy

\mu+\zeta_{*}^{3}=0,

(59)

which gives

\zeta_{*}\sim\mu^{1/3}.

(60)

Now perturb the equilibrium solution as

\zeta=\zeta_{*}+\delta.

(61)

Substituting into equation
58
and keeping only linear terms in
\delta
, we obtain

\lambda=3\zeta_{*}^{2}.

(62)

Since

zeta_{*}\sim\mu^{1/3},

(63)

the eigenvalue scales as

\lambda\sim\mu^{2/3}.

(64)

Therefore the relaxation timescale behaves as

\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-2/3}.

(65)

Thus, unlike the Schwarzschild-AdS black hole which belongs to the saddle-node bifurcations, the RN-AdS black hole exhibits broken pitchfork bifurcation characterized by a cubic normal form and a different slowing-down exponent.

IV
Euler-Heisenberg Black Hole and Higher-Order Criticality

For the Euler-Heisenberg black hole bifurcation function is

F(z,h)=\frac{1}{8}\left[4\left(-1+\frac{q^{2}}{z^{2}}+4\pi hz-3z^{2}\right)-\frac{\tilde{\alpha}q^{4}}{z^{6}}\right].

(66)

The equilibrium configurations are determined from

F(z,h)=0.

(67)

The critical point is obtained from the higher degeneracy conditions

\displaystyle F(z_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{z}(z_{c},h_{c})=0,

(68)

\displaystyle F_{zz}(z_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{zzz}(z_{c},h_{c})=0.

To study the dynamics near criticality, we introduce

z=z_{c}+\zeta,\qquad h=h_{c}+\mu,

(69)

where
\mu
measures the distance from the critical point.

Expanding the flow equation near the critical point, the linear, quadratic and cubic terms vanish due to the degeneracy conditions. The leading-order dynamics therefore becomes

\dot{\zeta}=a\mu+b\zeta^{4}.

(70)

After suitable rescaling, this reduces to the universal quartic normal form

\dot{\zeta}=\mu+\zeta^{4}.

(71)

The equilibrium solutions satisfy

\mu+\zeta_{*}^{4}=0,

(72)

which gives

\zeta_{*}\sim\mu^{1/4}.

(73)

Now perturb the equilibrium solution as

\zeta=\zeta_{*}+\delta.

(74)

Substituting into Eq. (
71
) and keeping only linear terms in
\delta
, we obtain

\lambda=4\zeta_{*}^{3}.

(75)

Since

\zeta_{*}\sim\mu^{1/4},

(76)

the eigenvalue scales as

\lambda\sim\mu^{3/4}.

(77)

Therefore the relaxation timescale behaves as

\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-3/4}.

(78)

Thus the Euler-Heisenberg black hole exhibits a higher-order thermodynamic bifurcation characterized by a quartic normal form and a distinct critical slowing-down exponent.

V
6D Gauss-Bonnet Black Hole

For the 6D Gauss-Bonnet black hole, the thermodynamic flow equation is

\dot{r}=F(r,h),

(79)

with bifurcation function

F(r,h)=-\frac{-3q^{2}+18r^{6}-32\pi hr^{7}+30r^{8}+36r^{4}\tilde{\alpha}-384\pi hr^{5}\tilde{\alpha}}{32\pi r^{4}}.

(80)

The critical point satisfies

\displaystyle F(r_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{r}(r_{c},h_{c})=0,

(81)

\displaystyle F_{rr}(r_{c},h_{c})=0,\qquad F_{rrr}(r_{c},h_{c})=0.

Expanding near the critical point,

r=r_{c}+\zeta,\qquad h=h_{c}+\mu,

(82)

the leading-order dynamics becomes

\dot{\zeta}=\mu+\zeta^{4}.

(83)

Therefore,

\zeta_{*}\sim\mu^{1/4},\qquad\lambda\sim\mu^{3/4},\qquad\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-3/4}.

(84)

Thus the 6D Gauss-Bonnet black hole belongs to the same quartic universality class as the Euler-Heisenberg black hole.

VI
Conclusion

In this work, we investigated black hole thermodynamic criticality from the perspective of nonlinear dynamical systems and bifurcation theory. By constructing a effective bifurcation equations, we interpreted equilibrium black hole configurations as fixed points of a thermodynamic landscape and introduced a phenomenological flow parameter that governs the relaxation toward equilibrium states.

We showed that different black hole systems shows distinct bifurcations characterized by different orders of degeneracy. The Schwarzschild-AdS black hole displays the standard saddle-node bifurcation with relaxation scaling

\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-1/2},

(85)

while the RN-AdS black hole exhibits a broken pitchfork-type structure with

\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-2/3}.

(86)

For Euler–Heisenberg-AdS and 6D Gauss–Bonnet-AdS black holes, higher-order multifold saddle-node structures emerge, leading to

\tau_{\rm relax}\sim\mu^{-3/4}.

(87)

A larger magnitude of the exponent implies a stronger delay in the relaxation process near criticality. Since the relaxation time diverges faster for larger exponents, higher-order bifurcation structures correspond to increasingly delayed approaches toward equilibrium near the critical point. In this sense, the slowing-down exponent quantifies the degree of critical bottlenecking induced by the local degeneracy of the thermodynamic landscape.

Our results demonstrated that the critical slowing down exponent can be determined entirely by the local bifurcation structure near the critical point. Consequently, distinct black hole systems may belong to the same dynamical universality class despite possessing different global phase structures.
The present framework provides a simple dynamical interpretation of black hole phase transitions, we hope that this approach may offer further insights into universal aspects of gravitational thermodynamics and related dynamical processes.
