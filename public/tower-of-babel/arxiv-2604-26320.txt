On a group ring identity related to the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture
János Nagy, Péter Pál Pach

Abstract
. In this note we formulate a conjecture about two group ring identities and prove that it would imply the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture.



2020 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary. 11B30, 15A03, 15B33, 11B25

1.
Introduction

The Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture
[
2
,
3
]
, first proposed in 1981 is the following:

Conjecture 1

(Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi)
.

For any field
\mathbb{F}
with
|\mathbb{F}|\geq 4
and any nonsingular matrix
M
over
\mathbb{F}
, there is a vector
x
such that both
x
and
Mx
have only nonzero entries.

Alon and Tarsi
[
2
]
reached this conjecture while trying to generalize some simple properties of sparse graphs to more general matroids.
Note that the conjecture is trivial when
\mathbb{F}
is an infinite field. Alon and Tarsi
[
2
]
settled the case when
|\mathbb{F}|\geq 4
is a
proper
prime power using the polynomial method from
[
1
]
. However, the case
\mathbb{F}=\mathbb{F}_{p}
(with
p\geq 5
being a prime) remained open. Recently, we
[
4
]
proved the conjecture for sufficiently large primes, namely, when
61<p\neq 79
.

Our proof made use of group ring identities and
we proposed the following conjecture:

Conjecture 2
.

Let
p>3
be a prime and let
M
be a nonsingular
n\times n
matrix over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
with
row vectors
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
, finally, let the standard basis vectors of
G:=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
be
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
.

Then the
\mathbb{Z}
group ring identity

\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-g^{e_{i}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-g^{a_{i}})=0\in\mathbb{Z}[G]

follows from the mod
p
group ring identity

\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-g^{e_{i}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq i\leq n}(1-g^{a_{i}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

When
p
is sufficiently large (
61<p\neq 79
), in
[
4
]
we could prove both Conjecture
1
and Conjecture
2
. However, the cases when
5\leq p\leq 61
or
p=79
remained open.
In this note we show that the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture would follow from Conjecture
2
in the case of these “small” primes, as well.

Theorem 1
.

For every prime
p>3
Conjecture
2
implies the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture.

2.
Notations

Throughout the paper we use the notation
[n]:=\{1,2,\dots,n\}
and we denote by
[a,b]:=\{k\ |\ a\leq k\leq b,\ k\in\mathbb{Z}\}
the set of integers between
a
and
b
included.

In the paper if we consider a group ring
R[G]
, where
R
is an arbitrary ring and
G=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
for some
n\geq 1
and
v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
is an arbitrary vector, then we use the multiplicative notation as follows. The
group ring

R[G]
is the ring of formal expressions
\sum_{v\in G}r_{v}g^{v}
, where
r_{v}\in R
, and
g
is a formal variable. (Here, the formal exponentiation
g^{v}
is used to turn the additive structure of
G
into a multiplicative one.) Addition and multiplication are defined in the natural way, that is,

\left(\sum_{v\in G}r_{v}g^{v}\right)+\left(\sum_{v\in G}r^{\prime}_{v}g^{v}\right)=\sum_{v\in G}(r_{v}+r_{v}^{\prime})g^{v},

and

\left(\sum_{v\in G}r_{v}g^{v}\right)\cdot\left(\sum_{v\in G}r^{\prime}_{v}g^{v}\right)=\sum_{v\in G}\left(\sum_{w\in G}r_{w}r^{\prime}_{v-w}\right)g^{v}.

3.
Proof of Theorem 1

In this section, we shed light on the structural importance of Conjecture
2
and demonstrate how the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi (AJT) conjecture follows from the proposed equivalence of group ring identities.

Proof of Theorem 1.

For the sake of contradiction, let us assume that Conjecture
2
holds, but the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture fails for some prime
p>3
.

Let
n
be the smallest dimension for which there exists a counterexample to the AJT conjecture, and let
M
be such a nonsingular
n\times n
matrix (over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
) with row vectors
a_{i}
(
i\in[n]
). Let
G:=\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}
denote the underlying additive group.

Based on the polynomial reformulation and the properties of counterexamples to the AJT conjecture
[
4
, Appendix B, Corollary 4]
, we know that the following
\mathbb{Z}
group ring identity must hold:

\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{Z}[G].

Consequently, by considering the coefficients modulo
p
, the corresponding identity in the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
group ring also holds:

\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

(1)

To achieve the desired contradiction through induction on the dimension, we first establish a reduction lemma that allows us to eliminate one of the factors.

Lemma 1
.

Let
i\in[n]
be a fixed index. Under the assumption that
M
is a minimal counterexample, the following identity holds in the
\mathbb{Z}
group ring:

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{Z}[G].

Proof.

Let us write each row vector as
a_{j}=a^{\prime}_{j}+a_{j,i}e_{i}
, where
a^{\prime}_{j}
belongs to the subgroup
G^{\prime}:=\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1},e_{i+1},\dots,e_{n}\rangle\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-1}
.

In the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
, for each
j\in[n]
, the term
(1-g^{a_{j}})
can be expanded as:

1-g^{a_{j}}=(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})+g^{a^{\prime}_{j}}(1-g^{a_{j,i}e_{i}}).

Since
(1-g^{ke_{i}})
is always divisible by
(1-g^{e_{i}})
in the group ring, we can write
1-g^{a_{j}}=(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})+(1-g^{e_{i}})\cdot y_{j}
for some elements
y_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
.

Substituting these expressions into the mod
p
identity (
1
), we obtain:

B:=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}((1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})+(1-g^{e_{i}})\cdot y_{j})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

The element
B
can be viewed as a polynomial in the variable
(1-g^{e_{i}})
. Since
(1-g^{e_{i}})^{p}=1-g^{pe_{i}}=0
in
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
, we can write:

0=B=\sum_{1\leq k\leq p-1}b_{k}(1-g^{e_{i}})^{k},

where each coefficient
b_{k}
lies in the group ring
\mathbb{F}_{p}[G^{\prime}]
.

By examining the linear term (the coefficient of
(1-g^{e_{i}})^{1}
), and noting that the first product
\prod_{j}(1-g^{e_{j}})
already contains one factor of
(1-g^{e_{i}})
, we find:

b_{1}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G^{\prime}].

Since the expansion of
B
must vanish, the linear independence of the powers
(1-g^{e_{i}})^{k}
(
k=0,1\dots,p-1
) yields, in particular, that
b_{1}=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G^{\prime}]
.

Now, our crucial claim is that this mod
p
identity over
G^{\prime}
implies the corresponding identity over
\mathbb{Z}
:

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{Z}[G^{\prime}].

(2)

Notice that this implication is structurally similar to the statement of Conjecture
2
. However, we cannot obtain it directly, since the vectors
\{a^{\prime}_{j}\}_{j\in[n]}
do not form a basis of the subgroup
G^{\prime}\cong\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n-1}
; indeed, there is one additional vector in this set.

Assume to the contrary that the
\mathbb{Z}
group ring identity does not hold:

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})\neq 0\in\mathbb{Z}[G^{\prime}].

Based on this assumption and our original counterexample
M
to the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture, we will construct a counterexample to Conjecture
2
in a higher-dimensional setting.

By reindexing the rows if necessary, we may assume without loss of generality that the subset
\langle a^{\prime}_{2},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n}\rangle
generates the subgroup
G^{\prime}
.

Let us consider a vector space
V^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{F}_{p}
defined by the following basis vectors:

V^{\prime}=\langle e_{1},\dots,e_{n},e_{j,k}\mid j\in[2n],k\in[n],k\neq i\rangle.

Note that the dimension of this space is
\dim(V^{\prime})=2n^{2}-n
.

We shall now construct two specific bases,
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
, for the space
V^{\prime}
.

To simplify our expressions, let us write
v_{j}:=\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n,\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}a^{\prime}_{1,k}e_{j,k}
for
j\in[2n]
, and let
w_{j}:=\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq n}a_{j,k}e_{k}
denote the original row vectors for
j\in[n]
.

First, we define
B_{1}
as the following disjoint union:

B_{1}=B^{\prime}_{1}\cup\bigcup\limits_{1\leq t\leq 2n}B^{\prime}_{1,t},

where the primary part is given by

B^{\prime}_{1}=\left\{e_{j}+v_{j}\ |\ j\in[n]\right\},

and the auxiliary components for each
t\in[2n]
are

B^{\prime}_{1,t}=\{e_{t,k}\mid k\in[n],k\neq i\}.

Similarly, we define the second basis
B_{2}
of
V^{\prime}
as the disjoint union
B_{2}=B^{\prime}_{2}\cup\bigcup\limits_{1\leq t\leq 2n}B^{\prime}_{2,t}
, where the primary components are

B^{\prime}_{2}=\left\{\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq n}a_{j-n,k}e_{k}+v_{j}\ |\ j\in[n+1,2n]\right\},

and the auxiliary parts for each
t\in[2n]
are given by

B^{\prime}_{2,t}=\left\{\sum\limits_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq k\leq n,\\
k\neq i\end{subarray}}a^{\prime}_{\ell,k}e_{t,k}\ \Big|\ \ell\in[2,n]\right\}.

It can be easily checked that
B_{1}
and
B_{2}
are indeed two bases of the vector space
V^{\prime}
.

First, we claim that the following identity holds in the mod
p
group ring:

\prod_{b\in B_{1}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod_{b\in B_{2}}(1-g^{b})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}].

For each index
t\in[2n]
, let us introduce the following notation:

U_{t}:=\prod\limits_{b\in B^{\prime}_{1,t}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod\limits_{b\in B^{\prime}_{2,t}}(1-g^{b}).

By the properties of the vectors
a^{\prime}_{1},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{n},e_{1},\ldots,e_{i-1},e_{i+1},\ldots,e_{n}
, we know that for each
t\in[2n]
, the identity
U_{t}\cdot(1-g^{v_{t}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}]
is satisfied. (This identity indeed holds, since the vector configuration occurring in
U_{t}\cdot(1-g^{v_{t}})
is equivalent to the vector configuration from (
2
).)

Observe that for any
j\in[n]
, we can decompose the terms as:

1-g^{e_{j}+v_{j}}=(1-g^{e_{j}})+(1-g^{v_{j}})z_{j},

where
z_{j}=g^{e_{j}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}]
.

Similarly, for each
j\in[n]
, we can write:

1-g^{w_{j}+v_{j+n}}=(1-g^{w_{j}})+(1-g^{v_{j+n}})r_{j},

where
r_{j}=g^{w_{j}}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}]
.

It follows from these equations and the distributive properties of the group ring that:

\prod_{b\in B_{1}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod_{b\in B_{2}}(1-g^{b})=\prod_{1\leq t\leq 2n}U_{t}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{w_{j}})\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}].

On the other hand, since
M
is a counterexample to the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture, we know that

\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{w_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}],

which indeed yields that

\prod_{b\in B_{1}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod_{b\in B_{2}}(1-g^{b})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[V^{\prime}].

Next, we will prove that, on the other hand, we have:

\prod_{b\in B_{1}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod_{b\in B_{2}}(1-g^{b})\neq 0\in\mathbb{Z}[V^{\prime}].

For this, we have to show that there exists a vector
x\in V^{\prime}
such that
\langle x,b\rangle\neq 0
for every
b\in B_{1}\cup B_{2}
, where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
denotes the standard scalar product corresponding to the basis vectors
e_{1},\dots,e_{n}
and
e_{j,k}
.

Let us fix the values of
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}
in an arbitrary way. We show that we can extend these with values of
x_{j,k}
in a proper way such that the conditions hold.

Indeed, we obtain the following conditions for the values
x_{j,k}
for some
c_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
:

for each
j\in[2n]

x_{j,k}\neq 0\quad\text{for each }k\in[n],\ k\neq i\leq n,

\sum_{k\neq i}a^{\prime}_{\ell,k}x_{j,k}\neq 0\quad\text{for each }2\leq\ell\leq n,

\sum_{k\neq i}a^{\prime}_{1,k}x_{j,k}\neq c_{j}.

Now, from our assumptions on the vectors
a^{\prime}_{1},\dots,a^{\prime}_{n},e_{1},\dots,e_{i-1},e_{i+1},\dots,e_{n}
, by using
[
5
, Lemma 17]
we can find a vector
y\in\langle e_{j,k}\mid k\in[n],k\neq i\rangle
such that the first two types of conditions hold and

\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq n,k\neq i}a^{\prime}_{1,k}y_{j,k}\neq 0.

If
c_{j}=0
, then we can choose
x_{j,k}=y_{j,k}
, and if
c_{j}\neq 0
, then we can choose
x_{j,k}=\lambda_{j}\cdot y_{j,k}
with some appropriate
0\neq\lambda_{j}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

Thus, we have established that

\prod\limits_{b\in B_{1}}(1-g^{b})\cdot\prod\limits_{b\in B_{2}}(1-g^{b})\neq 0\in\mathbb{Z}[V^{\prime}],

which directly contradicts our assumption that Conjecture
2
holds.

We have therefore proven that

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{Z}[G^{\prime}].

Let us define the product:

Q:=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}}).

Observe that
Q\in\langle 1-g^{e_{i}}\rangle\subseteq\mathbb{Z}[G]
, where
\langle 1-g^{e_{i}}\rangle
is the principal ideal generated by the element
1-g^{e_{i}}\in\mathbb{Z}[G]
. This follows from identity (
2
) by using the projection
G\to G^{\prime}
(in the direction of
e_{i}
).
Consequently, we may write
Q=q\cdot(1-g^{e_{i}})
.

Now, observe that we have
Q\cdot(1-g^{e_{i}})=0
, which means that
q\cdot(1-g^{e_{i}})^{2}=0
.

However, in the group ring
\mathbb{Z}[G]
, the kernel of the multiplication by the element
(1-g^{e_{i}})^{2}
is identical to the kernel of the multiplication by the element
(1-g^{e_{i}})
.

Indeed, for an element
\sum\limits_{v\in G}c_{v}g^{v}
, both the equation
(\sum\limits_{v\in G}c_{v}g^{v})(1-g^{e_{i}})=0
and the equation
(\sum\limits_{v\in G}c_{v}g^{v})(1-g^{e_{i}})^{2}=0
is equivalent to the condition that
c_{v}=c_{v-e_{i}}
for every
v\in G
.

∎

Now, let us return to the proof of Theorem
1
. We know that the following identity holds in the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
group ring:

\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

(3)

For an element
x=\sum\limits_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}x_{v}g^{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
and an index
i\in[n]
, let us define the operation:

\partial_{i}(x):=\sum\limits_{v\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{n}}x_{v}\cdot v_{i}\cdot g^{v}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

It is easy to check that
\partial_{i}
satisfies the property
\partial_{i}(x\cdot y)=\partial_{i}(x)\cdot y+x\cdot\partial_{i}(y)
if
x,y\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
.

If we apply the partial derivative
\partial_{i}
to (
3
), we obtain that

g^{e_{i}}\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})+\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}a_{k,i}g^{a_{k}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

On the other hand, by our main lemma (Lemma
1
), we know that the first term vanishes:

\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq i\end{subarray}}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G],

so we obtain for every
i\in[n]
that:

\sum_{1\leq k\leq n}a_{k,i}g^{a_{k}}\cdot\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq n,\\
j\neq k\end{subarray}}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

Since the matrix
M
is invertible, its columns span the entire space, which implies that

g^{a_{k}}\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

Therefore,
u:=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G]
, since
g^{a_{k}}
is invertible.

We may assume (by reindexing, if necessary) that the determinant of the matrix obtained from
M
by deleting its first row and first column is nonzero.

Let us write
a_{j}=a_{j,1}e_{1}+a^{\prime}_{j}
, then
a^{\prime}_{2},\ldots,a^{\prime}_{n}
is a basis of the subgroup
H:=\langle e_{2},\dots,e_{n}\rangle
.

Since for
j\in[2,n]
we have the expansion
1-g^{a_{j}}=(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})+(1-g^{e_{1}})y_{j}
, we obtain that

u=\prod_{1\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}\left((1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})+(1-g^{e_{1}})y_{j}\right)=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[G].

We may write
0=u=\sum\limits_{1\leq k\leq p-1}(1-g^{e_{1}})^{k}u_{k}
, where each
u_{k}
belongs to
\mathbb{F}_{p}[H]
.

On the other hand, by examining the coefficient of the first power
(1-g^{e_{1}})
, we find that

u_{1}=\prod\limits_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod\limits_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[H].

Hence, finally, we obtain the identity in the lower-dimensional group ring:

\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{e_{j}})\cdot\prod_{2\leq j\leq n}(1-g^{a^{\prime}_{j}})=0\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[H].

However, by using again the statement of Conjecture
2
for the nonsingular
(n-1)\times(n-1)
submatrix (whose rows are
a_{2}^{\prime},\dots,a_{n}^{\prime}
), we would obtain a counterexample to the Alon-Jaeger-Tarsi conjecture with a smaller dimension than
n
. This contradicts our original choice of
n
as the minimal dimension for a counterexample.

This contradiction finishes the proof of Theorem
1
.
∎
