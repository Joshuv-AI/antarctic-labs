Introduction

1.1
Tilings in graphs of large minimum degree

Given two graphs
H
and
G
, an
H
-
tiling
in
G
is a collection of vertex-disjoint copies of
H
in
G
.
H
-tilings are generalizations of matchings, which correspond to the case where
H
is a single edge. An
H
-tiling is called
perfect
(or an
H
-
factor
) if it covers all vertices of
G
.

While Tutte’s theorem provides a complete characterization for the existence of a
K_{2}
-factor, no analogous characterization exists for most other connected graphs
H
. Indeed, Hell and Kirkpatrick
[
7
]
showed that the problem of deciding whether a graph
G
has an
H
-factor is NP-complete precisely when
H
contains a component with at least three vertices. Consequently, research has focused on identifying sufficient conditions that guarantee the existence of an
H
-factor. A foundational result in this direction is the Hajnal-Szemerédi theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(Hajnal and Szemerédi
[
4
]
)
.

If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph,
r\mid n
, and
\delta(G)\geq(1-1/r)n
, then
G
has a
K_{r}
-factor.

In the 1990s, Alon and Yuster generalized Theorem
1.1
to arbitrary graphs
H
by incorporating the chromatic number
\chi(H)
.

Theorem 1.2

(Alon and Yuster
[
1
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and for every integer
h
, there
exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,h)
such that for every graph
H
on
h
vertices, any graph
G
on
n>n_{0}
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi(H))n

contains at least
(1-\varepsilon)n/h
vertex-disjoint copies of
H
.

Theorem 1.3

(Alon and Yuster
[
2
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
and for every integer
h
, there
exists
n_{0}=n_{0}(\varepsilon,h)
such that for every graph
H
on
h
vertices, any graph
G
on
n>n_{0}
vertices and
\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi(H)+\varepsilon)n
, has an
H
-factor.

Alon and Yuster conjectured that the error terms involving
\varepsilon n
in Theorems
1.2
and
1.3
could be replaced by constants. In
[
1
]
, they remarked that this is essentially best possible. These conjectures were resolved by Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi.

Theorem 1.4

(Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi
[
10
]
)
.

For every graph
H
, there exists a constant
C
such that if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with

\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi(H))n+C
,
then
G
has an
H
-factor.

Theorem 1.5

(Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi
[
10
]
)
.

Given the conditions of Theorem
1.4
, if

\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi(H))n
,
then
G
has an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
C
vertices.

Kühn and Osthus
[
13
,
14
]
later showed that for any graph
H
, either the standard chromatic number or the critical chromatic number is the relevant parameter. The
critical chromatic number

\chi_{cr}(H)
of a graph
H
is defined as

\chi_{cr}(H)=(\chi(H)-1)\frac{|H|}{|H|-\sigma(H)},

where
\sigma(H)
denotes the size of the smallest color-class in any
\chi(H)
-coloring of
H
. Throughout this paper, we consider only graphs
H
containing at least one edge (without explicit mention), ensuring
\chi_{cr}(H)
is well-defined. Note that
\chi(H)-1<\chi_{cr}(H)\leq\chi(H)
for all graphs
H
, with equality
\chi_{cr}(H)=\chi(H)
holding precisely when every
\chi(H)
-coloring of
H
has color-classes of equal size.

In the context of almost perfect tilings, Komlós was the first to demonstrate the relevance of the critical chromatic number.

Theorem 1.6

(Komlós
[
11
]
)
.

For any graph
H
and
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a threshold
n_{0}=n_{0}(H,\varepsilon)
such that if
n\geq n_{0}
and an
n
-vertex graph
G
satisfies
\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi_{cr}(H))n
,
then
G
contains an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
\varepsilon n
vertices.

Komlós also conjectured that the number of uncovered vertices can be reduced to a constant depending only on
H
. This was proved by Shokoufandeh and Zhao.

Theorem 1.7

(Shokoufandeh and Zhao
[
16
]
)
.

For any
k
-chromatic graph
H
on
h
vertices with smallest color-class of
order
\sigma
, there exists an
n_{0}
such that, for all
n\geq n_{0}
, if
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with

\delta(G)\geq(1-1/\chi_{cr}(H))n
,
then
G
contains an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
\dfrac{5(k-2)(h-\sigma)^{2}}{\sigma(k-1)}
vertices of
G
.

1.2
Ore-type conditions for tilings in graphs

Beyond the minimum-degree conditions discussed above, it is natural to seek other degree-based criteria that force the existence of
H
-tilings. One of the most important such criteria is the
Ore-type
condition, which gives a lower bound on the sum of the degrees of any two nonadjacent vertices. This notion originates from Ore’s theorem
[
15
]
, which asserts that a graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices contains a Hamiltonian cycle whenever

d(x)+d(y)\geq n

for every pair of nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in V(G)
.
A notable consequence of Kierstead and Kostochka’s work on equitable colorings
[
6
]
yields an Ore-type refinement of Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.8

(Kierstead and Kostochka
[
6
]
)
.

If
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with
r\mid n
and
d(x)+d(y)\geq 2(1-1/r)n-1
for all nonadjacent
x,y\in V(G)
, then
G
contains a
K_{r}
-factor.

Cheng, Li, Sun, and Wang
[
3
]
proved that if
d(x)+d(y)\geq 2(1-1/r)n-2
for all nonadjacent
x,y\in V(G)
, then the graph
G
either contains a
K_{r}
-factor or belongs to one of two extremal graph classes.
In a related direction, Kawarabayashi
[
5
]
investigated the Ore-type condition that guarantees a
K_{4}^{-}
-tiling covering a specified number of vertices, where
K_{4}^{-}
denotes the graph obtained from
K_{4}
by deleting one edge.
Extending these results to general graphs
H
, Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown
[
12
]
established an Ore-type analogue of Theorem
1.6
regarding almost perfect tilings.

Theorem 1.9

(Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown
[
12
]
)
.

For every graph
H
and each
\eta>0
, there exists an integer
n_{0}=n_{0}(H,\eta)
such that if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices and

d(x)+d(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi_{\mathrm{cr}}(H)}\right)n

for all nonadjacent
x\neq y\in V(G)
, then
G
has an
H
-tiling covering all but at
most
\eta n
vertices.

Motivated by Theorem
1.7
, which states that the number of uncovered vertices in the minimum degree setting can be reduced to a constant depending only on
H
, Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown
[
12
]
conjectured that the same should hold for the Ore-type condition. By adapting the proof techniques developed in
[
16
]
, we confirm this conjecture.

Theorem 1.10
.

For every graph
H
, there exists a constant
C=C(H)
and an integer
n_{0}
such that if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices and

d(x)+d(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi_{\mathrm{cr}}(H)}\right)n

for all nonadjacent
x,y\in V(G)
, then
G
has an
H
-tiling covering all but at
most
C
vertices.

1.3
Notation

Let
G
be a graph. For a vertex subset
X\subseteq V(G)
, let
G[X]
be the subgraph of
G
induced on
X
and
G-X=G[V(G)\setminus X]
. For a vertex
v\in V(G)
, we write
G-v
for
G-\{v\}
. Denote the set of neighbors of
v
in
X
by
N_{G}(v,X)
, and let
d_{G}(v,X)=|N_{G}(v,X)|
be the degree of
v
in
X
. When
X=V(G)
, we use the standard abbreviations
N_{G}(v)
and
d_{G}(v)
, respectively.
For two subsets
A,B\subseteq V(G)
, we define
e_{G}(A,B)=\sum_{v\in A}d_{G}(v,B)
. Note that when
A
and
B
are disjoint,
e_{G}(A,B)
is the number of edges in
G
with one endvertex in
A
and the other in
B
.
For a directed graph
D
, let
N_{D}^{+}(v)=\{u\in V(D):(v,u)\in E(D)\}
denote the out-neighborhood of
v
, and let
d_{D}^{+}(v)=|N_{D}^{+}(v)|
denote the out-degree of
v
. We omit the subscripts
G
and
D
when the graph or digraph is clear from the context.

A bipartite graph with vertex partition
(A,B)
is denoted by
G(A,B)
. We use
K_{n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}}
to denote the complete
r
-partite graph with partition sizes
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}
. In particular,
K_{r}(s)
denotes the complete
r
-partite graph with each part of size
s
. The graph
K_{1,t}
is a
t
-star
. Denote by
\nu_{t}(G)
the maximum number of vertex-disjoint
t
-stars in
G
.
For integers
p
and
q
, we let
[p,q]=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}:p\leq i\leq q\}
.

1.4
Main theorem and structure of the paper

Our proof relies on the concept of
bottle-graph
. A
k
-
chromatic bottle-graph

\mathcal{B}
is a complete
k
-partite graph with partition sizes
(\sigma,\omega,\omega,\ldots,\omega)
, where
\sigma=\alpha\omega
for some
0<\alpha\leq 1
. We refer to
\sigma
and
\omega
as the
neck
and
width
of
\mathcal{B}
, respectively. The critical chromatic number of
\mathcal{B}
is

\displaystyle\chi_{cr}(\mathcal{B})=(\chi(\mathcal{B})-1)\frac{|\mathcal{B}|}{|\mathcal{B}|-\sigma(\mathcal{B})}=(k-1)\frac{\sigma+(k-1)\omega}{\sigma+(k-1)\omega-\sigma}=k-1+\alpha.

The vector

\beta=\left(\frac{\alpha}{k-1+\alpha},\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha},\ldots,\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha}\right)

is the
color-vector
of
\mathcal{B}
.

For a
k
-chromatic
h
-vertex graph
H
with smallest color-class size
\sigma=\sigma(H)
, we define the
bottle-graph of
H
, denoted
\mathcal{B}(H)
, as the smallest bottle-graph that contains an
H
-factor with the color-vector
\beta=(s,t,\ldots,t)
, where
s=\sigma/h
and
t=(1-s)/(k-1)
. Then

\chi_{\mathrm{cr}}(\mathcal{B}(H))=k-1+\frac{s}{t}=\frac{k-1}{1-s}\ \mbox{and}\ \chi_{\mathrm{cr}}(H)=(k-1)\frac{h}{h-\sigma}=(k-1)\frac{1}{1-\frac{\sigma}{h}}=\frac{k-1}{1-s}.

To verify the existence of such a bottle-graph, let
\{\sigma,\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\ldots,\sigma_{k-1}\}
be the sizes of the color-classes in a
k
-coloring of
H
. We can construct
\mathcal{B}(H)
by taking
k-1
vertex-disjoint copies of
H
and cyclically shifting the color-classes. Specifically, in the
i
-th copy of
H
, we assign the color-classes of size
\sigma,\sigma_{i},\sigma_{i+1},\ldots,\sigma_{k-1},\sigma_{1},\ldots,\sigma_{i-1}
to the partitions
1,2,\ldots,k
of
\mathcal{B}(H)
. Under this construction, the order of
\mathcal{B}(H)
is at most
(k-1)h
. Therefore, it is sufficient to
prove Theorem
1.10
when
H
is a bottle-graph.

Theorem 1.11
.

Let
H
be a
k
-chromatic bottle-graph with neck
\sigma
and width
\omega
. There exist constants
C=C(H)
and
n_{0}
such that if
G
is a graph on
n\geq n_{0}
vertices and

\displaystyle d(x)+d(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi_{\mathrm{cr}}(H)}\right)n=2\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n

(1.1)

for all nonadjacent
x,y\in V(G)
, where
\alpha=\sigma/\omega
and
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{(k-1)(k-1+\alpha)}
, then
G
contains an
H
-tiling covering all but at most
C
vertices.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces some technical tools. The proof of Theorem
1.11
is presented in Sections
3
-
6
.

2
Tools and clique cover

2.1
Regularity Lemma and Blow-up Lemma

In the proof of our main theorem, we will use Szemerédi’s Regularity Lemma
[
17
]
and the Blow-up Lemma of Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi
[
9
]
. We begin by introducing the necessary definitions.
Let
G=G(A,B)
be a bipartite graph. The
density
between
A
and
B
is defined as

d(A,B)=\frac{e(A,B)}{|A||B|}.

For
\varepsilon>0
, the pair
(A,B)
is
\varepsilon
-regular
if for all
X\subset A
and
Y\subset B
satisfying
|X|>\varepsilon|A|
,
|Y|>\varepsilon|B|
, we have
|d(X,Y)-d(A,B)|<\varepsilon
. Furthermore, for
d>0
, the pair
(A,B)
is
(\varepsilon,d)
-super-regular
if it is
\varepsilon
-regular and satisfies:

d_{G}(a)>d|B|\quad\text{for all }a\in A,\quad\text{and}\quad d_{G}(b)>d|A|\quad\text{for all }b\in B.

The Regularity Lemma provides a structural partition of a large and dense graph into a bounded number of clusters, where the edges between most pairs of clusters behave in a pseudo-random manner. We utilize the degree form of this lemma, which provides a structural partition
of the graph while discarding a small number of edges.

Lemma 2.1

(
Regularity Lemma-Degree Form
, Szemerédi
[
17
]
)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
m=m(\varepsilon)
and
M=M(\varepsilon)
such that if
G
is any graph and
d
is any real number with
0\leq d\leq 1
, then there exists a partition of
V
into
\ell+1
classes
V_{0},V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
and a subgraph
G^{\prime}\subseteq G
such that

•

m\leq\ell\leq M
,

•

|V_{0}|\leq\varepsilon|V(G)|
,

|V_{1}|=|V_{2}|=\ldots=|V_{\ell}|=L\leq\varepsilon|V(G)|
,

•

d_{G^{\prime}}(v)>d_{G}(v)-(d+\varepsilon)|V(G)|
for all
v\in V(G)
,

•

e(G^{\prime}[V_{i}])=0
for all
i\in[1,\ell]
,

•

For all
1\leq i<j\leq\ell
, the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular in
G^{\prime}
and has density either
0
or greater than
d
.

The sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
are called
clusters
,
V_{0}
is called the
exceptional set
, and the
vertices in
V_{0}
are
exceptional vertices
.
The
reduced graph

R
of
G
is
the graph whose vertices are
V_{1},\ldots,V_{\ell}
, where an edge exists between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
if the pair
(V_{i},V_{j})
is
\varepsilon
-regular with density greater than
d
in
G^{\prime}
. The following lemma demonstrates that the Ore-type condition is almost preserved in the reduced graph.

Lemma 2.2

(Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown
[
12
]
)
.

Given a constant
C
, let
G
be a graph such that
d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)\geq C|V(G)|
for all nonadjacent
x\neq y\in V(G)
. Let
R
be the reduced graph obtained by applying Lemma
2.1
with parameters
\varepsilon
and
d
. Then for all nonadjacent
V_{i}\neq V_{j}\in V(R)
,
d_{R}(V_{i})+d_{R}(V_{j})\geq(C-2d-4\varepsilon)|V(R)|
.

The Blow-up Lemma guarantees that if a target graph
H
with bounded maximum degree can be embedded into a "complete blow-up" graph of
R
, it can also be embedded into a super-regular "blow-up" graph
G
.

Lemma 2.3

(
Blow-up Lemma
, Komlós, Sárközy, and Szemerédi
[
9
]
)
.

Given a graph
R
of order
r
and positive parameters
\delta
and
\Delta
, there exists an
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\delta,\Delta,r)>0
such that the following holds. Let
n_{1},n_{2},\ldots,n_{r}
be arbitrary positive integers, and let us replace vertices
v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}
of
R
with pairwise disjoint sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{r}
of sizes
n_{1},\ldots,n_{r}
(blowing up). We construct two graphs on the same vertex set
V=\bigcup_{i\in[1,r]}V_{i}
.
The first graph
R_{b}
is obtained by replacing each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(R)
with the complete bipartite graph between the corresponding vertex sets
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
.
A sparser graph
G
is constructed by replacing each edge
v_{i}v_{j}\in E(R)
arbitrarily with an
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-super-regular pair between
V_{i}
and
V_{j}
.
If a graph
H
with
\Delta(H)\leq\Delta
is embeddable into
R_{b}
, then
H
is also embeddable into
G
.

2.2
Clique cover

In this section, we assume that
\mathcal{G}
is a graph on
\ell
vertices and for all nonadjacent
x,y\in V(\mathcal{G})
,

\displaystyle d(x)+d(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell.

(2.1)

A
k
-
clique-cover

\Phi=\{\Phi_{k},\Phi_{k-1},\ldots,\Phi_{1}\}
of
\mathcal{G}
is a collection of pairwise vertex-disjoint cliques such that
V(\mathcal{G})=\bigcup_{i=1}^{k}V(\Phi_{i})
, where
\Phi_{i}
denotes the set of cliques of order
i
for each
i\in[1,k]
.
A
k
-clique-cover
\Phi
is
maximal
if the vector
(|\Phi_{k}|,|\Phi_{k-1}|,\ldots,|\Phi_{1}|)
is lexicographically maximal among all
k
-clique-covers of
\mathcal{G}
. We denote a clique of order
i
in the family
\Phi_{i}
as
K^{i}
.
For
K^{i}\in\Phi_{i}
and
K^{j}\in\Phi_{j}
with
1\leq i\leq j\leq k
, we define the connectivity between these two cliques as follows:

•

K^{i}
is
well-connected
to
K^{j}
, denoted by
K^{i}\hookrightarrow K^{j}
, if
e(\{v\},K^{j})=j-1
for all
v\in K^{i}
.

•

K^{i}
is
over-connected
to
K^{j}
, denoted by
K^{i}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 2.94443pt>}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\hookrightarrow$}}$}\hskip 3.99994ptK^{j}
, if
e(K^{i},K^{j})\geq i(j-1)
but
K^{i}\not\hookrightarrow K^{j}
.

•

K^{i}
is
under-connected
to
K^{j}
, denoted by
K^{i}\raisebox{-1.72218pt}{$\overset{\scriptscriptstyle\hskip 2.94443pt<}{\scalebox{0.8}{$\hookrightarrow$}}$}\hskip 3.99994ptK^{j}
, if
e(K^{i},K^{j})<i(j-1)
.

The following properties of maximal clique-covers are essential to our proof. Proposition
2.4
was established by Shokoufandeh and Zhao
[
16
]
.

Proposition 2.4

(Shokoufandeh and Zhao
[
16
]
)
.

Let
1\leq i\leq j<k
. Then

(i)

e(K^{i},K^{j})\leq i(j-1)
.
In particular, if
e(K^{i},K^{j})=i(j-1)
, then
K^{i}\hookrightarrow K^{j}
.

(ii)

For any
K^{k}\in\Phi_{k}
and any set of
k
cliques
\{K_{1}^{i},\ldots,K_{k}^{i}\}\subseteq\Phi_{i}
, we have
e(\bigcup_{p=1}^{k}K_{p}^{i},K^{k})\leq ik(k-1)
.

(iii)

If
K^{j}\in\Phi_{j}
and
K_{1}^{i},K_{2}^{i}\in\Phi_{i}
satisfy
e(K_{1}^{i},K^{j})=e(K_{2}^{i},K^{j})=i(j-1)
, then
K_{1}^{i}\hookrightarrow K^{j}
and
K_{2}^{i}\hookrightarrow K^{j}
.
Furthermore,
K^{j}
can be partitioned as
K^{j}=A_{i}(K^{j})\cup B_{i}(K^{j})
such that
|A_{i}(K^{j})|=i
,
|B_{i}(K^{j})|=j-i
, and for all
v\in A_{i}(K^{j})
, for all
u\in B_{i}(K^{j})
, we have
d(v,K_{1}^{i})=d(v,K_{2}^{i})=i-1
and

d(u,K_{1}^{i})=d(u,K_{2}^{i})=i
.
Finally, let

S_{i}^{j}=\{K^{j}\in\Phi_{j}:e(K_{1}^{i},K^{j})=e(K_{2}^{i},K^{j})=i(j-1)\}\ \text{and}\ A_{i}^{j}=\{A_{i}(K^{j}):K^{j}\in S_{i}^{j}\}.

Then
\mathcal{G}[A_{i}^{j}]
is an
i
-partite subgraph with
|S_{i}^{j}|
vertices in each color-class. Moreover,
\mathcal{G}[A_{i}^{i}\cup A_{i}^{i+1}\cup\ldots\cup A_{i}^{k}]
is an
i
-partite graph with
\sum_{j=i}^{k}|S_{i}^{j}|

vertices in each color-class.

Proposition 2.5
.

If
K_{1},\ldots,K_{p}
are
p\geq 2
cliques in
\Phi_{i}
for some
i\in[1,k-1]
, then

e\left(\bigcup\limits_{j=1}^{p}K_{j},\mathcal{G}\right)\geq pi\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell.

Proof.

\mathcal{K}^{k}\in\Psi_{k}^{\prime}

For each cluster

Large parts.

Let

,

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(t_{1})=\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(\frac{L^{\prime}}{i}\right)

Small parts.

Together with the small parts of the clusters in

\underbrace{it_{2},\ldots,it_{2}}_{k-i},\underbrace{(i-1+\alpha^{\prime})t_{2},\ldots,(i-1+\alpha^{\prime})t_{2}}_{i}.

This graph can be decomposed into

Thus, using one well-connected

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}\left(\frac{(1-\alpha^{\prime})L^{\prime}}{i(i-1+\alpha^{\prime})}\right)

.
Since each cluster of

.
Repeating the above procedure with this many well-connected

Step 3

For each

, convert every atypical clique in
\Psi_{k-i}
into copies of unbalanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-cliques.

The procedure is the same as in Step
2
, except that we now use
k
-cliques which are over-connected to the atypical clique
\mathcal{K}
. If

Since

. We choose

, and let the remaining clusters form

.

Step 4

Partition the remaining
k
-cliques in
\Psi_{k}^{\prime}
and all (different-size) unbalanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-cliques into copies of
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
.

Here

, where the constant

.

Proof of Lemma
4.1
.

Note that Lemma
4.1
holds if the Decomposition Algorithm given above is correct. So we prove the correctness of the algorithm. For Step
3
, by Proposition
2.4
(ii), any
k
-clique that is over-connected to

does not participate in the conversion of any other (typical or atypical) clique. Since each
\mathcal{K}\in\Psi_{k-i}
requires
\frac{(i-1+\alpha^{\prime})L}{(1-\alpha^{\prime})L^{\prime}}
good
k
-cliques in
\Psi_{k}^{\prime}
, the total number of over-connected
k
-cliques is at least

c_{t}\frac{L}{L^{\prime}}=\left\lceil\frac{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}\right\rceil\frac{L}{L^{\prime}}\geq\frac{(i-1+\alpha^{\prime})L}{(1-\alpha^{\prime})L^{\prime}},\quad\text{for }i<k.

Thus Step
3
can be performed for every atypical clique.

During Step
2
, we considered all typical cliques in
\Psi_{k-1},\ldots,\Psi_{1}
sequentially. For a typical clique
\mathcal{K}\in\Psi_{k-i}
, we can ignore the existence of its over-connected
k
-cliques in
our computation, since
c_{t}\ll\ell
. Thus, the term
\lambda_{i}\ell
in Proposition
2.8
gives a lower bound on the number of well-connected
k
-cliques in
\Psi_{k}
. It follows that the new family
\Psi_{k}^{\prime}
contains at least
\lambda_{i}\ell L/L^{\prime}
well-connected
k
-cliques for
\mathcal{K}
. We need to show

\frac{\lambda_{i}\ell L}{L^{\prime}}>\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{(l-1+\alpha^{\prime})L}{(1-\alpha^{\prime})L^{\prime}}\varphi_{k-l}\ell.

Equivalently, we need to prove

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{l-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}\varphi_{k-l}>0.

(4.2)

Firstly, we compute

\displaystyle I_{i}=\lambda_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{l-1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\varphi_{k-l}.

By Proposition
2.8
,
\lambda_{1}\geq(k-1)\gamma
.
By (
2.2
),
\varphi_{k-1}=\frac{1}{k-1}\left(1-k\varphi_{k}-\sum_{j=2}^{k-1}(k-j)\varphi_{k-j}\right).

Then

\displaystyle I_{1}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{1}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\varphi_{k-1}\geq(k-1)\gamma-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{k-1}\left(1-k\varphi_{k}-\sum_{j\geq 2}(k-j)\varphi_{k-j}\right)

\displaystyle{\overset{\eqref{eq:varphik}}{=}}(k-1)\gamma-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{k-1}\left(1-k\bigl((k-1)\gamma+s+\sum_{j\geq 2}(j-1)\varphi_{k-j}\bigr)-\sum_{j\geq 2}(k-j)\varphi_{k-j}\right)

\displaystyle=(k-1)\gamma-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\frac{1}{k-1}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\left(-k\gamma-\frac{k}{k-1}s-\sum_{j\geq 2}j\varphi_{k-j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{k}{k-1}\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}s+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\sum_{j\geq 2}j\varphi_{k-j},

(4.3)

where the last equality holds as
\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{(k-1)(k-1+\alpha)}
.
Similarly,

\displaystyle I_{2}

\displaystyle=\lambda_{2}-\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\varphi_{k-1}-\frac{1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\varphi_{k-2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{s}{k-1}+\frac{k}{k-1}\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}s+\sum_{j\geq 3}\varphi_{k-j}+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\sum_{j\geq 3}j\varphi_{k-j}.

In general,

\displaystyle I_{i}\geq\left(\frac{i-1}{k-1}+\frac{k\alpha}{(k-1)(1-\alpha)}\right)s+\sum_{j>i}\left(i-1+\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}j\right)\varphi_{k-j}\geq\frac{k\alpha}{(1-\alpha)(k-1)}s>\mu_{1},

(4.4)

where
\mu_{1}=\frac{k\alpha}{(1-\alpha)(k-1)}\mu
and the last inequality holds as
s\geq\mu
.

For all
l\in[1,k-1]
, we have
\frac{l-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{l-1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}=\frac{l(\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha)}{(1-\alpha^{\prime})(1-\alpha)}
.
Hence, using (
4.1
) and the fact that
\sum_{l=1}^{i}\varphi_{k-l}\leq 1
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{l=1}^{i}\varphi_{k-l}\left(\frac{l-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{l-1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)\leq\mu_{1}<I_{i}.

(4.5)

So

\lambda_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{i}\frac{l-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}\varphi_{k-l}=I_{i}-\sum_{l=1}^{i}\varphi_{k-l}\left(\frac{l-1+\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{l-1+\alpha}{1-\alpha}\right)>0.

Thus (
4.2
) holds, and Step
2
can be carried out.
∎

4.2
Handling exceptional vertices

Let
h=|V(H)|
. The general subcase
s\geq\mu
follows from the following lemma.

Lemma 4.3
.

Assume
\varepsilon\leq\theta\ll\rho\ll 1
, and let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph satisfying the Ore-type condition (
1.1
), containing an exceptional vertex set
V_{0}
with
|V_{0}|\leq\theta n
. If the
subgraph
G^{\prime\prime}=G\setminus V_{0}
has an
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
-factor with
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha+\rho
and
L_{1}
is sufficiently large, then
G
contains an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
\left(\dfrac{5k^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}\omega+h\right)
vertices.

Before proving Lemma
4.3
, we introduce some notation. Let
\mathbf{R}
be the
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
-factor of
G^{\prime\prime}
, and let
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbf{R}
be an unbalanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-clique
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
. We write
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
, where
U_{k}
denotes the smallest cluster, of size
\alpha^{\prime}L_{1}
, and the other clusters have size
L_{1}
. When no confusion arises, we simply call
\mathcal{R}
a

.

Proof of Lemma
4.3
.

The proof consists of two phases. In Phase I, we insert the exceptional vertices into suitable regular
k
-cliques and cover them by copies of
H
. In Phase II, we use the connections between different regular
k
-cliques to reduce the number of uncovered vertices to a constant depending only on
H
.

First, partition
V_{0}
into two subsets. Let

V_{0}^{1}=\left\{x\in V_{0}:d_{G}(x)<\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n\right\}
and
V_{0}^{2}=V_{0}\setminus V_{0}^{1}
. By the Ore-type condition (
1.1
),
V_{0}^{1}
induces a clique in
G
. Then
G[V_{0}^{1}]
can be tiled by copies of
H
, leaving at most
h-1
vertices uncovered. It remains to cover the vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
. We will remove pairwise disjoint copies of
H
, each containing exactly one vertex of
V_{0}^{2}
, while ensuring that no cluster in the factor
\mathbf{R}
loses too many vertices.

Phase I: Covering the exceptional vertices.

We shall use the following corollary of the Key Lemma
[
8
]
.

Lemma 4.4

(Komlós and Simonovits
[
8
]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=\{V_{1},\ldots,V_{k}\}
be an
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-clique in
G^{\prime\prime}
. Assume
W_{i}\subset V_{i}
with
|W_{i}|=d^{\prime}L
for all
i\in[1,k]
, where
d^{\prime}\gg\varepsilon
. Let
\mathcal{R}_{0}=\{W_{1},\ldots,W_{k}\}
. Then
K_{k}(h)\subset\mathcal{R}_{0}
. In particular,
H\subset\mathcal{R}_{0}
, and we can put its small color-class in any

.

For a vertex
v\in V_{0}^{2}
and a cluster
U\in V(R)
, we write

if
d_{G}(v,U)\geq d|U|
.

The procedure of Phase I is as follows. We process the vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
in any order. For
each
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, we pick an element
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
such that

\displaystyle v\sim U_{i}\ \text{for at least}\ k-1\ \text{clusters}\ U_{i}\in\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}.

(4.6)

Then we construct a copy of
H
containing
v
and vertices from
\mathcal{R}
as follows.

•

If
v\sim U_{i}
for all
i\in[1,k-1]
, then we place
v
in the small color-class of
H
. Thus we remove a copy of
H
consisting of
v
,
\sigma-1
vertices from
U_{k}
, and
\omega
vertices from each
U_{i}
for
i\in[1,k-1]
.

•

Otherwise, there exists some
j\neq k
such that
v\sim U_{i}
for all
i\neq j
. Then we place
v
in the color-class corresponding to
U_{j}
. Thus we remove
\omega-1
vertices from
U_{j}
,
\sigma
vertices from
U_{k}
, and
\omega
vertices from each

with

.

In both cases, Lemma
4.4
guarantees the required copy of

, since
v
has many neighbors in each cluster used for the other color-classes. To prevent a cluster from losing too many vertices, we stop considering an element of
\mathbf{R}
if it has been selected
\theta_{1}L_{1}
times, where
\theta_{1}=\sqrt{\theta}
. The following proposition guarantees that an available element satisfying (
4.6
) always exists.

Proposition 4.5
.

For any vertex
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, regardless of the processing order, at least
\alpha/2
proportion of the elements in
\mathbf{R}
satisfy (
4.6
).

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{0}^{2}
be the
(t+1)
-st vertex to be processed, where
t
denotes the number of vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
that have already been processed. For these first
t
vertices, we have removed
t
pairwise disjoint copies of
H
, each containing exactly one processed vertex of
V_{0}^{2}
and
h-1
vertices from elements of
\mathbf{R}
.
Let
G^{*}
be the subgraph induced by the vertices that remain after these
t
copies of
H
have been removed. Since
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, we have
d_{G}(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n
and

\displaystyle d_{G}(v,V(G^{*}))\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n-\theta n-t(h-1).

(4.7)

Let
m
be the fraction of elements in
\mathbf{R}
satisfying (
4.6
). We derive a lower bound on

. Recall that every element
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbf{R}
has size
(k-1+\alpha^{\prime})L_{1}
. If
\mathcal{R}
does not satisfy (

), then at least two clusters

fail

, and hence each such cluster contributes fewer than

neighbors of

. Thus such a bad element contributes at most
(k-2)L_{1}+dL_{1}+d\alpha^{\prime}L_{1}=(k-2+d+\alpha^{\prime}d)L_{1}

. We have the upper bound on

.

\displaystyle d_{G}(v,V(G^{*}))\leq\left((1-m)\frac{k-2+\alpha^{\prime}d+d}{(k-1)+\alpha^{\prime}}+m\right)\bigl(n-\theta n-(h-1)t\bigr).

(4.8)

Combining (
4.7
) and (
4.8
), and using

, we obtain

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-\theta h\leq 1-\frac{1+\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}d-d}{(k-1)+\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1+\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}d-d}{(k-1)+\alpha^{\prime}}m,

which implies

\frac{1+\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}d-d}{(k-1)+\alpha^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha}-\theta h\leq\frac{1+\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha^{\prime}d-d}{(k-1)+\alpha^{\prime}}m.

Therefore,

m>\frac{\alpha-2d-kh\theta}{1+\alpha^{\prime}-d-\alpha^{\prime}d}>\frac{\alpha}{2}

holds since we may assume that

.
∎

Since no element of
\mathbf{R}
is selected more than

times, the proportion of unavailable elements is at most

\frac{\dfrac{\theta n}{\sqrt{\theta}L_{1}}}{\dfrac{n}{(k-1+\alpha^{\prime})L_{1}}}<\sqrt{\theta}k.

By choosing

k\sqrt{\theta}<\frac{\alpha}{2}
.
Hence Proposition
4.5
ensures that Phase I can be carried out.

After all vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
have been processed, and after tiling

, at most

original exceptional vertices remain uncovered. We now make the regular pairs super-regular. Let
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
. For any vertex

, if there exists

, then we move

to

. By

-regularity, each cluster loses at most

vertices in this way. Since

, these newly exceptional vertices can be handled by the same procedure as above, after again separating them into low-degree and high-degree vertices. This does not affect the preceding estimates. After this cleaning step, every pair inside each remaining element of
\mathbf{R}
is
(\varepsilon,d/2)
-super-regular.

Phase II: Reducing the number of uncovered vertices.

Let
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
denote the resulting clique-cover at the end of Phase I. Since some vertices have been removed from the clusters during Phase I, the sizes of resulting clusters vary.
Recall that
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha^{\prime}}_{k}(L_{1})\supseteq\mathcal{R}^{\alpha}_{k}(L_{2})\cup\mathcal{R}_{k}^{1}(L_{0})
, where
L_{2}=\frac{1-\alpha^{\prime}}{1-\alpha}L_{1}
and
L_{0}=\frac{\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha}{1-\alpha}L_{1}
. Since
\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha=\rho\gg\theta_{1}
, the number of vertices removed from each cluster in Phase I is much smaller than

. Hence each element
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
contains at least one balanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-clique
\mathcal{R}_{k}^{1}(L_{0}/2)
. Thus,
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
contains an
H
-tiling, where the
\sigma
-vertex classes can be taken from any cluster of element
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

This flexibility allows us to transfer unused vertices among the clusters of a fixed element
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. Indeed, suppose we want to move
\omega-\sigma
unused vertices from a cluster
U_{1}
to another cluster
U_{2}
in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. We simply switch the color-classes for one
copy of
H
such that the
\sigma
-vertex class which was supposed to come from
U_{1}
will come
from
U_{2}
. This results in a loss of
\omega-\sigma
vertices from
U_{1}
while
U_{2}
will gain
\omega-\sigma
vertices. This observation will help us establish the following result.

Proposition 4.6
.

Each element of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
has an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
(k-1)(2\omega-\sigma)+\omega
vertices.

Proof.

Consider an element
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
. Since all cluster-pairs in
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
are super-regular, we can apply the Blow-up Lemma (Lemma
2.3
) to
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
. We pick an
H
-tiling of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
(which leaves
a_{1},a_{2},\ldots,a_{k}
vertices uncovered from clusters
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
, respectively), satisfying the following two conditions:

•

It is one of the best
H
-tilings, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}
is the minimum over all
H
-tilings of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
.

•

It is the most balanced, i.e.,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|a_{i}-\omega|
is the smallest among the best tilings.

Assume that
a_{i_{0}}=\min\{a_{i}:i\in[1,k]\}
. We claim that
a_{i_{0}}<\omega
. Indeed, if
a_{i}\geq\omega
for all
i\in[1,k]
, then, since
\sigma\leq\omega
, the uncovered vertices in the
k
clusters would contain enough vertices to form one additional copy of
H
. This contradicts the maximality of the chosen tiling. Moreover, we claim that for all
i\neq i_{0}
, we have
a_{i}<2\omega-\sigma
. Suppose otherwise that
a_{i}\geq 2\omega-\sigma
for some
i\neq i_{0}
, then we can move
(\omega-\sigma)
unused vertices from
U_{i}
to
U_{i_{0}}
. This preserves
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}
, but decreases
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|a_{i}-\omega|
. A contradiction to the choice of the tiling. Therefore,

\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}=a_{i_{0}}+\sum_{i\neq i_{0}}a_{i}<\omega+(k-1)(2\omega-\sigma).

Thus
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
has an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
(k-1)(2\omega-\sigma)+\omega
vertices.
∎

If we apply Proposition
4.6
directly to all elements of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, we obtain an
H
-tiling of

that leaves at most
\bigl((k-1)(2\omega-\sigma)+\omega\bigr)|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|
vertices uncovered. Recall that

and
\ell\leq M(\varepsilon)
(from Lemma

). However, we need a bound depending only on
H
, and hence independent of
\varepsilon
. To achieve this, we use the connections between different elements of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
.

Define a directed graph
\mathcal{D}
as follows. The vertex set

consists of the clusters of
G^{\prime\prime}
. A directed edge
(U,U_{1})\in E(\mathcal{D})
if and only if

and
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
for all

. Thus, a directed edge
(U,U_{1})
means that one vertex from

can be used together with vertices from the other

\mathcal{R}
to form a copy of
H
. In this way, an extra vertex in

can be moved to
U_{1}
.

Since Phase I removes only a small number of vertices from each cluster, the adjacency in the reduced graph
R
, and hence the directed graph
\mathcal{D}
, is unchanged.

In a directed graph

, the

of a vertex

is defined as
\mathcal{W}(v)=\{u\in V(D):\text{there exists a directed path from }u\text{ to }v\}
. It is easy to see that
|\mathcal{W}(u)|\geq|\mathcal{W}(v)|
if
u\in N^{+}(v)
. A set
\mathcal{S}(D)\subseteq V(D)
is called a
sink set
if
V(D)=\bigcup_{v\in\mathcal{S}(D)}\mathcal{W}(v)
. We will show that
\mathcal{D}
has a sink set
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D})
of size at most
\dfrac{5k^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}\omega
.

We now describe the transfer procedure.

Step 1

For each element
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
, choose a largest
H
-tiling as in Proposition
4.6
, but do not yet remove the corresponding copies. For each cluster
C
, let
\operatorname{extra}(C)
denote the number of vertices of
C
left uncovered by this tiling.

Step 2

Let
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D})
be a sink set. For every cluster
C\notin\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D})
with

\operatorname{extra}(C)>0
, choose a directed path
C=C^{0},C^{1},\ldots,C^{t}
in
\mathcal{D}
, where
C^{t}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{D})
.

We transfer the extra vertices of
C
along this path. For each
i\in[1,t]
, let
\mathcal{R}_{i}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
be the element containing
C^{i}
. Since
(C^{i-1},C^{i})\in E(\mathcal{D})
, the cluster
C^{i-1}
is adjacent in
R
to every cluster of
\mathcal{R}_{i}
except possibly
C^{i}
. Therefore, using the Blow-up Lemma, we can remove copies of
H
, each containing one vertex from
C^{i-1}
and the remaining
h-1
vertices from the other clusters of
\mathcal{R}_{i}
.

This operation decreases
\operatorname{extra}(C^{i-1})
and increases
\operatorname{extra}(C^{i})
by the same amount. Repeating this along the directed path moves all extra vertices originally in
C
into the sink set.

Since the total number of extra vertices being transferred is bounded by a constant and
L_{1}
is sufficiently large, these additional removals do not affect the super-regularity of the relevant pairs.

Step 3

After all extra vertices have been transferred into the sink set, apply the Blow-up Lemma to the remaining vertices in each element of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
. Combining these copies of
H
with the copies removed in Phase I and during the transfer process gives an
H
-tiling of
G
.

It remains to prove that
\mathcal{D}
has a small sink set. Let

J_{L}=\left\{U\in V(\mathcal{D}):d_{R}(U)<\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)|V(R)|\right\}

and let
J_{H}=V(\mathcal{D})\setminus J_{L}
. By the Ore-type condition (
3.3
), the set
J_{L}
induces a clique in
R
. Hence the extra vertices transferred to clusters in
J_{L}
can be handled in this clique, leaving at most
k
additional vertices uncovered. Thus it remains to find a small sink set for
J_{H}
.
We use the following elementary lemma.

Lemma 4.7
.

Let
D
be a directed graph with minimum out-degree at least
\delta
. Then
D
has a sink set of size at most

\frac{|V(D)|}{\delta+1}
.

Proof.

Let
x_{1}\in V(D)
such that
|\mathcal{W}(x_{1})|=\max_{v\in V(D)}|\mathcal{W}(v)|
. We claim that

N_{D}^{+}(x_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{W}(x_{1})
. For any
u\in N_{D}^{+}(x_{1})
, any directed path to
x_{1}
can be extended to
u
via the edge
(x_{1},u)
, which implies
\mathcal{W}(x_{1})\subseteq\mathcal{W}(u)
.
By the maximality of
|\mathcal{W}(x_{1})|
, we have
\mathcal{W}(x_{1})=\mathcal{W}(u)
, and therefore
u\in\mathcal{W}(x_{1})
. It follows that
|\mathcal{W}(x_{1})|\geq d_{D}^{+}(x_{1})+1\geq\delta+1
.

Delete
\mathcal{W}(x_{1})
and repeat the same argument in the remaining directed graph. If a vertex outside
\mathcal{W}(x_{1})
had an out-neighbour in
\mathcal{W}(x_{1})
, then it could reach
x_{1}
, a contradiction. Thus the minimum out-degree in the remaining digraph is still at least
\delta
. Hence at least
\delta+1
vertices are deleted at each step. The selected vertices form a sink set
S(D)
, and

|S(D)|\leq\frac{|V(D)|}{\delta+1}
.
∎

Proposition 4.8
.

For any
U\in V(\mathcal{D})
with
d_{R}(U)\geq(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon)|V(R)|
, we have

d_{\mathcal{D}}^{+}(U)\geq\frac{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}{k}|V(\mathcal{D})|
.

Proof.

Since the connections in
R
are not influenced by the insertion of exceptional vertices in Phase I, we may assume that the sizes of clusters in each clique
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbf{R}
are still
L_{1}
and
\alpha^{\prime}L_{1}
.

For a given
U\in V(\mathcal{D})
, let
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
, and
m_{3}
denote the fraction of cliques
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
for which
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
for all
i\in[1,k-1]
but
(U,U_{k})\notin E(R)
,
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
but
(U,U_{j})\notin E(R)
for all
i\neq j
with
j\neq k
, and
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
for all
i\in[1,k]
, respectively. By the degree condition of
U
in the assumption, we have

\displaystyle 1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\frac{k-2}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{1}+\frac{\alpha^{\prime}}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{2}+\frac{1+\alpha^{\prime}}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{3}

\displaystyle<\frac{k-2}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{1}+\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{2}+\frac{2}{k-1+\alpha^{\prime}}m_{3}

\displaystyle<\frac{k-2}{k-1+\alpha}+\frac{1}{k-1+\alpha}(m_{1}+m_{2}+2m_{3})\quad(\text{as}\ \alpha<\alpha^{\prime}<1)

\displaystyle=\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}-(k-2)\gamma\right)+\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)(m_{1}+m_{2}+2m_{3}),

that is,

(k-1)\gamma\leq\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)(m_{1}+m_{2}+2m_{3}).

By the definition of
\mathcal{D}
,
U
has exactly one out-neighbor into
\mathcal{R}
for the cases corresponding to
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, and
k
out-neighbors for the case corresponding to
m_{3}
. Since
k\geq 2
, we have
m_{1}+m_{2}+km_{3}\geq m_{1}+m_{2}+2m_{3}
. Note that
|\mathbf{R}|=\frac{|V(\mathcal{D})|}{k}
, we have

d_{\mathcal{D}}^{+}(U)=(m_{1}+m_{2}+km_{3})|\mathbf{R}|\geq\frac{(k-1)\gamma}{\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma}\frac{|V(\mathcal{D})|}{k}>\frac{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}{k}|V(\mathcal{D})|.\qed

Let
\mathcal{W}(J_{L})=\bigcup_{U\in J_{L}}\mathcal{W}(U)
. Every cluster in
\mathcal{W}(J_{L})
can reach a low-degree cluster, and hence its extra vertices can be transferred into
J_{L}
. As noted above, the extra vertices in
J_{L}
can be handled with at most
k
additional uncovered vertices.
Now consider the induced subdigraph
\mathcal{D}_{H}=\mathcal{D}-\mathcal{W}(J_{L})
.
If
\mathcal{D}_{H}
is non-empty, then every vertex of
\mathcal{D}_{H}
belongs to
J_{H}
. Moreover, no vertex of
\mathcal{D}_{H}
has an out-neighbour outside
\mathcal{D}_{H}
; otherwise it could reach
J_{L}
. Therefore Proposition
4.8
gives
\delta^{+}(\mathcal{D}_{H})\geq\frac{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}{k}|V(\mathcal{D})|
.
By Lemma
4.7
,
\mathcal{D}_{H}
has a sink set
S(\mathcal{D})
with

|S(\mathcal{D})|\leq\frac{|V(\mathcal{D})|}{\frac{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}{k}|V(\mathcal{D})|+1}\leq\frac{k}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}.

Thus, after all extra vertices have been transferred, the only uncovered vertices lie in at most

\frac{k}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}
high-degree sink clusters, together with at most
k
additional vertices arising from the low-degree clique structure. By Proposition
4.6
, the total number of uncovered vertices is therefore at most

((k-1)(2\omega-\sigma)+\omega)\left(\frac{k}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}\right)+k+h\leq\frac{5k^{2}}{(k-1)^{2}\gamma}\omega+h

since
\sigma\leq\omega
.
This completes the proof of Lemma
4.3
.
∎

5
The extremal subcase when

By Lemma
4.3
, the general subcase where

was resolved in Section
4
. In this section, we assume that
s<\mu
. Recall that
\Phi=\{\Phi_{k},\ldots,\Phi_{1}\}
is a maximal clique-cover of

. Substituting (

\displaystyle(k-1)\varphi_{k}+(k-1)\gamma+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}\varphi_{k-i}(i-1)+s+(k-1)\varphi_{k-1}+\sum_{i=2}^{k-1}\varphi_{k-i}(k-i)=1,

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi_{i}=\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{s}{k-1}\geq\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{\mu}{k-1}.

(5.1)

Also from (

\displaystyle(k-1)k\gamma+ks+(k-1)\sum_{j\geq 1}j\varphi_{k-j}=1.

For
i<k

. We have

\sigma_{k-1}=\frac{1}{k-1}-k\gamma-\frac{k}{k-1}s=\frac{1-\alpha}{k-1+\alpha}-\frac{k}{k-1}s\geq\alpha_{0},

where
\alpha_{0}=\frac{1-\alpha}{k-1+\alpha}-\frac{k}{k-1}\mu

Let
C=\sqrt[k]{\alpha_{0}/\varepsilon}
. Since
\sigma_{k-1}\geq\alpha_{0}=C^{k}\varepsilon
, there exists an integer
t\in[1,k-1]
and an integer
j_{0}\leq k
such that
\sigma_{t}\geq C^{j_{0}}\varepsilon
and
\sigma_{t-1}\leq C^{j_{0}-1}\varepsilon
. Set
\varepsilon^{\prime}=k(C^{j_{0}-1}\varepsilon+2\mu)
and
\mu^{\prime}=C^{j_{0}}\varepsilon
.

We first move all vertices of
G
in
\Phi_{j}
for
j<t
into
V_{0}
. The size of the resulting exceptional set (still denoted by
|V_{0}|
) is bounded by

|V_{0}|<\varepsilon n+\sum_{j<t}j\varphi_{j}n<\varepsilon n+k\sum_{j<t}\varphi_{j}n<\varepsilon n+k\sigma_{t-1}n\leq\varepsilon n+kC^{j_{0}-1}\varepsilon n<\varepsilon^{\prime}n.

Following the proof of Proposition
2.7
, it is not hard to show that the number of
k
-cliques that are over-connected to some smaller clique is at most
2t\mu\ell
. Since the size of
\Phi_{t}
is not small, most
t
-cliques are not over-connected to any
k
-clique. Fix two such
t
-cliques
K_{1},K_{2}\in\Phi_{t}
. For
j\in[0,k-t]
, let
\Phi_{k-j}^{0}=\{K\in\Phi_{k-j}:e(\{K_{1},K_{2}\},K)<2t(k-j-1)\}
and
m_{j}=|\Phi_{k-j}^{0}|/\ell
. Using similar computations as in Proposition
2.8
, we obtain

m_{0}+m_{1}+\ldots+m_{k-t}<\frac{2t}{k-1}s+4dt<\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{k}.

Recall that in Proposition
2.4
(iii), we divide each clique
K^{j}
(
j\geq t
) which is well-connected to
K_{1},K_{2}
into sets
A_{t}(K^{j})
and
B_{t}(K^{j})
, where

consists of the clusters which are adjacent to all but one of

(or
K_{2}
), and
B_{t}(K^{j})
are those which are adjacent to all of
K_{1}
(or
K_{2}
). When combining
A_{t}(K)
for all clusters
K\notin\Phi_{t}^{0}\cup\ldots\cup\Phi_{k}^{0}
, we obtain a cluster set

such that

\displaystyle|A|=t\left(\sum_{j=t}^{k}\varphi_{j}-\sum_{j=0}^{k-t}m_{j}\right)\ell{\overset{\eqref{eq:sum}}{\geq}}t\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{2k\mu}{k-1}\right)\ell.

On one hand,
A
is covered by a family of
t
-cliques. On the other hand, by Proposition
2.4
(iii),
A
is the disjoint union of
t\leq k-1
independent sets
U_{1},\ldots,U_{t}
, with
|U_{j}|\geq\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{2k\mu}{k-1}\right)\ell\geq\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{k-1}\right)\ell
.
Because each
U_{i}
forms an independent set in

, any two vertices in
U_{i}
are nonadjacent. By (
3.3
), the sum of their degrees must be at least
2\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell
. So at most one vertex in each
U_{i}
can have degree less than
\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell
.
Thus, after moving at most one vertex from each
U_{i}
into
V_{0}
, we may assume that every
c\in U_{i}
satisfies

d_{R}(c)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell
.
Since
U_{i}
is independent, all neighbors of
c\in U_{i}
lie in

. Moreover,

|R\setminus U_{i}|\leq\ell-\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{k-1}\right)\ell
.
Hence, for all

, we have

\displaystyle d_{R}(c,R\setminus U_{i})=d_{R}(c)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell\geq|R\setminus U_{i}|-\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{k-1}\ell.

(5.2)

In other words, all but at most one element of
U_{i}
are almost adjacent to all the clusters outside
U_{i}
.
Depending on the value of
t
, we will consider two separate cases.

5.1
Extremal subcase (I):
t=k-1

Let
U_{k}=V(R)\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{k-1}U_{i}
, and we also write
U_{i}
(for
1\leq i\leq k
) for the underlying vertex sets in
G
. We first move all exceptional vertices from

to
U_{k}
. Then, we move a vertex

to
U_{i}
(for some
i<k
) if

, where
\varepsilon^{\prime}\ll\varepsilon_{1}\ll 1
. The resulting sets are still denoted by
U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}
.

In the ideal case,
|U_{i}|=\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
for all
i<k
and
|U_{k}|=(k-1)\gamma n
. Due to the superregularity between every pair in
\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}
, we can apply Lemma
2.3
to find the desired
H
-factor in
G
.

If
|U_{i}|<\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
for all
i<k
, we can construct copies of
H
by placing their
\omega
-classes in
U_{k}
. An argument analogous to Proposition
4.6
then shows that all but at most
2k\omega
vertices of

are covered by an
H
-tiling. Thus, we may assume
|U_{1}|>\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
, while
|U_{i}|<\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
for

, and
|U_{k}|<(k-1)\gamma n
. The other cases are symmetric.

To balance the parts, we move vertices from
U_{1}
to the deficient sets. More precisely, whenever there exists a vertex
v\in U_{1}
satisfying
d_{G}(v,U_{1})>\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|
, we move
v
into some set
U_{i}
,
i>1
, whose size is below the ideal value. This operation preserves the super-regularity of the pairs between distinct parts, since
v
has many neighbors outside
U_{1}
. We continue this process until either the ideal sizes are reached, or every vertex
v\in U_{1}
satisfies
d_{G}(v,U_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|
.
In the first case, the Blow-up Lemma completes the proof. Thus we may assume the latter condition holds.
Let
x=|U_{1}|-\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
.
We partition
U_{1}
into
U_{H}=\left\{v\in U_{1}:d_{G}(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n\right\}

U_{L}=U_{1}\setminus U_{H}
.
For any

, we have

d_{G}(v,U_{1})=d_{G}(v)-d_{G}(v,V(G)\setminus U_{1})\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k-1}+\gamma\right)n-\bigl(n-|U_{1}|\bigr)=x.

Since we are assuming
d_{G}(v,U_{1})\leq\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|
for all

, it follows that
x\leq\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|
.
By the Ore-type condition (
1.1
),
U_{L}
induces a clique in

. Since

, we have
|U_{L}|\leq\Delta(G[U_{1}])+1\leq\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|+1
.
Thus, for sufficiently large

|U_{H}|\geq(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|
.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a positive integer and let
\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})=\min_{v\in U_{H}}d_{G}(v,U_{1})

Then

\nu_{i}(G[U_{1}])\geq\frac{(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|}{2(i+1)\Delta(G[U_{1}])}.

Let

be a maximum collection of

. Note that
|V(\mathcal{S})|=t(i+1)

. For any vertex

It implies that

\displaystyle e_{G}(U,V(\mathcal{S}))

\displaystyle\geq e_{G}(U\cap U_{H},V(\mathcal{S}))\geq|U\cap U_{H}|(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)

\displaystyle\geq(|U_{H}|-|V(\mathcal{S})|)(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)\geq((1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|-t(i+1))(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1).

\displaystyle((1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|-t(i+1))(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)\leq t(i+1)\Delta(G[U_{1}]).

\displaystyle t\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)}{(i+1)(\Delta(G[U_{1}])+\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)}\geq\frac{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|(\delta_{U_{1}}(U_{H})-i+1)}{2(i+1)\Delta(G[U_{1}])},

where the last inequality holds as

\displaystyle\nu_{\omega}(G[U_{1}])\geq(x-\omega+1)\frac{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})|U_{1}|}{2(\omega+1)\varepsilon_{1}|U_{1}|}=(x-\omega+1)\frac{(1-2\varepsilon_{1})}{2(\omega+1)\varepsilon_{1}}

, and we may leave these vertices uncovered. Thus we may assume that

After this operation, the remaining sets

Extremal subcase (II):

. As before,
\mathcal{K}
denotes the cluster clique, i.e., the subgraph of
G
corresponding to a clique
K
in the reduced graph. Similarly,
\mathcal{B}_{i}
denotes the family of cluster cliques corresponding to
B_{i}
for
i\in[0,k-t]
. Our goal is to convert
every cluster clique
\mathcal{K}\in\mathcal{B}_{1},\mathcal{B}_{2},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{k-t-1}
to a copy of
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k-t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
. The remaining graphs in
\mathcal{B}_{k-t}
and
\mathcal{B}_{0}
are naturally partitioned into copies of
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k-t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
.

To explain why
this algorithm is feasible, we will reuse the calculations in Section
4
. It is easy to see that if
K^{\prime}\in B_{j-t}
,
K^{\prime\prime}\in B_{k-t}
(for
t\leq j<k
), came from
K^{j}\in\Phi_{j}
and
K^{k}\in\Phi_{k}
, respectively, and
K^{j}\hookrightarrow K^{k}
, then
K^{\prime}\hookrightarrow K^{\prime\prime}
. Thus, for any
K\in B_{j-t}
, the number of well-connected
(k-t)
-cliques is at least
\Lambda(K^{j})\geq\lambda_{i}
, if
K
was generated from
K^{j}
. In the key expressions (
4.3
) and (
4.4
), even though
s<\mu
, we use
\sigma_{i}=\sum_{j\geq i}j\varphi_{k-j}\geq C^{j_{0}}\varepsilon=\mu^{\prime}
for all
i\leq t
; then
\mu^{\prime}
plays the same role as
\mu
. Specifically, the term
\mu_{1}
in (
4.5
) is replaced by
\frac{\alpha}{1-\alpha}\mu^{\prime}
. Hence,
\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha=c_{1}\mu^{\prime}
, and eventually we find an
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k-t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
-factor of
V_{B}
.

Before inserting
V_{0}
into
V_{B}
, we will move at most
2\varepsilon^{\prime}|V_{B}|
vertices from
V_{B}
to
U_{i}
if

\displaystyle d(v,U_{i})<\beta_{1}|U_{i}|,

(5.3)

where
\varepsilon^{\prime}\ll\beta_{1}\ll\mu^{\prime}
. We may move additional vertices from
\mathcal{R}_{k-t}^{\alpha^{\prime}}(L_{1})
to
V_{0}
in order to maintain the ratio of cluster sizes as
(1,\ldots,1,\alpha^{\prime})
. If any cluster loses more than half of its vertices due to (
5.3
), all vertices in that clique are removed to
V_{0}
. It is easy to verify that the resulting exceptional set
V_{0}
satisfies
|V_{0}|<2\varepsilon^{\prime}n/\alpha^{\prime}
.

To complete the proof of the extremal cases, we proceed by cases based on the sizes of
U_{i}
.

(a) The ideal case:

|U_{i}|=\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
for all
i\in[1,t]
.

Since the density between all pairs of
U_{i}
is almost
1
, we first apply the Blow-up Lemma (Lemma
2.3
) to
U_{1}\cup\ldots\cup U_{t}
to obtain a
K_{t}(\omega)
-factor. Note that by (
5.2
), the neighborhood of any copy of
K_{t}(\omega)
in
V_{B}
is almost the entire
V_{B}
. Let
V_{bad}=\{v\in V_{B}:d_{G}(v,A)\leq(1-\beta_{2})|A|\}
,
where
\varepsilon^{\prime}\ll\beta_{2}\ll\beta_{1}
. It is easy to see that
|V_{bad}|<\frac{\varepsilon^{\prime}}{\beta_{2}}n<\beta_{2}n
.

We assume that after inserting
V_{0}
into
V_{B}
, we obtain an
H_{0}
-tiling such that each copy of
H_{0}
contains at most one vertex of
V_{bad}
. This assumption leads to an easy matching
between
H_{0}
copies from
V_{B}
and
K_{t}(\omega)
copies from
V_{A}
. We can first use the greedy algorithm to
match those
H_{0}
’s that contain vertices from
V_{bad}
, then apply Hall’s Theorem to handle the remaining vertices. The number of uncovered vertices in the
H
-tiling of
G
will be proportional to the number of leftover vertices from the
H_{0}
-tiling in
V_{B}
.

To ensure each copy of
H_{k-t}
contains at most one
V_{bad}
vertex, we move all
V_{bad}
vertices (and some additional vertices to maintain cluster size ratios in those cliques) to
V_{0}
. Since
\beta_{2}\ll\mu
, we can still insert vertices in the new
V_{0}
back into
V_{B}
.

(b) The defective cases:
Suppose first that for some
i_{1}\in[1,t]
,

|U_{i_{1}}|<\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
. In this case, instead of using only copies of
K_{t}(\omega)
in
V_{A}
, we use copies of the
t
-partite graph obtained by placing the
\sigma
-vertex class in
U_{i_{1}}
and the
\omega
-vertex classes in the remaining
U_{i}
’s. To complete each copy of
H
, we match such a copy in
V_{A}
with a copy of
K_{k-t}(\omega)
in
V_{B}
. This is feasible because
\alpha^{\prime}-\alpha=c_{1}\mu^{\prime}\gg\varepsilon^{\prime}
, so the tiling of
V_{B}
contains enough flexibility to supply the required number of
K_{k-t}(\omega)
-copies.

If for some
i_{1}\in[1,t]
,
|U_{i_{1}}|>\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
. As in Subcase (I), we find
|U_{i_{1}}|-\left(\frac{1}{k-1}-\gamma\right)n
vertex-disjoint
\omega
-stars inside
G[U_{i_{1}}]
. We then move their centers to parts whose sizes are below the ideal value, either to another
U_{i}
or to
V_{B}
, and remove the corresponding copies of
H
. This balances the sizes of the parts up to a bounded error. After this balancing step, the argument reduces to the ideal case above.

This completes the proof of the extremal case
t<k-1
, and hence finishes the proof of Theorem
1.11
when
\sigma<\omega
.

6
The case when
\sigma=\omega

In this section, we prove Theorem
1.10
in the case
\sigma=\omega
. In this case
H=K_{k}(\omega)
,
h=|V(H)|=k\omega
, and
\alpha=\sigma/\omega=1
. The Ore-type condition (
1.1
) is equivalent to the following: for all nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in V(G)
,

\displaystyle d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n.

(6.1)

The proof follows the same general strategy as in Section
4
, but is simpler because the target graph is balanced. Recall that

\displaystyle 0<\varepsilon\ll d\ll\mu\ll 1.

By Lemma
2.2
, for all nonadjacent clusters
V_{i},V_{j}\in V(R)
,

d_{R}(V_{i})+d_{R}(V_{j})\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell.

In the balanced case, it is enough to find a
K_{k}
-tiling in the reduced graph
R
. The following Ore-type lemma allows us to handle the error term
2(d+2\varepsilon)\ell
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices, and let
k\geq 2
be an integer and
s
be a natural number. If for all nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in V(G)
,

d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n-2s,

then
G
has a
K_{k}
-tiling covering all but at most
k(k-1)s+(k-1)^{2}
vertices.

Proof.

Let
r
be an integer with
0\leq r\leq k-1
such that
\widetilde{n}:=n+ks+r
is divisible by
k
. Add
ks+r
new vertices to
G
, and join every new vertex to every other vertex. Denote the resulting graph by
\widetilde{G}
. For any two nonadjacent vertices
x,y\in V(\widetilde{G})
, we must have
x,y\in V(G)
. Hence

d_{\widetilde{G}}(x)+d_{\widetilde{G}}(y)=d_{G}(x)+d_{G}(y)+2(ks+r)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n-2s+2(ks+r)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)\widetilde{n}.

Therefore, by Theorem
1.8
, the graph
\widetilde{G}
has a
K_{k}
-factor.

Removing all copies of
K_{k}
that contain at least one new vertex leaves a
K_{k}
-tiling of
G
. The number of new vertices is at most
ks+k-1
, and each copy of
K_{k}
containing a new vertex contains at most
k-1
original vertices of
G
. Therefore, the number of uncovered vertices of
G
is at most
(k-1)(ks+k-1)=k(k-1)s+(k-1)^{2}
.
∎

6.1
Decomposition lemma

To prepare for the application of the Blow-up Lemma, we prove a balanced analogue of Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 6.2
.

For sufficiently large
\ell
, after moving at most
2k^{2}dn
vertices of
G
into the exceptional set
V_{0}
, the graph
G^{\prime\prime}=G\setminus V_{0}
can be decomposed into vertex-disjoint balanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-cliques

\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{1}(L)
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.2
, for all nonadjacent
X,Y\in V(R)
,
d_{R}(X)+d_{R}(Y)\geq 2\left(1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell
. Applying Lemma
6.1
to
R
with
s=(d+2\varepsilon)\ell
, we obtain a
K_{k}
-tiling of
R
covering all but at most
k(k-1)(d+2\varepsilon)\ell+(k-1)^{2}
vertices of
R
.

We move all clusters corresponding to these uncovered vertices of
R
into the exceptional set
V_{0}
. Since
\varepsilon\leq d\ll 1
and
\ell
is sufficiently large, the number of vertices moved is at most

\left(k(k-1)(d+2\varepsilon)\ell+(k-1)^{2}\right)L\leq 2k(k-1)dn\leq 2k^{2}dn.

Thus, after updating
V_{0}
, we still have
|V_{0}|\leq 2k^{2}dn
.
Each
K_{k}
in the
K_{k}
-tiling of
R
corresponds to
k
clusters
V_{i_{1}},V_{i_{2}},\ldots,V_{i_{k}}
in
G^{\prime\prime}
, such that every pair of clusters forms an
\varepsilon
-regular pair of density greater than
d
by the definition of the reduced graph. Since all clusters have the same size
L
, these clusters form a balanced
\varepsilon
-regular
k
-clique
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{1}(L)
.
The collection of all such regular
k
-cliques gives the desired decomposition of
G^{\prime\prime}
.
∎

6.2
Handling exceptional vertices and bounding the leftover vertices

The following lemma is the balanced-case analogue of Lemma
4.3
.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that
\varepsilon\leq\theta\ll 1
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex graph satisfying (
6.1
), and let
V_{0}\subseteq V(G)
be an exceptional set with
|V_{0}|\leq\theta n
. If
G^{\prime\prime}=G\setminus V_{0}
has an
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{1}(L_{1})
-factor for sufficiently large
L_{1}
. Then
G
contains an
H
-tiling covering all but at most
3k^{2}\omega+h
vertices.

We use notation consistent with Subsection
4.2
.

•

Let
\mathbf{R}
be the
\mathcal{R}_{\varepsilon,k}^{1}(L_{1})
-factor of
G^{\prime\prime}
, with each clique
\mathcal{R}\in\mathbf{R}
having color-classes
U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}
and
|U_{i}|=L_{1}
for all
i\in[1,k]
.

•

For a vertex
v\in V_{0}
and a cluster
C\in V(R)
, we write
v\sim C
if
d_{G}(v,C)\geq d|C|
.

Proof of Lemma
6.3
.

We partition
V_{0}
into two subsets. Let
V_{0}^{1}=\left\{x\in V_{0}:d_{G}(x)<\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n\right\}
and let

V_{0}^{2}=V_{0}\setminus V_{0}^{1}
. By the Ore-type condition (
6.1
), the set
V_{0}^{1}
induces a clique in
G
. Hence
G[V_{0}^{1}]
can be tiled by copies of
H=K_{k}(\omega)
, leaving at most
h-1
vertices uncovered. It remains to cover the vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
.

Phase I: Inserting exceptional vertices.

We process the vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
in an arbitrary order. For each
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, we choose an available clique
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
such that

v\sim U_{i}\quad\text{for all but at most one cluster }U_{i}\in\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}.

(6.2)

Lemma
4.4
guarantees that we can construct a copy of
H
containing
v
as follows.

•

If
v\sim U_{i}
for all
i\in[1,k]
, choose any cluster, say
U_{1}
, to contain the color-class of
H
containing
v
. We take
\omega-1
additional vertices from
U_{1}
, and
\omega
vertices from each of the other
k-1
clusters.

•

If
v\nsim U_{j}
for exactly one
j\in[1,k]
, then we place
v
in the color-class corresponding to
U_{j}
. We take
\omega-1
additional vertices from
U_{j}
, and
\omega
vertices from each of the other
k-1
clusters.

To prevent a cluster from losing too many vertices, we stop considering an element of
\mathbf{R}
if it has been selected
\theta_{1}L_{1}
times, where
\theta_{1}=\sqrt{\theta}
.
The following proposition guarantees that sufficiently many valid cliques are always available.

Proposition 6.4
.

For every vertex
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, regardless of the order in which the vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
are processed, at least a
1/3
-proportion of the elements of
\mathbf{R}
satisfy (
6.2
).

Proof.

Let
v\in V_{0}^{2}
be the
(t+1)
-st vertex to be processed, where
t
denotes the number of vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
that have already been processed. For these first
t
vertices, we have removed
t
pairwise disjoint copies of
H
, each containing exactly one processed vertex of
V_{0}^{2}
and
h-1
vertices from elements of
\mathbf{R}
.

Let
G^{*}
be the subgraph induced by the vertices remaining after these
t
copies have been removed. Since
v\in V_{0}^{2}
, we have
d_{G}(v)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n
.
Moreover, at most
|V_{0}|\leq\theta n
vertices lie in the exceptional set, and at most
t(h-1)
vertices have been removed from elements of
\mathbf{R}
. Hence

d_{G}(v,V(G^{*}))\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}\right)n-\theta n-t(h-1).

(6.3)

Let
m
be the fraction of cliques in
\mathbf{R}
satisfying (
6.2
). If a clique does not satisfy (
6.2
), then
v
fails to be
d
-adjacent to at least two of its clusters. Hence such a clique contributes at most
(k-2+2d)L_{1}
neighbors of
v
. A clique satisfying (
6.2
) contributes at most
kL_{1}
neighbors. Therefore,

d_{G}(v,V(G^{*}))\leq\left((1-m)\frac{k-2+2d}{k}+m\right)(n-\theta n-t(h-1)).

(6.4)

Combining (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), we obtain
1-\frac{1}{k}-\theta h\leq(1-m)\frac{k-2+2d}{k}+m
.
Since
d,\theta\ll 1
, so
m\geq\frac{1-2d-k\theta h}{2-2d}\geq 1/3
.
∎

The proportion of unavailable cliques is at most

\frac{|V_{0}|/(\theta_{1}L_{1})}{|\mathbf{R}|}\leq\frac{\theta n/(\sqrt{\theta}L_{1})}{n/(kL_{1})}=k\sqrt{\theta}.

Since
k\sqrt{\theta}<1/3
, Proposition
6.4
ensures that an available clique satisfying (
6.2
) exists for every vertex of
V_{0}^{2}
. Thus all vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
can be covered.
After processing all vertices of
V_{0}^{2}
, at most
h-1
vertices from
V_{0}^{1}
remain uncovered.

We now make all regular pairs super-regular. For each clique
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
and each vertex
u\in U_{i}
, if there exists
j\neq i
such that
d_{G}(u,U_{j})<(d-\varepsilon)|U_{j}|
, then we move
u
into the exceptional set. By
\varepsilon
-regularity, each cluster loses at most
(k-1)\varepsilon|U_{i}|
vertices in this way. Hence the number of newly exceptional vertices is at most
k(k-1)\varepsilon n
. Since
\varepsilon\ll\theta
, these new exceptional vertices can be processed by the same argument as above. After doing this, every pair inside each remaining clique is
(\varepsilon,d/2)
-super-regular.

Let
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
denote the resulting family of super-regular balanced
k
-cliques.

Phase II: Bounding the leftover vertices.

We first bound the number of uncovered vertices in a single super-regular balanced clique.

Proposition 6.5
.

Each element of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
has an
H
-tiling that covers all but at most
k(\omega-1)
vertices.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
. Take a maximum
H
-tiling of
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
, and let
a_{i}
be the number of uncovered vertices in
U_{i}
. We claim that
a_{i}<\omega
for every
i\in[1,k]
. Otherwise, if
a_{i}\geq\omega
for every
i
, then the uncovered vertices contain
\omega
vertices from each cluster. Since the pairs inside
\mathcal{R}^{\prime}
are super-regular, the Blow-up Lemma gives one more copy of
H=K_{k}(\omega)
, contradicting the maximality of the tiling. Hence
a_{i}\leq\omega-1
for all
i\in[1,k]
. Therefore,
\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\leq k(\omega-1)
.
This proves the proposition.
∎

Applying Proposition
6.5
independently to every element of
\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
gives an
H
-tiling with at most
k(\omega-1)|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|
uncovered vertices. We now use the same directed-graph transfer argument as in Subsection
4.2
to reduce this to a constant independent of
\varepsilon
.

Define a directed graph
\mathcal{D}
on the clusters of
G^{\prime\prime}
. Let
U
be a cluster, and let
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}^{\prime}
. There is an edge
(U,U_{1})\in E(\mathcal{D})
if
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)

for all
i\neq 1
. The adjacencies in
R
are not affected by the vertex removals in Phase I, so
\mathcal{D}
is well-defined.

We next estimate the out-degree of high-degree clusters.

Proposition 6.6
.

For any
U\in V(\mathcal{D})
with
d_{R}(U)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\right)|V(R)|
, we have
d_{\mathcal{D}}^{+}(U)\geq\frac{1}{2k}|V(\mathcal{D})|
.

Proof.

For a given
U\in V(\mathcal{D})
, let
m_{1}
,
m_{2}
denote the fraction of cliques
\mathcal{R}=\{U_{1},U_{2},\ldots,U_{k}\}\in\mathbf{R}
for which
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
but
(U,U_{j})\notin E(R)
for all
i\neq j
with
j\neq k
, and
(U,U_{i})\in E(R)
for all
i\in[1,k]
, respectively. By the same degree counting as in Proposition
4.8
, the degree condition of the reduced graph
d_{R}(U)\geq\left(1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\right)\ell
gives

1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\leq\frac{k-2}{k}+\frac{m_{1}}{k}+\frac{2m_{2}}{k}.

Therefore,
m_{1}+2m_{2}\geq 1-k(d+2\varepsilon)\geq\frac{1}{2}
, provided
d
and
\varepsilon
are sufficiently small.

By the definition of
\mathcal{D}
, a clique counted by
m_{1}
gives one out-neighbor of
U
, while a clique counted by
m_{2}
gives
k
out-neighbors of
U
. Since
|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|=|V(\mathcal{D})|/k
, we obtain

d_{\mathcal{D}}^{+}(U)=(m_{1}+km_{2})|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|\geq(m_{1}+2m_{2})|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2}|\mathbf{R}^{\prime}|\geq\frac{1}{2k}|V(\mathcal{D})|.\qed

Let
J_{L}=\left\{U\in V(\mathcal{D}):d_{R}(U)<\left(1-\frac{1}{k}-d-2\varepsilon\right)|V(R)|\right\}
, and let
J_{H}=V(\mathcal{D})\setminus J_{L}
. By the Ore-type condition on
R
, the set
J_{L}
induces a clique in
R
. Thus any leftover vertices transferred into clusters of
J_{L}
can be handled inside this clique structure, leaving at most
k
additional vertices uncovered.

For
J_{H}
, Proposition
6.6
and Lemma
4.7
imply that the high-degree part of the sink set has size at most
\frac{|V(\mathcal{D})|}{\frac{1}{2k}|V(\mathcal{D})|+1}\leq 2k
.
We transfer leftover vertices as in Subsection
4.2
. For each cluster
C
, let
\operatorname{extra}(C)
be the number of vertices left uncovered by the tiling from Proposition
6.5
. If
C
is not in the sink set, choose a directed path from
C
to the sink set and move the extra vertices along this path. Each directed edge allows us to remove copies of
H
using one vertex from the preceding cluster and
\omega
vertices from each of the other
k-1
clusters of the next clique. Since the total number of transferred vertices is bounded by a constant and
L_{1}
is sufficiently large, this does not affect super-regularity.

After all extra vertices have been transferred, all leftover vertices are concentrated in at most
2k
high-degree sink clusters, together with at most
k
additional vertices arising from the low-degree clique structure. Hence, by Proposition
6.5
, the total number of uncovered vertices is at most
k(\omega-1)\cdot 2k+k+h\leq 3k^{2}\omega+h
.

This proves Lemma
6.3
.
∎

Combining Lemma
6.2
and Lemma
6.3
, we obtain an
H
-tiling of
G
covering all but at most
3k^{2}\omega+h
vertices. Since
H
is fixed, this is a constant depending only on
H
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.11
in the case
\sigma=\omega
.

7
Concluding remarks

In this paper, we strengthen Theorem
1.7
of Shokoufandeh and Zhao by replacing the minimum-degree condition with an Ore-type condition. This also resolves a conjecture proposed by Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown
[
12
]
.
In the same paper, Kühn, Osthus, and Treglown raised several problems concerning the existence of perfect tilings in graphs with respect to the critical chromatic number. Relatedly, Kühn and Osthus
[
13
]
obtained a minimum-degree condition for perfect tilings and provided a polynomial-time algorithm. It would be interesting to establish Ore-type conditions for perfect tilings involving the critical chromatic number, from both theoretical and algorithmic perspectives.
