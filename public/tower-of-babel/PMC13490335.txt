Efficacy and safety of transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) with tislelizumab and tyrosine kinase inhibitors versus TACE for intermediate and advanced HCC
Authors: Saikang Tang, Xue Yan, Tao Gong, Weihua Zhi, Yingming Gao, Yue Han
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background & AimsTransarterial chemoembolization (TACE) combined with targeted therapy and immunotherapy is a promising approach for intermediate and advanced hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC). This study aimed to compare the efficacy and safety of TACE plus tislelizumab and tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) versus TACE alone.MethodsThis retrospective study analyzed patients with intermediate and advanced HCC treated from February 2018 to September 2023. Patients were divided into two groups: the combination therapy group (TACE plus tislelizumab and TKIs) and the monotherapy group (TACE alone). The primary endpoint was progression-free survival PFS), and secondary endpoints included objective response rate (ORR), disease control rate (DCR), overall survival (OS), and safety. Cox regression, sensitivity analysis, Kaplan–Meier survival curves, and restricted mean survival time (RMST) analysis were performed to assess the robustness of the PFS benefit.ResultsA total of 62 patients were included (30 in the combination group, 32 in the monotherapy group), with balanced baseline characteristics. After a median follow-up of 33.3 months, median PFS was 11.7 months in the combination group and 3.8 months in the monotherapy group (hazard ratio [HR], 0.43; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.24–0.77; P = 0.0034). This benefit persisted after multivariable adjustment (adjusted HR, 0.28; 95% CI 0.14–0.57; P < 0.001) and was confirmed by sensitivity and RMST analyses at 12, 24, 36, and 48 months (all P < 0.01). The combination group also showed favorable outcomes in secondary endpoints including ORR, DCR, and OS rates. Four patients (13.3%) in the combination group underwent downstaging to curative treatment, with PFS ranging from 14.8 to 49.2 months. Grade ≥ 3 adverse events occurred in 10.0% of the combination group and in none of the monotherapy group, with no treatment-related deaths.ConclusionsTACE combined with tislelizumab and TKIs significantly improved PFS compared with TACE alone, with a manageable safety profile, in patients with intermediate and advanced HCC. This regimen may offer a potential conversion option for selected patients in this population.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00432-026-06541-6.

Introduction

Primary liver cancer PLC) is the sixth most common cancer worldwide and ranks third in cancer-related deaths (Bray et al. 2024; Sung et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2023a). In China, PLC is the fourth most common cancer and the second leading cause of cancer death (Wei et al. 2020; Zhou et al. 1990; Chen et al. 2015), with an age-standardized five-year relative survival rate of 14.4% (Zeng et al. 2019). Hepatocellular carcinoma (HCC) accounts for 75–85% of all cases of PLC, most commonly arising in the context of cirrhosis secondary to chronic hepatitis B virus (HBV) or hepatitis C virus (HCV) infection (Bray et al. 2024; Sung et al. 2020; Yang et al. 2023a). Due to its subtle early symptoms, about 70–80% of cases are diagnosed at advanced stages, which means limited effective treatment options and poor prognosis.

Transarterial chemoembolization (TACE) remains the standard treatment for intermediate HCC (Reig et al. 2022; Chen et al. 2020; Zhou et al. 2022; Cho et al. 2023; Park 2022; Lencioni et al. 2016) and is also widely used as a locoregional intervention to control intrahepatic disease in selected patients with advanced HCC, particularly in Asian clinical practice (Chen et al. 2020; Zhou et al. 2022; Cho et al. 2023; Park 2022); however, one-year survival rates with TACE alone is limited, which can be as low as 35.3% in patients with portal vein tumor thrombus PVTT) (Yuan et al. 2023). The advent of targeted therapy, mainly tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs), and immunotherapy targeting programmed death-1 PD-1) and programmed death-Ligand 1 PD-L1), has opened new avenues for combining these treatments with TACE to improve outcomes in both intermediate and advanced HCC (Reig et al. 2022; Zhou et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2023b, 2024; Lin et al. 2023).

A series of studies have progressively highlighted the potential benefits of such combinations. Retrospective analyses, such as the CHANCE001 multi-center study (Zhu et al. 2023), demonstrated improved progression-free survival PFS), overall survival (OS), and objective response rate (ORR) with TACE combined with PD-1/PD-L1 inhibitors and molecular targeted therapies, compared to TACE alone. Building on these findings, the CHANCE2211 study (Jin et al. 2023) specifically investigated the efficacy of TACE in combination with camrelizumab and apatinib, further underscoring the importance of exploring specific combination therapies. More recently, the randomized phase II CHANCE2005/CARES-005 trial demonstrated that TACE combined with camrelizumab and rivoceranib significantly prolonged PFS compared with TACE alone in patients with unresectable HCC (median PFS, 10.8 vs. 3.2 months; hazard ratio [HR], 0.34; 95% confidence interval [CI], 0.24–0.50; P < 0.001), providing prospective evidence for this combination strategy (Zhu et al. 2026). Prospective phase III studies such as LEAP-012 (Kudo et al. 2025) and EMERALD-1 (Sangro et al. 2025) have further supported the potential of combining TACE with specific regimens like lenvatinib plus pembrolizumab or durvalumab plus bevacizumab to enhance survival and response rates in advanced HCC.

However, the immunotherapy backbones in these studies were primarily camrelizumab, pembrolizumab, or durvalumab; data on TACE combined with tislelizumab and TKIs remain scarce. Tislelizumab, a humanized IgG4 anti-PD-1 monoclonal antibody, has demonstrated non-inferior OS compared to sorafenib as monotherapy for HCC in the multi-center, prospective RATIONALE-301 study (Qin et al. 2023; Finn et al. 2024), but its monotherapy PFS was limited, underscoring the need for more effective combination approaches.

Therefore, this study aimed to explore the value of this triple therapy approach by comparing the efficacy and safety of TACE alone versus TACE combined with tislelizumab and TKIs in patients with intermediate and advanced HCC, and to contribute real‑world evidence to this evolving field.

Materials and methods

Patients

Data of patients with intermediate and advanced HCC treated at the Department of Interventional Therapy, Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences, between February 2018 and September 2023, were retrospectively analyzed. Patients were divided into two groups based on treatment regimen: the combination therapy group who received TACE supplemented with tislelizumab and TKIs (lenvatinib or regorafenib, based on clinical treatment needs), and the monotherapy group who received TACE alone. Of note, some patients with extrahepatic metastasis in the monotherapy group received TACE alone, as they were enrolled early in the study period when combined systemic therapy was not yet widely accessible.

Inclusion criteria: (1) diagnosed with HCC by clinical or pathological evaluation; (2) Barcelona Clinic Liver Cancer (BCLC) stage B or C; (3) treatment with TACE plus tislelizumab and TKIs, or TACE alone; (4) Child–Pugh class A or B; (5) Albumin-bilirubin(ALBI) grade 1or 2; and (6) Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group Performance Status (ECOG PS) score of 0 or 1. Exclusion criteria: (1) no assessable lesions by modified Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (mRECIST) criteria; (2) absence of baseline or follow-up imaging data; and (3) diagnosis of another malignancy within the past five years.

Clinical data included age, gender, hepatitis virus infection status, Child–Pugh class, ALBI grade (albumin‑bilirubin grade, calculated from serum albumin and total bilirubin), ECOG PS score, BCLC stage, baseline alpha-fetoprotein (AFP) levels, and tumor-related variables, including the number of tumors, maximum tumor diameter, tumor burden according to the up‑to‑7 criteria (sum of the largest tumor diameter in centimeters and the number of tumors), presence of macrovascular invasion (defined as tumor invasion into major vessels such as the portal vein or hepatic vein), and presence of extrahepatic metastasis.

This study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the Cancer Hospital, Chinese Academy of Medical Sciences (NCC5598). All data have been fully anonymized. Individual informed consent was waived given the anonymized nature of data and minimal risk to participants.

Clinical treatment procedure

All patients underwent TACE. Using the Seldinger technique, a catheter was inserted percutaneously into the femoral artery and positioned in the celiac trunk or common hepatic artery for digital subtraction angiography, identifying tumor-feeding arteries. Chemotherapeutic agents (oxaliplatin, paclitaxel, lobaplatin, hydroxycamptothecin) and embolic materials (iodized oil, gelatin sponge particles) were delivered intra-arterially. If necessary, chemotherapy infusion through tumor-feeding arteries was performed. The frequency of TACE was determined by patient response and changes in liver function and physical condition.

In the combination therapy group, tislelizumab and TKIs were initiated within 6 weeks before or after the first TACE session. The specific interval was determined by the treating physician based on the patient’s clinical condition and readiness for systemic treatment. Tislelizumab was administered intravenously at 200 mg every three weeks. Dose reduction due to adverse events (AEs) was not allowed, but intermittent dosing was permitted. Treatment continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity. TKIs, including lenvatinib (8 or 12 mg daily based on body weight < 60 kg or ≥ 60 kg, respectively) and regorafenib (160 mg daily for 3 weeks on/1 week off), were administered at standard doses, with dose reductions allowed in response to AEs. Treatment with TKIs continued until disease progression or unacceptable toxicity.

For patients considered for conversion to curative therapy (surgery or ablation), multidisciplinary team consensus was mandatory. Eligibility criteria included: (1) tumor downstaging or significant shrinkage meeting radical intervention criteria; (2) treatment tolerance: hepatic reserve (ICG-R15 ≤ 20%), cardiopulmonary fitness, ECOG ≤ 1, with ablation substituted for high-surgical-risk cases; (3) documented patient/family consent after comprehensive risk–benefit counseling.

Safety

AEs were monitored and evaluated based on the Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events (CTCAE, version 5.0). Due to the retrospective design, AE attribution to specific interventions (TACE, TKI, or immunotherapy) was not systematically recorded. Instead, a qualitative attribution based on known safety profiles and the differential incidence of events between the two groups was performed to facilitate interpretation of the safety data.

Follow-up and outcome assessment

Follow-up visits occurred approximately every six weeks, including enhanced CT or MRI scans. OS was defined as the time from the first TACE, tislelizumab, or TKIs treatment to death or last follow-up. PFS was the time from initial treatment to disease progression or last follow-up. The 12-month and 18-month OS rates were calculated. The mRECIST criteria were used to assess efficacy, measuring ORR and disease control rate (DCR). The primary endpoint was PFS, and secondary endpoints included ORR, DCR, OS, and safety.

Statistical analysis

All enrolled patient data were subjected to statistical analysis. Continuous variables were presented as the median and interquartile range (IQR). Categorical variables were presented as counts and percentages. Qualitative variables were assessed using the chi-square or Fisher’s exact test, while quantitative variables were analyzed using the t-test or Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Kaplan–Meier methods were used to estimate OS and PFS rates. Univariable and multivariable Cox proportional hazards regression models were performed to identify factors associated with PFS. In the univariable analysis, variables with a P value < 0.10 or those considered clinically important were candidates for multivariable regression analysis. Backward stepwise selection was applied. The HR values and 95% CIs were calculated. The restricted mean survival time (RMST) model provides a robust estimation of survival by estimating the difference in mean survival time between groups (RMST difference). In this study, RMST differences for PFS were calculated at 12, 24, 36, and 48 months, comparing the combination group versus the monotherapy group. A positive RMST difference indicates longer mean PFS time in the combination group, reflecting improved clinical outcomes. A sensitivity analysis was performed excluding patients who received regorafenib in the combination group, to assess whether TKI heterogeneity influenced the primary PFS result. Statistical significance was defined as a P value < 0.05. All data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics for Windows (version 27.0; IBM Corp., Armonk, N.Y., USA) and R (version 4.6.0).

Results

Baseline characteristics

A total of 62 patients were finally enrolled, including 30 patients in the combination group (median age 55.0 years, IQR 49.0–66.5 years; 28 male) and 32 in the monotherapy group (median age 60.5 years, IQR 50.0–66.8 years; 29 male). Baseline characteristics were generally balanced between the two groups (P > 0.05 for all comparisons; Table 1). BCLC stage C disease accounted for 46.7% of the combination group and 50.0% of the monotherapy group. Child‑Pugh class A was observed in 63.3% and 75.0% of patients, and ALBI grade 1 in 50.0% and 68.8%, respectively. Macrovascular invasion was present in 36.7% and 50.0% of patients, and extrahepatic spread in 23.3% and 21.9%. According to the up‑to‑7 criteria,22 patients (73.3%) in the combination group and 25 patients (78.1%) in the monotherapy group were beyond the criteria (> 7). Baseline median AFP was 72.1 ng/mL in the combination group and 304.1 ng/mL in the monotherapy group. In the combination group, 23 patients (76.7%) received lenvatinib, and 7 (23.3%) received regorafenib following progression on prior first‑line therapy. Effectiveness assessment

The median follow-up was 33.3 months based on reverse Kaplan–Meier method based on all enrolled patients, with no significant difference between the two groups (P = 0.11). The median PFS (mPFS) was 11.7 months (95% CI 5.1–18.4) in the combination group, significantly longer than 3.8 months (95% CI 0.9–6.7) in the monotherapy group (HR, 0.43; 95% CI 0.24–0.77; P = 0.0034) (Fig. 1). To further quantify the PFS benefit, RMST was estimated at 12, 24, 36, and 48 months. The combination group had consistently longer RMST than the monotherapy group, and the between‑group difference was statistically significant at all time points (RMST difference at 12 months, 1.6 months [95% CI 1.2–2.2]; P < 0.001; RMST difference at 24 months, 1.8 months [95% CI 1.2–2.6]; P = 0.004; RMST difference at 36 months, 2.0 months [95% CI 1.3–3.2]; P = 0.002; RMST difference at 48 months, 2.3 months [95% CI 1.4–3.8]; P < 0.001) (Supplementary Table S1).

A sensitivity analysis excluding the 7 regorafenib‑treated patients confirmed that the PFS benefit was not driven by TKI heterogeneity. Among the 23 patients in the lenvatinib‑only subgroup and the 32 patients in the monotherapy group, the lenvatinib‑only subgroup had a significantly longer median PFS (15.2 months; 95% CI 9.2–21.2) compared to the monotherapy group (3.8 months; 95% CI 0.9–6.7; HR, 0.096; 95% CI 0.037–0.252; P < 0.01) (Supplementary Table S2).

OS data remain immature, with only 10 events (33.3%) in the combination group and 18 events (56.3%) in the monotherapy group. At 12 and 18 months, OS rates were 78 versus 51% and 64 versus 47% in the combination and monotherapy groups, respectively. The ORR and DCR were also significantly higher in the combination group than monotherapy group (ORR, 50.0 vs. 18.8%, P = 0.009; DCR, 96.7 vs. 75.0%, P = 0.016) (Table 3). Safety

The incidence of AEs was 86.7% in the combination therapy group and 81.3% in the monotherapy group (Table 4). Grade 3 or higher AEs occurred in 10.0% of patients in the combination group, while no such events were observed in the monotherapy group. The most common AEs included fever (63.3 vs. 68.8%), abdominal pain (36.7 vs. 40.6%), and nausea (26.7 vs. 37.5%). In the combination group, 5 patients (16.7%) discontinued either immunotherapy or TKIs due to AEs. Importantly, no treatment-related deaths were reported in either group. Discussion

In this retrospective study, TACE combined with tislelizumab and TKIs significantly prolonged PFS compared with TACE alone in patients with intermediate and advanced HCC (median PFS, 11.7 vs. 3.8 months; HR, 0.43; P = 0.0034). This PFS benefit persisted after multivariable adjustment and was further supported by sensitivity and RMST analyses, underscoring the robustness of the primary endpoint finding. The combination group also showed numerically higher 12‑ and 18‑month OS rates and higher ORR and DCR. Additionally, four patients (13.3%) in the combination group—all of whom received lenvatinib as the TKI component—achieved sufficient downstaging to undergo curative treatment. While the incidence of AEs was higher in the combination group, the majority were mild to moderate, and no treatment‑related deaths occurred, underscoring the regimen’s manageable safety profile.

In the RATIONALE-301 multi-center prospective study (Qin et al. 2023; Finn et al. 2024), tislelizumab monotherapy demonstrated non-inferior mOS compared to sorafenib in patients with BCLC B or C stage HCC, though it was associated with a limited mPFS of 2.1 months (95% CI 2.1–3.5) versus 3.4 months (95% CI 2.2–4.1) with sorafenib. Similarly, lenvatinib monotherapy, despite its efficacy, reported an mPFS of 7.4 months (95% CI 6.9–8.8) in advanced HCC (Kudo et al. 2018; Vogel et al. 2021). In contrast, our study indicates that the combination of tislelizumab with TACE and targeted therapy achieved a substantially longer mPFS of 11.7 months (95% CI 5.1–18.4), underscoring the potential superiority of combination therapy over single-agent targeted therapy or immunotherapy in this setting.

While monotherapies demonstrate limited efficacy, dual regimens combining tislelizumab and lenvatinib have shown improved outcomes; however, the addition of TACE may confer further benefits. A recent multi-center, single-arm, Phase II trial investigating the combination of tislelizumab and lenvatinib reported an mPFS of 8.2 months (95% CI 6.8–NA) and a 12-month OS rate of 88.6% (95% CI 77.7%–94.4%), with an ORR of 46.8% (95% CI 34.0%–59.9%) and a DCR of 90.3% (95% CI 80.1%–96.4%) (Xu et al. 2024). The mPFS observed in our study was notably longer, suggesting that the addition of TACE to the tislelizumab-lenvatinib regimen may further enhance disease control. While the ORR in our study was comparable (50.0% vs. 46.8%), the DCR was slightly higher (96.7% vs. 90.3%). The 12-month OS rate in our study was lower (78% vs. 88.6%), likely reflecting the higher proportion of patients with macrovascular invasion in our cohort (36.7% vs. 10.9%). Moreover, multi-center evidence confirms that the full triplet regimen significantly outperforms TACE plus lenvatinib alone in both OS and PFS (mOS: 26.0 months versus 20.0 months, P = 0.004; mPFS: 14.0 months versus 9.0 months, P = 0.039) (Zhao et al. 2024), suggesting that TACE, tislelizumab, and TKIs each contribute essential efficacy benefits to the combination strategy.

Compared with other TACE-based combination regimens evaluated in prospective trials, our findings are similarly encouraging. The LEAP-012 study reported an mPFS of 14.6 months for TACE plus lenvatinib and pembrolizumab (Kudo et al. 2025), and EMERALD-1 reported an mPFS of 15.0 months for TACE plus durvalumab and bevacizumab (Sangro et al. 2025). Although these mPFS values are numerically higher than our 11.7 months, both LEAP-012 and EMERALD-1 predominantly enrolled intermediate-stage patients, with low rates of macrovascular invasion (0% and 8%, respectively) and no extrahepatic metastasis, whereas our cohort included 36.7% of patients with macrovascular invasion and 22.6% with extrahepatic spread. More recently, the randomized phase II CHANCE2005/CARES-005 trial evaluated TACE combined with camrelizumab and rivoceranib versus TACE alone in a population with high tumor burden (89% beyond up-to-7 criteria, 42% with macrovascular invasion) but without extrahepatic metastasis, and reported an mPFS of 10.8 months in the combination arm (Zhu et al. 2026). This is consistent with our mPFS of 11.7 months, despite our cohort additionally including patients with extrahepatic metastasis. The comparable PFS observed across these two studies, which used different PD-1 inhibitors and TKIs, suggests that this triple therapy approach warrants further investigation. Whether this strategy may be applicable to patients with extrahepatic disease remains to be determined in prospective studies.

The superior efficacy of TACE combined with targeted therapy and immunotherapy is biologically plausible. TACE induces tumor ischemia and hypoxia, leading to upregulation of pro-angiogenic factors, including VEGF, VEGFR, FGF, PDGF, and angiogenins, which, may synergize with TKIs and immunotherapy by targeting these pathways (Liu et al. 2024; Song et al. 2024; Liang et al. 2024). Moreover, the high expression of PD-L1 observed in residual tumor tissue post-TACE provides an optimal therapeutic target for immune checkpoint inhibitors (Liang et al. 2024; Li et al. 2023; Llovet et al. 2021). Concurrently, TACE promotes tumor necrosis and the release of tumor antigens, enhancing the activation of tumor-specific CD8 + T cells while suppressing the proliferation of regulatory T cells, thereby amplifying the anti-tumor immune response Pinato et al. 2024; Tan et al. 2023). In turn, the combination of anti-PD-1 agents with TKIs further facilitates vascular normalization in HCC and potentiates immune-mediated anti-tumor effects (Xing et al. 2021; Shigeta et al. 2020). Through these synergistic mechanisms, TACE-based triple therapy may convert immunosuppressed "cold tumors" into immunologically active "hot tumors", thereby enhancing overall anti-tumor immunity.

Although the OS difference between the two groups did not reach statistical significance, a numerical improvement in 12- and 18-month OS rates was observed with the combination therapy. This is likely attributable to the small sample size and the immaturity of OS data, with median OS not reached in the combination group. Therefore, PFS was chosen as the primary endpoint to more directly reflect the treatment effect of the initially assigned regimen.

Regarding conversion therapy, reported rates for TACE combined with systemic therapy in the literature range from 12 to 56%, varying by treatment regimens and patient disease stages (Li et al. 2024; Chiang et al. 2023; Sun et al. 2021; Bi et al. 2023). In our study, four patients (13.3%) achieved sufficient downstaging to undergo curative-intent interventions, with PFS ranging from 14.8 to 49.2 months. All four patients received lenvatinib as the TKI component. While these observations are encouraging and suggest that first-line TKI‑based triple therapy may facilitate conversion in a subset of patients, they remain preliminary due to the small number of cases and the non‑randomized design. Prospective studies with standardized conversion criteria are warranted to further explore this potential.

In terms of safety, while the combination therapy group experienced a higher incidence of AEs, the majority were mild to moderate and manageable, aligning with the established safety profiles of the therapies used. The safety profile was deemed acceptable, with no treatment-related deaths reported in either group. A qualitative attribution of AEs based on known safety profiles is provided, with fever, abdominal pain, nausea, and vomiting predominantly related to TACE, and hand-foot syndrome, fatigue, hypertension, and diarrhea consistent with TKI-related toxicities. However, due to the retrospective design, formal causality assessment was not possible. Future prospective studies should incorporate protocol-specified toxicity stratification by therapeutic component to delineate agent-specific safety profiles. Notably, tislelizumab and relevant TKIs have been included in China’s National Reimbursement Drug List, which has improved treatment accessibility and supports the real-world applicability of this regimen.

This study has several limitations. First, as a single-center retrospective analysis with a small sample size, it is subject to selection bias; furthermore, the cohort was predominantly HBV-related, and generalizability to other etiologies requires further evaluation. Second, overall survival data remain immature, with median OS not reached in the combination group. Finally, AE attribution to specific interventions was not prospectively recorded, limiting agent-specific safety comparisons. Future prospective, multi-center studies with extended follow-up and protocol-specified safety assessment are warranted to validate these findings.

Conclusion

TACE combined with tislelizumab and TKIs significantly improved PFS compared with TACE alone in patients with intermediate and advanced HCC, with an acceptable safety profile. In addition, this regimen was associated with encouraging response rates, and downstaging to curative treatment was achieved in a subset of patients, suggesting a potential conversion role for selected patients in this population.
