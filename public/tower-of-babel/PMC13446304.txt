Single-cell and spatial transcriptomics uncover a THBS2⁺ CAF–KRT17⁺ malignant cell axis drives progression and immunotherapy resistance in pancreatic cancer
Authors: Rui Ma, Yao Xiao, Weixiong Zhu, Haofei Chen, Yeqian Feng, Yusheng Cheng, Junhong Guan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Highlights•THBS2+ CAFs are enriched in liver metastases and drive immune exclusion in PDAC.•PRRX2 is a master regulator of THBS2+ CAF differentiation.•THBS2-SDC4 axis mediates THBS2+ CAFs-KRT17+ malignant cells crosstalk in PDAC.•Co-infiltration of THBS2+ CAFs and KRT17+ malignant cells predicts unfavorable prognosis and resistance to immunotherapy.Keywords: Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, Tumor microenvironment, Cell-cell interaction, Immune evasion, Prognosis

Introduction

Pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma (PDAC) remains one of the most lethal malignancies, characterized by a dismal five-year survival rate and limited therapeutic efficacy [1,2]. The tumor microenvironment (TME) of PDAC is markedly complex, featuring dense desmoplastic stroma that constitutes up to 90% of the tumor mass [[3], [4], [5]]. This fibrotic stroma not only serves as a physical barrier limiting drug delivery but also creates an immunosuppressive niche that facilitates immune evasion and therapeutic resistance [6,7]. Cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs) represent the predominant cellular component of this stromal compartment and exhibit substantial functional heterogeneity [8]. While recent single-cell transcriptomic studies have identified distinct CAF subpopulations—including inflammatory CAFs (iCAFs), myofibroblastic CAFs (myCAFs), and antigen-presenting CAFs (apCAFs)—the precise functional specialization of these subsets in metastatic progression and immune regulation remains incompletely understood [9].

The heterogeneity of CAFs extends beyond their phenotypic diversity to their spatial distribution and dynamic evolution during tumor progression. Emerging evidence suggests that specific CAF subsets may differentially influence metastatic seeding, with certain fibroblast populations promoting organ-specific colonization through paracrine signaling and extracellular matrix (ECM) remodeling [10,11]. Furthermore, the reciprocal crosstalk between CAFs and malignant epithelial cells constitutes a critical determinant of tumor aggressiveness [12,13]. However, the ligand-receptor axes governing these interactions, particularly those facilitating metastatic niche formation, have not been systematically characterized in the context of PDAC.

Concurrently, the epithelial compartment of PDAC displays considerable transcriptional plasticity, with distinct malignant subpopulations exhibiting varying metastatic potentials and therapeutic responses. Recent studies employing non-negative matrix factorization (NMF) have identified transcriptional metaprograms associated with different phenotypes or biological activities in malignant cells [14]. Nevertheless, how specific malignant subpopulations interact with specialized CAF subsets to coordinate metastatic spread and immune evasion remains largely unexplored. Understanding these stromal-epithelial communication networks is essential for developing targeted interventions that disrupt the pro-tumorigenic microenvironment.

In this study, we performed an integrated analysis of 469,137 single cells from eight independent PDAC cohorts to construct a comprehensive atlas of cellular heterogeneity. Through systematic sub-clustering of fibroblast populations, we identified a THBS2⁺ ECM-remodeling CAF subset that, while sharing some features with previously described matrix-producing CAFs, exhibits a distinct transcriptional program characterized by specific enrichment of THBS2, POSTN, and collagen family members, with unique enrichment in liver metastases and spatial organization within immune-excluded niches. This subset has not been systematically characterized in the context of PDAC metastasis and immune exclusion. These THBS2⁺ CAFs exhibited specific enrichment in liver metastases and demonstrated potent capacity for establishing an immune-excluded microenvironment. We further discovered that THBS2⁺ CAFs engage in specific ligand-receptor interactions with KRT17⁺ malignant cells via the THBS2-syndecan-4 (SDC4) axis, forming a reciprocal signaling network that promotes metastatic progression. Notably, the co-infiltration of THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells emerged as a robust predictor of poor prognosis and immunotherapy resistance across multiple independent cohorts. Our findings elucidate a novel stromal-epithelial axis driving PDAC progression and provide a rationale for therapeutic strategies targeting this interaction.

Materials and methods

Data acquisition and single-cell RNA sequencing data integration

Publicly available single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) datasets from 8 independent studies of PDAC were downloaded from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) and Genome Sequence Archive (GSA) databases, including CRA001160 [15], GSE154778 [16], GSE197177 [17], GSE205013 [18], GSE231535 [19], GSE242230 [20], GSE278688 [21], and GSE281288 [22]. These datasets encompassed diverse clinical stages (Stage I-IV), tissue sources (normal pancreas, primary tumors, and metastatic lesions), and sequencing platforms (10x Genomics Chromium, Smart-seq2). Quality control was performed for each dataset independently using the Seurat package (v4.0) in R (v4.1.0). Cells with fewer than 200 detected genes, greater than 25% mitochondrial reads, or fewer than 3 cells per gene were excluded. DoubletFinder (v2.0.3) was applied with an expected doublet rate of 7.5%. The pK parameter was determined using the paramSweep_v3 function with 50 principal components, and the optimal pK was selected based on the maximum mean-variance normalized bimodality coefficient. Doublets were identified and removed using the doubletFinder_v3 function with the specified pK and expected doublet rate. Gene expression matrices were normalized using the LogNormalize method with a scale factor of 10,000, followed by identification of highly variable features using the FindVariableFeatures function with selection method "vst" and nfeatures = 2000. To mitigate batch effects across studies while preserving biological variation, we employed the Harmony algorithm (v0.1.0) for data integration. Principal component analysis (PCA) was performed on the integrated dataset, and the first 30 principal components were used for UMAP dimensionality reduction. Unsupervised graph-based clustering was performed using the FindNeighbors and FindClusters functions with resolution parameters optimized for major lineage identification (resolution = 0.8) and CAF sub-clustering (resolution = 1.2). Cell type annotation was performed using canonical marker genes established in the literature and cross-referenced with the CellMarker database.

Cancer-associated fibroblast subtyping and functional annotation

Fibroblast populations were extracted from the integrated dataset based on expression of established fibroblast markers (COL1A1, COL1A2, PDGFRA, THY1). Sub-clustering analysis was performed to identify transcriptionally distinct CAF subsets. Marker genes for each CAF subtype were identified using the FindAllMarkers function with parameters min.pct = 0.25 and logfc.threshold = 0.25. Differential gene expression analysis between THBS2⁺ CAFs and other CAF subsets was performed using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with Bonferroni correction. Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathway enrichment analyses were conducted using the clusterProfiler package (v4.0). Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (GSEA) was performed using the fgsea package (v1.18.0) with the Molecular Signatures Database (MSigDB) hallmark gene sets [23]. PROGENy (v1.12.0) was employed to infer pathway activity scores from gene expression data [24]. AUCell (v1.14.0) was used to calculate signature scores for established CAF subtypes (iCAF, myCAF, apCAF, vCAF, pCAF, lpCAF) based on published gene signatures. The ratio of observed to expected cell numbers (Ro/e score) was calculated to assess tissue preference of each CAF subtype across normal, primary, and metastatic tissues. Radar plots were generated using the fmsb package to visualize multi-dimensional CAF signature scores.

Pseudotime trajectory and transcriptional regulatory network analysis

To reconstruct developmental trajectories of CAF subpopulations, we employed Monocle2 (v2.18.0) for pseudotime ordering [25]. The DDRTree algorithm was used for dimensionality reduction and branch construction. Differential gene expression analysis along pseudotime was performed using the differentialGeneTest function. Branch expression analysis was conducted to identify fate-specific gene clusters using the BEAM function. Heatmaps of gene expression dynamics were generated using the plot_pseudotime_heatmap function with 100 genes per cluster. Epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) scores were calculated using the AddModuleScore function based on established EMT gene signatures. Transcriptional regulatory network analysis was performed using pySCENIC (v0.11.2) [26]. Regulon activity scores were calculated using the AUCell algorithm, and differentially activated regulons were identified using the limma package (v3.48.0). Regulon specificity scores (RSS) were computed to rank transcription factors by their specificity to each CAF subtype. Feature plots and violin plots were generated to visualize regulon activity and transcription factor expression across the CAF landscape.

Spatial transcriptomic data processing and deconvolution

Spatial transcriptomic data from PDAC tissue sections were obtained from publicly available datasets (GSM7498811, GSM7498813, GSM8552957, GSM8552958) [21,27]. Raw sequencing data were processed using the Space Ranger pipeline (v1.3.0, 10x Genomics) for alignment, barcode assignment, and unique molecular identifier (UMI) counting. Spot-level gene expression matrices were imported into Seurat for downstream analysis. Unsupervised clustering of spatial spots was performed using standard Seurat workflows. CellTrek (v1.0) was employed for spatial deconvolution, integrating scRNA-seq and spatial transcriptomic data to map fibroblast and epithelial subtypes onto tissue sections [28]. SpaGene (v1.0) was used to identify spatially variable gene programs and calculate Jaccard indices of gene program overlap between cell populations. SpatialFeaturePlots were generated to visualize gene expression and signature scores across tissue sections. Co-localization analysis was performed by calculating spatial correlation coefficients between ligand-receptor pairs using Pearson correlation.

Cell-cell communication analysis

Cell-cell communication networks were inferred using CellChat (v1.1.3) with the CellChatDB.human ligand-receptor database [29]. Communication probability was calculated using the computeCommunProb function with parameters raw.use = TRUE and population.size = TRUE. Differential interaction analysis between primary and metastatic samples was performed using the compareInteractions and netVisual_diffInteraction functions. Signaling role analysis was conducted to identify dominant signal senders (sources) and receivers (targets) within the cellular communication network. Two-dimensional scatter plots of signaling roles were generated using the netAnalysis_signalingRole_scatter function. Detailed ligand-receptor interaction pairs between THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells were extracted and visualized using dotplots and heatmaps.

Epithelial cell subtyping and metaprogram analysis

Epithelial cells were extracted from the integrated dataset based on expression of EPCAM and KRT19. Sub-clustering analysis was performed to identify malignant epithelial subpopulations. GeneNMF (v0.1.0) was employed for non-negative matrix factorization decomposition to identify robust transcriptional programs (metaprograms). The number of metaprograms was optimized using cophenetic correlation coefficient analysis. Jaccard similarity indices between metaprograms were calculated and hierarchically clustered to identify metaprogram families. Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) was performed using the GSVA package (v1.40.0) with MSigDB hallmark gene sets to infer pathway activity in epithelial subtypes. Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) enrichment analysis was performed to identify significant signaling pathways enriched in different cell subtypes.

Bulk transcriptomic data analysis and clinical correlation

Bulk RNA sequencing data from 12 independent PDAC cohorts (TCGA-PAAD, ICGC-PAAD-CAseq, GSE85916, GSE79668, GSE71729, EMTAB6134, GSE21501, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229) were downloaded from GEO and UCSC Xena databases [30,31]. Gene expression matrices were normalized using TPM (Transcripts Per Million) or FPKM (Fragments Per Kilobase of transcript per Million mapped reads) values. Signature scores for THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells were calculated using the single-sample Gene Set Enrichment Analysis (ssGSEA) algorithm implemented in the GSVA package. T-cell exclusion scores were calculated using the T cell exclusion signature established in previous studies. Pearson correlation analysis was performed to assess associations between THBS2⁺ CAF scores, KRT17⁺ malignant cell scores, and T-cell exclusion scores. For immunotherapy response analysis, the IMvigor210 cohort of urothelial carcinoma patients treated with atezolizumab (anti-PD-L1) was used as a validation dataset [32]. Patients were stratified into responder (complete response [CR] + partial response [PR]) and non-responder (stable disease [SD] + progressive disease [PD]) groups based on RECIST criteria.

Survival analysis and statistical methods

Kaplan-Meier survival analysis was performed using the survival (v3.2–13) and survminer (v0.4.9) packages. Patients were stratified into high and low infiltration groups based on median signature scores. Overall survival was calculated from the date of diagnosis or treatment initiation to the date of death or last follow-up. Log-rank tests were used to compare survival curves between groups. Univariate and multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression analyses were performed to assess the independent prognostic value of THBS2⁺ CAF and KRT17⁺ malignant cell infiltration, adjusting for age, gender, stage, and other clinical covariates. Hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated. Statistical significance was determined using two-tailed tests with α = 0.05. For multiple comparisons, p-values were adjusted using the Benjamini-Hochberg method. All analyses were performed in R (v4.1.0) and Python (v3.8.0).

Results

Integrated single-cell atlas reveals the cellular heterogeneity landscape of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma

To construct a comprehensive cellular landscape of PDAC, we performed integrated analysis of 469,137 single cells from 8 independent studies, which include diverse clinical stages, tissue sources (normal pancreas, primary tumors, and metastatic lesions), and patient demographics (Fig. 1A). UMAP visualization revealed distinct clustering patterns segregating by tissue type, with tumor samples displaying markedly greater transcriptional heterogeneity compared to normal tissues, suggesting extensive cellular diversification during malignant transformation (Fig. 1B).

Unsupervised clustering and canonical marker annotation identified 12 major cell lineages: acinar cells (expressing PRSS1 and PTF1A), ductal cells (KRT19, EPCAM), endocrine cells (CHGB, INS), fibroblasts (COL1A1, ACTA2), endothelial cells (PECAM1, VWF), smooth muscle cells (SMCs; ACTA2, MYH11), and immune populations including T/NK cells (CD3D, CD3E, KLRD1), B cells (CD79A, MS4A1), plasma cells (JCHAIN, MZB1), myeloid cells (CD68, S100A9), mast cells (CPA3, TPSAB1), and proliferating cells (MKI67) (Fig. 1C). Dot plot analysis systematically validated the expression specificity of established lineage markers, quantifying both the percentage of expressing cells and mean expression levels across populations, thereby confirming the robustness of our cell type classification (Fig. 1D). Density plots further illustrated the continuous expression distributions of representative markers, revealing distinct peaks corresponding to assigned cell identities and validating the separation of major lineages (Fig. 1E).

Analysis of cell type proportions across clinical metadata revealed substantial inter-patient heterogeneity and disease-associated remodeling of the tumor microenvironment. Notably, fibroblasts and myeloid cells exhibited consistent enrichment in tumor tissues compared to normal pancreas, while acinar cells were predominantly restricted to normal samples (Fig. 1F). Furthermore, we observed distinct distribution patterns across disease stages, with progressive changes in immune cell composition during tumor progression, and notable variations according to metastatic status and anatomical location (head, body, tail of pancreas). These data establish a high-resolution cellular atlas of PDAC, providing a foundational framework for investigating subtype-specific functional states and intercellular communication networks.

Heterogeneous CAF subpopulations exhibit distinct spatial distributions and metastatic enrichment

Focusing on the tumor stroma, we performed sub-clustering analysis of fibroblasts and identified nine transcriptionally distinct cancer-associated fibroblast (CAF) subsets, each characterized by unique marker gene signatures: APOD⁺ CAFs (lipid metabolism-associated), CCL19⁺ CAFs (immune-modulatory), CFD⁺ CAFs (complement component-expressing), COL10A1⁺ CAFs (chondrocyte-like), CXCL8⁺ CAFs (inflammatory), MKI67⁺ CAFs (proliferating), PDGFRA⁺ CAFs (progenitor-like), SEPP1⁺ CAFs (antioxidant), and THBS2⁺ CAFs (ECM-remodeling) (Fig. 2A). Proportional abundance analysis across tissue sources revealed striking niche-specific enrichment patterns, with THBS2⁺ CAFs showing significant accumulation in liver metastasis samples compared to primary tumors and normal pancreatic tissues, suggesting a specific role in metastatic colonization (Fig. 2B-C).

Comprehensive marker analysis confirmed the molecular identity of each CAF subtype, with THBS2⁺ CAFs uniquely co-expressing high levels of ECM structural proteins including COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, and POSTN, alongside matricellular protein THBS2 (Fig. 2D). Radar plot visualization using Area Under the Curve (AUCell) algorithm scores for six established CAF signatures (iCAF, myCAF, apCAF, vCAF, pCAF, lpCAF) demonstrated that THBS2⁺ CAFs exhibited a hybrid myofibroblastic phenotype with high myCAF scores, distinct from the inflammatory characteristics of other subsets (Fig. 2E). Pathway activity analysis using PROGENy revealed that THBS2⁺ CAFs displayed enhanced activity in extracellular matrix organization, collagen synthesis, focal adhesion, and TGF-β signaling pathways, indicating a highly activated, matrix-producing phenotype (Fig. 2F-G).

KEGG enrichment analysis of differentially expressed genes further supported these functional specializations, with THBS2⁺ CAFs showing significant enrichment in protein processing in endoplasmic reticulum, focal adhesion, and TGF-β signaling cascades (Fig. 2H). Quantitative assessment of tissue preference using the Ro/e (Ratio of observed to expected cell numbers) score confirmed the specific enrichment of THBS2⁺ CAFs in metastatic lesions (Ro/e > 1), whereas other subtypes such as CCL19⁺ and CXCL8⁺ CAFs preferentially localized to primary tumors; For tissue preference, Ro/e scores are categorized as: +++, Ro/e > 1; ++, 0.8 < Ro/e ≤ 1; +, 0.2 ≤ Ro/e ≤ 0.8; ±, 0 < Ro/e < 0.2; –, Ro/e = 0 (Fig. 2I). To validate these findings at spatial resolution, we employed CellTrek deconvolution analysis of spatial transcriptomic data from representative samples, which confirmed the distinct spatial distributions of fibroblast subtypes, with THBS2⁺ CAFs localized to dense stromal regions surrounding malignant ducts (Fig. 2J). Analysis of CAF infiltration dynamics across disease stages revealed progressive remodeling of the fibroblast compartment, with THBS2⁺ CAFs showing a significant increasing trend from Stage I to Stage IV disease, suggesting their involvement in tumor progression (Fig. 2K).

Pseudotime trajectory analysis reveals PRRX2 as a master regulator of fibroblast activation and ECM remodeling

To investigate the developmental relationships and differentiation trajectories among CAF subpopulations, we performed pseudotime analysis using Monocle2. The reconstructed trajectory revealed a branched differentiation path originating from APOD⁺ CAFs (putative progenitor population) and progressing through intermediate states toward distinct terminal fates, including the ECM-remodeling THBS2⁺ CAFs and the chondrocyte-like COL10A1⁺ CAFs (Fig. 3A-C). Pseudotime ordering of cells demonstrated a gradual transition from early to late differentiation states, with THBS2⁺ CAFs occupying the tips of the reconstructed trajectory branches. (Fig. 3B).

Density analysis of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT) scores along the trajectory revealed progressive acquisition of mesenchymal characteristics, with highest EMT activity observed in the THBS2⁺ CAF branch, consistent with their activated myofibroblastic phenotype (Fig. 3D). Branch-dependent expression analysis identified gene clusters specifically associated with each cell fate trajectory. The THBS2⁺ CAF fate branch exhibited significant enrichment of ECM organization, collagen fibril organization, and TGF-β signaling pathways, whereas the alternative branch showed distinct functional signatures (Fig. 3E). Temporal expression analysis along pseudotime revealed sequential upregulation of ECM-associated structural genes (COL6A1, COL6A3, COL10A1, POSTN) and transcriptional regulators (STAT1, STAT2, VCAN), delineating a coordinated program of fibroblast activation (Fig. 3F).

To identify the transcriptional regulators governing these differentiation programs, we performed regulon analysis using pySCENIC. Heatmap visualization of transcription factor activity revealed distinct regulon activation patterns across CAF subtypes, with THBS2⁺ CAFs displaying specific activation of transcription factors associated with matrix production and tissue remodeling (Fig. 3G). Regulatory network analysis identified interconnected transcription factor modules, with THBS2⁺ CAFs showing unique regulatory architecture compared to other subpopulations (Fig. 3H). Feature plot visualization of regulon module activity scores confirmed the spatial segregation of regulatory programs across the CAF landscape (Fig. 3I). Notably, paired-related homeobox 2 (PRRX2) emerged as the top differentially activated transcription factor specifically enriched in THBS2⁺ CAFs (Fig. 3J), suggesting that it may serve as a key regulator of this fibroblast subset. UMAP visualization and violin plot analysis confirmed PRRX2 expression was highest in THBS2⁺ CAFs compared to all other fibroblast subsets (Fig. 3K-L), suggesting a potential regulatory role in driving the differentiation of ECM-remodeling fibroblasts in the PDAC microenvironment.

THBS2⁺ CAFs establish an ECM-remodeling and immune-excluded tumor microenvironment

To elucidate the functional impact of THBS2⁺ CAFs on the tumor ecosystem, we performed gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) comparing THBS2⁺ CAFs against all other CAF subtypes. This analysis revealed significant enrichment of epithelial-mesenchymal transition (EMT), Hippo signaling, TGF-β signaling, proteoglycans in cancer, and focal adhesion pathways in THBS2⁺ CAFs, confirming their activated, matrix-producing phenotype (Fig. 4A). Given the established role of dense desmoplasia in restricting immune cell infiltration in PDAC, we investigated whether THBS2⁺ CAF abundance correlates with immune exclusion. Correlation analysis across six independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts (TCGA-PAAD, ICGC-PAAD-CAseq, GSE85916, GSE79668, GSE71729, and EMTAB6134) demonstrated a highly significant positive association between THBS2⁺ CAF signature scores and T-cell exclusion scores (all p < 1 × 10⁻⁵), indicating that tumors with high THBS2⁺ CAF infiltration exhibit restricted T cell access to tumor nests (Fig. 4B).

To assess the clinical relevance of this immune exclusion phenotype, we analyzed immunotherapy response data across six independent cohorts. Violin-box plot analysis revealed that non-responders to immune checkpoint blockade therapy exhibited significantly higher THBS2⁺ CAF scores compared to responders (all p < 1 × 10⁻⁵), supporting the role of THBS2⁺ CAFs in promoting an immune-excluded tumor microenvironment and therapeutic resistance (Fig. 4C). To validate these findings at spatial resolution, we analyzed spatial transcriptomic data from two PDAC samples. UMAP visualization of spatial spots revealed distinct clustering patterns corresponding to tissue architecture (Fig. 4D), which mapped accurately onto histological tissue sections showing epithelial and stromal regions (Fig. 4E).

Spatial feature analysis demonstrated that regions with high THBS2⁺ CAF signatures co-localized with elevated EMT, hypoxia, and TGF-β signaling activity, while exhibiting markedly reduced T/NK cell signatures, creating spatially segregated immune-excluded niches (Fig. 4F). High-resolution spatial mapping confirmed co-localization of THBS2 and COL1A1 expression with epithelial-enriched (EPCAM⁺) regions that were concurrently depleted of T/NK cells (CD3D⁺), providing direct visual evidence of an immune-excluded stromal niche (Fig. 4G). Analysis of immune checkpoint molecule expression in the TCGA-PAAD cohort revealed that THBS2⁺ CAF-high tumors exhibited significantly elevated expression of inhibitory receptors including CD274 (PD-L1), TIGIT, HAVCR2 (TIM-3), CD276 (B7-H3), and CTLA4 (Fig. 4H), suggesting that immune exclusion is accompanied by compensatory upregulation of inhibitory pathways, potentially contributing to immunotherapy resistance.

Comprehensive profiling identifies KRT17⁺ malignant cells as the epithelial subset most strongly correlated with THBS2⁺ CAFs

To investigate epithelial-stromal crosstalk in PDAC, we first examined global communication patterns between fibroblasts and malignant cells. Circle plot analysis of CellChat-derived interactions revealed substantial remodeling of fibroblast-malignant cell communication in metastasis compared to primary tumors, with increased interaction strength and number in metastatic lesions (Fig. 5A-B). Sub-clustering of epithelial cells identified nine transcriptionally distinct malignant subpopulations: AQP1⁺, CFTR⁺, ERO1A⁺, FXYD3⁺, JUN⁺, KRT17⁺, LAMB3⁺, MUC5B⁺, and TFF3⁺ malignant cells, each characterized by unique marker gene expression patterns (Fig. 5C-D).

To dissect the modular organization of transcriptional programs in malignant cells, we applied GeneNMF (Non-negative Matrix Factorization) decomposition analysis. Jaccard similarity analysis of robust NMF programs revealed clustering into distinct metaprogram families (MP1-MP9), each associated with specific biological processes including cell cycle regulation, hypoxia response, EMT, and metabolism, as annotated by Gene Ontology Biological Process (GOBP) enrichment analysis (Fig. 5E). Proportional analysis of epithelial subtypes across clinical stages and tissue sources revealed that KRT17⁺ (keratin 17-positive) malignant cells were preferentially enriched in liver metastases and Stage IV disease, suggesting an aggressive, metastasis-associated phenotype (Fig. 5F).

Feature plot visualization of metaprogram activities across the epithelial landscape demonstrated distinct functional specialization, with KRT17⁺ malignant cells exhibiting high scores for EMT, hypoxia, and stress response metaprograms (Fig. 5G). KRT17 (keratin 17) was used in this study as a marker gene to identify this specific aggressive malignant subpopulation based on its differential expression and co-enrichment with THBS2⁺ CAFs. KRT17⁺ malignant cells displayed high scores for EMT, hypoxia, and stress response metaprograms, suggesting that KRT17 expression marks a population with an aggressive, metastasis-associated transcriptional state. However, whether KRT17 itself plays a functional role in driving this phenotype or serves as a lineage marker for a broader EMT-like state remains to be determined, as our transcriptomic analysis does not distinguish between these possibilities. Gene Set Variation Analysis (GSVA) of hallmark pathways confirmed that KRT17⁺ malignant cells displayed elevated activity in EMT, hypoxia, glycolysis, and apical junction pathways compared to other epithelial subtypes (Fig. 5H). GOBP enrichment analysis of the top 100 marker genes for each subtype further delineated their functional characteristics, with KRT17⁺ cells showing enrichment in cell adhesion and migration pathways (Fig. 5I). Quantitative Ro/e score analysis confirmed the strongest tissue preference of KRT17⁺ malignant cells for liver metastasis and advanced stage disease (Stage IV) (Fig. 5J). Statistical comparison verified significantly higher infiltration of KRT17⁺ malignant cells in metastatic versus primary sites (Fig. 5K) and progressive accumulation across clinical stages (Fig. 5L), establishing KRT17⁺ malignant cells as a key epithelial component of advanced PDAC.

THBS2⁺ CAFs interact with KRT17⁺ malignant cells via the THBS2-SDC4 axis to promote metastatic progression

Given the co-enrichment of THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells in metastatic lesions, we investigated their potential functional relationship. Correlation analysis across 12 independent bulk PDAC datasets (including TCGA-PAAD, ICGC, GSE85916, GSE79668, GSE71729, EMTAB6134, GSE21501, GSE28735, GSE57495, GSE62452, GSE78229, and GSE79668) revealed consistent and significant positive correlations between THBS2⁺ CAF scores and KRT17⁺ malignant cell scores (all R > 0.4, p < 0.05), suggesting a coordinated infiltration pattern (Fig. 6A). Cell-cell communication analysis using CellChat demonstrated enhanced ligand-receptor interactions between fibroblast and epithelial subclusters in metastasis compared to primary tumors, with specific enrichment of ECM-mediated signaling (Fig. 6B-C).

Two-dimensional signaling role analysis positioned THBS2⁺ CAFs as dominant signal senders (sources) within the cellular communication network, while KRT17⁺ malignant cells emerged as the top signal receivers (targets), indicating a directional communication axis (Fig. 6D). Detailed ligand-receptor interaction mapping revealed that THBS2⁺ CAFs signal to KRT17⁺ malignant cells primarily through the THBS2 (thrombospondin-2) - SDC4 (syndecan-4) axis, alongside other ECM-receptor interactions including collagen-integrin and fibronectin-integrin pairs (Fig. 6E). Reverse signaling analysis showed that KRT17⁺ malignant cells communicate back to THBS2⁺ CAFs through midkine (MDK) - LRP1 (low-density lipoprotein receptor-related protein 1) and osteopontin (SPP1) - integrin (ITGAV/ITGB1) pathways, potentially establishing a reciprocal activation loop (Fig. 6F).

To validate these interactions at spatial resolution, we examined the expression patterns of THBS2 and SDC4 in four independent spatial transcriptomic samples. Spatial feature plots confirmed robust co-expression and physical proximity of THBS2 (ligand) and SDC4 (receptor) in the tumor microenvironment, with highest expression observed at the epithelial-stromal interface, supporting the existence of a spatially organized THBS2-SDC4 signaling niche that facilitates CAF-malignant cell crosstalk (Fig. 6G).

Co-infiltration of THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells predicts poor prognosis and immunotherapy resistance

To evaluate the clinical significance of the THBS2⁺ CAF-KRT17⁺ malignant cell axis, we performed comprehensive survival analysis across four independent bulk transcriptomic cohorts (TCGA-PAAD, GSE79668, ICGC-PAAD-CAseq, and E-MTAB-6134). Kaplan-Meier survival analysis revealed that high infiltration of either THBS2⁺ CAFs alone or KRT17⁺ malignant cells alone was significantly associated with reduced overall survival (all log-rank p < 0.05) (Fig. 7A, left and middle panels). Importantly, stratification by combined infiltration status revealed that patients with high co-infiltration of both THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells exhibited the poorest clinical outcomes, with significantly shorter survival compared to all other groups, indicating a synergistic prognostic effect (Fig. 7A, right panels).

High infiltration of THBS2+ CAFs and KRT17+ malignant cells is associated with poor prognosis and immunotherapy resistance.

(B) Survival analyses for patients with low and high infiltration of THBS2+ CAFs alone (left), KRT17+ Malignant cells alone (middle), and both (right) in the anti-PD-L1 immunotherapy cohort (IMvigor210 cohort).

(C) Bar plot shows that patients with low co-infiltration of THBS2+ CAFs and KRT17+ Malignant cells exhibit a greater proportion of CR/PR responses, whereas SD/PD responses were elevated in the high co-infiltration group.

(D) Heatmap shows the Jaccard index of gene programs in each sample identified by SpaGene calculated based on the overlap of top-ranked genes.

(E) For four PAAD samples (left to right): SpatialFeaturePlot of specific gene signatures of the specific gene pattern, THBS2+ CAFs and KRT17+ malignant cells; Dotplot shows their significant positive spatial correlation; CellTrek-based deconvolution of their distributions; and SpaGene mapping of THBS2-SDC4 ligand-receptor colocalization.

To assess the predictive value for immunotherapy response, we analyzed the IMvigor210 cohort of patients treated with anti-PD-L1 (atezolizumab). Survival analysis confirmed that high infiltration of THBS2⁺ CAFs, KRT17⁺ malignant cells, or both was associated with significantly worse outcomes following immunotherapy (Fig. 7B). Response rate analysis demonstrated that patients with low co-infiltration of both populations exhibited higher rates of complete response (CR) and partial response (PR) to immunotherapy (CR: 10.2% vs. 2.2%; PR: 15.4% vs. 8.7%), whereas stable disease (SD) and progressive disease (PD) responses were markedly elevated in the high co-infiltration group (SD: 58.7% vs. 38.5%; PD: 20.5% vs. 8.7%), indicating that the THBS2⁺ CAF-KRT17⁺ axis is a robust predictor of therapeutic resistance (Fig. 7C).

To validate the spatial co-localization of these populations at genomic resolution, we performed SpaGene analysis to calculate Jaccard indices of gene program overlap across spatial transcriptomic samples. Heatmap visualization revealed distinct patterns of spatial co-expression, with specific samples showing high similarity scores between THBS2⁺ CAF and KRT17⁺ malignant cell gene programs (Fig. 7D). Detailed spatial analysis of four representative PDAC samples confirmed: (1) distinct spatial expression patterns of THBS2⁺ CAF and KRT17⁺ malignant cell signatures; (2) statistically significant positive spatial correlation between these populations (Pearson's r > 0.5, p < 0.001); (3) CellTrek-based deconvolution revealing physical co-localization at the tumor-stroma interface; and (4) SpaGene mapping confirming spatial co-localization of THBS2-SDC4 ligand-receptor pairs (Fig. 7E). Collectively, these findings establish the THBS2⁺ CAF-KRT17⁺ malignant cell axis as a critical determinant of tumor progression, immune evasion, and therapeutic resistance, with significant implications for patient stratification and targeted intervention strategies in pancreatic cancer.

Discussion

This study presents an integrative single-cell analysis of pancreatic cancer that identifies a functional axis between THBS2⁺ cancer-associated fibroblasts and KRT17⁺ malignant cells. Through comprehensive transcriptomic profiling of 469,137 cells across diverse clinical stages, we delineated nine distinct CAF subpopulations, among which THBS2⁺ CAFs represent a specialized subset characterized by enhanced ECM remodeling capabilities and specific tropism for metastatic lesions. Our findings reveal that THBS2⁺ CAFs not only contribute to physical barrier formation through collagen deposition but also actively establish an immune-excluded microenvironment via TGF-β signaling and ECM-mediated T cell exclusion, thereby fostering therapeutic resistance to immune checkpoint inhibitors. It is important to contextualize the THBS2⁺ CAF subset identified here relative to previously described CAF populations. While myofibroblastic CAFs (myCAFs) characterized by α-SMA expression have been extensively studied in PDAC, the THBS2⁺ CAFs described here represent a transcriptionally distinct population that expresses high levels of ECM components (COL1A1, COL1A2, COL3A1, POSTN) and the matricellular protein THBS2, but show relatively lower ACTA2 expression compared to classical myCAFs. Compared to previously reported ECM-remodeling CAF signatures, our THBS2⁺ CAF signature includes unique enrichment of THBS2 and specific transcription factor activity (PRRX2) that has not been previously associated with this phenotype. Furthermore, while THBS2 expression has been noted in CAFs from other malignancies, our study provides the first systematic characterization of this subset in PDAC with specific linkage to liver metastasis, immune exclusion, and resistance to immunotherapy.

The developmental trajectory analysis provides insights into the differentiation dynamics of CAF subpopulations. The identification of PRRX2 as a master regulator of THBS2⁺ CAF differentiation suggests that targeting this transcription factor or its downstream effectors could potentially reprogram the fibroblastic compartment toward a less tumor-promoting state. The observation that THBS2⁺ CAFs occupy terminal differentiation branches in the pseudotime trajectory implies that these cells represent an activated, terminal state rather than a transient phenotype, highlighting the stability of this pro-tumorigenic fibroblast population.

Our discovery of the THBS2-SDC4 signaling axis between CAFs and malignant cells adds to the growing repertoire of stromal-epithelial communication mechanisms in PDAC. The specific enrichment of this interaction in metastatic lesions suggests that THBS2⁺ CAFs may prepare the metastatic niche prior to or concurrent with malignant cell seeding. The reciprocal signaling involving MDK-LRP1 and SPP1-integrin pathways indicates a self-reinforcing loop where malignant cells further stimulate CAF activation, creating a vicious cycle of stromal desmoplasia and tumor progression. This bidirectional communication likely explains the strong correlation between THBS2⁺ CAF and KRT17⁺ malignant cell abundances observed across bulk transcriptomic datasets. The importance of thrombospondin signaling in cancer metastasis is further underscored by a recent study in osteosarcoma, where THBS1 was shown to drive pulmonary metastasis via ITGA1/ITGA6-mediated cytoskeleton remodeling and dedifferentiation [33]. Our work focuses on THBS2 derived from CAFs acting through SDC4, the convergence on cytoskeletal and FAK pathway activation suggests a conserved pro-metastatic mechanism within the thrombospondin family, warranting future exploration of THBS–receptor interactions as pan-cancer therapeutic targets.

From a clinical perspective, the THBS2⁺ CAF-KRT17⁺ malignant cell axis represents a promising prognostic biomarker and therapeutic target. The synergistic effect of co-infiltration on patient survival suggests that simultaneous targeting of both compartments may be necessary to achieve meaningful clinical benefits. Furthermore, the association of this axis with immune checkpoint inhibitor resistance provides a mechanistic explanation for the limited efficacy of immunotherapy in PDAC and identifies potential strategies for patient stratification.

Several limitations warrant consideration. First, while our study identifies strong correlational associations between THBS2⁺ CAFs, T-cell exclusion, KRT17⁺ malignant cells, and clinical outcomes, causality remains to be functionally established. Our conclusions regarding immune exclusion and the THBS2-SDC4 signaling axis are based on spatial transcriptomic co-localization and computational inference, but direct evidence from co-culture experiments is needed. We propose that future studies should employ in vitro co-culture systems using primary PDAC CAFs (THBS2⁺ vs. THBS2⁻) with activated T cells or PDAC tumor cells to assess whether THBS2⁺ CAFs directly suppress T-cell function or promote tumor cell aggressiveness, alongside orthogonal approaches such as CAF-specific THBS2 knockdown in orthotopic mouse models followed by immune cell profiling and tumor progression assessment. Second, the spatial transcriptomic analysis, while supportive, is limited in sample size; future studies employing high-resolution imaging mass cytometry or multiplexed ion beam imaging could provide more definitive evidence of physical colocalization and protein-level validation. Third, the mechanisms underlying the specific enrichment of THBS2⁺ CAFs in liver metastases remain to be fully elucidated and may involve organ-specific chemokine gradients or metabolic cues. Fourth, our immunotherapy resistance findings using the IMvigor210 cohort represent cross-cancer validation in urothelial carcinoma rather than PDAC-specific evidence. While this supports the generalizability of our signature as a predictor of immune checkpoint blockade resistance, we acknowledge the biological differences between these tumor types. The lack of publicly available PDAC immunotherapy cohorts with paired transcriptomic data is a significant limitation. Future studies should prioritize the generation and analysis of PDAC-specific cohorts treated with immune checkpoint inhibitors to validate our findings in the relevant disease context. Additionally, spatial profiling of immune checkpoint inhibitor-treated PDAC samples would provide direct evidence of the association between THBS2⁺ CAF infiltration and treatment resistance.

In conclusion, our study establishes THBS2⁺ CAFs as critical architects of the metastatic and immune-excluded microenvironment in PDAC through their interaction with KRT17⁺ malignant cells. These findings advance our understanding of stromal-epithelial coevolution in pancreatic cancer and provide a foundation for developing targeted therapies aimed at disrupting this pathogenic axis. Future investigations should focus on therapeutic strategies that simultaneously inhibit THBS2-SDC4 signaling and reprogram the fibroblastic microenvironment to restore immune surveillance and suppress metastatic progression.

Conclusion

Our integrative multi-omics analysis establishes a pro-metastatic and immunosuppressive axis between THBS2⁺ CAFs and KRT17⁺ malignant cells mediated by the THBS2-SDC4 ligand-receptor interaction in PDAC. These findings identify the co-infiltration of these populations as a robust prognostic biomarker and provide a rationale for developing targeted therapeutic strategies to disrupt this stromal-epithelial crosstalk in pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma.
