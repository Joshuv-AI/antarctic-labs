Semiclassical algebraic reconstruction for type III algebras
Haocheng Zhong

Abstract
In this work, we address the unresolved type III cases of the algebraic reconstruction theorem by integrating crossed product algebras and semiclassical approximations. We first derive that the relative entropy in crossed product algebras factorizes into contributions from the original algebra and observer wavefunctions. By constructing “holographic” crossed product algebras for “bulk” and “boundary” type III factors, we extend the algebraic reconstruction theorem to include the algebraic Ryu-Takayanagi (RT) formula semiclassically, which provides a complete algebraic description of the reconstruction theorem, as an intrinsic framework for the algebraic version of bulk-boundary correspondences in holographic duality.

2.1
Hilbert space, von Neumann algebra and modular theory

Definition 2.1
.

A Hilbert space is a complex vector space
\mathcal{H}
endowed with the inner product
\langle\cdot|\cdot\rangle:\mathcal{H}\times\mathcal{H}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
and the norm
||v||=\sqrt{\langle v\vphantom{v}|v\vphantom{v}\rangle}
, such that the following properties are satisfied:

•

Linearity for the second variable:
\langle\xi|\psi_{1}+\alpha\psi_{2}\rangle=\langle\xi|\psi_{1}\rangle+\alpha\langle\xi|\psi_{2}\rangle
,

•

Hermiticity:
\langle\psi\vphantom{\xi}|\xi\vphantom{\psi}\rangle^{*}=\langle\xi\vphantom{\psi}|\psi\vphantom{\xi}\rangle
,

•

Positive semi-definiteness:
\langle\psi\vphantom{\psi}|\psi\vphantom{\psi}\rangle\geq 0
,

•

Non-degeneracy:
\langle\psi\vphantom{\psi}|\psi\vphantom{\psi}\rangle=0\Leftrightarrow|\psi\rangle=0
,

•

Completeness:
\mathcal{H}
is complete (i.e. all Cauchy sequences converge) for the norm.

An important example of a Hilbert space is the square-integrable space on
\mathbb{R}
, which is defined as

L^{2}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{f~\bigg|\int_{\mathbb{R}}|f(x)|dx<\infty\right\}.

(12)

In Dirac notation, we write
|f\rangle\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as

|f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx~f(x)|x\rangle

(13)

where we call
|x\rangle
the “position” basis, then
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is endowed with an inner product
\langle f\vphantom{g}|g\vphantom{f}\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}f^{*}(x)g(x)dx
and a norm
||f||=\sqrt{\langle f\vphantom{f}|f\vphantom{f}\rangle}
. For physics interest, hereafter we only consider normalized element in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, i.e.
||f||=1
. One can also work in the “momentum” space such that

|f\rangle=\int_{\mathbb{R}}dp~\mathcal{F}_{f}(p)|p\rangle
with

\mathcal{F}_{f}(p)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dx~f(x)e^{-ipx},\quad f(x)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}dp~\mathcal{F}_{f}(p)e^{ipx},

(14)

and the relations between the two bases are given by

\displaystyle\langle x\vphantom{x^{\prime}}|x^{\prime}\vphantom{x}\rangle=\delta(x-x^{\prime}),\quad\langle p\vphantom{p^{\prime}}|p^{\prime}\vphantom{p}\rangle=\delta(p-p^{\prime}),\quad\langle x\vphantom{p}|p\vphantom{x}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{ipx}.

(15)

We also introduce the corresponding “position” operator
X
and the corresponding “momentum” operator
P
as follows,

\displaystyle P|p\rangle=p|p\rangle,\quad X|x\rangle=x|x\rangle,\quad[X,P]=i

(16)

\displaystyle e^{isP}|x\rangle=|x-s\rangle,\quad e^{isX}|p\rangle=|p+s\rangle.

2.2
Algebraic entropies

In this subsection, we brief introduce the algebraic version of relative entropy and von Neumann entropy. For more related discussions, readers can consult

;
Zhong:2026syt

The algebraic generalization of the relative entropy is defined as the expectation value of the relative modular Hamiltonian due to Araki
araki1975relative
;
araki1975inequalities
:

S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\phi;\mathcal{A})\equiv\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\phi|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\phi|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\phi|\psi}}\right\rangle

(25)

with
|\psi\rangle
being cyclic and separating with respect to the corresponding algebra
\mathcal{A}
. See
Witten:2018zxz
for details about how the algebraic relative entropy coincides with the quantum relative entropy in finite-dimensional cases.

For a generic factor
\mathcal{A}
of type I/II, there exists a tracial state
|\tau\rangle
satisfying

\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}\mathcal{P}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{P}\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle,\quad\forall\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{A}

(26)

and each tracial state defines a trace function on
\mathcal{A}
:

\text{Tr}_{\mathcal{A}}(\mathcal{O})\equiv\left\langle\tau\vphantom{\mathcal{O}\tau}\right|\mathcal{O}\left|\tau\vphantom{\tau\mathcal{O}}\right\rangle.

(27)

The trace function is unique up to a rescaling which may contributes to the algebraic von Neumann entropy an additional constant. Such constant does not cause the ambiguity of the algebraic von Neumann entropy, since only the difference between entropies has physical meanings. The non-existence of algebraic von Neumann entropy in factors of type III is due to the non-existence of tracial state or trace function.

The algebraic generalization of the von Neumann entropy in factors of type I/II is then defined in terms of the algebraic relative entropy between a cyclic and separating state
|\psi\rangle
and the tracial state
\tau

;
ohya2004quantum
;

S(\psi;\mathcal{A})\equiv-S_{\text{rel}}(\psi|\tau;\mathcal{A})=-\left\langle\psi\vphantom{h_{\tau|\psi}\psi}\right|h_{\tau|\psi}\left|\psi\vphantom{\psi h_{\tau|\psi}}\right\rangle.

(28)

2.3
Crossed product algebra

Consider a von Neumann algebra
\mathcal{A}
with a corresponding Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
and a cyclic and separating state
|\Omega\rangle
with a modular flow
\sigma^{\Omega}
defined by:

\sigma^{\Omega}_{s}(a)=e^{ish_{\Omega}}ae^{-ish_{\Omega}},\quad a\in\mathcal{A},~\forall s\in\mathbb{R}

(29)

where
h_{\Omega}
is the modular Hamiltonian of
|\Omega\rangle
with respect to
\mathcal{A}
, we construct the product Hilbert space

\widehat{\mathcal{H}}\equiv\mathcal{H}\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R})=\left\{|\Psi_{f}\rangle\equiv|\Psi\rangle\otimes|f\rangle\right\},

(30)

with the crossed product algebra

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega}\equiv\mathcal{A}\rtimes_{\sigma^{\Omega}}\mathbb{R}=\left\{a_{P},\mathbbm{1}\otimes e^{itX}\big|a\in\mathcal{A},t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}^{\prime\prime},\quad a_{P}\equiv e^{ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}(a\otimes\mathbbm{1})e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P},

(31)

where the double commutant is to ensure that
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega}
is a von Neumann algebra. There exists an equivalent definition of crossed product algebra which works as the subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
fixed under the flow generated by
h_{\Omega}\otimes\mathbbm{1}+\mathbbm{1}\otimes X
(we write
h_{\Omega}+X
for simplicity)
Jensen:2023yxy
:

\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega}\equiv\{\widehat{a}\in\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))\big|e^{is(h_{\Omega}+X)}\widehat{a}e^{-is(h_{\Omega}+X)}=\widehat{a},\forall s\in\mathbb{R}\}.

(32)

In this definition, one can regard
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
as an auxiliary register which keeps track of some “modular time” to the original algebra
\mathcal{A}
, and the crossed product construction is equivalent to imposing the gauge symmetry
h_{\Omega}+X
to the product algebra
\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{B}(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}))
. Previous results
Chandrasekaran:2022cip
;
Chandrasekaran:2022eqq
;
Jensen:2023yxy
regarding quantum gravity introduce an auxiliary observer to the corresponding region whose degrees of freedom are characterized by
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with
X
being the observer Hamiltonian (i.e.
s\in\mathbb{R}
is the time measured by the observer), while
h_{\Omega}
corresponds to the geometrical constraints such that
h_{\Omega}+X
generates an invariant flow in the original algebra coupled with the observers’ degrees of freedom.

There is a useful theorem stating that for two cyclic separating states
|\Omega_{1}\rangle
and
|\Omega_{2}\rangle
, the crossed product algebras are isometric:
\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega_{1}}\simeq\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega_{2}}

Witten:2021unn
. In this sense, the crossed product algebra is unique up to an isometry, while practically one would prefer to use some specific cyclic separating state for either simplifying cumbersome calculations or concerning some physical situations.

The key advantage of applying the crossed product construction to type III factors is that, the crossed product algebra is of type II
takesaki1973duality
;
connes1973classification
;
connes2008noncommutative
such that one is able to define a trace function and also the density operator for the crossed product algebra
Witten:2021unn
;
Chandrasekaran:2022eqq
;
Jensen:2023yxy
;
AliAhmad:2023etg
, hence one can study the algebraic entropies. For example, following
Jensen:2023yxy
we define the trace for the crossed product algebra as follows,

\widehat{\text{Tr}}(\widehat{a})\equiv 2\pi\left(\langle\Omega|\otimes\langle 0|_{P}\right)e^{X}\widehat{a}\left(|\Omega\rangle\otimes|0\rangle_{P}\right),\quad\forall\widehat{a}\in\widehat{\mathcal{A}}_{\Omega}

(33)

where
|0\rangle_{P}
is the zero-momentum eigenstate of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
. In the position basis, one can apply (
15
) and find that

\displaystyle\widehat{\text{Tr}}(\widehat{a})=

\displaystyle 2\pi\left(\langle\Omega|\otimes\langle 0|_{P}\right)e^{X}\widehat{a}\left(|\Omega\rangle\otimes|0\rangle_{P}\right)

(34)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\pi\int dxdx^{\prime}\left(\langle\Omega|\otimes\langle 0|_{P}\right)\left(|x\vphantom{x}\rangle\langle x\vphantom{x}|\right)e^{X}\widehat{a}\left(|x^{\prime}\vphantom{x^{\prime}}\rangle\langle x^{\prime}\vphantom{x^{\prime}}|\right)\left(|\Omega\rangle\otimes|0\rangle_{P}\right)

\displaystyle\int dxdx^{\prime}\langle\Omega|\langle x|e^{X}\widehat{a}|x^{\prime}\rangle|\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dxdx^{\prime}\delta(x-x^{\prime})e^{x}\langle\Omega|\widehat{a}|\Omega\rangle

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int dx~e^{x}\langle\Omega|\widehat{a}|\Omega\rangle

which coincides with an alternative definition of the trace function in
Witten:2021unn

AliAhmad:2023etg
. The expression of a generic density operator is rather complicated and is not relevant to the rest of the work, one is encouraged to consult

3
Semiclassical description

In this section, we demonstrate how to practically apply the semiclassical approximation in the presence of crossed product algebra, and one can consult
Chandrasekaran:2022cip
;
Chandrasekaran:2022eqq
;
Jensen:2023yxy
for more details. After imposing the approximation, we then prove that the relative entropy between two states in the crossed product algebra factorizes into a sum of two relative entropies: the relative entropy intrinsic to the original algebra, and the relative entropy quantifying the distinguishability between the respective observers.

While promoting the algebra into the crossed product algebra via introducing an auxiliary observer with a wavefunction
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
,
|\Psi\rangle
is promoted into
|\Psi_{f}\rangle\equiv|\Psi\rangle\otimes|f\rangle
and the condition of semiclassical approximation is to select a special class of observers whose degrees of freedom are not strongly entangled with the quantum field degrees of freedom. One can think of it as follows, the degrees of freedom between the observer and the state, which are presented as operators in the respective algebras in the algebraic language, are entangled due to the conjugation
e^{ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}(a\otimes\mathbbm{1})e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}
in the crossed product algebra (
31
). Therefore, we can require that
f(x)
is slowly varying such that due to the uncertainty principle its Fourier mode
\mathcal{F}_{f}(p)
is sharply peaked around some specific momentum, which we can set to be
p=0
without loss of generality. Practically, the semiclassical approximation is to consider a set of wavefunctions
\{f\}
approximately satisfies
4
4

See section 5.3 in

.

e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}|\Psi_{f}\rangle\approx|\Psi_{f}\rangle,\quad\forall|\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H},

(35)

which implies that
\forall a\in\mathcal{A},~\forall|\Psi\rangle\in\mathcal{H}:

\displaystyle a_{P}|\Psi_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}(a\otimes\mathbbm{1})e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}|\Psi_{f}\rangle

(36)

\displaystyle\approx e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}\left(a\otimes\mathbbm{1}\right)|\Psi_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=e^{-ih_{\Omega}\otimes P}|(a\Psi)_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle\approx|(a\Psi)_{f}\rangle

\displaystyle=\left(a\otimes\mathbbm{1}\right)|\Psi_{f}\rangle.

For such states

Discussions

In this work, we first derived several useful relations between operators in the crossed product algebra and that in the original algebra within the semiclassical description, and proved that the relative entropy in the crossed product algebra factorizes into the relative entropies in the original algebra and between the observers, respectively. Second, by applying the crossed product algebra to the algebraic reconstruction theorem

for type I/II factors, we promote the factors to be generic and define the corresponding holographic crossed product algebras such that semiclassically the algebraic RT formula in type III cases are included in the theorem
4.2
