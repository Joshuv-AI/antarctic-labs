 1) Summary:
 The objective of this project was to investigate the dynamic interactions between non-thermal
plasma (NTP) and biological targets suspended in a physiological liquid medium.
 The experiments under this collaborative study were designed to combine theoretical and
experimental approaches to test the hypothesis that when living cells (and the growth medium
surrounding them) encounter plasma, they reciprocally and dynamically modify each other.
 We investigated changes in dose-dependent cell behavior resulting from exposure to NTP by
measuring change in cell size and oxidative stress in two different cell targets. To evaluate changes
in plasma properties in response to the altered biological target we measured electrical, chemical
and ionic changes produced in liquids surrounding them.
 We had a major challenge in the timely execution of some experiments and the analysis of
resulting data because of a prolonged delay in receiving the sensors proposed in this study. The
sensors arrived from the vendor (Zimmer Peacock, based in Europe) late 2025. The experimental
data has been collected and analysis is ongoing.

be made once the hydrogen peroxide and nitric oxide sensor data files are analyzed.
 Effect of NTP-mediated change in ionic strength of cell suspension (Cells + Culture
Medium) on cell morphology: To determine NTP-mediated changes, we measured the
concentration of calcium and potassium ions in the suspension immediately after NTP exposure
using electrochemical sensors. We also measured the oxidation reduction potential of the system
as a representative of the combined change induced by NTP species (hydrogen peroxide and nitric
oxide) and species produced by cells in response to the oxidative stress imposed by NTP. This data
is being analyzed currently. Preliminary analysis suggests that the concentration of potassium ions
increases within the first 5 seconds and is more pronounced for THP-1 cells than Jurkat cells. This
may partially explain why Jurkats experience greater change in cell morphology. However, we did
pivot and used an alternative approach to investigate the effect of ionic changes in culture
medium on the two cell types.
