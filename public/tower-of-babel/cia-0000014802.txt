ESPIONAGE

Harry GOLD has advised that he received expense money from all his Sov
superiors: from YAKOVLEV he rec'd a total of ca $750 to $1,000. GOLD has also
advised that ir his last meeting with YAKOVLEV, 26 Dec 46, YAKOVLEV gave him
2 or 3 hundred $ for his past espionage expenses; at that time GOLD didn't give
him a receipt, although normally he did. At that meeting with YAKOVLEV, YAKOVLEV
told GOLD to prepare for a trip to Paris in early spring 1947. GOLD was to meet
a Frenchman at a subway stop in Paris; the man was to approach GOLD and give him
certain info which GOLD would then take to England and turn over to a subsequent
contact. At about this point, YAKOVLEV asked GOLD where he was employed, and
when GOLD said he was employed by BROTHMAN, YAKOVLEV got very upset and left,
not completing his discussion. GOLD has advised that he believed this European
assignment had to do with collecting Atomic info and that it would maybe involve
more than one European trip. GOLD has also advised that on at least two occasions,
YAKOVLEV told him he'd introduce GOLD to a young woman, whose husband was in the
US Army, who would perform the function of doing leg work between YAKOVLEV and
GOLD. GOLD recalled that she lived in upper Manhattan, in the neighborhood of
Lexington Ave. and she may have been Russian born or of Russian descent, although

_ GOLD never did neet her. (DFB-34870)
