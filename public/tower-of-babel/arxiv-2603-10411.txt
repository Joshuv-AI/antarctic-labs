Remarks on the heat flow of harmonic maps into CAT(0)-spaces
Fang-Hua Lin, Changyou Wang

Abstract
. In this paper, we present an alternate, elementary proof of the local Lipschitz regularity of the suitable weak solution of heat flow of harmonic maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces, whose existence was established by Lin, Segatti, Sire, and Wang through an elliptic regularization approach. The ideas of the proof are inspired by Korevaar and Schoen, and they work for any CAT(0)-metric space ( X , d ) (X,d) as the target and any complete Riemanan manifold ( M , g ) (M,g) , with positive injectivity radius and bounded curvature, as the domain.



1.
Introduction

In the seminal paper
[
6
]
, Eells and Sampson first introduced the heat flow of harmonic maps between compact Riemannian manifolds
(M,g)
and
(N,h)
without boundaries. When the sectional curvature of
(N,h)
is non-positive, it was shown by
[
6
]
that for any initial map
u_{0}\in C^{\infty}(M,N)
, there exists a unique smooth solution
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[0,\infty),N)
of the heat flow of harmonic map such that
u(\cdot,t)\xrightarrow{t_{k}\nearrow\infty}u_{\infty}
in
C^{2}(M,N)
and

u_{\infty}
is a minimizing harmonic map in the free homotopy class

[u_{0}]
(see also related work by Hartman
[
10
]
and Hamilton
[
9
]
).

In his 1984’s paper
[
21
]
, Schoen established, under the assumption
(N,h)
has non-positive curvature,
the convexity of Dirichlet energy functional
E(u(t)):[0,1]\to[0,\infty)
for any
u\in C^{\infty}(M\times[0,1],N)
satisfying that
for
x\in M
,
u(x,\cdot):[0,1]\to N
is a constant speed geodesic. This in turn provides a variational approach of the existence of minimizing harmonic maps in any free homotopy class
\alpha\in[M,N]
,
when
(N,h)
has non-positive curvature. In order to establish rigidity theorems of geometric groups, Gromov-Schoen
[
7
]
initiated the study of harmonic map into
non-positively curved (NPC) metric spaces
(X,d)
, and showed, in particular, its Lipschitz regularity when
(X,d)
are Euclidean buildings
(i.e., locally finite Riemannian simplicial complexes). Subsequently, Korevaar-Schoen
[
14
,
15
]
developed
a general theory of minimizing harmonic maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces and established their Lipschitz regularity, see also the work
by Jost
[
12
]
. Since then, we have seen many interesting development on harmonic maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces, especially further allowing the domains to be Alexandrov spaces with curvature bounded from below. We refer the interested reader to Chen
[
2
]
, Jost
[
13
]
, Lin
[
16
]
, Daskalopoulos-Messe
[
3
,
4
,
5
]
, Zhang-Zhu
[
22
]
, Gigli
[
8
]
,
and Mondino-Semola
[
19
]
.

On the other hand, it has been a natural, but difficult problem how to extend Eells-Sampson’s theorem and develop a successful theory of heat flow of harmonic maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces. There were fewer
results available in the literature. U. Mayer
[
18
]
extended the Crandall-Liggett method to construct global weak solutions
of the negative gradient flow of Dirichlet energy functional for maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces, that enjoy the semi-group property, but left
the question of regularity for such solutions unanswered. Very recently, in a joint work with A. Segatti and Y. Sire
[
17
]
, we developed a new method, namely, the elliptic regularization approach of the heat flow of harmonic maps, and proved the existence of a unique global suitable weak solution of the heat flow of harmonic maps into CAT(0)-metric spaces
(X,d)
, that is Hölder continuous when
(X,d)
additionally is locally compact. Furthermore, we introduced the parabolic version of Almgren type frequency functions for such a heat flow of harmonic maps and showed it is a monotonically increasing function. As an immediate application of such a monotonicity property, we proved the spatial Lipschitz regularity of any suitable weak solution of the heat flow of harmonic maps into a wide class of CAT(0)-metric spaces.

Now we briefly review the main results by
[
17
]
. First, through this paper,
(X,d)
is assumed to be a CAT(0)-metric space, that is,
(X,d)
is a complete and separable metric space, that satisfies
the following two conditions:

(i)

(X,d)
is a complete length space: For any pair of points
P,Q\in X
,
d(P,Q)
equals to the length of a rectifiable curve
\gamma:[0,1]\to X

such that
\gamma(0)=P
and
\gamma(1)=Q
. Such a
\gamma
is called a geodesic between
P
and
Q
.

(ii)

For any three points
P,Q,R\in X
, let
\gamma_{Q,R}
be a geodesic between
Q
and
R
. For
0<\lambda<1
,
let
Q_{\lambda}\in\Gamma_{Q,R}
be such that
d(Q_{\lambda},Q)=\lambda d(Q,R)
and
d(Q_{\lambda},R)=(1-\lambda)d(Q,R)
, then it holds that

(1.1)

d^{2}(P,Q_{\lambda})\leq(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,Q)+\lambda d^{2}(Q,R)-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(Q,R).

Let
(M,g)
be a compact (or complete)
n
-dimensional Riemannian manifold without boundary. We closely follow Korevaar-Schoen
[
14
]

for the definitions of Sobolev spaces between

(or
M\times(0,\infty)
) and
X
, and of the Dirichlet energy density

|\nabla u|^{2}
(or
|\partial_{t}u|^{2}
) as well as the directional energy density
|u_{*}(Z)|^{2}
for a map
u\in H^{1}(M,X)
, where
Z
is a smooth tangential vector field
Z
on
M
. Recall the (generalized) Dirichlet energy functional
E:L^{2}(M,X)\to[0,\infty]
is defined by

E(u)=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g},&u\in H^{1}(M,X),\\
+\infty&\mbox{ otherwise in }L^{2}(M.X),\end{cases}

with domain
D(E)=H^{1}(M,X)
.
For
u_{0}\in H^{1}(M,X)
and
\varepsilon>0
, let
u_{\varepsilon}\in\mathfrak{V}_{u_{0}}
be the (unique) minimizer of the Weighted Energy Dissipation (or WED) energy functional

(1.2)

\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(v)=\frac{1}{2}\iint_{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}\frac{e^{-t/\varepsilon}}{\varepsilon}\left(\varepsilon\left|{\partial_{t}v}\right|^{2}+\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2}\right)\,dv_{g}dt

over the configuration function space

(1.3)

{\mathfrak{V}_{{u}_{0}}=\left\{v:M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}\to X:\,v(0)={u}_{0},\,\,\iint_{M\times\mathbb{R}_{+}}(\left|{\partial_{t}v}\right|^{2}+\left|{\nabla v}\right|^{2})e^{-t/\varepsilon}\,dv_{g}dt<+\infty\right\}}.

It was shown by
[
17
, Section 4.3]
that
\{u_{\varepsilon}\}
is bounded in
H^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(M\times(0,\infty))
, that is

(1.4)

\displaystyle\iint_{M\times(0,\infty)}|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dv_{g}dt\leq E(u_{0}),\ \ \ \iint_{M\times[0,T]}|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dv_{g}dt\leq(T+\varepsilon)E(u_{0}),\ \ \forall T>0.

Recall the induced
L^{2}
-distance on
L^{2}(M,X)
by
(X,d)
is given by

d_{2}(u,v)=\Big(\int_{M}d^{2}(u(x),v(x))\,dv_{g}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}},\ \ \forall u,v\in L^{2}(M,X).

Recall that by
[
17
, Definition 1.2]
,
u\in{\rm{AC}}^{2}\big([0,\infty),L^{2}(M,X))\cap L^{\infty}(0,\infty),H^{1}_{\rm{loc}}(M,X)\big)
is called a suitable weak solution of the heat flow of harmonic maps from
(M,g)
to
(X,d)
with an initial condition

u_{0}\in H^{1}(M,X)
, if
u\big|_{t=0}=u_{0}
and if
u
satisfies
the Evolution Variational Inequality (EVI):

(1.5)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}d_{2}^{2}(u(t),v)+E(u(t)\leq E(v)\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(0,\infty),\ \forall v\in H^{1}(M,X).

The following theorem was proven by
[
17
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(X,d)
be a CAT(0)-metric space,
and
u_{0}\in H^{1}(M,X)
. If
u_{\varepsilon}={\rm{argmin}}\big\{\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}(u),\ u\in\mathfrak{V}_{u_{0}}\big\}
, then there exists a unique suitable weak solution
u
of the heat flow of harmonic map from
(M,g)
to
(X,d)

such that

u_{\varepsilon}\xrightarrow{\varepsilon\to 0}u\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}(M\times(0,\infty)).

If
(X,d)
additionally is locally compact, then
u\in C^{\alpha}(M\times(0,\infty))
for some
0<\alpha<1
.

Recall that there exists a nonnegative Radon measure
\nu=\nu_{t}dt
on

M\times(0,\infty)
such that

e_{\varepsilon}(u_{\varepsilon})\,dxdt=(\varepsilon|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}+|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2})\,dxdt\rightharpoonup\mu=\mu_{t}\,dt:=(|\nabla u|^{2}+\nu_{t})\,dt

as weak convergence of Radon measures.

When
(M,g)=(\mathbb{R}^{n},dx^{2})
, we introduced in
[
17
]
a parabolic frequency function for the suitable weak solution
u
given by Theorem
1.1
, that is, for
z_{0}=(x_{0},t_{0})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)

and
0<R\leq\sqrt{t_{0}}
,

N(u;z_{0},R,u(z_{0}))=\frac{E(u;z_{0},R)}{H(u;z_{0},R,u(z_{0})},

where

E(u;z_{0},R)=2R^{2}\int_{t=t_{0}-R^{2}}G_{z_{0}}(x,t)\,d\mu_{t}(x),

and

H(u;z_{0},R,u(z_{0}))=\int_{t=t_{0}-R^{2}}G_{z_{0}}(x,t)d^{2}(u(x,t),u(z_{0}))\,dx,

where
G_{z_{0}}
denotes the backward heat kernel in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,t_{0})
.
From both the inner and outer variations of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
at
u_{\varepsilon}
, we proved in
[
17
, Therorem 4.4]
that
N(u;z_{0},R,u(z_{0}))
is a monotonically increasing function of
R

in
(0,\sqrt{t_{0}})
. In
particular,
N(u;z_{0},u(z_{0}))
exists and is upper semi-continuous
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
. As an application of this monotonicity
property of
frequency function, we were able to show the spatial Lipschitz regularity of
u
for a wide class of CAT(0)-spaces including Euclidean buildings
and homogeneous trees. Namely,

Theorem 1.2
.

If
u_{0}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to(X,d)
, a CAT(0)-metric space, satisfies

E(u_{0})<\infty
and
d(u_{0},Q)\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
Q\in X
,
then there exists a unique suitable weak solution
u:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)\to X
of the heat flow of harmonic map,
with initial data
u_{0}
, which is Lipschitz continuous in
x
and
\frac{1}{2}
-Hölder continuous in
t
.

From
[
17
]
, the local spatial Lipschitz continuity is a consequence of the fact that
the value of frequency function
N(u;z_{0},u(z_{0}))\geq 1
for all

z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(0,\infty)
.

Very recently, Zhang and Zhu
[
23
]
proved the Lipschitz regularity of suitable weak solutions of heat flow of harmonic maps through a different method. In particular,
they showed the local Lipschitz regularity for suitable weak solutions holds in
(x,t)
when
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold with
Ricci curvature bounded from below and
(X,d)
is a CAT(0)-space.
Roughly speaking, among other ingredients their arguments are
mainly based on 1) the observation that
|\partial_{t}u|^{2}
is a sub solution of the heat equation on

M\times(0,\infty)
so that for
t>0
,
\phi^{t}(x,y)=d(u(x,t),u(y,t))

can be made into a sub-solution of the coupling elliptic equation

\Delta_{x,y}\phi^{t}(x,y)\geq-C
on
M\times M
, and 2) the approximation of
\phi^{t}
by the sup-convolution from the viscosity theory of nonlinear elliptic equations, that converts
\phi^{t}
into a semi-convex sub-solution
\phi^{t}_{\varepsilon}
which has almost everywhere twice differentiability by Aleksandrov’s theorem, this led them to establish a crucial parabolic perturbation Lemma. The idea of sup-convolution
might date back to R. Jensen’s original proof of uniqueness of viscosity solutions of second order fully nonlinear elliptic equations, see Jensen
[
11
]
or Caffarelli-Cabré
[
1
]
.

In this paper, we make a new observation of suitable weak solutions
u
to the heat flow of harmonic maps. It is motivated by Korevaar-Schoen’s
derivation of weak Bochner type inequality for minimizing harmonic maps
into CAT(0)-spaces, which is based on Reshetnyak’s quadrilateral comparison property for
d^{2}
, see
[
14
]
. More precisely, we show in Section 2 below that if
u
satisfies the EVI (
1.5
), then

(1.6)

\displaystyle\iint_{M\times(0,\infty)}|\nabla u|^{2}(\partial_{t}\eta+2\Delta\eta+C\eta+C|\nabla\eta|)\,dv_{g}dt\geq-C\iint_{M\times(0,\infty)}\eta|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\,dv_{g}dt

holds for any
0\leq\eta\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M\times(0,\infty))
,
with
C>0
depending on the
C^{1,1}
-norm of
g
.

On the other hand, as already observed by
[
23
]
, we have
that
|\partial_{t}u|^{2}
satisfies

(1.7)

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}-2\Delta)|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\geq 0\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ \mathcal{D}^{\prime}(M\times(0,\infty)).

See Section 3 below for a derivation slightly different from

[
23
, Theorem 3.4]
. It is clear that (
1.7
) leads to the local

-bound of

, which enables us to apply Moser’s proof of Harnack inequality
(cf.
[

]
) to (

)
to obtain the local

-bound of
|\nabla u|^{2}
.
This enables us to prove

Theorem 1.3
.

Assume
(X,d)
is a CAT(0)-metric space and
(M,g)
is a complete Riemannian manifold with positive inj
(M)
and bounded curvature.
For
u_{0}\in H^{1}(M,X)
, let

, with
u\in L^{\infty}_{t}H^{1}(M,X)
and

to

with initial value

. Then

is locally Lipschitz on

. Furthermore, for any

(1.8)

\displaystyle|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\nabla u|^{2}\leq C,\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ M\times[\delta,\infty),

where the constant

\delta,\ E(u_{0})
, inj
(M)
, and bound of the curvature of

As a byproduct of derivation of (

It is not hard to see from this definition

Remark 2.1
.

As already proved by
[
17
]

that there exists a unique suitable weak solution

to the heat flow of harmonic maps such that

u_{\varepsilon}\rightarrow u\ \ {\rm{in}}\ \ L^{2}_{\rm{loc}}\cap C^{0}_{\rm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}).

Sending

holds in the weak sense.

2.2.
(M,g)
: complete Riemann manifold without boundary

The main difficulty for the domain being a Riemannian manifold
arises from the fact that the translation of
a minimizer
u_{\varepsilon}
of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
,
u_{\varepsilon}^{*}(x,t)=u_{\varepsilon}(\overline{x}(x,s),t)
, is a minimizer of
\mathcal{I}_{\varepsilon}
associated with the pull-back metric
g^{*}=\overline{x}^{*}(g)
rather than
g
. In particular,
the errors of terms involving
|\partial_{t}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
are very difficult to
control. However, as observed by Korevaar-Schoen
[
14
]
the errors of terms involving
|\nabla u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}
can be controlled as long as the metric
g\in C^{1,1}(M)
. This latter observation leads us to consider the Evolution Variational Inequality (or EVI) that is the very definition of
u
being a suitable weak solution given by

[

]

First, recall that since any suitable solution

\displaystyle\int_{M}d^{2}(u(x,t),w(x))\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),w(x))\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{t-\delta}^{t}(E(w;g)-E(u(\tau);g))\,d\tau

(2.10)

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta(E(w;g)-E(u(t);g)),\ \forall w\in H^{1}(M,X),\ 0<\delta<t.

Here
E(u;g)
is defined by

E(u;g)=\int_{M}\frac{1}{2}g^{ij}\pi_{u}\big(\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}},\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\big)\,dv_{g},

and we have used the fact that
E(u(t);g)
is monotonically decreasing
in the last inequality.

We follow closely the presentation of Korevaar-Schoen

along with suitable modifications. Let

For

u_{s\omega}(x,t)=u(\overline{x}(x,s),t),\ \forall z=(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,\infty).

Then it is not hard to see that

\displaystyle\leq 2\int_{t-\delta}^{t}(E(w;g_{s\omega})-E(u(\tau);g_{s\omega}))\,d\tau

(2.11)

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta(E(w;g_{s\omega})-E(u(t);g_{s\omega})),\ \forall w\in H^{1}(M,X),\ 0<\delta<t.

Take

to

By choosing

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big(d^{2}(u(x,t),u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))\Big)\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\ +\int_{M}\Big(d^{2}(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))\Big)\,dv_{g_{s\omega}}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\Big(E(u_{\eta(t)}(t);g)+E(v_{\eta(t}(t);g_{s\omega})-E(u(t);g)-E(v(t);g_{s\omega})\Big).

\displaystyle\quad+2\delta\int_{M}\Big(\pi_{u_{1-\eta(x,t)}(x,t)}-\pi_{v(x,t)})_{ij}\Big)\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}\Big(d^{2}(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))\Big)\Big(dv_{g}-dv_{g_{s\omega}}\Big)

(2.13)

\displaystyle=I+II+III.

Now we want to estimate

separately as follows.
We can deduce from

\displaystyle I\leq 2\delta\Big(-\int_{M}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla d^{2}(u,v)\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)_{ij}g^{ij}\,dv_{g}+\int_{M}Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)\,dv_{g}\Big),

where

and

\frac{d^{2}\big(u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t),u_{\eta(x,t)}(\overline{x}(x,\varepsilon),t)\big)}{\varepsilon^{2}}\,dv_{g}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{P}(u,v,\eta)(Z,Z)\,dv_{g}

for any smooth tangent vector field

. Here

\frac{d}{ds}\overline{x}(x,s)=Z(\overline{x}(x,s)),\ \overline{x}(x,0)=x.

We can estimate

(2.15)

\displaystyle|III|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\Big|d^{2}(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))\Big|\Big|dv_{g}-dv_{g_{s\omega}}\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Cs\int_{M}d(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))(d(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))+d(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t)))\,dv_{g}.

The most difficult term is

, which has been

[

. Here we sketch it for convenience of the reader. First, decompose

\displaystyle\pi_{u_{1-\eta}}-\pi_{v}=\big(\pi_{u_{1-\eta}}-\mathcal{P}(u,v,1-\eta)\big)+\big(\mathcal{P}(u,v,1-\eta)-\eta\pi_{u}-(1-\eta)\pi_{v}\Big)-\eta\Big(\pi_{v}-\pi_{u}\Big).

And we can write

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta\big(\pi_{v}-\pi_{u}\big)_{ij}\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\eta\big(\pi_{u_{s\omega}}-\pi_{u}\big)_{ij}\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)

\displaystyle\quad+\int_{M}(\pi_{u})_{ij}\big(\eta_{-s\omega}-\eta\big)\big(g^{ij}dv_{g}-(g^{ij}dv_{g})_{-s\omega}\big).

Thus, we can obtain

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}\eta\big(\pi_{v}-\pi_{u}\big)_{ij}\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)\Big|\leq Cs^{2}\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g},

where

under the coordinate system, or equivalently the bound of curvature.
While

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}\big(\pi_{u_{1-\eta}}-\mathcal{P}(u,v,1-\eta)\big)\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)\Big|

\displaystyle\leq Cs\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla\eta|d(u,v)(|\nabla u|_{1}+|\nabla v|_{1})+|Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)|\Big)\,dv_{g},

\displaystyle\Big|\int_{M}\big(\mathcal{P}(u,v,1-\eta)-\eta\pi_{u}-(1-\eta)\pi_{v}\Big)\Big((g^{ij}dv_{g})_{s\omega}-g^{ij}dv_{g}\Big)\Big|\leq Cs\int_{M}\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)\,dv_{g}.

Putting these estimate together, we obtain that

\displaystyle|II|

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\Big[s\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla\eta|d(u,v)(|\nabla u|_{1}+|\nabla v|_{1})+|Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)|\Big)\,dv_{g}+s^{2}\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}

(2.16)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad+s\int_{M}|\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)|\,dv_{g}\Big].

Next we need to estimate the left hand side of the inequality (
2.2
).
For this, we claim that

(2.17)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\Big(d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),v(x,t-\delta))\Big)\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\Big[d^{2}(u(x,t),u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))+d^{2}(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))

\displaystyle\ \ \ \ -d^{2}(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))+d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))\Big]\,dv_{g}.

To see (
2.17
), recall that, for
P,Q,S\in X
,
let
P_{\lambda}=(1-\lambda)P+\lambda Q
, for
0\leq\lambda\leq 1
,
be the unique point on the geodesic connecting
P
to
S

such that

d(P_{\lambda},P)=\lambda d(P,S).

the following inequality follows directly from (
1.1
)

(2.18)

\displaystyle\lambda\Big(d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(P,S)-d^{2}(Q,S)\Big)\leq d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(P,P_{\lambda})-d^{2}(Q,P_{\lambda}).

Applying (
2.18
) with
P=u(x,t),Q=u(x,t-\delta),S=v(x,t)
,
and
\lambda=\eta(x,t)
, we have

(2.19)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\Big[d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),v(x,t)\Big]\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\Big[d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(u(x,t),u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(u_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))\Big]\,dv_{g}.

Similarly, applying (
3
) with
P=v(x,t),Q=v(x,t-s),S=u(x,t)
,
and

, we have

(2.20)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\Big[d^{2}(v(x,t),v(x,t-s))+d^{2}(v(x,t),u(x,t))-d^{2}(v(x,t-s),u(x,t)\Big]\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\Big[d^{2}(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))-d^{2}(v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))\Big]\,dv_{g}.

Also, recall the following inequality (see
[

, (2.1vi)]

(2.21)

d^{2}(P,R)+d^{2}(Q,S)-d^{2}(P,Q)-d^{2}(R,S)\leq d^{2}(Q,R)+d^{2}(P,S)-(d(R,S)-d(P,Q))^{2},

Applying (
2.21
) with
P=u(x,t),Q=v(x,t),R=v(x,t-s)

and

, we can deduce that

\displaystyle d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t))+d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),v(x,t))

(2.22)

\displaystyle-d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))\geq-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),v(x,t-\delta)).

Hence, with the help of (

), adding (

)

Finally, we can combine (

(2.23)

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\Big(d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t))-d^{2}(u(x,t-\delta),v(x,t-\delta))\Big)\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{M}\big[d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))\big]\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+2\delta\Big(-\int_{M}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla d^{2}(u,v)\,dv_{g}-\int_{M}\Big(\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)_{ij}g^{ij}-Cs|\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)|\Big)\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+C\delta\int_{M}|Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)|\,dv_{g}+C\delta s^{2}\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+C\delta s\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla\eta|d(u,v)(|\nabla u|_{1}+|\nabla v|_{1})+|Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)|\Big)\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+Cs\int_{M}d(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))(d(v(x,t),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t))+d(v(x,t-\delta),v_{\eta(x,t)}(x,t)))\,dv_{g}.

Observe that since
\mathcal{C}(u,v,\eta)
is positive-definite, bilinear tensor, we have that for sufficiently small

, it holds

Also it is easy to show that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\big[d^{2}(u(x,t),u(x,t-\delta))+d^{2}(v(x,t),v(x,t-\delta))\big]\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\delta\int_{t-\delta}^{t}\int_{M}\big(|\partial_{t}u|^{2}+|\partial_{t}v|^{2}\big)\,dv_{g}dt.

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\partial_{t}d^{2}(u(x,t),v(x,t))\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+C\Big[s\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla\eta|d(u,v)(|\nabla u|_{1}+|\nabla v|_{1})+|Q(\eta,\nabla\eta)|\Big)\,dv_{g}+s^{2}\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}\Big]

(2.24)

\displaystyle+Cs\int_{M}\eta|\partial_{t}v(x,t)|_{1}d(u(x,t),v(x,t))\,dv_{g}.

Now we can replace
\eta
by
\varepsilon\eta
and send

to obtain
that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta(x,t)\partial_{t}d^{2}(u(x,t),u_{s\omega}(x,t))\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq-2\int_{M}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla d^{2}(u,u_{s\omega})\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle+C\Big[s\int_{M}\Big(|\nabla\eta|d(u,v)(|\nabla u|_{1}+|\nabla u_{s\omega}|_{1})\Big)\,dv_{g}+s^{2}\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}\Big]

\displaystyle+Cs\int_{M}\eta|\partial_{t}u_{s\omega}|_{1}(x,t)d(u(x,t),u_{s\omega}(x,t))\,dv_{g}.

Finally, dividing both sides by
s^{2}
and sending

, we have that

\displaystyle\int_{M}\eta\partial_{t}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}+2\int_{M}\nabla\eta\cdot\nabla|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}+C\int_{M}\eta|\partial_{t}u|_{1}|\nabla u|_{1}\,dv_{g}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{M}(\eta+|\nabla\eta|)|\nabla u|^{2}\,dv_{g}+C\int_{M}\eta|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\,dv_{g},

where we use the Hölder inequality in the last step.

, this is equivalent to

(2.27)

\displaystyle\iint_{M\times(0,\infty)}|\nabla u|^{2}(\partial_{t}\eta+2\Delta\eta+C\eta+C|\nabla\eta|)\,dv_{g}dt\geq-C\iint_{M\times(0,\infty)}\eta|\partial_{t}u|^{2}\,dv_{g}dt,

3.
Sub-Caloricity of
|\partial_{t}u|^{2}

In this section, we sketch how to use the EVI (
2.2
)
to derive sub-caloricity of
|\partial_{t}u|^{2}
for any suitable weak
solution to the heat flow of harmonic maps into CAT(0)-space

(X,d)
. Note that this property was previously deduced by

[
23
]
. Our derivation is slightly different
from
[
23
]
.

Observe that for any
\delta>0
,
v(x,t)=u(x,t+\delta):M\times[0,\infty)\to(X,d)
is also a suitable weak solution of the heat flow of harmonic maps,
with the initial condition
v(x,0)=u(x,\delta)\in H^{1}(M,X)
.
For any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M\times(0,\infty))
, with
0\leq\phi\leq 1
,
let
u_{\phi(z)}(z)
be the unique point on
the geodesic joining
u(z)
to
v(z)

for
z\in M\times(0,\infty)
, satisfying

d(u_{\phi(z)}(z),u(z))=\phi(z)d(u(z),v(z)),

and
v_{\phi(z)}(z)
be the unique point on
the geodesic joining
u(z)
to
v(z)

for
z\in M\times(0,\infty)
, satisfying

d(v_{\phi(z)}(z),v(z))=\phi(z)d(u(z),v(z)).

It is easy to see that

v_{\phi(z)}(z)=u_{1-\phi(z)}(z),\ \forall\ z\in M\times(0,\infty).

Applying (
2.2
) to both solutions
u
and
v
, we obtain
that for
0<s<t
, it holds

\displaystyle d_{2}^{2}(u(t),u_{\phi}(t))-d_{2}^{2}(u(t-s),u_{\phi}(t))+d_{2}^{2}(v(t),v_{\phi}(t))-d_{2}^{2}(v(t-s),v_{\phi}(t))

(3.1)

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\ \ \ \leq 2s\big[E(u_{\phi}(t))-E(u(t))+E(v_{\phi}(t))-E(v(t))\big].

Now we claim that

(3.2)

\displaystyle E(u_{\phi}(t))-E(u(t))+E(v_{\phi}(t))-E(v(t))\leq-\int_{M\times\{t\}}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\big((1-2\phi)d^{2}(u,v)\big)\,dv_{g}

holds for any
\phi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(M\times(0,\infty))
with

0\leq\phi\leq 1
.

The proof of (
3.2
) is based on Reshetnyak’s quadrilateral comparison property for
d^{2}
, see
[
14
, Theorem 2.1.2]
. The original proof was given
by
[
23
, Lemma 3.2]
. Here we sketch a slightly different derivation just for convenience of the interested reader.

For
x,y\in M,t>0
, denote
P=u(x,t),Q=u(y,t),R=v(y,t),S=v(x,t)
,
\lambda=\phi(x,t)
,
\mu=\phi(y,t)
,

P_{\lambda}=u_{\phi}(x,t)
,
P_{1-\lambda}=v_{\phi}(x,t)
,

Q_{\mu}=u_{\phi}(y,t)
, and
Q_{1-\mu}=v_{\phi}(y,t)
.
Then

\displaystyle d^{2}(P_{\lambda},Q_{\mu})+d^{2}(P_{1-\lambda},Q_{1-\mu})

\displaystyle\leq\Big[\mu(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,R)+\lambda(1-\mu)d^{2}(Q,S)+\lambda\mu d^{2}(R,S)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)d^{2}(P,Q)-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)\Big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\Big[\lambda(1-\mu)d^{2}(P,R)+\mu(1-\lambda)d^{2}(Q,S)+(1-\lambda)(1-\mu)d^{2}(R,S)

\displaystyle\ \ \ +\ \lambda\mu d^{2}(P,Q)-\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)\Big]

\displaystyle=d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(R,S)+(\lambda+\mu-2\lambda\mu)\Big[d^{2}(P,R)+d^{2}(Q,S)-d^{2}(P,Q)-d^{2}(R,S)\Big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ -2\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-2\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)

\displaystyle\leq d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(R,S)+(\lambda+\mu-2\lambda\mu)\Big[d^{2}(Q,R)+d^{2}(P,S)-(d(R,S)-d(P,Q))^{2}\Big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ -2\lambda(1-\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-2\mu(1-\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)

\displaystyle\leq d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(R,S)-(\lambda-\mu)\Big[(1-2\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-(1-2\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)\Big]

\displaystyle\ \ \ -[\lambda+\mu-2\lambda\mu](d(R,S)-d(P,Q))^{2}

(3.3)

\displaystyle\leq d^{2}(P,Q)+d^{2}(R,S)-(\lambda-\mu)\Big[(1-2\lambda)d^{2}(P,S)-(1-2\mu)d^{2}(Q,R)\Big],

where have used the following inequality (see
[
14
, (2.1vi)]
):

(3.4)

d^{2}(P,R)+d^{2}(Q,S)-d^{2}(P,Q)-d^{2}(R,S)\leq d^{2}(Q,R)+d^{2}(P,S)-(d(R,S)-d(P,Q))^{2},

and

\lambda+\mu-2\lambda\mu\geq\lambda^{2}+\mu^{2}-2\lambda\mu=(\lambda-\mu)^{2}\geq 0,

since
0\leq\lambda,\mu\leq 1
.

It is readily seen, after substituting the values
of
P,Q,R,S
and

\lambda,\mu
, that (
3
) yields
