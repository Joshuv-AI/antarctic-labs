Perturbative calculations of light nuclei up to N 3 LO in chiral effective field theory
Oliver Thim, Andreas Ekström, Christian Forssén

Abstract
We predict ground-state energies of 3 H, 4 He, and 6 Li in chiral effective field theory up to next-to-next-to-next-to-leading-order (N 3 LO) using a power counting guided by renormalization-group invariance. Subleading two-nucleon interactions are treated perturbatively, and for 4 He and 6 Li, we calculate the perturbative corrections from numerical derivatives of ground-state energies obtained with Lanczos diagonalization. We find that including the 3 H binding energy in the calibration is essential for robust predictions of 4 He and 6 Li. This work demonstrates that the employed power counting can be applied to construct nuclear interactions with predictive power for light nuclei, bringing nuclear structure predictions closer to a foundation in quantum chromodynamics.



Introduction.

A central challenge in nuclear effective field theory is to construct nuclear interactions, guided by renormalization-group (RG) invariance, and accurately predict the properties of atomic nuclei. Chiral effective field theory (
\chi

The vast majority of

Nucleon-nucleon potential up to N

In
\chi
EFT, the nuclear potential is constructed from irreducible Feynman diagrams, including pion-exchange contributions and short-range contact interactions. The PC quantifies the importance of a given diagram in terms of the expansion parameter
(Q/\Lambda_{b})^{\nu}
, where the pion-mass
m_{\pi}
represents the typical low-energy scale
Q\approx m_{\pi}
and
\Lambda_{b}
is the
\chi
EFT breakdown scale. Estimates of the breakdown scale in WPC range between
\Lambda_{b}\approx 500-600
MeV
32
;
33

\Lambda_{b}\approx 300
MeV, in the Long and Yang PC
47

16
;
37

Formalism for perturbative bound-state calculations.
-

For
3
H, we implement Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory in J-NCSM with the Hamiltonian expanded in a spherical harmonic-oscillator basis of frequency
\omega
, including basis states up to
N_{\mathrm{max}}
oscillator quanta above the lowest configuration. At LO, we solve the Schrödinger equation

H^{(0)}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{n}}=E^{(0)}_{n}\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{n}},

(1)

non-perturbatively as described in Ref.
48
, to obtain the LO spectrum
\{E^{(0)}_{n},\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{n}}\}_{n}
. The LO Hamiltonian
H^{(0)}=T+V^{(0)}
is the sum of the intrinsic kinetic energy and the LO potential
V^{(0)}
.

Perturbative corrections to the ground-state energy (
n=0
) are computed by insertions of the subleading potentials
V^{(\nu>0)}
as

\displaystyle E^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{0}}

(2)

\displaystyle E^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(2)}|\Psi_{0}}+\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{|\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{k}}|^{2}}{E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{k}}

(3)

\displaystyle E^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(3)}|\Psi_{0}}

\displaystyle+2\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(2)}|\Psi_{k}}\braket{\Psi_{k}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{0}}}{E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{k}}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k\neq 0}\sum_{m\neq 0}\frac{\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{k}}\braket{\Psi_{k}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{m}}\braket{\Psi_{m}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{0}}}{(E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{k})(E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{m})}

\displaystyle-\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{0}}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{|\braket{\Psi_{0}|V^{(1)}|\Psi_{k}}|^{2}}{\left(E^{(0)}_{0}-E^{(0)}_{k}\right)^{2}},

(4)

where
\ket{\Psi_{n}}\equiv\ket{\Psi^{(0)}_{n}}
.
These sums are explicitly evaluated using the full LO spectrum. We find that the energy denominators do not suppress highly excited states enough to be neglected, and all intermediate states must be considered.

While it is feasible to compute the full LO spectrum in a J-NCSM calculation of
3
H, as done here, it is in general impossible to obtain more than a few converged eigenstates in many-body methods applied beyond the lightest nuclei.
Indeed, when calculating
4
He and
6
Li with the M-NCSM method, we are restricted to iterative solutions for selected states, e.g., the ground-state, using the Lanczos algorithm.

The corrections
E^{(\nu>0)}
to the LO ground-state energy
E^{(0)}
, listed in
Eqs.

2
,
3
and
4
, can be obtained without explicit knowledge of the full spectrum by computing numerical derivatives of the selected eigenvalues to the Schrödinger equation. We add the subleading potentials multiplied by parameters
\bm{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in\mathbb{R}^{3}
to the LO Hamiltonian and define

H(\bm{x})\equiv H^{(0)}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{3}x_{\nu}V^{(\nu)},

(5)

for which the Schrödinger equation

H(\bm{x})\ket{\Psi_{n}(\bm{x})}=E_{n}(\bm{x})\ket{\Psi_{n}(\bm{x})},

(6)

can be solved, yielding
\bm{x}
-dependent states and eigenvalues.
The perturbative corrections to ground-state energy can be formulated as derivatives of
E_{0}(\bm{x})
as

\displaystyle E^{(1)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{1}}E_{0}(\bm{x}),

(7)

\displaystyle E^{(2)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{2}}E_{0}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{2}\partial^{2}_{x_{1}}E_{0}(\bm{x}),

(8)

\displaystyle E^{(3)}

\displaystyle=\partial_{x_{3}}E_{0}(\bm{x})+\partial_{x_{1}}\partial_{x_{2}}E_{0}(\bm{x})+\frac{1}{6}\partial^{3}_{x_{1}}E_{0}(\bm{x}),

(9)

where all derivatives are evaluated at
\bm{x}=0
. This approach was applied at NLO in Refs.
56
;
34
and we will refer to it as the finite-difference (FD) method.

We perform numerical derivatives with finite-difference stencils
13
, where sufficient numerical accuracy is needed to compute third-order derivatives. If the eigenvalue,
E_{0}(\bm{x})
, is computed with precision
\epsilon
(e.g.
\epsilon=10^{-7}
for single precision) the relative error in an
n
:th order derivative follows the general form
E_{\epsilon}(h)=\epsilon h^{-n}+Kh^{p}
, where
h
is the stencil step length. The first term is the floating point round-off error and the second is the error from using a finite-order (
p\in\mathbb{N}
) stencil, where
K
is a constant. Thus, lowering
h
will lower the stencil truncation error but increase the numerical round-off error—especially for the higher-order derivatives. A complete computation up to N
3
LO with
h^{2}
(
h^{4}
) stencils requires 13 (31) exact diagonalizations for different values of
\bm{x}
.

Summary and conclusions.
-

We have shown that accurate perturbative computations of the ground-state energies in
3
H,
4
He, and
6
Li up to N
3
LO can be obtained in the NCSM using numerical derivatives from a set of Lanczos diagonalizations. We validated the numerical accuracy of this approach in
3
H by comparing with conventional Rayleigh-Schrödinger perturbation theory. We see no hurdles in applying this versatile numerical technique in other few- and many-body methods, also for higher mass numbers, since only non-perturbative solutions to the Schrödinger equation are needed.

Many-body computations are currently limited to relatively low values of the momentum cutoff, making it a challenge to analyze the RG invariance of predictions in all but selected few-nucleon Faddeev- and Faddeev-Yakubovsky calculations
42
;
24
.

We have constructed and evaluated several different interactions with LECs calibrated using neutron-proton phase shifts and
2,3
H ground-state energies. We found that different LEC calibration procedures had a large impact (
\approx 10
MeV) on predicted ground-state energies for
4
He and
6
Li. In particular, the predictions for the ground-state energies of
A>3
systems were significantly improved by tuning the LECs in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}{}^{3}D_{1}
channel to reproduce the
3
H binding energy. This induced a cutoff dependence in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}{}^{3}D_{1}
mixing angle at N
3
LO, which may be remedied by the inclusion of three-nucleon forces.

Future developments thus include accounting for three-nucleon forces, Coulomb interactions and isospin-breaking, and performing a multi-order Bayesian inference of the LECs with
\chi
EFT uncertainties. Enabling technologies like automatic differentiation
4
and emulators
15
;
23
;
9
tailored to perturbative computations further pave the way for systematically improvable
ab initio
calculations rooted in quantum chromodynamics.

Numerical accuracy of perturbative computations.
-

We now analyze the convergence of the FD method for computing perturbative corrections in more detail. The relative error in an
n
:th-order finite-difference derivative follows from
E_{\epsilon}(h)=\epsilon h^{-n}+Kh^{p}
, where
\epsilon
is the numerical precision of the function being differentiated, and
Kh^{p}
is the stencil truncation error. Optimal accuracy is obtained for sufficiently small
h
, prior to the onset of round-off error dominance.

We compare Rayleigh-Schrödinger and FD computations for
3
H in
Fig.

4
. The left panel shows the relative error between the two methods for J-NCSM, and the dashed line displays the error model,

and
\epsilon=2\times 10^{-15}
, compared to the N
3
LO result. This demonstrates that the observed error in the FD computations follows the expected form consistent with double numerical precision, and that a relative error of
10^{-4}\ \%
is achievable for an optimal choice of
h
. The right panel of

4

E_{\epsilon}(h)

h=0.06
.

For the
4

Calibration of interactions.
-

We employ five different interactions in this work, with (A)–(C) shown in the main text and two variants (B’), (C’) introduced here.
For these interactions, the LECs (shown in
Table

2
) are calibrated using different sets of data. For high partial waves, the interactions are identical and calibrated according to the procedure in Ref.
47
from the phase shift data listed in
Table

3
.
In the
{}^{1}S_{0}
and
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}{}^{3}D_{1}

Table

4

S

Interaction (A) is calibrated as in Ref.
47
using only neutron-proton scattering phase shifts. Interaction (B’) is a modification of (A) where the mixing angle calibration datum is replaced by the
3

2

3

T_{\mathrm{lab}}=25

{}^{1}S_{0}

3
LO in interaction (B), as seen in
Table

4
.

In interaction (C’), we change the LO calibration data from phase shifts to the singlet scattering length
(a_{s})
and the deuteron ground-state energy
(E_{d})

3
H binding energy at N
2
LO and N
3
LO. The shift in the
T_{\mathrm{lab}}=25

{}^{1}S_{0}

Deuteron properties

We compute deuteron properties for all five interactions. The results are shown in
Fig.

S2
. The deuteron wave functions are computed by solving the Schrödinger equation projected to the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}{}^{3}D_{1}
channel

(T+V^{(0)})\ket{\psi^{(0)}_{n}}=E^{(0)}_{n}\ket{\psi^{(0)}_{n}},

(S1)

and sub-leading corrections are obtained as

\ket{\psi^{(\nu)}_{n}}=\sum_{m\neq n}\frac{\braket{\psi^{(0)}_{m}|V^{(\nu)}|\psi^{(0)}_{n}}}{E^{(0)}_{n}-E^{(0)}_{m}}\ket{\psi^{(0)}_{m}},\quad\nu=2,3.

(S2)

The expressions simplify since the NLO potential
V^{(1)}
is zero in the
{}^{3}S_{1}\mathrm{-}{}^{3}D_{1}
channel. Furthermore, we define the full ground state wave function at order
\nu
as

\ket{\Psi^{(\nu)}_{0}}=\sum_{i=0}^{v}\ket{\psi^{(i)}_{0}}.

(S3)

There are two approaches to computing the deuteron properties, and observables in general. You can employ the perturbatively computed wave function
\ket{\Psi^{(\nu)}_{0}}
as if it was a non-perturbative solution to the Schrödinger equation. In this case, the deuteron matter radius reads

r^{2}_{D}=\braket{\Psi^{(\nu)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\Psi^{(\nu)}_{0}}=\sum_{i,j=0}^{\nu}\braket{\psi^{(i)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(j)}_{0}}

(S4)

One can also perform a strictly perturbative expansion, and only consider the terms in
Eq.

S4
up to the given order

\displaystyle r^{2}_{D}

\displaystyle=\braket{\psi^{(0)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(0)}_{0}}+\left[\braket{\psi^{(2)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(0)}_{0}}+\text{ h.c.}\right]

\displaystyle+\left[\braket{\psi^{(3)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(0)}_{0}}+\text{ h.c.}\right]+\dots

(S5)

where we define
\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{0}\equiv\braket{\psi^{(0)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(0)}_{0}}
, and
\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{\nu}\equiv[\braket{\psi^{(\nu)}_{0}|\hat{r}^{2}|\psi^{(0)}_{0}}+\text{h.c.}]
, for
\nu=2,3
. By expanding the square root, the radius at each order reads

\displaystyle\mathrm{LO:}\quad r_{D}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{0}},

(S6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}^{2}\mathrm{LO:}\quad r_{D}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{0}}\left(1+\frac{\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{2}}{2\braket{r^{2}}_{0}}\right),

(S7)

\displaystyle\mathrm{N}^{3}\mathrm{LO:}\quad r_{D}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{0}}\left(1+\frac{\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{2}+\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{3}}{2\braket{\hat{r}^{2}}_{0}}\right).

(S8)

The strictly perturbative computations are denoted
r^{(p)}_{D}
in
Fig.

S2
.

We note a discrepancy between the radius at N
3
LO computed with the expressions in
Eq.

S4
and
Eqs.

S6
,
S7
and
S8
for interactions (A), (B’), and (B), but a good agreement for interactions (C’) and (C). This is likely a result of the poor reproduction of the deuteron binding energy and radius at LO in interaction (A), (B’), and (B). The better reproduction of the radius at N
3
LO in the non-perturbative computation from
Eq.

S4
can be related to the improved convergence enjoyed by the so-called
Z
-parameterization
41
, where the included higher-order terms in
Eq.

S4
improved the tail-description of the wave function relevant for the radius observable.

(Q_{D})
and
D
-state probability
(p_{D})
using the non-perturbative method similar to
Eq.

S4
, see, e.g.,
31
. The results are also shown in
