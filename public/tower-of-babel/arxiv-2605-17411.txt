Combinatorics of Schur ultrafilters Thanks: The research of the author was funded in whole by the Austrian Science Fund FWF [10.55776/ESP399].
Serhii Bardyla

Abstract
. In this paper, we provide a combinatorial characterization of the elements of Schur ultrafilters on countable commutative groups. Using this characterization, we construct a free Schur ultrafilter on ℤ \mathbb{Z} that is not infinitary Schur. Moreover, assuming the Continuum Hypothesis, we establish the existence of a free Schur P-point on ℤ \mathbb{Z} .

1.
Introduction and main results

A family
\mathcal{F}
of subsets of a set
X
is called a
filter on

X
if

(i)

\emptyset\notin\mathcal{F}
;

(ii)

A\cap B\in\mathcal{F}
for every
A,B\in\mathcal{F}
;

(iii)

if
A\in\mathcal{F}
and
B\supseteq A
, then
B\in\mathcal{F}
.

A filter
\mathcal{F}
on a set
X
is called an
ultrafilter
if
\mathcal{F}
is maximal with respect to the inclusion among all filters on
X
or, equivalently, for each
A\subseteq X
either
A\in\mathcal{F}
or
X\setminus A\in\mathcal{F}
. A family
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{F}
is called a
base
of a filter
\mathcal{F}
if for each
F\in\mathcal{F}
there exists
B\in\mathcal{B}
such that
B\subseteq F
. A filter
\mathcal{F}
is called
free
if
\bigcap\mathcal{F}=\emptyset
.

The Stone-Čech compactification
\beta(X)
of a discrete space
X
is the set of all ultrafilters on
X
endowed with a topology
\tau
generated by the base
\mathcal{B}=\{\langle A\rangle:A\subseteq X\}
, where

\langle A\rangle=\{u\in\beta(X):A\in u\}.

Recall that each element
x\in X
is identified with the principal ultrafilter
\{A\subseteq X:x\in A\}
. If
S
is a discrete semigroup, then the semigroup operation on
S
can be canonically lifted to a semigroup operation on
\beta(S)
as follows: if
u,v\in\beta(S)
, then
uv
is a filter generated by the base consisting of the sets
\bigcup_{x\in U}xV_{x}
, where
U\in u
and
\{V_{x}:x\in U\}\subseteq v
are arbitrary.
By
[
4
, Theorem 4.1]
, the defined above semigroup operation on
\beta(S)
is unique among those extending the operation of
S
and satisfying the following two natural conditions:

(i)

for each
u\in\beta(S)
the right shift
\rho_{u}:\beta(S)\rightarrow\beta(S)
,
x\mapsto xu
is continuous;

(ii)

for each
s\in S
the left shift
\lambda_{s}:\beta(S)\rightarrow\beta(S)
,
x\mapsto sx
is continuous.

The classical Ellis’s theorem (see
[
4
, Theorem 2.5]
) implies the existence of free idempotent ultrafilters on every infinite semigroup. In other words, for each infinite semigroup
S
there exists a free ultrafilter
u\in\beta(S)
such that
uu=u
.
For more about the algebraic structure of
\beta(S)
see the monograph
[
4
]
and references therein.

Definition
.

An ultrafilter
u
on a semigroup is called

(i)

Schur
if for each
U\in u
there exist
a,b\in U
such that
ab\in U
;

(ii)

infinitary Schur
if for each
U\in u
there exist
a\in U
and an infinite subset
B\subseteq U
such that
aB\subseteq U
.

It is straightforward to check that an ultrafilter
u
on a semigroup is idempotent, if and only if for each
U\in u
there exists
x\in U
and
V_{x}\in u
such that
xV_{x}\subseteq U
.
Thus, for a free ultrafilter
u
on a semigroup the following implications hold:

u\hbox{ is idempotent }\Longrightarrow u\hbox{ is infinitary Schur }\Longrightarrow u\hbox{ is Schur}.

Schur ultrafilters were introduced by Protasov
[
7
]
to construct certain group topologies. Recently
[
1
,
9
]
it was shown that these ultrafilters play a crucial role in a description of the Bohr compactification of topological groups, as well as in the automatic continuity of group operations in so-called chart groups.

Definition
.

For a discrete semigroup
S
let

(i)

\operatorname{Sch}(S)
be the subspace of
\beta(S)
consisting of all free Schur ultrafilters;

(ii)

\operatorname{Sch}^{\infty}(S)
be the subspace of
\beta(S)
consisting of all free infinitary Schur ultrafilters;

(iii)

\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{fin}}(S)
be the subspace of
\beta(S)
consisting of all free Schur ultrafilters which are not infinitary Schur.

The following problem was posed in
[
1
, Problem 3.15]

Problem 1.1

(Bardyla, Zlatoš)
.

Does there exist a free Schur ultrafilter on a countable group
G
that is not infinitary Schur?

The first principal result of this paper is the following solution of Problem
1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The set
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is an open dense subset of
\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Definition
.

An ultrafilter
u
on a countable set
X
is called

(i)

weak P-point
if
u
is not in the closure of any countable subset of
\beta X\setminus(X\cup\{u\})
;

(ii)

P-point
if for every countable subset
\{U_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
u
, there exists
U\in u
such that
U\subseteq^{*}U_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(iii)

selective
if for every partition
\{C_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of
X
such that
C_{n}\notin u
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
U\in u
such that
|U\cap C_{n}|\leq 1
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

It is known that each selective ultrafilter is a P-point and each P-point is a weak P-point.
Kunen
[
5
]
showed that ZFC implies the existence of a free weak P-point on
\mathbb{Z}
. However, the existence of free P-points and selective ultrafilters on
\mathbb{Z}
is consistent with ZFC, but cannot be derived from ZFC alone (see
[
2
,
8
]
). Notice that a free idempotent ultrafilter
u
on a countable group
G
cannot be a weak P-point, as
u
is an accumulation point of the countable family
\{xu:x\in G\}\subseteq\beta G\setminus G
.

The following result was proven in
[
1
, Propositions 3.12 and 3.13]

Proposition 1.3

(Bardyla, Zlatoš)
.

For a countable group
G
the following assertions hold:

(i)

if
u\in\operatorname{Sch}^{\infty}(G)
, then
u
is not a P-point;

(ii)

if
u\in\operatorname{Sch}(G)
and
G
is commutative, then
u
is not selective.

Proposition
1.3
motivates the following problem, posed in
[
1
, Problem 3.16]
.

Problem 1.4

(Bardyla, Zlatoš)
.

Is the existence of a free Schur ultrafilter on
\mathbb{Z}
that is a P-point consistent with ZFC?

By
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{p}}(\mathbb{Z})
we denote the set of all free Schur ultrafilters on
\mathbb{Z}
which are P-points. The second principal result of this paper solves Problem
1.4
under the Continuum Hypothesis (shortly, CH).

Theorem 1.5
.

(CH) The set
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{p}}(\mathbb{Z})
is dense in
\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})
.

We left the following question open:

Question 1.6
.

Does ZFC imply there existence of a free infinitary Schur ultrafilter on
\mathbb{Z}
that is a weak P-point?

A surjective function
c:X\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,r\}
is called an
r
-coloring
of a set
X
.

Definition
.

Let
G
be a countable commutative group. A subset
A=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq G
is called
Schur
if for every
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
there exists a minimal number
S(r,k)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
r
-coloring of
X=\{x_{n}:n\leq S(r,k)\}
there exist
a\in X
and
\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}\subseteq X
such that
\{a\}\cup\{b_{1},\ldots,b_{k}\}\cup\{a+b_{i}:i\leq k\}
is a monochromatic subset of
X
.

It is easy to see that the Schur property of a subset
A
does not depend on the enumeration of
A
. In contrast, the numbers
S(r,k)
do depend on the enumeration of
A
. The following combinatorial description of elements of free Schur ultrafilters on countable commutative groups, which is crucial for the proofs of Theorems
1.2
and
1.5
, is the third principal result of this paper.

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
G
be a countable commutative group. A subset
A\subseteq G
belongs to some free Schur ultrafilter on
G
if and only if
A
is Schur.

It is clear that the set
\mathbb{N}
of positive integers with its usual increasing enumeration is a Schur subset of
\mathbb{Z}
. Thus, for each
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
the positive integer
S(r,k)
is well-defined.
We refer to these integers as
two dimensional Schur numbers
. Recall that the classical Schur numbers
S(r)
,
r\in\mathbb{N}
are defined as follows:
S(r)
is the minimal positive integer such that for every
r
-coloring of the set
X=\{n\in\mathbb{N}:n\leq S(r)\}
there exist
a,b\in X
such that the set
\{a,b,a+b\}
is monochromatic. In our terminology, we have
S(r)=S(r,1)
for each
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Despite its relatively simple definition, the computational complexity of the classical Schur numbers is extremely high. In particular, the equality
S(5)=161

was established as recently as 2017 by Heule
[
3
]
through the use of massive parallel computing. In particular, their computation required 2 petabytes of space, see the news article
[
6
]
. This motivate the following fairly general question:

Question 1.8
.

What can be said about the values of two-dimensional Schur numbers
S(r,n)
, where
r,n\in\mathbb{N}
?

2.
Proofs of main results

The following result was proven in
[
1
, Corollary 3.11]
.

Proposition 2.1

(Bardyla, Zlatoš)
.

Let
u
be a Schur ultrafilter on a commutative group
G
. Then for each
U\in u
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
x\in U
such that
|\{y\in U:x+y\in U\}|\geq n
.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
u
be a free Schur ultrafilter on a countable commutative group
G
. Then every
U\in u
is a Schur set.

Proof.

Fix an enumeration
U=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Assuming the contrary, there exist
r,k\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
exists an
r
-coloring
\phi_{p}
of
\{x_{n}:n\leq p\}
with the following property: for each monochromatic subset
B
of
\{x_{n}:n\leq p\}
, for each
y\in B
we have
|\{z\in B:y+z\in B\}|<k
. The Pigeonhole Principle yields a color
c_{1}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that the set
M[1]=\{p\in\mathbb{N}:\phi_{p}(x_{1})=c_{1}\}
is infinite. Similarly, there exists a color
c_{2}\in\{1,\ldots,r\}
such that the set
M[2]=\{p\in M[1]:\phi_{p}(x_{2})=c_{2}\}
is infinite. Proceeding in this way, we obtain a decreasing family
\{M[n]:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
of infinite subsets of
\mathbb{N}
, and a finite coloring
\psi
of
U
that colors
x_{n}
into
c_{n}
. Since
u
is an ultrafilter, there exists
q\leq r
such that
\psi^{-1}(q)\in u
. By Proposition
2.1
, there exist
a\in\psi^{-1}(q)
and
B\subseteq\psi^{-1}(q)
such that
|B|=k
and
a+B\subseteq\psi^{-1}(q)
. Let
p^{\prime}
be any positive integer such that for each
x_{n}\in\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)
we have
n\leq p^{\prime}
.
Fix any
d\in M[p^{\prime}]
and notice that the colorings
\phi_{d}
and
\psi
have the same traces on
\{x_{n}:n\leq p^{\prime}\}
. But then the monochromatic subset
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)\subseteq\{x_{n}:n\leq p^{\prime}\}
contradicts the choice of
\phi_{d}
.
∎

The proof of the following lemma is straightforward and thus is left to the reader.

Lemma 2.3
.

If
A
is a Schur subset of a commutative group
G
, then every subset
B\subseteq G
such that
A\subseteq^{*}B
is Schur.

Recall that a family
\mathcal{R}
of nonempty subsets of a set
X
is called
partition regular
if for each finite family
\mathcal{A}
of subsets of
X
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{A}\in\mathcal{R}
we have that there exists
A\in\mathcal{A}
and
B\in\mathcal{R}
such that
B\subseteq A
(see
[
4
, Chapter 3.1]
for more information).

The following result implies that the family of all Schur subsets of a countable commutative group
G
is partition regular.

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
A
be a Schur subset of a countable commutative group
G
. Then for every partition
A=X\sqcup Y
either
X
is Schur or
Y
is Schur.

Proof.

Let
A=\{a(n):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
be an enumeration of
A
.
Seeking a contradiction, assume that there exists a partition
A=X\sqcup Y
such that neither
X
nor
Y
is Schur. By Lemma
2.3
, without loss of generality we can assume that both
X
and
Y
are infinite. Let

X=\{a(n_{l}):l\in\mathbb{N}\}\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad Y=\{a(m_{l}):l\in\mathbb{N}\},

where the sequences
\{n_{l}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and
\{m_{l}:l\in\mathbb{N}\}
are increasing. By the assumption, there exist
r_{1},k_{1}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
r_{1}
-coloring
\phi^{X}_{p}
of
\{a(n_{k}):k\leq p\}
with the following property:
for each monochromatic subset
B
of
\{a(n_{k}):k\leq p\}
, for each
b\in B
we have
|\{z\in B:b+z\in B\}|<k_{1}
. Similarly, there exist
r_{2},k_{2}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
p\in\mathbb{N}
there exist
r_{2}
-coloring
\phi^{Y}_{p}
of
\{a(m_{k}):k\leq p\}
with the following property: for each monochromatic subset
B
of
\{a(m_{k}):k\leq p\}
, for each
b\in B
we have
|\{z\in B:b+z\in B\}|<k_{2}
.

Let
r=r_{1}+r_{2}
and
k=\max\{k_{1},k_{2}\}
. Since
A
is a Schur set, Proposition
2.2
yields a number
S(r,k)\in\mathbb{N}
such that for every
r
-coloring of the set
T=\{a(n):n\leq S(r,k)\}
there exist
a\in T
and
B\subseteq T
such that
|B|=k
and the set
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)\subseteq T
is monochromatic. Put

f=|X\cap T|\qquad\hbox{ and }\qquad g=|Y\cap T|.

By the choice of enumerations of
X
and
Y
we get

T=\{a(n_{k}):k\leq f\}\cup\{a(m_{k}):k\leq g\}.

Define an
r
-coloring
\psi
of
T
as follows:

\psi(a)=\begin{cases}\phi_{f}^{X}(a),&\hbox{if }a\in\{a(n_{k}):k\leq f\};\\
\phi_{g}^{Y}(a)+r_{1},&\hbox{if }a\in\{a(m_{k}):k\leq g\}.\end{cases}

By the choice of
S(r,k)
, there exist
a\in T
and
B\subseteq T
such that
|B|=k
and the set
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)\subseteq T
is monochromatic with respect to the coloring
\psi
. By the definition of the coloring
\psi
, either
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)\subseteq X
or
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)\subseteq Y
. In the former case the set
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)
is monochromatic with respect to the coloring
\phi^{X}_{f}
, whereas in the latter case the set
\{a\}\cup B\cup(a+B)
is monochromatic with respect to the coloring
\phi^{Y}_{g}
. Taking into account the definition of
k
, we obtain a contradiction with the choice of
\phi^{X}_{f}
and
\phi^{Y}_{g}
.
∎

The implication (

.

The implication (
\Leftarrow

We are in a position to prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Fix any free Schur ultrafilter
u
on
\mathbb{Z}
and its element
U
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
U\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. Consider the increasing enumeration
U=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. By Theorem
1.7
, to show that
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is dense in
\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})
, it suffices to construct a Schur subset
W\subseteq U
such that for each
a\in W
the set
\{b\in W:a+b\in W\}
is finite. By Proposition
2.2
,
U
is a Schur set. Hence the number
p_{1}=S(1,1)
is well defined for the given enumeration of
U
. Let
B_{1}=\{x_{n}:n\leq p_{1}\}
. Since the ultrafilter
u
is free, we have
U_{1}=\{x_{n}:n>2p_{1}\}\in u
. Consider the following partition of the set
U_{1}
into the sets
A_{i}^{1}
,
0\leq i\leq x_{p_{1}}
: for every
a\in U_{1}
, we have that
a\in A_{i}^{1}
if and only if the remainder of the division of
a
by
x_{p_{1}}+1
equals
i
. Since
u
is an ultrafilter and
U_{1}\in u
, there exists
0\leq i\leq x_{p_{1}}
such that
A_{i}^{1}\in u
. Fix the increasing enumeration
A_{i}^{1}=\{a^{1}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. By Proposition
2.2
, the set
A^{1}_{i}
is Schur. Thus the number
p_{2}=S(2,2)
is well defined for the given enumeration of
A^{1}_{i}
.
Let
B_{2}=\{a^{1}_{n}:n\leq p_{2}\}
.
Note that
(B_{1}+B_{1})\cap B_{2}=\emptyset
, as
U_{1}\cap\{x_{n}:n\leq 2p_{1}\}=\emptyset
and
B_{2}\subseteq A^{1}_{i}\subseteq U_{1}
. Recall that for each
a\in B_{1}
we have
a<x_{p_{1}}+1
. Then for every
y\in B_{2}
the remainder of the division of
x+y
by
x_{p_{1}}+1
does not equal
i
, which implies
x+y\notin B_{2}
. Thus
(B_{1}+B_{2})\cap B_{2}=\emptyset
. Also, it is clear that
(B_{2}+B_{2})\cap B_{1}=\emptyset
. Hence for any
i,j,m\in\{1,2\}
we have

(B_{i}+B_{j})\cap B_{m}\neq\emptyset\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad i=j=m.

By the choice of
B_{2}
, for each
2
-coloring of
B_{1}\cup B_{2}
there exist
b\in B_{2}
and
\{c,d\}\subseteq B_{2}
such that
\{b,c,d,b+c,b+d\}
are monochromatic.

Assume that we have already constructed a family of finite pairwise disjoint subsets
\{B_{j}:j\leq k\}
of
U=\{x_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
, such that

(1)

for all
i,j,m\in\{1,\ldots,k\}
we have
(B_{i}+B_{j})\cap B_{m}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
i=j=m
;

(2)

for every
j\leq k
, for each
j
-coloring of
\bigcup_{i\leq j}B_{i}
there exist
b\in B_{j}
and a subset
C\subseteq B_{j}
of cardinality
j
such that the set
\{b\}\cup C\cup(b+C)\subseteq B_{j}
is monochromatic.

Let
q=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}:x_{n}\in\bigcup_{j\leq k}B_{j}\}
. Since
u
is a free Schur ultrafilter, we have that the set
U_{k}=\{x_{n}:n>2q\}\subseteq U
is a Schur set that belongs to
u
. Consider the following partition of the set
U_{k}
into the sets
A_{i}^{k}
,
0\leq i\leq x_{q}
: for every
a\in U_{k}
, we have that
a\in A_{i}^{k}
if and only if the remainder of the division of
a
by
x_{q}+1
equals
i
. Since
U_{k}\in u
and
u
is an ultrafilter, there exists
0\leq i\leq x_{q}
such that
A_{i}^{k}\in u
. Fix the increasing enumeration
A_{i}^{k}=\{a^{k}_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
. Since the set
A^{k}_{i}
is Schur, the number
p_{k+1}=S(k+1,k+1)
is well defined for the given enumeration of

. Put
B_{k+1}=\{a^{k}_{n}:n\leq p_{k+1}\}
. Similarly as in the case

, one can check that the family
\{B_{j}:j\leq k+1\}
satisfies the inductive assumptions (1), (2) for

.

Upon completing the recursion we get a family
\{B_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
consisting of pairwise disjoint finite subsets of

which satisfies the following two conditions:

(1)

(B_{i}+B_{j})\cap B_{m}\neq\emptyset
if and only if
i=j=m
;

(2)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for each
n
-coloring of

there exist
b\in B_{n}
and a subset
C\subseteq B_{n}
of cardinality

such that the set
\{b\}\cup C\cup(b+C)\subseteq B_{n}
is monochromatic.

Put

. It is clear that

.
Condition (2) ensures that

is a Schur set. Condition (1) implies that for every

the set

is finite, as it is contained in

. Hence
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is dense in
\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})
.

\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})

, witnessing that the set
\operatorname{Sch}^{\operatorname{fin}}(\mathbb{Z})
is open in
\operatorname{Sch}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Fix a Schur subset
A\subseteq\mathbb{Z}
. Without loss of generality we can assume that
A\subseteq\mathbb{N}
. By Theorem
1.7
, it suffices to construct a Schur

-point
u
such that
A\in u
.
Enumerate the set of all infinite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}
as
\{A_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\operatorname{Succ}(\mathfrak{c})\}
, where
\operatorname{Succ}(\mathfrak{c})
is the set of all successor ordinals in
\mathfrak{c}
, and
A=A_{0}
. Let
U_{0}=A_{0}=A
. Assume that for all
\xi<\gamma<\mathfrak{c}
we constructed Schur subsets
U_{\xi}
of
\mathbb{Z}
which satisfy the following two conditions:

(1)

U_{\alpha}\subseteq^{*}U_{\beta}
for all
\beta\leq\alpha<\gamma
;

(2)

for every successor ordinal
\xi\in\gamma
we have either
U_{\xi}\subseteq A_{\xi}
or
U_{\xi}\subseteq G\setminus A_{\xi}
;

If
\gamma=\delta+1
, then Lemma
2.4
implies that either
U_{\delta}\cap A_{\gamma}
is a Schur set, or
U_{\delta}\cap(G\setminus A_{\gamma})
is a Schur set. If
U_{\delta}\cap A_{\gamma}
is a Schur set, then put
U_{\gamma}=U_{\delta}\cap A_{\gamma}
. Otherwise, set
U_{\gamma}=U_{\delta}\cap(G\setminus A_{\gamma})
.

Assume that
\gamma
is a limit ordinal. Since
\mathfrak{c}=\aleph_{1}
, there exists a cofinal increasing subset
\{\mu(n):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
in
\gamma
. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
let
V_{n}=\bigcap_{i\leq n}U_{\mu(i)}
.
Note that
U_{\mu(n)}\subseteq^{*}V_{n}
for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Lemma
2.3
implies that the sets
V_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are Schur. For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
fix the increasing enumeration
V_{n}=\{x^{n}_{k}:k\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
Then for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
the number
p_{n}=S(n,n)
is well defined for the given enumeration of
V_{n}
. Let
B_{n}=\{x^{n}_{k}:k\leq p_{n}\}
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Note that the family
\{B_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

B_{n}\subseteq V_{n}\subseteq U_{\mu(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
;

(ii)

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, for each
\leq n
-coloring of
\bigcup_{i\in\mathbb{N}}B_{i}
there exist
b\in B_{n}
and a subset
C\subseteq B_{n}
of cardinality
n
such that the set
\{b\}\cup C\cup(b+C)\subseteq B_{n}
is monochromatic.

Put
U_{\gamma}=\bigcup_{n\in\mathbb{N}}B_{n}
.
Condition (ii) ensures that
U_{\gamma}
is a Schur set. Since
V_{n+1}\subseteq V_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
and the sets
B_{n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
are finite, condition (i) implies that
U_{\gamma}\subseteq^{*}V_{n}\subseteq U_{\mu(n)}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Taking into account that the sequence
\{\mu(n):n\in\mathbb{N}\}
is cofinal in
\gamma
, the inductive assumption (1) implies that
U_{\gamma}\subseteq^{*}U_{\alpha}
for each
\alpha\leq\gamma
.

Upon completing the recursion, we obtain a family
\mathcal{U}=\{U_{\alpha}:\alpha\in\mathfrak{c}\}
which is well ordered by the relation
\supseteq^{*}
. Let us verify that the filter
u
generated by the family
\mathcal{U}
is a Schur P-point. Fix any infinite subset
X
of
\mathbb{Z}
. There exists a successor ordinal
\alpha\in\mathfrak{c}
such that
X=B_{\alpha}
. The set
U_{\alpha}
witnesses that either
B_{\alpha}\in u
, or
\mathbb{Z}\setminus B_{\alpha}\in u
. Hence
u
is an ultrafilter. Since
\mathcal{U}
is a base of
u
consisting of Schur sets, Lemma
2.3
implies that
u
is a Schur ultrafilter. Finally, fix a countable family
\{X_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}\subseteq u
. Since
\mathcal{U}
is a base of
u
, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
there exists
\xi(n)\in\mathfrak{c}
such that
U_{\xi(n)}\subseteq X_{n}
. Put
\xi=\sup\{\xi_{n}:n\in\mathbb{N}\}
and note that
U_{\xi}\subseteq^{*}X_{n}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Hence
u
is a P-point.
∎
