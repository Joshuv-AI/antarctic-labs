Risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression in cervical ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament after laminoplasty
Authors: Hao Zhou, Dongzhen Zhou, Bicheng Wang, Qianxi Jin, Zeng Xu, Lei Liang, Huajiang Chen
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractPurposeThis study aimed to analyze the risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression in cervical ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) after laminoplasty (LP).MethodsPatients with OPLL who underwent LP at our institution and were followed for more than 12 months were enrolled. Three-dimensional models were reconstructed using CT images to measure the OPLL volume preoperatively and at final follow-up, and the ossification volume of each segment was calculated. The annual volume growth rate (AVGR) of OPLL was calculated by dividing the volume change by the follow-up duration. OPLL segmental ossification volume with an AVGR greater than 12.24% were classified into the progression group. Variables were categorized into segmental ossification-level factors and patient-level factors. Segmental ossification-level factors included facet tropism (FT), facet angle (FA), and segmental range of motion (SROM), among others. Patient-level factors included age, body mass index (BMI), intraoperative blood loss, follow-up duration, and smoking status, among others. Correlation analyses were performed to assess the associations between segmental ossification-level factors, patient-level factors and segmental ossification AVGR. Factors with P < 0.05 were entered into a generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) to identify risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL after LP.ResultsA total of 37 patients (172 segments) were included. The mean age was 55.19 ± 10.53 years, and the mean follow-up duration was 23.11 ± 14.01 months. There were 114 segments in the progression group and 58 segments in the non-progression group, with no significant difference in the distribution of ossified segments between the two groups. Correlation analyses showed that FT (P = 0.002), ROM (P < 0.001), smoking (P = 0.029), alcohol consumption (P = 0.025), and operative time (P = 0.031) were significantly positively correlated with AVGR, whereas age (P = 0.040), follow-up duration (P = 0.007), and diabetes mellitus (P < 0.001) were significantly negatively correlated with AVGR. The GLMM demonstrated that FT (OR = 2.169, P = 0.012) and greater SROM (OR = 3.182, P < 0.001) were independent risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression of OPLL after LP. The area under the curve (AUC) of the model was 0.933.ConclusionSROM and FT are risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression of OPLL. Background

Cervical ossification of the posterior longitudinal ligament (OPLL) is a common spinal disease, particularly prevalent in East Asian populations [1]. Compared with laminectomy and fusion (LF), laminoplasty (LP) has distinct advantages in achieving effective decompression while preserving cervical range of motion (ROM). However, the growth rate of ossification volume after LP has been reported to be up to seven times higher than that after LF [2]. Previous studies have shown that ossification volume progression accounts for 50% of reoperations within two years after surgery for OPLL, representing a major challenge that limits long-term surgical outcomes [3, 4]. Therefore, identifying the risk factors for OPLL ossification volume progression after LP is critical for surgical decision-making.

Most existing studies have focused on predicting overall ossification volume progression after LP. Although relatively high predictive accuracy has been achieved, these models remain unable to identify the specific segments in which ossification volume progression leads to recurrent spinal cord compression, thereby providing limited benefit in reducing the risk of reoperation [5]. Accordingly, the present study aimed to identify the risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL.

Previous studies have confirmed that greater cervical C2/C7 ROM is a risk factor for overall ossification volume progression, suggesting that segmental range of motion (SROM) should be considered when predicting segmental ossification volume progression [2]. Facet tropism (FT), which reflects asymmetry between the left and right facet joints, has been reported to be associated with degenerative spinal conditions such as cervical disc herniation [6–8]. More recently, FT has been shown to be significantly associated with OPLL [9]. Therefore, FT was also included as a potential risk factor in the present study.

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first study to identify risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression after surgery for OPLL. Given the potential influence of patient-level factors and patient-level factors were incorporated into the analysis. A generalized linear mixed model (GLMM) was used to evaluate the risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL after LP.

Materials and methods

Study design and population

The study protocol was approved by the Ethics Committee of Shanghai Changzheng Hospital (approval number: 2024SL027). Patients diagnosed with OPLL who underwent LP between 2013 and 2024 were included. The inclusion criteria were as follows: (1) OPLL confirmed by CT imaging; (2) treatment with LP; and (3) a postoperative follow-up CT interval of more than 12 months. All patients were instructed to wear a cervical collar for 2 months after surgery and were followed up at 2 months, 6 months, and annually thereafter. Imaging data and clinical data were collected. Imaging data included cervical ossified segments of OPLL, SROM, facet angle (FA), and FT. Clinical data included baseline patient characteristics, surgical variables, and comorbidities.

Surgical procedure

A posterior midline longitudinal incision was made in the cervical region. According to the surgical levels, the skin was incised longitudinally along the spinous processes of the corresponding segments. The subcutaneous tissue, fascia, and ligamentum nuchae were divided using electrocautery, and hemostasis was achieved layer by layer. The paraspinal muscles were subperiosteally dissected along the bilateral laminae to expose the lateral masses, exposing the laminae of the surgical segments.

Using a punch and a Kerrison rongeur, two bone grooves parallel to the spinous processes were created on both sides at the transitional zone between the lamina and the lateral mass. The inner cortical bone of the left-side bone groove was removed with a Kerrison rongeur, taking care to avoid damaging the dural sac. The right side was designated as the hinge side, and the left side as the opening side. The laminae were firmly grasped with a lamina holder, and the laminae were gently lifted and opened toward the right side, centering on the hinge, until the opening width reached approximately 10 mm.

The hypertrophic ligamentum flavum was dissected using a nerve dissector and then resected. After confirming the absence of residual compression, a mini titanium plate was implanted to maintain the open-door status. One negative-pressure drainage tube was placed. The skin was sutured layer by layer with silk thread, and sterile dressing was applied. The procedure was then completed. All patients followed the same standardized rehabilitation protocol (wearing a cervical collar for 2 months).

Imaging measurements

Cervical spine radiographs and CT scans were collected. Cobb angles of each cervical segment were measured on preoperative flexion and extension radiographs, and the difference between the Cobb angles in these two positions was defined as SROM (Fig. 1). Using preoperative CT images, bilateral FA values at each cervical segment were measured on axial images, and the difference between the left and right FA values was calculated. The definition and measurement of FA were consistent with previous studies. Briefly, axial CT images with comparable cross-sectional areas of the superior and inferior articular processes were selected. The midpoint of the anterior and posterior ends of the superior and inferior articular process was defined as the facet line. A vertical line perpendicular to the posterior wall of the vertebral body was then drawn, and the angle between this line and the facet line was defined as the FA (Fig. 2). The absolute difference between bilateral FA values was defined as the facet joint angle difference (FAD) and a segment was classified as having FT when the FAD was ≥ 7° [9].

OPLL volume measurement

Cervical CT scans were performed preoperatively and within 7 days postoperatively, followed by CT examinations every 12 months. CT scans were obtained using axial slices with a thickness of 3 mm. Three-dimensional volumetric measurements of OPLL were performed using Philips Nebula software, covering the region from C2 to C7. The volume of OPLL posterior to each vertebral body and intervertebral disc was measured. The segmental ossification volume was defined as the region extending from the superior endplate of the upper vertebra to the inferior endplate of the lower vertebra (Fig. 3). OPLL volumes were measured preoperatively and at the final follow-up. The annual volume growth rate (AVGR) of segmental OPLL was calculated. AVGR was calculated as follows: [(“final follow-up volume”-“preoperative volume”)/“preoperative volume”]/ (“follow-up months”/12) ×100%. According to previously reported criteria for ossification progression, segmental ossification volume with an AVGR greater than 12.24% was defined as progression in the present study [10].

Measurement reliability

Imaging parameters were independently measured by one orthopedic surgeon and one radiologist to assess inter-observer reliability. The same two observers repeated the measurements after a 1-month interval to assess intra-observer reliability. Ten randomly selected patients were used to evaluate inter-observer and intra-observer reliability of OPLL volume measurements using the methods described above.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS version 25 and R software (version 4.3.1, The R Foundation for Statistical Computing). The following R packages were primarily used: lme4, glmmTMB, caret, and pROC. Descriptive analyses were conducted for demographic variables. The chi-square test was used to compare the distribution of ossified segments between the progression and non-progression groups. Spearman and Pearson correlation analyses were performed to explore the associations between segmental ossification-level factors, patient-level factors, and AVGR. Segmental ossification-level factors significantly associated with AVGR were entered into a multivariable logistic regression model, and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves were generated to calculate the area under the curve (AUC). Segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors significantly associated with AVGR were further incorporated into a GLMM to predict segmental ossification volume progression. A P value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Associated patient-level factors were entered as fixed effects with constant values for all segments of the same patient, while a patient-level random intercept accounted for the correlation among segments within each patient. This nested structure is appropriately handled by the GLMM.

Model construction

Model evaluation

Model performance was evaluated using AUC, sensitivity, specificity, accuracy, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). These metrics were used to comprehensively assess the predictive performance of the model and its potential clinical utility.

Combined model of segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors

Based on AVGR, segmental ossification was classified into progression and non-progression groups. Segmental-level factors included FT, FA, and SROM. Patient-level factors included age, body mass index (BMI), comorbidities (hypertension, diabetes mellitus), smoking status, alcohol consumption, operative time, intraoperative blood loss, and follow-up duration. The effects of segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors on the AVGR of segmental ossification were analyzed, and variables with P < 0.05 were included in the GLMM. In this model, the outcome variable was defined as segmental ossification volume progression status. Segmental ossification-level and patient-level variables were included as fixed effects, while a patient-level random intercept was included to account for inter-patient variability. Results

Patient characteristics

A total of 37 patients were included, with a mean age of 55.19 ± 10.53 years, including 25 males and 12 females. Fifteen patients (40.54%) had hypertension, seven (18.92%) had diabetes mellitus, five (13.51%) had a history of long-term alcohol consumption, and eleven (29.73%) were smokers. The mean follow-up duration was 23.11 ± 14.01 months (range, 12–118 months). The mean number of operated segments was 4.35 ± 0.59 (range, 2–6). A total of 172 segmental ossification segments were evaluated, including 29 at C2/3, 36 at C3/4, 37 at C4/5, 37 at C5/6, and 33 at C6/7, with a mean of 4.35 segments per patient. The mean intraoperative blood loss was 333.78 ± 547.35 mL, and the mean operative time was 144.86 ± 67.71 min. The mean preoperative OPLL volume was 1847.47 ± 1047.09 mm³, and the mean OPLL volume at final follow-up was 2269.69 ± 1274.77 mm³. The mean AVGR was 12.24% ± 9.74%.

Using an AVGR threshold of 12.24%, segments were classified into a progression group (AVGR > 12.24%) and a non-progression group (AVGR ≤ 12.24%). The progression group included 114 segments, and the non-progression group included 58 segments. There was no significant difference in the distribution of ossified segments between the two groups (Table 1). Inter-observer and intra-observer reliability results are shown in Table 2. The intra-observer intraclass correlation coefficients (ICCs) for SROM were 0.865 for the orthopedic surgeon and 0.881 for the radiologist, and the inter-observer ICC was 0.786, indicating good to excellent reliability.

Potential factors associated with OPLL progression

Univariable regression analyses were performed to evaluate associations between AVGR and potential risk factors at both segmental and patient levels (Table 3). At the segmental level, FT (Coefficient = 0.237, P = 0.002) and SROM (Coefficient = 0.369, P < 0.001) were positively correlated with AVGR, whereas FA was not significantly associated with AVGR. At the patient level, smoking (Coefficient = 0.136, P = 0.029), alcohol consumption (Coefficient = 0.171, P = 0.025), and operative time (Coefficient = 0.165, P = 0.031) were positively correlated with AVGR, while age (Coefficient = − 0.157, P = 0.040), follow-up duration (Coefficient = − 0.203, P = 0.007), and diabetes mellitus (Coefficient = − 0.247, P < 0.001) were negatively correlated with AVGR. No significant associations were observed with BMI, sex, hypertension, intraoperative blood loss, or the total number of operated segments.

Discussion

This study investigated the effects of segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors on segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL after LP. We found that the AVGR of segmental ossification volume was positively correlated with FT, SROM, smoking, alcohol consumption, and operative time, and negatively correlated with follow-up duration, age, and diabetes mellitus. Among these factors, FT and greater SROM were identified as risk factors for predicting segmental ossification volume progression.

Substantial evidence indicates that cervical C2–C7 ROM is a risk factor for OPLL overall ossification volume progression and is a classic indicator reflecting cervical spine biomechanics [2]. However, no previous studies have investigated whether SROM affects segmental ossification volume progression. Our results demonstrated a significant positive association between SROM and segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL (OR = 3.182, P < 0.001). This finding supports existing evidence regarding the relationship between cervical C2-C7 ROM and overall ossification volume progression and further highlights the importance of SROM in segmental ossification volume progression. Mechanical stress is a critical risk factor for OPLL, and increased SROM may elevate stress on the ligament at the corresponding segment, thereby accelerating ossification volume progression [11].

At the molecular level, prior experimental studies have elucidated how mechanical loading drives OPLL progression. Cyclic stretch upregulates osteogenic markers (ALP, osteopontin) and induces BMP-2/BMP-4 expression in OPLL-derived ligament cells via stretch-activated Ca²⁺ channels, with BMP proteins secreted into the extracellular environment to act in an autocrine/paracrine manner [12]. Mechanical strain also upregulates connexin 43, a gap-junction protein essential for osteogenic signal transmission, and siRNA-mediated Cx43 knockdown abolishes strain-induced osteocalcin and type I collagen expression [13]. In parallel, cyclic stretch activates the prostaglandin I₂/cAMP pathway and triggers extracellular ATP release that signals through P2Y1 purinoceptors, further promoting osteoblastic differentiation [14]. More broadly, BMP signaling is recognized as a mechanosensitive cascade modulated by tension, compression, and substrate stiffness. Collectively, these findings suggest that segments with increased SROM and FT are subjected to repetitive aberrant mechanical stimuli that converge on multiple osteogenic pathways, thereby accelerating segmental ossification progression.

FT was identified as another important factor influencing segmental ossification volume progression, with a significant positive association between FT and progression (OR = 2.169, P = 0.012). FT is a parameter used to assess asymmetry between bilateral facet joints, and a segment is considered to exhibit FT when the difference in FA exceeds 7° [15]. The facet joints and intervertebral disc together form a three-joint complex that plays a crucial role in maintaining spinal stability [16]. Rong et al. reported that compared with symmetric facets, severe FT increases intervertebral disc pressure by 49.02% during cervical flexion, thereby accelerating disc degeneration [17]. Weng et al. further demonstrated that FT leads to uneven stress distribution in the annulus fibrosus [18]. Although previous studies have established close associations between FT and spinal stability, degenerative spinal disorders, its relationship with OPLL volume progression has not been fully elucidated. The present study is the first to establish a clear association between FT and segmental ossification volume progression in OPLL, suggesting that FT, may alter spinal load distribution, induce asymmetric loading, and contribute to spinal instability, thereby accelerating ossification volume progression.

In this study, segmentation of overall OPLL volume into segmental ossification volume was inspired by the functional spinal unit (FSU) concept [19]. The FSU, composed of two adjacent vertebrae, represents the basic biomechanical unit of the spine and reflects subtle biomechanical changes. The facet joints and intervertebral disc form a three-joint complex that bears axial load and maintains spinal stability, and dysfunction of any component can result in instability [16]. These biomechanical principles are consistent with our findings, as GLMM results demonstrated the critical roles of SROM (OR = 3.182, P < 0.001) and FT (OR = 2.169, P = 0.012) in segmental ossification volume progression.

Interestingly, diabetes mellitus was significantly negatively correlated with AVGR in this study (Coefficient = − 0.247, P < 0.001), while previous studies have reported a higher incidence of OPLL in patients with diabetes mellitus, potentially related to impaired insulin secretion responses stimulating osteoprogenitor cells [20, 21]. However, ossification volume progression was inhibited in diabetic patients in our cohort, which may be associated with regular metformin use. Metformin has emerged as a promising agent for inhibiting heterotopic ossification by activating the AMPK pathway and suppressing NF-κB signaling [22, 23]. Future studies are warranted to further clarify the role of metformin in OPLL volume progression. Of note, while diabetes mellitus exhibited a significant negative univariate association with AVGR (coefficient = − 0.247, P < 0.001), this association was no longer significant in the fully adjusted GLMM (OR = 0.369, P = 0.330), likely reflecting the adjustment for within-patient clustering and the limited number of patients with diabetes (n = 7).

By integrating segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors, we constructed a GLMM that demonstrated superior predictive performance compared with a multivariable logistic regression model based solely on segmental ossification-level factors (AUC = 0.749). The GLMM achieved a higher predictive accuracy (AUC = 0.933) and provides a robust statistical framework for future studies.

Most prior studies on OPLL progression after laminoplasty have examined patient-level risk factors, such as younger age, continuous or mixed OPLL type, and greater preoperative ossification volume, to predict global disease progression [2, 5]. Only one study has investigated progression at the individual motion segment level: Lee et al. reported that segmental ROM > 5° is risk factor for OPLL thickness progression following laminoplasty [24]. The present study extends this work by using three-dimensional volumetric measurement rather than single-plane thickness, by incorporating facet tropism as a novel segment-specific biomechanical parameter, and by employing a GLMM that appropriately accounts for within-patient clustering of segments.

The findings of this study have important implications for surgical decision-making in OPLL. This model enables more precise prediction of postoperative ossification volume progression across spinal segments and supports individualized treatment strategies. In clinical practice, for patients with multiple segments exhibiting FT and high SROM—both of which are established risk factors for ossification progression in this study—laminectomy and fusion (LF) may be the preferred option, as it eliminates the dynamic mechanical stimulus that drives progression. Conversely, for patients with fewer or no such high-risk segments, LP remains an appropriate choice to preserve cervical motion. Patient-level risk factors, such as smoking and alcohol consumption, should also be addressed in perioperative management. Modification of these factors may reduce the risk of postoperative progression. Individualized treatment strategies allow surgeons to tailor surgical approaches and rehabilitation protocols, thereby improving clinical outcomes. Furthermore, the role of metformin in OPLL progression warrants further investigation.

Several limitations should be acknowledged. First, this was a single-center retrospective study with a relatively small sample size. Multicenter, large-scale prospective studies are needed to validate our findings. Second, manual delineation of OPLL may introduce measurement bias; automated delineation models are currently under development. Third, biomechanical studies are required to further elucidate the mechanistic roles of FT and SROM in OPLL volume progression. Finally, although our GLMM achieved a high apparent AUC of 0.933 with supportive internal cross-validation, the risk of overfitting cannot be entirely excluded given the relatively small single-center sample. External validation in an independent, larger cohort is necessary to confirm the model’s generalizability.

Conclusion

This study is the first to investigate risk factors for ossification progression using segmental ossification volume in OPLL as the primary outcome. SROM and FT were identified as key risk factors for segmental ossification volume progression. By applying a GLMM that integrates segmental ossification-level and patient-level factors, we developed a predictive model that provides a novel framework for individualized management of patients with OPLL. Future studies are needed to further validate these findings and to support more precise therapeutic strategies.
