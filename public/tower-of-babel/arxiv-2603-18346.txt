Large friction limit of the almost pressureless Euler-Poisson system
Xin Liu

Abstract
The goal of this work is to investigate the almost pressureless Euler-Poisson (EP) system with repulsive force in the large friction limit. The leading order equations in the limit are shown to be the hyperbolic-elliptic Keller-Segel (KS) system of consumption type. Under suitable assumptions on the initial data, we establish the unique global-in-time solutions to both the EP system and the KS system by establishing the global stability in the large friction limit. In particular, no singularity forms in the asymptotic limit. Moreover, the time asymptotic behavior of the one-dimensional KS flow with vacuum is also discussed. Keywords: Large friction limit, Euler-Poisson system, Keller-Segel system, Global stability. MSC2020: 35D35; 76N10; 76S99; 76X05.

Background and literatures

The Euler-Poisson system

The EP system with repulsive force consists of the conservation of mass (

To obtain a global smooth solution, it is then necessary to consider initial data with certain restriction/smallness. By taking advantage of the dispersive effect of the associated Klein-Gordon effect, the authors in

This paper establishes a new kind of global smooth solutions to the repulsive EP system in the almost pressureless and large friction regime. Only the smallness of the initial velocity

Methodology and remarks

Instead of using the relative entropy method as in
[
30
]
, where it is required that the solution to the limit KS system is more regular than that to the primitive EP system, we use a direct singular limit method. To be more precise, we introduce an intermediate system of system (
1.3
), where the feature/structure of the KS system is explicit; see (
1.9
), below. At the same time, the singular structure of the system will be separated from the limit system as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
and can be controlled in the singular limit regime. The benefit of this method is that the regularity of the solutions to the singular EP system and the limit KS system are exactly the same. It is then clear that, in this case, no shock or any singularity will form in the EP system as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
.

Our initial data is required to be somewhat prepared to have naturally bounded energy in the singular limit regime; see section
2.2
. However, this is different from the well-prepared initial data as in the study of the small Mach number limit
[
51
]
. As indicated below by the spectrum analysis in section
1.5
, our initial data is “large” in term of order. Moreover, the time derivative is in general not uniformly bounded with respect to
\varepsilon
; see (
3.2
), below. This is due to the strong initial layer as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
, when the damping effect damps out the error between the solutions of the EP system and the KS system within a very small initial time interval. In particular, we do not require the initial energy to be small; see (
1.10
), below. Therefore we cannot obtain a convergence rate estimate without better prepared data.

Our result holds also for the attracting EP system and the KS system of production type, for non-vacuum data and for finite time. Namely, the solutions to the attracting EP system will converge to the solution of the KS system of production type as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
in the same setting but the convergence holds only for finite time. This is the best result one can obtain for the high friction limit since both the attracting EP system and the KS system of production type are expected to blow up in finite time with general initial data.

Lastly, we remark that the non-vacuum condition, i.e., (
1.11
), below, is essential for our result. In fact, when vacuum appears, it has been shown in
[
48
]
that the local well-posedness theory for the KS system is nontrivial and does not fit into the current framework. This can be also seen from the one-dimensional flow as discussed in section
4
. Indeed, any initial vacuum interval will shrink to a vacuum point as
t\rightarrow\infty
in the KS system of consumption type, while the solution approaches the global equilibrium
\sigma_{e,2}=M
almost everywhere. Therefore, the (non-weighted)
H^{s}
-norm,
s\geq 1
, of the solution will grow to infinity as
t\rightarrow\infty
. Such a behavior of the solution does not fit into our framework for the large friction limit and is left as a future work.

\displaystyle\lambda M{\bf U}+\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}M^{\gamma-1}\nabla\zeta+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2}}M{\bf U}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon}M\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\zeta

\displaystyle=0.

(1.23b)

Then one can derive from system (
1.23
) that

-(\varepsilon^{2}\lambda^{2}+\lambda+M)\zeta=\gamma M^{\gamma-1}\varepsilon^{\alpha}(-\Delta)\zeta,

(1.24)

and

{\bf U}=-\frac{1}{(1+\varepsilon^{2}\lambda)M}\bigl[\varepsilon M\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}\zeta+\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}\gamma M^{-\gamma-1}\nabla\zeta\bigr].

(1.25)

Then one can see from (
1.24
) and (
1.25
) that in the asymptotic regime
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0
,
\lambda<0
and therefore the equilibrium is linearly stable. Moreover, for any fixed
\lambda
, one has that

\zeta=\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\bf U}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}).

(1.26)

Consequently, it is reasonable to assume

\delta\rho=\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta{\bf u}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha+1}).

(1.27)

However, our assumption of
\rho_{0}
and
{\bf w}_{0}
in (
1.10
) and the ansatz (
1.8
) implies that

\rho|_{\tau=0}=\mathcal{O}(1)\qquad\text{and}\qquad{\bf u}|_{\tau=0}=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)+\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon^{\alpha/2}),

(1.28)

which is weaker than (
1.27
).

The goal of this section to derive uniform-in-
\varepsilon
estimates for the solution of system (
1.9
), in particular, the uniform bounds for the density
\rho

(2.1)

We will verify this assumption in section

. Moreover, all calculation below will be done in

, while the calculation in

will be similar with minimal modification and thus is omitted.

2.1
The Keller-Segel map

Thanks to (
1.6
), one has that
-\Delta{\bf v}_{\rho}=-\nabla(\rho-M)
. Hence, the elliptic regularity theory yields that
for any
s
and
1<p<\infty
, we have

\displaystyle\|{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{W^{s+2,p}}\leq C_{s}\|\nabla(\rho-M)\|_{W^{s,p}},

(2.2)

and, in particular

\displaystyle\|\nabla{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\lesssim\|{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{W^{2,4}}\lesssim\|\nabla(\rho-M)\|_{L^{4}}\lesssim\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}.

(2.3)

2.2
The
L^{2}
-estimate

Taking the
L^{2}
-inner product of (
1.9b
) with
{\bf w}
leads to the following:

\begin{gathered}\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\biggl\{\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{2}\int\rho|{\bf w}|^{2}\ d{\bf x}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int\bigl(\rho^{\gamma}-M^{\gamma}-\gamma M^{\gamma-1}(\rho-M)\bigr)\ d{\bf x}\biggr\}+\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}}\int\rho|{\bf w}|^{2}\ d{\bf x}\\
=\int\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}-\varepsilon\int\rho\partial_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\rho}\cdot{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}-\int\rho{\bf u}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}_{\rho}\cdot{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}=:\sum_{j=1}^{3}I_{j},\end{gathered}

(2.4)

where we have used (
1.9a
) to calculate

\begin{gathered}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\int\nabla p(\rho)\cdot{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}=\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\int\nabla\rho^{\gamma-1}\cdot\rho{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}=\frac{\gamma}{\gamma-1}\int\rho^{\gamma-1}(\partial_{\tau}\rho+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho{\bf v}_{\rho}))\ d{\bf x}\\
=\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int\partial_{\tau}\rho^{\gamma}\ d{\bf x}+\int\rho^{\gamma}\mathrm{div}\,{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}=\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int\bigl(\rho^{\gamma}-M^{\gamma}-\gamma M^{\gamma-1}(\rho-M)\bigr)\ d{\bf x}\\
-\int\nabla\rho^{\gamma}\cdot{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}.\end{gathered}

(2.5)

Next, we estimate the terms
I_{j}
for
j=1,2,3
. Thanks to (
1.6
) and (
1.8
), we have

\begin{gathered}|I_{3}|=|-\int\rho(\varepsilon{\bf v}_{\rho}+\varepsilon^{\alpha}{\bf w})\nabla{\bf v}_{\rho}\cdot{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}|\lesssim\varepsilon\overline{\rho}^{1/2}\|{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}\\
+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|\nabla{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\lesssim\varepsilon\overline{\rho}^{1/2}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}\\
+\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}\|\varepsilon^{\alpha/2-1}\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}\overline{\rho}\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\end{gathered}

(2.6)

where we have used (
2.2
) and (
2.3
) and applied Cauchy-Schwartz inequality.

To estimate
I_{2}
, we notice that, thanks to (
1.6
) and (
1.9a
), we have

\partial_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\rho}=\nabla(-\Delta)^{-1}(\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\mathrm{div}\,(\rho{\bf w})+\mathrm{div}\,(\rho{\bf v}_{\rho})),

and hence,

\|\partial_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\overline{\rho}^{1/2}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}+\overline{\rho}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}.

(2.8)

Therefore, we have

\begin{gathered}|I_{2}|\lesssim\varepsilon\overline{\rho}^{1/2}\|\partial_{\tau}{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}\lesssim\varepsilon^{2}\overline{\rho}\|\varepsilon^{\alpha/2-1}\rho^{1/2}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\alpha}\overline{\rho}^{2}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{gathered}

(2.9)

To estimate
I_{1}
, we apply integration by parts as follows:

\begin{gathered}I_{1}+\gamma(\underline{\rho}/2)^{\gamma-1}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq I_{1}+\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-1}(\rho-M)^{2}\ d{\bf x}\\
=\int\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}\nabla(\rho-M)\cdot{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}+\gamma\int\rho^{\gamma-1}(\rho-M)^{2}\ d{\bf x}=-\int\gamma(\rho-M)\nabla\rho^{\gamma-1}\cdot{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}\\
=-\gamma(\gamma-1)\int\rho^{\gamma-2}(\rho-M)\nabla(\rho-M)\cdot{\bf v}_{\rho}\ d{\bf x}\\
\lesssim_{\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla(\rho-M)\|_{H^{-1}}\|{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{H^{1}}\\
\lesssim\|\rho-M\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.\end{gathered}

(2.10)

To conclude this section, let
\mathcal{E}_{0}
be the energy functional defined by

\mathcal{E}_{0}(t):=\frac{\varepsilon^{\alpha}}{2}\int\rho|{\bf w}|^{2}\ d{\bf x}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\int[\rho^{\gamma}-M^{\gamma}-\gamma M^{\gamma-1}(\rho-M)]\ d{\bf x}.

(2.11)

Thanks to (
2.1
), one has that

\mathcal{E}_{0}{\gtrsim}_{\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}}\varepsilon^{\alpha}\|{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(2.12)

Meanwhile, let
\mathcal{D}_{0}
be the dispersion functional defined by

\mathcal{D}_{0}(t):=\varepsilon^{\alpha-2}\|{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho-M\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\gtrsim_{\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}}\mathcal{E}_{0}.

(2.13)

Then from (
2.4
)–(
2.10

\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\mathcal{E}_{0}(t)+\mathcal{D}_{0}(t)\lesssim_{\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}}(\varepsilon^{2}+\varepsilon^{2-\alpha})(\|\rho-M\|_{H^{2}}+1)\mathcal{D}_{0}(t)+\|\rho-M\|_{L^{\infty}}\mathcal{E}_{0}(t).

(2.14)

(2.16)

where we have used (

\begin{gathered}\int\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\rho^{\gamma-1}\nabla\partial^{3}\rho\cdot\partial^{3}{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}=-\int\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\partial^{3}\rho\nabla\rho^{\gamma-1}\cdot\partial^{3}{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}-\int\frac{\gamma}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\rho^{\gamma-1}\partial^{3}\rho\mathrm{div}\,\partial^{3}{\bf w}\ d{\bf x}\\

(2.19)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} |I_{7}|\lesssim_{\overline{\rho}}&\varepsilon\|\partial_{\tau}\partial^{3}{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{L^{2}}\|\partial^{3}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{def:dtau-v-rho}}}{{\lesssim}}\varepsilon(\varepsilon^{\alpha-1}\|\rho{\bf w}\|_{H^{3}}+\|\rho{\bf v}_{\rho}\|_{H^{3}})\|\partial^{3}{\bf w}\|_{L^{2}}\\

2.5
Decay estimate of

Let

From (

(2.39)

Direct calculation yields that, for any

,

\begin{gathered}\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{p}}+\underline{\rho}\|\rho-M\|_{L^{p}}\lesssim_{\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}}\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\|\mathrm{div}\,{\bf w}\|_{L^{p}}+\frac{1}{p}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\|\mathrm{div}\,{\bf w}\|_{L^{\infty}}+1)\|\rho-M\|_{L^{p}}\\
\lesssim\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\|{\bf w}\|_{H^{3}}+\frac{1}{p}(\frac{1}{\varepsilon^{1-\alpha}}\|{\bf w}\|_{H^{3}}+1)\|\rho-M\|_{L^{p}}.\end{gathered}

(2.40)

Sending

in (

Then one can write down from (
4.9
) that

\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\xi(x,\tau)=-(2\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)-M)\xi(x,\tau).

(4.20)

Thanks to (
4.11
), one can conclude from (
4.20
) that

\lim_{\tau\rightarrow\infty}\xi(x,\tau)=0.

(4.21)

Therefore,

\partial_{x}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=\frac{1}{\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau)}\dfrac{d}{dx}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=\frac{1}{\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau)}\xi(x,\tau)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\tau\rightarrow\infty}}{{\rightarrow}}0.

(4.22)

Now we discuss the evolution
along the vacuum trajectory
, i.e., for fixed
x
such that
\sigma_{0}(x)=0
. Indeed, (
4.16
) is then reduced to

\partial_{\tau}\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau)=-M\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau),\qquad\partial_{x}\eta(x,0)=1,

(4.23)

which implies that

\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau)=e^{-M\tau}.

(4.24)

Meanwhile, (
4.20
) is reduced to

\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\xi(x,\tau)=M\xi(x,\tau).

(4.25)

Therefore, one has that

\xi(x,\tau)=e^{M\tau}\xi(x,0)=e^{M\tau}\partial_{x}\sigma_{0}(x).

(4.26)

Consequently, one has that, similar to (
4.22
),

\partial_{x}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=\frac{\xi(x,\tau)}{\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau)}=e^{2M\tau}\partial_{x}\sigma_{0}(x).

(4.27)

This implies that the
\|\sigma(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}
is growing exponentially in time thanks to the embedding inequality
\|\sigma(\tau)\|_{H^{2}}\gtrsim\|\partial_{x}\sigma(\tau)\|_{L^{\infty}}\simeq e^{2M\tau}
, provided there exists some point such that
\partial_{x}\sigma_{0}(x)\neq 0
and
\sigma_{0}(x)=0
(
x=0
or
1
for the initial data in (
4.3
) for instance).

This, in particular, implies that one
should not expect global uniform-in-
\tau
Sobolev regularity of solutions to the limit system (
4.2
) in the case when there exists vacuum initially
. In other words, condition (
1.11
) is necessary for theorem
1.3
and theorem
1.5
.

High order derivatives of
\sigma
along the flow trajectory

In general, assume that the first nontrivial derivative of
\sigma_{0}(x),\ x=0,1
is the
k
-th derivative, for some
k\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, i.e.,

\begin{gathered}\partial_{x}^{j}\sigma_{0}(x)=0,\quad\forall\ j\leq k-1,\\
\partial_{x}^{k}\sigma_{0}(x)\neq 0.\end{gathered}

(4.28)

Such
k
always exists for nontrivial data.
Then from (
4.9
), one has that

\begin{gathered}\dfrac{d}{d\tau}{\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau)=A({\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau)){\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau),\\
\text{with}\qquad{\bf V}_{k}(x,0)=0,\end{gathered}

(4.29)

where
{\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau):=(\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau),\dfrac{d}{dx}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau),\cdots,\dfrac{d^{k-1}}{dx^{k-1}}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau))^{\top}
is the tube of derivatives with orders smaller than
k
, and
A({\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau))
is a non-singular matrix. Then one can derive that,

{\bf V}_{k}(x,\tau)\equiv 0.

(4.30)

That is, after applying the chain rule,

0\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ene:925}}}{{=}}\dfrac{d^{j}}{dx^{j}}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=\partial_{x}^{j}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)(\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau))^{j}+\mathrm{l.o.t},\quad\forall\ j\leq k-1.

(4.31)

Here,
\mathrm{l.o.t}
denotes the terms involving only derivatives with orders smaller than
j
.
Thanks to (
4.24
), one can derive from (
4.31
) by induction that

\partial_{x}^{j}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)\equiv 0,\qquad\forall j\leq k-1.

(4.32)

Meanwhile, for the
k
-th derivative, thanks to (
4.31
), one can calculate from (
4.9
) that

\dfrac{d}{d\tau}\dfrac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=M\dfrac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau).

(4.33)

Consequently,

e^{M\tau}\partial_{x}^{k}\sigma_{0}(x)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{ene:927}}}{{=}}\dfrac{d^{k}}{dx^{k}}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=\partial_{x}^{k}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)(\partial_{x}\eta(x,\tau))^{k}+\mathrm{l.o.t}.

(4.34)

Here,
\mathrm{l.o.t}
denotes the terms involving only derivatives with orders smaller than
k
. Thanks to (
4.24
) and (
4.32
), one can conclude from (
4.34
) that

\partial_{x}^{k}\sigma(\eta(x,\tau),\tau)=e^{(k+1)M\tau}\partial_{x}^{k}\sigma_{0}(x).

(4.35)
