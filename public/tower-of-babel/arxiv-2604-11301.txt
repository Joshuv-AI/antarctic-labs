Branched covers of ℙ 1 {\mathbb{P}}^{1} and divisibility in class group
Kalyan Banerjee, Kalyan Chakraborty and Azizul Hoque

Abstract
. We start with n n -torsions in the Jacobian of an m m -gonal curve and produce n n -torsions in the class group of certain number field K K .

1.
Introduction

One of the classical problem in algebraic number theory is to understand the structure of the class group of a given number field. It is known that the class group is finite for all number fields. The question is how to find an element of say order
n
in the class group of the given number field. One of the approaches is to use algebro-geometric methods to find out such elements. This idea of using algebraic geometry to find elements of large order was first introduced in
[
1
]
by Agboola and Pappus. Later, Gillibert and Levin
[
5
]
approached the problem by pulling back torsion line bundles to the class group. In
[
6
]
, Gillibert has shown how to pull back torsion elements on a hyperelliptic curve to the class group of quadratic number fields.

Our approach to this problem is to start with a branched cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}
over
{\mathbb{Q}}
and spread it over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Then we get a fibration say
{\mathscr{C}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where
{\mathscr{C}}
is a fixed spread of
C
over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Suppose we take a torsion element in the Jacobian of
C
defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
, and spread this torsion divisor on
{\mathscr{C}}
and consider restriction of this divisor on the fiber at a point
P\in{\mathbb{Z}}
, such that the fiber is smooth (that is we consider the good primes). Let us call it
{\mathscr{C}}_{P}
. The spectrum of the ring of integers
{\mathscr{O}}
in the fiber
C_{P}
corresponds to an affine open subset of
{\mathscr{C}}
. We argue that this element in the corresponding class group of
{\mathscr{O}}
is a nontrivial torsion element.

To accomplish this, we start with the theory of Chow schemes and Hilbert schemes for arithmetic varieties which parametrize cycles on an arithmetic variety, and then use Étale monodromy of a fibration to conclude that the torsion element mentioned above vary in a family. Precisely, we prove the following result.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let the Tate module
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
be non-trivial, where
\bar{\eta}
is the spectrum of a separably closed field (e.g. in case of
\bar{{\mathbb{Q}}}
the assumption implies
H^{1}(C_{\bar{\mathbb{Q}}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is
\neq 0
). Then there exist infinitely number fields
L
whose class group contain
l^{i}
torsions for some
i
and for some prime
l
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Let
C
be a smooth projective curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
with a
m:1
regular map to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. This map is branched and there exists no non-trivial étale cover of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. We consider an integral model of
C
and
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Then we have a morphism of
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
-schemes
{\mathscr{C}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, where
{\mathscr{C}}
is the chosen integral model of
C
over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
. Then for a general scheme-theoretic point
{P}
on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, the normalization of the fiber over it, is the ring of integers of the field
f^{-1}(P)
, where
f
is from
C\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
and
P
is considered as a general scheme theoretic point on
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Let us consider a simple example. Suppose that
C
is a smooth projective curve defined over
{\mathbb{Q}}
embedded into
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
, whose equation is given by

y^{m}=f(x)=a_{0}x^{n}+\cdots+a_{n}

where all
a_{i}
’s are rational numbers. Then we write

a_{i}=m_{i}/n_{i}

and put in the above equation. Then it becomes

y^{m}=\sum_{i}m_{i}/n_{i}x^{n-i}

Clearing out the denominator, we have

ny^{m}=\sum_{i}m_{i}^{\prime}x^{n-i}\;.

Further we consider a general point
p
on
{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}\subset{\mathbb{A}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Q}}}
. Putting
x=p
for a general prime in the above equation we have

ny^{m}=\sum_{i}m_{i}^{\prime}p^{n-i}

and we have an extension of
{\mathbb{Z}}
given by

{\mathbb{Z}}[y]/ny^{m}-\sum_{i}m_{i}^{\prime}p^{n-i}\;.

The integral closure of the above is the ring of integers of

K={\mathbb{Q}}[\sqrt[m]{f(t)}]\;.

This
p
is chosen such that the above ring is a Dedekind domain and it’s spectrum inherits a smooth scheme structure.

Now we consider some element in the Chow group of codimension one cycles on
C
of degree zero (denoted by
A^{1}(C)
), that is isomorphic to the Jacobian variety or the Picard Variety of
C
. Let
\alpha
be the element which is a non-trivial
n
-torsion on
A^{1}(C)
which is naturally identified with
J(C)
, the Jacobian of
C
.

Next we consider a fixed spread of the cycle
\alpha
over
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
denoted by
\tilde{\alpha}
and consider the fiber of the spread at some general scheme theoretic point
P
, that is
\tilde{\alpha}_{P}
. This is a torsion element in the Chow group of the smooth arithmetic variety
{\mathscr{C}}_{P}
, which contains
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
as a Zariski open set. Restricting
\tilde{\alpha}_{P}
to
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
gives rise to an element in the class group of
{\mathscr{O}}_{K}
.
Let
U\subset{\rm{Spec}}{{\mathbb{Z}}}
be the set of all primes at which the fibers are smooth.
Now consider the following subset

B=\{P\in U:\tilde{\alpha}_{P}=0\}\subset U\;,

and prove that:

Theorem 2.1
.

The set

{\mathscr{Z}}_{d}:=\{(z,b)\in C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U)|Supp(z)\subset{{\mathscr{C}}_{b}},[z]=0\in{\rm{CH}}^{1}({{\mathscr{C}}_{b}})\}

is a countable union of Zariski closed subsets in the Chow variety
C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U)
parametrizing the pairs of degree
d
subvarieties of the arithmetic variety
{\mathscr{C}}_{U}
.

Proof.

This theorem in the case of a surface fibered over
{\mathbb{P}}^{2}
was proved in
[
2
]
, but we reconstruct the proof for the curve case for the convenience and completeness.
There are some crucial points to be noted here:

(I) The notion of Hilbert scheme and the Hom-scheme make sense for an arithmetic variety. This is as explained in
[
3
, Chapter: Hilbert schemes and Quot schemes, §5]
.

(II) The family of Weil divisors of a smooth fibration over
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathbb{Z}})
are parametrized by a Chow variety which is actually given by the Picard scheme parametrizing relative Cartier divisors of the same family
[
9
, Corollary 11.8]
. In our case the family is
{\mathscr{C}}_{U}
which is of finite presentation over
{\mathbb{Z}}
and it is a standard smooth algebra
1
1

1

in the sense, stack exchange
[
10
]
, Definitions 10.136.6 and 29.32.1.
over
{\mathbb{Z}}
. This enables us to formulate the definition of rational equivalence for arithmetic varieties as in
[
4
, §3.3]
in the following way:

Two Weil divisors
D_{1},D_{2}
are rationally equivalent on a fiber
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
, if there exists a morphism

f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\to C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U})

such that

(f\circ 0)|_{b}=D_{1}+B
and
(f\circ\infty)|_{b}=D_{2}+B
,
where
B
is a positive Weil divisor and
0,\infty
are two fixed sections from
U
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}
.

Let us assume that the divisor
D_{b}=D_{b}^{+}-D_{b}^{-}
is rationally equivalent to zero. This means that there exists a map

f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}\to C^{1}_{d,d}({{\mathscr{C}}_{b}})

such that

f(0)=D_{b}^{+}+\gamma\text{ and }f(\infty)=D_{b}^{-}+\gamma,

where
\gamma
is a positive divisor on
{{\mathscr{C}}_{b}}
.
In other words, we have the following map:

{\it{ev}}:Hom^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U))\to C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U)\times C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U)

given by
f\mapsto(f(0),f(\infty))
and that the fiber of
f
at
b
is contained in
C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
.

Let us denote
C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}_{U}/U)
by
C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}})
for simplicity.
We now consider the subscheme
U_{v,d}({\mathscr{C}})
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}))
consisting of the pairs
(b,f)
such that image of
f
is contained in
C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
(such a universal family exists, for example see
[
7
, Theorem 1.4]
or
[
3
]
, [Chapter on Hilbert schemes and Quot schemes]. This gives a morphism from
U_{v,d}({\mathscr{C}})
to

{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})

defined by

(b,f)\mapsto(b,f(0),f(\infty)).

Again, we consider the closed subscheme
{\mathscr{V}}_{d,d}
of
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}})
given by
(b,z_{1},z_{2})
, where
(z_{1},z_{2})\in C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
. Suppose that the map from
{\mathscr{V}}_{d,u,d,u}
to
{\mathscr{V}}_{d+u,u,d+u,u}
is given by

(A,C,B,D)\mapsto(A+C,C,B+D,D).

Let us denote the fiber product by
{\mathscr{V}}
of
U_{v,d}({\mathscr{C}})
and
{\mathscr{V}}_{d,u,d,u}
over
{\mathscr{V}}_{d+u,u,d+u,u}
. If we consider the projection from
{\mathscr{V}}
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}})
, then we observe that
A
and
B
are supported as well as rationally equivalent on
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
. Conversely, if
A
and
B
are supported as well as rationally equivalent on
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
, then we get the map

f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\to C^{1}_{d+u,u,d+u,u}({\mathscr{C}})

of some degree
v
satisfying

f(0)=(A+C,C)\text{ and }f(\infty)=(B+D,D),

where
C
and
D
are supported on
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
. This implies that the image of the projection from
{\mathscr{V}}
to
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}})
is a quasi-projective subscheme
W_{d}^{u,v}
consisting of the tuples
(b,A,B)
such that
A
and
B
are supported on
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
, and that there exists a map

f:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\to C^{1}_{d+u,u}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})

such that

f(0)=(A+C,C)

and

f(\infty)=(B+D,D)\;.

Here
f
is of degree
v
, and
C,D
are supported on
{\mathscr{C}}_{b}
and they are of co-dimension
1
and degree
u
cycles. This shows that
W_{d}
is the union
\cup_{u,v}W_{d}^{u,v}
. We now prove that the Zariski closure of
W_{d}^{u,v}
is in
W_{d}
for each

and
v
. For this, we prove the following:

W_{d}^{u,v}=pr_{1,2}(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(W^{0,v}_{d+u}\times W^{0,v}_{u})),

where

\widetilde{s}:{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d,u,u}({\mathscr{C}})\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d+u,d+u,u,u}({\mathscr{C}})

defined by

\widetilde{s}(b,A,B,C,D)=(b,A+C,B+D,C,D).

We assume
(b,A,B,C,D)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times C^{1}_{d,d,u,u}({\mathscr{C}})
in such a way that
\widetilde{s}(b,A,B,C,D)\in W^{0,v}_{d+u}\times W^{0,v}_{u}
. This implies that there exists an element

(b,g)\in{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{p}_{d+u}({\mathscr{C}}))

and an element

(b,h)\in{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{p}_{u}({\mathscr{C}}))

satisfying

g(0)=A+C,~g(\infty)=B+D\text{ and }h(0)=C,h(\infty)=D

and

C^{1}_{d+u}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
and

Also if
f=g\times h
then
f\in{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{p}_{d+u,u}({\mathscr{C}}))
such that the image of

C^{1}_{d+u,u}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
as well as it satisfies the following:

f(0)=(A+C,C)\text{ and }(f(\infty))=(B+D,D).

This shows that
(b,A,B)\in W^{d}_{u,v}

On the other hand, if we assume that
(b,A,B)\in W^{d}_{u,v}
, then there exists
f\in{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{1}_{d+u,u}({\mathscr{C}}))
such that

f(0)=(A+C,C)\text{ and }f(\infty)=(B+D,D),

and image of
f
is contained in the Chow scheme of
\bar{{\mathscr{C}}_{b}}
.

We now compose
f
with the projections to
C^{1}_{d+u}({\mathscr{C}}_{b})
and to

to get a map
g\in{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{1}_{d+u}({\mathscr{C}}))
and a map

satisfying

g(0)=A+C,\quad g(\infty)=B+D

and

h(0)=C,\quad h(\infty)=D.

Also, the image of
g
and

are contained in the respective Chow varities of the fibers

W_{d}=pr_{1,2}(\widetilde{s}^{-1}(W_{d+u}\times W_{u})).

We are now in a position to prove that the closure of
W_{d}^{0,v}

. Let
(b,A,B)

whose closure contains

is an affine neighborhood of

is non-empty. Then there is an irreducible curve

(b,A,B)

be the Zariski closure of

e:U_{v,d}({\mathscr{C}})\subset{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U}\times{\rm Hom}^{v}({\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{U},C^{1}_{d}({\mathscr{C}}))\to C^{1}_{d,d}({\mathscr{C}})

is regular and

In this section, we prove Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let us consider the fibration
{\mathscr{C}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
. Restricting this fibration to some Zariski open set
U
in
{\mathbb{P}}^{1}_{{\mathbb{Z}}}
, we have an étale morphism from
f_{U}:{\mathscr{C}}_{U}\to U
. Now consider the higher direct image sheaf
R^{1}f_{U*}{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}
denotes the constant sheaf on
U
and
f_{U*}
denote the pushforward at the level of sheaves. Then it is well known that this sheaf

R^{1}f_{U*}{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{n}{\mathbb{Z}}

is a locally constant, constructible sheaf. Also its fiber over the geometric point
\bar{\eta}
of
U
is isomorphic to the étale cohomology

H^{1}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}},{\mathbb{Z}}/l^{n}{\mathbb{Z}})

and that, this cohomology group is a
\pi_{1}(U,\bar{\eta})
module, where
\pi_{1}
stands for the étale fundamental group of
U
. Therefore taking the inverse limit with varying
n
, we have an action of
\pi_{1}(U,\bar{\eta})
on
H^{1}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}},{\mathbb{Z}}_{l})
, where
{\mathbb{Z}}_{l}
denote the group of
l
-adic integers. Taking the tensor product with
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
, we have
H^{1}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}},{\mathbb{Q}}_{l})
is a
\pi_{1}(U,\bar{\eta})
module, which is dual to the Tate module
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
.

Now suppose
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
is a non-zero
\pi_{1}
module. Therefore for
\bar{\eta}
, there exists some
l^{i}
torsion on
J({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
(after having a finite extension of
{\mathbb{Q}}
). Since
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
is a non-zero
\pi_{1}
-module it corresponds to a locally constant sheaf in some Zariski open neighborhood
U^{\prime}
of
\bar{\eta}
. Considering any other geometric point
\bar{\eta}^{\prime}
in
U^{\prime}
, the Tate module
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}})
is also of the same rank as
T_{l}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
as a
{\mathbb{Q}}_{l}
module.

Now
{\rm{CH}}^{1}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}})
is the colimit of
{\rm{CH}}^{1}(V)
, for
V
Zariski open in
{\mathscr{C}}_{U^{\prime}}
. Therefore there is a Zariski open set
V
in
{\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}}
, such that there exists
l^{i}
-torsion in
{\rm{CH}}^{1}(V)
. Now given a
P
in
V
, over which we have
\bar{\eta}^{\prime}
, the
l^{i}
torsion restricted to
{\rm{CH}}^{1}(V_{P})
is non-zero, because if it is zero then its pullback to
{\rm{CH}}^{1}({\mathscr{C}}_{\bar{\eta}^{\prime}})
is zero, which is the non-zero element we started with. Such a
V_{P}
is
{\rm{Spec}}({\mathscr{O}}_{L})
, where
L
is a finite extension of
K
mentioned above. Therefore there exists
l^{i}
torsions in
{\rm{CH}}^{1}({\rm{Spec}}({\mathscr{O}}_{L}))
, which is the class group of
{\mathscr{O}}_{L}
(this follows from localization exact sequence). Considering the presence of étale monodromy it follows that infinitely many number fields of a fixed degree has an element of order
l^{i}
.
∎

3.
An example

Let us consider the super-elliptic curve given by
y^{5}=x^{5}-31
. Since the Tate module of this curve is non-trivial, by Theorem
1.1
, there exist infinitely many number fields which are finite extensions of
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{5})
, the
5
-th degree cyclotomic field, such that the class group of the finite extension of
K
(which is
L
) is divisible by
l^{i}
for some
i
and some prime
l
. In particular, if we take
l=5
and the degree of the extension of
[L:K]
is not divisible by
5
then the class group of the finite extension surjects onto the class group of
K={\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{5})
and we get
5
-divisibility of the class group of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{5})
.

In fact for any prime
p
, there exists a finite extension
L
of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})
such that the class number of
L
is divisible by
p
. Such an extension can be obtained from the super-elliptic curve

y^{p}=x^{p}-(2^{p}-1)
(specializing at the prime
2
).

If
p
does not divide
[L:{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})]
, then
p
divides the class number of
{\mathbb{Q}}(\zeta_{p})
. Hence by the result of Herbrandt-Ribet
[
8
]

p
must divide at least one of the Bernoulli numbers
B_{k}
, where
2\leq k\leq p-3
and
k
even.
