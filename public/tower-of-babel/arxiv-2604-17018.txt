On Regular Higher Power Rational Diophantine Triples
Alen Andrašek

Abstract
. A rational Diophantine m m -tuple is a set { a 1 , … , a m } \{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\} of distinct nonzero rational numbers such that a i ​ a j + 1 a_{i}a_{j}+1 is a square for all 1 ≤ i < j ≤ m 1\leq i<j\leq m . Similarly, we may ask when a i ​ a j + 1 a_{i}a_{j}+1 is a k k -th power. Here, we study the case k = 4 k=4 and produce some non-trivial infinite families of such triples. We show that there are infinitely many triples with positive elements for k = 4 k=4 . We also briefly consider the k = 6 k=6 (sextic) and k = 8 k=8 (octic) cases, explaining the difficulties in extending the method to higher exponents. Keywords : Higher Power Rational Diophantine Triples, Regular Diophantine Triples, Quartic Diophantine Triples



1.
Introduction

A rational Diophantine
m
-tuple is a set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
of distinct nonzero rational numbers such that
a_{i}a_{j}+1

\displaystyle\left\{\frac{1}{16},\frac{33}{16},\frac{17}{4},\frac{105}{16}\right\}.

Fermat found the integer quadruple
\{1,3,8,120\}
and Euler extended it to a rational quintuple

\displaystyle\left\{1,3,8,120,\frac{777480}{8288641}\right\}.

Stoll
[
Stoll_2019
]
proved that this extension was unique. Dujella
[
9
]
proved that the size
m
of an integer Diophantine
m
-tuple is at most
5
and it was proved that an integer Diophantine quintuple does not exist (see
[
13
]
). On the other hand, there exist one-parameter infinite families for rational Diophantine sextuples (see
[
10
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
7
]
). No rational Diophantine septuple is known and no upper bounds on the size
m
of a rational Diophantine
m
-tuple have been given. For more background on Diophantine
m
-tuples, see
[
10
]
.

A
k
-th power rational Diophantine

-tuple is a set
\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{m}\}
of distinct nonzero rationals such that
a_{i}a_{j}+1=t_{ij}^{k}
for some rational
t_{ij}
. Dujella and Bugeaud
[
3
]
give the following examples: in the case

\displaystyle\{2,171,25326\}

and in the case

by

\displaystyle\{1352,9539880,9768370\}.

Batta, Szikszai and Tengely showed in
[
1
]
that there exists a rational
k
-th power Diophantine triple for any positive integer

by giving explicit parametric families. For
even

k

(1)

\displaystyle\left\{-\frac{1}{r^{\frac{k}{2}}},\frac{r^{k}-1}{r^{\frac{k}{2}}},r^{\frac{k}{2}}\right\}

is a Diophantine triple over
\mathbb{Q}(r)
. Similarly, they also give parametric families for
odd

k
. In the case
k=3
, they show that every cubic Diophantine pair except
\{-1,1\}
and
\{-3,3\}
can be extended to a Diophantine triple (this was also shown by Byeon and Fuchs
[
4
]
). They also exhibited a parametric family of third power Diophantine quadruples. On the other hand, Dujella and Bugeaud in
[
3
]
showed that the size
m
of a
k
-th power
integer

m
-tuple is bounded for any
k
but again no bound is known in the rational case.

In this work, we will mainly study the case

(which we will refer to as the

higher power triple (Definition

) in complete analogy with the classical definition of regularity (see

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}r^{2}-1}{s^{2}-r^{2}}=t^{2},

which we interpret as an elliptic curve with a parameter. By finding parametric points of infinite order, we find some parametric families of triples.

This is done in a couple of ways. In all cases, the points are constructed by requiring an additional condition on

. From these we find rational parametrizations for

. To the author’s knowledge, no parametric solutions were known, except for
(\ref{BST_parametrization})
. Using the new expressions, we show that there are infinitely many triples with all elements positive (the above parametric family (

Finally, we comment on ideas of how to generalize these ideas to
2k
-th powers, in particular
sextic
(i.e.

\displaystyle X^{k}+Y^{k}

\displaystyle=Z^{k}+W^{k},

\displaystyle XYZW

\displaystyle=\square,

where

are rational numbers.
We use the notation

2.
Quartic Regular Triples

First, let us fix some notation with the following definition. The notation is in complete analogy with the usual notation for Diophantine triples.

Definition 1
.

The triple
\{a,b,c\}
of distinct nonzero rationals is a
quartic
Diophantine triple if

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}ab+1&=r^{4},\\
ac+1&=s^{4},\\
bc+1&=t^{4},\end{split}

for some rational
r,s,t
.

We want to find rational
a,b,c
that satisfy such equations. Immediately, we can satisfy the first two equations by defining
b,c
so that

(3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}b&=\frac{r^{4}-1}{a},\\
c&=\frac{s^{4}-1}{a}.\end{split}

This leaves us with the problem of determining when the third equation in (
2
) is satisfied. To simplify things, we introduce an analogue to the concept of a
regular
Diophantine triple, in which case the third equation reduces to that of an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}(r)
.

Definition 2
.

With notation as in Definition

1
, a quartic Diophantine triple is called
regular
if
a=s^{2}-r^{2}
(or equivalently if
c=a+b+2r^{2}
).

To find a triple
\{a,b,c\}
such that it is a quartic Diophantine triple, express
b,c
as in (
3
) and plug into the third equation to find that,

\displaystyle t^{4}=bc+1

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{4}-1}{a}\frac{s^{4}-1}{a}+1

\displaystyle=\frac{(r^{4}-1)(s^{4}-1)+a^{2}}{a^{2}}.

Now we can use regularity,

\displaystyle t^{4}=bc+1

\displaystyle=\frac{(r^{4}-1)(s^{4}-1)+(s^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}{(s^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{(s^{2}r^{2}-1)^{2}}{(s^{2}-r^{2})^{2}}.

Taking square roots, we are left to determine when

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}r^{2}-1}{s^{2}-r^{2}}=\pm t^{2}.

Without loss of generality, we may assume the plus sign, for a minus sign would just amount to switching
r
and
s
.

Proposition 1
.

Suppose
r,s
are rationals such that
s,r\neq\pm 1
,
s\neq\pm r
and

(4)

\displaystyle\frac{s^{2}r^{2}-1}{s^{2}-r^{2}}

\displaystyle=t^{2}.

Then
(a,b,c)
is a rational quartic Diophantine triple with
a=s^{2}-r^{2}
,

b=\frac{r^{4}-1}{a}=t^{2}-r^{2}
,
c=\frac{s^{4}-1}{a}=s^{2}+t^{2}
.

Proof.

We have already proved that this is a sufficient condition for a quartic Diophantine triple to exist. Notice that (
4
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle r^{2}s^{2}+r^{2}t^{2}=1+s^{2}t^{2}.

We may rewrite this as

\displaystyle r^{4}-1

\displaystyle=(r^{2}-t^{2})(r^{2}-s^{2}).

Now
r^{4}-1=ab
and
s^{2}-r^{2}=a
, so upon dividing by
a
we get that

\displaystyle b=t^{2}-r^{2}.

Finally,
c=a+b+2r^{2}=s^{2}+t^{2}
. This proves the expressions for
b,c
.
∎

We shall see that there are infinitely many rational solutions to equation (
4
) and, in fact, there is even an integral solution as indicated by a computer search. Some smaller integer
r,s
are given by

\displaystyle(r,s)=(337,339),(337,3107),(507,1242).

These are the only ones with
r<s<10000
. Unfortunately, the third pair does not give an integer Diophantine triple
(a,b,c)
(the corresponding
t
is not an integer in that case). The first two pairs give the same regular triple

\displaystyle(a,b,c)=(1352,9539880,9768370),

as already found by Dujella and Bugeaud
[
3
]
. Thus, the only known integer quartic triple is in fact regular. We also note that the rational parametric triple (
1
) is regular.

2.1.
A Special Case

Notice that the pair
(r,s)=(337,339)
satisfies

. More generally, let

. Then (

) becomes

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}(r+\alpha)^{2}-1}{\alpha(2r+\alpha)}=t^{2}.

In the special case that

\displaystyle\frac{r^{2}(r+\alpha)^{2}-1}{\alpha(2r+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{(r^{2}+\alpha r+1)(r^{2}+\alpha r-1)}{\alpha(2r+\alpha)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left((2r+\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{2}+4\right)\left((2r+\alpha)^{2}-\alpha^{2}-4\right)}{16\alpha(2r+\alpha)}.

We see that the

. Take

1

1

\displaystyle t^{2}=\frac{1}{4}(r+1)(r^{2}+2r-1).

The curve (

\displaystyle y^{2}=x^{3}+4(u^{4}-1)^{2}x.

\alpha=-3067553610/35113
respectively, which have a significantly smaller denominator compared to
r
of similar size. We calculated the ratio with
r
and found

\displaystyle\frac{18817}{10864}=1.73205081001\ldots

\displaystyle\frac{35113}{40545}=0.86602540387\ldots

These seemed to be very good approximations for
\sqrt{3}
and
\sqrt{3}/2
. Looking up the numbers in the OEIS shows that these numbers are denominators of even convergents of
\sqrt{3}
.
The reason for this turned out to be, that if
p/r
is an even numbered convergent of
\sqrt{3}
, then as is well known,
p,r
satisfy the Pell equation,

(16)

\displaystyle p^{2}-3r^{2}=1.

Since we were searching through integers, we found integer
r
such that they are a solution for the Pell equation above, but any rational numbers would work as well.

Proposition 5
.

Let
r\in\mathbb{Q}
,
r\neq 0,\pm 1
and assume there is a
p\in\mathbb{Q}
such that
p^{2}-3r^{2}=1
. Then
P=(r^{2}+1,pr(1-r^{2}))
is a point of infinite order on the curve

\displaystyle E_{r}:y^{2}=(x+2r^{2})(x-2r^{2})(x-r^{4}-1).

Proof.

Simply plug in
x=r^{2}+1
,

\displaystyle y^{2}

\displaystyle=(r^{2}+1+2r^{2})(r^{2}+1-2r^{2})(r^{2}+1-r^{4}-1)

\displaystyle=(3r^{2}+1)(1-r^{2})(r^{2}-r^{4})

\displaystyle=(3r^{2}+1)r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{2}

\displaystyle=p^{2}r^{2}(1-r^{2})^{2}.

We see that
(r^{2}+1,pr(1-r^{2}))
is a point on the curve
E_{r}(\mathbb{Q})
and it is also not one of the eight torsion points in (
2
) (except for
r=0,\pm 1)
. ∎

Remark 4
.

It is interesting from a computational point of view that for
r=2911
, the PARI/GP function
ellrank
did not find the generator with default settings, even though this
r
also solves the Pell equation (
16
) above, meaning there must be a point of infinite order. So we see that if we cannot find small examples, perhaps we may have more luck with larger ones. With a different approach, e.g. using
mwrank
, one may determine the rank computationally.

With the previous proposition, we can construct a point of infinite order when
r
satisfies (
16
). Parametrize this quadratic equation to find

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{2u}{3-u^{2}},

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=\frac{3+u^{2}}{3-u^{2}},

for some rational
u
. Then using (
9
), we will get a corresponding
\alpha
and
s
and thus a quartic Diophantine triple
(a,b,c)
.
We can combine the above point of infinite order with any of the eight torsion points. These eight points give the following possible
s
,

\displaystyle\pm\frac{u^{4}-9}{8u^{2}},\pm\frac{8u^{2}}{u^{4}-9},\pm\frac{2u(u^{2}-3)}{u^{4}+2u^{2}+9},\pm\frac{u^{4}+2u^{2}+9}{2u(u^{2}-3)}.

For each of these possible
s
, the corresponding
t
from (
4
) will also be one of these expressions. Due to symmetry, switching
s,t
or changing signs gives the same parametric family of triples. Thus, only two of them are actually distinct, with
s
reciprocal in the second one. Defining
(a,b,c)
as in Proposition
1
, we have the following two propositions.

Proposition 6
.

Let
(r,s)=\left(\frac{2u}{3-u^{2}},\frac{8u^{2}}{u^{4}-9}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}a&=-\frac{4(u^{2}-9)(u^{2}-1)u^{2}}{(u^{4}-9)^{2}},\\
b&=\frac{(u^{4}-2u^{2}+9)(u^{2}+3)^{2}}{4(u^{2}-3)^{2}u^{2}},\\
c&=\frac{(u^{8}+46u^{4}+81)(u^{2}+9)(u^{2}+1)}{4(u^{4}-9)^{2}u^{2}}\end{split}

is a quartic Diophantine triple if
u\neq 0,\pm 1,\pm 3
.

Proposition 7
.

Let
(r,s)=\left(\frac{2u}{3-u^{2}},\frac{u^{4}-9}{8u^{2}}\right)
. Then

\displaystyle\begin{split}a&=\frac{(u^{4}+2u^{2}+9)^{2}(u^{2}-1)(u^{2}-9)}{64(u^{2}-3)^{2}u^{4}},\\
b&=-\frac{64(u^{4}-2u^{2}+9)u^{4}}{(u^{4}+2u^{2}+9)^{2}(u^{2}-3)^{2}},\\
c&=\frac{(u^{8}+46u^{4}+81)(u^{2}+9)(u^{2}+1)(u^{2}-3)^{2}}{64(u^{4}+2u^{2}+9)^{2}u^{4}}\end{split}

is a quartic Diophantine triple if
u\neq 0,\pm 1,\pm 3
.

For brevity, the following two factorizations were not included above

\displaystyle u^{4}+2u^{2}+9

\displaystyle=(u^{2}+2u+3)(u^{2}-2u+3),

\displaystyle u^{8}+46u^{4}+81

\displaystyle=(u^{4}+2u^{3}+2u^{2}-6u+9)(u^{4}-2u^{3}+2u^{2}+6u+9).

We note that the parametric families given in the previous propositions indeed can be distinguished. For example, the parametric family in Proposition
7
has
b
always negative. Thus, it cannot prove Theorem
1
. On the other hand, Proposition
6
can do so completely analogously to the proof of Theorem
1
. (Indeed, there
b,c
have all factors positive and one simply chooses
u
such that the factor
-(u^{2}-9)(u^{2}-1)
of
a
is positive.)

Remark 5
.

We can choose
u
so that
r
itself is a square, thus at least one of the right hand side numbers in (
1
) will be an
8
-th power as well. In fact, setting
r=\frac{2u}{3-u^{2}}=\square
, this equation transforms into the elliptic curve
y^{2}=x^{3}-12x
of rank
1
and we get an infinite sequence of such triples. This is interesting because even though
[
1
]
give a parametric family for any power, the parametrization there always has one of the
r,s,t
equal to
0
and here this is not the case. The generator
P
of the Mordell-Weil gives a trivial case which we disregard. But
2P
gives
u=-2
and
r=4
. The corresponding triple from Proposition
6
is

\displaystyle(a,b,c)=\left(\frac{240}{49},\frac{833}{16},\frac{69745}{784}\right).

6.
Generalization to Higher Power Tuples

Our concept of
regularity
can be extended to any even power Diophantine triple in the following way:

Definition 3
.

The triple
\{a,b,c\}
is a rational
2k
-th power Diophantine triple if

(17)

\displaystyle\begin{split}ab+1&=r^{2k},\\
ac+1&=s^{2k},\\
bc+1&=t^{2k},\end{split}

for some rational
r,s,t
. We will call it regular if
a=s^{k}-r^{k}
or equivalently
c=a+b+2r^{k}
.

Remark 6
.

From the second expression, write
c-a-b=2r^{k}
. Upon squaring this, we can eliminate
r
to get the equivalent regularity condition

(18)

\displaystyle a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}-2ab-2bc-2ca=4

which applies equally for any even power Diophantine triple.

In analogy with the quartic case, let
b=\frac{r^{2k}-1}{a}
,
c=\frac{s^{2k}-1}{a}
so the first two equations in (
17
) hold, and assume regularity (i.e.
a=s^{k}-r^{k})
. The third equation will be satisfied if

\displaystyle t^{2k}=bc+1

\displaystyle=\frac{r^{2k}-1}{a}\frac{s^{2k}-1}{a}+1

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{r^{k}s^{k}-1}{s^{k}-r^{k}}\right)^{2}.

Upon taking square roots, we find that we must have

\displaystyle\frac{r^{k}s^{k}-1}{s^{k}-r^{k}}=t^{k},

in complete analogy with equation (
4
) which we studied in depth for the quartic case. Multiply this out and rewrite to get

\displaystyle r^{k}s^{k}+r^{k}t^{k}=s^{k}t^{k}+1.

If we set
x=rs
,
y=rt
,
z=st
, then
xyz=(rst)^{2}
. Therefore, if we can find rational
x,y,z
such that

(19)

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{k}+y^{k}&=z^{k}+1,\\
xyz&=\square,\end{split}

we will have found a regular
2k
-th triple by setting
r=\sqrt{\frac{xy}{z}}
etc.

Remark 7
.

We may homogenize the equations (
19
) to get

(20)

\displaystyle\begin{split}X^{k}+Y^{k}&=Z^{k}+W^{k},\\
XYZW&=\square.\end{split}

Thus we are looking for numbers that can be written as a sum of two
k
-th powers in two (non-trivially) different ways (generalized taxicab numbers)
and
we would require that the product of bases of those powers is a square.

Since it is not known whether a number exists such that it is expressible as a sum of two
5
-th powers in two ways, any progress in this direction will be difficult with current methods except for the cases
k=3,4
.

Thus we restrict ourselves here to a short analysis of
sextic
and
octic
Diophantine triples.

6.1.
Sextic Diophantine Triples

For sextic Diophantine triples, take
k=3
in (
19
), to get

(21)

\displaystyle\begin{split}x^{3}+y^{3}&=z^{3}+1,\\
xyz&=\square.\end{split}

The solution set to the first equation was already parametrized by Euler (see
[
5
]
, pp. 552-554, cf.
[
Choudhry
]
,
[
11
]
). It has been referred to as the
Fermat cubic surface
at times.
The general parametrization is too unwieldy to yield a (simple) condition when
xyz
is a square.

From the second condition,
z=q^{2}xy
for some rational
q
so one can write the two equations in one as

\displaystyle x^{3}+y^{3}=1+q^{6}x^{3}y^{3}.

Generically, this has genus
4
over
\mathbb{Q}(q)
(checked with MAGMA).

To make things more manageable, we tried our luck with simpler methods as described up to now. Unfortunately, this did not work, but we record the work done.

Proposition 8
.

Considering
x^{3}+y^{3}=z^{3}+1
as an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}(z)
, its long Weierstrass form is given by

\displaystyle Y^{2}-9(z^{3}+1)Y=X^{3}-27(z^{3}+1)^{2}

which has trivial torsion and a point of infinite order
P=(3(z^{2}-z+1),9(z^{2}-z+1))
over
\mathbb{Q}(z)
.

The point
P
unfortunately gives
r=\pm 1
i.e. one of the
a,b,c
would have to be
0
. Choosing multiples of
P
did not help. For example, on the curve above, the point
2P
maps back to

\displaystyle x

\displaystyle=-\frac{2z^{3}-1}{z^{3}-1},

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{z(z^{3}+2)}{z^{3}-1}.

If
xyz=(rst)^{2}
is to indeed be a rational square, then we would necessarily have

\displaystyle xyz=-\frac{z^{2}(2z^{3}+1)(z^{3}+2)}{(z^{3}-1)^{2}}=q^{2}.

But this already leads to a hyperelliptic equation, which will then only have finitely many rational solutions, if any.

Another attempt was the following.

Proposition 9
.

Set
x+y=k
. Considering
x^{3}+y^{3}=z^{3}+1
as an elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}(k)
, its Weierstrass form is given by

\displaystyle Y^{2}=X^{3}-\frac{27k^{3}(k^{3}-4)}{4},

which has trivial torsion and rank (at least)
2
over
\mathbb{Q}(k)
. Two independent points of infinite order are given by

\displaystyle P

\displaystyle=(3k^{2}-3k,\frac{9}{2}k^{2}(k-2)),

\displaystyle Q

\displaystyle=(3k^{2}+6k+9,\frac{9}{2}(k^{3}+4k^{2}+6k+6)).

We omit the details, but again, no combination of points of small order gave conditions that would be amenable to producing rational solutions. In fact, all resulting equations seemed to be of higher genus, thus if there were any rational solutions, they should be very rare.

Still, there are solutions to (
21
). By brute-force, we found the following identities

\displaystyle 243^{3}+1600^{3}

\displaystyle=484^{3}+1587^{3},

\displaystyle 78^{3}+2809^{3}

\displaystyle=289^{3}+2808^{3}.

The corresponding products are, respectively, squares. These identities can be reordered and then dehomogenized in different ways leading to different triples (with different signs). The triples with all positive elements that one obtains this way are

\displaystyle(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{28041978419}{287496000},\frac{32882791256000}{318751954191},\frac{131810257007}{1915864488000}\right),

\displaystyle(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{116256402521}{15260373670464},\frac{3299834241680237}{503598378816},\frac{4782288288960}{731432701}\right).

The identities above are particularly notable, since we can also write them as

(22)

\displaystyle\begin{split}40^{6}+3^{3}\cdot 9^{6}&=22^{6}+3^{3}\cdot 23^{6},\\
53^{6}+78^{3}\cdot 1^{6}&=17^{6}+78^{3}\cdot 6^{6}.\end{split}

This shows a decomposition of a number into the form
x^{6}+h^{3}y^{6}
in two ways. No number is known that is decomposable in two ways as
x^{6}+y^{6}
.

The solutions above were found in multiple ways. A search with the explicit parametrization yielded the first example, but not the second one. Both examples were also found simply by brute-force search. No further examples have been found with a search in the range
0\leq X,Y,Z,W\leq 30000
.

Going back to (
21
), we note that in our two solutions we can choose, e.g.
x=\square
(in the two examples above
1600/484
and
2809/289
are squares). Thus, assume
x=\chi^{2}
and
z=k^{2}y
for some rational
k
. Then we find that

\displaystyle y^{3}=\frac{k^{6}-1}{\chi^{6}-1},

showing another possible way to view the equation. This also shows that whenever one of
x,y,z
is a square, we will have an identity as in (
22
).
Finally we note that a few dozen examples of sextic Diophantine triples that are not regular have been found by brute force search.

6.2.
Octic Diophantine Triples

To find octic regular diophantine triples, we would need to solve the following in rationals,

(23)

\displaystyle X^{4}+Y^{4}

\displaystyle=Z^{4}+W^{4},

(24)

\displaystyle XYZW

\displaystyle=\square.

We found no examples. Trying out Euler’s parametric family (
[
5
]
, pp. 644-647 for the classical derivation),

\displaystyle X

\displaystyle=a^{7}+a^{5}b^{2}-2a^{3}b^{4}+3a^{2}b^{5}+ab^{6},

\displaystyle Y

\displaystyle=a^{6}b-3a^{5}b^{2}-2a^{4}b^{3}+a^{2}b^{5}+b^{7},

\displaystyle Z

\displaystyle=a^{7}+a^{5}b^{2}-2a^{3}b^{4}-3a^{2}b^{5}+ab^{6},

\displaystyle W

\displaystyle=a^{6}b+3a^{5}b^{2}-2a^{4}b^{3}+a^{2}b^{5}+b^{7}.

The condition
XYZW=\square
is homogeneous and so we can take
b=1
. Then

\displaystyle XZ

\displaystyle=(a^{12}+2a^{10}-3a^{8}-2a^{6}+6a^{4}-13a^{2}+1)a^{2},

\displaystyle YW

\displaystyle=a^{12}-13a^{10}+6a^{8}-2a^{6}-3a^{4}+2a^{2}+1.

Notice that the above polynomials in
a
are reciprocal. If we set
YW=f(a)
, then
XZ=a^{2}f(1/a)
. Thus,
XYZW
can be written in terms of
u=a+1/a
and we get the polynomial

\displaystyle XYZW=v^{2}=u^{12}-23u^{10}+141u^{8}-266u^{6}-80u^{4}+351u^{2}+324.

We may further set
u=t^{2}
and arrive at

\displaystyle v^{2}=t^{6}-23t^{5}+141t^{4}-266t^{3}-80t^{2}+351t+324.

In any case, this is a genus
2
curve and has only finitely many points, if any. Therefore, this is not the right approach to generate many examples.
Further parametric families can be found in a recent paper of Bremner
[
2
]
among others, but due to their complexity, they will probably not be of use here.

Further remarks

Recently, a question on MathOverflow
[
12
]
asked a very related question. Most of the parametric families mentioned there were already known to the author. The only one not already appearing here is due to Mathoverflow user ”GH from MO” (going again back to Euler and Hillyer, see
[
5
]
, p. 174) which leads to the family of regular triples with

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle=\frac{(u-1)(3u+1)}{2(3u^{2}+1)},

\displaystyle s

\displaystyle=\frac{(u+1)(3u-1)}{4u},

\displaystyle r

\displaystyle=\frac{9u^{4}+22u^{2}+1}{2(9u^{4}+6u^{3}+2u^{2}-2u+1)},

and as before,
a=s^{2}-r^{2}
,
b=t^{2}-r^{2}
,
c=s^{2}+t^{2}
. We omit the explicit expressions for
a,b,c
, but we note that
c
is a perfect square in
\mathbb{Q}(u)
. This suggests a similar approach to our first parametric family, except that one would ask that

s^{2}+t^{2}

is a square. With this condition, (
4
) would transform to the Weierstrass model,

\displaystyle y^{2}=x^{3}-u^{2}(u^{2}-1)^{2}x,

which is
2
-isogenous to the curve (
15
) of our second parametric solution (even though we started with a condition that looks like the condition of the first parametric solution). We simply note that the parametric family given above comes from a particular fibration when
u=\frac{3v^{2}+v}{v-1}
when the above curve has an extra point of infinite order that maps to the
s,t
above. We can further find some other parametric families from this curve, but we will stop here.

8.
Conclusion

In this paper we demonstrated many non-trivial parametric families of quartic Diophantine triples by assuming the regularity condition in Definition
2
and further conditions on the right hand side variables
r,s,t
. We proved that there are infinitely many quartic Diophantine triples with positive elements.

What about higher powers? We explored the sextic and octic cases, highlighting the difficulties in extending these methods to higher exponents. Are there infinitely many sextic Diophantine triples with positive elements? What about the octic case? Is there an octic Diophantine triple that does not come from (
1
)? Is there a positive one? Are there infinitely many?

We hope that these infinite families help to better understand the structure of quartic Diophantine triples and facilitate the search for further interesting examples of higher order Diophantine
m
-tuples.
