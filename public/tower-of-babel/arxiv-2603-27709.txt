pseudo-torsion classes
Kiyoshi Igusa, RAY MARESCA

Abstract
. For a finite dimensional algebra Λ \Lambda , we consider a torsion class 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} in m ​ o ​ d mod - Λ \Lambda , which is not necessarily finitely generated. We construct a wall-and-chamber structure for 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} where the chambers are the connected components of the complement of the union of walls. We also consider “infinitesimal chambers”. To each chamber we associate a “pseudo-torsion class” and a “pseudo-torsionfree class” and show that they are all distinct. We consider “green paths” in the stability space and associate to them Harder-Narasimhan stratifications of 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} . This paper is part of a series of papers whose goal is to study the “ghosts” which are remnants of the indecomposable Λ \Lambda -modules which do not lie in 𝒢 {\mathcal{G}} . In the special case when our torsion class is all of m ​ o ​ d mod - Λ \Lambda , we are in the classical well-known setting. All of our results apply to this classical setting. The “pseudo-torsion classes” are torsion classes. We point out that we do not take the closure of the set of walls. So, we get more chambers and our results are new even in this classical setting.

Introduction

Given a torsion class
{\mathcal{G}}
in
mod\text{-}\Lambda

[
BR
]
we consider a subcategory of
{\mathcal{G}}
with the same objects but with only “strict” morphisms. Strict morphisms, along with other properties, have kernels in
{\mathcal{G}}
and, as with all morphisms in
{\mathcal{G}}

{\mathcal{G}}

with its strict morphisms which are closed under strict extension and contain the kernel, image and cokernel of every strict morphism between its objects. When

1.
Basic definitions

Let
\Lambda
be a finite dimensional algebra over any field. Let
n
be the rank of
\Lambda
. Then, up to isomorphisms, there are
n
simple modules
S_{1},S_{2},\cdots,S_{n}
covered by

\Lambda

We consider a fixed
torsion class

{\mathcal{G}}
in
mod\text{-}\Lambda
. This is a class of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules which is closed under extension and under taking of quotients. This means that for any short exact sequence of
\Lambda
-modules

0\to A\to B\to C\to 0

if

are in
{\mathcal{G}}
then so is
B
. And if
B\in{\mathcal{G}}
then

.

The kernel

of a morphism

in
{\mathcal{G}}
might not be in
{\mathcal{G}}
and this is a problem that we need to get around. We will construct a new category: the “strict category
{\mathcal{G}}
” with the same objects as
{\mathcal{G}}
but with only “strict morphisms”. This new category will have good properties. Every morphism will have kernel, image and cokernel in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Using these new morphisms we will construct “pseudo-torsion classes” and pseudo-torsion free classes.

1.1.
Strict morphisms and strict extensions

Definition 1.1
.

Given an object

Lemma 1.2

If

Theorem 1.3
.

Any composition of cofibrations is a cofibration.

Proof.

Suppose that

1.2.
Pseudo-torsion pairs

Now we define “pseudo-torsion classes” and “pseudo-torsionfree classes” and show that they form strict Hom-perpendicular pairs.

Definition 1.18
.

A nonempty set of objects
{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
is called a

if the following hold.

(1)

{\mathcal{P}}
is closed under strict quotients, i.e., if

is a fibration and

then so is
B
. In particular,

.

(2)

{\mathcal{P}}
is closed under strict extensions.

Definition 1.19
.

A nonempty class
{\mathcal{Q}}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
is called a
pseudo-torsionfree class
if the following hold.

(1)

{\mathcal{Q}}
is closed under strict subobjects, i.e., if
A\rightarrowtail B
is a cofibration and
B\in{\mathcal{Q}}
then so is
A
. In particular,
0\in{\mathcal{Q}}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{Q}}
is closed under strict extensions.

Definition 1.20
.

For any
{\mathcal{X}}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
, its
right perpendicular class

{\mathcal{X}}^{\perp}
is defined to be the class of all
Y\in{\mathcal{G}}
so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms

for any
X\in{\mathcal{X}}
. Similarly, for any
{\mathcal{Y}}\subseteq{\mathcal{G}}
, the
left perpendicular class

\,{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{Y}}
is the class of all
X\in{\mathcal{G}}
so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms from
X
to any object in
{\mathcal{Y}}
.

Lemma 1.21
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}\subset{\mathcal{G}}
be a subclass of objects in a torsion class

.

(1)

\,{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{X}}
is a pseudo-torsion class.

(2)

{\mathcal{X}}^{\perp}
is a pseudo-torsionfree class.

Proof.

We prove (1) as the proof of (2) is analogous. Let
M\in\,^{\perp}{\mathcal{X}}
and
f:M\twoheadrightarrow N
be a strict quotient of
M
. Suppose that
g:N\rightarrow X
is a nonzero strict morphism where

. Then, the composition
M\twoheadrightarrow N\to X
is a nonzero strict morphism contradicting the assumption that

. Therefore,
\,{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{X}}
is closed under strict quotients.

Now suppose

and

is a strict extension. Let

be a strict morphism. The composition

. So,

factors though a morphism

. By Proposition

is strict and therefore equal to 0. So,

and

.

Since

is closed under strict quotients and strict extensions, it is a pseudo-torsion class.
∎

. In particular,
0\in{\mathcal{W}}
.

(2)

{\mathcal{W}}
is closed under strict extensions in
{\mathcal{G}}
.

By Proposition
1.17
and the definition of strict morphisms,
{\mathcal{G}}
is a pseudo-wide subcategory of itself. Although pseudo-wide subcategories have many of the properties of abelian categories, Example
1.15
shows that they may not be additive categories.

1.4.
Relative pseudo-torsion classes

Definition 1.26
.

Let
{\mathcal{X}}
be pseudo-wide subcategory of
{\mathcal{G}}
. Then a
relative pseudo-torsion class
in
{\mathcal{X}}
is a subset
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
of
{\mathcal{X}}
which is closed under strict extensions and under strict quotients which lie in
{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus, if
P\twoheadrightarrow X
is a strict epimorphism with
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
and
X\in{\mathcal{X}}
then
X\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
.
Relative pseudo-torsionfree classes
in
{\mathcal{X}}
are defined analogously.

The
relative perpendicular classes
are defined to be
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{X}}
and
\,{}^{\perp}{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}\cap{\mathcal{X}}
. Thus, for instance, the relative right perpendicular class of
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
is the class of all
X\in{\mathcal{X}}
so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms from any
P\in{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
to
X
.

Theorem 1.27
.

Taking relative perpendicular classes gives a bijection, i.e., the left relative perpendicular class of the right relative perpendicular class of
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
is the original class
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
and similarly starting with
{\mathcal{Q}}_{0}
. Furthermore, every object of
{\mathcal{X}}
has a canonical strict subobject in
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}
with quotient in
{\mathcal{P}}_{0}^{\perp}\cap{\mathcal{X}}
.

The proof is very similar to the proof of the absolute cases: Theorems
1.22
and
1.23
. We will not need these in Part I (the present paper).

2.
Wall and chamber structure

Recall that
\Lambda
is a finite dimensional algebra over a field of rank
n
. Up to isomorphism, there are
n
indecomposable projective modules
P_{1},\cdots,P_{n}
whose tops
S_{i}=P_{i}/rad\,P_{i}
are the simple
\Lambda
-modules. Let
F_{i}
denote the division algebra

F_{i}:=\End_{\Lambda}(P_{i})/rad\End_{\Lambda}(P_{i})

For any finitely generated right
\Lambda
-module
M
let
\undim M
denote its
dimension vector

\undim M=(d_{1},\cdots,d_{n})

where
d_{i}
is the dimension of
\Hom_{\Lambda}(P_{i},M)
over
F_{i}
. This is also the number of times
S_{i}
occurs in the composition series of
M
. Then

\undim M\in K_{0}\Lambda\cong{\mathbb{Z}}^{n}.

Definition 2.1
.

We define the
stability space
of
\Lambda
to be

V_{\Lambda}:=\Hom_{\mathbb{Z}}(K_{0}\Lambda,{\mathbb{R}})\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Elements of
V_{\Lambda}
are called
stability conditions
. For any stability condition
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
and
M\in mod\text{-}\Lambda
, let
\theta(M)
denote

\theta(M):=\theta(\undim M)\in{\mathbb{R}}.

Basic examples of pseudo-torsion classes, pseudo-torsionfree classes and pseudo-wide subcategories are given by “stability conditions”. We could also take the “reduced” stability space of
{\mathcal{G}}
which is

V_{\mathcal{G}}:=\Hom(\overline{K}_{0}{\mathcal{G}},{\mathbb{R}}).

This would be more natural and, in general, will give more pseudo-torsion classes for
{\mathcal{G}}
. See Section
4
for explanations and examples. However, we study in detail stability diagrams for
{\mathcal{G}}
in the standard stability space
V_{\Lambda}
since this will be more familiar to the readers. Also, we will be able to compare the stability diagrams of
{\mathcal{G}}
and
mod\text{-}\Lambda
as subsets of the same space
V_{\Lambda}
. (On the other hand,
{\mathcal{G}}\subseteq mod\text{-}\Lambda
, so
V_{\Lambda}\subseteq V_{\mathcal{G}}
.)

Definition 2.2
.

For any object
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
, we define the
extended dimension vector
of
M
to be the collection of all dimension vectors of all strict subquotients of
M
. More precisely, it is the set of all ordered triples
([A],[B],[C])
of elements of
K_{0}\Lambda={\mathbb{Z}}^{n}
where
(A,B,C)
gives the subquotients of a strict filtration of
M
:

M_{1}\rightarrowtail M_{2}\rightarrowtail M

with
A=M_{1}
,
B=M_{2}/M_{1}
,
C=M/M_{2}
.

Note that, although there may be infinitely many
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
and infinitely many strict filtrations of each
M
, there are only finitely many extended dimension vectors of bounded total length. Consequently, the set of extended dimension vectors of all objects of
{\mathcal{G}}
is only countably infinite.

2.1.
Walls thick and thin

Let
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
be the set of all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)\neq 0
. We will see that this is the union of “walls”
D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. The chambers will be the path components of the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
in
V_{\Lambda}
. We also need “infinitesmal chambers” which arise when a wall is a limit of other walls. In general, each wall will have two infinitesmal chambers, one on each side of the wall. We will show that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is constant on each chamber and distinct for distinct chambers. The same holds for
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
since
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
determine each other by Theorem
2.15
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given an object
M
in a torsion class
{\mathcal{G}}
we define the
pseudo-wall

D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
to be the set of all stability conditions
\theta
so that
\theta(M)=0
and
\theta(M^{\prime})\leq 0
for all strict subobjects
M^{\prime}
of
M
. Equivalently,
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M
. The
interior

int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
is the set of all
\theta\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
so that
\theta(M^{\prime})<0
for all nonzero proper strict subobjects
M^{\prime}\subset M
. The
boundary
of
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
is the complement of its interior:

\partial D_{\mathcal{G}}(M):=D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)-int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(M).

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is defined to be the class of all objects
X
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta\in D_{{\mathcal{G}}}(X)
. That is, the class of all
X
in
{\mathcal{G}}
such that
\theta(X)=0
and
\theta(X^{\prime})\leq 0
for all strict subobjects
X^{\prime}
of
X
. Equivalently,
\theta(X^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X
.

By its definition,
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
is a closed convex subset of the stability space
V_{\Lambda}
. We also note that the set
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)\subset V_{\Lambda}
depends only on the extended dimension vector of
M
(Def.
2.2
). So, there are only countably many pseudo-walls.

There is a big distinction between what we call “thin” wall and “thick” walls.

Definition 2.4
.

The
thin walls
are the path components of the set of all
\theta\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
so that
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is generated by one object
B
. Thus, the objects of
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
will be the iterated strict self-extensions of
B
. Points on the rest of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
will be called
thick
. The
thick walls
are
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
for
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
minus the thin walls.

Proposition 2.5
.

For any path
\theta_{t}
,
t\in[0,1]
in a thin wall,
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
is constant, i.e., the generator
B_{t}
of
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
is the same for all
0\leq t\leq 1
.

Proof.

Whether an object
M
lies in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
depends only on the extended dimension vector of
M
. So, if this lies on a thin wall, the generator
B_{t}
is uniquely determined by its extended dimension vector. (Any other object
B
with the same extended dimension vector as
B_{t}
must lie in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
and therefore must be isomorphic to
B_{t}
.)
The set of all
t\in[0,1]
with a fixed generator
B
is a closed set since
D_{\mathcal{G}}(B)
is a closed set. If the generator
B_{t}
of
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
is not a constant function of
t
, we would then get the compact connected set
[0,1]
being a disjoint union of countably many closed subsets which is not possible by
[
S
]
.
∎

Proposition 2.6
.

Every
\theta\in\partial D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
lies in
int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(\overline{M})
for some strict quotient
\overline{M}
of
M
.
\theta
also lies in
int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(M^{\prime})
for some strict subobject
M^{\prime}
of
M
.

Proof.

Suppose
\theta\in\partial D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
. Then
\theta(M)=0
and
\theta(M^{\prime})=0
for some strict subobject of
M
. Take
M^{\prime}
minimal. Then
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})>0
for every strict subobject
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\theta\in int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(M^{\prime})
. The corresponding statement holds for strict quotients of
M
by transitivity of strict quotients.
∎

Corollary 2.7
.

The union of all the walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
is equal to the union of all their interiors
int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
:

\bigcup_{M\in{\mathcal{G}}}D_{{\mathcal{G}}}(M)=\bigcup_{M\in{\mathcal{G}}}intD_{{\mathcal{G}}}(M).

A nonzero object
B\in{\mathcal{G}}
is called a
pseudo-brick
if every nonzero strict endomorphism
f:B\to B
is an isomorphism. Note that, if
M
is not a brick and
f:M\to M
is a nonzero strict endomorphism with kernel
K
, image
I
and cokernel
C
then, for any
\theta\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
we have
\theta(K)=\theta(I)=\theta(C)=0
. So, the interior of
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
would be empty. This shows the following.

Corollary 2.8
.

The union of the walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
is equal to the union of all interiors
int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(B)
for all pseudo-bricks
B
:

\bigcup_{M\in{\mathcal{G}}}D_{{\mathcal{G}}}(M)=\bigcup_{\text{bricks }B\in{\mathcal{G}}}intD_{{\mathcal{G}}}(B).

2.2.
Pseudo-torsion classes given by stability conditions

We are now ready to show how pseudo-torsion classes and pseuo-torsionfree classes arise from stability conditions.

Definition 2.9
.

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
we define
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta),\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta),{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta),\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
as follows.

(1)

{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is the class consisting of
0
and all nonzero objects
M
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta(M)>0
and
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})>0
for all nonzero strict quotients
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M
.

(2)

\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is the class of all objects
M
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta(M)\geq 0
and
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M
.

(3)

{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
is the class consisting of
0
and all nonzero objects
N
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta(N)<0
and
\theta(N^{\prime})<0
for all nonzero strict subobjects
N^{\prime}
of
N
.

(4)

\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
is the class of all objects
N
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta(N)\leq 0
and
\theta(N^{\prime})\leq 0
for all strict subobjects
N^{\prime}
of
N
.

Note that
0
is an object in each of these classes.

Lemma 2.10
.

When
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)=0
we have
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
. When
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is nonzero, we have strict inclusions
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)\subsetneq\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)\subsetneq\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
. When
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)\neq 0
, the inclusion is strict since
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is contains in
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
but disjoint from
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
. When
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)=0
, we have equality. To see this, take any
X\in\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
. Then
\theta(X^{\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
X^{\prime}
of
X
. If
\theta(X^{\prime})=0
then we would have
X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. So, we must have
\theta(X^{\prime})>0
for all
X^{\prime}
strict quotients of
X
including
X^{\prime}=X
. So,
X\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and we have equality:
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
. The argument for
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
is similar.
∎

Theorem 2.11
.

For every
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
are pseudo-torsion classes which are closed under extensions. In particular, they are closed under direct sums.

Proof.

Let
M\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and let
M^{\prime\prime}
be a strict quotient of
M
. Then
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})>0
and
\theta(N)>0
for any strict quotient
N
of
M^{\prime\prime}
since
N
is also a strict quotient of
M
. So
M^{\prime\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is closed under strict quotients.

Next, we show that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is closed under extensions, in particular strict extensions. We let
0\to A\to B\to C\to 0
be a short exact sequence where
A,C\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
. Then
B\in{\mathcal{G}}
since
{\mathcal{G}}
is closed under extensions and

\theta(B)=\theta(A)+\theta(C)>0.

By Proposition
1.7
, any strict quotient
B^{\prime\prime}
of
B
is an extension of a strict quotient
A^{\prime\prime}
of
A
by a strict quotient
C^{\prime\prime}
of
C
. Then

\theta(B^{\prime\prime})=\theta(A^{\prime\prime})+\theta(C^{\prime\prime})>0.

So,
B\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
as claimed. The proof for
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is similar.
∎

An analogous argument, with inequalities reversed, proves the following.

Theorem 2.12
.

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
are pseudo-torsionfree classes which are closed under extensions.

Theorem 2.13
.

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is a pseudo-wide subcategory of
{\mathcal{G}}
which is closed under extensions.

Proof.

Let
X,Y\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
and let
f:X\to Y
be a strict morphism with image
I
, kernel
K
and cokernel
C
. Then
\theta(X)=0=\theta(Y)
and
\theta(I)\leq 0
being a strict subobject of
Y
and
\theta(I)\geq 0
being a strict quotient of
X
. So,
\theta(I)=0
. This makes
\theta(K)=\theta(X)-\theta(I)=0
and
\theta(C)=\theta(Y)-\theta(I)=0
.

Any strict subobject
K^{\prime}
of
K
is also a strict subobject of
X
. So
\theta(K^{\prime})\leq 0
and
K\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. Similarly,
I\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
since any strict subobject of
I
is also a strict subobject of
Y
. Finally,
C\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
since any strict quotient
C^{\prime\prime}
of
C
is a strict quotient of
Y
making
\theta(C^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
.

Next, we show that
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is closed under extensions and, therefore under strict extensions. Given a short exact sequence

0\to X\to Z\to Y\to 0

where
X,Y\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
we have
\theta(Z)=\theta(X)+\theta(Y)=0
. By Proposition
1.7
, any strict subobject
Z^{\prime}
of
Z
is an extension of a strict subobject
X^{\prime}
of
X
by a strict subobject
Y^{\prime}
of
Y
. So,
\theta(Z^{\prime})=\theta(X^{\prime})+\theta(Y^{\prime})\leq 0
. So,
Z\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
.
∎

Remark 2.14
.

Not all pseudo-torsion classes are closed under extension. For example, in the
B_{2}
example below (
2.5.1
), the class of objects
{\mathcal{P}}=add(P_{1}\oplus I_{1})
is not closed under extention of
\Lambda
-modules since
0\to P_{1}\to I_{2}\oplus I_{2}\to I_{1}\to 0
is an extension and
I_{2}\oplus I_{2}
is not in
{\mathcal{P}}
. However,
{\mathcal{P}}
is a pseudo-torsion class since there are no strict morphisms from
P_{1}
or
I_{1}
into
mI_{2}
for any
m\geq 1
. So,
{\mathcal{P}}
is closed under strict extensions and also under strict quotients since the only strict quotient of
nP_{1}\oplus mI_{1}
is a direct summand.

The fact that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is closed under
\Lambda
-extensions is a consequence of the fact that this is defined for
\theta
in the stability space of
\Lambda
. If we look at the stability space of
{\mathcal{G}}
we will see two more chambers giving two new pseudo-torsion classes. The first is
{\mathcal{P}}=add(P_{1}\oplus I_{1})
discussed above and the second is
add(I_{2})
, the collection of all objects
mI_{2}
. This is closed under strict quotients since the epimorphism
2I_{2}\to I_{1}
is not a strict morphism.

2.3.
Perpendicular classes

We will see that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
are perpendicular to each other according to the following definition if
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)=0
. When
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)\neq 0
, we will see that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}=\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)^{\perp}={\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
. Recall from Definition
1.20
that we only consider strict morphisms when referring to perpendicular classes.

Theorem 2.15
.

If
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)=0
then
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is the left perpendicular class of
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
and (equivalently)
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}
.

Proof.

This follows from Theorem
2.16
below since
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)=0
implies
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
by Lemma
2.10
.
∎

Theorem 2.16
.

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is the left perpendicular class of
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
and (equivalently)
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}
.

Proof.

If
M\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
N\in\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
then there cannot be a nonzero strict morphism
f:M\to N
since its image
\im f
lies in both
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
making
\theta(\im f)>0
and
\theta(\im f)\leq 0
which is a contradiction unless
\im f=0
. Thus
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)\subset{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}
.

Conversely, suppose that
Y
is not in
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
. Then there is a strict subobject
Y^{\prime}
of
Y
so that
\theta(Y^{\prime})>0
. Take
Y^{\prime}
minimal. Then
\theta(Z)\leq 0
for all nonzero strict subobjects of
Y
which are properly contained in
Y^{\prime}
. Then

\theta(Y^{\prime}/Z)=\theta(Y^{\prime})-\theta(Z)>0.

This implies that
Y^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and the inclusion map
Y^{\prime}\rightarrowtail Y
is a nonzero strict morphism. So,
Y
is not in
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}
. We conclude that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}=\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
.
∎

By a similar argument we have the following.

Theorem 2.17
.

For any
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
,
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is the left perpendicular class of
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
and (equivalently)
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)^{\perp}
.

The statements that
\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}
and
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)^{\perp}
follow from similar arguments. They also follow from the more general formula in Theorem
1.22
.

2.4.
Pseudo-chambers

Recall that
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
was defined to be the set of all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)\neq 0
. This is the same as the union of all pseudo-walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
for
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
because if
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)\neq 0
, it lies in the wall
D_{{\mathcal{G}}}(M)
for some
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
. The standard definition of a “chamber” is a component of the complement of the closure of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
in
V_{\Lambda}
. By a
pseudo-chamber
we mean a path component in the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
. We will show that each pseudo-chamber has a well-defined pseudo-torsion class and a well-defined pseudo-torsion-free class and that these are different for different chambers.

Definition 2.18
.

For every object
M
of
{\mathcal{G}}
, let
{\mathcal{U}}(M)
denote the set of all
\theta
so that
M\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
. Let
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}(M)
denote the set of all
\theta
so that
M\in\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
. Let
{\mathcal{V}}(M)
denote the set of all
\theta
so that
M\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
. Similarly,
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}(M)
is the set of all
\theta
so that
M\in\overline{{\mathcal{Q}}}(\theta)
.

Clearly,
{\mathcal{U}}(M),{\mathcal{V}}(M)
are open,
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}(M),\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}(M)
are closed and all four sets are convex.

Lemma 2.19
.

a)
\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}(M)
is the closure of
{\mathcal{U}}(M)
.

b)
\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}(M)
is the closure of
{\mathcal{V}}(M)
.

c)
\partial{\mathcal{U}}(M)=\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}(M)-{\mathcal{U}}(M)
is contained in the union of walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(\overline{M})
where
\overline{M}
is a a strict quotient of
M
(including
\overline{M}=M
).

d)
\partial{\mathcal{V}}(M)=\overline{{\mathcal{V}}}(M)-{\mathcal{V}}(M)
is contained in the union of walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(M^{\prime})
where
M^{\prime}
is a strict subobject of
M
(including
M^{\prime}=M
).

Proof.

a) For any
\theta\in\overline{{\mathcal{U}}}(M)
we have
\theta(\overline{M})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
\overline{M}
of
M
. So
\theta+\varepsilon\eta\in{\mathcal{U}}(M)
for all
\varepsilon>0
. These converge to
\theta
so,
\theta
is in the closure of
{\mathcal{U}}(M)
.

c) If
\theta\in\partial{\mathcal{U}}(M)
then
\theta(\overline{M})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
\overline{M}
of
M
and equality holds for at least one such
\overline{M}
. Taking
\overline{M}
to be minimal we see that
\theta\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(\overline{M})
.

(b) and (d) are proved analogously.
∎

Theorem 2.20
.

{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
is constant on path component of the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{t}
,
0\leq t\leq 1
, be a continuous path in the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
. If
M\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})
and
M\notin{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
then
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{U}}(M)
and
\theta_{1}\notin{\mathcal{U}}(M)
. So, the path
\theta_{t}
must pass through
\partial{\mathcal{U}}(M)
which is a union of walls, a contradiction. So,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
as claimed.
∎

Theorem 2.21
.

If
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}
lie in distinct path components of the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
then
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})\neq{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
.

Proof.

Take the linear path
\theta_{t}=t\theta_{1}+(1-t)\theta_{0}
. Since
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}
are in different path components, the path crosses some wall, say
\theta_{t_{0}}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. Take
X
to be minimal. Then
\theta_{t_{0}}\in int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. There are two cases. Either the path is parallel to this wall or it crosses the wall transversely.

In the second case we may assume, by symmetry, that
\theta_{0}(X)<0
and
\theta_{1}(X)>0
. So,
X\notin{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})
. We claim that
X\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
. Suppose not. Then
\theta_{1}(X^{\prime})\leq 0
for some nonzero strict quotient
X^{\prime}
of
X
. Take
X^{\prime}
minimal. Then
\theta_{1}(X^{\prime\prime})>0
for all strict quotients
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X
. But
\theta_{t_{0}}(X^{\prime})>0
since
\theta_{t_{0}}\in int\,D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. Since the path
\theta_{t}
is linear, there is some
t_{1}\in(t_{0},1]
so that
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime})=0
and
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime\prime})>0
for all proper strict quotients
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X^{\prime}
. Then
\theta_{t_{1}}\in D(X^{\prime})
contradicting the minimality of
X
.

Suppose now that the path is parallel to the wall
D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. Then we have
\theta_{0}(X)=0=\theta_{1}(X)
. Since
\theta_{1}
does not lie on any wall, there must be some strict subobject
X^{\prime}
of
X
so that
\theta_{1}(X^{\prime})>0
. Take
X^{\prime}
minimal. Since
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})<0
, there must be some
t<s<1
so that
\theta_{s}(X^{\prime})=0
. By minimality of
X^{\prime}
we have
\theta_{1}(Z)\leq 0
for any
Z\subset X^{\prime}
. So,
\theta_{s}(Z)\leq 0
giving
X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{s})
contradicting the minimality of
X
.

Since
\theta_{1}(X^{\prime})>0
and
\theta_{1}(Z)\leq 0
for all strict
Z\subset X^{\prime}
, we have
\theta_{1}(X^{\prime}/Z)>0
for all such
Z
making
X^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
. But
\theta_{0}(X^{\prime})<0
. So,
X^{\prime}\notin{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})
. So
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})\neq{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
.
∎

Remark 2.22
.

The proof of Theorem
2.21
shows that the path components of the complement of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
are convex. Otherwise the straight line connecting two points would pass through some walls making the value of
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t})
different at the two endpoints contradicting Theorem
2.20
.

Remark
2.22
can be formalize into the following theorem.

Theorem 2.23
.

Given
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
if and only if the following are satisfied.

(1)

The linear path
\theta_{t}=t\theta_{1}+(1-t)\theta_{0}
does not cross any walls transversely in its interior. By this we mean that, if
\theta_{t}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
for some
0<t<1
then the entire path
\theta_{t}
lies in the hyperplane perpendicular to
\undim M
. I.e.,
\theta_{t}(M)=0
for all
t\in[0,1]
.

(2)

If
\theta_{0}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(L)
for some
L\in{\mathcal{G}}
then

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(L)|_{t=0}\leq 0.

(3)

If
\theta_{1}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(R)
for some
R\in{\mathcal{G}}
then

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(R)|_{t=0}\geq 0.

Proof.

As observed in Remark
2.22
, if the path
\theta_{t}
does not cross any walls transversely, then
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t})
is constant for
0<t<1
. For
t=0
, and object
L
would be added to
\theta_{0}
if the wall
D_{\mathcal{G}}(L)
was oriented in the other direction (so that
\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(L)|_{t=0}>0
). This not being the case, we have
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1/2})
. Similarly for the case
t=1
. So,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t})
is constant for
t\in[0,1]
assuming
(1),(2),(3)
.

Conversely, suppose that the path
\theta_{t}
crosses some wall
D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
at time
t=t_{0}
. Then
X
or some strict quotient of
X
would be added to
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t})
for
t>t_{0}
(or for
t<t_{0}
) depending on the sign of
\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(X)|_{t=t_{0}}
. So,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})\neq{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})
if any of the conditions
(1),(2),(3)
were violated.
∎

Similarly we have the following.

Theorem 2.24
.

Given
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in V_{\Lambda}
,
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{0})={\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{1})
if and only if the following are satisfied.

(1)

The linear path
\theta_{t}=t\theta_{1}+(1-t)\theta_{0}
does not cross any walls transversely in its interior.

(2)

If
\theta_{0}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(L)
for some
L\in{\mathcal{G}}
then

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(L)|_{t=0}\geq 0.

(3)

If
\theta_{1}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(R)
for some
R\in{\mathcal{G}}
then

\frac{d}{dt}\theta_{t}(R)|_{t=0}\leq 0.

Corollary 2.25
.

For
\theta
on any thin wall,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
are constant.

Corollary 2.26
.

Given any pseudo-torsion class
{\mathcal{P}}
, the set
S({\mathcal{P}})
of all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}
is convex. Similarly, for any pseudo-torsionfree class
{\mathcal{Q}}
, the set
T({\mathcal{Q}})
of all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)={\mathcal{Q}}
is convex.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in S({\mathcal{P}})
. Then Theorem
2.23
implies that, for all
t\in[0,1]
,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t})={\mathcal{P}}
. Therefore,
S({\mathcal{P}})
is convex. Similarly,
T({\mathcal{Q}})
is convex by Theorem
2.24
.
∎

Theorem 2.28
.

\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is the pseudo-torsion class generated by
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. More precisely, every object
M
of
\overline{P}(\theta)
has a unique strict subobject
M_{0}
which lies in
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
so that the quotient
M/M_{0}
is an object of the pseudo-wide subcategory
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. Equivalently,

{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)^{\perp}\cap\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)={\mathcal{W}}(\theta).

In particular, this implies that
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is the pseudo-torsion class generated by
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
.

Proof.

Let
M
be an object of
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
which is not in
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
. Then,
\theta(M^{\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
M^{\prime}
of
M
. If
M\notin{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
then equality must hold for some nonzero strict quotient
M^{\prime}
of
M
. Take
M^{\prime}
maximal with this property. Then
\theta(M^{\prime})=0
and
\theta(M^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients
M^{\prime\prime}
of
M^{\prime}
. So,
M^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. If
M^{\prime}=M
we are done. Otherwise
\theta(M)>0
by maximality of
M^{\prime}
. Let
M_{0}\rightarrowtail M
be the kernel of
M\twoheadrightarrow M^{\prime}
. Then
\theta(M_{0})=\theta(M)>0
, for any proper strict subobject
N^{\prime}\rightarrowtail M_{0}
we have that
M/N^{\prime}
is a strict quotient of
M
larger than
M^{\prime}
. So,
\theta(M/N^{\prime})=\theta(M_{0}/N^{\prime})>0
. So,
M_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
giving the desired strict filtration
M_{0}\rightarrowtail M\twoheadrightarrow M^{\prime}
with
M_{0}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
M^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
.
∎

Corollary 2.29
.

For
\theta
on a thin wall
D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
,
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
is a minimal extension of
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
with extending object
X
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathcal{P}}
be a pseudo-torsion class in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)\subseteq{\mathcal{P}}\subseteq\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
. Then either
X\in{\mathcal{P}}
or it isn’t. If
X\in{\mathcal{P}}
then
{\mathcal{P}}=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)
since this is generated by
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
X
. If
X\notin{\mathcal{P}}
then every object
P\in{\mathcal{P}}
must lie in
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
since
P
has a filtration
P_{0}\hookrightarrow P\twoheadrightarrow W
where
W\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. Since this is a thin wall,
W
must be an iterated self-extension of
X
. So,
W
, being a strict quotient of
P
, must be 0.
∎

Every pseudo-wall has a negative or “green” side and a positive or “red” side. We also need to consider “virtual green walls” and “virtual red walls” which are defined as follows.

Definition 2.30
.

By
virtual green walls
we mean the set of all points
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
which do not lie on any wall so that, for all
\varepsilon>0
the set of points
\theta+t\eta
for
\eta=(1,1,\cdots,1)
crosses some wall for
t\in(0,\varepsilon)
. This means in particular that
\theta
lies in the closure of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
denote the union of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
and the set of virtual green walls. The
virtual red walls
are defined similarly. These are points
\theta
not on any wall so that
\theta-t\eta
crosses the set of wall for
t>0
arbitrarily small. Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
be the union of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathcal{G}})
and the set of virtual red walls.

Both sides, together with the virtual walls will be collected into disjoint unions of path connected subsets which we call “green components” and “red components”. Thus the set
{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
will have a “green partition” and
{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
will have a “red partition” which we can now construct.

Definition 2.31
.

Let
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
. Then we define the
green component
of
\theta_{0}
to be the set of all
\theta\in{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
so that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})
. The
red component
of any
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
is defined similarly: It is the set of all
\theta\in{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
so that
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)={\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{0})
. (Equivalently, by Theorem
2.17
,
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta_{0})
.) When the green component containing
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
contains all points
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
with
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})
, we call the green component an
infinitesmal green chamber
.
Infinitesmal red chambers
are defined analogously. They are the red components of some
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
which contain all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta)=\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta_{0})
.

We need to verify the following.

Theorem 2.32
.

Let
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
. Then the green component of
\theta_{0}
is path connected. Similarly, the red component of any
\theta_{0}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{+}({\mathcal{G}})
is path connected.

Proof.

Let
\theta_{0},\theta_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
be in the same green component, i.e,
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{0})={\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{1})={\mathcal{P}}
. (By definition of green component we have that
{\mathcal{P}}\neq{\mathcal{G}}
.) Then, by Theorem
2.23
, the straight line
\theta_{t}=t\theta_{1}+(1-t)\theta_{0}
lies in the set
S({\mathcal{P}})
of all
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
so that
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)={\mathcal{P}}
. But this line segment might not lie in
{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
.

By Theorem
2.23
,
{\mathcal{P}}(\gamma_{t}(s))={\mathcal{P}}
for all
0\leq s\leq s_{0}
. So,
\gamma_{t}(s_{0})
is a path in
{\mathcal{W}}_{-}({\mathcal{G}})
from
\theta_{0}
to
\theta_{1}
. Using the fact that
S({\mathcal{P}})
is convex, one can show that this path is continuous. The argument for red components is similar.
∎

3.
Harder-Narasimhan stratification

3.1.
Green paths and FHO sequences

We show that “green paths” satisfy a “strict forward Hom-orthogonality” condition. This will lead to a “strict Harder-Narasimhan stratification” of our torsion class
{\mathcal{G}}
.

Definition 3.1
.

By a
smooth green path
in
V_{\Lambda}
we mean a smooth path
\theta_{t}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, satisfying the following conditions.

(1)

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\theta_{t}(M)>0
whenever
\theta_{t}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(M)
, i.e., whenever
\theta_{t}(M)=0
and
\theta_{t}(M^{\prime})\leq 0
for all strict subobjects
M^{\prime}
of
M
.

(2)

For sufficiently large
t
, we have
\theta_{t}(M)>0
for all
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

(3)

For sufficiently large
t
, we have
\theta_{-t}(M)<0
for all
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
.

A
linear green path
is a path of the form
\theta_{t}=\theta_{0}+t\eta
for
\eta
any real vector with all coordinated being positive.

Definition 3.2
.

For any stability condition
\theta
, we recall that
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
is the set of all objects
X
in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
\theta\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. Let
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta)
be the set of all
X
in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
so that no proper strict subobject of
X
is in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
. Equivalently,
\theta
is in the interior of
D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
.

Note that every object
X\in{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta)
is a pseudo-brick. (Every nonzero strict endomorphism of
X
is an automorphism of
X
.)

Lemma 3.3
.

Suppose
\theta_{t}
is a green path and
\theta_{t_{0}}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(X)
. Then
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})>0
for all
t>t_{0}
and all nonzero strict quotients
X^{\prime}
of
X
including
X^{\prime}=X
.

Proof.

By Definition
3.1
(1),
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})>0
for
t=t_{0}+\varepsilon
for small
\varepsilon>0
for all nonzero strict quotients
X^{\prime}
of
X
, including
X^{\prime}=X
.

To show that
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})>0
for all
t>t_{0}
and all nonzero strict quotients
X^{\prime}
of
X
, suppose not. Then, we have
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})\leq 0
for some
t>t_{0}
and some
X^{\prime}
. Since the set of all dimension vectors of such
X^{\prime}
is finite, the set of such
t
is a closed subset of the interval
(t_{0},1]
which does not have
t_{0}
as a limit point. So, there is a minimum such
t
, call it
t_{1}>t_{0}
. Then
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime})=0
and
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime\prime})\geq 0
for all other strict quotients
X^{\prime\prime}
of
X
. Therefore,
\theta_{t_{1}}\in D_{\mathcal{G}}(X^{\prime})
. By Definition
3.1
(1),
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})
is strictly increasing at
t=t_{1}
. So,
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})<0
for
t=t_{1}-\varepsilon
contradicting the minimality of
t_{1}
. This proves the lemma.
∎

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\theta_{t}
be a smooth green path and let
t_{0}<t_{1}
. Then, there are no nonzero strict morphisms
X_{0}\to X_{1}
where
X_{i}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{i}})
. Similarly, there are no nonzero strict morphisms between nonisomorphic objects of
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
.

Proof.

Suppose
f:X_{0}\to X_{1}
is a nonzero strict morphism. Then, its image
I
is a nonzero strict quotient of
X_{0}
and a strict subobject of
X_{1}
. Since
\theta_{t_{0}}\in D(X_{0})
,
\theta_{t_{0}}(X_{0})=0
and
\theta_{t_{0}}(I)\geq 0
. By the lemma,
\theta_{t_{1}}(I)>0
. But,
\theta_{t_{1}}(I)\leq 0
since
I
is a strict subobject of
X_{1}
. This is a contradiction.

Now suppose there is a nonzero strict morphism
f:X_{0}\to X_{1}
where
X_{0},X_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
are nonisomorphic. Then,
\ker f,\im f,\coker f
lie in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
by Theorem
2.13
. By definition of
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
, we must have
X_{0}=\im f=X_{1}
contradicting the assumption that
X_{0}\not\cong X_{1}
.
∎

Using the following definition we can rephrase Theorem
3.4
.

Definition 3.5
.

By a
strict forward Hom-orthogonal (FHO) sequence
of pseudo-bricks we mean a set
{\mathcal{B}}
of pseudo-bricks together with a indexing function
\pi:{\mathcal{B}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
so that there are no nonzero strict morphisms
B\to B^{\prime}
(except for isomorphisms) if
\pi(B)\leq\pi(B^{\prime})
.

Theorem
3.4
can be rephrased by saying that a smooth green path
\theta_{t}
gives a strict FHO sequence, namely, the collection of all pseudo-bricks in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
for all
t
with indexing function
\pi(B)=t
if
B\in{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
. In the next subsection we will show that this strict FHO sequence is maximal.

3.2.
HN-stratifications

We show that each smooth green path
\theta_{t}
for a torsion class
{\mathcal{G}}
gives a “strict HN-stratification” of
{\mathcal{G}}
that is minimal and conclude that the corresponding “strict Hom-orthogonal sequence” is maximal.

Definition 3.6
.

By a
strict filtration
of an object
X\in{\mathcal{G}}
we mean a sequence of cofibrations:

X_{0}\rightarrowtail X_{1}\rightarrowtail\cdots\rightarrowtail X_{n}=X.

By Theorem
1.13
, this is equivalent to an increasing sequence
X_{1}\subset X_{2}\subset\cdots\subset X_{n}=X
where each
X_{i}
is a strict subobject of
X
. Then
X_{i}\rightarrowtail X_{j}
is strict for all
i<j
. The subquotients
X_{j}/X_{j-1}
are strict subquotients of
X
by Theorem
1.13
.

Lemma 3.7
.

Any object
X\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
has a strict filtration having strict subquotients in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta)
.

Proof.

If
X
is not in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta)
, it has a subobject
K
so that
\theta(X/K)=0
. Then
\theta(K)=0
. So
K,X/K\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
and, by induction on length,
K,X/K
have filtrations with subquotients in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta)
. Pulling back the filtration of
X/K
to
X
, we obtain the desired filtration of
X
.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\theta_{t}
be a smooth green path. Then, for any object
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
, there is a sequence of real numbers
t_{1}>t_{2}>\cdots>t_{m}
and a filtration of
M
by strict subobjects

M=M_{0}\supset M_{1}\supset M_{2}\supset\cdots\supset M_{m}=0

in
{\mathcal{G}}
so that
M_{k-1}/M_{k}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{k}})
. Furthermore, this filtration can be refined: Each
M_{k}
has a filtration by strict objects:

M_{k0}=M_{k+1}\subset M_{k1}\subset\cdots\subset M_{kn}=M_{k}

so that the quotients of consecutive terms lie in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{k+1})
.

Proof.

First note that there are only finitely many real numbers
t
having the property that
\theta_{t}(X)=0
for some strict quotient
X
of
M
. This is because the condition
\theta_{t}(X)=0
depends only on the dimension vector of
X
and, although
M
may have infinitely many strict quotients, there are only finitely many dimension vectors of strict quotients. In this finite set, let
t_{1}
be maximal so that
\theta_{t_{1}}(X)=0
for some nonzero strict quotient
X
of
M
. Such
t_{1}
exists since
\theta_{t}(X)>0
for
t>>0
and
\theta_{t}(X)<0
for
t<<0
.

Then,
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime})\geq 0
for all other strict quotients
X^{\prime}
of
M
since, if
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime})<0
, then
\theta_{t}(X^{\prime})=0
for some
t>t_{1}
contradicting the maximality of
t_{1}
.

Let
X_{1}
be a maximal strict quotient of
M
such
\theta_{t_{1}}(X_{1})=0
and let
M_{1}
be the kernel of the epimorphism
M\twoheadrightarrow X_{1}
. Then
\theta_{t_{1}}(X^{\prime})\geq 0
for all strict quotients of
X_{1}
since every strict quotient of
X_{1}
is a strict quotient of
M
. So,
X_{1}=M/M_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
. If
M_{1}=0
, we have the desired filtration of
M
with only one term. So, we are done.

If
M_{1}\neq 0
then, by maximality of
X_{1}
we have
\theta_{t_{1}}(M_{1})=\theta_{t_{1}}(M)>0
. Also, we have
\theta_{t_{1}}(Y)>0
for all nonzero strict quotients of
M_{1}
since
M
modulo the kernel of
M_{1}\twoheadrightarrow Y
is a strict quotient of
M
larger than
X_{1}
. Thus,
M_{1}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
.

Let
t_{2}<t_{1}
be maximal so that
\theta_{t_{2}}(Y)=0
for some nonzero strict quotient
Y
of
M_{1}
. Let
X_{2}
be a maximal strict quotient of
M_{1}
so that
\theta_{t_{2}}(X_{2})=0
and let
M_{2}
be the kernel of
M_{1}\to X_{2}
. Then
X_{2}=M_{1}/M_{2}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
and
M_{2}\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{2}})
. Continuing in this way, we get the first filtration of
M
.

The second filtration follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

This implies that we have a “strict HN-stratification” of
{\mathcal{G}}
in the following sense.

Definition 3.9
.

By a
strict HN-stratification
of
{\mathcal{G}}
we mean a family of pseudo-wide subcategories
{\mathcal{W}}_{s},s\in{\mathcal{S}}
of
{\mathcal{G}}
parametrized by a totally ordered set
{\mathcal{S}}
satisfying the following.

(1)

There are no nonzero strict morphism from
X\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s_{0}}
to
Y\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s_{1}}
if
s_{0}<s_{1}
.

(2)

Every object
M
of
{\mathcal{G}}
has a strict filtration

M=M_{0}\supset M_{1}\supset M_{2}\supset\cdots\supset M_{m-1}\supset M_{m}=0

so that
M_{k-1}/M_{k}
lies in
{\mathcal{W}}_{s_{k}}
where
s_{1}>s_{2}>\cdots>s_{m}
. We call this the
strict HN-filtration
of
M
with respect to the strict HN-stratification
{\mathcal{W}}_{s},s\in{\mathcal{S}}
.

The pseudo-wide subcategories
{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
are called the
slices
of the HN-stratification.

Lemma 3.10
.

Let
f:M\to N
be a strict morphism and let
X
be a subobject of the kernel of
f
. Then the induced map
\overline{f}:M/X\to N
is strict.

Proof.

Since
f,\overline{f}
have the same image, we may assume they are both onto. Then
f:M\twoheadrightarrow N
is a strict epimorphism with kernel, say
K
and
X\subseteq K
. By Lemma
1.5
,
K/X\rightarrowtail M/X
is a cofibration. So, the quotient map
M/X\twoheadrightarrow(M/X)/(K/X)\cong M/K\cong N
is strict.
∎

Lemma 3.11
.

Let
M
have strict HN-filtration as given above. Then there is no nonzero strict morphism from
M
to any
Y\in{\mathcal{W}}_{s}
where
s>s_{1}
.

Proof.

Any strict morphism
f:M\to Y
must be 0 on
M_{k-1}
since
s_{k}<s_{1}<s
. The induced map
\overline{f}:M/M_{k-1}\to Y
is strict by Lemma
3.10
. By induction on
k
,
\overline{f}=0
.
∎

Proposition 3.12
.

The strict HN-filtration of
M
with respect to a fixed strict HN-stratification of
{\mathcal{G}}
is unique.

Proof.

Given a strict HN-filtration of
M
as in Definition
3.9
,
let
N=M
with another strict HN-filtration

N=N_{0}\supseteq N_{1}\supseteq N_{2}\supseteq\cdots\supseteq N_{n-1}\supseteq N_{n}=0

with
N_{j-1}/N_{j}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{t_{j}}
. We claim that
t_{1}=s_{1}
. Otherwise, we have an inequality, say
t_{1}>s_{1}
. By Lemma
3.11
this would imply that there are no strict morphisms
M\to N/N_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{t_{1}}
. In other words,
M\subset N_{1}
a contradiction since
M=N
.

So,
t_{1}=s_{1}
. Then, the same argument shows that
N_{1}\subseteq M_{1}
and
M_{1}\subseteq N_{1}
. So,
N_{1}=M_{1}
. By induction on
n,m
we get
N_{k}=M_{k}
for all
k
and
n=m
. So, these two filtrations of
M
are the same and the filtration is unique.
∎

Theorem 3.13
.

Given a smooth green path
\theta_{t}
and
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, an object
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
lies in
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
if and only if the strict HN-filtration of
M
has strict subquotients in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
for
t<t_{0}
.

Proof.

This follows from the proof of Theorem
3.8
. Given
M\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
, we have that
\theta_{t_{0}}(X)>0
for any nonzero strict quotient
X
of
M
. So,
\theta_{t_{1}}(X)=0
implies that
t_{1}<t_{0}
. The other
t_{i}
are
<t_{1}
. So, all strict subquotients of
M
lie in some
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{i}})
where
t_{i}\leq t_{1}<t_{0}
.

Conversely, suppose
M\notin{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
. Then
\theta_{t_{0}}(X)\leq 0
for some nonzero strict quotient
X_{1}
of
M
. So, there is some
t_{1}\geq t_{0}
so that
\theta_{t_{1}}(X_{1})=0
for some strict quotient
X_{1}
of
M
. As in the proof of Theorem
3.8
, we take
X_{1}
maximal and obtain
X_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
. This proves the theorem.
∎

To obtain the analogous theorem for
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
we need to build the strict HN-filtration of any
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
from the bottom up.

Lemma 3.14
.

For any
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
, let
t_{1}
be minimal so that
M
has a strict subobject
Y
with
\theta_{t_{1}}(Y)=0
. The maximal such
Y
is the bottom layer of the unique strict HN-filtration of
M
, i.e.,
M/Y
has HN-filtration with strict subsquotients in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
for
t>t_{1}
.

Proof.

Minimality of
t_{1}
implies that
Y\in{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
. Maximality of
Y
implies that the other subquotients of
M
must lie in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
for
t>t_{1}
.
∎

Theorem 3.15
.

Let
\theta_{t_{0}}
be a point on a smooth green path. Then an object
M\in{\mathcal{G}}
lies in
{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
if and only if the strict HN-filtration of
M
has strict subquotients in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
for
t>t_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
M\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
. Then
\theta_{t_{0}}(Y)<0
for all nonzero strict subobjects
Y
of
M
. So,
\theta_{t_{1}}(Y)=0
implies
t_{1}>t_{0}
. By Lemma
3.14
, the bottom of the strict HN-filtration of
M
lies in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
for
t_{1}>t_{0}
. The other strict subquotient lie in higher slices
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t})
for
t>t_{1}
. Conversely, suppose that the bottom subquotient
Y
of
M
lies in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta_{t_{1}})
where
t_{1}>t_{0}
. By minimality of
t_{1}
, this implies that
\theta_{t_{0}}(Y)<0
for all nonzero strict subquotients
Y
of
M
. So,
M\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta_{t_{0}})
.
∎

Corollary 3.16
.

Let
\theta\in V_{\Lambda}
not lie on any pseudo-wall. Then every object
M
of
{\mathcal{G}}
has a unique strict filtration
tM\rightarrowtail M\twoheadrightarrow fM
where
tM\in{\mathcal{P}}(\theta)
and
fM\in{\mathcal{Q}}(\theta)
.

Proof.

This is Theorem
1.23
. We are just observing that it also follows from Theorem
3.15
above.
∎

We use the strict HN-filtration of any object of
{\mathcal{G}}
to obtain a maximality result for Theorem
3.4
. Recall that, for any smooth green path
\theta_{t}
, the collection
{\mathcal{B}}
of all pseudo-bricks in all
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
with indices
\pi(B)=t
for
B\in{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t})
forms a strict FHO sequence.

Theorem 3.17
.

This FHO collection of pseudo-bricks is maximal in the sense that no new brick
B_{0}
can be added to the set with index
\pi(B_{0})=t_{0}
for some
t_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
without destroying the FHO property.

Proof.

Suppose we attempt to add a pseudo-brick
B_{0}
with
\pi(B_{0})=t_{0}
. Then we will show that
B_{0}
is isomorphic to an element of
{\mathcal{B}}
with index
t_{0}
.

By Theorem
3.8
, there is a strict filtration of
B_{0}
with strict quotient
B_{1}
with index
t_{1}
and strict subobject
B_{n}
with index
t_{n}
where
t_{n}\leq t_{1}
. The strict morphism
B_{0}\to B_{1}
indicates that
t_{1}\leq t_{0}
and the morphism
B_{n}\to B_{0}
indicates that
t_{0}\leq t_{n}
. Then
t_{0}=t_{1}=t_{n}
and all strict subquotients of
B_{0}
are in
{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t_{0}})
. Since
B_{0}
is a pseudo-brick, there is only one layer and
B_{0}=B_{1}\in{\mathcal{W}}_{0}(\theta_{t_{0}})
as claimed.
∎

The same proof shows the following.

Theorem 3.18
.

The collection of bricks
{\mathcal{B}}
described above is minimal for the HN-stratification of
{\mathcal{G}}
in the sense that, if any element
B_{0}\in{\mathcal{B}}
were to be deleted, the rest of

4.
Resolution of thick walls

This is a preview of our next paper
[
IM
]
in which we discuss maximal green sequences given by green paths and resolve the problem that arises when the green path goes through thick walls.

The poset of pseudo-torsion classes in a torsion class
{\mathcal{G}}
forms a lattice since any intersection of pseudo-torsion classes is a pseudo-torsion class and any collection of pseudo-torsion classes generates a pseudo-torsion class. A
maximal green sequence
in
{\mathcal{G}}
is defined to be a maximal linearly order tower in this lattice. Given a green path
\theta_{t}
which only goes through thin walls
D_{\mathcal{G}}(B_{\beta})
at times
t_{\beta}
, we obtain a FHO-sequence
\{B_{\beta}\}
with
\pi(B_{\beta})=t_{\beta}
. This is a possibly countably infinite collection of pseudo-bricks. This gives a possibly continuum number of pseudo-torsion classes
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{\delta})
where
\delta\in{\mathbb{R}}
ranges though the Dedekind cuts in the countable family of real numbers
t_{\beta}
. This includes both the components of the complement of the set of
t_{\beta}
and the infinitesmal green and red side of each pseudo-wall, namely
{\mathcal{P}}(\theta_{t_{\beta}})
and
\overline{{\mathcal{P}}}(\theta_{t_{\beta}})
.

When a green path goes through a thick wall, such as the null domain for a tame hereditary algebra, it cannot distinguish between the objects in
{\mathcal{W}}(\theta)
since they all have the same (or proportional) dimension vectors. To separate these objects we need a new stability space which may have uncountable dimension. We will assume the cardinality of the underlying field
K
is at most that of
{\mathbb{R}}
. Then the set of isomorphism classes of finitely generated
\Lambda
-modules will have cardinality at most the continuum. Then we can find smooth green paths which pass through a possibly continuum number of walls at different times.

We take the pseudo-wide subcategory

4.2.
Level 2 thick walls

We need to arrange that the walls with pseudo-simple labels are quasi-thin. To do this, we choose a Hamel basis
{\mathcal{H}}
. This is a basis for
{\mathbb{R}}
as vector space over
{\mathbb{Q}}
. Hamel showed that the Hamel basis has the cardinality of the continuum
[
H
]
. See also
[
Ma
]
. By translation we may choose a Hamel basis in the unit interval
{\mathcal{H}}\subset[0,1]
. Since there are at most a continuum number of pseudo-simple objects
S_{\alpha}
, indexed by a set
{\mathcal{A}}=\{\alpha\}
, which give a basis for
\overline{K}_{0}{\mathcal{G}}
, (not counting the indecomposable objects of the form
nX
as in Example
2.5.1
), we can find a monomorphism
\lambda:{\mathcal{A}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{H}}
. Then, let
\widetilde{\theta}_{t}
be the linear green path in

given by
\theta_{t}(S_{\alpha})=t-\lambda(\alpha)
. Then we claim that, for each
\alpha\in{\mathcal{A}}
,
{\mathcal{W}}(\widetilde{\theta}_{\lambda(\alpha})
is a quasi-thin wall. The other wall have objects of dimension at least twice the minimum dimension of any
S_{\alpha}
. So, if we repeat the process the objects will have unbounded size and that will allow us to get, after a lot of effort, iterated refinements of any green path which will give a “maximal green sequence”, a maximal chain in the lattice of quasi-torsion classes in
{\mathcal{G}}
. Details will be in the next paper
