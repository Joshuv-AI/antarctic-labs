Theoretical and experimental investigation of NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3 nanoparticles to study their structural, magnetic, and polarization properties for energy-related applications
Authors: Hassan Ali, Samar Fatima, Tanvir Hussain, Khawar Ismail
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work presents a comparative theoretical and experimental investigation of spinel nickel ferrite (NiFe2O4) and perovskite calcium canganite (CaMnO3) nanoparticles synthesized via the sol-gel method. NiFe2O4 samples were calcined at temperatures ranging from 400 to 1000 °C, and the CaMnO3 samples were calcined at temperatures ranging from 300 to 600 °C. X-ray diffraction (XRD) confirmed the formation of both materials, and the highest peak was observed at 2θ = 35.816° for NiFe2O4 and 2θ = 32.45° for CaMnO3, with Miller planes (311) and (121), respectively. Using the Scherrer formula, the best crystalline size of nickel iron oxide was found to be 25.03 nm, and of calcium manganese oxide was 14.65 nm. Structural properties were also calculated theoretically using the Quantum Espresso code and compared with the XRD results. Moreover, field-emission scanning electron microscopy (FESEM) was used for morphological analysis, the results were further processed to calculate the particle size distribution. Polarization and magnetic properties were analyzed using a multiferroic testing machine and a vibrating sample magnetometer (VSM), respectively. The hysteresis loop of NiFe2O4 showed a saturation magnetization of 29.61 emu/g, making it a strongly ferromagnetic material compared to CaMnO3, which had 1.71 emu/g.Keywords: Sol-gel technology, Quantum espresso, Ferromagnetic, PolarizationSubject terms: Chemistry, Materials science, Nanoscience and technology, Physics

Introduction

With the increasing reliance on renewable energy sources, various functional oxide nanomaterials have attracted significant attention of researchers due to their versatile structural, magnetic, dielectric, and electronic properties, which make them promising candidates for a wide range of technological applications1. Among these, spinel-ferrites and Perovskite-oxides represent two important and extensively studied material families owing to their structural stability and tunable physical properties2,3.

Spinel-ferrites with the general formula MFe2O4 (M = Ni, Co, Zn, Mn, etc.) are well known for their strong magnetic ordering, chemical robustness, and multifunctional behavior at the nano-scale. In particular, nickel ferrite (NiFe2O4) has been widely investigated due to its moderate saturation magnetization, low coercivity, and stability under thermal and chemical environments. These characteristics make NiFe2O4 relevant for applications in magnetic devices, electromagnetic shielding, and functional dielectric systems4. On the other hand, Perovskite-oxides with the general formula ABO₃ exhibit remarkable structural flexibility and rich electronic phenomena5. Calcium manganite (CaMnO3), an orthorhombic perovskite, has also been explored for applications in catalysis, thermoelectrics, and oxide electronics due to its stable crystal structure and temperature-dependent electrical behavior6.

Perovskite materials are a distinctive emerging class of oxide materials that display outstanding applications in energy storage devices because they possess both electric and magnetic properties7,8. Perovskites can be easily synthesized within a short time interval using a simple and less expensive methodology9. Equivalently, ferrites are important for several advantages owing to their exceptional electromagnetic abilities and advancing the scientific community side by side with Perovskites10.

Previous studies have demonstrated the potential of spinel perovskite oxides in energy storage applications11. For instance, Jundale et al. (2022) explored thin films of metal oxides such as NiM2O4 (M = Co, Fe) and MFe2O4 (M = Zn, Mn, Cu) as super-capacitors12. Kesic et al. (2016) investigated the catalytic activity of complex calcium-based oxides and other metals, having the general formula CaxMyOz (M = Ti, Mn, Zr, Fe), CaMnO3, CaZrO3, CaTiO3, and Ca2Fe2O513–15. Anwar et al. (2011) explained in detail that the synthesis route also affects the electrochemical behavior of capacitors. Anwar used different synthesis routes to check this16. Restrepo et al. (2017) studied NiFe2O4 having particle sizes of 14 nm, 21 nm, and 31 nm at room temperature when a magnetic field was present and in the absence of the magnetic field17,18. Dey et al. Studied the current-voltage curve and explained the hysteresis and electrical memory effects19,20.

In the present research, a comparative theoretical and experimental study of spinel NiFe2O4 and perovskite CaMnO3 nanoparticles is presented. The materials are synthesized via the same sol-gel route and calcinated at controlled conditions. Structural properties are studied using the XRD technique and theoretically by using first principles DFT. Morphological properties are studied using the field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM). Moreover, the magnetic and polarization measurements are taken using vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM) and a ferroelectric setup. The research will help to investigate the applications of these materials in energy storage devices and other types of functional devices by providing an indirect insight into the magnetic and dielectric response of two different oxide systems.

Experimental details

Analytical grade Nickel nitrate hexahydrate (Ni(NO3)2.6H2O), Iron nitrate hexahydrate, Citric acid, Calcium chloride dihydrate, Manganese chloride tetrahydrate, and Ammonia hydroxide were purchased from Sigma Aldrich21.

The synthesis of NiFe2O4 was carried out using a 1 M solution of precursor nickel nitrate and iron nitrate. The molar solutions were prepared by dissolving the respective salts in 50 mL of distilled water in two separate beakers. 9.71 g of citric acid (1:1 with respect to precursors) was also dissolved in distilled water. All three solutions were then mixed and are magnetically stirred by keeping the temperature and speed of the stirrer at a medium. At this initial stage, the total volume of solution was 150 mL, the pH of the solution was 1, and the temperature was less than 30 °C. Then, ammonia hydroxide was added to maintain the pH of the solution at 722. At a temperature of 85 °C, the solution gradually turned dark green, indicating the onset of gel formation. At 100 °C, a reddish-brown colored gel was observed, and after 15–20 min, the brown gel was burnt. Combustion resulted in a powder residue identified as nickel ferrite. The leftovers were kept in a furnace in ceramic crucibles, and the temperature of the furnace was 400 °C for calcination. Sample 1 was obtained at 400 °C by keeping nickel iron oxide for 2 h in the furnace. Similarly, sample 2 was obtained at 600 °C, sample 3 at 800 °C, and sample 4 at 1000 °C23. The flow diagram is shown in Fig. 1.

Flow diagram of sol-gel synthesis of NiFe2O4.

For CaMnO3, 7.33 g of calcium chloride dihydrate are dissolved in 50 mL of Isopropanol. 9.88 g of manganese chloride tetrahydrate were dissolved in 50 mL of distilled water. Both solutions were mixed in another beaker and put on a magnetic stirrer. The stirrer was turned on at medium speed and temperature. After ten minutes, the color of the solution started changing, and soon it began evaporating. By adding a few drops of ammonia hydroxide to maintain the pH of the solution at 6. At 80 °C, the solution was left with almost half. At 100 °C, a gel clearly formed, as shown in Fig. 2. It was kept in the oven for 2 h to remove excess water vapor. Above 100 °C gel started solidifying, and the color of the gel became pink24,25.

Photographic representation of the sol-gel synthesis process of CaMnO3.

The gel was dried, and its color became gray to black, and it was ready for calcination. Calcium manganese oxide was obtained, and the temperature of the furnace was set at 300 °C. Calcium manganese oxide was kept in a ceramic crucible in the furnace to make for 1 h. After one hour, sample 1 was formed at 300 °C. In the same way temperature of the furnace was set at 400 °C, 500 °C, and 600 °C to prepare samples 2, 3, and 4 of calcium manganese oxide, respectively26–28.

XRD analysis

Physical analysis was carried out using an X-ray diffractometer on samples to calculate parameters such as compound name, crystal system, space group, space group number, lattice parameters, position, peaks list and peaks pattern, density and volume, and crystalline size. The diffraction peaks of the synthesized material by the sol-gel method matched with JCPD card no.01-088-0380, confirming the formation of NiFe2O432,33. The material has cubic symmetry, and the XRD patterns of NiFe2O4 exhibit the characteristic peaks corresponding to (220), (311), (400), (511), (440) planes, as shown in Fig. 7. Moreover, the material has space group Fd-3m having number 227 and lattice parameters a = 0.8353 nm, b = 0.83353 nm and c = 0.83353 nm, density calculated (g/cm3) = 5.52, and volume of cell (106 pm3) = 579.11. In Fig. 8, the graph of lattice parameters “a” of Nickel iron oxide at different temperatures was plotted. At low temperatures such as 400 °C, 600 °C, and 800 °C, the lattice parameter “a” remained the same. But at high temperature, it increased from 8.3353 Å to 8.340 Å. This increase occurs due to thermal expansion34, as in the FESEM results, voids can be seen between particles, which allow lattice parameters to grow35,36.

Morphological analysis

For morphological study, field emission scanning electron microscope (FESEM) was used to describe the particle’s shape and distribution of particles. Nanoparticles of both NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3 exhibited irregular morphology and significant agglomeration, as observed in Figs. 11 and 12, respectively. The high resolution of FESEM down to the nanometer scale makes images even clearer and less distorted. There were many reasons for agglomeration46. Such as nanoparticles were opposite in charge and attracted one another, particles were magnetic in nature, and so close that they got intermingled, also the agglomeration can be due to van der Waals attractive forces47, moreover the particles can be pore-free, which can cause agglomeration48. But in this case, as NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3 were prepared at a diversity of temperatures during calcination to synthesize particles at nano level, this heat treatment acted as a function of calcination temperature, and nanoparticles were agglomerated49. Image J software analyzed the FESEM results and particle size histogram of NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3 in Figs. 13 and 14, respectively. These particle size distributions obtained from FESEM are consistent with crystallite sizes calculated from XRD (25 nm for NiFe2O4 and 14 nm for CaMnO3), confirming the nanoscale nature of the synthesized material.

Magnetic analysis

To study the magnetic behavior of NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3, samples of both materials were analyzed using vibrating sample magnetometry (VSM). A magnetic field of ± 15 K Oe was applied, and values of the applied field were plotted along the x-axis and magnetization along the y-axis. M-H hysteresis was obtained. The hysteresis curve for NiFe2O4 is shown in Fig. 15(a). The deep analysis of the M-H curve showed values of saturation magnetization (Ms=28.61 emu/g), remanence magnetization (Mr=8.10emu/g), and coercivity (Hc=-145.4 Oe) along the negative axis. The slim loop shows ferromagnetic behavior11. When the field was applied, magnetic domains arranged in a particular direction and retained magnetization even external field was removed, as can be observed from the loop. The hysteresis loop for CaMnO3 was drawn by plotting a graph between the magnetic field along the x-axis in the range from − 15,000 to 15,000 Oe. The Y axis had a scale from − 2.0 to 2.0 emu/g. When the field was applied, magnetization started, and it was maximum at 1.71 emu/g on the upside of the loop, and it was negative on the downside of the loop, as shown in Fig. 15(b). NiFe2O4 has greater saturation magnetization than CaMnO3 as shown in Table 2. NiFe2O4 exhibits stronger ferromagnetic behavior compared to CaMnO3. The squareness ratio S shows both materials fall in the signle domain, as the sqaureness ratio is too small, these materials in the range of Pseudo-Single Domain6,50.

P-E curves analysis

When a material is kept under the influence of an external electric field, polarization starts, and the ability to hold polarization is described as ferroelectric-like behavior due possible leakage current and lossy behavior. When the external field was removed, polarization could be reversed, and the P-E loop was obtained. To get the P-E loop for NiFe2O4, ferroelectric analysis was done by making pellets of NiFe2O4 with an area of 0.346 cm2 and a thickness of 1000 micrometers. Pallet pressing pressure was 100 MPa, and the pallets were sintered for 4 h at 1000 °C, moreover, the electrodes used were platinium coated with silver for high electrical conductivity. Using a radiant multiferroic testing machine operating at voltages of 10 V, 20 V, 30 V, and 40 V and a maximum electric field of 0.2 KV/cm, all the measurements of polarization were recorded. A graph plotted on Origin Lab between electric field and polarization, and a hysteresis loop displayed as P-E loop at 10–40 V in Fig. 16(a)51. From the P-E loop values of maximum polarization (Pm), remanent polarization (Pr), and coercivity (Ec) were noted in Table 3. NiFe2O4 showed P-E loops for 20 V, 30 V, and 40 V. At 20 V, maximum polarization was 5.07 × 10− 4 (µC/cm2), at 30 V, maximum polarization was 7.9 × 10− 4 (µC/cm2), and at 40 V, maximum polarization was 9.5 × 10− 4 (µC/cm2). The graph for CaMnO3 was plotted. An open P-E curve was obtained in Fig. 16(b) for a maximum field of 0.2 KV/cm. The values of Pm, Pr, and Ec were calculated. CaMnO3 also showed ferroelectric-like or polarization properties because of the leakage current, however CaMnO3 higher values of polarization at low voltages as compared to NiFe2O4. From the values of maximum polarization and Remanent polarization, the ability to store energy can be estimated52,53. However both the materials show very low polarization values as compared to typical Perovskite like BiFeO354. It can be concluded that NiFe2O4 showed polarization even at high voltages, but CaMnO3 only gave results at 10 V.

Conclusion

Nanoparticles of spinel nickel ferrite NiFe2O4 and perovskite CaMnO3 were synthesized using sol-gel method. XRD analysis confirmed the formation of both materials. The average crystallite size was calculated with the Scherrer formula. The average crystallite size of NiFe2O4 was 25.03 nm and of CaMnO3 was 14.65 nm. These materials were observed from a field emission scanning electron microscope using scales from micrometers to nanometers, using high resolutions and high magnification. FESEM images showed irregular, agglomerated nanoparticles. Magnetic and polarization characteristics of NiFe2O4 and CaMnO3 examined by VSM and multiferroic tester by measuring values of saturation magnetization, remanence magnetization, and coercivity of magnetic field, and maximum polarization, remnant polarization, and coercivity of the electric field. NiFe2O4 showed a saturation magnetization of 28.61 emu/g, on the other hand, that of CaMnO3 showed 1.71 emu/g which shows strong ferromagnetic behavior in NiFe2O4. Moreover, the P-E curves of both materials show that CaMnO3 have higher values of polarization at low voltages as compared to NiFe2O4. To conclude, it can be said that both materials show co-existence of magnetic and polarization characteristics, the study lacks the application of energy storage devices, however future study can be conducted based on these materials for energy storage devices.
