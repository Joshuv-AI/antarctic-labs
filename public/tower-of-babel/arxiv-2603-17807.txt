Emergent superconformal symmetry in the phase diagram of a 1D ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} lattice gauge theory
Bachana Beradze, Mikheil Tsitsishvili, Sergej Moroz

Abstract
We investigate the phase diagram and critical properties of a one-dimensional ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} lattice gauge theory describing an orthogonal metal, where spinless fermions and Ising spins are minimally coupled to a deconfined ℤ 2 \mathbb{Z}_{2} gauge field. Working at half-filling of fermions, we derive an exact gauge-invariant formulation that maps the model onto decoupled XXZ and transverse-field Ising chains. This mapping enables a controlled low-energy field-theory description in terms of a perturbed Luttinger liquid and Ising conformal field theories. Combining analytical arguments with numerical simulations, we determine the full phase diagram and identify various critical and multi-critical regimes. Along a specific multi-critical line, where the fermionic and bosonic velocities coincide, we find strong evidence for an emergent superconformal symmetry. Our results establish a minimal lattice realization of emergent superconformal criticalities in a gauge-matter system and provide a route toward its exploration in quantum simulators.

Introduction

Recent breakthroughs in quantum simulation with ultracold atoms in optical lattices, trapped ions, superconducting circuits, and programmable Rydberg-atom arrays have opened a new chapter in the study of quantum lattice gauge theories (LGTs), where local constraints and gauge–matter interactions can be engineered and probed directly in real time.
Such out-of-equilibrium settings are beyond the reach of many classical methods.

In this paper, we investigate quantum phases of a one-dimensional realization of the orthogonal metal problem.

II
The System

II.1
The model

We study a one-dimensional orthogonal metal, a model of interacting one-component fermions and spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
degrees of freedom that are coupled to
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field.
It is defined by the following Hamiltonian:

\begin{split}H=&-t\sum_{j}\left(f^{{\dagger}}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}f^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\
&+V\sum_{j}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&-J\sum_{j}\tau^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{z}_{j+1}-\Gamma\sum_{j}\tau^{x}_{j}.\end{split}

(1)

Here,
f^{{\dagger}}_{j}
and
f^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}
are the creation and annihilation operators of a spinless fermionic matter field, defined at each site
j
.
The composite
n_{j}=f^{{\dagger}}_{j}f^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}
is the fermion occupation number.
Additionally, we have a spin matter field
\tau^{x,y,z}_{j}
residing at every site.
The coupling between the spin and the fermionic matter fields is mediated by a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge degree of freedom
\sigma^{x,y,z}_{j+\ifrac{1}{2}}
, defined on links
j+\ifrac{1}{2}
, connecting sites
j
and
j+1
.
Both
\tau^{\alpha}
and
\sigma^{\alpha}
(
\alpha=x,y,z
) are mutually commuting sets of Pauli matrices, obeying a standard SU(2) algebra.
The Hamiltonian, Eq. (
1
), consists of a fermion and spin parts:
In the fermionic sector, the tunneling amplitude
t
minimally couples fermions and the gauge field through the Peierls substitution.
Fermions residing at the nearest-neighbor sites interact with the density-density interaction
V
.
The
\tau
-spin sector consists of a
\Gamma
magnetic field along the
x
-axis and an Ising type exchange interaction
J
, which minimally couples the spins and the gauge field through the Peierls substitution.
In the Hamiltonian Eq. (
1
), the gauge fields appear only via the minimal coupling, while the dynamical (electric) terms are not included. The gauge sector is thus in the deconfined regime.
We work at a particle-hole (PH) symmetric regime of a half-filled band for fermions, without imposing additional constraints on the spin matter field and the gauge degree of freedom.
The system is thought to be infinitely large unless specified otherwise.

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) is invariant under a local
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge transformation generated by

G_{j}=\sigma^{x}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{n_{j}}\tau^{x}_{j}\sigma^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

(2)

These generators act on the matter fields as

\begin{split}f_{j}\rightarrow f^{\prime}_{j}&=G_{j}^{-1}f_{j}G_{j}=-f_{j},\\
\tau^{y,z}_{j}\rightarrow\tau^{\prime y,z}_{j}&=G_{j}^{-1}\tau^{y,z}_{j}G_{j}=-\tau^{y,z}_{j},\end{split}

(3)

while

\sigma^{y,z}_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}\rightarrow\sigma^{\prime y,z}_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}=G_{j}^{-1}\sigma^{y,z}_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}G_{j}=-\sigma^{y,z}_{j\pm\frac{1}{2}}.

(4)

Due to the local gauge invariance, the Hilbert space is split into
2^{L}
distinct sectors characterized by
G_{j}=\pm 1
, each of which evolves independently.
We will work in the sector without static charges. Throughout the paper, we enforce the strict constraint
{G_{j}=1}
at every lattice site, known as the (even) Gauss law.

We solve the Gauss law for
\tau_{j}^{x}

\tau^{x}_{j}=\sigma^{x}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{n_{j}}\sigma^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(5)

and express Eq. (
1
) in terms of gauge-invariant degrees of freedom as

\begin{split}H=&-t\sum_{j}\left(c^{{\dagger}}_{j}Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\
&+V\sum_{j}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\\
&-J\sum_{j}Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-\Gamma\sum_{j}X_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{n_{j}}X_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(6)

with a new gauge-invariant fermionic field defined on sites

c_{j}\equiv f_{j}\tau^{z}_{j},\quad n_{j}\equiv c^{{\dagger}}_{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}=f^{{\dagger}}_{j}f^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}

(7)

and new gauge-invariant spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
Pauli operators defined on links

X_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\sigma^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\quad Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\equiv\tau^{z}_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\tau^{z}_{j+1}.

(8)

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) represents the main subject of study in this paper.

II.2
Symmetries

In a chain with
L
sites, the total number of fermions
{N=\sum_{j=1}^{L}c^{{\dagger}}_{j}c_{j}}
is conserved as a result of the global U(1) symmetry
{c_{j}\to e^{\mathrm{i}\alpha}c_{j}}
. In case of periodic boundary conditions, the model becomes translationally invariant.

Throughout the paper, we work at half-filling
{N=L/2}

{(L\in 2\mathbb{N})}
.
This case is of particular importance due to the appearance of the PH symmetry generated by:

\begin{split}Cc_{j}C^{-1}&=(-1)^{j}c_{j}^{{\dagger}},\\
CX_{j+\frac{1}{2}}C^{-1}&=(-1)^{j}X_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(9)

Note that in our model the PH symmetry acts not only on fermions but on spin degrees of freedom, which is indeed necessary to compensate the sign flip of the
\Gamma
term in the Hamiltonian Eq. (
6
).
The generator
C
acts in the Hilbert space as a unitary operator:

\displaystyle C

\displaystyle=C_{0}W_{0},\quad C^{2}=CC^{{\dagger}}=1,

(10)

\displaystyle C_{0}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{i}^{L}\prod_{j=1}^{L}\left(c_{j}^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}+(-1)^{j}c_{j}^{{\dagger}}\right),\quad W_{0}=\prod_{j=1}^{L/2}Z_{2j-\frac{1}{2}},

(11)

forming a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
group, which will be denoted by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
.

Additionally, the spin sector enjoys the so-called magnetic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetry, generated by:

W=\prod_{j=1}^{L}Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.

(12)

The
W
-transformation globally flips the sign of
X_{j+\ifrac{1}{2}}
. We denote magnetic symmetry group by
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{W}
.

III
Analytical treatment

III.1
Decoupling into XXZ and Ising Sectors

The Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) can be decomposed into a sum of mutually commuting, exactly solvable components: a spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
Heisenberg XXZ chain and quantum Ising (QI) models.
This must not be surprising in light of the model symmetries: the fermion sector has the U(1)
\otimes\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry, while the spin sector is
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{W}
invariant. Using non-local transformation (see Appendix
A
for details)

\begin{split}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j}=\frac{\mu^{x}_{j}-i\mu^{y}_{j}}{2}\prod^{j-1}_{k=1}&(-\mu^{z}_{k})\prod_{k=0}^{j-1}(-\mu^{z}_{k+\frac{1}{2}}),\\
X_{j+\frac{1}{2}}{=}\,\mu^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\prod^{j}_{k=1}(-\mu^{z}_{k}),&\quad Y_{j+\frac{1}{2}}{=}\,\mu^{y}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\prod^{j}_{k=1}(-\mu^{z}_{k}),\\
n_{j}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{z}_{j},&\quad Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}=\mu^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}.\end{split}

(13)

we arrive at the following Hamiltonian in the decoupled form:

H=H_{\text{XXZ}}+H_{\text{QI}},

(14)

where

H_{\text{XXZ}}=\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j}\left(\mu^{x}_{j}\mu^{x}_{j+1}+\mu^{y}_{j}\mu^{y}_{j+1}\right)+\\
\frac{V}{4}\sum_{j}\mu^{z}_{j}\mu^{z}_{j+1}

(15)

corresponds to a Heisenberg spin chain with spins residing on the sites, while

H_{\text{QI}}=\Gamma\sum_{j}\mu^{x}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-J\sum_{j}\mu^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},

(16)

describes Ising spins located on the links.

In what follows, we consider only the case of repulsive fermions
V>0
.
Without loss of generality, we also assume
{t,\Gamma,J\geq 0}
, and introduce the following dimensionless parameters:

\Delta\equiv\frac{V}{2t},\quad g\equiv\frac{\Gamma}{J},\quad\kappa\equiv\frac{\Gamma}{t},

(17)

where
\Delta
is the anisotropy parameter of the XXZ model, while
g
is the dimensionless Ising coupling.
Additionally, as elaborated in Sec.
III.2.1
,
\kappa
measures the ratio of excitation velocities in the two models.

It follows straightforwardly that the original lattice symmetries are mapped to the following transformations in terms of
\mu
-spins:

•

the particle number U(1) symmetry becomes spin rotation acting as:

\mu^{\pm}_{j}\to e^{\mp\mathrm{i}\alpha}\mu^{\pm}_{j};

(18)

•

the particle-hole
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry becomes spin inversion acting as:

\begin{split}(\mu^{x}_{j},\mu^{y}_{j},\mu^{z}_{j})\to(-\mu^{x}_{j},\mu^{y}_{j},-\mu^{z}_{j}),\end{split}

(19)

•

the magnetic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{W}
symmetry acts as:

W=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\mu^{z}_{j+\ifrac{1}{2}}.

(20)

III.2
The effective low energy description

The paradigmatic models of one-dimensional XXZ and QI chains have been extensively studied using various analytical
8
;
41
;
25
;
27
;
21
;
15
and numerical methods
29
;
13
.
Both models are exactly solvable
8
;
25
;
27
;
21
, with their phase diagram, the nature of the phase transitions, and all the correlation functions fully characterized.

The phase diagram of the XXZ model (or equivalently, of the interacting spinless fermion model in Eq. (
58
)) is well understood
25
;
27
;
21
: for
|\Delta|\leq 1
, the system remains critical, exhibiting power-law correlations with
\Delta
-dependent exponents, i.e., the Luttinger liquid (LL) phase.
At
\Delta=-1
, the XXZ chain undergoes a first-order transition into a ferromagnetic phase
(\Delta<-1)
with finite total magnetization.
At
\Delta=1
, on the contrary, the model undergoes a continuous quantum phase transition into a gapped Néel phase
(\Delta>1)
with spontaneously broken
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry.
This symmetry-broken phase hosts long-range charge-density-wave (CDW) order, corresponding to staggered magnetization in the spin representation.
The transition at
\Delta=1
belongs to the BKT universality class.
In this work, since we focus on repulsive spinless fermions
V\geq 0
, we restrict our attention to
\Delta\geq 0
.

Similarly, QI exhibits a phase transition upon crossing
g=1
. For
g>1
, the system develops a doubly-degenerate ferromagnetically ordered phase with nonzero spontaneous magnetization
\langle\mu^{x}_{i}\rangle\neq 0
oriented along
\pm x
.
For
g<1
, the system is paramagnetic with nonzero magnetization along the
+z
direction, which is usually called the disordered phase.

III.2.1
Effective Field Theory

In this section, we present the low-energy theory of the model in Eq. (
6
).
Since it decouples into XXZ and QI parts, so does the corresponding effective action:

\mathcal{S}[\phi,\psi]=\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}[\phi]+\mathcal{S}_{\text{F}}[\psi].

(21)

Here
\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}[\phi]
describes the infrared limit of the XXZ sector, which in the Euclidean formalism is given by the sine-Gordon model
25
;
27
;
21
:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}[\phi]=\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}^{*}[\phi]+\frac{v\Delta}{2\pi^{2}}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{x}{:}\cos{ \sqrt{4K} \phi}{:}

(22)

of a compactified boson field,

\phi\sim\phi+2\pi R,

(23)

with the compactification radius
R=1/\sqrt{K}
with
K
being the so-called Luttinger parameter.
The Gaussian contribution reads:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}^{*}[\phi]=\frac{v}{8\pi}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{x}\bigg[\frac{1}{v^{2}}(\partial_{\tau}\phi)^{2}+(\partial_{x}\phi)^{2}\bigg],

(24)

while the colon notation in the perturbing cosine term denotes the usual normal ordering.
The action in Eq. (
22
) is controlled by two parameters: the sound velocity
v
and the Luttinger parameter
K
, which are exactly obtained from the Bethe ansatz solution
35
;
52
:

\displaystyle v(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi v_{0}}{2}\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta^{2}}}{\arccos{\Delta}},\quad v_{0}\equiv 2ta_{0},

(25)

\displaystyle K(\Delta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi}{2(\pi-\arccos{\Delta})},\quad|\Delta|\leq 1.

(26)

The cosine perturbation appearing in
\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}[\phi]
carries a scaling dimension
d_{g}=4K
.
Within the Luttinger liquid regime
|\Delta|<1
we have
d_{g}>2
, implying that the perturbation is RG-irrelevant and may be neglected.
This way, the gapless XXZ sector corresponds to a compact boson CFT with central charge
c_{\text{B}}=1
and is described by the fixed point action in Eq. (
24
).
On the other hand, for
\Delta>1
, we get
d_{g}<2
, and thus the cosine perturbation becomes relevant, driving the system to a strong coupling regime with a dynamically generated mass gap and vanishing central charge.
This spontaneously breaks the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry, developing a long range CDW order and exponentially decaying correlation functions.
The critical point of
\Delta=1
belongs to the BKT universality class described by the SU(2)
1
WZNW theory perturbed by a marginally irrelevant perturbation.
This transition occurs across the solid and dashed red lines on Fig.
1
, for any finite value of
\kappa
.

The behavior of the QI sector is captured by a Majorana fermion field theory
15
;
27
.
The action functional is expressed as a perturbed CFT:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{F}}[\psi]=\mathcal{S}_{\text{F}}^{*}[\psi]+\frac{\mathrm{i}m}{2\pi}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{x}\,\psi\bar{\psi},

(27)

where the fixed-point action

\begin{split}\mathcal{S}_{\text{F}}^{*}[\psi]=\frac{u}{4\pi}\int\differential{\tau}\differential{x}\bigg[~&\psi\left(\frac{1}{u}\partial_{\tau}+\mathrm{i}\partial_{x}\right)\psi\\
+&\bar{\psi}\left(\frac{1}{u}\partial_{\tau}-\mathrm{i}\partial_{x}\right)\bar{\psi}~\bigg],\end{split}

(28)

represents the QI model at criticality, with
\psi
and
\bar{\psi}
denoting left- and right-moving Majorana field components, respectively.
The corresponding excitation velocity and mass are

u\equiv 2\Gamma a_{0},\quad m\equiv 2J(1-g).

(29)

The mass term is always strongly relevant and gaps the Ising sector of the system, thus for
m\neq 0
we have vanishing central charge
c_{\text{F}}=0
.
On the other hand, at the critical point of
m=0
, the central charge is
c_{\text{F}}=\ifrac{1}{2}
.
This transition belongs to the Ising universality class and occurs across the solid and dashed blue lines on Fig.
1
, for any finite
\kappa
.

In the quadrant on the surface above the solid blue line on Fig.
1
, the QI and XXZ sectors are simultaneously gapless,
0\leq\Delta\leq 1
and
m=0
.
This multi-critical theory has a total central charge of
{c_{\text{tot}}=c_{\text{B}}+c_{\text{F}}=\ifrac{3}{2}}
.
Most importantly, for

\kappa=\kappa_{\text{SUSY}}=\frac{\pi}{2}\frac{\sqrt{1-\Delta^{2}}}{\arccos{\Delta}},

(30)

the velocities of the fermionic and bosonic sectors coincide (on the dotted blue line).
In this case, the theory exhibits an emergent superconformal symmetry, governed by the following action:

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\text{SUSY}}

\displaystyle[\phi,\psi]

(31)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\differential[2]{z}\left(\partial_{z}\phi\partial_{\bar{z}}\phi+\psi\partial_{\bar{z}}\psi+\bar{\psi}\partial_{z}\bar{\psi}\right).

Here,
\partial_{z}
and
\partial_{\bar{z}}
denote derivatives with respect to the complex coordinates
{z=v\tau+\mathrm{i}x}
and
{\bar{z}=v\tau-\mathrm{i}x}
and
{\differential[2]{z}\equiv v\differential{\tau}\differential{x}}
.
Throughout the interval
0\leq\Delta<1
, the system possesses
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superconformal symmetry.
At the endpoint
\Delta=0
(a noninteracting case with
K=1
), the bosonic contribution in
{\mathcal{S}_{\text{SUSY}}[\phi,\psi]}
can be reformulated in terms of two massless Majorana fermions; consequently, it falls into the SU(2)
2
WZNW universality class.
The supersymmetric line ends at
\Delta=1
(BKT point with
K=\ifrac{1}{2}
), where the supersymmetry is enhanced to
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
.

For further discussions on conformal field theories of the compact boson and Majorana fields, the SU(2)
k
WZNW models, and superconformal theories, see Appendices

and
C
and references therein.

III.2.2
Observables

The LL and CDW phases can be probed by studying the structure factor

S(q)=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{j,j^{\prime}}e^{iq(j-j^{\prime})}\left\langle\left\langle\mu^{z}_{j}\mu^{z}_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle\right\rangle,

(32)

with
\left\langle\left\langle\mu^{z}_{j}\mu^{z}_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle\right\rangle=\left\langle\mu^{z}_{j}\mu^{z}_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle-\left\langle\mu^{z}_{j}\rangle\langle\mu^{z}_{j^{\prime}}\right\rangle
. Within the LL regime and for small momenta
q\ll 2k_{F}
(
k_{F}
being the Fermi-momentum), the structure function scales as

S(q\ll 2k_{F})\approx\frac{2K}{\pi}|q|.

(33)

On the other hand, in the CDW phase
S(q)
should develop a strong peak at momenta that characterizes the underlying long-range order.
In our case, one should observe a peak at
q=2k_{F}
.
For systems of finite size
L
, the amplitude of the peak should scale as
S(2k_{F})\sim L^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha\to 1
for the true long-range order
25
;
47
;
21
.

Disordered and ordered Ising phases can be diagnosed via order and disorder parameters, respectively. In particular, we will use the parity operator
\mathcal{O}_{P}
and the two-point correlation function of the local order parameter
\mathcal{O}_{xx}

36
:

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{P,i,r}&=\left\langle\prod^{i+r}_{j=i}\mu^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle,\\
\mathcal{O}_{xx,i,r}&=\left\langle\mu^{x}_{i+\frac{1}{2}}\mu^{x}_{i+\frac{1}{2}+r}\right\rangle.\end{split}

(34)

In the disordered phase one finds
\mathcal{O}_{P,i,r}\neq 0
and
\mathcal{O}_{xx,i,r}=0
, whereas in the ordered phase the situation is reversed, with
\mathcal{O}_{P,i,r}=0
and
\mathcal{O}_{xx,i,r}\neq 0
.

, symmetry-broken phases appear: for
g<1
(SSB) the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry is broken, while for
g>1
(SSB

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
and
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{W}
are spontaneously broken.
Transitions along solid and dashed red lines are of BKT type, whereas those along solid and dashed blue lines are in the Ising universality class.
The SSB–SSB
∗
transition has central charge
c=\ifrac{1}{2}
, and the LL–LL
∗

c=\ifrac{3}{2}
due to an additional gapless Luttinger liquid mode. Superconformal field theories (SUSY CFTs) appear on the dashed blue line, for
\kappa

).

IV.1
g\to 0
regime

In the
g\to 0
limit, the
\Gamma
term can be neglected and the spin sector fully polarizes along the
+z
direction.
Consequently, we may set
Z_{j+\ifrac{1}{2}}\to+1
on all links.
Under this condition, the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
6
) reduces to an interacting spinless-fermion model:

\begin{split}H_{0}=-&t\sum_{j}\left(c^{{\dagger}}_{j}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\
+&V\sum_{j}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right).\end{split}

(35)

In this limit, two phases emerge: the LL phase with power-law correlations and continuously varying critical exponents, and a CDW phase with spontaneously broken
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry.
They are separated by a BKT transition line, shown in Fig.
1
as the solid red line between the LL and SSB phases in the
\Delta
–
g
plane for any finite
\kappa
.

IV.2
g\to\infty
regime

In the opposite limit
g\to\infty
, the
J
term becomes negligible, and the following Gauss law is imposed energetically:

\widetilde{G}_{j}=X_{j-\frac{1}{2}}(-1)^{n_{j}}X_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\to+1,

(36)

which implies that the spin degrees of freedom effectively behave as an emergent
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field.
In this regime, the Hamiltonian simplifies to

;
33

\begin{split}H_{\infty}=-&t\sum_{j}\left(c^{{\dagger}}_{j}Z_{j+\frac{1}{2}}c^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\
+&V\sum_{j}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right).\end{split}

(37)

As shown in
33
, in this
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
lattice gauge theory the system undergoes a transition from a gapless LL phase to a CDW-ordered phase via a BKT phase transition, marked by the red dashed line.
These phases are labeled LL
∗
and SSB
∗
in the
\Delta

g

A bosonic analogue of this model was recently investigated in Ref.

The LL

This follows from multiplying the Gauss law in Eq. (
36

P=\prod_{j=1}^{L}\widetilde{G}_{j}=1.

(38)

Here,
P\equiv(-1)^{\sum_{j}n_{j}}
is the fermion parity operator, generating a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{P}

Although the parity-even constraint is strictly enforced only in the

∗
phase (
g>1
) in the low-energy sector.
The same applies to the transition line separating LL
∗
and SSB
∗

∗
.
In the CFT framework, the only distinction from the standard BKT transition is that all operators that do not commute with fermion parity must be removed from the operator content.

VI
Conclusion and discussions

In this work, we analyzed the phase diagram of a one-dimensional orthogonal metal model.
The system consists of a 1D chain of spinless fermions and spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
matter fields, minimally coupled to a
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
gauge field with a hard Ising gauge constraint.

The model is exactly solvable: first, employing a standard resolution of the Gauss law, we recast the original lattice Hamiltonian in terms of gauge-invariant fermionic and spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
variables.
A non-local and non-linear transformation, Eq. (
13
), then maps the system onto the decoupled spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
XXZ Heisenberg chain (with
z
-anisotropy
\Delta
) and a transverse-field quantum Ising (QI) chain (with dimensionless exchange
g
).
Since both models are exactly solvable, we can precisely describe the structure of the phase diagram of the original Hamiltonian across the parameter space.

In the three-dimensional parameter space
(\Delta,g,\kappa)
, we identified several gapless regimes, alongside gapped phases that exhibit spontaneous symmetry breaking. For weak fermionic repulsion (
|\Delta|<1
) and with strong transverse
Z
–field in the spin sector (
g<1
), the fermions form an ordinary gapless Luttinger liquid (LL).
In the weak transverse
Z
–field regime (
g>1
), the fermions remain gapless, but the Gauss law Eq. (
36
) emerges at low energies. This effectively restricts the system to the even-fermion-parity sector, giving rise to the LL
∗
phase.
For strong repulsion (
\Delta>1
), the fermions develop a charge-density-wave (CDW) order with a dynamically generated gap, spontaneously breaking the particle-hole
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{C}
symmetry. In the
g<1
regime, this results in a doubly degenerate ground state (SSB).
In the
g>1
regime, the CDW order intertwines with the emergent gauge constraint, forcing the spins to simultaneously break the magnetic
\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{W}
symmetry, which leads to a four-fold degenerate ground state (SSB
∗
).
At
\Delta=1
, the fermionic sector exhibits a BKT transition, while at
g=1
the spin sector undergoes a quantum phase transition belonging to the Ising universality class.

In addition to these criticalities, the specific values of
\kappa
are characterized by additional emerging symmetries.
Namely, when the excitation velocities of a critical Ising model and the XXZ theory become equal, one encounters supersymmetric conformal field theories of various universality classes.
For the velocities of bosonic (XXZ) and fermionic (QI) to coincide, one has to fine tune the parameters to
g=1
and
{\kappa=\kappa_{\text{SUSY}}=\pi\sqrt{1-\Delta^{2}}/2\arccos{\Delta}}
.
Along the SUSY line,
\kappa=\kappa_{\text{SUSY}}
, for any
0\leq\Delta<1
the theory is described by a
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
superconformal field theory.
Exactly at
\Delta=0
, the criticality belongs to SU(2)
2
WZNW universality class.
On the other hand, at
\Delta=1
the effective low-energy theory of the model can be unified into a
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
SUSY CFT.

To validate the theoretical predictions, we investigated the microscopic Hamiltonian, Eq. (
6
), using finite and infinite DMRG.
For system sizes up to
L=256
, the numerical data are in excellent agreement with the analytical results.
In the LL and LL
∗
phases, the Luttinger parameter
K
obtained from DMRG matches the exact Bethe ansatz values, and the onset of charge-density-wave order in the SSB and SSB
∗
phases is accurately reproduced.
Correlation functions relevant to the QI sector are likewise determined with high precision.
From the scaling of the von Neumann entanglement entropy at various points in the phase diagram, we extract central charges consistent with the underlying CFTs.
Crucially, the factorization of the model into independent XXZ and QI Hamiltonians allows for the direct determination of bosonic and fermionic velocities.
The finite-size DMRG at the SU(2)
2
and
{\mathcal{N}=(3,3)}
SUSY points shows that these velocities are in good agreement and converge to the predicted values
{v=2t}
(free-fermion limit) at SU(2)
2
and
{v=\pi t}
(Bethe ansatz) at
{\mathcal{N}=(3,3)}
.

Our exact results provide a natural starting point to explore the stability of various phases against additional perturbations. Namely, a particularly interesting direction is to investigate the deformations of the original model at the SUSY points, which could reveal whether the
\mathcal{N}=(1,1)
and
\mathcal{N}=(3,3)
regimes can host extended supersymmetric phases or if they are unstable isolated criticalities. Furthermore, while the current work relies on deconfined gauge fields, introducing gauge fields dynamics into the Hamiltonian of the orthogonal metal – specifically, electric field fluctuations – would be an interesting extension of our model. This perturbation couples the QI and XXZ sectors to each other in a very non-local fashion and thus can lead to new exotic phases. Additionally, such electric fields mediate confinement of the fractionalized degrees of freedom and it would be of interest to investigate quantum dynamics in this model including the string-breaking phenomenon.

\begin{split}c_{j}=\frac{s^{x}_{j}-\mathrm{i}s^{y}_{j}}{2}\prod_{l<j}(-s^{z}_{l}),\quad n_{j}=\frac{1}{2}s^{z}_{j}+\frac{1}{2},\end{split}

(54)

and rename the spins on links,

s^{x}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv X_{j-\frac{1}{2}},\quad s^{z}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\equiv Z_{j-\frac{1}{2}},

(55)

where Pauli matrices
s^{\alpha}_{k}
,
\alpha=x,y,z
live in the extended lattice of sites and links
k=\ifrac{1}{2},1,\ifrac{3}{2},\cdots,L
. In terms of these degrees of freedom, we obtain the following spin model:

\begin{split}H=-&\frac{t}{2}\sum_{j}\left(s^{x}_{j}s^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}s^{x}_{j+1}+s^{y}_{j}s^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}s^{y}_{j+1}\right)\\
+&\frac{V}{4}\sum_{j}s^{z}_{j}s^{z}_{j+1}\\
-&\Gamma\sum_{j}s^{x}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}s^{z}_{j}s^{x}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-J\sum_{j}s^{z}_{j+\frac{1}{2}},\end{split}

(56)

featuring next-to-nearest-neighbor three-spin couplings with amplitudes
t
and
\Gamma
.
Applying now the JW transformation on the extended lattice,

\begin{split}s^{x}_{k}&=\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{k}+a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{k}\right)\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\pi\sum_{l<k}a^{{\dagger}}_{l}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{l}\right),\\
s^{y}_{k}&=-\mathrm{i}\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{k}-a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{k}\right)\exp\left(\mathrm{i}\pi\sum_{l<k}a^{{\dagger}}_{l}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{l}\right),\\
s^{z}_{k}&=2n_{k}-1,\quad k=\frac{1}{2},1,\frac{3}{2},\cdots,L,\end{split}

(57)

one sees explicitly that the spinless fermions living on the sites and on the links mutually decouple,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\text{XXZ}}=~&t\sum_{j}\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{j}a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+1}+\text{h.c.}\right)\\
+&V\sum_{j}\left(n_{j}-\frac{1}{2}\right)\left(n_{j+1}-\frac{1}{2}\right),\end{aligned}

(58)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} H_{\text{QI}}=~&\Gamma\sum_{j}\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}-a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j-\frac{1}{2}}\right)\left(a^{{\dagger}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}+a^{\phantom{{\dagger}}}_{j+\frac{1}{2}}\right)\\
-&J\sum_{j}(2n_{j+\frac{1}{2}}-1).\end{aligned}

(59)

One clearly recognizes spinless fermion realizations of the spin-
\ifrac{1}{2}
Heisenberg XXZ chain in Eq. (
15
) and of the quantum Ising model in Eq. (
16
), respectively. This can be mathematically obtained via the final JW transformations on the fermions living on the sites and on the links independently:

\begin{split}a_{m}&=\frac{\mu^{x}_{m}-\mathrm{i}\mu^{y}_{m}}{2}\prod_{l<m}(-\mu^{z}_{l}),\quad n_{m}=\frac{1}{2}+\frac{1}{2}\mu^{z}_{m},\end{split}

(60)

where
m
and
l
run over sites (
m,l=1,2,\dots,L
) or over links (
m,l=\ifrac{1}{2},\ifrac{3}{2},\dots,L-\ifrac{1}{2}
).
Combining all of the above into a single composite transformation gives us the total transformation Eq. (
13
).

Appendix B
Review of CFTs

In the
1+1
-dimensional Euclidean plane, parametrized by the complex coordinates
{z=v\tau+\mathrm{i}x}
and
{\bar{z}=v\tau-\mathrm{i}x}
, conformal transformations are implemented as (local) holomorphic and antiholomorphic mappings

z\to w(z),\quad\bar{z}\to\bar{w}(\bar{z}).

(61)

The conformal structure of a given conformally invariant field theory (CFT)
26
;
15
is completely determined by its energy-momentum tensor, whose holomorphic and antiholomorphic components are denoted by
T(z)
and
\bar{T}(\bar{z})
, respectively, together with a special set of local fields
\{\mathcal{O}(z,\bar{z})\}
, known as primary fields.
These primary fields satisfy the following operator product expansions (OPEs) with the components of the energy-momentum tensor:

\begin{split}T(z)\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})\sim\frac{h\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})}{z-w},\\
\bar{T}(\bar{z})\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})\sim\frac{\bar{h}\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})}{(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{\bar{w}}\mathcal{O}(w,\bar{w})}{\bar{z}-\bar{w}},\end{split}

(62)

where the pair of constants
(h,\bar{h})
are called the conformal dimensions.
With a proper normalization, primary fields satisfy the following OPEs:

\mathcal{O}_{h,\bar{h}}(z,\bar{z})\mathcal{O}_{h^{\prime},\bar{h}^{\prime}}(w,\bar{w})\sim\frac{\delta_{h,h^{\prime}}\delta_{\bar{h},\bar{h}^{\prime}}}{(z-w)^{2h}(\bar{z}-\bar{w})^{2\bar{h}}}.

(63)

An important characteristic of the given CFT is its central charge
c
, defined from the OPE:

T(z)T(w)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{c}{(z-w)^{4}}+\frac{2T(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}T(w)}{z-w}.

(64)

B.1
Compact boson CFT

The fixed point (Gaussian) action in Eq. (
24
) of the compact boson field
15
;
2
;
26
;
27

\phi\sim\phi+2\pi R,

(65)

where
R=1/\sqrt{K}
is the compactification radius, in a manifestly conformally invariant form, reads:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{B}}^{*}[\phi]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\differential[2]{z}\partial_{z}\phi\partial_{\bar{z}}\phi,\quad\differential[2]{z}\equiv v\differential{\tau}\differential{x}.

(66)

The field
\phi(z,\bar{z})
has a logarithmic correlator:

\langle\phi(z,\bar{z})\phi(w,\bar{w})\rangle=-\ln[(z-w)(\bar{z}-\bar{w})]

(67)

which suggests a chiral decomposition:

\phi(\tau,x)=\varphi(z)+\bar{\varphi}(\bar{z}),

(68)

with

\langle\varphi(z)\varphi(w)\rangle=-\ln(z-w),

(69)

and similarly for the antiholomorphic part.
In terms of these, the holomorphic energy-momentum tensor is given by

T_{\text{B}}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}{:}\partial_{z}\varphi(z)\partial_{z}\varphi(z){:},

(70)

which forms the following OPE with itself:

T_{\text{B}}(z)T_{\text{B}}(w)\sim\frac{1}{2}\frac{1}{(z-w)^{4}}+\frac{2T_{\text{B}}(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}T_{\text{B}}(w)}{z-w}.

Hence, the central charge of the compact boson
c_{\text{B}}=1
.

Since the Bose fields have logarithmic correlators, they are not primaries; however, their derivatives are

J(z)=\mathrm{i}\partial_{z}\varphi(z),\quad\bar{J}(\bar{z})=\mathrm{i}\partial_{\bar{z}}\bar{\varphi}(\bar{z}),

(71)

with conformal dimensions (1,0) and (0,1), respectively.
In fact,
J(z)
and
\bar{J}(\bar{z})
are conserved Noether currents corresponding to U(1)
{}_{\text{L}}\otimes
U(1)
{}_{\text{R}}
symmetry of field shifts:

\varphi\to\varphi+c,\quad\bar{\varphi}\to\bar{\varphi}+\bar{c},

(72)

where
c,\bar{c}
are arbitrary constants.

Now, in terms of the chiral components, one defines the dual field

\theta(\tau,x)=\varphi(z)-\bar{\varphi}(\bar{z}),

(73)

obeying the duality relations

\partial_{z}\phi=\partial_{z}\theta,\quad\partial_{\bar{z}}\phi=-\partial_{\bar{z}}\theta.

(74)

The dual field is also compact

\theta\sim\theta+2\pi\widetilde{R}

(75)

with radius

\widetilde{R}=\frac{2}{R}=\sqrt{4K}.

(76)

The primary operator content of the compact boson, apart from the U(1) currents, is then determined by the following vertex operators:

\mathcal{V}_{n,m}(z,\bar{z})\equiv{:}\exp\!\left(\mathrm{i}\frac{n}{R}\phi+\mathrm{i}\frac{Rm}{2}\theta\right){:},\quad n,m\in\mathbb{Z},

(77)

having conformal dimensions:

\begin{split}h_{n,m}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{R}+\frac{Rm}{2}\right)^{2},\\
\bar{h}_{n,m}&=\frac{1}{2}\left(\frac{n}{R}-\frac{Rm}{2}\right)^{2}.\end{split}

(78)

Clearly, currents and the vertex operators satisfy the following OPEs:

\displaystyle T_{\text{B}}(z)J(w)\sim\frac{J(w)}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}J(w)}{z-w},

(79)

\displaystyle T_{\text{B}}(z)\mathcal{V}_{n,m}(z,\bar{w})

(80)

\displaystyle\hskip 45.5244pt\sim\frac{h_{n,m}\mathcal{V}_{n,m}(w,\bar{w})}{(z-w)^{2}}+\frac{\partial_{w}\mathcal{V}_{n,m}(w,\bar{w})}{z-w},

(81)

and similarly for antiholomorphic counterparts.

B.2
Ising CFT

Among the series of unitary minimal models, the Ising CFT represents the simplest example with the central charge
c_{\text{F}}=\ifrac{1}{2}
.
It is well known
15
;
2
;
26
;
27
to be equivalent to the massless Majorana field theory given by the action in Eq. (
28
), which in complex coordinates admits the form:

\mathcal{S}_{\text{F}}^{*}[\psi]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\differential[2]{z}\left(\psi\partial_{\bar{z}}\psi+\bar{\psi}\partial_{z}\bar{\psi}\right).

(82)

The chiral components of the Majorana field
\psi(z)
and
\bar{\psi}(\bar{z})
are primaries of conformal dimensions
(\ifrac{1}{2},0)
and
(0,\ifrac{1}{2})
, respectively, satisfying the OPEs:

\psi(z)\psi(w)\sim\frac{1}{z-w},\quad\bar{\psi}(\bar{z})\bar{\psi}(\bar{w})\sim\frac{1}{\bar{z}-\bar{w}},

(83)

while the energy-momentum tensor

T_{\text{F}}(z)=-\frac{1}{2}{:}\psi(z)\partial_{z}\psi(z){:}.

(84)

The mass field,
\varepsilon(z,\bar{z})\equiv\mathrm{i}\psi(z)\bar{\psi}(\bar{z})
, is also primary of conformal dimensions
(\ifrac{1}{2},\ifrac{1}{2})
.
That is precisely the perturbation in the action in Eq. (
27
), which drives the system away from criticality.

The Ising CFT contains two more fields, the order field
\sigma(z,\bar{z})
and the disorder field
\mu(z,\bar{z})
. They are non-locally related to the fermion fields and both have conformal dimensions
(\ifrac{1}{16},\ifrac{1}{16})
.
In the ordered phase
\langle\sigma\rangle\neq 0
and
\langle\mu\rangle=0
, while in the disordered phase
\langle\sigma\rangle=0
and
\langle\mu\rangle\neq 0
.
Under the Kramers-Wannier transformation, these two are mapped onto each other
\sigma\leftrightarrow\mu
.

Appendix C
CFTs with additional symmetries

C.1
SU(2)
k
WZNW models

Some of the nontrivial generalizations from conventional CFTs are realized by including additional structure in the theory.
CFTs with additional SU(2) invariance, generated by currents
{\mathbf{J}(z)\equiv(J^{1}(z),J^{2}(z),J^{3}(z))}
carrying conformal weights
(1,0)
and satisfying the following SU(2) current algebra at level

J^{a}(z)J^{b}(w)\sim\frac{\mathrm{i}\varepsilon^{abc}J^{c}(z)}{z-w}+\frac{k}{2}\frac{\delta^{ab}}{(z-w)^{2}},

(and similar expressions for the antiholomorphic sector) serve as the simplest examples of such generalizations.
Then the manifestly SU(2) symmetric holomorphic (Sugawara) energy-momentum tensor admits the form

T(z)=\frac{1}{k+2}{:}\mathbf{J}^{2}(z){:}.

(86)

Such theories, known as SU(2)
k
WZNW models

;
24
;
15
, are characterized by central charges

c_{\text{SU(2)}_{k}}=\frac{3k}{k+2}.

(87)

The simplest case of
k=1
, having the central charge
{c_{\text{SU(2)}_{1}}=1}
, can be realized as the compact boson CFT fine tuned to a BKT point.
Indeed, setting
R=\sqrt{2}
in Eqs. (
78
) allows the existence of two additional currents with conformal dimensions
(1,0)

J^{\pm}(z)\equiv J^{1}(z)\pm\mathrm{i}J^{2}(z)={:}\exp(\pm\mathrm{i} \sqrt{2} \varphi_{\text{L}}(z)){:},

(88)

which together with
{J^{3}(z)\equiv\ifrac{\mathrm{i}\partial_{z}\varphi(z)}{\sqrt{2}}}
satisfies the current algebra in Eq. (

Thus, the chiral
{\text{U(1)}_{\text{L}}\otimes\text{U(1)}_{\text{R}}}
symmetry of the Gaussian model is promoted to
{\text{SU(2)}_{\text{L}}\otimes\text{SU(2)}_{\text{R}}}
symmetry precisely at the BKT point (the full spin-rotational invariant limit in the XXZ language).

Another paradigmatic model corresponds to the case
k=2
, with the central charge
c_{\text{SU(2)}_{2}}=\ifrac{3}{2}
, and describes the universality class of three massless Majorana fields, described by an O(3)-symmetric action

\mathcal{S}_{\text{SU(2)}_{2}}[{\bf\it\psi}]=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int\differential[2]{z}\left({\bf\it\psi}\cdot\partial_{\bar{z}}{\bf\it\psi}+{\bf\it\bar{\psi}}\cdot\partial_{z}{\bf\it\bar{\psi}}\right).

(89)

In terms of Majorana fields
{\bf\it\psi}\equiv(\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\psi_{3})

J^{a}(z)\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{i}}{2}\varepsilon^{abc}\psi_{b}(z)\psi_{c}(z),

(90)

and similarly for the antiholomorphic sector.
