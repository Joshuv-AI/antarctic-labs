Abstract
. We establish existence of probabilistically strong solutions and pathwise uniqueness for a class of quasilinear stochastic evolution equations on bounded domains. Our results combine recent weak existence results for quasilinear stochastic evolution equations in an L p L^{p} -setting (with p > 2 p>2 ) with Yamada–Watanabe theory. To establish pathwise uniqueness, we rely on an L 1 L^{1} -contraction argument.

Introduction

In this article, we investigate strong existence and pathwise uniqueness of global solutions to the quasilinear stochastic evolution equation on a bounded, open set
D\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

(QL)

\displaystyle\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}u&=\big[\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(u)\partial_{j}u)+\partial_{i}\Phi_{i}(u)+\phi(u)\big]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{n\geq 1}\big[b_{n,j}(u)\partial_{j}u+g_{n}(u)\big]\,\mathrm{d}w_{n},\ \ &\text{ on }D,\\
u&=0,\ \ &\text{ on }\partial D,\\
u(0)&=u_{0},\ \ &\text{ on }D,\end{aligned}\right.

where
u
is scalar-valued and we use Einstein’s summation convention.
A common strategy to establish such results involves three steps: proving the existence of a probabilistically weak solution by means of a stochastic compactness method, establishing pathwise uniqueness via an
L^{1}
-contraction argument, and finally applying Yamada–Watanabe-type results to deduce strong existence. While this strategy has been successfully applied to quasilinear equations on the torus in
[
3
,
5
]
and conservative SPDEs on the full space in
[
4
]
, equations on domains with boundary conditions were not treated yet.

As a first step towards equations on domains, existence of probabilistically weak solutions to (
QL
) was established for finite time intervals
[0,T]
in the article
[
2
]
by the first-named author and Veraar – even in the case of systems. The authors raise the question whether strong existence and pathwise uniqueness results could also be proved in their setting (see
[
2
, Rem. 6.8]
). Using the approach outlined above, we answer their question in the affirmative in this work.

(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\mathcal{F}_{t}))

on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P},(\mathcal{F}_{t}))
and a

-measurable initial datum

The coefficients

h

)

Moreover, the initial datum

is assumed to take values in

2.
Probabilistically weak solutions

The proof of Theorem
1.1
relies on a version of the Yamada–Watanabe theorem.
Although the precise details of the latter are subtle and technical, it is summarized by: ‘Existence of a weak solution and pathwise uniqueness imply existence of strong solutions’. In this statement, weak and strong solutions are meant in a probabilistic sense. For details on Yamada–Watanabe theory for SPDEs with variational-like solution notions, we refer to
[
7
]
.
Our notion of strong solutions was already given in Definition
1.4
. To discuss
weak solutions, we consider the following equation:

(QLw)

\displaystyle\left\{\quad\begin{aligned} \,\mathrm{d}\tilde{u}&=\big[\partial_{i}(a_{ij}(\tilde{u})\partial_{j}\tilde{u})+\partial_{i}\Phi_{i}(\tilde{u})+\phi(\tilde{u})\big]\,\mathrm{d}t\\
&\qquad\qquad+\sum_{n\geq 1}\big[b_{n,j}(\tilde{u})\partial_{j}\tilde{u}+g_{n}(\tilde{u})\big]\,\mathrm{d}\tilde{w}_{n},\ \ &\text{ on }D,\\
\tilde{u}&=0,\ \ &\text{ on }\partial D.\end{aligned}\right.

The notion of a weak solution to (

) is as follows.

Pathwise uniqueness

Throughout this section, we let

Lemma 3.1

(Approximation of the absolute value)
.

For every
m\geq 1
there exists a function
\psi_{m}\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
which is twice continuously differentiable, has bounded second derivative and satisfies the following properties for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}

(5)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=0,

(6)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(7)

\displaystyle\frac{\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)}{\xi}

\displaystyle\geq 0\quad(\xi\neq 0),

(8)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq 0,

(9)

\displaystyle\psi_{m}(\xi)

\displaystyle\geq|\xi|-\mathrm{e}^{1-m}

(10)

\displaystyle|\psi_{m}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq|\xi|,

(11)

\displaystyle|\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\xi)|

\displaystyle\leq 1,

(12)

In particular, for every

Proposition 3.3

(Pathwise uniqueness)
.

Fix
h>1
and let Assumption
QL(

h

)
be satisfied.
Also, let
T\in(0,\infty)
.
Suppose that
(\tilde{u},\tilde{W},\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}))
and
(\tilde{v},\tilde{W},\tilde{\Omega},\tilde{\mathcal{F}},\tilde{\mathbb{P}},(\tilde{\mathcal{F}}_{t}))
are weak solutions to (

) on
[0,T]
in the sense of Definition

\tilde{u}(0)=\tilde{v}(0)

\tilde{u}=\tilde{v}
a.s. in
\mathrm{C}([0,T];L^{2}(D))

Proof.

Let
t\in[0,T]
be arbitrary.
For every
m
define the functional
\Psi_{m}\colon L^{2}(D)\to\mathbb{R}
by
\Psi_{m}(f)=\int_{D}\psi_{m}(f)\,\mathrm{d}x
, where
\psi_{m}
is as in Lemma
3.1
. By the properties of
\psi_{m}
stated in Lemma
3.1
, the Itô formula from
[

, Th. 4.2, p. 65]
is applicable with the functional
\Psi_{m}
, and applying it to the process
\tilde{u}-\tilde{v}
yields the identity

(16)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\int_{D}\psi_{m}(\tilde{u}(t)-\tilde{v}(t))\,\mathrm{d}x\\
=-&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})(a_{ij}(\cdot,\tilde{u})\partial_{j}\tilde{u}-a_{ij}(\cdot,\tilde{v})\partial_{j}\tilde{v})\partial_{i}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
\quad-&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})(\Phi_{i}(\cdot,\tilde{u})-\Phi_{i}(\cdot,\tilde{v}))\partial_{i}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+&\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})(\phi(\cdot,\tilde{u})-\phi(\cdot,\tilde{v}))\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
+&\sum_{n}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})[b_{n,j}(\cdot,\tilde{u})\partial_{j}\tilde{u}-b_{n,j}(\cdot,\tilde{v})\partial_{j}\tilde{v}+g_{n}(\cdot,\tilde{u})-g_{n}(\cdot,\tilde{v})]\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}w_{n}(s)\\
+&\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})\sum_{n}\Bigl|\sum_{j}(b_{n,j}(\cdot,\tilde{u})\partial_{j}\tilde{u}-b_{n,j}(\cdot,\tilde{v})\partial_{j}\tilde{v})+g_{n}(\cdot,\tilde{u})-g_{n}(\cdot,\tilde{v})\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s\\
=-&\textrm{I}-\textrm{II}+\textrm{III}+\textrm{IV}+\textrm{V}.\end{split}

Note that we suppress certain variable dependencies to increase readability.

To handle term

Then, for
\varepsilon>0
, we apply the inequality
\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}|y+z|^{2}\leq(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\varepsilon)y^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}z^{2}
to obtain

(17)

V

\displaystyle\leq(\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}+\varepsilon)\int_{0}^{t}\int_{D}\psi_{m}^{\prime\prime}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})\sum_{n}\Bigl|\sum_{j}b_{n,j}(\cdot,\tilde{u})\partial_{j}(\tilde{u}-\tilde{v})\Bigr|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}s
