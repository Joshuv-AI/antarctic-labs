MA(1) processes with uniform innovations conditioned to stay positive in the non-expanding regime
Frank Aurzada, Virginia Worf

Abstract
We study an MA(1)-process with uniform innovations conditioned to stay positive. Representing the model as a Markov chain, we prove the existence of the limiting finite-dimensional distributions under this conditioning and identify the limiting process explicitly as a Doob h h -transform. In the non-expanding case, i.e. when the coupling parameter θ \theta satisfies θ ∈ [ − 1 , 1 ) \theta\in[-1,1) , we compute the relevant generating functions, extract sharp persistence asymptotics, and give explicit formulas for the eigenfunction h h and the persistence exponent. The resulting transition kernel of the limiting process is therefore fully explicit and displays a phase-dependent structure in the parameters. This provides a rare solvable example of a Markov chain on a continuous state space conditioned on persistence.

Introduction and main results

Stochastic processes conditioned to satisfy long-time constraints form a classical theme in probability, going back at least to Doob’s seminal work on conditioning Brownian motion to be positive
[
20
]
. Conditional distributions which appear from constraining stochastic processes form interesting new models and give insights into the conditioning mechanism. In the present paper, we study this conditioning problem for a class of moving-average processes and give an explicit Doob-type description of the limiting process under the constraint of staying positive.

The present case is a rare instance where the quantities involved in the Doob-transform are all explicit but non-trivial. Indeed, although persistence probabilities for moving-average processes are already delicate (cf. the phase diagram in Figure

Beyond being new, our results can be seen as an expository example, where the non-trivial limiting Doob kernel is not only known to exist but can actually be written down explicitly.

Extensions.

In the present work, we restrict to the non-expanding regime
|\theta|\leq 1
. In
[
5
]
, the generating function of the
(p_{n}^{a}(\theta))

However, the dualities used there are not applicable for fixed starting points. Nonetheless, our techniques can be extended to the case

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}_{y}(M_{1}\in C_{1}\times\{y\},M_{2}\in C_{2}\times C_{1},\dots,M_{m}\in C_{m}\times C_{m-1}\penalty\ |\Omega_{n})

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{[-a,1]^{m}}\mathds{1}_{C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{m}}(\mathbf{x})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}p(x_{i-1},\mathrm{d}x_{i})\mathds{1}_{S}(x_{i},x_{i-1})\right]\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x_{m}}(\Omega_{n-m})}{\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(\Omega_{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-a,1]^{m}}\mathds{1}_{C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{m}}(\mathbf{x})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}p(x_{i-1},\mathrm{d}x_{i})\mathds{1}_{S}(x_{i},x_{i-1})\right]\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{\mathbb{P}_{x_{m}}(\Omega_{n-m})}{\mathbb{P}_{x_{0}}(\Omega_{n})}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-a,1]^{m}}\mathds{1}_{C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{m}}(\mathbf{x})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\frac{1}{a+1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}\mathds{1}_{S}(x_{i},x_{i-1})\right]\frac{h(x_{m})}{h(x_{0})}\lambda^{-m}

\displaystyle=\int_{[-a,1]^{m}}\mathds{1}_{C_{1}\times\dots\times C_{m}}(\mathbf{x})\left[\prod_{i=1}^{m}\mathds{1}_{S}(x_{i},x_{i-1})\frac{h(x_{i})}{h(x_{i-1})}\frac{1}{\lambda}\frac{1}{a+1}\,\mathrm{d}x_{i}\right],

where we used Lemma

The blue region

Recall that in the blue region,
\theta\in(0,1)

Lemma 3.1

From a recursion of the probabilities

\frac{E\left(\theta,\frac{-\theta zy}{a+1}\right)}{E\left(\theta,\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)}

By the choice of the parameters in the blue region, we get that

\theta y\in[-a,1]

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{n}}\int_{\theta y}^{1}\int_{\theta x_{2}}^{1}\dots\int_{\theta x_{n}}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{3}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+1}\int_{\theta y}^{1}\left[\frac{1}{(a+1)^{n-1}}\int_{\theta x_{2}}^{1}\dots\int_{\theta x_{n}}^{1}\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{3}\right]\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\frac{1}{a+1}\int_{\theta y}^{1}p_{n-1}^{a,x_{2}}(\theta)\mathrm{d}x_{2}.

Multiplying with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)\cdot z^{n}=\frac{1}{a+1}\penalty\ \frac{\partial}{\partial y}\int_{\theta y}^{1}\left(\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n-1}^{a,x_{2}}(\theta)\cdot z^{n}\right)\mathrm{d}x_{2}

where we used that
p_{0}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{0}=1
is constant in the first step and the fact that the integral is bounded and the series is absolutely convergent within the disk of convergence so that we can exchange the sum and the integral.
In conclusion, we found a differential equation for the function

with the additional constraint
\hat{P}_{a,\frac{1}{\theta},\theta}(z)=1

Lemma 3.2

We want to use Lemma

\displaystyle\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}=\frac{f(z)}{z_{0}-z}\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{for all}\penalty\ |z|<z_{0}=\frac{1}{\lambda}

which is analytic on

Recall that
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}=\frac{E\left(\theta,\frac{-\theta zy}{a+1}\right)}{E\left(\theta,\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)}

Lemma 4.1
.

In the green region, we have

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\begin{cases}1,\penalty\ \penalty\ &\penalty\ -a\leq y<\frac{1}{\theta},\\

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(\{X_{2}\geq\theta y,X_{3}\geq\theta X_{2},\dots,X_{n+1}\geq\theta X_{n}\}\cap A^{c})=0.

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2}\cap B_{n})-\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2}\cap B_{n-1}\cap B_{n})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2})\mathbb{P}(B_{n})-\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2}\cap B_{n-1}\cap B_{n})

\displaystyle=p_{n-2}^{a,y}(\theta)q_{1}^{a}(\theta)-\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2}\cap B_{n-1}\cap B_{n}).

We therefore have

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)=p_{n-1}^{a,y}(\theta)-p_{n-2}^{a,y}(\theta)q_{1}^{a}(\theta)+\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{n-2}\cap B_{n-1}\cap B_{n}).

We proceed this way of transforming the terms of the form
\mathbb{P}(A_{1}\cap\dots\cap A_{i}\cap B_{i+1}\cap\dots\cap B_{n})
. In particular, for
n\geq 1
, we get the recursion

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}p_{n-k}^{a,y}(\theta)q_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)\right)+(-1)^{n}q_{n}^{a,y}(\theta).

Multiplying by
z^{n}
and taking the sum over
n
, this becomes

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)-1

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}(-1)^{k-1}p_{n-k}^{a,y}(\theta)q_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)z^{n}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\sum_{k=1}^{n}p_{n-k}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n-k}zq_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)(-1)^{k-1}z^{k-1}+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}(-1)^{n}q_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}p_{n-k}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n-k}zq_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)(-z)^{k-1}+\hat{Q}_{a,y,\theta}(-z)-1

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}zq_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)(-z)^{k-1}\sum_{n=k}^{\infty}p_{n-k}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n-k}+\hat{Q}_{a,y,\theta}(-z)-1

\displaystyle=\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}zq_{k-1}^{a}(\theta)(-z)^{k-1}+\hat{Q}_{a,y,\theta}(-z)-1

\displaystyle=\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)z\hat{Q}_{a,\theta}(-z)+\hat{Q}_{a,y,\theta}(-z)-1.

Rearranging and dividing by
1-z\hat{Q}_{a,\theta}(-z)
gives the claim.
∎

y\in[-a,1]
into the intervals

A_{m}\coloneqq\bigg[\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}},\frac{-a}{\theta^{m+1}}\bigg),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 0\leq m\leq p-1,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ A_{p}\coloneqq\bigg[\frac{-a}{\theta^{p}},1\bigg].

Note that for
y\in A_{m}
we have
m=\max\{n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:\theta^{n}y\geq-a\}
and that for all
1\leq m\leq p
, the case
y\in\big[\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}},\frac{-a}{\theta^{m+1}}\big)
is equivalent to
\theta y\in\big[\frac{-a}{\theta^{m-1}},\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\big)=A_{m-1}
.

For
m\geq 1
, we will use the following lemma to reduce the generating function in the region
A_{m}
to the region
A_{m-1}
:

Lemma 6.4
.

In the orange region, for
m\geq 1
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{-\theta z}{a+1}\hat{P}_{a,\theta y,\theta}(z),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ y\in A_{m},

(24)

and

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}},\theta}(z)=\lim_{y\nearrow\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}}\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z).

(25)

Proof.

Let
y\in A_{m}
. For
1\leq m\leq p
, we have
1\geq\theta y\geq\frac{-a}{\theta^{m-1}}\geq-a
. Therefore, for
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\theta y\leq X_{2},\dots,\theta X_{n}\leq X_{n+1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(a+1)^{n}}\int_{\theta y}^{1}\bigg[\int_{-a}^{1}\mathds{1}_{\{\theta x_{2}\leq x_{3}\}}\dots\int_{-a}^{1}\mathds{1}_{\{\theta x_{n}\leq x_{n+1}\}}\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}\dots\mathrm{d}x_{3}\bigg]\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a+1}\int_{\theta y}^{1}p_{n-1}^{a,x_{2}}(\theta)\mathrm{d}x_{2}.

Multiplying with
z^{n}
and summing over
n
, we get

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=1+\frac{z}{a+1}\int_{\theta y}^{1}\hat{P}_{a,x_{2},\theta}(z)\mathrm{d}x_{2}.

(26)

Taking the derivative with respect to
y
gives us the equation (
24
).
Note that for
y\in A_{m}
, the function
\hat{P}_{a,\theta y,\theta}(z)
is the generating function in the region
A_{m-1}
since
\theta y\in A_{m-1}
. Moreover, the function

y\mapsto\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)
is continuous on
y\in[-a,1]
, so in particular at
y=\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}
, which implies (
25
).
∎

In order to represent the generating function
\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}
, we will use the following notation:
Define
H_{k}(x)\coloneqq\frac{x^{k}}{k!}
and note that
H^{\prime}_{k+1}=H_{k}
and
H_{k}(\theta x)=\theta^{k}H(x)
. Now we start by treating the cases
y\in A_{0}
and
y\in A_{1}
and use the ideas to deduce a structure for general
y\in A_{m}
.

Lemma 6.5
.

It holds

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=c_{0,0}(\theta,a,z)H_{0}(y),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ y\in A_{0},

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{1}c_{1,i}(\theta,a,z)H_{i}(y),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ y\in A_{1},

with

\displaystyle c_{0,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{0,0}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1-zF(z)},

\displaystyle c_{1,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{1,0}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq c_{0,0}+\frac{-az}{a+1}\,c_{0,0}\qquad\text{and}\qquad c_{1,1}\coloneqq c_{1,1}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq\frac{-z\theta}{a+1}\,c_{0,0}.

Proof.

First, consider
y\in A_{0}
, which means that
\theta y<-a
. So for
n\geq 1
, we have

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(\theta y\leq X_{2},\theta X_{2}\leq X_{3},\dots,\theta X_{n}\leq X_{n+1})=p_{n-1}^{a}(\theta),

so
\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=1+\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)z^{n}=1+z\hat{P}_{a,\theta}(z)
.
The generating function
\hat{P}_{a,\theta}(z)
can be taken from Lemma 10 of
[
5
]
(cf. (
20
)) so that

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=1+\frac{zF(z)}{1-zF(z)}=\frac{1}{1-zF(z)}=c_{0,0}.

Using Lemma
6.4
, we now get a closed form in case
y\in A_{1}
: For
y\in A_{1}
, we have
\theta y\in A_{0}
and thus,

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{-z\theta}{a+1}c_{0,0}y+c_{0,0}+\frac{-az}{a+1}c_{0,0},

and the claim follows.
∎

The previous lemma suggests the following general structure:

Corollary 6.6
.

In the orange region, for any
0\leq m\leq p
, it holds

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{m,i}(\theta,a,z)H_{i}(y),\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ y\in A_{m},

with radius of convergence
|z|<\frac{1}{\lambda}
and recursive definitions

\displaystyle c_{0,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{0,0}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq\frac{1}{1-zF(z)},

\displaystyle c_{m,i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{m,i}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq\frac{-z\theta^{i}}{a+1}c_{m-1,i-1},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ 1\leq i\leq m,\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \text{and}

\displaystyle c_{m,0}

\displaystyle\coloneqq c_{m,0}(\theta,a,z)\coloneqq c_{m,0}\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(c_{m-1,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)-c_{m,i+1}H_{i+1}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)\right).

Proof.

We prove the corollary via induction. For
y\in A_{0}
and
y\in A_{1}
, we have already verified the claim in Lemma
6.5
.
Assume that for
\tilde{y}\in A_{m-1}
, we have
\hat{P}_{a,\tilde{y},\theta}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_{m-1,i}H_{i}(\tilde{y})
. In particular, this holds for
\tilde{y}=\theta y
when
y\in A_{m}
.
We show that the function
y\mapsto\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i}(y)
on
A_{m}
satisfies the claims of Lemma
6.4
. First, we check the differential equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial y}\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i-1}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\frac{-z\theta^{i}c_{m-1,i-1}}{a+1}H_{i-1}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z\theta}{a+1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m-1,i-1}H_{i-1}(\theta y)=\frac{-z\theta}{a+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_{m-1,i}H_{i}(\theta y)

\displaystyle=\frac{-z\theta}{a+1}\hat{P}_{a,\theta y,\theta}(z).

To check continuity in
\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}
, we compute
\lim_{\tilde{y}\nearrow\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}}\hat{P}_{a,\tilde{y},\theta}(z)
with the obtained formula for
\hat{P}_{a,\tilde{y},\theta}(z)
and
\tilde{y}\in A_{m-1}
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\lim_{\tilde{y}\nearrow\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}}\hat{P}_{a,\tilde{y},\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}c_{m-1,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)+\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}\left(c_{m-1,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)-c_{m,i+1}H_{i+1}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)+c_{m,0}H_{0}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{m,i}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right),

which is equal to
\hat{P}_{a,\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}},\theta}(z)
. This completes the proof.
∎

Now we iterate the coefficients from the last lemma to get a form only depending on the coefficients
c_{i,0}
for
0\leq i\leq m
:

Corollary 6.7
.

In the orange region, for
0\leq m\leq p
, it holds

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\left(\frac{-zy}{a+1}\right)^{i}c_{m-i,0},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ y\in A_{m},

(27)

where
c_{0,0}=\frac{1}{1-zF(z)}
and

\displaystyle c_{k,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-ki}\left(\frac{za}{a+1}\right)^{i+1}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\left(\frac{(a+1)(i+1)}{za}-\theta^{i+1-k}\right)c_{k-1-i,0},\penalty\ \penalty\ \penalty\ k\geq 1.

(28)

Proof.

By induction, we have for
m\geq 0
and
0\leq i\leq m

\displaystyle c_{m,i}=\left(\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)^{i}\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}c_{m-i,0}.

(29)

Corollary
6.6
therefore yields (
27
).
Plugging (
29
) into the formula for
c_{k,0}
for
1\leq k\leq m
yields the second part:

\displaystyle c_{k,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)^{i}\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}c_{k-1-i,0}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{k}}\right)-\left(\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)^{i+1}\theta^{\frac{(i+2)(i+1)}{2}}c_{k-1-i,0}H_{i+1}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{k}}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\left(\frac{-z}{a+1}\right)^{i}\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}c_{k-1-i,0}H_{i}\left(\frac{-a}{\theta^{k}}\right)\left(1-\frac{za\theta^{i+1-k}}{(a+1)(i+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-ki}\left(\frac{za}{a+1}\right)^{i+1}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\left(\frac{(a+1)(i+1)}{za}-\theta^{i+1-k}\right)c_{k-1-i,0}.\qed

Using the just obtained representation of the coefficients, we can eventually find a closed form of the generating function, isolating the dependence on the function
F
.

Corollary 6.8
.

In the orange region, for
0\leq m\leq p
, we have

\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)}{1-zF(z)}

where for
y\in A_{m}
, we set

\displaystyle G_{a,y,\theta}(z)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{m}\left(\frac{-zy}{a+1}\right)^{i}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\sum_{{\ell}=0}^{m-i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}=m-i-{\ell}\\
i_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}s_{z}(m-i-(r-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}i_{j},i_{r}),

with the convention that the sum on the right-hand side is equal to
1
for
{\ell}=0
,
i=m
and equal to
0
for
{\ell}=0
,
i<m
, and

\displaystyle s_{z}(k,i)\coloneqq\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}-ki}\left(\frac{za}{a+1}\right)^{i+1}\frac{1}{(i+1)!}\left(\frac{(a+1)(i+1)}{za}-\theta^{i+1-k}\right).

Proof.

Iterating (
28
), we have for
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle c_{k,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{k-1}s_{z}(k,i)c_{k-1-i,0}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1}=0}^{k-1}s_{z}(k,i_{1})\sum_{i_{2}=0}^{k-2-i_{1}}s_{z}(k-1-i_{1},i_{2})c_{k-2-i_{1}-i_{2},0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-zF(z)}\sum_{{\ell}=1}^{k}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}=k-{\ell}\\
i_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}s_{z}(k-(r-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}i_{j},i_{r}),

where we summed over all possible decompositions
k=\ell+i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}
.
Plugging this into Corollary
6.7
gives

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\left(\frac{-zy}{a+1}\right)^{i}c_{m-i,0}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1\!-\!zF(z)}\!\sum_{i=0}^{m}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\left(\frac{-zy}{1\!+\!a}\right)^{i}\sum_{{\ell}=0}^{m-i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}=m-i-{\ell}\\
i_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}s_{z}(m\!-\!i\!-\!(r\!-\!1)\!-\!\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}i_{j},i_{r}).\qed

In the proofs of the asymptotic behaviour of
(p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta))
, we shall need some more auxiliary results.

Lemma 6.9
.

Let
0\leq m\leq p
and
y\in A_{m}
. Then there exists
\kappa(y)>0
such that, for all
n\geq m+1
, we have

p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)\geq\kappa(y)p_{n-m-1}^{a}(\theta).

As a consequence,

\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)\geq\kappa(y)z^{m+1}\hat{P}_{a,\theta}(z),\qquad z\geq 0.

Proof.

Let us start with the case
m=0
. Then
\theta y\leq-a
and we have

p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)=p_{n-1}^{a}(\theta),

so that the claim holds for
m=0
with
\kappa(y)\coloneqq 1
. For
m\geq 1
, we proceed by induction. Let
y\in A_{m}
. Assume there exist
\alpha=\alpha(y)
and
\beta=\beta(y)
with

-a\leq\alpha<\beta\leq 1,\qquad\alpha\geq\theta y,\qquad\beta\in A_{m-1}.

(30)

Then, by the Markov property and the monotonicity of
x\mapsto p_{n-1}^{a,x}(\theta)
,

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)=

\displaystyle\,\int_{-a}^{1}\mathds{1}_{x_{2}\geq\theta y}\,p_{n-1}^{a,x_{2}}(\theta)\,\frac{1}{a+1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\geq\,\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\,p_{n-1}^{a,x_{2}}(\theta)\,\frac{1}{a+1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\,\int_{\alpha}^{\beta}\,p_{n-1}^{a,\beta}(\theta)\,\frac{1}{a+1}\mathrm{d}x_{2}=\,\frac{\beta-\alpha}{a+1}\,p_{n-1}^{a,\beta}(\theta).

Since
\beta\in A_{m-1}
, we can apply the induction hypothesis and estimate

p_{n-1}^{a,\beta}(\theta)\geq\kappa(\beta)p_{(n-1)-(m-1)-1}^{a}(\theta)=\kappa(\beta)p_{n-m-1}^{a}(\theta),

so that for
y\in A_{m}
we can set
\kappa(y)\coloneqq\kappa(\beta(y))\frac{\beta(y)-\alpha(y)}{a+1}
and obtain the claim by combining the last two displays.

We still have to show that there are valid choices for
\alpha=\alpha(y)
and
\beta=\beta(y)
satisfying (
30
): set

\alpha\coloneqq\theta y,\qquad\beta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta y+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right).

Then
\alpha=\theta y\geq-a
, since
m\geq 1
. Further,

\beta-\alpha=\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta y+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)-\theta y=\frac{1}{2}\left(-\theta y+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)>\frac{1}{2}\left(-\theta\frac{-a}{\theta^{m+1}}+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)=0,

which in particular means

\beta>\alpha=\theta y\geq\frac{-a}{\theta^{m-1}}
.
Finally,

\beta=\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta y+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)<\frac{1}{2}\left(\theta\frac{-a}{\theta^{m+1}}+\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\right)=\frac{-a}{\theta^{m}}\leq 1,

so that indeed
\beta\in A_{m-1}
. Note that in case
m=p
, we additionally used the fact that
\theta<\frac{-a}{\theta^{p}}
in the inequalities above.
∎

A consequence from the last auxiliary result is the following statement about
G_{a,y,\theta}
at
z_{0}
.

Lemma 6.10
.

We have
G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})>0
.

Proof.

Using Corollary
6.8
, Lemma
6.9
and (
20
), we have for real
0\leq z<z_{0}
and with
\kappa(y)>0
that

\displaystyle\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)}{u(z)}

\displaystyle=\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)\geq\kappa(y)z^{m+1}\hat{P}_{a,\theta}(z)=\kappa(y)z^{m+1}\,\frac{F(z)}{u(z)},

where
u
is as in (
21
).
Since
u(z)>0
for all
0\leq z<z_{0}
(recall that
z_{0}
is the smallest positive real zero of
u
and
u(0)=1
), we know that for all
0\leq z<z_{0}

\displaystyle G_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle\geq\kappa(y)z^{m+1}F(z),

and letting
z\uparrow z_{0}
, we see that, using the definition of
z_{0}
,

\displaystyle G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})

\displaystyle\geq\kappa(y)z_{0}^{m+1}F(z_{0})=\kappa(y)z_{0}^{m+1}\,\frac{1}{z_{0}}>0.\qed

Using the last auxiliary result, we can now show that the radius of convergence of
\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}
is indeed
z_{0}>0
as defined in Lemma
6.1
.

Recall that Corollary
6.8
states that

\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)}{u(z)},

where
G_{a,y,\theta}(z)
is a polynomial in
z
. Recall from Lemma
6.1
that there exists
z_{0}>0
such that
u(z_{0})=0
and
z_{0}
is a simple root of
u
. While Lemma
6.1
is concerned with real solutions to the equation
zF(z)=1
, i.e. the real roots of
u(z)=0
, we will show that all other roots have modulus that is strictly larger than
z_{0}
.

Lemma 6.11
.

Let
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z_{1}|\leq z_{0}
and
u(z_{1})=0
. Then
z_{1}=z_{0}
. In particular, there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that for any
z_{1}\neq z_{0}
that is a zero of
u(z)
we have
|z_{1}|>z_{0}+\varepsilon
.

Proof.

Let
z_{1}\in\mathbb{C}
with
|z_{1}|\leq z_{0}
and
u(z_{1})=0
. Assume
z_{1}\neq z_{0}
.

We start with some initial definitions. Recall that
u
is a polynomial of degree
\leq p+1
which has
z_{0}
and
z_{1}
as zeros. Denoting the multiplicity of the zero
z_{1}
by
r
, we note that
r\in\{1,\ldots,p\}
. Further, we define

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(y)

\displaystyle:=\lim_{z\to z_{1}}(z_{1}-z)^{r}\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{1})}{\lim_{z\to z_{1}}\frac{u(z)}{(z_{1}-z)^{r}}},\qquad y\in[-a,1].

(31)

We note that
G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{1})
is some finite number (which may be zero), because
G_{a,y,\theta}(z)
is a polynomial in
z
, while the limit in the denominator exists and is non-zero because
r
is the exact multiplicity of
z_{1}
as a zero of
u
.
Note that
\phi_{1}
is not identically zero on
[-a,1]
. Indeed, at least for
y\in A_{0}
we have

\phi_{1}(y)=\lim_{z\to z_{1}}(z_{1}-z)^{r}\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\lim_{z\to z_{1}}(z_{1}-z)^{r}\frac{1}{u(z)},

because
\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{1}{u(z)}
; and the limit does not vanish, as argued above.

Define

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(y):=\lim_{z\to z_{0}}(z_{0}-z)\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})}{\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\frac{u(z)}{z_{0}-z}}=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})}{-\lim_{z\to z_{0}}\frac{u(z)-u(z_{0})}{z-z_{0}}}=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})}{-u^{\prime}(z_{0})},

and
G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})
is some finite number, as argued above.
Note that the last chain of equalities also shows that
\phi_{0}
is strictly positive and finite on
[-a,1]
, by Lemma
6.10
and Lemma
6.1
.

We will now obtain eigenvalue equations for
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{0}
.
It is known from (
26
) that, for
y\in[-a,1]
,

\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)=1+\frac{z}{a+1}\int_{\{x\geq\theta y\}\cap[-a,1]}\hat{P}_{a,x,\theta}(z)\mathrm{d}x.

(32)

In fact, we showed this for
y\in A_{m}
,
m\geq 1
, only, but for
y\in A_{0}
it is easy to verify.
We multiply (
32
) by
(z_{1}-z)^{r}
and take the limit when
z\to z_{1}
. Then,

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(y)=\frac{z_{1}}{a+1}\int_{\{x\geq\theta y\}\cap[-a,1]}\phi_{1}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(33)

Here, we exchanged the limit and the integral. This is justified by the dominated convergence theorem, as visible from (
31
): the fraction
\frac{(z_{1}-z)^{r}}{u(z)}
can be uniformly bounded when
z\to z_{1}
, because
z_{1}
is a zero of
u
of exact multiplicity
r
. Moreover,
G_{a,x,\theta}(z)
is a polynomial in
z
whose coefficients are uniformly bounded in
x\in[-a,1]
. Hence the integrand is dominated by an integrable constant, and dominated convergence applies. Equation (
33
) implies that
\phi_{1}
is continuous.

Completely analogously (but multiplying this time (
32
) by
(z_{0}-z)
), we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(y)=\frac{z_{0}}{a+1}\int_{\{x\geq\theta y\}\cap[-a,1]}\phi_{0}(x)\mathrm{d}x.

(34)

The last equation shows that
\phi_{0}
is in particular continuous.
We can now iterate (
33
) to get

\displaystyle\phi_{1}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{1}^{n}}{(a+1)^{n}}\int_{-a}^{1}\int_{-a}^{1}\cdots\int_{-a}^{1}\mathds{1}_{x_{2}\geq\theta y,x_{3}\geq\theta x_{2},\ldots,x_{n+1}\geq\theta x_{n}}\mathrm{d}x_{2}\ldots\mathrm{d}x_{n}\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}

\displaystyle=z_{1}^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}\mathbb{P}(X_{2}\geq\theta y,X_{3}\geq\theta X_{2},\ldots,X_{n}\geq\theta X_{n-1},x_{n+1}\geq\theta X_{n})\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}

\displaystyle=:z_{1}^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y,x_{n+1})\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}.

(35)

Clearly,
K_{n}(y,x_{n+1})>0
for any
y,x_{n+1}\in[-a,1]
if
n
is large enough (in fact,
n\geq p+1
already works).
By (
34
), the analogous fact is true for
\phi_{0}
:

\displaystyle\phi_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=z_{0}^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y,x_{n+1})\phi_{0}(x_{n+1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}.

(36)

We finally show some decisive estimates between
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{0}
. Since
\phi_{0}(y)>0
for all
y\in[-a,1]
, we can define

M:=\max_{y\in[-a,1]}\frac{|\phi_{1}(y)|}{\phi_{0}(y)}.

Since
\phi_{1}
and
\phi_{0}
are continuous, the definition of
M
allows to choose a
y_{\ast}\in[-a,1]
such that

\displaystyle M=\frac{|\phi_{1}(y_{\ast})|}{\phi_{0}(y_{\ast})}.

Using (
35
) and (
36
) and the triangle inequality, for
n\geq p+1
, we obtain

\displaystyle M\phi_{0}(y_{\ast})

\displaystyle=|\phi_{1}(y_{\ast})|

\displaystyle=\left|z_{1}^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y_{\ast},x_{n+1})\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|z_{1}|^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y_{\ast},x_{n+1})|\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})|\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}

(37)

\displaystyle\leq z_{0}^{n}\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y_{\ast},x_{n+1})M\phi_{0}(x_{n+1})\,\frac{\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}}{a+1}

\displaystyle=M\phi_{0}(y_{\ast}).

Since
M
and
\phi_{0}(y_{\ast})
are positive, the inequalities must in fact be equalities, so that in particular
|z_{1}|=z_{0}
. Further, since
K_{n}(y_{\ast},x_{n+1})>0
and the integrands
\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})
and
M\phi_{0}(x_{n+1})
are continuous, the definition of
M
and fact that

\int_{-a}^{1}K_{n}(y_{\ast},x_{n+1})(M\phi_{0}(x_{n+1})-|\phi_{1}(x_{n+1})|)\mathrm{d}x_{n+1}=0

imply that we must have
|\phi_{1}(x)|=M\phi_{0}(x)
for all
x\in[-a,1]
. Additionally, the triangle inequality step (
37
) can only be true if the complex argument of
\phi_{1}(x)
is identical for Lebesgue almost all
x
; and by continuity of
\phi_{1}
, we must have
\phi_{1}(x)=e^{i\psi}M\phi_{0}(x)
for some
\psi\in[0,2\pi)
.
Using this complex representation of
\phi_{1}(y)
and (
33
) in the first and (
34
) in the last step, we obtain

\displaystyle e^{i\psi}M\phi_{0}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{1}}{a+1}\int_{\{x\geq\theta y\}\cap[-a,1]}e^{i\psi}M\phi_{0}(x)\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}}\,e^{i\psi}M\,\frac{z_{0}}{a+1}\int_{\{x\geq\theta y\}\cap[-a,1]}\phi_{0}(x)\mathrm{d}x=\frac{z_{1}}{z_{0}}e^{i\psi}M\,\phi_{0}(y),

which implies
z_{1}=z_{0}
. The second part of the assertion follows simply by the fact that
u
is a polynomial, so that the different zeros are necessarily separated.
∎

As a next step, we prove the asymptotic behavior of
(p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta))
for
y\in A_{m}
,
m\geq 1
.

Lemma 6.12
.

In the orange region, for
0\leq m\leq p
and
y\in A_{m}
, it holds

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)\sim\lambda^{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}y^{i}c_{i}(\theta,\lambda,a,m)

with

\displaystyle c_{i}(\theta,\lambda,a,m)\coloneqq\frac{-1}{u^{\prime}\big(\frac{1}{\lambda}\big)}\left(\frac{-1}{\lambda(a+1)}\right)^{i}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\sum_{{\ell}=0}^{m-i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}=m-i-{\ell}\\
i_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}s(m\!-\!i\!-\!(r\!-\!1)\!-\!\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}i_{j},i_{r}),

where
s(k,i)=s_{\frac{1}{\lambda}}(k,i)
is given in (
6
).

Proof.

Our goal is to use Lemma
2.6
. By Corollary
6.8
, we have with
z_{0}=\frac{1}{\lambda}

\displaystyle\hat{P}_{a,y,\theta}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)}{1-zF(z)}=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)\frac{z_{0}-z}{1-zF(z)}}{z_{0}-z},

and we have to show that for some
z_{1}>z_{0}
, the function
f(z)=G_{a,y,\theta}(z)\frac{z_{0}-z}{1-zF(z)}=\frac{G_{a,y,\theta}(z)(z_{0}-z)}{u(z)}
is analytic on
|z|<z_{1}
and
f(z_{0})\neq 0
.

The fact that
G_{a,y,\theta}(z)
is entire follows directly from its definition. The fact that
z\mapsto\frac{z_{0}-z}{1-zF(z)}
is analytic on
|z|<z_{1}
for some
z_{1}>z_{0}
is a conclusion from Lemma
6.1
and Lemma
6.11
. Further,
G_{a,y,\theta}(z_{0})\neq 0
follows from Lemma
6.10
.
Now, Lemma
2.6
gives

\displaystyle p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)

\displaystyle\sim f(z_{0})\lambda^{n+1}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n+1}\frac{-1}{u^{\prime}(z_{0})}\sum_{i=0}^{m}\left(\frac{-z_{0}y}{a+1}\right)^{i}\frac{\theta^{\frac{i(i+1)}{2}}}{i!}\sum_{{\ell}=0}^{m-i}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1}+\dots+i_{\ell}=m-i-{\ell}\\
i_{r}\geq 0\end{subarray}}\prod_{r=1}^{\ell}s(m-i-(r-1)-\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}i_{j},i_{r})

\displaystyle=\lambda^{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}y^{i}c_{i}(\theta,\lambda,a,m)

with coefficients given in (
5
).
∎

Now we can infer the claim of Theorem
1.2
for the orange region.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
(O).

Lemma
6.12
gives

p_{n}^{a,y}(\theta)\sim\lambda^{n+1}\sum_{i=0}^{m}y^{i}c_{i}(\theta,\lambda,a,m)=\lambda^{n+1}h(y)
for
0\leq m\leq p
and
y\in A_{m}
and
h
as defined in the claim of Theorem
1.2
(O).
Like in the other regions, we use Lemma
2.3
to conclude to the Doob limit. The theorem also states the value of the eigenfunction for the case
\theta x\in A_{p}=[\frac{-a}{\theta^{p}},1]
. That part does not follow from the asymptotics and is not required for the application of Lemma
2.3
. The value of
h
for this case can be verified directly from the eigenvalue equation.
∎
