2.2

Ellingsrud-Skjelbred spectral sequences are constructed as follows: Consider the following sequence of functors

(i)\quad Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{(-)^{G}}Mod(S)\xrightarrow{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}}Mod(S),

(ii)\quad Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}}Mod(S[G])\xrightarrow{(-)^{G}}Mod(S)

We then notice

(a)

The above compositions are equal.

(b)

It is possible to use Grothendieck spectral sequence of composite of functors to both (i) and (ii) above; see

[
7
, 8.6.2]
.

Following Ellingsrud-Skjelbred we let
H^{n}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,-)
denote the

right derived functor of this composite
functor. So by (i) and (ii) we have two first quadrant spectral sequences for each

-module

(\alpha)\colon\quad\quad E_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}_{\mathfrak{a}}(H^{q}(G,M))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,M),\ \text{and}

(\beta)\colon\quad\quad\mathcal{E}_{2}^{p,q}=H^{p}(G,H^{q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\Longrightarrow H^{p+q}_{\mathfrak{a}}(G,M).

2.4

Let

-vector space of dimension

. Define
Define

(1)

(2)

for all

(3)

(4)

.

We note that

is a ring homomorphism of degree zero.

By separating out homogeneous components we obtain

\mathcal{P}^{i}\colon\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q}[V]
by the requirement

P(\xi)(f)=\sum_{i\geq 0}\mathcal{P}^{i}(f)\xi^{i}.

Note
\mathcal{P}^{0}
is the identity.
The operations
\mathcal{P}^{i}
are called the
Steenrod reduced power operations
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and when

, also denoted by
\mathcal{S}{q}^{i}
and refereed to as

.
We will use the following property:

\mathcal{P}^{k}(uv)=\sum_{i+j=k}\mathcal{P}^{i}(u)\mathcal{P}^{j}(v).

.

Let

on

by giving the trivial action on

. We note that

\mathcal{P}^{i}
restrict to maps from

to

.

The following result follows easily from the definition of morphism of Cartan
S
-modules.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
M,N
be Cartan
S
-modules and let

-modules. Then
\ker f,\operatorname{image}f,\operatorname{coker}f
are Cartan

\ker f\rightarrow M
,
\operatorname{image}f\rightarrow N
and the surjection
N\rightarrow\operatorname{coker}f
are morphism of Cartan

As a consequence we obtain

Corollary 3.4

Let

of Cartan

By

are Cartan submodules of

The following Lemma is critical.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
M
be a Cartan
S
-module. Then
\operatorname{ann}M
is a
\mathcal{P}^{*}
-invariant ideal of
S
.

Proof.

Let
s\in\operatorname{ann}M
. We show
\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\in\operatorname{ann}M
for all
i\geq 0
. This is certainly true when
i=0
as
\mathcal{P}^{0}
is the identity. Let
m\in M
.
Then we have

0=\mathcal{Q}^{1}(sm)=\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)m+s\mathcal{Q}^{1}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)m.

The second equality is as
\mathcal{Q}^{1}(m)\in M
. Thus
\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)\in\operatorname{ann}M
.

Assume
\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\in\operatorname{ann}M
for

where

. Then we have

0=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(sm)=\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(m)=\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)m.

The second equality is because
\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\in\operatorname{ann}M
for
i<r
. By the above equality we get
\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)\in\operatorname{ann}M
.
The result follows.
∎

4.
The Cartan

-module structure on

In this section we prove two critical results. We first prove

(with setup as in

for all

The next result is also critical.

.

(with setup as in
1.1
). Let

be homogeneous. Then the natural map

is morphism of Cartan
S
-modules (the Cartan module structures on

We first give

Proof of Theorem
4.1
.

We first define the Cartan
S
-module structure on
M=S_{x}
. Let
u\in M
. Write
u=a/x^{m}
with
m\geq 0
(minimal).
Define
\mathcal{Q}^{0}(u)=u
. Assume
\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u)
is defined for
i<r
. Define

\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)=\frac{1}{x^{m}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right).

We first show that we dont need a minimal representation of
u
to define
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)
. More precisely let
u=b/x^{l}
where
l>m
. Define
\mathcal{Q}^{0}_{b}(u)=u
. Assume
\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{b}(u)
is defined for
i<r
. Define

\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)=\frac{1}{x^{l}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{l})\mathcal{Q}^{j}_{b}(u)\right).

Claim 1:
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)
for all
r\geq 0
.

We prove the claim by induction on
r\geq 0
. We have
\mathcal{Q}^{0}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{0}_{b}(u)=u
.
Set
c=l-m
. We have
b=x^{c}a
. We first prove the case
r=1
.
We have

\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})u,\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{P}^{1}(b)=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}_{b}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{l})u.

We have
b=x^{c}a
.
So we have

\mathcal{P}^{1}(b)=x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})a.

We have

\displaystyle x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})a

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{1}(b)

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}_{b}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{l})u

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}_{b}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})x^{m}u+x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})u.

As
x^{m}u=a
, we obtain
x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}_{b}^{1}(u)+x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{1}(m)u
. By comparing earlier expression of
\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)
in terms of
u
we obtain
x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{b}(u)
. Cancelling
x^{l}
we obtain
\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{b}(u)
.

Now assume we have proved
\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{b}(u)
for
i<r
. We prove it for
i=r
.
We have

\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u).

Set

\xi=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u).

Note that it suffices to prove
\xi=\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)
.
We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{l})\mathcal{Q}^{j}_{b}(u),

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{l})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u),\ \ \text{by induction hypothesis}.

So

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{l})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{l}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)+x^{c}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{c}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-1}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-1-i}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)

\displaystyle=x^{c}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(x^{c})\left(\sum_{i=0}^{r-2}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-2-i}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=3}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{c}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=3}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)

\displaystyle=\cdots\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle=x^{c}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\cdots+\mathcal{P}^{r}(x^{c})a.

We also have

b=x^{c}a
. So we have

\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)=\mathcal{P}^{r}(x^{c}a)=x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(x^{c})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\cdots+\mathcal{P}^{r}(x^{c})a.

Comparing terms we get
x^{c}\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)=x^{c}\xi
. So
\xi=\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)
. As discussed earlier this implies
\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{b}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)
. Thus Claim 1 is proved.

Next we prove that the operators
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(-)\colon S_{x}\rightarrow S_{x}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear.

Let
u=a/x^{m}
and
v=b/x^{n}
. We first show
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u+v)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+\mathcal{Q}^{r}(v)
.
We may assume that
n=\max\{m,n\}
. Set
u=c/x^{n}
. We prove the assertion by induction on
r
. When
r=0
then
\mathcal{Q}^{0}
is the identity map. So we have nothing to prove.
Assume
r\geq 1
and that we have proved
\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u+v)=\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u)+\mathcal{Q}^{i}(v)
for
0\leq i<r
. We have
b+c=x^{n}(u+v)
. We have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(b+c)

\displaystyle=x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u+v)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{n})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u+v),

\displaystyle=x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u+v)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{n})\left(\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)+\mathcal{Q}^{j}(v)\right).

The second equality holds by induction hypothesis.
We also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(c)

\displaystyle=x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{n})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u),\text{and}

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)

\displaystyle=x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(v)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{n})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(v).

As
\mathcal{P}^{r}(b+c)=\mathcal{P}^{r}(b)+\mathcal{P}^{r}(c)
it follows that
x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u+v)=x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+x^{n}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(v)
. Canceling
x^{n}
we get that
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u+v)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+\mathcal{Q}^{r}(v)
. Thus by induction we get that
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(-)
are additive.

Let
u=a/x^{m}
and let
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
. We want to show that
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(\alpha u)=\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)
for all
r\geq 0
. When
r=0
then
\mathcal{Q}^{0}
is the identity map. So we have nothing to prove.
Assume
r\geq 1
and that we have proved
\mathcal{Q}^{i}(\alpha u)=\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u)
for
0\leq i<r
.
Then by definition

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}^{r}(\alpha u)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{m}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{r}(\alpha a)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(\alpha u)\right),

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{x^{m}}\left(\alpha\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right),\quad\text{by induction hypothesis},

\displaystyle=\alpha\frac{1}{x^{m}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right),

\displaystyle=\alpha\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)

Thus
\mathcal{Q}^{r}(-)
are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear for all
r\geq 0
.

Claim-2: If
u\in S_{x}
and
s\in S
then

\mathcal{Q}^{r}(su)=\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\quad\text{for all $r\geq 0$}.

We prove this result by induction on
r
. When
r=0
we have nothing to show as
\mathcal{Q}^{0}
and
\mathcal{P}^{0}
are identity maps.
Let
u=a/x^{m}
. So
a=x^{m}u
and
sa=x^{m}su
. We first show the result when
r=1
. By definition we have

\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})u,\quad\text{and}

\mathcal{P}^{1}(sa)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(su)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})su.

We also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{1}(sa)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)a+s\mathcal{P}^{1}(a),

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)a+sx^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})su,

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)x^{m}u+sx^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})su.

So we obtain
x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}(su)=x^{m}\mathcal{P}^{1}(s)u+x^{m}s\mathcal{Q}^{1}(u)
. Cancelling
x^{m}
we obtain Claim-2 for
r=1
.

Let
r\geq 2
. Assume Claim-2 is satisfied for all
i<r
. We prove Claim 2 for
i=r
.
We have

\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u),\quad\text{and}

\mathcal{P}^{r}(sa)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(su)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(su).

We also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(sa)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)a+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\mathcal{P}^{i}(a),

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)a+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{i-j}(u)\right),

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)a+x^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{i}(u)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{i-j}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=x^{m}\left(\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})\left(\sum_{i+j=r-1}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{i-j}(u)\right)

\displaystyle=x^{m}\left(\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)+\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-1}(su)

\displaystyle\quad+\mathcal{P}^{2}(x^{m})\left(\sum_{i+j=r-2}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{r-i}(s)\left(\sum_{j=3}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{i-j}(u)\right),

\displaystyle=\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle=x^{m}\left(\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(su).

So we obtain

x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}(su)=x^{m}\left(\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(s)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right).

Claim-2 for
i=r
follows by cancelling
x^{m}
.

It is clear that if
u=s\in S
then we may consider
u=s/x^{0}
.
So we have

\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)=\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(1)\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)=1.\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u).

We now prove uniqueness of our Cartan module structure on
S_{x}
. Suppose
S_{x}
has another set of
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear operators
\mathcal{H}^{r}(-)
giving it a Cartan module structure such that
\mathcal{H}^{r}(s)=\mathcal{P}^{r}(s)
for all
s\in S
.
We prove that
\mathcal{H}^{r}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u)
for all
r\geq 0
and for all

. This is trivial for
r=0
as
\mathcal{H}^{0}(-)
and
\mathcal{Q}^{0}(-)
are identity. Suppose
\mathcal{H}^{i}(-)=\mathcal{Q}^{i}(-)
for
i<r
. Let
u=a/x^{m}
with

.
Then
a=x^{m}u
. So we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{H}^{r}(a)=\sum_{i+j=r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{H}^{j}(u)=x^{m}\mathcal{H}^{r}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{H}^{j}(u)

\displaystyle=x^{m}\mathcal{H}^{r}(u)+\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u),\text{by induction hypothesis}

So we obtain

\mathcal{H}^{r}(u)=\frac{1}{x^{m}}\left(\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)-\sum_{i+j=r,j<r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{j}(u)\right)=\mathcal{Q}^{r}(u).

The result follows by induction.
∎

Next we give

Proof of Theorem
4.2
.

Set
M=S_{x}
and let
\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{M}\colon M\rightarrow M

. Set

and

\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)=\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})u+x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{M}(u)

\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad=\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})y^{m}u+x^{m}\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})u+x^{m}y^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{N}(u).

We also have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{1}(ay^{m})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}^{1}(a)y^{m}+a\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})

\displaystyle=(\mathcal{P}^{1}(x^{m})u+x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{1}_{M}(u))y^{m}+a\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m}).

\mathcal{P}^{1}(ay^{m})

. Thus the result holds for
r=1
.

Let
r\geq 2
. Assume
\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{M}(u)=\mathcal{Q}^{i}_{N}(u)
for

.
We have

\mathcal{P}^{r}(a)=x^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{M}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u).

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(ay^{m})

\displaystyle=x^{m}y^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{N}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m}y^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{N}(u)

So we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(ay^{m})

\displaystyle=x^{m}y^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{N}(u)+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u),

\displaystyle=x^{m}y^{m}\mathcal{Q}^{r}_{N}(u)+y^{m}\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\sum_{i=1}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{r}(ay^{m})

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})\left(\sum_{i=1}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i-1}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)\right)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\sum_{i=2}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=2}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(y^{m})\left(\sum_{i=2}^{r}\mathcal{P}^{i-2}(x^{m})\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}(u)\right)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=3}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-2}(a)+

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\sum_{i=3}^{r}\left(\sum_{j=3}^{i}\mathcal{P}^{j}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{i-j}(x^{m})\right)\mathcal{Q}^{r-i}_{M}(u)

\displaystyle=\cdots\cdots\cdots

\displaystyle=y^{m}\xi+\mathcal{P}^{1}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-1}(a)+\mathcal{P}^{2}(y^{m})\mathcal{P}^{r-2}(a)+\cdots+\mathcal{P}^{r}(y^{m})a.

∎

Finally we give

Proof of Corollary
1.11
.

Assume
S_{P}
is not Cohen-Macaulay.
If
P
is not a homogeneous prime ideal of

then let

be the prime ideal generated by all homogeneous elements of
P
. Then

; see
[
3
, 1.5.8]
; and
0ptS_{P}=0ptS_{P^{*}}+1
; see
[

. Thus
S_{P^{*}}
is not Cohen-Macaulay. So it suffices to assume

is a homogeneous prime ideal of
S
.

Let
P
be a homogeneous prime ideal in
S
with

not Cohen-Macaulay. Suppose if possible

x\in\{\mathbf{d}_{d,0},\mathbf{d}_{d,1},\mathbf{d}_{d,2}\}

∗
