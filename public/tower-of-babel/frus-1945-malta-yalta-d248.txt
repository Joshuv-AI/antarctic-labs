The President to the Ambassador in the Soviet Union (Harriman)[2]

[Excerpts]

top secret priority

[Washington,] 4 October 1944.

Number 76. Top Secret and Personal from the President to Ambassador Harriman.

. . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Will you please deliver the following message to Marshal Stalin at once:

. . . . . . . . . . . . . .

You will, by this time, have received from General Deane, the statement of our Combined Chiefs of Staff position relative to the war against Japan[3] and I want to reiterate to you how completely I accept the assurances which you have given us on this point. Our three countries are waging a successful war against Germany and we can surely join together with no less success in crushing a nation that I am sure in my heart is as great an enemy of Russia as she is of ours.

. . . . . . . . . . . . . .

Roosevelt

Source: Roosevelt Papers: Telegram

Notes
[2] Sent to the United States Naval Attaché, Moscow, via Navy channels. For the full text of the message, see ante, pp. 6–7.
[3] The President evidently was referring to the Joint Chiefs of Staff position alluded to infra.
