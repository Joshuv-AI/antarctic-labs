Isoenergetic degeneracy generically creates meandering invariant tori
Vadim Kaloshin, Illya Koval, Yi Pan

Abstract
. Consider the set of isoenergetically degenerate integrable Hamiltonians with two degrees of freedom. We show that a cusp-generic perturbation of a generic Hamiltonian in this set gives rise to meandering invariant tori – embedded Lagrangian tori which are not graphs. Moreover, an exponentially dense subset of perturbations admits higher order meandering tori, of all orders from two to infinity. These infinite order meanders have an endless nested structure.

3.
Some history and examples

3.1.
A brief historical survey

The study of meanders has a rich history. It dates back to 1956: in
[
17
]
meandering invariant tori have been found for Hamiltonian with one degree of freedom. Through the decades, many other numerical examples have been studied. We show some of them in the next subsection.

For an area-preserving twist map on
\mathbb{T}\times\mathbb{R}
, Birkhoff
[
3
]
proved that its essential invariant curves are graphs of Lipschitz functions. Furthermore, invariant curves always exist in a nearly-integrable case,
as shown by Moser
[
11
]
.
It is a natural question to ask what happens when the twist condition is violated.

In this nontwist setting, Herman
[
8
]
proved the existence of meandering invariant tori for some symplectic diffeomorphisms.
Later, Simó
[
15
]
studied invaraint curves of general nontwist maps. In fact, he proposed an idea to show the existence of meanders for a generic perturbation of a nontwist area-preserving map. Furthermore, he provided a numerical example of a higher order meander. These insights and examples serve as the starting point for our study. Although our current results are established in the Hamiltonian setting, we expect similar results for area-preserving nontwist maps.

As for Hamiltonian systems, the Birkhoff theorem was extended by Arnaud
[
1
]
to Tonelli Hamiltonians on compact and connected manifolds, showing that zero section isotopic invariant Lagrangian submanifolds are always Lagrangian graphs. Here, Lagrangian assumption is important – as without it Arnaud
[
2
]
constructed a counterexample by essentially gluing smaller dimensional invariant tori together. Moving beyond Tonelli Hamiltonians, we work with isoenergetically degenerate Hamiltonian systems, constructing meandering invariant tori that are Lagrangian and isotopic to graphs.

More recently, González-Enríquez, Haro and De la Llave
[
7
,
6
]
studied
nontwist systems and creation of meanders using the methods of bifurcation theory. Besides, non-essential invariant curves of meandering shape
appear to be common even for twist maps. Whenever a generic map has an elliptic periodic point whose eigenvalue passes through
\exp(2\pi i/3)
, a non-twist invariant torus arises around the periodic orbit
[
5
]
. In particular, this mechanism gives rise to invariant curves of meandering shape for the area-preserving Hénon map
[
5
]
.

3.2.
Possible applications

Theorem
D

[

]

This example models the atmosphere of the planet and it may explain the meanders we frequently see on weather maps. Here,

Example 3.2

(

The Kepler problem in celestial mechanics is one of the most classical integrable systems. In Delaunay variables, the Hamiltonian depends only on one action

4.
Structure of the proof

A brief outline of the proof was given in Section
1.1
. We now explain each major stage in detail by presenting simplified versions of theorems. We also provide a roadmap of the proof, highlighting the order and role of each stage in Figure
10
.

4.1.
Constructing first step meanders

The first step has two goals. The first is to construct a first-step meander. The second, relevant when a higher order meander is desired, is to carry out the first step of its construction. Both goals are achieved via the same scheme, as the higher-order meander is built upon the first-order one. So, the two cases diverge only at the final stage. We therefore start with describing the scheme of the first step meander construction.

Stage 1: Resonant Normal forms and approximation by one degree of freedom

We start with a Hamiltonian with two degrees of freedom

(4.1.1)

H(\varphi,I)=h(I)+p(\varphi,I),\qquad\varphi\in\mathbb{T}^{2},\ \ \ I\in\mathbb{R}^{2}.

Hamiltonians with one degree of freedom are always integrable, with invariant curves given by energy levels. Therefore, a natural approach to studying invariant tori of a two-degree-of-freedom Hamiltonian is to reduce it to one degree of freedom. The resonant normal form provides precisely such a reduction, up to a stretched exponential small error:

Theorem 1
.

If
\ \nabla h(I_{*})\cdot(j_{1},j_{2})=0
, then there exists a symplectic coordinate change
L\circ\Psi
such that in the new coordinates
H
has a form

(4.1.2)

H(\varphi,I)=h(I)+g(\varphi_{1},I)+f(\varphi,I),\qquad\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}),

where
g(\varphi_{1},I)
is of order
\varepsilon
and
f(\varphi,I)
is stretched exponential small in
\varepsilon
.

This coordinate change is a composition of
L
and
\Psi
.
L
is simply a linear coordinate change in
\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z})
that reduces the case of arbitrary
(j_{1},j_{2})\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}\backslash\{0\}
into
(1,0)
. The second coordinate change
\Psi
is more complicated, given by cohomological equation solving to get the resonant normal form, where
[
14
]
is applied.

Stage 2: Constructing a meandering channel with one degree of freedom

Since
f
is stretched exponential small, we will first study the leading Hamiltonian
h(I)+g(\varphi_{1},I)
. Since the system is independent of
\varphi_{2}
, the action
I_{2}
is conserved and may be treated as a parameter. Consequently,
h(I)+g(\varphi_{1},I)
defines a one-degree-of-freedom Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}_{I_{2}}(\varphi_{1},I_{1})
parametrized by
I_{2}
.

Figure 9
.

h(I)+p(x,I)=I-I^{3}/3+\varepsilon\cos 2\pi x

\varepsilon=0.2

\varepsilon=1

We aim to show that the Hamiltonian
\mathcal{H}
has a meandering channel, where an example is shown in Figure

. In fact, our non-degeneracy conditions guarantee the following result.

Theorem 2
.

Assume
h
is generic isoenergetically degenerate and
g
is
\|p\|_{r,s}^{1+\eta}
-non-degenerate. There exists a parameter
I_{2}^{*}
, such that the Hamiltonian

(4.1.3)

\mathcal{H}_{I_{2}^{*}}(\varphi_{1},I_{1})=h(I_{1},I_{2}^{*})+g(\varphi_{1},I_{1},I_{2}^{*})

has a meandering channel.

Stage 3: Action-angle estimates

To extend the existence of invariant tori from
\mathcal{H}_{I_{2}^{*}}
to
\mathcal{H}_{I_{2}^{*}}+f
, we will apply a KAM-type theorem. As KAM theorem requires the unperturbed Hamiltonian to depend on actions only, we pass to action-angle coordinates as follows.

Theorem 3
.

There exists a local action-angle coordinate change

\Phi:(\theta,J)\rightarrow(\varphi,I)
,
so that locally we have

(h+g)\circ\Phi(\theta,J)=h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J).

Moreover, its analytic properties (like the complex domain of definition and bounds on its image) are given in a quantitative way.

We point out that analytic estimates are needed throughout. After all the coordinate changes above, the original system
h+p
takes the form

(4.1.4)

h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)+f\circ\Phi(\theta,J).

we require
f\circ\Phi
to remain sufficiently small so that (
4.1.4
) can be regarded as a perturbation of
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
.

On one hand, the smallness of
f\circ\Phi
allows us to apply a KAM theorem. In addition, KAM requires conditions on the integrable part
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
: namely, non-degeneracy and a Diophantine rotation number.

On the other hand, if we seek for a higher order meander, (
4.1.4
) has the same structure as the original system (
4.1.1
), with
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
playing the role of
h
and
f\circ\Phi
that of
p
. As in the first step, we require
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
to be generic isoenergetically degenerate, that is, to satisfy Conditions (
a
)–(
e
). This is made precise by the following result.

Theorem 4
.

The expressions in Conditions (
a
)–(
e
) for
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
w.r.t. action-angle variables
(\theta,J)
can be restated quantitatively in terms of expressions in the original variables
(\varphi,I)
.

Stage 4: Choosing an invariant curve in the meandering channel

We note that the conditions required for KAM and for the higher-order meander preparation are distinct. For instance, KAM requires a Diophantine rotation number, whereas Condition (
e
) requires it to be rational. Nevertheless, in both cases one can adjust the parameter
I_{2}
to change the channel, and vary the choice of torus within the channel. Since choosing an invariant torus amounts to choosing
J
, we have the following result.

Theorem 5
.

Both of the following cases are realizable by appropriate choices of
I_{2}
and
J
:

(1)

the rotation number is Diophantine and
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
is non-degenerate at
J
,

(2)

h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
is generic isoenergetically degenerate at
J
.

The subsequent argument splits into two cases according to Theorem
5
. Case (2) leads to the second step and the construction of a higher-order meander. Case (1) allows us to apply a KAM theorem and conclude the existence of a first order meander. Applying the KAM theorem with the quantitative bounds of
[
13
]
, we obtain the following.

Theorem 6
.

If case (1) of Theorem
5
holds, then the Hamiltonian (
4.1.4
) admits an invariant torus close to that of
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)
.

Finally, we transfer the invariant torus given by Theorem

By case (2) of Theorem

, the second step begins with
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}(J)+f\circ\Phi(\theta,J)
, where
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
is generically isoenergetically degenerate and
f\circ\Phi(\theta,J)
is small. The overall framework is thus analogous to the first step.

The key difficulty, however, is that Theorem
5
provides no information on
f\circ\Phi
beyond smallness. In particular, it does not supply the non-degeneracy conditions (
f
)–(

), which are essential for meanders to appear. This is the main technical obstacle of the second step.

4.2.1.
Introducing corrector
\mu

To address this difficulty, we allow a small modification
p
. Replacing
p
by
p+\mu
introduces a correction to
f

f\rightarrow f+\mu\circ L\circ\Psi,

giving us some control over
f
. Since
f
arises from the resonant normal form (Theorem
1
), to guarantee that the corrector
\mu
does not average out,
\mu
must be chosen small enough not to exceed the upper bound on
f
given there.

However, the irregularity of
\Psi
forces
\mu
to be smaller than any polynomial of
\|f\|
. This correction is therefore too small to directly establish Conditions (
f
)–(
h
). Instead, we work with more delicate non-degeneracy conditions of types I–IV.

As in the first step, a parallel resonant normal form Theorem
1
yields
g_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
and
f_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
, and an analogue of Theorem
2
provides a channel for some parameter
I_{2}
. However, without any non-degeneracy assumption on
g_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
, this channel may be degenerate. It is precisely where the corrector
\mu
plays its role.

4.2.2.
Resolving degeneracies with
\mu

We establish the following:

Theorem 7
.

There exists
\mu
such that, for some value of
I_{2}
, the Hamiltonian
h_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}+g_{\mbox{\raisebox{-2.33333pt}{\resizebox{894449}{458752}{\hbox{\raisebox{1.75pt}{$(2)$}}}}}}
admits a non-degenerate meandering channel.

Constructing an analytic
\mu
explicitly turns out to be difficult. Instead, we work with a smooth approximation and recover real analyticity afterwards. We consider a parametrized family of functions and estimate the measure of the set of parameters for which the resulting channel is degenerate. By a careful choice of family, this degenerate set has sufficiently small measure, yielding the genericity condition on
p
in the second step.

In particular, we point out that by pulling back the smooth functions to the original coordinates and approximating by analytic functions on
W_{s}\mathbb{T}^{2}\times V_{r}D
, we manage to deform
p
without loss of analyticity.

The remainder of the second step parallels the first. Theorems
3
and
4
apply directly. Once the channel is non-degenerate, an analogue of Theorem
5
guarantees that both the KAM case and the generically isoenergetically degenerate case are realizable for certain invariant torus within the channel. In the former case, we conclude the second step via a KAM theorem parallel to Theorem
6
; in the latter, we proceed to the third step.

4.3.
Constructing general step meander

To construct a Hamiltonian with meanders of arbitrary or infinite order, we iterate the procedure from the second step. All subsequent steps follow the same plan as the second step and Theorem
C
is established by induction.

A key feature of the iteration is that the deformation
\mu_{(n)}

at step
n
is extremely small. To be precise, its size is bounded by an exponential tower in
1/\varepsilon
with
n-1
exponentials. To construct a meander of infinite order, the smallness condition on
\varepsilon
must not accumulate additional constraints at each step. In fact, we prove that all inequalities involving exponential towers hold uniformly in
n
. And the construction of deformation by analytic approximation as in second step preserves the region of analyticity.
This completes the outline of the proof.
