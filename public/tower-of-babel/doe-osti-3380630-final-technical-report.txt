Title: Broadening participation in (sub)Urban watershed Science in the Southeast (BUSS)
 Period of Performance: July 21, 2022 - December 31, 2025
 PI: Sarah H. Ledford

 1

The objectives of this grant were to (1) develop partnerships between Georgia State University
(GSU) Geosciences and the Department of Energy (DOE) by engaging students and faculty at
GSU with Oak Ridge National Lab (ORNL) facilities and research; (2) create research and
training opportunities at ORNL for students from GSU; and (3) solidify and continue the
partnership beyond the length of this grant. This was done by providing funding for M.S. and
undergraduate students at GSU, with M.S. students being partially supervised by DOE staff. In
addition, the grants supported travel to the DOE ESS PI meeting for two years for the PI and
graduate students.

Summary of Activities:
The key activity for this grant was the financial support of research projects for GSU Geoscience
graduate students that included research experience at ORNL and research mentoring from
ORNL staff. This grant supported Graduate Research Assistantships for three M.S. students in
Geosciences at GSU, all of whom also interned over the summer at ORNL through the MSIPP
program. Two students have graduated the program, each with an ORNL scientist on their thesis
committee, while one student has completed her first year and is currently an intern at ORNL.
Graduate students and the P.I. attend at the DOE ESS PI meetings in 2023 and 2024 to share
research and build collaborations.

Along with graduate students, this grant funded eight undergraduate research assistants at GSU
from fall 2022-spring 2024 who gained key field and lab experiences. Undergraduate research
assistants collected samples weekly to measure E. coli across the South River watershed in
southeastern Atlanta.

Six papers, theses, and data sets came out of this grant, contributing to our knowledge of streams

 • Presswood, Deandre*. Hydrologic Disturbances and Land Cover: A Study on Stream
 Metabolism Dynamics. Aug. 2024, Georgia State University.
       https://doi.org/10.57709/37408716. M.S. Thesis.
 • In-stream metabolism, modeled from dissolved oxygen time-series, in the
 Piedmont ecoregion is impacted by both flow and land cover. Gross Primary
 Productivity (GPP) resistance to storm events decreases as the flow size increases,
 while Ecosystem Respiration (ER) remains stable across flow event size. GPP
 also took longer to recover after larger storms. Watersheds with mixed land cover
 (agricultural, forested, and urban) had lower GPP resistance and longer recovery
 times than urban or forested watersheds, while ER was not impacted by land
 cover.
 • Ledford, Sarah H.+, Richard Milligan+, Zakia Riaz, Jessica Sterling, Michael Meyer, and
 Jacqueline Echols. 2025. Examining drivers of E. coli distribution across urban

 headwaters using community science. Environmental Research: Water. doi:10.1088/3033-

 • Four years of E. coli monitoring in a watershed draining Atlanta, GA, showed that
 storm events increase E. coli levels, explaining about 50% of temporal variability.
 Median E. coli levels across watersheds were not well explained by land cover or
 socio-demographic descriptors of the contributing watersheds. However, some
 sites have consistent E. coli whiles others are more variable in time, and
 differences were correlated to development density.

 • Field samples of water and sediment from a stream from below a hazardous waste
 site show elevated concentrations of heavy metals and other trace metals.
 However, there are differences in the transport mechanisms between rare earth

 • In preparation: Legacy pollution in Atlanta urban streams: Bringing historical context to
 the spatial distribution of heavy metals. For submission to Journal of Environmental
 Management
 • This manuscript focuses on identifying the source of elevated metal contaminants
 in the South River watershed through a field-based investigation of their spatial
 distribution. It was found that elevated heavy metal concentrations were sourced
 from the mismanagement of legacy pollution from the nearby Tift Hazardous
 Waste Site. The project was enhanced through collaborations with community
 leaders, facilitated by working with members of the Community-Soil-Air-Water
 NSF-funded learning ecosystem, as well as with research staff at ORNL.
 Specifically, the staff at ORNL assisted in the analytical measurements conducted
 on the aqueous samples previously collected in Atlanta.
 • In preparation: Exploration of elevated rare earth elements in an Atlanta, USA stream:
 source, transport, and transformation. For submission to Applied Geochemistry
 • This manuscript focuses on analyzing the geochemical dynamics of the movement
 of rare earth elements in the same urban watershed. Briefly, we determined
 elevated REEs in the South River are sourced from leaching of the riverbed and
 their fate further downstream are controlled by shifts in pH and interactions with
 phosphate. ORNL was critical in either measuring the samples themselves or
 training me on analytical instrumentation, so the lead student author could
 perform his own measurements.

Students from GSU Geosciences are actively applying for, and being awarded, research
opportunities at ORNL. Along with the student supported on this grant, one additional GSU M.S.
student has already received support through the MSIPP program to research at ORNL during
summer 2026. In addition, research collaborations between GSU faculty and ORNL staff,
especially around stream metabolism and heavy metals, continue, focused on the Watershed
Dynamics and Evolution (WaDE) Scientific Focus Area.
