Sobolev regularity of the symmetric gradient of solutions to a class of ϕ \phi -Laplacian systems
Flavia Giannetti - Antonia Passarelli di Napoli

Abstract
. The paper deals with the second order regularity properties of the weak solutions u ∈ W 1 , ϕ ​ ( Ω , ℝ n ) u\in W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}) of systems of the form − div ​ A ​ ( x , ℰ ​ u ) = f , -\mathrm{div}\,A(x,\mathcal{E}u)=f, in a bounded domain Ω ⊂ ℝ n \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n} , n > 2 n>2 , where the operator A ⁡ ( x , P ) A(x,P) is Lipschitz continuous with respect to the x x -variable and satisfies growth conditions with respect to the second variable expressed through a Young function Φ \Phi . We prove the Sobolev regularity of a function of the symmetric gradient ℰ ​ u \mathcal{E}u that takes into account the nonlinear growth of the operator A ⁡ ( x , P ) A(x,P) , assuming that the force term f f belongs to a suitable Orlicz-Sobolev space. The main result is achieved through some uniform higher differentiability estimates for solutions to a class of approximating problems, constructed adding singular higher order perturbations to the system.

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we study the regularity of weak solutions to systems of the type

(1.1)

-\mathrm{div}\,A(x,\mathcal{E}u)=f,\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,\Omega

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, is a bounded domain,
\mathcal{E}u
denotes the symmetric part of the gradient
Du
, i.e.

\mathcal{E}u:=\tfrac{1}{2}\big(Du+(Du)^{T}\big),

and the function

f:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a given measurable inhomogeneity.

The mapping

A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
, where
\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
denotes the space of symmetric
n\times n
matrices, will be assumed continuous in
x
and satisfying some ellipticity and growth conditions with respect to the second variable expressed through a Young function. More precisely,
we shall assume that
P\to A(x,P)\in C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\setminus\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}})
and that there exist positive constants
\nu,L_{1},L_{2},K
such that

(1.2)

\displaystyle\big\langle A(x,P)-A(x,Q),P-Q\big\rangle

\displaystyle\geq\nu\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|^{2}

(1.3)

\displaystyle|A(x,P)-A(x,Q)|

\displaystyle\leq L_{1}\,\phi^{\prime\prime}(|P|+|Q|)|P-Q|,

(1.4)

\displaystyle|A(x,P)|

\displaystyle\leq L_{2}\,\phi^{\prime}(|P|),

(1.5)

\displaystyle|A(x,P)-A(y,P)|

\displaystyle\leq K|x-y|\phi^{\prime}(|P|),

for a.e.
x\in\Omega
and every
P,Q\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\mathrm{sym}}
. Here
\phi\in C^{2}([0,\infty))
is a Young function satisfying the
\Delta_{2}
and
\nabla_{2}
conditions that can be formulated introducing the so called Simonenko indices of
\phi^{\prime}
shifted by
1
, i.e.

(1.6)

\displaystyle i_{\phi}=\inf_{t>0}\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}+1\quad\text{and}\quad s_{\phi}=\sup_{t>0}\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)}+1.

and requiring that

(1.7)

\displaystyle 1<i_{\phi}\leq s_{\phi}<\infty.

Note that
\phi(t)
is not necessarily a power function and therefore the operator
A(x,P)
can be strongly nonlinear in the second variable.

Systems such as in (
1.1
) naturally arise in mathematical models describing numerous physical phenomena. One of the most relevant example is given by systems describing the motion of non-Newtonian fluids, which are
incompressible, subject to constant pressure and for which the relation between the deformation velocity
\mathcal{E}u
and the deviatoric tensor stress
A(x,P)

is nonlinear.

Moreover, it happens to run into systems as in (
1.1
) when studying classical theories of plasticity and nonlinear elasticity. Here we deal with higher differentiability properties of
\mathcal{E}u
for weak solutions
u
to the system (
1.1
). It is worth pointing out that,
recently, many papers have been devoted to the study of this kind of regularity of
\mathcal{E}u
.

For example, we mention
[
24
]
and
[
11
]
, where
p
-Navier-Stokes and
p
-Stokes systems have been examined, respectively for
p\geq 2
and for
1<p<2
, and
[
25
,
15
]
where Navier-Stokes systems with an Orlicz growth of the type considered here, possibly of power type
\phi(t)=\frac{t^{p}}{p}
for any
p\in(1,\infty)
, are studied. Interior and global regularity for nonlinear systems of the type

-\mathrm{div}\,A(\mathcal{E}u)=f,

have been the subject of
[
34
]
and
[
5
]
, respectively under

p
-growth,
1<p<2
, and
\phi
-growth.

The nonlinear nature of the problem implies that one cannot expect, in general,
the existence of the second weak derivatives. However, the extra differentiability can be expressed through
a nonlinear function of the symmetric gradient that takes into account the precise growth of the operator
A(x,P)
. Actually, already in the case
\Delta_{p}u=0
, the quantity
V(Du):=|Du|^{\frac{p-2}{2}}Du
plays a key role in the regularity theory of the gradient of weak solutions (see
[
27
]
and the references therein).

Accordingly, here we shall express the regularity of
\mathcal{E}u
through the function
V:\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
associated to
\phi
defined by

(1.8)

V(P):=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\sqrt{\frac{\phi^{\prime}(|P|)}{|P|}}\,P&\mbox{ if $P\neq 0$}\\[4.30554pt]
0&\mbox{ if $P=0$},\end{array}\right.

introduced in
[
17
]
and since then widely used in the context of Orlicz growth.

Most of the above mentioned papers deal with an inhomogeneity
f
that is not weakly differentiable and this, in general, results in a higher differentiability for the solutions of fractional order.
Indeed, the higher differentiability of integer order cannot be expected already for solutions to the
p
-harmonic type equations, if
p>2
, unless some differentiability even of fractional order is assumed on the datum
f
, while for
1<p<2
, the situation changes (see
[
6
,
5
]
and
[
16
,
11
]
). It is worth pointing out that if one wishes to express the higher differentiability of the symmetric gradient through the operator itself, it is sufficient to assume the datum
f\in L^{2}
(see for example
[
14
]
).

Since we deal with a general Young function
\phi
, we shall assume
f
differentiable,
f\in W^{1,\phi^{*}}
with
\phi^{*}
conjugate of
\phi
to be precise, even if such assumption can be more restrictive than necessary for some choices of
\phi
.

Related results on the
W^{1,2}_{\rm loc}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
regularity of
V(\mathcal{E}u)
for local solutions to Stokes systems, under various growth conditions on the differential operator and different integrability assumptions of the right-hand side, can be found in the papers
[
7
,
8
,
19
,
32
]
. However, all of those contributions concern operators independent of the space variable.

To our knowledge, no higher differentiability results of integer order for solutions of systems as in (
1.1
) are available in case of operators depending on the space variable and with Orlicz growth and our aim here is to fill this gap. Our main result states as follows

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A:\Omega\times\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}
be a mapping that is in
C^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}}\setminus\{0\})\cap C^{0}(\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}_{\textup{sym}})
with respect to the second variable and satisfying
the properties (
1.2
) (
1.3
), (
1.4
) and (
1.5
). Assume that (
1.7
) is in force and that
f\in W^{1,\phi^{\star}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
. If

is a weak solution to (

), then

and the following inequality holds for every pair of concentric balls

(1.9)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{\rho}}|D(V(\mathcal{E}u))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{r}}\phi(|Du|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\int_{B_{r}}\phi^{*}\left(|Df|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

for a constant
c=c(\nu,L_{1},L_{2},K,i_{\phi},s_{\phi},n,\rho,r)
.

We refer to
[
5
]
for an analogous result for operator
\phi
-Laplace systems independent of the
x
-variable.

We rely on the following notion of weak solutions to systems of the type (
1.1
).

Definition 1.2
.

We call

a weak solution of
the system (
1.1
) if

(1.10)

\int_{\Omega}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}u),\mathcal{E}\eta\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}f\cdot\eta\,\mathrm{d}x

is satisfied for every

\eta\in W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Higher differentiability results for both the full and the symmetric gradient are usually obtained by means of the well known difference quotient methods (
[
1
,
26
,
27
]
). Among the numerous more recent contributions obtained under different growth conditions on the differential operator and on the integrability of the right hand side, we mention
[
2
,
3
,
12
,
9
,
22
,
28
,
30
,

]
.

Our proof is based on the construction of a suitable family of approximating problems, whose solutions are smooth enough so that their second derivatives can be used as test function.
A central idea of our approach
is approximating the original problem adding higher order perturbations.

It is worth noting that, in this respect, our reasoning have features in common with
[
10
]
and with
[
19
]
.
Next, we shall establish uniform higher differentiability estimates
for the symmetric gradient of the solutions of the auxiliary problems, mainly through the use of an identity that relates the derivatives of the symmetric gradient of a
C^{2}
function with its second derivatives. It seems that, avoiding the use of the difference quotient method has the advantage of making the
ensuing calculations easier, since we deal with smooth solutions.
The conclusion will follow by proving that the a priori higher differentiability estimates are preserved in passing to the limit.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section 2 we shall recall the notion and some properties of Young functions that will be needed in our proofs. In particular, we shall state a Korn-type inequality, implying the equivalence of the norm of
\mathcal{E}u
and of
Du
and a modular Poincaré inequality in the context of Orlicz spaces. The approximation argument will be introduced in Section 3, which is devoted to the proof of the uniform a priori estimate. Finally, the proof of the main result will be the content of Section 4.

2.2.
Interlude on Young Functions

Here we introduce a
few auxiliary functions and some basic properties of Young functions, that will be used in what follows.

A function
\phi:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty)
is called a
Young function if and only if it is a left-continuous convex function that vanishes at
0
and is not constant.
It is said to
satisfy the
\Delta_{2}
-condition if and only if there is a constant

c>1
such that
\phi(2t)\leq c\,\phi(t)
for every
t>0
. The smallest
constant
c
with this property can be denoted by
\Delta_{2}(\phi)
.

The conjugate of a Young function
\phi
is defined as

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(t):=\sup_{s\geq 0}\{st-\phi(s)\},\quad t\geq 0.

One has that also
\phi^{*}
is a Young function and it holds

(2.1)

st\leq\phi(s)+\phi^{*}(t)\qquad\mbox{for $s,t\geq 0$}.

For
i_{\phi}
and
s_{\phi}
defined in (
1.6
), one can verify that

(2.2)

\displaystyle i_{\phi}\leq\frac{t\phi^{\prime}(t)}{\phi(t)}\leq s_{\phi}\quad\text{for $t>0$}

and that

(2.3)

\displaystyle s_{\phi}^{\prime}\leq\frac{t{\phi^{*}}^{\prime}(t)}{\phi^{*}(t)}\leq i_{\phi}^{\prime}\quad\text{for $t>0$,}

where
i_{\phi}^{\prime}
and
s_{\phi}^{\prime}
denote the Hölder conjugates of
i_{\phi}

and
s_{\phi}
.

From (
2.2
) and (
2.3
), one can deduce respectively that

(2.4)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}}\}\phi(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}}\}\phi(t)\quad\text{for $t,\lambda\geq 0$}

(2.5)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}\}\phi^{*}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi^{*}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}}\}\phi^{*}(t)\quad\text{for $t,\lambda\geq 0$.}

Moreover, the following useful inequality holds

(2.6)

\displaystyle\phi^{*}(\phi^{\prime}(t))\leq\phi(2t)\qquad\text{for $t>0$.}

We shall take advantage from the use of the shifted Young function. Such a notion was first given in
[
17
]
, here we appeal to the slight variant
of
[
18

We define the shifted Young functions
\phi_{a}(t)
for

and

(2.7)

\displaystyle\phi_{a}(t):=\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi^{\prime}(a\vee s)}{a\vee s}s\,ds,

where

The index

the shift. Obviously,
\phi_{0}=\phi
. Moreover, if

\phi_{a}(t)\eqsim\phi_{b}(t)

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \phi_{a}(t)&\eqsim(a\vee t)^{p-2}t^{2},\\
\phi_{a}^{\prime}(t)&\eqsim(a\vee t)^{p-2}t,\end{aligned}

with constants only depending on
i_{\phi}

Observe that for
\phi\in C^{2}(0,\infty)
one has that
\phi_{a}\in C^{2}((0,\infty)\setminus\{a\})
and the following identity holds

\frac{t\phi_{a}^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi_{a}^{\prime}(t)}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{cl}1,&\mbox{ if $0\leq t<a$,}\\[3.44444pt]
\frac{t\phi^{\prime\prime}(t)}{\phi^{\prime}(t)},&\mbox{ if $t>a$.}\end{array}\right.

Consequently, (

Moreover, for
\phi

1.7

(2.9)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{a}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{s_{\phi}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}(t)\quad\text{for $\lambda,t\geq 0$}.

Hence, by (
2.3

(2.10)

\displaystyle\min\{\lambda^{s_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}^{*}(t)

\displaystyle\leq\phi_{a}^{*}(\lambda t)\leq\max\{\lambda^{i_{\phi}^{\prime}},\lambda^{2}\}\phi_{a}^{*}(t)\quad\text{for $\lambda,t\geq 0$.}

and, recalling the convexity of

\phi_{a}(s+t)\leq\max\{2^{s_{\phi}},4\}\phi_{a}\Big(\frac{s+t}{2}\Big)\leq\max\{2^{s_{\phi}-1},2\}\big(\phi_{a}(s)+\phi_{a}(t)\big)

Equations (

2.3.
Orlicz spaces

Let
\phi
be a Young function. The Orlicz space
L^{\phi}(\Omega)
is defined as

\displaystyle L^{\phi}(\Omega)=\bigg\{u:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{n}:\exists\lambda>0\,\,\text{s.t.}\,\,\int_{\Omega}\phi\bigg(\frac{|u|}{\lambda}\bigg)\,\mathrm{d}x<\infty\bigg\}

and it becomes a Banach space with the Luxemburg norm

\|u\|_{L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\inf\bigg\{\lambda>0\colon\int_{\Omega}\phi\bigg(\frac{|u(x)|}{\lambda}\bigg)\mathrm{d}x\leq 1\bigg\}.

The Orlicz-Sobolev space
W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the space of
functions
u\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
that are weakly differentiable
with
Du\in L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})
. It is equipped with the
norm

\|u\|_{W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\|u\|_{L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})}+\|Du\|_{L^{\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n\times n})}.

We refer to the space
W^{1,\phi}_{0}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})

of functions in
W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
vanishing in
\partial\Omega
as the subspace of functions in

W^{1,\phi}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
whose extension by
0
to
\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega
is weakly differentiable in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
We conclude the Section recalling a Korn-type inequality in the Orlicz setting (see

[
21
, Thms. 6.10, 6.13]
) and a modular Poincaré inequality,
whose proof can be found for example in
[

,
20
]
or in
[

4
]
for more general domains.

\displaystyle c\left(1+\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f|)\,\mathrm{d}x\right).

Therefore, there exists
v\in{u+}W^{1,\phi}_{0}(B_{R})
such that

\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}\rightharpoonup\mathcal{E}v\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,L^{\phi}(B_{R}),

as
\varepsilon\to 0
. On the other hand, recalling that
\{u_{k,\varepsilon}\}
satisfy (
3.7
), estimate (
4.6
), and the Gagliardo Nirenberg interpolation inequality, give

(4.9)

\int_{B_{\rho}}|D(V(\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\tilde{c}\left(1+\int_{B_{R}}\!\!\!\!\Big({\phi(|Du|)}+\phi^{*}(|f|)+\phi^{*}(|Df|)\Big)\,\mathrm{d}x\right),

where
\tilde{c}
also depends on
k,r,\rho
but is independent of
\varepsilon
. Therefore, by (
4.9
), we deduce the existence of
w\in W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{R})
such that , for
\varepsilon\to 0
, it holds

V(\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon})\rightharpoonup w\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,W^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{R}).

Hence, as
\varepsilon\to 0
, we have

V(\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon})\to w\qquad\qquad\text{in}\,\,L^{2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{R})

and, up to a subsequence,

V(\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon})\to w\quad\text{a.e. in}\,\,B_{R}.

Now, the uniqueness of the weak limit and the continuity of the function
V(\xi)
ensure that

V(\mathcal{E}v)=w\qquad\text{a.e. in}\,\,B_{R}.

Therefore, by the last equivalence in (
2.22
), it follows that

(4.10)

\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}\to\mathcal{E}v\qquad\qquad\text{strongly in}\,\,L^{\phi}(B_{R}).

Our next aim is to prove that
u=v
a.e. in
B_{R}
. Using the first equivalence in (
2.22
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon})-A(x,\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x-\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}u),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})-A(x,\mathcal{E}u),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}\langle D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon},D^{k}(u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}f_{\varepsilon}\cdot(u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}f\cdot(u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\,dx-\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})-A(x,\mathcal{E}u),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,dx+\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}\langle D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon},D^{k}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle-\int_{B_{R}}\langle A(x,\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})-A(x,\mathcal{E}u),\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}\rangle\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{B_{R}}(f_{\varepsilon}-f)\cdot(u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})\,\mathrm{d}x,

where, in the second last identity, we used that
u_{k,\varepsilon}
solves problem (
3.1
) and that
u
solves equation (
1.10
).
Moving the first integral in the right and side to the left hand side of previous estimate and using in turn (
2.21
), inequalities (
2.15
) and (
2.12
) with
a=0
, the Poincaré inequality at (
2.24
) and Korn’s inequality (
2.23
), we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}|A(x,\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon})-A(x,\mathcal{E}u)||\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}||D^{k}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}}|f_{\varepsilon}-f|\cdot|u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{\prime}_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|)|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}||D^{k}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\int_{B_{R}}|f_{\varepsilon}-f|\cdot|u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\sigma\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|u_{k,\varepsilon}-\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\sigma\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|Du_{k,\varepsilon}-D\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{1}{4}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\sigma\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

Reabsorbing the second and the third integral in the right hand side by the left hand side of the previous estimate, we obtain

\displaystyle{\frac{3}{4}}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\sigma\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\sigma}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c_{\delta}\cdot\sigma\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+\delta\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where, in the last inequality, we used (
2.16
). Choosing
\sigma=\frac{1}{2c_{\delta}}
, we can reabsorb the second last integral in the right hand side by the left hand side getting

(4.11)

\displaystyle{\frac{1}{4}}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.15)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)

(4.19)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\frac{\tilde{\varepsilon}}{2}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we used the change of shift in Lemma
2.2
and (
2.20
). Now, we observe that

(4.20)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.22)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x

(4.24)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|}(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x,

where we used (
2.11
) and again the change of shift together with the last equivalence at (
2.22
). Combining (
4.11
) and (
4.20
), we get

\displaystyle\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u-\mathcal{E}u_{\varepsilon}|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle+c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi^{*}(|f_{\varepsilon}-f|)\,\mathrm{d}x+c\cdot\delta\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}\tilde{u}_{\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|)+c\cdot\delta\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x.

By our choice of
\tilde{\varepsilon}
at (
3.2
) and since (
4.4
) and (

) hold, taking the limit first as

and then as

in previous estimate, we deduce that

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\tilde{\varepsilon}\int_{B_{R}}|D^{k}u_{k,\varepsilon}|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=0

and

(4.26)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

As a consequence of Korn’s inequality (
2.23
), from the last formula, we also have

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|Du_{k,\varepsilon}-Du|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

Now observe that, by (
2.16
), for every
\delta>0
there exists a constant
c_{\delta}>0
such that the following inequality holds

\int_{B_{R}}\phi\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c_{\delta}\int_{B_{R}}\phi_{|\mathcal{E}u|}\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+\delta\int_{B_{R}}|V(\mathcal{E}u)|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, taking again the limit first as
\varepsilon\to 0
and then as
\delta\to 0
, from (
4.26
) we obtain

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}\phi\left(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}-\mathcal{E}u|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

and so, recalling(
4.10
), we deduce that
u=v
a.e. in
B_{R}
. Observing that

(4.27)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u_{k,\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|\mathcal{E}u|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

and

(4.28)

\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0}\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|Du_{k,\varepsilon}|)\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\int_{B_{R}}\phi(|Du|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

taking the limit as
\varepsilon\to 0
in estimate (
3.7
), by (
4.25
), (
4.27
) and (
4.28
), we get

\int_{B_{\rho}}|D(V(\mathcal{E}u))|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq c\left(1+\frac{k^{2}}{(r-\rho)^{2}}\right)\int_{B_{\tilde{r}}}\phi\left(|Du|\right)\,\mathrm{d}x+ck\int_{B_{r}}\phi^{*}(|Df|)\,\mathrm{d}x,

which gives the conclusion.
