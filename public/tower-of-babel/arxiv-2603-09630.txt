The eikonal spin-dependent Odderon and gluon Sivers function of a proton, and its small- x x evolution Preprint: ZTF-EP-26-05
Sanjin Benić, Adrian Dumitru, Florian Hechenberger, Tomasz Stebel

Abstract
The matrix element in the proton of the eikonal Odderon operator, with a helicity flip, has been shown to correspond to the dipole gluon Sivers function. We employ a three quark light-front model of the proton to determine the Sivers function at moderately small x 0 ∼ 0.1 x_{0}\sim 0.1 and transverse momentum k ⟂ ≲ 1 k_{\perp}\lesssim 1 GeV. The model light-cone (LC) wave function predicts the properties of x ​ f 1 ​ T ⟂ g ​ ( x , k ⟂ ) xf_{1T}^{\perp g}(x,k_{\perp}) such as its overall magnitude, the position of its peak in k ⟂ k_{\perp} , and its behavior at small k ⟂ k_{\perp} . We then compute numerically the BFKL anomalous dimension characterizing the power-law tail at k ⟂ ≳ 1.5 k_{\perp}\gtrsim 1.5 GeV of the gluon Sivers function at small LC momentum fractions, α s ​ log ⁡ x 0 / x = 1 \alpha_{s}\log x_{0}/x=1 : x ​ f 1 ​ T ⟂ g ​ ( x , k ⟂ ) ∼ k ⟂ − 3.3 xf_{1T}^{\perp g}(x,k_{\perp})\sim k_{\perp}^{-3.3} .



I
Introduction

The Sivers function describes a left-right asymmetry, relative to
the spin vector, of the transverse momentum dependent parton
distributions in a transversely polarized proton
Sivers 1990
;
Boussarie et al. 2023
.
Finite parton
transverse momentum allows for
\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
“spin-orbit”
correlations in the proton. These correlations are probed even in
eikonal, high-energy scattering processes which involve transfer of transverse
momentum to the partons.

We are interested here in the (dipole) gluon Sivers function of the proton which
we denote
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
. To date, this element of the
structure of the proton is rather poorly known
Boer et al. 2015
, its measurement represents
one of the main goals of the physics program at the future
Electron-Ion Collider (EIC)
Accardi et al. 2016
;
Abdul Khalek et al. 2022
.
In the eikonal approximation the gluon Sivers function is given by the

C
-odd component of the dipole scattering amplitude evaluated for
a transversely polarized proton, i.e. the so-called spin
dependent Odderon
Boer et al. 2016
;
Zhou 2014
;
Yao et al. 2019
;
Boussarie et al. 2020
.
In fact, the eikonal contribution to the quark Sivers function of
the proton is also obtained from the spin-dependent Odderon
Dong et al. 2019
;
Kovchegov and Santiago 2021
;
Kovchegov and Santiago 2022
.

In this paper we determine the eikonal spin dependent Odderon and the
associated gluon Sivers function from an effective three-quark
light-cone wave function (LCwf) of the proton, whose analytical form is given in appendix
A
.
In the helicity basis we can appreciate the transversely polarized proton matrix element in terms of the helicity flips between the incoming and the outgoing proton states. Even though the eikonal color current operator
J^{+a}J^{+b}J^{+c}
, sourcing the small-
x
gluon fields in the Odderon, conserves the individual quark helicities, the proton helicity flip is ensured by the quark orbital angular momentum
Pasquini et al. 2008
, leading to an overlap of the proton wave functions differing by one unit of orbital angular momentum.
Our empirical knowledge
of the proton suggests that the three quark
LCwf should
provide an adequate model for the partonic structure of the proton for
moderately small
x\sim 0.1
, and transverse momenta up to

k_{\perp}\sim 1
GeV. To assess the accuracy of this approach,
we confront the Dirac and Pauli electromagnetic form factors
obtained in this model with up to date data in appendix
B
.

We then employ the eikonal Odderon derived from the non-perturbative
LC quark model
wave function as initial condition for QCD evolution to smaller
x
.
That is, we
solve the small-
x
dipole evolution equation
Mueller 1994

from this initial condition to determine the gluon Sivers function
and the anomalous dimension associated with its
transverse momentum dependence.

So far, the only model for the eikonal spin-dependent Odderon available
in the literature was constructed by Zhou
Zhou 2014
within the
framework of an extended McLerran-Venugopalan model with cubic Casimir
Jeon and Venugopalan 2005
.
In that model the random static sources for the soft gluon fields are assumed
to reside
in a high-dimensional representation of color-SU(3), and therefore also in
a high-dimensional representation of spin-SU(2).
Here, we construct a model where these sources are in the fundamental
representations of both color-SU(3) and spin-SU(2).

The spin dependent Odderon features in a variety of processes
in DIS at high energies such as
the spin dependent part of the cross section for inclusive open charm production
on transversely polarized protons
Yao et al. 2019
;
Zheng et al. 2018
.
Furthermore, in exclusive forward
production of
C
-even mesons
Boussarie et al. 2020
;
Benic et al. 2025
(see also
Ma 2003
).
Other observables for the spin dependent Odderon include a

T
-odd nucleon energy correlator (NEC) which generates a single transverse spin
asymmetry for the energy pattern in the target fragmentation region
Mäntysaari et al. 2025
;
as well as transverse energy-energy correlations
(TEEC) at small
x
in DIS
Bhattacharya et al. 2025
.
Most recently, Hatta and Teryaev discussed a left-right asymmetry w.r.t. the spin of the
recoil
baryon in DIS
Hatta and Teryaev 2026
.

II
The spin dependent Odderon(s)

The
{\cal S}
-matrix for eikonal scattering of a
q-\bar{q}
dipole from a target proton
can be written, in dipole factorization, in the form
Iancu and Venugopalan 2003
;
Kovchegov and Levin 2013

1-{\cal P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}({\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}},{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}})-i{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}({\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}},{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}})=\frac{1}{N_{c}}\frac{\langle P^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}|\,{\rm tr}\,V({\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}})V^{\dagger}({\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}})|P\Lambda\rangle}{\langle P\Lambda|P\Lambda\rangle}\,.

(1)

Here,
{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}={\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}+{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}/2

and

{\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}}={\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}-{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}/2

are the transverse positions of the quark and the anti-quark, respectively, and
V({\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}})

are eikonal Wilson lines at transverse position
{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}
:

V({\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}})=\hat{P}\,\exp\left(-ig\int\mathrm{d}x^{-}\,A^{+a}(x^{-},\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp})\,t^{a}\right)~.

(2)

This fundamental Wilson line describes the eikonal propagation of a
quark and corresponds to the covariant derivative
(D^{\mu})_{ij}=\partial^{\mu}\delta_{ij}+igA^{\mu a}(t^{a})_{ij}
.

In eq. (
1
),
\Lambda,\Lambda^{\prime}=\pm 1
denote (twice) the helicities
of the incoming and scattered proton, respectively.

C
-even Pomeron exchange relates to

{\cal P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}({\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}},{\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}})
,
and the amplitude for
\Lambda^{\prime}\neq\Lambda
has been derived in ref.
Benić and Dumitru 2025
; this amplitude vanishes when the transverse momentum
transfer
\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp}\to 0
.
C
-odd Odderon exchange is related to
i{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}({\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}},{\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp}})
.
A key point is that despite taking the eikonal limit, the helicity flip Odderon exchange
amplitude is non-zero even in the forward limit,
where it is integrated over the impact parameter

{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}

Zhou 2014
;
Boussarie et al. 2020
;
Benic et al. 2025
.
We shall confirm this by an explicit computation of the above matrix element
at third order in
gA^{+}
for a
light-cone quark model of the proton
|P\Lambda\rangle
.
Non-zero contributions to
\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}\,i{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\neq\Lambda}({\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}},{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}})
arise
when the three eikonal,
t
-channel gluons attach to three
different

quarks in the proton.

Expanding the Wilson lines to cubic order in the field
gA^{+a}
we
can write the Odderon amplitude in the form
Dumitru et al. 2018

\begin{split}{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})&=\frac{-\mathrm{i}g^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{16N_{c}^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp}}\frac{G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})}{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp}^{2}}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}\\
&\times\left[\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\right)+\frac{1}{3}\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\right)\right]\,.\end{split}

(3)

G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})
represents the
C
-odd part of the
eikonal coupling of three static
t
-channel gluons to the proton;
we shall provide explicit expressions below. We have used abbreviation for transverse momentum integrals
\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}/(2\pi)^{2}
. Similarly, for position space, we use
\int_{\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}}\equiv\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}
.

Performing a Fourier transform
from impact parameter to momentum transfer we obtain
the Odderon in mixed representation,

\begin{split}{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})&=\int_{\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}}{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp})\\
&=\frac{-\mathrm{i}g^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{48N_{c}^{2}}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}}\frac{G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})}{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})^{2}}\\
&\hskip-56.9055pt\times\left[\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot(2\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\right)+\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot(2\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}-\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\right)+\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-2\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-2\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})\right)+\sin\left(\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\right)\right]\,.\end{split}

(4)

Forward scattering of a very small dipole scales like
\sim r^{3}_{\perp}
as the
coupling of each one of the exchanged gluons to the almost point-like
dipole operator brings about one factor of
r_{\perp}
.

A further Fourier transform from dipole vector
\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}
to transverse momentum
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}

gives

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})&=-\frac{g^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{32N_{c}^{2}}(2\pi)^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}}\frac{G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})}{\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}^{2}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})^{2}}\Bigg[\delta^{(2)}\left(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\right)\\
&-\delta^{(2)}\left(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\right)+\frac{1}{3}\delta^{(2)}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\right)-\frac{1}{3}\delta^{(2)}\left(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\frac{1}{2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\right)\Bigg]\,.\end{split}

(5)

Note that in this expression the anti-symmetry of the Odderon under
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}\to-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
is manifest. Hence, the
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
integral
of this function is zero; it
would represent the imaginary part of a twist-2 PDF which, however, must be
real.

As is customary in the literature, when referring to the forward matrix element at
zero momentum transfer we will also use the notation
{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})\equiv{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}=0)
, or

{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})
for its Fourier transform to transverse momentum space. We have

\begin{split}\mathcal{O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})&=-\frac{g^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{32N_{c}^{2}}(2\pi)^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}}\sum_{s=\pm 1}\frac{sG_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(s\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp},-s\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}^{2}(s\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})^{2}}\\
&=-\frac{g^{6}(N_{c}^{2}-4)(N_{c}^{2}-1)}{16N_{c}^{2}}(2\pi)^{2}\int_{\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}}\frac{G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp},-\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})}{\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}^{2}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})^{2}}\,.\end{split}

(6)

In the second step we used the anti-symmetry of
G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}
under
a sign flip of all its arguments, which applies when
\Lambda^{\prime}=-\Lambda
,
see the following sec.
II.1
.

The most general angular decomposition of the Odderon in the helicity basis is
Boussarie et al. 2020

{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})=\delta_{\Lambda\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathrm{i}\cos(\phi_{r\Delta}){\cal O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})+\delta_{\Lambda,-\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathrm{i}\Lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Lambda\phi_{r}}{\cal O}_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})+\delta_{\Lambda,-\Lambda^{\prime}}\mathrm{i}\Lambda\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\Lambda\phi_{\Delta}}\cos(\phi_{r\Delta}){\cal O}^{\perp}_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\,.

(7)

There are three independent real functions in general. The first one is the usual
spin-independent (also known as “b-dependent”) Odderon while the second and third ones
are spin-dependent Odderons. The second one becomes the Sivers function in the forward limit, and that is our focus here.

We may also consider the matrix element of the Odderon operator in a (transverse)
spin basis which is usually employed to relate it to the gluon Sivers function.
To obtain a state of definite spin vector one constructs a superposition of
states of definite LC helicity
Diehl and Hagler 2005

|\vec{S}\rangle=\cos\frac{\theta_{S}}{2}\,|\Lambda=+\rangle\,+\,\sin\frac{\theta_{S}}{2}\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{S}}\,|\Lambda=-\rangle~.

(8)

Here,
\vec{S}=(\sin\theta_{S}\cos\phi_{S},\sin\theta_{S}\sin\phi_{S},\cos\theta_{S})
.
In case of a transverse spin vector
\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}
we have
\theta_{S}=\frac{\pi}{2}
and

|\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|\Lambda=+\rangle\,+\,\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}\phi_{S}}\,|\Lambda=-\rangle\right)~.

(9)

Then,

\begin{split}{\cal O}_{\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})&=\sum_{\Lambda^{\prime},\Lambda}\langle\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}|\Lambda^{\prime}\rangle\,{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}\,\langle\Lambda|\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\rangle\\
&=\cos(\phi_{r\Delta})\mathrm{i}\mathcal{O}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})-\frac{\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{r_{\perp}}\mathcal{O}_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})-\frac{\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}}{\Delta_{\perp}}\cos(\phi_{r\Delta})\mathcal{O}_{S}^{\perp}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\,,\end{split}

(10)

with
\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}=r_{\perp}\sin(\phi_{r}-\phi_{S})
. In the
\Delta_{\perp}\to 0

forward limit,

{\cal O}_{\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})\to-\frac{\boldsymbol{S}_{\perp}\times\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{r_{\perp}}\mathcal{O}_{S}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}=0)~.

(11)

The gluon Sivers function of the eikonal dipole model can be obtained from its
forward, helicity flip Odderon
Boer et al. 2016
;
Zhou 2014
;
Yao et al. 2019

xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})=\frac{N_{c}k_{\perp}M_{p}}{2\pi^{3}\,g^{2}}\mathrm{e}^{-\mathrm{i}\phi_{k}}\,\mathcal{O}_{-+}(\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp})\,.

(12)

On the l.h.s. of this expression, the Sivers function does not depend on
the direction of
\boldsymbol{k}_{\perp}
.

M_{p}=0.938
GeV denotes the mass of the proton.

In Zhou’s model

the integral of

xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
over
\mathrm{d}k_{\perp}^{2}

\int\limits_{0}^{\infty}\mathrm{d}k_{\perp}\,k_{\perp}\,xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})=0~.

(13)

Given the expressions for
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
obtained here,
eq. (
13

However, a numerical
evaluation confirms it at a level better than 0.5%.

The above relation implies that the Sivers function is not sign-definite,

\mu^{2}

(also called the

Kang and Qiu 2008

(14)

II.1
Eikonal triple gluon coupling to the proton

The
C
-odd coupling
of three small-
x
,
t
-channel gluons (in covariant gauge) to the proton is given
by the expectation value of three light-front color charge
density operators
\rho^{a}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})\equiv J^{+a}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})
, i.e. of the fundamental cubic Casimir of SU(3). These color charges source the soft gluon fields,
A^{+a}
, appearing in the
Wilson lines in eq. (
1
).

\begin{split}d^{abc}\langle P^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}|\rho^{a}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp})\rho^{b}(\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp})\rho^{c}(\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})|P\Lambda\rangle&\equiv\frac{(N_{c}^{2}-1)(N_{c}^{2}-4)}{4N_{c}}\,G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\\
&=N_{c}C_{3F}\,G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\,.\end{split}

(15)

(We have stripped the r.h.s. of this expression of the delta-functions
Dumitru and Stebel 2019
for
conservation of LC and transverse momentum.)

To proceed, we adopt an effective light-cone three-quark model of the proton
intended to describe its partonic structure for LC momentum fractions
of roughly
10^{-1}
(and higher), and for transverse momenta up to approximately 1 GeV.
We write the proton state in the light-front (LF) quark model in the form

\begin{split}|P,\Lambda\rangle&=\int[\mathrm{d}x_{i}]\int\left[\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}\right]\sum_{j_{1},j_{2},j_{3}}\frac{\epsilon^{j_{1}j_{2}j_{3}}}{\sqrt{N_{c}!}}\,\sum_{\{\lambda_{i}\}}\,\Psi_{\Lambda}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp},\lambda_{i})\,\,|\{x_{i}P^{+},\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}+x_{i}\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp},\lambda_{i},j_{i}\}\rangle\,.\end{split}

(16)

Here,
x_{i}
denotes the LC momentum fraction of the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
quark,
where
i=1\dots N_{c}=3
;

\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}
is its transverse momentum relative to the center of mass transverse
momentum
\boldsymbol{P}_{\perp}
of the proton;
j_{i}
refers to its color;
\lambda_{i}
to its
helicity; a flavor quantum number is not required for the present
purposes. The explicit form of the
momentum space integrals and of the three-quark LC wave function
are provided in appendix
A
. In appendix
B

we confront the model with up to date data for the Dirac and Pauli
electromagnetic form factors of the proton.

Given a light-front Fock space expansion of the proton,
this matrix element can be computed explicitly, in principle.
For a proton effectively
made of three valence quarks, as also assumed here,
such computations have previously been carried out in ref.
Dumitru et al. 2018
;
Dumitru and Stebel 2019
;
Dumitru et al. 2020

considering a helicity flip of the proton.
An analogous matrix element for eikonal

-gluon exchange
with

helicity flip has been presented in ref.
Benić and Dumitru 2025
.
We refer to those papers for technical details. Here we just quote our
result,

\begin{split}G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp},\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})&=\sum_{\lambda_{i}}\sum_{j=1}^{3}\int_{x_{i}}4\pi\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}}(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{3\perp})\\
&\times\Big[\Psi^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i(j)\perp},\lambda_{i})-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{l=1}^{3}\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}\Psi^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i(jj^{\prime})\perp}^{(l)},\lambda_{i})\\
&+\sum_{j^{\prime}\neq j}\sum_{j^{\prime\prime}\neq j^{\prime}\neq j}\Psi^{*}_{\Lambda^{\prime}}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i(jj^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime})\perp},\lambda_{i})\Big]\Psi_{\Lambda}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp},\lambda_{i})\,,\end{split}

(17)

where

\begin{split}\boldsymbol{k}_{i(j)\perp}&=\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}+(x_{i}-\delta_{ij})(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})\,,\\
\boldsymbol{k}_{i(jj^{\prime})\perp}^{(l)}&=\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}+(x_{i}-\delta_{ij})(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})-(\delta_{ij^{\prime}}-\delta_{ij})\boldsymbol{q}_{l\perp}\,,\\
\boldsymbol{k}_{i(jj^{\prime}j^{\prime\prime})\perp}&=\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}+x_{i}(\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp})-\delta_{ij}\boldsymbol{q}_{1\perp}-\delta_{ij^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{q}_{2\perp}-\delta_{ij^{\prime\prime}}\boldsymbol{q}_{3\perp}\,.\end{split}

(18)

We note that
G_{3\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}

is symmetric under the exchange of any two of its transverse momentum arguments.
Also, when
\Lambda=\Lambda^{\prime}
(
\Lambda\neq\Lambda^{\prime}
), it is even (odd) under
a simultaneous sign flip of all three
\boldsymbol{q}_{i\perp}
.

In this LCwf model the helicity transfer between the two proton states is ensured by the quark orbital angular momentum that is originating from the Melosh rotation, cf.
Pasquini and Yuan 2010
.
The proton wave functions

contain quark helicity contributions that go beyond the static quark-model spin structure. For example, a helicity

30
)). As the eikonal operator in (

-1/2

-wave. In the full exoression, the overlaps contain not only

-wave and

-wave and

For forward scattering the first and second terms in eq. (
17

the three

-channel gluons attach to one or two quarks in the proton, respectively, vanish when
\Lambda^{\prime}\neq\Lambda

at least three target partons.
Mathematically this comes about in eq. (
17
)
due to the odd parity of the helicity wave function overlap

under a simultaneous sign flip of all quark transverse momenta
\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}
;
see appendix
A
for explicit expressions for the

For this same reason, the two-gluon exchange matrix element with helicity
flip requires non-zero momentum transfer
\Delta_{\perp}

II.2
BFKL evolution of the helicity flip Odderon

With increasing energy or decreasing
x
the phase space for gluon emissions
opens up. It becomes necessary then to resum pairwise interactions, i.e. gluon
exchanges among the three gluons exchanged by the dipole and the proton.
Such resummation is achieved by the small-
x
QCD evolution
equation. The non-linear evolution equation for the Odderon(s) has been derived in refs.
Kovchegov et al. 2004
;
Hatta et al. 2005
. Here, however, we only require
the linear BFKL evolution

;
Kuraev et al. 1977
;
Balitsky and Lipatov 1978

both the
two- and three-gluon exchange amplitudes (for a perturbative dipole,
r_{\perp}\ll\Lambda_{\mathrm{QCD}}^{-1}

{\cal P}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})
,
{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})\ll 1
.
In the leading

Mueller’s dipole approach

the evolution equation formulated in position space reads

\frac{\partial}{\partial Y}{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})=\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{s}}{2\pi}\int_{\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}}\,\frac{(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})^{2}}{(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})^{2}\,(\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})^{2}}\,\left[{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp})+{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})-{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\right]~.

(19)

\bar{\alpha}_{s}=\alpha_{s}N_{c}/\pi

Y=\log x_{0}/x

x_{0}\sim 0.1

For dipole scattering with Odderon exchange, the above is equivalent
to the Bartels-Kwiecinski-Praszalowicz (BKP) linear evolution equation

Kwiecinski and Praszalowicz 1980

ref.
Kovchegov et al. 2004

Assuming translational invariance,
{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp},\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})\to{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}-\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})
,
and integrating over the impact

\boldsymbol{b}_{\perp}=(\boldsymbol{x}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{y}_{\perp})/2

\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}\to 0

\frac{\partial}{\partial Y}{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})=\frac{\bar{\alpha}_{s}}{2\pi}\int_{\boldsymbol{s}_{\perp}}\,\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}^{2}}{\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}-\boldsymbol{s}_{\perp}\right)^{2}\,\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}+\boldsymbol{s}_{\perp}\right)^{2}}\,\left[{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}-\boldsymbol{s}_{\perp}\right)+{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}\left(\frac{\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp}}{2}+\boldsymbol{s}_{\perp}\right)-{\cal O}_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{r}_{\perp})\right]~.

(20)

Deep in the perturbative regime of very small dipoles, however, the
initial condition
will be of cubic order in

The BFKL resummation is expected to generate an anomalous dimension

\gamma(Y)<1

IV
Summary and discussion

To date the most commonly used parameterization for the gluon Sivers function
of the proton is the model by D’Alesio
et al.

D’Alesio et al. 2015
.
However, this model does not originate from the correspondence at small
x
to the
helicity flip Odderon, and is not well suited as an initial condition for
BFKL evolution.

Our goal here was to develop an alternative based on the eikonal
approach, and on an effective quark model LCwf
for the proton. Such a model incorporates empirical knowledge of the proton
at moderate energy and transverse resolution scales, and can be tuned to
provide a satisfactory description of Dirac and Pauli form factors.
In particular, the model specifically involves sources (for the soft

t
-channel gluons exchanged in high-energy scattering) in the fundamental
representations of both color-SU(3) and spin-SU(2).

The above light-cone quark model for the proton enables the explicit computation of
the matrix element of the eikonal dipole operator between proton states of
opposite helicity. While the
C
-even part of this matrix element requires
non-zero momentum transfer
Benić and Dumitru 2025
,
the
C
-odd part is finite
even in the limit of forward scattering, where it defines the “spin dependent
Odderon” and the gluon Sivers function
Boer et al. 2016
;
Zhou 2014
;
Yao et al. 2019
;
Boussarie et al. 2020
,
a transverse momentum dependent parton distribution.

Our main results for the small-
x
dipole gluon Sivers function are as follows.
We confirm the statements in refs.
Zhou 2014
;
Yao et al. 2019
that

xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
, as a function of transverse momentum
k_{\perp}
,
is not sign definite. This leads to substantial cancellations in the first

k_{\perp}^{2}
moment of the Sivers function,
xf_{1T}^{\perp(1)}(x,\mu^{2})
,
which is related to the twist three collinear function frequently denoted the
“tri-gluon PDF”
O(x,\mu^{2})
; this is true
especially for relatively low scales
\mu
of order
1-3
GeV.
A related phenomenological consequence is that forward exclusive photoproduction
of
\chi_{c1}
axial vector mesons is dominated by photon rather than
Odderon exchange
Benic et al. 2025
.

We provide the following parameterization of the eikonal gluon Sivers function
at
x=x_{0}\sim 0.1
:

k_{\perp}\,xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})=\frac{24M_{p}}{\pi}\,\alpha_{s}^{2}\,\mathcal{N}\frac{k_{\perp}}{Q_{b}}\log\left(\frac{k_{\perp}}{Q_{b}}\right)e^{-k_{\perp}^{2}/(4Q_{c}^{2})}\,.

(26)

The fit parameters are
\mathcal{N}=0.036\text{ GeV}^{-2},\ Q_{b}=0.331\text{ GeV},\ Q_{c}=0.220\text{ GeV}
.
4
4

4

Allowing the exponent of
(k_{\perp}/Q_{b})\to(k_{\perp}/Q_{b})^{a}
to vary as well, we obtain a statistically more significant fit with
\mathcal{N}=0.029\text{ GeV}^{-2},\ a=0.901,\ Q_{b}=0.333\text{ GeV},\ Q_{c}=0.232\text{ GeV}
, albeit at the cost of an additional parameter.
This fit does not apply beyond
k_{\perp}\sim 1
GeV.

The gluon Sivers function in this model exhibits a peak (at
k_{\perp}>Q_{b}
)
at a transverse momentum scale typical of the quarks in the proton.
This is unlike the parameterization of
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
by D’Alesio

et al.

D’Alesio et al. 2015
which is a Gaussian, and has its maximum
at
k_{\perp}=0
. The peak obtained in our model resembles the one found in
saturation models of the Odderon
Yao et al. 2019
albeit its physical origin
in our purely perturbative computation of three gluon exchange is rather
different. The forward matrix element, in the proton, of the
eikonal
dipole operator
for reversed helicity, involves orbital angular momentum (OAM)
transfer to the sources of the soft gluons.
The peak of
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})
corresponds to the scale where the
transfer of OAM is most efficient.
Numerically, for the model LCwf used here the peak
occurs at about
k_{\perp}\simeq 0.5
GeV where the magnitude of
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})

is
(4-5)\times 10^{-3}
GeV
-2
, assuming

\alpha_{s}=0.25
.

For small
k_{\perp}
we obtain a logarithmic singularity
of the Sivers function rather than power-law decay
Yao et al. 2019

or approach to a finite constant
D’Alesio et al. 2015
. Hence, there could be
potentially significant contributions from small, non-perturbative transverse
momentum imbalances to charm single spin asymmetries (which are also sensitive to
parton shower effects and to transverse momentum acquired in parton
fragmentation
Zheng et al. 2014
;
Cassar et al. 2025
;
Caucal et al. 2025
).
We intend to investigate open charm production more quantitatively in the future.

The BFKL evolution of the spin dependent Odderon in this model generates
an anomalous dimension of its power-law tail. At high
k_{\perp}\gtrsim 1.5
GeV
we find that
xf_{1T}^{\perp}(x,k_{\perp})\sim k_{\perp}^{-4\gamma(Y)}
, with

\gamma(Y)\simeq 0.8
at
Y\equiv\log x_{0}/x=1/\alpha_{s}
.
Also, given the above initial condition, we observe that
the magnitude of the perturbative small-
r
tail of the
BFKL spin-dependent Odderon
\mathcal{O}_{S}(Y,r_{\perp})
increases with
Y
as
Y\to 1/\alpha_{s}
.
The non-forward, spin-
in
dependent dipole Odderon amplitude off the proton
also increases with decreasing
x
if a gluon is added into the three quark
wave function
Dumitru et al. 2023a
.
Asymptotically, the BFKL resummed Odderon amplitudes are expected to be
(at most) independent of energy
Kovchegov et al. 2004

{\Phi}_{\uparrow}(\uparrow,\downarrow,\downarrow)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a_{1}a_{2}k_{3R}-2a_{1}k_{2R}a_{3}-k_{1L}k_{2R}k_{3R}\right)\,,\\
{\Phi}_{\uparrow}(\downarrow,\uparrow,\downarrow)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a_{1}a_{2}k_{3R}-2k_{1R}a_{2}a_{3}-k_{1R}k_{2L}k_{3R}\right)\,,\\
{\Phi}_{\uparrow}(\downarrow,\downarrow,\uparrow)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(a_{1}k_{2R}a_{3}+k_{1R}a_{2}a_{3}+2k_{1R}k_{2R}k_{3L}\right)\,,\\
{\Phi}_{\uparrow}(\uparrow,\uparrow,\uparrow)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(-a_{1}k_{2L}a_{3}-k_{1L}a_{2}a_{3}+2a_{1}a_{2}k_{3L}\right)\,,\\
{\Phi}_{\uparrow}(\downarrow,\downarrow,\downarrow)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N_{1}N_{2}N_{3}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{6}}\left(-a_{1}k_{2R}k_{3R}-k_{1R}a_{2}k_{3R}+2k_{1R}k_{2R}a_{3}\right)\,,\\
\end{split}

(30)

with
a_{i}=m+x_{i}\mathcal{M}
and

and
(\uparrow,\downarrow)\equiv(+,-)
in our notation.
These helicity wave functions pertain to the
|uud\rangle
state; there is
no need here to consider quark flavor permutations since all the matrix

Dirac and Pauli electromagnetic form factors

To exhibit the connection of the form factors to Generalized Parton Distributions (GPDs)
we start from their usual definitions

P^{+}\int\frac{\mathrm{d}z^{-}}{2\pi}\mathrm{e}^{ixP^{+}z^{-}}\langle P^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}|\bar{\psi}(-z/2)\gamma^{+}\psi(z/2)|P\Lambda\rangle=\bar{u}(P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\left[\gamma^{+}H_{q}(x,\xi,\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})+\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma^{+\nu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M_{p}}E_{q}(x,\xi,\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\right]u(P,\Lambda)\,,

(31)

where
\Delta=P^{\prime}-P
. We integrate this over
x
and take the eikonal limit of
vanishing skewness,
\xi\to 0
or
P^{\prime+}\to P^{+}
:

\langle P^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}|\bar{\psi}(0)\gamma^{+}\psi(0)|P\Lambda\rangle=\bar{u}(P^{\prime},\Lambda^{\prime})\left[\gamma^{+}F_{1}^{q}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})+\frac{\mathrm{i}\sigma^{+\nu}\Delta_{\nu}}{2M_{p}}F_{2}^{q}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\right]u(P,\Lambda)\,.

(32)

where

F_{1}^{q}=\int\mathrm{d}xH_{q}\,,\qquad F_{2}^{q}=\int\mathrm{d}xE_{q}\,,

(33)

are form-factors per flavor.

Eq. (
32

q^{+}\to 0
defines
the eikonal LC charge operator

\sum_{f}e_{f}\int\mathrm{d}z^{-}\int\mathrm{d}^{2}\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}\mathrm{e}^{\mathrm{i}q^{+}z^{-}-\mathrm{i}\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}\cdot\boldsymbol{z}_{\perp}}\bar{\psi}_{f}(z)\gamma^{+}\psi_{f}(z)=\int\mathrm{d}z^{-}\mathrm{e}^{iq^{+}z^{-}}J^{+}(z^{-},\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})\approx\int\mathrm{d}z^{-}J^{+}(z^{-},\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})\equiv\rho(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})\,.

(34)

This charge operator can be written
in terms of quark creation and annihilation operators
Dumitru et al. 2018

in order to evaluate explicitly the matrix element on the l.h.s. of (
32
).
This leads to

\langle P^{\prime}\Lambda^{\prime}|\rho(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp})|P\Lambda\rangle=2P^{+}(2\pi)\delta(P^{\prime+}-P^{+})(2\pi)^{2}\delta^{(2)}(\boldsymbol{q}_{\perp}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})F_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})\,,

(35)

\begin{split}F_{\Lambda^{\prime}\Lambda}(\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp})&=\sum_{\lambda_{i}}\int_{x_{i}}4\pi\delta(1-x_{1}-x_{2}-x_{3})\int_{\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}}(2\pi)^{2}\delta(\boldsymbol{k}_{1\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{2\perp}+\boldsymbol{k}_{3\perp})\\
&\times\Big[e_{d}\Psi_{\Lambda^{\prime}}^{*}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp}-x_{i}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp}+\delta_{i3}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{\perp},\lambda_{i})\Psi_{\Lambda}(x_{i},\boldsymbol{k}_{i\perp},\lambda_{i})\\

(36)

corresponds to a kind of helicity form factor.

(38)
