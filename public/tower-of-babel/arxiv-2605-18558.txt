1.
Introduction

The study of volumes of three-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds and hyperbolic polyhedra is an important direction in the development of modern geometry. It is of interest to describe sets of volumes and their properties for various classes of polyhedra, see, for example,
[
8
,
15
]
. On the one hand, one can fix the combinatorial structure of a polyhedron and ask for which dihedral angles the polyhedron (with finite or ideal vertices) has maximal volume. For example, by the well-known result of Milnor
[
25
]
, the greatest volume of a hyperbolic 3-simplex is attained on the regular ideal 3-simplex, that is, on such a simplex whose all vertices are ideal and all dihedral angles are equal to each other, and hence are equal to
\pi/3
. An analogous result also holds in the
n
-dimensional case. As shown in
[
19
]
, the greatest volume of a hyperbolic
n
-simplex is attained on the regular ideal
n
-simplex. On the other hand, one can fix the dihedral angles and ask which polyhedron has maximal volume for a given number of faces or vertices. A hyperbolic polyhedron is called right-angled if all its dihedral angles are equal to
\pi/2
. For the construction of three-dimensional hyperbolic manifolds corresponding to torsion-free subgroups in reflection groups of right-angled polyhedra, see
[
34
]
. The aim of this paper is to study volumes of right-angled polyhedra normalized by the number of vertices of the polyhedra.

Following
[
33
]
, for a hyperbolic polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
of finite volume
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})
with a finite number of vertices
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
we define the
normalized volume
as the quantity
\omega(\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})/\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
. Let
\mathcal{R}
be some set of hyperbolic polyhedra. The set
\Omega(\mathcal{R})=\{\omega(\mathcal{P})\mid\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}\}
of normalized volumes of polyhedra from
\mathcal{R}
will be called the
spectrum of normalized volumes
of
\mathcal{R}
.

Two-sided estimates of volumes of right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra in terms of the number of their faces were obtained by Atkinson in
[
7
]
. A list of the
825
compact right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra of smallest volume was given by Inoue in
[
21
]
. A list of the first
248
smallest values of volumes of ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra is given in
[
35
]
.

Denote by
\mathcal{R}_{ideal}
the set of all ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra, and by
\mathcal{R}_{comp}
the set of all compact right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra. In both cases we assume that the polyhedra have finite volume and a finite number of vertices.

The normalized volumes of these sets and their spectra are described in the following theorems, where the constants used have the following values to six decimal places:

v_{\mathrm{oct}}=3,663862
and
v_{\mathrm{tet}}=1,014941
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The spectrum of normalized volumes of ideal right-angled polyhedra
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})=\{\omega(\mathcal{P})\mid\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{ideal}\}
belongs to the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
, where the lower bound is attained on the octahedron and the upper bound is sharp. The spectrum is discrete on the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
and everywhere dense on the interval
\left[\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
.

Theorem 1.2
.

The spectrum of normalized volumes of compact right-angled polyhedra
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{comp})=\{\omega(\mathcal{P})\mid\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{comp}\}
belongs to the interval
\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right)
, where the upper bound is sharp. On the interval
\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{32}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
the spectrum is discrete, and on the interval
\left[\frac{5}{16}v_{\mathrm{tet}},\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
it is everywhere dense.

The behavior of the spectrum
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{comp})
on the interval
\left[\frac{1}{32}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{5}{16}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
remains unclear for now; see Question
6.2
.

Let us note the connection of the obtained results with the theory of hyperbolic knots and links. For a hyperbolic link
L\subset S^{3}
, consider the volume of its complement endowed with the complete metric of constant negative curvature and denote it by
\mathrm{vol}(L)
. Let
c(L)
be the minimal number of crossings among all diagrams of the link
L
. In
[
14
]
, Champanerkar, Kofman, and Purcell defined the
volume density
of a link as follows:
\operatorname{vd}(L)=\mathrm{vol}(L)/c(L)
. It is known that
\operatorname{vd}(L)\leq v_{\mathrm{oct}}
. A sequence of links
L_{n}
such that
c(L_{n})\to\infty
is called
geometrically maximal
if
\lim_{n\to\infty}\operatorname{vd}(L_{n})=v_{\mathrm{oct}}
. Examples of geometrically maximal sequences of links were constructed in
[
14
]
.

Subsequently, Burton
[
12
]
showed that the set of volume densities of all hyperbolic links is everywhere dense in the interval
\left[0,v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
. Adams, Calderon, and Mayer showed in
[
1
]
that the set of volume densities of hyperbolic links in thickened surfaces
S_{g}\times I
, where
g\geq 2
, is everywhere dense in the interval
\left[0,2v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
.

In
[
23
]
, Kwon and Tham studied a special class of hyperbolic links, which are called fully augmented links (or FAL for short). For them, the natural measure of complexity is the number of augmentations (they are also called vertical components of the link). For this class, Kwon and Tham defined the volume density as the ratio of the volume of the link complement to the number of its vertical components and showed that the set of densities lies in the interval
\left[v_{\mathrm{oct}},10v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
, with both bounds sharp. In addition, they established that the spectrum is discrete in
\left[v_{\mathrm{oct}},2v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
and everywhere dense in
\left[2v_{\mathrm{oct}},10v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
.

There is a natural geometric connection between fully augmented links and ideal right-angled polyhedra: the complement of each such link decomposes into two mutually isometric ideal right-angled polyhedra
[
24
,
28
,
36
]
(for an analogous decomposition of complements of links in the thickened torus, see
[
22
]
). At the same time, the volume of the link is equal to twice the volume of the polyhedron, and the number of vertical components of the link is related to the number of vertices of the polyhedron. Consequently, using a suitable normalization, the spectrum of volume densities of links can be embedded in the spectrum of normalized volumes of polyhedra. However, the class of ideal right-angled polyhedra is substantially broader than the subclass of polyhedra arising from links, and therefore the question of describing the volume spectrum of the class of all ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra naturally arises.

The paper has the following structure.
Section
2
gives the main definitions and properties of ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra. Section
3
is devoted to compact right-angled hyperbolic polyhedra, in particular, to Löbell polyhedra. Sections
4
and
5
contain the proofs of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, respectively. Section
6
compares the obtained results for normalized volumes of ideal and compact right-angled polyhedra with the results of Kwon and Tham on the spectrum of volume density of augmented links. In conclusion we formulate some open questions and one conjecture.

2.
Ideal right-angled polyhedra

By an
abstract polyhedron
we mean such a cell complex on
S^{2}
that can be realized as a convex polyhedron in three-dimensional Euclidean space. According to the classical theorem of Steinitz, see, for example,
[
27
, Ch. 1]
, realizability of a cell complex as a convex Euclidean polyhedron is equivalent to its one-dimensional skeleton being a 3-connected graph without loops and multiple edges. Recall that a graph is called
k
-connected if it contains at least
k+1
vertices and any two of its distinct vertices can be joined by at least
k
independent paths. By the Menger-Whitney theorem, a graph containing at least
k+1
vertices is
k
-connected if and only if, after deleting any of its
k-1
vertices (and the edges incident to them), the resulting graph is connected. Denote by
\operatorname{E}(P)
the set of edges of an abstract polyhedron
P
. A
labeling
of an abstract graph
P
is a map
\mathcal{E}:\operatorname{E}(P)\to(0,\pi/2]
. The pair
(P,\mathcal{E})
will be called a
labeled abstract polyhedron
. We will say that a labeled abstract polyhedron is
realized as a hyperbolic polyhedron
if there is a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
in hyperbolic space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
such that there exists a label-preserving graph isomorphism sending the one-dimensional skeleton
\mathcal{P}^{(1)}
, with its edges labeled by dihedral angles, to the labeled abstract polyhedron
(P,\mathcal{E})
. Below we will consider such polyhedra in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
for which all dihedral angles are equal to
\pi/2
, and we will call them
right-angled
. A vertex of a polyhedron in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
is called
ideal
if it lies on the absolute
\partial\mathbb{H}^{3}
. For more details on polyhedra in the space
\mathbb{H}^{3}
, see
[
3
]
.

Let
G
be a plane graph and let
G^{*}
be the graph dual to
G
. A simple closed curve consisting of
k\geq 3
edges in
G^{*}
will be called a
k
-circuit
. A
prismatic
k
-circuit
will mean such a
k
-circuit
\gamma
that no two edges of the graph
G
crossing
\gamma
have a common vertex.

Necessary and sufficient conditions for the realization in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
of a labeled abstract polyhedron of finite volume were obtained in the works of Andreev
[
5
,
6
]
; see also
[
29
]
. We will give the result for the special case of right-angled polyhedra following
[
7
]
. In this case the necessary and sufficient conditions are purely combinatorial.

Theorem 2.1
.

A right-angled abstract polyhedron
(P,\mathcal{E})
is realized as a hyperbolic polyhedron, which we denote by
\mathcal{P}
, if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

P
has at least six faces;

(2)

each vertex in
P
has valency
3
or
4
;

(3)

for any three faces
(F_{i},F_{j},F_{k})
in
P
such that
F_{i}\cap F_{j}
and
F_{j}\cap F_{k}
are edges in
P
with distinct endpoints, we have
F_{i}\cap F_{k}=\emptyset
;

(4)

P^{*}
has no prismatic
4
-circuits.

Moreover, each 3-valent vertex in
P
corresponds to a finite vertex in
\mathcal{P}
, each 4-valent vertex in
P
corresponds to an ideal vertex in
\mathcal{P}
, and the realization is unique up to isometry.

By an
ideal right-angled polyhedron
we will mean a right-angled hyperbolic polyhedron all of whose vertices are ideal. Ideal right-angled polyhedra are naturally related to knot theory, since complements of hyperbolic fully augmented links decompose into pairs of isometric ideal right-angled polyhedra; see
[
24
,
28
,
36
]
.

For a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{ideal}
, denote by
F
the number of faces, by
V
the number of vertices, and by
p_{k}
the number of
k
-gonal faces, where
k\geq 3
. Then
F=\sum_{k\geq 3}p_{k}
. Since all vertices of the polyhedron are 4-valent, the Euler formula for polyhedra implies that
F=V+2
, whence

(1)

p_{3}=8+\sum_{k\geq 5}(k-4)p_{k}.

In particular, the ideal right-angled polyhedron with the smallest number of faces is the octahedron.

Traditionally, volumes of polyhedra in three-dimensional hyperbolic space are computed using the

\Lambda(\theta)=-\int_{0}^{\theta}\log|2\sin t|dt.

In particular, for

Upper and lower estimates of the volumes of ideal right-angled polyhedra in terms of the number of vertices were obtained by Atkinson in
[
7
]

Theorem 2.3
.

[
7
, Theorem 2.2]

If an ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
has
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
vertices, then

(4)

\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{4}(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-2)\leq\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{2}(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-4).

Both inequalities become equalities for the regular ideal hyperbolic octahedron, the unique ideal right-angled polyhedron with six vertices.
Moreover, the upper estimate is asymptotically sharp: there exists a sequence of ideal right-angled polyhedra
\{Q_{i}\}
with
\mathrm{ver}(Q_{i})\to\infty
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{\mathrm{vol}(Q_{i})}{\mathrm{ver}(Q_{i})}=\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}.

Note that by Theorem
2.1
, inequality (
4
) assumes that
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\geq 6
. Subsequently, the upper estimate from (
4
) was improved in
[

]
for the case
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\geq 8
, and in
[
4
]
for the case
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\geq 24
.

3.
Compact right-angled polyhedra

By a
compact right-angled polyhedron
we will mean a right-angled hyperbolic polyhedron all of whose vertices are finite. By Theorem
2.1
, all vertices of a compact right-angled polyhedron are 3-valent. Note, see for example
[
20
, Th. 3.2]
, that each face of a compact right-angled polyhedron is a right-angled polygon and, consequently, has at least
5
sides.

For a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{comp}
, denote by
F
the number of its faces, by
V
the number of its vertices, and by
p_{k}
the number of its
k
-gonal faces,
k\geq 5
. Then
F=\sum_{k\geq 5}p_{k}
. From the Euler formula and the 3-valency of the vertices of the polyhedron it follows that
2F=V+4
, whence

(5)

p_{5}=12+\sum_{k\geq 7}(k-6)p_{k}.

In particular, the compact right-angled hyperbolic polyhedron with the smallest number of faces, and hence with the smallest number of vertices, is the dodecahedron.

Since the conditions for realizing an abstract right-angled polyhedron as a compact right-angled polyhedron in
\mathbb{H}^{3}
were first studied by Pogorelov
[
26
]
, these polyhedra are also called
Pogorelov polyhedra
. Information about the life and scientific research of Aleksei Vasil’evich Pogorelov is presented in the article
[

Proof of Theorem

.

We establish that
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})\subset\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
, with the lower bound attained on the octahedron and the upper bound sharp.
From inequality (
4
) we obtain the inequality

\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{4}\frac{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-2}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}\leq\omega(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{2}\frac{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-4}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})},

where
\omega(\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})/\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
and
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\geq 6
.
Consider the lower estimate. Since for
N\geq 6
the function
f(N)=\frac{N-2}{N}
is monotonically increasing, we have
\min_{N\geq 6}f(N)=f(6)=\frac{2}{3}
, whence
\omega(P)\geq\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
, and equality is attained on the octahedron.
Consider the upper estimate. For
N\geq 6
the function
g(N)=\frac{N-4}{N}
is monotonically increasing and tends to 1 as
N\to\infty
; therefore, if
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\geq 6
, the inequality
\omega(\mathcal{P})<\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
holds.
At the same time, since by Theorem
2.3
the upper estimate in inequality (
4
) is asymptotically sharp, there exists a sequence of polyhedra for which the normalized volume tends to
\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
.
Thus, the upper bound of the spectrum is
\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
, but it is not attained.

We show that on the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
the spectrum is discrete.
Indeed, for arbitrary
\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}}<C<\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
consider the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C\right]
.
Suppose that
\omega(\mathcal{P})\in\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C\right]
.
Then from the lower estimate in inequality (
4
) we have:

\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{4}\left(1-\frac{2}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}\right)\leq\omega(\mathcal{P})\leq C.

It follows that

1-\frac{2}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}\leq\frac{4C}{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}\quad\text{and further,}\quad\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{2v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{v_{\mathrm{oct}}-4C}.

By the choice of
C
, the expression on the right-hand side of the inequality is positive.
Thus, the number of vertices
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
is bounded above by a quantity depending only on
C
and on the constant
v_{\mathrm{oct}}
.

According to Theorem
2.1
, an ideal right-angled hyperbolic polyhedron is uniquely determined up to isometry by its 1-skeleton, which is a 3-connected planar 4-valent graph.
Since the number of such graphs with a given number of vertices is finite, the set of possible values of volumes (and hence of normalized volumes) is also finite.
Thus, any interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C\right]
, where
C<\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
, contains only finitely many values of the spectrum.
Consequently, the spectrum is discrete on the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
.

Now we establish that the spectrum of normalized volumes is everywhere dense on the segment
\left[\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
.
For convenience of exposition, introduce auxiliary quantities. For a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{ideal}
, define the
modified number of vertices

\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})
by the formula

\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-3
, and also define the
modified normalized volume

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
by setting:

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})}.

By inequality (
1
), every polyhedron from
\mathcal{R}_{ideal}
has at least eight triangular faces.
It is well known (see, for example,
[
18
, Prop. 2.5]
) that in Lobachevsky geometry any two ideal triangles are isometric.
This property makes it possible to correctly define the operation of
connected sum
, that is, gluing two ideal right-angled polyhedra along their triangular faces.
Let
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{ideal}
. Choose triangular faces
T_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
T_{2}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}
.
Since
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
are isometric, we can glue
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
along these faces.
Denote the resulting polyhedron by
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Under the operation of connected sum of ideal right-angled polyhedra along triangular faces, volume behaves additively:

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{2}),

and the number of vertices changes as follows:

\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-3.

Moreover, the polyhedron
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
is ideal right-angled.

Proof.

When right-angled polyhedra are glued, the faces adjacent to
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
pairwise form flat dihedral angles of magnitude
\pi
and merge into complete geodesic hyperfaces. The right angles with the remaining faces are preserved, and therefore
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
is again a right-angled polyhedron.
Since the two-dimensional gluing face has zero three-dimensional volume, the equality
\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{2})
holds. When the gluing faces are identified, the three ideal vertices of the triangle
T_{1}
are pairwise identified with the three ideal vertices of the triangle
T_{2}
. Since these vertices lie on the absolute, after gluing they remain ideal vertices. Thus the
6
original vertices corresponding to
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
merge into
3
vertices, which gives the formula
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-3
. The lemma is proved.
∎

Note that under the connected-sum operation the modified number of vertices behaves additively:

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-3)-3

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})-3)+(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-3)=\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2}).

Compute the modified normalized volume of the connected sum:

(9)

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}=\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})\cdot\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})\cdot\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}.

Thus, the quantity
\widetilde{\omega}
for the connected sum of polyhedra behaves as a weighted average of the values of
\widetilde{\omega}
of the summands, where the weights are the modified numbers of vertices.

Consider the sequence of polyhedra

obtained by successively gluing

\mathcal{P}
.
Obviously,
\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
.
Note that

\omega(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})+3}=\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})\cdot\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})+3}.

Since
\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})=k\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})\to\infty
as

, the fraction on the right tends to

.
Thus,

\lim_{k\to\infty}\omega(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}).

Therefore, in order to approximate an arbitrary value in the interval

by normalized volumes, it is enough to approximate it by modified normalized volumes.

for which
|\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}-\widetilde{\omega}(Q_{i_{\varepsilon}})|<\varepsilon
. Applying to the polyhedra
\mathcal{P}_{1,\varepsilon}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2,\varepsilon}
the same arguments that we applied above to the polyhedra
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
, we obtain that the normalized volume is everywhere dense on the interval
\left(\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}+\varepsilon,\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}-\varepsilon\right)
.
Thus the normalized volume is everywhere dense on the segment
\left[\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right]
. The proof of Theorem
1.1
is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

We establish that
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{comp})\subset\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right)
.
Dividing all parts of inequality (
8
) by
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
, we obtain the following inequality for the normalized volume:

\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{32}\frac{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-8}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}\leq\omega(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}\frac{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-10}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}.

As follows from formula (
5
), the minimum number of vertices for a polyhedron from
\mathcal{R}_{comp}
is
20
.
For
N\geq 20
the function
h(N)=\frac{N-8}{N}=1-\frac{8}{N}
is monotonically increasing.
The minimal values of normalized volumes of polyhedra from
\mathcal{R}_{comp}
with the number of vertices from
20
to
46
are presented in Table
2
.
Substituting
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})=48
, we obtain:

\omega(\mathcal{P})\geq\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{32}\cdot\frac{48-8}{48}=\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}}.

As is seen from Table
2
, the quantity
\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
does not exceed the minimal values of normalized volumes of polyhedra with the number of vertices from
20
to
46
; therefore,
\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
can be taken as an estimate for the lower bound of the spectrum.
For the upper bound, consider the function
u(N)=\frac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}(1-\frac{10}{N})
. It tends to
\frac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}
as
N\to\infty
.
Since by Theorem
3.3
the upper estimate in (
8
) is asymptotically sharp, the upper bound of the spectrum is
\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}
.

We show that on the interval
\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{32}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
the spectrum is discrete.
Indeed, for arbitrary
\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}}<C<\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{32}
consider the segment
[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C]
.
Suppose that
\omega(\mathcal{P})\in[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C]
. Then from the lower estimate in inequality (
8
) we have:

\frac{v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{32}\left(1-\frac{8}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}\right)\leq C\quad\text{and further,}\qquad\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})\leq\frac{8v_{\mathrm{oct}}}{v_{\mathrm{oct}}-32C}.

By the choice of
C
, the expression on the right-hand side of the inequality is positive.
Thus, the number of vertices
\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})
is bounded above by a quantity depending only on
C
and on the constant
v_{\mathrm{oct}}
.

According to Theorem
2.1
, a compact hyperbolic polyhedron is uniquely determined up to isometry by its 1-skeleton, which is a 3-connected planar 3-valent graph.
Since the number of such graphs with a given number of vertices is finite, the set of possible values of volumes (and hence of normalized volumes) is also finite.
Thus, any interval
\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},C\right]
contains only finitely many values of the spectrum.
Consequently, the spectrum is discrete on the interval
\left[\frac{5}{192}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{32}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
.

We show that in the interval
\left[\frac{5}{16}v_{\mathrm{tet}},\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
the spectrum is everywhere dense.
Define the operation of
connected sum
(gluing) of two polyhedra from
\mathcal{R}_{comp}
along two isometric pentagonal faces.

For a polyhedron
\mathcal{P}\in\mathcal{R}_{comp}
, define the modified number of vertices
\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})=\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-10
, and also define the modified normalized volume
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
by setting

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})}.

The relation between the usual normalized volume
\omega(\mathcal{P})
and the modified one
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
is given by the relation:

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-10}=\omega(\mathcal{P})\cdot\frac{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})-10}.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1},\mathcal{P}_{2}\in\mathcal{R}_{comp}
. Suppose that there are mutually isometric pentagonal faces
F_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
F_{2}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}
.
Glue the polyhedra
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
along these faces and denote the resulting polyhedron by
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
.
We will call this operation the
connected sum along pentagonal faces
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Under the operation of connected sum of compact right-angled polyhedra along pentagonal faces, volume behaves additively:

\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}_{2}),

and the number of vertices changes as follows:

\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-10.

Moreover, the polyhedron
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
is compact right-angled.

Proof.

When right-angled polyhedra are glued, the faces adjacent to
F_{1}
and
F_{2}
pairwise form flat dihedral angles of magnitude
\pi
and merge into complete geodesic hyperfaces. The right angles with the remaining faces are preserved, and therefore
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
is again a right-angled polyhedron. In a compact right-angled polyhedron all vertices are 3-valent. Each vertex of the gluing face is formed by the intersection of this face itself and two adjacent faces perpendicular to it. Under gluing, the face goes into the interior, and the two adjacent faces merge smoothly. Consequently, their intersection point becomes an interior point of an edge and ceases to be a vertex. Since the two glued pentagonal faces contain a total of
10
vertices, all of them disappear from the 1-skeleton under identification, which gives the required formula. The lemma is proved.
∎

At the same time, the modified number of vertices behaves additively:

\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=(\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P}_{2})-10)-10=\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2}).

Consequently, the modified normalized volume of the connected sum along pentagonal faces is a weighted average:

(10)

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2})=\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})\cdot\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})\cdot\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}.

Consider successive gluing of
m
copies of one and the same polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
. Denote the resulting polyhedron by
\#_{m}\mathcal{P}
.
Then
\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
, since this is the average of identical quantities.
The relation between the usual and modified normalized volume is described by the formula:

\omega(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})}=\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})+10}=\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})\frac{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})}{\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})+10}.

Since
\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})=m\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P})\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
, the fraction on the right tends to
1
.
Consequently,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\omega(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{m}\mathcal{P})=\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}).

This means that any value of the modified normalized volume
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P})
is a limit point for the spectrum of the usual normalized volumes
\omega
.
Thus, in order to approximate an arbitrary value in the interval
[\frac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{16},\frac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}]
by normalized volumes, it is enough to approximate it by modified normalized volumes.

Let
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
be two polyhedra from
\mathcal{R}_{comp}
.
Let
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1})=A
and
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{2})=B
, where
A<B
.
Consider the polyhedron
\#_{k}\mathcal{P}_{1}\#_{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{2}
, which is the connected sum of
k
copies of
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\ell
copies of
\mathcal{P}_{2}
along mutually isometric pentagonal faces.
Then:

\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{k}\mathcal{P}_{1}\#_{\ell}\mathcal{P}_{2})=\frac{k\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})\cdot A+\ell\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})\cdot B}{k\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})+\ell\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}.

For arbitrary
\alpha\in(0,1)
choose sequences of integers
\{k_{i}\}
and
\{\ell_{i}\}
such that

\lim_{i\to\infty}\frac{k_{i}}{\ell_{i}}=\frac{\alpha\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{2})}{(1-\alpha)\cdot\widetilde{\mathrm{ver}}(\mathcal{P}_{1})}.

Then

\lim_{i\to\infty}\widetilde{\omega}(\#_{k_{i}}\mathcal{P}_{1}\#_{\ell_{i}}\mathcal{P}_{2})=\alpha\cdot A+(1-\alpha)\cdot B.

Consequently, the set of values of
\widetilde{\omega}
is dense in the interval between any two values
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{1})
and
\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{P}_{2})
.

Now we pass to concrete families of polyhedra.
To realize the connected sum, we need polyhedra with isometric pentagonal faces.
Consider Löbell polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n)
and their towers
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
.
For fixed
n
, all lateral pentagonal faces of these polyhedra are isometric to each other. Denote the set of such pentagonal faces by
\mathcal{F}_{n}
.
The polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n)
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
each have
2n
pairwise isometric pentagonal faces.

Moreover, note that if a pair of arbitrary polyhedra
\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}
has
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
pairwise isometric pentagonal faces from the set
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, respectively, then the polyhedron
\mathcal{P}_{1}\#\mathcal{P}_{2}
obtained by a connected sum along a pair of such faces has at least
p_{1}+p_{2}-12
isometric pentagonal faces of the class
\mathcal{F}_{n}
. Indeed, under gluing the two faces
F_{1}\subset\mathcal{P}_{1}
and
F_{2}\subset\mathcal{P}_{2}
go into the interior of the polyhedron. In addition, the faces incident to the gluing faces may change their combinatorial type (and thereby cease to belong to the class
\mathcal{F}_{n}
), and there are exactly
5
of them in each of the polyhedra. In the worst case, all these
12
faces belonged to
\mathcal{F}_{n}
and were lost.

Further we will assume that
n>6
. Then after several connected-sum operations applied to copies of the polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n)
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
along pentagonal faces from the set
\mathcal{F}_{n}
, the resulting polyhedron will have no fewer pentagonal faces of the class
\mathcal{F}_{n}
than
\mathcal{L}(n)
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
have.
This makes it possible to repeat the connected-sum operation on copies of the polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n)
and
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
any required number of times.

First of all, note that by formula (
7
), the endpoints of the interval of interest
\left[\frac{5}{16}v_{\mathrm{tet}},\frac{5}{8}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
can be obtained as limits of sequences of modified volumes of the polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n)
(letting
n
tend to infinity) and
\mathcal{L}_{k}(n)
(letting
n
and
k
tend to infinity).

To complete the proof, note that by formula (
7
), for an arbitrarily small
\varepsilon>0
there are natural numbers
k_{\varepsilon}
and
n_{\varepsilon}
such that

\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{L}(n_{\varepsilon}))\in[\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{16},\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{16}+\varepsilon],\qquad\widetilde{\omega}(\mathcal{L}_{k_{\varepsilon}}(n_{\varepsilon}))\in[\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}-\varepsilon,\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}].

Thus, we can construct connected sums of the polyhedra
\mathcal{L}(n_{\varepsilon})
and
\mathcal{L}_{k_{\varepsilon}}(n_{\varepsilon})
of the following form:

\#_{m}\mathcal{L}(n_{\varepsilon})\#_{s}\mathcal{L}_{k_{\varepsilon}}(n_{\varepsilon})

and apply the procedure described above to approximate any number in the interval
[\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{16}+\varepsilon,\tfrac{5v_{\mathrm{tet}}}{8}-\varepsilon]
.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is complete.
∎

6.
Comparison of spectra and open questions

As we already mentioned in the introduction, there is a close connection between volumes of fully augmented links and volumes of ideal right-angled polyhedra. It is known, see
[
24
,
28
,
36
]
, that the complement of any fully augmented hyperbolic link
L\subset S^{3}
can be cut into two mutually isometric ideal right-angled polyhedra
\mathcal{P}
.
In this case the following relations hold:

(11)

\mathrm{vol}(L)=2\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P}),\qquad\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})=3a(L),

where
a(L)
is the number of augmentations (vertical components) of the link.

Kwon and Tham
[
23
]
studied the volume density spectrum
\operatorname{vd}(L)
of a fully augmented link
L
, which is defined as follows:
\operatorname{vd}(L)=\mathrm{vol}(L)/a(L)
.
Express this quantity through the normalized volume of the polyhedron
\mathcal{P}
corresponding to the link
L
using the relations (
11
):

\operatorname{vd}(L)=\frac{2\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})/3}=6\frac{\mathrm{vol}(\mathcal{P})}{\mathrm{ver}(\mathcal{P})}=6\,\omega(\mathcal{P}).

In
[
23
]
it is shown that the spectrum
\operatorname{vd}(L)
is contained in the interval
\left[v_{\mathrm{oct}},10v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
, is discrete in
\left[v_{\mathrm{oct}},2v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
, and is everywhere dense in
\left[2v_{\mathrm{oct}},10v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
. Recalculating these intervals for
\omega(\mathcal{P})
(by division by
6
), we obtain that for the subclass of polyhedra corresponding to fully augmented links, the spectrum lies in the interval
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{5}{3}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
, is discrete in
\left[\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{1}{3}v_{\mathrm{oct}}\right)
, and is everywhere dense in
\left[\frac{1}{3}v_{\mathrm{oct}},\frac{5}{3}v_{\mathrm{tet}}\right]
.

Comparing the spectrum
\Omega(\textrm{FAL})
of volume densities of augmented links with the spectrum
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})
of normalized volumes of ideal right-angled polyhedra, note the following facts; see Fig.
10
.

(1)

The lower bounds of the spectra coincide and are equal to
d=\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
.

(2)

The upper bound of the spectrum
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})
is
k=\frac{1}{2}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
, which exceeds the upper bound
h=\frac{5}{3}v_{\mathrm{tet}}
of the spectrum
\Omega(\textrm{FAL})
. This difference is explained by the fact that the class of all ideal right-angled polyhedra is substantially broader than the class of polyhedra arising from link diagrams. For example, in polyhedra arising from links, each vertex is incident to two triangles that intersect exactly at this vertex).

(3)

The structures of the spectra (first there is a discrete part, and then an everywhere dense part) coincide qualitatively. The transition from the discrete part to the everywhere dense one in the case of
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})
occurs at the value
f=\frac{1}{4}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
, whereas in the case of
\Omega(\textrm{FAL})
it occurs at the value
g=\frac{1}{3}v_{\mathrm{oct}}
.

(4)

As is seen from the proof of Theorem
1.1
and Table

, the only normalized volume from
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{ideal})
that falls into the dense part of the spectrum
\Omega(\mathcal{R}_{comp})
is
d=\frac{1}{6}v_{\mathrm{oct}}=0,610643\in[d,e)
, corresponding to the octahedron.
