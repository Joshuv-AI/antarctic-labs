Positivity of vector bundles and dominance
F. Laytimi, W. Nahm

Abstract
. Let E E be a vector bundle and S a S_{a} , S b S_{b} the Schur functors associated to partitions a a and b b . Previously we have shown that ampleness of S a ​ E S_{a}E implies ampleness of S b ​ E S_{b}E when a a is greater than b b in the dominance partial order. Here we prove that this result generalizes to k k -ample, semiample and nef vector bundles. Our proof uses the common algebraic nature of these three properties and an investigation of the Littlewood-Richardson rules.

1.
Algebraic Positivities

Various forms of positivity for line bundles have been studied in great depth, but for vector bundles even basic issues remain underexplored, despite old foundational work (
[
1
]
,
[
2
]
) and a more recent resurgence following
[
3
]
. Our own motivation comes from connectedness and vanishing theorems
[
4
]
, but here we shall explore more basic properties.

We will consider vector bundles
E
on a fixed projective manifold
X
. Since we refer to
[
2
]
, we work with complex numbers, though extensions to other fields of characteristic zero are straightforward. When not stated otherwise, we transfer positivity properties from line bundles to vector bundles by the procedure of Hartshorne
in
[
1
]
, who was inspired by Grothendieck’s approach in EGA II. Accordingly, a positivity property of
E
is defined as the corresponding property of the line bundle
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(1)
. The points of
{\mathbb{P}}E
are given by a point
x
in
X
and a hyperplane in the fibre
E_{x}
of
E
over
x
. We realize them as hyperplanes annihilated by
span(u)
, where
u
is a vector in
E_{x}^{*}
. The fibre of
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(1)
over such a point can be identified with
span(u)^{*}
. When
E
is a line bundle
L
, then
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}L}(1)
has a natural identification with
L
, because
{\mathbb{P}}L
reduces to
X
and
span(u)
to
L_{x}^{*}
.

The most commonly encountered form of positivity is ampleness, but the much weaker semiampleness or the intermediate form of
k
-ampleness in the sense of Sommese
[
2
]
are also of considerable interest. Recall that a line bundle
L
over
X
is semiample when
L^{n}
is spanned by global sections for some positive integer
n
. Such a semiample bundle is
k
-ample if the fibers of the corresponding map

X\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}(H^{0}(X,L^{n}))
have at most dimension
k
. Thus
k
-ampleness is equivalent to ampleness for
k=0
and becomes weaker for increasing
k
until it reduces to semiampleness for
k
equal to the dimension of
X
. A vector bundle

E
is semiample when

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(n)
is spanned by global sections for some positive integer
n
, and so on.

We shall generalize some properties of ampleness to such weaker forms of positivity. To avoid unnecessary repetition, we introduce a general concept.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
{\mathcal{A}}
be a property of isomorphism classes of vector bundles on
X
. We write
{\mathcal{A}}(E)
when
E
has the property
{\mathcal{A}}
. We define algebraic properties
{\mathcal{A}}
by the following three conditions

Additivity:

{\mathcal{A}}(E\oplus F)
if and only if
{\mathcal{A}}(E)
and
{\mathcal{A}}(F)

Multiplicativity:

if
{\mathcal{A}}(E)
and
{\mathcal{A}}(F)
then
{\mathcal{A}}(E\otimes F)

Exponent elimination:

if
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes n})
then
{\mathcal{A}}(E)
.

Note that
{\mathcal{A}}(E)
implies
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes n})
by the multiplicativity condition. It is well known that ampleness is algebraic (
[
1
]
, see
[
5
]
for characteristic
p
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Semiampleness,
k
-ampleness and nefness are algebraic properties.

Proof.

For exponent elimination, we make use of the inclusion
j_{n}:{\mathbb{P}}E\rightarrow{\mathbb{P}}E^{\otimes n}
given by

u\mapsto u^{n}
, for which

j_{n}^{*}{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E^{\otimes n}}(1)={\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(n).

Exponent elimination for
{\mathcal{A}}
is immediate when it is true for line bundles
L
and when
{\mathcal{A}}(L)
implies
{\mathcal{A}}(j^{*}L)
for injections
j
, because in this case
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E^{\otimes n}}(1))
implies
{\mathcal{A}}({\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(1)^{n})
by using
j_{n}^{*}
. For
k
-ampleness this is stated in (
[
2
]
, Corollary 1.9).

We give the argument for semiampleness. Semiampleness of a line bundle
L
means that there is a positive power
L^{l}
that is generated by sections. Thus semiampleness of
E^{\otimes n}
means that

{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E^{\otimes n}}(l)
and consequently its
j_{n}^{*}
image
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(1)^{nl}
are generated by sections. Thus
{\mathcal{O}}_{{\mathbb{P}}E}(1)

and by definition
E
are semiample, too.
For nef vector bundles, the required properties are proven in (
[
3
]
, Thm. 6.2.12).

In
[
6
]
we have proven additivity and multiplicativity for semiampleness. For
k
-ampleness additivity and multiplicativity are given by Corollary 1.10 in
[
2
]
.
∎

Bigness is not an algebraic property, because it is not additive. Indeed, if
E,F
are vector bundles over
X
and
E
is big, then
E\oplus F
is big, without condition on
F
. This is an immediate consequence of Proposition 3.4 in
[
7
]
.

Even for semiampleness one has to be careful about the way one generalizes from line bundles to vector bundles. Demailly’s definition of strong semiampleness of
E
states that some symmetric power
S^{r}E
is generated by sections (
[
8
]
, 1.1). This property is not additive. Indeed, choose
X
and a line bundle
L
on
X
so that
L
has no section, but
L^{2}
is the trivial bundle
I
. Then
L
and of course
I
are Demailly semiample, but
L\oplus I
is not. Demailly semiampleness is a stronger assumption than what we use, because it states that for any non-vanishing
U
in
S^{r}E_{x}^{*}
there is a section
s
of
S^{r}E
for which
\langle s(x),U\rangle\neq 0
, whereas we require this only for
U
of the form
u^{r}
with
u\in E_{x}^{*}
.

As exemplified by Demailly’s definition, using symmetric powers is as natural as using tensor powers. In our definition of algebraic properties, replacement of
E^{\otimes n}
by
S^{n}E
yields an equivalent result. In one direction, for characteristic zero,
S^{n}E
is a direct summand of
E^{\otimes n}
. Thus when
{\mathcal{A}}
is additive,
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes n})
implies
{\mathcal{A}}(S^{n}E)
. Conversely, by multiplicativity,
{\mathcal{A}}(S^{n}E)

implies
{\mathcal{A}}((S^{n}E)^{\otimes N})
for any positive integer
N
. For suitable
N
we will show as part of our main result Theorem
2.16
that
{\mathcal{A}}((S^{n}E)^{\otimes N})
implies
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes Nn})
.
Indeed, both
{\mathcal{A}}((S^{n}E)^{\otimes N})
and
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes Nn})
can be written as direct sums of terms that are isomorphic to
S_{a}E
for a finite set of Schur functors
S_{a}
, with certain nonvanishing multiplicities. We shall prove that for suitable
N
the two sets of Schur functors are exactly the same. Thus when
{\mathcal{A}}
is additive,
{\mathcal{A}}((S^{n}E)^{\otimes N})
and
{\mathcal{A}}(E^{\otimes Nn})
are equivalent.

We recall some basic facts. The Schur functors
S_{a}
are labeled by integral partitions
a
. Let
E
be a vector bundle of rank
d
on
X
.
Let
a=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{r})
with
a_{i}\geq a_{i+1}
,
a_{r}>0
, be a partition of length
r
with
r\leq d
. Then
S_{a}E
is a nontrivial vector bundle on
X
. Recall that the weight of
a
is given by

|a|=\sum_{k=1}^{r}a_{i},

that
S_{a}E=S^{|a|}E
for
r=1
and
S_{a}E=\Lambda^{|a|}E
for
a_{1}=1
. More generally,
S_{a}E
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
E^{\otimes|a|}
for any
a
.

For ease of notation, let
a_{k}=0
for
k>r
. Partitions are partially ordered by dominance, a relation we write as
\succsim
.
For
a,b
of equal weight,
a\succsim b
means that

\sum_{k=1}^{l}a_{i}\geq\sum_{k=1}^{l}b_{i}

for
l=1,\ldots,d
. For arbitrary
a,b
one can compare
|b|a
and
|a|b
, because both partitions have the weight
|a||b|
. When

|b|a\succsim|a|b
and
|a|b\succsim|b|a

then
|b|a=|a|b
, so that
a
and
b
are proportional, for example
b=na
.

When
|b|a\succsim|a|b
and
S_{a}E
is ample, then
S_{b}E
is ample, as we proved in
[
6
]
. Here our main result is a generalisation of this fact to any algebraic positivity.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
a,b
be partitions so that
|b|a\succsim|a|b
. When
{\mathcal{A}}
is an algebraic property, then
{\mathcal{A}}(S_{a}E)
implies

{\mathcal{A}}(S_{b}E)
. In particular, for positive integers
n
,
{\mathcal{A}}(S_{na}E)
is equivalent to
{\mathcal{A}}(S_{a}E)
.

For the proof of this theorem we will use Theorem
2.16
, which does not depend on this introduction. Let
M,N
be positive integers so that
M|a|b=N|b|a
. By multiplicativity and additivity, the algebraic property
{\mathcal{A}}(S_{a}E)
carries over to direct summands of
(S_{a}E)^{\otimes N}
. By Theorem
2.16
, for suitable
N
any direct summand of
(S_{b}E)^{\otimes M}
is isomorphic to a direct summand of
(S_{a}E)^{\otimes N}
. By additivity and exponent elimination this establishes
{\mathcal{A}}(S_{b}E)
.

Our proof for the special property of ampleness given in
[
6
]
was based on properties of the Littlewood-Richardson rules, supplemented by a cohomological criterion. Our new proof is based on the Littlewood-Richardson rules only, but needs a slightly deeper investigation of their structure. On the other hand, it also covers the case of ampleness. Theorem
2.16
will be proved in the next section.

Some readers may be interested in combinatorics but not geometry. Accordingly, section 2 is self-contained and does not refer to section 1.

Definition 2.1

We define the dominance partial order by

Lemma 2.2

For
A\succ B
,
|A\setminus B|=k
, there is an interpolating sequence

. Let

Theorem 2.16
.

For any
A\in{\mathcal{P}}(l)
there exists
N_{A}\in{\mathbb{N}}
so that for
n\geq N_{A}
and any partition
B
with
B\preccurlyeq nA
one has
B\lhd A^{\otimes n}
.

Proof.

The set of partitions
B
for which
B\preccurlyeq nA
for some
n\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}
forms a cone
{\mathcal{C}}(A)
in
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{l}
. By equation (
2.6
), the partitions
B
for which
B\lhd A^{n}
for some
n
form a subsemigroup
{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(A)
of
{\mathcal{C}}(A)
.
Let
\mathcal{J}
be the set of all subdivisions of
\{1,\ldots,l\}
.
By Lemma (
2.12
),
G_{A}(J)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(A)
for any
J\in{\mathcal{J}}
.
In (
[
6
]
, Lemma (3.11)) we have shown that
{\mathcal{C}}(A)\otimes{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
is generated by the set of
G_{A}(J)
with
J\in{\mathcal{J}}
. The cone
{\mathcal{C}}(A)
can be written as a finite union of simplicial cones. These simplicial cones are labeled by subsets
S\in{\mathcal{J}}
such that
the sequences
G_{J}(A)
with
J\in S
are linearly independent.
Any
B
in such a simplicial cone has the form

B=\sum_{J\in S}\kappa_{J}G_{A}(J)
, where the
\kappa_{J}
are numbers in
{\mathbb{Q}}_{\geq 0}
with bounded denominators. We write
\kappa_{J}=\lfloor\kappa_{J}\rfloor+\lambda_{J}
.
The partitions
P
of the form

form a finite subset
\Phi
of
{\mathcal{C}}(A)
. To show that
{\mathcal{C}}(A)\setminus{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(A)
is a finite set, it suffices to show that for any
P\in\Phi
and any
J\in{\mathcal{J}}
there is an integer

such that
N(P,J)G_{A}(J)+P\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(A)
. Indeed, then
\sum_{J\in\Sigma}\rho_{J}G_{A}(J)\in{\mathcal{C}}^{\prime}(A)
whenever

for at least one
J\in{\mathcal{J}}
.
Let
|P|=n_{P}|A|
,
|P\setminus n_{P}A|=k_{P}
.
Since
G_{J}(A)\preccurlyeq L(l)A
, one as
P\preccurlyeq n_{P}A
.
By Lemma (
2.15
) one has

(k_{P}n_{P}G_{J}(A)+P)\lhd(n_{P}A)^{\otimes k_{P}L(r)}\otimes n_{P}A

. Thus choosing

, where

is the maximum of

over

in
\Phi

∎
