A. Executive Summary

The general goal of this project is to prepare next-generation quantum systems for novel quantum
information science applications. Decades of research on quantum optics have provided
revolutionary systems for manipulating atomic and photonic quantum coherence in transformative
ways. Our hypothesis is that the next-generation quantum systems will come from nano-molecular
quantum optics. Specifically, we are interested in coupling the electronic states of molecules or
nanoparticles to the quantized radiation field inside an optical cavity to create a set of new photon-
matter hybrid excitations, called polaritons. As opposed to atoms, the vibrational modes of
molecules and nanoparticles provide new degrees of freedom to mediate the quantum
transduction between electronic and photonic states, offering new ways to tune and ultimately
control the quantum coherence of the integrated system.

In this project we are interested in designing, fabricating, and characterizing the polaritons that
arise from coupling CdSe nanoplatelets (NPLs) to a Fabry-Pérot optical cavity. Specific attention
was paid to parameters of the system (cavity mode volume, quality factor, etc.) that would
maximize the collective coupling strength. Through a combined theoretical and experimental
approach, we were able to make important contributions to understanding NPL exciton-polariton
photophysics including how to use cavity loss as a tunable parameter in order to manipulate the
populations of the upper and lower polariton states. Finally, using state-of-the-art theoretical
methods, we were able to show how the collective coupling of many molecular excitons in a cavity
can protect polariton coherence from vibrationally-induced decoherence. In particular, for NPL-
cavity polaritonic systems, the quantum coherence can be extended by over an order of
magnitude at room temperature.

B. Main Body

The project had three major objectives that would support our long term vision of nano-molecular
polaritons as a vehicle for designing the next-generation systems for quantum information science
and technology.

Our specific objectives were:

1) Designing and fabricating optical cavities that facilitate strong coupling of colloidal
semiconductor nanocrystals. This goal includes maximizing cavity quality factor, minimizing
mode volume, and directing the NPLs to the largest regions of electric field.

2) Understanding the complex energetics and dynamics between molecular polariton
states and the molecular “dark” states. This goal involves a synergistic theoretical and
experimental approach to drive an understanding of how cavity detuning and polariton coupling
strength intersect with polariton photoluminescence lifetime and upper, lower polariton and dark
state population dynamics.

3) Determining the matter and optical cavity parameters that affect the quantum coherence
of the polariton state. State-of-the-art theory and experiments used to pursue this goal included
ultrafast coherent electronic spectroscopy techniques to directly characterize the polaritonic
coherence, which will be compared to theoretical predictions.

coupled to the optical cavity, is responsible for generating a long-lived coherence at room
temperature, up to 200 fs between upper and lower polariton states. Further, we theoretically
showed that the prolonged polariton coherence should be visible in the 2D spectroscopic
experiments as a beating between the upper and lower polariton states, which has not been
observed experimentally before.

Our theoretical work provides strong evidence that collective light–matter coupling leads to long-
lived polariton coherence that can be measured in 2DES spectra. However, during the project, a
clear 2DES signal from the NPLs was never isolated, primarily due to equipment failure. After the
project was over, it was discovered that the excitation laser, which was new to the project at the
outset, had a broad radiofrequency noise peak centered on the detection frequency used in the
nonlinear spectroscopy. The origin of this noise peak is unclear; however, it was likely
confounding the measurements and preventing useful 2D spectra from the nanoplatelet
polaritons.

Scientific and Technological Impact of Findings
Through an elucidation of the fundamental mechanisms of polariton decoherence for strong light-
matter coupling, and an understanding of the factors that control it, we have begun to provide the
chemistry community with a comprehensive roadmap for extending electronic coherences into a
practical regime. In addition to providing for polariton states as possible new candidates for
molecular qubits, our project has great promise for enabling low-threshold lasers, or exotic optical
switches, which are truly groundbreaking potential applications that could provide significant
benefits to society.
