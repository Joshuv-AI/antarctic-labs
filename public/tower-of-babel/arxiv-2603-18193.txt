On Non-Existence of Stabilizer Absolutely Maximally Entangled States in Even Local Dimensions
Jakub Wójcik, Owidiusz Makuta, Wojciech Bruzda, Remigiusz Augusiak

Abstract
We demonstrate that absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states consisting of N = 4 ​ k N=4k qudits with k ∈ ℕ + k\in\mathbb{N}_{+} , each of even local dimension, cannot be realized as graph states. This result imposes strong constraints on AME states in composite local dimensions and characterizes the limitations of graph-state constructions for highly entangled multipartite quantum systems. In particular, this study provides an independent solution of the recently discussed case of the AME state of four quhexes and clarifies its characterization within the stabilizer formalism, complementing the results of Cha [ arXiv:2603.13442 ].



I
Introduction

Absolutely maximally entangled (AME) states, defined as pure multipartite quantum states whose every balanced reduction is maximally mixed, represent one of the most extreme forms of multipartite entanglement
7
. These states generalize the well known maximally entangled Bell states
11
to multi-party scenarios and they play a central role in quantum information theory. AME states appear in many contexts including quantum error correction
15
;
16
, quantum secret sharing
9
and even quantum gravity where, under known under the name
perfect tensors
, they serve as fundamental building blocks of tensor-network models of the AdS/CFT correspondence
17
.

A special subclass of AME states is formed by
stabilizer states
, defined as simultaneous
+1
eigenstates of an abelian subgroup of the generalized Pauli group
6
;
12
. Due to their algebraic structure and efficient classical description, the experimental realization of such states is generally more feasible compared with generic highly entangled ones. However, stabilizer states alone do not provide universal quantum computational power. States outside this class, called
magic
, appear to be necessary for universal quantum computation
1
.

Since the discovery of the first representative of an AME(4,6) state
18
, followed by a series of subsequent results
19
;
2
;
5
;
3
, it has been the subject of ongoing debate whether any such states belong to the class of stabilizer states. Although individual cases have been ruled out, a general consensus has been lacking. In the recent paper
4
, the authors present a proof that a stabilizer AME
(4,6)
state does not exist. The argument relies on structural properties of stabilizer states in dimension
6
, namely that every stabilizer state of local dimension six is locally unitarily equivalent to a tensor product of a qubit and a qutrit stabilizer state. Using this decomposition, it is shown that if a stabilizer AME
(4,6)
state existed, it would necessarily contain an AME
(4,2)
stabilizer state as a component. However, such states are known not to exist
10
;
13
, leading to a contradiction. The work further generalizes this argument to arbitrary composite local dimensions, concluding that no stabilizer AME
(4,d)
state exists for
d\equiv 2\,(\bmod\,4)
.

In this paper, we independently show an explicit nonexistence theorem for a whole infinite family of states. Namely, no stabilizer state AME
(4k,2\ell)
for any
k,\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{+}=\{1,2,3,...\}
. This approach is different from the one presented in Ref.
4
as it is based on a direct graph-state analysis and rules out AME configurations at the level of their graph-theoretical structure. Our result is strictly more general, as it not only rules out the AME
(4,6)
stabilizer state, but also eliminates an entire infinite family of multi-partite stabilizer candidates.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
II
, we introduce the formalism of graph states and give a formal definition of AME states. Section
III
presents the main result of this work, namely a theorem establishing the non-existence of an infinite family of graphs AME states with
4k
parties, for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and even local dimension. In the final section, we summarize our findings and discuss their implications, as well as possible directions for future research.

II
Preliminaries

A graph
G
is a tuple of a set of vertices
V
, which in this work we denote by
\{1,2,\ldots,N\}=[N]
, and a set of edges
E
connecting them. Here, we focus on multigraphs, which are a generalisation of a standard graph definition that allows for
E
to be a multiset, i.e., a single edge can appear more than once. However, for convenience, we will be referring to multigraphs as graphs.

With every graph
G
one can associate an adjacency matrix
\Gamma
that encodes the connectivity of
G
, namely,
\Gamma_{i,j}
is equal to the number of times that an edge
(i,j)
appears in
E
. As we consider undirected, unlooped graphs, we have that
\Gamma_{i,j}=\Gamma_{j,i}
and
\Gamma_{i,i}=0
.

A graph state
\ket{G}\in(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes N}
is a unique state satisfying
g_{i}|G\rangle=|G\rangle
for all
i\in[N]
, where

g_{i}=X_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{N}Z_{j}^{\Gamma_{ij}},

(1)

with
\Gamma
being the adjacency matrix of
G
,
X_{i}=\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(i-1)}\otimes X\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\otimes(N-i)}
, and

X=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}|j\oplus_{d}1\rangle\langle j|,\qquad Z=\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}\omega^{j}|j\rangle\langle j|\quad:\quad\omega=\exp\frac{2\pi i}{d}.

(2)

The stabilizer
\mathbb{S}
of
|G\rangle
is a group generated by operators
g_{i}
;
\mathbb{S}=\langle g_{1},g_{2},...,g_{N}\rangle
. The projector onto
|G\rangle
is then given by

|G\rangle\langle G|=\frac{1}{d^{N}}\prod_{i=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{j=0}^{d-1}g_{i}^{j}\right)=\frac{1}{d^{N}}\sum_{S\in\mathbb{S}}S.

(3)

An
N
-partite state
\ket{\psi}
is absolutely maximally entangled (AME) if

\forall_{\begin{subarray}{c}Q\subset[N]\\
|Q|=\lceil N/2\rceil\end{subarray}}\quad{\rm Tr}_{Q}\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\big)=\frac{1}{d^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes\lfloor N/2\rfloor},

(4)

where
\mathbb{I}
is an
d\times d
identity matrix, and
{\rm Tr}_{Q}(\cdot)
is a partial trace over
Q
, and
\lceil\cdot\rceil
being a ceiling function. To make the notation more concise, we will write instead

{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}\big(|\psi\rangle\langle\psi|\big)=\frac{1}{d^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes\lfloor N/2\rfloor}.

(5)

Then, a graph state
|G\rangle
is AME if

{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}\big(|G\rangle\langle G|\big)=\frac{1}{d^{\lfloor N/2\rfloor}}\mathbb{I}^{\otimes\lfloor N/2\rfloor}.

(6)

which, by virtue of Eq. (
3
) is equivalent to

{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}\left(\sum_{S\in\mathbb{S}\setminus\{\mathbb{I}\}}S\right)=0.

(7)

III
Main Result

Before we proceed with the proof of the main result of this work, we first need to introduce a simplified, necessary and sufficient condition on AME for graph states.

Lemma 1
.

A graph state
\ket{G}
stabilized by
\mathbb{S}
is AME iff

\forall\,S\in\mathbb{S}\setminus\{\mathbb{I}\}\,\,\,:\,\,\,{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}S=0.

(8)

Proof.

Clearly, Eq. (
8
) implies Eq. (
7
), and so we only need to prove the implication in the opposite direction. Let us assume condition (
7
) holds and condition (
8
) does not, so

\underset{A\subset\mathbb{S}\setminus\{\mathbb{I}\}}{\exists}\,\,\,\,\underset{S\in A}{\forall}{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}(S)\neq 0.

(9)

Since
{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}(\underset{S\in A}{\sum}S)=0
, then also
\underset{S\in A}{\sum}{\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}(S)=0
. From Eq. (
9
) we can claim that

\underset{S\in A}{\forall}S={\rm Tr}_{\lceil N/2\rceil}(S)\otimes\mathbb{I}^{\lceil N/2\rceil}

(10)

and therefore,
\underset{S\in A}{\sum}S=0
. However, this implies

\underset{S\in A}{\sum}S|G\rangle=0,

(11)

which means
A
is an empty set which contradicts Eq. (
9
). This leads us to the conclusion that Eq. (
7
)
\iff
Eq. (
8
).
∎

Lastly, we will also need the following fact, which we also prove for completeness.

Fact 1
.

Let
A\in M_{n\times n}(\mathbb{Z}_{d})
be a matrix over a finite ring
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, where
d
is a composite number. If
\det(A)
is not a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
then
\ker(A)\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

If
A
is invertible then
\det(A)
is a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
. Conversly, since
\det(A)
is not a unit,
A
is not invertible, therefore
A
is not a bijection. Since
A
is an endomorphism over a finite set of elements, and every such injective endomorphism is surjective, then if
A
is not bijective, then it is not injective. Therefore, there exist two distinct vectors
x_{1},x_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}^{n}
such that
Ax_{1}=Ax_{2}
. This immediately implies that
x_{1}-x_{2}
is in a kernel of
A
, and so
\ker(A)\neq\emptyset
.
∎

With that, we can proceed to the main result of this work, which we formulate as the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

There are no AME
N
partite graph states of local dimension
d
for
N=4k
for
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
and even
d
.

Proof.

To prove this theorem, we will show that for each graph
G
, number of particles
N
divisible by
4
, and even local dimension
d
, there exists a stabilizing operator of a graph state
\ket{G}
that contains
N/2
identity operators, implying that
\ket{G}
is not AME as per Lemma
1
.

Let
N=4k
and let us denote by
\mathbb{B}
a subset of stabilizer operators of
\ket{G}
where each
S\in\mathbb{B}
can be expressed as

S=\left(\prod_{i=1}^{2k-1}g_{i}^{\alpha_{i}}\right)g_{j}^{\alpha_{j}},

(12)

where
j\geqslant 2k
, and
\alpha_{i}\in[d-1]
for all
i\in[2k-1]\cup\{j\}
.
To show that
\ket{G}
is not AME, we will prove that

\exists\,S\in\mathbb{B}:{\rm Tr}_{|2k|}(S)\neq 0.

(13)

To satisfy this condition, we need to find
S\in\mathbb{B}
that acts with
\mathbb{I}
on
2k
qudits. Notice that from Eq. (
12
) it follows that
S
will never act with
\mathbb{I}
on the first
2k-1
qudits, nor on the
j
’th qudit. This is because, on these qudits,
S
acts either with a nontrivial power of
X
. Therefore,
S
can only act with
\mathbb{I}
on a qudit
r
if
r\notin[2k-1]\cup\{j\}
.

Then, satisfying Eq. (
13
) for a given
S
is equivalent to satisfying the following system of equations

\forall_{r\notin[2k-1]\cup\{j\}}\;\sum_{i=1}^{2k-1}\Gamma_{i,r}\alpha_{i}+\Gamma_{j,r}\alpha_{j}=0\pmod{d}.

(14)

Let us label the determinant of this system of equations as
\Delta_{j}
, where
\Gamma_{i,r}
and
\Gamma_{j,r}
are the entries of the associated matrix, and
\alpha_{i},\alpha_{j}
are the variables. To show that
\ket{G}
is not AME for any
G
we need to prove that for each
G
there exists a set of equations as above with a nontrivial solution, i.e., the kernel of the matrix associated with
\Delta_{j}
is nontrivial. And so, by virtue of Fact
1
, we need to show that
\Delta_{j}
is not a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
.

To this end, let us consider the following sum

\sum_{j=2k}^{4k}(-1)^{j}\Delta_{j}.

(15)

Using Laplace expansion on the last column of each matrix associated with
\Delta_{j}
, we get

\Delta_{j}=\sum_{r\notin[2k-1]\cup\{j\}}(-1)^{2k+r-H(j-r)}\Gamma_{r,j}\Delta_{r,j},

(16)

where
H(\cdot)
is the Heaviside step function, and
\Delta_{r,j}
is a determinant of a minor constructed by deleting a column corresponding to qudit
j
and a row corresponding to qudit
r
. To see why
H(j-r)
appears, consider that
r
corresponds to the
(r-2k-1)
th equation in Eq. (
14
) if
j>r
or to the
(r-2k)
th equation if
j<r
.

Substituting Eq. (
16
) into Eq. (
15
) we get

\sum_{j=2k}^{4k}(-1)^{j}\Delta_{j}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j,r=2k\\
r\neq j\end{subarray}}^{4k}(-1)^{j+2k+r-H(j-r)}\Gamma_{r,j}\Delta_{r,j}.

(17)

Notice that
\Gamma_{a,b}=\Gamma_{b,a}
and
\Delta_{a,b}=\Delta_{b,a}
for all
a,b\geqslant 2m
. Therefore, each
\Gamma_{a,b}
appears in the above exactly twice, multiplied by the same minor determinant
\Delta_{a,b}
. However, notably, one of these terms is multiplied by
(-1)^{2k+a+b}
while the other by
(-1)^{2k+a+b-1}
, leading to the conclusion that

\sum_{j=2k}^{4k}(-1)^{j}\Delta_{j}=0,

(18)

which can also be written as

\sum_{j=0}^{k}\Delta_{2j+2k}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}\Delta_{2j+2k-1}.

(19)

Let us assume that all determinants are a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
. This implies that all of the determinants are odd, and so if the right side of Eq. (
19
) is even, then the left-hand side is necessarily odd (and vice versa), hence we get a contradiction. This implies that one of the determinants is not a unit in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
, meaning that Eq. (
14
) has a nontrivial solution for some
j\geqslant 2k
, and therefore
\ket{G}
is not AME.
∎

In the case of
d=2
, Theorem
1
holds for all stabilizer states, not just graph states, as per Ref.
6
, all qubit stabilizer states are equivalent to qubit graph states up to local unitaries. Interestingly, this conclusion, combined with
(
4
, Theorem 4)
allows us to strengthen our result for
d\equiv 2\,(\bmod\,4)
for a more generic case involving many-body scenario beyond the single four-party case:

Corollary 1
.

There are no
N
-partite stabilizer AME states of local dimension
d\equiv 2\,(\bmod\,4)
, for
N=4k
and
k\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.

IV
Summary

We have shown that a large class of qudit graph states, namely those with the number of qudits divisible by
4
and an even local dimension, cannot be absolutely maximally entangled. Moreover, combining our result with a recent work
4
it implies a stronger statement that no stabilizer state is absolutely maximally entangled for configurations in which the local dimension
d\equiv 2\,(\bmod\,4)
and the number of qudits is a multiple of
4
.

Stabilizer states offer a simplified framework for state construction, as they can be generated using Clifford circuits. In many multipartite qudit systems a significant fraction of known AME states are stabilizer ones, see Ref.
14
. The states from this class provide pure quantum error-correcting codes, which includes all states that are derived from maximum-distance separable codes (MDSC)
8
. The internal classification of AME states has been partially addressed in Ref.
3
, however, in general, the problem of existence and structure of stabilizer or graph AME states is not fully understood.

It remains open to determine how far the present exclusion results extend beyond the
4k
-party setting. It would be beneficial to understand if similar obstructions appear for other numbers of parties and local dimensions. Such an analysis would help establish a better boundary between stabilizer structures and non-stabilizer forms of multipartite quantum entanglement.
