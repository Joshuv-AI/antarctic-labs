On Posets of Classes of Subgroups with same set of orders of elements
Sachin Ballal and Tushar Halder

Abstract
In this paper, we study the posets of classes of subgroups of finite group having same set of orders of elements. We show that this poset is a chain only in the case of p p -groups and moreover, we characterize all finite groups for which this poset is C 2 C_{2} , the chain with two elements. We also show that this poset forms a lattice in the case of finite cyclic and dihedral groups and give a characterization when this lattice is distributive and modular. Keywords: Posets, lattices, subgroup lattices, poset of class of subgroups, etc. AMS Subject Classifications: 06A06, 06B99, 20D30, etc.

\mathbf{\widetilde{\Pi}(\emph{G})}

For a finite group

H_{1}\equiv H_{2}\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if and only if}\hskip 5.69046pt\pi_{e}(H_{1})=\pi_{e}(H_{2}),

[H_{1}]\lesssim[H_{2}]\hskip 5.69046pt\text{if and only if }\hskip 5.69046pt\pi_{e}(H_{1})\subseteq\pi_{e}(H_{2}),

In the following result, we have characterized all finite groups

Theorem 2.1
.

The poset
\widetilde{\Pi}(G)
is a chain if and only if
G
is a
p
-group.

Proof.

Let
\widetilde{\Pi}(G)
be a chain and on the contrary, assume that
G
is not a
p
-group, then for distinct prime factors
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
of
|G|
, there exists elements
a
and
b
in
G
of order
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
, respectively. Clearly
\pi_{e}(\bigl<a\bigr>)=\{1,p_{1}\}
and
\pi_{e}(\bigl<b\bigr>)=\{1,p_{2}\}
and consequently,
[\bigl<a\bigr>]
and
[\bigl<b\bigr>]
are incomparable in
\widetilde{\Pi}(G)
, a contradiction. So,
G
is a
p
-group.

Conversely, let
G
be a
p
-group of order
p^{n}
and
A
and
B
be two subgroups of
G
with
\text{exp}(A)=p^{m_{1}}
and
\text{exp}(B)=p^{m_{2}}
. Without the loss of generality, if
m_{1}\leq m_{2}
, then
\pi_{e}(A)=T(p^{m_{1}})\subseteq T(p^{m_{2}})=\pi_{e}(B)
, which implies that
[A]
and
[B]
are comparable and consequently
\widetilde{\Pi}(G)
is a chain.
∎

Corollary 2.1.1

Let

\widetilde{\Pi}(G_{1})\cong\widetilde{\Pi}(G_{2})

Theorem 2.2

For a finite group

We now focus on finite cyclic groups. The next result shows that our poset under study coincides with subgroup lattice in the case of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}

Theorem 2.3
.

For all positive integer
n
, the poset
{\widetilde{\Pi}({\mathbb{Z}_{n}})}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
and in particular,
{\widetilde{\Pi}({\mathbb{Z}_{n}})}
is a lattice.

Proof.

Consider the map
\Phi:\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})\to\widetilde{\Pi}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
by
\Phi(H)=[H]
. It is clear that
|{\widetilde{\Pi}({\mathbb{Z}_{n}})}|\leq|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|
. Now, let
H
and
K
be two distinct subgroups of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, with order
m_{1}
and
m_{2}
, respectively. Certainly, when
m_{1}>m_{2}
,
\pi_{e}(H)\neq\pi_{e}(K)
as
m_{1}\notin\pi_{e}(K)
but
m_{1}\in\pi_{e}(H)
and therefore,
[H]
and
[K]
are distinct classes. Thus,
|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|\leq|{\widetilde{\Pi}({\mathbb{Z}_{n}})}|
and hence,
|{\widetilde{\Pi}({\mathbb{Z}_{n}})}|=|\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})|
. This implies that
\Phi
is well defined and bijective.

If
[H_{1}]\lesssim[H_{2}]
, then
\pi_{e}(H_{1})\subseteq\pi_{e}(H_{2})
. Let
x\in H_{1}
be arbitrary, then
o(x)\in\pi_{e}(H_{1})
, and as
\pi_{e}(H_{1})\subseteq\pi_{e}(H_{2})
so, there is
y\in H_{2}
with
o(y)=o(x)
. As, subgroups of order
o(x)
is unique, we have
<y>=<x>
, therefore there is a positive integer
m^{\prime}
with
x=y^{m^{\prime}}
and thus
x\in H_{2}
, since
x
is arbitrary, we have
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
. Moreover, if
H_{1}\leq H_{2}
, then certainly,
\pi_{e}(H_{1})\subseteq\pi_{e}(H_{2})
and thus
[H_{1}]\lesssim[H_{2}]
. So,
\Phi
is a bijective isotone between
\mathcal{L}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
and
\widetilde{\Pi}(\mathbb{Z}_{n})
, which implies that
\Phi
is a lattice isomorphism.
∎

In order to show that
\widetilde{\Pi}(D_{n})
is a lattice, we essentially need the following result.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
G
be a finite group and
[H_{1}],[H_{2}]\in\widetilde{\Pi}(G)
. Consider the set

X=\bigcap_{\mathclap{{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\\
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(\bar{L})\end{subarray}}}}\pi_{e}(\bar{L}).

The set
\{[H],[K]\}
has a least upper bound if and only if there exists a subgroup
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(G)
with
\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})=X
. Moreover,
[H]\vee^{\prime}[K]=[L^{\prime}]
.

Similarly, consider the set

Y=\bigcup_{\mathclap{{\begin{subarray}{c}\widehat{L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\\
\pi_{e}(\widehat{L})\subseteq\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\end{subarray}}}}\pi_{e}(\widehat{L}).

The set
\{[H],[K]\}
has a greatest lower bound if and only if there exists a subgroup
L^{\prime\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(G)
with
\pi_{e}(L^{\prime\prime})=Y
. Moreover,
[H]\wedge^{\prime}[K]=[L^{\prime\prime}]
.

Proof.

Let there exists a subgroup
L^{\prime}\in\mathcal{L}(G)
with
\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})=X
. Since
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq X
, it follows that
[L^{\prime}]
is an upper bound of
\{[H],[K]\}\}
. Moreover, if
[S]
is an upper bound of
\{[H],[K]\}\}
, then
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(S)
and thus
\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})\subseteq X\subseteq\pi_{e}(S)
and therefore
[H]\vee^{\prime}[K]=[L^{\prime}]
.

Conversely, suppose
[H]\vee^{\prime}[K]=[L^{\prime}]
, then by definition of
X
it follows that
X\subseteq\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})
. Now suppose
\bar{L}
is such that
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\bar{L}
, then
[\bar{L}]
is an upper bound of
[H]
and
[K]
. Since
[L^{\prime}]
is the least upper bound of
[H]
and
[K]
, it follows that
\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})\subseteq X
. The second part follows on similar lines.
∎

In the light of Lemma 2.4, we prove the following:

Theorem 2.5
.

For positive integer
n
, the poset
\widetilde{\Pi}(D_{n})
is a lattice.

Proof.

Let
n=2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}}
, where
p_{i}
’s are distinct odd primes with
\alpha,t_{i}^{\prime}s\geq 0

[H],[K]\in\widetilde{\Pi}(D_{n})
be arbitrary classes. It is sufficient to show that the least upper bound and the greatest lower bound of
\{[H],[K]\}
exists. So consider the following cases:

Case 1:
If
2\notin\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)
, then
H=\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>
and
K=\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{v_{i}}}\biggr>
, where
0\leq u_{i},v_{i}\leq t_{i}
and
1\leq i\leq k
, and therefore,

\pi_{e}(H)=T\biggl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}-u_{i}}\biggr)\hskip 5.69046pt\text{and}\hskip 5.69046pt\pi_{e}(K)=T\biggl(\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}-v_{i}}\biggr).

So, we claim that
[L^{\prime}]
is the least upper bound of
\{[H],[K]\}
, where
L^{\prime}=\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}}\biggr>
. Clearly,
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})
as
H,K\leq L^{\prime}
and therefore,

\bigcap_{\mathclap{{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\\
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(\bar{L})\end{subarray}}}}\pi_{e}(\bar{L})\subseteq\pi_{e}(L^{\prime}).

Now, suppose that
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{x_{i}}\in\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})
with
0\leq x_{i}\leq t_{i}-\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
,
1\leq i\leq k
and
\bar{L}\leq G
with
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(\bar{L})
. Then
\bar{L}
has an element of order
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}-u_{i}}
, say,
\biggl<r^{a_{1}2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>
and an element of order
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}-v_{i}}
, say,
\biggl<r^{a_{2}2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{v_{i}}}\biggr>
. Now, as
\biggl<r^{a_{1}2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>=\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>

, so
\biggl<r^{a_{1}2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>\vee\biggl<r^{a_{2}2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{v_{i}}}\biggr>=\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{u_{i}}}\biggr>\vee\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{v_{i}}}\biggr>\leq\bar{L}
and therefore,
\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}}\biggr>\leq\bar{L}
, and consequently,
\bar{L}
contains an element of order
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{t_{i}-\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}
. Note that,
\biggl<r^{2^{\alpha}\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}}}\biggr>
contains elements of order
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{x}
where
0\leq x\leq t_{i}-\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}

\bar{L}
contains an element of order
\prod\limits_{i=1}^{k}p_{i}^{x}
where
0\leq x\leq t_{i}-\min\{u_{i},v_{i}\}
. Since
\bar{L}
was arbitrary, we have,

\pi_{e}(L^{\prime})\subseteq\bigcap_{\mathclap{{\begin{subarray}{c}\bar{L}\in\mathcal{L}(G)\\
\pi_{e}(H),\pi_{e}(K)\subseteq\pi_{e}(\bar{L})\end{subarray}}}}\pi_{e}(\bar{L}).

[L^{\prime}]
is the least upper bound of
\{[H],[K]\}
.

[L^{\prime\prime}]

Subcase 2.2:
If

Theorem 2.6
.

We first note that,

Theorem 2.10
.

The lattice

is modular if and only if

.
