Abstract




Gaussian pulse scattering by a chiral spherical shell

Abstract

Theory was formulated for scattering by a coated chiral sphere of a plane wave of arbitrary polarization state with amplitude modulated by a Gaussian pulse. The spherical core and the concentric shell of the sphere were composed of two different homogeneous materials, both isotropic chiral. Calculations of energy efficiencies for extinction, total scattering, and absorption were carried out for the shell material with experimentally determined constitutive parameters, the core being vacuous.
All three energy efficiencies depend on the relative thickness of the shell and the circular polarization state of the carrier plane wave.

Introduction

Frequency-domain scattering by a sphere is a classic problem in electromagnetic field theory. The scattering of a
linearly polarized
plane wave
by a homogeneous sphere composed of an isotropic dielectric material was solved by Lorenz

[
1
]

and Mie
[
2
]
well over a century ago. Subsequently, the theory has been extended to spheres made of an isotropic dielectric-magnetic material
[
3
,
4
]
or an isotropic chiral material
[
5
,
6
,
7
]
, as well as to concentrically multilayered spheres
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
]
. However, the focus of theoretical work has almost entirely been on linearly polarized incidence. Circularly polarized incidence, which is a more natural choice for
spheres made of isotropic chiral materials
[
12
]
, and the more general elliptically polarized incidence
have received scant attention
[
13
,
14
]
.
Whereas a few analyses of time-domain scattering by an isotropic dielectric sphere
[
15
,
16
,
17
]

(or isotropic dielectric cylinder
[
18
]
) have been reported, including for a sphere undergoing uniform translational motion
[
19
]
,
time-domain scattering by an isotropic chiral sphere has not been addressed hitherto, to the best of our knowledge.

In this letter we develop the theory for scattering by a coated chiral sphere of a signal carried by a plane wave of arbitrary polarization state. A Gaussian pulse is chosen for amplitude modulation of the carrier plane wave. Numerical results, based on the measured frequency-dependent constitutive parameters
of a real isotropic chiral material
[
20
]
,
are presented to highlight the dependencies of the energy efficiencies
for extinction, total scattering, and absorption
[
19
]
on the polarization state of the incident
carrier plane wave.

2
Theory

2.1
Coated chiral sphere

The coated chiral sphere is centered at the coordinate origin
{\bf r}={\bf 0}
.
The core
r<a
is made of a homogeneous material labelled 1, whereas the shell
a<r<b
is made of a homogeneous material labelled 2.
The two materials are specified by the frequency-domain constitutive relations
[
12
]

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf D}({\bf r},\omega)=\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\,\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)+i\,\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\,\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf H}({\bf r},\omega)\\
{\bf B}({\bf r},\omega)=\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\,\mu_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf H}({\bf r},\omega)-i\,\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}\mu_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\,\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\,{\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)\end{array}\right\},

(1)

where
{\bf E}({\bf r},\omega)\in\mathbb{C}^{3}
, etc., are phasors.
Furthermore, the relative permittivity
\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}
,
relative permeability

\mu_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}
, and relative chirality parameter
\kappa_{\ell}(\omega)\in\mathbb{C}

for
\ell\in\left\{1,2\right\}
.

2.2
Incident signal

Without loss of generality,
the incident signal propagates along the
+z
axis. Thus, the electric field of the incident signal may be expressed as

\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)=\mathrm{Re}\left\{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}f(\tau)\right\},

(2)

wherein the scalar
\tau=t-(z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
.
Following Garner
et al.

[
19
]
,
we set

f(\tau)=\exp\left(i2\pi\nu_{\mathrm{c}}\tau\right)\,\exp\left(-\frac{\tau^{2}}{2\sigma^{2}}\right),

(3)

with
\nu_{\mathrm{c}}
being the frequency of the carrier plane wave and
\lambda_{\mathrm{c}}=c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}/\nu_{\mathrm{c}}
being the carrier wavelength.
The Gaussian pulse is centered at
t=z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
and the standard deviation
\sigma
effectively determines the pulse duration.

The polarization-state vector
{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}=a_{s}\hat{{\bf u}}_{x}+a_{p}\hat{{\bf u}}_{y}
with
a_{s}\in\mathbb{C}
and
a_{p}\in\mathbb{C}

specifies an elliptically polarized plane wave propagating along the
+z
axis. The carrier plane wave
is linearly polarized if either
a_{s}=0
or
a_{p}=0
, left-circularly polarized (LCP) if
a_{p}=ia_{s}
, and
right-circularly polarized (RCP) if
a_{p}=-ia_{s}
.

For an adequate discrete-time representation of
\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)

with sampling period
t_{\mathrm{s}}
, the rate of sampling
1/t_{\mathrm{s}}
must be at least twice
as great as the highest frequency in the Fourier transform of the incident signal.
The total number
N
of samples has to be selected by trial and error
to ensure that

Nt_{\mathrm{s}}
exceeds the larger of the durations of the incident signal and the scattered signal.

The discrete Fourier transform of
f(\tau)
is given by

\bar{f}(\nu_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{q=0}^{N-1}f(\tau_{q})\exp\left(i\omega_{p}\tau_{q}\right),

(4)

with

\tau_{q}=qt_{\mathrm{s}}-(z/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0})
,
\omega_{p}=2\pi\nu_{p}
, and
\nu_{p}=p/Nt_{\mathrm{s}}
. Hence, inversion of the discrete Fourier transform delivers

{f}(\tau_{q})=\dfrac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{p=0}^{N-1}\bar{f}(\nu_{p})\exp\left(-i\omega_{p}\tau_{q}\right).

(5)

By substituting Eq. (
5
) into Eq. (
2
), the
electric field for the incident signal can be approximated by an ensemble of
N
plane waves as

\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)\approx\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Re}\left\{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}\,\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\,\exp\left[i\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}z-\omega_{p}t\right)\right]\right\},

(6)

where
\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})
denotes the Fourier transform of
{f}(\tau)
at
z=0
.
Each plane-wave component has
frequency

\nu_{p}
,
wavenumber
k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}=2\pi\nu_{p}/c_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}
, and
electric field phasor

{\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},2\pi\nu_{p})=\frac{\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\,\exp(ik_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}z)}{N}\,{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}.

(7)

Following Lorenz–Mie theory
[
3
,
4
]
,

{\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})
is expressed in terms of
the vector spherical harmonics

{\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)
and
{\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)
as

{\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)\right]\Big\},

(8)

where the coefficients

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}\mathcal{A}_{emnp}^{(1)}=-2n(n+1)i^{n}a_{p}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\delta_{m1}\\
\mathcal{A}_{omnp}^{(1)}=2n(n+1)i^{n}a_{s}\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})\delta_{m1}\end{array}\right\}

(9)

with
\mathcal{B}_{emnp}^{(1)}=-i\mathcal{A}_{omnp}^{(1)}
and

\mathcal{B}_{omnp}^{(1)}=i\mathcal{A}_{emnp}^{(1)}
,
\delta_{mm^{\prime}}
is the Kronecker delta, and

D_{mn}=(2-\delta_{m0})\frac{2n+1}{4n(n+1)}\frac{(n-m)!}{(n+m)!}

(10)

is a normalization factor.
Explicit expressions for
{\bf M}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)
and
{\bf N}_{smn}^{(1)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)

are available elsewhere
[
13
,
14
]
.

2.3
Scattered signal

Each plane-wave component with electric field phasor
{\bf E}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})

gives rise to a
scattered field with electric field phasor

{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}{\bf M}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}{\bf N}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)\right]\Big\}

(11)

for
r>b
.
Explicit expressions for
{\bf M}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)
and
{\bf N}_{smn}^{(3)}\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{\bf r}\right)
are also available elsewhere
[
13
,
14
]
.

Our task now is to derive expressions for the coefficients

\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}
and
\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}
. We do so by enforcing
boundary conditions on the surfaces
r=a
and
r=b
.
Since the coated sphere is made of isotropic chiral materials, we use the left- and right-handed vector spherical Beltrami functions
[
8
,
12
]

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{\bf L}^{(\ell,g)}_{smnp}({\bf r})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[{\bf M}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{L}_{\ell p}{\bf r})+{\bf N}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{L}_{\ell p}{\bf r})\right]\\
{\bf R}^{(\ell,g)}_{smnp}({\bf r})=\dfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[{\bf M}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{R}_{\ell p}{\bf r})-{\bf N}_{smn}^{(g)}(k^{R}_{\ell p}{\bf r})\right]\end{array}\right\},

(12)

with
\ell\in\left\{1,2\right\}
,
g\in\left\{1,3\right\}
, and the wavenumbers

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}k^{L}_{\ell p}=k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}\left[\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}\sqrt{\mu_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}+\kappa_{\ell}(\omega_{p})\right]\\
k^{R}_{\ell p}=k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}\left[\sqrt{\varepsilon_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}\sqrt{\mu_{\ell}(\omega_{p})}-\kappa_{\ell}(\omega_{p})\right]\end{array}\right\}\,.

(13)

Consequently the internal electric field phasor for the spectral component of frequency
\nu_{p}

may expressed as

{\bf E}_{{2}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{g\in\{1,3\}}\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{C}_{smnp}^{(g)}{\bf L}_{smnp}^{(2,g)}({\bf r})+\mathcal{D}_{smnp}^{(g)}{\bf R}_{smnp}^{(2,g)}({\bf r})\right]\Big\}

(14)

in the shell
b>r>a
and

{\bf E}_{{1}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\Big\{D_{mn}\left[\mathcal{E}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf L}_{smnp}^{(1,1)}({\bf r})+\mathcal{F}_{smnp}^{(1)}{\bf R}_{smnp}^{(1,1)}({\bf r})\right]\Big\}

(15)

in the core
r<a
.

The coefficients

\mathcal{C}_{smnp}^{{(g)}}
and
\mathcal{D}_{smnp}^{{(g)}}
in Eq. (
14
) as well as the coefficients
\mathcal{E}_{smnp}^{{(1)}}
and
\mathcal{F}_{smnp}^{{(1)}}
in Eq. (
15
) are determined by imposing the standard electromagnetic boundary conditions at
r=a
and
r=b
, and exploiting the orthogonalities of the trigonometric functions and associated Legendre functions
[
8
]
.
Thereby, the scattered-field coefficients
are related to the incident-field coefficients as

\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{A}^{(3)}_{smnp}\\
\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\end{matrix}\right)=\left(\begin{matrix}{c}_{np}&{d}_{np}\\
{d}_{np}&{e}_{np}\end{matrix}\right)\mbox{ \tiny{${}^{\bullet}$} }\left(\begin{matrix}\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(1)}\\
\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(1)}\end{matrix}\right).

(16)

Explicit expressions for the matrix components
{c}_{np}
,
{d}_{np}
, and
{e}_{np}
are provided in Appendix 1.
Parenthetically,in the case of a homogeneous isotropic chiral sphere, compact expressions for
{c}_{np}
,
{d}_{np}
, and
{e}_{np}
are available elsewhere

[
13
,
14
]
.

At sufficiently great distances from the coated chiral sphere, the electric phasor for each spectral component of the scattered field may be approximated as

{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},\omega_{p})\approx{\bf F}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\dfrac{\exp(i\,{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}}r)}{r},

(17)

where the
r
-independent vector

{{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\frac{1}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}}\Big[{L}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\frac{\hat{{\bf u}}_{\theta}+i\hat{{\bf u}}_{\phi}}{\sqrt{2}}+{R}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\frac{\hat{{\bf u}}_{\theta}-i\hat{{\bf u}}_{\phi}}{\sqrt{2}}\Big]\,.

(18)

The complex amplitudes of the left- and right-handed components of
k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}{{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)
are

\left.\begin{array}[]{l}{L}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\dfrac{\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)-i\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)}{\sqrt{2}}\\
{R}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\dfrac{\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)+i\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)}{\sqrt{2}}\end{array}\right\}\,,

(19)

with

\Theta_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\bigg\{(-i)^{n}D_{mn}\left[-i\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)\right]\bigg\}

(20)

and

\Phi_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)=\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\{e,o\}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\displaystyle\sum_{m=0}^{n}\bigg\{(-i)^{n}D_{mn}\left[i\mathcal{A}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)+\mathcal{B}_{smnp}^{(3)}\ f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)\right]\bigg\}\,.

(21)

Explicit expressions for the orientation-dependent coefficients
f_{smn}(\theta,\phi)
and
g_{smn}(\theta,\phi)

are available elsewhere
[
13
,
14
]
.

Finally, the electric field
\tilde{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},t)
of the scattered signal
in the far zone emerges as

\tilde{\bf E}_{\mathrm{sca}}({\bf r},t)\approx\frac{1}{N}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}{{\bf F}}_{\mathrm{sca},p}(\theta,\phi)\dfrac{\exp\left[i\left(k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}r-\omega_{p}t\right)\right]}{r}\right\}\,.

(22)

2.4
Energy efficiencies

The presence of the coated chiral sphere results in extinction of the incident signal. This extinction is attributable to both scattering by the sphere and absorption by the chiral materials within the sphere.

In terms of each spectral component of frequency
\nu_{p}
belonging to the incident signal, the extinction power efficiency is given by
[
13
,
14
]

{Q}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\nu_{p})=-\frac{2}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}^{2}b^{2}}{\mathrm{Re}}\left\{\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(2n+1)(c_{np}+e_{np})\right]-4\frac{{\mathrm{Im}}\left(a_{s}a_{p}^{*}\right)}{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}}\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left[(2n+1)d_{np}\right]\right\}

(23)

and the total power scattering efficiency by

\displaystyle\hskip-19.91692pt{Q}_{\mathrm{sca}}(\nu_{p})=\frac{2}{k_{{\scriptscriptstyle 0}p}^{2}b^{2}}\Bigg(\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}(2n+1)\left[{|c_{np}|^{2}+|e_{np}|^{2}+2|d_{np}|^{2}}\right]

\displaystyle\hskip-14.22636pt-4\frac{\mathrm{Im}(a_{s}a_{p}^{*})}{|{{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}}|^{2}}\mathrm{Re}\left\{\displaystyle\sum^{\infty}_{n=1}\left[(2n+1)(c_{np}+e_{np})d_{np}^{*}\right]\right\}\Bigg)\,.

(24)

The power absorption efficiency can then be obtained as
{Q}_{\mathrm{abs}}(\nu_{p})={Q}_{\mathrm{ext}}(\nu_{p})-{Q}_{\mathrm{sca}}(\nu_{p})
.

Whereas
{Q}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}(\nu_{p})
formulated in terms of the time-averaged powers of the incident and the scattered waves are appropriate for the scattering of time-harmonic fields
[
4
,
21
]
, analogous quantities for time-dependent fields require temporal integration. Thus, the energy extinction efficiency, total energy scattering efficiency, and the energy absorption efficiency are given as
[
22
]

{\tilde{Q}}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}=\frac{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}t_{\mathrm{s}}}{2U_{\mathrm{inc}}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\frac{1}{N}\displaystyle\sum_{{p=0}}^{{N-1}}\left[{Q}_{\mathrm{ext,sca,abs}}(\nu_{p})|\bar{f}_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}(\nu_{p})|^{2}\right]\,,

(25)

where

U_{\mathrm{inc}}=\frac{1}{{2}\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}\displaystyle\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left|\tilde{\bf{E}}_{\mathrm{inc}}({\bf r},t)\right|^{2}\mathrm{d}t={\frac{|{\bf{\mathfrak{p}}}|^{2}\sigma\sqrt{\pi}}{2\eta_{\scriptscriptstyle 0}}}

(26)

is the energy density of the incident signal.

4
Closing remarks

We formulated time-domain scattering by a coated sphere composed of homogeneous isotropic chiral materials. A Gaussian pulse modulating the amplitude of a carrier plane wave of arbitrary polarization state was adopted as the incident signal. The incident signal was discrete-Fourier-transformed into a finite ensemble of plane waves with different frequencies. For each plane-wave component,
closed-form expressions for the scattered-field coefficients were related to the incident-field
coefficients. While the coated sphere was composed of a spherical core with a concentric shell, the formulation may be straightforwardly extended to a concentrically multilayered sphere.

The signal scattered in any direction was obtained by an inverse discrete Fourier transform. The standard extinction, total scattering, and absorption efficiencies for each plane-wave component were used to determine the energy extinction
efficiency, total energy scattering efficiency, and the energy absorption efficiency of the coated sphere.

A real isotropic chiral material, with experimentally determined constitutive parameters
[
20
]
,
was used in numerical studies of a coated sphere with a vacuous core to illustrate the theory.
Variations in the scattered signal, depending upon the relative thickness of the shell, were studied.
The energy efficiencies were found to be sensitive to the polarization state of the carrier plane wave when the shell is thin.
The reliability of our results has been confirmed by comparison with results for pulse scattering by an achiral homogeneous sphere (i.e., a sphere for which the relative permittivities

\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{2}
,
the relative permeabilities

\mu_{1}=\mu_{2}
, and
the chirality parameters
\kappa_{1}=\kappa_{2}=0
), as considered in Ref.
[
19
]
.
Detailed analyses of diverse numerical results will be reported in due course of time.

In closing, we emphasize that no analysis of time-domain scattering by an isotropic chiral sphere has been presented hitherto. Several
frequency-domain analyses
involving scattering of continuous-wave beams by isotropic chiral spheres have been undertaken but these analyses are not directly relevant to the present study and their results are not comparable to those presented here.
