In

Conjecture 1.1

If
(R,\mathfrak{m})

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
the Bass numbers

\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)):=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(\mathfrak{p})}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(R)_{\mathfrak{p}}))

In the case where

[

[

Note that if

[

Also, Bahmanpour et al. in

of

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that
R
is regular,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
and set
d:=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=\mu^{d+c}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

(2)

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{d+c-1}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
. The equality holds if
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
.

(3)

If
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
, then
\mu^{d-2}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{d+c-2}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

The above theorem yields the following result concerning the injective dimension of local cohomology modules.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
R
be a regular ring and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)

(1)

If

\operatorname{id}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))<\operatorname{dim}(R)

(2)

If

\operatorname{id}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))<\operatorname{dim}(R)-1

(3)

If

\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, then
\operatorname{id}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))<\operatorname{dim}(R)-2
.

Moreover, if
\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)

(3)

The following version of the Grothendieck spectral sequences will be used in the paper.

Lemma 2.1
.

(
[
17
, Theorem 10. 47]
)
Let
\mathcal{A}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{F}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{B}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\mathcal{G}}}{{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{C}

be covariant additive functors, where
\mathcal{A}
,
\mathcal{B}
, and
\mathcal{C}
are abelian categories with enough injectives. Assume that
\mathcal{F}
is left exact and that
\mathcal{G}(E)
is right
\mathcal{F}
-acyclic for every

in
\mathcal{A}
. Then, for every object
A
in
\mathcal{A}
, there is a third quadrant spectral sequences with

E_{2}^{i,j}=R^{i}\mathcal{F}(R^{j}\mathcal{G}(A))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}R^{i+j}\mathcal{F}\mathcal{G}(A).

The following convergence of spectral sequences plays a crucial role in the paper.

Lemma 2.2
.

Assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is local and
N
is an
R
-module. Then there exists the following convergence of spectral sequences.

(2.1)

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}^{i+j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},N).

Proof.

Let
E
be an injective
R
-module. Then, by
[
5
, Proposition 2. 1. 4]
,
\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(E)
is an injective
R
-module, too.
Also, we have the following isomorphisms of
R
-modules

(2.2)

\displaystyle\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(N))

\displaystyle\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},\underset{\overset{\longrightarrow}{t\in\mathbb{N}}}{\lim}\,\,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{a}^{t}},N)

(by
[
5

)

\displaystyle\cong\underset{\overset{\longrightarrow}{t\in\mathbb{N}}}{\lim}\,\,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{a}^{t}},N))

(by (
[
19
, 24. 10]
)

\displaystyle\cong\underset{\overset{\longrightarrow}{t\in\mathbb{N}}}{\lim}\,\,\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}}\otimes_{R}\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{a}^{t}},N)

\displaystyle=\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},N).

The above isomorphisms are functorial, i.e.

(2.3)

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},\Gamma_{\mathfrak{a}}(-))\cong\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},-):\mathfrak{C}_{R}\rightarrow\mathfrak{C}_{R},

In the following theorem, we consider Bass numbers
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
where
g
is close to the
\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, the length of a maximal
M
-regular sequence contained in
\mathfrak{a}
.

Recall that if
X
is an
R
-module and
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)
, then for any
i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, the
i
-th Bass number of
X
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
is defined by

(2.4)

\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p},X):=\operatorname{dim}_{\kappa(p)}(\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(\kappa(\mathfrak{p}),X_{\mathfrak{p}})).

The really important property of the

i
-th Bass number of
X
with respect to
\mathfrak{p}
is that it gives, when the (uniquely determined)
i
-th term in the minimal injective resolution of
X
is expressed as a direct sum of indecomposable injective
R
-modules, the number of those direct summands which
are isomorphic to
E_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}})
, the injective hull of the
R
-module
\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{p}}
.

Here,
\operatorname{Var}(\mathfrak{a})
is used to denote the set
\{\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Spec}(R)\mid\mathfrak{a}\subseteq\mathfrak{p}\}
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Var}(\mathfrak{a})
and
g\leq\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=\mu^{g}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

(2)

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{g+1}(\mathfrak{p},M)
and if
\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
, then the equality holds.

(3)

If
\mu^{2}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
, then
\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{g+1}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

(4)

If
\mu^{3}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
, then
\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{g+2}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

Proof.

First of all note that,
\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)\leq\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})
and since
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Var}(\mathfrak{a})
, by the flat base change theorem (
[
5
, Theorem 4. 3. 2]
), after replacing
R
with
R_{\mathfrak{p}}
we may assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is local and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
. Now, consider the convergence of spectral sequences
2.1
,

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}^{i+j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M).

(1)

Since
g\leq\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, by
[
5
, Theorem 6. 2. 7]
,

(2.5)

0=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{i,j}=E_{\infty}^{i,j}\,\,\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{and all}\,\,j<g.

Also, there exists a filtration

0\subseteq\Phi_{g}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{1}\subseteq\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),

of submodules of
\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
such that

E_{\infty}^{i,j}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{i}}{\Phi_{i+1}},\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{with}\,\,i+j=g.

Now,
2.5
implies that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{0,g}=E_{\infty}^{0,g}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{g}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M).

(2)

Using
2.5
we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{1,g}=E_{\infty}^{1,g}.

Furthermore, there exists a filtration

(2.6)

0\subseteq\Phi_{g+1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{2}\subseteq\Phi_{1}\subseteq\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),

of submodules of
\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
such that

E_{\infty}^{i,j}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{i}}{\Phi_{i+1}},\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{with}\,\,i+j=g+1.

2.5
implies that the filtration
2.6
is actually of the form

0=\Phi_{g+1}=\cdots=\Phi_{2}\subseteq\Phi_{1}\subseteq\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M).

Therefore,

(2.7)

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\Phi_{1},

is a submodule of
\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
. If
\mu^{0}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
then

0=E_{\infty}^{0,g+1}=\tfrac{\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)}{\Phi_{1}}.

Hence, using
2.7
we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{g+1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),

and the result follows.

(3)

Assume that
\mu^{2}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
. Then
E_{2}^{2,g}=0
and by
2.5
we deduce that

\operatorname{Hom}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{0,g+1}=E_{\infty}^{0,g+1}.

Using the concept of convergence of spectral sequences, as used in parts (1) and (2), the latter module is a subquotient of
\operatorname{Ext}^{g+2}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
. This proves the claim.

(4)

Let
\mu^{3}(\mathfrak{p},H^{g}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
. Then
E_{2}^{3,g}=0
and by
2.5
we have

\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{1,g+1}=E_{\infty}^{1,g+1}.

Therefore, using similar argument as in the above parts,
\operatorname{Ext}^{1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
is a subquotient of
\operatorname{Ext}^{g+2}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
. In other words,

\mu^{1}(\mathfrak{m},H^{g+1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{g+2}(\mathfrak{m},M)<\infty.

∎

Recall that an
R
-module
X
is said to have ﬁnite Goldie dimension
if
X
does not contain an inﬁnite direct sum of non-zero submodules, or equivalently the
injective hull
E_{R}(X)
of
X
decompose as a ﬁnite direct sum of indecomposable (injective)
submodules.

Note that by
[
4
, Theorem 2. 2]
,
H^{\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
has finitely many associated prime ideals. The above theorem now yields the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

H^{\operatorname{grade}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
has ﬁnite Goldie dimension.

The cohomological dimension
of an
R
-module
X
with respect
\mathfrak{a}
is defined by

(2.8)

\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X):=\sup\{i\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\arrowvert H^{i}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)\neq 0\}.

Note that, in view of
[
5
, Corollary 3. 3. 3]
,
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(X)<\infty.

The cohomological dimension, the last non-vanishing level of local cohomology, is a significant invariant in this theory and has attracted considerable attention; see, for example,
[
7
,
14
]
and
[
16
]
.

In the following theorem, we study the Bass numbers
\mu^{i}(\mathfrak{p},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
in the case where
R
is regular and
j\in\{\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M),\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)-1\}
.

Theorem 2.5
.

Assume that
R
is regular,
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
and set
d:=\operatorname{ht}(\mathfrak{p})
and
c:=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
. Then the following statements hold.

(1)

\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=\mu^{d+c}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

(2)

\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{d+c-1}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
. The equality holds if
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
.

(3)

If
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
then
\mu^{d-2}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{d+c-2}(\mathfrak{p},M)<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\mathfrak{p}\in\operatorname{Supp}_{R}(H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))
. Then, by the Flat Base Change Theorem (
[
5
, Theorem 4. 3. 2]
),
\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}R_{\mathfrak{p}}}(M_{\mathfrak{p}})=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)=c
. Therefore, we may assume that
(R,\mathfrak{m})
is a regular local ring of dimension
d
and
\mathfrak{p}=\mathfrak{m}
.

(1)

Consider the following convergence of spectral sequences (
2.1
)

E_{2}^{i,j}=\operatorname{Ext}^{i}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{j}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\underset{i}{\Rightarrow}\operatorname{Ext}^{i+j}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M).

Since
R
is regular of dimension
d
and
c=\operatorname{cd}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M)
, we have

(2.9)

E_{\infty}^{i,j}=E_{2}^{i,j}=0\,\,\text{ for all}\,\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{with}\,\,i>d\,\,\text{ or}\,\,j>c.

Therefore, one can deduce that

\operatorname{Ext}^{d}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{d,c}=E_{\infty}^{d,c}\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),

this proves part (1).

(2)

By
2.9
,

(2.10)

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=E_{2}^{d-1,c}=E_{\infty}^{d-1,c}.

Also, there exists a filtration

(2.11)

0\subseteq\Phi_{d+c-1}\subseteq\cdots\subseteq\Phi_{2}\subseteq\Phi_{1}\subseteq\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),

of submodules of
\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
such that

E_{\infty}^{i,j}\cong\tfrac{\Phi_{i}}{\Phi_{i+1}},\,\,\text{for all}\,\,i,j\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\,\,\text{with}\,\,i+j=d+c-1.

Using
2.9
, the above filtration is actually of the form

(2.12)

0=\Phi_{d+c-1}=\cdots=\Phi_{d+1}\subseteq\Phi_{d}\subseteq\Phi_{d-1}=\cdots=\Phi_{0}=\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M).

Therefore, by
2.10
,

\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\tfrac{\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)}{\phi_{d}}

is a subquotient of

\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M),
hence
\mu^{d-1}(\mathfrak{m},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\leq\mu^{d+c-1}(\mathfrak{m},M)
.
If
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
then
E_{\infty}^{d,c-1}=E_{2}^{d,c-1}=0
and in the filtration

2.12
,
0=\Phi_{d+1}=\Phi_{d}
. Hence,
\operatorname{Ext}^{d-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},H^{c}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))\cong\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-1}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
and the result follows.

(3)

If
\mu^{d}(\mathfrak{p},H^{c-1}_{\mathfrak{a}}(M))=0
then
E_{2}^{d,c-1}=0
. Therefore,

E_{2}^{d-2,c}=E_{3}^{d-2,c}=E_{\infty}^{d-2,c}.

Again, using the concept of convergence of spectral sequences,
E_{\infty}^{d-2,c}
is a subquotient of
\operatorname{Ext}^{d+c-2}_{R}(\tfrac{R}{\mathfrak{m}},M)
and this proves the claim.

∎

(1)

(2)
