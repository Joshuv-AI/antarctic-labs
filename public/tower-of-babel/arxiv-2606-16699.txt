Quantitative Oppenheim Conjecture for Random Quadratic Forms and Optimal Variance Bounds in Function Fields
Jiyoung Han, Noy Soffer Aranov

Abstract
. We prove a quantitative version of Oppenheim’s conjecture in the function field setting. In order to do so, we compute the higher moments of the Siegel transform. In particular, we find an optimal bound on the variance of the number of lattice points in a set. Moreover, we compute the exact variance of the number of lattice points in a ball, which is of independent interest.

Primary: 11E08, 11H06, 11K38. Secondary: 11J61

1.
Introduction

Let
Q
be a non-degenerate indefinite irrational quadratic form in
n\geq 3
variables. Oppenheim’s conjecture
[
23
]
states that every irrational non-degenerate indefinite quadratic form
Q
has a dense image, i.e.,
\overline{Q(\mathbb{Z}^{n})}=\mathbb{R}
. Margulis
[
21
]
proved that Oppenheim’s conjecture holds by using homogeneous dynamics. In her seminal paper, Ratner
[
25
]
proved the orbit closure theorem for unipotent flows, which implies Oppenheim’s conjecture.

Quantitative versions of Oppenheim’s conjecture, which handle the rate at which the image of
Q
becomes dense, were also established.
This direction was originally initiated by Eskin, Margulis, and Mozes
[
10
]
, who established that for every non-degenerate, indefinite, irrational quadratic form
Q
of signature
(p,q)
for
p\geq 3
and
q\geq 1
, for every fixed interval
I
, and for every large enough
t
, one has

\#\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{v})\in I,\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq t\right\}\sim\lambda_{Q}|I|t^{n-2},

where
\lambda_{Q}
is a constant depending only on
Q
. A few years later, the same authors
[
11
]
showed that the same asymptotic formula holds for split quadratic forms, and recently, Wooyeon Kim
[
17
]
proved that such an asymptotic holds for the 3-dimensional case. In both cases, the quadratic forms have to satisfy some Diophantine condition.

The error term in the above formula was later bounded by Athreya and Margulis
[
3
]
and by Kelmer and Yu
[
15
]
for almost every quadratic form.
Recently, Kelmer and Yu
[
16
]
improved the aforementioned error term for generic quadratic forms of signature
(n-1,1)
. These results are deduced from
moment formulae for Siegel transforms
about the various set-ups. For further historical remarks about Siegel transforms and their moments and application to Oppenheim conjecture-typed problems, see
[
16
]
.

The Oppenheim conjecture has been studied over different fields as well. For example, Borel and Prasad
[
6
]
proved that the Oppenheim conjecture holds over
S
-arithmetic fields whose ground field has characteristic zero. Furthermore, quantitative versions of Oppenheim’s conjecture have been established for isotropic forms, that is, forms
Q
for which there exists some non-zero vector
\mathbf{v}
, such that
Q(\mathbf{v})=0
, in the
S
-arithmetic setting by the first named author, Lim, and Mallahi-Karai
[
12
]
and for generic quadratic forms of rank
3
and
4
by the first named author
[
13
]
.

Mohammadi
[
22
]
proved that Oppenheim’s conjecture holds over local fields of positive characteristic. We note that since Ratner’s orbit closure theorem
[
25
]
has not been established in the function field setting, Mohammadi’s proof closely follows the topological ideas in Margulis’ proof of the real Oppenheim conjecture
[
21
]
, which do not lead to quantitative results. Despite being well studied over fields with zero characteristic, quantitative versions of Oppenheim’s conjecture have not been studied over local fields of positive characteristic. In this paper, we fill this gap by proving a quantitative version of the result of Mohammadi in a generic sense. To prove our main theorem, we establish a function field analogue of Rogers’ higher moment formula of the Siegel transform
[
26
]
. First, we briefly introduce the function field setting.

1.1.
Function Fields

Let
q
be the power of an odd prime, let
\mathcal{R}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[x]
, and let
\mathcal{K}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(x)
be the field of fractions of
\mathcal{R}
. Equip
\mathcal{K}
with an absolute value
\left|\frac{f}{g}\right|=q^{\mathrm{deg}(f)-\mathrm{deg}(g)}
, so that the topological completion of
\mathcal{K}
with respect to
|\cdot|
is
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}(\!(x^{-1})\!)
. It is well known that every local field of positive characteristic is isomorphic to some field of the form
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
as above, and therefore, this paper will only handle these types of fields.

The maximal compact order in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
is
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[[x^{-1}]]
and its maximal ideal is
\mathfrak{m}=x^{-1}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
. In this paper,
m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}
denotes the translation-invariant Haar measure on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
satisfying
m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}\left(\mathcal{O}_{\infty}\right)=1
. Let
\operatorname{Vol}
denote the
n
-dimensional product measure
m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}^{n}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
.

For
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, we define the norm of
\mathbf{v}
by
\|\mathbf{v}\|=\max_{i=1,\dots,n}|v_{i}|
. A metric ball in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
is a set of the form
B(\alpha,r):=\alpha+x^{r}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}
, where
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}
. The norm
\|\cdot\|
satisfies the ultrametric inequality: For
\mathbf{u},\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, we have

(1)

\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|\leq\max\{\|\mathbf{u}\|,\|\mathbf{v}\|\}
;

(2)

If
\|\mathbf{u}\|\neq\|\mathbf{v}\|
, then
\|\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{v}\|=\max\{\|\mathbf{u}\|,\|\mathbf{v}\|\}
.

The ultrametric inequality, in particular (
2
), often enables us to compute norms in a highly accurate way. Moreover, in the ultrametric setting, a pair of balls are either disjoint or one is contained in the other. This property enables very accurate lattice point counting bounds (see Proposition
1.7
).

1.2.
Quadratic forms and the Space of Lattices

We say that two non-degenerate quadratic forms
Q
,
Q^{\prime}
on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
are
equivalent
if there exists
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
so that

Q^{\prime}(\mathbf{v})=Q(g\mathbf{v}),\quad\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}.

It is known that two non-degenerate quadratic forms over a function field are equivalent if they have the same rank, discriminant and Hasse-Witt invariant (see
[
9
, Remark 5.12]
or
[
19
, V.3.17]
for example). Moreover

\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})=\bigsqcup_{g\in S}\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{\times}g\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}),

where
S=\left\{\operatorname{diag}\{x^{t_{1}},x^{t_{2}},\cdots,x^{t_{n}}\}:t_{i}\geq 0,0\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}t_{i}\leq n-1\right\}
and

\mathrm{G}:=\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})=\left\{g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}):|\det g|=1\right\}.

Thus, up to scalar multiplication and multiplication by a matrix from the set
S
, the space of non-degenerate quadratic forms is a finite disjoint union of
\mathrm{G}
-orbits, where each of them is isomorphic to
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q_{0})\setminus\mathrm{G}
for some quadratic form
Q_{0}
and

{\mathrm{SO}}(Q_{0})=\{g\in\mathrm{G}:Q_{0}(g\mathbf{v})=Q_{0}(\mathbf{v}),\;\forall\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\}.

On the other hand, the density
Q(\mathcal{R}^{d})
of a quadratic form at integral vectors is invariant under the action of
\Gamma:=\mathrm{SL}_{d}(\mathcal{R})
, i.e., if
Q^{\prime}=Q\circ\gamma
for some
\gamma\in\Gamma
, then
Q(\mathcal{R}^{d})
is dense if and only if
Q^{\prime}(\mathcal{R}^{d})
is dense.

Thus, this density is a property which can be defined on the bi-quotient space
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q_{0})\setminus\mathrm{G}/\Gamma
, where
Q_{0}
is a fixed quadratic form. Equivalently, this property can be regarded as an
{\mathrm{SO}}(Q_{0})
-invariant property on
\mathcal{L}_{n}:=\mathrm{G}/\Gamma
, the space of of unimodular
\mathcal{R}
-lattices, where
g\Gamma
corresponds to
g\mathcal{R}^{n}
. In this philosophy, we associate the quadratic form
Q=Q_{0}\circ g
, where
g\in\mathrm{G}
, with the lattice
g\mathcal{R}^{n}
.
The advantage of considering
\mathcal{L}_{n}
is that this homogeneous space supports a natural
\mathrm{G}
-invariant probability measure, denoted by
m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
.

1.3.
Main Results

Recall that any isotropic quadratic form
Q
is equivalent to

Q_{0}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})=2x_{1}x_{n}+Q^{\prime}(x_{2},\ldots,x_{n-1}),

where
Q^{\prime}
is some non-degenerate (on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n-2}
) quadratic form with
n-2
variables with coefficients in
\mathcal{R}
.
Our first main result is a quantification of the result of
[
22
]
for generic non-degenerate isotropic quadratic forms.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
I\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
be a bounded measurable subset. For every
\frac{1}{2}<\delta<1
and for almost every non-degenerate isotropic quadratic form
Q
, there exists
C_{Q}>0
depending only on
Q
, such that

(1.1)

\#\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{v})\in I,\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq q^{t}\right\}=C_{Q}q^{(n-2)t}m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(I)+O_{Q,I}\left(q^{\delta t(n-2)}\right).

To prove Theorem
1.1
, we compute the volume of the set
\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{v})\in I,\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq q^{t}\}
, establish a formula for the higher moments of the Siegel transform, and then apply this formula to obtain (
1.1
).

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
n\geq 3
.
Let
Q
be a non-degenerate isotropic quadratic form, and let
I\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
be a one-dimensional ball. There exists
J_{Q}>0
, depending only on
Q
, such that for every
t>t_{Q,I}
, we have

(1.2)

\operatorname{Vol}\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{v})\in I,\|\mathbf{v}\|=q^{t}\right\}=J_{Q}q^{(n-2)t}m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(I).

Moreover, there exists
t_{Q,I}>0
, such that for every
t>t_{Q,I}
, we have

\operatorname{Vol}\left\{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}:Q(\mathbf{v})\in I,\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq q^{t}\right\}=C_{Q}q^{(n-2)t}m_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}}(I)+O_{Q,I}(1),

where
C_{Q}>0
is defined as in Theorem
1.1
. Furthermore, we have
C_{Q}=J_{Q}\sum_{m=0}^{\infty}q^{-(n-2)m}
.

Remark 1.3
.

Unlike the real setting, where the volume estimates as in the left hand side of (
1.2
) are only asymptotic
[
15
,
12
,
13
]
, in the function field setting, the volume of such sets can be computed exactly, as in (
1.2
).

To deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.2
, we define the function field analogue of the Siegel transform, which enables lattice point counting claims. Towards this end, we say that a function
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is
smooth
if there exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that
f
is constant on every ball of radius
\varepsilon
.

Definition 1.4

(Siegel Transform)
.

For
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
smooth with compact support, define the
Siegel transform of
f
,
\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{L}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
, by

\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda\setminus\{0\}}f(\mathbf{v}).

For example, if
f
is the indicator function of a set
B\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, then,
\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)=\#B\cap(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})
.

We first establish a function field analogue of Siegel’s Lemma
[
29
]
.

Theorem 1.5

(Function Field Analogue of Siegel’s Lemma)
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a smooth function. Then

(1.3)

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}=q^{n}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}}fd\operatorname{Vol}.

For a fixed set
B
, define a random variable
X_{B}=\#B\cap(\Lambda\setminus\{0\})
, where
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
. Theorem
1.5
states from a probabilistic point of view that
\mathbb{E}[X_{B}]=q^{n}\operatorname{Vol}(B)
, and

\operatorname{Var}(X_{B})=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{\mathbf{1}}_{B}^{2}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}-q^{2n}\operatorname{Vol}(B)^{2}.

To deduce Theorem
1.1
from Theorem
1.2
, we need the following analogue of Rogers’ second moment formula
[
26
]
pertaining to the variance of
X_{B}
.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
n\geq 3
and let
q
be a prime power.
For every measurable set
B\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, we have

\operatorname{Var}(X_{B})\leq C_{n}\operatorname{Vol}(B),

where

(1.4)

C_{n}=q^{n}(2q-1)(1-q^{-1})\frac{q^{n-2}}{q^{n-2}-1}.

We note that the upper bound
C_{n}
is optimal, by considering the case where
B
is a ball
B(\alpha,r)
with
\|\alpha\|\leq r
. More precisely, one can compute the exact value of
\operatorname{Var}(X_{B})
when
B
is a ball.

Proposition 1.7
.

Let
\alpha\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
and let
r>0
. Then, we have

\operatorname{Var}(X_{B(\alpha,r)})=\frac{q^{2n-2}}{q^{n-2}-1}(1-q^{-1})r^{n}\cdot\begin{cases}2q-1&r\geq\|\alpha\|\\
\left(\frac{r}{\|\alpha\|}\right)^{n-1}&r<\|\alpha\|\end{cases}.

In the real setting, it is easy to compute

\operatorname{Var}\left(X_{B(0,r)}\right)=\left(4\frac{\zeta(d-1)}{\zeta(d)}-2\right)\mathrm{Vol}(B(0,r))

for balls centered at the origin (see for instance the computation in the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 2.2]
). However, it seems difficult to find the exact variance for balls not centered at the origin.

An important component of the proof of Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
is proving that the Margulis-
\alpha
function has bounded moments. This can be viewed as a function field analogue of
[
10
, Lemma 3.10]
.

Definition 1.8

(Margulis
\alpha
-function)
.

Let
0\leq k\leq n
. For a
k
-sublattice
\Delta
of
\Lambda
, define
L(\Delta)=\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{k}\|
, where
\Delta=\mathcal{R}\mathbf{v}_{1}+\dots+\mathcal{R}\mathbf{v}_{k}
, and
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{K}^{n}_{\infty}
are linearly independent. Note that
L(\Delta)
represents the
q^{k}
-multiple of the covolume of
\Delta\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}.\Delta
. Define

\alpha_{k}(\Lambda)=\max\left\{\frac{1}{L(\Delta)}:\Delta\text{ is a }k\text{-sublattice of }\Lambda\right\}.

Define the
Margulis
\alpha
-function
by

\alpha(\Lambda)=\max_{k=0,\dots,n}\alpha_{k}(\Lambda).

Theorem 1.9
.

For every
1\leq m<n
, the
m
-th moment of
\alpha
is finite, i.e.,

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\alpha^{m}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}<\infty.

Remark 1.10
.

Theorem
1.5
, Theorem
1.6
and Lemma
1.7
hold over all positive characteristics, whereas we assume that
q
is the power of an odd prime in Theorem
1.1
.
When the ground field has characteristic
2
, a quadratic form does not correspond to a bilinear form, since
2
is not invertible, which makes Theorem
4.3
fail in this case; as does Theorem
1.1
.

2.
Bounded Moments of the Margulis-
\alpha
Function and Lattice Point Counting

In this section, we provide results about lattice point counting and geometry of numbers in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, which are necessary to prove Theorem
1.9
. Moreover, these results enable us to deduce Theorems
1.5
and
1.6
from Theorem
1.9
. For more extensive surveys about geometry of numbers in the function field setting, see
[
20
,
7
,
30
,
28
,
18
,
1
]
. We first define some groups in
\mathrm{G}
and their corresponding Haar measures.

Let
A<\mathrm{G}
be the group of diagonal matrices and let
N<\mathrm{G}
be the group of upper triangular matrices with
1
’s on the diagonal. These groups
N
,
A
, and
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
support Haar measures which are denoted by
m_{N}
,
m_{A}
, and
m_{\mathcal{O}_{\infty}}
, respectively. Let
A(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
be the group of diagonal matrices whose diagonal elements belong to
\mathcal{O}_{\infty}
. It follows that
A
is topologically isomorphic to
A(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})\times\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}
, where

\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}=\left\{\mathbf{k}=(k_{1},\dots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}:\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}=0\right\},

through the map
(\mathbf{o},\mathbf{k})\mapsto\operatorname{diag}\{x^{k_{1}}o_{1},\dots,x^{k_{n}}o_{n}\}=:\mathbf{o}\cdot x^{\mathbf{k}}
.
If we let the measure
m_{A}
satisfy
m_{A}(A(\mathcal{O}_{\infty}))=1
, then it decomposes as follows
[
8
]
:

(2.1)

dm_{A}(\mathbf{a})=\prod_{i=1}^{n-1}\frac{da_{i}}{|a_{i}|^{2(i-n)}}=q^{-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}2(i-n)k_{i}}dk_{1}\cdots dk_{n-1}do_{1}\cdots do_{n-1}.

In
\mathrm{G}
, we have the Iwasawa decomposition
[
7
,
8
,
1
]
, which states that
G=\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})AN
. The decomposition of the invariant measure in
\mathcal{L}_{n}
with respect to the Iwasawa decomposition is
dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(o\mathbf{a}u\mathcal{R}^{n})=dm_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})}(o)dm_{A}(\mathbf{a})dm_{N}(u)

[
8
,
24
,
7
]
. Since
A\cong A(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})\times\mathbb{Z}_{0}^{n}
, by the Iwasawa decomposition, we may assume that
\mathbf{a}=\operatorname{diag}\left\{x^{k_{1}},\dots,x^{k_{n}}\right\}
. Thus, by (
2.1
), we have

(2.2)

dm_{A}(\mathbf{a})=q^{-2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(i-n)k_{i}}dk_{1}\cdots dk_{n-1}.

If
\Lambda=g\mathcal{R}^{n}
, a convenient fundamental domain of
\Lambda
is
g\mathfrak{m}^{n}
and thus
\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)=q^{-n}|\det(g)|
. For the sake of simplicity, let us denote

\det(\Lambda)=|\det(g)|.

Mahler
[
20
, Equations (24) and (25)]
proved the following version of Minkowski’s 2nd theorem.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\Lambda=g\mathcal{R}^{n}\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
be a lattice, where
g\in\operatorname{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
. For
i=1,\dots,n
, define the
i
-th successive minima of
\Lambda
by

\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)=\min\{r\geq 0:\operatorname{rk}\left(\Lambda\cap B(0,r)\right)\geq i\}.

Then we have

(2.3)

\prod_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)=\det(\Lambda).

Due to Theorem
2.1
, the Margulis-
\alpha
function has the following form:

(2.4)

\alpha(\Lambda)=\prod_{i=1}^{k}\frac{1}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}.

Moreover, using the successive minima
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda),\dots,\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)
, the second named author
[
1
]
used Theorem
2.1
to refine the Iwasawa decomposition for
\mathcal{L}_{n}
.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
1
, Theorem 1.24]

Let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
. Then there exists
o\in\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, such that
\Lambda=o\operatorname{diag}\left\{x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)},\dots,x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)}\right\}\mathcal{R}^{n}
.

Another application of Theorem
2.1
is counting lattice points in a convex body. Recall that Mahler proved that all convex bodies in this setting are of the form
h\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}
, where
h\in\mathrm{GL}_{d}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})

[
20
]
. We utilize the following lattice point counting claim from
[
5
]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

[
5
, Lemma 6.2]

For every lattice
\Lambda
and for every convex body
\mathcal{C}=h\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}
, we have

\#\Lambda\cap\mathcal{C}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q}{\lambda_{i}(h^{-1}\Lambda)}\right\rceil,

where
\lceil t\rceil=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}:n\geq t\}
. In particular, if
\lambda_{n}(h^{-1}\Lambda)<q
, we have

\#\Lambda\cap\mathcal{C}=\frac{q^{n}\operatorname{Vol}(\mathcal{C})}{\operatorname{covol}(\Lambda)}.

We need the following generalization of Lemma
2.3
for translates of convex bodies. A convex body
\mathcal{C}
induces a norm on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
defined by

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}=\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\mathbf{v}\in x^{\log_{q}(r)}\mathcal{C}\right\}.

Define
B_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{v},q^{r})=\left\{\mathbf{u}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}:\|\mathbf{u}-\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}\leq q^{r}\right\}.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
and let
\mathcal{C}=h\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}
be a convex body. Let
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
. There exists
t_{0}
, depending on
\Lambda
,
\mathbf{v}
, and
\mathcal{C}
, such that for every
t\geq t_{0}
, we have

\#\Lambda\cap B_{\mathcal{C}}(\mathbf{v},q^{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(h^{-1}\Lambda)}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Note that it suffices to prove this for
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}
, since

(2.5)

\|\mathbf{v}\|_{\mathcal{C}}=\inf\left\{r\geq 0:\mathbf{v}\in x^{\log_{q}r}h\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}\right\}=\inf\left\{r\geq 0:h^{-1}\mathbf{v}\in x^{\log_{q}r}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}\right\}=\|h^{-1}\mathbf{v}\|.

By
[
1
, Theorem 1.21]
, there exists an
\mathcal{R}
-basis
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}
for
\Lambda
such that

\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

where
\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
is the
i
-th successive minima. Moreover, by combining Theorem
2.1
and
[
1
, Theorem 1.21]
, we obtain that
\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\}
is an orthogonal
\mathcal{R}
-basis of
\Lambda
. Denote by
\mathbf{v}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}
(
c_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\infty}
). It follows that

\begin{split}\mathbf{u}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\Lambda\cap B(\mathbf{v},q^{t})\quad&\Leftrightarrow\quad\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(a_{i}-c_{i})\mathbf{v}_{i}\right\|\leq q^{t}\\
&\Leftrightarrow\quad\max_{i=1,\ldots,n}|a_{i}-c_{i}|\cdot\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)\leq q^{t}.\end{split}

As a consequence,
|a_{i}-c_{i}|\leq\frac{q^{t}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}
for every
i
. If
t
is chosen so that
q^{t}>|c_{i}|\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
for every
i
, we have
\mathbf{u}\in B(\mathbf{v},q^{t})
if and only if
|a_{i}|\leq\frac{q^{t}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}
for every
i
. In this case, we have

\#\Lambda\cap B(\mathbf{v},q^{t})=\#\left\{(a_{1},\dots,a_{n})\in\mathcal{R}^{n}:|a_{i}|\leq\frac{q^{t}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\}=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil.

∎

To prove Theorem
1.5
and Theorem
1.6
from Theorem
1.9
, we need a function field analogue of Schmidt’s lemma, which states that
\widetilde{f}\ll\alpha
.

To prove such an analogue, for an operation
*\in\{=,\geq,>,\leq,<\}
and
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, define

\mathcal{L}_{n}^{*t}=\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}:\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)*q^{t}\}.

By Mahler’s compactness criterion
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
,
\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\geq q^{t}}
are increasing compact sets, and
\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{t}}\right)=0
. Moreover,
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
states that a closed set
\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathcal{L}_{n}
is compact if and only if there exists
c>0
such that
\inf_{\Lambda\in\mathcal{C}}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\geq c
.

A function
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is called smooth if there exists
\varepsilon>0
, such that
f
is constant on every ball of radius
\varepsilon
. We denote the space of smooth functions
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
C^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})
and the space of smooth, compactly supported functions
f:\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
by
C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})
.

Lemma 2.5

(Schmidt’s Lemma in Function Fields)
.

For any
t\in\mathbb{N}
and for any lattice
\Lambda\subseteq\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
, we have

(1)

\#\Lambda\cap B(0,q^{t})=q^{n(t+1)}
, if
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq q^{t+1}}
;

(2)

\#\Lambda\cap B(0,q^{t})\leq q^{n(t+1)}\alpha(\Lambda)
, if
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{t+1}}
.

(3)

As a consequence, for any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})
there exists
C>0
depending on
f
, such that for every
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
, we have

\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)\leq C\alpha(\Lambda).

Proof.

If
\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)\leq q^{t+1}
, then,
\left\lceil\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil=\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}
for every
i=1,\dots,n
. Hence, by Lemma
2.3
,

(2.6)

\#\Lambda\cap B(0,q^{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}=\frac{q^{(t+1)n}}{\det(\Lambda)}=q^{(t+1)n}\leq q^{t(n+1)}\alpha(\Lambda).

On the other hand, if
\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)>q^{t+1}
, then there exists some
j=1,\dots,n-1
, such that
\lambda_{j}(\Lambda)\leq q^{t+1}<\lambda_{j+1}(\Lambda)
. Let
\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{n}\in\Lambda
be a linearly independent set of vectors satisfying
\|\mathbf{v}_{i}\|=\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)
. Then, by Theorem
2.1
and Lemma
2.3
,

(2.7)

\#\Lambda\cap B(0,q^{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{j}\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}=\frac{q^{(t+1)j}}{\|\mathbf{v}_{1}\wedge\dots\wedge\mathbf{v}_{j}\|}\leq q^{n(t+1)}\alpha(\Lambda).

For (
3
), it suffices to prove that
\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)\leq C\alpha(\Lambda)
for
f=\mathbf{1}_{B(\mathbf{u},q^{t})}
for
t\in\mathbb{Z}
, since any
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})
can be approximated by sums of finitely many indicator functions. Note that in this case, we have

(2.8)

\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)=\#\Lambda\cap B(\mathbf{u},q^{t})=\#\{\mathbf{v}\in\Lambda:\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|\leq q^{t}\}.

Note that if
\|\mathbf{u}\|\leq q^{t}
, then
\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|\leq q^{t}
if and only if
\|\mathbf{v}\|\leq q^{t}
. Therefore, in this case, by Lemma
2.3
, the right hand side of (
2.8
) is equal to

(2.9)

\#\Lambda\cap B(0,q^{t})=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q^{t+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil\leq q^{nt}\alpha(\Lambda).

If
\|\mathbf{u}\|>q^{t}
, then
\|\mathbf{v}-\mathbf{u}\|\leq q^{t}
implies that
\|\mathbf{v}\|=\|\mathbf{u}\|
. As a consequence, (
2.6
), (
2.7
), and (
2.8
) imply that

\widetilde{f}(\Lambda)\leq\#\Lambda\cap\left(B(0,\|\mathbf{u}\|)\setminus B(0,q^{-1}\|\mathbf{u}\|)\right)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q\|\mathbf{u}\|}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil-\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{\|\mathbf{u}\|}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil\leq q^{n}\|\mathbf{u}\|^{n}\alpha(\Lambda).

∎

To conclude this section, we prove Theorem
1.9
.

Proof of Theorem
1.9
.

Note that
\alpha
is
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
-invariant. Thus, by (
2.4
) and Theorem
2.2
, for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}
, we have

(2.10)

\alpha(\Lambda)=\alpha\left(\operatorname{diag}\{x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)},\dots,x^{\log_{q}\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)}\}\mathcal{R}^{n}\right)=\max_{j=0,\dots,n}\prod_{1\leq i\leq j}\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)^{-1}.

Let

A_{0}=\left\{a=\operatorname{diag}\{x^{k_{1}},\dots,x^{k_{n}}\}:k_{i}\leq k_{i+1},k_{i}\in\mathbb{Z},\,\,\,\forall i=1,\dots,n-1\right\}\subseteq A.

By Theorem
2.2
, the set
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})A_{0}
contains a fundamental domain for the right action of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{R})
on
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
.
Thus, it suffices to compute

(2.11)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\alpha_{j}^{m}\mathrm{d}m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}&=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})A_{0}}\alpha_{j}^{m}(g\mathcal{R}^{n})dm_{\mathrm{G}}(g)\\
&=\int_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})}\int_{A_{0}}\alpha_{j}^{m}(oa\mathcal{R}^{n})\mathrm{d}m_{\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})}(o)\mathrm{d}m_{A}(a)\\
&\leq C\int_{A_{0}}\alpha_{j}^{m}(a\mathcal{R}^{n})\mathrm{d}m_{A}(a),\end{split}

where the last inequality stems from compactness of
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\infty})
. Hence, by (
2.2
), it suffices to compute

(2.12)

\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{dk_{1}\dots d_{k_{n-1}}}{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{j}mk_{i}+2\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-i)k_{i}}}.

For
a=\operatorname{diag}\{x^{k_{1}},\dots,x^{k_{n}}\}\in A_{0}
, denote
\beta_{i}=k_{i+1}-k_{i}\geq 0
, so that
k_{i+1}=k_{1}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j}
. Then,
k_{1}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}k_{i+1}=-(n-1)k_{1}-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j}
, so that
k_{1}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\beta_{j}
, and for every
i=1,\dots,n-1
, we have

(2.13)

\begin{split}k_{i+1}=-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}(n-j)\beta_{j}+\sum_{j=1}^{i}\beta_{j}=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=1}^{i}j\beta_{j}-\frac{1}{n}\sum_{j=i+1}^{n-1}(n-j)\beta_{j}.\end{split}

Hence by (
2.13
), the exponent of the denominator in (
2.12
) is equal to

(2.14)

\begin{split}&\frac{1}{n}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{j}m\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\ell\beta_{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)\beta_{\ell}\right)+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}(n-i)\left(\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\ell\beta_{\ell}-\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)\beta_{\ell}\right)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{n}m\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\beta_{\ell}\left(n+\ell(j+2-n)\right)-\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)\beta_{\ell}\right]\\
&\hskip 86.72377pt+\frac{2}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\beta_{\ell}\left[\ell\sum_{i=\ell}^{n-1}(n-i)-(n-\ell)\sum_{i=1}^{\ell-1}(n-i)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{n}m\left[\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\beta_{\ell}\left(n+\ell(j+2-n)\right)-\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{n-1}(n-\ell)\beta_{\ell}\right]+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{n-1}\beta_{\ell}\left(3\ell^{2}+2(n-1)\ell+n^{2}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\beta_{\ell}\left(mn+m\ell(j+2-n)+3\ell^{2}+2(n-1)\ell+n^{2}\right)\\
&\hskip 86.72377pt+\frac{1}{n}\sum_{\ell=j+1}^{n-1}\beta_{\ell}\left(3\ell^{2}+(2n-2+m)\ell+n(n-m)\right).\end{split}

Note that since
m\leq n-1
, then
3\ell^{2}+(2n-2+m)\ell+n(n-m)\geq 3\ell^{2}+n\geq 1
for every
\ell=j+1,\dots,n-1
. Moreover, for
\ell=1,\dots,j
, the coefficient of
\beta_{\ell}
in the second to last line of (
2.14
) is equal to

(2.15)

\begin{split}&mn(1-\ell)+m\ell(j+2)+2(n-1)\ell+n^{2}+3\ell^{2}\\
&\hskip 36.135pt=m\ell(j-n)+mn+2m\ell+2(n-1)\ell+n^{2}+3\ell^{2}\\
&\hskip 36.135pt\geq(n-1)(2\ell-m)+n^{2}+3\ell^{2}+2m\ell+mn\\
&\hskip 36.135pt\geq(n-1)(2-m)+n^{2}+3\ell^{2}+2m\ell+mn\\
&\hskip 36.135pt\geq n\left(n-m+2\right)+3\ell^{2}+2m\ell+mn>0.\end{split}

As a consequence of (
2.14
) and (
2.15
), there exist
c_{1},\dots,c_{n-1}\geq 1
, such that (
2.12
) is equal to

(2.16)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\cdots\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{d\beta_{1}\dots d\beta_{n-1}}{q^{\sum_{i=1}^{n}c_{i}\beta_{i}}}<\infty.

Thus, for every
j=1,\dots,n-1
, and for every
m=1,\dots,n-1
, the integral
\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\alpha_{j}^{m}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
converges. Since
\alpha^{m}(\Lambda)=\max_{j=1,\dots,n-1}\alpha_{j}^{m}(\Lambda)
, the integral
\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\alpha^{m}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
converges for every
m=1,\dots,n-1
.
∎

3.
Siegel’s and Rogers’ Formulae in Function Fields

In this section, we prove the first moment (Theorem
1.5
) and higher moment (Theorem
3.3
) formulae. Moreover, we deduce the upper bound for the variance of the Siegel transform (Theorem
1.6
) from the first and second moment formulae.

3.1.
First Moment

We begin with the following decomposition on the
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
-invariant measures on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\nu
be an
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
-invariant measure on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
. Then, there exist positive scalars
c_{0},c_{1}\in[0,\infty)
such that
\nu=c_{0}\delta_{0}+c_{1}\operatorname{Vol}
, where
\delta_{0}
is the Dirac-delta mass at
0
.

Proof.

Assume that
H
acts on a locally compact homogeneous space
X
with finitely many orbits,
X_{1},\dots,X_{k}
, and
\nu
is an
H
-invariant measure on
X
. Then,
\nu
can be decomposed into a sum
\nu=\sum_{i=1}^{m}\nu_{i}
, where each
\nu_{i}
is of the form
\nu_{i}(E):=\nu(E\cap X_{i})
and is an
H
-invariant measure supported on
X_{i}
. In this case, the
\operatorname{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty})
-action on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}
has two orbits, which are
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
and
\{0\}
(see
[
30
]
for more details). An invariant measure supported on
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\setminus\{0\}
is measure theoretically isomorphic to a scalar multiple of the Lebesgue measure, and an invariant measure supported on
\{0\}
is a scalar multiple of the Dirac-delta mass at
0
. Hence, there exist
c_{0},c_{1}\geq 0
, such that
\nu=c_{0}\delta_{0}+c_{1}\operatorname{Vol}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

By Lemma
2.5
(
3
) and Theorem
1.9
, the function
\widetilde{f}:\mathcal{L}_{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is integrable. It follows from the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani representation theorem that the linear functional
f\mapsto\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}
corresponds to some
\mathrm{G}
-invariant Radon measure
\nu
, i.e.,

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}=\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}}fd\nu=c_{0}f(0)+c_{1}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}}fd\mathrm{Vol}

for some
c_{0},\;c_{1}\in[0,\infty)
, where the last equality follows from Lemma
3.1
.

To compute
c_{1}
, consider the sequence of test functions
f_{m}=\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\mathbf{1}_{x^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
. By Lemma
2.3
, we have

(3.1)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f}_{m}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}=\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\#\left(\Lambda\cap x^{m}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}\right)-1\right)\>dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(\Lambda).\end{split}

Note that if
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq{q^{m+1}}}
, then,
\lambda_{n}(\Lambda)\leq q^{m+1}
, so that

(3.2)

\widetilde{f_{m}}(\Lambda)=\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\left\lceil\frac{q^{m+1}}{\lambda_{i}(\Lambda)}\right\rceil-\frac{1}{q^{mn}}=\frac{q^{(m+1)n}}{q^{mn}\det(\Lambda)}-\frac{1}{q^{mn}}=q^{n}-\frac{1}{q^{mn}}.

Observe that for any
\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}
, where
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)<\frac{1}{q^{(m+1)/(n-1)}}\quad\text{and hence}\quad\alpha(\Lambda)>q^{(m+1)/(n-1)}.

Moreover, in the spirit of the proof of Lemma
2.5
, one can take a uniform constant
C>0
for which
\widetilde{f_{m}}\leq C\alpha
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
. It follows that

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}&=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq q^{m+1}}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}+\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\\
&=\left(q^{n}-\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\right)m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq q^{m+1}}\right)+\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}.\end{split}

Thus by Lemma
1.9
,

\begin{split}&\left|\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}-\left(q^{n}-\frac{1}{q^{mn}}\right)m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq q^{m+1}}\right)\right|\leq C\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}}\alpha\>dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\\
&\hskip 28.90755pt=C\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}}\alpha^{1.5}\cdot\alpha^{-0.5}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\leq\frac{C}{q^{(m+1)/2(n-1)}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}^{>q^{m+1}}}\alpha^{1.5}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\rightarrow 0\end{split}

as
m\rightarrow\infty
.
By Mahler’s compactness criterion
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
(see also
[
20
]
),
\lim_{m\rightarrow\infty}m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\mathcal{L}_{n}^{\leq q^{m+1}}\right)=m_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(\mathcal{L}_{n})=1
.
On the other hand,

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}=c_{1}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}}f_{m}d\operatorname{Vol}+c_{0}f_{m}(0)=\frac{c_{1}q^{mn}+c_{0}}{q^{mn}}\rightarrow c_{1}

as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Thus,
c_{1}=q^{n}
.

Now, consider the sequence of test functions
h_{m}=\mathbf{1}_{x^{-m}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}}
,
m\in\mathbb{N}
, to compute
c_{0}
. By Lemma
2.3
,

(3.3)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{h}_{m}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\#\left(\Lambda\cap x^{-m}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}\right)-1\right)dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(\Lambda).\end{split}

Note that
\lambda_{1}(\Lambda)\geq q^{-(m-1)}
if and only if

\#\left(\Lambda\cap x^{-m}\mathcal{O}_{\infty}^{n}\right)=1.

Hence,

\begin{split}0\leq\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{h_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}&=\int_{\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}:\alpha_{1}(\Lambda)\geq q^{(m-1)}\}}\widetilde{h_{m}}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}\\
&\leq C\int_{\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}:\alpha_{1}(\Lambda)\geq q^{(m-1)}\}}\alpha\>dm_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}\\
&\leq\frac{C}{q^{(m-1)/2}}\int_{\{\Lambda\in\mathcal{L}_{n}:\alpha_{1}(\Lambda)\geq q^{(m-1)}\}}\alpha^{1.5}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}},\end{split}

where
C>0
is the same constant for functions
f_{m}
as before. By Lemma
1.9
again, the above quantity converges to zero as
m
goes to
\infty
.

On the other hand,

(3.4)

\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{h_{m}}\>dm_{\mathcal{L}_{m}}=q^{n}\int_{\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}}h_{m}d\operatorname{Vol}+c_{0}h_{m}(0)=q^{-(m-1)n}+c_{0}\rightarrow c_{0}

as
m\rightarrow\infty
. Thus
c_{0}=0
and one obtains the integral formula (
1.3
).
∎

3.2.
Higher Moments

We now compute the
k
-th moment of the Siegel transform

(3.5)

\begin{split}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{f}\>^{k}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}&=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\left(\sum_{\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f(g\mathbf{v})\right)^{k}dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(g\mathcal{R}^{n})\\
&=\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\sum_{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}f(g\mathbf{v}_{1})\cdots f(g\mathbf{v}_{k})dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(g\mathcal{R}^{n}).\end{split}

If
F:(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})^{k}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
is a smooth bounded function, and we define

\widetilde{F}(\Lambda)=\sum_{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}F(g\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,g\mathbf{v}_{k}),

then the
k
-th moment formula of
f
is the special case of the integral formula for
\widetilde{F}
, when
F=f\otimes\cdots\otimes f
.
We want to apply Riesz–Kakutani–Markov theorem again to deduce the higher moment formula and for this, we first divide
(\mathcal{R}^{n})^{k}
into the equivalence classes with respect to the relation

\begin{gathered}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{k})\sim(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{k})\in(\mathcal{R}^{n})^{k}\quad\text{if}\\
(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{k})=(g\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,g\mathbf{w}_{k})\quad\text{for some }g\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}).\end{gathered}

As in the real case
[
26
]
or
S
-arithmetic case
[
14
]
,
such equivalence classes correspond to rational subspaces in
\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{k}
, which can be represented by
admissible matrices
, which are defined below.

Let
\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}
be the set of all monic polynomials, that is, the set of
f\in\mathcal{R}
whose leading coefficient is one. We define the
great common divisor

D:=\mathrm{gcd}(f_{1},\dots,f_{d})\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}
for
f_{1},\dots,f_{d}\in\mathcal{R}\setminus\{0\}
as the unique monic polynomial satisfying the following properties:

•

D\mid f_{i}
for every
i=1,\dots,d
;

•

if
D^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}
is such that
D^{\prime}\mid f_{i}
for all
i
, then
D\mid D^{\prime}
.

Definition 3.2

(Admissible Matrices)
.

Let
1\leq r\leq k
.

(1)

We say that a matrix
D\in M_{r\times k}(\mathcal{R})
is
reduced
if
\mathrm{gcd}(\{D_{ij}:1\leq i\leq r,1\leq j\leq k\})=1
.

(2)

For
Q\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}
, let
\mathfrak{D}^{k}_{r,Q}
denote the set of reduced matrices
D\in M_{r\times k}(\mathcal{R})
such that there exist
1\leq i_{1}<\dots<i_{r}\leq k
such that

(a)

no column of
D
is the zero vector,

(b)

([D]^{i_{1}},\dots,[D]^{i_{r}})=Q\mathrm{Id}_{r}
, and

(c)

D_{\ell j}=0
for every
1\leq\ell\leq r,1\leq j<i_{\ell}
,

where
[D]^{j}
is the
j
-th column of
D
.

(3)

Let
N(D,Q)
denote the number of row vectors
\mathbf{v}\in\mathcal{R}^{r}_{\leq\mathrm{deg}(Q)}
, such that
\frac{1}{Q}\mathbf{v}D\in\mathcal{R}^{k}
.

In the real case,
N(D,q)
is defined as the number of row vectors in
\{0,1,\ldots,q-1\}^{r}
, which is a fundamental domain for
\mathbb{Z}^{r}/q\mathbb{Z}^{r}
. Thus,
\frac{1}{q}\mathbf{v}D\in\mathbb{Z}_{S}^{k}
, and
N(D,q)
is related to the covolume of the lattices in
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{k}
which are associated to the matrix
\frac{D}{q}
.
In the function field case, since
\operatorname{covol}(\mathcal{R}^{k})=q^{k}
, to avoid a redundant
q^{r}
-term in the moment formula, we restrict the definition of
N(D,Q)
to vectors in
\mathcal{R}^{r}_{\leq\mathrm{deg}(Q)}
, which is a fundamental domain for
(x\mathcal{R}/Q\mathcal{R})^{r}
. In particular,
N(\mathrm{Id}_{k},1)=q^{k}
and moreover,

Theorem 3.3
.

Let

be a bounded and compactly supported function. It holds that

(3.6)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{F}(g\mathcal{R}^{n})dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}(g\mathcal{R}^{n})=q^{nk}\int_{(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n})^{k}}F(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{n})d\mathbf{v}_{1}\cdots d\mathbf{v}_{n}\\
&\hskip 72.26999pt+\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}}\sum_{D\in\mathfrak{D}^{k}_{r,Q}}\frac{N(D,Q)^{n}}{|Q|^{nr}}\int_{\left(\mathcal{K}_{\infty}^{n}\right)^{r}}F\left(\frac{1}{Q}(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{r})D\right)d\mathbf{v}_{1}\dots d\mathbf{v}_{r}.\end{split}

Let
(\mathbf{v}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{v}_{k})\in(\mathcal{R}\setminus\{0\})^{k}
be such that

, where

i_{\ell}\leq j<i_{\ell+1}

In particular, the vectors

(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r})C=(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}).

Since

C_{\ell,j}=0

\Phi(D,Q)=\left\{(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r})\in(\mathcal{R}^{n})^{r}:\begin{matrix}\frac{1}{Q}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{w}_{r})D\in(\mathcal{R}^{n})^{k}\;\text{and}\\

Note that if

\begin{split}\{(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}):\mathbf{v}_{i}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\setminus\{0\}\}=\left\{(\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}):\operatorname{rk}\{\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}\}=k\right\}\\

By (

(3.10)

\begin{split}&\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\widetilde{F}(g\mathcal{R}^{n})dm_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\hskip-3.61371pt\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{matrix}\mathbf{v}_{1},\dots,\mathbf{v}_{k}\in\mathcal{R}^{n}\\

&\hskip 57.81621pt+\sum_{r=1}^{k-1}\sum_{Q\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}}\sum_{D\in\mathfrak{D}^{k}_{r,Q}}\int_{\mathcal{L}_{n}}\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}(\mathbf{w}_{1},\ldots,\mathbf{w}_{r})\\

Thus, Theorem

.

Proof of Theorem
3.4
.

For

Observe that if

is when

Let

be a positive integer such that
\frac{\|\alpha\|}{r}=q^{t}>1

with

First, note that (

, since
|a-Q|\geq 1
provided that
a\neq Q
.
For

with

by

, where

\begin{split}\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{R}:|a-Q|\leq q^{d-t}\right\}\cap P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}\mathcal{R}&=\#\left\{a^{\prime}\in\mathcal{R}:\left|a^{\prime}-\frac{Q}{P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}}\right|\leq\frac{q^{d-t}}{P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}}\right\}\\
&=\frac{q^{d-t+1}}{|P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}|},\end{split}

provided that we put

and noting that

is again a monic polynomial. Using the inclusion-exclusion principle in a similar manner as in (

\begin{split}&\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{R}:|a-Q|\leq q^{d-t}\;\text{and}\;\mathrm{gcd}(a,Q)=1\right\}\\
&=\#\left\{a\in\mathcal{R}:|a-Q|\leq q^{d-t}\right\}+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq\ell}\#\left\{a\in P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}\mathcal{R}:|a-Q|\leq q^{d-t}\right\}\\
&=q^{d-t+1}\left(1+\sum_{k=1}^{\ell}(-1)^{k}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<\cdots<i_{k}\leq\ell}\frac{1}{|P_{i_{1}}\cdots P_{i_{k}}|}\right)=q^{d-t+1}\frac{\varphi(Q)}{|Q|}\\
&=\frac{\varphi(Q)}{q^{t-1}}.\end{split}

\begin{split}\operatorname{Var}(X_{B})&=\sum_{d=t}^{\infty}\frac{q^{n}}{q^{dn}}r^{n}\sum_{\scriptsize\begin{array}[]{c}Q\in\mathcal{R}_{\text{monic}}\\
\mathrm{deg}(Q)=d\end{array}}q^{-t+1}\varphi(Q)\\
&=q^{n-t+1}r^{n}(1-q^{-1})\sum_{d=t}^{\infty}\frac{1}{q^{(n-2)d}}=\frac{q^{2n-1}}{q^{n-2}-1}(1-q^{-1})\left(\frac{r}{\|\alpha\|}\right)^{n-1}\mathrm{Vol}(B).\end{split}
