Heat transfer optimization in MHD Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid flow using CPC fractional modeling
Authors: Maryam Saba, Huma Rubab, Umair Khan, MD Shamshuddin, Syed Modassir Hussain, Justine Nakintu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractEfficient thermal management in microchannel systems is essential for modern cooling devices, biomedical systems, energy units, and compact heat exchangers. In this study, the unsteady heat transfer and flow behavior of a Casson-type ternary hybrid nanofluid in a parallel-plate microchannel are investigated under generalized magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) effects. Water is used as the base fluid, while gold , copper , and silver nanoparticles are uniformly suspended to improve the thermal transport capacity of the fluid. The main motivation of this work is to examine how memory effects, magnetic field strength, and enhanced nanoparticle properties influence the velocity and temperature distributions in a non-Newtonian microchannel flow. To describe the memory and hereditary characteristics of the flow, the governing equations are modeled using the Constant Proportional Caputo (CPC) fractional derivative. An implicit finite-difference scheme is also developed to support thus study results numerically. The model is validated by comparing the present profiles with the previous study results, and a close agreement is observed. In addition, a magnitude-based sensitivity analysis is carried out to identify the most influential physical parameters. The results show that the thermal Grashof number has the strongest effect on the velocity response, while the effective Prandtl number is the dominant parameter controlling the temperature field. The CPC fractional model provides smoother and more flexible transient behavior compared with the classical integer-order model. Furthermore, the ternary hybrid nanofluid shows better heat transfer performance than simple nanofluid and hybrid nanofluid cases because of its improved effective thermal conductivity.Keywords: Finite difference, Fractional solution, Casson fluid, Non-Newtonian fluid, Nanofluid, Heat transfer

Introduction

The influence of mass and heat transfer is a crucial element of the non-Newtonian fluid flow in some intricate scenarios. Using mass and heat transfer systems to model non-Newtonian fluid flow has many practical uses, such as the preservation of food, the generation of heat, the design of automotive carburetors, etc. In industrial engineering, non-Newtonian fluids play a crucial role. Fluid mechanics theorists give them a lot of attention, and to address the engineering significance, a number of theoretical models are developed and subsequently solved. Yang and Du [1] offer a novel investigation of non-Newtonian fluids that includes the flow of the boundary layer and heat transfer analysis. Using A fractional derivative of CF, Ahmad et al. [2] investigated water-based Maxwell hybrid nanofluids using mathematical modeling. Ayub et al. [3] used the effects of magnetic force and Newtonian heating to evaluate heat transmission in viscous flow inside a rotating domain.

Nanofluids are prepared by dispersing very small solid nanoparticles in a base fluid to improve its heat-transfer ability. The presence of nanoparticles usually increases the thermal conductivity of the working fluid, making nanofluids useful in many engineering and biomedical systems. Their applications include drug delivery, solar water heating, engine cooling, electronic cooling, transformer oil cooling, nuclear reactor cooling, and drag-reduction systems. The term “nanofluid” was introduced by Choi et al. [4] during the development of advanced coolants at Argonne National Laboratory. In general, these fluids contain nanoparticles with sizes smaller than 100 nm, which help enhance the thermal performance of conventional fluids. A clear understanding of fluid motion and heat transport is essential for designing efficient thermal systems. With the progress of nanotechnology, researchers have combined ordinary base fluids with different nanoparticles to obtain improved thermal properties. Among these advanced fluids, ternary hybrid nanofluids have attracted considerable attention because they contain three different types of nanoparticles suspended in a single base fluid. This combination can provide better thermal performance than mono and hybrid nanofluids due to the combined effects of the selected nanoparticles. Therefore, ternary hybrid nanofluids are considered promising working fluids for heat-transfer enhancement in modern engineering applications. Several recent studies have explored the behavior of nanofluids and hybrid nanofluids under different physical conditions. Alharbi [5] studied heat transfer in ternary nanofluid flow over a stretching or shrinking sheet and examined the effects of slip conditions and nanoparticle concentration. Usman et al. [6] analyzed three-dimensional power-law nanofluid flow with nonlinear thermal radiation and a uniform magnetic field, showing that these effects strongly influence boundary-layer behavior. Rashdi et al. [7] developed a numerical model for steady two-dimensional Maxwell nanofluid flow and reported that the velocity decreases when the Maxwell parameter increases. Sadighi et al. [8] investigated mass transfer in magnetohydrodynamic power-law nanofluid flow and found that a higher concentration exponent improves mass-transfer behavior.

A significant progress in the research of heat transfer (HT) engineering and nanotechnology, tri-hybrid nanofluids (THNFs) are a step beyond hybrid nanofluids (HNFs). Three types of nanoparticles are masterfully combined with a base fluid in THNFs to form a fantastic blend that enables remarkable enhancement in convective and thermal conductivity heat transfer. By utilizing the peculiarities of three different nanoparticles, THNFs open a new series of opportunities to enhance the effectiveness of heat transfer (HT) in different industrial and technical practices to a significant extent. Alqawasmi et al. [9] explored a 2D time independent model to numerically model the magnetohydrodynamic (MHD) Ternary hybrid nanofluid (THNF) flow. On increasing the estimates of the magnetic parameters, they found out that, with the increase of temperature, there is a decrease in velocity. A Shear-thinning liquid is known as a Casson fluid that has Infinite viscosity with no shear rate. Infinite viscosity and zero shear rate characterize Casson fluid, a shear-thinning liquid. If the imposed yield stress is greater than the shear stress, the fluid acts as a solid; if it is less than the shear stress, it starts to flow [10]. Casson fluids include things like jelly, intense fruit liquids, soup, tomato sauce, and honey. Human blood can also be used to make Casson fluid [11]. Casson fluid flow across the thin sheet that shrinks with varying thermal radiation and wall temperature, Bhattacharyya et al. [12] found the precise thermal boundary layer solution. Riaz et al. [13] studied nanoparticle solidification in MHD nanofluid flow using non-local fractional kernels and showed how fractional operators help describe heat-transfer behavior in porous media. Ali et al. [14] used a Prabhakar fractional approach to analyze MHD Oldroyd-B fluid flow with slip and Newtonian heating, highlighting the role of memory effects in thermal transport. Ali et al. [15]explored the influence of viscoelasticity on a thermal system in annular cylindrical regions under an inclined magnetic field. Further, recent studies regarding nanofluids can be analyzed in [16–19].

The researchers in the majority of previous studies mainly examined the steady state and seldom included the temporal component. The fluid speed at the surface of the solid must be greater than zero for slip to occur, which causes motion of the fluid with relation to the solid surface. Particularly, the no-slip assumption in microfluidics may lead to inaccurate estimations of flow dynamics; the slip condition has a significant impact on fluid dynamics. Slip may affect heat and mass transfer rates, change the transport properties of the fluid, and result in changes to velocity profiles. To build and improve microfluidic devices, including Microscale heat exchangers and lab-on-a-chip technologies, it is essential to understand slip at the boundary. The interfacial slip boundary condition was employed in Devi et al. [20] to accurately solve the pair stress fluid flow issue via a polluted fluid sphere. Yasin et al. [21] investigated Maxwell fluid with MHD viscosity and elasticity in slip conditions with a concentration on peristalsis. Theoretical and statistical analysis of Navier–Stokes equations with power law slip boundary condition was conducted by Djoko et al. [22]. Saqib et al. [23] analyzed the Casson nanofluid using the AB-fractional derivatives. Examining the unstable MHD flow of a Casson ternary nanofluid over an infinite vertical flat plate is what makes this work unique. In contrast to earlier research that used the Laplace transforms for analytical answers, such as [24] and [25]. The finite difference numerical approach is used in this study to get more precise and reliable results. Numerical computations are performed using the finite difference method. To compare analytical and numerical findings, an analysis is carried out. The performance of significant factors like temperature and velocity is the fundamental objective of the graphical analysis.

Based on the above discussion, it is clear that recent studies have made important progress in the analysis of Casson fluids, MHD flows, nanofluids, hybrid nanofluids, and fractional transport models. However, most available works are mainly limited to simple nanofluids or binary hybrid nanofluids, while the combined effects of ternary hybrid nanoparticles, Casson rheology, microchannel geometry, magnetic field, and fractional memory behavior have not been fully explored. In particular, very limited attention has been given to water-based Au–Cu–Ag ternary hybrid Casson nanofluid flow between parallel plates under a CPC fractional framework. This gap is critical as most of the microchannel flows are sensitive to wall effects, magnetic resistance, composition and the memory dependent transport behavior of the nanoparticles. This motivates the present study to investigate the unsteady MHD flow and heat transfer of a Casson ternary hybrid nanofluid in a parallel-plate microchannel. The governing equations are derived based on the CPC fractional derivative, which is able to more realistically represent the memory and non-local transport effects than the classical integer-order model. In addition, an implicit finite-difference scheme is formulated to check the validity and to calculate the numerical results. Besides, the classical and fractional solutions are compared, performance of NF, HNF, and THNF cases are assessed, and magnitude-based sensitivity analysis is carried out to find the most influential physical parameters. The validity of the model is also verified by comparing the results obtained in the present work with the published previous work.

Constant proportional caputo (CPC)

The concepts of proportionality and classical differentiation are combined in the CPC operator, a fractional calculus operator. This operator not only can model specific phenomena that cannot be modeled using typical integer order operators, but it also provides a more versatile structure for modeling dynamic systems.

The Constant Proportional Caputo (CPC) derivative is a generalized fractional derivative that extends the classical Caputo derivative by including a constant proportional term. This operator is useful for describing systems where the present behavior depends not only on the current state but also on the past history of the process. In fractional fluid dynamics, the CPC derivative is commonly used to represent memory and hereditary effects in complex transport phenomena, such as heat transfer and nanofluid flow. Numerical solution

In this section, the finite difference method is used to solve the non-dimensional governing equations numerically. This method is suitable for the present unsteady and coupled tri-hybrid nanofluid flow problem because it provides a stable and accurate numerical framework. The spatial domain is divided into a uniform grid, while the solution is advanced in time using a fixed time step. To preserve second-order accuracy, the diffusion terms are approximated at the half-time level, and the source and coupling terms are treated in a consistent manner. At each time level, the discretized equations produce tridiagonal linear systems, which are efficiently solved by the Thomas algorithm. Results discussion

In this study, the unsteady flow of Casson-type nanofluid over a vertical plate in the presence of an external magnetic field has been examined. The influence of the ramped and constant wall temperature conditions on the thermal behaviour of the fluid is given special consideration. The CPC fractional derivative is added to the mathematical model to capture the memory effects and non-classical transport behavior better than the classical approach. Gold (Au), Copper (Cu), and Silver (Ag) nanoparticles are suspended in the base fluid (water), to improve the thermal conductivity and heat transfer performance. Conclusion

In this study, the unsteady flow of a Casson nanofluid in a vertical microchannel under MHD effects has been investigated. The channel is filled with water-based nanofluid containing Ag, Au, and Cu nanoparticles to improve the thermal performance of the fluid. The CPC fractional derivative is used to include memory effects and non-classical diffusion behavior in the model. In addition, a second-order implicit finite difference method is applied to compute numerical results and support the analytical findings. The main conclusions of the present study are given below:
