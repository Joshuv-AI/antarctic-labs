A pathological set regarding the propagation of almost sure properties of Gaussian measures
Pablo Merino

Abstract
. We provide a complementary result to the quasi-invariance of Gaussian measures supported on Sobolev spaces with high regularity under the dynamics of the three-dimensional periodic defocusing nonlinear wave equation from Gunaratnam-Oh-Tzvetkov-Weber (2022). Namely, given p p and σ \sigma large enough, we prove the existence of dense sets of Sobolev spaces W σ , p ​ ( 𝕋 3 ) W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3}) which do not preserve the regularity σ \sigma throughout the aforementioned dynamics. This is in sharp contrast with the propagation under the flow of almost sure properties.

2.
Propagation of almost sure regularity under a Gaussian measure along the flow

We know the there is global well-posedness of the Cauchy problem (
1

\vec{H}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

(see

\Phi_{\text{NLW}}

\displaystyle\Phi_{\text{NLW}}(t):

\displaystyle\quad\vec{H}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3})\rightarrow\vec{H}^{r}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

\displaystyle\quad(u_{0},v_{0})\mapsto(u(t),\partial u(t)),

where

and
u_{1}\in H^{r-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})

Given

\displaystyle d\vec{\mu}_{s}=Z_{s}^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\|\vec{u}\|^{2}_{\vec{H}^{s}}}d\vec{u}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}Z_{s,k}^{-1}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle k\rangle^{2s}|\widehat{u}(k)|^{2}}e^{-\frac{1}{2}\langle k\rangle^{2(s-1)}|\widehat{v}(k)|^{2}}d\widehat{u}(k)d\widehat{v}(k).

Samples

\displaystyle u^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{g_{k}^{\omega}}{\langle k\rangle^{s}}e^{ikx}\text{ and }v^{\omega}(x)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}^{3}}\frac{h_{k}^{\omega}}{\langle k\rangle^{s-1}}e^{ikx},

(6)

.

Let

,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-\tau)|D|)}{|D|}F(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{q}_{T}W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\|\left\|\frac{\sin((t-\tau)|D|)}{|D|}F(\tau)\right\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\right\|_{L^{1}_{\tau}[0,T]}\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}[0,T]}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\left\|\frac{\sin((t-\tau)|D|)}{|D|}F(\tau)\right\|_{L^{q}_{t}([0,T],W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3}))}\right\|_{L^{1}_{\tau}[0,T]}

\displaystyle\lesssim\left\|\left\|F(\tau)\right\|_{H^{\mu+\sigma-1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})}\right\|_{L^{1}_{\tau}[0,T]}=\|F\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{\sigma+\mu-1}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

Considering the sharp case in the last inequality of (
28
),

\displaystyle\mu=\frac{3}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-\frac{1}{q}\Longrightarrow\mu+\sigma-1=\sigma+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}-\frac{1}{q}.

Take

to obtain (

). Furthermore, if we consider
\sigma+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}>0
and
F=u^{3}
we can apply Leibniz fractional rule

to obtain

\displaystyle\|F\|_{L^{1}_{T}H^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\leq T\sup_{|t|\leq T}\|u(t)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|u(t)\|_{H^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}-\frac{3}{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

∎

Proof of Theorem

.

Recall that

,

, and notice that under these conditions we cannot have

,

(32)

for

. To be more precise, once we prove (

), then we would have shown that, for any

\displaystyle u(\cdot,t)\notin W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3}).

Our choices of

,

and

•

From Lemma

•

Remark 5.5
.

Consider the following estimates from
[

]
,
[

]
(see also chapter

of
[

]
), suitably adapted to the periodic setting following the strategy from Proposition

:

(34)

with

\displaystyle\left|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right|\leq\frac{1}{d-1},\quad\alpha\leq 1-(d-1)\left|\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{2}\right|,\quad d\geq 2.

These estimates also allow us to control (

) when

. This comes from the following inequality, given

:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-\tau)|D|)}{|D|}u^{3}(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim T\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{2}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\sigma+\frac{3}{2}-\frac{5}{p}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

Indeed, the integrand in
\tau
of the Duhamel integral (
5
), given by

, solves the problem

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\partial_{tt}v-\Delta v=0,\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{3},\\

Therefore, from (
34
),

\displaystyle\|v(\cdot,t,\tau)\|_{W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim\|u^{3}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{W^{\sigma+\left(1-\frac{2}{p}\right)-1,p}}.

Thus, by the fractional Leibniz rule,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\sin((t-\tau)|D|)}{|D|}\right.

\displaystyle\left.u^{3}(\tau)d\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}W^{\sigma,p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\lesssim\sup_{|t|\leq T}\int_{0}^{T}\|u^{3}(\cdot,\tau)\|_{W^{\sigma-\frac{2}{p},p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}d\tau

\displaystyle\leq T\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}W^{\sigma-\frac{2}{p},p}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}.

(35)

Observe that

•

we have the Sobolev embedding

,

•

and

as long as

by Lemma

Therefore, given

,

and

, in (

\displaystyle\lesssim T\|u\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\sigma+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}\|u\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}H^{\sigma+\lambda-\frac{3}{2}-\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}

for some

Appendix A
Appendix: Bessel functions

We define Bessel functions in the following way. See
[
23
]
or Chapter
4
of
[
19
]
for further detail.

Definition A.1
.

Let
k>-1/2
and
t>0
. Then

\displaystyle J_{k}(t)=\frac{(t/2)^{k}}{\Gamma(2k+1)\Gamma(1/2)}\int_{-1}^{1}e^{its}(1-s^{2})^{(2k-1)/2}ds.

(36)

Moreover, for
t
large we have the following asymptotic behaviour:

\displaystyle J_{k}(t)=t^{-1/2}e^{it}\sum_{j=0}^{N}a_{j}t^{-j}+t^{-1/2}e^{-it}\sum_{j=0}^{N}b_{j}t^{-j}+R_{k}(t)

(37)

for suitable
(a_{j})_{j\geq 0},(b_{j})_{j\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{C}
, for any
N\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
R_{k}(t)=\mathcal{O}(t^{-N-\frac{3}{2}})
, where
\mathcal{O}
is given for
t\rightarrow\infty
.

We present the proof of Lemma
3.5
, which justifies the application of Bessel functions in this work.

Proof of Lemma
3.5
.

The case
f\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is proved in page 155 of
[
19
]
. To extend this result to any
f
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
1<p<\infty
, it suffices to use a density argument with radial mollifiers. Indeed, let
\phi\in C^{\infty}_{c}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be radial and denote
\phi_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon^{-n}\phi(\cdot/\varepsilon)
for any
\varepsilon>0
, so that
f_{\varepsilon}=\phi_{\varepsilon}\ast f\rightarrow f
as
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In particular,
f_{\varepsilon}(x)\rightarrow f(x)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
up to a subsequence. Observe that
f_{\varepsilon}
is radial because
f
and
\phi_{\varepsilon}
are radial, so we can write
f_{\varepsilon}(x)=f_{\varepsilon,0}(|x|)
for some
f_{\varepsilon,0}
, and apply the first part of the proof to each
\mathcal{F}[f_{\varepsilon}]
. Thus,
f_{\varepsilon,0}(|x|)\rightarrow f_{0}(|x|)
for a.e.
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, up to a subsequence.

Thanks to the dominated convergence theorem, for any
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
we have that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(x)dx\rightarrow\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\varphi(x)dx\text{ as }\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Moreover,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f_{\varepsilon}(x)\varphi(x)dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}[f_{\varepsilon}](\xi)\mathcal{F}[\varphi](\xi)d\xi

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(c(d)|\xi|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{\varepsilon,0}(\rho)\rho^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(\rho|\xi|)d\rho\right)\mathcal{F}[\varphi](\xi)d\xi.

where we used Plancherel theorem and the known result in
L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Since the integral

\displaystyle c(d)|\xi|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{0}(\rho)\rho^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(\rho|\xi|)d\rho

exists for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, by the a.e. pointwise convergence given above
f_{\varepsilon,0}(\rho)\rightarrow f_{0}(\rho)
and by dominated convergence theorem we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}[f](\xi)=c(d)|\xi|^{-\frac{d-2}{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}f_{0}(\rho)\rho^{\frac{d}{2}}J_{\frac{d-2}{2}}(\rho|\xi|)d\rho

for a.e.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
∎

Appendix B
Appendix: Homogeneous distributions

Let
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
and
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R})
. Observe that

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\infty}\mathbb{I}_{(1,\infty)}(x)x^{\alpha}\varphi(x)dx

is well-defined for any complex value of
\alpha
. In this section we will compute the inverse distributional Fourier transform of
\mathbb{I}_{(1,\infty)}(x)x^{\alpha}
, i.e. we will prove Lemma
3.7
. We need a preliminary result.

Lemma B.1
.

Let
\theta,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. Then

\displaystyle\lim_{\gamma\rightarrow 0^{+}}(\theta+i\gamma)^{\beta}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\theta^{\beta}&\text{for}&\theta>0\\
\\
(-1)^{\beta}\theta^{\beta}e^{i\beta\pi}&\text{for}&\theta<0.\end{array}\right.

Proof.

Observe that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}

\displaystyle(\theta+i\tau)^{\beta}=\exp[\beta(\log|\theta+i\tau|+i\text{arg}(\theta+i\tau))]

\displaystyle=\left\{\begin{array}[]{lcc}\theta^{\beta}&\text{for}&\theta>0\\
\\
(-1)^{\beta}\theta^{\beta}e^{i\beta\pi}&\text{for}&\theta<0,\end{array}\right.

given that
\text{arg}(\theta+i\tau)\overset{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\rightarrow}0
for
\theta>0
and
\text{arg}(\theta+i\tau)\overset{\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}}{\rightarrow}\pi
for
\theta<0
.
∎

Proof of Lemma
3.7
.

Consider

\displaystyle G_{\tau}(x)=x^{\alpha}e^{-\tau x}\mathbb{I}_{(1,\infty)}(x),

for
\tau>0
, so that we will compute
\mathcal{F}^{-1}[G_{\tau}]
and take the limit
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
. Given
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
, write

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[G_{\tau}](\theta)=\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha}e^{-\tau x}e^{ix\theta}dx=\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha}e^{izx}dx,

for
z=\theta+i\tau
. This implies that
z
is in the upper half-plane of
\mathbb{C}
, so that
\text{arg}(z)\in(0,\pi)
. We change variables through the rotation
\xi=-izx
, so
\text{arg}(\xi)=\text{arg}(z)-\frac{\pi}{2}\in(-\pi/2,\pi/2)
and

\displaystyle\int_{1}^{\infty}x^{\alpha}e^{izx}dx=\left(\frac{e^{i\pi/2}}{z}\right)^{\alpha+1}\int_{e^{i(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)}(1,\infty)}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi.

The next step is to prove that we can remove the rotation
e^{i(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)}
from the integration domain. Consider within the complex plane a contour
L
surrounding the sector between angles
0
and
\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2
. This contour departs from radius
1
and reaches some radius
M>0
which we will make arbitrarily large eventually.

Assume without loss of generality that
\text{arg}(z)\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)
, the case
\text{arg}(z)\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})
is proved in a similar way. We write
L=L_{1}+L_{2}+L_{3}+L_{4}
, where

\displaystyle L_{1}=\{x:x\in(1,M)\},\quad L_{2}=\{e^{i(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)}(M+1-x):x\in(1,M)\},

\displaystyle L_{3}=\{Me^{i\gamma}:\gamma\in(0,\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)\},

\displaystyle L_{4}=\{e^{i(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2-\gamma)}:\gamma\in(0,\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)\}.

Given that
\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}
is holomorphic in
\{\xi\in\mathbb{C}:|\xi|>0\}
, by Cauchy’s theorem we have that

\displaystyle\int_{L}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi=0.

The term from that integral corresponding to a constant radius
M
satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\int_{L_{3}}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi\right|=\left|\int_{0}^{\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2}M^{\alpha+1}e^{i(\alpha+1)\gamma}e^{-M(\cos\gamma+i\sin\gamma)}id\gamma\right|

\displaystyle\leq M^{\text{Re}(\alpha)+1}\left(\text{arg}(z)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\sup_{\gamma\in(0,\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2)}e^{-M\cos(\gamma)},

which tends to
0
as
M\rightarrow\infty
.

On the other hand, the sector of constant radius
1
satisfies

\displaystyle\left|\int_{L_{4}}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{0}^{\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2}e^{-i(\alpha+1)\gamma}e^{-(\cos(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2-\gamma)+i\sin(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2-\gamma))}id\gamma\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left(\text{arg}(z)-\frac{\pi}{2}\right)\sup_{\gamma\in(0,\text{arg}(z)-\frac{\pi}{2})}e^{-\cos(\text{arg}(z)-\pi/2-\gamma)}\leq\frac{\pi}{2}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[G_{\tau}](\theta)=\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi-\int_{0}^{1}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi-\int_{L_{4}}\xi^{\alpha}e^{-\xi}d\xi\right)\left(\frac{e^{i\pi/2}}{\theta+i\tau}\right)^{\alpha+1}.

Observe that

\displaystyle\max\left\{\left|\int_{0}^{\infty}\right.\right.

\displaystyle\left.\left.e^{-\xi}\xi^{\alpha}d\xi\right|,\left|\int_{0}^{1}e^{-\xi}\xi^{\alpha}d\xi\right|\right\}

\displaystyle\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-\xi}\xi^{\text{Re}(\alpha)}d\xi\leq\Gamma(\text{Re}(\alpha)+1),

where the latter is well-defined as long as we consider
\text{Re}(\alpha)\notin-\mathbb{N}
and an analytic continuation of
\Gamma
(see e.g.
[
18
]
).
All in all,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[G_{\tau}](\theta)=J(\alpha)\left(\frac{e^{i\pi/2}}{\theta+i\tau}\right)^{\alpha+1},

(38)

where
J(\alpha)
is a constant only depending on
\alpha
such that

\displaystyle|J(\alpha)|\leq\Gamma(\text{Re}(\alpha)+1)+\frac{\pi}{2}.

If we take the limit
\tau\rightarrow 0^{+}
in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R})
, from Lemma
B.1
we have that

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}^{-1}[x^{\alpha}\mathbb{I}_{(1,\infty)}(x)](\theta)

\displaystyle=J(\alpha)[e^{i\pi(\alpha+1)/2}(\theta\mathbb{I}_{(0,\infty)}(\theta))^{-\alpha-1}+e^{-i\pi(\alpha+1)/2}(|\theta|\mathbb{I}_{(-\infty,0)}(\theta))^{-\alpha-1}].

(39)

∎
