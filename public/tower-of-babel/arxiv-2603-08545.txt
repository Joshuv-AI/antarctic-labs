The image of the adelic Galois representation of an elliptic curve with complex multiplication
Álvaro Lozano-Robledo and Benjamin York

Abstract
. Let E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} be an elliptic curve and let ρ E : Gal ⁡ ( ℚ ¯ / ℚ ) → GL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ^ ) \rho_{E}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}) be the adelic Galois representation attached to E E . We describe and implement an algorithm to compute the image of ρ E \rho_{E} in GL ⁡ ( 2 , ℤ ^ ) \operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}) (up to conjugation) for an elliptic curve E / ℚ E/\mathbb{Q} with complex multiplication (CM) and j j -invariant not 0 0 or 1728 1728 . In the process, we prove certain entanglement results between division fields of elliptic curves over ℚ \mathbb{Q} with CM.

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}(E))\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})

\displaystyle\rho_{E}

\displaystyle\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to\operatorname{Aut}(T(E))\cong\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})

[

]

[
8

. In particular, Zywina has recently described an algorithm to compute the adelic image of

when
E/\mathbb{Q}
in
[
13
]
, and more generally for
E/F
over a number field in
[
14

The main result of this paper is as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication by an order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
of an imaginary quadratic field
K
, such that
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. Let
G_{E}
be the image of the Galois representation
\rho_{E}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}}/\mathbb{Q})\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Then,

(1)

There is a group
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
such that
G_{E}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
with index
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]=2
.

(2)

There is an explicitly computable integer
M\geq 2
such that
G_{E}=\pi_{M}^{-1}(\pi_{M}(G_{E}))
, where
\pi_{M}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
is the natural reduction mod-
M
map. In other words,
G_{E}
is defined modulo
M
.

(3)

The image
\pi_{M}(G_{E})
is explicitly computable.

The auxiliary constants

and
\phi

will be defined in Section

2.
Notation and previous results

In this section, we will set the notation that we will follow in the rest of the paper, define the matrix groups that are the maximal possible images in the CM case, and recall results from previous articles that we will use in later sections. Our main references are
[
6
]
and
[
3
]
.

Let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field, and let
\mathcal{O}_{K}
be the ring of integers of
K
with discriminant
\Delta_{K}
. Let
f\geq 1
be an integer and let
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
be the order of
K
of conductor
f
. Let
j_{K,f}
be a CM
j
-invariant corresponding to the order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
(thus,
j_{K,f}
is an algebraic integer and every CM
j
-invariant with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
is a Galois conjugate of
j_{K,f}
) and let
N\geq 2
. We define associated constants
\delta
and
\phi
as follows:

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 0\bmod 4
, let
\delta=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4
, and
\phi=0
.

•

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 4
, let
\delta=\frac{(\Delta_{K}-1)}{4}f^{2}
, let
\phi=f
.

We define matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

c_{\varepsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\varepsilon&0\\
-\varepsilon\phi&-\varepsilon\\
\end{array}\right),\quad c^{\prime}_{\varepsilon}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\varepsilon\\
\varepsilon&0\\
\end{array}\right),\quad\text{ and }\quad c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a+b\phi&b\\
\delta b&a\\
\end{array}\right)

where
\varepsilon\in\{\pm 1\}
and
a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
such that
\det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}
.
We define the Cartan subgroup
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
by

\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\{c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b):a,b\in\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z},\ \det(c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b))\in(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})^{\times}\right\},

and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)=\left\langle\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N),c_{1}\right\rangle
. We will sometimes call
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
the ‘‘normalizer’’ of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. In the case of a prime number
N
, then
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is the true group-theoretic normalizer, but this is often not the case for composite values of
N
(see
[
6
]
, Section 5). Finally, we write
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
, where the projective limit is taken over the natural projection maps
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
given by reduction mod-
N
when
M|N
, and regard the inverse limit as a subgroup
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. If
\ell
is a prime, we write
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})=\varprojlim\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
and
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})=\varprojlim\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
.

Remark 2.1
.

If
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 4
and
N
is odd, one can find a basis of
E[N]
such that the image of
\rho_{E,N}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,0}(N)
with
\delta=\Delta_{K}f^{2}/4
, which allows for simpler (and somewhat more familiar) descriptions of the images (see
[
6
, Thm. 1.1]
, and Remark
2.5
). However, under the same assumptions of
\Delta_{K}f^{2}\equiv 1\bmod 4
, we need to use the choices
\delta=\frac{(\Delta_{K}-1)}{4}f^{2}
and
\phi=f
in order to build a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis that is compatible modulo
N
, for every
N>1
.

2.1.
Notation for Galois representations

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
. We define several Galois representations attached to
E
, as follows. Let
G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}
be the absolute Galois group
\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f}))
of the field of definition
\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
, let
N\geq 2
be an integer, and let
\ell
be a prime. The natural action of
G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}
on
E[N]
, on the
\ell
-adic Tate module
T_{\ell}(E)=\varprojlim E[\ell^{n}]
, and on the adelic Tate module
T(E)=\varprojlim E[N]
induce Galois representations

\displaystyle\rho_{E,N}

\displaystyle\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}\to\operatorname{Aut}(E[N]),

\displaystyle\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}

\displaystyle\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T_{\ell}(E)),

\displaystyle\rho_{E}

\displaystyle\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T(E)).

Moreover, once we choose
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathbb{Z}_{\ell}
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-bases of
E[N]
,
T_{\ell}(E)
, and
T(E)
, respectively, we obtain non-canonical isomorphisms of the automorphism groups with the groups of matrices
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
,
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}_{\ell})
, and
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Thus, we will usually consider the images of the Galois representations as subgroups of matrices in
\operatorname{GL}_{2}
.

We will typically denote the images of
\rho_{E,N}
,
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
, and
\rho_{E}
by
G_{E,N}
,
G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
, and
G_{E}
, respectively. The results stated below show that there exists an appropriate choice of basis such that
G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})
, and
G_{E}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Finally, we will often need to restrict the Galois representations to the index-
2
subgroup
G_{K(j_{K,f})}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}/K(j_{K,f}))
of the absolute Galois group
G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})}
, and we will denote the restrictions by
\rho_{E/K,N}
,
\rho_{E/K,\ell^{\infty}}
, and
\rho_{E/K}
, and their images will be denoted by
G_{E/K,N}
,
G_{E/K,\ell^{\infty}}
, and
G_{E/K}
, respectively. Our choice of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis will be such that
G_{E/K}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
.

2.2.
Classification of
\ell
-adic images

For a prime
\ell
, the first author (in
[
6
]
) has given a complete classification of the possible
\ell
-adic images
G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}=\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})})
attached to elliptic curves
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, which we summarize next.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
6
]
, Theorems 1.1, 1.2, and 4.1)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and let
N\geq 2
.
Then:

(1)

There is a
\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z}
-basis of
E[N]
such that the image
G_{E,N}
of
\rho_{E,N}

is contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
, and the index of the image of
\rho_{E,N}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
is a divisor of the order of
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}/\mathcal{O}_{K,f,N}^{\times}
, where
\mathcal{O}_{K,f,N}^{\times}=\{u\in\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}:u\equiv 1\bmod N\mathcal{O}_{K,f}\}
.

(2)

With the basis chosen as in (1), and if the image of
\rho_{E,N}
is
G_{E,N}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
, then the image of
\rho_{E/K,N}
is
G_{E,N}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
.

(3)

There is a compatible system of bases of
E[N]
such that the image of
\rho_{E}
is contained in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, and the index of the image of
\rho_{E}
in
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
is a divisor of the order
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}^{\times}
. In particular, the index is a divisor of
4
or
6
.

The following theorem shows that, for a prime
\ell>3
, the image of
\rho_{E,\ell^{\infty}}
is, in fact, defined modulo
\ell
. Moreover, if the prime
\ell
does not divide
2\Delta_{K}f
, then the
\ell
-adic image is as large as possible.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
6
]
, Theorem 1.2)
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and let
\ell
be prime. If
\ell>3
, or
\ell>2

, then

is the full inverse image via the natural reduction mod-

, then

, and so

.

The following result describes the
\ell
-adic image in the case of a prime

\Delta_{K}f
.

3.
Level of Definition

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and let
\delta
and
\phi
be the associated constants defined in Section
2
. For
M,N\geq 2
where
M\mid N
, let

\pi_{N,M}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M)

be the natural projection map given by reduction modulo
M
.
We also let

\pi_{N}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)

be the natural projection map from the adelic normalizer of Cartan
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}=\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
, and for
\ell
prime and
n\geq 1
, we let

\pi_{\ell^{\infty},\ell^{n}}\colon\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty})\to\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})

be the natural projection map from the
\ell
-adic normalizer of Cartan. In terms of these projection maps, we make the following definitions about the level of an adelic or
\ell
-adic image.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and fix a compatible adelic basis for
T(E)
such that
G_{E}:=\im\rho_{E}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
. We say that the adelic image of
E
is
defined at level
N
for some
N\geq 1
or, equivalently, that
N
is an
adelic level of definition for
E
, if

G_{E}=\pi_{N}^{-1}(\pi_{N}(G_{E})).

We will further say that the
minimal adelic level of definition for
E
is the smallest level
N
for which the adelic image is defined. Likewise, we define the
minimal
\ell
-adic level of definition for
E
to be the least power of
\ell
, denoted
\ell^{n}
, for which

G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}=\pi_{\ell^{\infty},\ell^{n}}^{-1}(\pi_{\ell^{\infty},\ell^{n}}(G_{E,\ell^{\infty}})).

Remark 3.2
.

Note that if
N=1
is a level of definition, then
G_{E}=\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, i.e., the adelic index is
1
. Indeed,
\pi_{1}(G_{E})
is the trivial group, and therefore
\pi_{1}^{-1}(\pi_{1}(G_{E}))=\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
. In Cor.
5.6
, however, we will show that if
E
is defined over
\mathbb{Q}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, then the adelic index is always
2
. Thus, the minimal level of definition must be at least
N\geq 2
.

The rest of this section is dedicated to justify Definition
3.1
, and prove results about levels of definition of an adelic image. First, we show that if an adelic image is defined modulo
N
, then it is also defined at level
nN
for any
n\geq 1
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, suppose that the adelic image of
E
is defined modulo
N\geq 1
, and let
n\geq 1
. Then, the adelic image is also defined modulo
nN
.

Proof.

Suppose that
N
is a level of definition of the adelic image of
E
. Then, by definition, we have

G_{E}=\pi_{N}^{-1}(\pi_{N}(G_{E})).

Let
n\geq 1
and let
N^{\prime}=nN
. Since
N
is a level of definition, it follows that

\pi_{N^{\prime}}(G_{E})=\pi_{N^{\prime},N}^{-1}(\pi_{N}(G_{E})).

Thus,

\pi_{N^{\prime}}^{-1}(\pi_{N^{\prime}}(G_{E}))=\pi_{N^{\prime}}^{-1}(\pi_{N^{\prime},N}^{-1}(\pi_{N}(G_{E}))=\pi_{N}^{-1}(\pi_{N}(G_{E}))=G_{E},

as desired.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Under Definition
3.1
, the adelic image of a CM elliptic curve
E
is defined at any level
N
for which the index of the mod-
N
image is equal to the index of the adelic image. We explain why this is the case through the following results.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
G
and
H
be groups, and let
\varphi\colon G\to H
be a surjective group homomorphism. Let
H^{\prime}\subseteq H
be a subgroup of
H
, and define
G^{\prime}=\varphi^{-1}(H^{\prime})
to be the preimage of
H^{\prime}
.
Then

[G:G^{\prime}]\geq[H:H^{\prime}].

Proof.

It is sufficient to show that for every distinct coset representative of
H^{\prime}
in
H
, there is a distinct coset representative of
G^{\prime}
in
G
. Let
h_{1},h_{2}\in H
be distinct coset representatives of
H^{\prime}
, and let
g_{1},g_{2}\in G
be such that
\varphi(g_{i})=h_{i}
for
i=1,2
. Then, since
\varphi(G^{\prime})=H^{\prime}
by definition and
h_{1}H^{\prime}\cap h_{2}H^{\prime}=\emptyset
by hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle\varphi(g_{1}G^{\prime}\cap g_{2}G^{\prime})

\displaystyle\subseteq\varphi(g_{1}G^{\prime})\cap\varphi(g_{2}G^{\prime})=h_{1}H^{\prime}\cap h_{2}H^{\prime}=\emptyset,

so
g_{1}G^{\prime}\cap g_{2}G^{\prime}=\emptyset
as desired.
∎

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and suppose a compatible choice of bases is made so that
G_{E}=\im\rho_{E}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, that
G_{E,N}=\im\rho_{E,N}\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
, and that
G_{E,N}=\pi_{N}(G_{E})
for
N\geq 2
. Moreover, assume that
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N):G_{E,N}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]
. Then, the adelic image of
E
is defined at level
N
.

Proof.

Since
\pi_{N}(G_{E})=G_{E,N}
, it follows that
G_{E}
is a subgroup of
\pi_{N}^{-1}(G_{E,N})
. By Proposition
3.5
and our hypotheses, it follows that

[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N):G_{E,N}]\leq[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:\pi_{N}^{-1}(G_{E,N})]\leq[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N):G_{E,N}],

so
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:\pi_{N}^{-1}(G_{E,N})]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]
, and therefore
[\pi_{N}^{-1}(G_{E,N}):G_{E}]=1
.
∎

Note that a similar proof shows that if
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n}):G_{E,\ell^{n}}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{\infty}):G_{E,\ell^{\infty}}]
, then the
\ell
-adic image of

is defined at level
\ell^{n}
.

.

It would be reasonable at this stage to ask whether the minimal adelic level of definition of a CM elliptic curve, as stated, does not miss some arithmetic information about the corresponding elliptic curve. Namely, it is not clear a priori why the adelic image of a CM elliptic curve could not be defined at two positive integers
N
and

where
N
and

are relatively prime, in which case there could be said to be two minimal levels of definition capturing two different kinds of arithmetic information. Through the following propositions, we show that this is not an issue.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
\Delta(\mathcal{O}_{K,f})=\Delta_{K}f^{2}
be the discriminant of
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and define the constants
\delta
and
\phi
as in Section
2
. For integers
M,N\geq 2
, let

and
d=\gcd(M,N)

g_{M}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
and
g_{N}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
so that there is some
g_{d}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(d)
such that
\pi_{M,d}(g_{M})=g_{d}=\pi_{N,d}(g_{N})
. Then, there exists a unique
g_{D}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(D)
such that
\pi_{D,M}(g_{D})=g_{M}
and
\pi_{D,N}(g_{D})=g_{N}
.

Proof.

First, we note that if
u,v
are integers such that
u\equiv v\bmod d
, then the system of congruences

(1)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x\equiv u\bmod M,\\

has a unique solution modulo

M=M^{\prime}d
and
N=N^{\prime}d
, then the system (

) is equivalent to

(2)

\displaystyle\begin{cases}x\equiv u\bmod M^{\prime},\\
x\equiv u\bmod d,\\
x\equiv v\bmod N^{\prime},\\
x\equiv v\bmod d\end{cases}

which, by the Chinese remainder theorem, has a unique solution modulo
M^{\prime}N^{\prime}d=D
if and only if

.

In particular, the uniqueness of solutions of the system (
1

D
shows that for any

and

g_{D}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/D\mathbb{Z})
which reduces to
g_{N}
and

modulo

and

.

Now, if

and
g_{N}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
so that there is some

such that

and
g_{D}\equiv g_{N}\bmod N

g_{M}
is an element of the Cartan subgroup, it must be of the form

for some integers

(as in the notation established in Section

for some

be the unique solutions of the corresponding systems as in Eq. (

This is precisely the content of part (3) of

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, let
F=\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
, and let
N>M\geq 2
be relatively prime integers. Then,

(1)

\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(MN)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
via the natural isomorphism induced by the Chinese remainder theorem,

(2)

is isomorphic to a subgroup of
G_{E/K,M}\times G_{E/K,N}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)
,

(3)

the index
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(MN):G_{E/K,MN}]
is divisible by the degree of
F(E[M])\cap F(E[N])/FK
, and

(4)

if
j(E)\neq 0,1728
and
[F(E[M])\cap F(E[N]):FK]=2
, then
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E,M}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N):G_{E,N}]=1
.

Proof.

Part (1) follows from Prop.
3.8
with

and

. For an integer

, let
G_{E/K,S}
be the image of
\rho_{E/K,S}
. Then,

by Theorem

, and
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[S])/FK)\cong G_{E/K,S}
from the definition and properties of the Galois representation
\rho_{E/K,S}
. Since
F(E[MN])
is the compositum of the Galois extensions
F(E[M])
and
F(E[N])
of

, parts (2) and (3) now follow from Galois theory, because the index
[G_{E/K,M}\times G_{E/K,N}:G_{E/K,MN}]
is equal to the degree of the extension

, and

as claimed.

Now suppose that

. Then,
[G_{E/K,M}\times G_{E/K,N}:G_{E/K,MN}]=2
. Moreover, if either

or

is bigger than

, then the above displayed equation of indices shows that

is greater than

, which is impossible since

and so the index must be

or

. Thus,
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E,M}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N):G_{E,N}]=1
as desired. This shows (4).

Remark 3.14
.

The convention used in this paper is a departure from the way in which other authors have considered levels of definition. For example, in
[
9
, Proposition 12.1.4]
, the level of definition
\ell^{n}
for the
\ell
-adic image of a CM elliptic curve

agrees with ours, except the assumption is made that if

is another CM elliptic curve, where

is not conjugate to

is not conjugate to

in

This motivates the following definition, which is in line with what other authors would consider the ‘‘level of definition’’ of an image.

Definition 3.15

Let

. We say that

is an

for

(1)

is an adelic level of definition for

, and

(2)

-adic image of

we obtain an easy criterion to decide what twists are not simplest CM curves.

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an
\ell
-simplest CM curve and let
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be a twist of
E
. Further, suppose the following:

(1)

j(E)\neq 0,1728
, and
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
is a quadratic twist of
E
by a non-zero square-free
N\neq 1
with
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
(with
N^{\dagger}
as defined in Theorem
2.8
), or

(2)

j(E)=1728
and
\ell=2
, and
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
is a quartic twist
y^{2}=x^{3}+Nx
with
N
a non-zero fourth-power-free integer such that
N\neq\pm 1
and
\gcd(2,N)=1
, or

(3)

j(E)=0
, and

is a sextic twist
y^{2}=x^{3}+16N
with
N
a non-zero sixth-power-free integer such that

and
\gcd(3,N)=1
,

then
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
is not a simplest CM curve.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an
\ell
-simplest CM curve, and suppose
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
is a quadratic, quartic, or sextic twist by
N\neq 1
(non-zero, and square-, fourth-power-, or sixth-power-free, respectively) is a CM simplest curve, and
N
satisfies the conditions of the corollary. Then, by Theorem
4.1
, part (2d), it follows that
E^{\prime}
is isomorphic to a quadratic, quartic, or sextic twist by
N^{\prime}
, with
N^{\prime}
taking values as in the statement of (2d). Since

and both

and

are non-zero, and square, fourth-power, or sixth-power-free, respectively, then

(by

). Now, the conditions on

in the statement of the corollary, together with the conditions on the possible values of

lead to a contradiction. This proves the contrapositive statement of the corollary’s statement, and concludes the proof.
∎

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by

, and let
\ell
be the unique prime dividing

. By part (1) of Theorem

, there is an elliptic curve

-simplest, and

-simplest curve defined over

.

is isomorphic to a quadratic twist

of

, where

is the

, and

5.
Determining Level of Definition

In this section, we will determine a level of definition for the adelic image of an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. To compute adelic levels of definition, we first need the following result.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 3.4)
.

Let
E/F:y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
be an elliptic curve defined over a number field
F
, let
N>2
, and let
G_{E,N}
be the image of
\rho_{E,N}\colon\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{F}/F)\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. Let
\alpha\in F
and let
E^{\alpha}
be the quadratic twist of
E
by
\alpha
, i.e.,
E^{\alpha}:\alpha y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
. Then,

(1)

F(E^{\alpha}[N])\subseteq F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])
. In particular, if
\sqrt{\alpha}\in F(E[N])
, then
F(E^{\alpha}[N])\subseteq F(E[N])
.

(2)

If
[F(E^{\alpha}[N]):F]>[F(E[N]):F]
and
\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, then
F(E^{\alpha}[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])
.

(3)

If
\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, then
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
is conjugate to
\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
.

Next we prove a corollary of the previous lemma.

Corollary 5.2
.

Under the assumptions of Lemma
5.1
, if

(1)

\sqrt{\alpha}
does not belong to
F(E[N])
, and

(2)

-\operatorname{Id}
does not belong to
G_{E,N}
,

then
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
is conjugate to
\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
, and
F(E^{\alpha}[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])
.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
, part (3), we have
G_{E^{\alpha},N}
is conjugate to
\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})
. Thus,
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E[N])/F)\cong G_{E,N}
and
\operatorname{Gal}(F(E^{\alpha}[N])/F)\cong\langle-\operatorname{Id},G_{E,N}\rangle
. Hence,

[F(E^{\alpha}[N]):F]=2\cdot[F(E[N]):F]>[F(E[N]):F]

and by Lemma
5.1
, part (2), we have
F(E^{\alpha}[N])=F(\sqrt{\alpha},E[N])
as desired.
∎

, the quadratic twist of

by

-division field and its

-division field. In particular,

.

Proof.

Let

and

be as described above. Note that in all cases,

and

are greater than

, so

by Theorem
2.8

2.8

\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E^{N}[N^{\dagger}])

is contained in the intersection

4.1
, the prime
\ell

, and therefore

, and so

Let

2.2
implies that the index

\iota=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(D):G_{E^{N},D}]=[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(D):G_{E^{N}/K,D}]

Since

∎

Corollary 5.5
.

Corollary 5.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. Then:

(1)

The adelic index of the image of Galois
G_{E}
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
is equal to
2
, i.e.,
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]=2
.

(2)

All levels of definition for
G_{E}
are divisible by
\ell
, the unique prime dividing
\Delta_{K}
.

Proof.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. By Prop.
4.9
, there is an
\ell
-simplest CM curve
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
and a non-zero square-free integer
N
with
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
, such that

is isomorphic to
E^{\prime}
over
\mathbb{Q}
, and so
(E^{\prime})^{N}
is isomorphic to

. Then, by Cor.
5.5
, we have that
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(D):G_{E,D}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(D):G_{(E^{\prime})^{N},D}]=2
for
D=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
. Hence, the adelic index satisfies
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]\geq 2
. Since
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, we know that
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}:G_{E}]\leq 2
, and therefore the index equals
2
, as claimed in (1).

Now, for (2), suppose for a contradiction that
M\geq 2
is another level of definition for
G_{E}
, such that
\gcd(M,\ell)=1
. We have just shown that
D=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
is a level of definition, where
\gcd(\ell^{n},N^{\dagger})=1
. Suppose first that
N=1
, and so
E
is an
\ell
-simplest curve, and
\ell^{n}
is a level of definition. Then, by Prop.
3.12
, the adelic index would be
1
, which contradicts (1). Hence such a level

relatively prime to
\ell
cannot exist.

Now suppose that
E
is not
\ell
-simplest, so that

, and

. By Theorem
5.4
, we know that

3.10
, we have that

, that is, the image is not defined at level

or

∎

6.
Image of Complex Conjugation

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve given by a short Weierstrass equation
y^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
for some
A,B\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
d
be a non-zero square-free integer, and let
\chi=\chi_{d}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\{\pm 1\}
be the quadratic character attached to the field
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{d})
, defined by
\chi(\sigma)=\sigma(\sqrt{d})/\sqrt{d}
. Let
E^{\chi}
be the twist of
E
by
\chi
, given by the Weierstrass equation
dy^{2}=x^{3}+Ax+B
(see
[
11
, Ch. X, Example 2.4]
).

The adelic Tate module
T(E)
is naturally a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-module, such that

(n\cdot\sigma)(P)=[n](\sigma(P))

for any
n\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
,
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
, and
P\in T(E)
. Moreover, following the construction of
E^{\chi}
as in
[
11
, Ch. X, Example 2.4]
, one sees that the adelic Tate module of
E^{\chi}
is isomorphic as a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-module to
T(E)
together with the action defined by

(n\cdot\sigma)(P)=[n]([\chi(\sigma)]\cdot\sigma(P))=[\chi(\sigma)\cdot n](\sigma(P)).

In particular, if we define
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\chi}
to be the
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-module
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
together with the action
n\cdot\alpha=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot n)\cdot\alpha
, for any
n,\alpha\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, then it follows that

(3)

\displaystyle\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\chi}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}T(E)\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}T(E^{\chi})\cong T(E^{\chi})

as
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}[G_{\mathbb{Q}}]
-modules, where the copy of
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
in the middle is supposed to be understood as a one-dimensional
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-module with trivial Galois action. Notice that one can write an analogous isomorphism
T(E)\cong\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}T(E)
of adelic Galois modules.

By rephrasing Eq. (
3
) in terms of Galois representations, with the identification of the adelic Galois modules
T(E)
,
T(E^{\chi})
, and
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}^{\chi}
with the adelic Galois representations
\rho_{E}
,
\rho_{E^{\chi}}
, and
\chi
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
E^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic twist of
E
by a quadratic character
\chi
. Let
\rho_{E}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(T(E))
, and
\rho_{E^{\chi}}
, be the adelic Galois representations attached to
E
and
E^{\chi}
, respectively, and let
\chi\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{Aut}(\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
be the quadratic adelic character given by
\chi(\sigma)(n)=(\sigma(\sqrt{d})/\sqrt{d})\cdot n
, for any
n\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. Then, there is an isomorphism of adelic Galois representations:

\rho_{E^{\chi}}\cong\chi\otimes_{\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}}\rho_{E}.

Now, we fix a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{P,Q\}
of
T(E)
and the corresponding
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\{\phi(P),\phi(Q)\}
of
T(E^{\chi})
induced by the isomorphism
\phi\colon E\to E^{\chi}
that sends
\phi((x_{0},y_{0}))=(x_{0},y_{0}/\sqrt{d})
, and let
\rho_{E}
and
\rho_{E^{\chi}}
be the adelic Galois representations
G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
with respect to such
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-bases. Noticing that for
R=(x_{0},y_{0})\in T(E)
and
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
we have

\displaystyle\sigma(\phi(R))

\displaystyle=\sigma((x_{0},y_{0}/\sqrt{d}))

\displaystyle=(\sigma(x_{0}),\sigma(y_{0}/\sqrt{d}))

\displaystyle=(\sigma(x_{0}),\chi(\sigma)\cdot\sigma(y_{0})/\sqrt{d})

\displaystyle=[\chi(\sigma)](\phi(\sigma(R))),

and applying this for
R\in\{P,Q\}
, the elements of the adelic basis of
T(E)
, it follows that

\rho_{E^{\chi}}(\sigma)=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E}(\sigma),

as matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Thus, we have shown the following result.

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve, and let
E^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic twist of
E
by a quadratic character
\chi
. Let
\rho_{E}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
and
\rho_{E^{\chi}}
be the adelic Galois representations attached to
E
and
E^{\chi}
, respectively, with respect to the adelic bases specified above. Then:

\rho_{E^{\chi}}(\sigma)=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E}(\sigma),

as matrices in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
, for any
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
.

While the proposition above works for any elliptic curve over
\mathbb{Q}
, we need a slightly more precise statement for our purposes of CM elliptic curves, to ensure that the adelic bases we specified above are compatible with the choices we made in Section
2
. We clarify this point in the following result.

Proposition 6.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with complex multiplication, and let
E^{\chi}/\mathbb{Q}
be a quadratic twist of
E
by a quadratic character
\chi
. Let
\rho_{E}\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
be the adelic Galois representation attached to
E
as specified in Section
2
, with respect to a
\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
-basis
\mathcal{B}=\{P,Q\}
such that
\rho_{E}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
. Let
\mathcal{B}_{\chi}=\{\phi(P),\phi(Q)\}
be the corresponding adelic basis of
T(E^{\chi})
. Then,
\rho_{E^{\chi}/K}(G_{K})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
and
\rho_{E^{\chi}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
as well.

Proof.

Suppose
E/\mathbb{Q}
is as in the statement and we have chosen the basis
\mathcal{B}=\{P,Q\}
such that
\rho_{E}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\subseteq\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
as in Section
2
. With respect to this basis, for
\sigma\in G_{K}=\operatorname{Gal}(\overline{K}/K)
, the matrix
\rho_{E}(\sigma)\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
corresponds to the multiplication-by-
\alpha
matrix of an element
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
such that
\sigma
induces the automorphism of
T(E)
that is induced by
[\alpha]\in\operatorname{End}(E)
, i.e.,

\sigma(P)=[\alpha](P),\ \text{ and }\ \sigma(Q)=[\alpha](Q),

and so
\sigma(R)=[\alpha](R)
for any
R\in T(E)
. Let
\phi\colon E\to E^{\chi}
be the isomorphism defined above. Our work in this section shows that
\sigma(\phi(R))=[\chi(\sigma)](\phi(\sigma(R))
for any
R\in T(E)
. Thus,

\sigma(\phi(R))=[\chi(\sigma)](\phi(\sigma(R))=[\chi(\sigma)](\phi([\alpha](R)))=[\chi(\sigma)][\alpha](\phi(R))=[\chi(\sigma)\alpha](\phi(R))

and therefore
\sigma
acts as
[\chi(\sigma)\alpha]
on
T(E^{\chi})
, which corresponds to the equality

\rho_{E^{\chi}}(\sigma)=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E}(\sigma).

In particular,
\rho_{E^{\chi}}(\sigma)
is the matrix of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
that represents multiplication by
\chi(\sigma)\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\otimes\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
. This shows that
\rho_{E^{\chi}/K}(G_{K})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
.

Alternatively, for
\sigma\in G_{K}
, we have
\rho_{E}(\sigma)=c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
for some
a,b\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
, and

\rho_{E^{\chi}}(\sigma)=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E}(\sigma)=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)=c_{\delta,\phi}(\chi(\sigma)\cdot a,\chi(\sigma)\cdot b),

which belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
. Further, if
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\rho_{E}(c)=n\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, then by the definition of the normalizer, we have
n=c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\cdot c_{1}
, for some
a,b\in\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}
and
c_{1}
as defined in Section
2
. Thus,

\rho_{E^{\chi}}(c)=(\chi(c)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E}(c)=(\chi(c)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\cdot c_{1}=c_{\delta,\phi}(\chi(\sigma)\cdot a,\chi(\sigma)\cdot b)\cdot c_{1}=c_{\delta,\phi}(a,b)\cdot c_{\chi(c)},

which belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
. (Note that
-\operatorname{Id}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, so
c_{1}
and
c_{-1}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
.) Hence,
\rho_{E}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, as desired.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
M>1
be an integer, let
E,E^{\prime}
be elliptic curves over
\mathbb{Q}
and suppose that their Galois representations
\rho_{E,M}
and
\rho_{E,M^{\prime}}
are conjugate. Let
\chi\colon G_{\mathbb{Q}}\to\mu_{2}
be a quadratic character. Then, the Galois representations
\chi\otimes\rho_{E,M}
and
\chi\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime},M}
(with tensor products being over
\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
) are also conjugate.

Proof.

Suppose that
\rho_{E,M}
and
\rho_{E^{\prime},M}
are conjugate Galois representations. This means that there is a matrix
B\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
such that
B\cdot\rho_{E,M}(\sigma)\cdot B^{-1}=\rho_{E^{\prime},M}(\sigma)
, for all
\sigma\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
. Then,

\displaystyle B\cdot(\chi\otimes\rho_{E,M})(\sigma)\cdot B^{-1}

\displaystyle=B\cdot((\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E,M}(\sigma))\cdot B^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot B\cdot\rho_{E,M}(\sigma)\cdot B^{-1}

\displaystyle=(\chi(\sigma)\cdot\operatorname{Id})\cdot\rho_{E^{\prime},M}(\sigma)=(\chi\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime},M})(\sigma).

Therefore,
\chi\otimes\rho_{E,M}
and
\chi\otimes\rho_{E^{\prime},M}
are conjugate Galois representations as well, as claimed.
∎

6.2.
Elements not in the Cartan

In order to compute a mod-
M
image, we will need to find both the elements of the image in the Cartan subgroup (see next section), and one image element that is not in the Cartan. The next result provides a way to construct such non-Cartan elements.

Proposition 6.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, with
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, and let
N\geq 1
be a level of definition for
G_{E}
. Suppose an adelic basis of
T(E)
has been chosen, as in Thm.
2.2
, so that
G_{E}=\rho_{E}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})\subseteq\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}
, and let
G_{E/K}=\rho_{E}(G_{K})
. Then:

(1)

G_{E/K}=\rho_{E}(G_{K})
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
. Moreover,
G_{E/K}=G_{E}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
.

(2)

Let
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
be an element that does not fix
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and let
C=\rho_{E}(c)\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).
Then,
C
belongs to
\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
, and
G_{E}=\langle C,G_{E/K}\rangle
.

(3)

Let
G_{E,N}=\pi_{N}(G_{E})
, let
G_{E/K,N}=\pi_{N}(G_{K})
and let
C_{N}\in G_{E,N}\setminus G_{E/K,N}
. Then,
G_{E,N}=\langle C_{N},G_{E/K,N}\rangle
and
G_{E}=\pi^{-1}_{N}(\langle C_{N},G_{E/K,N}\rangle)
.

Proof.

Let
E
and
N
, and a choice of adelic basis of
T(E)
, be as in the statement. Then Theorem
2.2
, part (2), shows that
G_{E/K,M}=G_{E,M}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
for any
M\geq 1
, and therefore the equality also holds adelically, i.e.,
G_{E/K}=G_{E}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
. In particular,
G_{E/K}=\rho_{E}(G_{K})
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
. This shows (1).

Now, let
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
be an element that does not fix
K/\mathbb{Q}
, and let
C=\rho_{E}(c)\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}}).
Since
c
does not fix
K
, it follows that

, and since
G_{E/K}=G_{E}\cap\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
, it follows that
C
is not in
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
. Hence,
C\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
, as claimed.

Moreover, since

7.
Finding the Image of Cartan

Now that we are able to determine the image of complex conjugation, we can turn to understanding the image of Cartan at a level where the adelic image is defined. To do this, we need the following group theoretic result.

Proposition 7.1
.

For each
i=1,2
, let
G_{i}
be an abelian group, let
H_{i}\subseteq G_{i}
be a subgroup of index
2
, let
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, and let
\pi_{i}\colon G\to G_{i}
be the natural projection to the
i

g_{i}\in G_{i}
be elements such that
G_{i}=\langle g_{i},H_{i}\rangle
. Then, the subgroup
\mathcal{C}=\langle(g_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle
is the unique subgroup of
G
with the following properties:

(1)

H_{1}\times H_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
,

(2)

\mathcal{C}
is of index
2
in
G
,

(3)

\pi_{i}(\mathcal{C})=G_{i}
for

, and

(4)

\mathcal{C}\cap(H_{1}\times G_{2})=\mathcal{C}\cap(G_{1}\times H_{2})
is of index
4

G

Proof.

Let
G
,
\mathcal{C}
,
G_{i}
,
H_{i}
,
g_{i}
, and
\pi_{i}
be as in the statement of the proposition. We shall prove that
\mathcal{C}
verifies conditions (1), (2), (3), and (4), and
\mathcal{C}
is the unique such subgroup satisfying all four conditions.

First note that
H_{1}\times H_{2}
is of index
4
in
G=G_{1}\times G_{2}
, and in fact
G/(H_{1}\times H_{2})\cong\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z}
. By the fourth (or lattice) isomorphism theorem, there are precisely
5
subgroups of
G
containing
H_{1}\times H_{2}
, namely:

H_{1}\times H_{2},\langle(g_{1},e_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle,\langle(e_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle,\langle(g_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle,G.

Of these,

itself is of index
1
,
H_{1}\times H_{2}
is of index
4
, and the other
3
are of index
2
. Since
\pi_{i}((g_{1},g_{2}))=g_{i}
, and
H_{1}\times H_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, it follows that
\pi_{i}(\mathcal{C})=G_{i}
. Finally,

\mathcal{C}\cap(H_{1}\times G_{2})=\mathcal{C}\cap(G_{1}\times H_{2})=H_{1}\times H_{2},

and so
\mathcal{C}
as defined in the statement satisfies (1), (2), (3), and (4).

Now let
\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
be another subgroup of
G
that satisfies (1), (2), (3), and (4). By (1), we have
H_{1}\times H_{2}\subseteq\mathcal{C}^{\prime}
. By (2) and our arguments above, we have

\mathcal{C}^{\prime}=\langle(g_{1},e_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle,\ \langle(e_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle,\text{ or }\langle(g_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle

but of these three possibilities there is only one subgroup that satisfies conditions (3) and (4), namely
\langle(g_{1},g_{2}),H_{1}\times H_{2}\rangle
.
∎

In the following result we verify that the Chinese remainder theorem ‘‘commutes’’ with the connecting projection maps among Cartan subgroups.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
M,N
be relatively prime integers, and let
\delta,\phi
be fixed. Then, the isomorphism

\phi\colon\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N)\to\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(MN)

given by the Chinese remainder theorem preserves our choices of bases. In other words,

\phi((\pi_{M}(g),\pi_{N}(g)))=\pi_{MN}(g)

for any
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
.

Proof.

Let
g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}
be an adelic element of the Cartan subgroup, and consider
g_{MN}=\pi_{MN}(g)
,
g_{M}=\pi_{M}(g)
, and
g_{N}=\pi_{N}(g)
. Since our choices of bases are compatible under reduction maps, it follows that

\pi_{M}(\pi_{MN}(g))=\pi_{M}(g)\ \text{ and }\ \pi_{N}(\pi_{MN}(g))=\pi_{N}(g),

i.e.,

\pi_{MN,M}(g_{MN})=g_{M}\ \text{ and }\ \pi_{MN,N}(g_{MN})=g_{N}.

Now, by definition of the Chinese remainder isomorphism,
\phi((g_{M},g_{N}))
is the unique matrix

modulo
MN
such that

and
h\equiv g_{N}\bmod N
. Since
g_{MN}
satisfies these properties, and by the uniqueness of

, as desired.

\mathbb{Q}
) to some curve in Table
1
, then its image can be determined as per Theorem
4.1
. An explicit algorithm to determine this image is described in
[
9
, Section 12]
. Thus, we may assume
E
is not
\ell
-simplest from now on.

(4)

By Lemma
4.9
, there is a non-zero square-free integer
N
such that
E^{\prime}=E^{N}
is an
\ell
-simplest curve such that
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
(note that
N\neq 1
since
E
is assumed to be non-simplest). In order to determine
N
, we first find all indices
i\in I
such that
A\equiv A_{i}\bmod(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{2}
and
B\equiv B_{i}\bmod(\mathbb{Q}^{\times})^{3}
. Then we find the index
i\in I
, and a non-zero square-free
N
such that
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
and the twist
E^{N}
given by
y^{2}=x^{3}+N^{2}Ax+N^{3}B
is isomorphic to the
\ell
-simplest CM curve
E^{\prime}:y^{2}=x^{3}+A_{i}x+B_{i}
.

(5)

Let
n=n_{E,\ell}
be defined as in Remark
4.8
. By Corollary
5.5
, the integer
M=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
is an adelic level of definition for the image of
E
. (Note: Prop.
3.13
shows that the minimal level of definition
M_{E}
is a divisor of
M
, and by Cor.
5.6
the level
M_{E}
is divisible by
\ell
.)

(6)

Let
M:=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
. Proposition
6.6
shows that
G_{E,M}=\langle C,G_{E/K,M}\rangle
, where
C:=\rho_{E,M}(c)
for any element
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
that does not fix
K
and
G_{E/K,M}
is the image of
\rho_{E/K,M}\colon G_{K}\to\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

(7)

By Thm.
2.4
(a) or Thm.
2.6
, depending on whether
\ell>2
or
\ell=2
, the image
\rho_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}(G_{\mathbb{Q}})
contains an element of the form
c_{\varepsilon}
(see Section
2
). We remark here that
[
3
]
identifies a precise element
c_{\varepsilon}
in the mod-
\ell^{n}
image. In other words, there is
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
c_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}(c)
. Moreover,
c
does not fix
K
, i.e.,
c_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
. Since the adelic image of
E^{N}
is defined modulo
\ell^{n}
, we can choose any lift
c_{\varepsilon,M}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
of
c_{\varepsilon}\in\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
and so there is an element
c^{\prime}\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
such that
\rho_{E^{N},M}(c^{\prime})=c_{\varepsilon,M}
, and
c^{\prime}
does not fix
K
(because
c
does not, and
c^{\prime}
is equal to
c
when we restrict to the action on
\ell^{n}
-torsion). Finally, it follows from Prop.
6.2
that
\rho_{E,M}(c^{\prime})=(\chi_{N}(c^{\prime})\cdot\Id)\cdot\rho_{E^{N},M}(c^{\prime})
, where
\chi_{N}
is the quadratic character such that
E^{N}=E^{\chi_{N}}
. We let
C=\rho_{E,M}(c^{\prime})
.

(8)

From Theorem
7.3
, if
H_{A}
is a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(A)
corresponding to
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[A])/K(\sqrt{N}))
and
g_{A}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(A)\setminus H_{A}
for
A\in\{\ell^{n},N^{\dagger}\}
, then
G_{E/K,M}=\langle(g_{\ell^{n}},g_{N^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}\rangle
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)\cong\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
.

(9)

By Prop.
8.2
, we have
H_{\ell^{n}}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{N}/K,\ell^{n}}
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
, and by Prop.
8.3
, we have

H_{N^{\dagger}}=\{g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger}):\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{N}^{N^{\dagger}}\},

with
\mathcal{A}_{N}^{N^{\dagger}}
as in (
4
).

(10)

Note, from the proof of Theorem
7.3
, that
G_{E/K,M}=\langle(g_{\ell^{n}},g_{N^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}\rangle
is the unique subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
satisfying

(a)

H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}\subseteq G_{E/K,M}
,

(b)

[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E/K,M}]=2
,

(c)

G_{E/K,M}\bmod{\ell^{n}}=\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
and
G_{E/K,M}\bmod{N^{\dagger}}=\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
, and

(d)

G_{E/K,M}\cap(H_{\ell^{n}}\times\mathcal{C}(N^{\dagger}))=G_{E/K,M}\cap(\mathcal{C}(\ell^{n})\times H_{N^{\dagger}})=H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}

(11)

It is thus sufficient to compute
G_{E/K,M}
by realizing
H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}
,
H_{\ell^{n}}\times\mathcal{C}(N^{\dagger})
, and
\mathcal{C}(\ell^{n})\times H_{N^{\dagger}}
as subgroups of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
via the Chinese remainder theorem (see Prop.
7.2
) and then finding the unique subgroup
G_{E/K,M}
satisfying conditions (a)-(d) above.

(12)

Together, we finally compute
G_{E,M}=\langle C,G_{E/K,M}\rangle
, with
C=\rho_{E,M}(c^{\prime})
as above.

This work justifies the following algorithm, whose implementation completes the proof of Theorem
8.1
.

Algorithm 8.4
.

Input:

An elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
.

Output:

A Galois image
G_{E,M}:=\im\rho_{E,M}
where
M
is an adelic level of definition for
E
.

(1)

Compute a non-zero square-free integer
N
such that
E^{N}
is an
\ell
-simplest CM curve for a prime
\ell
.

(2)

If
N\in\{1,-\ell\}
and
\ell
is odd, or if
N\in\{\pm 1,\pm 2\}
and
\ell=2
, return
G_{E,\ell^{n}}
where
n:=n_{E,\ell}
as in Remark
4.8
. Otherwise, continue to (3).

(3)

Compute
N^{\dagger}
and
n:=n_{E,\ell}
as in Remark
4.8
, and let
M:=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
.

(4)

Compute
H_{\ell^{n}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
and
H_{N^{\dagger}}\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
as in Theorem
7.3
, and compute
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M).

(5)

Compute
H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}
,
H_{\ell^{n}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
, and
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})\times H_{N^{\dagger}}
as subgroups of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
via the Chinese remainder theorem.

(6)

For
g_{\ell^{n}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})\setminus H_{\ell^{n}}
and
g_{N^{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})\setminus H_{N^{\dagger}}
do:

(a)

Compute
\mathcal{C}:=\langle(g_{\ell^{n}},g_{N^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell^{n}}\times H_{N^{\dagger}}\rangle
as a subgroup of
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
via CRT.

(b)

Check if
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M):\mathcal{C}]=2
.

(c)

Check if
\mathcal{C}\bmod\ell^{n}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
and
\mathcal{C}\bmod N^{\dagger}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
.

(d)

Check if
\mathcal{C}\cap(H_{\ell^{n}}\times\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger}))=\mathcal{C}\cap(\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})\times H_{N^{\dagger}})
.

(7)

If all properties are satisfied, define
G_{E/K,M}:=\mathcal{C}
. Otherwise return to (6).

(8)

For
c\in G_{\mathbb{Q}}
with
c_{\varepsilon}=\rho_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}(c)
, compute
C:=(\chi_{N}(c)\cdot\Id)\cdot c_{\varepsilon}
, and compute
C_{M}
as a lift of
C
modulo
M
.

(9)

Return
G_{E,M}:=\langle C_{M},G_{E/K,M}\rangle
.

An implementation of this algorithm in
Magma
can be found in the GitHub repository
[
5
]
.

Remark 8.5
.

For an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, let
M=\ell^{n_{E,\ell}}N^{\dagger}
be the adelic level of definition for
E
found using Algorithm
8.4
. We note that
M
is not always the minimal level of definition, and in particular, if
\ell
or
N^{\dagger}
is even, we may find that the minimal adelic level of definition is a proper divisor of
M
. We illustrate these possibilities as follows.

Example 8.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-99x-378
be the elliptic curve with LMFDB label
288.d1
. This curve has CM by
\mathbb{Z}[2i]
, the order of conductor
2
in
K=\mathbb{Q}(i)
, which has discriminant
\Delta_{K}f^{2}=-16
. The elliptic curve
E^{-3}/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-11x+14
is a
2
-simplest CM curve with label
32.a2
. Thus, since
(-3)^{\dagger}=3
, we find that the adelic image of
E
is defined at level
2^{4}\cdot 3=48
.

We can then compute
G_{E,48}
via Algorithm
8.4
. However, reducing this image modulo
12
, we find that
[\mathcal{N}_{-4,0}(12):G_{E,12}]=2
, so by Theorem
3.6
, the adelic image of
E
is defined at level
12=2^{2}\cdot 3
. In this case, the adelic level of definition produced by our algorithm is not minimal because
[\mathcal{N}_{-4,0}(4):G_{E^{-3},4}]=2
, so the
2
-adic image of
E^{-3}
is defined at level
4
.

Example 8.7
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}=x^{3}-595x+5586
be the elliptic curve with LMFDB label
784.f3
. This curve has CM by
\mathbb{Z}[\sqrt{-7}]
, the order of conductor
2
in
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{-7})
, which has discriminant
\Delta_{K}f^{2}=-28
. The elliptic curve
E^{-1}/\mathbb{Q}:y^{2}+xy=x^{3}-x^{2}-37x-78
is a
7
-simplest CM curve with label
49.a3
. Thus, since
(-1)^{\dagger}=4
, the adelic image of
E
is defined at level
7\cdot 4=28
by Algorithm
8.4
.

We can compute
G_{E,28}
via Algorithm
8.4
. However, reducing this image modulo
14
, we find that
[\mathcal{N}_{-7,0}(14):G_{E,14}]=2
, so by Theorem
3.6
, the adelic image of
E
is defined at level
14=7\cdot 2
. In this case, the adelic level of definition produced by our algorithm is not minimal because there is an entanglement between the
2
- and
7
-division fields of
E
, rather than just between the
4
- and
7
-division fields of
E
. This occurs because
\mathbb{Q}(E[2])=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})
, and since
\sqrt{-1}
and
\sqrt{-7}
are contained in
\mathbb{Q}(E[7])
by Lemma
5.3
and Thm.
2.8
, respectively, we also have
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{7})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[7])
.

Remark 8.8
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, and let
M:=\ell^{n_{E,\ell}}N^{\dagger}
be the adelic level of definition for

found using Algorithm

. From the examples above, it is clear that we can determine the minimal level of definition for

by finding the smallest divisor

9.
Levels of Differentiation

In Definition
3.15
, we define an
adelic level of differentiation
for an elliptic curve
E/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
to be a positive integer
M\geq 2
such that
M
is an adelic level of definition for
E
, and if
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}(j_{K,f})
is another elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, then
G_{E}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
only if
G_{E,M}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

For
E/\mathbb{Q}
with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j(E)\neq 0,1728
, we have shown that if
N
is a non-zero square-free integer such that
E^{N}
is an
\ell
-simplest CM curve where
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
, then
M:=\ell^{n_{E,\ell}}N^{\dagger}
is an adelic level of definition for
E
(see Rem.
4.8
for notation). We wish to show, further, that
M
is an adelic level of differentiation as well.

We first wish to describe the structure of
N^{\dagger}
for
N
non-zero and square-free.

Lemma 9.1
.

Let
N
be a non-zero, square-free integer, and consider
N^{\dagger}
as in Remark
4.8
. Then
N^{\dagger}=2^{k}\cdot N^{\prime}
where
N^{\prime}:=(N^{\dagger})^{\text{sf}}
is the odd, square-free part of
N^{\dagger}
, and
k\in\{0,2,3\}
.

More specifically,

•

N^{\dagger}=N^{\prime}
if
N\equiv 1\bmod 4
, or

•

N^{\dagger}=4\cdot N^{\prime}
if
N\equiv 3\bmod 4
, or

•

N^{\dagger}=8\cdot N^{\prime}
if
N\equiv 2\bmod 4
.

Proof.

In Theorem
2.8
, we defined

N^{\dagger}=\disc(\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N})})=\begin{cases}|N|&\text{ if }N\equiv 1\bmod 4,\\
|4N|&\text{ if }N\equiv 2,3\bmod 4.\\
\end{cases}

The properties follow by inspection of the different cases for
N
.

∎

We now prove that the level of definition produced by Algorithm
8.4
shares all prime divisors with the minimal adelic level of definition.

Proposition 9.2
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
with
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. Let
N
be a non-zero square-free integer such that
E^{N}
is a simplest CM curve for the prime
\ell
where
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
, and let
n:=n_{E,\ell}
, so
M:=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
is an adelic level of definition for
E
. If
M_{E}
is the minimal level of definition for
E
, then
p\mid M_{E}
if and only if
p\mid M
for all primes
p
.

Proof.

Since
M_{E}\mid M
by Prop
3.13
, we suppose towards a contradiction that there is a prime
p
such that
p\mid M
but
p\nmid M_{E}
. Since
\ell
divides all levels of definition by Cor.
5.6
,
p\neq\ell
. In particular,
p\mid N^{\dagger}
since
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
.

Let
M_{E}=\ell^{m}N^{\prime}
with
1\leq m\leq n
and
N^{\prime}\mid N^{\dagger}
. If
N^{\prime}=1
, then
M_{E}=\ell^{m}
, so
E
is a simplest CM curve for the prime
\ell
. Since
E^{N}
is also an
\ell
-simplest CM curve, and
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
, we have
E^{N}\cong E
and
N=1
by Cor.
4.4
. Thus
M=\ell^{n}
, a contradiction.

Assume
N^{\prime}>1
. Define
d:=\nu_{p}(N^{\dagger})
, the
p
-adic valuation of
N^{\dagger}
, and define
P:=N^{\dagger}/p^{d}
. Note that we must have
N^{\prime}\mid P
and
P>1
. Also, by Lemma
9.1
,
p^{d}\geq 3
. Since
M_{E}=\ell^{m}N^{\prime}
is a level of definition for
E
and
M_{E}\mid\ell^{n}P
, it follows that
\ell^{n}P
is a level of definition for
E
as well. Therefore
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n}P):G_{E,\ell^{n}P}]=2
.

By Prop.
3.9
, we have that
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n}P):G_{E/K,\ell^{n}P}]=2
. However, since
P\mid N^{\dagger}
and
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger}):G_{E,N^{\dagger}}]=1
, we have
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P):G_{E/K,P}]=[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(P):G_{E,P}]=1
. Since we also have
[\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n}):G_{E/K,\ell^{n}}]=1
, that means that
G_{E/K,\ell^{n}P}
is an index
2
subgroup of
G_{E/K,\ell^{n}}\times G_{E/K,P}
. By Cor.
3.10
,
[\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{n}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[P]):K]=2
. Further, by Theorem
5.4
, we have
K(\sqrt{N})=\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{n}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[N^{\dagger}])
, and since
\mathbb{Q}(E[P])\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[N^{\dagger}])
, it must be that
K(\sqrt{N})=\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell^{n}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
.

We now wish to show that
\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])
and
\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
have a non-trivial entanglement. If
p
is odd, then
d=1
, and there is a choice of
p^{\ast}\in\{\pm p\}
such that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{\ast}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[p])=\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])
, and
(N/p^{\ast})^{\dagger}=P
. If
p=2
, then
d\geq 2
, and there is a choice of
p^{\ast}\in\{-1,2,-2\}
such that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{\ast}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{p^{d}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])
and
(N/p^{\ast})^{\dagger}=P
.

In either case, since
(N/p^{\ast})^{\dagger}=P
, it must be that
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N/p^{\ast}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{P})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
, and therefore
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N}),\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N/p^{\ast}}),
and
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{p^{\ast}})
are contained in
\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
. Further, since
p^{d}\geq 3
, we have that
K\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
, and so
K(\sqrt{p^{\ast}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[P])
. The fact that
K(\sqrt{p^{\ast}})/K
is a quadratic extension follows because
\ell
is the unique prime dividing
\Delta_{K}=\disc(\mathcal{O}_{K})
, and
\ell\neq p
.

This means that
[G_{E/K,p^{d}}\times G_{E/K,P}:G_{E/K,N^{\dagger}}]=[\mathbb{Q}(E[p^{d}])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[P]):K]\geq 2
. However,
G_{E/K,N^{\dagger}}\cong\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(N^{\dagger})
, so
[G_{E/K,p^{d}}\times G_{E/K,P}:G_{E/K,N^{\dagger}}]=1
, a contradiction.

∎

We next prove that, for the cases we wish to consider, conjugation at the levevl of the normalizer implies conjugation at the level of the Cartan.

Proposition 9.3
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, let
m\in\mathbb{Z}
be the square-free integer such that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
, and suppose
M>2
is a positive integer divisible by
|\Delta_{K}|=m^{\dagger}
.

Let
G_{E,M}:=\im\rho_{E,M}
be the mod-
M
Galois image of
E
and let
G_{E/K,M}:=\im\rho_{E/K,M}
. Using notation as in Prop.
8.3
, we have that

G_{E/K,M}=\{g\in G_{E,M}:\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}^{M}_{m}\}.

Proof.

Note that
K\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(E[M])
by Theorem
2.8
. By hypothesis,
G_{E,M}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[M])/\mathbb{Q})
and
G_{E/K,M}\cong\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[M])/K)
. Further, it follows that

\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[M])/\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M}))\cong G_{E,M}\cap\operatorname{SL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})=\{g\in G_{E,M}:\det(g)=1\},

and hence
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[M])/K)\cong\{g\in G_{E,M}:\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{m}^{M}\}
by Galois theory. Therefore
G_{E/K,M}=\{g\in G_{E,M}:\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{m}^{M}\}
as desired.

∎

Corollary 9.4
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
and
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be elliptic curves with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
, and let
M>2
be a positive integer divisible by, but not equal to,
|\Delta_{K}|
. If
G_{E,M}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
, then
G_{E/K,M}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Let
m
be the square-free integer such that
K=\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{m})
. Since
K/\mathbb{Q}
is a quadratic extension and
M\neq|\Delta_{K}|
, it follows that
\mathcal{A}_{m}^{M}
is an index
2
subgroup of
\det(G_{E,M})=\det(G_{E^{\prime},M})
by Galois theory. By Proposition
6.6
, we can choose
C_{M}\in G_{E,M}\setminus G_{E/K,M}
and
C^{\prime}_{M}\in G_{E^{\prime},M}\setminus G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
such that
G_{E,M}=\langle C_{M},G_{E/K,M}\rangle
and
G_{E^{\prime},M}=\langle C^{\prime}_{M},G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}\rangle
, and by Prop.
9.3
, we can make our choice so that
\det(C_{M})
and
\det(C^{\prime}_{M})
are not contained in
\mathcal{A}_{m}^{M}
. Therefore any conjugation sending
G_{E,M}
to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
must also send
G_{E/K,M}
to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
, since conjugation preserves determinants.
∎

In the following two propositions we show that images resulting from certain twists of simplest CM curves do not result in conjugate images.

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
and
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be elliptic curves with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and
j_{K,f}\neq 0,1728
. Let
\ell
be the unique prime dividing
\Delta_{K}
, and suppose that
\ell\neq 2
.

Let
P:=-2\cdot N
and
P^{\prime}:=2\cdot N
for
N
a square-free integer satisfying
N\equiv 1\bmod 4
.

If the twists
E^{P}
and
(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}
are
\ell
-simplest CM curves such that
\ell
is relatively prime to both
P^{\dagger}
and
(P^{\prime})^{\dagger}
, then for
M:=\ell P^{\dagger}
,
G_{E,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

Note that
P^{\dagger}=(P^{\prime})^{\dagger}=8N^{\dagger}
by Lemma
9.1
,
n_{E,\ell}=1
by Rem.
4.8
, and by Cor.
5.5
,
M=8\ell N^{\dagger}
is an adelic level of definition for both
E
and
E^{\prime}
.

By Theorem
5.4
, we have that

\mathbb{Q}(E[\ell])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E[8N^{\dagger}])=K(\sqrt{-2N})\quad\text{ and }\quad\mathbb{Q}(E^{\prime}[\ell])\cap\mathbb{Q}(E^{\prime}[8N^{\dagger}])=K(\sqrt{2N}).

We first note that, since
\ell\neq 2
and
E^{P}
and
(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}
have non-maximal
\ell
-adic images by hypothesis, the classification in Theorem
2.4
(a) shows that

G_{E^{P}/K,\ell}\cong G_{(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}/K,\ell}\cong J_{\delta,0}=\left\langle\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{2}&b\\
\delta b&a^{2}\\
\end{array}\right):a\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times},b\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\right\}\right\rangle

By Thm.
7.3
and from the discussion in Section
8.1
, we have that

G_{E/K,M}\cong\langle(g_{\ell},g_{P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}\rangle

and

G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}\cong\langle(g_{\ell},g_{P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N}\rangle,

where
H_{\ell}=J_{\delta,0}
,
H_{P^{\dagger}}^{m}=\{g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger}):\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{m}^{P^{\dagger}}\}

for
m=\pm 2N
, and where we choose
g_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell)\setminus H_{\ell}
and
g_{P^{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger})\setminus(H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}\cup H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N})
.

Note also that
\mathcal{A}_{2N}^{P^{\dagger}}\neq\mathcal{A}_{-2N}^{P^{\dagger}}
, and since conjugation preserves determinants, we cannot have
H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N}
conjugate to
H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/P^{\dagger}\mathbb{Z})
.

Suppose, towards a contradiction, that
G_{E/K,M}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
. Then there are matrices
B_{\ell}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})
and
B_{P^{\dagger}}\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/P^{\dagger}\mathbb{Z})
such that

(B_{\ell},B_{P^{\dagger}})\langle(g_{\ell},g_{P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}\rangle(B_{\ell},B_{P^{\dagger}})^{-1}=\langle(g_{\ell},g_{P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N}\rangle.

Notice that, if such a conjugation exists, then we may choose
B_{\ell}=\Id
.

Since
g_{P^{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger})\setminus(H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}\cup H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N})
, we can choose
g_{P^{+}}
such that
\det(g_{P^{\dagger}})\notin\mathcal{A}_{2N}^{P^{\dagger}}\cup\mathcal{A}_{-2N}^{P^{\dagger}}
. Hence
B_{P^{\dagger}}g_{P^{\dagger}}B_{P^{\dagger}}^{-1}
is not in
H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N}
, so
B_{\dagger}H_{P^{\dagger}}^{-2N}B_{\dagger}^{-1}=H_{P^{\dagger}}^{2N}
, a contradiction.

Therefore
G_{E/K,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
and by Corollary
9.4
,
G_{E,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

∎

Proposition 9.6
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
and
E^{\prime}/\mathbb{Q}
be elliptic curves with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and assume
j_{K,f}\neq 0,1728
. Denote by
\ell
the unique prime dividing
\Delta_{K}
, and assume
\ell\neq 2
.

Let
P:=-N
and
P^{\prime}\in\{\pm 2N\}
for
N
a square-free integer satisfying
N\equiv 1\bmod 4
, and suppose that

•

E^{P}
and
(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}
are
\ell
-simplest CM curves, and

•

\gcd(\ell,P^{\dagger})=1
.

Then for
M:=\ell P^{\dagger}
,
G_{E,M}
and
G_{E^{\prime},M}
are not conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof.

From Lemma
9.1
,
M=\ell P^{\dagger}=4\ell N^{\dagger}
and
(P^{\prime})^{\dagger}=8N^{\dagger}
so
\gcd(\ell,(P^{\prime})^{\dagger})=1
. By Rem.
4.8
,
n_{E,\ell}=1
, and by Cor.
5.5
,
M
and
2M
are adelic levels of definition for
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively. If
G_{E^{\prime},M}\cong\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
then we are done, so we assume that
M
is also a level of definition for
E^{\prime}
, hence
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E^{\prime},M}]=2
.

Since
\ell\neq 2
and
E^{P}
and
(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}
have non-maximal
\ell
-adic images by hypothesis, it follows from Theorem
2.4
(a) that

G_{E^{P}/K,\ell}\cong G_{(E^{\prime})^{P^{\prime}}/K,\ell}\cong J_{\delta,0}=\left\langle\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a^{2}&b\\
\delta b&a^{2}\\
\end{array}\right):a\in(\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z})^{\times},b\in\mathbb{Z}/\ell\mathbb{Z}\right\}\right\rangle.

Then by Thm.
7.3
and from the discussion in Section
8.1
, we have that

G_{E/K,M}\cong\langle(g_{\ell},g_{P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{P^{\dagger}}\rangle\quad\text{ and }\quad G_{E^{\prime}/K,2M}\cong\langle(g_{\ell},g_{2P^{\dagger}}),H_{\ell}\times H_{2P^{\dagger}}\rangle,

where
H_{\ell}=J_{\delta,0}
,
H_{P^{\dagger}}=\{g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger}):\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{P}^{P^{\dagger}}\}
, and
H_{2P^{\dagger}}=\{g\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(2P^{\dagger}):\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{P^{\prime}}^{2P^{\dagger}}\}
for some
g_{\ell}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell)\setminus H_{\ell}
,
g_{P^{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger})\setminus H_{P^{\dagger}}
, and
g_{2P^{\dagger}}\in\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(2P^{\dagger})\setminus H_{2P^{\dagger}}
.

For ease of notation, we let
\pi\colon\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(2M)\to\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
and
\pi^{\prime}\colon\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(2P^{\dagger})\to\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(P^{\dagger})
denote the natural projection maps between their respective Cartan subgroups.

By Proposition
7.2
, we have

\pi(G_{E^{\prime}/K,2M})=G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}\cong\langle(g_{\ell},\pi^{\prime}(g_{2P^{\dagger}})),H_{\ell}\times\pi^{\prime}(H_{2P^{\dagger}})\rangle.

Since
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8}),\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N^{\dagger}})\subseteq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8N^{\dagger}})
, by Theorem
2.8
,
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{P},\sqrt{P^{\prime}})\subsetneq\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8N^{\dagger}})
, so there exists a choice of
\sigma\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{8N^{\dagger}})/\mathbb{Q})
such that
\sigma(\sqrt{P^{\prime}})=\sqrt{P^{\prime}}
but
\sigma(\sqrt{P})\neq\sqrt{P}
. Thus, there is an element
B\in H_{2P^{\dagger}}
such that
\det(\pi^{\prime}(B))\notin\det(H_{P^{\dagger}})=\mathcal{A}_{P}^{P^{\dagger}}
. Further, we can choose
g_{P^{\dagger}}
such that
\det(g_{P^{\dagger}})\notin\mathcal{A}_{P}^{P^{\dagger}}
, and
g_{2P^{\dagger}}
such that
\det(\pi^{\prime}(g_{2P^{\dagger}}))\notin\det(\pi^{\prime}(H_{2P^{\dagger}}))
.

Then, by arguments similar to those found in the proof of Proposition
9.5
, it follows that
G_{E/K,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
, and therefore
G_{E,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
by Cor.
9.4
.

∎

We conclude with the main result of this section.

Theorem 9.7
.

Let
E/\mathbb{Q}
be an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
with
j(E)\neq 0,1728
. Let
N
be a non-zero square-free integer such that
E^{N}
is a simplest CM curve for the prime
\ell
where
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=1
, and let
n:=n_{E,\ell}
. Then
\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
is an adelic level of differentiation for
E
.

Proof.

Let
M:=\ell^{n}N^{\dagger}
be defined as above, which is the level of definition for
E
as in Algoritgm
8.4
. Suppose that
E^{\prime}
is an elliptic curve with CM by
\mathcal{O}_{K,f}
and that
G_{E,M}
and
G_{E^{\prime},M}
are conjugate as subgroups of
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
. We wish to prove that
E\cong_{\mathbb{Q}}E^{\prime}
, which implies
G_{E}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\widehat{\mathbb{Z}})
.

Since
M
is an adelic level of definition for
E
, by Thm
8.1
part (1), we have that
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E,M}]=2
, and hence
[\mathcal{N}_{\delta,\phi}(M):G_{E^{\prime},M}]=2
. So
M
is also an adelic level of definition for
E^{\prime}
by Thm.
3.6
. By a similar argument, if
m
is an adelic level of definition for either
E
or
E^{\prime}
, and
m\mid M
, then
m
is an adelic level of definition for both
E
and
E^{\prime}
. In particular, if
M_{E}
and
M_{E^{\prime}}
denote the minimal levels of definition for
E
and
E^{\prime}
, respectively, then
M_{E}=M_{E^{\prime}}
.

Suppose we have
M^{\prime}:=\ell^{n}P^{\dagger}
for a non-zero square-free integer
P
, where
\gcd(\ell,P^{\dagger})=1
and
(E^{\prime})^{P}
is an
\ell
-simplest CM curve. Then
M^{\prime}
is a level of definition for
E^{\prime}
by Cor.
5.5
.

From Lemma
9.2
, it follows that
M
,
M^{\prime}
, and
M_{E}=M_{E^{\prime}}
share all prime divisors. In particular, by Lemma
9.1
, if
N^{\prime}
and
P^{\prime}
are the odd, square-free parts of
N^{\dagger}
and
P^{\dagger}
, respectively, then
N^{\prime}=P^{\prime}
and
N^{\prime}\equiv 1\bmod 4
. Further, since
P^{\dagger}
and
N^{\dagger}
share all prime divisors, we are in one of three cases. Without loss of generality, we have that

(1)

N=P
and
N^{\dagger}=P^{\dagger}
, or

(2)

N\neq P
and
N^{\dagger}=P^{\dagger}=8N^{\prime}
, or

(3)

N\neq P
,
N^{\dagger}=4N^{\prime}
, and
P^{\dagger}=8N^{\prime}
.

In the first case, if
N=P=1
, then
N^{\dagger}=P^{\dagger}=1
,
E=E^{N}
and
E^{\prime}=(E^{\prime})^{P}
are both
\ell
-simplest CM curves, and
M=\ell^{n}
. Then
G_{E,\ell^{n}}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},\ell^{n}}
, so it follows from Theorem
4.3
(2e) that
E\cong_{\mathbb{Q}}E^{\prime}
, as desired.

In the second case, we can assume, without loss of generality, that
N=2N^{\prime}
and
P=-2N^{\prime}
, and in the third case, we can show that
N=-N^{\prime\prime}
and
P=\pm 2N^{\prime\prime}
for some positive, square-free integer
N^{\prime\prime}\equiv 1\bmod 4
. Note that in both the second and third cases,
N^{\dagger}
and
P^{\dagger}
are even, so
\ell\neq 2
since
\gcd(\ell,N^{\dagger})=\gcd(\ell,P^{\dagger})=1
.

Thus, the second and third cases are those described in Propositions
9.5
and
9.6
, respectively. In either case, we have shown that
G_{E,M}
is not conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
, which contradicts our hypothesis.

Thus, we assume
N=P\neq 1
and
N^{\dagger}=P^{\dagger}
, so
M=M^{\prime}
is a level of definition for both curves, and the steps of Algorithm
8.4
can be applied to construct the Galois image modulo
M
of both curves.

Recall that we assume
G_{E,M}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
. For
C\in\{E,E^{\prime}\}
, we define
G_{C,M,N}:=\{g\in G_{C,M}:\det(g)\in\mathcal{A}_{N}^{M}\}
, the subgroup of
G_{C,M}
isomorphic to
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(C[M])/\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N}))
. We also define
G_{C/K,M,N}:=G_{C,M,N}\cap G_{C/K,M}
, which is isomorphic to
\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(C[M])/K(\sqrt{N}))
. Note that
G_{E,M,N}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime},M,N}
and
G_{E/K,M,N}
is conjugate to
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M,N}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
since determinants are preserved by conjugation (see Proposition
9.3
).

Proposition
8.2
shows that
G_{E/K,M,N}\bmod{\ell^{n}}\cong G_{E^{N}/K,\ell^{n}}
and
G_{E^{\prime}/K,M,N}\bmod{\ell^{n}}\cong G_{(E^{\prime})^{N}/K,\ell^{n}}
, which are conjugate in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
since conjugation is preserved by reduction.

Now, let
\sigma_{E}\in\operatorname{Gal}(\mathbb{Q}(E[M])/\mathbb{Q})
be an automorphism that does not fix
K
and does not fix
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N})
. Under an appropriate choice of basis, this corresponds to an element
g_{E}:=\rho_{E,M}(\sigma_{E})\in G_{E,M}
that is not in
G_{E,M,N}
or in
\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
. Note that, since
\sigma_{E}
does not fix
\mathbb{Q}(\sqrt{N})
,
\chi_{N}(\sigma_{E})=-1
, where
\chi_{N}
is the quadratic character corresponding to the twist
E^{N}=E^{\chi_{N}}
. Thus, by Proposition
6.2
, we have that
(\chi_{N}(\sigma_{E})\cdot\Id)\cdot g_{E}=-\Id\cdot g_{E}\in G_{E^{N},M}
.

Now, let
g_{E,\ell^{n}}:=\pi_{M,\ell^{n}}(-\Id\cdot g_{E})
. Note that, since
g_{E}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
,
g_{E,\ell^{n}}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
, and therefore
G_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}=\langle g_{E,\ell},G_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}\rangle
by Proposition
6.6
.

Let
B\in\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})
be a conjugation matrix sending
G_{E,M}
to
G_{E^{\prime},M}
. We define
g_{E^{\prime}}:=Bg_{E}B^{-1}\in G_{E^{\prime},M}
, and note that
g_{E^{\prime}}
must also satisfy
g_{E^{\prime}}\notin G_{E^{\prime},M,N}
and
g_{E^{\prime}}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(M)
. Thus, for
g_{E^{\prime},\ell^{n}}:=\pi_{M,\ell^{n}}(g_{E^{\prime}})
, we have that
g_{E,\ell^{n}}\notin\mathcal{C}_{\delta,\phi}(\ell^{n})
, so
G_{(E^{\prime})^{N},\ell^{n}}=\langle g_{E^{\prime},\ell^{n}},G_{(E^{\prime})^{N}/K,\ell^{n}}\rangle
. Finally, by construction, we have
G_{(E^{\prime})^{N},\ell^{n}}
conjugate to
G_{E^{N},\ell^{n}}
in
\operatorname{GL}(2,\mathbb{Z}/\ell^{n}\mathbb{Z})
. Since
E^{N}
and
(E^{\prime})^{N}
are
\ell
-simplest, and
\ell^{n}
is an
\ell
-adic level of differentiation, it follows by Theorem
4.3
(2e) that
E^{N}\cong_{\mathbb{Q}}(E^{\prime})^{N}
, so
E\cong_{\mathbb{Q}}E^{\prime}
by the theory of twists.

∎
