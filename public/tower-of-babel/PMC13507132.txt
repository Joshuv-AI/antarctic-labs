Fault diagnosis method for gearbox systems using OVMD and KLIRVM
Authors: Siming Meng
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractTo achieve precise diagnostic outcomes for gearbox systems, this study proposes an integrated methodology combining Orthogonalized Variational Mode Decomposition (OVMD) with Kernel Learning Incremental Relevance Vector Machine (KLIRVM). Capitalizing on the inherent efficacy of Bessel basis functions in detecting abrupt transient fluctuations, OVMD achieves pristine isolation of non-stationary characteristics stemming from damaged rolling elements. A novel KLIRVM approach is introduced and formulated for gearbox fault classification, wherein adaptive kernel parameter learning is seamlessly integrated with incremental updates—enabling each basis function to dynamically adjust its position and width for accurate characterization of locally varying signal features in streaming data. Ablation studies are conducted to validate the superiority of the proposed OVMD-KLIRVM framework. Empirical findings demonstrate that this approach outperforms competing methodologies in diagnostic accuracy.Keywords: OVMD, KLIRVM, TCCSA, Kernel learningSubject terms: Engineering, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Gearboxes function as indispensable power transmission components within mechanical systems, pervading industrial manufacturing equipment and rail transportation infrastructure. Their functional integrity exerts decisive influence over the holistic reliability, operational efficiency, and safety assurance of parent machinery1,2. Incipient defects in gearbox mechanisms possess the propensity to trigger cascading system-wide failures, thereby accentuating the exigency for dependable prognostic and diagnostic frameworks3,4. The advent of embedded chip integration within gearbox architectures has precipitated a paradigm shift toward localized intelligent monitoring, facilitating real-time anomaly detection at the operational locus. Hence, meticulous identification of gearbox aberrations represents a research domain of pronounced theoretical and practical import. In recent years, novel intelligent fault diagnosis methods have been continuously proposed and have demonstrated remarkable effectiveness5–8.

The attainment of superlative diagnostic fidelity fundamentally hinges upon efficacious representation of gearbox vibratory signatures. Traditional signal decomposition paradigms encompass wavelet transform9, empirical mode decomposition10, empirical wavelet transform11, and so on. Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD) has emerged as a cornerstone technique for analyzing non-stationary vibration signals. By adaptively decomposing intricate waveforms into a set of intrinsic mode functions, each constrained within a specific frequency range, VMD has gained widespread popularity in the realm of machinery fault diagnosis12,13.For example, some scholars have proposed a comprehensive methodology that integrates Variational Mode Decomposition (VMD), Particle Swarm Optimization (PSO), and advanced machine learning techniques,which has applied to enhance bearing and gear fault diagnosis14.Orthogonalized Variational Mode Decomposition (OVMD) enhances the standard VMD by introducing an orthogonality objective into the minimization problem, which effectively prevents mode duplication and suppresses over-segmentation through an adaptive proportional filtering bandwidth strategy15. This modification ensures better orthogonality among decomposed modes, improves robustness against noise and parameter sensitivity, and delivers superior convergence performance compared to conventional VMD.

Compared to deep neural networks, the Relevance Vector Machine (RVM) demonstrates superior generalization performance16–18.To further enhance the generalization performance of RVM, this paper presents a novel Kernel Learning Incremental RVM method (KLIRVM).KLIRVM simultaneously optimizes kernel location and scale parameters during incremental learning, enabling adaptive modeling of heterogeneous data distributions while maintaining computational efficiency through fast marginal-likelihood maximization.The Tent Chaotic Cuckoo Search Algorithm (TCCSA) calibrates KLIRVM hyperparameters by fusing Tent chaotic mapping—utilized for initializing and perturbing populations—with the Lévy flight-enabled global search mechanism native to traditional Cuckoo Search. The incorporation of tent map chaotic sequences into the standard Cuckoo Search structure strengthens exploratory capacity via comprehensive state-space coverage and intrinsic stochastic properties, successfully preventing early entrapment in local optima.Therefore,this study proposes an integrated methodology combining Orthogonalized Variational Mode Decomposition (OVMD) with Kernel Learning Incremental Relevance Vector Machine (KLIRVM) to fault diagnosis for gearbox systems.Ablation experiments are adopted to verify the effectiveness of our proposed OVMD-KLIRVM method. Convolutional Neural Network-Long Short-Term Memory (CNN-LSTM) leverages the complementary strengths of Convolutional Neural Networks and Long Short-Term Memory networks, it constitutes an outstanding deep learning paradigm.In order to show the superiority of OVMD-KLIRVM over deep learning algorithms, CNN-LSTM, a representative paradigm of outstanding deep learning algorithms, is used to compare with the proposed OVMD-KLIRVM.

The procedure for converting time-frequency representations into texture descriptors proceeds as follows. First, grayscale images are generated from the time-frequency maps produced by OVMD. Next, the gray-level co-occurrence matrix (GLCM) is constructed to quantify spatial dependencies among pixel intensities. Four directional GLCMs (0°, 45°, 90°, and 135°) are computed at a specified pixel distance. Eight statistical texture measures are then extracted from these matrices: angular second moment (energy), contrast, correlation, variance, inverse difference moment (homogeneity), sum average, sum entropy, and difference entropy. Finally, the mean values of these attributes across all orientations are computed to produce a consolidated texture feature vector for the subsequent fault classification stage.

TCCSA optimizes KLIRVM hyperparameters by strategically combining Tent chaotic mapping—for population initialization and perturbation—with the globally-oriented Lévy flight mechanism of classical Cuckoo Search. By incorporating tent map chaotic sequences into the standard Cuckoo Search framework19–21, TCCSA achieves superior exploration through comprehensive state-space coverage and inherent stochasticity, effectively avoiding premature convergence to local optima. Leveraging the tent map’s uniform probability distribution and high sensitivity to initial conditions, this approach delivers faster convergence and enhanced global search capability compared to conventional random walk methods, making it especially suitable for complex optimization problems with numerous local optima. Discussion

A comprehensive examination of the experimental results presented in Tables 1–4 provides compelling evidence for the superior diagnostic efficacy of the proposed OVMD-KLIRVM framework. The comparative analysis demonstrates that the integration of Optimized Variational Mode Decomposition (OVMD) with the Kernel-based Robust Incremental Relevance Vector Machine (KLIRVM) consistently surpasses the performance of the conventional VMD-KLIRVM configuration across diverse operational scenarios. This marked improvement is fundamentally attributable to the enhanced signal decomposition characteristics inherent in the OVMD algorithm. Unlike standard VMD, which is susceptible to mode mixing and suboptimal parameter selection, the optimized variant yields time-frequency representations with substantially greater clarity and discriminative capacity. Consequently, the resulting refined vibration features exhibit enhanced separability among distinct gearbox health conditions, thereby enabling more reliable fault identification even under severe contamination from ambient industrial noise.

In parallel, the comparative evaluation between VMD-KLIRVM and VMD-RVM architectures offers substantial empirical support for the superiority of the kernel-enhanced classification framework. When both models are supplied with identical input feature sets derived from the same decomposition technique, the KLIRVM-based classifier achieves markedly higher recognition accuracy across various gearbox operational states compared to its conventional Relevance Vector Machine (RVM) counterpart. This performance differential underscores the critical contribution of the regularized kernel trick in capturing intricate nonlinear relationships within the feature space, thereby facilitating more precise boundary delineation between distinct fault categories. Notably, this advantageous trend remains consistent when comparing VMD-KLIRVM against VMD-RVM, further substantiating the hypothesis that the incorporation of robust kernel regularization mechanisms significantly enhances classification performance—particularly in challenging environments characterized by elevated noise levels and signal distortion.

Conclusions

This investigation introduces a novel OVMD methodology that integrates Bessel-function kernels within the VMD framework. Capitalizing upon the intrinsic aptitude of Bessel bases for resolving abrupt temporal variations, OVMD accomplishes unambiguous discrimination of non-stationary signatures originating from compromised components. Harnessing this characteristic, the devised approach efficaciously encapsulates the quintessential vibratory dynamics of gearboxes, engendering marked enhancements in diagnostic fidelity. Moreover, a KLIRVM is formulated for gearbox fault classification, KLIRVM integrates adaptive kernel parameter learning with incremental updates, allowing each basis function to flexibly adjust its position and width for accurate characterization of locally varying signal features in streaming data.The KLIRVM training hyperparameters are calibrated through TCCSA. Concurrently, the Tent Chaotic Cuckoo Search Algorithm (TCCSA) fortifies KLIRVM hyperparameter optimization by amalgamating Tent chaotic mapping—deployed for population initialization and perturbation—with the Lévy flight-propelled global exploration mechanism inherent to orthodox Cuckoo Search.These results indicate that OVMD-KLIRVM consistently outperforms VMD-KLIRVM, VMD-RVM, RVM ,CNN-LSTM, and RF regardless of ambient noise levels.
