Analysis of a Biofilm Model in a Continuously Stirred Tank Reactor with Wall Attachment
Katerina Nik, Christoph Walker

Abstract
. We investigate a mathematical model for a bacterial population in a continuously stirred tank reactor with wall attachment. The model couples a free-boundary value problem for substrate diffusion in the one-dimensional biofilm with a system of nonlinear ODEs for biofilm thickness, suspended biomass, and free substrate concentration. We establish global well-posedness and analyze the long-term dynamics. In particular, we characterize the local and global stability of the trivial (washout) equilibrium, prove the existence of a nontrivial equilibrium, and, under additional structural assumptions, establish its uniqueness and derive conditions for its local stability.

Introduction

Microbial communities have become a central topic of interdisciplinary research due to their fundamental role in natural, medical, and industrial processes. In many environments, microorganisms do not persist as isolated planktonic cells but instead form spatially structured aggregates, commonly referred to as biofilms. On submerged surfaces in aqueous systems, cells may attach to the boundary, transition to a sessile state, and develop layered structures.

In numerous engineered systems, such as continuously stirred tank reactors (CSTRs), suspended and surface-associated microbial populations coexist. These compartments are dynamically coupled: planktonic cells may attach to an established biofilm, while sessile cells may detach and re-enter the bulk phase. Although attachment and detachment processes are known to play a crucial role in microbial population dynamics, the underlying mechanisms remain only partially understood.

Despite extensive experimental investigations and application-oriented modeling efforts, comparatively little attention has been devoted to the systematic derivation and rigorous mathematical analysis of models capturing the coupled dynamics of suspended and attached populations. Motivated by

3.
Long-Term Dynamics and the Trivial Equilibrium

In the following, we fix
(h_{0},S_{0},Q_{0})\in[0,\infty)^{3}
and consider the large-time behavior of the unique global nonnegative solution
(h,S,Q,u)
provided by Theorem
2.3
.

We begin with a simple observation for the dynamics of

:

Lemma 3.1
.

Assume (
1.2
). Either

(3.1)

S(t)\searrow S^{*}\ \text{ as }\ t\to\infty

or there is
T_{*}\in[0,\infty)
such that

(3.2)

S(t)>S^{*}\ \text{ for }\ 0\leq t<T_{*}\quad\text{ and }\quad S(t)\leq S^{*}\ \text{ for }\ t\geq T_{*}\,.

Proof.

Since

S^{\prime}(t)\leq D\big(S^{*}-S(t)\big)\,,\quad t\geq 0\,,

it readily follows that
S(t)\leq\max\{S(T),S^{*}\}
for

and that

is strictly decreasing as long as

and that
\limsup_{t\to\infty}S(t)\leq S^{*}
. This implies the assertion.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Assume (
1.2
) and that

(3.3)

\lim_{z\to\infty}d(z)=\infty\,.

Then, the orbit
\{(h,S,Q)(t)\,;\,t\geq 0\}
is bounded in
\mathbb{R}_{+}^{3}
. In particular, the omega limit set
\omega(h_{0},S_{0},Q_{0})
is nonempty, invariant, compact, and connected.

Proof.

We argue similarly as in the proof of Theorem
2.3
. Recall from Lemma
3.1
and (
1.2
) that
0\leq S(t)\leq S_{1}
with
S_{1}=\max\{S_{0},S^{*}\}
and
0\leq r(u(t,\cdot))\leq r(S_{1})
for
t\geq 0
. It follows from (
2.9
) that

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\eta h+Q+\frac{1}{k_{1}}S\right)(t)

\displaystyle=h(t)\int_{0}^{1}\left[\eta g\bigl(r\bigl(u(t,y)\bigr)\bigr)-\frac{\rho}{k_{1}\kappa}r\bigl(u(t,y)\bigr)\right]\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\quad-(\eta-\beta)d\big(h(t)\big)h(t)+\frac{D}{k_{1}}(S^{*}-S(t))

\displaystyle\quad-\left(k_{2}+\alpha\left(1-\frac{\eta}{\beta}\right)\right)Q(t)

so that, choosing
\eta\in\left(\beta,\frac{k_{2}+\alpha}{\alpha}\beta\right)
and setting

\eta_{0}:=k_{2}+\alpha\left(1-\frac{\eta}{\beta}\right)>0\,,\qquad\eta_{1}:=\eta-\beta>0\,,\qquad g_{0}:=\max_{s\in[0,r(S_{1})]}|g(s)|\,,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\eta h+Q+\frac{1}{k_{1}}S\right)(t)

\displaystyle\leq\big[\eta g_{0}-\eta_{1}d\big(h(t)\big)\big]h(t)+\frac{D}{k_{1}}(S^{*}-S(t))-\eta_{0}Q(t)

for

we set

and obtain

\displaystyle\left(\eta h+Q+\frac{1}{k_{1}}S\right)(t)

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{\eta h_{0}+Q_{0}+\frac{1}{k_{1}}S_{0},\frac{1}{\delta}(g_{0}+1)\eta h_{1}+\frac{DS^{*}}{\delta k_{1}}\right\}

∎

3.1.
The Wash-Out Equilibrium

We examine stability properties of the trivial equilibrium

h=0\,,\quad S=S^{*}\,,\quad Q=0\,,\quad u\equiv S^{*}\,.

To apply the Principle of Linearized Stability we assume that
g(r)
and
\nu
are differentiable at
S^{*}
and that
d
is differentiable at
0
. Using the notation from Section
2
and recalling from Proposition
2.1
that
u[0,S^{*}]=S^{*}
, the Jacobian of
f
at this equilibrium is

\partial f(0,S^{*},0)=\left(\begin{matrix}g(r(S^{*}))-d(0)&0&\alpha/\beta\\[4.0pt]
-\frac{\rho}{\kappa}r(S^{*})&-D&-k_{1}\nu(S^{*})\\[4.0pt]
\beta d(0)&0&\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha\end{matrix}\right)\,.

The eigenvalues of
\partial f(0,S^{*},0)
are thus given by

\lambda_{0}:=-D<0

and

\lambda_{\pm}:=\frac{1}{2}\left(p+q\pm\sqrt{(p-q)^{2}+4\alpha d(0)}\right)

with

p:=g(r(S^{*}))-d(0)\,,\qquad q:=\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha\,.

Note that
\lambda_{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda_{+}\geq\lambda_{-}
.
Hence, the local stability of the equilibrium
(0,S^{*},0)
is determined solely by the sign of
\lambda_{+}
. In fact, we have
\lambda_{+}<0

and thus local stability provided that

-pq+\alpha d(0)<0\quad\text{ and }\quad p+q<0\,,

while

\lambda_{+}>0

and thus local instability provided that

-pq+\alpha d(0)>0\quad\text{ or }\quad p+q>0\,.

Consequently, we obtain:

Proposition 3.3
.

Assume (
1.2
) and that
g(r)
and
\nu
are differentiable at
S^{*}
and that
d
is differentiable at
0
. Then, the trivial equilibrium
(h,S,Q)=(0,S^{*},0)
is locally asymptotically stable in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
provided that

(S1)

\displaystyle d(0)\Big(\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}\Big)<g(r(S^{*}))\Big(\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha\Big)

and

(S2)

\displaystyle\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha<d(0)-g(r(S^{*}))

while it is unstable in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
provided that

d(0)\big(\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}\big)>g(r(S^{*}))\big(\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha\big)\quad\text{ or }\quad\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha>d(0)-g(r(S^{*}))\,.

Remark 3.4
.

If
d(0)=0
, then the stability condition can be simplified since (
S1
)
\&
(
S2
) are equivalent to
\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha<0
and
g(r(S^{*}))<0
. In particular, if
\nu
and
g
(and
r
) are nondecreasing, the wash-out equilibrium
(0,S^{*},0)
is stable for small

and unstable for large

.

Proof.

Let
d(0)=0
. Assume (
S1
) and (
S2
).
We claim that then
g(r(S^{*}))<0
. Assume for contradiction that
g(r(S^{*}))\geq 0
.
On the one hand, (
S1
) with
d(0)=0
implies

\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha<-g(r(S^{*}))\leq 0\,.

On the other hand,
(S_{1})
and
g(r(S^{*}))\geq 0
imply
0<\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha
and a contradiction. Hence, we do indeed have
g(r(S^{*}))<0
. But then
(S_{1})
implies that
\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha<0
.

Conversely,
\nu(S^{*})-k_{2}-\alpha<0
and

surely imply (

) and (

).
∎

We next show that the trivial equilibrium is globally asymptotically stable in
[0,\infty)^{3}
. To this end, we assume (
S1
) with

(3.4a)

is nondecreasing

and strengthen (
S2
) to

\text{$\nu$ is nondecreasing with }\ \nu(S^{*})<k_{2}\,.

Under these slightly stronger assumptions, the trivial equilibrium is globally asymptotically stable (hence there is no other equilibrium):

Corollary 3.5
.

Assume (
1.2
), (
S1
), (
3.4
), and
d(x)\geq d(0)
for

.
Then

is globally asymptotically stable in

Proof.

By continuity and assumptions (
S1
) and (

) there exists some
\delta>0
such that

(3.5)

\displaystyle d(0)\big(\nu(S^{*}+\delta)-k_{2}\big)<g(r(S^{*}+\delta))\big(\nu(S^{*}+\delta)-k_{2}-\alpha\big)

and

(3.6)

\displaystyle\nu(S^{*}+\delta)<k_{2}\,.

Since (

(3.7)

\displaystyle 0\leq\alpha d(0)<\big(d(0)-g(r(S^{*}+\delta))\big)\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*}+\delta)\big)

we first deduce that

due to (

\frac{\beta d(0)}{d(0)-g(r(S^{*}+\delta))}<\frac{\beta(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*}+\delta))}{\alpha}\,.

Noticing from (

\beta<\frac{\beta(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*}+\delta))}{\alpha}\,,

we may then choose
\eta

(3.8)

\displaystyle\max\left\{\beta,\frac{\beta d(0)}{d(0)-g(r(S^{*}+\delta))}\right\}<\eta<\frac{\beta(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*}+\delta))}{\alpha}\,.

Note from Lemma

that there exists
T_{\delta}>0
such that

for

. Therefore, we infer from (

\displaystyle g\bigl(r\bigl(u(t,y)\bigr)\bigr)\leq g(r(S(t)))\leq g(r(S^{*}+\delta))\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nu(S(t))\leq\nu(S^{*}+\delta)

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\left(\eta h+Q\right)(t)

Consequently,

In particular, given

so that (

∎

4.
Nontrivial Equilibrium

In this section, we investigate the existence, uniqueness, and stability properties of nontrivial equilibria to (
2.9
). A nontrivial equilibrium is a triplet
(h,S,Q)\in(0,\infty)^{3}
that, with the corresponding
u=u[h,S]\in C^{2}([0,1])
from Proposition
2.1
, satisfies the system

(4.1a)

\displaystyle\kappa\partial_{y}^{2}u

\displaystyle=h^{2}r(u)\,,\qquad 0<y<1\,,

(4.1b)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}u(0)

\displaystyle=0,\quad u(1)=S\,,

(4.1c)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=h\int_{0}^{1}g\big(r(u(y))\big)\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{\alpha}{\beta}Q-d(h)h\,,

(4.1d)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=D(S^{*}-S)-k_{1}Q\nu(S)-\frac{\rho h}{\kappa}\int_{0}^{1}r\bigl(u(y)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}y\,,

(4.1e)

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\big(\nu(S)-k_{2}\big)Q+\beta d(h)h-\alpha Q\,.

To begin with, we note that
several necessary conditions for the existence of a nontrivial equilibrium can be derived straightforwardly:

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(h,S,Q)\in(0,\infty)^{3}
be a nontrivial equilibrium. Then necessarily
S<S^{*}
and

(4.2)

\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)>0

with

(4.3)

Q=\frac{\beta d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}

and

(4.4)

\int_{0}^{1}g\big(r(u(y))\big)\,\mathrm{d}y=\frac{\big(k_{2}-\nu(S)\big)d(h)}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}\,.

Moreover, if
g
,
\nu
, and
d
are nondecreasing, then necessarily

(4.5)

\displaystyle g(r(S^{*}))\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)\geq d(0)\big(k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)\quad\text{ or }\quad\nu(S^{*})\geq k_{2}\,.

Proof.

It readily follows from (
4.1d
) that
S<S^{*}
while (
4.1e
) yields (
4.3
). The latter yields (
4.2
) and, together with (
4.1c
), that (
4.4
) holds true. Finally, if
g
,
\nu
, and
d
are nondecreasing, then Corollary
3.5
implies that there is no nontrivial equilibrium if

g(r(S^{*}))\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)<d(0)\big(k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)\quad\text{ and }\quad\nu(S^{*})<k_{2}\,.

This gives (
4.5
).
∎

4.1.
A General Existence Result

We now establish the existence of at least one nontrivial equilibrium when the stability condition (
S1
) from Proposition
3.3
is violated:

Theorem 4.2
.

Assume (
1.2
) with
g(0)<0
. Let
\nu\in{\rm C}^{1}([0,S^{*}])
be increasing, and let
d\in{\rm C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
with
\lim_{h\to\infty}d(h)h=\infty
. Moreover, assume that

(4.6)

\alpha+k_{2}>\nu(S^{*})

and that

(4.7)

\displaystyle g(r(S^{*}))\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)>d(0)\big(k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)\,.

Then, there is at least one nontrivial equilibrium

(h_{\star},S_{\star},Q_{\star})\in(0,\infty)\times(0,S^{*})\times(0,\infty)\,,\qquad u_{\star}=u[h_{\star},S_{\star}]\in{\rm C}^{2}([0,1])\big)

to (
2.9
).

Proof.

Let us first note that (
4.6
) and the monotonicity of
\nu
imply that

(4.8)

\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)>0\,,\quad S\in[0,S^{*}]\,,

and recall from Proposition
2.1
that

\Bigl[(h,S)\mapsto u[h,S]\Bigr]\in{\rm C}^{1}\big(\mathbb{R}^{2},{\rm C}^{2}([0,1])\big)\,,

where

is the solution to (

)-(

Next, owing to (

)
we can eliminate

and obtain from (

while (

) yields

That is, (
4.1
) is equivalent to solving (
4.9
) for

and then defining

by (
4.3
).

In order to solve (
4.9
), let

first be fixed. Using
u[h,0]\equiv 0
,
\nu(0)=0
,
r(0)=0
, and positivity, it readily follows that

(4.10)

Since

\displaystyle\partial_{S}E(h,S)

\displaystyle=D+\frac{\rho h}{\kappa}\int_{0}^{1}r^{\prime}\bigl(u[h,S](y)\bigr)\partial_{S}u[h,S](y)\,\mathrm{d}y+\frac{k_{1}\beta\nu^{\prime}(S)d(h)h(\alpha+k_{2})}{(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S))^{2}}\,,

it follows from Corollary
2.2

(a)
, and the monotonicity of

and
\nu
that

is strictly increasing with respect to

. Therefore, using (

) we find for each

a unique

such that

with

. In fact, the Implicit Function Theorem implies that

. Thus, setting

for

, we obtain
\mathsf{G}\in{\rm C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})
. Since

and

, it follows from assumption (

) that

(4.12)

\mathsf{G}(0)=g\big(r(S^{*})\big)-d(0)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})}\right)>0\,.

Next, the positivity of

and
\nu
yield that

Since

\lim_{h\to\infty}u[h,S(h)](y)=0\ \text{ uniformly with respect to $y\in[0,1]$}\,.

Therefore, it follows from (

), (

), and the assumption

that there is

such that

Hence, we obtain that

It thus follows from (

∎

Remark 4.3
.

Unfortunately, the proof above does not establish the uniqueness of the nontrivial equilibrium. Indeed,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}h}\mathsf{G}(h)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}(g\circ r)^{\prime}\big(u[h,S(h)](y)\big)\left\{\partial_{h}u[h,S(h)](y)+\partial_{S}u[h,S(h)](y)S^{\prime}(h)\right\}\,\mathrm{d}y

\displaystyle\qquad-d^{\prime}(h)\left(1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S(h))}\right)+d(h)\frac{\alpha\nu^{\prime}(S(h))S^{\prime}(h)}{(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S(h)))^{2}}

does not necessarily have a definite (negative) sign, even if

are nondecreasing, since

need not have a definite sign. Differentiating (

(recall from Proposition

while

4.2.
Uniqueness by a Shooting Argument

Theorem

establishes the existence of a nontrivial equilibrium. Under more restrictive assumptions we shall now derive uniqueness of a nontrivial equilibrium relying on a shooting argument as in

. More precisely, we assume that

for some

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{h}g\big(r(c(z))\big)\,\mathrm{d}z=a\int_{0}^{h}r(c(z))\,\mathrm{d}z-abh=a\kappa\partial_{z}c(h)-abh\,.

Combining (
4.17
) and (
1.1c
) we obtain at equilibrium the problem

(4.18a)

\displaystyle\kappa\partial_{z}^{2}c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}r(c)\,,\quad 0<z<h\,,

(4.18b)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}c(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0\,,\quad c(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}S\,,\quad a\kappa\partial_{z}c(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}d(h)h+abh-\frac{\alpha}{\beta}Q

for
c
and
h
, where
Q
and
S
we shall derive from the conditions

(4.18c)

\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)\big)Q\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}\beta d(h)h

respectively

(4.18d)

DS+k_{1}Q\nu(S)+\rho\partial_{z}c(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}DS^{*}\,.

Clearly, (
4.18c
) implies that
Q
is given by

(4.19)

Q=\frac{\beta d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}

and thus depends only on
h
and
S
. Plugging this into (
4.18d
) (using (
4.18b
)), we also obtain a relation only depending on
h
and

:

(4.20)

DS+\frac{\rho bh}{\kappa}+\frac{d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}\left[\left(k_{1}\beta-\frac{\rho}{a\kappa}\right)\nu(S)+\frac{\rho k_{2}}{a\kappa}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}DS^{*}\,.

Consequently, the existence of a unique nontrivial equilibrium to (
1.1
) is equivalent to find a unique nontrivial solution to (
4.18a
)-(
4.18b
) and (
4.19
)-(
4.20
). In order to do so, we first show that for any fixed
h
there exists a unique solution

S=S(h)
to (
4.20
). Together with (
4.19
), this allows us to reduce
(
4.18a
)–(
4.18b
) to a problem for the unknowns
h
and
c
only, which we then
solve by a shooting argument.

To carry through the technical details, we impose that

(4.21a)

\nu\in{\rm C}^{1}([0,S^{*}])\ \text{is nondecreasing}\,,\quad d\in{\rm C}^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{+})\ \text{is strictly increasing}\,,

and that

(4.21b)

\displaystyle k_{2}>\nu(S^{*})\quad\text{ and }\quad g(r(S^{*}))\big(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)>d(0)\big(k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})\big)\,.

Note that (
4.21a
)-(
4.21b
) imply in particular the instability of the wash-out equilibrium according to Proposition
3.3
and that

\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)\geq\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})>0\,,\quad 0\leq S\leq S^{*}\,.

We further impose that

(4.21c)

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha}{a(\alpha+k_{2})}\leq\frac{\beta k_{1}\kappa}{\rho}

or

(4.21d)

\displaystyle\text{$S\mapsto\frac{\nu^{\prime}(S)}{(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S))^{2}}$ is strictly monotone on $[0,S^{*}]$}\,.

Under these assumptions we can prove the existence of a
unique
nontrivial equilibrium:

Theorem 4.4
.

Assume (
1.2
), (
4.16
), and (
4.21
).
Then, there exists a unique nontrivial equilibrium solution

(h_{\star},S_{\star},Q_{\star})\in(0,\infty)\times(0,S^{*})\times(0,\infty)\,,\qquad c_{\star}\in{\rm C}^{2}([0,h_{\star}])\big)

to (
1.1
).

Note that conditions (
4.21c
) and (
4.21d
) both constitute structural assumptions that are independent of the magnitude of the inlet concentration
S^{*}
and are not needed for the instability of the trivial equilibrium (they are used in the proof of Proposition
4.6
below).
In this regard it is worth to note the following:

Remark 4.5
.

In
[
9
]
, the function
\nu
is of the form
\nu(S)=AS/(B+S)
with given
A,B>0
. It readily follows that such a function satisfies assumption (
4.21d
) provided that
\alpha+k_{2}\not=A
. Moreover, the functions

,

, and
g
in
[
9
]
satisfy (

), (
4.16
), and (

). Therefore, for the particular case considered in
[
9
]
, we prove herein the existence of a unique nontrivial equilibrium under the sole assumptions (
4.21b
) and
\alpha+k_{2}\not=A
.

The proof of Theorem
4.4
is divided into several steps.
Focusing first on (
4.20
) we express
S
as a function of
h
. For this purpose we set

F(h,S):=DS+\frac{\rho bh}{\kappa}+\frac{d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}\left[\left(k_{1}\beta-\frac{\rho}{a\kappa}\right)\nu(S)+\frac{\rho k_{2}}{a\kappa}\right]

for

and

.

Proposition 4.6
.

Assume (
1.2
), (
4.16
), and (
4.21
).
There exist a unique
h_{*}>0
and a unique, strictly decreasing function
S\in{\rm C}^{1}([0,h_{*}],[0,S^{*}])
with

and

such that

F\big(h,S(h)\big)=DS^{*}\,,\quad h\in[0,h_{*}]\,.

Proof.

Since
\nu(0)=0
, we have

F(h,0)=\frac{\rho h}{a\kappa}\left(ab+\frac{k_{2}d(h)}{\alpha+k_{2}}\right)\,,\quad h>0\,.

Consequently, there is a unique
h_{*}>0
such that

(4.22)

\displaystyle F(h,0)<DS^{*}\,,\quad 0\leq h<h_{*}\,,\qquad F(h_{*},0)=DS^{*}\,.

Next, we derive from (
4.21a
) and (
4.21b
) that

(4.23)

\displaystyle F(h,S^{*})=DS^{*}+\frac{k_{1}\beta\nu(S^{*})d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})}+\frac{\rho h}{a\kappa}\left(ab+\frac{k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S^{*})}d(h)\right)>DS^{*}

for
0<h\leq h_{*}
and

. Since

(4.24)

\displaystyle\partial_{S}F(h,S)=D+\frac{\nu^{\prime}(S)d(h)h}{(\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S))^{2}}\left[k_{1}\beta(\alpha+k_{2})-\frac{\rho\alpha}{a\kappa}\right]\,,

alternative (
4.21c
) implies that
\partial_{S}F(h,S)>0
while alternative (
4.21d
) implies that
\partial_{S}F(h,S)
is strictly monotone in

(and hence
F(h,\cdot)
is strictly convex or concave). In any case, for each

, the function
S\mapsto\partial_{S}F(h,S)
has at most one zero. Therefore, since
F(h,0)<DS^{*}<F(h,S^{*})
for

as observed above, it follows that for each

there is a unique

such that

.
Moreover, we have

and

by construction. In addition, since

or

is strictly convex or concave as just proven, we necessarily have

(4.25)

\displaystyle\partial_{S}F(h,S(h))>0\,,\quad h\in(0,h_{*})\,.

The Implicit Function Theorem and (

) then imply that
S\in C^{1}([0,h_{*}])
. It remains to show that

.
To this end, note that

\displaystyle\partial_{h}F(h,S)

\displaystyle=\frac{d^{\prime}(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}\left[k_{1}\beta\nu(S)+\frac{\rho}{a\kappa}(k_{2}-\nu(S))\right]

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{1}\beta\nu(S)d(h)}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}+\frac{\rho}{a\kappa}\left(ab+\frac{k_{2}-\nu(S)}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S)}d(h)\right)\,.

We infer from (

) and the monotonicity of

. Differentiating

for

due to (

.
∎

It remains to consider (

)-(

as in (

\displaystyle\kappa\partial_{z}^{2}c\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}r(c)\,,\quad 0<z<h\,,

(4.26b)

\displaystyle\partial_{z}c(0)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}0\,,\quad c(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}S(h)\,,\quad a\kappa\partial_{z}c(h)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{=}}abh+\frac{(k_{2}-\nu(S(h)))d(h)h}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu(S(h))}\,.

To this end, we use a shooting argument. The following result is readily seen, we refer to

Lemma 4.7

For each

\big[\mu\mapsto c(\cdot,\mu)\big]\in{\rm C}^{1}\big([0,S^{*}],{\rm C}^{2}([0,R])\big)

. Moreover,

\displaystyle\kappa\partial_{z}^{2}w(z,\mu)=r^{\prime}({\rm c}(z,\mu))w(z,\mu)\,,\quad z>0\,,

(4.28b)

Now, the aim is to show that there exists a unique

such that

We first comply with the Dirichlet boundary condition at

in (

Proposition 4.8
.

Assume (
1.2
), (
4.16
), and (
4.21

Let

(4.29)

\displaystyle{\rm c}\big(h(\mu),\mu\big)=S\big(h(\mu)\big)\,,\quad\mu\in[0,S^{*}]\,.

Define

Then, for fixed

Lemma 4.9

\displaystyle:=-a\big[r({\rm c}(z,\mu))-b\big]w(z,\mu)+a\kappa\big(\partial_{z}{\rm c}(z,\mu)-S^{\prime}(h(\mu))\big)\partial_{z}w(z,\mu)

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{k_{2}-\nu\big(S(h(\mu))\big)}{\alpha+k_{2}-\nu\big(S(h(\mu))\big)}d\big(h(\mu)\big)

4.3.
Stability of the Nontrivial Equilibrium

Finally, we provide conditions that ensure the local asymptotic stability of the unique nontrivial equilibrium

(h_{\star},S_{\star},Q_{\star})\in(0,\infty)\times(0,S^{*})\times(0,\infty)\,,\qquad c_{\star}\in{\rm C}^{2}([0,h_{\star}])\big)

We derive the local stability result:

As in Section

Under assumption (

(

\displaystyle m_{0}>0\,,\quad m_{1}>0\,,\quad m_{2}>0\,,\quad m_{1}m_{2}-m_{0}>0\,,

where

\displaystyle m_{0}:=-\det(A)\,,\quad m_{1}:=-\mathrm{trace}(A)\,,

\displaystyle m_{2}:=\det\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}&a_{12}\\
a_{21}&a_{22}\end{matrix}\right)+\det\left(\begin{matrix}a_{11}&a_{13}\\

We check the positivity of each term in (
