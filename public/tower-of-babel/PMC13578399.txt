Finite element analysis of variable hollowness and experimental validation of optimized uniformly tapered layered hollow roller bearings
Authors: Rajesh Joshi, Gurmitsingh Bassan, Pranav Mehta, A. Johnson Santosh
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The present study investigates the structural performance of uniformly tapered hollow roller (UTHR) and uniformly tapered layered hollow roller (UTLHR) bearings with varying hollowness levels using finite element analysis (FEA) and experimental validation. A comprehensive numerical investigation was conducted for hollowness levels ranging from 30% to 80% to evaluate maximum deflection, bending stress, von Mises stress, contact pressure, endurance-limit loading, and radial stiffness. The finite element results identified an optimum hollowness range of approximately 31–40%, where stress redistribution was achieved without excessive loss of stiffness. The analytical study predicted applied radial load of 30 kN, with a deviation of 0.1412%, which is validating the theoretical formulations. Moreover, simulations results realized reduced stress concentrations and optimized stiffness at optimum hollowness. The layered hollow roller configurations dominate over single hollow roller design in terms of lower contact pressure and improved stress distribution. The experimentally measured static failure loads were 53.01 kN and 76.17 kN for the UTHR and UTLHR bearings, respectively. The experimental results showed excellent agreement with the finite element predictions, with deviations below 2.5%. Accordingly, the effective similarities between the predicted and experimental results shows the reliability of the adopted modelling approach and highlights the structural advantages of layered hollow rollers within an optimized hollowness range.

Introduction

Numerous industries, not limited but including to, rail traction, heavy gear manufacturing, machine tools and automobiles demands optimized design configurations of the bearings that mainly includes the reduced weight as well as thermomechanical stability. Accordingly, hollow roller bearings (HRBs) are potential option for various applications due to their layered and tapered configurations make overall reduced Hertzian and subsurface stresses without altering the stiffness of the bearings1–3. Several studies shows that hollow cylindrical rollers realized reduced contact pressure and stress due to elastic ovality of roller. However, operating the bearing above the optimized range may lead to the increasing the stresses and pressure at the bore4,5. Several simulation studies and tribo-thermal studies have been carried out to identify the optimized design configurations and operating parameters that attains the key focus on rib contact has considerable significance in durability of tapered design, development of stress and temperature distributions mainly depends over the operating speed and lubricating conditions and stiffness plays an imperative role while designing any tapered or hollow roller bearing for industrial applications6. Researchers realized that, for rolling contact bearing, the wall thickness dominated the maximum shear stress development and bending strain gradients7. However, the experimental study also suggests that increasing the hollowness excessively results in development of the stress under the high-speed conditions8. One of the studied suggest the hollowness levels in the range of 60–70% results in improved stiffness and thermal behaviour by applying the bearing to the precision machine tools9. Finite element studies conducted to identify the optimized hollowness under pure and combines loading conditions reveals 60% optimized hollowness that realized minimum bore stresses within the flexural limits10,11. Moreover, the tangential tensile stress at the contact point dominated over the increased hollowness and stress reduction, that ultimately affects the service life of the bearing12. Researchers identified that hollow roller configurations results in reduced maximum contact stress and higher predicted life if operated within the optimized range for composite and layered configurations. Furthermore, cavity filling and layered shells are potential option in case of the elastic composite cylindrical rollers that realized reduced bending stress13. Apart from this, multi-shell layered roller configurations attain the stiffness tuning independent of the Hertzian compliance, that realized optimized hollowness of 61% with reduced bore tensile stress14. However, rib contact vis-à-vis study of lubrication nature are an imperative aspect while designing tapered roller bearing15. Various thermal models established for the double row tapered roller bearings shows strong coupling between operating and heat generations16,17. Subsequently, dynamic modelling pursued that internal compliance reduces roller vibration amplitude and suppresses edge stress spikes18. Apart from this, finite-element studies on cone-bit hollow rollers attained an optimal hollowness of 55%, consistent with the broader 55–65% range reported across diverse applications19. It was confirmed that, multi-objective FE-based stiffness optimization demonstrated that wall-thickness tuning can recover stiffness lost to hollowness while preserving mass savings20,21. Overall, the literature converges on tapered, layered hollow roller bearing as a rational high-speed architecture. Moreover, the tapered layered hollow roller bearing realized several merits over the hollow roller bearing, some of them are, not limited but including to, reduced maximum Hertzian stress under the same load conditions, uniform internal load distribution, reduced vibrations and improved fatigue life compared to simple configurations.

The tapered roller bearing is widely employed in industrial applications where combined radial and axial loads are required to support with high reliability. UTHR bearings are extensively utilized in automotive wheel hubs, differential carriers, heavy machinery, industrial gearboxes, railway axle boxes, and power transmission systems. Moreover, the proposed uniformly tapered layered hollow roller (UTLHR) configuration targets the same application domains while offering potential advantages such as reduced weight, improved stress distribution, effective lubrication and enhanced rolling contact performance. Consequently, UTLHR bearings are potential option for modern transportation systems, energy equipment, industrial drives, and off-road machinery where weight reduction, energy efficiency, and extended service life are imperative design requirements. Apart from this, the UTLHR bearings are suited for emerging technologies including electric aircraft propulsion systems, offshore renewable energy installations and smart bearing systems integrated with digital twins.

Objective of the study

The overriding study of the literature study suggests a possible gap in the domain of the hollow roller bearings, that demands the study of the tapered and layered configurations in the single bearing for reduced stress and pressure objectives. The present study evaluates the effect of the different roller hollowness for the tapered layered hollow roller bearing. The study was examined for stress distribution, pressure distribution, deflection, stiffness study and static-load carrying capacity of the developed configurations of the bearing. Moreover, the comparison of the uniformly tapered hollow rollers and uniformly tapered layered hollow rollers through finite element analysis and experimental testing was carried out that gives actual bearing dynamic scenarios. Moreover, stiffness and deflections were assessed comprehensively to identify the best configurations and operating standard for the designed bearing. Additionally, the work sought to examine the feasibility of manufacturing uniformly tapered layered hollow rollers using EDM techniques and to assess their practical applicability in industrial bearing systems subjected to combined radial and axial loads. Overall, the approach and testing uniform tapered layered hollow roller bearing in experimental test-ring of static load testing and validation against the finite element analysis would further add novel findings in the research domain of the hollow roller bearings. Compared to the previous optimization and finite element studies, that preliminary focussed on identification of the optimum hollowness, the present work experimentally tests the bearing configurations with optimized hollowness under static loading conditions22. Moreover, it assesses the coupled effects of stress redistribution, contact pressure, bending stress, stiffness, endurance-limit loading, and static load-carrying capacity in uniformly tapered hollow and layered hollow roller bearings.

The remainder of the article is structured as follows: Sect. 2: Methods; Sect. 3: Static load testing; Sect. 4: Results and discussion and Sect. 5: Conclusion.

Brief about hollowness

Hollowness is typically defined for both hollow and layered hollow rollers as the ratio of the inner diameter to the outer diameter, measured at the midpoint of the roller’s length. Effectively, for dynamic equilibrium during running conditions, it is preferable to have a uniform distribution of roller mass about the axis of symmetry. Accordingly, to incorporate uniform mass distribution, the taper has been kept uniform along the length of the roller. First, the diameter at the middle of the length is calculated and then according to the required hollowness percentage, the tapered hole is created in a taper roller. Moreover, for the case of layered hollow rollers, the dimensions of the outer layer/roller are first calculated as per percentage hollowness and then another hollow roller having an external diameter equal to the inner diameter of the outer roller and inner diameter as per hollowness percentage is embedded inside. Following the calculation of the large-end and small-end diameters of the rollers using the aforementioned methodology, geometric constraints precluded the consideration of hollowness values in the range of 1% to 29%. The negative values obtained for the small-end inner diameter of the inner roller result from the uniform taper maintained throughout the roller length to satisfy dynamic mass balancing requirements. Consequently, these configurations were excluded from further simulation studies. Hollowness exceeding 80% renders the roller weak and flexible, making it practically unusable. Thus, the hollowness range considered for the present study is 30% to 80%. Table 2, shows the roller dimensions for various percentages of hollowness.

For the finite element analysis, SAE 52,100 steel was assigned as the material for the bearing components, with mechanical properties defined as a Young’s modulus of 2.058 × 10⁵ N/mm2 and a Poisson’s ratio of 0.3. A coefficient of friction of 0.0018 was considered at the contact interfaces. Accordingly, the SAE 52,100 was selected based on the several characteristics as numerous applications in bearing industries, 60–65 HRC hardness, high fatigue strength, effective wear resistance and can able to withstand high cyclic stresses without premature failure. Additionally, the tapered roller bearing − 32,212 having internal diameter, outer diameter and width as 60 mm, 110 mm and 29.75 mm respectively is selected for the present experiments. Accordingly, the bearing is designed to sustain combined radial and axial loads, which makes it appropriate for applications requiring both load-carrying capacity and accurate shaft positioning. Its tapered geometry enables effective transmission of radial loads along with axial loads in one direction. Furthermore, the provision for preload adjustment allows precise control of stiffness and alignment, while its robust construction ensures reliable performance under conditions involving shock and vibration, such as heavy-duty and off-highway machinery.

Results and discussion

Finite element analysis for evaluating static load capacity on full bearings

For the determination of static load carrying capacity of UTHR bearing as well as UTLHR bearing, finite element simulation study is carried out. For finite element simulation, adaptive meshing is used. For the parts of fixture with large volume region, depending upon the geometry of the parts, adaptive meshing with tetrahedron and hexahedron elements are used. For critical contact surfaces of roller-raceway contact zone, the outer surface of roller has been considered as contact surface and is meshed with contact element (CONTA174) and the outside cylindrical surface of inner race has been considered as target surface and is meshed with target element (TARGE170). Since the accuracy of the analysis is highly influenced by mesh quality, a converged mesh with element size of 0.15 mm is used at critical contacts where contact pressure and stresses are measured.

For finite element simulation, radial load was applied on the bearing. The stress state in the roller under loading is complex, involving various components that can lead to failure. To assess this, the von Mises stress and bending stress were evaluated. Von Mises stress provides a measure of the equivalent stress experienced by the material, which is essential for understanding its susceptibility to yielding and plastic deformation under complex loading conditions. Together, these analyses offer a comprehensive understanding of the both UTLH and UTLHR failure mechanisms under applied load. Intensity of applied radial load was gradually increased and induced von mises stress values were monitored. Roller material considered for the analysis were having following properties in Table 3. Experimental static load testing for uniformly tapered hollow roller (UTHR) and uniformly tapered layered hollow roller (UTLHR) full bearings

After manufacturing the hollow and layered hollow rollers with attained optimum 36% hollowness22 which is derived based on minimum von Mises stress and contact pressure criteria for applied loading at roller-race interface for ‘0’ position rollers, static load carrying capacity test has been carried out on full UTHR and UTLHR bearings to determine their static load carrying capacity. Full UTHR and UTLHR bearings were mounted in a static load testing rig, and the complete assembly was placed under a universal testing machine. A compressive load was applied gradually in controlled increments. During the initial stages of loading, no significant deformation was observed. As the applied load approached the ultimate strength of the roller material, the roller located at the ‘0’ position failed abruptly, accompanied by a distinct cracking sound. The failure mode was characterized by a clear longitudinal split, resulting in the roller separating into two parts. The load recorded at this instant by the universal testing machine was considered as the static failure load of the roller. The static load test setup was used on universal testing machine. Figure 12 shows the setup configuration on universal testing machine.

Fig. 12Universal testing machine setup for static load test on bearings.

Universal testing machine setup for static load test on bearings.

A Universal Testing Machine (UTM) with a load capacity of 1000 kN was employed for the experimental investigation. The system provides an accuracy of ± 1% of the indicated load, a strain rate range of 0.5–80 mm/min, an electronic extensometer, 750 mm column clearance, 250 mm piston stroke, and a least count of 0.01 kN. The applied compressive load was recorded using a data acquisition system, while hydraulic servo controls were used to apply the load on the test bearing.

The preset study focusses on the performance assessment of the UTLHR bearing configurations under the static loading conditions rather than predictions of bearing life based on the fatigue loading conditions. Moreover, the bearing life is imperatively affected by the fatigue failure and operating conditions. The structural integrity and static load carrying capacity are also effective assessing domain for identifications of the high stress, pressure and deformation zones within the bearing. Therefore, both the finite element analysis and the experimental investigations were conducted under gradually increasing static loading conditions. Moreover, the close agreement between the experimentally measured failure loads and the corresponding FEA predictions (deviation less than 2.5%) confirms the suitability of the numerical model for predicting static failure behaviour and comparative load-carrying performance of the investigated bearing configurations.

Accordingly, despite from the internal geometry, the properties of the material, meshing model and boundary conditions are same as that of the uniform configurations. Additionally, a cylindrical hole was introduced instead of a uniformly tapered hole, resulting in a non-uniform wall thickness along the roller length. From a manufacturing standpoint, cylindrical holes are easier and more economical to produce; however, the resulting roller geometry becomes non-uniformly tapered. Therefore, a separate parametric FE study was conducted to evaluate and compare the structural performance of NUTHR and NUTLHR with that of UTHR and UTLHR. The results presented in Tables 4 and 5 are derived directly from these additional simulations. Apart from this, the differences between the two configurations were found to be relatively small, uniformly tapered hollow and layered hollow rollers remain preferable for practical applications due to their improved mass balance and dynamic performance under operating conditions. The observed increase in deflection with increasing hollowness is consistent with classical beam bending theory, where stiffness decreases with reduction in cross-sectional area. Similarly, the minimum von Mises stress observed at 36% hollowness is attributed to redistribution of stresses along the roller geometry, which balances weight reduction with adequate load-carrying stiffness. This behaviour agrees with Hertzian contact mechanics, where moderate flexibility of the roller allows uniform contact stress distribution. Beyond this optimum, further hollowness increases bending stresses disproportionately, confirming a trade-off between weight reduction and structural integrity.

The superior performance of the UTLHR configuration is not uniform across all performance measures. Accordingly, the results indicate that the layered hollow roller design effectively redistributes the contact load, leading to lower contact pressure and improved static load-carrying capacity compared with the UTHR configuration. The reduction in contact pressure is beneficial because it minimizes the risk of surface distress, pitting, and localized contact damage at the roller–raceway interface. However, the results also reveal that the UTLHR configuration may exhibit higher von Mises stress levels under higher loading conditions. This behaviour can be attributed to the layered architecture, which modifies the internal load-transfer mechanism and alters the stress distribution within the roller body. Consequently, although the contact stresses are reduced, the equivalent stress developed within certain regions of the layered roller may increase. Therefore, a reduction in contact pressure should not be interpreted as a simultaneous reduction in all stress measures.

The findings suggest that the UTLHR configuration offers significant advantages in terms of contact behaviour and static load-carrying capacity, but the increased von Mises stress observed at higher loads may influence fatigue life and long-term durability. Accordingly, the UTLHR design should not be considered universally superior under all operating conditions. Further investigations involving fatigue loading, dynamic response, thermal effects, and tribological performance are necessary to fully assess the long-term reliability of layered hollow tapered roller bearings.

The comparison across various relevant state-of-the-art studies have been recorded in Table 13. A review of existing studies indicates that prior work has primarily focused on cylindrical or hollow cylindrical roller bearings, with investigations largely limited to finite element or analytical approaches for determining optimum hollowness. While some studies have explored layered configurations, they lack experimental validation and consideration of tapered geometries. In contrast, the present work introduces a uniformly tapered layered hollow roller bearing and adopts an integrated analytical, numerical, and experimental methodology. The study identifies a narrower optimum hollowness range (31–40%), demonstrates a significant improvement in static load capacity (43.68% higher for UTLHR compared to UTHR), and validates results with minimal deviation (≤ 2.5%), thereby establishing both design innovation and practical applicability across multiple industrial sectors.

Conclusion

The present study assessed the performance of the uniform tapered layered hollow roller bearing with under the optimized hollowness in terms of simulation and experimental tests. The results are compared with the solid roller and uniform hollow roller configurations. Accordingly, the following conclusions are arrived at:

The analytical load distribution model and finite element framework successfully predicted the stress, contact pressure, deflection, and stiffness characteristics of hollow and layered hollow roller bearings under static radial loading.Roller hollowness strongly influences stress behaviour, stiffness, and load-carrying capacity.An optimum hollowness range of approximately 31–40% was identified from the numerical investigation, providing a balance between stress redistribution, contact-pressure reduction, and structural rigidity.Increasing hollowness above this threshold considerably diminishes the endurance limit load owing to heightened bending stresses.Static load tests were conducted on the optimized 36% hollowness UTHR and UTLHR bearing configurations. The experimentally measured failure loads showed excellent agreement with FEA predictions, with deviations below 2.5%, thereby validating the numerical modelling approach.The experimentally determined average static failure load of the UTLHR bearing was 76.17 kN compared with 53.01 kN for the UTHR bearing, demonstrating a 43.68% improvement in static load-carrying capacity for the layered hollow roller design.It is advisable to conduct more research under dynamic and fatigue loading conditions to assess long-term service performance. Coupled thermo-mechanical analysis would be advantageous for high-velocity applications. Moreover, the optimisation of layer thickness, material combinations, and production procedures may further augment performance. Field-level validation in automotive, railway, wind energy, and heavy machinery sectors can facilitate the practical implementation of the suggested layered hollow roller design.
