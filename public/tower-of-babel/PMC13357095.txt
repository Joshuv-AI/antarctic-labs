Development and validation of a DYX1C1- and GNAI1-based nomogram for predicting relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer treated with trastuzumab
Authors: Junjie Liu, Xiaoqian Li, Ziyan Li, Rui Zhang, Yunpeng Zhang, Wei Zhang, Jianjun He, Huimin Zhang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundResistance to trastuzumab and subsequent relapse remain critical challenges in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer management. This study aimed to develop and validate a multivariable prediction model for relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with trastuzumab and to explore the underlying biological mechanisms of the identified biomarkers.MethodsThis retrospective study utilized public gene expression profiles from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database and a clinical cohort from the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University. The analysis included a training cohort (GSE55348, n=51), an internal validation cohort (GSE58984, n=94), and an external validation cohort (I-SPY2/GSE181574, n=127). Relapse-associated hub genes were identified via differential expression analysis and weighted gene co-expression network analysis (WGCNA). Independent predictors were determined through multivariate Cox regression to construct a nomogram for predicting 3-year relapse-free survival (RFS). Model performance was evaluated using time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Biological mechanisms were investigated using gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA), immune infiltration analysis, and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq). Clinical relevance was further supported by immunohistochemistry (IHC) in a pilot cohort of 12 patients.ResultsDYX1C1 and GNAI1 were identified as robust, independent predictors of relapse. The constructed two-gene nomogram demonstrated excellent predictive accuracy, with an area under the curve (AUC) of 0.894 in the training cohort and 0.829 in the validation cohort. Mechanistically, DYX1C1 expression was significantly associated with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) characterized by reduced infiltration of B cells and CD8+ T cells, whereas GNAI1 expression correlated with stromal activation and extracellular matrix remodeling via the TGF-β signaling pathway. Single-cell analysis confirmed distinct cellular localizations of DYX1C1 in epithelial cells and GNAI1 in endothelial and mesenchymal cells. IHC analysis indicated that high protein expression of both biomarkers was associated with poor pathological complete response (pCR) to neoadjuvant therapy.ConclusionsThe DYX1C1- and GNAI1-based nomogram provides a simple, accurate tool for predicting relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer treated with trastuzumab. These biomarkers are associated with distinct pro-tumorigenic TME features—DYX1C1 with immune suppression and GNAI1 with stromal activation—offering hypothesis-generating insights into therapeutic resistance.

Highlight box

Key findings

• This study identified DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as robust, independent predictors of relapse in human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer treated with trastuzumab.

• We constructed a concise two-gene nomogram demonstrating high predictive performance (area under the curve up to 0.894) compared to existing models.

• This study revealed potential resistance mechanisms: DYX1C1 expression correlated with an immunosuppressive tumor microenvironment (TME) characterized by reduced infiltration of B cell and CD8+ T cell, whereas GNAI1 expression was associated with stromal activation and extracellular matrix remodeling involving the TGF-β pathway.

• Clinical relevance was supported via single-cell RNA sequencing localization and immunohistochemistry, suggesting an association with poor pathological complete response to dual-targeted therapy.

What is known and what is new?

• Trastuzumab resistance and relapse remain critical challenges in HER2-positive breast cancer; existing predictive models often lack simplicity or clear mechanistic insights.

• This is the first study to link DYX1C1 with relapse and an immunosuppressive TME, develop a simple yet powerful two-gene signature, map distinct cellular localizations of these genes, and implicate GNAI1 in a stromal-associated role.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• The two-gene signature offers a cost-effective tool for precise risk stratification and predicting response to dual-targeted therapy, and identifies distinct therapeutic vulnerabilities (immune vs. stromal).

• Prospective validation of the nomogram in multi-institutional cohorts; experimental investigation into targeting the DYX1C1-immune axis or GNAI1-stromal axis to overcome resistance.

Introduction

Based on the data of GLOBOCAN 2022, female breast cancer is the second leading cause of cancer incidence and the fourth leading cause of cancer-related death worldwide, with an estimated 2,308,897 new cases and 665,684 deaths in 2022 (1). Breast cancer is a highly heterogeneous tumor, with human epidermal growth factor receptor 2 (HER2)-positive breast cancer accounting for approximately 15–20% of all cases (2). Amplification of the HER2 gene is a defining feature of HER2-positive breast cancer, leading to a high risk of relapse and poor prognosis (3,4). This finding prompted the development of the HER2-targeted drug trastuzumab. While numerous studies have demonstrated that chemotherapy combined with trastuzumab significantly improves survival in HER2-positive breast cancer patients (5-7), the fact that 15–25% of patients still experience recurrence or distant metastasis highlights the need for more effective strategies (8,9). Consequently, a variety of novel HER2-targeted agents, tyrosine kinase inhibitors, and antibody-drug conjugates have been progressively employed to enhance treatment for HER2-positive breast cancer patients, achieving satisfactory outcomes (10). Among these strategies, the combination of pertuzumab and trastuzumab has been established as the standard-of-care neoadjuvant regimen for HER2-positive patients (11,12). Despite these therapeutic advances, a significant proportion of HER2-positive patients fail to achieve a pathological complete response (pCR rates of 46–53% with dual-target neoadjuvant therapy) (13,14), underscoring the persistent clinical challenge of therapeutic resistance and subsequent disease progression. Therefore, biomarkers and predictive models capable of predicting relapse in HER2-positive patients treated with trastuzumab or trastuzumab plus pertuzumab are urgently needed to guide clinical decision-making and avoid overtreatment or ineffective therapy.

Accurate prognostic stratification remains pivotal for tailoring therapeutic intensity in HER2-positive breast cancer. Several multivariable prediction models have previously been developed to address this clinical need. For instance, the HER2DX assay, a genomic test designed for early-stage HER2-positive disease, has demonstrated utility in predicting pathological response and survival outcomes (15). Similarly, multimodal deep learning approaches integrating histopathological images with clinical data have shown promise in estimating recurrence risk (4). However, these existing models often involve complex genomic signatures or require sophisticated computational infrastructure, which may limit their widespread clinical implementation. Furthermore, while these tools provide statistical risk estimates, they frequently lack transparent biological interpretability regarding the underlying mechanisms of trastuzumab resistance, specifically the role of the tumor microenvironment (TME). Consequently, there is a pressing need for a parsimonious, cost-effective, and biologically grounded model.

The TME refers to the surrounding microenvironment of tumor cells and includes immune cells, cancer-associated fibroblasts (CAFs), endothelial cells (ECs) and extracellular matrix (ECM) components. Initially, these nonneoplastic cells and components were thought to be bystanders of tumorigenesis and progression; however, increasing evidence suggests that the TME plays an important role in tumor progression (16). In the immune microenvironment of breast cancer, an increase in tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes (TILs, including cytotoxic T cells and helper T cells, B cells, macrophages, natural killer cells and dendritic cells) in HER2-positive breast cancer is strongly linearly associated with an improvement in relapse-free survival (RFS) (17,18). In addition, Ali et al. similarly demonstrated that the presence of CD8+ T cells in the TME significantly reduces the risk of HER2-positive breast cancer-specific death (19). In the stromal microenvironment of breast cancer, CAFs promote tumorigenesis and metastasis by altering the ECM characteristics of breast cancer and reducing clinical outcomes (20,21). In addition, high microvessel density in the stromal microenvironment also promotes proliferation and metastasis and worsens prognosis in breast cancer patients (22-24).

The primary objective of this study was to develop and validate a multivariable prediction model for relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with trastuzumab. Specifically, we aimed to identify key biomarkers via bioinformatics analysis and construct a nomogram. Furthermore, we investigated the association between these biomarkers and the TME to elucidate the underlying mechanisms driving relapse, followed by validation using immunohistochemistry (IHC) and single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data. We present this article in accordance with the TRIPOD reporting checklist (available at https://tcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tcr-2026-1-0391/rc).

Methods

Data acquisition and processing

The gene expression profiles and corresponding clinical data for HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with trastuzumab were retrieved from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO: https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/geo/) database. For relapse biomarker discovery and prediction model construction, two microarray datasets were analyzed: GSE55348 (Illumina HumanHT-12 WG-DASL V4.0 R2; model development cohort: 22 relapse vs. 29 relapse-free patients) and GSE58984 (Affymetrix HG‑U133 Plus 2.0; model validation cohort: 11 distant metastasis vs. 83 distant metastasis-free). Detailed clinical characteristics of the GSE55348 and GSE58984 cohorts are summarized in Table S1. For external validation of the biomarkers’ predictive value for treatment response, we used the GSE181574 dataset, which contains pre-treatment samples from 127 HER2-positive patients enrolled in the I-SPY2 trial, and compared biomarker expression between patients achieving pCR (n=64) and those with non-pCR (n=63). The GSE181574 cohort utilized two array platforms (Agendia32627_DPv1.14_SCFGplus and Agilent_human_DiscoverPrint_15746), for which a unified, ComBat-corrected gene-level dataset was provided by the I-SPY2 consortium. For all microarray data, raw expression matrices were downloaded using the R GEOquery (25) package, log2-transformed, and duplicate gene symbols were collapsed. To explore the cellular localization of biomarkers within the TME, single-cell RNA sequencing (scRNA-seq) data from six HER2-positive breast cancer samples were retrieved from the GSE161529 cohort.

Outcome definition

The primary outcome of this study was RFS. Relapse was defined as the first occurrence of either local-regional relapse (involving the ipsilateral breast, chest wall, or regional lymph nodes) or distant metastasis following initial treatment. RFS was calculated as the time interval from the completion of anti-tumor therapy to the first documented relapse event or metastasis. Patients without evidence of relapse or metastasis were censored at the last follow-up. The prediction model was specifically designed to estimate the 3-year RFS probability for HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with trastuzumab. For the GSE58984 validation cohort, the occurrence of distant metastasis was treated as the defining event for relapse, consistent with the RFS outcome definition.

Identification of relapse-associated hub genes

To identify genes central to relapse, we employed a two-pronged strategy integrating differential expression analysis and WGCNA. First, differentially expressed genes (DEGs) between relapse and relapse-free samples in GSE55348 and GSE58984 were identified using the R Limma (26) package, with thresholds of |log2FC| >0.5 and P<0.05. The intersection of upregulated and downregulated DEGs from both datasets was determined using a Venn diagram and further validated by the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Concurrently, a co-expression network was constructed on GSE55348 using the R WGCNA (27) package. The top 25% most variant genes (n=5,205) were used to build a scale-free network (soft threshold power β=10). Modules were identified via hierarchical clustering of the topological overlap matrix, and module eigengenes were correlated with the relapse phenotype. Hub genes were defined as the intersection of genes within significant relapse-associated modules and the robust DEGs identified in the previous step.

Development and validation of a relapse prediction model

Hub genes were first subjected to least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) Cox regression with 10-fold cross-validation to mitigate the risk of overfitting and rigorously select predictive variables. The optimal regularization parameter (λ) was determined using the minimum criteria, and genes with non-zero coefficients at the optimal λ were retained for subsequent analysis. Meanwhile, hub genes were subjected to univariate Cox regression analysis in the GSE55348 and GSE58984 cohorts to screen for prognostic factors (P<0.05). Significant factors were then entered into a multivariate Cox regression analysis to identify independent predictors of RFS. To mitigate cross-platform technical variation, batch effect correction between GSE55348 and GSE58984 was performed using the ComBat algorithm from the sva package, with dataset origin as the batch variable. Principal component analysis (PCA) before and after correction showed clear dataset-driven clustering before ComBat and effective mixing after correction, confirming substantial reduction of batch effects while preserving biological variability (Figure S1). The corrected expression matrices (designated GEO55348 and GEO58984) were used for downstream analyses. A nomogram to predict 3-year RFS was constructed based on these independent variables using the rms package in the GEO55348 training cohort. The model’s discriminative ability was evaluated using Harrell’s concordance index (C-index), with optimism correction performed via 1,000 bootstrap resamples. The optimism-corrected C-index and shrinkage factor were reported. Additionally, model performance was rigorously evaluated through time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis, calibration curves, and decision curve analysis (DCA). Kaplan-Meier (K-M) survival analysis was employed to compare RFS between high- and low-risk groups stratified by the median nomogram score. External validation of the nomogram’s predictive accuracy was performed in the independent GEO58984 cohort.

Functional characterization and TME analysis

To elucidate the biological functions of the identified biomarkers, single-gene gene set enrichment analysis (GSEA) was performed based on Gene Ontology (GO) and Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) pathways. Genes were ranked by their correlation with biomarker expression, and significance was defined as |NES| >1 with an adjusted P value (BH method) <0.05. Guided by the GSEA results, we investigated the biomarkers’ role in the TME. The immune and stromal landscapes were profiled using the R Estimate package (28) to calculate Immune, Stromal, and ESTIMATE scores. The differences in these scores between biomarker-high and -low expression groups were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test. Furthermore, immune cell infiltration was quantified using the R Immunedeconv package (29) integrating five deconvolution algorithms (xCell, TIMER, quanTIseq, EPIC, and ABIS). The association between biomarker expression and the infiltration levels of specific immune cells was evaluated using Spearman correlation analysis, and only correlations with P<0.05 were considered significant. The association between biomarkers and stromal components was explored by correlating their expression with curated gene sets for ECM (30), angiogenesis-related genes (ARGs) (31), and TGF-β signaling pathway genes (https://www.genome.jp/kegg/) (Tables S2-S4) using Spearman correlation analysis.

Single-cell resolution of biomarker expression

The scRNA-seq data (GSM4909289-GSM4909294) from GSE161529 were processed using the R Seurat package. Quality control criteria included retaining cells with >300 and <7,000 detected genes (nFeature_RNA), <10% mitochondrial gene content (percent.mt), and <3% erythrocyte gene content (percent.HB). Cells outside these thresholds were discarded to remove low-quality cells, multiplets, and erythrocyte contamination. After normalization using the LogNormalize method with a scale factor of 10,000, the top 3,000 highly variable genes were identified using the variance-stabilizing transformation method. PCA was performed based on these variable genes. Batch effects across samples were corrected using the Harmony algorithm with the sample origin (orig.ident) as the grouping variable. Dimensional reduction was performed with Uniform Manifold Approximation and Projection (UMAP) based on the top 20 principal components. Cell clustering was performed using the shared nearest neighbor (SNN) algorithm with a resolution of 0.5. Cell clusters were annotated based on canonical marker genes identified via the FindAllMarkers function (using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test with a log-fold change threshold of 0.25) and referencing public databases (e.g., CellMarker and PanglaoDB). Key marker genes used for annotation included CD3D (T cells), KRT18 (epithelial cells), CD68 (myeloid cells), IGHG1 (plasmablasts), DCN (fibroblasts), MS4A1 (B cells), PECAM1 (endothelial cells), PDGFRB (mesenchymal cells), and TPSB2 (mast cells). The expression patterns of the biomarkers were visualized across the annotated cell types using FeaturePlot and DotPlot functions to determine their cellular localization within the TME.

In silico drug sensitivity analysis

The corrected GEO55348 and GEO58984 datasets were merged into a unified cohort (GEOnew). The R OncoPredict (32) package was then employed to predict the in silico drug response for each sample, enabling the exploration of potential therapeutic strategies based on biomarker expression. Differences in drug sensitivity between the high- and low-risk groups were compared using the Wilcoxon rank-sum test.

Experimental validation by IHC

To validate the clinical relevance of the biomarkers, a retrospective cohort of 12 HER2-positive breast cancer patients from The First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University was assembled. This pilot cohort comprised six patients achieving pCR and six matched non-pCR counterparts following ≥4 cycles of dual-targeted neoadjuvant therapy (trastuzumab plus pertuzumab), matched for AJCC stage and treatment regimen. Detailed clinicopathological information is provided in Table S5. IHC was performed on diagnostic fine-needle aspiration biopsy (FNAB) specimens. After deparaffinization, rehydration, and antigen retrieval, sections were blocked and incubated with primary antibodies overnight at 4 ℃, followed by incubation with HRP-conjugated secondary antibodies (Table S6). Positive signals were visualized with DAB, and sections were counterstained with hematoxylin. The average optical density (AOD) of DYX1C1 and GNAI1 staining was quantified using ImageJ 1.54f (http://imagej.org) software. A paired t-test was used to compare AOD values between the pCR and non-pCR groups, with P<0.05 considered statistically significant. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of the First Affiliated Hospital of Xi’an Jiaotong University (No. XJTU1AF2023LSK-301) and all participants provided written informed consent prior to sample collection.

Statistical analysis

R (version 4.4.0) and RStudio (version 2023.9.1.494) were adopted to process data. P<0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Integrated bioinformatics identifies DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as relapse-associated biomarkers

To uncover the molecular determinants of relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer treated with trastuzumab, we first performed a differential expression analysis on two independent GEO cohorts (GSE55348 and GSE58984). This analysis identified 899 and 140 DEGs in GSE55348 and GSE58984, respectively (Figure 1A-1D). To pinpoint genes consistently associated with relapse, we intersected the DEG lists from both cohorts, revealing a core set of 7 genes that were consistently upregulated in relapsed tumors (Figure 1E,1F). Subsequent validation confirmed the significant upregulation of these 7 genes in the relapse groups of both datasets (Figure 1G-1J).

To further refine these candidates, we employed WGCNA on the GSE55348 cohort to identify modules of co-expressed genes correlated with the relapse phenotype. This analysis highlighted the blue and grey modules as most significantly associated with relapse (Figure 2A-2C). By intersecting the 7 consistently upregulated DEGs with the genes from these relapse-associated modules, we identified a final list of 6 hub candidates (Figure 2D). LASSO regression with 10-fold cross-validation selected DYX1C1, GNAI1, SGCE, and DPT as the predictive variables (Figure 2E,2F). Univariate and multivariate Cox regression analyses were then used to evaluate their prognostic value. Crucially, only DYX1C1 and GNAI1 emerged as independent risk factors for relapse in both the discovery (GSE55348) and validation (GSE58984) cohorts (Figure 2G-2J). These findings robustly establish DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as promising biomarkers for predicting relapse in trastuzumab-treated HER2-positive breast cancer.

A DYX1C1-GNAI1-based nomogram accurately predicts relapse and informs therapeutic strategies

Prior to model construction, PCA confirmed that ComBat correction effectively eliminated dataset-driven clustering between GSE55348 and GSE58984, yielding an intermingled distribution (Figure S1). While baseline characteristics revealed comparable age and hormone receptor status, the cohorts exhibited significant heterogeneity in nodal status and follow-up time (Table S1). This clinical divergence establishes a rigorous context for external validation, testing the model’s generalizability across distinct risk profiles.

Based on the independent prognostic power of DYX1C1 and GNAI1, we constructed a nomogram to quantitatively predict 3-year RFS (Figure 3A). The model demonstrated excellent discrimination in the training cohort (GEO55348: 22 relapse vs. 29 relapse-free patients), with an apparent C-index of 0.771 and an AUC of 0.894 (Figure 3B). Based on 1,000 bootstrap resamples, the optimism-corrected C-index was 0.763 (optimism =0.008), and the bootstrap-derived shrinkage factor of 0.986 indicated minimal overfitting. Calibration curves confirmed a high concordance between predicted and observed probabilities (Figure 3C), and DCA indicated substantial clinical net benefit across a wide range of threshold probabilities (Figure 3D). Patients stratified by the median nomogram score into high- and low-risk groups showed significantly different RFS outcomes, with the high-risk group exhibiting a markedly poorer prognosis (P<0.001) (Figure 3E).

The robustness of the nomogram was rigorously validated in the independent GEO58984 cohort (11 patients with distant metastasis vs. 83 metastasis-free patients). The model maintained strong predictive performance (optimism-corrected C-index =0.801; AUC =0.829) (Figure 3F), good calibration (Figure 3G), and clinical utility (Figure 3H). Furthermore, the risk stratification based on the training cohort’s cutoff effectively separated patients in the validation cohort into distinct prognostic groups (P=0.015) (Figure 3I). To explore the therapeutic implications of this risk stratification, we performed in silico drug sensitivity analysis. This revealed that the low-risk group was significantly more sensitive to a panel of agents, including the CDK4/6 inhibitor palbociclib and the PI3Kα inhibitor alpelisib (Figure S2). These results not only validate our nomogram as a powerful predictive tool but also suggest its potential to guide personalized therapeutic decisions.

DYX1C1 correlates with a relapse-associated immunosuppressive TME

To elucidate the biological role of DYX1C1, we performed single-gene GSEA, which revealed a significant enrichment of DYX1C1 in immune-related pathways (Figure 4A,4B). This prompted us to investigate its relationship with the TME. Using the ESTIMATE algorithm, we found that high DYX1C1 expression was associated with low Immune and ESTIMATE scores (Figure 4C), suggesting a more complex, yet potentially immunosuppressive TME. Indeed, a combined analysis of DYX1C1 expression and immune scores revealed that patients with high DYX1C1 expression but a low immune score had the worst RFS (Figure 4D), indicating that DYX1C1’s adverse effect is contingent on the immune contexture.

We then dissected the specific immune components involved. Multi-algorithm immune infiltration analysis consistently showed that DYX1C1 expression was negatively correlated with the infiltration of key anti-tumor immune cells, namely B cells and CD8+ T cells (Figure 4E). This was further confirmed by direct comparison of infiltration levels between DYX1C1-high and -low groups (Figure S3A-S3G) and by significant negative correlations between DYX1C1 and canonical markers of B cells (MS4A1, CD79A, CD19) and CD8+ T cells (CD8A) (Figure S3H). Collectively, these data suggest that DYX1C1 overexpression correlates with an immunosuppressive TME—characterized by B cell and cytotoxic T cell depletion—that may underlie relapse.

GNAI1 correlates with relapse-associated stromal activation and ECM remodeling

In parallel, we investigated the function of GNAI1. Single-gene GSEA pointed towards a strong association of GNAI1 with stromal and ECM-related pathways, including angiogenesis and the TGF-β signaling pathway (Figure 5A,5B). To explore these links, we performed co-expression analysis between GNAI1 and gene sets related to angiogenesis, ECM, and TGF-β signaling. We identified a substantial number of genes within these pathways that were significantly co-expressed with GNAI1 (Figure 5C-5H, Tables S7-S9). Notably, many of these co-expressed genes are core components of the ECM and key regulators of angiogenesis. These findings suggest that GNAI1 expression correlates with a pro-tumorigenic stromal phenotype—characterized by ECM remodeling and angiogenesis—that is associated with relapse, implicating the TGF-β pathway as a plausible upstream regulator.

Single-cell resolution maps DYX1C1 and GNAI1 to distinct cellular compartments

To pinpoint the cellular origins of DYX1C1 and GNAI1 within the TME, we analyzed a single-cell RNA-seq dataset (GSE161529) of HER2-positive breast cancer. After initial quality control, 29,835 cells were obtained. Unbiased clustering identified 9 main cell types on the basis of UMAP analysis according to the gene profiles and typical markers of the cells and these clusters were annotated using canonical lineage markers (Figure 6A,6B) (33). Feature plotting revealed distinct localization patterns: DYX1C1 expression was largely confined to malignant epithelial cells, whereas GNAI1 was expressed more broadly, with high levels in ECs, mesenchymal cells, and epithelial cells (Figure 6C,6D). This spatial resolution provides an anatomical context for our bulk-tissue findings: the epithelial-specific expression of DYX1C1 aligns with its association with immune cell exclusion, while the stromal expression of GNAI1 is consistent with its link to ECM and vascular remodeling.

Clinical and translational validation of DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as predictive biomarkers

Finally, we validated the clinical relevance of our findings at both the transcriptomic and protein levels. In the independent I-SPY2 trial cohort (n=127), DYX1C1 expression was significantly lower in patients achieving pCR following trastuzumab-based regimens, confirming its robust predictive value at scale (Figure S4). To bridge these transcriptomic findings to protein expression, we performed IHC on a pilot cohort (n=12). Consistent with the transcriptomic data, protein levels of both DYX1C1 and GNAI1 were significantly higher in non-pCR patients compared with pCR patients (Figure 6E,6F). While GNAI1 showed a similar trend in IHC, its transcriptomic difference in the I-SPY2 cohort was not significant, suggesting that DYX1C1 may be a more sensitive predictor of response to dual-target therapy.

Discussion

In this study, through an integrated bioinformatics framework, we identified and validated DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as robust, independent biomarkers for predicting relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer patients treated with trastuzumab. Leveraging these genes, we constructed a parsimonious yet highly accurate nomogram for predicting 3-year RFS. Beyond its predictive utility, our work proposes a compelling conceptual model wherein DYX1C1 and GNAI1 are associated with distinct pro-tumorigenic TME features—DYX1C1 with an immunosuppressive landscape and GNAI1 with stromal activation and remodeling. These findings not only offer a novel tool for risk stratification but also generate important hypotheses regarding the potential mechanisms of trastuzumab resistance.

The clinical management of HER2-positive breast cancer has been revolutionized by HER2-targeted therapies, yet relapse remains a critical challenge. Accurate prognostication is paramount for tailoring treatment intensity, particularly for de-escalating therapy in low-risk patients or intensifying it in high-risk cohorts. While existing models like HER2DX have advanced this field, our model offers a distinct, complementary approach. Because HER2DX is a proprietary assay, we could not perform a direct head-to-head statistical comparison on shared cohorts; thus, any cross-study performance comparison is inherently limited. Nevertheless, our two-gene model demonstrates numerically comparable performance (C-indices of 0.76 and 0.80) to the reported metrics of the HER2DX model (C-index of 0.74) and deep learning models (AUCs of 0.76 and 0.72) (15,16). Beyond predictive metrics, our model provides unique advantages: a parsimonious, cost-effective signature that is easier to implement, and transparent biological interpretability linking predictions to distinct TME resistance mechanisms. Future prospective studies with concurrent evaluation of both signatures are warranted to define their relative clinical utility.

The identification of DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as key drivers of relapse is particularly noteworthy given their established, yet distinct, roles in oncogenesis. GNAI1, a known promoter of cancer cell growth and invasion across various malignancies (34-37), aligns with our findings of its involvement in stromal activation. In contrast, the role of DYX1C1 in cancer, especially breast cancer, is less defined. While its upregulation in malignant breast tissue has been reported (38), our study is the first to link it directly to relapse and to define its function in shaping the immune microenvironment. The convergence of these two molecules, one from a developmental disorder context and the other a classic signal transduction protein, on the common outcome of trastuzumab resistance underscores the multifactorial nature of therapeutic failure.

Mechanistically, our computational analyses generate a compelling model for how DYX1C1 and GNAI1 may contribute to relapse by modulating distinct compartments of the TME. We found that high DYX1C1 expression is strongly associated with an immunologically “cold” tumor, characterized by a significant depletion of critical immune effector cells, such as B cells and CD8+ T cells. This is consistent with a large body of evidence demonstrating that robust infiltration of these cells correlates with improved survival in HER2-positive breast cancer (39,40). We therefore hypothesize that DYX1C1 overexpression may foster an immunosuppressive TME that blunts the efficacy of trastuzumab. Concurrently, GNAI1 emerges as a putative key player in the stromal compartment. Its positive correlation with genes involved in ECM remodeling, angiogenesis, and the TGF-β signaling pathway suggests that GNAI1 promotes the formation of a physical and biochemical barrier that supports tumor growth and metastasis. This finding resonates with previous studies linking Gαi1 to VEGF-induced angiogenesis (41) and ECM dynamics (42), and positions GNAI1 as a potential central node in the stromal response that may facilitate immune evasion and disease progression. Importantly, while our multi-omic analyses reveal robust associations, these computational findings are hypothesis-generating and require functional validation to establish causal relationships.

While RFS and pCR represent distinct biological endpoints, they are intrinsically linked in HER2-positive breast cancer, where achieving pCR is a robust surrogate for long-term survival (43,44). Although our RFS model was derived from trastuzumab monotherapy cohorts, we externally validated the biomarkers’ association with therapeutic resistance using the I-SPY2 cohort (pCR endpoint) and our IHC cohort (dual-targeted therapy). The consistent upregulation of DYX1C1 in non-pCR patients across these cohorts bridges its role in relapse prediction to its functional involvement in mediating trastuzumab resistance. This positions DYX1C1 as a promising, hypothesis-generating predictor for dual-targeted efficacy.

Despite these significant findings, our study has several limitations. First, while rigorously validated in public datasets, the predictive model requires independent, multi-institutional prospective validation. Second, our IHC validation involved a small pilot cohort (n=12). We acknowledge that this sample size is insufficient for definitive clinical conclusions; however, it is crucial to emphasize that the primary clinical validation of our biomarkers relied on the robust, large-scale I-SPY2 trial cohort (n=127). The IHC analysis served specifically as a proof-of-concept to bridge our mRNA-based signatures to protein expression. Future studies with larger prospective cohorts are warranted to confirm these protein-level associations. Third, our mechanistic insights are derived from comprehensive bioinformatic analyses. While we demonstrate robust multi-omic associations across bulk, single-cell, and protein levels, we acknowledge that computational correlations cannot establish definitive causality. Future studies employing rigorous functional validation—such as knockdown/overexpression assays, in vitro and in vivo modeling, and pathway-specific rescue experiments—are warranted to confirm these causal roles.

Conclusions

This study establishes DYX1C1 and GNAI1 as novel biomarkers for predicting relapse in HER2-positive breast cancer treated with trastuzumab, offering a concise nomogram for clinical risk stratification. Beyond prediction, our multi-omic data suggest these genes correlate with distinct pro-tumorigenic TME features—DYX1C1 with immune suppression and GNAI1 with stromal activation. These findings generate critical hypotheses for overcoming therapeutic resistance, pending functional validation, particularly highlighting the putative DYX1C1-immune and GNAI1-stromal axes as potential intervention targets.
