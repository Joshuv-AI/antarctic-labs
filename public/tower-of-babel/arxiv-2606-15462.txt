1.
Introduction

Let

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;27z\right)^{3}=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}z^{n}.

The sequence begins

1,\ 9,\ 135,\ 2439,\ 48519,\ 1023759,\ 22478121,\ldots.

The purpose of this paper is to prove the following prime-power supercongruence tower.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every prime
p\geq 5
and all integers
m,r\geq 1
,

A_{mp^{r}}\equiv A_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{4r}}.

We separate the new part from the existing level-three dictionary. Moy
[
4
, Section 5.2]
already recorded the exact pair

f_{1}=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{9}}{\eta(3\tau)^{3}},\qquad t=\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{12}}{\eta(\tau)^{12}},

and the expansion

f_{1}=1-9t+135t^{2}-2439t^{3}+48519t^{4}-1023759t^{5}+\cdots.

Moy’s framework yields the ordinary
p^{r}
-level congruences for these level-three expansions; for the first expansion
f_{1}
it gives
b_{1,\ell p^{r}}\equiv b_{1,\ell p^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{r}}
for all primes
p\geq 5
(by his Corollary 1.3, which removes the split condition of his Theorem 1.1). Thus neither the sequence, nor the modular parametrization, nor the ordinary
p^{r}
tower is claimed to be new here. The new point is the depth-preserving strengthening from the ordinary modulus
p^{r}
to the modulus
p^{4r}
for every prime
p\geq 5
.

The depth-one case
r=1
, namely

A_{mp}\equiv A_{m}\pmod{p^{4}},

was proved in
[
5
]
by a one-prime Hecke–Fricke descent. The present paper strengthens that result to the full prime-power tower by replacing the one-prime defect with prime-power Hecke lifts and sparse Cartier defects. A related split-prime supercongruence at the mixed CM point
(1/6,1/3;1)
was proved in
[
6
]
; that result is not used here.

Put

q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\qquad t(\tau)=\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{12}}{\eta(\tau)^{12}},\qquad B_{n}=(-1)^{n}A_{n}.

Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t^{n}={}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;-27t\right)^{3}=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{9}}{\eta(3\tau)^{3}}.

Define

C(q)=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t(q)^{n}\right)\frac{q}{t(q)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}q}.

Then
C=3E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}
, and hence

C(q)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}q^{n},\qquad c_{n}=3\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{3}(d)d^{4}.

The coefficient extraction is given by the Lagrange–Buermann formula

B_{m}=\CT_{q}\left(\frac{C(q)}{t(q)^{m}}\right).

The one-prime congruence follows from the vanishing modulo
p^{4}
of

\Lambda_{p}\left(\frac{C}{t^{mp}}\right)-\frac{C}{t^{m}},

where
\Lambda_{p}
is the Cartier operator
\sum a_{n}q^{n}\mapsto\sum a_{np}q^{n}
. For the full tower this full
q
-series vanishing is not the right object to iterate. The correct object is the sparse Cartier defect

\Lambda_{p}^{s}\left(\Lambda_{p}\left(\frac{C}{t^{mp^{s+1}}}\right)-\frac{C}{t^{mp^{s}}}\right).

The modular lift of this sparse defect is

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}:=T_{p^{s+1}}\left(\frac{C}{t^{mp^{s+1}}}\right)-T_{p^{s}}\left(\frac{C}{t^{mp^{s}}}\right).

Prime-power Hecke expansion shows that
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
differs from the sparse Cartier defect by a series divisible by
p^{4(s+1)}
, assuming the lower layers of the tower. The form
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
is weakly holomorphic of weight
5
and character
\chi_{3}
, with poles only at
\infty
. Since

M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})=\Span\{C,tC\},

it admits a cusp-adapted expansion

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}=\beta_{-1}tC+\sum_{j\geq 0}\alpha_{j}\frac{C}{t^{j}}.

The principal part gives
\alpha_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}
for
j\geq m
. Applying the Fricke involution
W_{3}
gives the exact order bound

\ord_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{m,s}|W_{3})\geq m+1,

which forces

\beta_{-1}=0,\qquad\alpha_{j}=0\quad(0\leq j\leq m-1).

Thus
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}
, and the same is true for the sparse Cartier defect. Taking constant terms gives Theorem
1.1
.

Conventions

All congruences between Laurent series are coefficientwise congruences in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}((q))
. The operator
\CT_{q}
denotes the coefficient of
q^{0}
in a Laurent series. The slash operator used below is determinant-normalized; it is defined explicitly in Section
4
.

2.
The modular parametrization

Let

\chi_{3}(n)=\left(\frac{n}{3}\right)

be the quadratic Dirichlet character modulo
3
.
Throughout the paper

q=e^{2\pi i\tau},\qquad\tau\in\mathfrak{H}.

Set

t(\tau)=\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{12}}{\eta(\tau)^{12}},\qquad\Theta(\tau)=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{9}}{\eta(3\tau)^{3}}.

Then

t(q)=q+12q^{2}+90q^{3}+508q^{4}+2391q^{5}+\cdots

and

H(q):=\frac{q}{t(q)}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}n\geq 1\\
3\nmid n\end{subarray}}(1-q^{n})^{12}\in 1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]].

Proposition 2.1

(Modular parametrization)
.

Let

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;27z\right)^{3}=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}z^{n},\qquad B_{n}=(-1)^{n}A_{n}.

Then

\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t(\tau)^{n}=\Theta(\tau).

Proof.

Let

a(q)=\sum_{m,n\in\mathbf{Z}}q^{m^{2}+mn+n^{2}},\qquad b(q)=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\eta(3\tau)},\qquad c(q)=3\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{3}}{\eta(\tau)}.

With this normalization, the cubic theory gives

a(q)^{3}=b(q)^{3}+c(q)^{3}

and

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{c(q)^{3}}{a(q)^{3}}\right)=a(q)

[
1
, Theorem 2.3 and Corollary 2.4]
.
Since

-27t(\tau)=-\frac{c(q)^{3}}{b(q)^{3}},

put
z=-27t(\tau)
. Pfaff’s transformation gives

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;z\right)=(1-z)^{-1/3}{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{2}{3};1;\frac{z}{z-1}\right).

Here

\frac{z}{z-1}=\frac{c(q)^{3}}{b(q)^{3}+c(q)^{3}}=\frac{c(q)^{3}}{a(q)^{3}},

and, with the branch normalized to have constant term
1
at
q=0
,

(1-z)^{-1/3}=\left(1+\frac{c(q)^{3}}{b(q)^{3}}\right)^{-1/3}=\frac{b(q)}{a(q)}.

Therefore

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;-27t(\tau)\right)=\frac{b(q)}{a(q)}a(q)=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{3}}{\eta(3\tau)}.

Cubing gives

\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t(\tau)^{n}={}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;-27t(\tau)\right)^{3}=\frac{\eta(\tau)^{9}}{\eta(3\tau)^{3}}.

∎

Define

C(\tau)=\Theta(\tau)\frac{Dt(\tau)}{t(\tau)},\qquad D=q\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}q}=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}\tau}.

Equivalently,

C(q)=\left(\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t(q)^{n}\right)\frac{q}{t(q)}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathrm{d}q}.

Proposition 2.2

(The logarithmic derivative)
.

The form
C
is the Eisenstein series

C=3E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}.

Consequently

C(q)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}q^{n},\qquad c_{n}=3\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{3}(d)d^{4}.

Proof.

The eta quotient
\Theta
has weight
3
and character
\chi_{3}
on
\Gamma_{0}(3)
, and
t
is a Hauptmodul on
X_{0}(3)
. Hence
C=\Theta\,Dt/t
is a meromorphic modular form of weight
5
and character
\chi_{3}
on
\Gamma_{0}(3)
.
The
q
-expansions

\Theta=1-9q+O(q^{2}),\qquad t=q+12q^{2}+O(q^{3})

give

\frac{Dt}{t}=1+12q+O(q^{2}),\qquad C=1+3q+O(q^{2}).

As proved independently in Proposition
4.1
, the form
C
is also holomorphic at the cusp
0
; hence
C\in M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
.
We now recall the needed dimension statement in this special case. The eta quotient
\Theta
is nonzero on
\mathfrak{H}
, has order
0
at
\infty
, and has order
1
at the cusp
0
by Proposition
4.1
. If
f\in S_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
, then
f/\Theta
is a holomorphic weight-two form with trivial character on
\Gamma_{0}(3)
and has zero constant term at
\infty
. Since
X_{0}(3)
has genus zero,
S_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(3))=0
, and
M_{2}(\Gamma_{0}(3))
is the one-dimensional Eisenstein space spanned by
E_{2}(\tau)-3E_{2}(3\tau)
, whose constant term at
\infty
is nonzero. Hence
f/\Theta=0
, and therefore
f=0
. Thus
S_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})=0
. The Eisenstein subspace has the two standard Eisenstein series associated with the decompositions
\chi_{3}=\chi_{0}\chi_{3}=\chi_{3}\chi_{0}
; equivalently, by the standard dimension formula for modular forms with character on
\Gamma_{0}(N)
,

M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})=\Span\{E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}},E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}\}.

Here

E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}(q)=\frac{1}{3}+\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{3}(d)d^{4}\right)q^{n}

and

E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}(q)=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\chi_{3}(n/d)d^{4}\right)q^{n}.

Thus

3E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}=1+3q+O(q^{2}),\qquad E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}=q+O(q^{2}).

Since
C
and
3E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}
have the same constant term and the same coefficient of
q
, their difference is a multiple of
E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}
with zero coefficient of
q
, and therefore is zero. The divisor-sum formula follows from the displayed Fourier expansion of
E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}}
.
∎

Remark 2.3
.

The same conclusion also follows directly from the usual dimension formula; see, for example,
[
2
, Sections 3.5–3.6]
. The quotient argument above is included to make explicit why the cusp-space contribution is zero in this particular weight and level.

3.
Lagrange–Buermann and integrality

The next proposition is the coefficient-extraction formula that converts congruences of Laurent series into congruences for
B_{m}
.

Proposition 3.1

(Lagrange–Buermann)
.

For every
m\geq 0
,

B_{m}=\CT_{q}\left(\frac{C(q)}{t(q)^{m}}\right).

Proof.

Since

\Theta(t)=\sum_{n\geq 0}B_{n}t^{n},

one has

B_{m}=\CT_{t}\left(\frac{\Theta(t)}{t^{m}}\right)=\Res_{t=0}\left(\frac{\Theta(t)}{t^{m}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right).

The series
t(q)=q+O(q^{2})
is a formal coordinate at
q=0
. Changing variables gives

\Res_{t=0}\left(\frac{\Theta(t)}{t^{m}}\frac{\mathrm{d}t}{t}\right)=\Res_{q=0}\left(\frac{\Theta(t(q))}{t(q)^{m}}\frac{q\,t^{\prime}(q)}{t(q)}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{q}\right).

The numerator is exactly
C(q)
. Hence

B_{m}=\Res_{q=0}\left(\frac{C(q)}{t(q)^{m}}\frac{\mathrm{d}q}{q}\right)=\CT_{q}\left(\frac{C(q)}{t(q)^{m}}\right).

∎

Corollary 3.2

(Integrality)
.

For every
m\geq 0
, one has
B_{m}\in\mathbf{Z}
and hence
A_{m}\in\mathbf{Z}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,

B_{m}=\CT_{q}\left(\frac{C(q)}{t(q)^{m}}\right).

Here
C(q)\in\mathbf{Z}[[q]]
by Proposition
2.2
, and

t(q)^{-m}=q^{-m}H(q)^{m},\qquad H(q)=q/t(q)\in 1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]].

Thus
C(q)t(q)^{-m}\in q^{-m}\mathbf{Z}[[q]]
, so its constant term is an integer.
Since
B_{m}=(-1)^{m}A_{m}
, this also gives
A_{m}\in\mathbf{Z}
.
∎

Remark 3.3

(Eisenstein coefficient tower)
.

The Eisenstein coefficients themselves satisfy the elementary congruence

c_{mp^{r}}\equiv c_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{4r}}\qquad(p\geq 5,\ m,r\geq 1).

Indeed, if
c_{n}=3\sigma_{4,\chi_{3}}(n)
and
m=p^{a}m_{0}
with
p\nmid m_{0}
, then multiplicativity gives

\sigma_{4,\chi_{3}}(mp^{r})-\sigma_{4,\chi_{3}}(mp^{r-1})=\chi_{3}(p)^{a+r}p^{4(a+r)}\sigma_{4,\chi_{3}}(m_{0}).

This observation is not used in the proof of the full tower; the tower below is obtained from prime-power Hecke defects.

Remark 3.4
.

Although the Eisenstein coefficients satisfy the stronger congruence

c_{mp^{r}}\equiv c_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{4r}}
of Remark
3.3
,
the formal Moy transfer (Proposition 3.1 of
[
4
]
) preserves only the
ordinary Dwork level
p^{r}
in this setting. The additional precision
p^{3r}

is not produced by the formal differential congruence alone; here it is
recovered from the prime-power Hecke expansion (Proposition
5.1
)
and the Fricke order drop (Theorem
7.1
).

4.
Fricke identities and cusp-adapted expansions

We use the determinant-normalized slash operator. For

\gamma=\begin{pmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{pmatrix}\in GL_{2}^{+}(\mathbf{Q})

and a function
f
of weight
k
, set

(f|_{k}\gamma)(\tau)=\det(\gamma)^{k/2}(c\tau+d)^{-k}f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{c\tau+d}\right).

Let

w_{3}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-1\\
3&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad W_{3}f=f|_{k}w_{3}

when
f
has weight
k
.

Proposition 4.1

(Fricke identities)
.

One has

t|_{0}W_{3}=\frac{3^{-6}}{t},\qquad\Theta|_{3}W_{3}=27i\,t\Theta,\qquad\left(\frac{Dt}{t}\right)\!\Big|_{2}W_{3}=-\frac{Dt}{t}.

Consequently

C|W_{3}=-27i\,tC,\qquad(tC)|W_{3}=-\frac{i}{27}C.

Moreover

\ord_{0}(C)=1,\qquad\ord_{0}(tC)=0,\qquad\ord_{0}\left(\frac{C}{t^{j}}\right)=j+1\quad(j\geq 0).

Proof.

We use the standard branch of the square root in the eta transformation formula

\eta(-1/\tau)=(-i\tau)^{1/2}\eta(\tau).

With this convention the constants below are exactly as written; only their non-vanishing and the resulting orders are used later.
The formula gives immediately

t\left(-\frac{1}{3\tau}\right)=\frac{3^{-6}}{t(\tau)},

which is the first identity.
For
\Theta
,

(\Theta|_{3}W_{3})(\tau)=3^{3/2}(3\tau)^{-3}\Theta\left(-\frac{1}{3\tau}\right).

Again using the eta transformation formula,

\Theta\left(-\frac{1}{3\tau}\right)=\frac{\eta(-1/(3\tau))^{9}}{\eta(-1/\tau)^{3}}=3^{9/2}(-i\tau)^{3}\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{9}}{\eta(\tau)^{3}}.

Thus

\Theta|_{3}W_{3}=27i\frac{\eta(3\tau)^{9}}{\eta(\tau)^{3}}=27i\,t\Theta.

For a weight-zero function
f
, the chain rule gives

D(f|_{0}\gamma)=(Df)|_{2}\gamma.

Applying this to
f=t
and
\gamma=w_{3}
gives

(Dt)|_{2}W_{3}=D\left(\frac{3^{-6}}{t}\right)=-\frac{3^{-6}}{t^{2}}Dt.

Dividing by
t|_{0}W_{3}=3^{-6}/t
gives

\left(\frac{Dt}{t}\right)\!\Big|_{2}W_{3}=-\frac{Dt}{t}.

Multiplying with the identity for
\Theta
yields

C|W_{3}=(\Theta|_{3}W_{3})\left((Dt/t)|_{2}W_{3}\right)=-27i\,tC.

Since
w_{3}^{2}=-3I
, one has
(f|W_{3})|W_{3}=-f
for weight
5
. Applying
W_{3}
to
C|W_{3}=-27i\,tC
gives

(tC)|W_{3}=-\frac{i}{27}C.

Finally
\ord_{\infty}(C)=0
and
\ord_{\infty}(tC)=1
, so

\ord_{0}(C)=\ord_{\infty}(C|W_{3})=1,\qquad\ord_{0}(tC)=\ord_{\infty}((tC)|W_{3})=0.

Since
\ord_{0}(t)=-1
, the formula

\ord_{0}(C/t^{j})=j+1

follows.
∎

Proposition 4.2

(Weight-five basis)
.

One has

M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})=\Span\{C,tC\}.

Equivalently,

C=3E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}},\qquad tC=E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}.

Proof.

The first identity is Proposition
2.2
. The form
tC
is holomorphic at both cusps by Proposition
4.1
; hence
tC\in M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
. Its expansion begins

tC=q+15q^{2}+81q^{3}+O(q^{4}),

which is the beginning of
E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}
. Since

M_{5}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})=\Span\{E_{5,\chi_{0},\chi_{3}},E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}\}

and
tC
has zero constant term and leading coefficient
1
, it follows that

tC=E_{5,\chi_{3},\chi_{0}}.

The two forms
C
and
tC
are linearly independent because their orders at
\infty
are
0
and
1
.
∎

Proposition 4.3

(Pole-lowering expansion)
.

Let
f\in M_{5}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
have poles only at the cusp
\infty
, and suppose the pole order at
\infty
is at most
N
. Then

f=\beta_{-1}tC+\sum_{j=0}^{N}\alpha_{j}\frac{C}{t^{j}}

for some
\beta_{-1},\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N}\in\mathbf{C}
.
If the
q
-expansion of
f
is in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}((q))
, then all these coefficients lie in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}
.

Proof.

For
N\geq 1
, the form
C/t^{N}=q^{-N}+O(q^{-N+1})
has leading coefficient
1
. Subtracting a unique multiple of
C/t^{N}
lowers the pole order by one. Repeating this process leaves a holomorphic form of weight
5
and character
\chi_{3}
, hence an element of
\Span\{C,tC\}
by Proposition
4.2
. This gives the expansion. The case
N=0
is exactly Proposition
4.2
.

If the
q
-expansion of
f
has coefficients in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}
, then the coefficients in the expansion are recovered by descending triangular elimination. The diagonal terms are

\frac{C}{t^{j}}=q^{-j}+O(q^{-j+1}),\qquad C=1+O(q),\qquad tC=q+O(q^{2}),

whose diagonal entries are
1
. Since each basis element has
q
-expansion in
\mathbf{Z}[[q,q^{-1}]]
, the recovered coefficients lie in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}
.
∎

5.
Hecke operators and Fricke intertwining

For a Laurent series

f(q)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n}

define

\Lambda_{p}f=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{np}q^{n},\qquad V_{p}f=f(q^{p})=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{pn}.

Then
\Lambda_{p}V_{p}=1
.
For
p\nmid 3
we use the Hecke normalization compatible with the determinant-normalized slash operator:

T_{p}f=p^{k/2-1}\left(\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}f|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}+\chi_{3}(p)f|_{k}\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\right)

on weight-
k
forms with character
\chi_{3}
. On
q
-expansions this gives

T_{p}f=\Lambda_{p}f+\chi_{3}(p)p^{k-1}V_{p}f.

The prime-power operators
T_{p^{a}}
are normalized by the standard Hecke recurrence

T_{p}T_{p^{a}}=T_{p^{a+1}}+\chi_{3}(p)p^{k-1}T_{p^{a-1}}\qquad(a\geq 1),

with
T_{1}
the identity; see, for instance,
[
3
, Chapter 4]
.

Proposition 5.1

(Prime-power Hecke expansion)
.

Let
p\geq 5
, put
\chi=\chi_{3}(p)
, and let
a\geq 0
. On
q
-expansions of weakly holomorphic forms of weight
5
and character
\chi_{3}
on
\Gamma_{0}(3)
,

T_{p^{a}}=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\chi^{i}p^{4i}V_{p}^{i}\Lambda_{p}^{a-i}.

In particular,

T_{p}=\Lambda_{p}+\chi p^{4}V_{p}.

Proof.

For
a=0
the formula is the identity operator, and for
a=1
it is exactly the
q
-expansion formula coming from the normalization fixed above:

T_{p}f=\Lambda_{p}f+\chi p^{4}V_{p}f.

Let

S_{a}=\sum_{i=0}^{a}\chi^{i}p^{4i}V_{p}^{i}\Lambda_{p}^{a-i}.

Only the identity
\Lambda_{p}V_{p}=1
is used here; we do not use the false identity
V_{p}\Lambda_{p}=1
. For
i\geq 1
one has
\Lambda_{p}V_{p}^{i}=V_{p}^{i-1}
. Hence

\displaystyle S_{1}S_{a}

\displaystyle=(\Lambda_{p}+\chi p^{4}V_{p})\sum_{i=0}^{a}\chi^{i}p^{4i}V_{p}^{i}\Lambda_{p}^{a-i}

\displaystyle=\left(\Lambda_{p}^{a+1}+\sum_{j=1}^{a+1}\chi^{j}p^{4j}V_{p}^{j}\Lambda_{p}^{a+1-j}\right)+\chi p^{4}\sum_{j=0}^{a-1}\chi^{j}p^{4j}V_{p}^{j}\Lambda_{p}^{a-1-j}

\displaystyle=S_{a+1}+\chi p^{4}S_{a-1}\qquad(a\geq 1).

This is the same recurrence as the Hecke operators

T_{p}T_{p^{a}}=T_{p^{a+1}}+\chi p^{4}T_{p^{a-1}}

for primes
p\nmid 3
and weight
5
. Since
S_{0}=T_{1}
and
S_{1}=T_{p}
, the displayed formula follows by induction on
a
.
∎

Lemma 5.2

(Holomorphy at the cusp
0
)
.

If
f\in M_{5}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
is holomorphic at the cusp
0
, then
T_{p^{a}}f
is holomorphic at the cusp
0
for every
a\geq 0
and every prime
p\geq 5
.

Proof.

It is enough to prove the assertion for
T_{p}
, since the prime-power operators are obtained from
T_{p}
by the Hecke recurrence. Write the cusps of
\Gamma_{0}(3)
as
\infty
and
0
. A reduced fraction
u/v
is equivalent to
\infty
precisely when
3\mid v
, and is equivalent to
0
otherwise.

In the usual formula for
T_{p}
, the arguments are
p\tau
and
(\tau+b)/p
for
0\leq b<p
. If
\tau=u/v
represents cusp
0
, then
3\nmid v
. The reduced denominator of
pu/v
is either
v
or
v/p
, and the reduced denominator of
(u+bv)/(pv)
is either
pv
or
v
. Since
p\neq 3
, all these denominators remain prime to
3
. Hence the Hecke correspondence over the cusp
0
maps only to the cusp
0
; equivalently, since
p\nmid 3
, the operator
T_{p}
preserves the cusp class
0
on
X_{0}(3)
. Therefore no pole at
\infty
can be pulled back to the cusp
0
, and
T_{p}f
is holomorphic at
0
.
∎

Proposition 5.3

(Fricke–Hecke intertwining)
.

For every
a\geq 0
and every prime
p\geq 5
,

T_{p^{a}}W_{3}=\chi_{3}(p)^{a}W_{3}T_{p^{a}}

on
M_{5}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
.

Proof.

The case
a=0
is trivial. The matrix
w_{3}
interchanges the two cusps
0
and
\infty
; with the determinant-normalized slash operator used here this conjugation does not introduce an additional character factor in the quadratic character case beyond the displayed powers of
\chi_{3}(p)
. For
a=1
, use the double-coset representatives

\alpha_{b}=\begin{pmatrix}1&b\\
0&p\end{pmatrix}\quad(0\leq b<p),\qquad\beta=\begin{pmatrix}p&0\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}.

With the normalization fixed above, specialized to weight
5
, the Hecke operator is

T_{p}f=p^{3/2}\left(\sum_{b=0}^{p-1}f|_{5}\alpha_{b}+\chi_{3}(p)f|_{5}\beta\right).

For
b\neq 0
, choose
b^{\prime}
modulo
p
such that
3bb^{\prime}\equiv-1\pmod{p}
, and set

\gamma_{b}=\begin{pmatrix}p&-b^{\prime}\\
-3b&(1+3bb^{\prime})/p\end{pmatrix}\in\Gamma_{0}(3).

Then

w_{3}\alpha_{0}=\beta w_{3},\qquad w_{3}\beta=\alpha_{0}w_{3},\qquad w_{3}\alpha_{b}=\gamma_{b}\alpha_{b^{\prime}}w_{3}\quad(b\neq 0).

If
d_{b}=(1+3bb^{\prime})/p
is the lower-right entry of
\gamma_{b}
, then
d_{b}p\equiv 1\pmod{3}
, hence

\chi_{3}(d_{b})=\chi_{3}(p)^{-1}=\chi_{3}(p).

Using the transformation law
f|_{5}\gamma=\chi_{3}(d)f
for

\gamma=\bigl(\begin{smallmatrix}*&*\\
&d\end{smallmatrix}\bigr)\in\Gamma_{0}(3)
, the preceding identities give

T_{p}(f|W_{3})=\chi_{3}(p)(T_{p}f)|W_{3}.

This proves the case
a=1
.

For
a\geq 2
, the Hecke recurrence

T_{p}T_{p^{a-1}}=T_{p^{a}}+\chi_{3}(p)p^{4}T_{p^{a-2}}

and the induction hypothesis give

\displaystyle T_{p^{a}}W_{3}

\displaystyle=T_{p}T_{p^{a-1}}W_{3}-\chi_{3}(p)p^{4}T_{p^{a-2}}W_{3}

\displaystyle=\chi_{3}(p)^{a-1}T_{p}W_{3}T_{p^{a-1}}-\chi_{3}(p)^{a-1}p^{4}W_{3}T_{p^{a-2}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{3}(p)^{a}W_{3}T_{p}T_{p^{a-1}}-\chi_{3}(p)^{a+1}p^{4}W_{3}T_{p^{a-2}}

\displaystyle=\chi_{3}(p)^{a}W_{3}T_{p^{a}}.

In the third line we used
\chi_{3}(p)^{2}=1
.
∎

Lemma 5.4

(Order estimate)
.

Let
a\geq 0
,
p\geq 5
, and let

g(q)=\sum_{n\geq N}a_{n}q^{n}

with
N\geq 0
. Then

\ord_{\infty}(T_{p^{a}}g)\geq\left\lceil\frac{N}{p^{a}}\right\rceil.

Proof.

Use Proposition
5.1
. The term
\Lambda_{p}^{a-i}g
has order at least
\lceil N/p^{a-i}\rceil
, and applying
V_{p}^{i}
multiplies the order by
p^{i}
. Thus

\ord_{\infty}(V_{p}^{i}\Lambda_{p}^{a-i}g)\geq p^{i}\left\lceil\frac{N}{p^{a-i}}\right\rceil\geq\left\lceil\frac{N}{p^{a}}\right\rceil.

Taking the minimum over
0\leq i\leq a
gives the claim.
∎

6.
The sparse Cartier defect

For
N\geq 0
put

f_{N}=\frac{C}{t^{N}}.

Since

C\in\mathbf{Z}[[q]],\qquad\frac{1}{t}=q^{-1}H(q),\qquad H(q)\in 1+q\mathbf{Z}[[q]],

one has

f_{N}=q^{-N}C(q)H(q)^{N}\in q^{-N}\mathbf{Z}[[q]].

For
s\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
, define the sparse Cartier defect

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}:=\Lambda_{p}^{s}\left(\Lambda_{p}f_{mp^{s+1}}-f_{mp^{s}}\right)=\Lambda_{p}^{s+1}f_{mp^{s+1}}-\Lambda_{p}^{s}f_{mp^{s}}.

Also define the modular Hecke lift

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}:=T_{p^{s+1}}f_{mp^{s+1}}-T_{p^{s}}f_{mp^{s}}.

Lemma 6.1

(Pole order of the sparse defect)
.

For every
s\geq 0
and
m\geq 1
,

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}=O(q^{-m+1})

at
\infty
.

Proof.

The two Laurent series
\Lambda_{p}f_{mp^{s+1}}
and
f_{mp^{s}}
both begin with
q^{-mp^{s}}
with leading coefficient
1
, so their difference is

\Lambda_{p}f_{mp^{s+1}}-f_{mp^{s}}=O(q^{-mp^{s}+1}).

Applying
\Lambda_{p}^{s}
keeps only exponents divisible by
p^{s}
and divides such exponents by
p^{s}
. The smallest multiple of
p^{s}
which is at least
-mp^{s}+1
is
-(m-1)p^{s}
. Hence

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}=O(q^{-m+1}).

∎

Lemma 6.2

(Comparison between Hecke and sparse defects)
.

Assume that for all
t<s
and all
M\geq 1
one has

\mathcal{F}_{M,t}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(t+1)}}.

Then, for every
m\geq 1
,

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{m,s}\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}.

For
s=0
the same conclusion holds without any hypothesis.

Proof.

Put
\chi=\chi_{3}(p)
. By Proposition
5.1
, the
i=0
part of
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
is exactly
\mathcal{F}_{m,s}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{m,s}-\mathcal{F}_{m,s}={}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{s}\chi^{i}p^{4i}V_{p}^{i}\left(\Lambda_{p}^{s+1-i}f_{mp^{s+1}}-\Lambda_{p}^{s-i}f_{mp^{s}}\right)

\displaystyle+\chi^{s+1}p^{4(s+1)}V_{p}^{s+1}f_{mp^{s+1}}.

For
1\leq i\leq s
, the expression in parentheses is

\Lambda_{p}^{s-i}\left(\Lambda_{p}f_{mp^{s+1}}-f_{mp^{s}}\right)=\mathcal{F}_{mp^{i},s-i},

because
mp^{s}=(mp^{i})p^{s-i}
. By the induction hypothesis it is divisible by

p^{4(s-i+1)}.

Multiplication by
p^{4i}
gives divisibility by
p^{4(s+1)}
. The final term is visibly divisible by
p^{4(s+1)}
. This proves the claim. For
s=0
, the sum over
1\leq i\leq s
is empty and only the final term remains.
∎

Lemma 6.3

(Modularity and cusps of
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
)
.

For all
m\geq 1
and
s\geq 0
, the form
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
belongs to
M_{5}^{!}(\Gamma_{0}(3),\chi_{3})
and has poles only at the cusp
\infty
.

Proof.

The form
f_{N}=C/t^{N}
is weakly holomorphic of weight
5
and character
\chi_{3}
. By Proposition
4.1
, it is holomorphic at the cusp
0
. Hecke operators preserve weak modularity and, by Lemma
5.2
, preserve holomorphy at the cusp
0
. Thus both terms defining
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
are weakly holomorphic and holomorphic at
0
. Since
X_{0}(3)
has only the two cusps
0
and
\infty
, all poles of
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
are at
\infty
.
∎

7.
Proof of the tower

Theorem 7.1

(Sparse Cartier tower)
.

For every prime
p\geq 5
, every
m\geq 1
, and every
s\geq 0
,

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}.

Proof.

We prove the statement by induction on
s
, uniformly in
m
.
Assume first that the statement is known for all lower values
t<s
; when
s=0
this assumption is empty.
By Lemma
6.2
,

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{m,s}\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}.

By Lemma
6.1
,

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}=O(q^{-m+1}).

Therefore

[q^{-j}]\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}\qquad(j\geq m).

Moreover,
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}
has
q
-expansion in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}((q))
: this follows from
f_{N}\in q^{-N}\mathbf{Z}[[q]]
and the prime-power expansion of Proposition
5.1
. Therefore, by Lemma
6.3
and Proposition
4.3
, there is a finite expansion

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}=\beta_{-1}tC+\sum_{j=0}^{J}\alpha_{j}\frac{C}{t^{j}}

with coefficients in
\mathbf{Z}_{(p)}
. The triangular form of the basis at
\infty
gives, by descending induction on
j
,

\alpha_{j}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}\qquad(j\geq m).

Indeed, the coefficient of
q^{-j}
in
C/t^{j}
is
1
, and only the terms
C/t^{\ell}
with
\ell\geq j
can contribute to
q^{-j}
.

It remains to kill the coefficients with
j<m
and the coefficient of
tC
. Apply
W_{3}
. By Proposition
5.3
,

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}|W_{3}=\chi_{3}(p)^{s+1}T_{p^{s+1}}(f_{mp^{s+1}}|W_{3})-\chi_{3}(p)^{s}T_{p^{s}}(f_{mp^{s}}|W_{3}).

By Proposition
4.1
,

f_{N}|W_{3}=\left(\frac{C}{t^{N}}\right)\Big|W_{3}=-27i\,3^{6N}Ct^{N+1}.

Thus

\ord_{\infty}(f_{mp^{s+1}}|W_{3})=mp^{s+1}+1,\qquad\ord_{\infty}(f_{mp^{s}}|W_{3})=mp^{s}+1.

Lemma
5.4
gives

\ord_{\infty}T_{p^{s+1}}(f_{mp^{s+1}}|W_{3})\geq\left\lceil\frac{mp^{s+1}+1}{p^{s+1}}\right\rceil=m+1

and

\ord_{\infty}T_{p^{s}}(f_{mp^{s}}|W_{3})\geq\left\lceil\frac{mp^{s}+1}{p^{s}}\right\rceil=m+1.

Therefore

\ord_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{m,s}|W_{3})\geq m+1.

On the other hand,

(tC)|W_{3}=-\frac{i}{27}C=-27i\,3^{-6}C

and, for
j\geq 0
,

\left(\frac{C}{t^{j}}\right)\Big|W_{3}=-27i\,3^{6j}Ct^{j+1}.

Hence

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}|W_{3}=-27i\left(\beta_{-1}3^{-6}C+\sum_{j=0}^{J}\alpha_{j}3^{6j}Ct^{j+1}\right).

The forms

C,\ Ct,\ Ct^{2},\ldots

have distinct orders
0,1,2,\ldots
at
\infty
. Since
\ord_{\infty}(\mathcal{G}_{m,s}|W_{3})\geq m+1
, the coefficients of
C,Ct,\ldots,Ct^{m}
vanish exactly. Therefore

\beta_{-1}=0,\qquad\alpha_{j}=0\quad(0\leq j\leq m-1).

Combining these exact equalities with the congruences for
j\geq m
gives

\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}.

Since
\mathcal{G}_{m,s}\equiv\mathcal{F}_{m,s}\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}
, we conclude

\mathcal{F}_{m,s}\equiv 0\pmod{p^{4(s+1)}}.

This completes the induction on
s
.
∎

Theorem 7.2

(Full
p^{4r}
tower)
.

Let

{}_{2}F_{1}\!\left(\frac{1}{3},\frac{1}{3};1;27z\right)^{3}=\sum_{n\geq 0}A_{n}z^{n}.

Then, for every prime
p\geq 5
and every pair of integers
m,r\geq 1
,

A_{mp^{r}}\equiv A_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{4r}}.

Proof.

By Proposition
3.1
,

B_{N}=\CT_{q}(f_{N}).

Let
r=s+1
. Then

B_{mp^{s+1}}-B_{mp^{s}}=\CT_{q}(f_{mp^{s+1}})-\CT_{q}(f_{mp^{s}}).

For any Laurent series
g
,
\CT_{q}(\Lambda_{p}g)=\CT_{q}(g)
; hence also
\CT_{q}(\Lambda_{p}^{s}g)=\CT_{q}(g)
. Therefore

B_{mp^{s+1}}-B_{mp^{s}}=\CT_{q}\left(\Lambda_{p}^{s+1}f_{mp^{s+1}}-\Lambda_{p}^{s}f_{mp^{s}}\right)=\CT_{q}(\mathcal{F}_{m,s}).

By Theorem
7.1
, the last constant term is divisible by
p^{4(s+1)}
. Thus

B_{mp^{r}}\equiv B_{mp^{r-1}}\pmod{p^{4r}}.

Finally
B_{n}=(-1)^{n}A_{n}
, and
p
is odd, so

(-1)^{mp^{r}}=(-1)^{mp^{r-1}}.

The same congruence therefore holds for
A_{n}
.
∎
