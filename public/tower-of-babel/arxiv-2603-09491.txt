The homotopy type of the moment-angle complex associated to the complex of injective words
Pedro Conceição

Abstract
Topological methods have emerged as valuable tools for analyzing the structural properties of directed graphs, particularly connectome data in computational neuroscience. This paper investigates the construction of topological spaces from combinatorial data of directed graphs using the polyhedral product functor, with particular emphasis on understanding their homotopy type, which is also of independent interest in topology and combinatorics. Specifically, we compute the homotopy type of the moment-angle complex over the face poset of the complex of injective words. This reveals a tight connection between homotopy and combinatorics: its homotopy type is determined by the h h -vector of complexes of injective words. We also construct an associated homotopy fibration of polyhedral products associated to ordered simplicial complexes, which in a way generalizes the analogous homotopy fibration for polyhedral products over abstract simplicial complexes.



Introduction

Ideas from combinatorial topology have recently found applications in computational neuroscience. A successful example is the construction of a topological space from the combinatorial data of an underlying connectome to study its global structure. In this context, a connectome is represented as a directed graph, commonly referred to as a digraph. Topological spaces serve as powerful tools for investigating connectome architecture, as their inherent properties reveal insights into the network’s structural organization. In particular, the directed flag complex is a higher dimensional topological space made out of directed cliques of the underlying directed graph, which has recently been used as such a tool and provided valuable insights to functional properties of the connectome
[
9
,
23
]
.

This paper investigates polyhedral product spaces as a more general construction of topological spaces from combinatorial data of digraphs and directed flag complexes, focusing on understanding their homotopy type. Polyhedral product spaces are topological spaces contructed by gluing together other spaces according to a combinatorial recipe determined by a poset
\mathcal{P}
. Introduced by Bahri, Bendersky, Cohen, and Gitler
[
1
]
, the polyhedral product space generalizes two fundamental families of topological spaces in toric topology: Davis-Januszkiewicz spaces, denoted by
DJ_{\mathcal{P}}
), and moment-angle complexes, denoted by
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}

[
11
]
. In particular, polyhedral product spaces are constructed from diagrams of spaces. Connection can be drawn to the work of Ziegler and Živaljević
[
28
]
, developed further by Welker, Ziegler and Živaljević
[
27
]
, which are the first combinatorial application of the theory of diagram of spaces.

While polyhedral product spaces were originally defined specifically for cases where
\mathcal{P}
is the face poset of a simplicial complex, this concept has been extended to increasingly general poset structures
[
21
,
20
]
. This generalization is essential for our study, as a directed flag complex is generally not a simplicial complex. The simplest example consists of a topological space defined by two vertices reciprocally connected; this a directed flag complex but it is not a simplicial complex.

Specifically, we study the moment-angle complex associated with the complex of injective words on
[n]:=\{1,…,n\}
letters, which we denote by
\mathcal{Z}_{\Gamma_{n}}
. The complex of injective words is a well-studied object in mathematics
[
13
,
5
,
8
,
19
]
and, in our setting, can be defined as the directed flag complex of the complete
n
-digraph. Directed flag complex and the complex of injective words are examples of what we call an ordered simplicial complexes. An ordered simplicial complex on
[n]
is a collection of finite ordered subsets of
[n]
that is closed under taking ordered subsets. Its 1-skeleton is a digraph with no loops and no multiple edges in the same direction.

While the cohomology of moment-angle complexes is well understood, their homotopy type remains largely unexplored. Previous work has identified cases where such complexes are homotopy equivalent to wedges of spheres
[
16
,
15
,
18
]
; however, these results have been restricted to moment-angle complexes associated to simplicial complexes. Our work extends this investigation to the ordered simplicial complex setting.

Our first main results starts from observation that the additive structure of the cohomology of the moment angle complex associated to the complex of injective words
\mathcal{Z}_{\Gamma_{n}}
can be precisely computed using the combinatorics properties of the complex of injective words. Based on this, we show that homototpy colimit construction of
\mathcal{Z}_{\Gamma_{n}}
can be computed precisely, yielding a tight connection to combinatorial proprieties of the complex on injective words.

Theorem A
.

\mathcal{Z}_{\Gamma_{n}}\simeq\bigvee_{k}h_{k}S^{2k}\text{ with }n\geq 2\text{ and }2\leq k\leq n,

where
h_{k}
is the
k
th element of the
h
-vector of the complex of injective words.

Our second result starts from the observation that any ordered simplicial complex injects into the
complex of injective words on the same vertex set. From this observation and Theorem

Theorem B
.

There is a homotopy fibration given by

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}(\Omega(\bigvee h_{k}S^{2k}),\Omega(\bigvee h_{k}S^{2k})\times S^{1})\to\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},*)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\Gamma_{n}}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},*),

where
\mathcal{P}
and
\Gamma_{n}
are, respectively, the face poset of an ordered simplicial complex and of the complex of injective words both on
n
vertices, and

is the the

th element of the
h
-vector of the complex of injective words.

Theorem

generalizes the well-known homotopy fibration of polyhedral products, which, like to the ordered case, relies on the fact that any simplicial complex injects into the simplex on the same vertex set.

\mathcal{Z}_{K}(D^{2},S^{1})\to\mathcal{Z}_{K}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},*)\to\mathcal{Z}_{\Delta^{(n-1)}}(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},*)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},

where
K
is a simplicial complex on
n
vertices and
\Delta^{(n-1)}
is the

-simplex.

Properties of the complex of injective words.

We now recall important properties of the complex of injective words.

Definition 1.4
.

A simplicial cell complex is pure if its facets (maximal dimension faces) have the same dimension.

Definition 1.5
.

A pure simplicial cell complex
K
of dimension
d
is
shellable
if one can linearly order its facets
F_{1},\ldots,F_{k}
so that for all
j\geq 2
,
F_{j}\cap(F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup\ldots\cup F_{j-1})
is a pure
(d-1)
-dimensional subcomplex of
\partial F_{j}
, the boundary of
F_{j}
. Such an order is called shelling order.

Although shellability can be defined for more general complexes
[
4
]
, the above definition suffices for our purposes. One way to think of shelling order is the following: the simplicial cell complex is built attaching facet by facet in a particular sequence where
F_{j}
is attached to
(F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup\ldots\cup F_{j-1})
over
F_{j}\cap(F_{1}\cup F_{2}\cup\ldots\cup F_{j-1})
. The combinatorial condition of being shellable has strong topological consequences.

Theorem 1.6
.

[
2
, Proposition 4.3]
Let
\Delta
be a pure simplicial cell complex of dimension
d+1
. If
\Delta
is shellable, then it is homotopy equivalent to a wegde sum of
d
-spheres and the number of
d
-spheres is indexed by the
d
-dimensional facets that are attached over the entire boundary.

Theorem 1.7
.

[
5
, Theorem 6.1]

The complex of injective words
\mathbf{Inj}[n]
is shellable via the lexicographic order
\leq_{l}
of the symmetric group
\Sigma_{n}
. Moreover,
\mathbf{Inj}[n]\simeq\bigvee_{d(n)}S^{n-1}
, where
d(n)
is the number of derangements in
\Sigma_{n}
, that is, the number of fixed point free permutations in
\Sigma_{n}
.

The number of derangements

is given by the formula
\sum_{i=0}^{n}(-1)^{i}\frac{n!}{i!}
, which can be derived using inclusion-exclusion principle argument. A proof can be found in
[
25
, Example 2.2.1]
. For example, the shelling order the facets of
\mathbf{Inj}[3]
is given by

F_{1}:=123\leq F_{2}:=132\leq F_{3}:=213\leq F_{4}:=231\leq F_{5}:=312\leq F_{6}:=321,

and

. Gluing the simplices along common faces following the order above we get

Polyhedral product spaces.

A
diagram of spaces
over a finite poset
\mathcal{P}
is a covariant functor
\mathcal{D}:\mathcal{P}\to\textbf{Top}
from
\mathcal{P}
into the category of compact generated topological spaces. Here we consider
\mathcal{P}
as a small category with a unique arrow
\sigma\to\tau
if
\sigma\leq\tau
. This means that for each
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}
we associate a topological space
\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}
and to any pair

in
\mathcal{D}
we associate a continuous map
d_{\sigma\tau}:\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}\to\mathcal{D}_{\tau}
such that
d_{\sigma\sigma}=\text{id}_{\mathcal{D}_{\sigma}}
and
d_{\sigma\mu}=d_{\tau\mu}\circ d_{\sigma\tau}
for
\sigma\leq\tau\leq\mu
. We call
\mathcal{D}
a
\mathcal{P}
-diagram of spaces.

We shall always consider the pairs
(X_{i},A_{i})
to be well-pointed pairs of
CW
-spaces with
A_{i}\subset X_{i}
and base point
x_{i}\in A_{i}
for every
i
in the vertex set of a simplicial poset
\mathcal{P}
, unless stated explicitly otherwise. Note that since we work with
CW
-spaces, the pairs
(X_{i},A_{i})
are
NDR
-pairs
[
17
, Proposition A4]
. Moreover, the inclusion of a base point is a closed cofibration
[
14
, Corollary 1.3.7]
.
Therefore, any pointed
CW
-space is well-pointed
[
22
, Definiton 1.1.5]
.

Definition 1.8
.

Let
\mathcal{P}
be a finite simplicial poset and
\textbf{(X,A)}:=\{(X_{i},A_{i})|i\in V_{\mathcal{P}}\}
a collection of pairs of pointed
CW
-spaces with
A_{i}\subset X_{i}
and base point
x_{i}\in A_{i}
for all

. The polyhedral product functor determined by

and

is defined as the
\mathcal{P}
-diagram

\displaystyle\mathcal{Z}:

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}\to\textbf{Top}

\displaystyle\sigma\mapsto\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}\textbf{(X,A)}

where
\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}\textbf{(X,A)}:=\prod_{i\in V_{\mathcal{P}}(\sigma)}X_{i}\times\prod_{i\not\in V_{\mathcal{P}}(\sigma)}A_{i}
. Whenever
\tau\leq\sigma
,
\mathcal{Z}(\tau\to\sigma)
maps it to an inclusion
\mathcal{Z}_{\tau}\textbf{(X,A)}\subseteq\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}\textbf{(X,A)}
. The polyhedral product
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}\textbf{(X,A)}
is defined as

\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}\textbf{(X,A)}:=\hocolim_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}\textbf{(X,A)}

The definition is functorial with respect to inclusion maps of
\mathcal{P}
and maps between pair of spaces.

We shall often write
\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}\textbf{(X,A)}
as
\mathcal{Z}_{\sigma}
when

is clear from context. When
\textbf{(X,A)}:=\{(\mathbb{C}P^{\infty},*)|i\in V_{\mathcal{P}}\}
, the resulting space is called the Davis-Januszkiewicz space, denoted by

. Now when

Polyhedral product spaces were firstly defined as colimits

21

Lemma 1.9
.

[

Let

\hocolim_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{D}\simeq\colim_{\mathcal{P}}\mathcal{D}.

The following definition was introduced in
[

Definition 1.10
.

Let

be a collection of subspaces of

. The collection

is said to be an arrangement, if the following conditions hold:

1.

For any

,

is a union of elements in
\mathcal{A}
, and

2.

for any

such that

The elements of an arrangement

admit a partial order given by inclusion. Let

. We define an

of

to be the subspace corresponding to

[

Cohomology of moment angle complexes.

Lü and Panov in
[
21
]
studied the cohomology of
\mathcal{Z}_{\mathcal{P}}
moment-angle complexes associated to simplicial posets, and we recall their results below, starting with the necessary definitions and notation.

Let
\mathbb{Z}[v_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\mathcal{P}]
be the graded polynomial ring with one generator
v_{\sigma}
of degree
\deg v_{\sigma}=2|\sigma|
for every
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}
. The Stanley-Reisner ring (over
\mathbb{Z}
) of a simplicial poset
\mathcal{P}
is the quotient

\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}]=\mathbb{Z}[v_{\sigma}:\sigma\in\mathcal{P}]/I_{\mathcal{P}},

where
I_{\mathcal{P}}
denotes the ideal generated by the element
v_{\emptyset}-1
and the elements
v_{\sigma}v_{\tau}
whenever
\sigma
and
\tau
have no common upper bound in
\mathcal{P}
, together with the relations
v_{\sigma}v_{\tau}-(v_{\sigma\wedge\tau})\cdot\left(\sum_{\eta\in[\sigma\vee\tau]}v_{\eta}\right)
otherwise. This definition extends the classical one for simplicial complexes
[
24
, Definition 3.3]
.

There is a
(\mathbb{Z}\oplus\mathbb{Z}^{n})
-graded decomposition of the
Tor
-algebra of the Stanley-Reisner ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}]
. The grading goes as follows. Firstly, consider the polynomial algebra
\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]
on
n
generators of degree
2
corresponding to the vertex set of
\mathcal{P}
and denote it by
\mathbb{Z}[n]
. A
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading in
\mathbb{Z}[n]
is defined by setting
\deg(v_{1}^{i_{1}}\ldots v_{n}^{i_{n}})=(2i_{1},\ldots,2i_{n})
. As the Stanley-Reisner ring
\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}]
is a
\mathbb{Z}[n]
-algebra via the map
\mathbb{Z}[v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}]\to\mathbb{Z}[\mathcal{P}]
, which maps each
v_{i}
identically, it inherits this
\mathbb{Z}^{n}
-grading. Recall that
\mathbb{Z}
has a
\mathbb{Z}[n]
-module structure given by the augmentation map sending each
v_{i}
to zero, and the so-called Kozul resolution is a standard way of creating a free resolution of
\mathbb{Z}
. We follow the convention used in
[
21

∎

In order to understand the situation when

Lemma 3.2
.

[
22
, Lemma 2.2.10]

Let
f=*:X\to Y
be the constant map, that is,
x\mapsto*
for all
x
in
X
. Then, the homotopy fibre
F_{f}
is homotopic to
X\times\Omega Y
.

Corollary 3.3
.

[
17
, Corollary 4.9]

Any map
S^{i}\to S^{n}
, where
0<i<n
is null homotopic, that is, homotopic to the constant map.

Corollary 3.4
.

The homotopy fiber of a map

is homotopy equivalence to

, where
1\leq k<n
.

Proof.

It follows immediately from Lemma

and Corollary
