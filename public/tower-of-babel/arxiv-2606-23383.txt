Near-Lipschitz stability of the Kim–Milman flow map
Sinho Chewi, Katharina Eichinger, Aram-Alexandre Pooladian

Abstract
We prove that the Kim–Milman flow map enjoys favorable stability properties with respect to variations in the target measure, provided that one of the target measures is sufficiently regular. Our results include stability in relative entropy, and more notably, Lipschitz stability in the 2 2 -Wasserstein distance up to a logarithmic factor. We complement our results with a general existence theorem for these maps for any target measure with finite second moment.

Introduction

Let
\mu
be a probability measure over
\mathbb{R}^{d}
and let
\gamma\coloneqq\mathcal{N}(0,I)
. The seminal work of
25
introduced a flow map
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
which transports
\gamma
to
\mu
, i.e., for
X\sim\gamma
,
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}(X)\sim\mu
. Unlike other transport maps, such as optimal transport maps
(
4
)
or the Schrödinger bridge
(
28
)
, the study of
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
does not typically proceed by viewing it as the solution to a variational problem
but instead leverages the theory of forward and reverse diffusions
(
20
)
; see Section
2.1
for more details on its construction. We henceforth call
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
the Kim–Milman flow map or simply Kim–Milman map.

The Kim–Milman map was developed as a means of generalizing Caffarelli’s celebrated contraction theorem beyond the case in which
\mu
is strongly log-concave
(
6
)
, and has remained a subject of intense investigation in recent years; see for example
37
;
27
;
36
;
19
;
5
;
12
;
21
;
32
;
44
for developments and applications in pure mathematics.

Whereas the works above mostly focus on regularity of
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
as a function of its input, here we focus on its
stability
with respect to the targets: For two measures
\mu
and
\nu
, can we accurately quantify the distance between the maps
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
and
\mathsf{T}^{\nu}

by some (pseudo-)metric between the measures? This question is motivated, in part, by extensive work on the analogous question for optimal and entropic transport maps, which we review in Section
2.3
.

Our main result is the following theorem, which states that if a Kim–Milman flow map is Lipschitz (with respect to its input), then it is also nearly Lipschitz stable with respect to variations in the target measure in the
2
-Wasserstein distance.

Theorem 1.1

(Informal; see Theorem
3.5
)
.

Suppose that
\mu
satisfies a suitable regularity condition, implying in particular that
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
is Lipschitz, and that
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
has a finite second moment. Then,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant c(\mu)\,W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\log\Bigl(2+\frac{c^{\prime}(\mu)}{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)}\Bigr)\,,

where
c(\mu)
,
c^{\prime}(\mu)
are constants that depend only on
\mu
.

The regularity condition assumed in Theorem
1.1
is ubiquitous in the literature, because it implies the Lipschitzness of the map
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
; we formally state the assumption later as the condition (

Θ

).
Thus, our theorem shows that this same condition suffices for quantitative stability bounds as well.

We mention here that this result is perhaps surprising, since optimal transport maps are at most
\frac{1}{3}
-Hölder stable w.r.t. the
2
-Wasserstein distance in general (see Section
2.3
).

Our proof uses the flow map interpretation of the Kim–Milman map and reduces the stability bound to estimates for the relative Fisher information along the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process. For large times, we combine the de Bruijn identity with a log-Harnack inequality to bound this in terms of the initial Wasserstein distance between the measures. For short times, we use an alternative bound which has better time dependence, but which does not depend on the initial Wasserstein distance. Balancing these two regimes yields our result. We believe the logarithmic factor is an artifact of our proof technique, although Section
3.5
discusses how certain estimates in our proof cannot be improved.

As a final contribution, we prove that Kim–Milman flow maps exist for any probability measure with finite second moment; see Lemma
2.3
. This result follows from a purely technical argument that exploits the regularizing property of the relative Fisher information.

Notation, preliminaries, and related work

We let
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
denote the space of probability measures on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with finite second moment, and let
\mathcal{P}_{2,\rm ac}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be the subset of absolutely continuous measures. For two measures
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we write the relative entropy between them as

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}(P\,\|\,Q)\coloneqq\int\log\bigl(\frac{\,\mathrm{d}P}{\,\mathrm{d}Q}\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}P\,,

whenever
P\ll Q
, with
{\mathsf{H}}(P\,\|\,Q)=+\infty
otherwise. We similarly define the relative Fisher information between
P
and
Q
as

\displaystyle\FI(P\,\|\,Q)\coloneqq\mathbb{E}_{P}\|\nabla\log P-\nabla\log Q\|^{2}\,,

whenever this expression makes sense; in this paper, we will only ever consider the relative Fisher information between
C^{\infty}
densities, due to the regularization along the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process.

For any
P,Q\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, recall that the
2
-Wasserstein distance is

\displaystyle W_{2}(P,Q)\coloneqq\Bigl(\inf_{\pi\in\Pi(P,Q)}\iint\|x-y\|^{2}\,\pi(\!\,\mathrm{d}x,\!\,\mathrm{d}y)\Bigr)^{1/2}\,,

where
\Pi(P,Q)
is the space of joint probability measures with marginals
P
and
Q
. For any
P\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, we write
\mathsf{m}_{2}(P)\coloneqq(\int\|x\|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}P(x))^{1/2}<\infty
.

2.1
Construction of the map

We first provide some background on the construction of the flow map due to
25
, which is based on time reversal of the Langevin dynamics. We differ from the usual construction in the literature based on inverting the forward map, and instead first invert the time before taking the limiting flow map.

Let
\gamma=\mathcal{N}(0,I)
and let
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be arbitrary. Our forward dynamics are governed by the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) process: for
X_{0}^{\rightarrow}\sim\mu
,

\displaystyle\,\mathrm{d}X_{t}^{\rightarrow}=-X_{t}^{\rightarrow}{\,\mathrm{d}t}+\sqrt{2}\,\mathrm{d}B_{t}\,,

(1)

where
(B_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
is a standard Brownian motion. Writing
q_{t}^{\mu}\coloneqq{\rm Law}(X_{t}^{\rightarrow})
,
(q_{t}^{\mu})_{t\geqslant 0}
satisfies the following Fokker--Planck equation
1
1

1

Here we abuse notation and identify measures with their Lebesgue densities.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q_{t}^{\mu}-\nabla\cdot(q_{t}^{\mu}\,({\rm id}+\nabla\log q_{t}^{\mu}))=0\,,\quad q_{0}^{\mu}=\mu\,.

Observe that if
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, a simple coupling argument yields that
q_{t}^{\mu}\to\gamma
(for instance, in
W_{2}
) as
t\to\infty
.

Performing a time reversal on the interval
[0,T]
of this equation yields

\displaystyle\partial_{t}q^{\mu}_{T-t}+\nabla\cdot(q^{\mu}_{T-t}\,({\rm id}+\nabla\log q^{\mu}_{T-t}))=0\,.

Recognizing this as a continuity equation, it describes the evolution of the marginal law for the following ODE, i.e.,
{\rm Law}(X_{t}^{\leftarrow})=q^{\mu}_{T-t}
with

\displaystyle\dot{X}_{t}^{\leftarrow}=X_{t}^{\leftarrow}+\nabla\log q^{\mu}_{T-t}(X_{t}^{\leftarrow})=\nabla\log\Bigl(\frac{\!\,\mathrm{d}q^{\mu}_{T-t}}{\!\,\mathrm{d}\gamma}\Bigr)(X_{t}^{\leftarrow})\,,\quad X_{0}^{\leftarrow}\sim q^{\mu}_{T}\,.

(2)

We can also write (
2
) using semigroup notation. Letting
(Q_{s})_{s\geqslant 0}
denote the OU semigroup, using
\frac{\,\mathrm{d}q^{\mu}_{T-t}}{\,\mathrm{d}\gamma}=Q_{T-t}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\,\mathrm{d}\gamma}
we have that

\displaystyle\dot{X}_{t}^{\leftarrow}=\nabla\log Q_{T-t}\Bigl[\frac{\!\,\mathrm{d}\mu}{\!\,\mathrm{d}\gamma}\Bigr](X_{t}^{\leftarrow})\,,\quad X_{0}^{\leftarrow}\sim q^{\mu}_{T}\,,

(3)

whenever
\mu\ll\gamma
.
Here, we made use of the fact that
\gamma
is the stationary distribution of the OU process.

Now, let
\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}\equiv(\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}_{t})_{t\in[0,T]}
denote the flow map solution to (
2
). So,
\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}_{t}(X^{\leftarrow}_{0})=X^{\leftarrow}_{t}
and
(\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}_{T-t})_{\sharp}q^{\mu}_{T}=q^{\mu}_{t}
. We now take the limit
T\to\infty
, and the transport map from
\gamma
to
\mu
is thus given by
\mathsf{T}^{\mu,\infty}\coloneqq\lim_{T\to\infty}\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}
.
We also abbreviate
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}\coloneqq\mathsf{T}^{\mu,\infty}
.

We will also make use of the flow map from
\gamma
to
q^{\mu}_{t}
given by

\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}\coloneqq\lim_{T\rightarrow\infty}\mathsf{T}^{\mu,T}_{T-t}

(4)

which satisfies
(\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow})_{\sharp}\gamma=q^{\mu}_{t}
.
The notation indicates that this is the KM map, stopped at
q_{t}^{\mu}
.

Stability in relative Fisher information

Recently,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\lesssim\sqrt{\FI(\nu\,\|\,\mu)}\,,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\gamma)}\lesssim\sqrt{{\mathsf{FI}}_{\infty}(\nu\,\|\,\mu)}\,,

Stability of optimal transport maps.

Letting

\displaystyle\mathsf{T}_{0}^{\rho\to\mu}\coloneqq\underset{\mathsf{T}\in\mathcal{T}(\rho,\mu)}{\rm argmin}\int\|x-\mathsf{T}(x)\|^{2}\,\rho(\!\,\mathrm{d}x)\,.

This line of work was inaugurated by

\rho,\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}(B(0,R))

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{0}^{\rho\to\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{0}^{\rho\to\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}\leqslant(4RL)^{1/2}\,W_{2}^{1/2}(\mu,\nu)\,.

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{0}^{\rho\to\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{0}^{\rho\to\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\rho)}\leqslant\Bigl(\frac{L}{\ell}\Bigr)^{1/2}\,W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\,.

Recent work has sought to characterize stability properties of optimal transport maps in the absence of regularity assumptions. Indeed,

to obtain

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{T\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{T\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\geqslant\exp\Bigl(-\int_{\delta}^{T}\theta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)\,\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\qquad{}-\int_{\delta}^{T}\exp\Bigl(-\int_{s}^{T}\theta_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\FI(q^{\nu}_{s}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{s})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

Letting
T\rightarrow\infty
then gives

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant L_{\delta}(\mu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\exp\Bigl(-\int_{s}^{\infty}\theta_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\FI(q^{\nu}_{s}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{s})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}s\,,

(18)

where
L_{\delta}(\mu)
has been defined in (
7
).
We now claim that

\displaystyle\FI(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}\leqslant-\sqrt{2}L_{t}(\mu)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}\,.

To prove it, observe that since
q_{t}^{\mu}=(\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow})_{\sharp}\gamma
with
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}
being Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{t}(\mu)
,

q_{t}^{\mu}
satisfies the following log-Sobolev inequality (recall (
8
)):

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\leqslant\frac{L^{2}_{t}(\mu)}{2}\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\,.

Using de Bruijn’s identity and the above log-Sobolev inequality, it holds that

\displaystyle-\sqrt{2}L_{t}\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}=L_{t}\,\frac{\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})}{\sqrt{2}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}}\geqslant\FI(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}\,,

which is the claim.

Substituting the claim into (
18
) then yields

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant-\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\exp\Bigl(-\int_{t}^{\infty}\theta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(\int_{t}^{\infty}\theta_{s}\,\mathrm{d}s\Bigr)\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\mathsf{H}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\,\bigl({\mathsf{H}}(q_{\delta}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{\delta}^{\mu})^{1/2}-{\mathsf{H}}(\gamma\,\|\,\gamma)^{1/2}\bigr)\,.

Now, for
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, let
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a regular approximating sequence for
\nu
as in Definition
A.1
.
Applying the above bound to
\nu_{n}
,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu_{n}}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{\delta}^{\nu_{n}}\,\|\,q_{\delta}^{\mu})^{1/2}\,.

As
n\to\infty
, the LHS converges to
\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{\delta\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{\delta\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}
, by Lemma
2.3
.
As for the RHS, as shown in
40
, the heat flow (or equivalently, the OU process) induces
W_{2}
continuity of the relative entropy, and thus
\KL(q_{\delta}^{\nu_{n}}\,\|\,q_{\delta}^{\mu})\to\KL(q_{\delta}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{\delta}^{\mu})
.
This yields

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{\delta}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{\delta}^{\mu})^{1/2}\leqslant\sqrt{2}L_{\delta}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(\nu\,\|\,\mu)^{1/2}\,,

by the data-processing inequality.
Finally, let
\delta\searrow 0
using Lemma
2.3
again.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Our initial proof of Theorem
3.3
is inspired by the proof of Talagrand’s inequality in
38
using the OU flow. In fact, carefully tracing their proof, one can recover the result of Theorem
3.3
for
\mu=\gamma
(and hence
\theta_{s}=0
). This proof (included in Appendix
B.3
for completeness) generalizes their Lyapunov function to accommodate for

satisfying (

Θ

), which might be of independent interest. Using the log-Sobolev inequality at the right moment yields the following differential inequality

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigl[\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-(2L^{2}_{t}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t}))^{1/2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geqslant-\theta_{t}\,\bigl[\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-(2L^{2}_{t}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t}))^{1/2}\bigr]\,,

and one concludes with Grönwall’s inequality and

.

3.3
Near-Lipschitz stability in
2
-Wasserstein

Now we leverage the regularizing effect of the heat flow to deduce stability of the Kim–Milman flow map with respect to the
2
-Wasserstein distance. Our goal is to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.5

(Main theorem)
.

Suppose
\mu
satisfies (

Θ

) and
\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with
\mathsf{m}_{2}(\mu),\mathsf{m}_{2}(\nu)\leqslant M
. In addition, let
\mathsf{C}_{\mu}\geqslant 0
be such that for all
0\leqslant s\leqslant t
, it holds that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\theta_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\leqslant\mathsf{C}_{\mu}\,.

(19)

Then,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\log\Bigl[e^{2}\max\Bigl\{1,\,\frac{4M^{2}+8d}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(6\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}\Bigr\}\Bigr]\,.

We give some remarks before continuing to the proof.

In known cases where
\mu
satisfies (

Θ

), condition (
19
) can be directly inferred; see Section
3.4
below for examples.

We emphasize that (

Θ

) does not imply bi-Lipschitzness of
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}
but merely Lipschitzness. It is perhaps surprising that nearly Lipschitz stability in
2
-Wasserstein is possible under this assumption, given that the closest result for optimal transport maps requires bi-Lipschitzness.

As for the log-factor, we believe this is an artifact of our proof technique and do not believe it is necessary. See Section
3.5
for more discussions on this point.

3.3.1
Proof via early stopping

To prove stability in relative entropy, we leveraged the log-Sobolev inequality in order to suitably bound the time-integrated (square-root of the) Fisher information by the relative entropy. In order to obtain stability bounds in Wasserstein distance, it is hence natural to use the regularizing effect along the OU semigroup comparing Fisher information and Wasserstein distance. This is captured in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\mu,\nu\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. Then

•

For any
t>0
,

{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\leqslant\frac{1}{2(e^{2t}-1)}\,W_{2}^{2}(\nu,\mu)\,.

(20)

•

If in addition
\mu
satisfies (

Θ

), then for any
t>t_{0}>0
,

{\FI}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\leqslant\frac{1}{c(t)}\,W_{2}^{2}(\nu,\mu)\,,

(21)

where we define

\displaystyle c(t)=2(e^{2t_{0}}-1)\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\exp\Bigl(\int_{s}^{t}2(1-2\theta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

(22)

Proof.

The first bound corresponds to the Harnack inequality introduced by
46
; more precisely, it is the log-Harnack inequality due to
3
. See also
1
for the version we use.

For the second bound, we use the following result from
47

to give an upper bound on the change in relative Fisher information along OU channels: For any
t>0
, it holds that

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\FI(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})\leqslant-2\,\mathbb{E}_{q_{t}^{\nu}}\Bigl\langle\nabla\log\frac{q_{t}^{\nu}}{q_{t}^{\mu}}\,,\,(-2\nabla^{2}\log q_{t}^{\mu}-I)\,\nabla\log\frac{q_{t}^{\nu}}{q_{t}^{\mu}}\Bigr\rangle\,.

In particular, if
\mu
is such that (

Θ

) holds, then an immediate corollary is the bound

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\leqslant-2(1-2\theta_{t})\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\,.

(23)

Grönwall’s inequality on
[s,t]
,
0<s\leqslant t
, then gives

\displaystyle{\FI}(q_{s}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{s}^{\mu})\geqslant{\FI}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\exp\Bigl(\int_{s}^{t}2(1-2\theta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\,.

Then, for
0<t_{0}<t
, we have by de Bruijn’s identity

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t_{0}}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t_{0}}^{\mu})

\displaystyle\geqslant{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t_{0}}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t_{0}}^{\mu})-{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\int_{t_{0}}^{t}{\FI}(q_{s}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{s}^{\mu})\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geqslant{\FI}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\exp\Bigl(\int_{s}^{t}2(1-2\theta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s\,.

We conclude by rearranging and using (
20
).
∎

Ideally, we start from (
16
) and use (
21
). Unfortunately, the right-hand side of (
21
) is not integrable at
0
, albeit barely. Thus, we require a different approach, based on “early stopping”.
For
0<\varepsilon\leqslant\delta
, we consider

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{\varepsilon\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}=\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{\delta\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{\delta\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

and later perform a trade-off to determine the choice of
\delta>0
.
Then, we send
\varepsilon\searrow 0
.

We bound the first term in a straightforward manner by employing the regularization of the Fisher information by the heat flow.
In what follows,
(\theta_{t})_{t\geqslant 0}
always refers to the regularity constants for
\mu
(not for
\nu
).

Proposition 3.7
.

Suppose that
\mu
,
\nu
satisfy (

Θ

).
Then,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{\delta\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{\delta\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\bigl(\textstyle\int_{\delta}^{t}\theta_{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigr)}{\sqrt{c(t)}}\,\mathrm{d}t\,,

where
c(t)
is defined in (
22
).

Proof.

By (
18
) in the proof of Theorem
3.3
,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}_{\delta\leftarrow}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant L_{\delta}(\mu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\exp\Bigl(-\int_{t}^{\infty}\theta_{r}\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\FI(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t\,.

We conclude by employing the second point from Lemma
3.6
.
∎

For the remaining interval, we obtain the following explicit bound.

Proposition 3.8
.

For any
\mu,\nu
satisfying (

Θ

), assuming that
\mathsf{m}_{2}(\mu),\mathsf{m}_{2}(\nu)\leqslant M
,

\displaystyle-\int_{\varepsilon}^{\delta}\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leqslant 2M\,(1-e^{-\delta})+2\sqrt{d\,(1-e^{-2\delta})}\,.

Proof.

From (
15
), we have by Cauchy–Schwarz

\displaystyle-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle=2\int\Bigl\langle\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\,,\,\nabla\log\frac{q_{t}^{\mu}}{\gamma}\circ\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\nabla\log\frac{q_{t}^{\nu}}{\gamma}\circ\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\Bigr\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\gamma

\displaystyle\leqslant 2\,\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\,\bigl(\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\mu}\,\|\,\gamma)}+\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,\gamma)}\bigr)

and thus

\displaystyle-\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\mu}\,\|\,\gamma)}+\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,\gamma)}\,.

By Lemma
B.1
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\mu}\,\|\,\gamma)}\vee\sqrt{\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,\gamma)}\leqslant Me^{-t}+\frac{\sqrt{d}e^{-2t}}{\sqrt{1-e^{-2t}}}\,.

We conclude by integration, as

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{\delta}e^{-2t}\,(1-e^{-2t})^{-1/2}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=\sqrt{1-e^{-2\delta}}\,.\qed

With these two inequalities, we can prove our main result.

Proof of Theorem
3.5
.

Combining both Proposition
3.7
and Proposition
3.8
, we have that along a regular approximating sequence
\{\nu_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
, for any
0<\varepsilon\leqslant\delta
,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{\varepsilon\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu_{n}}_{\varepsilon\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu_{n})\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\bigl(\textstyle\int_{\delta}^{t}\theta_{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigr)}{\sqrt{c(t)}}\,\mathrm{d}t+2M_{n}\,(1-e^{-\delta})+2\sqrt{d\,(1-e^{-2\delta})}\,,

where
M_{n}\coloneqq\max\{\mathsf{m}_{2}(\mu),\mathsf{m}_{2}(\nu_{n})\}
.
Passing
n\to\infty
and then
\varepsilon\searrow 0
via Lemma
2.3
,

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}

\displaystyle\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\int_{\delta}^{\infty}\frac{\exp\bigl(\textstyle\int_{\delta}^{t}\theta_{\omega}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\bigr)}{\sqrt{c(t)}}\,\mathrm{d}t+2M\,(1-e^{-\delta})+2\sqrt{d\,(1-e^{-2\delta})}\,,

with
M\coloneqq\max\{\mathsf{m}_{2}(\mu),\mathsf{m}_{2}(\nu)\}
.
We now turn to bound
c(t)
. By (
19
) and the choice
t_{0}=t/2
, we see that

\displaystyle\int_{t/2}^{t}\exp\Bigl(\int_{s}^{t}2(1-2\theta_{r})\,\mathrm{d}r\Bigr)\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\geqslant\exp(-4\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\int_{t/2}^{t}e^{2(t-s)}\,\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\exp(-4\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\,\frac{e^{2t}}{2}\,(e^{-t}-e^{-2t})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\exp(4\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}\,(e^{t}-1)\,,

and so we have the bound
c(t)\geqslant\exp(-4\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\,(e^{t}-1)^{2}
.
Rearranging and invoking (
19
) again, our bound now reads

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\int_{\delta}^{\infty}(e^{t}-1)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}t+2M\,(1-e^{-\delta})+2\sqrt{d\,(1-e^{-2\delta})}\,.

We also readily compute

\displaystyle\int_{\delta}^{\infty}(e^{t}-1)^{-1}\,\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle=-\log(1-e^{-\delta})\,.

Writing
\zeta\coloneqq 1-e^{-\delta}
, our bound reads

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\log(1/\zeta)+2M\zeta+\sqrt{8d\zeta}\,.

A simple choice is to make the last two terms at most

, by taking the choice
\zeta\coloneqq 1\wedge\frac{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}{2M}\wedge\frac{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(6\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}{8d}
.
This yields

\displaystyle W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\,(2+\log(1/\zeta))

\displaystyle\qquad=W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\log\Bigl[e^{2}\max\Bigl\{1,\,\frac{2M}{W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})},\,\frac{8d}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(6\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}\Bigr\}\Bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\leqslant W_{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(3\mathsf{C}_{\mu})\log\Bigl[e^{2}\max\Bigl\{1,\,\frac{4M^{2}+8d}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\exp(6\mathsf{C}_{\mu})}\Bigr\}\Bigr]\,,

where we used
A\leqslant\frac{1}{2}\,(1+A^{2})\leqslant\max\{1,A^{2}\}
to simplify the bound.
∎

In the statement of the informal theorem, we can use

for some

3.4
Some examples

We now go over some cases where (

Θ

) has been previously established. In all instances, we will write
\mu\propto\exp(-V)
for some function
V:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
, and state the assumption on
\mu
in terms of properties of
V
.

The most general hypothesis for validity of (

Θ

) has been established in
9
, more precisely in the proof of Theorem 1.4 therein. It is commonly referred to as asymptotic convexity, a hypothesis well-known in the coupling by reflection literature, ensuring among other things exponential contraction in the
1
-Wasserstein distance of the associated overdamped Langevin dynamics, see, e.g.,
17
. In order to state rigorously what we mean by asymptotic convexity, we define the convexity profile of
V
as follows

\displaystyle\kappa_{V}(r)\coloneqq\inf\Bigl\{\frac{\langle\nabla{V}(x)-\nabla{V}(y),x-y\rangle}{\|x-y\|^{2}}\,:\,\|x-y\|=r\Bigr\}\quad r>0\,.

The potential
V
is then said to be
\alpha
-asymptotically convex,
\alpha>0
, if

\displaystyle\liminf_{r\rightarrow+\infty}\kappa_{V}(r)\geqslant\alpha\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}\kappa_{V}^{-}(r)\,r\,\mathrm{d}r<+\infty\,,

where
\kappa_{V}^{-}(r):=\max\{0,-\kappa_{V}(r)\}
denotes the negative part of
\kappa_{V}(r)
.

Obviously, this class encompasses
\alpha
-strongly convex potentials
V
, as in this case
\kappa_{V}(r)\geqslant\alpha
for all
r>0
.

Under the assumptions of asymptotic convexity, by properly rescaling
(
9
, Proposition 2.9)
one has the following expression

\theta_{t}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha(e^{2t}-1)+1}-\frac{e^{2t}}{(e^{2t}-1)(\alpha(e^{2t}-1)+1)}+\frac{e^{2t}}{8(e^{2t}-1)^{2}}\,\mathbb{E}[X_{t}^{2}]\,,

where
X_{t}
is a random variable with density

\xi_{t}({\rm d}x)\propto\mathds{1}_{x\geqslant 0}\exp\Bigl(-\frac{1}{8}\int_{0}^{x}u\kappa_{V}^{-}(u)\,\mathrm{d}u\Bigr)\exp\Bigl(-\frac{x^{2}}{16(1-e^{-2t})}\Bigr)\,.

Let us give two more explicit examples of asymptotically convex potentials, where the constants are tractable.

Strongly convex with Lipschitz perturbation.

Suppose
V=W+H
where
W
is
\alpha
-strongly convex and
H
is a smooth
L
-Lipschitz perturbation. In this case

\kappa_{V}(r)\geqslant\alpha-2L/r

for
r\geqslant 0
, so
V
is asymptotically convex.

5
, see also
9
for a slight improvement of some absolute constants, showed that (

Θ

) holds with

\displaystyle\theta_{u}=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha(e^{2u}-1)+1}+\frac{e^{2u}L^{2}}{(\alpha(e^{2u}-1)+1)^{2}}+\frac{2Le^{2u}}{(\alpha(e^{2u}-1)+1)^{3/2}\sqrt{e^{2u}-1}}\,.

(24)

In the case where
\alpha\in(0,1]
, all these terms are positive and so we have the naïve bound

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\theta_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\leqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}\theta_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u=\frac{1}{2}\log(\alpha^{-1})+\frac{L^{2}}{2\alpha}+\frac{2L}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\,.

If
\alpha>1
, we can always drop the first term in (
24
), and obtain

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\theta_{u}\,\mathrm{d}u\leqslant\frac{L^{2}}{2\alpha}+\frac{2L}{\sqrt{\alpha}}\,,

which is the required bound for (
19
).

Strongly convex with Lipschitz perturbation with bounded Hessian.

If now
V=W+H
where
W
is strongly convex and
H
is a smooth Lipschitz function with bounded Hessian, then one can find suitable
\alpha,M>0
such that

\kappa_{V}(r)\geqslant\alpha-\frac{1}{r}\,f_{M}(r)\,,

where
f_{M}(r)\coloneqq 2\sqrt{M}\tanh(r\sqrt{M}/2)
.
Studying propagation of convexity profiles of this type has been pioneered by
14
, and further used in several works such as
13
. We cite the formula for
\theta_{t}
from
45

\displaystyle\theta_{t}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{\alpha}{\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{-2t}}+\frac{e^{-2t}}{(\alpha+(1-\alpha)e^{-2t})^{2}}\,M

\displaystyle=\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha(e^{2t}-1)+1}+\frac{e^{2t}M}{(\alpha(e^{2t}-1)+1)^{2}}\,.

As before, it is now immediate that (

Θ

) and (
19
) are satisfied.

3.5
Fisher information lower bound

The core of our proof hinges on (
21
), which upper bounds the relative Fisher information along OU channels by the
2
-Wasserstein distance. In this section, we prove that this Fisher information bound cannot be improved by more than a constant factor in general. By rescaling, it is sufficient to consider the example for the heat semigroup, see also the remark below.

Henceforth, for a probability measure
P\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
, we write
P_{t}\coloneqq P*\mathcal{N}(0,t)
. We will show the following.

Proposition 3.10
.

Fix
t>0
and let
\mu=\mathcal{N}(0,1)
; it holds that

\displaystyle\sup_{\nu\neq\mu}\frac{\FI(\nu_{t}\,\|\,\mu_{t})}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)}\geqslant\frac{1}{t^{2}}\,.

To prove this result, we will construct for each
t>0
, a
\nu\in\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{R})
that verifies the lower bound. To this end, let
\mu=\mathcal{N}(0,1)
and fix
t>0
.
Let
\mu_{t}=\mu*\mathcal{N}(0,t)=\mathcal{N}(0,1+t)
,
\nu_{t}=\nu*\mathcal{N}(0,t)
. Letting
R>0
(which will ultimately be taken to
+\infty
), define the intervals

\displaystyle A_{R}\coloneqq[R/(1+t),R/(1+t)+R^{-1}]\,,\quad B_{R}\coloneqq[R-R^{1/3},R+R^{1/3}]\,.

Our adversarial choice of
\nu
is given by

\displaystyle\nu\coloneqq\mu-\mu\big|_{A_{R}}+p_{R}\delta_{R}\,,

where
p_{R}\coloneqq\mu(A_{R})
. Note that
\nu\geqslant p_{R}\delta_{R}
always holds.

First, we bound the
2
-Wasserstein distance from above. Choosing the coupling to only move mass from inside the interval
A_{R}
to the point-mass
\delta_{R}
, we obtain

\displaystyle W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)\leqslant\int_{A_{R}}|x-R|^{2}\,\mu(\!\,\mathrm{d}x)=\frac{p_{R}t^{2}R^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}\,(1+o(1))\,,

(25)

where the asymptotics are for
R\to\infty
, with
t
fixed. If we then manage to show that

\displaystyle\FI(\nu_{t}\,\|\,\mu_{t})\geqslant\frac{p_{R}R^{2}}{(1+t)^{2}}\,(1-o(1))\,,

(26)

the proof concludes, as

\displaystyle\frac{\FI(\nu_{t}\,\|\,\mu_{t})}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)}\geqslant\frac{(p_{R}R^{2}\,(1+t)^{-2})\,(1-o(1))}{(p_{R}R^{2}\,(1+t)^{-2}\,t^{2})\,(1+o(1))}=\frac{1-o(1)}{t^{2}\,(1+o(1))}\,,

and hence

\displaystyle\limsup_{R\to\infty}\frac{\FI(\nu_{t}\,\|\,\mu_{t})}{W_{2}^{2}(\mu,\nu)}\geqslant\frac{1}{t^{2}}\,.

To obtain (

\displaystyle\FI(\nu_{t}\,\|\,\mu_{t})

\displaystyle=\int\bigl\lvert\bigl(\log\frac{\nu_{t}}{\mu_{t}}\bigr)^{\prime}\bigr\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}\geqslant\int_{B_{R}}\bigl\lvert\bigl(\log\frac{\nu_{t}}{\mu_{t}}\bigr)^{\prime}\bigr\rvert^{2}\,\mathrm{d}\nu_{t}

(27)

as well as

(28)

Note that the proof of (

, we have that

\displaystyle\geqslant p_{R}\,(\delta_{R}*N(0,t))(B_{R})

\displaystyle=p_{R}\,\mathbb{P}(|Y_{t}|\leqslant R^{1/3})

\displaystyle\geqslant p_{R}\,(1-2\exp(-R^{2/3}/2t))

\displaystyle=p_{R}\,(1-o(1))\,,

for the remaining details of the proof.

Remark 3.11

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigl(2L_{t}^{2}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\bigr)^{1/2}\leqslant-\FI(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t})^{1/2}-\theta_{t}\,\bigl(2L_{t}^{2}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\bigr)^{1/2}\,.

Proof.

Writing
\mathsf{h}_{t}\coloneqq 2L_{t}^{2}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})
for shorthand, we compute

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\mathsf{h}_{t}

\displaystyle=4L_{t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,L_{t}+2L_{t}^{2}\,\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=-2\theta_{t}\,\mathsf{h}_{t}-2L_{t}^{2}\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\,,

which follows from the definition of
L_{t}
and de Bruijn’s identity. By the chain rule, we arrive at

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,(\mathsf{h}_{t})^{1/2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,\mathsf{h}_{t}}{2\mathsf{h}_{t}^{1/2}}=-\theta_{t}\,\mathsf{h}_{t}^{1/2}-\frac{L_{t}\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})}{\bigl(2{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\bigr)^{1/2}}\,.

Using that
q_{t}^{\mu}=(\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow})_{\sharp}\gamma
with
\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}
being Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{t}(\mu)
, we deduce that

q_{t}^{\mu}
satisfies the following log-Sobolev inequality:

\displaystyle{\mathsf{H}}(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\leqslant\frac{L_{t}^{2}}{2}\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})\,.

Altogether, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\,(\mathsf{h}_{t})^{1/2}\leqslant-\theta_{t}\,\mathsf{h}_{t}^{1/2}-\FI(q_{t}^{\nu}\,\|\,q_{t}^{\mu})^{1/2}\,,

which concludes the proof.
∎

With this at hand we can now adapt the argument of
38
.

Proof of Theorem
3.3
.

For simplicity, we omit the approximation argument, which is the same as in the original proof of Theorem
3.3
, and focus on the main calculation.

Starting from (
16
) and subtracting off the inequality from Lemma
B.2
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\,\mathrm{d}}{\,\mathrm{d}t}\bigl[\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-(2L^{2}_{t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t}))^{1/2}\bigr]

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geqslant-\theta_{t}\,\bigl[\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{t\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{t\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-(2L^{2}_{t}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{t}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{t}))^{1/2}\bigr]\,.

A single application of Grönwall’s inequality then yields

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}_{T\leftarrow}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}_{T\leftarrow}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-\sqrt{2}L_{T}\,{\mathsf{H}}(q^{\nu}_{T}\,\|\,q^{\mu}_{T})^{1/2}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\geqslant\exp\Bigl(-\int_{0}^{T}\theta_{t}\,\mathrm{d}t\Bigr)\,\bigl(\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-\sqrt{2}L_{0}\,{\mathsf{H}}(\nu\,\|\,\mu)^{1/2}\bigr)\,.

To conclude, we take
T\rightarrow\infty
on both sides. By assumption,
\lim_{T\to\infty}L_{T}<+\infty
while
q_{T}^{\nu},q_{T}^{\mu}\to\gamma
, and thus the left-hand side vanishes; the resulting bound is

\displaystyle\|\mathsf{T}^{\mu}-\mathsf{T}^{\nu}\|_{L^{2}(\gamma)}-\sqrt{2}L_{0}(\mu)\,{\mathsf{H}}(\nu\,\|\,\mu)^{1/2}\leqslant 0\,.

Rearranging concludes the proof.
∎
