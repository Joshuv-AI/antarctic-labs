Multiple standard twists of L L -functions
J.Kaczorowski and A.Perelli

Abstract




Abstract.

The standard twist of
L
-functions plays a fundamental role in the Selberg class theory. It is defined as an absolutely convergent Dirichlet series and admits meromorphic continuation beyond the half-plane of absolute convergence. Nowadays, the analytic properties of the standard twist
F(s,\alpha)
of an
L
-function
F
are well-understood. For example, it has poles when the positive number
\alpha
belongs to the so-called spectrum of
F
, and is entire otherwise. In this paper, for a given set
{\boldsymbol{F}}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{N}\}
of
L
-functions and
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
, we consider the multiple standard twist
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
. This is defined initially on a certain half-space of
\mathbb{C}^{N}
, and we describe its meromorphic continuation to the whole space. Results in the multidimensional case are, in many ways, analogous to those in the one-dimensional case. In particular, the spectrum of a multiple standard twist is relevant to the description of the set of poles of
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
. There are also significant differences; for instance, in the structure of the singularities.

11M41, 11M32

Keywords:
Selberg class, standard twist, multiple Dirichlet series

1.
Introduction

Let

F(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}\qquad(\sigma>1)

(1.1)

be a function of degree
d_{F}>0
from the extended Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
. For reader’s convenience, we collect some basic definitions and notation in the next section. For
\alpha>0
, the standard twist of
F
is defined for
\sigma>1
by the absolutely convergent Dirichlet series

F(s,\alpha)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\frac{a(n)}{n^{s}}e(-\alpha n^{1/d_{F}}),

where
e(x)=\exp(2\pi ix)
. We refer to
[
10
]
and
[
12
]
for an in-depth study of
F(s,\alpha)
. Here we recall only that there exists an infinite countable set
{\rm Spec}(F)\subset(0,\infty)
, called the spectrum of
F
, with the following properties. If
\alpha\not\in{\rm Spec}(F)
then
F(s,\alpha)
is an entire function, otherwise it is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}
with at most simple poles at the points

s^{*}_{\ell}=\frac{d_{F}+1}{2d_{F}}-\frac{\ell}{d_{F}}-i\theta_{F}\qquad(\ell=0,1,2,\dots),

(1.2)

where
\theta_{F}
denotes the internal shift of
F(s)
.

In this paper we develop a multidimensional version of the above theory. Let
\alpha>0
,
N\geq 1
,
{\boldsymbol{F}}=\{F_{1},\dots,F_{N}\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}\}
with degrees
d_{1},\dots,d_{N}>0
and Dirichlet coefficients
a_{1}(n),\dots,a_{N}(n)
, and let

\mathbb{C}_{1}^{N}=\{{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s_{1},\dots,s_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N},\Re(s_{\nu})>1\ \text{for}\ \nu=1,\dots,N\}.

Then for
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}>0
such that

\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu}=1

(1.3)

we consider the
multiple standard twist

{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
of
F_{1},\dots,F_{N}
, defined for
s\in\mathbb{C}_{1}^{N}
as

{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\sum_{n_{1}=1}^{\infty}\ldots\sum_{n_{N}=1}^{\infty}\frac{a_{1}(n_{1})\cdots a_{N}(n_{N})}{n_{1}^{s_{1}}\cdots n_{N}^{s_{N}}}e(-\alpha n_{1}^{\kappa_{1}}\cdots n_{N}^{\kappa_{N}}).

The series is absolutely convergent, thus
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
is holomorphic on the half-space
\mathbb{C}_{1}^{N}
. Clearly, when
N=1
and
F_{1}=F
we have that
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=F(s,\alpha)
. Note that for
N\geq 2
and fixed
F_{1},\dots,F_{N}
there are infinitely many possibilities of choosing exponents
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}>0
satisfying (
1.3
). Hence, there are infinitely many multiple standard twists associated with the same set
{\boldsymbol{F}}
. Formally, we should stress this fact in the notation, but for simplicity we stay with the above lighter notation.

We define the
spectrum
of a multiple standard twist as

{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})=\left\{\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\left(\frac{n_{\nu}}{q_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu}^{d_{\nu}}}\right)^{\kappa_{\nu}}:\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}a_{\nu}(n_{\nu})\neq 0\right\},

where
q_{\nu}=q_{F_{\nu}}
denote the corresponding conductors. Moreover, let

d=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}\theta_{\nu},

(1.4)

where
\theta_{\nu}=\theta_{F_{\nu}}
denote the corresponding internal shifts; see (
2.1
). Finally, for integers
\ell\geq 0
we define the hyperplanes
H^{*}_{\ell}
in
\mathbb{C}^{N}
as

H^{*}_{\ell}=\Big\{{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N}:\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}=\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta.\Big\}.

(1.5)

Again, when
N=1
and
F_{1}=F
we have that
{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})={\rm Spec}(F)
and
H^{*}_{\ell}=s^{*}_{\ell}
. Note that, for
N\geq 2
, Spec
({\boldsymbol{F}})
depends on the choice of the parameters
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}
in (
1.3
), but the hyperplanes
H_{\ell}^{*}
are independent from such parameters. With the above notation, the basic analytic properties of
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
are described by the following theorem.

Theorem 1
.

For
N\geq 2
we have

(i)
if
\alpha\not\in{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})
, then
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
has analytic continuation to an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
;

(ii)
if
\alpha\in{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})
, then it has analytic continuation to a holomorphic function on

\mathbb{C}^{N}\backslash\bigcup_{\ell=0}^{\infty}H_{\ell}^{*},

and for every
\ell\geq 0
and
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in H^{*}_{\ell}
the limit

\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\lim_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{w}}\to{\boldsymbol{s}}\\
{\boldsymbol{w}}\not\in H^{*}_{\ell}\end{subarray}}\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}(w_{\nu}-s_{\nu})\Big){\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{w}},\alpha)

(1.6)

exists and is holomorphic on
H^{*}_{\ell}
.

Denoting as usual by
\Gamma
the Euler function, we have the following immediate corollary.

Collorary 1
.

For
\alpha\in{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})
the quotient

\frac{{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)}{\Gamma\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}-\frac{d+1}{2}+i\theta\Big)}

is an entire function on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
. In particular,
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
is meromorphic on
\mathbb{C}^{N}
.

The hyperplanes
H^{*}_{\ell}
,
\ell\geq 0
, are the ‘suspected’ polar sets of
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
. As already remarked, in the one-dimensional case such hyperplanes reduce to the points
s_{\ell}^{*}
, and there is a pole at
s_{\ell}^{*}
if and only if the corresponding structural invariant
d_{F}(\ell)
is non-zero; see Theorem 3 in
[
12
]
. In particular, we have always a pole at
s=s_{0}^{*}
. For the Riemann zeta function, this is the only singularity. For other
L
-functions, there can be infinitely many poles, see
[
6
]
, or just a finite number of them, see
[
11
]
. The case
N\geq 2
is different: all hyperplanes
H^{*}_{\ell}
are polar sets of
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
, for every choice of the parameters
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}
in (
1.3
).

Before formulating a theorem describing the situation, we introduce further notation. For
{\boldsymbol{n}}=(n_{1},\ldots,n_{N})
and
\kappa_{1},\dots,\kappa_{N}
satisfying (
1.3
) we write

\alpha_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\left(\frac{n_{\nu}}{q_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu}^{d_{\nu}}}\right)^{\kappa_{\nu}},

(1.7)

and for
\alpha\in{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})
we let

\Xi({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\omega_{\boldsymbol{F}}\sum_{n_{1}\geq 1}\dots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}n_{N}\geq 1\\
\hskip-39.83368pt\alpha_{\boldsymbol{n}}=\alpha\end{subarray}}\,\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{\overline{a_{\nu}(n_{\nu})}}{n_{\nu}^{1-s_{\nu}}},

(1.8)

\omega_{\boldsymbol{F}}=\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\omega_{\nu},

(1.9)

where
\omega_{\nu}=\omega_{F\nu}
denote the corresponding root numbers, see (
2.1
). Moreover, let
\xi_{\nu}=\xi_{F_{\nu}}
denote the corresponding
\xi
-invariants, see again (
2.1
). Clearly, the sums over the
n_{\nu}
in (
1.8
) are all finite, hence
\Xi({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
is an entire function of
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N}
. With the above notation, an explicit expression for the limit functions
\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
in (
1.6
) is given by the following theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
\alpha\in{\rm Spec}({\boldsymbol{F}})
. Then for every
\ell\geq 0
we have

\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\big(\frac{\pi}{2}d_{\nu}\,s_{\nu}+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\xi_{\nu}+d_{\nu}\,\theta_{\nu}\,\log(d_{\nu}\,\kappa_{\nu})\big)}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{1/d_{\nu}}}{2\pi}\Big)^{d_{\nu}\big(\frac{1}{2}-s_{\nu}\big)}W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})\Xi({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha),

(1.10)

where
W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})\in\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{s}}]
has degree
2\ell
. Moreover, for every
\ell\geq 0
the function
\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
does not vanish identically on
H_{\ell}^{*}
. In particular, every hyperplane
H_{\ell}^{*}
is a polar set of
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
.

The above results can also be viewed in the framework of the theory of multiple Dirichlet series. Such a theory, initiated by Siegel
[
18
]
, in recent years has been greatly developed by Dorian Goldfeld and his school, both from the theoretical and the applications viewpoint. We refer to the papers by Goldfeld-Hoffstein
[
5
]
, Diaconu-Goldfeld-Hoffstein
[
3
]
, Chinta-Friedberg-Hoffstein
[
2
]
and Bump
[
1
]
for a panoramic view on multiple Dirichlet series and a glimpse on applications. We conclude remarking that the ideas and techniques involved in our proofs differ from those in the multiple Dirichlet series theory.

2.
Definitions and lemmas

The extended Selberg class
{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
was introduced in
[
8
]
and consists of non-identically vanishing Dirichlet series as in (
1.1
) admitting a finite order meromorphic continuation to
\mathbb{C}
with the only possible singularity at
s=1
and satisfying a general Riemann type functional equation of the form

Q^{s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j})F(s)=\omega Q^{1-s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Gamma\big(\lambda_{j}(1-s)+\overline{\mu_{j}}\big)\overline{F}(1-s),

where
\overline{F}(s)=\overline{F(\overline{s})}
,
Q,\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{r}
are positive parameters, whereas
\omega,\mu_{1},\dots,\mu_{r}\in\mathbb{C}
with

|\omega|=1
and
\Re(\mu_{j})\geq 0
for
1\leq j\leq r
. We refer to our survey papers
[
7
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
13
]
,
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
for information on the analytic properties of the functions
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
.
Degree
d_{F}
, conductor
q_{F}
, root number
\omega_{F}
,
\tau
-invariant
\tau_{F}
,
\xi
-invariant
\xi_{F}
and internal shift
\theta_{F}
of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
are defined, respectively, as

d_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j},\quad q_{F}=(2\pi)^{d_{F}}Q^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2\lambda_{j}},

\omega_{F}=\omega\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{-2i\Im\mu_{j}},\quad\tau_{F}=\max_{1\leq j\leq r}\frac{\Im(\mu_{j})}{\lambda_{j}},\quad\xi_{F}=2\sum_{j=1}^{r}(\mu_{j}-{\textstyle{1\over 2}})=\eta_{F}+i\theta_{F}d_{F},

(2.1)

with
\eta_{F},\theta_{F}\in\mathbb{R}
. The spectrum of
F
with Dirichlet coefficients
a(n)
is defined by

\text{Spec}(F)=\left\{d_{F}\big(\frac{n}{q_{F}}\big)^{1/d_{F}}:a(n)\neq 0\right\}.

The functional equation of
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
with
d_{F}>0
can be written in the following asymmetric invariant form

F(s)=\omega_{F}S_{F}(s)h_{F}(s)\overline{F}(1-s),

(2.2)

where

S_{F}(s)=2^{r}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\sin(\pi(\lambda_{j}s+\mu_{j}))

and

h_{F}(s)=(2\pi)^{-r}\Big(\prod_{j=1}^{r}\lambda_{j}^{2i\Im\mu_{j}}\Big)Q^{1-2s}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\big(\Gamma(\lambda_{j}(1-s)+\overline{\mu}_{j})\Gamma(1-\lambda_{j}s-\mu_{j})\big).

Note that applying (
2.2
) twice we obtain

S_{F}(s)h_{F}(s)\overline{S_{F}}(1-s)\overline{h_{F}}(1-s)=1.

(2.3)

Lemma 1
.

Let
F\in{\mathcal{S}}^{\sharp}
with
d_{F}>0
. Then for
M\geq 0
and
|\arg(-s)|<\pi-\delta
with any fixed
0<\delta<\pi
we have

\begin{split}h_{F}(s)&=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\left(\frac{q_{F}^{1/d_{F}}}{2\pi d_{F}}\right)^{d_{F}(\frac{1}{2}-s)}\sum_{\ell=0}^{M}d_{F}(\ell)\Gamma\big(d_{F}(s_{\ell}^{*}-s)\big)\\
&\hskip 99.58464pt+O\Big(\frac{1}{|s|}|\Gamma(d_{F}(s_{M}^{*}-s))|\Big)\end{split}

with
s^{*}_{\ell}
as in (
1.2
), certain coefficients
d_{F}(\ell)
and an implicit constant depending on
F
and
M
. In particular, taking
M=0
we have

h_{F}(s)\asymp\left(\frac{q_{F}^{1/d_{F}}}{2\pi d_{F}}\right)^{-d_{F}\sigma}|\Gamma\big(d_{F}(s_{0}^{*}-s)\big)|.

Proof.

This lemma follows from the estimates in Section 3.2 of
[
12
]
.
∎

The coefficients
d_{F}(\ell)
are called the structural invariants of
F
.

Lemma 2
.

For
1\leq\nu\leq N
, let
\alpha_{\nu},r_{\nu}\in\mathbb{C}
and
A_{\nu}\in\mathbb{R}
be such that
|\alpha_{\nu}|,|r_{\nu}|\leq A_{\nu}
. Then

\left|\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}(\alpha_{\nu}+r_{\nu})-\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\alpha_{\nu}\right|\leq(2^{N}-1)\max_{1\leq\nu\leq N}\big(|r_{\nu}|\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq\mu\leq N\\
\mu\neq\nu\end{subarray}}A_{\mu}\big).

Proof.

By induction.

Lemma 3
.

For
M\geq 1
and
|\arg(z)|\leq\pi-\delta
with any fixed
0<\delta<\pi
we have

\log\Gamma(z+s)=(z+s-\frac{1}{2})\log z-z+\frac{1}{2}\log 2\pi+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{(-1)^{j+1}B_{j+1}(s)}{j(j+1)}\frac{1}{z^{j}}+O\big(\frac{1}{|z|^{M+1}}\big),

(2.4)

where
B_{j+1}(s)
are the Bernoulli polynomials. In particular, for every fixed
A<B
we have

\Gamma(x+iy)\ll e^{-\frac{\pi}{2}|y|}|y|^{x-\frac{1}{2}}

(2.5)

uniformly for
A<x<B
and
|y|\geq 1
.

Proof.

This is the well-known Stirling formula; see for instance
[
4
]
.
∎

We denote by
(z)_{m}
the Pochhammer symbol, defined by

(z)_{m}=\begin{cases}1&\text{if $m=0$}\\
z(z+1)\ldots(z+m-1)&\text{if $m>0.$}\end{cases}

We need the following result, which is a modified version of the Lemma in the Appendix in

.

Lemma 4
.

Let
A,b\in\mathbb{C}
with
A\neq 0
be fixed. Then for every
M\geq 1
there exist linear forms

R_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})\quad\text{and}\quad\widetilde{R}_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m-1})\qquad(1\leq m\leq M)

satisfying

R_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})=(-1)^{m}A^{m}X_{m}+\widetilde{R}_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m-1})

(2.6)

and a polynomial
Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)
with

\deg_{X_{j}}Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)\leq 1\quad\text{for $j=1,\dots,M$ and}\quad\deg_{T}Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)\leq M-1

such that

\sum_{k=1}^{M}\frac{X_{k}}{T^{k}}=\sum_{m=1}^{M}\frac{(-1)^{m}R_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m})}{(AT+b)_{m}}+\frac{Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)}{T^{M}(AT+b)_{M}}.

(2.7)

Proof.

We define the polynomials
R_{m}
,
\widetilde{R}_{m}
,
1\leq m\leq M
, and
Q_{M}
inductively. For
M
=1 we set

R_{1}(X_{1}):=-AX_{1},\quad\widetilde{R}_{1}(X_{1})\equiv 0\quad\text{and}\quad Q_{1}(X_{1},T):=bX_{1}.

For
M\geq 2
suppose tha thet linear forms
R_{m}(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})
,
\widetilde{R}_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m-1})
,
1\leq m\leq M-1
, and the polynomial

Q_{M-1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1},T)=E_{M-1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1})T^{M-2}+\dots+E_{1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1}),

say, satisfying (
2.7
) are already defined. We put

R_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M}):=(-1)^{M}\left(A^{M}X_{M}+AE_{M-1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1})\right),

\widetilde{R}_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1}):=(-1)^{M}AE_{M-1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1})

and

\begin{split}Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)&:=Q_{M-1}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M-1},T)T(AT+b+M-1)\\
&+X_{M}(AT+b)_{M}-(-1)^{M}R_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M})T^{M}.\end{split}

Then (
2.6
) holds,

\deg_{X_{j}}R_{m}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{m}),\deg_{X_{j}}Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)\leq 1\quad\text{for $j=1,\ldots,M$},

\deg_{T}Q_{M}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{M},T)\leq M-1

and (
2.7
) holds. The lemma now follows. ∎

Lemma 5
.

Let
N\geq 2
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
and
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{N}>0
be such that
\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}=1
. Moreover, let
a=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}a_{\nu}.
Then for
|\arg(w)|\leq\pi-\delta
with arbitrary
0<\delta<\pi
and
\min_{1\leq\nu\leq N}|\Im(a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w)|\geq 1
, and any integer
M\geq 1
, we have

\begin{split}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\Gamma(a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w)&=(2\pi)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}^{a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w-\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{m=0}^{M}P_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})\Gamma\big(a-\frac{N-1}{2}-w-m\big)\\
&+O\Big(\frac{1}{|w|}|\Gamma(a-\frac{N-1}{2}-w-M)|\Big),\end{split}

(2.8)

where the coefficients of
P_{m}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{1},\ldots,x_{N}]
depend on the
\lambda_{\nu}
’s. Moreover,
P_{0}\equiv 1
and for
m\geq 1

P_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})=\frac{1}{2^{m}m!}\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{a_{\nu}^{2}}{\lambda_{\nu}}-a^{2}\Big)^{m}+\widetilde{P}_{m}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})

(2.9)

with
\deg\widetilde{P}_{m}\leq 2m-1
. The implied constant in (
2.8
) depends on
\delta,M,
the
\lambda_{\nu}
’s and the
a_{\nu}
’s.

Proof.

Using (
2.4
) we have

\begin{split}\log\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\Gamma(a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w)&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\left\{(a_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\nu}w)\log(-w)+\lambda_{\nu}w+\frac{1}{2}\log(2\pi)\right.\\
&\left.\quad+(a_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2}-\lambda_{\nu}w)\log\lambda_{\nu}-\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{B_{j+1}(a_{\nu})}{j(j+1)}\frac{1}{(\lambda_{\nu}w)^{j}}\right\}+O(|w|^{-M-1})\\
&=(a-\frac{N}{2}-w)\log(-w)+w+\frac{N}{2}\log(2\pi)\\
&\quad+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(a_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})\log\lambda_{\nu}-\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}\log\lambda_{\nu}\big)w\\
&-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{1}{j(j+1)}\frac{B_{j+1}(a_{\nu})}{(\lambda_{\nu}w)^{j}}+O(|w|^{-M-1})\\
&=\log\Gamma(a-\frac{N-1}{2}-w)+\frac{N-1}{2}\log(2\pi)+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}(a_{\nu}-\frac{1}{2})\log\lambda_{\nu}\\
&\quad-\big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}\log\lambda_{\nu}\big)w+\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{Q_{j}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})}{w^{j}}+O(|w|^{-M-1}),\end{split}

where the polynomials
Q_{j}\in\mathbb{C}[a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}]
are given by

Q_{j}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})=\frac{1}{j(j+1)}B_{j+1}\Big(a-\frac{N-1}{2}\Big)-\frac{1}{j(j+1)}\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{B_{j+1}(a_{\nu})}{\lambda_{\nu}^{j}}.

(2.10)

Taking the exponential of both sides and then expanding into to power series we obtain

\begin{split}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\Gamma(a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w)&=(2\pi)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}^{a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\big(a-\frac{N-1}{2}-w\big)\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\times\exp\Big(\sum_{j=1}^{M}\frac{Q_{j}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})}{w^{j}}+O(|w|^{-M-1})\Big)\\
&=(2\pi)^{\frac{N-1}{2}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\lambda_{\nu}^{a_{\nu}-\lambda_{\nu}w-\frac{1}{2}}\Gamma\big(a-\frac{N-1}{2}-w\big)\\
&\hskip 85.35826pt\times\left(\sum_{k=0}^{M}\frac{V_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})}{w^{k}}+O(|w|^{-M-1})\right),\end{split}

(2.11)

where
V_{0}\equiv 1
and for
k\geq 1

V_{k}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N})=\sum_{m=1}^{k}\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{j_{1}\geq 1}\ldots\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{m}\geq 1\\
\hskip-36.98866ptj_{1}+\ldots+j_{m}=k\end{subarray}}\prod_{p=1}^{m}Q_{j_{p}}(a_{1},\ldots,a_{N}).

(2.12)

Applying Lemma
4
with

Lemma 8
.

The functions

j=1,\dots,J
, of the form

with distinct linear forms

, are linearly independent over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
N
. If
N=1
, then
L_{j}(s)=a_{j}s
for
j=1,\dots,J
, with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for
i\neq j
. Suppose that

for certain
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
. Differentiating
J-1
times and then putting
s=0
we obtain

\sum_{j=1}^{J}a_{j}^{k}A_{j}=0\quad\text{for}\quad k=0,\dots,J-1.

Since
\det[a_{j}^{k}]\neq 0
, we deduce that
A_{1}=\ldots=A_{J}=0
, and the case
N=1
follows. For
N\geq 2
let

L_{j}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}a_{j\nu}s_{\nu}\quad\text{for}\quad j=1,\dots,J,

and again suppose that

\sum_{j=1}^{J}A_{j}\exp(L_{j}({\boldsymbol{s}}))\equiv 0

for certain
A_{1},\dots,A_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
. Let

, where

, be the distinct numbers in the sequence

. We rewrite the last identity as follows

\sum_{k=1}^{m}\Big(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\
a_{jN}=b_{k}\end{subarray}}A_{j}\exp\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}a_{j\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\Big)\exp(b_{k}s_{N})\equiv 0.

Since the assertion is already proved for

we deduce that

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}1\leq j\leq J\\
a_{jN}=b_{k}\end{subarray}}A_{j}\exp\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}a_{j\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\equiv 0\quad\text{for}\quad k=1,\dots,m.

Moreover, the above linear forms in
N-1

have the same

-th coefficient. Now the lemma follows applying the inductive assumption.

(3.29)

\begin{split}&{\mathcal{I}}_{X}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=e^{-iA({\boldsymbol{s}})}B_{1}({\boldsymbol{s}})B_{2}({\boldsymbol{s}})\sum_{\ell_{1}=0}^{M}\ldots\sum_{\ell_{N}=0}^{M}\sum_{m=0}^{M}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\big(d_{F_{\nu}}(\ell_{\nu})(d_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu})^{-\ell_{\nu}}\big)P_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N},m}({\boldsymbol{s}})\\

Let

{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+m\leq\ell
, thus generating a negligible error term. Recalling (

) and (

&\hskip 241.84842pt+H_{X,\ell}^{(5)}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha);\end{split}

, and

\\
\hskip-45.52458pt{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+m=k\end{subarray}}^{M}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\big(d_{F_{\nu}}(\ell_{\nu})(d_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu})^{-\ell_{\nu}}\big)P_{\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N},m}({\boldsymbol{s}})

Therefore, the integrand in (

{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}

O(1)

and

. Thus, we obtain

{\boldsymbol{F}}_{X}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\Xi_{k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha){\mathcal{I}}_{X,k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)+H_{X,\ell}^{(6)}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha),

{\mathcal{I}}_{X.k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\frac{1}{2\pi i}\int_{(-K)}\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}-w\Big)\Gamma(w)(i\omega_{X})^{-w}\,\text{d}w

.

Now we compute
{\mathcal{I}}_{X,k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)

6

\Re\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\right)\geq\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell+1-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}\Re(s_{\nu})\geq\frac{1}{3}.

\xi=\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu},\quad c=\frac{1}{6}\quad\text{and}\quad\eta=i\omega_{X},

\begin{split}{\mathcal{I}}_{X,k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)&=\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\big((e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}X)^{\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}}-1\big)\\
&\hskip 56.9055pt-\sum_{\mu=1}^{[K]}\frac{(-1)^{\mu}}{\mu!}\Gamma\left(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}+\mu\right)(i\omega_{X})^{\mu}\\

similarly as before with

\xi=\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu},\quad c=\frac{1}{2}\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}\Re(s_{\nu})\Big),\quad\eta=i\omega_{X},

\begin{split}{\mathcal{I}}_{X,\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)&=\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\big((e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}X)^{\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}}-1\big)\\

\begin{split}{\boldsymbol{F}}_{X}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)&=\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\Xi_{k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\big((e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}X)^{\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}}-1\big)\\

{\mathcal{D}}
, and bounded since

We now prove by a finite induction on
-1\leq\ell\leq L:=\max\{\ell:{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\neq\emptyset\}
that the following statements hold true.

(A) For
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
the limit

R_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\lim_{X\to\infty}R_{X,\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)

(3.39)

exists and is holomorphic on
{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
.

(B) The standard twist
{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
admits analytic continuation to
{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\backslash H^{*}_{\ell}
, where the hyperplanes
H^{*}_{\ell}
with
\ell\geq 0
are defined in (
1.5
), whereas
H^{*}_{-1}=\emptyset
since for
\ell=-1
the sum in (
3.32
) is empty.

(C) For
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
, we have

R_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)={\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)+\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\Xi_{k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big).

For
\ell=-1
, (
3.38
) tells us that for
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{-1}
we have

{\boldsymbol{F}}_{X}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=R_{X,-1}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=O(1)

uniformly for
X\geq X_{0}
. Recalling (

), we see that the limit in (
3.39
) exists for
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{-1}\cap\mathbb{C}_{1}^{N}

{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{-1}
and is holomorphic in this domain. This shows (A), and statements (B) and (C) follow as well since
H^{*}_{\ell}=\emptyset
and
\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\equiv 0
for
\ell=-1
. Let now
0\leq\ell\leq L

{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell-1}
we have

\begin{split}R_{X,\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)&=R_{X,\ell-1}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\\
&-\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\big((e^{-i\frac{\pi}{2}}X)^{\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}}-1\big).\end{split}

For such
{\boldsymbol{s}}
, the exponent in the last factor on the right-hand side has negative real part. Thus, using in addition the inductive assumption, we infer that for
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}\cap{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell-1}
the limit

) exists and equals

\begin{split}&R_{\ell-1}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)+\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)\\
&\hskip 28.45274pt={\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)+\sum_{k=0}^{\ell}\Xi_{k}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\Gamma\Big(\frac{d+1}{2}-k-i\theta-\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big).\end{split}

(3.40)

{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}

is holomorphic in
{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
. Therefore, (
3.40

to

. Since
{\mathcal{D}}=\bigcup_{\ell=-1}^{L}{\mathcal{D}}_{\ell}
, from (B) we deduce that

{\boldsymbol{F}}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
admits analytic continuation to

now follows since

can be taken arbitrarily large.

4.
Proof of Theorem
2

A computation based on (
3.18
),(
3.23
) and (
3.27
) shows that

e^{-iA({\boldsymbol{s}})}B_{1}({\boldsymbol{s}})B_{2}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}e^{-i\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\big(\frac{\pi}{2}d_{\nu}\,s_{\nu}+\frac{\pi}{2}\,\xi_{\nu}+d_{\nu}\,\theta_{\nu}\,\log(d_{\nu}\,\kappa_{\nu})\big)}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\Big(\frac{\kappa_{\nu}q_{\nu}^{1/d_{\nu}}}{2\pi}\Big)^{d_{\nu}\big(\frac{1}{2}-s_{\nu}\big)}.

Hence (
3.33
) implies (
1.10
) with

W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\sum_{\ell_{1}\geq 0}\dots\sum_{\ell_{N}\geq 0}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}m\geq 0\\
\\
\hskip-45.52458pt{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+m=\ell\end{subarray}}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\big(d_{F_{\nu}}(\ell_{\nu})(d_{\nu}\kappa_{\nu})^{-\ell_{\nu}}\big)P_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N},m}({\boldsymbol{s}}),

(4.1)

where the polynomials
P_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N},m}({\boldsymbol{s}})
are defined in (
3.28
) and (
3.29
). Note that the upper limit
M
in the summations, present in (
3.33
), is not present in (
4.1
). Indeed,
{\mathcal{D}}
is a large
domain and
\ell
is such that
{\mathcal{D}}\cap H^{*}_{\ell}\neq\emptyset
. For
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in{\mathcal{D}}\cap H^{*}_{\ell}
we have
\ell\leq d(\|{\mathcal{D}}\|+{\textstyle{1\over 2}})+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}
and hence, recalling (
3.21
), the condition
{\boldsymbol{\ell}}+m=\ell
implies that
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{N},m\leq M
. Hence, the upper limit
M
in the summations in (
3.33
) can be dropped.

Next we compute the degree of
W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})
. For simplicity we denote by
\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}
the subspace of the polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[{\boldsymbol{s}}]
of degree
\leq n
. If
(\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N})\neq(0,\ldots,0)
then, according to Lemma
5
, we have

P_{\ell_{1},\ldots,\ell_{N},m}({\boldsymbol{s}})\equiv 0\,(\bmod\,\tilde{\Omega}_{2(\ell-1)}).

Thus, recalling that
|d_{F_{\nu}}(0)|=1
, see (1.22) in
[
12
]
, and using (
3.28
),(
3.29
) and (
2.9
) we deduce that

W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})\equiv P_{0,\ldots,0,\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})\,(\bmod\,\widetilde{\Omega}_{2(\ell-1)})\equiv\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\ell!}\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{d_{\nu}}{\kappa_{\nu}}s_{\nu}^{2}-\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{\ell}\,(\bmod\,\tilde{\Omega}_{2\ell-1}).

(4.2)

This shows that
\deg W_{\ell}=2\ell
since
N\geq 2
.

To conclude the proof we have to check that for every
\ell
the “residual function”
\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
does not vanish identically on the hyperplane
H^{*}_{\ell}
. For
{\boldsymbol{s}}=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{N})\in H^{*}_{\ell}
we have

s_{N}=-\frac{1}{d_{N}}\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}d_{\nu}s_{\nu}+\frac{d+1}{2d_{N}}-\frac{\ell}{d_{N}}-i\frac{\theta}{d_{N}},

(4.3)

and hence, according to (
4.2
), we obtain

W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})\equiv\frac{1}{2^{\ell}\ell!}\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\frac{d_{\nu}}{\kappa_{\nu}}s_{\nu}^{2}+\Big(\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\frac{d_{\nu}}{d_{N}}s_{\nu}\Big)^{2}\Big)^{\ell}\,(\bmod\,\tilde{\Omega}_{2\ell-1}\cap\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-1}]).

Therefore, the restriction
W_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}})_{|H^{*}_{\ell}}
is a polynomial in
\mathbb{C}[s_{1},\ldots,s_{N-1}]
of degree
2\ell
. In particular, it cannot vanish identically on
H^{*}_{\ell}
. Turning to
\Xi({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)
, for
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in H^{*}_{\ell}
and using (
4.3
) we rewrite (
1.8
) as

\Xi({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)=\omega_{\boldsymbol{F}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}{\boldsymbol{m}}\in\mathbb{N}^{N}\\
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}=\alpha\end{subarray}}A({\boldsymbol{m}})\exp(L_{{\boldsymbol{m}}}({\boldsymbol{s}})),

where

A({\boldsymbol{m}})=m_{N}^{\frac{1}{d_{N}}\big(\frac{d+1}{2}-\ell-i\theta\big)}\prod_{\nu=1}^{N}\frac{\overline{a_{\nu}(m_{\nu})}}{m_{\nu}}

and

L_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{s}})=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\Big(\log m_{\nu}-\frac{d_{\nu}}{d_{N}}\log m_{N}\Big)s_{\nu}.

If
\Xi_{\ell}({\boldsymbol{s}},\alpha)\equiv 0
on
H^{*}_{\ell}
then, according to Lemma
8
, not all linear forms
L_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{s}})
are different. Let
{\boldsymbol{m}}\neq{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}
be such that
\alpha_{\boldsymbol{m}}=\alpha_{{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}}=\alpha
and
L_{\boldsymbol{m}}({\boldsymbol{s}})=L_{{\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}}({\boldsymbol{s}})
for all
{\boldsymbol{s}}\in\mathbb{C}^{N-1}
. Then we have

\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\kappa_{\nu}\log m_{\nu}=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\kappa_{\nu}\log m_{\nu}^{\prime},

(4.4)

and for
1\leq\nu\leq N-1

\log m_{\nu}-\frac{d_{\nu}}{d_{N}}\log m_{N}=\log m_{\nu}^{\prime}-\frac{d_{\nu}}{d_{N}}\log m_{N}^{\prime}.

(4.5)

Recalling (
1.3
) we have

\begin{split}\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\kappa_{\nu}\log m_{\nu}&=\kappa_{N}\log m_{N}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\kappa_{\nu}\log m_{\nu}=\kappa_{N}\log m_{N}+\sum_{\nu=1}^{N-1}\kappa_{\nu}\Big(\log m_{\nu}^{\prime}+\frac{d_{\nu}}{d_{N}}\log\frac{m_{N}}{m_{N}^{\prime}}\Big)\\
&=\sum_{\nu=1}^{N}\kappa_{\nu}\log m_{\nu}^{\prime}+\frac{1}{d_{N}}\log\frac{m_{N}}{m_{N}^{\prime}}.\end{split}

This, together with (
4.4
), gives
m_{N}=m_{N}^{\prime}
. But then (
4.5
) implies
m_{\nu}=m_{\nu}^{\prime}
for
1\leq\nu\leq N-1
. Consequently,
{\boldsymbol{m}}={\boldsymbol{m}}^{\prime}
, contrary to our assumption. So we proved that all factors on the right-hand side of (
1.10
) do not vanish identically on
H^{*}_{\ell}
, and Theorem
2
follows.
