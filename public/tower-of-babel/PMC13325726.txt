Transurethral thulium laser incision of ectopic ureterocele with duplex renal collecting system: A 10-year follow-up case report
Authors: Zeen Feng, Yalin Sun
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
We report a 50-year-old female with bilateral duplex renal collecting systems and an ectopic ureterocele causing recurrent urinary tract infections and urgent incontinence. Transurethral incision using a thulium laser was performed. At 10-year follow-up, upper renal cortex thickness increased from 0.52 cm to 1.14 cm, hydronephrosis resolved, and symptoms did not recur. No secondary procedures were required. Thulium laser incision is a safe, durable nephron-sparing option for adult ectopic ureteroceles.

Introduction

Ureteroceles are cystic dilations of the distal ureter that may occur with single or duplex collecting systems. In duplex systems, the ureterocele typically involves the upper pole moiety following the Weigert-Meyer law.1 Although many ureteroceles are detected prenatally, some patients remain asymptomatic until adulthood, presenting with recurrent urinary tract infections (UTIs), void dysfunction, or urinary incontinence. Treatment goals include preservation of renal function, prevention of infection and obstruction, maintenance of continence, and avoidance of multiple surgeries. However, no single strategy for duplex-system ureteroceles has been universally accepted.

Endoscopic incision has become a minimally invasive first-line option, but its role in ectopic ureteroceles in adults remains debated due to risks of vesicoureteral reflux (VUR) and reoperation. Laser technology, particularly the thulium laser, offers precise tissue incision with minimal thermal spread and excellent hemostasis. Long-term follow-up data beyond five years for adult patients treated with thulium laser is scarce. We present a case of an adult woman with bilateral duplex systems and an ectopic ureterocele who underwent transurethral thulium laser incision with an exceptional 10-year follow-up.

Case presentation

A 50-year-old woman presented in December 2014 with a two-year history of urinary frequency, urgency, dysuria, and episodes of urgent urinary incontinence. She had recurrent UTIs every 2–3 months. She denied hematuria, flank pain, or prior urologic surgery, except for an unsuccessful double-J stent indwelling because of severely irritative urinary symptoms. Physical examination was unremarkable.

Contrast-enhanced computed tomography urography (CTU, Fig. 1) revealed bilateral duplex renal collecting systems: complete duplication on the left, and a Y-type conjoined ureter on the right. Hydronephrosis of the left upper moiety was marked: upper renal pelvis width 2.48 cm, and upper renal cortex thickness 0.52 cm. After obtaining written informed consent, the patient underwent transurethral incision under general anesthesia on December 12, 2014. A 120 W continuous-wave thulium:yttrium-aluminum-garnet (Tm:YAG) laser (Tiankun, Tianjin, China) operating at a wavelength of 2013 nm was used. Laser energy was delivered via a 550 μm core-diameter optical fiber. During incision, the laser was set to an output power of 30 W. A 30-mm incision with “smiley face” configuration at the inferomedial aspect of the ureterocele was made to preserve the flap valve mechanism (Fig. 2C). Immediate decompression was observed. A double-J stent (4.7 Fr, 24 cm) was placed in the upper ureter (Fig. 2D) and left for four weeks. Operative time was about 30 minutes with minimal bleeding. The patient was discharged on postoperative day 3.

Postoperatively, irritative symptoms and incontinence resolved within one week. Follow-up CTU at 6 months showed significant improvement: upper renal pelvis width decreased to 1.45 cm, and cortex thickness increased to 0.68 cm, pelvis width to 1.45 cm, and ureterocele width to 1.78 cm. At 10 years, CTU demonstrated sustained improvement: cortex thickness was 1.14 cm (117% increase), pelvis width 1.18 cm, and ureterocele width 1.19 cm. The patient remained asymptomatic, with no UTIs, incontinence, or additional interventions. Renal function was stable (creatinine 0.79 mg/dL). Discussion

We report a case of adult duplex-system ectopic ureterocele successfully treated with transurethral thulium laser incision, with an unprecedented 10-year follow-up. The patient achieved sustained clinical and anatomical improvement, including progressive thickening of the renal cortex and resolution of hydronephrosis, without the need for secondary surgery.

Management of duplex-system ureteroceles in adults remains challenging. Traditional options include open ureterocele excision with reimplantation, upper pole heminephrectomy, or ureteroureterostomy, all of which carry significant morbidity.2 Endoscopic incision offers a minimally invasive, nephron-sparing alternative, but concerns about VUR and reoperation rates persist, especially for ectopic ureteroceles.3 A recent meta-analysis of 3022 patients found that ectopic location (RR 2.42, 95% CI 1.89–3.11) and duplex systems (RR 2.50, 95% CI 1.60–3.91) are associated with significantly higher reoperation rates after endoscopic procedures.4 Our patient possessed both risk factors yet required no reoperation over 10 years, suggesting that meticulous thulium laser incision with proper stenting may mitigate these risks.

Thulium laser provides practical advantages over holmium:YAG or cold-knife incision. It offers precise, shallow ablation with minimal thermal damage to surrounding tissue, reducing the risk of de novo VUR while achieving effective decompression.5 In our case, grade I VUR at 10 years did not cause any symptoms or UTIs.

The progressive increase in renal cortex thickness (117% over 10 years) is of particular interest. While complete renal parenchymal regeneration in adults is limited, relief of long-standing obstruction can lead to partial functional recovery through reduced intrapelvic pressure and improved perfusion. This sustained improvement supports the durability of thulium laser decompression.

Limitations of this report include its single-case nature and the absence of preoperative and postoperative diuretic renography to quantify split renal function. Nonetheless, the 10-year follow-up is the longest reported to date for an adult with duplex-system ectopic ureterocele treated by thulium laser incision.

Conclusion

Transurethral thulium laser incision of an ectopic ureterocele with duplex renal collecting system in an adult patient provided durable clinical and anatomical improvement over 10 years. The procedure was safe, minimally invasive, and associated with progressive renal cortex thickening and complete symptom resolution without secondary interventions. For carefully selected adult patients with functioning upper pole moieties, this technique represents a reliable nephron-sparing option with excellent long-term outcomes.
