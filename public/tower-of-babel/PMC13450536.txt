Validity and reliability of the Chinese version of the Barriers to Physical Activity during Pregnancy Scale (BPAPS) in pregnant women
Authors: Linfei Ye, Xingchen Shang, Mang Gui, Liping Sun, Hongwen Yao, Ye Zhang, Lin Li, Fangfang Wang, Leila Amiri-Farahani, Yuexia Liao
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundDespite regular physical activity (PA) is beneficial to maternal health and her fetus, engagement in PA among pregnant women is inactive. It is essential to assess barriers to PA in this population to increase pregnant women’s PA level. However, there is a lack of reliable and valid tools to evaluate barriers to PA during pregnancy in China. Therefore, the study aimed to translate and validate the Barriers to Physical Activity during Pregnancy Scale (BPAPS) in Chinese pregnant women.MethodsA study of 448 pregnant women was conducted in China from November to December 2023. After obtaining the author’s permission, the translation of the BPAPS was carried out with a standardized procedure. The validity of the BPAPS was measured by the content validity, construct validity, and criterion validity in this study. The reliability of the BPAPS was evaluated using internal consistency and test-retest.ResultsThe final Chinese version of the BPAPS had four domains with 28 items. Background

Physical activity (PA) is any bodily movement produced by skeletal muscles that need energy expenditure [1]. Exercise is a subcategory of PA that is planned, structured, repetitive, and purposefully aimed at improving one or more components of physical fitness, which is an essential element of a healthy lifestyle [2]. Growing evidence suggests that PA can significantly affect maternal health as well as that of her fetus [3]. For the mother, the benefits of regular PA include reduced excessive gestational weight gain [4], low risk of gestational diabetes mellitus (GDM) [3, 5], less chance of cesarean Sect. [6], improved cardiorespiratory fitness [7], increased incidence of vaginal delivery [4], and improved sleep [8]. For the fetus, PA can help newborns have better body composition after birth [3], improve neurological development [9], and reduce the risk of obesity in childhood [10].

Although regular PA has many health benefits, most pregnant women have low adherence to PA and remain physically inactive during pregnancy [11, 12]. Many pregnant women do not comply with the World Health Organization (WHO) recommendations: all pregnant women without contraindications should do moderate-intensity PA at least 150 min per week through pregnancy [5, 13]. Studies conducted in several countries revealed that approximately 7.5% to 21% of pregnant women met PA guidelines of 150 min or more [14–16]. A survey in China demonstrated that only 2.8% of participants had good adherence levels to sufficient PA during pregnancy [17]. Thus, a pressing issue to address is how to increase PA in the daily lives of pregnant women. Inadequate PA in pregnant women is in part associated with barriers to PA [12]. Accordingly, it is essential to assess barriers to PA in this population to increase PA level.

Quantitative and qualitative research has formed the identification of perceptions of multiple barriers to PA during pregnancy. Among intrapersonal barriers, fatigue, lack of energy, physical discomfort, uncertainty about miscarriages and premature labor, awkwardness because of weight gain and changed size as pregnancy progressed, and lack of confidence were reported in previous studies [18–21]. Cultural norms of lack of PA habits, childcare responsibilities, lack of support from husband, family, relatives, and friends, lack of knowledge about how to do PA during pregnancy, and lack of support from health care providers were considered as the interpersonal barriers [19–21]. Environment barriers were lack of access to facilities, lack of safe and affordable places to be physically active, unfavorable weather, and air pollution [20–23]. The barriers that every pregnant woman perceives are different and affected by many factors to varying degrees [19]. Therefore, it is recommended that healthcare professionals design personalized and effective interventions targeted at barriers to PA that increase pregnant women’s participation in PA.

In previous studies, the Exercise Benefits/Barriers Scale (EBBS) is used as a tool to identify barriers to PA. Although the scale has shown good reliability and validity worldwide, it applies to whole populations and is not specific to pregnant women [18, 24, 25]. In addition, some studies relating to measuring barriers to PA during pregnancy have been conducted [26–28]. However, the reliability and validity of the tools in these studies have not been confirmed. Thus, there is a shortage of reliable tools to evaluate barriers to PA during pregnancy. The Barriers to Physical Activity during Pregnancy Scale (BPAPS) developed by Amiri-Farahani et al. was used to assess barriers to PA in pregnant women [29]. This scale takes a socio-ecological approach and has pregnancy-related intrapersonal, non-pregnancy related intrapersonal, interpersonal, and environmental domains [29]. The reliability and validity of the BPAPS were confirmed among Iranian pregnant women [12]. However, the Chinese version of the BPAPS has not been validated, and it is unclear whether the structure of this scale has good cultural adaptation. Therefore, this study aimed to translate the BPAPS into Chinese and test its reliability and validity in pregnant women.

Methods

Research design and participants

This cross-sectional study was conducted at 3 tertiary hospitals in Yangzhou, China from November to December 2023. Participants were included if they met the following eligibility criteria: (1) aged ≥ 18 years; (2) at any trimester with a singleton pregnancy; (3) can read and write in Chinese and participate voluntarily. Participants were excluded if they had the following contraindications for PA during pregnancy [3]: ruptured membranes, premature labor, unexplained persistent vaginal bleeding, placenta previa after 28 weeks’ gestation, preeclampsia, incompetent cervix, intrauterine growth restriction, high-order multiple pregnancy (e.g., triplets), uncontrolled type I diabetes, uncontrolled hypertension, uncontrolled thyroid disease, or other serious cardiovascular, respiratory or systemic disorder.

Data collection

Convenient sampling was applied to recruit participants in the hospitals’ obstetrics departments. Participants were recruited through direct verbal invitations to the participants. We approached the women who had registered and received routine prenatal care in the obstetrics department at the hospital and explained the aim and nature of the study to them face to face. Informed consent was obtained from the participants before the investigation. Participants were assured of the anonymity and confidentiality of their information and were free to withdraw from the study at any stage without any justification. Eligible participants were asked to complete the questionnaire independently in a quiet room. All pregnant women participated in this study entirely voluntarily without additional compensation. Investigators were responsible for the data collection, and they were all trained by the first author before the investigation. After completion, the investigators checked the questionnaire immediately to identify any missing data. This study followed the guidelines of the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the School of Nursing Ethics Committee, Yangzhou University (Approval No. YZUHL20230016).

Translation process

The Chinese version of the BPAPS was constructed strictly following the Brislin translation-back translation procedure [30]. Permission was obtained from the original author via e-mail. Firstly, the scale was translated into Chinese independently by a doctoral student majoring in nursing and a graduate student majoring in English (version A1 and A2, respectively). Subsequently, a nursing professor conducted a thorough review and verification of the translation, further ensuring its professional accuracy and precision. Draft A was obtained by a comparative analysis of the translated Chinese version A1 and A2. Secondly, two clinical specialists with a background in overseas study (who were not familiar with the original scale) back-translated the Chinese version A into English independently (version B1 and B2, respectively). Two nursing professors were invited to discuss and compare B1 and B2, forming the back-translated English version B. Finally, a committee of experts composed of two clinical obstetricians, four obstetric nursing experts, and two associate professors of nursing was invited to discuss and compare the original scale, the translated Chinese version A and the back-translated English version B. Controversial items were modified, focusing on linguistic and cultural adjustments to make the scale more appropriate for China. Experts suggested adding spouse support to pregnant women’s PA in the interpersonal barriers. After discussion, we decided to add the item “my husband and family do not support me in doing physical activity during pregnancy” to the interpersonal barriers. The entire translation process was reviewed, and a pre-final version of the BPAPS with 30 items (version C) was formulated. A pilot study using the BPAPS (version C) was conducted with 30 pregnant women to evaluate the usability and understanding of the scale in a Chinese cultural context. The participants reported no difficulty understanding each item, and the scale was convenient to fill out, so the final Chinese version of the BPAPS was developed. It is noteworthy that the results of the pilot test will not be included in the formal survey.

Measures

Interviewer-administered structured closed-ended questionnaire was used to collect quantitative data. All questions were completed via the self-reporting method by those who met the inclusion criteria. The questionnaire (54 questions in total) comprises three sections, including individual characteristics (10 questions), the BPAPS (30 questions), the EBBS (14 questions).

Individual characteristics

The questionnaire collected individual characteristics, including demographic information (e.g., age, education level, employment status, place of residence, and income) and pregnancy-related information (e.g., trimester, parity, pre-pregnancy body mass index (BMI) and whether there are pregnancy complications.

Barriers to Physical Activity during Pregnancy Scale (BPAPS)

Amiri-Farahani et al. developed the BPAPS to assess barriers to PA during pregnancy [29]. The scale consists of 29 items with 4 domains (pregnancy-related intrapersonal barriers, non-pregnancy related intrapersonal barriers, interpersonal barriers, and environmental barriers), rated on a Likert 5-point scale ranging from strongly disagree to strongly agree [12]. The total score is from 29 to 145, with higher scores associated with a greater perception of barriers to PA during pregnancy. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of the total scale and subscales of pregnancy-related intrapersonal barriers, non-pregnancy related intrapersonal barriers, interpersonal barriers, and environmental barriers were 0.824, 0.815, 0.732, 0.734, and 0.722, respectively. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of the BPAPS were 0.942.

Exercise Benefits/Barriers Scale (EBBS)

The EBBS designed by Sechrist et al. assesses the benefits and barriers of exercise [24]. The scale includes 43 items, of which 29 are benefits subscales, and 14 are barriers subscales. Each item is based on a Likert 4-point scale, ranging from ‘strongly agree’ to ‘strongly disagree’. Each item in this scale needs to be scored in reverse. The EBBS has been translated into Chinese by Tung et al., and the Chinese version of the scale has shown good reliability with Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of 0.910 [31]. Considering the aim of this study, we only used the barriers subscale. The barrier subscale consists of 4 constructs: time expenditure (3 items), exercise milieu (6 items), physical exertion (3 items), and family discouragement (2 items). The total score of this subscale ranges from 14 to 56, and higher scores indicate less perception of barriers. In this study, the Cronbach’s alpha coefficients of the EBBS were 0.919.

Sample size

The samples were divided into two subsamples for exploratory factor analysis (EFA) and confirmatory factor analysis (CFA) [32]. For EFA, the sample size should be 5–10 times total number of scale items. The original scale has 29 items, and the required sample size should be at least 145–290. For CFA, the sample size should be no less than 200 [32]. Considering the nonresponse deviation, the sample size should be increased by 10% [33]. Thus, the sample size for this study should be larger than 384.

Statistical analysis

SPSS version 27.0 and AMOS version 26.0 were used to analyze the data. Data entry was performed using a double-entry method to ensure accuracy. As the data was normally distributed, descriptive statistics of mean ± standard deviation (SD) and frequency (percentage) were analyzed for continuous and categorical variables, respectively.

Item analysis

The total scores of the scales of all participants were ranked from highest to lowest, with the top 27% in the high-score group and the bottom 27% in the low-score group. Scores for each item in both groups were analyzed using a two-tailed independent sample t-test. The difference between the high and low groups for each scale item was statistically significant (P < 0.05). Pearson correlation was used to analyze the correlation between each item and the total scale. The item was deleted if Pearson’s correlation coefficients (r) < 0.4. If Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the entire scale increased after an item was deleted, this item should be deleted.

Validity analysis

Content validity

The content validity index (CVI) was used to determine the content validity of the BPAPS. The CVI consists of an item-level content validity index (I-CVI) and an average scale-level content validity index (S-CVI/Ave) [34]. Experts with extensive research and practice experience working with pregnant women were invited to assess the scale’s content validity. Two rounds of expert consultations were conducted. Each expert was asked to rate the items using a 4-point Likert scale (1 = ‘not relevant’ to 4 = ‘very relevant’), which can be used to calculate the CVI. The I-CVI was ≥ 0.78, and the S-CVI/Ave was ≥ 0.9, suggesting good content validity [32].

Construct validity

EFA and CFA were performed to evaluate the construct validity of the BPAPS in Chinese. All participants were randomly divided into two groups: one for EFA (224 participants) and the other for CFA (224 participants).

A principal components analysis (PCA) with varimax rotation was used to determine the scale’s factor structure [35]. Before conducting the EFA, the Kaiser-Meyer-Olkin (KMO) test and Bartlett’s test of sphericity were used to evaluate sampling adequacy and suitability of factor analysis [36]. A KMO score of > 0.6 and a significant value of Bartlett’s test of sphericity indicated that the sampling was adequate and the data was appropriate for factor analysis [37]. The factors with eigenvalues of > 1.0 were extracted [37]. The cumulative variance contribution rate of the factors > 50% was acceptable. Items were deleted if they met one of the following criteria [38]: (1) an item had a factor loading of < 0.4; (2) an item was loaded on two or above factors, and the load difference was < 0.1.

A CFA was conducted to verify the consistency of the structure model obtained from EFA. Maximum-likelihood estimation was used for matrix calculations [39]. The goodness-of-fit of the model was evaluated by the chi-square to degrees of freedom ratio (χ2/df), the root mean-square-error of approximation (RMSEA), the comparative fit index (CFI), the incremental fit index (IFI) and the tusker-lewis index (TLI). A model with χ2/df < 3, RMSEA < 0.08, and CFI, IFI, and TLI > 0.9 was acceptable [39]. If the model did not fit well, the modification indices were used as a reference, and corrections were conducted according to the model correction principle [40].

Criterion validity

The reference scale was selected in this study based on the previous literature and the experience of researchers, namely the EBBS. Criterion validity was verified using the Pearson correlation between the EBBS and BPAPS scores. When the Pearson correlation coefficients (r) ranged from 0.4 to 0.8, they were considered acceptable [35].

Reliability analysis

Internal consistency reliability and test-retest reliability

The reliability of the questionnaire was evaluated using internal consistency reliability and test–retest reliability. Internal consistency reliability was assessed using Cronbach’s alpha coefficient, with Cronbach’s alpha values ≥ 0.7 indicating good internal consistency [41]. The intraclass correlation coefficient (ICC) was used to evaluate the test-retest reliability of the scale, which is considered an adequate test to assess the stability of the BPAPS over time [37]. ICC values greater than 0.7 are generally considered to have acceptable test–retest reliability [42].

Results

In total, 460 pregnant women were recruited in the study, and 5 did not meet the inclusion criteria. Four hundred fifty-five questionnaires were distributed and returned, and 7 invalid questionnaires were removed due to regular answers with the same options. Four hundred forty-eight valid questionnaires remained with an effective rate of 98.46%.

Item analysis

The two-tailed independent sample t-test showed a statistically significant difference between the items in the high and low groups (P < 0.05). The correlation coefficients between the scores of the 30 items and the total score of the BPAPS ranged from 0.480 to 0.738 (P < 0.01). The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the scale calculated after each deletion of an item was less than that of the scale before deletion. Therefore, no items were deleted.

Validity analysis

Content validity

Eight experts assessed the content validity of the BPAPS. In the second round of expert consultations, the I-CVI of all items ranged from 0.875 to 1.000, and the S-CVI/Ave was 0.931.

Construct validity

In this study, the KMO’s were 0.918, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant (χ2 = 4086.451, P < 0.001), indicating the adequacy of sample and factor analysis can be carried out. PCA with varimax rotation extracted four factors (eigenvalues > 1), explaining 62.332% of the total variance (Table 2). Because items 8 and 18 were loaded on two factors or above and the load difference was less than 0.1, the two items were deleted. The factor loading of the retained item ranged from 0.516 to 0.834, all of which were > 0.4. Based on the results of EFA, CFA was carried out to examine the four-factor model. The initial model did not fit well (χ2/df = 2.600, RMSEA = 0.084, CFI = 0.877, IFI = 0.878, and TLI = 0.862). After modification, the final model had a reasonable fit to the data (χ2/df = 2.439, RMSEA = 0.068, CFI = 0.913, IFI = 0.913, and TLI = 0.903). Criterion validity

The EBBS was used as the standard to measure the criterion validity. As expected, the BPAPS was moderately correlated with the EBBS, with a correlation coefficient 0.605 (P < 0.001).

Discussion

Evidence on measure tools that assess barriers to PA is crucial for physicians and midwives to evaluate PA during pregnancy and the effects of related interventions aimed at increasing PA. However, there is a lack of reliable and valid instruments to assess barriers to PA during pregnancy. This study was the first attempt to verify the scale that can measure the barriers to PA during pregnancy in China. The results of this study showed that the BPAPS is a reliable and valid tool to assess barriers to PA in pregnant women.

Validity reflects the degree to which the measuring tool accurately corresponds to the real world [43]. Content validity, construct validity, and criterion validity of the BPAPS were assessed in this study. According to the suggestions of our experts, we added the item “My husband and family do not support me in doing physical activity during pregnancy”. A systematic review of 32 studies shows that women’s partners or husbands were the most influential factor [19]. A qualitative study of 30 pregnant women also pointed out that the relative importance of advice received from husband and family contribute to initiating physical activity behavior [44]. In Chinese culture, spouses and family play a vital role in caring for the daily life of pregnant women, and support from them is critical to pregnant women in doing PA, so we added this item. The item was considered to be understandable and acceptable. The I-CVI of the Chinese BPAPS ranged from 0.875 to 1.000, and S-CVI/Ave was 0.931, well above the criterion for content validity, indicating acceptable content validity. According to these findings, all items were retained.

The KMO value was 0.909, and Bartlett’s test of sphericity was significant, indicating that the sample size was adequate and the data was appropriate for factor analysis [35]. In this study, four factors were extracted through EFA, explaining 62.332% of the variance in the total data. The number of factors is consistent with that of the original scale. In the original scale, item 10 belonged to the non-pregnancy related intrapersonal barriers. However, this item was classified into the interpersonal barriers in this study. Some systematic reviews reported that childcare responsibilities were identified as an interpersonal barrier to PA [18, 45]. Therefore, it is reasonable to change the domain of item 10 from the non-pregnancy related intrapersonal barriers to the interpersonal barriers. In addition, according to the calculating result, items 8 and 18 were deleted. Among the remaining items, although some items were loaded on two or more factors, the load difference was > 0.1. Therefore, all remaining items were retained. After adjusting the items, the total score changed from 28 to 140.

In CFA, the initial model did not show a good data fit. According to the model correction principle, the model was modified by adding residual correlation coefficients [35]. There was an apparent relationship between three paired items, such the item 23 (“The physician/midwife does not provide advice on the benefits of physical activity during pregnancy”) and 24 (“The physician/midwife does not provide advice on how to do physical activity safely during pregnancy”). Thus, it is reasonable to add these residual correlation coefficients. Based on the standard of model fitting indices, χ2/df < 3, RMSEA < 0.08, CFI > 0.9, IFI > 0.9, and TLI > 0.9. The model fit indices obtained by CFA indicated that the model fitting was suitable.

In this study, the EBBS, now widely used worldwide, was used as the criterion standard. The results showed that the BPAPS had a great correlation with the EBBS (r = 0.605, P < 0.001), revealing good criterion validity. This scale could effectively evaluate the barriers to PA during pregnancy. Overall, the Chinese version of the BPAPS had good validity among pregnant women.

Reliability refers to the consistency and stability of the measurement tool. The reliability of the BPAPS was evaluated using internal consistency and test-retest. The Cronbach’s alpha coefficient of the total scale and four subscales of pregnancy-related intrapersonal barriers, non-pregnancy related intrapersonal barriers, interpersonal barriers, and environmental barriers were > 0.7, indicating that the BPAPS had sufficient internal consistency. The Cronbach’s alpha in this study is higher than the results of the original scale, which may be due to the participants’ cultural differences and different occupations [29]. In addition, 30 pregnant women who participated in the first test were invited to re-complete the questionnaire after two weeks, which allowed time to prevent participants from recalling original answers while minimizing changes in the measured construct [46]. The ICC for the total scale and each domain ranged from 0.813 to 0.865, which was > 0.7 [40]. The results indicated the Chinese scale version had good stability over time. In short, the Chinese version of the BPAPS also had good reliability.

Many quantitative and qualitative studies have investigated barriers to PA in pregnant women. However, the tools used in these studies cannot be considered reliable and valid. Therefore, this study is important because the BPAPS is a reliable and valid measuring tool to assess barriers to PA in pregnant women. In this study, the scale demonstrated good reliability and validity following strict cross-cultural translation. It is simple and easy to understand and can be used in Chinese pregnant women. Identification of barriers to PA can help healthcare professionals take personalized interventions to improve engagement in PA during pregnancy.

To our knowledge, this is the first study to translate and validate the BPAPS in China. The results of our study provide important insight into PA during pregnancy. The BPAPS can serve as a comprehensive measurement tool designed to assess the barriers to physical activity during pregnancy. Furthermore, it offers tailored solutions grounded in the assessment findings, ultimately increasing physical activity during pregnancy. However, there were several limitations in this study. For this study, convenient sampling from hospital obstetrics departments may not capture the perspectives of women from rural or underserved areas. Therefore, these samples may limit the generalizability of the findings. Future studies should expand sample size, such as a multicenter sample. Moreover, the scale was only validated in two countries. The applicability of this scale should continue to be explored in other contexts. Further research is needed to examine potential cultural differences in response to the BPAPS. In this study, the use of self-report measures may result in participants offering more socially acceptable (rather than sincere) answers. Future studies conducted using more objective tools for the measurements are required. In this study, the number of pregnant women in the second and third trimesters was significantly higher than that in the first trimester. Due to constraints in terms of time and financial costs, it was challenging for us to achieve a relatively even distribution of participants across the three trimesters. Future research needs further improvement to better balance the sample distribution among different trimesters.

Conclusion

This study examined the reliability and validity of the Chinese version of the BPAPS in Chinese pregnant women, and the results showed that this scale could be an appropriate measure tool for barriers to PA during pregnancy. The localized version of the scale aligns well with the cultural and linguistic characteristics of Chinese pregnant women, making the assessment process smooth and enhancing the accuracy of the results. Assessment of barriers to PA in pregnant women is essential for healthcare professionals to identify the specific types of barriers to PA to provide multi-level intervention for pregnant women to increase their engagement in PA. Through professional assessment and personalized care, midwives can accurately identify and effectively addressing the barriers to PA in pregnant women, thereby enhancing their PA and overall well-being.
