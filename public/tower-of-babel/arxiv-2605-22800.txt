The Matching Principle: A Geometric Theory of Loss Functions for Nuisance-Robust Representation Learning
Vishal Rajput

Abstract
Robustness, domain adaptation, photometric/occlusion invariance, sensor drift, and alignment style are usually treated as separate literatures with separate method families. Under label-preserving deployment shift they largely share one geometric object: the covariance Σ task = Cov Q n ​ ( n ) \Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}=\mathrm{Cov}_{Q_{n}}(n) of ways inputs can change without changing the label. CORAL, adversarial training, augmentation, metric learning, Jacobian penalties, and alignment constraints are not independent tricks—they are estimators of Σ task \Sigma_{\mathrm{task}} . Fix that object and the Jacobian penalty is pinned by a matrix Σ ′ \Sigma^{\prime} whose range must cover range ⁡ ( Σ task ) \mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}) —the matching principle . We prove optimality in a linear-Gaussian model (Theorem A), necessity of range coverage for any quadratic Jacobian penalty that zeros deployment drift (Theorem G), and the same dichotomy at global minima (Theorem A ⋆ global {}^{\star}_{\mathrm{global}} ). Wrong-direction and signal-aligned controls (Lemma C; Corollaries E/E ⋆ ) and seven estimators (Lemmas D1–D7), together with the label-free Trajectory Deviation Index ( tdi ), yield a falsifiable recipe when Σ task \Sigma_{\mathrm{task}} must be learned. Thirteen pre-specified blocks (classical ML through Qwen2.5-7B) test matched vs isotropic vs wrong-direction penalties on geometry and deployment drift . Twelve match theory where identifiability holds; Office-31 is a named eigengap failure. Several partial passes are explicit: geometry can improve without every headline task metric moving (§ 7.2 ). A pilot 7 7 B dpo run (one epoch, 240 240 pairs): matched style- pmh preserves Style tdi where standard dpo degrades it. We do not claim that standard training reaches global minima (assumption (O) is open), that Σ ^ task \hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{task}} is always identifiable, or dominance on every leaderboard. We claim a falsifiable design recipe: estimate Σ task \Sigma_{\mathrm{task}} , match Σ ′ \Sigma^{\prime} , run the controls, and read task and geometry on separate axes.

1.1
The problem the field had

The post-2018 literature accumulated three structural difficulties at once. First, methods proliferated
faster than the theory could organise them, leaving comparison reduced to leaderboard-driven empirical
horse races. Second, every method came with its own ablation conventions—random projections, label
shuffling, signal masking—without any shared understanding of why one ablation falsifies a hypothesis
and another does not. Third, the
loss function
was treated as background: cross-entropy or MSE
or InfoNCE was assumed, and innovation took place in architectures, data, and compute. The matching
principle reorganises all three. It identifies the common population object the methods are estimating,
turns ablations into named falsification tests with predicted outcomes, and promotes the loss function
to a first-class design variable parametrised by one PSD matrix per nuisance type.

Setup and definitions

We work in the Perturbation Matching Hypothesis (PMH) framework: a task loss plus a trace penalty on
the encoder Jacobian, with an explicit deployment-nuisance law
Q_{n}
.

Spaces and maps.

x\in\mathcal{X}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{x}}
;
y\in\mathcal{Y}
(discrete or continuous); encoder

\phi_{\theta}:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Z}=\mathbb{R}^{d_{\phi}}

f_{\theta}=h_{\theta}\circ\phi_{\theta}

Training distribution.

condition:
either screening confounding (

3.1

Deployment-nuisance law.

Think of

(not a separate latent variable): noise, domain shift,

(1)

finite.

is the paper’s central object: which directions in input space move at deployment

without changing the label

P_{\mathrm{deploy}}(x,y)=\int P(x-n,y)\,dQ_{n}(n)

4.1
Theorem A: the matched penalty is the right answer

Reader map.

Part (i): cover
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
or drift stays
\Theta(1)
. Part (ii): within the matched range,
allocate via cube-root water-filling (QM9 tradeoff when
\lambda
is finite). Proof: Appendix

.

Intuition.

Imagine the deployment nuisance moves your input along a small set of directions in input space. If the
encoder’s Jacobian has energy along those directions, deployment perturbations shift the embedding and
the model’s prediction drifts. Theorem A says: in the linear-Gaussian model, the way to eliminate this
drift is to penalise the Jacobian along exactly those directions, and the penalty has to cover all of
them or it fails completely.

Theorem 4.1

(Matched-
\Sigma
optimality; linear-Gaussian)
.

For the linear-Gaussian regression model (Remark
A.1
) with deployment-nuisance covariance
\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}
,
the
pmh

w(\Sigma^{\prime})=(I+2\lambda\Sigma^{\prime})^{-1}v
satisfies

\tilde{D}_{Q}(w(\Sigma^{\prime}))\xrightarrow{\lambda\to\infty}0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mathrm{range}(\Sigma^{\prime})\supseteq\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}).

When this range condition fails,
\tilde{D}_{Q}(w(\Sigma^{\prime}))
converges to a constant independent of
\lambda
.
Within the range-matched class, the trace-constrained optimum is the cube-root water-filling allocation

\mu_{i}^{*}\propto(\tilde{v}_{i}^{2}\lambda_{i})^{1/3}
, with the proportional rule
\Sigma^{\prime}\propto\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}

recovering it under rotation-invariant regressor energy.

What this rules out.

Missing a nuisance direction in
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
cannot be fixed by increasing
\lambda
; the drift
floor is structural (same geometry as the centre panel of Figure

).

4.2
Theorem G: the range condition is not optional

Intuition.

Theorem A says range matching is
sufficient
. Theorem G says no other quadratic Jacobian penalty
can succeed without doing the same thing. Across all of CORAL, adversarial training, Mahalanobis metric
learning, augmentation, and Jacobian regularisation, the only way to drive deployment drift to zero on

every
task-relevant direction is to cover
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
.

Theorem G 4.2

(Necessity of
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
)
.

Let
A\succeq 0
define any quadratic Jacobian regulariser
\mathcal{R}_{A}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathrm{Tr}(J_{\phi}^{\top}J_{\phi}A)]
.
If
\tilde{D}_{Q}(w_{\lambda}(A))\to 0
for every effective regressor
v\in\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
, then

\mathrm{range}(A)\supseteq\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
.

What this rules out.

No quadratic Jacobian penalty can zero drift without covering
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
.

4.3
Three consequences of Theorem G: CORAL, PGD-AT, and augmentation as matched

pmh

Why this subsection exists.

Readers often treat CORAL, PGD-AT, and heavy augmentation as unrelated tricks. The point of the rewrites
below is
not
to claim we invented them, but to show they already estimate different

\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(k)}
and add a Jacobian penalty along it—so disagreements between methods
in §
8
are disagreements about
estimator quality and eigengap
, not about whether
a shared principle exists.

Theorem G says every successful quadratic Jacobian regulariser covers
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
. We make
that claim concrete on the three most-cited methods: rewrite each loss until its implicit
\Sigma^{\prime}
is
visible, then read off the Lemma D
k
failure mode that block tests.

Deep CORAL is matched
pmh
along the cross-domain Gram.

The Deep CORAL objective
[
15
]
adds a feature-level term

\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{CORAL}}=\|C^{\phi}_{S}-C^{\phi}_{T}\|_{F}^{2}

where
C^{\phi}_{S},C^{\phi}_{T}
are source and target feature covariances. Linearise the encoder around the
source mean:
\phi(x)\approx\phi(\mu_{S})+J_{\phi}(\mu_{S})(x-\mu_{S})
. Then

C^{\phi}_{S}-C^{\phi}_{T}\approx J_{\phi}\,(\mathrm{Cov}_{S}(x)-\mathrm{Cov}_{T}(x))\,J_{\phi}^{\top}=J_{\phi}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}\,J_{\phi}^{\top},

with
\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}=\mathrm{Cov}(x_{T}-x_{S})
the cross-domain Gram — which is exactly the Lemma D4
estimator
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(4)}
. By cyclic invariance of the trace and the operator-norm
bound,

\|J_{\phi}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}\,J_{\phi}^{\top}\|_{F}^{2}\;=\;\mathrm{Tr}\!\big((J_{\phi}\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}J_{\phi}^{\top})^{2}\big)\;\leq\;\|J_{\phi}\|_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\,\|\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,\mathrm{Tr}(J_{\phi}^{\top}J_{\phi}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}).

Up to bounded operator-norm factors, CORAL minimises the matched-
pmh
penalty along

\Sigma_{\mathrm{dom}}
—a second-moment surrogate of matched
pmh
under
A_{4}
(Lemma
A.11
).
When the cross-domain spectrum is nearly rank-deficient (Office-31, §
8.1
), the
A_{1}
/D1
eigengap condition fails and both CORAL and matched
pmh
degrade, as predicted.

PGD adversarial training is matched
pmh
along the gradient-direction Gram.

PGD-AT
[
9
]
samples
\hat{\delta}^{\star}(x)=\Pi_{\|\delta\|\leq\epsilon}\big[\alpha\,\mathrm{sign}(\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L})\big]^{\!K}
at each step and trains on

\mathcal{L}(\theta;x+\hat{\delta}^{\star})
. Taylor-expand the per-sample loss along
\delta
:

\mathcal{L}(\theta;x+\delta)=\mathcal{L}(\theta;x)+\delta^{\top}\nabla_{x}\mathcal{L}+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\delta^{\top}J_{\phi}^{\top}H_{\phi}J_{\phi}\,\delta+O(\|\delta\|^{3}),

where
H_{\phi}
is the Hessian of the decoder. Averaging over
\hat{\delta}^{\star}
at adversarial radius

\epsilon
gives an expected loss whose first non-trivial Jacobian term is

\tfrac{\epsilon^{2}}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathrm{Tr}(J_{\phi}^{\top}H_{\phi}J_{\phi}\,\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{PGD}})]
with

\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{PGD}}=\mathrm{Cov}(\hat{\delta}^{\star})
— the
A_{7}
/ D7 estimator. PGD-AT therefore
implements matched
pmh
along
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{PGD}}
(Lemma
A.14
), with two caveats:

(i)
decoder weighting via
H_{\phi}
is not the proportional allocation of Theorem
4.1
(ii)
— the Cor.
3.4
dissociation (T7B:
-14.8
pp clean vs. baseline, worse
tdi
than PGD-delta
pmh
).

(ii)

\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{PGD}}
is low-rank; explicit
pmh
with a full PGD-delta estimator
(T7B matched arm) can match robustness with better clean accuracy.

Data augmentation is matched
pmh
along the augmentation delta Gram.

A label-preserving augmentation pipeline
\mathcal{A}=\{a_{1},\ldots,a_{K}\}
trains on

\mathcal{L}(\theta;a_{k}(x))
for
k\sim\mathrm{Unif}\{1,\ldots,K\}
. Define the augmentation delta

\delta_{k}=a_{k}(x)-x
and its mixture covariance

\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{aug}}=\frac{1}{K}\sum_{k}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\delta_{k}\delta_{k}^{\top}]
. A second-order Taylor
expansion gives the expected loss

\mathbb{E}_{x,k}[\mathcal{L}(\theta;a_{k}(x))]=\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathcal{L}(\theta;x)]+\tfrac{1}{2}\,\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathrm{Tr}(J_{\phi}^{\top}H_{\phi}J_{\phi}\,\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{aug}})]+O(\|\delta\|^{3}).

The leading regularisation term is matched
pmh
along
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{aug}}
(Lemma
A.10

Augmentation wins when test corruptions lie in

Takeaway.

CORAL, PGD-AT, and augmentation are not separate mechanisms once linearised; they are matched
pmh

along different
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{task}}^{(k)}

Intuition.

Theorem A asks two separate questions: (1) “Did you penalise the

\Sigma^{\prime}

6.2
Trajectory Deviation Index (primary, label-free)

Definition 6.1

(Trajectory Deviation Index)
.

Let
\phi^{(1:\ell)}
denote the representation after the first
\ell
layers of a depth-
L

encoder
\phi
. For perturbation strength
\sigma>0
and
\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
,
the
Trajectory Deviation Index
is

\mathrm{TDI}(\phi,\sigma)\;:=\;\frac{1}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x,\delta}\!\left[\big\|\phi^{(1:\ell)}(x+\delta)-\phi^{(1:\ell)}(x)\big\|_{2}^{2}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\big\|\phi^{(1:\ell)}(x)\big\|_{2}^{2}\right]}.

(5)

Lower is better: an encoder with small, layer-balanced displacement under isotropic input noise scores
low; an encoder that “wiggles” strongly along many directions at many depths scores high.

Clean-input limit.

We write
\mathrm{TDI@0}(\phi):=\lim_{\sigma\to 0^{+}}\mathrm{TDI}(\phi,\sigma)
and estimate it at

\sigma=0.01
in all experiments (well below training noise; Taylor remainder
O(\sigma^{2})
gives

<0.1\%
relative error at this scale). Layer-averaging is used for deep
ViTs and ResNets (Types 2, 4, 7B); a single final-layer probe is used when only one embedding is
available (e.g. CLS or graph readout).

Link to
\tilde{D}_{Q}
.

At small
\sigma
, expanding
\phi(x+\delta)-\phi(x)\approx J_{\phi}(x)\delta
in
Eq. (
5
) yields

\mathrm{TDI}(\phi,\sigma)\;\approx\;\frac{\sigma^{2}}{L}\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\frac{\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\|J_{\phi^{(1:\ell)}}(x)\|_{F}^{2}\right]}{\mathbb{E}_{x}\!\left[\|\phi^{(1:\ell)}(x)\|_{2}^{2}\right]}\;\propto\;\sigma^{2}\,\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi)\quad\text{(isotropic $\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}=\sigma_{\mathrm{task}}^{2}I$)},

(6)

so
\mathrm{TDI}
measures exactly the isotropic path-length distortion that Theorem
3.1

bounds, normalised by representation scale. It is a
post-hoc, label-free
probe complementary to
Eq. (
4
), not a substitute for accuracy or CKA.

6.3
Class-layout
\textnormal{{tdi}}_{0}
(labeled probe)

When class labels are available on a held-out probe set, we report a second statistic that does

not
require injecting input noise, but measures how tightly each class cluster sits relative
to between-class separation:

\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}(\phi)\;:=\;\frac{\overline{d}_{\mathrm{intra}}(\phi)}{\overline{d}_{\mathrm{inter}}(\phi)},

(7)

where
\overline{d}_{\mathrm{intra}}
is the mean pairwise distance among same-class embeddings and

\overline{d}_{\mathrm{inter}}
is the mean distance between class centroids (embeddings

L_{2}
-normalised; up to
200
samples per class). This is the probe used for T4A/T4B pixel-aligned
tables and for T7B
compute_tdi_cls

trajectory
tdi
in Type 2 replication code. Lower means tighter class structure relative to
separation.

Proposition 6.2

(Proposition F: one-sided layout envelope)
.

If the linearised deployment drift vanishes,
\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi)\to 0
, then

\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}(\phi)\;\leq\;\Phi\!\left(d_{\mathrm{between}}(\phi)\right),

where
d_{\mathrm{between}}
is mean inter-class centroid distance and

\Phi:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
is continuous and increasing, depending only on latent
class geometry, not on
Q_{n}

Proof sketch.
Zero drift implies
\phi(x+n)-\phi(x)=o(\|n\|)
for
n
in the
deployment subspace, so any layout statistic computed under

perturbation collapses to the clean
embedding geometry, which is bounded by inter-class separation via standard discriminant arguments.
Full proof in Appendix
A.10
.
One-sided only:
low
\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}

does not imply low
\tilde{D}_{Q}
(collapsed classes can score artificially low); we never use it as the
sole metric.

7.3
When the matching principle does not apply

Three structural limits to the framework

•

Linearisability.
The Jacobian linearisation requires
\|n\|
small relative to the
encoder’s second-order envelope. Catastrophic corruption and severe domain shift can break this;
nothing in the framework rules out a higher-order correction, and that is a natural next paper.

•

Identifiability.
Each Lemma D
k
has its own structural assumption
A_{k}
. When no

A_{k}
describes the deployment nuisance, the framework offers no estimator and is silent. In practice,

quality of
\hat{\Sigma}_{\mathrm{task}}
—not the penalty formula alone—determines whether matched
pmh

helps; run the eigengap pre-flight (§
5.1
) before committing compute.

•

Label preservation.

Q_{n}
must satisfy Definition
2.1

(
p(y\mid x+n)=p(y\mid x)
). When the shift is label-changing (Definition
2.1
,
second bullet),
\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}
is undefined; use causal / multi-environment methods instead.

These are honest scope conditions, not gaps in the proofs. They tell the practitioner exactly when
to reach for a different tool, and they are what makes the matching principle falsifiable rather than
unconditionally true.

10.1
What changes if the matching principle is correct

If the principle is correct.

Robustness reduces to one estimation problem: which
A_{k}
describes deployment, and how well Lemmas D1–D7
recover
\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}
.
CORAL, adversarial training, augmentation, and Jacobian penalties are estimators of one matrix, not
independent method families.
The loss becomes a design variable—one PSD matrix per nuisance, closed-form optimum
(Theorem
4.1
), and two falsification controls fixed in advance (Lemma
4.12
,
Corollaries
4.13
/
4.14

Wide eigengap
\Rightarrow
win; marginal gap
\Rightarrow
Office-31-type loss (Lemma D1).
Block T7A (§
8
) extends the same story to style nuisances at 7B; vision robustness is
block T7B.

Adjacent literatures.

Relative to the information bottleneck, matched
pmh
suppresses
\phi
along deployment
\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}

rather than all of
I(\phi;x)
. Relative to equivariance, it estimates a covariance when no clean group
action exists (photometry, accent, style). Relative to distributionally robust optimisation and
GroupDRO—which minimise worst-case risk over an
ambiguity set
of distributions—the matching
principle instead
estimates
the label-preserving nuisance law and penalises Jacobian motion along

\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
; the views align when perturbations are generated by that law, but differ when the
ambiguity set includes label-changing or mis-specified shifts (IRM/GroupDRO row,
Table
1
). Relative to interpretability, it prescribes pre hoc what the
encoder should be insensitive to.

Remark A.1

(Gaussian linear model)
.

s\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d_{s}})
,
n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d_{n}})
,
s\perp n
,

y=\langle w_{s},s\rangle+\rho\langle w_{n},n\rangle+\varepsilon
with
\varepsilon\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma_{\varepsilon}^{2})
,

\|w_{s}\|_{2}=\|w_{n}\|_{2}=1
,
\rho>0
, and
x=(s,n)
. Write
J_{\phi}=[J_{\phi,s}\mid J_{\phi,n}]
so

\|J_{\phi}\|_{F}^{2}=\|J_{\phi,s}\|_{F}^{2}+\|J_{\phi,n}\|_{F}^{2}
.

Lemma A.2

(Sub-block inequality)
.

For
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
and unit
v\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:
\|Av\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|A\|_{F}^{2}
.
For
A=[A_{1}\mid A_{2}]
:
\|A_{2}v\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|A_{2}\|_{F}^{2}\leq\|A\|_{F}^{2}
.

Proof.

Cauchy–Schwarz on
Av=\sum_{j}v_{j}Ae_{j}
with
\sum_{j}v_{j}^{2}=1
.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Linearised vs. exact drift)
.

If
\phi
has
\beta
-Lipschitz Jacobian and
\sup_{x}\|J_{\phi}(x)\|_{F}\leq M
, then for

\delta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
,

D_{Q}(\phi)=\mathbb{E}_{x,\delta}[\|\phi(x+\delta)-\phi(x)\|_{2}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\|J_{\phi}(x)\|_{F}^{2}]+R(\phi,\sigma)

with
|R(\phi,\sigma)|\leq C(M\beta d^{3/2}\sigma^{3}+\beta^{2}d^{2}\sigma^{4})
for a universal
C>0
.
In particular
D_{Q}\geq\tilde{D}_{Q}-O(\sigma^{4})
for small
\sigma
, and

D_{Q}(\phi,\sigma)\geq\tfrac{1}{2}\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi,\sigma)
once
\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}(M,\beta,d,\rho,L)
.

Proof.

Integral form of the mean-value expansion; the leading term is

\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi}\delta\|_{2}^{2}]=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}_{x}[\|J_{\phi}\|_{F}^{2}]
; higher orders are
O(\sigma^{3})
–
O(\sigma^{4})
.
∎

Lemma A.4

(ERM must encode the nuisance direction)
.

Under Remark
A.1
, any population MSE minimiser
f^{*}=h\circ\phi^{*}
satisfies

\mathbb{E}_{x}[\partial_{w_{n}}f^{*}(x)]=\rho
for
\partial_{w_{n}}:=\langle w_{n},\nabla_{n}\rangle
.
Hence
J_{\phi,n}
is non-zero on a set of positive measure.

Proof.

Bayes predictor
f^{*}(x)=\langle w_{s},s\rangle+\rho\langle w_{n},n\rangle
.
Stein’s identity for
n\sim\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d_{n}})
gives

\mathbb{E}[\partial_{w_{n}}f^{*}]=\mathbb{E}[f^{*}\cdot\langle w_{n},n\rangle]=\rho\|w_{n}\|_{2}^{2}=\rho
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

By Lemma
A.4
and the chain rule,

\rho=\mathbb{E}[\nabla_{\phi}h^{\top}J_{\phi,n}w_{n}]\leq L\,\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi,n}w_{n}\|_{2}]
.
Jensen gives
\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi,n}w_{n}\|_{2}^{2}]\geq\rho^{2}/L^{2}
.
Lemma
A.2
yields
\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi}\|_{F}^{2}]\geq\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi,n}w_{n}\|_{2}^{2}]\geq\rho^{2}/L^{2}
, hence

\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi^{*},\sigma)=\sigma^{2}\mathbb{E}[\|J_{\phi}\|_{F}^{2}]\geq\sigma^{2}\rho^{2}/L^{2}
.
Lemma
A.3
upgrades this to
D_{Q}\geq\sigma^{2}\rho^{2}/(2L^{2})
for
\sigma\leq\sigma_{0}
.

\square

Lemma A.5

(Bregman gap for strictly proper losses)
.

Let
\mathcal{L}
be strictly proper with Bregman generator
\psi
. For conditionals
p,q
on

\mathcal{Y}\mid x
:

\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p}[\mathcal{L}(q,y)]-\mathbb{E}_{y\sim p}[\mathcal{L}(p,y)]=d_{\psi}(p\|q)\geq 0,

with equality iff
p=q
a.s.

Proof.

Definition of strict properness: the expected loss is uniquely minimised at
q=p
.
∎

Lemma A.6

(Risk excess of an
n
-blind predictor)
.

Under correlated nuisance (direct influence:
p(y\mid x)\not\equiv p(y\mid s(x))

Proof.

Top-

singular vectors of

coincide with top-
r
eigenvectors of
\hat{C}
.

.
∎

A.10
Proposition F: class-layout
\textnormal{{tdi}}_{0}
envelope

Setting (Proposition
6.2
).

Labelled probe set
\{(x_{i},y_{i})\}_{i=1}^{N}
,
C\geq 2
; class-layout

\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}=\overline{d}_{\mathrm{intra}}/\overline{d}_{\mathrm{inter}}
as in
Eq. (
7
). This is a

, not a drift identity—trajectory

tdi
(Eq. (
5
)) is the primary label-free link to
Eq. (
3
).

Step 1 (local invariance).

If
\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi)=0
, then
\mathbb{E}_{x}[\mathrm{Tr}(J_{\phi}^{\top}J_{\phi}\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})]=0
, hence

J_{\phi}(x)^{\top}J_{\phi}(x)\,\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}}=0
for
P_{X}
-a.e.
x
on
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
. For

n=W\eta
with
W
spanning
\mathrm{range}(\Sigma_{\mathrm{task}})
,

\phi(x+n)-\phi(x)=J_{\phi}(x)n+o(\|n\|)=o(\|n\|)

to first order, so embeddings are locally invariant along deployment directions.

Step 2 (layout under noise reduces to clean layout).

Consider the idealised noisy embedding
\tilde{\phi}(x)=\phi(x+n)
with
n\sim Q_{n}
i.i.d. per
sample. To first order
\tilde{\phi}(x)\approx\phi(x)
, so within-class scatter and between-class
centroid separation of
\tilde{\phi}
agree with those of
\phi
up to
O(\mathbb{E}\|n\|)
. Thus any
layout functional
L(\phi)
that depends only on clean pairwise geometry satisfies

L(\tilde{\phi})=L(\phi)+O(\sqrt{\tilde{D}_{Q}(\phi)})
by Lipschitz continuity of centroids in finite
samples.

Step 3 (bound by
d_{\mathrm{between}}
).

For fixed
C
,
\overline{d}_{\mathrm{intra}}
is bounded by the diameter of the embedding set,
while
\overline{d}_{\mathrm{inter}}\geq d_{\mathrm{between}}:=\min_{c\neq c^{\prime}}\|\mu_{c}-\mu_{c^{\prime}}\|_{2}

for class means
\mu_{c}
. Hence

\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}\leq\mathrm{diam}/d_{\mathrm{between}}=:\Phi(d_{\mathrm{between}})

with
\Phi
continuous and increasing when class means are separated.
\square

Remark.

Low
\mathrm{TDI}_{0}^{\mathrm{cls}}
does not certify low
\tilde{D}_{Q}
, and the converse fails at
intermediate drift; do not substitute layout
tdi
for trajectory
tdi
in the recipe.
