Physical activity and sleep disturbances in individuals with alcohol use disorder: protocol of the APhysOH pilot study for a randomized controlled trial
Authors: Lilou Duquet, Anastasia Boitel, Silvio Galli, Karine Charriere, Frédéric Mauny, Emmanuel Haffen, Julie Giustiniani
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundPhysical activity has been shown to improve sleep and may serve as an effective adjunctive therapy in the treatment of alcohol use disorder (AUD). However, few studies have examined the impact of Adapted Physical Activity (APA) programs on sleep in individuals with AUD. This pilot randomized controlled trial (RCT), APhysOH, aims to assess the feasibility of an RCT evaluating the effects of an APA intervention on sleep, alcohol use, and mental and physical health outcomes in individuals with AUD.MethodsForty participants will be recruited and randomized to either an intervention group, referred to as the supervised group (n = 30), or a control group, referred to as the autonomous group (n = 10). The intervention consists of a 12-week APA program tailored to the abilities of patients with AUD and designed to improve sleep. It includes aerobic and resistance-based exercises delivered through three one-hour morning sessions per week. The control group will continue usual care and receive educational material on the benefits of physical activity for AUD and sleep. The primary objective of this study is to assess feasibility. Secondary objectives are to evaluate the impact of a supervised APA program on sleep, addiction severity, physical fitness, functional capacity, and mental health in patients with AUD. Both objective and subjective measures will be used, including biomarkers, actigraphy, physical performance tests, validated questionnaires, and diaries recording sleep, physical activity, and alcohol consumption.DiscussionFindings from this pilot RCT protocol will inform the design of future large-scale trials by evaluating the feasibility of this type of intervention. The study will also generate preliminary data to estimate the potential effects of APA on sleep, addiction severity, and physical and mental health in individuals with AUD.Trial registrationClinicalTrials.gov identifier: NCT06706349. Introduction

Worldwide, in 2019, approximately 2.6 million deaths were attributable to alcohol consumption according to the World Health Organization (WHO). The burden of alcohol-attributable mortality is concentrated among individuals with higher levels of consumption, with risk increasing sharply as intake rises [1]. Alcohol use disorder (AUD) is a global public health concern that affects psychological, physical, and social functioning [2]. Patients with AUD are individuals diagnosed with alcohol addiction [3]. In this population, sleep problems, such as insomnia, are overrepresented [4], and 79% of patients with AUD report poor sleep quality [5].

The detrimental effects of alcohol on sleep begin during acute consumption. Sleep onset latency (SOL) is reduced during the declining phase of blood alcohol concentration after alcohol consumption [6]. In some studies, this effect has been linked to the fact that one in ten individuals self-medicates sleep disturbances with alcohol [7]. Acute alcohol consumption induces multiple awakenings, particularly during the second half of the night, leading to reductions in total sleep time (TST) and sleep efficiency (SE) [4].

In cases of chronic alcohol consumption, the detrimental effects of alcohol on sleep are amplified. Chronic alcohol use disrupts sleep–wake cycles [8], increases sleep fragmentation and rapid eye movement (REM) sleep, and reduces slow-wave sleep (SWS) [4, 6], thereby reducing sleep quality. Alcohol use is also associated with circadian misalignment due to melatonin suppression and reduced responsiveness of circadian photoreceptors [9, 10]. During withdrawal, patients with AUD often experience increased SOL, alongside delayed melatonin onset and peak levels. Although partial recovery is possible shortly after withdrawal, some alterations may persist over time, including increased REM sleep, sleep fragmentation, wake after sleep onset (WASO), SOL, stage 1 sleep, and insomnia, along with reduced SWS, SE, and TST [4, 6].

Thus, alcohol impairs sleep, while poor sleep is itself a risk factor for chronic conditions, including addiction. Sleep disturbances are associated with an increased risk of relapse and may contribute to the maintenance and progression of AUD, including during abstinence [4, 6]. Specifically, sleep fragmentation, elevated REM sleep, and periodic limb movements are significant predictors of relapse [6]. Individuals who habitually use alcohol or hypnotic medications as sleep aids before admission to a residential alcohol cessation treatment program appear to be at increased risk of post-treatment relapse [11].

In patients with AUD, improving sleep may support recovery and promote abstinence from alcohol [6]. Cognitive behavioral therapy has been shown to improve insomnia and alcohol-related outcomes in individuals with heavy drinking [12] and AUD [13]. Another non-pharmacological intervention known to improve sleep in non-clinical populations is physical activity. Moreover, Adapted Physical Activity (APA) provides several additional health benefits, including improvements in comorbidities, craving, and mental health, and appears to be a feasible treatment that is well received by patients with AUD [14], with high adherence rates reported in previous studies [15, 16]. However, unlike cognitive behavioral therapy, the impact of physical activity on sleep has not yet been tested in this specific population. This warrants further investigation.

The positive effects of physical activity on sleep in the general population are well established. Acute exercise—defined as less than one week of aerobic and/or anaerobic activity—improves TST, SE, and SWS, while reducing SOL, REM sleep, WASO, and stage 1 sleep, when performed at least three hours before bedtime [17]. These immediate benefits may motivate individuals to engage in regular physical activity to improve sleep [17]. Regular exercise—defined as at least one week of aerobic and/or anaerobic activity—is associated with improvements in TST, SE, SOL, and overall sleep quality, as well as a reduced need for sleep medication [18], particularly among individuals with pre-existing sleep complaints [17, 18]. Physical activity also contributes to circadian clock regulation by modulating melatonin secretion depending on the time of day, light exposure, exercise intensity, and duration [10]. In addition, regular physical activity decreases cortisol levels, which are correlated with changes in TST [19]. Although the relationships between sleep and alcohol use, and the independent benefits of physical activity on both, are well established, evidence remains limited regarding the effects of physical activity on sleep in individuals with AUD. However, recent literature suggests potential sleep-related benefits of physical activity in this population [20].

Patients with AUD may be receptive to APA because it may be perceived as less stigmatizing than other interventions [14, 21]. Patients may also perceive broad benefits of physical activity as an adjunctive treatment, extending beyond sleep to include improvements in alcohol consumption, craving, abstinence, mental health, physical condition, and quality of life [22, 23]. Despite this, patients with AUD and insomnia are less likely to engage in physical activity than those without insomnia (21% vs. 70%) [15].

This pilot randomized controlled trial aims to assess the feasibility and estimate the effects of a supervised adapted physical activity program primarily on sleep outcomes, and secondarily on physical and mental health parameters, in patients with AUD. The protocol of this study is presented below in accordance with the SPIRIT 2022 checklist guidelines.

Methods

Study design and objectives

This study is a feasibility randomized controlled trial (RCT) with two parallel groups of individuals with AUD. The allocation ratio is 3:1, with three participants assigned to the intervention group for every one participant in the control group. This pilot RCT follows the CONSORT guidelines for randomized trials. The clinical trial is registered under the number NCT06706349 (registered on 2 June 2024; last updated on 26 November 2024) and is sponsored by CHU Besançon, France. All data are collected at a single research site. Final approval from the relevant ethics committees was obtained in September 2024.

The primary objective of this study is to assess the feasibility of conducting an RCT exploring the impact of physical activity on sleep in patients with AUD. The secondary objective is to evaluate the effects of a supervised physical activity program on sleep, addiction severity, physical fitness, functional capacity, and mental health in this population.

Study setting

The study is conducted at the Day Hospital for Addictology (DAH) of the University Hospital of Besançon (CHU Besançon, France), an outpatient unit. Recruitment began in October 2024 and is expected to continue for 24 months, ending in October 2026. The total study duration is three years, with completion anticipated in April 2027, as illustrated in Fig. 1.

Recruitment procedures

Participants are recruited through multiple channels. Articles about the study are published in the local press. Information is also disseminated via the hospital website and social media, and posters and flyers are displayed in several hospital departments. As part of routine care activities, the DAH informs its partners (e.g., general practitioners, addiction support services) about the study. Finally, the study is presented at local meetings involving regional stakeholders in addictology.

Sample size

A total of 40 participants with AUD, ongoing alcohol consumption, and sleep complaints will be recruited, in accordance with recommendations for pilot and feasibility studies [24]. Participants will be randomized in a 3:1 ratio: 30 in the intervention group and 10 in the control group. This allocation balances the need for reliable estimates while accounting for limited recruitment capacity, in line with CONSORT 2010 guidelines [25].

The control group will serve as an internal reference to descriptively estimate (via 95% confidence intervals, without formal statistical testing) the magnitude of differences between groups. This approach provides a more relevant estimate of the potential effect of APA for designing a future superiority trial than relying solely on external data, and is not affected by unequal group sizes.

This sample size is sufficient to generate hypotheses for future trials. If the results are promising, the findings will support the design of a larger randomized trial evaluating the effectiveness of APA. The proposed sample size of 40 participants is justified, as 30 subjects in the intervention group is considered adequate to establish feasibility in quantitative studies [26], while a control group of 10 aligns with established rules of thumb for pilot trial control arms [27].

Intervention

Supervised group (intervention)

All participants receive usual care (e.g., psychotherapy, and medication if stabilized and not contraindicated). Participants randomized to the supervised group will additionally receive a supervised APA program.

Prior to starting the APA program, a motivational interview is conducted with each participant by an APA instructor. The aim of this interview is to identify participants’ preferences, needs, capacities, and limitations. Based on this information, physical activity sessions are tailored to individual characteristics to enhance adherence.

The APA program was developed in accordance with national guidelines and scientific evidence regarding the effects of exercise on sleep, while also taking into account population-specific characteristics. It consists of a 12-week program including moderate- to high-intensity sessions combining aerobic and anaerobic exercise. Three group-based sessions per week are scheduled.

All sessions are supervised by an APA instructor, take place from 9:00 to 10:00 a.m., and include a maximum of six participants per session. All participants arrive at the same time. Using a progressive approach, participants are integrated into the group, and sessions are individually adapted according to functional capacity and therapeutic needs. Peer support and social interaction are actively encouraged.

Autonomous group (control)

In this study, the control condition—referred to as the “autonomous group”—is designed as a minimal-level usual care intervention. Participants already engaged in addiction treatment will continue their usual care, while those not yet in care will receive standard treatment initiation.

All participants in the control group will also receive standardized verbal and written recommendations regarding physical activity. This design enhances external validity by reflecting routine clinical practice and maintains ethical standards by ensuring that participants are not deprived of basic health advice, while preserving the integrity of an active control condition through standardized messaging [32].

Assignment of interventions

Sequence generation and allocation concealment

Randomization is performed at inclusion using CleanWeb®, based on a pre-established allocation sequence generated by a data manager. The allocation is concealed from the investigators and assigns participants randomly in a 1:3 control-to-intervention ratio. Investigators are responsible for enrolling participants and assigning them to groups.

Blinding

After randomization, neurologists and nurses from the sleep unit responsible for sleep assessments are blinded to group allocation. Investigators, who inform participants of their group assignment, and the APA instructor conducting the sessions are not blinded. Participants themselves cannot be blinded due to the nature of the intervention (physical activity).

Secondary outcomes

Sleep

Sleep outcomes aim to quantify the effects of the supervised APA program on both objective and subjective sleep parameters in individuals with AUD and sleep complaints. Another objective is to identify the most sensitive psychometric sleep scales for detecting changes associated with the intervention.

Measurements

Objective sleep is assessed before (T1) and after (T3) the intervention using validated tools and circadian rhythm analyses. These include actigraphy [33] and measurement of 6-sulfatoxymelatonin concentrations [34].

Participants wear an actimeter (MotionWatch 8, CamNtech, class I CE medical device) for 14 consecutive nights at T1 and T3. This device measures: Sleep duration (SD); Sleep efficiency (SE); Sleep onset latency (SOL); Wake after sleep onset (WASO); Fragmentation index (FI); Non-parametric circadian rhythm analysis (NPCRA) markers. Data collection procedures

Data are initially recorded on paper case report forms (CRFs) and subsequently transcribed into electronic CRFs (eCRFs) using CleanWeb®. As shown in Table 3, data are collected using a range of validated tools assessing all study outcomes at multiple time points (before, during, and after the intervention). Data collection involves several professionals, including investigators, nurses, APA instructors, neurologists, and psychiatrists.

Data management

Data quality control includes verification of 20% of CRFs selected at random by an independent operator throughout the study to ensure transcription accuracy.

All data are stored on secure, dedicated servers with restricted access. After database lock, only the data manager will have access to the finalized dataset. Other authorized personnel involved in data analysis will be granted read-only access.

Monitoring

The study will be monitored by a Clinical Research Associate appointed by the sponsor, independently of the investigators, in accordance with CHU Besançon procedures and applicable regulations. Monitoring visits will include review of investigator files and informed consent forms.

No independent Data Monitoring Committee has been established due to the single-center design and the low-risk nature of the study.

The sponsor and principal investigator reserve the right to suspend or terminate the trial prematurely for medical or administrative reasons, upon mutual agreement. Reasons for early termination will be documented and reported to the relevant authorities. Participants may withdraw their consent at any time without consequences.

Additional stopping criteria include technical issues preventing study continuation, participant inability to complete study procedures, death, loss to follow-up, or failure to meet predefined feasibility thresholds (e.g., insufficient recruitment or excessive dropout rates).

Harms

In accordance with French Jardé law (L.1123–10), all adverse events occurring after informed consent must be reported in compliance with regulatory requirements. This includes events related to medication, medical devices, or care procedures. Investigators will report adverse events through the ENNOV system, specifying trial participation. Any new safety information must be promptly communicated to the local vigilance unit.

Auditing

The study may be audited by a Clinical Research Associate mandated by the sponsor, independently of the investigators, in accordance with CHU Besançon procedures and applicable regulations. Audits will include review of investigator files and consent documentation.

Statistical analysis

Statistical analyses will be primarily descriptive. The study aims to describe feasibility outcomes and to estimate differences between the APA and autonomous groups for secondary outcomes. Measures of central tendency and dispersion will be reported. For quantitative variables, these include the mean, median, first and third quartiles, minimum and maximum values, range, variance, standard deviation, and coefficient of variation. For qualitative variables, absolute and relative frequencies will be presented. Data will also be displayed graphically using histograms, pie charts, and bar charts.

As a feasibility study, this trial is not powered to detect treatment effects. Differences between groups will be reported descriptively with 95% confidence intervals, without formal hypothesis testing. These preliminary estimates, based on a small sample, are intended solely to inform the design of future adequately powered trials and should not be interpreted as definitive effect estimates.

Adherence to the intervention and follow-up will be described over time.

Discussion

Limitations

This pilot randomized controlled trial evaluates the feasibility of assessing the effects of physical activity on sleep in individuals with AUD. A major challenge is that this population may experience difficulties adhering to care. Indeed, studies report that only 21% of patients with AUD consistently adhere to prescribed medications [40]. Although adherence to supervised physical activity programs has been reported to range from 70% to 78% [15, 16], it may drop to 21% among patients with both AUD and insomnia [15]. Therefore, adherence and attendance represent key feasibility concerns, highlighting the importance of feasibility outcomes to inform future RCTs.

Another limitation is the small sample size, which requires confirmation of the effects of physical activity on sleep in larger studies. In addition, although polysomnography would provide a more detailed assessment of sleep architecture, its requirement for overnight hospitalization may reduce acceptability in this population.

Furthermore, changes in alcohol consumption during the intervention could confound the effects of physical activity on sleep. However, sleep disturbances associated with chronic alcohol use are known to persist for months or even years after cessation [4, 6]. Future adequately powered studies should investigate whether changes in alcohol use moderate the effects of physical activity on sleep outcomes.

Finally, substantial interindividual variability is expected. Future research may benefit from incorporating n-of-1 designs or complementary qualitative approaches to better characterize within-person effects of physical activity on sleep and related health outcomes [41].

Conclusion

This pilot RCT addresses a gap in the literature by evaluating an intervention targeting both sleep and addiction-related outcomes. The findings will inform the design of future randomized controlled trials by providing insights into feasibility, protocol implementation, assessment strategies, and intervention delivery.

In addition, this study will generate preliminary data on the potential effects of physical activity on sleep, substance use, physical health, and mental well-being in individuals with AUD. Future studies should build on these findings to design adequately powered trials, explore the effects of physical activity across different stages of AUD, and refine exercise prescriptions by comparing different intervention modalities.

Declarations

Ethics approval and consent to participate

This study includes an intervention specific to the research, thus it falls within the scope of research involving the human person. It involves only minimal risks and constraints. It was approved by Ethical Review Board: Comité de Protection des Personnes Sud Méditerranée ID-RCB 2024-A00754-43 on 02/06/2024. Informed consent will be obtained by investigators authorized in the study and listed on the delegation of tasks log. Only competent adult participants capable of making decisions for themselves will be included. Consent will be collected in person during a consultation. No additional consent provisions for ancillary studies or use of biological specimens are applicable.

Consent for publication and dissemination policy

Manuscript does not contain any individual person’s data in any form. Trial results will be published in scientific journals or included in the final study report. Authorship eligibility will follow international guidelines (ICMJE). Members of the Clinical Investigation Center (CIC1431) involved in protocol design, data analysis, and manuscript preparation will be included as authors according to standard authorship criteria. Participants may request access to study results.

Declaration of generative AI and AI-assisted technologies in the writing process

During the preparation of this work, the authors used ChatGPT solely to improve the readability of the manuscript, as English is not their native language. After using this tool, the authors carefully reviewed and edited the content and take full responsibility for the final version of the manuscript.

Protocol amendments

This is a single-center study with a clear responsibility matrix defining communication roles. The Principal Investigator (PI) requests the modification submission, which is prepared and followed up by the project manager. After positive feedback from the Ethics Committee (CPP) is received by the Research Department (DRCI), the PI, project manager, and all relevant parties are promptly informed.

Confidentiality

The Sponsor is the data controller and commits to collecting only data strictly necessary for the research objectives. Data are pseudonymized using a unique code, excluding direct identifiers. Only the investigator and authorized staff can link codes to participant identities. A responsibility policy ensures data security and confidentiality, compliant with applicable regulations (including MR001 and GDPR). Participants are informed about data use, their rights, and data retention periods. Data collection sources are the participants themselves, their legal representatives, and involved professionals.
