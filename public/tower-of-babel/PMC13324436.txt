When Pneumonia is not Pneumonia: Pulmonary Venous Thrombosis After Scimitar Syndrome Repair
Authors: María Rodil Riera, Claudia Mañana Valdés
Published: 2026

A 39-year-old man with a history of surgically corrected scimitar syndrome 3 months earlier by intracardiac baffle repair of partial anomalous pulmonary venous return (PAPVR), anticoagulated with acenocoumarol, presented with pleuritic chest pain, cough, mild dyspnea, and self-limiting hemoptysis. Chest radiograph on admission showed right lower lung consolidation with air bronchogram, initially suggestive of community-acquired pneumonia (CAP) (Fig. 1A). Despite the absence of fever or laboratory evidence of infection, the patient's symptoms led to an initial diagnosis of CAP, and empirical antibiotic therapy with ceftriaxone, azithromycin, and linezolid (the latter added to cover possible endocarditis in the context of recent cardiac surgery) was initiated. However, due to persistent hemoptysis and recent surgery, chest computed tomography (CT) revealed extensive thrombosis of the right pulmonary venous drainage extending to the intracardiac shunt (Fig. 1B), along with centrilobular consolidations and ground-glass opacities consistent with diffuse alveolar hemorrhage (Fig. 1C). Acenocoumarol was discontinued, and anticoagulation was initiated with unfractionated heparin followed by enoxaparin guided by anti-Xa levels, with good clinical progress. Pulmonary venous thrombosis is a recognized complication after scimitar syndrome repair and should be considered in patients with pneumonia-like findings.
