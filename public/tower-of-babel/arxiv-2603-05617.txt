NotAI.AI : Explainable Detection of Machine-Generated Text via Curvature and Feature Attribution
Oleksandr Marchenko Breneur, Adelaide Danilov, Aria Nourbakhsh, Salima Lamsiyah

Abstract
We present NotAI.AI , an explainable framework for machine-generated text detection that extends Fast-DetectGPT by integrating curvature-based signals with neural and stylometric features in a supervised setting. The system combines 17 interpretable features, including Conditional Probability Curvature, ModernBERT detector score, readability metrics, and stylometric cues, within a gradient-boosted tree (XGBoost) meta-classifier to determine whether a text is human- or AI-generated. Furthermore, NotAI.AI applies Shapley Additive Explanations (SHAP) to provide both local and global feature-level attribution. These attributions are further translated into structured natural-language rationales through an LLM-based explanation layer, which enables user-facing interpretability. The system is deployed as an interactive web application that supports real-time analysis, visual feature inspection, and structured evidence presentation. A web interface allows users to input text and inspect how neural and statistical signals influence the final decision. Related Work

Detection Paradigms for AI-Generated Text.

Recent advances in LLMs have led to extensive research on machine-generated text detection
24
. Early approaches exploited surface-level statistical artifacts such as token likelihood ranks, burstiness, and repetition. Tools like GLTR visualized token-level probability information to assist human judgment
11
, and empirical analyses showed that while humans are often fooled by fluent generations, automatic detectors can still rely on distributional irregularities
13
. A complementary line of work introduced likelihood-based criteria that probe the model’s probability geometry: DetectGPT measures curvature under perturbations
19
, and Fast-DetectGPT improves efficiency via conditional probability curvature estimation
2
. In parallel, supervised neural detectors fine-tune Transformer encoders such as RoBERTa
17
and are evaluated across multi-domain and multi-generator benchmarks
22
. However, large-scale robustness studies reveal substantial degradation under distribution shift and adversarial settings
9
. Watermarking approaches embed detectable signals during generation
16
;
14
, but their applicability depends on generator-side cooperation and resilience to paraphrasing or post-editing.

Interpretability and User-Facing Detection Systems.

Beyond detection accuracy, recent research emphasizes interpretability, fine-grained analysis, and user-facing systems. Visualization-driven and tool-oriented frameworks demonstrate the importance of exposing evidence rather than returning only a probability score
11
;
1
. Feature-level analyses further investigate which latent or stylistic patterns distinguish human and machine text
15
, while emerging scenarios such as human-AI co-authorship require more nuanced attribution mechanisms
21
. Despite these advances, many systems either focus on a single class of signals (statistical, neural, or watermark-based) or provide limited transparency into their decisions. Our demo builds on curvature-based detection
19
;
2
and supervised neural encoders, and integrates heterogeneous statistical and neural features with explicit feature attribution and an interactive interface, aiming to unify robustness, interpretability, and practical usability in a single, user-centered system.

3.1
Feature Extraction: Neural + Statistical + Stylometric Signals

We extract 17 complementary and interpretable features capturing distributional, structural, and stylistic properties of text.

Neural Encoder.

Neural encoder-based features provide contextual likelihood signals that capture generation priors and semantic fluency patterns beyond surface-level statistics. We chose a fine-tuned ModernBERT
3
3

3

Model:
GeorgeDrayson/modernbert-ai-detection

23
;
8
for extracting neural detection probabilities. The resulting neural detection probability (
bert_ai_score
) is incorporated as a feature and combined with conditional probability curvature and stylometric signals within the Boosted Tree meta-classifier.

Conditional Probability Curvature (CPC).

We integrate CPC from Fast-DetectGPT
2
, which quantifies second-order variation in token likelihood under local perturbations. Formally, the CPC captures how sharply the log-probability distribution changes around a given text. Human-written text tends to exhibit more irregular and higher-variance probability landscapes, whereas machine-generated text often produces smoother and more stable likelihood profiles.

Readability and Lexical Diversity.

We compute interpretable linguistic indicators, including Flesch Reading Ease, sentencDetectione count, average sentence length, token count, average word length, Type-Token Ratio (lexical diversity), and the hapax legomena ratio (proportion of words appearing only once). These features capture structural regularity, vocabulary richness, and stylistic variability.

Surface and Stylometric Cues.

We further incorporate stopword ratio, cliché ratio, maximum repeated
n
-gram frequency (repetitiveness), overall punctuation count, comma count, period count, and semicolon/colon count. These features model repetition patterns, lexical redundancy, syntactic pacing, and stylistic punctuation behavior, which frequently differ between human and AI-generated text.

3.2
Meta-Classification

All extracted features are aggregated and fed into an Extreme Gradient Boosting (XGBoost) classifier
6
, which learns nonlinear interactions between neural confidence scores, curvature statistics, and stylometric indicators.

3.3
Explainability: From Feature Attribution to User-Facing Rationale

To provide explanations that are both technically grounded and accessible to end users, we combine feature-level attribution with structured natural-language rationale generation.

SHAP-Based Feature Attribution.

We apply SHAP
18
to quantify the contribution of each feature to the Boosted Tree decision. Positive SHAP values indicate evidence toward the “AI” label, while negative values support the “Human” label. SHAP provides
local interpretability
by explaining individual predictions through feature-level attribution, and
global interpretability
by aggregating attribution scores across the dataset to reveal overall feature importance and systematic model behavior.

However, although SHAP is mathematically rigorous, it may be difficult for non-expert users to interpret directly.

LLM-Based Structured Explanation.

To bridge this gap, we introduce a structured LLM explainer that converts feature attributions into concise, user-friendly explanations. In our case we use Google
gemma-3-27b-it
LLM
12
.

The explainer receives structured JSON input containing:
raw_text
,
label
,
probability_ai
,
features_positive
,
features_negative
. It is prompted as follows:

You are an explainer of the AI detector output. Given the provided context, explain why the model produced its decision. The detector aggregates multiple interpretable features using a meta-classifier. You will receive feature values and their importance scores.

Task:
1. Explain in 1-2 sentences per feature how the values support the final prediction.
2. Keep explanations concise, non-technical, and strictly grounded in the supplied values.

The model returns a structured JSON:

{
"top_ai_evidence": {...},
"top_human_evidence": {...},
"summary": "Plain-language rationale"
}

This two-layer explanation mechanism (SHAP + LLM summarization) enables both technical transparency and accessible reasoning.

Experimental Results

4.1
Evaluation Dataset

Balancing the RAID dataset.

The original RAID dataset
9
is highly imbalanced in its
non-adversarial
subset. After removing all adversarially perturbed instances (i.e., retaining only samples with no applied attack), the resulting split contains approximately
2.86\%
human-written text and
97.14\%
AI-generated text. Such skew can cause supervised detectors to overfit the majority class, demonstrating high accuracy while performing poorly on the minority (human) class.
To obtain a more reliable evaluation, we construct a balanced set with a
1{:}1
human/AI class ratio. We keep
all
human-written instances and downsample the AI-generated portion in a stratified manner to prevent bias toward any single generator model.
This procedure ensures an approximately balanced human/AI split overall with equal representation across generators. Sampling is performed without replacement using a fixed seed (
random_state
=42) to ensure reproducibility. The final balanced dataset is formed by concatenating the full human subset with the per-generator sampled AI subsets and resetting indices.

The effect of the
CPC
on the decision (Fig.

5
Conclusion

We present
NotAI.AI
, an ensemble AI-text detection framework that combines statistical and neural detector signals within a single meta-classifier, and pairs this predictor with a natural, user-facing explainability layer. By using SHAP-based feature attributions and translating them into understandable rationales inside an interactive demo, our work addresses not only accurate classification, but also transparent, inspectable decision-making that is better aligned with real-world use by non-experts.

6
Limitations and Future Work

Although
NotAI.AI
achieves strong performance on a balanced subset of RAID
9
, robustness under severe domain shifts, unseen generators, or adversarial paraphrasing remains a limitation common to supervised detectors. Broader cross-domain and attack-aware evaluation is, therefore, an important next step.
Moreover, SHAP
18
provides principled feature-level attribution; however, its explanations operate at a macro level and may remain abstract for non-expert users. Future work will extend interpretability toward micro-level localization of influential words, phrases, and structural patterns, potentially through interactive token-level heatmaps integrated into the user interface.
Further directions include robustness enhancement, uncertainty calibration, and extension to multilingual and human–AI co-authored scenarios.
