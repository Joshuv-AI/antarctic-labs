Shuffle and Joint Differential Privacy for Generalized Linear Contextual Bandits
Sahasrajit Sarmasarkar

Abstract
We present the first algorithms for generalized linear contextual bandits under shuffle differential privacy and joint differential privacy. While prior work on private contextual bandits has been restricted to linear reward models—which admit closed-form estimators—generalized linear models (GLMs) pose fundamental new challenges: no closed-form estimator exists, requiring private convex optimization; privacy must be tracked across multiple evolving design matrices; and optimization error must be explicitly incorporated into regret analysis. We address these challenges under two privacy models and context settings. For stochastic contexts, we design a shuffle-DP algorithm achieving O ~ ​ ( d 3 / 2 ​ T / ε ) \tilde{O}(d^{3/2}\sqrt{T}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}) regret. For adversarial contexts, we provide a joint-DP algorithm with O ~ ​ ( d ​ T / ε ) \tilde{O}(d\sqrt{T}/\sqrt{\varepsilon}) regret—matching the non-private rate up to a 1 / ε 1/\sqrt{\varepsilon} factor. Both algorithms remove dependence on the instance-specific parameter κ \kappa (which can be exponential in dimension) from the dominant T \sqrt{T} term. Unlike prior work on locally private GLM bandits, our methods require no spectral assumptions on the context distribution beyond ℓ 2 \ell_{2} boundedness.

1.1
Technical Overview

Extending private contextual bandits from linear to generalized linear models introduces fundamental obstacles that require new techniques beyond combining existing tools. We outline the three main challenges and our solutions.

Challenge 1: No closed-form estimator.

In linear bandits, the ridge estimator
\widehat{\theta}=(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}+\lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{r}
is computed in closed form, so privacy is achieved by adding noise directly to the sufficient statistics
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{r}
. For GLMs, no such closed-form solution exists; the maximum likelihood estimator requires iterative convex optimization. This introduces two complications: (i) each optimization call consumes privacy budget, and (ii) the optimization error must be explicitly incorporated into the confidence bound analysis.

We address this by integrating the shuffle-private convex optimizer of
[
14
]
into our bandit estimation subroutine. A key observation is that optimization error
\nu
contributes only
O(\sqrt{\nu})
to the confidence radius (Lemma
E.4
), allowing us to balance the privacy-utility tradeoff by controlling the number of optimizer iterations.

Challenge 2: Non-monotone design matrices under continual release.

Both algorithms must release design matrices (
\mathbf{V}
for exploration,
\mathbf{H}_{k}
for exploitation) at multiple time points. In the stochastic-context setting (M1), batching resolves this: we release one matrix per batch using shuffled vector summation. In the adversarial-context setting (M2), decisions must be made every round, requiring the binary-tree mechanism
[
12
,
18
]
for continual release. However, the noise added by this mechanism makes the design matrices non-monotone over time, breaking the standard rarely-switching argument
[
1
]
that bounds the number of policy updates.

We introduce a modified switching criterion that compares against the
running maximum
determinant observed so far, rather than the current determinant. Lemma
O.4
proves this modification preserves both the
O(d\log T)
bound on the number of switches and the validity of confidence sets under the noisy matrices.

Challenge 3: Privacy leakage through policy-switching times.

In Algorithm
2
, the decision to trigger an exploration switch at round
t
depends on the context set
\mathcal{X}_{t}
and the current design matrix
\mathbf{V}_{t}
, which itself depends on all prior switching decisions. Thus, the index set
\mathcal{T}_{o}
of exploration rounds is data-dependent and could leak information about other users’ context-reward pairs. This issue does not arise in prior linear bandit work, where switches are either deterministic (based on time) or the estimator update mechanism differs fundamentally.

We prove that
\mathcal{T}_{o}\setminus\{t\}
is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-indistinguishable across any two datasets differing only at time
t
. The proof (Lemma
I.5
) decomposes the log-likelihood ratio across the binary tree structure: only nodes whose subtrees contain the differing index contribute, and there are at most
\log T
such nodes. Applying composition for Gaussian mechanisms yields the desired privacy bound. This likelihood-ratio analysis for policy switches, inspired by techniques from
[
33
,
52
]
, is new to the bandit literature.

Removing
\kappa
from the leading regret term.

A key feature of our bounds is that the instance-dependent parameter
\kappa
—which can be exponential in the problem dimension for logistic regression—does not appear in the dominant
\sqrt{T}
term. This matches the non-private guarantees of
[
45
]
. We achieve this through a two-phase approach: an initial exploration phase (
\mathcal{T}_{o}
rounds in Algorithm
2
, batch
\mathcal{B}_{1}
in Algorithm
1
) builds a coarse estimate with
\kappa
-dependent regret, while subsequent exploitation uses scaled design matrices that absorb the
\kappa
factor into the confidence width rather than the regret accumulation. We experimentally show this phenomenon in
Section

8
with additional experiments in
Appendix

A

Related Work

Non-private GLM bandits.

Generalized linear contextual bandits have been studied extensively in the non-private setting
[
24
,
38
,
45
]
. The key challenge is that, unlike linear models, GLMs lack closed-form estimators for
\widehat{\theta}
, requiring iterative optimization.
[
45
]
recently achieved order-optimal regret with limited adaptivity, which inspires our algorithmic framework.

Private linear bandits.

Differential privacy in linear contextual bandits has been studied under various trust models. Under joint DP,
[
46
]
first achieved sublinear regret using tree-based mechanisms;
[
4
,
5
]
improved bounds under relaxed assumptions. Under shuffle DP,
[
16
,
25
]
obtained amplified privacy guarantees. All these works exploit closed-form ridge estimators for linear models.

Private GLM bandits.

The only prior work on private GLM bandits is
[
29
]
, which studies local DP under restrictive spectral assumptions on the context distribution. To our knowledge, no prior work addresses shuffle or joint DP for GLM bandits. Our work fills this gap, achieving
\widetilde{O}(\sqrt{T/{\epsilon}})
regret under both privacy models with only
\ell_{2}
boundedness assumptions.

Comparison with prior techniques.

We highlight the key technical differences from prior private bandit algorithms that necessitate our new techniques:

1.

[
46
,
4
]
study joint-DP linear bandits using tree-based mechanisms for the ridge estimator. Their analysis exploits the closed-form solution
\widehat{\theta}=(\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}+\lambda\mathbf{I})^{-1}\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{r}
, where adding noise to
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{X}
and
\mathbf{X}^{\top}\mathbf{r}
suffices. For GLMs, we must instead privatize the optimization process itself.

2.

[
45
]
provide the non-private baseline we build upon. Their key insight—using policy switches based on design matrix determinants to remove
\kappa
from regret—does not directly transfer to the private setting because (a) the switch times leak information, and (b) noisy matrices are non-monotone.

See Appendix
C
for extended discussion of related work on privacy amplification by shuffling and limited adaptivity in bandits.

Privacy Models

We consider two privacy models for contextual bandits, depending on the context generation process.

Shuffle DP (stochastic contexts).

In the shuffle model
[
20
,
15
]
, each user applies a local randomizer to their data and sends noisy messages to a trusted shuffler, which randomly permutes all messages before forwarding them to the learner. This amplifies privacy: even weak local randomization yields strong central-DP-level guarantees after shuffling. We adopt the multi-round shuffle protocol of
[
14
,
16
]
, where users participate across multiple batches.

Joint DP (adversarial contexts).

Joint DP
[
34
,
46
]
is a relaxation appropriate when contexts are adversarial. It requires that the action
a_{t}
recommended at time
t
be differentially private with respect to the context-reward pairs
(\mathcal{X}_{s},r_{s})
at all other times
s\neq t
. This avoids the impossibility result of
[
46
]
, which shows that standard central DP incurs linear regret in contextual bandits.

Formal definitions of local DP, shuffle DP, and joint DP appear in Appendix
B
.

Definition 4.1
.

A generalized linear model (GLM) with parameter vector
\theta^{*}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a real valued random variable
r
that belongs to the exponential family of distributions with density function

P(r\mid x)=\exp(r \cdot\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle- b\left(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle\right) + c(r)).

Function
b(\cdot)
(called the log-partition function) is assumed to be twice differentiable and
\dot{b}
is assumed to be monotone. Further, we assume
r\in[0,R]
and
\|\theta^{*}\|\leq S
.

Standard properties of GLMs include
\mathbb{E}[r]=\dot{b}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)
and
\mathrm{Var}(r)=\ddot{b}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)
. We define the link function as
\mu(z):=\dot{b}(z)
. This formulation is standard in the contextual bandit literature
[
24
,
45
]
.

Next we describe the two contextual bandit problems with GLM rewards that we address in this paper. Let
T\in{\mathbb{N}}
be the total number of rounds. At each round
t\in[T]
, we receive an arm set
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
K=|{\mathcal{X}}_{t}|
arms, and must select an arm
x_{t}\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
. Following this selection, we receive a reward
r_{t}
sampled from the GLM distribution
P(r|x)
with unknown parameter
\theta^{*}
.

Problem 1 (
M1
).

In this setting, we assume that at each round
t
, the context set
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is drawn from an unknown distribution
{\mathcal{D}}
. The algorithm decides the lengths of each batch a priori, where the policy remains fixed within each batch and updates only at the start of each batch. We study
({\epsilon},\delta)
-shuffle differential privacy in this setup. The goal is to minimize the expected cumulative regret:

{}R_{T}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}\mu(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mu(\langle x_{t},\theta^{*}\rangle)\right],

(1)

where the expectation is over the randomness of the algorithm, the distribution of rewards
r_{t}
, and the distribution of arm sets
{\mathcal{D}}
.

Problem 2 (
M2
).

In this setting, we make no assumptions on the context sets
{\mathcal{X}}_{t}
—they may be chosen adversarially. The goal is to minimize the cumulative regret while satisfying
({\epsilon},\delta)
-joint differential privacy:

{}R_{T}=\sum_{t=1}^{T}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}\mu(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)-\sum_{t=1}^{T}\mu(\langle x_{t},\theta^{*}\rangle).

(2)

For both problems, the Maximum Likelihood Estimator (MLE) of
\theta^{*}
is computed by minimizing the sum of log-losses. The log-loss for arm
x
, reward
r
, and parameter estimate
\theta
is
\ell(\theta,x,r):=-r\cdot\langle x,\theta\rangle+\int_{0}^{\langle x,\theta\rangle}\mu(z)\,dz
. After
t
rounds, the regularized MLE is
\widehat{\theta}=\argmin_{\theta}\left(\sum_{s=1}^{t}\ell(\theta,x_{s},r_{s})+\frac{\lambda}{2}\|\theta\|^{2}\right)
.

4.1
Instance-dependent non-linear parameters

Following
[

22

can be exponentially large in

\kappa=\Theta(e^{S})
in logistic regression. For any arm set
{\mathcal{X}}

denote the optimal arm. In setup

\displaystyle{}\kappa:=\max_{{\mathcal{X}}\in\supp({\mathcal{D}})}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}\frac{1}{\dot{\mu}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)},\quad

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hat{\kappa}}=\mathbb{E}_{{\mathcal{X}}\sim{\mathcal{D}}}\left[\dot{\mu}(\langle x^{*},\theta^{*}\rangle)\right],

\displaystyle\hskip-60.00009pt1/\kappa^{*}:=\max_{{\mathcal{X}}\in\supp({\mathcal{D}})}\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}{\dot{\mu}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)}.

(3)

In setup

{}\kappa:=\max_{x\in\cup_{t\in[T]}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}\frac{1}{\dot{\mu}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)},\quad 1/\kappa^{*}=\max_{x\in\cup_{t\in[T]}{\mathcal{X}}_{t}}{\dot{\mu}(\langle x,\theta^{*}\rangle)}.

(4)

Note that
\hat{\kappa}\geq\kappa^{*}
. Similar to the non-private bounds in

]
, our regret bounds have no

dependence in the dominant

term. In practice, it suffices to have access to an upper bound on

and lower bounds on

and

Assumption 4.2

(Boundedness)
.

The reward
r_{t}
at each time step

is bounded by a known constant

. Every context vector has

, i.e.,

(

For any subset

\displaystyle\max_{x\in{\mathcal{X}}}x^{\top}\left({\epsilon}{\bm{I}}+\mathbb{E}_{y\sim{\mathcal{K}}_{\mathcal{X}}}yy^{\top}\right)^{-1}x\leq d.

Shuffle-DP GLM Bandits with Stochastic Contexts

We present our algorithm for the stochastic-context setting (
M1
). The algorithm partitions the horizon into batches, uses
G
-optimal design for arm selection, and privately aggregates statistics via the shuffle model.

Our design adapts the batched framework of
[
45
]
, with three key modifications for privacy:

•

Private covariance estimation.
Design matrices
{\bm{\mathsfit{V}}}
and
{\bm{\mathsfit{H}}}_{k}
are computed via shuffled vector summation
[
14
]
, which aggregates noisy outer products across users.

•

Private parameter estimation.
The parameter estimate
\hat{\theta}_{k}
at each batch is computed using a shuffle-private convex optimizer on the regularized negative log-likelihood.

•

Privacy-aware batch sizes.
Batch sizes are scaled to absorb both statistical error and privacy-induced noise, ensuring confidence bounds remain valid.

Batch structure.

We partition the horizon into
M\leq\log\log T
batches

\mathcal{B}_{1},\ldots,\mathcal{B}_{M}
of lengths

B_{1},\ldots,B_{M}

[

]

\displaystyle B_{1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{\sqrt{\kappa}\,e^{3S}d^{2}\gamma^{2}}{S}\,\alpha\right)^{2/3},\qquad B_{2}=\alpha,

(6)

\displaystyle B_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha\sqrt{B_{k-1}},\quad k\in[3,M],

where

ensures the total horizon stays within
T

Privacy-aware parameters.

To balance privacy noise with estimation accuracy, we set

\displaystyle\lambda

\displaystyle=20dR\log T+\sigma\!\left(6\sqrt{\log(2dMT/\delta)}\right),

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=30RS\sqrt{dR\log T+12\sigma\sqrt{\log(2dMT/\delta)}}+\sqrt{4RS\nu}.

\displaystyle\sigma=O\!\left(\frac{R\log(d^2/\delta)}{\kappa^{*}\varepsilon}\right),\qquad\nu=O\!\left(\frac{RS^{2}}{\varepsilon}\sqrt{d\log^{3}(Td/\delta)}\right).

7
Lower Bound

Abeille et al.
[
2
]
show in Theorem 2 that, for generalized linear bandits with fixed contexts, any algorithm incurs regret at least

\Omega(d\sqrt{T/\kappa^{*}})
. This argument extends to both stochastic and adversarial
contexts. To capture the impact of privacy, we focus on an alternative model
where contexts are fixed and public, and the reward sequence is protected
under central
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP with respect to the reward sequence. Any lower bound in this regime
immediately extends to stochastic or adversarial contexts when contexts
are revealed to the learner.

Lemma 7.1

(Private lower bound under fixed public contexts)
.

There exists a fixed context set
\mathcal{X}
such that,
for any algorithm whose
public action sequence

X_{1:T}

is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-differentially private with respect
to the reward sequence under central DP, we have

\mathbb{E}[R_{T}]\;\;\geq\;\;c\,\frac{d}{\varepsilon},\qquad\text{for all }T\;\geq\;C\,\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}+\delta\log(1/\delta)},

for universal constants
c,C>0
.

The proof (see
Appendix

Q
) follows a standard
Assouad construction with
d
coordinate arms and mutual-information
bounds under DP. Combining this with the non-private bound yields
\Omega\!\left(d\sqrt{\tfrac{T}{\kappa^{*}}}\;+\;\tfrac{d}{\varepsilon}\right)
for generalized linear contextual bandits under
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-joint DP, valid in both stochastic and adversarial
settings when

9
Conclusion

Our work initiated the study of contextual bandits with generalized linear models under the dual requirements of joint differential privacy and shuffle privacy. The proposed algorithm achieves regret similar to its non-private analogue, and the leading term remains free of the non-private parameter
\kappa
. In the shuffled-privacy setting, however, the method cannot leverage distribution-optimal design for stochastic contexts, leading to an additional
\sqrt{d}
factor in the regret. By contrast, under joint differential privacy the algorithm is near-optimal—up to poly-logarithmic factors in
T
—and introduces no extra dependence on the dimension
d
. Future work will focus on tightening these regret bounds for generalized linear models and establishing matching lower bounds for this privacy setting.

Privacy Definitions

Let
{\mathcal{D}}
denote the data universe and
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
the number of users. Let
D_{i}\in{\mathcal{D}}
denote the data of user
i
, and
D_{-i}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{n-1}
denote the data of all users except
i
. Let
\varepsilon>0
and
\delta\in(0,1]
be privacy parameters.

Definition B.1

(Differential Privacy
[
19
]
)
.

A mechanism
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP if, for each user
i\in[n]
, each pair of datasets
D,D^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}^{n}
differing only in user
i

in the range of
{\mathcal{M}}
,

\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}(D_{i},D_{-i})\in E\right]\leq e^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}(D_{i}^{\prime},D_{-i})\in E\right]+\delta.

Definition B.2

(Local Differential Privacy)
.

A mechanism
{\mathcal{M}}
satisfies
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-LDP if for each user
i\in[n]
, each pair of data points
D_{i},D_{i}^{\prime}\in{\mathcal{D}}
, and each event
E
,

\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}(D_{i})\in E\right]\leq e^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}(D_{i}^{\prime})\in E\right]+\delta.

A shuffle protocol
{\mathcal{P}}=({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
consists of: (i) a local randomizer
{\mathcal{R}}
, (ii) a shuffler
{\mathcal{S}}
, and (iii) an analyzer
{\mathcal{A}}
. Each user
i
applies
{\mathcal{R}}
to their data
D_{i}
and sends the resulting messages to the shuffler, which uniformly permutes all messages before forwarding them to the analyzer. We consider a multi-round protocol
[
14
,
40
]
where users participate across
I
rounds.

Definition B.3

(Shuffle Differential Privacy)
.

A multi-round protocol
{\mathcal{P}}=({\mathcal{R}},{\mathcal{S}},{\mathcal{A}})
for
n
users satisfies
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-SDP if the mechanism
{\mathcal{S}}\circ{\mathcal{R}}^{n}=\left[{\mathcal{S}}({\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}(D_{1}),\ldots,{\mathcal{R}}^{(i)}(D_{n}))\right]_{i=1}^{I}
satisfies
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-DP.

Definition B.4

(Joint Differential Privacy
[
34
,
46
]
)
.

A learning algorithm
{\mathcal{M}}:{\mathcal{U}}^{T}\to{\mathcal{A}}^{T}
for contextual bandits is
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-JDP if for all
t\in[T]
, all user sequences
U_{T},U_{T}^{\prime}
differing only in user
t
, and all action sets

,

\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}_{-t}(U_{T})\in A_{-t}\right]\leq e^{\varepsilon}\mathbb{P}\left[{\mathcal{M}}_{-t}(U_{T}^{\prime})\in A_{-t}\right]+\delta,

where

.

Joint DP requires that each user’s action be private with respect to all other users’ data. This is weaker than central DP but sufficient to avoid the linear regret lower bound of
[
46
]
. By the Billboard Lemma
[
34
]
, shuffle DP implies joint DP.

Appendix C
Extended Related Work

Privacy amplification by shuffling.

The shuffle model bridges local and central DP, showing that randomly permuting locally privatized data achieves central-DP-level protection
[
9
,
20
,
15
,
23
]
. Multi-message protocols for binary summation and histograms demonstrate that sending a few short messages per user significantly tightens shuffling guarantees
[
26
,
7
,
27
]
.

Limited adaptivity in bandits.

Several works explore limited adaptivity in linear contextual bandits
[
44
,
1
,
28
,
30
]
, motivated by settings where policies cannot change frequently, such as clinical trials and online advertising. Extensions to logistic bandits
[
22
]
and GLMs
[
38
,
45
]
show that polylogarithmic policy switches suffice for optimal regret. Our algorithms build on the batching framework of
[
45
]
.

Shuffle DP convex optimization.

[
14
]
developed shuffle-DP algorithms for stochastic convex optimization, achieving near-central-DP accuracy. We adapt their techniques for the GLM estimation subroutine in our stochastic-context algorithm.

Detailed comparison with private linear bandits.

Shuffle DP for linear contextual bandits has been studied in
[
25
,
16
]
, and for
K
-armed bandits in
[
47
]
. Joint DP for linear contextual bandits was first introduced in
[
46
]
, using binary tree mechanisms
[
18
]
to update design matrix estimators at each step.
[
4
]
improved regret bounds under relaxed assumptions where context sets are public and only rewards require privacy protection.
[
43
]
further tightened regret bounds for sparse linear contextual bandits.

Local DP in contextual bandits has been studied in
[
53
]
, but their regret scales as
\tilde{O}(T^{3/4}/\varepsilon)
. More recently,
[
36
]
proposed an alternative approach that achieves
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T}/\varepsilon)
regret, though their method incurs exponential dependence on the dimension
d
.

Near-free privacy in linear bandits.

In linear bandits (without contexts),
[
31
]
show that joint DP can be achieved almost for free, with only an additive logarithmic term involving
\varepsilon
.
[
4
]
extend this to linear contextual bandits under relaxed assumptions—specifically, with public contexts and private rewards—and demonstrate that privacy comes at negligible cost.
[
43
]
further show that in sparse linear contextual bandits, one can obtain nearly optimal joint DP guarantees when the sparsity parameter satisfies
s\ll d
.
[
5
]
identifies the characterization of optimal regret for
(\varepsilon,\delta)
-joint DP linear contextual bandits in the general case as an open problem.

Private GLM bandits.

Prior work on differentially private contextual bandits under GLMs is limited.
[
29
]
analyze the stronger local DP model and achieve
\tilde{O}(T^{1/2}/\varepsilon)
regret, but their results rely on eigenvalue assumptions on the context distribution. To the best of our knowledge, no existing work addresses shuffle or joint DP in GLM bandits prior to ours. Our algorithms close this gap: we impose no spectral assumptions on the context distribution and achieve
\tilde{O}(\sqrt{T/\varepsilon})
regret in both privacy settings.

Lemma Q.3

(Bitwise action-KL and Hamming error)
.
