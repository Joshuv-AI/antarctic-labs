1.
Introduction

An arithmetic function
f\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be
multiplicative
if
f(1)=1
and
f(mn)=f(m)f(n)
for all coprime
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Additionally,
f
is
completely multiplicative
if
f(mn)=f(m)f(n)
holds for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
. Multiplicative functions are determined by their values at the prime powers. We call a function
f

k
-additive on a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
, if

f(a_{1}+\cdots+a_{k})=f(a_{1})+\cdots+f(a_{k})\quad\text{for all }a_{1},\dots,a_{k}\in S.

A multiplicative function that is also
k
-additive for some
k\geq 2
is forced to be the identity function in most cases. An early result in this direction is due to Spiro
[
12
]
, who showed that if a multiplicative function
f
is
2
-additive on the set of primes and
f(p_{0})\neq 0
for some prime
p_{0}
, then
f
is the identity function (i.e.,
f(n)=n
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
). This result was later extended to functions that are
k
-additive on the primes for
k=3
by Fang
[
5
]
, and for all
k\geq 3
by Dubickas and Šarka
[
3
]
.
Following Spiro
[
12
]
, we define a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{N}
to be a
k
-additive uniqueness set
for a class
\mathcal{F}
of arithmetic functions if
\mathcal{F}
has exactly one element
f
satisfying the
k
-additivity on
S
. The case
k=2
corresponds to the standard definition of an
additive uniqueness set
. The characterisation of additive uniqueness sets, along with the exceptional functions that arise in the absence of uniqueness, has become an area of active research.

Figurate numbers often serve as additive uniqueness sets for the class of multiplicative functions. Chung
[
2
]
demonstrated that uniqueness fails for the
2
-additive case on the set of squares by characterising all such multiplicative and completely multiplicative functions. In contrast, Park
[
8
]
later showed that any multiplicative function that is
k
-additive on the set of squares for
k\geq 3
must be the identity function. In other words, the set of squares is a
k
-additive uniqueness set for multiplicative functions whenever
k\geq 3
. Besides the set of squares, the sets of triangular and tetrahedral numbers were established as
2
-additive uniqueness sets for the class of multiplicative functions in the earlier work of Chung and Phong
[
1
]
. Park
[
9
]
extended this result for
k\geq 3
, by showing that the set of triangular numbers is a
k
-additive uniqueness set for the collection of multiplicative functions. Kim et al.
[
7
]
showed that the set of all nonzero generalised pentagonal numbers is a
2
-additive uniqueness set for the collection of multiplicative
functions. This result was later extended to the
k
-additive case by Hasanalizade
[
6
]
for
k\geq 3
, where the proof is conditional on the Generalised Riemann Hypothesis for
k=3
and unconditional for
k\geq 4
.

A related variant of these problems groups the summands into pairs, requiring additivity on sums of elements of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
rather than on individual squares. Here

\mathcal{E}_{2}=\{\,a^{2}+b^{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}a,b\geq 1\,\}=\{2,5,8,10,13,17,18,20,25,26,\dots\}

is the set of sums of two nonzero squares. Park
[
10
]
determined all multiplicative functions satisfying

f(a^{2}+b^{2}+c^{2}+d^{2})=f(a^{2}+b^{2})+f(c^{2}+d^{2}),\qquad a,b,c,d\geq 1.

More precisely, Park showed that any such function
f
is the identity function provided that
f(3)f(11)\neq 0
; otherwise,
f(n)=0
for all
n\geq 2
, except for
n\in\{3,9,11\}
. In other words, Park determined all functions that are
2
-additive on
\mathcal{E}_{2}
; the companion problem of classifying functions that are
2
-additive on
T_{2}
, the set of sums of two positive triangular numbers, was settled in
[
11
]
. In each of these two-summand problems, the function is essentially forced to be the identity, apart from a small family of exceptional functions.

In this article, we deal with the higher analogue of
[
10
]
obtained by summing
k
pairs. We study multiplicative functions
f
satisfying

(1)

f\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{2k}x_{i}^{2}\Bigr)=\sum_{j=1}^{k}f\bigl(x_{2j-1}^{2}+x_{2j}^{2}\bigr),\qquad x_{1},\dots,x_{2k}\geq 1.

Because every element of
\mathcal{E}_{2}
is of the form

with
x,y\geq 1
,
(
1
) is equivalent to

(2)

f\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{k}A_{i}\Bigr)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}f(A_{i})\qquad\text{for all }A_{1},\dots,A_{k}\in\mathcal{E}_{2},

In other words, we classify the multiplicative functions that are
k
-additive on
\mathcal{E}_{2}
for
k\geq 3
. The case

is precisely
[
10
, Theorem 1]
, and we treat the remaining cases

in this article.

The change in analysis from

comes from a structural change in the representability of integers as sums of
2k

, the set of sums of

20
(resp.,

[
10

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

. If

for all positive integers

For

must eventually behave as either the identity or the zero function, regardless of the value of

.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let

and let

be multiplicative and satisfy (

). Then either
f(n)=n
for all positive integers

, or

The proofs follow the same route as in
[
10
]
. For

, one starts with the equivalent form (

) and uses small decompositions in

, combined with multiplicativity and assumption

, to evaluate

nonzero squares, it can be expressed as a sum of

), we show that

is eventually constant for all

, forcing

to satisfy one of the forms in Theorem

. In all cases, we repeatedly use Corollary

The remainder of the paper is organised as follows. Section

.

by handling the cases
