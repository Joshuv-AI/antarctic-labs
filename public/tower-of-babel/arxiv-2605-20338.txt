Higher-Rank Connections and Deformed Schrödinger Operators Preprint: ZMP-HH/26-10 CERN-TH-2026-110 DESY-26-067
Jonah Baerman, Alba Grassi, Giovanni Ravazzini

Abstract
We study the connection problem for a class of linear differential equations of order N N closely related to the Baxter equation of the quantum Toda chain. The space of solutions is N N -dimensional and several linearly independent solutions decay at each singularity, leading to a rich structure of boundary value problems. We derive the weakest quantization conditions compatible with decaying behavior at both singularities, and formulate these conditions in terms of the associated monodromy data. In doing so, we prove the quantization conditions predicted by the topological string/spectral theory duality for a family of deformed Schrödinger equations. More generally, our results point to a hierarchy of spectral problems interpolating between the minimal conditions studied here and the maximally decaying boundary conditions of the N N -particle quantum Toda chain.

Introduction

In this work we investigate the spectral properties and solutions of linear differential equations of order
N
.

When
N=2
, these reduce to standard Schrödinger equations, whose spectral properties have been extensively studied in the literature, see e.g.
5

For confining potentials, one requires square-integrable solutions, while for potentials admitting resonances one may impose the Gamow–Siegert boundary conditions. Such boundary conditions lead to a quantization condition for the spectrum of the operator and, once this condition is imposed, the corresponding eigenfunctions are uniquely determined (up to normalization).

For higher-order equations,

Relation to TS/ST Correspondence

The topological string/spectral theory (TS/ST) correspondence is an exact duality relating topological string theory on local Calabi-Yau threefolds to the spectral theory of quantum mirror curves. This correspondence naturally decomposes into two complementary sectors: a closed string sector and an open string sector, originally formulated in
20
;
12
and
35
;
36
;
16
;
15
, respectively.
In the closed sector, the fundamental objects on the string side are the closed topological string partition functions, which are conjecturally equivalent to spectral quantities encoding the spectrum of the associated quantum operator, such as Fredholm determinants or spectral traces. In contrast, the open sector provides a direct bridge between open topological string partition functions and the exact eigenfunctions of the corresponding quantum spectral problem.
This duality has been extensively tested in a wide variety of examples, both numerically and analytically, and some of its aspects have been demonstrated rigorously for instance in
3
;
28
;
27
;
7
;
32
;
18

From this perspective, the results presented in this work provide a mathematical derivation of certain predictions of the TS/ST correspondence.

Outlook

In this work, based on

15
15

15

For

Equivalently, as follows from

From a quantum mechanical perspective, thes again correspond to

Our results suggest the existence of a broader family of quantization conditions, labeled by an integer

21
(corresponding to

) and that of
4
(corresponding to

). From the perspective of the Fourier transformed difference operator (

A related and intriguing feature of equation

More generally, the spectral theory of the operators with

We hope to return to these questions in future work.

Appendix A
Conventions for the
\mathfrak{su}(N)
Root System

Our conventions follow those of
15
, which we briefly summarize here for completeness and to ensure a self-consistent presentation. Our conventions for the root system of
\mathfrak{su}(N)
are as follows:

\begin{cases}\left\{\boldsymbol{b}_{i}\right\}_{i\in\left\{1,\cdots,N\right\}}&\text{standard euclidean orthonormal basis;}\\
\left\{\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\right\}_{i\in\left\{1,\cdots,N\right\}}&\text{weights of the fundamental representation;}\\
\left\{\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}\right\}_{k\in\left\{1,\cdots,N-1\right\}}&\text{fundamental weights, basis of the weight lattice;}\\
\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\ell}\right\}_{\ell\in\left\{1,\cdots,N-1\right\}}&\text{simple roots, dual to the fundamental weights;}\end{cases}

(105)

where

\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\cdot\boldsymbol{e}_{j}=\delta_{i,j}-\frac{1}{N}\,,\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=0\,.

(106)

We also recall that

\boldsymbol{e}_{i}=\boldsymbol{b}_{i}-\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j=1}^{N}\boldsymbol{b}_{j}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k}=\boldsymbol{e}_{k}-\boldsymbol{e}_{k+1}=\boldsymbol{b}_{k}-\boldsymbol{b}_{k+1}\,,\qquad\boldsymbol{\lambda}_{k}=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{e}_{i}\,.

(107)

We define the set of positive roots as:

\Delta^{+}=\left\{\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{k,\ell}=\boldsymbol{e}_{k}-\boldsymbol{e}_{\ell}\,\Big|\,1\leqslant k<\ell\leqslant N\right\}.

(108)

The Weyl group
\mathcal{W}_{N}
is isomorphic to the symmetric group
S_{N}
and acts on the weight lattice by permuting the indices of
e_{i}
. The Weyl orbit of an element

{\boldsymbol{v}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}v_{i}e_{i}\,,

(109)

is denoted as

\mathcal{W}_{N}\cdot v=\left\{w({\boldsymbol{v}})\,|\,w\in\mathcal{W}_{N}\right\}.

(110)
