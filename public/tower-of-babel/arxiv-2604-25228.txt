1.
Introduction

For a Tychonoff space
X

we denote by
C(X)
the linear space of all continuous real-valued functions on
X
.

C^{*}(X)
is the subspace of
C(X)
consisting of the bounded functions.
We write
C_{p}(X)
(resp.,
C_{p}^{*}(X)
) if
C(X)
(resp.,
C^{*}(X)
) is endowed with the point-wise convergence topology.
More information can be found in

[
2
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
12
]
,
[
13
]
.

Throughout the paper by dimension we mean the
covering dimension
\dim
.
Recall that for a Tychonoff space
X
and an integer
n\geq 0
,
\dim X\leq n
if every finite functionally open cover of the space
X

has a finite functionally open refinement of order
\leq n
, see
[
3
]
.
According to that definition, we have
\dim X=\dim\beta X
, where
\beta X
is the Čech-Stone compactification of
X
.
After the striking results of Pestov
[
11
]
and Gul’ko
[
5
]
that
\dim X=\dim Y
for any Tychonoff spaces
X
and
Y

provided
C_{p}(X)
and
C_{p}(Y)
are linearly homeomorphic or uniformly homeomorphic, Arhangel’skii
[
1
]
posed the problem
whether
\dim Y\leq\dim X
if there is continuous linear surjection from
C_{p}(X)
onto
C_{p}(Y)
.
This question was answered negatively by Leiderman-Levin-Pestov
[
7
]
and Leiderman-Morris-Pestov
[
8
]
.
For every finite-dimensional metrizable compact space
Y
there exists a continuous linear surjection
T:C_{p}([0,1])\to C_{p}(Y)

[
8
]
.

However, it turned out that the zero-dimensional case is an exception. It was shown in
[
7
]
that if there is a linear continuous surjection

T:C_{p}(X)\to C_{p}(Y)
for compact metrizable spaces
X
and
Y
,
then
\dim X=0
implies that
\dim Y=0
. The last result was extended for arbitrary compact spaces by Kawamura-Leiderman
[
6
]

who raised the question if their result holds for arbitrary Tychonoff spaces
X
and
Y
.
Recently, this difficult question was answered positively in
[
4
]
. In the present paper we show this is also true for linear continuous surjections

T:D_{p}(X)\to D_{p}(X)
, see Corollary 1.3 below.

Everywhere below, by
D(X)
we denote either
C^{*}(X)
or
C(X)
, and
D_{p}(X)
stays for
D(X)
endowed with the point-wise convergence topology.
If
T:D_{p}(X)\to D_{p}(Y)
is a continuous linear map, then there are four possible cases:
D(X)
is either
C(X)
or
C^{*}(X)
and
D(Y)
is either
C(Y)
or
C^{*}(Y)
. Suppose
\overline{Y}
is a compactification of a space
Y
and
E^{*}(Y)
is a subalgebra of
C^{*}(Y)
, we say that
E^{*}(Y)
is
associated with
\overline{Y}
if every
f\in E^{*}(Y)
can be extended to a function
\overline{f}\in C(\overline{Y})
and
C(\overline{Y})=\{\overline{f}:f\in E^{*}(Y)\}
. Similarly, a subalgebra
E(Y)
of
C(Y)
is
associated with
\overline{Y}
if every
f\in E(Y)
can be extended to a continuous function

\overline{f}:\overline{Y}\to\overline{\mathbb{R}}=\mathbb{R}\cup\{\pm\infty\}
and
\{\overline{f}|Y:f\in C(\overline{Y})\}\subset E(Y)
.

Zakrzewski
[
14
, Theorem 3.12]
proved the following result: If
X
and
Y
are
\sigma
-compact spaces
and there is a continuous linear map
T:C_{p}(X)\to C_{p}(Y)
such that
T(C_{p}(X))
is dense in
C_{p}(Y)
and
|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq m
for every
y\in Y
, then

\dim Y\leq m\cdot\dim X+m+m!-1
. Here,
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
denotes the support of the linear continuous map
l_{y}:C_{p}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
, defined by

l_{y}(f)=T(f)(y)
, see the precise definition below. In the present paper we improve the last inequality.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
and
Y
be spaces and
F(Y)
be a subalgebra of
D_{p}(Y)
associated with a compactification
\overline{Y}
of
Y
. Suppose
T:D_{p}(X)\to F_{p}(Y)
is a surjective continuous linear map such that
|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq m
for every
y\in Y
. Then
there exits a
\sigma
-compact subset
H\subset\overline{Y}
containing
Y
such that
\dim H\leq m\cdot\dim X
.

Corollary 1.2
.

Suppose
X,Y
are spaces and there is a linear continuous surjection
T:D_{p}(X)\to D_{p}(Y)
such that
|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq m
for all
y\in Y
. Then
\dim Y\leq m\cdot\dim X
.

Note that in case both
X
and
Y
are metrizable, Corollary 1.2 was established in
[
9
]
.

Corollary 1.3
.

If
X
and
Y
are spaces and
T:D_{p}(X)\to D_{p}(Y)
is a surjective continuous linear map such that
\dim X=0
, then
\dim Y=0
.

Note that the particular case of Corollary 1.3 when
T:C_{p}(X)\to C_{p}(Y)
is a surjective continuous linear map was established in
[
4
, Theorem 1.4]
.

2.
Preliminary results

Let
\mathbb{Q}
be the set of rational numbers. A subspace
E(X)\subset D(X)
is called a
QS
-algebra

[
5
]
if it satisfies the following conditions: (i) If
f,g\in E(X)
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{Q}
, then all functions
f+g
,
f\cdot g
and
\lambda f
belong to
E(X)
; (ii) For every
x\in X
and its neighborhood
U
in
X
there is
f\in E(X)
such that
f(x)=1
and
f(X\backslash U)=0
.

We need the following facts from
[
5
]
:

(2.1)

If
X
has a countable base and
\Phi\subset D(X)
is a countable set, then there is a countable
QS
-algebra
E(X)\subset D(X)
containing
\Phi
;

(2.2)

If
E(X)
is a
QS
-algebra on
X
and
U\subset X
is an open set containing the points
x_{1},x_{2},..,x_{k}
and
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},..,\lambda_{k}\in\mathbb{Q}
, then there exists
f\in E(X)
such that
f(x_{i})=\lambda_{i}
for each
i
and
f(X\backslash U)=0
.

(2.3)

We consider the following condition for a
QS
-algebra
E(X)
on
X
: For every compact set
K\subset X
and an open set
W
containing
K
there exists
f\in E(X)
with
f|K=1
,
f|(X\backslash W)=0
and
f(x)\in[0,1]
for all
x\in X
. Note that
if
X
has a countable base
\mathcal{B}
, then there is a countable
QS
-algebra
E(X)
on
X
satisfying that condition. Indeed, we can assume that
\mathcal{B}
is closed under finite unions and find
U,V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
K\subset V\subset cl(V)\subset U\subset cl(U)\subset W
. Then consider the set
\Phi
of all functions
f_{U,V}:X\to[0,1]
, where
cl(V)\subset U
with
U,V\in\mathcal{B}
, such that

f_{V,U}|cl(V)=1
and
f_{V,U}|(X\backslash U)=0
. According to
(2.1)
,
\Phi
can be extended to a countable
QS
-algebra
E(X)
on
X
.

Suppose
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
are compactifications of the spaces
X
and
Y
, respectively. Let
E(X)\subset D(X)
and
E(Y)\subset D(Y)
be
QS
-algebras on
X
and
Y
such that every
f\in LE(X)
and
g\in LE(Y)
can be extended to maps
\overline{f}\in C(\overline{X},\overline{\mathbb{R}})
and
\overline{g}\in C(\overline{Y},\overline{\mathbb{R}})
, where
EL(X)
and
EL(Y)
are the linear hulls of
E(X)
and
E(Y)
, respectively. Suppose also that there is a continuous surjection

\varphi_{0}:E(X)\to E(Y)
such that
\varphi_{0}(f_{1}\pm f_{2})=\varphi_{0}(f_{1})\pm\varphi_{0}(f_{2})
for all
f_{1},f_{2}\in E(X)
(such a map
\varphi_{0}
is called semi-linear). Let
\varphi:LE_{p}(X)\to LE_{p}(Y)
be a linear continuous map extending
\varphi_{0}
.
For every
y\in\overline{Y}
we define the
support of
y
to be the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
of all
x\in\overline{X}
satisfying the following condition: for every neighborhood
U\subset\overline{X}
of
x
there is
f\in LE(X)
such that
f(X\backslash U)=0
and
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)\neq 0
, where

\overline{\varphi(f)}\in C(\overline{Y},\overline{\mathbb{R}})
is the extension of
\varphi(f)
.
Obviously, each
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
is a closed subset of
\overline{X}
.

The next proposition is an analogue of
[
4
, Proposition 4.1]
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\overline{X}
and
\overline{Y}
are compactifications of the spaces
X
and
Y
, respectively, and
E(X)\subset D(X)
,
E(Y)\subset D(Y)
are
QS
-algebras on
X
and
Y
such that every
f\in LE(X)
and
g\in LE(Y)
can be extended to maps
\overline{f}\in C(\overline{X},\overline{\mathbb{R}})
and
\overline{g}\in C(\overline{Y},\overline{\mathbb{R}})
. Suppose the following conditions hold:

(1)

For every finite open cover
\gamma=\{U_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
of
\overline{X}
there is a partition of unity
\{\overline{f}_{i}\}_{i=1}^{k}
subordinated to
\gamma
with
f_{i}\in E(X)
;

(2)

The family
E(\overline{X})=\{\overline{f}:f\in E(X)\}
contains a
QS
-algebra of function from
C(\overline{X})
satisfying condition
(2.3)
;

(3)

The real-valued elements of
E(\overline{Y})=\{\overline{g}:g\in E(Y)\}
is dense in
C_{p}(\overline{Y})
;

If there is a continuous semi-linear map
\varphi_{0}:E(X)\to E(Y)
which can be continuously extended to a linear map
\varphi:LE_{p}(X)\to LE_{p}(Y)
between the linear hulls of
E(X)
and
E(Y)
, then we have:

(a)

If
U\subset\overline{X}
is open and contains
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
, then
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)=0
for every
f\in LE(X)
with
f(U\cap X)=0
;

(b)

For every
y\in\overline{Y}
the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
is a non-empty closed subset of
\overline{X}
;

(c)

The set-valued map
y\rightsquigarrow{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
is lower semi-continuous, i.e. the set
{\mathrm{supp}}\,^{-1}(U)=\{y\in\overline{Y}:{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)\cap U\neq\varnothing\}
is open in
\overline{Y}
for every open
U\subset\overline{X}
;

(d)

For any
p,k
the sets
\overline{Y}_{p,k}=\{y\in\overline{Y}:|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq k{~}\hbox{and}{~}a(y)\leq p\}
and
\overline{Y}_{k}=\{y\in\overline{Y}:|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq k\}

are closed in
\overline{Y}
such that
Y\subset\bigcup_{p,k\geq 1}\overline{Y}_{p,k}
, where

a(y)=\sup\{|\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)|:f\in LE(X){~}\hbox{and}{~}|\overline{f}(x)|<1{~}\forall x\in supp(y)\}.

(e)

If
y\in cl(\overline{Y}_{p,k}\cap Y)\backslash\overline{Y}_{k-1}
, then there exist unique real numbers
\lambda_{i}(y)
,
i=1,2,..,k
, such that
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(y)\overline{f}(x_{i}(y))
for all
f\in LE(X)
and
\sum_{i=1}^{k}|\lambda_{(}y)|=a(y)
, where
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)=\{x_{1}(y),.,x_{k}(y)\}
. Moreover, the functions
\lambda_{i}
are continuous on the set
A(y)=\{z\in cl(\overline{Y}_{p,k}\cap Y)\backslash\overline{Y}_{k-1}:{\mathrm{supp}}\,(z)={\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)\}
.

Proof.

(a)
Let
U\subset\overline{X}
be an open set containing
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
for some
y\in\overline{Y}
and
f(U\cap X)=0
with
f\in LE(X)
. Since
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
is closed in
\overline{X}
, we can assume that
U
is a finite union of open sets
V_{i}
,
i=1,2,..,k
.
Every
x\in\overline{X}\backslash U
has a neighborhood
V_{x}
such that
\overline{\varphi(g)}(y)=0
for any
g\in LE(X)
with
g(X\backslash V_{x})=0
.
Take a finite open cover
\{V_{x_{1}},..,V_{x_{m}}\}
of
\overline{X}\backslash U
. Then

\gamma=\{V_{1},..,V_{k},V_{x_{1}},..,V_{x_{m}}\}
is an open cover of
\overline{X}
and there exits a partition of unity
\{\overline{h}_{1},.,\overline{h}_{k},\overline{\theta}_{1},..,\overline{\theta}_{m}\}\subset E(\overline{X})
subordinated to
\gamma
. Hence,
h_{i}\cdot f,\theta_{j}\cdot f\in LE(X)
for all
i,j
and

f=\sum_{i=1}^{k}h_{i}\cdot f+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\theta_{j}\cdot f
. Take a net
\{y_{\alpha}\}\subset Y
with
\lim y_{\alpha}=y
.
So,
\varphi(f)(y_{\alpha})=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\varphi(h_{i}\cdot f)(y_{\alpha})+\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi(\theta_{j}\cdot f)(y_{\alpha})
.
Observe that
(h_{i}\cdot f)(x)=0
for all
x\in X
, so
\varphi(h_{i}\cdot f)
is the zero function on
Y
and
\varphi(h_{i}\cdot f)(y_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha
and
i=1,..,k
.
Consequently,
\varphi(f)(y_{\alpha})=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\varphi(\theta_{j}\cdot f)(y_{\alpha})
.
On the other hand,
(\theta_{j}\cdot f)(x)=0
for all
x\in X\backslash V_{x_{j}}
.
So,
\overline{\varphi(\theta_{j}\cdot f)}(y)=0
. Since
\lim_{\alpha}\varphi(\theta_{j}\cdot f)(y_{\alpha})=\overline{\varphi(\theta_{j}\cdot f)}(y)
for all
j=1,..,m
and

\lim_{\alpha}\varphi(f)(y_{\alpha})=\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)
, we have
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)=0
. This easily implies that
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)=\overline{\varphi(g)}(y)
for any
f,g\in LE(X)
with
f|(U\cap X)=g|(U\cap X)
.

(b)
Take
y\in\overline{Y}
and a function
g\in E(Y)
with
\overline{g}(y)\neq 0
(this can be done
because the set of real-valued functions from
E(\overline{Y})
is dense in
C_{p}(\overline{Y}))
.
Choose
f_{y}\in E(X)
such that
\varphi(f_{y})=g
, so
\overline{\varphi(f_{y})}(y)=\overline{g}(y)
. If
supp(y)=\varnothing
, then
\varnothing
is a neighborhood of
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
and, since
f_{y}(\varnothing)=0
, we have
\overline{g}(y)=0
, a contradiction.

(c)
Let
x_{0}\in supp(y_{0})\cap U
, where
y_{0}\in\overline{Y}
and
U\subset\overline{X}
is open. Take a neighborhood
W
of
x_{0}
in
\overline{X}
with

cl(W)\subset U
. Then there is
f\in LE(X)
such that
f(X\backslash W)=0
and
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y_{0})\neq 0
.
Striving for a contradiction, we can find a net
\{y_{\alpha}\}\subset\overline{Y}
converging to
y_{0}
such that
supp(y_{\alpha})\cap U=\varnothing
for every
\alpha
.
Hence,
\overline{X}\backslash cl(W)
is a neighborhood of each
supp(y_{\alpha})
. Since
f(X\backslash cl(W))=0
,
\overline{\varphi(f)}(y_{\alpha})=0
for all
\alpha
. Therefore,

\lim_{\alpha}\overline{\varphi(f)}(y_{\alpha})=\overline{\varphi(f)}(y_{0})=0
, a contradiction.

(d)
Obviously for every
p,k
we have
\overline{Y}_{p,k}=\overline{Y}_{k}\cap\widetilde{Y}_{p}
, where
\overline{Y}_{k}=\{y\in\overline{Y}:|{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)|\leq k\}
and
\widetilde{Y}_{p}=\{y\in\overline{Y}:a(y)\leq p\}
. Since the support map
{\mathrm{supp}}\,:\overline{Y}\rightsquigarrow\overline{X}
is lower semi-continuous, the sets

\overline{Y}_{k}
are closed. Indeed, if
y\not\in\overline{Y}_{k}
, then
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
contains at least
k+1
different points
x_{1}(y),x_{2}(y),..,x_{k+1}(y)
and we choose disjoint neighborhoods
O_{i}
of
x_{i}(y)
. Then there exists a neighborhood
U
of
y
in
\overline{Y}
such that
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(z)
meets each
O_{i}
for all
z\in U
. Hence,
U\subset\overline{Y}\backslash\overline{Y}_{k}
.

Let show that the sets
\widetilde{Y}_{p}
are also closed in
\overline{Y}
. Suppose that
a(y)>p
for some
y\in\overline{Y}
.
Then there exists
f\in LE(X)
such that
|\overline{f}(x)|<1
for all
x\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
and

|\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)|>p
. Take
a neighborhood
U
of
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
with
U\subset\{x\in\overline{X}:|\overline{f}(x)|<1\}
and
choose another neighborhood
W
of
{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
such that
cl(W)\subset U
. Since
E(\overline{X})
contains a
QS
-algebra satisfying condition
(2.3)
,
there is
h\in E(X)
such that
\overline{h}(cl(W))=1
,

\overline{h}(\overline{X}\backslash U)=0
and
h(x)\in[0,1]
for all
x\in X
. Then

g=h\cdot f\in LE(X)
and
|\overline{g}(x)|<1
for all
x\in\overline{X}
. Moreover,

g|(W\cap X)=f|(W\cap X)
. So, by condition
(a)
,
|\overline{\varphi(g)}(y)|=|\overline{\varphi(f)}(y)|>p
. Therefore,

V=\{z\in\overline{Y}:|\overline{\varphi(g)}(z)|>p\}
is a neighborhood of
y
with
V\cap\overline{Y}_{p}=\varnothing
. Hence,
all
\overline{Y}_{p,k}
are closed in
\overline{Y}
.

It remains to show that
Y\subset\bigcup_{p,k\geq 1}\overline{Y}_{p,k}
. To this end, fix
y\in Y
. Because
\varphi
is continuous, for every

y\in Y
the equality
l_{y}(f)=\varphi(f)(y)
defines a continuous linear functional
l_{y}:LE_{p}(X)\to\mathbb{R}
. Thus, there are
\varepsilon>0
and a finite set

such that
|l_{y}(f)|<1
for all

with
|f(x)|<\varepsilon
,

.
Using the linearity of
l_{y}
, one can show that
l_{y}(g)=0
for all
g\in LE(X)
with
g(x_{i}(y))=0
for all
i
.
Since
E(X)
is a
QS
-algebra, for every
i
there is a function
g_{i}\in E(X)
such that
g_{i}(x_{i}(y))=1
and
g_{i}(x_{j}(y))=0
with

. Now, if

f\in LE(X)
then

g=f-\sum_{i=1}^{k}g_{i}\cdot f(x_{i}(y))\in LE(X)
and
g(x_{i}(y))=0
for all

. So,
l_{y}(g)=0
and

l_{y}(f)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(y)f(x_{i}(y))
, where
\lambda_{i}(y)=\varphi(g_{i})(y)
.
Note that each
\lambda_{i}(y)
is a real number because so is
\varphi(g_{i})(y)
. Hence, for every

we have
\varphi(f)(y)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}(y)f(x_{i}(y))
with
\lambda_{i}(y)=\varphi(g_{i})(y)
. This implies that

. Indeed, if

U
of

in
\overline{X}
such that

. Then for every

with

\varphi(f)(y)=0

, so
z\not\in{\mathrm{supp}}\,(y)
. Let show there is
p
with
y\in\overline{Y}_{p,k}
. It suffices to show that

, where

is a
QS

and

. The reverse inequality

,

.

(e)
Let

, so

points from
\overline{X}
.

\overline{g}_{i}|\overline{X}\backslash U_{i}=0
, where

with

(such functions exist because

contains a

). Since

On the other hand, the lower semi-continuity of the support map and the fact that

.
One can also show that

Proposition 2.2

with
\dim H_{k}=0
. Then, by the Countable Sum Theorem for
\dim
, the set
H=\bigcup_{k\geq 1}H_{k}
is
0
-dimensional. Since
Y\subset H
and
\dim\beta H=\beta Y

\dim Y=0
