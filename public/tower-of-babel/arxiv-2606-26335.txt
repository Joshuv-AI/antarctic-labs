Embedding Equitable Rectangles
Amin Bahmanian, Anna Johnsen-Yu

Abstract
. Completing partial Latin squares is NP-complete. Ryser characterized precisely when an r × s r\times s Latin rectangle can be completed to an n × n n\times n Latin square. We extend this theorem to a broader setting. Let M M be an n × n n\times n array whose top-left r × s r\times s subarray is filled with symbols from { 1 , 2 , … , k } \{1,2,\dots,k\} , where 1 ≤ k ≤ n 2 1\leq k\leq n^{2} . We determine necessary and sufficient conditions under which the remaining cells of M M can be filled so that each symbol ℓ ∈ { 1 , 2 , … , k } \ell\in\{1,2,\dots,k\} appears exactly ρ ℓ \rho_{\ell} times in total, while the numbers of occurrences of each symbol in any two rows and in any two columns differ by at most one. Equivalently, our result characterizes when a partial edge-coloring of K r , s K_{r,s} can be extended to an edge-coloring of K n , n K_{n,n} in which each color class is spanning and almost regular, with prescribed color-class sizes. In this sense, our theorem generalizes Baranyai’s construction of almost-regular colorings of complete uniform multipartite hypergraphs, restricted to the bipartite case. Our theorem unifies and generalizes several classical results. When s = k = ρ 1 = ⋯ = ρ k = n s=k=\rho_{1}=\cdots=\rho_{k}=n , it reduces to Hall’s theorem, and when k = ρ 1 = ⋯ = ρ k = n k=\rho_{1}=\cdots=\rho_{k}=n , it yields Ryser’s completion theorem for Latin rectangles. Additional special cases recover results of Goldwasser, Hilton, Hoffman, and Özkan, as well as a theorem of Bahmanian. Thus, our work may be viewed as a common generalization of these completion theorems and Baranyai’s theorem.

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|\geq\max\{n-r,n-s\}\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor.

If

\displaystyle a_{\ell}\leq(n-r)\Bigl(\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Bigr),\;\;b_{\ell}\leq(n-s)\Bigl(\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Bigr).

\ell

All remaining occurrences must therefore lie in

The rows of
\overline{M}

\displaystyle|I|\Big(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-n\Big)\leq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\min\left\{r\left\lceil\frac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil+(n-r)\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor+a_{\ell}-\rho_{\ell},\;\eta_{I}(\ell)\right\}.

By a similar argument,

\displaystyle|J|\Big(\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-n\Big)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\min\left\{s\left\lceil\frac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil+(n-s)\left\lfloor\frac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor+b_{\ell}-\rho_{\ell},\;\eta_{J}(\ell)\right\}

\displaystyle\forall J\subseteq[s],

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}-(n-s)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor-s\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil

\displaystyle\leq b_{\ell}\leq\eta_{[s]}(\ell)+\rho_{\ell}-s\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-(n-s)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor

\displaystyle|\,^{j}\mathbb{P}|

3.
Consequences of the Main Result and Open Problems

It is natural to identify cases in which the conditions of our theorem are simplified; in particular, cases in which the feasible sequence already exists.

For technical reasons, we have assumed throughout the paper that
r,s\in\mathbb{N}
. If this assumption is relaxed to allow
r=s=0
, then Lemma
2.2
with
r=s=0
and
v=w=n
yields a construction of equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin squares. In this case, Lemma
2.1
is trivial, since one may take
T
to be a single cell containing
\rho_{\ell}
copies of the symbol
\ell
for each
\ell\in[k]
.

Corollary 3.1
.

For
n,k\in\mathbb{N}
,
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}:=(\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k})
with
1\leq\rho_{1},\dots,\rho_{k},k\leq n^{2}
and
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\rho_{\ell}=n^{2}
, there exists an equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin square of order
n
.

We next state a result that extends Hall’s theorem and its generalization by Goldwasser et al.
[
9
]
to equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin rectangles.

Corollary 3.2
.

An
r\times n
equitable
\bm{\rho}
-Latin rectangle
M
extends to an
n\times n
equitable
\bm{\rho}
-Latin square if and only if the following conditions are met.

\displaystyle\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|}{n-r}\approx\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}

\displaystyle\text{for }\ell\in[k],

\displaystyle n|J|\geq\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(|J|\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\min\left\{n\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\rho_{\ell},\eta_{J}(\ell)\right\}\Big)

\displaystyle\text{for }J\subseteq[s].

Proof.

In Theorem
1.3
, let
s=n
. Then (
2
) for
J\subseteq[s]
reduces exactly to the second condition above, and (
1
) becomes
\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|\geq(n-r)\left\lfloor\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rfloor
for
\ell\in[k]
, which is precisely the lower bound in the first condition of this corollary. As
|M_{\ell}|\leq r\left\lceil\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rceil
, the existence of a feasible sequence when
s=n
reduces to the existence of a sequence
\{(a_{\ell},0)\}_{\ell\in[k]}
such that for
\ell\in[k]
, the following hold.

\displaystyle\begin{cases}a_{\ell}\geq\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-(n-r)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor,\vskip 2.84526pt\\
a_{\ell}\leq\min\Big\{n\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil,\rho_{\ell}+\eta_{[r]}(\ell)\Big\}-r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-(n-r)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor,\vskip 2.84526pt\\
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}a_{\ell}=(n-r)\Big(n-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Big).\end{cases}

We claim that the sequence
\{(a_{\ell},0)\}_{\ell\in[k]}
such that
a_{\ell}=\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-(n-r)\left\lfloor\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rfloor
for
\ell\in[k]
is feasible.
By the upper bound on
\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|
given by the first necessary condition of the corollary, we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|\leq(n-r)\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil,

and since
|M_{\ell}|\geq r\left\lceil\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rceil-\eta_{[r]}(\ell)
, we have that

\displaystyle\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|\leq\rho_{\ell}+\eta_{[r]}(\ell)-r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil.

Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|-(n-r)\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Big)=(n-r)\Big(n-\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Big).

Thus, this sequence is feasible.

Finally, note that for
I\subseteq[r]
and
\ell\in[k]
, we have that
\eta_{I}(\ell)\leq\eta_{[r]}(\ell)=r\left\lceil\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|
. Hence, given the feasible sequence defined above, we have for
\ell\in[k]
that

(8)

\displaystyle\min\Big\{r\Big(\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor\Big)+n\left\lfloor\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rfloor-\rho_{\ell}+a_{\ell},\eta_{I}(\ell)\Big\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\Big\{r\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-|M_{\ell}|,\eta_{I}(\ell)\Big\}\geq\eta_{I}(\ell).

In any equitable
r\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin rectangle, we have that
\sum_{\ell\in[k]}|M_{\ell}^{i}|=n
and that
|M_{\ell}^{i}|\geq\left\lfloor\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rfloor
, and so
\left\lceil\rho_{\ell}/n\right\rceil-\eta_{i}(\ell)=|M_{\ell}^{i}|
. It follows that for
I\subseteq[r]
in any equitable
r\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin rectangle,

\displaystyle n|I|=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}|M_{\ell}^{i}|=\sum_{i\in I}\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\eta_{i}(\ell)\Big)=\sum_{\ell\in[k]}\Big(|I|\left\lceil\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\right\rceil-\eta_{I}(\ell)\Big).

By (
8

2

, and so the corollary follows directly from our main result.
∎

The complexity of our main result arises from the presence of free symbols. When every symbol is forced, the necessary conditions simplify considerably. The next two corollaries follow by imposing this restriction on Theorems
3.2
and
1.3
. In particular, setting
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\cdots=\rho_{k}=n

Corollary 3.3
.

When
n\mid\rho_{\ell}
for
\ell\in[k]

r\times n
equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin rectangle can be extended to an
n\times n

\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin square.

Proof.

As
n\mid\rho_{\ell}

3.2

.
∎

If we set
\rho_{1}=\rho_{2}=\cdots=\rho_{k}=n

|M_{\ell}|\geq r+s-n
for
\ell\in[k]

Corollary 3.4
.

When
n\mid\rho_{\ell}
for
\ell\in[k]

r\times s
equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}
-Latin rectangle
M

n\times n
equitable
\operatorname{\bm{\rho}}

\displaystyle\max\Big\{n-r,n-s\Big\}\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\leq\rho_{\ell}-|M_{\ell}|\leq(2n-r-s)\dfrac{\rho_{\ell}}{n}\quad\text{for }\ell\in[k].

\{(0,0)\}_{\ell\in[k]}

2
) holds trivially. Hence, this result follows directly from Theorem
1.3
.
