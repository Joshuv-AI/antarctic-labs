Adjacency Spectral Radius Under Laplacian Sparsification: Deterministic and Probabilistic Bounds
Joshua Steier

Abstract
Spielman–Srivastava spectral sparsification preserves Laplacian quadratic forms to within ( 1 ± ε ) (1\pm\varepsilon) , but does not directly control the adjacency spectral radius λ 1 ​ ( A G ) \lambda_{1}(A_{G}) —which governs the NIMFA epidemic threshold and arises in spectral clustering. We prove | λ 1 ​ ( A H ) − λ 1 ​ ( A G ) | ≤ ε ⁡ ( 2 ​ Δ − λ 1 ​ ( A G ) ) |\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})) deterministically, with a sharp ε ​ λ 1 ​ ( A G ) \varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G}) bound for reweighting sparsifiers via Perron–Frobenius monotonicity. Under effective-resistance sampling, Matrix Bernstein gives O ⁡ ( ε ​ Δ / c ) O(\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}) with high probability. Combining eigenvector delocalization with resolvent perturbation theory, we establish that for graphs with delocalized Perron eigenvectors and δ gap = Ω ⁡ ( Δ ) \delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\Omega(\Delta) , | λ 1 ​ ( A H ) − λ 1 ​ ( A G ) | ≤ O ⁡ ( ε ​ Δ ​ log ⁡ n n ) + O ⁡ ( ε 2 ​ Δ 2 δ gap ) , |\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\;\leq\;O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon\,\Delta\,\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)\;+\;O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}}\right), with corollaries for Erdős–Rényi graphs, regular expanders, and stochastic block models. Lower bounds establish tightness for regular graphs.

1
Introduction

In computational epidemiology, large-scale simulations of SIS and SIR dynamics on contact
networks are often bottlenecked by graph size. Spielman–Srivastava spectral
sparsification
[
16
]
offers an attractive preprocessing step: it
samples edges proportional to their effective resistances, retaining only

O(n\log n/\varepsilon^{2})
edges while provably preserving all Laplacian quadratic forms
to within
(1\pm\varepsilon)
. Empirical studies confirm that epidemic dynamics on the
sparsified graph closely match the original
[
14
]
.

However, the theoretical guarantees of Laplacian sparsification do not directly address
the adjacency spectrum. In the
N
-intertwined mean-field approximation (NIMFA) of the
SIS model, the epidemic threshold is
\tau_{\mathrm{mf}}(G)=1/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
, where
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})

is the spectral radius of the adjacency
matrix
[
22
,
23
]
. The quantity
1/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
is the
threshold of the mean-field approximation, not the exact sharp threshold of the
finite-state SIS Markov process, which is more subtle on general finite
graphs
[
20
]
. Nevertheless,
1/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
is the standard spectral proxy
used throughout computational epidemiology and network science
[
21
]
.

Since
A=D-L
, the adjacency spectrum depends on both the Laplacian and the degree
matrix, and the degree matrix is only approximately preserved under sparsification. This
raises a precise mathematical question:

If
H
is a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
, what is the tightest
provable bound on
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|
?

To our knowledge, the specific question of bounding
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|
for
Laplacian-preserving sparsifiers has not been addressed in the literature, though the
surrounding space is not empty. That effective-resistance sparsification approximately
preserves the adjacency spectral radius—and hence the mean-field epidemic
threshold—has been observed empirically
[
14
]
, but we are not
aware of any
provable, quantitative
bound on this distortion; supplying one is
the purpose of this work.
Random edge-sampling schemes such as DOULION
[
19
]

retain each edge independently and reweight by the inverse sampling probability,
and have been used as a general-purpose sparsification primitive; our contribution
is to provide
rigorous, quantitative
bounds on adjacency spectral radius
distortion specifically for
Laplacian-preserving
sparsifiers, which offer
the additional structural guarantee of preserving all Laplacian quadratic forms.
The Spielman–Srivastava and
BSS
[
4
]
papers bound Laplacian eigenvalues only.
Achlioptas–McSherry
[
2
]
and related work on matrix sparsification
use entry-wise sampling to approximate a matrix in spectral norm, but do not address the
graph-specific setting of Laplacian-preserving subgraphs. Kolla et
al.
[
12
]
study subgraph sparsification (retaining a subgraph of a
given graph union while preserving Laplacian quality), which is a different structural
question. Purohit et al.
[
15
]
minimize the change in
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
under
graph
coarsening
(node merging), which changes the vertex set and is thus
incomparable with our edge-sparsification setting. Bravo Hermsdorff and
Gunderson
[
5
]
provide a unifying framework for sparsification and
coarsening that preserves the Laplacian pseudoinverse, but do not analyze adjacency
eigenvalue distortion.

More recently, Jin, Karmarkar, Musco, Sidford, and Singh
[
11
]

develop nuclear sparsifiers that yield approximation guarantees for the
spectral density of the
normalized
adjacency/Laplacian matrix in Wasserstein-1
distance, which is a distributional statement about all eigenvalues of a different
matrix operator. Cai, Chen,
and Peng
[
6
]
prove leverage-score-based lower bounds on eigenvalues
after row/edge removal, which is structurally adjacent to our perturbation framing.
Neither addresses the top adjacency eigenvalue
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
under Laplacian-preserving
sparsification, which is the focus of this work.

This gap matters practically because many graph analysis pipelines sparsify a graph once
for computational efficiency (using effective resistance because it preserves diffusion
properties) but later analyze the adjacency spectrum for different purposes—mean-field
epidemic thresholds, spectral clustering, centrality estimation. Our results quantify the
collateral damage to
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
from this workflow.

Remark 1.1
.

Since the sparsifier
H
is a weighted graph, the NIMFA threshold
1/\lambda_{1}(A_{H})
assumes a
weighted SIS model where infection rates scale linearly with edge weights.

Contributions.

Our main result (Theorem
7.4
) shows that for graphs with
delocalized Perron eigenvectors and spectral gap
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\Omega(\Delta)
,
Spielman–Srivastava sparsification preserves
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
to within

O(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n})+O(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta)
with high probability—far tighter
than the generic operator-norm bound
O(\varepsilon\Delta)
.
The paper develops three layers of analysis leading to this result:

(a)

Deterministic bounds.

For any
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier:

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
absolutely
(Theorem
3.4
), with a sharp
\varepsilon
-relative bound

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
for reweighting sparsifiers via Perron–Frobenius
monotonicity (Theorem
4.2
). We introduce an eigenvector-degree
parameter
\gamma(G)
that governs the one-sided bound
(Theorem
3.2
) and an effective degree
\alpha_{w}(G)
that
isolates the true driver of distortion (Theorem
5.1
).

(b)

Probabilistic refinement via resolvent perturbation theory.

Under SS sampling, a Matrix Bernstein bound gives

\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}\leq 2\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}
(Theorem
6.2
).
A second-order resolvent decomposition (Theorem
6.4
)
combined with scalar concentration of the first-order term and eigenvector
delocalization yields the main two-term bound
(Theorem
7.4
), with explicit corollaries for
Erdős–Rényi graphs,
d
-regular expanders, and balanced stochastic
block models.

(c)

Lower bounds and tightness.

Reweighting constructions establish that the deterministic bound is tight for
regular graphs and that
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\geq\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
for all graphs.
Whether edge
deletion
can cause
\omega(\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
distortion
remains open (Open Problem
8.5
).

(d)

Comparison with entry-wise sparsification.

At equal edge count, the SS eigenvalue distortion for well-connected graphs
is a factor of
\sqrt{n/\log n}
below what entry-wise sampling achieves,
while additionally preserving the Laplacian (Section
9
).

Organization.

Section
2
sets notation.
Section
3
proves the deterministic bounds and discusses
limitations of the
\gamma
-bound.
Section
4
establishes the sharp Perron–Frobenius bound for
reweighting sparsifiers.
Section
5
gives the eigenvector-weighted deterministic refinement.
Section
6
proves the probabilistic operator-norm bound.
Section
7
establishes the delocalized distortion bound,
with corollaries for specific graph families and a table of resolvent conditions.
Section
8
establishes lower bounds and tightness.
Section
9
compares with entry-wise sparsification.
Section
10
presents computational validation.
Section
11
discusses open problems.

2
Preliminaries

Let
G=(V,E)
be a connected simple graph on
n=|V|
vertices with

m=|E|
edges. We write
A_{G}
for the adjacency matrix,

D_{G}=\operatorname{diag}(d_{1},\ldots,d_{n})
for the degree matrix, and

L_{G}=D_{G}-A_{G}
for the combinatorial Laplacian. Let
\Delta=\max_{i}d_{i}

and
\delta=\min_{i}d_{i}
denote the maximum and minimum degrees.

The eigenvalues of
A_{G}
are
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{n}
.
We write
v_{1}
for the unit eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})

(i.e.,
A_{G}v_{1}=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})v_{1}
,
\|v_{1}\|_{2}=1
). The
spectral gap

is
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}>0
(strict positivity holds
for connected graphs by the Perron–Frobenius theorem).

For each edge
e=(u,v)\in E
, the
effective resistance
is

R_{e}=\mathbf{b}_{e}^{\top}L_{G}^{+}\mathbf{b}_{e}
where
\mathbf{b}_{e}=\mathbf{e}_{u}-\mathbf{e}_{v}
and
L_{G}^{+}
is
the Moore–Penrose pseudoinverse. By Foster’s theorem,

\sum_{e\in E}R_{e}=n-1
for unweighted graphs.

We write
\Phi:=A_{H}-A_{G}
for the perturbation matrix.

Definition 2.1

(Effective-resistance sampling scheme)
.

Given
G
and a sample budget
q\geq 1
:

1.

Set sampling probabilities
p_{e}=R_{e}/(n-1)
for each
e\in E
.

2.

Draw
q
edges i.i.d. from distribution
(p_{e})_{e\in E}
.

3.

For edge
e
sampled
k_{e}
times, assign weight
\tilde{w}_{e}=k_{e}/(qp_{e})
in
H
.

Theorem 2.2

(Spielman–Srivastava
[
16
]
)
.

There exists an absolute constant
C>0
such that for any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
if
q\geq C\cdot n\log n/\varepsilon^{2}
, then the output
H
of
Definition
2.1
satisfies

(1-\varepsilon)L_{G}\preceq L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
with high probability.

Definition 2.3

(Eigenvector-degree parameter)
.

For a connected graph
G
with unit Perron eigenvector
v_{1}
, define

\alpha(G)=v_{1}^{\top}D_{G}v_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}\,v_{1,i}^{2},\qquad\gamma(G)=\frac{2\alpha(G)}{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}-1.

Remark 2.4
.

Since
L_{G}=D_{G}-A_{G}
is positive semidefinite,
v_{1}^{\top}D_{G}v_{1}\geq v_{1}^{\top}A_{G}v_{1}=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
, so
\gamma(G)\geq 1
. For
d
-regular graphs,

v_{1}=\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{n}
,
\alpha=d
,
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=d
, so
\gamma=1
.
For the star
K_{1,n-1}
,
\gamma=\Theta(\sqrt{n})
.
In general,
\gamma\leq 2\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-1
.

Definition 2.5

(Delocalization)
.

A connected graph
G
is
K
-delocalized
if its unit Perron eigenvector satisfies

\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq K/\sqrt{n}
.

Remark 2.6
.

For
d
-regular graphs,
v_{1}=\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{n}
, so
K=1
.
For Erdős–Rényi
G(n,p)
with
np\geq C\log n
,
the general eigenvector delocalization bound of
He et al. 2019

gives
\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
(i.e.,

K=n^{o(1)}
). For the Perron eigenvector specifically, the entrywise
perturbation theory of
Abbe et al. 2020
yields the stronger

K=O(1)
in the regime
np/\log n\to\infty
; see
Corollary
7.5
for details.

3
Deterministic Bounds

Lemma 3.1

(Degree preservation)
.

If
(1-\varepsilon)L_{G}\preceq L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
, then for every vertex
i
:

(1-\varepsilon)\,d_{i}(G)\leq d_{i}(H)\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,d_{i}(G).

Proof.

Evaluate the Laplacian quadratic form at
x=\mathbf{e}_{i}
:

x^{\top}Lx=L_{ii}=d_{i}
for any graph with no self-loops.
The two-sided Laplacian guarantee then gives the result directly.
∎

Theorem 3.2

(Deterministic
\gamma
-bound, lower)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
. Then

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\!\left[1-\varepsilon\,\gamma(G)\right].

Proof.

From
L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
we get

A_{H}=D_{H}-L_{H}\succeq D_{H}-(1+\varepsilon)(D_{G}-A_{G})
.
Using Lemma
3.1
(
D_{H}\succeq(1-\varepsilon)D_{G}
):

\displaystyle v_{1}^{\top}A_{H}v_{1}

\displaystyle\geq(1-\varepsilon)\,v_{1}^{\top}D_{G}v_{1}-(1+\varepsilon)\,v_{1}^{\top}D_{G}v_{1}+(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G})

\displaystyle=-2\varepsilon\,\alpha(G)+(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\!\left[1-\varepsilon\,\gamma(G)\right].

Since
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq v_{1}^{\top}A_{H}v_{1}
by the Rayleigh quotient, the result follows.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Deterministic upper bound)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
. Then

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\leq(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+2\varepsilon\Delta.

Proof.

From
L_{H}\succeq(1-\varepsilon)L_{G}
:

A_{H}\preceq D_{H}-(1-\varepsilon)L_{G}=D_{H}-(1-\varepsilon)(D_{G}-A_{G})
.
Using
D_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)D_{G}
: for any unit vector
u
,

\displaystyle u^{\top}A_{H}u

\displaystyle\leq(1+\varepsilon)\,u^{\top}D_{G}u-(1-\varepsilon)\,u^{\top}D_{G}u+(1-\varepsilon)\,u^{\top}A_{G}u

\displaystyle=2\varepsilon\,u^{\top}D_{G}u+(1-\varepsilon)\,u^{\top}A_{G}u\leq 2\varepsilon\Delta+(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G}).

Taking
u
to be the top eigenvector of
A_{H}
and noting

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=u^{\top}A_{H}u
completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 3.4

(Deterministic absolute bound)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
. Then

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))\leq 2\varepsilon\Delta.

Proof.

This combines the two one-sided bounds.
From Theorem
3.2
, using
\alpha(G)\leq\Delta
:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})[1-\varepsilon\gamma(G)]=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-2\varepsilon\alpha(G)\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})).

From Theorem
3.3
:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\leq(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+2\varepsilon\Delta=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})).

Combining gives
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
.
The weaker bound
\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))\leq 2\varepsilon\Delta
follows from
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\geq 0
.
∎

Corollary 3.5

(Deterministic relative bound)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of a connected graph
G
. Then:

\frac{|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|}{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}\leq\varepsilon\!\left(\frac{2\Delta}{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}-1\right)\leq\varepsilon\!\left(2\sqrt{\Delta}-1\right).

Proof.

The first inequality is immediate from Theorem
3.4
.
For the second, we use the classical bound
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\geq\sqrt{\Delta}
, which holds
because the neighborhood of any maximum-degree vertex
v
spans at least
\Delta

edges, so
G
contains the star
K_{1,\Delta}
as a subgraph (after identifying
v

with the hub and its neighbors with the leaves). Embedding
K_{1,\Delta}
into
G

shows that
(A_{G})_{ij}\geq(A_{K_{1,\Delta}})_{ij}
entry-wise (in the principal
submatrix indexed by
\{v\}\cup N(v)
, with zeros elsewhere). Since
A_{G}
is
nonnegative and irreducible, the monotonicity of the Perron root under entry-wise
domination
[
10
, Corollary 8.1.19]
gives

\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\geq\lambda_{1}(K_{1,\Delta})=\sqrt{\Delta}
.
∎

Corollary 3.6

(NIMFA threshold preservation)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
, and let

\tau_{\mathrm{mf}}(G)=1/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
be the NIMFA epidemic threshold.
If
\varepsilon\,(2\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-1)<1
, then

\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon(2\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-1)}\leq\frac{\tau_{\mathrm{mf}}(H)}{\tau_{\mathrm{mf}}(G)}\leq\frac{1}{1-\varepsilon\,\gamma(G)}.

The lower bound in Corollary
3.6
uses only
\Delta
and
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
(both
computable in
O(m)
and
O(m\log n)
time respectively). The tighter upper bound
uses
\gamma(G)=2\alpha(G)/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-1
, which requires the Perron eigenvector; in
practice, a few steps of power iteration on
A_{G}
suffice for an accurate estimate.

Proposition 3.7

(Jensen bias)
.

Let
H
be the random output of the effective-resistance sampling scheme
(Definition
2.1
). Then
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
.

Proof.

The map
A\mapsto\lambda_{1}(A)
is convex (supremum of linear functions).
Since
\mathbb{E}[A_{H}]=A_{G}
(unbiasedness of the SS estimator), Jensen’s inequality gives

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]\geq\lambda_{1}(\mathbb{E}[A_{H}])=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
.
∎

3.1
Limitations of
\gamma(G)
for Heterogeneous Graphs

For heterogeneous graphs,
\gamma(G)
can be large, making the
\gamma
-bound vacuous.

Proposition 3.8

(Star graph
\gamma
)
.

For the star
K_{1,n-1}
,

\gamma(K_{1,n-1})=n/\sqrt{n-1}-1=\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
.

Proof.

For
K_{1,n-1}
:
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\sqrt{n-1}
,
v_{1,\mathrm{hub}}=1/\sqrt{2}
,

v_{1,\mathrm{leaf}}=1/\sqrt{2(n-1)}
. Then

\alpha=(n-1)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2}+(n-1)\cdot\tfrac{1}{2(n-1)}=n/2
,
giving
\gamma=n/\sqrt{n-1}-1=\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
.
∎

When
\gamma(G)=\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
, the
\gamma
-bound is vacuous for moderate

\varepsilon
; Corollary
3.5
still provides
O(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta})
relative
distortion unconditionally. For power-law graphs with degree exponent

\beta\in(5/2,3)

[
7
]
,
\gamma
can reach

\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
; extending the probabilistic analysis to such localized
eigenvector regimes is an important open direction
(Section
11
).

4
Sharp Bound for Reweighting Sparsifiers

When the sparsifier retains all edges of
G
(with modified weights), the adjacency
perturbation can be bounded much more tightly than the generic Laplacian-based
approach. This yields a relative distortion of exactly
\varepsilon
, independent of
\Delta
.

Definition 4.1

(Reweighting sparsifier)
.

A
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-reweighting sparsifier
of
G
is a weighted graph
H
on the same
vertex and edge set as
G
, with edge weights
w_{e}\in[(1-\varepsilon),(1+\varepsilon)]
for every

e\in E
, such that
(1-\varepsilon)L_{G}\preceq L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
.

Theorem 4.2

(Perron–Frobenius relative bound)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-reweighting sparsifier of a connected graph
G

(Definition
4.1
). Then

(1-\varepsilon)\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\;\leq\;\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\;\leq\;(1+\varepsilon)\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G}).

In particular,
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
.

Proof.

Since
H
retains every edge of
G
with weight
w_{e}\in[(1-\varepsilon),(1+\varepsilon)]
, the
adjacency matrices satisfy the entry-wise inequalities

(1-\varepsilon)\,(A_{G})_{ij}\;\leq\;(A_{H})_{ij}\;\leq\;(1+\varepsilon)\,(A_{G})_{ij}\qquad\forall\;i,j.

Both
A_{G}
and
A_{H}
are nonnegative and irreducible (since
G
is connected and all
edge weights are positive). By the monotonicity of the Perron root for irreducible
nonnegative matrices (see, e.g.,
Horn and Johnson 2012
, Corollary 8.1.19): if

0\leq B\leq C
entry-wise with
C
irreducible, then
\rho(B)\leq\rho(C)
, with
equality if and only if
B=C
.

Applying this to
A_{H}\leq(1+\varepsilon)A_{G}
:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=\rho(A_{H})\leq\rho\!\bigl((1+\varepsilon)A_{G}\bigr)=(1+\varepsilon)\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G}).

Applying this to
(1-\varepsilon)A_{G}\leq A_{H}
:

(1-\varepsilon)\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\rho\!\bigl((1-\varepsilon)A_{G}\bigr)\leq\rho(A_{H})=\lambda_{1}(A_{H}).

Combining gives the stated two-sided bound.
∎

A natural class of reweighting sparsifiers arises from Laplacian-preserving
modifications of tree substructures.

Proposition 4.3

(Trees: Laplacian
\Leftrightarrow
per-edge bounds)
.

Let
T
be a tree on
n
vertices. A weighted graph
H
on the same vertex and edge set
as
T
with edge weights
(w_{e})_{e\in E(T)}
satisfies

(1-\varepsilon)L_{T}\preceq L_{H}\preceq(1+\varepsilon)L_{T}
if and only if

w_{e}\in[(1-\varepsilon),(1+\varepsilon)]
for every edge
e
.

Proof.

For a tree, the Laplacian quadratic form decomposes as

x^{\top}L_{H}x=\sum_{e=(u,v)}w_{e}(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}
.
Each term
(x_{u}-x_{v})^{2}
is independently controllable: for any

e_{0}=(u_{0},v_{0})\in E(T)
, one can construct
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with

x_{u_{0}}\neq x_{v_{0}}
and
x_{u}=x_{v}
for all other edges
e=(u,v)\neq e_{0}

(this is possible precisely because
T
is a tree, so removing
e_{0}
disconnects
T

into two components; set
x
to different constants on each component).
For such
x
, the Laplacian ratio reduces to
w_{e_{0}}
. The forward direction is
immediate; the reverse follows from the decomposition.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

For any tree
T
and any
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier
H
that retains all edges of

T
, we have
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{T})|\leq\varepsilon\,\lambda_{1}(A_{T})
.

Proof.

Combine Proposition
4.3
with Theorem
4.2
.
∎

5
Refined Deterministic Bound via Quadratic Form Decomposition

The deterministic bound
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
arises from
bounding
D_{H}-D_{G}
and
L_{H}-L_{G}
independently. We now prove a refined bound
that exploits the correlation between these perturbations.

Theorem 5.1

(Eigenvector-weighted deterministic bound)
.

Let
H
be a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier of
G
. Let
v_{1}
be the unit Perron
eigenvector of
A_{G}
and let
w
be the unit eigenvector of
A_{H}
achieving
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})
.
Then:

(a)

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\leq 2\varepsilon\,\alpha_{w}(G)
,
where
\alpha_{w}(G):=w^{\top}D_{G}w=\sum_{i}d_{i}w_{i}^{2}
.

(b)

\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\leq 2\varepsilon\,\alpha(G)=2\varepsilon\,v_{1}^{\top}D_{G}v_{1}
.

The quantity
\alpha_{w}(G)\leq\Delta
always, but for delocalized eigenvectors

\alpha_{w}(G)=O(\Delta\cdot K^{2})
independently of
n
.

Proof.

Part (a).
From the proof of Theorem
3.3
:

w^{\top}A_{H}w\leq 2\varepsilon\,w^{\top}D_{G}w+(1-\varepsilon)\,w^{\top}A_{G}w\leq 2\varepsilon\,\alpha_{w}(G)+(1-\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G}),

so
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=w^{\top}A_{H}w\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+2\varepsilon\,\alpha_{w}(G)-\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+\varepsilon(2\alpha_{w}(G)-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
.
Since
\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\geq 0
, we obtain the cleaner bound

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\leq 2\varepsilon\alpha_{w}(G)
.

Part (b).
From the proof of Theorem
3.2
:

v_{1}^{\top}A_{H}v_{1}\geq-2\varepsilon\alpha(G)+(1+\varepsilon)\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-\varepsilon(2\alpha(G)-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})).

So
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-2\varepsilon\alpha(G)+\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-2\varepsilon\alpha(G)
.
∎

6
Probabilistic Bounds via Matrix Concentration

The deterministic bound
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
treats the
perturbation
A_{H}-A_{G}
as adversarial. When
H
is constructed via SS sampling,
we can exploit the randomness to obtain high-probability bounds.

6.1
Matrix Bernstein Operator Norm Bound

Lemma 6.1

(Effective resistance bounds for edges)
.

Let
G
be a connected unweighted graph with maximum degree
\Delta
.
For every edge
e=(u,v)\in E
:

\frac{1}{\min(d_{u},d_{v})}\;\leq\;R_{e}\;\leq\;1.

In particular,
1/\Delta\leq R_{e}\leq 1
.

Proof.

Upper bound.

The effective resistance
R_{e}
equals the minimum energy of a unit
(u,v)
-flow.
Routing one unit of flow along the single edge
e
(which has unit conductance) gives
a feasible flow with energy
1
. Therefore
R_{e}\leq 1
.

Lower bound.

Apply a unit potential difference across
e
: set the voltage at
u
to
1
and
the voltage at
v
to
0
. By the maximum principle for harmonic functions on graphs,
every other vertex
w\in V\setminus\{u,v\}
has voltage
V(w)\in(0,1)
.
By Ohm’s law (each edge has unit resistance), the current flowing out of
u

along edge
(u,w)
is
V(u)-V(w)=1-V(w)
. Since
V(w)\in(0,1)
for every
neighbor
w
of
u
, each such current is positive and at most
1
. The total
current out of
u
(which equals
1/R_{e}
by definition of effective resistance) is

\frac{1}{R_{e}}=\sum_{w\in N(u)}\bigl(1-V(w)\bigr)\leq\sum_{w\in N(u)}1=d_{u}.

Therefore
R_{e}\geq 1/d_{u}
. By symmetry (applying the same argument at
v

with voltages reversed),
R_{e}\geq 1/d_{v}
. Combining:

R_{e}\geq 1/\min(d_{u},d_{v})\geq 1/\Delta
.
∎

Theorem 6.2

(Universal operator norm bound)
.

Let
H
be the output of the effective-resistance sampling scheme
(Definition
2.1
) with

q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
samples,
c\geq 4
, and
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
.
Then with probability at least
1-2n^{-1/2}
:

\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}\leq\frac{2\varepsilon\Delta}{\sqrt{c}}.

Proof.

Step 1: Independent sum decomposition.

Each of
q
i.i.d. draws selects edge
e_{k}=(u_{k},v_{k})
with probability

p_{e_{k}}=R_{e_{k}}/(n-1)
. Define

Y_{k}=\frac{1}{q\,p_{e_{k}}}\,P_{e_{k}},\qquad P_{e}=\mathbf{e}_{u}\mathbf{e}_{v}^{\top}+\mathbf{e}_{v}\mathbf{e}_{u}^{\top}.

Then
A_{H}=\sum_{k=1}^{q}Y_{k}
and
\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]=A_{G}/q
(by unbiasedness).
Setting
X_{k}=Y_{k}-\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]
:

A_{H}-A_{G}=\sum_{k=1}^{q}X_{k},\qquad\mathbb{E}[X_{k}]=0,\quad X_{1},\ldots,X_{q}\text{ i.i.d.}

Step 2: Almost-sure bound
B
.

By Lemma
6.1
,
1/\Delta\leq R_{e}\leq 1
for every edge
e
.

From these bounds:
p_{e}=R_{e}/(n-1)\leq 1/(n-1)
and

p_{e}\geq 1/((n-1)\Delta)
, so
p_{\min}\geq 1/((n-1)\Delta)
.
Define
B:=2(n-1)\Delta/q
.
Since
\|P_{e}\|_{2}=1
, we have
\|Y_{k}\|_{2}=1/(qp_{e_{k}})
.
By the triangle inequality,

\|X_{k}\|_{2}\leq\|Y_{k}\|_{2}+\|\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]\|_{2}=\frac{1}{qp_{e_{k}}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}{q}.

Now
1/(qp_{e_{k}})\leq(n-1)\Delta/q
and
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})/q\leq\Delta/q\leq(n-1)\Delta/q

(using
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\leq\Delta
and
n-1\geq 1
), so

\|X_{k}\|_{2}\leq\frac{(n-1)\Delta}{q}+\frac{(n-1)\Delta}{q}=B.

Step 3: Variance proxy
v
.

Since
X_{k}=Y_{k}-\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]
, we have

\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]\preceq\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}^{2}]
.
Using
P_{e}^{2}=\mathbf{e}_{u}\mathbf{e}_{u}^{\top}+\mathbf{e}_{v}\mathbf{e}_{v}^{\top}
:

\mathbb{E}[Y_{1}^{2}]=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{e}\frac{1}{p_{e}}(\mathbf{e}_{u}\mathbf{e}_{u}^{\top}+\mathbf{e}_{v}\mathbf{e}_{v}^{\top}).

This is diagonal with
(i,i)
entry
(n-1)/(q^{2})\cdot\sum_{e\ni i}1/R_{e}
.
Using
R_{e}\geq 1/\Delta
:
\sum_{e\ni i}1/R_{e}\leq d_{i}\cdot\Delta\leq\Delta^{2}
, so

\sigma^{2}:=q\,\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]\|_{2}\leq v:=\frac{(n-1)\Delta^{2}}{q}.

Substituting
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil\geq cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}
:

v\leq\frac{(n-1)\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{cn\log n}<\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{c\log n}.

(1)

Step 4: Matrix Bernstein application.

By the Matrix Bernstein inequality
[
18
]
: for i.i.d. mean-zero
symmetric
n\times n
matrices with
\|X_{k}\|_{2}\leq B
a.s.,

\Pr\!\left[\left\|\textstyle\sum_{k}X_{k}\right\|_{2}\geq t\right]\leq 2n\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+Bt/3}\right).

Set
t=2\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}
. We verify the two key quantities.

Ratio
t^{2}/(2v)
.

\frac{t^{2}}{2v}=\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/c}{2\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/(c\log n)}=2\log n.

Ratio
Bt/3
vs.
v
.

Recall
B=2(n-1)\Delta/q
and
v=(n-1)\Delta^{2}/q
, so

\frac{Bt}{3v}=\frac{2(n-1)\Delta\cdot 2\varepsilon\Delta/(q\sqrt{c})}{3(n-1)\Delta^{2}/q}=\frac{4\varepsilon}{3\sqrt{c}}.

For
\varepsilon\leq 1/2
and
c\geq 4
:
Bt/(3v)\leq 4/(3\cdot 2\cdot 2)=1/3
.
Therefore
\sigma^{2}+Bt/3\leq v+v/3=(4/3)v
, and

\frac{t^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+Bt/3}\geq\frac{t^{2}/2}{(4/3)v}=\frac{3}{4}\cdot\frac{t^{2}}{2v}=\frac{3}{4}\cdot 2\log n=\frac{3}{2}\log n.

The failure probability is therefore at most

2n\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{3}{2}\log n\right)=2n\cdot n^{-3/2}=2n^{-1/2}.\qed

Corollary 6.3

(Eigenvalue distortion bound)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
6.2
, with probability at least

1-2n^{-1/2}
:

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\frac{2\varepsilon\Delta}{\sqrt{c}}.

Proof.

By Weyl’s inequality,
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}
.
∎

6.2
Second-Order Resolvent Remainder Bound

When the spectral gap
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\lambda_{1}-\lambda_{2}
is large
relative to the perturbation, we can refine the bound using the known eigenvector
v_{1}
.

Theorem 6.4

(Resolvent remainder bound)
.

Let
\Phi=A_{H}-A_{G}
and suppose
\|\Phi\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
. Then:

0\leq(\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))-v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}\leq\frac{\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}}.

In particular,
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+v_{1}^{\top}\Phi v_{1}
always.

The condition
\|\Phi\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
holds with probability at least

1-2n^{-1/2}
(by Theorem
6.2
) whenever

4\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
.

Proof.

Write
A_{H}=A_{G}+\Phi
in the eigenbasis
\{v_{1},v_{2},\ldots,v_{n}\}
of
A_{G}
.
The eigenvalue equation
(A_{G}+\Phi)u=\lambda_{1}(A_{H})u
decomposes into
a
1\times 1
block coupled to the
(n-1)
-dimensional
orthogonal complement. Eliminating the complement via the Schur complement gives:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=\lambda_{1}+\Phi_{11}+\mathbf{e}^{\top}\!\left(\lambda_{1}(A_{H})I-\Lambda_{\perp}-\Phi_{\perp}\right)^{-1}\!\mathbf{e}

where
\Phi_{11}=v_{1}^{\top}\Phi v_{1}
,

\mathbf{e}=(v_{2}^{\top}\Phi v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}^{\top}\Phi v_{1})^{\top}
,

\Lambda_{\perp}=\mathrm{diag}(\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{n})
,
and
\Phi_{\perp}
is the restriction of
\Phi
to the orthogonal complement
(cf.
Stewart and Sun 1990
, Chapter V).

When
\|\Phi\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})\geq\lambda_{1}-\|\Phi\|_{2}>\lambda_{2}+\|\Phi\|_{2}\geq\lambda_{j}+\|\Phi_{\perp}\|_{2}

for all
j\geq 2
. The resolvent is therefore positive definite with minimum
eigenvalue
\geq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}>0
.
Thus:

0\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}-\Phi_{11}\leq\frac{\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}}\leq\frac{\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}}

where the last step uses
\|\mathbf{e}\|^{2}=\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}-\Phi_{11}^{2}\leq\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}
.
∎

Proposition 6.5

(Expected perturbation variance)
.

Under SS sampling with
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
:

\mathbb{E}\!\left[\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}\right]\leq\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{c\log n}.

Proof.

Since
\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{q}X_{k}
with
X_{k}
i.i.d. and mean-zero,

\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{\top}\Phi]=q\,\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]
. Therefore

\mathbb{E}[\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}]=v_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[\Phi^{\top}\Phi]v_{1}=q\cdot v_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]v_{1}\leq q\,\|\mathbb{E}[X_{1}^{2}]\|_{2}\leq(n-1)\Delta^{2}/q<\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/(c\log n)
.
∎

7
Distortion Bound Under Delocalization

We now prove a refined distortion bound
for graphs satisfying two structural hypotheses: eigenvector delocalization and a
sufficiently large spectral gap. These conditions hold for dense Erdős–Rényi
graphs,
d
-regular expanders, and balanced stochastic block models.

7.1
Scalar Concentration of the First-Order Term

Lemma 7.1

(First-order perturbation concentration)
.

Let
G
be
K
-delocalized and let
H
be the SS sparsifier with

q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
,
c\geq 4
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
.
There exists a threshold
n_{0}=n_{0}(K,c)
such that for all
n\geq n_{0}
,
with probability at least
1-2n^{-3/2}
:

|v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}|\;\leq\;\frac{4K\,\varepsilon\,\Delta\,\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{cn}}.

Proof.

Write
\Phi=\sum_{k=1}^{q}X_{k}
with
X_{k}
i.i.d. mean-zero.
Define the scalar random variables
Z_{k}:=v_{1}^{\top}X_{k}v_{1}
. These are
i.i.d. with
\mathbb{E}[Z_{k}]=0
, and
v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}=\sum_{k=1}^{q}Z_{k}
.

Almost-sure bound.

Since
X_{k}=Y_{k}-\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]
with
Y_{k}=P_{e_{k}}/(q\,p_{e_{k}})
, and

v_{1}^{\top}P_{e}v_{1}=2v_{1,u}v_{1,v}
for edge
e=(u,v)
:

|Z_{k}|\leq|v_{1}^{\top}Y_{k}v_{1}|+|v_{1}^{\top}\mathbb{E}[Y_{k}]v_{1}|=\frac{2|v_{1,u_{k}}\,v_{1,v_{k}}|}{q\,p_{e_{k}}}+\frac{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}{q}.

Using
\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq K/\sqrt{n}
,
1/p_{e_{k}}\leq(n-1)\Delta

(since
R_{e_{k}}\geq 1/\Delta
gives
p_{e_{k}}\geq 1/((n-1)\Delta)
), and

\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\leq\Delta
:

|Z_{k}|\leq\frac{2K^{2}(n-1)\Delta}{nq}+\frac{\Delta}{q}\leq\frac{(2K^{2}+1)\Delta}{q}=:b.

Variance.

\displaystyle\Var\!\left(\sum_{k}Z_{k}\right)

\displaystyle=q\cdot\mathbb{E}[Z_{1}^{2}]\leq q\cdot\mathbb{E}[(v_{1}^{\top}Y_{1}v_{1})^{2}].

Expanding:

\mathbb{E}[(v_{1}^{\top}Y_{1}v_{1})^{2}]=\frac{1}{q^{2}}\sum_{e}\frac{(2v_{1,u}v_{1,v})^{2}}{p_{e}}=\frac{4(n-1)}{q^{2}}\sum_{e=(u,v)}\frac{v_{1,u}^{2}\,v_{1,v}^{2}}{R_{e}}.

Using
R_{e}\geq 1/\Delta
and the estimate

v_{1,u}^{2}v_{1,v}^{2}\leq\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}^{2}\cdot(v_{1,u}^{2}+v_{1,v}^{2})/2\leq(K^{2}/(2n))(v_{1,u}^{2}+v_{1,v}^{2})
:

\displaystyle\sum_{e}\frac{v_{1,u}^{2}v_{1,v}^{2}}{R_{e}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{K^{2}\Delta}{2n}\sum_{e}(v_{1,u}^{2}+v_{1,v}^{2})=\frac{K^{2}\Delta}{2n}\sum_{i}d_{i}\,v_{1,i}^{2}=\frac{K^{2}\Delta\,\alpha(G)}{2n}\leq\frac{K^{2}\Delta^{2}}{2n}.

Therefore:

\sigma^{2}:=\Var\!\left(\sum_{k}Z_{k}\right)\leq q\cdot\frac{4(n-1)}{q^{2}}\cdot\frac{K^{2}\Delta^{2}}{2n}=\frac{2K^{2}(n-1)\Delta^{2}}{qn}\leq\frac{2K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{cn\log n}=:U.

Bernstein application.

By the scalar Bernstein inequality:

\Pr\!\left[\left|\sum_{k}Z_{k}\right|\geq t\right]\leq 2\exp\!\left(-\frac{t^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+bt/3}\right).

Set
t=4K\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{cn}
. We verify the exponent.

Main term.

Since the numerator
t^{2}=16K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}\log n/(cn)
and

2U=4K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/(cn\log n)
:

\frac{t^{2}}{2U}=\frac{16K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}\log n/(cn)}{4K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/(cn\log n)}=4(\log n)^{2}.

Correction term.

\frac{bt}{3}=\frac{(2K^{2}+1)\Delta}{3q}\cdot\frac{4K\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{cn}}.

Substituting
q\geq cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}
:

\frac{bt}{3}\leq\frac{4K(2K^{2}+1)\varepsilon^{3}\Delta^{2}}{3c^{3/2}n^{3/2}(\log n)^{1/2}}.

We need
bt/3\leq U
(the proved upper bound on
\sigma^{2}
), i.e.,

\frac{4K(2K^{2}+1)\varepsilon^{3}\Delta^{2}}{3c^{3/2}n^{3/2}(\log n)^{1/2}}\leq\frac{2K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{cn\log n}.

Canceling
\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}
from both sides and rearranging:

\sqrt{\frac{n}{\log n}}\;\geq\;\frac{2(2K^{2}+1)\,\varepsilon}{3K\sqrt{c}}.

For
K=O(1)
,
\varepsilon\leq 1/2
,
c\geq 4
, this holds whenever

n\geq n_{0}(K,c)
for an explicit threshold depending only on
K
and
c
.
When this holds,
bt/3\leq U
, so
\sigma^{2}+bt/3\leq U+U=2U
.
Therefore:

\frac{t^{2}/2}{\sigma^{2}+bt/3}\geq\frac{t^{2}/2}{2U}=\frac{t^{2}}{4U}=2(\log n)^{2}\geq 2\log n,

giving failure probability at most
2e^{-2\log n}=2n^{-2}\leq 2n^{-3/2}
.
∎

Remark 7.2

(Role of the correction term)
.

The Bernstein correction
bt/3
is of order

O(K^{3}\varepsilon^{3}\Delta^{2}/(c^{3/2}n^{3/2}\sqrt{\log n}))
,
which is dominated by the variance upper bound
U=O(K^{2}\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/(cn\log n))

whenever
\sqrt{n/\log n}\geq C(2K^{2}+1)\varepsilon/(K\sqrt{c})

for an explicit constant
C=2/3
. This holds for all
n\geq n_{0}(K,c)

where
n_{0}
depends only on
K
and
c
(not on the graph).
For example, with
K=1
,
c=4
,
\varepsilon=1/2
: the condition becomes

\sqrt{n/\log n}\geq 1/2
, which holds for
n\geq 2
.

7.2
Remainder Control via the Operator Norm

Lemma 7.3

(Resolvent remainder under delocalization)
.

Let
G
be
K
-delocalized with spectral gap
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
. Under the SS
sampling scheme with
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
,
c\geq 4
,

\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
, suppose that
8\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
.
Then with probability at least
1-2n^{-1/2}
:

0\;\leq\;(\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))-v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}\;\leq\;\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{c\,\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.4
, when
\|\Phi\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
:

0\leq(\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))-v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}\leq\frac{\|\Phi\,v_{1}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}}.

By Theorem
6.2
,
\|\Phi\|_{2}\leq 2\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}

with probability at least
1-2n^{-1/2}
. The hypothesis

8\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
ensures

2\|\Phi\|_{2}\leq 4\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
,
so the resolvent condition
\|\Phi\|_{2}<\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
is satisfied.

For the numerator:
\|\Phi\,v_{1}\|^{2}\leq\|\Phi\|_{2}^{2}\leq 4\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/c
.

For the denominator:

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}\geq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-4\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\geq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2=\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2
,
where the second inequality uses the hypothesis. Therefore:

\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/c}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-4\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}}\leq\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/c}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}/2}=\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{c\,\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}}.\qed

7.3
Main Theorem

Theorem 7.4

(Delocalized distortion bound)
.

Let
G
be a connected,
K
-delocalized graph with spectral gap

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-\lambda_{2}
. Let
H
be the SS sparsifier with

q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
samples,
c\geq 4
,
\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
.
Suppose that
8\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
and
n\geq n_{0}(K,c)

(the threshold from Lemma
7.1
). Then with probability at
least
1-2n^{-1/2}-2n^{-3/2}\geq 1-4n^{-1/2}
:

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\;\leq\;\frac{4K\,\varepsilon\,\Delta\,\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{cn}}\;+\;\frac{8\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}}{c\,\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}}.

Proof.

By Theorem
6.4
:

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=v_{1}^{\top}\Phi\,v_{1}+R,\qquad 0\leq R\leq\frac{\|\Phi v_{1}\|^{2}}{\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}-2\|\Phi\|_{2}}.

Therefore

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq|v_{1}^{\top}\Phi v_{1}|+R
.
Applying Lemma
7.1
and Lemma
7.3
,
by a union bound the stated inequality holds with probability
at least
1-2n^{-3/2}-2n^{-1/2}\geq 1-4n^{-1/2}
.
∎

7.4
Corollaries for Specific Graph Families

The following corollaries instantiate Theorem
7.4
for specific
random graph models by importing standard spectral properties from the random
matrix theory literature. In each case, we explicitly identify which facts are
proved in this paper (the sparsification distortion bounds) and which are drawn
from prior work (spectral gap concentration, eigenvector delocalization, and degree
concentration for the underlying random graph model).

Corollary 7.5

(Erdős–Rényi graphs)
.

Let
G\sim G(n,p)
with
np/\log n\to\infty
(i.e.,
p\gg(\log n)/n
).
Let
H
be the SS sparsifier with
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil
,
c\geq 4
,

\varepsilon\in(0,1/2]
. Then with probability
1-o(1)
over both
G
and
the SS sampling:

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\;\leq\;O\!\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{np\log n}\right)\;+\;O\!\left(\varepsilon^{2}\,np\right).

In particular, if additionally
\varepsilon=O\!\left(\sqrt{\log n/(np)}\right)
,
then the second term is absorbed and

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|=O\!\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{np\log n}\right)=O\!\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n}\right)
.

Proof.

We combine Theorem
7.4
(proved above) with standard spectral
properties of
G(n,p)
drawn from the random matrix theory literature.
The following facts hold simultaneously with probability
1-o(1)
over the draw
of
G\sim G(n,p)
in the regime
np/\log n\to\infty
:

(i)

Maximum degree concentration
(by Chernoff bounds and the union bound over

n
vertices):

\Delta=(1+o(1))np
. This requires
np\gg\log n
so that the fluctuation

O(\sqrt{np\log n})
is
o(np)
.

(ii)

Leading eigenvalue
:
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=(1+o(1))np
.
This follows from (i) and the classical bounds
\bar{d}\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\leq\Delta

for connected graphs, where the average degree
\bar{d}=(1+o(1))np
by the
law of large numbers.

(iii)

Spectral gap
:
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=np(1-o(1))
.
For
p
bounded away from
0
and
1
, the semicircle law gives

\lambda_{2}(A_{G})=(2+o(1))\sqrt{np(1-p)}

[
8
]
,
so
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=np(1-o(1))
.
For the sparser regime
np/\log n\to\infty
with
p\to 0
, we use the weaker
but sufficient bound from
Le et al. 2017
:

\|A_{G}-\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]\|_{2}=O(\sqrt{np})
w.h.p. Since the rank-one expectation matrix

\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]=p(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-I)
has top eigenvalue

p(n-1)=(1-o(1))np
and all other eigenvalues equal to
-p
,
Weyl’s inequality gives
\lambda_{2}(A_{G})\leq-p+O(\sqrt{np})=O(\sqrt{np})
.
Therefore
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-\lambda_{2}\geq np-O(\sqrt{np})=np(1-o(1))
, which is all that is needed for
Theorem
7.4
.

(iv)

Perron eigenvector delocalization
:
K=O(1)
.
General eigenvector delocalization results
[
9
]
give

\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}\leq n^{-1/2+\varepsilon}
for any
\varepsilon>0
(i.e.,
K=n^{o(1)}
)
down to the critical scale
np=O(\log n)
.
For the
Perron eigenvector
specifically, the stronger bound
K=O(1)

follows from the entrywise eigenvector analysis of
Abbe et al. 2020
.
Because the loopless model gives
\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]=p(\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-I)
,
which is
full
rank, we apply their Corollary 2.1 to the diagonally shifted
matrix
M:=A_{G}+pI
. Its expectation
\mathbb{E}[M]=p\,\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}
has
exact rank one and the same eigenvectors as
A_{G}
, so the leading eigenvector
v_{1}

is the rank-one (
r=1
) instance of their Corollary 2.1, with eigengap

\Delta^{*}=\Theta(np)
, incoherent population eigenvector

\|U^{*}\|_{2\to\infty}=1/\sqrt{n}
, and independent centered-Bernoulli noise

W=A_{G}-\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]
satisfying
\|W\|_{2}=O(\sqrt{np})

[
13
]
.
In the regime
np/\log n\to\infty
the noise-to-signal ratio

\|W\|_{2}/\Delta^{*}=O(1/\sqrt{np})\to 0
, so their entrywise approximation gives

v_{1,i}=(1+o(1))/\sqrt{n}
uniformly, hence
K=O(1)
.

Application of Theorem
7.4
(proved in this paper).

Properties (i)–(iv) hold simultaneously with probability
1-o(1)
over the
draw of
G
.
Conditionally on this event, Theorem
7.4
gives the distortion bound
with probability at least
1-4n^{-1/2}
over the SS sampling.
Composing: the overall failure probability is
o(1)+4n^{-1/2}=o(1)
.

Given (i)–(iv), the hypothesis
8\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c}\leq\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}
is
satisfied for all sufficiently large
n
whenever
8\varepsilon/\sqrt{c}<1
(strictly);
in particular, it holds for
\varepsilon<1/4
and
c=4
, or for
\varepsilon\leq 1/4
and
c>4
.
Applying Theorem
7.4
with
\Delta=(1+o(1))np
,
K=O(1)
,

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=(1-o(1))np
:

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\leq O\!\left(\frac{\varepsilon\,np\sqrt{\log n}}{\sqrt{n}}\right)+O(\varepsilon^{2}\,np)=O(\varepsilon\sqrt{np\log n})+O(\varepsilon^{2}\,np).

The simplification of the first term uses
\Delta=\Theta(np)=O(n)
(since
p\leq 1
),
so
\varepsilon\,np\,\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{n}=\varepsilon\sqrt{(np)^{2}\log n/n}=\varepsilon\sqrt{np\cdot(np/n)\cdot\log n}\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{np\log n}=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n}
.
The second term is
O(\varepsilon^{2}\,np)
. For
p
bounded away from zero (dense regime),

np=\Theta(n)
and the second term is
O(\varepsilon^{2}n)
. This is absorbed by the first
term
O(\varepsilon\sqrt{n\log n})
only when
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt{\log n/n})
.
For general
p
, the absorption condition is
\varepsilon^{2}\,np\leq C\varepsilon\sqrt{np\log n}
,
i.e.,
\varepsilon\leq C^{\prime}\sqrt{\log n/(np)}
.
∎

Remark 7.6

(Regime of validity)
.

The hypothesis
np/\log n\to\infty
is stronger than connectivity. For
constant
\varepsilon
in the dense regime (
p=\Theta(1)
), the distortion is

O(\varepsilon^{2}n)
(the resolvent remainder dominates); the simplified bound

O(\varepsilon\sqrt{n\log n})
holds only when
\varepsilon=O(\sqrt{\log n/n})
.

Corollary 7.7

(

-regular graphs)
.

Let
G
be a connected
d

vertices with

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=d-\lambda_{2}
. Let

,
then with probability at least

:

Apply Theorem
7.4
with

and

.
∎

Lemma 7.8

(Spectral properties of the balanced SBM)
.

Let
G\sim\mathrm{SBM}(n,k,p,r)
be a balanced
k
-community SBM with

k=O(1)
,
np/(k\log n)\to\infty
, and
r=\Theta(p)
.
Then with probability
1-o(1)
:

(i)

\Delta=\Theta(np)
and
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\Theta(np)
.

(ii)

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\Theta(np)=\Theta(\Delta)
.

(iii)

G
is
K
-delocalized with
K=O(\sqrt{k})=O(1)
.

Proof.

Population quantities.

The population adjacency matrix
\bar{A}=\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]
for the loopless SBM has zero
diagonal, so
\bar{A}=(p-r)\,\mathrm{blkdiag}(J_{n/k},\ldots,J_{n/k})+rJ-pI
,
where
J
is the all-ones matrix and
J_{n/k}
are block all-ones matrices.
The
-pI
term makes
\bar{A}
full rank (all eigenvalues are shifted by
-p

relative to the rank-
k
matrix
\bar{A}+pI
). However, the spectral gap
is unaffected by diagonal shifts: the top two eigenvalues of
\bar{A}
are

\bar{\lambda}_{1}=(n/k)(p+(k-1)r)-p
and

\bar{\lambda}_{2}=(n/k)(p-r)-p
(with multiplicity
k-1
),
giving population spectral gap

\bar{\delta}_{\mathrm{gap}}=\bar{\lambda}_{1}-\bar{\lambda}_{2}=nr=\Theta(np)
.

Transfer to actual graph.

Under
np/(k\log n)\to\infty
and
r=\Theta(p)
, the spectral norm of the
centered matrix satisfies
\|A_{G}-\bar{A}\|_{2}=O(\sqrt{np})
with high
probability
[
13
]
(the hypothesis
np/(k\log n)\to\infty

with
k=O(1)
implies
np/\log n\to\infty
, which is stronger than the

np\geq C\log n
threshold needed for spectral-norm concentration).
Since the perturbation
O(\sqrt{np})
is
o(np)=o(\bar{\delta}_{\mathrm{gap}})
,
Weyl’s inequality gives

\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=(1-o(1))\bar{\delta}_{\mathrm{gap}}=\Theta(np)=\Theta(\Delta)
.
Similarly,
\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=(1+o(1))\bar{\lambda}_{1}=\Theta(np)
and

\Delta=\Theta(np)
by degree concentration (Chernoff bounds and union bound).
This establishes (i) and (ii).

Eigenvector delocalization (iii).

Property (iii) requires an
entrywise
(
\ell^{\infty}
) eigenvector bound, not the

\ell^{2}
subspace closeness supplied by Davis–Kahan. We obtain it from the entrywise
eigenvector analysis of
Abbe et al. 2020
, applied to the

leading
eigenvector of the diagonally shifted matrix
M:=A_{G}+pI
. The shift
is needed because
\mathbb{E}[A_{G}]=\bar{A}
is full rank for the loopless model, whereas

\bar{M}=\bar{A}+pI=(p-r)\,\mathrm{blkdiag}(J_{n/k},\ldots,J_{n/k})+rJ
has exact
rank
k=O(1)
, with population eigenvectors piecewise constant on the
k
balanced
communities (entries
\Theta(1/\sqrt{n})
, hence incoherent), Perron eigengap

\Delta^{*}=nr=\Theta(np)
, and independent bounded centered-Bernoulli noise

W=A_{G}-\bar{A}
with
\|W\|_{2}=O(\sqrt{np})

[
13
]
. In the
regime
np/(k\log n)\to\infty
with
k=O(1)
and
r=\Theta(p)
the noise-to-signal
ratio
\|W\|_{2}/\Delta^{*}=O(1/\sqrt{np})\to 0
, so Corollary 2.1 yields

\|v_{1}\|_{\infty}=O(\sqrt{k/n})
, hence
K=O(\sqrt{k})=O(1)
. We apply their general
low-rank corollary to the leading eigenvector; we do not invoke their Section 3.2
stochastic-block-model analysis, which concerns the
second
eigenvector.
∎

Corollary 7.9

(Balanced stochastic block model)
.

Let
G\sim\mathrm{SBM}(n,k,p,r)
be a balanced
k
-community SBM with

n/k
vertices per community, intra-community edge probability
p
, and
inter-community probability
r<p
, where
k=O(1)
,
np/(k\log n)\to\infty
,
and
r=\Theta(p)
.
Then with probability
1-o(1)
(over both
G
and the SS sampling):

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\;\leq\;O\!\left(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n}\right)\;+\;O\!\left(\varepsilon^{2}\,\Delta\right),

where

is the typical maximum degree.
The simplified form
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|=O(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n})
holds when

\varepsilon=O(\sqrt{\log n/\Delta})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
7.8
, with probability
1-o(1)
over the draw of
G
,
the graph satisfies:

\Delta=\Theta(np)
,
\delta_{\mathrm{gap}}=\Theta(\Delta)
, and

K
-delocalization with
K=O(1)
.
Conditionally on this event, Theorem
7.4
gives the stated bound
with probability at least
1-4n^{-1/2}
over the SS sampling.
Composing: the overall failure probability is
o(1)+4n^{-1/2}=o(1)
.

The first term is

O(K\,\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{n})=O(\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{n})
.
Since
\Delta=\Theta(np)=O(n)
(because
p\leq 1
), we have

\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{n}=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\cdot(\Delta/n)\cdot\log n}\leq\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n}

The second term is
O(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta^{2}/\delta_{\mathrm{gap}})=O(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta)
.
∎

Remark 7.10

(SBM regime limitation)
.

The condition

restricts this corollary to SBMs with relatively dense

(well-separated communities), the spectral

remains valid unconditionally.

Remark 7.11

(Scope of the bound)
.

Theorem

applies to all graph
families satisfying: (i)

Perron eigenvector localizes on high-degree vertices (e.g., heavy-tailed power-law graphs
with
\beta<3
) is an important direction for future work
(see Section
11
).

8
Lower Bounds and Tightness

We now establish that the deterministic bounds are tight up to the
Perron eigenvalue factor, and characterize the exact worst-case distortion for important
graph classes.

8.1
Tightness for Regular Graphs

Proposition 8.1

(Deterministic bound is tight for regular graphs)
.

For any connected
d
-regular graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
,
there exists a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier
H
(in fact, a reweighting sparsifier)
such that

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|=\varepsilon\,d=\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})),

matching the upper bound of Theorem
3.4
.

Proof.

Set
H
to be the graph
G
with every edge weight equal to
(1+\varepsilon)
.
Then
L_{H}=(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
, so
H
is a valid
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier.
Since
A_{H}=(1+\varepsilon)A_{G}
, we have
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=(1+\varepsilon)d
,
so
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\varepsilon d=\varepsilon(2d-d)
.
∎

8.2
Lower Bound for Heterogeneous Graphs

Proposition 8.2

(Star graph: tight characterization)
.

For the star
K_{1,n-1}
(
n\geq 3
) and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
, the worst-case
distortion over all
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifiers retaining all edges is exactly

\max_{H}|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{K_{1,n-1}})|=\varepsilon\sqrt{n-1}=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta},

while the deterministic upper bound gives
\varepsilon(2(n-1)-\sqrt{n-1})\approx 2\varepsilon\Delta
.
The gap is a factor of
2\sqrt{\Delta}-1=\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.3
, any
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian
sparsifier retaining all edges has edge weights
w_{j}\in[1-\varepsilon,1+\varepsilon]
for

j=1,\ldots,n-1
. For a weighted star with weights
(w_{j})
, the nonzero eigenvalues
of
A_{H}
are
\pm\sqrt{\sum_{j}w_{j}^{2}}

(the eigenvalue equation gives
\lambda^{2}=\sum w_{j}^{2}
).

Maximizing: the unique maximizer
is
w_{j}=1+\varepsilon
for all
j
, giving
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{n-1}
. Hence

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\varepsilon\sqrt{n-1}=\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta}
.
∎

Theorem 8.3

(General lower bound)
.

For any connected graph
G
and any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
:

\sup_{H\in\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(G)}|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\;\geq\;\varepsilon\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G}),

where
\mathcal{S}_{\varepsilon}(G)
denotes the set of all
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifiers
of
G
.

Moreover, equality holds for the uniform reweighting
H_{\pm}=(1\pm\varepsilon)\cdot G
.

Proof.

The uniform scaling
H_{+}
with all edge weights
(1+\varepsilon)
satisfies

L_{H}=(1+\varepsilon)L_{G}
. Since
A_{H}=(1+\varepsilon)A_{G}
, we get

\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
.
∎

Corollary 8.4

(Tightness gap)
.

The ratio between the deterministic upper bound (Theorem
3.4
) and the
reweighting lower bound (Theorem
8.3
) is exactly

\frac{\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))}{\varepsilon\,\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}=\frac{2\Delta}{\lambda_{1}(A_{G})}-1.

This ratio is
1
for regular graphs (the bound is tight) and

\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
for star-like graphs. The gap arises because the
deterministic proof bounds the degree and Laplacian perturbations independently,
but in actual sparsifiers these are correlated.

Open Problem 8.5
.

Is there a connected graph
G
and a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian
sparsifier
H
(with some edges deleted) such that
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|=\omega(\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
?

8.3
Lower Bound for SS Sampling on the Star

Proposition 8.6

(Expected SS distortion on the star)
.

For the star
K_{1,n-1}
under SS sampling with
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil

samples:

0\;\leq\;\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\;\leq\;\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{n-1}}{2c\log n}\!\left(1+O(1/n)\right).

In particular,
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=O(\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\Delta}/\log n)
.

Proof.

For
K_{1,n-1}
, all effective resistances are
R_{e}=1
, so

p_{e}=1/(n-1)
for all
e
. Edge
e
is sampled
k_{e}
times where

(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1})\sim\mathrm{Multinomial}(q;1/(n-1),\ldots,1/(n-1))
,
and receives weight
w_{e}=k_{e}(n-1)/q
.

The spectral radius is
\lambda_{1}(A_{H})=\sqrt{\sum_{e}w_{e}^{2}}=\frac{n-1}{q}\sqrt{\sum_{e}k_{e}^{2}}
.
Since
k_{e}\sim\mathrm{Binomial}(q,1/(n-1))
:

\mathbb{E}[k_{e}^{2}]=\Var(k_{e})+(\mathbb{E}[k_{e}])^{2}=\frac{q(n-2)}{(n-1)^{2}}+\frac{q^{2}}{(n-1)^{2}}=\frac{q(q+n-2)}{(n-1)^{2}}.

Therefore

\mathbb{E}[\sum_{e}k_{e}^{2}]=q(q+n-2)/(n-1)
,
and

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})^{2}]=\frac{(n-1)^{2}}{q^{2}}\cdot\frac{q(q+n-2)}{n-1}=(n-1)\!\left(1+\frac{n-2}{q}\right).

Lower bound.

Since
A\mapsto\lambda_{1}(A)
is convex and
\mathbb{E}[A_{H}]=A_{G}
, Jensen’s inequality gives

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]\geq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
.

Upper bound.

Since
x\mapsto\sqrt{x}
is concave, Jensen’s inequality gives

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]=\mathbb{E}[\sqrt{\lambda_{1}(A_{H})^{2}}]\leq\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})^{2}]}
.
Using
\sqrt{1+x}\leq 1+x/2
for
x\geq 0

(which follows from
(1+x/2)^{2}=1+x+x^{2}/4\geq 1+x
):

\sqrt{\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})^{2}]}=\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\sqrt{1+\frac{n-2}{q}}\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\!\left(1+\frac{n-2}{2q}\right).

Since
q=\lceil cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}\rceil\geq cn\log n/\varepsilon^{2}
, we have

1/q\leq\varepsilon^{2}/(cn\log n)
, giving

\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\!\left(1+\frac{n-2}{2q}\right)\leq\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{n-1}\,(n-2)}{2cn\log n}.

Since
(n-2)/n=1-2/n
, the upper bound is

\lambda_{1}(A_{G})+\frac{\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{n-1}}{2c\log n}(1+O(1/n))
.
∎

Remark 8.7
.

SS sampling on the star yields expected distortion at most

O(\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{\Delta}/\log n)
, strictly smaller than both the
deterministic bound
O(\varepsilon\Delta)
and the reweighting worst case
\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta}
.
The exact computation
\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})^{2}]-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})^{2}=\varepsilon^{2}(n-1)/(c\log n)\cdot(1+o(1))

strongly suggests the asymptotic

\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})\sim\varepsilon^{2}\sqrt{n-1}/(2c\log n)
;
the matching lower bound would follow from showing

\Var(\lambda_{1}(A_{H}))=o((\mathbb{E}[\lambda_{1}(A_{H})]-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))^{2})
, which we conjecture holds but do not
prove here.

9
Comparison with Entry-wise Sparsification

Achlioptas–McSherry
[
2
]
and related work
on matrix sparsification approach the problem from a different angle. We now make the
comparison precise.

Proposition 9.1

(Entry-wise sparsification bound (after Achlioptas–McSherry))
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
m
edges and maximum degree
\Delta
.
Suppose
H
is constructed by independently retaining
each entry of
A_{G}
with probability
p=s/n
(for a parameter
1\leq s\leq n
)
and rescaling by
n/s
. Then:

\left\|A_{H}-A_{G}\right\|_{2}=O\!\left(\sqrt{\frac{n\Delta\log n}{s}}+\frac{n\log n}{s}\right)

with probability at least
1-O(n^{-1})
.
For
s=\Omega(n\log n/\Delta)
, the first term dominates and
the bound simplifies to
O(\sqrt{n\Delta\log n/s})
; note that this simplified
regime requires
\Delta=\Omega(\log n)
to satisfy the constraint
s\leq n
.
The resulting sparsifier
H
has
\Theta(ms/n)
edges in expectation but does
not

preserve the Laplacian in the
(1\pm\varepsilon)
sense.

Proof.

Each entry
(A_{H})_{ij}
is an independent random variable equaling
A_{ij}/p
with
probability
p=s/n
and
0
otherwise. The centered matrix

A_{H}-A_{G}
has independent (up to symmetry) entries. For the
(i,j)
entry
with
A_{ij}=1
:

\Var\!\left[(A_{H})_{ij}-A_{ij}\right]=\frac{1}{p}(1-p)\cdot A_{ij}^{2}\leq\frac{n}{s}.

The variance per row is
\sum_{j}\Var[(A_{H})_{ij}-A_{ij}]\leq d_{i}\cdot n/s\leq\Delta n/s
.
Since the entries are bounded by
n/s
in absolute value, the Matrix Bernstein
inequality
[
18
,
3
]
gives

\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}\leq C\!\left(\sqrt{\Delta n/s\cdot\log n}+\frac{n\log n}{s}\right)

with probability at least
1-O(n^{-1})
, for an absolute constant
C>0
.
∎

Proposition 9.2

(Comparison of bounds)
.

For
dense
graphs with
m=\Theta(n\Delta)
and
\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\Theta(1)

(quasi-regular), we equalize the expected number of retained edges between the two
schemes. The SS scheme retains
q=\Theta(n\log n/\varepsilon^{2})
edges.
Entry-wise sampling with parameter
s
retains
\Theta(ms/n)=\Theta(\Delta\,s)
edges.
Equalizing:
s=\Theta(n\log n/(\Delta\varepsilon^{2}))
.
This requires
s\leq n
, i.e.,
\Delta\varepsilon^{2}\geq C\log n
for a constant
C
,
which holds for sufficiently dense graphs.

Under this matching:

(i)

SS Bernstein bound
(Theorem
6.2
):

\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}=O(\varepsilon\Delta/\sqrt{c})
.

(ii)

Entry-wise bound
(Proposition
9.1
):

\|A_{H}-A_{G}\|_{2}=O\!\left(\sqrt{n\Delta\log n/s}\right)=O(\varepsilon\Delta)
.

Thus, at equal edge count, the generic operator-norm bounds are of the same order.
However, for graphs satisfying the delocalization and spectral-gap hypotheses of
Theorem
7.4
, the SS
eigenvalue
distortion improves to

O(\varepsilon\Delta\sqrt{\log n}/\sqrt{n})+O(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta)
—with the first term a factor
of
\sqrt{n/\log n}
below the
operator-norm bound—while entry-wise sampling provides no analogous eigenvalue
refinement. Moreover, SS preserves the Laplacian while entry-wise sampling does not.

Remark 9.3

(Density requirement)
.

The comparison requires
\Delta\varepsilon^{2}\geq C\log n
for the edge budgets to match;
for sparser graphs, entry-wise sampling would need
s>n
. When the density condition
holds and Theorem
7.4
applies, the SS eigenvalue distortion is

O(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta\log n})+O(\varepsilon^{2}\Delta)
for quasi-regular dense graphs,
well below the generic
O(\varepsilon\Delta)
operator-norm level.

11
Discussion and Open Problems

11.1
Implications for Epidemic Modeling

These are the first analytic bounds on how the NIMFA
epidemic threshold surrogate
\tau_{\mathrm{mf}}=1/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
changes under
Spielman–Srivastava sparsification. For graphs with
\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=O(1)

(including
d
-regular graphs, dense Erdős–Rényi, and balanced SBM),
the relative distortion is
O(\varepsilon)
, confirming that spectral sparsification
safely preserves the mean-field threshold.

For heterogeneous networks with
\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})=\Theta(\sqrt{\Delta})
,
the relative distortion grows as
O(\varepsilon\sqrt{\Delta})
under the deterministic bound.
Practitioners should validate threshold-sensitive analyses against the unsparsified graph
when operating on networks with high degree heterogeneity.

11.2
Open Questions

The main question left open by this work concerns
edge deletion:

Open Problem
8.5
(Edge-deletion distortion).

Is there a connected graph
G
and a
(1\pm\varepsilon)
-Laplacian sparsifier

H
with some edges deleted such that
|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|=\omega(\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
?

Our lower bounds (Section
8
) show that

|\lambda_{1}(A_{H})-\lambda_{1}(A_{G})|\geq\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
is achievable via uniform reweighting,
and the deterministic upper bound
\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
is tight for
regular graphs. But the gap between
\varepsilon\lambda_{1}(A_{G})
and

\varepsilon(2\Delta-\lambda_{1}(A_{G}))
for heterogeneous graphs is a factor of

2\Delta/\lambda_{1}(A_{G})-1
, and it remains unknown whether edge-deleting
sparsifiers can exploit this gap. A resolution would determine whether the

\gamma(G)
-dependence in the deterministic bounds is an artifact of the proof
technique or a genuine phenomenon.

Further open directions:

(1)

Localized eigenvector regimes.

For graphs whose Perron eigenvector concentrates on high-degree vertices—such as
Chung–Lu power-law models with
\beta\in(5/2,3)
—the delocalization hypothesis
of Theorem
7.4
fails, and the deterministic bound

O(\varepsilon\Delta)
may be far from tight. Extending the probabilistic analysis to
this regime requires entrywise eigenvector perturbation results beyond
Davis–Kahan.

(2)

Removing the
\sqrt{\log n}
factor.

The
\sqrt{\log n}
factor in Theorem
7.4
arises from
Bernstein’s inequality. Is it necessary, or can it be removed?

(3)

Power-law networks with
\beta\leq 5/2
.

For
\beta\leq 5/2
, the spectral radius is no longer governed by a single hub’s
star neighborhood, and eigenvector localization has a different character.

11.3
Scope

The SS sparsifier produces
O(n\log n/\varepsilon^{2})
edges in expectation. This yields
genuine sparsification when
m\gg n\log n/\varepsilon^{2}
, which holds for dense graphs.
For sparse graphs with
m=O(n)
, the sampling budget can
exceed
m
, and the support of
H
may saturate near the full original edge set.
The deterministic bounds remain valid in this regime but are most practically useful
when actual edge reduction occurs.
