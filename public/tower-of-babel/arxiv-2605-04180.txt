MedFabric and EtHER : A Data-Centric Framework for Word-Level Fabrication Generation and Detection in Medical LLMs
Tung Sum Thomas, Kwok*, Qian, Qian*, Xiaofeng, Lin*, Dongxu, Zhang, Jun, Han, Zhichao, Yang, Davin, Hill, Tamer, Soliman, Sanjit Singh, Batra, Robert, Tillman, Guang, Cheng

Abstract
Large Language Models exhibit strong reasoning and semantic understanding capabilities but often hallucinate in domains that require expert knowledge, among which fabrications, the generation of factually incorrect yet fluent statements, pose the greatest risk in medical contexts. Existing medical hallucination datasets inadequately capture fabrication phenomena due to limited fabrication coverage, stylistic disparities between human and LLM-authored texts, and distributional drift during hallucinated sample synthesis. To address this, we propose a data-centric pipeline to generate realistic and word-level fabrications that preserve syntactic and stylistic fidelity while introducing subtle factual deviations, resulting in MedFabric . Building upon this dataset, we introduce EtHER , a modular word-level fabrication detector integrating Text2Table Decomposition, Word Masking and Filling and Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation to enhance factual alignment. Empirical results demonstrate that EtHER outperforms state-of-the-art detectors by over 15% on word-level fabrication benchmarks while maintaining consistent performance across structural similarities, offering a comprehensive framework for reliable and domain-specific factuality detection. Our results can be reproduced via the repository: https://anonymous.4open.science/r/medfabric-74D2/README.md .

Introduction

Large Language Models (‘LLM’s) have shaken up the world with its strong semantic understanding, in-context learning, and logical reasoning capabilities
[
13
]
. However, without domain-focused training, LLM commonly hallucinates in domains that require expertise knowledge
[
30
]
. LLM hallucinations consist of multiple forms, such as misinterpreting question, providing incomplete information and misusing concepts
[
7
]
, the most problematic of which being fabrication, which generates factually contradictory information
[
9
]
, especially in medical and legal fields which emphasize on factuality
[
15
]
, hence highlighting the need of fabrication detection.

We argue that training medical-specific hallucination detector (classifier) requires specialized datasets due to domain shift. Existing medical hallucination datasets are curated by gathering correct and wrong answers from different sources, e.g. Med-HALT and MedHallu
[
26
,
28
]
gather ground truths from human sources and generates hallucinations with LLMs. Other medical datasets gather both correct and wrong answers from human-written sources, e.g. MedMCQA and Medqa-USMILE gathers question, correct and wrong answers from multiple-choice questions of medical licensing exams
[
25
,
12
]
.

Nevertheless, existing hallucination training + detection pipelines exhibit two critical limitations, one on training data and the other on model. Training data does not mimic realistic LLM fabrication scenarios so that models trained on it overfit to confounding variables. Hallucinated samples often display distinct stylistic and structural patterns compared to their truthful counterparts
[
28
,
33
]
. This introduces confounding cues that detectors exploit, leading to spurious classification based on surface style rather than factual content.

On the other hand, existing detector models are fragile when stylistic artifacts are controlled. Existing detectors trained under data from general domain show an asymmetric prediction performance towards ground truth rather than fabricated samples in out-of-distribution (‘OOD’) medical samples. Specifically, embedding-based detectors tend to equate stylistic similarity with semantic accuracy
5
, while LLM-based evaluators depend heavily on prompt phrasing and thus exhibit inconsistent judgments across runs
[
8
]
.

These issues collectively produce asymmetric predictive behavior between factual and fabricated samples, motivating the development of a new hallucination training + detection pipeline containing (1) a data generation pipeline capable of producing stylistically consistent yet factually incorrect fabrications, and (2) a robust detection model framework that captures word-level fabrications with precision and reproducibility.

To address limitation in training data, we develop a data generation pipeline to create realistic and controllable
word-level fabrications
that mimics realistic LLM fabricating behaviors. Our method operates directly at the lexical level, preserving both syntax and writing style of authentic LLM outputs. This pipeline consists of three stages:
i)
rewriting human-authored ground truths into LLM-style counterparts to align stylistic distributions,
ii)
generating evidence-conditioned fabrications that introduce fine-grained factual deviations while maintaining contextual coherence, and
iii)
enforcing semantic and structural similarity constraints to ensure fabricated statements remain lexically close to their truthful counterparts. The resulting dataset,
MedFabric
, offers a challenging and realistic benchmark with high structural similarity between ground truth and fabricated samples, and subtle word difference that causes text semantic shifts, which then illustrates failure modes of existing detectors, both LLM- and embedding-based.

Regarding limitations on detecting model, we further introduce a modular word-level fabrication detector designed to address the shortcomings of existing systems, named ‘EvaluaTe Hallucination with TablE decomposition and woRd masking’
EtHER
. The proposed framework simplifies medical texts into in-distribution samples and leverage individual strength of embedding-based and LLM-based detectors collectively by integrating three complementary components:
i) a Text2Table Decomposition
module that transforms unstructured medical statements into structured entity known as description tables, hence improving semantic interpretability and reducing contextual ambiguity;
ii) a Word Masking and Filling
module that performs fine-grained factual alignment by reconstructing masked text segments with phrases retrieved from verified biomedical knowledge; and
iii) a Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation
module that combines embedding-based similarity metrics with LLM-based reasoning for stable, low-variance factuality judgments.

Our empirical results show that
EtHER
not only consistently outperform existing detectors on prediction performance in word-level fabrication data like
MedFabric
by about 15% overall, but also show robust and consistent prediction performance on existing dataset with lower structural similarity and its rewritten variations.

Contributions

This work establishes a unified paradigm for studying, detecting, and ultimately mitigating fabrication-type medical hallucinations in LLMs, paving the way toward trustworthy LLM systems for high-stakes medical applications and reducing workload of medical expertise in addressing patients consultations. To conclude, the contribution of this work includes:

•

Uncovering overlooked significance of stylistic pattern between ground truth and fabricated medical samples on detector training;

•

Suggesting a data generation pipeline to simulate realistic medical fabrication scenarios; and

•

Proposing a fabrication detector to align with word-level fabrications;

Evaluation Process: Preserving Distributional Alignment

To mitigate distributional shifts between fabricated and ground truth samples, we adopt a two-stage quality control process (Algorithm
1
) during fabrication (Sec.
3.1
) that jointly evaluates semantic and structural similarity. Structural similarity is quantified using the ROUGE-L recall score
[
20
]
, where generations with score below 0.7
[
6
]
are discarded as structurally divergent. Next, we adopt a self-play preference optimization (‘SPPO’) strategy
[
32
]
by providing LLM with both the generated fabrication from Sec.
3.1
and the LLM-rewritten ground truth from Sec.
3.1
. This LLM agent evaluates the generated fabrication alongside the corresponding rewritten ground truth, and we retain only those fabrications that are misclassified by the agent, which represent LLM’s factuality ‘blind spots’. This process iterates until all samples satisfy both semantic and structural similarity constraints. The resulting dataset presents fabrication cases that are structurally realistic yet semantically deceptive, providing a rigorous foundation for assessing and improving LLM robustness in factuality detection.

3.2
Word-Level Fabrication Detector

Most existing fabrication detectors suffer from significant performance degradation as structural similarity between ground truth and fabrication increases (Fig.
2
). To evaluate this, we construct an intermediate ablation dataset by relaxing the word-change constraint in the fabrication process, where the rewriting step that transforms human-written ground truths to LLM-based style introduces a slight drop in structural similarity. By further incorporating the word-change limit, the average ROUGE-L recall score is capped above 0.7, approaching the 0.8 threshold that prior work
[
6
]
considers as near-exact matches.

Figure 2
:
Increasing structural similarity leads to severe performance degradation in existing detectors to overall F1 below 50%.

These findings suggest that existing hallucination detectors largely overfit to stylistic and structural patterns instead of learning factual consistency. Such over-reliance results in three key limitations, especially in the medical domain where factual precision is emphasized:

1.

Low fabrication retrieval recall.
Existing models consistently exhibit poor recall for fabricated samples relative to their performance on ground truths. This asymmetric performance poses unignorable risks for medical use cases, as undetected fabrications may propagate misinformation in diagnostic or clinical contexts
[
29
]
;

2.

Embedding-based detectors fail on word-level fabrications.
For highly structurally similar datasets such as
MedFabric
, embedding-based approaches cannot effectively distinguish between fabrications and ground truths (Fig.
5
) as both texts exhibit high embedding similarity. In the contrary, word-level detectors such as zero-shot LLM-based evaluators achieve more balanced and accurate results (Fig.
2
)
[
2
,
24
]
, and;

3.

Instability of zero-shot LLM evaluations.
LLM-as-a-judge systems
[
8
]
are typically sensitive to sampling randomness
[
31
]
on OOD samples, due to information loss in discrete rating systems and misalignment of data distribution between training and testing data. This indicates the need for a repeatable and generalisable evaluation framework.

To address these challenges, we propose
EtHER
, a modular word-level fabrication detector designed for stable and interpretable factuality assessment.
EtHER
consists of three core modules:
(1) a
Text2Table Decomposition
module that converts long medical statements into structured entity–description tables to enhance semantic sensitivity
3.2
;
(2) a
Word Masking and Filling
module that enables word-level factual alignment with retrieved evidence (Fig.
3.2
; and
(3) a
Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation
module that combines embedding-based and LLM-based reasoning to enhance judgment consistency and low-variance (Sec.
3.2
).

Figure 3
:
EtHER
comprises of three modules: (1) Text2Table Decomposition improves semantic sensitivity to identify fabricated statements, (2) Word Masking and Filling performs word-level alignment between model outputs and retrieved evidence, and (3) Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation provides structured, low-randomness verification using both embedding and LLM-based comparison.

Text2Table Decomposition

To improve detection sensitivity, this module converts unstructured medical statements into structured tables of biomedical entities with their contextual relations. The system extracts key entities from LLM responses and links each to its description (e.g., “TGFBI expression”
\Rightarrow
“unfavorable lymphatic recurrence” in Fig.
3
). Complex sentences are decomposed into multiple entity–context pairs, while pronouns are replaced with explicit references to ensure self-contained rows. Unlike knowledge-graph triples, this decomposition preserves nuanced semantic dependencies without distorting meaning, yielding a structured, interpretable representation that supports precise downstream fabrication detection (Sec.
3.2
).

Word Masking and Filling

To assess textual and semantic alignment between LLM responses and retrieved evidence, we introduce a word-masking and knowledge-filling framework. From the entity–description table (Sec.
3.2
), two complementary masked tables are generated by alternately masking distinct word sets so every word is hidden once. Each table is reconstructed by an LLM using a domain-specific RAG system that fills masks with verbatim phrases from retrieved evidence, minimizing semantic drift. The resulting paired reconstructions share identical structure, enabling direct sentence-level comparison, where discrepancies reveal factual inconsistencies indicative of potential fabrications.

Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation

To detect subtle word-level fabrications while minimizing LLM randomness in OOD samples, we introduce a hybrid evaluation module that combines embedding-based similarity with LLM-based reasoning. Sentence pairs from reconstructed tables (Sec.
3.2
) are first encoded using open-source embedding models to measure semantic distance, with pairs exceeding a threshold flagged as inconsistent. These candidates are then re-evaluated by an LLM agent to distinguish factual conflicts from benign lexical variations. Final decisions are aggregated via a rule-based layer to determine response-level fabrication. This hybrid design merges the stability of embeddings with the interpretive precision of LLM reasoning, while preceding modules (Sec.
3.2
,
3.2
) ensure concise, self-contained inputs that reduce hallucination and multi-turn drift
[
22
,
17
]
even if initial inputs contain OOD behavior.

4.1
Outperformance in identifying subtle fabrications

EtHER
shows outperformance in detecting subtle fabrications compared to state-of-the-art baselines, especially in datasets with high structural similarity between fabricated and ground-truth samples. It retrieves nearly twice as many fabrications as the model average (68.6% vs. 34.8%) while maintaining comparable F1 performance on truthful answers (57.5% vs. 58.3%), yielding a higher overall F1 score (60.6% vs. 44.3%). All three zero-shot models, i.e.
EtHER
, LLM-as-a-judge, and GCA, achieve balanced and robust results, outperforming fine-tuned detectors that overfit to stylistic artifacts rather than factual cues. In contrast,
EtHER
leverages LLMs’ general knowledge with a structured, word-level workflow to simplify domain-specific semantics into general reasoning space, enabling accurate and consistent detection of fine-grained fabrications.

4.2
Robust generalization across different structural similarity

EtHER
maintains stable performance across datasets with varying structural similarity. As shown in Fig.
4
, it achieves comparable performance with fine-tuned baselines, having about 70% accuracy across datasets with low ROUGE scores, i.e. the original MedHallu dataset
[
28
]
and a ground-truth-rewritten variation. Fine-tuned models, however, perform well only at low similarity and degrade sharply as similarity increases with the average F1 score dropping from 69.54% to 59.84% in MedHallu ground-truth-rewritten variation, and further to 42.10% in
MedFabric
. This pattern indicates overreliance on stylistic cues rather than factual grounding. In contrast, zero-shot models remain stable with 51.10 – 54.05% F1, while
EtHER
enhances generalizability through a structured workflow for consistently outperformance at 60.60 - 70.10%.

4.3

We achieve a prediction variance comparable to zero-shot LLM-as-a-judge, despite having significantly more generation processes in the workflow of

5
Summary and Future Work

In this work, we address the challenge of detecting subtle, word-level fabrications in medical domain by introducing a unified data-model framework designed to advance fabrication detection beyond stylistic pattern. This includes a novel generation pipeline that results in
MedFabric
and a word-level fabrication detector
EtHER
.
MedFabric
is generated by a data-centric pipeline that rewrites human-authored answers with LLMs, constructs realistic fabrications conditioning on evidence and enforces structural and semantic similarity through iterative quality control. Building on this foundation,
EtHER
serves as a word-level fabrication detector that integrates structured reasoning and factual alignment into LLM evaluation, containing a Text2Table Decomposition module to convert long-form medical statements into semantically rich and interpretable tables, a Word Masking and Filling module to perform fine-grained factual consistency checks with retrieved knowledge, and a Hybrid Sentence Pair Evaluation module to integrate embedding-based evaluation with LLM-based reasoning for stable and generalisable judgments. Together, these innovations motivate robust detection of subtle factual inconsistencies, and outperform existing detectors across datasets of different structural similarity. This work not only bridges the gap between linguistic realism and factual rigor in hallucination benchmarks, but also establishes a scalable foundation for trustworthy hallucination detection for realistic, domain-specific LLM use cases.

Appendix 0.A
Formal Definitions for Generating
MedFabric

This section provides formal definitions for rewriting (App.
0.A
) and fabrication (App.
0.A
) from Sec.
3.1
. We denote by
\mathcal{Q}
,
\mathcal{K}
and
\mathcal{X}
the space of medical questions, retrieved knowledge sources, and natural-language answers respectively.

Rewriting.

Given a question
q\in\mathcal{Q}
, knowledge
k\in\mathcal{K}
, and a human-authored answer
x^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\text{human}}\in\mathcal{X}
, the LLM rewriting operator
f_{\mathrm{LLM}}:\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
generates a rewritten ground truth
\hat{x}^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\mathrm{LLM}}=f_{\mathrm{LLM}}(q,k,x^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\text{human}})
, whose semantic content satisfies
\text{Content}(\hat{x}^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\mathrm{LLM}})\approx\text{Content}(x^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\text{human}})
and remains entailed by the retrieved evidence (
k\models\hat{x}^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\mathrm{LLM}}
).

Fabrication.

Conditioned on the rewritten ground truth
\hat{x}^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\mathrm{LLM}}
and context
(q,k)
, the fabrication operator
g_{\mathrm{LLM}}:\mathcal{Q}\times\mathcal{K}\times\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{X}
produces a fabricated answer
\hat{x}^{\mathrm{fab}}_{\mathrm{LLM}}=g_{\mathrm{LLM}}(q,k,\hat{x}^{\mathrm{gts}}_{\mathrm{LLM}})
, which diverges from factual correctness while preserving stylistic and structural similarity. This yields hard-negative variations which align with the sample’s linguistic distribution.
