Minimal spin-rotor model for Barnett and Einstein–de Haas physics
Saikat Banerjee

Abstract
The Barnett effect is usually understood through an effective magnetic field generated by mechanical rotation, while its reciprocal Einstein–de Haas effect describes the transfer of spin angular momentum to mechanical motion. We show that this effective-field picture changes qualitatively once the mechanical degree of freedom itself is quantized. To demonstrate this, we introduce an exactly solvable minimal spin-rotor model in which a spin- 1 / 2 1/2 is coupled to a quantum rotor. In a fixed angular-momentum sector, the model reproduces the conventional Barnett splitting and remains formally equivalent to a Zeeman problem. For a superposition of rotor sectors, however, the Barnett field becomes operator-valued and the resulting dynamics generates coherent spin-rotor entanglement. This is directly visible in the reduced spin purity, rotor coherence, and entanglement entropy. Our results identify a minimal quantum setting in which the Barnett effective-field picture departs from its classical form and acquires a reciprocal manifestation through spin-dependent rotor coherence.



Introduction
–
Mechanical rotation and magnetization are linked by angular momentum transfer. In its conventional form, the Barnett effect
Barnett 1915
;
Barnett 1935
refers to the magnetization generated by rotating a rigid body, whereas the reciprocal Einstein–de Haas response
Richardson 1908
;
Frenkel’ 1979
;
Jaafar et al. 2009
;
Mentink et al. 2019
;
Ganzhorn et al. 2016
converts spin angular momentum into mechanical motion. In many practical settings, spin-rotation coupling can be recast as an effective Barnett field, so that mechanical rotation appears formally similar to a Zeeman-like coupling in the rotating frame
Matsuo et al. 2011a
;
Maekawa et al. 2023
;
Funato et al. 2025
;
Zhang et al. 2016
;
Matsuo et al. 2011b
;
Chudo et al. 2021a
;
Ono et al. 2015
. A natural question is therefore whether this effective-field description remains complete when the mechanical degree of freedom itself is quantized. When the rotor is classical, its angular momentum may be treated as a continuous parameter, and the spin sees a static effective field. In the quantum limit, by contrast, the rotor may occupy a superposition of discrete angular-momentum eigenstates, so that the Barnett field becomes an operator rather than a fixed number. This observation provides the starting point for a quantum formulation of Barnett physics. Unlike recent platform-specific spin-rotor studies
Wachter et al. 2026
, the present work is centered on a minimal exactly solvable model that isolates the crossover from a classical effective Barnett field to a quantum operator-valued one.

It is useful to distinguish the present setting from the extensively studied Dicke and Rabi spin–oscillator models
Bakemeier et al. 2013
;
Bakemeier et al. 2012
. In those models, a spin or collective spin is coupled to a harmonic-oscillator coordinate, and the central questions often concern superradiant quantum phase transitions, classical spin or oscillator limits, and the associated critical or semiclassical dynamics. By contrast, the present model involves a compact mechanical rotor and a coupling to its angular momentum
L_{z}
, which is conserved. Consequently, there is no Dicke-type soft mode, no superradiant order parameter, and no symmetry-breaking quantum phase transition. The central issue here is instead whether the Barnett field can still be regarded as a classical parameter when the source of rotation is itself quantized. We show that it cannot: a rotor superposition turns the Barnett field into an operator-valued quantity and produces coherent spin–rotor entanglement already in a minimal exactly solvable single-spin problem.

Model
–
We consider a single spin-
1/2
coupled to a planar quantum rotor,

{\cal H}=\frac{L_{z}^{2}}{2I}+\Delta S_{x}+\mathsf{g}L_{z}S_{z},

(1)

where
L_{z}
is the rotor angular momentum,
I
is the moment of inertia,
\Delta
is a transverse spin splitting, and
\mathsf{g}
denotes the spin-rotation coupling. The first term describes the rotor, the second prevents the problem from being trivially diagonal in the spin sector, and the third encodes the Barnett coupling between mechanical rotation and the local spin degree of freedom. Despite its simplicity, Eq. (
1
) already contains the essential ingredients needed to distinguish the classical effective-field picture from a genuinely quantum, operator-valued Barnett field.

The same model may also be viewed as an effective local description of a spin-orbit-coupled moment hosted by a rotating transition-metal ligand environment, for example in octahedral or tetrahedral units, or in gyroscope-like metal complexes
Swart and Gruden 2016
;
Ehnbom and Gladysz 2021
;
Fry and Woodward 2013
;
Shotwell et al. 2025
;
Setaka and Yamaguchi 2013
. In that setting, the coupling
\mathsf{g}
should be understood not as a bare microscopic spin-orbit coupling, but as an effective low-energy parameter generated by crystal-field and spin-orbit physics. This provides a natural bridge between the present minimal model and more realistic local-moment settings in correlated materials
Banerjee et al. 2022
;
Kumar et al. 2022
;
Kim et al. 2024
;
Schaffer et al. 2016
;
Paschen and Si 2021
;
Chudo et al. 2021b
.

The aim of the present brief report is intentionally modest. Rather than constructing a fully realistic model with many competing parameters, we focus on an exactly solvable toy model that captures one aspect of Barnett physics in the clearest possible way. This choice is deliberate: it keeps the main physical picture transparent and avoids the technical complications that often arise in more elaborate strongly correlated models. Within this simple setting, we show that the model reproduces the classical Barnett effect when the rotor angular momentum is well defined, while in the quantum regime it gives rise to spin-rotor entanglement and to a minimal reciprocal backaction on the rotor through spin-state-dependent phase evolution.

Exact solution
–
We begin by noting that the Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) conserves the rotor angular momentum,
i.e.
,
[L_{z},{\cal H}]=0
. As a result, for a rotor eigenstate satisfying
L_{z}\ket{m}=m\hbar\ket{m}
with integer
m
, the problem reduces to a simple two-level spin Hamiltonian,

{\cal H}_{m}=\frac{m^{2}\hbar^{2}}{2I}+\Delta S_{x}+\mathsf{g}m\hbar S_{z}.

(2)

Using the Pauli-matrix representation
{\bf S}=(\sigma_{x},\sigma_{y},\sigma_{z})/2
, the exact eigenenergies are (here we set
\hbar=1
)

\varepsilon^{\pm}_{m}=\frac{m^{2}}{2I}\pm\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\mathsf{g}^{2}m^{2}}.

The corresponding eigenstates are spinors aligned with the effective field

{\bf B}_{\rm eff}=\Delta\hat{\bf x}+\mathsf{g}m\hat{\bf z},

(3)

with tilt angle
\theta_{m}=\arctan(\Delta/\mathsf{g}m)
. Equation (
3
) shows that a nonzero rotor angular momentum produces a spin splitting,
i.e.
, a Barnett effect already at the level of the exact spectrum. For a rotor state localized near a definite
m_{0}
, the spin experiences an effective Zeeman field proportional to
\mathsf{g}m_{0}
, and the problem reduces to the familiar classical picture of rotation-induced magnetization. The corresponding Barnett-split branches are shown in Fig.
1
(a).

Classical-to-quantum crossover
–
The classical Barnett limit is recovered when the rotor angular momentum is treated as a fixed
c
-number. In that case, the spin-rotor Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) reduces to an ordinary static Zeeman problem, up to an additive rotor energy. Replacing
L_{z}\to m_{0}
, one obtains

{\cal H}_{\mathrm{cl}}=\frac{m_{0}^{2}}{2I}+\Delta S_{x}+\mathsf{g}m_{0}S_{z}.

(4)

The spin therefore experiences a static effective field set by the fixed rotor angular momentum, and the Barnett effect is fully captured by the corresponding Zeeman-like splitting. In this sense, a sharply defined angular-momentum sector reproduces the familiar classical picture of rotation-induced magnetization.

The situation changes qualitatively when the rotor is prepared in a quantum superposition of angular-momentum sectors as
\ket{\psi}=\sum_{m}c_{m}\ket{m}\otimes\ket{\sigma}
, with
\ket{\sigma}
the initial spin state. Since each angular-momentum sector evolves with its own spin Hamiltonian
{\cal H}_{m}
, the full time-evolved state becomes

\ket{\psi(t)}=\sum_{m}c_{m}e^{-i\frac{m^{2}t}{2I}}\ket{m}\otimes e^{-i(\Delta S_{x}+\mathsf{g}mS_{z})t}\ket{\sigma}.

(5)

The corresponding spin precession frequency in sector
m
is

\Omega_{m}=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\mathsf{g}^{2}m^{2}}.

Thus, different rotor sectors drive different spin dynamics. As long as the initial rotor state contains more than one
m
component, the spin evolution becomes correlated with the rotor state and the two sectors generically become entangled. In this regime, the Barnett field is no longer a fixed number, but an operator whose action depends on the quantum state of the rotor. The central consequence of this crossover is reflected in the reduced spin purity shown in Fig.
1
(b).

Entanglement classification
–
A simple way to characterize the entanglement discussed above is to choose the smallest genuinely quantum spin-rotor state, namely a rotor prepared in an equal superposition of two opposite
L_{z}
sectors and a spin initialized in the
\ket{\uparrow}
state,

\ket{\psi}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{m}+\ket{-m}\right)\otimes\ket{\uparrow}.

(6)

If we define
\lambda_{m}=\mathsf{g}m
and
\Omega_{m}=\sqrt{\Delta^{2}+\lambda_{m}^{2}}
, then the time-evolved state can be written as

\ket{\psi(t)}=\frac{e^{-\frac{i\lambda_{m}^{2}t}{2I\mathsf{g}^{2}}}}{\sqrt{2}}\left(\ket{m}\otimes\ket{\sigma_{+}(t)}+\ket{-m}\otimes\ket{\sigma_{-}(t)}\right),

(7)

where the two spin states are

\ket{\sigma_{\pm}(t)}=(c\mp ibs)\ket{\uparrow}-ias\ket{\downarrow},

with
a=\Delta/\Omega
,
b=\lambda_{m}/\Omega
,
c=\cos\tfrac{\Omega t}{2}
, and
s=\sin\tfrac{\Omega t}{2}
. Here, we have used
\Omega_{m}=\Omega_{-m}\equiv\Omega
. The quantum Barnett regime becomes explicit by considering the overlap

K(t)=\braket{\sigma_{+}(t)|\sigma_{-}(t)},

for which one obtains

K(t)=1-\frac{2\lambda_{m}^{2}}{\Delta^{2}+\lambda_{m}^{2}}\sin^{2}\tfrac{\Omega t}{2}+2i\frac{\lambda_{m}}{\Omega}\cos\frac{\Omega t}{2}\sin\frac{\Omega t}{2}.

(8)

The quantity

|K(t)|=\sqrt{1-4\frac{\Delta^{2}\lambda_{m}^{2}}{\Omega^{4}}\sin^{4}\frac{\Omega t}{2}}

plays a central role in the present problem. If
|K(t)|=1
, the two spin branches differ at most by a phase and no spin–rotor entanglement is generated. By contrast, any deviation of
|K(t)|
from unity signals that the two opposite angular-momentum sectors drive different spin dynamics. In this sense, the overlap in Eq. (
8
) directly measures the breakdown of the simple classical effective-field picture. The special point
|K(t)|=0
corresponds to orthogonal spin branches and therefore to maximal spin-rotor entanglement within the two-sector subspace. This occurs at the balanced point
\lambda_{m}=\Delta
and at times satisfying
\sin^{2}(\Omega t/2)=1
.

To make this more explicit, we first trace out the rotor degree of freedom and construct the reduced density matrix for the spin sector,

\rho_{\rm spin}(t)={\rm Tr}_{\rm rotor}\ket{\psi(t)}\bra{\psi(t)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{\sigma_{+}(t)}\bra{\sigma_{+}(t)}+\ket{\sigma_{-}(t)}\bra{\sigma_{-}(t)}\right).

(9)

This result already shows the essential distinction from an ordinary Zeeman problem. In a fixed angular-momentum sector, the spin evolves unitarily in a single static effective field and therefore remains in a pure state. Here, however, the spin is driven simultaneously by two opposite Barnett fields associated with the
\ket{m}
and
\ket{-m}
sectors. As a result, the spin sector alone becomes mixed once the rotor is traced out. The Barnett field is therefore no longer a fixed external parameter, but a quantum operator correlated with the rotor state.

A convenient measure of this effect is the spin purity,

{\cal P}_{\rm spin}(t)={\rm Tr}\left[\rho_{\rm spin}^{2}(t)\right]=\frac{1}{2}\left[1+|K(t)|^{2}\right].

(10)

For
{\cal P}_{\rm spin}=1
, the spin remains pure and the dynamics is effectively classical. Whenever
{\cal P}_{\rm spin}<1
, the spin is entangled with the rotor and the simple Zeeman interpretation breaks down. In particular,
{\cal P}_{\rm spin}=1/2
corresponds to maximal entanglement in the present two-sector problem. Thus, the first genuine quantum correction to the classical Barnett effect appears not as a small renormalization of the effective field, but as coherent spin-rotor entanglement. The time dependence of
{\cal P}_{\rm spin}(t)
is shown in Fig.
1
(b).

The reciprocal aspect of the problem is seen most directly by tracing out the spin degree of freedom. This gives the reduced rotor density matrix

\rho_{\rm rotor}(t)={\rm Tr}_{\rm spin}\ket{\psi(t)}\bra{\psi(t)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\ket{m}\bra{m}+\ket{-m}\bra{-m}+K^{*}(t)\ket{m}\bra{-m}+K(t)\ket{-m}\bra{m}\right).

(11)

Since
K(t)
controls the off-diagonal matrix elements between the
\ket{m}
and
\ket{-m}
sectors, it also controls the coherence of the rotor itself. When
|K(t)|=1
, the rotor remains in a fully coherent superposition of opposite angular-momentum sectors up to a relative phase. When
|K(t)|
decreases, this coherence is reduced by the spin dynamics, even though the total evolution remains fully unitary. In this sense, the spin leaves a measurable imprint on the mechanical sector.

This rotor-side description provides the natural reciprocal interpretation of the same physics. The Barnett effect is seen in the way a given rotor sector acts on the spin through an effective field, while the reciprocal response is seen in the way the spin dynamics feeds back onto the rotor coherence. In the present minimal setting, this reciprocal effect does not first appear as a classical torque, but rather as a spin-state-dependent modification of the rotor density matrix. The quantity
K(t)
therefore serves as a common measure of both effects: it quantifies the departure from a classical Barnett field in the spin sector and, at the same time, the induced backaction on the rotor sector. This observation also points to a natural extension in which angle-dependent spin-flip terms, such as
S_{+}e^{-i\phi}+S_{-}e^{i\phi}
, would promote the coherence backaction discussed here into an explicit torque-level Einstein–de Haas exchange while preserving total angular momentum.

Because the total state
\ket{\psi(t)}
remains pure, the reduced spin and rotor density matrices have the same nonzero eigenvalues,

p_{\pm}(t)=\frac{1\pm|K(t)|}{2},

(12)

so that the entanglement entropy may be written in the compact form

S_{\rm ent}(t)=-\sum_{\nu=\pm}p_{\nu}(t)\ln p_{\nu}(t).

(13)

This makes it clear that the spin and rotor provide two equivalent but physically complementary views of the same entangled quantum Barnett regime. The corresponding time dependence of
S_{\rm ent}(t)
is shown in Fig.
1
(c).

Discussion and conclusion
–
We have shown that the effective-field description of the Barnett effect breaks down qualitatively once the source of rotation is itself quantized. While a fixed rotor sector reproduces the conventional Zeeman-like Barnett splitting, a superposition of rotor sectors changes the problem more fundamentally: the Barnett field ceases to be a fixed parameter and becomes an operator correlated with the quantum state of the rotor. The resulting dynamics can no longer be understood in terms of a single static effective field, but instead gives rise to coherent spin–rotor entanglement. This is directly reflected in the reduced spin purity and the associated entanglement entropy, which together identify the onset of a genuinely quantum Barnett regime. From the reciprocal point of view, the same physics appears as a minimal form of spin-dependent rotor backaction, visible in the coherence of the rotor sector.

The present construction is therefore complementary to Dicke-model studies of spin–oscillator entanglement and classical limits: rather than addressing a superradiant transition or semiclassical spin–oscillator dynamics, it isolates the quantum-mechanical status of the Barnett field itself. In this sense, the spin–rotor model provides a minimal exactly solvable setting in which Barnett physics and reciprocal rotor backaction depart qualitatively from their classical effective-field description. Beyond its minimal form, it also offers a useful conceptual starting point for extensions to many-body, driven, and dissipative spin-rotation systems.
