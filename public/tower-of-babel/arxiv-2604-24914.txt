SPDEs with time-independent Lévy colored noise
Raluca M. Balan, Jinxin Wang

Abstract
In this article, we introduce a time-independent version of the Lévy colored noise considered in [ 2 , 5 ] . We study the existence of the solution of a linear stochastic partial differential equation with this type of noise, and we identify some necessary conditions which guarantee that the solution has finite p p -th order moments. Using tools from Malliavin calculus, we investigate the existence of the solution for the equation with multiplicative noise. As examples, we consider the stochastic heat and wave equations in any dimension d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 .

Introduction

SPDEs have been studied intensively in the literature in the last three decades, using either the random field approach initiated by Walsh
[
28
]
or the semigroup approach of Da Prato and Zabczyk
[
11
]
. We mention briefly some important developments and few of classical references, focusing only on the random field approach, without aiming to include a comprehensive list.

Classical models based on the random field approach involve equations perturbed by a space-time Gaussian noise, a space-time generalization of Brownian motion. In
[
10
]
, Dalang introduced a spatially homogeneous Gaussian noise (which is correlated in space), and developed an Itô integral with respect to this noise. Subsequently, Nualart and his co-authors studied equations with temporally-correlated Gaussian noise, using tools from Malliavin calculus, e.g.
[
22
]
. Many interesting properties of the solutions of these equations have been discovered in the recent years, such as intermittency
[
18
]
, or exact asymptotic behavior of moments
[
7
]
.

At the same time, models driven by a noise which resembles a Lévy process have also been studied, but with more limited success. This noise was called a
Lévy white noise

[
2
]
, or a
cylindrical Lévy noise

[
19
]
, depending on what approach was used. If the noise has finite variance, one can develop an Itô calculus
[
1
]
and a Malliavin calculus
[
20
]
, using the compensated version of the underlying Poisson random measure of the noise. If the noise has infinite variance, truncation techniques have to be used to eliminate the large jumps of the noise
[
8
,
9
]
. The
Lévy colored noise

was introduced in
[
2
]
using the convolution with a “coloration” kernel, and an extensive study of SPDEs with this type of noise can be found in
[
5
]
.

In all the afore-mentioned references, the noise includes a time component. In
[
15
]
, Hu studied for the first time an SPDE (namely the Parabolic Anderson Model, or PAM), driven by a time-independent Gaussian noise, and discovered results which were slightly different compared with the time-dependent case. Still in the Gaussian case, a parallel analysis of various properties of the solutions (for instance Feynman-Kac-type formulas, intermittency and Hölder continuity) has been carried out in
[
16
]
for (PAM) with time-dependent and time-independent case. More recently,
[
4
]
extended to the time-independent noise, the Quantitative Central Limit theorem (QCLT) obtained in
[
17
]
for (PAM) with space-time Gaussian white noise.

In this context, a natural question arises: to what extent, some of the (limited) results that are known for the time-dependent Lévy noise remain valid in the time-independent case? The major barrier that one faces is the lack of any type of martingale structure, which means that Itô calculus cannot be used, leaving Malliavin calculus as the only option. Another barrier is the absence of an estimate for the
p
-th moment of a multiple Poisson integral: unlike the Gaussian case, on the Poisson space, hyper-contractivity property does not hold. A “restricted” hyper-contractivity property was proved in
[
21
]
. Despite these significant challenges, some positive answers to the question above exist, and we gather them here. More precisely, in the present article, we show that most the properties related to moments of the solutions of linear SPDEs discovered in
[
5
]
for the Lévy colored noise remain valid in the time-independent case, under natural assumptions on the fundamental solution of the differential operator. Moreover, under slightly stronger versions of these assumptions, the equations with multiplicative noise have unique solutions, for which we provide the explicit Poisson-chaos expansion. Finally, we show that these assumptions are satisfied by the fundamental solutions of the heat and wave operators in any dimension, provided that the spectral measure
\mu
associated with the spatial coloration kernel
\kappa
of the noise satisfies Dalang’s condition (as in the Gaussian case).

Assumption A1.

Let

(a) the Fourier transform

(b)

(c) there exists a tempered function

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|h(\xi)|^{2}\phi(\xi)d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}f(x)\mathcal{F}\phi(x)dx\quad\mbox{for all $\phi\in\mathcal{S}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$}.

(4)

Then

(5)

For any

2
Moment estimate

In this section, we present an inequality for the stochastic integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
in the case of deterministic integrand. This inequality will be used in Section
3
below for estimating the moments of the solution of the linear equation with noise
X
.

A process
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is called
càdlàg
if it is right-continuous and has left limits.
We recall Rosenthal’s inequality for càdlàg martingales (see e.g. Lemma 2.1 of
[
12
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(Rosenthal’s inequality)
.

Let
\{M_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be a càdlàg square-integrable martingale with
M_{0}=0
and
\langle M\rangle
be its predictable quadratic variation. We denote by
(\Delta M)_{t}
the jump size of
M
at time
t
. Then for any
p\geq 2
, there exists a constant
\mathcal{B}_{p}>0
depending on
p
such that for any
t>0
,

\|\sup_{s\leq t}|M_{s}|\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}\Big(\|\langle M\rangle_{t}^{1/2}\|_{p}+\|\sup_{s\leq t}|(\Delta M)_{s}|\|_{p}\Big).

We consider the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
, where
({\bf Z},\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
is defined by (
1
).

We denote

I_{1}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}\phi(x,z)\widehat{N}(dx,dz)\quad\mbox{for $\phi\in\mathcal{H}$}.

We denote by
\|\cdot\|_{p}
the norm in
L^{p}(\Omega)
, for any
p\geq 1
. We have the following result.

Theorem 2.2
.

For any function
\phi\in\mathcal{H}
and for any
p\geq 2
,

\|I_{1}(\phi)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}(\|\phi\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\phi\|_{L^{p}({\bf Z})}),

(7)

where
\mathcal{B}_{p}
is the constant from Theorem
2.1
.

Proof.

Step 1.
In this step, we proved that for any
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and
\Gamma\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})
fixed,

\|I_{1}(\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma})\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}(\|\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma}\|_{L^{p}({\bf Z})}).

(8)

To avoid trivial situations, we assume that
|B|>0
and
\nu(\Gamma)>0
.

For any
t>0
, let
A_{t}=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d};|x|\leq t\}
. We consider the filtration

\mathcal{F}_{t}=\sigma\big(\big\{N(A\times C);A\in\mathcal{B}(\mathbb{R}^{d}),A\subseteq A_{t},C\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})\big\}\big)\vee\mathcal{N},

where
\mathcal{N}
is the class of
\mathbb{P}
-negligible sets. We define
M_{0}=0
and

M_{t}:=I_{1}(\phi 1_{(B\cap A_{t})\times\Gamma})=\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)\phi(x,z)\widehat{N}(dx,dz),\quad\ \mbox{for $t>0$}.

Then
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a martingale with respect to
(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0}
since for any
0\leq s<t
,

M_{t}-M_{s}=\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}\verb 2\2A_{s}}(x)\phi(x,z)\widehat{N}(dx,dz)\quad\mbox{is independent of $\mathcal{F}_{s}$},

and
\mathbb{E}[M_{t}-M_{s}]=0
. Moreover,
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
: if
t_{n}\to t
, then

\mathbb{E}|M_{t_{n}}-M_{t}|^{2}=\int_{B\times\Gamma}|1_{A_{t_{n}}}(x)-1_{A_{t}}(x)||\phi(x,z)|^{2}dx\nu(dz)\to 0.

By Doob’s regularity theorem (see e.g. Theorem 3.40 of
[
23
]
), any stochastically continuous submartingale has a càdlàg modification. Hence,
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
has a càdlàg modification, which we denote also
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
.

We apply Theorem
2.1
. Clearly, the predictable quadratic variation of
(M_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is:

\langle M\rangle_{t}=\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)|\phi(x,z)|^{2}dx\nu(dz).

Since the sets
B
and
\Gamma
are bounded,
M_{t}
can be written as

M_{t}=\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)\phi(x,z)N(dx,dz)-\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)\phi(x,z)dx\nu(dz)=:M_{t}^{(d)}-M_{t}^{(c)},

where
(M_{t}^{(c)})_{t\geq 0}
is continuous. To study the jumps of
(M_{t}^{(d)})_{t\geq 0}
, we recall the following equality in distribution:

N|_{B\times\Gamma}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}\sum_{i=1}^{\Lambda}\delta_{(X_{i},Z_{i})}=:N^{\prime},

where
\Lambda
is a Poisson random variable with mean
\lambda=|B|\nu(\Gamma)
, and
\{(X_{i},Z_{i})\}_{i\geq 1}
are i.i.d. on
B\times\Gamma
with law
\lambda^{-1}({\rm Leb}\times\nu)
, independent of
\Lambda
; see e.g. Section 5.4.2 of
[
24
]
. Hence
I_{1}(\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma})
has the same law (and hence, the same
p
-th moment) as
\int_{B\times\Gamma}\phi(x,z)\widehat{N}^{\prime}(dx,dz)
. Therefore, without loss of generality, we can assume that

N|_{B\times\Gamma}=\sum_{i=1}^{\Lambda}\delta_{(X_{i},Z_{i})}\quad\mbox{a.s.}

which means that with probability 1,

M_{t}^{(d)}=\sum_{i=1}^{\Lambda}1_{A_{t}}(X_{i})\phi(X_{i},Z_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{\Lambda}1_{\{|X_{i}|\leq t\}}\phi(X_{i},Z_{i})\quad\mbox{for all $t>0$}.

A deterministic function
f:\mathbb{R}_{+}\to\mathbb{R}
given by
f(t)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}1_{\{|x_{i}|\leq t\}}a_{i}
for some
a_{1},x_{1},\ldots,a_{n},x_{n}\in\mathbb{R}
with
|x_{i}|\not=|x_{j}|
for all
i\not=j
, is càdlàg and has jumps at
t_{i}=|x_{i}|
with jump sizes
\Delta f(t_{i})=a_{i}
, for
i=1,\ldots,k
. In our case,

\mathbb{P}(|X_{i}|=|X_{j}|)=\frac{1}{|B|^{2}}\int_{B^{2}}1_{\{|x|=|y|\}}dxdy=0\quad\mbox{for any $i\not=j$}.

Then the event
F=\bigcup_{i\not=j}\{|X_{i}|=|X_{j}|\}
has probability 0. We will work on
\widetilde{\Omega}=F^{c}
. On this event, the function
t\mapsto M_{t}^{(d)}
has jumps at
T_{i}=|X_{i}|
with jump sizes
\phi(X_{i},Z_{i})
, for any
i=1,\ldots,\Lambda
, if
\Lambda>0
, or has no jumps at all if
\Lambda=0
. The same thing is true about the function
t\mapsto M_{t}
. It follows that on
\widetilde{\Omega}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{s\leq t}|\Delta M_{s}|^{p}\leq\sum_{s\leq t}|\Delta M_{s}|^{p}=\sum_{|X_{i}|\leq t}|\phi(X_{i},Z_{i})|^{p}=\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{\{|x|\leq t\}}|\phi(x,z)|^{p}N(dx,dz).

Taking expectation, we infer that:

\mathbb{E}\big[\sup_{s\leq t}|\Delta M_{s}|^{p}\big]\leq\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)|\phi(x,z)|^{p}dx\nu(dz),

and hence

\big\|\sup_{s\leq t}|\Delta M_{s}\big\|_{p}\leq\left(\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)|\phi(x,z)|^{p}dx\nu(dz)\right)^{1/p}.

Using Theorem
2.1
, we infer that for any
t>0
,

\|M_{t}\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}\left\{\left(\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)|\phi(x,z)|^{2}dx\nu(dz)\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{B\times\Gamma}1_{A_{t}}(x)|\phi(x,z)|^{p}dx\nu(dz)\right)^{1/p}\right\}

Next, we consider a sequence
(t_{n})_{n\geq 1}
such that
t_{n}\to\infty
. By the isometry property,
M_{t_{n}}\to I_{1}(\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma})
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,
M_{t_{n}}\to I_{1}(\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma})
a.s., along a subsequence. By Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\|I_{1}\big(\phi 1_{B\times\Gamma}\big)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\|M_{t_{n}}\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}\left\{\left(\int_{B\times\Gamma}|\phi(x,z)|^{2}dx\nu(dz)\right)^{1/2}+\left(\int_{B\times\Gamma}|\phi(x,z)|^{p}dx\nu(dz)\right)^{1/p}\right\}

This proves (
8
).

Step 2.
In this step, we prove (
7
). For this, we consider a sequence
(B_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of sets in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
B_{k}\uparrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
and a sequence
(\Gamma_{k})_{k\geq 1}
of sets in
\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}_{0})
such that
\Gamma_{k}\uparrow\mathbb{R}_{0}
.

By
Step 1
, we know that for any
k\geq 1
,

\|I_{1}(\phi 1_{B_{k}\times\Gamma_{k}})\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}(\|\phi 1_{B_{k}\times\Gamma_{k}}\|_{\mathcal{H}}+\|\phi 1_{B_{k}\times\Gamma_{k}}\|_{L^{p}({\bf Z})}).

The conclusion follows letting
k\to\infty
, using Fatou’s lemma on the left-hand side.

∎

Corollary 2.3
.

If
p\geq 2
is such that
m_{p}<\infty
, then for any function
\varphi\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

\|L(\varphi)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{B}_{p}\Big(m_{2}^{1/2}\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+m_{p}^{1/p}\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\Big(\|\varphi\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|\varphi\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big),

(9)

where
\mathcal{B}_{p}
is the constant from Theorem
2.1
and
\mathcal{C}_{p}=\mathcal{B}_{p}(m_{2}^{1/2}\vee m_{p}^{1/p})
.

Proof.

We apply Theorem
2.2
to the function
\phi(x,z)=\varphi(x)z
.
∎

Applying (

3
Linear equation

In this section we study the linear equation

\mathcal{L}v(t,x)=\dot{X}(x)\quad t>0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(11)

where
\mathcal{L}
is a second-order partial differential operator with coefficients that do not depend on the space variable. We denote by
G_{t}
the fundamental solution of the equation
\mathcal{L}u=0
.

We recall that
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
is the space of distributions with rapid decrease. The definition and key properties of such distributions are recalled in Appendix
A
. We will work under the following assumption.

Hypothesis A.
For any
t>0
,
G_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We will be interested in the following two examples:

Example 3.1

(Heat equation)
.

If
\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta
is the heat operator,
then

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi t)^{d/2}}e^{-\frac{|x|^{2}}{2t}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}.

In this case,
G_{t}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and Hypothesis A holds by Lemma
A.3
.

Example 3.2

(Wave equation)
.

If
\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\Delta
is the wave operator, then

G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2}1_{\{|x|<t\}}\quad\mbox{if \ $d=1$},\quad G_{t}(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\frac{1}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|x|^{2}}}1_{\{|x|<t\}}\quad\mbox{if \ $d=2$},

and

G_{t}=\frac{1}{4\pi t}\sigma_{t}\quad\mbox{if \ $d=3$},

where
\sigma_{t}
is the surface measure on the sphere
\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{3};|x|=t\}
.

If
d\geq 4
is even,
G_{t}
is a distribution with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by: (see e.g. relation (38) on p. 80 of
[
13
]
)

G_{t}=\frac{1}{2\cdot 4\cdot\ldots\cdot n}\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2}}(t^{d}\Upsilon_{t}),\quad(\Upsilon_{t},\varphi)=\frac{1}{\alpha_{d}}\int_{B(0,1)}\frac{\varphi(tz)}{\sqrt{t^{2}-|tz|^{2}}}dz,

where
\alpha_{d}=\frac{2\pi^{d/2}}{d\Gamma(d/2)}
is the volume of the unit ball
B(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If

d\geq 5
is odd,
G_{t}
is a distribution with compact support in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
given by: (see e.g. relation (31) on p. 77 of
[
13
]
)

G_{t}=\frac{1}{1\cdot 3\cdot 5\ldots\cdot(d-2)}\left(\frac{1}{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\right)^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(t^{d-2}\Sigma_{t}),\quad(\Sigma_{t},\varphi)=\frac{1}{\omega_{d}}\int_{\partial B(0,1)}\varphi(tz)d\sigma(z),

where
\omega_{d}=d\alpha_{d}
is the surface area of the unit sphere
\partial B(0,1)
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and
\sigma
is the surface measure on
\partial B(0,1)
.

It is known that

\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)=\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}\quad\mbox{for any $d\geq 1$}.

Hypothesis A holds since the space
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
of distributions with compact support is included in
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. (If
d\leq 3
, we can view
G_{t}
as a distribution. This distribution has compact support, since
G_{t}
has compact support as a function, or as a measure.)

3.1
Random field solution

In this section, we show that under some conditions, equation (
11
) has a (unique) solution. Then, we show that these conditions are verified for the stochastic heat and wave equation.

Definition 3.3
.

The process
\{v(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
defined by

v(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)X(dy)ds

(12)

is called a
random-field solution
of equation (
11
), provided that the integral is well-defined.

To prove the existence of the solution, we use the following result.

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
S\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
be such that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}S(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
. Then
S\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
S*\kappa\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}S(x)X(dx)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(S*\kappa)(x)L(dx)\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$.}

(13)

Proof.

The fact that
S\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}
is clear. Since
k
induces a tempered distribution,
S*\kappa\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(by Theorem
A.1
) and
\mathcal{F}(S*\kappa)=\mathcal{F}S\mathcal{F}\kappa
(by Theorem
A.2
). Note that
\mathcal{F}(S*\kappa)\in L^{2}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}(S*\kappa)(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}S(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=(2\pi)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}S(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

By Lemma A.1 of
[
5
]
, it follows that
S*\kappa\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

It remains to prove (
13
). For this, we proceed by approximation. Since
S\in\mathcal{P}_{0,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, there exists a sequence
(\varphi_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
such that
\|\varphi_{n}-S\|_{0}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
. Hence,

X(\varphi_{n})\to X(S)\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

On the other hand,
X(\varphi_{n})=L(\varphi_{n}*\kappa)\to L(S*\kappa)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, since

\varphi_{n}*\kappa\to S*\kappa
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
: by Plancherel theorem,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|(\varphi_{n}*\kappa)(x)-(S*\kappa)(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\varphi_{n}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}S(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=\|\varphi_{n}-S\|_{0}^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

Hence,
X(S)=L(S*\kappa)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
∎

We introduce the following hypotheses.

Hypothesis B.
For any
t>0
,
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
.

Hypothesis C.

For any
t>0
and for any sequence
t_{n}\to t
,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{n}}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\to 0.

Remark 3.5
.

To verify Hypothesis C, it suffices to show that:

(i) the function
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(ii) for any
t>0
, there exists
\varepsilon_{t}>0
and a measurable function
k_{t}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
with the property that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|k_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
, such that for any
h\in[-\varepsilon_{t},\varepsilon_{t}]
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\mathcal{F}G_{t+h}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|\leq k_{t}(\xi).

Then, apply the dominated convergence theorem.

Under Hypothesis B, for
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed, we apply Lemma
3.4
to the distribution

S(s)=G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)1_{[0,t]}(s)\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
since
\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)(\xi)=e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)}
, and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

It follows that for any
0\leq s\leq t
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)\in\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

Y^{(t,x)}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)X(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)=:Z^{(t,x)}(s)\quad\ \mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

(14)

To study the joint measurability in
(\omega,s)
of
Y^{(t,x)}
, we introduce another hypothesis:

Hypothesis D.
The map
(t,\xi)\mapsto\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)dt<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $T>0$}.

Lemma 3.6
.

Under Hypotheses A to D, for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
\{Y^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
and
\{Z^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
have jointly measurable modifications denoted by
\{\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
, respectively
\{\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
, and with probability 1,

\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)|ds<\infty\quad\mbox{and}\quad\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)|ds<\infty.

Moreover, for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

v(t,x):=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)ds\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

The process
\{v(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
is the random field solution of equation (
11
).

Proof.

By Hypothesis C, the processes
\{Y^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
and
\{Z^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
are continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
: if
s_{n}\to s\in[0,t]
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y^{(t,x)}(s_{n})-Y^{(t,x)}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|Z^{(t,x)}(s_{n})-Z^{(t,x)}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s_{n}}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

By Proposition 3.21 of
[
23
]
, these processes have jointly measurable modifications denoted by
\{\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
, respectively
\{\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
. This means that for any
s\in[0,t]
,

\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)=Y^{(t,x)}(s)=Z^{(t,x)}(s)=\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)\quad\mbox{a.s.}

By Fubini theorem and Hypothesis D,

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)|^{2}ds=\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)|^{2}ds=m_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)ds<\infty.

Hence,
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)|ds<\infty
, which implies that
\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)|ds<\infty
a.s. Similarly,
\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)|ds<\infty
a.s.
Finally, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Fubini’s theorem,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)ds-\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)ds\right|^{2}\leq t\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)ds-\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)|^{2}ds=0

∎

We will write

v(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)X(dy)ds=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)ds,

(15)

with the convention that the integrands
Y^{(t,x)}(s)
and
Z^{(t,x)}(s)
of the two
ds
integrals are interpreted as
\widetilde{Y}^{(t,x)}(s)
, respectively
\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)
.

To verify that Hypotheses B, C and D hold for the fundamental solutions of the heat and wave equations,
we introduce
Dalang’s condition
:

(D)\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

Remark 3.7
.

a) If
k=R_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in(0,d)
, then
f=R_{d,\alpha}
,
\mu(d\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}|\xi|^{-\alpha}d\xi
, and (D) holds if and only if

\alpha\in(d-2,d)
.

b) If
k=B_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha>0
, then
f=B_{d,\alpha}
,
\mu(d\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-\alpha/2}d\xi
, and (D) holds if and only if

\alpha>d-2
.

c) If
k=H_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha>0
, then
f=H_{d,\alpha}
,
\mu(d\xi)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\exp(-\alpha|\xi|^{2}/2)d\xi
, and (D) holds for any
\alpha>0
.

Lemma 3.8
.

Hypotheses B, C and D hold for the fundamental solutions of the heat and wave equations, for any
d\geq 1
, provided that (D) holds. In this case, the linear stochastic heat equation:

\frac{\partial v}{\partial t}(t,x)=\frac{1}{2}\Delta v(t,x)+\dot{X}(x)\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(16)

with zero initial conditions has a random field solution, in any dimension
d\geq 1
. The same thing is true for the linear stochastic wave equation:

\frac{\partial^{2}v}{\partial t^{2}}(t,x)=\Delta v(t,x)+\dot{X}(x)\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(17)

with zero initial conditions.

Proof.

i) We consider first the heat equation. In this case,
G_{t}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t>0
.
Hypothesis B holds since

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\mu(d\xi)\leq 2\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi),

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>1}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|>1}\frac{1}{1+t|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)\leq\frac{1}{t\wedge 1}\int_{|\xi|>1}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi).

To show that Hypothesis C holds, we use Remark
3.5
.
Clearly,
t\mapsto e^{-t|\xi|^{2}/2}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To check (ii),
we fix
t>0
and we let
h\in[-\varepsilon_{t},\varepsilon_{t}]
, for some
\varepsilon_{t}>0
(to be chosen later). If
h\in[0,\varepsilon_{t}]
, then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}G_{t+h}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|=e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\big(1-e^{-\frac{h|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\big)\leq e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}},

whereas if
h\in[-\varepsilon_{t},0]
,

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}G_{t+h}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|=e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\big(e^{-\frac{h|\xi|^{2}}{2}}-1\big)\leq e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{2}}\big(e^{\frac{\varepsilon_{t}|\xi|^{2}}{2}}-1\big)\leq e^{-\frac{(t-\varepsilon_{t})|\xi|^{2}}{2}}.

Therefore, it suffices to choose
\varepsilon_{t}=\frac{t}{2}
and
k_{t}(\xi)=e^{-\frac{t|\xi|^{2}}{4}}
. Finally, Hypothesis D holds, since there exist some constants
C_{T}^{(1)},C_{T}^{(2)}>0
such that: (see e.g. Lemma 6.1 of
[
25
]
)

C_{T}^{(1)}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{T}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}dt\leq C_{T}^{(2)}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}.

ii) We consider next the wave equation. In this case,
G_{t}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
t>0
.
Hypothesis B holds since

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq t^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\mu(d\xi)\leq 2t^{2}\int_{|\xi|\leq 1}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi),

\displaystyle\int_{|\xi|>1}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\int_{|\xi|>1}\frac{1}{|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)\leq 2\int_{|\xi|>1}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi).

To show that Hypothesis C holds, we use Remark
3.5
.
Clearly,
t\mapsto\frac{\sin(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|}
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. To check (ii), we fix
t>0
and we let
h\in[-\varepsilon_{t},\varepsilon_{t}]
, for some
\varepsilon_{t}>0
(to be chosen later). Then

\displaystyle|\mathcal{F}G_{t+h}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|

\displaystyle=\frac{\big|\sin\big((t+h)|\xi|\big)-\sin\big(t|\xi|\big)\big|}{|\xi|}=\frac{2\big|\sin\big(\frac{h|\xi|}{2}\big)\cos\big(\frac{(2t+h)|\xi|}{2}\big)\big|}{|\xi|}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\big|\sin\big(\frac{h|\xi|}{2}\big)\big|}{|\xi|}

We bound the last expression by
|h|\leq\varepsilon_{t}
if
|\xi|\leq 1
, and by
\frac{2}{|\xi|}
if
|\xi|>1
. Therefore, it suffices to choose
\varepsilon_{t}=1
and
k_{t}(\xi):=1_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}+\frac{2}{|\xi|}1_{\{|\xi|>1\}}
.
Finally, Hypothesis D holds since there exist some constants
C_{T}^{(3)},C_{T}^{(4)}>0
such that: (see e.g. Lemma 6.1 of
[
25
]
)

C_{T}^{(3)}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\leq\int_{0}^{T}\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}dt\leq C_{T}^{(4)}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}.

∎

We will use the following result.

Theorem 3.9

(Stochastic Fubini Theorem)
.

Let
\Phi:[0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
be such that:

a)
\Phi(t,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
t\in[0,T]
, and the map
t\mapsto\Phi(t,\cdot)
is continuous in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
;

b)
\Phi\in L^{2}([0,T]\times\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Then the process
\{Z(t)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Phi(t,x)L(dx)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
has a jointly measurable modification denoted by
\{\widetilde{Z}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
,

\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Z}(t)|dt<\infty\ \mbox{a.s.},\quad\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi(t,x)|dt<\infty\ \mbox{for almost all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$,}

the function
x\mapsto\int_{0}^{T}\Phi(t,x)dt
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{Z}(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{T}\Phi(t,x)dt\right)L(dx)\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

(18)

Proof.

The process

\{Z(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
is continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
: if
t_{n}\to t\in[0,T]
, then

\mathbb{E}|Z(t_{n})-Z(t)|^{2}=m_{2}\|\Phi(t_{n},\cdot)-\Phi(t,\cdot)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\to 0\quad\mbox{as $n\to\infty$}.

By Proposition 3.21 of
[
23
]
,
\{Z(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
has a jointly measurable modification

\{\widetilde{Z}(t)\}_{t\in[0,T]}
. By condition b),

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Z}(t)|^{2}dt=\int_{0}^{T}\mathbb{E}|Z(t)|^{2}dt=m_{2}\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(t,x)|^{2}dxdt<\infty.

Hence,
\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Z}(t)|dt<\infty
, which implies that
\int_{0}^{T}|\widetilde{Z}(t)|dt<\infty
a.s.

On the other hand, condition b) shows that the right-hand side of (
18
) is well-defined. The fact that
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi(t,x)|^{2}dxdt<\infty
implies that
\int_{0}^{T}|\Phi(t,x)|^{2}dt<\infty
for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and hence
\varphi(x):=\int_{0}^{T}\Phi(t,x)dt
is well-defined for almost all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By Hölder’s inequality,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\varphi(x)|^{2}dx\leq T\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(t,x)|^{2}dxdt<\infty
, and hence, and
L(\varphi)
is well-defined.

Finally, it is enough to prove (
18
) for functions
\Phi
of the form
\Phi(t,x)=1_{A}(t)1_{B}(x)
for
A\in\mathcal{B}([0,T])
and
B\in\mathcal{B}_{b}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. In this case,
Z(t)=1_{A}(t)L(B)
is jointly measurable in
(\omega,t)
, so that we may take
\widetilde{Z}(t)=Z(t)
, and
\varphi(x)=|A|1_{B}(x)
. Hence,

\int_{0}^{T}\widetilde{Z}(t)dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)L(dx)=|A|L(B).

∎

We introduce the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis E.

For any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, either one of the following conditions holds:

(i) the map
(s,y)\mapsto\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)
is measurable on
[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(ii) there exists a measurable function
g:[0,t]\times\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
depending on
(t,x)
, such that

g(s,\cdot)=G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\ \mbox{in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})$, \ for almost all $s\in[0,t]$}.

(19)

We identify
G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa
with
g(s,\cdot)
, and we write
G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa
instead of
g(s,\cdot)
.

Theorem 3.10
.

Suppose that Hypotheses A to E hold.

Let
\{\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
be a jointly measurable modification of the process
\{Z^{(t,x)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
defined in (

(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, the function

y\mapsto\int_{0}^{t}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)ds
belongs to
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and

v(t,x)=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Z}^{(t,x)}(s)ds=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)ds\right)L(dy).

(20)

Proof.

We fix
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. We apply Theorem
3.9
to the function

\Phi(s,y)=\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)\quad\mbox{for $s\in[0,t],y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

Hypotheses B and C ensure that condition a) is satisfied:
\Phi(s,\cdot)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for all
s\in[0,t]
(by Lemma
3.4
), and if
s_{n}\to s\in[0,t]
, then by Plancherel’s theorem,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(s_{n},y)-\Phi(s,y)|^{2}dy=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s_{n}}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\to 0.

Hypotheses D and E ensure that condition b) is satisfied:
(s,y)\mapsto\Phi(s,y)
is measurable on

, and by Plancherel’s theorem,

\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\Phi(s,y)|^{2}dyds=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}d\xi<\infty.

∎

From relations (

We check the validity of Hypothesis E for the heat and wave equations.

Lemma 3.13
.

a) Hypothesis E.(i) holds for the fundamental solution of the heat equation with

b) Hypothesis E.(ii) holds for the fundamental solution of the wave equation with

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we take

\big(G_{t}*\kappa\big)(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t}(x-y)\kappa(y)dy.

b) We apply Proposition 1.2.25 of
[
14
]
to the spaces
S=[0,T],T=\mathbb{R}^{d},X=\mathbb{R}
and the function

Then

is measurable with respect to the Borel

. By Proposition 1.2.2,

is Bochner integrable. By Proposition 1.2.25,

, there exists a measurable function

in

3.2
Moments of the solution

In this section, we study the existence of the moments of the random field solution of the linear stochastic heat and wave equations, in the case of some examples of kernels
\kappa
.

As in
[
5
]
, for any
t>0
and
p\geq 2
, we define

J_{p}(t)=\|G_{t}*\kappa\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}.

We have the following general result.

Lemma 3.15
.

Suppose that Hypotheses B and C hold. Let
v
be the random field solution of equation (
11
) and
p\geq 2
be such that
m_{p}<\infty
. Then for any
t>0
,
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, we have:

\|v(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\int_{0}^{t}\Big(\big(J_{2}(s)\big)^{1/2}+\big(J_{p}(s)\big)^{1/2}\Big)ds,

where
\mathcal{C}_{p}
is the constant from Corollary
2.3
.

Proof.

Using representation (
15
) and Minkowski’s inequality, we have:

\|v(t,x)\|_{p}\leq\int_{0}^{t}\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)\right\|_{p}ds.

Applying Corollary
2.3
, we infer that:

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)\right\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq\mathcal{C}_{p}\Big(\|G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}+\|G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}\Big)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{C}_{p}\Big(\big(J_{2}(s)\big)^{1/2}+\big(J_{p}(s)\big)^{1/2}\Big).

∎

By focusing on the heat and wave equations and particular kernels
\kappa
, we will obtain explicit conditions on
p
which guarantee that the solution
v(t,x)
has a finite
p
-th moment.

We will need the following result. Recall that the kernels
H_{d,\alpha}
and
R_{d,\alpha}
are given by Examples
3.17
and
1.2
.

Theorem 3.16

(Theorem 4.4 of
[
5
]
)
.

a) If
\kappa=H_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha>0
, then

J_{p}(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}e^{-\frac{\alpha|\xi|^{2}}{2}}d\xi\quad\mbox{for any $p>0$}.

b) If
\kappa=R_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in(0,d)
, then

J_{p}(t)\leq C\|G_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad p>\frac{2d}{2d-\alpha},

provided that
G_{t}\in L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, where
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}
.

c) If
\kappa\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
(in particular if
\kappa=B_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha>0
), then

J_{p}(t)\leq C\|G_{t}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}\quad\mbox{for any}\quad p\geq 1,

provided that
G_{t}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. (Here
C>0
is a constant which depends on
(d,\alpha,p)
.)

We now examine the existence of the moments for the solution of the linear stochastic heat and wave equations. We note that the conditions that we obtain below are
weaker
than those encountered in Examples 4.7-4.12 for the linear heat and wave equations driven by a
time-dependent
Lévy colored noise with the same spatial covariance kernel
\kappa
.

Example 3.17

(Heat Equation)
.

In this case,
G_{t}^{q}(x)=c_{q}t^{\frac{d}{2}(1-q)}G_{t/q}(x)
, and hence

\|G_{t}\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}=c_{q}^{\frac{1}{q}}t^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{q}-1\big)}\quad\mbox{for any $q>0$}.

(21)

Assume that (D) holds, and let
v
be the random field solution of (
16
).

(i)

Suppose that
\kappa=H_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha>0
. By Theorem
3.16
.a),

By Lemma
3.15

\|v(t,x)\|_{p}\leq Ct\quad\mbox{for any $p\geq 2$ such that $m_{p}<\infty$}.

(ii)

Suppose that
\kappa=R_{d,\alpha/2}
for some
\alpha\in\big((d-2)\vee 0,d\big)

By Lemma
3.15
,

\displaystyle\|v(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(s^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}-1\big)}+s^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}+\frac{\alpha}{2d}-1\big)}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=Ct^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{\alpha}{2d}-1\big)+1}\big(t^{\frac{d}{4}}+t^{\frac{d}{2p}}\big),

for any

and

, i.e.

, condition (

) holds for any

. Since we must also have

(iii)

Suppose that

. By Theorem

.c) and (

J_{p}(t)\leq Ct^{d\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)}\quad\mbox{for any $p\geq 1$}.

By Lemma

\displaystyle\|v(t,x)\|_{p}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{0}^{t}\Big(s^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{2}-1\big)}+s^{\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)}\Big)ds

\displaystyle=Ct^{-\frac{d}{2}+1}\big(t^{\frac{d}{4}}+t^{\frac{d}{2p}}\big),

for any
p\geq 2
such that
m_{p}<\infty
and
\frac{d}{2}\big(\frac{1}{p}-1\big)+1>0
, i.e.

p(d-2)<d.

If

In summary, (

) holds in the following cases:

\displaystyle d\leq 2,

\displaystyle\quad p\geq 2\ \mbox{such that}\ m_{p}<\infty,

\displaystyle d=3,

3.3
Distribution-valued solution

In this section, we introduce another concept of solution for the linear equation, and we analyze its relationship with the random field solution, similarly to Section 4 of
[
10
]
.

The new definition is motivated by the following formal calculation. We fix
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We multiply (
12
) by
\phi(x)
and we integrate
dx
. Using a formal stochastic Fubini theorem, we obtain:

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}v(t,x)\phi(x)dx=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)\phi(x)dx\right)X(dy)ds=\int_{0}^{t}X\big(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\big)ds.

Under Hypothesis A,
X\big(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\big)
is well-defined in
L^{2}(\Omega)
for any
s\in[0,t]
, since
\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and a function in
\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
induces a distribution which belongs to
\overline{\mathcal{P}}_{d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. But the map
s\mapsto X\big(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\big)
may not be measurable or integrable on
[0,t]
. To avoid this problem, we will work with a modification, which we define below.

Lemma 3.20
.

If Hypothesis A holds, then
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t})(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
for any
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proof.

By Theorem A.2 and Hypothesis A,
\mathcal{F}G_{t}\in\mathcal{O}_{M}
. Hence, there exist constants
C,k>0
(depending on
t
) such that
|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|^{2})^{k}
for all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
.

On the other hand, by Assumption A1.(b),
|\mathcal{F}\kappa|^{2}
is tempered, so there exists
\ell>0
such that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\,|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<\infty.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t})(\xi)|^{2}\,\mu(d\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}\,|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\,\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2k}\,|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\sup_{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\Big(|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{2k+\ell}\Big)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\right)^{\ell}\,|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<\infty,

where for the last line we used the fact that
\mathcal{F}\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
∎

We fix
s\in[0,t]
. By Lemma
A.3
, the smooth function
\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}
induces a distribution in
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.
Applying Lemma
3.4
to
S=\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}
, we infer that:

Y^{(t,\phi)}(s):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\big)(y)X(dy)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)=:Z^{(t,\phi)}(s)\quad\mbox{in $L^{2}(\Omega)$}.

We introduce the following hypothesis.

Hypothesis F.
(i) The function
t\mapsto\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)
is continuous on
\mathbb{R}_{+}
, for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
;

(ii)
|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|\leq C_{t}
for any
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
C_{t}>0
is non-decreasing in
t
.

Due to part (i) of Hypothesis F, processes
\{Y^{(t,\phi)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
and
\{Z^{(t,\phi)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
are continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, since if
s_{n}\to s\in[0,t]
, then

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|Y^{(t,\phi)}(s_{n})-Y^{(t,\phi)}(s)|^{2}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}|Z^{(t,\phi)}(s_{n})-Z^{(t,\phi)}(s)|^{2}=m_{2}\big\|\phi*\big(\widetilde{G}_{t-s_{n}}-\widetilde{G}_{t-s}\big)*\kappa\big\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s_{n}}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)\to 0.

Hence, there exists a jointly measurable process
\{\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)\}_{s\in[0,t]}
such that
Y^{(t,\phi)}(s)=\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)
a.s. for any
s\in[0,t]
. Moreover, part (ii) of Hypothesis F allows us to infer that

\mathbb{E}\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)|^{2}ds=m_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)ds<\infty.

Hence, with probability 1,
\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)|^{2}ds<\infty
and
\int_{0}^{t}|\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)|ds<\infty
.

In summary, if Hypotheses A and F hold, we define for any
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

v^{(t)}(\phi):=\int_{0}^{t}\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)ds.

(31)

We say that
\{v^{(t)};t\geq 0
} is a
distribution solution
of (
11
).
The following result gives the relation between the distribution solution and the random field solution.

Theorem 3.21
.

Suppose that Hypotheses A and F hold.
Let

H_{t}(\xi):=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)ds\qquad t>0,\ \xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}.

In order that there exists a jointly measurable, locally mean-square bounded process

V=\{V(t,x);t\geq 0,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
, continuous in mean square, such that for any
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,

v^{(t)}(\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx\quad\mbox{a.s.},

(32)

it is necessary and sufficient that

\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

(33)

and

\lim_{t\to s}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)=0.

(34)

If in addition, the random-field solution
v
of (
11
) exists and has representation (
20
),
then
V
is a modification of
v
.

Proof.

(Necessity). Assume that there exists a process
V
satisfying the conditions of the theorem. We proceed as in the proof of Theorem 11 of
[
10
]
. Fix
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Let
\psi_{n}(x)=n^{d}\psi(nx),
where
\psi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
,
\psi\geq 0
, and
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\psi(x)dx=1.
Set
\phi_{n}(x)=\psi_{n}(x-x_{0}).
We compute
\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}
in two ways. First, using (
32
) and Fubini’s theorem, we have

\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[V(t,x)V(t,y)]\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy\to\mathbb{E}|V(t,x_{0})|^{2},\qquad\mbox{as }n\to\infty,

by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem. Secondly, using (
31
) and Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi_{n})}(s)\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi_{n})}(r)\big]dsdr

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(\phi_{n}*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}*\kappa)(y)(\phi_{n}*\widetilde{G}_{t-r}*\kappa)(y)dydsdr

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t-r}(\xi)}\mu(d\xi)dsdr

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)ds\right|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

(35)

Since
\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)\to 1
as
n\to\infty
, Fatou’s lemma yields

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)ds\right|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)|^{2}\left|\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)ds\right|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{2}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{2}}\mathbb{E}|V(t,x_{0})|^{2}<\infty.

Therefore, since
V
is locally mean-square bounded,

\displaystyle\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{m_{2}}\sup_{t\in[0,T]}\mathbb{E}|V(t,x_{0})|^{2}<\infty.

Next, we prove that (
34
) holds.
By (
32
),

v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})-v^{(s)}(\phi_{n})=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}(V(t,x)-V(s,x))\phi_{n}(x)dx\quad\mbox{a.s.}

Hence, by Fubini’s theorem,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})-v^{(s)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}[(V(t,x)-V(s,x))(V(t,y)-V(s,y))]\phi_{n}(x)\phi_{n}(y)dxdy

\displaystyle\to\mathbb{E}|V(t,x_{0})-V(s,x_{0})|^{2}

as
n\to\infty
, again by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem.

On the other hand, similarly to (
35
),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})-v^{(s)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

Therefore, by Fatou’s lemma,

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi_{n}(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{2}}\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi_{n})-v^{(s)}(\phi_{n})|^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m_{2}}\mathbb{E}|V(t,x_{0})-V(s,x_{0})|^{2}.

Since
V
is continuous in mean square, the right-hand side converges to
0
as
t\to s
.

(Sufficiency). Formally,

\displaystyle\mathcal{F}\left(\int_{0}^{t}(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa)ds\right)(\xi)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa\big)(\xi)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)(\xi)\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)ds

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{t}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)}\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)ds

\displaystyle=e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)\int_{0}^{t}\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t-s}(\xi)}ds.

For
t>0
and
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define
K_{t,x}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
by

\mathcal{F}K_{t,x}(\xi)=e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)\overline{H_{t}(\xi)}.

This is well defined since the Fourier transform is an isomorphism on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
and

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}K_{t,x}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi=(2\pi)^{d}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty.

We then set

V(t,x):=L(K_{t,x}).

We first show that
V
is continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. By the isometry of
L
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|V(t,x)-V(s,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle=m_{2}\|K_{t,x}-K_{s,y}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})}^{2}=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left|e^{-i\xi\cdot x}{H_{t}(\xi)}-e^{-i\xi\cdot y}{H_{s}(\xi)}\right|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

Hence

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}|V(t,x)-V(s,y)|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)

\displaystyle\quad+2m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|e^{-i\xi\cdot x}-e^{-i\xi\cdot y}|^{2}|H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

The first term converges to
0
as
t\to s
by the assumed continuity of
t\mapsto H_{t}
in

L^{2}(\mu)
. The second converges to
0
as
x\to y
by dominated convergence, since

|e^{-i\xi\cdot x}-e^{-i\xi\cdot y}|^{2}|H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 4|H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}

and
|H_{s}|^{2}\in L^{1}(\mu)
. Therefore
V
is continuous in
L^{2}(\Omega)
. In particular,
V
is continuous in probability,
and hence admits a jointly measurable modification, which we denote again by
V
.
Moreover, for every
T>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T,\ x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}|V(t,x)|^{2}=m_{2}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty,

so
V
is locally mean-square bounded.

Next, fix
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
. We claim that

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx\right|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\left(v^{(t)}(\phi)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx\right)=\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi)|^{2},

and hence,

\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx-v^{(t)}(\phi)\right|^{2}=0
, which implies (
32
).
Indeed,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathbb{E}\left[V(t,x)V(t,y)\right]\phi(x)\phi(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}m_{2}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{t,x}(z)K_{t,y}(z)dz\right)\phi(x)\phi(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}K_{t,x}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}K_{t,y}(\xi)}d\xi\right)\phi(x)\phi(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot(x-y)}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)\phi(x)\phi(y)dxdy

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

Similarly,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}V(t,x)\phi(x)dx\right)v^{(t)}(\phi)\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\mathbb{E}\big[V(t,x)v^{(t)}(\phi)\big]dx

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{t}\mathbb{E}\big[V(t,x)\widetilde{Y}^{(t,\phi)}(s)\big]dsdx

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}K_{t,x}(z)(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}*\kappa)(z)dz\right)dsdx

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\int_{0}^{t}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\mathcal{F}K_{t,x}(\xi)\overline{\mathcal{F}(\phi*\widetilde{G}_{t-s}*\kappa)(\xi)}d\xi\right)dsdx

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\phi(x)\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-i\xi\cdot x}\overline{\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi\right)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{m_{2}}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

Finally,

\mathbb{E}|v^{(t)}(\phi)|^{2}=m_{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}\phi(\xi)|^{2}|H_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\mu(d\xi).

Suppose now that the random-field solution
v
of (
11
) exists and
v(t,x)=L(J_{t,x})
where

J_{t,x}(y)=\int_{0}^{t}(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)*\kappa)(y)ds
.
Then
J_{t,x}
coincide with
K_{t,x}
in
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, since they have the same Fourier transforms. Therefore

v(t,x)=L(J_{t,x})=L(K_{t,x})=V(t,x)
in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
So
V
is a modification of the random-field solution
v
.
∎

Corollary 3.22
.

Suppose that (D) holds, and
\mathcal{L}
is the heat operator or the wave operator in dimension
d\geq 1
. Let
\{v^{(t)};t\geq 0\}
and
\{v(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
be the distribution solution, respectively the random field solution, of equation (
11
). Then relation (
32
) holds for any
t>0
and
\phi\in\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, and
V
is a modification of
v
.

Proof.

Hypothesis F clearly holds. Conditions (
33
) and (
34
) hold under (D),
since by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

|H_{t}(\xi)-H_{s}(\xi)|^{2}\leq(t-s)\int_{s}^{t}|\mathcal{F}G_{r}(\xi)|^{2}dr\quad\mbox{for all}\ 0\leq s<t\leq T.

∎

4
Equations with multiplicative noise

In this section, we study equations with multiplicative noise, of the form:

\mathcal{L}u(t,x)=u(t,x)\dot{X}(x)\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(36)

with initial condition 1. When
\mathcal{L}
is the heat operator, equation (
36
) is called the
parabolic Anderson model
, and when
\mathcal{L}
is the wave operator, equation (
36
) is called the
hyperbolic Anderson model
.

Intuitively, a solution
u
to (
36
) satisfies:

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s}(x-y)u(s,y)X(dy)ds

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)u(s,\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)L(dy)ds

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{0}}\big(G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)u(s,\cdot)*\kappa\big)(y)z\widehat{N}(dy,dz)ds.

Two problems arise immediately from this formulation:

(i) there is no stochastic integral with respect to
X
,
L
or
\widehat{N}
for
random
integrands since this noise does not have a time component, and there is no concept of “predictability”;

(ii)
G_{t-s}(x-\cdot)u(s,\cdot)
is a product between a distribution and a function.

Therefore, the only tool that we have for studying equation (
36
) will be based on the Poisson-chaos expansion.

4.1
Malliavin calculus preliminaries

In this section, we include some basic material about Malliavin calculus with respect to the compensated Poisson random measure
\widehat{N}
.

We recall that
\mathfrak{H}=L^{2}(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
, where
(Z,\mathcal{Z},\mathfrak{m})
is given by (
1
).
Then
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}=L^{2}(Z^{n},\mathcal{Z}^{n},\mathfrak{m}^{n})
. We let
\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}
be the set of symmetric functions in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.

\bullet

Any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
which is
\mathcal{F}^{N}
-measurable has the
Poisson-chaos expansion
:

F=\mathbb{E}(F)+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}(f_{n}),\quad\mbox{for some $f_{n}\in\mathcal{H}^{\odot n}$},

(37)

where
I_{n}
is the multiple integral with respect to
\widehat{N}
, and the series is orthogonal in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.
For any
f\in\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}
,
I_{n}(f)=I_{n}(\widetilde{f})
,

\mathbb{E}[I_{n}(f)]=0\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathbb{E}|I_{n}(f)|^{2}=n!\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2},

where
\widetilde{f}
is the symmetrization of
f
, defined by:

\widetilde{f}(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})=\frac{1}{n!}\sum_{\rho\in S_{n}}f(\xi_{\rho(1)},\ldots,\xi_{\rho(n)}),\quad\mbox{for $\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n}\in{\bf Z}$},

and
S_{n}
is the set of permutations of
1,\ldots,n
.

\bullet

For any random variable
F\in L^{2}(\Omega)
with chaos expansion (
37
), we define the
Malliavin derivative
of
F
(with respect to
\widehat{N}
) by:

D_{\xi}F=\sum_{n\geq 1}nI_{n-1}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,\xi)\big),\quad\mbox{for all}\quad\xi\in{\bf Z},

provided that

\mathbb{E}\|DF\|_{\mathfrak{H}}^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}nn!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\|_{\mathfrak{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}<\infty.

In this case, we write
F\in{\rm Dom}(D)
.

\bullet

We denote by
\delta:{\rm Dom}(\delta)\to L^{2}(\Omega)
the adjoint of
D
, where
{\rm Dom}(\delta)
is the set of
V\in L^{2}(\Omega;\mathfrak{H})
for which there exists a constant
C=C_{V}>0
depending on
V
, such that

\big|\mathbb{E}\langle DF,V\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}\big|\leq C\|F\|_{2}\quad\mbox{for any $F\in{\rm Dom}(D)$}.

We say that
\delta(V)
is

the Skorohod integral
of
V
with respect to
\widehat{N}
, and we write

\delta(V)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{0}}V(x,z)\widehat{N}(\delta x,\delta z).

By duality, for any
V\in{\rm Dom}(\delta)
,

\mathbb{E}\langle DF,V\rangle_{\mathfrak{H}}=\mathbb{E}[F\delta(V)]\quad\mbox{for any $F\in{\rm Dom}(D)$}.

4.2
Existence of solution

In this section, we study the existence of the solution of equation (
36
).

Assume that a solution
u
exists. We proceed heuristically to derive the Poisson-chaos expansion of
u(t,x)
.
Formally,

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s_{1}}(x-y_{1}^{\prime})u(s_{1},y_{1}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime})dy_{1}^{\prime}L(dy_{1})ds_{1}.

We write

u(s_{1},y_{1}^{\prime})=1+\int_{0}^{s_{1}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{s_{1}-s_{2}}(y_{1}^{\prime}-y_{2}^{\prime})u(s_{2},y_{2}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{2}-y_{2}^{\prime})dy_{2}^{\prime}L(dy_{2})ds_{2}

and we insert this into the equation above. We obtain:

\displaystyle u(t,x)=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}G_{t-s_{1}}(x-y_{1}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime})dy_{1}^{\prime}L(dy_{1})ds_{1}+

\displaystyle\int_{0<s_{2}<s_{1}<t}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{4}}G_{t-s_{1}}(x-y_{1}^{\prime})G_{s_{1}-s_{2}}(y_{1}^{\prime}-y_{2}^{\prime})u(s_{2},y_{2}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{1}-y_{1}^{\prime})\kappa(y_{2}-y_{2}^{\prime})dy_{1}^{\prime}dy_{2}^{\prime}L(dy_{1})L(dy_{2})ds_{1}ds_{2}.

We replace
u(s_{s},y_{2}^{\prime})
, and we continue in this manner.
Denoting (still formally, since
G_{t}
may be a distribution),

f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)=G_{t-t_{n}}(x-x_{n})\ldots G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}(x_{2}-x_{1})1_{\{0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}},

we arrive at:

\displaystyle u(t,x)

\displaystyle=1+\int_{0}^{t}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\big(f_{1}(s_{1},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa\big)(y_{1})L(dy_{1})ds_{1}+

\displaystyle\quad\int_{0<s_{2}<s_{1}<t}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}}\big(f_{2}(s_{2},\cdot,s_{1},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes 2}\big)(y_{2},y_{1})L(dy_{2})L(dy_{1})ds_{2}ds_{1}+\ldots

and then we pass to the multiple integrals with respect to
\widehat{N}
.

This heuristic argument motivates the following rigorous definition.

We denote
\boldsymbol{t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})
and
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
, and we define

T_{n}(t)=\{\boldsymbol{t}\in[0,t]^{n};0<t_{1}<\ldots<t_{n}<t\}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\kappa^{\otimes n}(\boldsymbol{x})=\prod_{j=1}^{n}\kappa(x_{j}).

For any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
fixed, let

f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)
be the distribution in
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
whose Fourier transform is:

\mathcal{F}f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})=e^{-i(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{n})\cdot x}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\overline{\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})},

with
\boldsymbol{\xi}=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{n})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
, and the convention

t_{n+1}=t
,
x_{n+1}=x
.

In the case of the heat equation or wave equation with
d\leq 2
,
G_{t}
is a function, and
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)
is a function given by:

f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)=\prod_{j=1}^{n}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(x_{j+1}-x_{j}),\quad\mbox{for $\boldsymbol{x}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}$},

(38)

and

\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(y_{j+1}-y_{j})\kappa(x_{j}-y_{j})d\boldsymbol{y}.

We will need the following stronger forms of Hypotheses A, B, D and E.

Hypothesis A’.
For any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\geq 1
and
\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
,

f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)\in\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd}).

Hypothesis B’.
For any
t>0
,

K(t):=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi+\eta)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)<\infty
.

Hypothesis D’.
The map
(t,\xi)\mapsto\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and

A_{T}:=\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi+\eta)|^{2}\mu(d\xi)dt<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $T>0$}.

(39)

Hypothesis E’.

For any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\geq 1
, either one of the following conditions holds:

(i) the map
({\boldsymbol{t}},\boldsymbol{x})\mapsto\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})
is measurable on
T_{n}(t)\times\mathbb{R}^{nd}
;

(ii) there exists a measurable function
g_{n}:T_{n}(t)\times\mathbb{R}^{nd}\to\mathbb{R}
depending on
(t,x,n)
, such that

g_{n}({\boldsymbol{t}},\cdot)=f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\ \mbox{in $L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})$, \ for almost all $\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)$}.

(40)

We identify
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}
with
g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\cdot)
, and we write
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}
instead of
g_{n}(\boldsymbol{t},\cdot)
.

For any
(x_{1},z_{1}),\ldots,(x_{n},z_{n})\in{\bf Z}
, we denote

f_{n}^{*}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z_{n},x;t):=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{t},

By Hypothesis E’ and Fubini theorem, the integral above is well-defined (but may be infinite), and
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)
is measurable on
{\bf Z}^{n}
.

We introduce now the definition of the solution to equation (
36
).

Definition 4.1
.

The process
\{u(t,x);t\geq 0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\}
with
\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}<\infty
for all
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and the Poisson-chaos expansion:

u(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}I_{n}\big(f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\big),

(41)

is the
(Skorohod) solution
of equation (
36
), provided that
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\geq 1
, and the series converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
.

We denote

J_{n}(t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}.

(42)

Theorem 4.2
.

Suppose that Hypotheses A’, B’, D’ and E’ hold. Then
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
for any
n\geq 1
and
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. If in addition,

\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n}t^{n}J_{n}(t)<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $t>0$},

(43)

then equation (
36
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

Step 1.
In this step, we show that

f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
for any
n\geq 1
.

By Theorems
A.1
and
A.2
,
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\in\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
and

\mathcal{F}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{\xi})=\mathcal{F}f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\prod_{i=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi_{i}),

for any
\boldsymbol{\xi}\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}
. By definition (
5
) of
\mu
and Hypothesis B’, for all
\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
,

\displaystyle\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\Big|\mathcal{F}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Big|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi}

\displaystyle\quad=(2\pi)^{nd}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})

\displaystyle\quad\leq(2\pi)^{nd}K(t-t_{n})K(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\ldots K(t_{2}-t_{1})<\infty,

(44)

and hence,

\mathcal{F}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
. By Lemma A.1 of
[
5
]
, it follows that
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
for all
\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
. By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality and Plancherel’s theorem,

\displaystyle\|f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}_{0})^{n}}\big|f_{n}^{*}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z_{n},x;t)\big|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}\nu(dz_{1})\ldots\nu(dz_{n})

\displaystyle\quad=m_{2}^{n}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\left(\int_{T_{n}(t)}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})d\boldsymbol{t}\right)^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle\quad\leq\frac{m_{2}^{n}t^{n}}{n!}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\big|\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\big|^{2}d\boldsymbol{x}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{m_{2}^{n}t^{n}}{n!}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{nd}}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\Big|\mathcal{F}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\Big|^{2}\prod_{j=1}^{n}|\mathcal{F}\kappa(\xi_{j})|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi}d\boldsymbol{t}

\displaystyle\quad=\frac{m_{2}^{n}t^{n}}{n!}J_{n}(t).

Note that by (
44
),

J_{n}(t)\leq\int_{T_{n}(t)}K(t-t_{n})K(t_{n}-t_{n-1})\ldots K(t_{2}-t_{1})d\boldsymbol{t}\leq A_{t}^{n}<\infty.

This proves that
f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}
.

Step 2.
In this step, we prove that the series (
41
) converges in
L^{2}(\Omega)
, for any
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
.
Using the fact that
\|\widetilde{f}\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}\leq\|f\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}
, we see that

\sum_{n\geq 1}\mathbb{E}\big|I_{n}\big(f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\big)\big|^{2}=\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|f_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n}t^{n}J_{n}(t).

∎

Next, we examine the existence of the solution for the heat and wave equations. We start by establishing for validity of Hypotheses A’, B’, D’ and E’.

Lemma 4.3
.

Hypothesis A’ holds for the fundamental solutions of the heat and wave equations, for any
d\geq 1
.

Proof.

In the case of the heat equation, the function
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
and Hypothesis A’ follows by Lemma
A.3
.

In the case of the wave equation with
d\leq 2
,
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)
is an integrable function with compact support. This function induces a distribution in
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
.

If
d\geq 3
,
G_{t}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
for any
t>0
. Let
(t,x)\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
n\geq 1
and

{\bf t}=(t_{1},\ldots,t_{n})\in T_{n}(t)
. Then the tensor product

G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes G_{t_{n+1}-t_{n}}\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd}).

By definition,
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)
is obtained from the distribution

G_{t_{2}-t_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes G_{t_{n+1}-t_{n}}
by the change of variables

(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\mapsto(x_{2}-x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}-x_{n-1},x-x_{n}).

It follows that
f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)\in\mathcal{E}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

Hypotheses B’ and D’ hold for the fundamental solutions of the heat and wave equations with
d\geq 1
, provided that (D) holds.

Proof.

In the case of the heat equation, it is known that for any
t>0
,

K(t)=\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t|\xi+\eta|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi),

(45)

(see e.g. relation (13) of
[
4
]
), and
A_{T}=\int_{0}^{T}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}e^{-t|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)dt<\infty
, by Lemma
3.8
.

Consider next the wave equation. Note that

|\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}=\frac{\sin^{2}(t|\xi|)}{|\xi|^{2}}\leq D_{t}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\quad\mbox{with $D_{t}=2(t^{2}\vee 1)$},

(46)

and

\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi+\eta|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)=:C_{\mu}.

(47)

Hence,
K(t)\leq D_{t}C_{\mu}
for any
t>0
, and

A_{T}\leq D_{T}\int_{0}^{T}\sup_{\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\frac{1}{1+|\xi+\eta|^{2}}\mu(d\xi)dt=TD_{T}C_{\mu}.

∎

Lemma 4.5
.

a) Hypothesis E’.(i) holds for the fundamental solution of the heat equation with
d\geq 1
, and for the fundamental solution of the wave equation with
d\leq 2
.

b) Hypothesis E’.(ii) holds for the fundamental solution of the wave equation with
d\geq 3
, provided that (D) holds.

Proof.

a) The map
(t,x)\mapsto G_{t}(x)
is measurable on
\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\mathbb{R}^{d}
. By Fubini’s theorem,

(\boldsymbol{t},\boldsymbol{x})\mapsto\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{x})\quad\mbox{is measurable on $T_{n}(t)\times(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}$}.

b) We apply Proposition 1.2.25 of
[
14
]
to the spaces
S=T_{n}(t),T=\mathbb{R}^{nd},X=\mathbb{R}
and the function
F_{n}:T_{n}(t)\to L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
given by

F_{n}(\boldsymbol{t})=f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}.

By Lemmas
4.4
and
3.8
, Hypotheses B’ and C are satisfied, since (D) holds.

F_{n}
is well-defined, since by Hypothesis B’ and (
44
), for any
\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
,

\|f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})}^{2}=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{nd}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}\big|\mathcal{F}\big(f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)*\kappa^{\otimes n}\big)(\boldsymbol{\xi})\big|^{2}d\boldsymbol{\xi}<\infty.

Moreover, by Hypothesis C,
F_{n}
is continuous, since if
\boldsymbol{t}^{(k)}\to\boldsymbol{t}\in T_{n}(t)
as
k\to\infty
, then

\displaystyle\|F_{n}(\boldsymbol{t}^{(k)})-F_{n}(\boldsymbol{t})\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})}^{2}=

\displaystyle\quad\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}^{(k)}-t_{j}^{(k)}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})-\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}(\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j})\Big|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})\to 0,

as
k\to\infty
, by the dominated convergence theorem. To justify the application of this theorem, we use the following inequality, which can be proved by induction: for any
z_{1},w_{1},\ldots,z_{n},w_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
,

\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}z_{j}-\prod_{j=1}^{n}w_{j}\Big|\leq\sum_{j=1}^{n}|z_{1}\ldots z_{j-1}||z_{j}-w_{j}||w_{j+1}\ldots w_{n}|.

Hence,

\displaystyle\Big|\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}^{(k)}-t_{j}^{(k)}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\xi_{\ell}\big)-\prod_{j=1}^{n}\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\xi_{\ell}\big)\Big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq n\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{j-1}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{i+1}^{(k)}-t_{i}^{(k)}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\xi_{\ell}\big)\big|^{2}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}^{(k)}-t_{j}^{(k)}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\xi_{\ell}\big)-\mathcal{F}G_{t_{j+1}-t_{j}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{j}\xi_{\ell}\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\prod_{i=j+1}^{n}\big|\mathcal{F}G_{t_{i+1}-t_{i}}\big(\sum_{\ell=1}^{i}\xi_{\ell}\big)\big|^{2}

\displaystyle\quad\leq 8nD_{t}^{n-1}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\prod_{i=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{i}|^{2}},

which is integrable with respect to
\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})
. For this bound, we used estimate (
46
), and the fact that for any
t>0
,
h\in[-1,1]
and
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

|\mathcal{F}G_{t+h}(\xi)-\mathcal{F}G_{t}(\xi)|^{2}\leq 1_{\{|\xi|\leq 1\}}+\frac{4}{|\xi|^{2}}1_{\{|\xi|>1\}}\leq\frac{8}{1+|\xi|^{2}},

which was shown in the proof of Lemma
3.8
.

Hence,
F_{n}
is measurable with respect to the Borel
\sigma
-field of
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
.
Since
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})
is separable,
F_{n}
is strongly measurable (by Corollary 1.1.10 of
[
14
]
), and because the Lebesgue measure
\lambda_{nd}
on
\mathbb{R}^{nd}
is
\sigma
-finite,
F_{n}
is strongly
\lambda_{nd}
-measurable (by Proposition 1.1.16,
ibid
). Moreover,

\int_{T_{n}(t)}\|F_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})}^{2}d\boldsymbol{t}=\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nd}}|\mathcal{F}f_{n}(t_{1},\cdot,\ldots,t_{n},\cdot,t,x)(\boldsymbol{\xi})|^{2}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})=J_{n}(t)<\infty,

and hence,
\int_{T_{n}(t)}\|F_{n}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{nd})}dt<\infty
. By Proposition 1.2.2,
ibid.
,
F_{n}
is Bochner integrable. The conclusion follows by Proposition 1.2.25,
ibid
.

∎

Corollary 4.6
.

Let
d\geq 1
be arbitrary.
Suppose that
\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}-\frac{1}{2}\Delta
or

\mathcal{L}=\frac{\partial^{2}}{\partial t^{2}}-\Delta
. If condition (D) holds, then equation (
36
) has a unique solution.

Proof.

i) We consider first the heat equation.
By (
45
),

J_{n}(t)\leq\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}e^{-(t_{j+1}-t_{j})|\xi_{j}|^{2}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}=:h_{n}(t).

Therefore, invoking Lemma 3.8 of
[
3
]
, we infer that

\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n}t^{n}J_{n}(t)\leq\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n}t^{n}h_{n}(t)<\infty\quad\mbox{for any $t>0$}.

ii) We consider next the wave equation. Then (
43
) holds, since by (
46
) and (
47
),

J_{n}(t)\leq D_{t}^{n}\int_{T_{n}(t)}\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{n}}\prod_{j=1}^{n}\frac{1}{1+|\xi_{1}+\ldots+\xi_{j}|^{2}}\mu(d\xi_{1})\ldots\mu(d\xi_{n})d\boldsymbol{t}\leq D_{t}^{n}C_{\mu}^{n}\frac{t^{n}}{n!}.

∎

Remark 4.7

(Connection with the Gaussian case)
.

We consider the following equation

\mathcal{L}U(t,x)=\sqrt{m_{2}}U(t,x)\dot{W}(t,x),\quad t>0,x\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

(48)

with initial condition 1, where
\{W(\varphi);\varphi\in\mathcal{P}_{0,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\}
is an isonormal Gaussian process with covariance:

\mathbb{E}[W(\varphi)W(\psi)]=\langle\varphi,\psi\rangle_{0}

and
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{0}
is given by (
6
). The solution of equation (
48
) is defined similarly as above, using the Skorohod integral with respect to
W
.
Moreover, under Hypotheses A’, B’, C’ and E’, equation (
48
) has a unique solution, whose Wiener-chaos expansion is

U(t,x)=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n/2}I_{n}^{W}\big(f_{n}(\cdot,x;t)\big),

where
I_{n}^{W}
is the multiple Wiener integral of order
n
with respect to
W
, and

f_{n}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n},x;t)=\int_{T_{n}(t)}f_{n}(t_{1},x_{1},\ldots,t_{n},x_{n},t,x)d\boldsymbol{t}.

By direct calculation,

\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(x_{1},z_{1},\ldots,x_{n},z_{n},x;t)=\prod_{i=1}^{n}z_{i}(\widetilde{f}_{n}\big(\cdot,x;t)*\kappa^{\otimes}\big)(\boldsymbol{x}),

and hence,
\|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=m_{2}^{n}\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\big(\cdot,x;t)\|_{(\mathcal{P}_{0,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\otimes n}}^{2}
. This means that the solutions
u
and
U
have the same second moments:

\mathbb{E}|u(t,x)|^{2}=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}^{*}(\cdot,x;t)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{\otimes n}}^{2}=1+\sum_{n\geq 1}m_{2}^{n}n!\|\widetilde{f}_{n}\big(\cdot,x;t)\|_{(\mathcal{P}_{0,d}(\mathbb{R}^{d}))^{\otimes n}}^{2}=\mathbb{E}|U(t,x)|^{2}.

Appendix A
Distributions with rapid decrease

In this section, we recall some properties of distributions which are needed in the sequel, following Chapter VII of
[
26
]
. In this section, all functions and distributions are considered on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, but we drop
\mathbb{R}^{d}
from the notation, e.g. we write
\mathcal{D}
instead of
\mathcal{D}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

We begin by recalling the definitions of several spaces of functions, and of distributions.

•

\mathcal{D}
is the set of
C^{\infty}
-functions with compact support, and
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}
is the set of distributions.

•

\mathcal{S}
is the set of rapidly decreasing
C^{\infty}
-functions, and
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
is the set of tempered distributions. A
C^{\infty}
-function
\varphi:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is

rapidly decreasing
if for any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d}),\beta=(\beta_{1},\ldots,\beta_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
,

\sup_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}|x^{\alpha}D^{\beta}\varphi(x)|<\infty,

where
x^{\alpha}=x_{1}^{\alpha_{1}}\ldots x_{d}^{\alpha_{d}}
and
D^{\beta}=\frac{\partial^{|\beta|}}{\partial x_{1}^{\beta_{1}}\ldots\partial x_{d}^{\beta_{d}}}
, with
|\beta|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}\beta_{i}
.

•

\mathcal{E}
is the set of
C^{\infty}
-functions, and
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
is the set of distributions with compact support.

•

\mathcal{O}_{M}
is the set of
C^{\infty}
-functions
\varphi
with
slow increase
(see p.243 of
[
26
]
), i.e. for any
\alpha=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{d})\in\mathbb{N}^{d}
, there exist
C>0
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
such that

|D^{\alpha}\varphi(x)|\leq C(1+x^{2})^{k}\quad\mbox{for all $x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}$}.

•

\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}
is the set of distributions
F

with rapid decrease
(see p.243 of
[
26
]
), i.e.

(1+x^{2})^{k/2}F
is a bounded distribution, for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

(The space of bounded distributions is the dual of
\mathcal{D}_{L^{1}}=\{\varphi\in\mathcal{E};\,D^{\alpha}\varphi\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{d})\linebreak\mbox{for all $\alpha\in\mathbb{N}^{d}$}\}
; see p. 200 of
[
26
]
.)

Then

\mathcal{D}\subset\mathcal{S}\subset\mathcal{O}_{M}\subset\mathcal{E}\quad\mbox{and}\quad\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{D}^{\prime}.

The

of

\big(\mathcal{F}F,\psi\big)=\big(F,\mathcal{F}\varphi\big)\quad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{S}$}.

If

\big(G*F,\varphi\big)=\big(F,\varphi*\widetilde{G}\big)\quad\mbox{for all $\varphi\in\mathcal{D}$},

Lemma A.3
.

Any function
\varphi\in\mathcal{S}
can be viewed as a distribution in
\mathcal{O}_{C}^{\prime}
.

Proof.

Let
T_{\varphi}
be the distribution induced by
\varphi
, given by
(T_{\varphi},\phi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}\varphi(x)\phi(x)dx
for all
\phi\in\mathcal{D}
. We want to prove for any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,

(1+x^{2})^{k/2}T_{\varphi}\in(D_{L^{1}})^{\prime}.

For any
k\in\mathbb{N}
,
b_{k}:=(1+x^{2})^{k/2}\varphi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, hence it is a bounded function. For any
\psi\in D_{L^{1}}
, let
T_{k}(\psi):=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b_{k}(x)\psi(x)dx
. Note that
T_{k}
is well-defined, since

\left|\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d}}b_{k}(x)\psi(x)dx\right|\leq\|b_{k}\|_{\infty}\|\psi\|_{L^{1}}.

Moreover,
T_{k}
is linear and continuous on
D_{L^{1}}
, so
T_{k}\in(D_{L^{1}})^{\prime}
.
T_{k}
is exactly the distribution associated with
(1+|x|^{2})^{k/2}T_{\varphi}
. Hence,
(1+x^{2})^{k/2}T_{\varphi}=T_{k}\in(D_{L^{1}})^{\prime}
.
