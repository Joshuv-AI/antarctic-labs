Affine transverse foliations in sphere bundles
Ilya Alekseev, Ivan Nasonov, Gaiane Panina

Abstract
. Let S n − 1 → E → M n S^{n-1}\rightarrow E\rightarrow M^{n} be an oriented sphere bundle over an oriented closed manifold with amenable fundamental group. We provide an elementary proof that the Euler number of the bundle vanishes whenever the bundle admits a smooth transverse affine foliation.



2000 Mathematics Subject Classification
55R10, 53C12

1.
Introduction

By an
oriented sphere bundle
over a closed smooth oriented manifold
M^{n}
we mean a locally trivial fiber bundle

\pi:E\rightarrow M^{n}

whose fibers are oriented spheres
S^{n-1}
.

An
oriented sphere bundle with a smooth transverse affine foliation

F
(or an
affinely foliated bundle
, for short) is an oriented sphere bundle
E
that is obtained from the following construction:

There is an oriented real vector bundle
\widetilde{E}\rightarrow M^{n}
of rank
n
(transition functions are orientation preserving homomorphisms of vector spaces).
The total space
\widetilde{E}
carries a
transverse foliation

\widetilde{F}
whose smooth
n
-dimensional leaves are transverse to the fibers.
Equivalently,
E
admits a flat connection.
The foliation
\widetilde{F}
is
affine
: the defining connection (isomorphisms between nearby fibers) are linear homomorphisms.
In particular, the zero section is a leaf.
Equivalently, the image of the holonomy representation of
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
induced by
\widetilde{F}
lies in
{\rm SL}(n;\mathbb{R})
.
The foliation
\widetilde{F}
induces a transverse foliation
F
on the associated sphere bundle
E
.

Once orientations of
M^{n}
and the fibers are fixed, the Euler class of the bundle may be identified with an integer
\mathcal{E}
.
When
n
is odd, the Euler number vanishes for any oriented
S^{n-1}
-bundle, so the content is in even dimensions.

Example.
The trivial bundle
M^{n}\times S^{n-1}\rightarrow M^{n}
has a trivial foliation by
horizontal leaves

M^{n}\times\{\omega\}
. Its Euler number is zero.

Our main aim is to give an elementary proof of the following theorem:

Theorem 1
.

Let
M^{n}
be a closed oriented smooth manifold whose fundamental group is amenable and let
S^{n-1}\rightarrow E\rightarrow M^{n}
be an oriented sphere bundle.
If
E
admits a smooth transverse affine foliation, then the Euler number of the bundle vanishes.

In other words, for amenable
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
, the existence of an affine transverse structure forces the primary obstruction to a global section to vanish.

Our proof relies on ideas of D. Sullivan
[
12
]
and on M. Kazarian’s averaging principle
[
7
]
, together with the Følner property of amenable groups.

We also sketch below a proof of Theorem
1
based on deep insights of Gromov, Sullivan, Thurston, and Ivanov.

Example.

If
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is finite, transverse foliations exist only for bundles with zero Euler number.

This is a well-known fact; we give a proof for completeness and as a warm-up.
If
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is finite, the universal cover
\mathcal{U}
of
M^{n}
is a compact closed manifold. A bundle with a transverse foliation over
M^{n}
induces a bundle with a transverse foliation over
\mathcal{U}
.
Since
\mathcal{U}
is simply connected, each leaf of the induced foliation is a continuous section of the bundle, hence the Euler number vanishes.
On the other hand, the Euler numbers of the two bundles differ by the multiple
|\pi_{1}(M^{n})|
.

Example.
For
n=2
all circle bundles that support affine transverse foliations are found by J. Milnor:

Theorem 2
.

[
9
,
10
]

An oriented circle bundle
E\xrightarrow[\text{}]{\pi}S_{g}
over a closed oriented surface of genus
g

admits a smooth transverse affine foliation if and only if the Euler number of the bundle satisfies

|\mathcal{E}|\leqslant g-1.

Remark.
The group
\pi_{1}(S_{g})
is amenable if and only if
g\leqslant 1
(for
g>1
it has an exponential growth); so Theorem
2
agrees with Theorem
1
.

1.1.
How to compute the Euler number

The Euler number of an oriented sphere bundle
E\rightarrow B
is the obstruction to the existence of a continuous section. We briefly recall the following standard construction (see
[
2
]
).

Assume
that a partial section of the bundle
s:M^{n}\setminus\{x_{i}\}\rightarrow E
is defined everywhere except for a finite set of points
x_{1},...,x_{m}\in M^{n}
.
For each point
x_{i}
, choose a small neighborhood
U_{i}
bounded by a sphere
C_{x_{i}}\subset M^{n}
. The sphere inherits the orientation from
M^{n}
.
Next, choose a trivialization of the bundle in the neighborhood
U_{i}
.
The restriction of the section
s

defines a map

s_{\mid_{C_{x_{i}}}}:C_{x_{i}}\rightarrow S^{n-1}.

The source and the target spaces are two oriented spheres,
so the degree of the map is well-defined. Set

ind_{s}(x_{i}):=deg\penalty\ s_{\mid_{C_{x_{i}}}}.

The individual indices
ind_{s}(x_{i})
depend on the section
s
, but their sum does not:

Proposition 1
.

[
9
]
,
[
2
]
.
In the above notation, the Euler number of the bundle equals the sum of indices:

\mathcal{E}(E\rightarrow M^{n})=\sum_{i}ind_{s}(x_{i}).

This construction applies not only for smooth manifolds, but also for finite cell complexes of dimension
n
provided that the points
x_{i}
lie inside
n
-cells.

1.2.
Proof of Theorem
1
via ingredients of M. Gromov, J. Hirsh, W. Thurston, and D. Sullivan

The proof can be divided into three steps:

(1)
D. Sullivan
[
12
]
proved that if an affine sphere bundle has a smooth transverse foliation, then its Euler number does not exceed the number of
n
-simplices in any triangulation of the base
M^{n}
.

Proof.

Assume there is an affine oriented bundle with a transverse foliation. Take a cell decomposition of
M^{n}
which is dual to some triangulation.
Choose a partial section which is defined on the interiors of the cells by taking parts of leaves of the foliation. Using the affine structure of the foliation, extend the partial section first to the interiors of the cells of codimension
1
, then to the interiors of the cells of codimension
2
, etc. Eventually one arrives at a continuous partial section
s
defined everywhere except for the vertices of the cell decomposition. It is easy to show that each vertex
v

contributes an index
ind_{s}(v)
whose absolute value is at most
1
, which completes the proof.
∎

We are going to make use of this idea in our proof of Theorem
1
.

(2)
If
\pi_{1}(M^{n})
is amenable, then the simplicial volume
||M^{n}||
vanishes.

Proof.

This follows from the combination of the following facts:

(A)
The simplicial volume
||M^{n}||
vanishes iff the top bounded cohomologies are trivial.
(B)
Bounded cohomology depends only on the fundamental group
[
5
,
6
]
.
(C)
Bounded cohomologies of amenable groups are trivial
[
4
]
.
∎

(3)
If
||M^{n}||
vanishes,
then the statement of Theorem
1
is valid for
M^{n}
.

Proof.

By definition, if
||M^{n}||
vanishes, there is a singular cycle

N^{n}\rightarrow M^{n}

with a small number
S
of simplices in
N^{n}
and a big degree
deg
of the map More precisely, the ratio
S/deg
can be made arbitrarily small by a choice of
N^{n}
.

Each foliated sphere bundle with Euler number
\mathcal{E}
can be pulled back to a bundle over
N^{n}
. The Euler class
\widetilde{\mathcal{E}}
of the pullback equals
deg\cdot\mathcal{E}
. On the other hand, by Sullivan’s estimate from step (1), the absolute value of this Euler number is bounded by

, which implies
|\mathcal{E}|\leqslant\frac{S}{deg}
.

∎

Evaluation of

The vertices of

An ordered

For each vertex

\mathbb{E}(ind_{s}(v))=\frac{\mathcal{NC}(v,+)-\mathcal{NC}(v,-)}{k(v)(k(v)+1)(k(v)+2)...(k(v)+n)}.

Proof.

So

Each choice of a sheet over each of the

A contribution of each

All the

\widetilde{\mathcal{B}}

As before, a choice of a random partial section over the

The repetitions are excluded by construction, since the points of

(1)

(2)

Let us explain the matching of tuples more precisely. We may assume that the entries

r_{i_{2}}^{j_{2}}

So the tuples that are not canceled, are those that do not contain two entries of type

They look like

Such tuples exist only for a vertex of type

The number of all possible tuples equals

So for the absolute value of averaged index we have:

\displaystyle\Big|\mathbb{E}(ind_{s}(v))\Big|

\displaystyle\leqslant\frac{k(v)\cdot N\cdot(N-1)\cdot...\cdot(N-n)}{k(v)(k(v)+1)...(k(v)+n)\cdot N\cdot(N-1)\cdot...\cdot(N-n)}
