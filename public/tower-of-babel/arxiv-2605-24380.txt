Fibrations, the First Betti Number, and Almost Nonnegative Ricci Curvature
Hongzhi Huang, Xian-Tao Huang, Jikang Wang, and Xingyu Zhu

Abstract
. In this paper, we prove fibration theorems for manifolds with almost nonnegative Ricci curvature and certain extra regularity assumptions. We show that a closed n n -manifold M M satisfying diam ( M ) 2 sec M ≥ − κ and diam ( M ) 2 Ric M ≥ − δ , \displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\sec_{M}\geq-\kappa\quad\text{and}\quad\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\mathrm{Ric}_{M}\geq-\delta, where δ > 0 \delta>0 is sufficiently small depending only on n n and κ \kappa , fibers over a b 1 ​ ( M ) b_{1}(M) -torus. This removes the upper sectional curvature bound required in the earlier result of Yamaguchi [ 54 ] . As a corollary, we obtain a refinement of Yamaguchi’s smooth fibration theorem ( [ 55 ] ), showing that the fiber itself (rather than a finite cover of it) fibers over a b 1 b_{1} -torus. Our results extend to manifolds satisfying a generalized Reifenberg condition introduced in [ 32 ] , which encompasses both a lower bound on sectional curvature and the local rewinding Reifenberg condition. In the nonsmooth setting, a similar result also holds for a non-collapsed RCD ⁡ ( − ϵ ⁡ ( D , r , n ) , n ) \mathrm{RCD}(-\epsilon(D,r,n),n) space whose diameter is bounded by D D and which satisfies the ( r , δ ⁡ ( n ) ) (r,\delta(n)) -local rewinding Reifenberg condition. The proofs rely on an equivariant regularity theorem for almost submetries under a lower Ricci curvature bound. In addition, we study the stability of rank of Abelian actions along equivariant Gromov-Hausdorff convergence in this paper. 2010 Mathematics Subject Classification : 53C21, 53C23, 53B21.



1.
Introduction

A closed manifold
M
is said to have
almost nonnegative Ricci curvature
if there exists a small constant
\delta(n)>0
, depending only on the dimension
n
of
M
, such that its diameter and the minimal eigenvalue of its Ricci tensor satisfy

(1.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\mathrm{Ric}_{M}\geq-\delta(n).

Gromov
[
27
]
(see also Gallot
[
25
]
) proved that if
M
satisfies (
1.1
) for a sufficiently small
\delta(n)
, then the first Betti number
b_{1}(M)\leq n
.
Furthermore, Gromov conjectured that the equality
b_{1}(M)=n
implies that
M
is diffeomorphic to the flat
n
-torus, a conjecture that was finally resolved by Colding
[
19
]
.

A natural question arises: under the pinching condition (
1.1
), what specific geometric structure is guaranteed when
b_{1}(M)<n
? Given an upper bound on sectional curvature, Yamaguchi proved the following fibration theorem.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
54
]
)
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
and
\kappa>0
, there exists
\delta(n,\kappa)>0
such that the following holds. Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold satisfying (
1.1
) and

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\,\sec_{M}\leq\kappa.

Then
M
fibers over a
b_{1}(M)
-dimensional torus.

Yamaguchi (
[
54
]
) further conjectured that the conclusion remains true even without the upper bound on sectional curvature. However, Anderson (
[
2
]
) constructed a counterexample as follows: for
n\geq 4
, there exists a sequence of closed
n
-manifolds
\{M_{i}\}
such that
|\mathrm{Ric}_{M_{i}}|\to 0
,
b_{1}(M_{i})=k
(where
k\leq n-1
), and
M_{i}
converges in the Gromov–Hausdorff sense to a flat torus
T^{k}
, but no cover of
M_{i}
fibers over
S^{1}
.

When the pinching condition (
1.1
) is strengthened to the case of almost nonnegative sectional curvature, Yamaguchi established the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
55
]
)
.

There exists a constant
\delta(n)>0
, depending only on
n
, such that any compact
n
-manifold
M
satisfying

(1.2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\sec_{M}\geq-\delta(n)

has the following properties:

(a)

A finite cover of
M
fibers over a torus of dimension
b_{1}(M)
;

(b)

If
b_{1}(M)=n
, then
M
is diffeomorphic to the
n
-torus.

In fact, this theorem is a special case of Yamaguchi’s general smooth fibration theorem in
[
55
]
. The general theorem states that if a manifold with uniformly lower bound of sectional curvature collapses in the Gromov-Hausdorff sense to a lower-dimensional smooth manifold, then the collapsing yields a smooth fibration whose fiber satisfies the topological properties described above.

Note that under the hypotheses of Theorem
1.1
, the manifold
M
admits a uniform lower bound on its sectional curvature, which depends only on
n
and
\kappa
. In
[
54
]
, this two-sided bound on sectional curvature is crucial for establishing regularity estimates for exponential maps and for carrying out a certain smoothing procedure of Riemannian metrics.

Recent advances in metric geometry under lower Ricci curvature bounds allow us to remove the upper sectional curvature assumption in Theorem
1.1
and to avoid passing to a finite cover in Theorem
1.2
. A main result of this paper unifies Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
as follows.

Theorem A
.

For any
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
,
\kappa>0
, there exists
\delta(n,\kappa)>0
, such that the following holds. Let
M
be an
n
-dimensional closed Riemannian manifold satisfying (
1.1
), and

(1.3)

\mathrm{diam}(M)^{2}\,\sec_{M}\geq-\kappa.

Then
M
fibers over a
b_{1}(M)
-torus with connected fibers.

By
[
36
]
, manifolds satisfying (
1.1
) have virtually nilpotent fundamental groups.
The following is an immediate corollary of the above theorem.

Corollary 1.3
.

Suppose
M
satisfies assumptions of Theorem

\pi_{1}(M)
is infinite, then the Euler characteristic number
M
is
0
.

Furthermore, we can adapt Yamaguchi’s smooth fibration theorem (
[

Theorem 1.4
.

Given

r_{0}>0

Suppose

\displaystyle\sec_{M}\geq-1,

\displaystyle d_{\mathrm{GH}}(M,N)<\delta.

Then there exists a smooth fibration

Remark 1.5

Suppose we have a fibration

with connected fiber

(1.7)

\displaystyle\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{n}(F)\rightarrow\pi_{n}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{n}(T^{k})\rightarrow\pi_{n-1}(F)\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow\pi_{0}(E)\rightarrow 0.

In particular, we have

\displaystyle 0=\pi_{2}(T^{k})\rightarrow\pi_{1}(F)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(M)\rightarrow\pi_{1}(T^{k})\rightarrow\pi_{0}(F)=0.

Can one relax the smoothness assumption on

Now we turn to fibration theorems in the

Our next result also contributes to Problem

Remark 1.8
.

Thanks to the work of Cheeger-Fukaya-Gromov

Our next result is as follows.

Theorem B
.

Let

Another contribution in this paper is the following observation on the stability of rank of Abelian actions along equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff convergence.

Let

Assume that

2.1.
Equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff convergence and isometry groups

We review the notion of equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff convergence introduced by Fukaya and Yamaguchi
[
24
,
23
]
.

Let
(X,p)
and
(Y,q)
be two pointed metric spaces. Let
H
and
K
be closed subgroups of
\mathrm{Isom}(X)
and
\mathrm{Isom}(Y)
, respectively. For any
r>0
, define the sets

H(r)=\{h\in H|d(hp,p)<r\},\ K(r)=\{k\in K|d(kq,q)<r\}.

For
\epsilon>0
, a pointed
\epsilon
-equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff approximation (or simply an
\epsilon
-eGHA) is a triple of maps
(f,\phi,\psi)
, where

f:B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(p)\to B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\epsilon}(q),\quad\phi:H\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\to K\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right),\quad\psi:K\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)\to H\left(\frac{1}{\epsilon}\right)

satisfy the following conditions:

(1)

f(p)=q
,
f(B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(p))
is
2\epsilon
-dense in
B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}+\epsilon}(q)
and
|d(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))-d(x_{1},x_{2})|\leq\epsilon
for all
x_{1},x_{2}\in B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(p)
;

(2)

d(\phi(h)f(x),f(hx))<\epsilon
for all
h\in H(\frac{1}{\epsilon})
and
x\in B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(p)
;

(3)

d(kf(x),f(\psi(k)x))<\epsilon
for all
k\in K(\frac{1}{\epsilon})
and
x\in B_{\frac{1}{\epsilon}}(p)
.

The equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff (eGH in short) distance
d_{GH}((X_{i},p_{i},G_{i}),(X,p,G))
is defined as the infimum of
\epsilon
so that there exists an
\epsilon
-eGHA.
We say a sequence of metric spaces with isometric actions
(X_{i},p_{i},G_{i})
converges to a limit space
(X,p,G)
, if
d_{\mathrm{GH}}((X_{i},p_{i},G_{i}),(X,p,G))\to 0
. We denote the convergence by
(X_{i},p_{i},G_{i})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{GH}}(X,p,G)
for short.

We have the following pre-compactness theorem for equivariant Gromov–Hausdorff convergence (see
[
24
,
23
]
).

Theorem 2.1

(
[
24
,
23
]
)

Let
(X_{i},p_{i})
be a sequence of metric spaces converging to a limit space
(X,p)
in the pointed Gromov–Hausdorff sense. For each

, let
G_{i}
be a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}(X_{i})
. Then passing to a subsequence if necessary,

(X_{i},p_{i},G_{i})\xrightarrow{\mathrm{GH}}(X,p,G),

where

is a closed subgroup of
\mathrm{Isom}(X)
. Moreover, the quotient spaces
(X_{i}/G_{i},\bar{p}_{i})
pointed Gromov–Hausdorff converge to
(X/G,p)
.

6.

6.1.
Upper semi-continuity of the rank

For an Abelian Lie group

Lemma 6.1
.

Let

Proof.

The case

\mathrm{rank}(G_{i})=k

6.3.1.
Groupification of local groups

In this subsection, we explain how local groups determine a covering space in the

Let

Let
