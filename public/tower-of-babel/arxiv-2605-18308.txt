The edit distance of word-representable and comparability graphs
Sergey Kitaev, Ryan R. Martin

Abstract
. In this paper, we establish that the maximum edit distance of an n n -vertex graph from the hereditary property of word-representable graphs is n 2 / 8 − o ⁡ ( n 2 ) n^{2}/8-o(n^{2}) . In addition, we establish that the maximum edit distance of an n n -vertex graph from the hereditary property of poset comparability graphs is 5 ​ n 2 / 32 − o ⁡ ( n 2 ) 5n^{2}/32-o(n^{2}) . In fact, we determine the edit distance function over all edge densities p ∈ [ 0 , 1 ] p\in[0,1] for the property of word-representable graphs, for the property of k k -word-representable graphs for each k ≥ 2 k\geq 2 , and for the property comparability graphs. The latter has a peculiar structure that requires an infinite sequence of colored regularity graphs.

1.
Introduction

1.1.
Word representation of graphs

Word representations of graphs enable one to encode a graph as a string and were introduced by Pyatkin and the first author
[
14
]
. A
word-representation
of a graph
G=(V,E)
is a word over the alphabet
V
such that vertices
v_{1}
and
v_{2}
are adjacent in
G
if and only if they alternate in the word. A graph
G
is said to be
k
-word-representable
if there exists a word representing
G
that contains exactly
k
copies of each letter, that is, a
k
-uniform word. It was shown in
[
14
]
that a graph is word-representable if and only if it is
k
-word-representable for some positive integer
k
. This allows one to define the
representation number
of a word-representable graph
G
, denoted by
{\mathcal{R}}(G)
, as the smallest positive integer
k
such that
G
is
k
-word-representable. By definition,
{\mathcal{R}}(G)=\infty
for any non-word-representable graph.

1.2.
Comparability graphs

The
comparability digraph
of a finite poset
P
, denoted
D_{P}
is a directed graph where the vertex set is the elements of
P
and
x\rightarrow_{D_{P}}y
is a directed edge if and only if
x<_{P}y
.
The
comparability graph
of a finite poset
P
is simply the underlying graph of its comparability digraph.

There is a strong connection between comparability graphs and word-representable graphs, as indicated in
[
14
]
. Namely, if a graph is word-representable, then the neighbourhood of any vertex in it must be a comparability graph. The converse of this statement is not true
[
16
]
. It is known
[
15
]
that every comparability graph can be represented by a concatenation of permutations.

1.3.
Hereditary properties of graphs

A property of graphs is
hereditary
if it is closed under isomorphism and deleting vertices.
That is, if
H
is in a hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
, then every induced subgraph of
H
is also in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

For a graph
F
, let
{\rm Forb}(F)
denote the hereditary property of graphs with no induced copy of
F
.
For a set of graphs
{\mathcal{F}}
, let
{\rm Forb}({\mathcal{F}})
denote the hereditary property of graphs with no induced copy of
F
for any
F\in{\mathcal{F}}
.
That is,
{\rm Forb}({\mathcal{F}})=\bigcap_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}}{\rm Forb}(F)
.
In fact, for every hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
, there exists a (possibly infinite) set
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that
{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcap_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})}{\rm Forb}(F)
.

A hereditary property is
trivial
if it is finite, otherwise it is
nontrivial
.
By Ramsey theory, a hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
is trivial if and only if there are positive integers
a
and
b
such that
{\mathcal{H}}\subseteq{\rm Forb}\bigl(K_{a}\bigr)\cap{\rm Forb}\bigl(\overline{K_{b}}\bigr)
.

1.4.
The edit distance in graphs

The
edit distance between graphs
G
and
H
on the same labeled vertex set of size
n
is

{\rm dist}(G,H)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\bigl|E(G)\triangle E(H)\bigr|/{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}},

where, in this case the
\triangle
notation denotes the symmetric difference.
The
edit distance between a graph
G
and hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
is

{\rm dist}(G,{\mathcal{H}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\min\bigl\{{\rm dist}(G,H):H\in{\mathcal{H}},V(H)=V(G)\bigr\}.

The
edit distance of a hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
is

{\rm dist}(n,{\mathcal{H}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm def}}{{=}}\max\bigl\{{\rm dist}(G,{\mathcal{H}}):|V(G)|=n\bigr\}.

The study of the edit distance in graphs was developed simultaneously by Axenovich, Kézdy and the second author
[
3
]
, by Alon and Stav
[
2
]
, and (in a different context) by Marchant and Thomason
[
17
]
.

The edit distance function was introduced by Balogh and the second author
[
5
]
.

Definition 1

(Balogh-M.
[
5
]
)
.

The
edit distance function
of a hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}
is a function of
p\in[0,1]
, is denoted
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(p)
, and is defined to be

{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(p)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\max\bigl\{{\rm dist}\bigl(G,{\mathcal{H}}\bigr):\bigl|V(G)]\bigr|=n,\bigl|E(G)\bigr|=\bigl\lfloor p{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}\bigr\rfloor\bigr\}.

The limit was proven to exist in
[
5
]
.

Theorem 2

(Balogh–M.
[
5
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a nontrivial hereditary property.
If
G(n,p)
denotes the binomial Erdős–Rényi random graph
[
7
]
1
1

1

In fact, this binomial model of the random graph is due to Gilbert
[
9
]
.
, then

{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(p)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\rm dist}\bigl(G(n,p),{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)\bigr].

Furthermore, for every
p\in[0,1]
, the quantity
{\rm dist}\bigl(G(n,p),{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)
is concentrated around its mean.
That is, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\mathbb{P}\Bigl(\bigl|{\rm dist}\bigl(G(n,p),{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)-\mathbb{E}\bigl[{\rm dist}\bigl(G(n,p),{\mathcal{H}}\bigr)\bigr]\bigr|>\epsilon\Bigr)\rightarrow 0

as

.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}
denote the hereditary property of word-representable graphs.
For a positive integer
k
, let
{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}}
denote the hereditary property of
k
-word-representable graphs.
Note that, according to the way the term is defined, an induced subgraph of a
k
-word-representable graph also has a

-representation, thus
{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}}
is, indeed, a hereditary property.
Let
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}
denote the hereditary property of comparability graphs.

Hefty, Horn, Muir, and Owens
[
12
]
established that
{\rm dist}\bigl(n,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}\bigr)=\Omega\bigl(n^{2}\bigr)
.
In this paper, we asymptotically resolve the value of
{\rm dist}\bigl(n,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}\bigr)
by proving the following:

Theorem 3
.

For all
p\in[0,1]
,
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}}(p)=\min\bigl\{p/3,1-p\bigr\}
.
Consequently,
{\rm dist}\bigl(n,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{4}{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}-o\bigl(n^{2}\bigr)
.
Moreover, if
{\rm dist}\bigl(G,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word})\geq\bigl(\tfrac{1}{4}-\epsilon\bigr){\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}
, then
\bigl|E(G)\bigr|/{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}\in\bigl[\tfrac{3}{4}-3\epsilon,\tfrac{3}{4}+\epsilon\bigr]
.

We also know the edit distance function for
{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}}
, the property of being
k
-word-representable.

Theorem 4
.

Let
k\geq 2
.
For all
p\in[0,1]
,
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}}}(p)=\min\bigl\{p,1-p\bigr\}
.
Consequently,
\displaystyle{\rm dist}\bigl(n,{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}}\bigr)=\tfrac{1}{2}{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}-o\bigl(n^{2}\bigr)
.
Moreover, if
{\rm dist}\bigl(G,{\mathcal{H}}_{k{\rm-word}})\geq\bigl(\tfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon\bigr){\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}
, then
\bigl|E(G)\bigr|/{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}\in\bigl[\tfrac{1}{2}-\epsilon,\tfrac{1}{2}+\epsilon\bigr]
.

We note that the result is the same for any
k\geq 2
.
As far as
{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm-word}}
, the only 1-word-representable graphs are complete graphs and so
{\rm dist}\bigl(G,{\mathcal{H}}_{1{\rm-word}}\bigr)=\binom{n}{2}-|E(G)|
for any graph
G
on
n
vertices.

Finally, we address the hereditary property of comparability graphs, denoted
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}
.

Definition 5
.

For every integer
k\geq 2
, let
p_{k}
satisfy
\frac{2p_{k}-1}{1-p_{k}}=2\cos\bigl(\frac{2\pi}{k+1}\bigr)
.

Equivalently,
p_{k}=1-1/\bigl(2\cos\bigl(\frac{2\pi}{k+1}\bigr)+2\bigr)
.
Observe that
p_{2}=0
,
p_{3}=1/2=0.5
,
p_{4}=\bigl(\sqrt{5}-1\bigr)/2\approx 0.618
,
p_{5}=2/3\approx 0.667
,
p_{6}\approx 0.692
,
p_{7}=\sqrt{2}/2\approx 0.707
and so on.
Furthermore the sequence
\{p_{k}\}_{k\geq 2}
increases to a limit of
3/4
.

Theorem 6
.

There is a sequence of functions
g_{2}(p)=p/2,g_{3}(p)=\frac{p}{1+2p},g_{4}(p)=\frac{-1+3p-p^{2}}{6p-2},g_{5}(p)=\frac{-2+4p-p^{3}}{-4+6p+3p^{2}},g_{6}(p),g_{7}(p),\ldots
such that for all

,

\textstyle{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)=\min\bigl\{\frac{p}{2},\frac{p}{1+2p},\frac{-1+3p-p^{2}}{6p-2},\frac{-2+4p-p^{3}}{-4+6p+3p^{2}},g_{6}(p),g_{7}(p),\ldots,1-p\bigr\}.

Furthermore, for each
k\geq 2
,
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)=g_{k}(p)
for
p\in\bigl(p_{k},p_{k+1}\bigr]
and
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)=1-p

Consequently,
{\rm dist}\bigl(n,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}\bigr)=\tfrac{5}{18}{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}-o\bigl(n^{2}\bigr)
and the maximum of
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)
occurs at

.
Moreover, if
\epsilon\in(0,0.001)
and
{\rm dist}\bigl(G,{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp})\geq\bigl(\tfrac{5}{18}-\epsilon\bigr){\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}
, then
\bigl|E(G)\bigr|/{\textstyle\binom{n}{2}}\in\bigl(\tfrac{2}{3}-\sqrt{2\epsilon},\tfrac{2}{3}+12\epsilon\bigr)
.

2.
Techniques for computing the edit distance function

The key objects in computing the edit distance function were called colored regularity graphs (CRGs) by Alon and Stav
[
2
]
.

Definition 7
.

A
colored regularity graph (CRG)

K
is a simple complete graph together with a partition of its vertices into white and black vertices and a partition of its edges into white, black, and gray edges.
We will use
K=\bigl({\rm VW},{\rm VB};{\rm EW},{\rm EB},{\rm EG}\bigr)
to denote that
K
is a CRG with white vertex set
{\rm VW}
, black vertex set
{\rm VB}
, white edge set
{\rm EW}
, black edge set
{\rm EB}
and gray edge set
{\rm EG}
.

As far as notation, the dependence on
K
is usually suppressed if the CRG is understood.

Colored regularity graphs come from the analysis of graphs using Szemerédi’s regularity lemma.
In particular, one can apply the regularity lemma a second time, inside each of the clusters given by the first iteration and then Ramsey’s theorem.
A convenient form of the regularity lemma for these purposes is given by Alon, Fischer, Krivelevich, and Szegedy
[
1
]
.

CRGs are used to represent a graph after the editing is finished.
As such, we need to demonstrate how forbidden graphs are not in the edited graph.
This yields the idea of a colored homomorphism.
If
G
is a graph, then
\overline{G}
denotes the graph complement.
The notation
\sqcup
denotes the disjoint union of sets.

Definition 8
.

A
colored homomorphism
from a graph
G
to a CRG
K=({\rm VW},{\rm VB};

{\rm EW},{\rm EB},{\rm EG})
is a map
\varphi:V(G)\rightarrow V(K)
such that

•

If
uv\in E(\overline{G})
, then either
\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)\in{\rm VW}
or
\varphi(u)\neq\varphi(v)
and
\varphi(u)\varphi(v)\in{\rm EW}\sqcup{\rm EG}
.

•

If
uv\in E(G)
, then either
\varphi(u)=\varphi(v)\in{\rm VB}
or
\varphi(u)\neq\varphi(v)
and
\varphi(u)\varphi(v)\in{\rm EB}\sqcup{\rm EG}
.

If there is a colored homomorphism from
G
to
K
, then we write
G\mapsto K
.
If there is no colored homomorphism from
G
to
K
, then we write
G\not\mapsto K
.

Finally, we need to classify the colored regularity graphs that can represent graphs in
{\mathcal{H}}
.

Definition 9
.

Given a hereditary property
{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcap_{F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})}{\rm Forb}(F)
, where
{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})
denotes the set of graphs that do not appear as induced subgraphs of members of
{\mathcal{H}}
, the set of
CRGs associated with
{\mathcal{H}}
, denoted
{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
, are the CRGs
K
, for which
F\not\mapsto K
for all
F\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})
.

For each CRG
K
, there are two functions,
f_{K}(p)
and
g_{K}(p)
that asymptotically measure the amount of editing that is done to
G(n,p)
according to
K
.
In the case of
f_{K}(p)
, it is where the graph is partitioned into
|V(K)|
equally-sized parts.
In the case of
g_{K}(p)
, it is where the graph is partitioned optimally.

Definition 10
.

Let
K=\bigl({\rm VW},{\rm VB};{\rm EW},{\rm EB},{\rm EG}\bigr)
be a CRG on vertex set
{\rm VW}\cup{\rm VB}=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
.
Let
{\bf M}_{K}(p)
denote the matrix with entries defined as follows:

m_{K}(p)_{ij}=\begin{cases}p,&\text{if }i\neq j\text{ and }v_{i}v_{j}\in{\rm EW}\text{ or if }i=j\text{ and }v_{i}\in{\rm VW};\\
0,&\text{if }i\neq j\text{ and }v_{i}v_{j}\in{\rm EG};\\
1-p,&\text{if }i\neq j\text{ and }v_{i}v_{j}\in{\rm EB}\text{ or if }i=j\text{ and }v_{i}\in{\rm VB}.\end{cases}

The functions
f_{K}
and
g_{K}
are defined as follows:

\displaystyle f_{K}(p)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{k^{2}}\bigl[p\bigl(|{\rm VW}|+2|{\rm EW}|\bigr)+(1-p)\bigl(|{\rm VB}|+2|{\rm EB}|\bigr)\bigr]=\tfrac{1}{k^{2}}{\bf 1}^{T}{\bf M}_{K}(p){\bf 1}

\displaystyle g_{K}(p)

\displaystyle=\min\bigl\{{\bf x}^{T}\cdot{\bf M}_{K}(p)\cdot{\bf x}:{\bf x}^{T}{\bf 1}=1,{\bf x}\geq{\bf 0}\bigr\}.

The vector
{\bf 0}
is the all-zeroes vector,
{\bf 1}
is the all-ones vector and vector inequalities are coordinatewise.

In the theorem below, which shows that we only need to consider CRGs to compute the edit distance function, the “
\inf
” expressions are due to Balogh and the second author whereas the “
\min
” equality was established by Marchant and Thomason.

Theorem 11

(Balogh-M.
[
5
]
; Marchant-Thomason
[
17
]
)
.

Let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a nontrivial hereditary property. For any
p\in[0,1]
,

\displaystyle{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(p)

\displaystyle=\inf\bigl\{f_{K}(p):K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})\bigr\}=\inf\bigl\{g_{K}(p):K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})\bigr\}

\displaystyle=\min\bigl\{g_{K}(p):K\in{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})\bigr\}.

If we have a CRG
K
and can delete a vertex without raising the value of
g_{K}(p)
, we would prefer to work with the smaller CRG.
Thus, for any value of
p
, we show that we can restrict to a certain type of CRG which we call

-core.

Definition 12
.

If
K

For any

For any

to be the set of all CRGs in

, which is equivalent to
P\in\Bigl(2\cos\bigl(\frac{2\pi}{k+1}\bigr),2\cos\bigl(\frac{\pi}{k}\bigr)\Bigr)
, which is equivalent to
p\in\bigl(p_{k},p_{2k-1}\bigr)
.

Third, let
p\in[3/4,1)
and consider
K=\widetilde{P}_{k}
.
As we discussed before, if
K
is not
p
-core, then there is an
\ell<k
such that
g_{\widetilde{P}_{\ell}}(p)=g_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(p)
.
But by changing one edge from black to gray, we get a CRG that consists of white vertices, and all black edges except for a spanning cycle induced by gray edges, denote it
\widetilde{C}_{\ell}
.
Thus
g_{\widetilde{C}_{\ell}}(p)<g_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(p)
.

Since
{\bf x}=\frac{1}{\ell}{\bf 1}
gives that
M_{\widetilde{C}_{\ell}}(p)\cdot{\bf x}
is a constant vector and
\widetilde{C}_{\ell}
is
p
-core, then by Theorem
17
, it is the unique vector satisfying

g_{\widetilde{C}_{\ell}}(p)=\frac{p}{\ell}+(\ell-3)\frac{1-p}{\ell}=(1-p)+\frac{4p-3}{\ell}\geq 1-p,

which establishes that
g_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(p)>1-p
for
p\in[3/4,1)
.

Fourth, we observe that since
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)
is continuous and concave down, and that
g_{\widetilde{P}_{4}}(p)=\frac{-1+3p-p^{2}}{2(-1+3p)}
is increasing for
p\in\bigl(p_{4},p_{5}\bigr]=\bigl(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},\frac{2}{3}\bigr]
and
g_{\widetilde{P}_{5}}(p)=\frac{-2+4p-p^{3}}{-4+6p+3p^{2}}
is decreasing for
p\in\bigl(p_{5},p_{6}\bigr]\supset\bigl(\frac{2}{3},0.692\bigr]
.
Hence,
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)
achieves its maximum at
p=2/3
and that maximum is
\frac{5}{18}
.

Fifth, note that
g_{\widetilde{P}_{4}}(p)\leq\frac{5}{18}-\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{2}{3}-p\bigr)^{2}
for all
p\in\bigl(p_{4},2/3\bigr]
and
g_{\widetilde{P}_{5}}(p)\leq\frac{5}{18}-\frac{1}{12}\bigl(p-\frac{2}{3}\bigr)
for all
p\in\bigl(2/3,p_{6}\bigr]
.
Thus, if
g>5/18-\epsilon
and
\epsilon<0.001<\min\Bigl\{\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\frac{2}{3}-p_{4}\bigr)^{2},\frac{1}{12}\bigl(p_{6}-\frac{2}{3}\bigr)\Bigr\}
, then

\frac{2}{3}-\sqrt{2\epsilon}<p<\frac{2}{3}+12\epsilon.

This completes the proof of Lemma
31
.
∎

Furthermore, this also completes the proof of Theorem
6
, which establishes that
{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}}(p)
is equal to
p/2
for
p\in[0,1/2]
, is equal to
g_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(p)
for
p\in[p_{k},p_{k+1}]

for

6.2.
Transitions between CRGs in
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}

In the case of

, we need some definitions from Cox, McGinnis and the second author
[
6
]
.
Given a nontrivial herediary property
{\mathcal{H}}
, we say that
p\in[0,1]
is a
regular point
if there is an
\epsilon>0
and a finite set of CRGs
{\mathcal{K}}^{\prime}\subseteq{\mathcal{K}}({\mathcal{H}})
such that

{\rm ed}_{{\mathcal{H}}}(p)=\min_{K\in{\mathcal{K}}}g_{K}(p),\qquad\mbox{for every $p\in\bigl(p_{0}-\epsilon,p_{0}+\epsilon\bigr)\cap\bigl[0,1\bigr]$.}

Otherwise,
p
is called an
accumulation point
.

There are many examples of hereditary properties for which
0
is an accumulation point such as
{\rm Forb}\bigl(K_{3,3}\bigr)
found by Marchant and Thomason
[
17
]
.
Observe that if
p
is an accumulation point for
{\mathcal{H}}=\bigcap_{H\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})}{\rm Forb}\bigl(H\bigr)
, then
1-p
is an accumulation point for
\bigcap_{H\in{\mathcal{F}}({\mathcal{H}})}{\rm Forb}\bigl(\overline{H}\bigr)
.

The question of whether a number other than
0
or
1
could be an accumulation point was established in the affirmative in
[
6
, Theorem 1.5]
where it was shown that
1/4
is an accumulation point of the hereditary property
{\rm Forb}\bigl(\bigl\{K_{1,4},C_{5},C_{6},C_{7},\ldots\bigr\}\bigr)
.
This is very similar to the the complement of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}
, that is, it is similar to
{\rm Forb}\bigl(\bigl\{\overline{F_{1}^{\prime}},\overline{F_{2}^{\prime}},C_{5},C_{6},C_{7},\ldots\bigr\}\bigr)
which has an accumulation point of
1/4
also.

The second author and Riasanovsky
[
19
, Theorem 39]
established that there are no accumulation points in
\bigl(2-\varphi,\varphi-1\bigr)=\bigl(0.382\ldots,0.618\ldots\bigr)
, where
\varphi
is the golden ratio.
This leads to several questions and conjectures.

6.6.
Edit distance for oriented graphs

An orientation of a graph is
semi-transitive
if it is acyclic (i.e., contains no directed cycles), and for any directed path
v_{0}\rightarrow v_{1}\rightarrow\cdots\rightarrow v_{k}
, either there is no edge between
v_{0}
and
v_{k}
, or
v_{i}\rightarrow v_{j}
is an edge for all
0\leq i<j\leq k
. A fundamental result in the theory of word-representable graphs by Halldórsson, Pyatkin, and the first author
[
11
]
states that a graph is word-representable if and only if it admits a semi-transitive orientation.

In the case of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm word}
, one may ask about the maximum edit distance of an oriented graph from one admitting a semi-transitive orientation or, in the case of
{\mathcal{H}}_{\rm comp}
, the maximum edit distance of an oriented graph from one admitting a transitive orientation. This was addressed to some extent by Axenovich and the second author
[
4
]
, although there is little other literature on the edit distance of oriented graphs, and the interested reader may wish to consider alternative measures of edit distance.

Appendix D
Eigenvalues and eigenvectors of a symmetric tridiagonal Toeplitz matrix

We want to compute the eigenvalues and eigenvectors of the matrix
\widehat{\bf M}_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(P)
which is defined to be

\bigl(\widetilde{m}_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(P)\bigr)_{ab}=\begin{cases}-P,&\mbox{if $a=b$;}\\
1,&\mbox{if $|a-b|=1$; and}\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The eigenvalues are
\lambda_{a}=2\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)-P
and the eigenvector corresponding to
\lambda_{a}
is
{\bf w}_{a}
where

\bigl(w_{a}\bigr)_{b}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\sin\biggl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\biggr).

Proposition
39
establishes that each
{\bf w}_{a}
is a unit vector and establishes that
{\bf w}_{a}^{T}{\bf 1}=0
if
a
is even and that
{\bf w}_{a}^{T}{\bf 1}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\,\frac{1+\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}
if
a
is odd.

Proposition 39
.

For any integers
k
and
a
,

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{k}\sin^{2}\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=(k+1)/2\qquad\mbox{and}

\displaystyle\sum_{b=1}^{k}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{ if $a$ is even;}\\
\frac{1+\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)},&\mbox{ if $a$ is odd.}\end{cases}

Proof.

Observe that
\sin 0=0
and so we can start the sums at
b=0
.

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{k}\sin^{2}\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{2}-\sum_{b=0}^{k}\left[\exp\Bigl\{\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr\}+\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr\}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{2}-\sum_{b=0}^{k}\left[\frac{1-\exp\bigl\{\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot(k+1)\bigr\}}{1-\exp\bigl\{\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}+\frac{1-\exp\bigl\{-\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot(k+1)\bigr\}}{1-\exp\bigl\{-\frac{2a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{k+1}{2}.

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{k}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\sum_{b=0}^{k}\left[\exp\Bigl\{\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr\}-\exp\Bigl\{-\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr\}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\left[\frac{1-\exp\{a\pi i\}}{1-\exp\bigl\{\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}-\frac{1-\exp\{-a\pi i\}}{1-\exp\bigl\{-\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}\right].

If
a
is even, then
\exp\{a\pi i\}=\exp\{-a\pi i\}=1
and
\sum_{b=0}^{k}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)=0
.
If
a
is even, then
\exp\{a\pi i\}=\exp\{-a\pi i\}=-1
and

\displaystyle\sum_{b=0}^{k}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\cdot b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2i}\left[\frac{2}{1-\exp\bigl\{\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}-\frac{2}{1-\exp\bigl\{-\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i}\left[\frac{\exp\bigl\{\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}-\exp\bigl\{\frac{-a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}}{\bigl(1-\exp\bigl\{\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}\bigr)\bigl(1-\exp\bigl\{-\frac{a\pi i}{k+1}\bigr\}\bigr)}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{1-\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}=\frac{1+\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}.

∎

To verify the eigenvectors, let us compute
\widehat{\bf M}_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(P)\cdot{\bf w}_{a}
.
For any
b\in\{1,\ldots,k\}

\displaystyle\Bigl(\widehat{\bf M}_{\widetilde{P}_{k}}(P)\cdot{\bf w}_{a}\Bigr)_{b}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\left[\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,(b-1)\Bigr)-P\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\Bigr)+\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,(b+1)\Bigr)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b-\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)+\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b+\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\bigr)}-P\right]\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{2\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\bigr)\,\cos\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\bigr)}{\sin\bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\bigr)}-P\right]\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\Bigr)

\displaystyle=\biggl[2\cos\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\Bigr)-P\biggr]\sqrt{\frac{2}{k+1}}\sin\Bigl(\frac{a\pi}{k+1}\,b\Bigr),

as required.
