Abstract

A conjecture of Simon Donaldson is that on a compact
4
-manifold
X^{4}
one can flow from a hypersymplectic structure to a hyperkähler structure while remaining in the same cohomology class. To this end the hypersymplectic flow was introduced by Fine–Yao. In this paper the notion of a positive triple on
X^{4}
is used to describe a hypersymplectic and hyperkähler structure. Given a closed positive triple one can define either a closed
G_{2}
structure or a coclosed
G_{2}
structure on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
. The coclosed
G_{2}
structure is evolved under the modified
G_{2}
-Laplacian coflow. The coflow descends to a flow of the positive triple on
X^{4}
, which is again the Fine–Yao hypersymplectic flow.

1
Introduction

The notion of a hyperkähler manifold first arose in Berger’s classification of possible holonomy groups in his paper
[
1
]
, they are known in the literature to be a class of manifolds with special holonomy. Restricting to dimension
4
, it was Donaldson who posed the idea of loosening the conditions of a hyperkähler structure to one where the triple of Kähler forms was only symplectic. This is what we call in this paper a hypersymplectic structure. In
[
4
]
, Donaldson conjectured that up to isotopy the only example of a hypersympletic manifold is a hyperkähler manifold. We state the conjecture below and refer the reader to
[
8
]
for more details on the conjecture and how it arose in the literature.

Conjecture 1.1
.

(Donaldson’s Conjecture
[
4
]
)
Let
M
be a compact oriented
4
-manifold. Let
\underline{\omega}
be a hypersymplectic structure on
M
. There exists a one-parameter family of diffeomorphisms
F_{t}:M\to M
connected to the identity, that take a hypersymplectic structure
\underline{\omega}(0)=\underline{\omega}
to a hyperkähler structure
\underline{\omega}(1)=F_{1}^{*}\underline{\omega}
, so that for
0\leq t\leq 1
,
\underline{\omega}(t)=F_{t}^{*}\underline{\omega}
remains in the same cohomology class.

In a particular setting, Fine and Yao have done work to prove this conjecture. In
[
7
]
the authors define a closed
G_{2}
structure on
M^{7}=\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
where
X^{4}
is compact and hypersymplectic.
Let
t^{i}
be the flat coordinates on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
and let
\underline{\omega}
be a hypersymplectic structure on
X^{4}
. The
3
-form
\varphi
on
M^{7}
is given by

\varphi=dt^{123}-dt^{1}\wedge\omega_{1}-dt^{2}\wedge\omega_{2}-dt^{3}\wedge\omega_{3}.

(1)

This form gives a closed
G_{2}
structure on
M^{7}
that is invariant under
\mathbb{T}^{3}
, see
[
7
]
.
Using the particular
\varphi
from (
1
), in
[
7
]
the authors computed that the metric
g_{\varphi}
, given by

g_{\varphi}(a,b)\mu_{\varphi}=\frac{1}{6}\left(a\mathbin{\lrcorner}\varphi\right)\wedge\left(b\mathbin{\lrcorner}\varphi\right)\wedge\varphi

(2)

is a warped product of the metric on
X^{4}
given by the hypersymplectic structure and the Euclidean metric on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. It is given as

g_{\varphi}=Q_{ij}dt^{i}\otimes dt^{j}+g_{\underline{\omega}},

(3)

where
g_{\underline{\omega}}
is a metric on
X^{4}
coming from the hypersymplectic triple
\underline{\omega}
. It is defined in (
10
). They evolve the
G_{2}
structure under the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow to create a new geometric flow on
X^{4}
that could deform the hypersymplectic structure to a hyperkähler structure. This flow is known as the hypersymplectic flow and is given by

\partial_{t}\underline{\omega}=d\left(Qd^{*}\left(Q^{-1}\underline{\omega}\right)\right).

(4)

Letting
Q^{ij}
be the entries of
Q^{-1}
, we can write the hypersymplectic flow more explicitly on each form as

\frac{\partial\omega_{i}}{\partial t}=d\left(Q_{ik}d^{*}\left(Q^{kl}\omega_{l}\right)\right).

(5)

Furthermore, in
[
7
]
the authors show that any solution of the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow that begins with initial condition (
1
) remains
\mathbb{T}^{3}
invariant for as long as it exists. Thus any solution of the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow of the form (
1
) remains of this form along the flow. In
[
8
]
the authors show that when
Q
is constant,
\underline{\omega}
is a fixed point of the flow. In such cases there exists a hyperkähler structure whose metric is equal (up to a positive constant) to the hypersymplectic metric. The hypersymplectic flow
(
4
)
descended from the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow therefore one can say that the flows are equivalent in this particular set up. Thus the hypersymplectic flow inherits the properties of short-time existence and uniqueness from the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow. Furthermore, if the initial
G_{2}
structure
\varphi_{0}
is closed then under the
G_{2}
-Laplacian flow, the structure
\varphi(t)
remains closed and belongs to the same cohomology class as
\varphi_{0}
for all time
t
, see
[
2
]
,
[
15
]
. Thus the same can be said about the hypersymplectic flow. If
\underline{\omega}_{0}
is the initial hypersymplectic structure then as we flow using
(
4
)

\underline{\omega}(t)
remains closed and cohomologous to
\underline{\omega}_{0}
for as long as it exists. In
[
7
]
the authors are able to show long time existence of the flow provided the torsion tensor of the
G_{2}
structure stays uniformly bounded as we deform the hypersymplectic structures. In
[
6
]
the authors prove Donaldson’s conjecture explicitly on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times\mathbb{T}^{4}
, under the assumption that the hypersymplectic structure on
\mathbb{T}^{4}
is
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-invariant for some
\mathbb{T}^{3}\subset\mathbb{T}^{4}
. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
and
[
6
]
for more details. Presently
[
7
]
and
[
6
]
are the only known convergence results for the hypersymplectic flow.

In this paper we construct a coclosed
G_{2}
structure on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
where
X^{4}
is compact and hypersymplectic. We flow this structure under the modified
G_{2}
-Laplacian coflow. To this end we introduce some background for the modified Laplacian coflow and refer the reader to
[
11
]
and
[
10
]
for additional details. One can define a
G_{2}
structure and its corresponding Riemannian metric using a positive
4
-form
\psi
(up to a choice of orientation). So instead of flowing the
3
-form
\varphi
in time, one might choose to flow the
4
-form in time, thus creating a different type of
G_{2}
geometric flow. Using this idea, the Laplacian coflow was first introduced in
[
14
]
. This flow is given by

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=dd^{*}\psi

(6)

on the cohomology class
[\psi]=\{\psi_{0}+d\eta\}
with initial conditions
\psi(0)=\psi_{0}
and
d\psi_{0}=0
. This flow shares some properties with the Laplacian flow. In particular the solutions stay coclosed and remain in the same cohomology class for their entire existence, see
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
. However the main difference between the flows is that it is still not known if the Laplacian coflow has short time existence. In
[
2
]
, the Laplacian flow starting from closed
G_{2}
structures is weakly parabolic in the direction of closed
3
-forms and thus an application of DeTurk’s trick transforms this flow to a strictly parabolic flow which then has short-time existence. However the same cannot be done for the Laplacian coflow. This flow is not weakly parabolic in the direction of closed
4
-forms and thus cannot be shown to be equivalent to some parabolic flow, see
[
11
]
. In the hopes of finding coclosed
G_{2}
-structures that can be deformed to torsion-free
G_{2}
structures, Grigorian introduced the modified Laplacian coflow in
[
10
]
. This flow is given by

\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}=dd^{*}\psi+2d\left(\left(A-\Tr\mathrm{T}_{\psi}\right)*_{\psi}\psi\right)

(7)

where
\Tr\mathrm{T}_{\psi}
is the trace of the full torsion tensor
\mathrm{T}_{\psi}
of the
G_{2}
-structure defined by
\psi
and
A
is a fixed constant. We note that we can pick
A=0
and this is exactly what we do in Section
3
. It can be seen that when
A=0
and
\Tr\mathrm{T}_{\psi}=0
the coflow and modified coflow agree. In
[
10
]
, when starting from a coclosed
G_{2}
-structure the author shows that the modified coflow (
23
) is weakly parabolic in the direction of closed forms and hence an application of DeTurk’s trick can be used to show that the flow has short time existence.
The author also uses the Nash-Moser inverse function theorem to show that the modified coflow also has uniqueness. These are the same techniques used by Bryant-Xu for the Laplacian flow in
[
2
]
. We summarize this to say that the following initial value problem

\begin{split}\frac{\partial\psi}{\partial t}&=dd^{*}\psi+2d\left(\left(A-\Tr\mathrm{T}_{\psi}\right)*_{\psi}\psi\right),\\
\psi(0)&=\psi_{0},\\
d\psi_{0}&=0,\end{split}

(8)

on the space
\{\psi_{0}+d\eta\}
has short-time existence and uniqueness. By construction any solution
\psi(t)
remains in the same cohomology class
[\psi_{0}]
for as long as it exists, and thus any solution stays coclosed for all time, see
[
10
]
.

Following analogously the ideas of Fine and Yao, we construct a coclosed
G_{2}
structure on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
and show that this
G_{2}
structure is invariant under
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. We evolve this
G_{2}
structure under the modified
G_{2}
-Laplacian coflow and find that its solutions remain
\mathbb{T}^{3}
invariant for as long as the flow exists. Furthermore, we find that for this particular ansatz the modified Laplacian coflow descends to a flow of the hypersymplectic structure on
{X}^{4}
, which is the same flow that Fine-Yao discovered known as the hypersymplectic flow.

Notations and conventions:

We give a brief explanations of the notation and conventions that are used regularly throughout this paper. The Einstein convention is used. A multi-index in an exponent is used to mean the wedge product. For example, given three
1
-form
e^{i},e^{j},e^{k}
,

e^{ijk}=e^{i}\wedge e^{j}\wedge e^{k}.

Let
dt^{j}
be
1
-forms on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. The notation
\hat{dt^{j}}
is used to express the
2
-form on
\mathbb{T}^{3}
that omits
dt^{j}
. That is

\hat{dt^{j}}=dt^{i}\wedge dt^{k}

where
i,j,k
are cyclically permuted
i\to j\to k
. The notation
\varepsilon_{ijk}
is the permutation symbol on
\{1,2,3\}
.

Geometric structures

In this section we explain the different structures needed to understand the results of this paper. For this section we let
X^{4}
be any oriented manifold of dimension four. We fix a volume form
\mu
on
X^{4}
.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
X^{4}
be an oriented manifold of dimension four, let
\mu
be a non-vanishing top form on
X^{4}
. Let
\underline{\omega}=\left(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3}\right)
be a triple of
2
-forms on
X^{4}
. Let
Q
be the symmetric
3\times 3
matrix valued function such that

\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}=2Q_{ij}\mu.

(9)

We say the triple
\underline{\omega}
is a positive triple if
Q
is positive definite at all points.

Remark 2.2
.

When
Q
is positive definite, the
2
-forms
\omega_{i}
are non-degenerate as

\omega_{i}^{2}=2Q_{ii}\mu\neq 0

which is non-vanishing since
Q_{ii}>0
by positive definiteness.

We also note that the matrix
Q
depends on
\underline{\omega}
but also on
\mu
. One can see that as
\mu
changes,
Q
also changes. There is a particular choice of volume form
\mu
so that
\det(Q)=1
, this detail is explained in lemma
2.5
. This is the volume form that is chosen in
[
7
]
. Furthermore, the positive triple
\underline{\omega}
gives rise to a Riemannian metric
g_{\underline{\omega}}
defined by

g_{\underline{\omega}}(u,v)\mu_{\underline{\omega}}=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(u\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega_{i}\right)\wedge\left(v\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega_{j}\right)\wedge\omega_{k}.

(10)

It is important to note that
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
is the Riemannian volume form of
g_{\underline{\omega}}
and is different from our reference form
\mu
on
X^{4}
. However
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
can be expressed in terms of
Q
and
\mu
, as we show in the following lemma. We want to make clear that
Q
scales depending on
\mu
but
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
is always expressed in the same way as in lemma
2.5
. Furthermore, it is not obvious that (
10
) gives a Riemannian metric, this is due to the positive definiteness of
Q
. We refer the reader to
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
for more details regarding this metric construction.

Remark 2.3
.

Each
\omega_{i}
in the positive triple is self-dual with respect to the metric and orientation given in (
10
)
[
7
]
.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S
be a
3\times 3
matrices. Let
\det(S)
be the determinant of
S
. Then

\begin{split}\sum_{i,j,k}\varepsilon_{ijk}S_{ip}S_{jq}S_{kl}&=\left(\det(S)\right)\varepsilon_{pql}.\end{split}

(11)

Proof.

The identity
\varepsilon_{ijk}S_{ip}S_{jq}S_{kl}
is skew in
p,q,
and
l
thus we can write the expression as being equal to
\lambda\varepsilon_{pql}
for some scalar
\lambda
. That is,

\sum_{i,j,k}\varepsilon_{ijk}S_{ip}S_{jq}S_{kl}=\lambda\varepsilon_{pql}.

If we take the above and contract it with
\varepsilon_{pql}
we obtain

\displaystyle 6\det(S)=\sum_{p,q,l}\sum_{i,j,k}\varepsilon_{pql}\varepsilon_{ijk}S_{ip}S_{jq}S_{kl}

\displaystyle=\sum_{p,q,l}\lambda\varepsilon_{pql}\varepsilon_{pql}=6\lambda.

We substitute
\lambda=\det(S)
and we obtain the desired result.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\underline{\omega}
be a positive triple on
X^{4}
. Let
\mu
be the reference volume form for
\underline{\omega}
as in definition
2.1
. Let
g_{\underline{\omega}}\otimes\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
be defined as in (
10
). Then
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}=\left(\det(Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mu
.

Proof.

To begin we emphasize that we are doing these computations at some fixed point in
X^{4}
as this result and proof is entirely linear algebra. Let
\underline{\omega}
be a positive triple. Let
\mu
and
Q
be such that
2Q_{ij}\mu=\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}
. Since
Q
is a symmetric matrix, there exists an invertible
3\times 3
matrix
P
such that if
\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}
are defined by

\theta_{i}=P_{il}\omega_{l}\quad\text{then}\quad\theta_{i}\wedge\theta_{j}=2\delta_{ij}\mu.

We note that
\underline{\theta}=\left(\theta_{1},\theta_{2},\theta_{3}\right)
is still a positive triple whose matrix
Q_{\underline{\theta}}=\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3}
. When we expand the expression for the wedge product above, we obtain

P_{ik}\omega_{k}\wedge P_{jl}\omega_{l}=P_{ik}P_{jl}\omega_{k}\wedge\omega_{l}=P_{ik}P_{jl}2Q_{kl}\mu,

\implies P_{ik}Q_{kl}P_{jl}=\delta_{ij}.

This tells us that
PQP^{T}=\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3}
. Let
M=P^{-1}
. By taking the determinant of the expression above, we have

\det(P)\det(Q)\det(P)=1\quad\text{and hence}\quad\det(Q)=(\det(M))^{2}.

Since we let
M=P^{-1}
, we can say that
\omega_{i}=M_{il}\theta_{l}
. Now let
g_{\underline{\theta}}
be the metric coming from the triple
\underline{\theta}
defined as in (
10
). One can show that there locally exists a coframe
e^{0},e^{1},e^{2},e^{3}
that is orthonormal with respect to
g_{\underline{\theta}}
so that
\theta_{i}=e^{0}\wedge e^{i}+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}e^{j}\wedge e^{k}
and
\mu=e^{0}\wedge e^{1}\wedge e^{2}\wedge e^{3}
. Let
e_{i}
be the dual frame to
e^{i}
. We use the formula for the metric (
10
) to say

\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(e_{a}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{i}\right)\wedge\left(e_{b}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{j}\right)\wedge\theta_{k}=\delta_{ab}\mu.

(12)

We know that given a basis,
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}=\sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})}\mu
where
g_{\underline{\omega}}
is the metric determined by the triple
\underline{\omega}
. We wish to determine
\sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})}
. To do this we know that
g_{\underline{\omega}}
can be expressed as a
4\times 4
matrix, thus if we let
R
be a
4\times 4
matrix such that
R_{ab}\mu=g_{\underline{\omega}}(e_{a},e_{b})\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
and use lemma
2.4
and (
12
) then

\displaystyle R_{ab}\mu=g_{\underline{\omega}}(e_{a},e_{b})\mu_{\underline{\omega}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{ijk}\left(e_{a}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega_{i}\right)\wedge\left(e_{b}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\omega_{j}\right)\wedge\omega_{k}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\varepsilon_{ijk}M_{ip}M_{jq}M_{kl}\left(e_{a}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{p}\right)\wedge\left(e_{b}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{q}\right)\wedge\theta_{l}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{6}\det(M)\varepsilon_{pql}\left(e_{a}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{p}\right)\wedge\left(e_{b}\mathbin{\lrcorner}\theta_{q}\right)\wedge\theta_{l}

\displaystyle=\det(M)\delta_{ab}\mu.

The above computation determines that
R=\det(M)\mathbb{I}_{4\times 4}
. We use this computation and the fact that
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}=\sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})}\mu
to obtain

\begin{split}R=g_{\underline{\omega}}\sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})}=\det(M)\mathbb{I}_{4\times 4}.\end{split}

(13)

We apply the determinant to (
13
) to obtain

\displaystyle\det(g_{\underline{\omega}} \sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})})

\displaystyle=\det(\det(M) \mathbb{I}_{4 \times 4})

\displaystyle\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})^{3}

\displaystyle=\det(M)^{4}

\displaystyle\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})^{3}

\displaystyle=\det(Q)^{2}\quad\text{since}\quad\det(Q)=\det(M)^{2}

\displaystyle\sqrt{\det(g_{\underline{\omega}})}

\displaystyle=\det(Q)^{\frac{1}{3}}.

We substitute this last equation back into the formula for
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
in terms of
\mu
and obtain our desired result
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}=\left(\det(Q)\right)^{\frac{1}{3}}\mu
.
∎

This lemma tells us that when
\det(Q)=1
the volume form
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
is equal to the reference volume form
\mu
. In particular, it tells us how to choose
\mu
so that the determinant of
Q
is always
1
. If we first construct
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
using the formula for the metric (
10
) and choose
\mu=\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
this ensures that
\det(Q)=1
. This is important in particular because as we flow our geometric structures in time
\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
,
\underline{\omega}
, and
Q
will depend on time but the determinant of
Q
at any fixed point in time will always be
1
.

The relation (
9
) and the Riemannian metric (
10
) give us three potential structures on
X^{4}
. The first happens when
Q
is the identity matrix. In this case the triple
\underline{\omega}
forms an
\text{SU}(2)
structure, see
[
9
]
. The second is a hypersymplectic structure, this is the case where the forms
\omega_{i}
are symplectic, meaning they are closed, that is
d\omega_{i}=0
. In this case
Q
need not necessarily be the identity matrix, see
[
7
]
. The last is a hyperkähler structure. In this case the triple forms an
\text{SU}(2)
structure and a hypersymplectic structure, meaning the forms
\underline{\omega}
are symplectic (that is they are closed) and the matrix
Q
is the identity, see
[
6
]
,
[
5
]
. We give the full definition of hypersymplectic and hyperkähler below.

Definition 2.6
.

Let
(M,g)
be a Riemannian manifold equipped with three complex structures
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}\colon TM\to TM
such that

J_{1}^{2}=J_{2}^{2}=J_{3}^{2}=J_{1}J_{2}J_{3}=-1.

If
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}
are parallel then
(M,g,J_{1},J_{2},J_{3})
is a hyperkähler manifold. Furthermore, we have
\omega_{i}(x,y)=-g(x,J_{i}y)
for the Kähler forms. There is one for each complex structure
J_{1},J_{2},J_{3}
. These
2
-forms are closed and non-degenerate.

Remark 2.7
.

When a positive triple
\underline{\omega}
is hyperkähler, where the forms
\omega_{i}
are given as in the definition above, the matrix
Q
that comes from the positive triple is the identity. We show this below.

To verify the remark made above, let
M
be a
4
-dimensional hyperkähler manifold. Let
e_{0},e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
be orthonormal basis with respect to
g
as in definition
2.6
, let
e^{i}
for
i=0,1,2,3
be its dual, and let
\mu=e^{0123}
. Let
J_{i}
be the three complex parallel structures on
M
so that
\omega_{i}(x,y)=-g(x,J_{i}y)
and
J_{i}(e_{0})=e_{i}
, then we have the following relations:

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(e_{0},e_{i})

\displaystyle=-g(e_{0},J_{i}(e_{i}))=1,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(e_{0},e_{j})

\displaystyle=-g(e_{0},J_{i}(e_{j}))=-g(e_{0},e_{k})=0,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(e_{j},e_{i})

\displaystyle=-g(e_{j},J_{i}(e_{i}))=g(e_{j},e_{0})=0,

\displaystyle\omega_{i}(e_{j},e_{k})

\displaystyle=-g(e_{j},J_{i}(e_{k}))=g(e_{j},e_{j})=1.

This allows us to say that in this basis
\omega_{i}=e^{0}\wedge e^{i}+e^{j}\wedge e^{k}
. It is then easy to see that when the triple is written in this form the relation
\omega_{i}\wedge\omega_{j}=2\delta_{ij}\mu
is satisfied. Since
Q=\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3}
and
\det(Q)=1
we have
\mu=\mu_{\underline{\omega}}
. Furthermore, one can show that
g_{\underline{\omega}}
is indeed the hyperkahler
g
from definition
2.6
.

Remark 2.8
.

When the
2
-forms
\omega_{i}
are all closed, the complex structures they define are integrable, see
[
12
]
,
[
3
]
. Since the intrinsic torsion of an
\text{SU}(2)
structure vanishes if and only if each
2
-form
\omega_{i}
is closed, see
[
9
]
, one can think of a hyperkähler structure in dimension
4
as a torsion free
\text{SU}(2)
structure.

Definition 2.9
.

Let
X^{4}
be a
4
dimensional oriented smooth manifold. A hypersymplectic structure on
X^{4}
is a positive triple of symplectic forms
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
.

We can always take a hypersymplectic triple
\underline{\omega}
and diagonalize so that
Q=\mathbb{I}_{3\times 3}
. This gives us a
\text{SU}(2)
structure at the cost of the closed-ness of the triple. When the matrix
Q
coming from the hypersymplectic triple is constant, we can apply a linear transformation to our triple
\underline{\omega}
so that it is now a triple
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
for which
Q
is now the identity matrix, and
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
is closed thus hyperkähler. Then
\underline{\omega}
induces the same metric as
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
up to a constant factor. We show this explicitly now. Let
\underline{\omega}
be a hypersymplectic triple where
Q
given as in definition
2.9
is constant. Then there exists some constant matrix
A
that diagonalizes
Q
, meaning we can define another triple
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
by
\tilde{\omega}_{i}=A_{ik}\omega_{k}
where
\tilde{\omega_{i}}\wedge\tilde{\omega_{j}}=2\delta_{ij}\mu
. Since
A
is constant the triple
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
is still closed since

d\tilde{\omega}_{i}=dA_{ik}\wedge\omega_{k}+A_{ik}d\omega_{k}=0.

Furthermore, let the Riemannian metric as defined in (
10
) derived using the triple
\tilde{\underline{\omega}}
be
g_{\tilde{\underline{\omega}}}
and let the metric defined using
\underline{\omega}
be denoted by
g_{\underline{\omega}}
. Then the metrics are constant multiples of one another:

g_{\tilde{\underline{\omega}}}=\det(A)g_{\underline{\omega}}.

(14)

This computation can be easily seen from some of the computations done in the proof of lemma
2.5
. Since
A
is a constant matrix,
\det(A)
is also constant. Therefore the metric
g_{\underline{\omega}}
is still Ricci-flat. We conclude that when
Q
is constant for a hypersymplectic triple we are able transform to a hyperkähler triple whose metric is the same up to a constant factor.

3.1
Dual positive triple and a coclosed

Given some positive triple
\underline{\omega}

is also symmetric and positive definite. We use this to define a new positive triple

called the dual positive triple.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
X^{4}
be an oriented manifold of dimension four, let
\underline{\omega}=(\omega_{1},\omega_{2},\omega_{3})
be a positive triple on

.
Let
Q

and the volume form

\underline{\sigma}=(\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\sigma_{3})

\sigma_{i}=\left(Q^{-1}\right)_{ik}\omega_{k}=Q^{ik}\omega_{k}

(15)

is another a positive triple on

.

Proof.

We remark that since

is symmetric and positive definite, so is

. Thus if

then the triple

\begin{split}\sigma_{i}\wedge\sigma_{j}=\left(Q^{ik}\omega_{k}\right)\wedge\left(Q^{jl}\omega_{l}\right)&=Q^{ik}Q^{jl}\omega_{k}\wedge\omega_{l}\\
&=2Q^{ik}Q^{jl}Q_{kl}\mu_{\underline{\omega}}\\
&=2Q^{ik}\delta_{jk}\mu_{\underline{\omega}}\\
&=2Q^{ij}\mu_{\underline{\omega}}.\end{split}

(16)

Since
Q^{-1}
is positive definite this along with the computation (
16
) confirms that the triple
\underline{\sigma}
given as in (

) defines a positive triple on
X^{4}
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(N^{3},g_{3})
an
(X^{4},g_{4})
be two Riemannian manifolds. Let
M^{7}=N^{3}\times X^{4}
be a Riemannian manifold where at each point the metric splits as
g_{7}=g_{3}\oplus g_{4}
. Let
\alpha\in\Omega^{k}(N^{3})
and
\beta\in\Omega^{l}(X^{4})
. Then

*_{7}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=(-1)^{l(k+1)}(*_{3}\alpha)\wedge(*_{4}\beta).

Proof.

Let
\operatorname{vol}_{7}
,
\operatorname{vol}_{3}
, and
\operatorname{vol}_{4}
be the volume forms on
M^{7}
,
N^{3}
, and
X^{4}
respectively thus
\operatorname{vol}_{7}=\operatorname{vol}_{3}\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{4}

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge*_{7}(\alpha\wedge\beta)

\displaystyle=|\alpha\wedge\beta|^{2}_{g_{7}}\operatorname{vol}_{7}

\displaystyle=|\alpha|^{2}_{g_{3}}|\beta|^{2}_{g_{4}}\operatorname{vol}_{3}\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{4}

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge*_{3}\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge*_{4}\beta

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l\left(3-k\right)}\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge*_{3}\alpha\wedge*_{4}\beta

\displaystyle=(-1)^{l\left(k+1\right)}\alpha\wedge\beta\wedge*_{3}\alpha\wedge*_{4}\beta

Therefore we can conclude that
*_{7}(\alpha\wedge\beta)=(-1)^{l(k+1)}(*_{3}\alpha)\wedge(*_{4}\beta)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
X^{4}
is a
4

\underline{\omega}
. Let

\begin{split}*_{3}dt^{1}&=Q^{13}dt^{12}+Q^{11}dt^{23}+Q^{21}dt^{31},\\
*_{3}dt^{2}&=Q^{23}dt^{12}+Q^{21}dt^{23}+Q^{22}dt^{31},\\
*_{3}dt^{3}&=Q^{33}dt^{12}+Q^{31}dt^{23}+Q^{32}dt^{31},\end{split}

(19)

and

\begin{split}*_{3}dt^{31}&=Q_{12}dt^{1}+Q_{22}dt^{2}+Q_{32}dt^{3},\\
*_{3}dt^{12}&=Q_{31}dt^{1}+Q_{32}dt^{2}+Q_{33}dt^{3},\\
*_{3}dt^{23}&=Q_{11}dt^{1}+Q_{12}dt^{2}+Q_{13}dt^{3}.\end{split}

(20)

Proof.

Let us start with proving (

\displaystyle\alpha\wedge*_{7}dt^{i}

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge*_{3}dt^{i}\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{4}

\displaystyle\implies g_{7}(\alpha,dt^{i})\operatorname{vol}_{3}\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{4}

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge*_{3}dt^{i}\wedge\operatorname{vol}_{4}

\displaystyle\implies g_{7}(\alpha,dt^{i})dt^{123}

\displaystyle=\alpha\wedge*_{3}dt^{i}

which tells us that

can be computed using the metric

. Furthermore since

has no mixed pieces we need only consider

and thus in that case we need only consider

. Returning to
*_{3}dt^{i}
, we only show the details for
*_{3}dt^{1}
as the remaining computations are done similarly. Let the entries of

\displaystyle=g_{7}(dt^{1},dt^{1})dt^{123}

\displaystyle Bdt^{123}

\displaystyle=Q^{11}dt^{123}

\displaystyle\implies B

\displaystyle=Q^{11},

\displaystyle dt^{2}\wedge*_{3}dt^{1}

\displaystyle=g_{7}(dt^{2},dt^{1})dt^{123}

\displaystyle Cdt^{123}

\displaystyle=Q^{21}dt^{123}

\displaystyle\implies C

\displaystyle=Q^{21},

\displaystyle dt^{3}\wedge*_{3}dt^{1}

\displaystyle=g_{7}(dt^{3},dt^{1})dt^{123}

\displaystyle\implies A

\displaystyle=Q^{31}.

Combining these together we obtain that
*_{3}dt^{1}=Q^{13}dt^{12}+Q^{11}dt^{23}+Q^{21}dt^{31}
. We can repeat this same process for

\displaystyle dt^{12}\wedge*_{3}dt^{31}

\displaystyle=-\left(Q^{23}Q^{11}-Q^{13}Q^{21}\right)dt^{123}

\displaystyle\implies C

\displaystyle=Q_{32},

\displaystyle dt^{31}\wedge*_{3}dt^{31}

\displaystyle=g_{7}(dt^{31},dt^{31})dt^{123}

\displaystyle Bdt^{123}

\displaystyle=(Q^{33}Q^{11}-(Q^{13})^{2})dt^{123}

\displaystyle\implies B

\displaystyle=Q_{22},

\displaystyle dt^{23}\wedge*_{3}dt^{31}

\displaystyle=g_{7}(dt^{23},dt^{31})dt^{123}

The hypersymplectic flow from the Laplacian coflow

[
10

(
1
)
the Laplacian coflow and modified coflow agree. Let us recall the forms

\begin{split}\varphi&=dt^{123}-dt^{1}\wedge\omega_{1}-dt^{2}\wedge\omega_{2}-dt^{3}\wedge\omega_{3},\\
\psi&=\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}-dt^{12}\wedge\sigma_{3}-dt^{31}\wedge\sigma_{2}-dt^{23}\wedge\sigma_{1},\end{split}

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
\psi(t)
be a solution of the modified Laplacian coflow (

\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
with initial condition
\psi_{0}
given by

\psi_{0}=\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}dt^{jk}\wedge\sigma_{i}

remains of the form (

Proof.

Let
\psi_{0}
be as in (
24

\psi_{0}
is
\mathbb{T}^{3}
invariant than so is any solution of the modified Laplacian coflow with this initial condition. Let
F
be any diffeomorphism and given that
\psi
does not determine an orientation we fix the standard orientation on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
. Let
\psi_{t}
be a solution for the modified coflow with initial condition
\psi_{0}
. Then if
F
is orientation preserving we fix the same orientation on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
for
F^{*}\psi_{t}
and if
F
is orientation reversing we choose the opposite orientation for

, this ensures in the computation below that the pullback of

commutes with the Hodge star

, that is
*_{F^{*}_{\psi_{t}}}(F^{*}\alpha)=F^{*}(*_{\psi_{t}}\alpha)

-form
\alpha

\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
.
Then we compute:

\begin{split}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}F^{*}\psi_{t}&=F^{*}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\psi_{t}\right)\\
&=F^{*}\left(dd^{*_{\psi_{t}}}\psi_{t}+2d\left(\left(A-\mathrm{Tr}T_{\psi_{t}}\right)*_{\psi_{t}}\psi_{t}\right)\right)\\
&=dd^{*_{F^{*}\psi_{t}}}F^{*}\psi_{t}+2d\left(\left(A-\mathrm{Tr}T_{F^{*}\psi_{t}}\right)*_{F^{*}\psi_{t}}F^{*}\psi_{t}\right).\end{split}

(25)

This means that
\psi_{t}
and

both solve the modified coflow with the same initial condition

and therefore by uniqueness of solutions of the flow we obtain

for all

. Thus
\psi_{t}
is
\mathbb{T}^{3}
-invariant for all time
t
for which it exists. Next we claim that any solution
\psi(t)
of the modified coflow has the form

\psi(t)=\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)-\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}dt^{jk}\wedge\sigma_{i}(t)

(26)

where
\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)
is the volume form that depends on the initial coclosed
G_{2}
structure coming from the hypersymplectic triple
\underline{\sigma}
. To see this we let
\psi(t)
be a solution of the modified coflow with initial condition
\psi_{0}
. We can write
\psi(t)
as general as possible as

\begin{split}\psi(t)=\lambda(t)\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)+dt^{i}\wedge A_{i}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}dt^{ij}\wedge B_{k}(t)+dt^{123}\wedge C(t)\end{split}

(27)

where
\lambda(t)\in C^{\infty}(X^{4})
,
A_{i}(t)\in\Omega^{3}(X^{4})
,
B_{k}(t)\in\Omega^{2}(X^{4})
for all

and
C(t)\in\Omega^{1}(X^{4})
. Since we know that the modified coflow preserves the closedness of the

-form we have that all of the forms in (

is constant on

and thus only depends on

. Now let
\theta:\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}\to\mathbb{T}^{3}\times X^{4}
be the map that takes

, since

. Then

\begin{split}\theta^{*}\psi_{t}&=\lambda(t)\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)-dt^{i}\wedge A_{i}(t)+\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}dt^{ij}\wedge B_{k}(t)-dt^{123}\wedge C(t)\\
&=\psi_{t}\end{split}

(28)

which tells that

for all

and

. Now we claim that

is constant. To see this we compute

\begin{split}\int_{X^{4}}\psi_{t}&=\int_{X^{4}}\lambda(t)\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)+\int_{X^{4}}\frac{1}{2}\varepsilon_{ijk}dt^{ij}\wedge B_{k}(t)\\
&=\int_{X^{4}}\lambda(t)\mu_{\underline{\sigma}}(0)+0\\
&=\lambda(t)\mathrm{vol}_{0}(X^{4}).\end{split}

Now we take the time derivative of both sides of (

\begin{split}0=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\int_{X^{4}}\psi_{t}\right)&=\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\lambda(t)\mathrm{vol}_{0}(X^{4})\right)=\lambda^{\prime}(t)\mathrm{vol}_{0}(X^{4}).\end{split}

(30)

Hence
\lambda(t)
is constant and therefore

for all

then any solution

Now we can return to the modified coflow and show that in our particular setting the coflow (
