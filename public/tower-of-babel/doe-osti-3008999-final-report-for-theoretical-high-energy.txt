FINAL TECHNICAL REPORT
GRANT NAME: University System of New Hampshire
GRANTE NUMBER: DE-SC0020220
PERIOD OF PERFORMANCE: 06/1/2019-05/31/2025
TITLE OF THE GRANT: Theoretical High Energy Physics at the University of New Hampshire
PI’S NAME: Chanda Prescod-Weinstein

This award supported the research activities three faculty in the University of New Hampshire
Department of Physics and Astronomy: Dr. Chanda Prescod-Weinstein (PI), Dr. Per Berglund
(Co-I), and Dr. David Mattingly (Co-I). These research activities broadly spanned areas of high
energy physics that included dark matter cosmology, quantum gravity, and string theory.

Dr. Prescod-Weinstein received direct support in the form of one month of summer salary. Some
funding also paid undergraduate research assistant Jessica Roberts. The bulk of her share of the
funding was used to provide summer salary for a PhD student, now Dr. Anthony Mirasola.
Funding was also used to support both Dr. Prescod-Weinstein and Dr. Mirasola’s travel to
conference to present results from their grant-funded research. Work product included four
papers where Dr. Mirasola and Dr. Prescod-Weinstein were co-authors, publicly available on the
arXiv:
https://arxiv.org/abs/2007.07438
https://arxiv.org/abs/2110.08921
https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.13220
https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02663
The grant played a major role in Dr. Mirasola’s ability to complete these four papers and
successfully defend his dissertation in Spring 2025. The results enhanced our understanding of
how self-interactions, the presence of black holes, and multi-field scenarios shape the evolution
of dark matter halos comprised of axion-like particles.

Support from this award provided summer salary for Prof. Mattingly, three graduate students
(including Dr. Adam Dukehart who has now graduated), and an undergraduate. Funding also
supported attendance and presentations by Prof. Mattingly and graduate students at domestic and
international conferences based on the results in the papers above. The research funded by the
award developed new avenues for quantum gravity phenomenology, new results in black hole
thermodynamics, and strengthened the interplay between gravity and quantum information. The
following papers were supported by the grant:

During the grant period, Prof. Berglund received partial summer support, as did two PhD
students at UNH, both of whom have since graduated and are now postdoctoral scholars. Prof.

Berglund also worked with six undergraduate students at UNH on a number of machine learning
projects in theoretical particle physics, especially focusing on the topology and geometry of
Calabi-Yau manifolds in string compactifications. In addition, Prof. Berglund had several
collaborations, both in the US and overseas, on various topics in string theory, such as analytical
studies of Calabi-Yau manifolds, machine learning applications in string compactifications, the
cosmological constant problem, and a novel approach to quantum gravity by “gravitizing” the
Born rule. Travel support was provided to allow Prof. Berglund and students to travel to
