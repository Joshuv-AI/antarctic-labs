Bulk-Surface Coupled PDE with an Open Boundary
Charles L. Epstein, Yoichiro Mori, Han Zhou

Abstract
. We study a bulk–surface coupled Laplace system involving an embedded open boundary. The problem is reformulated as an integro-differential equation using boundary integral representations, for which we establish existence and uniqueness of the solution. A Wiener–Hopf technique is employed to study the solution regularity and derive asymptotic expressions for the edge singularity. Building on these results, we develop a finite element method that incorporates the singularity structure and provide a rigorous error analysis. Numerical experiments confirm the theoretical convergence rates.

1.
Introduction

Let
\Omega_{1}
be a simply connected bounded domain with boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega_{1}
and
S
be an open portion of
\Gamma
with two endpoints
\partial S=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{n}:\Gamma\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be the unit normal that points from
\Omega_{1}
to
\Omega_{2}
.
Denote by
\Omega_{2}=\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}
.
Let
\boldsymbol{\nu}
be the outer normal vector on
\partial S
.
Given
f:S\to\mathbb{R}
and
g:\partial S\to\mathbb{R}
, we consider the coupled bulk-surface system

(1.1a)

\displaystyle-\Delta u

\displaystyle=0,\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{2}\backslash\overline{S},

(1.1b)

\displaystyle u

\displaystyle=U,\quad\text{ on }S,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}U+\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]

\displaystyle=f,\quad\text{ on }S,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}U

\displaystyle=g,\quad\text{ on }\partial S,

(1.1e)

\displaystyle u(x)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{O}(|x|^{-1}),\quad|x|\to\infty,

where
u:\mathbb{R}^{2}\to\mathbb{R}
and
U:S\to\mathbb{R}
are the unknown solutions.
Here,
\nabla_{\Gamma}
is the surface gradient on
\Gamma
and
\Delta_{\Gamma}=\nabla_{\Gamma}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}
is the Laplace-Beltrami operator on
\Gamma
.
The notation
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]
is the jump of the normal derivative across
S
, defined as

(1.2)

\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right](x)=\lim_{\varepsilon\to 0+}\boldsymbol{n}(x)\cdot(\nabla u(x-\varepsilon\boldsymbol{n})-\nabla u(x+\varepsilon\boldsymbol{n})),\quad x\in\Gamma.

The functions
f
and
g
are assumed to satisfy the compatibility condition

(1.3)

\int_{S}f(x)\,ds_{x}+\sum_{p\in\partial S}g(p)=0,

which ensures consistency with the decay of the solution at infinity.

Bulk–surface coupled partial differential equations (PDEs) arise in a wide range of physical, biological, and chemical processes where dynamics in a bulk domain interact with processes occurring on its boundary or on an embedded surface. Typical examples include chemical reactions and pattern formation on cell membranes coupled to cytoplasmic diffusion
[
20
,
21
,
23
,
3
]
, as well as coupled diffusion in crystalline systems
[
10
,
17
]
. In such systems, quantities diffuse within both the bulk and the surface and are coupled through interfacial fluxes or dynamic boundary conditions that govern exchange between the two regions. The system (
1.1
) considered in this work represents the time-independent formulation of such a coupled diffusion problem.

There has been a growing body of theoretical and computational research on bulk–surface coupled PDEs involving closed surfaces in recent years
[
11
,
29
,
30
,
4
,
19
,
1
,
7
,
21
,
27
]
. To the best of our knowledge, the present paper is the first to address both the well-posedness and numerical approximation of coupled bulk–surface PDEs with an open surface.
Compared to problems with closed surfaces, systems involving open surfaces pose additional challenges in both analysis and numerical approximation. The domain is typically non-Lipschitz, and the solution lacks standard boundary regularity. For instance, in screen-type problems, the solution often exhibits singularities at the edge of the open boundary. Such singularities prevent the solution from attaining higher regularity even when the data are smooth.

There has been a long and systematic line of research on PDEs posed on open surfaces, particularly on screen problems in wave scattering and crack problems in continuum mechanics. The earliest rigorous studies on existence and uniqueness for the Helmholtz equation with an open boundary are due to Hayashi
[
12
,
14
,
13
]
. The Wiener–Hopf decomposition technique for analyzing boundary regularity in pseudodifferential equations on bounded domains and manifolds was developed by Eskin
[
9
]
, providing a systematic framework for deriving asymptotic expressions of edge singularities. This technique was further applied to screen and mixed boundary value problems by Stephan
[
24
,
26
,
25
]
, and to crack problems by Duduchava et al.
[
6
]
. Subsequently, Costabel et al.
[
5
]
conducted a more refined analysis of the singularity structure and demonstrated that the logarithmic term predicted by the general theory is a “shadow term” that should not appear. More recent studies have extended these results to problems involving multiple or fractal screens
[
15
,
16
,
2
]
.

The remainder of the paper is organized as follows. Section
2
introduces the notation and functional spaces used throughout. In Section
3
, we establish the existence and uniqueness of weak solutions. Section
4
analyzes the regularity and provides the asymptotic expansion of the edge singularity. In Section
5
, we develop a finite element method that incorporates the identified singularities and provide a corresponding convergence analysis.

2.
Notation and functional spaces

Throughout this work, we focus on dimensions
n=1,2
. Following
[
9
]
, we define the Fourier transform and its inverse as follows

(2.1)

\displaystyle(\mathcal{F}\varphi)(\xi)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\varphi(x)e^{-i(x\cdot\xi)}\,dx,\quad(\mathcal{F}^{-1}\widehat{\varphi})(x)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)e^{i(x\cdot\xi)}\,d\xi.

For
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the Sobolev space
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined via Fourier transform

(2.2)

H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in\mathscr{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):\int_{\mathbb{R}^{2}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{u}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi<+\infty\}.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a non-empty open subset. We denote by
p_{\Omega}
the restriction operator to
\Omega
and
\widetilde{\cdot}:H^{s}(\Omega)\to H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
as an extension operator with
\left\lVert\widetilde{u}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\Omega)}
. Particularly, when
\Omega=\mathbb{R}_{+}
, we write
p_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}
as
p_{+}
, and use
u_{+}
to denote the extension of
u
by zero outside
\mathbb{R}_{+}
.
For an integer
k\geq 0
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, the
C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)
norm is defined as

(2.3)

\|u\|_{C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\sum_{|\beta|\leq k}\|D^{\beta}u\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}+\sum_{|\beta|=k}[D^{\beta}u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}.

where the
C^{0}
-norm and the Hölder seminorm are given by

(2.4)

\|f\|_{C^{0}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\sup_{x\in\Omega}|f(x)|,\quad[f]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in\Omega,x\neq y\end{subarray}}\frac{|f(x)-f(y)|}{|x-y|^{\alpha}}.

The Hölder space
C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)
is defined by

(2.5)

C^{k,\alpha}(\Omega)\coloneqq\left\{u\in C^{k}(\Omega)\;:\;[D^{\beta}u]_{C^{0,\alpha}(\Omega)}<\infty\text{ for all multi-indices }\beta\text{ with }|\beta|=k\right\},

We also define the following Sobolev spaces

(2.6)

\displaystyle H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})=\{u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\text{supp }u\subset\overline{\Omega}\},\quad H^{s}(\Omega)=\{u|_{\Omega}:u\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\}.

The space
H^{s}(\Omega)
can be understood as the quotient
H^{s}(\Omega)=H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})/H^{s}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, with the norm
\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\Omega)}=\inf_{v\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),v|_{\Omega}=u}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
The spaces
H^{-s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
H^{s}(\Omega)
are canonically dual with respect to the duality pairing extended by the inner-product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:L^{2}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\Gamma)\times L^{2}(\Omega)\to\mathbb{C}
, defined as

(2.7)

\left(u,v\right)_{L^{2}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\left(u,\widetilde{v}\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},\quad u\in L^{2}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),v\in L^{2}(\Omega),\left.\widetilde{v}\right|_{\Omega}=v.

If
\Omega
is Lipschitz, the space
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
can be equivalently characterized as the closure of
C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
within
H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})

[
22
, Theorem 3.29, 3.30]
.
Functions in
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
are defined on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
but have compact support contained in
\overline{\Omega}
. Therefore, for any larger domain
O\supset\Omega
, the space
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equivalent to
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(O)
.

Lemma 2.1
.

For any non-empty open sets
\Omega,O\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
where
\overline{\Omega}\subset O
, it follows that
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is equal to
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(O)
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.1
.
∎

We also introduce functional spaces on the boundary
\Gamma=\partial\Omega
.
For
s\in\mathbb{N}
, we define the Sobolev space on
\Gamma
as

(2.8)

H^{s}(\Gamma)=\{u\in L^{2}(\Gamma):X^{\alpha}u\in L^{2}(\Gamma)\text{ for }|\alpha|\leq s\},

where
X^{\alpha}
is the vector field tangent to
\Gamma
.
For
s\in\mathbb{R}_{+}\setminus\mathbb{N}^{*}
, the fractional Sobolev spaces are defined by interpolation
H^{k+\theta}(\Gamma)=[H^{k}(\Gamma),H^{k+1}(\Gamma)]_{\theta}
with
s=k+\theta
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. They can also be characterized with the trace operator

(2.9)

H^{s}(\Gamma)=\{u|_{\Gamma}:u\in H^{s+\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\},\quad s>0.

The negative Sobolev space for
s<0
is defined via duality

(2.10)

H^{s}(\Gamma)=\left(H^{-s}(\Gamma)\right)^{\prime},\quad s<0.

Similarly, for the open portion
S\subset\Gamma
, we define the Sobolev spaces

(2.11)

\displaystyle H^{s}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)=\{u\in H^{s}(\Gamma):\operatorname{supp}\,u\subset\overline{S}\},\quad H^{s}(S)=\{u|_{S}:u\in H^{s}(\Gamma)\}.

On the boundary
\partial S
, which consists of a finite number of points, we define the inner-product and norm as

(2.12)

\left(f,g\right)_{\partial S}=\sum_{p\in\partial S}f(p)\overline{g(p)},\quad\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{\partial S}=\sqrt{\left(f,f\right)_{\partial S}}.

We denote by
\mathbb{H}=H^{1}(S)\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma).
For any
\boldsymbol{u}=(u_{1},u_{2}),\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{1},v_{2})\in\mathbb{H}
, the inner-product and induced norm are naturally given by

(2.13)

\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right)_{\mathbb{H}}=\left(u_{1},v_{1}\right)_{H^{1}(S)}+\left(u_{2},v_{2}\right)_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)},\quad\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}=\sqrt{\left(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\right)_{\mathbb{H}}}.

For any integer
k\geq 1
and
\alpha\in(0,1]
, let
\boldsymbol{X}\in C^{k,\alpha}(I)
where
I=(-1,1)
be a regular parameterization of
S
.
Here, we say the parametrization
\boldsymbol{X}
is regular if
|\partial_{s}\boldsymbol{X}(s)|>0
for all
s\in I
.
We assume that
S
is not self-intersecting and satisfies the arc–chord condition

(2.14)

\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}\coloneqq\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}s,\eta\in I,s\neq\eta\end{subarray}}\frac{|\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(\eta)|}{|s-\eta|}>0.

Furthermore, we assume that
\boldsymbol{X}
admits an extension

\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
, which parametrizes the closed curve
\Gamma
and satisfies
|\partial_{s}\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}|>0
and
|\widetilde{\boldsymbol{X}}|_{*}>0
where
\mathbb{T}=\mathbb{R}/(2\pi\mathbb{Z})
is the one-dimensional circle.
The following norm equivalence result will be useful.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
f\in H^{s}(\Gamma)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\boldsymbol{X}\in C^{k,\alpha}(\mathbb{T})
with
|s|\leq k
be a regular parameterization of
\Gamma
, we have

(2.15)

\displaystyle c\left\lVert f\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}\leq C\left\lVert f\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{T})},

where the constants
c,C>0
are independent of
f
.

Proof.

See Appendix
A.2
.
∎

The above result immediately leads to the following norm equivalence for functions in
H^{s}(S)
and
H_{\overline{S}}^{s}(\Gamma)
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
f\in H^{s}(S)
and
g\in H^{s}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
for
s\in\mathbb{R}
and
\boldsymbol{X}\in C^{k,\alpha}(I)
with
|s|\leq k
be a regular parameterization of
S
, we have

(2.16)

\displaystyle c\left\lVert f\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(I)}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(S)}\leq C\left\lVert f\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(I)},

(2.17)

\displaystyle c\left\lVert g\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{T})}\leq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq C\left\lVert g\circ\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{H^{s}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{T})},

where the constants

are independent of
f
and
g

3.
Existence and uniqueness

To establish the well-posedness of (
1.1
), we begin by reformulating the problem as an integro-differential equation on
S
via boundary integral operators.
We represent the solution
u
as a single-layer potential,

(3.1)

u(x)=V_{\Omega}\psi(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

where the single-layer potential
V_{\Omega}\psi:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{1}_{loc}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
is defined as

(3.2)

V_{\Omega}\psi(x)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\Gamma}\log|x-y|\psi(y)\,ds_{y},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

and
\psi=\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
is a single-layer density with
\text{supp }\psi\subset\overline{S}
.
Let
V_{\Gamma}\psi
be the trace of
V_{\Omega}\psi
on
\Gamma
and
V_{S}\psi=p_{S}V_{\Gamma}\psi
be the restriction on
S
.
We now consider a slightly more general integro-differential system given by

(3.3a)

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}U+\psi

\displaystyle=f,\quad\text{ on }S,

(3.3b)

\displaystyle V_{S}\psi-U

\displaystyle=h,\quad\text{ on }S,

(3.3c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\nu}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}U

\displaystyle=g,\quad\text{ on }\partial S.

The case when
h=0
corresponds to the problem (
1.1
).

We first state a result concerning the uniqueness of the solution to problem (
1.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

The pair of functions
(u,U)\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})\times H^{1}(S)
solves the homogeneous version of (
1.1
) if and only if
u\equiv 0
and
U\equiv 0
.

Proof.

Let
B_{r}
be a ball of radius
r
centered at the origin, with
r
large enough such that
\Gamma\subset B_{r}
. Since
u\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
, its traces satisfy
\left.u\right|_{\Gamma}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and
\left.u\right|_{\partial B_{r}}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{r})
. Let
u_{1}=p_{\Omega_{1}}u
and
u_{2}=p_{B_{r}\setminus\overline{\Omega_{1}}}u
. By
[
8
, Lemma 7.1]
and
[
28
]
, the normal derivatives satisfy
\left.\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u_{i}\right|_{\Gamma}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
and
\left.u_{2}\right|_{\partial B_{r}}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\partial B_{r})
.
The jump in the normal derivative across
\Gamma
is given by
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]=\left.\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u_{1}\right|_{\Gamma}-\left.\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u_{2}\right|_{\Gamma}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
.
Since
u
satisfies the Laplace equation (
1.1a
) in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S}
, it is smooth away from
\overline{S}
, which implies
\left[u\right]=0
and
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]=0
on
\Gamma\setminus\overline{S}
.
Thus,
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
.
Multiplying both sides of (
1.1a
) by
u
, integrating over
B_{r}
, and applying the integration by parts formula, we obtain

(3.4)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\,ds+\int_{\Gamma}(u_{1}\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u_{1}-u_{2}\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u_{2})\,ds,

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\,ds+\int_{S}U\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\right]\,ds.

Using the homogeneous equations (
1.1b
)–(
1.1d
) (with
f=g=0
) and integrating by parts on
S
, we have

(3.5)

\displaystyle\int_{B_{r}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,d\boldsymbol{x}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\,ds+\int_{S}U\Delta_{\Gamma}U\,ds=\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\,\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\,ds-\int_{S}|\nabla_{\Gamma}U|^{2}\,ds.

Since
u
is harmonic in
\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{B_{r}}
and satisfies the decay condition (
1.1e
), it follows that
\left\lvert\nabla u\right\rvert=\mathcal{O}(r^{-2})
by
[
22
, Theorem 8.8]
. Consequently, we have
\int_{\partial B_{r}}u\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}u\,ds\to 0
as
r\to\infty
.
Taking the limit
r\to\infty
, we conclude that both
\nabla u
and
\nabla_{\Gamma}U
must vanish, implying that
u
and
U
are constant functions.
The decay condition
u=o(1)
as
|\boldsymbol{x}|\to\infty
then forces
u\equiv 0
and
U\equiv 0
everywhere.
∎

We now show the correspondence between solutions of (
1.1
) and (
3.3
).

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
(U,\psi)
be the solution to (
3.3
) with
h=0
, then
(V_{\Omega}\psi,U)
is the unique solution to (
1.1
).

Proof.

Suppose
(U,\psi)
is a solution to (
3.3
). By integrating both sides of (
3.3a
) and applying integration by parts, we obtain

(3.6)

\int_{S}\psi(x)\,ds_{x}=\int_{S}f(x)\,ds_{x}+\sum_{p\in\partial S}g(p)=0,

which implies that the decay at infinity (
1.1e
) is automatically satisfied as long as
f
and
g
satisfy the compatibility condition (
1.3
).
Since
V_{\Omega}\psi
satisfies the Laplace equation together with the boundary conditions on
S
, it follows that
(V_{\Omega}\psi,U)
is a solution to problem (
1.1
). Combining the uniqueness result Proposition
3.1
, we complete the proof.
∎

To establish the existence and regularity of solutions to (
3.3
), we begin with a weak formulation and seek weak solutions. In this setting, we assume that the curve is of class
C^{1,\delta}
.
Here,
h
is allowed to be nonzero.
Given
(f,h)\in\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<\infty
, we say
\boldsymbol{u}=(U,\psi)\in\mathbb{H}
is a weak solution to (
3.3
) if it satisfies

(3.7)

a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=b(\boldsymbol{v}),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}=(\phi,\zeta)\in\mathbb{H},

where the bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and the linear form
b(\cdot)
are given by

(3.8)

\displaystyle a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\left(\nabla_{\Gamma}U,\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\right)_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\langle\psi,\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\langle V_{S}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}-\left\langle U,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)},

(3.9)

\displaystyle b(\boldsymbol{v})

\displaystyle=\left\langle f,\phi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\langle h,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}+\left(g,\phi\right)_{\partial S}.

Lemma 3.3
.

The bilinear form
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous and the linear form
b(\cdot)
is bounded on
\mathbb{H}
.

Proof.

By definition,
\left\langle V_{S}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\left\langle V_{\Gamma}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}
for every
\zeta,\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
and

(3.10)

\left\lVert V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}\leq\left\lVert V_{\Gamma}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}=C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)},\quad\forall\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma).

where we have used that the single-layer integral on a closed curve
V_{\Gamma}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is bounded
[
22
, Theorem 7.1]
.
For every
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}
,

(3.11)

\displaystyle|a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})|

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}\left\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}\phi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)})

\displaystyle\leq C(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}

\displaystyle+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)})=C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

For every
\boldsymbol{v}=(\phi,\zeta)\in\mathbb{H}
, since
\phi\in H^{1}(S)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\frac{1}{2}}(S)
, we have

(3.12)

\displaystyle|b(\boldsymbol{v})|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+(\sum_{p\in\partial S}|g(p)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}(\sum_{p\in\partial S}|\phi(p)|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+C\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\right)\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

This completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\Gamma
be of class
C^{1,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,1)
and non-self-intersecting. The operator
V_{\Gamma}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
can be decomposed into
V_{\Gamma}=V_{\Gamma,0}+V_{\Gamma,1}
where
V_{\Gamma,0}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
is positive and bounded below, i.e., there exist
C>0
such that

(3.13)

\left\langle V_{\Gamma,0}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\geq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}^{2},\quad\forall\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma),

and
V_{\Gamma,1}:H^{-1+s}(\Gamma)\to H^{s+\varepsilon}(\Gamma)
is bounded for every
s\in[0,1]
and
\varepsilon<\delta
.

Proof.

The proof is technical and is provided in the Appendix
A.3
.
∎

The above result can be used directly to obtain a decomposition for
V_{S}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

Let
S
be of class
C^{1,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,1)
and non-self-intersecting. The operator
V_{S}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)
can be decomposed into
V_{S}=V_{S,0}+V_{S,1}
where
V_{S,0}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)
is positive and bounded below, i.e., there exist
C>0
such that

(3.14)

\left\langle V_{S,0}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}\geq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}^{2},\quad\forall\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma),

and
V_{S,1}:H^{-1+s}_{\overline{S}}\to H^{s+\varepsilon}(S)
is bounded for every
s\in[0,1]
and
\varepsilon<\delta
.

Proof.

Since
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
is a closed subspace of
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)
, we can define
V_{S,i}
as restrictions of
V_{\Gamma,i}
for
i=0,1
such that,

(3.15)

V_{S,i}\psi=V_{\Gamma,i}\psi,\quad\forall\psi,\zeta\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma).

Then it follows that for every
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
,

(3.16)

\left\langle V_{S,0}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\left\langle V_{\Gamma,0}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}\geq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}^{2}=C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}^{2},

and, for any
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
,

(3.17)

\left\lVert V_{S,1}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)}\leq\left\lVert V_{\Gamma,1}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\Gamma)}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}(\Gamma)}=C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}.

Since the embedding
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)\hookrightarrow H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)
is compact for
\varepsilon>0
, the operator
V_{S,1}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)
is compact.
∎

In view of the proof of Lemma
3.4
, we also have

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
S
be of class
C^{1,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,1)
and non-self-intersecting. The operator
V_{S}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\to H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
is bounded for every
|\varepsilon|<\min(\frac{1}{2},\delta)
.

Since
a(\cdot,\cdot)
is continuous in
\mathbb{H}
, we can define a bounded linear operator
\mathcal{A}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
by

(3.18)

\left\langle\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}.

It can be shown that the operator
\mathcal{A}
satisfies a Gårding inequality.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
S
be of class
C^{1,\delta}
with
\delta\in(0,1)
. The following results hold:

(i) There exist a compact operator
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
and a constant
C>0
such that the Gårding inequality holds

(3.19)

\left\langle(\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B})\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}\geq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}^{2},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{H}.

(ii) Given
f\in H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<\infty
, there exist a unique weak solution
(U,\psi)\in\mathbb{H}
to (
3.7
) with

(3.20)

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\right).

Proof.

Define the operator
\mathcal{B}
as

(3.21)

\left\langle\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\left(U,\phi\right)_{L^{2}(S)}-\left\langle V_{S}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in L^{2}(S)\times H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma).

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{\delta}{2})
, by Lemma
3.5
, we have,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{H^{-1+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{(\phi,\zeta)\neq 0}\frac{|\left(U,\phi\right)_{L^{2}(S)}-\left\langle V_{s}\psi,\zeta\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}|}{\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1-\varepsilon}(S)}+\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{(\phi,\zeta)\neq 0}\frac{\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{-1+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1-\varepsilon}(S)}+\left\lVert V_{s}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)}\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}}{\left\lVert\phi\right\rVert_{H^{1-\varepsilon}(S)}+\left\lVert\zeta\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{-1+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)}\leq\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}=\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

This implies
\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
is compact since
H^{s}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow\hookrightarrow H^{t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
for
s>t
.
The Gårding inequality (
3.19
) follows directly,

(3.23)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}+\left\langle V_{S}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\geq\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}^{2}-\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}+C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}^{2}+\left\langle V_{S,1}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\geq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}-\left\langle\mathcal{B}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u}=(U,\psi)\in\mathbb{H}.

Hence,
\mathcal{A}+\mathcal{B}:\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
is strictly coercive and
\mathcal{A}
is a Fredholm operator with index zero.
Therefore, the solvability of the problem (
3.7
) is equivalent to the uniqueness.
In order to show uniqueness, we consider the homogeneous problem for
\boldsymbol{u}\in\mathbb{H}
,

(3.24)

a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})=0,\quad\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}.

Let
\boldsymbol{v}=(1,0)
, we have

(3.25)

\left\langle 1,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\left\langle 1,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0.

Let
\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{u}
, we have

(3.26)

\left\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}+\left\langle V_{S}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}=\left\lVert\nabla_{\Gamma}U\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}+\left\langle V_{\Gamma}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0.

Define
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{0}(\Gamma)
as

(3.27)

H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{0}(\Gamma)=\{\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma):\left\langle 1,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}=0\}.

By
[
22
, Theorem 8.12]
,
V_{\Gamma}
is strictly positive-definite on
H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{0}(\Gamma)
, that is,

(3.28)

\left\langle V_{\Gamma}\psi,\psi\right\rangle_{L^{2}(\Gamma)}>0\quad\forall\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{0}(\Gamma)\setminus\{0\}.

So we have
\psi=0
. Let
\phi=0
, we have
\left\langle U,\zeta\right\rangle=0,\forall\zeta\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
and so
U=0
. By the Fredholm alternative,
\mathcal{A}
is bijective and has bounded inverse. By Lemma
3.3
, it holds that

(3.29)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\prime}}=C\sup_{0\neq\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}}\frac{a(\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v})}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}=C\sup_{0\neq\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}}\frac{b(\boldsymbol{v})}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{v}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(S)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

4.
Regularity of solutions

4.1.
Half-line case

We discuss the case in which the boundary is given by
S=\mathbb{R}_{+}\times\{0\}
, where
\mathbb{R}_{+}=(0,+\infty)
is the half-line.
Let
A:H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{s+1}(\mathbb{R})
and
B:H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R})
be pseudo-differential operators with symbols
\widehat{A}(\xi)=(1+\xi^{2})^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
\widehat{B}(\xi)=1+\xi^{2}
, respectively. In fact,
B
is a differential operator
\mathcal{I}-\partial_{ss}
.
We consider the pseudo-differential equations on the half-line,

(4.1)

p_{+}A\psi_{+}=f_{1},\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+},

and,

(4.2)

Bv=f_{2},\quad\text{ on }\mathbb{R}_{+},\quad\partial_{x}v(0)=g,

where
p_{+}
is the restriction to the half-line
\mathbb{R}_{+}
. Here,
\psi_{+}=\psi(x)
for
x>0
and
\psi_{+}(x)=0
for
x<0
. The operator
A
represents the leading-order behavior of the single-layer integral operator and has a suitable form for applying the Wiener–Hopf technique
[
9
]
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
f_{1}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be given with
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
. The solution
\psi_{+}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})
and there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4.3)

\left\lVert\psi_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

Proof.

Define symbols
\widehat{A}_{\pm}(\xi)=(\xi\pm i)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
. Then,
\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi+i\tau)
is analytic in the closed upper half plane where
\tau>0
and
\widehat{A}_{-}(\xi-i\tau)
is analytic in the closed lower half plane. We factor
\widehat{A}(\xi)
as

(4.4)

\widehat{A}(\xi)=(\xi+i)^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\xi-i)^{-\frac{1}{2}}=\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi)\widehat{A}_{-}(\xi).

Let
\widetilde{f}_{1}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})
be an arbitrary extension of
f_{1}
satisfying

(4.5)

\left\lVert\widetilde{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})},

and let

(4.6)

\psi_{-}=\widetilde{f}_{1}-A\psi_{+}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{-}}}(\mathbb{R}).

Then the equation can be written as

(4.7)

\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi)\widehat{A}_{-}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)+\widehat{\psi_{-}}(\xi)=\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}(\xi).

Multiplying both sides by
\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}(\xi)
gives

(4.8)

\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)+\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{-}}(\xi)=\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}(\xi),

where each term belongs to
\mathcal{F}(H^{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}))
.
Furthermore, by the Paley-Wiener theorem, we have

(4.9)

\widehat{A}_{\pm}\widehat{\psi_{\pm}}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{\pm}}}(\mathbb{R})).

For any
\varphi\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
with
s\in\mathbb{R}
, the operator
\Pi_{+}(\widehat{\varphi})=\mathcal{F}(\chi_{[0,\infty)}\varphi)
can be expressed as

(4.10)

\Pi_{\pm}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi)=\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(\xi)}{2}\pm\Pi_{0}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi),\quad\Pi_{0}(\widehat{\varphi})(\xi)=\frac{i}{2\pi}\text{p.v.}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\widehat{\varphi}(\eta)d\eta}{\xi-\eta}.

Since
\mathcal{F}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))=L^{2}(\mathbb{R},(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}d\xi)
, it can be shown that
\Pi_{0}:\mathcal{F}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))\to\mathcal{F}(H^{s}(\mathbb{R}))
is bounded for
|s|<\frac{1}{2}
.
On noting that

(4.11)

\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{-}\widehat{\psi_{-}}](\xi)=0,\quad\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{+}\widehat{\psi_{+}}](\xi)=\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)\in H^{\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}),

by applying
\Pi_{+}
to both sides of (
4.8
), we have

(4.12)

\widehat{A}_{+}(\xi)\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)=\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi),

and so

(4.13)

\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)=\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})).

Thus,
\psi_{+}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})
and

(4.14)

\left\lVert\psi_{+}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{f}_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}.

∎

For more regular data, we can derive the singular behavior of the solution near the boundary based on the Wiener-Hopf technique.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f_{1}\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
be given with
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
. The solution
\psi_{+}
has the form

(4.15)

\psi_{+}(x)=c\chi_{[0,\infty)}(x)e^{-x}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\psi_{r}(x),\quad\psi_{r}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}),

where
c
is a constant depending on
f_{1}
.

Proof.

We will first consider the general case for
f_{1}\in H^{n+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
where
n\in\mathbb{N}_{+}
.
Recall that the solution is given by

(4.16)

\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)=\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi).

Since
\Pi_{+}:\mathcal{F}(H^{s}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}))\to\mathcal{F}(H^{s}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}))
is only bounded for
|s|<\frac{1}{2}
, we need to treat
\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{n+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}))\subset\mathcal{F}(H^{\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}))
and conclude that
\psi_{+}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})
.
We will apply the expansion formula for the operator
\Pi_{+}

[
9
, Lemma 5.5]
to extract the singular terms.
Define the symbols
\widehat{\Lambda}_{\pm}(\xi)=\xi\pm i
.
We can expand
\Pi_{+}[\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi)
such that

(4.17)

\widehat{\psi_{+}}(\xi)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-k}(\xi)\Pi^{\prime}[\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{k-1}\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}]+\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-n}(\xi)\Pi_{+}[\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{n}\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi),

where
\Pi^{\prime}
is defined by

(4.18)

\Pi^{\prime}[\widehat{h}]=\frac{i}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\widehat{h}(\eta)\,d\eta.

We can see that

(4.19)

\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-n}(\xi)\Pi_{+}[\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{n}\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}](\xi)\in\mathcal{F}(H^{n-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})),

which has optimal regularity, and

(4.20)

\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-k}(\xi)=(\xi+i)^{-k+\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathcal{F}(H^{k-\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R})),\quad k=1,2,\cdots,n,

are the more singular terms. The singular terms can be computed explicitly.

(4.21)

\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-k}(\xi))(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}(\xi+i)^{-k+\frac{1}{2}}e^{-ix\xi}\,d\xi=\frac{e^{(1/4-k/2)i\pi}}{\Gamma(k-\frac{1}{2})}\chi_{[0,\infty)}(x)e^{-x}x^{k-\frac{3}{2}}.

Now the solution can be expressed as

(4.22)

\psi_{+}(x)=\sum_{k=1}^{n}c_{k}\psi_{k}(x)+\psi_{r}(x),

where

(4.23)

\psi_{r}=\mathcal{F}^{-1}(\widehat{A}_{+}^{-1}(\xi)\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{-n}(\xi)\Pi_{+}[\widehat{\Lambda}_{+}^{n}\widehat{A}_{-}^{-1}\widehat{\widetilde{f}_{1}}])\in H^{n-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}),

is the most regular term and
\psi_{k}
are given by

(4.24)

\psi_{k}(x)=\chi_{[0,\infty)}(x)e^{-x}x^{k-\frac{3}{2}},\quad k=1,2,\cdots,n.

The coefficients satisfy the bound

(4.25)

|c_{k}|\leq M_{k,a}\left\lVert f_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{n+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon-a}(\mathbb{R})},\quad 0\leq a<n+\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon-k.

Applying the result for
n=1
proves the lemma.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
f_{2}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
s>-\frac{1}{2}
. Then there exists a unique solution
v\in H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
to the boundary value problem (
4.2
). Furthermore, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(4.26)

\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{H^{s+2}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert f_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|g|\right).

Proof.

The existence of the solution
v
can be established by representing it as

(4.27)

v(x)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\frac{\widehat{\widetilde{f}}_{2}(\xi)e^{ix\xi}}{1+\xi^{2}}\,d\xi+ce^{-x},

where
\widetilde{f}_{2}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R})
is an extension of
f_{2}
to
\mathbb{R}
, and the constant
c
is uniquely determined by the boundary condition specified in (
4.2
).
∎

We are also interested in the coupling of (
4.1
) and (
4.2
), where the function
f_{2}
may include the solution
\psi
and has a singular behavior. This will also affect the regularity of the solution
v
.

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
f_{2}=f_{s}+f_{r}
, where
f_{s}(x)=ce^{-x}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
with
c\in\mathbb{R}
, and
f_{r}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the solution
v
to
Bv=f_{2}
admits the decomposition

(4.28)

v(x)=-\frac{4}{3}ce^{-x}x^{\frac{3}{2}}+v_{r}(x),

where the remainder
v_{r}\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
satisfies the bound

(4.29)

\left\lVert v_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert f_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}+|c|+|g|\right).

Proof.

Let
v_{s}(x)=-\frac{4}{3}e^{-x}x^{\frac{3}{2}}
be the singular part. A direct computation shows that the operator
B=\mathcal{I}-\partial_{xx}
acting on
v_{s}
yields

(4.30)

Bv_{s}=v_{s}-v_{s}^{\prime\prime}=e^{-x}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}-4e^{-x}x^{\frac{1}{2}}.

Substituting
v=cv_{s}+v_{r}
into the governing equation
Bv=f_{s}+f_{r}
, we find that the remainder
v_{r}
satisfies

(4.31)

Bv_{r}=f_{s}+f_{r}-cBv_{s}=f_{r}+4ce^{-x}x^{\frac{1}{2}},

with the boundary condition
\partial_{x}v_{r}(0)=g
. Since
f_{r}
and
4ce^{-x}x^{\frac{1}{2}}
both belong to
H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})
for
\left\lvert\varepsilon\right\rvert<\frac{1}{2}
, the result follows by applying the elliptic regularity from Lemma
4.3
to
v_{r}
.
∎

4.2.
Open arc case

We now investigate higher regularity properties of the solution.
In particular, when the given data are more regular, we aim to derive the asymptotic behavior of the boundary singularities.
From this point on, we assume that the open arc
S
is smooth and parameterized by arc length, that is,

\boldsymbol{X}\in C^{\infty}(I)
and
|\boldsymbol{X}^{\prime}(s)|=1
.
Although a classical solution to (
1.1
) only requires the curve to be
C^{2}
in order for the Laplace-Beltrami operator to be defined pointwise, we do not pursue this level of generality here.
Throughout the discussion, we denote by
u\circ\boldsymbol{X}
simply as
u
, with the understanding that all functions are pulled back to the parameter domain
I
.
The single-layer operator on the open arc is then expressed as

(4.32)

(V_{S}\psi)(s)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{I}\log|\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(\eta)|\,\psi(\eta)\,d\eta.

We also introduce the corresponding operator on the straight interval,

(4.33)

(V\psi)(s)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{R}}\log|s-\eta|\,\psi(\eta)\,d\eta.

For convenience, let
R=V-V_{S}
denote the difference between the two operators.
Under this parametrization, the system (
3.3
) can be equivalently written as

(4.34)

\displaystyle-\partial_{ss}U+\psi

\displaystyle=f,\quad\text{in }I,

(4.35)

\displaystyle V_{S}\psi-U

\displaystyle=h,\quad\text{in }I,

(4.36)

\displaystyle\partial_{s}U(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=\pm g(\pm 1).

We take a partition of unity
\{\varphi_{n}\}_{n=1}^{3}
such that
\varphi_{1}(s)
and
\varphi_{3}(s)
are equal to
1
in neighborhoods of the endpoints
-1
and
1
respectively, and
\varphi_{2}(s)
is supported away from the boundary
\partial I
, satisfying

(4.37)

0\leq\varphi_{n}\leq 1,\quad\sum_{n=1}^{3}\varphi_{n}(s)=1,\quad\forall\,s\in I.

We express the solutions as

(4.38)

\psi(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{3}\varphi_{n}(s)\psi(s),\quad U(s)=\sum_{n=1}^{3}\varphi_{n}(s)U(s),\quad s\in I.

We will analyze the local solutions
\varphi_{n}\psi,\varphi_{n}U
and then patch them up to obtain the regularity of the solution.

Theorem 4.5
.

Given
f\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
,
h\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}<+\infty
for
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
. Then the solutions
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})
and
U\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
and

(4.39)

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}\right).

Proof.

Since
f\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
belongs to
H^{-1}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})
and
h\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
belongs to
H^{\frac{1}{2}}(I)
, Theorem
3.7
applies and we have
U\in H^{1}(I)
and
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})
with

(4.40)

\displaystyle\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\right)\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}\right).

We first consider the interior regularity of
\psi
.
We choose a smooth cutoff function
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(I)
. We consider the equation

(4.41)

A(\varphi\psi)=(A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi+\varphi R\psi+\varphi V_{S}\psi,

where the commutator
[V,\varphi]\psi
is defined as

(4.42)

[V,\varphi]\psi=V(\varphi\psi)-\varphi V\psi.

The operator defined by pointwise multiplication by
\varphi
is a pseudo-differential operator of order
0
, and it holds that

(4.43)

\left\lVert\varphi u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall s\in\mathbb{R}.

The complete symbol of the operator
V
is given by
\widehat{V}(\xi)=\frac{1}{2|\xi|}
. Let
V_{0}
be the pseudo-differential operator with symbol
\frac{1-\rho(\xi)}{2|\xi|}
where
\rho(\xi)\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})
is a cutoff function being
1
for
|\xi|<\frac{1}{2}
and
\rho(\xi)=0
for
|\xi|>1
.
Then
V-V_{0}
defines a smoothing operator and
V-V_{0}:H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{r}(\mathbb{R})
is bounded for all
s<r
.
We also have

(4.44)

|\widehat{A}(\xi)-\widehat{V}_{0}(\xi)|\leq C(1+|\xi|)^{-3},

and so

(4.45)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(A-V)\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(A-V_{0})\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert(V-V_{0})\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s-3}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s-3}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},

for every
s\geq\frac{5}{2}
.
We split
[V,\varphi]\psi
as

(4.46)

[V,\varphi]\psi=[V_{0},\varphi]\psi+[V-V_{0},\varphi]\psi.

Since
V_{0}
and
\varphi
are pseudo-differential operators of order
-1
and
0
, respectively. The commutator
[V_{0},\varphi]
defines a pseudo-differential operator of order
-2
. The operator
[V-V_{0},\varphi]
is a smoothing operator. Then

(4.47)

\displaystyle\left\lVert[V,\varphi]\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert[V_{0},\varphi]\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert[V-V_{0},\varphi]\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},

for every
s\geq\frac{3}{2}
.
Now we consider
\varphi R\psi
, which can be written as

(4.48)

\displaystyle\varphi R\psi(s)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\varphi(s)\int_{I}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert\psi(t)\,dt,

where
\Delta\boldsymbol{X}=\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(t)
.
Note that,
\forall N
,
\boldsymbol{X}(t)-\boldsymbol{X}(s)=\sum_{j=1}^{N}\frac{\boldsymbol{X}^{(j)}(s)}{j!}(t-s)^{j}+\mathcal{O}(|t-s|^{N+1})
.
So
\frac{\boldsymbol{X}(t)-\boldsymbol{X}(s)}{t-s}\in C^{\infty}(\overline{I}\times\overline{I})
and is nowhere vanishing, which implies the kernel
\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert
is smooth.
Since
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})\subset H_{\overline{I}}^{-1}(\mathbb{R})
, it can be written as

(4.49)

\psi(s)=\psi_{0}+\partial_{s}F(s),\quad s\in I,\quad\psi_{0}=\int_{I}\psi(s)\,ds,\quad F\in L^{2}(I).

Then, after integration by parts, we have

(4.50)

\displaystyle\int_{I}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert\psi(t)\,dt=\psi_{0}\int_{I}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert\,dt-\int_{I}\partial_{t}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert F(t)\,dt.

and for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
, by Cauchy-Schwarz inequality,

(4.51)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{s}^{k}\int_{I}\partial_{t}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert F(t)\,dt\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}=\int_{I}\left(\int_{I}\partial_{s}^{k}\partial_{t}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert F(t)\,dt\right)^{2}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq\int_{I}\int_{I}\left(\partial_{s}^{k}\partial_{t}\log\left\lvert\frac{\Delta\boldsymbol{X}}{s-t}\right\rvert\right)^{2}\,dt\,ds\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert F\right\rVert_{L^{2}(I)}^{2}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}.

This implies that
R\psi\in H^{k}(I)
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
Thus, let
\widetilde{R\psi}
be an extension of
R\psi
with
\left\lVert\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\left\lVert R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{k}(I)}
we have

(4.52)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\varphi\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{k}(I)}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By interpolation we have

(4.53)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall s\geq 0.

Recall that
V_{S}\psi=U+h
on
I\supseteq\text{supp }\varphi
.
Let
\widetilde{U}
,
\widetilde{h}
be extensions of
U
and
h
with
\left\lVert\widetilde{U}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}
and
\left\lVert\widetilde{h}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq 2\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}
.
Then,
\varphi V_{S}\psi=\varphi(U+h)=\varphi(\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h})
and

(4.54)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\varphi(\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h})\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right),

In summary, for every
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
,

(4.55)

\displaystyle\left\lVert(A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi+\varphi R\psi+\varphi V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{R})}}\right).

The inverse operator
A^{-1}
has symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}
and is of order
1
.
Using (
4.41
), we have

(4.56)

\left\lVert\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{R})}}\right).

To study the regularity of
\psi
near the boundary
\partial I
, we shift the left endpoint
x=-1
to the origin under the transformation
x^{\prime}=1+x
and let
\varphi
be a smooth cutoff function localized at the origin, satisfying
\text{supp }\varphi\supset(-\delta,\delta)
for some
\delta>0
and
\text{supp }\varphi\cap\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}\subset[0,1)
.
We now consider the following equation on the half-line,

(4.57)

p_{+}A(\varphi\psi)=p_{+}\left((A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi+\varphi R\psi+\varphi V_{S}\psi\right).

The previous results show that

(4.58)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{+}\left((A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi\right)\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert(A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s-2}(\mathbb{R})},

for every
s\geq\frac{3}{2}
, and

(4.59)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{+}\varphi R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}=\left\lVert p_{+}\varphi\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C\left\lVert\varphi\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{R\psi}\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{k}(I)}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall k\in\mathbb{N}.

By interpolation, we have

(4.60)

\left\lVert p_{+}\varphi R\psi\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq C\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})},\quad\forall s\geq 0.

Since
\text{supp }\varphi\cap\mathbb{R}_{+}\subset I
, it holds that
p_{+}\varphi V_{S}\psi=p_{+}\varphi(U+h)=p_{+}\varphi(\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h})
.
Then

(4.61)

\left\lVert p_{+}\varphi V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}\leq\left\lVert\varphi(\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h})\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

Thus, by Lemma
4.1
, we obtain

(4.62)

\left\lVert\varphi\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\right).

Patching up local solutions and using Theorem
3.7
, we obtain

(4.63)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{R})}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

To study the regularity of
U
, we use the cutoff function
\varphi
with
\text{supp }\varphi\subset I
and consider the equation

(4.64)

B(\varphi U)=\varphi U-\partial_{ss}\varphi U-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}U-\varphi\partial_{ss}U.

Recalling that
\partial_{ss}U=\psi-f
on
I
and
\text{supp }\partial_{s}^{k}\varphi\subset I
for
k\geq 0
, we have

(4.65)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\varphi U-\partial_{ss}\varphi U-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}U-\varphi\partial_{ss}U\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\varphi(\widetilde{U}-\psi+f)-\partial_{ss}\varphi\widetilde{U}-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}\widetilde{U}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{U}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

where we have used
\left\lVert\varphi f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\varphi f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}
since
\text{supp }(\varphi f)\subset I
.
The inverse operator
B^{-1}
has the symbol
(1+|\xi|^{2})^{-1}
and is of order
-2
. Hence,

(4.66)

\left\lVert\varphi U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\leq\left\lVert\varphi U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

To study the boundary regularity of
U
, we use the same cutoff function
\varphi
and consider the following equation on the half-line:

(4.67)

p_{+}B(\varphi U)=p_{+}\left(\varphi U-\partial_{ss}\varphi U-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}U-\varphi\partial_{ss}U\right),

with boundary condition
\partial_{s}(\varphi U)(0)=\partial_{s}U(0)=g(0)
.
For the right-hand side, we have

(4.68)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{+}\left(\varphi U-\partial_{ss}\varphi U-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}U-\varphi\partial_{ss}U\right)\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert p_{+}\left(\varphi(\widetilde{U}-\psi+f)-\partial_{ss}\varphi\widetilde{U}-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}\widetilde{U}\right)\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\varphi(\widetilde{U}-\psi+f)-\partial_{ss}\varphi\widetilde{U}-2\partial_{s}\varphi\partial_{s}\widetilde{U}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\widetilde{U}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

By Lemma
4.3
, we have

(4.69)

\left\lVert\varphi U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{1}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}\right).

Patching up local solutions and using Theorem
3.7
, we obtain

(4.70)

\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\leq C\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

This completes the proof.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Given
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
,
h\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
, and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial I}<\infty
, the solutions
\psi
and
U
have the form

(4.71)

U(x)=\sum_{p\in\partial I}c_{2}(p)\varphi(|x-p|)|x-p|^{\frac{3}{2}}+U_{r}(x),\quad U_{r}\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(I),

and

(4.72)

\psi(x)=\chi_{I}(x)\sum_{p\in\partial I}c_{1}(p)\varphi(|x-p|)|x-p|^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\psi_{r}(x),\quad\psi_{r}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R}),

where
c_{1}(p),c_{2}(p)
are constants depending on
p
and
\varphi(d)
is a smooth cut-off function.

Proof.

We continue to consider equation (
4.57
) for the local solution
\varphi\psi
.
Applying Theorem
4.5
, we have

(4.73)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{+}\varphi V_{S}\psi\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\varphi(\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h})\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}\leq C\left\lVert\widetilde{U}+\widetilde{h}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

Combining equations (
4.58
) and (
4.60
), we obtain

(4.74)

\displaystyle\left\lVert p_{+}\left((A-V)(\varphi\psi)+[V,\varphi]\psi+\varphi(V-V_{S})\psi+\varphi V_{S}\psi\right)\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+})}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert U\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}+\left\lVert\psi\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R})}+\left\lVert h\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)}\right).

By Lemma
4.2
, if
\text{supp }\varphi\cap\{0\}\neq\emptyset
, the local solution has the form

(4.75)

\varphi\psi(x)=c\chi_{[0,\infty)}(x)e^{-x}x^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\psi_{r}(x),\quad\psi_{r}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{\mathbb{R}_{+}}}(\mathbb{R}),

and if
\text{supp }\varphi\subset I
, the local solution
\varphi\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})
. Summing all local solutions yields (
4.72
).

Using (
4.72
) in the local equation for
\varphi U
with
\text{supp }\varphi\cap\{0\}\neq\emptyset
and applying Lemma
4.4
, the local solution
\varphi U
has the form

(4.76)

\varphi U(x)=de^{-x}x^{\frac{3}{2}}+U_{r}(x),\quad U_{r}\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{R}_{+}),

where
d
is some constant.
For the interior solution, since
\varphi\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{I}}(\mathbb{R})
when
\text{supp }\varphi\subset I
, we have
\varphi U\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(I)
.
Patching together the local solutions for
U
yields (
4.71
).
∎

Mapping the functions back to
S
, by Lemma
2.3
, we consequently have

Theorem 4.7
.

Given
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
,
h\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<+\infty
, the equation (
3.3
) has a unique solution
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
with
\left.\psi\right|_{K}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(K)
and
U\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)\cap H_{loc}^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
for every
|\varepsilon|<\frac{1}{2}
and compact subset
K\Subset S
. In particular, the solutions have the form

(4.77)

U=\sum_{p\in\partial S}c_{2}(p)\varphi(d)d^{\frac{3}{2}}+U_{r},\quad\psi=\sum_{p\in\partial S}c_{1}(p)\varphi(d)d^{-\frac{1}{2}}+\psi_{r},\quad\text{on }S,

with
U_{r}\in H^{\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
and
\psi_{r}\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
. Here,
d
is the arclength along
\Gamma
near the boundary
\partial S
, and
\varphi
is a smooth cutoff function.

We can further show that
(U,\psi)
and
(V_{\Omega}\psi,U)
are classical solutions.

Proposition 4.8
.

Given
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
,
h\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<+\infty
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, then
U\in C^{2}(S)\cap C^{1}(\overline{S})
,
\psi\in C(S)
, and the equation (
3.3
) is satisfied pointwise.

Proof.

Since
H^{s}(\mathbb{R})\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\mathbb{R})
for

0<\alpha<s-\frac{1}{2}
, Theorem
4.7
implies that

U\in C^{1,\alpha}(S)
. Moreover, for every compact subset
K\Subset S
,

\left.U\right|_{K}\in C^{2,\alpha}(K)
and

\left.\psi\right|_{K}\in C^{0,\alpha}(K)

for any
\alpha\in(0,\varepsilon)
.
Consequently,
U\in C^{2}(S)\cap C^{1}(\overline{S})
and
\psi\in C(S)
.
In addition, by Lemma
3.6
, we have

V_{S}\psi\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(S)
.
Since the pair
(U,\psi)
satisfies the weak formulation (
3.7
), it follows that (
3.3
) is satisfied pointwise.
∎

Proposition 4.9
.

Given
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(S)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<+\infty
for
\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, then
u=V_{\Omega}\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S})\cap C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
U\in C^{2}(S)\cap C^{1}(\overline{S})
, and the equation (
1.1
) is satisfied pointwise.

Proof.

Since the kernel
\log|x-y|
is smooth at
x\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S}

for
y\in S
, it follows immediately that

V_{\Omega}\psi\in C^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S})
, and the Laplace
equation (
1.1a
) is satisfied pointwise in

\mathbb{R}^{2}\setminus\overline{S}
.
The decay condition (
1.1e
) follows from the compatibility
condition (
1.3
), while the boundary condition
(
1.1d
) was established in the previous proposition.
Therefore, it remains to verify that (
1.1b
)–(
1.1c
) hold pointwise.

By Theorem
4.7
and
[
22
, Theorem 6.12]
, since

\psi\in H_{\overline{S}}^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\Gamma)
for some

\varepsilon\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, it follows that for any bounded open set

O\subset\mathbb{R}^{2}
and any cutoff function

\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
with
\mathrm{supp}\,\chi\subset O
, we have
\chi V_{\Omega}\psi\in H^{1+\varepsilon}(O)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(O)
where
\alpha<\varepsilon.

This implies that
V_{\Omega}\psi\in C(\mathbb{R}^{2})
and
\left.u\right|_{S}=V_{S}\psi=U
pointwise on
S
.

We now prove the jump relation

\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}V_{\Omega}\psi\right]=\psi
pointwise on
S
.
Fix a point
x\in S
and choose
r>0
sufficiently small such that

\mathrm{dist}(x,\partial S)\geq 2r
.
Let
\chi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
satisfy

\chi(y)\equiv 1
for
|x-y|\leq r/2
and
\chi(y)\equiv 0
for
|x-y|\geq r
.
Then
\mathrm{supp}(\chi\psi)\subset S
, and hence

\chi\psi\in H_{\overline{S}}^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}(\Gamma)\hookrightarrow C^{0,\alpha}(\Gamma)
.
By
[
18
, Theorem 6.19]
, we obtain
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\bigl(V_{\Omega}(\chi\psi)\bigr)\right](x)=\psi(x).

On the other hand, since
\log|x-y|(1-\chi(y))
is smooth at
x
, it follows that

V_{\Omega}\bigl((1-\chi)\psi\bigr)
is smooth at
x
, and therefore
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}V_{\Omega}((1-\chi)\psi)\right](x)=0.

Combining these results yields
\left[\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}V_{\Omega}\psi\right](x)=\psi(x),

and consequently (
1.1c
) holds pointwise on
S
due to Proposition
4.8
.

∎

5.
Finite element Approximation

In this section, we introduce a finite element discretization of the system (
3.7
) and prove an a priori error estimate.

5.1.
Discretization

The preceding analysis shows that the boundary density
\psi
exhibits the singular behavior
\psi\sim d^{-\frac{1}{2}}
near an open endpoint, so that
\psi\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}
for some
\varepsilon\in\bigl[0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr).

Hence, a conventional piecewise‐polynomial finite element space can achieve at best
\|\psi-\psi_{h}\|_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\Gamma)}=O\bigl(h^{\varepsilon}\bigr).

Similarly, the solution
U
behaves like
U\sim d^{\frac{3}{2}}
near the endpoint, and satisfies
U\in H^{\frac{3}{2}+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for some
\varepsilon\in\bigl[0,\frac{1}{2}\bigr),
yielding at most
\|U-U_{h}\|_{H^{1}(\Omega)}=O\bigl(h^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}\bigr).

Since these singular components are localized at the boundary while the remainder of the solution remains smooth, a more effective remedy is to enrich the finite element space by incorporating basis functions that capture the
d^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and
d^{\frac{3}{2}}
singularities explicitly.

Let
N
be a positive integer and introduce a (quasi‐uniform) partition of
\overline{I}:-1=x_{0}<x_{1}<\cdots<x_{N}=1
with mesh‐size
h=\max_{1\leq j\leq N}(x_{j}-x_{j-1})
. Let
\mathcal{T}_{h}=\cup_{j=1}^{N}\overline{\sigma_{j}}
be the computational mesh with
\sigma_{j}=(x_{j-1},x_{j})
.
First, we define the space of singular elements

(5.1)

S^{q}=\text{span}\bigl\{(1+x)^{q}\varphi(1+x),(1-x)^{q}\varphi(1-x)\bigr\},

where
\varphi
is a smooth cutoff function satisfying
\varphi(x)=1
for
x\leq 1/8
and
\varphi(x)=0
for
x\geq 1/4
. For example, we choose
\varphi(x)=\eta(8x-1)
with

(5.2)

\eta(t)=\frac{\phi(t)}{\phi(t)+\phi(1-t)},\quad\phi(t)=\begin{cases}\exp\bigl(-1/t\bigr),&t>0,\\
0,&t\leq 0,\end{cases}.

Let
V_{h}
be the continuous piecewise-polynomial finite element space, defined as

(5.3)

V_{h}=\{\widetilde{v}=v\circ\boldsymbol{X}^{-1},v\in C(\overline{I}):\left.v\right|_{\overline{\sigma_{j}}}\in\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{\sigma_{j}}),j=1,2,\cdots,N\},

where
\mathbb{P}_{1}(\overline{\sigma_{j}})
is the function space of polynomials on
\overline{\sigma_{j}}
of degree no greater than
1
.
Define the finite element spaces for the solutions
U
and
\psi
respectively.

(5.4)

\displaystyle W^{1}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{v}\in C(\overline{S}):\widetilde{v}=v+w\circ\boldsymbol{X}^{-1},v\in V_{h},w\in S^{\frac{3}{2}}\},

(5.5)

\displaystyle W^{2}_{h}

\displaystyle=\{\widetilde{v}\in H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma):\left.\widetilde{v}\right|_{\overline{S}}=v+w\circ\boldsymbol{X}^{-1},v\in V_{h},w\in S^{-\frac{1}{2}}\}.

Let
\mathbb{H}_{h}=W^{1}_{h}\times W^{2}_{h}
. It is evident that we have the inclusion
W^{1}_{h}\subset H^{1}(I)
,
W^{2}_{h}\subset H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{I}}(R)
and
\mathbb{H}_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}
.
The Galerkin finite element method is formulated as finding
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=(U_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
, such that

(5.6)

a(\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=b(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=(\phi,\zeta)\in\mathbb{H}_{h}.

5.2.
A priori error estimate

Let
\pi_{L^{2}}:L^{2}(S)\to V_{h}
be the
L^{2}
projection operator such that for
u\in L^{2}(S)
, it holds that

(5.7)

\left(u-\pi_{L^{2}}u,v_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(S)}=0,\quad\forall v_{h}\in V_{h}.

We state the well-known property of the
L^{2}
projection operator for approximating functions in
H^{s}(S)
,
s\geq 0
with piecewise linear finite element space.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
0\leq t\leq s
and
u\in H^{s}(S)
. There exist
C>0
independent of
h
such that

(5.8)

\left\lVert u-\pi_{L^{2}}u\right\rVert_{H^{t}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{\min(1,s-t)}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\Gamma)}.

The following result is for the approximation of
L^{2}
functions in negative norms.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
t\in(0,1]
. For every
u\in L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
, there exist
C>0
such that

(5.9)

\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert u-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{t}\left\lVert u-\pi_{L^{2}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}.

Proof.

By using Lemma
5.1
, we obtain

(5.10)

\displaystyle\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert u-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq\left\lVert u-\pi_{L^{2}}u\right\rVert_{H^{-t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}=\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{t}(S)}=1}\left(u-\pi_{L^{2}}u,w\right)_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle=\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{t}(S)}=1}\left(u-\pi_{L^{2}}u,w-\pi_{L^{2}}w\right)_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{H^{t}(S)}=1}\left\lVert u-\pi_{L^{2}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert w-\pi_{L^{2}}w\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}\leq Ch^{t}\left\lVert u-\pi_{L^{2}}u\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}.

∎

The following lemma states the optimal approximation property of the finite element space
\mathbb{H}_{h}
.

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(U,\psi)\in\mathbb{H}
be the solution that admits the decomposition (
4.77
). For every
s\in[1,\frac{5}{2}+\varepsilon]
,
t\in[0,\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon]
, there exist
C>0
independent of
h
, such that

(5.11)

\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C\left(h^{\min(1,s-1)}\left\lVert U_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(S)}+h^{\frac{1}{2}+t}\left\lVert\psi_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\right).

Proof.

By Lemma
5.1
and
5.2
, we have

(5.12)

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\inf_{\phi_{h}\in W_{h}^{1}}\left\lVert U-\phi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\inf_{\zeta_{h}\in W_{h}^{2}}\left\lVert\psi-\zeta_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{\phi_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert U_{r}-\phi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\inf_{\zeta_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert\psi_{r}-\zeta_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert U_{r}-\pi_{L^{2}}U_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+Ch^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\psi_{r}-\pi_{L^{2}}\psi_{r}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(h^{\min(1,s-1)}\left\lVert U_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{s}(S)}+h^{\frac{1}{2}+t}\left\lVert\psi_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{t}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\right).

∎

Lemma 5.4
.

Let
\boldsymbol{u}=(U,\psi)\in\mathbb{H}
be the exact solution to (
3.7
). Let
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=(U_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
be the numerical solution. Let
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, there holds the error estimate in lower norms

(5.13)

\left\lVert U-U_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left(\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert U-U_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}\right).

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
be the adjoint of
\mathcal{A}
, defined as

(5.14)

\left\langle\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle=\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},\mathcal{A}\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle=a(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{u}),\quad\forall\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}.

It is clear that
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}
has the same regularity properties as
(\mathcal{A})^{-1}
.
Let the norm
\left\lVert\cdot\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{X}}
be

(5.15)

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}\right\rVert_{Z}=\left\lVert u_{1}\right\rVert_{L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert u_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}(S)}.

Then
Z=L^{2}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)\times H^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}(S)\subset\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
.
For every
\boldsymbol{w}\in\mathbb{H}^{\prime}
and an arbitrary
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
, we have

(5.16)

\displaystyle|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}\right)_{L^{2}}|=|\left\langle\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}|=|\left\langle\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\mathcal{A}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}^{\prime}}\left\lVert(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\left\lVert(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

We also have the approximation property for
(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}
by
\mathbb{H}_{h}
,

(5.17)

\displaystyle\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}}\left\lVert(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\inf_{v_{h}\in W^{1}}\left\lVert\phi_{1}-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\inf_{v_{h}\in W^{2}}\left\lVert\phi_{2}-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert\phi_{1}-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\inf_{v_{h}\in V_{h}}\left\lVert\phi_{2}-v_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert\phi_{1}-\pi_{L^{2}}\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1}(S)}+\left\lVert\phi_{2}-\pi_{L^{2}}\phi_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left(\left\lVert\phi_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{1+\sigma}(S)}+\left\lVert\phi_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left(\left\lVert w_{1}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert w_{2}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}(S)}\right)\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{w}\right\rVert_{Z}.

Therefore, we have

(5.18)

|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}\right)_{L^{2}}|\leq C\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\left\lVert(\mathcal{A}^{\prime})^{-1}\boldsymbol{w}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{w}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{X}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

and

(5.19)

\left\lVert U-U_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\leq\sup_{\boldsymbol{w}\neq 0}\frac{|\left(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{w}\right)_{L^{2}}|}{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{w}\right\rVert_{\boldsymbol{X}}}\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}.

∎

Since the problem (
3.7
) satisfies the Gårding inequality (
3.19
), the solvability and convergence of the finite element approximation (
5.6
) is also guaranteed, and we have the following theorem.

Theorem 5.5
.

Given
f\in H^{-1}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
and
g
with
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}<+\infty
, there exist
h_{0}>0
such that for any
h
,
0<h<h_{0}
, the problem (
5.6
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=(U_{h},\psi_{h})\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
. Moreover, if
f\in H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)
, then we have the error estimate

(5.20)

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq Ch\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\right).

Proof.

We begin by deriving an estimate for any solution to (
5.6
) that may exist. Note that we always have

(5.21)

a(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})=0,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}.

For any
\sigma\in(0,\frac{1}{2})
, By Lemma
3.5
and
5.4
, we have

(5.22)

\displaystyle|\left\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}|

\displaystyle\leq|\left\langle V_{S,1}(\psi-\psi_{h}),(\psi-\psi_{h})\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}|+|\left(U-U_{h},U-U_{h}\right)_{L^{2}(S)}|

\displaystyle\leq\left\lVert V_{S,1}(\psi-\psi_{h})\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\sigma}(S)}\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert U-U_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\left\lVert\psi-\psi_{h}\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}-\sigma}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert U-U_{h}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(S)}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{\sigma}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

For any
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}
, by the continuity of
a(\cdot,\cdot)
and the Gårding inequality (
3.19
),

(5.23)

\displaystyle C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}\leq a(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h})+\left\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle=a(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h},\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h})+\left\langle\mathcal{B}(\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}),\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rangle_{L^{2}(S)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}+Ch^{\sigma}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}^{2}.

Since
\sigma>0
, there exist
h_{0}>0
such that for every
h\in(0,h_{0})
, we have

(5.24)

\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq C\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}},\quad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}.

Then, applying Lemma
5.3
, we have

(5.25)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq\inf_{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{H}_{h}}\left\lVert\boldsymbol{u}-\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rVert_{\mathbb{H}}\leq Ch\left(\left\lVert U_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{2}(S)}+\left\lVert\psi_{r}\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}\right)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left(\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}+\varepsilon}_{\overline{S}}(\Gamma)}+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{\partial S}\right).

We now discuss the existence and uniqueness of the solution to (
5.6
). Since
\mathbb{H}_{h}\subset\mathbb{H}
is finite-dimensional, the system (
5.6
) has the same number of equations and unknowns. Therefore, it suffices to consider the homogeneous problem. For
f\equiv 0
and
g\equiv 0
, we have
\boldsymbol{u}\equiv 0
by Theorem
3.7
. For sufficiently small
h
, (
5.25
) also implies
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}\equiv 0
. This shows that the finite element approximation (
5.6
) has a unique solution
\boldsymbol{u}_{h}=(U_{h},\psi_{h})
.
∎

A.1.
Proof of Lemma
2.1

The inclusion
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\subset H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(O)
holds since

(A.1)

\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(O)}=\inf_{g\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n}),\left.g\right|_{O}=f}\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}\leq\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

for every
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
.
We choose
\varphi\in C_{c}^{\infty}(O)
such that
\varphi=1
in
\Omega
and
\varphi=0
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus\overline{O}
, and there exists a constant
C
such that for a multi-index
\alpha
,

(A.2)

|\partial^{\alpha}\varphi|\leq C,

where the constant
C
only depends on
\Omega,O,\alpha
.
For an integer
k\geq 0
and
f\in C_{c}^{\infty}(\Omega)
, we have

(A.3)

\displaystyle\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\left\lVert\varphi f\right\rVert_{H^{k}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}=\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}(\varphi f)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sum_{|\beta|+|\gamma|=|\alpha|}\left\lVert\partial^{\beta}\varphi\partial^{\gamma}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(O)}^{2}\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\sum_{|\beta|+|\gamma|=|\alpha|}\left\lVert\partial^{\beta}\varphi\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(O)}^{2}\left\lVert\partial^{\gamma}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(O)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{|\alpha|\leq k}\left\lVert\partial^{\alpha}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(O)}^{2}=C\left\lVert f\right\rVert_{H^{k}(O)}^{2},

where the constant
C
only depends on
\Omega,O,k
.
By the density argument, we have
H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(O)=H^{s}_{\overline{\Omega}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
.

Proof of Lemma

s=m

u\in H^{-1}(\mathbb{T})
and
1\in H^{1}(\mathbb{T})
,

(A.62)

\displaystyle|\overline{u}|=\frac{1}{2\pi}|\left\langle u,1\right\rangle_{L^{2}}|\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-1}},

(A.63)

\displaystyle\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}^{2}=\sum_{k\neq 0}|\widehat{v}_{k}|^{2}=\sum_{k\neq 0}\left\lvert\frac{\widehat{u}_{k}}{ik}\right\rvert^{2}=\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}}{|k|^{2}}\leq 2\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}}{1+|k|^{2}}=2\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-1}}^{2}.

We can rewrite
\mathcal{L}[u]
as

(A.64)

\mathcal{L}[u](s)=\int_{\mathbb{T}}L(s,t)\left(\overline{u}+\partial_{t}v(t)\right)\,dt=\overline{u}\int_{\mathbb{T}}L(s,t)\,dt+\int_{\mathbb{T}}K(s,t)v(t)\,dt.

Since the kernel
L(s,t)
is bounded pointwise,

(A.65)

|L(s,t)|\leq\left\lvert\log\frac{|\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(t)|}{|s-t|}-\log\frac{|2\sin\left((s-t)/2\right)}{|s-t|}\right\rvert\leq\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\log(\pi/2),

for any
w\in L^{1}(\mathbb{T})
, it holds that

(A.66)

\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[w]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\log(\pi/2)\right)\left\lVert w\right\rVert_{L^{1}}.

By using the relation
\mathcal{Q}[u]=\partial_{s}\mathcal{L}[u]
and (
A.60
), we obtain

(A.67)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[u]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}\leq|\overline{u}|\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[1]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}+\left\lVert\mathcal{K}[v]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}\leq\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-1}}\left(\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[1]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}+\left\lVert\mathcal{Q}[1]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}\right)+\left\lVert\mathcal{K}[v]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\log(\pi/2)\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-1}}+C\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}^{2}}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}^{2}}\left\lVert v\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}^{2}}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}^{2}}+\log(\pi/2)\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-1}}.

Then, we consider the map
\mathcal{L}:L^{2}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})
.
For
u\in L^{2}(\mathbb{T})
, by using (
A.66
) and (
A.60
),

(A.68)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[u]\right\rVert_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\log(\pi/2)\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}},

(A.69)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\partial_{s}\mathcal{L}[u]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle=\left\lVert\mathcal{Q}[u]\right\rVert_{H^{\varepsilon}}\leq C\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}^{2}}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}^{2}}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

Therefore, we obtain

(A.70)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{L}[u]\right\rVert_{H^{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}+\frac{1}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}}+\frac{\left\lVert\boldsymbol{X}\right\rVert_{C^{1,\delta}}^{2}}{\left|\boldsymbol{X}\right|_{*}^{2}}+\log(\pi/2)\right)\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{L^{2}}.

For every
s\in[0,1]
, by the interpolation
H^{-1+s}(\mathbb{T})=[H^{-1}(\mathbb{T}),L^{2}(\mathbb{T})]_{s}
and
H^{s+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})=[H^{\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T}),H^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathbb{T})]_{s}
, we complete the proof.
∎

Now we are ready to prove Lemma
3.4
.
By the norm equivalence Lemma
2.2
, we set
\psi(s)
as
(\psi\circ\boldsymbol{X})(s)
and work on the torus
\mathbb{T}
.
The operator
V_{\Gamma}
can be written as

(A.71)

\displaystyle V_{\Gamma}\psi(s)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log|\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(t)|\psi(t)\,dt

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left\lvert 2\sin\frac{s-t}{2}\right\rvert\psi(t)\,dt-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left\lvert\frac{\boldsymbol{X}(s)-\boldsymbol{X}(t)}{2\sin\frac{s-t}{2}}\right\rvert\psi(t)\,dt

\displaystyle\coloneqq\mathcal{L}_{1}\psi(s)+\mathcal{L}_{2}\psi(s).

The Fourier multiplier for
\mathcal{L}_{1}
is given by

(A.72)

\widehat{\mathcal{L}_{1}}(k)=-\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{\mathbb{T}}\log\left\lvert 2\sin\frac{s}{2}\right\rvert e^{-iks}\,ds=\begin{cases}0,&k=0,\\
\frac{1}{2|k|},&k\in\mathbb{Z}\setminus\{0\}.\end{cases}

Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{1}:H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})\to H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})
is bounded

(A.73)

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathcal{L}_{1}u\right\rVert_{H^{\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\sum_{k\neq 0}(1+|k|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}\frac{1}{4|k|^{2}}|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{1}{(1+|k|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}.

Furthermore,

(A.74)

\displaystyle\left(u,\mathcal{L}_{1}u\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}}{2|k|}\geq\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{k\neq 0}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}}{(1+|k|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4\pi}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\frac{|\widehat{u}_{k}|^{2}}{(1+|k|^{2})^{\frac{1}{2}}}-\frac{1}{4\pi}|\widehat{u}_{0}|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left\lVert u\right\rVert_{H^{-\frac{1}{2}}(\mathbb{T})}^{2}-\left(u,\mathcal{R}u\right)_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T})},

where
\mathcal{R}
is defined as

(A.75)

\mathcal{R}u(s)=\frac{1}{2}\widehat{u}_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\mathbb{T}}u(x)\,dx,

and, it maps any integrable function to a constant function, which belongs to
C^{\infty}(\mathbb{T})
.
Let
V_{\Gamma,0}=\mathcal{L}_{1}+\mathcal{R}
. The above implies that
V_{\Gamma,0}
is strictly coercive.
Let
V_{\Gamma,1}=\mathcal{L}_{2}-\mathcal{R}
.
Since
R
maps any integrable function to a constant function, which belongs to
H^{s}(\mathbb{T})
for every
s\in\mathbb{R}
.
By using Lemma
A.3
, we complete the proof of the lemma.
