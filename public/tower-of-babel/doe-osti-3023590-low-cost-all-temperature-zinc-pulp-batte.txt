1. Public Executive Summary
The CREATE program targeted development of a safe, low-cost, wide-temperature-range
battery for grid and stationary applications. The WH-Power (WHP)–UMD–Yale team developed a
zinc-pulp (Zn/CNF) battery system that combines an aqueous high-entropy electrolyte (HEE)
with a cellulose-derived (Zn-CNF) ion-conducting membrane. The program demonstrated:

• Electrolyte operating window ≈ 2.1 V and stable cycling from –50 °C to 80 °C.
• Zn-cellulose membranes with ~80 MPa wet strength and enhanced Zn²⁺ transport.
• Dry-compressed MnO₂ cathodes up to 250 mg cm⁻² with >200 mAh g⁻¹ first-cycle capacity;
 optimized 60 mg cm⁻² for cost/energy.
• Pouch cells exceeding 100 mAh with up to 98% capacity retention after 80 cycles at C/5.
• A validated <$30/kWh cost pathway using stainless-steel current collectors and slurry
 coating.

These results advance ARPA-E’s mission by enabling non-flammable, low-cost storage for
renewable integration and resilient grids.

4. Project Activities
4.1 Electrolyte Optimization (UMD)
A ZnCl₂ + NaCl high-entropy electrolyte (HEE) extended the electrochemical stability to ~2.1 V
while maintaining high ionic conductivity. A bio-safe additive, 4-hydroxy-3-
methoxybenzaldehyde (HE), suppressed hydrogen evolution and improved Zn plating/stripping
reversibility at both 0.5 and 2 mA cm⁻². Transition to ZnSO₄-based electrolytes enabled stainless-
steel current collectors by eliminating Cl⁻-induced corrosion while preserving high CE.
4.2 Zn-Pulp Ion Conductor and Cathode Development (Yale & WHP)
Cellulose treated in Zn–NaOH formed a Zn-CNF-II network with enhanced mechanical strength
and ion transport. As a binder and membrane, Zn-CNF enabled uniform thick MnO₂ electrodes.
Dry-compression yielded crack-free, current-collector-free pellets up to 250 mg cm⁻²; for
optimal energy and cost balance, 60 mg cm⁻² slurry-cast cathodes were selected.
4.3 Pouch-Cell Fabrication and Testing (WHP)
Using Zn-CNF membranes and HEE/modified electrolytes, >100 mAh pouch cells were
assembled. Stainless-steel collectors with SO₄²⁻-based HEE delivered up to ~98% capacity
retention after 80 cycles at C/5. Root causes of fading (Zn dendrites, electrolyte infiltration
dynamics) were identified via post-mortem examinations and mitigated via pre-soaking and
additive tuning.
4.4 Cost and Tech-to-Market Activities (WHP)
A design-based techno-economic model linked cell architecture to delivered $/kWh. Replacing
carbon cloth with stainless steel and adopting slurry coating reduced materials cost and
simplified manufacturing. Engagements with EverZinc, Salient Energy, BASF, Octet, and USDA
FPL advanced supply-chain readiness and scale-up pathways.

7. Conclusion
The WHP–UMD–Yale collaboration met the principal objectives of the CREATE effort: a safe,
low-cost Zn-pulp battery with wide-temperature operability and a validated <$30/kWh cost
pathway. Electrolyte innovations (HEE + HE additive), robust Zn-CNF ion conductors, and thick
MnO₂ cathodes were demonstrated in pouch cells. Remaining challenges—thick-electrode
cycling stability and Zn dendrite suppression—are the focus of proposed continuation efforts.
The program established a strong foundation for commercialization of low-cost, non-flammable,
long-duration zinc batteries for stationary storage.
SUMMARY

• Demonstrated Feasibility:– Thick MnO₂ cathodes with modified HEE delivered >100 mAh
 pouch cells.– Stainless steel current collectors + slurry coating identified as a cost-reduction
 pathway.– Bio-safe HE additive validated: suppresses HER, enhances Zn reversibility,
 effective in SO₄²⁻ electrolytes.

• Remaining Challenges:– Thick cathode cycling stability and Zn dendrite suppression remain
 critical hurdles.– Scale-up of slurry processing and uniform electrode fabrication must be
 advanced.– Coupling cycle-life improvement with cost reduction is essential for market
 entry.

• Path Forward:– Optimize MnO₂ morphology/architecture to balance stability and capacity.–
 Advance stainless steel current collector strategy to eliminate carbon paper dependency.–
 Integrate electrolyte additive strategy into scaled pouch cells for validation.– Leverage T2M
 modeling to align technical progress with commercialization timelines.

• Year 2 Progress vs. Deliverables:– >100 mAh pouch cells achieved (capacity milestone).–
 Electrolyte development showed effective HER suppression and Zn reversibility.– <$30/kWh
 cost pathway demonstrated (Task 4.2 deliverable).– Commercialization milestones
 identified, positioned for Year 3.

• Bottom Line:Year 2 established a strong foundation: pouch cells exceeded 100 mAh,
 electrolyte strategy validated, and <$30/kWh cost feasibility demonstrated. With
 cathode/electrolyte refinements, Zn anode stabilization, and T2M-guided scaling, the
 program is positioned to meet ARPA-E cost and cycle-life targets in the next phase.
