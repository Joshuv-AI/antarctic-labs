Removing Ostrogradsky modes in multi-field higher-order scalar-tensor theories
Hamed Bouzari Nezhad

Abstract
We study multi-field higher-order scalar-tensor theories and examine how the unwanted Ostrogradsky modes can be removed. For a general class of theories with an arbitrary number 𝒩 \mathcal{N} of scalar fields and quadratic dependence on their second derivatives, we perform an ADM decomposition and develop the Hamiltonian analysis in the branch where the metric kinetic block is invertible. The primary degeneracy condition takes the form of a matrix condition in field space, but in a genuine multi-field theory it is not by itself sufficient: preserving the primary degeneracy constraints generates additional consistency conditions, some of which are antisymmetric in the field-space indices and have no direct analogue in the single-field case. Together with the primary degeneracy condition and a final rank condition on the constraint algebra, these conditions are sufficient for the theory to propagate 2 + 𝒩 2+\mathcal{N} degrees of freedom, the two tensor modes of gravity together with one scalar mode per field, and no additional Ostrogradsky mode. We illustrate the construction in the single-field limit, in a multi-field quadratic Horndeski-type subclass, and in an explicit degenerate multi-field subclass that shows these conditions can be satisfied.



1
Introduction

Scalar-tensor theories provide one of the most natural extensions of general relativity. They arise in many different contexts, from effective descriptions of gravity to models of inflation and late-time cosmic acceleration. A central question in constructing such theories is how derivative interactions can be introduced without propagating unwanted degrees of freedom. In the single-field case, the most general scalar-tensor theory with second-order field equations was constructed by Horndeski
10
. Related developments appeared in the language of Galileons and generalized Galileons, where derivative self-interactions of scalar fields are organized so as to avoid higher-order equations of motion
16
;
4
;
5
;
12
.

Second-order field equations provide a sufficient condition for avoiding the Ostrogradsky problem
20
, but they are not necessary. Higher-order theories may still propagate the correct number of degrees of freedom when their kinetic structure is degenerate. In such cases, the degeneracy gives rise to constraints which remove the would-be extra mode from the physical phase space. This observation led to theories beyond Horndeski and to degenerate higher-order scalar-tensor theories
7
;
13
;
14
;
2
. The relevant criterion is therefore not only the differential order of the field equations, but the full constraint structure generated by the kinetic degeneracy.

The problem becomes richer when more than one scalar field is present. In a multi-field theory, the scalar sector is not described by a single kinetic coefficient, but by matrices in field space. Consequently, degeneracy is no longer characterized by a single scalar condition. The null directions of the kinetic matrix may involve different combinations of scalar and metric velocities, and different degeneracy patterns can lead to different constraint structures. This makes the multi-field problem qualitatively different from the single-field one. It is also consistent with the general observation that the Ostrogradsky problem in systems with several fields can be more subtle than in theories with only one higher-order variable
3
;
15
;
11
;
1
.

Multi-field scalar-tensor theories have been studied from several complementary viewpoints. Multi-Galileon theories provide important examples of derivative interactions involving several scalar fields
18
;
19
. Bi-scalar extensions of Horndeski theory have also been classified by imposing second-order field equations
17
. The approach followed here is different. We do not take second-order field equations as the defining principle. Instead, we consider a multi-field scalar-tensor theory containing terms quadratic in the second covariant derivatives of the scalar fields, and study the degeneracy and consistency conditions required to remove the unwanted higher-order modes.

The aim of this paper is to formulate and analyze a general quadratic multi-field higher-order scalar-tensor theory in a form where its Hamiltonian constraint structure can be followed explicitly. We introduce the covariant action and its independent building blocks, and then perform an ADM decomposition
8
. To isolate the kinetic structure, we use an auxiliary-field formulation, which makes the velocities of the scalar and metric variables manifest. The corresponding Hessian has a block structure, with scalar, metric, and mixed scalar-metric components, and this structure provides a natural way to classify the possible primary degeneracy branches.

Among these branches, we focus on the case in which the metric kinetic block is invertible. This branch keeps the metric kinetic sector under control and lets the degeneracy arise through the mixing between scalar and metric velocities. The primary degeneracy condition then becomes a matrix condition in field space and leads to primary constraints associated with the higher-order scalar sector. We then follow the time evolution of these constraints in the Hamiltonian formalism
6
;
9
. This step is essential: degeneracy of the Hessian guarantees the existence of primary constraints, but it does not by itself guarantee that the unwanted modes are removed. For more than one field, preservation of the primary constraints generates further consistency conditions, and some of these are antisymmetric in the field-space indices and so have no analogue in the single-field theory. This is the central structural feature of the multi-field problem: primary degeneracy is necessary, but it is not by itself sufficient.

The main result is a set of sufficient conditions, within the metric-invertible branch, under which the quadratic multi-field theory propagates no Ostrogradsky modes associated with the higher-order scalar sector. These conditions are the primary degeneracy condition, the primary-primary consistency conditions generated by preserving the primary constraints, and a final rank condition ensuring that the constraint algorithm closes in the desired way. When they hold, the theory propagates
2+\mathcal{N}
degrees of freedom, the two tensor modes of gravity together with one scalar mode for each of the
\mathcal{N}
scalar fields. These are sufficient conditions, not a complete classification of healthy multi-field theories.

The paper is organized as follows. In section
2
we introduce the multi-field quadratic action, define the relevant covariant building blocks and rewrite the theory in auxiliary-field form. In section
3
we perform the ADM decomposition and identify the coefficient blocks controlling the velocity sector. In section
4
we formulate the primary degeneracy conditions and distinguish the possible degeneracy branches. In section
5
we focus on the metric-invertible branch and derive the complete primary degeneracy condition. In section
6
we carry out the Hamiltonian analysis, derive the primary-primary consistency conditions, obtain the secondary constraints and give the degree-of-freedom count. In section
7
we discuss the single-field limit, a multi-field Horndeski-type subclass and a constructive degenerate multi-field subclass that shows these conditions can be satisfied. We discuss our results and conclude in section
8
. The appendices collect several lengthy coefficient expressions and give further details of the constraint analysis.

2
Covariant action and auxiliary field formulation

We consider a theory of
\mathcal{N}
scalar fields
\phi^{i}
, with
i=1,\cdots,\mathcal{N}
, which generalizes the single-field quadratic higher-order scalar-tensor construction by promoting the scalar
\phi
to a collection
\phi^{i}
. Field-space indices are denoted by Latin letters
i,j,k,\cdots
, while spacetime indices are denoted by Greek letters
\mu,\nu,\rho,\cdots
and are raised and lowered with
g_{\mu\nu}
. The first and second covariant derivatives of the scalar fields are

\displaystyle\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i},\qquad\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{i},

(1)

where
\nabla_{\mu}
is the Levi-Civita covariant derivative of
g_{\mu\nu}
. Since
\nabla_{\mu}
is torsion-free, the scalar Hessian
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric,
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=\phi^{i}{}_{(\mu\nu)}
. The kinetic matrix in field space is

\displaystyle X^{ij}=g^{\mu\nu}\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}\phi^{j}{}_{\nu},

(2)

which is symmetric,
X^{ij}=X^{(ij)}
. The coefficient functions of the theory depend on the scalar fields
\phi^{i}
and on the independent components of
X^{ij}
; an argument such as
(\phi^{i},X^{ij})
is understood schematically as dependence on all
\mathcal{N}
fields and on all independent components of the symmetric kinetic matrix.

The interactions are built from the metric, the first derivatives
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}
, and the scalar Hessian
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
, organized by their power of
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
. At linear order in the Hessian there are two parity-even structures: the trace

\displaystyle\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}=g^{\mu\nu}\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu},

(3)

and the contraction with two first derivatives,
\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}
. At quadratic order in the Hessian the parity-even contractions built from
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}
are

\displaystyle L_{1}^{ij}=\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}\phi^{j\mu\nu},\qquad L_{2}^{ij}=\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}\phi^{j}{}_{\nu}{}^{\nu},\qquad L_{3}^{ijkl}=\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}\phi^{l}{}_{\rho}{}^{\rho},

\displaystyle L_{4}^{ijkl}=\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\rho}\phi^{l}{}_{\nu}{}^{\rho},\qquad L_{5}^{ijklmn}=\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k\rho}\phi^{l\sigma}\phi^{m}{}_{\mu\nu}\phi^{n}{}_{\rho\sigma}.

(4)

These five blocks are obtained by classifying the scalars quadratic in
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
according to the number of first-derivative factors they carry: none for
L_{1}^{ij}
and
L_{2}^{ij}
, two for
L_{3}^{ijkl}
and
L_{4}^{ijkl}
, and four for
L_{5}^{ijklmn}
. They form the complete parity-even basis quadratic in
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
within the sector built from
g_{\mu\nu}
and
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}
with no explicit curvature tensor. Further contractions of first derivatives with one another carry no Hessian factor and are absorbed into the arbitrary
X^{ij}
-dependence of the coefficient functions, so they generate no independent structures.

The symmetry of the Hessian induces field-space symmetries of these blocks. The blocks
L_{1}^{ij}
and
L_{2}^{ij}
are symmetric under
i\leftrightarrow j
. The block
L_{3}^{ijkl}
is symmetric under
i\leftrightarrow j
, since
\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric in its spacetime indices. The block
L_{4}^{ijkl}
is invariant under the simultaneous exchange
(i,k)\leftrightarrow(j,l)
, and
L_{5}^{ijklmn}
is invariant under
i\leftrightarrow j
, under
k\leftrightarrow l
, and under the simultaneous exchange
(i,j,m)\leftrightarrow(k,l,n)
of its two linear-Hessian factors. The linear structure
\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric under
i\leftrightarrow j
.

Collecting these structures together with the non-minimal curvature coupling, the action is

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big[F(\phi^{i},X^{ij})R+P(\phi^{i},X^{ij})+Q_{i}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}+B_{ijk}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}

(5)

\displaystyle+A^{(1)}_{ij}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})L_{1}^{ij}+A^{(2)}_{ij}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})L_{2}^{ij}+A^{(3)}_{ijkl}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})L_{3}^{ijkl}+A^{(4)}_{ijkl}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})L_{4}^{ijkl}

\displaystyle+A^{(5)}_{ijklmn}(\phi^{i},X^{ij})L_{5}^{ijklmn}\Big],

where
R
is the Ricci scalar of
g_{\mu\nu}
and the coefficient functions
F
,
P
,
Q_{i}
,
B_{ijk}
and
A^{(I)}
are arbitrary functions of
\phi^{i}
and
X^{ij}
, subject only to the field-space symmetries below. Because each block contributes to the action only through the part of its coefficient that shares its symmetry, we may impose without loss of generality

\displaystyle B_{ijk}=B_{jik},\qquad A^{(1)}_{ij}=A^{(1)}_{ji},\qquad A^{(2)}_{ij}=A^{(2)}_{ji},\qquad A^{(3)}_{ijkl}=A^{(3)}_{jikl},\qquad A^{(4)}_{ijkl}=A^{(4)}_{jilk},

\displaystyle A^{(5)}_{ijklmn}=A^{(5)}_{jiklmn}=A^{(5)}_{ijlkmn}=A^{(5)}_{klijnm}.

(6)

The antisymmetric parts of the coefficients with respect to these transformations multiply identically symmetric blocks and drop out of the action.

If
\mathsf{n}(\cdot)
denotes the number of independent field-space components, the symmetries in Eq. (
2
) give

\displaystyle\mathsf{n}\left({B_{ijk}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{N}^{2}(\mathcal{N}+1)}{2},\quad\mathsf{n}\left({A^{(1)}_{ij}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)}{2},\quad\mathsf{n}\left({A^{(2)}_{ij}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)}{2},\quad\mathsf{n}\left({A^{(3)}_{ijkl}}\right)=\frac{\mathcal{N}^{3}(\mathcal{N}+1)}{2},

(7)

Equation (
5
) is written in a reduced parity-even basis, in which the only explicit curvature term is the coupling
FR
. Curvature-gradient operators constructed from the Ricci tensor, the ordinary Riemann tensor, or the double-dual Riemann tensor are not added as independent terms. For example, using the Ricci identity and integrating by parts, one obtains

\displaystyle C_{ij}R_{\mu\nu}\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle C_{ij,\phi^{k}}X^{jk}\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}-C_{ik,\phi^{j}}\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}+C_{ij}\left(L_{2}^{ij}-L_{1}^{ij}\right)

(8)

\displaystyle+2C_{ik,X^{jl}}\left(L_{3}^{ijlk}-L_{4}^{ijkl}\right),

where, here and in the following,
\simeq
denotes equality up to boundary terms and

\displaystyle C_{ij,\phi^{k}}=\frac{\partial C_{ij}}{\partial\phi^{k}},\qquad C_{ij,X^{kl}}=\frac{\partial C_{ij}}{\partial X^{kl}}.

(9)

Since
R_{\mu\nu}
is symmetric, only the symmetric part of
C_{ij}
contributes to the left-hand side of Eq. (
8
). Moreover, since
X^{ij}=X^{ji}
, we take
C_{ij,X^{kl}}=C_{ij,X^{lk}}
. The four terms on the right-hand side of Eq. (
8
) are already contained in the present basis. The first term has the trace-linear form
Q_{i}\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}{}^{\mu}
. The second term has the linear Hessian form
B_{ijk}\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}
, after symmetrizing its coefficient in the two first-derivative labels. The third term belongs to the quadratic blocks
L_{1}^{ij}
and
L_{2}^{ij}
, while the last term belongs to the quadratic blocks
L_{3}^{ijkl}
and
L_{4}^{ijkl}
. Thus the Ricci coupling does not have to be included independently.

Similarly, ordinary Riemann-gradient couplings of the form

\displaystyle D_{ijkl}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\phi^{i\alpha}\phi^{j\beta}\phi^{k\gamma}\phi^{l\delta},

(10)

are reducible, up to integration by parts and coefficient redefinitions, to structures already present in Eq. (
5
). In particular, their reduction gives terms belonging to the linear Hessian sector
B_{ijk}\phi^{i\mu}\phi^{j\nu}\phi^{k}{}_{\mu\nu}
and to the quadratic blocks
L_{3}^{ijkl}
,
L_{4}^{ijkl}
and
L_{5}^{ijklmn}
.

Parity-even contractions involving the double-dual Riemann tensor do not define an independent sector in four dimensions. With our convention

\displaystyle{}^{*}R^{*}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}\equiv\frac{1}{4}\epsilon_{\mu\nu}{}^{\alpha\beta}R_{\alpha\beta\gamma\delta}\epsilon^{\gamma\delta}{}_{\rho\sigma},

(11)

one has

\displaystyle{}^{*}\!R^{*}_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}=-R_{\mu\nu\rho\sigma}-Rg_{[\mu|\rho|}g_{\nu]\sigma}+2g_{[\mu|\rho|}R_{\nu]\sigma}-2g_{[\mu|\sigma|}R_{\nu]\rho}.

(12)

Therefore its ordinary Riemann part reduces to the
B_{ijk}
sector and to the blocks
L_{3}^{ijkl}
,
L_{4}^{ijkl}
and
L_{5}^{ijklmn}
, while its Ricci part reduces according to Eq. (
8
). Its Ricci scalar part is absorbed into the non-minimal coupling
FR
, since the remaining contractions of first derivatives give functions of
\phi^{i}
and
X^{ij}
. Parity-odd couplings involving a single dual Riemann tensor are not considered in the present parity-even action.

For the Hamiltonian analysis it is convenient to remove the higher derivatives of the scalar fields by introducing an auxiliary co-vector field
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
for each scalar, with the relation between
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
and
\phi^{i}
enforced by a Lagrange multiplier. In the auxiliary formulation we replace

\displaystyle\phi^{i}{}_{\mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu},\qquad\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}\equiv\nabla_{(\mu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\nu)},\qquad X^{ij}\rightarrow g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{j}{}_{\nu}.

(13)

The symmetrized derivative
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
reflects the symmetry of the original scalar Hessian
\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
. Off shell
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
is an independent field, so
\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\nu}
is not symmetric in general; on shell
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
is constrained to the gradient of
\phi^{i}
. The Lagrange multiplier contribution is

\displaystyle S_{\lambda}=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu}\left(\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i}-\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}\right).

(14)

Variation with respect to
\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu}
imposes

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}=\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i}.

(15)

On the constraint surface one then has

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=\nabla_{(\mu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\nu)}=\nabla_{\mu}\nabla_{\nu}\phi^{i}=\phi^{i}{}_{\mu\nu},

(16)

and the auxiliary expression for
X^{ij}
reduces to Eq. (
2
). Hence the first-order action obtained from Eq. (
5
) by the replacements in Eq. (
13
), together with
S_{\lambda}
, is equivalent to the original higher-order action, and provides the starting point for the ADM decomposition and Hamiltonian analysis of the following sections.

3
ADM decomposition of the action

We now rewrite the covariant action in a form suitable for the Hamiltonian analysis, using the standard ADM decomposition
8
. We foliate spacetime by spacelike hypersurfaces
\Sigma_{t}
labelled by a time function
t
and choose coordinates
x^{\mu}=(t,y^{a})
adapted to this foliation, where
y^{a}
are coordinates on
\Sigma_{t}
. Spatial indices on
\Sigma_{t}
are denoted by Latin letters
a,b,c,d,e,f
. In these adapted coordinates, the line element is written as

\displaystyle ds^{2}=-N^{2}dt^{2}+h_{ab}(dy^{a}+N^{a}dt)(dy^{b}+N^{b}dt),

(17)

where
N
is the lapse,
N^{a}
is the shift vector, and
h_{ab}
is the induced metric on
\Sigma_{t}
. The future-directed unit normal satisfies
n_{\mu}n^{\mu}=-1
and, in the adapted coordinates above, is given by

\displaystyle n_{\mu}=(-N,0,0,0),\qquad n^{\mu}=\frac{1}{N}(1,-N^{a}).

(18)

The time-flow vector is decomposed as

\displaystyle t^{\mu}=Nn^{\mu}+e^{\mu}{}_{a}N^{a},

(19)

where
e^{\mu}{}_{a}
are tangent basis vectors on
\Sigma_{t}
. They convert spatial tensors into spacetime tensors, while their duals project spacetime tensors onto the hypersurfaces. The induced metric is

\displaystyle h_{ab}=e^{\mu}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}g_{\mu\nu},

(20)

and its spacetime form is
h_{\mu\nu}=g_{\mu\nu}+n_{\mu}n_{\nu}
, or equivalently

\displaystyle g_{\mu\nu}=h_{\mu\nu}-n_{\mu}n_{\nu},\qquad h_{\mu}{}^{\nu}=\delta_{\mu}{}^{\nu}+n_{\mu}n^{\nu}.

(21)

Tensors fully projected by
e^{\mu}{}_{a}
are intrinsic to
\Sigma_{t}
. Their spatial covariant derivative is denoted by
D_{a}
and is compatible with the induced metric,
D_{a}h_{bc}=0
.

The derivative of the normal vector is decomposed as

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}n_{\nu}=K_{\mu\nu}-n_{\mu}a_{\nu},

(22)

where
K_{\mu\nu}
is the extrinsic curvature of
\Sigma_{t}
and is tangent to the hypersurfaces in both indices. Its spatial components are
K_{ab}=e^{\mu}{}_{a}e^{\nu}{}_{b}K_{\mu\nu}
, and
K=h^{ab}K_{ab}
. Contracting Eq. (
22
) with
n^{\mu}
gives the acceleration of the normal congruence,

\displaystyle a_{\mu}=n^{\nu}\nabla_{\nu}n_{\mu},\qquad a_{a}=e^{\mu}{}_{a}a_{\mu}=D_{a}\ln{N}.

(23)

The Ricci scalar is decomposed by the Gauss-Codazzi relation. In the above convention,

\displaystyle R={}^{(3)}\!R+K_{ab}K^{ab}-K^{2}+2\nabla_{\mu}\left(Kn^{\mu}-a^{\mu}\right),

(24)

where
{}^{(3)}\!R
is the Ricci scalar of
h_{ab}
. The last term in Eq. (
24
) contributes nontrivially because
F
depends on
\phi^{i}
and
X^{ij}
. After introducing the auxiliary field and using the auxiliary form of
X^{ij}
from Eq. (
13
), integration by parts gives

\displaystyle 2\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}F\nabla_{\mu}(Kn^{\mu}-a^{\mu})

\displaystyle\simeq

\displaystyle 2\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}(F_{\phi^{i}}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}+2F_{X^{ij}}\mathcal{A}^{i\nu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{j}{}_{\nu})(a^{\mu}-Kn^{\mu}),

where

\displaystyle F_{\phi^{i}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial\phi^{i}},\qquad F_{X^{ij}}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial X^{ij}}.

(26)

Since
X^{ij}
is symmetric by Eq. (
2
),
F_{X^{ij}}
is symmetric in
i,j
. We next decompose the scalar and auxiliary variables into their normal and tangential parts. For the scalar fields,

\displaystyle\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i}=e_{\mu}{}^{a}D_{a}\phi^{i}-n_{\mu}\phi_{*}^{i},\qquad\phi_{*}^{i}=n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i}.

(27)

For the auxiliary co-vector,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}=e_{\mu}{}^{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}-n_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i},\qquad\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}=e^{\mu}{}_{a}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu},\qquad\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}=n^{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}.

(28)

The spatial part of the auxiliary constraint, enforced by the corresponding component of the Lagrange multiplier
\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu}
, identifies
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
with
D_{a}\phi^{i}
. Since this constraint contains no velocities, it can be used before extracting the kinetic structure of the ADM action. With this understanding, no independent normal derivative of
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
appears, and the symmetrized derivative
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
decomposes as

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=n_{\mu}n_{\nu}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}-2n_{(\mu}e_{\nu)}{}^{a}(D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ib}K_{ab})+e_{\mu}{}^{a}e_{\nu}{}^{b}(S^{i}{}_{ab}-\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}K_{ab}),

(29)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}=n^{\mu}n^{\nu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu},\qquad S^{i}{}_{ab}=D_{(a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b)}.

(30)

Only the symmetric spatial derivative
S^{i}{}_{ab}
appears because the covariant action depends on
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}
, which is symmetric in its spacetime indices. Contracting Eq. (
29
) with
g^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle g^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu\nu}=-\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}+S^{i}-\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}K,

where

\displaystyle X^{ij}=\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ij}-\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j},\qquad\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ij}=\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ja}.

(32)

Finally, the Lagrange multiplier is decomposed as

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu}=e^{\mu}{}_{a}\hat{\lambda}_{i}{}^{a}-n^{\mu}\lambda_{*i},\qquad\hat{\lambda}_{i}{}^{a}=e_{\mu}{}^{a}\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu},\qquad\lambda_{*i}=n_{\mu}\lambda_{i}{}^{\mu}.

With this sign convention, the multiplier sector gives

contain the velocities of

,

\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}

Thus, up to a boundary term, the acceleration contribution can be traded for a term without explicit dependence on

4
Primary degeneracy conditions

4.1
Velocity Hessian and primary constraints

We now derive the primary degeneracy conditions. The relevant object is the Hessian of the Lagrangian density with respect to the variables that contain time derivatives. In the ADM action (
34
), these variables are
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
and
K_{ab}
. As discussed in section
3
, the spatial components
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
are fixed by their own constraint and carry no independent velocity. Therefore the velocity space relevant for the primary degeneracy analysis is spanned only by
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
and
K_{ab}
. Terms that are independent of these variables, or only linear in them, do not contribute to this Hessian. They can shift the canonical momenta and affect the explicit form of the constraints, the Hamiltonian and the secondary constraint analysis, but they do not affect the rank of the velocity Hessian. Therefore the primary degeneracy condition is determined by the part of the Lagrangian density quadratic in
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
and
K_{ab}
.

The quadratic velocity sector is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\rm{Kin}}=\sqrt{h}N\left(\mathcal{G}_{ij}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{j}+2\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}K_{ab}+\mathcal{K}^{abcd}K_{ab}K_{cd}\right).

(37)

We regard this expression as a quadratic form on the space of velocity variables

\displaystyle q^{A}=\left(\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i},K_{ab}\right),

(38)

where
K_{ab}=K_{ba}
. The composite index
A
labels the direct sum of the
\mathcal{N}
-dimensional field-space sector spanned by
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
and the six-dimensional space of symmetric spatial tensors spanned by
K_{ab}
. Hence the velocity space has dimension
\mathcal{N}+6
.

The primary degeneracy is then determined by the velocity Hessian on this space,

\displaystyle\mathbb{H}_{AB}=\frac{\partial^{2}\mathcal{L}_{\rm{Kin}}}{\partial q^{A}\partial q^{B}}=2\sqrt{h}N\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{G}_{ij}&\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{cd}\\
\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{ab}&\mathcal{K}^{abcd}\end{pmatrix}.

(39)

The overall factor
2\sqrt{h}N
does not affect the rank of
\mathbb{H}_{AB}
. The Hessian controls the invertibility of the Legendre map from the velocities
\left(\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i},K_{ab}\right)
to the corresponding canonical momenta. If
\mathbb{H}_{AB}
is invertible, the velocities can be solved in terms of the momenta and no primary constraint arises from the kinetic sector. If
\mathbb{H}_{AB}
has null directions, the Legendre map is singular and primary constraints appear.

Let

\displaystyle Z^{A}=\left(\xi^{i},\sigma_{ab}\right),\qquad\sigma_{ab}=\sigma_{ba},

(40)

be a null vector of the Hessian. The condition
\mathbb{H}_{AB}Z^{B}=0
gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ij}\xi^{j}+\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{cd}\sigma_{cd}=0,

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{ab}\xi^{j}+\mathcal{K}^{abcd}\sigma_{cd}=0.

(41)

On a regular region of phase space where the rank of
\mathbb{H}_{AB}
is constant, the number of primary constraints generated by the velocity degeneracy is

\displaystyle N_{\rm{Prim}}=\dim\ker\mathbb{H}=\mathcal{N}+6-\operatorname{rank}\mathbb{H}.

(42)

This is a primary-level statement. The existence of primary constraints is necessary for removing Ostrogradsky modes, but it is not by itself sufficient. One must also verify that the primary constraints are preserved under time evolution and that the required secondary constraints are generated.

4.2
Classification of primary degeneracy branches

The system (
4.1
) can be simplified whenever one of the diagonal blocks can be inverted, because one set of null-vector components can then be eliminated in terms of the other. The classification below records which block, if any, is available for this elimination.

The general degeneracy conditions (
4.1
) can be classified according to the invertibility properties of the two diagonal blocks
\mathcal{G}_{ij}
and
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
. In what follows,
\det\mathcal{G}
denotes the determinant of
\mathcal{G}_{ij}
in field space, while
\det\mathcal{K}
denotes the determinant of
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
as a linear map on the six-dimensional space of symmetric spatial tensors. Similarly, determinants of Schur complements are understood on the corresponding field-space or symmetric-tensor space. With this convention, the following three branches are exhaustive and non-overlapping.

4.2.1
Branch I: scalar-invertible branch with singular metric sector

The first branch is characterized by

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{K}=0,\qquad\det\mathcal{G}\neq 0.

(43)

In this case the metric kinetic block is singular, while the scalar block can be inverted. We denote the inverse of
\mathcal{G}_{ij}
by
\left(\mathcal{G}^{-1}\right)^{ij}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}_{ik}\left(\mathcal{G}^{-1}\right)^{kj}=\delta_{i}{}^{j}.

(44)

The first equation in (
4.1
) can then be solved for the scalar component of the null vector,

\displaystyle\xi^{i}=-\left(\mathcal{G}^{-1}\right)^{ij}\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}.

(45)

Substitution into the second equation in (
4.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{abcd}\sigma_{cd}=0,

(46)

where

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}^{abcd}=\mathcal{K}^{abcd}-\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\left(\mathcal{G}^{-1}\right)^{ij}\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{cd}.

(47)

Thus the primary degeneracy condition in this branch is

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{T}=0.

(48)

The null directions are parameterized by symmetric tensors
\sigma_{ab}\in\ker\mathcal{T}
, and the corresponding scalar components are fixed by Eq. (
45
).

This branch is a legitimate possibility at the level of the primary degeneracy conditions. However, since the metric kinetic block is already singular, the corresponding constraint structure can involve degeneracies intrinsic to the metric sector. Such theories require a separate analysis of the tensor dynamics and of the full Hamiltonian constraint algebra. We therefore do not pursue this branch in the present work.

4.2.2
Branch II: metric-invertible branch

The second branch is characterized by

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{K}\neq 0.

(49)

This condition does not impose any invertibility assumption on
\mathcal{G}_{ij}
. It only states that the metric kinetic block can be inverted as a map on symmetric spatial tensors. We denote its inverse by
\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}
, satisfying

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{abef}\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{efcd}=\delta^{a}{}_{(c}\delta^{b}{}_{d)}.

(50)

The second equation in (
4.1
) can then be solved for the metric component of the null vector,

\displaystyle\sigma_{ab}=-\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{cd}\xi^{i}.

(51)

Substituting this into the first equation in (
4.1
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}\xi^{j}=0,

(52)

where the effective scalar degeneracy matrix is

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\mathcal{G}_{ij}-\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{cd}.

(53)

The number of primary constraints in this branch is therefore

\displaystyle N_{\rm{Prim}}=\dim\ker\mathcal{S}.

(54)

Thus
\det\mathcal{S}=0
guarantees at least one primary constraint, while the number of independent primary constraints is determined by the dimension of the kernel of
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
.

This branch is the one selected for the rest of the analysis. Its defining advantage is that the metric kinetic block is non-degenerate, so the degeneracy is controlled by the effective scalar matrix
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
. The detailed implementation of this condition, including the distinction between partial and complete scalar-sector degeneracy, will be discussed in the next section.

4.2.3
Branch III: fully singular branch

The third branch is characterized by

\displaystyle\det\mathcal{K}=0,\qquad\det\mathcal{G}=0.

(55)

In this case neither of the diagonal blocks is invertible, and neither Schur complement is available in general. The degeneracy must therefore be analyzed directly from the coupled system (
4.1
). Within this branch, different types of null directions can occur.

A pure scalar null direction has the form

\displaystyle\xi^{i}\neq 0,\qquad\sigma_{ab}=0,\qquad\mathcal{G}_{ij}\xi^{j}=0,\qquad\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\xi^{i}=0.

(56)

Such a direction lies in the kernel of the scalar block and is also annihilated by the mixing block. It therefore produces a primary constraint without involving the metric velocity sector.

A pure metric null direction has the form

\displaystyle\xi^{i}=0,\qquad\sigma_{ab}\neq 0,\qquad\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\sigma_{ab}=0,\qquad\mathcal{K}^{abcd}\sigma_{cd}=0.

(57)

Such a direction lies in the kernel of the metric block and is also annihilated by the mixing block. It produces a primary constraint intrinsic to the metric velocity sector.

Finally, one may have mixed null directions,

\displaystyle\xi^{i}\neq 0,\qquad\sigma_{ab}\neq 0,

(58)

where the pair
\left(\xi^{i},\sigma_{ab}\right)
solves the full coupled system (
4.1
). In this case the scalar and metric components of the null vector cannot be disentangled by inverting one of the diagonal blocks. The three cases above are types of null directions within the fully singular branch; a given theory may contain more than one type.

This branch is the most general singular case, but also the least controlled one. Since the metric kinetic block is singular, the theory may contain additional primary constraints in the metric sector, modified tensor dynamics or strongly coupled directions. These possibilities are not excluded by the primary degeneracy analysis alone, but they require a separate Hamiltonian treatment. We therefore leave this branch outside the scope of the present work.

4.3
Choice of branch and scope of the analysis

The classification above separates the primary degeneracy problem into three distinct cases. Branch I and Branch III both begin with a singular metric kinetic block and require a separate Hamiltonian analysis. In the remainder of this work we focus on Branch II, where
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
is invertible and the degeneracy is encoded in the field-space matrix
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
.

For a single scalar field, this matrix reduces to a single coefficient. For
\mathcal{N}\geq 2
, however,
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
is an
\mathcal{N}\times\mathcal{N}
matrix, and its rank contains information that has no counterpart in the single-field case. In particular,
\det\mathcal{S}=0
only ensures that at least one combination of higher-derivative scalar modes is constrained. Removing all such modes requires enough independent primary constraints, and therefore requires a stronger condition on the rank of
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
. This point will be made precise in the next section.

5
Complete degeneracy in the metric-invertible branch

We now implement the Branch II degeneracy of section
4
, where
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
is invertible, Eq. (
49
), and the primary degeneracy is governed by the effective scalar matrix
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
of Eq. (
53
). The stronger condition anticipated there is complete degeneracy of the scalar sector,
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
. Because
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
contains the inverse of
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
, we represent that inverse through an auxiliary tensor and then reduce the condition to a set of conditions on the coefficient functions of the action.

5.1
Auxiliary tensor for the inverse metric block

The Schur complement in Branch II contains the inverse of
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
as a map on the space of symmetric spatial tensors. Instead of writing this inverse explicitly, we introduce, for each field-space index
i
, a symmetric spatial tensor
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
defined by

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{abcd}\mathcal{W}_{icd}=\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}.

(59)

Since
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
is invertible in this branch, this equation uniquely determines
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
as a tensor. Equivalently,

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{iab}=\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{cd}.

(60)

This auxiliary tensor is introduced only to represent the action of
\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}
without computing the inverse metric block explicitly.

Because
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}
are built only from
h_{ab}
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
, the map
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
preserves the span generated by
h_{ab}
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{k}{}_{b}
. Therefore
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
lies in the same span and admits, with no loss of generality, the expansion

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{iab}=\alpha_{i}h_{ab}+\beta_{ijk}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{k}{}_{b},\qquad\beta_{ijk}=\beta_{ikj}.

(61)

Here
\alpha_{i}
and
\beta_{ijk}
are coefficient functions, not new dynamical variables. For each fixed leading index
i
, the coefficient vector contains one scalar coefficient
\alpha_{i}
and
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)/2
symmetric-pair coefficients
\beta_{ijk}
. Thus the number of coefficient functions to be solved for each
i
is

\displaystyle 1+\frac{\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)}{2}.

(62)

If the generating set is overcomplete, which can occur for
\mathcal{N}\geq 3
or on special field configurations, the coefficient functions need not be unique, but the tensor
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
and the Schur complement constructed from it are unique.

5.2
Coefficient system

Acting with
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
on the decomposition (
61
) and expanding the result in the same basis gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}^{abcd}\mathcal{W}_{icd}=\left(\mathsf{A}\alpha_{i}+\mathsf{B}^{jk}\beta_{ijk}\right)h^{ab}+\left(\mathsf{C}_{jk}\alpha_{i}+\mathsf{D}_{jk}{}^{lm}\beta_{ilm}\right)\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ja}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{kb},

(63)

where the coefficients
\mathsf{A}
,
\mathsf{B}^{ij}
,
\mathsf{C}_{ij}
and
\mathsf{D}_{ij}{}^{kl}
are collected in appendix
B
.

Similarly, the mixing coefficient
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}
is decomposed in the same basis as

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}=\mathcal{Z}_{i}h^{ab}+\mathcal{Z}_{ijk}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ja}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{kb},\qquad\mathcal{Z}_{ijk}=\mathcal{Z}_{ikj}.

(64)

The explicit expressions for
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
and
\mathcal{Z}_{ijk}
are also given in appendix
B
.

Matching Eqs. (
63
) and (
64
), the defining equation (
59
) is equivalent to the coefficient system

\displaystyle\mathsf{A}\alpha_{i}+\mathsf{B}^{jk}\beta_{ijk}=\mathcal{Z}_{i},

\displaystyle\mathsf{C}_{jk}\alpha_{i}+\mathsf{D}_{jk}{}^{lm}\beta_{ilm}=\mathcal{Z}_{ijk}.

(65)

For each fixed leading index
i
, this is a linear system on the coefficient space spanned by the scalar slot and the symmetric pair
(jk)
. In matrix notation,

\displaystyle\mathbb{M}\begin{pmatrix}\alpha_{i}\\
\beta_{ilm}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathcal{Z}_{i}\\
\mathcal{Z}_{ijk}\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathbb{M}=\begin{pmatrix}\mathsf{A}&\mathsf{B}^{lm}\\
\mathsf{C}_{jk}&\mathsf{D}_{jk}{}^{lm}\end{pmatrix}.

(66)

The matrix
\mathbb{M}
acts on the coefficient space of dimension
1+\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)/2
, which may be larger than the dimension of the symmetric-tensor space represented by this basis. The leading index
i
labels the source vector on the right-hand side and is not an index of
\mathbb{M}
. Solving Eq. (
66
) determines the tensor
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
. If the expansion coefficients are not unique, they differ only by directions that leave
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
unchanged, and therefore give the same Schur complement
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
.

5.3
Effective scalar degeneracy matrix

Using
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
, the Schur complement can be written as

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\mathcal{G}_{ij}-\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\mathcal{W}_{jab}.

(67)

Although the right-hand side is not manifestly symmetric in
i,j
, it is symmetric. Indeed, using Eq. (
60
), the second term is

\displaystyle\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\mathcal{W}_{jab}=\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}\mathcal{B}_{j}{}^{cd},

(68)

which is symmetric under
i\leftrightarrow j
because
\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}
inherits the pair-exchange symmetry of
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
.

Substituting Eq. (
61
) into Eq. (
67
) gives

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\mathcal{G}_{ij}-\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\left(\alpha_{j}h_{ab}+\beta_{jkl}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{k}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{l}{}_{b}\right).

(69)

The required contractions
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}h_{ab}
and
\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{k}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{l}{}_{b}
, together with the expanded form of
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
, are given in appendix
B
.

5.4
Complete degeneracy condition

In Branch II, the number of primary constraints is
\dim\ker\mathcal{S}
, Eq. (
54
). Therefore the condition
\det\mathcal{S}=0
only guarantees at least one primary constraint. To remove all
\mathcal{N}
higher-derivative scalar directions at the primary level, one must impose complete degeneracy of the effective scalar matrix,

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0.

(70)

This is a system of
\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{N}+1)/2
independent conditions on the coefficient functions of the action, since
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=\mathcal{S}_{ji}
. When these conditions hold,
\mathcal{S}_{ij}
vanishes identically, its kernel is the full field space, and the velocity degeneracy produces
\mathcal{N}
independent primary constraints, before imposing their preservation in time. The secondary constraint analysis will be carried out in the following sections. For
\mathcal{N}=1
, the matrix condition (
70
) reduces to the single-field condition discussed in appendix
C
.

6
Hamiltonian analysis

We now perform the Hamiltonian analysis of the completely degenerate theory in the metric-invertible branch. The aim is to show how the primary degeneracy condition
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
leads to primary constraints, how the preservation of these constraints can generate the required secondary constraints, and how the resulting constraint structure removes the Ostrogradsky modes associated with the variables
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
. We use the standard terminology of constrained Hamiltonian systems
6
;
9
. In particular, weak equality, denoted by
\approx
, means equality on the constraint surface; Poisson brackets must be computed before imposing weakly vanishing constraints.

6.1
Canonical momenta

We start from the ADM Lagrangian density obtained in section
3
. We keep
\mathcal{U}
unredefined, so that the contribution generated by integrating the acceleration term by parts appears explicitly as
-D_{a}\mathcal{E}^{a}
. Thus

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{h}N\Big[F{}^{(3)}\!R-D_{a}\mathcal{E}^{a}+\mathcal{U}+\mathcal{C}_{i}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}+\mathcal{M}^{ab}K_{ab}+\mathcal{G}_{ij}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{j}+2\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}K_{ab}+\mathcal{K}^{abcd}K_{ab}K_{cd}

(71)

\displaystyle+\lambda_{*i}(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}-\phi_{*}^{i})+\hat{\lambda}_{i}{}^{a}(D_{a}\phi^{i}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a})\Big].

The extrinsic curvature is related to the time derivative of the spatial metric by

\displaystyle K_{ab}=\frac{1}{2N}\left(\dot{h}_{ab}-D_{a}N_{b}-D_{b}N_{a}\right).

(72)

Therefore the momentum conjugate to
h_{ab}
is

\displaystyle\pi^{ab}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{h}_{ab}}=\sqrt{h}\left(\frac{1}{2}\mathcal{M}^{ab}+\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}+\mathcal{K}^{abcd}K_{cd}\right).

(73)

The velocity of
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
is contained in
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
. Since

\displaystyle n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{i}-N^{a}D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}\right),

(74)

and, using
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}=n^{\mu}\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
together with the convention (
22
) and
a_{a}=D_{a}\ln{N}
,

\displaystyle n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}=\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}+\frac{1}{N}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ia}D_{a}N,

(75)

we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{i}-N^{a}D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}-\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ia}D_{a}N\right).

(76)

The momentum conjugate to
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
is therefore

\displaystyle p_{*i}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{i}}=\sqrt{h}\left(\mathcal{C}_{i}+2\mathcal{G}_{ij}\mathcal{V}_{*}^{j}+2\mathcal{B}_{i}{}^{ab}K_{ab}\right).

(77)

For the scalar fields,

\displaystyle\phi_{*}^{i}=n^{\mu}\nabla_{\mu}\phi^{i}=\frac{1}{N}\left(\dot{\phi}^{i}-N^{a}D_{a}\phi^{i}\right).

(78)

With the multiplier convention used in Eq. (
71
), the momentum conjugate to
\phi^{i}
is

\displaystyle p_{i}=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\dot{\phi}^{i}}=-\sqrt{h}\lambda_{*i}.

(79)

The spatial auxiliary field
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
has no time derivative in the Lagrangian, so that
\partial\mathcal{L}/\partial\dot{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}^{i}{}_{a}=0
identically and its conjugate momentum is a primary constraint,

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0.

(80)

Similarly, the lapse and shift are configuration variables of the ADM decomposition, but their time derivatives do not appear in the Lagrangian. Their conjugate momenta are therefore primary constraints,

\displaystyle p_{N}\approx 0,\qquad p_{a}\approx 0.

(81)

The canonical pairs are

\displaystyle(h_{ab},\pi^{ab}),\qquad(\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i},p_{*i}),\qquad(\phi^{i},p_{i}),\qquad(\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a},\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}),\qquad(N,p_{N}),\qquad(N^{a},p_{a}).

(82)

These pairs fix the phase-space structure used in the constraint analysis that follows. The non-vanishing fundamental Poisson brackets are

\displaystyle\{h_{ab}(x),\pi^{cd}(y)\}=\delta^{c}{}_{(a}\delta^{d}{}_{b)}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}(x),p_{*j}(y)\}=\delta^{i}{}_{j}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

\displaystyle\{\phi^{i}(x),p_{j}(y)\}=\delta^{i}{}_{j}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

\displaystyle\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}(x),\hat{p}_{j}{}^{b}(y)\}=\delta^{i}{}_{j}\delta_{a}{}^{b}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

(83)

together with those of the lapse and shift, listed separately because their momenta are the primary constraints associated with the ADM gauge variables,

\displaystyle\{N(x),p_{N}(y)\}=\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

\displaystyle\{N^{a}(x),p_{b}(y)\}=\delta^{a}{}_{b}\delta^{(3)}(x-y).

(84)

6.2
Primary degeneracy constraints

We now impose the complete degeneracy condition
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
of section
5
at the level of the canonical momenta, where
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
is the auxiliary tensor defined in Eq. (
59
). From Eqs. (
73
) and (
77
), consider the combination
p_{*i}-2\mathcal{W}_{iab}\pi^{ab}
. The terms proportional to
K_{ab}
cancel by Eq. (
59
), and the terms proportional to
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
cancel by the degeneracy condition
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
, Eq. (
70
). Therefore

\displaystyle p_{*i}-2\mathcal{W}_{iab}\pi^{ab}=\sqrt{h}\left(\mathcal{C}_{i}-\mathcal{W}_{iab}\mathcal{M}^{ab}\right).

(85)

Defining

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=\mathcal{W}_{iab}\mathcal{M}^{ab}-\mathcal{C}_{i},

(86)

we obtain the primary degeneracy constraints

\displaystyle\Psi_{i}=p_{*i}-2\mathcal{W}_{iab}\pi^{ab}+\sqrt{h}\Delta_{i}\approx 0.

(87)

These
\mathcal{N}
primary constraints are the primary-level consequence of complete degeneracy. They remove the possibility of solving all scalar velocities
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
in terms of canonical momenta. By themselves, however, they do not yet guarantee that the Ostrogradsky modes are removed; this is decided by their preservation in time, which we analyze in the remainder of this section.

For the constraint analysis it is useful to separate
\Delta_{i}
into a part free of spatial derivatives and the parts carrying spatial derivatives of the scalar and auxiliary fields,

\displaystyle\Delta_{i}=\Lambda_{i}+\mathcal{J}_{ij}{}^{a}D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}+\mathcal{I}_{ij}{}^{ab}S^{j}{}_{ab}.

(88)

Here
\Lambda_{i}
is the part of
\Delta_{i}
that contains no
D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}
or
S^{j}{}_{ab}
,
\mathcal{J}_{ij}{}^{a}
is the coefficient of
D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}
, and
\mathcal{I}_{ij}{}^{ab}
is the coefficient of
S^{j}{}_{ab}
, symmetric in its spatial indices. The explicit expressions are collected in appendix
D
.

6.3
Canonical and total Hamiltonians

The canonical Hamiltonian is obtained from the Legendre transform

\displaystyle H_{\rm{can}}=\int d^{3}x\left(\dot{h}_{ab}\pi^{ab}+\dot{\mathcal{A}}_{*}^{i}p_{*i}+\dot{\phi}^{i}p_{i}+\dot{\hat{\mathcal{A}}}^{i}{}_{a}\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}-\mathcal{L}\right).

(89)

Using the velocity relations (
72
), (
76
) and (
78
) together with the momentum definitions (
79
) and (
80
), and integrating by parts the terms containing derivatives of
N
and
N^{a}
, the canonical Hamiltonian can be written as

\displaystyle H_{\rm{can}}=\int d^{3}x\left(N\mathcal{H}_{0}+N^{a}\mathcal{H}_{a}+\ell_{i}{}^{a}\chi^{i}{}_{a}\right),

(90)

where

\displaystyle\chi^{i}{}_{a}=\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}-D_{a}\phi^{i},

(91)

and

\displaystyle\ell_{i}{}^{a}=\sqrt{h}N\hat{\lambda}_{i}{}^{a}-p_{i}N^{a}.

(92)

The Hamiltonian density is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p_{i}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{h}}\left(\pi^{ab}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{M}^{ab}\right)\left(\mathcal{K}^{-1}\right)_{abcd}\left(\pi^{cd}-\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{h}\mathcal{M}^{cd}\right)

(93)

\displaystyle-\sqrt{h}\left(F{}^{(3)}\!R+\mathcal{U}-D_{a}\mathcal{E}^{a}\right)-D_{a}\left(p_{*i}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ia}\right),

and the momentum density is

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{a}=p_{i}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}+p_{*i}D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}-2D_{b}\pi_{a}{}^{b}.

(94)

The expression (
94
) is the form relevant on the auxiliary constraint surface
\chi^{i}{}_{a}\approx 0
and
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0
. The full spatial-diffeomorphism generator differs from Eq. (
94
) by terms proportional to
\chi^{i}{}_{a}
and
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}
, which vanish on the auxiliary constraint surface and do not affect the degeneracy analysis.

The term
\ell_{i}{}^{a}\chi^{i}{}_{a}
in Eq. (
90
) shows that
\chi^{i}{}_{a}\approx 0
is an auxiliary primary constraint. In the total Hamiltonian, every primary constraint is included with an arbitrary multiplier. The specific coefficient
\ell_{i}{}^{a}
is therefore absorbed into a new arbitrary multiplier. The total Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{\rm{T}}=\int d^{3}x\Big(N\mathcal{H}_{0}+N^{a}\mathcal{H}_{a}+u^{i}\Psi_{i}+v_{N}p_{N}+v^{a}p_{a}+\mu^{i}{}_{a}\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}+\nu_{i}{}^{a}\chi^{i}{}_{a}\Big).

(95)

Here
u^{i}
,
v_{N}
,
v^{a}
,
\mu^{i}{}_{a}
and
\nu_{i}{}^{a}
are Lagrange multipliers enforcing the primary constraints
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
,
p_{N}\approx 0
,
p_{a}\approx 0
,
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0
and
\chi^{i}{}_{a}\approx 0
, respectively.

6.4
Auxiliary constraints and reduced phase space

Preservation of the lapse and shift momenta gives

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{N}=\{p_{N},H_{\rm{T}}\}=-\frac{\delta H_{\rm{T}}}{\delta N}=-\mathcal{H}_{0}\approx 0,

(96)

and

\displaystyle\dot{p}_{a}=\{p_{a},H_{\rm{T}}\}=-\frac{\delta H_{\rm{T}}}{\delta N^{a}}=-\mathcal{H}_{a}\approx 0.

(97)

Thus we obtain the secondary constraints

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{0}\approx 0,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{a}\approx 0.

(98)

They are the Hamiltonian and momentum constraints associated with spacetime diffeomorphism invariance.

We next consider the auxiliary constraints

\displaystyle\chi^{i}{}_{a}\approx 0,\qquad\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0.

(99)

Their non-vanishing Poisson bracket is

\displaystyle\{\chi^{i}{}_{a}(x),\hat{p}_{j}{}^{b}(y)\}=\delta^{i}{}_{j}\delta_{a}{}^{b}\delta^{(3)}(x-y),

(100)

so this pair is second class. This means that the matrix of their mutual Poisson brackets is invertible, and their preservation fixes the associated multipliers rather than generating new independent constraints.

Indeed,

\displaystyle\dot{\chi}^{i}{}_{a}=\{\chi^{i}{}_{a},H_{\rm{T}}\}=\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a},H_{\rm{T}}\}-D_{a}\{\phi^{i},H_{\rm{T}}\}.

(101)

Using

\displaystyle\{\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a},H_{\rm{T}}\}=\mu^{i}{}_{a},

(102)

and

\displaystyle\{\phi^{i},H_{\rm{T}}\}=N\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}+N^{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a},

(103)

we find

\displaystyle\dot{\chi}^{i}{}_{a}=\mu^{i}{}_{a}-D_{a}\left(N\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}+N^{b}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b}\right)\approx 0.

(104)

Therefore

\displaystyle\mu^{i}{}_{a}\approx D_{a}\left(N\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}+N^{b}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b}\right).

(105)

Similarly, preservation of
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0
gives

\displaystyle\dot{\hat{p}}_{i}{}^{a}=\{\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a},H_{\rm{T}}\}=-\frac{\delta H_{\rm{T}}}{\delta\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}}\approx 0.

(106)

Since
H_{\rm{T}}
depends on
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
both algebraically and through
S^{i}{}_{ab}=D_{(a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b)}
, this is a functional derivative. Isolating the multiplier
\nu_{i}{}^{a}
gives

\displaystyle\nu_{i}{}^{a}\approx-\frac{\delta H_{\rm{can}}}{\delta\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}}-\frac{\delta}{\delta\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}}\int d^{3}xu^{j}\Psi_{j}.

(107)

Thus preservation of
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0
fixes
\nu_{i}{}^{a}
and does not produce a new constraint.

Since the auxiliary pair is second class, it may be eliminated by the corresponding Dirac bracket. Equivalently, after this reduction one can impose
\chi^{i}{}_{a}=0
and
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}=0
strongly. We denote the resulting bracket by
\{\ ,\ \}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}
and the reduced canonical Hamiltonian by

\displaystyle H_{\rm{red}}=\int d^{3}x\left(N\mathcal{H}_{0}+N^{a}\mathcal{H}_{a}\right),

(108)

with the understanding that the auxiliary constraints have been imposed. The reduced total Hamiltonian is

\displaystyle H_{\rm{T}}^{\rm{red}}=H_{\rm{red}}+\int d^{3}x\left(u^{i}\Psi_{i}+v_{N}p_{N}+v^{a}p_{a}\right).

(109)

6.5
Preservation of the primary degeneracy constraints

We now impose preservation of the primary degeneracy constraints
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
. Since
\Psi_{i}
contains
D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
and
S^{i}{}_{ab}=D_{(a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b)}
through
\Delta_{i}
, the local bracket
\{\Psi_{i}(x),\Psi_{j}(y)\}
contains derivatives of the delta distribution. It is therefore more transparent to work with smeared constraints,

\displaystyle\Psi[f]=\int d^{3}xf^{i}\Psi_{i},

(110)

where
f^{i}
is an arbitrary test function. The preservation condition is

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}[f]=\{\Psi[f],H_{\rm{T}}^{\rm{red}}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0.

(111)

By construction,
\Psi_{i}
depends only on the canonical variables and has no explicit dependence on
N
or
N^{a}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\{\Psi[f],p_{N}[v_{N}]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}=0,\qquad\{\Psi[f],p_{a}[v^{a}]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}=0,

(112)

where

\displaystyle p_{N}[v_{N}]=\int d^{3}xv_{N}p_{N},\qquad p_{a}[v^{a}]=\int d^{3}xv^{a}p_{a}.

(113)

Thus

\displaystyle\dot{\Psi}[f]=\{\Psi[f],H_{\rm{red}}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}+\{\Psi[f],\Psi[u]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0.

(114)

The second term is the possible obstruction to obtaining secondary constraints. If it contains the arbitrary multiplier
u^{i}
, or derivatives of
u^{i}
, with non-vanishing coefficients, then Eq. (
114
) fixes
u^{i}
instead of producing a new constraint. Therefore, to generate secondary constraints associated with
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
, the primary-primary bracket must vanish weakly as a differential operator acting on the smearing functions.

To analyze this operator, we compute
\{\Psi[f],\Psi[g]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}
for two arbitrary test functions
f^{i}
and
g^{i}
. The preservation equation is recovered by setting
g^{i}=u^{i}
. With this convention, derivative terms in
g^{i}
become derivative terms in the multiplier
u^{i}
. Computing these brackets and integrating by parts until at most one derivative acts on the smearing functions, the bracket can be organized as

\displaystyle\{\Psi[f],\Psi[g]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}=\int d^{3}xf^{i}g^{j}\mathfrak{C}_{ij}+\int d^{3}x\left(f^{i}D_{a}g^{j}-g^{i}D_{a}f^{j}\right)\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}.

(115)

The coefficient
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
multiplies the derivative-smearing structure and would therefore multiply
D_{a}u^{j}
in
\{\Psi[f],\Psi[u]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}
. The coefficient
\mathfrak{C}_{ij}
multiplies the non-derivative smearing structure. We decompose it as

\displaystyle\mathfrak{C}_{ij}=\mathfrak{M}_{ij}+\mathfrak{N}_{ij},

(116)

where
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}
is the part proportional to the canonical momentum
\pi^{ab}
, while
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
is independent of
\pi^{ab}
. Because the Poisson bracket changes sign under exchange of the two smearing functions, the non-derivative coefficient is antisymmetric in field space,
\mathfrak{C}_{ij}=-\mathfrak{C}_{ji}
, and hence so are
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}
and
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
. Both therefore vanish identically for a single scalar field: they are intrinsically multi-field conditions with no single-field analogue.

Before writing the explicit expressions, we define the algebraic derivative used below. The subscript
{\rm{alg}}
denotes the derivative with respect to the independent spatial fields
h_{ab}
,
\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
and
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
, acting on all explicit occurrences, including the dependence of the kinetic matrix on these fields through its ADM form (
32
), in which
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{ij}=h^{ab}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{j}{}_{b}
carries the dependence on
h_{ab}
. It does not act on the explicit spatial derivatives
D_{a}\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}
and
S^{i}{}_{ab}
. These are ordinary partial derivatives of the coefficient functions, not functional derivatives.

The constraint
\Psi_{i}
depends on
\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{a}
not only algebraically but also through
S^{i}{}_{ab}=D_{(a}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{i}{}_{b)}
, which enters
\Delta_{i}
with coefficient
\mathcal{I}_{ij}{}^{ab}
in Eq. (
88
). In the primary-primary bracket this dependence is carried by the variation of the spatial connection inside
S^{i}{}_{ab}
, which we collect in the tensor

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}_{i}{}^{abc}=-\frac{1}{2}\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{jc}\mathcal{I}_{ij}{}^{ab}+\hat{\mathcal{A}}^{j(a}\mathcal{I}_{ij}{}^{b)c}.

(117)

This tensor enters the derivative-smearing coefficient
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
and, through
D_{c}\mathcal{R}_{j}{}^{abc}
, the momentum-independent coefficient
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
. In terms of it, the derivative-smearing coefficient is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}=\sqrt{h}\left(\mathcal{J}_{ji}{}^{a}+2\mathcal{W}_{ibc}\mathcal{R}_{j}{}^{bca}\right).

(118)

Collecting the terms linear in
\pi^{ab}
, the momentum-dependent coefficient is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\bigg[2\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{jab}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{iab}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}+4\left(\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{iab}}{\partial h_{cd}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\mathcal{W}_{jcd}-\mathcal{W}_{icd}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{jab}}{\partial h_{cd}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\right)\bigg]\pi^{ab}.

Collecting the terms independent of
\pi^{ab}
, the momentum-independent coefficient is

\displaystyle\mathfrak{N}_{ij}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sqrt{h}\bigg[\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{i}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}}-\frac{\partial\Delta_{j}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}-2\left(\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{i}}{\partial h_{ab}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\mathcal{W}_{jab}-\mathcal{W}_{iab}\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{j}}{\partial h_{ab}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\right)

(120)

\displaystyle+2\mathcal{W}_{[i|a|}{}^{a}\Delta_{j]}-2D_{a}\mathcal{J}_{[ij]}{}^{a}+4\mathcal{W}_{[i|ab|}D_{c}\mathcal{R}_{j]}{}^{abc}\bigg].

Here brackets on field-space indices denote weighted antisymmetrization, for example

\displaystyle T_{[ij]}=\frac{1}{2}\left(T_{ij}-T_{ji}\right).

(121)

With the organization in Eq. (
115
), the terms associated with derivatives of the smearing functions are kept in
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
and are not moved into
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
by further integration by parts. Equivalently, the split between
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
and
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
is partly a convention for organizing the smeared bracket, while the full differential-operator condition is invariant. Appendix

describes the assembly of this bracket and the origin of the individual terms in

,
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}
, and
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}

The necessary Dirac requirement is weak vanishing of the primary-primary bracket on the already imposed constraint surface. Equivalently, all coefficients in Eq. (

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}\approx 0,\qquad\mathfrak{M}_{ij}\approx 0,\qquad\mathfrak{N}_{ij}\approx 0.

(122)

Weak vanishing means that these quantities may vanish only after using the constraints already obtained. For a theory-level classification, however, it is useful to impose a stronger sufficient requirement: the same coefficients are set to zero as identities on phase space,

\displaystyle\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}=0,\qquad\mathfrak{M}_{ij}=0,\qquad\mathfrak{N}_{ij}=0.

(123)

These strong conditions are not logically necessary in the most general Dirac analysis, but they guarantee that the multiplier
u^{i}
is not fixed by the preservation of
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
.

Since
\sqrt{h}\neq 0
in the non-degenerate ADM branch, the strong version of
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}=0
is equivalently the coefficient condition

\displaystyle\mathcal{J}_{ji}{}^{a}+2\mathcal{W}_{ibc}\mathcal{R}_{j}{}^{bca}=0.

(124)

Since
\pi^{ab}
is an independent canonical momentum, the strong version of
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}=0
is equivalently the coefficient condition

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{jab}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}}-\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{iab}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}+2\left(\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{iab}}{\partial h_{cd}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\mathcal{W}_{jcd}-\mathcal{W}_{icd}\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{W}_{jab}}{\partial h_{cd}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\right)=0.

(125)

Similarly, the strong version of
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}=0
is

\displaystyle\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{i}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{j}}-\frac{\partial\Delta_{j}}{\partial\mathcal{A}_{*}^{i}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}-2\left(\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{i}}{\partial h_{ab}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\mathcal{W}_{jab}-\mathcal{W}_{iab}\left(\frac{\partial\Delta_{j}}{\partial h_{ab}}\right)_{\rm{alg}}\right)

\displaystyle+2\mathcal{W}_{[i|a|}{}^{a}\Delta_{j]}-2D_{a}\mathcal{J}_{[ij]}{}^{a}+4\mathcal{W}_{[i|ab|}D_{c}\mathcal{R}_{j]}{}^{abc}=0.

(126)

When the weak conditions (
122

\displaystyle\{\Psi[f],\Psi[u]\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0,

(127)

for arbitrary

and arbitrary multipliers
u^{i}
. The preservation equation (
114

\displaystyle\Omega[f]\equiv\{\Psi[f],H_{\rm{red}}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0.

(128)

Equivalently, in local notation,

\displaystyle\Omega_{i}\equiv\{\Psi_{i},H_{\rm{red}}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0.

(129)

These are the secondary constraints associated with the primary degeneracy constraints
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
.

6.6
Secondary constraints and rank condition

The final step in the scalar degeneracy sector is to preserve the secondary constraints. Introducing the smeared constraint

\displaystyle\Omega[\eta]=\int d^{3}x\eta^{i}\Omega_{i},

(130)

we require

\displaystyle\dot{\Omega}[\eta]=\{\Omega[\eta],H_{\rm{T}}^{\rm{red}}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\approx 0.

(131)

At this stage the reduced total Hamiltonian contains the multiplier term
\Psi[u]
, so Eq. (
131
) involves the bracket between
\Omega_{i}
and
\Psi_{j}
acting on the multipliers
u^{j}
. If this primary-secondary bracket is non-degenerate in the scalar sector, preservation of
\Omega_{i}\approx 0
fixes the multipliers
u^{i}
rather than generating further constraints.

Since the constraints contain spatial derivatives, the bracket
\{\Omega_{i},\Psi_{j}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}
should be understood as a matrix of differential operators acting on the smearing functions or on the multipliers
u^{j}
. The required condition is that this operator be invertible in the scalar sector, up to possible boundary zero modes or genuine gauge modes. Symbolically, we write

\displaystyle\operatorname{rank}\left(\{\Omega_{i},\Psi_{j}\}_{D,{\rm{aux}}}\right)=\mathcal{N}.

(132)

Under this rank condition, the equations
\dot{\Omega}_{i}\approx 0
determine the
\mathcal{N}
multipliers
u^{i}
and no further scalar constraints are generated. Thus the pairs
\left(\Psi_{i},\Omega_{i}\right)
are second class in the scalar degeneracy sector.

6.7
Counting degrees of freedom

We now count the physical degrees of freedom. We assume that the metric block
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
is invertible, complete primary degeneracy
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
holds, the primary-primary bracket conditions (
122
) hold, the rank condition (
132
) is satisfied, and the scalar second-class pair
(\Psi_{i},\Omega_{i})
leaves the diffeomorphism constraints first class.

Before eliminating the auxiliary pair, the canonical pairs are those of Eq. (
82
), with total phase-space dimension

\displaystyle 12+2\mathcal{N}+2\mathcal{N}+6\mathcal{N}+2+6=20+10\mathcal{N}.

(133)

The auxiliary constraints
\chi^{i}{}_{a}\approx 0
and
\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a}\approx 0
are second class and remove
6\mathcal{N}
phase-space dimensions. By the assumption above on the diffeomorphism sector,
p_{N}
,
\mathcal{H}_{0}
,
p_{a}
and
\mathcal{H}_{a}
remain first class on the reduced phase space, so the ADM constraints

\displaystyle p_{N}\approx 0,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{0}\approx 0,\qquad p_{a}\approx 0,\qquad\mathcal{H}_{a}\approx 0

(134)

are first class and remove
16
phase-space dimensions. Finally, the
\mathcal{N}
primary constraints
\Psi_{i}\approx 0
and the
\mathcal{N}
secondary constraints
\Omega_{i}\approx 0
form
\mathcal{N}
second-class pairs in the scalar degeneracy sector, and remove
2\mathcal{N}
phase-space dimensions. Therefore the final physical phase-space dimension is

\displaystyle 20+10\mathcal{N}-6\mathcal{N}-16-2\mathcal{N}=4+2\mathcal{N}.

(135)

The number of physical degrees of freedom is half of this number:

\displaystyle N_{\rm{dof}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(4+2\mathcal{N}\right)=2+\mathcal{N}.

(136)

Thus the theory propagates the two tensor degrees of freedom of the metric and one scalar degree of freedom for each of the
\mathcal{N}
scalar fields. The additional Ostrogradsky modes associated with the higher derivatives are removed by the second-class pairs
\left(\Psi_{i},\Omega_{i}\right)
.

7
Consistency checks and special subclasses

In this section we apply the degeneracy and consistency conditions of section
6
to several special cases. The purpose is twofold. First, we check that the general construction reduces correctly in the single-field limit. Second, we examine two multi-field subclasses: a Horndeski-type quadratic subclass, for which most conditions simplify but a nontrivial closure condition remains, and a constructive ADM-level subclass for which the full set of strong conditions can be satisfied explicitly.

Single-field case

8
Discussion and conclusion

In this work we developed a Hamiltonian framework that yields sufficient conditions for removing the Ostrogradsky modes of multi-field higher-order scalar-tensor theories. We considered
\mathcal{N}
scalar fields with coefficient functions of
\phi^{i}
and the kinetic matrix
X^{ij}
, and with quadratic dependence on the second derivatives of the fields. Introducing auxiliary co-vectors
\mathcal{A}^{i}{}_{\mu}
and performing an ADM decomposition isolated the velocity sector built from the scalar velocities
\mathcal{V}_{*}^{i}
and the metric velocity
K_{ab}
, and let us formulate the degeneracy problem directly in terms of the ADM coefficient blocks.

In the metric-invertible branch, where
\mathcal{K}^{abcd}
is invertible, the scalar-metric kinetic mixing is carried by the auxiliary tensor
\mathcal{W}_{iab}
, and complete primary degeneracy is the vanishing of the Schur complement,
\mathcal{S}_{ij}=0
, Eq. (
70
). When this condition holds, the Legendre map is degenerate and one obtains
\mathcal{N}
primary constraints
\Psi_{i}
, Eq. (
87
), the multi-field generalization of the primary constraint that removes the higher-derivative scalar mode in the single-field case.

The qualitative difference from the single-field case appears at the next step. For
\mathcal{N}\geq 2
, preservation of the primary constraints produces new conditions on the coefficient functions. After eliminating the auxiliary second-class pair
(\chi^{i}{}_{a},\hat{p}_{i}{}^{a})
, the smeared primary-primary bracket separates into a derivative-smearing coefficient
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
and the non-derivative coefficients
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}
and
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
; the latter two are antisymmetric in the field-space indices and therefore have no single-field analogue. For the primary constraints to generate secondary constraints, rather than to fix their own multipliers, these must vanish on the constraint surface, Eq. (
122
). This is the central structural point of the multi-field analysis: primary degeneracy is necessary, but for a genuine multi-field theory it is not by itself sufficient for full constraint consistency.

Once the conditions (
122
) hold, preservation of
\Psi_{i}
gives
\mathcal{N}
secondary constraints
\Omega_{i}
. If the primary-secondary bracket has maximal rank, Eq. (
132
), the pairs
(\Psi_{i},\Omega_{i})
are second class and remove the unwanted scalar phase-space directions. Under the assumptions of subsection
6.7
, the number of propagating degrees of freedom is
N_{\rm{dof}}=2+\mathcal{N}
, Eq. (
136
), the two tensor modes of gravity together with one scalar mode per field, and no additional Ostrogradsky mode.

We examined the construction in several special cases. In the single-field limit, the antisymmetric coefficients
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}
and
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}
vanish identically, while the derivative-smearing coefficient
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}
vanishes once the degeneracy condition is imposed. Thus, in the single-field case, the primary-primary bracket imposes no additional condition beyond primary degeneracy, and the degeneracy condition reduces to the expected polynomial form in the normal component
\mathcal{A}_{*}
. In the multi-field quadratic Horndeski-type subclass of subsection
7.2
, primary degeneracy is automatic in the metric-invertible branch, the conditions
\mathfrak{D}_{ij}{}^{a}=0
and
\mathfrak{M}_{ij}=0
hold identically, whereas
\mathfrak{N}_{ij}=0
imposes a nontrivial closure condition on the coefficient functions. This shows directly why the multi-field problem is more restrictive than a term-by-term extension of the single-field case.

We also constructed a degenerate multi-field subclass with nonvanishing scalar-metric kinetic mixing,
\mathcal{W}_{iab}\neq 0
. It satisfies the strong form of the primary-primary conditions, Eq. (
123
), and shows that the Hamiltonian conditions derived here define a nonempty class beyond the single-field and Horndeski-type limits. This subclass should be read as a proof that the conditions are nonempty, not as a covariant model-building prescription: it is formulated at the level of the ADM coefficient blocks, and reconstructing covariant actions that realize such blocks is a separate problem requiring its own analysis.

Several directions remain open. The present analysis is restricted to the metric-invertible branch; branches in which the metric block is itself degenerate may give further classes of theories and call for a separate constraint analysis. The covariant realization of the ADM-level subclasses identified here is a natural next step, after which their perturbative dynamics and cosmological applications can be investigated. Finally, the rank condition should be verified in explicit covariant representatives, since accidental degeneracies may produce additional constraints or further gauge symmetries. These results give a set of sufficient constraint-algebra conditions for multi-field higher-order scalar-tensor theories in which the Ostrogradsky modes are removed, and provide a starting point for building healthy multi-field theories beyond direct single-field generalizations.
