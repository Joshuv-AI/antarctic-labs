Thermoacoustic wave propagation in hydrodynamic poroelastic media with temperature-dependent thermal conductivity
Authors: Ahmed M Alshehri, Khaled Lotfy
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis work presents a generalized theoretical model of thermoacoustic wave propagation in saturated poroelastic media that incorporates hydrodynamic interactions and variable thermal conductivity. The model integrates generalized thermoelasticity and Biot’s poroelastic theory with a temperature-dependent heat conduction law to describe coupled thermal, mechanical, and acoustic processes in a fluid-saturated porous medium. The governing system includes equations of motion, Darcy-type fluid flow, and a generalized heat conduction equation in which the thermal conductivity varies nonlinearly with temperature. The normal mode analysis technique is applied to obtain analytical expressions for the field variables in one-dimensional form. Numerical simulations illustrate the influence of the thermal conductivity variation parameter, porosity, and hydrodynamic coupling on the thermoacoustic wave behavior. The results reveal that variable conductivity significantly modifies the amplitude, phase velocity, and attenuation of thermal and acoustic waves, particularly under transient heating. The proposed framework offers enhanced theoretical insight into the sensitivity of thermoelastic, acoustic, and pore-pressure waves to temperature-dependent conductivity, providing a foundation for future calibration against experimental observations in porous semiconductors, geophysical rocks, and engineered porous compositesKeywords: Thermoacoustic waves, Variable thermal conductivity, Poroelastic media, Hydrodynamic interaction, Laser-induced heatingSubject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Mathematics and computing, Physics

Introduction

The study of thermoacoustic wave propagation in porous and hydrodynamic media lies at the intersection of several advanced scientific disciplines and engineering applications. It combines the principles of thermoelasticity, acoustics, porous media mechanics, and heat transfer, core subjects in modern physics, mechanical and materials engineering. Research in this area addresses complex coupling between temperature, mechanical deformation, and fluid motion, phenomena that are fundamental to understanding energy conversion, signal transmission, and heat management in advanced materials. The overlap of these fields has gained substantial attention in recent years due to its direct relevance to semiconductor technology, biomedical imaging, geophysical exploration, energy harvesting, and thermal protection systems. In modern industries, controlling thermal and acoustic wave interactions in porous and functionally graded materials enables the development of high-efficiency sensors, microelectronic cooling systems, acoustic insulation layers, and nondestructive testing techniques. Therefore, investigating thermoacoustic behavior in poroelastic materials with hydrodynamic effects and variable thermal conductivity advances theoretical physics and provides a scientific foundation for smart material design, nanostructured composites, and next-generation energy devices.

The theory of thermoelasticity, which describes the coupling between mechanical deformation and temperature variations in solids, has undergone continuous refinement over the past decades. Classical thermoelasticity, governed by Fourier’s law (CD), is founded on Fourier’s law and assumes an infinite heat propagation speed, which is unrealistic in high-frequency or microscale applications1–3. To overcome this limitation, generalized thermoelasticity theories, such as those proposed by Lord and Shulman (LS) and Green and Lindsay (GL), introduced relaxation times into the heat conduction equation, ensuring finite thermal wave speeds and more realistic transient responses4,5. Later developments, including Tzou’s unified field approach6 and Youssef’s two-temperature model7, extended the applicability of thermoelasticity from macroscopic to microscopic regimes. The concept of variable thermal conductivity emerged from the need to describe realistic heat conduction in materials where the thermal properties change with temperature, microstructure, or position. Classical thermoelasticity models, based on Fourier’s law, assumed constant conductivity, which proved insufficient for systems exposed to high thermal gradients or rapid heating. Early modifications to Fourier’s model introduced temperature-dependent thermal conductivity to account for nonlinear heat transfer in metals, ceramics, and semiconductors subjected to laser or mechanical excitation8. Numerous analytical and numerical studies9–15 have highlighted the flexibility of generalized thermoelastic frameworks in addressing problems involving microstructure, variable thermal conductivity, rotation, fractional-order, and magnetic fields. These theoretical foundations form the basis for current research on non-Fourier heat conduction in complex and porous materials, particularly when variable thermal conductivity or fractional heat flux memory is introduced.

Porous materials, such as foamed metals, ceramics, and semiconductors, exhibit distinctive thermo-mechanical responses due to their heterogeneous structure of interconnected pores saturated with fluids. Incorporating hydrodynamic and poroelastic effects alongside variable thermal conductivity introduces an additional level of realism to thermoelastic modeling. In porous and fluid-saturated materials, the interaction between the solid matrix and the interstitial fluid generates coupled mechanical and thermal fields governed by Biot’s theory. When the thermal conductivity varies with temperature, this coupling becomes nonlinear, as heat transfer directly affects pore pressure, fluid motion, and stress distribution. Such interdependence is particularly important in semiconductors, rocks, and composite structures, where temperature gradients modify both the material conductivity and the fluid viscosity within the pores. Consequently, the combined influence of temperature-dependent heat conduction and hydrodynamic coupling provides a more accurate description of thermoacoustic and energy transport phenomena. This integrated framework forms the foundation for the present model, which extends generalized thermoelasticity to variable-conductivity hydro-poroelastic media subjected to thermal and acoustic excitations. Recent works have explored a variety of configurations, including optimization of porous materials for sound barriers16, thermal stress analysis in generalized thermoelastic solids with heat sources17, and two-temperature relaxation models in filtration and transport processes18. Fractional-order models19 and studies on wave reflection and refraction at thermoelastic–poroelastic interfaces20 have further expanded understanding of transient behaviors in porous structures. Schanz’s review21 and subsequent computational works22–24 provided the theoretical and numerical basis for modern poroelastodynamic modeling. These studies underline that thermal conductivity variations, caused by temperature gradients, microstructural changes, or local phase transitions, can play a decisive role in determining the propagation characteristics of thermoacoustic waves in porous and composite media.

Recent years have seen rapid progress in coupled thermo-poroelastic modeling and experimental investigations of acoustic behavior in porous media. Jin & Peterson25 developed a thermo-poroelastic model for partially saturated porous media, explicitly accounting for thermally induced variations in multiple fluid contents and advancing Biot-type constitutive relations for two-fluid systems; their results highlight the importance of multi-phase saturation and non-equilibrium fluid effects in thermally driven poroelastic responses. Das et al.26 investigated fractional and memory-dependent operators in wave reflection problems for pre-stressed microstructured solids and porous media, demonstrating that fractional derivatives can significantly modify reflection/attenuation characteristics compared with classical integer-order models, a motivation for our future fractional extensions. At the experimental and application levels, Boutin and Venegas27 examined acoustic propagation in wet porous media, including evaporation/condensation effects, and showed that phase change in the pore fluid introduces additional attenuation and dispersion mechanisms that are not captured in single-phase poroelastic models. Likewise, Ahmadihosseini et al.28 provided applied multiphysics simulations for geothermal reservoirs, demonstrating the practical significance of coupled thermo-poroelastic processes in field-scale deformation and heat-extraction scenarios.

Li et al.29 present a two-temperature thermoporoelastic model tailored to glycerin-saturated tight sandstones, in which the solid matrix and pore fluid are assigned distinct thermal fields to accurately capture thermal nonequilibrium and wave attenuation phenomena. Their study fits experimental data for wave propagation in porous rocks, demonstrating how differential heating between solid and fluid phases influences both attenuation and dispersion. While this dual-temperature formulation captures important thermal–mechanical coupling, our work significantly extends their framework: rather than assuming constant thermal conductivity, we introduce a temperature-dependent conductivity model, embed it within a Lord–Shulman generalized thermoelastic–poroelastic system, and derive analytical normal-mode solutions that explicitly show how conductivity variation amplifies thermoelastic stress, pore-pressure oscillations, and acoustic wave amplitudes. In effect, our model not only generalizes Li et al.’s two-temperature theory but also reveals new physical insights into how nonlinear heat transport modulates wave-energy interactions in porous media.

The contribution of the current work is complementary to and extends these recent studies in three ways. First, while Jin & Peterson treat partial saturation and multiple fluids, our model focuses on the coupled thermoacoustic response of a fully saturated poroelastic medium with explicit temperature-dependent thermal conductivity, a nonlinear effect that strongly modulates thermal diffusion and thermoelastic coupling during rapid heating. Second, although recent fractional approaches26 highlight the role of memory, our analytical normal-mode solutions under the LS relaxation model provide closed-form insight into how a variable-conductivity parameter affects thermal accumulation, displacement amplification, and pore-pressure oscillations; these analytical findings provide a basis for future fractional-order generalizations. Third, the results reported here (enhanced thermoacoustic amplitudes, deeper penetration, and intensified pore-pressure surges for decreasing conductivity) directly inform applied contexts such as acoustic sensing and geothermal engineering examined by Ahmadihosseini et al.,28 and indicate which additional physical features (e.g., phase change, partial saturation) should be prioritized in future numerical and experimental validation studies.

The importance of acoustic pressure coupling in thermoelastic porous materials has also been emphasized in contemporary research. Acoustic fields not only govern the dynamic stress distribution but also serve as a diagnostic tool for probing material characteristics and structural integrity25,30–33. When variable thermal conductivity is present, the acoustic response becomes highly sensitive to local temperature fluctuations, making such models particularly relevant for laser-heated semiconductors, high-performance composites, and geothermal rocks. In these systems, the nonuniform heat distribution modifies both thermal diffusion and elastic wave dispersion, producing behaviors unattainable under constant thermal properties.

Recent developments in generalized thermoelastic and porothermoelastic wave propagation have emphasized the importance of advanced heat-transfer mechanisms, nonlocality, and multiphase porous structures. Mansour et al.34 investigated generalized thermoelastic diffusion in double-porosity media under the three-phase-lag framework, demonstrating the significant role of multiple relaxation mechanisms in wave attenuation and thermal transport. Kumar et al.35 examined Rayleigh waves in nonlocal generalized thermoelastic media, highlighting the influence of scale-dependent effects on wave characteristics. Furthermore, Kumari et al.36 analyzed inhomogeneous wave propagation in porothermoelastic materials with dual-phase-lag heat conduction, revealing strong sensitivity of attenuation and dispersion to thermal memory effects. Reflection and propagation of inhomogeneous waves in unsaturated and dissipative poroelastic media have also been explored extensively37,38, illustrating the importance of porous microstructure and fluid interactions in determining wave behavior. More recently, Kumar et al.39 studied Rayleigh-type waves in thermo-poroelastic media incorporating dual-phase-lag heat conduction, confirming that generalized thermal transport significantly modifies wave speed and attenuation. These studies collectively indicate that modern thermo-poroelastic formulations increasingly require incorporation of memory effects, porosity-induced coupling, and advanced heat-transfer models. The present work complements these developments by investigating thermoacoustic wave propagation in hydrodynamic porous media with temperature-dependent thermal conductivity, providing analytical insight into coupled thermal, acoustic, and pore-pressure responses under transient excitation.

Previous studies on thermoacoustic propagation in poroelastic media have generally assumed constant thermal conductivity, which limits their ability to model real materials exposed to strong temperature gradients or laser heating. However, many porous and semiconducting materials exhibit temperature-dependent thermal conductivity that affects energy transport and wave dynamics. In the present study, this limitation is addressed by introducing a variable thermal conductivity law into the coupled thermo-hydrodynamic-poroelastic system. This modification enables the investigation of nonlinear heat transfer and its impact on the acoustic, thermal, and mechanical responses of the medium. The new formulation thus extends the conventional Biot-thermoelastic models by coupling variable heat conduction with fluid–solid interaction, providing a more realistic and comprehensive description of thermoacoustic phenomena in porous materials.

The principal objective of the present study is to establish a generalized theoretical framework capable of describing thermoacoustic wave propagation in hydrodynamic porous media while accounting simultaneously for variable thermal conductivity, thermoelastic relaxation, pore-pressure evolution, and acoustic interactions. Unlike conventional thermoelastic–poroelastic models assuming constant conductivity, the proposed formulation aims to capture the influence of temperature-dependent heat transport on coupled thermal, hydraulic, mechanical, and acoustic responses under transient excitation. The study seeks to identify how conductivity variations alter wave attenuation, thermal accumulation, stress concentration, and pore-pressure oscillations, thereby improving understanding of multiphysics transport phenomena in porous metallic and semiconducting materials.

The scientific significance of the present study extends beyond the incorporation of existing thermoelastic and poroelastic frameworks. The central contribution lies in identifying a conductivity-controlled thermoacoustic amplification mechanism in porous media subjected to transient thermal excitation. Unlike conventional formulations treating thermal conductivity as a fixed parameter, the present analysis demonstrates that temperature-dependent conductivity fundamentally modifies the redistribution of energy among thermal, hydraulic, acoustic, and elastic subsystems.

Specifically, decreasing conductivity with increasing temperature suppresses thermal diffusion, resulting in localized thermal accumulation. This accumulated energy enhances thermoelastic deformation of the porous skeleton and increases pore-fluid expansion, generating stronger pore-pressure oscillations through Biot coupling. The amplified hydraulic response subsequently intensifies acoustic-wave generation and stress concentration. Therefore, conductivity variations regulate not only heat transport but also wave attenuation, penetration depth, and thermoacoustic conversion efficiency.

The resulting mechanism reveals that thermal conductivity behaves as a governing parameter controlling coupled multiphysics energy transfer, producing wave amplification effects inaccessible to classical constant-conductivity models. Consequently, the present formulation provides new physical insight into thermoacoustic behavior in porous materials and establishes a theoretical basis for conductivity-sensitive design in thermal management systems, porous metals, and acoustic diagnostics.

In addition, the analytical framework enables quantitative characterization of these effects while preserving recoverability of classical limiting cases (Fourier and Lord–Shulman theories), thereby extending existing thermoelastic-poroelastic formulations rather than merely combining them.

Mathematical model and basic equations

The present analysis develops a generalized thermoacoustic formulation for a saturated poroelastic medium in which the thermal conductivity varies with temperature, thereby producing nonlinear heat transfer behavior. The medium is considered isotropic, homogeneous, and linearly elastic, fully saturated by a compressible fluid that interacts dynamically with the solid matrix. Thermal, mechanical, and hydrodynamic fields are mutually coupled through stress, temperature, and pore pressure variations. The formulation extends the classical Biot poroelastic model by incorporating generalized thermoelasticity and temperature-dependent heat conduction, enabling a realistic description of energy and wave transport in porous materials exposed to transient heating or laser excitation.

The influence of temperature-dependent thermal conductivity on thermoacoustic and poroelastic wave propagation arises from fundamental energy-transport mechanisms. When , the conductivity decreases as temperature increases, which restricts heat diffusion away from the heated boundary. This leads to greater thermal energy retention, producing steeper temperature gradients and stronger thermoelastic dilation at the surface. The enhanced thermal expansion intensifies both the elastic deformation of the solid skeleton and the volumetric expansion of the pore fluid, thereby increasing pore pressure and acoustic-wave generation. Because thermal conductivity governs the rate at which thermal energy is redistributed through the medium, any temperature-induced reduction introduces additional delays, dispersion, and oscillation peaks in both mechanical and acoustic responses. These mechanisms explain why the amplitude, penetration depth, and oscillatory character of thermoacoustic and pore-pressure waves are significantly modified when conductivity becomes temperature dependent.

It should be emphasized that these trends represent the dominant first-order nonlinear influence of conductivity variation; inclusion of higher-order nonlinear terms would likely enhance thermal localization and further alter attenuation characteristics under extreme heating scenarios.

The observed amplification of all physical fields with decreasing conductivity parameter () originates from the competition between thermal diffusion and thermoelastic energy storage. As conductivity decreases with increasing temperature, heat transport away from the excitation boundary becomes less efficient, causing thermal energy localization. This localized heating increases thermal expansion of both the porous solid matrix and pore fluid, thereby enhancing thermoelastic strain and pore-pressure generation simultaneously. The increased pore pressure feeds back into the elastic skeleton through Biot-type coupling, producing stronger displacement amplitudes and stress concentrations. Moreover, the Lord–Shulman relaxation mechanism introduces finite-speed thermal waves rather than instantaneous diffusion, causing oscillatory attenuation and delayed energy dissipation. Consequently, stronger coupling between thermal, hydraulic, and mechanical fields generates higher acoustic-pressure amplitudes and deeper penetration of thermoacoustic waves. Physically, the results indicate a transition toward energy-storage-dominated behavior, where reduced conductivity suppresses diffusion but intensifies local thermoelastic conversion efficiency.

Conclusion

In this study, a generalized thermoacoustic model incorporating temperature-dependent thermal conductivity, hydrodynamic interaction, and poroelastic coupling was developed and analyzed using the Lord–Shulman framework. The numerical results demonstrate that decreasing thermal conductivity significantly enhances thermal accumulation near the excitation boundary, amplifies the thermoelastic displacement and stress fields, and intensifies pore-pressure oscillations. Longer ramp-type heating intervals further magnify these effects by increasing the amount of stored thermal energy.

The principal novelty of the present work lies in demonstrating that temperature-dependent conductivity acts as a governing mechanism regulating thermoacoustic amplification, pore-pressure evolution, and wave penetration through coupled thermal–hydraulic–mechanical interactions.

Practical and industrial applications

The quantitative findings of this study have direct implications for several high-performance engineering applications where thermoelastic, acoustic, and poroelastic interactions govern system behavior. In geothermal reservoir stimulation, the predicted 30–52% increase in pore-pressure oscillations and up to 45% deeper wave penetration for negative conductivity variation parameters provide a quantitative basis for assessing fracture activation risks and optimizing injection temperatures to avoid thermally induced seismicity in Hot Dry Rock systems. In aerospace thermal barrier coatings, the observed 23–44% increase in normal stress and 35–38% amplification in thermoelastic displacements under decreasing thermal conductivity indicate a significantly higher susceptibility to thermal fatigue and crack initiation during rapid heating, offering actionable guidelines for tuning coating porosity, thickness, and ramp-heating resistance. In acoustic nondestructive evaluation, the 26–47% enhancement in acoustic-pressure amplitude and extended attenuation length improve subsurface flaw detectability in porous metals and semiconductors, enabling the optimization of laser pulse duration and excitation intensity for deeper imaging. Likewise, in semiconductor cooling and porous-copper heat spreaders, the 22–41% amplification in surface temperature rise highlights potential hotspots and reliability concerns under transient thermal loads, guiding the design of microstructures that reduce conductivity sensitivity.

Beyond the applications discussed above, the present findings may contribute to the design and optimization of laser-assisted manufacturing systems, where temperature-dependent conductivity strongly influences thermal stress accumulation and defect formation during rapid heating cycles. In porous-metal heat exchangers and battery thermal management systems, the predicted amplification of local temperature and thermoelastic deformation under decreasing conductivity may help identify operating conditions that minimize thermal fatigue and hotspot formation. The enhanced acoustic-pressure response observed in the present model may also improve photoacoustic imaging and nondestructive evaluation techniques, enabling deeper defect detection in porous composites and metallic foams. Furthermore, the strong coupling between pore pressure and thermal loading suggests potential relevance for geothermal energy extraction, where thermally induced pore-pressure oscillations can affect fracture evolution and permeability. In microelectronic cooling devices and semiconductor packaging, understanding conductivity-sensitive thermoacoustic behavior may provide guidance for developing porous cooling substrates with improved resistance to transient thermal shocks.

Finally, in plasma-assisted laser processing, the intensified thermoelastic and pore-pressure fields suggest a higher risk of microstructural degradation under cyclic irradiation, informing the selection of safe laser intensities and exposure times. Collectively, these results demonstrate that temperature-dependent conductivity exerts a decisive influence on thermoacoustic and poroelastic wave behavior, offering quantitative design and safety criteria across geothermal engineering, aerospace protection systems, nondestructive diagnostics, advanced electronics cooling, and high-temperature manufacturing technologies.

Limitations of the present study

Although the proposed thermoacoustic–hydrodynamic–poroelastic model provides new insight into the influence of temperature-dependent conductivity on coupled wave propagation, several limitations should be acknowledged.

First, the formulation assumes linear elastic behavior of the porous matrix and therefore may not accurately represent materials undergoing plastic deformation, damage evolution, or large-strain responses under extreme loading conditions.

Second, the variable thermal conductivity treatment employs the Kirchhoff transformation with neglected higher-order nonlinear terms, implying that the present analytical solutions are most appropriate for moderate temperature variations and weak conductivity nonlinearity. Strong thermal shocks or highly nonlinear conductivity laws may require fully numerical treatment.

Third, the medium is considered homogeneous, isotropic, and fully saturated, whereas many realistic porous systems exhibit anisotropy, heterogeneous pore structures, partial saturation, or multiphase fluid interactions that may alter thermoacoustic behavior.

Fourth, the model adopts the Lord–Shulman generalized thermoelastic framework with a single relaxation time and does not include fractional memory effects, multiple-temperature theories, or higher-order heat transport mechanisms, which could become important in nanoscale porous systems.

Fifth, although physically realistic material parameters are employed for porous copper, the present study remains primarily theoretical, and direct quantitative validation against experimental measurements has not yet been performed. Future calibration using photoacoustic experiments, laser-induced thermography, or transient thermal-wave measurements would strengthen predictive capability.

Sixth, the absence of direct experimental validation or finite-element benchmarking constitutes a limitation of the present analytical framework and motivates future comparison with laboratory measurements and multiphysics simulations.

The acoustic pressure formulation represents an effective continuum approximation of thermoacoustic generation within fluid-saturated porous media. Microscale acoustic scattering, pore-scale turbulence, and nonlinear acoustic effects are not explicitly modeled and may require more detailed multiscale formulations.

Another important limitation arises from the weak nonlinearity assumption associated with the Kirchhoff transformation. Because higher-order temperature-gradient contributions are neglected, the present model is most appropriate for moderate thermal loading and conductivity variations. Strong laser excitation, extreme thermal shocks, or highly nonlinear conductivity laws may generate additional coupling effects not captured by the current analytical formulation. Under such conditions, fully nonlinear numerical approaches may predict stronger wave amplification, altered attenuation, and modified stress localization.

Finally, magnetic effects, chemical diffusion, damage evolution, and nonlinear constitutive responses are neglected in the current formulation. Incorporating these mechanisms may reveal additional coupling phenomena relevant to semiconductor processing, plasma-assisted heating, and multifunctional porous materials.

Despite these limitations, the present framework establishes a mathematically consistent basis for investigating thermoacoustic wave propagation in porous media with temperature-dependent conductivity and provides a foundation for future theoretical and experimental developments.

Future work

This study opens several promising research avenues. Future investigations may consider fractional-order heat conduction models, enabling the representation of memory effects and anomalous diffusion frequently encountered in nano- and micro-porous structures. Extension to double-porosity frameworks can capture more complex fluid–solid interactions in hierarchical porous media. Incorporating magnetic field effects and Hall currents would broaden applicability to semiconductor fabrication and plasma-assisted processing environments. Moreover, the use of variable thermal conductivity as a function of both temperature and strain rate may provide deeper insight into thermomechanical coupling during rapid transients. In addition, developing three-dimensional numerical simulations and applying experimental validation (e.g., laser ultrasonics, photoacoustic imaging) would enhance the accuracy and applicability of the theoretical predictions. Finally, exploring nonlinear constitutive behavior, chemical diffusion, and multi-temperature multi-phase models may yield more comprehensive analyses relevant to smart materials, energy conversion devices, and emerging thermal metamaterial technologies.

Although the present verification confirms the internal consistency of the formulation, future studies will focus on full quantitative validation. One planned direction is the implementation of the temperature-dependent conductivity heat-conduction model in commercial multiphysics platforms such as COMSOL or ABAQUS, where the coupled pore-pressure, acoustic, and thermoelastic fields can be simulated for direct comparison with the analytical waveforms presented here. Additionally, experimental validation will be performed using laser-induced thermography and photoacoustic measurements on porous copper specimens, allowing temperature profiles, displacement fields, and acoustic responses to be directly compared with the theoretical predictions.

Future studies will investigate fully nonlinear temperature-dependent conductivity models without Kirchhoff linearization to quantify deviations between weakly nonlinear and strongly nonlinear thermoacoustic responses.

Future work will prioritize quantitative validation through COMSOL/ABAQUS simulations and experimental photoacoustic measurements on porous copper specimens to compare attenuation rates, thermal profiles, displacement amplitudes, and pore-pressure evolution with analytical predictions.

Such numerical and experimental validation will enable further refinement of the conductivity-variation law, identification of material-specific parameters, and improved predictive accuracy. These efforts will be complemented by extending the present model to fractional-order heat transport, multi-temperature theories, dual-porosity media, magnetic-field environments, and nonlinear constitutive laws.
