Warm Inflation Beyond the Markovian Limit
Mayukh R. Gangopadhyay, Nilanjana Kumar

Abstract
Warm inflation is commonly studied under the assumption that the stochastic force sourcing inflaton fluctuations is Markovian. Realistic thermal systems, however, possess finite relaxation times and can therefore generate colored noise with non-zero correlation time. In this work we investigate warm inflation beyond the Markovian limit and determine how finite correlation time modifies the primordial scalar power spectrum. We show that memory effects suppress the scalar spectrum relative to the standard white-noise result and derive a simple expression for this correction in terms of the background thermal dynamics. In particular, we relate the size of the non-Markovian effect directly to the thermal ratio between the bath temperature and the Hubble scale, thereby establishing a transparent link between warm-inflation background quantities and the validity of the Markovian approximation. We also derive the corresponding modification of the tensor-to-scalar ratio and the induced shifts in the scalar spectral index and the running of the scalar spectral index. Our results provide a simple and practical diagnostic for identifying when finite correlation-time effects become relevant in warm-inflation model building.

The purpose of the present work is to analyze the consequences of colored noise for the scalar power spectrum in warm inflation and to do so in a way that remains closely tied to the background dynamics. \eta_{c}=(3H+\Upsilon)\tau_{c}=3H(1+Q)\tau_{c},

A key result of this paper is that

(H,V^{\prime},Q)\longrightarrow T/H\longrightarrow\eta_{c}(Q)\longrightarrow\delta(Q),

A second important point is interpretational. The present work is not merely a calculation of a correction to the scalar spectrum. It also provides a criterion for the validity of the Markovian approximation used throughout much of the warm-inflation literature. Indeed, since

\eta_{c}=\frac{3(1+Q)}{\alpha(T/H)},

the standard white-noise treatment is valid only when

\alpha(T/H)\gg 3(1+Q).

Conversely, when

\alpha(T/H)\lesssim 3(1+Q),

2
Warm inflation background and the thermal ratio
T/H

The homogeneous inflaton field obeys:

\ddot{\phi}+3H\dot{\phi}+V^{\prime}(\phi)=-\Upsilon\dot{\phi},

(2)

where
\Upsilon
is the dissipative coefficient. The standard dissipative ratio is defined by

Q\equiv\frac{\Upsilon}{3H}.

(3)

In the slow-roll regime Eq. (
2
) reduces to

3H(1+Q)\dot{\phi}\simeq-V^{\prime}.

(4)

The radiation bath evolves according to

\dot{\rho}_{R}+4H\rho_{R}=\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}.

(5)

During warm inflation one usually works in the quasi-steady regime in which
\dot{\rho}_{R}
is subleading, giving

4H\rho_{R}\simeq\Upsilon\dot{\phi}^{2}.

(6)

Using Eq. (
3
), this becomes

\rho_{R}=\frac{\Upsilon}{4H}\dot{\phi}^{2}=\frac{3}{4}Q\,\dot{\phi}^{2}.

(7)

Substituting the slow-roll velocity from Eq. (
4
),

\dot{\phi}^{2}=\frac{V^{\prime 2}}{9H^{2}(1+Q)^{2}},

(8)

one obtains

\rho_{R}=\frac{Q\,V^{\prime 2}}{12H^{2}(1+Q)^{2}}.

(9)

The temperature of the thermal bath is related to the radiation density by

\rho_{R}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{30}g_{*}T^{4},

(10)

with
g_{*}
the effective number of relativistic degrees of freedom. Combining Eqs. (
9
) and (
10
) gives

T^{4}=\frac{30}{\pi^{2}g_{*}}\,\frac{Q\,V^{\prime 2}}{12H^{2}(1+Q)^{2}}.

(11)

Dividing by
H^{4}
yields the explicit thermal ratio

\boxed{\frac{T}{H}=\left[\frac{30}{\pi^{2}g_{*}}\,\frac{Q\,V^{\prime 2}}{12H^{6}(1+Q)^{2}}\right]^{1/4}.}

(12)

Equation (
12
) makes the dependence on the dissipative ratio completely explicit. For fixed
H
,
V^{\prime}
, and
g_{*}
, the nontrivial
Q
dependence is controlled by
Q/(1+Q)^{2}
. Consequently,

\displaystyle Q\ll 1

\displaystyle:\qquad\frac{T}{H}\propto Q^{1/4},

(13)

\displaystyle Q\gg 1

\displaystyle:\qquad\frac{T}{H}\propto Q^{-1/4}.

(14)

Thus the thermal ratio typically increases in the weak dissipative regime, peaks around
Q\sim\mathcal{O}(1)
, and decreases slowly for strong dissipation.

3
Stochastic dynamics beyond the Markovian limit

3.1
Langevin equation

The Fourier modes of the inflaton perturbation satisfy the stochastic equation

\ddot{\delta\phi}_{k}+(3H+\Upsilon)\dot{\delta\phi}_{k}+\frac{k^{2}}{a^{2}}\delta\phi_{k}=\xi_{k},

(15)

where
\xi_{k}
is the stochastic force sourced by interactions with the thermal bath. In the Markovian approximation one takes
\xi_{k}
to be white noise,

\langle\xi(t)\xi(t^{\prime})\rangle\propto\delta(t-t^{\prime}).

(16)

To go beyond this limit we assume that the force has finite correlation time
\tau_{c}
. The simplest and most useful model is exponentially correlated noise,

\langle\xi(t)\xi(t^{\prime})\rangle=D\,e^{-|t-t^{\prime}|/\tau_{c}},

(17)

which is the standard Ornstein–Uhlenbeck kernel.
A simple realization of colored noise is provided by the Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

\dot{\xi}=-\frac{\xi}{\tau_{c}}+\eta(t),

(18)

where
\eta(t)
is white noise. Solving this process yields the exponential correlator
given in (
17
).

3.2
Colored-noise parameter

The deterministic part of Eq. (
15
) contains the damping timescale

\tau_{d}=(3H+\Upsilon)^{-1}=\frac{1}{3H(1+Q)}.

(19)

It is therefore natural to define the dimensionless colored-noise parameter

\boxed{\eta_{c}\equiv(3H+\Upsilon)\tau_{c}=3H(1+Q)\tau_{c}.}

(20)

This parameter measures the ratio of the noise correlation time to the relaxation time of the inflaton perturbation.

To connect the correlation time to thermal physics we parametrize

\tau_{c}=\frac{1}{\alpha T},

(21)

where
\alpha
is an effective inverse correlation-time parameter for the inflaton-sourcing sector. Combining Eq. (
21
) with the background result (
12
), one obtains

\boxed{\eta_{c}(Q)=\frac{3(1+Q)}{\alpha(T/H)}.}

(22)

Defining

A\equiv\alpha(T/H),

(23)

one simply has

\eta_{c}=\frac{3(1+Q)}{A}.

(24)

3.3
Detailed derivation of the scalar suppression factor

Equation (
15
) is linear, so the formal solution may be written in terms of the retarded Green function
G_{k}(t,t^{\prime})
:

\delta\phi_{k}(t)=\int^{t}dt^{\prime}\,G_{k}(t,t^{\prime})\,\xi_{k}(t^{\prime}).

(25)

The fluctuation variance then follows as

\langle|\delta\phi_{k}(t)|^{2}\rangle=\int^{t}dt_{1}\int^{t}dt_{2}\,G_{k}(t,t_{1})G_{k}(t,t_{2})\,\langle\xi(t_{1})\xi(t_{2})\rangle.

(26)

In the white-noise limit the correlator is proportional to
\delta(t_{1}-t_{2})
, and the double integral reduces to the familiar Markovian result. For exponentially correlated noise, by contrast, the source remains correlated over a finite interval of width
\tau_{c}
.

In the present problem the relevant response timescale is the damping time
\tau_{d}=(3H+\Upsilon)^{-1}
. If the response is dominated by this single effective timescale, the influence of the exponential kernel can be estimated analytically. In this single-timescale approximation one finds

\langle|\delta\phi_{k}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm col}=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{c}^{2}}\langle|\delta\phi_{k}|^{2}\rangle_{\rm white}.

(27)

Since the curvature perturbation is proportional to the inflaton fluctuation, the same suppression factor carries directly to the scalar power spectrum:

\boxed{P_{\rm col}(k)=\delta(Q)\,P_{\rm white}(k),\qquad\delta(Q)\equiv\frac{1}{1+\eta_{c}(Q)^{2}}.}

(28)

\boxed{\frac{P_{\rm col}}{P_{\rm white}}=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{c}^{2}},\qquad\eta_{c}=\frac{3(1+Q)}{\alpha(T/H)}.}

(29)

This relation represents the central result of the present work and provides a direct diagnostic for the validity of the Markovian approximation in warm inflation.

Substituting Eq. (
22
) gives the explicit background-driven suppression factor

\boxed{\delta(Q)=\frac{1}{1+\left[\frac{3(1+Q)}{\alpha(T/H)}\right]^{2}}.}

(30)

4
Consequences for cosmological observables

Since tensor perturbations remain vacuum generated and do not couple directly to the thermal stochastic force at leading order, the tensor spectrum is unchanged while the scalar spectrum is suppressed. The tensor-to-scalar ratio therefore becomes

r_{\rm col}=\frac{r_{\rm white}}{\delta(Q)}.

(31)

Equivalently,

\boxed{\frac{r_{\rm col}}{r_{\rm white}}=\frac{1}{\delta(Q)}.}

(32)

Figure 4:
Enhancement of the tensor-to-scalar ratio due to colored noise,
r_{\rm col}/r_{\rm white}=1/\delta(Q)
.

The scalar spectral index is defined by

n_{s}-1\equiv\frac{d\ln P_{\zeta}}{d\ln k}.

(33)

Using
P_{\zeta}^{\rm col}=\delta P_{\zeta}^{\rm white}
, we have

n_{s}^{\rm col}-1=(n_{s}^{\rm white}-1)+\frac{d\ln\delta}{d\ln k}.

(34)

Hence the colored-noise shift in the scalar tilt is

\boxed{\Delta n_{s}\equiv n_{s}^{\rm col}-n_{s}^{\rm white}=\frac{d\ln\delta}{d\ln k}.}

(35)

Similarly, the running is defined by

\alpha_{s}\equiv\frac{dn_{s}}{d\ln k},

(36)

so the colored-noise contribution is

\boxed{\Delta\alpha_{s}\equiv\alpha_{s}^{\rm col}-\alpha_{s}^{\rm white}=\frac{d^{2}\ln\delta}{d(\ln k)^{2}}.}

(37)

To evaluate these derivatives in a simple and plot-ready way we introduce a mild scale dependence

Q(k)=Q_{*}\left(\frac{k}{k_{*}}\right)^{q},

(38)

which implies

\frac{d\ln Q}{d\ln k}=q.

(39)

By the chain rule,

\Delta n_{s}=q\,\frac{d\ln\delta}{d\ln Q},\qquad\Delta\alpha_{s}=q^{2}\,\frac{d^{2}\ln\delta}{d(\ln Q)^{2}}.

(40)

).

5
Regimes of validity and physical interpretation

Since

\eta_{c}=\frac{3(1+Q)}{\alpha(T/H)},

(41)

the physics naturally splits into three regimes.

In the deep Markovian regime,

\eta_{c}\ll 1\qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad\alpha(T/H)\gg 3(1+Q),

(42)

the correlation time of the stochastic force is much shorter than the response time of the inflaton mode, and the white-noise approximation is valid.

In the transitional regime,

\eta_{c}\sim 1,

(43)

the correlation time becomes comparable to the inflaton relaxation time. In this regime the Markovian approximation begins to fail and the scalar spectrum starts to deviate appreciably from the standard result.

In the strong non-Markovian regime,

\eta_{c}\gg 1,

(44)

the force varies slowly compared with the relaxation time of the inflaton perturbation, so the stochastic driving becomes inefficient and the scalar spectrum is strongly suppressed.

A microphysical estimate of the parameter
\alpha
further sharpens this interpretation. In typical warm-inflation realizations the thermal relaxation rate is expected to scale parametrically as

\Gamma_{\rm th}\sim g^{2}T,

(45)

so the correlation time may be estimated as

\tau_{c}\sim\Gamma_{\rm th}^{-1}\sim\frac{1}{g^{2}T}.

(46)

Comparing with Eq. (
21
), one identifies

\alpha\sim g^{2}.

(47)

The breakdown criterion for the Markovian approximation then becomes

g^{2}(T/H)\lesssim 3(1+Q).

(48)

7
Conclusions

We have investigated warm inflation beyond the Markovian limit by incorporating finite correlation time into the stochastic dynamics of inflaton fluctuations. The main physical consequence is a suppression of the scalar power spectrum relative to the standard white-noise result, quantified by the universal factor

\delta(Q)=\frac{1}{1+\eta_{c}(Q)^{2}}.

(51)

The key new step is that the colored-noise parameter can be expressed directly in terms of the background thermal ratio
T/H
, producing the simple and useful pipeline

(H,V^{\prime},Q)\rightarrow T/H\rightarrow\eta_{c}(Q)\rightarrow\delta(Q)\rightarrow(r,n_{s},\alpha_{s}).

The analysis also yields a practical criterion for the validity of the Markovian approximation commonly used in warm-inflation calculations. The standard white-noise treatment is reliable only when

\alpha(T/H)\gg 3(1+Q),

while finite correlation-time effects become relevant when

\alpha(T/H)\lesssim 3(1+Q).

The result obtained here provides a simple diagnostic criterion that can be readily applied in warm-inflation model building to determine when finite correlation-time effects may become relevant.
