Ulnar Collateral Ligament Hybrid Reconstruction With Palmaris Longus Autograft, Suture Brace Augmentation, and Ulnar Nerve Transposition
Authors: Andrew S. Bi, Jacob T. Morgan, Tomas F. Vega, Logan D. Moews, Tanner Nishioka, Nikhil N. Verma
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Many overhead athletes, both amateur and professional, experience an increasing number of injuries to the ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) as a result of overuse or trauma. As a result of this increasing frequency, multiple techniques have been developed to reconstruct or repair the UCL with success and faster return to sports (RTS) for athletes who are ideal candidates for a repair. However, a large proportion of UCL tear patients cannot undergo repair due to chronic injury, leading to poor tissue quality, midsubstance tears, or older age, which predispose them to slower healing and a more difficult path to total rehabilitation. The senior author's “hybrid” reconstruction combines a palmaris longus autograft for reconstruction with a repair-style suture brace augmentation that allows for increased load-to-failure strength that parallels the native UCL, while allowing for native anatomic motion of the ligament when ranging the elbow.Indications:This technique is indicated for patients with a UCL tear confirmed by magnetic resonance imaging, poor to moderate UCL tissue quality, midsubstance tears, and older patients who have a palmaris longus tendon to harvest and persistent ulnar nerve paresthesias.Technique Description:This video demonstrates the senior author's technique of harvesting a palmaris autograft, drilling proximal and distal UCL graft passage tunnels, augmenting the graft with an internal suture brace, fixation of the graft, and finally an ulnar nerve transposition to alleviate ulnar nerve paresthesia.Results:Outcomes of retrospective studies demonstrate good to excellent outcomes of UCL hybrid reconstructions with suture brace augmentation and RTS rates of at least 85%. Complication rates are low, with the most common complication being graft failure (14%) and the next most common being temporary ulnar nerve paresthesia.Discussion/Conclusion:Overall, this video demonstrates a highly effective technique at restoring native-like biomechanics and function for patients with UCL tears that are too complex to repair. Patients have good to excellent outcomes and RTS rates, although time to RTS may take 12 months or longer.Patient Consent Disclosure Statement:The author(s) attests that consent has been obtained from any patient(s) appearing in this publication. If the individual may be identifiable, the author(s) has included a statement of release or other written form of approval from the patient(s) with this submission for publication.

Video Transcript

This is our technique for ulnar collateral ligament (UCL) hybrid reconstruction with UCL repair, palmaris autograft reconstruction, suture-tape augmentation, and ulnar nerve transposition.

The products seen in this video are not Food and Drug Administration-approved for UCL reconstruction, and consent was obtained from each patient.

Background

The UCL is the primary valgus restraint from 30° to 120° of elbow flexion, with an increasing incidence, with higher velocity in pitchers and higher pitch counts in younger ages.3,5,14 UCL reconstruction was first developed in 1974 by Frank Jobe for pitcher Tommy John, with a >90% return to sport (RTS), but a long rehabilitation process. 6 There have been gradual modifications over the years—including the modified Jobe and docking techniques. There has been a drastic shift in UCL management in recent years, with UCL repair augmented with collagen-coated suture tape becoming increasingly popular, with comparable RTS and revision rates and significantly shorter time to return to throwing. 6

The hybrid reconstruction technique is a recent development that combines UCL repair with internal suture brace augmentation and reconstruction to provide both initial biomechanical stability and the potential for long-term healing and graft augmentation.2,10,12 Indications for the hybrid reconstruction are similar to those for historical UCL reconstruction.

Indications

Young patients with good UCL tissue quality and injuries limited to proximal or distal UCL avulsions should be strongly considered for primary repair with suture tape augmentation.

The patient is a 17-year-old, right-hand dominant male pitcher and third baseman with 2 years of medial elbow pain that has begun to limit his throwing. One month ago, he also felt a pop while throwing to first base, and he does have aspirations to play in college. On physical examination, there is tenderness to palpation of the medial epicondyle and flexor pronator mass, pain with resisted wrist flexion, as well as pain with the valgus stress test and the milking maneuver. There is a negative Tinel sign at the cubital tunnel; however, there is a subluxating ulnar nerve. There is 4+/5 grip strength. Radiographs demonstrate no acute osseous abnormalities, while magnetic resonance imaging demonstrates a proximal UCL tear with moderate UCL tissue quality.

Technique Description

The operative site is marked, and the patient is brought to the operating room and placed supine, and an appropriate timeout is performed.

We start initially with the palmaris tendon autograft harvest with a small 1 cm transverse incision over the location of the palmaris tendon. The tendon is controlled with a hemostat and felt proximally in the forearm. A small counter incision is made, and adhesions superficial and deep to the tendon are released with blunt Metzenbaum scissors. The distal tendon is then controlled with a 2-0 Vicryl whipstitch and then transected distally. Remaining adhesions are released, and the tendon is removed from the proximal counter incision. The tendon is then passed through a tendon stripper, and the palmaris tendon autograft is harvested.

Attention is then turned to the UCL approach. A medial approach to the elbow is performed. The medial epicondyle is identified, and the ulnar nerve is identified and dissected posterior to the medial epicondyle. A split through the flexor pronator mass is performed, taking care to dissect distally enough to allow for exposure of the distal reconstruction. Blunt and sharp dissection using dissecting scissors, a freer, and army navies are used to get to the floor of the flexor pronator mass. The remnant muscle is then feathered off the floor of the flexor pronator mass with a 15-blade. This exposes the borders of the UCL and the sublime tubercle. The UCL is incised from the sublime tubercle to the medial epicondyle, and a cricket retractor is placed, exposing the ulnohumeral joint.

First, the drill for the suture tape augmentation anchor is placed. A fish-mouth drill guide is extremely helpful to anchor on the ridge of the sublime tubercle without slipping. A 2.6-mm pilot hole is then drilled, and a tap is used to prepare for the insertion of the anchor.

The distal converging ulnar tunnels are then drilled. Two 3.5-mm tunnels are drilled, converging on each other approximately 1.5 cm distal to the joint line and about 8 to 10 mm distal to the brace suture anchor, which was placed 7 mm distal to the joint line to recreate the broad insertion of the distal UCL. A curved needle with a nitinol loop is used to pass a suture through the ulnar tunnel. A humeral tunnel is then drilled. Using the same fish-mouthed guide and 2.6-mm drill, a pilot hole is drilled in the medial epicondyle. Care should be taken to identify the isometric point of the proximal UCL origin, which is deep in the medial epicondyle and not superficial or medial.

A 4.5-mm drill tunnel is then drilled using the hard stop drill guide. A small split in the flexor pronator mass is made with a bovie, and then the drill guide for the humeral converging tunnels is used. This is used to drill 2 humeral tunnels that converge on the 4.5-mm humeral tunnel. A sled and nitinol loop are then used to pass 2 passing sutures through these converging humeral tunnels to prepare for the docking technique. The palmaris autograft is then passed through the ulnar tunnel.

Next, the suture tape augmentation anchor is placed in the previously drilled tunnel, and these are snapped out of the way.

Next, attention is then turned to the UCL repair. Using preloaded 0 high-tensile-strength suture, the UCL is repaired at the distal extent. Next, using a 2-0 high-tensile strength suture, the middle of the UCL ligament is then repaired in a figure-of-8 fashion. Both of these repair sutures are then cut.

Then, 1 limb of the graft, along with 1 suture tape, is passed through 1 of the humoral tunnels. These are fully docked in the humeral tunnel, and then the other limb of the graft is folded over the medial epicondyle and marked to be cut and prepped in a similar fashion with a whipstitch suture. Grafts should be marked at the aperture of the humeral tunnel, as well as approximately 15 mm distal to that. The graft is then whipstitched and cut.

The newly prepared limb of the palmaris autograft, as well as the second limb of the suture tape augmentation, is passed through the humeral tunnel. This is extremely important to ensure that the suture tape lies deep to the UCL reconstruction graft to avoid soft-tissue impingement.

Both the suture tape augmentation and the graft sutures are tied over the humerus bone bridge. Because the ulnar nerve will be transposed, the knot stack should be placed on the posterior ridge of the humerus. In cases in which the ulnar nerve is not transposed, the knot stacks should be purposely tied in the flexor pronator mass split that was made earlier. The suture tape augmentation and the palmaris tendon autograft reconstruction can then be unitized using a figure-of-8 stitch. The flexor pronator mass split is then closed with a sequential Vicryl suture.

Finally, attention is turned to the ulnar nerve transposition. The ulnar nerve is fully decompressed through the superficial and deep heads of the flexor carpi ulnaris (FCU). The deep branch of the FCU is preserved, and the ulnar nerve is decompressed proximally through the medial intermuscular septum. The nerve is controlled with a vessel loop, avoiding undue tension on the nerve, and the nerve is freed from soft-tissue attachments, taking care to preserve the FCU branch. The nerve can be freed from relative posterior tethering from the branch of the FCU by dissecting out the branch of the FCU distally to free that branch up as well. Following transposition anteriorly, the remnant medial intermuscular septum can be closed to the posterior soft tissue or the medial border of the triceps to cover the graft sutures to avoid nerve irritation.

Lastly, a subcutaneous transposition is performed with a 0-Vicryl suture through the superficial fascia of the flexor pronator mass and the underlying subcutaneous tissue. The incision is copiously irrigated and then closed in a layered fashion. The elbow is then placed in a hinged elbow brace locked at 90°, and range-of-motion exercises begin at 1 week.

Results and Discussion

At 1 week, the brace is unlocked to begin gentle range of motion exercises, and physical therapy starts at 2 weeks postoperatively. The goal is full painless range of motion by 6 weeks, and light strengthening begins at 6 weeks postoperatively. At 12 weeks, a full-strength program begins, followed by a return-to-throwing program at 5 months, with a return to throwing at 6 months, and pitching off a mound at 9 months. Gameplay can begin at 12 months postoperatively.

Long-term results following a hybrid UCL reconstruction technique demonstrate transient ulnar nerve paresthesias in approximately 2% of patients, with none of the 34 patients having infection. 8 However, there was up to a 14% failure rate and 1% graft morbidity at the palmaris autograft harvest site.7,8 In addition, this same cohort demonstrated an 85% RTS at the previous level or greater using this hybrid reconstruction technique. 8 Biomechanical studies have shown that adding an internal suture brace to UCL reconstructions results in moderate improvements to valgus displacement and may improve load to failure.9,11,13

Multiple alternative techniques exist to add a suture brace to UCL reconstructions.1,4,9,12 Our's uses intraosseous suture-anchor-based augmentation, while another notable technique uses an overlaid suture tape with footprint anchor fixation. 4
