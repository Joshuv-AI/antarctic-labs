 Abstract

The historic December 5, 2022 experiment at Lawrence Livermore National Lab’s (LLNL)
National Ignition Facility (NIF) reached fusion energy ignition for the first time. This is the most
important scientific breakthrough of the 21st century paves the way to future clean inertial fusion
energy (IFE). The diamond (high density carbon (HDC)) ablator material used in this experiment
displays detrimental effects due to the development of hydrodynamic instabilities at the
diamond/fuel interface under shock compression. New alternatives to diamond ablators are
required to step up the energy yield in ICF experiments. The unique combination of mechanical
strength (approaching that of diamond), the ability to accommodate high-Z dopants (in contrast
to diamond), and the tunability of the properties (through synthesis material with varying sp3
content) make amorphous carbon (a-C) a promising material for next-generation IFE ablative
capsules. However, despite its critical importance to the IFE program, the behavior of a-C carbon
at extreme temperatures and pressures remains largely unexplored.

The primary goals of this project were to perform groundbreaking dynamic compression
experiments and predictive simulations to uncover the fundamental high-energy-density physics
of amorphous carbon. Our goals were (1) to uncover the metastability range of amorphous
carbon and probe phase transitions to diamond or metastable supercooled liquid carbon; (2) to
acquire high-quality equation of state (EOS) data and develop an experimentally validated EOS
from machine-learning MD simulations of the complex states of carbon; and (3) to uncover the
complex behavior of carbon liquid in both thermodynamically stable and metastable supercooled
states by accessing large areas of carbon phase diagram with amorphous samples with variable
sp3 content.

Our proposed experimental program included measurements of equation of state and diffraction
measurements using the Omega EP laser at the Laboratory of Laser Energetics at the University
of Rochester. The theoretical/simulation program involved the development of machine-learning
models of the complex response of amorphous carbon under dynamic compression by
performing molecular dynamics simulations at experimental time and length scales using
leadership class DOE supercomputers. Simulations guided experiments to observe predicted
phenomena and acquire critical experimental data in specific pressure-temperature domains to
validate theoretical models.

This research delivered fundamental properties of novel amorphous carbon IFE ablator material,
including phase diagram and EOS. These results will aid in IFE target design and implosion
experiments. A unique combination of predictive simulations and dynamic and static experiments
provided a highly inspirational intellectual environment for graduate students and postdocs
involved in this project.

1. Major goals of the project
 The main objective of this collaborative experimental and simulation project is to investigate
the high-pressure/temperature behavior of amorphous carbon, a novel Inertial Fusion Energy
(IFE) ablator material under compression by IFE drivers aiming to achieve high gain in low-
adiabat implosions. The scientific goals are: (1) to uncover the atomic-scale evolution of the a-C
microstructure under shock compression, including potential phase transitions to diamond or
metastable supercooled liquid carbon at high pressures and temperatures; (2) to accurately
describe the complex states of carbon under conditions relevant to IFE and obtain high-quality
EOS through a combination of experimental measurements and machine-learning molecular
dynamics simulations.

2. Research Accomplishments
Experiments: We performed EOS and X-ray diffraction (XRD) experiments using a-C samples at
the Omega EP Laser Facility. The experimental time was awarded to us via LaserNetUS program.
Our diffraction PXRDIP experiments aimed to determine the metastability range of a-C and probe
any phase transitions to diamond. The experiments are uniquely coupled to predictive molecular
dynamics (MD) simulations which guided the experiment to observe the predicted phenomena.
We have successfully fielded 8 PXRDIP shots in Fall 2023. In these experiments we used

(EuXFEL) HED facility using a-C samples synthesized by our collaborators from NIF target
fabrication team at LLNL. The EuXFEL shots are currently being analyzed.

Simulations: Our machine-learning MD simulations investigated the atomic-scale evolution of
a-C microstructure under shock compression, including potential phase transitions to diamond,
and obtained high-quality EOS to support our experiments at Omega EP laser facility and
EuXFEL. The major accomplishment from our MD simulations is the prediction of the a-C phase
diagram. We found that within the wide range of pressures from 0 to 250 GPa and moderate
temperatures up to 3,000 K a-C increases significantly the fraction of sp3 atoms. At higher
temperatures a formation of diamond is observed followed by a region of metastable supercooled
carbon liquid between 4,000 and 5,500 K. In explicit MD shock simulations we observed that the
nanocrystalline diamond (NCD) nucleates at pressures above 30 GPa up to 100 GPa under shock
compression. a-C transforms to NCD/liquid mixed-phase state at pressures above 50 GPa and
completely exits into liquid at pressures above 100 GPa. NCD diamond nucleates in metastable
carbon liquid behind shock front at very short time scale <100 ps and then slowly evolves toward
the final state at the time scale ~ several nanoseconds. The calculated pressure-density Hugoniot
was used to design the Omega EP and EuXFEL experiments (see above). Our MD simulations of
materials transformation along shock Hugoniot also provided the X-ray diffraction patterns at
varying shock intensities. By comparing the experimental and simulation XRD patterns we were
able to determine the corresponding pressure and temperature, which were not directly measured
in experiment. We have performed double shock compression MD simulations to support our
experiments at Omega EP laser facility of Laboratory for Laser Energetics which were conducted
in Fall 2023. Our MD simulations predicted that a-C transforms upon double shock compression
to NCD from 100 to 200 GPa. Our Omega EP experiments in Fall 2023 confirmed our prediction
by observing diamond peaks of excellent quantity in measured XRD pattern in the shot at 200
GPa.

3. Training and professional development
 This project provided excellent training opportunities for students and postdocs. The project
involved one graduate student at USF (Chamara Somarathna) and three postdocs: USF (Joseph
Gonzalez) and Carnegie Institution of Washington (Sota Takagi and Ian Szumila). Students and
postdocs were trained in shot design, execution, data analysis, and simulations under the
effective mentorship of senior team members.
