Durable benefit in a crowded frontline landscape: final results for nivolumab-cabozantinib in metastatic renal cell carcinoma
Authors: Gregory M. Hemenway, Daniel M. Geynisman
Published: 2026

The frontline treatment landscape for advanced renal cell carcinoma (RCC) has undergone a fundamental transformation over the past 20 years, from interleukin-2 (IL-2) and interferon alpha to vascular endothelial growth factor receptor (VEGFR) tyrosine kinase inhibitor (TKI) monotherapy with sunitinib (SUN) and pazopanib, to now combination regimens incorporating immune checkpoint inhibition (ICI). Phase III trials have established pembrolizumab plus axitinib (PEM + AXI), pembrolizumab plus lenvatinib (PEM + LEN), avelumab plus axitinib (AVE-AXI) and nivolumab plus cabozantinib (NIVO + CABO) as standards of care across international metastatic RCC (mRCC) database consortium (IMDC) risk categories (1-4).

In a recent issue of ESMO Annals of Oncology, Motzer et al. presented the final results of the phase III CheckMate 9ER trial with a median follow-up of 5.6-years, providing one of the most mature datasets for an ICI-TKI regimen in untreated mRCC (5). At primary analysis, NIVO + CABO demonstrated significant improvements in progression-free survival (PFS), overall survival (OS), and objective response rate (ORR) compared with SUN, leading to regulatory approvals in Europe and the United States (2). The current report demonstrates sustained benefit in these key endpoints while providing exploratory and descriptive analyses with respect to depth of response and outcomes, stratified by IMDC prognostic risk group and baseline organ sites of metastases.

At the landmark 5-year analysis, the median PFS was nearly doubled with NIVO + CABO compared to SUN, with an 11.0-month median improvement in OS. Importantly, the separation of survival curves persists over time, with a meaningful proportion of patients remaining progression-free and alive at 60 months. These long-term data reinforce that the clinical benefit of NIVO + CABO is not limited to early disease control but extends into a durable survival advantage. For the 115 patients who completed the per-protocol 2 years of NIVO (irrespective of CABO), the median time to initiation of subsequent systemic therapy was an impressive 35 months, underscoring the durability of disease control.

When stratified by IMDC risk group, NIVO + CABO favored most efficacy endpoints across all IMDC groups, with the exception of OS benefit in favorable-risk patients. However, this subgroup was relatively small, and the trial was not powered to detect a difference here. Moreover, the absence of an OS signal in favorable-risk disease mirrors findings from other phase III trials evaluating ICI-VEGFR TKI combinations against SUN. Demonstrating survival improvement in a population characterized by VEGF-pathway sensitivity, prolonged post-progression survival and effective subsequent therapies remains inherently challenging.

In addition to efficacy, long-term safety of ICI + TKI regimens remains an important consideration as patients continue therapy for extended periods of time. With a median 5.6 years of follow-up, no new safety signals emerged with NIVO + CABO. Common toxicities associated with CABO including diarrhea, palmar-plantar erythrodysesthesia, and hypertension remain. Treatment-related adverse events of any grade led to the discontinuation of either study drug in 90 (28.1%) of 320 treated patients in the NIVO + CABO arm [NIVO only, 32 (10.0%); CABO only, 34 (10.6%); NIVO + CABO both simultaneously, 20 (6.3%); and NIVO + CABO both sequentially, 4 (1.3%)], and in 35 (10.9%) of 320 treated patients in the SUN arm (5). The relatively low rate of permanent discontinuation of both agents supports the feasibility of prolonged combination therapy in routine practice. CABO dosing at 40 mg once daily certainly plays a large part in this.

While cross-trial comparisons are methodologically imperfect, the four approved ICI + TKI regimens appear broadly similar in OS, with modest differences in response rates and toxicity profiles. Thus, regimen selection in practice remains guided primarily by clinical features: disease burden and phenotype, anticipated TKI toxicities, and patient, as well as provider, preference. These authors may favor PEM + LEN in a young, robust patient with aggressive disease biology as meta-analyses have shown improved ORR and PFS compared to other regimens while understanding that treatment-related toxicities are increased (6). Conversely, NIVO + CABO may be favored in an older patient with comorbidities and less aggressive disease biology when limiting toxicities becomes increasingly important but with comparable OS.

In the future, novel imaging and molecular biomarkers may provide additional objective measures to refine treatment selection beyond the clinical gestalt described above. Post hoc analyses from CheckMate 9ER presented by Braun and colleagues at ESMO 2024 identified serum glycoproteins related to complement activation and lipid metabolism as potential predictors of response to NIVO + CABO (7). Earlier exploratory analyses from the phase III METEOR trial, which compared CABO vs. everolimus (EV) in previously treated patients with metastatic RCC, suggested that low baseline levels of hepatocyte growth factor (HGF), GAS6, and VEGF were independently prognostic for improved OS with CABO (8). Together, these findings raise the possibility that circulating biomarkers could refine patient selection in the future.

Perhaps more intriguing is the effort to integrate, rather than isolate, biomarkers. At ASCO 2025, an additional ad hoc analysis utilizing the CheckMate 9ER dataset was presented (9). Applying artificial intelligence-enabled single-cell resolution mapping of the tumor microenvironment (TME), high endothelial cell density correlated with improved PFS across treatment arms. In peripheral blood samples, specific extracellular matrix components were associated with inferior outcomes in the NIVO + CABO cohort. Multivariable models integrating tumor architecture and circulating biomarkers outperformed single-feature analyses, suggesting that treatment response is governed by a complex network of interacting biological forces. Prospective validation will be essential before such approaches enter clinical practice.

Treatment sequencing adds yet another layer of complexity. Most patients who receive an ICI + TKI combination in the frontline setting are typically treated with a different VEGR TKI or belzutifan (BEL) as second-line therapy. However, attrition between lines of therapy remains substantial, and only a minority of patients ultimately reach third-line treatment. Residual toxicities and patient comorbidities clearly influence the opportunity for further treatment and physician decision-making. Until recently, the available agents in the later-line space had not been compared head-to-head.

The randomized phase II LenCabo trial offers insight into this question in the post-ICI setting. Lenvatinib plus everolimus (LEN + EV) significantly prolonged PFS compared with CABO alone among patients previously treated with ICI-based regimens (10). Response rates of 52.6% and 38.6% for LEN + EV and CABO respectively are quite encouraging. However, this again comes at the expense of increased toxicity with LEN + EV, resulting in 20% treatment discontinuation, as compared to 10% with CABO. Twelve-month survival exceeded 80% in both arms, although OS data remain immature. Notably, most enrolled patients had received either dual ICI therapy or PEM + AXI in the frontline setting, limiting extrapolation to those treated with NIVO + CABO. As second-line data mature, frontline decisions may increasingly be shaped not only by immediate efficacy but also by downstream strategy. For example, some providers may keep LEN + EV as a second-line regimen, owing to its improved efficacy and utilize NIVO + CABO in the frontline setting, whereas others (like in the scenario described above) may use PEM + LEN first in which case CABO monotherapy would be an excellent second-line option.

Additionally, interim data from the phase III LITESPARK-011 trial recently demonstrated superior PFS and ORR for the combination of LEN + BEL vs. CABO in advanced RCC following prior exposure to immunotherapy (11,12). This is the first phase III study of a hypoxia-inducible factor (HIF)-2α inhibitor plus a VEGFR-TKI and the first to show improved outcomes vs. a modern VEGFR-TKI. The safety profile of LEN + BEL was largely consistent with what is expected from each individual agent. However, one potential benefit to using BEL as a single agent in the later-line setting is its alternative mechanism of action and a respite from the substantial toxicities of VEGR-TKIs. OS data remains immature but nonetheless, this combination represents another viable treatment options for patients after progression on immunotherapy.

The question of treatment intensification also persists. The final results of the COSMIC-313 were recently reported. Here, CABO was combined with NIVO plus ipilimumab (IPI) vs. placebo plus NIVO + IPI in the frontline setting for patients with intermediate or poor risk mRCC (13). While PFS was significantly improved with the triplet [16.6 months were 11.2 months; hazard ratio (HR) 0.82, 95% confidence interval (CI): 0.69–0.98], there was no OS advantage (HR 1.02, 95% CI: 0.85–1.23, P=0.84) and rates of grade >3 treatment-related adverse events were 75% vs. 43%, respectively. When contextualized against the 60% rate of grade ≥3 adverse events observed with NIVO + CABO in CheckMate 9ER, the toxicity burden of triplet therapy appears clinically meaningful. These data caution against the adage that ‘more is always better’. The field patiently awaits the results of the triplet combination of PEM + LEN + BEL, which is being compared to PEM + LEN in a phase III trial and may lead to regulatory approval of the first triplet regimen.

In parallel, dual immune checkpoint blockade with NIVO + IPI has emerged as a biologically distinct frontline strategy, particularly in patients with intermediate and poor risk disease (14,15). Recent 9-year follow-up of the CheckMate 214 trial comparing NIVO + IPI to SUN showed that ~25% of patients remain progression free and even favorable risk patients appear to derive an OS benefit from dual ICI compared to VEGR-TKI over an extended period of follow-up (16). Thus, regardless of IMDC risk, NIVO + IPI also affords the potential for durable complete responses in patients with advanced RCC.

Within a crowded frontline landscape, the standard of care is seemingly no longer determined solely by HRs. Durability, tolerability, sequencing potential, and biologic plausibility all factor into the equation. The final CheckMate 9ER analysis solidifies NIVO plus CABO as a regimen capable of delivering sustained, clinically meaningful benefit across risk groups, particularly in patients with intermediate- and poor-risk disease. As the field advances toward biomarker-driven personalization, adaptive escalation or de-escalation of treatment, and optimized sequencing, mature datasets such as this serve as an essential anchor in an increasingly complex therapeutic era.
