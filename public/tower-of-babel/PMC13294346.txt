IUM-hybrid model for enhanced CAD diagnosis using deep learning and VS Grad-CAM visualization
Authors: C K Revathi, H Santhi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCoronary artery disease(CAD) is a serious health issue worldwide. Early identification of CAD is used to prevent several complications, such as myocardial infarction and unexpected death. In existing studies, InceptionV3 is computationally intensive and struggles with long-range dependencies, whereas MobileNetV2 faces challenges in extracting intricate features from medical-image data. Similarly, U-NetR, despite its transformer-based encoding, requires large datasets for optimal performance and is computationally expensive because of its self-attention mechanism. To overcome these limitations, this study focuses on merging InceptionV3, U-NetR, and MobileNetV2 to enhance CAD classification performance. This approach involves utilizing pre-trained models and fine-tuning them using an angiographic dataset. The hybrid IUM model incorporates dynamic weighting to maximize prediction accuracy. Furthermore, this study employed VS Grad-CAM visualization to elucidate the classifier decisions using precise heatmaps, thereby improving interpretability. This method achieved exceptional diagnostic metrics: 0.97 accuracy, 0.99 F1-score, 0.98 specificity, and 0.97 sensitivity. This novel approach enhances diagnostic precision, minimizes manual errors, and facilitates real-time applications, making it a scalable and efficient solution for clinical application. Its prompt and accurate identification of CAD has the potential to enhance patient outcomes and optimize healthcare.Keywords: CAD, Inception V3, IUM hybrid model, Mobile-NetV2, U-NetR, VS Grad-CAMSubject terms: Cardiology, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing

Introduction

Coronary artery disease (CAD) is an essential medical complication affecting global health, resulting in nearly seven million deaths per year in low- and middle-income countries1. In 2022, there were more than 699,000 deaths worldwide due to heart disease, more than seven times COVID-19 and cancer combined2. Coronary angiography remains the gold standard modality for CAD diagnosis; however, it involves considerable manual interpretation, is time-consuming, and is prone to human error. To address this challenge, deep learning (DL) has emerged as a pioneering approach for the diagnosis of medical images4. In particular, models such as InceptionV3 demonstrate excellent classification performance for associating CAD-related patterns in medical images5, whereas MobileNetV2 offers significant computational efficiency, making it suitable for real-time diagnosis6. CNN-based architectures have also shown excellent performance in identifying and classifying stenosis7.

In this study, we propose a Hybrid IUM that combines three models, InceptionV35, U-NetR14, and MobileNetV26, to enhance segmentation and classification tasks. Combining InceptionV3 for feature extraction, U-NetR for vessel segmentation, and MobileNetV2 for lightweight classification to achieve a fused output enhances the overall diagnostic accuracy and clinical utility9. Moreover, for interpretability, we utilize VS Grad-CAM to enhance feature localization, which is executed on weighted gradient maps and results in sharper and more reliable heatmaps2021,22,. This approach provides a more precise visual explanation for CAD diagnosis, supporting informed clinical decision-making. The representations of CAD and non-CAD images are shown in Fig. 1.

Related work

Coronary artery disease (CAD) remains a major global health concern, with significant research efforts directed toward improving early diagnosis using medical imaging and deep learning techniques1,2. Traditional CAD diagnosis using angiography is time-consuming and prone to variability, motivating the development of automated computer-aided diagnostic systems3.

With the advancement of deep learning (DL), feature extraction techniques have evolved significantly. Early studies have utilized convolutional neural networks (CNNs) to improve the feature representation in medical images4. Inception-based architectures have demonstrated strong capabilities in capturing multi-scale spatial features, thereby enabling improved classification performance in medical imaging tasks5. Similarly, lightweight architectures such as MobileNet have been explored to reduce computational complexity while maintaining reasonable accuracy, particularly in real-time and resource-constrained environments6.

In addition to feature extraction, segmentation of the coronary vessels plays a crucial role in CAD diagnosis and treatment. Deep CNN-based models, including ResNet variants, have been effective in extracting hierarchical features for medical-image analysis7. CNN-based stenosis detection systems have been proposed to identify coronary abnormalities; however, precise localization remains a challenge8. Benchmark studies have highlighted the trade-offs between deep and lightweight architectures in terms of their accuracy and efficiency9. The introduction of datasets such as ARCADE has supported the development of segmentation and classification frameworks, although challenges such as class imbalance and a lack of temporal information persist10.

Several approaches have focused on improving vessel segmentation accuracy. Deep learning-based segmentation methods have been successful in identifying major vessels but often struggle with small or overlapping structures11. Architectures such as AngioNet have achieved promising results; however, performance degradation has been observed in complex anatomical scenarios12. More advanced models that incorporate multiple constraints or ensemble learning strategies have improved segmentation performance but require large annotated datasets and higher computational resources13,15. Dense and Inception-based U-Net variants further enhance feature reuse but increase model complexity14.

Recently, transformer-based models have been introduced to capture global context information. Vision Transformer (ViT)-based architectures have shown strong potential in modeling long-range dependencies; however, their performance is often limited by data availability and computational cost16.

Following segmentation, classification models are used to detect and assess CAD. Deep neural networks have demonstrated effectiveness in cardiac disorder classification tasks, although they remain sensitive to data quality and noise17. The applications of MobileNet in medical diagnosis, including breast and lung cancer detection, have demonstrated its efficiency and adaptability in real-world scenarios18,19.

Interpretability is a critical requirement for medical AI systems. Gradient-based visualization techniques, such as Grad-CAM, have been widely used to highlight diagnostically relevant regions20.. Recent advancements, including Grad-CAM + + and spatially sensitive variants, have improved localization accuracy and reduced noise in activation maps21,22.

Several studies have explored advanced computational techniques to improve the performance, optimization, and reliability of these systems. The study in23 investigated analytical and computational modeling approaches, emphasizing the importance of an efficient system design and performance evaluation. Similarly24, focused on optimization strategies and modeling techniques to enhance the system efficiency and accuracy in engineering applications. In25, data-driven and intelligent methodologies were applied to improve prediction performance, highlighting the role of adaptive learning mechanisms. Furthermore26, presents recent advancements in intelligent systems and machine learning–based frameworks, demonstrating improved decision-making capabilities in complex environments. Additionally, [27] explored optimization and automation techniques to enhance the system performance and robustness in practical applications.

Although these studies contribute significantly to performance improvement, optimization, and intelligent decision-making, they do not specifically address the integration of segmentation, feature extraction, and classification within a unified deep learning framework for medical image analysis. In contrast, the proposed Hybrid IUM combines complementary architectures to provide a more effective and integrated solution for CAD diagnosis.

Methodology

This study presents a system that comprises two primary steps. First, the images were processed and split into smaller images. Next, the images were identified and labeled. First, InceptionV3 was used for multiscale feature extraction, enabling the capture of both fine and coarse details of the CAD images. Second, a U-NetR model was used to segment coronary arteries. In Stage three, the MobileNetV2 pre-trained model was applied to identify coronary arteries based on CAD incidence using patient images. A five-fold cross-validation method was implemented to test these pretrained models.

Dataset description

The dataset included X-ray angiographic images of 100 individuals who underwent coronary angiography. The dataset consisted of 8,325 grayscale images with dimensions ranging from 512 × 512 to 1000 × 1000 pixels. These images were captured using two systems: Siemens’ Coroscop and GE Healthcare’s Innova. This dataset was previously employed in the research titled “Real-time Coronary Artery Stenosis Detection Based on Modern Neural Networks” and now underpins ongoing studies on advanced image analysis methods for detecting coronary artery diseases. Although the dataset is limited in terms of patient count, it provides sufficient variability at the image level for effective training of the model. To further enhance diversity and mitigate overfitting, data augmentation techniques, such as rotation, flipping, and intensity variation, were applied during training. The inclusion of images from multiple imaging systems further supports the robustness of the model across different acquisition settings. These factors collectively provide a reliable basis for evaluating the proposed model under the specified experimental conditions.

The dataset provides sufficient variability in vessel morphology and imaging characteristics because of the inclusion of images acquired from multiple imaging systems. This diversity enhances the generalization capability of our model. Furthermore, to ensure a robust evaluation and reduce bias, 5-fold cross-validation was employed, enabling the model to be validated across multiple data splits and providing a reliable assessment of its performance.

However, the dataset splitting was performed at the image level rather than the patient level, which may introduce potential data leakage due to the presence of multiple images from the same patient. This limitation is acknowledged, and patient-level validation will be considered in future work to further strengthen model generalization and evaluation reliability.

Image preprocessing

The preprocessing stage is designed to improve the quality and consistency of angiographic images, which is crucial for the accurate segmentation and classification of coronary artery disease (CAD). Because angiographic images often suffer from low contrast, noise, and variability in acquisition conditions, appropriate preprocessing is essential to enhance feature representation. Initially, all images were converted to RGB format and resized to a fixed resolution to ensure uniformity across the dataset and enable consistent processing by deep learning models, while also reducing computational complexity without losing important structural details of coronary vessels. Subsequently, Min–Max normalization is applied to scale the pixel intensity values to a fixed range, preserving the original intensity distribution, which is critical in medical imaging, where pixel variations correspond to anatomical structures. This normalization also stabilizes model training by preventing large gradient variations and improves the feature fusion efficiency across the integrated architectures (InceptionV3, MobileNetV2, and U-NetR). Furthermore, contrast-limited adaptive histogram equalization (CLAHE) was employed to enhance the local contrast, which is particularly important in angiographic images, where vessels are often faint and difficult to distinguish. Unlike standard histogram equalization, CLAHE enhances small and thin vessel structures in localized regions while controlling the noise amplification. As a result, vessel boundaries became more prominent, leading to improved segmentation accuracy and better feature extraction for the proposed Hybrid IUM.

Feature extraction using inceptionv3

InceptionV3 is suitable for classifying coronary artery disease (CAD) because of the multi-scale features it extracts through parallel convolutions of different sizes, which enable it to discern both fine details and broader spatial patterns in angiographic images. Its early layers are used to detect low-level features, such as edges and vessel boundaries. In contrast, the mid and deep layers learn anatomical structures and more complex spatial relationships that are essential for detecting stenosis. Factorized convolutions (for example, 13 and 31) are employed to improve computational efficiency without compromising accuracy, and auxiliary classifiers are utilized to further enhance training stability. By replacing the top layers with custom classifiers and fine-tuning them on medical datasets, InceptionV3 effectively differentiates between CAD and non-CAD cases, making it an efficient and robust model for CAD classification.

Segmentation using unetr

The U-Net Transformer (UNETR) is an innovative architecture that merges Vision Transformers (ViTs) with the U-Net model to enhance medical image segmentation. By capturing both local details and the broader context, UNETR is particularly effective for binary segmentation tasks, such as distinguishing coronary artery vessels from the background in medical-image analysis.

Classification (mobilenetv2)

MobileNetV2 was chosen to classify coronary artery disease (CAD) because of its lightweight design and computational efficiency. This model is particularly suited for real-time medical applications because it employs depth-wise separable convolutions that significantly reduce the number of parameters and computational load while maintaining a high classification accuracy. One of the main benefits of MobileNetV2 is its inverted residual structure with linear bottlenecks, which improves feature propagation and minimizes the information loss. Its compact size and efficient processing make it ideal for use in resource-constrained environments, such as mobile and edge computing devices, enabling potential on-device CAD diagnoses. MobileNetV2 also exhibits strong transfer learning capabilities, allowing it to be fine-tuned on medical imaging datasets with limited training data. Its reduced parameter count decreases the risk of overfitting and enhances its robustness for medical image analysis. By combining efficiency, high accuracy, and adaptability, MobileNetV2 offers a dependable deep learning solution for CAD detection in angiographic images, supporting rapid and precise diagnosis and enabling real-time implementation.

Hybrid ium model

The architecture of the proposed IUM hybrid model is shown in Fig. 2. The proposed hybrid model integrates three advanced deep learning frameworks—InceptionV3, U-NetR, and MobileNetV2—to enhance the accuracy and reliability of classifying coronary artery disease (CAD). This method harnesses the distinct advantages of each architecture to develop a robust system that can capture high-level semantic features while maintaining detailed anatomical structures. Within this setup, InceptionV3 was tasked with identifying intricate patterns and textures associated with CAD, utilizing its multi-scale feature extraction capabilities to enhance recognition accuracy. U-NetR produces binary segmentation masks that emphasize the regions of interest (ROIs) in angiographic images. By delivering accurate anatomical details, U-NetR enhances the ability of the system to focus on essential vascular structures. MobileNetV2, similar to InceptionV3, facilitates feature extraction while prioritizing computational efficiency, making it well-suited for real-time analysis. The additional semantic data from MobileNetV2 further bolstered the ensemble feature representation. To obtain the final classification result, an average prediction strategy was employed, effectively combining the outputs of the three models.

VS GRAD-CAM

Visual Score-Weighted Gradient-weighted Class Activation Mapping (VS-GradCAM), an alternative to Grad-CAM, incorporates score-weighted activation maps to provide a more accurate and focused representation of regions of interest relevant to image classification in CAD. This also reduced background noise and false activations, outperforming Grad-CAM + + in terms of vessel structure depth. Because Grad-CAM + + requires significant computational resources, VS-GradCAM provides efficient and gradient-based interpretability, ensuring a high level of reliability when enhancing the transparency of image models and serving as a tool for clinical decision-making in angiogram analysis.

Results

Performance and evaluation metrics

The selected evaluation metrics were chosen to provide a comprehensive assessment of both the classification and segmentation performance. Accuracy, F1-score, sensitivity, and specificity were used to evaluate the classification effectiveness, ensuring a balanced assessment of positive and negative cases. For segmentation, the Intersection over Union (IoU), Hausdorff Distance– 95th percentile (HD95), and Boundary F1-score (BF1) were employed to measure region overlap, boundary accuracy, and spatial consistency, respectively. Together, these metrics provide a reliable and detailed evaluation of the proposed model’s performance.

Accuracy

Accuracy is the total correctness of a classification model; the ratio of correct predictions (TP + TN) to the total number of cases. It is a function of how well the model performs across both the positive and negative classes.

Sensitivity

Sensitivity is the degree to which the model can correctly identify true positive cases (TP/(TP + FN)). This is essential in medical diagnostics, where the absence of a true positive can besevere.

Specificity

Specificity refers to the model’s ability to correctly identify negative cases (TN/(TN + FP)). This prevents instances of true negatives from being automatically classified as positives.

F1 score

The F1 Score, that is, the harmonic mean of precision and recall, accounts for both false positives and false negatives. It is generally useful for imbalanced datasets, where both accuracy and recall are desirable.

Precision refers to the fraction of optimistic predictions that are accurately classified as genuine positive.

The ratio of accurately detected optimistic predictions to the total number of actual positive cases is called recall (or sensitivity).

Intersection over union (IoU)

The IoU measures the segmentation accuracy by the overlap between the predicted and ground truth masks. The IoU is the ratio of the intersection area to the union area of the expected and actual regions.

In our study, the Intersection over Union (IoU) metric was crucial for evaluating the performance of the U-NetR model in segmenting the coronary arteries. A higher IoU value indicates improved segmentation accuracy, ensuring that the identified regions of interest (ROIs) closely correspond to the actual vessel structure.

Experimental results

The X-ray angiography image dataset was divided into 70% for training, 15% for validation, and 15% for testing. The dataset was used in the training, testing, and validation phases, alongside the development of the hybrid IUM model. We implemented dynamic parameters (epochs = 30, momentum = 0.9, decay = 0.0005, number of training iterations = 100, maximum depth = 10, reduced dynamically, learning rate = 0.001) and an early stopping strategy during training to prevent overfitting. A deep learning network model was developed using TensorFlow in Python 3.6, with the Keras API on the front end for the proposed segmentation and classification system. The hardware setup included a GPU: NVIDIA RTX 3090/A100/Tesla V100 (24GB VRAM), RAM: 32GB+, CPU: Intel i9 (10th Gen or later)/AMD Ryzen 9, and Storage: 100GB NVMe SSD for high-speed data processing. Adam optimizer was chosen as it is adaptively adapted to different learning rates of each parameter making it appropriate for training complex hybrid models like CNN-UNETR. It can efficiently handle sparse gradients, achieve fast convergence, and demonstrate reliability against noisy updates (in contrast to other optimization algorithms, such as SGD and RMSprop). It also performed well in both segmentation (UNETR) and classification (MobileNetV2) tasks, as well as multi-scale feature extraction using InceptionV3. Several regularization strategies were employed during model training to prevent overfitting. Data augmentation techniques, including rotation, flipping, scaling, and contrast enhancement (CLAHE), were applied to increase the data diversity and improve generalization. In addition, L1/L2 regularization was incorporated to constrain the model complexity, and early stopping was used to terminate the training when the validation performance ceased to improve. Furthermore, 5-fold cross-validation was conducted to ensure a robust evaluation across different data splits and reduce the bias associated with limited datasets. The proposed model was trained on X-ray angiographic images of CAD and non-CAD cases.

To ensure a robust evaluation and reduce the risk of overfitting, 5-fold cross-validation was employed in this study. The dataset was divided into five subsets, wherein, in each iteration, four subsets were used for training and one subset was used for testing. This process was repeated across all folds, allowing the model to be evaluated on multiple independent data sets. This approach provides a more reliable and unbiased assessment of the model performance than a single train–test split.

Figure 3 illustrates the original input image, corresponding ground truth mask, and segmented output generated by the proposed system, along with the associated performance metrics. The model demonstrated strong segmentation performance, achieving a mean Intersection over Union (IoU) of 96%, indicating a high degree of overlap with the ground truth. The Hausdorff Distance at the 95th percentile (HD95) was 18, reflecting accurate localization with minor boundary deviations. In addition, the Boundary F1-score (BF1) of 89.6% indicates effective boundary detection with limited false positives and false negatives. These results confirm that the proposed system provides reliable and clinically meaningful segmentation performance while maintaining realistic evaluation criteria.

Explainable artificial intelligence (XAI)

In recent years, Explainable Artificial Intelligence (XAI) techniques have gained significant importance in cardiovascular disease (CVD) prediction and diagnosis, as they enhance model transparency and improve clinical trust. Methods such as SHAP (SHapley Additive exPlanations) and LIME (Local Interpretable Model-agnostic Explanations) provide feature-level interpretability by quantifying the contribution of individual input features to model predictions.

Recent studies have demonstrated the effectiveness of XAI-integrated frameworks in cardiovascular applications. For instance, weighted feature selection combined with ensemble classifiers has been used to improve interpretability and prediction reliability in CVD diagnosis. Similarly, deep learning-based explainable models, such as EfficientNet-based architectures for arrhythmia detection using ECG signals, provide both high accuracy and meaningful visual explanations. Other approaches, including MFCC-based heart sound classification using LSTM networks, and hybrid machine learning frameworks incorporating adaptive neural networks and fuzzy inference systems, further emphasize the importance of interpretability in medical decision-making.

VS Grad-CAM is specifically designed for deep learning models and provides visual heatmaps that highlight regions of interest influencing model predictions, making it highly suitable for medical imaging tasks. In contrast, SHAP and LIME are model-agnostic methods that explain predictions by estimating feature importance. SHAP provides theoretically consistent explanations by attributing contributions to each feature, but it is computationally intensive, especially for high-dimensional image data. LIME offers localized explanations by approximating the model behavior around a specific prediction; however, its results may vary due to random sampling and may lack stability. Compared to these methods, VS Grad-CAM is more efficient and directly interpretable for image-based applications, although it provides relatively coarse localization. Therefore, VS Grad-CAM is well-suited for enhancing transparency in CAD diagnosis while maintaining computational efficiency.

Although the proposed model demonstrates strong performance, certain limitations remain. First, the study is conducted on a relatively small dataset, which may limit the generalization capability of the model across diverse patient populations. Second, the approach is based on a single imaging modality (X-ray coronary angiography), which may not fully capture complementary diagnostic information available from other modalities such as CT or MRI. Third, the model has not been evaluated on multi-center or external datasets, and therefore its robustness across different clinical environments, imaging protocols, and acquisition devices remains to be validated. Future work will focus on incorporating larger, multi-center datasets and multimodal data to further enhance the reliability and clinical applicability of the proposed framework.

Despite the promising performance of the proposed model, several limitations should be acknowledged. First, the model is evaluated on a dataset of limited size and may not fully capture the variability present in larger and more diverse clinical populations. Second, the study is based on a single imaging modality (X-ray angiography), which may restrict the generalizability of the model to other modalities such as CT or MRI. Third, the model is trained and validated using data from a specific dataset, and its performance may vary when applied to data from different institutions or acquisition settings.

Conclusion

This work presents a hybrid IUM model that integrates InceptionV3, U-NetR, and MobileNetV2 to enhance the segmentation and classification of coronary angiographic images for coronary artery disease (CAD) detection. By combining multi-scale feature extraction, transformer-based segmentation, and lightweight classification, the proposed framework achieves high diagnostic performance while addressing challenges such as feature complexity and limited data. The incorporation of VS Grad-CAM further improves model interpretability by providing visual explanations, thereby supporting better understanding of model decisions. The experimental results demonstrate that the proposed model achieves strong performance in terms of accuracy, F1-score, sensitivity, and specificity, highlighting its potential for assisting CAD diagnosis.

Despite these promising results, further improvements can be explored to enhance the scalability and practical applicability of the proposed approach. Although the framework demonstrates potential for future real-time and clinical applications, external clinical validation and deployment-based evaluation were not conducted in the current study. Future work will extend the framework beyond 3D CT angiography by incorporating advanced learning paradigms and deployment strategies. In particular, federated learning can enable collaborative model training across multiple institutions while preserving data privacy. Additionally, self-supervised pretraining can improve feature learning from limited labeled medical data by leveraging large-scale unlabeled datasets. Furthermore, optimizing the model for edge deployment on mobile and IoT-based medical devices may facilitate real-time CAD diagnosis in resource-constrained environments. These directions aim to improve generalization, scalability, and future clinical applicability of the proposed framework.
