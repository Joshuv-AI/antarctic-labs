Durable control of recurrent dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans using wide-margin Mohs micrographic surgery and flap reconstruction without adjuvant radiotherapy: a case report and literature review
Authors: Ehsanollah Rahimi-Movaghar, Melina Ghaneiyan, Amir Ehtemami, Tahmineh Tahouri, Maryam Hosseini
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) is a rare, locally aggressive cutaneous sarcoma characterized by a high risk of local recurrence and minimal metastatic potential. However, it remains unresolved whether adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) is necessary in all cases of resectable recurrent disease when complete histologic margin clearance is achieved.Case presentation:A 41-year-old man presented with a localized recurrent DFSP after a prior wide local excision with reportedly negative margins. He underwent Mohs re-excision with 3-cm peripheral margins extending to the fascia, followed by flap reconstruction. Intraoperative frozen-section analysis confirmed microscopically negative margins. No adjuvant RT or systemic therapy was administered. Over a 4-year follow-up period, including serial MRI, no evidence of further recurrence or metastasis was noted.Clinical discussion:This case illustrates durable local control of recurrent truncal DFSP achieved solely through Mohs re-excision with confirmed negative margins and no adjuvant therapy. These findings indicate that durable oncologic control can be achieved through meticulous re-excision alone, and escalation to adjuvant RT is not invariably required in carefully selected patients if negative margins are reliably secured. Additionally, flap reconstruction enabled wide resection while optimizing cosmesis and function compared with skin grafting. Therefore, potential treatment-related morbidity can be avoided without compromising oncologic outcomes.Conclusion:Mohs micrographic surgery re-excision with confirmed negative margins achieved sustained local control over 4 years in recurrent truncal DFSP. The outcome supports individualized surgical management without adjuvant RT in selected resectable recurrent cases. Margin adequacy might serve as the primary determinant of local control while avoiding long-term radiation morbidity.

Introduction

Dermatofibrosarcoma protuberans (DFSP) is an uncommon and locally aggressive cutaneous sarcoma with a propensity for local recurrence and a low risk of metastasis[1,2]. The trunk is the most common site of involvement, although it may occur at other anatomical sites as well[3]. The standard treatment for resectable DFSP is complete surgical resection with a wide local excision (WLE) approach. Mohs micrographic surgery (MMS) has emerged as an alternative approach associated with lower recurrence rates due to precise margin control[5]. However, despite its effectiveness, the application of MMS may be limited for large or extensive tumors due to practical considerations[6]. Furthermore, adjuvant radiotherapy (RT) is often prioritized for most recurrent cases to reduce the risk of local recurrence, particularly when surgical margins are close or positive[7,8].

This case illustrates an alternative therapeutic management approach for recurrent DFSP, consisting of repeated surgical excision with adequate margins without adjuvant RT. By presenting this case, we aim to emphasize the need for individualized management strategies in recurrent disease, which remain insufficiently addressed in the existing literature. This case report has been prepared in accordance with the Surgical Case Reports (SCARE) checklist[9].

Case presentation

Patient

A 41-year-old male presented with a recurrent lesion on the anterior right chest wall. He had undergone WLE of DFSP at the same site 1 year earlier, which had been reported with tumor-free surgical margins at that time. He had no history of RT or imatinib adjuvant therapy.

Treatment

Approximately 1 year after the initial surgery, a recurrent lesion developed along the previous surgical scar (Fig. 1A). Staging imaging revealed no evidence of distant metastases. The patient underwent radical re-excision in MMS fashion with a 3-cm-wide margin extending down to the fascia (Fig. 1B). Intraoperative frozen section analysis (Fig. 2A–C) was performed to ensure clear margins. Molecular confirmation of the characteristic t(17;22) translocation was not performed, although it would not have altered management given the clear margins achieved. Reconstruction of the resulting defect was achieved immediately by employing a random-pattern Limberg rotational flap from the abdominal region to provide durable soft-tissue coverage (Fig. 3). Outcome and follow-up

The patient was followed up every 6 months during the first year and annually thereafter for a total of 4 years, with clinical evaluation and MRI imaging. No evidence of local recurrence or metastasis was observed during this period. We achieved preserved function and a satisfactory aesthetic outcome of the surgical site.

Discussion

This case report describes the successful long-term management of recurrent DFSP in accordance with the latest National Comprehensive Cancer Network guidelines[10]. The approach illustrates an individualized strategy in which surgical management alone may be sufficient for durable control in carefully selected cases of resectable recurrent DFSP, avoiding potential RT-related morbidity.

DFSP is a locally aggressive cutaneous sarcoma with low metastatic potential. Recurrence is primarily driven by residual microscopic disease, particularly after inadequate primary excision. DFSP is typically asymptomatic and grows slowly. On the other hand, the fibrosarcomatous subtype exhibits aggressive behavior, higher recurrence, and metastasis. In addition, most DFSP cases harbor an unbalanced reciprocal translocation between chromosomes t(17;22) and t(q22;q13). These genetic alterations affect the platelet-derived growth factor-B (PDGF-B) gene locus[11]. Consequently, tyrosine kinase inhibitors (e.g., imatinib mesylate) may provide therapeutic benefit by interfering with the activation of PDGF receptors[12].

Surgical excision is the first-line treatment, and achieving complete tumor removal during the first surgery reduces the risk of recurrence. If recurrence occurs, repeat surgical resection or adjuvant therapy may be considered (including radiation, imatinib)[10]. Our observations align with other reports emphasizing the curative potential of aggressive re-excision alone in recurrent DFSP when clear margins are secured. A handful of studies have reported favorable long-term outcomes for recurrent DFSP by implementing repeat WLE with a minimum 3–4 cm margin, combined with split-thickness skin-graft reconstruction and no adjuvant RT or systemic therapy[1,13,14]. Histopathology confirmed negative margins with no further recurrence during follow-up. They indicated that local control was primarily dependent on surgical adequacy rather than the routine use of adjuvant therapies, and if negative margins are confirmed using these approaches, additional adjuvant therapy may be unnecessary. Currently, MMS or other methods employing comprehensive peripheral and deep en-face margin assessment (PDEMA) are preferred over conventional WLE. Moreover, the reconstruction of the defect following WLE, while excising with an adequate margin, establishes cosmetic considerations. Although skin grafts provide relatively simple reconstruction, flap reconstruction offers superior aesthetic and function with lower scarring compared to skin grafts[15]. In addition, flaps demonstrate better tolerance to RT if the staging indicates adjuvant RT[16].

In contrast, other investigations report higher recurrence risk or advocate the routine incorporation of adjuvant RT in recurrent DFSP. For instance, Ballo et al[6] reported improved local control rates in patients receiving postoperative RT, particularly in cases with close or positive margins or multiple recurrences. Likewise, Monica et al[17] described a recurrent breast DFSP treated with WLE followed by adjuvant RT to enhance local disease control. A meta-analysis of 167 patients receiving adjuvant RT reported a pooled local recurrence rate of 11.7% overall and 14.2% among those with positive or close margins. Although comparison with surgery alone did not reach statistical significance, the authors noted a consistent trend favoring RT when margins were compromised[18].

The key outcome of this study is the absence of further local recurrence or metastasis over a 4-year follow-up period, as confirmed by serial clinical examinations and MRI. Given the elevated rates of local recurrence associated with DFSP and the mean time to recurrence of 38 months, structured long-term surveillance is essential. Although the 4-year follow-up exceeds many published case reports, DFSP is also known for late recurrences beyond 5 or even 10 years[1]. Therefore, continued clinical follow-up focusing on the primary surgical site at intervals of approximately 6–12 months is recommended, with prompt biopsy of any suspicious lesions for early detection of recurrence[10]. Furthermore, our experience also shows the necessity of surveillance of surgical scars.

In the broader context of DFSP management, this case contributes to ongoing discussions regarding overtreatment and the balance between local control and treatment-related morbidity. RT, while effective, carries potential long-term risks, including fibrosis, telangiectasia, secondary malignancies, and functional impairment, particularly in younger patients[6,7]. Demonstrating sustained disease control without RT in selected recurrent cases reinforces the importance of individualized decision-making and multidisciplinary evaluation. The findings also highlight the value of local flap reconstructive techniques in enabling wider resections while preserving functional and aesthetic outcomes.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this case demonstrates that MMS re-excision and confirmed negative margins without adjuvant therapy can achieve durable remission in recurrent truncal DFSP. These findings contribute to the evolving evidence base supporting individualized, margin-driven management strategies for recurrent DFSP while avoiding long-term radiation-related morbidity and maintaining optimal oncologic outcomes.
