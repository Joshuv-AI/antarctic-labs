A Betti Geometric Casselman–Shalika Equivalence
Colton Sandvik

Abstract
. Whittaker sheaves are ubiquitous in geometric representation theory; however, their definition requires one to restrict the sheaf-theoretic setting to either étale sheaves or D D -modules. Gaitsgory and Lysenko proposed a solution to this problem called the Kirillov model, which is well-defined for many sheaf theories. In this paper, we advance the study of the Kirillov model in the setting of Betti sheaves with a particular emphasis on developing a theory of Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves on the affine Grassmannian. Using this framework, we prove a Betti geometric Casselman–Shalika equivalence, which relates Iwahori–Whittaker perverse sheaves on the affine Grassmannian with the Satake category.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Geometric Satake

Let
\mathbbm{k}
be a commutative noetherian ring of finite global dimension.
Let
G
be a complex connected reductive group, and write
\mathcal{L}G
and
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
for its loop group and positive loop group, respectively.
We will denote the affine Grassmannian for
G
by
\Gr=\mathcal{L}G/\mathcal{L}^{+}G
.

The celebrated geometric Satake equivalence of Mirković–Vilonen
[
MV
]
gives an equivalence of symmetric monoidal categories,

P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Rep(G_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee}).

Here
P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
denotes the category of
L^{+}G
-equivariant perverse sheaves on
\Gr
, and
\Rep(G_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee})
denotes the category of representations of the Langlands dual split reductive group over
\mathbbm{k}
on finitely generated
\mathbbm{k}
-modules.
Under this equivalence, the convolution of spherical perverse sheaves corresponds to the tensor product of
G_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee}
-modules.

One of the remarkable features of the geometric Satake equivalence is the sheer generality in which it holds. Namely, it makes only minor restrictions on the coefficient ring
\mathbbm{k}
.
The equivalence also makes sense for other sheaf theories on the automorphic side. For example, while we stated the equivalence using Betti sheaves, analogous versions exist for étale and de Rham sheaves.

1.2.
The Geometric Casselman–Shalika Equivalence

We will momentarily switch our sheaf-theoretic setting to étale sheaves.
Let
\mathbb{F}
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic, and let
\mathbbm{k}
be either a finite field of characteristic
\ell\neq\textnormal{char}(\mathbb{F})
, or a finite extension of
\mathbb{Q}_{\ell}
, or the ring of integers of such an extension.
We now view
G
as being a connected reductive group over
\mathbb{F}
. Similarly, the loop group, positive loop group, and affine Grassmannian are viewed as
\mathbb{F}
-schemes.

There is another notable description of the automorphic side of the geometric Satake equivalence provided by Bezrukavnikov–Gaitsgory–Mirković–Riche–Rider
[
BGMRR
]
.
Their description gives an equivalence of categories

P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})

where
P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
denotes the category of Iwahori–Whittaker perverse sheaves on
\Gr
.
We call this the
geometric Casselman–Shalika equivalence
.

The Iwahori–Whittaker category is not visibly monoidal unlike spherical perverse sheaves; however it two key advantages over the spherical category. First, the (co-)standard objects have more explicit descriptions as Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves.
As a result, the highest weight structure on
P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is quite transparent.
Second, the realization functor

D^{b}P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{\mathcal{IW}}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})

is an equivalence of categories. Combining this with the geometric Satake equivalence, one obtains an equivalence of triangulated categories

D_{\mathcal{IW}}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\cong D^{b}\Rep(G_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee}).

As a result, the derived category of
G_{\mathbbm{k}}^{\vee}
-representations admits a geometric realization. This is in contrast with
D_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
where the spectral side is more complicated (see
[
BF
]
).

We note that a de Rham version of the geometric Casselman–Shalika equivalence
[
ABBGM
]
predates the étale version of
[
BGMRR
]
.
The restriction to the de Rham or étale setting has historically been a necessary inconvenience when working with Whittaker models.
Namely, the starting point of the theory is the existence of a non-trivial multiplicative sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
, e.g., the exponential
D
-module or an Artin–Schreier sheaf.
However, no such sheaf exists in the Betti setting. A solution to this problem has been proposed by Gaitsgory and Lysenko called the
Kirillov model

[
Gai2
,
GL
]
.
Philosophically, the solution replaces the exponential
D
-module or Artin–Schreier sheaf by the Fourier–Laumon kernel, a certain
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant sheaf on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.
This affords a version of the Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves in the Betti setting which does not require any restrictions on the coefficient ring.

We can now state the main theorem of the paper, a Betti version of the geometric Casselman–Shalika equivalence.

Theorem 1.2.1
.

There is an equivalence of categories,

P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k}).

The general strategy of proving Theorem
1.2.1
is closely modeled on that of
[
ABBGM
]
.
However, complications are added when using the Kirillov model.

(1)

There is a certain lack of symmetry in the Kirillov model. In fact, there is a
!
- and
*
-version of the Kirillov model, and Verdier duality exchanges them.
Likewise, the
!
-Kirillov model (resp.
*
-Kirillov model) only behaves well with
!
-pushforwards and
*
-pullbacks (resp.
*
-pushforwards and
!
-pullbacks).
As a result, more care has to be employed when performing sheaf-theoretic arguments. This problem is primarily formal. We will address it by introducing new sheaf functors which serve as replacements for the ones missing due to the lack of symmetry.

(2)

The additional
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariance makes certain cohomological arguments more complicated. For example, there is no obvious fully faithful forgetful functor from
D_{\mathcal{IW}}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
to
D_{\cons}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
.

(3)

The Fourier–Laumon kernel is not a local system on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. As a result, even after forgetting
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariance, one cannot use Artin vanishing theorem style arguments when studying the cohomology of a variety with respect to a Fourier–Laumon kernel.

Remark 1.2.2
.

In the final stages of preparing this paper, the author was made aware of related work of Cass, van den Hove, and Scholbach
[
CvdHS
]
that uses the Kirillov model to give a motivic-variant of Theorem
1.2.1
.
More precisely, the categories of Betti perverse sheaves can be replaced by categories of (reduced) mixed Tate motives.
T. van den Hove explained to the author that Theorem
1.2.1
can be formally deduced from
[
CvdHS
, Theorem 1.6]
by taking a Lurie tensor product over Tate motives on
\Spec(\mathbb{Q})
with Betti sheaves on a point.

2.
Affine Grassmannians and Affine Flag Varieties

2.1.
Notation

Choose a Borel subgroup
B^{-}\subset G
and let
T\subset B^{-}
denote its maximal torus.
We denote by the Borel subgroup opposite to
B^{-}
with respect to
T
by
B^{+}
, and let
U^{+}
be its unipotent radical.
Let
\Phi
be the root system of
G
, and let
\Phi^{+}\subset\Phi
be the set of positive roots consisting of
T
-weights in
\Lie(U^{+})
.
We also write
\Phi_{s}\subset\Phi
for the subset of simple roots.
Let
\mathbf{X}=X^{*}(T)
denote the set of weights of
T
,
\mathbf{X}^{+}
the set of dominant weights (with respect to
\Phi^{+}
).
We also consider the coweight lattice
\check{\mathbf{X}}=X_{*}(T)
, the dominant coweights
\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}\subset\check{\mathbf{X}}
, and the strictly dominant coweights
\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}\subset\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
.
Let

\rho=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Phi^{+}}\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}\mathbf{X}.

For each
\alpha\in\Phi_{s}
, we fix a pinning
u_{\alpha}:U_{\alpha}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{G}_{a}
, where
U_{\alpha}
is the root subgroup of
G
associated to
\alpha
.

We will assume that there exists
\zeta\in\check{\mathbf{X}}
such that
\langle\zeta,\alpha\rangle=1
for any simple root
\alpha\in\Phi_{s}
. In particular,
\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}=\zeta+\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
.
The requirement that
\zeta
exists is relatively weak and completely inconsequential from the perspective of the representation theory of
G
.
Namely, such a cocharacter always exists if
G
is semisimple and of adjoint type. We will fix
\zeta
throughout the paper.

Let
W_{f}
be the (finite) Weyl group of
(G,T)
. Define the affine and extended affine Weyl groups by
W_{\aff}\coloneq W\ltimes\mathbb{Z}\Phi^{\vee}
and
W\coloneq W_{f}\ltimes\check{\mathbf{X}}
, respectively.
The affine Weyl group is a Coxeter group whereas the extended affine Weyl group is not.
Nonetheless, the length function
\ell
on
W_{\aff}
extends to a length function on
W
.
The key obstruction to
W
being a Coxeter group is the presence of length 0 elements, denoted by
\Omega
.
The multiplication induces a group isomorphism
W_{\aff}\rtimes\Omega\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}W
.
Similarly, the Bruhat order on
W_{\aff}
extends to a partial order on
W
.

2.2.
Geometry

The loop group is the ind-scheme
\mathcal{L}G:\Sch^{\op}\to\textnormal{Set}
defined by
\mathcal{L}G(X)=X(\mathbb{C}((z)))
.
We can also consider the positive loop group
\mathcal{L}^{+}G:\Sch^{\op}\to\textnormal{Set}
defined by
\mathcal{L}^{+}G(X)=X(\mathbb{C}[[z]])
which is a group subscheme of
\mathcal{L}G
.
Let
I^{-}\subset\mathcal{L}^{+}G
denote the Iwahori subgroup associated with
B^{-}
, i.e., the subgroup defined by

I^{-}=\{g\in\mathcal{L}^{+}G\mid g|_{z=0}\in B^{-}\}.

We also denote by
I^{+}
the opposite Iwahori subgroup of
I^{-}
. The pro-unipotent radical of
I^{-}
(resp.
I^{+}
) will be denoted by
I_{u}^{+}
(resp.
I_{u}^{-}
).
We can now define the affine Grassmannian as the fppf-sheaf quotient

\Gr\coloneq\mathcal{L}G/\mathcal{L}^{+}G,

which is represented by an ind-variety.

Any
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}
defines a point
z^{\lambda}\in\mathcal{L}T\subset\mathcal{L}G
, and hence a point on the affine Grassmannian
L_{\lambda}\coloneq z^{\lambda}\mathcal{L}^{+}G/\mathcal{L}^{+}G\in\Gr
.
The Bruhat decomposition yields a decomposition of
\Gr
into
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
-orbits,

\Gr=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}\Gr^{\lambda}

where
\Gr^{\lambda}\coloneq\mathcal{L}^{+}G\cdot L_{\lambda}
.
We set
j_{\lambda}:\Gr^{\lambda}\hookrightarrow\Gr
.
Each
\Gr^{\lambda}
is smooth and simply connected, moreover, we have that
\dim\Gr^{\lambda}=\langle 2\rho,\lambda\rangle
(see
[
Lus
]
).
The closures of
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
-orbits are governed by the partial order on dominant coweights,

\overline{\Gr^{\lambda}}=\bigcup_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\Gr^{\mu}.

For
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}
, we will write
P_{\lambda}\subset G
for the parabolic subgroup of
G
containing
B^{-}
associated with the subset of simple roots
\{\alpha\in\Phi_{s}\mid\langle\alpha,\lambda\rangle=0\}
.
Note that
P_{\lambda}
is the stabilizer in
G
of
L_{\lambda}
, so that we have a canonical isomorphism
G/P_{\lambda}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}G\cdot L_{\lambda}
.
The map

p_{\lambda}:\Gr^{\lambda}\to G/P_{\lambda},\qquad x\mapsto\lim_{t\to 0}t\cdot x

where
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-acts on
\Gr
by loop rotation is a morphism of algebraic varieties. Moreover,
p_{\lambda}
realizes
\Gr^{\lambda}
as an affine bundle over
G/P_{\lambda}
(cf.,
[
NP
, Lemme 2.3]
).

For
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
we set

\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}\coloneq I^{+}\cdot L_{\lambda}\subset\Gr.

These are locally closed subvarieties and there is a decomposition

\Gr=\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}.

We will write
j_{\lambda}^{+}:\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}\hookrightarrow\Gr
for the inclusion map. Note that
\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}
is dense in
\Gr^{\lambda}
. As a result, we have that

\dim(\Gr_{\lambda}^{+})=\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle

and

\overline{\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}}=\bigcup_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\Gr_{\mu}^{+}.

2.3.
Constructible Sheaves

Let
X
be an ind-scheme of ind-finite type over
\mathbb{C}
.
We will write
D_{\cons}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
for the bounded derived category of constructible sheaves on
X
with respect to the analytic topology on
X
.
Let
H
be a group scheme of the form
H=\lim_{\leftarrow}H_{k}
for some linear algebraic groups
H_{k}
.
We denote the bounded derived category of
H
-equivariant constructible sheaves by
D_{H}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.
To ensure this is well-defined (cf.,
[
Gai1
]
), we assume the action is “nice,” i.e., every closed subscheme
Z
of
X
is contained in a larger closed subscheme
Z^{\prime}
such that
Z^{\prime}
is
H
-stable and the action of
H
on
Z^{\prime}
factors through some
H_{k}
.
We will write
P_{H}(X,\mathbbm{k})
for the heart of the perverse
t
-structure on
D_{H}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.
For simplicity, all the derived categories of sheaves in this paper are triangulated; although, our arguments are equally valid when working with stable
\infty
-categories as well.

We are primarily interested in the constructible derived category
D_{c}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
and its equivariant variations.
Let
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
.
We have three
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
-equivariant perverse sheaves associated with
\lambda
:

\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq{}^{p}H^{0}(j_{\lambda!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\Gr^{\lambda}}[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]),\quad\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq{}^{p}H^{0}(j_{\lambda*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\Gr^{\lambda}}[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]),\quad\mathcal{J}_{!*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq j_{\lambda!*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\Gr^{\lambda}}[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle].

Note that when
\mathbbm{k}
is a field,
\mathcal{J}_{!*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})
is a simple perverse sheaf.
We will recall some well-known facts about these sheaves.

Lemma 2.3.1

(​​
[
MV
, Proposition 8.1]
)
.

Let
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
.

(1)

We have isomorphisms

\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\cong\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}^{L}\left(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbb{Z})\right)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\cong\mathbbm{k}\otimes_{\mathbb{Z}}^{L}\left(\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbb{Z})\right).

(2)

There is an isomorphism

\mathbb{D}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}).

Lemma 2.3.2

(​​
[
BGMRR
, Remark 3.2]
)
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
. We have

\Ext_{P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})}^{n}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}),\mathcal{J}_{*}(\mu,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\begin{cases}\mathbbm{k}&\text{if }n=0\text{ and }\lambda=\mu;\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

3.
The Kirillov Model

The usual notion of Whittaker sheaves depends on the existence of a multiplicative local system
\Psi
on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
which satisfies
R\Gamma(\Psi)=R\Gamma_{c}(\Psi)=0
.
In the de Rham setting, one can take
\Psi
to be the exponential
D
-module. Likewise, in the étale setting, one can take
\Psi
to be an Artin–Schreier sheaf.
Unfortunately, in the Betti setting, no such local system exists since
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is contractible.
To remedy this problem, one can take inspiration from Laumon’s homogenous Fourier transform
[
Lau
]
.
In this setting, one imposes an additional
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariance on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. There are essentially two different
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant sheaves on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
that one can consider.
Let
u:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
be the obvious inclusion. The sheaves
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
and
u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
are known as the Fourier–Laumon kernels.
Laumon
[
Lau
]
proved that they satisfy a version of multiplicativity and that
R\Gamma(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1])=R\Gamma_{c}(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1])=0
.
Note that
R\Gamma(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1])
and
R\Gamma_{c}(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1])
are both nonzero, so the vanishing is not symmetric.

In this section, we will study a variation of Whittaker sheaves for general varieties that philosophically corresponds to looking at
(\mathbb{A}^{1}/\mathbb{G}_{m},u_{?}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1])
-equivariant sheaves where
?\in\{*,!\}
.
This gives rise to the
Kirillov model
introduced by Gaitsgory and Lysenko
[
Gai2
,
GL
]
. The Kirillov model has the benefit of being defined in the Betti setting with
\mathbb{Z}
-coefficients; however, it does require our varieties to admit an additional
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariance.
Unlike the étale and de Rham settings, one has to consider both a
*
- and
!
-version of the Kirillov model corresponding to the lack of symmetry between
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
and
u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
.

3.1.
Definition and First Properties

Let
X
be an ind-scheme of ind-finite type over
\mathbb{C}
. Let
H
be a group scheme of the form
H=\lim_{\leftarrow}H_{k}
for some linear algebraic groups
H_{k}
.
Assume that
H
acts nicely on
X
.
Fix a non-degenerate additive character
\chi:H\twoheadrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}
, and denote its kernel by
K_{\chi}
.
We will fix a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
H
, for example, one given by a cocharacter
\mathbb{G}_{m}\to H
.
We also assume that the
H
-action on
X
extends to an
H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
X
. Define averaging functors,

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}\coloneq\For_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}\Av_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},!}^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})

\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\coloneq\For_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}\Av_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},*}^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k}).

We define the
!
-
Kirillov model
and
*
-
Kirillov model
for
X
by

\Kir_{(H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},\chi),!}(X,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq\ker(\Ind_{!}^{\chi})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Kir_{(H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},\chi),*}(X,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq\ker(\Ind_{*}^{\chi}).

The Kirillov model depends on the choice of
\chi
. We often omit the subscript
(H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},\chi)
from the notation when obvious from context.
We may also occasionally use
?
in place of
!
or
*
in the subscript when discussing results that hold for both variations of the Kirillov model.
Note that
\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
and
\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})
are full triangulated subcategories of
D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.

Lemma 3.1.1
.

The triangulated category
\Kir_{?}(X,\mathbbm{k})
admits a
t
-structure obtained by restricting the perverse
t
-structure on
D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.

Proof.

We will just verify the lemma for the
!
-Kirillov model. The
*
-variant can be obtained by a similar argument.
We must check that for
\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
that
{}^{p}\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{F}
and
{}^{p}\tau_{\geq 0}\mathcal{F}
are both in
\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.
First, observe that
\Ind_{!}^{\chi}
is right
t
-exact, i.e.,

(1)

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\leq 0})\subseteq{}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\leq 0}.

Moreover, since
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is unipotent of dimension 1, we also have that

(2)

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\geq 0})\subseteq{}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\geq-1}.

We can now consider the distinguished triangle induced from truncation

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{F})\to\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})\to\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}\tau_{\geq 1})\to.

The middle term is
0
by assumption, so there is an isomorphism

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}\tau_{\geq 1})\cong\Ind_{!}^{\chi}({}^{p}\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{F})[1]

The left-hand side of this isomorphism is in
{}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\geq 0}
by (
2
).
The right-hand side of the isomorphism is in
{}^{p}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\leq-1}
by (
1
).
As a result, we conclude that both sides are in fact 0. Hence,
{}^{p}\tau_{\leq 0}\mathcal{F}
and
{}^{p}\tau_{\geq 0}\mathcal{F}
are objects in
\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.
∎

There are fully faithful
t
-exact functors

\iota_{!}:\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\iota_{*}:\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})

coming from
\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
and
\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})
being full subcategories of
D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.

Since there is a natural isomorphism of functors
\mathbb{D}\circ\Ind_{!}^{\chi}\cong\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\circ\mathbb{D}
, one has that Verdier duality exchanges the
!
- and
*
-Kirillov models.
In particular,
\mathbb{D}
restricts to an equivalence of categories

\mathbb{D}:\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})^{\op}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k}).

3.2.
The Prototypical Example

Before we continue with the general theory, we will take a short interlude to focus on
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant sheaves on
\mathbb{A}^{1}
.
We can define two maps

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{G}_{m}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathbb{A}^{1}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pt}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces u}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

as the obvious inclusion maps.
The sheaves

and
u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
are called the

. By
[

, they satisfy

Let
\sigma:\mathbb{A}^{2}\to\mathbb{A}^{1}
denote the map given by
(x,y)\mapsto x+y
.
We can make
\mathbb{A}^{2}
into a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-variety by
t\cdot(x,y)=(tx,ty)
. Under this action,
\sigma
is
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant.
There are two monoidal structures on

given by

- and
*
-convolution,

(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(-)\coloneq\sigma_{!}\For^{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(-\boxtimes-):D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\times D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\to D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k}),

(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}(-)\coloneq\sigma_{*}\For^{\mathbb{G}_{m}^{2}}_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}(-\boxtimes-):D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\times D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\to D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k}).

Since
\mathbb{A}^{1}
is a commutative group scheme, both of these monoidal structures are symmetric.
The monoidal unit for both
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}
and
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}
are given by

.
Verdier duality induces a symmetric monoidal equivalence of categories

\mathbb{D}:(D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})^{\op},\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}(D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k}),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}).

Lemma 3.2.1
.

There are isomorphisms

u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\cong u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\qquad\text{and}\qquad u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\cong u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1].

Proof.

We will just prove the first isomorphism. The second follows from Verdier duality.
There is an excision triangle

s_{*}\mathbbm{k}\to u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}[1]\to.

We can apply
(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
to produce a distinguished triangle

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to.

After forgetting the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariance, the last term can be identified with
\Av^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}_{!}(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}})
which vanishes by (
3
).
Therefore,
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
is an isomorphism.
∎

Example 3.2.2
.

We may consider the Kirillov model for
\mathbb{A}^{1}
. We claim that
\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
is generated as a triangulated category by
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
.
Indeed, from (
3
), one has that
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]
is in
\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
.
On the other hand, suppose that
\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
and consider the excision triangle

s_{*}s^{!}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}u^{*}\mathcal{F}\to

It follows from (

) that
u_{*}u^{*}\mathcal{F}

\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
.
In particular, we have isomorphisms

Therefore,
\mathcal{F}\cong u_{*}u^{*}\mathcal{F}

As a consequence, the functor

R\Hom(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1],-):\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\to D^{b}(\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod})

is a

-exact equivalence of categories.

A symmetric argument shows that
\Kir_{*}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
is generated

.

3.3.
Averaging and Translation Functors

We return to the general setting from §
3.1
.
We can make
D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
into a module over
(D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k}),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}})
and
(D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k}),\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}})
using both
*
- and
!
-convolution.
Let
a:H\times X\to X
denote the action map.
Note that the pullback along
\chi
defines an equivalence of categories
\chi^{*}:D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}(H,\mathbbm{k})
.
Let
\tilde{a}:H\times^{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}X\to X
be the map induced from the action map

We can then define

(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(-)\coloneq\tilde{a}_{!}(\chi^{*}(-)\tilde{\boxtimes}-):D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\times D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k}),

(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}(-)\coloneq\tilde{a}_{*}(\chi^{*}(-)\tilde{\boxtimes}-):D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\times D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k}).

Here
\chi^{*}(\mathcal{F})\tilde{\boxtimes}(-)
is the functor sending an object
\mathcal{G}
to the unique object in

whose pullback along

is isomorphic to
\chi^{*}\mathcal{F}\boxtimes\mathcal{G}\in D_{(K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m})^{2}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})

Lemma 3.3.1
.

(1)

The tautological functor
\iota_{!}:\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})\hookrightarrow D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
admits a right adjoint

\Av^{!}_{\Kir}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k}),\qquad\mathcal{F}\mapsto u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}.

\iota_{!}\Av^{!}_{\Kir}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{F}\to\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})\to

for
\mathcal{F}\in D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.

(2)

The tautological functor
\iota_{*}:\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})\hookrightarrow D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
admits a left adjoint

\Av^{*}_{\Kir}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k}),\qquad\mathcal{F}\mapsto u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}.

\Ind_{*}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})\to\mathcal{F}\to\iota_{*}\Av_{*}^{\Kir}(\mathcal{F})\to

\mathcal{F}\in D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})

Proof.

We will just prove the statement for the right adjoint of
\iota_{!}
. The left adjoint of
\iota_{*}
is similar.
Let
\mathcal{F}\in D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\to s_{*}\mathbbm{k}\to\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}[2]\to.

We can apply
(-)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}
to construct a distinguished triangle

(4)

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\to\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}}[2]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\to.

The last term of (

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})
.
We can then apply
\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})
to (
4
) to get a new distinguished triangle

\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})\to\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Ind_{!}^{\chi}\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(\mathcal{F})\to.

The second morphism is an isomorphism since

is unipotent. As a result, we must have that
\Ind_{!}^{\chi}(u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})=0
, and hence
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})

It follows from (

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}

One can readily check from these observations that

is indeed the right adjoint of

.

Define a functor

by the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k}).}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{!}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Av_{\Kir}^{*}}

This functor admits a right adjoint
T_{*}^{!}:\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
given by the composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k}).}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota_{*}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Av_{\Kir}^{!}}

In light of Lemma

,
T_{!}^{*}
(resp.

) is given by the convolution
u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}(-)
(resp.
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(-)
).
We call

and

the

. Since Verdier duality exchanges

-convolution, it follows that there is a natural isomorphism

.

Proposition 3.3.2
.

The functor
T_{!}^{*}:\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbbm{k})
is a perverse
t
-exact equivalence of categories with inverse
T_{*}^{!}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
. By Lemma
3.3.1
, there is a distinguished triangle

\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\to\mathcal{F}\to u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\to.

We can then apply
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(-)
to the above distinguished triangle to yield

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})\to.

Note that since
\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(X,\mathbbm{k})
, we have that
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F}\cong\mathcal{F}
.
We claim that
\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})
is an isomorphism. It suffices to show that
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}=0
.
Again by Lemma
3.3.1
, there is a distinguished triangle

u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\to\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eta}}{{\to}}\Ind_{!}^{\chi}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\to.

On the other hand, we have a morphism
\epsilon:\Ind_{!}^{\chi}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}\to\Ind_{*}^{\chi}
induced by the counit of the adjunction
(\Av^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},!}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}},\For^{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}})
.
By the zigzag identity,
\eta\circ\epsilon\cong\id
. Moreover,
\epsilon
is necessarily an isomorphism since
\mathbb{G}_{a}
is contractible, and so
\eta
is an isomorphism.
We deduce that
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}\Ind_{*}^{\chi}\mathcal{F}=0
, and hence that
\mathcal{F}\to u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})

By a final application of Lemma

, we see that
u_{*}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle!}}{{\star}}(u_{!}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}\mathcal{F})
can be naturally identified with
(T_{*}^{!}\circ T_{!}^{*})(\mathcal{F})
.
Therefore,
T_{*}^{!}
is a left inverse for
T_{!}^{*}
. The argument that
T_{*}^{!}
is also a right inverse follows from a symmetric argument and is omitted.

It remains to show that
T_{!}^{*}
is perverse
t
-exact. Note that
\iota_{!}
is
t
-exact and
\Av_{\Kir}^{*}
is right
t
-exact because
\iota_{*}
is
t
-exact.
As a result,
T_{!}^{*}
is right
t
-exact. Since
T_{!}^{*}
is an equivalence of categories, this is enough to deduce that
T_{!}^{*}
is
t
-exact.
∎

.

By Lemma

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(Y,\mathbbm{k})}}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f_{!}}

Lemma 3.4.1
.

With the same notation as above, there are natural isomorphisms

\mathbb{D}\circ f_{!}\cong f_{*}\circ\mathbb{D},\qquad\mathbb{D}\circ f^{*}\cong f^{!}\circ\mathbb{D},\qquad\mathbb{D}\circ f_{*}^{\Kir}\cong f_{!}^{\Kir}\circ\mathbb{D},\qquad\mathbb{D}\circ f_{\Kir}^{!}\cong f_{\Kir}^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}.

Proof.

The first two isomorphisms are immediate.
The latter two follows from the fact that
\mathbb{D}\circ\Av_{\Kir}^{!}\cong\Av_{\Kir}^{*}\circ\mathbb{D}
which can be deduced from the definitions.
∎

Definition 3.4.2
.

We say that

admits an

-equivariant compactification
if there exists an ind-scheme of ind-finite type
\overline{X}
with a nice
H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action such that
f
factors as

where

is an
H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant open embedding and
\pi:\overline{X}\to Y
is an
H\times\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant proper map.

Lemma 3.4.3
.

Assume that
f:X\to Y
admits an
H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
-equivariant compactification. Then there are natural isomorphisms

T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{!}\cong f_{!}^{\Kir}\circ T_{!}^{*},\qquad T_{!}^{*}\circ f^{*}\cong f_{\Kir}^{*}\circ T_{!}^{*},\qquad T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{*}^{\Kir}\cong f_{*}\circ T_{!}^{*},\qquad T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{\Kir}^{!}\cong f^{!}\circ T_{!}^{*}.

Proof.

Since
f
admits a compactification, we only have to check these isomorphisms hold when
f
is proper or
f
is an open embedding.

First, assume that
f
is proper. It is easy to check from functorality that
f_{*}=f_{!}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(Y,\mathbbm{k})
is
D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
-linear with respect to the
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}
-action.
In this case,
f_{!}^{\Kir}=f_{!}
and
f_{*}^{\Kir}=f_{*}
, and so we can conclude that
T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{!}\cong f_{!}^{\Kir}\circ T_{!}^{*}
and
T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{*}^{\Kir}\cong f_{*}\circ T_{!}^{*}
.
By taking adjoints and using Proposition
3.3.2
, we deduce that
T_{!}^{*}\circ f^{*}\cong f_{\Kir}^{*}\circ T_{!}^{*}
and
T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{\Kir}^{!}\cong f^{!}\circ T_{!}^{*}
as well.

Now assume that

is open. By base change,
f^{*}=f^{!}:D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(Y,\mathbbm{k})\to D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbbm{k})
is
D_{\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})
-linear with respect to the
\stackrel{{\scriptstyle*}}{{\star}}
-action.
Moreover,
f_{\Kir}^{*}=f^{*}
and
f_{\Kir}^{!}=f^{!}
. It then follows from the definitions that
T_{!}^{*}\circ f^{*}\cong f_{\Kir}^{*}\circ T_{!}^{*}
and
T_{!}^{*}\circ f_{\Kir}^{!}\cong f^{!}\circ T_{!}^{*}
.
The remaining isomorphisms follow from taking adjoints and Proposition
3.3.2
.
∎

3.5.
Riemann–Hilbert Correspondence

For an ind-variety
X
with a nice
H
-action, we denote the bounded derived category of holonomic
D
-modules by
D\textnormal{-mod}_{H}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)
. We will also write
D\textnormal{-mod}_{H}^{\textnormal{rh}}(X)
for the full subcategory of
D\textnormal{-mod}_{H}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)

consisting of regular holonomic
D
-modules.

Let
\mathbbm{k}=\mathbb{C}
. We can apply the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence to change our sheaf-theoretic setting to that of regular holonomic
D
-modules.
In particular, there is a commutative square

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbb{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{b}(X,\mathbb{C})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\textnormal{-mod}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\textnormal{rh}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\textnormal{-mod}_{H\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\textnormal{rh}}(X).}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ind_{!}^{\chi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Ind_{!}^{\chi}}

The definition of the Kirillov model can be made regardless of sheaf-theoretic setting, so we can consider the
D
-module variant,

\Kir_{*}^{\dR}(X)\coloneq\ker\left(\Ind_{*}^{\chi}:D\textnormal{-mod}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)\to D\textnormal{-mod}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)\right).

Here we are using holonomic
D
-modules so that we can later compare with the Whittaker model.
Then the above commutative square gives a
t
-exact fully faithful functor

(5)

\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\Kir_{*}^{\dR}(X).

On the other hand, in the de Rham setting there is an exponential
D
-module, denoted
\exp\in D\textnormal{-mod}^{\textnormal{hol}}(\mathbb{A}^{1})
.
We define the
Whittaker model
for
X
,

\Whit^{\dR}(X)\coloneq D\textnormal{-mod}_{(H,\chi^{*}\exp)}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X),

as the category of
(H,\chi^{*}\exp)
-equivariant holonomic
D
-modules on
X
.

Consider the composition of functors

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Whit^{\dR}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\textnormal{-mod}_{K_{\chi}}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D\textnormal{-mod}_{K_{\chi}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}}^{\textnormal{hol}}(X)}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Av_{*}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}}

where the first arrow is the forgetful functor. Since
\Av_{*}^{\mathbb{G}_{m}}
commutes with
\Av_{*}^{\mathbb{G}_{a}}
, it is easy to see that the image of this composition belongs to
\Kir_{*}^{\dR}(X)
.
In particular, there is a functor

(6)

\Whit^{\dR}(X)\to\Kir_{*}^{\dR}(X).

It is shown in
[

, §1.6.3]
(see also
[
GL
, Proposition A.3.2]
) that (

) is an equivalence of categories.

Composing the functors (

) and (
6
), we obtain a fully faithful functor

\mathcal{S}:\Kir_{*}(X,\mathbb{C})\hookrightarrow\Whit^{\dR}(X).

Note that
\mathcal{S}
commutes with
!
-pullbacks and
*
-pushforwards.

In general, there is no reason to believe that
\mathcal{S}
is essentially surjective. We will later see that this holds at least for the affine Grassmannian.
The author expects that
\mathcal{S}
is

-exact in general, but such a proof will not be provided. In the special case of the affine Grassmannian, the

-exactness will be proved in the next section.

4.
Iwahori–Whittaker Sheaves

The goal of this section is to apply the Kirillov model in two important cases: partial (finite) flag varieties and the affine Grassmannian.

4.1.
Whittaker Sheaves on Finite Flag Varieties

Let
P\subset G
be a parabolic subgroup containing
B^{-}
with Levi subgroup
L
.
Let
W_{L}\subset W
be the Weyl group of
L
with respect to

, and denote the set of minimal length coset representatives for

by

.

Consider the character
\chi:U^{+}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
defined by the composition

U^{+}\to U^{+}/[U^{+},U^{+}]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\prod_{\alpha}u_{\alpha}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\prod_{\alpha\in\Phi_{s}}\mathbb{G}_{a}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\textnormal{sum}}}{{\to}}\mathbb{G}_{a}.

Write
U_{\chi}^{+}=\ker\chi
. The cocharacter
\zeta:\mathbb{G}_{m}\to T
affords an action of
U^{+}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}
on the partial flag variety

.

Consider the
!
-Kirillov model on

with respect to
(U^{+}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},\chi)

D_{\Wh,!}^{b}(G/P,\mathbbm{k})\coloneq\Kir_{(U^{+}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m},\chi),!}(G/P,\mathbbm{k}).

The Bruhat decomposition gives a stratification of

into
B^{+}
-orbits,

for some lift

of

Note that the

Before proceeding with the general theory, we can focus on the case of

. Let

denote the inclusion map.
Note that
X_{e}^{B^{-}}\cong U^{+}
as

-varieties.
As a result,
\chi
induces an isomorphism

\overline{\chi}:U_{\chi}^{+}\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{m}\backslash X_{e}^{B^{-}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\mathbb{G}_{m}\backslash\mathbb{A}^{1}

acts on

by dilation. We then have a functor

\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\overline{\chi}^{*}}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Kir_{!}(X_{e}^{B^{-}},\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle h_{e!}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D_{\Wh,!}^{b}(G/B^{-},\mathbbm{k}).

Proposition 4.1.1
.

(1)

If

, then
\Kir_{!}(X_{w}^{P},\mathbbm{k})=0
. In particular,

.

(2)

If

, then the following composition

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces D^{b}(\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{!}(\mathbb{A}^{1},\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\Kir_{!}(X_{e}^{B^{-}},\mathbbm{k})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{D_{\Wh,!}^{b}(G/B^{-},\mathbbm{k})}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\sim}

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h_{e!}}

-exact equivalence of categories.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{F}\in\Kir_{!}(X_{w}^{P},\mathbbm{k})
. Note that for all
\beta\in\Phi^{+}
, we have that

is stable under the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action.
If

or

, we can find a simple root

such that

.
Since

to be

-equivariant.
Note that

We have now shown (1) and the equivalence

. It remains to show that

is a

-exact equivalence of categories.
This follows from the discussion found in

, §5.8.4]
.
∎

-Kirillov model on

is an equivalence of categories.

It is also true that
P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is a highest weight category; however, the argument is rather involved since the
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
-orbits are not affine spaces.
The key difference with Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves is that the highest weight structure is essentially immediate from abstract sheaf theory (see
[
BGS
, §3.3]
).
The second point is blatantly false when Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves is replaced by
\mathcal{L}^{+}G
-equivariant sheaves.
The goal of this subsection is to prove these two statements.

For
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
M\in\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg}
, we set

\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)\coloneq(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{!}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{!}(\mathbbm{k})[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)\coloneq(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{*}^{\Kir}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{!}(M)[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle].

These sheaves are called the
standard
and
costandard
Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves in
D_{\mathcal{IW},!}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
.
Note that the standard sheaves are perverse by
[
BBDG
, Corollaire 4.1.3]
and the costandards sheaves are perverse by Lemma
3.4.5
.
We can analogously define standard and costandard Iwahori–Whittaker sheaves in
D_{\mathcal{IW},*}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
,

\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)\coloneq(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{!}^{\Kir}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{*}(M)[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]\qquad\text{and}\qquad\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)\coloneq(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{*}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{*}(M)[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle].

By Lemma
3.4.3
and Example
3.3.3
, there are isomorphisms

(8)

T_{!}^{*}\left(\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)\right)\cong\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)\qquad\text{and}\qquad T_{!}^{*}\left(\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)\right)\cong\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M).

Likewise, the standard and costandard sheaves are related by Verdier duality by the isomorphisms

(9)

\mathbb{D}\left(\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(\mathbbm{k})\right)\cong\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(\mathbbm{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\mathbb{D}\left(\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(\mathbbm{k})\right)\cong\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(\mathbbm{k}).

We will almost always drop the
*
- and
!
-superscripts from the notation of the standard and costandard sheaves. One can infer from context whether the objects live in the
!
- or
*
-Kirillov model.

For
\lambda,\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
, a standard argument (cf.,
[
BGS
, §3.3]
) using Lemma
3.4.4
and Proposition
4.2.2
shows that

(10)

\Hom_{D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})}(\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M),\nabla_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(N)[n])\cong\begin{cases}\Hom_{D^{b}(\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg})}(M,N[n])&\text{if }\lambda=\mu,\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

The natural transformation
(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{!}\to(j_{\lambda}^{+})_{*}^{\mathcal{IW}}
induced by adjunction gives a canonical morphism in
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)\to\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)
which generates the Hom-space as a
\mathbbm{k}
-module when
M=\mathbbm{k}
.
We will denote by
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},!}(M)
the image of this generator.
Likewise, there is a canonical morphism
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)\to\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)
induced by adjunction that generates its Hom-space as a
\mathbbm{k}
-module when
M=\mathbbm{k}
.
We denote the image of this map by
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW},*}(M)
.
These sheaves are called the
IC sheaves
.
As with the standard and costandard objects, we will often drop the
!
- or
*
-superscript from the notation of the IC sheaves.
When
\mathbbm{k}
is a field, the objects
\{\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}}
form a complete set of pairwise non-isomorphic simple objects of the finite-length abelian category
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
(see
[
BBDG
, Proposition 1.4.26]
).

Note that since
\zeta
is minimal in
\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
under the partial order
\preceq
, we have that

(11)

\Delta_{\zeta}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)\cong\IC_{\zeta}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)\cong\nabla_{\zeta}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M).

Lemma 4.3.1
.

D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is generated by the set of standard sheaves
\{\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}}
, or alternatively, the set of costandard sheaves
\{\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}}
.

Proof.

The lemma is a routine application of recollement (Lemma
3.4.4
) and Proposition
4.2.2
.
∎

Lemma 4.3.2
.

Assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is a field.
The perverse
t
-structure on
D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is uniquely characterized by each of the following statements:

(1)

{}^{p}D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})^{\leq 0}
is generated under extensions by
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})[n]
with
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
n\geq 0
.

(2)

{}^{p}D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})^{\geq 0}
is generated under extensions by
\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})[n]
with
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
n\leq 0
.

Proof.

Let
\text{D}^{\leq 0}\subseteq D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
be the smallest full subcategory that is stable under extensions and contains all
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})[n]
for
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
n\geq 0
.
Likewise, let
\text{D}^{\geq 0}\subseteq D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
be the smallest full subcategory that is stable under extensions and contains all
\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})[n]
for
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
n\leq 0
.
It is easy to see that
\text{D}^{\leq 0}\subset{}^{p}D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})^{\leq 0}
and
\text{D}^{\geq 0}\subset{}^{p}D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})^{\geq 0}
.
To see that these containments are in fact equalities, it suffices to show that
(\text{D}^{\leq 0},\text{D}^{\geq 0})
defines a
t
-structure on
D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
.
This follows from
[
Bez1
, Proposition 1]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3.3
.

Assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is a field.
The category
P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is a highest weight category with weight poset
(\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++},\preceq)
, standard objects
\{\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}}
, and costandard objects
\{\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}}
.

Proof.

The lemma follows from a standard argument using (
10
). See
[
BGS
, §3.3]
.
∎

Lemma 4.3.4
.

The functor

\mathcal{S}:D_{\mathcal{IW},*}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbb{C})\to\Whit^{\dR}(\Gr)

is a
t
-exact equivalence of categories.

Proof.

All the constructions and results so far from this section make sense for
\Whit^{\dR}(\Gr)
as well (see
[
ABBGM
]
).
It can be easily checked that
\mathcal{S}
takes costandard sheaves to costandard sheaves. By Lemma
4.3.1
and Lemma
4.3.2
, this implies that
\mathcal{S}
is left
t
-exact and essentially surjective.
We had already seen that
\mathcal{S}
is fully faithful in §
3.5
; therefore,
\mathcal{S}
is an equivalence of categories.
The right
t
-exactness is then determined by the left
t
-exactness and the fact that
\mathcal{S}
is an equivalence of categories.
∎

Lemma 4.3.5
.

Let

.
The result then follows from Lemma
4.3.4
and

.
∎

Remark 4.3.6
.

It should be possible to give a proof of Lemma
4.3.5
which avoids using the Riemann–Hilbert correspondence.
One strategy would be to prove that
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}
are parity sheaves in the sense of
[
JMW
]
.
Unfortunately, known methods for proving this (for example
[
BGMRR
, Lemma 3.4]
) make use of the decomposition theorem.
The decomposition theorem cannot be directly employed since the Fourier–Laumon kernel is not a simple perverse sheaf.

We say that an object
\mathcal{F}
of
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
has a
standard filtration
(resp.
costandard filtration
) if it admits a filtration whose subquotients are of the form
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
(resp.
\nabla_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
) for
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
.
When
\mathbbm{k}
is a field, this notion agrees with the usual notion of (co-)standard filtrations in highest weight categories.
Note that when
\mathbbm{k}
is not a field, we are only allowing
\mathbbm{k}
-free (co-)standard objects to appear as subquotients in the filtration.

For
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
, we will write

\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+}=\bigcup_{\mu\prec\lambda}\Gr_{\mu}^{+}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+}=\bigcup_{\mu\preceq\lambda}\Gr_{\mu}^{+}.

For a locally closed union
X
of finitely many
I^{+}
-orbits in
\Gr
, we will write
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(X,\mathbbm{k})
for the perverse heart
\Kir_{?}(X,\mathbbm{k})
.
The costandard, standard, and IC objects indexed by
\mu\prec\lambda
(resp.
\mu\preceq\lambda
) for
\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
can naturally be regarded as objects in
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
(resp.
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
).

Lemma 4.3.7
.

Any object of
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is a successive extension of
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
for some
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules
M
.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is closely based on
[
RSW
, Lemma 2.1.4]
. Since
loc. cit.
only works with
\mathbbm{k}
being a Dedekind domain and since our theory of IC-sheaves is slightly exotic, we will provide the necessary details.
We will just prove the case when
?=!
as the case of
?=*
is symmetric.
Since every sheaf
\mathcal{F}\in P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
is in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
for some
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
, we can prove the lemma using the following inductive refinement of the lemma.

(
IH
) Every object
\mathcal{F}
in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
is a successive extension of
\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
for some
\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
with
\mu\preceq\lambda
and indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules
M
.

The base case when
\lambda=0
follows from Proposition
4.2.2
.
Let
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
. Write
i:\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+}
and
j:\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}\hookrightarrow\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+}
for the inclusion maps.
The functor
i_{*}:P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})\to P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
is fully faithful, so we may assume by induction that any object in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
is a successive extension of
\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
for some
\mu\prec\lambda
and indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules
M
.
Let
\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary object in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
.
By
[
BBDG
, Proposition 1.4.17 (ii)]
, the natural morphism
\mathcal{F}\to i_{*}{}^{p}H^{0}(i^{*}\mathcal{F})
induced by adjunction is surjective. We denote its kernel by
\mathcal{K}
, and consider the short exact sequence of perverse sheaves

0\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{F}\to i_{*}{}^{p}H^{0}(i^{*}\mathcal{F})\to 0.

By induction, it then suffices to prove that
\mathcal{K}
is a successive extension of
\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
for some
\mu\preceq\lambda
and indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules
M
.
Note that
\mathcal{K}
has no non-zero quotient supported on
\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}
. By Lemma
3.4.3
and
[
BBDG
, Proposition 1.4.17 (ii)]
, the natural morphism
i_{*}{}^{p}H^{0}(i_{\Kir}^{!}\mathcal{K})\to\mathcal{K}
is injective. We denote its cokernel by
\mathcal{Q}
.
There is then a short exact sequence

0\to i_{*}{}^{p}H^{0}(i_{\Kir}^{!}\mathcal{K})\to\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{Q}\to 0.

By induction, it suffices to prove that
\mathcal{Q}
is a successive extension of
\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
for some
\mu\preceq\lambda
and indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules
M
.
Moreover,
\mathcal{Q}
has no non-trivial subobject or quotient supported on
\Gr_{\lambda}^{+}
.
Define the intermediate-extension functor

j_{!*}^{\Kir}:P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})\to P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k}),\qquad\mathcal{F}\mapsto\im(j_{!}\mathcal{F}\to j_{*}^{\Kir}\mathcal{F}).

Note that
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)=j_{!*}^{\Kir}\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{!}(M)[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]
.
The characterization of IC sheaves given in
[
BBDG
, Corollaire 1.4.25]
can be easily translated to our setting, and in particular, we conclude that there is an isomorphism
\mathcal{Q}\cong j_{!*}^{\Kir}j^{*}\mathcal{Q}
.
By Proposition
4.2.2
and Proposition
4.1.1
, we must have that
(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{*}\mathcal{Q}
is either 0 (when
\lambda\notin\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
) or of the form
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{!}(M)[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]
where
M\in\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg}
.
Of course,
M
is a direct sum of indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-modules, and hence, the lemma follows.
∎

Lemma 4.3.8
.

For all
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
, there exists a projective object
\mathcal{P}
in
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
which admits a standard filtration and a surjection
\mathcal{P}\twoheadrightarrow\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.

Proof.

The proof of the lemma is closely based on
[
RSW
, Proposition 2.3.1]
. By Lemma
3.4.3
and Proposition
3.3.2
, it suffices to just prove the
!
-Kirillov variant.
As in Lemma
4.3.7
, we will prove the lemma using the following inductive refinement.

(
IH
) For all
\mu\preceq\lambda
with
\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
, there exists a projective object
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\in P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
which admits a standard filtration and a surjection
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\to\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.

The base case when
\lambda=0
is obvious. Let
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
and assume the inductive hypothesis holds for all
\lambda^{\prime}\prec\lambda
.
We define
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}=\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
when
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
.
It is clear from its definition that
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
has a standard filtration and admits a surjective morphism
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
We can first check that
\mathcal{P}_{\lambda}
is projective. For any
\mathcal{F}\in P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
, there is an isomorphism

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\lambda},\mathcal{F})\cong\Hom_{\Kir_{!}(\Gr_{\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})}(\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{!}[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle],(j_{\lambda}^{+})^{*}\mathcal{F}[1])

The right-hand side vanishes by Proposition
4.2.2
and Proposition
4.1.1
.

Now take
\mu\prec\lambda
. By induction, we have a projective object
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda}
in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
which admits a standard filtration and a surjective morphism to
\Delta_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
It remains to check that
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda}
can be enlarged to a projective object in
P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
which admits a standard filtration and a surjective morphism to
\Delta_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
To do so, consider
E=\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda},\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))
and let
E_{\textnormal{free}}
be a finitely generated free
\mathbbm{k}
-module with a surjection
E_{\textnormal{free}}\twoheadrightarrow E
.
Let
E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}
denote the dual
\mathbbm{k}
-module of
E_{\textnormal{free}}
.
The sequence of canonical morphisms

\mathbbm{k}\to E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}E_{\textnormal{free}}\to E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}E\cong\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda},E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))

gives rise to a distinguished extension

0\to E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\to\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\to\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda}\to 0.

By induction
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
has a standard filtration. Moreover, it admits a surjection to
\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
via the composition
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda}\twoheadrightarrow\IC_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
To complete the proof of the lemma, it remains to check that
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is projective.

Step 1.

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\mathcal{F})=0
for
\mathcal{F}\in P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
. From the definition of
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
, there is a long exact sequence

\ldots\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda},\mathcal{F})\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\mathcal{F})\to\Ext^{1}(E_{\textnormal{free}}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\mathcal{F})\to\ldots.

The desired vanishing then follows from
\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
being projective in
P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
and
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda}
being projective in
P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr_{\prec\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
.

Step 2.

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))=0
. There is a long exact sequence

\displaystyle\ldots\to\Hom(E_{\textnormal{free}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})})\to\Ext^{1}

\displaystyle(\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda},\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))

\displaystyle\to\Ext^{1}(E_{\textnormal{free}^{*}}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))\to\ldots

By adjunction, one has that
\Ext^{1}(\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))=0
, so it suffices to show that the first map is surjective.
However, this map can be explicitly described under the canonical isomorphism

\Hom(E_{\textnormal{free}^{*}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})})\cong E_{\textnormal{free}}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}(\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))\cong E_{\textnormal{free}}

as the map
E_{\textnormal{free}}\to E=\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu}^{\prec\lambda},\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))
, which is surjective by construction.

Step 3.

\Hom(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\mathcal{F}[2])=0
for all
\mathcal{F}\in P_{\mathcal{IW}}(\Gr_{\preceq\lambda}^{+},\mathbbm{k})
. By Lemma
4.3.7
, it suffices to show that
\Hom(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\IC_{\nu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M))=0
for all
\nu\preceq\lambda
and
M
finitely generated
\mathbbm{k}
-modules.
Let
\mathcal{Q}
denote the cokernel of the canonical inclusion
\IC_{\nu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)\to\nabla_{\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
. We then have a long exact sequence

\ldots\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\mathcal{Q})\to\Hom(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\IC_{\nu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)[2])\to\Hom(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\nabla_{\nu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)[2])\to\ldots.

The first term vanishes from Step 1. Since
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
has a standard filtration, the third term will vanish by (
10
).

Step 4.

\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M))=0
for all
M\in\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg}
.
We can find a finitely generated free
\mathbbm{k}
-module
M_{\textnormal{free}}
with a surjection
M_{\textnormal{free}}\twoheadrightarrow M
.
It can be checked as in
[
Ach
, Lemma 3.3.5]
that the functor
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(-):\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg}\to P_{\mathcal{IW},!}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
taking
N
to
\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(N)
takes surjective maps to surjective maps.
In particular, there is a surjective map
M_{\textnormal{free}}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\to\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
. We denote its kernel by
\mathcal{K}
.
We can now consider the long exact sequence

\ldots\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},M_{\textnormal{free}}\otimes_{\mathbbm{k}}\Delta_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))\to\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M))\to\Hom(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\mathcal{K}[2])\to\ldots.

The first and third terms are zero by Steps 2 and 3 respectively.

Finally, we have seen from Steps 1 and 4 that
\Ext^{1}(\mathcal{P}_{\mu},\IC_{\nu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M))=0
for all
\nu\preceq\lambda
and
M\in\mathbbm{k}\textnormal{-mod}^{\fg}
. We conclude from Lemma
4.3.7
that
\mathcal{P}_{\mu}
is projective.
∎

Lemma 4.3.9
.

For any
\mathcal{F}\in P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
, there exists a projective object
\mathcal{P}
in
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
which admits a standard filtration and a surjection
\mathcal{P}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{F}
.
In particular, there are enough projective objects in
P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.3.7
, we may assume that
\mathcal{F}=\IC_{\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(M)
where
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{++}
and
M
is an indecomposable
\mathbbm{k}
-module.
When
M=\mathbbm{k}
, the desired projective object can be constructed by Lemma
4.3.8
.
For a general
M
, we can find a finitely generated free
\mathbbm{k}
-module
M_{\textnormal{free}}
along with a surjection
M_{\textnormal{free}}\twoheadrightarrow M
.

Proposition 4.3.10
.

The realization functor

Proof.

Our proof will closely follow

. By a standard homological argument, it suffices to prove that the natural morphism

with a standard filtration, and

If

By Lemma

j_{\lambda!}\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{\Gr^{\lambda}}[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]\to\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbb{C})
induces an isomorphism

H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle}(S_{\mu},(j_{\lambda!}\underline{\mathbb{C}}_{\Gr^{\lambda}})|_{S_{\mu}}\otimes^{L}(\chi_{\mu}^{\prime})^{*}\exp)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}H_{c}^{\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle}(S_{\mu},\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbb{C})|_{S_{\mu}}\otimes^{L}(\chi_{\mu}^{\prime})^{*}\exp).

By base change, the left-hand side identifies with
H_{c}^{\langle\mu,2\rho\rangle}(\Gr^{\lambda}\cap S_{\mu},((\chi_{\mu}^{\prime})^{*}\exp)|_{\Gr^{\lambda}\cap S_{\mu}})
.
However, since
\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathcal{J}_{!*}(\lambda,\mathbb{C})
(see for example
[
Gai1
, Proposition 1]
), the right-hand side vanishes by the geometric Casselman–Shalika formula (
15
).
This contradicts our assumption that
\chi_{\mu}(Z^{\prime})=0
which completes the proof.
∎

Lemma 5.3.3
.

Let
\lambda,\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
such that
\lambda\neq\mu
, then

H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n}(Y_{\lambda,\mu},((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})=0

for all
n>0
.

Proof.

Consider the excision triangle

s_{*}^{\prime}\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}^{0}}[-2]\to\underline{\mathbbm{k}}_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}}\to((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}}\to.

We then get a long exact sequence

\ldots\to H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n}(Y_{\lambda,\mu},\mathbbm{k})\to H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n}(Y_{\lambda,\mu},((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})\to H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n-1}(Y_{\lambda,\mu}^{0},\mathbbm{k})\to\ldots.

Recall that
Y_{\lambda,\mu}
is a subvariety of the Mirković–Vilonen cycle
\Gr^{\lambda}\cap S_{\mu}
.
By Lemma
5.3.2
, we have that
\dim Y_{\lambda,\nu}^{0}<\langle\lambda+\mu,\rho\rangle
.
Moreover, in the proof of Lemma
5.3.2
, we saw that
\dim Y_{\lambda,\nu}\leq\langle\lambda+\mu,\rho\rangle
.
Therefore, we conclude that both
H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n}(Y_{\lambda,\mu},\mathbbm{k})=0
and
H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+n-1}(Y_{\lambda,\mu}^{0},\mathbbm{k})=0
.
The desired vanishing then follows from exactness.
∎

Lemma 5.3.4
.

Assume that
\mathbbm{k}
is a field. For any
\lambda,\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
with
\lambda\neq\mu
we have

\Hom_{P_{\mathcal{IW},*}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})}(\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})),\nabla_{\zeta+\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}))=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
5.3.1
, it suffices to prove that

H^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+1}\left(R\Gamma_{c,\mathbb{G}_{m}}(((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})\right)=0.

We can compute this cohomology by taking the long exact sequence of the distinguished triangle from Lemma
5.2.1
,

\displaystyle\ldots\to H_{c}^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+1}\left(Y_{\lambda,\mu},(((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})\right)

\displaystyle\to H^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+1}\left(R\Gamma_{c,\mathbb{G}_{m}}(((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})\right)

\displaystyle\to H^{\langle\lambda+\mu,2\rho\rangle+3}\left(R\Gamma_{c,\mathbb{G}_{m}}(((\chi_{\mu}^{\zeta})^{*}u_{*}\mathbbm{k})|_{Y_{\lambda,\mu}})\right)\to\ldots

The first term vanishes by Lemma
5.3.3
while the last term vanishes by Lemma
5.3.1
.
The lemma then follows by exactness.
∎

Proposition 5.3.5
.

For
\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
, there are isomorphisms

\Phi_{?}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Phi_{?}(\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\nabla_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}).

Proof.

We will first prove that
\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.

Let
\tau:\mathcal{L}^{+}G\to\Gr
be the projection.
We will write
m_{\zeta,\lambda}
for the restriction of the multiplication map
\mathcal{L}G\times^{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}\Gr\to\Gr
to
\tau^{-1}(\overline{\Gr^{\zeta}})\times^{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}\overline{\Gr^{\lambda}}
.
It is explained in the proof of
[
BGMRR
, Theorem 3.9]
that the following hold:

•

m_{\zeta,\lambda}
takes values in
\overline{\Gr^{\zeta+\lambda}}=\overline{\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}}
;

•

the restriction of
m_{\zeta,\lambda}
to the preimage of
\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}
is an isomorphism;

•

this preimage is contained in
\tau^{-1}(\Gr_{\zeta}^{+})\times^{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}\Gr^{\lambda}
.

One can then deduce that
\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))
is supported on
\overline{\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}}
and that its restriction to
\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}
is isomorphic to
\mathcal{K}_{\lambda}^{*}(\mathbbm{k})[\langle\lambda,2\rho\rangle]
.
Therefore, by Lemma
3.4.4
, there is a canonical morphism

f_{\lambda}^{\mathbbm{k}}:\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})\to\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))

whose restriction to
\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}
is an isomorphism.

We claim that
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbbm{k}}
is an isomorphism.
Since our constructions are compatible with extension-of-scalars (see Lemma
2.3.1
and Lemma
3.4.6
), it suffices to prove that
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is an isomorphism.
If
\mathbbm{k}\in\{\mathbb{Q},\mathbb{F}_{p}\}
, by Lemma
5.3.4
and Lemma
4.3.3
, we know that
\Phi_{*}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))
has no quotient of the form
\IC_{\zeta+\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
with
\mu\neq\lambda
; therefore,
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbbm{k}}
is surjective.
Since
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{F}_{p}}
is surjective for all primes
p
, we can deduce that
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is also surjective.
Note that by Lemma
4.3.5
,
\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbb{Q})
is simple, as a result
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Q}}
being surjective forces
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Q}}
to be an isomorphism.
In particular,
\ker(f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is a torsion object.
However,
\ker(f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is a subobject of
\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbb{Q})
which is torsion free; therefore,
\ker(f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}})
is also torsion free and hence
0
.
We conclude that
f_{\lambda}^{\mathbb{Z}}
is an isomorphism.

From the first case, by Lemma
2.3.1
and Lemma
4.2.1
, we can deduce that
\Phi_{!}(\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}))\cong\nabla_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})
.
The other two isomorphism then follow from the already established isomorphisms using Lemma
4.2.1
, Lemma
3.4.3
, and Example
3.3.3
.
∎

5.4.
Completing the Proof

Proof of Theorem
5.0.1
.

It suffices to prove that the induced functor

D^{b}P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle D^{b}\Phi_{?}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D^{b}P_{\mathcal{IW},?}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\ref{prop:realization_is_equivalence}}}{{\longrightarrow}}D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})

is an equivalence of categories. It is easy to see that
D^{b}P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
(resp.
D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})
) is generated as a triangulated category by both
\{\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}
and
\{\mathcal{J}_{*}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}
(resp.
\{\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}
and
\{\nabla_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}\}_{\lambda\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}}
).
Therefore, by Proposition
5.3.5
, it suffices to prove that for any
\lambda,\mu\in\check{\mathbf{X}}^{+}
and any
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, the functor
\Phi_{?}
induces an isomorphism

(17)

\Ext_{P_{\mathcal{L}^{+}G}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})}^{n}(\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k}),\mathcal{J}_{*}(\mu,\mathbbm{k}))\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\sim}}{{\to}}\Hom_{D_{\mathcal{IW},?}^{b}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})}(\Gr,\mathbbm{k})(\Delta_{\zeta+\lambda}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k}),\nabla_{\zeta+\mu}^{\mathcal{IW}}(\mathbbm{k})[n]).

By (
10
) and Lemma
2.3.2
, we have that both sides of (
17
) vanish unless
\lambda=\mu
and
n=0
.
Let
\xi^{\mathbbm{k}}:\mathcal{J}_{!}(\lambda,\mathbbm{k})\to\mathcal{J}_{*}(\mu,\mathbbm{k})
denote the canonical map induced by adjunction.
It suffices to show that
\Phi_{?}(\xi^{\mathbb{F}})
is nonzero for every field
\mathbbm{k}\to\mathbb{F}
.
Of course, the cone of
\xi^{\mathbb{F}}
is supported on
\overline{\Gr^{\lambda}}\setminus\Gr^{\lambda}
.
On the other hand, by Proposition
5.3.5
, the cone of
\Phi_{?}(\xi^{\mathbb{F}})
is supported on
\overline{\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}}\setminus\Gr_{\zeta+\lambda}^{+}
.
In particular, we must have that
\Phi_{?}(\xi^{\mathbb{F}})
is nonzero.
Therefore, (
17
) is an isomorphism for
\lambda=\mu
and
n=0
which completes the proof.

\square
