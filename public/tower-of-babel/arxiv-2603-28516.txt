Abstract
We calculate the dimension-six Δ ​ Q = 0 \Delta Q=0 four-quark matrix elements describing heavy-meson lifetime ratios using the gradient flow with its short flow-time expansion ( SFTX ) as a renormalization procedure. On six RBC/UKQCD 2+1-flavor domain-wall fermion ensembles, we determine flowed bag parameters for physical charm and strange quarks and match to the MS ¯ \overline{{\text{MS}}} scheme with perturbative SFTX coefficients through next-to-next-to-leading order ( NNLO ) . A multi-scale matching procedure using renormalization-group running improves the extrapolation to zero flow time. For the operators relevant to τ ⁡ ( D s ) / τ ⁡ ( D 0 ) \tau(D_{s})/\tau(D^{0}) at the SU(3) F symmetric point, we obtain B 1 MS ¯ ​ ( 3 ​ GeV ) = 1.0524 ​ ( 97 ) B_{1}^{\overline{{\text{MS}}}}(3\,\text{Ge\hskip-1.00006ptV})=1.0524(97) , B 2 MS ¯ ​ ( 3 ​ GeV ) = 0.9621 ​ ( 70 ) B_{2}^{\overline{{\text{MS}}}}(3\,\text{Ge\hskip-1.00006ptV})=0.9621(70) , ϵ 1 MS ¯ ​ ( 3 ​ GeV ) = − 0.2275 ​ ( 76 ) \epsilon_{1}^{\overline{{\text{MS}}}}(3\,\text{Ge\hskip-1.00006ptV})=-0.2275(76) , and ϵ 2 MS ¯ ​ ( 3 ​ GeV ) = − 0.0005 ​ ( 8 ) \epsilon_{2}^{\overline{{\text{MS}}}}(3\,\text{Ge\hskip-1.00006ptV})=-0.0005(8) using a specific choice of evanescent operators. This is the first lattice- QCD determination of Δ ​ Q = 0 \Delta Q=0 four-quark operators with a full error budget. It opens the path towards higher-precision predictions of heavy-meson lifetimes and similar quantities exhibiting operator mixing under renormalization.



Introduction —

The physics of heavy hadrons provides insight into several crucial aspects of
the
SM
. While the binding of the quarks is governed by strong interactions,
mixing and decay of the hadrons is induced by weak interactions, with strong
sensitivity to the
Cabbibo-Kobayashi-Maskawa (
CKM
)
matrix elements. The comparison of precise
measurements and theoretical predictions of the heavy-hadron properties thus
provides crucial input for the indirect search of physics beyond the
SM
. A
summary of the theoretical and experimental status can be found in
Refs.
Banerjee et al. 2026
;
Navas et al. 2024
;
Albrecht et al. 2024
.

Theoretical predictions of heavy-hadron properties need to account for both
perturbative (short-distance) and non-perturbative (long-distance) effects.
A suitable framework for this is provided by effective field theories, where the
perturbative effects are captured by the Wilson coefficients, while the
determination of the associated matrix elements requires non-perturbative
methods.

To date, sum-rule techniques have been used to estimate these non-perturbative
effects for lifetimes and lifetime ratios in the framework of

heavy-quark effective theory (
HQET
)

Kirk et al. 2017
;
Kirk 2018
;
King et al. 2022a
;
King et al. 2022b
;
King 2022
;
Black et al. 2025a
.
Lattice field theory (
LFT
)
calculations have so far
delivered phenomenologically relevant results for short-distance contributions
to heavy-meson
mixing
Carrasco et al. 2014a
;
Carrasco et al. 2015
;
Bazavov et al. 2018
;
Carrasco et al. 2014b
;
Aoki et al. 2015
;
Gamiz et al. 2009
;
Bazavov et al. 2016
;
Dowdall et al. 2019
;
Boyle et al. 2022
,
but, despite similarities in the calculation and early work
Di Pierro and Sachrajda 1998
;
Di Pierro et al. 1999
;
Becirevic 2001
,
LFT
is yet to
provide the non-perturbative input for heavy-meson lifetimes with full error
budgets.

In this paper, we consider
D_{s}
mesons, which constitute an ideal laboratory
for establishing and validating our method to non-perturbatively renormalize the
matrix elements on the lattice. The gradient flow
is used for the operator renormalization and enables us to take the
continuum limit. Subsequently, we convert the results to the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}
scheme using
perturbatively-evaluated matching coefficients. In this proof-of-principle
calculation, we limit ourselves to the slightly simpler scenario of lifetime
ratios, neglecting contributions due to so-called “eye diagrams” and mixing
with lower-dimensional operators which vanish in the limit of exact SU(3)-flavor
symmetry among the
u,d
, and
s
quarks. The effect of eye diagrams was found
to be
O(0.2\%)
in
HQET
using the sum-rules approach, and the strange quark-mass
corrections
O(0.1\%)

King et al. 2022b
;
King 2022
.

Nevertheless, our approach is applicable also to the calculation of absolute
lifetimes in principle, even if some of the steps may require additional efforts
in order to reach similar precision. The analogous calculation for the simpler
case of heavy-meson mixing and further details on the lifetime calculation are
presented in an accompanying paper
Black et al. 2026
, while preliminary results have been presented in
Black et al. 2024
;
Black 2024
;
Black et al. 2025b
.

Operators and bag parameters —

Within the
heavy-quark expansion (
HQE
)

Nikolaev 1973
;
Neubert 1994
, the lifetime of a heavy hadron
H
is
governed by the imaginary part of the forward
H\to H
matrix elements of local
\Delta Q=0
operators.
The leading spectator effects incorporating the meson’s light valence quark arise at order
1/m_{Q}^{3}

and are given by the following dimension-six four-quark operators (see e.g. Ref.
Albrecht et al. 2024
):

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{1}

\displaystyle=(\bar{Q}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})q)(\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})Q)\,,

(1)

\displaystyle{\cal O}_{2}

\displaystyle=(\bar{Q}(1-\gamma_{5})q)(\bar{q}(1+\gamma_{5})Q)\,,

\displaystyle T_{1}

\displaystyle=(\bar{Q}\gamma_{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})T^{A}q)(\bar{q}\gamma^{\mu}(1-\gamma_{5})T^{A}Q)\,,

\displaystyle T_{2}

\displaystyle=(\bar{Q}(1-\gamma_{5})T^{A}q)(\bar{q}(1+\gamma_{5})T^{A}Q)\,,

where the
T^{A}
are the generators of SU(3) color. A
LO
Feynman diagram
contributing to the forward matrix elements of these operators is shown in

Fig.

1
(a). These matrix elements are parametrized as

\displaystyle\langle H|{\cal O}_{1}|H\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{H}^{2}\,M_{H}^{2}\,{B}^{H}_{1}\,,

(2)

\displaystyle\langle H|{\cal O}_{2}|H\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{H}^{2}}{(m_{Q}+m_{q})^{2}}f_{H}^{2}\,M_{H}^{2}\,{B}_{2}^{H}\,,

\displaystyle\langle H|T_{1}|H\rangle

\displaystyle=f_{H}^{2}\,M_{H}^{2}\,\epsilon_{1}^{H}\,,

\displaystyle\langle H|T_{2}|H\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{M_{H}^{2}}{(m_{Q}+m_{q})^{2}}f_{H}^{2}\,M_{H}^{2}\,\epsilon_{2}^{H}\,,

with
M_{H}
the mass of the heavy meson
H
. The bag parameters
B_{1}^{H}
,

B_{2}^{H}
,
\epsilon_{1}^{H}
, and
\epsilon_{2}^{H}
as well as the decay constant
f_{H}

encode contributions due to strong interactions whose determination requires
non-perturbative methods. In this paper, we focus on a bound charm-strange state, i.e.
H=D_{s}
. We expect our method to apply also to other
D
or
B
mesons;
we leave this for future work.

Aside from diagrams like
Fig.

1
(a), the operators in

Eq.

1
also lead to diagrams where the light valence quark of the meson
is not directly connected to the composite operator, sometimes called
“eye diagrams”; see
Fig.

1
(b). Their consistent treatment
requires an extension of the operator basis in
Eq.

1
. In a first step
towards the full calculation of heavy-meson lifetimes, we neglect such
contributions in this paper. Our results are applicable to lifetime

ratios
like
\tau(D_{s})/\tau(D^{0})
, where eye diagrams cancel up
to small SU(3)
F
-breaking effects, as pointed out above
King et al. 2022b
.

Lattice calculation —

Figure

2
illustrates the calculation of a three-point
function on the lattice. Mesons are created at Euclidean times
t_{0}
and

t_{0}+\Delta T
. The four-quark interactions described by the operators defined
in
Eq.

1
are contracted at
t\in[t_{0},t_{0}+\Delta T]
. By placing the
sources at equal time-separations, propagators can be reused to calculate
three-point functions with different separation
\Delta T
concurrently
(cf. Ref.
Boyle et al. 2018
).

The bag parameters can be obtained from the ratio

\displaystyle R_{X}(t_{0},t,\Delta T)

\displaystyle=\frac{C^{\rm 3pt}_{X}(t_{0},t,t_{0}+\Delta T)}{C_{X}(t_{0},t)\,C_{X}(t_{0}+\Delta T,t)}\to B_{X}\,,

(3)

where the arrow indicates the limits
t_{0}\ll t\ll t_{0}+\Delta T
and
\Delta T\to\infty
. The two- and three-point
functions are given by

\displaystyle C^{\text{3pt}}_{X}(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

\displaystyle=\langle 0|j_{P}(t_{1})X(t_{2})j_{P}^{\dagger}(t_{3})|0\rangle\,,

(4)

\displaystyle C_{X}(t_{1},t_{2})

\displaystyle=\langle 0|j_{X}(t_{2})j_{P}^{\dagger}(t_{1})|0\rangle\,,

where
t_{1}<t_{2}<t_{3}
are generic positions, and
j_{P}=\bar{q}\gamma_{5}Q
and
j_{X}=\bar{q}\Gamma_{X}Q

are zero-momentum currents. Note that we are using
X
both as an operator and as an index in
Eq.

4
.
In order to have appropriate overlap with the bag parameters,

\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}_{1}}=\Gamma_{T_{1}}=\gamma_{0}\gamma_{5}
, and

\Gamma_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}=\Gamma_{T_{2}}=\gamma_{5}
.

Gradient flow —

Bare correlators as well as the ratio of
Eq.

3
are

ultra-violet (
UV
)
divergent. We obtain the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}
-renormalized hadronic matrix elements by
closely following the method of
Refs.
Suzuki 2013
;
Makino and Suzuki 2014
;
Taniguchi et al. 2020
;
Suzuki and Takaura 2021
;
Black et al. 2025c
;
Francis et al. 2025a
;
Francis et al. 2025b

which is based on the

gradient flow (
GF
)

Narayanan and Neuberger 2006
;
Lüscher 2010a
;
Lüscher 2010b
;
Lüscher 2013
and
the
SFTX

Lüscher and Weisz 2011
;
Suzuki 2013
;
Lüscher 2014
. To be
specific, we replace the operators
X
and the currents
j_{X}
in
Eq.

4
by their flowed versions
\widetilde{X}(\tau)
and
\widetilde{j}_{X}(\tau)
, i.e. the fields in these quantities are evolved according to

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\tau)

\displaystyle=D_{\nu}(\tau)G_{\nu\mu}(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\tau=0)

\displaystyle=A_{\mu}\,,

(5)

\displaystyle\partial_{\tau}\chi(\tau)

\displaystyle=D^{2}(\tau)\chi(\tau)\,,

\displaystyle\chi(\tau=0)

\displaystyle=\psi\,,

with
\tau
indicating the flow time.

\chi(\tau)
represents the quark fields,
G_{\mu\nu}
is the field-strength tensor, and
D_{\mu}

gluon field
A_{\mu}
replaced by its flowed version
\mathcal{A}_{\mu}(\tau)

On the lattice, we use the Wilson kernel to evolve the gauge fields, and apply the
lattice Laplace operator to the propagators
Lüscher 2014
.
For finite
\tau>0
, the
GF
regularizes
the
UV

combining results from the
different ensembles can be taken. Power-divergent operator mixing on the lattice is absent in this approach and shifted to the
SFTX

renormalizations for the flowed and unflowed quark fields cancel in the ratio of

Eq.

3
.

At this point, the continuum bag parameters depend on the flow
time
\tau

GF
scheme
denoting them by
\widetilde{B}_{X}(\tau)
. They can be converted to the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}

Lüscher and Weisz 2011
;
Suzuki 2013
;
Lüscher 2014

matrices,

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}B_{i}(\tau,\mu)\\
\epsilon_{i}(\tau,\mu)\end{pmatrix}=\zeta^{-1}_{B,i}(\tau,\mu)\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{B}_{i}(\tau)\\
\widetilde{\epsilon}_{i}(\tau)\end{pmatrix}\,,

(6)

for
i\in\{1,2\}
. The matching matrices are given by

\displaystyle\zeta_{B,1}=\zeta_{1}\zeta_{A}^{-2}\,,\qquad\zeta_{B,2}=\zeta_{2}\zeta_{P}^{-2}\,,

(7)

where
\zeta_{P}
and
\zeta_{A}

SFTX
matching coefficients for the
pseudo-scalar and the axial-vector current, and the
\zeta_{i}
are the matching
matrices of the four-quark operators
\{\mathcal{O}_{i},{T}_{i}\}

Eq.

1

NNLO
in
QCD

Harlander and Lange 2022
;
Borgulat et al. 2024
;
Black et al. 2026

The quantities on the l.h.s. of

6

\overline{{\text{MS}}}
bag parameters by taking the

\tau\to 0

B_{X}^{\overline{{\text{MS}}}}(\mu)=\lim_{\tau\to 0}B_{X}(\tau,\mu).

(8)

This limit is non-trivial and involves identifying a suitable
region from which such an extrapolation can be performed as we discuss later.

Zero-flow-time limit —

In the final step of our procedure, the flowed bag parameters are multiplied by
the inverse matching matrices defined in
Eqs.

6
and
7
, and the
result is extrapolated to
\tau=0

\overline{{\text{MS}}}

To derive the bag parameters in the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}
scheme at a
certain value of
\mu=\mu_{0}

matching matrices at this value,
\zeta^{-1}(\tau,\mu_{0})

\displaystyle\mu^{2}\frac{\rm d}{{\rm d}\mu^{2}}\zeta(\tau,\mu)=\zeta(\tau,\mu)\gamma(\alpha_{\text{s}}(\mu))\,,

Conclusion —

We have presented the first lattice-
QCD
determination of the dimension-six
\Delta Q=0

four-quark bag parameters that govern the leading spectator effects
for the lifetime ratios of heavy mesons with full error budget away from the static
limit.
Ratios of heavy-meson lifetimes play an important role to validate the
HQE
but also enter many phenomenological determinations such as decay-rate ratios of
b\to s\ell\ell
currents.
Including “eye diagrams” in the future will enable us to calculate absolute lifetimes of
D
and
B
mesons as well as their ratios. Moreover, we plan to add baryon lifetimes to our program.

Our result is based on
GF
combined with the
SFTX
to obtain renormalized bag parameters in the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}
scheme.

divergences are regularized at positive flow time such that the continuum limit can be taken without encountering power-divergent mixing, and results can be
matched to the
\overline{{\text{MS}}}
scheme using perturbatively-computed
SFTX
coefficients
(here through
NNLO
). A robust
\tau\to 0
extrapolation –
restricted to a window where both discretization effects and perturbative
instabilities are under control – completes the connection to standard
continuum phenomenology.
The determination of lifetime-ratio operators for a charm-strange system demonstrates that the
GF
+
SFTX
is a viable renormalization procedure for lattice calculations, which can be further applied to complex renormalization structures including power-divergent operator mixing.
Further possible applications may include other four-quark operators exhibiting operator mixing under renormalization such as, for example, nucleon electric dipole moments.
