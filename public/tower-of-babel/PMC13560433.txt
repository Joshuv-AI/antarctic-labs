Hsa_circ_0077658 serves as a potential diagnostic biomarker for severe pneumonia in children and alleviates lipopolysaccharide-induced damage to bronchial epithelial cells by modulating the miR-15a-5p/NF1 axis
Authors: Hui Zhang, Chang Liu, Hui Qi, Mei Yang
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSevere pneumonia in children typically presents with the most prominent early lesion being damage to the bronchial mucosa.AimTo explore the diagnostic value of hsa_circ_0077658 in pediatric severe pneumonia and its possible mechanism of action.Materials and methods116 children with severe pneumonia, 87 children with pneumonia, and 60 healthy children were included. Lipopolysaccharide (LPS) was used to stimulate BEAS-2B cells to establish a pneumonia cell model. Reverse Transcription quantitative PCR (RT-qPCR) was performed to detect the expression levels of hsa_circ_0077658, miR-15a-5p and Neurofibromatosis type 1(NF1). The Enzyme-Linked Immunosorbent Assay (ELISA) method was performed to detect the levels of oxidative stress markers and inflammatory factors. The Annexin V/PI and Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) kits were used to assess the apoptosis and proliferation levels of BEAS-2B cells, respectively. Person’s correlation analysis, dual luciferase assays and RNA Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation (RIP) experiments were performed to demonstrate the relationship between genes.ResultsHsa_circ_0077658 and NF1 were downregulated in patients with pneumonia and in LPS-induced BEAS-2B cells, while miR-15a-5p was upregulated. Hsa_circ_0077658 showed excellent diagnostic performance, with an AUC of 0.820 (95% CI: 0.749–0.890) in healthy vs. pneumonia children comparison and 0.931 (95% CI: 0.894–0.966) for distinguishing pneumonia children from severe pneumonia children. Following exposure to LPS, BEAS-2B cells exhibited heightened oxidative stress, apoptosis, and inflammatory responses, while cellular proliferation was suppressed. Hsa_circ_0077658 targets miR-15a-5p, and NF1 is the downstream effect target of miR-15a-5p. Overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 can alleviate the oxidative stress, apoptosis and inflammatory response in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, thereby promoting proliferation. This effect was reversed by overexpression of miR-15a-5p. Furthermore, overexpression of NF1 can reverse the exacerbating effect of miR-15a-5p overexpression on the damage caused by LPS to BEAS-2B cells.ConclusionHsa_circ_0077658 may be a biomarker for pediatric severe pneumonia, potentially influencing disease progression via the miR-15a-5p/NF1 axis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1186/s12865-026-00867-z.Keywords: Severe pneumonia, Hsa_circ_0077658, miR-15a-5p, NF1, Apoptosis, Proliferation

Introduction

Severe pneumonia in children is one of the major diseases threatening children’s health worldwide [1]. Its onset is closely associated with bacterial or viral infections, immunodeficiency, and environmental factors, often leading to respiratory failure and multiple organ damage [2]. Studies have shown that the incidence of this disease is significantly higher in developing countries than in developed countries, and the proportion of severe cases is on the rise, placing a heavy burden on the medical system [3]. At present, clinical diagnosis mainly relies on clinical symptoms such as refusal to eat, persistent vomiting, lethargy, severe respiratory distress, chest X-rays and chest ‌Computed Tomography (CT) scans, as well as laboratory indicators such as white blood cells (WBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), and Interleukin-6 (IL-6) [4, 5]. The treatment mainly involves anti-infection, respiratory support, symptomatic treatment, and nutritional support. Some patients can also use drugs such as acetylcysteine to assist in reducing inflammation and oxidative stress damage [6, 7]. However, traditional methods suffer from limitations such as insufficient sensitivity and time-consuming procedures, particularly struggling to accurately identify severe disease risk in the early stages, leading to delayed treatment windows [8]. The current treatment mainly involves the use of antibiotics, oxygen therapy and supportive measures. However, intervention targeting the molecular mechanisms of the underlying causes is still insufficient. It is urgent to explore new diagnostic markers to optimize clinical treatment plans.

Bronchial epithelial cells, as the lung’s defensive barrier, play a crucial role in the pathological progression of severe pneumonia [9, 10]. The oxidative stress response can cause lipid peroxidation of the cell membrane, leading to epithelial cell apoptosis and disruption of the barrier function [11]. Additionally, excessive activation of inflammatory mediators such as IL-6, Tumor Necrosis Factor-alpha‌ (TNF-α), and Interleukin-1 beta (IL-1β) exacerbates tissue edema and microcirculatory dysfunction, creating a vicious cycle [12]. The inflammatory storm can lead to abnormal changes in cell proliferation capacity, which may affect alveolar repair and exacerbate respiratory insufficiency. These mechanisms collectively form the cellular and pathological basis of severe pneumonia in children, providing a theoretical foundation for targeted interventions.

Studies have shown that circular RNAs (circRNAs) can participate in the inflammatory response and cell survival process by adsorbing microRNAs (miRNAs) or regulating signaling pathways [13]. For instance, circFADS2 protects lung cells from LPS-induced apoptosis by down-regulating miR-133a [14]. CircESPL1 regulates MAPK14 by sponging miR-326, thereby alleviating LPS-induced lung cell damage [15]. Knocking down circ_0000033 inhibited the proliferation, migration, invasion and angiogenesis of breast cancer cells [16]. Circ_0005397 promotes the proliferation, migration, invasion and angiogenesis of hepatocellular carcinoma through the miR-1283/HEG1 axis, while inhibiting apoptosis [17]. However, hsa_circ_0077658 is a newly discovered circRNA, and its correlation with diseases has not been reported yet. In the preliminary phase, our research group identified the dataset GSE217763 from the Gene Expression Omnibus (GEO) database, which pertains to pulmonary diseases. Analysis showed that among all downregulated circRNAs in ARDS patients relative to healthy controls, hsa_circ_0077658 was the most significantly decreased, with a log2(fold change) value of -4.07. Consequently, this study focuses on investigating the impact of hsa_circ_0077658 on pediatric severe pneumonia.

This study aims to verify the feasibility of hsa_circ_0077658 as a diagnostic marker, and to explore its molecular mechanism in alleviating LPS-induced damage to bronchial epithelial cells, in order to provide new intervention targets for clinical applications.

Material and method

Patients

This study included 116 severe pneumonia children (SPC, n = 116) and 87 pneumonia children (PC, n = 87). Additionally, 60 healthy children (Health, n = 60) who underwent health examinations at Beijing Children’s Hospital during the same period were enrolled. Inclusion criteria: Patients aged 2 to 12 years old with pneumonia or severe pneumonia, who have complete clinical data. Exclusion criteria: Patients with a history of pneumonia in the past 1 year, those with acute lung injury, respiratory distress syndrome, developmental disorders or malnutrition, severe dysfunction of the heart, liver, or kidneys, and those with autoimmune diseases or malignancies are excluded. The study was performed in line with the principles of the Declaration of Helsinki. Approval was granted by the Ethics Committee of Beijing Children’s Hospital before the study began. Written informed consent has been obtained from the participants involved.

Clinical data and serum sample collection

Clinical data such as age, gender, white blood cells (WBC), C-reactive protein (CRP), procalcitonin (PCT), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), lymphocytes, Neutrophils, erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR) and D-dimer of the three groups of participating children were collected. All participants had their venous blood samples (5 mL each) collected after enrollment. The blood samples were centrifuged and promptly stored at -80℃.

Pre-experiment on LPS stimulation of BEAS-2B cells

Cell viability was assessed using the Cell Counting Kit-8 (CCK-8) assay. Briefly, BEAS-2B cells were seeded in 96-well plates at a density of 2 × 10³ cells/well and cultured overnight at 37 °C with 5% CO₂. For the dose-gradient experiment, cells were treated with 0, 5, 10, 15, or 20 µg/mL LPS for 24 h. For the time-gradient experiment (Figure B), cells were treated with 10 µg/mL LPS for 0, 12, 24, 36, or 48 h. After treatment, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent was added to each well, and the plates were incubated for another 4 h at 37 ℃. The absorbance at 450 nm was measured using a microplate reader, and relative cell viability was calculated as a percentage of the control group.

Cell culture and stimulation

The immortalized normal bronchial epithelial cells (BEAS-2B, SCSP-5067) were purchased from the National Collection of Authenticated Cell Cultures. The cells were cultured in the BEAS-2B specific complete culture medium (SCSP-652). The culture conditions for the cells were 95% air, 5% CO2 and 37℃. To establish a pneumonitis cell model, BEAS-2B cells were stimulated with 10 µg/mL LPS (12352201, MeRCK, Darmstadt, Ger.) for 24 h.

Cell transfection

The overexpression vectors of hsa_circ_0077658 (oe-circ) and Neurofibromatosis type 1 gene (NF1) (oe-NF1), as well as their negative control vectors, were purchased from GenePharma Genetics Company. The miR-15a-5p mimics, miR-15a-5p inhibitors, and their negative controls were purchased from ThermoFisher. The plasmid was transfected into BEAS-2B cells using LipoBooster 3000 (Yeasen Biotechnology (Shanghai) Co., Ltd., 40801ES).

RT-qPCR

Total RNA was extracted from serum and BEAS-2B cells using the Trizol method, and the content and purity of the RNA were detected by Prove 600 plus (MeRCK, Darmstadt, Germany). The levels of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p were detected as follows: cDNA was synthesized using the TaqMan MicroRNA Reverse Transcription Kit, and then amplified using SYBR Green PCR Master Mix. The level of NF1 mRNA was detected as follows: Reverse transcription was performed using the TaKaRa reverse transcription kit, followed by amplification. The relative expression levels of hsa_circ_0077658, miR-15a-5p and NF1 were calculated using the 2−ΔΔCt method. U6 and GAPDH were used as internal references for circRNA/miRNA and mRNA, respectively. The primer sequences of this study are shown in Supplementary Table 1.

MDA and GSH assay

Malondialdehyde‌ (MDA) assay kit (ml076371, mlbio, Shanghai, China) and glutathione (GSH) assay kit (ml092952, mlbio, Shanghai, China) were used to measure MDA and GSH. In summary, cells were lysed using a lysis buffer, then centrifuged at 2000×g and 4℃ for 10 min, after which the supernatant was collected. The MDA and GSH levels in each group were measured strictly according to the reagent instructions.

ROS assay

Reactive Oxygen Species (ROS) kit (ml092661, mlibo, Shanghai, China) was used to detect ROS. First, Dichlorodihydrofluorescein diacetate‌ (DCFH-DA) was diluted to 10 µM. Next, the BEAS-2B cells in each group were cultured with 10 µM DCFH-DA for 20 min. After washing the cells, the signal of (ROS) was measured under EVOS™ M5000 (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA, USA).

Cell apoptosis assay

Annexin V/PI assay kits (Merck, Darmstadt, Germany) were used to assess apoptosis levels in BEAS-2B cells across each group. In short, BEAS-2B cells are mixed with 500 µL of 1× Binding Buffer. After adding 5 µL Annexin V-FITC and 10 µL PI, the cells were gently mixed and incubated for 15 min at room temperature (25℃) under light-protected conditions. Subsequently, 400 µL of 1× Binding Buffer was added to each tube, and the samples were analyzed by flow cytometry within 1 h. Finally, the apoptosis rate was detected using a flow cytometer (ThermoFisher, Waltham, MA, USA).

IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β assay

Human IL-6 ELISA KIT (ml058097), Human TNF-α ELISA KIT (ml077385) and Human IL-1β ELISA KIT (ml058059) purchased from mlbio. In summary, the target analyte was captured by coating the corresponding capture antibody onto an ELISA plate. Subsequently, biotin-labeled detection antibody and streptavidin-conjugated horseradish peroxidase were added in sequence for incubation. After incubation, the TMB developing solution and stop solution were added sequentially. Finally, the OD value was measured at 450 nm using an enzyme detector, and the concentrations of IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β were calculated.

CCK-8 assay

In 96-well plates, BEAS-2B cells were seeded at a density of 2 × 103 cells per well and cultured under standard conditions (37 °C, 5% CO₂). After incubation for 24 h, 10 µL of CCK-8 reagent (ml052166, mlbio) was added to each well. Following another 4 h, the OD value of each well was detected at 450 nm using a microplate reader (ecan Infinite M200 Pro, Tecan Group Ltd., Männedorf, Switzerland; catalog number: 12345678).

Dual luciferase assay

To validate the interactions between hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p, as well as between miR-15a-5p and NF1, wild-type (circ-wt/NF1-wt) and mutant (circ-mut/NF1-mut) sequence fragments containing the miR-15a-5p binding site within the hsa_circ_0077658 and NF1 3’UTR regions were separately cloned into the pGL3 reporter vector (E1751, Promega). Subsequently, these vectors were co-transfected with miR-15a-5p mimics/inhibitors or a negative control (nc) into BEAS-2B cells. After 48 h, firefly luciferase and rellina luciferase activities were measured. The supplementary material presents the sequence of hsa_circ_0077658 and the 3’UTR fragment of NF1.

RIP assay

RIP™ RNA-Binding Protein Immunoprecipitation Kit (Merck Millipore, 17–701) was used to conduct RIP detection. In the RIP lysis buffer, the BEAS-2B cells were resuspended. At room temperature, magnetic beads loaded with A/G were incubated for 30 min with anti-AGO2 antibody (Merck Millipore) or control anti-IgG antibody (Merck Millipore). In the RIP immunoprecipitation buffer, the lysate was incubated with the antibody complex. The next day, the RNA-protein-bead complexes were washed. After incubation with proteinase K, qRT-PCR was used to analyze the immunoprecipitated RNA.

Statistical analysis

Experimental data are expressed as mean ± SD. Statistical analysis was performed by GraphPad Prism 10.1.2 software. Differences between the two groups were compared using t-tests. For multiple comparisons, factorial analysis of variance (ANOVA) was performed, followed by Tukey’s post hoc test to compare pairwise differences. p < 0.05 indicates statistically significant differences. All experiments have at least three independent biological replicates.

Results

The expression and diagnostic value of hsa_circ_0077658

The dataset GSE217763 screened and validated circRNAs abnormally expressed in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) exosomes from ARDS patients induced by severe pneumonia. The volcano plot indicates that 35 circRNAs were upregulated, 154 were downregulated, and 22 showed no significant difference in the GSE217763 dataset (Fig. 1A). Moreover, among all downregulated circRNAs, hsa_circ_0077658 exhibited the smallest log2(Fold Change) (Fig. 1A). This suggests that the abnormal downregulation of this gene may have a significant impact on severe pneumonia. Table 1 presents the clinical characteristics of the study children. Analysis of hsa_circ_0077658 levels in serum samples revealed a downregulated trend in both PC and SPC patients compared to the healthy control group, with SPC patients exhibiting the most pronounced reduction (Fig. 1B). Next, the diagnostic value of hsa_circ_0077658 for severe pneumonia in children was analyzed. The AUC of the ROC curve for the comparison between Health and PC was 0.820, with a 95% confidence interval (CI) spanning from 0.749 to 0.890. Its sensitivity and specificity were 0.783 and 0.724, respectively (Fig. 1C). In addition, for the ROC curve comparing PC and SPC, the AUC was 0.931, with a 95% CI ranging from 0.894 to 0.966. Moreover, the sensitivity and specificity of this ROC curve were 0.828 and 0.922, respectively (Fig. 1D). Analysis of the ROC curve indicates that hsa_circ_0077658 effectively distinguishes between healthy individuals and PC, as well as between PC and SPC. It is highly likely to serve as a biomarker for diagnosing severe pediatric pneumonia.

The effect of LPS stimulation on BEAS-2B cells

As shown in Supplementary Fig. 1, the cytotoxic effects of LPS on BEAS-2B cells are both dose-dependent and time-dependent. Based on these results, treatment with 10 µg/mL LPS for 24 h is an appropriate condition for establishing a cell damage model (as it ensures significant inhibition of cell viability whilst avoiding excessive toxicity that would lead to massive cell death, thereby facilitating subsequent functional experiments). The exposure to LPS caused abnormal increases in the expression levels of ROS and MDA in BEAS-2B cells, while the level of GSH decreased significantly (Fig. 2A). The apoptosis results indicated that LPS significantly induced apoptosis in BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 2B). Furthermore, exposure to LPS resulted in an abnormal increase in IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β levels within BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 2C). The CCK-8 results indicated that LPS stimulation significantly inhibited the proliferation of BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 2D). Finally, the RT-qPCR results showed that the stimulation with LPS inhibited hsa_circ_0077658 levels in BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 2E). The above results indicate that LPS successfully induced a pneumonia model in BEAS-2B cells.

Hsa_circ_0077658 targets miR-15a-5p

As shown in the Venn diagram of Fig. 3A, a total of 24 miRNAs were predicted by the starBase database to potentially have a targeting relationship with hsa_circ_0077658, while 118 miRNAs were predicted by the circBank database. miR-15a-5p is the only common gene they share. The RT-qPCR results indicated that after LPS treatment, miR-15a-5p expression in BEAS-2B cells significantly increased (Fig. 3B). Analysis of the miR-15a-5p levels in serum samples revealed that the levels of miR-15a-5p in both PC and SPC patients showed an upward trend, and the increase was most significant in SPC patients (Fig. 3C). It is worth noting that the levels of miR-15a-5p and hsa_circ_0077658 are inversely proportional to each other (Fig. 3D). More importantly, the dual luciferase experiment results showed that the miR-15a-5p mimics significantly weakened the luciferase activity of cells containing the hsa_circ_0077658 wild-type plasmid, while the miR-15a-5p inhibitors significantly enhanced this activity. What is even more interesting is that no such phenomenon was observed in the cells containing the mutant plasmid of hsa_circ_0077658 (Fig. 3E). The above results collectively indicate that miR-15a-5p and hsa_circ_0077658 have a targeting relationship.

Upregulating miR-15a-5p expression reverses the effects of hsa_circ_0077658 overexpression on LPS- BEAS-2B cells

To explore the possible mechanism by which hsa_circ_0077658 affects severe pneumonia in children, hsa_circ_0077658 was overexpressed in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, or hsa_circ_0077658 was co-overexpressed with miR-15a-5p. RT-qPCR results indicated that oe-circ reversed the expression level of hsa_circ_0077658 in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, indicating that oe-circ was successfully transfected into BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 4A). The RT-qPCR results showed that oe-circ reversed the increase in miR-15a-5p levels induced by LPS, while oe-circ + mimics significantly upregulated miR-15a-5p expression, indicating that oe-circ and miR-15a-5p mimics were successfully co-transfected into BEAS-2B (Fig. 4B). The results of analyzing the oxidative stress levels revealed that hsa_circ_0077658 overexpression reduced the levels of ROS and MDA in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, while co-overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p reversed this effect (Fig. 4C and D). Conversely, overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 markedly increased GSH levels in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, whereas co-overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p reversed this effect (Fig. 4E). Apoptosis results indicated that overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 markedly suppressed apoptosis in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, whereas co-overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p reversed this effect (Fig. 4F). Analysis of inflammatory cytokines revealed that overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 markedly suppressed IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β levels in LPS- BEAS-2B cells, whereas co-overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p reversed this effect (Fig. 4G–I). CCK-8 assay results indicated that overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 significantly promoted the proliferation of LPS-BEAS-2B cells, whereas co-overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 and miR-15a-5p reversed this effect (Fig. 4J). The above results collectively indicated that hsa_circ_0077658 affects the functions of LPS-BEAS-2B cells through miR-15a-5p.

NF1 is the downstream target of miR-15a-5p

As illustrated in the Venn diagram of Fig. 5A, a total of 211 targets associated with severe pneumonia were retrieved from the GeneCards database. Additionally, 397 and 1017 potential targets of miR-15a-5p were predicted from the miRDB and starBase databases, respectively. NF1 is the only common gene they share (Fig. 5A). RT-qPCR results demonstrated that NF1 expression levels were abnormally reduced in BEAS-2B cells following LPS treatment (Fig. 5B). Analysis of serum samples revealed that NF1 levels exhibited a downward trend in both PC and SPC patients compared to healthy controls, with the most pronounced decrease observed in SPC patients (Fig. 5C). Furthermore, the Pearson correlation analysis indicated that there was a negative correlation between NF1 and miR-15a-5p (Fig. 5D). More importantly, dual luciferase assay results indicate that in cells containing the wild-type NF1 plasmid, miR-15a-5p mimics significantly suppressed luciferase activity, while miR-15a-5p inhibitors greatly enhanced this activity. Interestingly, no comparable effect was observed in cells containing the mutant NF1 plasmid (Fig. 5E). Finally, the RIP results revealed that the levels of miR-15a-5p and NF1 were significantly increased in the Anti-AGO2 group (Fig. 5F). The above results indicate that NF1 is the downstream target of miR-15a-5p.

Overexpression of NF1 reverses the effect of miR-15a-5p upregulation on LPS-treated BEAS-2B cells

To explore the specific mechanism by which hsa_circ_0077658 affects severe pneumonia in children, miR-15a-5p was overexpressed in LPS-BEAS-2B cells, or miR-15a-5p was co-overexpressed with NF1. RT-qPCR results demonstrated that miR-15a-5p overexpression further promoted the reduction in LPS-induced NFI levels. Co-overexpression of miR-15a-5p with NF1, however, reversed NF1 expression levels (Fig. 6A). Analysis of oxidative stress markers indicates that co-overexpression of miR-15a-5p and NF1 reduced ROS and MDA levels in LPS-treated BEAS-2B cells, while conversely increasing GSH levels (Fig. 6B–D). The results of cell apoptosis showed that the co-overexpression of miR-15a-5p and NF1 significantly inhibited the apoptosis of LPS-BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 6E). The analysis of inflammatory factors revealed that the co-overexpression of miR-15a-5p and NF1 inhibited the secretion of IL-6, TNF-α and IL-1β in LPS-BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 6F–H). The CCK-8 results indicated that the co-overexpression of miR-15a-5p and NF1 promoted the proliferation of LPS-BEAS-2B cells (Fig. 6I). The above results indicate that miR-15a-5p affects the function of LPS-BEAS-2B cells through NF1.

Discussion

The pathological process of severe pneumonia in children begins with damage to the bronchial mucosa as the primary event. Its occurrence is closely related to pathogen infections such as viruses and bacteria, as well as the imbalance of the host’s immune system [18]. This study, through clinical samples and cell models, confirmed that hsa_circ_0077658 may be a potential diagnostic biomarker for severe pneumonia in children. By regulating the miR-15a-5p/NF1 axis, it intervenes in the pathological chain of “pneumonia → oxidative stress → epithelial apoptosis → inflammatory storm → proliferation inhibition → barrier collapse → respiratory failure”, thereby alleviating lung damage.

Bronchial epithelial cells, serving as the lung’s defensive barrier, exhibit damage that is closely associated with the malignant progression of severe pneumonia [19]. This study, using the BEAS-2B cell model induced by LPS, revealed that the oxidative stress response is a key driving factor in the early stage of the disease. It is widely recognized that ROS serve as triggers for oxidative stress, MDA reflects the terminal damage of lipid peroxidation and disrupts membrane stability, while GSH constitutes the core of antioxidant defense, with its depletion signaling oxidative imbalance [20]. The stimulation by LPS disrupted the dynamic balance of the oxidative and antioxidant systems within the bronchial epithelial cells [21]. The exposure to LPS led to a significant increase in ROS production within BEAS-2B cells, along with an elevation in MDA levels and a decrease in GSH levels. It is noteworthy that LPS-induced oxidative stress triggers apoptosis in epithelial cells [22]. Increased epithelial cell apoptosis constitutes a pivotal component of pathological lung injury and serves as a significant catalyst for amplified inflammatory responses [23]. Under the dual effects of apoptosis and LPS, inflammatory mediators are released in large quantities from BEAS-2B cells. More significantly, the persistent inflammatory storm further inhibits the proliferation of bronchial epithelial cells, constituting a crucial factor that impedes lung injury repair and exacerbates the pathological process. The results of this study show that overexpression of hsa_circ_0077658 can significantly alleviate oxidative stress, apoptosis, and inflammatory response in LPS-induced BEAS-2B cells, while promoting their proliferation and achieving the effect of protecting BEAS-2B cells. And this effect can be reversed by overexpressing miR-15a-5p. However, overexpression of NF1 can once again reverse the damaging effect of miR-15a-5p on BEAS-2B cells. Our research findings are in line with existing reports, such as that MANCR can enhance cell vitality, inhibit cell apoptosis and inflammatory responses to alleviate severe pneumonia in children [24]. Inhibition of circESPL1 reversed the LPS-induced cell proliferation, apoptosis and inflammatory response [15]. This indicates that our research is highly consistent with the pathological basis and molecular targets in previous studies, and it has further refined the pathological regulatory network of severe pneumonia in children.

At the mechanism level, this study for the first time demonstrated that Hsa_circ_0077658 participates in disease progression by regulating the miR-15a-5p/NF1 axis. The dual luciferase assay and RIP experiment showed that hsa_circ_0077658 can directly target miR-15a-5p, and NF1 is the downstream effect target of miR-15a-5p. And in the existing reports, there are multiple studies whose disease regulatory mechanisms are highly consistent with those of this research. For instance, Circ_0044411 alleviates LPS-induced infant lung injury by inhibiting the expression of CCL16 through the sponge miR-141-3p [25]. Circ_0012535 regulates mir-338-3p / IL6R to alleviate the progression of infant pneumonia [26]. Circ_0038467 reduces LPS-induced damage in 16HBE cells through the miR-545-3p/TRAF1 axis, thereby alleviating neonatal pneumonia [27]. The reports in these previous studies have provided strong theoretical support for the molecular regulatory mechanism of this research.

However, this study has the following limitations: our findings are currently limited to in vitro models, lacking in vivo validation in animal models of ARDS; the relatively small clinical sample size may limit the generalizability of our circRNA screening results, requiring larger cohorts for further validation; and we focused only on one specific ceRNA axis, leaving other potential downstream targets and pathways unexplored.

Conclusion

In summary, the downregulation of hsa_circ_0077658 strongly correlates with the pathological features of severe pneumonia, suggesting its potential as a biomarker for diagnosing severe pneumonia in children. Furthermore, its molecular mechanism of action via the miR-15a-5p/NF1 axis offers novel therapeutic targets for disease intervention.
