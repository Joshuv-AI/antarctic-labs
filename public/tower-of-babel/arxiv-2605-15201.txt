Mixed-State Long-Range Entanglement from Dimensional Constraints
Leonardo A. Lessa, Tsung-Cheng Lu

Abstract
We present a new mechanism for long-range entanglement (LRE) in strongly symmetric many-body mixed states that does not rely on symmetry anomalies or long-range correlations. Our primary example is the maximally mixed state in the translation-invariant subspace on a one-dimensional ring. This state is LRE because translationally symmetric short-range entangled states span a subspace whose dimension grows only polynomially with system size, whereas the full translation-invariant subspace grows exponentially. We further discuss certain unconventional properties of this state, including logarithmically growing conditional mutual information, strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry-breaking, and Rényi-index-dependent operator-space entanglement. We also construct a geometrically non-local Lindbladian to stabilize this state as the steady state. Our results identify dimensional mismatch as a novel route to LRE that is intrinsic to many-body mixed states.



One central goal in quantum many-body physics is the exploration of long-range entangled (LRE) quantum states, which underlie a variety of exotic phenomena such as topological orders and quantum criticality. While traditional studies have largely focused on isolated systems described by pure states, any realistic quantum system inevitably interacts with its environment and is therefore described by a mixed state. Then, a natural question arises: how should one define long-range entanglement for many-body mixed states? One commonly adopted definition is that a mixed state is LRE if it does not admit an ensemble of short-range entangled (SRE) pure states, each of which can be adiabatically connected to a product state
17
. Although this is a natural generalization from pure states to mixed states, such a definition makes certifying long-range entanglement challenging in general. In particular, since a mixed state admits an infinite number of ensemble realizations, how should one conclusively claim that the mixed state cannot admit an ensemble of SRE states? So far, there have been three mechanisms that guarantee long-range entanglement:

In this work, we identify a new mechanism that guarantees mixed-state long-range entanglement. In the rest of the manuscript, we prove that
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
exhibits long-range entanglement, analyze its correlation properties and information-theoretic measures, and conclude with possible generalizations and future directions. As a warm-up, we first present a theorem regarding the on-site non-separability of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, before treating its LRE properties:

Theorem 1
.

The translation symmetry MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is entangled (i.e., not fully separable) for large enough
L
. Specifically,
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\neq\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}(\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\!\bra{\psi_{\alpha}})^{\otimes L}
, for
L\geq 3
when
d\geq 3
, and for
L\geq 4
when
d=2
, and is fully separable otherwise.

We prove this theorem by contradiction: assuming the contrary, then there exists a separable decomposition:
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}(\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\!\bra{\psi_{\alpha}})^{\otimes L}
. For that, the set of states in the decomposition has to span the whole
\mathcal{T}
subspace. However, the product state
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}^{\otimes L}
can at most span a
permutationally
symmetric subspace
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{T}
(see Appendix.
I.1
). This leads to a contradiction for large enough
L
, as
\dim(\mathcal{S})=\Theta(L^{d-1})
grows only polynomially, whereas
\dim(\mathcal{T})=\Theta(d^{L}/L)
grows exponentially with
L
, based on the following two Lemmas (see Appendix.
I.2
and
I.3
for proofs):

Lemma 1
.

The dimension of the permutationally symmetric subspace
\mathcal{S}\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes L}
of
L
qudits is

\dim(\mathcal{S})=\binom{L+d-1}{d-1}.

(2)

To leading order in
L
,
\dim(\mathcal{S})
scales as
\frac{L^{d-1}}{(d-1)!}
.

Lemma 2
.

The dimension of the translationally symmetric subspace
\mathcal{T}\subseteq(\mathbb{C}^{d})^{\otimes L}
of
L
qudits is

\dim(\mathcal{T})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{i=0}^{L-1}d^{\gcd(i,L)}.

(3)

To leading order in
L
,
\dim(\mathcal{T})
scales as
d^{L}/L
, up to a correction that is exponentially small in L.

For system sizes
L
satisfying the conditions stated in Theorem
1
, one finds
\dim(\mathcal{S})<\dim(\mathcal{T})
(see Appendix.
I.4
for detailed comparison), and therefore
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is entangled. Otherwise, one has
\mathcal{S}=\mathcal{T}
and
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\rho_{\mathcal{S}}
, and we prove that the MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{S}}
is fully separable in Appendix.
I.1
.

From Theorem
1

\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is not fully separable for a large enough

Theorem 2
.

The translation symmetry MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is bipartite entangled for large enough
L

\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\neq\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{A_{\alpha}}\!\bra{A_{\alpha}}\otimes\ket{B_{\alpha}}\!\bra{B_{\alpha}}

L\geq 3

d\geq 3

L\geq 4

This theorem follows from the following Lemma:

Lemma 3
.

A translationally symmetric pure state
\ket{\Psi}\in\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}
(i.e.
T\ket{\Psi}\propto\ket{\Psi}
) that is bipartite separable with respect to contiguous regions
A|B

\ket{\Psi}=\ket{A}\ket{B}

\ket{\Psi}=\ket{\psi}^{\otimes L}

The central proof idea for this Lemma is to note that when
\ket{\Psi}=\ket{A}\ket{B}

Within the MPS formalism, we prove

Theorem 3
.

The translation symmetry MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\neq\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\!\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}
, where
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}
is a constant bond-dimension uMPS for large enough

To appreciate how this theorem constrains the entanglement structure of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, we first recall that in the context of MPSs,
\ket{\psi(A)}
is called short-range entangled (SRE)
9
if it is a normal MPS with finite bond dimension
4
4

4

An MPS is normal if and only if its MPS tensor, under blocking a finite number of sites, defines an injective map from the virtual space to the physical space
9
. This is in contrast to the long-range entangled GHZ state, whose MPS representation is not normal.
. Normal MPSs have several important properties: they arise as unique ground states of a local gapped Hamiltonian, and their transfer matrices have a unique largest eigenvalue, implying a finite spectral gap and a finite correlation length. Crucially, every translationally invariant normal MPS admits a uMPS representation
9
. One therefore has the following result:

Corollary 1
.

The translation symmetry MMIS is long-range entangled for large enough
L
. Specifically,
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\neq\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\!\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}
, where
\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}
is an SRE state represented by uMPS.

We note that Theorem
3
imposes a stronger constraint on the structure of the mixed state
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
than Corollary
1
. This is because uMPS with constant bond dimension form a broader class of states than just short-range entangled uMPS, including for example GHZ-type states.

The proof for Theorem
3

Lemma 4
.

The dimension of the linear span of all uMPSs is upper bounded by

\dim(\spn(\uMPS(\chi,d,L)))\leq\binom{L+d\chi^{2}-1}{d\chi^{2}-1},

(6)

where
\uMPS(\chi,d,L)
is the set of uMPS of bond dimension
\chi
over
L

d
-dimensional sites. In particular, it can grow at most polynomially as
O(L^{d\chi^{2}-1})
as
L\to\infty
.

48

Correlation structure.
So far, we have shown that
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is long-range entangled (LRE), but its precise correlation and entanglement structure have yet to be determined. Indeed, LRE states can exhibit very different behaviors: they can exhibit volume-law entanglement scaling as in Haar random states, or appear as area-law entangled ground states of local Hamiltonians (as in the GHZ state, topological orders, or quantum critical states), with varying correlation and entanglement structures. In the following, we will present results on the correlation structures of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
. Detailed calculations and proofs can be found in Appendix.

.

As a warm-up, we first notice that
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
has a weak symmetry under

. For instance, consider an operator of the form
O_{a,b}=\prod_{i=1}^{L}X_{i}^{a_{i}}Z_{i}^{b_{i}}

are the generalized Pauli operators acting on

Information-theoretic measures
. After presenting the correlation structure of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, we now explore the properties of the state using information-theoretic measures. To quantify the bipartite entanglement in such a mixed state, one natural measure is the entanglement of formation
4

A
minimized over all possible pure-state decompositions of the mixed state

E_{f}(\rho)=\min_{\{p_{\alpha},\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\}_{\rho}}\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}S_{A}(\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}),

(12)

where
\{p_{\alpha},\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\}_{\rho}
denotes the set of decompositions of
\rho=\sum_{\alpha}p_{\alpha}\ket{\psi_{\alpha}}\!\bra{\psi_{\alpha}}
. While we are not able to provide a precise estimate of
E_{f}(\rho_{\mathcal{T}})
, it is straightforward to derive an upper bound:

Theorem 6
.

The entanglement of formation
E_{F}
of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
w.r.t. any bipartition is upper bounded by
\log L
.

To prove this theorem, consider the decomposition introduced right after
Eq.

1
,
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}P_{\mathcal{T}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\!\bra{\bm{\sigma}}P_{\mathcal{T}}
. For each
P_{\mathcal{T}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}
, the reduced density matrix has a rank bounded by
L
, so that the bipartite entanglement entropy of
P_{\mathcal{T}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}
is bounded by
\log L
. This in turn implies the
\log L
upper bound for the entanglement of formation.

We contrast this bound with the average entanglement of Haar random pure states with translation symmetry, which was found to follow volume-law bipartite entanglement
30
, instead of the much lower

from the decomposition above.

Another quantity of interest is the conditional mutual information (CMI)

SW-SSB of lattice translation.
Strongly symmetric many-body mixed states can exhibit a novel phenomenon without pure-state counterparts known as the strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SW-SSB)
18
;
34
;
21
, in which the strong symmetry can be spontaneously broken down to the weak one. The canonical example is the strong
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
symmetric maximally mixed state
\rho\propto\mathds{1}+\prod_{i}X_{i}
, which, despite lacking true long-range order
\lim_{|i-j|\to\infty}\Tr[\rho Z_{i}Z_{j}]=0
, it exhibits long-range order for certain non-linear observables such as the Rényi-2 correlator
R^{(2)}=\langle\!\langle\rho|Z_{i}\overline{Z}_{i}Z_{j}\overline{Z}_{j}|\rho\rangle\!\rangle=1
, where
|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
is the doubled state (associated with
\rho
) that has been properly normalized as
\langle\!\langle\rho|\rho\rangle\!\rangle=1
.

For simplicity, we choose a particular non-local operator
O_{k}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{x=0}^{L-1}e^{ikx}Z_{x}
that is charged under the lattice translation,
T^{\dagger}O_{k}T=e^{ik}O_{k}
. Then, we introduce the following
variance-normalized
Rényi-2 correlator defined as

\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}=\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|(O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k})^{2}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}}.

(17)

This variance-normalized correlator
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
is more appropriate to diagnose the SSB of translation due to the following reasoning: an SSB order parameter can be thought of as a probe for a response w.r.t. inserting symmetry charges, and this is exactly captured by
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
, which is the wavefunction overlap between two wavefunctions with and without the insertion of charged operators. Importantly, computing this overlap requires appropriate normalization given the non-unitary and non-local nature of
O_{k}
. This additional normalization factor distinguishes
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
from the Rényi-2 correlator
R^{(2)}
commonly defined in the literature
18
;
21
;
34
. For most previously considered cases, this distinction does not change whether a state has SW-SSB or not, since either the charged operator is unitary or the difference in normalization amounts to a constant. For the case of the MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, however, this difference matters: as shown in Appendix.
VI
, its variance-normalized correlator
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
is a non-zero constant, implying SW-SSB of the translation symmetry, despite
R^{(2)}
vanishing in the thermodynamic limit. This also makes
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
follow the same SW-SSB pattern previously observed in MMISs associated with other symmetries, such as the
\mathds{1}+\prod_{i}X_{i}
state.

Summary and discussion:
In this work, we have revealed a new mechanism for long-range entanglement of mixed states: whenever the rank of a mixed state
\rho
with a strong symmetry
S
is greater than the dimension of the space spanned by
S
-symmetric short-range entangled states, the mixed state
\rho
must be LRE. This mechanism is intrinsic to mixed states without any pure-state counterpart, therefore providing a new perspective to explore the landscape of LRE mixed-states and likely leading to more examples beyond the ones we discuss in this work.

Within this new mechanism, we discuss the MMIS of translation
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
and several of its interesting features. In particular, it provides an example of SW-SSB of a non-onsite and non-gaugeable symmetry, whose diagnostic requires certain non-linear observables based on non-local charged operators. Exploring the stability of such SW-SSB and examples in higher dimensions is a natural future direction.

As we have shown, the emergence of the MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
as a steady state requires collective dissipation in order to preserve strong translation symmetry. It would be interesting to explore more detailed properties of such Linbladians from other perspectives, such as the mixing time and the spectrum. Relatedly, the steady state of any Markovian dissipative dynamics always admits a natural holographic wavefunction in one higher spatial dimension as discussed in Ref.
26
. Exploring the physical aspects of such a wavefunction may potentially provide new insights into such dynamics and the possible LSM-type constraints associated with it.

Finally, as we have emphasized, the LRE mechanism based on dimensional constraints is a phenomenon specialized to mixed states. It is therefore natural to suspect that
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is an intrinsically mixed quantum state, defined as one that is not two-way connected to any pure (either SRE or LRE) state through finite-depth local quantum channels. One such example is the one-dimensional CZX mixed state:
\rho_{\text{CZX}}\propto\mathds{1}+\prod_{i}X_{i}\prod_{i}\text{CZ}_{i,i+1}
, due to its intriguing feature of being bipartite separable but tripartite long-range entangled
20
. Whether
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
and its generalizations are intrinsically mixed in this sense is an interesting question, which we leave for future work.

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{T})=\Tr(P_{\mathcal{T}})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{n}).

(27)

We now compute

acts by cyclically shifting the string

by

(28)

A string is invariant under a shift by

\gcd(n,L)
sites, giving

\displaystyle\Tr(T^{n})=d^{\gcd(n,L)}.

(29)

Substituting back, we obtain

\displaystyle\dim(\mathcal{T})=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{n=0}^{L-1}d^{\gcd(n,L)},

(30)

where
\gcd(n=0,L)=L
.

We note that since
\gcd(n,L)\leq\frac{L}{2}
for
n=1,2,...,L-1
, the dominant contribution in the summation comes from

\dim(\mathcal{T})\to\frac{d^{L}}{L}.

(31)

Finally, we note that this expression coincides with the number of distinct necklaces of length

with

II
Proof of Lemma
3
: bipartite separabiltiy implies full separabiltiy

In this main text, we introduce Lemma
3
, which states that a translationally symmetric pure state that is bipartite separable with respect to contiguous bipartitioning
A|B
, i.e.
\ket{\Psi}=\ket{A}\ket{B}
, must be fully separable:
\ket{\Psi}=\ket{\psi}^{\otimes L}
. Here we will present the proof. For that, we first introduce a lemma that will be useful for our proof:

Lemma 7

(Overlapping pure marginals imply tripartite separability)
.

If
\rho_{ABC}
is a state on a tripartite system
\mathcal{H}_{A}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{B}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{C}
with pure reduced density matrices on
AB
and
BC
:

\displaystyle\rho_{AB}

\displaystyle=\Tr_{C}[\rho_{ABC}]=\ket{\Psi_{AB}}\!\bra{\Psi_{AB}},

(32)

\displaystyle\rho_{BC}

\displaystyle=\Tr_{A}[\rho_{ABC}]=\ket{\Phi_{BC}}\!\bra{\Phi_{BC}},

(33)

then
\rho_{ABC}
is a tripartite separable pure state
\rho_{ABC}=\ket{\Xi_{ABC}}\!\bra{\Xi_{ABC}}
,
\ket{\Xi_{ABC}}=\ket{\psi_{A}}\ket{\xi_{B}}\ket{\phi_{C}}
.

Proof.

Since
\rho_{AB}=\ket{\Psi_{AB}}\!\bra{\Psi_{AB}}
is pure,
\rho_{ABC}=\ket{\Psi_{AB}}\!\bra{\Psi_{AB}}\otimes\rho_{C}
. In particular,
B
and
C
are uncorrelated, which implies that
\ket{\Phi_{BC}}
factorizes into
\ket{\Phi_{BC}}=\ket{\xi_{B}}\otimes\ket{\phi_{C}}
. By the same reasoning applied to
\rho_{BC}
, we have
\ket{\Psi_{AB}}=\ket{\psi_{A}}\otimes\ket{\xi_{B}}
. The only possible extension compatible with both marginals is the tripartite separable pure state
\ket{\psi_{A}}\ket{\xi_{B}}\ket{\phi_{C}}
.
∎

Equipped with Lemma
7
, we now present the proof of Lemma
3
:

Proof.

Without loss of generality, let us assume
A
comes before
B
in the site index order set by the translation operator
T
. Then, divide each region as
A=A_{L}\sqcup A^{\prime}
and
B=B_{L}\sqcup B^{\prime}
, where
A_{L}
and
B_{L}
are the leftmost sites of regions
A
and
B
, respectively (with
A^{\prime}
or
B^{\prime}
possibly empty).

By assumption, given the state
\ket{\Psi}
, the reduced density matrix on the region
A=A_{L}A^{\prime}
is also a pure state. By translation symmetry, the reduced density matrix on
A^{\prime}B_{L}
is pure as well because
A^{\prime}B_{L}
is the translation of
B
by one site. Since the regions
A=A_{L}A^{\prime}
and
A^{\prime}B_{L}
overlaps on
A^{\prime}

7
, the state on
A_{L}|A^{\prime}|B_{L}
is pure and tripartite separable. By a similar logic applied to regions
B
and its translated version
B^{\prime}A_{L}
, we conclude that the state is also tripartite separable with respect to the
B_{L}|B^{\prime}|A_{L}
tripartition. Both facts together imply four-partite separability w.r.t.
A_{L}|A^{\prime}|B_{L}|B^{\prime}
.

By repeating the procedure above, each time starting with a bipartitioning one site shifted to the right compared to the previous one (e.g.,
A^{\prime}B_{L}|B^{\prime}A_{L}
instead of

), we iteratively factorize
\ket{\Psi}
into a product state, which has to be homogeneous due to translation symmetry.
∎

IV.1
Two-point function for prime

Given
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\frac{P_{\mathcal{T}}}{\mathcal{N}}
, with
P_{\mathcal{T}}=\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}T^{k}
and
\mathcal{N}
the normalization constant, we label the lattice site from 0 to
L-1
. Below we will calculate the two-point correlation
\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{0}Z_{j}^{-1}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\Tr\left(T^{k}Z_{0}Z_{j}^{-1}\right)
. First, one has

\Tr(T^{k}Z_{0}Z_{j}^{-1})=\Tr(Z_{k}T^{k}Z_{j}^{-1})=\Tr(T^{k}Z_{k}Z^{-1}_{j}).

(36)

In other words, the two-point function between site
0
and site
j
is exactly equal to that between site
k
and site
j
. Then one can repeat this process to find

\Tr(T^{k}Z_{0}Z^{-1}_{j})=\Tr(T^{k}Z_{mk}Z^{-1}_{j}).

(37)

with
m
being any positive integer and the subscript
mk
in
Z_{mk}
is defined as mod
L
. When
L
and
k
are co-prime, i.e.
\text{gcd}(L,k)=1
, any site
j
can be obtained by
mk\text{ mod }L
. Namely, the generator
T^{k}
is ergodic: it can generate the entire cyclic group defined on
L
sites.

\Tr(T^{k}Z_{0}Z^{-1}_{j})=\Tr(T^{k}Z_{j}Z^{-1}_{j})=\Tr(T^{k}).

(38)

When
L
is a prime number, the above is true for any
1\leq k\leq L-1
. Therefore,

\Tr(\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{0}Z^{-1}_{j})=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{k})=\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{k})}{\sum_{k=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{k})}=\frac{\Tr(T+...+T^{L-1})}{\Tr(1+T+...+T^{L-1})}

(39)

where we used
\Tr Z_{0}Z_{j}^{-1}=0
for
j\neq 0
.

We consider the Pauli-Z basis
\ket{\sigma}\equiv\ket{\{\sigma_{i}\}}
with
\sigma_{i}\in\{0,1,2,...,d-1\}
to calculate the trace:
\Tr(T^{k})=\sum_{\sigma}\bra{\sigma}T^{k}\ket{\sigma}
, which is equal to the number of
\sigma
strings invariant under a shift over
k
sites. The shift over
k
sites can define an orbit, within which each of
\sigma
strings can be connected using
k-
shift, and the number of disconnected orbits is
\text{gcd}(k,L)
. If the entire
\sigma
string of size
L
is invariant under
k-
shift, the only requirement is that within each orbit, the
\sigma_{i}
should be the same for all
i
, which can take
d
possible values. Since there are
\text{gcd}(k,L)
orbits, one finds
\Tr T^{k}=2^{\text{gcd}(k,L)}
for
1\leq k\leq L-1
. As such, when
L
is prime,
\Tr T^{k}=d
, and one finds

\Tr(\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{0}Z_{j}^{-1})=\frac{d(L-1)}{d^{L}+d(L-1)},

(40)

which is exponentially small in
L
.

|\rho\rangle\!\rangle
in the enlarged Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}\otimes\mathcal{\overline{H}}^{\otimes L}
:

|\rho\rangle\!\rangle=\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}\rho\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}},

(96)

where
\rho
only acts on the first copy (i.e., the Hilbert space of
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}
).

When choosing
\rho
as the MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}}\sum_{p=0}^{L-1}T^{p}\propto P_{\mathcal{T}}
, where
\mathcal{N}
is a normalization constant, the associated doubled state
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
defined in the enlarged Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}\otimes\mathcal{\overline{H}}^{\otimes L}
is:

|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}P_{\mathcal{T}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}(\mathds{1}+T+...+T^{L-1})\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}.

(97)

In the following, we will consider the case of qubits (
d=2
), but the calculation can be generalized to any qudit dimensions as well. Also, for simplicity, we consider the case where
L
is prime. We will derive the connected correlation function of the doubled state, defined as

R^{(2)}_{ij}=\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|Z_{i}\overline{Z}_{i}Z_{j}\overline{Z}_{j}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle-\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|Z_{i}\overline{Z}_{i}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|Z_{j}\overline{Z}_{j}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle,

(98)

where
Z_{i}
and
\overline{Z}_{i}
denote the Pauli-Z acting on
i
-th site of the
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}
and
\mathcal{\overline{H}}^{\otimes L}
, respectively, and
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
has been appropriately normalized.

We will show that there is a long-range order between the two local operators
Z_{i}\overline{Z}_{i}
and
Z_{j}\overline{Z}_{j}
: the correlation does not decay exponentially with their separation, albeit the
\frac{1}{L}
decay in magnitude. This is already sufficient to prove the long-range entanglement of the doubled state
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
. Namely,
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
cannot be prepared by a finite-depth local unitary circuit starting from a product state.

We also note that the above correlation function of the doubled state is exactly equal to the connected Rényi-2 correlator, defined as

R^{(2)}_{ij}=\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}-\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{j}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}.

(99)

Hence, we will perform the calculation based on this expression.

As a warm-up, we first calculate
\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}
:

\begin{split}\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p}Z_{i}T^{q}Z_{i})\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p}T^{q}Z_{i-q}Z_{i})\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{i})\\
\end{split}

(100)

When
q+p\neq 0~\text{mod}~L
, one has

\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{i})=\Tr(T^{q+p})=2.

(101)

For the first equality, we have used the ergodicity of the
(q+p)
-shift (because
L
is prime) and the cyclic property of the trace to move the pairs of Pauli-Zs to a single site for cancellation, as in Eq.
38
for the two-point function calculation. In the second equality, we expand the trace in the computational basis
\bm{\sigma}
, and notice that there are only two possible
\bm{\sigma}
s that can contribute:
\sigma_{i}=0~\forall~i
and
\sigma_{i}=1~\forall~i
.

When
q+p=0~\text{mod}~L
, one has

\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{i})=\Tr(Z_{i-q}Z_{i})=\delta_{q,0}~2^{L}.

(102)

Combining the results for both
p+q\neq 0
and
p+q=0
, one finds

\begin{split}\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[2^{L}+2L(L-1)\right]=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[2^{L}+O(L^{2})\right].\end{split}

(103)

On the other hand,

\begin{split}\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p+q})\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[\sum_{p+q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p+q})+\sum_{p+q\neq 0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p+q})\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[L2^{L}+2L(L-1)\right]\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[L2^{L}+O(L^{2})\right]\end{split}

(104)

By combining Eq.
103
and Eq.
104
, one finds

\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{L}+O(2^{-L}L).

(105)

The quantity
\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}
can be calculated using a similar strategy:

\begin{split}\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p}Z_{i}Z_{j}T^{q}Z_{i}Z_{j})\\
\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{p,q=0}^{L-1}\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{j-q}Z_{i}Z_{j})\end{split}

(106)

For the summation with
p+q\neq 0
, one again finds

\sum_{p+q\neq 0}\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{j-q}Z_{i}Z_{j})=\sum_{p+q\neq 0}\Tr(T^{p+q})=2L(L-1).

(107)

For the summation with
p+q=0
, one finds

\sum_{p+q=0}\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{j-q}Z_{i}Z_{j})=\sum_{p+q=0}\Tr(Z_{i-q}Z_{j-q}Z_{i}Z_{j})

(108)

For
i\neq j
, only the term corresponding to
p=q=0
can give a non-vanishing value, and therefore,

\sum_{p+q=0}\Tr(T^{p+q}Z_{i-q}Z_{j-q}Z_{i}Z_{j})=2^{L}

(109)

Combining the results for both
p+q\neq 0
and
p+q=0
, one finds

\begin{split}\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[2^{L}+2L(L-1)\right].\end{split}

(110)

With the results for both
\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}
and
\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}
from Eq.
104
and Eq.
110
, one finds

\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}Z_{i}Z_{j}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}}=\frac{1}{L}+O(2^{-L}L).

(111)

Finally, the connected Rényi-2 correlator is

R^{(2)}_{ij}=\frac{1}{L}-\frac{1}{L^{2}}+\cdots,

(112)

where
\cdots
denotes the contribution that vanishes exponentially in
L
. In the leading order,
R^{(2)}_{ij}
scales as
\frac{1}{L}
.

VI.2
SWSSB of translation

Here we will show that the MMIS
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
exhibits a strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) of the lattice translation. To this end, below we will first explain the physical mechanism of SWSSB, and then discuss the diagnostic.

Heuristically, SWSSB refers to a phenomenon where a strongly symmetric mixed state exhibits no long-range order, but develops a long-range order in the pure states that form an ensemble of the mixed state
18
;
34
;
21
. Correspondingly, the probe of such order typically relies on certain order parameters non-linear in the density matrix.

One simple way to think about SWSSB is to consider the double-state representation of the mixed state
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
:

|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}P_{\mathcal{T}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\propto\sum_{\bm{\sigma}}(\mathds{1}+T+...+T^{L-1})\ket{\bm{\sigma}}\ket{\bm{\sigma}}.

(113)

defined in the doubled Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}\otimes\mathcal{\overline{H}}^{\otimes L}
. Under such representation, the original strong symmetry of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, i.e.
T\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\rho_{\mathcal{T}}=\rho_{\mathcal{T}}T^{\dagger}
, translates to
T\times\overline{T}
symmetry of the doubled state
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
, namely,
|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle=T|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle=\overline{T}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle
, where
T
and
\overline{T}
act as the translations in
\mathcal{H}^{\otimes L}
and
\mathcal{\overline{H}}^{\otimes L}
, respectively.

The strong symmetry
T\times\overline{T}
can spontaneously be broken down to the diagonal symmetry generated by
T\overline{T}
, which corresponds to the weak symmetry of
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
and manifests as
T\rho_{\mathcal{T}}T^{\dagger}=\rho
. If this occurs, we say
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
exhibits the strong-to-weak spontaneous symmetry breaking (SWSSB) of the translation symmetry. To probe the symmetry-breaking pattern, as in the conventional SSB, we investigate whether there is a corresponding operator charged under the symmetry that exhibits a long-range order or correlation. To probe the SWSSB in the doubled-state representation, we investigate the correlation of operators that are charged under the individual translation
T
and
\overline{T}
, but are neutral under
T\overline{T}
. One such operator is
O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}
defined as

\begin{split}&O_{k}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{x=0}^{L-1}e^{ikx}Z_{x},\\
&\overline{O}_{k}=\frac{1}{L}\sum_{x=0}^{L-1}e^{ikx}\overline{Z}_{x},\end{split}

(114)

where
x=\{0,1,2,...,L-1\}
labels the real-space position, and
k\in\{\frac{2\pi n}{L}|n=0,1,2,...,L-1\}
labels the momenta. Since
TO_{k}T^{-1}=e^{-ik}O_{k}
and
\overline{T}\overline{O}_{-k}\overline{T}^{-1}=e^{ik}\overline{O}_{-k}
,
O_{k}
and
\overline{O}_{-k}
are individually charged under
T
and
\overline{T}
as long as
k\neq 0~\text{mod}~2\pi
, but are neutral under
T\overline{T}
due to the cancellation of the phase.

With this operator, one can then diagnose SWSSB using the so-called Rényi-2 correlator, which is also equal to the Rényi-1 correlator since the mixed state
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
is a projector. As we will show below in Appendix.
VI.2.1
, such a correlation exhibits
\frac{1}{L^{2}}
decays in the leading order, which does not conclusively identify SWSSB. In Appendix.
VI.2.2
, we introduce the ‘variance normalized’ Rényi-2 correlator, which is more appropriate due to the non-unitary nature of the charged operators. We analytically show that this newly defined quantity is a non-zero constant of the system size
L
, thereby indicating the SWSSB of translation.

VI.2.1
Rényi-2 correlations

With the charged operator defined above, we can explore SWSSB by studying the following correlation function defined for the doubled state:

\begin{split}R^{(2)}_{k}&=\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}\\
\end{split}

(115)

where we have considered
k\neq 0~\text{mod}~2\pi
. In terms of the physical mixed state
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
, the above can also be expressed in terms of

R^{(2)}_{k}=\frac{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}}{\Tr\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}},

(116)

which is also dubbed the Rényi-2 correlator.

We begin by calculating the numerator:

\begin{split}\Tr\rho O_{k}O_{-k}\rho O_{k}O_{-k}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{x,y,a,b}\sum_{p,q}e^{ik(x-y)+ik(a-b)}\Tr\left(T^{p}Z_{x}Z_{y}T^{q}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{x,y,a,b}\sum_{p,q}e^{ik(x-y)+ik(a-b)}\Tr\left(T^{p+q}Z_{x-q}Z_{y-q}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\frac{1}{L^{4}}\sum_{x,y,a,b}\sum_{p,q}e^{ik(x-y)+ik(a-b)}\Tr\left(T^{p+q}Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)\\
&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\frac{1}{L^{3}}\sum_{x,y,a,b}\sum_{p}e^{ik(x-y)+ik(a-b)}\Tr\left(T^{p}Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)\end{split}

(117)

where
x,y,a,b
denote the position labels arising from expanding
O_{k},O_{-k}
, and
p,q
denote the position labels arising from expanding
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
. In the last step, we have redefined the variable
p\to p-q
, and summed over
q
. To proceed, one can divide the summation
\sum_{p}
into the cases where
p\neq 0
and
p=0
.

When
p\neq 0
, one can again use the ergodicity of the
p
-shift and the cyclicity of the trace to find

\Tr\left(T^{p}Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)=\Tr(T^{p})=2,

(118)

as in the calculation discussed in Appendix.
VI.1
.

On the other hand, for
p=0
, one has

\Tr\left(T^{p}Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)=\Tr\left(Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)=2^{L}\left[\delta_{x,y}\delta_{a,b}+\delta_{x,a}\delta_{y,b}+\delta_{x,b}\delta_{y,a}-2\delta_{x,y}\delta_{x,a}\delta_{x,b}\right].

(119)

The above follows from the fact that the trace can be non-vanishing (specifically,
2^{L}
) only when all Pauli-Zs vanish; the first three terms come from different pair-wise pairing of Pauli-Zs, and the last term compensates the over-counting when all indices are the same. It follows that

\begin{split}\sum_{x,y,a,b}e^{ik(x-y)+ik(a-b)}\Tr\left(Z_{x}Z_{y}Z_{a}Z_{b}\right)&=2^{L}\left[L^{2}+|\sum_{x}e^{2ikx}|^{2}+L^{2}-2L\right]\\
&=2^{L}\left[2L^{2}+L^{2}(\delta_{k,0}+\delta_{k,\pi})-2L\right]\\
\end{split}

(120)

Therefore, in the leading order,

\Tr\rho O_{k}O_{-k}\rho O_{k}O_{-k}=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}2^{L}(\frac{2}{L}+\frac{1}{L}(\delta_{k,0}+\delta_{k,\pi})+O(\frac{1}{L^{2}})).

(121)

We note that in the above equation, the exponentially small correction from
p\neq 0
(Eq.
118
) has been included in
O(\frac{1}{L^{2}})
as well.

On the other hand, Eq.
104
shows

\begin{split}\Tr\rho^{2}&=\frac{1}{\mathcal{N}^{2}}\left[L2^{L}+O(L^{2})\right].\end{split}

(122)

It follows that the Rényi-2 correlator for
k\neq 0
is

\boxed{R^{(2)}_{k}=\frac{1}{L^{2}}\left(2+\delta_{k,\pi}\right)+O(\frac{1}{L^{3}})}

(123)

Therefore,
R^{(2)}_{k}
exhibits
\frac{1}{L^{2}}
scaling in the leading order. As a side note,
\delta_{k,\pi}=0
since
k=\pi
is never allowed for a finite prime
L>2
.

VI.2.2
Variance-normalized Rényi-2 correlations

To motivate the variance-normalized Rényi-2 correlator, we consider an alternative aspect of SSB from the response of inserting charged operators. In particular, we define
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
as the overlap between the normalized doubled state
\frac{|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}}
and the doubled state
subject to the application of the non-unitary operator
O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k}
, namely,
\frac{O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|(O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k})^{2}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}}
. Specifically,

\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}=\frac{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}{\sqrt{\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle\langle\!\langle\rho_{\mathcal{T}}|(O_{k}\overline{O}_{-k}O_{-k}\overline{O}_{k})^{2}|\rho_{\mathcal{T}}\rangle\!\rangle}}.

(124)

In terms of the original density matrix
\rho_{\mathcal{T}}
,
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
can be expressed as

\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}=\frac{\Tr[\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}]}{\sqrt{\Tr[\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}]\Tr[(O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k})^{2}]}},

(125)

Note that if the charged operator is unitary, the denominator will be reduced to
\Tr[\rho_{\mathcal{T}}^{2}]
, and
\tilde{R}_{k}^{(2)}
becomes the conventional Rényi-2 correlator.

Compared to Appendix.
VI.2.1
, the new quantity we need to consider is
\Tr[(O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k})^{2}]
, which can be calculated as follows:

\begin{split}&\Tr[(O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k})^{2}]\\
&=\Tr[O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}O_{k}O_{-k}\rho_{\mathcal{T}}O_{k}O_{-k}]\\
&=\frac{1}{L^{4}\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{\{x_{i},y_{i}|i=1,2,3,4\}}\sum_{p,q}e^{ik(x_{1}-x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{4})}e^{ik(y_{1}-y_{2}+y_{3}-y_{4})}\Tr[T^{p}Z_{x_{1}}Z_{x_{2}}Z_{x_{3}}Z_{x_{4}}T^{q}Z_{y_{1}}Z_{y_{2}}Z_{y_{3}}Z_{y_{4}}]\\
&=\frac{L}{L^{4}\mathcal{N}^{2}}\sum_{\{x_{i},y_{i}|i=1,2,3,4\}}\sum_{p}e^{ik(x_{1}-x_{2}+x_{3}-x_{4})}e^{ik(y_{1}-y_{2}+y_{3}-y_{4})}\Tr[T^{p}Z_{x_{1}}Z_{x_{2}}Z_{x_{3}}Z_{x_{4}}Z_{y_{1}}Z_{y_{2}}Z_{y_{3}}Z_{y_{4}}].\end{split}

(126)

For prime
L
, as we have seen in Appendix.
VI.2.1
, the contribution from
p\neq 0
will be exponentially small compared to the contribution from
p=0
. Hence below we will only focus on the
p=0
contribution. In this case, the central quantity we need to calculate is
\Tr[Z_{x_{1}}Z_{x_{2}}Z_{x_{3}}Z_{x_{4}}Z_{y_{1}}Z_{y_{2}}Z_{y_{3}}Z_{y_{4}}]
. In order to have a non-vanishing trace (specifically,
2^{L}
), the 8 Pauli-Zs must be an identity, which can arise from various pair-wise pairing patterns of Pauli-Zs. For instance, one can have the following pairing pattern:
