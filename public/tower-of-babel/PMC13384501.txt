Comments on the Letter – Intraoperative Tissue Oxygenation and Postoperative Creatinine Rise: Interpreting the Microcirculatory Context
Authors: Bhakti Banerjee, Haripada Das, Lini Srivastava, Snigdha Pramanik, Diptimay Majumder
Published: 2026

Dear Editor,

We hereby express our high appreciation towards to the readers’ interest in our study titled “A Prospective Observational Study on Role of Intraoperative Renal & Muscle Oxygen Saturation on Post-Operative Serum Creatinine Level in Patients Undergoing Cardiac Surgery on Cardiopulmonary Bypass.” We would also like to express our gratitude to your esteemed journal to instigate this thoughtful constructive discussion on the context of utilizing NIRS to dive deeper into study of regional perfusion during CPB.

Authors of the letter titled “Intraoperative Tissue Oxygenation and Postoperative Creatinine Rise: Interpreting the Microcirculatory Context” have raised very interesting discussion points in our study. We do agree with the readers’ concern regarding serum creatinine kinetics in the immediate postoperative period. Immediate post-operative serum creatinine changes do not necessarily have to amount to AKI. We tried to find the relationship between drop in SrO2 & SmO2 with changes in serum creatinine, to foresee actual AKI in at risk cases by means of NIRS.

It was truly a deliberate methodology obtained to exclude patients with a skin-to-kidney depth of less than 4 cm. One of the ideas of our study was to get a future direction in using NIRS sensors on thenar muscle (being readily accessible) in cases which preclude the sensors being placed over loin area for renal perfusion monitoring, and to obtain a surrogate marker of regional perfusion in renal area.

A brilliant point of agreement with readers where we also hypothesized that conventional CPB-targets of organ perfusion cannot be only global markers like MAP or mixed venous oxygen saturation. NIRS is a potential monitor of regional perfusion influenced by number of variables like hemoglobin concentration, systemic vascular resistance as well as MAP. The conventional global CPB-targets of organ perfusion may be moved towards more organ specific microcirculatory marker obtained by NIRS. We do completely agree on the requirement of larger robust studies with indexed perfusion parameters to accept NIRS for setting CPB-targets.

To summarize, the NIRS has been studied to explore intra-CPB organ perfusion characteristics as well as its potential application on a readily accessible site to guide organ specific targets of CPB.
