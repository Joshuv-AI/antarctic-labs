Magnetic and electric properties of the metallic kagome antiferromagnet CrRhAs Preprint: Draft Bin Shen
Franziska Breitner, Bin Shen, Anton Jesche, Alexander A. Tsirlin, Philipp Gegenwart

Abstract
CrRhAs is an antiferromagnetic kagome metal predicted to host a nontrivial spin texture with vector spin chirality [Huang et al. , npj Quantum Mater. 8 , 32 (2023)]. We report the synthesis and basic characterization of CrRhAs single crystals, which exhibit an antiferromagnetic transition with T N T_{\rm N} = 150 K, evidenced by electrical transport, heat capacity, and magnetization measurements. Hall resistivity varies linearly with magnetic field, i.e., there is no nonlinear Hall contribution. Intriguingly, the Hall coefficient changes sign between the configurations of j | a ​ b , H ⟂ a ​ b j\parallel ab,H\perp ab and j | c , H ⟂ c j\parallel c,H\perp c , which is likely connected to a peculiar topology of the Fermi surface. Furthermore, for j | a ​ b j\parallel ab , the Hall coefficient shows a pronounced and continuous enhancement below T N T_{\rm N} , signaling a significant reconstruction of the Fermi surface or an extra scattering from the magnons. Our results offer guidance for exploring anomalous electric transport phenomena in exotic magnetic systems.



I
I. Introduction

Kagome metals have emerged as a pivotal platform for exploring frustrated geometry and its interplay with electronic band topology. The two-dimensional kagome lattice leads to flat bands, van Hove singularities, and topological band crossings
Wang et al. 2013
;
Kiesel et al. 2013
;
Profe et al. 2024
, while antiferromagnetic interactions on the kagome network stabilize noncollinear spin configurations
Wang et al. 2024
. These distinctive magnetic structures are predicted to generate nonzero Berry curvature, which can manifest itself experimentally through the anomalous Hall effect (AHE)
Chen et al. 2014
;
Nakatsuji et al. 2015
;
Busch et al. 2020
.

In real kagome materials, additional factors can influence their electronic properties and magnetism. For example, interlayer couplings between the kagome planes and distortions of the ideal kagome lattice are commonly present in many of the kagome compounds. The ZrNiAl-type structure (space group
P\bar{6}2m
) represents a prominent family of intermetallic compounds featuring a distorted kagome lattice, where the transition-metal or rare-earth atoms occupy positions that deviate from the perfect symmetry of the ideal kagome network. Unlike the ideal kagome lattice, the twisted kagome geometry of the ZrNiAl structure incorporates two types of triangles rotated oppositely around the crystallographic
c
-axis, leading to a loss of inversion symmetry. This structural distortion profoundly modifies the electronic band structure and magnetism and may lead to new emergent quantum phenomena, such as novel spin textures and AHE observed in HoAgGe
Zhao et al. 2020
;
Zhao et al. 2024
.

CrRhAs crystallizes in the ZrNiAl-type structure, where the magnetic Cr atoms form a twisted kagome lattice in the
ab
-plane
Deyris et al. 1974
. Early studies on polycrystalline samples of this material revealed an antiferromagnetic transition at

T_{\mathrm{N}}\approx 165
K
Ohta et al. 1990
;
Ohta et al. 1995
, accompanied by a minute
expansion of the
c
-axis and an equally small contraction of the
ab
-plane at
T_{\mathrm{N}}

Ohta et al. 1995
. Magnetization measurements showed a linear field dependence up to 15 T in the antiferromagnetic phase
Kaneko et al. 1992
. Hydrostatic pressure experiments demonstrated a slight suppression of
T_{\mathrm{N}}
with increasing pressure
Ohta et al. 1995
. Recent density functional theory (DFT) calculations confirm antiferromagnetic exchange couplings and predict spin textures where individual triangles adopt
120^{\circ}
order either with positive or with negative vector chirality, and suggest the possible existence of nontrivial topological features in the electronic band structure of CrRhAs
Huang et al. 2023
.

Here, we report the successful growth of CrRhAs single crystals and present their fundamental properties alongside Hall effect measurements. Our study identifies CrRhAs as a correlated kagome antiferromagnet, likely adopting a coplanar noncollinear magnetic structure. No nonlinear Hall effect is observed in either the paramagnetic or antiferromagnetic state. Instead, the sign of the Hall coefficient reverses from positive for in-plane (
ab
-plane) current to negative for out-of-plane (
c
-axis) current configurations. Moreover, the Hall coefficient undergoes a dramatic change below the Néel temperature. This behavior suggests an anisotropic reconstruction of the Fermi surface or scattering rate driven by antiferromagnetic ordering.

II
II. Methods

Single crystals of CrRhAs were synthesized using a bismuth flux method. The starting materials were weighed in a stoichiometric ratio (Cr:Rh:As:Bi = 1:2:1:15) and placed inside a standard Canfield crucible set consisting of two Al
2
O
3
crucibles and a strainer. This set was sealed inside a quartz tube under 200 mbar argon pressure. The crystal growth was performed inside a standard Muffle furnace placed under the fume hood. The sample was heated to 600
∘
C over the course of 6 h and cured at this temperature for another 6 h. The temperature was then increased to 1100
∘
C over 3 h and held for 2 h, followed by a slow cooldown to 650
∘
C with a cooling rate of 2.5
∘
C/h. The crystals were then separated from the flux by centrifugation. Any remaining flux was removed by hydrochloric acid etching. The chemical composition and phase purity were verified by energy-dispersive x-ray spectroscopy (EDX) and x-ray diffraction (XRD).

Additionally, high-resolution powder XRD data
Tsirlin et al. 2025
were collected at the ID22 beamline of ESRF (Grenoble, France) using the wavelength of 0.4 Å and multi-analyzer detector setup
Fitch et al. 2023
. A portion of crystals together with a small amount of residual flux was ground into fine powder and loaded into thin borosilicate glass capillaries that were spun during the measurement. Sample was cooled with the He-flow cryostat.
Jana2006
was used for the structure refinement
Petr̆íc̆ek et al. 2014
.

Electrical resistivity was measured in a Physical Property Measurement System (PPMS, Quantum Design) using a standard four-probe configuration. Heat capacity was determined via the relaxation method in PPMS. Magnetization measurements were performed in an MPMS3 SQUID magnetometer (Quantum Design).

For Hall effect measurement, three samples (S1, S2, and S3) were prepared with orthogonal current-field geometries. Samples S1 and S2 were measured with the current applied along the crystallographic
c
-axis, whereas S3 was measured with the current within the
ab
-plane. To optimize sensitivity, all samples were polished to thicknesses of 5–20
\mu
m prior to measurement.

Density-functional theory (DFT) band-structure calculations were performed in the
FPLO

Koepernik and Eschrig 1999
and
VASP

Kresse and Furthmüller 1996a
;
Kresse and Furthmüller 1996b
codes using the experimental crystal structure at 50 K. The Perdew-Burke-Ernzerhof version of the exchange-correlation potential
Perdew et al. 1996
was applied.

IV
IV. Discussion

CrRhAs stands apart from other kagome metals in that its ground state is antiferromagnetic. Antiferromagnetism arises from the mostly localized magnetic moments that can be tentatively assigned to Cr
3+
. It is also instructive to analyze the role of correlation effects in this material, as the Sommerfeld coefficient of
\gamma=33
mJ mol
-1
K
-2
appears to be quite high for a
3d
compound. Indeed, the nonmagnetic DFT calculation (Fig.
7
) returns the density of states (DOS) at the Fermi level of
N(E_{\rm{F}})\simeq 4.5
eV
-1
/f.u. that corresponds to the broad peak of Cr
3d
states near the Fermi level. Introducing magnetism splits these states and largely removes them from the Fermi level, resulting in
N(E_{\rm{F}})\simeq 2.1
eV
-1
/f.u. for the collinear up-up-down magnetic structure or 2.3 eV
-1
/f.u. for the
120^{\circ}
magnetic structure. We thus obtain the bare Sommerfeld coefficient of
\gamma_{\rm bare}\simeq 5.2
mJ mol
-1
K
-2
and the sizable renormalization factor of
m^{*}/m_{e}=\gamma/\gamma_{\rm bare}\simeq 6.3
indicative of moderate electronic correlations in CrRhAs.

Another interesting observation is the large effective moment of
4.3-4.4

\mu_{B}
obtained from the Curie-Weiss analysis of the magnetic susceptibility. It resonates with the local magnetic moment of about 3
\mu_{\rm{B}}
on Cr atoms in DFT (PBE). CrRhAs shows a strong tendency toward localization of the Cr
3d
electrons even without electronic correlations introduced. The itinerant nature of the material should be at least partially attributed to the residual Rh and As states at the Fermi level.

Although magnetic structure of CrRhAs has not been determined experimentally yet, our data suggest a noncollinear spin configuration with spins lying in the
ab
plane, because the measured
M(T)
resembles that of noncollinear antiferromagnets
Johnston 2012
;
Jin et al. 2019
;
Huddart et al. 2019
. Such a magnetic structure has been confirmed in a sister compound, FeCrAs
Jin et al. 2019
;
Huddart et al. 2019
. It was also shown to be energetically favorable in CrRhAs
Huang et al. 2023
.
A natural question at this juncture is why the Hall resistivity of the material is essentially linear, as in ordinary metals, and lacks any additional contributions that would typically occur in metals with complex magnetic order.

Two of such contributions are usually considered. Topological Hall effect originates from a nonzero scalar spin chirality (SSC) associated with a noncoplanar magnetic structure in the real space and does not require spin–orbit coupling (SOC)
Chen et al. 2024
;
Jiang et al. 2024
. However, CrRhAs more likely adopts a coplanar antiferromagnetic configuration
Huang et al. 2023
, for which the SSC vanishes. Therefore, a topological Hall contribution may be absent in this case.

Density of states for CrRhAs calculated using the PBE functional with (bottom) and without (top) introducing the spin polarization. The Fermi level is at zero energy.

On the other hand, the intrinsic anomalous Hall effect arises from the Berry curvature in the momentum space and generally relies on a finite SOC
Chen et al. 2014
;
Busch et al. 2020
. In systems with weak SOC, the resulting Berry curvature and anomalous Hall conductivity are typically small. In
CrRhAs
, the SOC strength is relatively weak
Huang et al. 2023
, and symmetry constraints associated with its antiferromagnetic structure may further suppress any intrinsic anomalous Hall response.
Within experimental resolution, no discernible nonlinear component can be identified, making it difficult to extract any possible anomalous contribution. These considerations suggest the absence of an observable anomalous Hall effect in CrRhAs.

One unusual feature of the Hall response in CrRhAs is its sign change upon swapping the directions of current and applied magnetic field. In the simplest form, the Hall coefficient
R_{\rm{H}}
provides a direct measure of the carrier concentration
n
through the relation:

R_{\rm{H}}=\frac{1}{nq}

(1)

where
q
is the elementary charge. This relation usually breaks down in multiband systems. Thus, a two-band version is conventionally adopted,

R_{\rm{H}}=\frac{p\mu_{h}^{2}-n\mu_{e}^{2}}{e(p\mu_{h}+n\mu_{e})^{2}}

(2)

where
n
(
p
) and
\mu_{e}
(
\mu_{h}
) represent the electron (hole) concentrations and mobilities, respectively. However, under low-field conditions (
\omega_{c}\tau\ll 1
) where the Drude relaxation time (
\tau
) is significantly shorter than the electron cyclotron period (as in our case), electrons traverse only a small segment of the Fermi surface before undergoing scattering events (e.g., due to phonons or impurities). In this regime, the Hall effect predominantly reveals the anisotropic scattering rate, electron velocity, and effective mass at the specific Fermi surface points, rather than providing a direct information about the true carrier type or the volume of the Fermi surface
Banik and Overhauser 1978
;
Hurd 2012
;
Ong 1991
;
Ouisse et al. 2019
, especially in highly anisotropic systems. Typically, the Hall response is dominated by the segments of the Fermi surface that have high Fermi velocity and large curvature in semiclassical Boltzmann formalism. Therefore, local Fermi surface geometry is important for interpreting the Hall effect.

. The orange shade indicates the segments with a high Fermi velocity.

In anisotropic systems, the Hall coefficient can display sign reversals originating from Fermi surface topology and competing carrier dynamics
Eltsev et al. 2002
;
Araki et al. 2014
;
Uchida et al. 2015
;
Zhang et al. 2019
;
Mondal et al. 2020
;
Luo et al. 2023
. Particularly in systems with multiple carrier types, the dominant contribution to transport properties becomes direction-dependent, with anisotropic carrier mobilities determining the observed Hall response based on the current orientation relative to the crystallographic axes. This sign reversal can even occur in anisotropic single-band systems
He et al. 2019
. DFT predicts a multiband nature of CrRhAs, and a highly anisotropic band topology in terms of the Fermi velocity and the curvature of the Fermi surface
Huang et al. 2023
. An inspection of the electronic bands of CrRhAs reveals a Fermi surface with a concave shape in combination with a high Fermi velocity (as schematically shown in Fig.
8
, and Fig. 8h in Ref.
Huang et al. 2023
). This peculiar topology has been implied as the main driver of the sign change of the Hall coefficient, as observed in NaSn
2
As
2

He et al. 2019
, and in LaRh
6
In
4

Luo et al. 2023
, which could also qualitatively explain the similar observation in CrRhAs.

In the paramagnetic phase of CrRhAs, the Hall coefficient exhibits a relatively weak temperature dependence—a behavior typical of conventional metals, which originated in phonon scattering in Bloch-Boltzmann theory
Ong 1991
. A pronounced change in the Hall coefficient between the paramagnetic and antiferromagnetic phases has been reported in other antiferromagnetic systems
Rhyne 1969
;
Mun et al. 2009
;
Feng et al. 2015
;
Shi et al. 2024
. Such a transition is often attributed to either Fermi surface reconstruction (e.g., via the opening of a spin-density-wave gap), or changes in the anisotropic scattering rate (e.g., due to the scattering from magnons or a spin-dependent scattering). The underlying mechanism in CrRhAs requires further dedicated investigation of the Fermi surface to elucidate the origin of these Hall effect anomalies.

V
V. Summary

In summary, we have grown single crystals of the kagome antiferromagnet CrRhAs and uncovered several features of its low-temperature behavior: i) In-plane spin direction in the antiferromagnetically ordered state, most likely with a non-collinear configuration arising from the frustrated kagome geometry; ii) Absence of nonlinear contributions to the Hall effect; iii) Sign change of the Hall coefficient depending on the directions of the applied field and current; iv) Pronounced change in the Hall coefficient the onset of antiferromagnetic order. Our results elucidate the strong interplay between magnetism and electronic transport in antiferromagnetic CrRhAs, highlighting its distinctive behavior among metallic kagome magnets and providing an interesting counterpart to the more common ferromagnetic kagome metals.
