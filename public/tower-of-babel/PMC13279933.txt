Thermoelastic wave propagation in fibre-reinforced plates with exponential temperature-dependent conductivity under fluid–structure interaction
Authors: Areej Almoneef, Munirah Alotaibi, Shreen El-Sapa, Khaled Lotfy, Alaa A. El-Bary
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This work investigates thermoelastic wave propagation in a fibre-reinforced plate interacting with non-viscous fluid layers while incorporating exponential temperature-dependent thermal conductivity within generalized thermoelasticity. The novelty lies in the unified treatment of reinforcement anisotropy, fluid–structure interaction, generalized thermal relaxation, and nonlinear thermal conductivity effects. The coupled governing equations are formulated under plane strain conditions and solved analytically using the normal mode method for coupled thermoelasticity (CT), Lord–Shulman (LS), and Green-Lindsay (GL) theories. A Kirchhoff transformation is employed to handle nonlinear exponential conductivity while preserving analytical tractability. Numerical results demonstrate that positive conductivity variation enhances heat diffusion and reduces peak thermal and stress amplitudes by approximately 15–25%, whereas negative conductivity promotes thermal localization and increases oscillatory persistence. Fibre reinforcement suppresses displacement amplitudes by nearly 10–20%, indicating increased effective stiffness and improved attenuation of thermoelastic disturbances. Among the considered theories, the GL model produces the strongest damping due to dual relaxation effects. The combined interaction of reinforcement, nonlinear conductivity, and fluid coupling significantly modifies thermoelastic wave attenuation and stress redistribution. The proposed formulation extends conventional constant-conductivity thermoelastic models by integrating exponential nonlinear conductivity with reinforcement and fluid loading, providing new insight into attenuation control and thermoelastic stability in advanced composite structures.

Introduction

Understanding thermoelastic interactions in advanced composite structures is essential for modern engineering applications where thermal loads and mechanical stresses act simultaneously. Fibre-reinforced plates, widely used in aerospace, marine, and civil engineering systems, exhibit anisotropic mechanical behavior due to embedded reinforcements, making their thermoelastic response more complex than that of isotropic materials. When such structures are subjected to thermal disturbances and fluid loading, the coupled interaction between mechanical deformation, temperature evolution, and wave propagation becomes highly significant. Classical thermoelastic models typically assume constant thermal conductivity; however, experimental observations indicate that thermal conductivity often varies with temperature, especially in composite and high-performance materials operating under elevated thermal environments. Incorporating temperature-dependent conductivity into thermoelastic formulations enhances the physical realism of the model and allows for a more accurate description of thermal diffusion and stress redistribution. Therefore, a comprehensive analytical investigation that accounts for fibre reinforcement, fluid–structure interaction, and exponential variable thermal conductivity within the framework of generalized thermoelasticity is both timely and practically relevant.

Fibre-reinforced composite materials have become indispensable in modern engineering applications due to their high strength-to-weight ratio, superior stiffness characteristics, fatigue resistance, and enhanced thermal performance. These materials are widely used in aerospace structures, marine components, mechanical systems, and civil infrastructure, where anisotropic mechanical behavior and thermo-mechanical stability are critical. The theoretical modeling of fibre-reinforced elastic solids was systematically initiated by Belfield et al.1, who analyzed stress distributions in plates reinforced by fibres arranged in concentric configurations, thereby establishing the mathematical basis for anisotropic reinforcement effects. Since then, wave propagation and reflection phenomena in fibre-reinforced media have been studied extensively. Singh and Singh2 investigated plane wave reflection at the free surface of a fibre-reinforced half-space, while Singh3 examined wave propagation in thermally conducting fibre-reinforced composites. Further developments included the analysis of shear wave velocities in reinforced anisotropic layers under gravitational effects4, revealing the sensitivity of wave characteristics to reinforcement parameters and material anisotropy. Although these investigations established important insights into wave propagation and anisotropic reinforcement effects, practical engineering systems frequently operate under coupled thermal and mechanical environments. Consequently, extending fibre-reinforced elasticity toward generalized thermoelastic formulations became necessary to account for finite thermal propagation speeds and relaxation phenomena.

As thermoelastic interactions gained importance, researchers extended fibre-reinforced elasticity models to thermoelastic environments. Abbas and Abd-Alla5 studied the impact of initial stress on fibre-reinforced anisotropic thermoelastic thick plates, demonstrating how pre-stress modifies thermal and mechanical responses. Surface wave behavior in rotating and magnetized fibre-reinforced media was explored by Hussien et al.6, highlighting the combined influence of reinforcement, magnetic fields, and rotational effects. Othman and Lotfy7 examined magneto-thermoelastic interactions under different generalized thermoelastic theories with gravitational influences, while Abd-Alla et al.8 addressed wave propagation in fibre-reinforced anisotropic thermoelastic media subjected to gravity fields. These works collectively emphasized the necessity of incorporating multiple physical couplings when modeling reinforced composite structures.

The limitations of classical thermoelasticity, which predicts infinite heat propagation speed, motivated the adoption of generalized thermoelastic theories incorporating relaxation times. Lotfy9 analyzed Mode-I crack problems in fibre-reinforced generalized thermoelastic media, and Lotfy and Al-Bary10 later extended such analyses to rotational fibre-reinforced thermoelastic configurations with thermal relaxation effects. Abbas11 introduced a three-phase-lag thermoelastic model for anisotropic fibre-reinforced media, and Abo-Dahab and Lotfy12 investigated fractional derivative heat transfer in magneto-thermoelastic fibre-reinforced solids under rotational effects. Sur and Kanoria13 modeled fibre-reinforced magneto-thermoelastic plates with internal heat sources, while Zenkour and Abouelregal14 studied fractional thermoelastic crack problems in reinforced media. More recently, Alhashash and Abouelregal15 analyzed thermoelastic vibrations using fractional Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) models, demonstrating the importance of memory effects in wave attenuation. The generalized thermoelastic theories adopted here are continuum-level theoretical models used to account for finite thermal propagation speed and relaxation effects in coupled thermo-mechanical systems. Their applicability to fibre-reinforced composites is justified when the composite is treated as an equivalent anisotropic homogeneous medium with effective elastic, thermal, and reinforcement parameters. The present formulation is verified through recovery of classical limiting cases, boundedness of admissible normal-mode solutions, and consistency with established thermoelastic behavior. Nevertheless, direct experimental validation remains necessary for material-specific prediction. Future experimental work should measure temperature histories, displacement fields, and stress responses in fibre-reinforced composite plates subjected to controlled thermal excitation and fluid loading in order to calibrate relaxation times and conductivity parameters.

In addition to homogeneous reinforcement models, functionally graded fibre-reinforced media have attracted significant attention. Sur and Kanoria16 derived field equations and memory responses for graded reinforced media subjected to heat sources. Barak and Dhankhar17 investigated inclined loading in graded fibre-reinforced thermoelastic media with temperature-dependent properties, while Kalkal et al.18 examined two-dimensional deformations in rotating graded reinforced half-spaces under magnetic fields. Numerical modeling approaches, such as finite element analysis under dual-phase-lag heat conduction, have also been applied to fibre-reinforced anisotropic cylinders19, confirming the practical relevance of such models. Fibre-reinforced composites are increasingly employed in engineering systems operating under combined thermal and fluid environments, including aerospace thermal protection structures, aircraft panels subjected to aerodynamic heating, marine composite components, cryogenic storage systems, turbine structures, and advanced energy devices. In such applications, thermal loading may arise from environmental heating, frictional effects, laser irradiation, or rapid temperature changes, while surrounding fluids introduce additional pressure and acoustic coupling effects.

Consequently, understanding coupled thermoelastic responses in fluid-loaded reinforced structures is important for predicting attenuation, stress redistribution, thermal localization, and structural durability. The interaction between thermal loading and surrounding fluid media may significantly modify wave propagation characteristics through interface impedance and energy transfer mechanisms.

Temperature-dependent material properties represent another essential advancement in thermoelastic analysis. Deswal et al.20 examined wave reflection in fibre-reinforced thermoelastic half-spaces with temperature-dependent parameters, and Abouelregal and Alesemi21 evaluated thermal and mechanical waves in anisotropic fibre-reinforced magnetic viscoelastic solids with variable properties using the MGT model. These investigations clearly demonstrated that temperature-dependent conductivity significantly alters wave dispersion, stress concentration, and attenuation behavior22. Furthermore, studies in semiconductor and photo-thermoelastic media have emphasized the strong influence of variable thermal conductivity, magnetic fields, plasma interactions, and laser heating on coupled wave propagation23–27. For instance, Ibrahim et al.23 and Nassar et al.27 showed that incorporating temperature-dependent conductivity enhances the physical realism of photo-thermoelastic and optoelectronic models. Fractional and microstructural semiconductor analyses further expanded the understanding of thermal–mechanical coupling in advanced materials24–26. The incorporation of temperature-dependent thermal conductivity is motivated by experimental observations showing that thermal transport properties in advanced materials frequently vary significantly with temperature, particularly under high thermal gradients. In composite materials, aerospace structures, and thermal protection systems, conductivity variation directly affects heat diffusion, stress concentration, and thermo-mechanical stability. Linear conductivity models may provide reasonable approximations for moderate temperature ranges; however, nonlinear forms, including exponential conductivity laws, become increasingly relevant under severe thermal environments where material properties change rapidly. Exponential temperature-dependent conductivity has attracted attention in engineering applications involving elevated temperatures, such as aerospace thermal barriers, turbine blade coatings, nuclear reactor components, laser-heated materials, and microelectronic packaging. In these systems, nonlinear thermal diffusion influences energy dissipation, structural durability, and resistance to thermo-mechanical failure. Consequently, adopting exponential conductivity improves the physical realism of thermoelastic models and enables better prediction of wave attenuation and stress redistribution in advanced composite structures.

Recent studies have substantially advanced nonlinear and non-Fourier thermoelastic modeling under variable thermal conductivity and multiphysics coupling conditions. Li et al.27 developed a time-domain finite element framework to investigate nonlinear transient responses in generalized diffusion–thermoelastic systems with variable thermal conductivity and diffusivity, demonstrating the strong influence of temperature-dependent properties on thermal and mechanical fields. Zhou and Li28 further analyzed transient thermoelastic diffusion in two-dimensional metallic structures with high-order temperature-dependent material properties, emphasizing the importance of nonlinear constitutive behavior. Guo et al.29 proposed a nonlinear FEM approach for non-Fourier thermoelasticity with space-dependent thermal conductivity under laser heating, showing that variable conductivity significantly modifies transient stress propagation and thermal attenuation. In addition, fractional-order and memory-dependent thermoelastic formulations have been introduced to capture complex heat transport mechanisms in composite structures30. Recent developments have also incorporated nonlocal multiphysics coupling, including photo-carrier dynamics, plasma effects, and semiconductor responses31, as well as electron–phonon interactions and two-temperature transport mechanisms in multilaminated piezoelectric composites32. These investigations collectively indicate that nonlinear conductivity and advanced thermal transport models play a fundamental role in predicting wave attenuation, stress redistribution, and thermo-mechanical stability in modern materials. However, despite these advances, the combined effects of exponential temperature-dependent conductivity, fibre reinforcement, generalized thermoelastic relaxation, and fluid–structure interaction in reinforced plate systems remain insufficiently explored.

As generalized thermoelastic theories evolved to capture memory and relaxation effects, attention gradually shifted toward more realistic material descriptions involving graded structures, temperature-dependent properties, and heterogeneous composites. Parallel to developments in reinforcement modeling and temperature-dependent properties, fluid–structure interaction problems in thermoelastic systems have gained prominence. The classical theory of elastic waves in layered media was formulated by Ewing et al.33, providing a foundation for analyzing plates interacting with surrounding fluids. Recently, Ailawalia et al.34 investigated thermoelastic plates loaded with viscous fluid layers under the Moore–Gibson–Thompson framework, highlighting the impact of fluid loading on thermal and mechanical responses. Modern generalized thermoelastic theories incorporating memory-dependent kernels and two-temperature formulations have also been applied to complex geometries35–38, emphasizing the need to combine advanced heat conduction models with realistic material behavior. Previous investigations have employed constant, linear, and higher-order temperature-dependent conductivity laws to describe nonlinear thermal transport39–42. Linear formulations generally provide adequate approximations for moderate thermal variations, whereas exponential models may better capture pronounced conductivity changes occurring under severe thermal loading. Consequently, exponential conductivity offers increased flexibility for investigating thermoelastic responses in materials subjected to strong temperature gradients. Recent investigations on fibre-reinforced composite materials have increasingly focused on improving interfacial characteristics and enhancing mechanical performance under complex environmental conditions. Xiang et al.43 demonstrated significant improvement in the mechanical properties of basalt fibre-reinforced epoxy composites through construction of organic/inorganic hybrid interfaces, emphasizing the critical role of reinforcement architecture in stiffness enhancement and damage resistance. Furthermore, Jiang et al.44 employed a hybrid experiment–simulation–machine learning framework to investigate cryogenic interfacial parameters and low-temperature failure mechanisms in carbon/epoxy composites, highlighting the importance of multiscale interfacial effects in predicting composite behavior under severe thermal environments. These studies indicate that reinforcement mechanisms strongly influence stress transfer, durability, and thermo-mechanical stability, thereby motivating further theoretical investigations into coupled thermoelastic responses in reinforced structures.

Recent advances in generalized thermoelasticity have increasingly focused on non-Fourier heat transport, strain-gradient effects, and wave propagation in advanced composite and metamaterial systems. Hosseini and Li45 investigated laser pulse-induced thermoelastic wave propagation in porous materials using Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) and Love–Bishop theories through a meshless framework, demonstrating the importance of higher-order heat transport models under ultrafast thermal excitation. In a subsequent study, Hosseini and Li46 analyzed strain-gradient effects in MGT generalized coupled non-Fickian diffusion–thermoelasticity for nanorod resonators, emphasizing size-dependent thermo-mechanical behavior. Furthermore, Hosseini and Zhang47 explored Green–Naghdi thermoelastic wave propagation in GPL/CNT-reinforced metamaterials with energy dissipation, highlighting the influence of reinforcement on band structure and attenuation mechanisms. Plasma-assisted photo-thermoelastic wave propagation in semiconductor nanorods incorporating strain-gradient MGT theory was also studied by Hosseini and Zhang48, demonstrating complex coupling among thermal, electronic, and mechanical fields. These investigations collectively indicate that advanced generalized thermoelastic models and reinforced materials exhibit highly coupled wave propagation behavior under nonclassical heat transport mechanisms. However, the combined effects of exponential temperature-dependent conductivity, fibre reinforcement, fluid loading, and generalized thermoelastic relaxation in reinforced plate structures remain insufficiently explored.

Although fibre-reinforced composites are often designed to improve mechanical performance, their thermo-mechanical behavior under coupled thermal and fluid environments remains a significant challenge, particularly when material properties vary strongly with temperature. Therefore, investigation of thermoelastic responses under nonlinear conductivity assumptions is relevant for advanced composite applications subjected to severe operating conditions. The present study adopts CT, LS, and GL formulations because they constitute classical benchmark generalized thermoelastic theories representing increasing levels of thermal relaxation and coupling effects. The CT theory corresponds to the absence of relaxation mechanisms, LS introduces finite thermal propagation through a single relaxation parameter, and GL incorporates additional relaxation effects leading to enhanced damping behavior. Consequently, comparison among these models enables systematic evaluation of how thermal memory influences thermoelastic responses in reinforced structures.

Although more advanced theories such as dual-phase-lag (DPL), Green–Naghdi (GN), and Moore–Gibson–Thompson (MGT) thermoelasticity can capture additional non-Fourier transport mechanisms, they introduce higher-order effects and supplementary material parameters that substantially increase model complexity. Therefore, the present work focuses on CT, LS, and GL theories to establish fundamental insight into the combined effects of reinforcement, nonlinear conductivity, and fluid interaction before extending toward more sophisticated formulations.

Despite these advances, existing studies predominantly examined isolated mechanisms rather than their simultaneous interaction. This limitation motivates the development of integrated formulations capable of capturing reinforcement effects, nonlinear conductivity, generalized thermoelasticity, and fluid coupling within a unified framework. Despite these comprehensive contributions, there remains a notable gap in the literature regarding the combined effects of fibre reinforcement, generalized thermoelasticity, fluid loading, and exponential temperature-dependent thermal conductivity in plate configurations. Most previous investigations considered either constant thermal conductivity in reinforced plates or studied temperature dependence in different geometrical settings without fluid interaction. The exponential form of variable thermal conductivity, which more accurately reflects nonlinear heat diffusion in composite materials subjected to high thermal gradients, has not yet been analytically incorporated into the thermoelastic analysis of a fibre-reinforced plate loaded by fluid layers.

Motivated by the above limitations, the present work develops a unified analytical framework to investigate thermoelastic wave propagation in a fibre-reinforced plate interacting with non-viscous fluid layers while incorporating exponential temperature-dependent thermal conductivity. The formulation aims to clarify how nonlinear thermal diffusion, reinforcement anisotropy, and fluid coupling collectively influence attenuation behavior, stress redistribution, and thermoelastic stability.

Novelty and distinction from previous studies

Although fibre-reinforced thermoelasticity, generalized thermoelastic theories, fluid-loaded structures, and temperature-dependent material properties have individually been investigated in previous studies, the present formulation differs through the simultaneous coupling of reinforcement anisotropy, fluid–structure interaction, generalized thermoelasticity, and exponential nonlinear thermal conductivity within a unified analytical framework. Existing investigations typically considered only subsets of these mechanisms.

Unlike constant-conductivity models, the exponential conductivity assumption modifies the effective heat diffusion process nonlinearly, thereby affecting attenuation rates, stress localization, and thermoelastic wave persistence. Consequently, the present work aims to explore the combined influence of these interacting mechanisms rather than introducing an isolated constitutive modification.

Therefore, the contribution of the present study lies in the multiphysics coupling framework and the resulting physical implications for reinforced thermoelastic structures subjected to fluid interaction.

The governing constitutive relations and generalized thermoelastic equations adopted in this study originate from established thermoelastic theories developed in previous literature. Consequently, certain mathematical structures resemble earlier formulations because they represent accepted physical foundations rather than newly introduced constitutive laws. The objective of the present work is instead to examine how exponential temperature-dependent conductivity modifies thermoelastic responses in a fibre-reinforced plate interacting with fluid layers. Therefore, the novelty arises from the combined physical framework and resulting wave characteristics rather than replacement of underlying thermoelastic principles.

Existence, uniqueness, and admissibility of the normal-mode solution

The coupled thermoelastic system obtained in Eqs. The normal-mode transformation converts the governing partial differential equations into a finite system of ordinary differential equations with constant coefficients.

The resulting characteristic equation determines attenuation parameters governing wave propagation. Parameter sensitivity and stability considerations

The response of coupled thermoelastic systems depends strongly on conductivity variation, reinforcement parameters, and relaxation times. To evaluate robustness qualitatively, numerical computations were repeated for different parameter values, showing that the general attenuation trends remain consistent despite quantitative changes in amplitudes. This indicates stability of the obtained solutions with respect to moderate parameter perturbations.

The nonlinear conductivity parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{K}_{1}$$\end{document} exhibits strong sensitivity because it directly modifies effective thermal diffusion. Positive values accelerate attenuation, whereas negative values increase thermal localization. Reinforcement parameters mainly affect stiffness and stress transmission, producing comparatively smoother variations in displacement amplitudes.

Therefore, conductivity variation acts as the dominant control parameter governing thermoelastic wave persistence in the present model.

Admissible solutions satisfy:\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:\mathfrak{R}\mathrm{e}\left({m}_{j}\right)>0,$$\end{document} ensuring attenuation of propagating modes and excluding unbounded thermoelastic growth. Consequently, conductivity variation modifies localization characteristics without violating boundedness conditions within the considered parameter ranges.

Using the normal mode formulation and the adopted exponential variable thermal conductivity model, the temperature field can be expressed in terms of the transformed thermal potential. By employing the Kirchhoff-type transformation and solving the governing heat equation in the normal-mode framework, the physical temperature increment can be recovered from the auxiliary variable \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${\Theta ^*}$$\end{document}. This expression establishes the nonlinear relationship between the actual temperature field and the transformed thermal variable, clearly illustrating the influence of variable thermal conductivity.

Since the present formulation employs analytical normal-mode solutions rather than discretized numerical approximations, conventional mesh convergence analysis is not applicable. Stability is instead associated with the admissibility of characteristic roots and boundedness of resulting amplitudes.

Physical interpretation of thermoelastic wave mechanisms

The coupled interaction among reinforcement anisotropy, generalized thermoelastic relaxation, nonlinear conductivity variation, and fluid loading governs the attenuation and propagation characteristics of thermoelastic waves. Fibre reinforcement increases effective stiffness, reducing deformation amplitudes and enhancing stress redistribution. Conversely, nonlinear conductivity modifies thermal diffusion rates, altering wave localization and attenuation behavior. Fluid coupling further affects transmission of mechanical energy through interface continuity conditions, producing additional damping and modifying stress propagation patterns.

Comparison with previous studies and quantitative advancement of the present model

The present study advances the theoretical modeling of thermoelastic wave propagation by integrating fibre reinforcement, generalized thermoelasticity, fluid–structure interaction, and exponential variable thermal conductivity within a unified analytical framework. A detailed comparison with representative previous works highlights the novelty and significance of the current contribution.

Early foundational works on fibre-reinforced elastic plates were established by Belfield et al.1, while wave reflection and propagation in reinforced elastic and thermoelastic half-spaces were studied by Singh and Singh2 and Singh22. These investigations assumed constant thermal conductivity and classical thermoelasticity. Abbas and Abd-Alla3 and Othman and Lotfy7 extended the formulation to generalized thermoelasticity under CT, LS, and GL theories but retained constant material properties. Fractional and phase-lag heat conduction models were later introduced for reinforced media by Abbas8, Abo-Dahab and Lotfy9, Zenkour and Abouelregal11, and Alhashash and Abouelregal15; however, these studies did not incorporate nonlinear temperature-dependent conductivity in plate–fluid configurations.

Temperature-dependent material properties were considered in reinforced thermoelastic media by Deswal et al.12 and Abouelregal and Alesemi13, and more recently in semiconductor or photo-thermoelastic models by Ibrahim et al.23, Nassar et al.27, and related works24–26. Nevertheless, these studies either adopted linear conductivity dependence or focused on different geometries, such as cylinders or semiconductor plates, without fluid interaction. Fluid-loaded thermoelastic plates were examined by Ailawalia et al.28, but reinforcement anisotropy and nonlinear conductivity effects were not incorporated simultaneously.

Influence of fluid–structure interaction on wave propagation

Although the present figures primarily emphasize the effects of reinforcement and variable thermal conductivity, fluid–structure interaction remains embedded through the interface continuity conditions imposed between the fibre-reinforced plate and surrounding non-viscous fluid layers. Specifically, continuity of displacement and normal stress at \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:y=\pm\:d$$\end{document} couples mechanical disturbances in the solid to acoustic pressure waves within the fluid regions. Consequently, part of the mechanical energy is transmitted across interfaces rather than remaining confined within the plate, contributing to modified attenuation and redistribution of stress amplitudes.

The surrounding fluid therefore acts as an additional energy transfer medium, influencing thermoelastic wave propagation indirectly through boundary coupling. This effect is expected to become stronger with increasing fluid density or compressibility, which would alter attenuation rates and wave transmission characteristics. However, because fluid parameters were fixed in the present numerical examples, their quantitative influence was not isolated and remains an important topic for future investigation.

Wave propagation and attenuation characteristics

Thermoelastic wave propagation exhibits oscillatory attenuation governed by reinforcement, relaxation times, and conductivity variation. Increasing attenuation indicates stronger dissipation mechanisms, whereas persistent oscillations correspond to reduced damping and localized energy concentration. Therefore, conductivity variation serves as a control parameter regulating thermoelastic wave persistence.

Although the exponential conductivity assumption provides a flexible nonlinear approximation, calibration against material-specific experimental measurements remains necessary for quantitative prediction.

Limitations and future perspectives

The present analytical formulation provides insight into coupled thermoelastic responses in fibre-reinforced fluid-loaded structures; however, several assumptions restrict its applicability to more complex engineering systems.

First, the normal mode method is derived under the assumption of an infinite plate geometry, which neglects edge effects and resonance phenomena that may arise in finite structures. In finite plates, reflected waves from boundaries could interact with propagating thermoelastic disturbances, producing standing waves, modal coupling, and altered attenuation characteristics.

Second, the analysis is based on linearized thermoelastic equations and small deformation assumptions. Consequently, nonlinear geometric effects, large strains, and material nonlinearities are not captured. Under severe thermal loading, such effects may significantly modify stress distributions and wave propagation behavior.

Third, surrounding fluid layers are modeled as non-viscous media. Inclusion of fluid viscosity would introduce additional dissipation mechanisms through shear effects, potentially increasing attenuation and altering interface energy transfer. Viscous fluid interaction may also affect resonance frequencies and dynamic stability.

Fourth, the adopted boundary conditions represent idealized interfaces and may not fully describe imperfect bonding or interfacial damage occurring in practical composite systems.

Therefore, future studies should extend the present framework toward finite geometries, viscous fluid interaction, nonlinear constitutive behavior, and experimentally calibrated material models to improve predictive capability under realistic operating conditions.

Mathematical limitations

Although boundedness and admissibility conditions are discussed, a rigorous operator-theoretic proof of well-posedness for the fully coupled nonlinear conductivity problem remains beyond the scope of the present work and constitutes an interesting direction for future research.

Experimental limitation

The present study is analytical in nature and does not include direct experimental measurements. Future work should compare predicted thermoelastic responses with laboratory observations in fibre-reinforced composites subjected to coupled thermal and fluid loading.

Limitations of numerical analysis

Although extensive parameter studies were performed, systematic global sensitivity analysis was beyond the scope of the present work. Future investigations may employ uncertainty quantification and parameter sensitivity methods to identify dominant mechanisms governing thermoelastic responses.

Scope of contribution

The present study extends existing thermoelastic frameworks through multiphysics coupling rather than proposing new constitutive laws. Accordingly, its contribution should be interpreted as an analytical exploration of combined mechanisms affecting thermoelastic wave behavior.

Conclusion

This study presents a comprehensive analytical investigation of thermoelastic wave propagation in a fibre-reinforced plate interacting with non-viscous fluid layers while incorporating exponential temperature-dependent thermal conductivity within the Green–Lindsay generalized thermoelastic framework. The results demonstrate that the exponential conductivity parameter \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$${K_1}$$\end{document} plays a critical role in controlling thermal diffusion, wave attenuation, and stress redistribution. Among the considered generalized thermoelastic models, the GL theory consistently exhibits stronger attenuation and lower thermoelastic amplitudes than LS and CT models, indicating the dominant influence of relaxation mechanisms on wave dissipation and stress redistribution.

The surrounding non-viscous fluid layers influence the thermoelastic response mainly through normal acoustic pressure and interface impedance, modifying stress transmission and attenuation while exerting no shear traction on the plate surfaces.

The combined influence of thermal loading and surrounding fluid media may significantly affect thermoelastic stability in reinforced composite structures operating under severe environmental conditions.

The results demonstrate that reinforcement anisotropy and nonlinear conductivity jointly regulate thermoelastic attenuation through competing mechanisms of stiffness enhancement and thermal localization. This interaction may provide practical strategies for controlling wave propagation and mitigating stress concentration in advanced composite systems.

The combined interaction between reinforcement anisotropy, fluid coupling, and nonlinear conductivity establishes a mechanism controlling thermoelastic attenuation and stress localization. These effects may provide practical strategies for tailoring wave dissipation and improving stability in advanced composite structures.

The reinforcement parameters significantly influence deformation and stress transmission by enhancing structural rigidity and redistributing wave energy more efficiently. The presence of dual relaxation times in the GL theory introduces additional damping mechanisms, producing stronger attenuation compared with classical (CT) and single-relaxation (LS) models. Quantitatively, the inclusion of exponential VTC modifies peak temperature amplitudes by up to 25–30% and alters stress magnitudes by nearly 20% relative to the constant-conductivity case. The combined effect of reinforcement and nonlinear thermal diffusion establishes a robust stabilization mechanism that governs thermoelastic wave behavior in advanced composite systems.

Although the analytical framework captures essential coupled mechanisms, extension toward finite geometries and viscous fluid interaction remains necessary for broader engineering applicability.

The ability of the formulation to recover classical limiting cases and previously established thermoelastic behavior supports the consistency and physical plausibility of the model.

Applications

The results of the present study have important implications for advanced engineering systems where fibre-reinforced composite plates are subjected to coupled thermal and mechanical loading in fluid environments. The incorporation of exponential temperature-dependent thermal conductivity provides a more realistic description of heat diffusion in high-temperature applications such as aerospace panels, thermal protection systems, and lightweight structural components exposed to aerodynamic heating. The fluid–structure interaction framework is particularly relevant to marine and underwater composite structures, where hydrodynamic pressures and thermal gradients coexist. In microelectronic and optoelectronic packaging, where layered composite substrates experience significant temperature variations, the nonlinear conductivity parameter offers a mechanism to control thermal stress concentration and deformation. Furthermore, in high-performance composite materials and smart adaptive structures, the interaction between reinforcement anisotropy and nonlinear thermal diffusion can be exploited to tune wave attenuation, reduce vibration amplitudes, and enhance structural stability.

The present model may be relevant to a wide range of engineering applications involving coupled thermal and fluid environments, including aerospace composite skins subjected to aerodynamic heating, underwater and marine reinforced structures interacting with surrounding fluids, cryogenic composite systems exposed to severe thermal gradients, thermal protection materials operating under high-temperature conditions, and fibre-reinforced components undergoing rapid heating and cooling cycles. In such systems, the combined effects of thermal loading and fluid interaction can significantly influence thermoelastic wave attenuation, stress concentration, energy redistribution, and long-term structural reliability. Consequently, understanding these coupled mechanisms is important for improving durability, thermal stability, and performance optimization in advanced composite structures operating under extreme environmental conditions.
