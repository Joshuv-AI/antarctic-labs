0.1
Motivation: approximating reals by rational and quadratic

Continued fractions as coding geodesics in the modular surface

Classical diophantine approximation concerns the approximation of numbers in the real projective line
\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}{}
by numbers in the rational projective line
\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}{}
whose complexity is measured by height.
It began with the Euclidean algorithm: every
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
has a unique continued fraction expansion

\xi^{+}=\lfloor\,{x_{0};x_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor=x_{0}+\tfrac{1}{x_{1}+\dots}\quad\mathrm{with}\quad x_{j}\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}

that is finite if and only if
\xi^{+}
is rational in which case it is required to have even length
2k\in 2\mathbb{N}

\xi^{+}
is quadratic.

One may rewrite this in terms of the Euclidean monoid
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
freely generated by the parabolic linear-fractional transformations
R\colon z\mapsto z+1
and

[0,\infty]
so that
\xi^{+}\in(1,\infty)
is the image of
\infty
by the finite or infinite product
X=R^{x_{0}}L^{x_{1}}\cdots

\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})=\{R,L\}^{\star}\qquad R=\begin{pmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{pmatrix}\quad L=\begin{pmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{pmatrix}\qquad\xi^{+}=X\cdot\infty=\lim_{k\to+\infty}(R^{x_{0}}\cdots L^{x_{2k-1}}\cdot\infty)

This yields the correspondence with the symbolic encoding of geodesics on the modular orbifold.

The real projective line
\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}\subset\mathbb{C}\Proj^{1}{}
is the boundary of
\mathbf{HP}=\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\Im(z)>0\}

The

The geodesic

The real

Lagrange and Markov constants measure approximation by rationals

For
\xi^{+}=\lfloor\,{x}\,\rfloor\in\mathbb{R}
, its
Lagrange constant
measures the quality of its best rational approximations:
\LC(\xi^{+})^{-1}=\liminf_{n}\left(q_{n}\lvert q_{n}\xi^{+}-p_{n}\rvert\right)

In particular
\LC(\xi^{+})=0\iff\xi\in\mathbb{Q}
and more importantly
\LC(\xi^{+})<\infty\iff\limsup_{n}(x_{n})<\infty

Since

\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
.

In
\mathbf{M}

of the cusp

\mathbf{M}

.

Approximation of algebraic numbers by rationals and quadratics

Algebraic numbers of degree
>2
are expected to behave like Lebesgue-normal numbers with respect to the Gauss-Kuzmin statistics (see Remark
1.2
), in particular they would satisfy
\limsup_{n}\tfrac{\log x_{n}}{\log n}=1
,
and should be neither too well nor too badly approximable by rationals or quadratics.
However at this time of writing, very little is known about this.
Indeed, there is not a single example (explicit or not) of an algebraic number
\xi^{+}
of degree
>2
for which we can decide whether its continued fraction expansion
x
is bounded or unbounded.
A longstanding conjecture going back to Khintchine (
[
5
, §4]
) asserts that if
x
is bounded then
\xi^{+}
is rational or quadratic or transcendental.
This trichotomy is the leitmotive of this work.

The best result we have concerning good rational approximations to
\xi^{+}
(upper bounds on
x
), remains Roth’s theorem implying that if an irrational
\xi^{+}
is algebraic then
\log(x_{n+1})=o(\log q_{n})
, thus algebraic numbers cannot have exponentially better rational approximations than normal numbers (for which
\limsup\log(x_{n+1})\sim\log(n)\sim\log\log q_{n}
).
This dramatic improvement on Liouville’s theorem enables to extend the construction of transcendental numbers, but we need more.

The main improvement of Roth’s theorem is Schmidt’s subspace theorem, which implies in particular that if an irrational
\xi^{+}
is algebraic then it cannot have too good quadratic approximations. This means that the geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}
cannot get infinitely often too close to a periodic geodesics.
The sequence
x\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{N}}
has
long repetitions
when there is
\epsilon>0
such that there are infinitely many times
n\in\mathbb{N}
at which the prefix
(x_{0},\dots,x_{n})
contains two disjoint occurrences of a word over
\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
of length
>\epsilon n
.
This enabled
[
15
]
to show that if
x
is bounded aperiodic but with long repetitions then
\xi^{+}
is transcendent.

Let us mention that we may unify the last two transcendence results for irrational
\xi^{+}
as follows.
Say that
X\in\{R,L\}^{\mathbb{N}}
has long repetitions when there is
\epsilon>0
such that there are infinitely many times
N\in\mathbb{N}
at which the prefix
(X_{0},\dots,X_{n})
contains two disjoint occurrences of a factor
U\in\{R,L\}^{\star}
with
\len(U)>\epsilon N
.
If
X
has long repetitions then
\xi^{+}
is quadratic or transcendent.

Mahler’s exponents measure approximation by algebraic numbers.

Let us briefly recall Mahler’s exponents and hierarchy of real numbers (see
[
12
,
11
]
).

The
height
of a polynomial
P(x)=\sum_{0}^{d}a_{k}x^{k}\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
is
H(P)=\max\{\lvert a_{k}\rvert\colon 0\leq k\leq d\}\in\mathbb{Z}
, and the
height
of an algebraic number
\alpha\in\bar{\mathbb{Q}}
is that of its minimal polynomial in
\mathbb{Z}[x]
.

For a real number
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
and
d\in\mathbb{N}
, its
Mahler exponent

w_{d}(\xi)
and
\hat{w}_{d}(\xi)
for best and uniform approximations are defined as the supremum of
w\in\mathbb{R}
such that the equations

P\in\mathbb{Z}[x]\quad\deg(P)\leq d\quad H(P)\leq h\qquad 0<\lvert P(\xi^{+})\rvert\leq h^{-w}

have solutions for infinitely many
h\in\mathbb{N}
and for all sufficiently large
h\in\mathbb{N}
, respectively.

By definition and the Schubfachprinzip, for
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, if
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
is not algebraic of degree
\leq d
, then
d\leq\hat{w}_{d}(\xi^{+})\leq w_{d}(\xi^{+})
. For Lebesgue-normal numbers we have
n=\hat{w}_{d}(\xi^{+})=w_{d}(\xi^{+})
.

By definition,
\hat{w}_{d}
and
w_{d}
are increasing. Letting
w_{\infty}(\xi^{+})=\limsup_{n}\tfrac{1}{n}w_{d}(\xi^{+})\in[0,\infty]
, we may now give Mahler’s hierarchy of numbers: the number
\xi^{+}
is of
Mahler class

\mathrm{A}

when
w_{\infty}(\xi^{+})=0

\mathrm{S}

when
0<w_{\infty}(\xi^{+})<\infty

\mathrm{T}

when
w_{\infty}(\xi^{+})=\infty
but for all
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
it has
w_{d}(\xi^{+})<\infty

\mathrm{U}

when there exists
n\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
such that
w_{d}(\xi^{+})=\infty

The class
\mathrm{A}
coincides with the set of algebraic numbers, and the Schmidt subspace theorem implies that if
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
is algebraic with
\deg(\xi)\in\mathbb{N}_{>1}
then
w_{d}(\xi^{+})=\hat{w}_{d}(\xi^{+})=\min\{d,\deg(\xi^{+})-1\}
.
Moreover, if numbers are algebraically dependent then they belong to the same Mahler class.

On the other hand, if
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}
is neither rational nor quadratic, then denoting
\phi=\tfrac{1+\sqrt{5}}{2}
we have
2\leq\hat{w}_{2}(\xi)\leq 1+\phi
, and there are such
\xi^{+}
that achieve equalities (see
[
11
, Theorem 2.7]
).

Since our geometric setting naturally leads to approximations by (pairs of) quadratic numbers, the Mahler measure
w_{2}
will play a special role.

Transcendence of simple geodesics on modular covers

Main conjecture: transcendence of simple geodesics on modular covers

Consider now a finite-index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
, corresponding to a finite cover
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{M}
.
The cusps of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
correspond to the orbits of rational points
\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
, hence to the conjugacy classes of parabolic elements in
\Gamma^{\prime}
.
A cusp
o\in\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
has a
width
defined as the index of stabilisers
w=[\Stab(o,\Gamma)\colon\Stab(o,\Gamma^{\prime})]
.

The space of geodesics of
\mathbf{HP}
identifies with
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{HP})=\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}\times\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}\setminus\mathrm{diagonal}
.
The space of geodesics on
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
identifies with the quotient
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M})=\Gamma^{\prime}\backslash\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{HP})
by the diagonal
\Gamma^{\prime}
-action.
A geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
has a future whose geometry and dynamics are governed by the continued fraction expansion of
\xi^{+}
; in particular:
\xi^{\prime}
escapes into a cusp if and only if
\xi^{\prime}
is rational, whereas
\xi^{\prime}
accumulates on a periodic geodesic if and only if
\xi^{+}
is quadratic.

A geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is
simple
when there are no
\gamma\in\Gamma^{\prime}
such the pairs
\{\xi^{-},\xi^{+}\}
and
\{\gamma\xi^{-},\gamma\xi^{+}\}
are disjoint and linked with respect to the cyclic order on
\partial\mathbf{HP}
, namely it has no transverse intersection points in
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.
The space of simple geodesics on
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is a closed subset
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\subset\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
.
The following well known result was extensively used by

[
29
]
.

Lemma 0.1

(simple geodesics have bounded cusp excursions)
.

Consider irrationals
(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\in(-1,0)\times(1,\infty)
with continued fraction expansions
-1/\xi^{-}=\lfloor\,{x_{-1};x_{-2},\dots}\,\rfloor
and
\xi^{+}=\lfloor\,{x_{0};x_{1},x_{2},\dots}\,\rfloor
.

The geodesic
\xi=(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\subset\mathbf{HP}
projects
\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
to a geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.

If
\xi^{\prime}
is simple then for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
we have
x_{n}<M
where
M
is the maximal cusp-width of
\Gamma^{\prime}
.

In the light of Khintchine’s conjecture, it is natural to propose the following.

Conjecture 0.2

(transcendence of simple geodesics in finite modular covers)
.

Consider a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma
corresponding to a finite cover
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{M}
.

A geodesic
\xi=(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{HP})
projects
\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
to a geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
.

If
\xi^{\prime}
is simple then
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}{}
is either rational or quadratic or transcendental.

Main result: Mahler measures of minimal geodesics on modular covers

We will prove Conjecture
0.2
under an additional topological assumption on the simple geodesic.
A
geodesic lamination
of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is a compact subset
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
which is a disjoint union of complete simple geodesics called its leaves (it identifies with a certain compact of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
). It is
minimal
when every one of its leaves
\xi^{\prime}
has a past and future that are dense in
\Xi^{\prime}
.

The closure of a simple geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is a geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.

Theorem 0.3

(transcendence of minimal lamination leaves)
.

If
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is minimal, then either
\xi^{\pm}
are quadratic conjugates or else
\xi^{\pm}
are both transcendent. More precisely in the transcendent case, either
w_{2}(\xi^{\pm})=\infty
(of type
\mathrm{U}_{2}
) or else there exists
c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
such that for all
d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
we have
w_{d}(\xi^{+})\leq\exp\left(c(\log 3d)^{3}(\log\log 3d)^{2}\right)
(of class
\mathrm{S}
or
\mathrm{T}
).

Outline and moral of the proof.

We may act by
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
to assume that
(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\in(-1,0)\times(1,\infty)
so that the continued fraction expansions of
-1/\xi^{-},\xi^{+}
encode the trajectory of
\xi^{\prime}
on
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
as a bounded sequence
x=(x^{-},x^{+})\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
If the geodesic
\xi^{\prime}
is a leaf of a minimal lamination, then it is (uniformly) recurrent in space, and each time its position returns to a small neighbourhood in space of the base point, its momentum must also align closely to that of the base point, so much so that the geodesic will travel almost parallel to itself for some time, which is proportional to the return time.
Thus minimality and topological simplicity implies the bounded continued fraction expansion
x^{\pm}\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{N}}
has sub-affine factor complexity, hence long repetitions.
This enables to apply
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
on the transcendence of bounded continued fractions with long repetitions and
[
13
, Theorem 2.2]
to control the Mahler exponents of
\xi^{\pm}
from the subaffine complexity of
x
.

In conclusion, we are able to prove transcendence not only because
x
is bounded (which only says that
\xi^{\pm}
is badly approximable by rationals), but especially because
x
has long repetitions and subaffine factor complexity (which is saying that
\xi
has too good quadratic approxmiations).
∎

Remark 0.4

(isolated leaves in non-minimal laminations)
.

By
[
9
, Proposition 3]
, every geodesic lamination in
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is a disjoint union of finitely many minimal sublaminations, and finitely many isolated leaves whose ends spiral along the minimal sublamination or escape into a cusp.

Hence, to prove the full conjecture

\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
such that their closure
\Xi^{\prime}
has least two minimal components: the geodesic
\xi^{\prime}
spirals successively from one to the other, and we have not found a way to control its symbolic complexity.

0.3
Simple geodesics in the modular torus: Christoffel, Markov, Sturm

In Section
1
we explain why for the derived subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}=[\Gamma,\Gamma]
, the full Conjecture
2.3
is known. Indeed, the corresponding modular cover
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{M}
has total space a hyperbolic once-punctured torus, in which all simple geodesics either escape to a cusp or belong to a minimal lamination.

In fact, there is a much more precise description of simple geodesics in the corresponding modular torus
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.
We recall this well known story in the spirit of
[
38
]
, while emphasizing some details either missing from or dispersed in the literature.

The trichotomy distinguishes Markov rational numbers, Markov (pairs of) quadratic numbers and Sturmian (pairs of) transcendent numbers, which are precisely those whose (Markoff and) Lagrange constant lie before and on the first accumulation point
3
of the spectra.

The Lagrange spectrum
\LC(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 1})
begins with a discrete countable set increasing to its first accumulation point
3
, and the corresponding
\xi^{+}
are precisely the
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
orbits of Markov quadratic irrationals corresponding to simple periodic geodesics in
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.
There are uncountably many numbers
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}_{>1}
with
\LC(\xi^{+})=3
, which we call Sturmian since up to the action of
\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
they are precisely the to numbers whose continued fraction expansions are the aperiodic Sturmian sequences on
\{1,2\}
, and they correspond to simple aperiodic geodesics in
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
.
The transcendence of Sturmian numbers was shown in
[
4
, Theorem 7]
.

We will also take this opportunity to describe the structure of the metabelian quotient
\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime\prime}
(in Theorem
3.1
, first observed in
[

1.2

The monoïd

Remark 1.2

(Gauss-Kuzmin statistics)
.

The continued fraction expansions of random numbers follow the Gauss-Kuzmin statistics as we now recall.

The Gauss–Kuzmin measure
\mu_{GK}
on
(0,1)
is defined on intervals
I\subset(0,1)
by
\mu_{GK}(I)=\tfrac{1}{2}\int_{I}\frac{dt}{1+t}
.
For a finite sequence of positive integers
x=(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})\in(\mathbb{N}^{*})^{n}
the interval of real numbers in
(0,1)
whose continued fraction expansion begins with
(0,x_{1},\dots,x_{n})
forms an interval denoted
I(x)
.
For a real number sampled according to the Lebesgue measure, the frequency with which the finite sequence
x
of positive integers appears in its continued fraction expansion is given by
\mu_{GK}(I(x))
.
For instance on cylinders
x=(x_{1})
of length
1
, we find that the probability to witness the integer
x_{1}
is given by
\log_{2}\left(1+\tfrac{1}{x_{1}(2+x_{1})}\right)

We refer to
[
40
]
for an elegant characterisation of the Gauss-Kuzmin measure.

Remark 1.3

(transcendence badly approximable numbers?)
.

A positive real number whose continued fraction expansion has bounded entries is called
badly approxmimable
.
A longstanding conjecture going back to Khintchine (see
[
5
, §4]
) asserts a badly approximable numbers is rational or quadratic or transcendental.
This trichotomy is the leitmotiv of this work.
Let us warn that to this date, there is not a single example (explicit or non-explicit) of algebraic number with degree
>2
for which we can decide whether its continued fraction expansion is bounded or unbounded.

In fact, it is more generally expected that algebraic numbers of degree
>2
are normal with respect to the Gauss-Kuzmin statistics, which would imply in particular that their corresponding
\{R,L\}
-expansion has exponential factor complexity (a notion that we will introduce in
Section

1.4
).

Let us recall two transcendence criteria from
[

Recall that
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
is the free monoïd of words over the letters
\{R,L\}
, and that the transposition
A\mapsto A^{{\dagger}}
is the anti-involution that reverses the word while exchanging the letters

We define a

as a finite subset

Example 1.4

(
\Sigma\subset\Sigma_{M}^{n}
)
.

For
M\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}
, the set
\Sigma_{M}=\{R^{c_{0}}L^{c_{1}}\colon 1\leq c_{0},c_{1}\leq M\}
is a set of syllables.
A syllable set

can equivalently be defined as a
{\dagger}
-invariant subset of
\Sigma^{n}_{M}
for some

For a word

and a real

as the prefix of
\Sigma
-length
\lceil r\len(W)\rceil
of the word

.
For instance if

, the word

-length

Definition 1.5

(diophantine exponent)
.

Consider a sequence
X\in\Sigma_{M}^{\mathbb{N}}
.

For

has

and

Its

\Dio(X)\in[1,\infty]
is the supremum of
\rho
such that

has exponent
\geq\rho
.

Definition 1.6

(factor complexity)
.

For a sequence
X\in\Sigma_{M}^{\mathbb{N}}
, its

is the subset

.

A sequence

has an attractive fixed point

-conversion of

.

Remark 1.7

(aperiodic complexity gap)

Let

(simple geodesics have bounded cusp excursions)
.

Consider irrationals

Geodesic laminations

9

A
geodesic lamination
of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is a compact subset
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
which is a disjoint union of complete simple geodesics called its leaves (it identifies with a certain compact of
\mathcal{S}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
).

\mathcal{T}(3)\subset\Gamma(3)

A geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is
minimal
when every one of its leaves
\xi^{\prime}
has both its past and future
\xi^{\pm}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
that are dense in
\Xi^{\prime}
.
By
[

: every geodesic lamination is a disjoint union of finitely many minimal sublaminations, and finitely many isolated leaves whose ends spiral along the minimal sublamination or escape into a cusp (see
[

).

Remark 2.4

(the future determines the past)
.

Note that in a minimal geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
, every leaf
\xi^{\prime}\subset\Xi^{\prime}
is determined by its future
\xi^{+}\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
.
More generally for
\xi^{+}\in\partial\mathbf{HP}
, if there exists
\xi^{-}\in\partial\mathbf{HP}
such that the geodesic
\xi^{\prime}=(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
on
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is simple, then there exists a unique such
\xi^{-}\in\partial\mathbf{HP}
such that the closure of
\xi^{\prime}
is a minimal geodesic lamination.

Since
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
retracts by deformation onto the embedded graph
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, every geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
is
carried by the embedded graph

\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
: all its leaves are homotopic relative to their ends to a combinatorial geodesic in
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
.
The geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
is
orientable
when for every edge in
\mathcal{T}
, at most one of its directions appears among all codings of its leaves as sequences over
\vec{E}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
).

Subshifts and languages

Let us recall some notions about subshifts and languages (a good general reference is
[
25
]
).

Fix a finite set
\mathcal{A}
called the alphabet of
syllables
, and let
\mathcal{A}^{\star}=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\mathcal{A}^{n}
be the free monoïd of
words
.
A
language
is a subset
\mathcal{L}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\star}
.
It is called
factorial
when it is closed by taking subwords (if
V\in\mathcal{L}
is a subword of
W\in\mathcal{L}^{\star}
then
V\in\mathcal{L}
) and is called
bi-extendable
when every word is a bi-proper subword of another one (if
V\in\mathcal{L}
then there exist non-trivial
U,W\in\mathcal{A}^{\star}
such that
UVW\in\mathcal{L}
).
It is
uniformly recurrent
when for every word
V\in\mathcal{L}
, there is an integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
such that every word
W\in\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}^{n}
contains
V
as a subword.
Its
factor complexity
is the function
\fac(\mathcal{L})\colon\mathbb{N}\to\mathbb{N}
whose value at
n\in\mathbb{N}
is
\fac_{n}(\mathcal{L})=\Card{(\mathcal{L}\cap\mathcal{A}^{n})}
.

The set of bi-infinite sequences
\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
with the product topology is a compact space; a basis of neighbourhoods for the topology is given by the clopen sets
\operatorname{Cyl}_{k}(W)=\{Y\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\colon Y_{[k,k+n)}=W\}
for
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
W\in\mathcal{A}^{n}
.
The function
\Shift\colon\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}\to\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
defined by
\Shift(Y)_{n}=Y_{n+1}
is a homeomorphism.
A
subshift

(\Xi^{\prime},\Shift)
of
(\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}},\Shift)
consists of a compact
\Shift
-invariant subset.
A subshift
(\Xi^{\prime},\Shift)
is
minimal
when every leaf
\xi^{\prime}
has an orbit
(\Shift^{k}\xi)_{k}
that is dense in
\Xi^{\prime}
as
k\to+\infty
and as
k\to-\infty
.

There is a correspondence between subshifts
(\Xi^{\prime},\Shift)\subset(\mathcal{A}^{\star},\Shift)
and languages
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\star}
that are factorial and bi-extendable, given by
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}=\{\xi^{\prime}_{[k,k+n)}\colon\xi^{\prime}\in\Xi^{\prime},k\in\mathbb{Z},n\in\mathbb{N}\}
.
For example, a sequence
X\in\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
has shift-closure
\operatorname{Closure}{(\{\Shift^{k}(X)\colon k\in\mathbb{Z}\})}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\mathbb{Z}}
a subshift whose language consists of all subwords of
X
.
A subshift is minimal if and only if its language is uniformly recurrent.

Symbolic complexity of minimal geodesic laminations

Let us assemble the notions introduced in the previous paragraphs to explain Proposition

From now on, we assume that

Proposition 2.5

(roughly linear complexity)
.

For a minimal geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
as above, its lamination and involution languages
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}
have roughly linear factor complexity:

\exists\kappa\in\mathbb{N},\;\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad|\fac_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}})-\kappa n\lvert<\infty.

Proof.

The proposition follows from much more precise results in the literature, as we now explain.
All methods of proof boil down to computing and bounding the discrete derivative of the complexity functions
n\mapsto\fac_{n+1}(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}})-\fac_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}})
, by enumerating special factors (see
[
18

).

By definition, the involution language
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\star}
is the image of the lamination language
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}\subset\vec{E}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{\star}
by the natural decoding morphism
\mathcal{A}^{\star}\to\vec{E}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{\star}

[

, their quasi-linear complexities are equivalent.

If
\Xi^{\prime}
is orientable then the lamination language
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}\subset\vec{E}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})^{\star}

[

\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}\subset\mathcal{A}^{\star}
is that of an interval exchange transformation for its natural encoding (see
[
6
]

\fac_{n}(\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}})\leq(\Card\mathcal{A}-1)n+1
, with equality if and only if the IET satisfies Keane’s infinite distinct orbits condition (see
[
24
]
).

If
\Xi^{\prime}
is non-orientable then by
[

there is a ramified double cover of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
in which
\Xi^{\prime}
lifts to an oriented minimal lamination carried by the lift of
\mathcal{T}^{\prime}
, whose lamination language has sub-affine complexity: this shows that
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}
is the morphic image of a language with sub-affine complexity, so it also has sub-affine complexity.

Context 2.6

Consider a finite index subgroup
\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma

\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{M}

\xi\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{HP})

\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}

\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})

are either quadratic conjugates or both transcendent, in which case either

\exists c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}\;\forall d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\colon w_{d}(\xi^{\pm})\leq\exp\left(c(\log 3d)^{5}(\log\log 3d)^{4}\right)
(Mahler class
\mathrm{S}
or
\mathrm{T}

Since the conclusion only concerns
\xi^{\pm}\in\mathbb{R}\Proj^{1}{}

\Gamma^{\prime}\subset\Gamma

\xi^{\prime}
is simple with closure a geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
that is minimal.
Thus, we may pass to any finite index subgroup of
\Gamma^{\prime}

\xi^{\prime}
to the corresponding finite cover of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}

Hence we may assume
\Gamma^{\prime}
normal in
\Gamma
and normalized by

In that case, the action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
on
\mathbf{HP}

\Gamma/\Gamma^{\prime}
by deck transformations of the cover
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\to\mathbf{M}

The action of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

\mathcal{T}

\xi\subset\mathbf{HP}
follows a combinatorial geodesic in
\mathcal{T}
which may be assumed to pass through the base arc so that
(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})\in(-1,0)\times(1,\infty)

The continued fraction expansions
-1/\xi^{-}=\lfloor\,{x_{-1};x_{-2},\dots}\,\rfloor
and
\xi^{+}=\lfloor\,{x_{0};x_{1},x_{2},\dots}\,\rfloor
define the sequence
x\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{Z}}
.
Consider the sequence
X\in\{R,L\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
whose exponents are given by

, namely

.

Now the projected geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
is homotopic relative to its ends to a combinatorial geodesic in

The geodesic lamination

has lamination language in

given by the set of subwords of
\vec{E}(\xi^{\prime})

Note that
\xi^{\pm}
are quadratic conjugates if and only if
\Xi^{\prime}
consists of a single periodic leaf.
Otherwize by minimality

are not ultimately periodic, and

has no periodic leaves, its space of leaves forms a Cantor set.
By minimality, the languages of

and

Since

is minimal, we are in the situation leading to Proposition

freely generating a submonoïd of

such that

can be uniquely recoded as a sequence in

Thus Theorem

Let us recall a few notions on mapping class groups (see
[
23
]
).

The group of orientation preserving mapping classes
\Mod^{o}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
contains the pure mapping class group
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})=\Homeo^{o}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})/\Homeo_{0}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
as a finite index normal subgroup: it is the subgroup of
\Out(\Gamma^{\prime})
which fixes all parabolic conjugacy classes of
\Gamma^{\prime}
(associated to the so called
peripheral loops
which surround the cusps), or the kernel of the surjective morphism
\Mod^{o}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})\to\mathfrak{S}(\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
to the permutation group of the punctures.
The group
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is generated by Dehn-twists along simple closed loops (and to generate
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
one must also add half-twists braiding any two punctures, as well as any orientation reversing class).
In any case, every element in
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
has a finite power in
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
.

Every element in
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
has infinite order, and is represented by an isometry of the hyperbolic surface
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
, which is unique modulo an isometry isotopic to the identity.
Hence the group
\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
acts on the space of simple geodesics, and on the space of geodesic laminations.

Example 2.8

(pseudo-Anosov classes)
.

An element
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is
pseudo-Anosov
when no simple closed geodesics are invariant under its action.
Such a pseudo-Anosov element admits a unique stable fixed geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}\subset\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
, it is minimal with no closed leaves, and uniquely ergodic (namely it admits a unique transverse measure
[
32
]
).
In this stable lamination, there may sometimes also be a simple geodesic that is fixed by
\varphi^{\prime}
.

By Thurston classification of mapping classes (see
[
43
,
23
]
), a non-trivial
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
has a maximal closed embedded subsurface with geodesic boundary on which it acts trivially (which may be empty or disconnected), and on each connected component of the complement it acts as a pseudo-Anosov mapping class with a unique stable geodesic lamination, which is minimal aperiodic and filling that subsurface (in the sense that is intersects any non-peripheral simple loop): call those the
\varphi^{\prime}
-stable minimal aperiodic laminations
.
When such a lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
contains a geodesic
\xi^{\prime}\subset\Xi^{\prime}
that is preserved by
\varphi^{\prime}
, we will say this geodesic is
preserved and expanded
.

Theorem 2.9

(transcendence of geodesics fixed by isometries)
.

If
\xi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
is preserved and expanded by a mapping class
\varphi^{\prime}\in\Mod(\mathbf{M}^{\prime},\partial\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
, then
\xi^{\pm}
are transcendent numbers of Mahler class
\mathrm{S}
or
\mathrm{T}
, more precisely
\exists c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\;\forall d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\colon w_{d}(\xi^{\pm})\leq\exp\left(c(\log 3d)^{3}(\log\log 3d)^{2}\right)
.

Proof.

The mapping class
\varphi^{\prime}
preserves the lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
, hence it induces an endomorphism of monoïds
\Phi\colon\Sigma^{\star}\to\Sigma^{\star}
which preserves the involution language
\mathcal{L}_{\Xi^{\prime}}
, and the word
X
is, up to a shift
\Shift
, a fixed point of that morphism, namely
\Phi(X)=\Shift^{k}(X)
.
This means that
\Phi^{2k}(X)=\Shift^{2k}(X)
hence
\Shift^{k}X
is fixed by the conjugate morphism
\Shift^{k}\Phi^{2k}\Shift^{-k}
.
Note that the pair of numbers
(\Shift^{k}X)^{\pm}\cdot\infty
is the translates of
\xi^{\pm}
by an element of
\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
so their Mahler exponents are all the same.
Hence the result follows from Theorem
1.11
.
∎

Remark 2.10

(generalisations to infinite products)
.

There are many ways of generalizing the previous result, to bridge the gap between geodesics or minimal geodesic laminations that are stably-expanding by isometries of
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
and all others.

First it is convenient to fix a projective class of measures on the minimal geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
(that is a point in a finite dimensional real convex polytope associated to
\Xi^{\prime}
). If
\Xi^{\prime}
is stable and expanding under the action of an isometry, then this projective measure is unique.
This enables to work in the piecewize linar space of measured geodesic laminations, and makes it easyer to define the notion of stably-expanding fixed point by a mapping class.

Next, every measured minimal geodesic lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
fills a certain subsurface
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}[\Xi^{\prime}]
, and is a stably-expanding fixed point point by an infinite product of mapping classes that are pseudo-Anosov in restriction
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}[\Xi^{\prime}]
.

Such an infinite product would yield an
S
-adic representation by substitutions of the associated symbolic space
X\subset\vec{E}(\mathcal{T}^{\prime})
.
However, this product may not be unique, and it is a subtle matter to define a procedure for producing a preferred sequence of (pseudo-Anosov) mapping classes with the desired lamination as fixed point.

On the other hand, one may start with special kinds of infinite products of mapping classes with a unique stably-expanding lamination
\Xi^{\prime}
and discuss the diophantine approximation properties of its laves in terms of the complexity of the sequence defining the product (growth of exponents or stretch factors).

For instance, one may consider an alphabet of pseudo-Anosov elements
\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{d}
, and assume that the stretch factor increases under products, namely
\lambda(\varphi_{i}\varphi_{j})>\max\{\lambda(\varphi_{i}),\lambda(\varphi_{j})\}
.

Another example would be to consider two non-empty simple multiloops
\alpha=\sqcup_{1}^{m}\alpha_{i}
and
\beta=\sqcup_{1}^{n}\beta_{j}
, and denote the associated Dehn multitwists by
D_{\alpha}=\prod_{i}^{m}D_{\alpha_{i}}
and
D_{\beta}=\prod_{1}^{n}D_{\beta_{j}}
.
Assume that the union
\alpha\cup\beta
is filling
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
, in the sense that
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}\setminus(\alpha\cup\beta)
is homotopic to a disjoint union of discs with at most one puncture.
In that case the product
D_{\alpha}D_{\beta}
is pseudo-Anosov (this is the so called Penner construction for pseudo-Anosov mapping classes, see
[
43
, §6]
). Now for a sequence
x\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{N}}
let
D_{x}=D_{\alpha}^{x_{0}}D_{\beta}^{x_{1}}\dots
be the infinite product of those Dehn multitwists.
By contrast with the previous example, ne may allow arbitrarilly large powers of Dehn multitwists.

[
26
, Lemma 5.1]
); the types are: cusp-to-cusp, cusp-to-periodic, periodic-to-cusp, periodic, cusp-to-aperiodic, aperiodic-to-cusp, aperiodic.
The action of
\Mod^{o}(\mathbf{M}^{\prime})
preserves simple geodesics and their topological type.

The geodesics on
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
correspond to sequences over the symmetric set of generators
\{A^{\pm 1},B^{\pm 1}\}
of the fundamental group
\Gamma^{\prime}
, indexed by an interval of
\mathbb{Z}
which is considered up to shift of indices (which acts as cyclic permutation on finite words).
For simple geodesics, this sequence involves at most two letters: up to the action of the finite order mapping classes
\AD_{S},\AD_{T}\in\Out(\Gamma^{\prime})
by
\AD_{S}(A,B)=(A^{-1},B^{-1})
,
\AD_{T}(A,B)=(B^{-1},AB^{-1})
, we may assume that the alphabet is
\{A,B\}
.

Definition 3.2

(Christoffel word, Cohn matrix, Markov numbers)
.

To a rational slope
\sigma\in[1,\infty]
with continued fraction expansion
\sigma=\lfloor\,{c_{0};c_{1},\dots,c_{2k-1}}\,\rfloor
, we associate the following data.

The (lower)
Christoffel word
in
\{A,B\}^{\star}
is obtained by applying the composition of substitutions
D_{\sigma}=D_{R}^{c_{0}}\dots D_{L}^{c_{2k-1}}
to the seed
A
.
This yields a matrix in
\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
, whose factorisation in
\{L,R\}^{\star}
is obtained from the substitutions
(A,B)\mapsto(RL,LR)
, that turns out to be conjugate to a unique symmetric matrix in
R\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})L
, which we call the
Cohn matrix

C_{\sigma}\in\Gamma^{\prime}\cap\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})
.
We thus define the
Markov rational

\rho_{\sigma}=C_{\sigma}\cdot\infty\in\mathbb{Q}_{>1}
and
the
pair of Markov quadratic conjugates
as its (repulsive, attractive) fixed points:
(\xi^{-}_{\sigma},\xi^{+}_{\sigma})=(C_{\sigma}^{-\infty}\cdot\infty,C_{\sigma}^{+\infty}\cdot\infty)\in(-1,0)\times(1,\infty)
.

Remark 3.3

(cyclically primitive and symmetric)
.

Since
C_{\sigma}\in R\PSL_{2}(\mathbb{N})L
is cyclically primitive and symmetric, the continued fraction expansion of
\rho_{\sigma}
is a palindrome which is the minimal period of the purely periodic number
\xi^{+}_{\sigma}
, whose Galois conjugate satisfies
\xi^{-}_{\sigma}=-1/\xi^{+}_{\sigma}
.

Remark 3.4

(all rational slopes)
.

We may extend Definition
3.2
to all slopes in
\mathbb{Q}_{\geq 0}
using the action of
J\colon\sigma\mapsto 1/\sigma
hence of
D_{J\sigma}=D_{J}D_{\sigma}
, so that
C_{J\sigma}
is obtained from
C_{\sigma}
by applying the substitution
(A,B)\mapsto(B,A)
namely
\AD_{J}\colon(R,L)\mapsto(L,R)
, which by
{\dagger}
-symmetry amounts to reversing the order of the
\{L,R\}
word and inverting
\rho_{\sigma},\xi_{\sigma}^{\pm}
(so for
\sigma\in[0,1)
we have
\rho_{\sigma},\xi_{\sigma}^{+},-1/\xi_{\sigma}^{-}\in(0,1)
).

One could further extend Definition
3.2
to all slopes in
\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}
using the action of
S\colon(0,\infty]\ni\sigma\mapsto-1/\sigma\in(-\infty,0]
so that
D_{S\sigma}=D_{S}D_{\sigma}
hence
C_{S\sigma}
is obtained from
C_{\sigma}
by applying the substitution
(A,B)\mapsto(B,A^{-1})
(that is
D_{S}\colon(A,B)\mapsto(A^{-1}BA,A^{-1})

Remark 3.5

(slope is frequency of
C_{\sigma}

The slope
\sigma\in\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}

Proposition 3.7

(simple geodesics in
\mathbf{M}^{\prime}
with rational slope)
.

Consider a primitive vector

\sigma=m/n\in\mathbb{Q}\Proj^{1}

The simple closed geodesic homologous to

is

of the geodesic

\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}
of the geodesic

There are two more simple geodesics avoiding
\xi^{\prime}_{\sigma}
, their past arise from the cusp and future accumulate on

\bmod{\Gamma^{\prime}}

(0,\xi^{+}_{\sigma})
and
(\infty,\xi^{-}_{\sigma})
in
\mathcal{G}(\mathbf{HP})
.

These

\sigma
are disjoint.

Every simple geodesic of the type cusp-to-cusp or cusp-to-periodic or periodic arises as such, in a unique way (namely distinct slopes give rise to distinct quadruples), and any two such geodesics with different slope have intersections.

Definition 3.8

(Sturmian sequences and numbers)
.

Define the set of
Sturmian parameters
as the (slope, intercepts)
(\sigma,\tau)\in(0,1)\times[0,1)
such that
\sigma\notin\mathbb{Q}
and
\tau\notin(1/2+\mathbb{Z})+\sigma(1/2+\mathbb{Z})
.

To such Sturmian parameters, is associated the (lower)
Sturmian sequence

X_{\sigma,\tau}\in\{A,B\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by

\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}\colon\quad X_{\sigma,\tau}[n]=A^{r_{\sigma,\tau}(n)}B\quad\mathrm{where}\quad r_{\sigma,\tau}(n)=\lfloor(n+1)\sigma+\tau\rfloor-\lfloor n\sigma+\tau\rfloor\in\{0,1\}

encoding the
\mathbb{Z}
-orbit of
\tau
under addition by
\sigma_{1}
for the partition
\mathbb{R}\bmod{\mathbb{Z}}=[-\sigma_{1},1-\sigma_{1})
in two intervals
[-\sigma_{1},1-2\sigma_{1})\sqcup[1-2\sigma_{1},1-\sigma_{1})
, as
r_{\sigma,\tau}(n)
is the
n
-the return time to the left interval.

We may thus define the
pair of conjugate Sturmian numbers

(\xi^{-}_{\sigma,\tau},\xi^{+}_{\sigma,\tau})\in(-\infty,-1)\times(0,1)
so that
-1/\xi^{-}_{\sigma,\tau}
and
1/\xi^{+}_{\sigma,\tau}
are the fixed points of the infinite products of the generators
A,B\in\Gamma^{\prime}
:

-1/\xi^{-}_{\sigma,\tau}=X_{\sigma,\tau}[-1]\cdot X_{\sigma,\tau}[-2]\cdots\cdot\infty\qquad\xi^{+}_{\sigma,\tau}=X_{\sigma,\tau}[0]\cdot X_{\sigma,\tau}[1]\cdots\cdot\infty

whose continued fraction expansions in
\{1,2\}^{\mathbb{N}}
can be recovered from
X\in\{A,B\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
by applying the morphisms
(A,B)\mapsto(RL,LR)
to find two sequences in
\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and grouping exponents.

When
t=0
, the sequence
X_{\sigma,0}
and numbers
\xi^{\pm}_{\sigma,0}
are often called
characteristic Sturmian
.

Remark 3.9

(upper Sturmian sequence)
.

One may also define the upper Sturmian sequence
X_{\sigma,\tau}
using a ceiling in the definition of
r_{\sigma,\tau}(n)
, but the
\{L,R\}
-encoding of

will only differ by
\Shift

Remark 3.10

(all irrational slopes)
.

We may extend Definition
3.8
to all positive irrational slopes
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
by the action of
J\colon(\sigma,\tau)\mapsto(1/\sigma,1-\tau)
which changes

D_{J}\colon(A,B)\mapsto(B,A)

We may further extend Definition

(A,B)\mapsto(B,A^{-1})

Hence we may similarly define the associated Sturmian numbers.

Remark 3.11

(From Ostrowski to
S
-adic expansion)
.

Consider Sturmian parameters
(\sigma,\tau)\in(0,1)\times[0,1)
and write
\sigma=\lfloor\,{0,s_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor
.The
Ostrowski expansion
of
\tau
in base

is the unique

\tau=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\tfrac{t_{n}}{\sigma_{n}}=t_{1}\cdot\lfloor\,{0;s_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor+t_{2}\cdot\lfloor\,{0;s_{2},\dots}\,\rfloor+\dots

With the sequences
s\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{N}_{\geq_{1}}}
and
t\in(\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1})^{\mathbb{N}_{\geq_{1}}}

\xi_{\sigma,\tau}\in\{A,B\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
as the attractive fixed point under an infinite composition of homeomorphisms of

, namely the
\Shift

X_{\sigma,\tau}=\Shift^{t_{1}}\circ D_{L}^{s_{1}}\circ\Shift^{t_{2}}\circ D_{R}^{s_{2}}\circ\Shift^{t_{3}}\circ D_{L}^{s_{3}}\circ\Shift^{t_{4}}\circ D_{R}^{s_{4}}\circ\dots\cdot(A^{\mathbb{Z}})

In particular

by the fixed point of a single morphism of the monoid

are eventually periodic, which means that
\sigma
is quadratic and

Note that in the characteristic Sturmian case

It follows that a Sturmian number

Remark 3.12

Behold the Euclidean plane

We finally mention the initial motivation of this work,

Context 3.17
.

Fix an irrational slope
\sigma\in\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, consider its continued fraction expansion
\sigma=\lfloor\,{s_{0},s_{1},\dots}\,\rfloor
, and denote
\lambda_{\sigma}=\limsup_{k}\lfloor\,{s_{k};s_{k-1},\dots,s_{1},s_{0}}\,\rfloor
.

Let
\tau\in(0,1)
such that
\tau\notin(1/2+\mathbb{Z})+\sigma(1/2+\mathbb{Z})
so as to obtain the Sturmian sequence
X_{\sigma,\tau}\in\{A,B\}^{\mathbb{Z}}\subset\{L,R\}^{\mathbb{Z}}
and real numbers
\xi^{\pm}_{\sigma,\tau}\in\mathbb{R}
.

The transcedence of
\xi^{\pm}_{\sigma,\tau}
was initially proved in
[
4
, Proposition 3 and Theorem 7]
: their strategy consists of showing that a Sturmian number is very well approximated by quadratic numbers, well enough to satisfy the hypotheses in the following of Schmidt
[
37
]
: for a real irrational
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}

and infinitely many quadratic irrationals
\xi^{+}_{k}
such that
\lvert\xi^{+}-\xi^{+}_{k}\rvert<H(\xi_{k})^{-3-\epsilon}
, then
\xi^{+}
must be transcendental.
Let us mention another proof of transcendence using the fact that they begin with infinitely many palindromes (
[
1
, §2]
).
The following
[
2

gives the state of the art on their Mahler measures.

Theorem 3.18

(transcendence of Sturmian numbers)

The numbers
\xi^{\pm}_{\sigma,\tau}
are transcendent, and more precisely: if
s
is unbounded then
w_{2}(\xi^{\pm}_{\sigma,\tau})=\infty
(Mahler type
\mathrm{U}_{2}
), and if
s
is bounded then
\exists c\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},\;\forall d\in\mathbb{N}_{\geq 1}\colon\>w_{d}(\xi^{+})\leq\exp\left(c(\log 3d)^{3}(\log\log 3d)^{2}\right)
(Mahler class
\mathrm{S}
or
\mathrm{T}

[
11

computes the Mahler measures for characteristic Sturmian numbers.

Theorem 3.21

(Lagrange constants of Markov and Sturmian numbers)
.

For
\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\mathbb{Q}
:

•

\LC(\xi^{+})>3\iff\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot\xi^{+}
contains a Markov quadratic

•

\LC(\xi^{+})=3\iff\PGL_{2}(\mathbb{Z})\cdot\xi^{+}
contains a Sturmian transcendental

In those cases, the orbit

contains a unique Markov or Sturmian number up to the action of
S
and of the
\Shift
on
\{R,L\}^{\mathbb{N}}

\xi^{+}\in\mathbb{R}

\xi^{-}\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{\xi^{+}\}
such that
\MC(\xi^{-},\xi^{+})=\LC(\xi^{+})
