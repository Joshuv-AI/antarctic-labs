A scalar field equation on hyperbolic space with indefinite sign nonlinearity
Debabrata Karmakar, Atanu Manna, Bhakti Bhusan Manna

Abstract
In this article, we study threshold phenomena for the semilinear double-power elliptic equation − Δ 𝔹 N ​ u − λ ​ u = | u | p − 1 ​ u − | u | q − 1 ​ u , u ∈ H 1 ​ ( 𝔹 N ) , -\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u-\lambda u=|u|^{p-1}u-|u|^{q-1}u,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{B}^{N}), on the hyperbolic space 𝔹 N \mathbb{B}^{N} for N ≥ 3 N\geq 3 . For parameters 1 < p ≤ 2 ∗ − 1 1<p\leq 2^{*}-1 (though we occasionally allow for supercritical exponents) and q > 0 q>0 , we seek to identify the optimal spectral regimes for λ ∈ ℝ \lambda\in\mathbb{R} that delineate the existence and non-existence of positive-energy solutions. We achieve a complete resolution of these thresholds across all exponent configurations: p < q p<q , 0 < q < 1 < p 0<q<1<p , and 1 < q < p 1<q<p . Our results demonstrate that the boundary separating these regimes is governed by an explicit critical spectral parameter, which depends on p p , q q , and N N in the regime where p < q p<q , but depends solely on N N in the remaining cases.

1.1
Double-power nonlinearities in the Euclidean space

Consider the following problem posed on the Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
:

-\Delta u+\mu u=|u|^{p-1}u-|u|^{q-1}u,\quad u\in H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
1<p<q
and
\mu>0
. The right-hand side is commonly referred to as a
double-power nonlinearity
, featuring a competition between a focusing term (with exponent
p
) and a defocusing term (with exponent
q
). Let us define the function:

g(u)=-\mu u+|u|^{p-1}u-|u|^{q-1}u.

In Euclidean spaces, the existence and qualitative properties of solutions to this equation are well understood. For
N\geq 3
,
MR0695535
established that the equation admits a positive, radially symmetric, radially decreasing
C^{2}
solution with exponential decay at infinity if and only if the primitive of
g
is strictly positive somewhere. Namely:

G(\zeta):=\int_{0}^{\zeta}g(s)\,ds>0,\quad\text{for some }\zeta>0.

The necessity of this condition is a direct consequence of the Pohozaev identity:

\frac{N-2}{2}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}|\nabla u|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{N}}G(u)\,dx.

Moreover, it exhibits a strict existence threshold:

\mu^{*}=\frac{2(q+1)^{\frac{p-1}{q-p}}(p-1)^{\frac{p-1}{q-p}}(q-p)}{(p+1)^{\frac{q-1}{q-p}}(q-1)^{\frac{q-1}{q-p}}}>0.

For
\mu\geq\mu^{*}
, the equation possesses no non-trivial solutions. Conversely, for all
\mu\in(0,\mu^{*})
, it has a unique positive solution (up to Euclidean translations) that vanishes at infinity
MR4171857
. Furthermore, when
1<q<p<2^{*}-1
, an infinite number of solutions can exist
MR0454365
.

The behavior of the equation shifts dramatically when
\mu\leq 0
(i.e.
g^{\prime}(0)\geq 0
). For
\mu=0
and any
1<p\leq 2^{*}-1
, the Pohozaev identity precludes the existence of any non-trivial finite energy solutions
MR3210961
. When
1<p<2^{*}-1
, the non-existence of ground states has also been confirmed using the method of moving spheres
MR2403112
. However, entering the supercritical regime (
p>2^{*}
), a unique radial ground state solution does exist (see
MR0695535
for existence and
MR1165421
for uniqueness).

In this regime,
\mu<0
(
g^{\prime}(0)>0
), the problem falls under the classification of Helmholtz equations. Under these conditions, the equation admits no non-trivial solutions in
H^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{N})
. The classical Pohozaev identity explicitly rules out solutions when
1<q\leq 2^{*}-1\leq p
(with
p\neq q
). Despite its interesting dynamics, this specific regime has received relatively little attention in the literature; for existing discussions, we refer to
MR3149060
;
MR3709292
.

The case of sub-linear defocusing (
0<q<1
) presents distinct geometric features. For
\mu=0
and
N\geq 3
,
MR1364001
demonstrated that a unique non-trivial classical solution exists. A remarkable property of this regime is that every weak solution possesses compact support. By employing the moving plane method, they also established that every connected component of the positivity set
\left\{u>0\right\}
is a ball, and the solution
u
is radially symmetric with respect to its center.

Further studies have expanded on double-power nonlinearities across various Euclidean contexts. For instance, the zero-mass problem admits radial nodal bubble-tower solutions when
p
is sufficiently close to
2^{*}-1
and
q
is taken to be either in
\left(\frac{N}{N-2},2^{*}-1\right)
or super critical, where
p
is sub critical in the former and super critical in the latter case
MR3668363
. Recent works have also investigated the asymptotic behavior of least-energy solutions in singularly perturbed elliptic equations under Dirichlet boundary conditions
MR2602148
;
MR2824573
, and the existence of ground states with prescribed
L^{2}
mass in both subcritical and critical regimes
MR4096725
;
MR4107073
. Furthermore, competing nonlinearities have been explored on the Cayley graphs of discrete groups
MR4564936
. For a other overview of Euclidean results, see
MR0695536
;

;

This specific type of competing nonlinearity has also been generalized to non-local and geometric settings. For the fractional Laplacian,

considered the analogue:

(-\Delta)^{s}u-\theta\frac{u}{|x|^{2s}}=u^{p}-u^{q}\quad\text{in }\mathbb{R}^{N},\quad 0<u\in\dot{H}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{N})\cap L^{q+1}(\mathbb{R}^{N}),

where
s\in(0,1)
and

denote the best constant in the fractional Hardy inequality. For

Extending these problems to general Riemannian manifolds

introduces significant geometric interplay. For the equation
-\Delta_{g}u=u^{p}-u^{q}

In the context of hyperbolic geometry, research has recently expanded to include equations featuring sign-changing logarithmic nonlinearities. For instance,
MR4832071
investigated the problem:

-\Delta_{\mathbb{B}^{N}}u-\lambda u=|u|^{p-1}u+\theta u\log u^{2},

where
\theta\in\mathbb{R}
and
1<p\leq 2^{*}-1
. This model shares a notable structural resonance with our double-power equation, as the logarithmic term

This characteristic generates sharp threshold phenomena that dictate the existence and non-existence of positive energy solutions, closely paralleling the competitive dynamics seen in our work.
