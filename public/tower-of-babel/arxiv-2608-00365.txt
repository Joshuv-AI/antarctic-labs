Hamiltonian Thresholds for Objective Records
Jie Gu

Abstract
Classical objectivity requires an environment to witness a pointer value, not merely to decohere it. We derive microscopic Hamiltonian laws for this distinction. Exact QND product monitoring yields a certified two-edge strong-Darwinism window: sufficient witnesses cross the optimal record-discrimination edge while retaining an unobserved complement that crosses the residual-coherence edge. Under additional regularity assumptions, the supplement gives converse bounds at the same logarithmic scale. For controlled-phase monitors, environmental asymmetry gives a single-fragment information bound, a redundancy converse, and, in efficient i.i.d. streams, an achievable block-capacity law for redundant witnesses. A finite-temperature spin-phase bath shows the physical separation: hot probes can decohere efficiently while losing the local asymmetry needed for objective witnesses.



Introduction.—

Macroscopic reality is normally learned indirectly. We do not interrogate the
center-of-mass state of a dust grain, a spin, or a measuring apparatus itself;
we intercept photons, phonons, scattered molecules, or other environmental
carriers that have already interacted with it. The quantum-to-classical problem
therefore has two logically distinct parts. Decoherence explains why
interference between preferred pointer states becomes locally invisible in the
reduced state of the system
[
33
,
34
,
28
]
.
Objectivity requires more: many independently accessible fragments of the
environment must carry the same classical record, so that different observers
can infer the same pointer value without perturbing the system or one another

[
24
,
25
,
6
,
5
,
35
,
27
]
.

This distinction is not merely semantic. A bath can decohere a system very
efficiently while failing to provide locally readable records. Conversely, a
fragment may be strongly correlated with the system but still owe part of its
mutual information to quantum correlations that are not objective records.
The usual Darwinistic signature, a plateau of mutual information versus
fragment size, therefore does not by itself certify classical objectivity

[
38
,
19
]
. Recent work has sharpened this warning by
separating redundancy from consensus, by quantifying when independently
accessed fragments support observer agreement, by showing that non-averaged
mutual information can be misleading in inhomogeneous environments, by recasting
emergent objectivity as a metrological information-acquisition problem, and by
identifying branching structure as the low-discord state structure compatible
with Darwinistic classicality
[
10
,
31
,
11
,
17
,
29
]
. Spectrum-broadcast-structure and strong
quantum Darwinism make the missing requirement explicit: an objective fragment
must contain the full pointer Holevo information and have negligible
pointer-basis discord with the system
[
18
,
16
,
21
]
. This is also the operational content
of local broadcasting: only classical correlations can be redundantly and
locally proliferated
[
26
,
7
]
.

The microscopic question is then sharper than “does the environment decohere
the system?” It is: which Hamiltonian resources make a fragment a witness,
and how many such witnesses can coexist? This question has become especially
timely because recent work has moved beyond state-level diagnostics toward
amplification bounds, consensus measures, metrological witnesses, encoding
transitions, von-Neumann measurement models, Hamiltonian classifications, and
thermalization-based mechanisms for Darwinistic redundancy

[
30
,
40
,
31
,
17
,
14
,
13
,
1
,
8
]
. This Letter answers the
finite-fragment version of that question for repeated controlled monitoring.
We show that strong quantum Darwinism is governed by two competing Hamiltonian
exponents. An observed fragment is certified as an objective witness when it
crosses a record-discrimination edge, controlled by quantum Chernoff information

[
15
,
3
,
23
,
37
,
30
,
40
]
, while the unobserved complement
still crosses a residual-decoherence edge that removes pointer coherences from
the joint state of the system and fragment. Under the regular finite-model
assumptions stated in the supplement, failure to cross the same edges gives a
matching logarithmic converse. Thus the certified observer window is finite:
fragments that are too small are unreadable, whereas fragments that are too
large leave insufficient environment to classicalize their correlation with the
system.

We further identify the resource behind this window for controlled-phase
monitors. Decoherence can arise from phase dispersion, but readable local
records require environmental asymmetry relative to the coupling generator,
closely related to coherence-asymmetry resources
[
4
,
22

. The resulting asymmetry bound is a converse for all such phase monitors; for
homogeneous i.i.d. streams with a positive surplus-dephasing exponent it is
supplemented by an explicit achievable block-capacity law. A
finite-temperature spin-phase bath illustrates the physical separation: hot
probes can suppress system coherences efficiently while losing the local
asymmetry needed to support objective witnesses.

We use the standard fragmented-environment architecture of quantum Darwinism, in
which many environmental carriers acquire conditional imprints of a preferred
system observable and are later grouped into independently accessible fragments

F

,

,

]
. In

monitoring sector. The

H_{k}^{\rm QND}(t)=H_{S,k}^{\Pi}(t)+H_{E_{k}}(t)+\sum_{x}\Pi_{x}\otimes B_{k,x}(t).

(1)

Here
H_{S,k}^{\Pi}(t)
is pointer diagonal,
H_{E_{k}}(t)

self-Hamiltonian, and

conditionally drives carrier
E_{k}
when
the pointer value is
x

We work in the interaction picture with respect to the pointer-diagonal system
Hamiltonian, or assume that
H_{S,k}^{\Pi}
acts as a scalar on each pointer
sector.
The corresponding collision is an ideal controlled
monitor,

U_{k}^{0}=\sum_{x}\Pi_{x}\otimes U_{k,x},\qquad\rho_{k|x}=U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x}^{\dagger}.

(2)

The environmental input is initially uncorrelated with the system and factorized
across carriers,

\rho_{SE}(0)=\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k}.

(3)

Let
\{\Pi_{x}\}
be the pointer projectors of a finite-dimensional system. All
entropies and information quantities are measured in bits, with

S(\rho)=-\Tr(\rho\log_{2}\rho)
; logarithms in Chernoff and dephasing
exponents are natural logarithms. For a joint state
\rho_{SF}
, define

\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho_{SF})=\sum_{x}(\Pi_{x}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})\rho_{SF}(\Pi_{x}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F}).

(4)

The pointer probabilities and pointer entropy are

p_{x}=\Tr[(\Pi_{x}\otimes\mathbb{I}_{F})\rho_{SF}],\qquad H_{\Pi}=-\sum_{x}p_{x}\log_{2}p_{x}.

(5)

With
I(A:B)_{\omega}
denoting mutual information in the state
\omega
, the
pointer Holevo information and pointer discord are

\displaystyle\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)

\displaystyle=I(S:F)_{\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho_{SF})},

(6)

\displaystyle D_{\Pi}(S:F)

\displaystyle=I(S:F)_{\rho_{SF}}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F).

(7)

We use the local strong-QD deficit

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F)\equiv\left[H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\right]_{+}+D_{\Pi}(S:F).

(8)

Small
\Delta_{\rm SQD}
means that the fragment stores the pointer value and that its
remaining correlation with the system is classical in the pointer basis. The
first term is a record-accessibility deficit, while the second is a residual
pointer-discord deficit; the following sections show that they are controlled by
distinct Hamiltonian exponents.

Certified Two-Edge Observer Window for Product Monitoring.—

The two terms in Eq. (
8
) impose complementary
finite-fragment requirements. The record term asks whether an observer of

F
can distinguish the conditional fragment states associated with
different pointer values; for product monitoring this is a quantum
hypothesis-testing problem, with the quantum Chernoff information setting the
Darwinistic amplification exponent
[
3
,
23
,
37
]
. This gives a lower record-discrimination edge. The
discord term asks whether the remaining
S:F
correlation is classical in
the pointer basis. Since a mutual-information plateau may still contain
discord
[
38
,
19
]
, spectrum-broadcast structure,
strong quantum Darwinism, and no-local-broadcasting require the unobserved
complement
\bar{F}
to suppress the residual pointer coherences

[

16
,
21
,
26
,
7
]
. This gives an upper
surplus-dephasing edge.

We now turn these two requirements into finite-size certificates for exact QND product monitoring.
For a fragment
F\subset E
, let
\overline{F}=E\setminus F
denote the unobserved
complement. In exact QND product monitoring,

\rho_{F|x}=\bigotimes_{k\in F}\rho_{k|x},\qquad\rho_{k|x}=U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x}^{\dagger}.

(9)

The two Hamiltonian exponents controlling strong Darwinism are the record exponent
and the surplus-dephasing exponent.

The record exponent of the observed fragment is the multiple quantum Chernoff exponent

C_{\rm rec}(F)=\min_{x\neq x^{\prime}:\,p_{x}p_{x^{\prime}}>0}\Big[-\log\inf_{0\leq s\leq 1}\prod_{k\in F}\Tr\!\big(\rho_{k|x}^{s}\rho_{k|x^{\prime}}^{1-s}\big)\Big].

(10)

It quantifies how well the states
\{\rho_{F|x}\}
can be distinguished by an optimal
observer of
F
. The minimum is restricted to pointer labels with nonzero prior probability; equivalently, one may assume the finite pointer alphabet has full support.
In a homogeneous i.i.d. stream with
|F|=m
,

C_{\rm rec}(F)
scales with
m
linearly.

The residual coherence left by the unobserved complement is

\displaystyle\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\in\overline{F}}\Tr\!\left(U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right),

(11)

\displaystyle R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})

\displaystyle=\max_{x\neq x^{\prime}}{}^{\prime}\left|\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}\right|^{2},

(12)

where the prime restricts the maximum to active coherences,

\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\neq 0
. The surplus-dephasing exponent is

C_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})=-\log R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F}).

(13)

It quantifies the decohering power still supplied by the environment outside

F
. In a homogeneous i.i.d. stream with
N
total carriers
and
|F|=m
,
C_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})
scales linearly with
N-m
.

The record exponent therefore sets the lower edge of the observer window: a fragment
that is too small has not accumulated enough distinguishability. The surplus-dephasing
exponent sets the upper edge: a fragment that is too large leaves too small an unobserved
complement to remove the pointer-basis coherence that still purifies the system.

Theorem 1

Certified two-edge window.

Consider exact QND product monitoring with a finite full-support pointer alphabet.
Under the regular finite-model assumptions, up to additive

\mathcal{O}(1)
constants in the exponent thresholds, the two inequalities

\begin{cases}C_{\rm rec}(F)\geq\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{O}(1),\\[2.84526pt]
C_{\rm dec}(\bar{F})\geq\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{O}(1)\end{cases}

(14)

are a sufficient finite-accuracy certificate for

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F)=\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon)
. The constants may depend on the fixed model and
pointer prior, but not on
F
or
\varepsilon
.

The regular finite-model assumptions and the converse bounds at
the same logarithmic scale are stated in the Supplemental Material (SM).
Theorem
1
gives a finite-size certificate which,
counterintuitively, is not monotone in fragment size. It combines the
record-discrimination requirement for the observed fragment and the
residual-decoherence requirement for its complement as two competing
Hamiltonian thresholds. The lower edge is an optimal multi-hypothesis
discrimination problem on the observed carriers. The upper edge is the residual
coherence left by the unobserved carriers. For a target accuracy
\varepsilon
,
Eq. (
14
) certifies objective records only when both edges
are crossed. A small fragment has not accumulated enough distinguishability,
while a nearly complete fragment captures degrees of freedom that still purify
the system. This is complementary to recent analyses of
redundancy, consensus, and metrological readout precision, which emphasize that
fragment averages, observer agreement, and measurement choice carry operational
information not contained in a single mutual-information curve

[
10
,
11
,
31
,
17
]
.

Asymmetry as the Resource for Redundant Witnesses.—

The certified-window theorem identifies the kinematics of objective fragments, but
not the microscopic resource that pays for the record edge. Here we isolate
that resource in the QND, or phase-monitoring, sector of product monitoring:
when conditional environmental dynamics is generated by a fixed local Abelian
algebra, redundant readable records require symmetry breaking in the initial
environment.
The basic single-generator case,

H_{\rm int}=A_{S}\otimes G_{E},\qquad A_{S}=\sum_{x}a_{x}\Pi_{x},\qquad G_{E}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}G_{k},

(16)

is the familiar pure-dephasing Hamiltonian underlying central-spin and
hazy-environment models of redundant records

[
5
,
36
,
38
,
37
]
. Here
G_{k}
is the Hermitian local generator whose
exponential implements the pointer-conditioned phase rotation of carrier
k
,
and whose eigenspaces define the charge sectors relative to which asymmetry is
measured.

We allow each carrier to have a finite commuting family of local generators

\bm{G}_{k}=(G_{k}^{1},\ldots,G_{k}^{r_{G}}),[G_{k}^{\mu},G_{k}^{\nu}]=0,

(17)

with conditional phase action

U_{k,x}=\exp\!\left[-\mathrm{i}\sum_{\mu=1}^{r_{G}}\theta_{k,x}^{\mu}G_{k}^{\mu}\right].

(18)

For a fragment
F
,

\displaystyle U_{F,x}

\displaystyle=\bigotimes_{k\in F}U_{k,x}

\displaystyle=\exp\!\left[-\mathrm{i}\sum_{k\in F}\sum_{\mu=1}^{r_{G}}\theta_{k,x}^{\mu}G_{k}^{\mu}\right],

(19)

\displaystyle\rho_{F|x}

\displaystyle=U_{F,x}\sigma_{F}U_{F,x}^{\dagger}.

(20)

Let
\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}
denote the pinching onto the joint eigenspaces
of the local commuting family

\bm{G}_{F}=\{G_{k}^{\mu}:\ k\in F,\ \mu=1,\ldots,r_{G}\}.

(21)

The local Abelian asymmetry of
F
is

A_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\tau)=S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}\tau)-S(\tau).

(22)

For a nondegenerate joint charge basis this is the relative entropy of
coherence; with degeneracies it is the relative entropy of asymmetry for the
Abelian algebra
[
4
,
22
]
. The locality is
essential: coherences hidden inside a coarse total charge do not yield
independent records for disjoint observers.
This separates dephasing from witnessing. If

\Delta_{\bm{G}_{k}}(\sigma_{k})=\sigma_{k}
, then

U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x}^{\dagger}=\sigma_{k}
for all
x
, so carrier
k

stores no readable record. Yet its single-carrier contribution

\Gamma_{xx^{\prime}}^{(k)}=\Tr(U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger})
to the system
coherence factor may still have modulus below one. Diagonal charge uncertainty can dephase the
system; readable local records require coherence between charge sectors.

Theorem 2

Consider an exact product monitor with finite pointer alphabet whose conditional
environmental dynamics is the Abelian phase monitor of
Eq. (
20
), and assume that
Eq. (
22
) is finite. Then every fragment
F

obeys the asymmetry converse

\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq A_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\sigma_{F}).

(23)

Thus an Abelian phase monitor can convert pre-existing local environmental
asymmetry into pointer information, but cannot create more readable record
information than the fragment’s local asymmetry budget.

Consequently, for a product environment
\sigma_{E}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k}
, if
F_{1},\ldots,F_{R}
are disjoint

\delta
-accurate strong-QD witnesses, meaning

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F_{i})\leq\delta
for every
i

N
-carrier environment, then
their maximum number
R_{\delta}(N)
satisfies

R_{\delta}(N)\leq\frac{\sum_{k=1}^{N}A_{\bm{G}_{k}}(\sigma_{k})}{H_{\Pi}-\delta},\qquad 0<\delta<H_{\Pi}.

(24)

For a homogeneous i.i.d. stream,
\sigma_{E}=\sigma^{\otimes N}
and

U_{k,x}=U_{x}
, assume that complements of fixed-size blocks have a positive
surplus-dephasing exponent, i.e., for each fixed block size
m
there are
constants
b_{m},c_{m}>0
such that

R_{\rm dec}(\bar{F})\leq b_{m}e^{-c_{m}(N-m)}
for every
|F|=m
.
If
\chi_{m}
is the pointer Holevo information
in an
m
-carrier block, define

m_{\rm rec}(\delta)=\min\{m\geq 1:\chi_{m}\geq H_{\Pi}-\delta\}.

(25)

Then, up to the unavoidable integer rounding of block size, the asymptotic
density of independent objective witnesses is

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta):=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{R_{\delta}(N)}{N}=\frac{1}{m_{\rm rec}(\delta)}

(26)

at every continuity point of the integer-valued function
m_{\rm rec}(\delta)
. At
jump points, the corresponding one-sided liminf–limsup statement retains the
same integer block-size rounding.

The theorem places local Abelian asymmetry between the Hamiltonian mechanism
and the state-level criteria for objectivity. Quantum Darwinism introduced
redundancy as the proliferation of environmental records of a pointer
observable
[
24
,
5
,
35
]
; spectrum
broadcast structure and strong quantum Darwinism sharpened this into locally
accessible classical information with no residual quantum correlations

[

,
21
,
19
]
; generic Darwinism
explains why redundantly accessible information must be classical and tied to
one effective system observable
[

]
. In the
Abelian phase-monitoring sector, Theorem

adds the
resource account: crossing the lower edge of the certified observer window

Discussion and outlook.—

This Letter gives finite-fragment Hamiltonian certificates for objective
records. In exact QND product monitoring, strong quantum Darwinism requires
two simultaneous conditions: the observed fragment must resolve the pointer
alternatives, and the unobserved complement must still erase the residual
pointer coherence. Objectivity is therefore windowed rather than monotone in
fragment size, refining the usual redundancy picture of quantum Darwinism and
its strong/SBS variants

[
24
,
35
,
16
,
21
,
19
]
.

For Abelian phase monitors, the lower edge of this window has a resource
meaning. A carrier can become a local witness only by spending asymmetry
relative to the coupling generator; dephasing alone is not enough

[
4
,
22
]
. The tilted spin-phase bath makes
the separation explicit: thermal mixing reduces transverse asymmetry, raises
the record edge, and lowers the block redundancy density even when the residual
dephasing exponent remains finite

[
36
,
39
,
37
]
.

The next step is to test these two edges in platforms where both system
coherence and fragment information can be measured, such as central-spin
systems, ancilla arrays, superconducting readout chains, and engineered
collision models

[
30
,
32
,
12
,
13
]
. Open problems include non-Abelian
monitoring, correlated inputs, interacting baths, and finite-time departures
from product QND dynamics.

This appendix proves the finite-size estimates used in the certified two-edge theorem,
Theorem
1
, and explains how the QND microscopic Hamiltonian
in Eq. (
1
) leads to the exact product-monitoring dynamics in
Eq. (
2
). The final subsection extends this QND
construction to weak non-QND perturbations and records the associated stability
correction.

We start from an initially uncorrelated environment,

\rho_{SE}(0)=\rho_{S}(0)\otimes\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k}.

(S1)

Since the QND Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
) is diagonal in the pointer
projectors, the propagator for the
k
th collision has the controlled form

U_{k}^{0}=\sum_{x}\Pi_{x}\otimes U_{k,x}.

(S2)

The conditional carrier state is therefore

\rho_{k|x}=U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x}^{\dagger},

(S3)

and, for a product monitor, the conditional state of a fragment
F
factorizes:

\rho_{F|x}=\bigotimes_{k\in F}\rho_{k|x}.

(S4)

Tracing out the complement
\overline{F}=E\setminus F
gives the controlled reference
state of
S:F
,

\displaystyle\rho^{0}_{SF}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,x^{\prime}}\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\otimes\left[\bigotimes_{k\in F}U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}},

(S5)

where

\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}=\prod_{k\in\overline{F}}\Tr\!\left(U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right).

(S6)

For
x=x^{\prime}
,
\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx}=1
. For
x\neq x^{\prime}
,

\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}
is the residual pointer coherence left after the
unobserved complement has been discarded. Pointer dephasing removes precisely
these off-diagonal blocks:

\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF})=\sum_{x}\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x}\otimes\bigotimes_{k\in F}\rho_{k|x}.

(S7)

Thus the dephased state is a classical-quantum ensemble with label
x
and
record state
\rho_{F|x}
.

S1.1
Record contribution

The record part of the strong-QD deficit is

[H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)]_{+}.

(S8)

For the controlled state in Eq. (
S7
),

\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)
is the Holevo information of the ensemble

\mathcal{E}_{F}=\{p_{x},\rho_{F|x}\}.

(S9)

Let
P_{e}^{\star}(F)
be the minimum probability of incorrectly guessing
x
by
an arbitrary POVM on
F
. For any POVM with classical outcome
Y
, the
Holevo bound gives

I(X:Y)\leq\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}.

(S10)

Using
I(X:Y)=H(X)-H(X|Y)
and
H(X)=H_{\Pi}
, this implies

H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq H(X|Y).

(S11)

Choose a POVM attaining
P_{e}^{\star}(F)
, up to an arbitrarily small slack. Fano’s
inequality for a
q
-valued pointer alphabet gives

H(X|Y)\leq h_{2}(P_{e}^{\star})+P_{e}^{\star}\log_{2}(q-1),

(S12)

where
h_{2}
is the binary entropy. Hence

H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq h_{2}(P_{e}^{\star})+P_{e}^{\star}\log_{2}(q-1).

(S13)

This is the finite-size direction used for the lower edge of
Theorem
1
: a fragment that can discriminate the pointer
label with small error has small Holevo deficit. For a binary pointer,

q=2
, the second term vanishes.

S1.2
Surplus-dephasing contribution

The pointer-basis discord is

D_{\Pi}(S:F)=I(S:F)_{\rho}-I(S:F)_{\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho)}.

(S14)

For the controlled reference state, the only difference between
\rho^{0}_{SF}

and
\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF})
is the off-diagonal operator

X_{SF}=\rho^{0}_{SF}-\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF}),

(S15)

namely

\displaystyle X_{SF}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\otimes\left[\bigotimes_{k\in F}U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right]\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}.

(S16)

Each environmental factor has trace norm one,

\left\|U_{k,x}\sigma_{k}U_{k,x^{\prime}}^{\dagger}\right\|_{1}=\|\sigma_{k}\|_{1}=1.

(S17)

Therefore

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{0}_{SF}-\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF})\right\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\left\|\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}\left|\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C_{\rho}\max_{x\neq x^{\prime}}{}^{\prime}\left|\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}\right|,

(S18)

where the prime restricts the maximum to active coherences and

C_{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\neq x^{\prime}}\left\|\Pi_{x}\rho_{S}(0)\Pi_{x^{\prime}}\right\|_{1}.

(S19)

Let

\eta_{0}=\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{0}_{SF}-\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF})\right\|_{1}.

(S20)

Since
\rho^{0}_{F}=\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF})_{F}
, the discord can be written as a
difference of conditional entropies and system entropies. The
Alicki-Fannes-Winter continuity bound for conditional entropy, together with the
ordinary Fannes-Audenaert bound for the system entropy, gives for

0\leq\eta_{0}\leq 1/2

D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq g_{d_{S}}(\eta_{0}),

(S21)

where
d_{S}=\dim\mathcal{H}_{S}
, with the nonoptimized envelope

g_{d}(\eta)=4\eta\log_{2}d+4h_{2}(\eta).

(S22)

The relevant dimension is
d_{S}
, not the fragment dimension, because the
conditional-entropy continuity bound depends on the conditioned subsystem.
Combining Eqs. (
S18
) and
(
S21
) yields

D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq g_{d_{S}}\!\left(C_{\rho}\max_{x\neq x^{\prime}}{}^{\prime}|\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}|\right).

(S23)

This is the finite-size surplus-dephasing estimate used for the upper edge of
Theorem
1
.

S1.3
Stability under non-QND terms

The main text treats the QND Hamiltonian in Eq. (
1
). To include
weak departures from QND monitoring, replace it by

H_{k}^{\rm act}(t)=H_{k}^{\rm QND}(t)+V_{k}^{\perp}(t),

(S24)

where
V_{k}^{\perp}(t)
contains the terms that connect different pointer
sectors during the readout. Define the accumulated non-QND action

\xi_{N}=\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}\|V_{k}^{\perp}(t)\|\,\mathrm{d}t,

(S25)

with
\tau_{k}
the duration of the
k
th collision. Let
U^{0}
be the
product controlled propagator generated by the QND part and
U^{\rm act}
the
propagator generated by Eq. (
S24
). In the interaction picture
with respect to the controlled part,

W=(U^{0})^{\dagger}U^{\rm act}=\mathcal{T}\exp\!\left[-\mathrm{i}\sum_{k=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{\tau_{k}}V_{k,I}^{\perp}(t)\,\mathrm{d}t\right],

(S26)

where unitary conjugation gives

\|V_{k,I}^{\perp}(t)\|=\|V_{k}^{\perp}(t)\|
. The Dyson expansion gives

\|W-\mathbb{I}\|\leq\exp(\xi_{N})-1=\xi_{N}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{N}^{2}).

(S27)

Hence, for every density operator,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\|W\rho W^{\dagger}-\rho\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|W-\mathbb{I}\|.

(S28)

After applying the controlled unitary and tracing out arbitrary environmental
subsystems,

\frac{1}{2}\left\|\rho^{\rm act}_{SF}-\rho^{0}_{SF}\right\|_{1}\leq\xi_{N}+\mathcal{O}(\xi_{N}^{2}).

(S29)

Thus the exact product-monitor estimates above remain valid for the full
Hamiltonian, with the two phase boundaries broadened only by continuity
corrections controlled by
\xi_{N}
.

S2.
Certified observer window and regular-model converse bounds

This appendix proves the sufficient direction of Theorem
1

and records the corresponding regular-model converse bounds. The proof separates
the record edge, controlled by optimal discrimination of the observed fragment,
from the residual-coherence edge, controlled by the unobserved complement.

S2.1
Record edge

The record edge concerns the first term in Eq. (
8
). The finite-size estimates above give the direct implication

P_{e}^{\star}(F)\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\to 0,

(S30)

because the right-hand side of Eq. (
S13
) tends to zero with

P_{e}^{\star}(F)
.

For the regular-model converse we use the record-regularity assumption.
For the finite-alphabet product ensembles considered here, record regularity
means that vanishing Holevo deficit and vanishing optimal discrimination error
are equivalent. Equivalently, there are functions
r_{+}(\eta)
and

r_{-}(\eta)
, both tending to zero with
\eta
, such that

\displaystyle P_{e}^{\star}(F)\leq\eta

\displaystyle\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq r_{+}(\eta),

(S31)

\displaystyle H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\eta

\displaystyle\quad\Longrightarrow\quad P_{e}^{\star}(F)\leq r_{-}(\eta).

(S32)

The first implication is supplied by Eq. (
S13
). The second
excludes pathological product ensembles in which the Holevo information approaches

H_{\Pi}
while the pointer labels remain operationally indistinguishable. Under
this assumption,

H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\to 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad P_{e}^{\star}(F)\to 0.

(S33)

Let

K_{\rm rec}(F)=-\log P_{e}^{\star}(F).

(S34)

For a product fragment,

\rho_{F|x}=\bigotimes_{k\in F}\rho_{k|x}.

(S35)

Thus the pairwise Chernoff quantity factorizes:

\displaystyle\inf_{0\leq s\leq 1}\Tr\!\left[\rho_{F|x}^{s}\rho_{F|x^{\prime}}^{1-s}\right]

\displaystyle=\inf_{0\leq s\leq 1}\prod_{k\in F}\Tr\!\left(\rho_{k|x}^{s}\rho_{k|x^{\prime}}^{1-s}\right).

(S36)

The multiple quantum Chernoff theorem, together with the Chernoff-regularity
assumption used for the regular-model converse, identifies the optimal multi-hypothesis error
exponent with the worst pairwise exponent up to subexponential prefactors:

K_{\rm rec}(F)=C_{\rm rec}(F)+o(C_{\rm rec}(F)),

(S37)

where
C_{\rm rec}(F)
is Eq. (
10
). In particular,

K_{\rm rec}(F)\to\infty\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad C_{\rm rec}(F)\to\infty.

(S38)

Equation (
S37
) fixes only the leading exponential
rate and, by itself, does not control finite-size subexponential prefactors.
For the finite-accuracy implication we therefore use the dimension-free
one-shot pairwise bound for multiple quantum hypothesis testing

[
9
]
. Writing

C_{xx^{\prime}}(F)=-\log\inf_{0\leq s\leq 1}\Tr\!\left[\rho_{F|x}^{s}\rho_{F|x^{\prime}}^{1-s}\right],\qquad C_{\rm rec}(F)=\min_{x\neq x^{\prime}}C_{xx^{\prime}}(F),

(S39)

the one-shot bound gives, for a full-support alphabet of fixed size
q
,

\displaystyle P_{e}^{\star}(F)

\displaystyle\leq 4\sum_{x<x^{\prime}}\exp[-C_{xx^{\prime}}(F)]

\displaystyle\leq 2q(q-1)\exp[-C_{\rm rec}(F)].

(S40)

Consequently,

K_{\rm rec}(F)\geq C_{\rm rec}(F)-\log[2q(q-1)].

(S41)

Unlike Eq. (
S37
), this is a nonasymptotic estimate
with a fragment-independent additive constant.

At finite accuracy
\varepsilon
, set

\Phi_{q}(p)=h_{2}(p)+p\log_{2}(q-1),

(S42)

and let
\Phi_{q}^{-1}
denote the inverse of
\Phi_{q}
on its small-error
branch. Equation (
S13
) gives the exact discrimination
threshold

K_{\rm rec}(F)\geq\log\frac{1}{\Phi_{q}^{-1}(\varepsilon)}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon.

(S43)

Combining Eqs. (
S41
) and
(
S43
) gives the explicit sufficient Chernoff
threshold

C_{\rm rec}(F)\geq\log\frac{2q(q-1)}{\Phi_{q}^{-1}(\varepsilon)}=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\log\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\mathcal{O}(1)\quad\Longrightarrow\quad H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon,

(S44)

where we used

\Phi_{q}^{-1}(\varepsilon)=\Theta\!\left(\varepsilon/\log(1/\varepsilon)\right)
as

\varepsilon\downarrow 0
. Thus the
\log\log(1/\varepsilon)
term comes from the
small-error inversion of the Fano envelope, while the finite-size
multi-hypothesis discrimination prefactor contributes only the displayed

\log[2q(q-1)]
, which is absorbed into
\mathcal{O}(1)
.
For the regular-model converse, if
\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon
, then the record term alone gives

H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon
, hence record regularity forces

P_{e}^{\star}(F)\leq r_{-}(\varepsilon)
. Therefore

C_{\rm rec}(F)+o(C_{\rm rec}(F))\geq\log\frac{1}{r_{-}(\varepsilon)}.

(S45)

If the record exponent remains below this scale, no measurement on
F
can
supply the required pointer record.

S2.2
Residual-coherence edge

The upper edge concerns the discord term in Eq. (
8
). Define

\lambda_{\overline{F}}=\max_{x\neq x^{\prime}}{}^{\prime}|\Gamma_{\overline{F}}^{xx^{\prime}}|,\qquad R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})=\lambda_{\overline{F}}^{2},

(S46)

where the prime again restricts to active coherences. Equation
(
S23
) gives

D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq g_{d_{S}}\!\left(C_{\rho}\lambda_{\overline{F}}\right)=g_{d_{S}}\!\left(C_{\rho}\sqrt{R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})}\right).

(S47)

Thus residual coherence tending to zero is sufficient for vanishing
pointer-basis discord:

R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\to 0.

(S48)

For the converse, set

\omega_{SF}=\Delta_{\Pi}^{S}(\rho^{0}_{SF}),\qquad X_{SF}=\rho^{0}_{SF}-\omega_{SF}.

(S49)

The additional nondegenerate active-coherence assumption used for the converse
means that the active diagonal blocks of
\omega_{SF}
are bounded away from the
singular directions on which
X_{SF}
has support. The entropy can therefore
be expanded around
\omega_{SF}
. For a full-rank state
\omega
and a
traceless perturbation
X
,

S(\omega+X)=S(\omega)-\Tr(X\log_{2}\omega)-\frac{1}{2\ln 2}\langle X,\Omega_{\omega}^{-1}(X)\rangle+\mathcal{O}(\|X\|_{1}^{3}),

(S50)

where
\Omega_{\omega}^{-1}
is the positive BKM inverse. Here the linear terms
vanish because
X_{SF}
and
X_{S}=\Tr_{F}X_{SF}
are off-diagonal in pointer
blocks, whereas
\log\omega_{SF}
and
\log\omega_{S}
are block diagonal:

\Tr(X_{SF}\log\omega_{SF})=\Tr(X_{S}\log\omega_{S})=0.

(S51)

Using
\rho^{0}_{F}=\omega_{F}
, the discord is

D_{\Pi}(S:F)=[S(\rho^{0}_{S})-S(\omega_{S})]-[S(\rho^{0}_{SF})-S(\omega_{SF})].

(S52)

Substituting Eq. (
S50
) gives

\displaystyle D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\ln 2}\left[\langle X_{SF},\Omega_{\omega_{SF}}^{-1}(X_{SF})\rangle-\langle X_{S},\Omega_{\omega_{S}}^{-1}(X_{S})\rangle\right]+\mathcal{O}(\|X_{SF}\|_{1}^{3}).

(S53)

The square bracket is nonnegative by monotonicity of the BKM metric under partial
trace. The nondegeneracy condition makes it strictly positive on the active
coherence directions. Hence, for sufficiently small residual coherence, there
exist constants
c_{-},c_{+}>0
, independent of the fragment size, such that

c_{-}R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\leq D_{\Pi}(S:F)_{\rho^{0}}\leq c_{+}R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})+\mathcal{O}\!\left(R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})^{3/2}\right).

(S54)

Without full-rank regularity the upper side may be replaced by the continuity
bound in Eq. (
S47
); the lower side is the converse needed for
the upper edge. If
D_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon
, then

R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\leq\varepsilon/c_{-}
, so

C_{\rm dec}(\overline{F}):=-\log R_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\geq\log\frac{c_{-}}{\varepsilon}.

(S55)

Conversely, the upper side of Eq. (
S54
) gives a
matching sufficient logarithmic scale. For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
, there is
a fragment-independent constant
c_{+}^{\prime}>0
such that

C_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\geq\log\frac{c_{+}^{\prime}}{\varepsilon}=\log\frac{1}{\varepsilon}+\log c_{+}^{\prime}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad D_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\varepsilon.

(S56)

Without the nondegenerate quadratic expansion, the continuity estimate
Eq. (
S47
) gives the weaker but still logarithmic sufficient
condition

C_{\rm dec}(\overline{F})\geq 2\log\frac{C_{\rho}}{g_{d_{S}}^{-1}(\varepsilon)}.

(S57)

Combining the record threshold in Eq. (
S44
) with the
surplus-dephasing threshold in Eq. (
S56
) proves the
sufficiency direction of Theorem
1
. Under the additional
regularity assumptions used above, the two converse arguments show that an

\varepsilon
-accurate strong-QD witness lies inside the same two-edge window up to
the finite-alphabet, Chernoff-regularity, and active-coherence constants displayed
above.

S2.3
Exactly solvable finite model used for Fig. 1

This section specifies the exactly solvable finite model used to generate
Fig. 1 of the main text. The purpose of the figure is only to visualize the
two competing requirements for a certified strong-QD witness: the observed
fragment has to carry an accessible pointer record, while the unobserved
complement has to decohere the pointer.

We consider a binary pointer with equal priors,

p_{+}=p_{-}=\frac{1}{2},\qquad H_{\Pi}=1,

(S58)

and an environment of
N
identical pure probes. The global branching state is

|\Psi\rangle_{SE}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left(|+\rangle_{S}|e_{+}\rangle^{\otimes N}+|-\rangle_{S}|e_{-}\rangle^{\otimes N}\right),

(S59)

where the single-probe conditional overlap is

s=|\langle e_{+}|e_{-}\rangle|,\qquad 0<s<1.

(S60)

For a fragment
F
containing
m
probes, the conditional fragment states are

\rho_{F|\pm}=|e_{\pm}\rangle\langle e_{\pm}|^{\otimes m},

(S61)

so their overlap is
s^{m}
. The unobserved complement
\bar{F}
contains

N-m
probes and has conditional overlap
s^{N-m}
.

Throughout this subsection entropies are measured in bits, and

h_{2}(q)=-q\log_{2}q-(1-q)\log_{2}(1-q)

(S62)

denotes the binary entropy. The mixture of two equiprobable pure states with
overlap
r
has nonzero eigenvalues

\lambda_{\pm}(r)=\frac{1\pm r}{2}.

(S63)

Therefore the Holevo record in a fragment of size
m
is

\chi_{\Pi}(m)=S\!\left[\frac{1}{2}\rho_{F|+}+\frac{1}{2}\rho_{F|-}\right]=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{m}}{2}\right).

(S64)

The reduced entropy of the pointer is determined by the full-environment
overlap
s^{N}
,

S(\rho_{S})=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N}}{2}\right),

(S65)

while the entropy of
SF
is, by purity of the global state, the entropy of
the complement
\bar{F}
,

S(\rho_{SF})=S(\rho_{\bar{F}})=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N-m}}{2}\right).

(S66)

The pointer-basis discord contribution used in the main text is therefore

D_{\Pi}(m)=I(S:F)-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)=S(\rho_{S})-S(\rho_{SF})=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N}}{2}\right)-h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N-m}}{2}\right).

(S67)

The strong-QD deficit plotted in Fig. 1 is

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(m)=H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(m)+D_{\Pi}(m),

(S68)

that is,

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(m)=1-h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{m}}{2}\right)+h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N}}{2}\right)-h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+s^{N-m}}{2}\right).

(S69)

The horizontal axis in Fig. 1 is the fragment fraction

f=\frac{m}{N},\qquad m=0,1,\ldots,N.

(S70)

For the displayed figure we used

N=80,\qquad s=0.78.

(S71)

The shaded observer window is the set of fragment sizes satisfying

\frac{\Delta_{\rm SQD}(m)}{H_{\Pi}}<0.05.

(S72)

Since
H_{\Pi}=1
in this binary equal-prior example, this condition is simply

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(m)<0.05.

(S73)

This finite-product construction is not an additional assumption in the
general theorems. It is only a minimal exactly solvable instance used to draw
the schematic window in Fig. 1. In this model the left edge of the window is
controlled by the decay of the record error, equivalently by the growth of

\chi_{\Pi}(m)
, while the right edge is controlled by the decay of residual
coherence in the complement, equivalently by the growth of
D_{\Pi}(m)
as

m
approaches
N
.

S3.
Asymmetry bound and achievable redundancy capacity

This appendix proves the three claims in Theorem
2
:
the single-fragment asymmetry bound, the global redundancy converse, and the
i.i.d. achievability statement.

S3.1
Single-fragment asymmetry bound

For the Abelian phase monitor of Theorem
2
,

U_{F,x}=\exp\!\left[-\mathrm{i}\sum_{k\in F}\sum_{\mu=1}^{r_{G}}\theta_{k,x}^{\mu}G_{k}^{\mu}\right],

(S74)

and

\sigma_{F|x}=U_{F,x}\sigma_{F}U_{F,x}^{\dagger}.

(S75)

All conditional states have the same entropy. Therefore the pointer Holevo
information is

\displaystyle\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)

\displaystyle=S\!\left(\sum_{x}p_{x}\sigma_{F|x}\right)-\sum_{x}p_{x}S(\sigma_{F|x})

\displaystyle=S(\bar{\sigma}_{F})-S(\sigma_{F}),

(S76)

where

\bar{\sigma}_{F}=\sum_{x}p_{x}U_{F,x}\sigma_{F}U_{F,x}^{\dagger}.

(S77)

Let

\bm{G}_{F}=\{G_{k}^{\mu}:k\in F,\ \mu=1,\ldots,r_{G}\}
, and let

\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}
be the pinching onto the joint eigenspaces of this
commuting local-generator family. If
P_{\bm{g}_{F}}
is one of these
joint eigenspace projectors, then

U_{F,x}P_{\bm{g}_{F}}=\exp[-\mathrm{i}\Phi_{x}(\bm{g}_{F})]P_{\bm{g}_{F}},\qquad\Phi_{x}(\bm{g}_{F})=\sum_{k\in F}\sum_{\mu=1}^{r_{G}}\theta_{k,x}^{\mu}g_{k}^{\mu}.

(S78)

Therefore the phase cancels inside each pinched block,

P_{\bm{g}_{F}}U_{F,x}\sigma_{F}U_{F,x}^{\dagger}P_{\bm{g}_{F}}=P_{\bm{g}_{F}}\sigma_{F}P_{\bm{g}_{F}}
, and hence

\displaystyle\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\bar{\sigma}_{F})

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p_{x}\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}\!\left(U_{F,x}\sigma_{F}U_{F,x}^{\dagger}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x}p_{x}\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\sigma_{F})=\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\sigma_{F}).

(S79)

Pinching cannot decrease entropy, so

S(\bar{\sigma}_{F})\leq S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}\bar{\sigma}_{F})=S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}\sigma_{F}).

(S80)

Substituting this into Eq. (
S76
) gives

\displaystyle\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)

\displaystyle\leq S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F}}\sigma_{F})-S(\sigma_{F})

\displaystyle=A_{\bm{G}_{F}}(\sigma_{F}),

(S81)

which is Eq. (
23
). The local-family pinching is essential:
coarse dephasing by the total charge
G_{F}
alone can leave coherences inside
degenerate total-charge sectors and therefore is not a stable resource account
for disjoint local records.

S3.2
Redundancy converse

Let
F_{1},\ldots,F_{R}
be disjoint fragments. Applying
Eq. (
S81
) to each fragment gives

\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\leq\sum_{i=1}^{R}A_{\bm{G}_{F_{i}}}(\sigma_{F_{i}}).

(S82)

Let
F_{\rm all}=\cup_{i}F_{i}
. The joint local-generator family on

F_{\rm all}
is
\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}=\{G_{k}^{\mu}:k\in F_{\rm all},\ \mu=1,\ldots,r_{G}\}
, and

\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}}
acts locally across the disjoint
fragments. A direct subtraction gives

\displaystyle A_{\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}}(\sigma_{F_{\rm all}})-\sum_{i=1}^{R}A_{\bm{G}_{F_{i}}}(\sigma_{F_{i}})

\displaystyle\quad=\left[\sum_{i}S(\sigma_{F_{i}})-S(\sigma_{F_{\rm all}})\right]-\left[\sum_{i}S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F_{i}}}\sigma_{F_{i}})-S(\Delta_{\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}}\sigma_{F_{\rm all}})\right].

(S83)

The first bracket is the total mutual information among the fragments before
local-generator dephasing; the second bracket is the same quantity after the
local dephasing channel. Local quantum channels cannot increase total mutual
information, so

A_{\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}}(\sigma_{F_{\rm all}})\geq\sum_{i=1}^{R}A_{\bm{G}_{F_{i}}}(\sigma_{F_{i}}).

(S84)

Partial trace is covariant with respect to the local phase symmetry and cannot
increase the relative entropy of asymmetry. Therefore

A_{\bm{G}_{F_{\rm all}}}(\sigma_{F_{\rm all}})\leq A_{\bm{G}_{E}}(\sigma_{E}),\qquad\bm{G}_{E}=\{G_{k}^{\mu}:k=1,\ldots,N,\ \mu=1,\ldots,r_{G}\}.

(S85)

Combining Eqs. (
S82
),
(
S84
), and (
S85
),

\sum_{i=1}^{R}\chi_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\leq A_{\bm{G}_{E}}(\sigma_{E}).

(S86)

If every counted fragment is a
\delta
-accurate strong-QD witness,

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F_{i})\leq\delta,

(S87)

then Eq. (
8
) implies

\chi_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\geq H_{\Pi}-\delta.

(S88)

Consequently, for the maximum number
R_{\delta}(N)
of disjoint such fragments,

R_{\delta}(N)[H_{\Pi}-\delta]_{+}\leq A_{\bm{G}_{E}}(\sigma_{E}),

(S89)

which is the global redundancy converse. If the environmental input is a
product state,
\sigma_{E}=\bigotimes_{k=1}^{N}\sigma_{k}
, then the local-family
asymmetry is additive:

A_{\bm{G}_{E}}(\sigma_{E})=\sum_{k=1}^{N}A_{\bm{G}_{k}}(\sigma_{k}).

(S90)

This gives Eq. (
24
).

S3.3
Achievability and capacity

Assume a homogeneous i.i.d. exact monitoring stream,

\sigma_{E}=\sigma^{\otimes N}
, and a positive surplus-dephasing exponent for
the complement of every fixed-size block. Equivalently, for each fixed block
size
m
there are constants
b_{m},c_{m}>0
such that, for every block

|F|=m
,

R_{\rm dec}(E\setminus F)\leq b_{m}e^{-c_{m}(N-m)}.

(S91)

Let
\chi_{m}
be the pointer Holevo information of any block of
m
carriers,
and define

m_{\rm rec}(\eta)=\min\{m\geq 1:\chi_{m}\geq H_{\Pi}-\eta\}.

(S92)

The converse is a packing bound. If
F_{i}
is counted by
R_{\delta}(N)
, then
Eq. (
S88
) gives

\chi_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\geq H_{\Pi}-\delta
. By the i.i.d. definition of

m_{\rm rec}(\delta)
, the fragment must contain at least
m_{\rm rec}(\delta)
carriers.
Disjointness therefore implies

R_{\delta}(N)\leq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{m_{\rm rec}(\delta)}\right\rfloor,

(S93)

and hence

\limsup_{N\to\infty}\frac{R_{\delta}(N)}{N}\leq\frac{1}{m_{\rm rec}(\delta)}.

(S94)

For achievability, fix
0<\eta<\delta
and partition the first

\lfloor N/m_{\rm rec}(\eta)\rfloor m_{\rm rec}(\eta)
carriers into consecutive blocks of
size
m_{\rm rec}(\eta)
, ignoring the leftover carriers. Each block satisfies

H_{\Pi}-\chi_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\leq\eta.

(S95)

Its complement contains
N-m_{\rm rec}(\eta)
carriers, so Eq. (
S91
)
and the continuity bound (
S47
) give

D_{\Pi}(S:F_{i})\to 0\qquad(N\to\infty)

(S96)

uniformly over the blocks in the partition. Therefore, for all sufficiently
large
N
,

\Delta_{\rm SQD}(S:F_{i})\leq\delta

(S97)

for every block in the partition, and hence

R_{\delta}(N)\geq\left\lfloor\frac{N}{m_{\rm rec}(\eta)}\right\rfloor\qquad(N\ \text{sufficiently large}).

(S98)

Taking
N\to\infty
gives

\liminf_{N\to\infty}\frac{R_{\delta}(N)}{N}\geq\frac{1}{m_{\rm rec}(\eta)}.

(S99)

Together, Eqs. (
S94
) and (
S99
)
give the sandwich bound for the capacity density. If

m_{\rm rec}
is continuous at
\delta
as an integer-valued step function, then
letting
\eta\uparrow\delta
yields

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\frac{R_{\delta}(N)}{N}=\frac{1}{m_{\rm rec}(\delta)}.

(S100)

At jump points one retains the one-sided liminf–limsup statement; the residual
mismatch is exactly the integer rounding of the minimal detector block size.

Finally, suppose the controlled-phase orbit converts local asymmetry into pointer
Holevo information without asymptotic loss,

\chi_{m}=\min\{H_{\Pi},mA_{\bm{G}}(\sigma)\}

(S101)

up to vanishing finite-size tolerances, where
\bm{G}
is the single-carrier generator family. Then

m_{\rm rec}(\delta)=\left\lceil\frac{H_{\Pi}-\delta}{A_{\bm{G}}(\sigma)}\right\rceil,

(S102)

and

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta)=\left[\left\lceil\frac{H_{\Pi}-\delta}{A_{\bm{G}}(\sigma)}\right\rceil\right]^{-1}.

(S103)

Thus

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta)\leq\frac{A_{\bm{G}}(\sigma)}{H_{\Pi}-\delta},

(S104)

and asymmetry-saturating monitors attain this converse up to the integer block-size
rounding in Eq. (
S103
).

S4.
Tilted thermal spin-phase bath

This appendix derives Eqs. (
29
)–(
36
).
The example is a repeated-collision spin bath: the carrier state is prepared by
a tilted thermal Hamiltonian, and the monitoring collision itself is the Abelian
phase kick in Eq. (
28
). This keeps the model inside
the controlled-product assumptions of Theorem
1
and the
Abelian phase assumptions of Theorem
2
.

Let

\bm{n}_{0}=(\sin\vartheta,0,\cos\vartheta),\qquad\sigma=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+r\bm{n}_{0}\cdot\bm{\tau}),\qquad r=\tanh\frac{\beta\Omega}{2}.

(S105)

When the temperature dependence is emphasized, we write this one-carrier state as

\sigma_{T}
. The conditional phase rotations

U_{\pm}=\exp(\mp\mathrm{i}\phi\tau_{z}/2)
send the Bloch direction to

\bm{n}_{\pm}=(\sin\vartheta\cos\phi,\ \pm\sin\vartheta\sin\phi,\ \cos\vartheta),\qquad\rho_{\pm}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+r\bm{n}_{\pm}\cdot\bm{\tau}).

(S106)

The inhomogeneous case is obtained by adding a carrier index to

r,\vartheta,\phi
.

S4.1
Residual coherence

For the binary pointer, the single-carrier contribution to the off-diagonal
system block is

\displaystyle\gamma

\displaystyle=\Tr(U_{+}\sigma U_{-}^{\dagger})=\Tr(\sigma e^{-\mathrm{i}\phi\tau_{z}})

\displaystyle=\Tr\!\left[\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+r\sin\vartheta\,\tau_{x}+r\cos\vartheta\,\tau_{z})(\cos\phi\,\mathbb{I}-\mathrm{i}\sin\phi\,\tau_{z})\right]

\displaystyle=\cos\phi-\mathrm{i}r\cos\vartheta\sin\phi.

(S107)

Therefore

|\gamma|^{2}=\cos^{2}\phi+r^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\phi,\qquad c_{\rm dec}=-\log|\gamma|^{2}.

(S108)

For a complement
\bar{F}
, the product form gives

|\Gamma_{\bar{F}}|^{2}=\prod_{k\in\bar{F}}|\gamma_{k}|^{2},\qquad C_{\rm dec}(\bar{F})=\sum_{k\in\bar{F}}c_{{\rm dec},k}.

(S109)

This is the surplus-dephasing edge associated with Eq. (
29
).

S4.2
Record Chernoff exponent

The two conditional states have the same eigenvalues

\lambda_{\pm}=(1\pm r)/2
. For equal-spectrum qubit states related by a
reflection
\phi\mapsto-\phi
, the Chernoff function is symmetric in

s\leftrightarrow 1-s
and is minimized at
s=1/2
. Write

\sqrt{\rho_{\pm}}=a\mathbb{I}+b\bm{n}_{\pm}\cdot\bm{\tau}
, where

a^{2}=\frac{1+\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}{4},\qquad b^{2}=\frac{1-\sqrt{1-r^{2}}}{4}.

(S110)

Then

\displaystyle q

\displaystyle=\inf_{0\leq s\leq 1}\Tr(\rho_{+}^{s}\rho_{-}^{1-s})=\Tr(\sqrt{\rho_{+}}\sqrt{\rho_{-}})

\displaystyle=2(a^{2}+b^{2}\bm{n}_{+}\cdot\bm{n}_{-})=1-\left(1-\sqrt{1-r^{2}}\right)\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\phi.

(S111)

Thus
c_{\rm rec}=-\log q
, and for a product fragment

C_{\rm rec}(F)=\sum_{k\in F}c_{{\rm rec},k}.

(S112)

This is the record edge associated with Eq. (
30
).

S4.3
Local Abelian asymmetry and one-carrier Holevo information

The local generator is
G=\tau_{z}/2
. Pinching in its eigenbasis removes the
transverse Bloch component:

\Delta_{G}(\sigma)=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbb{I}+r\cos\vartheta\,\tau_{z}).

(S113)

Hence

A_{G}(\sigma)=S(\Delta_{G}\sigma)-S(\sigma)=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+r\cos\vartheta}{2}\right)-h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right).

(S114)

For equal pointer priors, the one-carrier average state is

\bar{\rho}_{1}=\frac{\rho_{+}+\rho_{-}}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\mathbb{I}+r(\sin\vartheta\cos\phi\,\tau_{x}+\cos\vartheta\,\tau_{z})\right],

(S115)

whose Bloch radius is

\bar{r}_{1}=r\sqrt{\cos^{2}\vartheta+\sin^{2}\vartheta\cos^{2}\phi}.

(S116)

Therefore

\chi_{1}=h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+\bar{r}_{1}}{2}\right)-h_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right)\leq A_{G}(\sigma),

(S117)

because
\bar{r}_{1}\geq|r\cos\vartheta|
and the binary entropy decreases as
the Bloch radius increases on
[0,1]
. Equality holds for a relative

\pi
-phase flip,
\phi=\pi/2
, where the conditional ensemble performs the
local dephasing twirl exactly. For arbitrary fragments, the general proof of
Theorem
2
gives

\chi_{\Pi}(S:F)\leq\sum_{k\in F}A_{G_{k}}(\sigma_{k}),

(S118)

which is the fragment form of the main-text bound in Eq. (
23
).

S4.4
Block Holevo information and redundancy capacity

For a homogeneous stream, the Holevo information in a block of
m
carriers is

\chi_{m}=S\!\left(\frac{\rho_{+}^{\otimes m}+\rho_{-}^{\otimes m}}{2}\right)-mh_{2}\!\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right).

(S119)

The numerical evaluation used for Fig.
2

is exact but low-dimensional. Since
\rho_{\pm}^{\otimes m}
are permutation
invariant, Schur-Weyl decomposition gives

\rho_{\pm}^{\otimes m}\cong\bigoplus_{j}\mathbb{I}_{\nu_{j}}\otimes\frac{\exp(2\eta\bm{n}_{\pm}\cdot\bm{J}_{j})}{(2\cosh\eta)^{m}},\qquad\eta=\operatorname{arctanh}r,

(S120)

where
\bm{J}_{j}
are spin-
j

\nu_{j}=\binom{m}{m/2-j}-\binom{m}{m/2-j-1}

(S121)

with invalid binomial coefficients set to zero. The largest block has dimension

m+1
, so evaluating
\chi_{m}
does not require diagonalizing a

2^{m}
-dimensional matrix.

Given a target deficit
\delta
, the operational record length is

m_{\rm rec}(\delta;T)=\min\{m:\chi_{m}(T)\geq 1-\delta\}.

(S122)

The achievability part of Theorem
2
partitions a long
stream into blocks of this size, while Theorem
1
requires
the remaining complement to supply surplus dephasing. Therefore, at continuity
points of
m_{\rm rec}

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta;T)=\frac{1}{m_{\rm rec}(\delta;T)}.

(S123)

The asymmetry converse gives the density bound

\mathcal{C}_{\rm red}(\delta;T)\leq\frac{A_{G}(\sigma_{T})}{1-\delta},

(S124)

which is the upper curve in Fig.
2
.

S4.5
High-temperature scaling and certified window

For

,

r=\tanh\frac{\beta\Omega}{2}\simeq\frac{\Omega}{2k_{B}T}.

(S125)

Expanding Eqs. (
S108

S111

(
S114

\displaystyle c_{\rm rec}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{r^{2}}{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\phi,

\displaystyle A_{G}(\sigma)

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{r^{2}\sin^{2}\vartheta}{2\ln 2},

\displaystyle c_{\rm dec}

\displaystyle=-\log\left(\cos^{2}\phi+r^{2}\cos^{2}\vartheta\sin^{2}\phi\right)\longrightarrow-\log\cos^{2}\phi.

(S126)

Thus the record exponent and the witness capacity are suppressed as

while the dephasing exponent remains finite whenever

(S128)

which is the finite-temperature specialization of Eq. (
