Action accessibility in the variety of skew braces Note: This work was partially supported by the Dipartimento di Matematica e Fisica “Ennio De Giorgi” - Università del Salento. The authors are members of GNSAGA (INdAM).
Andrea ALBANO, Paola STEFANELLI

Abstract
In this paper, we answer negatively to a question posed in the context of the Oberwolfach Mini-Workshop “The Yang–Baxter Equation and Representations of Braid Groups” [ 22 ] regarding the existence of split extensions classifiers in the category of skew braces. To this end, we show that the variety of skew braces is not action accessible by investigating the categorical notion of centraliser in the sense of Huq [ 31 ] . In light of their intrinsic relationship with skew braces, an analogous result is proven for the variety of post-Lie algebras over a fixed field.

Introduction

Skew brace theory finds its roots in early foundational works
[
25
,
35
,
43
]
related to the investigation of non-degenerate solutions to the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation
[
24
]
.
These structures first arose in a commutative setting in
[
40
]
as a generalization of Jacobson radical rings and were later extended in
[
30
]
.
Following the formulation given in
[
19
,
30
]
, a skew (left) brace is a set
B
equipped with two group operations
+
and
\circ
satisfying the following identity

\displaystyle\forall\,a,b,c\in B\qquad a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c.

Despite its evidently group theoretical flavour, the study of such structures has deeply benefited from categorical and universal algebraic considerations.
For instance, in
[
10
]
the authors employed the Freese–McKenzie commutator theory to obtain a natural notion of (central) nilpotency that turned out to be stronger than previous definitions already present in the literature, see
[
40
,
42
]
.
In a similar vein, protomodular aspects of the category
Skb
of skew braces were investigated by the authors of
[
12
]
who proved that the so-called
Smith is Huq
condition holds in
Skb
and provided a set of generators for the commutator of two ideals in a skew brace.
These results have been the basis for further studies as found, for example, in

[
8
]
, where a systematic investigation of central nilpotency was carried out, and in

[
7
]
where a novel definition of solvability for skew braces was formulated.

Hopf-theoretic methods have also had an important role in enriching the theory of skew braces, finding their natural setting in Hopf braces, introduced in
[
3
]
and
for which similar considerations as above hold,
see
[
2
,
29
]
and references therein.
A particular example of this interaction, inspired by the notion of a module over a Hopf brace
[
49
]
, is the representation theory of skew braces.
The latter was introduced in
[
34
]
and has received attention especially in connection with the representation theory of groups, for example see
[
33
,
38
]
.

The main motivation that drives the current work is the necessity of investigating the measure in which the representation theory of skew braces can be put in a categorical-algebraic framework.
As one would expect,
a representation of a skew brace
B
should be equivalent, if possible, to the datum of a natural skew brace of linear automorphisms
S(V)\subseteq\text{GL}_{k}(V)
acting naturally on a
k
-vector space
V
through a skew brace homomorphism
B\xrightarrow{\,}S(V)
.
Here, naturality refers to the fact that there should exist a bijective correspondence between morphisms
B\xrightarrow{\,}S(V)
as above and split short exact sequences of skew braces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces V}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{V\rtimes B}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces B}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 0}
.
In light of the semi-abelianity of
Skb
, these considerations lead us to address the question of whether
action representability
holds in this category, in the sense of
[
11
]
.
It is indeed through this lens that we interpret a question posed during the “The Yang–Baxter Equation and Representations of Braid Groups”
[
22
, p. 2635]
, which asked

“Does there exist an internal Aut-object
[X]
in the category of skew braces that classifies split extensions (or actions) via a universal property?”,

where
[X]
is
S(V)
as above.
We respond negatively to this question precisely by verifying that
Skb
is not action accessible, thus not action representable, showing in this way that the above heuristic for skew brace representations is not valid.

The paper is divided into three sections.
The first section contains a brief introduction to semi-abelianity and its characterisation for varieties in the sense of Universal Algebra, along with a concise overview on the relationship between action representability and action accessibility.
The second section collects basic facts about skew braces that are needed to prove our main result
2.13
.
The third and last section can be read independently from the second and contains an analogous result for the category of post-Lie algebras.
The latter were introduced in
[
47
]
and have found fruitful applications in numerical integration
[
23
]
as well as in the study of the Yang–Baxter equation, thanks to the fact that they are the infinitesimal objects associated with (smooth) skew braces, in the sense of
[
5
,
45
]
.

1
Semi-abelian varieties

In the present section, we state the definition of a semi-abelian variety in the sense of Universal Algebra following its characterisation given in
[
13
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Moreover, even though our interest lies in this particular context, we set up the language and notions that are necessary for the rest of the paper, providing appropriate statements for general semi-abelian categories whenever possible.
For a comprehensive treatment, we refer the reader to

[
32
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A pointed category
\mathcal{C}
with finite products and finite coproducts is
semi-abelian
if
\mathcal{C}
is Barr-exact and Bourn-protomodular.

A category
\mathcal{C}
is Barr-exact if it is regular and all its equivalence relations arise from a kernel pair.
Moreover, as shown in
[
32
]
, under the condition that a Barr-exact category
\mathcal{C}
has a zero object, Bourn-protomodularity is equivalent to the validity of the so-called split short five lemma.
Moreover, if
\mathcal{C}
further admits all finite coproducts (sums), then there is an equivalence between the split short five lemma and the following property: for all split short exact sequences

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces E}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces k}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\varepsilon}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces s}

the sum
k+s:X+A\xrightarrow{\,}E
is an epimorphism.
Exactness of the above sequence refers to the fact that
k=\text{Ker}\,\varepsilon
and
\varepsilon:E\xrightarrow{\,}A
is a regular epimorphism, i.e. the coequalizer of a pair of parallel morphisms.

When dealing with a variety
\mathcal{V}
in the sense of Universal Algebra, to verify that
\mathcal{V}
is semi-abelian it is enough to prove that
\mathcal{V}
admits a sufficient number of
terms
satisfying certain properties.
In general, consider a set
\mathcal{F}
of basic operation symbols for some universal algebra, each with its corresponding arity.
Let
X
be a set of
variables
that can be assumed to be disjoint from
\mathcal{F}
.

The set of
terms of type
\mathcal{F}
over
X
is the smallest set
T(X)
containing
X
as well as the constants in
\mathcal{F}
and such that, if
\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\in T(X)
and
\omega\in\mathcal{F}
is an
n
-ary function symbol, then the string
\omega(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
is in
T(X)
.
For each term
\alpha\in T(X)
, it is possible to assign an arity
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, where
n
is the number of variables that occur explicitly in
\alpha
.
For example, if
\mathcal{F}=\{+\}
then
x
,
x+y
and
x+(x+y)
are terms of type
\mathcal{F}
over
X=\{x,y\}
.

Definition 1.2

(cf. Theorem 1.1 in
[
13
]
)
.

A variety
\mathcal{V}
is
semi-abelian
if and only if it admits a unique constant
0
, a set of binary terms
t_{1},\ldots,t_{n}
for some
n\in\mathbb{N}
, together with an
(n+1)
-ary term
t
such that the identities

\displaystyle t_{i}(x,x)=0\qquad\text{and}\qquad t\left(x,t_{1}(x,y),\,\ldots,t_{n}(x,y)\right)=y\,,

hold in
V
, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.

Example 1.3
.

Many classical examples of varieties such as groups and Lie algebras form semi-abelian varieties.
Reflecting our interest, the variety
Skb
of skew braces is also semi-abelian.
This fact was already noted in
[
26
]
and can be directly verified through
1.2
by considering the terms

t_{1}(x,y):=-x+y\qquad\text{and}\qquad t(x,y):=x+y

in
Skb
together with its unique constant
0
.

\mathcal{C}
be a semi-abelian category and let
A,X
be objects of
\mathcal{C}
.
In

internal action
of

\Act(\boldsymbol{-},X):\mathcal{C}^{\text{op}}\xrightarrow{\,}\mathbf{Set}

Let us now turn our attention to

[
11
, Definition 3.5]
.

Definition 1.5
.

A semi-abelian category
\mathcal{C}
has
representable internal actions
if, for all objects
X\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists an object
T(X)\in\mathcal{C}
and a natural isomorphism

\displaystyle\eta_{X}:\Split(\boldsymbol{-}\,,X)\xrightarrow{\sim}\mathcal{C}(\boldsymbol{-}\,,T(X))\,.

(1.1)

A semi-abelian category that has representable internal actions is said to be
action representable
.
If
X
is an object of such a category, then the object
T(X)
is called the
actor
of
X
while a morphism
B\xrightarrow{\,}T(X)
in the image
\eta_{X}(B)
takes the name of
acting morphism
, for all objects
B
.

Action representable categories belong to a wider class of “well-behaved” categories satisfying the so-called notion of
action accessibility
, as defined in
[
14
]
and which we state in what follows.

Before doing this, given an object
X
in a semiabelian category
\mathcal{C}
, let us denote with
\Split(X)
the category of (isomorphism classes of) split extensions in
\mathcal{C}
with kernel
X
(in the sense of
1.5
).
Recall that an object
\Sigma
in
\Split(X)
is called
faithful
if all objects in
\Split(X)
admit at most one morphism into
\Sigma
.
Moreover, an object in
\Split(X)
is
accessible
if it admits a morphism into a faithful object of
\Split(X)
, i.e. into a
\Sigma
as above.
For more details we refer the reader to
[
14
]
.

Definition 1.6
.

A a semi-abelian category is called
action accessible
if every object of
\Split(X)
is accessible, for all
X\in\mathcal{C}
.

Remark 1.7
.

A semi-abelian, action representable category
\mathcal{C}
is action accessible as a consequence of

[
11
, Theorem 6.3]
.
Indeed, in its terms, given an object
X\in\mathcal{C}
we obtain a natural faithful object in
\Split(X)
as the split extension

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\rtimes T(X)}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{T(X)}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mu}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces p}

where
T(X)
is the actor of
X
while
\mu
,
p
and the section
T(X)\xrightarrow{\,}X\rtimes T(X)
are appropriate canonical morphisms.

Action accessibility is a property
inherited by the Birkhoff subcategories of an action accessible category, as shown by
[
14
, Proposition 2.3]
.
In particular, if we restrict the interest in varieties in the sense of Universal Algebra, then we can state the aforementioned result in the following manner.

Theorem 1.8
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is a semi-abelian and action accessible variety, then all subvarieties of
\mathcal{V}
are action accessible.

2
Action accessibility of skew braces

The aim of this section is to show that the variety
Skb
of skew braces is not action accessible and therefore not action representable.
We will proceed by first stating some basic properties of skew braces and then by recalling the notion of
centraliser
of a subobject in a semi-abelian category, essentially due to
[
31
]
.
Our main argument relies on
[
21
, Corollary 2.6]
and will be laid out by providing an example of a skew brace admitting a proper ideal with a non-normal centraliser.

2.1
Basics on skew braces

This brief subsection introduces fundamental notions and fixes notation related to skew braces.
We refer the reader to
[
20
,
30
]
for further details.

Definition 2.1
.

A skew (left) brace is a set
B
equipped with two group operations
+
and
\circ
satisfying the following identity:

\displaystyle a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b-a+a\circ c\,,

(2.1)

for all
a,b,c\in B
.

It is a routine computation to verify that the identity of the multiplicative group
(B,\circ)
coincides with the identity
0
of the additive group
(B,+)
. As a consequence, the equality (
2.1
) can also be written as

\displaystyle a\circ(b+c)=a\circ b+a\circ(a^{-}+c),

(2.2)

for all
a,b,c\in B
, where by
a^{-}
we denote the inverse of
a
with respect to the operation
\circ
, for all
a\in B
.

Note that, if
\left(B,+\right)
is a group, then
\left(B,+,+\right)
and
\left(B,+,+^{op}\right)
are skew braces that are called the
trivial
and the
almost trivial skew brace
on the group
\left(B,+\right)
, respectively.
The first proper examples can be found in
[
30
]
and
[
41
]
.
Moreover, for explicit classifications of skew braces of small orders, the reader is referred to
[
9
]
, while a wide variety of techniques for structural constructions has been extensively developed throughout the literature.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace and, for all
a\in B
, define a map
\lambda_{a}:B\xrightarrow{\,}B
by setting
\lambda_{a}(b)=-a+a\circ b
, for all
b\in B
. The importance of such maps lies in the following basic properties listed below.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. Then the following hold:

(1)

\lambda_{a}\in\Aut(B,+)
, for all
a\in B
;

(2)

the map
\lambda:(B,\circ)\xrightarrow{\,}\Aut(B,+),\,a\mapsto\lambda_{a}
is a group homomorphism.

Since the class of all skew braces form a variety, usual notions from Universal Algebra apply in this context.
For instance, a sub-skewbrace of a skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is an additive subgroup
I\leq(B,+)
which is closed under the
\circ
operation.

Definition 2.3
.

An additive subgroup
I
of a skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is said to be:

(1)

a
left ideal
if
I
is
\lambda
-invariant, i.e. such that
\lambda_{a}(I)\subseteq I
, for all
a\in B
;

(2)

an
ideal
of
B
if
I
is a left ideal such that
(I,+)
is a normal subgroup of
(B,+)
and
(I,\circ)
is a normal subgroup of
(B,\circ)
.

Consequently, if
I
is an ideal of
B
then there is a well-defined map

\displaystyle\lambda^{I}:(B,\circ)\xrightarrow{\,}\Aut(I,+)

defined by restricting
\lambda_{b}
on
I
, i.e., by setting
\lambda^{I}(b):=(\lambda_{b})_{\mid_{I}}
, for all
b\in B
.
One can easily check that the equivalence

\displaystyle b\in\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}\ \iff\ \forall\ x\in I\quad b\circ x=b+x\,

is satisfied.

As it is clear from the first definition of this section, it is possible to develop an analogous theory for skew
right braces
in which an identity dual to (
2.1
) holds.
In particular, a natural class of skew braces that arises through this elementary consideration is that of
two-sided
skew braces.

Definition 2.4
.

A skew brace
(B,+,\circ)
is
two-sided
if the identity

\displaystyle(a+b)\circ c=a\circ c-c+b\circ c

(2.3)

holds, for all
a,b,c\in B
.

Note that if
R
is a Jacobson radical ring, then the structure
(R,+,\circ)
is a two-sided brace, where
\circ
is the classical
adjoint operation
defined by
a\circ b:=ab-a-b
, for all
a,b\in R
.
Two-sided skew braces and their structure were investigated in different works such as
[
37
,
44
,
46
]
and have often proven to be an optimal ground to address important brace-theoretic questions.
Moreover, in
[
36
]
the authors introduced an obstruction for two-sidedness in the wider class of dual weak braces and linked it to the study of solutions to the set-theoretic Yang–Baxter equation.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a two-sided skew brace.
For all
a\in B
, let
\sigma_{a}:B\xrightarrow{\,}B
the map defined by setting

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}(b):=b\circ a-a\,,

for all
b\in B
.
It is straightforward to verify that
\sigma_{a}\in\Aut(B,+)
as well as the fact that
\sigma:(B,\circ)\xrightarrow{\,}\Aut(B,+)\,,a\mapsto\sigma_{a}

is a group anti-homomorphism.

Remark 2.5
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace and consider an additively and multiplicatively normal subgroup of
B
.
Then
I
is an ideal of
B
if and only if it is
\sigma
-invariant in the sense that
\sigma_{a}(I)\subseteq I
, for all
a\in B
.
This follows from the fact that

\displaystyle\sigma_{a}(x)=a+\lambda_{a}(a^{-}\circ x\circ a)-a\in I,\,,

for all

and

.

We finish this subsection by recording the following identities for later use.

Proposition 2.6
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a skew brace. Then the following identity

\displaystyle a+b=a\circ\lambda_{a}^{-1}(b)

(2.4)

holds, for all
a,b\in B
.
Moreover, if

is two-sided, then the identity

\displaystyle a+b=\sigma_{b}^{-1}(a)\circ b

.

2.2
Centralisers and action accessibility

Definition 2.7
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed category with binary products and let

be two morphisms in

We say that

and

making the following diagram commute:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{X\times Y}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces Y}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces A}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{\id_{X},\,0\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces f}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\psi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\{0,\id_{Y}\}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}

where

is the unique morphism into

\id_{X}
and
0_{X,Y}
factor through the projections

and

, respectively. Analogous considerations hold for

If

is unique and takes the name of the
cooperator
of

and
g
in
A
.

In what follows we are interested in a specialization of the above definition to sub-objects.
In detail, recall that for all objects
A
in a category

it is possible to define a partial order on the (class of) monomorphisms
\mu:X\rightarrowtail A
,
\nu:Y\rightarrowtail A
into
A
by setting
\mu\leq\nu
if there exists a morphism
\varphi:X\xrightarrow{\,}Y
such that
\mu=\nu\varphi
.
A sub-object of

is an equivalence class of monomorphisms
\mu,\nu
into
A
such that
\mu\leq\nu
and
\nu\leq\mu
.

In this context, if
X
and

are sub-objects of an object

in
\mathcal{C}
then we will say that
X
and
Y
cooperate if their respective inclusions
X\rightarrowtail A
,
Y\rightarrowtail A
cooperate in the sense of the above definition.

Definition 2.8
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
be a pointed category with finite products, let
A
be an object of
\mathcal{C}
and consider a sub-object
X
of
A
.
The largest sub-object of
A
cooperating with
X
, if it exists, is called the
centraliser
of
X
in
A
and is denoted by
Z_{A}(X)
.

Example 2.9
.

If
G
is a group then a sub-object of
G
is simply identified with the structure of a subgroup of
G
.
As a consequence, two subgroups
X,Y\leq G
cooperate in
G
if and only if
xy=yx
holds, for all
x\in X
and
y\in Y
.
In particular, the centraliser of
X
in
G
coincides with its usual group-theoretic centraliser.
Analogously, if
A
is a skew brace then a sub-object

of
A
is (the equivalence class of the inclusion of) a sub-skew brace of
A
.

The following result is of fundamental importance in our argument and justifies the use of centralisers.
For more details we refer the reader to
[
21
, Corollary 2.6]
.

Proposition 2.10
.

Let
A
be an object of a semi-abelian category
\mathcal{C}
and let
X
be a normal sub-object of
A
.
If
\mathcal{C}
is action accessible then
X
admits a centraliser in
\mathcal{C}
and the latter is normal.

Let us turn our attention back to skew braces.
As shown in
[
12
]
, a sub-skew brace
I
of a skew brace

is normal if and only if

is an ideal of

.
It is now important to characterise the cooperating sub-objects in

.
To this end, for a given skew brace

and one of its sub-skew braces

, we denote by

and

the centraliser of

in

in relation to the additive

Lemma 2.11
.

Let
B
be a skew brace and let
I
be an ideal of
B
.
The
centraliser of

in

is the largest sub-skew of

contained in the set

\displaystyle C(B,I):=C^{+}_{B}(I)\cap C^{\circ}_{B}(I)\cap\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}\,,

(2.6)

if the former exists.

Proof.

Let

be a
sub-skew brace of

and assume it cooperates with

To prove the assertion it is sufficient to verify that

.
To this aim, first note that

since
(J,+)
and

cooperate with
(I,+)
and
(J,\circ)
, respectively.
Consider now the cooperator

of

and

as in

and let

and

. Then we have

\displaystyle y\circ x=\psi\left((0,y)\circ(x,0)\right)=\psi(x,y)=\psi\left((0,y)+(x,0)\right)=y+x\,,

from which follows that

.
Therefore

, thus concluding the proof.
∎

Note that if

coincides with the

of

.
Despite this fact, it is worth remarking that the set

may generally fail to be a sub-skew brace, as the following example shows.

Example 2.12

Consider the skew brace

of

[

and obtained by setting

.
Explicitly,

\displaystyle(n,\,m)\circ(x,\,y)=\left(n+(-1)^{\frac{m(m-1)}{2}}x,\,m+(-1)^{m}y\right)

The annihilator

.
It is straightforward to verify that

\displaystyle C(B,I)=\{(0,0),(0,1),(1,0),(1,1),(2,0),(2,1)\}

.

.

The category

Proposition 2.16
.

Let
(B,+,\circ)
be a two-sided skew brace and

I
an ideal of
B
.
Then the set
C(B,I)
defined in (
2.6
) is an ideal of
B
.

Proof.

Let
a,b\in C(B,I)
and
x\in I
.
Naturally,
a-b\in C^{+}_{B}(I)
.
Moreover, we have that

\displaystyle(a-b)\circ x

\displaystyle=a\circ x-x+(-b)\circ x=a\circ x-x+x-b\circ x+x=a-b+x\,,

as well as

x\circ(a-b)=x\circ a-x+x\circ(-b)=a+x-x\circ b+x=a-b+x,

thus showing that
a-b\in C^{\circ}_{B}(I)
.
From the same calculations we deduce that

\lambda_{a-b}(x)=b-a+(a-b)\circ x=x,

hence that
a-b\in\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}
.
This shows that
C(B,I)
is an additive subgroup of
B
.

Now, to prove that
C(B,I)
is a multiplicative subgroup of
B
first note that

\displaystyle a\circ b^{-}+x

\displaystyle=a+\lambda_{a}(b^{-})+x=a+\lambda_{a}\big(b^{-}+\lambda^{-1}_{a}(x)\big)

\displaystyle=a+\lambda_{a}\big(\lambda^{-1}_{a}(x)+b^{-}\big)=a+x+\lambda_{a}(b^{-})

\displaystyle=x+a+\lambda_{a}(b^{-})=x+a\circ b^{-}\,,

where we used the fact that
\lambda_{a}^{-1}(x)\in I
.
Besides, since
\lambda_{a\circ b}(x)=x=\lambda_{a^{-}}(x)
and from the fact that
C^{\circ}_{B}(I)
is already closed under
\circ
, we thus deduce the same assertion for
C(B,I)
.
Therefore,
C(B,I)
is a sub-skew brace of
B
.
Let us now prove that
C(B,I)
is
\lambda
-invariant.
To this aim, let
h\in B
,
a\in C(B,I)
and first note that

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(a)+x=\lambda_{h}\left(a+\lambda_{a}^{-1}(x)\right)=\lambda_{h}\left(\lambda_{h}^{-1}(x)+a\right)=x+\lambda_{h}(a)\,,

holds, for all
x\in I
, thus showing that
\lambda_{h}(a)\in C^{+}_{B}(I)
.
Furthermore, we have that

\displaystyle\lambda_{h}(a)\circ x=h\circ\left(h^{-1}+a\right)\circ x=h\circ\left(h^{-1}\circ x-x+a\circ x\right)=x+\lambda_{h}(a)\,,

proving that
\lambda_{h}(a)\in\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}
.
Finally,

\displaystyle x\circ\lambda_{h}(a)

\displaystyle=x\circ h\circ\left(h^{-}+a\right)=h\circ\left(h^{-}\circ x\circ h\right)\circ\left(h^{-}+a\right)

\displaystyle=h\circ\left(y\circ h^{-}-y+y\circ a\right)\,,

where we set
y:=\left(h^{-}\circ x\circ h\right)\in I
. It follows that

\displaystyle x\circ\lambda_{h}(a)=h\circ\left(y\circ h^{-}+a\right)=x-h+h\circ a=x+\lambda_{h}(a)\,,

showing that
\lambda_{h}(a)\in C^{\circ}_{B}(I)
.
Therefore,
C(B,I)
is
\lambda
-invariant.

It remains to verify that
C(B,I)

is a normal subgroup of
(B,+)
and
(B,\circ)
, respectively.
To this end, let
h\in B
,
a\in C(B,I)
and first note that obviously
-h+a+h\in C^{+}_{B}(I)
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\left(-h+a+h\right)\circ x

\displaystyle=(-h)\circ x-x+a\circ x-x+h\circ x=x-h\circ x+a+h\circ x

\displaystyle=x-\lambda_{h}(x)-h+a+h+\lambda_{h}(x)=-h+a+h+x\,,

namely,
-h+a+h\in\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}
.
To show that
-h+a+h\in C^{\circ}_{B}(I)
it is sufficient to note that

\displaystyle x\circ(-h+a+h)

\displaystyle=x\circ(-h)-x+x\circ a-x+x\circ h=x-x\circ h+a+x\circ h

\displaystyle=x-h-\sigma_{h}(x)+a+\sigma_{h}(x)+h=x-h+a+h\,,

since, by
2.5
,
\sigma_{h}(x)\in I
.
Let us lastly check that
h^{-1}\circ a\circ h\in C(B,I)
.
Naturally,
h^{-1}\circ a\circ h
is already contained in
C^{\circ}_{B}(I)
. Furthermore, notice that

\displaystyle h^{-}\circ a\circ h+x

\displaystyle=h^{-}\circ a\circ h\circ\lambda_{h}^{-1}\lambda_{a}^{-1}\lambda_{h}(x)=h^{-}\circ a\circ h\circ x\,,

while,

\displaystyle x+h^{-}\circ a\circ h

\displaystyle=\sigma_{h^{-}\circ a^{-}\circ h}(x)\circ h^{-}\circ a\circ h=h^{-}\circ a\circ h\circ\lambda^{-1}_{h^{-}\circ a\circ h}(x)

\displaystyle=h^{-}\circ a\circ h+x

where in the last equality we use
2.6
. Hence,

h^{-}\circ a\circ h\in C^{+}_{B}(I)
. Finally, the same calculations ensure that
h^{-}\circ a\circ h\in\text{Ker}\,\lambda^{I}
, thereby proving the assertion.
∎

.

Is the variety of two-sided skew braces action accessible?
More generally, is the subvariety of two-sided skew braces the largest action accessible subvariety of
Skb
?

3
Action representability of post-Lie algebras

In this section, we show that the category
\text{\bf PostLie}_{k}
of post-Lie algebras (over a fixed field
k
) is not action representable.
In particular, we prove the stronger statement according to which the subvariety
\text{\bf PreLie}_{k}
of pre-Lie algebras is not action accessible.
For more details on these structures, we refer the reader to recent papers
[
6
,
16
]
and references therein.

Let us begin by first recalling a useful characterisation of action accessibility for an
operadic variety
, namely,
a variety of non-associative algebras over a field
k
determined by a set of multilinear identities (see
[
39
]
).
Specifically, as proved in
[
28
]
and pointed out in
[
15
]
, we have the following rather useful result.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{V}
be an operadic variety of non-associative algebras over a field
k
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

\mathcal{V}
is action accessible;

(2)

There exist scalars
\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{8}
,
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{8}\in k
such that the following

\displaystyle x(yz)=\lambda_{1}(xy)z

\displaystyle+\lambda_{2}(yx)z+\lambda_{3}z(xy)+\lambda_{4}z(yx)+\lambda_{5}(xz)y

\displaystyle+\lambda_{6}(zx)y+\lambda_{7}y(xz)+\lambda_{8}y(zx)\,,

(3.1)

\displaystyle(yz)x=\mu_{1}(xy)z

\displaystyle+\mu_{2}(yx)z+\mu_{3}z(xy)+\mu_{4}z(yx)

\displaystyle+\mu_{5}(xz)y+\mu_{6}(zx)y+\mu_{7}y(xz)+\mu_{8}y(zx)

(3.2)

are identities in
\mathcal{V}
.

Let us now introduce the main definition of this section.

Definition 3.2
.

A
post-Lie algebra

[
47
]
(over a field
k
) is the datum of a
k
-vector space
A
together with two bilinear maps
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
,
\cdot:A\times A\xrightarrow{\,}A
such that

(1)

(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a Lie algebra;

(2)

The identities

\displaystyle x\{y,z\}

\displaystyle=\{xy,z\}+\{y,xz\}\,,

(3.3)

\displaystyle\{x,y\}z

\displaystyle=a(x,y,z)-a(y,x,z)\,,

(3.4)

hold for all
x,y,z\in A
,
where
a(x,y,z):=x(yz)-(xy)z
, is the so-called
associator
on
(A,\cdot)
.

A
pre-Lie algebra
is a non-associative algebra
(A,\cdot)
such that

\displaystyle x(yz)-(xy)z=y(xz)-(yx)z\,,

(3.5)

holds, for all
x,y,z\in A
, or, equivalently such that

\displaystyle a(x,y,z)=a(y,x,z)\,,

holds, for all
x,y,z\in A
.

Remark 3.3
.

(1)

It is important to note that a pre-Lie algebra
(A,\cdot)
is a particular post-Lie algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\cdot)
with trivial bracket
\{\cdot,\cdot\}
.
Hence, the variety
PreLie
is a (proper) subvariety of
PostLie
. Despite this fact, pre-Lie algebras have attracted interest independently from post-Lie algebras, (see
[
6
]
for a comprehensive treatment).

(2)

Any pre-Lie algebra satisfies the identity (
3.1
) in
3.1
(by considering
\lambda_{1}=\lambda_{7}=1
,
\lambda_{2}=-1
, and the other coefficient
\lambda_{i}=0
).

Examples 3.4
.

(1)

An associative algebra is a pre-Lie algebra whose associator is identically zero, i.e., such that
a(x,y,z)=0
, for all
x,y,z\in A
.

(2)

A Lie algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
always admits a structure of post-Lie algebra
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\cdot)
defined by setting
x\cdot y=0
, for all
x,y\in A
.

(3)

If
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a
2
-step nilpotent Lie algebra then
(A,\{\cdot,\cdot\},\{\cdot,\cdot\})
is a post-Lie algebra.

Let us now turn our attention to the claim of this section.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
k
be a field. The category

\text{\bf PostLie}_{k}
of post-Lie algebras over
k
is not action accessible.

Proof.

In light of
1.8
, it is sufficient to prove the assertion for the variety
\text{\bf PreLie}_{k}
of pre-Lie algebras over
k
.
Moreover, thanks to
3.1
, it is sufficient to display a pre-Lie algebra in which either (
3.1
) or (
3.2
) does not hold.
To this aim, consider the four dimensional pre-Lie algebra
I_{4}
from
[
17
, Example 1.1]

with basis
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3},e_{4}\}
and whose non-trivial products are given by

\displaystyle e_{1}e_{2}

\displaystyle=e_{2}\,,\quad e_{1}e_{3}=e_{3}\,,\quad e_{1}e_{4}=e_{4}\,,

\displaystyle e_{1}e_{1}

\displaystyle=2e_{1}\,,\quad e_{2}e_{2}=e_{3}e_{3}=e_{4}e_{4}=e_{1}\,.

The term
(e_{2}e_{2})e_{3}=e_{3}
does not belong to the subalgebra of
I_{4}
generated by the elements

\displaystyle(e_{3}e_{2})e_{2}\,,\ (e_{2}e_{3})e_{2}\,,\ e_{2}(e_{3}e_{2})\,,\ e_{2}(e_{2}e_{3})\,,

due to the fact that the latter terms are all equal to zero.
Consequently, the identity (
3.2
) cannot be satisfied in
A
for any choice of
\mu_{1},\ldots,\mu_{8}
.
Therefore, we conclude that
\text{\bf PreLie}_{k}
is not action accessible.
∎

As a consequence of
3.5
and
1.7
, we deduce that the category
\text{\bf PostLie}_{k}
of post-Lie algebras (over any field
k
) is not (weakly) action representable.
If instead we restrict our attention to Novikov algebras, then we can prove the following result.

Proposition 3.6
.

The variety of Novikov algebras
(
over an arbitrary field
k
)
is action accessible.

Proof.

By (2) in
3.3
, the identity (
3.1
) is satisfied. Moreover, (
3.2
) trivially holds (by considering as unique non-zero coefficient
\mu_{1}=0
).
Therefore, the claim follows.
∎

Let
A
be the pre-Lie algebra in the proof of
3.5
.
Let us underline that this counterexample heavily relies on the fact that
A
is not Novikov since, for example,
(e_{2}e_{2})e_{3}\neq(e_{2}e_{3})e_{2}
.
Therefore, it is natural to state the following question.

Question 3.7
.

If
\mathcal{V}
is an action accessible subvariety of
\text{\bf PreLie}_{k}
and
