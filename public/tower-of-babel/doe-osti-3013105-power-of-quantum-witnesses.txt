1 Original Project Goals
A central theme in the study of quantum information is to understand
whether quantum resources are more powerful than their classical coun-
terparts. One such resource is quantum witness and understanding the
power of quantum witnesses is one of the fundamental questions of quan-
tum complexity theory.
 The class NP, non-deterministic polynomial time is the cornerstone of
classical complexity theory. Knill [Kni96] formulated the quantum notion
of non-determinism and eventually the quantum complexity class analogue
of NP, called QMA (Quantum Merlin Arthur) was defined and studied by
Kitaev [Kit99]. In this context, Quantum Merlin Arthur (QMA) was de-
fined (by Kitaev) to be the class of languages for which membership of
any input can be proven via a quantum witness such that the witness can
be verified in quantum polynomial time. After a decade of research on
QMA and many significant findings later, we are still far from fully un-
derstanding the power of quantum witnesses and pinpointing the relation
between QMA and the other well understood complexity classes. In this
project, we proposed to explore QMA and its relation to other complexity
classes from a totally new perspective with the aim of making progress
towards some of the decade old open problems in quantum complexity theory. The broad object of this
project was to understand the power of quantum witnesses and related objects and their properties: In this
direction, this project addressed three key broader category of questions: 1) Are quantum witnesses more
powerful than the classical witnesses? 2) How easy it is to copy quantum witnesses and what are their
complexity theoretic implications? 3) Can quantum witnesses shed light or help provide super-polynomial
quantum speedups on problems for which super-polynomial quantum speedups are shown to be not possible
in general?

2 Accomplishments
The research conducted under this grant has directly addressed the three core pillars of the original proposal:
characterizing the computational power of quantum witnesses, understanding their uncloneability, implica-
tions for complexity theory and identifying structural regimes for super-polynomial speedups. Our primary
achievements are categorized below:

I. Power and Uncloneability of Quantum Witnesses We showed that uncloneable quantum proof
and advice states are not just a technical curiosity but are sometimes strictly necessary. We formalize this
by defining strictly uncloneable versions of major quantum complexity classes such as QMA, BQP/qpoly, and

 1

FEQP/qpoly, where every valid proof or advice state must be uncloneable, i.e., no polynomial time procedure
can create multiple usable copies for independent verifiers. We then prove that such languages actually exist:
in the quantum oracle model, we construct languages that lie in strictly uncloneable QMA and BQP/qpoly,
yielding oracle separations from their cloneable counterparts, and unconditionally, we show that a known
relational problem separating FEQP/qpoly from FBQP/poly is also strictly uncloneable. as shown in [CKP25]
(STOC 2025). Complementing this, we investigated complexity theoretic implications of quantum witnesses
in “The Role of Piracy in Quantum Proofs,” providing the first construction of uncloneable proof system for
a language in NP [BGPS24] (LatinCrypt 2025). We also showed that in the world of cloneability, QMA(2)
and QMA are the same (a major open problem in the Turing machine world).

II. Foundational Impacts The funding also enabled exploration into the boundaries of quantum mea-
surement and counting complexity. We characterized the power of BQP under non-collapsing and correlated
measurements [MP25][MP24b] (CCC 2025), offering a new perspective on the traditional complexity classes.
Additionally, our work in the SIAM Journal on Computing [BDCG+ 24] unearthed the relationship between
symmetries, graph properties, and quantum query advantages.

III. Structural Complexity and Quantum Speedups We also partially resolved long-standing open
questions in Decision Tree Complexity, bridging the gap between abstract witness theory and concrete
complexity measures. Specifically, we achieved tight quadratic relations between block sensitivity (bs) and
degree (deg) for important class of functions like, graph properties and “zebra functions” (functions that are
captures both symmetric and monotone as subcases) [CPS22] (FSTTCS 2023). This provides a mathematical
path toward understanding fine-grained quantum speedups. Furthermore, as a culminating achievement, our
work on “Quantum Speedups Through Function Extensions” (to be submitted to CCC 2026) utilizes these
structural insights to find noble way to capture quantum speedups. Determining the properties responsible
for super-polynomial quantum speedups over classical algorithms is a key question in quantum complexity
theory, however, identifying such functions remains a challenge. In that work, we present a new framework
for proving quantum non-speedup results. Specifically, we show that if a partial function can be extended
to a total function while maintaining a polynomial relationship in any complexity measure, then no super-
polynomial quantum speedup is possible for the original partial function with respect to that measure. This
approach has the advantage of eliminating the need to independently establish a lower bound for any given
measure of interest and a corresponding upper bound for classical algorithm to demonstrate the absence of
super-polynomial speedup. This instead allows for a single step process by either finding an extension whose
complexity of the measure is polynomially related, or by proving that no such extension exists.
 Together, these results provide a tighter integration between concrete complexity measures and the
physical properties of quantum states, directly answering the fundamental questions regarding the power
and “copyability” of quantum help.

4 Conclusions and Future Directions
■ Distributed Verification of Quantum Message from Quantum Data Center: In the near-term
future, quantum data processing centers, such as AWS Braket, IBM Cloud, will potentially provide com-
putational help as quantum data to various QPUs with limited capabilities, enabling them to perform
computations more efficiently. However, such QPU users might not fully trust the data centers. In such
cases, the data centers function similarly to computationally bounded Merlin in the well-studied Merlin-
Arthur (MA) games, where any data the server provides must be verified for correctness. Interestingly,
the power and properties of such quantum data in the single-verifier setting remain an active area of
research in quantum complexity theory. In fact, the famous P vs. NP problem is a closely related classical
manifestation of this question. PI Podder’s this DOE funded project has made a significant progress toward
understanding properties of these quantum help states. When there are two verifiers, the setting aligns
with the well-known quantum communication complexity model. In this model, it is demonstrated
that quantum help can provide an exponential advantage over classical help [BGPS24, CKP24, KP14].
 A natural and exciting generalization arises when we extend this to a multi-verifier setting, where a
quantum network consists of sparsely connected QPUs receiving untrusted messages from a quantum data
center. Emerging quantum internet architectures fit perfectly in this framework. In the future projects,
we aim to investigate the computational power of quantum help states in the multi-verifier setting—under

■ Implications of Quantum Advantage. We would also explore the foundational implications of quan-
tum advantage through the lens of complexity theory. Here we propose to investigate how assumptions such
as BQP ̸= NP or QMA ̸= BQP or even milder assumption such as BQP ̸= BPP#P shape our understanding
of uniquely quantum phenomena. The main two research directions are as follows.

 4

• Hardness of cloning and uncloneable proofs: This direction investigates which problems admit
  uncloneable quantum proof systems, aiming to construct white-box uncloneable systems under standard
  complexity assumptions. It builds on recent progress on uncloneability and Hamiltonian phase state
  constructions [HN24, Wer98, BHL+ 05, BHHP24].
• Quantum cryptography from milder assumptions: This direction proposes cryptographic prim-
  itives based on milder assumptions such as BQP ̸= BPP#P , motivated by connections (made by the
  PI in CCC 2025 [MP24a] [MP25]) between quantum computation with correlated measurements and
  classical counting complexity [KT24b, KT24a].
