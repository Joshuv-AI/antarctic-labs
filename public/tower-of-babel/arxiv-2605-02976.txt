On the energy balance of Newtonian Gravitation
R. Trasarti-Battistoni 1 , Máté Pszota 2 , 3 , ∗ {}^{2,3,^{*}} and P. Ván 2,4

Abstract
. It is shown that the energy density formulas of Newtonian gravity by Maxwell, Bondi and Ohanian cannot be distinguished by boundary conditions, and also the corresponding energy balances are identical. However, they are not equivalent. From a thermodynamic point of view, the Ohanian energy density is distinguished.

1.
Introduction

The energy of the gravitational field is not uniquely defined. Several expressions for energy density and energy flux have been proposed, and this ambiguity is usually attributed to boundary conditions at spatial infinity
[
1
]
.

From a physical point of view, however, assumptions about spatial infinity are unsatisfactory. In this paper, we argue that the problem should instead be analyzed locally, using boundary conditions on energy fluxes rather than on energy densities. In this way, the issue can be reformulated entirely in terms of differential energy balances. Because reference frames play a crucial role in such a local treatment, we draw on concepts and methods from fluid mechanics.

The paper is organised as follows. First, we review the basic concepts of energy and energy flux in Newtonian gravity (NG) and show that mass balance plays an essential role. We then introduce the necessary continuum-mechanical framework, in particular the distinction between local and substantial balances and between laboratory and comoving frames. Next, we discuss the energy balances associated with the Ohanian, Maxwell, and Bondi energy densities, and we demonstrate their equivalence starting from the Ohanian form. Here, the concept of classical holography is illuminating. The concept of classical holography is central to this analysis: it allows the different balances to be transformed into one another and their source terms to be expressed either in bulk-force or in surface-force form. In Section 5, we show that the differences among these balances are governed by an observer-dependent, sourceless balance, the
Green balance
. Finally, we discuss the implications of these results.

The paper also includes several appendices. Appendix (
A
) discusses the origin of the Ohanian energy balance from a general perspective and emphasizes an energy-based argument in comparison to the original thermodynamic one.
Appendix (
B
) presents further implications and properties of the Green balance. Appendix (
C
) analyses gravitational energy density from the point of view of the Lagrangian theory of classical fields.

2.
Newtonian Gravity and Continuum Description

For notational convenience, we adopt units such that
4\pi G=1
, where
G
is Newton’s universal gravitational constant. We use the standard shorthand notation

\partial_{t}\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial t}
for the partial (local, Eulerian) time derivative and
\nabla\equiv\frac{\partial}{\partial{\bf x}}
for the usual spatial gradient in Cartesian coordinates. The total (substantial, convective or Lagrangian) time derivative is denoted by
{}^{\cdot}\equiv\frac{d}{dt}\equiv\partial_{t}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla
.
In particular, for an arbitrary scalar field
\phi({\bf x},t)
, its spatial gradient is the vector
{\bf V}:=\nabla\phi
,
and
(\nabla\phi)^{\cdot}\equiv\frac{d{\bf V}}{dt}
denotes the substantial time derivative applied to the entire vector field.

Consistently with Newtonian gravity, the background geometry is assumed to be Euclidean.

2.1.
Fields and fluids

We make the following assumptions:

(1)

We assume the fields are regular at each position
{\bf x}
and time
t
, so that they are sufficiently differentiable. Some fields are also assumed to vanish sufficiently rapidly at spatial infinity. The material system is modeled as a continuous (one-component) fluid characterized by a mass density distribution field
\rho({\bf x},t)
, a velocity field
{\bf v}({\bf x},t)
, and an associated convective mass current density (or momentum density)
{\bf J}_{v}({\bf x},t):=\rho{\bf v}
.
All gravitational effects are fully described by the gravitational field strength
{\bf g}({\bf x},t)
, and the associated gravitational scalar potential field
\varphi({\bf x},t)
.

(2)

The essential requirement is the existence and near-uniqueness of the gravitational scalar potential
\varphi({\bf x},t)
, defined such that it is related to the gravitational vector strength field
{\bf g}({\bf x},t)
by:

\displaystyle{\bf g}({\bf x},t)=:-\nabla\varphi({\bf x},t)

(1)

Here, ”near-uniqueness” means uniqueness up to a largely but not completely irrelevant spatially uniform offset
\varphi_{*}(t)
, which may vary in time. This represents a simple gauge freedom, or a ”gauge ambiguity”.

(3)

The time evolution of the fluid is governed by two equations of motion:

(i) The evolution of
\rho({\bf x},t)
is governed by the mass conservation equation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho{\bf v})=0

(2)

(ii) The evolution of
{\bf v}({\bf x},t)
is governed by the momentum balance (Cauchy equation):

\displaystyle\rho\dot{\bf v}+\nabla\cdot{\bf P}=\rho\left(\partial_{t}{\bf v}+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}\right)+\nabla\cdot{\bf P}=\mathbf{f}:=\left(\mathbf{f}_{g}+\mathbf{f}_{other}\right)

(3)

where the pressure force density is
\mathbf{f}_{p}=-\nabla\cdot{\bf P}
, and the total force density is
\mathbf{f}=\mathbf{f}_{g}+\mathbf{f}_{other}
, with gravitational contribution
\mathbf{f}_{g}=\rho{\bf g}
. In what follows, we assume
\mathbf{f}_{other}\equiv 0
.

(4)

The spatial distribution of the gravitational fields is determined by two equations:

(i) The intuitive picture that field lines of gravity
{\bf g}=-\nabla\varphi

are ”sourced” by (more precisely: ”sink” into) the mass density distribution
\rho
,
and the way
{\bf g}
”spreads” all around
\rho
according to Newton’s inverse square law
is formally described by
the NG Gauss theorem (in terms of
{\bf g}
) or the Newton-Gauss-Poisson equation (in terms of
\varphi)
,
defining the Laplacian operator
\Delta:=\nabla\cdot\nabla
:

\displaystyle\Delta\varphi=-\nabla\cdot{\bf g}=\rho

(4)

(ii) The intuitive picture that field lines of
{\bf g}
neither ”turn around” nor ”close upon themselves”
is formally described by the NG ”irrotationality/conservativity condition”:

\displaystyle\nabla\times\nabla\varphi=-\nabla\times{\bf g}\equiv 0

(5)

Remarkably, within NG (
i.e.
assuming equation (
4
)), the gravitational force density
\mathbf{f}_{g}
can be written in divergence form, analogously to the pressure force density
\mathbf{f}_{p}=-\nabla\cdot{\bf P}
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}_{g}=\rho{\bf g}=-\nabla\cdot{\bf P}_{g}

(6)

Here
{\bf P}_{g}
is a gravitational pressure tensor, analogous to the Maxwell stress tensor in electromagnetism. The property described by the relation in eq. (
6
) is referred to as the
classical holographic property
, and its importance is discussed in detail in Section
4.2
.

Both (
4
) and (
5
)
for the fields
{\bf g}
or
\varphi
themselves
are well-known equations of NG. Considerably less well-known are the following relations, that involve their
partial time derivatives
\partial_{t}{\bf g}
and
\partial_{t}\varphi
.

First of all, the
gravitational ”displacement current”
field is defined as:

\displaystyle{\bf J}_{g}({\bf x},t):=-\partial_{t}{\bf g}({\bf x},t)

(7)

It is (with the opposite sign) the NG analogue of Maxwell’s electric ”displacement current”
{\bf J}_{E}:=\epsilon_{0}\partial_{t}{\bf E}
of EM, proposed by Maxwell himself

[
2
,
3
,
4
,
5
]
.

Although
{\bf J}_{g}=-\partial_{t}{\bf g}
, is not a mass current, it has the same physical dimensions as the convective mass current
{\bf J}_{v}=\rho{\bf v}
, which motivates the term ”current” and the notation
{\bf J}
.
The word ”displacement” goes back to an obsolete theoretical model originally used by Maxwell.

Considering
-\partial_{t}{\bf g}
, taking its divergence, exchanging derivatives
\nabla\cdot
and
\partial_{t}
yields:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left(-\partial_{t}{\bf g}\right)=\partial_{t}\rho=-\nabla\cdot(\rho{\bf v})

(8)

where the l.h.s. equality comes from the NG Poisson equation (
4
), while the r.h.s. equality comes from mass-flow continuity (
2
). Relation (
8
) plays a key role in the rest of the paper,
and it could be referred to as the ”displacement-continuity” equation.

3.
NG energy densities, energy current densities, and balances

This Section summarizes, in a systematic way, results previously obtained or used in Synge
[
6
]
, Peters
[
1
]
, Sebens
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, Dewar & Weatherall
[
9
]
, Duerr & Read
[
10
]
, Vasyliunas
[
11
]
, Eklund
[
12
]
, Bengtsson & Eklund
[
13
]
.
Each of these works considered some of the cases dealt with here,
but only
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
13
]
treated all the cases together.
Altogether, the pairs of energy density – energy current density referred here below as
Maxwell-Livens and Bondi-Bondi have received, so far, comparably more attention than the case Ohanian-No-Name, which is instead somewhat favoured by the present paper.

Energy balance, in general:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\varepsilon_{a}+\nabla\cdot{\bf S}_{a}=w

(9)

The first term on the l.h.s. is the time variation of the local energy density
\varepsilon_{a}
, and the second term on the l.h.s. is the spatial divergence of the corresponding local energy current density
{\bf S}_{a}
.
Here the index
a
conveniently parametrizes various energy density / energy current density pairs
[
13
]
.
Other Authors used similar, mathematically equivalent parametrizations earlier on,
but the parametrization of
[
13
]
is the most convenient here.

The precise mathematical role of the parameter
a
will become clear in Sect.(
3.5
). For the moment, one can regard the value of
a
just as a useful mnemonic device:
a=1
for Maxwell;
a=0
for Ohanian;
a=1/2
for Bondi; and just
a
for ”arbitrary”.

The source term on the r.h.s. is the power density
w=-\mathbf{f}_{f}\cdot{\bf v}
delivered to the matter by the field force density
\mathbf{f}_{f}
acting upon it.
It is the NG analogue of Joule’s power, delivered from the electric field to the current, in EM.
This is the usual point of view.

Alternatively
[
7
]
, one might define the opposite
+\mathbf{f}_{m}=-\mathbf{f}_{f}

as the force exerted by matter upon the field,
and correspondingly the power
+\mathbf{f}_{m}\cdot{\bf v}=-\mathbf{f}_{f}\cdot{\bf v}

delivered to the field by the matter force density
\mathbf{f}_{m}
acting upon it.
Still alternatively, if the force can be re-expressed as
\mathbf{f}_{f}=-\nabla\cdot{\bf P}_{f}
, then the power can be re-expressed in terms of tensors
[{\bf P}]:[\nabla{\bf v}]
instead of in terms of vectors as
(\nabla\cdot{\bf P}_{f})\cdot{\bf v}
. However, fixing the power density on the r.h.s. of (
9
) does not uniquely fix either of the two terms on the l.h.s. separately, but only their sum.

3.1.
NG energy density
\varepsilon({\bf x},t)

In fact, there is an infinite number of energy densities
\varepsilon_{a}({\bf x},t)

(a continuous 1-parameter family described by
a
), which are locally different, but globally equivalent:
their distributions are different point-by-point in space,
but when integrated over all space
under suitable boundary conditions at spatial infinity

(vanishing surface integrals),
and
assuming the Poisson equation
(”to turn
\varphi
into
\rho
”, and
vice versa
, so to say),
they return the same total gravitational energy
E_{g}
, even for
{a^{\prime}}\not={a"}
:

\displaystyle\int d^{3}{\bf x}\varepsilon_{a^{\prime}}=E_{g}=\int d^{3}{\bf x}\varepsilon_{a"}

(10)

Remarkably interesting, for various reasons, are three energy densities
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
.
For clarity, they are all listed and defined, with their characteristic properties, here:

(1)

(
a=1
) Maxwell
[
2
]
, pure
field
energy density (negative):

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Maxwell}\equiv\varepsilon_{1}:=-\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}^{2}=-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\leq 0

(11)

Expression (
11
) was originally suggested by Maxwell himself,
in the
Note on the attraction of gravity

at the end of his paper
A dynamical theory of the electromagnetic field

[
2
]
.
Maxwell clearly declared himself puzzled because
\varepsilon_{1}
is negative,
and he was not able to find a physical interpretation that was convincing to him.

(2)

(
a=0
) Ohanian
[
14
]
. It is a matter-energy
interaction

+ pure
field
energy density:

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Ohanian}\equiv\varepsilon_{0}:=+\rho\varphi+\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}^{2}=\rho\varphi+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}

(12)

Both terms are interpreted as associated with gravitation, either through
\varphi
or
{\bf g}
.
The first term
+\rho\varphi\leq 0
can be interpreted as matter-field inter-action
(
not potential
energy
\frac{1}{2}\rho\varphi

i.e.

not
mutual matter-matter interaction energy,
no prefactor
1/2
to avoid double counting of particle pairs),
the second term
+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}>0
as (positive) energy density
purely due to the gravitational field
{\bf g}=-\nabla\varphi
.

Remarkably, Ohanian’s energy density (
12
) equals (the opposite of)

\mathcal{L}_{g}
, the well-known ”gravitational” Lagrangian density
commonly used to derive the field equations (
4
), (
5
) of Newtonian Gravitation
from the point of view of the Lagrangian theory of classical fields, e.g.
[
15
,
9
]
.
It was then further modified by Eklund
[
12
]
and Bengtsson & Eklund
[
13
]
,
to derive also the equations of motions (
2
), (
3
) of the matter fields.
For details see Appendix (
C
).

Finally, (
12
) is the same starting expression assumed by Ván and Abe
[
16
]

in their thermodynamic framework for gravity.
For details see Appendix (
A
).

(3)

(
a=1/2
) Bondi
[
17
]

potential
energy density
(=
1/2
matter-field interaction, negative for
\varphi\to 0
at spatial infinity). It is the arithmetical mean of the energy densities (
11
) and (
12
):

\displaystyle\varepsilon_{Bondi}\equiv\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}:=+\frac{1}{2}\rho\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\varphi\Delta\varphi\leq 0

(13)

It is the gravitational interaction energy of particles. The prefactor
1/2
avoids double-counting the interactions.

3.2.
NG energy current densities
{\bf S}({\bf x},t)

Now, fixing both the power on the r.h.s. of (
9
)
and one at will of the two terms on its r.h.s.
does uniquely fix the remaining third term on the l.h.s.
However, fixing
\varepsilon_{a}
as above, and hence
\partial_{t}\varepsilon_{a}
as a consequence,
only fixes
\nabla\cdot{\bf S}_{a}
but not
{\bf S}_{a}
itself.
As for energy densities
\varepsilon_{a}
, there is an infinite number of energy currents
{\bf S}_{a}({\bf x},t)

locally different, but globally equivalent.
But the ambiguity for energy currents is even wider than for energy densities themselves:
for a given
a
hence given
\varepsilon_{a}
and
\partial_{t}\varepsilon_{a}
, there can be two (more precisely, infinite)
locally different energy currents
{\bf S}^{\prime}_{a}\not={\bf S}^{"}_{a}={\bf S}^{\prime}_{a}+\nabla\times{\bf W}
,
with an arbitrary vector (potential) field
{\bf W}({\bf x},t)
,
which nevertheless share the same divergence
\nabla\cdot{\bf S}_{a}^{\prime}=\nabla\cdot{\bf S}_{a}^{"}
,
since
\nabla\cdot(\nabla\times)\equiv 0
.
A similar problem is encountered in the context of classical Electro-Magnetism,
where it is widely discussed, see e.g.
[
18
]
,
[
19
]
,
[
20
]
, and references therein.

Remarkably interesting, for various reasons,
are the three energy current densities
{\bf S}_{1}
,
{\bf S}_{0}
,
{\bf S}_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
which in some sense most directly correspond to
the three energy densities
\varepsilon_{1}
,
\varepsilon_{0}
,
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
defined above.
Each of them contains two parts:
one related to mass motion (advection),
{\bf S}_{v}:=(\rho{\bf v})\varphi
,
like in electric engineering
[
20
]
,
and another related to the time variation of gravitation.
In turn, there are two alternative gravity-related energy currents:

{\bf S}_{g}:=(-\partial_{t}{\bf g})\varphi
(Synge
[
6
]
eq.(5.11)),
proportional to the scalar potential
and parallel (assuming
\varphi<0
) to the vector field time-variation,
or instead

{\bf S}_{\varphi}:={\bf g}(\partial_{t}\varphi)
(Bengtsson & Eklund
[
13
]
eq.(51)),
proportional to the scalar potential time-variation,
and parallel to the vector field.

The three energy current densities are:

(1)

(a=1)
, Livens-like
[
21
]
,
Vasyliunas
[
11
]
eq.(20),
scalar potential advection by the total (displacement + mass) current:

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{1}=({\bf S}_{g}+{\bf S}_{v})=(-\partial_{t}{\bf g})\varphi+\rho{\bf v}\varphi=(+\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi)\varphi+\rho{\bf v}\varphi

(14)

(2)

(a=0)
”No-Name” of its own,
potential variation
\times
gravitational field
+ scalar potential advection by mass current:

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{0}=({\bf S}_{\varphi}+{\bf S}_{v})=+{\bf g}(\partial_{t}\varphi)+\rho{\bf v}\varphi=(-\nabla\varphi)(\partial_{t}\varphi)+\rho{\bf v}\varphi

(15)

(3)

(a=1/2)
Vasyliunas
[
11
]
eq.(34),
arithmetical mean of the energy current densities (
14
) and (
15
),
including scalar potential advection by mass current:

\displaystyle{\bf S}_{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{({\bf S}_{1}+{\bf S}_{0})}{2}=\frac{1}{2}\left[-(\partial_{t}{\bf g})\varphi+{\bf g}(\partial_{t}\varphi)\right]+\rho{\bf v}\varphi=\frac{1}{2}\left[(\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi)\varphi-(\nabla\varphi)(\partial_{t}\varphi)\right]+\rho{\bf v}\varphi

(16)

The expression in square brackets is the arithmetic mean of
{\bf S}_{g}
and
{\bf S}_{\varphi}
,
which Synge
[
6
]
Sect.8 interprets as
”equal sharing of interaction energy between two interacting subsystems”.
Without mass advection term
\rho{\bf v}\varphi

it reduces to Synge’s eq.(8.1), who considered
\rho=0
.

Note that the purely advective energy current
\rho{\bf v}\varphi
appears intact in all three energy currents
{\bf S}_{1}
,
{\bf S}_{0}
, and
{\bf S}_{\frac{1}{2}}
. Note also that the two gravity-related terms in (22) handle the gauge ambiguity of the spatial offset
\varphi_{*}(t)
differently. It might initially appear that in
{\bf S}_{\varphi}=(-\partial_{t}\varphi)(\nabla\varphi)
, the ambiguity is removed because the derivatives act upon distinct factors. However, if the arbitrary offset varies in time (
\dot{\varphi}_{*}(t)\neq 0
), then the partial time derivative shifts as
\partial_{t}\tilde{\varphi}=\partial_{t}\varphi+\dot{\varphi}_{*}
. Therefore,
{\bf S}_{\varphi}
also perpetuates the offset ambiguity, just as
{\bf S}_{g}=+\varphi(\partial_{t}\nabla\varphi)
does. Remarkably, despite the gauge-dependence of these individual currents, this ambiguity perfectly and rigorously cancels out in every local energy balance (
17
)-(
19
), guaranteeing the physical consistency of the overall framework.

3.3.
Energy balances

Substituting each pair
\varepsilon_{a},{\bf S}_{a}
in (
9
) gives a corresponding energy balance,
as in
[
8
]
,
[
12
]
, and
[
13
]
.

All the energy balances in the form above for the cases
a=1,0,1/2
are explicitly listed here, with their characteristic properties, pro’s and con’s:

(1)

(a=1)
Maxwell-Livens;
Maxwell’s gravitational field energy density
\varepsilon_{1}
has a very simple mathematical expression, Maxwell’s displacement current in
{\bf S}_{1}
, not quite such a simple expression;
rather well-established physical interpretations for both, yet somewhat obscure;
favored by analogy of NG with EM:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(-\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}^{2}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[-(\partial_{t}{\bf g})\varphi+(\rho{\bf v})\varphi\right]=-\rho{\bf v}\cdot{\bf g}

(17)

(2)

(a=0)
Ohanian-No-Name;
not too simple mathematical expressions of
\varepsilon_{0}
and
{\bf S}_{0}
; somewhat unclear physical picture of both;
but favored by different arguments from Classical Lagrangian Field Theory App.(
C
),
and Thermo-Dynamics of continuous media App.(
A
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(+\rho\varphi+\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}^{2}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[+{\bf g}(\partial_{t}\varphi)+(\rho{\bf v})\varphi\right]=-\rho{\bf v}\cdot{\bf g}

(18)

(3)

(a=1/2)
Bondi-Bondi;
Bondi’s gravitational potential energy of particles
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
with a simple expression and well-established physical interpretation in NG;
all the contrary for Bondi’s current
{\bf S}_{\frac{1}{2}}
,
awkward mathematical expression, and unclear physical interpretation;
arithmetical mean of the energy balances (
17
) and (
18
):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(+\frac{1}{2}\rho\varphi\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[+\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}(\partial_{t}\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}(\partial_{t}{\bf g})\varphi+(\rho{\bf v})\varphi\right]=-\rho{\bf v}\cdot{\bf g}

(19)

This expression can be extracted from the energy balances in the standard textbook
Galactic Dynamics by Binney & Tremaine
[
22
]
, appendix F.1.3.

Note that, while the power source on the r.h.s. is the same for all three cases (and in general, arbitrary
a
),
the energy densities and energy current densities on the l.h.s.,
while having clearly different mathematical expressions, have also markedly different physical distributions in space.
For example, this is vivedly illustrated by
Fig.1 in
[
23
]
for
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
vs
\varepsilon_{1}
, in the case of a spherical ball of uniform mass density,
and Fig.2.6 in
[
12
]
for
{\bf S}_{\frac{1}{2}}
vs
{\bf S}_{1}
vs
{\bf S}_{0}
,
in the case of two equal masses orbiting around each other.

3.4.
The gravitational Poynting vector

Remarkably, the case
a=1
can be rewritten in a very suggestive, alternative way, closely analogous to its EM counterpart.

First of all, as originally noted by Heaviside
[
24
,
25
]
,
there are many and strong analogies between NG and classical (Maxwell+Lorentz) EM, and/or
with Gravito-Electro-Magnetism (GEM), see e.g. Sebens
[
23
]
, Hermann & Polig
[
26
]
.
However,
NG is a fully non-relativistic theory (instantaneous speed of propagation of physical effects, action-at-distance),
while GEM like EM is a fully relativistic theory (finite speed of propagation of physical effects, retardation.)
So it is more appropriate to compare NG with
quasi-static
sub-models of full dynamic EM,
in particular the Electro-Quasi-Static (EQS) model, discussed e.g. by Larsson
[
27
]
.

As a consequence of such analogies,
one can define the
gravito-magnetic
field
{\bf b}

(sometimes called ”gravinetic” field
[
28
]
),
the NG analog of the magnetic field of EM.
This has been considered by many authors, from several different theoretical points of view,
leading to slightly different versions of the NG vs EM analogy, e.g.:
Heaviside
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
from NG in analogy with EM,
Bedford & Krumm
[
29
]
from NG plus SR,
Kolbenstvedt
[
30
]
from SR plus a little bit of GR (gravitational time dilation),
Harris
[
31
]
or Ohanian & Ruffini
[
14
]
,
from GR plus linear approximations (weak field, slow motions),
Buchert
[
32
]
or Vasyliunas
[
11
]
purely from NG, and so on.
Note that units, signs, and notation can vary widely amongst different works,
even worse than in usual EM.
In particular, there are different factors of
2
or
4

in the various definitions of
{\bf b}
and/or of the gravitational Lorentz force,
that in some cases give different physical predictions, not just different mathematical definitions.
(And in some versions, e.g.
[
31
]
there are additional terms,
with no strict EM analogues, due to residual non-linearities inherited from GR.)

In all versions of the NG-EM analogy, the gravito-magnetic field
{\bf b}
satisfies two field equations,
the NG analogue of the Ampére-Maxwell equation:

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf b}=-\rho{\bf v}+\partial_{t}{\bf g}

(20)

and the NG analogue of the magnetic Gauss theorem (”no magnetic poles”):

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf b}=0

(21)

Note that the ”obvious” NG analogue of the Faraday’s induction equation would be, but is not:

\displaystyle\nabla\times{\bf g}=-\partial_{t}{\bf b}.

(22)

This equation holds in GEM and EM (with
\partial_{t}
replaced by
\frac{1}{c}\partial_{t}
),
but it
does not
hold in strict NG and EQS (and furthermore, there is no
c
in NG):
it might well happen that
\partial_{t}{\bf b}\not=0
at some instant and position,
but for sure it will be
\nabla\times{\bf g}\equiv 0
always and everywhere, because of the field equation (
5
).
In contrast with GEM or EM, in NG and EQS the (gravito)magnetic field is just an ”auxiliary” field,
”accompanying” the (gravito)electric field in its time-evolution,
but it pays no dynamic role on its own:
because of (
5
) there is no gravitational ”Faraday” induced electric field and force,

4.
Continuum foundations

4.1.
Balances

The balances of continuum mechanics and nonequilibrium thermodynamics express the spacetime properties of extensive physical quantities. A local balance of a scalar
A
can be written as

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho\alpha)+\nabla\cdot{\bf J}_{\alpha}=\sigma_{\alpha}.

(26)

Here
\rho\alpha
is the density of
A
and
\alpha
is the (mass)specific
A
.
{\bf J}_{\alpha}
is the total current density of
A
and
\sigma_{\alpha}
is the production density rate of
A
.
\partial_{t}
is the partial time derivative and
\nabla\cdot
is the divergence. One can obtain the substantial form of the balance by introducing the substantial time derivative, which for
\alpha
reads
\dot{\alpha}=\partial_{t}\alpha+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\alpha
. Then

\displaystyle(\rho\alpha\dot{)}+\nabla\cdot\left({\bf J}_{\alpha}-\rho\alpha{\bf v}\right)+(\rho\alpha)\nabla\cdot{\bf v}=\sigma_{\alpha},

(27)

and
\rho\alpha{\bf v}
is the advective current density,
while
{\bf j}_{\alpha}:=({\bf J}_{\alpha}-\rho\alpha{\bf v})
is the conductive current density of
A
.

If
\alpha=1
, one gets the mass balance. Mass is conserved, therefore
\sigma_{m}=0
;
and the mass current density has only an advective part
{\bf J}_{v}=\rho{\bf v}
,
so the conductive part of the current density of mass is zero,
{\bf j}_{m}:=({\bf J}_{m}-\rho{\bf v}_{m})=0
.
Therefore, the local and substantial forms of the mass balance are:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\rho+\nabla\cdot(\rho{\bf v})=0,\qquad\dot{\rho}+\rho\nabla\cdot{{\bf v}}=0.

(28)

Then the general form of substantial balances, that is (
27
) simplifies to

\displaystyle\rho\dot{\alpha}+\nabla\cdot{\bf j}_{\alpha}=\sigma_{\alpha}.

(29)

The difference of local and substantial balances is related to changes of reference frames. The local balance is the form from the point of view of an external laboratory frame. The substantial balance is the form obtained when the reference frame is fixed to the continuum itself. Naturally, it requires that the defining fields of the continuum must have some regularity requirements. Moreover, in a substantial balance the comoving form is expressed with the physical quantities relative to the laboratory frame (e.g. the relative velocity field
{\bf v}
is the same). The most important background knowledge is that the balance itself is observer independent: it is a spacetime divergence of a four-quantity. This is an evident fact in special relativity but it is the same in Galilean relativistic (nonrelativistic) spacetime as well (see e.g.
[
38
]
).
The local balance is a laboratory frame version of the covariant law, the substantial balance is the same in a comoving frame, but expressed with the relative quantities of the labframe. The substantial time derivative is comoving with the fluid.

It is remarkable, that the substantial time derivative and the spatial derivative
\nabla
are not commutative operations:

\displaystyle(\nabla\varphi\dot{)}=\nabla(\dot{\varphi})-\nabla\varphi\cdot\nabla{\bf v}

\displaystyle,\qquad(\partial_{i}\varphi\dot{)}=\partial_{i}(\dot{\varphi})-\partial_{k}\varphi\partial_{i}v^{k}.

(30)

4.2.
Classical holography

It is also remarkable that the bulk force due to
\mathbf{f_{g}}=\rho{\bf g}
of the gravitational field
{\bf g}=-\nabla\varphi
in any spatial region can be expressed as a surface traction on the boundary, because it can be written as a divergence of a related gravitational pressure tensor. This is called the
classical holographic property
of the gravitational field.
In terms of gravitational pressure tensor
{\bf P}_{grav}
and force density
{\bf f}_{g}
:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot{\bf P}_{grav}={\bf f}_{g}.

(31)

Expressing the gravitational Maxwell stress tensor in terms of the field strength
{\bf g}
:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left[\frac{1}{2}{\bf g}^{2}{\bf I}-{\bf g}\circ{\bf g}\right]=+\rho{\bf g}

(32)

or again, in terms of gravitational potential
\varphi
and its gradient
\nabla\varphi
:

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left[\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}{\bf I}-\nabla\varphi\circ\nabla\varphi\right]=-\Delta\varphi\nabla\varphi=-\rho\nabla\varphi.

(33)

The first equality is an identity, but the second one requires the validity of the Poisson equation.
While eq. (
31
) mathematically resembles the standard boundary-traction equivalence of a Cauchy stress tensor, it represents a deeper constraint, especially when viewed through the lens of weakly nonlocal thermodynamics as a constitutive property of the perfect fluid pressure tensor. In this framework, the energy balances discussed here are simplifications of a fundamental entropic formulation. For an ideal, dissipation-free continuous field, that is for perfect fluids, the vanishing bulk entropy production defines the pressure and the classical holographic property emerges as a consequence of zero dissipation
[
36
,
37
]
.

Consequently, the thermodynamic information required to specify the bulk equations of motion—specifically, the emergence of the Poisson equation—is entirely encoded by the boundary fluxes
[
36
]
. This reduction of bulk thermodynamic degrees of freedom to boundary information provides a non-relativistic, classical analogue to the holographic principle
[
39
,
40
]
.

However, it is crucial to distinguish this classical thermodynamic holography from its quantum counterpart. While the thermodynamic state and macroscopic constraints are boundary-bound, the kinematic evolution of the fluid—the specific micro-arrangements of the density field
\rho({\bf x},t)
and momentum density field
\rho({\bf x},t){\bf v}({\bf x},t)
—remains strictly extensive. This macroscopic-microscopic duality mirrors Susskind’s complexity argument
[
41
]
, which posits that while a system’s entropy (information) is bounded by its boundary area, the computational complexity required to specify its exact internal dynamical state continues to scale linearly with the three-dimensional bulk volume. Thus, the classical weakly nonlocal fluid is thermodynamically holographic, yet kinematically volumetric.

5.
Gravitational energy balances: a continuum approach

The evolution of any field quantity in continuum physics, therefore the corresponding energy balance of any scalar field, is restricted by the Second Law of Thermodynamics. The obtained thermodynamic restrictions are particularly remarkable in the marginal case of ideal continua, with zero dissipation. If the entropy depends on the gradient of the field, then the second law restrictions lead to similar consequences as one could obtain from a Hamiltonian variational principle. 6.

Balances are considered secondary in theories of physics, the primary concepts are the field equations. Moreover, the energy density alone determines the other parts of the balance. The current density and the source term, the dissipated energy can be calculated in nonequilibrium thermodynamics. The simplest relevant method separates the currents from the source; therefore, it is called

\displaystyle\rho\left(\varphi+\frac{(\nabla\varphi)^{2}}{2\rho}\right)^{\bf\cdot}=\rho\dot{\varphi}+\rho\frac{(\nabla\varphi)^{2}}{2}\left(-\frac{\dot{\rho}}{\rho^{2}}\right)+\nabla\varphi(\nabla\varphi\dot{)}=

\displaystyle=\nabla\cdot\left[\dot{\varphi}\nabla\varphi\right]+\nabla{\bf v}:\left[\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}{\bf I}-\nabla\varphi\circ\nabla\varphi\right]+\dot{\varphi}(\rho-\Delta\varphi).

Here, the first term, the total divergence identifies an energy current density. The second term is the power due to the bracketed gravitational pressure, which is the source term of the energy balance. The last term,
\dot{\varphi}(\rho-\Delta\varphi)

Thermodynamic reasoning proves that in an ideal system, without dissipation, this term is zero and the Poisson equation follows — the system is forced on-shell by the Second Law. The balance above is not the reason; it is a consequence.

Therefore we have obtained the substantial balance of gravitational energy (

7.
Summary and discussion

Balances are characteristic of classical field theories and continuum theories. An analysis of various energy densities and energy balances of Newtonian gravitation leads to the following conclusions.

(1)

The energy balances of Ohanian, Maxwell and Bondi are equivalent from a physical point of view: they differ only by the Green balance (
52
), the time derivative of the Green identity read as a conservation law. This equivalence holds locally, independently of any particular boundary conditions at spatial infinity.

(2)

The bulk force power and the surface traction power source terms of the gravitational energy balance are equivalent because of the classical holographic property (
31
).

(3)

The distinction of Eulerian and Lagrangian representations, the local and substantial forms is a key aspect for transforming the energy current density terms and for understanding the role of the gravitational displacement current density. One must distinguish between forms where the mass balance is the condition and when the Poisson equation is a that.

Among the energy densities (and balances) the Ohanian form seems distinguished. It is independently supported by three different lines of reasoning. From the thermodynamic point of view, it emerges naturally from the Second Law analysis of a weakly nonlocal scalar field
[
16
,
37
]
. None of the other energy densities can be used in the thermodynamic theory (see Appendix (
A
)).
From classical Lagrangian field theory, its opposite serves as the gravitational Lagrangian density from which the Euler–Lagrange equation yields the Poisson equation
[
15
,
9
,
13
]
(Appendix (
C
)).

The coincidence between the thermodynamic and the variational (Lagrangian) selection of the Ohanian energy density is not accidental. Both rely on the off-shell structure: the matter-field coupling term
\rho\varphi
in
\varepsilon_{0}
is essential for generating the Poisson equation, whether through the Euler–Lagrange equation or through thermodynamic divergence separation. The Green balance freedom, which mediates between the on-shell equivalent energy balances, cannot create or destroy this coupling term, since off-shell it is a pure mathematical identity involving
\varphi\Delta\varphi
, not
\rho\varphi
. Thus, the gauge-like freedom of the balances operates at a different (on-shell) level than the generating principle (off-shell), and the Second Law acts as a gauge-fixing that selects the unique generator.

The
Green balance
(
52
), the time derivative of the Green identity read as a sourceless conservation law. It represents a gauge-like freedom in the balance structure, distinct from the gauge ambiguity of the gravitational potential discussed by Dewar & Weatherall
[
9
]
: their ambiguity resides in the field variable (
\varphi\to\varphi+f(t)
), whereas here the freedom acts at the level of the energy balance itself. Adding
a
times the Green balance to the Ohanian balance redistributes energy between density and current without affecting the source term. Off-shell, the Green identity is purely mathematical; only when the Poisson equation holds can its terms be identified as the difference of physical energy densities,

\varepsilon_{0}-\varepsilon_{1}=\rho\varphi+(\nabla\varphi)^{2}=\nabla\cdot(\varphi\nabla\varphi)
,
rendering them equivalent upon integration. The thermodynamic approach
does not presuppose this on-shell condition but derives it: the Second
Law, applied to the Ohanian energy density via divergence separation,
forces
\Delta\varphi=\rho
as the dissipation-free limit. In this sense,
the Second Law acts as a physical gauge-fixing principle that selects the
Ohanian balance from the family of equivalent alternatives.

A more complete four-dimensional energy-momentum balance in Galilean relativistic continuum theory may provide further insight, in particular regarding this gauge-like aspect as a hidden feature of balance-based continuum theories. However, the covariant energy balance in the Galilean relativistic framework is surprisingly complex and conceptually different from the traditional scalar formula: the kinetic energy becomes a relative transformation property due to the four-tensor transformation properties of the energy-momentum tensor
[
38
]
. This direction remains open for future investigation.

Appendix A
The general balance of energy

In the field theoretic framework of nonequilibrium thermodynamics there is a methodology for constructing evolution equations. It was developed for modeling properties of dissipative and perfect materials with the help of internal variables
[
44
]
. The evolution equations cannot violate the Second Law of Thermodynamics and must be compatible with any given constraints, including the fundamental balances. The simplest version of the methodology was introduced by de Groot and Mazur
[
42
]
. Here, the method is applied directly to construct the energy balance, without the thermodynamic background, as it is extracted and simplified from the calculations of
[
37
]
.

The key of connecting the mechanical and thermodynamic concepts is internal energy. In general entropy is the function of the total energy and the various other field. Internal energy is defined by subtracting the identified energies from the total energy. Internal energy that is distributed evenly in thermodynamic equilibrium considering all other constraints. If the momentum balance and gravitational field equation are the constraints, then one subtracts the kinetic energy and also the energy of gravity from the total energy and obtains the internal energy. In classical irreversible thermodynamics the difference of the total, conserved energy and the kinetic energy gives the classical form of the internal energy. Then the entropy depends on this classical internal energy
e
, and also on the other field variables in a special way:

\rho_{s}(\rho_{tote},\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi)=\hat{\rho}_{s}(\rho_{tote}-\varepsilon(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi),\rho)
where
\varepsilon
denotes the energy density of the gravitation. Then it is clear, how the entropy balance is connected to the energy balance
[
45
,
37
]
.

Then let us consider the specific (
i.e.
, per unit mass) energy
e=\frac{\varepsilon}{\rho}
as a general function of the gravitational potential, its gradient and the mass density

e=e(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi):=\frac{\varepsilon(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi)}{\rho}

(61)

Now we restrict ourselves calculate its balance, separating the surface and production terms, where the compatibility with the balance of mass is required. First, the mass balance is considered in the following substantial form:

\displaystyle\dot{\rho}+\rho\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}=0.

(62)

We expand the differential of
e
as

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}e=\partial_{\rho}e\mathrm{d}\rho+\partial_{\varphi}e\mathrm{d}\varphi+\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e\mathrm{d}(\nabla\varphi),

(63)

where the coefficients of the differentials are the following partial derivatives:

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}e:=\left.\frac{\partial e}{\partial\rho}\right|_{\varphi,\nabla\varphi},

\displaystyle\partial_{\varphi}e:=\left.\frac{\partial e}{\partial\varphi}\right|_{\rho,\nabla\varphi},

\displaystyle\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e:=\left.\frac{\partial e}{\partial(\nabla\varphi)}\right|_{\rho,\varphi}.

Calculating the substantial time derivative of the specific energy
\dot{\varepsilon}
, the following familiar balance form emerges:

\rho\dot{e}+\nabla\cdot\mathbf{j}_{e}=-\mathbf{P}:\nabla\mathbf{v}.

(64)

Thus, we rewrite
\rho\dot{e}
using the above equations with this derivation:

\displaystyle\rho\dot{e}=\rho\partial_{\rho}e\dot{\rho}+\rho\partial_{\varphi}e\dot{\varphi}+\rho\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e(\nabla\varphi)^{\cdot}=

(65)

\displaystyle=-\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}e\nabla\cdot\mathbf{v}+\rho\partial_{\varphi}e\dot{\varphi}+\rho\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e(\nabla\dot{\varphi}-(\nabla\varphi)\cdot(\nabla\mathbf{v}))=

\displaystyle=-\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}e\mathbf{I}:(\nabla\mathbf{v})+\rho\partial_{\varphi}e\dot{\varphi}+\rho\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e\cdot(\nabla\dot{\varphi})-\rho(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e)(\nabla\varphi):(\nabla\mathbf{v}).

Therefore, with some additional rearrangements, the following general form is obtained

\displaystyle\rho\dot{e}+\nabla\cdot\left[-\rho\dot{\varphi}\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e\right]=

\displaystyle\qquad\dot{\varphi}(\rho\partial_{\varphi}e-\nabla\cdot(\rho\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e))-\left[\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}e\mathbf{I}+\rho(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e)(\nabla\varphi)\right]:(\nabla\mathbf{v}).

(66)

Then, the last term is the mechanical dissipation. In the penultimate term, one may recognize a functional derivative as the coefficient of the substantial time derivative of the gravitational potential, and the current density of the balance is also identified.

Alternatively, instead of using the substantial time derivative, one can perform similar calculations with partial time derivatives and obtain the local form of the energy balance (now using the local form of mass balance, eq. (
2
), as well):

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho e)=\partial_{\rho}(\rho e)\partial_{t}\rho+\partial_{\varphi}(\rho e)\partial_{t}{\varphi}+\partial_{\nabla\varphi}(\rho e)\partial_{t}(\nabla\varphi)=

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\rho}(\rho e)\nabla\cdot(\rho\mathbf{v})+\partial_{\varphi}(\rho e)\partial_{t}{\varphi}+\nabla\cdot(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}(\rho e)\partial_{t}\varphi)-\nabla\cdot(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}(\rho e))\partial_{t}\varphi=

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}(\rho e)\partial_{t}\varphi-\rho\mathbf{v}\partial_{\rho}(\rho e))+(\partial_{\varphi}(\rho e)-\nabla\cdot(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}(\rho e))\partial_{t}\varphi+\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\cdot\partial_{\rho}(\rho e).

Regrouping the terms one obtains

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\rho e)+\nabla\cdot\left[\rho\partial_{\rho}(\rho e)\mathbf{v}-\rho\partial_{t}\varphi\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e\right]=\partial_{t}{\varphi}\left(\rho\partial_{\varphi}e-\nabla\cdot(\rho\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e)\right)+\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla(\partial_{\rho}(\rho e)),

(67)

as the general form of eq. (
36
) with extra terms. Here the coefficient of the partial time derivative of
\varphi
is the functional derivative of the energy density
\rho e
and the last term is the power of the force density
\rho\nabla(\partial_{\rho}(\rho e))
. It is straightforward to check that (
67
) is identical to (
66
), because

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\left[\rho^{2}\partial_{\rho}e\mathbf{I}+\rho(\partial_{\nabla\varphi}e)(\nabla\varphi)\right]=\rho\nabla(\partial_{\rho}(\rho e))

(68)

The divergence of the gravitational pressure equals the power of the force density: the classical holographic property in general form.

The most important, and somewhat hidden property of the equations above is that the last but one term in the balances (
66
) and (
67
), represents the off-shell dissipation. The on-shell form, with the field equation defined by the zero functional derivative
\delta_{\varphi}e=0
, represents zero dissipation. The thermodynamic argument shows that it is a dissipative term. Therefore, if gravity is nondissipative, the term must be zero, regardless of whether the gravitational potential is increasing or decreasing. We refer to
[
37
]
regarding the detailed justification.

Now we specialize the specific energy (
61
) to the cases coreesponding to the gravitational energy densities
discussed in the main text:
Maxwell’s
\varepsilon_{1}
eq.(
11
), Ohanian’s
\varepsilon_{0}
eq.(
12
), Bondi’s
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
eq.(
13
).
In the Ohanian case, with

e_{0}(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi):=\frac{\varepsilon_{0}(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi)}{\rho}:=\frac{\rho\varphi+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}}{\rho},

(69)

substituted in eq.(
66
) we obtain the following form:

\rho\dot{e}_{0}+\nabla\cdot\left[-\dot{\varphi}\nabla\varphi\right]=\dot{\varphi}(\rho-\Delta\varphi)-\left[(\nabla\varphi)(\nabla\varphi)-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\mathbf{I}\right]:(\nabla\mathbf{v}).

(70)

Alternatively, with the local form from eq. (

\partial_{t}\left(\rho e_{0}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[\rho\varphi\mathbf{v}-\partial_{t}\varphi\nabla\varphi\right]=\partial_{t}\varphi\left(\rho-\Delta\varphi\right)+\rho\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\varphi.

(71)

So we have obtained the off-shell form of (

Instead, using the specific Maxwell’s energy, with

e_{1}(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi):=\frac{\varepsilon_{1}(\nabla\varphi)}{\rho}:=\frac{-\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}}{\rho},

(72)

substituted in eq. (

\rho\dot{e}_{1}+\nabla\cdot\left[\dot{\varphi}\nabla\varphi\right]=\dot{\varphi}\Delta\varphi-\left[\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\mathbf{I}-(\nabla\varphi)(\nabla\varphi)\right]:(\nabla\mathbf{v}),

(73)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\left(\rho{e}_{1}\right)+\nabla\cdot\left[\partial_{t}\varphi\nabla\varphi\right]=\partial_{t}\varphi\Delta\varphi.

(74)

One can see that the energy balances of the gravitational energy with the Maxwell energy density (

The Bondi case, with

e_{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi):=\frac{\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}(\rho,\varphi)}{\rho}:=\frac{+\frac{1}{2}\rho\varphi}{\rho}=\frac{1}{2}\varphi

(77)

It is even more strange than the Maxwell balance. None of them

Appendix B
About the generalisation of the Green balance

One can start with the following identity

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\nabla\cdot\bf{q}=\nabla\cdot\partial_{t}\bf{q}

(78)

where
\bf{q}
is a contravariant vector field.
The upper convected time derivative of
\bf q
is

\displaystyle\bf{q}^{\nabla}=\partial_{t}\bf q+{\bf v}\cdot\nabla\bf{q}-\bf{q}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}=\dot{\bf q}-\bf{q}\cdot\nabla{\bf v}

(79)

The Truesdellian time derivative of
\bf q
is
[
46
]

\displaystyle{\bf q}^{\diamond}:={\bf q}^{\nabla}+{\bf q}\nabla\cdot{\bf v}

(80)

Then it is easy to see, that (
78
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle(\nabla\cdot{\bf q}\dot{)}-\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{\nabla}-{\bf q}\cdot\nabla(\nabla\cdot{\bf v})=\rho\left(\frac{\nabla\cdot{\bf q}}{\rho}\right)^{\cdot}-\nabla\cdot{\bf q}^{\diamond}=0.

(81)

In index notation (with upper an lower indexes denoting the contravariant and covariant components respectively):

\displaystyle(\partial_{i}q^{i})^{\cdot}-\partial_{i}\left(\dot{q}^{i}-q^{k}\partial_{k}v^{i}\right)-q^{i}\partial_{i}(\partial_{k}v^{k})=\rho\left(\frac{\partial_{i}q^{i}}{\rho}\right)^{\cdot}-\partial_{i}\left(\dot{q}^{i}-q^{k}\partial_{k}v^{i}+q^{i}\partial_{k}v^{k}\right)=0.

(82)

Here the continuity equation was used.

Appendix C
Ohanian’s energy density vs Lagrangian field theory

Remarkably,
Ohanian’s energy density (
12
) equals (the opposite of)

\mathcal{L}_{g}
, the well-known ”gravitational” Lagrangian density
(see e.g.
[
9
]
eq.(1),
[
12
]
Sect.1.4.0,
or
[
15
]
Sect.II.B):

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{g}(\varphi,\nabla\varphi|\rho):=-\left(\varphi\rho+\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}\right)=-\varepsilon_{0}(\rho,\varphi,\nabla\varphi)

(83)

Substituting
\mathcal{L}_{g}
in the Euler-Lagrange equation:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial\varphi}=\nabla\cdot\left(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial(\nabla\varphi)}\right)

(84)

gives the two field equations of Newtonian Gravitation, namely
the Gauss-Poisson eq.(
4
) and the irrotationality condition condition (
5
).

In the usual physical interpretation,
the pure gravitational term
\frac{1}{2}(\nabla\varphi)^{2}
describes the gravitational field ”on its own”,
while the coupling” term
\rho\varphi
describes the ”inter-action” of matter
\rho
and gravity
\varphi
.
However, note that in
\mathcal{L}_{g}
in (
83
)
one is assuming a mass density distribution
\rho({\bf x},t)
=fixed, given a priori,
regarded as a ”parametric variable (function)”, but not as a dynamically variable field itself.
Indeed, the partial derivatives of
\mathcal{L}_{g}
in (
84
) are only w.r.t.
the fields
\varphi
and
\nabla\varphi
, but not w.r.t.
\rho
.
Therefore, in this context,
modifying
\rho
”by hand” obviously modifies the resulting
\varphi
,
instead varying
\varphi
to satisfy the Euler-Lagrange equation does not modify
\rho
already fixed a priori.
So to say: in this context
the ”inter-action” is only ”action” (the generation of gravitation from matter, i.e. the Gauss-Poisson field equation)
but there is no corresponding ”back-reaction” (the equations of motions of matter under the force of gravity).
Paraphrasing a famous statement usually attributed to John Archibald Wheeler:
”matter tells gravity how to spread in space, but gravity does not (yet) tell matter how to move”.

The l.h.s. partial derivative
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial\varphi}

is sometimes called the canonical (Lagrangian, generalized) force corresponding
to the canonical (Lagrangian, generalized) coordinate
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{F}_{\varphi}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial\varphi}=-\rho

(85)

while the r.h.s. partial derivative
\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial(\nabla\varphi)}

is called the canonical (Lagrangian, generalized) momentum conjugated to
\varphi
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}_{\varphi}:=\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}_{g}}{\partial(\nabla\varphi)}=-\nabla\varphi=+{\bf g}

(86)

Substituting (
85
) and (
86
) into (
84
)
gives the Gauss-Poisson field equation (
4
):

\displaystyle-\rho=\nabla\cdot(-\nabla\varphi)=+\nabla\cdot{\bf g}

(87)

The other field equation (
5
) of NG, the irrotationality condition
\nabla\times{\bf g}=0
,
is already implied by:
(i) the physical assumption of
\varphi

as an appropriate field ”coordinate” in (
84
),
(ii) the mathematical definition (
1
) of
{\bf g}=-\nabla\varphi

as an ”auxiliary” vector field,
derived from the more fundamental scalar field
\varphi
, ass assumed in (i), and
(iii) the mathematical vector identity
\nabla\times\nabla\equiv 0
.

Note that, if one chooses an arbitrary energy density
\varepsilon_{a}

(among those compatible with the energy balances) in place of
\varepsilon_{0}
,
and then repeats the above steps from (
83
) to (
87
), one gets instead:

\displaystyle-(1-a)\rho=+(1-2a)\nabla\cdot{\bf g}

(88)

Comparing this ”Poisson-like” equation (
88
)
with the true Poisson equation (
87
),
and assuming in general
\rho\not=0
and
\nabla\cdot{\bf g}\not=0
,
gives
1-a=1-2a
, with unique remaining solution
a=0
.
In particular, Maxwell’s
\varepsilon_{1}
gives
0=\nabla\cdot{\bf g}

(which might apply to the gravitational field
{\bf g}
, but only in empty space
\rho=0
)
and Bondi’s
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
gives
-\frac{1}{2}\rho=0

(which also applies only to empty space, but furthermore it is totally silent about the gravitational field
{\bf g}
).
So, in this context Ohanian’s energy density
\varepsilon_{0}
is definitely distinguished,
because it emerges immediately (with no other assumptions) from the initial expression for
\mathcal{L}_{g}=-\varepsilon_{0}
,
while its two direct rivals
\varepsilon_{1}
and
\varepsilon_{\frac{1}{2}}
perform rather poorly.
