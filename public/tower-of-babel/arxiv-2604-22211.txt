A data-driven model reduction approach for backward fractional diffusion-wave equations
Dakang Cen, Zhiyuan Li, Wenlong Zhang

Abstract
In this work, we propose an observation system based on the available data which solution is one-be-one mapping to the forward problem(with the unknown initial function) solution. It implies their solutions share the same linear structure in the finite dimensional space. Theoretical results show model reduction approaches constructed for the observation system also work well for the forward problem, which significantly improve the efficiency of solving the inverse problem. Several numerical examples are presented to support our finding.



Keywords—
backward fractional wave, model reduction method, inverse crime

Introduction

Assuming that
\alpha\in(1,2)
and that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

, we consider the following fractional wave equation:

\begin{cases}\partial_{t}^{\alpha}u-\Delta u=f(x,t),&(x,t)\in\Omega\times(0,T),\\
u(x,0)=a_{0}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
\frac{\partial}{\partial t}u(x,0)=a_{1}(x),&x\in\Omega,\\
u(x,t)=0,&(x,t)\in\partial\Omega\times(0,T),\end{cases}

(1.1)

where the operator

is referred to as the Caputo derivative of order

, defined by

\partial_{t}^{\alpha}\psi(t):=\frac{1}{\Gamma(2-\alpha)}\int_{0}^{t}(t-\tau)^{1-\alpha}\psi^{\prime\prime}(\tau)d\tau,\hskip 9.24994ptt>0.

In our previous work
[
1
,
2
]
, we investigated recovering

from the terminal measurement of (
1.1
). We established stability of the inversion and introduced a scattered point measurement-based regularization method. The optimal error estimates not only balance discretization error, the noise, and the number of observation points, but also propose an a priori choice of regularization parameters. The iteration optimization method is used to find the true initial value of the backward problem. It has to solve the forward diffusion-wave equation one or two times in each iteration. However, (
1.1
) is a non-local model, the current solution
u(t_{k})
,
k\in\mathbb{N}^{+}
depends on all previous solutions
u(t_{i})
,
i=0,\cdots,k-1
. The computation time is much longer than that for traditional wave equations, especially for high-dimensional problems.

This prompts us to consider how we could save time on calculations. One of the straightforward ideas is to improve the efficiency of solving forward problems. In our previous work, space discretization is based on the finite element method. The computational time grows rapidly as the sizes of discrete problems increase.
Inspired by
[
4
,
10
]
, we investigate the model reduction low-rank approximation by the proper orthogonal decomposition (POD) method. The so-called data-driven approach is to train a set of POD basis functions based on the observation data of the inverse problem to achieve a significant dimension reduction in the solution space. It does not rely on the prior information of the solution, which avoids the inverse crime.

3
Proper orthogonal decomposition

In this section, we investigate the approximation properties of solutions in the POD space generated by the observation system.
Our aim is to derive an error estimate for the solution of the forward problem and its corresponding POD space that snapshots come from the observation system (
2.6
) but not the forward problem.

First, we recall the general framework of POD. For
N\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}\subset X:=H_{0}^{1}(\Omega)
be an ensemble of snapshots. The POD basis functions
\{\psi_{j}\}_{j=1}^{r}
are constructed by minimizing the following projection error:

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|y_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(y_{n},\psi_{j})_{X}\psi_{j}\|_{X}^{2}.

On the other hand, it can be reduced to the following eigenvalue problem:

Kv=\lambda v,

where the correlation matrix
K
is calculated from the snapshots
\{y_{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
with the entries
K_{ij}=\frac{1}{N}(y_{i},y_{j})_{X}
, and
K
is symmetric and semi-positive definite. The corresponding approximation error is presented
[
8
]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{r}>0
be the positive eigenvalues of the correlation matrix
K
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{r}\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
be the corresponding orthonormal eigenvectors. Then a POD basis of rank
m\leq r
is given by

\psi_{j}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda_{j}}}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(v_{j})^{n}y_{n},

where
(v_{j})^{n}
denotes the
n
-th component of the eigenvector
v_{j}
. Moreover, the error is given by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|y_{n}-\sum_{j=1}^{r}(y_{n},\psi_{j})_{X}\psi_{j}\|_{X}^{2}=\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}R_{h}^{m}\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n},\varphi_{m})+(\nabla\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})-(q(x)\omega_{2-\alpha}(t_{n}),\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}(R_{h}^{m}\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n}-\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n}),\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle-(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{n},\varphi_{m}).

Substituting
\varphi_{m}=\theta_{v}^{n}
into the above equation, it gives that

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\theta_{v}^{n},\theta_{v}^{n})+(\nabla\theta_{u}^{n},\nabla\theta_{v}^{n})=-(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{n},\theta_{v}^{n}).

(3.4)

Furthermore, one has

\displaystyle(\nabla\theta_{v}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle=(\nabla R_{h}^{m}\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})-(\nabla\widetilde{V}_{m}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle:=(\nabla\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})-(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\widetilde{U}_{m}^{n},\nabla\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle=(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla(\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n}-\widetilde{U}_{m}^{n}),\nabla\varphi_{m})

\displaystyle=(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla(\rho_{u}^{n}+\theta_{u}^{n}),\nabla\varphi_{m}).

Substituting
\varphi_{m}=\theta_{u}^{n}
into the above equation, we have

\displaystyle(\nabla\theta_{v}^{n},\nabla\theta_{u}^{n})=(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{n},\nabla\theta_{u}^{n})+(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\theta_{u}^{n},\nabla\theta_{u}^{n}).

(3.5)

For (
3.5
), from (
3.4
), we get

\displaystyle(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\theta_{v}^{n},\theta_{v}^{n})+(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\theta_{u}^{n},\nabla\theta_{u}^{n})=-(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{n},\nabla\theta_{u}^{n})-(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{n},\theta_{v}^{n}).

(3.6)

From Lemma
3.2
, for (
3.6
), we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\|\theta_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\|\nabla\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\|\theta_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}})(\|\nabla\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\theta_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}).

Lemma
3.3
gives that

\displaystyle\|\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\big(\|\theta_{v}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\nabla\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\big)

\displaystyle\leq C\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}),~1\leq n\leq N.

Suppose
\max_{1\leq k\leq n}\sum_{j=1}^{k}P_{k-j}^{(k)}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}})=\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}P_{n_{0}-j}^{({n_{0}})}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}})
,
n_{0}\leq n
, from Lemma
3.4
, one has

\displaystyle\|\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}P_{n_{0}-j}^{({n_{0}})}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}})\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n_{0}}\tau_{j}^{\alpha/2}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}})\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha/2}(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}})\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla\rho_{u}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\rho_{v}^{j}\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq CnN^{1-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

Then, we have

\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CN^{-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}\sum_{n=1}^{N}n\leq CN^{2-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

(3.7)

Combining (
3.3
) and (
3.7
), the error between
e_{m}^{n}=\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n}-\widetilde{U}_{m}^{n}
is given

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n}-\widetilde{U}_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{2}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}(\|\rho_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\theta_{u}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2})\leq CN^{2-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

∎

3.2
POD approach for the forward problem

Recall Remark
2.2
, we consider the solution of the forward system approximated by a finite-dimensional truncation. For
U_{h}^{n}\approx\sum_{k=1}^{L}(U_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}
, there exists a positive constant
\varepsilon
such that
\|U_{h}^{n}-\sum_{k=1}^{L}(U_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\|_{L^{2}}<\epsilon
,
1\leq n\leq N
. Under the assumption that

is small enough, the error estimate between the solution in the finite element space (
3.8
) and its in the POD space (
3.9
) is derived following the idea of Theorem
1
.
A fully discrete scheme is as follows: find
U_{h}^{n}
,
V_{h}^{n}\in X_{h}
for
n=1
, 2,
\cdots
,
N

\begin{cases}(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}V_{h}^{n},\varphi)+(\nabla U_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi)=(a_{1}(x)\omega_{2-\alpha}(t_{n}),\varphi),&\forall\varphi\in X_{h},\\
(\nabla V_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi)=(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla U_{h}^{n},\nabla\varphi),\end{cases}

(3.8)

where

,

,

,

(\nabla V_{m}^{n},\nabla\varphi)=(\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla U_{m}^{n},\nabla\varphi).\end{cases}

(3.9)

Remark 3.1
.

The POD space

in (

Theorem 2
.

Let
U_{h}^{n}
and
U_{m}^{n}
be the solutions of (
3.8
) and (
3.9
). Then there holds

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CL^{4/d+2}N^{2-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

Proof.

Repeating the process in Theorem
1
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|U_{h}^{n}-R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(3.10)

Let
I_{1}:=\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|U_{h}^{n}-R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
and
I_{2}:=\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
.
For the term
I_{1}
, from Remark
2.1
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|U_{h}^{n}-R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigg\|(I-R_{h}^{m})\sum_{k=1}^{L}(U_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigg\|(I-R_{h}^{m})\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{(a_{1})_{k}}{q_{k}}(\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\max_{k}\bigg|\frac{(a_{1})_{k}}{q_{k}}\bigg|^{2}L\sum_{k=1}^{L}\bigg\|(I-R_{h}^{m})(\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{N}L^{4/d+1}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\bigg\|(I-R_{h}^{m})(\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{4/d+1}\sum_{k=1}^{L}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\bigg\|(I-R_{h}^{m})(\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n},\phi_{k})\phi_{k}\bigg\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{4/d+2}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

(3.11)

Similar to (
3.4
)-(
3.7
) and (
3.11
), for
I_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\|R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\bigg(\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha/2}\big(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla(U_{h}^{j}-R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{j})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}(V_{h}^{j}-R_{h}^{m}V_{h}^{j})\|_{L^{2}}\big)\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\bigg(\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\nabla(U_{h}^{j}-R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{j})\|_{L^{2}}+\|\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}(V_{h}^{j}-R_{h}^{m}V_{h}^{j})\|_{L^{2}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{4/d+2}N\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}\sum_{j=1}^{n}\tau_{j}^{\alpha}

\displaystyle\leq CL^{4/d+2}nN^{1-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j},

then it gives

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\|R_{h}^{m}U_{h}^{n}-U_{m}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq CL^{4/d+2}N^{2-\alpha}\sum_{j=m+1}^{r}\lambda_{j}.

∎

4
Numerical algorithm for the backward problem

In this section, we propose an algorithm for quickly reconstructing initial values based on data-driven ideas. The core lies in training a low-rank approximation space(POD space) for the forward system, based on observational data. Reconstructing the initial values in the POD space can improve computational efficiency.

First step.
Perform quasi-uniform sampling on the observations of
q(x)
over the entire region to obtain discrete observational data
\{q(x_{i})\}_{i=1}^{m}
.

Second step.
Perform data mollification, we obtain regularized data
q^{r}
(For details, refer to section
2.2
).

Third step.
Solve the observation system with
q^{r}
(
3.1
) to obtain snapshots, and construct a POD space
X_{h}^{m}
.

Fourth step.
Solve the following minimization problem (
4.1
):

\displaystyle a_{pod}=\arg\min\limits_{a_{1}\in X_{h}^{m}}\frac{1}{m}\sum_{i=1}^{m}|(Sa_{1})(x_{i})-q(x_{i})|^{2}+\lambda\|a_{1}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

(4.1)

where
S
is the forward operator
Sa_{1}\rightarrow u(T)
.

Unlike
[
1
,
2
]
, we recover the initial function in a low-dimensional intrinsic space based on the model but not in the finite element space. The choice of
\lambda
, we refer to
[
1
]
.

Remark 4.1
.

In previous sections, we give the estimates for POD approaches. Specifically, we take fully discrete solutions
\{\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
and fractional difference quotients
\{\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\widetilde{U}_{h}^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}
,
\{\bar{\partial}_{t}^{\nu}\widetilde{V}_{h}^{n}\}_{n=1}^{N}

5
Concluding remarks

A model reduction approach is proposed based on measurement data. It recovers the initial value in much less time and avoids the inverse crime. It provides a fast algorithm idea to improve the efficiency of solving the linear inverse problems based on the iteration optimization method.
