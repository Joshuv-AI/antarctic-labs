1. Executive Summary
This project is an optimization and adaptation of nascent capability developed at Los Alamos National
Laboratory (LANL) to measure trace levels of 238Pu by isotope dilution thermal ionization mass
spectrometry (id-TIMS). The research supports the following priorities laid out in the IAEA Development
and Implementation Support Programme for Nuclear Veriﬁcation 2024-2025, STR-405 (D&ISP):

 • SGAS-002, priority T.2.C8: “Ability to deliver timely environmental sample analysis results…by
 requesting additional capacity from existing NWAL.” LANL is a current NWAL laboratory
 measuring environmental samples, using alpha counting for 238Pu data if/when requested.
 Implementation of a new TIMS method to collect 238Pu data alongside all other Pu isotopes
 would reduce the requirement for duplicate chemistry eﬀorts and lengthy count times. This
 research improves the capacity of LANL to provide more and faster sample analysis with current
 resources.
 • SGA-003, priorities 1 and 2: “Provide timely, accurate analysis of ES and NM samples by the
 NWAL,” and “Expand the overall capacity of NWAL for the analysis of environmental samples
 while maintaining high-quality analysis.” Duplicate chemistry requirements and maintenance of
 two separate measurement systems can be eliminated by incorporating 238Pu measurement into
 the existing mass spectrometry analysis stream for NWAL environmental samples.

This research also supports the following priorities laid out in the document IAEA Enhancing Capabilities
for Nuclear Veriﬁcation (formerly known as the R&D Plan), STR-399, January 2022:

 • Focus Area T.2.C1: “Ability to reliably and quickly deliver sample analysis results for special and
 high priority demands.”

Because 238Pu measurement by mass spectrometry is impacted by the presence of naturally occurring
238
 U background, it was not historically measured by this method but instead was measured by alpha
spectrometry. However, alpha spectrometry cannot be used to measure trace level 239, 240, 241, 242Pu due to
low speciﬁc activity (and lack of alpha decay in the case of 241Pu), and mass spectrometry must be
utilized for those isotopes to achieve the detection limits needed. The use of two diﬀerent analytical
techniques requires samples that are large enough to split and measure twice (or measure ﬁrst by alpha
spectrometry, repurify the Pu from the counted planchette, and then measure it by TIMS), which is a
time-consuming and labor-intensive process. Furthermore, given the 87.7-year half-life of 238Pu (NuDat
3.0) 1, alpha spectrometry count times for low-level samples (fg – 100s of fg level) can be on the order of
weeks to months to meet uncertainty requirements (generally < 10%), whereas a TIMS measurement
would take around 1 hour (post-chemistry in both cases).

A few advancements applied in this work now make the TIMS measurement feasible: 1) Multi-collector
TIMS instruments that can monitor both U and Pu simultaneously, 2) The addition of 235U tracer to the
measured sample (after chemically removing the sample’s U content) for monitoring the ingrowth of

cycle of collected TIMS data, and 4) Cleaner resin beads and better-controlled U background.

Results of this work show good accuracy and precision of 238Pu down to ~5 femtograms (fg), with
increasing uncertainty below that value as the detection limit is approached. The critical level, Lc, is used
in this work as the detection limit and has been calculated at 0.6 fg, or 0.38 mBq, 238Pu. This lower limit
combined with the upper limit for 239Pu of ion counting detectors constrains the range of measured
238

2. Background
The 238Pu TIMS measurement capability was ﬁrst explored and developed in a 2-year project funded by
LANL Laboratory-Directed Research and Development (LDRD) funds. 2 The current project broadened and
adapted the original study to bulk environmental swipe collections from the Network of Analytical
Laboratory (NWAL). Archived NWAL samples previously measured at LANL were used for the
demonstration. This project also included measurements of process blanks for detection limit
determination, puriﬁed Pu isotopic standards (NIST CRM-136 and -137), and environmentally collected
matrix-containing reference materials (NIST 4357, Ocean Sediment). An additional reference material,
IAEA-385 Irish Sea Sediment, was planned, but not executed due to a late project start (funding arrived
in December for the ﬁscal year). This project was divided into 4 Tasks, per the Plan of Work (POW):

 • Task 1: Sample Selection
 • Task 2: Chemical Pu Puriﬁcation
 • Task 3: TIMS Routine Development
 • Task 4: Sample Measurement and Evaluation

Each of the tasks is discussed below.

3. Task 1
Sample selection included a mix of blanks, puriﬁed standards, reference materials representing real-
world environmental matrix, and safeguards swipes. Samples were chosen to demonstrate the relative
accuracy and precision achievable for low-level samples, as well as the favorability of the method to
safeguard swipes, particularly to higher burnup Pu signatures. Because some samples were previously
measured 3 (blanks, IAEA-384, MAPEP proﬁciency test soils and waters, and surrogate nuclear debris),
this study included both lower-concentration, lower 238Pu/239Pu ratio samples as well as higher level,
higher 238Pu/239Pu ratio samples than the previous study, widening the evaluated range of sample types.
This study also accumulated more data to establish blank and standard control charts that better
characterize the accuracy and precision of the method. Finally, a set of 3 NWAL swipes were chosen to
demonstrate the applicability of this measurement technique to that sample type.

4. Task 2:
Samples required dissolution in some cases (NIST 4357 and IAEA-385) but were previously dissolved and
required only chemical puriﬁcation in other cases (blanks, CRMs, NWAL COC 127). For sediments, 10g
aliquots of powder were dissolved to avoid inhomogeneity between replicates due to under sampling.
Samples are ﬁrst ashed in a muﬄe furnace to remove organics and then undergo hotplate dissolution
with nitric acid/hydroﬂuoric acid/perchloric acid to remove silica and digest minerals. A stock solution of
the dissolved sediment is then made in hydrochloric acid at a concentration of ~10 mg sediment / g
solution. NIST 4357 and IAEA 385 were ashed and dissolved from January 19 to January 29, 2026.

 Page 4 of 14

Aliquots are taken from the stock solution for chemical puriﬁcation, with aliquot sizes dependent on the
Pu content. In the case of NIST 4357, aliquots ranged from 1 g sediment to 1.5 g sediment. IAEA-385,
which has lower Pu content, will require 3 g sediment to bring the 238Pu amount above the critical level.
NWAL swipes were previously ashed and dissolved by another team at LANL according to their standard
operating procedure. Dilutions (~50x, Table 2) of the NWAL swipe stock solutions were necessary prior
to aliquoting due to the relatively high Pu content in those samples.

Chemistry proceeded on dissolved aliquots according to established methods within our laboratory.2 In
short, chemistry begins with spiking (244Pu, produced in-house at LANL and calibrated to NIST 4334g,
242
 Pu activity standard) and spike equilibration (by fuming with concentrated perchloric acid), then an
oxalate co-precipitation and hydroxide co-precipitation, and ﬁnally two anion exchange columns (Biorad
AG 1x4, 100-200 mesh) to remove Am, U, Th, Np, and other matrix elements. This chemistry achieves a
decontamination factor of U from Pu of 107 – 108, which is generally suﬃcient to remove naturally-
occurring concentrations of U from Pu. After puriﬁcation, Pu fractions are combined with NBL U970,
enriched 235U standard, and bound to single anion exchange beads, which are placed in the canoe of a
rhenium ﬁlament as the TIMS source. Dates of processing are indicated in Table 1. CRMs were chemically
puriﬁed once and then aliquoted and spiked, but bead loading was performed on a few aliquots at a
time, to achieve maximum variability in instrument gains and loading conditions.

5. Task 3
TIMS routines were developed for two diﬀerent instrument conﬁgurations. One instrument is an IsotopX
Phoenix equipped with 8 channel electron multipliers (Sjuts detectors) and an axial Daly detector. The
other TIMS is a GV Instruments Isoprobe-T equipped with 7 discrete dynode channeltron ion counters
and an axial Daly. These two instruments are capable of simultaneously measuring 235U (source
monitoring tracer), 238(U+Pu), 239Pu, 240Pu, 241Pu, 242Pu, mass 243 (background noise monitor), and 244Pu
(isotope dilution tracer).

A data reduction spreadsheet for each instrument conﬁguration was developed. The spreadsheets
combine information from the instrument gains measurement (calibration) and the sample analysis.
They perform a correction to the measured 238Pu/244Pu ratio for each cycle of collected data according to
Equation 1.

This mathematical correction is made possible by the diﬀerent ionization temperatures of U and Pu, and
the fact that they are partially separated during the TIMS measurement. Figure 1 illustrates a typical
ionization proﬁle of a Pu sample (top) and a process blank (bottom) with added 235U tracer in our TIMS.
The Pu beam can be seen to “die” by 1620°C when the 244Pu count rate drops to zero, at which point a
manual current increase to the ﬁlament causes U count rates to grow. The drop shown in 238 count rate
at 1620°C reveals the highest contribution of 238U to the 238Pu counts throughout the analysis and is
roughly 1/3 of the peak count rate of 238(U+Pu) at that cycle.

In addition to calculating the corrected 238Pu values, the new spreadsheets also calculate the combined
standard uncertainty (CSU) for isotope ratios at each cycle of collected data according to the Guide for
Uncertainty in Measurement (GUM). 4 The early cycles generally have very little correction, because U
has not grown-in to signiﬁcant count rates yet, and the later cycles generally have a larger correction, but
because the count rates are higher, they also have a lower uncertainty. The spreadsheets also determine
an uncertainty budget for the overall analysis. After the 238Pu/239Pu ratios are determined, further
corrections are made to the result for process blank correction and scaling to the total sample from the
aliquot fraction (for assay determinations).

The average of all 53 blanks is 0.14 fg 238Pu and the calculated critical level is 0.6 fg. The blanks
analyzed for this project are possibly more variable than previously measured blanks, especially for
the bead-loaded, non-chemistry type. Based on this new data, it seems more likely that the beads
and reagents used in the bead loading process are responsible for the higher variability rather than
the Np separation chemistry reagents. After bead loading, there are diﬀerent sources of 238U that can
be measured in the TIMS, including U in the ﬁlament rhenium itself, in the glue used to aﬃx the
bead, within the structure of the bead, and bound to the bead active sites including both 235U spike
and leftover 238U in the sample solution. These diﬀerent sources could potentially ionize diﬀerently
(at diﬀerent times/temperatures) during the analysis, leading to fractionation of 238U/235U
throughout the analysis. Although our new mathematical correction can account well for relatively
high concentrations of U loaded on the bead active sites, it may not be as eﬀective at accounting for
238
 U ionizing from within the ﬁlament, which we assume would get counted later in the run and even
after the loaded 235U is gone. For this reason, we postulate it is of particular importance to verify the
absence of U ionization from the ﬁlaments prior to measurement—an area of future investigation.

7. Conclusions
Overall, we consider this project a success for the following reasons:

 • All planned tasks were completed except analysis of IAEA-385, due to a late project start (~2
 months)
 • Sediment results for 238Pu concentration are accurate compared to the certiﬁcate value
 • NWAL swipe results for 238Pu/239Pu are accurate compared to the alpha spectrometry results for
 identical replicates and have both high reproducibility between replicates, and better precision
 for individual replicates compared to alpha spectrometry results
 • Blanks demonstrate a low detection limit, comparable to that of alpha spectrometry, and are
 consistent with previous blanks measured during the initial LDRD work to develop this method
 • The method is especially viable for high-burn Pu samples, even at low Pu concentrations

Transitioning this method to other laboratories or teams (e.g. the NWAL team at LANL) would require
those labs/teams to have the following:

 • A multi-collector TIMS capable of simultaneously monitoring both U and Pu. The ideal number
          of collectors is 8 (mass 235, 238, 239, 240, 241, 242, 243, 244), but an instrument with 7 would

                                                                                                                                 Page 13 of 14

 generate useful data and lack only the 243 background correction. An instrument with 6 ion
 counters could be used with a 242Pu tracer (instead of 244Pu) but would forfeit a result for 242Pu in
 the sample itself and have increased concentration uncertainty due to unknown contribution of
 242
 Pu from the sample to the tracer.
 • An available, well-characterized 244Pu solution for a tracer
 • An available, well-characterized, enriched 235U (or 236U) solution with low 238U content for a
 tracer
 • A clean laboratory for well-controlled U background
 • Stringent chemistry that is suﬃciently characterized for U decontamination from Pu
 • A QA/QC system for pre-checking ﬁlaments. Both the ﬁlament temperature at the method’s
 starting current and the 238U background count rate should be pre-determined before use with a
 sample

Future work should include a more thorough investigation of the phenomenon shown in Figure 7,
regarding the ionization proﬁle of 235U. Also, diﬀerent lots of resin beads used to prepare the TIMS
source should be investigated, because diﬀerent beads do have diﬀerent 238U content. Finally, a
preliminary check of the 238 count rates of new ﬁlament rhenium should be implemented as a standard
component of the method.
