Enhancing stock market price prediction through FinGPT-driven sentiment analysis, technical indicators, historical price data, and attention-based hybrid deep learning models
Authors: Mobin Golabzaei, Esmaeil Taheripour, Seyed Jafar Sadjadi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Accurate stock price forecasting is increasingly dependent on models that can integrate heterogeneous market signals beyond traditional quantitative indicators. Classical sentiment analysis tools and lexicon-based methods often fail to capture the contextual and domain-specific nuances of financial language, limiting their predictive contribution. To address these limitations, this study introduces a multimodal forecasting framework that leverages FinGPT, an advanced financial large language model specifically trained for market-related text understanding. FinGPT is employed to extract high-resolution sentiment polarity scores from Twitter data, replacing conventional sentiment models and providing a more robust representation of investor psychology. These sentiment features are fused with historical stock prices and technical indicators to form a comprehensive input space. The integrated dataset is used to evaluate four deep learning architectures: CNN, LSTM, Attention-based LSTM (ALSTM), and a newly proposed Hybrid ALSTM-CNN model with an attention mechanism. Experiments performed on eight major NASDAQ and NYSE stocks demonstrate that incorporating FinGPT-derived sentiment significantly enhances forecasting accuracy across all models. The Hybrid model consistently achieves the best performance, effectively capturing both long-term dependencies and short-term market dynamics. Results confirm that FinGPT-driven sentiment modeling provides substantial predictive value compared with traditional sentiment approaches, enabling more realistic, behavior-aware stock price forecasting. For instance, in the case of AMD stock, it recorded the lowest MSE (46.06) and the highest R² (0.624), highlighting its superior ability to capture nonlinear dependencies and market dynamics. Overall, the proposed sentiment-enhanced hybrid framework provides a more robust and realistic procedure for stock price prediction by integrating quantitative and qualitative market signals.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at https://doi.org/10.1038/s41598-026-58717-2.

Introduction

Precise stock price forecasting serves as a vital tool for investors, enabling them to make well-informed decisions, recognize profitable opportunities, and mitigate financial risks. Anticipating market trends and price fluctuations allows investors to design effective trading strategies and enhance portfolio performance amid dynamic and volatile financial conditions. AI has profoundly transformed the landscape of time series anticipation, notably in financial domains such as stock market prediction. Traditional econometric models including ARIMA are often limited by their linear assumptions and inability to adapt to the nonlinear, chaotic, and highly volatile nature of financial data1. In contrast, AI-based models such as ANNs, DL architectures, and metaheuristic-optimized systems are capable of learning complex nonlinear relationships and temporal dependencies directly from data without requiring strong prior assumptions about distributional properties. Stock price prediction has remained a central challenge in computational finance because of the inherently complex and nonlinear behavior of financial markets. Models relying solely on historical price information often fail to account for behavioral and informational factors that drive market dynamics.

Researchers have demonstrated that the sentiment polarity derived from tweets or financial news exhibits a measurable correlation with short-term market fluctuations, indicating that investors’ collective emotions often precede price trends. In recent years SA has developed as a critical complementary approach leveraging textual data such as financial news and social media posts to capture public mood and investor perception, which significantly affect stock price movements2,3. In the past, SA in financial studies primarily relied on lexicon-based approaches and traditional LSTM-based models to interpret textual data. However, with recent advancements in DNN and artificial intelligence, modern research increasingly employs transformer-based architectures such as BERT, FinBERT, and FinGPT, which offer superior contextual understanding and domain-specific sentiment interpretation in financial text analysis. A FinBERT-LSTM framework was developed to integrate financial news sentiment into a DL model, demonstrating that textual sentiment extracted from market-, industry-, and stock-related news can enhance forecasting accuracy beyond models relying solely on price data4,5. Likewise, it has been established that aggregated Twitter sentiment exhibits a significant correlation with the DJIA, indicating that social media information can serve as a valuable proxy for investor psychology6,7.

Building upon these insights, a growing body of research emphasizes the integration of multi-source or multimodal data to improve the robustness and interpretability of forecasting models. Rather than relying on a single information stream, contemporary approaches combine historical price data, technical indicators, and textual sentiment into unified predictive systems. The multimodal design reflects the complex interplay between market fundamentals, technical patterns, and behavioral sentiment cues8. Studies highlight that the fusion of structured numerical data with unstructured textual content leads to richer feature representations and improved learning performance9. A hybrid RNN architecture combining GRU, LSTM, and Bi-LSTM layers is introduced to improve stock market prediction through the integration of numerical price data and sentiment extracted from financial news. It is shown that the incorporation of VADER-based sentiment scores significantly enhances forecasting accuracy, leading to lower MAE and RMSE compared with standalone LSTM, GRU, or traditional RNN models10. Studies underline the importance of multi-source learning, where the combination of technical and textual signals enables models to better reflect real-world market behavior.

However, the persistent uncertainty surrounding the predictability of financial markets, together with an overreliance on historical observations and the application of parameters estimated from past data to real-world decision-making, continues to pose significant challenges for investors11,12. Significant advancements have been achieved in ML and DL frameworks for predicting financial outcomes. Early studies relied on statistical and shallow learning techniques such as SVM, RF, and regression models13. However, the nonlinearity and volatility of stock price dynamics necessitated the development of DL architectures capable of learning hierarchical temporal patterns. RNNs, particularly LSTM and GRU variants, have proven effective for modeling sequential dependencies in time-series data. The integration of convolutional layers with LSTM networks has further enhanced model performance by enabling simultaneous learning of temporal and spatial patterns in multivariate financial inputs. Hybrid models such as CNN-LSTM, ALSTM, and HRNN have been found to outperform traditional models by detecting both short-term fluctuations and long-term dependencies in stock prices14. Moreover, the combination of DL with sentiment-aware modules such as FinBERT-LSTM and hybrid RNNs has yielded superior forecasting accuracy by embedding semantic understanding of financial narratives within temporal models4,15,16.

In light of this evolving literature, the present study contributes to the field by developing an integrated DL framework that leverages three distinct data modalities historical prices, technical indicators, and textual sentiment scores to forecast stock prices. The proposed approach aims to demonstrate that the inclusion of sentiment polarity, computed from FinGPT-based analysis of tweets, enhances predictive precision and stability across multiple DL architectures, particularly in the hybrid ALSTM-CNN model. Through this integration of quantitative and qualitative information, the research seeks to provide empirical evidence supporting the hypothesis that models incorporating both market behavior and investor sentiment outperform those relying solely on numerical data.

Literature review

In recent years, the rapid advancement of AI has significantly expanded its applications in the field of financial forecasting, notably in stock price prediction17. Concurrently, the widespread use of social media platforms has provided valuable opportunities to analyze investor sentiment, which has developed as a critical factor impacting market behavior and improving the performance of predictive models. The integration of AI-driven analytical techniques with SA enables researchers and investors to capture both quantitative trends and qualitative market perceptions, thereby enhancing predictive precision and decision-making effectiveness. Accordingly, identifying and examining these interconnected domains AI-based forecasting, SA, and multimodal data integration plays a crucial role in developing more robust and precise predictive models. The following subsections review the existing literature across these three areas to establish the theoretical foundation for the present study.

Stock price prediction using AI algorithms

Recent work consistently shows a shift from classical predictors (e.g., ARIMA or linear models) toward ML and DL, chiefly because market dynamics are nonlinear, high‑dimensional, and temporally dependent. A representative review reports renewed interest in recurrent architectures for capturing temporal structure in financial time series and notes their empirical advantages over traditional baselines, specifically when price sequences exhibit long‑range dependencies18,19 Complementing this perspective, Wang et al.20 present an LSTM-based model aimed at overcoming the shortcomings of traditional RNNs in stock prediction. By using gating mechanisms to manage long-term dependencies and applying preprocessing, denoising, and regularization, their approach enhances stability and improves forecasting accuracy, showing LSTM’s effectiveness for financial time-series modeling. Meanwhile, Mu et al.21 propose a model that integrates investor sentiment with historical trading data to enhance stock price prediction. The study builds a stock-specific sentiment dictionary and uses the Sparrow Search Algorithm to optimize LSTM hyperparameters. Results show that combining sentiment indicators with optimized deep learning improves forecasting robustness and captures market behavior more effectively.

Empirically, Li et al.22 develop an LSTM-based model to predict the closing prices of major technology stocks using a decade of historical data. Their two-layer LSTM architecture captures temporal dependencies in price movements and demonstrates reliable trend-learning capability. The study highlights LSTM’s strength in modeling nonlinear financial time series and its suitability for stock market forecasting. At the application level, Varadharajan et al.23 investigate an LSTM-RNN framework for predicting Amazon’s closing stock prices. By comparing hyperparameter settings and validating performance on both synthetic ARIMA-based data and real market series, the study shows that LSTM networks effectively learn nonlinear temporal patterns and closely track actual price movements, confirming their suitability for robust financial time-series forecasting.

Another line of evidence shows that prediction improves when LSTMs are combined with information beyond raw prices. Vargas et al.24 introduce a hybrid deep-learning framework that combines financial news titles with technical indicators for predicting daily stock movements. Using a CNN to extract textual semantics and an LSTM to model temporal trends, their SI-RCNN model outperforms indicator-only baselines and shows the strong predictive value of news-driven features. Convolutional architectures also matter especially for volatility and feature‑rich inputs. Liu25 proposed an enhanced CNN model for stock-volatility forecasting, showing that convolutional networks effectively capture both local and global structures in noisy, high-dimensional financial data. By modeling volatility-driven patterns, the approach outperforms traditional ML methods and even surpasses some sequence-based models in volatility-focused settings.

Alkhatib et al.26 introduce an active deep-learning framework for stock price forecasting that evaluates six neural architectures, including MLP, GRU, LSTM, Bi-LSTM, CNN, and CNN-LSTM, across multiple companies. By engineering an expanded feature set, the study demonstrates that incorporating additional price-derived variables can enhance predictive stability and improve overall model performance. Lei et al.27 introduced the TFJ-DRL framework, combining deep learning and reinforcement learning with feature-gating and temporal-attention modules. The model effectively selects key indicators and captures time patterns, outperforming benchmark trading strategies in profit, return, and Sharpe ratio. Bansal et al.28 compared five ML models KNN, LR, SVR, DTR, and LSTM for predicting stock prices of 12 Indian companies using seven years of data. Results showed that LSTM achieved the highest accuracy due to its strength in modeling nonlinear and time-dependent patterns, highlighting the superiority of DL over traditional ML methods.

Also, Recent studies have also explored network-based and information-theoretic approaches for financial prediction. Choi and Kim29 proposed ETF forecasting frameworks that use statistical dependence-based and information-theoretic networks, including measures such as mutual information and transfer entropy, along with node embeddings, centrality measures, ensemble learning, and explainable AI techniques such as SHAP. Additionally, Choi and Kim30 on information-theoretic networks and node embeddings for ETF prediction demonstrates how centrality measures derived from financial networks can serve as informative features for gradient-boosting models. Their finding that network topology encodes predictive information about price dynamics is directly relevant to the current study’s claim of capturing “market dynamics.

Similarly, Kim et al.31 showed that effective transfer entropy can serve as a useful market explanatory variable and improve prediction performance across models such as LSTM and XGBoost. These studies demonstrate that network topology and inter-asset dependency structures can provide valuable predictive information. Chen and Wei32 emphasized the importance of incorporating information from companies related to a target firm when predicting stock prices. They constructed a company graph based on real-market investment relationships and applied node-embedding techniques to generate distributed representations for each firm. Their results showed that these graph-based representations can effectively capture inter-company relationships and improve the accuracy of stock price prediction models. In contrast, the present study focuses on single-asset forecasting through temporal multimodal fusion of historical prices, technical indicators, and FinGPT-derived sentiment features, rather than explicitly modeling cross-asset relational networks.

Another notable point in the existing literature is the growing attention paid to the use of multi-source data and hybrid frameworks in financial modeling and forecasting. Nti et al.33 proposed a multi-source information-fusion framework for stock price prediction using a hybrid deep learning architecture based on CNN and LSTM, named IKN-ConvLSTM. They integrated stock-related information from six heterogeneous sources, including numerical and textual data, to address the challenge of combining diverse financial indicators. Their empirical results on Ghana Stock Exchange data showed that fusing multiple data sources significantly improved prediction accuracy compared with using individual datasets separately. Ke et al.34 proposed a multi-source deep learning framework to predict cryptocurrency pin-bar reversal risk events by integrating blockchain metrics, social sentiment, and regulatory signals. Using an LSTM-based architecture, they showed that temporal anomalies can be effectively captured, while blockchain-related variables made a substantial contribution to predictive performance. Their findings also indicated that combining diverse data sources improves rare-event detection and offers practical value for financial risk monitoring under uncertain market conditions.

Ke et al.35 developed a hybrid methodological framework for technical feature analysis using high-frequency financial data. Their approach combined CNN for extracting local price–volume features, GRU for capturing temporal dependencies, and an improved genetic algorithm for dynamic hyperparameter optimization. They also introduced a multi-time-window fusion strategy and Bayesian adaptive weighting to integrate deep learning factors across different horizons. Their empirical findings showed that the proposed hybrid factor achieved stable performance and outperformed individual factors in both Chinese and U.S. markets. Accordingly, and in line with this growing research trend toward multi-source data integration and hybrid modeling frameworks, the present study proposes a new CNN-Attention-based LSTM model that combines price data, technical indicators, and Twitter sentiment to enhance financial prediction performance.

Technical Indicators in stock price prediction

The integration of TIs into stock price prediction models has become a cornerstone of contemporary financial forecasting, offering structured, quantitative insights into market behavior. Numerous studies emphasize that when used in conjunction with ML techniques, TIs substantially enhance the accuracy, interpretability, and robustness of prediction systems. Several studies adopt traditional and advanced ML algorithms to explore the predictive power of TIs. For instance, Ayala et al.36 combine MACD and TEMA with ML models such as SVR, ANN, and Random Forests. Their findings show that hybrid models, which blend technical strategies with ML techniques, outperform purely statistical or indicator-only approaches in generating actionable trading signals. Similarly, Saetia and Yokrattanasak37 incorporate indicators like RSI, SMA, WMA, and MACD alongside Google Trends data and ML algorithms such as XGBoost and Random Forest. Their study highlights that combining structured indicator data with auxiliary variables (like search trends) can lead to higher predictive performance under both normal and crisis conditions.

DL architectures also benefit from the inclusion of technical indicators. Fathali et al.38 evaluate LSTM, RNN, and CNN models on the Indian NIFTY 50 index and demonstrate that LSTM when coupled with effective feature selection based on Tis yields the lowest prediction MSE, reinforcing the idea that carefully selected indicators can guide deep temporal models to learn more meaningful patterns. A similar conclusion is drawn by Gao et al.39, who integrate multiple TIs into both LSTM and GRU architectures. Using LASSO and PCA for dimensionality reduction, they show that LASSO-based TI selection enhances model accuracy more than PCA, and both recurrent models perform well, though neither dominates decisively. The development of hybrid frameworks further underlines the role of TIs in predictive modeling.

Albahli et al.40 propose new model, which uses an autoencoder to reduce correlation among 18 technical indicators and feed the optimized representation into a 1D DenseNet. Their system not only predicts stock prices but also outputs trading decisions (buy/sell/hold), achieving state-of-the-art results in terms of MAPE. The paper underscores the dual role of TIs in both feature engineering and downstream classification in deep models. Moreover, integrating multimodal data sources including TIs has shown to boost model robustness. Liu9 proposes a multimodal factor-mining approach that fuses market data, financial text, social sentiment, and technical indicators using GNNs and NLP methods. This integration captures hidden cross-modal patterns and shows that technical indicators work synergistically with sentiment-based inputs to improve model robustness.

Mostafavi and Hooman41 examined 88 technical indicators across momentum, trend, volatility, and volume groups to predict S&P 500 prices using RF, XGBoost, SVR, and LSTM with PCA. LSTM achieved the best accuracy, while TEMA, Ichimoku, and MFI were the most influential features, offering a data-driven framework for improving stock forecasting.Finally, Vargas et al.24 show that combining technical indicators with financial news enhances forecasting accuracy. Their hybrid CNN-LSTM model learns from both numerical TI patterns and textual sentiment, outperforming models that rely on only one type of data.

Sentiment analysis

A first stream of evidence shows that text‑derived sentiment provides incremental signal beyond prices alone. Nguyen et al.42 used message-board discussions to extract topic-specific sentiment signals and showed that incorporating these sentiment features into a price-only model noticeably improves directional prediction accuracy especially for stocks that are typically more difficult to forecast. Their findings indicate that fine-grained, topic-conditioned sentiment helps explain market variations that technical features alone fail to capture. In a complementary framework that integrates news sentiment with historical price data, Khedr and Yaseen43 demonstrate that this fusion leads to substantially improved predictive accuracy compared to relying on either data source alone. Their results highlight how sentiment information helps models better distinguish market regimes and reduce noise during volatile periods.

Koukaras et al.44 show that social-media microstructure from Twitter and StockTwits becomes highly predictive when paired with modern ML models. Combining microblog sentiment extracted by VADER with classical classifiers especially SVM significantly improves stock-movement prediction, indicating that concise polarity signals from retail investor posts meaningfully enhance model performance. Beyond Twitter, Bharathi & Geetha45 exploit RSS news feeds and market indices; fusing these sources delivers accuracy gain over conventional baselines, indicating that SA’s benefits generalize across text channels and not merely social platforms.

DL studies further argue that sentiment is especially potent when fused with sequence models that capture temporal dependence. John & Latha10 introduce a hybrid recurrent architecture (stacked LSTM/GRU with Bi‑LSTM) and show the model’s performance improves when financial‑news sentiment is concatenated with price features, outperforming single‑family RNNs and traditional statistics consistent with sentiment providing higher‑level context that temporal encoders can retain and exploit. Pushing this idea, Jun Gu et al.4 couple FinBERT (domain‑specific transformer for financial text) with an LSTM over market data: FinBERT‑LSTM surpasses vanilla LSTM and DNN on MAE/MAPE/accuracy, illustrating that finance‑tuned language models extract richer tonal and event signals than generic lexicons, which then translate into tighter forecasts when synchronized with price histories.

Under high‑uncertainty market conditions, SA appears to improve robustness as well as accuracy. A study on U.S. and European bank stocks shows that combining LSTM forecasts with FinBERT-based sentiment from tweets and news outperforms models that depend only on long historical price data. The results indicate that sentiment analysis captures rapid narrative shifts that price-only models fail to detect during sudden market regime changes46. Awad et al.47 propose a DRL framework that fuses LSTM, ANN, NLP, and sentiment analysis using historical prices, news, and tweets. Their model combines BERT-based sentiment features, VMD-LSTM temporal learning, and Deep Q-learning for trading decisions. Results show this hybrid, sentiment-aware approach improves forecasting accuracy and trading performance over traditional methods.

Sousa et al.48 evaluated BERT for sentiment analysis of financial news to enhance stock prediction. After fine-tuning BERT on a labeled news dataset, the model surpassed classical methods by capturing richer contextual meaning. Their analysis showed that extracted sentiment trends align closely with major market movements, indicating that transformer-based models offer strong potential for real-time sentiment tracking and short-term financial forecasting. Pagolu et al.6 examined the link between Twitter sentiment and stock market movements using large-scale Microsoft-related tweets. By representing text with Word2Vec and N-grams and classifying sentiment via Random Forest, they showed that aggregated online sentiment over short windows strongly aligns with upcoming price shifts. Their findings highlight the value of social-media emotions for anticipating investor behavior and improving short-term market forecasts.

Peivandizadeh et al.49 introduced a TLSTM model combined with social-media SA to enhance stock forecasting. Their approach tackles imbalanced sentiment data and temporal dependencies by using an off-policy PPO classifier alongside TLSTM for dynamic pattern learning. By integrating sentiment signals from news and Twitter with historical prices, the framework captures both behavioral and market dynamics, outperforming traditional LSTM- and sentiment-only methods.

Research gap

The present study introduces several methodological and conceptual innovations intended to improve the predictive accuracy and interpretability of stock price forecasting models. In contrast to conventional methods that depend only on numerical or technical information, this research integrates behavioral sentiment signals with temporal and spatial learning architectures. The following contributions summarize the key advancements of this study in terms of data integration, model architecture, and empirical validation.

Integration of three complementary data sources: This study integrates three heterogeneous but complementary data types historical prices, technical indicators, and textual sentiment from Twitter into a unified forecasting framework. Historical data capture long-term market dynamics through variables such as opening, closing, high, low, and volume. Technical indicators (e.g., RSI, MACD, MA/EMA, and Williams %R) enhance these signals by quantifying momentum, volatility, and short-term trends, while textual sentiment introduces behavioral information derived from investor opinions. The combination of these rational (quantitative) and behavioral (qualitative) features allows the model to better represent market dynamics, improving prediction stability and reducing forecasting errors compared to single-source models.Sentiment quantification using FinGPT and polarity scoring: Unlike traditional lexicon-based or static NLP approaches, this research employs FinGPT, an advanced financial language model capable of context-aware sentiment interpretation. For each tweet, FinGPT outputs probabilities of positive, negative, and neutral sentiment. A polarity score is then computed as, ranging from \documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:-1\:$$\end{document}to +1. The daily average polarity score for each stock is calculated by aggregating all tweet scores within the same day. This continuous numeric feature allows unstructured textual sentiment to be transformed into a structured input for time-series models. FinGPT’s dynamic language understanding minimizes misinterpretation caused by financial jargon or sarcasm, producing more reliable sentiment signals for forecasting models.Development of a novel hybrid ALSTM–CNN model with attention mechanism: A new hybrid DL architecture is proposed that combines ALSTM and CNN components. The LSTM captures long-term temporal dependencies, the CNN extracts short-term and local features from financial time series, and the attention mechanism assigns adaptive importance weights to influential time steps. This hybridization enhances both the representational power and interpretability of the model. Compared to single-architecture approaches, the hybrid model can simultaneously learn slow-moving patterns and sudden fluctuations, yielding higher accuracy and robustness in dynamic market conditions.Fusion of sentiment features with historical and technical indicators: The DPS are fused with historical and technical features to create a comprehensive input matrix (10 features for the sentiment-based scenario). This integration enables the model to incorporate investor mood and market psychology alongside traditional numerical indicators. Empirical results demonstrate that adding sentiment features significantly improves prediction accuracy.Real-world case study on eight major stocks: The proposed framework was evaluated on eight major U.S. stocks AMD, AMZN, AAPL, GOOG, MSFT, PG, TSLA, and TSM over a one-year period (September 2021–September 2022). This period selection ensured alignment between historical data (from Yahoo Finance) and textual data (from Kaggle/Twitter). The dataset diversity, spanning sectors from technology to consumer goods, allowed for robust cross-domain testing. The results confirmed that the hybrid model with sentiment integration consistently achieved superior accuracy across both volatile and stable stocks, demonstrating high generalization capability under real market conditions.Systematic experimental design and comparative evaluation: A structured experimental setup was designed to evaluate four models CNN, LSTM, ALSTM, and Hybrid under two conditions: with and without SA. Data were partitioned into 80% for training and 20% for testing sets for all models to ensure fair comparison. This two-scenario framework quantitatively isolates the effect of sentiment inclusion, revealing that sentiment-enhanced models outperform baseline versions across all evaluation metrics. The hybrid model consistently achieved the best or near-best results, validating its robustness and adaptability in diverse stock environments.

The case study in this research focuses on the merging of multi-source data to predict stock prices using historical, technical, and textual sentiment information. To maintain consistency and ensure the temporal alignment of data sources, the same one-year period was selected for the collection of historical stock data from Yahoo Finance. The study investigates eight major publicly traded companies representing different sectors of the global market, each listed on the NASDAQ or NYSE. Technical indicators

Technical indicators are essential in improving the predictive accuracy of stock price prediction models by capturing hidden market patterns, trends, and momentum that are not directly observable in raw historical data. Incorporating such indicators helps models better represent investor behavior and market dynamics, leading to improved predictive performance. For instance, Patel et al.67 demonstrated that incorporating technical indicators like RSI, MACD, and MA into ML models markedly enhances forecasting accuracy compared with models relying solely on price data. In this study, to further improve prediction performance, five technical indicators were calculated for each trading day based on the closing price of the stocks. These indicators were incorporated as additional features in the input of the predictive models to enhance their learning capability and accuracy. The five selected indicators and their computational principles are briefly described in the following subsections.

RSI:

In technical analysis, the RSI ranks as one of the most frequently utilized momentum indicators, introduced by J. Welles Wilder68. For SA of the gathered textual data, this research utilizes FinGPT, a finance-oriented language model specifically trained to interpret and analyze financial language. FinGPT is an open-source LLM framework specifically adapted for financial NLP tasks, such as SA of news articles, tweets, and other market-related texts. Through instruction-tuning and domain-specific fine-tuning (e.g., using Low-rank Adaptation, LoRA), FinGPT enables the model to map financial texts into structured sentiment categories positive, neutral, or negative and produce robust polarity scores. In Kang and Choi76 study, SA was conducted on categorized news data using GPT-4o and FinBERT. In that research, news data were obtained both from Kaggle and through dynamic collection from The New York Times, and the models were evaluated based on headlines, descriptions, and different news sections. The results showed that GPT-4o, with optimized prompt design, outperformed the fine-tuned FinBERT model across all sections. These findings suggest that GPT/FinGPT-based approaches may achieve superior performance over FinBERT in financial sentiment analysis.

Unlike general-purpose LLMs, FinGPT addresses the distinctive challenges of financial language (such as numeric references, ticker symbols, domain jargon and semantic nuance) by leveraging curated data sources and financial-specific tasks. Attention-based LSTM

The ALSTM model represents an enhanced variant of the conventional LSTM, developed to overcome constraints in modeling long-term dependencies within time-series data. By integrating the memory retention capability of LSTM with an attention mechanism, the ALSTM can more effectively identify and emphasize important temporal patterns across extended sequences. This hybrid design enhances predictive accuracy in time-series forecasting tasks by simultaneously managing both short-term fluctuations and long-term trends. The model’s robustness lies in its ability to selectively focus on the most relevant time steps, thereby improving interpretability and efficiency in sequential learning. Since the LSTM architecture has been described earlier, the following section provides a concise overview of the attention layer and its role in this framework.

Attention mechanism: The attention mechanism functions as a dynamic weighting process that assigns learned importance scores to different input values over time, effectively allowing the model to concentrate on the most informative parts of a series. Acting as an adaptive memory component, it enhances the model’s ability to extract meaningful features and capture temporal dependencies more accurately. By prioritizing specific inputs, the attention mechanism mitigates information bottlenecks and improves learning efficiency. Moreover, it helps address the vanishing gradient issue by establishing direct connections among the encoder and decoder states, ensuring smoother information flow across time steps. Hybrid model

To improve the robustness and representational capability of the prediction structure, this study employs a hybrid model architecture that integrates temporal and spatial learning mechanisms within a unified structure. The model is developed to simultaneously extract the long-term temporal relationships present in temporal financial data and the localized feature correlations among multiple input variables, thereby improving its capacity to learn complex nonlinear relationships in stock price dynamics.

In the proposed framework, sequential input vectors containing ten features per trading day including historical, technical, and sentiment-based variables are first processed by the ALSTM layer. This recurrent component maintains both hidden and cell states across time steps, enabling the network to efficiently model long-range temporal relationships within the data. To further refine the temporal representations, an attention mechanism is applied to the series of hidden states produced by the ALSTM. Through a softmax-based weighting process, the attention layer calculates a context vector that emphasizes the most informative time steps, enabling the model to concentrate on selectively on patterns that have stronger predictive significance while mitigating the vanishing gradient problem commonly encountered in long sequences.

The contextualized temporal representations obtained from the ALSTM-attention module are then passed through a two-layer one-dimensional CNN, which operates as a feature extractor. The first convolutional layer, equipped with 16 filters and a kernel size of three, identifies short-term fluctuations and localized transitions in the hidden representations. The subsequent convolutional layer, with eight filters of the same kernel size, refines these feature maps to highlight essential temporal cues. An adaptive average pooling layer subsequently condenses the learned features into a compact, high-level representation that summarizes global temporal dynamics. This CNN branch enables the network to detect abrupt variations and localized dependencies that are often overlooked by purely recurrent models.

The outputs from the attention-based ALSTM module and the pooled CNN representation are integrated to form an extensive feature vector that integrates both global sequential context and localized temporal detail. This fused representation is then fed into a completely linked dense layer responsible for predicting the next-day closing price of the stock. The model was trained with the Adam optimization algorithm at a learning rate of 0.001, and training proceeds for 50 epochs under the MSE loss criterion. The implementation is carried out in PyTorch, with computations executed on a GPU when available to ensure efficient training and evaluation. This Fig. 8 presents the structure of the introduced Hybrid model.

Fig. 8The structure of the hybrid model.

The rationale behind this hybrid configuration lies in the complementary strengths of its constituent components. The ALSTM component provides the capability to model long-term dependencies and capture memory-driven sequential behavior, while the CNN component improves the model’s responsiveness to short-term variations and localized correlations within the input space. The attention mechanism further contributes interpretability and stability by dynamically adjusting the importance assigned to individual time steps based on their significance to the predictive task. Consequently, the hybrid model framework delivers a more robust, memory-aware, and spatially attentive architecture capable of effectively modeling the intricate and nonlinear dependencies that characterize financial time-series data.

The workflow starts by acquiring actual financial market data, which is divided into input and output datasets where data from previous trading day’s serve as input features and the subsequent day’s price represents the prediction goal. The data are then used to train four predictive models under specific hyperparameter settings. After training, the models’ performance is assessed by comparing the actual and predicted stock prices based on five statistical evaluation metrics: MAPE, MAE, MSE, R², and RMSE.

Fig. 9The workflow of the proposed methodology.

The workflow of the proposed methodology.

In this study, the stock prices of the eight selected companies were predicted using four forecasting models over the defined one-year observation period. For each model, the dataset was split into training and testing subsets, with 80% of the data utilized for training and the remaining 20% reserved for testing to assess out-of-sample performance. Two experimental settings were designed to assess the effect of incorporating sentiment information. In the model with SA, three types of data historical stock data, technical indicators, and sentiment polarity scores were integrated, resulting in a total of 10 input features for each predictive model. In contrast, in the model without SA, only two categories of data historical data and technical indicators were used, yielding a total of 9 input features. This comparative design allowed for a systematic evaluation of the contribution of sentiment-based features to the predictive performance of the proposed models. For each of the four prediction models, specific hyperparameters were tuned to maximize the model’s predictive performance. All hyperparameters were validated through five-fold cross-validation to ensure model robustness, generalization capability, and to avoid overfitting.

CNN:

The CNN model was implemented using a sequential architecture to extract short-term relationships and local patterns in stock price movements. The input shape was defined as (X_train.shape[1], 1). The model comprises a Conv1D layer with 64 filters and a kernel size of two using ReLU activation, followed by a MaxPooling1D layer (pool size = 2) to decrease feature space and prevent overfitting. The output was flattened and passed through a Dense hidden layer with 50 neurons (ReLU), and finally a Dense output layer with linear activation for price forecasting. The model was trained with the Adam optimization algorithm and MSE as the loss function for 50 epochs, using a batch size of 32. Hyperparameters such as filter number, kernel size, and hidden units were tuned using five-fold cross-validation to achieve optimal accuracy. The total trainable parameters were approximately 3,300, depending on the input dimension.

ALSTM:

The ALSTM model was designed to improve the interpretability and precision of sequential learning by assigning adaptive importance weights to each timestep in the input series. The model receives input vectors of size 9 features across 10-time steps (seq_len = 10) and processes them through an LSTM layer with a hidden size of 32, enabling it to capture long-term temporal relationships. An attention layer, implemented as a linear transformation mapping from the hidden dimension to a single scalar weight, computes the relative contribution of each hidden state to the final context vector. This mechanism enables the model to concentrate on selectively on the most informative patterns within the sequence.

The network was trained over 20 epochs with a mini-batch size of 32, employing the Adam optimizer and MSE loss function. The learning rate was set to 0.001, ensuring stable and efficient convergence. Training and evaluation were performed on both CUDA-enabled GPU and CPU, depending on availability. All hyperparameters were fine-tuned through five-fold cross-validation, ensuring an optimal trade-off between learning performance and generalization capability.

Discussion

In this study, we proposed a hybrid model for stock price prediction, which collected and integrated three distinct types of historical stock data, technical indicator data, and sentiment text data to improve the prediction accuracy of stock price prediction models. We used the FinGPT model to analyze sentiment on the collected texts. The dataset covered a one-year period for eight major stocks. Historical data, including open, close, high, low, and volume, were obtained from Yahoo Finance, while technical indicators were calculated based on closing prices. Sentiment text data were collected from Twitter using Kaggle’s Sunscape tool and processed to extract daily sentiment polarity scores for each stock. Four DL models were used for price prediction: CNN, LSTM, ALSTM, and a hybrid model. Each model was trained and evaluated using an 80/20 split for training and testing the dataset. Two experimental scenarios were considered: one with SA and one without SA. In the sentiment-aware configuration, the models received ten input features (historical data, technical indicators, and sentiment scores), while in the base configuration without SA, only nine features (historical and technical data) were used. The results showed that the use of SA consistently improved the forecasting performance across most models and stocks. Models with sentiment features calculated lower error measures (MSE, MAE, RMSE, and MAPE) and higher R2 values, indicating that market sentiment provides valuable behavioral information to the forecasting process. Among all the tested models, the proposed hybrid architecture provided the best overall performance, outperforming the CNN, LSTM, and ALSTM models individually. Its ability to combine spatial feature extraction and time sequence learning enabled it to capture both technical market patterns and psychological dynamics reflected in investor sentiment, thereby providing the most accurate and consistent forecasts.

Theoretical insights

The empirical findings obtained from the eight analyzed stocks provide several important theoretical insights regarding the integration of heterogeneous data sources and hybrid DL architectures in stock price forecasting. The results demonstrate that combining quantitative features (historical and technical indicators) with qualitative sentiment information significantly improves the model’s capability to capture the underlying intricacy of financial markets. This improvement can be theoretically explained by the complementarity between rational market indicators and behavioral market signals, where historical and technical data represent observable price dynamics, while sentiment data reflect investors’ collective emotions and expectations.

The superior efficiency of the Hybrid model further reinforces the theoretical argument that multi-level feature representation enhances forecasting accuracy. The CNN component effectively extracts spatial and structural dependencies from multidimensional input features, while the ALSTM layer captures long-term temporal dependencies and assigns adaptive attention weights to relevant time steps. This dual mechanism allows the model to balance local trend sensitivity and global sequence understanding, resulting in improved generalization and reduced overfitting compared to single-architecture models such as CNN or LSTM alone. From a theoretical perspective, these findings enhance to the increasing body of literature emphasizing the validity of data fusion and hybrid network design in financial prediction tasks. The evidence from all eight stocks suggests that market behavior is neither purely stochastic nor entirely rational; rather, it emerges from a dynamic interaction between technical signals, historical trends, and investor sentiment. Therefore, the integration of diverse data modalities within hybrid DL architectures offers a more holistic framework for understanding and modeling financial market movements.

Managerial insights

From a managerial and decision-making aspect, the results of this study provide valuable implications for portfolio managers, financial analysts, and corporate decision-makers seeking to enhance the precision and validity of stock price forecasts. The integration of SA into predictive modeling proved to be an effective strategy for improving forecast performance, particularly for stocks with higher volatility and greater sensitivity to investor perception, such as TSLA and AMZN. This finding underscores the managerial importance of monitoring market sentiment and social media signals as part of investment analytics, since emotional and behavioral factors increasingly influence short-term market fluctuations. The consistent superiority of the Hybrid CNN–ALSTM model across multiple stocks also offers key insights for data-driven management and risk assessment. Managers can utilize such hybrid DL frameworks to create dynamic forecasting systems that simultaneously process historical trends, technical indicators, and real-time sentiment data. This capability enables more adaptive investment strategies, allowing decision-makers to anticipate potential price reversals or volatility surges before they occur.

Moreover, incorporating hybrid models with sentiment-driven features can improve strategic asset allocation and risk diversification, particularly in fast-moving sectors such as technology and consumer goods. These models help reduce uncertainty in prediction-based decisions, supporting more evidence-based portfolio management. In addition, corporate executives may leverage these analytical tools to better understand investor sentiment toward their own firms, which can guide communication strategies, brand positioning, and timing of financial disclosures. In summary, the managerial insight derived from this research is that the integration of behavioral data (sentiment) and hybrid DL models provides a more comprehensive, flexible, and robust decision-support framework. This combination enables managers to move beyond purely historical analyses toward a behavior-aware, data-driven forecasting approach, improving both strategic planning and market responsiveness.

Limitation

In spite of the promising results achieved in this study, several limitations must be acknowledged. First, as with most AI–based forecasting systems, the models used in this research are subject to the phenomenon known as AI hallucination, in which deep learning models may generate seemingly plausible yet potentially misleading predictions based on patterns that do not truly exist in the underlying data. This issue is particularly relevant in financial time-series forecasting, where market noise, abrupt shocks, and non-stationary behavior may cause the model to overfit or overinterpret short-term fluctuations as meaningful signals. Accordingly, the outputs of such models should be interpreted with caution and should ideally be complemented by domain knowledge and expert judgment in real-world investment settings. In addition, the empirical setting of this study is limited in both temporal and geographic scope. The experiments were conducted using a one-year sample period and a small set of eight representative U.S. equities listed on NASDAQ and NYSE. While these markets are highly important, they also possess distinctive microstructure characteristics, including high liquidity, extensive institutional participation, continuous trading, and a relatively mature information environment. Therefore, the findings of this study should be interpreted within the context of large-cap U.S. equities, and they cannot be assumed to automatically generalize to other stock markets with different trading behaviors, investor structures, or information environments.

Second, the study was constrained by data availability and accessibility. Although multiple sources of information historical prices, technical indicators, and textual sentiment were integrated, the accessible dataset was limited to the data available through publicly obtainable sources, including Kaggle-based datasets, which restricted both the sample period and the range of markets examined. In particular, the sentiment component relied on English-language Twitter data, which may not adequately represent investor discourse in non-English-speaking markets or in regions where investors communicate primarily through other social media platforms. As a result, the applicability of the proposed sentiment-analysis framework to markets such as KRX, TSE, Shanghai, or Shenzhen remains unverified in the current study. Nevertheless, this limitation may be addressed in future work by collecting local-language financial news and social media content and, where appropriate, translating such content into English for processing through models such as FinGPT, or alternatively by employing multilingual financial language models directly. Furthermore, the selected sample period, spanning from September 2021 to September 2022, reflects a specific market regime shaped by post-pandemic recovery and the onset of monetary tightening; hence, the robustness of the proposed framework across alternative market conditions, such as prolonged bear markets, financial crises, or periods of extreme volatility, remains to be investigated. Future research with broader international datasets, longer time horizons, and multilingual sentiment sources would therefore be essential for improving the external validity and generalizability of the findings.

Conclusion

In this study, we attempted to present a multimodal model for stock price forecasting. The proposed model includes three input data components, namely textual data, price data, and technical indicators. The FinGPT model is employed for sentiment analysis. The findings of this study provide compelling empirical evidence that integrating sentiment-based features into stock price forecasting frameworks significantly enhances forecasting performance. In all evaluated models, the inclusion of SA resulted in a significant improvement in forecast accuracy, with a reduction in error measures such as MSE, MAE, and RMSE of between 10 and 20%, along with a consistent increase in R². These results confirm that market sentiment acts as a meaningful explanatory variable and complements traditional historical and technical indicators by taking into account behavioral and psychological aspects of investor decision-making that are often neglected in purely quantitative models. Among all configurations, the proposed hybrid model showed the highest level of robustness and accuracy compared to the baseline models. Its architecture effectively captures temporal dependencies and interactions of spatial features, enabling a deeper understanding of nonlinear relationships in financial time series. For example, in the case of TSM, the inclusion of sentiment data reduced the MSE from 11.05 to 8.36 and improved the R² from 0.54 to 0.65, while for TSLA, the inclusion of sentiment information reduced the MSE from 445.52 to 339.45, demonstrating the quantitatively tangible benefits of combining multimodal data. Such improvements highlight the superior adaptability of the hybrid structure in learning from diverse and dynamic market signals.

From a theoretical perspective, these findings reinforce the idea that stock market dynamics are not solely governed by quantitative principles, but also by behavioral finance principles, where collective investor sentiment and public perception have a measurable impact on price formation. Thus, the demonstrated success of emotion-enhanced DL architectures bridges the gap between psychological market behavior and data-driven financial modeling. From a managerial and practical perspective, this study emphasizes the strategic importance of incorporating sentiment-based analytics into investment and risk management systems. The ability to gauge market sentiment through advanced NLP models, such as FinGPT, enables portfolio managers and traders to detect early signals of market optimism or pessimism, optimize asset allocation, and proactively respond to volatility. As a result, the integration of such hybrid intelligence frameworks into financial decision support systems can lead to improved portfolio diversification, reduced exposure to market shocks, and increased profitability.

Several promising directions are identified for future research. The proposed framework can be extended to multi-asset forecasting by incorporating inter-asset dependency structures, enabling the model to capture relational market information alongside temporal and sentiment-based features. In future research, the proposed framework can also be expanded using broader data from international markets, longer time horizons, diverse market regimes, and multilingual news sources and social media, so that it can more accurately assess its generalizability, resilience, and performance in more varied financial environments. Also, future research may improve the proposed framework by using sentiment aggregation based on credibility weighting, adjustments of specific stock technical indicators, and broader evaluations under various market conditions. Furthermore, the inclusion of multi-source sentiment streams such as Reddit discussions, financial news headlines, and corporate announcements may provide a more comprehensive representation of market sentiment. Adopting XAI techniques can also increase interpretability and transparency, addressing a critical limitation of black-box DL models in financial contexts. Finally, extending this framework through crowd learning, transfer learning, or transformer-based architectures may yield more robust, scalable, and adaptive forecasting systems capable of dynamically responding to rapidly evolving market environments.
