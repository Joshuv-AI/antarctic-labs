Existence of twisted Hermitian-Einstein metrics on unstable vector bundles
Mingwei Wang, Xiaokui Yang, Shing-Tung Yau

Abstract
. In this paper, we demonstrate that twisted Hermitian-Einstein metrics on holomorphic vector bundles exist without obstruction. More precisely, for an arbitrary holomorphic vector bundle E E over a compact Kähler manifold ( M , ω g ) (M,\omega_{g}) , we prove that the twisted Hermitian-Einstein equation Λ ω g ​ ( − 1 ​ R h ) = λ ​ h + P \Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda h+P admits a unique smooth solution h h , provided that P ∈ Γ ⁡ ( M , E ∗ ⊗ E ¯ ∗ ) P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}) is positive-definite and λ < λ E − \lambda<\lambda_{E}^{-} . The constant λ E − \lambda_{E}^{-} is intrinsically associated with the stability constant of E E . This result extends the classical Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau (DUY) theorem for stable bundles and, in the limit P → 0 P\rightarrow 0 , gives a new proof of the DUY theorem. As an application, we obtain an intrinsic Chern number inequality for unstable vector bundles : ∫ M ( ( r − 1 ) ​ c 1 ​ ( E ) 2 − 2 ​ r ​ c 2 ​ ( E ) ) ∧ ω g n − 2 ≤ ⌊ r 2 4 ⌋ ​ ( λ E + − λ E − ) 2 4 ​ π 2 ​ n 2 ​ ∫ M ω g n . \int_{M}\left((r-1)c_{1}(E)^{2}-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor\frac{(\lambda_{E}^{+}-\lambda_{E}^{-})^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.



1.
Introduction

This paper continues the investigation initiated in
[
WYY26+
]
and
[
FWYY26+
]
, where the prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation for holomorphic (Higgs) vector bundles on compact Hermitian manifolds was introduced:

(1.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)=P,

which can be viewed as a vector bundle analogue of the Calabi–Yau theorem (
[
Cal57
,
Yau78
]
). It is well-known that the Calabi-Yau theorem asserts that: on a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, for any closed real
(1,1)
form
\Omega
representing
2\pi c_{1}(M)
, there exists a unique Kähler metric
\omega\in[\omega_{g}]
such that its Ricci curvature satsifies

(1.2)

\mathrm{Ric}(\omega)=\Omega.

Let’s recall the classical Donaldson–Uhlenbeck–Yau theorem (
[
Don85
,
UY86
,
Don87
]
) for stable vector bundles (see also the Hermitian analogue in
[
LY86
]
):

Theorem A
.

For a stable holomorphic vector bundle
E
over a compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
, there exists a unique Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
on
E
up to scaling, satisfying

(1.3)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda_{E}\cdot h,

where
R^{h}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
is the Chern curvature tensor of
(E,h)
.

Slope stability is a fundamental algebraic condition: for every non-zero proper coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}\subset E
, the following inequality holds:

(1.4)

\frac{\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})}{\operatorname{rk}(\mathcal{F})}<\frac{\deg_{\omega_{g}}(E)}{\operatorname{rk}(E)}.

Here the degree is defined by

(1.5)

\deg_{\omega_{g}}(\mathcal{F})=2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}(\mathcal{F})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1},

where
c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
denotes the first Bott–Chern class in
H^{1,1}_{\mathrm{BC}}(M,\mathbb{C})
, coinciding with the usual first Chern class
c_{1}({\mathcal{F}})
when
M
is Kähler. We refer to
[
Kob87
]
for more details.
For a coherent subsheaf or quotient sheaf
{\mathscr{Q}}
of
E
, the
stability constant

\lambda_{\mathscr{Q}}
is defined as

(1.6)

\lambda_{\mathscr{Q}}:=\frac{2\pi n\int_{M}c^{\mathrm{BC}}_{1}({\mathscr{Q}})\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-1}}{\mathrm{{\rm rk}}({\mathscr{Q}})\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}=\frac{\deg_{\omega_{g}}({\mathscr{Q}})}{{\rm rk}({\mathscr{Q}})\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}}.

The following extremal constants are well-known:

(1.7)

\lambda_{E}^{+}:=\sup\left\{\left.\lambda_{\mathscr{S}}\ \right|\ \mathscr{S}\text{ is a coherent subsheaf of }E,\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{S})>0\right\},

and

(1.8)

\lambda_{E}^{-}:=\inf\left\{\left.\lambda_{\mathscr{Q}}\ \right|\ \mathscr{Q}\text{ is a coherent quotient sheaf of }E,\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q})>0\right\}.

One immediately obtains the inequality

(1.9)

\lambda_{E}^{-}\leq\lambda_{E}\leq\lambda_{E}^{+}.

Moreover,
E
is semi-stable if and only if
\lambda_{E}^{-}=\lambda_{E}
or
\lambda_{E}^{+}=\lambda_{E}
; in this case,

(1.10)

\lambda_{E}^{-}=\lambda_{E}=\lambda_{E}^{+}.

The Calabi-Yau equation (
1.2
) and the Hermitian-Einstein equation (
1.3
) have been studied extensively over the past half-century, yielding numerous important applications and emerging as central topics in differential geometry, algebraic geometry, mathematical physics and etc.. For a comprehensive account of this topic, we refer to
[

and references therein.

The prescribed Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor equation (
1.1
) interpolates between the Calabi-Yau equation (
1.2
) and the Hermitian-Einstein equation (
1.3
). By inheriting the geometric and analytic features of both, it serves as a pivotal bridge connecting the geometry of vector bundles to the broader realm of Kähler and Hermitian geometry.

The main result of this paper establishes the existence of twisted Hermitian-Einstein metrics on arbitrary holomorphic vector bundles.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler (or Gauduchon) manifold and
E
be an arbitrary holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. If we define

(1.11)

\mu_{E}^{-}:=\sup\left\{\ \lambda\ \left|\ \text{ there exists a Hermitian metric }h\text{ such that }\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)>\lambda h\right.\right\},

then

(1.12)

\lambda_{E}^{-}=\mu_{E}^{-}.

Moreover, for every
\lambda<\lambda_{E}^{-}
and every positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h
on
E
satisfying the twisted Hermitian-Einstein equation

(1.13)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda\cdot h+P.

One of the key ingredients in the proof of Theorem
1.1
is the following existence result for the twisted Hermitian-Einstein equation:

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. For given
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
, if there exists a smooth Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
such that

(1.14)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}>0,

then for any positive-definite Hermitian tensor
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metirc
h
on
E
satisfying the twisted Hermitian-Einstein equation:

(1.15)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda h+P.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
proceeds as follows. The uniform a priori
C^{0}
-estimate and the uniqueness for the twisted Hermitian-Einstein equation (
1.15
) follow from the comparison principle below.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector
bundle over
M
. If there are Hermitian metrics
h
and
h_{0}

on
E
and a constant
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}>0
, and

(1.16)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\lambda h\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0},

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
. Then
h\leq h_{0}
.

Note that inequality (
1.16
) holds as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
,
but not as scaling invariant endomorphisms
in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
.

The existence of solutions to (
1.15
) is established by an implicit function theorem argument
and the following weak compactness result for twisted Hermitian-Einstein equations.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
(E,h_{0})
be a Hermitian vector bundle over
M
. Fix
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that the following conditions are satisfied

(1)

\{P_{m}\}\subset\mathrm{Herm}(E)

converges smoothly to some
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
;

(2)

\{\lambda_{m}\}\subset{\mathbb{R}}
converges to
\lambda
;

(3)

\{h_{m}\}\subset\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
satisfies

(1.17)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m}}\right)=\lambda_{m}h_{m}+P_{m};

(4)

there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(1.18)

C^{-1}h_{0}\leq h_{m}\leq Ch_{0},

holds for all
m
.
Then, there exists a subsequence
\{h_{m_{i}}\}
which converges smoothly to a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, and this limit satisfies

(1.19)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda h+P.

We briefly outline the proof of Theorem
1.1
. The inequality
\mu_{E}^{-}\leq\lambda_{E}^{-}
follows easily
from the definitions. We proceed by contradiction and assume
\mu_{E}^{-}<\lambda_{E}^{-}
. By Theorem
1.2
, for a sequence
\mu_{m}\nearrow\mu_{E}^{-}
, there exist Hermitian metrics
\{h_{m}\}
solving

(1.20)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{m}}\right)=\mu_{m}h_{m}+P.

The comparison principle Theorem
1.3

implies that
\{h_{m}\}
are uniformly bounded from below.
Case
1:
If
\{h_{m}\}
is unbounded, a technique adapted from
[
UY86
]

allows us to construct a coherent quotient sheaf
{\mathscr{Q}}
with

\lambda_{{\mathscr{Q}}}
satisfying
\lambda_{E}^{-}\leq\lambda_{{\mathscr{Q}}}\leq\mu_{E}^{-}
,
contradicting
\mu_{E}^{-}<\lambda_{E}^{-}
.
Case 2:
If
\{h_{m}\}
is
uniformly bounded, Theorem

1.4
yields a limit metric
h
solving

(1.21)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\bigl(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\bigr)-(\mu_{E}^{-}+\varepsilon)h=P-\varepsilon h>0,

for some
\varepsilon>0
. This contradicts the maximality of
\mu_{E}^{-}
.
Thus
\mu_{E}^{-}=\lambda_{E}^{-}
, and Theorem
1.1
follows immediately from Theorem
1.2
.

As an application of Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
, we obtain a precise quantitative approximation for the Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensors of arbitrary vector bundles:

Theorem 1.5
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Gauduchon manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a Hermitian metric
h_{\varepsilon}
on
E
such that

(1.22)

\left(\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon\right)\cdot h_{\varepsilon}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)\leq\left(\lambda_{E}^{+}+\varepsilon\right)\cdot h_{\varepsilon}.

As a consequence of Theorem
1.5
, we establish an intrinsic Chern number inequality for arbitrary holomorphic vector bundles, generalising the classical inequalities known for semi-stable vector bundles.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler manifold and
E
be an arbitrary holomorphic vector bundle over
M
of rank
r
. The following Chern number inequality holds:

(1.23)

\int_{M}\left((r-1)c_{1}(E)^{2}-2rc_{2}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\leq\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor\frac{(\lambda_{E}^{+}-\lambda_{E}^{-})^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Moreover, we obtain a new proof of the classical Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem on the existence of Hermitian-Einstein metrics on stable vector bundles (
[
Don85
,
UY86
,
Don87
]
,
[
LY86
]
).

Theorem 1.7
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Kähler (or Gauduchon) manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
.
If
E
is semi-stable, then for any
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h_{\varepsilon}
on
E
such that

(1.24)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}-\varepsilon)\cdot h_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon P,

and
E
is approximately Hermitian-Einstein.
Moreover, if
E
is stable, then a subsequence
\{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein metric
h
on
E
as
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
.

The argument for Theorem
1.7
is not a straightforward adaptation
of Theorem
1.1
or the methods in
[
Don85
,
UY86
,
Don87
,
LY86
]
. Indeed, the family of equations (
1.24
) is established by Theorem
1.1
and the uniform
C^{0}
-estimate

(1.25)

C\cdot h_{0}\leq h_{\varepsilon}

is obtained by detailed calculations together with the Moser iteration,
rather than via the comparison principle established in this paper.

Remark 1.8
.

The main results of the paper also hold for Higgs bundles over compact Hermitian manifolds,
by adapting the frames constructed in
[
FWYY26+
]
.
Further applications are obtained in
[
XYY26+
]
via the associated parabolic flows of the form

(1.26)

\frac{\partial h}{\partial t}=-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\,R^{h}\right)+\mathcal{F}_{0}(h),

where
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is a general map taking values in
\mathrm{Herm}(E)
and
R^{h}
represents the curvature of an affine connection; in particular, it includes the curvature associated with a Higgs bundle. Specific instances include:

(i)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=P
for some fixed
P\in\mathrm{Herm}(E)
;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=\lambda h+P
;

(iii)

\mathcal{F}_{0}(h)=\lambda h+P+(\mathrm{tr}_{h_{0}}h)h_{0}
for some fixed Hermitian metric
h_{0}
on
E
.

Moreover, the positivity assumption on
P
can be weakened using flow techniques.

2.
A comparison principle and proof of Theorem
1.3

We recall some background material for the reader’s convenience.
Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold of dimension
n
.
We say that
(M,\omega_{g})
is a
Gauduchon manifold
if

\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0.

It is well known that, for any Hermitian metric
\omega
on
M
,
there exists a smooth function
f
such that

\omega_{g}=e^{f}\omega

satisfies
\partial\overline{\partial}\,\omega_{g}^{\,n-1}=0
(
[
Gau84
]
). Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
of rank
r
. We use the following notations:

•

\mathrm{Herm}(E)
: the space of Hermitian tensors in

\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
positive-definite Hermitian tensors;

•

\mathrm{Herm}^{\geq 0}(E)
: the subspace consisting of
non-negative Hermitian tensors.

Given a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
, the Chern connection of

(E,h)
is denoted by
\nabla^{h}
. We shall use the natural
decomposition
\nabla^{h}={\partial}^{h}+{\overline{\partial}}
, where
{\partial}^{h}
is the

(1,0)
part and
{\overline{\partial}}
is the
(0,1)
part. Let
R^{h}
be the Chern
curvature tensor of
(E,h)
. In local holomorphic coordinates

\{z^{i}\}
of
M
and local holomorphic basis
\{e_{\alpha}\}
of
E
,

(2.1)

R^{h}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma\left(M,\wedge^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}\right).

The
Hermitian-Yang-Mills tensor

S^{h}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
of
(E,h)
is defined as

(2.2)

S^{h}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes\overline{e}^{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*}).

By using the Hermitian metric
h
, there
are natural lifts of
R^{h}
and
S^{h}
:

(2.3)

\Theta^{h}=R^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}dz^{i}\wedge d\overline{z}^{j}\otimes e^{\alpha}\otimes{e}_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E),

and

(2.4)

K^{h}=S^{h}\cdot h^{-1}=\left(g^{i\overline{j}}R_{i\overline{j}\alpha}^{\beta}\right)e^{\alpha}\otimes e_{\beta}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

The following transformation formula for the
Hermitian-Yang-Mills curvature tensors is essentially well-known
(e.g.
[
WYY26+
, Proposition 3.6]
)

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be
a compact Hermitian manifold and
E\rightarrow M
be a holomorphic vector
bundle. If
h
and
h_{0}
are smooth Hermitian metrics on
E
, then

(2.5)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left(({\partial}^{h_{0}}H)\cdot H^{-1}\right)

as tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
where
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}
.

The following linear algebraic result is established in
[
WYY26+
, Lemma 2.2]
:

Lemma 2.2
.

(1)

H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian.

(2)

If
P\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
is
h_{0}
-Hermitian, for any
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
,

(2.6)

h_{0}(P\cdot A,B)=h_{0}(A,P\cdot B),\quad h_{0}(A\cdot P,B)=h_{0}(A,B\cdot P).

(3)

If
A,B\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
are
h_{0}
-Hermitian and
A\geq 0,B\geq 0
, then

(2.7)

h_{0}(A,B)\geq 0.

Moreover, for any section
C\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
, one has

(2.8)

h_{0}(A\cdot C\cdot B,C)\geq 0.

The main result of this section is the following
comparison principle:

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Hermitian manifold and
E
be a holomorphic vector
bundle over
M
. If there are Hermitian metrics
h
and
h_{0}

on
E
and a constant
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
satisfying

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}>0
, and

(2.9)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\lambda h\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0},

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}{}^{*})
. Then
h\leq h_{0}
.

Proof.

Let
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. Define

(2.10)

\Omega^{\lambda}:=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{E}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E),

and

(2.11)

\Phi^{\lambda}:=\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\lambda\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right)\cdot H\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

In particular, the conditions in Theorem
2.3
imply that
\Omega^{\lambda}>0
and

(2.12)

\Omega^{\lambda}\leq\Phi^{\lambda}

with respect to
h_{0}
. On the other hand, by (
2.5
), we have

(2.13)

\Phi^{\lambda}\cdot H^{-1}-\Omega^{\lambda}=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right).

This is equivalent to the following form:

(2.14)

\Omega^{\lambda}\cdot(H^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E})=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\sqrt{-1}{\overline{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H\cdot H^{-1}\right)+(\Omega^{\lambda}-\Phi^{\lambda})\cdot H^{-1}.

The proof of
H\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
then follows by a strategy similar to that used in the proof of
[
FWYY26+
, Theorem 1.3]
. ∎

As an application of Theorem
2.3
, we obtain a version of uniform
C^{0}
-estimates:

Proposition 2.4
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Fix a constant
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
h_{0}
and
h
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
, and

(2.15)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{{h_{0}}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}>0.

If there exists a constant
C_{1}\geq 1
such that

(2.16)

C_{1}^{-1}h_{0}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{{h}}\right)-\lambda h\leq C_{1}h_{0}

as Hermitian tensors in
\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
, then
there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},C_{1})
such that

(2.17)

C_{2}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h\leq C_{2}h_{0}.

Proof.

Since
M
is compact, by (
2.15
) and (
2.16
), there exists
C_{2}=C_{2}(\omega_{g},h_{0},C_{1})
such that

(2.18)

C_{2}^{-1}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}\right)\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)-\lambda h\leq C_{2}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{0}}\right)-\lambda h_{0}\right).

Applying Theorem
2.3
with a suitable scaling yields

(2.19)

C_{2}^{-1}h_{0}\leq h\leq C_{2}h_{0}.

This completes the proof.
∎

3.
A priori estimates

In this section, we establish the following a priori estimates:

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over a compact Hermitian manifold
(M,\omega_{g})
. Fix a constant
\lambda\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Suppose that
h_{0}
and
h
are two Hermitian metrics on
E
. We set
H=h\cdot h_{0}^{-1}
and

(3.1)

\Phi^{\lambda}:=\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{{h}}\right)\right)\cdot h_{0}^{-1}-\lambda H\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E).

If there exist constants
C
,
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
such that
|\lambda|<c_{1}
,
|\Phi^{\lambda}|_{C^{1}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}<c_{2}

and

(3.2)

C^{-1}h_{0}\leq h\leq Ch_{0},

then the following
C^{1}
-estimate holds:

(3.3)

|H|_{C^{1}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}\leq C_{1},

where the constant
C_{1}
depends on
M,\omega_{g},h_{0},C,c_{1}
and
c_{2}
.

Let
{\mathscr{E}}:=T^{*1,0}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes E
be the Hermitian holomorphic vector bundle with the Hermitian metric induced by
g
on
T^{*1,0}M
and
h
on
E
. Let
T

We set

R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}=h^{\alpha\overline{\beta}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}},\quad R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}\quad\mbox{and}\quad s_{h}=g^{i\overline{j}}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}.

The following expressions are essentially well-known:

(6.19)

\int_{M}c^{2}_{1}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

and

(6.20)

\int_{M}c_{2}(E)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}=\frac{1}{8\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|_{g}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}_{h}+|R^{h}|_{g,h}^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

We define
T\in\Gamma(M,\Lambda^{1,1}T^{*}M\otimes E^{*}\otimes\overline{E}^{*})
:

(6.21)

T_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}=R^{h}_{i\overline{j}\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{n}g_{i\overline{j}}R^{(2)}_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}-\frac{1}{r}R^{(1)}_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}+\frac{1}{nr}g_{i\overline{j}}h_{\alpha\overline{\beta}}s_{h}.

A straightforward calculation shows that

(6.22)

|T|^{2}=|R^{h}|^{2}-\frac{1}{n}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}-\frac{1}{r}\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|^{2}+\frac{1}{nr}s_{h}^{2}.

One has

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(s_{h}^{2}-\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(1)}\right|^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}+r|R^{h}|^{2}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}\left(r|T|^{2}+\frac{n-1}{n}\left(s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n},

where we substitute
|T|^{2}
in the second identity.
Let
\{\lambda_{1},\cdots,\lambda_{r}\}
be the eigenvalues of
\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)
with respect to
h
. If we assume that

ah\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)\leq bh,

for
a,b\in{\mathbb{R}}
, then

a\leq\lambda_{k}\leq b
.
A straightforward combinatorial argument shows that

(6.23)

\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\leq\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor(b-a)^{2}.

This yields

(6.24)

s_{h}^{2}-r\left|\mathrm{Ric}^{(2)}\right|^{2}=\left(\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}\right)^{2}-r\sum_{k=1}^{r}\lambda_{k}^{2}=-\sum_{i<j}(\lambda_{i}-\lambda_{j})^{2}\geq-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor(b-a)^{2}.

Hence, the following Chern number inequality holds:

\int_{M}\left(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\geq-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor\frac{\left(b-a\right)^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}+\frac{r}{4\pi^{2}n(n-1)}\int_{M}|T|^{2}\omega_{g}^{n}.

By Theorem
6.2
, for any
\varepsilon>0
, we have
a=\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon
,
b=\lambda_{E}^{+}+\varepsilon
and
h=h_{\varepsilon}
. Hence,

\int_{M}\left(2rc_{2}(E)-(r-1)c^{2}_{1}(E)\right)\wedge\omega_{g}^{n-2}\geq-\Bigl\lfloor\frac{r^{2}}{4}\Bigr\rfloor\frac{\left(\lambda_{E}^{+}-\lambda_{E}^{-}+2\varepsilon\right)^{2}}{4\pi^{2}n^{2}}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}
, the conclusion follows.
∎

7.
Proof of Theorem
1.7

In this section, we provide an alternative proof of the Donaldson-Uhlenbeck-Yau theorem for stable vector bundles and prove Theorem
1.7
:

Theorem 7.1
.

Let
(M,\omega_{g})
be a compact Gauduchon manifold and

E
be a holomorphic vector bundle over
M
. If
E
is
semi-stable, then for any
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
and for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a unique smooth Hermitian metric
h_{\varepsilon}

on
E
such that

(7.1)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}-\varepsilon)h_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon P,

and
E
is approximately
Hermitian-Einstein. Moreover, if
E
is stable, then a subsequence

\{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
converges smoothly to a Hermitian-Einstein metric

h
on
E
as
\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0
.

Proof.

It is clear that for any vector bundle
\lambda_{E}\geq\lambda_{E}^{-}
. Since
E
is semi-stable, for any coherent quotient
sheaf
\mathscr{Q}
with
\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q})>0
, one has

\lambda_{E}\leq\lambda_{{\mathscr{Q}}}.
Hence,

(7.2)

\lambda_{E}=\lambda_{E}^{-}

Fix
P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
. We set

h_{0}=P
on
E
. For any
\varepsilon>0
, by Theorem
1.1
,
there exists a unique Hermitian metric
h_{\varepsilon}
on
E
such that

(7.3)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon)h_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon P.

Taking
trace with respect to
h_{\varepsilon}
in (
7.3
) yields

(7.4)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon)\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\varepsilon H_{\varepsilon}^{-1},

where
H_{\varepsilon}=h_{\varepsilon}\cdot h_{0}^{-1}\in\Gamma(M,E^{*}\otimes E)
. On the other hand, we know

(7.5)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=\lambda_{E}{\rm rk}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}=\lambda_{E}^{-}{\rm rk}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Taking the trace of (
7.4
) and integrating over
M
,
we deduce that

(7.6)

\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}={\rm rk}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

On the other hand, by a straightforward computation

\displaystyle\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right)=\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\overline{{\partial}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle+\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right).

Since
{\overline{\partial}}H_{\varepsilon}
and
{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}
are
h_{0}
-adjoint, by (3) of Lemma
2.2
, we
have

(7.7)

-\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\overline{{\partial}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot{\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right)\geq 0.

By (
2.5
)
and (
7.4
),

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\sqrt{-1}\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\overline{{\partial}}\left({\partial}^{h_{0}}H_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot\left((\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon)\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\varepsilon H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{0}}\right)\right)\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle(C-\varepsilon)\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{E}(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}),

where
C=C(M,\omega_{g},h_{0},\lambda_{E}^{-})
. Hence, for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,
one has

(7.8)

\Delta_{\mathbb{C}}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\geq(C-1)\cdot\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right).

The Moser iteration shows

(7.9)

\|\mathrm{tr}_{E}\bigl(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bigr)\|^{2}_{L^{\infty}}\leq\widetilde{C}_{1}\|\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

where
\widetilde{C}_{1}=\widetilde{C}_{1}(M,\omega_{g},C)>0
. By (
7.6
),
one concludes that for all
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

(7.10)

\mathrm{tr}_{E}\bigl(H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\bigr)\leq C_{1}

where

C_{1}=\widetilde{C}_{1}{\rm rk}(E)\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}
.
In particular, one obtains the uniform
C^{0}
-estimate:

(7.11)

H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\leq C_{1}\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

Moreover, for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
, one has

(7.12)

-C_{2}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\leq H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\leq C_{2}\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

where
C_{2}=\max\{C_{1},1\}
. In particular,
by (
7.4
),

(7.13)

\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left\|\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)-\lambda_{E}^{-}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}=\lim_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon\left\|H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}-\mathrm{Id}_{E}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})}=0.

Therefore,
E
is approximately Hermitian-Einstein.

Furthermore, we assume that
E
is stable. Under this assumption, we established that, for any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

(7.14)

H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\leq C_{1}\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

Now we turn to the
uniform upper bound for
h_{\varepsilon}
. Let

(7.15)

\Lambda_{\varepsilon}:=\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{\varepsilon})

denote the supremum over
M
of the
largest eigenvalue of
H_{\varepsilon}
. Define

(7.16)

\widetilde{h}_{\varepsilon}:=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot h_{\varepsilon},\quad\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}:=\Lambda_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}.

Then it is clear that
\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}
with respect to both
h_{0}
and
h_{\varepsilon}
.

Suppose that

(7.17)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{\varepsilon})=+\infty.

By (
7.6
), one has

(7.18)

{\rm rk}(E)\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\cdot H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}=\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}\leq{\rm rk}(E)\sup_{M}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}.

Hence, we deduce that
\Lambda_{\varepsilon}\leq\sup_{M}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})
and so

(7.19)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{M}\lambda_{\mathrm{max}}(\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}^{-1})=+\infty.

By
(
7.3
), one has

(7.20)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{\widetilde{h}_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon)\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\varepsilon H_{\varepsilon}^{-1}.

Hence, there
exists a constant
C_{3}=C_{3}(C_{1},\lambda_{E}^{-})>0
such that for
any
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
,

(7.21)

-C_{3}\mathrm{Id}_{E}\leq\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{\widetilde{h}_{\varepsilon}}\right)\leq C_{3}\mathrm{Id}_{E},\quad 0<\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}\leq\mathrm{Id}_{E}.

By using a strategy similar to that in the proof of
Theorem
1.1
, there exist a subsequence
\{\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
of
\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}
, and a sequence
\{\tau_{i}\}\subset(0,1/2]
decreasing to
0
such that

(1)

\lim_{i}\Lambda_{\varepsilon_{i}}=+\infty
.

(2)

\{\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}
converges to some
\widetilde{H}_{0}
in weak
W^{1,2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})
sense.

(3)

\{\widetilde{H}^{2\tau_{i}}_{0}\}
converges to some
\widetilde{H}
in weak
W^{1,2}(M,\omega_{g},h_{0})
sense.

(4)

\pi_{1}=\mathrm{Id}_{E}-\widetilde{H}
defines a weak holomorphic projection with respect to
h_{0}
. Let
{\mathcal{F}}_{1}
be the coherent subsheaf of
E
defined by
\pi_{1}
. By (
7.19
) and a uniform positive lower bound of
\left\|\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon}\right\|_{L^{2};h_{0}}
, one has

(7.22)

0<\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{F}_{1})<\mathrm{rk}(E).

Moreover, denoting
\mathscr{Q}_{1}=E/\mathcal{F}_{1}
, we obtain the
following estimate by a proof similar to that of
(
5.49
):

(7.23)

\mathrm{deg}_{\omega_{g}}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})\leq\limsup_{\tau_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\int_{M}\mathrm{tr}_{E}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}}\right)\cdot\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{j}}\right)\omega_{g}^{n}.

On the other hand, by
(
7.20
), for any
2\tau_{j}<1
,

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}}\right),\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{j}}\right)_{h_{0}}=\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left((\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon_{i})\mathrm{Id}_{E}+\varepsilon_{i}H_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{-1},\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{j}}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left((\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon_{i})\mathrm{Id}_{E},\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{k}}\right)_{h_{0}}+\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\varepsilon_{i}\left(H_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{-1},\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{j}}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{E}^{-}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E},\widetilde{H}^{2\tau_{j}}_{0}\right)_{h_{0}}.

As
\tau_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+}
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\limsup_{\varepsilon_{i}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\left(\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}\Theta^{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}}\right),\widetilde{H}_{\varepsilon_{i}}^{2\tau_{j}}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\limsup_{\tau_{j}\rightarrow 0^{+}}\lambda_{E}^{-}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E},\widetilde{H}^{2\tau_{j}}_{0}\right)_{h_{0}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\lambda_{E}^{-}\left(\mathrm{Id}_{E},\widetilde{H}\right)_{h_{0}}=\lambda_{E}^{-}\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})\left(\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}\right).

Hence, by (
7.23
), one has

(7.24)

\mathrm{deg}_{\omega_{g}}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})\leq\lambda_{E}^{-}\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})\left(\int_{M}\omega_{g}^{n}\right).

By (
7.24
) and the fact that
\lambda_{E}^{-}=\lambda_{E}
, one has

(7.25)

\frac{\mathrm{deg}_{\omega_{g}}(E)}{\mathrm{rk}(E)}\geq\frac{\mathrm{deg}_{\omega_{g}}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})}{\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})}.

Moreover,
0<\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{F}_{1})<\mathrm{rk}(E)
, and
0<\mathrm{rk}(\mathscr{Q}_{1})<\mathrm{rk}(E)
. This contradicts the assumption that
E
is stable.

Hence, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that

(7.26)

\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\Lambda_{\varepsilon}=\limsup_{\varepsilon\rightarrow 0+}\sup_{M}\lambda_{\max}(H_{\varepsilon})=C<+\infty.

Then we have

(7.27)

h_{\varepsilon}\leq Ch_{0},

for all
\varepsilon
close to
0
. We rewrite the equation
(
7.3
) as

(7.28)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=\lambda_{\varepsilon}h_{\varepsilon}+P_{\varepsilon},

where

\lambda_{\varepsilon}=\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon
and
P_{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon P
. It is
obvious that
\{P_{\varepsilon}\}
converges smoothly to
P=0
. By Theorem

4.1
, there exists a subsequence
\{h_{\varepsilon_{i}}\}

which converges smoothly to a smooth Hermitian metric
h
on
E
,
and this limit satisfies

(7.29)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h}\right)=\lambda_{E}^{-}h.

In particular,
(E,h)
is Hermitian-Einstein. This completes the
proof. ∎

Remark 7.2
.

If
E
is not stable, Theorem
1.1

guarantees the existence of a family of Hermitian metrics

h_{\varepsilon}
solving

(7.30)

\Lambda_{\omega_{g}}\left(\sqrt{-1}R^{h_{\varepsilon}}\right)=(\lambda_{E}^{-}-\varepsilon)\,h_{\varepsilon}+\varepsilon P,

for any fixed

P\in\mathrm{Herm}^{+}(E)
and
\varepsilon>0
. It is natural to study the
limit of
h_{\varepsilon}
as
\varepsilon\to 0^{+}
.
