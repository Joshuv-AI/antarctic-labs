1.0 Summary
 This field campaign extension has been a coordinated effort involving the U.S. Department of Energy
 Atmospheric Radiation Measurement (ARM) User Facility, the University of Wisconsin at Madison, and
 the Joint Polar Satellite System (JPSS) project to validate NOAA Unique Combined Atmospheric
 Processing System (NUCAPS) temperature and moisture sounding products from the Cross-track Infrared
 Sounder (CrIS) and the Advanced Technology Microwave Sounder (ATMS) instruments on board the
 NOAA-20 and NOAA-21 satellite platforms. In this arrangement, funding for radiosondes and balloons
 was provided by the JPSS project to ARM. These radiosondes were launched coincident with NOAA-20
 and NOAA-21 satellite overpasses at the ARM field sites at Eastern North Atlantic (ENA), North Slope
 Alaska (NSA), and Southern Great Plains (SGP). Combined with other ARM data, an assessment of the
 radiosonde data quality was performed and post-processing corrections applied. The dedicated
 radiosondes were integrated into the NOAA Products Validation System (NPROVS+), which collocated
 the radiosondes with satellite products (NOAA, National Aeronautics and Space Administration [NASA],
 European Organisation for the Exploitation of Meteorological Satellites [EUMETSAT], Geostationary
 Operational Environmental Satellite [GOES], Constellation Observing System for Meteorology,
 Ionosphere, and Climate [COSMIC]) and numerical weather prediction (NWP) forecasts for use in
 product assessment and algorithm development. This work is a part of the NOAA-20 and NOAA-21
 satellite retrieval validation efforts and provides critical accuracy assessments of the temperature and
 water vapor soundings.

 While this report is a summary of activities covering the two-year period ending 10-17-2024, this effort is
 part of a larger and longer effort which began in July of 2012 with the goal of providing ongoing retrieval
 validation for the Suomi National Polar-orbiting Partnership (SNPP) satellite. SNPP is the bridge satellite
 between NOAA’s legacy satellites and the current JPSS, which includes NOAA-20 (JPSS-1), NOAA-21
 (JPSS-2), and the future JPSS-3 and JPSS-4 satellites. The focus of these early validation efforts switched
 from SNPP to NOAA-20 in February 2018. Similarly, NOAA-21 dedicated radiosonde launches began in
 June 2023. During these periods, dedicated radiosonde launches were performed across sites, seasons, and
 in both day and night conditions, building up a statistically significant data set of radiosondes for
 validation. ARM is uniquely set up for this type of ongoing campaign.

 Radiosonde Launch Efforts

 During this reporting period, Vaisala RS41 radiosondes were launched targeting either NOAA-20 or
 NOAA-21 overpasses of the ENA, NSA, and SGP sites. NOAA-20 was targeted through the end of May
 2023, which covers approximately seven months of the effort focused on NOAA-20. A total of 20/22/9
 NOAA-20 overpasses were targeted at the ENA/NSA/SGP sites respectively. The radiosonde launch pace
 at SGP was slower than planned due to a helium supply shortage. Four of these NOAA-20 radiosondes
 were twin-sonde launches, while the remaining were single-sonde launches. NOAA-21 was targeted
 beginning in June 2023 and is ongoing. A total of 57/85/79 NOAA-21 overpasses were targeted at the
 ENA/NSA/SGP sites respectively with single-sonde launches. Details on the timing of single- and
 twin-sonde launches is shown in Figure 1 and the number of sondes launched during this reporting period
 in Table 1.
