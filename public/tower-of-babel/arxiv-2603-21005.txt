Ties in function field prime race
Graeme Bates, Ryan Jesubalan, Seewoo Lee, Jane Lu, and Hyewon Shim

Abstract
. Function field analogue of the Chebyshev’s bias was first studied by Cha. In this paper, we study ties in race, i.e. pairs of congruence classes a , b ∈ ( 𝔽 q ​ [ T ] / m ) × a,b\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times} where π ⁡ ( N , m , a ) = π ⁡ ( N , m , b ) \pi(N;m,a)=\pi(N;m,b) holds for infinitely many N N . We provide infinitely many examples of ( m , a , b ) (m,a,b) where the tie holds for N N satisfying some certain congruence conditions. We give two different proofs, by 1) using the explicit formula via L L -functions and matrix analogue of Möbius inversion formula, where exceptional Galois conjugate pairs of elements in the corresponding cyclotomic fields give ties, and 2) constructing an explicit bijection via GL 2 ​ ( 𝔽 q ) \mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q}) -action. Our examples also include characteristic 2 cases.

T^{3}+T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]

In 1853, Chebyshev
[
3
]
observed that there are typically more primes of the form
4n+3
then
4n+1
up to certain threshold, and conjectured that the counting is essentially
biased
towards primes
\equiv 3\pmod{4}
.
More precisely, if we denote
\pi(x;m,a)
for the number of primes
\leq x
which are congruent to
a
modulo
m
, he conjectured that the difference

\Delta(x):=\pi(x;4,3)-\pi(x;4,1)

is eventually positive for sufficiently large
x
.
However, Littlewood
[
7
]
proved that there are infinitely many sign changes of
\Delta(x)
; in particular, there are infinitely many
x
with
\Delta(x)<0
.
Later, under the Linear Independence conjecture (LI) for Dirichlet
L
-functions, Rubinstein and Sarnak
[
11
]
give more precise analysis by computing the
logarithmic density
of the primes
\equiv 3\pmod{4}
is about
0.9959
, which is close to 1 but not exactly.
Subsequent works studied such
prime number races
in more general setup; see
[
4
]
for such works.

Cha
[
2
]
studied Chebyshev’s bias over function fields.
Following Rubinstein and Sarnak’s argument, he proved that similar bias of prime counts toward non-quadratic residues exist under Grand Simplicity Hypothesis (GSH) for Dirichlet
L
-functions.
While LI is believed to be true over number fields, Cha found several examples of Dirichlet
L
-functions over function fields that
does not
satisfy GSH, which also give bias in unexpected directions.

In this paper, we study
ties
in Chebyshev bias over function fields.
Let
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a polynomial and
a\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
be a congruence class modulo
m
.
For
N\geq 1
, let
\pi(N;m,a)
be the number of irreducible monic polynomials in
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
of degree
N
which are congruent to
a
modulo
m
.
We found family of examples of modulus
m
and distinct congruence classes
c_{1},\dots,c_{k}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
where

\pi(N;m,c_{1})=\pi(N;m,c_{2})=\dots=\pi(N;m,c_{k})

holds for all
N
in certain arithmetic progressions.
For example, when
m=T^{3}+T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
, we show that there are ties

\pi(N;m,1)=\pi(N;m,T)=\pi(N;m,T+1)\quad\text{and}\quad\pi(N;m,T^{2})=\pi(N;m,T^{2}+T)=\pi(N;m,T^{2}+T+1)

(1)

for all
N\equiv 1\pmod{7}
(See Table
1
).

To prove such ties, we provide two different approaches.
First method uses explicit formula of the prime counts
\pi(N;m,a)
in terms of zeros of Dirichlet
L
-functions and a matrix version of Möbius inversioN; where ties come from “exceptional” Galois conjugates (Section
3
).
Second method construct bijection between primes in different congruence classes via
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-actions (Section
4
).
The accompanying Sage code that are used to implement explicit formulas and generate tables can be found in the GitHub repository
github.com/seewoo5/sage-function-field
.

2   Preliminaries

2.1   Arithmetic of function fields

Let
p
be a prime and
A=\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a polynomial ring over a finite field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
of size
q=p^{k}
.
We denote
A^{\circ}\subset A
for the subset of monic polynomials, and
\mathcal{I}\subset A^{\circ}
be the set of monic irreducible polynomials.
For each
N
, we denote by
\mathcal{I}(N)
for the set of degree
N
monic irreducible polynomials, and
\pi(N)=\#\mathcal{I}(N)
be the number of such polynomials.
The famous formula due to Gauss tells us that

\pi(N)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{d\mid N}\mu(d)q^{N/d}=\frac{q^{N}}{N}+O\left(\frac{q^{N/2}}{N}\right)

(2)

where
\mu
is the Möbius function, analogous to the prime number theorem.
When
m\in A^{\circ}
is a monic polynomial and
c\in A
is an another polynomial coprime to
m
, we write
\mathcal{I}(N;m,c)
for the set of monic irreducible polynomials of degree
N
that are congruent to
c
modulo
m
.
Then
\pi(N;m,c):=\#\mathcal{I}(N;m,c)
is known to be

\pi(N;m,c)=\frac{1}{\Phi(m)}\frac{q^{N}}{N}+O\left(\frac{q^{N/2}}{N}\right)

(3)

as
N\to\infty
, where
\Phi(m)=\#(A/m)^{\times}
is the Euler totient function for
A
, which is a function field analogue of Dirichlet’s theorem on arithmetic progressions.

For a Dirichlet character
\chi
of modulus
m\in A^{\circ}
, we define the corresponding
L
-function as

L(s,\chi):=\sum_{n\geq 0}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in A^{\circ}\\
\deg f=n\end{subarray}}\chi(f)\right)q^{-ns}=\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\in\mathcal{I}\\
P\nmid m\end{subarray}}(1-\chi(P)q^{-(\deg P)s})^{-1}

(4)

which is known to be a polynomial in
u=q^{-s}
of degree at most
M-1
, where
M=\deg m
.
We denote the corresponding polynomial as
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)\in\mathbb{C}[u]
.
Weil’s Riemann Hypothesis on function fields shows that the zeros of
L(s,\chi)
satisfy
s=0
or
\Re s=\frac{1}{2}
.

2.2   Chebyshev’s bias

Chebyshev’s bias
[
3
]
refers to the phenomenon that the number of primes of the form
4k+3
are usually larger than those of the form
4k+1
.
This generalizes to arbitrary modulus, where primes are biased against quadratic residues.
The major progress on the conjecture was made by Rubinstein and Sarnak
[
11
]
, where they proved such bias exists for any modulus under the Linear Independence hypothesis (LI) for Dirichlet
L
-functions, conjecturing that all the nonnegative ordinates of Dirichlet
L
-functions are linearly independent over
\mathbb{Q}
.

Some of the earlier works
[
5
,
6
,
9
,
8
]
also study the number of
sign changes
or
ties
of the bias, where the former gives the obvious lower bound for the latter in the same prime race.
Although
[
5
,
9
,
6
]
mostly focus on the sign change of
V(x):=\pi(x)-\mathrm{li}(x)
, same argument generalizes to the mod 4 race
\Delta(x)
.
Ingham
[
5
]
proved that
V(x)=\Omega(\log x)
, assuming that the upper bounds on the real part of the zeros of
\zeta(s)
is obtained by a zero (which is weaker than RH).
Unconditional (ineffective) lower bound of
V(x)=\Omega(\log\log x)
was first obtained by Knapowski
[
6
]
, where Pintz
[
9
]
proved the (effective) lower bound of
V(x)=\Omega(\sqrt{\log x}/\log\log x)
.
Martin and Ng
[
8
]
studied prime races under hypothesis weaker than LI, and proved that the ties have logarithmic density zero.

Cha
[
2
]
studied function field analogue of Chebyshev’s bias. By following Rubinstein and Sarnak’s argument, he proved analogous results for the polynomials over finite fields of odd characteristic.
More precisely, for a monic polynomial
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, if GSH holds for Dirichlet
L
-functions of Dirichlet characters modulo
m
, he proved that the natural density of degrees
N
where there are more non-quadratic residues of degree
N
than quadratic residues modulo
m
is larger than
\frac{1}{2}
.
Surprisingly, there are some examples where Grand Simplicity Hypothesis (GSH, which is a different name of LI for
L
-functions over function felds) is violated, and he also gave such examples where the bias happens in unexpected direction (e.g. towards quadratic residues).
He also proved that the bias vanishes as
\deg m\to\infty
.

Bailleul, Devin, Keliher, and Li studied exceptional biases in function fields
[
1
]
, such as
complete bias
,
lower order bias
, and
reversed bias
.
In particular, when
m
is a square-free monic polynomial and
\chi_{m}
be the unique principal quadratic character modulo
m
(so necessarily
q
is odd), they gave sufficient and necessary conditions for
\chi_{m}
admitting lower order bias, i.e. the set of degrees
N
with ties

\displaystyle\#\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]:f\text{ monic, irreducible, }\deg f=N,\chi_{m}(f)=1\}

(5)

\displaystyle=\#\{f\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]:f\text{ monic, irreducible, }\deg f=N,\chi_{m}(f)=-1\}

has a positive natural density.
For example, they proved that there is a lower order bias for
\chi_{m}
when a (completed)
L
-polynomial for
\chi_{m}
is even (see
[
1
, Lemma 5.2]
for the precise statement).

3   Explicit counting formulas

3.1   Matrix Möbius inversion and explicit counting formulas

As mentioned earlier in Section
2.1
, one can prove (
3
) by considering all Dirichlet
L
-functions together and use Weil’s RH to estimate error terms.
Here we compute all the terms more explicitly, without bounding any error terms and give exact formulas of
\pi(N;m,c)
as a certain Möbius inversion of matrices associated to the modulus
m
.

Let
M=\deg m
.
We denote the unit group
(A/m)^{\times}
and its character group as
\mathscr{U}_{m}:=(A/m)^{\times}
and
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}:=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathscr{U}_{m},\mathbb{C}^{\times})
, respectively.
Let
M^{\prime}=\Phi(m)=|\mathscr{U}_{m}|
be the size of the group.
For
\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}
and
n\geq 1
, define

A_{\chi}(n):=\sum_{c\in\mathscr{U}_{m}}\pi(N;m,c)\chi(c).

(6)

For nontrivial
\chi
, write
\alpha_{1}(\chi),\dots,\alpha_{d}(\chi)
with
d=d(\chi)
for the inverse zeroes of
\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)
, i.e.

\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\prod_{j=1}^{d(\chi)}(1-\alpha_{j}(\chi)u).

(7)

By taking logarithmic derivative, one can easily see that

u\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\log\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)=\frac{u\mathcal{L}^{\prime}(u,\chi)}{\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)}=-\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{d(\chi)}\alpha_{j}(\chi)^{n}\right)u^{n}=:\sum_{n\geq 1}c_{n}(\chi)u^{n}.

(8)

where

c_{n}(\chi):=-\sum_{j=1}^{d(\chi)}\alpha_{j}(\chi)^{n}.

(9)

By the way, taking the logarithmic derivative of the Euler product expansion (
4
) gives

\displaystyle u\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}u}\log\mathcal{L}(u,\chi)

\displaystyle=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{I}}\frac{\chi(P)\deg Pu^{\deg P}}{1-\chi(P)u^{\deg P}}=\sum_{P\in\mathcal{I}}\deg P\sum_{k\geq 1}\chi(P)^{k}u^{k\deg P}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{d\mid n}d\sum_{P\in\mathcal{I}(d)}\chi(P)^{n/d}u^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\sum_{d\mid n}d\sum_{c\in\mathscr{U}_{m}}\pi(N;m,c)\chi^{n/d}(c)u^{n}

\displaystyle=\sum_{n\geq 1}\left(\sum_{d\mid n}dA_{\chi^{n/d}}(d)\right)u^{n}

(10)

and comparing (
9
) and (
10
) gives

c_{n}(\chi)=\sum_{d\mid n}dA_{\chi^{n/d}}(d).

(11)

When
\chi=\chi_{0}
is the trivial character modulo
m
, we have

A_{\chi_{0}}(d)=\sum_{c\in\mathscr{U}_{m}}\pi(d;m,c)=\pi(d)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid m\,\text{irred}\\
\deg P=d\end{subarray}}1

(12)

and summing
dA_{\chi_{0}}(d)
over
d\mid n
gives

\sum_{d\mid n}dA_{\chi_{0}}(d)=\sum_{d\mid n}d\pi(d)-\sum_{d\mid n}d\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid m\,\text{irred}\\
\deg P=d\end{subarray}}1=q^{n}-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid m\,\text{irred}\\
\deg P\mid n\end{subarray}}\deg P,

(13)

where the last equality follows from
[
10
, Proposition 2.1]
.
We denote

s_{m,n}:=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}P\mid m\,\text{irred}\\
\deg P\mid n\end{subarray}}\deg P

(14)

so that the last term of (
13
) becomes
q^{n}-s_{m,n}
.
When
m
is irreducible, then
s_{m,n}=M\delta_{M\mid n}
where
\delta_{M\mid n}
is 1 (resp. 0) if
M\mid n
(resp.
M\nmid n
).

Now, define a map
Z(n)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\mathscr{U}_{m}},\mathbb{C}^{\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}})
as

v=(v_{a})_{a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}}\mapsto v^{\prime}=(v^{\prime}_{\chi})_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}},\,\,v^{\prime}_{\chi}=\sum_{a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}}\chi^{n}(a)v_{a}

(15)

for each
n\in\mathbb{Z}
, which can be thought as a matrix where
(\chi,a)
-th entry is
\chi^{n}(a)
.
Then (
11
) and (
13
) can be encoded as a system of linear equation

\begin{pmatrix}q^{n}-s_{m,n}\\
\vdots\\
c_{n}(\chi)\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{d\mid n}d\begin{pmatrix}A_{\chi_{0}}(d)\\
\vdots\\
A_{\chi}(d)\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}=\sum_{d\mid n}Z\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\begin{pmatrix}d\pi(1,m,d)\\
\vdots\\
d\pi(a,m,d)\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}.

(16)

To get formula for
\pi(c,m,d)
by solving the system of equation, we prove Möbius inversion formula for
Z(n)
.
For each
n\geq 1
, we define
Möbius inverse

\widetilde{Z}(n)\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathbb{C}^{\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}},\mathbb{C}^{\mathscr{U}_{m}})
inductively as

\displaystyle\widetilde{Z}(1)Z(1)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{id}_{\mathbb{C}^{\mathscr{U}_{m}}}

(17)

\displaystyle\sum_{d\mid n}\widetilde{Z}(d)Z\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)

\displaystyle=0\Leftrightarrow\widetilde{Z}(n)Z(1)=-\sum_{d\mid n,d<n}\widetilde{Z}(d)Z\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\quad(n\geq 2)

(18)

Then we can apply “Möbius inversion”, i.e. taking Dirichlet convolution between
\widetilde{Z}(n)
with (
16
) to get

\begin{pmatrix}\pi(N;m,1)\\
\vdots\\
\pi(N;m,a)\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{d\mid N}\widetilde{Z}(d)\begin{pmatrix}q^{\frac{N}{d}}-s_{m,\frac{N}{d}}\\
\vdots\\
c_{\frac{N}{d}}(\chi)\\
\vdots\end{pmatrix}

(19)

and if we write the
(a,\chi)
-th entry of
\widetilde{Z}(d)
as
\widetilde{Z}(d)_{a,\chi}
, then this gives

\pi(N;m,a)=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{d\mid N}\left(\widetilde{Z}(d)_{a,\chi_{0}}(q^{\frac{N}{d}}-s_{m,\frac{N}{d}})-\sum_{\chi\neq\chi_{0}}\widetilde{Z}(d)_{a,\chi}\sum_{j=1}^{d(\chi)}\alpha_{j}(\chi)^{\frac{N}{d}}\right).

(20)

Now, the main result of this section is the formula of
\widetilde{Z}(n)
(Proposition
3.1
).

Proposition 3.1
.

The
(a,\chi)
-th entry of
\widetilde{Z}(n)
is given by

\widetilde{Z}(n)_{a,\chi}=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}\\
b^{n}=a\end{subarray}}\chi(b)^{-1}.

(21)

Proof.

The
n=1
case follows from (
17
).
For
n\geq 2
, since
\widetilde{Z}(n)
is uniquely determined by the recurrence relation (
18
), it is enough to show that

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{d\mid n}\widetilde{Z}(d)Z\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)\right)_{(c_{1},c_{2})}=\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}}\sum_{d\mid n}\widetilde{Z}(d)_{c_{1},\chi}Z\left(\frac{n}{d}\right)_{\chi,c_{2}}=0

for all
c_{1},c_{2}\in\mathscr{U}_{m}
, given (
21
).
Expanding this gives

\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}}\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\mu(d)}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}\\
b^{d}=c_{1}\end{subarray}}\chi(b)^{-1}\chi(c_{2})^{n/d}=\frac{1}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}\\
b^{d}=c_{1}\end{subarray}}\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}}\chi(b^{-1}c_{2}^{n/d})

(22)

By the orthogonality of characters, the innermost sum of the right hand side of (
22
) is

\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}}\chi(b^{-1}c_{2}^{n/d})=\begin{cases}M^{\prime}&b=c_{2}^{n/d}\\
0&b\neq c_{2}^{n/d}\end{cases}

and plugging this in (
22
) gives

\displaystyle\sum_{\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}}\sum_{d\mid n}\frac{\mu(d)}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}\\
b^{d}=c_{1}\end{subarray}}\chi(b)^{-1}\chi(c_{2})^{n/d}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{M^{\prime}}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)M^{\prime}\delta_{c_{1},c_{2}^{n}}=\delta_{c_{1},c_{2}^{n}}\sum_{d\mid n}\mu(d)=0.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

(1)

If
\gcd(n,M^{\prime})=1
, then

\widetilde{Z}(n)=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}Z(n^{-1})^{H},\quad(\widetilde{Z}(n))_{a,\chi}=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\chi(a)^{-n^{-1}}

(23)

where
n^{-1}
is the inverse of
n
modulo
M^{\prime}
.

(2)

For a prime
\ell
dividing
M^{\prime}
, we have

\widetilde{Z}(p)_{a,\chi}=-\frac{\ell}{M^{\prime}}\times\begin{cases}\chi(a^{1/\ell})^{-1}&a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}^{\ell},\,\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}^{\ell}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(24)

where
\mathscr{U}_{m}^{\ell}\subset\mathscr{U}_{m}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}^{\ell}\subset\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}
) is image of the
\ell
-power map.
Note that
\chi(a^{1/\ell})
does not depend on the choice of
\ell
-th root
a^{1/\ell}
of
a
.

(3)

Assume
\mathscr{U}_{m}
is cyclic. Let
g=\gcd(n,M^{\prime})
. We have

\widetilde{Z}(n)_{a,\chi}=\frac{\mu(n)g}{M^{\prime}}\times\begin{cases}\chi(a^{1/g})^{-(n/g)^{-1}}&a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}^{g},\,\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}^{g}\\
0&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(25)

where
\mathscr{U}_{m}^{g}\subset\mathscr{U}_{m}
(resp.
\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}^{g}\subset\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}
) is image of the
g
-power map, and
(n/g)^{-1}
is the inverse of
n/g
modulo
M^{\prime}
.
Note that
\widetilde{Z}(n)_{a,\chi}
does not depend on the choice of the
g
-th root
a^{1/g}
of
a
.

Proof.

(1)

When
n
and
M^{\prime}
are coprime, the
n
-power map
x\mapsto x^{n}
is an isomorphism from
\mathscr{U}_{m}
to itself.
Thus for given
a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}
and
\chi\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}
, there exists a unique
b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}
with
b^{n}=a
and
a^{n^{-1}}=(b^{n})^{n^{-1}}=b^{n\cdot n^{-1}}=b
.
Then (
21
) gives

\widetilde{Z}(n)_{a,\chi}=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\chi(b)^{-1}=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\chi(b^{-1})=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\chi(a^{-n^{-1}})=\frac{\mu(n)}{M^{\prime}}\chi(a)^{-n^{-1}}.

(2)

When a prime
\ell
divides
M^{\prime}
,
\mathscr{U}_{m}^{\ell}\subset\mathscr{U}_{m}
is an index
\ell
-subgroup of
\mathscr{U}_{m}
, and for each
a\in\mathscr{U}_{m}^{\ell}
, there are
\ell
-many
b\in\mathscr{U}_{m}
with
b^{\ell}=a
.
If
b_{1}^{\ell}=b_{2}^{\ell}=a
and
\chi=\chi_{1}^{\ell}\in\widehat{\mathscr{U}}_{m}^{\ell}
,
(b_{1}^{-1}b_{2})^{\ell}=1
and
\chi(b_{1}^{-1}b_{2})=\chi_{1}(b_{1}^{-1}b_{2})^{\ell}=1
, so
\chi(b_{1})=\chi(b_{2})
.
Hence
\chi(a^{1/\ell})
does not depend on the choice of
a^{1/\ell}
and
\mu(\ell)=-1

Lemma 3.3

Let

4   Explicit bijections

In this section, we provide an alternative way to prove ties using
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-actions.

4.1   Bijection via
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
-action

For each
c\in(A/m)^{\times}
, define
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(N;m,c)
to be the set of irreducible polynomials
f\equiv c\pmod{m}
with degree
N
,
not necessarily monic
, and let
\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c):=\#\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(N;m,c)
be the number of such polynomials.
It trivially contains
\mathcal{I}(N;m,c)
.
For
f=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}T^{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, write
\mathrm{lc}(f):=a_{n}
for the leading coefficient of
f
.
By normalizing a polynomial, we have

f\equiv c\pmod{m}\,\,\Leftrightarrow\,\,\mathrm{lc}(f)^{-1}f\equiv\mathrm{lc}(f)^{-1}c\pmod{m}

(37)

which implies

\#\{f\in\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(N;m,c):\mathrm{lc}(f)=\lambda\}=\pi(\lambda^{-1}N;m,c).

By summing over all possible leading coefficients, we get

\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\pi(\lambda^{-1}N;m,c)=\sum_{\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}}\pi(\lambda N;m,c).

(38)

From this, we have the following “uninteresting“ ties between non-monic prime counts.

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
c\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
and
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
, we have

\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c)=\widetilde{\pi}(\lambda N;m,c).

(39)

Now, for a matrix
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, define

(f|_{n}B)(T):=(\gamma T+\delta)^{n}f\left(\frac{\alpha T+\beta}{\gamma T+\delta}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(\alpha T+\beta)^{i}(\gamma T+\delta)^{n-i}

(40)

For example, when
f(0)\neq 0
,
n=\deg f
and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, then
f|_{n}B
is a reciprocal of
f(T)
, i.e.

f(T)=a_{n}T^{n}+a_{n-1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{1}T+a_{0}\Rightarrow(f|_{n}B)(T)=a_{0}T^{n}+a_{1}T^{n-1}+\cdots+a_{n-1}T+a_{n}.

For each
n
, the map
f\mapsto f|_{n}B
defines an action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
on
\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
, i.e.
f|_{n}(B_{1}B_{2})=(f|_{n}B_{1})|_{n}B_{2}
for all
B_{1},B_{2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proposition 4.2
.

Let
N\geq 2
and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.
Assume that
m|_{M}B
is a nonzero constant multiple of
m
for
M=\deg m
.
Then the map

f\mapsto f|_{N}B

(41)

defines a bijection between
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(N;m,c)
and
\widetilde{\mathcal{I}}(N;m,c|_{N}B)
.
In particular, we have a tie

\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c)=\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c|_{N}B).

Proof.

To show that the map preserves the degree, note that the leading coefficient of
f|_{N}B
is

\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\alpha^{i}\gamma^{N-i}=\begin{cases}\gamma^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)^{i}=\gamma^{N}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)&\gamma\neq 0\\
a_{N}\alpha^{N}&\gamma=0.\end{cases}

(42)

When
\gamma\neq 0
,
f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)\neq 0
since
f
is irreducible of degree
N\geq 2
and cannot have a zero in
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.
When
\gamma
is 0,
\alpha\neq 0
(otherwise
B
is not invertible) and
a_{N}\alpha^{N}\neq 0
.
The map is bijection since the inverse map can be described as

f\mapsto f|_{N}B^{-1}.

Regarding irreducibility, if
f=gh
is reducible then
f|_{n}B=g|_{n_{1}}B\cdot h|_{n_{2}}B
for
n_{1}=\deg g
and
n_{2}=\deg h
, so
f|_{n}B
is also reducible and vice versa.
Finally, from
f\in\mathcal{I}(N;m,c)
, one can write
f=gm+c
, and applying
|_{N}B
on both sides gives
f|_{N}B=(g|_{\deg g}B)\cdot(m|_{M}B)+c|_{N}B=(g|_{\deg g}B)\cdot\alpha^{\prime}m+c|_{N}B
for some nonzero constant
\alpha\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, thus
f|_{N}B\equiv c|_{N}B\pmod{m}
.
∎

Although the congruence class
c|_{N}B
of the target
\mathcal{I}(N;m,c|_{N}B)
seems depending on the degree
N
, it only depends on
N
modulo certain number (for fixed
B
).

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a monic polynomial and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be a matrix such that
m|_{M}B=\lambda\cdot m
for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\times}
and
M=\deg m
.
For a polynomial
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
coprime to
m
, the sequence
N\mapsto c|_{N}B\pmod{m}
is periodic in
N
with the period equal to the order of
\gamma T+\delta
in
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
.

Proof.

We have
(c|_{n+1}B)(T)=(\gamma T+\delta)(c|_{n}B)(T)
.
Also, note that
c|_{n}B
is coprime to
m
for all
n
, which follows from Proposition
4.2
.
Then we have
c|_{n+k}B=c|_{n}B
for all
n
, where
k
is an order of
\gamma T+\delta
inside the group
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
.
∎

Corollary 4.4
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a monic polynomial and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
be a matrix satisfying the same assumptions as in Lemma
4.3
, and let
N_{0}
be the order of
\gamma T+\delta
in
(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
.
Fix
e\geq M-1
and let
c\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
be a polynomial coprime to
m
and
\deg c<M
.
For each
N\equiv e\pmod{N_{0}}
, we have a tie

\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c)=\widetilde{\pi}(N;m,c|_{e}B)

induced by the bijection (
41
).

However, our main interest is the ties for irreducible
monic
polynomials, and the map (
41
) does not preserve monicity in general.
We are interested in the case when we can choose
B
so that

\mathrm{lc}(f|_{N}B)=1\,\,\text{for all}\,f\in\mathcal{I}(N;m,c)

(43)

There are some cases where (
43
) holds.

Proposition 4.5
.

For
N\geq 2
and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\alpha&\beta\\
\gamma&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
, (
43
) holds if

(1)

q=2

(2)

\alpha^{N}=1
and
\gamma=0
(in particular, when
(\alpha,\gamma)=(1,0)
)

Proof.

By (
40
), the
N
-th coefficient of
f|_{N}B
is equal to

\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\alpha^{i}\gamma^{N-i}=\begin{cases}\gamma^{N}\sum_{i=0}^{N}a_{i}\left(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)^{i}=\gamma^{N}f\left(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\right)&\text{if }\gamma\neq 0\\
a_{N}\alpha^{N}&\text{if }\gamma=0\end{cases}

(44)

When
q=2
, note that the only nonzero element in
\mathbb{F}_{2}
is 1.
If
\gamma\neq 0
,
f(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma})\neq 0
since
f
is irreducible of degree
\geq 2
, hence
\gamma^{N}f(\frac{\alpha}{\gamma})=1
.
When
\gamma=0
,
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
gives
\alpha=1
and
N
-th coefficient of
f|_{N}B
is again nonzero.

For the second case, monicity is again preserved by (
44
).
∎

4.2   Examples

4.2.1
m=T^{2}+T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]

m(T)=T^{2}+T+1
is fixed under action of any matrix in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
.
For example, it is fixed under the action of
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{2})
, i.e.
T^{2}m\left(\frac{1}{T}\right)=m(T)
, which reflects the fact that
m(T)
is palindromic.

\gamma T+\delta=T
has order 3 in
(\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]/m)^{\times}\simeq\mathbb{Z}/3
, which means that there exist ties in the prime race modulo
m(T)
for each degree modulo 3.
In particular, the action sends

\displaystyle N\equiv 0\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:1\mapsto 1,\quad T\mapsto T+1,\quad T+1\mapsto T

\displaystyle N\equiv 1\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:1\mapsto T,\quad T\mapsto 1,\quad T+1\mapsto T+1

\displaystyle N\equiv 2\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:1\mapsto T+1,\quad T\mapsto T,\quad T+1\mapsto 1

and this gives ties

\displaystyle N\equiv 0\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:\pi(N;m,T)=\pi(N;m,T+1)

\displaystyle N\equiv 1\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:\pi(N;m,1)=\pi(N;m,T)

\displaystyle N\equiv 2\pmod{3}

\displaystyle:\pi(N;m,1)=\pi(N;m,T+1).

If we consider the translation matrix
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, it gives
\pi(N;m,T)=\pi(N;m,T+1)
for
all

N
.
Combining these, we get the same ties as in Section
3.2.1
(Table
2
).

More generally, any palindromic polynomial
m(T)\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
is fixed under the action of
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and it induces a tie
\pi(N;m,c)=\pi(N;m,c|_{e}B)
for any
c\in(\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]/m)^{\times}
for all
N\equiv e\pmod{N_{0}}
, where
N_{0}
is the order of
T
in
(\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]/m)^{\times}
.

.
Then

4.4
, we have ties
\pi(N;m,c)=\pi(c|_{e},m,N)
for each
e\in\{0,1,\dots,6\}
and
N\equiv e\pmod{7}
.
For example, when
e=1
, the
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-action sends

1\mapsto T,\quad T\mapsto T+1,\quad T+1\mapsto 1,

and

T^{2}\mapsto T^{2}+T,\quad T^{2}+T\mapsto T^{2}+T+1,\quad T^{2}+T+1\mapsto T^{2},

which give ties

\displaystyle\pi(N;m,1)

\displaystyle=\pi(N;m,T)=\pi(N;m,T+1),

\displaystyle\pi(N;m,T^{2})

\displaystyle=\pi(N;m,T^{2}+T)=\pi(N;m,T^{2}+T+1).

Similarly, we get the ties for other
N\pmod{7}
, which are the same as in Section
3.2.2
(Table
1
).

4.2.3
m=T^{2}+1\in\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]

Observe that
m
is irreducible in
\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]
. Let
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Then,
(m|_{2}B)(T)=m(T)
and
\gamma T+\delta=2
has order
2
in
\left(\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]/(T^{2}+1)\right)^{\times}
. We thus consider degrees modulo
2
; the action sends each congruence class as

\displaystyle N\equiv 0\pmod{2}:\quad

\displaystyle 1\mapsto 1,\quad 2\mapsto 2,\quad T\mapsto 2T,\quad T+1\mapsto 2T+1,\quad T+2\mapsto 2T+2,

\displaystyle 2T\mapsto T,\quad 2T+1\mapsto T+1,\quad 2T+2\mapsto T+2;

\displaystyle N\equiv 1\pmod{2}:\quad

\displaystyle 1\mapsto 2,\quad 2\mapsto 1,\quad T\mapsto T,\quad T+1\mapsto T+2,\quad T+2\mapsto T+1,

\displaystyle 2T\mapsto 2T,\quad 2T+1\mapsto 2T+2,\quad 2T+2\mapsto 2T+1;

and they give the same ties in Section
3.2.3
(Table
3
).

4.2.4
m=T^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]

Let
m(T)=T^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]
.
When
p=2
, one can take
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
1&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
so that for odd
N
and
c(T)=1
, we have

(c|_{N}B)(T)=(T+1)^{N}\equiv NT+1\equiv T+1\pmod{T^{2}},

and we get the tie
\pi(N;m,1)=\pi(N;m,T+1)
(Table
4
).

For odd
p
, we cannot use the same
B
since the map
f\mapsto f|_{N}B
does not preserve monicity in general (even if
m|_{2}B=m
).
Instead, we choose
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&\delta\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, and for
c(T)=c_{1}T+c_{0}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]/m)^{\times}
,

(c|_{N}B)(T)=\delta^{N}\left(c_{1}\frac{T}{\delta}+c_{0}\right)=\delta^{N-1}c_{1}T+\delta^{N}c_{0}.

(45)

If the order of
\delta\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
is
N_{0}
, then for each
e\geq 1
coprime to
p-1
, the action by
B
gives ties
\pi(N;m,c)=\pi(N;m,c|_{e}B)
for
N\equiv e\pmod{N_{0}}
.
When
\delta
is a generator of the multiplicative group
\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
(so has order
N_{0}=p-1
),
\delta^{e}
also has order
p-1
and
(c,c|_{e}B,c|_{e}B^{2},\dots,c|_{e}B^{p-2})
forms a cycle of length
p-1
that induces ties

\pi(N;m,c_{1}T+c_{0})=\pi(N;m,\delta^{e-1}c_{1}T+\delta^{e}c_{0})=\cdots=\pi(N;m,\delta^{(p-2)(e-1))}c_{1}T+\delta^{(p-2)e}c_{0})

In particular, when
e=1
, it shows that
\pi(N;m,c_{1}T+c_{0})
is independent of
c_{0}\neq 0
for
N\equiv 1\pmod{p-1}
.

For example, when
p=3
and
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&2\end{smallmatrix}\right)
,
\delta=2\in\mathbb{F}_{3}^{\times}
has order
2
and it gives ties

\displaystyle\pi(N;m,1)=\pi(N;m,2),\quad\pi(N;m,T+1)=\pi(N;m,T+2),\quad\pi(N;m,2T+1)=\pi(N;m,2T+2)

for odd
N
.
For even
N
, the same
B
gives ties between
c_{1}T+c_{0}
and
2c_{1}T+c_{0}
, i.e.

\displaystyle\pi(N;m,T+1)=\pi(N;m,2T+1),\quad\pi(N;m,T+2)=\pi(N;m,2T+2).

4.2.5
m=T^{p}-T-1\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]

Artin–Schreier polynomials
m(T)=T^{p}-T-a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]
are irreducible for any
a\in\mathbb{F}_{p}^{\times}
.
The polynomial is fixed under the action of
B=\left(\begin{smallmatrix}1&1\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right)
, i.e. translation
T\mapsto T+1
.
Hence it induces ties
\pi(N;m,c_{1})=\pi(N;m,c_{2})
for
c_{1},c_{2}\in(\mathbb{F}_{p}[T]/m)^{\times}
when

c_{2}(T)=c_{1}(T+b)\quad\text{for some}\,\,b\in\mathbb{F}_{p}.

For example, when
p=3
,
m(T)=T^{3}-T-1=T^{3}+2T+2
and
N=24
, we have

\pi(24;m,T^{2})=\pi(24;m,T^{2}+2T+1)=\pi(24;m,T^{2}+T+1)=452605575.

5   Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed two approaches that shows ties in prime counts over function fields, by explicit formula and by constructing bijections with
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-actions.
A natural question arises is how two approaches are related, and whether one method is always stronger than the other.
For example, we showed the ties
\pi(N;m,T+1)=\pi(N;m,T+2)=\pi(N;m,2T+1)=\pi(N;m,2T+2)
hold
m(T)=T^{2}\in\mathbb{F}_{3}[T]
and
N\equiv 0\pmod{4}
in Section
4.2.4
, but the bijection method can only show half of the ties and we could not find appropriate matrix that shows the same ties. However, we also found that it often easier to prove ties by finding suitable
B\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
giving the desired bijection, rather than finding exceptional Galois conjugates.

One may also ask if we have interesting ties for the cumulative version of prime counts that is considered in
[
2
]
and subsequent literature.
Although there are some examples with small
N
, we conjecture that such ties are very rare and almost never happen.

Conjecture 5.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]
and let
a,b\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}[T]/m)^{\times}
be two distinct congruence classes modulo
m
.
Then there are finitely many
N
such that

\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi(n;m,a)=\sum_{n=1}^{N}\pi(n;m,b).

For example, Table
6
shows the cumulative prime counts for
m=T^{3}+T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
, where the ties do not appear for
22\leq N\leq 40
.

In Remark
3.4
, we also proved that prime are always bias against
c=1
modulo
T^{2}+T+1\in\mathbb{F}_{2}[T]
, i.e. the inequality
\pi(N;m,1)<\pi(N;m,T)
holds when
N\geq 9
is a multiple of 3.
It might be interesting if we can prove it by constructing an
injection
from
\mathcal{I}_{2}(N;m,1)
to
\mathcal{I}_{2}(N;m,T)
.
