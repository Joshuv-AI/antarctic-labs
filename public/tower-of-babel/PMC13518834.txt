Ultra-wideband printed monopole antenna with cardioid-shaped fractal geometry
Authors: Luka Lazović, Branka Jokanovic, Vesna Rubežić, Ana Jovanović
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis paper presents a compact ultra-wideband (UWB) printed monopole antenna based on fractal cardioid geometry, designed to operate over the 4–30 GHz frequency range. The proposed antenna is intended for multi-service wireless applications, including IoT, WLAN, WiMAX, and satellite communications, while also being suitable for RF energy harvesting systems. Driven by the increasing density of wireless devices and ambient electromagnetic radiation, modern antenna systems are required to efficiently cover multiple frequency bands with a single compact structure. To address these requirements, the proposed antenna employs a fractal cardioid-based radiating element enabling significant bandwidth enhancement without increasing antenna size. The antenna is fabricated on a low-cost FR-4 substrate and experimentally validated. The measured results show good agreement with the simulated results, confirming the robustness of the design. The antenna achieves below − 10dB across a wide portion of the SHF band, with stable radiation characteristics, gain up to 5 dBi, and efficiency up to 80%. In addition, the antenna exhibits favorable impedance characteristics suitable for direct integration in rectenna systems without requiring a matching network. Owing to its compact dimensions, electrically small profile, low cost, ultra-wideband performance, and experimentally verified reliability, the proposed antenna is suitable for integration into next-generation IoT devices, multi-band communication systems, and RF energy harvesting applications.Subject terms: Energy science and technology, Engineering, Physics

Introduction

The rapid expansion of wireless communication technologies, particularly IoT, 5G, and emerging 6G systems, has led to a significant increase in the number of connected devices and the density of electromagnetic radiation in the environment. It is projected that tens of billions of IoT devices will be deployed in the near future. This trend will further increase the availability of ambient electromagnetic energy and strengthen the demand for efficient broadband antenna solutions1–4.

Modern wireless systems operate across a wide range of frequency bands, including cellular (3G, 4G, 5G), WLAN, ISM, and satellite communication bands5. Consequently, there is a growing demand for antennas capable of covering multiple frequency bands within a single compact structure. Ultra-wideband (UWB) antennas represent an effective solution, as they enable simultaneous operation across multiple services and reduce system complexity6,7.

In parallel, RF energy harvesting has emerged as a promising approach for powering low-power electronic devices and IoT sensor networks8,9. Although the power density of ambient electromagnetic energy is generally low, it is widely available across multiple frequency bands. Consequently, broadband antennas are particularly suitable for energy harvesting applications4. The overall efficiency of such systems strongly depends on antenna characteristics, especially bandwidth, radiation efficiency, and impedance matching performance.

Printed planar antennas are widely used because of their low cost, compact dimensions, ease of fabrication, and compatibility with integrated circuits. However, conventional microstrip and patch antennas are inherently narrowband. This limitation restricts their applicability in broadband and multi-service wireless systems. To address this issue, several techniques have been proposed, including ground-plane modification, slot loading, and the application of fractal geometries10–12.

Fractal antenna designs have attracted considerable attention due to their self-similarity and space-filling properties. These characteristics enable both antenna miniaturization and bandwidth enhancement5,13,14. Various fractal geometries, including Sierpinski, Koch, and other self-similar structures, have demonstrated improved impedance bandwidth and multi-resonant behavior13,15–17. Among these approaches, cardioid-based fractal geometries offer additional design flexibility. Their characteristics can be adjusted through the iteration order and scaling factors of the fractal structure5,18,19.

Recent studies have shown that UWB antennas can be implemented on low-cost FR-4 substrates while maintaining acceptable performance at higher frequencies5,12,20. In addition, antennas designed for RF energy harvesting can also be employed in communication systems, enabling multifunctional operation within a single device. Nevertheless, many reported designs rely on complex geometries, expensive substrate, or insufficient experimental validation.

A band-notched ultra-wideband monopole antenna employing a parasitic strip was proposed in21. The antenna achieves an operating bandwidth of approximately 3–17 GHz while effectively suppressing interference in the 5.15–5.825 GHz WLAN band. The design offers compact dimensions and favorable radiation characteristics. Furthermore, the notched-band response can be adjusted through modifications of the parasitic element geometry.

A dual-band monopole antenna based on metamaterial structures was presented in22. The antenna combines UWB operation (3.1–10.6 GHz) with a controllable narrowband resonance generated by CRLH transmission-line elements and reconfigurable switching. The design provides acceptable gain and efficiency while enabling frequency reconfigurability through integrated switching elements.

A compact UWB circular fractal monopole antenna was proposed in23. The design employs multiple nested ring structures and a modified ground plane to achieve an impedance bandwidth of approximately 2.83–10.16 GHz with high efficiency. Good radiation characteristics and relatively high gain are obtained despite the use of an FR-4 substrate. However, the multi-stage fractal geometry increases structural complexity and offers limited scalability toward higher-frequency applications.

A novel ultra-wideband double-pentagonal fractal antenna was presented in24. The design utilizes a self-similar geometry with an embedded inner pentagonal ring to generate multiple resonances and achieve an impedance bandwidth of approximately 3.84–22.4 GHz. High gain values of up to 10.2 dBi are reported, together with broad frequency coverage across the C-, X-, Ku-, and K-bands. However, the antenna exhibits increased structural complexity and larger physical dimensions. In addition, radiation-pattern distortion becomes more pronounced at higher frequencies.

In this paper, a compact ultra-wideband monopole antenna based on a novel fractal cardioid geometry is proposed. The antenna is designed for multi-service wireless applications in the SHF band, including IoT, WLAN, WiMAX, and satellite communication systems. For practical IoT implementation, the antenna must satisfy several important requirements, including low fabrication cost, compact dimensions, compatibility with low-cost FR4 substrates, and straightforward integration with the remaining electronic circuitry. In addition, a nearly omnidirectional radiation pattern is desirable in order to ensure reliable wireless connectivity regardless of the sensor orientation. The proposed design is developed by carefully balancing the ultra-wideband impedance response with other key antenna parameters, such as gain, radiation pattern, compactness, and fabrication simplicity.

Besides general wireless communication applications, the antenna is particularly intended for RF energy harvesting and short-range Doppler radar biosensing systems. In RF energy harvesting applications, a wide operating bandwidth is highly desirable because it enables the collection of ambient electromagnetic energy from multiple wireless services. In short-range Doppler radar biosensing systems, the antenna should be compact and easily integrable, while also providing high isolation between two closely spaced antennas used as transmitting and receiving elements.

A comprehensive parametric analysis is presented to illustrate the complete design procedure and the influence of the key geometrical parameters on the antenna performance. The obtained results provide valuable insight into the design methodology and may serve as a practical guideline for the development and optimization of compact ultra-wideband antennas adjusted to specific application requirements.

The fabricated prototype exhibits good agreement between simulated and measured results. This agreement confirms the robustness of the proposed design methodology and validates its practical implementation.

This paper is organized as follows. “Fractal geometry” section introduces the fundamental concepts of fractal geometry, including the Iterated Function System (IFS) approach and key parameters relevant to antenna design. “Cardioid” section provides a detailed description of the cardioid curve, which forms the basis of the proposed fractal geometry. In “Proposed antenna and parametric sweep” section, the design of the proposed antenna is presented, along with a comprehensive parametric analysis of key geometrical parameters and their influence on antenna performance. “Characteristics of the proposed antenna” section discusses the main characteristics of the antenna, including gain, efficiency, radiation patterns, current distribution, and impedance behavior. Experimental validation and comparison with simulated results are provided in “Experimental results and discussion” section, followed by a discussion of the results and comparison with existing designs. Finally, “Conclusion” section concludes the paper and outlines potential applications of the proposed antenna.

Fractal geometry

It is useful to begin by defining the concept of a fractal and the methods used for its generation and description. These definitions facilitate the reproducibility of the results presented in this work.

Most fractals can be constructed through an iterative process known as Iterated Function Systems (IFS). Fractals are defined as the union of self-similar copies, where each successive copy is typically scaled down relative to the previous one. The IFS approach is based on a set of affine transformations W, which are repeatedly applied to an initial geometric shape.

Reflection with respect to the x- or y-axis can be achieved by assigning negative values to either of the coefficients a or d. Fractal geometry, particularly in the context of antenna design, can instead be described using two parameters: the iteration factor (IF) and the iteration order (IO).

The iteration order represents the number of fractal iterations, while the iteration factor denotes the ratio between the dimensions of the second and the first fractal iteration. In general, most fractals and fractal antennas exhibit a constant across all iterations. In the design of multiresonant antennas, this typically results in harmonic resonant frequencies, i.e., equally spaced resonances. However, in many practical applications, antennas with non-harmonic resonant frequencies are required. Geometries in which the varies between successive iterations provide additional flexibility in antenna design. Such structures exhibit multifractal scaling and are frequently employed in the design of super-wideband (SWB) fractal antennas.

A geometry that may be of interest for antenna design is the nested geometry. A nested geometry can be defined as a set of geometries possessing the property that each geometry is contained within the previous one, and that the diameter or characteristic dimension of the geometries approaches zero as the number of geometries tends to infinity.

This raises the question of whether the geometry proposed in this paper can be classified as a fractal geometry. The answer, with respect to the geometry of the proposed antenna structure, is that they should be referred to as nested fractal geometry26–29.

Antenna impedance

The antenna impedance in this design plays a significant role in impedance matching for energy harvesting (EH) systems. As previously emphasized, in addition to other applications, the antenna is particularly suitable for EH systems i.e., rectennas, where no dedicated matching network is employed. Instead, a diode (serving as an RF-to-DC converter) is directly connected to the antenna. The optimal antenna impedance should be equal to the complex conjugate of the diode impedance. Experimental results and discussion

The antenna was fabricated using a simple photolithographic process. As a consequence of the low-cost fabrication technique, slight deviations from the intended dimensions may be present. Figure 12 shows the fabricated antenna with an SMA connector.

Realized fractal antenna with overall dimensions 25mm × 18.5 mm.

The antenna measurements were performed using an ANRITSU MS4647A vector network analyzer with a frequency range extending from 70 kHz to 110 GHz. The measurement setup consisted of a standard horn antenna used as the reference antenna, while the antenna under test (AUT) was mounted on a computer-controlled turntable and rotated to obtain the radiation characteristics in semi anechoic conditions.

The transmitting and receiving antennas were separated by a distance of 2 m. Considering the electrically small dimensions of the proposed antenna, this distance satisfies the far-field condition throughout the frequency range of interest. To cover the entire operating bandwidth, three broadband dual-ridged horn antennas were employed as reference antennas: ANT-BHA-2-8 (2–8 GHz), ANT-BHA-8-18 (8–18 GHz), and ANT-BHA-18-40 (18–40 GHz).

Prior to the measurements, a full two-port calibration was performed, with the calibration reference plane established at the SMA connector of the antenna under test. This procedure minimizes the influence of cables, connectors, and measurement fixtures on the measured results.

Radiation patterns

Figure 14 presents the simulated and measured radiation patterns in the E- and H-planes for frequencies ranging from 4 to 30 GHz.

Comparison of simulated and measured radiation patterns in the E- and H-planes.

As illustrated in Fig. 14, the measured radiation patterns are in good agreement with the simulated results, confirming the nearly omnidirectional radiation characteristics of the proposed antenna. At higher frequencies, some deviations from the simulated patterns can be observed. Nevertheless, the antenna retains its overall omnidirectional behavior, and these deviations do not compromise its intended practical applications. The observed distortions may be attributed to the omnidirectional nature of the antenna, which increases its sensitivity to parasitic reflections from surrounding objects and the measurement environment. Although the measurements were performed under semi-anechoic conditions, a certain level of scattering and multipath propagation may still contribute to the discrepancies between the simulated and measured results.

Such distortions are commonly observed in electrically small ultra-wideband antennas, particularly at the upper end of the operating frequency range. Furthermore, improving the radiation pattern stability over an extremely wide bandwidth often requires design trade-offs that may adversely affect other key antenna parameters, such as impedance bandwidth and compactness.

Time domain analysis

Time-domain analysis is an important tool for evaluating the performance of ultra-wideband antennas, as it provides insight into their ability to transmit and receive broadband signals with minimal waveform distortion. Therefore, the time-domain characteristics of the proposed antenna were investigated in order to assess its signal fidelity and transient response performance.

Two configurations were considered. In the first configuration, two identical antennas were positioned face-to-face at a separation distance of 20 cm, while in the second configuration the antennas were arranged side-by-side with the same separation distance. A Gaussian pulse was used as the excitation signal, and the normalized transmitted and received waveforms are presented in Fig. 15.

Normalized amplitudes of the transmitted and received pulses for the face-to-face and side-by-side antenna configurations.

The fidelity factor was calculated from the cross-correlation between the transmitted and received pulses, providing a quantitative measure of the similarity between the two signals. The calculation was performed according to the procedure described in36,37. The obtained fidelity factors are 76% for the side-by-side configuration and 77% for the face-to-face configuration.

The group delay characterizes the signal transit time through the antenna system and provides valuable insight into its time-domain performance and pulse-preserving capability36,37. The group delay responses for the two antenna configurations are presented in Fig. 16. It can be observed that the group delay remains below 1 ns across the entire operating band for both configurations, indicating very good time-domain performance and low signal distortion. Slightly better results are achieved for the side-by-side configuration, which exhibits a more stable group delay response over the frequency range of interest .

The isolation between two antenna ports quantifies the level of electromagnetic coupling between them and is characterized by the transmission coefficient (). High isolation corresponds to low mutual coupling, thereby enabling independent signal transmission and reducing unwanted interference between the antenna ports.

The simulated transmission coefficient () for both antenna configurations is presented in Fig. 16. As can be observed, the isolation remains better than 35 dB across the entire operating bandwidth for both configurations, indicating excellent decoupling performance. Such a high level of isolation is particularly beneficial in applications requiring closely spaced antennas, such as short-range Doppler radar systems, where minimizing leakage between the transmitting and receiving channels is essential for accurate signal detection and processing.

Antenna applications

The proposed antenna, operating over an ultra-wideband frequency range from 4 to 30 GHz, is suitable for a wide variety of wireless communication applications. Owing to its simple geometry, compact size, and low fabrication cost, the antenna is particularly attractive for Internet of Things (IoT) systems, where cost-effective and miniaturized solutions are highly desirable. An additional advantage of the proposed antenna is its capability to support multiple communication protocols within a single IoT sensor platform. This feature eliminates the need for multiple dedicated antennas, thereby reducing system complexity, physical footprint, and implementation cost while improving overall integration flexibility.

While the proposed antenna can be employed in a broad range of wireless systems, its characteristics make it especially suitable for two application areas. The first is RF energy harvesting and the second is short-range Doppler radar for biosensing applications.

The first application area is RF energy harvesting, where the ultra-wide operating bandwidth enables efficient collection of ambient electromagnetic energy from a large number of wireless sources. In general, two types of rectennas can be distinguished. The first category comprises narrowband rectennas that employ an impedance matching network between the antenna and the rectifying circuit. Although impedance matching can improve power transfer at a specific frequency, the design of broadband matching networks is challenging and often introduces additional losses, thereby reducing the overall RF-to-DC conversion efficiency. The second category consists of broadband rectennas in which a Schottky diode (e.g., SMS7630) is directly connected to the antenna without an intermediate matching network, serving as the RF-to-DC conversion element38,39. By eliminating the matching circuitry, higher conversion efficiencies can be achieved due to reduced power dissipation within the rectenna system.

Owing to its favorable input impedance characteristics over a wide frequency range, the proposed antenna is particularly suitable for integration into broadband rectenna systems. An example of an antenna array configuration for RF energy harvesting is shown on Fig. 17. In this architecture, the received RF power is rectified locally at each antenna element, while the generated DC power from all elements is combined and extracted through dedicated DC collection lines located at the edges of the array.

Layout of the proposed rectenna array for two polarisation. The incident RF power is locally rectified by each antenna.

To better illustrate the practical potential of the proposed antenna, a layout used for the two polarization energy harvesting system is shown on Fig. 17. Initially, two antenna elements are connected in series, and two such branches are subsequently connected in parallel to form a 2× 2 subarray. The narrow interconnecting traces do not significantly affect the RF performance of the antenna elements and are used solely for DC power routing. The resulting 2× 2 subarrays are then recursively interconnected through a combination of series and parallel connections, leading to the realization of the complete 16× 16 energy-harvesting array.

The second application area is short-range Doppler radar for biosensing applications, including non-contact monitoring of physiological parameters such as heart rate, respiration, sleep activity and room occupancy. In such systems, high isolation between the transmitting (Tx) and receiving (Rx) antennas is essential, as it reduces signal leakage between channels, simplifies subsequent signal processing, and improves the overall signal-to-noise ratio. At the same time, small antenna dimensions and ease of integration are critical requirements for practical implementations40. An additional advantage of the proposed antenna is its ultra-wideband operation, which enables its use across multiple radar frequency bands. Multi-frequency sensing has attracted considerable attention in recent years, as combining information obtained at different frequencies can improve the accuracy and reliability of physiological monitoring.

The proposed antenna exhibits excellent isolation characteristics when employed in a side-by-side radar configuration, which is not the case with commercially available antennas. Specifically, an isolation level better than 20 dB is achieved across the entire operating band for an edge-to-edge antenna separation of only 26.3 mm. Even when the antennas are placed directly adjacent to each other (d=8.5 mm), the mutual coupling remains below -15 dB over all operating bandwidth.

Conclusion

A compact ultra-wideband printed monopole antenna based on a novel cardioid-shaped fractal geometry is presented in this paper. The proposed antenna, fabricated on a low-cost FR-4 substrate, achieves a measured impedance bandwidth extending from 4 to 30 GHz, corresponding to a fractional bandwidth of approximately 153%, while maintaining compact overall dimensions of only 18.5 mm × 25 mm. A comprehensive parametric analysis demonstrated that the ultra-wideband response is strongly influenced by the cardioid scaling parameters, feed displacement, and ground-plane dimensions. The obtained results confirmed that the proposed first-order cardioid fractal geometry provides the most favorable compromise between impedance bandwidth, gain, radiation characteristics, and implementation simplicity. Experimental measurements showed good agreement with the simulated results, validating both the design methodology and the practical realization of the antenna.

In addition to its wide operating bandwidth, the antenna exhibits a peak gain of 5.6 dBi, radiation efficiency reaching 80%, nearly omnidirectional radiation patterns, favorable time-domain characteristics, and stable group-delay performance. These properties make the proposed design suitable for a broad range of modern wireless systems, including IoT devices, WLAN, WiMAX, satellite communications, and future multi-service SHF applications.

Particular importance is given to two application areas. First, the antenna exhibits favorable inductive impedance characteristics over a large portion of the operating band, making it highly suitable for broadband RF energy-harvesting and rectenna systems without the need for complex impedance-matching networks. Second, the antenna demonstrates excellent isolation performance when used as closely spaced transmitting and receiving antenna pairs, which is highly beneficial for short-range Doppler radar biosensing systems intended for contactless monitoring of respiration, heart activity, sleep behavior, and occupancy detection.

The combination of ultra-wideband operation, compact dimensions, low-cost fabrication, favorable impedance characteristics, and high isolation performance distinguishes the proposed cardioid fractal antenna from many previously reported UWB designs. These features make it a promising candidate for next-generation multifunctional wireless platforms integrating communication, sensing, and RF energy-harvesting capabilities within a single compact antenna architecture.
