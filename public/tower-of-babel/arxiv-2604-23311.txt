Core abaci and Diophantine equations I: Fundamental weight Thanks: Corresponding Author: Shasha Zhu
Yanbo Li, Jiansheng Zhang, Shasha Zhu

Abstract
. In the light of a series of papers on moving vectors, we define and study core abaci of classical affine types for arbitrary charge. This greatly extends the concept of cores with charge zero, and make us being able to parameterize the affine Grassmannian W j W^{j} by core abaci of charge j j for arbitrary classical affine types. By associating a core abacus ( λ , j ) (\lambda,j) to a weight Λ j − β \Lambda_{j}-\beta and an affine Weyl group element w λ , j w_{\lambda,j} , we prove that the height of β \beta is equal to the atomic length of w λ , j w_{\lambda,j} . This solves a generalized version of the open problem raised by Brunat, Chapelier-Laget and Gerber. Moreover, Diophantine equations of classical affine types are established by using the height formula that given by Uglov vector. The solutions of certain classes of these Diophantine equations are proved to be completely parameterised by core abaci. As another application, closed formulae for computing the number of certain kinds of core abaci are given.

1.
Introduction

Core partitions play an important role in different fields of mathematics
such as representation theory, enumerative combinatorics, number theory and so on.
We refer the reader to
[
1
,
4
,
9
,
11
]
for some details. In
[
14
]
, Hanusa and Jones generalized
the notion to types
B
,
C
and
D
, which are all described by cores of type
A
.

For a core partition
\lambda
, the size (number of nodes in the Young diagram
[\lambda]
) is undoubtedly essential.
In
[
29
]
Thiel and Williams established a formula for the size of
type
A
cores by using coroot lattice, and the formula was generalized to other classical types in
[
28
]
.
In 2024 Chapelier-Laget and Gerber introduced the so-called atomic length that is defined on the (affine) Weyl
group. This recover the constructions in
[
29
,
28
]
. In particular, by regarding a core
\lambda
as an affine Grassmannian element
at the fundamental weight
\Lambda_{0}
, it was shown that the size of
\lambda

is equal to the atomic length of a representative with minimal length in the affine Grassmannian.
The quadratic formula for the atomic length was then used to establish certain Diophantine equations
by Brunat, Chapelier-Laget and Gerber in
[
4
]
. As a result, the solutions of Diophantine equations
established could be partially parameterized by the associated cores.
Moreover, using the atomic length associated with the fundamental weight
\Lambda_{1}

in type
C_{2}^{(1)}
, they obtain a description of the solutions of the Diophantine equation

x^{2}+y^{2}=8N+1
in terms of extended Grassmannian elements. Inspired by this result, a natural problem that has been proposed
by Brunat, Chapelier-Laget and Gerber is to find a combinatorial model for arbitrary twisted or untwisted affine types in which the size of cores
is given by the
\Lambda_{1}
atomic length. This is precisely the starting point of our research.
The key of our study is to extend the notion of cores to arbitrary fundamental weight
\Lambda_{i}
.

By fixing an integer
j
, called charge, a partition
\lambda
can be expressed by an abacus, which first
appeared in the work of James
[
20
]
. Given a pair
(\lambda,j)
, one can associate it with a weight
\Lambda-\beta
.
It is well known that if
\lambda
is a core, then the defect of
\Lambda-\beta
is zero. In our opinion, the property of
being defect zero is the essence of cores (of type
A^{(1)}
). We extend the definition of cores to arbitrary classical types of arbitrary charges
based on this view. Note that each core abacus
(\lambda,j)
can be obtained from the empty abacus
by the action of a minima length element
w_{\lambda,j}
in the corresponding affine Weyl group, and consequently
we can parameterize the affine Grassmannian by the collection of
j
-core abaci for arbitrary classical affine types, where
j
is a charge.
The is the first gain of our generalization of cores.

Thanks to a series of papers
[
23
,
24
,
16
]
on the so-called moving vectors, which were introduced
by Li and Qi
[
23
]
to study the representation type of blocks of an Ariki-Koike algebra and were extended to
all twisted and untwisted classical affine types by the first author and his collaborator,
we can describe cores by abacus language. This makes it possible to give a formula on the height of a core by the so-called
Uglov vector obtained from Uglov map. To obtain this formula, we prove that the
\mathrm{q}
-vector obtained from
the semidirect product decomposition of
w_{\lambda,j}
is equal to the sum of an adjustment item and the Uglov-vector obtained from general Uglov map.
Based on the height formula, we find that for a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
the atomic length of
w_{\lambda,j}
is
equal to the height of
\beta
, which is just the size of
\lambda
in some cases.
The result can be viewed as an answer of the open problem raised by Brunat, Chapelier-Laget and Gerber in
[
4
]
.
This is the second gain of our generalization of cores.

Thirdly, we establish Diophantine equations by using the height formula and develop the relevant theory of classical affine types.
In particular, we study the problem that under what conditions all solution orbits are parameterized by core abaci.
Along this way, we obtain closed formulas of computing the number of certain
j
-core abaci in some special cases,
including affine types
C_{2}^{(1)}
,
D_{2}^{(2)}
,
B_{3}^{(1)}
,
D_{3}^{(2)}
,
B_{4}^{(1)}
and
D_{4}^{(1)}
.

The paper is organized as follows. We begin in Section 2 by reviewing some preliminaries on combinatorics and affine Weyl groups.
Then in Section 3, we define and study arbitrary charged core abaci of classical twisted and untwisted affine types.
It is helpful to point out that so far all the definitions of cores are of charge 0 as far as we know.
In particular, we establish a bijection between
j
-core abaci and the alcoves in a generalized Tits cone, which corresponds to an affine Grassmannian.
Moreover we study the compatibility between Uglov-vectors and
\mathrm{q}
-vectors.
In Section 4, we first establish the height formula and then
prove that the height of a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
is equal to the atomic length of
w_{\lambda,j}
.
Furthermore, we will construct Diophantine equations induced by the height formula.
In Section 5, we consider the relationship between core abaci and the solutions of the Diophantine equations
obtained in Section 4. Particularly, we prove that in some small rank cases, the solutions can be completely parameterized by core abaci.
This makes us being able to give closed formulae on enumerating the number of certain
j
-core abaci in some special cases.

To this aim, we need to define another index system for positions of an abacus, called

For a given affine type

x-2l-1,&\mbox{if }l+1\leq x\leq|I_{X}|-1\,{\rm and}\,l+1\in I_{X}.\end{cases}

Let

.
It is clear that there exists

such that

(1)
Type

. We divide the proof into two cases according to the value of

\displaystyle\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\lambda}+\overline{w^{\lambda}}(\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}(\mathrm{q}^{\sigma_{0}}+\overline{\sigma}_{0}(\mathrm{q}^{\mu}))+\overline{\sigma}_{0}\overline{w^{\mu}}(\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\sigma_{0}}+\overline{\sigma}_{0}(\mathrm{u}^{\mu})\,\,\,\,\text{(by induction hypothesis)}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\sqrt{2}a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}},0,...,0)+(-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}u_{1}^{\mu},...,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}u_{l}^{\mu})\,\,\,\,\text{(by Lemma \ref{3.26})}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\sqrt{2}a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}u_{1}^{\mu},...,\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}u_{l}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(\frac{2a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}^{\mu},...,u_{l}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{0}\mathrm{u}^{\mu}\,\,\,\,\text{(by Lemma \ref{3.26})}

\displaystyle=\mathrm{u}^{\lambda}.

Case 2.

i\neq 0
. It follows from

\displaystyle\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\lambda}+\overline{w^{\lambda}}(\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\mu}+\overline{w^{\mu}}(\omega_{j}))

\displaystyle=\sigma_{i}(\mathrm{u}^{\mu})=\mathrm{u}^{\lambda}.

(2)
Type

The proof is similar to Case 2 of Type

if

\displaystyle\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\lambda}+\overline{w^{\lambda}}(\omega_{j})

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\sigma_{0}}+\overline{\sigma}_{0}(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\mu}+\overline{w^{\mu}}(\omega_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\mathrm{q}^{\sigma_{0}}+\overline{\sigma}_{0}(\mathrm{u}^{\mu})\,\,\,\,\text{(by induction hypothesis)}

\displaystyle=(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}},\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}},0,...,0)+(-u_{2}^{\mu},-u_{1}^{\mu},...,u_{l}^{\mu})\,\,\,\,\text{(by Lemma \ref{3.26})}

4.
Diophantine equations arising from core abaci

The main task of this section is to generalize the height formula established in
[
28
]

to arbitrary fundamental weight for a core abacus and then connect it to certain Diophantine equations.

4.1.
Height formula of core abaci

In order to prove the height formula, we consider the height change of a core abacus being acted by
\sigma_{i}
,
which is a key step of the proof by induction.
We first study the action of
\sigma_{0}
, which is divided into three cases according to Lemma
3.29
.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
X_{n}^{r}
.
Then

{\rm ht}_{0}(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt{\rm ht}_{0}(\lambda,j)+(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}-u_{2}),&\text{\rm if $X=B_{l}^{(1)},D_{l}^{(1)}$ or $A_{2l-1}^{(2)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt{\rm ht}_{0}(\lambda,j)+(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-2u_{1}),&\text{\rm if $X=A_{2l}^{(2)}$ or $D_{l}^{(2)}$};\\
{\rm ht}_{0}(\lambda,j)+(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}),&\text{\rm if $X=C_{l}^{(1)}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

It follows from Lemma
3.29
that if

X=B_{l}^{(1)},D_{l}^{(1)}
or
A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
, then we have

u(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))=(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{2},\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1},\dots,u_{l})

and consequently the
difference between the number of beads in
{\rm\bf U}(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))
and that in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,j)

is
2(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}-u_{2})
. This implies that we need to do
\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}-u_{2}

times of
f_{0}
(or
e_{0}
) actions from
(\lambda,j)
to
\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j)
, which is that we need to prove.

If
X=A_{2l}^{(2)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
, then by Lemma
3.29
,
u(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))=(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
. Hence the difference between the number of beads
in
{\rm\bf U}(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))
and that in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,j)
is
\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-2u_{1}
.
We divide the proof into three cases according to
j
.

Case 1.

Let
j=0
. Note that in this case
(\lambda,0)
is a half abacus and

\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}=\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}=1
.
Without loss of generality, we assume that
u_{1}\leq 0
. This implies that the leftmost position in
(\lambda,0)
is empty.
Then we need to do
1-2u_{1}
times
f_{0}
,
one of which is putting a bead in the leftmost position in
(\lambda,0)
.

Case 2.

Let
1\leq j\leq l
.
Clearly, we need to do
\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-2u_{1}
times
f_{0}
(or
e_{0}
) action from
(\lambda,j)
to
\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j)
.

Case 3.

Let
j=l
. Note that in this case
(\lambda,l)
is a half abacus and

\frac{a_{l}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}=1
. Particularly, the rows in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,l)
is indexed by
\frac{1}{2}+\mathbb{Z}
.
This implies that the difference of beads
1-2u_{1}
is even. Note that each
2f_{0}
(or
2e_{0}
) action on
(\lambda,l)

makes the number of beads in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,l)
increased (or decreased) by two and the proof of this case is complete.

If
X=C_{l}^{(1)}
, then
u(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))=(\frac{2a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1},\dots,u_{l})
, and the difference between the number of beads
in
{\rm\bf U}(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))
and that in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,j)
is
\frac{2a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-2u_{1}
,
which corresponds to
\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1}
times of
f_{0}
(or
e_{0}
) action on
(\lambda,j)
.
∎

Next we study the effect of
\sigma_{i}
on the height of a core abacus for
1\leq i<l
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Given a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
, then for
1\leq i<l
,

{\rm ht}_{i}(\sigma_{i}(\lambda,j))={\rm ht}_{i}(\lambda,j)+(u_{i}-u_{i+1}).

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we assume that
u_{i}>u_{i+1}
.
We have from Lemma
3.29
that
u(\sigma_{0}(\lambda,j))=(u_{1},\dots u_{i+1},u_{i},\dots,u_{l})
if
1\leq i<l
. This implies that we need to do
u_{i}-u_{i+1}
times of
f_{i}

from
(\lambda,j)
to
\sigma_{i}(\lambda,j)
and then the proof is complete.
∎

The last one we need to consider is the action of
\sigma_{l}
.

Lemma 4.3
.

Given a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
, we have

{\rm ht}_{l}(\sigma_{l}(\lambda,j))=\begin{cases}{\rm ht}_{l}(\lambda,j)+u_{l},&\text{\rm if $X=A_{2l}^{(2)},A_{2l-1}^{(2)}$ or $C_{l}^{(1)}$};\\
{\rm ht}_{l}(\lambda,j)+2u_{l},&\text{\rm if $X=B_{l}^{(1)}$ or $D_{l}^{(2)}$};\\
{\rm ht}_{l}(\lambda,j)+(u_{l-1}+u_{l}),&\text{\rm if $X=D_{l}^{(1)}$}.\end{cases}

Proof.

If
X=D_{l}^{(1)}
, we have
u(\sigma_{l}(\lambda,j))=(u_{1},\dots,-u_{l},-u_{l-1})
by Lemma
3.29
. As a result, the difference between the number of beads in

{\rm\bf U}(\sigma_{l}(\lambda,j))
and that in
{\rm\bf U}(\lambda,j)
is
-2(u_{l-1}+u_{l})
, which derives from
u_{l-1}+u_{l}

times of
f_{l}
(or
e_{l}
) action on
(\lambda,j)
.

We now assume that
X\neq D_{l}^{(1)}
. Then
u(\sigma_{l}(\lambda,j))=(u_{1},\dots,u_{l-1},-u_{l})
by Lemma
3.29
. We divide the proof into two cases according to
whether
l+1
is in the column index set of
(\lambda,j)
.

Case 1.

X=A_{2l}^{(2)},A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
or
C_{l}^{(1)}
. Clearly, the difference of number of beads is
-2u_{l}
,
which derives from
u_{l}
times of
f_{l}
(or
e_{l}
) action on
(\lambda,j)
.

Case 2.

X=B_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
. Since
l+1
is in the column index set,
it is denoted by
2f_{l}
that moving a bead at position
(l,2k)
in
(\lambda,j)
to an empty position
(-l,2k+1)
.
Hence the difference
-2u_{l}
of number of beads derives from
2u_{l}

times of
f_{l}
(or
e_{l}
) action on
(\lambda,j)
.

∎

By combining Lemmas
4.1
-
4.3
, we can give a height formula of a core abaci calculated by Uglov vector.

Lemma 4.4
.

Given a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
, then

{\rm ht}_{i}(\lambda,j)=(\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\mathrm{u}-\omega_{i}^{\vee},\mathrm{u})-(\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\omega_{j}-\omega_{i}^{\vee},\omega_{j})

and consequently

{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{h}{2}(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}-|\omega_{j}|^{2})-(\mathrm{u}-\omega_{j},\rho^{\vee}),

where
\rho^{\vee}=\sum_{i=0}^{l}\omega_{i}^{\vee}
and
\mathrm{u}
is the weighted Uglov vector of
(\lambda,j)
.

Proof.

We shall only prove the
{\rm ht}_{i}
formula by induction on the length of the affine Weyl group element
w_{\lambda,j}
.
It is easy to check the theorem holds when
\ell(w_{\lambda,j})=0
. Now assume that
\ell(w_{\lambda,j})=k>0
, where
(\lambda,j)
is a core abacus.
Then there exists some core abacus
(\mu,j)
and
0\leq r\leq l
such that
w_{\lambda,j}=\sigma_{r}w_{\mu,j}
.
Write by
\mathrm{u}
and
\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}}
the
\mathrm{u}
-vector of
(\mu,j)
and
(\lambda,j)
, respectively.
Denote by

\Delta_{i}:=(\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}}-\omega_{i}^{\vee},\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}})-(\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{a_{i}}{2}\mathrm{u}-\omega_{i}^{\vee},\mathrm{u}).

Then it is easy to check that

\Delta_{i}=\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{a_{i}}{2}[(\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}},\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}})-(\mathrm{u},\mathrm{u})]+(\mathrm{u}-\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}},\omega_{i}^{\vee}).

Clearly we only need to prove that
\Delta_{i}={\rm ht}_{i}(\lambda,j)-{\rm ht}_{i}(\mu,j)
for each
0\leq i\leq l
.

We will only give the proof of type
C_{l}^{(1)}
because the proof of the other types are similar to type
C_{l}^{(1)}
. We first consider
r=0
. By Lemma
3.29
,

\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(\frac{2a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1},\ldots,u_{l}),

and thus

\Delta_{i}=(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1})(a_{i}-\sqrt{2}\omega_{i1}^{\vee}),

where
\omega_{i1}^{\vee}
is the first component of
\omega_{i}^{\vee}
.
Recall that

a_{i}=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }i=0,l\\
2,&\mbox{if }1\leq i\leq l\end{cases}

and

\omega_{i1}^{\vee}=\begin{cases}0,&\mbox{if }i=0\\
\sqrt{2},&\mbox{if }1\leq i\leq l-1\\
\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2},&\mbox{if }i=l,\end{cases}

and thus

\Delta_{i}=\begin{cases}\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}-u_{1},&\mbox{if }i=0\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

which is just the difference of
{\rm ht}_{i}
between
(\lambda,j)
and
(\mu,j)
by Lemma
4.1
.

If
1\leq r\leq l-1
, then by Lemma
3.29

\mathrm{u}^{\sigma_{r}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(u_{1},...,u_{r+1},u_{r},...,u_{l}),

and therefore

\Delta_{i}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(\omega_{ir}^{\vee}-\omega_{i,r+1}^{\vee})(u_{r}-u_{r+1}).

According to the definition of
\omega_{i}^{\vee}
, we have

\omega_{ir}^{\vee}-\omega_{i,r+1}^{\vee}=\begin{cases}0-0,&\mbox{if }i<r\\
\sqrt{2}-0,&\mbox{if }i=r\\
\sqrt{2}-\sqrt{2},&\mbox{if }i>r.\end{cases}

This implies that

\Delta_{i}=\begin{cases}u_{r}-u_{r+1},&\mbox{if }i=r\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise},\end{cases}

which is equal to
{\rm ht}_{i}(\lambda,j)-{\rm ht}_{i}(\mu,j)
for each
0\leq i\leq l
by Lemma
4.2
.

If
r=l
, then
u^{\sigma_{l}}=\frac{\sqrt{2}}{2}(u_{1},...,-u_{l})
and consequently

\Delta_{i}=\sqrt{2}u_{l}\omega_{il}^{\vee}=\begin{cases}u_{l},&\mbox{if }i=l\\
0,&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
4.3
, we have
\Delta_{i}={\rm ht}_{i}(\lambda,j)-{\rm ht}_{i}(\mu,j)
for each
0\leq i\leq l
.
∎

The main significance of Lemma
4.4
in this paper is the Diophantine equations arising from it.
But before we study the equations in the next subsection, we want to point out that the height of a core
(\lambda,j)

is just the
\Lambda_{j}
-atomic length of
w_{\lambda,j}
. This implies that we establish a combinatorics model for atomic length to a certain extent.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus. Then
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda_{j}}(w_{\lambda,j})
.

Proof.

Assume that
w_{\lambda,j}=t_{\mathrm{q}}\overline{w}
.

\displaystyle{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{h}{2}(|\mathrm{u}|^{2}-|\omega_{j}|^{2})-(\mathrm{u}-\omega_{j},\rho^{\vee})\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{4.4})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{0}^{\vee}}{a_{j}^{\vee}}\frac{h}{2}((\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}{\mathrm{q}}+\overline{w}(\omega_{j}),\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}{\mathrm{q}}+\overline{w}(\omega_{j}))-|\omega_{j}|^{2})-

\displaystyle\quad\,\,\textup{ht}(\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}{\mathrm{q}}+\overline{w}(\omega_{j})-\omega_{j})\quad\text{(by Lemma \ref{3.27})}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\frac{h}{2}|{\mathrm{q}}|^{2}+h({\mathrm{q}},\overline{w}(\omega_{j}))-\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}\textup{ht}({\mathrm{q}})+\textup{ht}(\omega_{j}-\overline{w}(\omega_{j}))

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}(\frac{h}{2}|{\mathrm{q}}|^{2}-\textup{ht}({\mathrm{q}}))+h({\mathrm{q}},\overline{w}(\omega_{j}))+\textup{ht}(\omega_{j}-\overline{w}(\omega_{j}))

\displaystyle=\mathscr{L}_{\Lambda_{j}}(w_{\lambda,j})\quad\text{(by \cite[cite]{[\@@bibref{}{BCG}{}{}, Proposition 3.2]})}.

The proof is complete.
∎

\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}^{2}+\dots+u_{l}^{2}-j)-u_{1}+1&=\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}^{2}+\dots+v_{l}^{2}+l-j)-(v_{1}+\dots+v_{l-1})\\
\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots&\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots\quad\quad\dots\dots\\
\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}^{2}+\dots+u_{l}^{2}+j)-(u_{1}+\dots+u_{l-1})&=\frac{1}{2}(v_{1}^{2}+\dots+v_{l}^{2})-v_{1}-\frac{1}{2}(l-j)+1\\
\frac{1}{4}(u_{1}^{2}+\dots+u_{l}^{2}+j)-\frac{1}{2}(u_{1}+\dots+u_{l})&=\frac{1}{4}(v_{1}^{2}+\dots+v_{l}^{2})-\frac{1}{4}(l-j)\\
\end{cases}

Consequently,
(v_{1},\dots,v_{l})=(1-u_{l},\dots,1-u_{1})
.
∎

4.2.
Diophantine equations

Let us consider Diophantine equations arising naturally from the height formula of core abaci by direct computation.
These equations will be called Diophantine equations of affine types.

We list equalities associated with core abaci type by type as follows without specific process.
Then Diophantine equations can be read out from the equalities easily.
Note that all the equations are of the form

\sum_{i}x_{i}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}
, where
N
is the height of a
j
-core (half) abacus of type
X
and
\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}
are determined by
l,j
.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
.
If
j=0,1
, then

8(2l-1)N+\frac{l(2l+1)(2l-1)}{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2(2l-1)u_{i}-(2(l-i)+1)\right)^{2};

if
2\leq j\leq l
, then

4(2l-1)N+\frac{l(2l+1)(2l-1)}{3}-j(2l-1)(2l-2j+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left((2l-1)u_{i}-(2(l-i)+1)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 4.10
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
A_{2l}^{(2)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
.
If
j=0
, then

8(2l+1)N+\frac{l(2l+1)(2l-1)}{3}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2(2l+1)u_{i}-(2(l-i)+1)\right)^{2};

if
1\leq j\leq l
, then

4(2l+1)N+\frac{l(2l+1)(2l-1)}{3}-j(2l+1)(2l-2j-1)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left((2l+1)u_{i}-(2(l-i)+1)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 4.11
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
B_{l}^{(1)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
. Then

4lN+\frac{l(l+1)(2l+1)}{6}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2lu_{i}-(l-i+1)\right)^{2},\,\,{\text{i}f}\,\,j=0,1;

2lN+\frac{l(l+1)(2l+1)}{6}-jl(l-j+1)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(lu_{i}-(l-i+1)\right)^{2},\,\,{\text{i}f}\,\,2\leq j\leq l-1;

4lN+\frac{l(l+1)(2l+1)}{6}-l^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2lu_{i}-(l-i+1)\right)^{2},\,\,{\text{i}f}\,\,j=l.

Lemma 4.12
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
C_{l}^{(1)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
.
Then for
0\leq j\leq l

8lN+\frac{l(2l+1)(2l-1)}{3}-4lj(l-j)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2lu_{i}-(2(l-i)+1)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 4.13
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
D_{l}^{(1)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
. If
j=0,1,l-1,l
, then

4(l-1)N+\frac{(l-1)l(2l-1)}{6}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2(l-1)u_{i}-(l-i)\right)^{2};

if
2\leq j\leq l-2
, then

2(l-1)N+\frac{(l-1)l(2l-1)}{6}-j(l-1)(l-j)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left((l-1)u_{i}-(l-i)\right)^{2}.

Lemma 4.14
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
D_{l}^{(2)}
with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
.
If
j=0,l
, then

4(l+1)N+\frac{l(l+1)(2l+1)}{6}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left(2(l+1)u_{i}-(l-i+1)\right)^{2};

if
1\leq j\leq l-1
, then

2(l+1)N+\frac{l(l+1)(2l+1)}{6}-j(l+1)(l-j)=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left((l+1)u_{i}-(l-i+1)\right)^{2}.

According to the above lemmas, we can define affine transformation
F
on Uglov vectors as follows.

Definition 4.15
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a core abacus of type
X
with Uglov vector
u
. Define

F(u)=\mathtt{k}u-\mathrm{c},

where
\mathtt{k}
and
\mathrm{c}=(\mathrm{c}_{1},\dots,\mathrm{c}_{l})
are defined as follows:

\mathtt{k}=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptl,&\text{\rm if $X=B_{l}^{(1)},2\leq j\leq l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptl-1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)}$, $2\leq j\leq l-2$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptl+1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(2)}$, $1\leq j\leq l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l,&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)},j=0,1,l$ {\rm or type} $C_{l}^{(1)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l-1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},2\leq j\leq l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l-2,&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)},j=0,1,l-1,l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l+1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l}^{(2)},j\neq 0$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l+2,&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(2)},j=0,l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt4l-2,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},j=0,1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt4l+2,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l}^{(2)},j=0$},\\
\end{cases}

\mathrm{c}_{i}=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2(l-i)+1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},A_{2l}^{(2)}\,{\rm or}\,C_{l}^{(1)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptl-i+1,&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)}$, $D_{l}^{(2)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0ptl-i,&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)}$}.\\
\end{cases}

Combining Lemmas
4.9
-
4.14
yields the following theorem.

Theorem 4.16
.

Let
(\lambda,j)
be a type
X
core abacus with
{\rm ht}(\lambda,j)=N
.
Then
F(u)
is a solution of the corresponding affine Diophantine equation

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b},

where
\mathtt{a},\mathtt{b}
are defined as follows.

\mathtt{a}=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2l,&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)},2\leq j\leq l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2(l-1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)}$, $2\leq j\leq l-2$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt2(l+1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(2)}$, $2\leq j\leq l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt4l,&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)},j=0,1,l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt4(l-1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)},j=0,1,l-1,l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt4(l+1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(2)},j=0,l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt8l,&\text{{\rm if} $X=C_{l}^{(1)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt8l-4,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},2\leq j\leq l$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt8l+4,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l}^{(2)},j\neq 0$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt16l-8,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},j=0,1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt16l+8,&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l}^{(2)},j=0$,}\end{cases}

\mathtt{b}=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt(2l+1)(\frac{1}{3}l(2l-1)-j(2l-2j-1)),&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l}^{(2)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt(2l-1)(\frac{1}{3}l(2l+1)-j(2l-2j+1)),&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},j\neq 1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{3}l(2l+1)(2l-1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=A_{2l-1}^{(2)},j=1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{3}l(2l+1)(2l-1)-4lj(l-j),&\text{{\rm if} $X=C_{l}^{(1)}$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{6}l(l+1)(2l+1)-jl(l-j+1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)}$, $j\neq 1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{6}l(l+1)(2l+1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=B_{l}^{(1)}$, $j=1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt(l-1)(\frac{1}{6}l(2l-1)-j(l-j)),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)},j\neq 1,l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{6}(l-1)l(2l-1),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(1)},j=1,l-1$};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt(l+1)(\frac{1}{6}l(2l+1)-j(l-j)),&\text{{\rm if} $X=D_{l}^{(2)}$}.\\
\end{cases}

Remark 4.17
.

In general, core abaci only provide partially parametrization for the solutions of the corresponding Diophantine equation.
We will consider in the next section under what conditions the parametrization is complete.

5.
Solutions of Diophantine equations and core abaci

The main task of this section is to answer the following question:
given a solution orbit
\rm O
of a classical affine Lie type Diophantine equation,
under what conditions
\rm O
is parameterized by core abaci? After giving some general results,
we obtain some special classes Diophantine equations completely parameterized by core abaci.
Consequently, some closed formulae on the number of certain core abaci are given.
We also raise two open problems at the end of this section.

5.1.
Some general results

It is well known that the Weyl group
W
of type
B_{l}
acts on the solution set of affine type
Diophantine equation
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}
naturally
(see Theorem 4.16 for notations
\mathtt{a}
and
\mathtt{b}
). For a given solution
t
,
define the associated solution orbit
{\rm O}_{t}=\{w.t\mid w\in W\}
.

For the convenience of description, we give a definition as below.

Definition 5.1
.

Let
t
be a solution of an affine type Diophantine equation
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}.
We say
t
is parameterized by a core abacus if
there exists certain height
N
core abacus
(\lambda,j)
with the
u
-vector
(u_{1},u_{2},\dots,u_{l})
such that
F(u)=t
.
A solution orbit
{\rm O}_{t}
is called to be parameterized by core abaci if there is a solution in
{\rm O}_{t}
being parameterized by a core abacus.
Denote by
{\rm O}^{\rm p}_{t}
the solutions in
{\rm O}_{t}
being parameterized by core abaci.
The Diophantine equation is said to be completely parameterized by core if each of its solution orbit being parameterized by core abaci.

Let us give an easy criterion for a solution being parameterized by a core abacus. The proof is omitted here.
Recall notations
\mathtt{k}
and
\mathrm{c}_{i}
in Definition
4.15
.

Lemma 5.2
.

A solution
t
of an affine type Diophantine equation
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}

is parameterized by a core abacus
(\lambda,j)
if and only if for
i=1,\dots,l

(1)

\frac{t_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}

and the number of odd
\frac{t_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}
is
j
, for type

C_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
j
,
or type
A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
,
A_{2l}^{(2)}
,
B_{l}^{(1)}
,
D_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
j

\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}=2
;

(2)

\frac{t_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}
, for type

A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
,
B_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
j
being
0
or
1
, or type
A_{2l}^{(2)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
j=0

(3)

\frac{t_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}\in\mathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
, for type

D_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
l-1
or
l
, or type
B_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
l

Based on Lemma
5.2

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
{\rm O}_{t}

Then

(1)

r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k}
or
r_{i}=\mathrm{c}_{i}
and the number of

r_{i}
satisfying
r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k}
is exact
j
, for type

C_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
j
, or type
A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
,
A_{2l}^{(2)}
,
B_{l}^{(1)}
,
D_{l}^{(1)}

or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
j
satisfying
\frac{a_{j}^{\vee}}{a_{0}^{\vee}}=2

(2)

r_{i}=\mathrm{c}_{i}
, for type

A_{2l-1}^{(2)}
,
B_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
j
being
0
or
1
, or type
A_{2l}^{(2)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
j=0
;

(3)

r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\frac{\mathtt{k}}{2}
, for type

D_{l}^{(1)}
with charge
l-1
or
l
, or type
B_{l}^{(1)}
or
D_{l}^{(2)}
with charge
l
.

Proof.

Since the all the proof of these three cases is similar, we shall only prove the first one.
Assume that
t_{i}=2\mathtt{k}k_{i}+r_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,l
.
Note that
r_{i}=\mathtt{k}-\mathrm{c}_{i}
or
r_{i}=\mathrm{c}_{i}
.
Take
v=(v_{1},\dots,v_{l})\in{\rm O}_{t}
, where

v_{i}=\begin{cases}t_{i},&\mbox{if }r_{i}=\mathtt{k}-\mathrm{c}_{i};\\
-t_{i},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Then

\frac{v_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}=\begin{cases}2k_{i}+1,&\mbox{if }r_{i}=\mathtt{k}-\mathrm{c}_{i};\\
2k_{i},&\mbox{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that the odd components of
(\frac{v_{1}+\mathrm{c}_{1}}{\mathtt{k}},\dots,\frac{v_{l}+\mathrm{c}_{l}}{\mathtt{k}})
is

.
Then the result follows by Lemma
5.2
.
∎

Next we study the relationship between two solution orbits. To this aim, we need to define an equivalence relation in
\mathbb{Z}^{l}
.

Definition 5.4
.

Let
k\in\mathbb{Z}
and
(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})\equiv(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
,
(n_{1},\dots,n_{l})\equiv(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime})\pmod{k}
,
where
b_{i},n_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}
for
i=1,\dots,l
. Define
(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})\sim_{k}(n_{1},\dots,n_{l})
if there exists

\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
such that
r_{i}=\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})
or
r_{i}+\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=k
for each
i=1,\dots,l
.

It is easy to check that
\equiv_{k}
is an equivalence relation.
The equivalence class including
(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})
is denoted by
[(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})]
.
The following lemma can be obtained by direct calculations and we omit the proof here.

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
t,t^{\prime}\in\rm O
be two solutions of the equation
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}.

Then
t\sim_{2\mathtt{k}}t^{\prime}
.

Based on Lemma
5.5
, we can say that a solution orbit in an equivalence class in the sense of Definition
5.4
.
It is easy to check that for arbitrary
(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})\in\mathbb{Z}^{l}
, there exists
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})\sim_{2\mathtt{k}}(b_{1},\dots,b_{l})

such that
0\leq r_{i}\leq\mathtt{k}
for each
i=1,\dots,l
.
Then we have the following lemma about the relation between two solution orbits.

Lemma 5.6
.

Let
{\rm O}_{v}
and
{\rm O}_{w}
be two solution orbits of an affine type
X
Diophantine equation

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}

with charge
j
, where

(v_{1},\dots,v_{l})\equiv(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})\pmod{2\mathtt{k}},

(w_{1},\dots,w_{l})\equiv(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime})\pmod{2\mathtt{k}}

and

(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})\sim_{2\mathtt{k}}(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime}).

If
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
satisfies one of the condition of Lemma
5.3
,
then
{\rm O}_{w}
is parameterized by core abaci.

Proof.

We assume that
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})
satisfies condition
(1)
of Lemma
5.3
because all the proof is similar.
Since
(r_{1},\dots,r_{l})\sim_{2\mathtt{k}}(r_{1}^{\prime},\dots,r_{l}^{\prime})
, by Definition
5.4
, there exists
\sigma\in\mathfrak{S}_{l}
such that

r_{i}=\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})
or
r_{i}+\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}
for each
i=1,\dots,l
.
Then clearly

\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\mathtt{k}+\mathrm{c}_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
2\mathtt{k}-\mathrm{c}_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k};\\
\mathtt{k}-\mathrm{c}_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
\mathrm{c}_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k}.\end{cases}

Take
z=(z_{1},\dots,z_{l})\in{\rm O}_{w}
, where

z_{i}=\begin{cases}-w_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
w_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k};\\
w_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
-w_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k}.\end{cases}

Suppose that
w_{i}=2\mathtt{k}k_{i}+\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})
for all
i=1,\dots,l
.
Then

\frac{z_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}}{\mathtt{k}}=\begin{cases}2k_{i}-1,&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
2k_{i}+2,&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})=2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k};\\
2k_{i}+1,&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}=\mathtt{k};\\
2k_{i},&\mbox{if }\sigma(r_{i}^{\prime})\neq 2\mathtt{k}-r_{i}\,\,\text{and}\,\,r_{i}+\mathrm{c}_{i}\neq\mathtt{k}.\end{cases}

By Lemma
5.2
, the result follows.
∎

By Lemma
5.6
, direct computation yields the following result, which can be used to simplify the related calculation in the next subsections.

Corollary 5.7
.

Let
{\rm O}_{v}
and
{\rm O}_{w}
be two solution orbits of an affine type
X

Diophantine equation
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+\dots+x_{l}^{2}=\mathtt{a}N+\mathtt{b}
with
[v]=[w]
.
Then the number of solutions being parameterized by core abaci in

is equal to that in
{\rm O}_{w}
.

(2)

(3)

;

(4)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=6N+2
;

(5)

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=8N+6
;

(6)

x^{2}+y^{2}=12N+5
;

(7)

x^{2}+y^{2}=6N+2
;

(8)

x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=8N+6
;

(9)

x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=6N+2
.

Proof.

We only prove (9) because the proof of all the others is similar.

Note that equation (9) is of affine type
D_{4}^{(1)}
with charge 2. Note that
\mathtt{k}=3
,
\mathrm{c}=(3,2,1,0)
in this case.
For a given nonnegative integer
N
, let
{\rm O}_{t}
be a solution orbit of equation (9) with
t
being a nonnegative solution and

(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3},t_{4})\equiv(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4})\pmod{6}
with
0\leq r_{1}\leq r_{2}\leq r_{3}\leq r_{4}\leq 5
.
Since
6N+2
is even, an easy computation gives that all
t_{i}
are all odd, all even or only two ones even.
Moreover, the residue
(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4})
has to be one of the
following 16 cases by a routine calculation:

\begin{array}[]{cccccc}(0,0,1,1)&(0,0,1,5)&(0,0,2,2)&(0,0,2,4)&(0,0,4,4)&(0,0,5,5)\\
(0,1,2,3)&(0,1,3,4)&(0,2,3,5)&(0,3,4,5)&(1,1,3,3)&(1,3,3,5)\\
(2,2,3,3)&(2,3,3,4)&(3,3,4,4)&(3,3,5,5)\end{array}

If
r=(r_{1},r_{2},r_{3},r_{4})=(0,0,1,1)
, denoting
(0,1,1,0)
by
\overline{r}
, then
\overline{t}=(t_{1},t_{3},t_{4},t_{2})\in{\rm O}_{t}
and
\overline{t}\equiv\overline{r}\pmod{6}
.
Clearly,
\overline{r}_{1}+\mathrm{c}_{1}=3
,
\overline{r}_{2}+\mathrm{c}_{2}=3
,
\overline{r}_{3}=\mathrm{c}_{3}
and
\overline{r}_{4}=\mathrm{c}_{4}
.
That is, all the conditions that Lemma
5.3
required for this case are satisfied and then the solution orbit is parameterized by core abaci.
Consequently, if
r\in[(0,0,1,1)]
, the corresponding solution orbit is parameterized by core by Lemma
5.6
.

It is easy to check that

\displaystyle[(0,0,1,1)]=

\displaystyle\left\{(0,0,1,1),(0,0,1,5),(0,0,5,5)\right\}

\displaystyle[(0,0,2,2)]=

\displaystyle\left\{(0,0,2,2),(0,0,2,4),(0,0,4,4)\right\}

\displaystyle[(1,1,3,3)]=

\displaystyle\left\{(1,1,3,3),(1,3,3,5),(3,3,5,5)\right\}

\displaystyle[(2,2,3,3)]=

\displaystyle\left\{(2,2,3,3),(2,3,3,4),(3,3,4,4)\right\}

\displaystyle[(0,1,2,3)]=

\displaystyle\left\{(0,1,2,3),(0,1,3,4),(0,2,3,5),(0,3,4,5)\right\}

in the sense of Definition
5.4
.
The proof is similar if
r
is in the other equivalence classes and we omit the details here.
∎

5.3.
Open problems and related results

In this subsection, we shall propose two open problems about core abaci and give some relevant results.
Note that all the quadratic form content from number theory used in this section can be found in
[
18
]
.

Open problem 1.
Given an affine type
X
, determine the set
\{|(\lambda,j)|\mid(\lambda,j)\in\mathcal{C}_{j}\}
.

Let us focus on the so-called positivity conjecture raised by Hanusa and Nath in
[
15
]
first.
We will give an equivalent description for it.

Conjecture 5.9
.

For affine type
C_{3}^{(1)}
, we have

\{|\lambda|\mid\lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\}=\mathbb{N}-\{2,12,13,73\}
.

Remark 5.10
.

In
[
1
]
Alpoge proved the conjecture for large enough
n
by applying deep results of Duke and Schulze-Pillot
[
7
]
.
However, the implied constant is ineffective because of the Landau–Siegel phenomenon. On the other hand,
Hanson and Jameson
[
13
]
proved the conjecture by assuming Generalized Riemann Hypothesis holds.

According to Subsections 5.1 and 5.2, we can give a new description of the set
\{|\lambda|\mid\lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\}

Proposition 5.11

For affine type
C_{3}^{(1)}

\{|\lambda|\mid\lambda\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\}=\{6(k_{1}^{2}+k_{2}^{2}+k_{3}^{2})+3k_{1}+k_{2}+5k_{3}\mid k_{1},k_{2},k_{3}\in\mathbb{Z}\}.

Proof.

Note that the equations of affine
C_{3}^{(1)}
type with charge
0
are of the form
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=24N+35

For a given nonnegative integer
N

be a solution of the equation.
It is easy to check that
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
are all odd and
{\rm O}_{t}
is in a unique
\sim_{12}
-equivalence class

[(1,1,3)]
,
[(1,3,5)]
or
[(3,5,5)]

5.4

5.3
and
5.6

the equivalence class
[(1,3,5)]

Without considering the order, the residue of a resolution in the equivalence class
[(1,3,5)]
has to be one of the following eight cases.

\begin{array}[]{cccc}(1,3,5)&(1,3,7)&(1,5,9)&(1,7,9)\\
(3,5,11)&(3,7,11)&(5,9,11)&(7,9,11).\end{array}

If the residue of
t=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
is
(1,3,5)

,
t_{2}=12k_{2}+3
and
t_{3}=12k_{3}+5
.
By substituting them into the equation, we get

If the residue of
t=(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
is
(1,3,7)

t_{1}=12k_{1}+1
,
t_{2}=12k_{2}+3
and

We obtain

The other cases can be handled by similar method and we omit the details. As a result, we have proved “
\subseteq

For the other direction “
\supseteq

and denote

We get
(12k_{1}+1)^{2}+(12k_{2}+3)^{2}+(12k_{3}+5)^{2}=24N+35
. It follows from Lemma
5.2
that
(-12k_{3}-5,-12k_{2}-3,-12k_{1}-1)

can be parameterized by core. This completes the proof.
∎

Open problem 2.
Enumerate core abaci of affine types with fixing certain parameters.

Closed formulae for enumerating certain cores will be given in some special cases.

The following lemma about the number of solutions parameterized by core abaci is important.

Lemma 5.14
.

Given a Diophantine equation of affine type
X=C_{2}^{(1)}
or
D_{2}^{(2)}

|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{\rm if }X=C_{2}^{(1)}\,{\rm or}\,X=D_{2}^{(2)},j=0,2;\\

It is a routine task of computation by Lemma
5.6

∎

We recall a notation from number theory that will appear in the closed formulae.

\chi_{4}(d)=\begin{cases}1,&\mbox{if }d\equiv 1\pmod{4};\\
-1,&\mbox{if }d\equiv-1\pmod{4};\\
0,&\mbox{if }d\equiv 0\,\,\mbox{or }2\pmod{4}.\end{cases}

Denote the representation number of a positive integer
n
represented as the sum of
k
squares by
r_{k}(n)
.
Then we can calculate the number of core abaci of affine type
C_{2}^{(1)}
and
D_{2}^{(2)}
with arbitrary charge and a given height.

Theorem 5.15
.

Denote the number of
j
-core abaci of affine type
X
with height
N
by
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{X}(N)|
. Then

|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{X}(N)|=\begin{cases}\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{8}r_{2}(16N+10)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{d\mid 16N+10}\chi_{4}(d),&\mbox{\rm if }j=0\mbox{ \rm or }2,\mbox{ \rm and }X=C_{2}^{(1)};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{8}r_{2}(12N+5)=\frac{1}{2}\sum\limits_{d\mid 12N+5}\chi_{4}(d),&\mbox{\rm if }j=0\mbox{ \rm or }2,\mbox{ \rm and }X=D_{2}^{(2)};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{4}r_{2}(16N+2)=\sum\limits_{d\mid 16N+2}\chi_{4}(d),&\mbox{\rm if }j=1\mbox{ \rm and }X=C_{2}^{(1)};\\
\vskip 3.0pt plus 1.0pt minus 1.0pt\frac{1}{4}r_{2}(6N+2)=\sum\limits_{d\mid 6N+2}\chi_{4}(d),&\mbox{\rm if }j=1\mbox{ \rm and }X=D_{2}^{(2)}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first consider 0-cores of affine type
C_{2}^{(1)}
. It follows from Lemma
5.14
that
there is a bijection between the cores of height
N
and solution orbits
for Diophantine equation
x^{2}+y^{2}=16N+10
, where
N
is a nonnegative integer. Moreover,
simple calculation yields that if
(t_{1},t_{2})
is a solution of the Diophantine equations then
t_{1}\neq t_{2}
and
t_{i}\neq 0
,
i=1,2
.
This implies that there are 8 solutions in each solution orbit of the Diophantine equations.
We have from Dirichlet formula that

r_{2}(16N+10)=4\sum\limits_{d\mid 16N+10}\chi_{4}(d)

and thus the proof of this case is complete.
Similarly, we can prove all of the other cases.
∎

Recall that a map
f:\mathbb{N}\longrightarrow\mathbb{N}

is said to be multiplicative if
f(ab)=f(a)f(b)
for
{\rm gcd}(a,b)=1
.
It is well-known that
\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\chi_{4}(d)
is multiplicative.
Then we can obtain a corollary of Proposition
5.15
.

Corollary 5.16
.

Given two nonnegative integer
N_{1},N_{2}
, then
|\mathcal{C}_{1}^{X}(N_{1})||\mathcal{C}_{1}^{X}(N_{2})|
is equal to

\begin{cases}|\mathcal{C}_{1}^{X}(8N_{1}N_{2}+N_{1}+N_{2})|,&\mbox{\rm if }X=C_{2}^{(1)},\,{\rm gcd}(8N_{1}+1,8N_{2}+1)=1;\\
|\mathcal{C}_{1}^{X}(3N_{1}N_{2}+N_{1}+N_{2})|,&\mbox{\rm if }X=D_{2}^{(1)},\,{\rm gcd}(3N_{1}+1,3N_{2}+1)=1.\end{cases}

Proof.

It is easy to check that
r_{2}(16N+2)=r_{2}(8N+1)
by Dirichlet formula.
Since
\sum\limits_{d\mid n}\chi_{4}(d)
is multiplicative, if
{\rm gcd}(8N_{1}+1,8N_{2}+1)=1
, then

\frac{1}{4}r_{2}(8N_{1}+1)\cdot\frac{1}{4}r_{2}(8N_{2}+1)=\frac{1}{4}r_{2}(8(8N_{1}N_{2}+N_{1}+N_{2})+1).

By Theorem
5.15
, for 1-core abaci of affine type

, this implies that

|\mathcal{C}_{1}(N_{1})||\mathcal{C}_{1}(N_{2})|=|\mathcal{C}_{1}(8N_{1}N_{2}+N_{1}+N_{2})|.

The proof of affine type
D_{2}^{(1)}
is similar.
∎

Next let us study the affine types
B_{3}^{(1)}
and

Recall that

Theorem 5.17
.

Given a nonnegative integer
N

|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{X}(N)|=\begin{cases}\frac{1}{24}r_{3}(8N+6),\,&\mbox{\rm if }X=D_{3}^{(2)},j=2,\ N\,\ \text{\rm is even};\\

The equations of affine type
D_{3}^{(2)}
with charge
2
are of the form
x^{2}+y^{2}+z^{2}=8N+6
.
For a given nonnegative integer
N
, let

Then two of
t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}
are odd and
{\rm O}_{t}

\sim_{8}
-equivalence class

[(1,2,3)]

5.4

5.7

(|{\rm O}_{t}|,|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|)=\begin{cases}(192,\,2),&{\rm if\,\,}\text{ $|t_{1}|=|t_{2}|$};\\
(384,\,4),&{\rm if\,\,}\text{ $|t_{1}|\neq|t_{2}|$}.\end{cases}

If
t\in[(2,3,3,4)]
, then

(|{\rm O}_{t}|,|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|)=\begin{cases}(192,\,2),&{\rm if\,\,}\text{ $|t_{2}|=|t_{3}|$};\\
(384,\,4),&{\rm if\,\,}\text{ $|t_{2}|\neq|t_{3}|$}.\end{cases}

Finally, if
t\in[(1,2,3,4)]
, then
(|{\rm O}_{t}|,|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|)=(384,\,2)
.

If
N
is even, then
8N+6\equiv 6\pmod{16}
. This forces the squares of two odd numbers in
t
simultaneously satisfying

x^{2}\equiv 1\pmod{16}
, or
x^{2}\equiv 9\pmod{16}
by analysis of modulo 16.
As a result,
t\in[(0,1,1,2)]
,
[(0,2,3,3)]
,
[(1,1,2,4)]
or
[(2,3,3,4)]
.
This implies that
|{\rm O}_{t}|/|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|=96
and thus
|\mathcal{C}_{j}^{X}(N)|=\frac{1}{96}r_{4}(8N+6)
.
On the other hand, for
N
being odd, the squares of two odd numbers in
t

satisfy that one is of the form
16n_{1}+1
and the other one
16n_{2}+9
.
That is,
t\in[(0,1,2,3)]
or
[(1,2,3,4)]
and hence
|{\rm O}_{t}|/|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|=192
.
The proof of affine type
B_{4}^{(1)}
is complete.

We now consider affine type
D_{4}^{(1)}
. Note that the corresponding equations are of the form
x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}+x_{4}^{2}=6N+2
.
If
N
is odd, then
4|6N+2
. Based on this fact, we can obtain by direct calculation
that a solution
t
of the equation is in a
\sim_{6}
equivalence class
[(0,0,2,2)]
or
[(1,1,3,3)]
.
If
t\in[(0,0,2,2)]
, without loss of generality, assume that
t_{1}=6k_{1}
,
t_{2}=6k_{2}
,
t_{3}=6k_{3}+2
and
t_{4}=6k_{4}+2
. Then

(|{\rm O}_{t}|,|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|)=\begin{cases}(24,\,1),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}=k_{2}=0$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(48,\,2),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}=k_{2}=0$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$};\\
(96,\,4),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}=0$, $k_{1}\neq k_{2}$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(192,\,8),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}=0$, $k_{1}\neq k_{2}$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$};\\
(96,\,4),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}\neq 0$, $|t_{1}|=|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(192,\,8),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}\neq 0$, $|t_{1}|=|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$};\\
(192,\,8),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}\neq 0$, $|t_{1}|\neq|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(384,\,16),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $k_{1}k_{2}\neq 0$, $|t_{1}|\neq|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$.}\end{cases}

If
t\in[(1,1,3,3)]
, then

(|{\rm O}_{t}|,|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|)=\begin{cases}(96,\,4),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $|t_{1}|=|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(192,\,8),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $|t_{1}|=|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$};\\
(192,\,8),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $|t_{1}|\neq|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|=|t_{4}|$};\\
(384,\,16),&{\rm if}\,\,\text{ $|t_{1}|\neq|t_{2}|$, $|t_{3}|\neq|t_{4}|$.}\end{cases}

That is,
|{\rm O}_{t}|/|{\rm O}_{t}^{\rm p}|=24
for all cases and the proof is complete.
