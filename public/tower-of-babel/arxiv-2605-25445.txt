1.
Introduction

The study of neighborhood bases at the identity in topological groups has long served as a vital intersection between general topology, topological algebra, and combinatorial set theory. Given a topological group
G
, the neighborhood filter at the identity, denoted by
\mathcal{N}_{e}(G)
and ordered by reverse inclusion, forms a directed partially ordered set (poset). The combinatorial complexity of this poset intrinsically encodes profound structural properties of the group. A remarkably delicate and powerful tool for classifying these local structures is the Tukey order. For two directed posets
P
and
Q
, we say
P
is Tukey below
Q
, denoted
P\leq_{T}Q
, if there exists a map from
Q
to
P
carrying cofinal sets to cofinal sets.

In recent years, the local structure of non-metrizable topological groups has attracted significant attention, particularly the class of groups admitting a
P
-base (i.e., when there exists a cofinal, order-preserving map from a directed poset
P
to
\mathcal{N}_{e}(G)
), see for example
[
6
,
8
,
14
,
16
]
. A celebrated instance is the class of topological groups with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base
[
2
,
3
,
10
,
17
,
18
]
. Initiated in the context of uniform spaces by Cascales and Orihuela
[
4
]
, and systematically explored for topological groups by Gabriyelyan, Kąkol, and Leiderman
[
10
]
, it was shown that the character
\chi(G)
of a non-metrizable group with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base must fall within the interval
[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{d}]
. The question of whether the lower bound
\mathfrak{b}
can be attained was affirmatively resolved by Leiderman, Pestov, and Tomita
[
17
]
, who constructed a group whose neighborhood base is Tukey equivalent to any given regular cardinal Tukey below
\omega^{\omega}
.

Motivated by these deep results, the first objective of this paper is to seek an intrinsically defined cardinal invariant for general directed posets that dictates the strict lower bounds of character and pseudocharacter. We introduce the
fineness index
, denoted
\operatorname{fi}(P)
. This invariant elegantly isolates the critical threshold at which a poset ceases to be dominated by its countable subsets. We demonstrate that for any topological group
G
with a
P
-base, its character is strictly bounded by
\chi(G)\in\{1,\omega\}\cup[\operatorname{fi}(P),\operatorname{cof}(P)]
, establishing a foundational generalization that subsumes previous results restricted to the
\omega^{\omega}
domain.

The second major focus of this paper confronts a structural problem regarding the exact cofinal types of free topological groups
F(X)
. In a pair of seminal papers
[
21
,
22
]
, Nickolas and Tkachenko developed the method of uniform covering trees to compute the character of free topological groups over compact spaces, a breakthrough later extended by Chis, Ferrer, Hernández, and Tsaban
[
5
]
. Their combinatorial machinery successfully yielded the cardinal equality
\chi(F(X,\mathcal{U}))=\operatorname{cof}(\mathcal{U}^{\omega})
. However, a profound structural gap remains: can this cardinal equality be lifted to a strict Tukey equivalence, namely
\mathcal{N}_{e}(F(X,\mathcal{U}))=_{T}\mathcal{U}^{\omega}
? For decades, establishing this equivalence has proven highly non-trivial due to the rigid requirement of Tukey maps to preserve cofinality across all scales, an obstruction that cardinal equalities inherently ignore.

In this paper, we bridge this gap for a broad class of uniform spaces. The crux of our approach lies in overcoming the combinatorial rigidity of standard uniform covering trees. We introduce the mechanism of
neat trees
, a refinement that meticulously orchestrates the branch structures to bypass the “product traps” that traditionally obstruct Tukey embeddings. By deploying this machinery, we establish that for any
\text{add}(\mathcal{U})
-precompact uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
, the strict equivalence
\mathcal{N}_{e}(F(X,\mathcal{U}))=_{T}\mathcal{U}^{\omega}
is achieved. Especially, this equivalence holds for any compact space.

Finally, we translate this abstract Tukey equivalence into exact evaluations for specific topological classes. To achieve this, we invoke Shelah’s Strong Hypothesis (
SSH
). Arising from pcf (possible cofinalities) theory,
SSH
asserts that the pseudo-power
pp(\lambda)=\lambda^{+}
for every singular cardinal
\lambda
. Utilizing
SSH
, we precisely evaluate the cofinality of the countable power of
[\tau]^{<\omega}
and collapse the product space, proving that for an infinite precompact uniform space of weight
\tau
with
\text{cof}(\tau)>\omega
, both
\mathcal{N}_{e}(F(X,\mathcal{U}))
and
\mathcal{N}_{e}(A(X,\mathcal{U}))
are exactly Tukey equivalent to
[\tau]^{<\omega}
.

1.1.
Tukey order of (pre-)ordered sets

Let
P
be a set. A
quasi-order
(or
pre-order
)
\leq
on
P
is a reflexive and transitive binary relation. If
\leq
is also antisymmetric, then it is called a
partial order
. In this case, the pair
(P,\leq)
is called a
partially ordered set
(briefly, a
poset
).

A pre-ordered set
(P,\leq)
is called
directed
if for every
x,y\in P
there exists
z\in P
such that
x\leq z
and
y\leq z
.

For
A,B\subseteq P
, we say that
A
is
dominated
by
B
if for every
a\in A
there exists
b\in B
such that
a\leq b
.

Let
(P,\leq)
be a directed pre-ordered set. A subset
A\subseteq P
is called

•

cofinal
in
P
if
P
is dominated by
A
;

•

bounded
in
P
if
A
is dominated by a single point of
P
.

We associate two cardinals with
P
:

•

the
cofinality

\operatorname{cof}(P)=\min\{\,|A|:A\text{ is cofinal in }P\,\};

•

the
additivity

\operatorname{add}(P)=\min\{\,|A|:A\text{ is unbounded in }P\,\}.

Note that a pre-ordered set may have no nonempty unbounded subset; this happens precisely when
P
has a (not necessarily unique) largest element. In this case, we put
\operatorname{add}(P)=1
, which is equivalent to
\operatorname{cof}(P)=1
.

It is easy to see that if
\operatorname{add}(P)\neq 1
, then
\operatorname{add}(P)
is a regular cardinal and
\operatorname{add}(P)\leq\operatorname{cof}(P)
. The well-known cardinal characteristics
\mathfrak{b}
and
\mathfrak{d}
are precisely the additivity and the cofinality of
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
, respectively, where
f\leq^{*}g
means that
f(n)\leq g(n)
for all but finitely many
n\in\omega
.

We now introduce a central notion in the theory of directed posets, due to Tukey
[
26
]
. Let
(P,\leq_{P})
and
(Q,\leq_{Q})
be directed pre-ordered sets. We say that
Q
is
Tukey above

P
, or equivalently that
P
is
Tukey below

Q
, and write
P\leq_{T}Q
, if there exists a
cofinal map

\varphi:Q\to P
, that is, a map which sends cofinal subsets of
Q
to cofinal subsets of
P
.

By a characterization of Schmidt
[
23
,
25
]
, this is equivalent to the existence of an
unbounded map

\psi:P\to Q
, that is, a map sending unbounded subsets of
P
to unbounded subsets of
Q
.

If, in addition,
P
and
Q
are Dedekind complete (i.e., every bounded subset has a least upper bound), then
P\leq_{T}Q
if and only if there exists an order-preserving map
f:Q\to P
such that
f(Q)
is cofinal in
P

[
12
]
.

Two posets
P
and
Q
are said to be
Tukey equivalent
, written
P=_{T}Q
, if
P\leq_{T}Q
and
Q\leq_{T}P
. A
cofinal type
is a Tukey equivalent class.
We list several well-known examples, which will also be used throughout the paper, to better understand the Tukey order.

.

1.2.
Uniform space

Let
X
be a set and let
A
and
B
be subsets of
X\times X
, i.e., relations on the set
X
. Let

-A=\{(x,y):(y,x)\in A\}

and

A+B=\{(x,z):\text{there exists a }y\in X\text{ such that }(x,y)\in A\text{ and }(y,z)\in B\}.

For a relation
A\subseteq X\times X
and a natural number
n
the relation
nA\subseteq X\times X
is defined inductively by the formulas

1A=A\text{ and }nA=(n-1)A+A.

The diagonal of the Cartesian product
X\times X
is
\Delta=\{(x,x):x\in X\}
. Every set
V\subseteq X\times X
that contains
\Delta
and satisfies the condition
V=-V
is called an entourage of the diagonal; the family of all entourages of the diagonal
\Delta
will be denoted by
\mathcal{D}_{X}
. If for a pair
x,y\in X
and a
V\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
we have
(x,y)\in V
, we write
|x-y|<V
; otherwise we write
|x-y|\geq V
.

Let
x_{0}\in X
and
V\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
. The set
B(x_{0},V)=\{x\in X:|x_{0}-x|<V\}
is called the
V
-ball about
x_{0}
.

A uniformity on a set
X
is a subfamily
\mathcal{U}
of
\mathcal{D}_{X}
which satisfies the following conditions:

(i)

If
V\in\mathcal{U}
and
V\subseteq U\in\mathcal{D}_{X}
, then
U\in\mathcal{U}
.

(ii)

If
V_{1},V_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
, then
V_{1}\cap V_{2}\in\mathcal{U}
.

(iii)

For every
V\in\mathcal{U}
there exists a
U\in\mathcal{U}
such that
2U\subseteq V
.

(iv)

\cap\mathcal{U}=\Delta
.

A family
\mathcal{B}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
is called a base for the uniformity
\mathcal{U}
if for every
V\in\mathcal{U}
there exists a
W\in\mathcal{B}
such that
W\subseteq V
.

w(\mathcal{U})=\text{min}\{|\mathcal{B}|:\mathcal{B}\text{ is a base for }\mathcal{U}\}

is called the weight of the uniformity
\mathcal{U}
.

A uniform space is a pair
(X,\mathcal{U})
consisting of a set
X
and a uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on
X
. The weight of a uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
is defined as the weight of the uniformity
\mathcal{U}
.

Any cover of the uniform space
(X,\mathcal{U})
which has a refinement of the form
\{B(x,V):x\in X\}
, where
V\in\mathcal{U}
, is called a uniform cover.

1.3.
Free Topological Groups over Uniform Spaces

Let
(X,\mathcal{U})
be a uniform space. The free (Abelian) topological group over
(X,\mathcal{U})
, denoted by
F(X,\mathcal{U})
(
A(F,{\mathcal{U}})
), is the free (abelian) group generated by
X
endowed with the finest group topology such that every uniformly continuous map from
(X,\mathcal{U})
into a topological (Abelian) group (equipped with its natural two-sided uniformity) uniquely extends to a continuous group homomorphism.

This framework naturally generalizes the classical Markov free topological (Abelian) group over a Tychonoff space
X
. Indeed, if
\mathcal{U}_{X}
denotes the fine uniformity on
X
, then
F(X)
is topologically isomorphic to
F(X,\mathcal{U}_{X})
. For any compatible uniformity
\mathcal{U}
on
X
, the relation
\mathcal{U}\subseteq\mathcal{U}_{X}
implies that the identity map induces a continuous isomorphism from
F(X)
onto
F(X,\mathcal{U})
; hence, the topology of
F(X,\mathcal{U})
is coarser than or equal to that of
F(X)
. The parallel results hold for free topological Abelian groups.

The transition to uniform spaces provides a decisive methodological advantage. While analyzing the identity neighborhood filter
\mathcal{N}_{e}(F(X))
is notoriously difficult due to the lack of quantitative control in arbitrary Tychonoff spaces, the entourages and uniform coverings of
\mathcal{U}
offer a precise combinatorial framework. This allows one to orchestrate neighborhood bases systematically via uniform covering trees, bypassing classical topological obstructions
[
21
,
22
]
.

Fineness Index of Posets and
P
-Base

2.1.
Fineness index: definition and basic computation

For a topological space
X
and
x\in X
, we denote by
{\mathcal{N}}_{x}(X)
the neighbourhood filter at
x
, ordered by reverse inclusion.

Let
P
be a directed poset. Following Dow and Feng
[
6
]
, we say that a topological group
G
has a
P
-base if there exists an order-preserving map from
P
to
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)
with cofinal image. Clearly, this is stronger than the condition
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)\leq_{T}P
. Since
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)
is always Dedekind complete, i.e., every bounded set admits a least bound, the two notions coincide, see
[
12
]
.

Among non-metrizable cases, the most extensively studied instance is
P=\omega^{\omega}
; see
[
2
,
8
,
10
,
17
]
. Such groups are said to have a
\mathfrak{G}
-base. One defines uniform spaces with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base similarly. Such spaces were first studied in
[
4
]
, where, among other things, every compact space with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base was shown to be metrizable. For further results on topological spaces, groups, and uniform spaces with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base, we refer the reader to
[
2
]
.

Topological groups with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base were systematically studied in
[
10
]
by Gabriyelyan, Kąkol and Leiderman. Among other results, it was shown that if a topological group admits an
\omega^{\omega}
-base, then its character, namely the cofinality of
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)
, belongs to

\{1,\omega\}\cup[\mathfrak{b},\mathfrak{d}].

This result may seem somewhat “weird”, since
\mathfrak{b}
is not a cardinal invariant arising directly from
\omega^{\omega}
, but rather from
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
, while the additivity of
\omega^{\omega}
is countable. It is therefore natural to look for another intrinsically defined cardinal invariant of posets that can serve as a lower bound for non-metrizable topological groups with a
P
-base. The following notion provides such a tool.

Definition 2.1
.

A directed pre-ordered set
(P,\leq)
is called
\kappa
-fine
if every subset

A\subseteq P
with
|A|\leq\kappa
is dominated by a countable subset of
P
.

When
\operatorname{cof}(P)>\omega
, let
\operatorname{fi}(P)
denote the least cardinal
\lambda
such that
P
is not
\lambda
-fine. This cardinal is called the
fineness index
of
P
.

For completeness, we set
\operatorname{fi}(P)=1
if
\operatorname{cof}(P)=1
, and
\operatorname{fi}(P)=\omega
if
\operatorname{cof}(P)=\omega
.

Remark 2.2
.

The cardinal invariant
\operatorname{fi}(P)
is closely related to
\operatorname{add}(P)
: informally, one obtains
\operatorname{fi}(P)
from the definition of
\operatorname{add}(P)
by replacing “
1
” with “
\omega
” (when
\operatorname{cof}(P)>\omega
). Indeed,
\operatorname{add}(P)
is the least cardinality of a subset of
P
that cannot be dominated by a subset of cardinality
1
.

The notion of
\kappa
-fineness was first introduced for topological groups by He, Tkachenko, Zhang and the second-listed author
[
13
]
: a topological group
G
is called
\kappa
-fine if
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)
is
\kappa
-fine. The invariant
\operatorname{fi}(G)
was then introduced naturally in subsequent work
[
27
]
. The weakest such property, namely
\omega_{1}
-fineness, already deserves special attention; see, for example,
[
19
]
, where
\omega_{1}
-filters are called
A
-filters
.

In these papers,
\kappa
-fineness is shown to be closely related to an important topic in the theory of topological groups, namely
\mathbb{R}
-factorizable topological groups

[
1
, Chap. 8]
.

It is straightforward to see that if
P
and
Q
are Dedekind complete directed posets and
P\leq_{T}Q
, then the
\kappa
-fineness of
Q
implies the
\kappa
-fineness of
P
. The next lemma shows that the assumption of Dedekind completeness can in fact be omitted.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
\kappa
be uncountable, and let
P
and
Q
be directed posets such that
P\leq_{T}Q
. If
Q
is
\kappa
-fine, then so is
P
.

Proof.

Fix a map
\varphi:P\to Q
sending unbounded subsets of
P
to unbounded subsets of
Q
.
Let
A\subseteq P
with
|A|\leq\kappa
. Since
Q
is
\kappa
-fine, the set
\varphi(A)
is dominated by a countable set
\{q_{n}:n\in\omega\}
in
Q
.

For each
n\in\omega
, define

A_{n}=\{a\in A:\varphi(a)\leq q_{n}\}.

Then
\varphi(A_{n})
is bounded in
Q
, and by the choice of
\varphi
, the set
A_{n}
must be bounded in
P
. Thus, for each
n
one can choose
b_{n}\in P
such that
a\leq b_{n}
for all
a\in A_{n}
.

Since
A=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A_{n}
, the countable set
\{b_{n}:n\in\omega\}
dominates
A
. Hence
P
is
\kappa
-fine.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

If
P\geq_{T}Q
and
\operatorname{cof}(Q)>\omega
, then
\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq\operatorname{fi}(Q)
.

It is immediate that if
P
has countable cofinality, then
P
is
\kappa
-fine for every uncountable cardinal
\kappa
. Thus we are mainly interested in pre-ordered sets of uncountable cofinality. The following elementary lemma shows that the essential case is when the additivity is countable.

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
P
be a directed pre-ordered set of uncountable cofinality. Then:

(i)

If
\operatorname{add}(P)>\omega
, then
\operatorname{fi}(P)=\operatorname{add}(P)
;

(ii)

\operatorname{add}(P)\leq\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq\operatorname{cof}(\operatorname{cof}(P))
;

(iii)

\operatorname{fi}(P)
is regular.

Proof.

(i) Assume
\operatorname{add}(P)>\omega
. Then every countable subset of
P
is bounded. Consequently, any subset of
P
dominated by a countable set is in fact bounded, i.e., dominated by a single point of
P
. Therefore
P
is
\kappa
-fine precisely when
\kappa<\operatorname{add}(P)
, so
\operatorname{fi}(P)=\operatorname{add}(P)
.

(ii) By (i), it suffices to consider the case
\operatorname{add}(P)=\omega
. The inequality
\operatorname{add}(P)\leq\operatorname{fi}(P)
is immediate. For the other inequality, note that
\operatorname{cof}(P)\leq_{T}P
, hence by Corollary
2.4
,

\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq\operatorname{fi}(\operatorname{cof}(P))\leq\operatorname{cof}(\operatorname{cof}(P)).

(iii) Suppose toward a contradiction that
\operatorname{fi}(P)
is singular. Let
\kappa<\operatorname{fi}(P)
be cofinal in
\operatorname{fi}(P)
, and let
\{X_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}
be a family of subsets of
P
such that
|X_{\alpha}|<\operatorname{fi}(P)
for each
\alpha
, while

X=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}X_{\alpha}

is not dominated by any countable subset of
P
.

For each
\alpha<\kappa
, since
|X_{\alpha}|<\operatorname{fi}(P)
, there exists a countable set
A_{\alpha}\subseteq P
dominating
X_{\alpha}
. Then

A=\bigcup_{\alpha<\kappa}A_{\alpha}

has cardinality at most
\kappa
, and dominates
X
. Since
\kappa<\operatorname{fi}(P)
and
P
is
\kappa
-fine, the set
A
is dominated by a countable subset of
P
, and hence so is
X
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 2.6
.

\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega})=\mathfrak{b}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
2.5
(i), applied to
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
, we have

\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega})\leq\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})=\mathfrak{b}.

For the reverse inequality, let
\kappa<\mathfrak{b}
, and let

A\subseteq\omega^{\omega}
satisfy
|A|\leq\kappa
.
Then
A
is bounded in
(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})
, so there exists

g\in\omega^{\omega}
such that
f\leq^{*}g
for every
f\in A
.

Now note that
f\leq^{*}g
holds iff there exist
m,n\in\omega
such that

f\leq g\vee c_{m,n},

where
c_{m,n}\in\omega^{\omega}
is defined by

c_{m,n}(k)=m
for
k\leq n
, and
c_{m,n}(k)=0
otherwise.

It follows that every
f\in A
is dominated (with respect to the pointwise order) by some member of the countable set

\{\,g\vee c_{m,n}:m,n\in\omega\,\}.

Thus
A
is dominated by a countable subset of
\omega^{\omega}
, so
\omega^{\omega}
is
\kappa
-fine.

Therefore
\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega})\geq\mathfrak{b}
, and hence

\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega})=\mathfrak{b}
.
∎

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
P
be a directed poset. Then every topological group with a
P
-base satisfies

\chi(G)\in\{1,\omega\}\cup[\operatorname{fi}(P),\operatorname{cof}(P)].

Proof.

If
G
is first-countable, then
\chi(G)=\omega
, and there is nothing to prove. So assume
G
is not first-countable. Then
\chi(G)
is uncountable and

\chi(G)=\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)).

Since
G
has a
P
-base, we have
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)\leq_{T}P
. Hence, by Corollary
2.4
,

\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq\operatorname{fi}({\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)).

Moreover,

\operatorname{fi}({\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G))\leq\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G))\leq\operatorname{cof}(P).

Combining these inequalities yields

\chi(G)=\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G))\in[\operatorname{fi}(P),\operatorname{cof}(P)].

Together with the trivial cases
\chi(G)=1
and
\chi(G)=\omega
, the conclusion follows.
∎

We shall see that an analogous result also holds for the pseudocharacter.
Recall that the pseudocharacter of a topological group
G
is the least cardinal
\tau
such that

\{e\}

is the intersection of
\tau
many open sets; we denote it by
\psi(G)
.

Proposition 2.8
.

Let
P
be a directed poset. Then every topological group with a
P
-base satisfies

\psi(G)\in\{1,\omega\}\cup[\operatorname{fi}(P),\operatorname{cof}(P)].

Proof.

Since
\psi(G)\leq\chi(G)
, it suffices to prove:

If
\psi(G)>\omega
, then
\psi(G)\geq\operatorname{fi}(P)
.

Let
\kappa=\psi(G)
, and suppose

\{e\}=\bigcap_{\alpha<\kappa}U_{\alpha},

where each
U_{\alpha}
is an open neighbourhood of
e
.
Suppose
\kappa<\operatorname{fi}(P)
. Then
P
is
\kappa
-fine, and hence so is
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)
by Lemma
2.3
. Therefore there exists a countable family of neighbourhoods
\{V_{n}:n\in\omega\}
such that each
U_{\alpha}
contains some
V_{n}
. It follows that

\{e\}=\bigcap_{n\in\omega}V_{n},

so
\psi(G)\leq\omega
, a contradiction.
∎

If one defines, for any directed poset
P
,

\operatorname{Spec}_{\chi}(P)=\{\chi(G):\text{$G$ is a non-metrizable topological group with a $P$-base}\},

called the
character spectrum of topological groups with a
P
-base
, then Theorem
2.7
yields

\operatorname{Spec}_{\chi}(P)\subseteq[\operatorname{fi}(P),\operatorname{cof}(P)].

When
P=\omega^{\omega}
, this recovers precisely the result of Gabriyelyan, Kąkol and Leiderman.

They asked whether there exists a topological group with an
\omega^{\omega}
-base whose character is exactly
\mathfrak{b}
. This was soon answered positively by Leiderman, Pestov, and Tomita, who proved the following stronger result.

Proposition 2.9
.

[
17
, Proposition 2.6]

If
\kappa
is a regular cardinal Tukey below
\omega^{\omega}
, then there exists a topological group
G
such that

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)=_{T}\kappa.

Since

\mathfrak{b}\leq_{T}(\omega^{\omega},\leq^{*})\leq_{T}\omega^{\omega}

(see Example
1.1
(ii)), this immediately yields a positive answer.

It is therefore natural to ask whether

Question 2.10
.

Let
P
be a directed poset. Do we have
\operatorname{fi}(P)\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\chi}(P)
? Or equivalently, is
\operatorname{fi}(P)
the least cardinal that can occur as the character of a topological group with a
P
-base?

We now give some discussion of this question.

We begin with a simple observation on constructing topological groups with prescribed cofinal type (together with some information about pseudocharacter).

Proposition 2.11
.

Let
P
be a directed poset. Then there exists a Boolean group
G
with a local base
\mathcal{B}
at
e
consisting of subgroups and

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)=_{T}P,\qquad\psi(G)=\operatorname{add}(P).

Proof.

If
P=_{T}1
, take
G
discrete. So assume
P\neq_{T}1
.

Let

G=([P]^{<\omega},\triangle),

where
\triangle
denotes symmetric difference. For each
p\in P
, let

\uparrow p=\{q\in P:q\geq p\},\qquad G_{p}=[\uparrow p]^{<\omega}.

It is straightforward that
\{G_{p}:p\in P\}
forms a neighbourhood base at the identity for a group topology on
G
, and

p\mapsto G_{p}

is an order embedding with cofinal image. Hence

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(G)=_{T}P.

Moreover,

\bigcap_{p\in P}G_{p}=\{\emptyset\},

so the topology is Hausdorff.

It remains to check that
\chi(G)=\operatorname{add}(P)
.
Assume first that
A
is a subset of
G
with
|A|<\operatorname{add}(P)
and take

x\in\bigcap_{a\in A}G_{a},

then
x\subseteq\uparrow a
for every
a\in A
. Since
A
is unbounded, this forces
x=\emptyset
. Thus

\psi(G)\leq\operatorname{add}(P).

Conversely, intersecting fewer than
\operatorname{add}(P)
many sets of the form
G_{p}
, their indices are bounded in
P
, so the intersection contains some nontrivial
G_{q}
. Hence it cannot equal
\{\emptyset\}
, and therefore

\psi(G)\geq\operatorname{add}(P).~~~\qed

By the proposition uncountable cardinal
\kappa
belongs to
\operatorname{Spec}_{\chi}(P)
iff

\kappa=\operatorname{cof}(Q)

for some
Q\leq_{T}P
.
Since

\kappa=\operatorname{cof}(Q)\leq_{T}Q,

this implies
\kappa\leq_{T}P
. Thus, using
\kappa=_{T}\operatorname{cof}(\kappa)
, one obtains that

\kappa\in\operatorname{Spec}_{\chi}(P)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\operatorname{cof}(\kappa)\leq_{T}P.

Since
\operatorname{fi}(P)
is regular, Question
2.10
is equivalent to the following.

Question 2.12
.

Do we have
\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq_{T}P
?

2.2.
The Countable Power

In this subsection, we study
\kappa
-fineness of the countable power of a poset under the coordinatewise order.

We first present a result closely related to Question
2.12
.

Proposition 2.13
.

For every directed poset
P
, one has

\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq_{T}P^{\omega}.

Proof.

If
\operatorname{add}(P)>\omega
, then by Lemma
2.5
,

\operatorname{fi}(P)=\operatorname{add}(P)\leq_{T}P=_{T}P^{\omega},

and we are done.

Suppose now that
\operatorname{add}(P)=\omega
. Let

\kappa=\operatorname{fi}(P),

and fix a family
\{p_{\alpha}:\alpha<\kappa\}\subseteq P
which is not dominated by any countable subset of
P
.

For each
f\in P^{\omega}
, let
\varphi(f)
be the least ordinal
\alpha<\kappa
such that
p_{\alpha}
is not dominated by

G_{f}=\{f(n):n\in\omega\}.

We claim that
\varphi:P^{\omega}\to\kappa
is a cofinal map.

First,
\varphi
is order-preserving. Indeed, if
f\leq g
in
P^{\omega}
, then
G_{g}
dominates
G_{f}
, so any initial segment dominated by
G_{f}
is also dominated by
G_{g}
. Hence

\varphi(f)\leq\varphi(g).

Next,
\varphi
has cofinal image. Fix
\alpha<\kappa
. Since
P
is
|\alpha|
-fine (because
|\alpha|<\operatorname{fi}(P)
), there exists a countable set
\{a_{n}:n\in\omega\}
dominating

\{p_{\beta}:\beta<\alpha\}.

Define
f\in P^{\omega}
by
f(n)=a_{n}
. Then
G_{f}
dominates every
p_{\beta}
for
\beta<\alpha
, so the first element not dominated by
G_{f}
must occur at some stage at least
\alpha
, i.e.

\varphi(f)\geq\alpha.

Thus the image of
\varphi
is cofinal in
\kappa
.

Therefore
\kappa\leq_{T}P^{\omega}
, i.e.

\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq_{T}P^{\omega}.~~~\qed

Now we have a partial positive answer to Question
2.12
:

If

P^{\omega}=_{T}P

—for example, if

—then

\operatorname{fi}(P)\leq_{T}P.

Proposition 2.14
.

For every directed poset
P
, one has

(i)

\operatorname{fi}(P^{\omega})=\operatorname{fi}(P)
, if
\operatorname{add}(P)>\omega
;

(ii)

\operatorname{fi}(P^{\omega})=\min\{\mathfrak{b},\operatorname{fi}(P)\}
, if
\omega=\operatorname{add}(P)<\operatorname{cof}(P)
;

(iii)

\operatorname{fi}(P^{\omega})=\mathfrak{b}
if
\operatorname{cof}(P)=\omega
.

Proof.

(i) follows immediately from the preceding lemma.

For (ii) and (iii), since
\omega^{\omega}\leq_{T}P^{\omega}
and
P\leq_{T}P^{\omega}
, Lemma
2.3
yields

\displaystyle\operatorname{fi}(P^{\omega})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{fi}(\omega^{\omega})=\mathfrak{b},

\displaystyle\operatorname{fi}(P^{\omega})

\displaystyle\leq\operatorname{fi}(P),\qquad\text{if }\operatorname{cof}(P)>\omega.

To prove the reverse inequality, let
\kappa<\min\{\mathfrak{b},\operatorname{fi}(P)\}
in (ii) and respectively
\kappa<\mathfrak{b}
in (iii), and let

A\subseteq P^{\omega}
have cardinality at most
\kappa
.
For each
n\in\omega
, let

A_{n}=\{x(n):x\in A\}\subseteq P.

Then
|A_{n}|\leq\kappa
, so
A_{n}
is dominated by a countable subset
B_{n}\subseteq P
, because
P
is
\kappa
-fine in each case of (ii) and (iii).
Without loss of generality, we may assume each

\omega

Then

dominates

is order-isomorphic to
\omega^{\omega}
.

For each
x\in A
, choose

with

Then

, and
B^{\prime}
dominates

Since

and
\omega^{\omega}
is
\kappa
-fine, the set

is dominated in

Thus

is
\kappa
-fine for every
\kappa<\min\{\mathfrak{b},\operatorname{fi}(P)\}

∎

3.
Cofinal Type of Free Topological (Abelian) Groups

If
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is a uniform space, then
{\mathcal{U}}
, viewed as a subset of the power set of
X\times X
, can be ordered by reverse inclusion, i.e.,
U\leq V
if and only if
U\supseteq V
. With this order,
{\mathcal{U}}
becomes a directed poset.

The set of all uniformly continuous pseudometrics on
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
bounded by
1
is denoted by
\mathcal{P}(X,{\mathcal{U}})
, and is ordered by
d_{1}\leq d_{2}
if
d_{1}(x,y)\leq d_{2}(x,y)
for all
x,y\in X
.

The following description of the local Tukey structure of free Abelian topological groups was made explicit by Gartside in
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
. It is worth noting, however, that this result had essentially already been proved much earlier by Nickolas and Tkachenko in
[
21
]
. Their original goal was to compute the character of free Abelian topological groups, but their argument in fact establishes the stronger Tukey equivalence below. This was only explicitly observed later by Gartside in 2021.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
11
,
21
]

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be a uniform space. Then

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}({\mathcal{U}}^{\omega},\leq)=_{T}\mathcal{P}(X,{\mathcal{U}}).

Combining this with Proposition
2.14
, we obtain the following.

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be a uniform space. Then

•

\operatorname{fi}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=\operatorname{fi}({\mathcal{U}})
, if
\operatorname{add}({\mathcal{U}})>\omega
;

•

\operatorname{fi}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=\min\{\mathfrak{b},\operatorname{fi}({\mathcal{U}})\}
, if
\operatorname{add}({\mathcal{U}})=\omega<\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}})
;

•

\operatorname{fi}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=\mathfrak{b}
, if
\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}})=\omega
.

In Section 3 of
[
21
]
, Nickolas and Tkachenko computed the character of the free topological group
F(X,{\mathcal{U}})
over an
\omega
-narrow uniform space, proving that

\chi(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=\chi(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}).

This result was later extended by Chis, Ferrer, Hernández and Tsaban
[
5
]
.

However, a careful inspection shows that their argument establishes an equality of characters, but not explicitly the stronger statement of Tukey equivalence. Before proving the above theorem, they first analyzed the case where
{\mathcal{U}}
has a countable base. In fact,
[
21
, Lemma 3.9]
proves:

If
\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}})\leq\omega
for an
\omega
-narrow uniform space
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
, then

\chi(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))\leq\mathfrak{d}.

Moreover, one easily checks that equality holds whenever
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is non-discrete, equivalently, whenever
\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}})\neq 1
. As in the Abelian case, their proof actually yields the stronger statement:

Theorem 3.3

(Nickolas–Tkachenko)
.

If
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is an
\omega
-narrow uniform space with
{\mathcal{U}}=_{T}\omega
, then

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}\omega^{\omega}.

This naturally leads to the question whether their cofinality formula can be strengthened to a Tukey equivalence.

Question 3.4
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be an
\omega
-narrow uniform space. Must

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}?

Since compact spaces are naturally
\omega
-narrow, our next goal is to give a positive answer to this question when
X
is compact, by modifying the proof of Nickolas and Tkachenko. Their remarkable argument relies on delicate combinatorial machinery, so we first recall some of the necessary tools.

We begin with some basic notions from combinatorial set theory. Let
A
be a nonempty set and let
n\in\omega\setminus\{0\}
. We denote by
A^{n}
the set of sequences

s=(s(0),s(1),\dots,s(n-1))

of length
n
with values in
A
, viewed as functions from
n
to
A
.

For
n=0
, we let
A^{0}=\{\emptyset\}
, the singleton consisting of the
empty sequence
. The length of a finite sequence
s
is denoted by
\operatorname{lh}(s)
, so in particular
\operatorname{lh}(\emptyset)=0
.

If
s\in A^{n}
and
m\leq n
, we write

s\restriction m=(s(0),s(1),\dots,s(m-1))

(hence
s\restriction 0=\emptyset
).
We say that
t
is an
initial segment
of
s
, and that
s
is an
extension
of
t
, if
t=s\restriction m
for some
m\leq\operatorname{lh}(s)
.

If
s\in A^{n}
and
a\in A
, we denote by

s^{\frown}a=(s(0),s(1),\dots,s(n-1),a)\in A^{n+1}

the concatenation of
s
with
a
. In particular,
\emptyset^{\frown}a=(a)\in A^{1}
.

As usual, we denote by
A^{<\omega}
the set of all finite sequences from
A
, i.e.,

A^{<\omega}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega}A^{n}.

A
tree
on
A
is a subset
T\subseteq A^{<\omega}
closed under taking initial segments.

Definition 3.5
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be a uniform space, and denote by
\mathcal{T}
the family of all nonempty open subsets of
X
. A
uniform covering tree
is a tree
T
on
\mathcal{T}
such that for every
s\in T
, the set

\{A\in\mathcal{T}:s^{\frown}A\in T\}

is a uniform cover of
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
.

If
T
is a uniform covering tree, then for each
t\in T
, we set

C_{T}(t)=\{A\in\mathcal{T}:t^{\frown}A\in T\}.

We denote by
\mathbf{UT}(X,{\mathcal{U}})
(or simply
\mathbf{UT}(X)
) the set of all uniform covering trees. For each nonzero
n\in\omega
and
s\in\mathcal{T}^{n}
, define a subset
W_{s}
of
F(X,{\mathcal{U}})
by

W_{s}:=\bigcup_{\varepsilon\in\{-1,1\}^{n}}s(0)^{-\varepsilon(0)}\cdots s(n-1)^{-\varepsilon(n-1)}s(n-1)^{\varepsilon(n-1)}\cdots s(0)^{\varepsilon(0)}.

For
T\in\mathbf{UT}(X)
, set
W_{T}=\bigcup_{s\in T}W_{s}
.

Finally, for a sequence
P=(P_{0},P_{1},\dots)\in\mathbf{UT}(X)^{\omega}
, define

O_{P}=\bigcup_{n\in\omega\setminus\{0\}}\ \bigcup_{\pi\in\operatorname{sym}(n)}W_{P_{\pi(0)}}\cdots W_{P_{\pi(n-1)}}.

Theorem 3.6
.

[
21
, Theorem 3.6]

The family
\Sigma:=\{O_{P}:P\in\mathbf{UT}(X,{\mathcal{U}})^{\omega}\}
forms a local base at
e
of the topological group
F(X,{\mathcal{U}})
.

We are now ready to give the final result.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
\tau
be an infinite cardinal. A uniform space
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is called
\tau
-precompact
if for every
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
there exists a subset
A\subseteq X
with
|A|<\tau
such that

X=\bigcup_{a\in A}B(a,U).

By definition, precompactness is the same as
\omega
-precompactness, while
\omega
-narrowness is equivalent to
\omega_{1}
-precompactness.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be a non-discrete uniform space. If
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is
\operatorname{add}({\mathcal{U}})
-precompact, then

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}.

Proof.

Let
\tau=\operatorname{add}(\mathcal{U})
. For each
U\in\mathcal{U}
, choose a subset
|Y_{U}|<\tau
such that

X=\bigcup_{y\in Y_{U}}B(y,U).

Define

\gamma_{U}=\{B(y,2U):y\in Y_{U}\}.

Then every
U
-ball is contained in some member of
\gamma_{U}
, hence
\gamma_{U}
is a uniform cover.

Let

\Gamma=\{\gamma_{U}:U\in\mathcal{U}\},

ordered by
\preceq
, where
\gamma_{U}\preceq\gamma_{V}
if either
U=V
or
2V\subseteq U
.

We claim that if
\gamma_{U}\preceq\gamma_{V}
, then
\gamma_{V}
refines
\gamma_{U}
.
Indeed, in the non-trivial case
U\neq V
, let
y\in Y_{V}
. Then there exists
z\in Y_{U}
such that
y\in B(z,U)
, and hence

B(y,2V)\subseteq B(y,U)\subseteq B(z,2U).

Thus every member of
\gamma_{V}
is contained in some member of
\gamma_{U}
, as required.

Claim 1
.

\Gamma=_{T}\mathcal{U}
.

Proof of Claim
1
.

Consider the map
\Gamma\to\mathcal{U}
given by
\gamma_{U}\mapsto U
.
This map is order-preserving and surjective, hence
\Gamma\geq_{T}\mathcal{U}
.

Conversely, let
\mathcal{V}\subseteq\mathcal{U}
such that
\{\gamma_{V}:V\in\mathcal{V}\}
is unbounded in
\Gamma
.
If
\mathcal{V}
were bounded in
\mathcal{U}
, then there would exist
W\in\mathcal{U}
such that
2W\subseteq V
for all
V\in\mathcal{V}
,
which implies
\gamma_{V}\preceq\gamma_{W}
for all
V\in\mathcal{V}
, contradicting the unboundedness.
Thus
\mathcal{V}
is unbounded in
\mathcal{U}
, and so
\Gamma\leq_{T}\mathcal{U}
.
∎

Call a uniform covering tree
T

neat
if for every
s,t\in T
of the same length,

C_{T}(s)=C_{T}(t)\in\Gamma

(see Definition
3.5
).
Let
\mathbf{NT}
denote the collection of neat trees.

For each neat tree
T
, there is a uniquely determined map

f_{T}:\omega\to\Gamma

defined by letting
f_{T}(n)=C_{T}(s)
for any
s\in T
with
\operatorname{lh}(s)=n
.
This correspondence is a bijection, and therefore
\mathbf{NT}
inherits the natural product order from
(\Gamma,\preceq)^{\omega}
.

Claim 2
.

The family

\Sigma^{*}:=\{O_{P}:P\in\mathbf{NT}^{\omega}\}

is a local base at
e
in
F(X,{\mathcal{U}})
.

Proof of Claim
2
.

By the definition of
O_{P}
, it suffices to show that for every

T\in\mathbf{UT}(X),

there exists
S\in\mathbf{NT}
such that

W_{S}\subseteq W_{T}.

Fix
T\in\mathbf{UT}(X)
. Since
|\gamma_{U}|<\tau
for each
U\in{\mathcal{U}}
, for every
n\in\omega
the set of elements of
T
of length
n
has cardinality
<\tau
.
Since

\operatorname{add}(\Gamma)=\operatorname{add}({\mathcal{U}})=\tau,

there exists
V_{n}\in{\mathcal{U}}
such that

C_{T}(s)\preceq\gamma_{V_{n}}

for every
s\in T
with
\operatorname{lh}(s)=n
.

Define a neat tree
S
by setting

f_{S}(n)=\gamma_{V_{n}}

for all
n\in\omega
.
We claim that
W_{S}\subseteq W_{T}
.

Indeed, for any

s=(A_{0},\dots,A_{n})\in S,\qquad A_{i}\in\gamma_{V_{i}},

one finds inductively an element

t=(B_{0},\dots,B_{n})\in T

such that
B_{i}\supseteq A_{i}

for all
i\leq n
.
Hence

W_{s}\subseteq W_{t},

which yields

W_{S}\subseteq W_{T}.

∎

Now suppose
T,S\in\mathbf{NT}
satisfy
T\leq S
. For any
s\in S
, one can choose
t\in T
of the same length such that

s(i)\subseteq t(i)

for each coordinate.
Hence

W_{T}\supseteq W_{S}.

Consequently, if
P_{1}\leq P_{2}
in
\mathbf{NT}^{\omega}
, then

O_{P_{1}}\supseteq O_{P_{2}}.

Thus the map

P\mapsto O_{P}

is order-preserving from
\mathbf{NT}^{\omega}
into
{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))
, and by Claim
2
its image is cofinal. Therefore,

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))\leq_{T}\mathbf{NT}^{\omega}=_{T}\Gamma^{\omega}=_{T}{\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}.

For the reverse Tukey reduction, note that
A(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is a quotient group of
F(X,{\mathcal{U}})
. Hence by Lemma
3.1
,

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))\geq_{T}{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}.

This completes the proof.
∎

]

.

Corollary 3.13
.

Assume
SSH
. Suppose that
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is an infinite precompact uniform space of weight
\tau
. If
\tau\geq\mathfrak{d}
and
\operatorname{cof}(\tau)>\omega
, then

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}[\tau]^{<\omega}.

Proof.

According to Lemma
3.9
, every precompact uniform space
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
satisfies

{\mathcal{U}}=_{T}[\tau]^{<\omega}.

Hence the theorem yields

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}.

It remains to observe that, by Lemma
3.11
, if
\tau\geq\mathfrak{d}
and

\operatorname{cof}(\tau)>\omega

then

\operatorname{cof}\big(([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\big)=\tau.

Therefore, by (v) of Example
1.1
,

([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\leq_{T}[\tau]^{<\omega},

while the reverse Tukey reduction is trivial. Hence

([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}=_{T}[\tau]^{<\omega}.~~~\qed

Recall that a uniform space
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is called a
uniform
P
-space
if the intersection of entourages in
{\mathcal{U}}
belongs to
{\mathcal{U}}
. This is equivalent to that
\operatorname{add}({\mathcal{U}})>\omega
.
By (vi) of Example
1.1
we have

Corollary 3.14
.

If
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is a
\omega
-narrow uniform
P
-space, then

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(A(X,{\mathcal{U}}))=_{T}{\mathcal{U}}

Remark 3.15
.

We note that in their second paper
[
22
]
, Nickolas and Tkachenko proved that for every infinite compact space
X
of weight
\tau
,

\chi(F(X))=\chi(A(X))=\mathfrak{d}\cdot\operatorname{cof}([\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}).

One might ask whether this cardinal equivalence can be lifted to the Tukey equivalence

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X))=_{T}\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}.

We shall see that this can never happen when
\tau
is uncountable.

By Corollary
3.10
it suffices to show that

([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\nleq_{T}\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}.

Claim 3
.

Every unbounded subset of
([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}
contains a countable unbounded subset.

Proof of Claim
3
.

Let
A\subseteq([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}
be unbounded. Then for some
n\in\omega
, the set

A(n)=\{a(n):a\in A\}

is unbounded in
[\tau]^{<\omega}
. Since every infinite subset of
[\tau]^{<\omega}
contains a countable unbounded subset, one can choose a countable
B\subseteq A
such that

B(n)=\{b(n):b\in B\}

remains unbounded. Hence
B
is unbounded in
([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}
.
∎

Now let

\varphi=(\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2}):([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\to\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}

be any map.
Since

([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\nleq_{T}\omega^{\omega},

the map
\varphi_{1}
does not send unbounded sets to unbounded sets. Thus there exists an unbounded set
A
such that
\varphi_{1}(A)
is bounded in
\omega^{\omega}
. By Claim
3
, passing to a subset if necessary, we may assume
A
is countable.

Since
\operatorname{add}([\tau]^{<\omega_{1}})=\omega_{1}
, the countable set
\varphi_{2}(A)
is bounded in
[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}
. Hence
\varphi(A)
is bounded in

\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}},

showing that
\varphi
cannot be a Tukey map. Therefore

([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}\nleq_{T}\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}},

and so

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X))\neq_{T}\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}.

On the other hand, the reverse reduction

\omega^{\omega}\times[\tau]^{<\omega_{1}}\leq_{T}([\tau]^{<\omega})^{\omega}

always holds. We leave this to the reader.

4.
Final Remarks and Open Problems

The main Question
3.4
remains open in general. Note that in
[
21
, Theorem 3.10]
, before proving their final theorem, Nickolas and Tkachenko established the estimate

\chi(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))\leq\mathfrak{d}\cdot\operatorname{cof}({\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}).

This suggests the following question.

Question 4.1
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be an
\omega
-narrow uniform space. Does one have

{\mathcal{N}}_{e}(F(X,{\mathcal{U}}))\leq_{T}\omega^{\omega}\times{\mathcal{U}}^{\omega}?

A positive answer might provide a first step toward a possible positive solution of Question
3.4
.

The following lemma, which yields Example
1.1
(v), is folklore; we include a proof for the reader’s convenience.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
P
be a directed preordered set, and let
\tau,\kappa
be infinite cardinals. If

\tau\leq\operatorname{add}(P)\leq\operatorname{cof}(P)\leq\kappa,

then

P\leq_{T}[\kappa]^{<\tau}.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, assume
|P|=\operatorname{cof}(P)\leq\kappa
, and fix an injection
\varphi:P\to\kappa
. Define

\Phi:P\to[\kappa]^{<\tau},\qquad x\mapsto\{\varphi(x)\}.

We claim that
\Phi
sends unbounded subsets of
P
to unbounded subsets of
[\kappa]^{<\tau}
. Indeed, if
A\subseteq P
is unbounded, then
|A|\geq\operatorname{add}(P)\geq\tau
, so
\Phi(A)
is unbounded in
[\kappa]^{<\tau}
. Thus
P\leq_{T}[\kappa]^{<\tau}
.
∎

As mentioned in the proof of Corollary
3.13
, if a compact uniform space
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
has weight
\kappa
, then

{\mathcal{U}}=_{T}[\kappa]^{<\omega}.

This motivates the following question.

Question 4.3
.

Let
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
be a
\tau
-precompact uniform space of weight
\kappa\geq\tau
. Must one have

{\mathcal{U}}\geq_{T}[\kappa]^{<\tau}?

What can be said in the case
\kappa=\omega_{1}
, that is, when
(X,{\mathcal{U}})
is
\omega
-narrow?

Now assume that
P
is a directed poset such that

\omega=\operatorname{add}(P)<\operatorname{cof}(P).

Proposition
2.14
shows that
\mathfrak{b}
is an upper bound for
\operatorname{fi}(P)
whenever
P
is of the form
Q^{\omega}
. This leads naturally to the following question.

Question 4.4
.

Is there a common upper bound for
\operatorname{fi}(P)
, as
P
ranges over all directed posets satisfying

\omega=\operatorname{add}(P)<\operatorname{cof}(P)?

We note, by comparison, that the gap between
\operatorname{add}(P)
and
\operatorname{cof}(P)
can be arbitrarily large; for instance,

[\kappa]^{<\omega}

provides such an example.
