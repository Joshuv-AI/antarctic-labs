Search for soft unclustered energy patterns containing muons in the final state in p ​ p pp collisions at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV with the ATLAS detector


Abstract
Soft unclustered energy patterns (SUEPs) refer to high-multiplicity, isotropic distributions of low-momentum particles that could arise in strongly-coupled hidden sector theories. A search for SUEPs whose decay products contain muons in the final state is presented using 140 ​ fb − 1 140\penalty\ \mathrm{fb}^{-1} of proton–proton collision data at s = 13 \sqrt{s}=13 TeV collected by the ATLAS detector at the Large Hadron Collider during 2015–2018. The analysis targets SUEP signatures via gluon–gluon fusion production of a scalar mediator in Hidden Valley scenarios. No significant excess over the Standard Model expectation is observed. Exclusion limits are set on the product of the mediator production cross section and the branching fraction for its decay into a SUEP, down to 0.05 fb for a mediator mass of m S = 750 m_{S}=750 GeV, 0.4 fb for m S = 400 m_{S}=400 GeV, and 70 fb for m S = 125 m_{S}=125 GeV; the last value, when identifying the mediator with the Standard Model Higgs boson, translates to an upper limit on the branching fraction of around 0.2%.

Introduction

Hidden Valley (HV) models
[
1
,
2
]
provide a well-motivated extension of the Standard Model (SM) in which a hidden sector, neutral under SM gauge interactions, is connected to the SM visible sector through weak, so-called
portal
interactions.
Such scenarios arise naturally in solutions to the hierarchy problem, which often introduce additional sectors with suppressed couplings to the SM
[
3
]
.
Strongly interacting hidden sectors are further motivated by the possibility that dark matter is composed of bound states of a QCD-like hidden force
[
4
,
5
]
, or that it resides in a dark sector with sizable self-interactions,
as suggested by small-scale structure anomalies in galaxies and their clusters
[
6
,
7
,
8
]
.

At the Large Hadron Collider (LHC), a range of searches has targeted these scenarios in regimes where the hidden sector undergoes a shower analogous to a QCD parton shower, giving rise to jet-like signatures once the hidden-sector particles decay to visible SM particles.
These include searches for so-called semi-visible jets
[
9
,
10
,
11
]
and emerging jets
[
12
,
13
]
, which exploit the resulting exotic jet topologies to distinguish such events from SM backgrounds.

However, if the hidden-sector force remains strongly coupled well above its hadronization scale, corresponding to a quasi-conformal coupling regime, its radiation pattern can differ qualitatively from that of QCD.
In particular, emissions need not be dominated by soft and collinear splittings, and the resulting shower may instead populate phase space more uniformly
[
14
,
15
,
16
,
17
]
.
For models in which the available energy is distributed among many particles, this results in a characteristic collider signature known as a
Soft Unclustered Energy Pattern
(SUEP),
consisting of a high multiplicity of low-momentum particles, distributed approximately isotropically in the center-of-mass frame
[
14
,
18
]
.
Figure
1
shows a representative signal diagram. The absence of hard or collimated objects makes such signatures challenging both to trigger on and to reconstruct.
Exploring such unconventional and experimentally challenging signatures is essential to ensure sensitivity to a broad range of possible extensions of the SM.

Figure 1
:

S
, followed by showering in the dark sector and the production of a high-multiplicity final state of light dark (pseudo-)scalars (
\phi
).
The
\phi
particles subsequently decay promptly to SM particles through their coupling to dark photons, which mix kinetically with the SM photon.

[

]
.

Both the existing searches for SUEP signatures performed by the CMS Collaboration
[
19
,
20
]

and the analysis presented here are based on the theoretical framework proposed in Ref.
[
18
]
.
In this framework, the hidden sector is accessed through a scalar portal, which may be the SM Higgs boson or a heavier scalar particle.
Within the dark sector, particles undergo showering and subsequently hadronize into a large number of dark mesons
\phi
, which are assumed for simplicity to consist of a single particle species.
A wide range of values for the dark-meson mass
m_{\phi}
is theoretically motivated.
Generally, values of order 1–10 GeV are well suited for SUEP scenarios,
as they allow for a rich set of decay channels to SM particles while still producing
the high-multiplicity, soft final states characteristic of this signature, which are not efficiently targeted by conventional searches.
The momenta of the dark mesons are expected to follow an approximately thermal distribution
[
21
]

and are modeled using a Maxwell–Boltzmann spectrum parameterized by a Hagedorn temperature
T

[
22
]
,
which sets the characteristic momentum scale of the hadronizing hidden sector and is expected to be of the same order as
m_{\phi}
.
The dark mesons are assumed to decay promptly into pairs of dark photons (
A^{\prime}
),
which subsequently decay promptly into charged SM particles via kinetic mixing with the SM photon
[
23
]
.
While other decay modes of dark mesons to SM particles are possible
[
24
]
,
this choice provides a convenient and realistic framework to realize a wide range of final-state particle compositions.
For a given dark-photon mass, the branching fractions to SM particles are fixed, leading to specific mixtures of electrons, muons, and hadrons in the final state.
For dark-photon masses of order 1 GeV, the dominant decay modes are
A^{\prime}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-}
,
A^{\prime}\rightarrow\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
, and
A^{\prime}\rightarrow\pi^{+}\pi^{-}
.

A previous search for SUEP signatures by the CMS Collaboration
[
19
]
targeted gluon–gluon fusion production of the mediator and
relied on high-transverse-momentum initial-state radiation to trigger events via large energy deposits in the calorimeters.
While this approach provided sensitivity over a broad range of
T
and
m_{\phi}
, it was primarily sensitive to scenarios with large mediator masses.
Subsequent searches targeting associated production of the Higgs boson with a vector boson
[
20
]
complemented this result.
However, the requirement of very high particle multiplicities to suppress background limited the sensitivity to scenarios with low
m_{\phi}
and
T
.

The search presented here complements these approaches by targeting final states with a high multiplicity of prompt muons, which are expected for a broad range of dark-photon masses
m_{A^{\prime}}
.
This strategy provides strong background rejection while maintaining sensitivity to scenarios with moderate particle multiplicities,
thereby extending the coverage of the SUEP parameter space well beyond that of previous searches.

Events are selected using multi-muon triggers and target prompt final states with a high multiplicity of muons.
They are further characterized by relatively low average muon transverse momentum, large charged-particle track multiplicity associated with the primary interaction,
and isotropic spatial distribution of the muons.
The signal region is defined to be sensitive to a broad range of charged-particle multiplicities and to preferentially select events in which muons
are distributed more isotropically than in the dominant background from QCD multijet production,
where muons are produced mainly in heavy-flavor decays, and which is estimated using a data-driven method.

ATLAS detector

The ATLAS detector
[
25
]
at the LHC covers nearly the entire solid angle around the
collision point.
1
1

1

ATLAS uses a right-handed coordinate system with its origin at the nominal interaction point (IP)
in the centre of the detector and the
z
-axis along the beam pipe. The
x
-axis points from the
IP to the centre of the LHC ring, and the
y
-axis points upwards. Polar coordinates
(r,\phi)

are used in the transverse plane,
\phi
being the azimuthal angle around the
z
-axis. The
pseudorapidity is defined in terms of the polar angle
\theta
as
\eta=-\ln\tan(\theta/2)

and is equal to the rapidity

y=\frac{1}{2}\ln\left(\frac{E+p_{z}}{E-p_{z}}\right)

in the relativistic limit. Angular distance is measured in units of

\Delta R\equiv\sqrt{(\Delta y)^{2}+(\Delta\phi)^{2}}
.
It consists of an inner tracking detector surrounded
by a thin superconducting solenoid, electromagnetic and hadronic calorimeters, and a muon
spectrometer incorporating three large superconducting air-core toroidal magnets.

The inner-detector system (ID) is immersed in a
2\text{\,}\mathrm{T}
axial magnetic field and provides
charged-particle tracking in the range
|\eta|<2.5
. The high-granularity silicon pixel detector
covers the vertex region and typically provides four measurements per track, the first hit generally
being in the insertable B-layer (IBL) installed before Run 2
[
26
,
27
]
. It is
followed by the SemiConductor Tracker (SCT), which usually provides eight measurements per track.
These silicon detectors are complemented by the transition radiation tracker (TRT), which enables
radially extended track reconstruction up to
|\eta|=2.0
. The TRT also provides electron
identification information based on the fraction of hits (typically 30 in total) above a higher
energy-deposit threshold corresponding to transition radiation.

The calorimeter system covers the pseudorapidity range
|\eta|<4.9
. Within the region

|\eta|<3.2
, electromagnetic calorimetry is provided by barrel and endcap high-granularity
lead/liquid-argon (LAr) calorimeters, with an additional thin LAr presampler covering

|\eta|<1.8
to correct for energy loss in material upstream of the calorimeters. Hadronic
calorimetry is provided by the steel/scintillator-tile calorimeter, segmented into three barrel
structures within
|\eta|<1.7
, and two copper/LAr hadronic endcap calorimeters. The solid angle
coverage is completed with forward copper/LAr and tungsten/LAr calorimeter modules optimised for
electromagnetic and hadronic energy measurements, respectively.

The muon spectrometer (MS) comprises separate trigger and high-precision tracking chambers measuring
the deflection of muons in a magnetic field generated by the superconducting air-core toroidal
magnets. The field integral of the toroids ranges between
2.0
and
6.0\text{\,}\mathrm{T}\text{\,}\mathrm{m}

across most of the detector. Three layers of precision chambers, each consisting of layers of
monitored drift tubes, cover the region
|\eta|<2.7
, complemented by cathode-strip chambers in
the forward region, where the background is highest. The muon trigger system covers the range

|\eta|<2.4
with resistive-plate chambers in the barrel, and thin-gap chambers in the endcap
regions.

The luminosity is measured mainly by the LUCID–2
[
28
]
detector that records Cherenkov light
produced in the quartz windows of photomultipliers located close to the beampipe.

Events were selected by the first-level trigger system implemented in custom hardware, followed by
selections made by algorithms implemented in software in the high-level trigger
[
29
]
.
The first-level trigger accepted events from the
40\text{\,}\mathrm{MHz}
bunch crossings at a rate close to

100\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
, which the high-level trigger further reduced in order to record complete events to
disk at about
1.25\text{\,}\mathrm{kHz}
.

A software suite
[
30
]
is used in data simulation, in the reconstruction and analysis
of real and simulated data, in detector operations, and in the trigger and data acquisition systems
of the experiment.

Data and simulated event samples

The analysis uses
140\penalty\ \text{fb}^{-1}
of proton–proton (

) collision data collected at
\sqrt{s}=13\penalty\ \text{TeV}

Only events recorded under stable-beam conditions with all detector subsystems operating normally are included, and standard data-quality requirements are applied to ensure reliable reconstruction of physics objects
[

The average number of simultaneous

\langle\mu\rangle

The search targets signal models in which a scalar mediator

following the framework described in Section
1

The mediator is taken to be either the SM Higgs boson with mass
m_{S}=125
GeV, or a heavier scalar particle with
m_{S}=400
GeV or
m_{S}=750
GeV.
The inclusion of heavier mediators allows the exploration of scenarios in which a larger total energy is injected into the hidden sector.
The dark-meson mass is set to the benchmark values
m_{\phi}=1.5

3

5

Consistent with the expectation that

T=m_{\phi}

Representative benchmark scenarios for

A dark-photon mass of

To probe the dependence on the final-state composition, two alternative benchmarks are considered.
An enhanced-muon scenario with

B(A^{\prime}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-},\mu^{+}\mu^{-},\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=(0.5,\,0.5,\,0.0)

In addition, a reduced-muon scenario with
m_{A^{\prime}}=0.7
GeV and

B(A^{\prime}\rightarrow e^{+}e^{-},\mu^{+}\mu^{-},\pi^{+}\pi^{-})=(0.15,\,0.15,\,0.7)

4
Event reconstruction

Events are selected using the multi-muon trigger with the lowest
p_{\mathrm{T}}
threshold that collected the full integrated luminosity in each data-taking period.
This corresponds to a trigger requiring at least three muons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>6
GeV in 2015 and 2016, and at least four muons with
p_{\mathrm{T}}>4
GeV in 2017 and 2018.
Simulated signal samples are corrected to reproduce the trigger efficiency expected in data.
A scale factor is applied to the simulated signal events, defined as the ratio of the trigger efficiency expected in data to that in simulation.
Following Ref.
[
66
]
, the expected multi-muon trigger efficiency is estimated from the corresponding single-muon trigger efficiencies
with identical transverse momentum thresholds, assuming that the efficiencies for individual muons are uncorrelated.

The single-muon efficiencies are measured in data and in the
Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
MC sample for muons passing the offline selection requirements, using a tag-and-probe method similar to that described in Ref.
[
66
]
.
In addition to the binning in
\eta
,
\phi
, and data-taking period used in Ref.
[
66
]
, an additional binning in
p_{\mathrm{T}}
is introduced to better model the reduced efficiency in the trigger turn-on region.
The
p_{\mathrm{T}}
bin edges are defined as
[4,6,8,10,\infty)
GeV.
At least three (four) muons reconstructed by the High Level Trigger are required to be within
\Delta R=0.07
of an offline muon in 2015 and 2016 (2017 and 2018).
The trigger efficiency ranges from approximately 1% for all signal models with
m_{\phi}=1.5
GeV and
T=1
GeV to nearly 100% for the model with
M=750
GeV,
m_{A^{\prime}}=0.5
GeV, and
m_{\phi}=T=5
GeV.
The corresponding scale factors range between 0.85 and 1.
The variations in trigger efficiency and scale factor are driven by differences in the muon kinematics, in particular the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectrum and multiplicity, between signal models.

2
illustrates this dependence for muons passing the selection requirements detailed below, before any event selection is applied.

p_{\mathrm{T}}>0.5\penalty\ \mathrm{{Ge\kern-1.00006ptV}}

, and

ensuring they are within the ID acceptance, reconstructed by the track reconstruction algorithms, and compatible

t\bar{t}
production and amounts to approximately 0.5% of events entering the search region,
while contributions from
Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
,
t\bar{t}Z
, and

or smaller.
Other processes involving heavy particles, such as
t\bar{t}W
,
t\bar{t}H
, and triboson production, are expected to contribute at a similar level.
As a result, the background is dominated by QCD multijet events, where muons are predominantly produced in decays of heavy-flavor hadrons, as well as kaons and pions.
As described in Section

S_{\mu}
and the track multiplicity

To reduce the impact of this boost, all muon four-momenta are first summed to define the total four-momentum of the system.
A Lorentz transformation is performed to express the muon four-momenta in the rest frame of the system.
In this frame, the spatial momentum components are used to build the quadratic momentum tensor,

S^{\alpha\beta}=\frac{\sum_{i}p_{i}^{\alpha}p_{i}^{\beta}}{\sum_{i}|\vec{p}_{i}|^{2}},

(1)

where the sum runs over all selected muons and
\alpha,\beta
denote spatial indices. The tensor is diagonalized to obtain its eigenvalues
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\lambda_{3}
, which satisfy
\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}=1
. The sphericity is then defined as

S_{\mu}=\frac{3}{2}(\lambda_{2}+\lambda_{3}).

(2)

This observable takes values close to zero for very collimated configurations and approaches unity for isotropic configurations.
As shown in Figure
4
, for signal models the value of
S_{\mu}
is larger than that obtained when computed in the laboratory frame, while in data the
S_{\mu}
distribution is shifted towards lower values compared to the laboratory-frame definition.

Event selection and background estimation

After the preselection requirement
\langle p_{\mathrm{T}}^{\mu}\rangle<10
GeV, the dominant background arises from QCD multijet events in which heavy-flavor hadrons decay into muons.

p_{\mathrm{T}}

The selection efficiency of the requirements on

,
d_{0}^{\textnormal{sig}}(\mu_{1})
, and
N_{\mu}
is close to unity for all signal models.

6
Systematic uncertainties

Experimental uncertainties on the simulated signal yields are evaluated to account for potential differences between data and simulated signal samples in the event reconstruction and selection.
The relative importance of the various uncertainty sources depends strongly on the signal model parameters.
For models with
m_{\phi},\,T\lesssim 3
GeV, where the acceptance is largely constrained by the
p_{\mathrm{T}}
thresholds of the online muon reconstruction, the dominant contribution is expected to arise from the trigger efficiency.
For models with
m_{\phi},\,T\gtrsim 3
GeV, uncertainties associated with ID tracking are expected to become largest.
This behavior is reflected in the ranges quoted for the individual uncertainty sources below.

Two sources of systematic uncertainty affecting the trigger efficiency are evaluated, both arising from the tag–and–probe procedure used to determine the single-muon trigger efficiencies.
The first originates from the limited statistical precision of the measurement, with its impact on the signal yield in the search region ranging from about 0.1% to 20%, depending on the model parameters.
The second is derived by varying the selection requirements applied to the probe muon.
The set of variations follows Ref.
[
66
]
, except for the probe-muon
p_{\mathrm{T}}
requirement, which is adapted to accommodate the additional
p_{\mathrm{T}}
binning used.
The effect of this second source on the signal yield in the search region ranges from roughly 0.1% to 4%, depending on the model parameters.

Offline muon reconstruction is affected by uncertainties in the resolution of the combined muon track and in the reconstruction efficiency.
The impact of the muon track-resolution uncertainty is found to be negligible.
The uncertainty associated with the mismodeling of the muon reconstruction efficiency leads to variations in the signal yield in the search region ranging from approximately 0.5% to 5%, depending on the model parameters.

An uncertainty of 100% is assigned to the estimated rate of tracks arising from combinatorial background in the ID, based on comparisons between data and simulation
of the reconstructed track multiplicity as a function of pile-up
[
67
]
.
Since the contribution of such tracks is small, its effect on
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
ranges from approximately 1% to 5%, depending on the model parameters.
For models predicting very high
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
, this uncertainty is largest in relative terms; however, its impact on the search sensitivity is sub-dominant,
since all events remain within the highest
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
bin under any variation.
Uncertainties on the ID track-resolution and reconstruction-efficiency modeling are found to be negligible for all signal models.

A pile-up modeling uncertainty is assigned to account for the difference between the predicted and measured inelastic cross-sections
[
72
]
.
Its effect is evaluated to be below 3% on the signal yield in the search region and below 1% on
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
for all signal models.

An uncertainty on
N^{\textnormal{PU}}_{\textnormal{tracks}}
is evaluated by repeating the measurement using either an alternative
Z\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
-enriched event selection or alternative offsets of
z_{\mathrm{shift}}=-6.5
mm and
z_{\mathrm{shift}}=7
mm.
The envelope of the variations is taken as the
\pm 1\sigma
uncertainty. The impact of this uncertainty on
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
is found to be below 0.5% for all signal models.

For signal models in which the mediator is the Higgs boson, acceptance uncertainties are evaluated by varying the renormalization and factorization scales, PDF eigenvectors and PDF sets, parton shower settings,
and by comparing the nominal NLO Higgs
p_{\mathrm{T}}
spectrum prediction with an alternative NNLO prediction and its variations.
The combined uncertainty from these sources is evaluated to be 20% and is applied to the expected signal yields in the
D_{i}
bins.

The uncertainty on the combined 2015–2018 integrated luminosity is 0.83%
[
71
]
, derived using the LUCID-2 detector
[
28
]
for the primary luminosity measurements.

The statistical uncertainty of the regions used to estimate the background is propagated as a systematic uncertainty on the prediction.
Additionally, a systematic uncertainty of 5–10% is assigned to the background yields in the
D_{i}
bins to account for the sensitivity of the prediction to the definition of the control regions.
This uncertainty is evaluated by varying both the lower and upper
S_{\mu}
boundaries of the
A
and
C_{i}
bins by
\pm 0.01
, corresponding to small changes around the nominal region.

As illustrated in Figure
7
, the ratio of the number of events satisfying
S_{\mu}>0.6
to those with
0.1<S_{\mu}<0.2
increases slightly with increasing
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
.
This trend is particularly pronounced in the validation region defined by
N_{\mu}\geq 5
and
d_{0}^{\textnormal{sig}}(\mu_{1})>5
.
To account for this residual correlation, the background model is allowed to deviate from the assumption of independence between
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
and
S_{\mu}
, by an amount determined in the validation regions.
The ratio shown in Figure
7
is fit with a linear function in each validation region, and the largest observed deviation from the expectation
of no correlation, corresponding to a zero slope, is incorporated as a systematic uncertainty in the background estimate.
Several possible sources of this positive correlation have been investigated. Those include slightly different composition in exclusive muon-multiplicity bins compared with the inclusive five or more muons required in the signal region,
a data-taking time dependence due to slightly different triggers active in 2015–2016 compared to 2017–2018, contributions from decays in flight of light charged hadrons (which were found to increase with the muon multiplicity), and
effects of correlated muon production through resonances as in
J/\psi\to\mu^{+}\mu^{-}
decays. None of these effects alone could fully explain the observed correlation,
although each contributed to some extent.

7
Statistical analysis and results

The statistical interpretation is based on profile-likelihood-ratio test statistics.
The goodness of fit of the background-only model is evaluated using a likelihood-ratio test with respect to a model that perfectly describes the observed data,
while each signal hypothesis is tested using profile-likelihood ratios comparing the background-only and signal-plus-background hypotheses.
In all cases,
p
-values are computed using asymptotic approximations
[
73
]
.

To account for the systematic uncertainty associated with the residual correlation between
N_{\textnormal{tracks}}
and
S_{\mu}
, described in Section
6

in bin

\displaystyle b_{A}

\displaystyle=\mu_{A},

\displaystyle b_{B}

8
Conclusion

A search for soft unclustered energy patterns is presented using
140\penalty\ \mathrm{fb}^{-1}
of
\sqrt{s}=13
TeV proton–proton collision data recorded by the ATLAS detector.
The analysis targets scenarios in which a scalar mediator decays into a strongly interacting, quasi-conformal hidden sector, giving rise to high-multiplicity final states containing low-momentum particles, including muons,
that are distributed approximately isotropically in the mediator center-of-mass frame.

Events are selected using multi-muon triggers, and the search region is defined by requirements on the muon multiplicity, average muon transverse momentum, and transverse impact-parameter significance, targeting prompt, high-multiplicity final states with soft muons.
The discrimination between signal and the dominant QCD multijet background is achieved using two observables:
the muon sphericity, constructed in the rest frame of the muon system, and the charged-particle track multiplicity, corrected for pile-up.
The background contribution is estimated by using a likelihood-based ABCD method that exploits the weak correlation between these observables, with residual correlations accounted for through a data-driven parameterization.

No significant deviation from the background-only hypothesis is observed in the search region.
The results are therefore interpreted in terms of upper limits at the 95% confidence level on the on the product of the mediator production cross section and the branching fraction for its decay into a SUEP.

This search extends the sensitivity to SUEP scenarios beyond previous LHC results
[
19
,
20
]
, particularly in regions of parameter space characterized by
moderate particle multiplicities and sizable muon fractions in the final state.
The analysis demonstrates the effectiveness of multi-muon-based strategies and provides a complementary approach
to existing searches targeting primarily hadronic final states.
