Upper Bounds on Turán Densities via Extremal Set Theory
Yaobin Chen, Xizhi Liu, Ningyuan Yang, Tianming Zhu

Abstract
We exhibit, in a systematic way, connections between hypergraph Turán problems and extremal set theory. More specifically, we construct natural families of uniform hypergraphs for which the upper bounds on their Turán densities reduce to classical problems in extremal set theory, including the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem, L L -intersecting families, and the Erdős matching problem.

Introduction

Let
r\geq 2
be an integer. An
r
-uniform hypergraph
, or
r
-graph
, is a family of
r
-subsets of a vertex set. For a hypergraph
H
, let
V(H)
denote its vertex set, and write
v(H)\coloneqq|V(H)|
for its number of vertices. We identify a hypergraph with its edge set, and hence write
|H|
for the number of edges of
H
.

Given a family
\mathcal{F}
of
r
-graphs, an
r
-graph
H
is
\mathcal{F}
-free
if it contains no member of
\mathcal{F}
as a subgraph. Its
Turán number
is

\ex(n,\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\max\left\{|H|:|V(H)|=n\text{ and }H\text{ is }\mathcal{F}\text{-free}\right\},

and its
Turán density
is

\pi(\mathcal{F})\coloneqq\lim_{n\to\infty}{\ex(n,\mathcal{F})}/{\tbinom{n}{r}}.

We write
\ex(n,F)
and
\pi(F)
when
\mathcal{F}=\{F\}
.

Turán theory is one of the central topics in extremal combinatorics, beginning with Mantel’s theorem
[
23
]
for triangles and Turán’s theorem
[
26
]
for complete graphs. For an ordinary graph
F
with at least one edge, its Turán density is governed by the chromatic number: the Erdős–Stone–Simonovits theorem
[
9
,
8
]
gives
\pi(F)=1-1/(\chi(F)-1)
. Hypergraph Turán theory is far from well understood. For
r\geq 3
, there is no comparable chromatic-type principle, and even the value of
\pi(K_{4}^{3})
, one of the central problems already present in Turán’s work, remains open. For broader context, see Sidorenko’s survey
[
25
]
and Keevash’s survey
[
21
]
on hypergraph Turán theory.

Extremal set theory is another central area of extremal combinatorics. Early landmarks include the Erdős–Ko–Rado theorem
[
7
]
and Katona’s foundational intersection theorem
[
20
]
, and the area asks how large a family of finite sets can be under restrictions on intersections, matchings, packings, shadows, and related forbidden configurations; representative results include Wilson’s exact
t
-intersecting theorem
[
28
]
, the Ahlswede–Khachatrian complete intersection theorem
[
1
]
, the Frankl–Füredi forbidden-intersection theorems
[
12
]
, the Frankl–Wilson algebraic method
[
15
]
, Bollobás’s set-pair inequality
[
2
]
, Rödl’s packing theorem
[
24
]
, and the Erdős matching theorem
[
6
]
, while Frankl and Tokushige’s survey
[
13
]
and monograph
[
14
]
give general references.

Connections between hypergraph Turán problems and extremal set theory have appeared in several forms in the literature. Lagrangian versions of intersecting-family and Erdős–Ko–Rado type results can drive hypergraph Turán theorems for extensions; examples include Hefetz and Keevash’s work
[
17
]
, the work of Jiang, Peng, and Wu on Lagrangian densities and Turán numbers of extensions
[
19
]
, and Watts, Norin, and Yepremyan’s work
[
27
]
in this direction. Frankl’s theorem
[
10
]
states that if
k\geq 1
and
C^{2k}_{3}
is the

-uniform expanded triangle whose vertex set is the disjoint union
K_{1}\cup K_{2}\cup K_{3}
of three
k
-sets and whose edges are
K_{1}\cup K_{2}

K_{1}\cup K_{3}
, and

\pi(C^{2k}_{3})=\frac{1}{2}
.
His proof
[
10
]
uses Katona’s cycle method, a fundamental method in extremal set theory.

In this work, we initiate a more systematic study of connections between hypergraph Turán problems and extremal set theory. We first fix the extension convention used in the definitions below. A
partial hypergraph
is a finite vertex set together with a finite collection
e_{1},\ldots,e_{t}
of subsets (not necessarily of the same size) of its vertex set, called
partial edges
. If all partial edges have size at most
r
, its
r
-uniform extension
is the
r
-graph obtained by adding, for each

, a private set

of

new vertices and declaring

to be an edge. The private sets are chosen disjointly for different partial edges; if
|e_{j}|=r
, then

We write
[q]\coloneqq\{1,\ldots,q\}
and
[p,q]\coloneqq\{p,p+1,\ldots,q\}
for integer intervals, with the convention

. We now list the applications proved later in the paper.

Matching constructions and the Erdős matching problem

Let
s\geq 2
and let
r,k,a
be integers satisfying

\begin{gathered}1\leq a\leq k<r\quad\text{and}\quad sk\leq r.\end{gathered}

(3)

Define
T^{r}_{a,[k,s]}
as follows. Choose disjoint sets
A
and
E
with
|A|=a
and
|E|=r
, and choose pairwise disjoint sets
B_{1},\ldots,B_{s}\subseteq E
with
|B_{i}|=k
for every

. Put
\bar{B}_{i}\coloneqq E\setminus B_{i}
. Let

be the

\{E,~A\cup\bar{B}_{1},~\ldots,~A\cup\bar{B}_{s}\}.

Let

M(r,k,s-1)\coloneqq\max\left\{|\mathcal{B}|:\mathcal{B}\subseteq\tbinom{[r]}{k}\text{ and }\nu(\mathcal{B})\leq s-1\right\},

where
\nu(\mathcal{B})
is the

of
\mathcal{B}
.

Theorem 1.3
.

Under the assumptions in
Eq.

3
,

\pi\bigl(T^{r}_{a,[k,s]}\bigr)\leq\frac{M(r,k,s-1)}{\binom{r}{k}}.

The extremal function

is the quantity in the Erdős matching conjecture
[
6
]
. In the present notation, the conjecture predicts

M(r,k,s-1)=\max\left\{\tbinom{ks-1}{k},\tbinom{r}{k}-\tbinom{r-s+1}{k}\right\};

the two terms come, respectively, from all
k
-sets inside a fixed
(ks-1)
-set and from all
k
-sets meeting a fixed
(s-1)
-set. The large-ground-set case needed here goes back to Erdős
[
6
]
, with important subsequent refinements by Bollobás–Daykin–Erdős
[
3
]
, Huang–Loh–Sudakov
[
18
]
, Frankl
[
11
]
, and Łuczak–Mieczkowska
[
22
]
, while Frankl and Tokushige’s monograph
[
14
]
gives a general account of matching problems in extremal set theory.

Thus, when
k
and
s
are fixed and

is sufficiently large, the Erdős matching theorem evaluates

M(r,k,s-1)=\tbinom{r}{k}-\tbinom{r-s+1}{k},

so

1.3
gives

\pi\bigl(T^{r}_{a,[k,s]}\bigr)\leq 1-\frac{\binom{r-s+1}{k}}{\binom{r}{k}}.

3.1

1.1
and

Throughout this subsection, let

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
H
be a finite
F
-hom-free

(X_{1},\ldots,X_{r})

\prod_{j=r-b-a+1}^{r-b}x_{j}\leq\frac{\Psi(r,b,L)}{\binom{r}{b}}.

Consequently, by

,
\pi(F)\leq{\Psi(r,b,L)}/{\binom{r}{b}}
.

Proof.

Let
\beta\coloneqq\prod_{j\in[r]}x_{j}
. Fix an
r

E=\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{r}\}
and an
a
-set

. For every

be the maximal partial

Order the vertices by
v_{1}<\cdots<v_{r}<w_{1}<\cdots<w_{a}

has the prefix
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{i}\}
as its unique maximal face with maximum

, and the vertex

has
\hat{B}\cup\{w_{1},\ldots,w_{\ell}\}
as its unique maximal face with maximum

1,&\text{otherwise.}\end{cases}

By

Proof of

Let

We first record the product set-theoretic estimate that will be used to control the support families in the proof of the product bound.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\subseteq\tbinom{V_{1}}{r_{1}}\times\cdots\times\tbinom{V_{m}}{r_{m}}
have the following property: for every two distinct tuples

For each

, let

and define a graph

on

by joining two distinct vertices

when

\alpha(G_{i})=\Psi(n,r_{i},L_{i})

is vertex-transitive. In the categorical product

, two tuples are adjacent exactly when their entries are adjacent in every coordinate. Thus, if two tuples
(A_{1},\ldots,A_{m})
and

of

are adjacent in this product, then

is independent in the product.

We now spell out the use of Zhang’s theorem. For two vertex-transitive graphs

\frac{\alpha(G\times H)}{|V(G)|\,|V(H)|}=\max\left\{\frac{\alpha(G)}{|V(G)|},\frac{\alpha(H)}{|V(H)|}\right\}.

Let

is vertex-transitive for every

gives

Induction on

therefore gives

\frac{\alpha(G_{1}\times\cdots\times G_{m})}{|X_{1}|\cdots|X_{m}|}=\max_{i\in[m]}\frac{\alpha(G_{i})}{|X_{i}|}.

Therefore

.
∎

. Extending the quotient by arbitrary bijections between the corresponding private vertex sets gives the desired homomorphism of the
r
-uniform extensions.

For
(iii)
, let
U_{B}
and
U_{C}
be the private vertex sets added to
A\cup B
and
A\cup C
, respectively, in the source extension. Choose subsets
S\subseteq U_{B}
and
D\subseteq C\setminus B
with
|S|=|D|=s
, and identify the vertices of
S
with the vertices of
D
by an arbitrary bijection. Put
B^{\prime}\coloneqq B\cup D
and
C^{\prime}\coloneqq C
. The image of the edge
E
is injective, the image of
A\cup B\cup U_{B}
is
A\cup B^{\prime}\cup(U_{B}\setminus S)
, and the image of
A\cup C\cup U_{C}
is
A\cup C^{\prime}\cup U_{C}
. These are the three edges in the extension of
T^{r}_{a,b+s,c,d+s}
, with
|B^{\prime}\cap C^{\prime}|=d+s
. This gives the required homomorphism.

Part
(iv)
is symmetric. Choose
S\subseteq U_{C}
and
D\subseteq B\setminus C
with
|S|=|D|=s
, identify them bijectively, and put
B^{\prime}\coloneqq B
and
C^{\prime}\coloneqq C\cup D
. The same edge-by-edge injectivity check gives a homomorphism to the extension of
T^{r}_{a,b,c+s,d+s}
.

For
(v)
, choose pairwise disjoint subsets
A_{i}\subseteq A
with
|A_{i}|=s_{i}
, which is possible because
\sum_{i}s_{i}\leq a
. For each
i

D_{i}\subseteq E_{i}\setminus(B_{i}\cup C_{i})
with
|D_{i}|=s_{i}
, and identify the vertices of
A_{i}
with the vertices of
D_{i}
by an arbitrary bijection. Put

A^{\prime}\coloneqq A\setminus\bigcup_{i}A_{i},\qquad B_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq B_{i}\cup D_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad C_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq C_{i}\cup D_{i}.

Then
|A^{\prime}|=a-\sum_{i}s_{i}
,
|B_{i}^{\prime}|=b_{i}+s_{i}
,
|C_{i}^{\prime}|=c_{i}+s_{i}
, and
|B_{i}^{\prime}\cap C_{i}^{\prime}|=d_{i}+s_{i}
. No identified pair lies in a common partial edge, so the quotient maps

E_{1},\ldots,E_{m},\quad A\cup\bigcup_{i}B_{i},\quad\text{and}\quad A\cup\bigcup_{i}C_{i}

injectively onto the corresponding partial edges of the target construction. The two side partial edges have the same sizes before and after the quotient, so their private vertex sets also have the same sizes. Extending over those private vertices by arbitrary bijections gives the claimed homomorphism.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The following statements hold.

(i)

Assume
Eq.

1
. Let

\displaystyle L_{s}^{(0)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[0,d+s-1]\cup[b-c+d+s+1,b+s],

\displaystyle L_{s}^{(1)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[0,d+s-1]\cup[b-c+d+2s+1,b+s],

\displaystyle L_{s}^{(2)}

\displaystyle\coloneqq[0,d+s-1]\cup[p_{s}-q_{s}+d+s+1,p_{s}],

.

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq s\leq\min\{r-a-b,c-d\}}\frac{\Psi(r,b+s,L_{s}^{(1)})}{\tbinom{r}{b+s}},~\min_{0\leq s\leq\min\{r-a-c,b-d\}}\frac{\Psi(r,p_{s},L_{s}^{(2)})}{\tbinom{r}{p_{s}}}\Bigg\}.

(ii)

Let

with parameters satisfying

4
and
5

4
(v)

.
Then

\pi(F)\leq\min_{\mathbf{s}}\max_{i\in[m]}\frac{\Psi(r,b_{i}+s_{i},L_{i}(s_{i}))}{\tbinom{r}{b_{i}+s_{i}}}.

In the third minimum of

5
Concluding remarks

\bullet
In two previously studied cases, the upper bounds in this work recover the upper-bound side of known exact Turán results. First, when specialized to expanded triangles,
Theorem

1.1
gives Frankl’s upper bound; the corresponding lower bound is Frankl’s theorem
[
10
]
, giving the known equality for
T^{2k}_{k,k,k,0}=C^{2k}_{3}
.
Second, for the
4
-uniform instance
T^{4}_{1,3,3,2}
, the
4
-graph on five vertices with three edges,
Theorem

1.1
gives the upper bound
1/4
; this upper bound was already obtained by Gunderson–Semeraro via a modification of de Caen’s counting argument
[
5
,
16
]
, and the corresponding lower bound follows from their construction
[
16
]
. It remains natural to ask for which other extension constructions the upper bounds given by this work are exact.

\bullet
Beyond the
L
-intersection and matching problems used here, it would be natural to look for constructions controlled by other theorems from extremal set theory such as cross-intersecting families, shadows, forbidden configurations, packing and covering problems, or stability theorems. Conversely, sharp and stability results for such set systems may suggest new candidate extremal constructions for hypergraph Turán problems.
