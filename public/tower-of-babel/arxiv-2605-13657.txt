Infinitesimal automorphisms and obstruction theory on the moduli of L L -valued G G -Higgs bundles
Sanghyeon Lee, Sang-Bum Yoo

Abstract
. For an arbitrary reductive group G G , we compute the infinitesimal automorphisms of L L -valued principal G G -Higgs bundles over a compact Kähler manifold X X , extending known results for Ω X 1 \Omega_{X}^{1} -valued G G -Higgs bundles. Using this computation, when G G is semisimple and X X is a smooth projective variety, we show that the moduli stack of stable L L -valued G G -Higgs bundles is a Deligne-Mumford (DM) stack. Furthermore, when X X is a smooth projective surface and L = K X L=K_{X} , we construct a symmetric perfect obstruction theory on this stable locus. We expect this will provide a foundation for defining Vafa-Witten invariants for reductive groups G G .



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact connected smooth Kähler manifold, equipped with a fixed Kähler form
\omega
. For a reductive linear algebraic group
G
, the Hitchin-Kobayashi type correspondence for the stability of principal
G
-bundles on
X
, and
G
-bundles with additional data has been studied for a long time.

In
[
4
]
, B. Anchouche and I. Biswas proved that a holomorphic principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}
has an Einstein-Hermitian connection if and only if
E_{G}
is polystable. (See
[
4
, Definition 3.5]
for the definition of polystability.) As an intermediate step, they also proved that if
E_{G}
is stable, then the adjoint vector bundle
\ad(E_{G}):=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
is polystable where
\mathfrak{g}
is the Lie algebra of the Lie group
G
.

Furthermore, in
[
5
]
, I. Biswas and G. Schumacher proved that an
\Omega_{X}^{1}
-valued
G
-Higgs sheaf admits a hermitian Yang-Mills-Higgs connection if and only if it is polystable.
1
1

1

\Omega_{X}^{1}
is a sheaf of holomorphic 1-forms over
X
.

Moreover, in
[
7
]
, I. Biswas proved that the infinitesimal automorphisms of an
\Omega_{X}^{1}
-valued stable
G
-Higgs sheaves is isomorphic to the center of the Lie algebra,
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.

In Sections
2
and
3
of this paper, we will extend the computation of infinitesimal automorphisms to
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles,
(E_{G},\varphi)
where
L
is a vector bundle on
X
and
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes L)
. For that, we use a generalized Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence in
[
2
]
. For a quiver
Q
, L. Álvarez-Cónsul and O. García-Prada proved that a holomorphic twisted quiver bundle is polystable if and only if it admits a hermitian metric satisfying a quiver vortex equation. Note that a twisted quiver bundle corresponding to a quiver
Q
is a collection of vector bundles attached to each vertex of the quiver
Q
, and the data of morphisms of vector bundles with some twistings attached to each arrow of
Q
. See
[
2
, Definition 1.1]
for details of the twisted quiver bundles. When
Q
is a quiver with a single vertex and a single arrow, and the twisting is given by
(\Omega_{X}^{1})^{\vee}
,
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
recovers the Kobayashi-Hitchin correspondence for
\Omega_{X}^{1}
-valued Higgs bundles in
[
11
,
19
]
.

As an application of the computation of the infinitesimal automorphisms to the case where
X
is a smooth projective variety, in Section
5
, we will prove that the moduli space of
L
-valued stable
G
-Higgs bundles, denoted by
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}^{s}(X)
is a Deligne-Mumford (DM) stack. Moreover, when
\mathrm{dim}(X)=2
and
L=K_{X}
, we will prove that the moduli
\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}^{s}(X)
is equipped with a symmetric perfect obstruction theory. We expect that this study may provide a foundation to define Vafa-Witten invariants for a general reductive group
G
.

Note that in the case when
G
is a symplectic group or special orthogonal group and
L=K_{X}
, a perfect obstruction theory is constructed in
[
18
]
. They considered the moduli space of symplectic Higgs bundles and orthogonal Higgs bundles as the fixed loci of the moduli space of Higgs bundles with fixed determinant(say
\mathrm{Higgs}_{\mathrm{SL}}
), via the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-action acting on the Higgs fields by
\varphi\mapsto\varphi^{\vee}
. They proved that the derived structure of
\mathrm{Higgs}_{\mathrm{SL}}
induces a derived structure on the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-fixed locus.

In the future, we would like to generalize the results of this paper to Gieseker stable
G
-Higgs sheaves. When we consider a minimal second Chern class
c_{2,\min}\in\mathbb{Z}

[
18

, the moduli space of
K_{X}
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles with the minimal second Chern class has a compact
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-fixed locus, where the action is given by scaling of the Higgs fields
\varphi
. Then, we can define Vafa-Witten invariants of the group
G
by using the equivariant virtual fundamental class in the same manner as in
[
22
]
.

2.
Stability condition and Yang-Mills-Higgs equation for principal
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles.

Let us denote by
\Lambda_{\omega}
the contraction operator with respect to the Kähler form
\omega
.
Let
G
be a connected reductive linear algebraic group defined over
\mathbb{C}
with Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
.
An
L
-valued principal
G
-Higgs bundle
on
X
is a pair
(E_{G},\varphi)
where
E_{G}
is a principal
G
-bundle on
X
and
\varphi
is a section:

\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G})\otimes L)

where
L
is a holomorphic vector bundle on
X
equipped with a Hermitian metric. We call the section
\varphi
an (
L
-valued) Higgs field.
Following
[
7
]
, we define the stability of
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundle as follows. In this paper, we call an open set
U\subset X
a
big open subset
if
\mathrm{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
.

On the other hand, when we use the term
L
-valued Higgs (not
G
-Higgs) bundle, it means a pair
(E,\varphi)
where
E
is a vector bundle and
\varphi\in\Hom(E,E\otimes L)
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundle
(E_{G},\varphi)
on
X
is called
semistable
(resp.
stable
) if for any maximal parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and its holomorphic reduction
E_{P}\subset E_{G}|_{U}
over some big open subset
U
such that the Higgs field
\varphi|_{U}\in H^{0}(U,\ad(E_{G})\otimes L)
restricts to
\varphi|_{U}\in H^{0}(U,\ad(E_{P})\otimes L)
, we always have the inequality:

\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{G}|_{U}))\geq\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{P}))\,(\text{resp. }\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{G}|_{U}))>\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{P})))\,.

Remark 2.2
.

In
[
4
]
, the definition of stability is slightly different as follows. In
[
4
]
, the authors considered the
L=\Omega_{X}^{1}
case. A principal (
\Omega_{X}^{1}
-valued)
G
-Higgs bundle
(E_{G},\varphi)
on
X
is called
semistable
(resp.
stable
) if for any maximal parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and for any reduction of the structure group
\sigma:U\to E_{G}/P:=E_{G}\times_{G}(G/P)
over some open subset
U
with
\mathrm{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
satisfying
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{P})\otimes\Omega_{X}^{1})
, we have the inequality
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{G}/P})\geq 0
(resp.
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{G}/P})>0
), where
T_{E_{G}/P}
is the relative tangent bundle for the natural projection
E_{G}/P|_{U}\to U
. We can see that this definition is equivalent to Definition
2.1
. Consider the short exact sequence of
P
-modules (the module structures are given by the adjoint representation)

0\to\mathfrak{p}\to\mathfrak{g}\to\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}\to 0,

where
\mathfrak{g}
and
\mathfrak{p}
are Lie algebras of
G
and
P
respectively. It induces the short exact sequence of vector bundles on
U

0\to\sigma^{*}(E_{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{p})\to\sigma^{*}(E_{G}\times_{P}\mathfrak{g})\to\sigma^{*}(E_{G}\times_{P}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p}))\to 0.

This is nothing but

0\to\ad(E_{P})\to\ad(E_{G})|_{U}\to\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{G}/P})\to 0.

Hence
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{G}/P})\geq 0
(resp.
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{G}/P})>0
) if and only if
\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{G}|_{U}))\geq\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{P}))
(resp.
\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{G}|_{U}))>\mathrm{deg}(\ad(E_{P}))
).

For a holomorphic vector bundle
{\cal E}
equipped with a Hermitian metric
h_{\cal E}
, there exists a unique connection
D_{\cal E}
, called the
Chern connection
, which satisfies two conditions:

(1)

It is compatible with the holomorphic structure:
D_{\cal E}^{0,1}=\overline{\partial}_{\cal E}
. Here,
\overline{\partial}_{\cal E}
is the Dolbeault operator defining the holomorphic structure of
{\cal E}
.

(2)

It preserves the metric
h
:
dh(s,t)=h(D_{\cal E}s,t)+h(s,D_{\cal E}t)
for sections
s,t\in H^{0}(X,{\cal E})
.

We denote the curvature of the Chern connection by
F_{\cal E}=D_{\cal E}^{2}
.

For an admissible Hermitian metric
h_{\mathfrak{E}}
on
\mathfrak{E}:=\ad(E_{G})
, the quiver vortex equation is given by
[
2
, Definition 2.1, Theorem 3.1]
by considering the quiver with a single vertex
a
and a single arrow
v
decorated by the vector bundle
M_{a}=L^{\vee}
:

(2.1)

i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{E}}^{1,1}+[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=\tau\id_{\mathfrak{E}}

where
\varphi^{*_{h}}
is the adjoint of
\varphi
(considered as an endomorphism
\mathfrak{E}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[-,\varphi]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathfrak{E}\otimes L
) with respect to the hermitian metric
h_{\mathfrak{E}}
on
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
and the hermitian metric on
L
, and
\tau=\tau_{\mathfrak{E}}\in\mathbb{R}
. Note that we can consider
\varphi^{*_{h}}
as an element of
\Hom(\mathfrak{E}\otimes L,\mathfrak{E})
.
Then
[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=\tr_{L}(\varphi\circ\varphi^{*_{h}})-\varphi^{*_{h}}\circ\varphi
where
\tr_{L}
is the trace map
\Hom(E\otimes L,E\otimes L)\to\Hom(E,E)
, so that
[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]\in\Hom(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{E})
.

Remark 2.3
.

When
L=K_{X}
, the above quiver vortex equation (
2.1
) is equivalent to the Yang-Mills-Higgs equation in
[
21
, Theorem 1.4]
.

3.
Infinitesimal Automorphisms of
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles

In this section, we generalize the computation of infinitesimal automorphisms of
\Omega_{X}^{1}
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles in
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
to the case of
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles.
For that, we establish an analogue of
[
5
, Lemma 3.4]
.
For a
G
-Higgs bundle
{\cal E}=(E_{G},\varphi)
, we let
{\cal A}({\cal E})
be the space of infinitesimal automorphism of
{\cal E}
. Same as in
[
7
]
, we have a natural isomorphism
{\cal A}({\cal E})\cong\{\tau\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G}))\,|\,[\tau,\varphi]=0\}
.

In this section, any section
\tau\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G}))
is sometimes considered as its induced endomorphism
\ad(E_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[-,\tau]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ad(E_{G})
, by an abuse of notation.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
(E_{G},\varphi)
be a stable
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundle on
X
. Let
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
and let
h_{\mathfrak{E}}
be the unique admissible Hermitian Yang-Mills-Higgs metric satisfying (
2.1
). Let
{\cal F}:={\cal E}nd(\mathfrak{E})
and let
s\in H^{0}(X,{\cal F})
be a holomorphic
endomorphism of
\mathfrak{E}
such that
[s,\varphi]=0
. (Here,
\varphi
is considered as its induced endomorphism.) Then
s
is parallel with respect to the Chern connection
D_{\cal F}
.

Proof.

Consider the Laplacian
\Delta_{d}=dd^{*}+d^{*}d
where
(-)^{*}
denotes the adjoint compatible with the Hermitian metric. Consider the holomorphic normal local coordinates
\{z_{k},\overline{z}_{k}\}
. Then the Kähler form is locally expressed by
\frac{i}{2}\sum_{k}dz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}
. Then locally the Laplacian is defined by
\sum_{k}\partial_{z_{k}}\partial_{\overline{z}_{k}}
. Since the section
s
is holomorphic, we have
D_{\overline{z}_{k}}s=0
(Here
D=D_{\cal F}
). Let
\langle-,-\rangle
be the Hermitian inner product via the Hermitian metric
h_{\mathfrak{E}}
. Let us abbreviate
\partial_{z_{k}},\partial_{\overline{z}_{k}},D_{z_{k}},D_{\overline{z}_{k}}
by
\partial_{k},\partial_{\overline{k}},D_{k},D_{\overline{k}}
. Then locally we have:

\displaystyle\Delta_{d}|s|^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\partial_{k}\partial_{\overline{k}}\langle s,s\rangle=\sum_{k}\partial_{k}(\langle D_{\overline{k}}s,s\rangle+\langle s,D_{k}s\rangle)=\sum_{k}\partial_{k}\langle s,D_{k}s\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{k}\langle D_{k}s,D_{k}s\rangle+\langle s,D_{\overline{k}}D_{k}s\rangle=\sum_{k}|D_{k}s|^{2}+\sum_{k}\langle s,D_{\overline{k}}D_{k}s\rangle\,.

Since
Ds=\sum_{k}(D_{k}s)dz_{k}
and
\langle dz_{i},dz_{j}\rangle=\delta_{ij}
(since
\{z_{k},\overline{z}_{k}\}_{k}
is the holomorphic local coordinate), we have
\sum_{k}|D_{k}s|^{2}=\frac{1}{2}|Ds|^{2}
since
|dz_{i}|^{2}=2
for all
i
.

On the other hand, locally we can write
F_{\cal F}^{1,1}=\sum_{k}F_{k\overline{k}}dz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}
where
F_{k,\overline{k}}=[D_{k},D_{\overline{k}}]
. Since
s
is holomorphic, we have
D_{\overline{k}}D_{k}s=-F_{k,\overline{k}}s
. Thus
\Delta_{d}|s|^{2}=|Ds|^{2}-\langle s,(\sum_{k}F_{k,\overline{k}})s\rangle
.

Moreover, from
F_{\cal F}^{1,1}=\sum_{k}F_{k\overline{k}}dz_{k}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}
, we have
i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\cal F}^{1,1}=2\sum_{k}F_{k,\overline{k}}
. Hence we have

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{d}|s|^{2}=|D_{\cal F}s|^{2}-\langle i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\cal F}^{1,1}s,s\rangle.

Then by Lemma
3.2
below, we obtain the following formula for a holomorphic section
s
:

(3.1)

2\Delta_{d}(|s|^{2})=|D_{\cal F}s|^{2}-\langle[i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}},s],s\rangle.

We must evaluate the curvature term. Using the Yang-Mills-Higgs equation (
2.1
), the curvature term in (
3.1
) becomes:

-\langle[i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}},s],s\rangle=\langle[[\varphi,\varphi^{*}],s],s\rangle.

Using the Jacobi identity and the hypothesis
[s,\varphi]=0
, we have
[[\varphi,\varphi^{*}],s]=[\varphi,[\varphi^{*},s]]
. Therefore we have:

(3.2)

\langle[[\varphi,\varphi^{*}],s],s\rangle=\langle[\varphi,[\varphi^{*},s]],s\rangle.

By Lemma
3.3
below, we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\langle[\varphi,[\varphi^{*},s]],s\rangle=\langle[\varphi^{*},s],[\varphi^{*},s]\rangle=|[\varphi^{*},s]|^{2}.

Thus, the Bochner formula (
3.1
) becomes:

2\Delta_{d}(|s|^{2})=|D_{\cal F}s|^{2}+|[\varphi^{*},s]|^{2}\geq 0.

Thus
|s|^{2}
is a subharmonic function on the compact manifold
X
. By the maximum principle,
|s|^{2}
is constant. Hence
\Delta_{d}(|s|^{2})=0
, which implies
|D_{\cal F}s|^{2}=0
, so that
D_{\cal F}s=0
.
∎

Lemma 3.2
.

For any
s\in H^{0}(X,{\cal F})
, we have

F_{\cal F}s=[F_{\mathfrak{E}},s]
.

Proof.

Let
v
be a local section of
\mathfrak{E}
. Then

D_{\mathfrak{E}}(s(v))=D_{{\cal F}}(s)(v)+s(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v)).

Applying
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
on both sides,

F_{\mathfrak{E}}(s(v))=D_{\mathfrak{E}}(D_{{\cal F}}(s)(v))+D_{\mathfrak{E}}(s(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v))).

By the (generalized) Leibniz rule, we see that

D_{\mathfrak{E}}(D_{{\cal F}}(s)(v))=D_{{\cal F}}(D_{{\cal F}}(s))(v)-D_{{\cal F}}(s)\wedge D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v)

and

D_{\mathfrak{E}}(s(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v)))=D_{{\cal F}}(s)\wedge D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v)+s(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v))).

Thus

F_{\mathfrak{E}}(s(v))=D_{{\cal F}}(D_{{\cal F}}(s))(v)+s(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(D_{\mathfrak{E}}(v)))=F_{{\cal F}}(s)(v)+sF_{\mathfrak{E}}(v),

that is,

F_{{\cal F}}(s)(v)=[F_{\mathfrak{E}},s](v).

∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
E
be a vector bundle over
X
with metric and let
F={\cal E}nd(E)
be the endomorphism bundle equipped with the metric induced from
E
. For a point
x\in X
and
A,B,C\in\End(E|_{x})
, we have

\langle[A,B],C\rangle_{x}=\langle B,[A^{*},C]\rangle_{x}.

Proof.

Note that
\langle\alpha,\beta\rangle_{x}=\tr(\alpha\beta^{*})
. Therefore

\displaystyle\langle[A,B],C\rangle_{x}

\displaystyle=\langle AB-BA,C\rangle_{x}=\tr(ABC^{*})-\tr(BAC^{*})=\tr(BC^{*}A)-\tr(BAC^{*})

\displaystyle=\tr(B(A^{*}C)^{*})-\tr(B(CA^{*})^{*})=\tr(B[A^{*},C]^{*})=\langle B,[A^{*},C]\rangle_{x}\,.

∎

Now, we are ready to prove the following theorem about infinitesimal automorphism of stable
L
-valued principal
G
-Higgs bundles.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
{\cal E}:=(E_{G},\varphi)
be a
stable

L
-valued principal
G
-Higgs bundle on a compact Kähler manifold
X
. Then the infinitesimal automorphism group is isomorphic to the center of the Lie algebra:

\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}).

Proof.

Again let
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
. Since
\mathcal{E}
is stable, Theorem
4.8
in the next section (an analogue of
[
4
, Theorem 2.6]
) guarantees that
(\mathfrak{E},\varphi)
is polystable. Then,
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]
guarantees the existence of an admissible Hermitian metric
h
on
\mathfrak{E}
satisfying the Yang-Mills-Higgs equation (
2.1
). Then this metric induces a Chern connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
on the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{E}
.

By definition,
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
consists of holomorphic sections
\xi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{G}))
satisfying
[\varphi,\xi]=0
. Let
\xi\in\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})
. Let
{\cal E}=\ad(E_{G})
. Since
[\varphi,\xi]=0
, the commutator relation for the induced endomorphisms
[\varphi,\xi]=0
also holds. Thus Lemma
3.1
applies, and hence we have
D_{\cal F}\xi=0
where
\xi
is considered as its induced endomorphism. This directly implies
D_{\mathfrak{E}}\xi=0
. In other words
\xi
is parallel with respect to
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
.

Assume that
\xi\notin\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. By Corollary
6.2
, there exists a connection
D_{E}
on
E_{G}
compatible with the holomorphic structure which induces the connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
on the adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{E}
of
E_{G}
.
Then, same as the proof of
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
, we can find a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and its holomorphic reduction,
E_{P}\subset E_{G}
, which is preserved by the connection
D_{E}
on
E_{G}
.
Let us denote the adjoint bundle
\ad(E_{P}):=E_{P}\times_{P}\mathfrak{p}
by
\mathfrak{E}_{P}
. Note that
\mathfrak{E}_{P}
becomes a subbundle of
\mathfrak{E}
.
Again, parallel to
[
7
, Theorem 3.1]
, we can check that the Higgs field
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)
restricts to
\varphi_{P}:=\varphi|_{\mathfrak{E}_{P}}\in H^{0}(X,\mathfrak{E}_{P}\otimes L)
. (See Remark
3.5
to see how we can construct the holomorphic reduction.)

We now derive a contradiction by using the slope stability. Let us recall the Yang-Mills-Higgs equation for
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
:

\displaystyle i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{E}}^{1,1}+[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=\tau\id_{\mathfrak{E}}.

The degree of the bundle
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
is given by the integral of the trace of the (1,1)-component of its curvature:

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(X)}\int_{X}\tr(i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}.

On the other hand, we observe that
\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(X)}\int_{X}\tr\left([\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]\right)\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=0
. Moreover, since
G
is reductive,
\mathfrak{E}\cong\mathfrak{E}^{\vee}
. Therefore,
\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E})=0
, so that we obtain
\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(X)}\int_{X}\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{E})\frac{\tau\omega^{n}}{n!}=\mathrm{rank}(\mathfrak{E})\tau=0
from (
2.1
). Thus
\tau=0
.

Since the connection
D_{E}
restricts to a connection
D_{P}
on
E_{P}
, the connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
also restricts to a connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}_{P}}
on
\mathfrak{E}_{P}
. Thus, the equation (
2.1
) restricts over
\mathfrak{E}_{P}
:

i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{D_{\mathfrak{E}_{P}}}^{1,1}+[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=0.

Here we used
\tau=0
. Therefore we have:

\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E}_{P})=\frac{1}{\mathrm{Vol}(X)}\int_{X}\tr(i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{D_{\mathfrak{E}_{P}}})\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=\frac{-1}{\mathrm{Vol}(X)}\int_{X}\tr([\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}])\frac{\omega^{n}}{n!}=0

where the last equality follows since
\tr([A,B])=0
.
But since
{\cal E}=(E_{G},\varphi)
is stable, we must have

0=\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E})>\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E}_{P}).

But in the above we proved that
\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E}_{P})=0
, which leads to a contradiction. Therefore, the assumption that
\xi\notin\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is false, so that we have
\mathcal{A}(\mathcal{E})\subset\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. On the other hand, it is clear that
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\subset{\cal A}({\cal E})
, so that we obtain
{\cal A}({\cal E})=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.
∎

Remark 3.5
.

We briefly review how to construct the holomorphic reduction
E_{P}\subset E_{G}
in the same way as in
[
7
]
. Let
\mathrm{Ad}(E_{G}):=E_{G}\times_{G}G
via the conjugate action of
G
on
G
itself. Let
x_{0}
be a fixed point in
X
. Note that
\mathrm{Ad}(E_{G})_{x_{0}}
is isomorphic to the automorphism group of
(E_{G})_{x_{0}}
which commutes with the action of
G
on
(E_{G})_{x_{0}}
. Let
H
be the subgroup of

Definition 4.2

For a Higgs bundle

, consider the Harder-Narasimhan filtration:

(0,0)=(E^{0},\varphi^{0})\subset(E^{1},\varphi^{1})\subset\cdots\subset(E^{\ell},\varphi^{\ell})=(E,\varphi)

\mu_{\max}(\text{resp. }\mu_{\min})((E,\varphi)):=\max(\text{resp. }\min)\{\mu(E^{i}/E^{i-1})\,|\,i=1,2,\dots,\ell\}.

Proposition 4.3

(cf.

Lemma 4.4

By Lemma

Suppose that

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
G

G^{\prime}
be reductive algebraic groups, and let

be a surjective homomorphism with
\mathrm{ker}f\subset Z(G)
, where

is the center of

. Let
(E,\varphi)
be an

-valued principal

-Higgs bundle and
(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
the

Then

is stable (resp. semistable) if and only if
(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
is stable (resp. semistable).

Proof.

Since
f:G\to G^{\prime}
is a surjective homomorphism with
\mathrm{ker}f\subset Z(G)
, it is easy to see that the induced morphism
\overline{f}:E/P\to E^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
is an isomorphism, where

are maximal parabolic subgroups of

respectively such that

. Then we have the induced isomorphism
\tilde{f}:E\times_{P,\mathrm{Ad}_{G}}(\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{p})\to E^{\prime}\times_{P^{\prime},\mathrm{Ad}_{G^{\prime}}}(\mathfrak{g}^{\prime}/\mathfrak{p}^{\prime})
, that is,
\tilde{f}:T_{E/P}\to T_{E^{\prime}/P^{\prime}}
. Further, since
df(\mathfrak{p})=\mathfrak{p}^{\prime}
,
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{P})\otimes L)
implies
\varphi^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}})\otimes L)
.

Assume that
(E,\varphi)
is not stable. Then there is a maximal parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and a reduction of the structure group
\sigma:U\to E/P
with
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E_{P})\otimes L)
such that
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E/P})\leq 0
. Let
\sigma^{\prime}=\overline{f}\circ\sigma
so that
\sigma^{\prime}:U\to E^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
is a reduction of the structure group. Then
\varphi^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}})\otimes L)
,

, which implies that
(E^{\prime},\varphi^{\prime})
is not stable.

Assume that

is stable. Let
\sigma^{\prime}:U\to E^{\prime}/P^{\prime}
be a reduction of the structure group with
\varphi^{\prime}\in H^{0}(X,\ad(E^{\prime}_{P^{\prime}})\otimes L)
. It is enough to show that
\sigma^{\prime}=\overline{f}\circ\sigma
for some reduction of the structure group

. But this is verified from the cohomological argument of the proof of

\ad(E_{G})
and
S
are reflexive,
{\cal H}om(\ad(E_{G}),S)
,
{\cal H}om(\ad(E_{G}),S\otimes L)
,
{\cal H}om(\ad(E_{G}),\ad(E_{G}))
,
{\cal H}om(S,\ad(E_{G}))
,
{\cal H}om(S,\ad(E_{G})\otimes L)
and
{\cal H}om(S,S)
are all normal by

, Proposition 5.5.21, 5.5.23]
, where a coherent sheaf
{\cal G}
on
X
is said to be normal if for every open set
V
in
X
and every analytic subset
A
of
V
of codimension at least
2
, the restriction
\Gamma(V,{\cal G})\to\Gamma(V\setminus A,{\cal G})
of sections is an isomorphism. So the following commutative diagram over
U

\textstyle{\ad(E_{G})|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\scriptstyle{\varphi}
\textstyle{S|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\varphi_{S}}
\textstyle{\ad(E_{G})\otimes L|_{U}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\cong}
\textstyle{S\otimes L|_{U}}

extends over

. Thus the above holomorphic decomposition of

extends to

, that is,
(\ad(E_{G}),\varphi)
is polystable.
∎

5.
Application: Moduli stack of
L
-valued Higgs G-sheaves and the perfect obstruction theory.

In this section, we will assume that

is a smooth projective variety. It is well known that the moduli stack of principal
G
-bundles on

\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)
is an algebraic stack. Let

be the universal
G
-bundle over
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)\times X
. Then we can also consider the universal adjoint bundle
\ad(U_{G}):=U_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
. Next, we consider the moduli stack of
L
-valued
G
-Higgs fields. We can consider the stack associated to the coherent sheaf
(p_{1})_{*}\left(\ad(U_{G})\otimes p_{2}^{*}L\right)
where
p_{1},p_{2}
are the projections from
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)\times X
to
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)

[
8
, Section 3]
and

, Section 3]
for the definition of the stack associated to the coherent sheaf.) We denote this stack by

.

Then, this stack is an algebraic stack, locally of finite presentation over

, with quasi-affine diagonal over
\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)
by

Proposition 5.1
.

When
G
is semisimple, the stability condition in Definition
2.1
is an open condition. That is,
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
is an open substack of the stack of
L
-valued
G
-Higgs fields,
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)
.

Proof.

We will first show that the stability condition for
G
-bundles is an open condition. When
\varphi=0
, the stability condition in Definition
2.1
is reduced to the stability of principal
G
-bundles.

Let
S
be a scheme of finite type over
\mathbb{C}
and let
E_{G}\to X\times S
be a flat family of principal
G
-bundles. We will show that the locus
S^{\text{st}}=\{s\in S\mid E_{G}|_{s}\text{ is stable}\}
is an open subset of
S
.
E_{G}|_{s}
is not stable if and only if there exists a maximal parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
and a reduction
\sigma:U\to E_{s}/P
on an open set
U
with
\text{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
, such that:

\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{s}/P})\leq 0.

On the other hand, the reduction
\sigma
to the parabolic subgroup
P
over
U
is equivalent to a vector subbundle
\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{U}
whose fibers are conjugate to the parabolic Lie algebra
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, where
\mathfrak{E}_{s}=(E_{G}|_{s})\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
by Lemma
5.2
.

Note that we have
\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{s}/P})\cong(\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{U})/\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}
. Since the group
G
is semisimple, the Killing form on the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
gives an isomorphism
\mathfrak{E}_{s}\cong\mathfrak{E}_{s}^{\vee}
. Thus
\mathrm{deg}\mathfrak{E}_{s}=0
. Therefore,
\mathrm{deg}\,\sigma^{*}(T_{E_{s}/P})\leq 0
if and only if
\mathrm{deg}\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}\geq 0
.

Because
X
is a smooth (hence normal) projective variety and
\text{codim}(X\setminus U)\geq 2
, the vector subbundle
\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}
extends uniquely to a saturated, torsion-free coherent subsheaf
{\cal F}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}
on
X
. Then we have
\mathrm{deg}({\cal F})=\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma})\geq 0
. Note that
{\cal F}
is saturated since
(\mathfrak{E}_{s}/{\cal F})|_{U}\cong(\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{U})/\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}
is locally free.

Therefore,
E_{G}|_{s}
is not stable if and only if its adjoint bundle
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
contains a saturated coherent subsheaf
\mathcal{F}
such that
\mathrm{deg}({\cal F})\geq 0
and over a big open set
U
(codimension of
X\setminus U\geq 2
) each fiber of
{\cal F}|_{U}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{U}
is a parabolic subalgebra conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}\subset\mathfrak{g}
.

By
[
20
, Theorem 1.1, Proposition 1.8]
,
for the family of vector bundles
\mathfrak{E}
over
X\times S
, flat over
S
, the set of all saturated subsheaves of
\mathfrak{E}
,
s\in S
whose degrees are bounded from below (
\mathrm{deg}\geq 0
) forms a bounded family, denoted by
Y^{\text{dst}}
.

Consequently, there are only finitely many possible Hilbert polynomials
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{k}
that the quotient sheaf
\mathcal{Q}=\mathfrak{E}_{s}/\mathcal{F}
can have, where
{\cal F}
is an element of the above family
Y^{\text{dst}}
. Because the set of possible Hilbert polynomials is finite, we can parameterize the entire search space algebraically. We construct the relative Quot scheme:

\mathcal{Q}uot:=\coprod_{i=1}^{k}\text{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})

which parameterizes all coherent quotients
q:\mathfrak{E}_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{Q}
possessing one of the destabilizing Hilbert polynomials
\Phi_{1},\dots,\Phi_{k}
. Because
X
is a projective variety,
the structure morphism

\pi:\mathcal{Q}uot\to S

is a projective (and therefore proper) morphism by
[
12
, Theorem 2.2.4]
.

On the other hand,
{\cal Q}uot
parametrizes all subsheaves
\mathcal{F}=\mathrm{ker}(q)
. We want to consider the sublocus of
{\cal Q}uot
where
\mathcal{F}
is isomorphic to the adjoint bundle of the parabolic reduction of
{\cal E}_{s}
over a big open set
U
. (That is, there exists a reduction
E_{P}
of
E_{G}|_{U}
for a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
such that
{\cal F}|_{U}\cong E_{P}\times_{P}\mathfrak{p}
.)

We consider the following parabolic type condition on
\mathcal{Q}uot
. Let
[0\to{\cal F}\to\mathfrak{E}_{s}\to{\cal Q}\to 0]\in{\cal Q}uot
be an element. The parabolic type condition is that over a big open set
U\subset X
,
{\cal F}|_{x}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x}\cong\mathfrak{g}
is a parabolic subalgebra conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}
.

Inside the Grassmannian bundle
\operatorname{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E})
, the locus of subspaces that are parabolic subalgebras conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}
forms a closed subvariety whose fiber over each
x\in U
is isomorphic to
G/P
. It can be shown as follows. For each closed point
s\in S
and a closed point
x\in U
we can consider an orbit map
G\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
defined by:

\varphi:g\mapsto g\mathfrak{p}g^{-1}.

Then we can easily see that the stabilizer of this orbit map is isomorphic to
P\subset G
. Therefore, there is an injective morphism
\overline{\varphi}:G/P\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
. Since
G/P
is a generalized flag variety, it is projective. Therefore,
\overline{\varphi}
is a closed immersion, so that the locus of subspaces that are parabolic subalgebras conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}
is a closed subvariety and we denote it by
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E})
. Note that it is in fact isomorphic to
E_{G}/P
.

We claim that the condition that the generic fiber of
\cal{F}
is contained in this
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E}_{s})
gives us a closed condition by Lemma
5.3
. Therefore, for each
\Phi_{i}
, there is a closed subscheme
{\cal Q}_{\mathfrak{p},\Phi_{i}}\subset\mathcal{Q}uot
parametrizing the subsheaves
{\cal F}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}
such that over a certain big open set
U\subset X
,
{\cal F}|_{U}
is a vector subbundle of
\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{U}
and
\mathfrak{F}_{\sigma}|_{x}\subset({\cal E}_{s})_{x}
is isomorphic to a parabolic subalgebra conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}
for all
x\in U
. Since
{\cal Q}uot
is a projective scheme, so is
{\cal Q}_{\mathfrak{p},\Phi_{i}}
. Therefore, the projection
\pi:{\cal Q}_{\mathfrak{p},\Phi_{i}}\to S
is also projective (thus it is proper). Hence
\pi({\cal Q}_{\mathfrak{p},\Phi_{i}})
is a closed subscheme of
S
.

Note that there are only finitely many conjugacy classes of parabolic subalgebras when we fix the maximal torus of
T\subset G
and the corresponding Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset\mathfrak{g}
, which corresponds to a finite subset of the set of simple roots. Let
\mathfrak{p}_{1},\dots,\mathfrak{p}_{\ell}
be the collection of all parabolic subalgebras.

Therefore,
S^{\text{dst}}:=\displaystyle\bigcup_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},\Phi_{i}}\pi({\cal Q}_{\mathfrak{p}_{j},\Phi_{i}})\subset S
is the locus where
\mathfrak{E}_{s}
admits a destabilizing subsheaf
{\cal F}
whose restriction on a big open set
U\subset X
is isomorphic to the adjoint of a principal
P
-bundle
E_{P}\subset E_{G}|_{U}
for a parabolic subgroup
P\subset G
by Lemma
5.2
. Therefore, by the above argument, the sublocus
S^{\text{st}}
of
S
where
E_{G}|_{s}
is stable is equal to an open subset
S\,\setminus\,S^{\text{dst}}
.

Next we will generalize the above proof to the general case, where the Higgs field is not necessarily zero. Over
X\times S
, we consider a flat family of Higgs
G
-sheaves
(E_{G},\varphi)
,
\varphi\in H^{0}(X\times S,\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)
where
\mathfrak{E}=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
. We have the induced family of adjoint vector bundles with
L
-valued Higgs fields,
(\mathfrak{E},\varphi)
,
\varphi:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}\otimes L
defined by taking Lie bracket on the right.

Then, in Lemma
5.3
, we will modify the subscheme
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}
by the sublocus satisfying the additional condition, that is,
\varphi({\cal F})\subset{\cal F}\otimes L\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}\otimes L
. This condition is interpreted as follows. From the universal sequence, we can consider the universal morphism, defined as the following composition:

v:\widetilde{{\cal F}}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\widetilde{\varphi}}}{{\longrightarrow}}q_{X\times S}^{*}(\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)\to q_{X\times S}^{*}(\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)/(\widetilde{{\cal F}}\otimes L)

where
q_{X\times S}:\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})\times_{S}(X\times S)\to(X\times S)
is the projection and
\widetilde{\varphi}
is the morphism by taking Lie bracket for the Higgs field on the right. Then we can consider a new subscheme
Z^{\prime}_{\mathfrak{p}}\subset Z_{\mathfrak{p}}
which is the zero locus of

, which is again closed. Then the above proof for the case when the Higgs field is zero, directly extends to the general case.
∎

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}:=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
be an adjoint of a principal
G
-bundle over
X
and let
{\cal F}\subset\mathfrak{E}
be a subsheaf whose fiber over any point
x
in a big open set
U\subset X
,
{\cal F}|_{x}
is a parabolic subalgebra of
\mathfrak{E}|_{x}\cong\mathfrak{g}
which is conjugate to
\mathfrak{p}
. Then there exists a
P
-bundle
E_{P}\subset E_{G}|_{U}
such that
{\cal F}|_{U}\cong E_{P}\times_{P}\mathfrak{p}
.

Proof.

By the assumption,
{\cal F}|_{U}
gives a section
s:U\to\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E})
where
r=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{p}
. Moreover, the image of

lies in
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E}|_{U})\cong(E_{G}/P)|_{U}
. This is a reduction of
E_{G}|_{U}
, where
E_{P}
is defined by the pull-back of the principal
P
-bundle
E_{G}\to E_{G}/P
. We can easily check that
{\cal F}|_{U}\cong E_{P}\times_{P}\mathfrak{p}
.
∎

Lemma 5.3
.

Inside the relative quot scheme
{\cal Q}=\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})
, consider the sublocus of quotients
q:\mathfrak{E}_{s}\to{\cal Q}
where the kernel
{\cal F}:=\mathrm{ker}\,q
satisfies the
parabolic condition
that over a big open set
U\subset X
,
{\cal F}|_{U}
is locally free and each fiber
{\cal F}_{x}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x}\cong\mathfrak{g}
is a Lie subalgebra which is a conjugate to a fixed parabolic subalgebra
\mathfrak{p}
for each
x\in U
. Let us denote this sublocus by

. Then
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})
.

Proof.

From the inclusion
{\cal F}=\mathrm{ker}\,q\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}
, there is a morphism
\wedge^{r}{\cal F}\subset\wedge^{r}\mathfrak{E}_{s}
, where
r=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{p}
. As we have seen above,
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
is a closed subscheme of
\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
and therefore, it is a closed subscheme in the Plücker embedding
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{r}\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
. Then,
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
is defined by an ideal
I
of the projective coordinate ring of
\mathbb{P}(\wedge^{r}\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
, and we may assume that

is generated by homogeneous elements of degrees up to

, namely

. In other words, there are sections
s_{i}\in\Hom(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(V),(I_{i})^{\vee})
where

such that
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E}_{s}|_{x})
is the zero locus of
s_{1},\dots,s_{d}
.

From this fiberwise observation, we conclude that
\mathrm{Gr}(\mathfrak{p},\mathfrak{E})\subset\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E})
is the zero locus of the sections
s_{i}\in\Hom(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}\mathfrak{E}),{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee})\cong H^{0}\left(\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E}),\Hom_{\mathrm{Gr}(r,\mathfrak{E})}(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}\mathfrak{E}),p^{*}{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee})\right)
.

Over
\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})\times_{S}(X\times S)
, there is a universal sequence

0\to\widetilde{{\cal F}}\to q_{X\times S}^{*}\mathfrak{E}\to\widetilde{Q}\to 0

where
q_{X\times S}:\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})\times_{S}(X\times S)\to(X\times S)
is the projection. Consider the composition of the morphism

\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}\widetilde{{\cal F}})\to\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}q_{X\times S}^{*}\mathfrak{E})\to q_{X\times S}^{*}{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee}

and denote it by
u_{i}
. The morphism
u_{i}
is an element of
H^{0}({\cal Q},\Hom_{\cal Q}(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}\widetilde{{\cal F}}),q_{X\times S}^{*}{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee}))
where
{\cal Q}:=\mathrm{Quot}_{X\times S/S}(\mathfrak{E},\Phi_{i})
. Let us show that
Z_{\mathfrak{p}}
is a common zero locus of
u_{1},\dots,u_{d}
. For
s\in S
, if
{\cal F}\subset\mathfrak{E}_{s}
satisfies the parabolic condition, then by the construction,
u_{i}|_{[\mathfrak{E}_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{E}_{s}/{\cal F}]}\in H^{0}(X,{\cal H}om(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}{\cal F}),{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee}))
vanishes over a big open set
U\subset X
. Since
{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee}
is locally free,
{\cal H}om(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}{\cal F}),{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee})
is torsion free. Hence
u_{i}|_{[\mathfrak{E}_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{E}_{s}/{\cal F}]}\in H^{0}(X,{\cal H}om(\mathrm{Sym}^{i}(\wedge^{r}{\cal F}),{\cal I}_{i}^{\vee}))=0
over the entire
X
.

Conversely, assume that an element
[\mathfrak{E}_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{E}_{s}/{\cal F}]\in{\cal Q}
satisfies the condition that
u_{i}|_{[\mathfrak{E}_{s}\twoheadrightarrow\mathfrak{E}_{s}/{\cal F}]}\equiv 0
for all

. Since
{\cal F}
is a subsheaf of the locally free sheaf

, it is torsion free. Therefore, there exists a big open set

where

is locally free. Then for any

is a parabolic subalgebra conjugate to

. Therefore,

is indeed the common zero set of the sections

and hence it is a closed subscheme of

.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of this section.

Proposition 5.4
.

For a semisimple Lie group
G
,
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
is a DM stack.

Proof.

By
[
1
, Theorem 4.6.4]
, it is enough to show that every point of
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
has a discrete and reduced stabilizer group. It is enough to show that the sheaf of infinitesimal automorphisms,
H^{-1}(T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}}|_{x})=0
for every point
x
of
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
where
T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}}
is the tangent complex. By the upper semi-continuity, it is enough to show that
H^{-1}(T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}}|_{x})=0
for every closed point
x
. Let
x={\cal E}=[E_{G},\varphi]
be an arbitrary closed point of
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
. But by Theorem
3.4
, the group of infinitesimal automorphisms
{\cal A}({\cal E})
is equal to
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0
since
G
is semisimple.
∎

Furthermore, when
G
is semisimple,
X
is a smooth projective surface, and
L=K_{X}
, we will show that
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
is equipped with a perfect obstruction theory. By
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
, it is known that there is a natural morphism (called deformation-obstruction theory)
\phi_{B}:T_{\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)}\to E_{B}:=R(p_{1})_{*}\ad(U_{G})[1]
such that
H^{0}(\phi_{B})
is an isomorphism and
H^{1}(\phi_{B})
is injective. Moreover, by
[
8
, Proposition 2.5, Proposition 3.1]
, it is also known that there is a relative perfect obstruction theory
\phi_{H/B}:T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)/\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)}\to E_{H/B}:=R(p_{H})_{*}\pi^{*}(\ad(U_{G})\otimes(p_{2})^{*}K_{X})
where
p_{H}:\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)\times X\to\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)
is the projection and
\pi:\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)\times X\to\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)\times X
is the forgetful morphism.

On the other hand, there is a universal complex

U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}:=\left[\pi^{*}\ad(U_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle U}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi^{*}(\ad(U_{G})\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X})\right]

over
\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)\times X
, where the morphism
U
is obtained by taking the Lie bracket on the right of the universal section of
\pi^{*}((p_{1})_{*}(\ad(U_{G})\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X}))
. Then there is a distinguished triangle

\pi^{*}\ad(U_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle U}}{{\longrightarrow}}\pi^{*}(\ad(U_{G})\otimes(p_{2})^{*}K_{X})\to U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}[1]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}

Therefore, by taking
R(p_{H})_{*}
, we have the distinguished triangle:

q^{*}E_{B}[-1]\to E_{H/B}\to E_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}

where
q:\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)\to\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)
is the forgetful morphism and
E_{H}:=R(p_{H})_{*}(U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}[1])
. Then by the axioms of triangulated categories, there is a morphism
\phi_{H}:T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)}\to E_{H}
which completes the morphism between distinguished triangles, from

\left[q^{*}T_{\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)}[-1]\to T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)/\mathrm{Bun}_{G}(X)}\to T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}\right]
to
\left[q^{*}E_{B}[-1]\to E_{H/B}\to E_{H}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle+1}}{{\longrightarrow}}\right]
.
Then, by a simple direct diagram chase, we can check that
H^{0}(\phi_{H})
is an isomorphism and
H^{1}(\phi_{H})
is injective.

Over the stable locus
\mathrm{Higgs}^{s}_{K_{X}}(X)
, we will show that
\phi_{H}
is a perfect obstruction theory. By the above argument, it is enough to show that
E_{H}=R(p_{H})_{*}(U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}[1])
is perfect and its cohomology is supported in degrees
0,1
. Since
\phi_{B}
and
\phi_{H/B}
are perfect obstruction theories,
\phi_{H}
is also perfect by the parallel argument as in
[
16
, Construction 3.13]
. Since the cohomologies of
E_{B}[-1]
and
E_{H/B}
are both supported in degrees
0,1,2
, the cohomologies of
E_{H}
are supported in degrees
-1,0,1,2
.

Over a closed point
x=[(E_{G},\varphi)]\in\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)
, we have

E_{H}|_{x}=R(p_{x})_{*}(\,(\ad(E_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[-,\varphi]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ad(E_{G})\otimes K_{X})[1]\,)

where
p_{x}:\{x\}\times X\to\{x\}
is the projection. Therefore we have
H^{-1}(E_{H}|_{x})\cong{\cal A}({\cal E})=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})=0
by Theorem
3.4
when
G
is semisimple. Moreover, we have

(\ad(E_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[-,\varphi]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ad(E_{G})\otimes K_{X})\simeq((\ad(E_{G})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle[-,\varphi]}}{{\longrightarrow}}\ad(E_{G})\otimes K_{X})^{\vee}\otimes K_{X})[-1]

Therefore, by Serre duality, we have

H^{2}(E_{H}|_{x})\cong H^{-1}(E_{H}|_{x})^{\vee}=0.

Then, parallel to the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 4.2]
, we conclude that
E_{H}
is locally represented by a complex of vector bundles
[F^{0}\to F^{1}]
in degrees
0
and
1
.

Moreover, we have
U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}\cong(U_{\mathrm{Higgs}}[1])^{\vee}\otimes p_{2}^{*}K_{X}
.
Then the relative Grothendieck-Verdier duality tells us

E_{H}\cong(E_{H})^{\vee}[-1]

in the bounded derived category
D^{b}(\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)^{s})
. Therefore,
E_{H}
is a symmetric perfect obstruction theory.

Theorem 5.5
.

When the reductive group
G
is semisimple and
L=K_{X}
, then the moduli stack of stable
K_{X}
-valued
G
-Higgs bundles
\mathrm{Higgs}_{L}(X)^{s}
is a DM stack equipped with the symmetric perfect obstruction theory
\phi_{H}:T_{\mathrm{Higgs}_{K_{X}}(X)^{s}}\to E_{H}
.

6.
Appendix

Here we will show that a Chern connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
on
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
is always induced from a connection
D_{E}
on
E_{G}
.

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
E_{G}
be a principal
G
bundle and let
\mathfrak{E}:=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
be the adjoint bundle. Assume that a connection
D
on
\mathfrak{E}
satisfies the Leibniz rule relative to the Lie bracket. That is, for any two sections
s,t\in\Gamma(\mathfrak{E})
,
D
satisfies:

D[s,t]=[Ds,t]+[s,Dt].

Moreover, we assume that the restriction of
D
to
\mathfrak{Z}:=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
is
d
, that is,
D|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
. (Note that
\mathfrak{Z}
is canonically isomorphic to the trivial bundle
X\times\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.)
D|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
means
D|_{\mathfrak{Z}}(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})=(df_{1},\dots,df_{r})
where
r=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and
(f_{1},\dots,f_{r})
is a (local) section of
X\times\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
.)

Then the connection
D
on
\mathfrak{E}
is induced from a connection on
E_{G}
compatible with the holomorphic structure.

Proof.

By Remark
6.3
, we can choose a Chern connection
D_{0}
and it induces a connection
D_{\mathfrak{E},0}
on
\mathfrak{E}
. Thus it satisfies the above Leibniz rule by Lemma
6.4
, and
D
satisfies the Leibniz rule by assumption.

Therefore,
\alpha:=D-D_{\mathfrak{E},0}\in\Gamma\left(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes{\cal E}nd(\mathfrak{E})\right)
is a derivation-valued 1-form, that is, for any local sections
s,t
of
\mathfrak{E}
, it satisfies the Leibniz rule:

\alpha([s,t])=[\alpha(s),t]+[s,\alpha(t)].

Note that the space of derivations
Der(\mathfrak{g})
is isomorphic to
Der(\mathfrak{g}_{ss})\oplus\End(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))
where
\mathfrak{g}_{ss}:=[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]
, and it is known that
Der(\mathfrak{g}_{ss})
is isomorphic to the space of inner derivations. Thus we have
Der(\mathfrak{g}_{ss})\cong\mathfrak{g}_{ss}
and
Der(\mathfrak{g})\cong Der(\mathfrak{g}_{ss})\oplus\End(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}))
. Furthermore we have

Der(\mathfrak{E})\cong(E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}_{ss})\oplus\End(\mathfrak{Z})

where
\mathfrak{Z}:=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, which is in fact isomorphic to the trivial bundle
X\times\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. Therefore (local) sections of
\mathfrak{Z}
are of the form
(f_{1},\dots f_{r})
where
r=\mathrm{dim}_{\mathbb{C}}\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
and
f_{1},\dots,f_{r}
are (local) functions on
X
.

Since
D_{\mathfrak{E},0}
is a connection induced from
D_{0}
, by the definition of the induced connection (
6.2
),
D_{\mathfrak{E},0}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
. Moreover,
D|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
by the assumption. Therefore
\alpha|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d-d=0
, and thus we have
\alpha\in\Gamma\left(X,\Omega^{1}_{X}\otimes(E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}_{\ad})\right)
.

Let
\ad:\mathfrak{g}\to\End(\mathfrak{g})
be the morphism sending
g\in\mathfrak{g}
to
[g,-]
and let
\mathfrak{g}_{\ad}:=\text{Im}(\ad)\cong\mathfrak{g}/\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
. On the other hand, since
G
is reductive, we have a decomposition

\mathfrak{g}\cong\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus[\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{g}]=\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{ss}.

Thus we have
\mathfrak{g}_{\ad}\cong\mathfrak{g}_{ss}
and we have a split exact sequence:

0\to\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})\to\mathfrak{g}\xrightarrow{\ad}\mathfrak{g}_{ss}\to 0.

Since the above exact sequence splits, there is a section
s:\mathfrak{g}_{ss}\to\mathfrak{g}
such that
\ad\circ s=\id_{\mathfrak{g}_{ss}}
. Then the section
s
induces the injective morphism of vector bundles:

Der(\mathfrak{E})\cong\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes(E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}_{\ad})\hookrightarrow\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes(E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g})=\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{E}.

Recall that
\alpha\in\Gamma(Der(\mathfrak{E}))
. Let
\widetilde{\alpha}
be the image of
\alpha
in
\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{E})
. Then we have
\ad(\widetilde{\alpha})=\alpha
. Therefore, if we define the new connection
D_{E}:=D_{0}+\widetilde{\alpha}
, it induces the connection
D_{\mathfrak{E},0}+\alpha=D
on
\mathfrak{E}
. Moreover, since
\alpha
,
\widetilde{\alpha}
, and
D_{0}
are compatible with the holomorphic structure, so is
D_{E}=D_{0}+\widetilde{\alpha}
.

∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Let
(E_{G},\varphi)
be an
L
-valued
G
-Higgs bundle. The Chern connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
on
\mathfrak{E}=\ad(E_{G})
induced by the Hermitian metric
h_{\mathfrak{E}}
satisfying the quiver vortex equation (
2.1
) is always induced from a connection
D_{E}
on
E_{G}
.

Proof.

By Proposition
6.1
, it suffices to show that
D_{\mathfrak{E}}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
and
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
satisfies the Leibniz rule relative to the Lie bracket.

We first show that
D_{\mathfrak{E}}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d
. By (
2.1
) combined with the fact that
\tau=0
(since
\mathrm{deg}(\mathfrak{E})=0
), we have

i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{E}}^{1,1}+[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=0\in\End(\mathfrak{E}).

Since
\varphi\in\Hom(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)
, we observe that
[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=0
.

i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{E}}^{1,1}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=0.

Then, parallel to (
3.1
), for any
s\in H^{0}(X,\mathfrak{Z})
, we have

2\Delta_{d}|s|^{2}=|D_{\mathfrak{Z}}s|^{2}-\langle i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{Z}}s,s\rangle=|D_{\mathfrak{Z}}s|^{2}\geq 0

where
D_{\mathfrak{Z}}:=D_{\mathfrak{E}}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}
,
F_{\mathfrak{Z}}:=F_{\mathfrak{E}}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}
. Thus
|s|^{2}
is a subharmonic function on the compact manifold
X
, therefore
|s|^{2}
is a constant by the maximum principle. Hence
\Delta_{d}(|s|^{2})=0
and therefore
|D_{\mathfrak{Z}}s|^{2}=0
,
D_{\mathfrak{Z}}s=0
. Since
\mathfrak{Z}
is isomorphic to a trivial bundle
X\times\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g})
, we have constant sections
e_{1},\dots,e_{r}
, which form a basis on each fiber. Since
D_{\mathfrak{Z}}(e_{1})=\dots=D_{\mathfrak{Z}}(e_{r})=0
, for any (local) section of the form
s=f_{1}e_{1}+\dots+f_{r}e_{r}
, we have
D_{\mathfrak{Z}}(s)=df_{1}e_{1}+\dots+df_{r}e_{r}
. Therefore, we also obtain
D_{\mathfrak{E}}|_{\mathfrak{Z}}=D_{\mathfrak{Z}}=d.

Next we show that
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
satisfies the Leibniz rule relative to the Lie bracket. Let
{\cal W}:={\cal H}om(\mathfrak{E}\otimes\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{E})
. Let the Lie bracket
B:\mathfrak{E}\otimes\mathfrak{E}\xrightarrow{[-,-]}\mathfrak{E}
be the morphism defined by the Lie bracket. Then
B
is a holomorphic (global) section of
{\cal W}
. The connection
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
induces a connection
D_{\cal W}
in a natural way. For (local) sections
s,t
of
\mathfrak{E}
, we have

(D_{\mathcal{W}}B)(s\otimes t)=D_{\mathfrak{E}}\big(B(s\otimes t)\big)-B(D_{\mathfrak{E}}s\otimes t)-B(s\otimes D_{\mathfrak{E}}t).

By substituting
B(s\otimes t)=[s,t]
, we have

(D_{\mathcal{W}}B)(s\otimes t)=D_{\mathfrak{E}}([s,t])-[D_{\mathfrak{E}}s,t]-[s,D_{\mathfrak{E}}t].

Therefore, the condition that
D_{\mathfrak{E}}
satisfies the Leibniz rule relative to the Lie bracket is equivalent to
D_{\cal W}B=0
.

Since
B
is a holomorphic section of
{\cal W}
, by (
3.1
), we have

(6.1)

\displaystyle 2\Delta_{d}(|B|^{2})=|D_{\mathcal{W}}B|^{2}-\langle(i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathcal{W}})B,B\rangle.

Note that we have
i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}=-[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]
. Similar to the proof of Lemma
3.2
(and since
F_{\cal W}=(D_{{\cal W}})^{2}
), we have

\displaystyle(i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{{\cal W}}B)(s\otimes t)

\displaystyle=i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}(B(s\otimes t))-B(i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}s\otimes t)-B(s\otimes i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}t)

\displaystyle=i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}([s,t])-[i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}s,t]-[s,i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}t].

We will show that the last term vanishes. Recall the equation
i\Lambda_{\omega}F_{\mathfrak{E}}^{1,1}+[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]=0
, and recall that
\varphi:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}\otimes L
is defined by an abuse of notation,
\varphi=[-,\varphi]\in\Hom(\mathfrak{E},\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)
, which is the morphism given by the adjoint action of the Higgs field
\varphi\in H^{0}(X,\mathfrak{E}\otimes L)
. Therefore, by the Jacobi identity, we have

i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}([s,t])-[i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}s,t]-[s,i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{\mathfrak{E}}t]=[\psi,[s,t]]-[[\psi,s],t]-[s,[\psi,t]]=0

where
\psi:=-[\varphi,\varphi^{*_{h}}]
. Hence we obtain
i\Lambda_{\omega}F^{1,1}_{{\cal W}}B=0
.

Then, by (
6.1
), we have
\Delta_{d}(|B|^{2})=|D_{\cal W}B|^{2}\geq 0
. Therefore, by the maximum principle for subharmonic functions on the compact manifold
X
,
|B|^{2}
is a constant so that
\Delta_{d}(|B|^{2})=|D_{\cal W}B|^{2}=0
,
D_{\cal W}B=0
.
∎

Remark 6.3
.

Let
X
be a compact Kähler manifold and let
E_{G}
be a holomorphic principal
G
-bundle, where
G
is a reductive group. Let
K\subset G
be a maximal compact subgroup. Then, by
[
6
, Section 2]
, any smooth reduction of the structure group
E_{K}\subset E_{G}
induces a unique connection on
E_{G}
compatible with the holomorphic structure (Chern connection).

Moreover, the reduction of structure group
E_{K}\subset E_{G}
is equivalent to a section of the fiber bundle
E_{G}/K\to X
. But since
G/K
is contractible, this section always exists. In summary, we can always find a connection on
E_{G}
compatible with the holomorphic structure.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
\nabla
be a connection on the principal
G
-bundle
E_{G}\to X
and let
\nabla_{ad}
be the induced connection on
\mathfrak{E}=E_{G}\times_{G}\mathfrak{g}
. Then
\nabla_{ad}
satisfies the Leibniz rule.

Proof.

Note that the connection
\nabla
is represented by a connection 1-form
\omega\in\Omega^{1}(E_{G},\mathfrak{g})
. We also note that every (local) section
s\in\Gamma(U,\mathfrak{E})
for an open subset
U\subset X
corresponding to a smooth function
f_{s}:E_{G}\to\mathfrak{g}
satisfies the
G
-equivariance condition:

f_{s}(p\cdot g)=\text{Ad}(g^{-1})\big(f_{s}(p)\big)

for any
p\in E_{G}|_{U}
and
g\in G
(
[
15
, Chapter II, Example 5.2]
). Then the induced connection
\nabla_{ad}
on
\mathfrak{E}
is defined by

(6.2)

\displaystyle\nabla_{ad}(f_{s}):=df_{s}+[\omega,f_{s}].

(See
[
15
, Lemma in page 115]
and
[
15
, Chapter II, Lemma 1 (1)]
for details.) Note that in fact
\nabla_{ad}(f_{s})\in\Omega^{1}(E_{G}|_{U},\mathfrak{g})
is horizontal, hence it becomes an element of
\Gamma(X,\Omega_{X}^{1}\otimes\mathfrak{E})
.

Now let
f_{1},f_{2}
be
G
-equivariant functions corresponding to the local sections
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
of
\mathfrak{E}
. Then the section
[s_{1},s_{2}]
corresponds to the pointwise Lie bracket of the functions
[f_{1},f_{2}]
. Then we have

\nabla_{ad}([f_{1},f_{2}])=d([f_{1},f_{2}])+[\omega,[f_{1},f_{2}]].

Then, by the product rule for differentiation, we have

d([f_{1},f_{2}])=[df_{1},f_{2}]+[f_{1},df_{2}].

Moreover, by the Jacobi identity, we have:

[\omega,[f_{1},f_{2}]]=[[\omega,f_{1}],f_{2}]+[f_{1},[\omega,f_{2}]].

Therefore, the induced connection
\nabla_{ad}=d+[\omega,-]
also satisfies the Leibniz rule.

∎
