Abstract
. The study of permutation automorphism groups of cyclic codes is a central topic in algebraic coding theory. A cyclic code over 𝔽 q \mathbb{F}_{q} is called irreducible if its check polynomial is irreducible over 𝔽 q {\mathbb{F}}_{q} . Such a code is standard if its permutation automorphism group is equal to the group generated by the cyclic shift and the Frobenius automorphism, and non-standard otherwise. In this paper, we give a complete classification of all non-standard non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes, using the classification of finite simple groups. Our result shows that, apart from a small number of explicit exceptional families and their descendants under certain secondary constructions, every non-degenerate irreducible cyclic code is standard, and up to four explicit exceptions, every degenerate cyclic code is non-standard. This classification has several consequences. First, it yields a general description of non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups, extending the earlier work of Brison and Nogueira; secondly it establishes the Schmidt-White conjecture for all non-standard irreducible cyclic codes. Moreover, our results provide strong evidence in support of the conjecture of Berger and Charpin that almost all cyclic codes are standard. Keywords : irreducible cyclic codes, permutation group, non-standard, Aschbacher’s theorem, linear recurring sequence subgroups. MSC (2020) : 94B15, 20B25, 05E20, 05E18

Let

-linear subspace of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
; its

, denoted by

are called the

of

. In this paper, we are dealing with cyclic codes, so as usual, we enumerate the coordinate positions with

and identify them with the elements of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, the (additive) group of integers modulo

. The symmetric group

, considered as the group of all permutations on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
, acts on the vectors of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}
by permuting the coordinate positions, that is, for all

and for all
\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{0},v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1})\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{n}

\boldsymbol{v}^{\pi}=(v_{\pi^{-1}(0)},\,\ldots,\,v_{\pi^{-1}(n-1)})

of

Let

-ary linear code of length

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)

{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]/(x^{n}-1)

In this paper, when dealing with cyclic codes, we will assume that

; note that, as a consequence,

has no multiple zeros. Observe that any ideal of

. We will write

-ary cyclic code of length

. Moreover, we will write

]

-ary cyclic code

]
*Section 6.1,
[

]
*Corollary 5.19. In addition, if

generated by

and

*Corollary 5.14 (for more details, see Section

So if

is a non-degenerate irreducible cyclic code, then

always contains the group of standard permutation automorphisms generated by the cyclic shift

and the Frobenius map

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a non-degenerate irreducible cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where we assume that
\gcd(n,q)=1
. If

is non-standard, then

is one of the cyclic codes in Examples

-
3.5
, or it can be obtained from one of the codes in Examples

-

-

.

We remark that, in general, a degenerate cyclic code

of length

satisfies

, with exactly
four (known) exceptions, cf. Lemma

. We further remark that we also believe that if

is an arbitrary non-degenerate cyclic code of length

, even without the condition that

, then in general

is standard, that is,

In the remainder of this section, we will focus on
q
-ary non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes of length
n
, so with a check polynomial of order

. As was observed in

]
*Proposition 5.19, all

-ary non-degenerate irreducible codes of length

is non-standard. We remark that this is the approach taken in

,

]

,
28
]
really meant non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes.

Let

be the multiplicative subgroup of order

in some extension field

of

, where

and

. Note that

can be arranged in a non-cyclic way such that the corresponding periodic sequence of period

is a linear recurring sequence over

and has an irreducible characteristic polynomial. In that case, we call

a non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroup of

. Brison and Nogueira investigated such subgroups in a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
. By
[

]
*Corollary 4.13, a non-degenerate irreducible cyclic code of length

over

is non-standard if and only if

is a non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroup, which occurs if and only if the pair
(n,q)
is non-standard. Here, note that the check polynomials of non-degenerate
q
-ary irreducible codes of length

are precisely the minimal polynomials of the generators of

. As a consequence of Theorem

, we have generalized the results of Brison and Nogueira to a great extent, see Section

There has been extensive work on the determination of the weight distributions of irreducible cyclic codes due to real-world applications, cf.
[
18
,
45
,
38
,
20
]
. This problem is extremely difficult in general, so codes with few weights are the focus of study in the literature. Two-weight irreducible cyclic codes are of particular interest, due to their connections with two-intersection sets in projective spaces, strongly regular Cayley graphs, and subdifference sets of the Singer difference set, cf.
[
51
]
. Schmidt and White gave a necessary and sufficient numerical condition for an irreducible cyclic code to have at most two nonzero weights in
[

In the same paper, they made the conjecture that all two-weight irreducible cyclic codes are either subfield codes, semiprimitive codes, or one of the eleven explicit codes with relatively small parameters. We refer to
[
54
]
for some remarks by Vega on the conjecture, and for definitions of the above terms. As an application of our main theorem, in Section

, we confirm the Schmidt-White conjecture for non-standard irreducible cyclic codes by using Theorem
1.1
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we first establish our notation, including some group-theoretic notation, and some important facts that we use throughout this paper. Then we justify the remark about degenerate cyclic codes following Theorem

We also consider the asymptotic behavior of the density of non-standard pairs in connection with the Berger-Charpin conjecture. We conclude this paper with some remarks on related open problems and future research directions.

Lemma 2.2

Let

Let

, where

2.3.
Standard and non-standard non-degenerate cyclic codes

We now introduce some notation that will be used frequently throughout this paper.

Definition 2.3
.

Assume that
\gcd(n,q)=1
, and let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
be the smallest positive integer such that
n
divides
q^{m}-1
. Let
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
be the multiplicative subgroup of order
n
of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, and let
\xi
be a generator of
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
. Let
\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
be the group of all invertible
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear transformations of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and let
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
be the set-wise stabilizer of
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
in
\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
be the subgroup of
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
generated by
\sigma
and
\psi
, where
\sigma(x)=\xi x
and
\psi(x)=x^{q}
for
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. Note that
|{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)|=nm
. In the remainder of this paper, we always use
n_{0}
to denote
\gcd(n,q-1)
, that is,
n_{0}:=\gcd(n,q-1)
.

We adopt the notation of Definition
2.3
. Let
C
be a
q
-ary cyclic code of length
n
. We can identify the coordinate positions
0,1,\ldots,n-1
in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
with
1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{n-1}
, respectively, so that the two definitions of
\sigma
coincide. By a slight abuse of notation, we also use
\psi
to denote the corresponding permutation of coordinate positions
i\mapsto qi
on
{\mathbb{Z}}_{n}
induced by the action of
\psi
. Then
\psi
is also in
\textup{PAut}(C)
, cf.
[
48
, Corollary 5.19]
. More generally, in
[
28
, Theorem 5.28]
it is shown that if
C
has check polynomial
h(x)
, then the map
\mu_{t}:x\mapsto tx
in
\textup{AG}(n)
is in
\textup{PAut}(C)
if and only if the map
\mu_{t}:x\mapsto x^{t}
with
\gcd(n,t)=1
permutes the zeros of
h(x)
; note that
\mu_{q}=\psi
. In what follows, we let
\textup{PAut}_{\rm st}(C):=\textup{PAut}(C)\cap\textup{AG}(n)
. We will say that a
cyclic code
C
is
standard
if
\textup{PAut}(C)=\textup{PAut}_{\rm st}(C)
, that is, if
\textup{PAut}(C)
is a subgroup of
\textup{AG}(n)
. It is shown in
[
28
, Lemma 5.30]
that every cyclic code
C
of length
n\leq 3
has
\textup{PAut}(C)\subseteq\textup{AG}(n)
, so every cyclic code of length at most
3
is standard.

An
irreducible
cyclic code is a code
C=C_{n,h,q}
whose check polynomial
h(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, see, e.g.,
[
41
, Definition 6.1.6]
,
[
51
, Definition 2.2]
. By
[
28
, Corollary 5.14]
,

(2.1)

\textup{PAut}_{\textup{st}}(C)=\langle\sigma,\psi\rangle.

2.4.
Degenerate cyclic codes

For the sake of completeness, we investigate which degenerate cyclic codes only possess permutation automorphisms contained in
\langle\sigma,\psi\rangle
or only in
\textup{AG}(n)
. The result is as follows.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
C=C_{n,h,q}
be the
q
-ary cyclic code of length
n
with check polynomial
h(x)
. Let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
, let
m^{\prime}=\deg(h)
, and let
n^{\prime}=\textup{ord}(h)

(
the least positive integer such that
h(x)\mid(x^{n^{\prime}}-1)
)
. Then
n^{\prime}\mid n
and
{\rm dim}(C)=m^{\prime}
. If
n^{\prime}<n
, then
C
is periodic and consists of all the codewords of the form
(\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime},\ldots,\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime})
with
\boldsymbol{c}^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}=C_{h,q}
, the
q
-ary non-degenerate cyclic code of length
n^{\prime}
with check polynomial
h(x)
. Moreover, with
k:=n/n^{\prime}
,

\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{k}\wr\textup{PAut}(C^{\prime}),

so that
|\textup{PAut}(C)|=(k!)^{n^{\prime}}|\textup{PAut}(C^{\prime})|
.
Finally,
C
is non-standard, that is,
\textup{PAut}(C)\nsubseteq\textup{AG}(n)
, with the exception of the following four cases:

(1)

n=2
,
n^{\prime}=1
,
h(x)=x-1
,
q
is odd, and
C
is the repetition code of length 2; here
\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{2}=\textup{AG}(2)=\langle\sigma,\psi\rangle
.

(2)

n=3
,
n^{\prime}=1
,
h(x)=x-1
,
q\equiv 1,2\bmod 3
, and
C
is the repetition code of length 3; here
\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{3}\cong\textup{AG}(3)
.

(3)

n=4
,
n^{\prime}=2
,
h(x)=x+1
,
q
is odd, and
C
is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-span of
(1,-1,1,-1)
; here
\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{2}\wr S_{2}\cong\textup{AG}(4)
.

(4)

n=4
,
n^{\prime}=2
,
h(x)=x^{2}-1
,
q
is odd, and
C={\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{2}
; here
\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{2}\wr S_{2}\cong\textup{AG}(4)
.

Proof.

Since
h(x)\mid(x^{n}-1)
, we have
n^{\prime}\mid n
and
\dim C=\deg h=m^{\prime}
. If
n^{\prime}<n
, so
k=n/n^{\prime}\geq 2
, then by
[
28
, Lemma 5.22 and Corollary 5.24]
,

C=\{(\mathbf{c}^{\prime},\ldots,\mathbf{c}^{\prime}):\ \mathbf{c}^{\prime}\in C^{\prime}\},\qquad\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{k}^{n^{\prime}}\rtimes\textup{PAut}(C^{\prime}),\qquad C^{\prime}=C_{h,q}.

Thus
|\textup{PAut}(C)|=(k!)^{n^{\prime}}|\textup{PAut}(C^{\prime})|\geq(k!)^{n^{\prime}}\cdot n^{\prime}\cdot\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)\geq(k!)^{n^{\prime}}n^{\prime}
.

On the other hand,
|\textup{AG}(n)|=n\varphi(n)=kn^{\prime}\varphi(kn^{\prime})<(kn^{\prime})^{2}
. Now since
k\geq 2
, we have
(k!)^{n^{\prime}-2}\geq n^{\prime}
for
n^{\prime}\geq 4
. Using this, a routine verification shows that
the inequality
(k!)^{n^{\prime}}n^{\prime}\leq(kn^{\prime})^{2}
cannot occur when
n^{\prime}\geq 4
, and can only occur when
k\leq 3
if
n^{\prime}=1
and
k\leq 2
if
n^{\prime}\in\{2,3\}
. In particular, this leaves the possibilities
(n^{\prime},k)\in\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,2),(3,2)\}
.
However, the case
(n^{\prime},k)=(3,2)
is impossible, since then
n=6
and

|\textup{PAut}(C)|\geq(2!)^{3}\cdot 3=24>|\textup{AG}(6)|=12
.
Hence
|\textup{PAut}(C)|\leq|\textup{AG}(n)|
can occur only for

(n^{\prime},k)\in\{(1,2),(1,3),(2,2)\}
. We handle these three exceptional cases one-by-one. The condition on
q
is obtained from the assumption
\gcd(n,q)=1
.
If
(n^{\prime},k)=(1,2)
, then
n=2
,
h(x)=x-1
.
If
(n^{\prime},k)=(1,3)
, then
n=3
,
h(x)=x-1
, yielding (2).
If
(n^{\prime},k)=(2,2)
, then
n=4
,
h(x)\in\{x+1,x^{2}-1\}
, yielding (3) and (4). This completes the proof.

∎

As a consequence of Lemma
2.4
, degenerate cyclic codes of length
n
are periodic and possess permutation automorphisms outside
\textup{AG}(n)
, with only a few exceptions. By the above theorem, the automorphism group of such a code is completely determined by the smaller code that it extends periodically.

So in the remainder of this paper, we assume that the
q
-ary irreducible cyclic codes of length
n
under discussion are
non-degenerate
, so with a check polynomial of order
n
, and have dimension
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
.
For convenience, we use the abbreviations
SIC codes
(resp.,
NSIC codes
) for standard (resp., non-standard) non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes, respectively.

2.5.
Non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes

In the rest of this paper, we will mostly consider non-degenerate
q
-ary irreducible cyclic codes
C
of length
n
. If
C
is such a code, then by
[
28
, Theorem 5.15]
,
\textup{PAut}(C)
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
and
\textup{PAut}_{\textup{st}}(C)
is isomorphic to
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
under our identification of coordinate positions with the elements of
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
. In particular, each permutation in
\textup{PAut}(C)
corresponds to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear transformation of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
that stabilizes
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
. If
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)\varsubsetneqq{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
, then
C
is a
non-standard
irreducible cyclic code, and
C
is
standard
otherwise. Note that by the above, this definition coincides with the definition given earlier for general irreducible cyclic codes.

Irreducible cyclic codes can be represented as trace codes. We give the detailed explanations below. Let
C
be an irreducible cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
. Let
h(x)
be the check polynomial of
C
, and let
m^{\prime}:=\deg(h)
and
n^{\prime}=\textup{ord}(h)
. Then
n^{\prime}\mid n
, and since
h(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, we have
m^{\prime}=\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)={\rm dim}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(C)
, and hence
m^{\prime}\mid m
. Let
\beta\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}
be such that
\beta^{-1}
is a root of
h(x)
.
Note that
\beta
has order
n^{\prime}
. Then by
[
48
, Theorem 5.25]
or
[
14
, Section 7.2]
, the code
C
can be represented as a trace code:

(2.2)

\boldsymbol{c}_{\alpha}:=\big(\textup{Tr}(\alpha),\textup{Tr}(\alpha\beta),\ldots,\textup{Tr}(\alpha\beta^{n-1})\big)\mid\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}},

where
\textup{Tr}=\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
is the trace function from
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
The next lemma should be compared to
[
28
, Corollary 5.24]
.

Lemma 2.5
.

With the above notation, suppose that
n^{\prime}<n
. Then the codewords of
C=C_{n,h,q}
are periodic; more precisely, for every
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}
, we have
c_{\alpha}=({c_{\alpha}^{\prime},c_{\alpha}^{\prime},\ldots,c_{\alpha}^{\prime}})
(repeated
n/n^{\prime}
times),
where
c_{\alpha}^{\prime}
is a codeword of the
q
-ary irreducible cyclic code
C^{\prime}=C_{h,q}
of length
n^{\prime}
and dimension
m^{\prime}
with check polynomial
h(x)
.

Proof.

By the above arguments, the
q
-ary code
C^{\prime}
of length
n^{\prime}
and check polynomial
h(x)
has codewords
c^{\prime}_{\alpha}=(\textup{Tr}(\alpha),\textup{Tr}(\alpha\beta),\ldots,\textup{Tr}(\alpha\beta^{n^{\prime}-1}))
for
\alpha\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}
. Since
\beta
has order
n^{\prime}
, the claim immediately follows from (
2.2
).
∎

3.
Examples of non-standard non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes

We list some known examples of non-standard non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes (NSIC codes) below. In Examples
3.1
-
3.4
,
\textup{PAut}(C)
is nonsolvable, and it is worth noting that the extension ovoid codes in Example
3.3
have not been identified non-standard before.

Example 3.1

(Duals of repetition codes)
.

[
11
, Theorem 5.2]
,
[
28
, Example 3]

Let
n
be a prime such that
n\geq 5
, and let
p
be a prime such that
p
has order
m=n-1
modulo
n
, i.e.,
\textup{ord}_{n}(p)=m
. Let
C
be the dual code of a repetition code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
. Then
C
is an NSIC code with
\textup{PAut}(C)=S_{n}
.

Example 3.2

(Duals of primitive BCH codes of designed distance 2)
.

[
11
, Theorem 4.3]
,
[
28
, Example 1]

Let
n=q^{m}-1
, where
m\geq 2
and
(m,q)\neq(2,2)
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}={\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}^{*}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)=\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
. The corresponding irreducible cyclic code
C
of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-standard, and it is permutation equivalent to the dual of a primitive narrow-sense BCH code of designed distance
2
, cf.
[
43
, Chapter 9.1]
.

Example 3.3

(Extension ovoid codes)
.

Let
n=(q-1)(q^{2}+1)
, so that
m=4
. Let
\gamma
be a nonzero element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}
such that
\gamma+\gamma^{q}=0
, and define
Q(x)=\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{2}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\gamma x^{q^{2}+1})
for
x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{4}}
. Then
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}=\{x\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{4}}^{*}:\,Q(x)=0\}
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
contains
\textup{GO}_{4}^{-}(q)
. The corresponding irreducible cyclic code
C
of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-standard.

Example 3.4

(Duals of Golay codes)
.

[
28
, Example 4, Example 5]

Let
C
be the dual of the binary Golay code of length
n=23
or the dual of the ternary Golay code of length
n=11
. The automorphism group of
C
is the Mathieu group
M_{23}
or
2.M_{11}
, and
\textup{PAut}(C)
is
M_{23}
or
\textup{PSL}_{2}(11)
respectively. In both cases,
\textup{PAut}(C)
contains a full cycle of length
n
and
C
is an NSIC code.

We have the following new family of NSIC codes
[
27
, Section VI]
,
[
29
, Section 6]
, some special cases of which have already appeared in
[
28
]
or
[
11
, Theorem 6.5]
.

Example 3.5

(Equally-spaced check polynomials)
.

Let
(n,n^{\prime},k)
be a triple with
n\geq 6
that satisfies the following conditions:

(3.1)

n=n^{\prime}k,\quad k\geq 2,\quad\textup{ord}_{n}(q)=k\cdot\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q).

Then the irreducible cyclic code
C
of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-standard, and
\textup{PAut}(C)
contains a subgroup isomorphic to
(C_{n^{\prime}}\rtimes C_{m^{\prime}})^{k}\rtimes S_{k}
, where
m^{\prime}=\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)
.

Remark 3.6
.

We shall give a proof that the code in Example
3.5
is non-standard in Section
4
. In Proposition
4.4
, we shall also give an arithmetic characterization of the triples
(n,n^{\prime},k)
that satisfy the conditions in (
3.1
).

We next present some secondary constructions of NSIC codes from known ones. The first two constructions are due to the second-named author, cf.
[
27
,
28
,
29
]
, where the first construction is based on
[
11
, Theorem 3.1]
(called “Lifting” there). We employ the notation that we have introduced in Definition
2.3
, and identify a nonzero element of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with an element of
\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
via left multiplication.

Construction 3.7

(Extension method)
.

Let
C
be an NSIC code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
and
u
be a divisor of

(q-1)/n_{0}
(recall that
n_{0}=\gcd(n,q-1)
). The irreducible cyclic code
C^{\prime}
of length
n^{\prime}=nu
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-standard.

Proof.

It is routine to check that
\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)=m
,
and that
{\mathcal{U}}_{n^{\prime},q}
is generated by
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
and a subgroup
H
of order
u\gcd(q-1,n)
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
. We also have

{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)={\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)H
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(n^{\prime},q)
contains
\langle{\mathcal{L}}(n,q),H\rangle
. The subgroup
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)\cap H
has order
\gcd(n,q-1)
and is contained in
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
, so
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)\cap{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)={\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
by Dedekind’s law. It follows that
{\mathcal{L}}(n^{\prime},q)\neq{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)
, so
C^{\prime}
is non-standard.
For more details, see
[
29
, Section 5]
.
∎

Construction 3.8

(Lifting method)
.

Let
C
be an NSIC code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
. Take
t\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\gcd(m,t)=1
. The code
C^{\prime}=C\otimes{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
, which is the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
-span of
C
, is an NSIC code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
.

Proof.

It is routine to check that
C^{\prime}
is a cyclic code with the same generator polynomial
f(x)
as
C
, and
f(x)
is irreducible over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
because
\gcd(m,t)=1
. We have
\textup{ord}_{n}(q^{t})=m
,
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}={\mathcal{U}}_{n,q^{t}}
,
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q^{t})\cap\textup{GL}_{m}(q)={\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
, and
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q^{t})\cap\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
contains
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
which is strictly larger than
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
. Hence
C^{\prime}
is non-standard.
∎

Construction 3.9

(Descending method, special case)
.

Let
n\geq 4
, and let
r
be a divisor of
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
. Let
C_{0}
be an NSIC code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}
with check polynomial
h_{0}(x)
, of degree
m_{0}=\textup{ord}_{n}(q^{r})
. Let
\beta^{-1}
be a zero of
h_{0}(x)
, and let
h(x)
denote the minimal polynomial of
\beta^{-1}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Let
C
be the irreducible cyclic code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
with check polynomial
h(x)
. Then
C=\{\left(\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(c_{1}),\ldots,\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(c_{n})\right)\mid(c_{1},\ldots,c_{n})\in C_{0}\}
, and
C
is non-standard of dimension
m
.

Proof.

Note that
m_{0}=m/r
.
The expression for the codewords of
C
follows directly from the trace representations as in (
2.2
) for
C
and
C_{0}
.
Since
\textup{GL}_{m}(q)
and
\textup{GL}_{m_{0}}(q^{r})
are the
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{r}}
-linear transformations of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
respectively, the former group contains the latter. It follows that
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)\cap\textup{GL}_{m_{0}}(q^{r})={\mathcal{L}}(n,q^{r})
. We also have
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)\cap\textup{GL}_{m_{0}}(q^{r})={\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q^{r})
. Since
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q^{r})>{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q^{r})
by the fact
C_{0}
is non-standard, we deduce that
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)\varsupsetneqq{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n,q)
, so
C
is non-standard. As an irreducible cyclic code over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
of length
n
,
C
has dimension
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
.
∎

Construction 3.10

(Tensor-product method)
.

Let
C
be an NSIC code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and let
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
. Take an integer
s>1
such that
t=\textup{ord}_{s}(q)>1
and
\gcd(m,t)=1
, and set
n^{\prime}=\lcm(n,s)
. The irreducible cyclic code
C^{\prime}
of length
n^{\prime}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is
non-standard.

Proof.

We will write
{\mathcal{U}}_{a}
to denote the multiplicative subgroup
{\mathcal{U}}_{a,q}
; we will repeatedly use the fact that
{\mathcal{U}}_{a}\cap{\mathcal{U}}_{b}={\mathcal{U}}_{\gcd(a,b)}
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{a}{\mathcal{U}}_{b}={\mathcal{U}}_{\lcm(a,b)}

Let
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
have order
n
and degree
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, where
m
is the degree of the minimal polynomial of
\xi
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. Since
\gcd(m,t)=1
, we also have
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q^{t})
, hence the minimal polynomials of
\xi
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
and over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
are identical. So the basis
\{e_{0}:=1,e_{1}:=\xi,\ldots,e_{m-1}:=\xi^{m-1}\}
for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is also a basis for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{mt}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
. As a consequence, every
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map
L
on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
extends uniquely to an
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear map on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{mt}}
, where if
x=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x_{i}e_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{mt}}
with
x_{i}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
for all
i
, then
L(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m-1}x_{i}L(e_{i})
. Let
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(n,q)
and
{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}^{*}(n,q)
denote the groups of extensions as above corresponding to
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
and
{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n,q)
, respectively.

Since
{\mathcal{U}}_{n^{\prime}}={\mathcal{U}}_{n}{\mathcal{U}}_{s}
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{s}\leq{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{t}}
, we see that
{\mathcal{L}}(n^{\prime},q)
contains
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(n,q)
and
{\mathcal{U}}_{s}
, hence contains
{\mathcal{L}}^{\prime}(n,q){\mathcal{U}}_{s}
. Next, a map
L\in{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n^{\prime},q)
has the form
L:x\mapsto\mu x^{q^{i}}
for some
\mu\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n^{\prime}}
and
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,mt-1\}
; again since
{\mathcal{U}}_{n^{\prime}}={\mathcal{U}}_{n}{\mathcal{U}}_{s}
, we have
\mu=\lambda\alpha
with
\lambda\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n}
and
\alpha\in{\mathcal{U}}_{s}
, and we see that the restriction of the map
\alpha^{-1}L
to
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
is contained in
{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n,q)
. So we conclude that
{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n^{\prime},q)={\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n,q){\mathcal{U}}_{s}
. By comparing orders, we see that
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)
is a proper subgroup of
{\mathcal{L}}^{*}(n,q)\cdot{\mathcal{U}}_{s}
. We conclude that
{\mathcal{L}_{\rm st}}(n^{\prime},q)<{\mathcal{L}}(n^{\prime},q)
and hence
C^{\prime}
is non-standard as claimed.
∎

4.
A new family of non-standard non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes

In this section, we present the proof for the fact that the irreducible cyclic codes in Example
3.5
are non-standard. This is achieved by establishing the following slightly stronger result.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
n=n^{\prime}k
with
k\geq 2
and
n^{\prime}\geq 2
, and set
m=\textup{ord}_{n}(q)
,
m^{\prime}=\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)
. Further, assume that
m^{\prime}k=m
. Then
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)\wr S_{k}\leq{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
, and the irreducible cyclic code of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
is non-degenerate and non-standard if and only if
n\geq 6
.

Proof.

Take notation as in Definition
2.3
. Since
\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}k}(q)=m=m^{\prime}k
, there exists
\xi\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}k}}
of order
n^{\prime}k
such that
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\xi)
. Then
\xi^{k}
has order
n^{\prime}
and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}={\mathbb{F}}_{q}(\xi^{k})
. Since
\langle\xi\rangle=\cup_{i=0}^{k-1}\langle\xi^{k}\rangle\xi^{i}
and
m=km^{\prime}
, we conclude that
\{1,\xi,\ldots,\xi^{k-1}\}
is a basis for
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}
.

We claim that
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
contains
{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime},q)\wr S_{k}
. For
\pi\in S_{k}
and
L_{0},\ldots,L_{k-1}\in{\mathcal{L}}(n^{\prime},q)
, define the map
L=L_{\pi,L_{0},\ldots,L_{k-1}}
by setting
L(\xi^{i}a):=\xi^{\pi(i)}L_{i}(a)
for
i=0,\ldots,k-1
and
a\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m^{\prime}}}
, and extend
L
by
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linearity to a map on
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
. By the above,
L
is well-defined and
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
-linear, and moreover
L
fixes
\langle\xi\rangle
, so that
L\in{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
. This proves the claim.

By the previous claim,
{\mathcal{L}}(n,q)
has size at least
k!(n^{\prime}m^{\prime})^{k}
. It is larger than
|{\mathcal{L}}_{\textup{st}}(n^{\prime}k,q)|
if and only if
(n^{\prime}m^{\prime})^{k-1}(k-1)!>k
, that is, if
k\geq 3
or if
k=2
and
n^{\prime}\geq 3
, so when
n\geq 6
. In the remaining case, we have
k=2
,
n^{\prime}=2
and
m^{\prime}=1
, so
q
is odd,
n=4
, and
m=2
. From the factorization
x^{4}-1=(x^{2}+1)(x+1)(x-1)
, we conclude that
\xi
has minimal polynomial
x^{2}+1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
, and then the generator polynomial is
x^{2}-1
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. So the corresponding irreducible cyclic code
C
is the span of
(1,-1,1,-1)
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. It is now easily checked that
|\textup{PAut}(C)|=nm=8
, so in this case,
C
is standard.
∎

We next determine the triples
(n,n^{\prime},k)
that satisfy the conditions in (
3.1
), i.e.,

n=n^{\prime}k,\;k\geq 2,\;\textup{ord}_{n}(q)=k\cdot\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q).

We need some preparation. For an integer
n\geq 1
and a prime
p
, we use
\pi(n)
to denote the set of prime divisors of
n
, and we use
\nu_{p}(n)
to denote the largest non-negative integer
i
such that
p^{i}
divides
n
. We need a well-known technical result, which is actually a direct consequence of a result often referred to as ‘‘Lifting the Exponent’’ Lemma
1
1

1

https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lifting-the-exponent_lemma
.
. For the sake of completeness, we include a proof here.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
r
be a prime, and let
N\neq 1
be an integer with
f:=\nu_{r}(N-1)\geq 1
.

(1)

If
e
is a positive integer with
r\not\,\mid e
, then
\nu_{r}(N^{e}-1)=f
.

(2)

We have

\nu_{r}(N^{r}-1)=f+1
if
(r,f)\neq(2,1)
and
\nu_{r}(N^{r}-1)\geq f+2
if
(r,f)=(2,1)
.

Proof.

By assumption, there is an integer
b
relatively prime to
r
such that
N=1+br^{f}
. If
r\nmid e
, then
N^{e}\equiv 1+ebr^{f}\not\equiv 1\bmod r^{f+1}
, and the first claim follows. Next, since
r
divides
\binom{r}{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
, we have
N^{r}\equiv 1+br^{f+1}\bmod r^{f+2}
unless
r=2
and
f=1
. In the latter case, we have
N=1+2b
with
b
odd, hence
N^{2}-1=4b+4b^{2}\equiv 0\bmod 8
.
∎

As a consequence, we have the following.

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
n^{\prime}\geq 2
and
q\geq 2
be integers such that
\gcd(n^{\prime},q)=1
. Define
m^{\prime}:=\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)
, and set
\Delta:=(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)/n^{\prime}
. Let
r
be a prime such that
\gcd(r,q)=1
.

(1)

If
r\not\in\pi(n^{\prime})
, then
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
divides
(r-1)m^{\prime}
.

(2)

If
r\in\pi(n^{\prime})
, then
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
is equal to
rm^{\prime}
if

\nu_{r}(\Delta)=0
and equal to
m^{\prime}
if
\nu_{r}(\Delta)>0
.

(3)

For every positive integer
k
with
\gcd(k,q)=1
, we have
\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)\leq k\cdot\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)
.

Proof.

(1) We have
r\mid(q^{r-1}-1)
, hence if
\gcd(r,n^{\prime})=1
, then
\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}r}(q)=\lcm(\textup{ord}_{r}(q),\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q))\mid(r-1)m^{\prime}
.

(2) Next, assume that
r
divides
n^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.2
we deduce that
rn^{\prime}\mid(q^{rm^{\prime}}-1)
, so
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
divides
rm^{\prime}
. It follows that
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
is either
m^{\prime}
or
rm^{\prime}
. Now if
r\mid(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)/n^{\prime}
, then
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)=m^{\prime}
, and if
r\nmid(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)/n^{\prime}
, then
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
is not equal to
m^{\prime}
and so it is equal to
rm^{\prime}
.

(3) If
k=1
, the claim is trivially true; and if
r
is a prime with
r\mid k
, then by Lemma
4.3
, parts (1) and (2), we have
\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)\leq r\cdot\textup{ord}_{(k/r)n^{\prime}}(q)
. Now the claim follows by induction.
∎

With the above preparation, we are now ready to derive necessary and sufficient conditions for condition (
3.1
) to hold.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
n,n^{\prime},k
be positive integers with
n=kn^{\prime}
, and set
m^{\prime}=\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)
. Write
\pi^{*}(n^{\prime}):=\{r\in\pi(n^{\prime})\,:\,\nu_{r}(n^{\prime})=\nu_{r}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)\}
. Then
\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)=km^{\prime}
if and only if
\pi(k)\subseteq\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})
and, moreover, if
2\in\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})
and
n^{\prime}\equiv 2\bmod 4
, then
\nu_{2}(k)\leq 1
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
k
. The claim holds trivially if
k=1
. Assume that
k\geq 2
, and let
r\mid k
. By applying Lemma
4.3
, part (3) twice, we have

\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)=\textup{ord}_{(k/r)(rn^{\prime})}(q)\leq(k/r)\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)\leq k\cdot\textup{ord}_{n^{\prime}}(q)=km^{\prime},

hence
\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)=km^{\prime}
if and only if
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)=rm^{\prime}
and
\textup{ord}_{kn^{\prime}}(q)=(k/r)\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)
. By Lemma
4.3
again,
\textup{ord}_{rn^{\prime}}(q)=rm^{\prime}
if and only if
r\in\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})
. Next, we claim that if
r\in\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})
, then

\pi^{*}(rn^{\prime})=\begin{cases}\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})\setminus\{2\}&\text{if }r=2\text{ and }n^{\prime}\equiv 2\pmod{4};\\
\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Note that if
r
is odd and
n^{\prime}
is even, then
rn^{\prime}\equiv n^{\prime}\pmod{4}
, so if the above claim holds, the proposition follows by induction.

To prove this claim, suppose that
r\in\pi^{*}(n^{\prime})
, and consider a prime
s
dividing
rn^{\prime}
. If
s\neq r
, then
\nu_{s}(rn^{\prime})=\nu_{s}(n^{\prime})
and by Lemma
4.2
, part (1), we have
\nu_{s}(q^{rm^{\prime}}-1)=\nu_{s}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)
. And when
s=r
, we have
\nu_{r}(rn^{\prime})=\nu_{r}(n^{\prime})+1
and by Lemma
4.2
, part (2), we have
\nu_{r}(q^{rm^{\prime}}-1)=\nu_{r}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)+1
unless
r=2
and
\nu_{2}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)=1
. Since
r=2
and
r\in\pi^{*}(n)
, we have
\nu_{2}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)=\nu_{2}(n^{\prime})
, hence
\nu_{2}(q^{m^{\prime}}-1)=1
holds if and only if
\nu_{2}(n^{\prime})=1
, that is,
n^{\prime}\equiv 2\bmod 4
. The proof of the claim is now complete.
∎

6.1.
Linear recurring sequence subgroups

NSIC codes are closely related to non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups, cf.
[
28
]
. Brison and Nogueira investigated them in a series of papers
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
]
.
A sequence
{\bf s}=s_{0},s_{1},\ldots
in the algebraic closure
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
is termed a linear recurring sequence of order
m
if it satisfies a (homogeneous) linear recurrence relation of the form

(6.1)

s_{k}=a_{m-1}s_{k-1}+\cdots+a_{1}s_{k-m+1}+a_{0}s_{k-m}

for all integers
k\geq m
, where
m\geq 1
,
a_{0}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}
and
a_{1},\ldots,a_{m-1}\in{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. The monic polynomial
f(x)=x^{m}-a_{m-1}x^{m-1}-\cdots-a_{1}x-a_{0}
in
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}[x]
with
f(0)=-a_{0}\neq 0
is called the characteristic polynomial of the recurrence relation (
6.1
), and such a sequence
{\bf s}
is referred to as an
f
-sequence. We say that such an
f
-sequence
{\bf s}
in
\overline{{\mathbb{F}}}_{q}
is
cyclic
if there exists
\alpha\in\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}
such that
s_{k+1}=\alpha s_{k}
for all
k\geq 0
. Moreover, the check polynomial
f(x)
of
s
corresponds to the minimal polynomial of
\xi\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
. For further information on linear recurrence relations and linear recurring sequences over finite fields, please refer to
[
36
,
40
,
44
,
57
]
. As a consequence,
an
f
-sequence
{\bf s}=(s_{0},s_{1},\ldots,s_{n-1})
of period
n
can represent the finite multiplicative subgroup
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}\leq(\overline{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}})^{*}
; in this case, we refer to
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
as an
f
-subgroup. Further, we say that
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
is a non-standard
f
-subgroup if a non-cyclic
f
-sequence can represent it, and we can call
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}
a standard
f
-subgroup otherwise. There is a one-to-one correspondence between non-standard pairs and non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups, see
[
28
, Section 4]
.
To sum up, the classification of NSIC codes is equivalent to the classification of non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups. This seems to have been first investigated by Somer
[
52
,
53
]
.

By using our main result, Theorem
1.1
, we give a classification of non-standard cyclic codes for the case
m=2
. This concludes the study of linear recurring sequence subgroups with
m=2
initiated and systematically studied by Brison and Nogueira
[
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

6.2.
The Schmidt-White conjecture

In this section, we verify the Schmidt-White conjecture for NSIC codes. Suppose that
q=p^{f}
with
p
prime, and let
n
be a divisor of
q^{m}-1
for some positive integer
m
. Take a primitive
n
-th root of unity
\xi

{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and set

(6.2)

c_{\alpha}=(\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha),\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha\xi),\ldots,\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha\xi^{n-1})).

Let

, cf.
[
51
, Definition 2.2]
. All irreducible cyclic codes of length
n
over
{\mathbb{F}}_{q}
are permutationally equivalent to

. For a codeword

w_{H}(c)=\#\{1\leq i\leq n:c_{i}\neq 0\}
for the Hamming weight of

w_{H}(c_{\alpha})=n-\#\{x\in{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}:\textup{Tr}_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}/{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(\alpha x)=0\}.

A code is called an

N
. We say that the code

is

if
{\mathcal{U}}_{n,q}\cdot{\mathbb{F}}_{q}^{*}\cup\{0\}
is a subfield of
{\mathbb{F}}_{q^{m}}
, and we say that

is

if there is an integer

such that

, where

, cf.
[
54
, Definition 6]
. By
[
19
]
, the code

The Schmidt-White conjecture asserts that a one- or two-weight irreducible cyclic code

is either a subfield code, a semiprimitive code, or belongs to an exceptional set of eleven codes that appears in
[
51
, Table 1]
, cf.
[
51
, Conjecture 4.4]
. Vega gave a critical review of one- and two-weight irreducible cyclic codes in
[

]
, and most importantly, he modified the definition of semiprimitive codes so that the conjecture holds for the two-weight irreducible cyclic codes constructed in
[
19
]
and
[
49
]
. The one-weight irreducible cyclic codes are well understood, so we focus on the two-weight cases. The reformulated version of the Schmidt-White Conjecture asserts that a two-weight irreducible cyclic code is either a semiprimitive code or appears in
[
51
, Table 1]
up to certain equivalence as explained in
[
54
, Remark 5]
, cf.
[
54
, Conjecture 2]
. We shall refer to
[
54
, Conjecture 2]
as the Schmidt-White conjecture. In this subsection, we verify it for NSIC codes.

is semiprimitive. This completes the proof.

7.
Concluding remarks

The permutation automorphism group of an irreducible cyclic code always contains a well-defined subgroup of the affine group generated by the cyclic shift and by the Frobenius permutations that permute the defining zeros of the code. We refer to these as standard permutation automorphisms; all other permutation automorphisms are called non-standard. An irreducible cyclic code is correspondingly called standard if all of its permutation automorphisms are standard, and non-standard otherwise.

In this paper, we classify the non-standard non-degenerate irreducible cyclic codes over finite fields. Our main theorem (Theorem
1.1
) gives a complete classification: apart from a small number of explicit exceptional families and their descendants under certain natural constructions, every non-degenerate irreducible cyclic code is standard.

As applications, we obtained a general description of non-standard linear recurring sequence subgroups, extending earlier work of Brison and Nogueira
[
8
,
9
,
10
,
11
,
12
,
13
]
, and we confirmed the Schmidt-White conjecture
[
51
]
for all NSIC codes. These results show that the exceptional codes with extra symmetries are very limited in nature.

There remain a number of natural problems for further study. One is to extend the classification of standard and non-standard codes to more general cyclic codes, including degenerate and reducible cases, or codes with
\gcd(n,q)\neq 1
. We expect that, except for a few rare examples, the automorphism group should always lie inside the affine group. Another is to determine the permutation automorphism groups of cyclic codes in full generality. Even in the irreducible case with
\gcd(n,q)=1
, while we have identified the non-standard situations, the precise structure of
\textup{PAut}(C)
in general remains to be described. We also note that the determination of automorphism groups is much more complicated if
\gcd(n,q)\neq 1
, and it may be out of reach to obtain a complete classification in that generality.

Finally, it would be interesting to investigate to what extent the methods developed here can be applied to other classes of codes, such as quasi-cyclic codes, and to understand more systematically the relation between group actions and code parameters. More broadly, the connection between irreducible cyclic codes, linear recurring sequences, and permutation group theory suggests further applications, for example, to the study of weight distributions or to other open conjectures related to two-weight codes.
