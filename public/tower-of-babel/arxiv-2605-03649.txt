Virasoro flow, monodromy, and indecomposable structures in critical AdS 3 topologically massive gravity
Yannick Mvondo-She

Abstract
We develop a representation-theoretic framework for the relation between asymptotic symmetry evolution and monodromy in critical topologically massive gravity at the chiral point μ ​ ℓ = 1 \mu\ell=1 . We show that continuous evolution generated by the Virasoro zero mode L 0 L_{0} and analytic continuation around branch points can be unified as different regimes of a single complex one-parameter flow. At the chiral point, L 0 L_{0} becomes non-diagonalizable and takes the form L 0 = h ​ 𝟏 + N L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N , with N N nilpotent. We demonstrate that this nilpotent component governs identical mixing structures in both real and imaginary flow parameters, producing linear mixing under continuous evolution and logarithmic mixing under monodromy. In this sense, the logarithmic sector is characterized by a single indecomposable structure in state space probed uniformly by both transformations. Logarithmic modes arise naturally as generalized eigenstates of L 0 L_{0} , and the sector decomposition admits an algebraic interpretation in terms of invariant and generalized invariant subspaces. This provides a unified description of logarithmic structures in critical topologically massive gravity and clarifies their role in the representation theory of asymptotic symmetries.

2
Background

2.1
Logarithmic structure in critical topologically massive gravity

Topologically Massive Gravity (TMG) in three dimensions
[
5
,
4
,
3
,
12
,
18
]
provides a minimal setting in which propagating gravitational degrees of freedom coexist with asymptotic conformal symmetry. At the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, the theory exhibits a degeneracy between the massive and left-moving graviton modes
[
14
]
. As a consequence, the linearized equations of motion become non-diagonalizable, leading to the appearance of logarithmic solutions
[
10
]
.

This degeneracy is reflected in the structure of asymptotic states. Instead of forming irreducible highest-weight representations of the Virasoro algebra, the relevant states organize into indecomposable representations. Concretely, there exists a pair of states
(\psi^{L},\psi^{\log})
such that

L_{0}\psi^{L}=h\psi^{L},\qquad L_{0}\psi^{\log}=h\psi^{\log}+\psi^{L},

(2)

which defines a rank-2 Jordan block. This structure is the hallmark of logarithmic conformal field theories (LCFTs)
[
11
,
9
]
and underlies the non-unitary features of the theory.

2.2
Fefferman–Graham expansion and logarithmic modes

The logarithmic nature of the theory is also visible in the asymptotic form of the metric. In Fefferman–Graham coordinates
[
6
]
, the
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
metric can be written as

ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+e^{2\rho}g_{ij}(x,\rho),dx^{i}dx^{j},

(3)

with
\rho\to\infty
corresponding to the conformal boundary. In Einstein gravity, the asymptotic expansion takes the form

g_{ij}(x,\rho)=g_{ij}^{(0)}(x)+e^{-2\rho}g_{ij}^{(2)}(x)+\cdots,

(4)

where
g_{ij}^{(0)}
is the boundary metric and
g_{ij}^{(2)}
determines the expectation value of the dual stress tensor
[
1
]
.

At the chiral point of TMG, the degeneracy of the equations of motion allows for an additional logarithmic term
[
10
]

g_{ij}(x,\rho)=g_{ij}^{(0)}+e^{-2\rho}\big(g_{ij}^{(2)}+\rho b_{ij}^{(2)}\big)+\cdots.

(5)

The presence of the
\rho,e^{-2\rho}
contribution signals the existence of logarithmic modes. Since
\rho\sim\log r
, this term corresponds to a
\log r
behavior in the radial coordinate.

From the holographic perspective, the coefficients
(g_{ij}^{(2)},b_{ij}^{(2)})
are associated with a pair of operators
(T_{ij},t_{ij})
forming a logarithmic multiplet
[
9
]
. The appearance of two independent contributions at the same order in the asymptotic expansion reflects the underlying indecomposable representation structure.

2.3
Monodromy and logarithmic mixing

A useful way to characterize logarithmic behavior is through monodromy. In conformal field theory, the Virasoro zero mode
L_{0}
generates dilatations, and its exponentiation defines the transformation of fields under analytic continuation around singular points

\mathcal{M}=e^{2\pi iL_{0}}.

(6)

In a diagonalizable representation,
\mathcal{M}
acts multiplicatively. However, in the logarithmic case, the Jordan block structure of
L_{0}
implies that
\mathcal{M}
is non-diagonalizable. Writing

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0,

(7)

one finds

\mathcal{M}=e^{2\pi ih}(\mathbf{1}+2\pi iN).

(8)

As a result, the logarithmic partner transforms as

\psi^{\log}\longrightarrow\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\psi^{L},

(9)

while
\psi^{L}
remains an eigenstate.

This non-trivial monodromy is the origin of logarithmic terms in correlation functions
[
11
]
. In particular, the requirement of invariance under analytic continuation fixes the structure of two-point functions in logarithmic sectors.

In the context of critical TMG, monodromy can be understood as arising from analytic continuation in the bulk spacetime. In particular, the logarithmic dependence on the radial coordinate implies that under
\rho\to\rho+2\pi i
, the logarithmic mode acquires an admixture of the primary mode. This provides a geometric realization of the Jordan block structure in terms of the analytic properties of bulk solutions.

2.4
Motivation

The discussion above highlights two closely related features of critical TMG

•

The presence of logarithmic modes associated with a non-diagonalizable action of
L_{0}
,

•

The appearance of non-trivial monodromy governed by the same operator.

These observations suggest that both continuous symmetry transformations and analytic continuation are controlled by a common algebraic structure. In the following sections, we make this relation precise by treating both as manifestations of a single complex flow generated by
L_{0}
, and by identifying the nilpotent component as the source of logarithmic mixing in both cases.

3
Virasoro flow and the relation between evolution and monodromy

In this section we show that the Virasoro zero mode
L_{0}
generates a single complex one-parameter flow whose real and imaginary regimes correspond respectively to continuous asymptotic symmetry evolution and monodromy. The logarithmic sector is characterized by the failure of this flow to be diagonalizable.

3.1
Complex flow generated by
L_{0}

Let
L_{0}
act on the space of asymptotic states
\mathcal{H}
. We define the complex flow

\psi(s)=e^{sL_{0}}\psi,\qquad s\in\mathbb{C},

(10)

which satisfies the differential equation

\frac{d}{ds}\psi(s)=L_{0}\psi(s),\qquad\psi(0)=\psi.

(11)

Thus,
L_{0}
generates a complex one-parameter family of transformations
e^{sL_{0}}
, analytic in the parameter
s
.

We distinguish two physically relevant slices:

•

s=t\in\mathbb{R}
: continuous evolution generated by
L_{0}
, interpreted as time evolution in the boundary theory (in the sense of radial quantization),

•

s=2\pi i
: monodromy transformation.

3.2
Diagonalizable sector

If
L_{0}
is diagonalizable on a state
\psi
,

L_{0}\psi=h\psi,

(12)

then the flow is purely multiplicative

e^{sL_{0}}\psi=e^{sh}\psi.

(13)

In this case, both real evolution and monodromy act only by phases and scalings, with no mixing between states.

3.3
Logarithmic sector and Jordan decomposition

At the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, the action of
L_{0}
becomes non-diagonalizable. On the relevant subspace it admits a Jordan decomposition

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0.

(14)

There exists a pair of states
(\psi^{L},\psi^{\log})
such that

N\psi^{L}=0,\qquad N\psi^{\log}=\psi^{L}.

(15)

To make this structure explicit, it is useful to represent the action of
L_{0}
on the two-dimensional subspace spanned by (
\psi^{L},\psi^{\log}
) in matrix form. In the ordered basis (
\psi^{L},\psi^{\log}
), one has

\displaystyle L_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}h&1\\
0&h\end{array}\right)=h\mathbf{1}+\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right),

where the second term defines the nilpotent operator
N
, satisfying
N^{2}=0
.

The exponential can then be evaluated explicitly

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}1&s\\
0&1\end{array}\right),

making manifest that the off-diagonal mixing is linear in the flow parameter
s
.

3.4
Exact form of the flow

Since
N^{2}=0
, the exponential truncates exactly

e^{sL_{0}}=e^{sh}e^{sN}=e^{sh}(\mathbf{1}+sN).

(24)

This yields the exact action on the Jordan pair

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi^{L}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}\psi^{L},

(25)

\displaystyle e^{sL_{0}}\psi^{\log}

\displaystyle=e^{sh}(\psi^{\log}+s\psi^{L}).

(26)

The key structural consequence is that the logarithmic partner acquires a linear admixture of the primary state under the flow, controlled entirely by
N
.

3.5
Real evolution and monodromy as a single structure

Evaluating the general flow in the two physical regimes

(i) Continuous evolution (
s=t\in\mathbb{R}
):

e^{tL_{0}}\psi^{\log}=e^{th}(\psi^{\log}+t\psi^{L}).

(27)

(ii) Monodromy (
s=2\pi i
):

e^{2\pi iL_{0}}\psi^{\log}=e^{2\pi ih}(\psi^{\log}+2\pi i\psi^{L}).

(28)

Both transformations are governed by the same operator
N
, differing only by the value of the flow parameter
s
.

3.6
Structural consequence

The essential point is that the same nilpotent operator controls both regimes

\text{mixing}\;\;\propto\;\;sN.

(29)

Hence

•

real evolution produces linear growth in the logarithmic direction,

•

monodromy produces discrete logarithmic shifts,

•

both originate from the same indecomposable representation of
L_{0}
.

This establishes that the logarithmic sector is precisely the regime in which asymptotic symmetry evolution and analytic continuation are not independent, but unified through the nilpotent structure of
L_{0}
.

Main structural result

In the logarithmic sector of critical topologically massive gravity, the action of the Virasoro zero mode
L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N
, with
N^{2}=0
, generates a complex one-parameter flow
e^{sL_{0}}
such that

•

for
s\in\mathbb{R}
, it describes continuous asymptotic symmetry evolution,

•

for
s=2\pi i
, it reproduces the monodromy transformation.

In both cases, the mixing of states is governed by the same nilpotent operator
N
, and is proportional to the flow parameter
s
. Thus, continuous evolution and monodromy probe the same indecomposable structure in the space of asymptotic states.

4
Bulk interpretation of
N

In this section we provide a unified bulk realization of the nilpotent operator
N
. We show that
N
arises from the degeneracy of the linearized bulk equations at the chiral point and admits equivalent realizations in: (i) the linearized differential operator, (ii) radial (Fefferman–Graham) evolution, and (iii) analytic continuation of the radial coordinate.

4.1
Degeneracy of linearized equations

In topologically massive gravity, linearized metric perturbations satisfy a third-order equation of the form
[
2
]

(\mathcal{D}^{L}\mathcal{D}^{R}\mathcal{D}^{M})h_{\mu\nu}=0,

(30)

where
\mathcal{D}^{L}
and
\mathcal{D}^{R}
are left- and right-moving first-order operators, and
\mathcal{D}^{M}
is the massive graviton operator.

At the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, the massive and left-moving sectors degenerate
[
14
]

\mathcal{D}^{M}\equiv\mathcal{D}^{L},

(31)

so that the equations reduce to

(\mathcal{D}^{L})^{2}\mathcal{D}^{R}h_{\mu\nu}=0.

(32)

This degeneracy implies the existence of generalized solutions
[
10
]

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{L}h^{L}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=0,

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}^{L}h^{\log}_{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=h^{L}_{\mu\nu}.

(34)

This structure is identical to a rank-2 Jordan chain and suggests the identification

N\;\sim\;\mathcal{D}^{L}\quad\text{on the degenerate solution space.}

(35)

4.2
Radial realization in Fefferman–Graham coordinates

In Fefferman–Graham coordinates, the metric takes the form

ds^{2}=d\rho^{2}+e^{2\rho}g_{ij}(x,\rho)\,dx^{i}dx^{j}.

(36)

At the chiral point, the asymptotic expansion acquires a logarithmic branch:

g_{ij}=g^{(0)}_{ij}+e^{-2\rho}\left(g^{(2)}_{ij}+\rho\,b^{(2)}_{ij}\right)+\cdots.

(37)

The key feature is the presence of the term

\rho e^{-2\rho}\sim(\log r)\,r^{-2},

(38)

which mixes logarithmic and ordinary falloffs.

4.3
Action of radial evolution

Consider infinitesimal radial translations generated by
\partial_{\rho}
. Their action on the two independent asymptotic structures is:

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(e^{-2\rho})

\displaystyle=-2e^{-2\rho},

(39)

\displaystyle\partial_{\rho}(\rho e^{-2\rho})

\displaystyle=-2\rho e^{-2\rho}+e^{-2\rho}.

(40)

The second line shows explicitly that radial evolution is not diagonal on the space of solutions: it maps the logarithmic mode into itself plus the primary mode.

This allows a decomposition of the radial generator as

\partial_{\rho}=-2\mathbf{1}+N_{\text{bulk}},

(41)

where the nilpotent part acts as

N_{\text{bulk}}(\rho e^{-2\rho})=e^{-2\rho},\qquad N_{\text{bulk}}(e^{-2\rho})=0,

(42)

so that

N_{\text{bulk}}^{2}=0.

(43)

Thus,
N_{\text{bulk}}
is precisely the operator that extracts the primary component from the logarithmic branch.

4.4
Identification with the Virasoro generator

The asymptotic symmetry analysis of
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}
gravity (Brown-Henneaux) relates the action of the Virasoro generator
L_{0}
on asymptotic states to scaling transformations of the boundary data. In Fefferman-Graham coordinates, this scaling behavior is reflected in radial evolution, whose action on the asymptotic expansion reproduces the same mixing structure observed in the logarithmic sector.

In particular, while
\partial_{\rho}
is not itself an asymptotic symmetry generator, its action on the space of solutions provides a bulk realization of the non-diagonal part of the
L_{0}
action. This allows one to identify the decomposition

\displaystyle L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,

(44)

where the nilpotent operator
N
is inherited from the off-diagonal structure of radial evolution.

4.5
Monodromy and analytic continuation

Using
\rho\sim\log z
, analytic continuation
z\to e^{2\pi i}z
implies

\rho\to\rho+2\pi i.

(45)

Acting on the logarithmic structure,

\rho e^{-2\rho}\to(\rho+2\pi i)e^{-2\rho}=\rho e^{-2\rho}+2\pi i\,e^{-2\rho}.

(46)

This produces the same mixing structure:

\text{log mode}\;\longrightarrow\;\text{log mode}+(2\pi i)\,\text{primary},

(47)

with the same operator
N
governing the transformation.

4.6
Summary: unified bulk origin of
N

The nilpotent operator
N
admits three equivalent bulk realizations:

•

as the degeneracy operator
\mathcal{D}^{L}
in the linearized equations,

•

as the off-diagonal part of radial evolution in Fefferman–Graham coordinates,

•

as the generator of logarithmic shifts under analytic continuation.

In all cases,
N
encodes the failure of bulk scaling evolution to remain diagonalizable at the chiral point. It is therefore the geometric origin of the indecomposable Virasoro structure acting on asymptotic states.

5
Summary and outlook

In this work, we have analyzed the role of the Virasoro zero mode
L_{0}
in critical topologically massive gravity at the chiral point
\mu\ell=1
, focusing on its action on the space of asymptotic states. We have shown that continuous symmetry transformations and monodromy can be understood as different regimes of a single complex one-parameter flow generated by
L_{0}
. In this framework, real values of the flow parameter describe continuous evolution under asymptotic symmetries, while imaginary values generate analytic continuation and monodromy.

In the logarithmic sector, where
L_{0}
becomes non-diagonalizable, the decomposition

L_{0}=h\mathbf{1}+N,\qquad N^{2}=0,

(48)

plays a central role. The nilpotent operator
N
governs the mixing of states and induces identical algebraic structures in both real and imaginary regimes of the flow. This provides a unified representation-theoretic perspective on logarithmic behavior, in which both continuous evolution and monodromy probe the same indecomposable structure in state space.

Within this setting, logarithmic modes arise naturally as generalized eigenstates of
L_{0}
, and the conventional distinction between untwisted and logarithmic sectors can be reformulated in terms of invariant and generalized invariant subspaces under its action. This reinterpretation clarifies the algebraic origin of logarithmic mixing and its relation to asymptotic symmetries.

Taken together with the analysis of logarithmic Virasoro flows and bulk monodromy presented in
[
17
]
, the present results provide a more refined representation-theoretic underpinning of the structures anticipated in the proposed
\mathrm{AdS}_{3}/\mathrm{LCFT}_{2}
correspondence
[
10
,
9
]
. In particular, both works exhibit a common nilpotent mechanism controlling logarithmic mixing, suggesting that indecomposability is not an auxiliary feature of special states, but rather a structural property of the Virasoro action on asymptotic data.

From this perspective, the logarithmic sector can be understood as the regime in which asymptotic symmetry evolution, radial dynamics, and monodromy become different realisations of a single underlying flow generated by
L_{0}
. This strengthens the interpretation of logarithmic modes as intrinsic components of the holographic dictionary in critical topologically massive gravity, rather than merely as solutions arising from degeneracy at the chiral point.

Several directions for further investigation remain. It would be useful to develop a more precise characterization of the bulk realization of the nilpotent operator
N
, in particular its relation to the structure of linearized solutions and asymptotic charges. It would also be interesting to explore whether the framework presented here extends to other settings in which logarithmic structures arise, such as higher-spin theories or higher-dimensional generalizations. Finally, a more detailed analysis of correlation functions within this approach may help clarify the connection to logarithmic conformal field theory and the holographic dictionary.

We hope that the perspective developed here provides a useful starting point for understanding logarithmic phenomena in three-dimensional gravity from a unified algebraic viewpoint.
