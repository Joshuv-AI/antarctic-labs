β-Adrenergic receptors as immunomodulators in T cells: mechanisms of neuroimmune crosstalk and pathophysiological implications
Authors: Junyan Yao, Suli Zhang, Fei Sun, Mingyang Chen, Huirong Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Regulation of T cell function by the sympathetic nervous system via β-adrenergic receptors is a pivotal research focus in neuroimmunology, with most studies centered on β2-adrenergic receptors. However, the roles of other subtypes remain unclear. This review systematically summarizes differential expression and signaling of β2- and β1-adrenergic receptors across T cell subsets. While β2-adrenergic receptor bidirectionally modulates immune responsiveness through classical or non-classical pathways, β1-adrenergic receptor is predominantly expressed in regulatory and exhausted T cells, exerting immunosuppression via the cAMP-CREM axis. Furthermore, this review examines the pathophysiological roles of β-adrenergic receptor signaling in the tumor immune microenvironment, autoimmune diseases, infections and cardiovascular diseases, highlighting β1-adrenergic receptor as a candidate immune checkpoint. Ultimately, elucidating the subtype-specific mechanisms of β-adrenergic signaling provides a critical framework for understanding immune dysregulation and highlights the potential of targeting these pathways to restore immune homeostasis in various pathological contexts.Graphical abstract

Introduction

The sympathetic nervous system (SNS) and the immune system are both essential for responding to internal and external threats and for maintaining homeostasis. It was once believed that the nervous and immune systems functioned independently, each responding to threats without direct interaction. However, as patterns between psychological stress and disease were recognized, together with anatomical evidence of neural regulation of immune organs, it became clear that the nervous system can modulate lymphoid tissues and influence immune responses (Kin and Sanders 2006; Mueller 2022). Although both the sympathetic and parasympathetic nervous systems contribute to immune regulation, primary control of immune organs, including the bone marrow, thymus, lymph nodes, and spleen, resides with the SNS (Nance, and Sanders 2007). Sympathetic nerve fibers innervate the vascular system and parenchymal regions of the central and secondary lymphoid organs of all mammals studied (Bellinger and Lorton 2014). Moreover, it has been confirmed that adrenergic receptors are expressed on the surface of various innate and adaptive immune cells, such as macrophages, dendritic cells, T lymphocytes, and B lymphocytes (Yu et al. 2007; Scanzano and Cosentino 2015; Udit et al. 2022; Smith and Ascione 2023; Daneshmandi et al. 2024), which provides a conduit for the direct communication between the SNS and immune cells. Yet, while a wealth of research has shown the phenomenon that SNS modulates immune cell behavior, the mechanistic role of adrenergic receptors is often overlooked. To address this gap, we will first examine the function and mechanisms of β-adrenergic receptors (β-ARs) in T cells, as this receptor-cell axis represents a paradigmatic component of SNS-immune cell communication.

Adaptive immunity has drawn increasing attention in neuroimmunological research for its potent and specific defense against infectious and malignant disorders (Kin and Sanders 2006; Devi et al. 2021; Chi et al. 2024). As central mediators of adaptive immunity, T lymphocytes play a pivotal role in pathogen clearance as well as in cancer and autoimmune therapy (Sun L et al. 2023). Emerging evidence has investigated the regulatory influence of the SNS on T cell populations, most of which describe adrenergic signaling as immunosuppressive, through induction of apoptosis, inhibition of migration, and suppression of T cell activation and cytokine production (Gu et al. 2000; Schiller et al. 2021; Lei et al. 2023; Fan et al. 2020). These effects were long attributed solely to β2-AR signaling because of its predominant expression in T cells (Grebe et al. 2009; Estrada et al. 2016; Bucsek et al. 2017). However, recent evidence demonstrates that other adrenergic receptors, including β1-ARs, may exhibit unique expression signatures, and exert independent or synergistic effects on SNS-mediated regulation of T cell function (Cao et al. 2003; Lorton 2014; Pilipović et al. 2019; Devi et al. 2021; Yuki 2021; Globig et al. 2023). Together with β2-ARs, these receptor signals establish an integrated adrenergic signaling network within T cells, coordinating SNS-driven modulation of T cell responses in both physiological and pathological contexts.

In this review, we summarize advances in neuroimmuno-regulation from the receptor-specific perspective, with particular emphasis on β-AR signaling. We highlight the roles of both classical β2-AR and emerging β1-AR signaling in shaping T cell phenotype and function. Our aim is to elucidate novel mechanisms through which adrenergic signaling regulates immune activity and to explore the therapeutic potential of targeting adrenergic receptors or their ligands in contexts such as malignancies and cardiovascular diseases.

Sympathetic neuroimmune architecture and general T cell modulation

Neural innervation of immune organs

Since the early 20th century, observations in both humans and animals have suggested the association between mental state and immune function, linking psychological status to health outcomes (Kin and Sanders 2006). However, anatomical studies of immune organ innervation did not emerge until the 1980s. Bulloch et al. first reported parasympathetic and sympathetic innervation of the mouse thymus, with parasympathetic fibers localized to the cortex-medulla junction and subcapsular region, and sympathetic fibers aligned along the trabeculae (Bulloch and Moore 1981; Bulloch and Pomerantz 1984). In line with these findings, Felten et al. described noradrenergic innervation of the mouse thymus, with fibers entering via perivascular bundles and plexuses, extending from the subcapsular and vascular plexuses into the cortex, and branching within the parenchyma. They further demonstrated sympathetic innervation in the spleen, lymph nodes, gut-associated lymphoid tissue, and bone marrow across multiple species (Felten et al. 1985). These nerve endings terminate near lymphocytes, particularly in T cell and plasma cell regions, but are sparse in B cell-rich nodular areas (Felten et al. 1985; Nance, and Sanders 2007). More recently, a novel population of glial cells has been identified in the murine spleen, residing in the white pulp, closely surrounding sympathetic axons, and interacting with T and B cells to form neuroimmune junctions that may mediate signaling (Lucas et al. 2021). Moreover, complementary work by Cao et al. revealed distinct patterns of autonomic innervation in primate and human immune organs, showing dense sympathetic fiber distribution in the spleen parenchyma, sparse innervation in other immune organs, and virtually no parasympathetic innervation (Cao et al. 2023). Beyond lymphoid organs, the sympathetic nervous system also innervates immune tissues within metabolic organs such as the liver, pancreas, and adipose tissue, thereby linking immune responses with metabolic homeostasis (Martinez-Sanchez et al. 2022; Ren et al. 2024; Lee 2025). Collectively, these findings establish that the nervous system—particularly the sympathetic branch—maintains direct and functionally significant connections with immune organs and tissues, exerting regulatory effects on immune cells.

Adrenergic receptor expression landscape in immune cells

With evidence of neural innervation of immune organs, naturally, subsequent studies focused on whether immune cells express adrenergic receptors, the key mediators of neural regulation of immunity. It is striking that, in the earliest relevant studies, unconventional T cells were found to express β-ARs. SUZUKI et al. used radioligand binding assays to identify unconventional T cell subsets (including γδ T cells and extrathymic T cells) with innate-like features in human peripheral blood that express β-AR, and showed that both their absolute numbers and proportions exhibit a characteristic daytime rhythm (SUZUKI et al. 1997). Minagawa et al. reported that NKT cells in the mouse liver tend to express high densities of β-AR on the cell surface, undergo substantial expansion in response to adrenergic signaling during the early phase following partial hepatectomy, and display enhanced autoreactive cytotoxicity against syngeneic thymocytes (Minagawa et al. 2000). Sanders et al. first reported that CD4+ T cells and B cells mainly express β2-AR, with B cell levels approximately twice those of CD4+ T cells (Kohm and Sanders 2001; Kin and Sanders 2006). This may be explained by the fact that B cells require more receptors to compensate for less surrounding innervation. However, Th2 effector cells were found to lack expression of adrenergic receptor subtypes altogether (Nance, and Sanders 2007). Subsequent studies then established β2-AR as the principal adrenergic receptor expressed across nearly all innate and adaptive immune cell subsets, and its signaling mediates the suppression of immune responses, which has been well documented in multiple disease models, including tumors (Scanzano and Cosentino 2015; Moriyama et al. 2018; Gonzatti et al. 2023; Cáceres et al. 2024; Kyritsi et al. 2025).

Beyond β2-AR, accumulating evidence has expanded the scope of receptor involvement. Yu et al. provided direct evidence that all three β-receptor subtypes are expressed in human peripheral lymphocytes at both mRNA and protein levels (Yu et al. 2007). Peklo et al. further quantified β2-AR and β1-AR expression in peripheral blood mononuclear cells, reporting β1-AR expression in monocytes and natural killer (NK) cells for the first time (Peklo et al. 2025). A study published in 1995 showed that SNS participates in the inhibition of lipopolysaccharide-induced tumor necrosis factor-α (TNF-α) release through β1-AR, which can be reversed by atenolol (Elenkov et al. 1995). In esophageal squamous cell carcinoma, β1-AR expression is increased in CD8+ T cells after neoadjuvant therapy, associated with exhaustion markers (Li et al. 2025a, b). In neuroblastoma models, blocking β3-ARs increases the number of CD8+ T cells, NK cells, and dendritic cells, reduces immunosuppressive cells such as regulatory T cells (Tregs) and myeloid-derived suppressor cells, and inhibits programmed cell death ligand 1 expression, thereby enhancing antitumor immune responses (Bruno et al. 2023). In melanoma and pregnancy, β3-AR signaling can also promote immune tolerance by modulating Tregs and NK cells (Calvani et al. 2020). Therefore, β-AR signaling is generally considered immunosuppressive in many tissues and organs in animals and humans (Schiller et al. 2021; Klein Wolterink et al. 2022), especially for CD8+ T cell-mediated cellular immunity (Mravec et al. 2020). Nevertheless, inconsistencies remain across studies, which will be discussed in detail later.

Regarding α-adrenergic receptors (α-ARs), Kavelaars first noted that only α1-ARs appear to be expressed in the innate immune system, restricted to specific innate immune cell subsets (Kavelaars 2002). Subsequent studies using ligand-binding and transcriptomics, however, revealed that α2-ARs are co-expressed with α1-ARs in both innate and adaptive immune cells (Nance, and Sanders 2007; Pilipović et al. 2019; Yuki 2021; Zhu et al. 2023). Unlike β-AR, α-AR primarily enhances the activity of innate effector cells, such as promoting phagocytosis, circulation, migration, and proinflammatory cytokine secretion in macrophages. When homeostasis is disrupted, signaling in innate immune cells may shift from β2-AR to α1-AR dominance (Bellinger and Lorton 2014). Both α1-AR and α2-AR are thought to mediate pro-inflammatory effects by strengthening macrophage-dependent T cell activation, driving polarization toward Th1 and Th17 subsets, and reducing Treg generation, thereby sustaining the clearance of pathogens or tumor cells (Pilipović et al. 2019; Yuki 2021; Zhu et al. 2023). In recent years, pan-cancer T cell and myeloid-cell atlases, cross-tissue immune-cell atlases, and integrated single-cell/spatial transcriptomic resources now allow these receptor transcripts to be examined across T cell states, myeloid populations, stromal compartments and spatial tumor niches, which is critical for understanding the adrenergic receptor dynamics in T cells at sufficient resolution.

It should be noted that the methods used to detect receptor expression vary considerably across studies. Protein-level quantification provides a relatively high level of evidence, whereas pharmacological agonism/antagonism or knockout models cannot directly confirm receptor expression. Given this methodological heterogeneity, inferences regarding the expression of a particular receptor subtype on T cells differ among studies. Moreover, factors such as the limitations of β-AR antibody-based detection, receptor desensitization and internalization, and the discrepancies between transcript and surface protein expression may also affect the results and should be taken into consideration.

Collectively, both α- and β-ARs are found on nearly all immune cells, except Th2 cells, and regulate their functions, with β2-AR serving as the predominant mediator (Table 1). Receptor expression levels vary according to cell state, maturation, activation, and differentiation (Elenkov et al. 2000; Scanzano and Cosentino 2015), and functional outcomes further diverge depending on signal strength, nature of stimulus, and receptor-receptor interactions (Lorton and Bellinger 2015). Thus, precisely assessing receptor function under specific physiological or pathological conditions by measuring variables at different levels of the “neuron-neurotransmitter-receptor-cell” axis represents a critical direction for future research.

General effects of sympathetic modulation on T cells

With in-depth research on neuroimmune communication, it is now widely accepted that neural regulation of the immune system is primarily mediated through the hypothalamic-pituitary-adrenal axis (HPA, which regulates immune cells through glucocorticoids) and the SNS, the latter releases catecholamines that act on adrenergic receptors on immune cells (Ricon-Becker and Cole 2024). The effects of sympathetic modulation on T cells are therefore determined by neurotransmitter availability, signal intensity, stimulation duration, cellular activation state, and tissue context. This is important because many studies investigating sympathetic regulation of T cell immunity rely on systemic stress models, chemical sympathectomy, exercise-induced SNS activation, or non-selective β-blockers, therefore providing evidence for general adrenergic modulation.

One major consequence of sympathetic modulation is altered T cell trafficking and tissue distribution. Early studies demonstrated that infusion of epinephrine into animals induces a rapid increase in circulating leukocytes, with lymphocytes peaking within 30 min and gradually decreasing after several hours (Benschop et al. 1996; Mueller 2022). Exercise has also been shown to induce the emigration of T lymphocytes from the thymus via adrenergic signaling (Mueller 2022; Markov et al. 2023). Conversely, chronic adrenergic nerve input can promote lymphocyte retention within lymph nodes and maintain the balance of lymphocyte distribution in lymphoid tissues (Nakai et al. 2014). Sympathetic activity also contributes to circadian regulation of lymphocyte trafficking, as reduced sympathetic tone during sleep is associated with increased lymphocyte emigration, whereas increased sympathetic activity favors lymphocyte retention (Suzuki et al. 2016). Chronic stress may further inhibit the expression of cutaneous C-C motif chemokine ligand 27, thereby reducing CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cell recruitment to inflammatory sites in the skin (Saul et al. 2005). These findings indicate that SNS activity can either mobilize or restrain T cell trafficking depending on the duration, intensity, and anatomical context of adrenergic signaling.

The functional consequences of SNS activation are similarly context-dependent. Acute sympathetic activation can enhance immune mobilization and effector readiness. Acute stress may increase interferon production in CD4+ T cells (Kohm and Sanders 2001; Antoni and Dhabhar 2019), while acute systemic SNS activation induced by exercise significantly boosts the mobilization, ex vivo expansion, and anti-tumor activity of virus-specific T cells and γδ T cells in healthy individuals (Baker et al. 2020; Kunz et al. 2020). In contrast, sustained adrenergic stimulation associated with chronic stress, malignant disease, or persistent inflammation tends to favor immunosuppression, T cell dysfunction, and exhaustion. In cancer patients with chronic stress (Liu et al. 2022a, b), coordinated activation of the SNS and HPA axis causes sustained norepinephrine release within the tumor microenvironment (TME) (Rudak et al. 2021; Tian et al. 2021). Under such conditions, heightened SNS activity may suppress anti-tumor immune responses and thereby create a permissive microenvironment for tumor progression (Sun et al. 2025).

Consistent with this concept, studies using chemical sympathectomy or non-selective β-adrenergic blockade have shown that reducing sympathetic input can enhance anti-tumor T cell immunity. 6-hydroxydopamine-mediated chemical sympathectomy enhances the immune response of CD8⁺ T cells against multiple tumors in mice (Reel et al. 2024). In chronic cold-stress tumor models, propranolol restores TCR signaling in CD8⁺ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes, while propranolol treatment or genetic disruption of adrenergic signaling reduces tumor growth, decreases exhausted tumor-infiltrating T cells, increases progenitor-like exhausted T cells, and enhances cytokine production (Qiao et al. 2021). In multiple murine metastasis models, blockade of β-adrenergic signaling significantly enhanced the Th1 polarization and cytotoxic capacity of CD4+ T cells, resulting in attenuated tumor metastasis (Fjæstad et al. 2025). These studies establish a broad role for sympathetic modulation in shaping T cell immunity, while also underscoring the need for receptor subtype-specific analyses in subsequent sections.

β2-AR–mediated shaping of T cell function

β2-AR expression on T cells

β₂-AR is a key mediator of neural signaling from the SNS to the immune system, maintaining immune homeostasis by regulating immune activation and suppression. Among immune populations, the effects of β₂-AR on T cells have been most extensively studied. Upon antigen exposure, T cells undergo recognition, activation, clonal expansion, effector differentiation, and memory formation (Wik and Skålhegg 2022). Naive CD4+ T cells differentiate into Th1, Th2, Th17, and Treg subsets depending on the surrounding cytokine milieu, accompanied by the upregulation of lineage-defining transcription factors T-bet, GATA-3, RORγt, and FoxP3, respectively. These subsets, in turn, regulate the activity of other immune cells (Ruterbusch et al. 2020; Chang et al. 2020; Friedman et al. 2023). Naive CD8+ T cells differentiate into cytotoxic T lymphocytes (CTLs), which exert direct cytolytic activity against target cells (Philip and Schietinger 2022).

During these processes, β₂-AR expression varies according to T cell activation state and lineage. Naive CD4+ T cells, Th1 cells, and Treg cells primarily express β₂-AR (Bellinger and Lorton 2014). As naive CD4⁺ T cells differentiate into Th1 cells, β₂-AR expression gradually increases. By contrast, Th2 cells lack β₂-AR expression, as histone modification and DNA methylation prevent the expression of the β₂-AR gene (McAlees et al. 2011). β₂-AR is present on Th17 cells, although its relative expression compared with other subsets remains unclear (Carvajal Gonczi et al. 2017). Notably, β₂-AR expression is downregulated as naive CD4+ T cells differentiate into Treg or memory T cells (Guereschi et al. 2013; Rosenberg and Singh 2019). In CD8+ T cells, naive subsets express higher levels of β₂-AR than CTLs, which may explain why β₂-AR signaling preferentially inhibits CD8+ T cell priming in lymph nodes (Daher et al. 2019). Moreover, differential expression has been observed between effector memory T cells and central memory/naive T cells, with effector memory T cells exhibiting higher β2-AR expression (Estrada et al. 2016).

β2-AR-associated T cell signaling

β₂-AR couples to Gs, Gi, and β-arrestin, thereby activating distinct intracellular signaling pathways and exerting complex effects on cellular activities (Dorotea and Ha 2021). In T cells, catecholamines primarily trigger the classical Gs pathway (Lorton and Bellinger 2015) (Fig. 1). Upon binding of norepinephrine or epinephrine, β₂-AR undergoes a conformational change to bind to heterotrimeric G proteins (Gαsβγ). The Gαs subunit then dissociates from Gβγ, exchanges GDP for GTP to become activated. The activated Gαs-GTP complex dissociates and translocates to adenylate cyclase, which converts ATP into cyclic adenosine monophosphate (cAMP). In the immune system, cAMP acts as a potent negative regulator of T cell receptor (TCR)-mediated T cell activation by suppressing proinflammatory signaling cascades and cytokine production (Tian et al. 2011; Wehbi and Taskén 2016; Zhang et al. 2024). Activated cAMP subsequently stimulates protein kinase A (PKA), of which only the PKA-1 isoform contributes to T cell signaling (Wehbi and Taskén 2016; Farooq et al. 2023). Enhanced cAMP-PKA signaling activates C-terminal Src kinase, which in turn inactivates lymphocyte-specific protein tyrosine kinase (Lck) (Bosch et al. 2024). Lck is crucial for the activation of the immunoreceptor tyrosine-based activation motifs on CD3 chains, zeta-chain-associated protein kinase 70, and other substrates (such as Erk and NF-κB) that initiate downstream signal transduction (Wehbi and Taskén 2016; Farooq et al. 2023). Therefore, β₂-AR shut down TCR signaling by phosphorylating and inactivating Lck via the cAMP-PKA pathway. Of note, an alternative PKA-independent Gαs-Lck pathway has been reported to induce T cell apoptosis (Gu et al. 2000). Exchange proteins directly activated by cAMP (EPAC) are also downstream targets of cAMP, however, the selective activation of PKA rather than EPAC leads to the loss of cytokine expression in T cells (Bosch et al. 2024).

The β₂-AR signaling pathway can also enhance Treg-mediated immune suppression. The co-expression of the ecto-ATP diphosphohydrolase (CD39) and ecto-5’-nucleotidase (CD73) in Tregs generates adenosine, which activates the adenosine A2A receptor (A2A). A2A expressed on T cells is tightly coupled with β₂-AR, with β₂-AR mediating an increase in cAMP following adenosine signaling to induce inhibitory signal transduction (Grover et al. 2021). PKA also phosphorylates the cAMP response element-binding protein (CREB), which promotes expression of FoxP3 (a transcription factor essential for Treg development and function) and inhibits the expression of the proinflammatory transcription factor NF-κB (Dorotea and Ha 2021; Lepore et al. 2024).

β2-AR-mediated T cell differentiation

Typically, β2-AR signaling strongly inhibits IFN-γ production to prevent Th1 cell differentiation, thereby indirectly promoting Th2 polarization (Lorton 2014; Sun et al. 2025). However, in the presence of specific cytokines, β2-AR signaling can drive differential Th cell differentiation. In vitro studies have shown that in the presence of IL-12, β2-AR agonists induce activated naive CD4+ T cells to produce more IFN-γ and promote T-bet expression, thus promoting Th1 polarization (Lorton and Bellinger 2015). Furthermore, IFN-γ production increases as IL-12 concentration rises in the environment, attributed to an increase in the amount of IFN-γ produced per CD4+ T cell rather than an increase in cell numbers (Sanders 2012). In contrast, stimulation of β2-AR in differentiated effector Th1 cells inhibits IFN-γ secretion, yet unactivated naive CD4+ T cells remain unaffected by β2-AR signaling, indicating that under physiological conditions, β2-AR maintains immune homeostasis by regulating the magnitude of T cell immune responses.

Regarding Th2 polarization, in the presence of IL-4 and IL-10, β2-AR stimulation induces unactivated naive CD4+ T cells to upregulate GATA-3 expression and secrete IL-4, while reducing IFN-γ production, thus promoting Th2 differentiation (Lorton 2014; Lorton and Bellinger 2015). However, when activated naive CD4+ T cells are co-cultured with IL-4 and exposed to β2-AR agonists, IL-4 reduces Th2 differentiation and decreases IL-4 production. Notably, β2-AR agonists do not directly influence the IL-4 secretion capability of effector Th2 cells, confirming that Th2 cells lack β2-AR expression (Bellinger and Lorton 2014).

It is well established that β2-AR signaling enhances the immunosuppressive effect mediated by Treg cells (Guereschi et al. 2013; Thapa and Cao 2023). However, the impact of β2-AR signaling on Th17/Treg differentiation remains controversial, primarily due to variations in culture conditions, differentiation stages, and agonists. In the presence of autoantigens, naive CD4+ T cells recognize autoantigen peptides presented by antigen-presenting cells. β2-AR signaling at this stage promotes retinoic acid-mediated Treg differentiation to induce immune tolerance. Furthermore, β2-AR stimulation in the presence of Tregs enhances the conversion of naive CD4+ T cells into Tregs, with Tregs themselves playing a key role in mediating this process (Guereschi et al. 2013). Contrary to the above, when IL-6 and TGF-β are present, β2-AR signaling promotes Th17 differentiation and IL-17 A and IL-23 secretion in naive and activated CD4+ T cells, while inhibiting IL-17 A production in effector Th17 cells (Kim, and Jones 2010; Lauten et al. 2025a, b). The effect of β2-AR on the Th17/Treg balance varies depending on the specific agonist as well. For example, terbutaline promotes Treg differentiation in an in vitro induction system by activating glycogen synthase kinase-3 (Mohammadpour et al. 2021), and aids Treg recovery in immune thrombocytopenia models (Zhang et al. 2023), but it also increases the proportion of Th17 cells and the production of IL-17 A in memory Th cells through the cAMP-PKA pathway (Carvajal Gonczi et al. 2017, 2023). However, nebivolol, a β-arrestin-biased β2-AR agonist, inhibits the differentiation of naive CD4+ T cells into Th17 cells and downregulates RORγt expression by selectively suppressing NF-κB phosphorylation (Hajiaghayi et al. 2024).

These seemingly contradictory findings may indicate a context-dependent signal integration mechanism for β2-AR-mediated T cell differentiation and functioning. In this framework, the functional outcome of β2-AR activation is not hardwired, but is dynamically governed by three intersecting variables: cytokine-driven priming, ligand-biased signal transduction and temporal epigenetic gating. Firstly, the aforementioned studies strongly suggest that β2-AR signaling does not autonomously dictate cell lineage commitment, rather, it functions as an immunological rheostat that amplifies the dominant transcription factor network already initiated by the local cytokine microenvironment. In fact, this phenomenon has been documented in other cell types. STAT3 responds to IL-6 signaling and drives Th17 cell differentiation. In myeloid-derived suppressor cells and cardiomyocytes, STAT3 can be activated by β2-adrenergic signaling, thereby regulating cell survival and function (Zhang et al. 2016; Mohammadpour et al. 2019). It is logically plausible that similar pathway crosstalk occurs in other cytokine-driven cell differentiation, yet this has not been experimentally confirmed. Secondly, the divergence in T cell fate is highly dependent on agonist-induced conformational changes. Canonical agonists (e.g., terbutaline) preferentially activate the Gαs-cAMP-PKA-CREB pathway, and skew the balance towards Th17 or Treg depending on the context. Conversely, β-arrestin-biased agonists (e.g., nebivolol) circumvent cAMP production to selectively suppress NF-κB, thereby inhibiting Th17 generation. Thirdly, we presume that the window of vulnerability to adrenergic regulation is strictly gated by T cell’s activation and differentiation state, as lineage commitment dynamically reshapes β2-AR expression, as exemplified by the silencing of β2-AR in Th2 cells. This might be the consequence of epigenetic alterations. Future studies utilizing transcriptomic profiling and temporal knockout models are required to test this integrated framework and define the precise molecular nodes where adrenergic and cytokine signaling converge.

Besides, the β2-AR plays a critical role in regulating the differentiation of CD8+ T cells. Activation of β₂-AR modulates the early transcriptional program of CD8⁺ T cells to facilitate the expression of IL-2Rα and the expansion of effector cells(Estrada et al. 2022). It also drives the expression of circadian rhythm genes, enabling CD8⁺ T cells to easily differentiate into memory precursors during daytime, while driving their differentiation into terminal effectors at night(Sharma et al. 2025). Therefore, β2-AR regulates the proportion and balance of functional T cell subsets through temporal cues.

β2-AR-mediated T cell migration

The migration of T lymphocytes through blood vessels, secondary lymphoid organs, and lymphatic fluid is essential for efficient pathogen recognition and elimination. This process depends on the regulation of lymphocyte homing and migration by various signaling molecules, including adhesion molecules, chemokines, and sphingosine-1-phosphate (S1P), with the C-C chemokine receptor type 7 (CCR7)-C-C motif chemokine ligand 19/21 (CCL19/21) axis and the sphingosine-1-phosphate receptor 1 (S1PR1)-S1P axis being the most important (Masopust and Schenkel 2013). A body of research has addressed the impact of catecholamines on T cell migration via the β₂-AR (Suzuki et al. 2016; Geng et al. 2023; Wang et al. 2025). β₂-AR signaling inhibits the movement and migration of T cells in lymph nodes and the circulation (Wang et al. 2025). Stimulation with β₂-AR agonists can inhibit the emigration of lymphocytes from lymph nodes and reduce lymphocyte infiltration into tissues. β2-AR agonists rapidly impair CD8⁺ T cell movement in tissues and temporarily suppress CD4⁺ T cell mobility, indicating that sympathetic nervous system stimulation can impair leukocyte mobility, potentially weakening the immune response (Devi et al. 2021; Fan et al. 2020). This effect is mediated by β₂-AR and α₁-AR, but not β₁-AR or α₂-AR. The underlying mechanisms involve impairment of cell motility and disruption of chemokine receptor-ligand interactions. To be specific, norepinephrine inhibits leukocyte movement, characterized by transient loss of motility, pseudopod retraction, and cell rounding (Devi et al. 2021). SNS signaling promotes Ca²⁺ influx into T cells by inducing tissue hypoxia, and intracellular calcium overload disrupts actomyosin motility downstream of TCR signaling (Devi et al. 2021). Furthermore, β₂-AR activation enhances retention signals mediated by CCR7 and C-X-C motif chemokine receptor 4 (CXCR4), inhibiting lymphocyte egress from lymph nodes (Nakai et al. 2014). Interestingly, opposed to our belief, CCR7 primarily facilitates B cell retention, while CXCR4 is mainly responsible for T cell retention. It is noteworthy that β₂-AR signaling does not appear to affect lymphocyte homing to lymph nodes (Nakai et al. 2014). The conflicting results regarding the effects of β₂-AR signaling on T cell migration may be attributed to differences in the duration and intensity of adrenergic signaling. Higher concentrations and longer durations of catecholamines or agonists are more likely to impair T cell migration into the circulation.

Pathological implications of β2-AR in T cell immunity

β2-AR-mediated T cell suppression in cancer

Cancer patients often experience chronic stress. Under this circumstance, the SNS is characterized by overgrowth and chronic activation. Tumor-infiltrating nerves, along with tumor and immune cells, are critical components of the TME (Restaino and Vermeer 2021). Under the influence of neurotrophic factors, such as nerve growth factor secreted by tumor cells, these nerves accelerate growth and innervate the TME, thereby promoting cancer progression and metastasis via the neural signaling network (Lee HP& CH 2024). Sensory and sympathetic nerves are the primary types that infiltrate the TME, notably, sensory nerves can be reprogrammed into sympathetic nerves by molecules such as semaphorins, further facilitating tumor invasion (Griffin et al. 2018; Hu et al. 2022; Hung et al. 2023; Fan et al. 2024). Unfortunately, cancer patients often endure mental stress and prolonged anxiety during diagnosis and treatment (Götze et al. 2020), as a result, in the context of chronic adrenergic stress, the SNS contributes to tumor progression either directly by innervating tumor cells or indirectly by modulating immune cell activity (Hernandez et al. 2022; Zhang et al. 2025; Chen et al. 2025) (Fig. 2).

When the SNS is hyperactive, aberrant β₂-AR signaling in the TME may impact all stages of the T cell response, thereby disrupting the normal function of the immune system (Mravec et al. 2020; Thapa and Cao 2023). Analysis of mouse T-lymphoma cells S49 and primary thymocytes revealed that β₂-AR stimulation can induce T cell apoptosis (Gu et al. 2000). In other researches, blocking β₂-AR signaling through various strategies increased the frequency of CD8⁺ tumor-infiltrating lymphocytes increased in breast cancer and melanoma. Additionally, the ratio of effector CD8⁺ T cells to CD4⁺ Tregs was elevated, CD4⁺ T cells showed a tendency to differentiate into the Th1 cell population, and there was a reduction in the expression of programmed cell death protein 1 (PD-1) (Bucsek et al. 2017; Zhang et al. 2025). Analysis of the cellular composition of the lymphoid fraction in MCA205 tumors revealed that propranolol increased CD4⁺ T cell infiltration into the tumor, which occurred without altering the ratio of Tregs to total CD4⁺ T cells, and was also accompanied by an increase in intratumoral CD8⁺ T cells (Fjæstad et al. 2022). Furthermore, chronic β₂-AR signaling enhances the accumulation of immunosuppressive cells, such as Tregs and myeloid-derived suppressor cells, thereby inhibiting the immune activity of effector T cells, particularly in breast cancer patients (Lei et al. 2023).

The immunosuppressive and exhausted phenotypes of T cells induced by sustained β₂-AR stimulation may result from T cell metabolic disorders and signal crosstalk. Under normal conditions, T cells increase glucose uptake and undergo metabolic reprogramming upon activation (Bantug et al. 2018; Romero-Carramiñana et al. 2024; Li et al. 2025a, b). Numerous studies have shown that β₂-AR signaling disrupts the metabolic reprogramming, glycolysis, and oxidative phosphorylation necessary for CD8⁺ T cell differentiation (Qiao et al. 2019, 2021; Thapa and Cao 2023; Switzer et al. 2024). This is attributed to the downregulation of glucose transporter 1, reduced glucose uptake, and decreased mitochondrial mass, respiratory rate, and membrane potential (Kokolus et al. 2013; Qiao et al. 2019), which ultimately leads to a cellular energy crisis and intrinsic apoptosis (Zhang et al. 2022). Furthermore, in patients with lung adenocarcinoma, elevated norepinephrine levels further promote the production of the immunosuppressive metabolite adenosine in tumor cells through the regulation of the WNT7A/β-catenin signaling pathway. As a result, the production of cAMP is increased, which diminishes the proportion and function of CD8⁺ T cells in the tumor microenvironment (Geng et al. 2023).

β2-AR-mediated T cell response in autoimmune diseases

β2-AR signaling plays a crucial regulatory role in autoreactive T cell-dependent autoimmune diseases (ADs), such as multiple sclerosis (MS) and rheumatoid arthritis (RA) (Thapa and Cao 2023), due to the abnormal sympathetic nerve activity in these diseases (Calcagni and Elenkov 2006; Keller et al. 2014; Constantinescu et al. 2024). The disturbance of systemic catecholamines in ADs leads to a shift in the balance of Th cells toward Th1 and Th17-associated autoreactive cells (Moser et al. 2020; Loos et al. 2020), resulting in excessive production of pro-inflammatory cytokines, such as IL-17 and TNF-α, while Th2 cell activity and IL-10 production remain insufficient (Calcagni and Elenkov 2006), thus driving the progression of ADs(Amoriello et al. 2024). Although substantial evidence has indicated that abnormalities in the SNS exacerbate the inflammatory response of autoreactive T cells in ADs, the specific underlying mechanism remains incompletely understood.

Multiple sclerosis (MS)

MS is a chronic, inflammatory, and demyelinating autoimmune disease of the central nervous system (CNS), primarily characterized by the immune system mistakenly attacking myelin (Albelo-Martínez and Rizvi 2025). The regulatory effect of β2-AR on T cells is influenced by the progression of MS (Fig. 3). In early stages of the disease, factors such as pro-inflammatory cytokines, vitamin D deficiency, and intestinal flora imbalance collectively contribute to increased SNS activity and elevated norepinephrine levels (Ghareghani et al. 2018; Pilipović et al. 2023a, b). High norepinephrine levels interact with immune cells, particularly T cells and microglia (Pilipović et al. 2023a, b), through β2-AR, which has a higher density on these cells (Calcagni and Elenkov 2006). Although the classical cAMP-PKA pathway activated by β2-AR typically exhibits an immunosuppressive effect (Wehbi and Taskén 2016), β2-AR can switch to non-canonical signaling in immune cells when SNS is hyper-activated. In microglia, β2-AR activation has been shown to trigger ERK-dependent, PKA-independent pathways (Qian et al. 2009), while β-adrenergic signaling under stress or experimental autoimmune encephalomyelitis (EAE) conditions can promote microglial activation and neuroinflammation (Sugama et al. 2019; Pilipović, Stojić-Vukanić, Prijić, Jasnić, et al. 2020; ten Bosch et al. 2021). Given that MAPK/ERK overactivity in MS-associated microglia is linked to local CNS inflammation, excessive adrenergic signaling in early disease may shift β2-AR-mediated responses toward pro-inflammatory neuroimmune activation. In Lewis rats with early-stage EAE, a commonly used laboratory model of MS, genetic or pharmacological blockade of β-AR reduces the infiltration of pathogenic Th cells into the spinal cord, the production of IL-17 A, and microglial activation, thus alleviating neuroinflammation in these rats (Pilipović, Stojić-Vukanić, Prijić, Jasnić, et al. 2020; Pilipović et al. 2023a, b; Lauten et al. 2025a, b).

Unlike the hyperactivity of the SNS in early stages of MS, as the disease progresses, the locus coeruleus neurons (the primary source of norepinephrine in the CNS) undergo degenerative damage in advanced MS (Poe et al. 2020), leading to damage in central sympathetic nerves and a reduction in norepinephrine levels in their projection areas (Pilipović et al. 2020a, b, 2023a, b). This disruption impairs the classical β-adrenergic immunosuppressive effect, negatively affecting the disease course (Carandini et al. 2021). Treatment with norepinephrine reuptake inhibitors alleviates symptoms in EAE mice and reduces inflammation in the CNS. This is due to enhanced β₂-AR signaling on T cells, which limits the development of EAE and the production of encephalitogenic CD4⁺ T cells that secrete IFN-γ (Araujo et al. 2019). Similarly, norepinephrine inhibits the production of IL-17 and IFN-γ by CD4+ T cells derived from patients with MS through β2-AR signaling (Melnikov et al. 2022). Furthermore, isoprenaline and terbutaline can also inhibit chronic/relapsing EAE in Lewis rats (Chelmicka-Schorr et al. 1989; Wiegmann et al. 1995), suggesting the potential of β₂-AR agonists in the prevention and treatment of MS. A population-based case-control study showed that the use of fenoterol, a β₂-AR agonist, reduced the risk of developing MS by 51% within 5 years (Tsai et al. 2014).

In conclusion, this suggests that targeting β₂-AR or its ligand norepinephrine could serve as a highly promising adjuvant therapy for both early prevention and late-stage treatment of MS by improving immune homeostasis. Additional data from animal studies and clinical samples may offer deeper insights into the pathogenesis of MS and its clinical interventions.

Rheumatoid arthritis (RA)

RA is a chronic inflammatory disease driven by abnormal autoimmunity, characterized by T cell activation, autoantibody production (such as anti-citrullinated peptide antibodies), and release of pro-inflammatory cytokines. These processes lead to synovial inflammation in joints and progressive bone destruction (Smolen et al. 2016). The sympathetic nerve fibers in the synovial tissue of patients with long-standing RA are significantly reduced, potentially resulting in the local loss of their anti-inflammatory effects (Miller et al. 2000). In arthritis-susceptible Lewis rats, sympathectomy using 6-hydroxydopamine increases the severity of adjuvant-induced arthritis (AIA) (Lorton et al. 1996), which is associated with the dysregulation of β2-AR. For instance, the single nucleotide polymorphism at codon 16 of the β2-AR gene shows a positive correlation with the expression of anti-cyclic citrullinated peptide antibodies and the pathogenesis of RA (Malysheva et al. 2008). Moreover, the expression of β2-AR is significantly downregulated in lymphocytes isolated from the synovial fluid of both RA patients and mice (Liu et al. 2018; Wu et al. 2018). Selective activation of β2-AR may alleviate inflammation and disease symptoms. A study showed that β2-AR signaling inhibits the differentiation and function of Th17 cells, exerting an anti-inflammatory effect in a mouse model of collagen-induced arthritis (CIA) (Liu et al. 2018). In this study, CIA induced the production of IL-17 and IL-22, norepinephrine or terbutaline reduced the CIA-induced transition of CD4+ T cells to the Th17 phenotype, while the β2-AR antagonist ICI 118,551 blocked this effect. Similarly, in AIA rats, terbutaline reduces the proliferation of T cells in the spleen, mesenteric lymph nodes, and draining lymph nodes, and decreases the production of pro-inflammatory cytokines in the spleen and MLN (Lorton 2014). Salbutamol also exhibits similar inhibitory effects on CIA in mice (Calcagni and Elenkov 2006), further suggesting the potential role of β₂-AR agonists in the treatment of RA. A multi-cohort analysis showed that among RA patients receiving anti-rheumatic drug treatment, concurrent use of β-blockers was associated with a reduced remission rate (Abuhelwa et al. 2021).

Acute graft-versus-host disease (aGvHD)

aGvHD is a common and severe complication following allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation, caused by donor-derived immune effector cells attacking the recipient’s tissues and organs, and is characterized by high morbidity and mortality (Malard et al. 2023). In allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation, β2-AR can influence aGvHD by regulating the differentiation of donor T cells into cytotoxic cells or Tregs. Compared to β₂-AR−/− donor T cells, the expression and signal transduction of β₂-AR in donor T cells were associated with attenuated aGvHD, and treatment with bambuterol can reduce the severity of aGvHD in allogeneic hematopoietic cell transplantation recipients (Mohammadpour et al. 2021). This may be due to the activation of β₂-AR signaling that leads to the activation of glycogen synthase kinase-3 and results in an increase in Treg generation and a decrease in cytotoxic T cells (Mohammadpour et al. 2021).

In addition to the aforementioned diseases, β2-AR is also involved in conditions such as myasthenia gravis (Ghazanfari et al. 2014; Gafurova et al. 2024), systemic lupus erythematosus (Morand 2018; Bellinger and Lorton 2018), Graves’ disease (Park et al. 2016) and several other ADs in their development. However, there is no corresponding evidence regarding whether and how β2-AR regulate immune cells—particularly T lymphocytes—in these diseases. It may be stated that β-AR-mediated neuroimmune crosstalk remains a crucial avenue for understanding the etiology and mechanisms of ADs. Clarifying the signal crosstalk and phenotypic abnormalities caused by its alterations may facilitate the development of innovative therapeutic strategies.

β1-adrenergic receptor-mediated T cell immune response: novel insights

β1-AR expression on T cells

In addition to β₂-AR, which are predominantly expressed on immune cells, β₁-AR has gained attention for its impact on immune function, particularly T cell immunity. β₁-AR is expressed on various immune cells, including T cells, with its expression in human peripheral lymphocytes being about one-third that of β₂-AR (Yu et al. 2007). When it comes to T cells, the expression of β1-AR in naive or effector T cells is far lower than that of β2-AR(Xu et al. 2019; Globig et al. 2023). However, its expression in regulatory T cells, including CD4⁺CD25⁺FoxP3⁺ natural regulatory T cells and CD4⁺FoxP3⁺CXCR3⁺ mucosa-associated invariant regulatory T cells (Freier et al. 2010; Xu et al. 2019; Fu et al. 2023), terminally exhausted T cells (Globig et al. 2023; Li et al. 2025a, b) and memory Th cells (Hajiaghayi et al. 2024) exceeds that of β2-AR, making it the predominant adrenergic receptor in these populations. Yet, it’s interesting that single-cell sequencing and flow cytometry revealed specific expression of β₁-AR gene on γδ T cells in the dermis of mouse skin, with significantly more β₁-AR⁺ γδ T cells than β₁-AR⁺ αβ T cells in imiquimod-induced psoriatic lesions; these β₁-AR⁺ γδ T cells exacerbated psoriasiform skin inflammation through robust secretion of IL-17 A and IL-17 F (Yu et al. 2024). This suggests that β₁-AR is widely expressed across diverse T cell subsets, and its function may be linked to T cell receptor composition and downstream signaling. In classical αβ T cells, β1-AR appears to be a more reliable indicator of the immunosuppressive status than β2-AR.

β1-AR-mediated T cell response in infections

In many infectious diseases, the immunosuppressive effect mediated by β₁-AR impairs effector T cell responsiveness to infectious agents, contributing to disease persistence. Early studies on stress and anti-infection immunity have shown that acute cold/restraint stress reduces mice’s resistance to Listeria monocytogenes through β₁-AR, primarily affecting the adaptive immune response (Cao et al. 2003). Either alone or in combination with β₂-AR, β₁-AR reduces lymphocyte responsiveness under catecholamine stimulation (Jiang et al. 2020). This phenomenon has also been validated in other disease models. High expression of β₁-AR was found in exhausted CD8⁺ T cells from both animal and human samples with chronic lymphocytic choriomeningitis virus infections. In EAE mice with early life trauma, β₁-AR expression is downregulated, leading to the infiltration of proinflammatory CD4⁺ T cells and severe neuroinflammation. However, administration of a β₁-AR agonist before EAE onset rescues the inflammatory phenotype induced by CD4⁺ T cells (Khaw et al. 2021). Additionally, in a mouse model of sepsis induced by cecal ligation and puncture, β₁-AR activation reduces CD4⁺ T cell proliferation and enhances Treg immunosuppressive capacity, leading to immunosuppression during sepsis (Durand et al. 2022; Liu et al. 2022a, b). The cAMP response element modulator (CREM), a member of the CREB family and another effector of the cAMP-PKA pathway, has been linked to its downstream pathway (Fig. 4). β₁-AR signaling promotes the differentiation of progenitor-like exhausted CD8+ T cells into a more advanced exhausted phenotype via the cAMP-CREM pathway, which can be blocked by the selective β₁-AR antagonist atenolol (Globig et al. 2023). Other supportive evidence also demonstrates CREM promotes exhaustion by suppressing interleukin-2 (IL-2) expression (Trojan et al. 2025), indicating a critical role in CD4+ T cell dysfunction (Maine et al. 2016). Esmolol, an ultra-short-acting β1-AR blocker, reduces T cell apoptosis and restores Th1/Th2 balance in rats during sepsis (Ma et al. 2023). Similarly, a retrospective study indicated that, in patients with relatively good initial immune status, esmolol improves sepsis treatment by regulating the immune response, significantly increasing CD4⁺ and CD8⁺ T cell frequency and improving sepsis survival rates (Jing et al. 2025).

β1-AR-mediated T cell suppression in cancer

Similar to β2-AR, β1-AR is also involved in immunosuppression within the TME. Analysis of scRNA-seq datasets of CD8⁺ T cells across 19 cancer types revealed β1-AR enrichment. Chronic β₁-AR stimulation significantly reduced IFNγ, TNF, and granzyme B production in CD8⁺ CTLs, while increasing the expression of PD-1 and TIM-3 (Wu et al. 2023a, b). A similar phenotype was also identified in a recent scRNA-seq analysis of esophageal squamous cell carcinoma (Li et al. 2025a, b). Additionally, FoxP3⁺CXCR3⁺ MAITreg, a subset of MAIT cells found in peritumoral tissues and tumors of hepatocellular carcinoma patients, enhances the production of IL-10, TGF-β, and IL-17 A via β₁-AR signaling and the cAMP-PKA pathway, promoting immunosuppression in hepatocellular carcinoma (Fu et al. 2023). These findings suggest the potential use of β-blockers in tumors resistant to immune checkpoint blockade (ICB) therapy targeting PD-1 and cytotoxic T-lymphocyte antigen 4 (CTLA-4). Studies have shown that combined blockade of β₁-AR and β₂-AR with ICB significantly reduces the growth of pancreatic ductal adenocarcinoma, whereas ICB alone or ICB combined with either β₁-AR or β₂-AR blockers has no effect (Globig et al. 2023). In colon cancer and melanoma models, tumors treated with the combination therapy of atenolol and ICB were significantly smaller compared to untreated tumors or those treated with atenolol alone, with CD8⁺ T cells secreting more cytokines (Globig et al. 2023). Taken together, these studies suggest β₁-AR as a candidate immune checkpoint that may contribute to T cell exhaustion and immune suppression within the TME, and support further investigation of β-AR blockade as an adjunct to ICB.

β1-AR-mediated T cell response in cardiac dysfunction

The positive regulatory effect of β₁-AR on cardiac function is well-established, primarily through its influence on myocardial cells and the conduction system, and dysregulation of β₁-AR signaling can lead to pathological changes in the heart (Parichatikanond et al. 2024). However, additional evidence suggests that β1-AR also affects cardiac-infiltrating lymphocytes, thereby contributing to the progression of heart diseases. This process is often associated with the presence of β1-AR autoantibodies (β1-AA) (Fig. 5). β1-AA are IgG-class immunoglobulins that specifically target β1-AR and are commonly detected in various cardiovascular diseases, with positive rates of 40%-60% in patients with dilated cardiomyopathy and chronic heart failure, and 17% in patients with hypertensive heart disease (Holthoff et al. 2012). Upon binding to β₁-AR, β₁-AA act in an agonist-like manner (Zweck et al. 2023), inducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis and myocardial fibrosis by continuously activating β₁-AR and preventing their endocytosis, thereby exacerbating cardiac remodeling and arrhythmias (Du et al. 2019a, b, c; Düngen et al. 2020; Chen et al. 2021; Sun et al. 2023a, b). Clinical studies have shown that β₁-AA are independently associated with the readmission rate for heart failure and serve as an independent predictor of sudden cardiac death in patients with chronic heart failure(Pei et al. 2012; Markousis-Mavrogenis et al. 2023). It is generally believed that the production of β1-AA is associated with either molecular mimicry by Trypanosoma cruzi or the exposure of antigenic determinants on β1-AR itself (Jahns et al. 2006), though the exact mechanism has not yet been clarified. In fact, multiple peptide segments of β1-AR have been identified as autoantigens of the heart (Martini et al. 2025), which may potentially serve as a preventive vaccine against hypertension and cardiac insufficiency (Ke et al. 2023).

Despite most studies focusing on the direct effect of β₁-AA on cardiomyocyte function (consistent with the distribution of β₁-AR), some researchers have highlighted its regulatory role in cardiac-infiltrating lymphocytes. In two similar studies, monoclonal antibodies of β1-AA promoted T cell proliferation and IL-6 production, and inhibited IFN-γ via the cAMP/PKA and p38 MAPK signaling pathways, in which IL-6 is mainly responsible for inducing cardiomyocyte apoptosis and hypertrophy (Du et al. 2012, 2019a, b, c). In wild-type mice passively immunized with β1-AA, the ratio of CD4+ to CD8+ T cells was significantly increased, accompanied by left ventricular hypertrophy and dysfunction. This may be due to the enhanced activity of MMP2/MMP9 and increased production of TGF-β1 mediated by T lymphocytes, which in turn promotes cardiac fibrosis (Liu et al. 2002; Du et al. 2019a, b, c). In contrast, long-term immunoadsorption therapy reduced the number of activated T cells and increased the level of Tregs in patients with DCM (Bulut et al. 2010). These studies indicate that β1-AA, the pathological ligand of β1-AR, can activate and promote the differentiation of proinflammatory T cells, whose infiltration into the myocardium contributes to the exacerbation of inflammation and myocardial injury. Such abnormal hyperactivity of immune activity triggered by β1-AA may be associated with the activation of non-canonical pathways. However, another study has proposed that β1-AA can enhance the shift of pre-activated T cells toward mitochondrial fatty acid oxidation metabolism. This shift thereby promotes the differentiation of natural regulatory T cells rather than effector T cells, and this process is achieved through the activation of AMP-activated protein kinase and a decrease in mitochondrial membrane potential (Xu et al. 2019). These illustrate that β₁-AA exerts pleiotropic effects on T cells, potentially depending on the activation status of the T cells. Relevant research is still in its early stages, and future studies should clarify the subset composition and functional profile of T lymphocytes induced by β₁-AA, providing a basis for immunological approaches to treat or prevent heart diseases.

Targeting β-ARs as therapeutic approaches for cancer

Despite ICB therapy has demonstrated outstanding efficacy in treating various tumors, many patients exhibit resistance to ICB either initially or after a certain period of treatment (Haslam and Prasad 2019; Bell and Zou 2024). Given the effects of β-AR on immune dysfunction and tumor growth under chronic stress, accumulating studies have used β-AR blockers or combined them with ICB for the adjuvant treatment of tumors, aiming to reduce the resistance to immunotherapy (Conceição et al. 2021; Dobosz et al. 2022; Jin et al. 2022; Yang et al. 2023; Wen et al. 2025). Results from clinical trials suggest that this method can reduce tumor metastasis, enhance immune cell infiltration, and improve the overall survival rate of cancer patients (Table 2). In metastatic melanoma, the combination of propranolol and pembrolizumab is safe and exhibits robust antitumor activity, achieving an objective response rate of 78% and significantly prolonging overall survival (Gandhi et al. 2021). Similarly, β-blocker administration correlates with improved survival in pancreatic adenocarcinoma. In breast cancer, a one-week preoperative regimen of propranolol has been shown to reduce intratumoral mesenchymal polarization and facilitate immune cell infiltration, thereby diminishing biomarkers linked to metastatic potential (Hiller et al. 2020). Furthermore, β-blocker use is significantly associated with superior objective responses to ICB across entire cohorts and lung cancer subgroups, suggesting a synergistic effect that enhances ICB efficacy (Yan et al. 2022). Regarding selectivity, non-selective β-blockers demonstrate a stronger association with reduced cancer-specific mortality, particularly in patients with localized disease (Udumyan et al. 2017), appearing more effective than selective β-blockers in inhibiting tumor proliferation and extending patient survival (Watkins et al. 2015; Montoya et al. 2016; Blair et al. 2020; Udumyan et al. 2022; Carnet Le Provost et al. 2023; Massalee and Cao 2024).

However, other clinical trials that have advanced to later stages suggest that the combination of ICB and β-blockers yields only marginal benefits for cancer patients. A study synthesizing 12 clinical investigations involving 4,293 patients with solid tumors revealed that, irrespective of cancer type or the specific β-blocker employed, combination therapy failed to significantly extend overall survival or progression-free survival, which contradicted preclinical evidence (Tokunaga et al. 2025). A multi-institutional study has demonstrated that β-blockers failed to improve overall survival, progression-free survival, or objective response rate in patients with unresectable hepatocellular carcinoma receiving immunotherapy (Wu et al. 2023a, b). A meta-analysis has shown that the concurrent use of β-blockers and ICB in patients with various solid tumors is not significantly associated with improved overall survival or progression-free survival (Zhang et al. 2021). Similarly, many clinical trials evaluating ICB plus β-blockers for cancer treatment have been prematurely terminated.

The failure of this combinatorial strategy to demonstrate anticipated efficacy in humans may be attributed to several facts. First, many cancer patients already on β-blockers suffer from cardiovascular comorbidities, which independently elevate mortality risk and confound the true role of β-blockers in tumor treatment. Consequently, a substantial proportion of these patients receive β1-selective blockers rather than the β2-selective agents validated in most preclinical studies. Second, the absence of biomarker-based patient selection, combined with the enrollment of individuals predominantly presenting with advanced metastatic solid tumors, renders the reinvigoration of tumor-specific T cells particularly challenging (Tokunaga et al. 2025), as epigenetic fixation driven by DNA methylation and chromatin closure likely makes the functional recovery of T cells irreversible (Pauken et al. 2016; Philip et al. 2017; Ghoneim et al. 2017). Third, immune-related adverse events induced by broad β-AR blockade and ICB, such as rash and hemophagocytic lymphohistiocytosis, may confound trial endpoints (Gandhi et al. 2021). Last, with respect to species differences, although sympathetic innervation of immune organs has been characterized in both mice and humans, the relative density of innervation has rarely been quantitatively compared across species. Furthermore, standard cold-temperature housing is thought to elevate baseline sympathetic tone in laboratory mice, potentially resulting in higher norepinephrine levels than under human physiological conditions (Hylander et al. 2017, 2022). Additionally, β-blocker studies in animal models often focus on localized endpoints, and the positive results obtained may not mirror the overall therapeutic efficacy or survival benefits seen in human patients. These factors may compromise the translational relevance of preclinical studies.

Collectively, preclinical studies and early-phase clinical trials have shown promising efficacy of combining ICB with β-blockers against malignancies, largely owing to rigorous experimental design and optimized treatment timing. However, most clinical studies are retrospective and typically include all-comer populations, in which β-blockers prescribed for other indications are evaluated for their antitumor effects, limiting assessment of the true therapeutic potential of this combination. Future prospective studies should be specifically designed to address this question by selecting cancers with high sympathetic stress, such as pancreatic and breast cancer, administering non-selective β-blockers during the early stages of tumor progression when immune function is preserved, and minimizing confounding effects from comorbidities to accurately define the actual efficacy of this strategy.

Summary and Outlook

The concept of neuroimmune interaction has been fully emphasized over the past few decades. This review provides a comprehensive summary of the SNS innervation of immune organs across different species, the expression of adrenergic receptors in immune cells—particularly T lymphocytes—and systematically organizes the T cell alterations and pathological impacts mediated by β₂-AR and β₁-AR. The regulatory effects mediated by β2-AR are highly context-dependent (Table 3), while β₁-AR is enriched in Tregs and exhausted T cells and promotes immunosuppression.

It is of note that while the role of β2-AR in regulating T cell migration and lymph node retention is increasingly well-documented, the impact of β₁-AR on these processes remains largely an unexplored area. Current limited evidence suggests that unlike β2-AR, acute β₁-AR stimulation does not mediate the rapid impairment of T cell motility (Devi et al. 2021). However, given that β1-AR is predominantly expressed on highly specialized subsets like Tregs and exhausted T cells, it is imperative to investigate whether chronic β1-AR signaling governs their selective recruitment, infiltration, or retention within pathological niches, such as the tumor microenvironment or chronic infections.

Recent studies have demonstrated that β1-AR signaling contributes to T cell exhaustion and the formation of immune tolerance in diseases such as tumors and sepsis (Durand et al. 2022; Globig et al. 2023). These mechanistic insights hold significant clinical implications: Over the past decade, β-blockers, originally approved for cardiovascular diseases, have found new indications, including promising applications in cancer therapy (Fumagalli et al. 2020; Lorente et al. 2025). Both retrospective and prospective studies have illustrated that β-blockers—especially non-selective ones—exert a synergistic effect with cancer radiotherapy, chemotherapy, or immunotherapy by enhancing T cell-mediated anti-tumor responses and inhibiting tumor growth, ultimately improving patient survival outcomes (Gandhi et al. 2021; Carnet Le Provost et al. 2023; Lin et al. 2023). Notably, preclinical and clinical studies collectively indicate that non-selective β-blockers are more effective than selective β1-AR blockade in suppressing tumor growth and enhancing anti-tumor immunity. This phenomenon may be explained by two factors. First, β1-AR expression within the tumor microenvironment remains limited, it is predominantly found on exhausted T cells and suppressive immune cells, which constitute only a minor fraction of the immune populations involved in anti-tumor responses. Moreover, early blockade is required to prevent irreversible exhaustion. The stringent requirements for both the target cell population and the therapeutic time window impose significant constraints on efficacy. Second, β2-AR signaling in T cells exhibits complicated effects, which are often deliberately induced under experimental conditions for mechanistic investigation. However, in the intact organism, the immunosuppressive effects attributed to β2-AR likely reflect the integrated outcome of multiple convergent factors. Consequently, when non-selective β-blockers are administered in cancer therapy, the simultaneous blockade of both β1- and β2-AR manifests as a synergistic, rather than antagonistic, effect overall.

Interestingly, in patients with cardiomyopathy who have β1-AA in their bloodstream, β1-AA-dependent β1-AR signaling appears to enhance the proinflammatory effects of T cells in the heart. This discovery connects catecholamine signaling, traditionally associated with endocrine and neural processes, to autoimmune mechanisms, offering a new perspective on the pathogenesis of diseases like heart failure and cardiomyopathy. Moreover, recent work by our team found that both β₁-AA and norepinephrine promote the activation of CD4⁺ T cells infiltrating the heart, driving an inflammatory phenotype and the loss of co-stimulatory molecules in these cells. These effects are mediated exclusively by β₁-AR, not α-AR or β₂-AR. In summary, β-AR signaling is complex and multifaceted in T cell biology, offering new avenues for the development of β-AR-targeted immunomodulatory strategies in various diseases.

The innovative application of technologies and advances in methodologies have facilitated our characterization of β-AR signaling and its spatiotemporally specific regulation. For instance, receptor visualization and motion analysis developed based on high-affinity fluorescent ligands for receptors and line-scanning fluorescence correlation spectroscopy technology enable high-resolution imaging of β-AR in living cells, capture the motion characteristics of receptors at the sub-second scale, and simultaneously eliminate functional interference caused by direct modification of receptors (Bathe-Peters et al. 2021).This provides a powerful tool for further exploring adrenergic signaling in immune cells. Additionally, the development and application of optogenetics and photopharmacology enable more specific regulation of receptor behavior. For example, functionally selective photosensitive β2-ARs and light-controlled ligands allow researchers to precisely modulate the downstream effects of β2-ARs in a spatiotemporally specific manner (Siuda et al. 2015; Shi et al. 2025). The significance of these technologies lies in their ability to precisely activate or inhibit specific receptors and achieve tissue-specific β-AR regulation through localized light irradiation, offering a new perspective for targeted therapy of local tissue cells.

Certain clinical studies and systematic reviews have highlighted the impact of genetic polymorphisms in β1-AR and β2-AR on the therapeutic effects of β-blockers, particularly in cardiovascular diseases (Duarte and Cavallari 2021; Abbes et al. 2025). Among these, stronger evidence supports the association of two β₁-AR gene variants (ADRB1 rs1801252 and ADRB1 rs1801253) with reduced responsiveness to β-blocker therapy and an increased risk of major adverse cardiovascular events (Magvanjav et al. 2017; Duarte and Cavallari 2021). This underscores the potential value of β-AR genotyping in guiding β-blocker use. Nevertheless, these findings have mainly been studied in cardiovascular pharmacogenetic contexts, while the effect of β-blockers (or agonists) on immune cells expressing different β-AR variants remains unexplored, with a notable absence of genome-wide association studies (GWAS) in particular. Further research is needed to clarify the role of β-AR genetic polymorphisms in immune cells and their relevance in treating diseases through large-scale GWAS and prospective clinical cohorts, thus confirming the need for personalized treatment strategies based on patients’ β-AR expression profiles.

In addition to pharmacological modulation of the sympathetic β‑adrenergic signaling axis, the parasympathetic nervous system exerts significant control over innate and adaptive immune responses, representing a complementary and clinically relevant avenue for neuroimmune intervention. Vagus nerve stimulation (VNS) engages the cholinergic anti‑inflammatory pathway by promoting the release of acetylcholine that interacts with α7‑nicotinic acetylcholine receptors on macrophages to suppress production of pro‑inflammatory cytokines such as TNF‑α, IL‑1β, and IL‑6, and enhancing shifts in T helper cell subset balance and regulatory T cell frequencies in preclinical models (Liu et al. 2024; Ye et al. 2026). Systematic reviews of both animal and clinical studies support the concept that electrical or non-invasive VNS can attenuate inflammatory markers and alter immune cell profiles across inflammatory conditions. For instance, bioelectronic VNS in patients with RA significantly reduced circulating TNF-α levels and disease activity scores (Koopman et al. 2016), while selective optogenetic stimulation of vagal afferent fibers in mice with kidney injury engaged a C1 neuron-sympathetic-splenic nerve axis attenuated renal inflammation and tissue damage (Tanaka et al. 2021), suggesting that neuromodulation may serve as a promising adjunctive therapy to conventional pharmacotherapy.

Despite growing evidence supporting the role of β-AR in T cell immunoregulation, some key limitations remain. The lack of highly specific genetic tools or pharmacological interventions for distinct receptor subtypes hampers in-depth dissection of causal mechanisms. The current evidence supporting β-AR-mediated immunomodulation should be interpreted according to the experimental system from which it is derived. In vitro studies are valuable for defining receptor-coupled signaling pathways, such as cAMP-PKA-CREM activation, TCR inhibition, or cytokine regulation, but they cannot fully recapitulate neural input, tissue architecture, stromal interactions, systemic catecholamine dynamics, or drug metabolism. Mouse models provide important causal evidence linking sympathetic signaling to T cell dysfunction, tumor progression, autoimmunity, or sepsis, however, these findings may be influenced by species-specific receptor expression, housing temperature, stress exposure, disease model design, and pharmacological dose. By contrast, human evidence remains more limited and is often derived from retrospective clinical cohorts, in vitro immune-cell assays, or small interventional studies. Therefore, β1-AR and β2-AR should currently be viewed as promising neuroimmune targets whose therapeutic relevance requires further validation in prospective, biomarker-guided human studies.
