The Family Physician Training Program in the World: A Scoping Review Protocol
Authors: Fariba Ahmadi, Mostafa Amini-Rarani, Alireza Jabbari
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Background:Since the emergence of family medicine in the world, different approaches to training family physicians have been utilized. However, there are no united criteria or processes defined for the field. Therefore, this study intends to gather and use the worldwide experience in training family physicians.Methods:This protocol uses the 2020 updated version of Joanna Briggs Institute’s (JBI) scoping review and the PCC framework (population, concept, context). The study will review databases and use search engines and related websites by considering the entrance criteria. Resources will be entered into EndNote. The study will use PRISMA for the selection process and content analysis to analyze the studies.Results:As this is a protocol, no empirical data are presented. The anticipated findings will systematically map family physician training programs worldwide, summarizing extracted data via tables and narrative synthesis across key dimensions: admission criteria, curriculum, core competencies, training duration, teaching/assessment methods, accreditation standards, and career pathways. The synthesis will also highlight contextual variations, knowledge gaps, thematic convergences, and barriers across different health systems. The mapped evidence will inform policymakers on transferable models and contextual adaptations, while acknowledging limitations such as publication and language bias.Conclusions:It seems that the study results could help policymakers familiarize themselves with different dimensions of different methods of training family physicians in the world and create training plans adoptable by healthcare systems.

Introduction

In the last two decades, healthcare systems have been under pressure because of not fulfilling the needs (e.g., controlling non-communicable diseases)[1] and healthcare expectations (quality control and full accessibility)[2] and the urge to carry on with programs matching the situation (reforms).[3] As such, we could point at family medicine programs as the first contact point between society and health care[4] that strengthen primary health care (PHC) and improve universal health care (UHC).[5] Family physicians are the leaders of the family medical team and the gatekeepers of health care.[6] Almost every study emphasizes that a family physician is different than a general practitioner and the necessity to pass related courses to provide family medicine services.[7]

This field aims to train specialists who provide continuing, comprehensive, and personal care for people in coverage.[3] According to different studies, there are various methods to train family practitioners worldwide, such as modular and academic education, which vary in admission requirements, educational content, and course length. In most countries, the time length to become a family physician is 3 to 5 years.[34]

The goal of this study is to identify various methods of training family physicians in different countries to help policymakers attain the criteria, processes, and challenges in various family physician training methods and use these to adopt proper and standard planning for training family physicians internationally.

Methods and Analysis

A scoping review is determined as an appropriate method for the goal of this study since it describes the process for producing a general theme of a specific topic. To carry out the study, in addition to the published articles in different databases, gray literature is also investigated. Resources are searched and reviewed by the first member of the research team and approved by the second and third members. When two researchers cannot decide about an article, the responsible writer makes the decision on the argued source. The research results are presented in a PRISMA diagram. Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) protocol, updated in 2020, is used for gathering data. This 9-stage protocol includes introducing the methods and plan details.[8]

Stage 1: Defining and aligning the objective/s and questions

The precision and clarity of the research questions help with determining the effective search strategy. Also, choosing the inclusion and exclusion criteria aids in restoring records, improving protocol, facilitating the effectiveness of text search, and providing a clear structure for presenting the scoping review reports.[9] The goal of this protocol is to identify the criteria, processes, and challenges in training family physicians in different countries. A scoping review is utilized to answer the following questions:

Stage 2: Developing and aligning the inclusion criteria

The inclusion criteria include: 1) studies from 1969- when family medicine was introduced as a specialty in the world- to 2023 and 2) published studies in English and Farsi. The exclusion criteria are studies without their available full text. This study uses the PCC framework using [Table 1] the JBI framework.[10]

Stage 3: Search strategy

The search strategy adheres to the three-stage process recommended by the JBI. The first stage includes the initial search of at least two online databases pertinent to the topic. The second stage entails a meticulous review of related terms found in the titles and abstracts of articles, leading to the compilation of a list of phrases. Then, with the assistance of a librarian and utilizing the compiled phrases, the search strategy is formulated and executed across credible databases [Table 2]. The third and last stage contains manual searching on Google and related websites.

Stage 4: Searching for the evidence

The study uses databases including Emerald, ProQuest, PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, Google browsing, and related websites such as WONCA to restore the gray literature.

Stage 5: Selecting the evidence

The study results will be entered into EndNote. Duplicated articles are removed. Article selection contains two steps. First step: titles and abstracts of all articles are reviewed at least by two people based on the checklist. The accountable writer will decide on the argued matters. Ineligible titles and abstracts are removed, and other articles will be thoroughly studied by two researchers. Any contrasting opinions will be assessed at the end of the screening process. When two researchers cannot agree about an article, the assigned writer would decide on the argued resource. Second step: Restoring the full text of articles. Drawing 1 shows the article selection protocol.

Stage 6: Extracting the evidence

General information, such as the lead author, article title, study type, studied the country, publication time, publication type (original article and gray literature), and conceptual details, including inclusion criteria and processes and challenges in training family physicians, are collected through data mining. Indeed, with more extensive reviewing, this stage can be edited. The chart will be updated according to the corrections.

Stage 7: Analysis of the evidence

Content analysis is used to analyze the obtained resources. Researchers avoid using predetermined categories. In return, they allow categories and their names to emerge from the data. Thus, the results are classified into main conceptual topics such as criteria, processes, and challenges in training family physicians separately.

Stage 8: Presentation of the results

The search results, study selection, restoration, and final abstract are presented based on the PRISMA flowchart [Figure 1].

PRISMA flowchart for Scoping review

During the review and improvement of the protocol, the writers assess and correct the drafts of charts and diagrams according to the reviewers’ knowledge of the study contents. After approval, the result will be published. The goals of the reviewed resources, adopted concepts, and the results from the three questions: 1) reviewing criteria, 2) processes, and 3) challenges in training family physicians are described.

Stage 9: Summarizing the evidence, making conclusions, and noting any implications of the findings

To making a useful summary of the data, the study synthesizes all criteria, processes, and challenges in training family physicians, restored from all resources, and produces a list of criteria, processes, and challenges in training family physicians. Consequently, these will be organized into new categories. The primary goal is extracting the criteria, processes, and challenges in training family physicians. Eventually, the summary will be handed to policymakers as a report and presented as an article for researchers in the future.

Conclusions

The results from this study will be published as an article in a reputable journal for the use of researchers. It seems that the study results could help policymakers familiarize themselves with different dimensions of different methods of training family physicians in the world and create training plans adoptable by healthcare systems.

Dissemination

The results from this study will be presented as an article that can provide policymakers with visions to make effective policies for training family physicians based on the healthcare of their countries. Also, the findings of this study can identify the potential grounds for standardizing family physician training at an international level.
