Crystallizations of generalized lens spaces
Basudeb Datta

Abstract
We present some natural crystallizations of the generalized lens spaces L ⁡ ( p , q 1 , … , q n ) L(p,q_{1},\dots,q_{n}) for integers p ≥ 2 p\geq 2 , n ≥ 1 n\geq 1 and integers q 1 , … , q n q_{1},\dots,q_{n} relatively prime to p p . These crystallizations are quotients of triangulations of the sphere S 2 ​ n + 1 S^{2n+1} .

1
Introduction

A simplicial cell structure
K
of a
d
-dimensional manifold
M
contains at least
d+1
vertices. If
K
has exactly
d+1
vertices and
K
induces a pl structure on
M
then
K
is called a
crystallization
of
M
. In that case, the dual graph
\Lambda(K)
has a natural proper edge coloring
j
with
d+1
colors. In the literatures, the
(d+1)
-regular colored graph
(\Lambda(K),j)
is called a crystallization of
M
. In
[
7
]
, Pezzana showed existence of crystallization for every closed connected PL manifold. For
p\equiv\pm 1
(mod
q
), crystallization of the lens space
L(p,q)
was constructed in
[
2
]
. In Section
3
, we construct crystallizations of the generalized lens spaces
L(p,q_{1},\dots,q_{n})
for integers
p\geq 2
,
n\geq 1
and integers
q_{1},\dots,q_{n}
relatively prime to
p
. The derived subdivisions of these crystallizations give us triangulations of all generalised lens spaces. These crystallizations are more natural than any other simplicial cell structures or triangulations of (generalised) lens spaces.
These crystallizations are quotient of triangulations of the sphere
S^{2n+1}
. The case where
p=2
is known. Recently, it is shown in
[
1
]
that
L(2,1,\dots,1)
is the unique facet minimal crystallization of
\mathbb{RP}^{2n+1}
.

2
Preliminaries

By a poset, we mean a finite set
X
with a partial ordering
\leq
. By
\alpha<\beta
, we mean
\alpha\leq\beta
and
\alpha\neq\beta
. A bijection
f:(K,\leq)\to(L,\leq)
between two posets is called an
isomorphism
if
a\leq b
if and only if
f(a)\leq f(b)
for
a,b\in K
. In such a case, we say
(K,\leq)
and
(L,\leq)
are
isomorphic
. An isomorphism
f:(K,\leq)\to(K,\leq)
is called an
automorphism
of
(K,\leq)
. The set
\operatorname{Aut}(K)
of all the automorphisms of
(K,\leq)
is a group under composition and is called the
automorphism group
of
(K,\leq)
. Any subgroup of
\operatorname{Aut}(K)
is called a
group of automorphisms
of
K
.

A CW-complex is said to be
regular
if all of its closed cells are homeomorphic to closed balls. Given a CW-complex
M
, let
{\mathcal{M}}
be the set of all closed cells of

together with the empty set

. Then
{\mathcal{M}}
is a poset, where the partial ordering is given by set inclusion. This poset

is said to be the

of

. If

and
N
are two finite regular CW-complexes with isomorphic face posets then
M
and
N
are homeomorphic. A regular CW-complex

is said to be
simplicial
, if the
boundary complex

\partial\sigma:=\{\alpha\in{\mathcal{M}}:\alpha<\sigma\}
of each cell
\sigma
of
M
is isomorphic (as a poset) to the boundary complex of a simplex.

A
simplicial cell complex
or
simplicial poset

K=(K,\leq)
of dimension
d
is a poset isomorphic to the face poset
{\mathcal{M}}
of a
d
-dimensional simplicial CW-complex
M
. The topological space
M
is called the
geometric carrier
of
K
and is also denoted by
|K|
. If a topological space
N
is homeomorphic to
|K|
, then
K
is said to be a
simplicial cell structure
(or
simplicial cell decomposition
or
pseudotriangulation
)
of
N
. Simplicial posets are also referred to in the literature as
CW posets
,
pseudo-simplicial complexes
or
pseudocomplexes
(see
[
3
,
4
,
6
]
for details). For
\alpha,\beta\in K
, if
\alpha\leq\beta
, then we say that
\alpha
is a
face
of
\beta
. For a
d
-dimensional simplicial poset
K
, let
f_{j}(K)
denote the number of

0\leq j\leq d
. The
(d+1)
-tuple

is called the

or

of

.

For a cell
\alpha
in a simplicial poset
K

is a simplicial poset and is said to be the
boundary
of
\alpha
. If
\partial\alpha
is isomorphic to the boundary complex of a
j

is a called a

-cell
of
K
. The 0-cells and 1-cells of a simplicial poset

are called the
vertices
and

of

respectively. The set

of vertices of a simplicial poset

is called the
vertex set
of
K
. Two vertices of

are called
adjacent
in

if they are faces of an edge. For two cells
\alpha\neq\beta
, the intersection
\partial\alpha\cap\partial\beta
may not be a single cell (and its faces). If
\partial\alpha\cap\partial\beta
is a cell (and its faces) for any two cells
\alpha\neq\beta
then the simplicial post is a simplicial complex. Cells of a simplicial complex are called
simplices
. For a simplex
\alpha
in a simplicial complex

,

is called the
vertex set
of
\alpha
. Since a simplex

of a simplicial complex

is uniquely determined by
V(\alpha)
, we identify
\alpha
with
V(\alpha)
.

A
d
-dimensional simplicial cell complex is called
pure
if all its maximal cells are
d
-cells. In a
d
-dimensional simplicial cell complex

, the
d
-cells and the
(d-1)
-cells are called

and

respectively.
The

of a pure simplicial cell complex

is the graph whose vertices are the facets of

and edges are the ordered pairs
(\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2}\},\alpha)
, where
\alpha
is a ridge and is a common face of the facets
\sigma_{1}
,
\sigma_{2}
. Since each 1-cell of

corresponds to a geometric
1
-simplex, it follows that the graph
\Lambda(K)
has no loops. Observe, however, that
\Lambda(K)
can have multiple edges running between the same two vertices.

Clearly, a simplicial cell structure

of a (closed)
d
-manifold

contains at least

vertices. If

has exactly

vertices and

. Suppose

. For any facet

.
Clearly, if

-regular colored graph

Suppose

is the face poset of the simplicial CW-complex

. Let

be the derived subdivision of

Since the topological space

are same, we can identify

A simplicial complex

is called a

if the geometric carrier

with induced pl structure is a PL manifold. In that case, we say

(or

)

. Therefore, if

is a combinatorial manifold.

Let

and

be two simplicial complexes with disjoint vertex sets. Since

,

is a simplicial complex (called the

and

) containing both

and

. Note that the vertex set

Let

be a group of automorphisms of a poset

. Suppose,

is the face poset of a CW-complex

. Then

has a natural action on

and

is again a CW-complex. Clearly, the quotient poset

is isomorphic to the face poset of the CW-complex

need not be a simplicial cell complex for a simplicial cell complex

. In

, the authors have defined good action and shown that the good actions on the category of simplicial cell complexes are natural actions. The action of

on simplicial cell complex

is

, if two vertices of

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
X
be a simplicial cell complex, and let

.
The quotient poset

is a simplicial cell complex if and only if the action of

on

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a simplicial cell complex, and let
G\leq\operatorname{Aut}(X)
.
If the action of

on

is good, then
\left|X/G\right|\cong|X|/G
.

For

, let
S^{\hskip 0.56905pt2n+1}=\{(z_{0},z_{1},\dots,z_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{\hskip 0.56905ptn+1}\,:\,|z_{0}|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2}+\cdots+|z_{n}|^{2}=1\}

\displaystyle:=(e^{2\pi i/p}z_{0},e^{2\pi iq_{1}/p}z_{1},\dots,e^{2\pi iq_{n}/p}z_{n}).

(1)

Then

.
