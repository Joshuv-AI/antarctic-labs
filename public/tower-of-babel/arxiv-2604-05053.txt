Coherent Sheaves in Logarithmic Geometry Thanks: This document was obtained by merging two manuscripts which independently produced similar results. One project was joint work of Dell, Kennedy-Hunt, and Manali Rahul. The other project was joint work of Hu and Schimpf.
Hannah Dell, Xianyu Hu, Patrick Kennedy-Hunt, Kabeer Manali Rahul, Maximilian Schimpf

Abstract
. This paper introduces an abelian category of logarithmic coherent sheaves that arranges coherent sheaves across all expansions and root stacks of a simple normal crossing degeneration. Formally, logarithmic coherent sheaves are coherent sheaves in the full logarithmic étale topology. We develop a suite of tools that reduces the evaluation of the basic functors of homological algebra to the conventional calculation on a computable logarithmic alteration. A second paper will establish good properties of the associated logarithmic derived category. We thus offer a unified perspective on logarithmic moduli spaces of coherent sheaves: The logarithmic Quot spaces motivated by Maulik and Ranganathan’s logarithmic Donaldson–Thomas theory, the logarithmic Picard group constructed by Molcho and Wise, and moduli spaces of logarithmic parabolic sheaves as developed by Borne, Talpo, and Vistoli. In establishing the connection with logarithmic Picard groups, we offer a new interpretation of chip firing as the combinatorial shadow to a logarithmic version of S-equivalence.

0.2.
Static quasi-coherent sheaves

A conventional quasi-coherent sheaf on

the equations of the toric boundary divisors of

form a

\text{QCoh}_{\text{sta}}(\underline{X})

Theorem B

After restricting its domain, the up functor

0.4.
Moduli of invertible logarithmic sheaves

We now assume that

An

is a rank one locally free logarithmic coherent sheaf. Let

In conventional algebraic geometry, the coarse space associated to the stack of line bundles on a smooth and proper curve, called the Picard scheme, is an abelian scheme, and is in particular separated and universally closed. The functors of points of both

1.2.
Logarithmic étale sheaves

In this section, we study an appropriate notion of log étale
\mathcal{O}
-modules and express them in terms of conventional étale
\mathcal{O}
-modules on log alterations. Translating computations involving log étale
\mathcal{O}
-modules to computations involving only conventional
\mathcal{O}
-modules is a theme of this paper.

Definition 1.2.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a DM
k
-stack with a log structure. We define the
logarithmic étale structure sheaf

\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
as the sheaf on
\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}
associated to the presheaf
\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\text{pre}}
defined by
\Gamma(\mathcal{U}\rightarrow\mathcal{X},\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\text{pre}})=\Gamma(\underline{\mathcal{U}},\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{U}}})
. Let
\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}\coloneqq(\text{Sh}(\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}),\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}})
be the resulting ringed topos. For any morphism
f\colon\mathcal{X}\rightarrow\mathcal{Y}
, we denote by
f_{\text{l\'{e}t}}\colon{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}\rightarrow{\mathcal{Y}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}
the corresponding morphism of ringed topoi.

Remark 1.2.2
.

(1)

To our knowledge, the above definition of structure sheaf was first recorded by Niziol
[
81
, Section 2.2]
.

(2)

The sheafification step is necessary as
[
80
, Remark p. 671]
and
[
75
, Example 6.5]
detail an example of a log modification
q\colon X^{\prime}\rightarrow X
for which the associated
\underline{q}^{\sharp}\colon\Gamma(\underline{X},\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}})\rightarrow\Gamma(\underline{X}^{\prime},\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}^{\prime}})
is not injective, despite the fact that
q
is a log étale cover.

The bridge between conventional
\mathcal{O}
-modules and log étale
\mathcal{O}
-modules is provided by the adjoint pair of functors introduced in Construction
1.2.3
. Proposition
1.2.5
will provide an alternative way to think about of these functors.

Construction 1.2.3
.

Given a log alteration
q\colon\mathcal{X^{\prime}}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, consider the composite morphism

\mathsf{rest}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\colon{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}\xrightarrow{q_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{-1}}{(\mathcal{X}^{\prime})}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}\xrightarrow{\pi_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}{(\underline{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{triv}}^{\prime})}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}^{r}=(\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime})_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{r}

to the conventional ringed étale topos of
\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}
, where
\pi\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}_{\text{triv}}
is the canonical morphism to the underlying DM stack equipped with the trivial log structure. Note here that
q_{\text{l\'{e}t}}
is an equivalence of ringed topoi as
q
is a monomorphic log étale cover.

Associated to any morphism of ringed topoi are an adjoint pair of pullback and pushforward functors
[
90
, 03D5]
, which we call

and

\Uparrow\coloneqq\mathsf{rest}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}^{*}\colon\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}})\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\textstyle\longrightarrow\\[-1.29167pt]
\textstyle\longleftarrow\end{subarray}}\operatorname{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}})\colon(\mathsf{rest}_{\mathcal{X},\mathcal{X}^{\prime}})_{*}\eqqcolon\Downarrow_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}.

Remark 1.2.4
.

Using the counterexample

of Remark

(

, we see that

factors through

Proposition 1.2.5
.

Let

be a log alteration. Then for any sheaf

on

and strict étale neighborhood

Remark 1.2.6

Note that

does not arise from a morphism of sites and hence

is not a priori defined on the level of presheaves. However, there is still a functor

Proof of Proposition

Using the notation of Construction

The second claim reduces to the fact that

for a similar statement.

55
, Theorem B]
.
∎

Remark 2.5.3
.

(1)

The Gröbner stratification can be explicitly computed (often by hand) using Gröbner theory and any finite piecewise linear stratification can always be refined by a fan. Thus, Proposition
2.5.2
gives an algorithm to compute statifications of
F
.

(2)

More generally, this algorithm applies whenever
X
is of finite type over
k
, since locally, one can always embed
X
into a toric variety, whose singularities can always be resolved by toric blowup.

We present the following examples to illustrate Remark
2.5.3

(
1
)
.

Example 2.5.4
.

Consider the counterexample of Remark
1.2.2

(
2
)
, which is given by

i\colon X=V(x^{2},y^{2})\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{2}_{x,y}

with log structure induced by the toric log structure on
\mathbb{A}^{2}_{k}
.
To compute a statification of the structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}
, we use the following resolution:

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}\xrightarrow{(y^{2},-x^{2})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}^{\oplus 2}\xrightarrow{(x^{2},y^{2})}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}\longrightarrow\underline{i}{}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}\longrightarrow 0.

The Gröbner stratification of
k[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}]\cdot(y^{2},-x^{2})\subset k[x^{\pm},y^{\pm}]^{\oplus 2}
coincides with the fan of
q\colon\text{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{A}^{2}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{2}
, hence it follows from Proposition
2.5.2
that the structure sheaf of
X^{\prime}=X\times_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}\text{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{A}^{2}
is static.

Example 2.5.5
.

We repeat the above analysis for
[
75
, Example 6.8]
, where
X
is the strict closed subscheme

i\colon X=V(x^{3}z-xy^{2}z,\;xy^{3}-xyz^{2},\;yz^{3}-x^{2}yz)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{A}^{3}_{x,y,z}.

The structure sheaf
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}
admits a resolution

0\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}\xrightarrow{(y,z,x)}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}^{\oplus 3}\xrightarrow{(x^{3}z-xy^{2}z,\;xy^{3}-xyz^{2},\;yz^{3}-x^{2}yz)}\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{3}}\longrightarrow\underline{i}{}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}}\longrightarrow 0,

which leads us to consider the Gröbner stratification of
k[x^{\pm},y^{\pm},z^{\pm}]\cdot(y,z,x)\subset k[x^{\pm},y^{\pm},z^{\pm}]^{\oplus 3}

is static.

2.6.
What is staticity good for?

2.6.1.
Logarithmic étale descent and exactness

In conventional scheme theory, there is a close connection between modules and quasi-coherent sheaves on an affine scheme. Corollary
2.6.2
demonstrates that in log geometry there is a parallel close connection between static sheaves and global sections on an affine patch.

Proposition 2.6.1
.

Let
f\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
be a morphism between DM
k
-stacks with log structures, and
F
be a static quasi-coherent sheaf on
\mathcal{X}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
.

(1)

If
f
is log flat, then
L_{1}\underline{f}^{*}F=0
.

(2)

If
f
is log flat and
\mathcal{X}
is static, then
L_{i}\underline{f}^{*}F=0
for all
i\geq 1
.

(3)

If
f
is a log alteration, then we have

F=R\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F.

Proof.

The claims about derived pullback are local in
\mathcal{X}
and
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
, hence we may assume that
\mathcal{X}=\text{Spec}(P\rightarrow A)
,
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}=\text{Spec}(Q\rightarrow B)
,
F=\widetilde{M}
for an
A
-module
M
, and
f
admits a chart
P\hookrightarrow Q
given by an inclusion of monoids. Since
k[Q^{\text{gp}}]
is a free
k[P^{\text{gp}}]
-module, it is flat over
k[P]
. Hence

\text{Tor}^{k[P]}_{i}(k[Q],M)=\text{Tor}^{k[P]}_{i+1}(k[Q^{\text{gp}}]/k[Q],M)=0

for all
i\geq 1
as
M
is static. By Lemma
2.3.3

(
1
)
, we thus have
\text{Tor}_{1}^{A}(A\otimes_{k[P]}k[Q],M)=0
, which implies the
L_{1}\underline{f}^{*}F=0
. In the case that
A
is static, the vanishing of all higher Tors follows from Lemma
2.3.3

(
2
)
.

It suffices to verify the last claim for log modifications and root stacks separately. Indeed, for root stacks, this holds as

R\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=\mathcal{H}^{0}(R\underline{f}{}_{*}L\underline{f}^{*}F)=\mathcal{H}^{0}(F\otimes_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{\mathcal{X}}}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\underline{f}{}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X^{\prime}}})=F,

where we used the projection formula
[
35
, Corollary 4.12]
and Proposition
1.1.2
(note that this did not use that
F
is static). Since the claim for log modifications is local in
\mathcal{X}
, we may therefore assume that
\mathcal{X}=\text{Spec}(P\rightarrow A)
,
F=\widetilde{M}
and
f
arises as a pullback

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{\mathcal{X}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Bl}_{k[I]}\text{Spec}(k[P])}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\text{Spec}(k[P])}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces i}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\underline{f}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces g}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces h}

for some ideal
I\subset P
. Since
g
is affine, it follows that the natural homomorphism
F\rightarrow R\underline{f}{}_{*}L\underline{f}^{*}F\rightarrow R\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F
is an isomorphism if and only if
R\underline{g}{}_{*}F=\underline{g}{}_{*}F\rightarrow R\underline{g}{}_{*}R\underline{f}{}_{*}(f^{*}F)
is an isomorphism. However, by affine base change
[
90
, 02KG]
, we have

R\underline{g}{}_{*}R\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=R\underline{h}{}_{*}R\underline{i}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=R\underline{h}{}_{*}\underline{i}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=R\underline{h}{}_{*}\underline{h}^{*}\underline{g}{}_{*}F.

Hence, it suffices to prove the claim for
\underline{g}{}_{*}F
instead, which is also static by Lemma
2.3.1
. Therefore, we may assume that
\mathcal{X}=\text{Spec}(k[P])
is static and hence
L_{i}\underline{f}^{*}F=0
for all
i\geq 1
as shown above. The claim now follows from the projection formula:

R\underline{f}{}_{*}\underline{f}^{*}F=R\underline{f}{}_{*}(L\underline{f}^{*}F)=F\otimes_{\mathcal{O_{\mathcal{X}}}}^{\mathbb{L}}R\underline{f}{}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}}=F,

where

R\underline{f}{}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}^{\prime}}
=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}}

follows from
[
49
, Theorem 11.3]
.
∎

Corollary 2.6.2
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a DM
k
-stack with a log structure and

a static quasi-coherent sheaf on

. Then we have

and for any log alteration

.

Moreover, for any log étale morphism

If in addition

is static, then

for all

.

Proof.

The claims follow from Corollary
1.3.6

(

)
and
(

)
as well as Proposition
2.6.1
.
∎

Remark 2.6.3
.

(1)

In particular, for any exact sequence

of quasi-coherent sheaves on

(2)

If

satisfies log étale descent in the sense of
[

[
75

.

(3)

If
\mathcal{X}

for any statification

. As a result,
\Downarrow\!\!\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
is coherent, which answers and strengthens
[

in our setting.

(4)

In particular, if

is one of the log modifications in Example

or

(5)

Using
(

)

that the map

More generally,

is not exact in the sense that it does not commute with taking cohomology sheaves - even for complexes consisting of static quasi-coherent sheaves. However, this holds under stronger hypotheses:

Proposition 2.6.4
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a DM
k
-stack with a log structure and
E^{\bullet}
be a complex of static quasi-coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
so that
E^{i}=0
for
i\gg 0
. If for some integer
n
we have

(6)

\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})\text{ is static for all }i\geq n,

then
\underline{f}^{*}\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})=\mathcal{H}^{i}(\underline{f}^{*}E^{\bullet})
and
\arrowup\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})=\mathcal{H}^{i}(\arrowup E^{\bullet})
for every log flat morphism
f\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
and
i\geq n-1
. Moreover, if
\mathcal{X}
is Noetherian and the
\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})
are coherent, then for any
n

satisfies
(

Remark 2.6.5
.

In particular, if
E^{\bullet}
is an acyclic complex of static quasi-coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
that is bounded from above, then
\Uparrow\!\!E^{\bullet}
is also acyclic.

Proof.

Using the exact sequences

(7)

0\longrightarrow\text{Ker}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})\longrightarrow E^{i}\longrightarrow\text{Im}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})\longrightarrow 0,

and

0\longrightarrow\text{Im}(d^{i-1}_{E^{\bullet}})\longrightarrow\text{Ker}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})\longrightarrow 0

and the fact that staticity is stable under taking kernels of surjections, we see by downward induction in
i
that
\text{Ker}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})
and
\text{Im}(d^{i-1}_{E^{\bullet}})
are static for all
i\geq n
. Since
\underline{f}^{*}
and
\Uparrow
commute with
\text{Im}({-})
, it thus follows from Proposition
2.6.1
, Corollary
2.6.2
and
(
7
)
that
\underline{f}^{*}\text{Ker}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})=\text{Ker}(\underline{f}^{*}d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})
for all
i\geq n-1
and hence

(8)

\underline{f}^{*}\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})=\underline{f}^{*}\text{Ker}(d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})/\underline{f}^{*}\text{Im}(d^{i-1}_{E^{\bullet}})=\text{Ker}(\underline{f}^{*}d^{i}_{E^{\bullet}})/\text{Im}(\underline{f}^{*}d^{i-1}_{E^{\bullet}})=\mathcal{H}^{i}(\underline{f}^{*}E^{\bullet})

as desired (and the same for
\Uparrow
).

For the last claim, note that the pullback
\underline{f}^{*}E^{\bullet}
along any log flat
f\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
preserves our hypotheses. Indeed, Corollary
2.3.4
implies that the
\underline{f}^{*}E^{i}
are still static and by right exactness of
\underline{f}^{*}
there is always a surjection
\underline{f}^{*}\mathcal{H}^{i}(E^{\bullet})\twoheadrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{i}(\underline{f}^{*}E^{\bullet})
similar to
(
8
)
and so the
\mathcal{H}^{i}(\underline{f}^{*}E^{\bullet})
are again coherent. Since
E^{\bullet}
is bounded from above, the second claim thus follows from Theorem
2.4.2
, as the first claim allows one to inductively make the cohomology sheaves static.
∎

Using the machinery described above, we can finally show that
\Uparrow
is in general not exact.

Example 2.6.6
.

Consider
q\colon X^{\prime}=\text{Bl}_{0}\mathbb{A}^{2}\rightarrow X=\mathbb{A}^{2}
as a toric log modification. We claim that

0\longrightarrow I=(x,y)\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{2}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{0}\longrightarrow 0,

does not remain left exact after applying
\Uparrow
. Indeed, pulling back along
\underline{q}
yields an exact sequence

(9)

0\longrightarrow L_{1}\underline{q}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{0}\longrightarrow\underline{q}^{*}I\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\underline{X}^{\prime}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}\longrightarrow 0,

where
\underline{E}\subset\underline{X}^{\prime}
is the exceptional divisor. One can compute that
L_{1}\underline{q}^{*}\mathcal{O}_{0}=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}(-1)
and so
(
9
)
remains exact after applying
\Uparrow
as all terms are static. Moreover, staticity of
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}(-1)
also implies that
\Downarrow_{X^{\prime}}\arrowup\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}(-1)=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}(-1)
and in particular
\Uparrow\!\!\mathcal{O}_{\underline{E}}(-1)\neq 0
, which shows the claim.

2.6.2.
Logarithmic étale sheaf cohomology

We complete our discussion of static sheaves by showing that cohomology behaves in the expected way, see Corollary
2.6.8
.

Proposition 2.6.7
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a DM
k
-stack with a log structure and
F
and
G
be quasi-coherent sheaves on
\mathcal{X}
with
G
being static.

(1)

The canonical morphism

is an isomorphism;

(2)

If for every log alteration
q\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
, we have
L^{n}\underline{q}^{*}F=0
for all

for all

.

Moreover, the assumption on
F
holds in any of the following cases

(a)

F
is conventionally flat on
\mathcal{X}
;

(b)

Both
F
and
\mathcal{X}
are static.

Proof.

Statement
(

)
follows from the adjunction

and

as
G
is static.

To show statement
(
2
)
, we first note that

by Corollary
1.3.6

(
4
)
. Thus, we have

(see

Logarithmic (quasi-)coherent sheaves

3.1.
Logarithmic coherent sheaves

In this section, we introduce logarithmic coherent sheaves and
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-modules of finite presentation. Parallel to conventional scheme theory, these definitions coincide when
\mathcal{X}
is locally Noetherian, see Theorem
3.1.4
.

3.1.1.
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-modules of finite presentation

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a DM
k
-stack with a log structure. Recall from
[
90
, 03DL]
that a
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-module
\mathcal{F}
is said to be
of finite presentation
if there exists a log étale cover

\{\mathcal{U}_{i}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}\}_{i\in I}

such that for each
i
there exists an exact sequence of
\mathcal{O}_{(\mathcal{U}_{i}){}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-modules
of the form:

\mathcal{O}_{(\mathcal{U}_{i})_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\oplus m_{i}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{O}_{(\mathcal{U}_{i})_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\oplus n_{i}}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}|_{\mathcal{U}_{i}}\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\text{FP}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}})\subset\text{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}})
the full subcategory of finitely presented
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-modules.

By the right exactness of the up functor, every finitely presented
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
-module on some log alteration
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
gives rise to a finitely presented
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-module
\Uparrow\!\!F
. For the converse, we have:

Proposition 3.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a qcqs DM
k
-stack with a log structure. The functor

\mathop{\text{colim}}_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}}\ \text{FP}(\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}{}^{\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}})\longrightarrow\text{FP}(\mathcal{O}_{{\mathcal{X}}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}})

induced by
\Uparrow
is an equivalence,
where the (filtered) colimit of categories runs over all log alterations and pullbacks between them.

Corollary 3.1.2
.

An
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-module
\mathcal{F}
is of finite presentation if and only if it arises as
\mathcal{F}=\arrowup F
for some conventional finitely presented sheaf
F
on a log alteration
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
. Moreover, for any morphism
\phi\colon\arrowup F\rightarrow\arrowup G
between finitely presented
\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}{}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
-modules
F
and
G
, there exists a log alteration
q\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
and a morphism
\phi^{\prime}\colon\underline{q}{}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{*}F\rightarrow\underline{q}{}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{*}G
such that
\phi=\arrowup\phi^{\prime}
.

Proof of Proposition
3.1.1
.

We will first show that for any log alteration
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}
and any two finitely presented modules
F
and
G
on
\underline{\mathcal{X}}{}^{\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}
, the morphism of sheaves

(10)

\mathop{\text{colim}}_{q\colon\mathcal{X}^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\underline{q}{}^{\text{\'{e}t}}_{*}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}{}^{\prime\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}}(\underline{q}{}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{*}F,\underline{q}{}_{\text{\'{e}t}}^{*}G)\longrightarrow\arrowdown_{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}\mathcal{H}om_{\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}}(\arrowup F,\arrowup G)

is an isomorphism. From this fact, full faithfulness of the functor in Proposition
3.1.1
follows since taking
\Gamma(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},{-})
on both sides yields the desired equality of Hom spaces, where we use that
\Gamma(\mathcal{X}^{\prime},{-})
commutes with filtered colimits as
\mathcal{X}^{\prime}
is qcqs. Since
(
10
)
can be checked strict étale locally we may assume that
F
is globally of finite presentation. From Corollary
1.3.6

(
3
)
it follows that
(
10
)
is an isomorphism in the case that
F=\mathcal{O}_{\underline{\mathcal{X}}{}^{\prime}_{\text{\'{e}t}}}
. By left exactness of
\mathcal{H}om
, pushforward, filtered colimits and right exactness of
\Uparrow
and pullback,
(
10
)
holds whenever
F
admits a global finite presentation.

For essential surjectivity, note that any
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-module
\mathcal{F}
that admits a global finite presentation

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\oplus m}\xrightarrow{\;\;\phi\;\;}\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}^{\oplus n}\longrightarrow\mathcal{F}\longrightarrow 0

is in the essential image since by full faithfulness we have
\phi=\arrowup\phi^{\prime}
for some morphism

on some log alteration

3.2.
Logarithmic quasi-coherent sheaves

We will now introduce a notion of log quasi-coherence that generalizes log coherence and behaves similarly to quasi-coherence in conventional algebraic geometry. In particular, we show in Theorem

that the category of log quasi-coherent sheaves is the smallest subcategory of

Definition 3.2.1

An

-module
\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}
-modules. The category of log quasi-coherent sheaves is denoted
\mathcal{L}\text{QCoh}(\mathcal{X})\subset\text{Mod}(\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{X}_{\text{l\'{e}t}}}).

It follows from Corollary

4.1.
Three flavours of logarithmic Picard

In conventional algebraic geometry, the

In the log situation, we will study three related moduli stacks. See Remark

Parallel to conventional algebraic geometry, a

For an

Let

Lemma 4.1.3

This follows from descent of
