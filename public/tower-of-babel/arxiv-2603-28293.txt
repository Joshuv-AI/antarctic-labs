Definition 2.1

A row

We recall the well-known ”Swan-Weibel’s homotopy trick”, which is the main ingredient to handle the graded case.

Definition 2.3

Let

For any

Remark 2.4

(via evaluation at

3
The Main Theorems

In this section, we prove the main results Theorem
1.3
and Theorem

.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
R
be a smooth affine algebra of dimension
3
over an algebraically closed field
K
with
3!\in K^{*}
. Then

[(i)]

1.

\text{Um}_{4}(R)=e_{1}\text{Sp}_{4}(R)

2.

\text{Um}_{4}(R[X])=e_{1}\text{Sp}_{4}(R[X]).

Proof.

[(i)]

1.

Let
v\in\text{Um}_{4}(R)
. In view of Theorem
2.22
, there exists
\sigma\in\text{SL}_{4}(R)
such that

\displaystyle v=e_{1}\sigma.

Note that
\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma
is an alternating matrix, therefore we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma=V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})

for some
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in\text{Um}_{3}(R)
such that
v^{\prime}\cdot(w^{\prime})^{t}=1
.
Since
\text{Um}_{3}(R)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(R)
by Theorem
2.23
, there exists
\rho\in\text{SL}_{3}(R)
such that

\displaystyle v^{\prime}=e_{1}\rho.

By
[
12
, Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2]
, in
W_{E}(R)
we have

\displaystyle[V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle[V(e_{1}\rho,e_{1}(\rho^{-1})^{t})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)].

Thus there exists
\epsilon\in\text{E}_{4}(R)
such that

\displaystyle V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})=\epsilon^{t}(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)\epsilon.

In view of Lemma
2.17
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}\in\text{E}_{3}(R)
such that
\epsilon=\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1})^{-1}
for some
\delta_{1}\in\text{ESp}_{4}(R)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma=(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1})^{t}\delta_{1}^{t}(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}).

Thus
,

\displaystyle(1\perp\rho^{-1})^{t}(\delta_{1}^{-1})^{t}(1\perp\epsilon_{1})^{t}\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})=\psi_{2}.

Thus
\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})\in\text{Sp}_{4}(R)
. Let
\delta=\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma=\delta(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}).

In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle[e_{1}\sigma]

\displaystyle=[e_{1}(\delta(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}))]

\displaystyle=[e_{1}\delta]\ast[e_{1}(1\perp\rho)]\ast[e_{1}\delta_{1}]\ast[e_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1})].

Thus
[e_{1}\sigma]=[e_{1}\delta\delta_{1}]
in
\frac{\text{Cm}_{4}(R)}{\text{E}_{4}(R)}
. Therefore, there exists
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\text{E}_{4}(R)
such that

\displaystyle e_{1}\sigma=(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon^{\prime}.

In view of Lemma
2.16
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}\in\text{ESp}_{4}(R)
such that

\displaystyle(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon^{\prime}=(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}.

Thus
e_{1}\sigma=e_{1}(\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime})
. Note that
\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}\in\text{Sp}_{4}(R)
. Therefore,
v=e_{1}\sigma=e_{1}(\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime})\in e_{1}\text{Sp}_{4}(R)
.

2.

First, we show the following:

\displaystyle(a)~\text{Um}_{4}(R[X])=e_{1}\text{SL}_{4}(R[X])

\displaystyle(b)~\text{Um}_{3}(R[X])=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(R[X]).

(a)  Let
v(X)\in\text{Um}_{4}(R[X])
. By Lemma
2.18
,
v(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}v(0)
. Now by Theorem
2.22
,
v(0)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}
. Thus
v(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}
.

(b)  Let
v(X)\in\text{Um}_{3}(R[X])
. By Lemma
2.19
,
v(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}v(0)
. Now by Theorem
2.23
,
v(0)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}
. Thus
v(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}
.

The proof of part (ii) proceeds similarly to that of part (i).
∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
R
be a smooth affine algebra of dimension
4
over an algebraically closed field
K
. Assume that
4!\in K^{*}
and
\text{W}_{E}(R)
is divisible. Then

\text{Um}_{3}(R)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(R)
. As a consequence
\text{Um}_{4}(R)=e_{1}\text{Sp}_{4}(R)
.

Proof.

First, we will show that
\text{Um}_{3}(R)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(R)
. Let
v=(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})\in\text{Um}_{3}(R)
. By
[
4
, Corollary 7.9]
, the set
\frac{\text{Um}_{3}(R)}{\text{E}_{3}(R)}
is in bijection with
\text{W}_{E}(R)
and is thus endowed with the structure of an abelian group. By assumption
\text{W}_{E}(R)
is divisible and
\frac{\text{Um}_{3}(R)}{\text{E}_{3}(R)}
is in bijection with
\text{W}_{E}(R)
,
\frac{\text{Um}_{3}(R)}{\text{E}_{3}(R)}
is a divisible group prime to the characteristic of
K
. Therefore, there exists a unimodular row
(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})\in\text{Um}_{3}(R)
such that

\displaystyle[(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})]=[(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})]^{2}

in
\frac{\text{Um}_{3}(R)}{\text{E}_{3}(R)}
. Since
-1
is a square in
K
, Lemma
2.7
, shows that
[(v_{0},v_{1},v_{2})]^{n}=[(v_{0}^{n},v_{1},v_{2})]
in
\frac{\text{Um}_{3}(R)}{\text{E}_{3}(R)}
for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Thus
[(u_{0},u_{1},u_{2})]^{2}=[(u_{0}^{2},u_{1},u_{2})]
. By Theorem
2.20
, there exists
\alpha\in\text{SL}_{3}(R)
such that

\displaystyle(u_{0}^{2},u_{1},u_{2})=e_{1}\alpha

Thus,
\text{Um}_{3}(R)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(R)
. Note that the implicit result of Fasel-Rao-Swan (cf. Theorem
2.23
) asserts that
\text{Um}_{4}(R)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{4}(R)
. Now the proof of the last statement of the theorem is analogous to the proof of the part (i) of the Theorem
3.1
.

∎

Proposition 5.1
.

Let

be a graded ring of dimension

, with
\frac{1}{d!}\in A
. Assume

is a local ring. Then
\text{Um}_{d+1}(A)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{d+1}(A)
.

Proof.

Let
v\in\text{Um}_{d+1}(A)
. Consider the Swan-Weibel’s homotopy map:

\displaystyle\epsilon:A

\displaystyle\rightarrow A[X]

\displaystyle a_{0}+a_{1}+a_{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot

\displaystyle\mapsto a_{0}+a_{1}X+a_{2}X^{2}+\cdot\cdot\cdot+a_{n}X^{n}+\cdot\cdot\cdot

where

for

\epsilon(v)(X)\in\text{Um}_{d+1}(A[X])

2.18

\displaystyle\epsilon(v)(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}\epsilon(v)(0).

Note that

and since

is a local ring,

\displaystyle\epsilon(v)(0)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}.

Thus,

\displaystyle\epsilon(v)(X)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}.

Evaluating at

, we obtain

\displaystyle v=\epsilon(v)(1)\sim_{\text{SL}}e_{1}.

Hence
\text{Um}_{d+1}(A)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{d+1}(A)
.
∎

v\in\text{Um}_{4}(A)
. In view of Proposition
5.1
, there exists
\sigma\in\text{SL}_{4}(A)
such that

\displaystyle v=e_{1}\sigma.

Note that
\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma
is an alternating matrix, therefore we have

\displaystyle\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma=V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})

for some
v^{\prime},w^{\prime}\in\text{Um}_{3}(A)
such that
v^{\prime}\cdot(w^{\prime})^{t}=1
.
Now by Proposition
5.2
,
\text{Um}_{3}(A)=e_{1}\text{SL}_{3}(A)
, there exists
\rho\in\text{SL}_{3}(A)
such that

\displaystyle v^{\prime}=e_{1}\rho.

By
[
12
, Lemma 5.1, Theorem 5.2]
, in
W_{E}(A)
we have

\displaystyle[V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})]=

\displaystyle[V(e_{1}\rho,e_{1}(\rho^{-1})^{t})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)].

Thus there exists
\epsilon\in\text{E}_{4}(A)
such that

\displaystyle V(v^{\prime},w^{\prime})=\epsilon^{t}(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)\epsilon.

In view of Lemma
2.17
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}\in\text{E}_{3}(A)
such that
\epsilon=\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1})^{-1}
for some
\delta_{1}\in\text{ESp}_{4}(A)
.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma=(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1})^{t}\delta_{1}^{t}(1\perp\rho)^{t}\psi_{2}(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}).

Thus,

\displaystyle(1\perp\rho^{-1})^{t}(\delta_{1}^{-1})^{t}(1\perp\epsilon_{1})^{t}\sigma^{t}\psi_{2}\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})=\psi_{2}.

Thus
\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})\in\text{Sp}_{4}(A)
. Let
\delta=\sigma(1\perp\epsilon_{1})\delta_{1}^{-1}(1\perp\rho^{-1})
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\sigma=\delta(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}).

In view of Lemma
2.2
, we have

\displaystyle[e_{1}\sigma]

\displaystyle=[e_{1}(\delta(1\perp\rho)\delta_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1}))]

\displaystyle=[e_{1}\delta]\ast[e_{1}(1\perp\rho)]\ast[e_{1}\delta_{1}]\ast[e_{1}(1\perp\epsilon_{1}^{-1})].

Thus
[e_{1}\sigma]=[e_{1}\delta\delta_{1}]
in
\frac{\text{Cm}_{4}(A)}{\text{E}_{4}(A)}
. Therefore, there exists
\epsilon^{\prime}\in\text{E}_{4}(A)
such that

\displaystyle e_{1}\sigma=(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon^{\prime}.

In view of Lemma
2.16
, there exists
\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}\in\text{ESp}_{4}(A)
such that

\displaystyle(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon^{\prime}=(e_{1}\delta\delta_{1})\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}.

Thus
e_{1}\sigma=e_{1}(\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime})
. Note that
\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime}\in\text{Sp}_{4}(A)
. Therefore,
v=e_{1}\sigma=e_{1}(\delta\delta_{1}\epsilon_{1}^{\prime})\in e_{1}\text{Sp}_{4}(A)
.
∎
