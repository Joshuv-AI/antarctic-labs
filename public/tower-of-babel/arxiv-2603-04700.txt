The Asymptotic Behaviour of Oldroyd-B Fluids is Almost Newtonian
Matthias Hieber, Thieu Huy Nguyen, César J. Niche, Cilon F. Perusato

Abstract
. Consider a viscoelastic fluid of Oldroyd-B type. It is shown that its stress tensor τ \tau and its Newtonian deformation tensor D ⁡ ( u ) D(u) decay at the same rate, while the elastic part ε = τ − 2 ​ ω ​ D ​ ( u ) \varepsilon=\tau-2\omega D(u) decays faster. As a consequence, the stress tensor of a viscoelastic fluid exhibits an almost Newtonian behaviour for large times.

1.
Introduction

The study of estimates for decay and of long time behaviour of solutions to equations arising in Fluid Mechanics has a long history. For example, there are many such results for the Navier-Stokes equations in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, starting with the work by Kato
[
18
]
, Masuda
[
22
]
, M.E. Schonbek
[
28
,
29
]
and Wiegner
[
32
]
. In this article we consider decay estimates for solutions to the equations for incompressible Oldroyd-B fluids in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
describing certain classes of viscoelastic fluids.
Given
\omega\in(0,1)
and
a\in[-1,1]
, these read as

(1.1)

\left\{\begin{aligned} Re\left(\partial_{t}u+(u\cdot\nabla)u\right)-(1-\omega)\Delta u+\nabla P&=\mbox{div}\,\tau,&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)\\
\mbox{div}\,u&=0,&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)\\
We\left(\partial_{t}\tau+u\cdot\nabla\tau+g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)\right)+\tau&=2\omega\,D(u),&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3}\times(0,\infty)\\
(u,\tau)(0)&=\left(u_{0},\tau_{0}\right)&\mbox{ in }\mathbb{R}^{3},\\
\end{aligned}\right.

where
g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)=\tau W(u)-W(u)\tau-a\left(D(u)\tau+\tau D(u)\right)
and where
D(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{T})
denotes the deformation tensor and
W(u)=\frac{1}{2}(\nabla u-(\nabla u)^{T})
the vorticity tensor. Here
u
denotes the velocity of the fluid,
p
its pressure,
\tau
the symmetric constraint tensor and
Re
and
We
the associated Reynolds and Weissenberg numbers, respectively. The nonlinear constitutive law for
\tau
was proposed by Oldroyd
[
25
]
to describe certain classes of viscoelastic fluids.

The analysis of the above set of equations started with the pioneering paper by Guillopé and Saut
[
13
]
in 1990, who proved local existence of strong solutions in suitable Sobolev spaces on bounded domains. This solution is global provided the data as well as the coupling constant
\omega
are small enough.
The existence of a global weak solution in the case of
\mathbb{R}^{3}
was established by Lions and Masmoudi in
[
21
]
for
a=0
.
For further results in these directions we refer to the works by Constantin and Kliegl
[
6
]
, Fang and Zhi
[
10
]
, Kupferman, Mangoubi and Titi
[
19
]
,
and Lei, Masmoudi and Zhou
[
20
]
. Similar results, but for exterior domains, can be found in Fang, Hieber and Zhi
[
11
]
.
Properties of models in which either some dissipation or damping in (
1.1
) is not present have been studied by Constantin, Wu, Zhao and Zhi
[
7
]
,
Elgindi and Liu
[
8
]
, Elgindi and Rousset
[
9
]
, Wang, Wu, Xu and Zhong
[
31
]
and Zhu
[
33
]
. Finally, for a survey article also
describing some physical aspects of the Oldroyd-B model, see Renardy and Thomases
[
26
]
.

The main goal of this article is to provide precise decay estimates for solutions
z=(u,\tau)
to the Oldroyd-B model using the decay character of the initial data
u_{0}
and
\tau_{0}
. Let us emphasize that our approach does not only allows us to improve known decay estimates for viscoelastic fluids, but also to prove the surprising fact that the asymptotic behaviour of
Oldroyd-B fluids is almost Newtonian. In fact,
we show that the full stress tensor and its Newtonian part decay at the same rate, while the viscoelastic part decays at a strictly faster rate. As a consequence, the full stress tensor and the
Newtonian deformation tensor become asymptotically aligned.

We now describe the results mentioned above in a more precise way. In order to do so, we recall that to an initial datum
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
we can associate a decay character
r^{\ast}(v_{0})
which is key for establishing sharp decay estimates for dissipative linear equations, see Section
2.1
for details. In Theorem
2.1
we consider
the corotational case in (
1.1
), i.e.
a=0
, and prove decay estimates for weak solutions with arbitrary large initial data, of the form

\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\,\}\,\right\}},\quad t>0,

hereby improving previous results by Hieber, Wen and Zi
[
15
]
and Chen, Li, Yao and Yao
[
5
]
.

Furthermore, our approach also allows us to improve known decay estimates for strong solutions for
a\neq 0
, albeit for small data. In Theorem
2.2
we prove that for
any given
0<\omega<1
, there is
\epsilon=\epsilon(\omega)
such that if
\|\left(u_{0},\tau_{0}\right)\|_{H^{2}}\leq\epsilon

then for
k=0,1,2
and large times we have

\|\nabla^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{k}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(k+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right\}\right)},

and for
k=0,1

\|\nabla^{k}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(k+1+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right\}\right)}.

These results improve bounds obtained by Hieber, Wen and Zi
[
15
]
and Chen, Li, Yao and Yao
[
5
]
.

These estimates for
\tau
and
Du
lead us to the question of whether the elastic part
\varepsilon
of the stress tensor, i.e.
\varepsilon=\tau-2\omega D(u)
, allows for faster decay estimates than those. This is indeed the case. In fact, under the same assumptions as above, in Theorem
2.3
we prove that

\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{\,r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\,\}\,\right\}\right)},\quad\,t>0.

This suggests that, as
\varepsilon
decays faster than
\tau
and
D(u)
, the latter would tend to align in such a way that the viscoelastic stress tensor asymptotically
matches the Newtonian deformation tensor. Note, however, that the above estimates are just upper bounds and do not preclude the possibility that these quantities decay faster than as described above.

In Theorem
2.4
we are able to prove that for certain classes of initial data we have lower bounds for
\tau
and
D(u)
that are equivalent to their upper bounds. For example, for
(u_{0},\tau_{0})
satisfying
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})
and
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 0
, we obtain

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)}\leq\|D(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)},\quad t>1.

The conditions on the decay characters of initial data imply that

\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}(u_{0})\right)},\quad\mbox{ for all }\,t>0.

Hence, the elastic part
\varepsilon
decays faster than both the full stress tensor
\tau
and its Newtonian part
Du
. A similar result is valid under the conditions
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq r^{\ast}(u_{0})
and
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq 0
. This is all summarized in Theorem
2.5
. Thus, we have shown that the faster decay of the elastic part of the stress tensor implies that the viscoelastic fluid exhibits an almost Newtonian behavior for large times.

The above estimates for strong solutions are similar to results by Guterres, Melo, Niche, Perusato and Zingano
[
14
]
in the context of micropolar fluids. It is shown there that the vorticity and micro-rotation of a
3D
micropolar flow tend to align asymptotically. For related results, see also Shang
[
30
]
.

A different kind of limit behaviour for viscoleastic fluids has been obtained by Bresch and Prange
[
4
]
and Molinet and Talhouk
[
23
]
. They
proved, assuming that the Weissenberg number
We
goes to zero in (
1.1
), that solutions
u
converge in appropriate spaces to solutions to the Navier-Stokes equations while
\tau

converges to
2\omega D(u)
.

This article is organized as follows. In Section 2 we start by recalling properties of the decay character of
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
and we then state our Theorems. In Section 3 we prove decay results for the linear part of (
1.1
). In Section 4 we provide the proofs of Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
concerning decay of
solutions and its derivatives and prove, in Theorems
2.3
and
2.4
, the almost Newtonian behaviour of the Oldroyd-B flow.

2.
Preliminaries and Main Results

2.1.
Preliminaries

Let us start by recalling several properties of the decay character of a function
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. This number describes the “algebraic order” of
v_{0}
near the origin,
comparing
|\widehat{v_{0}}(\xi)|
to
f(\xi)=|\xi|^{r}
at
\xi=0
, for some
r\in\mathbb{R}
. This concept was first introduced by Bjorland and M.E. Schonbek in
[
1
]
and refined later by Niche and M.E. Schonbek
[
24
]
and Brandolese
[
2
]
.

For
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
,
r\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)
and a ball
B(\rho)
at the origin with radius
\rho
, we consider

P_{r}(v_{0}):=\lim_{\rho\to 0}\rho^{-2r-n}\int_{B(\rho)}\bigl|\widehat{v_{0}}(\xi)\bigr|^{2}\,d\xi.

The decay character of
v_{0}
, denoted by
r^{\ast}=r^{\ast}(v_{0})
is the unique
r\in\left(-\frac{n}{2},\infty\right)

satisfying
0<P_{r}(v_{0})<\infty
, provided this number exists. For a discussion of necessary and sufficient conditions for the existence of this limit, see Brandolese
[
2
]
and Brandolese, Perusato and Zingano
[
3
]
.

The decay character may be calculated for many functions. For example, given
v_{0}\in L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for
1\leq p<2
, we have that

r^{\ast}(v_{0})=-n(1-\frac{1}{p})
, see e.g.
[
12
]
.

For initial data
v_{0}\in H^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
, where
s>0
, there is a natural relation between the decay character of
\Lambda^{s}\,v_{0}:=(-\Delta)^{\frac{s}{2}}v_{0}
and that of
v_{0}
. Setting
r^{\ast}_{s}(v_{0}):=r^{\ast}(\Lambda^{s}v_{0})
, it follows from Niche and M.E. Schonbek
[
24
]
that

\frac{n}{2}+s<r_{s}^{\ast}(v_{0})<\infty
and
r_{s}^{\ast}(v_{0})=s+r^{\ast}(v_{0})
, provided
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{\ast}(v_{0})<\infty
.

Given a Hilbert space
X
consider a linear pseudodifferential operator
\mathcal{L}:X^{n}\to\left(L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\right)^{n}
. Assume that
\mathcal{L}
is diagonalizable, in the sense that
the associated symbol
M(\xi)
satisfies
M(\xi)=P^{-1}(\xi)D(\xi)P(\xi)
for a.a.
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, where
P(\xi)\in O(n)
and
D
satisfies
D(\xi)=-c_{i}|\xi|^{2\alpha}\delta_{ij}
for
c_{i}>c>0
and
0<\alpha\leq 1
. An example of such operator is provided by the fractional vectorial Laplacian. If
v
satisfies the equation
v_{t}=\mathcal{L}v
for all
t>0
, then

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-C\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|\xi|^{2\alpha}|\widehat{v}|^{2}\,d\xi

and the
L^{2}
-norm of
v
decreases. Niche and M.E. Schonbek
[
24
]
obtained sharp upper and lower bounds for the norms of solutions to such systems. More precisely, let
v_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
with decay character
r^{\ast}(v_{0})=r^{\ast}
and assume that
v
is a solution to
v_{t}=\mathcal{L}v
with initial data
v_{0}
. Then, if
-\frac{n}{2}<r^{\ast}<\infty
, there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(2.1)

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}\leq\|v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}\left(\frac{n}{2}+r^{\ast}\right)}.

Let us note here that the decay of solutions to the heat equation in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is given by (
2.1
) with
\alpha=1
.

2.2.
Main results

Our first result concerns the decay of weak solutions in the case
a=0
. Note that existence of global weak solutions was proved in this case by Lions and Masmoudi
[
21
]
, but their
uniqueness is not known until today.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
a=0
and
(u_{0},\tau_{0})\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
, with
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{\ast}(u_{0}),r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})<\infty
. Then, for any weak solution

z=(u,\tau)
to (
1.1
), there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.2)

\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}(u_{0}),1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\}\right\}},\quad t>0.

Let us note that (
2.2
) improves some known decay estimates. In fact, if
u_{0}\in L^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\tau_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
,
it is shown for
p=1
in Hieber, Wen and Zi
[
15
]
, and for
1\leq p<2
in Chen, Li, Yao and Yao
[
5
]
, that

\|z(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}\left(\frac{2}{p}-1\right)}.

From Section
2.1
we know that
r^{\ast}(f)=-3(1-\frac{1}{p})
for
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
1\leq p<2
. Hence, our Theorem
2.1
covers
in particular the above estimate.

Our second result deals with the decay of strong solutions in the general case
a\in[-1,1]
, for small enough initial data. For the existence of such global, strong solutions, we refer to Guillopé and Saut
[
13
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

Let
a\in[-1,1]
and
(u_{0},\tau_{0})\in H^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\times H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
satisfying
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{\ast}(u_{0}),r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})<\infty
. Then, for
k=0,1,2
there exist

\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\omega)>0
and
T=T(\omega)
such that if
\|(u_{0},\tau_{0})\|_{H^{2}}\leq\varepsilon
, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.3)

\|\nabla^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{k}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(k+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right\}\right)},\quad t>T.

Moreover, for
j=0,1
there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.4)

\|\nabla^{j}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(j+1+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right\}\right)},\quad t>T.

We note that Theorem
2.2
generalizes previous results in Hieber, Wen and Zi
[
15
]
and Chen, Li, Yao and Yao
[
5
]
, where for
u_{0}\in H^{2}_{\sigma}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\tau_{0}\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{3})\cap L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
it was shown that for
k=0,1,2

\|\nabla^{k}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{k}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(k+\frac{3}{2}\right)},\quad t>0.

We now address our main result, in which we consider the elastic part
\varepsilon
of the stress tensor given by the difference between
\tau
and the Newtonian part
2\omega D(u)
, i.e. we set

\varepsilon=\tau-2\omega D(u).

Estimates (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) in Theorem
2.2
imply that both
\tau
and
D(u)
obey the same decay estimates. The next result says that there is even a faster decay rate for
\varepsilon
.

Theorem 2.3
.

Under the assumptions in Theorem
2.2
, there is a constant
C>0
such that

(2.5)

\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{\,r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\,\}\,\right\}\right)},\quad t>T.

Estimate (
2.5
) suggests that as
\varepsilon
decays faster than
\tau
and
D(u)
, these may tend to align in such a way that the viscoelastic stress tensor
asymptotically matches the Newtonian one. However, note that (
2.3
) and (
2.4
) provide only upper bounds, which do not preclude the possibility of these quantities having
a faster decay than (
2.5
).

We show next that for certain initial data
(u_{0},\tau_{0})
it is possible to obtain upper as well as lower bounds for
Du
and
\tau
with the same decay rate.

Theorem 2.4
.

Let
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})
and
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

(2.6)

\|D(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)},\quad t>1.

On the other hand, if
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq r^{\ast}(u_{0})
and
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq 0
, then

(2.7)

\|D(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\geq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)},\quad\,t>1.

Similar lower bounds for a restricted class of initial data were obtained by Huang, Wang, Wen and Zi
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
. Chen, Li, Yao and Yao
[
5
]
claim to obtain lower bounds for the decay provided that
u_{0}\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R}^{3})
has nonzero mean. Note, however, that this condition is not fulfilled for any incompressible
u_{0}
, see page 721 of the article by M.E. Schonbek, T. Schonbek and Süli
[
27
]
. Hence, their claim is not justified.

In the following Theorem we just combine the estimates proved in Theorems
2.2
,
2.3
and
2.4
in order to state in a clearer way our main result: the faster decay of the elastic part
\varepsilon

of the stress tensor implies that the viscolelastic stress tensor exhibits an almost Newtonian behavior for large times.

Theorem 2.5
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
2.2
we have:

a)

Assume that
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})
and
r^{\ast}(u_{0})\leq 0
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)}\leq\|D(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{5}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)},\quad t>1.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)},\quad t>\max\left\{T,1\right\},

so

\tau=2\omega\,D(u)+O\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}(u_{0})\right)}\right).

b)

Assume that
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq r^{\ast}(u_{0})
and
1+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\leq 0
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

C_{1}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)}\leq\|D(u)(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{2}(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{7}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)},\quad\forall\,t>1.

Moreover, there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{9}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)},\quad t>\max\left\{T,1\right\},

so

\tau=2\omega\,D(u)+O\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{9}{2}+r^{\ast}(\tau_{0})\right)}\right).

3.
Analysis of the Linear Part

In this section we prove decay estimates for the linear part of (
1.1
), which is given by the equations

\displaystyle\partial_{t}u-(1-\omega)\Delta u-\mathbb{P}\,div\,\tau

\displaystyle=0,

(3.1)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau+\tau-2\omega\,D(u)

\displaystyle=0.

We first provide an explicit expression in frequency space for the solution
(u_{l},\tau_{L})^{T}
of this equation given by

(u_{L}(t),\tau_{L}(t))^{T}=\mathcal{G}\ast(u_{0},\tau_{0})^{T}
,
where
\mathcal{G}
denotes the fundamental solution to (
3
) and
u_{0},\tau_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
.

Proposition 3.1
.

(Zi, Fang and Zhang
[
34
]
, Lemma 3.1).
Let
(u_{L},\tau_{L})
be a solution to (
3
) and for
t>0,\,\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
set

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}(\xi,t):=\frac{e^{\lambda_{+}t}-e^{\lambda_{-}t}}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}},\quad\mathcal{B}(\xi,t):=\frac{\left(\lambda_{+}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{+}t}-\left(\lambda_{-}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{-}t}}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}},\quad\mathcal{C}(\xi,t):=\frac{\left(\lambda_{-}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{+}t}-\left(\lambda_{+}+1\right)e^{\lambda_{-}t}}{\lambda_{+}-\lambda_{-}},

where

\lambda_{\pm}(\xi)=\frac{-(1+(1-\omega)|\xi|^{2})\pm\sqrt{\left(1+(1-\omega)|\xi|^{2})\right)^{2}-4|\xi|^{2}}}{2}.

Then, in frequency space the solution
\widehat{L}(\xi,t)=\left(\widehat{u_{L}}(\xi,t),\widehat{\tau_{L}}(\xi,t)\right)
to (
3
) is given by

\left(\widehat{u_{L}}\right)_{j}(\xi,t)=\mathcal{B}(\xi,t)\left(\widehat{u_{0}}\right)_{j}(\xi)+\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)\,i\,\xi_{l}(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}})\left(\widehat{\tau_{0}}\right)_{lk}(\xi),

and

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{\tau_{L}}\right)_{jk}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=e^{-t}\left(\widehat{\tau_{0}}\right)_{jk}(\xi)

\displaystyle-\left(\mathcal{C}(\xi,t)+e^{-t}\right)\left(\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{l}}{|\xi|^{2}}\left(\delta_{km}-\frac{\xi_{k}\xi_{m}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\left(\widehat{\tau_{0}}\right)_{lm}(\xi)+\frac{\xi_{k}\xi_{l}}{|\xi|^{2}}\left(\delta_{jm}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{m}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\left(\widehat{\tau_{0}}\right)_{lm}(\xi)\right)

\displaystyle+\,i\,\omega\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)\left(\xi_{j}\left(\widehat{u_{0}}\right)_{k}(\xi)+\xi_{k}\left(\widehat{u_{0}}\right)_{j}(\xi)\right).

The following pointwise estimates for
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
and
\mathcal{C}
were obtained by Hieber, Wen and Zi in
[
15
]
.

Proposition 3.2
.

(Hieber, Wen and Zi
[
15
]
, Lemma 2.2)
Given
R>0
, there exist positive constants

\displaystyle\theta(\omega,R)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\min\left\{\frac{1-\omega}{2},\frac{1}{1+R^{2}(1-\omega)}\right\}

\displaystyle C_{1}(\omega,R)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{\frac{4\sqrt{1-\omega}}{\min\left\{\sqrt{\omega},1-\sqrt{\omega}\right\}},8(1+\sqrt{\omega})^{2}\max\left\{\frac{2}{1-\omega},1+R^{2}(1-\omega)\right\}\right\}

\displaystyle C_{2}(\omega,R)

\displaystyle=2\max\left\{1+(1-\omega)R^{2},\sqrt{\omega}R\right\}C_{1}(\omega,R)

\displaystyle C_{3}(\omega,R)

\displaystyle=\max\left\{C_{2}(\omega,R),\frac{\max\left\{3\omega\sqrt{1-\omega},\frac{2(1-\sqrt{\omega})}{\sqrt{1-\omega}}\right\}}{\min\left\{\sqrt{\omega},1-\sqrt{\omega}\right\}}\right\}

such that for
|\xi|\leq R
and
t>0
we have

\displaystyle|\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}(\omega,R)e^{-\theta(\omega,R)|\xi|^{2}t},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{B}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{2}(\omega,R)e^{-\theta(\omega,R)|\xi|^{2}t},

\displaystyle|\mathcal{C}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C_{3}(\omega,R)\left(e^{-\frac{\theta(\omega,R)}{4(1+\sqrt{\omega})^{2}}t}+|\xi|^{2}e^{-\theta(\omega,R)|\xi|^{2}t}\right).

The decay estimates for the solution to the linear equation reads as follows.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u_{0},\tau_{0}\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
with decay characters
-\frac{3}{2}<r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)<\infty
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that
the solution
(u_{L},\tau_{L})
to (
3
) satisfies

\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)},\quad t>0.

Proof.

Multiplying (
3
) by
\left(2\omega\,u_{L},\tau_{L}\right)
, integrating by parts and adding leads to

(3.2)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=-2\omega(1-\omega)\|\nabla u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

In frequency space consider the ball

B(t)=\left\{\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\xi|\leq\frac{g(t)}{\sqrt{1-\omega}}\right\},

for some continuous, decreasing function
g
with g(0) = 1, which will be determined later. Then

-2\omega(1-\omega)\|\nabla u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-2\omega(1-\omega)\int_{B(t)^{c}}|\xi|^{2}|\widehat{u_{L}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq-2\omega g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)^{c}}|\widehat{u_{L}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi,

which together with (
3.2
) leads to

(3.3)

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\omega\,g^{2}(t)\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\omega\,g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{u_{L}}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi.

We now establish a pointwise estimate for
|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|
in
B(t)
. Proposition
3.1
yields

|\widehat{u_{L}}(x,t)|^{2}\leq C\left(|\mathcal{B}(\xi,t)|^{2}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}+|\xi|^{2}|\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)|^{2}|\widehat{\tau_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\right).

Suppose now that
|\xi|\leq g(t)\leq R
, for some fixed
R
. Then, after choosing
g^{2}(t)=C(1+t)^{-1}
for some
C>0
, from this and Proposition
3.2
we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{u_{L}}(x,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\int_{B(t)}e^{-Ct|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{u_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi+\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}e^{-Ct|\xi|^{2}}|\widehat{\tau_{0}}(\xi)|^{2}\,d\xi\right)

\displaystyle\leq C\|e^{t\Delta}u_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-1}\|e^{t\Delta}\tau_{0}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\left(1+\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)},

where we used the decay of solutions to the heat equation, i.e., estimates in (
2.1
) with
\alpha=1
. Plugging this into (
3.3
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\omega\,(1+t)^{-1}\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\leq C\omega\,(1+t)^{-1}\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)}+(1+t)^{-\left(1+\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)}\right).

Given
A>1
, we see that
\frac{A}{t+1}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
. Multiplying (
3
)
by the integrating factor
h(t)=C(t+1)^{A}
, where

A>\max\left\{1,\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right\}
,
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(C(t+1)^{A}\left(\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\right)\leq C(t+1)^{A-1}\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right)\right)}+(1+t)^{-\left(1+\frac{d}{2}+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\right)}\right).

This finally leads to

\omega\|u_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau_{L}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{d}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)},\quad t>0.

∎

4.1.
Proof of Theorem
2.1

Proof.

Standard computations lead to the energy inequality

\frac{d}{dt}\left(\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq-2\omega(1-\omega)\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

from which we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}\left(\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)

\displaystyle+\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+2\omega\,g^{2}(t)\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\omega\,g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{u}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

(4.1)

\displaystyle=:2C\omega\,g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)}\left(|\widehat{L}(\xi,t)|^{2}+|\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)|^{2}\right)\,d\xi.

Here
B(t)\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
is defined as in Proposition
3.3
and
\widehat{L}(\xi,t)
is as in Proposition
3.1
. As

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{u}(t)\\
\widehat{\tau}(t)\end{pmatrix}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{\widehat{G}}\begin{pmatrix}\widehat{u}_{0}\\
\widehat{\tau}_{0}\end{pmatrix}-\int_{0}^{t}\widehat{\mathcal{G}}(t-s)\begin{pmatrix}\mathbb{P}\,div(u\otimes u)\\
div(u\otimes\tau)+g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)\end{pmatrix}^{\wedge}\,ds=\widehat{L}(\xi,t)+\widehat{NL}(\xi,t),

then

\displaystyle\left(\widehat{u}\right)_{j}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{B}(\xi,t)\left(\widehat{u_{0}}\right)_{j}(\xi)+\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)\,i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\left(\widehat{\tau_{0}}\right)_{lk}(\xi)

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}(\xi,t-s)i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{u_{l}u_{k}}(\xi,s)\,ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}(\xi,t-s)i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\left(\widehat{div\,(u\otimes\tau)}+\widehat{g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)}\right)(\xi,s)\,ds.

The first two terms describe the components of the solution to the linear problem (
3
). Hence, Proposition
3.3
yields

(4.2)

\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{L}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)},\quad t>0.

Taking
|\xi|\leq g(t)=R
, from Proposition
3.2
we have

(4.3)

|\mathcal{A}(\xi,t)|\leq C_{1}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(1-\omega)|\xi|^{2}t},\,\,|\mathcal{B}(\xi,t)|\leq C_{2}e^{-\frac{1}{4}(1-\omega)|\xi|^{2}t},\quad t>0,

for some constants
C_{i}=C_{i}(\omega,R),i=1,2
. As

\displaystyle\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{B}(\xi,t-s)i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{u_{l}u_{k}}(\xi,s)\,ds-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}(\xi,t-s)i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{div\,(u\otimes\tau)}(\xi,s)\,ds

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{t}\mathcal{A}(\xi,t-s)i\,\xi_{l}\left(\delta_{jk}-\frac{\xi_{j}\xi_{k}}{|\xi|^{2}}\right)\widehat{g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)}(\xi,s)\,ds,

and as we also have

\displaystyle|\widehat{u_{l}u_{k}}(\xi,t)|

\displaystyle\leq C\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},\,\quad|\widehat{div\,(u\otimes\tau)}(\xi,t)|\leq C|\xi|\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}},

(4.4)

\displaystyle|\widehat{g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)}(\xi,s)|\leq C\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left(\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right),

estimates (
4.3
) and (
4.1
) imply

\displaystyle\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right)^{2}\,d\xi+C\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|^{2}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}\,ds\right)^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle+C\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}|\xi|\left(\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\,ds\right)^{2}d\xi

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{B(t)}t|\xi|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\right)\,d\xi+C\int_{B(t)}t|\xi|^{4}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right)\,d\xi

\displaystyle+C\int_{B(t)}t|\xi|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\right)\,d\xi+C\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}\,d\xi

\displaystyle\leq Ct\,g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\right)\,d\xi+Ct\,g^{4}(t)\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\right)\,d\xi

(4.5)

\displaystyle+Ct\,g^{2}(t)\int_{B(t)}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\right)\,d\xi+C\int_{B(t)}|\xi|^{2}\left(\int_{0}^{t}\|\nabla u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{2}\,d\xi.

Setting
g(t):=\left(2C\omega(1+t)\right)^{-\frac{1}{2}}
and using the a priori estimates
\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
and
\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C
, the fact that
u\in L^{2}_{t}\dot{H}^{1}_{x}
(see Lions and Masmoudi
[
21
]
) and using
{\text{V}ol}\,B(t)=C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}
in (
4.1
), we obtain

\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}}C(1+t)^{-\frac{5}{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad t>0.

Combining this with (
4.1
) and (
4.2
) yields

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)+\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(1+t)^{-1}\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-1}\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),\quad t>0.

Proceeding as in the proof of Proposition
3.3
, we multiply by the integrating factor
h(t)=C(1+t)^{A}
, where
A>\max\left\{1,\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right\}
. We then obtain

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}

\displaystyle\left(C(1+t)^{A}\left(\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)\right)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{A-1}\left((1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}}\right),

from which we obtain the preliminary estimate

(4.6)

\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad t>0.

We now bootstrap this decay rate. If the slower term on the right-hand side is the second one, i.e.

\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}},\quad t>0,

then

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C\ln(1+t)\leq C(1+t)^{\mu},\quad\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\leq C\ln(1+t)\leq C(1+t)^{\mu}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\leq C\ln(1+t)\leq C(1+t)^{\mu},

for some
0<\mu<1
. From (
4.1
) we obtain

\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}+\mu},\quad t>0.

Proceeding as before we get

\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}+\mu},\quad t>0.

Suppose that again the slower term is the last one on the right hand side. Note that as
-\frac{3}{2}+\mu<-\frac{1}{2}
, then

(4.7)

\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\leq C,\,\,\,\,\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\leq C,\,\,\,\,\int_{0}^{t}\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\leq C.

Going back to (
4.1
) and then following the same argument we obtain

\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{3}{2}},\quad t>0.

If the slower term were still the second one, we circle back to (
4.7
) and no improvement is obtained.

Now suppose in (
4.6
) the slower term were the first one, i.e.

\gamma:=-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)>-\frac{1}{2}.

This leads to

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{2\gamma+1},\quad\int_{0}^{t}\|u(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,ds\leq C(1+t)^{2\gamma+1}

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{t}\|\tau(s)\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\,ds\leq C(1+t)^{2\gamma+1},

where
0<2\gamma+1<1
. Hence

\int_{B(t)}|\widehat{NL}(\xi,t)|^{2}\,d\xi\leq C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\gamma},\quad t>0,

so

\displaystyle\omega\|u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{2}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)}+C(1+t)^{-\frac{1}{2}+2\gamma},\quad t>0

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{3}{2}+\min\{r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\}\right)},

because
2\gamma<0
. So, our estimate stays the same.

If at any time during the iterations in the first case the slower term were the one coming from the linear part instead of that arising from the nonlinear part, we would switch to the latter part of the argument, as just described.
We have thus established our result.

∎

4.3.
Proof of Theorem
2.3

Proof.

For notational convenience we set

\alpha=\alpha(r^{\star}(u_{0}),r^{\star}(\tau_{0}))=\min\left\{\frac{3}{2},\frac{3}{2}+\min\{\,r^{\ast}\left(u_{0}\right),1+r^{\ast}\left(\tau_{0}\right)\,\}\,\right\}.

Recall that
\varepsilon=\tau-2\omega D(u)
. Taking the time derivative and using (
1.1
) yields

\partial_{t}\varepsilon+\varepsilon-(1-\omega)\Delta\varepsilon=-(u\cdot\nabla)\tau-g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)-2\omega D\,\mathbb{P}div\,\tau+2\omega\,D\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u-(1-\omega)\Delta\tau,\quad t>0.

Multiplication by
\varepsilon
and integration leads to

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1-\omega)\|\nabla\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,(u\cdot\nabla)\tau\,dx-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)\,dx-2\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,D\,\mathbb{P}div\,\tau\,dx

\displaystyle+2\omega\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,D\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u\,dx-(1-\omega)\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,\Delta\tau\,dx.

Notice first that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,(u\cdot\nabla)\tau\,dx

\displaystyle\leq\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\tau(t)\|_{L^{3}}\leq\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)},\quad t>0,

where we have used (
4.20
) and the decay estimates proved in Theorem
2.2
. Recalling that
g_{a}
is a sum of products of
\tau
and
\nabla u
, we verify that

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,g_{a}(\tau,\nabla u)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,C(1+t)^{-\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}

\displaystyle=C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{9}{4}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)},\quad t>0,

where we used (
4.20
) again, as well as the decay estimates from Theorem
2.2
. Noting that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,D\,\mathbb{P}div\,\tau\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D\varepsilon\,\mathbb{P}div\,\tau\,dx,

we have

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,D\mathbb{P}div\,\tau\,dx\leq C\|D\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|div\,\tau(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\alpha\right)},\quad t>0.

Next, we observe that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,D\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D\varepsilon\,\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u\,dx.

Then, we obtain

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}D\varepsilon\,\mathbb{P}(u\cdot\nabla)u\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\|D\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|u(t)\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|D\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(1+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{2}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{4}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}\,(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\alpha}{4}\right)}=C(1+t)^{-\left(\frac{9}{4}+\frac{3\alpha}{2}\right)},\quad t>0.

Finally, since

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\varepsilon\,\Delta\tau\,dx=-\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\varepsilon\,\nabla\tau\,dx,

we see that

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{3}}\nabla\varepsilon\,\nabla\tau\,dx\leq\|\nabla\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{2}u(t)\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla\tau\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\alpha\right)},\quad t>0.

As

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+(1-\omega)\|\nabla\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

we obtain that

\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\varepsilon(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C(1+t)^{-\left(2+\alpha\right)},\quad t>0,

which after integration leads to our result.

∎
