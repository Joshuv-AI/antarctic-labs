A characterization of maximal inhomogeneous-quadratic-free sets
Gonzalo Muñoz, Joseph Paat, Felipe Serrano

Abstract
The intersection cut framework is a versatile tool for generating valid inequalities in optimization. Its main ingredients are so-called S S -free sets: convex sets whose interiors do not intersect a given set S S . Among these, inclusion-wise maximal S S -free sets are particularly important, as they yield the strongest intersection cuts. In the integer programming setting, maximal lattice-free sets are well studied and admit explicit characterizations. In the quadratic optimization context, Muñoz, Paat, and Serrano (2025) characterized maximal S S -free sets when S S is defined by a homogeneous quadratic inequality. In this work, we characterize maximal S S -free sets when S S is defined by an inhomogeneous quadratic inequality. As in the homogeneous case, our characterization is built using non-expansive functions. Together with the results in the homogeneous case, our results complete a characterization of every maximal quadratic-free set via non-expansive functions.

Introduction

Let
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a closed set.
A closed convex set
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
\bm{S}
-free
if the interior of
C
is disjoint from
S
.
An
S
-free set is
maximal

S
-free if it is not a strict subset of another
S
-free set.

A major application of maximal
S
-free sets is the generation of cutting planes in optimization. Such cuts are created using the intersection cut framework, which we describe next. Consider an optimization problem

\min\{\bm{c}{}^{\top}\bm{s}:\ \bm{s}\in P\cap S\},

where
\bm{c}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
P\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a closed set that models constraints aside from inclusion in
S
.
Let
\overline{\bm{s}}\not\in S
be a vertex of a polyhedral relaxation of
P\cap S
.
The intersection cut framework constructs a valid linear inequality for
P\cap S
that strictly separates
\overline{\bm{s}}
, using an
S
-free set
C
such that
\overline{\bm{s}}
is in the interior of
C
.
Among all intersection cuts, those derived from maximal
S
-free sets yield the strongest cuts.
For a general reference on intersection cuts including their precise derivation from an
S
-free set, we refer the reader to
[
17
, Chapter 6]
.

In this article, we study maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets, where

Q_{g}:=Q_{g}(\bm{a},\bm{d}):=\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}:\ \|\bm{x}\|\leq\|\bm{y}\|\penalty\ \text{and}\penalty\ \bm{a}{}^{\top}\bm{x}+\bm{d}{}^{\top}\bm{y}=-1\}

for
(\bm{a},\bm{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
with
n,m\geq 1
and
\max\{\|\bm{a}\|,\|\bm{d}\|\}=1
.
Here,
\|\cdot\|
denotes the
\ell_{2}
-norm.
Together with maximal
Q_{h}
-free sets, where

Q_{h}:=\left\{(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}:\|\bm{x}\|\leq\|\bm{y}\|\right\},

the study of maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets is sufficient to describe
every
maximal quadratic-free set, that is,
S
-free sets when
S
has the form

\{\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \bm{s}{}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{s}+\bm{b}{}^{\top}\bm{s}+f\leq 0\}.

The reduction from quadratic-free sets to
Q_{h}
- and
Q_{g}
-free sets is based on a homogenization and diagonalization argument; we describe this in detail in Section
2.1
.
A characterization of maximal
Q_{h}
-free sets was given in
[
23
]
using so-called non-expansive functions; this prior work characterizes all maximal
homogeneous
-quadratic-free sets.
In this article, we characterize maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets using non-expansive functions as well, consequently completing a characterization of all maximal
inhomogeneous
-quadratic-free sets.
Hence, our work completes a characterization of

Our characterization is explicit and enables the construction of a wide range of maximal quadratic-free sets beyond what is currently known.
Prior work has implemented intersection cuts using only one family of such sets, with positive results

14

15

A complete description of all maximal quadratic-free sets significantly expands known families of valid inequalities for non-convex QCQPs, thus offering a new avenue to strengthen cutting-plane methods in this setting.

After fixing

Whenever we refer to a set

2
Background results

In this section, we collect known background results for completeness.

2.1
Reduction from quadratic-free sets to
Q_{h}
- and
Q_{g}
-free sets

In this subsection, we reduce the characterization of maximal quadratic-free sets to maximal
Q_{h}
- and
Q_{g}
-free sets.
This discussion also appears in
[
25
]
.

Consider a set of the form

S:=\left\{\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \bm{s}{}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{s}+\bm{b}{}^{\top}\bm{s}+c\leq 0\right\},

where
\bm{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is symmetric,
\bm{b}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
c\in\mathbb{R}
.
If
\bm{A}
is positive semi-definite, then
S
is convex.
In this case, full-dimensional maximal
S
-free sets are half-spaces that define supporting hyperplanes of
S
.
If
\bm{A}
is negative semi-definite, then
S
is reverse convex, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus S
is convex.
In this case,
\cl(\mathbb{R}^{d}\setminus S)
is the unique full-dimensional maximal
S
-free set.

Assume
\bm{A}
is indefinite.
If the quadratic form is homogeneous, i.e.,

S=\left\{\bm{s}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\ \bm{s}{}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{s}\leq 0\right\},

then we can diagonalize
\bm{A}
to map
S
to a set of the form
\{(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}:(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in Q_{h}\}
.
The
\bm{x}
(respectively,
\bm{y}
and
\bm{z}
) variables come from the positive (respectively, negative and zero) eigenvalues of
\bm{A}
.
Both
\bm{x}
and
\bm{y}
variables exist because
\bm{A}
is indefinite.
The
\bm{z}
variables are free, so after projecting them out, we deduce that maximal
Q_{h}
-free sets capture all maximal
S
-free sets.

If the quadratic form defining
S
is inhomogeneous, we consider a homogenization of
S
:

S^{\prime}:=\left\{(\bm{s},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:\ \bm{s}{}^{\top}\bm{A}\bm{s}+(\bm{b}{}^{\top}\bm{s})\cdot t+c\cdot t^{2}\leq 0,\,t=1\right\}.

There is a one-to-one correspondence between maximal
S^{\prime}
-free sets contained in the hyperplane
\{(\bm{s},t)\in\mathbb{R}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}:\,t=1\}
and maximal
S
-free sets.
Then, we can proceed similarly to the homogeneous case. Via a diagonalization, map
S^{\prime}
to a set the form

Q_{g}^{\prime}:=\{(\bm{x},\bm{y},\bm{z})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}:\|\bm{x}\|\leq\|\bm{y}\|,\;\bm{a}^{\top}\bm{x}+\bm{d}^{\top}\bm{y}+\bm{h}^{\top}\bm{z}=-1\},

where
(\bm{a},\bm{d},\bm{h})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
.
Here, the
\bm{x}
(respectively,
\bm{y}
and
\bm{z}
) variables come from the positive (respectively, negative and zero) eigenvalues in the quadratic form defining
S^{\prime}
.
This transformation is linear and one-to-one.
Thus, maximal
Q_{g}^{\prime}
-free sets capture all maximal
S
-free sets in this case.

The next step is to remove the
\bm{z}
variables.
Muñoz and Serrano
[
25
, Remark 3.2]
argue that if
\bm{h}\neq\bm{0}
, then all maximal
Q_{g}^{\prime}
-free sets are of the form
C\times\mathbb{R}^{\ell}
, where
C
is a maximal
Q_{h}
-free set.
Thus, we can further reduce to the case where
\bm{h}=\bm{0}
in
Q_{g}^{\prime}
, i.e., to
Q_{g}
.

The final step is to ensure
\max\{\|\bm{a}\|,\|\bm{d}\|\}=1
.
The set
S
(and thus
Q_{g}
) is not empty because
\bm{A}
is indefinite.
Hence,
\max\{\|\bm{a}\|,\|\bm{d}\|\}>0
.
We can achieve
\max\{\|\bm{a}\|,\|\bm{d}\|\}=1
by rescaling the variables.

2.2
Basic properties of full dimensional maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets

Theorem
2
states maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets have the form
C^{G}_{\Gamma}\cap H
.
The next result is a simpler variation of Theorem
2
.
The result follows from Zorn’s Lemma, using the proof strategy of
[
16
, Theorem 4.4]
.
We use this simpler variation as a stepping stone to prove Theorem
2
later.

Lemma 1
.

If
K\subseteq H
is a full-dimensional maximal
Q_{g}
-free set, then there exists a function
\Gamma:D^{m}\to D^{n}
such that
C_{\Gamma}
is a full-dimensional maximal
Q_{h}
-free set and
K=C_{\Gamma}\cap H
.

Proof.

The affine hull of
K
does not contain the origin, so
\cone(K)\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m}
is
(n+m)
-dimensional.
Furthermore, if
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\inte(\cone(K))
, then
\lambda\cdot(\bm{x},\bm{y})\in\relint(K)
for some
\lambda>0
.
As
K
is
Q_{g}
-free, it follows that
\lambda\cdot(\bm{x},\bm{y})\not\in Q_{g}
.
Hence,
\|\bm{x}\|>\|\bm{y}\|
, implying that
(\bm{x},\bm{y})\not\in Q_{h}
.
Thus,
\cone(K)
is
Q_{h}
-free.

Consider the family
\mathcal{F}
of
Q_{h}
-free sets containing
\cone(K)
, along with the partial-order defined by set containment.
Let us argue that each chain
(P_{i})_{i=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathcal{F}
has an upper bound in
\mathcal{F}
, namely,
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}
.
If
\inte(\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i})\cap Q_{h}\neq\emptyset
, then
\inte(P_{i})\cap Q_{h}\neq\emptyset
for some
i
.
The set
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}
is convex because it is a nested union of convex sets.
Hence,
\cup_{i=1}^{\infty}P_{i}
is
Q_{h}
-free.
By Zorn’s Lemma, there is a maximal element in
\mathcal{F}
.
By Theorem
3
, this maximal element has the form
C_{\Gamma}

Lemma 2
.

Let
\Gamma:D^{m}\to D^{n}
be such that

is full-dimensional.

4
A sufficient condition for maximal
Q_{g}
-free sets

Muñoz et al. developed a sufficient condition for full-dimensional maximal
S
-free sets for general
S
in
[
23
, Theorem 1.4]
.
We could project

into an
(n+m-1)
-dimensional space and apply their result there, where

is full-dimensional.
However, in light of Lemma

, it will be convenient to stay in

and maintain a perspective of

-free sets as slices of
Q_{h}
-free sets; this preference prevents us from directly using their result.
Instead, we modify their criterion to handle ‘full-dimensionality’ in the
(n+m-1)
-dimensional hyperplane

.
In this section, we restate the necessary definitions and prove the necessary results needed in our modification.

We begin with the definition of an exposing sequence, which is the main ingredient in the characterization of
[
23
]
.

Definition 1

(Exposing sequence)
.

Let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a convex set, and let
\bm{\alpha}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\overline{\alpha}
, with
\bm{\alpha}\neq\bm{0}
, be a valid inequality for
C
.
A sequence
(\bm{x}_{t})_{t=1}^{\infty}
in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
is an
exposing sequence for
\bm{\alpha}{}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\overline{\alpha}
with respect to
\bm{C}
if

\lim_{t\to\infty}(\bm{\delta}_{t},{\varepsilon_{t}})=(\bm{\alpha},\overline{\alpha})

for every sequence
((\bm{\delta}_{t},\varepsilon_{t}))_{t=1}^{\infty}
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times\mathbb{R}
such that
\|\bm{\delta}_{t}\|=\|\bm{\alpha}\|
,
\bm{\delta}_{t}{}^{\top}\bm{x}\leq\varepsilon_{t}
is a valid inequality for
C
, and
\bm{\delta}_{t}{}^{\top}\bm{x}_{t}\geq\varepsilon_{t}
for each
t
.

The following is our maximality criterion.

Theorem 7
.

The inclusion
