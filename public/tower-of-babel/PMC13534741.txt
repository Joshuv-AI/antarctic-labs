An uncertainty-aware vision transformer–BiLSTM Bayesian framework for reliable clinical decision support using chest X-rays
Authors: Fuad S Al-Duais, Sahar Almenwer, Afrah Alanazi, Refga Abdallah Eltieb Elebeed
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractDespite recent progress in deep leaning for medical image analysis, there are still issues of reliability, interpretability, and uncertainty estimation that must be addressed for the safe adoption of deep learning into clinical practice. Conventional CNN- based models have achieved excellent accuracy but have also typically shown prediction confidence levels that are too extreme, limiting confidence in very high-stakes diagnostic environments. To tar- get these deficiencies, we propose a suitable competence-based uncertainty-aware infrastructure that combines Vision Transformers, BiLSTM modeling, followed by a Bayesian fusion layer to permit high reliability in disease detection through the use of chest X-rays. The recently proposed Vision Transformer – BiLSTM Bayesian Fusion (ViT – BiLSTM – BF) architecture is trained and eval- uated on two large-scale datasets, the MIMIC-CXR-JPG and PadChest-GR datasets, looking into Pneumonia, Pulmonary Fibrosis, and Pleural Effusion in study depth. Normalization, U-Net (Convo- lutional Neural Network)-based lung segmentation and extensive data augmentation were completed as preprocessing steps. The ViT encoder extracts global spatial features while contextual (temporal) information is maintained by the BiLSTM across the patch embeddings. A Bayesian fusion module that integrates predictive distributions to quantify a priori and a posteriori aleatoric uncertainty and epistemic uncertainty was developed. Performance metrics of accuracy, AUC-ROC, F1 score, Brier Score, ECE and statistical results were used to assess performance. The developed model displayed superior performance over other models achieving accuracy of 95.5% and AUC-ROC of 0.986 and calibration error was extremely small (ECE = 0.028). The Bayesian fusion layer developed produced well calibrated measures of confidence that would be needed to distinguish reliably between high confidence and high risk predictions. In all comparative analyses improvements over others in CNN, DenseNet, Bayesian CNN and the individual ViT alone disgracefully performed. The results of this study demonstrate that by virtue of combining transformer based spatial modelling, temporal feature understanding, and probabilistic fusion, there is a highly reliable and clinically meaningful diagnostic system realised. The framework ultimately offers a real form of solution to the critical issues regarding uncertainty estimation and model reliability and offers potentially interesting possibilities for feasible implementation within at least the development of clinical decision support systems.Keywords: Bayesian fusion, Chest X-rays, Clinical decision support, Medical imaging, Uncertainty estimation, Vision transformerSubject terms: Computational biology and bioinformatics, Diseases, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Chest X-ray (CXR) remains one of the most popular diagnostic tools for the identification of pulmonary diseases like pneumonia, pulmonary fibrosis and pleural effusion2. The growing number of radiological examinations and the need for quick analysis have brought the systems with AI to the fore, promising great assistance to doctors in the interpretation of medical images3. Nevertheless, in spite of the progress that has been made, a majority of the existing computer-aided diagnosis systems are still working as deterministic models giving one prediction without reflecting uncertainty4. Uncertainty in these predictions could be dangerous in a medical environment, where the confidence in diagnosis is one of the most important factors that will influence clinical decision-making, prioritisation of treatment and minimisation of clinical error5. Recently, there have been some studies that have applied probabilistic and federated deep learning approaches to enhance the reliability of models and data privacy issues in medical imaging6. Secure multi-institutional training and model uncertainty estimation have been shown to yield promising results for Federated Bayesian CNN architectures. However, such models are based primarily on the detection of presence or absence of disease, and ignore the complex nature of disease state and disease progression. Furthermore, CNN-based models suffer from the short view field and thus miss capturing of global dependencies and long-range feature relationships in radiographic structures. Moreover, the lack of multimodal reasoning—like fusing image features with textual radiology reports—greatly reduces the systems’ interpretability and clinical relevance7. Therefore, the current systems of AI are robust and transparent enough only to be used in the real world. To overcome these shortfalls, the research we focus on proposes an Uncertainty-Aware Vision Transformer–BiLSTM Bayesian Framework for Reliable Clinical Decision Support Using Chest X-Rays8. The suggested model integrates the Vision Transformer (ViT) model’s global representation capabilities with the sequential and contextual learning capability of the Bidirectional Long Short-Term Memory (BiLSTM) network, which enables the capture of both spatial and temporal dependencies in the radiographic data9. The combination of the two modules uses a Bayesian fusion mechanism which enables the simultaneous estimation of epistemic and aleatoric uncertainties, resulting in robust and well-calibrated predictions10. The model is tested on two large-scale CXR datasets (MIMIC- CXR-JPG and PadChest GR) for both disease detection and disease severity classification, and uncertainty-aware triage recommendation. The novelty of the work lies in the use of an integrated Vision Trans- former–BiLSTM Bayesian structure that encompasses spatial, contextual and probabilistic dependencies, enabling uncertainty-aware decision making, and enhancing the reliability and interpretability of clinical AI systems based on chest X-rays. This probabilistic hybrid approach is likely to substantially enhance diagnostic certainty, and aligns with the growing criteria of medical imaging AI models that are explainable and trustworthy. This study is significant because it is the Uncertainty-Aware Vision Transformer–BiLSTM Bayesian Framework that is a significant advancement in the field of AI-error radiology. Through the use of spatial attention, contextual learning, and probabilistic reasoning, the model can not only detect and classify lung diseases really well with X-rays but also measure the uncertainty of the outputs to help the doctor decide what to do11. This creates a situation where the doctor will be able to rely on it and it is really sure about its predictions. The radiologist will always have that the machine is super confident about its predictions. The study will not only increase the number of AI-assisted doctors but also the quality of the relationship between humans and machines12. This novelty of the study is the integration of Vision Transformer, BiLSTM, and Bayesian fusion to classify diseases and estimate uncertainties simultaneously, surpassing current transformer-based diagnostic models in providing accurate, explainable, and trustworthy clinical decision support. In current fusions of attention mechanism-based frameworks for interpretation and generation of reports, this results also in inefficiency of algorithms and lack of generalization ability towards different modalities of data because models of attention usually rely on combinations of large amounts of data generated through computing resources for algorithm update and learning20. Bayesian and probabilistic models for uncertainty measurement also only partially solve this problem due to low efficiency of computing results with complexity of helping functions associated with learning algorithms21. Additionally, models do avoid reassuringly the combination of different modalities of image data and learning algorithm or a contextual related- ness towards situations arising in real life clinical environments encountered and tried to be solved and understood with these models. The necessity of available, generalized computationally easy learning algorithms for communication, learning and interpretation in different but also repeat- able clinical image applications is urgent and very pressing, giving access to reliable, scalable, trust- worthy algorithms for diagnostics in medical imaging. The study addresses this issue by introducing the Uncertainty Aware Vision Transformer – BiLSTM Bayesian Framework society that asserts on both spatial and contextual means of reasoning towards clinical access to reliable support for decision making through the results provided in terms of reliable diagnostic content inferred from objects learnt through study of the chest with the use of X rays in widely available clinical practice.

Proposed methodology

The methodology adopted presents an uncertainty-aware deep learning framework that provides reliable and clinically interpretable decisions from chest X-ray images. This framework is based on the hybrid Vision Transformer-BiLSTM architecture which is useful for analysis of images exhibiting physical properties in global spatial configurations and temporal sequential anatomical properties which conventional CNNs cannot easily evaluate. Also included within this layer is a Bayesian combination of outputs to provide quantifiable epistemic and aleatoric uncertainty estimates such that decisions born from the model predict reliable outputs expressed with correspondingly accurate measures of uncertainty or credibility. The workflow begins with curation and preprocessing of the dataset to improve image quality and ensure diagnostic consistency across a variety of sources. This is followed by patch-based feature extraction through vision transformers and bi-directionally modeled tokens through BiLSTM. Methods utilized also include a fused probabilistic output stemming from these feature representations which can be calibrated and modified towards an even greater reliability in the realization of a conventional clinical environment. Dedicated uncertainty analysis methods allow high-risk and/or equivocal cases to be flagged for expert opinion thus increasing patient safety. The whole pipeline is trained and validated using strict statistical validation methods, in order to ensure reliable performance and generalizing.

Dataset collection

The image database used for this research consists of multiple institutions’ database including MIMIC- CXR-JPG22 and PadChest-GR23 which contains chest X-rays taken in various hospitals. The data sets are good quality chest X-rays annotated for Pneumonia, Pulmonary Fibrosis and Pleural Effusion. The careful construction of the data ensures reliability and a good fit for training the proposed federated Bayesian CNN framework.

Data preprocessing

Data preprocessing is a vital aspect of this work designed to guarantee that images of chest X- rays from MIMIC-CXR-JPG and PadChest-GR datasets lend to the input into the federated Bayesian CNN model proposed here. There are differences between the raw X-ray images in both datasets: they differ in quality, in brightness, in the amount of noise in the images, and in the system of labelling used in the two sets of images. Preprocessing is performed to normalize these differences and remove unwanted artifacts, and to normalize the disease labelling for consistent classification. Proper preprocessing makes for improvements in model convergence, reductions in computational complexity, and improved feature extraction accuracy. Additionally, pre-processing ensures that the model is able to learn patterns that are clearly relevant and meaningful to Pneumonia, Pulmonary Fibrosis, and Pleural Effusion. During this phase image resizing and normalization, lung segmentation, label harmonization, and data augmentation are performed. All of these processes help to reach robust, interpretable and reproducible results in the context of federated learning across various sources. The images from the chest X-ray data are resized and intensity normalized to a common spatial and intensity scale for use with convolutional neural networks. All images are resized to 224 × 224 pixels in order correlate the input dimensions sufficiently for further convolution operations24. The standardization of input dimensions means that the convolution operations can be standardized across both datasets. The intensity normalization was applied to scale pixel values to a fixed range during training in order to reduce variation that may have occurred due to different exposures or imaging devices used. Here the model would be able to focus on the underlying features of disease that would not be affected by irrelevant intensity differences if the different exposure backgrounds were observed. This is also recommended as a means of speeding up convergence of training through the iterations of training and also aiding stability of training in federated aggregation. Label encoding

To make it easier for the machine to understand the diagnostic labels given to each chest X-ray, label encoding was used. The research is all about differentiating three important respiratory conditions: Pneumonia, Pulmonary Fibrosis, and Pleural Effusion, hence a binary vector representation was produced for every image. According to this one versus all coding system, each disease category was given a distinct position in the vector, with ”1” representing the disease’s presence and ”0” representing its absence. The method also gives the model the ability to learn features for each condition independently and at the same time cope with instances where more than one abnormality shows up in the same X-ray. Furthermore, the labels were checked for consistency across the two datasets to eliminate consistency problems in the labels and also to use the same semantics of the labels during training. With the aim of retaining class proportions, stratified sampling was used to split the data set into 70% training, 15% validation, and 15% testing. The data in the training set was used for parameter learning and the validation set was used for hyper- parameter tuning and early stopping. The test set was not used to fine-tune the model to achieve optimal fit to the training data, but instead to evaluate performance and uncertainty estimates of the model.

Model architecture

The study presents a novel hybrid Vision Transformer–BiLSTM Bayesian Fusion (ViT–BiLSTM–BF) model that combines spatial attention, context-aware feature extraction, and probabilistic reasoning to provide accurate clinical decision support in chest X-ray analysis. It starts with a Vision Transformer module to learn global spatial features with patch embedding and multi-head self-attention, which leads to more effective detection of subtle lung abnormalities. The features in these layers are then fed into a Bidirectional LSTM network that captures forward and backward context between the spatial tokens to model anatomical coherence within the thoracic region. The outputs of the ViT and BiLSTM branches are fused in a Bayesian fusion mechanism, which estimates epistemic uncertainty and aleatoric uncertainty, to increase the trust in the diagnosis. The probabilistic integration yields calibrated predictions, and avoids “overconfident” misclassifications in clinically important cases. The combined representation is subsequently passed to a classification head equipped with Monte Carlo Dropout to further support Bayesian inference. Overall, the architecture delivers a robust, uncertainty-aware decision support system capable of improving the reliability and interpretability of automated chest X-ray diagnosis.

This single pipeline ensures a consistent, reliable and uncertainty calibrated output generated from results suitable for clinical decision support.

Methodology framework.

Vision transformer (ViT) encoder

A Vision Transformer (ViT) is a state-of-the-art learning model that takes an image as input and processes it by cutting it into patches of a spec- ified size and treating each patch like a token in the same sequence as words in natural language26. The ViT applies linear embeddings and positional encodings to keep the spatial relationships intact in the image. It does so by self-attention that has multiple heads, thus the ViT is capable of recognizing long-range interactions between far- off regions of a chest X-ray, which in turn leads to a better understanding of the global anatomy pattern. This is why it is very good at finding minor flaws that might be hidden in the lungs. The ViT is able to see the whole picture instead of molecules like traditional techniques through local filters, thus it goes beyond the limitations of the conventional CNNs. Being very powerful in terms of representation, the ViT is the perfect tool for the medical imaging area where there is a need for high sensitivity and dependability.

The original chest X-ray is initially sliced into tiny flattened image patches, which are subsequently linearly projected and enriched with positional embeddings for preserving their spatial character. The projector sends these patches straight to the transformer encoder, where different lung regions are connected by the multi-head self-attention and the normalization layers that capture such long-range dependencies. Being part of a global sequence, the encoder allows each patch to be processed in a way that the model detects very subtle and distributed disease clues that are usually missed by CNNs. The resulting sequence is then given to the MLP head for high-level feature representation after which it is passed to the downstream BiLSTM and Bayesian fusion components. This structure guarantees that the ViT provides a strong global contextual feature that is able to render pneumonia, pulmonary fibrosis, and pleural effusion predictions both accurate and uncertainty-aware. Bayesian fusion layer

The research presented here employs the Bayesian Fusion Layer, which merges the predictive distributions produced by the Vision Transformer and BiLSTM branches to generate a single out- put that is aware of uncertainty27. Rather than merging features in a deterministic manner, it combines their probability distributions so as to take into account variation in both model parameters and input data. This combination allows for the concurrent estimation of epistemic uncertainty (model uncertainty) and aleatoric uncertainty (data noise). The layer assigns a calibrated posterior probability to each disease class, which in turn increases the reliability of the diagnosis. By measuring the uncertainty, it aids in the separation of high-certainty predictions from doubtful cases. This feature is the main support of the model’s clinical decision-making power. This probabilistic formulation allows the model to account for both epistemic uncertainty, arising from limited model knowledge, and aleatoric uncertainty, stemming from inherent noise in chest X-ray images. Learning framework and optimization process

The optimization of the model was performed with the AdamW optimizer which was chosen for its ease of implementation, leading to improved stability and convergence speed without having to directly manipulate the weight decay parameter; the weight decay is just incorporated into the update of the weights in the gradients28. It has advantages of better regularization control and not having any degradation phenomena during the training process of models for a long time period when using the normal Adam optimization. An appropriate initial learning rate was thus chosen as 1e-4 to guarantee a proper learning speed and stability. A cosine annealing schedule was adopted for the model so that learning rate reduction would occur across various epochs to prevent oscillation of the model in terms of learning, and premature collapse of the model in terms of learning. This adaptive schedule allows better exploration of loss space during early iterations, and allows for better learning towards the later epochs. Categorical cross entropy was adopted as the principal loss function for the multi-class disease classification, since it allows for a good learning of the gradient propagation over all classification classes. The loss function was augmented with an uncertainty calibration component whereby the probabilities predicted on current learning will reflect the model’s actual confidence values. Together these optimization regimes reinforce a stable environment for training the hybrid ViT – BiLSTM framework.

The training procedure was performed for 100 epochs, which have allowed for the model to per- form sufficient iterations for it to effectively be able to learn the complex spatial and temporal dependencies present in chest X-ray images. Early stopping was utilized in the training work- flow, monitoring validation AUC as improvement criteria to avoid overfitting and minimize unnecessary computational burden. This will ensure the model has good generalization performance and is not at risk for memorization effects; it will also aid in aiding the learning of effective representations that will aid the performance of the model. Mini-batch optimization over batches of data was performed in order to stabilize gradient updates and improve the use of GPU processing. Throughout the training cycle, Monte Carlo dropout was also employed to simulate the variance effects of Bayesian sampling, which allows the model to learn in a stochastic manner and to naturally incorporate estimates of uncertainty. Data augmentation operations were also applied during training to allow for the model to successfully interface with a variety of radiological variations and allow it to minimize sensitivity to noise. All experiments were performed in a controlled environment, using the PyTorch and TensorFlow backends to ensure reproducibility and computational consistency. This structured training pipeline enables the model to achieve high accuracy while still retaining reliability in clinical decision support.

Reliability calibration and uncertainty quantification

In order to make the model’s predictions accept- able in everyday clinical practice, a post-hoc temperature scaling method was utilized to adjust the output probabilities and rectify overconfident fore- casts. Subsequently, uncertainty was estimated by means of Monte Carlo sampling, during which several stochastic forward passes were made by turning on dropout at the time of inference. The model thereby expressed epistemic uncertainty, which is the uncertainty in the model parameters, as the measurement of variability across repeated pre- dictions. Epistemic uncertainty comes from a lack of knowledge in the models, and can be mitigated with more training data. Aleatoric uncertainty is the inherent variability (noise) of chest X-ray images and cannot be reduced with an increasing number of samples. At the same time, aleatoric uncertainty was extracted from the output variance, which is the representation of noise and ambiguity intrinsic to the imaging data. The fusion of both types of uncertainty resulted in a confidence score for each diagnostic output that was exhaustive. The final confidence score is based on the posterior distribution from the Bayesian analysis and accounts for both the certainty of the prediction and the size of the uncertainty. Increased diagnostic ambiguity and prioritization for expert clinical review are reflected in lower confidence score. These uncertainty scores were then employed for the categorization of predictions into three groups based on confidence levels: high-confidence, moderate- confidence, and low-confidence. High-confidence cases denote automated decisions that can be trusted, whereas moderate-confidence cases indicate that a clinician’s intervention is required and low-confidence cases underscore and prompt the need for urgent expert attention. This systematic prioritization process leads to an improved insight of the model and at the same time plays a key role in the safe use of the model in clinical processes.

Discussion

The results of this work show that the proposed Vision Transformer–BiLSTM Bayesian Fusion framework has significantly improved the disease detection from chest X-rays in terms of accuracy, robustness and reliability. The model per- formed significantly higher than other models in the evaluation metrics of accuracy, AUC-ROC, F1 score, Brier Score and calibration errors, providing evidence of good predictive performance and well-calibrated confidence estimates. The uncertainty quantification results corroborate this and provide evidence of the model being able to dis- criminate its high-confidence and high-risk pre- dictions which are vital for real-world applications of clinical decision support. High-confidence predictions can be used for routine screening, while high-risk predictions with high uncertainty can be sent to a specialist radiologist for a second opinion to increase the safety of the diagnosis and decision support. In comparison to existing methods of deep learning including CNNs, DenseNet and Bayesian CNN variants, the proposed model provides improved performance across the board subjective of the evaluation metrics used for all tasks, particularly with regards to capturing the global spatial dependencies and modulating the complex interactions of the feature maps. The juxtaposition of ViT with BiLSTM gave a boost to contextual justification, while the Bayesian fusion improved the interpretability and put forth greater risk-awareness which is an issue not addressed in many of the preceding studies. The study mostly satisfied the aspects of researched advancement presented in later works but enhanced those previous studies by resolving the issues of accuracy and uncertainty and examined these parameters concurrently, thus filling an as yet underexamined deficit in the literature concerning observations in medical imaging. The proposed framework is unique in that it integrates global feature learning, contextual reasoning and probabilistic uncertainty modeling in one diagnostic architecture, rather than the recent transformer-based models which mostly focus on classification performance, and the uncertainty-aware models which mostly address uncertainty estimation without the context. Regardless of the encouraging and positive report of findings, there are limitations associated with this investigation which should be stated. The study concerned its investigations to that of chest X-ray images alone, where the response result and performance must be addressed to the exhaustive data sets made available from Multi-Modality or different imaging technique cohorts. Moreover, the computational cost of such transformer based models still has a greater performance cost than traditional CNN architecture with respect to their computational expense which will impose limits as to their deployment in low resource settings.

Conclusion and future works

This research aimed to design an uncertainty- aware deep learning framework that would provide reliable disease identification from chest X-ray images in light of the urgent need for trustworthy AI in the clinical setting. By using Vision Trans- formers, BiLSTM networks and combining with Bayesian fusion, the proposed framework success- fully integrated global feature learning, contextual representation and calibrated probabilistic reasoning. The results of the experiments showed that the proposed CNN-based model outperforms the baseline models (CNN and Bayesian) with respect to the accuracy, area under receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC-ROC), F1 score, and calibration metrics on the investigated chest X-ray dataset. The uncertainty estimation capability can be used to provide risk-informed clinical decision support and to highlight predictions that need further expert validation. The study also mentions limitations on the computational intensity of transformer-based architectures and the exclusive use of chest X-ray datasets. However, the proposed framework offers a promising framework for the development of reliable and uncertainty-aware diagnostic support systems, which need to be further validated in real-world clinical settings.

In the future, efforts should center on expanding this framework into the domain of multi- modal healthcare data, especially via the bringing together of radiological reports, CT scans and/or clinical metadata, in order to construct a more all-encompassing diagnostic system. To decrease computational expenses, optimization strategies such as pruning, quantization or knowledge distillation could also be used, in order to facilitate deployment in economically impoverished settings. Another area worth exploring is real-time uncertainty-guided triaging, where the model is used actively to prioritize, in order of their importance, high-risk or ambiguous cases that require specialist opinion. It may also prove beneficial to investigate areas of federated or privacy-preserving learning, which could enhance the general application of the framework to multi- institutional datasets. Finally, longitudinal studies in real clinical environments will be of major importance for assessing the real-life impact of the model and its efficacy and generalizability.
