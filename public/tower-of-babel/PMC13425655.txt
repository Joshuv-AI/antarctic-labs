Ayurvedic management of grade III medial meniscal injury: A case report
Authors: Manorma Singh, Poonam Garg, Ritu Thakur
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Tear of grade III in the posterior horn of medial meniscus is a serious knee injury. Arthroscopic surgery is usually advised to manage these cases with variable outcomes. A female housewife aged 32 years, presented with the complaints of pain, stiffness, swelling and occasional giving way of the left knee joint for 2 months; at outdoor patient department of Shalya Tantra (surgery) of our hospital, India on 02/05/2022. There was history of fall from stairs 2 months back. MRI findings were suggestive of grade III tear of posterior horn of medial meniscus extending up to inferior surface. She was advised arthroscopic surgery. Due to financial constraints, surgery was not opted. She was managed with oral Ayurvedic medicines: Abha Guggulu, Punarnavashtak Kwatha, Mukta Pishti, Brahma Rasayana; along with Matra Basti of Bala-Ashwagandha Taila and physiotherapy. After 9 months of systemic treatment and physiotherapy, complete healing was achieved as assessed by Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) and Western Ontario Meniscal Evaluation Tool (WOMET). Standalone Ayurvedic management resulted in positive outcomes. The objective is to unveil the inherent healing potential of Ayurveda that may serve as a lead for further research and a hope for those who defer from surgery due to various constraints.

Introduction

Isolated meniscal tears result from uneven rotational or shearing forces across the tibio-femoral joint under the impact of excessive axial load on the meniscus. Posterior horn of medial meniscus plays an important part in maintaining joint stability, weight bearing and shock absorption. Tear of MRI Grade III through posterior horn of medial meniscus may significantly affect the knee related quality of life. A female housewife aged 32 years, after falling from stairs, had sustained MRI grade III tear of posterior horn of medial meniscus extending up to inferior surface. She was advised arthroscopic surgery that was denied for financial reasons. After 9 months of systemic Ayurvedic treatment and physiotherapy, complete healing was achieved as assessed by Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) and Western Ontario Meniscal Evaluation Tool (WOMET) [1].

Patient information

After falling from stairs, a female housewife aged 32 years, post graduate by academic qualifications, middle class socio-economic status; presented with the complaints of pain, stiffness and swelling around the left knee joint, difficulty in going up and down the stairs, with occasional giving way of the knee joint for 2 months. Aggravation of pain and swelling was reported on prolonged standing in kitchen and on walking. She also reported incomplete evacuation of bowel. MRI findings were suggestive grade III tear of posterior horn of left medial meniscus extending up to inferior surface.

Clinical findings

Patient was having antalgic gait with difficulty in squatting due to pain around left knee. No visible scar or deformity was present. On palpation, no local rise of temperature was observed. As compared to the normal side, there were visible oedema with palpable crepitus and tenderness on the left knee joint line. Mc Murray's test for medial meniscus was found positive. Flexion was restricted to 90° and extension to 30° as assessed by goniometer.

Therapeutic interventions.

Physiotherapy: Physiotherapy was advised after three months of treatment with oral ayurvedic medicines. By this time, pain and swelling had subsided, so patient can comfortably follow the schedule of physiotherapy. Hot water fomentation for 15 minutes twice a day along with gradually progressive knee isometric exercises, heel press, straight leg raise (SLR) exercise, side line SLR, heel drag exercise, quadriceps strengthening exercises were initially started for four weeks. Afterwards, ½ kg weight cuff exercise with gradually increasing the weight up to 1½ kg was carried out.

Follow-up

Patient was followed up for 2 years after treatment. Initially, there were 2 follow-up visits each at 6 months interval for one year. No recurrence of symptoms was observed, rather an improvement of 10% on normal WOMET Score was observed on her second follow-up visit. Patient was contacted for third follow-up telephonically after 1 year and she reported a complete relief. Recently, on 17-05-2026, she was contacted telephonically. She reported no knee related health issues.

Patient was assessed before treatment, after systemic treatment of 9 months and after completing the schedule of physiotherapy of 9 months. Afterwards, in two follow-up visits at an interval of 6 months; she was assessed for the duration of one year. Percentage of normal WOMET score improved from its initial value of 25% to 77% after completion of nine months of systemic medicines and physiotherapy. Discussion

As compared to the lateral meniscus, tears of medial meniscus are more common [2]. It might be due to the restricted mobility resulting from its attachment to medial collateral ligament [3]. The most important weight bearing part of medial meniscus is its posterior horn. A tear in this part deleteriously affects the knee related quality of life. Prognosis depends upon the type and extent of tear as diagnosed by Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI). Usually, arthroscopic surgery is advised that has short term and long-term variable failure rates up to 30% [4]. Poor clinical outcomes have been reported with non-operative treatment of tear of posterior horn of medial meniscus [5]. The ideal time for surgery is the earlier the better to achieve good outcomes. Reluctant attitude towards surgical intervention of the public as well as availability and affordability of surgical technique leads to unusual delays. The patient was managed with oral Ayurvedic medicines along with Matra Basti and physiotherapy.

Matra Basti

Matra Basti a type of Anuvasana Basti (unctuous enema) in which medicated Sneha (mainly oil) in a low dose (Matra) is administered via anal-rectal route. Anuvasana Basti has been specifically indicated in treatment of Bhagna (musculoskeletal injuries) of the extremities [12]. For Matra Basti, Hrisva Matra (least amount) of Sneha has been advocated to be administered that does not cause any discomfort [13]. Here, only 10ml of Bala-Ashwagandhadi Taila was used. Merits of this much amount were better tolerance by the patient, in addition to its easy administration and good retention time.

Assessment tools

WOMET is a valid and reliable tool to assess the disease specific quality of life. It is comprised of 16 items related to three domains viz. physical, work/life style and emotions [1]. MRI provides more pertinent information regarding type and extent of the meniscal injury [14]. Here MRI of the affected knee was used to assess the extent of pathology and effect of management both.

Outcomes

Complete relief in physical symptoms like knee pain, swelling, stiffness, along with normalization of range of movements with overall improvement in quality of life was achieved. No adverse effect of the treatment was reported. Being a single case study, generalizablility of the results may not be there. Although the time period of management and recovery was fairly long, yet it can serve as an alternative to standard surgical treatment.

This case report substantiates healing potential of Ayurveda in the management of musculoskeletal injuries. This serves as a lead for extensive research like randomized controlled trials (RCTs) with collaborative interdisciplinary approach targeted to help those who cannot undergo surgical interventions due to various concerns.

Complete healing of grade III tear of posterior horn of medial meniscus by Ayurveda management invokes the researchers in this field. Ayurveda can help achieve healing with improvement in terms of knee pain, swelling, stiffness along with normal range of motion. Conservative management has always been a preference over surgical interventions by the general public. Such evidences sustain the hopes of suffering humanity.
