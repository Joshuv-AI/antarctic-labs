Global and China burden of maternal sepsis and infections: a systematic global burden of disease 2021 analysis with projections to 2036
Authors: Juntao Sun, Ye Gao, Ilham Mokni, Xinyi Ying, Yanru Wu, Zhuoqi Wang, Zixing Zhong, Suying Miao, Suxiao Liu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundMaternal sepsis and maternal infections (MSMIs) pose significant risks to maternal, fetal, and neonatal health. This study examines the burden trends of MSMIs in China from 1990 to 2021, comparing them with global patterns.MethodsThe burden of MSMIs in China and worldwide was evaluated using data from the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) spanning 1990–2021. Temporal trends were assessed through Joinpoint regression, calculating average annual percentage changes (AAPC) with 95% confidence intervals. Future burden projections up to 2036 were estimated using ARIMA modeling. Cross-regional comparisons were conducted to identify variations in disease impact.ResultsBetween 1990 and 2021, China exhibited distinct trends in the burden of MSMIs compared to global patterns. The country’s age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) declined from 29.62 to 23.95 per 100,000, reflecting an annual reduction of -0.73%, in contrast to a global increase to 37.40. Mortality indicators showed significant declines, with China’s age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) dropping by 77.7% to 4.04 (-5.10% per year), compared to a 56.9% global reduction. Disease burden indicators demonstrated accelerated improvements, as China’s age-standardized disability-adjusted life years rate (ASDR) fell by 78.3% to 93.73 (-5.31% per year), exceeding the global reduction of 53.1%. Prevalence patterns showed a contraction in China’s age-standardized prevalence rate (ASPR) to 390.24 (-0.47% per year), whereas international prevalence rose to 513.68. Age-stratified analysis revealed inverse relationships: ASIR declined progressively with aging, while mortality and disability indicators intensified in older populations, differing from the bimodal prevalence distribution that peaked in middle age.ConclusionFrom 1990 to 2021, China experienced a significant decline in the MSMIs burden, diverging from rising global trends. Projections suggest this divergence will persist, with China’s burden continuing to decrease by 2036, while global rates increase. Urgent targeted interventions are needed for high-risk regions and populations.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s42506-026-00227-0.

Introduction

Maternal sepsis and related infections (MSMIs), defined as life-threatening complications occurring during or after childbirth, pose severe risks to maternal and neonatal health. These infections encompass sepsis, genitourinary tract disorders (excluding sexually transmitted diseases), and postpartum complications such as wound infections and mastitis [1–4]. Sepsis, in particular, contributes significantly to global mortality and morbidity, disproportionately affecting vulnerable populations [5]. MSMIs can result in severe health consequences for both mothers and newborns [6, 7]. In women, maternal infections may lead to infertility, ectopic pregnancy, chronic pelvic inflammatory disease, and, in severe cases, death. A rare but potentially devastating disease caused by maternal herpes simplex virus infection further demonstrates the dire neonatal implications of maternal infections [5, 8]. One million neonates die due to maternal infections or sepsis annually [5–12]. The Global Burden of Disease (GBD) project, an extensive global health research initiative, measures the impact of injuries, diseases, and risk factors on population health by place and over time [13]. By measuring mortality, disability-adjusted life years (DALYs), and morbidity across a wide range of health conditions, the GBD provides essential data for shaping health policies and optimizing resource allocation in the public sector. Recent studies have examined global trends and estimated the 1990–2019 attributable risk for MSMIs [9, 14, 15], examined their association with age, and projected future burden trends [15–18]. These analyses, nevertheless, are primarily from a global perspective, often overlooking between-country and between-region heterogeneity. Therefore, they are not responsive to the specific situation of an individual country. As the world’s largest developing nation, China has undergone rapid economic growth in the past three decades and still has the world’s largest population. Examining MSMIs trends and projections in China offers valuable insights for other developing economies seeking to address the disease burden in densely populated urban areas. While previous research has reported on MSMIs burdens in China [15], its primary focus was on global patterns rather than country-specific trends. China warrants focus due to its massive population, health reforms since 2000 expanding universal coverage and maternal programs, and uniquely steep MSMIs declines, providing a replicable model for developing economies in urbanizing contexts.

This study utilized data from GBD 2021 to calculate age-standardized mortality and morbidity among MSMIs patients of reproductive age, exploring changes from 1990 to 2021 and projecting morbidity and mortality trends for the next fifteen years. The objective is to provide evidence-based insights for policy-makers regarding maternal health strategies.

Methods

Ethical approval

Given the public availability of the 2021 Global Burden of Disease (GBD) data, the institutional ethics committee did not require approval for this study. This research adhered to guidelines ensuring the accuracy and transparency of health assessment reporting. Consequently, ethical approval and informed consent were not required for this research.

Study design and participants

No involvement from patients and/or the public was sought in the conceptualization, conduct, reporting, or dissemination planning of this research.

Data acquisition

This study utilized epidemiological metrics systematically compiled within the Global Burden of Disease (GBD) collaborative platform, an extensive epidemiological database that tracks disease incidence, population-level prevalence, and mortality statistics. The dataset covers 87 etiological categories encompassing 369 distinct health conditions, spanning 204 geopolitical entities at national and subnational levels and 23 demographic cohorts stratified into 5-year age intervals. This multidimensional dataset facilitates detailed epidemiological investigations by providing standardized metrics harmonized across temporal (1990–2021), spatial (territory-level), and population (sex-specific) dimensions. The GBD framework, validated through its disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) calculation model, supports comparative health analyses [19]. The database integrates data from multiple sources, including cohort studies, randomized controlled trials, population-based assessments, and supplementary research efforts [13], accounting for methodological diversity. It synthesizes and quantifies health risks and vulnerabilities. MSMIs, a significant category of maternal and neonatal health conditions, are primarily sourced from the Global Health Data Exchange (GHDx). Additionally, this research utilized analytical tools available through the GBD Results platform at http://ghdx.healthdata.org/gbd-results-tool. The GBD methodology was employed to extract comprehensive datasets on disease incidence, prevalence, DALYs, and mortality rates in China and globally from 1990 to 2021, forming the basis for quantifying the health impact of MSMIs [20]. Institutional Ethics Committee approval was not required, as the 2021 GBD data are publicly accessible.

For most pathological conditions and traumatic injuries, the refined datasets underwent analytical processing by validated computational platforms to stratify participants along demographic dimensions, including age, sex, geographic distribution, and temporal trends. The present study utilized Cause-of-Death Ensemble Modeling (CODEm) and DisMod-MR, a Bayesian meta-regression software, as primary epidemiological standardization systems. DisMod-MR is a Bayesian hierarchical modeling system, systematically integrating multi-source epidemiological data on disease-specific measures like incidence, prevalence, remission rates, and mortality, to provide consistency of epidemiological estimates [18]. CODEm is a statistically integrative modeling system designed to systematize mortality attribution data. Using an ensemble of statistical models, this system evaluates quantitative markers, selecting covariates with optimal predictive generalizability [21, 22]. We utilized the default ensemble parameters optimized for maternal conditions, which internally address data sparsity via Bayesian borrowing across geographic hierarchies, minimizing single model bias while improving predictive validity across diverse regional contexts.

Statistical analysis

The GBD database was used to analyze the incidence, prevalence, mortality, and Disability-Adjusted Life Years (DALYs) of MSMIs, and their corresponding age-standardized rates (ASIR, ASPR, ASMR, and ASDR) and crude rates (CIR, CPR, CMR, and CDR) in China and globally [23–25]. Joinpoint software was employed to calculate average annual percentage change (AAPC) and 95% CI to assess trends in disease burden. The AAPC was derived from the formula 100×(exp(β) − 1), with 95% confidence intervals derived from the model. If the confidence intervals exceed 0, the age-standardized indicator is increasing; if they are below 0, a decreasing trend is indicated; if they include 0, the trend is considered stable.

The ARIMA models were utilized to project future trajectories of MSMIs case counts and age-standardized rates (ASR). Parameter optimization involved identifying (p, d, q) configurations that yielded minimal AIC and BIC metrics while achieving maximal R² performance.

The analytical workflow integrated R Statistical Environment (v4.2.2) and Joinpoint Trend Software (v4.9.0.0) to perform complementary computational processes. The R package “tidyverse” ecosystem enabled multivariate parametric modeling and Bayesian probability estimations, while Joinpoint’s segmented regression algorithms, combined with Monte Carlo permutation testing (10,000 iterations), facilitated breakpoint detection in temporal trends. Data visualization was executed using the ggplot2 package, employing a layered grammar of graphics for multidimensional representations. Statistical significance was determined using an α-level threshold of 0.05, with Benjamini-Hochberg corrections applied to control for Type I error inflation in longitudinal hypothesis testing.

Results

The burden and incidence of MSMIs in China and globally

The number of MSMIs cases in China declined from 3,859,571 (95% CI: 2,657,329-5,264,534) in 1990 to 1,556,591 (95% CI: 1,160,257-2,046,691) in 2021, representing a total reduction of 59.67%. In contrast, the global count of MSMIs cases increased from 22,450,868 (95% CI: 16,778,828 − 28,812,949) in 1990 to 19,047,404 (95% CI: 14,608,563 − 24,086,486) in 2021, reflecting a 15.16% overall rise. The global ASIR decreased from 764.031 (95% CI: 573.011-970.537) per 100,000 population in 1990 to 494.194 (95% CI: 377.345-623.898) per 100,000 in 2021. Meanwhile, China’s ASIR fell from 509.36 (95% CI: 356.898-690.164) per 100,000 in 1990 to 277.782 (95% CI: 202.894-365.424) per 100,000 in 2021. Between 1990 and 2021, China’s AAPC for the incidence rate declined by 1.92% (95% CI: -2.04 to -1.79), whereas the global incidence rate increased by 1.40% (95% CI: -1.45 to 1.35) (Table 1).

Tendency in the prevalence of MSMIs in China and globally between 1990 and 2021

China has experienced a significant rise in MSMIs prevalence, with epidemiological data showing a significant increase in reported cases over recent decades. The number of cases rose from 344,552 (95% CI: 190,363–565,515) in 1990 to 1,526,656 (95% CI: 940,466-2,401,050) in 2021, reflecting a total increase of 343.67%. In contrast, global MSMIs prevalence exhibited a slight decline, decreasing from 2,396,696 (95% CI: 1,554,877-3,637,746) in 1990 to 2,376,876 (95% CI: 1,678,866-3,421,377) in 2021, an overall reduction of 0.83%. The global ASPR also declined, falling from 85.397 per 100,000 population (95% CI: 56.747-127.671) in 1990 to 60.385 per 100,000 (95% CI: 42.186–87.241) in 2021. Similarly, China’s ASPR decreased from 46.714 per 100,000 (95% CI: 26.376–75.454) in 1990 to 25.793 per 100,000 (95% CI: 15.176–41.028) in 2021. Over the same period, the global AAPC for MSMIs prevalence declined by 1.11% (95% CI: -1.17 to -1.06), while in China, it dropped by 1.88% (95% CI: -1.99 to -1.78) (Table 1).

A comprehensive analysis of MSMIs mortality trends in China and globally between 1990 and 2021

On a global scale, MSMIs accounted for 17,665 deaths (95% CI: 14,628 − 21,191) in 2021, representing a 31.3% reduction compared to 1990. In China, the decline was even more pronounced, with MSMIs-related mortality decreasing by 99.63% over the same period. The global ASMR fell from 0.924 per 100,000 population (95% CI: 0.806–1.048) in 1990 to 0.452 per 100,000 (95% CI: 0.375–0.542) in 2021. Meanwhile, China’s ASMR dropped sharply from 0.107 per 100,000 (95% CI: 0.074–0.146) in 1990 to 0.004 per 100,000 (95% CI: 0.003–0.006) in 2021. During this period, global MSMIs mortality showed an AAPC decline of 2.29% (95% CI: -2.50 to -2.08), whereas China exhibited a much steeper annual reduction of 9.92% (95% CI: -10.45 to -9.38), as shown in Table 1.

A comprehensive analysis of MSMIs DALYs tendency in China and global between 1990 and 2021

In 1990, global DALYs for MSMIs were estimated at 1,680,167 (95% CI: 1,452,195-1,894,884), decreasing to 1,144,233 (95% CI: 956,988-1,352,034) in 2021, marking a 32.00% reduction. In contrast, China experienced a far greater decline of 82.81% over the same period. Globally, the ASDR per 100,000 population dropped significantly from 59.329 (95% CI: 51.235–67.094) in 1990 to 29.457 (95% CI: 24.634–34.767) in 2021. China demonstrated even greater progress, with ASDR decreasing from 8.603 (95% CI: 6.223–11.977) in 1990 to 1.403 (95% CI: 0.777–2.386) in 2021. AAPC analysis of DALYs showed a global decline of 2.24% per year (95% CI: -2.44 to -2.04), while China’s decline was significantly steeper at 5.70% per year (95% CI: -6.02 to -5.38), as detailed in Table 1.

The burden of MSMIs in China and globally: trends and developments between 1990 and 2019

Between 1990 and 2019, the ASDR of MSMIs in both China and globally showed a gradual decline, with a more pronounced decrease in China. During this period, China’s MSMIs prevalence patterns exhibited a complex temporal evolution. A significant reduction was observed between 1990 and 2010 (P < 0.05), followed by a moderate resurgence post-2010 before stabilizing after 2015, ultimately resulting in a net contraction in prevalence. A comparison of national and global patterns revealed distinct epidemiological trajectories. While global MSMIs prevalence remained relatively stable between 1990 and 2010, China experienced a subsequent increase. Longitudinal analysis further highlighted nuanced trends—China’s incidence and mortality metrics showed a steady decline between 1990 and 2019, whereas global trends followed a different path, with incidence gradually increasing while mortality declined (Fig. 3).

Analysis of the MSMIs across various age groups in China and globally: a comparison between 1990 and 2021

Figures 4 and 5 illustrate the differences in incidence, prevalence, mortality, and DALYs of MSMIs across various age groups in China and globally from 1990 to 2021, along with their respective crude rates. Epidemiological data indicate that in China, MSMIs were more frequently observed among individuals under 50, with the highest case concentration occurring in the 0–35 age group. The progression of MSMIs incidence rates followed a consistent pattern across study periods: incidence increased from adolescent cohorts (15–19 years) before gradually declining in adulthood (20–54 years), with the highest rates consistently recorded in the youngest age group in both 1990 and 2021. In contrast, the global CIR of MSMIs showed an upward trend across all age groups, with incidence rates exceeding those in China (Figs. 4A and 5A). CPR data followed a similar pattern. Epidemiological surveillance further revealed temporal shifts in MSMIs distribution, with China’s prevalence peak shifting from the 20–24 age group in 1990 to the 30–34 demographic in 2021 (Fig. 4B, Supplementary Fig. 1). Mortality patterns reflected this epidemiological transition, with peak fatal cases shifting between identical age groups over the decades. Case-fatality metrics showed a sustained age-dependent increase, with the highest mortality consistently observed in the 20–24 cohort during both surveillance periods (Fig. 4C). This trend contrasted with case concentration patterns, which gradually shifted towards older demographics over time. A similar age-related increase was seen in the CDR. In 1990, DALYs peaked in the 20–24 age group, but by 2021, the highest burden had shifted to the 25–29 cohort (Fig. 4D). On a global scale, the burden of MSMIs increased across all age groups, with the highest incidence and prevalence rates observed in older age groups, as shown in Figs. 5A and B. Additionally, mortality and DALYs also rose with age worldwide, as depicted in Supplementary Fig. 2, Fig. 5C, D.

Discussion

This study comprehensively examined the burden of MSMIs and other maternal infections (MSMIs) in China and globally from 1990 to 2021, while also projecting future trends over the next fifteen years. The findings revealed significant disparities between China and global trends in MSMIs burden. These improvements stem from China’s healthcare reform, which expanded universal coverage from under 30% in 2003 to over 95% by 2011 through the New Cooperative Medical Scheme, ensuring access to antenatal care and institutional deliveries. Targeted programs like the national “Reducing Maternal Mortality and Eliminating Neonatal Tetanus” initiative established standardized obstetric protocols and emergency referral networks. Concurrent socioeconomic development, including poverty reduction lifting 700 million people and rising female education, reduced underlying risk factors and healthcare barriers, contrasting sharply with persistent infrastructure limitations in low-resource settings globally [27–32]. While China experienced a downward trend in ASIR and ASPR, global trends persistently increased over these two metrics. Meanwhile, Chinese and global estimates both revealed decreases in ASDR and ASMR, implying sustained challenges in MSMIs control, particularly in low-resource settings in the Global South [33, 34]. Furthermore, China’s 5.70% annual DALY decline reflects integrated reforms expanding institutional delivery, standardizing infection control, and reducing case-fatality through early recognition. This contrasts with fragmented global programs limited by infrastructure gaps, demonstrating that coordinated policy implementation drives sustained MSMIs burden decline.

The MSMIs burden showed distinct age-specific trends, with prevalence being greatest in young women, incidence being highest in middle-aged women, and mortality rates rising in perimenopausal women. In China, the incidence of MSMIs was most evident in people aged 14–35. This trend could have implications for reproductive health, as supported by the increased demand for subfertility therapy in China [35]. The epidemiological burden shifted across age groups over time, with prevalence being highest in the 20–24 age group in 1990 and then transitioning to the 30–34 age group by 2021. Similarly, mortality rates showed an age-dependent increase, consistently peaking in the 20–24 age group across both time points. Disability-adjusted health metrics also reflected temporal age stratification, with the highest impact shifting from the 20–24 age group in 1990 to the 25–29 cohort in 2021. These findings highlight the need for increased awareness among healthcare providers and targeted interventions for young women of reproductive age, as they are particularly vulnerable to MSMIs [36–40]. Given that MSMIs remain a major cause of morbidity and mortality among women [41], especially in low- and middle-income countries, early screening and timely medical intervention are essential [42, 43]. The higher burden observed in younger women may be linked to factors such as limited access to healthcare, poor hygiene, and lack of education [40, 44, 45].

The study also highlighted substantial regional disparities in MSMIs burden. In China, mortality and incidence rates remained lower than global averages and most of the seven super regions in 2021. The highest mortality and incidence rates were recorded in Sub-Saharan Africa, emphasizing the urgent need for targeted interventions in these regions [46, 47]. Additionally, projections for the next 15 years suggest a continued decline in MSMIs burden in China, whereas the global burden is expected to rise. The ASIR and ASDR of MSMIs in China are projected to decrease further by 2035, while global ASIR and ASDR are anticipated to increase during the same period [46].

This study offers several strengths. Firstly, epidemiological analytical models were utilized to track MSMIs trends in China and global populations from 1990 to 2021. Age-period-cohort modeling was used to determine key factors driving disease trajectory changes. By using standardized measures from the GBD 2021 database, this study is reproducible and enables cross-regional comparisons of incidence, mortality, and DALY estimates. Secondly, advanced analytical instruments, including Joinpoint regression for nonlinear trend segmentation and ARIMA modeling for burden projection, were used to quantify annual percentage changes and forecast morbidity trends through 2036. These analytical instruments have significant implications for public health planning and resource allocation over the long term. Thirdly, this study stratified the MSMIs burden by age and region, and the findings identify vulnerable populations, for instance, young individuals and residents in underserved regions. This stratification offers a rationale for policy development at both the global and regional levels.

Despite these strengths, several limitations should be acknowledged. The use of GBD-derived epidemiological projections in the analysis has methodological limitations in that the validity of burden estimates is a function of input data completeness, including diagnostic validity and environmental exposure variables subject to change over time. In environments of limited healthcare access and economic volatility, MSMIs burden may be underestimated [39, 48, 49]. This underestimation likely affects global burden more substantially than China’s estimates, potentially magnifying the true disparity between China’s declining trend and global patterns. Consequently, our reported differences may represent a conservative measure of China’s relative progress. Temporal variations in diagnostic regimens and detection criteria may further affect the comparability of historical burden estimates. Notably, the transition from the ICD-9 to ICD-10 coding systems during the 1990–2021 period may have altered how maternal infections and sepsis were classified globally, potentially introducing artifacts into the trend analysis. Additionally, the association between MSMIs and subfertility is an ecological inference that does not imply causation, as infertility is multifactorial.Finally, while this analysis provides global-scale insights, caution should be taken in extrapolating findings to specific national or supranational environments. Variations in socioeconomic structures, geographic conditions, population genetics, and healthcare infrastructure may influence MSMIs trends differently across environments [47, 50–54]. Additionally, ARIMA projections assume trend continuity and cannot account for unforeseen disruptions such as pandemics or policy shifts.

Future research should use quasi-experimental designs to evaluate how policies like China’s Two-Child Policy and maternal health initiatives affected MSMIs trends. Studies must also incorporate unmeasured factors such as antibiotic resistance, water/sanitation access, and facility-level infection control to clarify residual disparities and guide targeted interventions [52–54].

Conclusion

China made significant progress in the reduction of MSMIs during the period 1990–2021, against the global trend of increasing disease prevalence. This divergence highlights the ongoing presence of geographical health disparities requiring prompt, place-specific responses. Addressing such disparities requires focused health interventions involving innovations in preventive medicine, optimized cross-sector resource allocation, and community-based risk reduction frameworks.
