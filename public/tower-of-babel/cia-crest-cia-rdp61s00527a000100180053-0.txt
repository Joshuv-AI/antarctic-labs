^Ref. POLTO 294,1hI7 31, 1957)

Decision

The draft reply to POLTO 294, as revised at this meeting, was approved by all
members for transmission to the USDEL. (See TOPOL 603, 9/6/57)

Further discussions will be held in the Executive Committee with respect to the
U. S. proposals for the forthcoming List II discussions, and also with respect to
quotas for shipments of List II commodities to Poland,

The State member will draft a message, for review by the members, in response
to the comments requested by the USDEL in POLTOs 262 and 470.

Discussion

The Chairman said response to POLTO 294 had been delayed because it had been
thought that some judgments could be obtained from the work presently being done by
WG I which would help us to come closer to estimating what the U„ S. position in the
future might be and thus have some bearing on the response. However, since the
studies have been delayed, instructions must be sent without benefit of that information.

The Chairman distributed a draft reply to POLTO 294 and asked for comments
from the members.

The Commerce member said he agreed that we should press for single quotas
for the entire Bloc but he questioned whether,, for the future,, Poland should be treated
as part of the Bloc or whether some special arrangement should be designated. He
thought it had been decided tentatively at an early stage that we envisaged that in 1958
Poland would not be part of the quota set up for the Bloc. In that regard,, he also
pointed out that we are in a somewhat awkward position in that we already had proposed
and generally accepted the idea that Poland,, for the rest of this year 0 could be in a
non-quota basis under certain circumstances. To revert to placing Poland entirely
in the quota for 1958 could be viewed as tightening up of current proposals, although
we are not bound by it for future periods.

He further felt that, whether or not we have special treatment for Poland, if we
are going to have a Sino-Soviet bloc approach to single quotas, we must review items
in the light of the Sino-Soviet bloc and, therefore, could not deal only with the items the
PCs would want discussed.

There was a discussion of what had actually been agreed upon with respect to
quotas for shipments to Poland. It was determined from TOPOL 233, July 24, and
COCOM Doc. 2578 that the present policy is as follows; When a country has exhausted
its List II quota for the Soviet Bloc, it should first resort to the use of the unallocated
quota. When that quota is exhausted it should seek, through the Committee, to borrow
from unused quotas of other member governments or obtain redistribution of unused
quotas. If other PCs are not agreeable to such borrowing or redistribution, the
country could export on an ex quota basis with the requirement that the matter be re-
ported to COCOM concurrently with the licensing.

There was a discussion of all aspects of the forthcoming discussions of List II
quotas, including the pros and cons of a review of all List II items in an effort to set
a new single quota for 1958 for the Sino-Soviet bloc; the manner in which the List II
discussions would proceed and quotas set if only the items proposed for review by the
PCs were considered; whether or not the PCs should be asked to open only those items
in which the Sino-Soviet bloc quota is inadequate for 1958; the problems proposed by
3(d) if all of the items are not discussed; and the possibility of proposing that the 1957
quotas for the European Soviet bloc provide the initial basis for discussion of 1958
quotas for the entire Sino-Soviet bloc.

The members felt that both the question of quotas for shipments to Poland and the
U. S. proposals for List II discussions needed further discussion before agreed in-
structions could be sent to the USDEL. Since the only instructions needed by the Del
at this time are on the question of whether there should be separate List II quotas for
Communist China or single quotas for the entire Sino-Soviet bloc and the matter of
timing of the discussions of both procedural matters and quotas, the Chairman's
draft message was revised to instruct the Del on these matters.
