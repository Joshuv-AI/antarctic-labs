The role of cultivation technology in shaping the health-promoting potential of potato tubers (Solanum tuberosum L.)
Authors: Katarzyna Gościnna, Elżbieta Wszelaczyńska, Jarosław Pobereżny, Małgorzata Szczepanek, Dominika Boguszewska-Mańkowska, Cezary Trawczyński, Milena Pietraszko
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe aim of the study was to evaluate the effect of selected agronomic factors (nitrogen fertilization, chemical protection, the application of a biostimulant, hydrogel, and supplementary irrigation) on the content of bioactive compounds and the antioxidant capacity of table potato tubers. The research hypothesis assumed that the applied agronomic treatments significantly modify the health-promoting potential of potato tubers. The research was carried out in 2020–21 on two edible potato varieties: Gardena and Denar. The results indicate that the factor differentiating polyphenol content is genotype. Of the tested varieties, in Gardena the content of these compounds was on average 23.8% higher. The effect of mineral nitrogen fertilization, chemical protection, the use of biostimulant and intervention irrigation on the tested parameters was also found to be statistically significant. The content of polyphenols was highest after fertilization at a dose of 150 kg N ha˗1 (Gardena − 3.105 g kg−1 f. m., Denar − 2.357 g kg−1 f. m.) and after full chemical protection combined with dressing tubers using a preparation containing silver nanoparticles (Huwa San TR 50) (Gardena − 2.888 g kg−1 f. m., Denar − 2.480 g kg−1 f. m). Moreover, the combined use of intervention irrigation and the Bioalgen S90 biostimulant was associated with a high content of polyphenolic compounds (Gardena − 3.038 g kg−1 f. m., Denar − 2.400 g kg−1 f. m.) and antioxidant capacity (Gardena − 7.99 mmol Fe2+ kg−1 f. m., Denar − 7.09 mmol Fe2+ kg−1 f. m). This study provides a comprehensive assessment of the effects of integrated cultivation technology, including the use of a biostimulant, hydrogel, interventional irrigation, and optimized fertilization and protection methods, on the concentration of health-promoting compounds in Gardena potatoes. The results indicate that Gardena is a Polish-bred, medium-early variety with exceptional agronomic potential for European cultivation.Keywords: Production technology, Organic variety, Biostimulant, Intervention irrigation, Chemical protection, PolyphenolsSubject terms: Biotechnology, Plant sciences

Introduction

The coming decade will be a challenging time for food producers, whose main task is to improve the global food system around the world. Producing more food of appropriate quality using existing resources is a global challenge. Due to its diversified model of distribution and cultivation and still-high demand, potato (Solanum tuberosum L.) is an important raw material in sustainable food production1. Potatoes are being grown on approximately 168 million hectares of agricultural land worldwide, with global annual production of 383 million tonnes2. Potato is a very attractive crop for agricultural production systems because it combines exceptionally high yield potential with high nutritional value and thus has great potential to combat human malnutrition3–5. Moreover, it is a good source of energy, minerals, protein, fats and vitamins6,7. In addition to the basic nutrients, potato tubers contain health-promoting compounds, such as polyphenols, flavonoids, chlorogenic acid, anthocyanins, and carotenoids8–10. The minerals, vitamins and phytonutrients in potatoes have a beneficial effect on human health5. Due to the antioxidant properties of the compounds contained in potato, its consumption can contribute to maintaining health and even support the treatment of various diseases5,8,11,12. Polyphenols are powerful antioxidants that eliminate the reactive oxygen species that cause many human diseases by damaging lipids, proteins, enzymes and DNA13,14. Moreover, by influencing the intestinal microbiome, they exhibit cytotoxic and anti-inflammatory properties and are considered to be potent inhibitors of carcinogenesis, obesity and cardiovascular disorders15–17. Anthocyanins prevent various chronic diseases and, by improving cognitive functions, prevent dementia18–21. The content of the discussed components varies in potato varieties, which indicates that it is dependent on a genetic trait22(Fig. 1). This diversity in the chemical composition of potato tubers promotes the plant’s resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses, which determines its suitability for cultivation in various habitats23. Edaphic factors such as environmental conditions, type of cultivation technology and stress during the vegetation period also considerably affect health-promoting value24,25. Recent research results report a positive correlation between the content of total polyphenols in potato tubers and environmental factors, such as average air temperature during the vegetation period26 or the use of fertilizers27. In general, the use of nitrogen and potassium fertilizers increases the content of polyphenols, anthocyanins and the antioxidant capacity of potato tubers, which increases the nutritional and functional value of the potato27. In turn, magnesium fertilizers improve the stress resistance of potato by reducing mechanical damage, which lowers the intensity of the reaction involving polyphenol oxidase. This may result in a reduction in polyphenol content28.

Potato varieties Gardena (left) and Denar (right) tested in the experiment.

Currently, a major global problem, both economically and environmentally, is climate change and associated drought. Drought is considered to be the most dangerous environmental stressor. It reduces crop productivity more than any other stressor29. Plant tolerance to drought can be improved through breeding methods and by introducing new production technologies, including preparations that allow water to be stored in the soil23,30. One solution is the use of hydrogels in production. Gels currently produced for agricultural use are purpose-designed, and they are fully biodegradable and therefore environmentally friendly. In addition to limiting the negative effects of drought, the use of hydrogel also revitalizes natural resources. This is of great importance for sustainable agriculture, which in recent years has been preferred worldwide31. The continuing growth in the world’s population, and thus the growing demand for food, has contributed to the intensification of agricultural practices. This in turn has led to the loss of biodiversity and the “chemicalization” of food. Currently, agriculture is looking for environmentally friendly technologies that will support the production of healthy and safe agricultural products32.

It is hypothesized that improving cultivation technology through the use of anti-stress preparations and interventional water supplementation during deficits will contribute to increasing the health-promoting value and functionality of edible potato tubers. Thus, consuming potatoes produced in this way will increase the supply of health-promoting compounds to the body33. Moreover, there is insufficient knowledge about the health-promoting value of potatoes in the context of integrated cultivation using anti-stress substances and irrigation. Therefore, this study contributes to a better understanding of the effects of a cultivation technology based on the use of a biostimulant, hydrogel, interventional irrigation, and different fertilization doses and protection methods on the concentration of compounds determining the health-promoting value of tubers of edible potato varieties.

Results and disscusion

Total polyphenols

The most important health-promoting compounds in potato tubers include polyphenols9,34. The average content of polyphenolic compounds for potatoes grown in Jadwisin was 2.485 g kg−1 of fresh matter (f.m.). The content of polyphenolic compounds in tubers ranged from 0.225 to 1.299 g Gallic acid equivalents (GAE) kg-1 of f.m. (Table 1)8,35. Regardless of the technology used and the year of cultivation, the average content of polyphenolic compounds in potato tubers was 2.820 g kg−1 of fresh weight for the Gardena variety and 2.149 g kg−1 of fresh weight for the Denar variety (Table 1). Averaging for the three experiments, the Denar variety had a 23.8% lower content of the tested compounds than the Gardena variety. Such large differences are mainly genetically conditioned, as many authors have indicated8,36,37. Soare et al.37 report that the total phenolic content in six varieties studied ranged from 0.340 to 0.635 g GAE kg-1f.m., and the highest content of these compounds was recorded in purple and red varieties. Similarly, Ru et al. 36 report that the content of total polyphenols in red and purple potatoes was 0.514–0.73 g GAE kg−1, whereas in yellow and white potatoes it was 0.088–0.199 g GAE kg−1 f.m. The same authors point out that, with potato polyphenols, there are a free (soluble) and a bound fraction. Most studies concern only free phenols, which are determined based on traditional extraction with organic solvents. Meanwhile, the bound fraction can constitute up to 40.0% of all potato polyphenols36, which means that the determined content of polyphenols is far greater than it would be if it were determined only based on free phenol. This in turn means that potatoes are a raw material of even greater health-promoting value.

In all three experiments, total polyphenol content was on average 15.4% higher in 2020 compared to 2021, despite meteorological conditions having been more favorable in 2021 (Table 1). It is widely known that a wetter potato vegetation period (as in 2021) favors the accumulation of polyphenolic compounds38. The 2020 results were 15.5%, 14.3% and 16.0% higher for the Gardena variety and 5.4%, 9.3% and 21.3% higher for the Denar variety for experiments I, II and III, respectively (Table 1). The results indicate that, in the fertilization experiment (I), the polyphenol content in tubers was at a similar level in the Gardena variety as in the Denar variety. In the experiment with protection (II), the Gardena variety exhibited a 5.0 p.p. higher polyphenol content than the Denar variety. In experiment III, the trend was the opposite; the Denar variety showed a 5.3 p.p. higher content of total polyphenols. These differences may result from the response of the varieties to different environmental conditions24–27.

In experiment I, at each applied dose, nitrogen fertilization stimulated an increase in the content of total polyphenolic compounds (by 12.1% on average) (Fig. 2a). In Gardena and Denar varieties alike, the highest statistically significant increase in the tested compounds compared to control was observed after the application of a dose of 150 kg N ha-1, at 18.6% and 20.8%, respectively (Fig. 2a). Significant relationships between nitrogen dose and total polyphenol content were found for both varieties in both study years (2020: Gardena, F = 4.48, p = 0.040, η² =0.627; Denar, F = 19.98, p = 0.004, η² = 0.886; 2021: Gardena, F = 48.75, p = 0.000, η² = 0.948; Denar, F = 14.75, p = 0.013, η² = 0.847). For potatoes, recommended N fertilization rates range from 70 to 330 kg N ha−139,40. At the same time, the cited authors state that the most economically justified nitrogen fertilization doses for potatoes range from 147 to 201 kg N ha−1. From an agronomic perspective, our findings demonstrate that the dose aligns perfectly with this economic threshold, while simultaneously delivering a substantial technological benefit by maximizing the accumulation of bioactive antioxidants in the tubers (up to over 20% increase). Nitrogen fertilization is reported by Wichrowskka41 and Petropoulos et al.42 to have a positive effect on the tested compounds. Musilová et al.43 and Lachman et al.44 hold a different opinion, noting a decrease in the content of polyphenolic compounds with increasing N doses. Similarly, Lombardo et al.40 did not demonstrate a significant effect of N fertilization on the content of polyphenolic compounds in potato tubers. The 140 and 280 kg N ha−1 doses in their studies considerably reduced the content of polyphenolic compounds compared to control. The authors also noted no statistically significant difference between 140 and 280 kg N ha−1 doses in terms of effect on polyphenol content in the potato tubers. This indicates that a dose of 140 kg N ha−1 was sufficient to obtain high polyphenol content. Many authors believe that nitrogen use efficiency (NUE) differs among plant species and depends heavily on the variety45–47. It was also shown that the increase in total polyphenol content at a dose of 150 kg N ha−1 was 24.3% in 2021 and 15.1% in 2020 (Fig. 2a). The higher increase in polyphenol content under the influence of fertilization in 2021 may be due to better N availability in 2021, the year of better meteorological conditions, i.e. higher precipitation. Rainfall increases the availability of nitrogen for plants by stimulating mineralization processes and improving the availability of nutrients48,49. The literature shows a need to select nitrogen fertilization doses that will, on the one hand, provide the necessary macronutrient for plants and, on the other hand, be safe for the environment50.

In our studies, the protection variants used (experiment II) were generally associated with a considerable increase in the content of polyphenolic compounds in potato tubers (2020: Gardena, F = 4.48, p = 0.040, ηp² = 0.626; Denar, F = 5.48, p = 0.024, ηp² = 0.673; 2021: Gardena, F = 2.61, p = 0.124, ηp² = 0.494; Denar, F = 11.80, p = 0.003, ηp² = 0.816). Regardless of experimental factors, the average increase in polyphenol content was 11.2%. The increase was 6.9% for the Gardena variety and 15.5% for the Denar variety (Fig. 3a). Notably, the increase in the content of these compounds was 7% higher in 2020 than 2021 (Table 1).

The best results in this respect were achieved by applying full chemical plant protection using fungicides in accordance with the IOR principles and simultaneously dressing the tubers with Huwa San TR 50 (Fig. 3a). Under this variant, the average increase in polyphenol content was 18.6% (10.1% for Gardena and 27.0% for Denar). Agronomically, the 18.6% average increase represents a substantial shift in raw material quality, which is highly desirable for the functional food market. Interestingly, the magnitude of this response was highly cultivar-dependent. Denar, which typically possesses a lower baseline antioxidant profile, exhibited a remarkable 27.0% surge, suggesting a high metabolic plastic response to chemical elicitation. In contrast, the red-skinned Gardena showed a more conservative increase (10.1%), likely because its constitutive synthesis of polyphenols is already highly elevated due to its genetic background, leaving a narrower margin for further up-regulation. Fungicide preparations and seed potato dressing are reported to positively affect polyphenol contents in potatoes by Fritz et al.51 and Orlikowski et al.52. The Huwa San TR-50 preparation contains disinfectants and induces resistance to biotic and abiotic stresses52, thus contributing to increasing polyphenol contents. Plants with higher polyphenol content acclimatize more easily to unfavorable environmental conditions53,54. According to Saltveit55, the concentration of polyphenols is an indicator of the plant’s tolerance to abiotic and biotic stresses, due to their increased synthesis during various environmental hardships (drought, presence of heavy metals, salinity, high/low temperature or ultraviolet radiation). Polyphenols have the ability to scavenge free radicals and can act as antioxidants protecting the plant from oxidative stress. Oxidative stress causes changes in redox homeostasis in the cell due to excessive production of reactive oxygen species (ROS), which cause peroxidation of cell membranes, damage cells and negatively affect the plant’s immune system54,56.

Dressing tubers with chemicals is not the only way to limit the impact that unfavorable external factors have on a plant. The use of biostimulants or hydrogels is increasingly popular and profitable in plant cultivation41,57–60. Our study (experiment III) also indicates the great importance of the interventional irrigation of plantations in combination with other treatments (Table 2). The results show that using intervention irrigation alongside the Bioalgen S90 biostimulant was the most effective in stimulating the increase in polyphenolic compound contents (Table 2). Compared to the control treatment, where no simultaneous irrigation and biostimulant were used, 11.5% and 22.9% increases in the content of these compounds were observed for Gardena and Denar varieties, respectively (Table 2). These findings suggest a synergistic effect between controlled water supply and seaweed-based biostimulation. From an agronomic perspective, this combined strategy may be valuable for sustainable crop management, indicating that the effectiveness of biostimulants can be enhanced when integrated with optimized water management. The observed increases in polyphenolic content (up to 22.9% in the Denar cultivar) further support this interaction. The stronger response observed in Denar compared with Gardena may reflect cultivar-dependent differences in metabolic responsiveness, where genotypes with lower baseline polyphenol levels exhibit greater inducibility under optimized agronomic conditions. Only limited knowledge on the simultaneous use of intervention irrigation and biostimulants for potatoes is available. However, research to date indicates that the use of a biostimulant alone in cultivating has a positive effect on the quality of potato10,41,61,62. Biostimulants enable the plant to more efficiently absorb nutrients from the substrate during vegetation and, under unfavorable environmental conditions, stimulate the plant to synthesize nutrients. Research by many authors10,62 has noted an increase in polyphenolic compound contents after the use of biostimulants in the cultivation of potatoes. The increase in polyphenol content induced by biostimulation, especially in combination with irrigation, may be explained by the activation of plant defense mechanisms. Active compounds in biostimulants (e.g., oligosaccharides, amino acids, or phytohormones derived from algae) act as elicitors that enhance the activity of phenylalanine ammonia-lyase (PAL). This is a key enzyme responsible for the phenylpropanoid pathway, in which polyphenols are synthesized55,56,58. However, the effectiveness of biostimulants is related to the dose, type and frequency of use of the preparation63,64.

It was also shown that the simultaneous use of intervention irrigation and hydrogel significantly increased the content of total polyphenols compared to a control in which irrigation and hydrogel were not used simultaneously (Table 2). The total polyphenol content increased by 5.2 and 6.9 p.p. (for Gardena and Denar varieties, respectively), which was lower than in the case of combined use of irrigation and biostimulant (Table 2). Hydrogels are a type of cross-linked polymers that can absorb significant amounts of water (400 times their weight) while maintaining structural integrity. In addition, they contribute to the more even supply of water to plants. In this way, they ensure the provision of moisture to plants under unfavorable conditions plants and thus counteract water stress and improve their overall water management65. Yoursy et al.66 used hydrogel and irrigation in the cultivation of the potato variety Lady Balfour and obtained the best quality of potato tubers under the combined simultaneous use of irrigation and hydrogel.

Flavonoids and anthocyanins

After phenolic acids, flavonoids are the second-largest group of polyphenolic compounds67. The most important flavonoids found in potatoes include catechin, quercetin, kaempferol, rutin and anthocyanins8. These last are water-soluble polyphenolic pigments that impart color to colored parts of plants, thanks to the characteristic chromophores they contain7. In our study, regardless of experimental factors, the average content of flavonoids and anthocyanins was 1.362 g kg−1 of fresh matter and 2.517 mg kg−1 of fresh matter, respectively (Table 1). The content of these compounds in potato tubers may vary and ranges from 0.0 to 2.936 g kg−1 f.m. for flavonoids and from 0.0 to 296.0 mg kg−1 f.m. for anthocyanins8,68,69. The afore-cited authors also report that the content of these compounds varies largely by genotype and even among different parts of the tuber.

Our studies also showed significant inter-varietal differences in the content of these compounds. The flavonoid and anthocyanin contents, respectively, of 1.378 and 4.702 g kg−1 f.m. in the Gardena variety were higher than the 1.343 and 0.332 mg kg−1 f.m. in Denar (Table 1). The color of tuber flesh and skin is clearly related to total anthocyanin concentration68–72. The tested Gardena variety was characterized by pink skin and yellow flesh, whereas the Denar variety had light yellow skin and flesh. Jansen and Flame68 examined 27 varieties and 4 clones that differed in skin and flesh colour (purple, red, white) and obtained a much higher average anthocyanin content of 311 mg kg−1 f.m. than in our own study. It should be noted that the largest group of tested varieties was those with purple skin and flesh. Additionally, the authors reported that the content of anthocyanin pigments was higher in the skin than in the tuber as a whole. The skin was richer in pigment contents than the tuber by a factor of 2.1 in purple potatoes and 2.5 in red-skinned potatoes68. Moreover, purple- and red-fleshed potatoes are characterized by higher anthocyanin concentrations (21.0 − 73.0 mg kg−1 cyanidin) compared to yellow-fleshed varieties (0.0–0.150 mg kg−1 f.m.)70–72. Similarly as with polyphenols, differences in the content of flavonoids and anthocyanins were observed for both research years. The content of flavonoids and anthocyanins was 9.5% and 3.0% higher in 2020 compared to 2021, respectively (Table 1).

A major effect of N fertilization on the content of the tested compounds was also demonstrated (Flavonoids – 2020: Gardena - F = 12.26, p = 0.0012, ηp² = 0.458; Denar - F = 4.30, p = 0.0411, ηp² = 0.328; 2021: Gardena - F = 20.60, p = 0.0004, ηp² = 0.385, Denar - F = 11.64, p = 0.0008, ηp² = 0.322; Anthocyanins − 2020: Gardena - F = 14.75, p = 0.0012, ηp² = 0.346; Denar - F = 3.16, p = 0.023, ηp² = 0.3426; 2021: Gardena - F = 14.73, p = 0.0012, ηp² = 0.346, Denar - F = 3.27, p = 0.0320, ηp² = 0.287). The Gardena variety exhibited a significant increase in the content of flavonoids and anthocyanins when fertilized (experiment I) at doses of 100 and 150 kg N ha-11(Fig. 2b, c). The increases over control were 1.1 and 14.6% (100 kg) and 3.8 and 21.0% (150 kg), respectively. However, for the Denar variety, the only sizeable increase (of 15.6%) was in anthocyanin content after the application of a dose of 150 kg N ha−1, and that only in 2020 (Fig. 2c). The effect of N fertilization on the content of the tested compounds is not clear68,73. In a study by Jansen and Flame68, the applied nitrogen fertilization doses had no significant effect on the pigment content in potato tubers. Zhang et al.73 have a different finding, that nitrogen level is a key factor in the synthesis and accumulation of anthocyanins due to its regulating the transcription and expression levels of regulatory factors and related genes involved in the anthocyanin biosynthetic pathway. They concluded that low nitrogen levels up to 90 kg N ha−1 promote the accumulation of anthocyanins, while high nitrogen doses have the opposite effect. This divergence highlights that the threshold for nitrogen-induced suppression of secondary metabolism is highly cultivar-specific. In our study, the red-skinned Gardena variety demonstrated a robust capacity to leverage higher nitrogen inputs, with the dose yielding a substantial 21.0% increase in anthocyanins rather than triggering a downregulation. From a practical agronomic viewpoint, this indicates that for pigmented potato cultivars like Gardena, moderate-to-high nitrogen fertilization (within the range) does not compromise the functional value of the crop. Instead, it serves as an effective tool to maximize the expression of its genetic polyphenolic potential, contrasting with the sharper economic and biological trade-offs observed in standard yellow-fleshed varieties like Denar.

The protection variants used in our own research (experiment II), in 2020 and 2021 alike, were not associated with any statistically significant changes in flavonoid contents – neither for the Gardena variety nor for the Denar variety, (Fig. 3b). However, the use of full chemical protection with additional dressing of tubers generally considerably increased anthocyanin pigment contents in both study years, by an average of 24.9% for Gardena and 15.0% for Denar (Fig. 3c). This suggests that flavonoids are less sensitive to changes in protection conditions compared to other phenolic compounds. At the same time, our studies indicate that some components of chemical protection can induce the biosynthesis of anthocyanins or influence their mechanisms of accumulation in potato tubers. It should be assumed that plant protection chemicals act as stress factors or inducers of secondary plant metabolism, leading to increased production of phenolic compounds – mainly anthocyanins. This mechanism may be related to the activation of plant defense pathways in which anthocyanins play a protective role74,75.

Moreover, it was shown that the combined use of intervention irrigation and a biostimulant effected the greatest increase in flavonoid and anthocyanin pigment contents in the tested potato tubers, similarly as with increases in polyphenolic compounds (Table 2). It should be noted that the only considerable effect was obtained in the Gardena variety, and it was 17.4% for flavonoids and 5.1% for anthocyanins (Table 2). This suggests that improving water conditions during potato cultivation and stimulating plants with a biostimulant promote the synthesis and accumulation of these compounds, as reported by Godlewska76. The same authors report that better water management favors the optimization of photosynthesis and the transport of precursors for the synthesis of pigments to the tubers. However, the presence of active substances contained in biostimulants may act as signaling factors that induce the expression of genes responsible for the synthesis of phenolic compounds. The strength of this response, however, depends on genetic traits, which indicates sizeable differences between the varieties in our own studies.

Antioxidant potential

Antioxidant capacity is known to be an important indicator of the health-promoting quality of potatoes36,62. The antioxidant capacity of potato tubers measured by the FRAP method in general (experiments I, II, III) was 7.12 mmol Fe2+ kg˗1 f.m. (Table 1). The literature contains studies showing large variability between the genetic conditions of potatoes and environmental influences on their antioxidant capacity38,77,78. The antioxidant potential of the Gardena variety averaged 7.76 mmol Fe2+ kg˗1 f.m., and for the Denar variety it was 6.47 mmol Fe2+ kg˗1 f.m. Tatarowska et al.77 evaluated 65 potato varieties and found that the antioxidant capacity significantly depends on the genotype, ranging from 296.0 mg 100 g (d.m.) (TE – Trolox Equivalent). In research by Keutgen et al.78, the antioxidant potential ranges from 13.0 to 17.8 mmol Fe2+ kg−1 d.m., and Hu et al.38 report a range from 6 to 64 mmol AAE kg−1(AAE – Ascorbic Acid Equivalent).

It was shown that the tested factors had broadly the same effect on antioxidant capacity as on polyphenol content (Figs. 2a and d and 3a and d; Table 2). Antioxidant capacity was found to be highest in each experimental plot under the following conditions: experiment I: a fertilization dose of 150 kg ha−1(8.2 and 6.9 mmol Fe2+ kg˗1 d.m. for Gardena and Denar cultivars, respectively); experiment II: full protection with tuber dressing (8.0 and 6.5 mmol Fe2+ kg˗1 d.m. for Gardena and Denar cultivars, respectively); and experiment III: intervention irrigation combined with a biostimulator (7.9 and 7.1 mmol Fe2+ kg−1 d.m. for Gardena and Denar cultivars, respectively). Compared to the control plot, the respective increases in the tested parameter were 10.4%, 9.1% and 11.1% (Figs. 2a and d and 3a and d; Table 2). Many studies indicate that the antioxidant potential of potato tubers is influenced by the content of total polyphenols, the most important of which are flavonoids77,78. According to Reddivari et al.79, the content of polyphenolic compounds in potato tubers is responsible for approximately 58.0–82.0% of total antioxidant activity. The increase in antioxidant potential may result from the action of the biostimulant, which improves water and mineral nutrient use efficiency, thereby enabling the plant to more effectively synthesize secondary metabolites that perform antioxidant functions69,72,79. This is also confirmed by the high correlation coefficients at the level of p < 0.01 in our study. Table 3 present the relationships between the study parameters. For each of the three experimental plots, statistically significant large positive correlations were found, as follows. In experiment I, for antioxidant capacity and polyphenol content were demonstrated for the Gardena variety in 2020 (r = 0.788, p < 0.01) and the Denar variety in 2020 and 2021 (r = 0.928 and r = 0.829, respectively, p < 0.01). In experiment II, for the Denar variety, antioxidant capacity correlated with polyphenol and flavonoid contents at the level of r = 0.813 and r = 0.883 (p < 0.01) in 2020 and with polyphenols at r = 0.739 (p < 0.01) in 2021. Finally, in experiment III, a strong correlation was noted between all parameters for the Gardena variety in both 2020 and 2021.

Conclusions

The content of bioactive compounds and the antioxidant capacity (FRAP) of potato tubers were influenced by genotype. The Gardena cultivar (red-skinned, yellow-fleshed) showed higher levels of bioactive compounds compared with the other cultivar evaluated in this study.

Phenolic compound content generally increased with nitrogen fertilization, with the highest values observed at 150 kg N ha⁻¹. Full chemical protection combined with tuber treatment using a preparation containing silver nanoparticles was associated with increased phenolic content, suggesting that resistance-inducing treatments may affect the antioxidant potential of potato tubers. Interventional irrigation, particularly when combined with the Bioalgen S90 biostimulant, promoted the accumulation of polyphenolic compounds, including anthocyanins. Among the anti-stress treatments evaluated, the biostimulant showed a more pronounced effect than the hydrogel on polyphenol content and FRAP antioxidant capacity.

The results obtained over the two-year field experiment indicate that the evaluated cultivation practices may influence the accumulation of selected bioactive compounds in potato tubers. However, since varietal responses differed and environmental conditions vary between growing seasons and locations, further multi-year studies are required to confirm the stability and general applicability of these findings and to better understand the mechanisms involved in the biosynthesis of bioactive compounds in potato tubers.

Under the experimental conditions of this study, the integration of optimized fertilization with mineral nitrogen, anti-stress treatments, and irrigation management was associated with changes in the nutritional and functional-related quality parameters of potato tubers.

Materials and methods

Materials

The experiment was conducted on two edible potato varieties: Gardena and Denar. The characteristics of the Gardena and Denar varieties are described in our earlier report10. All procedures with plants were conducted in accordance with the guidelines and regulations.

Treatment

Three parallel field experiments were conducted (in 2020 and 2021) in the Masovian Voivodeship at the station of the Plant Breeding and Acclimatization Institute (Instytut Hodowli i Aklimatyzacji Roślin – IHAR) Station in Jadwisin (52°47’N, 21°04’E) in central Poland. The soil and weather conditions during field experiments in Jadwisin were described by Wszelaczyńska et al.10 and Trawczyński et al.80. Field tests were performed in triplicate and laboratory analyses in duplicate. It was determined what effect on the content of health-promoting compounds and antioxidant capacity (FRAP) in tubers was provided by: mineral nitrogen fertilization (experiment I); the use of chemical protection against fungal diseases (experiment II); and the use of treatments preventing plant stress during potato cultivation (experiment III).

Experiment I – for a detailed description of the experiment, see the study by Trwczyński et al.80.

Experiment II – the effect of differentiated chemical protection against fungal diseases of potatoes of the Gardena and Denar varieties was analyzed. In this respect, the following study variables were specified: no fungicides (control); application of one fungicide in the form of Ridomil Gold MZ Pepite 67.8 WG – 2.5 kg ha−1 in BBCH 50 (protection 1); full protection against fungal diseases according to Wszelaczyńska et al.10 (protection 2); full protection against fungal diseases including dressing tubers with Huwa San TR 50 nano-preparation (0.5% solution for tuber dressing) (protection 3). The potatoes were planted in the 3rd ten-day period of April at a spacing of 0.75 × 0.33 m, and harvested in the 3rd ten-day period of September (cv. Denar—20.09.2020 and 21.09.2021; cv. Gardena—29.09.2020 and 30.09.2021). The experimental design was a split-split-plot (2–2–3), randomized block with four replications and a single plot size of 15 m2.

Experiment III – for a detailed description of the experiment, see the work by Wszelaczyńska et al.10.

Plant sampling and laboratory analyses

Sample were prepared for the study according to Wszelaczyńska et al.10 and the chemical composition of tubers was extracted according to the procedure described by Gościnna et al.81. Physicochemical analyses were carried out at the Food Processing Laboratory, Faculty of Agriculture and Biotechnology, Bydgoszcz University of Technology in Bydgoszcz (Food Processing Laboratory, Faculty of Agriculture and Biotechnology, Bydgoszcz University of Technology in Bydgoszcz). The spectrophotometric method using a Folin–Ciocâlteu reagent was used to determine total polyphenol content82. The assay procedure is described by Pobereżny et al.83. Total polyphenolic content was determined based on a calibration curve prepared for Gallic acid equivalents (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Flavonoids were determined using the spectrophotometric method described by Papoutsis et al.84. Results are expressed as mg of quercetin equivalents (Sigma-Aldrich, St. Louis, MO, USA). Total anthocyanin content was measured using the method of Cheng and Breen85. Anthocyanin content was calculated using the formula (A×M×R)×100/ε, where A = (A510–A800)pH1.0 – (A510–A800)pH4.5; M = 449.2 (molar mass); R = sample dilution factor; and ε = 26,900 (molar absorption coefficient for cyanidin-3-glucoside). Results are expressed as mg of cyanidin-3-glucoside equivalents. Antioxidant capacity was determined by FRAP method using the method developed by Gościnna et al.81 after Benzie and Strain86. Antioxidant activity was expressed as the ability to reduce Fe3+ iron ions to Fe2+. The concentration of Fe2+ ions in the sample was calculated based on the standard curve prepared for known concentrations of FeSO4 solution.

Statistical analysis

The obtained data were subjected to statistical analysis using Statistica 13.1 software (StatSoft, USA). Prior to the analysis, the normality of data distribution was verified using the Shapiro–Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was assessed using Levene’s test. To avoid confounding effects, statistical evaluations were performed separately for each vegetative year and each potato cultivar. The data originating from the three independent field experiments were analyzed using distinct analysis of variance (ANOVA) models tailored to their factor structures:

Experiment 1: Analyzed using a one-way ANOVA model to evaluate the main effect of nitrogen levels.

Experiment 2: Analyzed using a factorial two-way ANOVA model, formally testing the main effects of irrigation, biostimulant application, and their interaction effect.

Experiment 3: Analyzed using a one-way ANOVA model to evaluate the main effect of protection variants.

To handle the problem of multiple comparisons and control the family-wise error rate, treatment means within each model were separated using Tukey’s honest significant difference (HSD) post-hoc test at p ≤ 0.05. Additionally, regression analysis and Spearman’s rank correlation coefficients were used to evaluate relationships between the investigated variables. Results are presented as mean values ± standard deviation (SD).
