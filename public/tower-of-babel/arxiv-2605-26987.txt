Meromorphic functions bi-weighted weakly sharing pairs of small functions
Si Duc Quang 1.2, Phung Nguyen Ngoc Anh 1

Abstract
. Two meromorphic functions f f and g g are said to weakly share a small function a a with bi-weight ( n , k ) (n,k) if the functions f − a f-a and g − a g-a have the same zeros with multiplicities truncated at level n + 1 n+1 , while zeros whose multiplicities exceed k k are disregarded. In this article, we show that if f f and g g weakly share three distinct small functions with suitable bi-weights and are not related by a quasi-Möbius transformation, then for every other small function c c , the counting function N ⁡ ( r , ν f c ) N(r,\nu_{f}^{c}) is asymptotically equivalent to the characteristic function T ⁡ ( r , f ) T(r,f) . Moreover, the truncated counting function N ( 3 CLOSE ​ ( r , ν f c ) N_{(3}(r,\nu_{f}^{c}) , which counts only zeros of multiplicity at least 3 3 , is negligible. As an application, we further prove that f f and g g must be related by a quasi-Möbius transformation provided that they satisfy an additional condition, which is weaker than the usual assumption that they share a fourth pair of small functions.

Introduction

In 1926, Nevanlinna proved that two nonconstant meromorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}
must be Möbius transformations of each other if they share four distinct fixed values CM, that is, if they have the same inverse images of these values counting multiplicities. Since then, Nevanlinna’s theorem has been substantially generalized in several directions, including the replacement of fixed values by small functions or pairs of small functions, as well as the weakening of the CM sharing condition. Many remarkable results in this area have been obtained through the contributions of numerous authors, including Czubiak-Gundersen
[
3
]
, Li-Yang
[
5
,
6
]
, Quang-Quynh
[
10
,

[

]
, Zhang-Yan
[

]
, and others.

We now recall a theorem of Li and Yang from
[
6
]
, which is among the most elegant results currently available in this direction. First, we introduce the following notation and definitions.

In this article, all meromorphic functions are defined on
\mathbb{C}
. A divisor
\nu
on
{\mathbb{C}}
is a function from
{\mathbb{C}}
into
\mathbb{Z}
so that the set
\overline{\{z|\nu(z)\neq 0\}}
is a discrete set. For each positive integer
k
, we define

\nu_{\leq n}(z)=\begin{cases}\nu(z)&\text{ if }\nu(z)<n,\\
n&\text{ if }\nu(z)\geq n.\end{cases}

We say that a meromorphic function
a
is small with respect to a meromorphic function
f
if their characteristic function satisfy
\|\ T(r,a)=o(T(r,f))
(see Section 2 for the definition), where the notation “
\|\ P
” means the claim
P
holds for all
r\in(0,+\infty)
outside of a Borel subset of finite measure. For a subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}}
, we define

E^{S}_{n}(a,f)=\left\{\bigl(z,\nu^{a}_{f,\leq n+1}(z)\bigl)\bigl|z\not\in S\right\}.

Here, by
\nu^{a}_{f}
we denote the zero divisor of the function
f-a
. We will omit the character
S
(resp.
n
) in
E^{S}_{n}(a,f)
if
S=\emptyset
(resp.
n=+\infty
).

We say that two meromorphic functions
f
and
g
weakly share a pair of meromorphic functions
(a,b)
with the weight
n
if

E^{S}_{n}(a,f)=E^{S}_{n}(b,g),

for a discrete subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}}
with
\|N(r,\nu_{S})=o(T(r,f)+T(r,g))
, where the divisor
\nu_{S}
coincides with the characteristic function of the subset
S
on
{\mathbb{C}}
(see Section 2 for the definition of the counting function of a divisor
N(r,\nu)
). If
n=+\infty
(resp.
n=1
) then we say that
f
and
g
weakly share the pair
(a,b)
counting multiplicity (resp. ignoring multiplicity).

The function
f
is said to be a quasi-Möbius transformation of

if there exist four small functions
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3},\alpha_{4}
of
g
with
\alpha_{1}\alpha_{4}-\alpha_{2}\alpha_{3}\not\equiv 0
such that

f=\dfrac{\alpha_{1}g+\alpha_{2}}{\alpha_{3}g+\alpha_{4}}.

If all
\alpha_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq 4)
are constant then we say that
f
is a Möbius transformation of
g
. The result of Li and Yang is stated as follows.

Theorem A
(See
[
6
, Theorem 1]
).
Let
f
and
g
be non-constant meromorphic functions and
a_{i},b_{i}\ (i=1,2,3,4)\ (a_{i}\neq a_{j},b_{i}\neq b_{j},i\neq j)
be small functions (with respect to

and

and

weakly share three pairs
(a_{i},b_{i}),\ (i=1,2,3)
counting multiplicity, and weakly share the fourth pair
(a_{4},b_{4})
ignoring multiplicity, then

g
.

[

\displaystyle(n_{2}+1)\overline{N}_{(n_{3}+1,k)}(r,\nu^{a_{3}}_{f})

\displaystyle+\overline{N}_{(n_{1}+1,k)}(r,\nu^{a_{1}}_{f})+\dfrac{2n_{2}+2}{k+1}T(r)+S(r).

\displaystyle(n_{1}n_{2}-1)\overline{N}_{(n_{1}+1,k)}(r,\nu^{a_{1}}_{f})\leq(n_{2}+1)\left(\overline{N}_{(n_{3}+1,k)}(r,\nu^{a_{3}}_{f})+\dfrac{2}{k+1}T(r)\right)+S(r).

The lemma is proved.

Lemma 2.8
.

Let

\displaystyle+o(\sum_{i=1}^{n}T(r,f_{i}))+O(T(r,a)+T(r,b)).

(2.9)

\displaystyle\|\ T(r,f)\leq\sum_{0\leq i\leq 2}N(r,\nu^{0}_{hf_{i}})+N(r,\nu^{0}_{\sum_{i=0}^{2}hf_{i}})-N(r,\nu^{0}_{W(\tilde{f})})+o(T(r,f)).

.

. For

\displaystyle\nu(z)

\displaystyle\leq\nu^{0}_{hf_{2}}(z)+\nu^{0}_{h}(z)-\nu^{0}_{\det\left((hf_{i})^{(j)};1\leq i\leq 3,0\leq j\leq 2\right)}(z)\ \ \ \ \ \text{ [where $f_{3}=1$]}

\displaystyle\leq\nu^{0}_{hf_{2}}(z)+\nu^{0}_{h}(z)-\min_{0\leq s,t\leq 2,s\neq t}\{\nu^{0}_{(hf_{2})^{(s)}}(z)+\nu^{0}_{(hf_{3})^{(t)}}(z)\}

), we have

\displaystyle\|\ \Delta\sum_{i=1}^{3}N(r,\mu_{i})

\displaystyle\leq\Delta\sum_{i=1}^{3}\left(\overline{N}_{(n_{i}+1,k)}(r,\nu^{a_{i}}_{f})+\overline{N}_{(k+1}(r,\nu^{a_{i}}_{f})+\overline{N}_{(k+1}(r,\nu^{b_{i}}_{g})\right)+S(r)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)+3\Delta}{k+1}T(r)+S(r).

Also, by the main theorem, we have

\|\ N(r,\mu_{4})\leq N_{(3}(r,\nu^{a_{4}}_{f})+N_{(3}(r,\nu^{b_{4}}_{g})+S(r)\leq 2\left(\frac{2\lambda_{n}}{k+1}+\frac{6}{n+2}\right)T(r)+S(r),

where

\lambda_{n}=2^{n^{2}+2n+3}\left(\frac{2\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{3}{k+1}\right)

for a positive integer
n
(chosen later). Combining the above two inequalities, we obtain

(3.8)

\displaystyle\begin{split}4&\sum_{i=1}^{3}N(r,\mu_{i})+N(r,\mu_{4})\\
&\leq\left(\frac{8\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)+12\Delta}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{4\lambda_{n}}{k+1}+\frac{12}{n+2}\right)T(r)+S(r).\end{split}

We write
f=\frac{f_{0}}{f_{1}}
(resp.
g=\frac{g_{0}}{g_{1}}
), where
f_{0},f_{1}
(resp.
g_{0},g_{1}
) are holomorphic function without common zeros. Take meromorphic functions
h_{1},h_{2},h_{3},h_{4}
such that

f_{0}=h_{1}g_{0};f_{1}=h_{2}g_{1};f_{0}-f_{1}=h_{3}(g_{0}-g_{1});f_{0}-af_{1}=h_{4}(g_{0}-bg_{1}).

It is easy to see that

\nu^{0}_{h_{i}}+\nu^{\infty}_{h_{i}}=|\nu^{a_{i}}_{f}-\nu^{b_{i}}_{g}|\ (1\leq i\leq 4)

outside a discrete subset
S
of
{\mathbb{C}}
with
\|\ N(r,\nu_{S})=S(r)
.
From the definition of functions
h_{i}\ (1\leq i\leq 4)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{det}\left(\begin{array}[]{cccc}1&0&-h_{1}&0\\
0&1&0&-h_{2}\\
1&-1&-h_{3}&h_{3}\\
1&-a&-h_{4}&bh_{4}\end{array}\right)=0.

Then

\displaystyle(1-a)h_{1}h_{2}-h_{1}h_{3}+bh_{1}h_{4}+ah_{2}h_{3}-h_{2}h_{4}+(1-b)h_{3}h_{4}=0.

We define the following functions:

\displaystyle h_{1,2}

\displaystyle=(1-a)h_{1}h_{2},\ h_{1,3}=-h_{1}h_{3},\ h_{1,4}=bh_{1}h_{4},

\displaystyle h_{2,3}

\displaystyle=ah_{2}h_{3},\ h_{2,4}=-h_{2}h_{4},\ h_{3,4}=(1-b)h_{3}h_{4}.

Then we have

\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}h_{i,j}=0.

Let
d
be a meromorphic function on
{\mathbb{C}}
such that
dh_{i,j}\ (1\leq i<j\leq 4)
are all holomorphic functions on
{\mathbb{C}}
without common zero. Then it is easy to see that

\displaystyle\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 4}N^{[4]}(r,dh_{i,j})\leq

\displaystyle 3\sum_{i=1}^{4}(N^{[4]}(r,\nu^{0}_{h_{i}})+N^{[4]}(r,\nu^{\infty}_{h_{i}}))+S(r)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 12\sum_{i=1}^{3}N(r,\mu_{i})+3N(r,\mu_{4})+S(r).

Let
\mathcal{I}=\{(i,j)|1\leq i<j\leq 4\}
. For
I=(i,j)\in\mathcal{I}
, we denote
h_{i,j}
by
h_{I}
.
Take
I_{0}\in\mathcal{I}
arbitrarily. Then

dh_{I_{0}}=-\sum_{I\neq I_{0}}dh_{I}.

Denote by
t
the minimum number satisfying the following: There exist
t
elements
I_{1}
,…,
I_{t}\in\mathcal{I}
and
t
nonzero constants
b_{v}\in{\mathbb{C}}\ (1\leq v\leq t)
such that
dh_{I_{0}}=\sum_{v=1}^{t}b_{v}dh_{I_{v}}.

By the minimality of
t
, the family
\{dh_{I_{1}},...,dh_{I_{t}}\}
is linearly independent over
{\mathbb{C}}
.

Suppose that
t\geq 2
. Consider the linearly non-degenerate holomorphic mapping
h:{\mathbb{C}}\to{\mathbb{P}}^{t-1}({\mathbb{C}})
with the representation
h=(dh_{I_{1}}:...:dh_{I_{t}})
. By the second main theorem in Nevanlinna theory for hyperplanes, we have

(3.9)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\ T_{h}(r)&\leq\sum_{v=1}^{t}N^{[t-1]}_{dh_{I_{v}}}(r)+N_{dh_{I_{0}}}^{[t-1]}(r)+S(r)\\
&\leq\sum_{v=1}^{6}N^{[4]}_{dh_{I_{v}}}(r)+S(r)\ \ [\text{since $t\leq 5$}]\\
&\leq 12\sum_{i=1}^{3}N(r,\mu_{i})+3N(r,\mu_{4})+S(r).\end{split}

On the other hand, there exists two element, say
I_{i}
and
I_{j}
, such that
4\not\in(I_{i}\setminus I_{j})\cup(I_{j}\setminus I_{i})
. Therefore, by the main theorem we have

(3.10)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\|\ T_{h}(r)&\geq T(r,\frac{h_{I_{i}}}{h_{I_{j}}})+S(r)=T(r,\frac{h_{I_{i}}}{h_{I_{j}}}-\frac{\tilde{h}_{I_{i}}}{\tilde{h}_{I_{j}}})+S(r)\\
&\geq N(r,\min\{\nu^{0}_{f-a},\nu^{0}_{g-b}\})+S(r)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(N(r,\nu^{a}_{f})+N(r,\nu^{a}_{f})-N_{(3}(r,\nu^{a}_{f})-N_{(3}(r,\nu^{a}_{f})\right)+S(r)\\
&\geq\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{6\lambda_{n}}{k+1}-\frac{16}{n+2}\right)T(r)+S(r),\end{split}

where
\tilde{h}_{I_{i}},\tilde{h}_{I_{j}}
are obtained from
h_{I_{i}},h_{I_{j}}
by substituting
a,b
into
f,g
respectively.
From (
3.8
), (
3.9
) and (
3.10
), we have

\displaystyle\biggl\|\ \frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{6\lambda_{n}}{k+1}-\frac{16}{n+2}\right)T(r)

\displaystyle\leq 3\left(\frac{8\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)+12\Delta}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{4\lambda_{n}}{k+1}+\frac{12}{n+2}\right)T(r)+S(r).

Letting
r\longrightarrow+\infty
, we get

1\leq\frac{48\sum_{1\leq i<j\leq 3}(n_{i}+1)(n_{j}+1)+72\Delta}{\Delta(k+1)}+\frac{30\lambda_{n}}{k+1}+\frac{88}{n+2}.

This is a contradiction.

Therefore, we must have
t=1
, i.e., there is
I_{1}\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{I_{0}\}
such that
\dfrac{h_{I_{0}}}{h_{I_{1}}}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Hence, for every
I\in\mathcal{J}
, there exists
J\in\mathcal{I}\setminus\{I\}
such that
\dfrac{h_{I}}{h_{J}}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. We consider the following three cases:

Case a:
There are
I=(s,t),J=(s,\ell)
such that
\dfrac{h_{I}}{h_{J}}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Then
h_{t}=\alpha h_{\ell}
with a small meromorphic function (w.r.t
f
and
g
)
\alpha
. Therefore,
f
is a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
. This contradicts the supposition.

Case b:
There are
I=(t,s),J=(\ell,s)
such that
\dfrac{h_{I}}{h_{J}}\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus\{0\}
. Similarly as the above case,
f
is a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
and we arrive at the contradiction.

Case c:
There are small functions
\alpha,\beta
such that
h_{1,2}=\alpha h_{3,4}\text{ and }h_{1,3}=\beta h_{2,4}.

Then
\left(\dfrac{h_{1}}{h_{4}}\right)^{2}
is a small function, and so is
\dfrac{h_{1}}{h_{4}}
. Hence
f
is a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
. This is a contradiction.

From the above two cases, we always get a contradiction. Hence
f
is a quasi-Möbius transformation of
g
.
∎
