Random interlacements on transient weighted graphs: 0-1 laws and FKG inequality
Orphée Collin

Abstract
We study some properties of the random interlacement model on a transient weighted graph, which was introduced by A. Teixeira in [ 7 ] . We give a simple proof of the FKG-property and discuss the occurrence of several 0-1 laws for non-local events. We show in particular a 0-1 law for some increasing non-local events, without any assumption. AMS subject classifications (2010 MSC): 60K35, 82B43, 60F20, 60J10. Keywords: random interlacement model, percolation theory, 0-1 laws This work is licensed under a Creative Commons Attribution — 4.0 International licence (CC-BY 4.0, https://creativecommons.org/licenses/by/4.0/).

1
Overview

We consider an irreducible and reversible Markov chain
(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a countable state space. We use the framework of electrical networks: we consider a simple undirected graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathcal{E})
and a collection of positive weights (or conductances)
\mathbf{a}=(a_{x,y})_{(x,y)\in E}
(with
a_{x,y}=a_{y,x}
), and define on
\mathbb{V}
the Markov chain
X=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
by the following transition probabilities: with
a_{x}:=\sum_{z\sim x}a_{x,z}
,

p_{x,y}:=\begin{cases}\frac{a_{x,y}}{a_{x}},&\text{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{E},\\
0&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

(1)

We furthermore assume that
\mathcal{G}
is connected and locally finite, and that
X
is transient.

Following the work of A. Teixeira in
[
7
]
(generalizing the construction made in
[
6
]
for the SRW in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
in transient dimensions), we consider the random interlacement model built upon this transient weighted graph. The model consists of a random collection of biinfinite trajectories of
X
, which we will refer to as the
interlacement process
: it is indeed a (Poisson) point process on the space of transient nearest-neighbor trajectories on
\mathcal{G}
.
It is in particular interesting to focus on the
interlacement set

{\mathcal{I}}
, defined as the union of the ranges of all the trajectories. This random subset
{\mathcal{I}}
of
\mathbb{V}
can be viewed as a dependent percolation set. It is caracterised by the following property: for every finite subset
K
of
\mathbb{V}
,

\mathbb{P}({\mathcal{I}}\cap K=\emptyset)=\exp(-\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)),

(2)

where
\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)
denotes the capacity of
K
with respect to
X
.

In
[
7
]
, it was shown that the interlacement set
{\mathcal{I}}
satifies the FKG inequality. Here, we point out that this relies in a direct way on the poissonian nature of the interlacement process, and we slightly extend the scope of the FKG inequality.

Next, we study the occurence of a 0-1 law for this model. Contrarily to the case of the SRW on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},d\geq 3
, there is no natural candidate for an ergodic set of measure-preserving transformations. Our approach to the 0-1 law rather relies on
non-local
events, that is, events that
do not depend on the configuration inside any finite region
(in a sense that will be defined).

We establish this 0-1 law if
X
is tail-trivial - that is, when the
\sigma
-algebra of invariant events is trivial with respect to
X
. We also give a criterion for the occurence of the 0-1 law when this
\sigma
-algebra is purely atomic.
We further provide a quantitative criterion for the validity of the 0-1 law.
Additionally, we show that a weaker 0-1 law, concerning events that rely only on one tail of the trajectories, holds in full generality.
We finally show a 0-1 law for increasing events that are non-local in a slightly stronger sense.

2
The model

2.1
Some notations

As announced, we consider a simple undirected connected and locally finite graph
\mathcal{G}=(\mathbb{V},\mathcal{E})
. In the sequel, subsets of
\mathbb{V}
will be refered to as “vertex sets”.
We denote by
\mathcal{P}(\mathbb{V})
the set of all vertex sets, and by
\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V})
the set of finite vertex sets. They are endowed with the inclusion order. An exhaustion of
\mathbb{V}
is an increasing sequence
(K_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of finite subsets of
\mathbb{V}
such that
\bigcup_{n\geq 0}K_{n}=\mathbb{V}
.
For
f:P_{f}(\mathbb{V})\to{\mathbb{R}}
and
l\in{\mathbb{R}}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}
,
we say that
f
converges towards
l
,
and write
f(K)\underset{K\to\mathbb{V}}{\longrightarrow}l
, or
\lim_{K\to\mathbb{V}}f(K)=l
,
if for every exhaustion
(K_{n})_{n\geq 0}
of
\mathbb{V}
,
f(K_{n})\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}l
.
We observe that if
f
is monotonous, then
\lim_{K\to\mathbb{V}}f(K)
necessarily exists.

Furthermore, we consider a family
\mathbf{a}=(a_{x,y})_{(x,y)\in\mathcal{E}}
of positive conductances on the graph
\mathcal{G}
and the induced Markov chain
X=(X_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on
\mathbb{V}
, having the following transition probabilities:

p_{x,y}:=\begin{cases}\frac{a_{x,y}}{a_{x}},&\text{ if }(x,y)\in\mathcal{E},\\
0&\text{ else}.\end{cases}

(3)

with
a_{x}:=\sum_{z\sim x}a_{x,z}
.
For every vertex
x
, let
P_{x}
denote the law of
X
started from
x
and
E_{x}
the associated expectation.
We observe that
(a_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{V}}
is an invariant measure for
X
. Throughout the article, we work under the assumption that
X
is transient.
For every vertex set
K
, we define the two following stopping times:

\displaystyle\tau_{K}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\geq 0:X_{n}\in K\}\qquad\text{ (hitting time of }K),

(4)

\displaystyle\tau^{+}_{K}

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\geq 1:X_{n}\in K\}\qquad\text{ (return time to }K),

as well as the random time:

\lambda_{K}:=\sup\{n\geq 0:X_{n}\in K\}\qquad\text{ (time of the last visit in }K),

We use the conventions
\inf\emptyset=\infty
and
\sup\emptyset=-\infty
.
We also define the inner escape boundary of
K
:

\partial_{X}K:=\{x\in K:P_{x}[\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]>0\}.

(5)

We observe that, by transience, for any
x\in K
,
P_{x}
-almost surely,
\lambda_{K}<\infty
and
X_{\lambda_{K}}\in\partial_{X}K
.

We now introduce the notions of equilibrium measure, capacity and harmonic measure of a finite vertex set
K
. The equilibrium measure is the following measure on
\mathbb{V}
, supported on
\partial_{X}K
:

e_{K}(x):=\begin{cases}a_{x}P_{x}[\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]&\text{if }x\in K,\\
0&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

(6)

The total mass of
e_{K}
is called the capacity of
K
and denoted:

\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K):=e_{K}(\mathbb{V})=\sum_{x\in K}a_{x}P_{x}[\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty].

(7)

Finally, the harmonic measure of
K
(or normalized equilibrium measure) is:

\textrm{harm}_{K}(\cdot):=\frac{e_{K}(\cdot)}{\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)}\,.

(8)

Crucial to us is the following property, which derives elementarily from the reversibility of
X
.
For every finite vertex sets
K
and
L
with
K\subset L
, we have

\sum_{x\in L}e_{L}(x)P_{x}[X_{\tau_{K}}\in\cdot,\tau_{K}<\infty]=e_{K}(\cdot),

(9)

so that, denoting
P_{\textrm{harm}_{L}}(\cdot)=\sum_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\textrm{harm}_{L}(x)P_{x}(\cdot)
, we have

P_{\textrm{harm}_{L}}[\tau_{K}<\infty]=\frac{\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)}{\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(L)}

(10)

and

P_{\textrm{harm}_{L}}[X_{\tau_{K}}\in\cdot|\tau_{K}<\infty]=\textrm{harm}_{K}(\cdot).

(11)

2.2
Interlacement process

We now present the construction of the interlacement process, refering to
[
7
]
for details.

We consider the set of transient nearest-neighbor trajectories in
\mathcal{G}
indexed by
{\mathbb{N}}\cup\{0\}
(respectively, by
{\mathbb{Z}}
):

\displaystyle W^{+}=\{w=(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}:

\displaystyle\forall n\geq 0,w_{n}\sim w_{n+1}\text{ and }

(12)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{V},\{n\geq 0:w_{n}=x\}\text{ is finite}\},

\displaystyle W=\{w=(w_{n})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}:

\displaystyle\forall n\in{\mathbb{Z}},w_{n}\sim w_{n+1}\text{ and }

(13)

\displaystyle\forall x\in\mathbb{V},\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:w_{n}=x\}\text{ is finite}\}.

We endow
W^{+}
(respectively,
W
) with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{W^{+}}
(respectively,
\mathcal{W}
) generated by the coordinate maps
w\mapsto w_{n}
, for
n\geq 0
(respectively, for
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}
). Note that the laws
P_{x},x\in\mathbb{V}
, are probability measures on
(W^{+},\mathcal{W}^{+})
.

For every finite vertex set
K
, we denote by
W_{K}
the set of trajectories in
W
that hit
K
and we consider the measure
\nu_{K}
on
(W,\mathcal{W})
caracterized by:

\nu_{K}\left(\left\{w\in W:(w_{-n})_{n\geq 0}\in A,w_{0}=x,(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}\right\}\right)=P_{x}[A|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]e_{K}(x)P_{x}[B],

(14)

for every
x\in\mathbb{V}
and
A,B\in\mathcal{W^{+}}
. This measure has total mass
\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)
and is supported by the subset of
W_{K}
consisting of the trajectories that enter in
K
at time
0
.

We may then consider a Poisson point process with intensity measure
\nu_{K}
. For clarity, let us describe how this Poisson point process can be sampled:

•

sample a number
\xi
as a Poisson variable of parameter
\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)
,

•

independently for each
i=1,\dots,\xi
, sample a point
x_{i}
under
\textrm{harm}_{K}
,

•

independently for each
i=1,\dots,\xi
, attach to
x_{i}
a trajectory that enters in
K
through
x
, at time 0, as follows: independently,

–

for the negative indices, sample a trajectory under
P_{x}[\cdot|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]
and apply the function
n\mapsto-n
to the indices,

–

for the positive indices, sample a trajectory under
P_{x}[\cdot]
.

Now, let
K\subset L
be two finite vertex sets. Consider the Poisson point process with intensity measure
\nu_{L}
. Restrict it to trajectories that hit
K
, and reindex these trajectories so that they enter
K
at time 0. It follows from (
9
) that the obtained point process is Poisson, with intensity
\nu_{K}
; see Theorem 2.1 in
[
7
]
for details.

This consistency property allows us to extend the construction to the whole of
\mathcal{G}
: this gives the random interlacement model on
(\mathcal{G},\mathbf{a})
. Let us construct it formally. To begin with, due to the reindexation in the consistency property, we choose to look at trajectories in
W
up to time-translation. We define the equivalence relation:

w\sim w^{\prime}\quad\text{ if and only if }\quad\exists k\in{\mathbb{Z}},(w_{n+k})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}=(w_{n}^{\prime})_{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}}.

(15)

Denote by
W^{*}
the set of equivalence classes of
W
under this equivalence relation and by
\pi^{*}:W\to W^{*}
the associated projection. Denote by
\mathcal{W}^{*}
the
\sigma
-algebra on
W^{*}
inherited from
\mathcal{W}
. For each finite vertex set
K
, denote
W_{K}^{*}=\pi^{*}(W_{K})
.

Consider now the measure
\nu
on
(W^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*})
caracterised by the fact that for any finite vertex set
K
, the measure
\nu{\mathbf{1}}_{W_{K}^{*}}
, i.e, the restriction of
\nu
to trajectories (seen up to time-shift) that hit
K
, is equal to
\nu_{K}\circ(\pi^{*})^{-1}
.
One way to construct the measure
\nu
is to fix an exhaustion
(K_{n})_{n\geq 0}
with
K_{0}=\emptyset
and set

\nu=\left(\sum_{n\geq 1}\nu_{K_{n}}{\mathbf{1}}_{W_{K_{n}}\setminus W_{K_{n-1}}}\right)\circ(\pi^{*})^{-1}.

Then we consider a Poisson point process
(W^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*})
with intensity measure
\nu
. Formally, we define the space:

\Omega=\left\{\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}},w_{i}^{*}\in W^{*}:\,\forall K\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V}),\,\omega(W_{K}^{*})<\infty\right\}.

(16)

We endow
\Omega
with the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
generated by the maps
\omega\mapsto\omega(D)
for
D\in\mathcal{W}^{*}

and we consider on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
the law
\mathbb{P}
of a Poisson point process on
(W^{*},\mathcal{W}^{*})
with intensity measure
\nu
. We will refer to
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
(under
\mathbb{P}
) as the interlacement process, while

{\mathcal{I}}:=\bigcup_{i\geq 1}\text{Range}(w_{i}^{*}),\qquad{\mathcal{V}}=\mathbb{V}\setminus{\mathcal{I}}

(17)

(with obvious definition for
\text{Range}(w_{i}^{*})
) are respectively the interlacement set and the vacant set.
We recover the caracteristic property of
{\mathcal{I}}
, announced in (
2
), by observing that
{\mathcal{I}}\cap K=\emptyset
is equivalent to
\omega(W_{K}^{*})=0
and that
\omega(W_{K}^{*})
is distributed as a Poisson variable with parameter
\nu(W_{K}^{*})=\nu_{K}(W_{K})=\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)
.

Remark 1
.

It is possible to multiply the intensity measure
\nu
by a positive real
u
; or equivalently to multiply the family
\mathbf{a}
of conductances by
u
.
We thus get a family of interlacement processes indexed by
u
.
There is a natural way to construct all these models together through an increasing coupling:
consider a Poisson point process
\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{(w_{i}^{*},u_{i})}
on
\mathcal{W}^{*}\times[0,\infty)
with intensity measure
\nu\otimes du
;
then, for every
u>0
, set the interlacement process at level
u
to be
\omega^{u}:=\sum_{i\geq 1:u_{i}\leq u}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
.
As soon as
u\leq v
,
\omega^{u}
is dominated by
\omega^{v}
.
In this paper, we will mostly work with
u
fixed and equal to 1, and therefore disregard this coupling.

3
Theorems

3.1
FKG inequality

In his seminal paper, A.-S. Sznitman asked whether the interlacement set in
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d}
satifies the FKG inequality (see
[
6
]
, Remark 1.6).
This question was answered positively by A. Teixeira in
[
7
]
in the general setup of transient Markov chains, with a detailed proof.
Here, we point out that this property holds simply because Poisson point processes satisfy in general the FKG inequality.
We thus show that the interlacement process
\omega
, and not only the interlacement set
{\mathcal{I}}
, satifies the FKG inequality under
\mathbb{P}
.

Let us recall some definitions.
Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F},\leq)
be a mesurable space equipped with an order.
A measurable subset
A
of
\mathcal{X}
is said to be increasing if

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},\qquad(x\in A\text{ and }x\leq y)\implies y\in A\,.

(18)

If
(\mathcal{X},\leq)
and
(\mathcal{Y},\leq)
are two ordered sets, a function
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
is said to be non-decreasing if

\forall x,y\in\mathcal{X},\qquad x\leq y\implies f(x)\leq f(y)\,.

(19)

A probability measure
P
on
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F})
is said to satisfy the FKG property if for every increasing events
A
and
B
we have

P[A\cap B]\geq P[A]\times P[B]\,,

(20)

or, equivalently, that for every measurable nonnegative and nondecreasing measurable functions
f,g:\mathcal{X}\to{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

E[fg]\geq E[f]\times E[g]\,,

(21)

where
E
denotes the expectation under
P
, and
{\mathbb{R}}
is equipped with its Borelian
\sigma
-algebra and its usual order.
An
\mathcal{X}
-valued random variable is said to satisfy the FKG inequality if its law does.

The following lemma is an elementary fact.

Lemma 2
.

Let
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{F},\leq)
and
(\mathcal{Y},\mathcal{G},\leq)
be two mesurable spaces, equiped with orders. Let
X
be an
\mathcal{X}
-valued random variable and let
f:\mathcal{X}\to\mathcal{Y}
be a measurable and non-decreasing function.
If
X
satifies the FKG inequality, then so does
f(X)
.

We equip the set
\Omega
introduced in (
16
) with the order:

\omega\leq\omega^{\prime}\qquad\text{ if and only if }\qquad\forall D\in\mathcal{W}^{*},\,\omega(D)\leq\omega^{\prime}(D)\,.

(22)

Theorem 3
.

The law
\mathbb{P}
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
satifies the FKG inequality.

Proof.

It is a general fact that a Poisson point process on a measurable state space with
\sigma
-finite intensity measure satisfies the FKG inequality. See for example Section 20.3 of
[
3
]
.
∎

Applying Lemma
2
, we deduce from Theorem
3
that the following variables satisfy the FKG inequality:

•

the collection
\eta=(\eta_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\in\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{V}}
defined by

\eta_{x}:=\begin{cases}1&\text{if }x\text{ is visited by at least one trajectory in }\omega\,,\\
0&\text{else}\,,\end{cases}

the product set
\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{V}}
being equipped with the product order.
In other terms,
{\mathcal{I}}
(and therefore also
{\mathcal{V}}
) satisfies the FKG inequality (with respect to the inclusion order on subsets of
\mathbb{V}
). This is the FKG property shown in
[
7
]
.

•

the collection
\eta=(\eta_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{V}}
defined by

\eta_{x}:=\text{ number of trajectories in }\omega\text{ visiting }x\,,

the set
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}:=\{0\}\cup{\mathbb{N}}
being equipped with the usual order, and the set
{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{V}}
with the product order.

•

the collection
\eta=(\eta_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{V}}\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{\mathbb{V}}
defined by

\eta_{x}:=\text{ total number of visits to }x\text{ by trajectories in }\omega\,.

3.2
The
\sigma
-algebra of non-local events

We now turn to the main object of this article, which is to study 0-1 laws for random interlacements in our general framework of transient weighted graphs.

In the case of vertex- or edge-transitive weighted graphs, there is a natural collection of measure-preserving transformations to be considered and one can expect ergodicity, i.e., a 0-1 law for events which are invariant under these transformations. This is the case for the original work of Sznitamn
[
6
]
, i.e., for the SRW on
{\mathbb{Z}}^{d},d\geq 3
, where it was shown that translation-invariant events have probability either 0 or 1.
In the general case, we rather choose to consider events that are “non-local” in the sense that they do not depend on the restriction of the configuration
\omega
inside any finite region of the graph
\mathcal{G}
.

For every finite vertex set
K
, for every biinfinite trajectory
w\in W_{K}
, we define two relevant times:

\displaystyle\tau_{K}(w)

\displaystyle=\inf\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:w_{n}\in K\}\quad\text{ (hitting time of }K),

(23)

\displaystyle\lambda_{K}(w)

\displaystyle=\sup\{n\in{\mathbb{Z}}:w_{n}\in K\}\quad\text{ (time of the last visit in }K),

and we break
w
in three pieces according to these two times: we denote

•

by
Ins_{K}(w)
the finite piece of trajectory of
w
between times
\tau_{K}(w)
and
\lambda_{K}(w)
, re-indexed so that it starts at time 0:
Ins_{K}(w):=(w_{\tau_{K}(w)+n})_{0\leq n\leq\lambda_{K}(w)-\tau_{K}(w)}
,

•

and by
Out_{K}(w)=(Out_{\rightarrow K}(w),Out_{K\rightarrow}(w))
the pair consisiting of the two following semi-infinite pieces of the trajectory
w
:

–

Out_{\rightarrow K}(w)
the restriction of
w
up to time
\tau_{K}(w)
, re-indexed so that it ends at time 0:
Out_{\rightarrow K}(w):=(w_{\tau_{K}(w)+n})_{n\leq 0}
,

–

and
Out_{K\rightarrow}(w)
the restriction of
w
from time
\lambda_{K}(w)
on, re-indexed so that it starts at time 0:
Out_{K\rightarrow}(w):=(w_{\lambda_{K}(w)+n})_{n\geq 0}
.

Furthermore, we define two random variables
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}:\omega\mapsto\mathrm{Ins}_{K}(\omega)
and
\mathrm{Out}_{K}:\omega\mapsto\mathrm{Out}_{K}(\omega)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
as follows. For any
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
in
\Omega
, we pick for each
i\geq 1
any representative
w_{i}
of
w_{i}^{*}
, and define

\mathrm{Ins}_{K}(\omega)=\sum_{i\in I:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Ins_{K}(w_{i})}\,,

(24)

and

\mathrm{Out}_{K}(\omega)=\sum_{i\geq 1:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Out_{K}(w_{i})}+\sum_{i\in I:w_{i}^{*}\notin W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}\,.

(25)

We finally define on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of non-local events:

\mathcal{T}_{RI}=\bigcap_{K\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V})}\sigma(\mathrm{Out}_{K})\,.

(26)

We will be mainly interested in proving the 0-1 law for the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
: it is the property that every event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
has probability either 0 or 1 under
\mathbb{P}
.

This property does not hold in full generality: see Example
4
for a simple counter-example.

Example 4
.

Consider the Markov associated with the nearest neighbor graph on
{\mathbb{Z}}
and the family of conductances:

a_{n,n+1}=2^{n},\qquad a_{-n-1,-n}=2^{n},\qquad n\geq 0,

(27)

i.e., the Markov chain with the transition probabilities
p_{0,1}=p_{0,-1}=\frac{1}{2}
, and, for
n\geq 1
,
p_{n,n+1}=p_{-n,-n-1}=\frac{2}{3}
and
p_{n,n-1}=p_{-n,-n+1}=\frac{1}{3}
.
Let us consider the trajectories of
\omega
that go from
-\infty
to
\infty
. These trajectories have to pass through 0, so their number is distributed as a Poisson variable with parameter

\displaystyle P_{0}

\displaystyle[X_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\infty|\tau_{0}^{+}=\infty]e_{\{0\}}(0)P_{0}[X_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty]

(28)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P_{0}[X_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}-\infty,\tau_{0}^{+}=\infty]a_{0}P_{0}[X_{n}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}\infty].

This parameter is clearly positive and finite, so the number of trajectories in
\omega
going from
-\infty
to
\infty
is a nontrivial random variable. It is however measureable with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
.

More generally, let us give a necessary condition for the 0-1 law to hold. We consider the sub-
\sigma
-algebra of
(W^{+},\mathcal{W}^{+})
consisting of events that are invariant under time-shift. Denoting by

\theta:W^{+}\to W^{+},\,(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}\mapsto(w_{n+1})_{n\geq 0}
the time-shift, we set

\mathcal{T}_{MC}:=\left\{A\in\mathcal{W}^{+}:\theta^{-1}(A)=A\right\}.

(29)

For any events
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{MC}
, let us consider the set of trajectories
w\in W
such that
(w_{-n})_{n\geq 0}\in A
and
(w_{n})_{n\geq 0}\in B
and let us denote by “
A\rightarrow B
” its image under the projection
\pi^{*}
.
We observe that
\omega(A\to B)
, the random variable counting the number of trajectories in
\omega
that fall in “
A\rightarrow B
”, is measurable with respect to
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
and is distributed as a Poisson variable with parameter
\nu(A\rightarrow B)
.
The announced necessary condition follows: if the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
holds, then

\forall A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{MC},\qquad\nu(A\rightarrow B)\in\{0,\infty\}.

(30)

3.3
Under tail-triviality or tail-atomicity assumption

We give a first condition under which the 0-1 law holds.
We say that the Markov chain
X
is tail-trivial or that the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is satisfied if for some
x\in\mathbb{V}
, the invariant
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
is trivial under
P_{x}
, i.e.,
P_{x}
gives probability either 0 or 1 to all event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
. Note that, by irreducibilty, requiring this for some
x
in
\mathbb{V}
is equivalent to requiring it for all
x
’s in
\mathbb{V}
.

Theorem 5
.

Assume that the Markov chain
X
is tail-trivial. Then, under
\mathbb{P}
, every event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
has probability either 0 or 1.

Next we go beyond the tail-triviality hypothesis.
Given a probability space
(\mathcal{X},\mathcal{J},\mathbb{Q})
, we say that

•

an event
A\in\mathcal{J}
is atomic if
\mathbb{Q}(A)>0
and any event included in
A
has probability either equal to that of
\mathbb{Q}(A)
or to zero,

•

the probability space is purely atomic if there exists an at most countable collection
(A_{i})_{i\in I}
of atomic events partitioning
\mathcal{X}
.

We say that
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is purely atomic if
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is purely atomic with respect to
P_{x}
for some
x
in
\mathbb{V}
. Note that, by irreducibilty, requiring this for some
x
in
\mathbb{V}
is equivalent to requiring it for all
x
’s in
\mathbb{V}
.
Our next theorem states that, when
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is purely atomic, (
30
) is a sufficient condition for the 0-1 law to hold.

Theorem 6
.

Assume that
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is purely atomic.
Assume that for every
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{MC}
,
\nu(A\rightarrow B)
is either 0 or infinite.
Then, under
\mathbb{P}
, every event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
has probability either 0 or 1.

Remark 7
.

The conditon (
30
) can also be formulated as: for every
A,B\in\mathcal{T}_{MC}
, as soon as
P_{x}(A)>0
and
P_{x}(B)>0
for some
x\in\mathbb{V}
(equivalently, for all
x
’s in
\mathbb{V}
), the quantity
\nu(A\to B)
must be infinite.

We observe that

\nu(A\rightarrow B)=\lim_{K\to\mathbb{V}}\nu(A\rightarrow B,W_{K}^{*})
and that the quantity appearing in this limit can be expressed as

\nu(A\rightarrow B,W_{K}^{*})=\sum_{x\in\partial_{X}K}e_{K}(x)P_{x}[A|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]P_{x}[B].

(31)

3.4
Quantitative criterion

We further provide a quantitative criterion for the occurrence of the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
.
For every finite vertex set
K
, let us introduce the hinge measure on
K
, the measure on
\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{V}
defined by:

h_{K}(x,y)=e_{K}(x)\times P_{x}[X_{\lambda_{K}}=y]\,,

(32)

which is supported by
\partial_{X}K\times\partial_{X}K
and symmetric (by reversibility of
X
), whose marginals are both equal to the equilibrium measure
e_{K}
and whose total mass is equal to
\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)
.

Theorem 8
.

The 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
holds if and only if for every site
x\in\mathbb{V}
, for every
\epsilon>0
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\left(P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]-\epsilon\right)_{+}\to 0.

(33)

Remark 9
.

We stress that, due to (
9
),

\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]=\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime})P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty]=Cap(\{x\}).

(34)

The condition in the theorem can therefore be interpreted as the fact that this sum is “diluted” enough, that there is not too much mass supported on some couples
(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})
.

Using the bounds
b{\mathbf{1}}_{a>2b}\leq(a-b)_{+}\leq{\mathbf{1}}_{a>b}
for
a\in[0,1],b\geq 0
, the condition in the theorem can be equivalently formulated as follows: for every site
x\in\mathbb{V}
, for every
\epsilon>0
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}){\mathbf{1}}_{P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]>\epsilon}\to 0.

(35)

3.5
A weak 0-1 law

Let us also introduce another
\sigma
-algebra, focusing only on the future pieces of trajectory. For every finite vertex set
K
, we define a random variable
\mathrm{Out}_{K\rightarrow}:\omega\mapsto\mathrm{Out}_{K\rightarrow}(\omega)
on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A})
as follows. For any
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
in
\Omega
, we pick for each
i\geq 1
a representative
w_{i}
of
w_{i}^{*}
and denote

\mathrm{Out}_{K\rightarrow}(\omega)=\sum_{i\in I:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Out_{K\rightarrow}(w_{i})}+\sum_{i\in I:w_{i}^{*}\notin W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}\,.

(36)

The
\sigma
-algebra of non-local future events is defined as

\mathcal{T}_{RI\rightarrow}:=\bigcap_{K\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V})}\sigma(\mathrm{Out}_{K\rightarrow})\,.

(37)

This
\sigma
-algebra is a priori not so interesting because it lacks much information. We mention it however, since it has the nice property that it satisfies the 0-1 law in full generality.

Theorem 10
.

Under
\mathbb{P}
, every event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI\rightarrow}
has probability either 0 or 1.

Remark 11
.

By reversibility of the Markov chain
X
, reversing time for all the trajectories in
\omega
does not change the law of
\omega
under
\mathbb{P}
.
Thus, we obtain also the 0-1 law for the
\sigma
-algebra of non-local past events, defined by focusing on the past pieces of trajectory
Out_{\rightarrow K}(w)
instead of the future pieces
Out_{K\rightarrow}(w)
.

3.6
A 0-1 law for increasing non-local events

We now present two other ways to weaken the 0-1 law discussed in Section
3.2
, that may lead to 0-1 laws holding under more general assumptions.

First, one could ask only for the 0-1 law to hold for increasing events belonging to
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
. We observe that condition (
30
) remains necessary for this modified
0-1
law to hold. Indeed, if there exist events
A
and
B
in
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
such that
\nu(A\to B)\in(0,\infty)
then for any positive integer
k
, the event
\{\omega(A\to B)\geq k\}
is an increasing event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
which has probability different from 0 and from 1.
So this modified 0-1 law does not hold in full generality, and, in the case where
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
is tail-atomic, it is in fact equivalent to the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
.

Secondly, we consider the possibility of forgetting the pairings of the pieces of trajectories when defining the trace of configuration
\omega
outside
K
.
We set, for
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{\omega_{i}^{*}}
, picking for each
i\geq 1
any representative
w_{i}
of
w_{i}^{*}
,

\widetilde{\mathrm{Out}}_{K}(\omega):=\sum_{i\geq 1:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Out_{\to K}(w_{i})}+\sum_{i\geq 1:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Out_{K\to}(w_{i})}+\sum_{i\in I:w_{i}^{*}\notin W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}\,

(38)

and define

\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}:=\bigcap_{K\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V})}\sigma\left(\widetilde{\mathrm{Out}}_{K}\right).

Once again, the 0-1 law for
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
does not hold in full generality. For example, we observe that if there exists an event
A\in\mathcal{T}_{MC}
such that, with
A^{c}=W^{+}\setminus A
, we have
\omega(A\to A^{c})\in(0,\infty)
, then the variable

\omega(A\to W^{+})-\omega(W^{+}\to A)=\omega(A\to A^{c})-\omega(A^{c}\to A)

is mesurable with respect to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
and non-trivial.

Our final theorem states that, remarkably, combining the two approaches we just explored yields a 0-1 law that holds in full generality.

Theorem 12
.

Under
\mathbb{P}
, any increasing event in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
has probability either 0 or 1.

Remark 13
.

Of course, by taking complements, also decreasing events in
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
have probability 0 or 1 under
\mathbb{P}
.

4
Preliminaries for the proof of the 0-1 laws

4.1
Hinge decomposition of the interlacement process

Given a finite vertex set
K
and a trajectory
w\in W_{K}
, recall the definition of the times
\tau_{K}(w)
and
\lambda_{K}(w)
in (
23
) and define the hinge couple of
w
as:

Hin_{K}(w):=(w_{\tau_{K}(w)},w_{\lambda_{K}(w)}).

(39)

We define the
hinge process

\mathrm{Hin}_{K}(\omega)
associated to
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
by picking for each
i\geq 1
any representative
w_{i}
of
w_{i}^{*}
and setting:

\mathrm{Hin}_{K}(\omega)=\sum_{i\geq 1:w_{i}^{*}\in W_{K}^{*}}\delta_{Hin_{K}(w_{i})}.

(40)

Under
\mathbb{P}
, the random variable
\mathrm{Hin}_{K}
is distributed as a Poisson point process with intensity measure the hinge measure
h_{K}
, introduced in (
32
).

Let us describe how to sample
\omega
under
\mathbb{P}
with respect to the decomposition along the hinge process.
To sample the trajectories touching
K
,

•

sample first the hinge process, as a Poisson point process with intensity
h_{K}
.

•

to each hinge couple
(x,y)
, attach a trajectory
w^{*}
which decomposes as
Ins_{K}(w)
and
Out_{K}(w)=(Out_{K\rightarrow}(w),Out_{\rightarrow K}(w))
:

–

Ins_{K}(w)
is sampled according to
P_{x}[(X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{\lambda_{K}})\in\cdot|X_{\lambda_{K}}=y]

–

Out_{K\rightarrow}(w)
is obtained by applying a time reversal to a trajectory sampled according to
P_{x}[\cdot|\tau^{+}_{K}=\infty]
,

–

Out_{\rightarrow K}(w)
is sampled according to
P_{y}[\cdot|\tau^{+}_{K}=\infty]
.

Complete
\omega
by sampling the trajectories that do not touch
K
, as usual as a Poisson point process with intensity measure
\nu
restricted to
W^{*}\setminus W_{K}^{*}
.

We observe that the interlacement process satisfies a spatial Markov property: under
\mathbb{P}
,
\mathrm{Out}_{K}
depends on
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}
only through the hinge process
\mathrm{Hin}_{K}
. Let us thus describe how to go from a finite vertex set K to a larger finite vertex set L: this will be at the heart of our arguments for the 0-1 law.
To sample
\mathrm{Ins}_{L}
given
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}
, independently

•

extend each path in
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}
, say with extremities
(x,y)
, by adding two pieces of trajectory:

–

on the left, one obtained by applying a time reversal to a trajectory sampled according to
P_{x}[(X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{\lambda_{L}})\in\cdot|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]
,

–

on the right, one sampled according to
P_{y}[(X_{0},X_{1},\dots,X_{\lambda_{L}})\in\cdot|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]
,

•

sample additional trajectories inside
L
that do not touch
K
by sampling biinfinite trajectories under
\nu
restricted to
W_{L}^{*}\setminus W_{K}^{*}
and keeping only the pieces of trajectories inside
L
.

4.2
Coupling results

For
\mu
and
\nu
probability measures on an at most countable state space
\mathcal{X}
(equipped with the
\sigma
-algebra consisting of all its subsets), we denote by
d_{TV}(\mu,\nu)
their total variation distance, which is defined as

\displaystyle d_{TV}(\mu,\nu):

\displaystyle=\sup_{A\subset\mathcal{X}}|\mu(A)-\nu(A)|

(41)

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}:\mu(x)>\nu(x)}\mu(x)-\nu(x)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}|\mu(x)-\nu(x)|

\displaystyle=\inf_{(X,Y):X\sim\mu,Y\sim\nu}P(X\neq Y).

The above infimum is over couplings
(X,Y)
of
\mu
and
\nu
and it is achieved.

Using a classical approach to 0-1 laws, we now establish a sufficient condition for the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
in terms of total variation distances.
For any finite vertex set
K
,

•

we call admissible path inside
K
any nearest-neighbor path in
\mathcal{G}
with finite length, with starting time 0 and with extremities belonging to
\partial_{X}K
.

•

we call admissible configuration inside
K
any configuration
\eta
such that
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)>0
, that is, any configuration of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{\gamma_{i}}
, where
k
is a finite integer (possibly zero), and for each
i=1,\dots,k
,
\gamma_{i}
is an admissible path inside
K
.

Lemma 14
.

Assume that for every finite vertex set
K
, for every admissible configuration
\eta
inside
K
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Out}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Out}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)\right)\to 0.

(42)

Then every event in
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
has probability either 0 or 1 under
\mathbb{P}
.

Furthermore, the above condition can be restricted to
\eta
’s consisting of a single path.

Remark 15
.

We remark that, due to the Markovian property evoked in Section
4.1
, we can rewrite the total variation distance in (
42
) as

d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{K}=\rho),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{K}=0)\right),

(43)

where
\rho
is the hinge process on
K
corresponding to
\eta
.
So the condition in Lemma
14
really concerns the dependance of the hinge process
\mathrm{Hin}_{L}
on the hinge process
\mathrm{Hin}_{K}
.

Proof.

We assume that the condition in the lemma holds. Let
E\in\mathcal{T}_{RI}
. Let
K
be a finite vertex set and
\eta
an admissible configuration on
K
, and let us consider, under a probability
\mathbf{P}
, a coupling
(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
with
\omega
distributed under
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)
and
\omega^{\prime}
distributed under
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
, such that
\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega)=\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega^{\prime})
with maximal probability, i.e.,

\mathbf{P}[\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega)\neq\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega^{\prime})]=d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)\right)

(44)

and such that on the event
\{\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega)=\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega^{\prime})\}
,
\omega
and
\omega^{\prime}
coincide outside
L
, in the sense that
\mathrm{Out}_{L}(\omega)=\mathrm{Out}_{L}(\omega^{\prime})
. Since
E
belongs to
\sigma(Out_{L})
, on the latter event
\omega
realises
E
if and only if
\omega^{\prime}
does. It follows that:

\displaystyle|\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)-\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)|

(45)

\displaystyle=|\mathbf{E}[{\mathbf{1}}_{\omega\in E}-{\mathbf{1}}_{\omega^{\prime}\in E}]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}[{\mathbf{1}}_{\omega\in E}\neq{\mathbf{1}}_{\omega^{\prime}\in E}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega)\neq\mathrm{Hin}_{L}(\omega^{\prime}))

\displaystyle=d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)\right).

Sending
L
to
\mathbb{V}
, the hypothesis of the lemma yields
\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)=\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
.
Since this is true for any admissible
\eta
, we get that, under
\mathbb{P}
,
E
is independent of the variable
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}
.
Since the variables
(\mathrm{Ins}_{K},K\in\mathcal{P}_{f}(\mathbb{V}))
generate the
\sigma
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
, this implies that, under
\mathbb{P}
,
E
is independent of
\mathcal{A}
, and in particular of itself, hence
\mathbb{P}(E)\in\{0,1\}
. This concludes the law of the 0-1 law.

To justify that one can restrict to
\eta
’s of the form
\eta=\delta_{\gamma}
, where
\gamma
is an admissible path inside
{\mathcal{K}}
, we observe that for every admissible configuration
\eta
inside
K
and every admissible path
\gamma
inside
K
,

\displaystyle d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta+\delta_{\gamma}),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)\right)

(46)

\displaystyle\leq d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\delta_{\gamma}),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)\right).

Indeed, it is just a matter of coupling identically the trajectories corresponding to
\eta
in both processes.
The condition in the lemma derives by reasoning by induction on the number of paths in
\eta
, with the help of the triangular inequality for
d_{TV}
.
∎

In order to establish the quantitative criterion of Theorem
8
, we will show in Section
6.1.1
that the condition of Lemma
14
is also necesssary for the 0-1 law for
\mathcal{T}_{RI}
to hold. This will result from a more general statement that we present now, and that will also be useful (in Section
5.1
) to exploit the tail-triviality or tail-atomicity assumptions of Section
3.3
.

Let us first recall a useful fact.
For a (possibly time-inhomogeneous) Markov chain
Z=(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a countable state space
\mathcal{X}
,
for any starting points
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{X}
, there exists, under a probability measure
\mathbf{P}
, a coupling
(X,Y)=((X_{n})_{n\geq 0},(Y_{n})_{n\geq 0})
of the Markov chain started respectively at
x
and at
y

such that, almost surely,
X
and
Y
coincide after their first meeting time
T:=\inf\{n\geq 0:X_{n}=Y_{n}\}

and such that for every
n\geq 0
:

\mathbf{P}[T>n]=d_{TV}(P_{x}[Z_{n}\in\cdot],P_{y}[Z_{n}\in\cdot]).

(47)

This coupling is called the maximal coupling of the Markov chain
Z
, see
[
2
,
5
]
or Section 12.4.2 of
[
4
]
.

Lemma 16
.

Consider a (possibly time-inhomogeneous Markov chain)
(Z_{n})_{n\geq 0}
on a discrete countable state space. We fix
x_{0}\in\mathcal{X}
. The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For every
n\geq 0
, for every
x
and
y
in
\mathcal{X}
such that
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{n}=x)>0
and
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{n}=y)>0
, we have

d_{TV}(P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=x),P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=y))\underset{m\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0.

(48)

(ii)

Every event in
\mathcal{T}:=\bigcap_{n\geq 0}\sigma(Z_{m},m\geq n)
has probability either 0 or 1 under
P_{x_{0}}
.

Proof.

The proof of the implication
(i)\implies(ii)
follows the same scheme as the proof of Lemma
14
.
We assume that
(i)
holds and consider
E\in\mathcal{T}
.
We fix
n\geq 0
and
x,y
in
\mathcal{X}
such that
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{n}=x)>0
and
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{n}=y)>0
.
Let us consider now an integer
m\geq n
. We use, under a probability
\mathbf{P}
, a coupling
(X,Y)
of
P_{x_{0}}(\cdot|Z_{n}=x)
and
P_{x_{0}}(\cdot|Z_{n}=y)
such that
X_{m}=Y_{m}
with maximal probability, i.e.:

\mathbf{P}[X_{m}\neq Y_{m}]=d_{TV}(P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=x),P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=y)).

(49)

We further require that, on the event
X_{m}=Y_{m}
,
X
and
Y
coincide after time
m
. Since
E\in\sigma(Z_{k},k\geq m)
, on the latter event
X
realises
E
if and only if
Y
does. It follows that:

\displaystyle|P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=x)-P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=y)|

(50)

\displaystyle=|\mathbf{E}[{\mathbf{1}}_{X\in E}-{\mathbf{1}}_{Y\in E}]|

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}[{\mathbf{1}}_{X\in E}\neq{\mathbf{1}}_{Y\in E}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}[X_{m}\neq Y_{m}]

\displaystyle=d_{TV}(P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=x),P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=y))\,.

Sending
m
to infinity, condition
(i)
yields that
P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=x)=P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=y)
.
We have thus established that, under
P_{x_{0}}
,
E
is independent of the variable
Z_{n}
, for every
n\geq 0
. It follows that, under
P_{x_{0}}
,
E
is independent of
\sigma(Z_{n},n\geq 0)
, and in particular of itself, hence
P_{x_{0}}(E)\in\{0,1\}
and
(ii)
is proved.

Conversely, let us prove the implication
(ii)\implies(i)
. Let us assume that the 0-1 law holds, let us consider
n\geq 0
and
x,y\in\mathcal{X}
such that
P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=x)>0
and
P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=y)>0
and let us show that

\delta:=\lim_{m\to\infty}d_{TV}(P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=x),P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in\cdot|Z_{n}=y))

(51)

is zero. Note that the quantity in the limit is non-increasing by elementary arguments.
Let us consider, under a probability
\mathbf{P}
,
(X,Y)=((X_{m})_{m\geq n},(Y_{m})_{m\geq n})
the maximal coupling of the Markov chain started at time
n
respectively from
x
and
y
, in the sense of (
47
). For each
m\geq n
, we consider the subset
E_{m}
of
\mathcal{X}
defined as:

E_{m}:=\{z\in\mathcal{X},P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}=z|Z_{n}=x)>P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}=z|Z_{n}=y)\}\,,

which satisfies

P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in E_{m}|Z_{n}=x)\geq P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in E_{m}|Z_{n}=y)+\delta\geq\delta,

(52)

by the definition of
\delta
in (
51
) and by the definition of the total variation distance in (
41
).
Let us consider the event

E:=\{Z_{m}\in E_{m}\text{ for infinitely many }m\text{'s}\}
.
This event belongs to
\mathcal{T}
, thus
(i)
yields that
p:=P_{x_{0}}(E)
is either 0 or 1. We observe that then also
P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=x)
and
P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=y)
are equal to
p
.

On the one hand, if
p=0
, then
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in E_{m}\text{ i.o.}|Z_{n}=x)=0
, which implies that
P_{x_{0}}(Z_{m}\in E_{m}|Z_{n}=x)\underset{m\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0
, and then (
52
) yields that
\delta=0
.

On the other hand, if
p=1
, then
\mathbf{P}(Y_{m}\in E_{m}\text{ i.o.})=P_{x_{0}}(E|Z_{n}=y)=1
so,
\mathbf{P}
-almost surely, there exists an
m\geq n
(in fact, infinitely many
m
’s) such that
Y_{m}\in E_{m}
.
By maximality of the coupling,
Y_{m}\in E_{m}
implies almost surely that
T\leq m
. Thus,
T
is almost surely finite and
\delta=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbf{P}(T>m)=0
.
∎

We finally provide an estimate on the total variation distance between
Poi(\lambda)
, which denotes the Poisson distribution with parameter
\lambda
and
Poi(\lambda)+1
, which denotes the law of
X+1
, where
X
follows
Poi(\lambda)
.

Lemma 17
.

For every
\lambda>0
,

d_{TV}(Poi(\lambda),Poi(\lambda)+1)\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\lambda}}.

(53)

Proof.

With
X\sim Poi(\lambda)
,

\displaystyle 2\,d_{TV}(Poi(\lambda),Poi(\lambda)+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{\lambda^{k}}{k!}e^{-\lambda}\left|\frac{k}{\lambda}-1\right|

(54)

\displaystyle=E\left[\left|\frac{X}{\lambda}-1\right|\right]

\displaystyle\leq E\left[\left(\frac{X}{\lambda}-1\right)^{2}\right]^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\lambda}}\,.

∎

Lemma 18

As

•

\forall x,y\in K

,

and that we have

\frac{2}{\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}(K)}=\inf\left\{\sum_{x,y\in\mathbb{V}:x\sim y}\frac{1}{a_{x,y}}\theta(x,y)^{2}:\theta\text{ is a unit flow from }K\text{ to infinity}\right\},

6.1.2
Distance between Poisson point processes

If
\nu
is a finite measure on a finite set
\mathcal{X}
, let us denote by
PPP(\nu)
the law of a Poisson point process on
\mathcal{X}
with intensity measure
\nu
. If furthermore,
\pi
is a probability measure on
\mathcal{X}
, let us denote by
PPP(\nu)\oplus\pi
the law of the superposition of this same Poisson point process and a random point in
\mathcal{X}
sampled independently under
\pi
.

Lemma 23
.

For every
n\geq 1
we consider a finite set
\mathcal{X}_{n}
, and two finite measures
\nu_{n}
and
\pi_{n}
on
\mathcal{X}_{n}
,
\pi_{n}
being a probability measure. Then,

d_{TV}(PPP(\nu_{n})\oplus\pi_{n},PPP(\nu_{n}))\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0

(68)

if and only if, for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}_{n}}\left(\pi_{n}(x)-\epsilon\nu_{n}(x)\right)_{+}\underset{n\to\infty}{\longrightarrow}0\,.

(69)

Proof.

We consider a finite state space
\mathcal{X}
, and two measures
\nu
and
\pi
on
\mathcal{X}
,
\pi
being a probability measure.

Let us first show, for every
\epsilon>0
and
a>0
, that if
\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left(\pi(x)-\epsilon\nu(x)\right)_{+}\leq a
, then

d_{TV}(PPP(\nu)\oplus\pi,PPP(\nu))\leq\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{\epsilon}{1-a}}+a,

(70)

To prove this, we construct
PPP(\nu)
using an auxiliairy Poisson point process. We consider
\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta{(x_{i},u_{i})}
a Poisson point process on
\mathcal{X}\times[0,\infty)
with intensity measure
\rho=\mathbf{c}\otimes du
, where
\mathbf{c}:=\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\delta_{x}
is the counting measure on
\mathcal{X}
and
du
is the Lebesgue measure on
(0,\infty)
.
Then

\omega_{\nu}:=\sum_{i\geq 1:u_{i}\leq\nu(x_{i})}\delta_{x_{i}}

realises
PPP(\nu)
.
Furthermore, let us consider the domain

D=\left\{(x,u)\in\mathcal{X}\times(0,\infty):u\leq\frac{\pi(x)}{\epsilon}\wedge\nu(x)\right\}.

Let us tilt
\omega_{\nu}
only by changing the law of the number of points falling in
D
: let us make it follow the law of
Poi(\rho(D))+1
instead of the law
Poi(\rho(D))
.
Then, we obtain a point process which is the superposition of a
PPP(\nu)
and a point sampled under the renormalisation of the measure
\frac{\pi(\cdot)}{\epsilon}\wedge\nu(\cdot)
. This last distribution is at total-variation distance not larger than
a
from
\pi
.
Thus:

d_{TV}(PPP(\nu)\oplus\pi,PPP(\nu))\leq d_{TV}(Poi(\rho(D)),Poi(\rho(D))+1)+a,

(71)

We conclude the proof of (
70
) by using Lemma
17
and observing that
\rho(D)\geq\frac{1-a}{\epsilon}
.

Let us furthermore show for every
\epsilon>0
and
a>0
the existence of a positive real
D_{\epsilon,a}
such that if

\sum_{x\in\mathcal{X}}\left(\pi(x)-\epsilon\nu(x)\right)_{+}\geq a
, then

d_{TV}(PPP(\nu)\oplus\pi,PPP(\nu))\geq D_{\epsilon,a}.

(72)

Indeed, let us consider
A:=\{x\in\mathcal{X}:\pi(x)>\epsilon\nu(x)\}
. We have by assumption
\pi(A)\geq\epsilon\nu(A)+a
. Considering the number of points falling in
A
, we get the lower bound:

d_{TV}(PPP(\nu)\oplus\pi,PPP(\nu))\geq d_{TV}(Poi(\nu(A))+Ber(\pi(A)),Poi(\nu(A)))\geq D_{\epsilon,a}\,,

where we denote by
Poi(\lambda)+Ber(p)
the law of the independent sum of a Poisson variable of parameter
\lambda
and a Bernoulli variable of parameter
p
and we set

D_{\varepsilon,a}:=\inf_{\lambda\geq 0,\,p\in[0,1]:\,\epsilon\lambda+a\leq p}d_{TV}(Poi(\lambda)+Ber(p),Poi(\lambda)).

We observe that the function

(\lambda,p)\mapsto d_{TV}(Poi(\lambda)+Ber(p),Poi(\lambda))

defined on the compact

\{(\lambda,p):\lambda\geq 0,p\in[0,1],\epsilon\lambda+a\leq p\}

is continuous and positive, hence
D_{\varepsilon,a}>0
.
Thus, (
72
) is established and the lemma follows from (
70
) and (
72
).
∎

•

the law

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{K}=0)
, that is, a Poisson point process with intensity measure

h_{L}^{K}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):=h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})P_{x^{\prime}}(\tau_{K}=\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime})

•

and the law

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{K}=\delta_{(x,y)})
, that is, the law of the superposition of the above Poisson point process and an independent point sampled under the probability

p_{L}^{K,x,y}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime}):=P_{x}(X_{\lambda_{L}}=x^{\prime}|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty)P_{y}(X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty)
.

By Lemma
23
, it is enough to establish that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}\left(p_{L}^{K,x,y}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})-\epsilon h_{L}^{K}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\right)_{+}\underset{L\to\mathbb{V}}{\longrightarrow}0.

(78)

By reversibility:

\displaystyle a_{x}

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}=x^{\prime},\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{\lambda_{K}}=y]\mathbb{P}_{y}[X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]

(79)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle a_{x^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{L}^{+}=\infty]\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x,X_{\lambda_{K}}=y,X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]

hence

p_{L}^{K,x,y}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=\frac{h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})}{h_{K}(x,y)}\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x,X_{\lambda_{K}}=y|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}],

(80)

and therefore, playing on
\epsilon
, (
78
) is equivalent to the fact that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}

\displaystyle h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})

(81)

\displaystyle\times\big(P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x,X_{\lambda_{K}}=y|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]-\epsilon P_{x^{\prime}}(\tau_{K}=\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime})\big)_{+}

vanishes as
L\to\mathbb{V}
. Bounding
P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x,X_{\lambda_{K}}=y|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]
by
P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]
, the latter follows from the fact that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x^{\prime},y^{\prime}\in\partial_{X}L}h_{L}(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\left((1+\epsilon)P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]-\epsilon\right)_{+}\underset{L\to\mathbb{V}}{\longrightarrow}0,

(82)

which, finally, can be derived elementary from the condition of Theorem
8
by using the bound:

P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]\leq\sum_{z\in K}P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{z}<\infty|X_{\lambda_{L}}=y^{\prime}]

(83)

and playing on
\epsilon
.
∎

6.3
Proof of the weak 0-1 law

This section is devoted to showing Theorem
10
, that is, the 0-1 law for events that depend, for every finite vertex set
K
, only on the future pieces of trajectory outside
K
.

Let us observe that in the case of tail-atomicity, one could reproduce our proof (in Section
5.3
) to show that this 0-1 law holds as soon as
\nu(W^{+}\to A)\in\{0,\infty\}
for every
A\in\mathcal{T}_{MC}
.
And in fact, for
A
an atom of
\mathcal{T}_{MC}
under
P_{x}
(for arbitrary
x
), applying Lemma
18
to the Markov chain
X
conditioned on
A
yields that
\nu(A\to A)=\lim_{K\to\mathbb{V}}\mathop{\mathrm{cap}}_{A\to A}(K)=\infty
and a fortiori
\nu(W^{+}\to A)=\infty
.

Since we aim to prove Theorem
10
in full generality, we will rather rely on the quantitative approach of the present Section
6
.
We will proceed in two steps.
We will first prove that a condition similar to that of Theorem
8
holds in full generality. Then, we will prove Theorem
10
, using the same scheme of proof as in Section
6.2
.

Lemma 24
.

For every site
x\in\mathbb{V}
, for every
\epsilon>0
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime})\left(P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty]-\epsilon\right)_{+}\to 0.

(84)

Remark 25
.

We stress that, due to (
9
),

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime})P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty]=Cap(\{x\}).

(85)

The content of the lemma can therefore be interpreted as the fact that this sum is “diluted” enough, that there is not too much mass supported on some vertices
x
.

Using the bounds
b{\mathbf{1}}_{a>2b}\leq(a-b)_{+}\leq{\mathbf{1}}_{a>b}
for
a\in[0,1],b\geq 0
, the condition in the theorem can be equivalently formulated as follows: for every site
x\in\mathbb{V}
, for every
\epsilon>0
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime}){\mathbf{1}}_{P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty]>\epsilon}=e_{L}(\{x^{\prime}\in L:P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{x}<\infty]>\epsilon\})\to 0.

(86)

Proof of Lemma
24
.

Let us fix
x\in\mathbb{V}
and
\epsilon>0
. We consider the set

D:=\{y\in\mathbb{V}:P_{y}[\tau_{x}<\infty]>\epsilon\}.

As observed in Remark
25
, our aim is to show that
e_{L}(D)
vanishes as
L\to\mathbb{V}
.

Of course, if
D
is finite this is immediate: as soon as
L
contains
\overline{D}:=\{x\in\mathbb{V}:\exists y\in D,x\sim y\}
, we have
\partial_{X}L\cap D=\emptyset
and thus
e_{L}(D)=0
. But
D
can be infinite. Think for example of a simple random walk on
\mathbb{Z}
with a positive drift and
x=0
: in this example,
P_{y}[\tau_{x}<\infty]=1
for every
y\leq 0
.

Let us show that
D
is however necessarily transient, i.e., that for every
z\in\mathbb{V}
,

P_{z}[X_{n}\text{ visits }D\text{ infinitely many times}]=0
.
Our proof relies on the fact that
X
visits
x
almost surely finitely many times, due to transience.

For any
y
in
D
, we pick an integer
t_{y}
such that
P_{y}[\tau_{x}<t_{y}]\geq\frac{\epsilon}{2}
. Then we decompose the trajectory of the Markov chain
X
starting from
z
in disjoint excursions:

•

starting from
z
, we wait for the first visit to
D
, say at time
N_{1}
and at site
Y_{1}
; the first excursion is the piece of trajectory from time
N_{1}
to time
N_{1}+t_{Y_{1}}-1
;

•

we reiterate: for
n\geq 1
, after the
n
-th excursion, we wait for the first visit to
D
, say at time
N_{n+1}
and site
Y_{n+1}
, then we define the
(n+1)
-th excursion to be the piece of trajectory from time
N_{n+1}
to time
N_{n+1}+t_{Y_{n+1}}-1
.

There may be only finitely many excursions, meaning that at some point the trajectory stops visiting
D
; this is actually what we want to show happens almost surely.
We observe that every excursion has a probability at least
\frac{\epsilon}{2}
of visiting
x
. Thus, the expected number of excursions is not larger than
\frac{2}{\epsilon}
times the expected number of visits to
x
, which is finite by transience of
X
. We deduce that the number of excursions is almost surely finite, hence
D
is transient.

To conclude, we observe, using the definition of
D
and reversibility that

\displaystyle e_{L}(D)

\displaystyle=\sum_{y\in D}a_{y}P_{y}[\tau_{L}^{+}=\infty]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{y\in D}a_{y}P_{y}[\tau_{L}^{+}=\infty]P_{y}[\tau_{x}<\infty]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\sum_{y\in D}a_{x}P_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}=y]=\frac{a_{x}}{\epsilon}P_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}\in D].

The transience of
D
implies that
P_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}\in D]
vanishes as
L\to\mathbb{V}
and the proof is complete.
∎

Proof of Theorem
10
.

For convenience, we prefer to show the 0-1 law for the
\sigma
-algebra of non-local backward events, mentioned in Remark
11
and defined by focusing on the past pieces of trajectory
Out_{\rightarrow K}(w)
instead of the future pieces
Out_{K\rightarrow}(w)
. As justified in that remark, these two “weak” 0-1 laws are equivalent.

Analogously to Lemma
22
(or Lemma
14
), it is enough to show that for every finite vertex set
K
, for every
x\in\partial_{X}K
, as
L\to\mathbb{V}
,

d_{TV}\left(\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}=\delta_{x}),\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}=0)\right)\to 0.

(87)

where the random variable
\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}
is defined as

\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}(\omega)=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{X_{\tau_{K}(w_{i})}}

(88)

if
\omega=\sum_{i\geq 1}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
and
w_{i}
is a representative of
w_{i}^{*}
for each
i\geq 1
.
Now,

•

\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}=0)
is the law of Poisson point process on
\mathbb{V}
with intensity measure
e_{L}^{K}(x^{\prime}):=e_{L}(x^{\prime})P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}=\infty]
,

•

while
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Hin}_{\to K}=\delta_{x})
is the law of the superposition of this Poisson point process and an independent point in
\mathbb{V}
sampled under
p_{L}^{K,x}(x^{\prime}):=P_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}=x^{\prime}|\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]
.

By Lemma
23
, it is enough to establish that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}\left(p_{L}^{K,x}(x^{\prime})-\epsilon\,e_{L}^{K}(x^{\prime})\right)_{+}\underset{L\to\mathbb{V}}{\longrightarrow}.

(89)

By reversibility:

a_{x}\mathbb{P}_{x}[X_{\lambda_{L}}=x^{\prime},\tau_{K}^{+}=\infty]=a_{x^{\prime}}\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{L}^{+}=\infty]\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x]

(90)

hence

p_{L}^{K,x}(x^{\prime})=\frac{e_{L}(x^{\prime})}{e_{K}(x)}\mathbb{P}_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x],

(91)

and therefore, playing on
\epsilon
, (
89
) is equivalent to the fact that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime})\big(P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x]-\epsilon P_{x^{\prime}}(\tau_{K}=\infty)\big)_{+}\underset{L\to\mathbb{V}}{\longrightarrow}.

(92)

Bounding
P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty,X_{\tau_{K}}=x]
by
P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty]
, the latter follows from the fact that for every
\epsilon>0
,

\sum_{x^{\prime}\in L}e_{L}(x^{\prime})\left((1+\epsilon)P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty]-\epsilon\right)_{+}\underset{L\to V}{\longrightarrow}0,

(93)

which, finally, can be derived elementary from Lemma
24
by using the bound:

P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{K}<\infty]\leq\sum_{z\in K}P_{x^{\prime}}[\tau_{z}<\infty]

(94)

and playing on
\epsilon
.
∎

6.4
Proof of the 0-1 law for increasing events

In this section, we prove Theorem
12
. We begin with a lemma.

Lemma 26
.

For every finite vertex set
K
, for every admissibe path
\gamma
inside
K
, for every increasing event
E
belonging to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
, we have:

\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\delta_{\gamma})\leq\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0).

(95)

Proof of Lemma
26
.

We consider a finite vertex set
K
, and an admissibe path
\gamma
inside
K
, say with extremities
(x,y)\in\partial_{X}K\times\partial_{X}K
. Let furthermore
\epsilon>0
. We are going to construct a vertex set
L
(containing
K
) and, under a probability denoted
\mathbf{P}
, a coupling
(\omega,\omega^{\prime})
of
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\delta_{\gamma})\leq\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
together with random trajectories
w^{*},w_{1}^{*},w_{2}^{*}\in W^{*}
such that, with probability not smaller than
1-\epsilon
:

•

w^{*}
belongs to
\omega
and is precisely the trajectory of
\omega
that visits
K
,
w_{1}^{*}
and
w_{2}^{*}
belong to
\omega^{\prime}
;

•

\omega-\delta_{w^{*}}=\omega^{\prime}-\delta_{w_{1}^{*}}-\delta_{w_{2}^{*}}
;

•

Out_{\to L}(w)=Out_{\to L}(w_{1})
, i.e.,
w^{*}
and
w_{1}^{*}
coincide up to their entry in
L
, in particular their entry points in
L
coincide;

•

Out_{L\to}(w)=Out_{L\to}(w_{2})
, i.e.,
w^{*}
and
w_{2}^{*}
coincide from their last visit to
L
, in particular their last visit points in
L
coincide;

where
w,w_{1}
and
w_{2}
are representatives of
w^{*}
,
w_{1}^{*}
and
w_{2}^{*}
respectively.

To construct the coupling, we use twice the result stated in (
87
) and established during the proof of Theorem
10
, which we formulate in this way: for a large enough
L
, there is a way, given a Poisson point process
\omega_{entry}
under
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
to pick a point
X
in
\omega_{entry}
such that the couple
(\omega_{entry}-\delta_{X},X)
is at total variation distance at most
\epsilon/2
from the product law
\mathbb{P}(\mathrm{Hin}_{\to L}\in\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)\otimes p_{L}^{K,x}
, and similarly with
x
replaced by
y
.

So, consider the Poisson point process
\omega^{\prime}
distributed under
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
. Among the entry points to
L
of the trajectories in
\omega^{\prime}
, we pick a point
X
such as prescribed above, and denote the corresponding trajectory
w_{1}^{*}
. Among the exit points of
\omega^{\prime}-\delta_{w_{1}^{*}}
, also pick a point
Y
such as described above and denote the corresponding trajectory
w_{2}^{*}
. Furthermore, sample (under the law of the MC
X
) a trajectory inside
L
that links
X
and
Y
and leaves trace
\eta
on
K
. Set
w^{*}
to be the trajectory that coincides with
w_{1}^{*}
upp to its entry in
L
, is given inside
L
by the previously sampled trajectory, and coincides with
w_{2}^{*}
after its last visit to
L
. Finally set
\omega
to be
\omega^{\prime}-\delta_{w_{1}^{*}}-\delta_{w_{2}^{*}}+\delta_{w^{*}}
.

Let us finally derive the lemma. For every realisation of the coupling, we pick (arbitrarily) a trajectory
w_{add}\in W_{L}
such that
Out_{\to L}(w_{add})=Out_{\to L}(w_{2})
and
Out_{L\to}(w_{add})=Out_{L\to}(w_{1})
and we set
w_{add}^{*}=\pi(w_{add})
.
We observe that, when the coupling is successful, we have
\widetilde{\mathrm{Out}}_{L}(\omega+\delta_{w_{add}^{*}})=\widetilde{\mathrm{Out}}_{L}(\omega^{\prime})
. It follows that for every increasing event
E
belonging to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\delta_{\gamma})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{P}(\omega\in E)

(96)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(\omega+\delta_{w_{add}^{*}}\in E)

\displaystyle\leq\mathbf{P}(\omega^{\prime}\in E)+\epsilon

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)+\epsilon.

Since
\epsilon
is arbitrary, the lemma follows.
∎

Proof of Theorem
12
.

From Lemma
26
, we derive that for every finite vertex set
K
, for every admissible configuration
\eta
inside
K
, for every admissibe path
\gamma
inside
K
, for every increasing event
E
belonging to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
, we have:

\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta+\delta_{\gamma})\leq\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta).

(97)

This relies on the observation that if
E
is increasing and belongs to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
, then for every (finite) configuration
\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}
, with
k\in{\mathbb{N}}
, and
w_{i}^{*}\in W^{*}
, the event

\left\{\omega\in\Omega:\omega+\sum_{i=1}^{k}\delta_{w_{i}^{*}}\in E\right\}

(98)

is also increasing and also belongs to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
. Just apply this fact to the configuration arising from
\eta
to derive (
97
) from Lemma
26
.

By induction, it follows from (
97
) that for every finite vertex set
K
, for every admissible configuration
\eta
inside
K
, for every increasing event
E
belonging to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
, we have:

\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)\leq\mathbb{P}(E|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0).

(99)

We finally observe that the converse inequality holds, since
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=\eta)
dominates
\mathbb{P}(\cdot|\mathrm{Ins}_{K}=0)
. So we have the equality in (
99
) and we deduce that any increasing event
E
belonging to
\widetilde{\mathcal{T}}_{RI}
is independent of
\mathrm{Ins}_{K}
for every finite vertex set
K
, and therefore is independent of
\mathcal{A}
, thus also of itself, so that
\mathbb{P}(E)\in\{0,1\}
.
∎
