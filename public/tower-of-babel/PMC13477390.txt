Magnetic Resonance Imaging–Based Psoas Muscle Volume as an Additional Risk Factor for Fontan Failure: A Prospective Cohort Study
Authors: Sukran Erdem, Tarique Hussain, Orhan Erdem, Mohammed Qasim Mehdi, Ryan Butts, Franz Gerald Greil, Tarek Alsaied, Qing Zou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSarcopenia is prevalent in patients with Fontan circulation and may contribute to functional decline and adverse outcomes. It is commonly assessed using the cross‐sectional area of the psoas muscle (PMA), although volumetric assessment of psoas muscle volume (PMV) may better capture true muscle mass. The ability of PMA versus PMV to differentiate patients with Fontan circulation from healthy controls and to provide incremental prognostic information remains incompletely defined.MethodsIn this prospective study, abdominopelvic magnetic resonance imaging was used to quantify PMA and PMV in 70 patients with Fontan circulation and 51 healthy controls with similar age and sex distribution. PMA at the fourth lumbar level and total PMV were indexed to body surface area. Group differences were assessed using nonparametric testing. Prognostic associations were evaluated using multivariable logistic regression.ResultsMale patients with Fontan circulation had lower PMV and PMA indexed to body surface area than healthy male patients (P=0.029 and P=0.008, respectively), while no significant differences were observed among female patients (PMV indexed to body surface area, P=0.073; PMA indexed to body surface area, P=0.135). In multivariable analysis, Fontan‐associated liver disease, lower serum total protein, and oxygen saturation were independently associated with Fontan failure. PMV indexed to body surface area demonstrated a modest inverse association with Fontan failure and modestly improved model discrimination when added to the clinical base model (cross‐validated area under the receiver operating characteristic curve, 0.84 versus 0.81), whereas PMA indexed to body surface area did not improve predictive performance.ConclusionsPMV indexed to body surface area may provide incremental prognostic information and serve as an adjunctive imaging biomarker of sarcopenia in Fontan circulation, although its independent predictive value for adverse outcomes requires further validation in larger cohorts.

Sarcopenia is clinically characterized by the coexistence of reduced muscle mass and impaired muscle function. 1 This deficit contributes to frailty, reduced mobility, and increased morbidity among affected individuals. The high prevalence of skeletal muscle wasting and intrinsic metabolic abnormalities has been demonstrated in patients with cardiovascular diseases, including heart failure and congenital heart disease. 2

Generally, in clinical practice, muscle wasting or sarcopenia is diagnosed by measuring the cross‐sectional area of the psoas muscle at the lumbar third or fourth level on computed tomography images. 3 , 4 , 5 Recent studies suggest that measuring total psoas muscle volume (PMV) rather than cross‐sectional area yields a more precise diagnosis of sarcopenia and better correlates with adverse outcomes in various clinical situations. While cross‐sectional area has been widely used, volumetric assessment may better capture muscle bulk and metabolic reserve, offering superior prognostic value. 6 , 7 , 8

The Fontan procedure is a palliative surgery used in patients with a functional or anatomic single ventricle unsuitable for biventricular repair. 9 It is associated with chronically elevated systemic venous pressure, and skeletal and inspiratory muscle function is critical in facilitating venous return to the pulmonary arteries. Patients with Fontan circulation usually have an unfavorable body composition characterized by reduced muscle mass and increased adiposity. 10 , 11

This study aimed to compare magnetic resonance imaging (MRI)‐derived PMV and cross‐sectional area at the fourth lumbar vertebral level between patients with Fontan circulation and healthy controls, to determine which metric more effectively differentiates muscle characteristics between cohorts. Additionally, we hypothesized that PMV would exhibit a stronger association with Fontan circulatory failure.

Methods

Data Availability

The data that support the findings of this study are available from the corresponding author upon reasonable request.

Study Participants

This study was reviewed and approved by the University of Texas Southwestern Medical Center's Institutional Review Board. All participants were fully informed of the study procedures before participation, and written informed consent was obtained. This was a prospective observational study with a primarily cross‐sectional analysis of MRI‐derived psoas muscle indices. Psoas muscle area (PMA) and PMV were measured at a single time point at the time of abdominopelvic MRI. Clinical outcome data related to Fontan failure (FF) were obtained through review of available electronic medical records. Because this was not a study with protocolized longitudinal follow‐up visits, follow‐up frequency was not standardized.

Seventy patients with Fontan palliation and 51 healthy controls were enrolled from May 2023 to August 2025. Children and young adults with Fontan palliation who underwent contrast‐enhanced abdominal and pelvic MRI as part of their clinically indicated cardiovascular magnetic resonance examination were included in the study. The exclusion criteria were patients with a functional single ventricle who had not completed Fontan palliation, congenital heart disease other than a functional single ventricle, growth or thyroid hormone disorders, cancer, eating disorders, and primary malabsorption syndromes.

Electronic medical records were reviewed to obtain baseline demographic variables, including age, sex, body surface area, and body mass index. Fontan‐specific variables included the number of years since Fontan surgery and the type of Fontan connection. Fontan failure in this study is defined broadly as symptomatic insufficiency of Fontan circulation resulting in ≥1 of the following events: death, heart transplantation, Fontan revision/takedown, protein‐losing enteropathy or plastic bronchitis, or New York Heart Association Functional Classification class III or IV at last follow‐up. 12 Advanced Fontan‐associated liver disease (FALD) was defined as the presence of cirrhosis demonstrated on abdominal ultrasound and confirmed by an attending radiologist or hepatologist, or by the presence of ≥2 abnormal findings on abdominal ultrasound or MRI, including cirrhosis, ascites, hepatomegaly, heterogeneous liver parenchyma, hyperechoic lesions, surface nodularity, or splenomegaly. 13 Cardiovascular magnetic resonance results were reported quantitatively for ventricular function, end‐diastolic volume index, aortopulmonary collateral percentage, and severity of atrioventricular valve regurgitation. Blood test results were collected within 6 months of the abdominal MRI.

Additionally, healthy controls were recruited from research volunteers selected to achieve a similar age and sex distribution to the Fontan cohort. Controls had no history of cardiovascular disease, demonstrated normal cardiovascular magnetic resonance findings, and had no chronic systemic disease on the basis of review of electronic medical records. Individuals with chronic diseases were excluded. Comparability for age and sex was established at the group level rather than by individual pairwise matching; caliper‐based and bin‐based age matching were not used.

Imaging Protocol

All scans were performed on a clinical whole‐body 1.5T MR system (Ingenia Philips Healthcare, Best, Netherlands). The protocol included a postcontrast abdominopelvic MRI (Ferumoxytol) using the 3‐dimensional T1‐weighted gradient echo sequence to visualize abdominal and pelvic vascular structures in patients with Fontan palliation, and a non–contrast‐enhanced 3‐dimensional T2‐weighted gradient echo sequence with fat saturation for the control group. Image processing and reformatting were performed with commercially available analysis software Cvi42 (Release 5.17.0, Circle, Calgary, AB, Canada).

PMV and PMA Measurement

The PMA was measured using the method described by Ebbeling et al. 5 In this approach, the borders of the right and left psoas muscles are manually traced at the fourth lumbar level on axial abdominal images. The sum of the right and left PMAs at this level was then normalized to body surface area to obtain the PMA index (PMAi).

The PMV was quantified by manually delineating its borders on axial abdominal and pelvic MRI images across the entire muscle using image processing software that automatically computed the volume as the product of the traced area and slice thickness (Figure 1). The volumes of the right and left psoas muscles were summed and then indexed to body surface area. The PMV and PMA measurements were performed blindly, without access to participant‐identifying information or group assignment (Fontan versus control).

Psoas muscle segmentation and volumetric reconstruction.

Representative axial images from a ferumoxytol‐enhanced 3‐dimensional T1‐weighted gradient‐echo sequence are shown in the left column. The entire psoas muscle was divided into contiguous thin axial slices without interslice gaps, and the psoas muscle borders were manually delineated on each slice (left and middle columns). These segmented slices were then used by the image‐processing software to automatically reconstruct the 3‐dimensional psoas muscle volume and calculate volumetric measurements on the basis of the traced muscle areas and slice thickness (right column).

Statistical Analysis

Statistical analyses were conducted using Python (Python Software Foundation, Wilmington, DE), with a significance threshold set at P<0.05. Quantitative variables were tested for normality with the Shapiro–Wilk test. Normally distributed variables were compared using Welch's t test, whereas nonnormally distributed variables were compared using the Mann–Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using Fisher's exact test or the χ2 test, as appropriate.

Effect sizes were calculated using the rank‐biserial correlation, a nonparametric measure suitable for comparing 2 independent groups. Rank‐biserial correlation values range from −1 to +1, with values closer to ±1 indicating stronger effects. In this study, rank‐biserial correlation was used to quantify the magnitude of differences in PMV index (PMVi) and PMAi between groups, providing context beyond statistical significance.

Candidate variables associated with existing FF status were evaluated using univariate logistic regression and penalized regression modeling. Variables with >20% missingness were excluded, except for prespecified key indexed muscle variables (PMVi and PMAi). Remaining missing values were imputed using K‐nearest neighbors imputation (k=5, distance‐weighted). Potential multicollinearity among candidate variables was assessed by examining pairwise correlations. Variables demonstrating high correlation and overlapping variables were not entered simultaneously into the multivariable model.

Univariate logistic regression was first performed for each candidate predictor separately. To reduce the risk of excluding potentially relevant variables in this modest sample size, a liberal screening threshold of P<0.10 was used for candidate selection. Candidate variables were subsequently entered into a penalized logistic regression model using least absolute shrinkage and selection operator (LASSO) regression with 5‐fold stratified cross‐validation and area under the receiver operating characteristic curve (AUC) optimization. The optimal penalty parameter selected by cross‐validation corresponded to λ=0.234. Variables retained by LASSO were subsequently evaluated in a generalized linear model with backward elimination (P<0.05) to derive the final parsimonious base model.

Model performance was evaluated using 5‐fold cross‐validation and summarized using the AUC, accuracy, Brier score, and precision‐recall AUC. Additional models incorporating PMVi and PMAi were compared against the final base model. Nested logistic regression models were additionally compared using likelihood ratio (LR) testing.

Model diagnostics were performed for the final logistic regression models. Multicollinearity was assessed using variance inflation factors. Linearity of continuous predictors in the logit was evaluated using restricted cubic spline terms and LR testing. Influential observations were assessed using Cook's distance and standardized residuals. Calibration was evaluated using Brier scores and calibration plots based on cross‐validated predicted probabilities. Lower Brier scores indicated better calibration and overall probabilistic accuracy.

As a sensitivity analysis, we performed best‐subset logistic generalized linear model selection using the Bayesian information criterion (BIC) to verify the LASSO‐guided baseline model. Candidate predictors were the same noncollinear baseline variables considered in the LASSO analysis. Models with up to 5 predictors were evaluated, and the model with the lowest BIC was identified.

Reproducibility of PMVi measurements was evaluated by assessing intra‐ and interobserver variability. Agreement between repeated measurements was quantified using intraclass correlation coefficients, Bland–Altman analysis (mean bias and limits of agreement), and coefficients of variation.

Results

Study Population and Baseline Characteristics

After excluding 5 patients with Fontan palliation due to endocrine/genetic abnormalities, the final study cohort included 70 patients with Fontan circulation (37 male patients; median age, 15 [range, 9–28] years) and 51 healthy controls (27 male patients; median age, 17 [range, 9–26] years). Both groups had a similar mean age (16.2±4.9 versus 16.3±4.6 years) and comparable anthropometric measures (Table 1). When comparing the age distribution between the Fontan and control groups by sex, no significant differences were observed. Among female patients, the mean age was 15.7±5.1 years in the Fontan group and 16.7±5.2 years in the control group (P=0.50). For male patients, the mean age was 16.5±4.8 years in the Fontan group and 16.0±4.1 years in the control group (P=0.65).

Comparison of Muscle Indices Between Patients With Fontan Circulation and Controls

PMVi and PMAi demonstrated a strong positive correlation across the study cohort (r=0.90, P<0.001). The PMVi and PMAi differed significantly between patients with Fontan circulation and healthy controls (P=0.018 and P=0.016, respectively); however, the effect size was moderate (rank‐biserial correlation= −0.25 and −0.26; Figure 2A). The analysis was repeated separately for female and male patients using PMVi and PMAi. Male patients with Fontan palliation had significantly lower PMVi (184.3 versus 217.0 cm3/m2) and PMAi (1467.3 versus 1720.1 mm2/m2) compared with healthy male controls (P=0.029 and P=0.008, respectively; Figure 2B). In contrast, this comparison was not statistically significant for female patients (P=0.073 for PMVi and P=0.135 for PMAi) (Figure 2C).

Reproducibility of PMVi Measurements

Reproducibility of PMVi measurements was excellent (Table 3). Intraobserver variability demonstrated excellent agreement, with an intraclass correlation coefficient of 0.996 (95% CI, 0.99–1.00). Bland–Altman analysis showed a mean bias of 1.2 units, with limits of agreement ranging from −6.4 to 8.8 units (Figure 3A). The coefficient of variation was 1.5%.

Predictors of FF

FF occurred in 15 of 70 patients (21%). Before multivariable modeling, univariate logistic regression analyses were performed to assess the association of each candidate variable with FF; these results are presented in Table S2. After exclusion of variables with substantial missingness, 25 noncollinear candidate predictors remained for analysis. To evaluate whether muscle indices provide incremental prognostic value beyond established clinical variables, we constructed a series of nested logistic regression models.

In univariate logistic regression analyses, FALD, lower total protein, lower albumin, higher red cell distribution width, lower transcutaneous oxygen saturation (tO2), PMVi, and PMAi were associated with FF at P<0.10. Using this liberal screening threshold, 8 variables were entered into LASSO‐based variable selection.

LASSO retained 6 candidate predictors: FALD, total protein, albumin, red cell distribution width, tO2, and sex. Subsequent multivariable logistic regression with backward elimination identified 3 independent predictors of FF: FALD (β=2.36; P=0.004; odds ratio [OR], ≈10.6), lower total protein (β=−1.47; P=0.003; OR, ≈0.23 per unit increase), and lower tO2 (β=−0.22; P=0.012; OR, ≈0.80 per unit increase; Table 4).

The final base logistic regression model demonstrated acceptable fit, with a log‐likelihood of −23.946, residual deviance of 47.893 on 66 degrees of freedom, Pearson χ2 of 58.3 on 66 df, and Cox–Snell pseudo‐R 2 of 0.299.

Single‐predictor models using muscle indices demonstrated limited predictive performance when evaluated in isolation. For interpretability, ORs for PMVi and PMAi are reported per 1‐SD increase. PMVi showed a modest but statistically significant inverse association with FF (β=−0.017, P=0.038), with an OR per 1‐SD increase of 0.48 (95% CI, 0.24–0.96), yielding a cross‐validated AUC of 0.685 and accuracy of 0.786. PMAi demonstrated a similar directional trend (β=−0.002), although this did not reach statistical significance (P=0.057), with an OR of 0.51 (95% CI, 0.26–1.02), AUC of 0.673, and accuracy of 0.785. Overall, while both muscle indices showed a protective directionality, PMVi demonstrated the stronger and statistically significant association (Table 5).

LASSO logistic regression selected candidate predictors using an optimal cross‐validated penalty value of λ=0.23. After subsequent generalized linear model–based reduction for parsimony, the final base model retained serum total protein, tO2, and FALD. This model demonstrated good discrimination, with a cross‐validated AUC of 0.81, Brier score of 0.13, and precision‐recall AUC of 0.71. The in‐sample deviance was 47.89, with a log‐likelihood of −23.95. Addition of PMVi modestly improved model discrimination, increasing the AUC to 0.84 and improving the Brier score to 0.120. In contrast, the addition of PMAi did not improve predictive performance (AUC, 0.80; Brier score, 0.130). The combined model, including both PMVi and PMAi achieved an AUC of 0.82 with a Brier score of 0.13 (Table 5).

To further assess model robustness, we performed a BIC‐based best‐subset generalized linear model analysis. This approach selected sex, total protein, red cell distribution width, tO2, and FALD as predictors. Notably, all 3 variables retained in the LASSO‐derived baseline model (total protein, tO2, and FALD) were also selected in the BIC‐based model. The best BIC model demonstrated a log‐likelihood of −19.21, deviance of 38.41, Akaike information criterion of 50.41, and BIC of 63.90, compared with a deviance of 47.89 and BIC of 64.89 for the LASSO‐derived base model. Given the comparable BIC values despite the inclusion of 2 additional predictors, the more parsimonious 3‐variable model was retained as the primary baseline model.

LR testing demonstrated a trend toward incremental value for PMVi when added to the base model (LR statistic, 2.86; P=0.091), whereas PMAi did not significantly improve model fit (LR statistic, 0.61; P=0.44). The combined addition of PMVi and PMAi was also not statistically significant (LR statistic, 3.51; P=0.17).

Calibration plots demonstrated (Figure S1) generally acceptable agreement between predicted and observed probabilities for the multivariable models, whereas the single‐variable PMVi‐only and PMAi‐only models showed weaker discrimination and calibration performance. Overall, these findings suggest that PMVi may provide modest incremental prognostic value beyond clinical and laboratory predictors, while PMAi did not meaningfully improve model performance in this cohort.

Model diagnostics did not demonstrate substantial multicollinearity, with all variance inflation factors near 1. No influential observation was excluded after review for plausibility and data‐entry accuracy. Restricted cubic spline analyses with LR testing did not identify significant nonlinear relationships for total protein, oxygen saturation, PMVi, or PMAi across the evaluated models (Table S3); therefore, linear model specifications were retained. Calibration analyses demonstrated acceptable agreement between predicted and observed probabilities across the multivariable models.

Discussion

This study demonstrated reduced skeletal muscle indices in patients with Fontan palliation, particularly among male patients. Lower muscle mass was modestly associated with FF in this cohort, with PMVi showing a stronger association than PMAi. However, PMVi appears to provide adjunctive information related to sarcopenia and clinical risk in patients with Fontan circulation. PMVi may therefore serve as a noninvasive biomarker that complements existing clinical assessment and may help inform future risk stratification strategies. While area‐based indices may remain useful as practical diagnostic measures of sarcopenia in routine clinical practice, volumetric assessment may offer additional insight as more efficient and automated volumetric assessment tools become widely available.

Multiple studies, including the present investigation, have demonstrated reduced skeletal muscle mass in patients with Fontan circulation compared with healthy controls. 15 , 16 , 17 , 18 Tran et al further characterized an unfavorable body composition in young adults with Fontan physiology, defined by increased adiposity and decreased muscle mass, and introduced the term Fontan‐associated myopenia. 18 Several studies, including ours, have reported that the most pronounced muscle mass deficit occurs in male patients. 15 , 18 , 19

To determine whether the observed sex differences could be attributed to variations in baseline clinical or physiological factors, we compared key characteristics between male and female patients with Fontan circulation. Ventricular function, tO2, Fontan duration, and systemic ventricular type were similar between sexes, and age distributions were comparable. The only notable difference was a higher frequency of FF in male patients (11 versus 4). Collectively, these findings suggest that the sex‐related differences in muscle mass are unlikely to be explained by baseline clinical characteristics alone. Instead, sex‐based differences in muscle physiology might offer a plausible explanation. 20 Female skeletal muscle contains a higher proportion of mitochondria‐rich fibers that are relatively resistant to disease‐related catabolic loss, and estrogen promotes mitochondrial health and muscle regeneration. Pataky et al demonstrated that female skeletal muscle shows extensive estradiol‐associated transcriptional activity even when circulating estradiol levels are low. 21 Given the chronic reduction in cardiac output and impaired oxygen delivery characteristics of Fontan physiology, this enhanced mitochondrial efficiency may allow female patients to preserve muscle mass more effectively under sustained circulatory stress. 20 Conversely, male skeletal muscle exhibits greater growth potential due to testosterone‐mediated satellite cell proliferation but is more dependent on adequate testosterone levels for maintaining muscle mass. Chronic illness, conditions associated with lower circulating testosterone or reduced hormone delivery to tissues, can therefore lead to disproportionately greater muscle loss in male patients. 20 , 21

Prior work has also demonstrated that skeletal muscle deficits are prognostically significant in the Fontan population. Shiina et al reported that reduced PMV predicts hospitalization for heart failure in adults with Fontan circulation, suggesting that sarcopenia may adversely influence heart failure outcomes. 22 Given the absence of a subpulmonary ventricle and the reliance of Fontan physiology on skeletal and respiratory muscle function to augment venous return, 23 diminished muscle mass may represent an important determinant of adverse clinical trajectories in this population. Our observation that PMVi outperformed PMAi in a multivariable model further suggests that PMVi may be a more clinically informative marker of skeletal muscle deficits in this setting. However, whether sarcopenia primarily reflects global deterioration or actively contributes to worsening hemodynamics remains uncertain and warrants prospective validation.

FF has been variably described in the literature, and the hemodynamics of FF remain incompletely understood, with multiple modes of deterioration observed in children and adults. 14 In our cohort, FALD, low serum protein levels, and lower resting tO2 distinguished patients with FF from those with stable clinical progression, consistent with prior studies linking hepatic dysfunction, portal hypertension, altered protein homeostasis, and cyanosis to adverse outcomes in Fontan populations. 24 , 25 , 26 , 27 , 28 , 29 , 30 , 31 The consistency between the LASSO‐guided and BIC‐based generalized linear model approaches supports the robustness of total protein, oxygen saturation, and FALD as baseline predictors, although variable selection remains limited by the small sample size. Although underlying mechanisms were not assessed in the present study, these findings support serum protein and resting oxygen saturation as readily available clinical indicators of worsening Fontan physiology. The relatively modest performance of the single‐predictor models further suggests that skeletal muscle indices alone may not fully capture the complexity of FF risk and may be more informative when interpreted alongside established clinical predictors. Together, the coexistence of reduced muscle mass with extracardiac manifestations of Fontan physiology underscores the multisystem nature of late Fontan morbidity.

Limitations and Future Directions

Several limitations should be considered when interpreting these findings. Although PMVi showed a modest association with adverse outcomes, this was a single‐center study with a moderate sample size, limiting generalizability and precluding definitive conclusions on its independent prognostic value. The sample size also limits the stability of multivariable variable‐selection approaches, including LASSO. Therefore, LASSO was used primarily to guide parsimonious model development rather than to establish definitive independent predictors. Variable selection findings should be interpreted cautiously and validated in larger multicenter cohorts.

Nutritional status, dietary content, and habitual physical activity, all of which can influence skeletal muscle size and exercise capacity, were not assessed and may have confounded the observed associations.

FF represents a heterogeneous clinical syndrome with multiple phenotypes. Because of the limited number of patients with FF within each subtype, phenotype‐specific analyses were not feasible. Larger studies will be needed to determine whether skeletal muscle indices differ across distinct FF phenotypes.

Although reproducibility was excellent, manual PMVi segmentation is time consuming and underscores the need for scalable automated segmentation approaches. Standardized age‐ and sex‐specific cutoff values for sarcopenia based on PMV have not been established in either patients with Fontan palliation or healthy populations. Muscle strength, a key component of sarcopenia, was not assessed.

Future work should validate PMVi as an imaging‐derived marker of sarcopenia in multicenter, prospective studies across different populations and imaging platforms. Further studies should also define standardized PMV thresholds, incorporate functional assessments, and evaluate longitudinal changes in PMVi in patients with Fontan circulation with and without FF.

Conclusions

This study demonstrates that the PMV measurement is feasible using abdominopelvic MRI, showing a strong correlation with the PMA. Preliminary findings suggest that the low PMV was modestly associated with FF in this cohort and appeared to provide greater prognostic information than the PMA. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory rather than definitive PMV and may serve as an adjunctive imaging‐derived marker of sarcopenia and clinical risk in patients with Fontan circulation. Further validation in larger, multicenter studies incorporating functional assessments is needed to clarify its role in risk stratification and clinical decision making.
