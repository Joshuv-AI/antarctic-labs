Introduction

Congenital pseudoaneurysm of the mitral-aortic intervalvular fibrosa (P-MAIVF) is exceptionally rare, and prenatal recognition remains challenging.1,2 Advances in fetal imaging and earlier screening have increased detection,3 yet the natural history and role of postnatal multimodality imaging to guide clinical decision-making remain poorly defined.

Case presentation

A 32-year-old underwent fetal echocardiography at 26 weeks for a suspected atrial mass. Genetic screening was normal. A 10 × 12 mm isolated saccular structure with echogenic walls and central echolucency was identified between the atria, at atrioventricular junction close to the septum primum, expanding in systole and collapsing in diastole. Colour Doppler demonstrated a to-and-fro flow through a narrowed left ventricular outflow tract (LVOT) ostium. No additional cardiac abnormalities were identified, and cardiac function was normal (Figure 1).

The differential diagnosis included an isolated congenital coronary artery fistula, aorto-left ventricular tunnel, aneurysm of the sinus of Valsalva, and cardiac tumors. Ultimately, based on the findings, a diagnosis of P-MAIVF was suspected. Follow-up echocardiography at 32 weeks showed stability, and vaginal delivery was planned.

A male infant was born at 39 weeks, weighing 3.5 kg. Clinical evaluation and electrocardiogram were normal. Echocardiography confirmed the prenatal diagnosis. The lesion (11 × 14 mm) was located within the fibrous structure between the non-coronary and left aortic cusps, and the anterior mitral leaflet. Subcostal short-axis views demonstrated the posterior projection between the left and right atria. It communicated with the LVOT through a narrow 4-mm ostium with to-and-fro flow. There was no evidence of thrombus formation, fistula, valvar regurgitation, or outflow tract obstruction. (Figure 2)

He was discharged on day 2 with echocardiographic surveillance every 3 months. At 12 months, the pseudoaneurysm showed progressive enlargement (Figure 3A). Cardiac computed tomography (CT) confirmed a rounded formation (20 × 17 mm) at the level of the mitral-aortic intervalvular fibrosa, with contrast enhancement like the left ventricle. The ostium was enlarged (9 × 7 mm). There was a thinning of a portion of the pseudoaneurysm wall near the left atrium, consistent with a potential fistula. No mural thrombus was visualized. A relative mass effect on the atrial septum and the mitral kinetics was noted. (Figure 3B–D)

Given progression and focal wall thinning, surgical resection was performed to prevent potential complications, despite the child being asymptomatic. Three-dimensional (3D) reconstruction was valuable in confirming the exact location, size and extension, and guiding surgical management. (Figure 4). Intraoperatively, the lesion arised from the central ﬁbrous area of the LVOT with an 8-mm ostium. The defect was closed with an autologous pericardial patch, and the postoperative course was uneventful, with discharge on day 3. Six-month follow-up showed no recurrence and normal cardiac function.

Discussion

We present, to our knowledge, the first reported case of congenital P-MAIVF followed from prenatal diagnosis to surgery using a multimodality imaging approach. This anomaly is rare, mostly described in adults as an acquired lesion, typically occurring after cardiac surgery or as a complication of infective endocarditis.1,4 Congenital cases have been reported, but they are even rarer and challenging to diagnose in children.3,5–7 This defect can be isolated or associated with other congenital heart disease.8 Prenatal diagnosis is challenging due to its rarity, and few cases have been reported.2,3,9

The mitral-aortic intervalvular fibrosa is the fibrous continuity between the mitral and the aortic valves. This avascular structure is adjacent to the LVOT, where the anterior mitral leaflet becomes continuous with the non-coronary cusp of the aortic valve, and is bound by the left and right fibrous trigones. This area is susceptible to infections and trauma, and congenital forms are likely due to a weakness of the fibrosa with herniation of the endocardium resulting from high pressure in the left ventricle.1,10 Complications of P-MAIVF may occur, such as compression of surrounding structures, fistula to the atria, thrombus formation, or embolization.2,3,11,12 Management is variable, ranging from surveillance to surgical resection, depending on the patient’s age, clinical presentation, whether the lesion is isolated, and the presence of complications.5,6

In our case, surgical resection was performed due to progressive enlargement and features suggesting a potential risk of fistula formation into the left atrium. Rupture in the left atrium, which can result in heart failure, has been reported in adults, underscoring the importance of considering surgery even in asymptomatic patients.11 Surgical correction is typically achieved by direct suture or closure with a patch.5 Advanced imaging modalities, CT and resonance imaging, provide additional value in guiding management.8 In our patient, 3D reconstruction highlighted the potential risk of fistula formation into the left atrium, emphasizing the need for close monitoring in all cases. Congenital pseudoaneurysms of the mitral-aortic intervalvular fibrosa can be identified prenatally, and multimodality imaging may support decision-making and longitudinal surveillance for potential complications.
