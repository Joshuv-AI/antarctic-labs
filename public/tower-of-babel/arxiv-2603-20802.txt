Curvature bounds, regularity and inextendibility of spacetimes
Tobias Beran, John Harvey, Clemens Sämann

Abstract
We provide a completely new relation between curvature bounds and definiteness of the causal character of maximizers by exploiting the robust notion of synthetic curvature. This enables us to relate low-regularity inextendibility of spacetimes to unboundedness of curvature — which is at present unattainable using classical methods — thereby strengthening and complementing the results of [ 19 ] significantly. Keywords: nonsmooth spacetime geometry, general relativity, Lorentzian length spaces, inextendibility, curvature bounds. 2020 MSC: Primary: 53C50, Secondary: 51K10, 53C23, 53B30, 53C80, 83C99.

Introduction

A long-standing problem in general relativity is the development of a precise definition of a singularity. From a mathematical point of view Penrose suggested that a singularity is an incomplete causal geodesic, i.e., the world line of an observer that suddenly stops existing (or suddenly comes into existence). This definition has the advantage of being rigorously defined and Penrose, and then Hawking, used this for their famous
singularity theorems

[
32
,
20
]
. The disadvantage is that there is no clear implication to physical singularities, i.e., a blow-up of curvature or the existence of a horizon. To partly remedy this one should only consider
inextendible
spacetimes, i.e., spacetimes that cannot be viewed as proper subsets of another spacetime of the same dimension (which is called
the extension
).

However, should one consider smooth extensions only or should one allow extensions of low regularity? Both from a physical perspective and from the mathematical one, the latter is appropriate as low regularity extensions can still be non-singular. Sbierski recently invigorated this line of research by establishing the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-inextendibility of Schwarzschild spacetime
[
36
,
35
]
and e.g.
[
16
,
37
,
39
]
. The
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-inextendibility of FLRW-spacetimes has also been studied
[
17
]
, as have been boundary extensions
[
38
,
18
]
.

Recently there has been substantial progress in a
synthetic approach
to Lorentzian geometry and general relativity. In particular, it became possible to use curvature (bounds) in highly non-smooth situations (e.g.
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-spacetimes) or spaces that are not manifolds (discrete spaces, causal sets
[
42
,
10
]
etc.). See
[
14
,
34
,
26
,
9
]
for an overview of this line of research. The main idea is to describe curvature in a robust way by using just (Lorentzian) distances (i.e., time separation) and volumes. Such a perspective proved to be remarkably fruitful in the Riemannian case and led to the development of the fields of
metric geometry
(e.g. Alexandrov and
\mathsf{CAT}(k)
-spaces
[
11
,
12
,
3
]
) and
metric measure geometry
(e.g.
\mathsf{(R)CD}
-spaces
[
23
,
41
,
43
]
). The former generalizes sectional curvature bounds to metric spaces; the latter generalizes Ricci curvature bounds to metric spaces with a reference measure (i.e., the volume). In the Lorentzian case
timelike sectional curvature bounds
have been introduced in
[
21
]
and
timelike Ricci curvature bounds
in
[
15
]
building on
[
24
,
30
]
.

Now, the significant advantage of having a synthetic notion of curvature is that one has a notion of curvature at one’s disposal even for non-smooth extensions of classical smooth spacetimes, e.g.
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-extensions. Restricting to classical techniques does not at present allow us to investigate whether low regularity extensions have bounded curvature. A proof-of-concept of this was established in
[
19
]
but there has not been any follow-up since then. The goal of this article is to revitalize this line of research.

We give a completely novel result showing that
regularity
, i.e., that maximizers are either timelike or null, follows from timelike or causal curvature bounded below under mild causality assumptions.
From this we prove, among other results, that a smooth and strongly causal spacetime which is timelike geodesically complete cannot be extended as a weakly normal Lorentzian pre-length space having causal or timelike curvature bounded below.

Note that these results contrast to
[
19
]
where
upper
curvature bounds and the full strength of
Lorentzian length spaces
were used. Here we only work with
Lorentzian pre-length spaces
(see Section

for the precise definitions). Furthermore, a correction to

2
Lorentzian pre-length spaces and curvature bounds

We briefly recall the basics of Lorentzian pre-length spaces
[
21
]
and take new developments into account. In particular, there is no need to fix a particular background metric. It is enough to demand that the topology is metrizable and finer than the chronological topology, i.e., the one generated by the chronological futures and pasts (see below). This point of view has recently been taken in
[
29
]
. Moreover, it is enough to consider an extended time separation function and derive the timelike and causal relations from it. This has been advocated by McCann
[
25
]
and used several times since then, see e.g.
[
8
,
4
]
. Moreover there are other approaches e.g. the bounded Lorentzian metric spaces
[
27
,
13
]
of Minguzzi–Suhr(–Bykov) or the null distance of Sormani–Vega
[
40
]
.

Let
X
be a set and let
\ell\colon X\times X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
satisfy the
reverse triangle inequality
, i.e.,

(1)

\ell(x,y)+\ell(y,z)\leq\ell(x,z)\qquad\forall x,y,z\in X\,,

employing the convention that
-\infty+\infty=\infty+(-\infty)=-\infty
. Then, the
timelike

\ll
and
causal
\leq
relations
are
\ll:=\ell^{-1}((0,\infty])
and
\leq:=\ell^{-1}([0,\infty])
. We write
x<y
for
x\leq y
and
x\neq y
. Analogously to the classical spacetime case, the
chronological
and
causal future (past)
of
x\in X
are

\displaystyle I^{+}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in X:x\ll y\}\,,\qquad I^{-}(x):=\{y\in X:y\ll x\}\,,

\displaystyle J^{+}(x)

\displaystyle:=\{y\in X:x\leq y\}\,,\qquad J^{-}(x):=\{y\in X:y\leq x\}\,,

and the
chronological
and
causal diamonds
are
I(x,y):=I^{+}(x)\cap I^{-}(y)
,
J(x,y):=J^{+}(x)\cap J^{-}(y)
. Having the chronological relation at hand we define the
chronological topology
as the topology on
X
generated by the sub-base of chronological futures and pasts
I^{\pm}(x)
(
x\in X
). Then a Lorentzian pre-length space is defined as follows.

Definition 2.1

(Lorentzian pre-length space)
.

Let
X
be a set,
\ell\colon X\times~X\rightarrow\{-\infty\}\cup[0,\infty]
as above and
\ell(x,x)\geq 0
for all
x\in X
. If
X
is given a topology that is finer than the chronological one, then
(X,\ell)
is a
Lorentzian pre-length space
. Also,
\tau:=\max(0,\ell)
is the
time separation function
and
\ell
is called the
extended time separation function
.

Having now the causal relations at hand, we can define a
timelike
or
causal
curve as a continuous curve that is totally ordered by
\ll
and
\leq
, respectively. Then the length of a causal curve
\gamma\colon[a,b]\rightarrow X
is

(2)

\displaystyle L_{\tau}(\gamma):=\inf\Bigl\{\sum_{i=1}^{N}\tau(\gamma(t_{i-1}),\gamma(t_{i})):a=t_{0}<t_{1}<\ldots t_{N}=b\Bigr\}\,.

A
(causal) maximizer
is a causal curve whose length saturates the time separation between its endpoints, i.e.,
\tau(\gamma(a),\gamma(b))=L_{\tau}(\gamma)
. We also write
[x,y]
for a choice of maximizer from
x
to
y
. Moreover,
\tau
(and hence
(X,\ell)
) is
intrinsic
if

(3)

\displaystyle\ell(x,y)=\sup\bigl\{L_{\tau}(\gamma):\gamma\text{ causal from }x\text{ to }y\bigr\}\,.

A Lorentzian pre-length space is
future distinguishing
if
I^{+}(x)=I^{+}(y)
implies
x=y
for all
x,y\in X
. Analogously, it is called
past distinguishing
if
I^{-}(x)=I^{-}(y)
implies
x=y
. It is called
distinguishing
if it is both future and past distinguishing, cf.
[
28
, Def. 4.59]
. It is
locally (past/future) distinguishing
if for each neighbourhood
U
of
x
and
y
, the same holds when intersecting with
U
. We call
X

not locally timelike isolating
if for all neighbourhoods of all
x\in X
, both
I^{+}(x)\cap U
and
I^{-}(x)\cap U
are non-empty
[
33
, Def. 2.7]
. These causality assumptions will be used in Section
3
to obtain regularity from lower curvature bounds.

While locally distinguishing obviously implies distinguishing, the converse is also true for
timelike path-connected
spaces, i.e., spaces where all
x\ll y
can be connected by a timelike curve. For an example of a space that fails to be distinguishing but is not locally timelike isolating consider the Lorentz cylinder, i.e.,
\mathbb{R}\times[0,1]
mod
(t,0)\sim(t+1,1)
. For an example that is locally distinguishing but locally timelike isolating it suffices to consider a closed slim diamond in Minkowski spacetime, e.g.
\{(x,t):t\geq 0,|x|\leq t/2\}\cap\{(x,t):t\leq 1,|x|\leq(1-t)/2\}
.

A Lorentzian pre-length space is
strongly causal
if its (metrizable) topology is generated by the sub-base of chronological diamonds
I(x,y)
for
x,y\in X
. A strongly causal Lorentzian length space is distinguishing by
[
1
, Prop. 3.17]
. However, for Lorentzian pre-length spaces the situation is a bit more subtle.
In any case, in this paper the results establishing regularity rely directly on the distinguishing condition rather than on strong causality.

Finally, a Lorentzian pre-length space is called
regular
if every maximizer is either timelike or null.

Moreover, we introduce here a new notion, that of
weakly normal neighborhoods
, generalizing normal (or convex) neighborhoods for smooth spacetimes and localizing neighborhoods of
[
21
]
. We do not need the full power of the latter as we will see.

Definition 2.2

(Weakly normal neighborhood)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
p\in X
. A neighborhood
U
of

is

if

(i)

the time separation function
\tau
is finite on
U

\tau\rvert_{U\times U}<\infty

(ii)

maximizers exist, i.e., for all

there is a causal maximizer from
x
to
y
,

(iii)

and it is not locally timelike isolating, i.e.,
I^{\pm}(p)\cap U\neq\emptyset

Furthermore, we call
(X,\ell)

weakly normal
if every point has a weakly normal neighborhood.

Note that the maximizer required to exist by point (ii) need not be contained in
U
. One could also demand this or demand additionally that it is only maximizing among all curves in

(similar to localizing neighborhoods, cf.
[
21
, Def. 3.16]
) but this will not be needed for our purposes. Also note all previous notions of curvature comparison neighborhoods demand the existence of maximizers and
\tau
finite-valued there, cf. Definition
2.4
and
[
21
,
7
,
6
]
. Thus all spaces with curvature bounds (except the one for discrete spaces where existence of maximizers is not required) are weakly normal. Moreover, we have the following compatibility result.

Lemma 2.3

(Intrinsic, strongly causal and localizing implies weakly normal)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a strongly causal and localizing Lorentzian pre-length space that is intrinsic, then it is weakly normal.

Proof:
.

We can follow the argument of
[

, which we provide here for convenience: Let
p\in X
and
\Omega

, which is finite-valued. Strong causality implies that there is a neighbourhood

such that all causal curves with endpoints in

(via

At this point we recall curvature bounds for Lorentzian pre-length spaces, see
[
21
,
6
]
for more details. We will focus on

and the so-called

. The two-dimensional Lorentzian model spaces of constant curvature

\mathbb{L}^{2}(k):=\tilde{S}^{2}_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}})

\mathbb{L}^{2}(0):=\mathbb{R}^{2}_{1}

\mathbb{L}^{2}(k):=\tilde{H}^{2}_{1}(\frac{1}{\sqrt{-k}})

D_{k}:=\frac{\pi}{\sqrt{-k}}
if

otherwise. Here
\tilde{S}^{2}_{1}(r)
is the simply connected covering manifold of the two-dimensional Lorentzian pseudosphere of radius
r>0
(

\mathbb{R}^{2}_{1}
is two-dimensional Minkowski spacetime and
\tilde{H}^{2}_{1}(r)

is anti-de Sitter space).

A
timelike triangle
is a triple
\Delta(x,y,z)
of timelike related points

with a choice of maximizing sides. A
comparison triangle
of
\Delta(x,y,z)
is a timelike triangle
\Delta(\bar{x},\bar{y},\bar{z})
in

\Delta(x,y,z)

\tau(x,z)<D_{k}

and this is unique up to isometry of

[

(Triangle comparison)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
k\in\mathbb{R}

\geq k
-comparison neighborhood

(i)

\tau

(ii)

D_{k}

x
to
y
in
U

(iii)

Let
\Delta(x,y,z)

\tau(p,q)\leq\bar{\tau}(\bar{p},\bar{q})
.

Then
(X,\ell)
has

k
(in the triangle comparison sense
if every point has a
\geq k

Definition 2.5

(Four-point condition)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space and
k\in\mathbb{R}
. A
\geq k
-comparison neighborhood

U\subseteq X
such that for every causal four-point configuration
(y,x,z_{1},z_{2})

(z_{2},z_{1},x,y)

3
Curvature and Regularity

We now establish that a lower curvature bound implies regularity. This complements
[
21
, Prop. 4.15]
for curvature bounded above but dispenses with the unnatural assumption of local timelike geodesic connectedness (see the corrected arXiv-version
doi.org/10.48550/arXiv.1711.08990
).

We show this in three different settings. First we use causal curvature bounded below, in the strict causal 4-point sense. Then we use timelike curvature bounded below, both in the four-point sense and in the triangle comparison sense.

Curvature comparison conditions related to angles and hinges require regularity as part of the definition, as does the
\tau
-convexity definition. The results here show that conditions related to triangle comparison and four-point conditions force regularity under the mild causality assumptions of not locally isolating and locally distinguishing. It now follows that these assumptions can replace regularity in the equivalence proofs of the different characterisations of lower curvature bounds from
[
6
]
.
These local causality conditions can be replaced with weaker global conditions if the spaces are assumed to have global curvature bounds.

. However, we do use the fact that the strict causal sense does not require the causal relationship
z_{1}\leq z_{2}
. It is this additional strength of the strict condition that means we do not require the assumption that
X
is not locally isolating, which is used in the rest of this section.

Two weaker versions of the four-point curvature condition are curvature bounded below in the timelike four-point sense and in the (non-strict) causal four-point sense. Here we show that for these conditions, regularity is implied under the additional assumption that the space is not locally isolating.

Theorem 3.2

(4-point TLCBB implies regularity)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space that is locally distinguishing and not locally isolating.
If
X
has timelike curvature bounded below (TLCBB) (in the timelike 4-point sense) and has
\tau
continuous within small enough comparison neighborhoods, then
X
is regular.

Proof:
.

As in the previous proof, we can suppose towards a contradiction that there is a maximizer
\gamma\colon[0,1]\rightarrow X
with
\gamma(0)\ll\gamma(t_{0})\not\ll\gamma(1)
contained in a small enough
\geq k
-comparison neighborhood
U
. We want to show that
I^{+}(x)\cap U=I^{+}(\gamma(1))\cap U
. As before,
I^{+}(\gamma(1))\subseteq I^{+}(x)
is immediate.

Let
V\subset U
be a neighborhood of
\gamma(1)
and let
z_{1}\in I^{+}(\gamma(1))\cap V
. Such a point exists since
X
is not locally isolating.
Let
z_{2}\in J^{+}(z_{1})\cap U
. Note that
z_{2}\in I^{+}(\gamma(1))\cap U
.

Let
(\bar{y},\bar{x},\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})
be a four-point comparison configuration in
\mathbb{L}^{2}(k)
, where
k\in\mathbb{R}
is the timelike curvature bound.
Since comparison configurations vary continuously with the side lengths and as
\tau
is continuous in
U
, we have that as
V\searrow\{\gamma(1)\}
the comparison configurations converge. As in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, in the limit we have that
\bar{z}_{1}\to\bar{x}
.

Now by the reverse triangle inequality and curvature comparison, we have

(8)

\displaystyle\tau(x,z_{2})

\displaystyle\geq\tau(x,z_{1})+\tau(z_{1},z_{2})\geq\tau(z_{1},z_{2})

(9)

\displaystyle\geq\bar{\tau}(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})=\bar{\tau}(\bar{x},\bar{z}_{2})-\varepsilon(V)=\tau(x,z_{2})-\varepsilon(V)\,,

where as
V\searrow\{\gamma(1)\}
and
\bar{z}_{1}\to\bar{x}
, we have
\varepsilon(V)\to 0
.
Continuity of
\tau
yields
\tau(z_{1},z_{2})\to\tau(\gamma(1),z_{2})
, hence in the limit we obtain

(10)

\displaystyle\tau(x,z_{2})\geq\tau(\gamma(1),z_{2})\geq\tau(x,z_{2})\,.

For any point
z\in I^{+}(\gamma(1))\cap~U
, if we require that
V\subseteq I^{-}(z)
,
z
will be in
J^{+}(z_{1})
. Thus we have shown that
\tau(x,z)=\tau(\gamma(1),z)
for all
z\in I^{+}(\gamma(1))\cap~U
.

More generally, let
z\in I^{+}(x)\cap U
. Set
t_{1}=\sup\{t:\tau(\gamma(t),z)>0\}
. In particular,
t_{1}>t_{0}
and for all
t\in[t_{0},t_{1})
,
z\in I^{+}(\gamma(t))
. By the above
\tau(x,z)=\tau(\gamma(t),z)
and hence, as a function of
t
,
\tau(\gamma(t),z)
is constant on
(t_{0},t_{1})
. Since
\tau(\gamma(t_{0}),z)>0
and
\tau
is continuous in
U
we deduce that
t_{1}=1
,
\tau(\gamma(t),z)>0
for all
t\in[t_{0},1]
, and hence
z\in I^{+}(z_{1})
. Therefore we also have
\tau(x,z)=\tau(z_{1},z)
for all
z\in I^{+}(x)\cap U
.

As in Theorem
3.1
, this gives
\tau(x,.)=\tau(z_{1},.)
on
U
and the result follows.
∎

Corollary 3.3

(Non-strict 4-point CCBB implies regularity)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space that is locally distinguishing and not locally isolating.
If
X
has causal curvature bounded below (in the non-strict causal 4-point sense) and has
\tau
continuous within small enough comparison neighborhoods, then
X
is regular.

Looking at
[
6
, Thm. 5.1]
, we see that the different versions of curvature comparison are in general only equivalent under the assumption of regularity.
The six different versions of triangle comparison are equivalent without any additional assumptions, and hence the following result applies to all forms of triangle comparison lower curvature bounds.

Theorem 3.4
.

A locally distinguishing Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)
with TLCBB in the triangle comparison sense is regular.

Proof:
.

Let
\gamma
,
y=\gamma(0)
,
x=\gamma(t_{0})
and
z_{1}=\gamma(1)
be as in Theorem
3.1
.
Let
U
be a
\geq k
-comparison neighborhood for
x
and, as in the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we can assume that
\gamma
lies entirely in
U
. We claim that
I^{+}(x)\cap U=I^{+}(z_{1})\cap U
:
I^{+}(z_{1})\subseteq I^{+}(x)
is immediate. For now let
z_{2}\in I^{+}(z_{1})\cap U
.
Consider the timelike triangle
\Delta(y,z_{1},z_{2})
with comparison point
x\in[y,z_{1}]
.
As before, we have in the comparison configuration that
\bar{x}=\bar{z}_{1}
.
Curvature comparison implies
\tau(x,z_{2})\leq\tau(\bar{x},\bar{z}_{2})=\tau(\bar{z}_{1},\bar{z}_{2})=\tau(z_{1},z_{2})
and the reverse triangle inequality gives

\tau(x,z_{2})\geq\tau(x,z_{1})+\tau(z_{1},z_{2})=\tau(z_{1},z_{2})
.
Thus
\tau(x,z)=\tau(z_{1},z)
for all
z\in I^{+}(z_{1})\cap U
.

More generally, if
z\in I^{+}(x)\cap U
, the argument of Theorem
3.2
applies. As in Theorem
3.1
, this gives
\tau(x,.)=\tau(z_{1},.)
on
U
and the result follows.
∎

4
Inextendibility versus unboundedness of curvature

We now apply the results of Section
3
to the question of inextendibility of Lorentzian pre-length spaces or spacetimes. First, we recall and slightly generalize the notion of an extension of a Lorentzian pre-length space as used in
[
19
]
.

Definition 4.1

(Extension)
.

An Lorentzian pre-length space
(\tilde{X},\tilde{\ell})
is an extension of
(X,\ell)
if:

•

\tilde{X}
is connected.

•

There is a homeomorphism
\iota\colon X\rightarrow\tilde{X}
onto a proper open subset
\iota(X)
.

•

The map
\iota
preserves the time separation function, i.e., for all
x,y\in X
we have
\tilde{\ell}(\iota(x),\iota(y))=\ell(x,y)
.

One could always extend a Lorentzian pre-length space by adding points on the
spacelike boundary
, i.e., all points
\tilde{x}
such that
\tilde{\ell}(\tilde{x},\iota(x))=\tilde{\ell}(\iota(x),\tilde{x})=-\infty
, as introduced in
[
27
]
. If the space is future or past distinguishing, then there can be only one point in the spacelike boundary as then trivially
I^{\pm}(x)=\emptyset=I^{\pm}(y)
. Moreover, being not locally timelike isolating implies that there is no spacelike boundary.

Next, we recall the
TC
-condition from
[
19
]
that works as an analogue of timelike geodesic completeness: A Lorentzian pre-length space
(X,\ell)
satisfies the
TC
-condition if every inextendible timelike maximizer has infinite length. By
[
19
, Lem. 5.2]
a smooth and strongly causal spacetime is timelike geodesically complete if and only if it satisfies the
TC
-condition.

Theorem 4.2

(Inextendibility versus regularity)
.

Let
(X,\ell)
be a Lorentzian pre-length space satisfying the
TC
-condition. Then
X
is inextendible as a regular and weakly normal Lorentzian pre-length space without spacelike boundary.

Proof:
.

Assume a regular and weakly normal extension
\iota\colon(X,\ell)\rightarrow(\tilde{X},\tilde{\ell})
without spacelike boundary exists. By assumption
\partial\iota(X)\neq\emptyset
, let
\tilde{p}\in\partial\iota(X)
and let
\tilde{U}
be a weakly normal neighborhood of
\tilde{p}
. As
\tilde{U}
is weakly normal, let
\tilde{r}\in I^{+}(\tilde{p})\cap\tilde{U}
. Consequently,
\tilde{V}:=I^{-}(\tilde{r})\cap\tilde{U}
is an open neighborhood of
\tilde{p}
. First, assume that
\tilde{r}\not\in\iota(X)
and let
\tilde{q}=\iota(q)\in\tilde{V}\cap\iota(X)
. By assumption there is a causal maximizer
\tilde{\gamma}\colon[0,1]\rightarrow\tilde{X}
from
\tilde{q}
to
\tilde{r}
, that leaves
\iota(X)
at a boundary point
\tilde{w}=\tilde{\gamma}(t)
for the minimal
t\in(0,1]
. Moreover, by regularity and since
\tilde{q}\,\tilde{\ll}\,\tilde{r}
we know that
\tilde{\gamma}
is timelike. The curve
\gamma=i^{-1}\circ\tilde{\gamma}\rvert_{[0,t)}
is timelike and inextendible in
X
. However, its length is:

\displaystyle L_{\tau}(\gamma)

\displaystyle=\lim_{s\nearrow t}L_{\tau}(\gamma_{\rvert_{[0,s]}})=\lim_{s\nearrow t}\tau(q,\gamma(s))=\lim_{s\nearrow t}\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{q},\tilde{\gamma}(s))\leq\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{q},\tilde{w})

\displaystyle\leq\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{q},\tilde{r})<\infty\,,

where we used the reverse triangle inequality to see that
s\mapsto\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{q},\tilde{\gamma}(s))
is monotonically increasing and bounded by
\tilde{\tau}(\tilde{q},\tilde{w})
. This now contradicts the
TC
-condition. Second, if
\tilde{r}=\iota(r)\in\iota(X)
we can find
\tilde{q}\in\tilde{V}\cap\tilde{X}\backslash\iota(X)
. Now a time reversed version of the argument above yields the desired contradiction.
∎

In particular we have that

Corollary 4.3
.

A strongly causal smooth spacetime (or Lorentzian length space) that is timelike geodesically complete (i.e., satisfies the
TC
-condition) is inextendible as a regular Lorentzian length space.

Finally, this means that there cannot be an extension which has causal curvature bounded below by Theorem
3.1
(or Corollary
3.3
).

Theorem 4.4
.

A locally distinguishing Lorentzian pre-length space satisfying the
TC
-condition cannot be extended as a weakly normal Lorentzian pre-length space having causal curvature bounded below.

In particular, this applies to all strongly causal timelike geodesically complete spacetimes. Also, this gives a new
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-inextendibility result, as any
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-spacetime is a weakly regular Lorentzian pre-length space by using Ling’s time separation function
[
22
]
. In
[
19
]
one needed that the
\mathcal{C}^{0}
-spacetime is causally plain to view it as a Lorentzian pre-length space.
