Stern polynomials and algebraic independence
Daniel Duverney, Iekata Shiokawa

Abstract
. Let t ≥ 2 t\geq 2 and k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 be integers. Let H k ​ ( z ) H_{k}(z) with | z | < 1 \left|z\right|<1 be the limit of a certain subsequence of the Stern polynomials introduced by Dilcher and Eriksen. We use Mahler’s method to prove the algebraic independence of the values at nonzero algebraic points of the functions H k ​ ( z ) H_{k}(z) and H k ​ ( z t k ) H_{k}(z^{t^{k}}) .



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
11J81, 11A55, 11J85.

1.
Introduction

Throughout this paper, let
t\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
be any fixed integers.
Stern’s diatomic sequence
a(n),
defined by
a(0)=0,

a(1)=1,
and for

n\geq 1

a(2n)=a(n),\quad a(2n+1)=a(n+1)+a(n),

has been studied by many authors (cf. Sequence OEIS A002487 in
[
8
]
). In
2007, Klavzar, Milutinovic and Petr introduced Stern polynomials
a(n;z)

defined by
a(0;z)=0,

a(1;z)=1
and for
n\geq 1

a(2n;z)=za(n;z),\quad a(2n+1;z)=a(n+1;z)+a(n;z).

In 2018, Dilcher and Eriksen
[
3
]
extended them to (Type 1) Stern
polynomials
a_{t}(n;z)
defined by
a_{t}(0;z)=0,

a_{t}(1;z)=1
and for

n\geq 1

(1.1)

\displaystyle a_{t}(2n;z)

\displaystyle=za_{t}(n;z^{t}),\quad

(1.2)

\displaystyle a_{t}(2n+1;z)

\displaystyle=a_{t}(n+1;z^{t})+a_{t}(n;z^{t})

whose coefficients are only
0
or
1
(they also defined in
[
3
, Sec.5]
another ’Type 2’ Stern polynomials, which however will not be
discussed in this paper). They introduced the sequence of integers

\alpha_{n}=\alpha_{n}(k)=\frac{2^{kn}-\left(-1\right)^{n}}{2^{k}+1}\quad\left(n\geq 0\right)

and proved
[
3
, Prop.6.1]
that the subsequence
\left(a_{t}(\alpha_{n};z)\right)_{n\geq 0}
satisfies the three-term recurrence relation

(1.3)

a_{t}(\alpha_{n+1};z)=a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)a_{t}(\alpha_{n};z^{t^{k}})+a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})a_{t}(\alpha_{n-1};z^{t^{2k}}),

which generates
[
3
, Prop.6.2]
the finite continued fraction

(1.4)

\displaystyle\frac{a_{t}(\alpha_{n+1};z)}{a_{t}(\alpha_{n};z^{t^{k}})}\left.=\right.a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)+\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{k}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{2k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{2k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{\left(n-1\right)k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{\left(n-1\right)k}})}.

The polynomials
a_{t}(\alpha_{n+1};z)
and
a_{t}(\alpha_{n};z)
agree to
ever higher powers of
z,
which proves
[
3
, Prop.6.4]
the existence
of the limit

H_{k}(z)=\lim_{n\rightarrow\infty}a_{t}(\alpha_{n};z),

which defines a power series converging in
\left|z\right|<1
and
having only
0
or
1
as coefficients. Letting
n\rightarrow\infty
in

\left(\ref{Rec}\right)
and
\left(\ref{CF1}\right),
we find that

H_{k}(z)
satisfies the functional equation

(1.5)

H_{k}(z)=a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)H_{k}(z^{t^{k}})+a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})H_{k}(z^{t^{2k}}).

This generates
[
3
, Prop.6.5]
the infinite continued fraction

(1.6)

\displaystyle\frac{H_{k}(z)}{H_{k}(z^{t^{k}})}\left.=\right.a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)+\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{k}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{2k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{2k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{nk}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots},

which converges in
\left|z\right|<1,
where by
[
3
, Lem.6.1]

(1.7)

a_{t}(2^{k};z)=z^{\frac{t^{k}-1}{t-1}},\quad a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)=\sum_{i=1}^{k}z^{\frac{t^{k}-t^{i}}{t-1}}.

By
\left(\ref{Rec2}\right),

f_{1}(z)=H_{k}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)=H_{k}(z^{t^{k}})
satisfy the system of functional equations

(1.8)

\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}f_{1}(z^{t^{k}})\\
f_{2}(z^{t^{k}})\end{array}\right)=A(z)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}f_{1}(z)\\
f_{2}(z)\end{array}\right),

where

(1.9)

\displaystyle A(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})}B(z),\quad

\displaystyle B(z)

\displaystyle=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}0&a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}})\\
1&-a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z)\end{array}\right).

Our main result is

Theorem 1.1
.

For any algebraic number
\alpha
with
0<\left|\alpha\right|<1,
the numbers
H_{k}(\alpha)
and
H_{k}(\alpha^{t^{k}})

are algebraically independent.

As an application, we get

Corollary 1.1
.

The continued fraction

\frac{H_{k}(\alpha)}{H_{k}(\alpha^{t^{k}})}\left.=\right.a_{t}(2^{k}-1;\alpha)+\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};\alpha^{t^{k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;\alpha^{t^{k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};\alpha^{t^{nk}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;\alpha^{t^{nk}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}

is transcendental for algebraic
\alpha
with
0<\left|\alpha\right|<1,

Example 1.1
.

The following continued fractions are transcendental.

\displaystyle\frac{H_{1}(\alpha)}{H_{1}(\alpha^{t})}

\displaystyle=1+\frac{\alpha^{t}}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{\alpha^{t^{2}}}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{\alpha^{t^{3}}}{1}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots},

\displaystyle\frac{H_{2}(\alpha)}{H_{2}(\alpha^{t^{2}})}

\displaystyle=1+\alpha^{t}+\frac{\alpha^{t^{2}+t^{3}}}{1+\alpha^{t^{3}}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{\alpha^{t^{4}+t^{5}}}{1+\alpha^{t^{5}}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{\alpha^{t^{6}+t^{7}}}{1+\alpha^{t^{7}}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots},

\displaystyle\frac{H_{3}(\alpha)}{H_{3}(\alpha^{t^{3}})}

\displaystyle=1+\alpha^{t^{2}}+\alpha^{t+t^{2}}+\frac{\alpha^{t^{3}+t^{4}+t^{5}}}{1+\alpha^{t^{5}}+\alpha^{t^{4}+t^{5}}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{\alpha^{t^{6}+t^{7}+t^{8}}}{1+\alpha^{t^{8}}+\alpha^{t^{7}+t^{8}}}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}.

Our proof of Theorem
1.1
in Section
2
is an application of
Mahler’s transcendence method
[
7
]
to continued fractions, as
previously developed by Ku. Nishioka
[
7
, Section 5.2]
and Adamczewski

[
1
]
.

Another application of Mahler’s method to the transcendence of continued
fractions has been proposed by Bundschuh and Väänänen, and
Corollary
1.1
above could be also deduced from
[
2
, Th.1.1]
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.1

For the proof we prepare four lemmas.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
A(z)
be defined in
\left(\ref{Mat1}\right).
Then

0
is the only pole of
A(z).

Proof.

Immediate consequence of
\left(\ref{at1}\right).

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

H_{k}(1/2)/H_{k}(1/2^{t^{k}})
is irrational.

Proof.

By
\left(\ref{at1}\right),
we have

(2.1)

d_{n}:=\deg a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{nk}})=\deg a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})+t^{nk}=t^{nk}\frac{t^{k}-1}{t-1}.

Replacing
z
by
1/2
in
\left(\ref{CF2}\right)
yields

\displaystyle\frac{H_{k}(1/2)}{H_{k}(1/2^{t^{k}})}

\displaystyle=a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2)+\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{2k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{2k}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{3k}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{3k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{nk}})}{a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{nk}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots}

\displaystyle=a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2)+\frac{2^{d_{1}}a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{k}})}{2^{d_{1}}a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{k}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+}\frac{2^{d_{2}}a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{2k}})}{2^{d_{2}-d_{1}}a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{2k}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots+}\frac{2^{d_{n}}a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{nk}})}{2^{D_{n}}a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{nk}})}\genfrac{}{}{0.0pt}{}{{}}{+\cdots},\qquad

where
D_{n}=d_{n}-d_{n-1}+d_{n-2}-\cdots+\left(-1\right)^{n-1}d_{1}.

We note that
2^{d_{n}}a_{t}(2^{k};1/2^{t^{nk}})=1
and

2^{D_{n}}a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1/2^{t^{nk}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0},

since
D_{n}-\deg a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})>0
for all
n\geq 1.
Indeed, this
is true for
n=1
by
\left(\ref{Deg}\right)
since
D_{1}=d_{1},
and
true also by
\left(\ref{Deg}\right)
for
n\geq 2
since

\displaystyle D_{n}-\deg a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})\geq d_{n}-d_{n-1}-\left(d_{n}-t^{nk}\right)

\displaystyle=t^{nk}-t^{(n-1)k}\frac{t^{k}-1}{t-1}>0.

Hence
H_{k}(1/2)/H_{k}(1/2^{t^{k}})
is an infinite regular continued
fraction, which is known to be irrational.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

H_{k}(z)
is transcendental over
\mathbb{C}(z).

Proof.

We appeal to Fatou’s theorem
[
4
]
:
A power series whose
coefficients take only finitely many values is rational or transcendental.
As
the coefficients of
H_{k}(z)
are only
0
or
1,

H_{k}(z)
is rational or
transcendental. If
H_{k}(z)\in\mathbb{C}\left(z\right),
then

H_{k}(\alpha)\in\mathbb{Q}
for each
\alpha\in\mathbb{Q}
since
H_{k}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}\left[\left[z\right]\right],
and so
H_{k}(1/2)/H_{k}(1/2^{t^{k}})\in\mathbb{Q}
, which contradicts Lemma

2.2
. Therefore
H_{k}(z)\notin\mathbb{C}\left(z\right)
and is transcendental.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

The power series
f_{1}(z)=H_{k}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)=H_{k}(z^{t^{k}})
are algebraically independent over
\mathbb{C}(z).

Proof.

We apply
[
7
, Th. 5.2]
. By
\left(\ref{Mat}\right)
and Lemma

2.3
, the assumptions of the theorem are satisfied. Assume that

f_{1}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)
are algebraically dependent over
\mathbb{C}\left(z\right).
Then, setting

(2.2)

G^{(n)}(z)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}G_{11}^{(n)}(z)&G_{12}^{(n)}(z)\\
G_{21}^{(n)}(z)&G_{22}^{(n)}(z)\end{array}\right)=B(z^{t^{(n-1)k}})\cdots B(z^{t^{k}})B(z),

there exists
n_{0}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
such that at least one of the following
three conditions holds.

(i)
G_{12}^{(n)}(z)=0
for
n=n_{0},2n_{0},3n_{0},
…

(ii)
G_{21}^{(n)}(z)=0
for
n=n_{0},2n_{0},3n_{0},
…

(iii) There exist
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
and coprime
a(z),b(z)\in

\mathbb{C}\left[z\right]
satisfying

(2.3)

\frac{b(z)}{a(z)}=\frac{b(z^{t^{nk}})G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})+a(z^{t^{nk}})G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})}{b(z^{t^{nk}})G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})+a(z^{t^{nk}})G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})}

for
n=n_{0},2n_{0},3n_{0},\ldots.

We will prove that none of the properties (i)-(iii) is fulfilled, and Lemma

2.4
will follow by contradiction.

Let us prove first that neither the property (i) nor (ii) is fulfilled. By

\left(\ref{MatG}\right)
and
\left(\ref{Mat2}\right)
we have

(2.4)

\displaystyle G_{11}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=0,\text{ }G_{12}^{(1)}(z)=a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{k}}),\text{
}

(2.5)

\displaystyle G_{21}^{(1)}(z)

\displaystyle=1,\text{ }G_{22}^{(1)}(z)=-a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z),

and for
n\geq 1

(2.6)

\displaystyle G_{11}^{(n+1)}(z)\left.=\right.a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{(n+1)k}})G_{21}^{(n)}(z),\quad

(2.7)

\displaystyle G_{12}^{(n+1)}(z)\left.=\right.a_{t}(2^{k};z^{t^{(n+1)k}})G_{22}^{(n)}(z),

(2.8)

\displaystyle G_{21}^{(n+1)}(z)\left.=\right.G_{11}^{(n)}(z)-a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})G_{21}^{(n)}(z),

(2.9)

\displaystyle G_{22}^{(n+1)}(z)\left.=\right.G_{12}^{(n)}(z)-a_{t}(2^{k}-1;z^{t^{nk}})G_{22}^{(n)}(z).

Since
a_{t}(2^{k};0)=0
and
a_{t}(2^{k}-1;0)=1
by
\left(\ref{at1}\right),
we get from (
2.4
)-(
2.9
)

(2.10)

\displaystyle G_{11}^{(n)}(0)

\displaystyle=G_{12}^{(n)}(0)=0\quad\quad\left(n\geq 1\right),

(2.11)

\displaystyle G_{21}^{(n)}(0)

\displaystyle=-G_{22}^{(n)}(0)=\left(-1\right)^{n-1}\quad\quad\left(n\geq 1\right).

By (
2.11
),
G_{21}^{(n)}(z)\neq 0
and
G_{22}^{(n)}(z)\neq 0
for
n\geq 1.
Hence this yields
G_{12}^{(n)}(z)\neq 0
for
n\geq 2
by (
2.7
), and so
(i) and (ii) cannot hold.

Now assume that (iii) is fulfilled. Since
a(z)
and
b(z)
are coprime, we
see by
\left(\ref{Quot}\right)
that there exists
\kappa^{(n)}(z)\in\mathbb{C}\left[z\right]
such that

(2.12)

\displaystyle b(z^{t^{nk}})G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})+a(z^{t^{nk}})G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})

\displaystyle=\kappa^{(n)}(z)b(z),

(2.13)

\displaystyle b(z^{t^{nk}})G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})+a(z^{t^{nk}})G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})

\displaystyle=\kappa^{(n)}(z)a(z).

Thus by Cramer’s formulas

\displaystyle b(z^{t^{nk}})\Delta_{n}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{(n)}(z)\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}b(z)&G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})\\
a(z)&G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})\end{array}\right|,

\displaystyle a(z^{t^{nk}})\Delta_{n}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\kappa^{(n)}(z)\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})&b(z)\\
G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})&a(z)\end{array}\right|,

where

\Delta_{n}\left(z\right):=\left|\begin{array}[c]{cc}G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})&G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})\\
G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})&G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})\end{array}\right|.

By
\left(\ref{MatG}\right)
and
\left(\ref{Mat2}\right),
we have

\displaystyle\Delta_{n}\left(z\right)

\displaystyle=\det\left(B(z^{rt^{(n-1)k}})\cdots B(z^{rt^{k}})B(z^{r})\right)

\displaystyle=\left(-1\right)^{n}a_{t}(2^{k};z^{rt^{kn}})\cdots a_{t}(2^{k};z^{rt^{k}}).

Hence by
\left(\ref{at1}\right)

\Delta_{n}\left(z\right)=\left(-1\right)^{n}z^{\delta_{n}},

where
\delta_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}_{>0}
. So if
\kappa^{(n)}(\alpha)=0
for some

\alpha\neq 0,
then
a(\alpha^{t^{nk}})=b(\alpha^{t^{nk}})=0.
This is
impossible since
a(z)
and
b(z)
are coprime. Consequently

(2.14)

\kappa^{(n)}(z)=c_{n}z^{u_{n}},\quad c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast},\quad 0\leq u_{n}\leq\delta_{n}.

We distinguish two cases.

First case.
If
a(0)\neq 0,
then putting
z=0
in (
2.13
), we
get
\kappa^{(n)}(0)=\left(-1\right)^{n}
by (
2.10
) and (
2.11
). By
(
2.14
), this yields

(2.15)

\kappa^{(n)}(z)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\qquad\left(a(0)\neq 0\right).

Second case.
Let
a(0)=0.
Then

\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})&G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})\\
G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})&G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}b(z^{t^{nk}})\\
a(z^{t^{nk}})\end{array}\right)=\kappa^{(n)}(z)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}b(z)\\
a(z)\end{array}\right)

by (
2.12
) and (
2.13
), whence

(2.16)

\frac{\Delta_{n}(z)}{\kappa^{(n)}(z)}\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}b(z^{t^{nk}})\\
a(z^{t^{nk}})\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[c]{cc}G_{22}^{(n)}(z^{r})&-G_{21}^{(n)}(z^{r})\\
-G_{12}^{(n)}(z^{r})&G_{11}^{(n)}(z^{r})\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[c]{c}b(z)\\
a(z)\end{array}\right).

Taking (
2.14
) into account, let us define

(2.17)

C^{(n)}(z):=\frac{\Delta_{n}(z)}{\kappa^{(n)}(z)}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{c_{n}}z^{\delta_{n}-u_{n}}=\frac{\left(-1\right)^{n}}{c_{n}}z^{v_{n}},

with
c_{n}\in\mathbb{C}^{\ast}
and
0\leq v_{n}\leq\delta_{n}.
Since

a(0)=0,
putting
z=0
in (
2.16
) yields by (
2.11
)

C^{(n)}(0)b(0)=G_{22}^{(n)}(0)b(0)=\left(-1\right)^{n}b(0).

As
a(z)
and
b(z)
are coprime,
b(0)\neq 0
and so
C^{(n)}(0)=\left(-1\right)^{n}.
By (
2.17
), this yields

\frac{\Delta_{n}(z)}{\kappa^{(n)}(z)}=C^{(n)}(z)=\left(-1\right)^{n}.

Consequently

(2.18)

\kappa^{(n)}(z)=\left(-1\right)^{n}\Delta_{n}(z)\qquad\left(a(0)=0\right).

Now in both cases we see by (
2.15
) and (
2.18
) that
\left|\kappa^{(n)}(1)\right|=1.
Substituting
z=1
into (
2.12
) and
(
2.13
), we get

(2.19)

\displaystyle\left|b(1)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|b(1)G_{11}^{(n)}(1)+a(1)G_{21}^{(n)}(1)\right|,

(2.20)

\displaystyle\left|a(1)\right|

\displaystyle=\left|b(1)G_{12}^{(n)}(1)+a(1)G_{22}^{(n)}(1)\right|.

However we have
a_{t}(2^{k};1)=1
and
a_{t}(2^{k}-1;1)=k
by
\left(\ref{at1}\right)
and so by (
2.6
)-(
2.9
)

\displaystyle G_{11}^{(n+1)}(1)

\displaystyle=G_{21}^{(n)}(1),

\displaystyle G_{12}^{(n+1)}(1)

\displaystyle=G_{22}^{(n)}(1),

\displaystyle G_{21}^{(n+1)}(1)

\displaystyle=G_{11}^{(n)}(1)-kG_{21}^{(n)}(1),

\displaystyle G_{22}^{(n+1)}(1)

\displaystyle=G_{12}^{(n)}(1)-kG_{22}^{(n)}(1).

Hence for every

The characteristic equation

\gamma=\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}-k}{2},\quad\delta=-\frac{\sqrt{k^{2}+4}+k}{2}.

Thus there exists real constants

Substituting these into (
2.20
) and (

), we get

\displaystyle\left|a(1)\right|\left.=\right.\left|b(1)\left(c_{12}\gamma^{n}+d_{12}\delta^{n}\right)+a(1)\left(c_{22}\gamma^{n}+d_{22}\delta^{n}\right)\right|,

\displaystyle\left|b(1)\right|\left.=\right.\left|b(1)\left(c_{11}\gamma^{n}+d_{11}\delta^{n}\right)+a(1)\left(c_{21}\gamma^{n}+d_{21}\delta^{n}\right)\right|.

Noting that
0<\gamma<1
and
\left|\delta\right|>1,
we deduce that

a(1)=b(1)=0
by letting
n\rightarrow\infty
. This is impossible since
a(z)

and
b(z)
are coprime, and (iii) is not true.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Theorem
1.1
is an
immediate consequence of
[
7
, Th. 4.2.1]
. Indeed, the series

f_{1}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)\in\mathbb{Q}\left[\left[z\right]\right]

converge in
\left|z\right|<1
and satisfy the functional equation

\left(\ref{Mat}\right).
Moreover,
\alpha
is a nonzero algebraic number
and so
\alpha^{t^{nk}}
is not a pole of
A(z)
for all
n\geq 1
by Lemma

2.1
. Since
f_{1}(z)
and
f_{2}(z)
are algebraically independent
over
\mathbb{C}\left(z\right)
by Lemma
2.4
,
f_{1}(\alpha)

and
f_{2}(\alpha)
are algebraically independent by
[
7
, Th. 4.2.1]
.
