Sumit Roy

Abstract
. Let X X be a compact Riemann surface of genus g ≥ 2 g\geq 2 , and let D ⊂ X D\subset X be a fixed finite subset. We prove the semiprojectivity of the moduli space of semistable symplectic or orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles over X X . 1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a compact Riemann surface of genus
g\geq 2
, and let
D\subset X
be a finite subset. Parabolic bundles over
(X,D)
were introduced by Mehta and Seshadri in
[
11
]
in order to extend the Narasimhan–Seshadri correspondence to irreducible unitary representations of
\pi_{1}(X\setminus D)
. A parabolic bundle consists of a holomorphic vector bundle together with a weighted flag at each point of
D
. Bhosle and Ramanathan extended this notion to parabolic principal
G
-bundles, for
G
a connected reductive group, and constructed the corresponding moduli spaces in
[
5
]
.

A symplectic (resp. orthogonal) parabolic bundle is a parabolic bundle equipped with a suitably defined nondegenerate alternating (resp. symmetric) bilinear form with values in a holomorphic line bundle; see
[
2
]
. When the parabolic weights are rational, these objects can be interpreted in terms of parabolic principal
G
-bundles for
G=\mathrm{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
(resp.
G=\mathrm{SO}(n,\mathbb{C})
), again in the sense of
[
2
]
. Thus the symplectic and orthogonal parabolic cases naturally fit into the general framework of parabolic principal bundles.

A Higgs bundle on
X
is a pair
(E,\tau)
, where
E
is a holomorphic vector bundle and
\tau
, called the Higgs field, is a holomorphic
1
-form with values in
\mathrm{End}(E)
satisfying
\tau\wedge\tau=0
. On a compact Riemann surface, this is equivalent to a holomorphic morphism

\tau:E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\otimes K,

where
K
is the canonical bundle of
X
. Higgs bundles were introduced by Hitchin in
[
7
]
in his study of the self-duality equations on a Riemann surface. A parabolic Higgs bundle on
(X,D)
is a pair
(E_{*},\Phi)
, where
E_{*}
is a parabolic bundle and

\Phi:E\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E\otimes K(D)

is a parabolic Higgs field. Yokogawa constructed the moduli space

\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)

of semistable parabolic Higgs bundles of fixed rank
n
, degree
d
, and parabolic weights
\alpha
in
[
18
]
. The coefficients of the characteristic polynomial
\det(x\cdot I-\Phi)
define the Hitchin morphism

h:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}:=\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(X,K(D)^{i}),

which is proper; see
[
13
,
10
]
. The geometry of this morphism plays a central role in the study of Higgs bundles.

The aim of this paper is to study symplectic and orthogonal analogues of parabolic Higgs bundles from two related perspectives. The first concerns the global geometry of the corresponding moduli spaces, while the second concerns a Hitchin-theoretic characterization of very stability.

We begin with semiprojectivity. Recall that a quasi-projective variety
Z
equipped with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action is called
semiprojective
if the fixed point locus
Z^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
is proper and, for every
z\in Z
, the limit

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}t\cdot z

exists in
Z
. In the context of Higgs bundles, the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action is given by scaling the Higgs field. Semiprojectivity is a useful property because it provides control over the behaviour of this action and relates the geometry of the moduli space to that of the fixed point locus and the nilpotent cone. It also allows one to apply techniques such as Białynicki–Birula type decompositions in the study of the topology of these spaces. Semiprojectivity for moduli spaces of parabolic Higgs bundles was established in
[
17
]
. Our first result extends this to the symplectic and orthogonal settings.

Theorem 1.1
.

The moduli spaces

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha),\qquad\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha),\qquad\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha),

and their strongly parabolic analogues are semiprojective.

The second part of the paper is motivated by the notion of very stability. In the non-parabolic case, Pauly and Peón-Nieto proved in
[
14
]
that a stable vector bundle is very stable if and only if the Hitchin morphism associated to it is proper. Peón-Nieto extended this result to the parabolic setting in
[
15
]
, and Zelaci obtained an analogous criterion for principal
G
-bundles in
[
20
]
. Our goal is to obtain a corresponding result for symplectic parabolic bundles.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a symplectic parabolic bundle. We denote by

\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}=H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{SPEnd}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr)

the vector space of strongly parabolic symplectic Higgs fields on
(E_{*},\varphi)
. If
(E_{*},\varphi)
is stable, then each element of
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
determines a stable strongly parabolic symplectic Higgs bundle, and hence defines a morphism

h_{E,\mathrm{st}}:\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}

by composition with the symplectic Hitchin morphism. We say that
(E_{*},\varphi)
is
strongly very stable
if it admits no nonzero nilpotent strongly parabolic symplectic Higgs field. The following theorem provides a characterization of this condition.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a stable symplectic parabolic bundle. Then the following are equivalent:

\displaystyle(E_{*},\varphi)\ \textnormal{is strongly very stable}

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\ \textnormal{is finite}

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\ \textnormal{is proper}

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\ \textnormal{is quasi-finite}.

Thus, in the symplectic parabolic setting, strong very stability is completely determined by the geometry of the associated Hitchin morphism. We also indicate the corresponding statement in the orthogonal case (see Remark
4.10
).

The proofs of semiprojectivity rely on the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariance of the Hitchin morphism together with its properness. The very-stability criterion is obtained by combining the homogeneity of the Hitchin map with the linear structure of the space
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
.

The results of this paper suggest several directions for further study. One may investigate the topology and cohomology of these moduli spaces using their semiprojective structure, or study the geometry of the corresponding Hitchin fibres in the symplectic and orthogonal parabolic settings. It would also be natural to extend these results to more general parabolic principal
G
-Higgs bundles.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Parabolic bundles

Let us choose a set
D
of
r
distinct points
p_{1},...,p_{r}
on
X
.We will keep this set
D
fixed throughout this paper.

Definition 2.1
.

A
parabolic bundle

E_{*}
of rank
n
over the curve
X
is a rank
n
holomorphic vector bundle
E
over
X
with an additional structure (call the
parabolic structure
) along
D
, i.e. for every
p\in D
, we have

(1)

a descending filtration of subspaces of the fiber over
p

E_{p}\eqqcolon E_{p,1}\supsetneq E_{p,2}\supsetneq\dots\supsetneq E_{p,n_{p}}\supsetneq E_{p,n_{p}+1}=\{0\},

(2)

an ascending sequence of real numbers between
0
and
1
satisfying

0\leq\alpha_{1}(p)<\alpha_{2}(p)<\dots<\alpha_{n_{p}}(p)<1,

where
n_{p}
is an integer between
1
and
n
.

Here, the real numbers
\alpha_{i}(p)
’s are called the
parabolic weights
assigned to the subspace
E_{p,i}
, for all
p\in D
. A
quasi-parabolic structure
on
E
consists of flags (without the parabolic weights) over all points of
D
. For a fixed parabolic structure, we call the set of all parabolic weights by

\alpha=\{(\alpha_{1}(p),\alpha_{2}(p),\dots,\alpha_{n_{p}}(p))\}_{p\in D}.

The parabolic structure
\alpha
is said to have
full flags
if, for all
i
and for all
p\in D
:

\mathrm{dim}(E_{p,i}/E_{p,i+1})=1,

or equivalently
n_{p}=n
for all
p\in D
.

In
[
12
]
, Maruyama and Yokogawa provided a different way to define parabolic bundles using coherent sheaves. This approach helps define parabolic tensor products and parabolic duals.

Definition 2.2
.

The
parabolic degree
of a parabolic bundle
E_{*}
is defined by

\operatorname{pardeg}(E_{*})\coloneqq\deg(E)+\sum\limits_{p\in D}\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n_{p}}\alpha_{i}(p)\cdot\dim(E_{p,i}/E_{p,i+1}),

where
\deg(E)
is the degree of the underlying vector bundle
E
, and the
parabolic slope
of
E_{*}
is defined by

\mu_{\mathrm{par}}(E_{*})\coloneqq\frac{\text{pardeg}(E_{*})}{\mathrm{rank}(E)}.

Definition 2.3
.

A
parabolic subbundle

F_{*}
of a parabolic bundle
E_{*}
is a holomorphic subbundle
F\subset E
of the underlying bundle
E
, which also has a parabolic structure induced on it. This induced parabolic structure on
F
is defined as follows: for every
p\in D
, the filtration in
F_{p}
is given by

F_{p}\eqqcolon F_{p,1}\supsetneq F_{p,2}\supsetneq\dots\supsetneq F_{p,n^{\prime}_{p}}\supsetneq\{0\},

where
F_{p,i}=F_{p}\cap E_{p,i}
, i.e. we are looking at the intersection with the filtration already given in
E_{p}
, and we are also removing any repeated subspaces in the sequence. The parabolic weights
0\leq\alpha^{\prime}_{1}(p)<\alpha^{\prime}_{2}(p)<\dots<\alpha^{\prime}_{n^{\prime}_{p}}(p)<1
are chosen to be the largest possible weights among those allowed after the intersections, i.e.

\alpha^{\prime}_{i}(p)=\mathrm{max}_{j}\{\alpha_{j}(p)|F_{p}\cap E_{p,j}=F_{p,i}\}=\mathrm{max}_{j}\{\alpha_{j}(p)|F_{p,i}\subseteq E_{p,j}\}

In other words, the parabolic weight associated to the subspace
F_{p,i}
is the weight
\alpha_{j}(p)
such that
F_{p,i}\subseteq E_{p,j}
but
F_{p,i}\nsubseteq E_{p,j+1}
.

Definition 2.4
.

A parabolic bundle
E_{*}
is said to be
stable
(resp.
semistable
) if for all nonzero proper parabolic subbundle
F_{*}\subset E_{*}
, we have

\mu_{\mathrm{par}}(F_{*})<\mu_{\mathrm{par}}(E_{*})\hskip 5.69046pt(\mathrm{resp.}\hskip 5.69046pt\leq).

Definition 2.5
.

A
parabolic homomorphism

\phi:E_{*}\rightarrow E^{\prime}_{*}
between two parabolic bundles is a vector bundle homomorphism between the underlying vector bundles which satisfies

\alpha_{i}(p)>\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(p)\implies\phi(E_{p,i})\subseteq E_{p,j+1}^{\prime},

at each
p\in D
. Moreover, we say a homomorphism is
strongly parabolic
if, for every
p\in D
, we have

\alpha_{i}(p)\geq\alpha_{j}^{\prime}(p)\implies\phi(E_{p,i})\subseteq E_{p,j+1}^{\prime}.

We will use
\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})
and
\mathrm{SPEnd}(E_{*})
to denote the parabolic and strongly parabolic endomorphisms of
E_{*}
, respectively.

2.2.
Parabolic Higgs bundles

Let
K
be the canonical line bundle on
X
. We define
K(D)\coloneqq K\otimes\mathcal{O}(D)
with the trivial parabolic structure (i.e. weights are zero).

Definition 2.6
.

A
parabolic Higgs bundle
on
X
consists of a parabolic bundle
E_{*}
and a parabolic homomorphism

\Phi:E_{*}\rightarrow E_{*}\otimes K(D),

i.e. for every
p\in D
we have
\Phi(E_{p,i})\subset E_{p,i}\otimes\left.K(D)\right|_{p}
. This homomorphism
\Phi
is called the
parabolic Higgs field
.

A
strongly parabolic Higgs bundle
on
X
consists of a parabolic bundle
E_{*}
together with a
strongly parabolic Higgs field

\Phi:E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E_{*}\otimes K(D),

such that for every
p\in D
,

\Phi(E_{p,i})\subset E_{p,i+1}\otimes K(D)|_{p}.

Equivalently, the residue of
\Phi
at each marked point
p\in D
is nilpotent with respect to the induced filtration on the fiber
E_{p}
. Indeed, the above condition implies that the induced endomorphism

\operatorname{Res}_{p}(\Phi):E_{p}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow E_{p}

strictly lowers the filtration, and hence is nilpotent.

For a fixed parabolic bundle
E_{*}
, the set of strongly parabolic Higgs fields on
E_{*}
is given by

H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{SPEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr),

which is a vector subspace of

H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr).

Indeed, the condition

\Phi(E_{p,i})\subset E_{p,i+1}\otimes K(D)|_{p},\qquad p\in D,

is linear in
\Phi
. In particular, the strongly parabolic condition defines a closed condition in families, and hence the strongly parabolic locus is closed in the moduli space of parabolic Higgs bundles.

Definition 2.7
.

A subbundle
F_{*}\subset E_{*}
of a parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{*},\Phi)
is called
\Phi
-invariant
if
\Phi(F_{*})\subset F_{*}\otimes K(D)
.

Definition 2.8
.

A parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{*},\Phi)
is said to be
stable
(resp.
semistable
) if for every nonzero proper
\Phi
-invariant subbundle
F_{*}\subset E_{*}
, we have

\mu_{\mathrm{par}}(F_{*})<\mu_{\mathrm{par}}(E_{*})\hskip 5.69046pt(\mathrm{resp.}\hskip 5.69046pt\leq).

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
E_{*}
and
E^{\prime}_{*}
be parabolic bundles together with the (strongly) parabolic Higgs fields
\Phi_{E}
and
\Phi_{E^{\prime}}
respectively. Then there is a (strongly) parabolic Higgs field on the tensor product
E_{*}\otimes E^{\prime}_{*}
induced by
\Phi_{E}
and
\Phi_{E^{\prime}}
. Also,
\Phi_{E}
induces a (strongly) parabolic Higgs field on the dual
E_{*}^{\vee}
.

Proof.

See
[
3
, Lemma 4]
.
∎

2.3.
Moduli of parabolic Higgs bundles and Hitchin morphism

In
[
8
]
, Konno constructed the moduli space
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)
of semistable parabolic Higgs bundles of fixed rank
n
, degree
d
, and parabolic structure
\alpha
over
X
(see also
[
18
]
,
[
4
]
for more details). It is a normal quasi-projective variety. The stable locus of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)
is an open subvariety of
\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)
and is the smooth locus. The parabolic form of Serre duality (see
[
4
]
,
[
19
]
) states that

(2.1)

H^{1}(X,\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*}))\cong H^{0}(X,\mathrm{SPEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D))^{*}.

Hence, by deformation theory, there exists an open embedding

T^{*}\mathcal{M}(n,d,\alpha){\mathrel{\rightarrow}}\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha),

where
T^{*}\mathcal{M}(n,d,\alpha)
is the cotangent bundle of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}(n,d,\alpha)
of parabolic bundles (see
[
11
]
), and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)
is the locus of strongly parabolic Higgs bundles.

2.3.1.
Parabolic Hitchin morphism

Let
\mathcal{S}
denote the total space of
K(D)
. Let
p:\mathcal{S}\rightarrow X
be the natural projection map, and let
x\in H^{0}(\mathcal{S},p^{*}K(D))
be the tautological section. Let
(E_{*},\Phi)
be a semistable parabolic Higgs bundle of rank
n
, and let

\det(x\,\mathrm{Id}-p^{*}\Phi)=x^{n}+\tilde{s}_{1}x^{n-1}+\cdots+\tilde{s}_{n}

be the characteristic polynomial of
\Phi
, where
\tilde{s}_{i}=p^{*}s_{i}
for some

s_{i}\in H^{0}\bigl(X,K^{i}(D^{i})\bigr).

Here,

K^{i}(D^{j}):=K^{\otimes i}\otimes\mathcal{O}(D)^{\otimes j}.

Sometimes we will write
K^{i}(D^{i})
as
K(D)^{i}
. Therefore, the parabolic
Hitchin morphism
is defined by

\displaystyle h:\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(X,K^{i}(D^{i}))

\displaystyle(E_{*},\Phi)

\displaystyle\longmapsto(s_{1},\dots,s_{n}),

i.e.
h
maps a parabolic Higgs field to the coefficients of its characteristic polynomial. The Hitchin morphism
h
is independent of the parabolic structure at each
p\in D
because it only relies on the Higgs field
\Phi
and
K(D)
. Moreover,
h
is a proper morphism (see
[
10
]
for details).

If
\Phi
is a strongly parabolic Higgs field, then its residue at each marked point
p\in D
is nilpotent, and hence
s_{i}\in H^{0}(X,K^{i}(D^{i-1}))
. Therefore the restriction of the Hitchin morphism to the strongly parabolic locus is given by

h_{\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq\left.h\right|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(n,d,\alpha)\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{n}H^{0}(X,K^{i}(D^{i-1})).

The fiber
h^{-1}(0)
is called the
nilpotent cone
of the Hitchin morphism
h
. One can see that
h^{-1}(0)
consists of all nilpotent Higgs fields.

2.4.
Symplectic and orthogonal parabolic bundles

By
[
2
]
, for rational parabolic weights, symplectic and orthogonal parabolic bundles can be interpreted in terms of parabolic principal
G
-bundles, where
G
is the corresponding symplectic or orthogonal group. We will use this identification throughout.

Symplectic case.

Let us consider the standard symplectic form
J
on
\mathbb{C}^{2m}
given by

J=\begin{bmatrix}0&I_{m}\\
-I_{m}&0\end{bmatrix}.

The general symplectic group
\mathrm{Gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
is defined by

\mathrm{Gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})=\{M\in\mathrm{GL}(2m,\mathbb{C}):MJM^{t}=\mu_{M}J\text{ for some }\mu_{M}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}.

It is an extension of
\mathbb{C}^{*}
by
\mathrm{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
, that is, there exists a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathrm{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{Gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{p}\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow 1,

where
p(M)=\mu_{M}
. In particular,

\det(M)=p(M)^{m}=\mu_{M}^{m}

for all
M\in\mathrm{Gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
.

The Lie algebra of
\mathrm{Gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
is given by

\mathfrak{gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})=\{A\in\mathfrak{gl}(2m,\mathbb{C}):AJ+JA^{t}=\tfrac{\mathrm{tr}(A)}{m}J\},

and admits a decomposition

\mathfrak{gp}(2m,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathfrak{sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})\oplus\mathbb{C}.

Orthogonal case.

Let
B
be a symmetric non-degenerate bilinear form on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
, and denote by the same symbol the corresponding matrix. The general orthogonal group
\mathrm{GO}(n,\mathbb{C})
is defined by

(2.2)

\mathrm{GO}(n,\mathbb{C})=\{M\in\mathrm{GL}(n,\mathbb{C}):MBM^{t}=\mu_{M}B\text{ for some }\mu_{M}\in\mathbb{C}^{*}\}.

There is a short exact sequence

1\rightarrow\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{C})\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathrm{GO}(n,\mathbb{C})\xrightarrow{q}\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow 1,

where
q(M)=\mu_{M}
. In particular,

\det(M)^{2}=q(M)^{n}.

The subgroup
\mathrm{SO}(n,\mathbb{C})\subset\mathrm{O}(n,\mathbb{C})
consists of elements with determinant
1
.

Symplectic and orthogonal parabolic bundles.

Let
L_{*}
be a parabolic line bundle over
X
, and let
E_{*}
be a parabolic bundle. Let

\varphi:E_{*}\otimes E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L_{*}

be a parabolic homomorphism. By tensoring with the dual parabolic bundle, we obtain an induced parabolic homomorphism

(2.3)

\tilde{\varphi}:E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L_{*}\otimes E^{\vee}_{*}.

Definition 2.10
.

A
symplectic parabolic bundle
consists of a pair
(E_{*},\varphi)
as above, where
\varphi
is anti-symmetric and
\tilde{\varphi}
is an isomorphism.

An
orthogonal parabolic bundle
consists of a pair
(E_{*},\varphi)
where
\varphi
is symmetric and
\tilde{\varphi}
is an isomorphism.

Given a symplectic or orthogonal parabolic bundle
(E_{*},\varphi)
, let
E
be its underlying vector bundle. Then
E\otimes E
is a coherent subsheaf of the vector bundle underlying
E_{*}\otimes E_{*}
. Thus,
\varphi
induces a vector bundle homomorphism

(2.4)

\hat{\varphi}:E\otimes E\rightarrow L,

where
L
is the underlying line bundle of
L_{*}
.

Definition 2.11
.

A subbundle
F\subset E
of the underlying vector bundle
E
of
E_{*}
is said to be
isotropic
if
\hat{\varphi}(F\otimes F)=0
.

Definition 2.12
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a symplectic or orthogonal parabolic bundle on
X
. Thus,
\varphi:E_{*}\otimes E_{*}\rightarrow L_{*}
is a bilinear form inducing an isomorphism

\tilde{\varphi}:E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L_{*}\otimes E_{*}^{\vee}.

Let

\Phi\in H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr)

be a parabolic Higgs field. By Lemma
2.9
,
\Phi
induces a Higgs field
\Phi^{\vee}
on
E_{*}^{\vee}
, and hence a Higgs field on
L_{*}\otimes E_{*}^{\vee}
. We say that
\Phi
is
compatible
with
\varphi
if

(\mathrm{Id}_{L_{*}}\otimes\Phi^{\vee})\circ\tilde{\varphi}=(\tilde{\varphi}\otimes\mathrm{Id}_{K(D)})\circ\Phi.

Remark 2.13
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a symplectic or orthogonal parabolic bundle. The bilinear form
\varphi
induces an isomorphism

\widetilde{\varphi}:E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L_{*}\otimes E_{*}^{\vee}.

A parabolic Higgs field
\Phi
is said to be compatible with
\varphi
if it corresponds, under this identification, to the induced Higgs field on
L_{*}\otimes E_{*}^{\vee}
.

Equivalently, at the level of fibers, this condition means that
\Phi
is skew-symmetric (in the symplectic case) or symmetric (in the orthogonal case) with respect to the bilinear form
\varphi
(for example see
[
6
]
for reference).

Definition 2.14

(Symplectic or orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles)
.

A
symplectic
(resp.
orthogonal
)
parabolic Higgs bundle

(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
consists of a symplectic (resp. orthogonal) parabolic bundle
(E_{*},\varphi)
and a Higgs field
\Phi
on
E_{*}
that is compatible with
\varphi
.

Definition 2.15
.

A symplectic or orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
is called
stable
(resp.
semistable
) if for all nonzero isotropic
\Phi
-invariant subbundle
F\subset E
(i.e.
F_{*}
is
\Phi
-invariant), we have

\mu_{par}(F_{*})<\mu_{par}(E_{*})\hskip 11.38092pt(\text{resp.}\hskip 4.26773pt\mu_{par}(F_{*})\leq\mu_{par}(E_{*})).

2.4.1.
Moduli space

A description of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{G}(\alpha)
of semistable parabolic
G
-bundles with fixed parabolic structure
\alpha
and rank
r
and degree
d
was given in
[
1
]
and
[
5
]
. They showed that it is a projective variety. Moreover, if the parabolic structure
\alpha
has full flags at each marked points
p\in D
, then its dimension is given by

\dim\mathcal{M}^{G}(\alpha)=\dim Z(G)+(g-1)\dim(G)+r\dim(G/B),

where
r
is the number of points in
D
and
Z(G)
is the center of
G
. The final term arises because the flags considered at each point of
D
are full flags, and
B
represents the Borel subgroup of
G
specified by
\alpha
. We will assume that the parabolic structures at all points of
D
are full flags.

If
G=\mathrm{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
or
\mathrm{SO}(n,\mathbb{C})
, then

\dim\mathcal{M}^{G}(\alpha)=(g-1)\dim(G)+r\dim(G/B).

Moreover, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{G}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable parabolic
G
-Higgs bundles is a quasi-projective variety (see
[
18
]
) and by the parabolic version of Serre duality, we know that

\dim\mathcal{M}^{G,\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)=2\dim\mathcal{M}^{G}(\alpha),

where
\mathcal{M}^{G,\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\subset\mathcal{M}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
is the strongly parabolic locus.

For
G=\mathrm{Sp}(2m,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{SO}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
, the respective moduli spaces
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
and
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
have equal dimension, and it is given by

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)=\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)=2m(2m+1)(g-1)+2m^{2}r.

For
G=\mathrm{SO}(2m,\mathbb{C})
, the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
has dimension

\dim\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)=2m(2m-1)(g-1)+2mr(m-1).

We now use these structural properties of the moduli space to study the behaviour of the Hitchin morphism under the natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action.

3.
Semiprojectivity

In this section, we prove that the moduli spaces of semistable symplectic or orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles over a smooth projective curve are semiprojective varieties.

Definition 3.1

(Semiprojective variety)
.

Let
Z
be a quasi-projective variety with a
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action
z\mapstochar\rightarrow t\cdot z
, where
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
and
z\in Z
. We say that
Z
is a
semiprojective
variety if the following conditions hold:

(1)

the limit
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}(t\cdot z)_{t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}}
exists in
Z
for all
z\in Z
.

(2)

the fixed point set
Z^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}
of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on
Z
is a proper variety.

3.1.
Semiprojectivity of the moduli space of semistable symplectic parabolic Higgs bundles

An element
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
can be interpreted as a rank
2m
parabolic bundle
E_{*}
equipped with a non-degenerate symplectic form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
(i.e., the map
\varphi
) along with a holomorphic section
\Phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D))
that satisfies

\langle\Phi v,w\rangle=-\langle v,\Phi w\rangle.

This identity is equivalent to the compatibility condition in Definition
2.12
. Therefore, the characteristic polynomial of
\Phi
is of the form

\det(x\cdot I-\Phi)=x^{2m}+s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+s_{2m},

where
\{s_{2},\dots,s_{2m}\}
is a basis for the invariant polynomials of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sp}(2m)
. Thus the Hitchin morphism for the symplectic case is given by

(3.1)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\mathrm{Sp}}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)&\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(X,K(D)^{2i})\\
\Phi&\longmapsto(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m}),\end{split}

(see
[
16
]
for more details) and its restriction to the strongly parabolic locus is given by

(3.2)

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{Sp,st}}\coloneqq\left.h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\right|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp,st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp,st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(X,K^{2i}(D^{2i-1})).

There is a natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the bases
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
of the symplectic Hitchin morphisms, given by

t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m})\coloneqq(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m}s_{2m}).

Lemma 3.2
.

The Hitchin morphism
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.

Proof.

Let
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi))=(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m})
, where
s_{i}=\mathrm{tr}(\wedge^{i}\Phi)
. Then the characteristic polynomial corresponding to
t\Phi
is

\det(x\cdot I-t\Phi)=x^{2m}+t^{2}s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+t^{2m}s_{2m}.

Thus,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\big((t\cdot(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi))\big)

\displaystyle=h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\big((E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle=(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m}s_{2m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big).

Hence,
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.
∎

We now study the behaviour of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the moduli space and show that limits exist as
t\rightarrow 0
.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
be a semistable symplectic parabolic Higgs bundle. Then
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}(E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)
exists in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Consider the morphism

\displaystyle f:\mathbb{C}^{*}

\displaystyle\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)

\displaystyle t

\displaystyle\longmapsto(E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi).

Since the Hitchin map
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\big((E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)\big)=\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}t\cdot h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big)=0.

Therefore, the composition morphism

F\coloneqq h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\circ f:\mathbb{C}^{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}

extends to a morphism

\hat{F}:\mathbb{C}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}},

since the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the Hitchin base is algebraic, given by polynomial scaling, and the above limit shows that
\hat{F}(0)=0
. Since the Hitchin map
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is proper, it satisfies the valuative criterion of properness. The morphism
f:\mathbb{C}^{*}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
corresponds to a morphism from
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}((t)))
, and the extension
\hat{F}
gives a morphism from
\operatorname{Spec}(\mathbb{C}[[t]])
to
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
. Therefore, by the valuative criterion,
f
extends uniquely to a morphism

\hat{f}:\mathbb{C}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha).

Therefore,
\lim_{t\rightarrow 0}(E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)
exists in
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
.
∎

We next analyze the fixed point locus of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and relate it to the Hitchin fiber over
0
.

Lemma 3.4
.

The fixed point set under the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
is proper in
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{-1}(0)\subset\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
.

Proof.

Note that the zero point in the Hitchin base
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is the only point that is fixed by the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action. Thus,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}=\{0\}
.

Since the Hitchin morphism
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant by Lemma
3.2
, we have

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\subset h_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{-1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{\mathbb{C}^{*}})=h_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{-1}(0).

Moreover, the fixed point locus of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action is closed, since it is defined by algebraic conditions. Hence,

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}\subset h_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{-1}(0)

is a closed subset.

Since
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}
is proper, the fiber
h_{\mathrm{Sp}}^{-1}(0)
is proper. Therefore, being a closed subset of a proper variety,

\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)^{\mathbb{C}^{*}}

is proper.
∎

Theorem 3.5
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable symplectic parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

Proof.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
is a quasi-projective variety. Hence, the semiprojectivity follows from Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
.
∎

By the same argument, we obtain

Theorem 3.6
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable symplectic strongly parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

The same strategy applies to the orthogonal case, with the corresponding Hitchin bases determined by the invariant polynomials of
\mathfrak{so}(n)
.

3.2.
Semiprojectivity of the moduli space of semistable orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles

A point
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
(resp.
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
) can be interpreted as a rank
2m
(resp. rank
2m+1
) parabolic bundle
E_{*}
equipped with a non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle
(i.e., the map
\varphi
) along with a holomorphic section
\Phi\in H^{0}(X,\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})\otimes K(D))
that satisfies

\langle\Phi v,w\rangle=\langle v,\Phi w\rangle.

3.2.1.
Even orthogonal case
\mathrm{(}G=\mathrm{SO}(2m,\mathbb{C})\mathrm{)}

In the orthogonal case, similar to the symplectic case, the characteristic polynomial of a Higgs field
\Phi
is given by

\det(x\cdot I-\Phi)=x^{2m}+s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+s_{2m-2}x^{2}+s_{2m},

where
s_{i}=\mathrm{trace}(\wedge^{i}\Phi)
. In this case, the last coefficient
s_{2m}=p_{m}^{2}
, where
p_{m}\in H^{0}(X,K(D)^{m})
is called the Pfaffian. Thus
\{s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},p_{m}\}
is a basis for the invariant polynomials of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{so}(2m)
. Therefore, the even orthogonal Hitchin morphism is given by

(3.3)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)&\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m-1}H^{0}(X,K(D)^{2i})\oplus H^{0}(X,K(D)^{m})\\
\Phi&\mapstochar\rightarrow(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},p_{m}),\end{split}

and the restriction
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq\left.h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\right|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)}
of the Hitchin map to the strongly parabolic locus is given by

(3.4)

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m-1}H^{0}(X,K^{2i}(D^{2i-1}))\oplus H^{0}(X,K(D)^{m}).

There is a natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the bases
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}
given by

t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},p_{m})\coloneqq(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m-2}s_{2m-2},t^{m}p_{m}).

The next result follows from the homogeneity of the characteristic polynomial under scaling of the Higgs field; see, for example,
[
7
,
6
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

The Hitchin morphism
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.

Proof.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
and let

h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big)=(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},p_{m}^{2}).

Then the characteristic polynomial of
t\Phi
is

\det(x\cdot I-t\Phi)=x^{2m}+t^{2}s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+t^{2m-2}s_{2m-2}+t^{2m}p_{m}^{2}.

Thus,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\big((t\cdot(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi))\big)

\displaystyle=h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle=(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m-2}s_{2m-2},t^{m}p_{m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},p_{m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big).

Hence,
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.
∎

Theorem 3.8
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable even orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
is a quasi-projective variety and the Hitchin map
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant by Lemma
3.7
and proper, we can conclude the semiprojectivity of
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
by using a method similar to the one used in Lemma
3.3
and
3.4
.
∎

Similarly, we get

Theorem 3.9
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable even orthogonal strongly parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

3.2.2.
Odd orthogonal case
\mathrm{(}G=\mathrm{SO}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})\mathrm{)}

Assuming the Higgs field
\Phi
has distinct eigenvalues, its characteristic polynomial in this case is given by

\det(x\cdot I-\Phi)=x(x^{2m}+s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+s_{2m-2}x^{2}+s_{2m}).

Thus, the Hitchin morphism for the odd orthogonal group is similar to the symplectic case, and it is given by

(3.5)

\displaystyle\begin{split}h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)&\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\coloneqq\oplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(X,K(D)^{2i})\\
\varphi&\mapstochar\rightarrow(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},s_{2m}),\end{split}

and the restriction
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq\left.h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\right|_{\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)}
of the Hitchin map to the strongly parabolic locus is given by

(3.6)

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{i=1}^{m}H^{0}(X,K^{2i}(D^{2i-1})).

The natural
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action on the bases
\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}
are given by

t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m})\coloneqq(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m}s_{2m}).

Lemma 3.10
.

The Hitchin morphism
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}:\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.

Proof.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\in\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
and let

h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big)=(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m-2},s_{2m}).

Then the characteristic polynomial of
t\Phi
is

\det(x\cdot I-t\Phi)=x(x^{2m}+t^{2}s_{2}x^{2m-2}+\cdots+t^{2m}s_{2m}).

Thus,

\displaystyle h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\big((t\cdot(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi))\big)

\displaystyle=h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,t\Phi)\big)

\displaystyle=(t^{2}s_{2},\dots,t^{2m}s_{2m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot(s_{2},\dots,s_{2m})

\displaystyle=t\cdot h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}\big((E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)\big).

Hence,
h_{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}
is
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-equivariant.
∎

Therefore, we get the following theorem

Theorem 3.11
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable odd orthogonal parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

Proof.

The proof is identical to that of Theorem
3.8
, using the existence of limits of the
\mathbb{C}^{*}
-action and the closedness of the fixed point locus. Therefore,
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1)}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
is semiprojective.
∎

Similarly, we get

Theorem 3.12
.

The moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{SO}(2m+1),\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
of semistable odd orthogonal strongly parabolic Higgs bundles is a semiprojective variety.

4.
Very stable symplectic parabolic bundles

In this section, we establish a criterion for the (strong) very stability of a symplectic parabolic bundle in terms of the Hitchin morphism.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a symplectic parabolic bundle, where

\varphi:E_{*}\otimes E_{*}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow L_{*}

is a nondegenerate alternating parabolic form with values in a parabolic line bundle
L_{*}
. The symmetric power
\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E_{*})
is understood with the induced parabolic structure coming from the tensor product
E_{*}\otimes E_{*}
; see, for example,
[
12
,
18
]
. Define

\mathrm{PEnd}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(E_{*})\coloneqq\mathrm{Sym}^{2}(E_{*})\otimes L_{*}^{\vee}\subset\mathrm{PEnd}(E_{*})=E_{*}\otimes E_{*}\otimes L_{*}^{\vee},

and similarly let

\mathrm{SPEnd}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(E_{*})\subset\mathrm{SPEnd}(E_{*})

denote the sheaf of symmetric symplectic strongly parabolic endomorphisms of
E_{*}
.

Set

\mathcal{W}_{E}\coloneqq H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{PEnd}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr),

and

\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq H^{0}\bigl(X,\mathrm{SPEnd}_{\mathrm{Sp}}(E_{*})\otimes K(D)\bigr)\subset\mathcal{W}_{E}.

If
(E_{*},\varphi)
is stable, then every
\Phi\in\mathcal{W}_{E}
defines a stable symplectic parabolic Higgs bundle
(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi)
. Hence we obtain a natural morphism

\iota_{E}:\mathcal{W}_{E}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha),\qquad\Phi\longmapsto(E_{*},\varphi,\Phi).

Similarly, every
\Phi\in\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
defines a stable strongly parabolic symplectic Higgs bundle, and therefore we obtain a natural morphism

\iota_{E,\mathrm{st}}:\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}^{\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha).

We now define

(4.1)

h_{E}\coloneqq h_{\mathrm{Sp}}\circ\iota_{E}:\mathcal{W}_{E}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\mathrm{Sp}},

and

(4.2)

h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\coloneqq h_{\mathrm{Sp},\mathrm{st}}\circ\iota_{E,\mathrm{st}}:\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}.

Definition 4.1
.

A symplectic parabolic bundle
(E_{*},\varphi)
is said to be
very stable
if it admits no nonzero nilpotent symplectic parabolic Higgs field in
\mathcal{W}_{E}
. It is said to be
strongly very stable
if it admits no nonzero nilpotent strongly parabolic symplectic Higgs field in
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Every very stable symplectic parabolic bundle is strongly very stable, since

\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}\subset\mathcal{W}_{E}.

Lemma 4.3
.

If a symplectic parabolic bundle
(E_{*},\varphi)
is strongly very stable, then it is stable. Consequently, every very stable symplectic parabolic bundle is stable.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in
[
15
, Lemma
3.5
]
(see also
[
9
, Proposition
3.5
]
).
∎

We will use the following lemma for our purpose.

Lemma 4.4
.

[
20
, Lemma
1.3
]

Let
f=(f_{1},\dots,f_{k}):\mathbb{A}^{m}\mathrel{\mathchoice{{}\hbox{$\displaystyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\textstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptstyle{\meno}$}}{{}\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle{\meno}$}}}\mathrel{\mkern-3.0mu}\rightarrow\mathbb{A}^{k}
be a morphism defined by homogeneous polynomials. If
f^{-1}(0)=\{0\}
, then
f
is finite.

Theorem 4.5
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a stable symplectic parabolic bundle. Then

h_{E}\textnormal{ is quasi-finite }\implies(E_{*},\varphi)\textnormal{ is very stable }\implies h_{E}\textnormal{ is finite}.

Proof.

Assume first that
h_{E}
is quasi-finite. Suppose that
(E_{*},\varphi)
is not very stable. Then there exists a nonzero nilpotent Higgs field

0\neq\Phi\in\mathcal{W}_{E}

such that
h_{E}(\Phi)=0
. Since the Hitchin morphism is given by homogeneous invariant polynomials, for every
t\in\mathbb{C}^{*}
we have

h_{E}(t\Phi)=t\cdot h_{E}(\Phi)=0.

Hence

\mathbb{C}^{*}\cdot\Phi\subset h_{E}^{-1}(0).

Since
\mathcal{W}_{E}
is a vector space and
\Phi\neq 0
, the set
\mathbb{C}^{*}\cdot\Phi
is infinite, contradicting the quasi-finiteness of
h_{E}
. Therefore
(E_{*},\varphi)
is very stable.

Conversely, assume that
(E_{*},\varphi)
is very stable. Then the only nilpotent element in
\mathcal{W}_{E}
is
0
, so

h_{E}^{-1}(0)=\{0\}.

Since
h_{E}
is defined by homogeneous polynomials on the affine space
\mathcal{W}_{E}
, Lemma
4.4
implies that
h_{E}
is finite.
∎

We now focus on the strongly parabolic locus.

Proposition 4.6
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a stable symplectic parabolic bundle. Then

h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is finite }\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is proper}.

In particular, if
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is finite, then it is quasi-finite.

Proof.

Suppose
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is finite. Then it is proper and quasi-finite.

Now assume that
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is proper. Since both
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Sp}}
are affine varieties, the morphism
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is affine. A proper affine morphism is finite. Hence
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is finite.
∎

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a stable symplectic parabolic bundle. Then

h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is quasi-finite }\implies(E_{*},\varphi)\textnormal{ is strongly very stable }\implies h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is finite}.

Proof.

The proof is the same as in Theorem
4.5
, replacing
\mathcal{W}_{E}
by
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
and
h_{E}
by
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
.
∎

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
(E_{*},\varphi)
be a stable symplectic parabolic bundle. Then

\displaystyle(E_{*},\varphi)\textnormal{ is strongly very stable }

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is finite}

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is proper}

\displaystyle\iff h_{E,\mathrm{st}}\textnormal{ is quasi-finite}.

Proof.

If
(E_{*},\varphi)
is strongly very stable, then by Proposition
4.7
, the morphism
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is finite. By Proposition
4.6
, this is equivalent to properness, and in particular implies quasi-finiteness.

Conversely, if
h_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is quasi-finite, then by Proposition
4.7
,
(E_{*},\varphi)
is strongly very stable. This completes the proof.
∎

Example 4.9
.

Consider the case
G=\mathrm{Sp}(2,\mathbb{C})\cong\mathrm{SL}(2,\mathbb{C})
. In this case, a symplectic parabolic bundle reduces to a rank
2
parabolic bundle with trivial determinant equipped with a nondegenerate alternating form.

The space
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
consists of strongly parabolic traceless endomorphisms twisted by
K(D)
, and the Hitchin map is given by the determinant, which lies in
H^{0}(X,K(D)^{2})
. In this setting, strongly very stability means that there are no nonzero nilpotent Higgs fields, i.e., no Higgs fields with vanishing determinant.

Thus, Theorem
4.8
reduces in this case to the statement that the Hitchin map on
\mathcal{W}_{E,\mathrm{st}}
is proper if and only if there are no nonzero nilpotent Higgs fields.

Remark 4.10
.

Using the same method, one obtains an analogous characterization of strong very stability in terms of the corresponding Hitchin map in the orthogonal case, for stable locus of the moduli space
\mathcal{M}^{G,\mathrm{st}}_{\mathrm{Higgs}}(\alpha)
, where
G=\mathrm{SO}(2m,\mathbb{C})
or
\mathrm{SO}(2m+1,\mathbb{C})
. We omit the details, as the arguments are entirely parallel to the symplectic case.
