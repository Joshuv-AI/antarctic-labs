Higgs gap modes in superconducting circuit quantisation
Yun-Chih Liao, Benjamin J. Powell, Thomas M. Stace

Abstract
We extend a recently developed projective circuit quantisation approach to incorporate superconducting Higgs modes associated to gap dynamics. This approach starts from a microscopic fermionic Hamiltonian for mesoscopic superconductors, and projects the system onto a low-energy, multi-determinant ‘BCS’ Hilbert space. We derive analytical results for the superconducting Higgs mass, ‘spring’ constant, and oscillation frequency of the gap dynamics, which we validate numerically. We compute anharmonic corrections to the Higgs frequency for higher excitations of small superconducting islands, and compare our results to previous long-wavelength calculations.

\mathbf{\Delta}\equiv\Delta e^{i\phi}

The frequency of Higgs modes is predicted in Ginzburg-Landau (GL) mean-field theory to soften as the temperature

At long wavelength and

We start from a microscopic electronic Hamiltonian for a small, isolated island of superconducting metal consisting of
n
electronic modes near half-filling,

\hat{H}_{\text{el}}=\!\sum_{\bm{k}s}\!\epsilon_{\bm{k}}c_{\bm{k}s}^{\dagger}c_{\bm{k}s}-|g|^{2}\sum_{\bm{k}\bm{k}^{\prime}}\!c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}^{\prime}\downarrow}c_{\bm{k}^{\prime}\uparrow}+E_{F},

(1)

where
-\mathcal{B}\!<\!\epsilon_{\bm{k}}\!<\!\mathcal{B}
is the single-electron kinetic energy for mode
\bm{k}
in the bandwidth
\pm\mathcal{B}
,
s\in\{\uparrow,\downarrow\}
,
g
is the effective electron-electron binding energy,
c_{\bullet}^{(\dagger)}
are electronic mode creation and annihilation operators, and
E_{F}=n\mathcal{B}/2
.
The low-energy physics is controlled by the dimensionless material parameter
\lambda={|g|^{2}n}/({2\mathcal{B}})\ll 1
, which is independent of the system size
n
(since
|g|^{2}\propto 1/n
)
4
.

We assume that the island is smaller than the coherence length, so that the low-energy subspace is spanned by a basis of BCS states parameterised by a complex parameter
\mathbf{\Delta}
,
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BCS}=\rm{span}\{|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})\rangle\}_{\mathbf{\Delta}\in\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{C}}
, where

|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})\rangle={\prod}_{\bm{k}}(u_{\bm{k}}(\Delta)+v_{\bm{k}}(\Delta)e^{i\phi}c_{\bm{k}\uparrow}^{\dagger}c_{-\bm{k}\downarrow}^{\dagger})|0\rangle,

(2)

with

u_{\bm{k}}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(1+\epsilon_{\bm{k}}/\sqrt{\epsilon_{\bm{k}}^{2}+\Delta^{2}})=1-v_{\bm{k}}^{2}
,
\Delta\geq 0
, and
|0\rangle
is the electronic vacuum
4
;
19
. In the projective approach, we define a generalised projector
\hat{\Pi}=\int_{\mathcal{S}}d\mathbf{\Delta}|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})\rangle\langle\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})|
with which to project out a low-energy circuit Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\hat{\mathsf{H}}\equiv\hat{\Pi}\hat{H}_{\text{el}}\hat{\Pi}={\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\mathbf{\Delta}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})\rangle\langle\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|,

(3)

where
\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})=\langle\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})|\hat{H}_{\text{el}}|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})\rangle
is the Hamiltonian function for the superconducting system.

The BCS basis is normalised,
\langle{\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})}|{\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})}\rangle=1
(since
u_{\bm{k}}^{2}+v_{\bm{k}}^{2}=1
), but not orthogonal, so
\hat{\Pi}
is not idempotent,

\hat{\Pi}^{2}=\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{2}}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998ptd\mathbf{\Delta}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{W}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})\rangle\langle\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|\neq\hat{\Pi},

(4)

where
\mathsf{W}(\mathbf{\Delta},\!\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})\!=\!\langle{\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})}|{\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})}\rangle
is the overlap function.

Electronic eigenstates satisfy
\hat{H}_{\text{el}}|y\rangle=E|y\rangle
, and
assuming that low-energy eigenstates reside within
\mathcal{H}_{\rm BCS}
, i.e.
|y\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{S}}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,y(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})\rangle
, it follows that
\int_{\mathcal{S}}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,y(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})\hat{H}_{\text{el}}|\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})\rangle=E\int_{\mathcal{S}}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,y(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})|{\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})}\rangle
. This forms a natural superconducting generalisation of the multi-determinant wavefunctions familiar from post-Hartree-Fock methods
10
. Projecting over
\langle\Psi(\mathbf{\Delta})|
yields the generalised integral eigenvalue equation

{\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})y(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})=E{\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}\,\mathsf{W}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})y(\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime}).

(5)

The overlap
\mathsf{W}
was discussed in
19
as a function of the phase coordinate,
\phi
, under the BCS mean-field approximation
|\mathbf{\Delta}|=\Delta_{\rm BCS}=2\mathcal{B}e^{-1/\lambda}

4
, i.e. assuming
\mathbf{\Delta}\in\mathcal{S}=\{\Delta_{\rm BCS}e^{i\phi}\}_{\phi\in[-\pi,\pi)}
. That earlier analysis yielded low-energy quantised phase and charge operators
\hat{\phi}
and
\hat{n}
, as well as standard expressions for quantised superconducting circuit elements which depend on them.

Here, we extend the projective approach to expand the support of the gap parameter from
\Delta=\Delta_{\rm BCS}
to
\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
In the expanded ‘gap’ space, we follow a similar analysis to
19
, and compute

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{W}(\varphi;\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\approx e^{n\big(i\frac{\varphi}{2}-\frac{\pi(3(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})^{2}+20\Delta\Delta^{\prime})}{256\mathcal{B}(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})}\varphi^{2}-\frac{\pi(\Delta-\Delta^{\prime})^{2}}{16\mathcal{B}(\Delta+\Delta^{\prime})}\big)},

(6)

where the dependence on
\phi
and
\phi^{\prime}
reduces to the phase-difference,
\varphi\equiv\phi^{\prime}-\phi\in[-\pi,\pi)
. This reduction makes
\mathsf{W}

block-circulant
in the phase coordinates, and
\mathsf{W}
is a Gaussian localised near
\varphi=0
.

The Hamiltonian function is also block-circulant in
\varphi
,

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}(\mathbf{\Delta},\mathbf{\Delta}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}(\varphi;\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=\big(\mathsf{L}(\Delta_{+},\Delta_{-})+O(\varphi)\big)\,\mathsf{W}(\varphi;\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

(7)

where
\Delta_{\pm}=(\Delta\pm\Delta^{\prime})/2
and the leading,
\varphi
-independent
term in
\mathsf{H}/\mathsf{W}
is

\displaystyle\mathsf{L}(\Delta_{+},\Delta_{-})

\displaystyle=\tfrac{n}{2\mathcal{B}}\Big(\Delta_{+}^{2}\big(\!-\!\tfrac{1}{2}+\ln(2\mathcal{B}/\Delta_{+})\!-\!\lambda\ln^{2}(2\mathcal{B}/\Delta_{+})\big)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad+\Delta_{-}^{2}\big(\tfrac{2}{3}-\ln(2\mathcal{B}/\Delta_{+})\big)\Big).

(8)

\displaystyle\mathsf{W}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle={\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\varphi\,e^{-i(\nu+n/2)\varphi}\mathsf{W}(\varphi;\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle\mathsf{H}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle={\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int_{-\pi}^{\pi}$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\varphi\,e^{-i(\nu+n/2)\varphi}\mathsf{H}(\varphi;\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}).

\mathsf{W}

include a narrow Gaussian factor centred at

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{W}}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{W}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})/\mathsf{N}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=\big(1+\Delta_{-}^{2}/(16\Delta_{+}^{2})+O(\nu^{2})\big)e^{-\frac{n\pi\Delta_{-}^{2}}{8\mathcal{B}\Delta_{+}}},

\mathsf{N}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})=\sqrt{\mathsf{W}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta)\mathsf{W}_{\nu}(\Delta^{\prime},\Delta^{\prime})}

\displaystyle\bar{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\mathsf{H}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})/\mathsf{N}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=\big(\mathsf{L}(\Delta_{+},\Delta_{-})+O(\nu)\big)\,\bar{\mathsf{W}}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime}).

5

{\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\Delta^{\prime}\,\bar{\mathsf{H}}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})y_{\nu}(\Delta^{\prime})\!=\!E\!{\mathop{\mathord{\raise 0.49991pt\hbox{$\displaystyle\int$}}}\nolimits\kern-0.99998pt}d\Delta^{\prime}\,\bar{\mathsf{W}}_{\nu}(\Delta,\Delta^{\prime})y_{\nu}(\Delta^{\prime}),

\displaystyle=\langle\psi_{\gamma,\tilde{x}}|\hat{h}|\psi_{\gamma,\tilde{x}^{\prime}}\rangle,

\displaystyle=\mathsf{w}(\tilde{x},\tilde{x}^{\prime})\big(\kappa(\tilde{x}_{+}-x_{0})^{2}/2-{\rm v}_{0}-\tilde{x}_{-}^{2}/(2\gamma^{4}m)

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad{}+\kappa\gamma^{2}/4+1/(4\gamma^{2}m)\big).

\Delta=\Delta_{\rm BCS}
. With the correspondence
\Delta_{\pm}\leftrightarrow\tilde{x}_{\pm}
, we compare the overlap functions

and

Similarly, matching the coefficients of

\displaystyle=\big(2-2/\ln(2b)\big){E_{\rm min}}/\Delta_{\rm BCS}^{2},

\displaystyle 1/(2\gamma_{\rm H}^{4}m_{\rm H})

\displaystyle=1/(\kappa_{\rm H}m_{\rm H})^{1/4}\approx{\ln(2b)}^{1/4}\gamma_{\rm H}.

\displaystyle N_{\rm bound}

\displaystyle\approx E_{\rm min}/\omega_{\rm H}\approx n\pi/\big(32\,b\sqrt{\ln(2b)-{5}/{3}}\big).

Qualitatively, for large system size

We numerically validate the harmonic analysis above by discretising the gap parameter

, which corresponds to choosing a discretised set of gap-mode basis states

For numerics, we use a non-uniform grid over

We find good numerical convergence for
