2) specific objectives;
- For Aim 1, we have
 ● Designed a novel deep latent variable model, deep spatiotemporal point
 processes for sparse, irregularly-sampled time series.
 ● Designing family of new causal estimators that are robust to model
 misspecification and sample efficient considering spatial and temporal-causal
 constraints.
- For Aim 2, we have
 ● Enhance disentanglement in latent representations using alternative variational
 objectives and exploring disentanglement in different topologies
 ● Developed probabilistic equivariant networks which injects symmetry into the
 deep LVMs.
- For Aim 3, we have
 ● Developed Robust MCMC methods that guard against outlier data with a long tail
 distribution.
 ● Theoretical guarantees for both convergence and statistical benefits (in
 generalization power) of the low-rank variational Bayes method.

3) significant results, including major findings, developments, or conclusions
(both positive and negative);
- We published 10+ papers in the second year as the sub-team from UCSD under the
support of this project
- We have made significant progress toward the development of the deep LVM
framework, we have
 ● Developed Deep Spatiotemporal Point Process , a deep dynamics model that
 integrates spatiotemporal point processes. Our method is flexible, efficient, and
 can accurately forecast irregularly sampled events over space and time.
 ● Designed Multi-fidelity Hierarchical Neural Processes (MF-HNP), a unified neural
 latent variable model for multi-fidelity surrogate modeling. MF-HNP inherits the
 flexibility and scalability of Neural Processes. The latent variables transform the
 correlations among different fidelity levels from observations to latent space. The
 predictions across fidelities are conditionally independent given the latent states.
 It helps alleviate the error propagation issue in existing methods.

 ● propose a model-based meta-learning method called DyAd which can generalize
 across heterogeneous domains by partitioning them into different tasks. DyAd
 has two parts: an encoder which infers the time-invariant hidden features of the
 task with weak supervision, and a forecaster which learns the shared dynamics
 of the entire domain.

In conclusion, the current framework of deep LVM is a promising direction for better
understanding climate extreme data. We have already made significant progress in data
preparation, collection, problem formulation and initial framework development.
Combining expertise from the team including deep learning, uncertainty quantification,
causal reasoning and climate science is the key to the further development of the
project.
