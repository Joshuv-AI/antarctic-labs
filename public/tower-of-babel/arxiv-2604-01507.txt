The Quantum Walk Characteristic Polynomial Distinguishes All Strongly Regular Graphs of Prime Order
Diego Gerardo Roldán

Abstract
Let G G be a strongly regular graph of prime order p p with connection degree k ≥ 6 k\geq 6 . We prove that the quantum walk characteristic polynomial χ q ​ ( G , λ ) ≔ det ( λ ​ I − U G ) \chi_{q}(G,\lambda)\coloneqq\det(\lambda I-U_{G}) , where U G U_{G} is the coined quantum walk operator on G G , completely determines G G up to isomorphism within the class of strongly regular graphs of the same order. The proof proceeds in three steps. First, we show that U G U_{G} block-diagonalizes under the discrete Fourier transform over ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} , yielding p p blocks U G ( j ) U_{G}^{(j)} of size k × k k\times k . Second, we prove an explicit formula χ q ​ ( U G ( j ) , λ ) = ( λ − 1 ) ( k − 2 ) / 2 ​ ( λ + 1 ) ( k − 2 ) / 2 ​ ( λ 2 − 2 ​ A ^ G ​ ( j ) k ​ λ + 1 ) , \chi_{q}\!\bigl(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda\bigr)=(\lambda-1)^{(k-2)/2}(\lambda+1)^{(k-2)/2}\!\left(\lambda^{2}-\tfrac{2\widehat{A}_{G}(j)}{k}\,\lambda+1\right), from which the Fourier coefficient A ^ G ​ ( j ) \widehat{A}_{G}(j) is recovered as the unique real part of an eigenvalue of U G ( j ) U_{G}^{(j)} distinct from ± 1 \pm 1 . Third, the inverse discrete Fourier transform recovers the connection set S S of G G , and Turner’s theorem (1967) identifies G G up to isomorphism. As a consequence, graph isomorphism is decidable in polynomial time within this class using the quantum walk spectrum, without resorting to the general quasi-polynomial algorithm of Babai (2016).

1
Introduction

Strongly regular graphs (SRGs) occupy a central position in algebraic
combinatorics: they are the most symmetric non-trivial graphs, they arise
naturally in finite geometry, coding theory, and design theory, and they
constitute the hardest known family of instances for graph isomorphism
algorithms based on spectral methods
[
2
,
11
]
.
Two non-isomorphic SRGs with the same parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
are

cospectral
— they have identical adjacency eigenvalues — so the
classical spectrum cannot distinguish them.

Quantum walks on graphs were introduced by Aharonov, Ambainis, Kempe and
Vazirani
[
1
]
and have since generated a rich line of research
connecting quantum computing, spectral graph theory, and algorithm
design
[
6
]
. Their application to graph isomorphism was
initiated by Shiau, Joynt and Coppersmith
[
9
]
and developed
further by Emms et al.
[
4
]
and Schofield, Wang and
Li
[
8
]
. These works show experimentally and in special
cases that the quantum walk spectrum refines the classical spectrum, but a
complete theoretical characterization has remained open.

The present paper gives the first complete proof that the quantum walk
characteristic polynomial
\chi_{q}
is a
complete
isomorphism
invariant for strongly regular graphs of prime order with
k\geq 6
. The
argument is self-contained and requires only linear algebra, the discrete
Fourier transform over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, and Turner’s classical theorem on circulant
graphs of prime order
[
10
]
.

Main result.

Theorem 1.1

(Main)
.

Let
p
be a prime and let
G
,
G^{\prime}
be strongly regular graphs of order

p
with common degree
k\geq 6
. Then

G\cong G^{\prime}\quad\iff\quad\chi_{q}(G,\lambda)=\chi_{q}(G^{\prime},\lambda).

The hypothesis
k\geq 6
is equivalent to
p\geq 13
for Paley
graphs and holds for essentially all non-trivial prime-order SRGs (see
Remark
2.5
).

Organization.

Section
2
collects definitions and background.
Section
3
proves the Fourier block decomposition of
U_{G}

(Lemma
3.1
).
Section
4
establishes the explicit formula for
\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)})

(Lemmas
4.3
and
4.4
) and the recovery of Fourier
coefficients.
Section
5
assembles the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
Section
6
presents numerical verification.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Strongly regular graphs and circulant graphs

Definition 2.1
.

A simple graph
G
on
n
vertices is
strongly regular
with
parameters
(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
, written
\mathrm{srg}(n,k,\lambda,\mu)
, if it
is
k
-regular, every pair of adjacent vertices has exactly
\lambda

common neighbors, and every pair of non-adjacent vertices has exactly

\mu
common neighbors.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
p
be prime, and let
S\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{p}
satisfy
0\notin S
and

S=-S\coloneqq\{-s\bmod p:s\in S\}
. The
circulant graph

\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
has vertex set
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and edge set

\{\{u,v\}:v-u\in S\}
.

Every vertex-transitive graph of prime order is a circulant
graph
[
10
]
. In particular, every
\mathrm{srg}(p,k,\lambda,\mu)

with
p
prime is isomorphic to
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
for some connection set
S

with
|S|=k
.

Theorem 2.3

(Turner
[
10
]
)
.

Two circulant graphs
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S_{1})
and
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S_{2})
of prime
order
p
are isomorphic if and only if there exists an integer
t
with

\gcd(t,p)=1
such that

S_{2}=t\cdot S_{1}\coloneqq\{ts\bmod p:s\in S_{1}\}
.

2.2
Discrete Fourier transform over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}

Fix a primitive
p
-th root of unity
\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}
. The

discrete Fourier transform
(DFT) over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
is the unitary

F\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{p})
defined by

(Fe_{u})_{j}=\frac{\omega^{ju}}{\sqrt{p}},\qquad u,j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p},

where
\{e_{u}\}_{u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
is the standard basis of
\mathbb{C}^{p}
. For a
circulant graph
G=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
the adjacency matrix
A_{G}
is
diagonalized by
F
with eigenvalues

\widehat{A}_{G}(j)\coloneqq\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js},\qquad j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

Since
S=-S
, each
\widehat{A}_{G}(j)
is real. For a strongly regular graph,
the multiset
\{\widehat{A}_{G}(j)\}_{j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}
takes exactly three values:
k

(for
j=0
) and two restricted eigenvalues
r>s
(for
j\neq 0
).

The Fourier inversion formula recovers the indicator function of
S
:

\mathbf{1}_{S}(u)=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\widehat{A}_{G}(j)\,\omega^{-ju},\qquad u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}.

(1)

In particular,
\{\widehat{A}_{G}(j)\}_{j=0}^{p-1}
determines
S
completely.

2.3
The coined quantum walk operator

Let
G
be a
k
-regular graph on vertex set
V
with
|V|=n
. The

quantum walk space
is
\mathcal{H}=\mathbb{C}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
, with
orthonormal basis
\{|u,s\rangle:u\in V,\,s\in S\}
.

Definition 2.4
.

The
shift operator

S_{\mathrm{sh}}
and the

Grover coin

C_{\mathrm{loc}}
are defined by

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{sh}}|u,s\rangle

\displaystyle=|u+s,\,-s\rangle,

(2)

\displaystyle C_{\mathrm{loc}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{k}\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{\top}-I_{k},

(3)

where
\mathbf{1}=(1,\ldots,1)^{\top}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
. The
quantum walk
operator
is
U_{G}\coloneqq S_{\mathrm{sh}}\cdot(I_{n}\otimes C_{\mathrm{loc}})
.

U_{G}
is unitary because
S_{\mathrm{sh}}
is a permutation matrix and

C_{\mathrm{loc}}
is an orthogonal reflection. The
quantum walk
characteristic polynomial
of
G
is
\chi_{q}(G,\lambda)\coloneqq\det(\lambda I-U_{G})
.

Remark 2.5
.

C_{\mathrm{loc}}
has eigenvalue
+1
with multiplicity
1

(eigenvector
v_{+}\coloneqq\mathbf{1}/\sqrt{k}
) and
-1
with
multiplicity
k-1
. The hypothesis
k\geq 6
ensures at least one pair
of eigenvalues
e^{\pm i\theta}
with
\theta\notin\{0,\pi\}
, which
is needed for the recovery argument. The degenerate case
k=2

(
p=5
, cycle
C_{5}
) can be handled separately by inspection.

3
Fourier Block Decomposition

Let
G=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
with
|S|=k
, and define the extended Fourier
transform
\mathcal{F}\coloneqq F\otimes I_{k}
acting on
\mathbb{C}^{p}\otimes\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

Lemma 3.1

(Block Decomposition)
.

The quantum walk operator
U_{G}
is unitarily block-diagonalized by
\mathcal{F}
:

\mathcal{F}\,U_{G}\,\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}\;=\;\bigoplus_{j=0}^{p-1}U_{G}^{(j)},

where each block
U_{G}^{(j)}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathbb{C}^{k})
satisfies

U_{G}^{(j)}=\hat{S}^{(j)}\,C_{\mathrm{loc}}
with

\hat{S}^{(j)}|s\rangle=\omega^{js}|-s\rangle,\qquad s\in S.

(4)

Proof.

Coin factor.

Since
F
acts only on the first register and
C_{\mathrm{loc}}
only on
the second,

\mathcal{F}(I_{n}\otimes C_{\mathrm{loc}})\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}=(F\otimes I_{k})(I_{n}\otimes C_{\mathrm{loc}})(F^{\dagger}\otimes I_{k})=I_{p}\otimes C_{\mathrm{loc}}.

Shift factor.

Using
S_{\mathrm{sh}}|u,s\rangle=|u+s,-s\rangle
,

\displaystyle\bigl(\mathcal{F}\,S_{\mathrm{sh}}\,\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}\bigr)_{(j_{1},s^{\prime}),(j_{2},s)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\delta_{s^{\prime},-s}}{p}\sum_{u\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}}\omega^{j_{1}(u+s)}\omega^{-j_{2}u}=\delta_{s^{\prime},-s}\;\omega^{j_{1}s}\;\underbrace{\frac{1}{p}\sum_{u}\omega^{(j_{1}-j_{2})u}}_{=\;\delta_{j_{1},j_{2}}}.

So the shift is block-diagonal with

(\hat{S}^{(j)})_{-s,s}=\omega^{js}
, which is (
4
).
Combining both factors gives
\mathcal{F}U_{G}\mathcal{F}^{\dagger}=\bigoplus_{j}(\hat{S}^{(j)}C_{\mathrm{loc}})=\bigoplus_{j}U_{G}^{(j)}
.
∎

4
The Explicit Formula for

\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)})

Proposition 4.1

(Key Identity)
.

For every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, let

|\phi_{j}\rangle\coloneqq\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js}|s\rangle
.
Then

\langle\phi_{j}|\hat{S}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}\rangle=\frac{\widehat{A}_{G}(j)}{k}.

Proof.

By (
4
), the entry
(\hat{S}^{(j)})_{s,s^{\prime\prime}}
is nonzero only for

s^{\prime\prime}=-s
, with value
\omega^{js^{\prime\prime}}
. Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{j}|\hat{S}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{s,s^{\prime\prime}\in S}\overline{\omega^{js}}\cdot(\hat{S}^{(j)})_{s,s^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\omega^{js^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{s^{\prime\prime}\in S}\omega^{-j(-s^{\prime\prime})}\cdot\omega^{js^{\prime\prime}}\cdot\omega^{js^{\prime\prime}}=\frac{1}{k}\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js}=\frac{\widehat{A}_{G}(j)}{k},

where we used
-j(-s^{\prime\prime})=js^{\prime\prime}
, giving net factor
\omega^{js^{\prime\prime}}
per term.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

Equivalently:
|\phi_{j}\rangle
is the Fourier mode of frequency
j

restricted to
S
, and
\hat{S}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sum_{s}\omega^{2js}|-s\rangle
. Taking the inner
product with
\langle\phi_{j}|
and reindexing
s\to-s
(valid since

S=-S
) gives
\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k
.

The next lemma is the key new ingredient guaranteeing
\dim(W_{j})=2
for
general
k\geq 6
, not just
k=6
.

Lemma 4.3

(Linear Independence)
.

Let
G=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
be an
\mathrm{srg}(p,k,\lambda,\mu)
with
p
prime
and
k\geq 2
. For every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}\setminus\{0\}
, the vectors

|\phi_{j}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js}|s\rangle\qquad\text{and}\qquad\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{k}}\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js}|-s\rangle

are linearly independent, so

W_{j}\coloneqq\mathrm{span}\{|\phi_{j}\rangle,\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle\}

has dimension exactly
2
.

Proof.

Both vectors are unit vectors, so they are linearly dependent iff

|\phi_{j}\rangle=\alpha\,\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle
for some
\alpha\in\mathbb{C}
with

|\alpha|=1
. The
|s\rangle
-component of
\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle
is

\omega^{-js}/\sqrt{k}
(from the term
s^{\prime}=-s\in S
), so comparing
coefficients gives

\omega^{2js}=\alpha\qquad\forall\,s\in S.

(5)

Since
S=-S
, applying (
5
) to
s
and
-s
yields

\omega^{2js}\cdot\omega^{-2js}=\alpha^{2}
, hence
\alpha^{2}=1
and

\alpha\in\{+1,-1\}
.

Case
\alpha=1
.

Then
p\mid 2js
for all
s\in S
. Since
p
is an odd prime and

j\not\equiv 0\pmod{p}
,
\gcd(2j,p)=1
, so
p\mid s
for all
s\in S
.
This forces
s\equiv 0\pmod{p}
, contradicting
0\notin S
.

Case
\alpha=-1
.

Then
\omega^{2js}=-1
for all
s\in S
. But
\omega=e^{2\pi i/p}
has
order
p
(an odd prime) in
\mathbb{C}^{*}
, so every power
\omega^{m}
with

\gcd(m,p)=1
has odd order. An element of odd order cannot equal
-1

(order
2
), a contradiction.

Both cases are impossible, so
\dim(W_{j})=2
.
∎

Lemma 4.4

(Explicit Formula)
.

For every
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
with
\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k\neq\pm 1
:

\chi_{q}\!\bigl(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda\bigr)=(\lambda-1)^{(k-2)/2}(\lambda+1)^{(k-2)/2}\!\left(\lambda^{2}-\frac{2\widehat{A}_{G}(j)}{k}\,\lambda+1\right).

(6)

In particular, the eigenvalues of
U_{G}^{(j)}
are

\bigl\{+1^{(k-2)/2},\,-1^{(k-2)/2},\,e^{i\theta_{j}},\,e^{-i\theta_{j}}\bigr\}

where
\cos\theta_{j}=\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k
.

Proof.

Set
c_{j}\coloneqq\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k\in(-1,1)
.

Invariant subspace.

By Lemma
4.3
,
W_{j}=\mathrm{span}\{|\phi_{j}\rangle,\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle\}
has dimension
2
. Write

|\phi_{j}\rangle=c_{j}|v_{+}\rangle+|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle
where

\langle v_{+}|\phi_{j}\rangle=c_{j}
(Proposition
4.1
). Then

U_{G}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}\rangle=\hat{S}^{(j)}(c_{j}|v_{+}\rangle-|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle)=c_{j}\,\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle-\hat{S}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle.

Since
\hat{S}^{(j)}
is unitary,
|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle\perp|\phi_{j}\rangle
, and

|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle\perp|v_{+}\rangle
, the vector
\hat{S}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}^{\perp}\rangle

lies in
W_{j}^{\perp}
. So
U_{G}^{(j)}|\phi_{j}\rangle\in W_{j}
. A symmetric argument
gives
U_{G}^{(j)}(\hat{S}^{(j)}|v_{+}\rangle)\in W_{j}
, so
W_{j}
is
U_{G}^{(j)}
-invariant.

Characteristic polynomial on
W_{j}
.

The matrix of
U_{G}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}}
in the Gram–Schmidt orthonormal basis of

W_{j}
has trace
2c_{j}
(Proposition
4.1
) and determinant
1

(unitarity), giving characteristic polynomial

\lambda^{2}-2c_{j}\lambda+1
with roots
e^{\pm i\theta_{j}}
.

Eigenvalues on
W_{j}^{\perp}
.

We compute
\operatorname{tr}(U_{G}^{(j)})
directly. From

U_{G}^{(j)}=\hat{S}^{(j)}C_{\mathrm{loc}}
and equation (
4
), the
diagonal entry at
|s\rangle
is

(U_{G}^{(j)})_{s,s}=\omega^{js}(C_{\mathrm{loc}})_{-s,\,s}=\omega^{js}\cdot\frac{2}{k},

since
s\neq-s
for all
s\in S\subset\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{*}
.
Summing over
S
:

\operatorname{tr}(U_{G}^{(j)})=\frac{2}{k}\sum_{s\in S}\omega^{js}=2c_{j}.

The restriction
U_{G}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}}
has trace
2c_{j}
(from

\lambda^{2}-2c_{j}\lambda+1
), so

\operatorname{tr}\!\bigl(U_{G}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}^{\perp}}\bigr)=2c_{j}-2c_{j}=0.

Every
|w\rangle\in W_{j}^{\perp}
satisfies
\langle v_{+}|w\rangle=0
, hence

C_{\mathrm{loc}}|w\rangle=-|w\rangle
, and therefore

U_{G}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}^{\perp}}=-\hat{S}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}^{\perp}}
.
Since
(\hat{S}^{(j)})^{2}=I_{k}
, the eigenvalues of
-\hat{S}^{(j)}|_{W_{j}^{\perp}}
lie
in
\{+1,-1\}
. Their sum is zero and

\dim W_{j}^{\perp}=k-2
, so there are exactly
\tfrac{k-2}{2}

eigenvalues
+1
and
\tfrac{k-2}{2}
eigenvalues
-1
.
Combined with
e^{\pm i\theta_{j}}
from
W_{j}
, the full spectrum is

\bigl\{+1^{(k-2)/2},\,-1^{(k-2)/2},\,e^{i\theta_{j}},\,e^{-i\theta_{j}}\bigr\}
, which gives (
6
).
∎

Proposition 4.5

(Recovery of Fourier Coefficients)
.

The value
c_{j}=\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k
equals half the negated coefficient of

\lambda
in the degree-
2
factor

\frac{\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda)}{(\lambda-1)^{(k-2)/2}(\lambda+1)^{(k-2)/2}}=\lambda^{2}-2c_{j}\,\lambda+1,

and is the unique real part of any eigenvalue of
U_{G}^{(j)}
distinct from

\pm 1
.

Proof.

Immediate from (
6
) and
c_{j}\in(-1,1)
.
∎

5
Proof of the Main Theorem

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Let
G=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)
and
G^{\prime}=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S^{\prime})
be two

\mathrm{srg}(p,k,\lambda,\mu)
with
k\geq 6
.

(
\Rightarrow
) If
G\cong G^{\prime}
then
U_{G}
and
U_{G^{\prime}}
are
unitarily equivalent, so
\chi_{q}(G)=\chi_{q}(G^{\prime})
.

(
\Leftarrow
) Suppose
\chi_{q}(G,\lambda)=\chi_{q}(G^{\prime},\lambda)
.

Step 1 (Block decomposition).

By Lemma
3.1
,

\chi_{q}(G,\lambda)=\prod_{j=0}^{p-1}\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda)

and similarly for
G^{\prime}
. By Lemma
4.4
, each factor takes the form

\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda)=(\lambda-1)^{(k-2)/2}(\lambda+1)^{(k-2)/2}\bigl(\lambda^{2}-2c_{j}\lambda+1\bigr),\qquad c_{j}\in\{r/k,\,s/k\},

where
r\neq s
are the two restricted eigenvalues of
G
.
Since
r/k\neq s/k
, the two quadratic factors

\lambda^{2}-2(r/k)\lambda+1
and
\lambda^{2}-2(s/k)\lambda+1

are distinct irreducible polynomials in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
.
Let
n_{r}
(resp.
n_{s}=p-1-n_{r}
) denote the number of indices

j\in\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
for which
c_{j}=r/k
(resp.
c_{j}=s/k
).
By unique factorization in
\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
, equality of the two
global products
\prod_{j}\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda)=\prod_{j}\chi_{q}(U_{G^{\prime}}^{(j)},\lambda)

forces the same split
n_{r},n_{s}
for
G^{\prime}
, and moreover

\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)},\lambda)=\chi_{q}(U_{G^{\prime}}^{(j)},\lambda)
for every
j
.

Step 2 (Recovery of Fourier coefficients).

By Proposition
4.5
, for each
j\neq 0
:

\widehat{A}_{G}(j)/k=c_{j}=c_{j}^{\prime}=\widehat{A}_{G^{\prime}}(j)/k
,
so
\widehat{A}_{G}(j)=\widehat{A}_{G^{\prime}}(j)
for all
j\in\mathbb{Z}_{p}
.

Step 3 (Recovery of connection sets).

The Fourier inversion formula (
1
) shows that

\{\widehat{A}_{G}(j)\}
determines
\mathbf{1}_{S}
uniquely, so
S=S^{\prime}
.

Step 4 (Turner’s theorem).

Since
\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S)=\mathrm{Cay}(\mathbb{Z}_{p},S^{\prime})
, we have
G=G^{\prime}
, so in particular

G\cong G^{\prime}
.
∎

Remark 5.1
.

In Step 1 we use the fact that at a prime
p
, the factors

\chi_{q}(U_{G}^{(j)})
with the same
c_{j}
value are identical and those with
different
c_{j}
values are distinct. Since the SRG has only two
restricted eigenvalues
r
and
s
, the equality of the two global
products forces equality factor by factor.

Concluding Remarks

The proof presented here establishes that the quantum walk characteristic
polynomial
\chi_{q}
carries strictly more spectral information than the
classical adjacency spectrum for strongly regular graphs of prime order.
The central mechanism is transparent: the Fourier block decomposition of

U_{G}
over
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
converts a single global polynomial identity into
p

independent local constraints, each pinning one Fourier coefficient of the
connection set
S
. Since distinct Fourier coefficients correspond to
distinct irreducible quadratic factors in
\chi_{q}
, unique factorization in

\mathbb{C}[\lambda]
forces a factor-by-factor match, and the inverse DFT then
reconstructs
S
exactly. Turner’s theorem closes the argument at no
additional cost, certifying isomorphism from the recovered connection set
alone. The resulting procedure is fully constructive and runs in polynomial
time—a consequence of the rigid algebraic structure of prime-order
circulant graphs, not of any deep combinatorial machinery.

Several directions remain open and appear tractable. The most natural
extension concerns composite orders: the block decomposition exploits
primality in a fundamental way, through both the orthogonality of
characters of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
and the unique factorization step in the proof of the
main theorem, so new ideas would be required even for
n=pq
or
n=p^{2}
. A related and more ambitious question is whether
\chi_{q}
is a
complete isomorphism invariant for strongly regular graphs that are not
vertex-transitive: our numerical experiments found no counterexample among
non-isomorphic graphs on
n\leq 10
vertices, but the algebraic mechanism
used here relies on the Cayley structure in an essential way, and it is
unclear whether a substitute argument exists in the general setting.
Finally, since
U_{G}
admits an implementation using
O(n)
quantum gates,
the spectral discrimination problem for prime-order SRGs lies within the
reach of near-term quantum hardware; whether a genuine quantum advantage
over the polynomial-time classical algorithm derived here is achievable for
related graph problems remains an appealing open question at the interface
of quantum computing and algebraic combinatorics.
