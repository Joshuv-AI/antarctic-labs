Mapping occupational therapy interventions in caregiver skill training for children with neurodiversity: a scoping review protocol
Authors: Keshavaram Pandian, Harin Mohammed Manzoor, Shania Ann John, Nis Anna Benoy, Bessy Sunny
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Abstract AbstractIntroductionCaregivers of children with neurodiversity, particularly autism spectrum disorder and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder, experience sustained occupational, emotional and participation-related challenges. Occupational therapy (OT) plays a crucial role in supporting caregivers through skill training that enhances daily routines, participation and family functioning. Although caregiver training programmes are described across multiple disciplines, the scope, nature and specific contribution of OT-related caregiver skill training interventions remain insufficiently synthesised, with fragmented terminology, heterogeneous theoretical foundations and inconsistent reporting. To date, there appears to be limited synthesis specifically mapping OT-related caregiver skill training programmes for caregivers of children with neurodiversity.ObjectivesThis scoping review aims to identify and map the range, characteristics and implementation features of OT-related caregiver skill-training programmes for caregivers of children with neurodiversity. Secondary objectives are to describe how these programmes are structured and to examine delivery characteristics, including mode, provider, setting, duration and contextual or cultural adaptations.Methods and analysisThis protocol follows the Joanna Briggs Institute methodological framework for scoping reviews and will be reported in accordance with the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Extension for Scoping Review guidelines. Eligibility criteria are guided by the Population-Concept-Context framework. The review will include mixed-methods studies, intervention studies (randomised and non-randomised), feasibility studies and programme descriptions published in English from 2000 to 2025. Electronic searches will be conducted in PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Scopus and Web of Science. The PubMed search strategy will undergo peer review in accordance with the PRESS 2015 guidelines. Study selection and data charting will be performed independently by multiple reviewers. Data will be synthesised descriptively and narratively, with results presented in tables, conceptual maps and an evidence gap map.Ethics and disseminationEthical approval is not required as this review uses publicly available data. Findings will be disseminated through publication in a peer-reviewed journal and presentations at relevant scientific and professional forums to inform clinical practice, policy development and future research in caregiver-focused OT interventions.Keywords: Caregivers, Parents, MENTAL HEALTH

Introduction

Neurodiversity refers to naturally occurring variations in neurological development that influence how individuals think, learn, communicate and participate in daily occupations. Conditions such as autism spectrum disorder (ASD) and attention deficit hyperactivity disorder (ADHD) fall within this spectrum and often require long-term, individualised support across home, school and community contexts. Children with neurodiversity frequently experience challenges in sensory processing, emotional regulation, communication and participation, which, in turn, place substantial and persistent demands on their caregivers.1 2 Caregivers of neurodiverse children, most often parents, report elevated levels of stress, emotional burden, financial strain and reduced participation in meaningful family routines compared with caregivers of neurotypical children.3–6

Occupational therapists are uniquely positioned to support these caregivers because the core of occupational therapy (OT) practice involves enabling participation in daily activities, optimising routines and adapting environments to reduce functional difficulties.7 8 OT-related caregiver skill training may include parent coaching, modelling of play and daily living routines, sensory-regulation strategies, behavioural support and environmental modifications that enhance family functioning.9–12 However, although multiple studies describe caregiver training programmes across disciplines such as psychology, education and public health, the specific contribution, scope and characteristics of OT-related caregiver interventions remain poorly synthesised. The literature is highly fragmented, with diverse terminology, varying theoretical foundations and inconsistent reporting of intervention components and outcomes.13 14 As a result, clinicians and policymakers lack a consolidated understanding of what OT-related caregiver training programmes exist, how they are defined and what evidence supports them.

Although global initiatives such as the WHO Caregiver Skills Training (CST) programme provide structured caregiver training frameworks, there is limited synthesis focusing specifically on OT-related caregiver skill development.15 Existing reviews tend to examine general parent training, behavioural interventions or multidisciplinary programmes without isolating the OT contribution. Furthermore, caregiver-level outcomes in published studies indicate a critical gap in understanding how caregiver-focused OT interventions influence family well-being.16 17 Cultural and contextual variations, including adaptations for low-resource settings and diverse family structures, are also under-reported, further highlighting the need for a systematic mapping of this field.

This scoping review will consolidate and map the range of OT-related caregiver skill training programmes for caregivers of neurodiverse children across different settings and cultural contexts. By synthesising how these interventions are defined, implemented and evaluated, the review will provide conceptual clarity, reveal under-represented populations and contexts and identify gaps to inform clinical practice, policy development and future research.

An initial search of Scopus, the Cochrane Database of Systematic Reviews and Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) Evidence Synthesis identified no existing scoping reviews that specifically map OT-related caregiver skill training programmes designed for caregivers of children with neurodiversity. Existing reviews predominantly focus on parent-mediated interventions aimed at improving child outcomes rather than programmes centred on enhancing caregiver knowledge, competence and occupational engagement. This gap highlights the absence of a comprehensive synthesis that collates and clarifies the nature, components and outcomes of caregiver-focused training within the scope of OT practice. Given this clear need, a scoping review is the most appropriate approach to systematically map the available evidence, conceptual and contextual gaps and inform the development and refinement of a caregiver intervention module tailored for families of neurodiverse children.

Review questions (PCC-framed)

Primary question

What OT-related caregiver skill training programmes exist for caregivers of children with neurodiversity, and what are their key characteristics?

Secondary questions

How are these programmes structured?

What implementation characteristics (delivery mode, provider, setting, frequency) are described?

Methods

Study design and framework

This scoping review will be conducted following the JBI18 methodological framework for scoping reviews and will be reported using the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Review and Meta-Analysis Extension for Scoping Review (PRISMA-ScR)19 guidelines. The review will follow the structured stages of identifying the research question, identifying relevant studies, selecting studies, charting the data and summarising and reporting results; the anticipated timeline for these stages is summarised in table 1. The Population-Concept-Context (PCC) framework will guide eligibility criteria and study selection, as detailed in table 2. The protocol has been prospectively registered on the Open Science Framework (OSF) to ensure transparency and reproducibility.

Information sources

Electronic searches will be conducted in PubMed, Embase, CINAHL, Scopus and Web of Science. Searches will be designed to be sensitive to equity considerations using the PROGRESS-Plus framework to help identify populations or settings that may be underrepresented.

Search strategy and PRESS peer review

A three-step search strategy will be followed in line with JBI recommendations. First, an initial limited search in PubMed and CINAHL will help identify relevant text words and index terms. Second, a comprehensive search strategy will be developed combining controlled vocabulary (eg, MeSH, Emtree) and keywords related to caregivers, occupational OT, caregiver skill training, ASD and ADHD. The PubMed search strategy will undergo peer review by an experienced information specialist using the PRESS 2015 Guidelines,20 and all revisions will be documented to enhance methodological rigour and reproducibility. Third, the search strategy will be translated and adapted for all remaining databases and information sources. The full search strings for each database, including the filters and limits applied, are provided in online supplemental file 1 (Search String with Database).

Study selection

All identified citations will be imported into a reference management software such as EndNote or Zotero, where duplicates will be removed. Screening will be conducted using Rayyan software by four independent reviewers (HMM, NAB, SAJ and BS). During the title and abstract screening, reviewers will work independently using Rayyan’s blinding feature, ensuring that individual screening decisions are concealed from other reviewers until completion of the screening phase. Potentially eligible studies will then undergo full-text review by the same reviewers, who will continue to assess studies independently, with decisions remaining concealed until all assessments are completed. Discrepancies at either stage will be resolved through discussion and consensus, with a fifth reviewer (KP) acting as the final arbiter when agreement cannot be reached. Reasons for exclusion at the full-text stage will be documented. The overall study selection process will be reported using a PRISMA-ScR flow diagram (figure 1).

Use of automation tools

To improve efficiency, machine-assisted screening tools (eg, Rayyan AI prioritisation) may be used to help organise and prioritise citations; however, all inclusion and exclusion decisions will remain human-led. Any use of automation will be transparently reported, including the software, version, settings and human verification processes, in line with contemporary evidence synthesis recommendations.

Data extraction (charting)

Data will be extracted using a structured charting form developed in accordance with JBI guidance. A draft of this charting form is provided as online supplemental file 2 (Appendix II: Data Extraction). Extracted data will include study characteristics, participants, demographics, child diagnoses, intervention content, delivery mode, OT contribution, duration and intensity, outcome domains (particularly caregiver-level outcomes), measurement instruments used, implementation factors, cultural adaptations and key findings. Data extraction will be conducted independently by pairs of reviewers (HMM, NAB, SAJ and BS) using the standardised charting form. Prior to full extraction, the form will be piloted on a sample of studies and refined as necessary. Any discrepancies between the reviewers will initially be resolved through discussion and consensus. Where disagreements persist, an independent reviewer who was not involved in the initial extraction of the study will adjudicate the final decision.

Critical appraisal

Consistent with JBI guidance, no formal methodological quality appraisal will be conducted, as the purpose of a scoping review is to map the breadth and characteristics of existing evidence rather than assess risk of bias or intervention effectiveness. Nevertheless, notable methodological limitations of included studies may be summarised narratively to support contextual interpretation.

Data synthesis and presentation

Data will be synthesised descriptively and narratively. Quantitative data (eg, intervention, frequency, study design, outcome types) will be summarised using frequency counts and tabular mapping. Qualitative and conceptual data (eg, OT models, intervention components, caregiver experiences and contextual adaptations) will be synthesised narratively. The narrative synthesis will be informed by the framework proposed by Popay et al, which provides structured guidance for exploring patterns, relationships and contextual factors across heterogeneous evidence. Findings will be organised according to key intervention characteristics, implementation features, caregiver outcomes and contextual influences relevant to OT-related caregiver skill training programmes. An evidence gap map will be constructed to visually display intervention types against caregiver outcomes and contextual characteristics, highlighting where evidence is sparse. Results will be presented through structured tables, conceptual maps and narrative summaries aligned with the review objectives.

Protocol amendments and reporting

Any amendments to the protocol will be recorded in the OSF registry with justification, and deviations will be transparently reported in the final manuscript. Stakeholder consultation with OT clinicians or caregiver representatives may be integrated during interpretation of findings to enhance contextual relevance; if undertaken, this will be reported as an extension stage of the scoping review process.

Patient and public involvement

Patients and members of the public were not involved in the design, development or conduct of this scoping review protocol. As this study involves the synthesis of published literature, no patients were recruited or participated in the review process. Findings will be disseminated through peer-reviewed publication and scientific presentations. Stakeholder consultations with OT clinicians and caregiver representatives may be considered during the interpretation of findings to enhance contextual relevance.

Discussion

This scoping review will systematically map the breadth and characteristics of OT-related caregiver skill training programmes for caregivers of children with neurodiversity, with a particular focus on ASD and ADHD. By consolidating evidence across diverse study designs, settings and cultural contexts, the review aims to address persistent fragmentation in the literature and clarify the specific contribution of OT within caregiver-focused interventions.

Caregiver training has been widely examined across disciplines such as psychology, education and speech therapy. However, existing syntheses predominantly emphasise parent-mediated child outcomes rather than caregiver-level skill development, occupational participation or family routines.16–18 OT, by contrast, adopts a distinct focus on enabling participation in everyday activities, supporting routines and modifying environments to enhance family functioning.7 12 Mapping how these principles are operationalised in caregiver skill-training programmes will help delineate OT-specific intervention components that are currently embedded but often under-reported within broader multidisciplinary frameworks.

At a policy and practice level, findings from this review may support clinicians, educators and service planners by providing a consolidated overview of existing OT-related caregiver skill training approaches and identifying gaps in caregiver-level outcomes. Such mapping is timely, given global initiatives advocating caregiver empowerment and capacity building, including the WHO’s CST framework.15 Clarifying how OT aligns with and extends these initiatives may strengthen advocacy for caregiver-focused OT services within multidisciplinary care models.

In summary, this scoping review will provide a comprehensive and structured overview of OT-related caregiver skill-training programmes for caregivers of children with neurodiversity. By mapping existing evidence, identifying conceptual and contextual gaps and highlighting areas for future investigation, the review aims to advance caregiver-focused OT research and support more coherent translation into practice and policy.
