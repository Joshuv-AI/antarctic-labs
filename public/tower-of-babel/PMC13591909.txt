The correlation of CRP/Alb ratio with low muscle mass in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus: a cross-sectional research
Authors: Rui wang, Wenchao Hu, Guotao Liu, Zheng Mou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundSarcopenia is a prominent age-related condition with low muscle mass, yet its underlying pathogenesis remains unclear. Type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) is a well-recognized risk factor for this disorder. Recent data indicates that low muscle mass or sarcopenia is closely linked to high C-reactive protein-to-albumin (CRP/Alb) ratio in patients with esophageal, gastric and colorectal cancers.MethodsTherefore, this study aimed to investigate the association between the CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass in a T2DM population. This study included 1009 participants (549 males and 460 females) diagnosed with T2DM. Dual energy X-ray absorptiometry was utilized to measure the skeletal muscle index (SMI). Low muscle mass was identified based on the diagnostic criteria established by the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS).ResultsOur findings revealed that low muscle mass was present in 110 male patients (20.04%) and 62 female patients (13.48%). In univariate logistic regression analysis, the C-reactive protein -to-Albumin ratio (CRP/Alb) was significantly associated with low muscle mass in men with T2DM (Odds Ratio (OR) = 1.084 [95%CI = 1.014 ~ 1.158], p = 0.017). The association remained significant after adjusting for age, triglycerides (TG), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), diastolic blood pressure (DBP) and body mass index (BMI) in the multiple logistic regression model (OR = 1.142 [95%CI = 1.028 ~ 1.269], p = 0.013). No significant association between CRP/Alb ratio and the risk of low muscle mass was observed in the female participants (OR = 0.975 [95%CI = 0.868 ~ 1.095], p = 0.672). In multiple linear regression analyses, SMI was positively associated with Alb, BMI, total cholesterol (TC) and HbA1c in males, but negatively related to associated with age. In females, SMI was positively associated with BMI and smoking duration.ConclusionThe CRP/Alb ratio was positively related to low muscle mass in men with T2DM. Background

The aging population presents a significant challenge to global healthcare systems. This situation forces us to increase focus on age-related conditions, such as sarcopenia. Sarcopenia is defined as “age-related loss of skeletal muscle mass plus loss of muscle strength and/or reduced physical performance”, according to 2019 consensus guidelines from the Asian Working Group for sarcopenia (AWGS) [1]. Besides aging, various risk factors, including cancer, obesity have been linked to the development of primary sarcopenia. And conditions such as rheumatic disease and malnutrition can contribute to secondary sarcopenia [2]. Low muscle mass is a potential character of patients with sarcopenia. Consequently, recent years have seen a surge in studies aiming to clarify the pathogenesis of low muscle mass or sarcopenia and develop therapeutic strategies.

Aging and metabolic disorders, like type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) are accompanied by chronic inflammation. Inflammaging and alterations in lipid metabolism significantly contribute to the pathogenesis of sarcopenia [2, 3]. C-reactive protein (CRP) is an protein mediated by several pro-inflammatory cytokines. CRP is commonly increased followed by such conditions as tissue injury and inflammatory defense responses [4]. Elevated levels of CRP are significantly correlated with insulin resistance in males, and are associated with an increased risk for prediabetes or diabetes [5, 6]. Considerable evidence suggests that nutrition supplementation and physical exercise could improve muscle mass and strength among in older adults with sarcopenia [7]. Specifically, protein supplementation, such as leucine-enriched protein at doses of 1.2–6.0 g of leucine per day or β-hydroxy β-methyl butyrate at 2–3 g per day, is an effective method on improving muscle mass [8]. Albumin (Alb) is a biological marker that reflects both nutritional situation and systemic inflammatory activity. Previous research has demonstrated that Alb levels are negatively associated with low muscle mass or sarcopenia [4, 9, 10]. CRP/Alb ratio is a potential marker for emerging inflammation and nutritional status. Although low muscle mass is significantly associated with high CRP/Alb ratio in patients with esophageal, gastric and colorectal cancer [11, 12], no studies have investigated the relationship between CRP/Alb ratio and loss muscle mass in individuals with T2DM. Given that low muscle mass and T2DM share inflammatory and metabolic dysregulation as common features, we evaluated the CRP/Alb ratio and examined its association with low muscle mass in a cohort of patients with T2DM.

Materials and methods

Subjects

This cross-sectional study was conducted at the Endocrinology Department of Qilu Hospital (Qingdao) between September 2017 and September 2019. A cohort of 1009 patients diagnosed with T2DM participated in the study. Eligibility criteria mandated that all participants be at least 20 years of age. Exclusion criteria were pregnancy, severe hepatic or renal dysfunction, significant cardiovascular diseases, active malignancies, physical disabilities and missing data.

The definition of low muscle mass

The skeletal muscle index (SMI) was assessed using dual-energy X-ray absorptiometry (Hologic Discovery A), operated by the same technician to ensure measurement consistency. SMI is calculated by dividing the appendicular skeletal muscle mass (ASM, measured in kilograms) by the square of the participant’s height in meters (kg/m²). Based on the diagnostic criteria established by the Asian Working Group for Sarcopenia (AWGS), low muscle mass is defined as an SMI value below 7.0 kg/m² for males or 5.4 kg/m² for females.

Measurements

Basic clinical parameters were systematically assessed for all participants, including height, weight, systolic blood pressure (SBP), and diastolic blood pressure (DBP). Body mass index (BMI) was calculated as weight (kg) divided by height squared(m2). Following an overnight fast, venous blood samples were collected to analyze C-reactive protein (CRP) levels. Metabolic parameters, including alanine aminotransferase (ALT), aspartate aminotransferase (AST), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), insulin (INS), glycosylated hemoglobin (HbA1c), erythrocyte sedimentation rate (ESR), lipid profiles (triglycerides (TG), total cholesterol (TC), high-density lipoprotein cholesterol (HDL-C), and low-density lipoprotein cholesterol (LDL-C) were measured using standardized assays. CRP/Alb ratio was calculated using the following formula: CRP/Alb*10.

Statistical analysis

All statistical analyses were conducted using SPSS 25.0 (IBM, Armonk, NY, USA). Continuous data with a normal distribution are expressed as means ± standard deviation (SD), while those with a non-normal distribution are expressed as medians (interquartile range, Q1, Q3). Categorical variables were described by numbers and percentages. Continuous variables were compared between the two groups using the Mann-Whitney U test or unpaired t test. Logistic regression models were employed to assess relationships between CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass. Multiple linear regression analysis was utilized to investigate the correlations among CRP/Alb, SMI, and other variables. We performed collinearity diagnostics for all variables included in the multivariable model using variance inflation factors (VIF). VIF < 5 was considered acceptable. Statistical significance was set at p < 0.05.

Results

Baseline characteristics

Baseline characteristics of the study population diagnosed with T2DM were presented in Table 1. A total of 1009 participants were included in this research. Low muscle mass was diagnosed in 110 male patients (20.04%) and 62 female patients (13.48%). Compared to participants with normal muscle mass, those with low muscle mass were older and exhibited lower BMI, SMI, TG, but higher HDL-C in both genders. In addition, among females, no significant difference were observed in DBP between the low and normal muscle mass groups.

The correlation between CRP/Alb and low muscle mass

As shown in Tables 2 and 3, univariate logistic regression model revealed that age, TG, HDL-C, BMI, DBP and CRP/Alb ratio were risk factors for low muscle mass in males with T2DM. In univariate logistic regression analysis, the CRP/Alb ratio was significantly associated with low muscle mass in men with T2DM (Odds Ratio (OR) = 1.084 [95%CI = 1.014 ~ 1.158], p = 0.017). The association remained significant after adjusting for age, TG, HDL-C, DBP and BMI in the multiple logistic regression model (OR = 1.142 [95%CI = 1.028 ~ 1.269], p = 0.013). No significant association between CRP/Alb ratio and the risk of low muscle mass was observed in the female participants (OR = 0.975 [95%CI = 0.868 ~ 1.095], p = 0.672).

Discussion

This cross-sectional study aimed to explore the relationship between CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass in population with T2DM. Results reported in this research that CRP/Alb ratio is significantly related to low muscle mass in males with T2DM. But no significant association observed in female group.

Sarcopenia is an independent negative effect on outcomes after intensive care in old patients. The epidemiology studies displayed that the prevalence of sarcopenia ranged from 5.5% to 25.7%. And males have the dominant status (5.1%-21.0% in males vs. 4.1%-16.3% in women). AWGS 2019 contends that measurements of muscle quality is necessary for diagnosing sarcopenia [1]. Recent studies have focused on the correlation between specific metabolite profiles and skeletal muscle index [13]. Also, Karla E Merz et at. summarized that skeletal muscle is necessary for glucose clearance. Age-related muscle dysfunction can be exacerbated by T2DM [14]. Data shows that T2DM individuals are more prone to low muscle mass or sarcopenia. According to observations from Marika Leenders MSc et al. and Kyung-Soo Kim et al., compared with males without diabetes, those with T2DM had a lower appendicular skeletal muscle mass and impaired functional capacity [15–17]. Low muscle mass and strength gives an increased risk of hypoglycaemia, hospitalization and even mortality [14]. Also, the negative association between muscle mass and the metabolic disorders lead to insulin resistance of glucose [18]. Evaluation of muscle mass, muscle strength, and physical performance is necessary to diagnose sarcopenia, using DXA, bioelectrical impedance analysis (BIA) and other screening methods. Those methods usually require adequate hardware facilities. Identifying the associations between low muscle mass and clinical characteristics remains an important clinical priority [2, 19].

Aging is proved to be related to a chronic low-grade systemic inflammatory state, which is named as “inflammaging”. Inflammation-related parameters has been observed to be related to muscle loss in different disease models, such as cancer. Meanwhile, an association between inflammation and muscle-related body composition (BC) parameters has also been founded in patients with advanced gastric or esophagogastric junction cancer [20]. Sub-clinical inflammation is correlated with diminished muscle oxygenation, exercise capacity and quality of life in T2DM, irrespective of glycemic control [21]. The increase in CRP level is a marker for immunosenescence [22]. According to recent researches, CRP is used to predict all-cause mortality in cardiovascular disease and T2DM. Low CRP levels was founded to be associated with higher exercise capacity, And higher CRP levels are adversely affect exercise tolerance in healthy subjects. Elevated CRP levels in sarcopenia patients indicate chronic low-grade inflammation, which is a pivotal factor in muscle loss [23]. Numerous studies have shown that CRP may contribute to the development of low muscle mass or loss of muscle strength. Meanwhile, due to a meta-analysis by Anna Picca et.al., CRP was positively associated with frailty among hospitalized individuals, but not with muscle atrophy plus dynapenia. The exact interplay between CRP and low muscle mass remains a subject of ongoing debate. However, alterations in CRP levels may be associated with infection or trauma and are insufficient to predict the occurrence of sarcopenia. Therefore, CRP has not been clinically applied [24]. Alb is also an acute-phase protein, whose decrease is a biomarker of inflammation. According to Qiao Xiang’ s data, Alb had weaker correlation with muscle mass, while was strongly correlated with muscle strength and physical performance [25]. As a result of low serum albumin level, degradation of protein synthesis lead to muscle mass and / or function deteriorating, which increases the risk of incident disability in older adults [26]. Consistent with the previous studies, our research also observed that both gender participants with low muscle mass exhibited lower levels of Alb compared with those with normal muscle mass. And Alb level was positively associated with SMI.

The CRP/Alb ratio has emerged as a novel indicator of the balance between inflammation and nutrition status. Previous research has linked this biomarker to preoperative loss of skeletal muscle mass in individuals suffering from gastric, colorectal, and esophageal cancer [12, 27]. A retrospective research conducted in Turkey demonstrated that combining the CRP/Alb ratio with age and handgrip strength could effectively identify the risk of malnutrition among older adults with sarcopenia [28]. In hemodialysis cohorts, males exhibiting lower muscle quality faced a substantially higher risk of sarcopenia [29]. Building on these findings, our current cross-sectional study utilized Chinese population data to investigate the association between CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass specially in the context of T2DM. Even after adjusting for confounding variables, the CRP/Alb ratio still remained significantly associated with the prevalence of low muscle mass in males with T2DM. CRP/Alb ratio is positively associated with the prevalence of low muscle mass among male patients with T2DM. But no similar results were observed in females. Gender-specific disparities regarding sarcopenia or low muscle mass risk in the T2DM population remain controversial. For instance, a cross-sectional analysis of community-dwelling older adults (≥ 60 years) in New Taipei City reported that older women with poor glycemic control faced higher odds of possible sarcopenia (including reduction of skeletal muscle strength or decreased skeletal muscle mass) [30]. We explored the interaction effects between sex and CRP/Alb ratio using a formal interaction analysis. Our data exhibited that the interaction between gender and CRP/Alb ratio to estimate low muscle mass was not statistically significant (p = 0.122). Meanwhile, In our cohort, the mean FBG level among females was lower than that of males, which might partially explain this discrepancy. Subsequent expansion of the sample size is required to verify these gender-specific observations.

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, the small number of female participants with sarcopenia and the lack of a priori sample size calculation may limit the statistical power to detect the association between CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass in females. Second, methods that evaluate muscle strength and physical performance are needed to estimate sarcopenia according to the diagnostic criteria. Besides, as this research was a cross-sectional design, our findings can only demonstrate associations with prevalent low muscle mass. Further prospective studies are warranted to determine whether the CRP/Alb ratio could be a screening tool for low muscle mass risk in patients with T2DM. Finally, lifestyle factors, such as physical activity and dietary structure, maybe have potential relationship with muscle mass, these factors should be involved in further in-depth investigations.

In conclusion, our findings suggested that the CRP/Alb ratio is significantly associated with low muscle mass in males with T2DM. The prognostic value of this ratio for the future onset of clinical low muscle mass needs further exploration. Large-scale studies are required to elucidate the correlation between the CRP/Alb ratio and low muscle mass. This is essential for advancing early diagnostic strategies and developing timely therapeutic interventions for low muscle mass in population with T2DM.
