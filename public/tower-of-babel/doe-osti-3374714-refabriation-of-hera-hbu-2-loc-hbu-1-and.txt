 1. INTRODUCTION & BACKGROUND
 The development of advanced fuels for light water reactors (LWRs) is essential for improved fuel
performance and safety, including performance margins, uprates, and higher burnup. LWR fuels are
generally on the order of 4-m long, while the core height of research reactors is on the order of 1 m. This
mismatch in length means that commercial fuel cannot be used directly for follow-on experiments in
research reactors without some modification. Thus, the refabrication of preirradiated fuel is necessary for
characterizing fuel behavior during steady and transient irradiations.
 The developed fuel refabrication process involves six steps, as illustrated in Figure 1 below. This
process allows for the fabrication of fuel to achieve a desired form factor and selection from the parent
material for its representative interest. Defueling enables customizable rodlet internal components, such
as insulator pellets and instrumentation. Rodlet endcap welding and welding under pressure simulate
reactor conditions or targeted pressure differentials. Refabricated rodlets undergo leak testing and visual
examination to validate the rodlet condition.

Figure 1. Illustration of the major steps for fuel refabrication at the Hot Fuel Examination Facility
(HFEF).

 Historically, Idaho National Laboratory (INL) refabricated and instrumented commercially irradiated
rods for testing in the 1960s–1980s, such as the use of refabricated rods in the Power Burst Facility. Since
that time INL has made tremendous progress developing this capability and performing refabrication on
various fuel rodlets. For example, in 2021, INL demonstrated fuel refabrication capability using rodlets
irradiated in the Advanced Test Reactor (ATR). And in 2024, INL completed refabrication of the HERA-
HBu-1 test rod, which was a segment from a commercial rod with a distinct rod design.

 2. RESULTS
 In 2025, INL completed refabrication of the HERA-HBu-2 test rod, as well as performed significant
work on refabrication of the LOC-HBu-1 and LOC-HBu-2 test rods.
 Cutting plans were developed based on prior information provided to the program to maximize the
rodlet length and fueled region for each test. However, nondestructive examination (NDE) revealed a
mismatch between the design and the length of fuel in each span due to a greater-than-expected grid
spacer shadow, prompting a revision to the cutting plans shown in Figure 2.

Figure 2. Progressive Gamma Scan data overlayed on cutting plans for the 9EU rod, from which HERA-
HBu-1, LOC-HBu-1, and LOC-HBu-2 were cut.

 Preliminary chemistry results of a sample immediately below HERA-HBu-1 indicated 67.2
MWd/kgU. The initial planned depth for fuel removal was approximately 0.5 inches, but additional fuel
was removed from each segment to avoid peaking due to the grid spacer shadow. Table 1 summarizes key
rodlet dimensions and characteristics.
 Fuel was removed from HERA-HBu-2, LOC-HBu-1, and LOC-HBu-2 first with a precision mill in-
cell that utilizes a vacuum system to capture removed material. A lesson learned from the refabrication of

 3

HERA-HBu-1 in fiscal year 2024 (FY-24), smaller diameter drill bits were selected to allow for multiple
passes to remove the fuel where before it was essentially a single plunge into the rodlet. The milled rodlet
was wire brushed to remove residue inside the rodlet, then reaming and chemical removal in a nitric acid
bath was used to finish cleaning the rodlet surfaces, ensuring the desired internals and endcaps could be
inserted.
 Although refabrication of the LOC-HBU-1 and LOC-HBU-2 pins was planned to be completed with
HERA-HBu-2, the Department of Energy (DOE) directed a change in priority to pivot to refabricating
two additional HERA-HBu pins ahead of LOC-HBu-1 and LOC-HBu-2. As a result, only HERA-HBu-2
was welded in FY-25, and the sectioning of HERA-HBu-3 and HERA-HBu-4 were completed, with
defueling in process at the time of writing this report, with the plan to weld LOC-HBu-1 and -2
immediately after welding HERA-HBu-3 and -4 in FY-26.
 Precisely machined endcaps were press-fit onto both ends of HERA-HBu-2, and circumferential
welds were performed using an in-cell automated lathe welder. The rodlet was evacuated and backfilled
to a low pressure in a chamber via a weep hole in the rodlet endcap, and the weep hole was seal-welded at
the desired rodlet pressure. The pin was leak-tested in a bell jar under vacuum after sealing and underwent
visual exams to verify weld integrity.
 Post-refabrication inspections performed so far include visual examination, He-leak check, and ring
gauge verification. Profilometry, n-radiography, and axial gamma scans will be performed but have not
been completed by the time of the writing of this report. These inspections aimed to verify the overall
state of the rod and welds, ensure leak tightness, and crosscheck the burnup profile of the refabricated rod.

 3. CONCLUSION
 Refabrication of fuel rods for follow on experiments continues to be a key enabling technology and
process. Without this process it would be impractical to perform many if not all the planned experiments
to be irradiated in the TREAT reactor. Despite the successes achieved, opportunities for improvement
exist which are expected to reduce the time and cost associated with refabrication efforts. For example,
the current mechanical defueling operation is performed using a benchtop mini-mill. A more rigid
numerically controlled mill, planned to be installed in FY-26, and is expected to have more precise
tolerances, thus enabling more precision mechanical fuel removal. Some expansion of capability will
require further development. For example, the upcoming refabrication efforts associated with the HERA-

 4

HBu-3 and -4 experiments are derived from fuels which have a Cr layer on the Zr cladding. The removal
of the Cr layer to enable welding without the complication of the known low melting temperature Cr/Zr
eutectic will require development. A final example is the development of leak checking equipment and
process to enable a leak check to be performed at an intermediate stage of refabrication. This would be of
most use to perform after the completion of the endcap circumferential welds, but before the final seal
weld.
