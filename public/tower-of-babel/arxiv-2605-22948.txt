A Compositional Characterization of the Z Z -relation via Closure and Additivity
Aleksa Joksimović

Abstract
This paper gives a structural explanation for the Z Z -relation by modelling pitch-class sets as complete weighted graphs and encoding their interval content in a composition of n n via an additivity rule. We introduce the realization number R ⁡ ( μ , n ) R(\mu,n) — the count of T / I T/I -inequivalent compositions producing the same interval multiset μ \mu — and show that the Z Z -relation is precisely the condition R ⁡ ( μ , n ) ≥ 2 R(\mu,n)\geq 2 . We prove that the Z Z -relation cannot occur at cardinality k = 3 k=3 in any ℤ n \mathbb{Z}_{n} , construct an explicit primitive Z Z -related pair at k = 4 k=4 for every n n divisible by 4 4 with n ≥ 8 n\geq 8 , and establish a Scaling Theorem showing that any Z Z -pair in ℤ m \mathbb{Z}_{m} propagates to ℤ d ​ m \mathbb{Z}_{dm} for all d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 . All results are verified computationally for ℤ 12 \mathbb{Z}_{12} and ℤ 19 \mathbb{Z}_{19} .

Introduction

Take two pitch-class sets and compute their interval vectors. If the vectors
match but the sets are not related by transposition or inversion, the sets are
called
Z
-related. This has been known since Forte’s 1973 classification

[
1
]
, and there are exactly 23 such pairs in 12-tone equal
temperament. What has never been satisfactorily explained is why they exist —
what it is about these particular sets that forces two different configurations
to produce the same interval counts.

The standard tools work in the frequency domain. The interval vector is
equivalent to the squared magnitudes of the discrete Fourier transform

[
2
]
, and this observation underpins the theory of homometric sets

[
3
,
4
]
. These approaches characterize the

Z
-relation precisely but do not explain its mechanism.

This paper provides a structural explanation. A pitch-class set in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}

written in ascending order as
0=p_{0}<p_{1}<\cdots<p_{k-1}
has
consecutive differences
s_{i}=p_{i}-p_{i-1}
forming a composition of
n
.
Every interval class in the set is the interval class of a partial sum of these
steps — the entire interval structure is encoded in the composition. The

Z
-relation reduces to a combinatorial question: how many inequivalent
compositions of
n
produce the same multiset of partial sums? We call this
count the realization number
R(\mu,n)
and use it to give clean proofs of
when
Z
-relation can and cannot occur, and to construct explicit
Z
-related
pairs in full generality.

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
defines pitch-class
graphs and their two fundamental properties: closure and additivity.
Section
3
introduces the realization number and
characterizes the
Z
-relation. Section
4
proves
Z
-relation cannot
occur at
k=3
. Section
5
verifies all 23
Z
-related
pairs in
\mathbb{Z}_{12}
and extends to
\mathbb{Z}_{19}
.
Section
6
proves the Scaling Theorem and the general
k=4

construction. Section
7
situates the framework within the
existing literature and states open problems.

Pitch-Class Graphs

Definition 2.1

(Pitch class graph)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a pitch-class set. The
pitch class
graph
of
P
is the complete weighted graph
G(P)=(V,E,w)
where
V=P
,

E
is the set of all unordered pairs of distinct elements of
P
, and

w(\{p,q\})=\mathrm{ic}(p,q)
, where

\mathrm{ic}(p,q)=\min(|p-q|,\,n-|p-q|)\in\{1,\ldots,\lfloor n/2\rfloor\}.

The dihedral group
D_{n}
acts on
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
by transpositions
T_{s}(p)=p+s

and inversions
I_{s}(p)=s-p\pmod{n}
. Since
\mathrm{ic}(g(p),g(q))=\mathrm{ic}(p,q)
for all
g\in D_{n}
,
T/I
-equivalent sets produce isomorphic
pitch class graphs.

Lemma 2.2
.

\mathrm{ic}(g(p),g(q))=\mathrm{ic}(p,q)
for all
g\in D_{n}
.

Proof.

For transposition:
|T_{s}(p)-T_{s}(q)|=|(p+s)-(q+s)|=|p-q|
. For
inversion:
|I_{s}(p)-I_{s}(q)|=|(s-p)-(s-q)|=|p-q|
. In both cases

\mathrm{ic}
is preserved.
∎

Valid pitch class graphs must satisfy two properties.

Definition 2.3

(Closure)
.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
have cardinality
k
, written in ascending order
with
p_{0}=0
. The
steps
of
P
are

s_{i}=p_{i}-p_{i-1}\quad(i=1,\ldots,k-1),\qquad s_{k}=n-p_{k-1}.

The steps form a
composition
of
n
— an ordered partition of
n
into

k
positive parts — satisfying the
closure property
:

s_{1}+s_{2}+\cdots+s_{k}=n.

Definition 2.4

(Additivity)
.

Once the steps of
P
are fixed, every interval class is determined. The
interval class between adjacent pitch classes is
\mathrm{ic}(p_{i-1},p_{i})=\min(s_{i},n-s_{i})
. The interval class between any two pitch classes
p_{i}
and

p_{j}
with
j>i
is:

\mathrm{ic}(p_{i},p_{j})=\min\!\left(\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{j}s_{\ell},\;n-\sum_{\ell=i+1}^{j}s_{\ell}\right).

Non-adjacent interval classes are called
diagonals
and are each
determined by a single addition and a single min. This is the
additivity
rule
: the interval class between any two pitch classes equals the interval
class of the sum of the steps between them.

The collection of all
\binom{k}{2}
interval classes of
P
is the

interval multiset

\mu(P)
, equivalently the interval vector written as
a multiset. Since every interval class is determined by the composition

(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
, the entire interval structure of
P
is encoded in its
composition of
n
.

The Realization Number

Definition 3.1

(Realization)
.

A
realization
of an interval multiset
\mu
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
is a
composition
(s_{1},\ldots,s_{k})
of
n
into
k
positive parts whose interval
multiset, computed via the additivity rule, equals
\mu
. Since cyclic rotations
of a composition correspond to transpositions of the underlying pitch-class set
and reversals correspond to inversions, two realizations are
equivalent

if one can be obtained from the other by a cyclic rotation or a reversal of the
step sequence.

Definition 3.2

(Realization number)
.

The
realization number

R(\mu,n)
is the number of distinct realizations
of
\mu
in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, counted up to equivalence.

Proposition 3.3
.

Two pitch-class sets
P_{1},P_{2}\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
are
Z
-related if and
only if
R(\mu,n)\geq 2
, where
\mu=\mu(P_{1})=\mu(P_{2})
.

Proof.

Z
-related means same interval multiset and not
T/I
equivalent. The first
condition is
\mu(P_{1})=\mu(P_{2})=\mu
. The second is that their compositions
are not equivalent under cyclic rotation or reversal. Together these give

R(\mu,n)\geq 2
.
∎

An interval multiset with
R(\mu,n)=1
is
reconstructible
: its
interval vector uniquely determines the set up to
T/I
equivalence. An
interval multiset with
R(\mu,n)\geq 2
is
non-reconstructible
: two or
more
T/I
-inequivalent sets share it.

Z

Proposition 4.1
.

For any
n\geq 3
and any interval multiset
\mu
arising from a trichord in

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
, we have
R(\mu,n)=1
.

Proof.

Let
P\subseteq\mathbb{Z}_{n}
be a trichord with
p_{0}=0
and steps

(s_{1},s_{2},s_{3})
. By the additivity rule,

\mu(P)=\{\{\mathrm{ic}(s_{1}),\,\mathrm{ic}(s_{2}),\,\mathrm{ic}(s_{1}+s_{2})\}\}
.
Since
s_{3}=n-s_{1}-s_{2}
, the third step contributes

\mathrm{ic}(s_{3})=\mathrm{ic}(s_{1}+s_{2})
, already determined by
s_{1}
and
s_{2}
.

Suppose
(t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
is a second realization of
\mu
, inequivalent under
cyclic rotation and reversal. Then the two three-element multisets of interval
classes are equal. Any two trichord compositions with the same interval class
multiset must assign the same values to
\mathrm{ic}(s_{1}),\mathrm{ic}(s_{2}),\mathrm{ic}(s_{3})
in some order. The six permutations of three elements
correspond exactly to the three cyclic rotations and three reversals of the
composition, i.e. to transpositions and inversions of the underlying set.
Therefore any two realizations at
k=3
are equivalent.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

The minimum cardinality at which
Z
-relation can occur in any
\mathbb{Z}_{n}

is
k=4
.

Proof.

By Proposition
4.1
,
R(\mu,n)=1
for all
k\leq 3
. Theorem
6.3
in Section
6
exhibits
R(\mu,n)=2
at

k=4
for all
n
divisible by
4
with
n\geq 8
.
∎

6
The Scaling Theorem and the General
k=4
Construction

The two results in this section are complementary. The Scaling Theorem shows
that
Z
-relation propagates upward through the divisibility lattice: any

Z
-pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
generates an infinite family of
Z
-pairs in

\mathbb{Z}_{dm}
for all
d\geq 1
. The General
k=4
Construction shows
that primitive
Z
-pairs — those not arising as scalings — exist at every

n
divisible by
4
and gives them all explicitly.

6.1
The Scaling Theorem

Theorem 6.1

(Scaling)
.

Let
d\geq 1
and
m\geq 3
. If
(P_{1},P_{2})
is a
Z
-related pair in

\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, then

dP_{1}=\{dp\bmod dm:p\in P_{1}\},\quad dP_{2}=\{dp\bmod dm:p\in P_{2}\}

is a
Z
-related pair in
\mathbb{Z}_{dm}
.

Proof.

Same interval multiset.
For any
p,q\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
:

\mathrm{ic}(dp,dq,dm)=\min\bigl(d|p-q|_{m},\,dm-d|p-q|_{m}\bigr)=d\cdot\mathrm{ic}(p,q,m).

Therefore
\mu(dP_{i})=d\cdot\mu(P_{i})
for
i=1,2
, and since

\mu(P_{1})=\mu(P_{2})
, we have
\mu(dP_{1})=\mu(dP_{2})
.

T/I
inequivalence.
Suppose
dP_{1}\sim_{T/I}dP_{2}
in

\mathbb{Z}_{dm}
. Then there exists
t\in\mathbb{Z}_{dm}
such that

\{dp+t:p\in P_{1}\}\equiv\{dq:q\in P_{2}\}\pmod{dm}
or the inversion
analog. Since all elements of
dP_{1}
and
dP_{2}
are multiples of
d
, we need

t\equiv 0\pmod{d}
, so
t=ds
for some
s\in\mathbb{Z}_{m}
. Then

P_{1}+s\equiv P_{2}\pmod{m}
, giving
P_{1}\sim_{T}P_{2}
in
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
, a
contradiction. The inversion case is identical.
∎

(Inheritance)
.

If
m\mid n
and
\mathbb{Z}_{m}
has a
Z
-related pair at cardinality
k
, then

\mathbb{Z}_{n}
has a
Z
-related pair at cardinality
k
.

Proof.

Set
d=n/m
and apply Theorem
6.1
.
∎

The Scaling Theorem partitions all
Z
-pairs into
derived pairs
(scalings
of a smaller
Z
-pair) and
primitive pairs
(not arising as any scaling).
The inheritance corollary provides a sufficient but not necessary condition for

Z
-relation in
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
: a
Z
-pair may exist at a cardinality smaller
than any proper divisor can supply, via a primitive pair arising from the
intrinsic arithmetic of
\mathbb{Z}_{n}
itself. The case
n=20
illustrates
this:
k_{\min}(10)=5
, so inheritance from
\mathbb{Z}_{10}
predicts no

Z
-pair below

, yet

, achieved by the primitive pair
from Theorem
6.3
with

and

.

7
Relationship to Existing Invariants

7.1
The DFT and homometry

The interval vector of
P
is the inverse DFT of the squared magnitudes

|\hat{f}_{P}(\omega^{j})|^{2}

[
2
]
. Two sets are
homometric
—
sharing the same interval vector — if and only if their DFT magnitude spectra
agree. The DFT characterizes the
Z
-relation precisely but cannot separate

Z
-related sets, since both have identical magnitudes by definition and the
phase information that would distinguish them is discarded.

Reconstructibility theory explains this structurally. The condition

R(\mu,n)\geq 2
is the condition that two distinct phase patterns are
compatible with the same magnitude spectrum — the discrete phase retrieval
problem
[
4
]
.

7.2
The Rosenblatt-Seymour factorization

Rosenblatt and Seymour
[
4
]
characterize homometry via
convolution: two measures
A
and
B
are homometric if and only if
A=C*D

and
B=C*D^{*}
for some
C
and
D
. In the group ring

\mathbb{Z}[x]/(x^{n}-1)
, writing
[P]=\sum_{p\in P}x^{p}
, this is

[P_{1}]=[C][D]
and
[P_{2}]=[C][D]^{*}
. The realization number counts the
number of inequivalent compositions realizing
\mu
, which is the number of
distinct factorizations of the form required by the Rosenblatt-Seymour theorem,
up to
T/I
equivalence.
