An Adaptive Kalman Filter that Learns the Coloring Dynamics of the Process Noise
Mohammad Almuhaihi, Dennis S. Bernstein

Abstract
In many applications of state estimation, the process noise is colored; this case is addressed by applying the standard Kalman filter (KF) to dynamics that are augmented with the coloring dynamics. The present paper considers the case where the coloring dynamics are unknown, which renders the estimates obtained from the standard approach suboptimal. To address this problem, the present paper proposes an adaptive technique based on the principle that, if the measurement noise is white, then the innovations sequence is white if and only if the process noise is white. Leveraging this fact, an Innovations-Whitening Adaptive Kalman Filter (IWAKF) is developed, which learns the process-noise coloring online. By embedding an unknown coloring filter in a state-augmentation framework, IWAKF adapts its parameters by minimizing the empirical autocorrelation of the innovations, thereby driving them toward whiteness and restoring near-optimality without prior knowledge of the coloring dynamics.

The use of the innovations process for adaptivity traces back to the seminal study

This bridge from Mehra’s optimality test to real-time identification closes the gap for colored process noise and motivates the development that follows.

II-A

Coloring Filter

The standard approach for the case of colored process noise is to augment the system with a coloring filter that captures the autocorrelation of
v_{k}
. As a representative model, consider the second-order strictly proper transfer function

\displaystyle H({\bf q})=\frac{\gamma_{3}{\bf q}+\gamma_{2}}{{\bf q}^{2}+\gamma_{1}{\bf q}+\gamma_{0}},

(11)

where
{\bf q}
is the forward shift operator and
\gamma_{0,1,2,3}
are constants. In practice, however, these coloring dynamics are rarely known a priori. The objective of this article is therefore to develop an online algorithm that adapts to the unknown process-noise coloring in real time, thereby restoring near-optimality by driving the innovations towards whiteness.

III
Whiteness of Process Noise and Innovations

This section establishes an equivalence between the whiteness of the innovations and the whiteness of the process noise under the assumption that the process noise
v_{k}
and measurement noise
n_{k}
are mutually independent and measurement noise
n_{k}
is white.

Proposition 1.

Assume that the process noise
v_{k}
and measurement noise
n_{k}
for a linear time-invariant (LTI) system are mutually independent and measurement noise
n_{k}
is white.
Then,

\Gamma_{z}(\ell)=0\ \ \forall\,\ell\geq 1

(14)

if and only if

\Gamma_{v}(\ell)=0\ \ \forall\,\ell\geq 1,

(15)

where

\displaystyle\Gamma_{z}(\ell)\triangleq\mathrm{cov}(z_{k},z_{k-\ell}),\quad\Gamma_{v}(\ell)\triangleq\mathrm{cov}(v_{k},v_{k-\ell})

Proof:

We rewrite the data assimilation step by substituting (
5
) into (
8
), which yields

\displaystyle x_{k}^{\mathrm{da}}

\displaystyle=x_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+K_{k}\,(Ce_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+n_{k}).

Substituting back into (
9
) yields

\displaystyle e_{k}^{\mathrm{da}}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-x_{k}^{\mathrm{da}}

\displaystyle=x_{k}-x_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}-K_{k}\,(Ce_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+n_{k})

\displaystyle=e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}-K_{k}\,(Ce_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+n_{k})

\displaystyle=(I-K_{k}C)e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}-K_{k}n_{k}.

Propagating to the next forecast error yields

\displaystyle e_{k+1}^{\mathrm{fc}}

\displaystyle=Ae_{k}^{\mathrm{da}}+v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=A\!\left((I-K_{k}C)e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}-K_{k}n_{k}\right)+v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=A(I-K_{k}C)\,e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+v_{k+1}-AK_{k}n_{k}

\displaystyle=F_{k}\,e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}+v_{k+1}-AK_{k}n_{k},

where
F_{k}=A(I-K_{k}C)
, which is assumed to be stable, that is,
\rho(F)<1
.
Then
e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}
is unfolded under steady state as

\displaystyle e_{k}^{\mathrm{fc}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}F^{i-1}(v_{k-i+1}-AKn_{k-i})

Substituting back into (
5
) gives

\displaystyle z_{k}=n_{k}+\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}CF^{j}v_{k-j}-\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}CF^{i-1}AKn_{k-i}

Define

\displaystyle\mathcal{G}({\bf q}^{-1})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}CF^{i-1}AK{\bf q}^{-i}

(16)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}({\bf q}^{-1})

\displaystyle\triangleq\sum_{j=0}^{\infty}CF^{j}{\bf q}^{-j}

(17)

where
{\bf q}^{-1}
is the backward-shift operator. Rewriting yields

\displaystyle z_{k}=(I-\mathcal{G}({\bf q}^{-1}))n_{k}+\mathcal{H}({\bf q}^{-1})v_{k}

Transforming to the frequency domain gives

\displaystyle Z(e^{\jmath\phi})=(I-G(e^{\jmath\phi}))N(e^{\jmath\phi})+H(e^{\jmath\phi})v_{k},

where

\displaystyle G(e^{\jmath\phi})

\displaystyle=C(e^{\jmath\phi}I-F)^{-1}AKe^{-jw},

(18)

\displaystyle H(e^{\jmath\phi})

\displaystyle=C(e^{\jmath\phi}I-F)^{-1}.

(19)

Using linear spectral mapping, the Power Spectral Density (PSD) of the signal
z
can be computed as

\displaystyle\Phi_{z}(\phi)=T_{n}\phi_{\rm n}(\phi)T_{n}^{*}+T_{v}\Phi_{v}(\phi)T_{v}^{*}

and the cross-correlation between
n
and
v
is zero since independence between process and measurement noise is assumed.
Since
n_{k}
is white, it has a PSD of
R
and the terms
T_{n}
and
T_{v}
are
(I-G)
and
H

\displaystyle\Phi_{z}(\phi)=(I-G)R(I-G)^{*}+H\Phi_{v}(\phi)H^{*}.

(20)

Assuming that
\Phi_{v}(\phi)
is white with covariance

yields

\displaystyle\Phi_{z}(\phi)=(I-G)R(I-G)^{*}+HQH^{*},

which implies that
\Phi_{z}(\phi)
is constant across all frequencies, thereby ensuring that the signal

is white. This establishes sufficiency.

To prove necessity, let
\Phi_{z}(\phi)=S
. Substituting back into (
20

\displaystyle S=(I-G)R(I-G)^{*}+H\Phi_{v}(\phi)H^{*}.

Since
(C,F)
is observable, which is implied by
(A,C)
detectability,

has full column rank.
Thus, its pseudo-inverse

exists, resulting in

\displaystyle\Phi_{v}(\phi)=H^{\dagger}(S-(I-G)R(I-G)^{*})H^{{\dagger}*}

which holds only if
\Phi_{v}(\phi)
is constant across all frequencies, which is the definition of whiteness.

Innovations-Whitening Adaptive Kalman Filter (IWAKF)

perfect state augmentation results in a white innovations sequence. The Innovations-Whitening Adaptive Kalman Filter (IWAKF) utilizes this fact to adapt to the coloring dynamics of process noise. Let

V
DISCUSSION AND FUTURE RESEARCH

This paper developed an Innovations-Whitening Adaptive Kalman Filter (IWAKF) that learns the coloring dynamics of the process noise online by minimizing the empirical autocorrelation of the innovations under white measurement noise.
Simulations on a damped-pendulum example indicate that IWAKF attains performance comparable to a perfectly augmented KF that has complete knowledge of the true coloring filter, thereby restoring near-optimal estimation without prior spectral information.
The analysis herein relies on LTI assumptions to establish the equivalence between innovations whiteness and process-noise whiteness. A primary direction is to extend the theory and implementation to linear time-varying (LTV) systems with time-indexed dynamics and outputs, where conditions such as uniform detectability/stabilizability, bounded time-varying gains, and persistence of excitation replace their LTI counterparts. This requires a whiteness characterization that is valid under nonstationary spectra, together with finite-window identification tools and change detection to track drifting coloring dynamics.
