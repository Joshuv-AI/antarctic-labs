On superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genus 5 whose automorphism groups contain ( ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ ) 3 (\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
Ryo Ohashi, Momonari Kudo

Abstract
While the numbers of superspecial curves of genus at most 3 are well understood, and several computational approaches have been developed to count superspecial curves of genus 4 with large automorphism groups, much less is known in higher genera. In this paper, we construct a feasible algorithm to enumerate superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genus 5 whose automorphism groups contain ( ℤ / 2 ​ ℤ ) 3 \hskip-0.85358pt(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}\hskip-0.56905pt . We implement and executing our algorithm in Magma, we succeeded in enumerating such superspecial curves in every characteristic 11 < p < 1000 \hskip-0.56905pt11<p<1000 .



keywords
Curves of genus five,  Hyperelliptic curves,  Superspecial curves.

Introduction

Throughout, all the complexities are measured by the number of arithmetic operations in
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-0.85358pt
for a prime
p
, unless otherwise noted.
Soft-O notation omits logarithmic factors.
A
curve
means a non-singular projective variety of dimension one.
Let
K
be a field of characteristic
p>0
, and
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}
its algebraic closure.
A genus-
g
curve
C
defined over
K
is said to be
superspecial
when its Jacobian variety is isomorphic (over
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{K}
) to a product of supersingular elliptic curves.
Superspecial curves are important objects not only in theory but also in practical applications such as isogeny-based cryptography, which is a candidate for post-quantum cryptography.

For a given pair
(g,p)
, it is known that there exist only finitely many superspecial genus-
g
curves over
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
, and thus the problem of determining the number of them is of fundamental importance.
For the cases where
g\leq 3
, this problem has been completely solved for all
p>0
, based on the theory of principally polarized abelian varieties.
We refer to
10
for a survey of related works.

In contrast to the cases where
g\leq 3
, the problem for
g\geq 4
has not been solved for all primes.
However, over the last decade, several computational approaches for
g=4
have been proposed;  see
6
,
5
for the non-hyperelliptic cases, and
7
,
13
,
9
,
14
,
20
for the hyperelliptic cases.
In particular, Kudo, Nakagawa, and Takagi proposed in
9
an algorithm to find a superspecial hyperelliptic genus-4 curve with automorphism group containing
\mathbb{Z}/6\mathbb{Z}
, whose extended abstract was presented at CASC2022.
In
13
and
14
, the authors presented enumeration algorithms for superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genus 4 whose automorphism groups contain the Klein 4-group (or the dihedral group of order 8).
Their computational results in
14
implies an expectation that there exists a superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus 4 in characteristic
p
for any
p
with
p\geq 19
.

The next target is the case where
g=5
; however, there is almost no prior work on the enumeration of superspecial genus-5 curves (except for Kudo-Harashita’s result
8
on the trigonal case in characteristic
p\leq 13
).
In this paper, analogously to our prior works
13
and
14
for genus-4 curves, we restrict our attention to hyperelliptic curves of genus 5 with automorphism group containing
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
.
The main reason for studying such curves is that, via the decomposition of Jacobian varieties described in Section
3
, their superspeciality can be reduced to that of curves of genus at most 2, which allows us to construct superspecial curves efficiently.
Exploiting this property, we construct a feasible algorithm to enumerate such curves in characteristic
p>11
;  for an explicit description, see Section
5
.
Also, we can show that all the computations in our algorithm are carried out over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, and the complexity of our algorithm is proved to be
\widetilde{O}(p^{3})
.
By implementing and executing the algorithm in
Magma
Computational Algebra System, we obtain the following main result:

Theorem 1.1
.

There exists a superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus 5 whose automorphism group contains
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
in any characteristic
23\leq p<1000
, except for

\displaystyle p=\,

\displaystyle 37,41,43,53,61,67,73,97,181,193,197,233,241,277,313,331,373,421,

\displaystyle 541,571,613,643,673,709,769,877,977.

The number of
\accentset{{\cc@style\underline{\mskip 13.0mu}}}{\mathbb{F}}_{p}
-isomorphism
classes of such curves are summarized in Tables
2
and
3
.

The rest of this paper is structured as follows:
Section

In Sections
3

The two curves

1.

In this case, there exists an isomorphism

2.

In this case, there exists an isomorphism

3.

In this case, there exists an isomorphism

\psi:(x,y)\longmapsto\biggl(\frac{x+1}{x-1},\frac{\kappa y}{(x-1)^{6}}\biggr)\,\text{ with }\,\kappa^{2}=(2+a)(2+b)(2+c).\vskip-5.12149pt

4.

\psi:(x,y)\longmapsto\biggl(\frac{x+i}{x-i},\frac{\kappa y}{(x-i)^{6}}\biggr)\,\text{ with }\,\kappa^{2}=(2+a)(2+b)(2+c).\vskip-5.12149pt

The following corollary plays a central role in our algorithm (Algorithm
1

Corollary 3.3
.

The curve
H_{a,b,c}
is superspecial if and only if
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
are all supersingular and
C
is superspecial.
Moreover, if
H_{a,b,c}
is superspecial, then
a,b,c
all lie in

\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.

Proof.

It follows from the proof of Theorem
3.2
that there exists an isogeny

\phi:{\rm Jac}(H_{a,b,c})\longrightarrow E_{1}\times E_{2}\times E_{3}\times{\rm Jac}(C)\vskip 6.25958pt

whose degree is a power of
2
, which implies the former assertion.
Moreover, the latter assertion follows from the superspeciality of
C:y^{2}=x(x-1)(x-\lambda)(x-\mu)(x-\nu)
.
Indeed, it follows from
(

that

1-\lambda=\frac{4}{2-a},\ \ 1-\mu=\frac{4}{2-b},\ \ 1-\nu=\frac{4}{2-c}\vskip 3.69885pt

all belong to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
.
This implies that

also belong to
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, as desired.

Lemma 4.1
.

By squaring each element of the sets on both sides, we obtain the equality

(4.1)

2

(4.2)

4

(4.3)

Comparing the equalities (

5
Algorithms to enumerate superspecial our curves

In this section, we propose algorithms to enumerate superspecial hyperelliptic genus-5 curves whose automorphism groups contain
\hskip-0.56905pt\mathbf{C}_{2}^{3}

5.1
Generic case:
{\rm Aut}(H)\cong\mathbf{C}_{2}^{3}

As a consequence of the results established in Section
3
, we can construct superspecial hyperelliptic genus-5 curves of the form
H_{a,b,c}
given in equation (
2.1
) from superspecial genus-2 curves in Rosenhain form.
In particular, in the generic case, the isomorphism testing among these curves can be efficiently performed using Theorem
4.5

Then, the algorithm is summarized in Algorithm
1
.

Algorithm 1

Input:
A prime integer
p>11
.

Output:
A list of isomorphism classes of of superspecial curves
\hskip-0.56905ptH_{a,b,c}
of Type
4-1
in characteristic
p

Step 1:

List all superspecial genus-2 curves

in Rosenhain form, and denote the set of such curves by
\textsf{SSp}_{2}(p)
.
This can be done by generating the superspecial Richelot isogeny graph (cf.
(
5
, Section 5A)
).

Step 2:

For each
C_{\lambda,\mu,\nu}\hskip-0.56905pt\in\textsf{SSp}_{2}(p)
, check whether the three elliptic curves
E_{1},E_{2},E_{3}
given in (
3.1
) are all supersingular.
If this is the case, then compute
a,b,c
by using the relation (
3.2
); specifically, set

a\coloneqq 2\frac{\lambda+1}{\lambda-1},\quad b\coloneqq 2\frac{\mu+1}{\mu-1},\,\text{ and }\,c\coloneqq 2\frac{\nu+1}{\nu-1}\vskip-4.55244pt

and store the resulting triple
(a,b,c)
.

Step 3:

For each
(a,b,c)
stored in
Step 2
, check whether none of the permutations of the following six sets is already contained in the list
\hskip-0.85358pt\mathcal{L}
:

\displaystyle\{a,b,c\},\{-a,-b,-c\},\hskip 1.42262pt

\displaystyle\textstyle\{\frac{12-2a}{2+a},\frac{12-2b}{2+b},\frac{12-2c}{2+c}\},\{-\frac{12-2a}{2+a},-\frac{12-2b}{2+b},-\frac{12-2c}{2+c}\},

\displaystyle\textstyle\{\frac{12+2a}{2-a},\frac{12+2b}{2-b},\frac{12+2c}{2-c}\},\{-\frac{12+2a}{2-a},-\frac{12+2b}{2-b},-\frac{12+2c}{2-c}\}.

If this is the case, then we add
(a,b,c)
to the list
\mathcal{L}
as long as

.

Step 4:

Output the list of our curves

for

.

All computations occupied in Algorithm
1
can be done over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}
, and its complexity is
\widetilde{O}(p^{3})
.
Indeed,

Concluding remarks

We presented an algorithm with complexity
\widetilde{O}(p^{3})
arithmetic operations over
\mathbb{F}_{p^{2}}\hskip-1.13809pt
for enumerating superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genus 5 whose automorphism groups contain
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
.
Executing our algorithm in
Magma
Computational Algebra System, we succeeded in enumerating such curves for primes
p
with
11<p<1000
.

Our computational results also reveal an interesting phenomenon:  there are many primes
p
for which no superspecial hyperelliptic curve of genus
5
whose automorphism groups contain
(\mathbb{Z}/2\mathbb{Z})^{3}
exists in characteristic
p
, in contrast to the case of hyperelliptic curves of genus
4
whose automorphism groups contain
\hskip-0.56905pt\mathbf{D}_{8}
.
Hence, in order to confirm the existence of superspecial hyperelliptic curves of genus
5
for every prime
p
, it may be necessary to investigate a broader class (for example, Type
2-1
in Table
1
).
However, this would require preparing several superspecial curves of genus
3
, which significantly increases the computational complexity (cf.
15
).
As future work, we plan to construct an efficient enumeration algorithm for the case of non-hyperelliptic curves of genus
5
, analogous to the one developed in this work.
