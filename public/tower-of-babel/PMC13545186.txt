Temporal trends, risk factors, and future projections of gastric cancer burden in China, India, and the United States: a GBD 2023 analysis
Authors: Fen Lin, Xueying Wei, Lihuan Song, Liyan Liu, Jing Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundThe prevalence of gastric cancer (GC) in China, India, and the USA has different epidemiology, but comparative analyses with regard to their time trends and causes are underdeveloped.MethodsUsing the 2023 Global Burden of Disease (GBD) database (1990–2023), we evaluated GC trends via Joinpoint regression. India demonstrated the slowest decline in age-standardized rates, while incident cases nearly doubled from 42,800 to 85,400 during the study period. Introduction

Gastric cancer (GC) remains a persistent threat to global public health [1]. According to the GLOBOCAN 2022 estimates, GC ranks fifth in global cancer incidence and accounts for approximately 660,000 fatalities, representing 6.8% of all cancer-related deaths [2]. Since early-stage GC may have non-characteristic symptoms, diagnoses are frequently at a later stage when the best chance of performing curative surgery or endoscopy has been missed [3, 4]. In spite of new therapies such as targeted immunotherapy, the prognosis of advanced GC is still poor, with a median overall survival of approximately 12.6 months and a 5-year survival rate typically remaining below 10–20% for metastatic cases [5]. Furthermore, the gastric cancer burden exhibits profound geographic and socioeconomic disparities. It is driven by a complex interaction among Helicobacter pylori (H. pylori) infection, genetic predisposition, and modifiable lifestyle factors such as tobacco smoking, alcohol consumption, and high-sodium diets [6, 7].

Despite declining age-standardized incidence and mortality rates globally, the absolute number of incident cases and fatalities continues to rise, primarily driven by population growth and aging [8, 9]. To implement effective global GC control strategies, comparative analyses across diverse populations are essential. Prior research has predominantly focused on overall global trends or specific high-incidence regions like Japan and South Korea, where trends primarily reflect proactive nationwide endoscopic screening policies rather than the natural evolution of the disease burden [10, 11]. In contrast, systematic comparisons among China, India, and the United States of America (USA) remain inadequate [12]. Together, these three nations encompass over 40% of the world’s population and represent distinct epidemiological archetypes: the USA exemplifies a well-resourced healthcare system with a consistently low burden [2]. China faces a massive historical burden compounded by rapid population aging and healthcare modernization [13], and India exhibits low standardized rates but a surging absolute number of cases driven by explosive population growth [14].

Furthermore, contemporary studies examining the spatiotemporal patterns across these three nations present specific limitations. First, earlier research often fails to capture the latest epidemiological shifts, particularly trend fluctuations after 2020 [15]. Second, previous studies rarely quantify the competing effects of demographic changes versus true epidemiological progress due to a lack of decomposition analyses. Third, existing research seldom provides joint projections for future incidence and disability-adjusted life years (DALYs) burden.

To bridge these gaps, this study utilizes the latest Global Burden of Disease (GBD) 2023 dataset [16] to provide a multifaceted comparative assessment of the GC burden in China, India, and the USA. We advance current understanding by employing Joinpoint regression to identify recent temporal shifts, Das Gupta decomposition to isolate demographic driving factors, and the Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model to project the disease burden through 2040. Elucidating the distinct trajectories of these demographic superpowers will provide a critical reference for optimizing the allocation of endoscopic screening and tailoring GC prevention strategies to diverse levels of socioeconomic development.

Methods

Data source

In this study, data were extracted from the GBD 2023 database, officially released on October 12, 2025. Data were retrieved via the GBD Results Tool (https://vizhub.healthdata.org/gbd-results/), a public platform for global health metrics. Data on GC burden contained incidence, mortality and DALYs. For analyses across all age groups, age-standardized rates (ASRs) per 100,000 individuals were used to control for the confounding effects of different population age structures. Specifically, these metrics reflect distinct aspects of the disease burden: the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) measures the rate of newly diagnosed cases; the age-standardized mortality rate (ASMR) indicates the rate of deaths; and the age-standardized DALYs rate (ASDR) comprehensively quantifies the overall health loss by combining both years of life lost due to premature death and years lived with disability. Global-level data were extracted and utilized as a baseline benchmark to contextualize and compare the epidemiological shifts occurring within China, India, and the USA. The latest epidemiological data and refined, standardized methodologies were adopted in the process of data collection [17]. In the GBD 2023, GC cases were identified using the codes of the International Classification of Diseases (ICD) (https://www.cdc.gov/nchs/icd/icd-10-cm/) Tenth Revision (C16-C16.9, Z12.0, Z85.02-Z85.028). All the data used in this study included no identifiable personal or medical information.

Statistical analyses

Study metrics

In this study, the estimated annual percentage change (EAPC) was used for evaluating trends in ASIR, ASMR and ASDR. EAPC estimates the average yearly percentage change in age-standardized rates. EAPCs were acquired from a regression model (Y = α + βX + e) that was applied to ASR over time [18]. The EAPC formula is expressed as 100×[exp(β)–1]. A linear regression model was leveraged to calculate the 95% confidence interval (CI) for EAPC [19]. If EAPC and its 95% CI lower limit are positive, ASR shows an upward trend. If EAPC and its 95% CI upper limit are negative, ASR demonstrates a downward trend. Otherwise, ASR remains stable.

Joinpoint regression model

To analyze temporal trends and identify significant inflection points in ASRs (including ASIR and ASDR), we utilized the Joinpoint regression model (Version 5.4.0.0; National Cancer Institute) [20]. The maximum number of joinpoints was set to 5, with final model selection determined by permutation tests. For each identified segment, log-linear regression models were fitted to compute the annual percent change (APC). Additionally, the average annual percent change (AAPC) was calculated to summarize the overall trend across the entire study period [20]. An upward trend was indicated by a positive APC/AAPC and corresponding 95%CI, while a downward trend was indicated by a negative APC/AAPC and corresponding 95%CI.

Decomposition analysis

To pinpoint factors driving temporal variations in the absolute burden, we employed the Das Gupta decomposition method [21]. This approach mathematically decomposes temporal fluctuations in incident cases and DALYs into three fundamental drivers [22]: (1) population expansion (changes in total population size), (2) population aging (increases in the proportion of elderly individuals), and (3) epidemiological shifts (true variations in age-specific disease incidence or death rates). Notably, decomposition analysis is a descriptive mathematical method; therefore, these shifts represent mathematical variations in risk rather than direct causal evidence of specific healthcare improvements.

Attributable risk factor analysis

The disease burden ascribed to specific risk factors was estimated using the comparative risk assessment framework from GBD 2023 [23, 24]. We assessed the contribution percentages of three primary quantifiable factors: high-sodium diets, smoking, and high alcohol use. Importantly, while H. pylori infection is unequivocally the dominant etiologic factor for gastric cancer, it was excluded from this quantitative analysis. This omission is dictated by the current GBD framework, which lacks complete and standardized population attributable fraction (PAF) data for H. pylori across all evaluated regions and years. Therefore, our risk factor analysis is inherently constrained to the available behavioral and dietary metrics. The implications of this limitation are further explored in the Discussion section.

ARIMA model analysis for prediction

The Autoregressive Integrated Moving Average (ARIMA) model, denoted as ARIMA (p, d, q), was applied to forecast future trends [25]. In this structure, ‘p’ represents the number of autoregressive terms, ‘d’ the order of differencing, and ‘q’ the number of moving average terms. After stabilizing the time series via differencing and confirming stationarity using the KPSS test, we employed the auto.arima() function to automatically select the optimal p, d, and q parameters based on the lowest Akaike and Bayesian Information Criterion (AIC/BIC) values [26]. Model accuracy was assessed using root mean square error (RMSE) and mean absolute error (MAE). Given the lack of true external validation datasets and cross-model comparisons, the projections for 2024–2040 (reported with 95% prediction intervals) should be interpreted strictly as exploratory extrapolations of historical GBD trends rather than definitive predictive outcomes.

Statistical analyses

All statistical analyses and data visualizations were performed using R (version 4.4.2) and JD_GBDR (V2.37, Jingding Medical Technology Co., Ltd.). The Free Statistics software (version 1.9.2; Beijing FreeClinical Medical Technology Co., Ltd., Beijing, China) was also used to perform analysis. A two-tailed p-value below 0.05 was considered to show statistical significance. Data were visualized using “ggplot2”.

Results

Overview of GC burden in China, India and the USA

Detailed trends in estimated annual percentage changes (EAPCs) and age-standardized rates (ASRs) are summarized in Table 1; Fig. 1. Globally, the age-standardized incidence rate (ASIR) declined from 26.31 to 14.00 per 100,000 between 1990 and 2023. However, despite these rate reductions, the absolute incident cases worldwide increased from 1.05 to 1.28 million. China maintained the highest disease burden in 2023 (ASIR: 25.04/100,000) despite achieving the steepest reductions in ASIR, ASMR, and ASDR. India demonstrated the slowest decline in age-standardized rates, while its absolute incident cases nearly doubled from 42,800 to 85,400 during the study period. Meanwhile, the USA maintained the lowest burden throughout the study period.

Gender and age disparities of GC burden

The gastric cancer burden was consistently higher in males than in females across all regions. A distinct divergence emerged: while incidence rates peaked in the oldest demographic cohorts, the absolute burden peaked much earlier. Globally, male absolute incident cases reached their maximum in the 70–74 age group. In China, although male incidence rates peaked at ages 85–89, the absolute incident cases and DALYs surged much earlier in the 65–69 age group. Similar demographic shifts occurred in India and the USA. Notably, absolute incident cases in India concentrated in the 65–69 age group, with DALYs peaking even earlier at ages 60–64. The USA exhibited a comparatively delayed pattern, where incidence peaked in the oldest cohort (ages 90–94), while absolute cases peaked at ages 70–74 (Fig. 2 and Supplementary Table S1).

Temporal trends in GC burden

Between 1990 and 2023, the ASIR of GC exhibited consistent long-term downward trends globally and across the three evaluated countries. Joinpoint analysis identified a significant trend reversal in 2020; both China and the global cohort experienced a rapid short-term upward trend in ASIR from 2020 to 2023 (APC = 3.62 and 1.61, respectively) (Fig. 3A). A similar trajectory was observed for the overall disease burden (ASDR), where post-2020 increases interrupted the historical pattern of stable improvement (Fig. 3B).

Decomposition analysis

The absolute increase in the global incidence of GC was primarily driven by population aging (263.75%) and population growth (236.86%), which were substantially offset by improvements in epidemiological factors (− 400.62%). Nationally, the increased incidence burden in China was largely driven by aging (333.02%), whereas in the USA, it was predominantly driven by growth (417.87%). In both countries, these demographic increases were massively offset by epidemiological improvements (− 451.18% and − 546.75%, respectively). In India, epidemiological changes (− 46.17%) mitigated the burden but failed to counteract the demographic factors of aging (87.83%) and growth (58.34%) (Fig. 4A and Supplementary Table 3).

Analysis of risk factors

By 2023, smoking remained the leading analyzed risk factor, globally accounting for 11.45% of the GC mortality burden, followed by high-sodium diets (7.27%) and high alcohol use (1.44%) (Fig. 5). Nationally, the proportional mortality burden was disproportionately concentrated in China. Smoking accounted for 16.34% of GC deaths in China, substantially exceeding the global average, the USA (11.91%), and India (5.27%). Similar prominent patterns in China were observed for high-sodium diets and high alcohol use. Conversely, India consistently reported the lowest attributable proportions across all three factors.

China is anticipated to maintain the highest ASIR, reaching 15.99 per 100,000 (95% PI: 11.27–22.72) by 2040, which remains substantially higher than the projected rates for India and the USA. Discussion

This study demonstrates a persistent worldwide disparity in the gastric cancer (GC) burden, characterized by increasing absolute incident cases despite declining age-standardized rates. Distinct epidemiological patterns emerged across China, India, and the USA. The most significant fall in standardized mortality occurred in China, likely driven by improved living conditions, H. pylori eradication, and mass screening [12, 27]. However, as our ARIMA projections highlight, the ASIR in China is projected to remain substantially higher than that of the USA by 2040, necessitating targeted interventions tailored to its aging population.

Conversely, India presents a divergent profile where the rising total burden is predominantly driven by population expansion rather than aging, with peak DALYs occurring in much younger cohorts (ages 60–64). While traditional diets might offer some protective effects, it is highly probable that incomplete rural cancer registry coverage and unequal healthcare allocation result in the under-estimation of actual standardized rates [28–30]. Given the limitations of the current dataset, these explanations remain speculative and warrant direct evaluation in future targeted epidemiological studies. Meanwhile, the USA maintains a consistently low overall burden and an older incidence peak. This favorable national average is supported by robust healthcare infrastructure and complete registries (e.g., Surveillance, Epidemiology, and End Results [SEER]), though disparities persist among immigrant populations [31]. When interpreting these cross-national contrasts, the profound disparities in data infrastructure and registry maturity must be critically acknowledged.

A notable anomaly in the temporal analysis is the joinpoint around 2020, which interrupted decades of stable declines. Between 2020 and 2023, both China and the global average exhibited upward trends in standardized incidence and DALY rates. Although this rebound temporally coincides with the COVID-19 pandemic and its documented impacts on screening curtailments [32], diagnostic surges [33], and clinical stage shifts [34], drawing a direct causal link remains highly speculative. Because the present analysis relies on ecological data lacking patient-level linkage, these short-term fluctuations cannot be definitively attributed to pandemic-related healthcare disruptions. It is equally plausible that the observed trends reflect methodological artifacts, including GBD model recalibrations, delayed regional reporting, changes in coding practices, or temporary statistical instability. Consequently, longer-term observational data are strictly required to confirm whether this represents a genuine epidemiological reversal.

The examination of risk factors shows that lifestyles have a diverse effect. The results about risk attribution, however, should be interpreted with precaution. As GBD comparative risk assessment structure in those particular years does not have detailed quantitative estimates of H. pylori which is the leading causative factor behind GC, our results represent an incomplete picture of modifiable behavior. Within this evaluated scope, China ranks first in mortality attribution for smoking, high-sodium diets, and high alcohol use. Mechanistically, these behaviors synergistically promote carcinogenesis by damaging the gastric mucosal barrier, facilitating H. pylori colonization, and inducing oxidative DNA damage [35–37]. Conversely, India displays remarkably low burdens attributable to smoking (5.27%) and alcohol (0.47%). These figures must be interpreted cautiously, as strong cultural traditions and religious beliefs in many Indian communities may contribute to considerable survey under-reporting. Broadly, these ecological trends underscore the continued benefit of adherence to established frameworks (e.g., the WHO Framework Convention on Tobacco Control) and proactive sodium-reduction campaigns, emphasizing culturally appropriate interventions to curb behavioral risks [38].

This study builds upon previous research by utilizing the latest GBD 2023 iterations to capture post-2020 epidemiological transitions. Furthermore, our decomposition analysis quantitatively highlights the interplay between demographic dynamics and epidemiological shifts. Specifically, incidence growth in China and the USA is predominantly fueled by population aging, whereas India’s burden is propelled by population expansion. Within this zero-sum mathematical framework, favorable epidemiological trends drastically mitigated the potential burden; therefore, demographic factors had to more than compensate to account for the overall net increase in absolute cases, explaining the seemingly extreme relative percentages (e.g., exceeding − 400% globally). Finally, ARIMA projections indicate that while India and the USA may see continued declines in standardized rates, the ASIR and ASDR in China are projected to stabilize at a comparatively high level, underscoring the necessity of continuously adapting long-term national cancer control strategies.

This study has several inherent limitations. First, the GBD dataset offers no quantified estimates for H. pylori infection and omits significant determinants like obesity and socioeconomic status, introducing uncertainty into risk attribution and projections. Second, the absence of anatomical subtyping (cardia vs. non-cardia GC) constrains the disentanglement of divergent etiological pathways. Third, GBD estimates are based on secondary predictive modeling, leading to inherent uncertainty, especially in rural India where rates may be underestimated compared to mature registries. Fourth, ARIMA-based projections assume the continuity of historical trends; unprecedented disruptions, such as the pandemic, make long-term forecasts intrinsically less reliable. Despite these limitations and the lack of temporal split validation, ARIMA was utilized over alternative models because it is optimally suited for annual epidemiological time series lacking intra-year seasonality, effectively preventing overfitting. Forecasts remain probabilistic and should not be interpreted as precise deterministic outcomes.

Conclusion

In summary, this population-based study evaluated the temporal trends, associated factors, and future projections of the GC burden in China, India, and the United States. Despite long-term declines in standardized rates, population aging and demographic growth consistently contribute to an upward trajectory in absolute case volumes. These distinct epidemiological patterns highlight the necessity of tailoring interventions to specific national contexts. To manage its rapidly aging-associated burden, China would benefit from optimizing early screening programs and sustaining the control of behavioral risks, notably smoking and high-sodium diets. For India, improving rural healthcare access and expanding cancer registries are critical to addressing epidemiological inequities and managing surging absolute cases. Meanwhile, maintaining the overall decline in the USA requires targeted strategies to mitigate internal disparities among subpopulations. Acknowledging the inherent uncertainties of probabilistic forecasting, these national archetypes provide data-driven references for adapting and optimizing future global GC prevention and control strategies.
