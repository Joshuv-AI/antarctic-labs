Metabolic plasticity and therapeutic opportunity in cancer
Authors: Shensi Shen
Published: 2026

Introduction

Cancer metabolism has re-emerged as a central hallmark of tumor biology, reflecting the dynamic interplay between oncogenic signaling, cellular plasticity, and the tumor microenvironment. Building on foundational discoveries of altered glucose utilization and bioenergetics, recent advances have expanded this framework to encompass a diverse network of metabolic pathways, including lipid remodeling, nucleotide synthesis, redox homeostasis, and mitochondrial dynamics. These processes not only sustain tumor growth but also actively shape cancer cell fate, therapeutic response, and interactions with immune and stromal compartments.

Despite substantial progress, major challenges persist. Metabolic dependencies are highly context-specific, varying across tumor subtypes, developmental states, and microenvironmental constraints. Emerging evidence highlights the importance of metabolic competition within the tumor niche, as well as the role of specialized cell populations, such as drug-tolerant persister cells and cancer stem-like cells, in driving resistance and disease relapse. Furthermore, key metabolic regulators, including pathways governing iron handling, antioxidant systems, and ferroptotic sensitivity, often exhibit dual and context-dependent functions, complicating therapeutic targeting strategies.

Meanwhile, the field is undergoing rapid conceptual and technological expansion. Novel insights into metabolic crosstalk with immune function, lineage plasticity, and stress adaptation are opening new avenues for intervention. Advances in multi-omics approaches and spatially resolved analyses are enabling a more precise dissection of metabolic heterogeneity in situ.

This collection brings together a series of reviews that collectively capture these emerging themes, spanning fundamental mechanisms to translational opportunities. Together, they provide a timely and integrative perspective on how metabolism shapes cancer biology and how these insights may be leveraged to develop more effective and durable therapeutic strategies.

The renewed focus on cancer metabolism reflects a broader shift in oncology [1], from viewing metabolic reprogramming as a downstream consequence of oncogenic transformation to recognizing it as a central determinant of tumor evolution, adaptability, and therapeutic response. While early paradigms emphasized aerobic glycolysis and biomass accumulation, it is now evident that cancer cells exploit a far more intricate and flexible metabolic network, dynamically rewired in response to genetic [2], environmental [3], and therapeutic pressures [4].

A unifying theme emerging from recent work is the concept of metabolic plasticity [5]. Rather than relying on fixed metabolic programs, tumor cells transition between nutrient utilization strategies, redox states, and bioenergetic modes to sustain proliferation and survival. This flexibility is particularly evident under conditions of stress, such as nutrient limitation, hypoxia, or therapeutic intervention, where metabolic rewiring supports the persistence of drug-tolerant cell populations. These “persister” states, often characterized by altered mitochondrial function, lipid metabolism rewiring [6], and antioxidant capacity, represent a critical barrier to durable therapeutic responses [7]. Another key insight is the growing recognition of metabolism as a mediator of cellular identity and fate decisions. Metabolic pathways intersect with epigenetic regulation, translational control, and signaling networks to influence lineage plasticity and cell state transitions. For example, the availability of metabolites involved in nucleotide synthesis or redox balance can directly impact chromatin organization and gene expression programs [8], thereby facilitating transitions between differentiated and stem-like states. This integration of metabolism with cell fate regulation may underlie the emergence of aggressive phenotypes and therapy resistance across diverse cancer types.

The tumor microenvironment further amplifies these dynamics. Cancer cells exist within a complex ecosystem where resource competition shapes both tumor progression and immune surveillance [9]. Nutrient depletion, metabolic by-products, and oxygen gradients create selective pressures that differentially affect tumor and immune cells. In this context, metabolic pathways become critical regulators of anti-tumor immunity, influencing the function of T cells, macrophages, and other immune populations. Therapeutic strategies that modulate metabolic competition, either by restricting tumor access to key nutrients or enhancing immune cell fitness, are beginning to show promise in preclinical and clinical settings. Importantly, metabolic pathways also exhibit context-dependent vulnerabilities that can be therapeutically exploited. Dependencies on specific nutrients, such as amino acids, lipids [6], or nucleotides [8], often arise from oncogenic signaling or tissue-specific constraints. However, targeting these pathways remains challenging due to redundancy and adaptability within the metabolic network. Moreover, certain metabolic regulators display dual roles, acting as either tumor-promoting or tumor-suppressive factors depending on cellular context and environmental conditions. This duality necessitates a more nuanced approach to therapeutic development, integrating metabolic profiling with patient stratification.

Looking forward, several priorities emerge for the field. For instance, there is a need to better define the spatiotemporal organization of tumor metabolism, leveraging advances in single-cell and spatial omics technologies [10]. These approaches will be essential to capture metabolic heterogeneity within tumors and to identify niche-specific vulnerabilities. Beyond this integrative system, we also need to understand the crosstalk of metabolism with other layers of regulation, such as translation, proteostasis, and signaling. These axes will provide a more comprehensive understanding of how cancer cells coordinate adaptive responses. Finally, translating metabolic insights into effective therapies will require rational combination strategies, particularly those that target both tumor-intrinsic pathways and the tumor microenvironment. In this evolving landscape, cancer metabolism stands at the intersection of fundamental biology and clinical innovation. By moving beyond static models and embracing the dynamic and context-dependent nature of metabolic regulation, the field is poised to uncover new principles of tumor biology and to develop more effective therapeutic strategies. The challenge ahead lies not only in identifying metabolic vulnerabilities, but in understanding when, where, and how to target them for maximal clinical benefit.
