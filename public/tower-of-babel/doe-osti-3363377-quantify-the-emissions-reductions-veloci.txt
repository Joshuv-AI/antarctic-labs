 Executive Summary of CRADA Work:

 Contractor will quantify the net life cycle emission benefits resulting from manufacturing
 fertilizers via the diversion of biosolids from their current disposal practice relative to synthetic
 fertilizers.

 1

 CRADA benefit to DOE, Participant, and US Taxpayer:

 • Assists laboratory in achieving programmatic scope
 • Uses the laboratory’s core competencies
 Summary of Research Results:

 The development of a novel and automated python code-based framework to estimate the net
 lifecycle supply chain impacts reductions from biosolids-based fertilizer production relative to
 conventional Haber-Bosch ammonia production in the US. Results highlight the cumulative 30-
 year impacts reductions across all metrics due to nitrogen fertilizer production from dry biosolids
 via direct concentrated solar thermal energy from 2020 to 2050 for a supply of 177,000
 kilograms of nitrogen per year.

 Task 1: Data Collection

 Comparative lifecycle assessment (LCA) was performed to quantify the net lifecycle supply
 chain impacts reductions from manufacturing biosolids-based nitrogen fertilizer relative to
 ammonia from the conventional Haber-Bosch process. Participant-specific data was collected for
 the following lifecycle stages of the biosolids pathway: 1) processing and refinement, 2)
 manufacturing and assembly, 3) operation, 4) maintenance, 5) end-of-life, and 6) transportation.
 Data from the Participant included measurements and/or estimates on biosolids quantity and
 properties (e.g., moisture content, nutrients), thermal energy requirements for drying biosolids,
 materials used for constructing the solar drying technology, loading technology type, and
 transportation modes and distances. Data from literature and publicly available databases was
 used to represent the raw material extraction lifecycle stage, and upstream processes demanded
 by the biosolids-based fertilizer pathway.

 Available literature and data formed the backbone of all relevant unit processes that would occur
 during the lifetime (i.e., cradle-to-grave) of the conventional (Haber-Bosch process) and
 proposed (Biosolids-based) systems of nitrogen fertilizer production. Figure 1 illustrates the
 background and foreground lifecycle system boundaries of the LCA, and it visualizes the generic
 structure of the lifecycle stages by unit processes and associated inputs and outputs, which form
 the lifecycle inventory (LCI) databases of the conventional and proposed product systems. The
 LCIs define the lifecycle supply chain of each product system that were scoped by both the
 Participant and Contractor. The unit processes contained in the LCIs allow for the quantification
 of comparative impacts across the lifecycle supply chains of each product system. Unit process
 information and associated flow quantities for the lifecycle supply chain of each product system
 was collected from the Ecoinvent v3.9 database (Wernet et al. 2016). The data from the
 Participant formed the foreground of the proposed system, and the Ecoinvent data formed the
 background. In the case of the conventional system, Ecoinvent data was used to represent both
 the foreground and background of the conventional system. After collecting all the relevant data,
 the LCI analysis was reviewed between the Participant and Contractor to ensure all necessary
 data were to be included in the LCA.

 Lifecycle supply chain impacts are quantified by summing the gaseous, effluent, and solid
 wasteful outputs of all unit processes contained in the LCIs of each product systems’ lifecycle
 supply chain that occur over the 30-year system lifetime The calculations were executed using
 the Lifecycle Assessment Integrating Science and Operations (LiAISON) tool (Ghosh et al.
 2024). The LiAISON tool outputted the lifecycle supply chain impact factors from 2020 to 2050
 according to nine metrics of the Recipe for Life Cycle Impact Assessment (ReCiPe) method
 (Huijbregts et al. 2017) for the Ecoinvent activities (LCIs) chosen to represent the lifecycle
 supply chain of the proposed and conventional product systems. The activities are listed in Table
 1, and the application of impact factors described in Task 2. The Contractor met with the
 Participant to review the lifecycle inventory calculations and resulting lifecycle impact factors.

 Task 2: Perform Comparative Life Cycle Assessment

 A comparative lifecycle assessment was performed by utilizing the lifecycle impact factors to
 quantify the annual and cumulative (2020-2025) net lifecycle supply chain impacts reductions
 across nine metrics based on the ReCiPe method due to the displacement of synthetic fertilizer
 production with biosolids based fertilizer production. As supported by Figure 1, the lifecycle
 system boundary is cradle-to-grave; encompassing the production and acquisitions of feedstock
 sources for fertilizer production, the manufacturing of fertilizer, and the transport of the fertilizer
 product. While the use and disposal of fertilizer is excluded from the system boundary; the
 manufacturing, use, maintenance, and end-of-life stages of fertilizer production infrastructure is
 included in the system boundary. The functional unit is defined as 177,000 kilograms of plant-
 available nitrogen delivered to the agricultural field of application per year and it is considered
 for a 30-year LCA system lifetime.

 4

 The lifecycle supply chain impact factors of the conventional product system were scaled
 directly by the functional unit while those of the proposed product system were scaled according
 to the energy, material, and transport quantities demanded by the proposed system with respect
 to the functional unit. The quantities were estimated by parametrizing the foreground system of
 the biosolids based fertilizer lifecycle supply chain into a system of variables and equations. The
 parametrization enables the LCA framework to be easily modified by the Participant if input
 quantities of independent variables associated with the project change in the future. Although
 preliminary LCA results were reviewed with the Participant through a PowerPoint presentation
 to ensure the LCA inputs and outputs aligned with the processes and could be easily modified for
 future use if processes changed, the finalized results are presented in Figure 2. Figure 2
 illustrates the cumulative lifecycle supply chain impacts for the proposed and conventional
 product systems across nine metrics from 2020 to 2050, where the cumulative 30-year net
 lifecycle supply chain impacts reductions by the proposed system relative to the conventional
 system are listed in Table 2.

 The reviewing of preliminary results with the Participant and feedback received by the
 Contractor served as the basis of information to refine the LCA framework and finalize
 associated results. Discussions regarding the calculation methods of input quantities for
 independent variables, the implementation of independent variables in the parametrization of the
 proposed product system, and the modeled processes in the lifecycle supply chain of each
 product system enabled the LCA framework to be consistent with ISO 14044 Standards. The
 interpretation of quantitative outputs from the Framework by lifecycle stages and lifecycle
 processes provided critical insight to establish the final net lifecycle supply chain impacts
 reductions induced by the biosolids based fertilizer production pathway relative to the
 conventional Haber-Bosch process. As shown in Figure 2 and Table 2, the finalized lifecycle
 supply chain impacts are compared to the conventional Haber-Bosch synthetic fertilizer
 production pathway for an annual supply of 177,000 kilograms of nitrogen from 2020 to 2050.
 Results indicate lifecycle supply chain impacts reductions across all metrics from 25% (human
 toxicity potential) to 98% (water consumption potential) and average 69% (all metrics) across
 the 30-year system lifetime.

 Task 3: Additional Tasks

 As part of the works executed under Task 2, and at the direction of the Participant consistent
 with the scope and financials of the project, a publicly available open-source Python codebase
 (included as part of the associated NLR technical report) was developed to allow for use and
 reproducibility of the dynamic lifecycle supply chain impact assessment framework executed as
 part of this research agreement. The novel codebase parametrizes the lifecycle foreground
 quantities of material and energy flows required by the supply chain of the biosolids based
 fertilizer production pathway. It then takes as input the dynamic lifecycle impact factors of
 LiAISON for relevant Ecoinvent activities of the proposed and conventional product systems and
 quantifies prospective lifecycle supply chain impacts from 2020 to 2050. While the main
 analysis of LCA results fell under Task 2, the codebase is taken further under Task 3 to quantify
 the relative influence of modeled independent input parameters to LCA results for the lifecycle
 supply chain of the proposed product system.

 6

 Across all nine metrics of the ReCiPe method, the lifecycle supply chain impacts per kilogram of
 nitrogen supplied by the biosolids based fertilizer production pathway are most sensitive, and
 equally sensitive in magnitude and direction, to the input quantity of initial nitrogen content,
 nitrogen retention fraction, and plant available nitrogen fraction. Figure 3 visualizes the change
 in total 30-year lifecycle supply chain impacts when the baseline input quantity of each
 independent variable captured in the parametrized supply chain of the proposed product system
 is individually increased and decreased by 50%, respectively. The baseline input quantities for
 independent variables of the biosolids-based fertilizer production pathway are derived from the
 Participant-provided data acquired under Task 1, and the names of variables are shown on the y-
 axis of subplots in Figure 3. Across all nine metrics, lifecycle supply chain impacts per kilogram
 of nitrogen supplied are least sensitive, from greatest to least, to the input quantity of glass
 recycling ratio, transport nitrogen loss fraction, and glass transport distance.
