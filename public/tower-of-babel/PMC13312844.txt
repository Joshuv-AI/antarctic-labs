Assembly and sequence analysis of the chloroplast genome of Amorphophallus atroviridis Hett 1994
Authors: Haoliang Shi, Ying Qi, Penghua Gao, Min Yang, Yongteng Zhao, Jianwei Guo, Feiyan Huang, Jiani Liu, Jianrong Zhao, Lifang Li, Lei Yu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAmorphophallus is valued for its glucomannan-rich tubers and ornamental inflorescences. We sequenced the first complete chloroplast genome of Amorphophallus atroviridis Hett, 1994 using the PacBio Revio platform. The assembled chloroplast genome is 175,133 bp in length and contains a large single-copy region (94,561 bp), a small single-copy region (14,862 bp), and two inverted repeat regions (32,855 bp each). A total of 128 genes were annotated, including 84 protein-coding genes, 36 transfer RNA genes, and 8 ribosomal RNA genes. Phylogenetic analysis of 14 Araceae species confirmed a monophyletic Amorphophallus clade, with A. atroviridias closely related to five species. This study provides a fundamental resource for phylogenetic studies and further genetic research in Araceae.Keywords: Amorphophallus atroviridis, Araceae, Chloroplast genome, Phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

The genus Amorphophallus (family Araceae) comprises over 200 species of perennial herbaceous plants (Yin and Gao 2023a) and is characterized by underground tubers that allow plants to survive for multiple years, with new leaves sprouting annually. Primarily distributed across Asia and Africa, Amorphophallus species thrive in warm, humid, semi-shaded conditions and require well-drained, fertile soil. They are characterized by their striking inflorescences and massive underground tubers, which support diverse applications (Harmayani et al. 2014; Jain et al. 2025).

Several Amorphophallus species hold significant economic value due to their rhizomes, which are rich in glucomannan, a polysaccharide that serves as the primary raw material for producing konjac flour and konjac tofu. Notable examples include A. konjac, A. albus, A. bulbifer, and A. muelleri. This compound also underpins the medicinal value of these species in traditional and functional health products that promote digestive and metabolic health. Besides, certain varieties are prized for their ornamental value, featuring unique leaf shapes and distinctive floral structures, a spathe enveloping the spadix, that exhibit a striking color contrast (Tester and Al-Ghazzewi 2016).

Amorphophallus atroviridis Hett 1994 is native to Thailand and is an herbaceous plant with an underground tuber. Its leaves are highly ornamental, emerging deep purplish-red and maturing to a dark green with pink margins, while the undersides are purplish red. The inflorescence is characterized by a creamy-white spathe that envelops a long, yellowish spadix borne on a short stalk. Cultivation requirements include semi-shaded conditions and moderate watering; the plant thrives in moist, well-drained soils and can be propagated via its tubers (POWO 2023).

Current research on A. atroviridis has been largely limited to morphological, taxonomic, and distributional studies; however, genomic-level investigations, particularly regarding its phylogenetic placement, population genetic structure, and evolutionary relationships with related species, remain scarce. Studying its chloroplast genome is therefore important for clarifying its genetic composition and evolutionary history, thereby providing essential molecular insights to support the conservation, breeding, and systematics of Amorphophallus species.

Materials and methods

Plant material, DNA extraction, and sequencing

Young, healthy leaves of A. atroviridis were collected from the Germplasm Resource Nursery of Kunming University in Yunnan, China (Figure 1, Longitude 102°79′39″, Latitude 24°97′71″). A voucher specimen was deposited in the Herbarium of the Yunnan Urban Agricultural Engineering and Technological Research Center, Kunming University (voucher number: YBMY20250901, Hao-Liang Shi, 1816148023@qq.com). The collected leaves were immediately flash-frozen in liquid nitrogen and stored at −80 °C until further use. Genomic DNA was extracted using a commercial DNA extraction kit; kit purity and concentration were subsequently measured using both a NanoDrop 1000 spectrophotometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific) and a Qubit fluorometer (Thermo Fisher Scientific). A 15-kb library was constructed using the SMRTbell Express Template Prep Kit 2.0 (Pacific Biosciences, CA, USA) according to the manufacturer’s protocol and sequenced on the PacBio Revio platform (Pacific Biosciences, CA, USA).

Sequence assembly and annotation

The raw data were processed using the CCS algorithm (version 6.0.0, parameters: –minPasses 3, –minPredictedAccuracy 0.99, –maxLength 21,000) to generate highly accurate HiFi reads. The HiFi sequencing reads were then aligned to the reference chloroplast genome (NC_056329.1 A. titanum) using minimap2 (v2.15-r905) (Li 2018) to generate PAF alignment files. Reads with successful alignments were filtered based on the PAF files, followed by removal of low coverage reads (<50×). The chloroplast genome was then assembled using Flye v2.6 (Landman et al. 2024) with A. titanium (NC_056329.1) as a reference. To ensure structural accuracy and completeness, Bandage v.0.8.1 (Wick et al. 2015) was employed for structural visualization, ultimately obtaining the complete chloroplast genome of A. atroviridis. The complete chloroplast genome sequence was subsequently annotated using Plastid Genome Annotator (PGA) software, followed by manual curation. Finally, a circular gene map of the chloroplast genome was generated using the OGDRAW program (Lohse et al. 2007).

Phylogenetic analysis

To investigate the evolutionary relationships between A. atroviridis and 13 other Araceae species, chloroplast genomes were retrieved from the GenBank database. 59 single-copy gene coding sequences (CDS) were extracted from the chloroplast genomes of 14 species using the HiMT toolkit in TBtools-II (Chen et al. 2023; Tang et al. 2025). Each CDS underwent individual multiple sequence alignment (MSA) and trimming before concatenation. A maximum likelihood (ML) phylogenetic tree was subsequently constructed using IQ-TREE (Nguyen et al. 2015). Finally, the phylogenetic tree was visualized using the Simple Tree View toolkit in TBtools-II (Chen et al. 2023).

Results

Basic characteristics and phylogenetic analysis of the chloroplast genome of A. atroviridis

The HiFi reads exhibited an average length of ∼17.2 kb and a median quality score of Q34. The chloroplast genome of A. atroviridis spanned 175,133 bp with an average GC content of 34.81% and an average sequencing depth of 1732.69× (Figure S1). It comprised four distinct regions: the large single-copy region (LSC, 94,561 bp), the small single-copy region (SSC, 14,862 bp), and two inverted repeat regions (IRA and IRB, 32,855 bp) (Figure 2). The assembled and annotated chloroplast gene sequences were submitted to GenBank, obtaining the accession number PX508299. The A. atroviridis chloroplast genome was annotated with 128 genes, including 84 protein-coding genes, 8 rRNA genes, and 36 tRNA genes. Based on predicted functions, all genes can be categorized into three major groups: photosynthesis-related genes, self-replication-related genes, and other genes.

Among these genes, 17 genes were found to contain introns, with two genes (ycf3 and clpP) containing two introns and 15 genes (trnK-UUU, rps16, trnG-UCC, atpF, rpoC1, trnL-UAA, trnV-UAC, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhB, trnI-GAU, trnA-UGC, and ndhA) having a single intron. Besides, the rps12 gene exhibited trans-splicing (Table S1, Figure S2, and Figure S3). Furthermore, one trans-splicing gene (rps12) and 11 cis-splicing genes (rps16, atpF, rpoC1, ycf3, clpP, petB, petD, rpl16, rpl2, ndhA, ndhB) were identified.

The ML phylogenetic tree resolved all 12 Amorphophallus species as a monophyletic clade, which further split into two subclades, with Colocasia esculenta and Spirodela polyrhiza (outgroup) as sister taxa. This phylogeny also supported a close relationship among A. atroviridis, A. muelleri, A. kiusianus, A. coaetaneus, A. tonkinensis, and A. yunnanensis (bootstrap >90) (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

Amorphophallus represents an important economic crop in Asia, possessing not only edible and medicinal value but also demonstrating strong competitiveness in the ornamental plant market (Abdullah et al. 2021; Li et al. 2024a, 2024b). This study presents the first sequencing and annotation of the A. atroviridis chloroplast genome. Our results indicated a genome size of 175,133 bp with a GC content of 34.81%, closely matching the 175,465 bp and 34.90% GC content of A. coaetaneus (Gao et al. 2023) and A. paeoniifolius (176,258 bp and 34.80%) (Li et al. 2024c), reflecting the conservation of chloroplast genomes within the genus A. atroviridis. The chloroplast genome of A. atroviridis encoded 128 genes, a count comparable to congeneric species: A. krausei (130 genes) (Li et al. 2024b), A. coaetaneus (133 genes) (Gao et al. 2023), A. konjac (131 genes) (Li et al. 2024a), and A. paeoniifolius (130 genes) (Li et al. 2024c). In contrast, A. albus (113 genes) (Liu et al. 2019), A. bulbifer (111 genes) (Liu et al. 2019), and A. muelleri (112 genes) (Liu et al. 2019) exhibited significantly fewer genes.

This variation may be attributed to the contraction and expansion of inverted repeat (IR) regions within the chloroplast genomes of Amorphophallus species, a process that can precipitate partial gene loss (Liu et al. 2019). Alternatively, differences in environmental conditions may have driven the selective loss or retention of certain genes (To et al. 2021; Zhang et al. 2023). Our phylogenetic analysis revealed that 11 Amorphophallus species formed a monophyletic clade, suggesting high genetic homogeneity within the genus over evolutionary time. The chloroplast genome of A. atroviridis sequenced in this study serves as a valuable genetic resource for investigating the taxonomy, phylogenetic architecture, and evolutionary history of the genus Amorphophallus.
