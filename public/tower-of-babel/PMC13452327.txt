Prognostic value of serial NT-proBNP assay in adults with congenital heart disease and biventricular physiology
Authors: Mohamed Ellabbad, Marwan Ahmed, Ahmed E. Ali, Maan Jokhadar, David S. Majdalany, Alexander C. Egbe
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundThe available data about the prognostic role of N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) in adults with congenital heart disease (CHD) are derived from small sample size with limited follow-up. The purpose of this study was to assess the role of NT-proBNP assay for prognostication in adults with CHD based on a large cohort.MethodRetrospective cohort study of adults with CHD, biventricular physiology, and systemic morphologic left ventricle who had ≥2 NT-proBNP measurements (2003-2023). Temporal change in NT-proBNP was calculated as relative change from baseline levels (relative Δ_NT-proBNP).ResultsOf 3674 patients (age 42 ± 16 years; 47% males), median baseline and follow-up NT-proBNP levels were 178 (72; 539) pg/ml and 236 (103; 854) pg/ml, respectively. The relative Δ_NT-proBNP was 22% (−9, 51), and 2496 (68%) had temporal increase in NT-proBNP levels (i.e., relative Δ_NT-proBNP >0). The correlates of higher baseline NT-proBNP measurement were older age, atrial fibrillation, hepatorenal dysfunction, biventricular systolic dysfunction, and elevated biventricular filling pressures. Overall, 431 (12%) patients died during median follow-up. Both baseline NT-proBNP level and temporal increase in NT-proBNP were associated with all-cause mortality in all patient subgroups. Serial NT-proBNP measurement had superior prognostic value compared to single (baseline) NT-proBNP measurements and had superior prognostic performance compared to conventional clinical indices (echocardiographic indices, invasive hemodynamic indices, exercise test indices).ConclusionsNT-proBNP assay is inexpensive, readily available, and noninvasive, making it ideal for longitudinal monitoring. Further studies are required to determine whether NT-proBNP data can be used to guide therapeutic interventions in this population.

Introduction

Heart failure (HF) is common in adults with congenital heart disease (CHD), and it is the leading cause of mortality in this population [[1], [2], [3], [4]]. The causes of HF in CHD include myocardial disease resulting from ischemic injury during cardiac surgery or from pressure/volume overload related to valve disease, and rhythm abnormalities such as atrial arrhythmias and conduction system disease [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5]]. The management of HF in CHD is challenging because of the lack of empirically validated clinical indices for detection and monitoring of HF in this population [[1], [2], [3], [4], [5], [6]].

N-terminal pro-B-type natriuretic peptide (NT-proBNP) is a cardiovascular biomarker used for diagnosis and monitoring of HF in patients with acquired heart disease [7,8]. However, the role of NT-proBNP for HF risk stratification in adults with CHD is limited because of paucity of data, but the available data suggest that NT-proBNP may have prognostic value in this population [9,10]. Some of the limitations of prior studies include small sample studies with limited follow-up, and also because these studies combined CHD lesions with significantly different pathophysiologic mechanisms for HF in the same cohort. The current study aims to address these knowledge gaps and study limitations. The purpose of this study was to assess the role of serial NT-proBNP assay for prognostication in adults with CHD and biventricular physiology based on a large cohort with longitudinal follow-up.

Methods

Study population

We reviewed the Mayo Adult Congenital Heart Disease (MACHD) Registry and identified adults (age ≥18 years) with CHD, biventricular physiology, and the morphologic left ventricle (LV) as the systemic ventricle who received care at Mayo Clinic between January 1, 2003 and December 31, 2023. From this cohort, we identified consecutive patients who had ≥2 NT-proBNP measurements in the outpatient clinic with ≥1 year between measurements. We excluded patients that underwent cardiac surgical or transcatheter interventions between NT-proBNP measurement.

Study objectives

(1) Determine the correlates of baseline NT-proBNP measurement. (2) Evaluate the association between NT-proBNP measurements and all-cause mortality. (3) Compare the prognostic performance of NT-proBNP levels to conventional clinical indices. For the purpose of this study, we defined conventional clinical indices as echocardiographic indices (LV global longitudinal strain and right ventricular [RV] free wall strain), cardiac catheterization indices (right atrial pressure, pulmonary artery mean pressure, pulmonary artery wedge pressure), and exercise tests indices (%-predicted peak oxygen consumption).

Data collection

The first and second NT-proBNP measurements performed in the outpatient clinic within the study period were designated as NT-proBNP #1 and #2, respectively. The temporal change in NT-proBNP levels was calculated as absolute Δ_NT-proBNP (NT-proBNP #2 - NT-proBNP #1) and relative Δ_NT-proBNP ([NT-proBNP #2 – NT-proBNP #1] ÷ NT-proBNP #1 x100). For the purpose of this study, we defined temporal increase in NT-proBNP levels as relative Δ_NT-proBNP >0.

Echocardiographic data were retrieved from the MACHD Registry, and the procedural details for offline image analysis in the MACHD Registry Imaging Core Laboratory have been described [11]. Chamber (atrial and ventricular) size and function were assessed using 2-dimensional and speckle tracking echocardiography, respectively [[12], [13], [14]]. The clinical data (laboratory data, cardiac catheterization data, and exercise test data) obtained within 12 months from NT-proBNP #1 assay were used to define the baseline clinical characteristics of the cohort. Hepatorenal function was assessed using the model for end-stage liver disease excluding international normalized ratio (MELD-XI) score.

Statistical analysis

Data were presented as mean ± standard deviation, median (Q1, Q3), and count (%). The NT-proBNP data were log transformed for all analyses because of skewed distribution. The correlates of baseline NT-proBNP were assessed using linear regression analysis. The variables included in the linear regression models were chosen based on clinical relevance, and included demographic/anatomic indices, comorbidities, biomarkers of hepatorenal function, and echocardiographic indices. The covariates with p < 0.1 on univariable analysis were used to create multivariable models using stepwise backwards selection with p < 0.1 as the criterion for a variable to remain in the model. A subgroup analysis incorporating invasive hemodynamic indices was performed in the subset of patients who had cardiac catheterization data. In the absence of clinically validated NT-proBNP cutoff points for CHD patients, we dichotomized the cohort using the median baseline NT-proBNP as the threshold to define ‘elevated baseline NT-proBNP.’

All-cause mortality was assessed as time-to-event outcome and ascertained by review of medical records and Accurint mortality database using the date of NT-proBNP #2 assay as ‘time 0’. The cumulative incidence of mortality was estimated using Kaplan-Meier analysis and compared using log-rank test. The relationship between NT-proBNP levels (both baseline NT-proBNP and relative Δ_NT-proBNP) and all-cause mortality was assessed using Cox regression analysis, using similar criteria for covariate selection as described above. The incremental prognostic value of temporal change in NT-proBNP (i.e., relative Δ_NT-proBNP >0) relative to baseline NT-proBNP was assessed using c-statistic comparison. Similarly, we assessed the prognostic performance of NT-proBNP relative to conventional clinical indices using c-statistic comparison. Subgroup analyses were performed to assess prognostic performance of NT proBNP based on age, sex, atrial fibrillation, chronic kidney disease, obesity, and LV systolic function. All statistical analyses were performed with BlueSky Statistics software (version. 7.10; BlueSky Statistics LLC, Chicago, IL, USA), and JMP statistical software (version 18.1.0, JMP Statistical Discovery LLC, NC). P value < 0.05 was considered to be statistically significant for all analyses.

In the subset of patients with cardiac catheterization data (N = 943), the correlates of higher baseline NT-proBNP were older age, hepatorenal dysfunction (higher MELD-XI scores), LV systolic dysfunction, higher cath-derived right atrial pressure, and pulmonary artery wedge pressure (Table 2).

Temporal change in NT-proBNP measurement

The median NT-proBNP levels for NT-proBNP #1 and #2 were 178 (72; 539) pg/ml and 236 (103; 854) pg/ml, and the mean interval between measurements was 11 ± 3 months. The absolute Δ_NT-proBNP was 57 (−22, 118) pg/ml, while the relative Δ_NT-proBNP was 22% (−9, 51). Of 3674 patients, 2496 (68%) had temporal increase in NT proBNP levels (i.e., relative Δ_NT-proBNP >0).

All-cause mortality

The median follow-up from NT-proBNP #2 was 6.7 (2.1; 11.9) years, and during this period, 431 (12%) patients died. The 10-year and 20-year cumulative incidence of mortality was 14% and 31%, respectively. The patients with elevated baseline NT-proBNP (baseline NT-proBNP >178 pg/ml) had a higher incidence of mortality compared to those without elevated baseline NT-proBNP (10-year incidence of mortality 22% versus 6%, p < 0.001, and 20-year incidence of mortality 42% versus 18%, p < 0.001, respectively) (Fig. 1).Fig. 1Kaplan-Meier curves comparing cumulative incidence of all-cause mortality between groups dichotomized using the median baseline NT-proBNP measurement Abbreviations: NT-proBNP: N-terminal pro b-type natriuretic peptide.

Discussion

In this study, we assessed the correlates and prognostic value of serial NT-proBNP measurements in adults with CHD and systemic morphologic LV. The correlates of higher baseline NT-proBNP levels were older age, atrial fibrillation, hepatorenal dysfunction, biventricular systolic dysfunction, and elevated biventricular filling pressures. Higher baseline NT-proBNP levels was associated with all-cause mortality after adjustment for demographic indices, comorbidities, hepatorenal dysfunction, echocardiographic indices, and invasive hemodynamic indices. The prognostic performance of NT-proBNP was consistent across different patient subgroups. Serial NT-proBNP levels had superior prognostic value compared to single (baseline) NT-proBNP measurements and had superior prognostic performance compared to conventional clinical indices.

Early detection and monitoring of HF is a critical first step towards reducing the burden of HF-related mortality in adults with CHD [1,2,15]. However, HF risk stratification in CHD is challenging of the lack of validated prognostic metrics for risk stratification in this population [1]. NT-proBNP is routinely used for detection and monitoring of HF, but most of the available data were derived from patients with acquired heart disease [7,8,16,17]. However, a few studies have assessed the role of NT-proBNP measurement for risk stratification in adults with CHD [18,19]. In a prospective study of 589 adults with CHD who underwent NT-proBNP measurements in the ambulatory clinic, Baggen et al. observed that NT-proBNP levels correlated with CHD severity (higher in patients with complex CHD versus simple/moderate CHD), New York Heart Association functional class, atrial arrhythmias, and all-cause mortality [18]. Similar findings were also reported by Eindhoven et al. in a cross-sectional study of 475 adults with CHD [19]. In that study, Eindhoven et al. observed higher NT-proBNP levels in patients with Fontan physiology and patient with systemic RV [19]. While these prior studies provide empirically data supporting the role of NT-proBNP measurement for risk stratification in adults with CHD, there were limited by small sample size and limited follow-up, which did not allow for subgroup analyses to account for differences in anatomy and physiology for different lesions. The current study addressed these limitations by limiting the study inclusion criteria to patients with biventricular physiology and systemic LV, using a large cohort with long duration of follow-up, and performing subgroup analysis multivariable analysis, thereby improving robustness of the results. A novel finding from the current study was the observed incremental prognostic benefit of serial NT-proBNP measurement compared to single baseline measurement, and the superior prognostic performance of serial NT-proBNP measurements relative to conventional clinical indices. This has important clinical implications considering the advantages of NT-proBNP assay (inexpensive, readily available, and noninvasive) relative to conventional clinical indices (echocardiography, cardiac catheterization, and cardiopulmonary exercise test)

Clinical applications

One of the potential clinical applications of NT-proBNP assay is for the screening and early detection hemodynamic deterioration. NT-proBNP is a cardiovascular biomarker secreted by cardiomyocytes in response to hemodynamic stress, and this is consistent with our result showing correlation between higher NT-proBNP levels and biventricular systolic and diastolic function [20]. Serial monitoring of NT-proBNP levels would allow for early detection of cardiac dysfunction prior to the onset of overt chamber remodeling [16,17]. This in turn would provide opportunity for early treatment of reversible causes of cardiac dysfunction such as valvular heart disease and atrial arrhythmias, as well as initiation of guideline directed medical therapy to promote cardiac reverse remodeling (recovery of cardiac structure and function) [9]. Another potential clinical application of NT-proBNP assay is to monitor response to therapy. While not demonstrated in the current study, previous studies have shown temporal reduction in NT-proBNP following initiation of HF therapy in adults with CHD [21,22].

Limitations

This is a retrospective single-center study conducted in a tertiary CHD center, and it is therefore prone to selection and ascertainment bias. NT-proBNP was not measured in all patients, but in selected patients at the discretion of the primary cardiologist. This further compounds the selection bias inherent in the study design, and in turn limits the generalizability of the results since the clinical characteristics of study cohort may differ from the general adult CHD population. Although we restricted study inclusion to patients who did not undergo surgical or transcatheter cardiac intervention between NT proBNP measurements, we did not account for changes in medical therapy including diuretics which could have influenced loading conditions and the severity congestion. The strengths of the study include large cohort size with long duration of follow-up, subgroup and multivariable analyses controlling for confounders and population heterogeneity, and head-to-head comparisons of the prognostic performance of serial NT-proBNP measurements to conventional clinical indices. Finally, by restricting the inclusion criteria to patients with biventricular physiology and systemic LV, we controlled for some of the anatomic-physiologic heterogeneity inherent in the CHD population.

Conclusions

Higher NT-proBNP level was associated with all-cause mortality in the overall cohort, and across all patient subgroups. Serial NT-proBNP levels had superior prognostic value compared to single (baseline) NT-proBNP measurement, and serial NT-proBNP levels had superior prognostic performance compared to conventional clinical indices. NT-proBNP assay is inexpensive, readily available, and noninvasive, making it ideal for longitudinal monitoring. Further studies are required to validate our results, and to determine whether interventions to modify the correlates of elevated NT-proBNP levels would result in reduction in NT-proBNP level and improvement in clinical outcomes.
