Gravitational helicity in connection variables
Xiao-Kan Guo, Shupeng Song

Abstract
We study the gravitational helicity using the covariant phase space method. Starting from the covariant phase space of general relativity expressed in terms of connection variables, we construct the symplectic form and identify a duality transformation based on the internal Hodge dual. To make the symmetry manifest, we employ the complex self-dual Ashtekar variables, where the duality becomes a simple U ⁡ ( 1 ) U(1) phase rotation. The Noether charge associated with this duality yields a conserved quantity which is interpreted as the gravitational helicity. Remarkably, this helicity can be related to the Nieh-Yan topological term, when expressed in terms of the real variables, establishing a direct link between the gravitational helicity and a topological invariant.



I
Introduction

The optical helicity of electromagnetic fields is known to be the conserved quantity associated
with the duality symmetry of the vacuum Maxwell equations
Cal65
;
DT76
. The conservation
of helicity under duality transformation can also be understood by embedding Maxwell’s
theory into a duality-symmetric theory
Ran92
;
CB12
;
BBN13
.
It is found that the optical helicity can be expressed as the difference of two Chern-Simons
terms; the appearance of topological numbers agrees with the physical interpretation of the
optical helicity as the difference of left and right handed photons
AS96
.

Recently in
EDHZ16
, a symplectic approach to derive the electromagnetic helicity is
presented, which is based on the covariant phase space method
CW87
. In this approach,
the optical helicity in Chern-Simons form is recovered by the moment map of the duality
transformation. The use of the covariant phase space method greatly simplifies the derivation.

Based on the formal similarity between Maxwell’s electromagnetism and linearized general
relativity, the similar story of helicity has been told in linearized gravity

Bar14
;
AAB21
;
Tot22
. However, this kind of duality (and hence
helicity) for linearized gravity cannot be generalized to full nonlinear general
relativity if one directly uses the spacetime Hodge duality to define the electromagnetic
duality
DS05
. The point is that the linearized gravitational fields are actually
in the tangent space of the covariant phase space of the full nonlinear theory, so directly
working on the “base space” fails to retain the duality. This point of view is corroborated
by the recent work
Kol22
where the tangent space Hodge dual, i.e. the internal Hodge dual,
is exploited to define the duality for general relativity written in terms of tetrad variables.

We therefore expect a similar study of the duality and the helicity of
general relativity using the tetrads living in the tangent spaces of the spacetime manifold.
An interesting thing is that, in analogy to the electromagnetic helicity appearing as the
Chern-Simons term, the corresponding topological term resulting from the duality in general
relativity is expected to be the Nieh-Yan term
NY82
. This possibility has been discussed in

Kol22
, and more recently in
LZ26
it has been explicitly realized that the gravitational hecility flux is related to the Nieh-Yan term reduced on the null hypersurfaces.

The Nieh-Yan term, when included in the gravity action, is important for
introducing the Barbero-Immirzi parameter
\gamma
of loop gravity
Bar95
;
Imm97
;
Holst
;
Mercuri09
;
DKS09
. Therefore, we study in this paper the
gravitational helicity in the connection formulation of general relativity (or loop gravity) by using the covariant phase space method.
The relation between the Barbero-Immirzi parameter and parity violation in primordial
gravitational waves has been explored in the context of spinfoams and
\gamma
-duality

RW12
;
Bianchi24
. There it was shown that a
\gamma
-dual effective action leads to a
prediction for the polarization of tensor modes, which could allow a direct measurement of the Barbero-Immirzi parameter

\gamma
from cosmological observations. This provides strong motivation to understand the
underlying topological origin of such a duality, which we partially address in this paper.

On the other hand, the question of whether a global
U(1)
duality symmetry exists in
full nonlinear general relativity has been debated. While some authors have claimed its
existence in certain sectors
Kol22
;
KolYau23
, others have argued, using scattering amplitude arguments,
that such a symmetry is broken by interactions
Monteiro23
. Our construction of helicity must be examined in light of this controversy. We will discuss the
implications of our result in the conclusion.

We begin in the next section by recalling the covariant phase space approach to the
electromagnetic helicity
EDHZ16
. Then in Sec.
III
we develop the analogous
construction for general relativity and compute the moment map. We will work with the complex self-dual Ashtekar variables such that the internal duality symmetry is a
U(1)
phase rotation.
The Noether charge of this duality symmetry defines the gravitational helicity, which can be related to the Nieh-Yan term when transformed to the real variables. In Sec.
IV
we explicitly
evaluate the helicity on the Kerr-NUT solution as an example. Sec.
V
contains the conclusion and some discussions.

II
Electromagnetic helicity

Let us start with the action for the vacuum electromagnetic fields

S=-\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathsf{M}}d^{4}x\,F_{\mu\nu}F^{\mu\nu}

(1)

on Minkowski spacetime
\mathsf{M}
. The action is invariant under the duality transformation

F\mapsto\hat{F}=F\cos\theta+(*F)\sin\theta,\quad\theta\in\mathbb{R},

(2)

up to a surface term, where
F=dA
and
*F
is its spacetime Hodge dual.
To construct the covariant phase space, consider a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
.
The variation of the action on a region
\mathsf{M}^{\prime}=[t_{0},t_{1}]\times\Sigma
yields the symplectic current,
whose integral over
\Sigma
defines the presymplectic 2-form

\omega(\delta A,\delta^{\prime}A)=\int_{\Sigma}\big(\delta A\wedge*(\delta^{\prime}F)-\delta^{\prime}A\wedge*(\delta F)\big).

(3)

Quotienting by gauge transformations
A\to A+d\varphi
gives the physical phase space

\mathcal{M}
on which
\omega
is symplectic.

If we introduce a dual gauge potential
C
such that
*F=dC
, the duality transformation
(
2
) can be implemented at the level of connections as a rotation between
A
and
C
.
The infinitesimal generator
\delta_{\epsilon}A=\epsilon C
,
\delta_{\epsilon}C=-\epsilon A

is Hamiltonian, and its moment map gives the optical helicity

J=-\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(A\wedge dA+C\wedge dC),

(4)

which equals the difference
N_{L}-N_{R}
of left and right handed photons
AS96
;
EDHZ16
.

This symplectic approach to electromagnetic helicity motivates us to study the corresponding structure in the covariant phase space of loop gravity so as to find the gravitational helicity.

III
Gravitational helicity

In this section we start with the covariant phase space formulation for loop gravity. Then we identify the duality transformation, compute its Noether charge, and relate the resulting helicity to the Nieh-Yan topological term and the Barbero-Immirzi parameter. To make the duality symmetry completely transparent, we work with the
complex self-dual
Ashtekar variables
Ash86
.

III.1
Covariant phase space for self-dual loop gravity

The covariant phase space for loop gravity has been investigated, for example, in
DH97
;
LSZ98
. This provides the natural arena for discussing
duality symmetries in the full theory.

Let
(M,g)
be a four-dimensional spacetime with a foliation
M=\mathbb{R}\times\Sigma
.
The complex self-dual Ashtekar connection
A^{+}_{AB}
is defined as the projection of the spin
connection
\omega_{AB}
onto the self-dual subspace of the Lorentz Lie algebra:

A^{+}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\omega_{AB}-i\star\omega_{AB}\big),\qquad\star A^{+}_{AB}=iA^{+}_{AB},

(5)

where
(\star\omega)_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ABCD}\,\omega^{CD}
is the internal Hodge dual.
The conjugate momentum is the self-dual 2-form

\Sigma^{+}_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\big(\Sigma_{AB}-i\star\Sigma_{AB}\big),\qquad\Sigma_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{ABCD}\,e^{C}\wedge e^{D},

(6)

constructed from the tetrad
e^{A}
. The action for general relativity in these variables takes the
form

S[e,A^{+}]=\int_{M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB},

(7)

where
F^{+}_{AB}=dA^{+}_{AB}+A^{+}_{A}{}^{C}\wedge A^{+}_{CB}
is the curvature of the self-dual connection.

Varying the action with respect to
A^{+}
and
e
(or equivalently
\Sigma^{+}
) yields the field
equations. Indeed, consider a general variation of the action (
7
):

\delta S=\int_{M}\Big(\delta\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB}+\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta F^{+}_{AB}\Big).

(8)

The variation of the curvature is
\delta F^{+}_{AB}=d_{A^{+}}(\delta A^{+}_{AB})
, where

d_{A^{+}}
is the gauge-covariant exterior derivative. Using integration by parts, we have

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge d_{A^{+}}(\delta A^{+}_{AB})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{M}d\big(\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}\big)-\int_{M}d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}-\int_{M}d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB},

(9)

so that the variation of the action becomes

\displaystyle\delta S=\int_{M}\Big(\delta\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB}-

\displaystyle d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}\Big)+

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\int_{\partial M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}.

(10)

Imposing the variational principle
\delta S=0

gives the field equations:

F^{+}_{AB}=0,\qquad d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}=0,

(11)

which are respectively the self-dual Einstein equation and the torsion-free Gauss constraint.

On shell, the bulk term vanishes and the action variation reduces to a pure boundary term.
Evaluated on a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
, this boundary term defines the symplectic potential

\Theta\big((\delta\Sigma^{+},\delta A^{+})\big)=\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta A^{+}_{AB}.

(12)

The presymplectic
2
-form on the space of solutions
\mathcal{S}
is obtained by taking
an antisymmetrized variation of
\Theta
. For two tangent vectors

(\delta_{1}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{1}A^{+})
and
(\delta_{2}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{2}A^{+})
,

\displaystyle\Omega\big((\delta_{1}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{1}A^{+}),(\delta_{2}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{2}A^{+})\big)=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\delta_{1}\big[\Theta(\delta_{2}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{2}A^{+})\big]-\delta_{2}\big[\Theta(\delta_{1}\Sigma^{+},\delta_{1}A^{+})\big]=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\Sigma}\Big(\delta_{1}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta_{2}A^{+}_{AB}-\delta_{2}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta_{1}A^{+}_{AB}\Big).

(13)

This
2
-form is closed (
d\Omega=0
) and gauge invariant. Its kernel is generated by
infinitesimal gauge symmetries including Lorentz transformations, diffeomorphisms, and
U(1)
frame
rotations. Taking the quotient of
\mathcal{S}
by the gauge group
\mathcal{G}
yields the reduced
phase space
\mathcal{P}=\mathcal{S}/\mathcal{G}
, on which
\Omega
becomes a non-degenerate
symplectic form. Thus
(\mathcal{P},\Omega)
is a true symplectic manifold, providing
the arena for Hamiltonian mechanics and canonical quantization.

III.2
Duality transformation and its Noether charge

The infinitesimal duality transformation is given by

\delta_{\epsilon}A^{+}_{AB}=i\epsilon A^{+}_{AB},\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}\Sigma^{+}_{AB}=i\epsilon\Sigma^{+}_{AB},

(14)

where
\epsilon
is an infinitesimal real parameter. The finite version is

A^{+}\to e^{i\theta}A^{+}
,
\Sigma^{+}\to e^{i\theta}\Sigma^{+}
.
To extract the conserved quantity associated with this symmetry, we use
the covariant phase space version of the Noether theorem.

Consider the self-dual action (
7
).
Under
\delta_{\epsilon}
, the variation gives

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}S

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Big(\delta_{\epsilon}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB}+\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge\delta_{\epsilon}F^{+}_{AB}\Big)

\displaystyle=i\epsilon\int_{M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB}+\int_{M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge d_{A^{+}}\big(i\epsilon A^{+}_{AB}\big).

(15)

Integrating the second term by parts,

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge d_{A^{+}}(i\epsilon A^{+}_{AB})=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle i\epsilon\int_{\partial M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}-i\epsilon\int_{M}d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}.

(16)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}S=i\epsilon

\displaystyle\int_{M}\Big(\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge F^{+}_{AB}-d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}\Big)+

\displaystyle+i\epsilon\int_{\partial M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}.

(17)

The bulk term vanishes on shell by virtue of the field equations
F^{+}_{AB}=0

and the Gauss constraint
d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}=0
. Hence, on shell,

\delta_{\epsilon}S\big|_{\text{on-shell}}=i\epsilon\int_{\partial M}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}.

(18)

This shows that the duality symmetry is realized up to a boundary term.

Let us consider the Noether current related to this duality transformation.
Recall that, in the Iyer-Wald formalism
IW
, given a Lagrangian 4-form
\mathbf{L}
and
a symmetry
\delta_{\epsilon}\mathbf{L}=d\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}
, the
Noether current 3-form is

\mathbf{J}_{\epsilon}=\Theta(\delta_{\epsilon})-\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon},

(19)

where
\Theta(\delta_{\epsilon})
is the pull-back of the symplectic potential
to the boundary. In the present case,
the symplectic potential on a Cauchy slice
\Sigma
is (
12
), and hence
for the duality transformation

\Theta(\delta_{\epsilon})=i\epsilon\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}.

(20)

From (
18
), we identify the boundary form as

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=i\epsilon\,\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB},

(21)

so that the Noether current becomes

\mathbf{J}_{\epsilon}=i\epsilon\,\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}-i\epsilon\,\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}=0.

(22)

A vanishing Noether current is a typical signal of a boundary symmetry that
the genuine conserved charge is carried entirely by the boundary term itself.

Indeed, for any compact spacetime region
\mathcal{M}_{12}
bounded by two
Cauchy surfaces
\Sigma_{1}
and
\Sigma_{2}
, the on-shell identity

\delta_{\epsilon}S_{\mathcal{M}_{12}}=\int_{\partial\mathcal{M}_{12}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}

implies

\int_{\Sigma_{2}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}-\int_{\Sigma_{1}}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=0,

so the integral

Q_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{i\epsilon}\int_{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}

(23)

is independent of the choice of
\Sigma
and therefore conserved on the phase space.

In analogy to the optical helicity, we define the conserved quantity of the duality transform as the

gravitational helicity
:

\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}:=\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{+AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}.

(24)

We can check the gauge invariance of the gravitational helicity (
24
).
Under an infinitesimal Lorentz transformation with self-dual parameter

\lambda^{+}_{AB}
, the connection and momentum transform as

\delta_{\lambda}A^{+}=d_{A^{+}}\lambda^{+},\qquad\delta_{\lambda}\Sigma^{+}=[\lambda^{+},\Sigma^{+}].

(25)

Using the Gauss constraint
d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+AB}=0
and the identity

[\lambda^{+},\Sigma^{+}]^{AB}\wedge A^{+}_{AB}=-\lambda^{+AB}d_{A^{+}}\Sigma^{+}_{AB}

(up to a total derivative), one finds

\delta_{\lambda}\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\int_{\Sigma}d\bigl(\Sigma^{+AB}\lambda^{+}_{AB}\bigr),

(26)

which vanishes for a Cauchy surface without boundary, or for asymptotically flat
spacetimes with suitable fall-off conditions. Thus
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}
is
gauge invariant.

III.3
Relation to the Nieh-Yan term

To express
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}
in terms of real variables we must first clarify the conventions for the internal Hodge dual.
We work with a Lorentzian internal metric
\eta_{AB}={\rm diag}(-1,1,1,1)
and a completely antisymmetric tensor
\epsilon_{ABCD}
with
\epsilon_{0123}=+1
.
The internal Hodge star acts on an
\mathfrak{so}(1,3)
-valued form
X_{AB}
as

(\star X)_{AB}=\frac{1}{2}\epsilon_{AB}{}^{CD}X_{CD},\qquad\star^{2}=-1.

(27)

For any two such forms
X^{AB}
and
Y_{AB}
the following identities hold:

\displaystyle X^{AB}\wedge(\star Y)_{AB}

\displaystyle=(\star X)^{AB}\wedge Y_{AB},

(28)

\displaystyle(\star X)^{AB}\wedge(\star Y)_{AB}

\displaystyle=-X^{AB}\wedge Y_{AB}.

(29)

The self-dual connection and conjugate momentum are related to the real variables
\omega_{AB},\Sigma_{AB}
by (
5
),(
6
).
In terms of these real variables, the internal duality transformation takes the form of an

SO(2)
rotation mixing the connection
\omega^{AB}
and its internal Hodge dual
(\star\omega)^{AB}
:

\omega\to\cos\theta\,\omega+\sin\theta\,\star\omega,\qquad\star\omega\to-\sin\theta\,\omega+\cos\theta\,\star\omega.

(30)

This is the direct analog of the electromagnetic duality
EDHZ16
.

Inserting these (
5
),(
6
) into
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}
we obtain

\displaystyle\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\frac{1}{4}\int_{\Sigma}\Big[

\displaystyle\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}-i\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}-

\displaystyle-i(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}-(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}\Big].

(31)

Using (
28
) the second and third terms combine into
-2i\,(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}
, while (
29
) turns the fourth term into
+\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}
. Hence

\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}.

(32)

Now employ the identity
(\star\Sigma)^{AB}=-e^{A}\wedge e^{B}
, which follows directly from the definition of
\Sigma^{AB}
, so we have

(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}=-e^{A}\wedge e^{B}\wedge\omega_{AB}=-\omega_{AB}\wedge e^{A}\wedge e^{B}.

(33)

Using the definition of
the torsion 2‑form
T_{A}=de_{A}+\omega_{AB}\wedge e^{B}
, we compute

\displaystyle e^{A}\wedge T_{A}

\displaystyle=e^{A}\wedge\bigl(de_{A}+\omega_{AB}\wedge e^{B}\bigr)=

\displaystyle=e^{A}de_{A}-\omega_{AB}\wedge e^{A}\wedge e^{B}.

(34)

Consequently,

\displaystyle(\star\Sigma)^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}=

\displaystyle-\omega_{AB}\wedge e^{A}\wedge e^{B}=

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{A}\wedge T_{A}-e^{A}\wedge de_{A}.

(35)

Hence the gravitational helicity becomes

\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}-\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Sigma}e^{A}\wedge T_{A}+\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Sigma}e^{A}\wedge de_{A}.

(36)

Eq. (
36
) reveals that the gravitational helicity consists of two distinct geometric contributions.
The first term arises from the real part of the self-dual area flux and is naturally associated with the area and intrinsic rotation of the spatial surface
\Sigma
.
The second piece, encoded in
\frac{i}{2}\int_{\Sigma}(e^{A}\wedge T_{A}-e^{A}\wedge de_{A})
, originates from the internal Hodge dual
\star\Sigma\wedge\omega
and captures the twisting density of the tetrad.
In particular, the term
e^{A}\wedge de_{A}
measures how the orthonormal frame winds around the surface.

The integral in the second term in (
36
)
is precisely the boundary term of the Nieh–Yan topological invariant
NY82
. In four dimensions the Nieh-Yan invariant is

\mathcal{N}=T^{A}\wedge T_{A}-R_{AB}\wedge e^{A}\wedge e^{B}
, which satisfies
\mathcal{N}=d(e^{A}\wedge T_{A})
. Hence, on shell,

\int_{\Sigma}e^{A}\wedge T_{A}=\int_{M}\mathcal{N},

(37)

where is a 4-manifold with
\Sigma
a boundary,
revealing the topological nature of the gravitational helicity.

III.4
Including the Barbero-Immirzi parameter

If one starts instead from the real Ashtekar-Barbero connection

A^{AB}=\omega^{AB}+\frac{1}{\gamma}(\star\omega)^{AB}
, the appropriate
action is the Holst action
Holst

S_{\rm H}=\int_{M}\Bigl(\Sigma^{AB}\wedge R_{AB}-\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star R)_{AB}\Bigr).

(38)

The internal duality transformation acts on the spin connection as an
SO(2)

rotation mixing
\omega_{AB}
and its internal dual:

\delta_{\epsilon}\omega_{AB}=\epsilon\,(\star\omega)_{AB},\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}(\star\omega)_{AB}=-\epsilon\,\omega_{AB},

(39)

while the tetrad (and therefore
\Sigma^{AB}
) is left invariant,

\delta_{\epsilon}e^{A}=0
,
\delta_{\epsilon}\Sigma^{AB}=0
.

Under (
39
) the curvature transforms as

\delta_{\epsilon}R_{AB}=d_{\omega}\delta_{\epsilon}\omega_{AB}=\epsilon\,d_{\omega}(\star\omega)_{AB}
, and similarly for the dual
curvature
\delta_{\epsilon}(\star R)_{AB}=-\epsilon\,d_{\omega}\omega_{AB}
.
Thus

\displaystyle\delta_{\epsilon}S_{\rm H}

\displaystyle=\int_{M}\Bigl[\Sigma^{AB}\wedge d_{\omega}(\epsilon\star\omega_{AB})-\frac{1}{\gamma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\bigl(-\epsilon\,d_{\omega}\omega_{AB}\bigr)\Bigr]

\displaystyle=\epsilon\int_{M}\Bigl[\Sigma^{AB}\wedge d_{\omega}(\star\omega)_{AB}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge d_{\omega}\omega_{AB}\Bigr].

(40)

Integrating by parts and using the torsion‑free Gauss constraint

d_{\omega}\Sigma^{AB}=0
(on shell) we obtain a pure boundary term:

\delta_{\epsilon}S_{\rm H}\big|_{\text{on-shell}}=\epsilon\int_{\partial M}\Bigl(\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}\Bigr).

(41)

Following the same Noether analysis as in Sec. 3.2, the boundary form is

\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=\epsilon\Bigl(\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}\Bigr),

and the corresponding conserved charge on a Cauchy slice
\Sigma
is

Q_{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{\epsilon}\int_{\Sigma}\boldsymbol{\alpha}_{\epsilon}=\int_{\Sigma}\Bigl(\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}+\frac{1}{\gamma}\,\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}\Bigr).

(42)

Thus the gravitational helicity in the presence of the Barbero–Immirzi parameter can be defined as

\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}+\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge(\star\omega)_{AB}.

(43)

The same analysis gives rise to the final expression of gravitational helicity

\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}=\frac{1}{\gamma}\int_{\Sigma}\Sigma^{AB}\wedge\omega_{AB}+\int_{\Sigma}e^{A}\wedge T_{A}-\int_{\Sigma}e^{A}\wedge de_{A}

(44)

This is the real-variable counterpart of the
self-dual helicity (
36
). Consequently, the
gravitational helicity in the Holst formulation is not a pure boundary
term of the Nieh–Yan invariant; the topological contribution is
supplemented by the frame-twist integral, exactly as the internal duality
symmetry requires.

IV
Evaluation on the Kerr-NUT spacetime

We now present an explicit evaluation of the gravitational helicity on the Kerr-NUT
solution, a rotating vacuum spacetime with a NUT parameter
\ell
(gravitational magnetic charge).

In Plebański-Demiański coordinates
(p,q,\tau,\sigma)
, the Kerr-NUT metric
takes the form
Kol22
;
Griffiths07

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathcal{X}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}\,(d\tau+q^{2}d\sigma)^{2}+\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}}{\mathcal{X}}\,dp^{2}-

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\mathcal{Y}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}\,(d\tau-p^{2}d\sigma)^{2}+\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}}{\mathcal{Y}}\,dq^{2},

(45)

with the structure functions

\mathcal{X}=a^{2}-(p-\ell)^{2},\qquad\mathcal{Y}=a^{2}-\ell^{2}-2mq+q^{2}.

(46)

Here
m
,
a
, and
\ell
are respectively the mass, rotation, and NUT parameters.
An orthonormal tetrad
e^{A}
(
A=0,1,2,3
) compatible with the Lorentzian signature

\eta_{AB}=\mathrm{diag}(-1,1,1,1)
is chosen as

\displaystyle e^{0}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{X}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}}\,(d\tau+q^{2}d\sigma),\quad e^{1}=\sqrt{\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}}{\mathcal{X}}}\,dp,

\displaystyle e^{2}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{Y}}{p^{2}+q^{2}}}\,(d\tau-p^{2}d\sigma),\quad e^{3}=\sqrt{\frac{p^{2}+q^{2}}{\mathcal{Y}}}\,dq.

(47)

We evaluate the gravitational helicity on a Cauchy surface
\Sigma
defined by
q=\text{const}\to\infty
.
In this limit,
\mathcal{Y}\sim q^{2}-2mq+(a^{2}-\ell^{2})
, and the tetrad expands as

\displaystyle e^{0}

\displaystyle\sim q\sqrt{\mathcal{X}}\,d\sigma+\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{X}}}{q}\,d\tau-\frac{\sqrt{\mathcal{X}}p^{2}}{2q}d\sigma+\mathcal{O}(q^{-3}),

\displaystyle e^{1}

\displaystyle\sim\frac{q}{\sqrt{\mathcal{X}}}\,dp+\mathcal{O}(q^{-1}),

\displaystyle e^{2}

\displaystyle\sim\bigl(d\tau-p^{2}d\sigma\bigr)-\frac{m}{q}\,d\tau+\frac{mp^{2}}{q}\,d\sigma+\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}),

\displaystyle e^{3}

\displaystyle\sim dq+\frac{m}{q}\,dq+\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}).

(48)

From these tetrad expansions, the first Cartan structure equation

de^{A}+\omega^{A}{}_{B}\wedge e^{B}=0
yields the following non‑vanishing
spin connection components at leading orders:

\displaystyle\omega^{0}{}_{1}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{\mathcal{X}^{\prime}}{2\sqrt{\mathcal{X}}\,q}\,e^{0}-\frac{p}{q^{2}}\,e^{2}+\mathcal{O}(q^{-3}),

\displaystyle\omega^{0}{}_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq-\frac{p}{q^{2}}\,e^{1}+\mathcal{O}(q^{-3}),

\displaystyle\omega^{0}{}_{3}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{1}{q}\,e^{0}+\mathcal{O}(q^{-2}),

\displaystyle\omega^{1}{}_{2}

\displaystyle\simeq\frac{p}{q^{2}}\,e^{0}+\mathcal{O}(q^{-3}),

\displaystyle\omega^{1}{}_{3}

\displaystyle\omega^{2}{}_{3}

V
Conclusion and discussion

We have studied the gravitational helicity in classical general relativity using the covariant phase space method.
By working with the complex self-dual Ashtekar variables, we made the internal duality symmetry
manifest as a simple
U(1)
phase rotation. The Noether charge of this symmetry yields a conserved
quantity
\mathfrak{H}_{\text{grav}}
, which we identified as the gravitational helicity. When
expressed in terms of the real variables, this helicity contains the integral of
e^{A}\wedge T_{A}
over a Cauchy surface, which is precisely the
Nieh–Yan topological invariant, thereby revealing the topological origin of helicity in loop
gravity.
We have also evaluated the helicity on the Kerr-Taub-NUT spacetime.

It is important to note that the existence of a global
U(1)
duality symmetry in full
nonlinear general relativity has been contested
Monteiro23
. Scattering amplitude
arguments suggest that helicity conservation is violated by graviton interactions, and the
duality may only hold in restricted sectors of solutions (such as type D spacetimes). Our
construction, which relies on the self-dual Ashtekar formulation, should be understood as
applying to those sectors where the symmetry is realized.
On the other hand, the no-go theorem of Deser and Seminara
DS05
establishes that the electric-magnetic duality of linearized gravity,
based on the spacetime Hodge dual, cannot be extended to the full nonlinear theory.
The
U(1)
symmetry studied in this paper is of a different nature: it acts on the internal Lorentz indices
and rotates the self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of the connection. In the self-dual Ashtekar formulation,
this internal duality becomes a simple phase rotation
A^{+}\to e^{i\theta}A^{+}
,
\Sigma^{+}\to e^{i\theta}\Sigma^{+}
.
Because the symmetry acts on the
internal tangent space rather than on spacetime differential forms, it evades the assumptions of the Deser-Seminara theorem.
Thus, while the full nonlinear theory does not
admit a spacetime electric-magnetic duality, it does possess a well-defined internal duality
symmetry in the self-dual sector, whose moment map yields the gravitational helicity identified here.

The coupling to matter fields can be incorporated by enlarging the phase space
to include torsion, as discussed in
KolYau23
. In such an extended framework, the
duality current becomes the gradient of the axion field, and the helicity may generalize
to include matter contributions. Moreover, since the recent work by Long and Zhou
LZ26
obtains a similar formula for gravitational helicity in terms of Nieh-Yan term on null hypersurfaces, and our result is on space-like hypersurfaces, we can seek for a spacetime version of gravitational helicity in terms of Nieh-Yan type terms.
