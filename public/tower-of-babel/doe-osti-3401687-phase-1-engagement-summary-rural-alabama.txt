Executive Summary
The Rural Alabama Clean Transportation Planning and Development Project received technical
assistance through the U.S. Department of Energy’s Communities Local Energy Action Program
(LEAP) to expand awareness of and access to alternative transportation fuels through stakeholder
engagement efforts. Communities LEAP delivers customized support for communities to
identify, analyze, and implement locally driven energy projects that create long-term economic
opportunities and strengthen energy reliability. The initiative was led by the National Laboratory
of the Rockies (NLR)—the technical assistance provider—and a regional coalition formed by 10
counties in the region: Autauga County, Bibb County, Conecuh County, Dallas County, Greene
County, Hale County, Marengo County, Perry County, Sumter County, and Wilcox County.

Phase 1 of the project centered on outreach, relationship-building, and the identification of
community priorities. Through two workshops held in October and December 2024, the coalition
engaged over 120 stakeholders—including county commissioners, school district representatives,
and public and private fleet operators. These sessions included presentations from technical
experts and industry partners, as well as a vehicle showcase and structured discussions to assess
stakeholder needs and concerns.

Key findings from Phase 1 highlight both interest in alternative fuel technologies and the unique
challenges faced by rural communities in adopting these solutions. Participants emphasized the
need for:

 • Technical information on vehicle specifications and suitability for rural areas,
 • Guidance on procurement strategies and total cost of ownership,
 • Infrastructure planning and operational feasibility for long-distance and low-density
 contexts,
 • Economic development opportunities tied to alternative fuel infrastructure,
 • Policy clarity around future transportation revenue models, especially with declining gas
 tax revenues.
A stakeholder questionnaire reinforced these findings, identifying school transportation, public
EV charging, economic development, and public fleet transitions as top areas of interest.

Informed by these results, NLR’s team and the coalition identified three key priority areas for
Phase 2 technical assistance:

 1. Alternative fuel solutions tailored to rural transportation challenges.
 2. Economic benefits of alternative fuel infrastructure.
 3. Guidance for fleet planning and vehicle procurement.
Phase 2 will focus on developing practical, stakeholder-informed resources aligned with these
priorities, while expanding the coalition’s outreach to new partners and sectors. The goal is to
equip rural Alabama communities with the tools and knowledge needed to make informed
decisions about alternative fuels investments that align with local conditions and goals.

Background
The Rural Alabama Clean Transportation Planning and Development Project is an effort under
the Communities Local Energy Assistance Program (Communities LEAP) focused on providing
technical assistance related to alternative transportation fuels in rural Alabama. The primary goal
of this project is to engage with stakeholders across the region to build relationships and
awareness of alternative transportation fuel opportunities and identify specific interventions and
technologies to implement with interested stakeholders. This project utilized a phased scope,
focusing on outreach and engagement for Phase 1, and on technical resource delivery in Phase 2.
In the first phase, the team focused on strengthening the coalition’s network of stakeholders and
shared high-level information about potential benefits of alternative fuels. This report
summarizes the engagement efforts undertaken in Phase 1, which provide the basis for the
technical assistance and resource development planned for Phase 2.

This project aims to identify needs and opportunities related to alternative transportation fuels in
10 rural counties across the western portion of Alabama (Figure 1). The ten counties in the
region of interest are Autauga County, Bibb County, Conecuh County, Dallas County, Greene
County, Hale County, Marengo County, Perry County, Sumter County, and Wilcox County.

Workshop Design
Prior to engaging with LEAP, the coalition has identified a lack of awareness related to
alternative transportation fuels in the region. In rural areas, this challenge is compounded by
limited capacity to evaluate, plan, and implement new transportation solutions, as well as the
distinct characteristics of rural transportation needs—such as longer travel distances, reliance on
larger vehicle fleets for agriculture and industry, and fewer existing fueling and charging
infrastructure options. Given these factors, the coalition seeks to equip local stakeholders and
policymakers with the necessary resources and information to make informed decisions. By
expanding awareness of electric and propane vehicle opportunities, the coalition aims to support
cost-effective, reliable, and sustainable transportation solutions tailored to rural communities.

To achieve these goals, NLR collaborated with the LEAP community coalition to convene
stakeholders in educational workshops. Two educational workshops were held in October and
December 2024, featuring experts from research organizations, local governments, utilities and
other entities from the public sector, as well as private industries. At these events, NLR and the
coalition team engaged with stakeholders including county commissioners, public and private
fleet operators, and local private industry groups on alternative fuels topics, explored related
funding programs, and identified other incentives they can pursue based on their eligibility,
interests, and specific needs.

The coalition led outreach to organizations across the region, focusing on connecting with school
districts, local and regional governments, and private businesses with fleets. Most attendees at
the workshop represented local governments or school districts (Figure 2). Outreach was
conducted by Alabama Clean Fuels Coalition (ACFC) staff via direct email, phone calls, and
press releases and op-eds in local news outlets. Stakeholders were asked to register for the events
in advance and to respond to a questionnaire to identify their priorities related to transportation.
A total of 43 individuals responded to the questionnaire developed by ACFC with input from
NLR SMEs.

The first workshop was held in-person in Greensboro, Alabama on October 30, 2024.
Approximately 42 external stakeholders attended this workshop. The workshop agenda included
presentations from ACFC, private industry, local government fleets, and NLR. The event also
featured an alternative fuel vehicle showcase over lunch, which included a propane school bus, a
propane light duty truck, an electric light duty truck, and an electric SUV. After the presentations
and showcase, NLR led a discussion with the attendees focused on understanding questions and
concerns related to alternative transportation, especially in rural contexts.

The second workshop was held virtually on Zoom on December 19, 2024. Approximately 43
external stakeholders attended this workshop. The workshop agenda included presentations from
ACFC, private industry, and Auburn University. After the presentations, NLR led a brief
discussion on questions and concerns related to alternative transportation.

Results
Workshop results
During the workshops, NLR led discussions focused on understanding the primary questions and
concerns that stakeholders had related to alternative fuels and vehicles. Here, we highlight the
main themes that emerged from these discussions.

Interest in technology specifications
Workshop participants indicated high interest in
obtaining more detailed information about vehicle
technology, reinforcing the coalition's prior
assessment of a general lack of awareness about
alternative transportation fuels in the region. This
alignment between pre-identified gaps and
workshop findings underscores the need for
targeted educational resources. Attendees asked
questions about key vehicle performance metrics,
including speed, acceleration, range, weight, and
size, indicating a desire for technical clarity on
how alternatively fueled vehicles compare to
conventional options. Additionally, discussions
emerged regarding the suitability of electric and
propane-powered vehicles for the region’s hilly
terrain, highlighting concerns about operational
feasibility in areas with challenging topography.
These findings suggest that future outreach
efforts should address both general knowledge
gaps and region-specific performance
considerations to support informed decision-
making among local stakeholders. Figure 3. Map of workshop participant

Interest in use cases for different vehicle
classes
Participants were interested in understanding what types of alternative fuels would be best for
different vehicle classes across a fleet. Questions centered on the specific operational needs of
school buses, in-town municipal and public-sector vehicle fleets, private fleets, and freight
transportation. Attendees sought clarity on how different fuel types—such as electricity and
propane—align with the performance requirements, duty cycles, and operational constraints of
these vehicle categories. In addition to technical feasibility, participants emphasized the
importance of financial considerations, particularly the role of available incentive programs in
offsetting the higher upfront costs associated with alternative fuel vehicle adoption. Many
viewed financial support mechanisms as equally critical to technological performance in the
decision-making process.

Furthermore, there was broad interest in receiving structured guidance on vehicle procurement,
including best practices for evaluating alternative fuel options, understanding total cost of

 4

ownership, and navigating funding opportunities. Participants expressed a need for decision-
making frameworks that account for the distinct operational and financial considerations
associated with different vehicle classes. These findings suggest that future technical assistance
efforts should incorporate tailored procurement strategies and cost-benefit analyses to support
informed fleet transitions.

Interest in solutions that center rural needs
Participants expressed concerns about the viability of alternative transportation fuels in rural
settings, citing specific challenges associated with long-distance travel and operational
constraints. A key issue raised was whether alternative fuel vehicles, particularly electric and
propane-powered options, could provide sufficient range to accommodate the extensive routes
commonly required in rural areas. These concerns stem from the low population density and the
logistical demands placed on local fleets, which often operate with limited staff capacity and
must cover large geographic areas efficiently. Attendees also sought clarity on how different
alternative fuel technologies compare in terms of range, refueling/recharging infrastructure
availability, and operational reliability under rural conditions. The discussion highlighted the
need for targeted analyses that address range limitations, infrastructure deployment strategies,
and workforce capacity challenges unique to rural fleet operations. These insights suggest that
future outreach and technical assistance should focus on practical implementation strategies,
including infrastructure planning, fuel availability, and policy mechanisms that support rural
fleet transitions.

Interest in local benefits from alternative fuel infrastructure
Participants expressed concern that failure to act quickly to install alternative fuel infrastructure
could result in missed local economic opportunities. Specifically, they highlighted the potential
loss of revenue from fueling and charging stations, sales tax generation, job creation, and broader
economic development benefits associated with early adoption. This sense of urgency, driven by
a “fear of missing out,” suggests that communities recognize the economic potential of
alternative fuels but may require additional support in navigating the planning and
implementation process.

These findings indicate that efforts to promote alternative fuel adoption could benefit from
emphasizing the tangible local benefits, such as increased tax revenues, business growth, and
workforce development. Future outreach and technical assistance should incorporate economic
impact assessments, case studies of successful implementations, and strategies for maximizing
local economic gains to help communities make informed, proactive decisions.

Interest in the economics of electric vehicles
Participants expressed concern that replacing traditional internal combustion engine vehicles
with electric vehicles may reduce revenue from the existing “Gas Tax”. The “Gas Tax”
distributes funding to cities and counties across the state for infrastructure projects to improve
roads and bridges. Participants and experts discussed the potential transition from a traditional
gasoline tax to an electric vehicle (EV) registration fee as a means of maintaining transportation
infrastructure funding. However, stakeholders raised numerous questions regarding the logistical
and economic implications of such a shift, particularly concerning the mechanisms for collecting
and distributing revenue from the state to the local level. Key concerns included the rationale for

fee structures, potential disparities in revenue allocation, and the overall impact on local
transportation budgets.

These discussions highlight a critical need for further analysis and clear guidance on the
economic mechanisms involved in transitioning from fuel tax revenues to alternative funding
models for EVs. Future efforts should focus on providing stakeholders with detailed information
on policy design, revenue projections, and distribution strategies to ensure that local
governments can effectively plan for and adapt to this evolving financial landscape.

Questionnaire Results
Half of the workshop participants (n=43) completed a questionnaire when they registered to
attend a workshop, that was developed by ACFC and reviewed by NLR SMEs. This
questionnaire asked participants to select the transportation topics that were most of interest to
their organization.

The transportation topics of greatest interest to respondents are:

 1. School transportation
 2. Public electric vehicle charging
 3. Economic development
 4. Public vehicle fleets
 5. Workplace EV charging
 6. Private vehicle fleets
This ranked list of transportation topics of interest represents the priorities of the organizations
who engaged with the educational workshops led by the coalition, and not necessarily the
transportation priorities of organizations across the region, generally. It is important to note that
most of the workshop attendees represented local governments or school districts (Figure 2). As
a result, the ranked list of topics is driven primarily by the priorities of local governments and
school districts (Figure 3).

Priority Areas for Technical Assistance
The technical assistance provided through this project was designed to directly support the
community coalition in achieving its broader goals of advancing education, fostering economic
development, and promoting the adoption of alternative transportation fuels. To achieve these
objectives, we leveraged the findings from the workshop and the results of the survey to identify
priority areas of interest to inform the development of additional technical resources.

These priority areas are:

 • Rural transportation challenges and alternative fuel solutions
 o Many characteristics of rural communities present challenges to the transportation
 sector. Through this resource, we will collaborate with experts in rural
 transportation to identify these challenges and provide a high-level framework for
 how alternative fuel and advanced technology vehicles may offer solutions. This
 will support engagement with stakeholders in rural Alabama by enabling the
 coalition to present alternative fuel solutions that will address the specific
 transportation needs or challenges of a given stakeholder.
 • Economic benefits of alternative fuel infrastructure for rural communities
 o Alternative fuel vehicles and their associated infrastructure offer a range of
 benefits that could provide economic development opportunities for rural
 communities. This resource will provide insights for rural communities about the
 opportunities for transportation cost reduction, new revenue generation, and

 7

 workforce development associated with implementing alternative fuel
 infrastructure projects. Additionally, the resource developed for this topic area
 will highlight tradeoffs between technologies and their respective costs and
 benefits.
  • Alternative fuels for public and private vehicle fleets
 o Fleet managers need accessible information that will enable them to select
 vehicles across based on the needs of their fleet. Upgrading a fleet to incorporate
 advanced technology vehicles may result in cost savings for a fleet manager, and
 this resource will provide information about how fleet managers can select the
 right alternative fuel or advanced vehicle technology based on their individual
 goals.

Opportunity Space
In Phase 1 of this project, the team focused on strengthening the coalition’s network of
stakeholders and shared high-level information about potential benefits of alternative fuels. In
Phase 2, we will develop technical assistance resources on the priority areas above to provide
101 information and to help increase capacity at the regional level through the coalition.

Additionally, given the coalition’s goal to enhance awareness about alternative transportation
fuels across the region, we will develop a strategy to improve the coalition’s engagement with
new stakeholders across the region, with specific guidance to utilize the technical assistance
resources developed.

Technical Resource Development
In order to develop technical resources focused on the priority areas identified by stakeholders at
the educational workshops, the NLR team will convene subject matter experts. The subject
matter experts will support the project by providing expertise and data to develop three unique
resource documents that may be used by the coalition to share information about alternative fuels
with new stakeholders in the region. These resources will answer the most critical questions
identified by workshop attendees and identify opportunities to bring economic benefits from
alternative fuels to communities in the region.

Engagement and Implementation Guidance
To improve the efficacy of the technical resources, NLR will also create a guidance document to
share ideas and best practices for sharing the information with stakeholders. This will include an
analysis of the stakeholders who are already actively engaged with the coalition, and an
assessment of areas for future work to improve relationships and rapport with other types of
organizations who may be less engaged in the coalition’s efforts.

Key Findings
The first phase of the Rural Alabama Clean Transportation Planning and Development Project
identified challenges and opportunities for adoption of alternative transportation fuels in rural
communities. Through targeted outreach, educational workshops, and stakeholder feedback
mechanisms, the NLR team in partnership with the community coalition was able to assess local
awareness, identify region-specific needs, and establish a roadmap for technical assistance in
Phase 2. The findings outlined below reflect the most significant themes that emerged from
stakeholder engagement and were intended to inform resource development, planning strategies,
and ongoing engagement efforts.

Educational Awareness and Opportunities for Engagement
Stakeholders demonstrated strong curiosity about alternative fuel technologies despite a
widespread lack of foundational knowledge. The need for targeted educational resources was
reinforced by requests for detailed information on vehicle performance specifications—including
range, acceleration, size, and suitability for hilly terrain. This knowledge gap suggests a critical
need for foundational, yet technically robust, information that can support informed decision-
making across sectors.

 9

Prioritized Use Cases
Survey and workshop responses indicate that school transportation, public EV charging, and
public fleet transitions are the most pressing priorities. This finding is consistent with the
attendee profile, which was heavily weighted toward local governments and school districts. The
emphasis on public-sector needs suggests that early deployment strategies should be tailored to
public-use vehicles and infrastructure, where stakeholder capacity and interest are highest.

Need for Decision-Support Tools for Vehicle Procurement
Stakeholders consistently expressed a need for clear, structured guidance on vehicle selection,
funding access, and total cost of ownership. Participants were especially interested in matching
fuel types—such as propane or electricity—to specific vehicle classes and operational contexts.
These findings highlight the importance of developing decision-support tools that incorporate
technical, financial, and operational criteria to support procurement planning.

Context-Specific Infrastructure and Operational Barriers in Rural Settings
The viability of alternative fuel vehicles in rural areas was a recurring concern. Long travel
distances, limited charging and fueling infrastructure, and constrained operational capacity were
cited as barriers to adoption. This underscores the need for rural-specific analyses that address
logistical, infrastructural, and workforce limitations, and that can support the strategic planning
of resilient fueling networks.

Economic Development Framing to Accelerate Adoption
Many participants emphasized the potential local economic benefits associated with alternative
fuel infrastructure, including job creation, sales tax generation, and new business opportunities.
However, they also expressed concern that slow adoption could cause communities to miss out
on these benefits. This points to a strategic opportunity to frame alternative fuel deployment as a
pathway to economic revitalization in rural regions.

Concerns on Transportation Revenue and Policy Uncertainty
Stakeholders raised questions about the implications of transitioning away from gasoline tax
revenues, which currently support local infrastructure budgets. Concerns centered on the design
of alternative funding mechanisms—such as EV registration fees—and how revenue would be
allocated from the state to the local level. These discussions highlight the need for policy
analysis and economic modeling to guide future planning.

Identified Strategic Technical Assistance Areas
Three priority areas were identified for technical resource development: (1) rural transportation
challenges and alternative fuel solutions, (2) economic benefits of alternative fuel infrastructure,
and (3) procurement and fleet planning strategies for public and private fleets. These areas will
serve as the foundation for Phase 2 resource development and outreach activities.

Stakeholder Network and Opportunities for Scaling
The project successfully engaged more than 120 stakeholders, building trust and strengthening
the coalition’s regional presence. This foundation creates momentum for Phase 2 activities,
which will focus on scaling outreach, disseminating tailored technical resources, and expanding
engagement to new stakeholder groups.
