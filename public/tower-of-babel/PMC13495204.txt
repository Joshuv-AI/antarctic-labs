Postoperative exercise modulating gut microbiota and associated metabolites to facilitate bone-tendon healing in rotator cuff tears
Authors: Jialou Wu, Songye Wu, Tianmin He, Shanhong Fang, Huixin Zhu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundRotator cuff tear is a prevalent shoulder injury, and postoperative exercise is considered to facilitate healing. However, the effect of postoperative exercise on rotator cuff tear healing through gut microbiota and metabolites remains unclear.MethodsThe control (con) group (n = 10) underwent sham surgeries, while the model (mod) and postoperative exercise (sport) groups (each n = 10) underwent supraspinatus tendon repair surgeries. Starting on postoperative day 7, the sport group underwent treadmill exercise at 10 m/min for 20 min/day, 5 days/week, for 4 consecutive weeks. Subsequently, the supraspinatus tendon-humerus complex, fecal, and serum samples were collected.ResultsSynchrotron radiation micro-CT and histological studies indicated that postoperative exercise was associated with reduced bone loss and enhanced new bone formation at tendon-bone sites. Gut microbiome profiling revealed that exercise was associated with reshaped microbial composition, with increased Proteobacteria and Dubosiella, and decreased Lachnoclostridium and Allobaculum in the sport group compared with the model group. Functional analyses showed that the HIF-1 signaling pathway and β-Alanine metabolism were notably enriched following exercise. Network correlation analysis suggested that Dubosiella functioned as a central taxon within the microbial community. Metabolomics suggested that exercise was associated with altered metabolite abundances like Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg, which correlated with pathways like gap junction. Moreover, metabolomics further identified β-Alanine metabolism as the key overlapping pathway connecting microbial alterations with serum metabolic changes, with β-alanyl-L-arginine as a representative metabolite. Seven microbe–metabolite pairs showed significant correlations. Among them, Parabacteroides exhibited strong associations with multiple metabolites, including cycloheptyl derivatives, and demonstrated predictive value for tendon–bone healing (AUC = 0.88).ConclusionIn a murine model, postoperative exercise following rotator cuff tear repair surgery was associated with modulating gut microbiota and metabolites, which may contribute to rotator cuff tear healing. This finding provides preclinical insights into integrating postoperative exercise into rotator cuff tear recovery strategies.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12891-026-10083-w.Keywords: Rotator cuff tear healing, Postoperative exercise, Gut microbiota, Metabolites

Introduction

Rotator cuff tears are one of the most common upper extremity conditions, typically occurring at the tendon-bone insertion site. They result in joint dysfunction and recurrent pain, limiting activities of daily living and the range, and imposing a significant financial strain on society [1]. Surgical repair is commonly recommended for symptomatic full-thickness tears or patients with failed conservative treatment [2, 3]. Nevertheless, the rate of recurrent tears after repair ranges from 13% to 94% [4]. Research has indicated that multiple elements affect the restoration of rotator cuff lesions, such as increasing age, significant tear dimensions, poor muscle quality, cigarette smoking, and fatty infiltration [5]. In addition, insufficient repair or unsuitable rehabilitation plans after surgery can also be contributing factors [6]. Consequently, promoting effective regeneration and functional recovery after rotator cuff repair surgery remains a formidable clinical challenge.

Postoperative exercise after rotator cuff repair has been shown to enhance the mechanical properties of the repaired tendon, even though immobilization is still a common clinical treatment [7–9]. Postoperative exercise seems to hasten the initial stage of bone-tendon union, a progression that might be associated with the increased expression of peripheral neuropeptides, which are recognized to contribute to the restoration of tissues [10]. However, the precise mechanisms by which exercise improves tendon healing remain unclear, and further research is needed.

Many studies have shown that exercise can induce changes in gut microbial composition and function [11], and these changes can affect host physiology, metabolism, immunity, and behavior [12, 13]. In addition, exercise further boosts the body’s metabolism by promoting the production of short-chain fatty acids [14]. Given that intestinal microorganisms play a crucial role in physiological activities [15, 16], alterations in intestinal flora composition may influence the progression of joint diseases [17, 18]. Meanwhile, the gut microbiota interacts with the host by producing metabolites [19]. These metabolites not only influence genetic and epigenetic regulation but also regulate the metabolism of immune cells [20–22]. As a result, the combined analysis of the metabolome and microbiome has emerged as a commonly employed approach. For instance, the function of intestinal flora in maintaining immune balance and alleviating bone destruction among patients with rheumatoid arthritis is exerted through biochemical mediators, including short-chain fatty acids and bile acids [23]. However, there are currently no reports on the influence of exercise on mediating gut microbiota’s involvement in rotator cuff tear repair.

In this research, our objective was to explore the influence of postoperative exercise on the curative effect of rotator cuff tear repair surgery by modulating gut microbiota and metabolic pathways. Comparing the microbiome and metabolome profiles between mice that engaged in exercise post-rotator cuff tear surgery and those that did not, we identified key differential microorganisms and metabolites that significantly influence rotator cuff tear repair. These insights present a novel therapeutic approach aimed at enhancing the success rate of rotator cuff tear surgical interventions.

Materials and methods

Study design

All animal procedures in this study were approved by the Experimental Animal Welfare Ethics Committee of Fujian Medical University, as evidenced by the protocol number IACUC FJMU 2024 − 0313. In this research, 30 male C57BL/6 mice (8 weeks old, weighing 22–24 g) were used. The mice were obtained from Beijing SPF Biotechnology Co., Ltd. (Beijing, China) and maintained under specific pathogen-free conditions. Animals were fed a standard maintenance diet (SPF-F02-002; Beijing SPF Biotechnology Co., Ltd., China), sterilized by 60Co irradiation, with food and sterile drinking water provided ad libitum in autoclaved water bottles. Mice were housed in individually ventilated cages at a density of five animals per cage under controlled environmental conditions (12 h light–dark cycle; temperature 22 ± 2 °C; relative humidity 50–60%). To minimize cross-group transfer of microbiota, animals from different experimental groups were housed in separate cages throughout the study, and no co-housing between groups occurred. Cage effects were not modeled as an independent variable in the statistical analysis.

The animal experiment flowchart is illustrated in Fig. 1A. The mice were allocated randomly into three groups (n = 10 per group) using a random number generator. For the mice in the control group (con), surgical incisions were made similar to those in the model group (mod) and the postoperative exercise group (sport). However, no substantial surgical interventions were performed. These mice were permitted to move freely within their cages. The mod group underwent acute rotator cuff tear repair surgery, and were allowed to move freely in their cages after surgery. The sport group underwent acute rotator cuff tear repair surgery. Before surgery, the sport group carried out a one-week acclimatization training to establish an exercise tolerance threshold of 20 min. After the surgery, the sport group started a running training program on the seventh day and then conducted aerobic exercise training five days a week for the following four weeks.

The sample size of 10 mice per group was chosen based on prior murine omics studies that successfully detected biologically meaningful differences with similar numbers of biological replicates [24]. Histological analyses were performed on a randomly selected subset of 2–3 mice per group due to the qualitative nature of the assessment. No formal power calculation was performed, as this was an exploratory study.

Animal model construction

According to the previous protocol [25], C57BL/6 mice in the mod group and sport group underwent unilateral microsurgical procedures on the left shoulder. After anesthesia induction with tribromoethanol (AibeiBio, M2910, 0.2 mL/10 g) via intraperitoneal injection, the mice were shaved on the shoulder area, the skin was disinfected, and they were positioned in the right lateral recumbent position on a microsurgical table. The left shoulder skin was incised, and the deltoid muscle was dissected to expose the supraspinatus tendon (SST). A 5 − 0 suture was used to ligate and mark the SST, followed by securing the SST with a “figure of eight pattern” stitch using 5 − 0 sutures. Transverse bone tunnels were created anteriorly and posteriorly on the humeral head, through which the 5 − 0 sutures with tendons were passed and fixed. Finally, the SST at the marked site was transected, and the deltoid muscle and skin were sutured layer by layer. To minimize postoperative pain, bupivacaine (Merck, PHR1128) was administered for pain relief.

Treadmill exercise

The sport group mice were subjected to aerobic exercise training on a motorized treadmill (KW-PT; Kewbasis Co., Ltd., China). The treadmill apparatus featured four lanes, each equipped with an electric stimulus device at the terminal end. A mild electrical stimulus was delivered only when a mouse remained on the rear grid for > 2 s without resuming running, thereby avoiding continuous exposure. The use of electrical stimulation was limited to the early acclimatization period. During the formal exercise phase, gentle tapping served as the primary non-aversive motivation, and most animals ran voluntarily. A predefined compliance criterion was applied: animals that repeatedly received electrical stimulation within a session or persistently rested on the grid would be excluded; no animals met this criterion. All animals completed all scheduled exercise sessions without any interruptions or refusal to run.

Drawing from prior research [10], the mice in the sport group underwent a seven-day acclimatization regimen before surgical intervention. The treadmill was set at a speed of 10 m/min for 20 min daily. Surgery was only conducted once all mice in the sport group had developed the requisite tolerance for this activity. Postoperatively, on the seventh day, the mice in the sport group commenced active exercise, adhering to a specific protocol: they maintained a speed of 10 m/min on a horizontal treadmill for 20 min each day, five days per week, for four consecutive weeks. Throughout the intervention period, all mice in the Sport group completed every scheduled exercise session. No interruptions, refusals to run, or excessive use of the aversive stimulus were recorded, and no animals were excluded due to non-compliance with the exercise protocol. The exercise sessions for the sport group were consistently conducted during a fixed time window within the light phase of the day. To control for potential confounding effects of handling and environmental exposure, mice in the non-exercised groups (con and mod) were also transferred to the treadmill room and placed on a stationary treadmill for a duration matching the exercise protocol. The mice were euthanized 24 h following their final treadmill session, using gradual-fill CO₂ inhalation with a displacement rate of 30–70% of the chamber volume per minute, in accordance with AVMA guidelines for animal euthanasia.

Before euthanasia, blood samples were collected via retro-orbital bleeding under brief isoflurane anesthesia to obtain serum. Immediately after euthanasia, fecal material was aseptically collected from the colon. Subsequently, the SST complexes were collected for further experimental analyses. The fecal samples were analyzed to assess gut microbiome composition, serum samples were profiled to analyze metabolic components, and the SST complexes were subjected to a comprehensive evaluation of its microstructure and histological features.

Micro-computed tomography

After the mice were euthanized, the SST samples were fixed in 4% neutral buffered formalin for 24 h to ensure adequate fixation. They were then rinsed with 0.9% saline solution to remove any remaining formalin. The SST insertion specimens were scanned using the Hiscan XM Micro CT system (Suzhou Hiscan Information Technology Co., Ltd., China) to evaluate the microstructure of the newly generated bone at the tendon-bone interface. The samples were centered on the rotary table and scanned with a rotation step of 0.5°. The scanning parameters were set to 60 kV for beam energy, 134 µA for current, 500 ms for exposure time, and 10 μm for scanning resolution. Following the scans, the morphological parameters of the newly generated bone, such as bone volume fraction (BV/TV), trabecular thickness (Tb. Th), trabecular number (Tb. N), and trabecular separation (Tb.Sp) were carefully analyzed using Hiscan Analyzer software (Version 3.0, Suzhou Hiscan Information Technology Co., Ltd., China). All quantitative analyses were performed by an investigator who was blinded to the group allocation.

Histological evaluation

Following the micro-CT analysis, the SST samples were subjected to fixation, dehydration, and embedding. Subsequently, the specimens embedded in paraffin were sectioned with a thickness of 4 μm by microtome. After baking and dewaxing, the sections were rehydrated. Then, selected sections from each group were stained with either hematoxylin and eosin (H&E) or Safranin O/Fast Green (SO/FG) (Servicebio, G1053) to evaluate the repair characteristics of the SST insertion site. All histological images were captured under uniform illumination and settings using a light microscope. Histological sections were evaluated by an investigator who was blinded to the experimental group allocations.

Gut microbiota analysis

Fecal samples were collected from mice in groups of the con, mod, and sport for DNA extraction, purification, and amplification. The V3-V4 variable region of the 16S rRNA gene was amplified using the primers 341F (5’-CCTACGGNGGCWGCAG-3’) and 805R (5’-GACTACHVGGTATCTAATCC-3’). For each sample, specific barcodes were attached to the 5’ ends of the primers. The library was assembled using the products of amplified polymerase chain reaction (PCR). Following purification, the amplicons were quantified. After the amplification and enrichment processes, high-throughput sequencing was carried out using Miseq. The raw data obtained by Illumina sequencing were optimized by FLASH and fastp software. Subsequently, operational taxonomic units (OTUs) defined at a 97% similarity threshold were subjected to bioinformatics statistical analysis by default on the Usearch (version 11.0) software platform, thus obtaining a comprehensive OTU abundance table. To evaluate sample abundance, Alpha-diversity was assessed using the Shannon and Simpson indices and visualized as box plots in R. Moreover, indices such as Ace, Chao, and Sobs were analyzed through mothur (version 1.30.1) to evaluate the α diversity of the microbiota. Additionally, a Principal Coordinates Analysis (PCoA) diagram was drawn through R language to evaluate the β diversity among microbial communities. Also, a community composition bar chart was drawn using R language tools to analyze the composition of microbial communities. Then, a multi-group species difference test analysis was conducted through R’s stats package and Python’s scipy package. Referring to the Kyoto Encyclopedia of Genes and Genomes (KEGG) and the Clusters of Orthologous Groups (COG) database, the pathways and functional profiles of microbial communities were predicted by PICRUSt and Tax4Fun2.

Serum metabolomics analysis

Serum samples collected from the con, mod, and sport groups were processed as follows: 100 µL was added directly to 300 µL of pre-cooled methanol. After vortexing for 1 min and incubation at -20 °C for 2 h, the samples were centrifuged at 4,000 rpm for 20 min, and the supernatant was transferred to an autosampler vial for analysis using an LC-MS instrument (ThermoFisher, Q Exactive HF). The resulting MS and MS/MS spectral data were matched against various metabolic databases, including the HMDB (v 4.0) and Metlin. Metabolite annotation and data preprocessing were performed using Progenesis QI software (Waters Corporation, Milford, USA). Subsequently, orthogonal partial least-squares discriminant analysis (OPLS-DA) was performed using the R package ‘ropls’. This analysis was conducted to distinguish the differences in the overall metabolic profiles between the groups and to identify differential metabolites. In OPLS-DA analysis, variables with variable importance in projection (VIP) exceeding 1 were considered important. Permutation testing was used to prevent overfitting of the OPLS-DA model. The KEGG database was used to study the functions and metabolic pathways of these metabolites. Spearman’s rank correlation test was employed to analyze the correlation between genera and metabolites. Finally, the receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis method was applied to evaluate the predictive value of microbial genera and metabolites that exhibited significant correlations.

Statistics

Statistical analyses were performed using GraphPad Prism 9 and the R package ropls (version 1.6.2). Data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (SD). Micro-CT parameters were analyzed by one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey post hoc testing. For microbiome data, α-diversity indices and differential taxa were compared using the Kruskal–Wallis H test, and β-diversity was calculated based on distance matrices and visualized using ordination methods in R. For metabolomics, OPLS-DA was conducted to assess metabolic profile separation among groups. Correlation analysis and receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve analysis were also performed in R. To account for multiple testing in high-dimensional datasets, false discovery rate (FDR) correction was applied to differential microbiome analyses and metabolic pathway enrichment analysis. Given the exploratory nature of this study and the limited sample size, both unadjusted and FDR-adjusted results are reported. Findings based on unadjusted P values are interpreted as exploratory, whereas FDR-adjusted results are presented to control for multiple comparisons. A P-value < 0.05 was considered statistically significant.

Results

Postoperative exercise improves bone-tendon healing during rotator cuff repair in mice

To assess the effects of postoperative exercise on tendon–bone healing, micro-CT and histological analyses were conducted. Figure 1B displays representative 3D reconstruction images of the tendon enthesis, illustrating the microstructure at the tendon healing site. The unoperated intact SST enthesis was used as a reference to assess healing in all groups (Fig. 1C). Compared with the control enthesis, significant bone loss was observed after surgery, as evidenced by the decrease in Tb.Th (mean difference = -0.0092, 95% CI [-0.0109, -0.0075], P < 0.0001) and BV/TV (mean difference = -0.078, 95% CI [-0.101, -0.056], P < 0.001), and an increase of Tb.Sp (mean difference = 0.0218, 95% CI [0.0008, 0.0427], P < 0.05). When compared with the mod group, the sport group exhibited higher BV/TV (mean difference = 0.0336, 95% CI [0.0135, 0.0536], P < 0.01) and Tb.Th (mean difference = 0.0025, 95% CI [0.0011, 0.0040], P < 0.01), together with lower Tb.Sp (mean difference = − 0.0195, 95% CI [− 0.0383, − 0.0007], P < 0.05). However, Tb.N did not differ significantly between groups (P > 0.05). These structural differences suggest that postoperative exercise was associated with partial restoration of bone microarchitecture at the tendon–bone interface.

In addition, we analyzed the histomorphological features of the samples from a 2D perspective through H&E staining and SO&FG staining (Fig. 1D). H&E staining revealed that, in comparison with the con group, the mod group exhibited fibrocartilage formation at the tendon-bone junction. Additionally, noticeable gaps were identified at the tendon-bone interface, coupled with the presence of inflammatory cell infiltration. However, such gaps were less evident in the sport group. Moreover, SO&FG staining was employed to assess proteoglycan deposition. The proteoglycan in the sport group was higher than in the mod group. Together, these findings demonstrate that postoperative exercise was associated with improved fibrocartilage formation and reduced interface discontinuity in this murine model.

Postoperative exercise alters the gut microbiome in mice with rotator cuff tears

To explore the impact of postoperative exercise on gut microbiota after repair, 16 S rRNA gene sequencing was performed on fecal samples collected four weeks after surgery. The rarefaction curves for the Shannon and Simpson α-diversity indices approached a plateau, indicating adequate sequencing depth (Fig. 2A, Fig. S1A). However, no significant differences were noticed in the ACE, Chao, and Sobs indices among the three groups (Fig. 2B, Fig. S1B-C). Additionally, the β-diversity was further examined. The PCoA results indicated a separation trend among groups, with PC1 and PC2 accounting for 17.05% and 14.54% of the variance, respectively (ANOSIM R = 0.40321, P = 0.001; Fig. 2C).

We further analyzed the composition of the gut microbiota in the three groups, both at the phylum level and the genus level. At the phylum level, the dominant bacteria were Firmicutes, Bacteroidota, and Patescibacteria (Fig. 2D). The Kruskal-Wallis H test demonstrated that compared with the con group, Proteobacteria abundance was significantly reduced in the mod group, and postoperative exercise partially reversed this reduction (P < 0.05) (Fig. 2E and F). Moreover, the analysis at the genus level indicated that Lachnospiraceae_NK4A136_group, norank_f__Muribaculaceae, Lactobacillus, unclassified_f__Lachnospiraceae, and Dubosiella were the dominant genera (Fig. 2G). Notably, Dubosiella abundance was significantly decreased in the mod group compared with the con group (P = 0.0069), while postoperative exercise effectively reversed this decline, suggesting a potential association with rotator cuff injury and exercise (Fig. 2H and I). In contrast, the relative abundances of Allobaculum (P = 0.0008) and Lachnoclostridium (P = 0.0301) were markedly increased in the mod group compared with the con group, while significantly suppressed following postoperative exercise, indicating that these genera were correlated with injury status and were modulated by exercise (Fig. 2I). Interestingly, the abundance of Parabacteroides (P < 0.001) in the sport group was significantly higher compared to the mod group (Fig. 2I). Consistent with our findings, previous studies have shown that at the genus level, exercise can cause an increase in Parabacteroides compared to controls [26], and a 12-week HIIT intervention can enrich the abundance of Parabacteroides in individuals with celiac disease [27]. Additionally, Parabacteroides distasonis plays a significant role in promoting rotator cuff injury repair through intermittent fasting [28]. Postoperative exercise was associated with compositional shifts in the gut microbiota.

To further control for multiple comparisons, differential taxa were re-analyzed using the FDR method. No significantly altered taxa were identified at the phylum level after FDR correction. At the genus level, only four taxa (Allobaculum, Parabacteroides, norank_f__F082, and norank_f__Coriobacteriales_Incertae_Sedis) remained significantly different among groups (FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 2J). Because the number of taxa that passed FDR correction was limited, subsequent integrative and correlation analyses were performed based on taxa identified at P < 0.05. Therefore, the microbiome-related findings in the present study should be considered exploratory.

Postoperative exercise affects the functional pathway of gut microbes in a mouse model of rotator cuff tear

To predict the impact of postoperative exercise on the functional composition of the gut microbiota, PICRUSt analysis was performed on 16 S rRNA sequencing data, and KEGG pathways exhibiting significant differences were identified. Pathways with P < 0.05 were considered for exploratory screening (Fig. 3A–B). Among the pathways showing an exercise-associated reversal trend, the HIF-1 signaling pathway showed higher predicted abundance in controls, suppression after injury, and partial recovery with exercise, consistent with its role in regulating macrophage-mediated wound repair [29]. This pattern was also observed in Tax4Fun2 analysis (Fig. S2A), further supporting the association between exercise and HIF-1 pathway modulation. Additionally, sphingolipid metabolism was enriched in the mod group. Given that dysregulation of this pathway has been implicated in inflammatory and fibrotic processes in other contexts [30], its enrichment here raises the possibility that it may be linked to the degenerative environment of tendon injury.

As an exploratory extension, we also examined broader pathway trends (Fig. S3). β-Alanine metabolism displayed a similar recovery pattern; given its reported anti-inflammatory regulatory function [31], this suggests that microbiota-derived β-Alanine metabolism may contribute to exercise-related metabolic changes. In contrast, exercise suppressed IL-17 signaling, which was prominently enhanced after injury. IL-17 signaling is known to drive inflammation and tissue remodeling in rotator cuff pathology [32], and IL-17 A inhibition has been shown to alleviate tendon degeneration [33]. Overall, these results should be interpreted as exploratory, indicating that postoperative exercise is associated with partial restoration of microbiota-related pathways that may contribute to tendon repair, but causal relationships cannot be confirmed.

Analysis of key genus interactions in microbial communities

To elucidate the complex relationships among various species within the microbial community, we performed Spearman’s correlation analysis on the top 50 most abundant genera (Fig. 4). We then constructed a correlation network, displaying only correlations with a P-value below 0.05. Within the network, genera belonging to the phylum Firmicutes were particularly dominant, which aligns with previous research. Notably, Dubosiella, Lachnoclostridium, and Allobaculum, which have been of particular interest in prior studies, were detected within the network, suggesting their potential relevance in microbial interactions.

Postoperative exercise alters the composition of the serum metabolome of mice with rotator cuff tears

We performed metabolomic analysis on the serum samples from mice in the three groups. The OPLS-DA models were developed to distinguish metabolic patterns among sample groups, and their validity was assessed using 200 permutation tests. The permutation tests of the OPLS-DA model comparing the con group and the mod group indicated that the intercept of R2 was 0.0962 and the intercept of Q2 was 0.0064, suggesting a separation in serum metabolic profiles between the two groups (Fig. 5A, B). The differences between the con group and the sport group were significant, with R2 and Q2 intercept values of 0.9443 and − 0.1413, respectively (Fig. 5C, D), indicating marked differences in the serum metabolome between these two groups. The metabolic profile of the mod group and the sport group was significantly varied, as evidenced by the intercept of R2 being 0.9585 and the Q2 being − 0.1536 (Fig. 5E, F). The two groups were distinguishable, indicating that postoperative exercise was associated with marked alterations in the serum metabolome.

Next, differential metabolites were screened based on the criteria of VIP > 1 and P < 0.05. We found that there were 65 upregulated and 152 downregulated metabolites in the mod group relative to the con group (Fig. 6A), such as DG(18:0/18:2(9Z,12Z)/0:0) had a higher abundance in the mod group compared to the con group (Fig. 6B). In the sport group compared to the con group, there were 189 upregulated and 115 downregulated metabolites (Fig. 6C). Among them, Gentianine and Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg had lower abundance in the sport group, but Valine-betaxanthin had a higher abundance (Fig. 6D). Additionally, in the comparison between the sport group and the mod group, we found 126 upregulated and 235 downregulated metabolites in the sport group compared with the mod group (Fig. 6E). Notably, metabolites such as 2,5-Dimethylbenzenesulfonic acid were more abundant in the sport group, while Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg showed lower abundance in the sport group compared to the mod group (Fig. 6F). Studies have demonstrated that Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg can inhibit angiogenesis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells and adipocytes [34]; consistent with this, the observed reduction of Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg in the sport group may be relevant to the wound healing process, as angiogenesis is a critical component of tissue repair and regeneration [35]. Consistent with the exploratory nature of this study, the above metabolomic findings (based on nominal P < 0.05 and VIP > 1, without FDR correction) should be interpreted as exploratory and require validation.

Postoperative exercise alters the KEGG pathway in mice with rotator cuff tears based on altered metabolites

To identify the metabolic pathways potentially involved in the biological differences among groups, we conducted pathway enrichment analysis using the KEGG database. The significantly enriched pathways in the mod compared with the con group included D-amino acid metabolism, glycerophospholipid metabolism, phenylalanine, tyrosine and tryptophan biosynthesis, and retrograde endocannabinoid signaling (FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 7A). In the comparison between the sport and con, pathways such as neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, D-amino acid metabolism, retrograde endocannabinoid signaling, thyroid hormone synthesis, lysine degradation, and glycerophospholipid metabolism exhibited significant differences (FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 7B). When comparing sport versus mod, the key metabolic pathways include retrograde endocannabinoid signaling, serotonergic synapse, gap junction, and neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction, all of which were significantly different (FDR < 0.05) (Fig. 7C). The observed enrichment of the gap junction pathway in the Sport vs. Mod comparison is particularly noteworthy, as gap junctions are essential for angiogenesis during wound healing [36, 37].

Correlation between microbiota and metabolome

To identify gut microbes and metabolites potentially associated with the effects of postoperative exercise, KEGG enrichment analysis was performed on differential metabolites from both comparison directions. In the exercise-suppressed metabolite set, 11 overlapping metabolites were identified, but none were enriched in any pathways. In contrast, the exercise-enhanced metabolite set contained 26 overlapping metabolites, which were enriched in four KEGG pathways. Cross-referencing these four pathways with the 138 microbiota-associated pathways from the same comparison revealed β-Alanine metabolism as the sole shared pathway, with β-alanyl-L-arginine as the associated metabolite. The microbes most closely linked to this pathway included norank_f__norank_o__Rhodospirillales, Acinetobacter, Candidatus_Saccharimonas, and norank_f__Lachnospiraceae (Fig. 8A).

Spearman correlation analyses were performed to examine the association between gut microbiota and serum metabolome. Focusing on the differential metabolites and microbial genera between the mod and sport groups, the analysis uncovered several significantly correlated microbial genera and metabolites (Fig. 8B). Notably, a strong positive correlation was detected between Odoribacter and Serotonin (r = 0.6308, P < 0.001). Additionally, Parabacteroides exhibited significant positive correlations with Thymol Sulfate (r = 0.6686, P < 0.001) and Cyclohepty (r = 0.5865, P < 0.001). Conversely, a significantly negative correlation was also found between Parabacteroides and (23 S)-23,25-dihydroxy-24-oxovitamine D3 23-(beta-glucuronide) (r = -0.6896, P < 0.001). Research indicates that species within the genus Parabacteroides, including Parabacteroides distasonis, can mitigate non-alcoholic steatohepatitis (NASH) via metabolites such as pentadecanoic acid [38]. Additionally, these species produce succinic acid and secondary bile acids, which help to alleviate obesity and metabolic disorders [39]. Additionally, Akkermansia and Dubosiella were significantly negatively correlated with SM(d19:0/20:3(8Z,11Z,14Z)-2OH(5,6)) (r = -0.6073, P < 0.001; r = -0.5014, P = 0.005). Similarly, Faecalibaculum was significantly negatively correlated with DG(18:0/20:4(8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/0:0) (r = -0.6202, P < 0.001); and Eubacterium_siraeum_group and Roseburia were significantly negatively correlated with 10,11-dihydro-20-trihydroxy-leukotriene B5 (r = -0.5963, P < 0.001; r = -0.5769, P < 0.001). Furthermore, Allobaculum was negatively correlated with PS(22:5(7Z,10Z,13Z,16Z,19Z)/PGF2alpha) (r = -0.4770, P = 0.008). These correlations highlight the intricate associations between microbial genera and metabolites within the gut microbiota. These correlations were calculated without multiple-comparison correction, and the sample size was limited; thus, the findings are exploratory and descriptive, highlighting potential associations rather than causal relationships.

To explore whether these microorganisms and metabolites show associations with the recovery of rotator cuff repair, we conducted ROC analysis [Area Under Curve (AUC) > 0.7] on the microorganisms and metabolites with significant correlations. The analysis comparing the mod group and the sport group revealed that three specific microorganisms, namely Parabacteroides (AUC = 0.71), Faecalibaculum (AUC = 0.845), and Eubacterium_siraeum_group (AUC = 0.765), along with the metabolite DG (18:0/20:4 (8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/0:0) (AUC = 0.84), exhibited strong diagnostic potential for rotator cuff tear (Fig. 8C, F). Furthermore, the microorganism Parabacteroides (AUC = 0.88) and four metabolites- Thymol Sulfate (AUC = 1), Cycloheptyl (AUC = 1), DG (18:0/20:4 (8Z,11Z,14Z,17Z)/0:0) (AUC = 0.84), and SM (d19:0/20:3 (8Z,11Z,14Z)−2OH (5,6)) (AUC = 0.86) showed discriminatory potential between the sport and mod groups (Fig. 8E, F). These factors may be relevant to understanding the biological changes associated with rotator cuff injuries following postoperative exercise. However, the ROC analyses were conducted on the same dataset used for feature selection, without independent validation, so predictive performance may be overestimated. Both sets of results should therefore be interpreted cautiously as exploratory observations rather than definitive evidence of causality or diagnostic utility.

Discussion

Rotator cuff tear represents the most prevalent degenerative disorder of the shoulder joint. It exerts a severe influence on patients’ quality of life and imposes a substantial strain on the healthcare system [40]. Several studies have indicated that postoperative exercise following rotator cuff tear surgery facilitates healing [41]. Nevertheless, it remains uncertain whether it modulates the healing of rotator cuff tears via the microbiome and metabolome. In this study, we examined the gut microbiota and serum metabolome in mice with rotator cuff tears after undergoing postoperative exercise. We found that postoperative exercise was associated with changes in the gut microbiota and serum metabolome, and these changes were accompanied by improved tendon-bone healing in this murine model. Moreover, the microorganisms and metabolites possessing significant predictive value in this murine model of rotator cuff tears were also identified.

Several studies have suggested that exercise after rotator cuff repair surgery can facilitate healing. Zhou et al. reported that high-intensity interval training following surgery enhances the quality and function of the supraspinatus muscle after rotator cuff tear [41]. Zhang et al. have discovered that postoperative exercise can expedite the early healing of rotator cuff injuries by increasing the expression of peripheral neuropeptides [10]. The findings of our study are in harmony with those of previous investigations and further corroborate the advantageous effect of postoperative exercise on the healing of rotator cuff injuries in a murine model. A multitude of studies have demonstrated that exercise can modulate both the abundance and the function of gut microbiota [11], and it can also exert an influence on the progression of diverse human diseases. This includes alleviating the inflammatory signaling pathway [42] and regulating the brain-gut axis to relieve major depressive disorder [43]. Our study revealed that postoperative exercise was associated with alterations in the gut microbiota composition of mice with rotator cuff tears. Specifically, postoperative exercise was associated with increased Dubosiella and reduced Lachnoclostridium abundance in rotator cuff tear mice. Dubosiella has been associated with short-chain fatty acid (SCFA) production, particularly butyrate, which supports anti-inflammatory and barrier functions [44]. Lachnoclostridium showed the highest abundance in the rotator cuff tear model group and has been reported to increase in various inflammatory and metabolic diseases, including serving as a biomarker for colorectal adenoma and carcinoma [45]. It can deplete the anti-inflammatory metabolite indole-3-propionic acid and promote intestinal inflammation [46, 47].

Furthermore, Parabacteroides is a key microorganism that is widely present in the human gut and plays a significant role in a variety of diseases, and it is considered a strong candidate for the next generation of probiotics [48]. Parabacteroides can function through multiple mechanisms. On one hand, Parabacteroides can regulate host immune system and reduce inflammation. For example, Parabacteroides distasonis can significantly reduce the levels of IL-8 and IL-6 in vitro as well as the levels of IL-1β and TNF-α in the blood of mice [49–51]. It can also relieve liver inflammation through the inhibition of the NF-κB/MAPK signaling pathway and exert its anti-inflammatory properties by restraining the NF-κB and PI3K/Akt/mTOR pathways [52, 53]. Studies have also shown that it can relieve inflammatory arthritis [48]. Based on this, we speculate that Parabacteroides may be associated with the healing of rotator cuff tear wounds in mice by reducing the host’s inflammation level. On the other hand, Parabacteroides can regulate the host’s metabolic processes and secrete metabolites to affect the host’s health. Some species of Parabacteroides can produce short-chain fatty acids, which confer a range of benefits to the host [54]. In addition, Parabacteroides distasonis may improve liver fibrosis by regulating intestinal bile acid metabolism in male mice [55]. In this study, postoperative exercise markedly elevated the abundance of Parabacteroides, which exhibited a strong positive correlation with Thymol Sulfate and Cyclohepty, while displaying a significant negative correlation with (23 S)-23,25-dihydroxy-24-oxovitamine D3 23-(beta-glucuronide). Based on this, it is further speculated that Parabacteroides may be linked to the recovery of rotator cuff tears in this murine model, potentially through its associations with inflammation-related metabolites such as Thymol Sulfate.

Furthermore, pathway analysis showed that the HIF-1 signaling pathway was markedly suppressed after rotator cuff tear and partially restored by postoperative exercise. The HIF-1 pathway promotes tendon-bone healing by enhancing angiogenesis and osteogenic activity at the enthesis [56]. Macrophage-mediated angiogenesis through HIF-1 supports material-induced osteogenesis [57], and upregulation of HIF-1α can activate BMP4/SMAD signaling to increase osteogenic factor secretion [58]. Moreover, inhibition of HIF-1α attenuates tendinopathy by suppressing NF-κB and MAPK pathways [59], highlighting its dual role in coordinating inflammation and regeneration. Thus, in the murine model, exercise-mediated reactivation of HIF-1 signaling may be relevant to vascular remodeling and new bone formation during tendon-bone repair. Additionally, β-Alanine metabolism was similarly reduced after rotator cuff tear and restored by exercise. This pathway served as the key intersection between exercise-responsive gut microbes and serum metabolites. β-Alanine is involved in cellular energy regulation and resistance to oxidative stress [60], and is widely recognized as an exercise-enhancing supplement that improves skeletal muscle performance [61]. Recent evidence also suggests that β-Alanine metabolism exerts anti-inflammatory effects [31]. In the context of the present study, these properties raise the possibility that exercise-associated changes in β-Alanine metabolism may influence the local inflammatory microenvironment during tendon healing.

In the present murine model, exercise was associated with alterations in gut microbiota and metabolites that may be relevant to rotator cuff repair. These findings provide mechanistic insights into how exercise may influence healing through microbiota and metabolite modulation in a preclinical setting. Through non-targeted metabolomics, we have uncovered the differential metabolites that may be relevant to the recovery of rotator cuff injury in this murine model. Our results showed that the level of Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg was notably reduced in the sport group compared to the mod. Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg not only can selectively suppress fat intake but also can inhibit angiogenesis in human umbilical vein endothelial cells and adipocytes [34]. Consequently, postoperative exercise was associated with decreased Val-Pro-Asp-Pro-Arg levels in the serum of the mice in the rotator cuff tear model, suggesting a potential link between exercise, angiogenesis-related metabolites, and healing outcomes. Furthermore, concerning the signaling pathways influenced by exercise, our study demonstrated that, compared to the rotator cuff tear model group, exercise in this murine model was associated with enhanced abundance of the Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction pathway. Previous studies have revealed that exercise can result in derived extracellular vesicles strengthening the expression of genes related to the Neuroactive ligand-receptor interaction [62]. These findings suggest a potential link between exercise-induced changes in specific metabolites and signaling pathways and the repair process of rotator cuff tears in this preclinical model. This observation points toward testable hypotheses and provides a foundation for future investigations into novel treatment strategies.

Nevertheless, this study indeed has certain limitations. Firstly, the sample size of mice employed in our experiment was relatively small, which limits statistical power and increases the risk of false negatives, especially for high-dimensional omics data. Findings from these exploratory analyses should be interpreted with caution and considered hypothesis-generating. A bigger sample size would offer more solid evidence and enhance the generalizability of the findings. Secondly, this study was conducted exclusively in male mice. While this design helps control for the potential confounding effects of the estrous cycle and sex hormones, it also limits the extrapolation of our findings to female populations. Third, findings from murine models cannot be directly extrapolated to humans due to species-specific differences and therefore require validation in clinical cohorts. In addition, the specific mechanism by which exercise promotes the recovery of rotator cuff tears still requires further exploration.

Conclusion

In summary, in a murine model of rotator cuff tear, postoperative exercise was associated with reduced bone loss and enhanced new bone formation at the tendon-bone insertion site following rotator cuff tear surgery. Exercise was also associated with increased abundance of Parabacteroides and alterations in related metabolites, with Parabacteroides correlating with key metabolites and showing potential predictive value for healing outcomes. These findings provide preclinical evidence supporting the integration of postoperative exercise into rotator cuff repair strategies and offer mechanistic insights into the gut-microbiota-metabolite axis in tendon-bone healing.
