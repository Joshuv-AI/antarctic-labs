On the Howe–Moore property for automorphism groups of buildings
Andreas Thom

Abstract
. Let G < c Aut ( X ) G<_{c}\operatorname{Aut}(X) be a totally disconnected locally compact group acting strongly transitively on a locally finite building X X of finite-rank and minimal non-spherical type. For sufficiently large thickness, every weakly mixing strongly continuous unitary representation of G G is C 0 C_{0} . Consequently, if G G has no non-trivial finite-dimensional unitary representations, then G G has the Howe–Moore property. More concretely, this applies to rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic types of thickness q + 1 q+1 for q ≥ 19379 q\geq 19379 , if there are no compact quotients. As an application, we prove that the corresponding Caprace–Rémy Kac–Moody lattices in these types, which are known to be finitely presented simple and Kazhdan, are character-rigid: their extremal characters are only the regular and the trivial character. Consequently they also have no non-trivial invariant random subgroups.



1.
Introduction and main results

A locally compact group
G
has the Howe–Moore property if, for every
strongly continuous unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})

on a Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
without non-zero
G
-fixed vectors, the
matrix coefficient
g\mapsto\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle
vanishes at
infinity for all
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
.

The Howe–Moore property was first proved by Howe and Moore

[
23
]
and Zimmer
[
41
]
for connected
non-compact simple real Lie groups with finite center, and it has since
become a basic tool in rigidity theory. Howe and Moore also treated
isotropic simple algebraic groups over non-Archimedean local fields; via
Bruhat–Tits theory
[
6
]
, these groups act strongly
transitively on locally finite Euclidean buildings. For totally
disconnected groups beyond the algebraic case, the study of the
Howe–Moore property was initiated by Lubotzky–Mozes

[
29
]
for automorphism groups of regular trees, and was
developed further by Burger–Mozes
[
7
]
for
topologically simple groups acting 2-transitively on the boundary of a
locally finite tree. Ciobotaru later gave a unified proof covering the
classical real and non-Archimedean cases, as well as these tree
automorphism groups
[
12
]
.

Recall, a unitary representation is called

weakly mixing
if it has no non-zero finite-dimensional invariant
subspace, and it is called
mixing
or
C_{0}
if all its matrix coefficients vanish at infinity. We study the following property:

\mbox{weak mixing}\qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad C_{0}

for unitary representations of a group. This is formally weaker than the Howe-Moore property, but it is equivalent if the group has no non-trivial finite-dimensional unitary representations. In particular, it is equivalent for topologically simple groups.

We recall briefly the terminology; standard references for buildings are

[
1
,
38
]
.
A building comes with a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, where the group
W

is generated by the set
S
of simple reflections subject to the Coxeter
relations. This Coxeter system is called the type of the building, and its

rank
is the cardinality of
S
. In particular, each
panel
, that is, each
codimension-one face of a chamber, has a type
s\in S
. Throughout the
paper, automorphisms are assumed to be type-preserving.

The standard parabolic subgroups of
W
are the
subgroups
W_{I}=\langle I\rangle
, for
I\subset S
. Such a parabolic
subgroup, or the subset
I
, is called
spherical
if
W_{I}
is finite.
The system
(W,S)
is called
2-spherical
if
W_{I}
is spherical for all
I\subset S
with
\|I\|=2
.

Let
G<_{c}\operatorname{Aut}(X)
be a totally disconnected locally compact
group acting strongly transitively on a locally finite building
X
of
finite-rank.
After choosing a base chamber, the strongly transitive action gives a
BN-pair for
G
, with
B
the chamber stabilizer,
N
the normalizer of the chosen apartment, and
W=N/(B\cap N)
the Weyl group. Thus, for every subset
J\subset S
, there is a
corresponding standard parabolic subgroup of
G
:

P_{J}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{J}}B\dot{w}B,\qquad W_{J}=\langle J\rangle,

where
B
is the stabilizer of the base chamber and
\dot{w}
denotes a
representative of
w
in the normalizer of the chosen apartment. Equivalently,

P_{J}
is the stabilizer of the standard residue of type
J
containing
the base chamber. Its conjugates are the parabolic subgroups of type
J
.

We say that a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, or a building of that type, is

minimal non-spherical
if
W
is infinite, while
W_{I}
is finite for every
proper subset
I\subsetneq S
. These Coxeter systems are completely
classified, see Remark
3.2
, and
include the irreducible affine and the compact hyperbolic systems. All such Coxeter systems are 2-spherical apart from the dihedral group, which corresponds to the case of actions on bi-regular trees. The
easiest case is the rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic case, corresponding to triangle groups
W=\langle s,t,u\mid s^{2}=t^{2}=u^{2}=(st)^{m_{st}}=(tu)^{m_{tu}}=(us)^{m_{us}}=1\rangle
with
m_{st},m_{tu},m_{us}\in\{2,3,4,6\}
and
1/m_{st}+1/m_{tu}+1/m_{us}<1
,
which will be discussed in this paper as a running example.

The restriction to the case of minimal non-spherical Coxeter systems is necessary for our arguments. Indeed, suppose
(W,S)
is not minimal non-spherical and that

J\subsetneq S
is of non-spherical type. Consider the quasi-regular representation of
G
on

\ell^{2}(G/P_{J})
. If
G/P_{J}
is infinite, this representation is weakly mixing. However, the vector
\delta_{P_{J}}
is fixed
by
P_{J}
, and

\langle g\delta_{P_{J}},\delta_{P_{J}}\rangle=1

for every
g\in P_{J}
. Hence, this matrix coefficient does not vanish at infinity inside
P_{J}
and one cannot expect the classical
Howe–Moore property. Our main result says that for sufficiently large thickness, this is the only obstruction to the Howe–Moore property.

The
thickness
of a panel is the number of chambers
containing it. In the locally finite strongly transitive situations considered
below, these thicknesses are finite and depend only on the panel type.
The Coxeter type and the thickness parameters describe the local combinatorics relevant for our estimates, although buildings with the same type and the same thickness data need not be isomorphic nor are arbitrary choices of parameters necessarily realizable by a building.

Our main result is the following theorem:

Theorem A
.

Let
G<_{c}\operatorname{Aut}(X)
be a totally disconnected locally compact group acting
strongly transitively on a locally finite building
X
of finite-rank and minimal non-spherical type
(W,S)
. Suppose that the
thickness is large enough, depending only on
(W,S)
. Then every weakly
mixing strongly continuous unitary representation of
G
is
C_{0}
.

Corollary B
.

In the setting of Theorem
A
, suppose in addition that
G
has no compact quotients. Then
G
has the Howe–Moore property.

Corollary C
.

Let
q\geq 19379
be a prime power. Let
G<_{c}\operatorname{Aut}(X)
be a
non-discrete, non-compact, totally disconnected locally compact group acting
strongly transitively on a locally finite building
X
of rank-three
compact-hyperbolic crystallographic type
(W,S)
, and suppose that every
panel has thickness
q+1
. If every compact quotient of
G
is trivial,
then
G
has the Howe–Moore property.

Proof.

In a rank-three compact-hyperbolic Coxeter system,
W
is infinite and
every proper standard parabolic subgroup has rank at most two and is
spherical. Thus the type is minimal non-spherical. Lemma
4.8

shows that the required thickness bound is satisfied for
q\geq 19379
.
Every finite-dimensional unitary representation of
G
has compact image by Caprace–Monod
[
11
, Corollary 3.1]
.
Since every compact quotient of
G
is trivial by assumption, Corollary
B
applies.
∎

The rank-two case of Theorem
A
is covered by work of Burger–Mozes
[
7
]
and Ciobotaru
[
12
]
. In rank above two, we are in the 2-spherical situation, and the proof starts with local spectral estimates for the rank-two residues; following the work of Dymara–Januszkiewicz
[
17
]
and Kassabov
[
26
]
.
These estimates give quantitative Kazhdan constants for the rank-one
parahorics generating the ambient group.
This is then turned into decay of chamber-spherical matrix coefficients by
comparing the generators of the chamber Hecke algebra with the corresponding
orthogonal projections onto parahoric-invariant subspaces. A finite-depth
argument upgrades this chamber-spherical decay to decay for vectors fixed
by arbitrary compact open subgroups. Finally, density of compact-open fixed
vectors yields decay of all matrix coefficients.

We now spell out the application to Caprace–Rémy lattices. Let

\Lambda
be a split Kac–Moody group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with associated
twin building
\Delta=\Delta_{+}\sqcup\Delta_{-}
. Let
L_{\pm}
be the closure
of the image of
\Lambda
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{\pm})
, equipped
with the permutation topology. These are the building completions of

\Lambda
. In the rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic case
considered below, Caprace–Rémy
[
10
]
prove that the group

\Gamma=\Lambda^{\dagger}/Z(\Lambda^{\dagger})
, where
\Lambda^{\dagger}
is
generated by the root groups, is a finitely presented, infinite, simple
Kazhdan group and embeds irreducibly as a lattice in
L_{+}\times L_{-}
. They
also show that the completions
L_{+}
and
L_{-}
are second countable,
non-discrete, non-compact, topologically simple, totally disconnected
locally compact groups.

Recall that a character of a countable group is a conjugation-invariant
positive definite function normalized by
\chi(e)=1
; it is extremal if
it is an extreme point of the convex set of characters. As an application of results of Creutz-Peterson
[
13
,
14
]
, we obtain the following character rigidity result:

Theorem D
.

Let
q\geq 19379
be a prime power, and let
\Lambda
be a split
Kac–Moody group over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
whose Weyl group is of rank-three
compact-hyperbolic crystallographic type. Let
\Lambda^{\dagger}
be the
subgroup generated by the root groups and put

\Gamma=\Lambda^{\dagger}/Z(\Lambda^{\dagger})
. Then every extremal
character of
\Gamma
is either the regular character
\delta_{e}
or the
trivial character
1_{\Gamma}
.

An invariant random subgroup of a countable group
\Gamma
is a conjugation-invariant Borel probability measure on the space of subgroups of
\Gamma
. We obtain the following corollary:

Corollary E
.

In the setting of Theorem
D
,
\Gamma
has no non-trivial invariant random
subgroups.

Let us put this result in context. Previously known
examples of countably infinite groups without non-trivial invariant random subgroups
come from rather different sources and do not provide finitely presented
examples with property (T); see Gelander
[
21
]
for background. For instance, character-rigidity results of Kirillov

[
27
]
and Peterson and the author
[
34
]
imply that

\mathrm{PSL}_{n}(k)
,
n\geq 2
, has no non-trivial invariant random
subgroups for every infinite field
k
; in particular this applies to

\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathbb{Q})
. These groups are not finitely generated.

Another class is provided by Tarski
monsters, whose existence was proved by Ol’shanskii

[
32
]
. These are finitely generated infinite simple groups
all of whose proper non-trivial subgroups have prime order. In particular, there are only countably many such subgroups, a fact that easily implies that they
have no non-trivial invariant random subgroups. However, the classical
Tarski monsters are not finitely presented; indeed, the existence of
finitely presented Tarski monsters is one of the standard open
problems in the field, see Ol’shanskii–Sapir
[
33
]
.

Yet another source of examples is provided by the work of Dudko–Medynets
on finite factor representations of Higman–Thompson groups

[
16
]
. Their results imply, in particular, that the
commutator subgroups of a class of Higman–Thompson groups are finitely presented and
have no non-trivial invariant random subgroups. These groups have the Haagerup property by results of Hughes
[
24
]
and thus cannot have Kazhdan’s property (T).

Thus, to the best of our knowledge,
Corollary
E
provides the first examples of finitely presented infinite Kazhdan groups without
non-trivial invariant random subgroups. The search for such groups motivated this study and is of particular interest in the study of sofic approximations, see
[
2
]
.

2.
Local spectral estimates and Kazhdan constants

We fix the following notation. Let
G
be a totally disconnected locally
compact group acting strongly transitively and type-preservingly on a
locally finite building
\Delta
of type
(W,S)
. Let
C_{0}
be a base
chamber and let

B=\operatorname{Stab}_{G}(C_{0})

be its compact open stabilizer. The associated BN-pair gives the Bruhat
decomposition

G=\bigsqcup_{w\in W}B\dot{w}B,

where
\dot{w}
denotes a representative of
w
in the normalizer of the
chosen apartment. For
s\in S
, we write

P_{s}=B\cup B\dot{s}B

for the rank-one parahoric subgroup. More generally, for
J\subset S
, we
write

P_{J}=\bigcup_{w\in W_{J}}B\dot{w}B,\qquad W_{J}=\langle J\rangle,

for the corresponding standard parabolic subgroup.

For
r\geq 0
, let

B_{r}=\operatorname{Fix}_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{r}(C_{0}))

be the pointwise stabilizer of the radius-
r
ball around
C_{0}
. Thus

B_{0}=B
, and every
B_{r}
is compact open.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
G
be a group with a BN-pair of type
(W,S)
, chamber stabilizer

B
, and rank-one parahorics

P_{s}=B\cup B\dot{s}B,
for
s\in S.

Then
G=\langle P_{s}:s\in S\rangle
.

Proof.

The BN-pair gives

G=\bigcup_{w\in W}B\dot{w}B.

Since
W
is generated by the simple reflections, every representative

\dot{w}
can be written as a product of representatives
\dot{s}
,

s\in S
. But
B\subset P_{s}
and
\dot{s}\in P_{s}
. Hence every double
coset
B\dot{w}B
is contained in the subgroup generated by the
P_{s}
.
∎

The results in this section are based on Garland’s local spectral method
and, more specifically, on the fixed-subspace angle method of
Dymara–Januszkiewicz
[
17
]
. Their arguments already
give thickness-dependent Kazhdan constant bounds for groups acting strongly
transitively on locally finite
2
-spherical buildings. We use the
codistance and subspace-arrangement language of Kassabov
[
26
]

to state the estimates in the normalization needed below.

We begin by fixing the normalization of codistance. For a compact subgroup

M<G
and a unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
,
write
E_{M}^{\pi}
for the orthogonal projection onto
\mathcal{H}^{M}
. For
compact subgroups
M,N<G
, the codistance is

\codist(M,N)=\sup_{\pi}\left\|E_{M}^{\pi}E_{N}^{\pi}-E_{\langle M,N\rangle}^{\pi}\right\|.

The supremum is over all unitary representations.

The rank-two codistance estimate below is the fixed-space angle formulation
of Garland’s local spectral calculation for rank-two residues. Equivalently,
it follows from the spectrum of incidence graphs of finite generalized
polygons, as recorded in
[
5
, Chapter 6]
. In the
equal-thickness crystallographic case this is Garland’s Hecke-algebraic
calculation
[
20
, Proposition 7.10]
; see also

[
22
, Section 4.3]
and
[
17
]
. We use

[
26
, Section 2]
for the Friedrichs-angle formulation behind the
codistance estimate.

For
m\geq 2
and
a,b>0
, set

\delta_{m}(a,b)=\begin{cases}0,&m=2,\\[5.69054pt]
\displaystyle\left(\frac{a+b+2\sqrt{ab}\cos(2\pi/m)}{(a+1)(b+1)}\right)^{1/2},&m\geq 3.\end{cases}

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
s\neq t\in S
, and assume that
m_{st}<\infty
. Let
R_{st}

be the rank-two residue of type
I_{2}(m_{st})
through the base chamber.
Let
q_{s}+1
, respectively
q_{t}+1
, be the number of chambers in an

s
-panel, respectively a
t
-panel, of
R_{st}
. Let
P_{s}
and

P_{t}
be the corresponding rank-one parahoric subgroups. Then

\codist(P_{s},P_{t})\leq\delta_{m_{st}}(q_{s},q_{t}).

Proof.

Let
G_{st}=\langle P_{s},P_{t}\rangle
. The quotient of
G_{st}
by the
kernel of its action on
R_{st}
acts strongly transitively on the finite
rank-two building
R_{st}
. The two parahorics
P_{s}
and
P_{t}
are
the stabilizers of the two panels of the base chamber in this rank-two
building.

Let
\overline{G}_{st}
be this finite quotient, and let

\overline{P}_{s},\overline{P}_{t}
be the images of
P_{s},P_{t}
. The kernel of

G_{st}\to\overline{G}_{st}
fixes the residue
R_{st}
pointwise; in
particular it is a normal subgroup contained in
P_{s}\cap P_{t}
.

Let
X_{s}
and
X_{t}
be the two types of panels in
R_{st}
. The
incidence graph between
X_{s}
and
X_{t}
is the incidence graph of the
finite generalized
m_{st}
-gon of order
(q_{s},q_{t})
. By the spectrum
of finite generalized polygons, see

[
5
, Chapter 6]
, the normalized incidence operator

A_{st}\colon\ell^{2}(X_{t})\to\ell^{2}(X_{s})

has largest non-trivial singular value

\delta_{m_{st}}(q_{s},q_{t}).

We recall how this spectral estimate gives the fixed-space angle. By the
fixed-space angle formulation of Kassabov,
[
26
, Section 2]
,

\|E_{P_{s}}^{\pi}E_{P_{t}}^{\pi}-E_{G_{st}}^{\pi}\|

is the cosine of the Friedrichs angle between
\mathcal{H}^{P_{s}}
and

\mathcal{H}^{P_{t}}
, uniformly over unitary representations
\pi
.
Thus it is enough to bound
|\langle u,v\rangle|
for unit vectors

u\in\mathcal{H}^{P_{s}}\ominus\mathcal{H}^{G_{st}}
and
v\in\mathcal{H}^{P_{t}}\ominus\mathcal{H}^{G_{st}}.

The normal kernel of
G_{st}\to\overline{G}_{st}
fixes every such
u

and
v
, and hence the
G_{st}
-cyclic subspaces generated by them
factor through
\overline{G}_{st}
. We may therefore work with unitary
representations of the finite group
\overline{G}_{st}
.

For a finite group, every unitary representation is contained in a Hilbert
sum of copies of the regular representation. In the regular representation
of
\overline{G}_{st}
, the fixed spaces for
\overline{P}_{s}
and

\overline{P}_{t}
identify with

\ell^{2}(\overline{G}_{st}/\overline{P}_{s})
and
\ell^{2}(\overline{G}_{st}/\overline{P}_{t}),

hence with
\ell^{2}(X_{s})
and
\ell^{2}(X_{t})
. A coset of

\overline{P}_{s}
and a coset of
\overline{P}_{t}
meet precisely when the
corresponding two panels are incident in
R_{st}
. Under these
identifications,
E_{\overline{P}_{s}}E_{\overline{P}_{t}}
, after removing
the common
\overline{G}_{st}
-fixed line of constant functions, is exactly
the normalized incidence operator
A_{st}
on the non-trivial part.
Therefore

\left\|E_{P_{s}}^{\pi}E_{P_{t}}^{\pi}-E_{\langle P_{s},P_{t}\rangle}^{\pi}\right\|\leq\delta_{m_{st}}(q_{s},q_{t}).

Taking the supremum over all unitary representations
\pi
gives the
claim.
∎

We shall use the following quantitative form of the
projection criterion due to Dymara–Januszkiewicz and Kassabov. In the form needed
below, it is the equal-codistance specialization of Kassabov’s
subspace-arrangement estimate
[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
. The connection
with Kazhdan constants is the fixed-subspace criterion explained in

[
26
, Observation 2.1]
.

For Kac–Moody groups, related quantitative bounds were subsequently
improved and algebraized by Ershov–Jaikin-Zapirain

[
18
, Section 7]
and further developed by Ershov–Rall

[
19
]
.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
H
be generated by compact subgroups
P_{s}
,
s\in S_{0}
, where

|S_{0}|=n
. Suppose that
\codist(P_{s},P_{t})\leq\delta
for distinct

s,t\in S_{0}
, and that
(n-1)\delta<1
. Then

\Omega=\bigcup_{s\in S_{0}}P_{s}
is a
Kazhdan set for
H
. More precisely, every unitary representation

\pi:H\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
with
\mathcal{H}^{H}=\{0\}
satisfies

\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\pi(\omega)\xi-\xi\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\left(1-(n-1)\delta\right)}

for every unit vector
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
.
Thus
\Omega
has Kazhdan constant at least

\kappa_{\delta}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\left(1-(n-1)\delta\right)}.

Proof.

Let
V_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{P_{s}}
, and let
P_{V_{s}}
be the corresponding
orthogonal projection. By the definition of codistance,

\cos\angle(V_{s},V_{t})\leq\delta
for
s\neq t
, where the angle is the
Friedrichs angle, i.e. the operator norm of

P_{V_{s}}P_{V_{t}}-P_{V_{s}\cap V_{t}}
.

We apply the quantitative subspace-arrangement estimate of Kassabov

[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
. In the present equal-codistance situation,
the relevant comparison matrix is

A_{\delta}=\begin{pmatrix}1&-\delta&\cdots&-\delta\\
-\delta&1&\cdots&-\delta\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
-\delta&-\delta&\cdots&1\end{pmatrix}.

Its eigenvalues are
1-(n-1)\delta
on the line spanned by

(1,\ldots,1)
, and
1+\delta
on its orthogonal complement. Hence

A_{\delta}
is positive definite by
the assumption
(n-1)\delta<1
, and its smallest eigenvalue is

\lambda_{\delta}=1-(n-1)\delta
.

Kassabov’s estimate
[
26
, Theorem 5.1]
gives, for every

v\in\mathcal{H}
,

d\left(v,\bigcap_{s\in S_{0}}V_{s}\right)^{2}\leq d_{v}^{t}A_{\delta}^{-1}d_{v},

where
d_{v}
is the column vector with entries

d(v,V_{s})=\|(I-P_{V_{s}})v\|
.
Since the smallest eigenvalue of
A_{\delta}
is
\lambda_{\delta}
, we get

d\left(v,\bigcap_{s\in S_{0}}V_{s}\right)^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}}\sum_{s\in S_{0}}d(v,V_{s})^{2}.

Now let
\pi
be a unitary representation with
\mathcal{H}^{H}=\{0\}
,
and let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
be a unit vector. Put

\varepsilon=\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\pi(\omega)\xi-\xi\|
.
For each
s\in S_{0}
, let
Q_{s}
be the averaging projection onto

\mathcal{H}^{P_{s}}
. Since
P_{s}
is compact,

Q_{s}\xi=\int_{P_{s}}\pi(p)\xi\,dp
.
Writing
\xi=Q_{s}\xi+(\xi-Q_{s}\xi)
, and using the fact that

\xi-Q_{s}\xi\perp\mathcal{H}^{P_{s}}
, we have

\int_{P_{s}}\|\pi(p)\xi-\xi\|^{2}\,dp=2\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|^{2}.

Therefore
d(\xi,V_{s})=\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{\sqrt{2}}
.
Since the subgroups
P_{s}
generate
H
, one has

\bigcap_{s\in S_{0}}V_{s}=\mathcal{H}^{H}=\{0\}
. Hence

d\left(\xi,\bigcap_{s\in S_{0}}V_{s}\right)=\|\xi\|=1
.
The previous estimate gives

1\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}}\sum_{s\in S_{0}}d(\xi,V_{s})^{2}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda_{\delta}}\cdot n\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{2}.

Thus

\varepsilon^{2}\geq\frac{2\lambda_{\delta}}{n}=\frac{2}{n}\left(1-(n-1)\delta\right).

Therefore

\sup_{\omega\in\Omega}\|\pi(\omega)\xi-\xi\|\geq\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\left(1-(n-1)\delta\right)}.

This proves the claimed Kazhdan constant.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
S_{0}\subset S
be a finite subset with
|S_{0}|=n\geq 2
, and suppose
that
m_{st}<\infty
for all distinct
s,t\in S_{0}
. Let
q_{s}+1
be
the number of chambers in an
s
-panel. Put

\delta_{S_{0}}=\max_{s\neq t\in S_{0}}\delta_{m_{st}}(q_{s},q_{t}).

Let
H=\langle P_{s}:s\in S_{0}\rangle
and

\Omega_{S_{0}}=\bigcup_{s\in S_{0}}P_{s}
. If

(n-1)\delta_{S_{0}}<1
, then
\Omega_{S_{0}}
is a Kazhdan set for

H
, with Kazhdan constant at least

\kappa_{S_{0}}=\sqrt{\frac{2}{n}\left(1-(n-1)\delta_{S_{0}}\right)}.

Proof.

By Proposition
2.2
,

\codist(P_{s},P_{t})\leq\delta_{S_{0}}
for all distinct

s,t\in S_{0}
. The claim follows from
Proposition
2.3
.
∎

3.
A rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic case

In order to prove Corollary

, we follow the argument in detail in a specific case. Let

be a compact-hyperbolic crystallographic Coxeter system of

(m_{12},m_{13},m_{23})

and

\frac{1}{m_{12}}+\frac{1}{m_{13}}+\frac{1}{m_{23}}<1.

We have in mind the split Kac–Moody building over

of this

A_{1}\times A_{1},A_{2},B_{2}/C_{2},

-gons over

(q,q)
and every panel contains

[

for the construction of

[

for rank-two Moufang spherical buildings.

For the rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic case, we
obtain the following explicit lower bound for the Kazhdan constant from the results in the previous section.

Corollary 3.1
.

Assume that
|S|=3
, and that
(W,S)
is of compact-hyperbolic
crystallographic type. Put
\Omega=\bigcup_{s\in S}P_{s}
.
Assume

. Then

Here

, and
m_{st}\in\{2,3,4,6\}
for all distinct

. Since all panel thicknesses are

gives

∎

Remark 3.2
.

Let us recall the classification of minimal non-spherical Coxeter groups.
Let
(W,S)
be minimal non-spherical. Then
(W,S)
is irreducible.
Indeed, if
S=S_{1}\sqcup S_{2}
were a non-trivial decomposition into
orthogonal components, then the infinite factor among
W_{S_{1}}
and

W_{S_{2}}
would already be a proper non-spherical standard parabolic
subgroup.

The finite-rank classification is the following standard one. Let

A_{S}=(a_{st})_{s,t\in S}
be the Coxeter Gram matrix

a_{ss}=1,a_{st}=-\cos(\pi/m_{st}),

with the convention
a_{st}=-1
if
m_{st}=\infty
. The finite Coxeter
groups are exactly those for which this form is positive definite, and the
irreducible affine Coxeter groups are exactly the irreducible degenerate
positive semidefinite cases; see
[
25
, Sections 6.4–6.9]

and
[
4
, Ch. V, §§ 4–6]
. Since every proper standard
parabolic subgroup of
W_{S}
is finite, every proper principal submatrix
of
A_{S}
is positive definite. By interlacing,
A_{S}
has at most one
non-positive eigenvalue. Since
W_{S}
is infinite,
A_{S}
is not positive
definite. Hence precisely one of the following alternatives occurs.

First,
A_{S}
is positive semidefinite with one-dimensional kernel. Then

W_{S}
is an irreducible affine Coxeter group. Secondly,
A_{S}
is
non-degenerate of signature
(|S|-1,1)
. Then
S
is a Lannér diagram,
equivalently
W_{S}
is a compact hyperbolic Coxeter simplex group. The
Lannér diagrams were classified in
[
28
]
; see also

[
25
, Section 6.9]
.

Consequently, in finite rank, the minimal non-spherical groups are exactly those of irreducible affine type and those of
Lannér, or compact hyperbolic, type. Under the additional assumption that

(W,S)
is
2
-spherical, the rank-two affine case

\widetilde{A}_{1}
, equivalently
m_{st}=\infty
, cannot occur.

4.
Chamber-spherical Hecke estimates

We now turn the explicit Kazhdan constants from the previous section into norm bounds for elements of the chamber Hecke algebra.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a unitary representation and write

Q_{s}=E^{\pi}_{P_{s}}
. On
\mathcal{H}^{B}
, define the normalized chamber Hecke
operator

T_{s}=P_{B}\pi(\dot{s})P_{B}.

Let
q_{s}+1
denote the number of chambers in an
s
-panel.

Lemma 4.1
.

On
\mathcal{H}^{B}
, one has

Q_{s}=\frac{1}{q_{s}+1}(I+q_{s}T_{s}).

In particular,
\|T_{s}-Q_{s}\|\leq 1/q_{s}
.

Proof.

The parahoric subgroup is the disjoint union

P_{s}=B\sqcup B\dot{s}B.

Normalize Haar measure by
\mu(B)=1
. Since an
s
-panel contains

q_{s}+1
chambers, one has
\mu(B\dot{s}B)=q_{s}
. The projection onto

P_{s}
-fixed vectors is averaging over
P_{s}
:

Q_{s}=\frac{1}{q_{s}+1}\left(\int_{B}\pi(b)\,db+\int_{B\dot{s}B}\pi(g)\,dg\right).

On
\mathcal{H}^{B}
, the first integral is the identity and the normalized second
integral is
q_{s}T_{s}
. Therefore

Q_{s}=\frac{1}{q_{s}+1}(I+q_{s}T_{s}),

which gives the stated formula. Rearranging, we get

T_{s}=\frac{q_{s}+1}{q_{s}}Q_{s}-\frac{1}{q_{s}}I,

and hence

T_{s}-Q_{s}=(Q_{s}-I)/q_{s}.

Since
Q_{s}
is an orthogonal projection on
\mathcal{H}^{B}
, the
operator
I-Q_{s}
is also an orthogonal projection, so

\|Q_{s}-I\|\leq 1
. Therefore

\|T_{s}-Q_{s}\|=\frac{1}{q_{s}}\|Q_{s}-I\|\leq\frac{1}{q_{s}}

as claimed.
∎

For a reduced word
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{m}
, put

T_{w}=P_{B}\pi(\dot{w})P_{B}
.
For
w\in W
, let
\mu_{w}
denote the normalized
B
-bi-invariant
probability measure supported on
B\dot{w}B
. Thus

T_{w}=\pi(\mu_{w})\big|_{\mathcal{H}^{B}}.

The product below is convolution in the normalized chamber Hecke algebra.
If
\ell(uv)=\ell(u)+\ell(v)
, then

B\dot{u}B\dot{v}B=B\dot{uv}B

and
\mu_{u}*\mu_{v}
is a
B
-bi-invariant probability measure supported
on
B\dot{uv}B
. Hence
\mu_{u}*\mu_{v}=\mu_{uv}
. Consequently, for a
reduced expression
w=s_{1}\cdots s_{m}
, we obtain

T_{w}=T_{s_{1}}\cdots T_{s_{m}}.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
J\subset S
, and let
\kappa>0
be a Kazhdan constant for

\Omega_{J}=\bigcup_{s\in J}P_{s}
in the parabolic subgroup
P_{J}
.
Then, for every unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})

with
\mathcal{H}^{P_{J}}=\{0\}
, and every unit vector

\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{B}
, one has

\max_{s\in J}\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\geq\frac{\kappa}{2}.

Proof.

Let
\eta=\max_{s\in J}\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|
.
For
p\in P_{s}
, the vector
Q_{s}\xi
is
P_{s}
-fixed. Hence

\|\pi(p)\xi-\xi\|\leq\|\pi(p)(\xi-Q_{s}\xi)\|+\|\xi-Q_{s}\xi\|=2\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\leq 2\eta.

Taking the supremum over
p\in\Omega_{J}
gives
\kappa\leq 2\eta
.
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

Let
b=s_{1}\cdots s_{N}
be a reduced word, and let
J\subset S
be
contained in the support of
b
. Assume that, for every unit vector

\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{B}
,

\max_{s\in J}\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\geq\varepsilon.

Then

\|Q_{s_{1}}\cdots Q_{s_{N}}\|\leq\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2N+1}\right)^{2}}.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{B}
be a unit vector and put
v_{0}=\xi
and

v_{j}=Q_{s_{j}}v_{j-1}
for
1\leq j\leq N
. Set
\alpha=\|v_{N}\|
.
Since the
Q_{s}
are orthogonal projections,

\|v_{j}\|\leq\|v_{j-1}\|\leq 1
, and

\|v_{j-1}-v_{j}\|^{2}=\|(I-Q_{s_{j}})v_{j-1}\|^{2}=\|v_{j-1}\|^{2}-\|v_{j}\|^{2}\leq 1-\alpha^{2}.

Therefore

\|\xi-v_{j-1}\|\leq\sum_{i<j}\|v_{i}-v_{i-1}\|\leq N\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}
.
Using
Q_{s_{j}}v_{j-1}=v_{j}
and the fact that
Q_{s_{j}}
is a contraction,
we get

\displaystyle\|(I-Q_{s_{j}})\xi\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi-v_{j-1}\|+\|v_{j-1}-v_{j}\|+\|Q_{s_{j}}(v_{j-1}-\xi)\|

\displaystyle\leq(2N+1)\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}.

Since
J
is contained in the support of
b
, every
s\in J
occurs as
some
s_{j}
. Hence

\varepsilon\leq\max_{s\in J}\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\leq(2N+1)\sqrt{1-\alpha^{2}}.

Thus

\alpha\leq\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2N+1}\right)^{2}}
.
Taking the supremum over all unit vectors
\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{B}
gives

\|Q_{s_{1}}\cdots Q_{s_{N}}\|\leq\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\varepsilon}{2N+1}\right)^{2}}.

∎

Lemma 4.4
.

For every finite dihedral Coxeter type
I_{2}(m)
and every
\varepsilon>0
,
there is
Q(m,\varepsilon)
such that the following holds. If a finite
rank-two residue of type
I_{2}(m)
has all panel thicknesses at least

Q(m,\varepsilon)+1
, then the codistance between the two rank-one
parahoric fixed spaces is at most
\varepsilon
, uniformly over all
unitary representations.

Proof.

Let
a+1
and
b+1
be the two panel thicknesses of the residue. By
Proposition
2.2
, the relevant codistance is
bounded by
\delta_{m}(a,b)
. For
m=2
this bound is
0
. For

m\geq 3
, the displayed formula for
\delta_{m}(a,b)
shows that

\delta_{m}(a,b)\to 0
when
\min(a,b)\to\infty
. Choosing

Q(m,\varepsilon)
so that
\delta_{m}(a,b)\leq\varepsilon
whenever

\min(a,b)\geq Q(m,\varepsilon)
gives the claim.
∎

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
G
act strongly transitively on a locally finite building of finite
rank, 2-spherical minimal non-spherical type
(W,S)
. Suppose that the
thickness is large enough, depending only on
(W,S)
. Then, for every
unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
such that

\mathcal{H}^{G}=\{0\}
, and every sequence
w_{n}\in W
with

\ell(w_{n})\to\infty
, one has

P_{B}\pi(\dot{w}_{n})P_{B}\to 0

in operator norm.

Proof.

We give the standard large-thickness argument. For each spherical

I\subset S
, let
m_{I}
be the length of the longest element of
W_{I}
,
and put

N=1+\max\{m_{I}:I\subset S,\ W_{I}\ \text{spherical}\}.

Then every reduced word of length
N
has non-spherical support. Indeed,
if its support were spherical of type
I
, the corresponding element of
the finite Coxeter group
W_{I}
would have length at most
m_{I}<N
.
Since
(W,S)
is minimal non-spherical, the support of every reduced word
of length
N
is all of
S
.

By Lemma
4.4
, and since the type is
2-spherical and
S
is finite, the codistances

\codist(P_{s},P_{t})
,
s,t\in S
, are uniformly as small as desired once
the minimal thickness is large enough. Thus, by
Proposition
2.3
, the set

\bigcup_{s\in S}P_{s}
is a Kazhdan set for
G
, with Kazhdan constant
bounded below by some number
\kappa_{0}>0
.

Let
Q_{s}=E^{\pi}_{P_{s}}
. Since
\mathcal{H}^{G}=\{0\}
,
Lemma
4.2
gives, for every unit vector

\xi\in\mathcal{H}^{B}
,

\max_{s\in S}\|(I-Q_{s})\xi\|\geq\kappa_{0}/2.

If
b=s_{1}\cdots s_{N}
is a reduced word of length
N
, then its support
is all of
S
. Lemma
4.3
gives

\|Q_{s_{1}}\cdots Q_{s_{N}}\|\leq\rho_{0}:=\sqrt{1-\left(\frac{\kappa_{0}}{2(2N+1)}\right)^{2}}<1.

Let
q_{\min}
be the minimal panel thickness minus one. On

\mathcal{H}^{B}
, Lemma
4.1
gives

\|T_{s}-Q_{s}\|\leq q_{\min}^{-1}
. Therefore every reduced word
b
of
length
N
satisfies

\|T_{b}\|\leq\rho_{0}+\frac{N}{q_{\min}}.

Increasing the thickness if necessary, we may assume

\theta:=\rho_{0}+N/q_{\min}<1
. Decomposing an arbitrary reduced word

w
into consecutive reduced blocks of length
N
and a remainder gives

\|P_{B}\pi(\dot{w})P_{B}\|\leq\theta^{\lfloor\ell(w)/N\rfloor}.

This tends to zero when
\ell(w)\to\infty
.
∎

The rank-three example continued

We now return to the rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic running

. The following observation is elementary.

Lemma 4.6
.

Every reduced word in
W
of length
7
contains all
generators.

In that case

Corollary 4.7
.

In the rank-three compact-hyperbolic crystallographic setting, assume

\frac{2\sqrt{3q}}{q+1}<1
and

\theta:=\rho(q)+\frac{7}{q}<1
.
Then for every unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})

, we have

\|P_{B}\pi(\dot{w})P_{B}\|\ \leq\theta^{\lfloor\ell(w)/7\rfloor}

for all

Let

be a reduced word of length

. Put

and

,

By Lemma

By Lemma

Writing a reduced word

5.
From chamber-spherical decay to finite-depth decay

We next prove the formal reduction from chamber-spherical decay to decay
at every finite depth.

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a weakly mixing unitary
representation. Let

\overline{\pi}:G\to\mathcal{U}(\overline{\mathcal{H}})
be the conjugate
representation. Then

(\pi\otimes\overline{\pi})^{G}=\{0\}
.

Proof.

Suppose that
\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}
has a non-zero
G
-invariant
vector.
Using the standard identification

\mathcal{H}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}\simeq\HS(\mathcal{H})
, this vector
corresponds to a non-zero Hilbert–Schmidt operator
A
on
\mathcal{H}

satisfying
\pi(g)A\pi(g)^{-1}=A
for
g\in G
.
Thus
A
commutes with
\pi(G)
. Hence
A^{*}A
also commutes with

\pi(G)
. Since
A^{*}A
is compact, positive and non-zero, one of its
non-zero spectral projections has finite-dimensional range. This range is

\pi(G)
-invariant, contradicting weak mixing of
\pi
.
∎

The following theorem enhances decay at the chamber level to decay at every finite depth.

Theorem 5.2
.

Assume
G
has compact open chamber stabilizer
B
. Suppose that for
every unitary representation
\sigma:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
with

\mathcal{E}^{G}=\{0\}
, and every sequence
g_{n}\to\infty
in
G
, one
has

P^{\sigma}_{B}\sigma(g_{n})P^{\sigma}_{B}\to 0

weakly. Then for every weakly mixing unitary representation

\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
, every
r\geq 0
, and every sequence

g_{n}\to\infty
in
G
, one has

P^{\pi}_{r}\pi(g_{n})P^{\pi}_{r}\to 0

weakly, where
P^{\pi}_{r}
is the projection onto
\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
.

Proof.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be as in the statement, and put

\sigma=\pi\otimes\overline{\pi}
on

\mathcal{H}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{H}}
.
By Proposition
5.1
, the representation
\sigma

has no non-zero
G
-fixed vectors.

Fix
r\geq 0
, and let
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
. Define

\Xi=\int_{B}\pi(b)\xi\otimes\overline{\pi(b)\xi}\,db
and

\Upsilon=\int_{B}\pi(b)\eta\otimes\overline{\pi(b)\eta}\,db
.
Both
\Xi
and
\Upsilon
are
B
-fixed vectors for
\sigma
.

For every
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(g)\Xi,\Upsilon\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\left\langle\pi(gb)\xi\otimes\overline{\pi(gb)\xi},\pi(c)\eta\otimes\overline{\pi(c)\eta}\right\rangle\,db\,dc

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\left|\left\langle\pi(gb)\xi,\pi(c)\eta\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,db\,dc

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\left|\left\langle\pi(c^{-1}gb)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,db\,dc.

By the assumption applied to the representation
\sigma
, we have

\langle\sigma(g_{n})\Xi,\Upsilon\rangle\to 0

whenever
g_{n}\to\infty
.

Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle

does not tend to zero. Passing to a subsequence, there is
c>0
such that

|\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle|\geq c
for all
n
. Since

\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
, for all
b,c\in B_{r}
,

\left\langle\pi(c^{-1}g_{n}b)\xi,\eta\right\rangle=\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle
.
Therefore

\displaystyle\langle\sigma(g_{n})\Xi,\Upsilon\rangle

\displaystyle=\int_{B}\int_{B}\left|\left\langle\pi(c^{-1}g_{n}b)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,db\,dc

\displaystyle\geq\int_{B_{r}}\int_{B_{r}}\left|\left\langle\pi(c^{-1}g_{n}b)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|^{2}\,db\,dc

\displaystyle=\mu(B_{r})^{2}|\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle|^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\mu(B_{r})^{2}c^{2}>0,

contradicting
\langle\sigma(g_{n})\Xi,\Upsilon\rangle\to 0
. Thus

\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi,\eta\rangle\to 0
for all

\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
, which is exactly weak convergence

P_{r}^{\pi}\pi(g_{n})P_{r}^{\pi}\to 0
.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The proof gives a quantitative finite-depth version of the decay statement.
Indeed, if

\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
is weakly mixing, then, for every

r\geq 0
, every

\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
, and every
g\in B\dot{w}B
,

\left|\left\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\right\rangle\right|\leq\mu(B_{r})^{-1}\,\|\xi\|\,\|\eta\|\,\theta^{\frac{1}{2}\lfloor\ell(w)/N\rfloor}.

With the normalization
\mu(B)=1
, this constant is

\mu(B_{r})^{-1}=[B:B_{r}]
.

Thus on vectors fixed by
B_{r}
for some
r
, we obtain an explicit exponential
decay estimate with respect to the Weyl length. This is analogous in spirit to Oh’s
pointwise bounds for
K
-finite matrix coefficients of higher-rank
simple algebraic groups, see
[
31
]
. However, there, such an estimate was used to establish property (T). In the present setting, we have already established property (T) for the ambient group
G
, and use it to obtain decay of matrix coefficients.

Remark 5.4
.

The estimates above also give a weak form of the Kunze–Stein phenomenon for
B
-bi-invariant functions. We record this only as a byproduct.
Normalize Haar measure by
\mu(B)=1
. For
w\in W
, put

V_{w}=\mu(B\dot{w}B).

Let
m_{B}
denote the normalized Haar probability measure on
B
. The
operator

\lambda(m_{B}*\delta_{\dot{w}}*m_{B})

on
L^{2}(G)
is the regular-representation instance of the normalized
chamber Hecke operator
T_{w}
. Hence the proof of Proposition
4.5

gives constants
N\geq 1
and
0<\theta<1
such that

\bigl\|\lambda(m_{B}*\delta_{\dot{w}}*m_{B})\bigr\|_{B(G^{2}(G))}\leq\theta^{\lfloor\ell(w)/N\rfloor}

for all
w\in W
.
It follows that convolution by
B
-bi-invariant functions satisfies a
Kunze–Stein estimate for
p
sufficiently close to
1
. More precisely,
there exists
p_{0}>1
such that, for every
1\leq p<p_{0}
, there is a
constant
C_{p}
with

\|f*\xi\|_{2}\leq C_{p}\|f\|_{p}\|\xi\|_{2}

for every compactly supported
B
-bi-invariant function
f
on
G
and
every
\xi\in L^{2}(G)
.

Indeed, write

f=\sum_{w\in W}a_{w}\,1_{B\dot{w}B}

with finite support. Since

1_{B\dot{w}B}=V_{w}\,m_{B}*\delta_{\dot{w}}*m_{B},

we get

\|\lambda(f)\|\leq\sum_{w\in W}|a_{w}|\,V_{w}\,\theta^{\lfloor\ell(w)/N\rfloor}.

On the other hand,

\|f\|_{p}^{p}=\sum_{w\in W}|a_{w}|^{p}V_{w}.

Thus Hölder’s inequality gives

\|\lambda(f)\|\leq\|f\|_{p}\left(\sum_{w\in W}\theta^{p^{\prime}\lfloor\ell(w)/N\rfloor}V_{w}\right)^{1/p^{\prime}},

where
p^{\prime}
is conjugate to
p
. The last series is finite for
p

sufficiently close to
1
, because
W
has at most exponential growth
and
V_{w}
grows at most exponentially in
\ell(w)
.

6.
The Howe–Moore property

The following is a standard observation, see for example
[
39
, Lemma 1.1]
and the references therein.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
G
be a totally disconnected locally compact group. In every strongly
continuous unitary representation
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
,
the union of the subspaces
\mathcal{H}^{U}
, with
U<G
compact open, is
dense in
\mathcal{H}
.

Proof.

Let
\xi\in\mathcal{H}
. Since
G
is totally disconnected and locally
compact, compact open subgroups form a neighbourhood basis of the identity by van Dantzig’s theorem.
For such a subgroup
U
, let
P_{U}\xi=\int_{U}\pi(u)\xi\,du
be the
averaging projection. As
U
shrinks to the identity, strong continuity of

\pi
gives
P_{U}\xi\to\xi
.
Thus compact-open fixed vectors are dense.
∎

Lemma 6.2
.

The subgroups
B_{r}=\Fix_{G}(\mathcal{N}_{r}(C_{0}))
,
r\geq 0
, form a neighbourhood
basis of the identity in
G
.
In particular, every open subgroup
U<G
contains
B_{r}
for some

r
.

Proof.

The topology on
G<_{c}\operatorname{Aut}(X)
is the subspace topology
from the permutation topology on
\operatorname{Aut}(X)
.
Hence pointwise stabilizers of finite
subsets form a neighbourhood basis of the identity. Since the balls

\mathcal{N}_{r}(C_{0})
exhaust the building, the pointwise stabilizers
B_{r}
form
such a basis.
∎

Proposition 6.3
.

Assume that for every weakly mixing unitary representation

\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
, every
r\geq 0
, and every sequence

g_{n}\to\infty
in
G
, one has

P_{r}^{\pi}\pi(g_{n})P_{r}^{\pi}\to 0
weakly.
Then every weakly mixing unitary representation of
G
is
C_{0}
.

Proof.

In the present setting,
G=\bigcup_{w\in W}B\dot{w}B
, and
W
is
countable. Thus
G
is
\sigma
-compact and metrizable. Hence a matrix
coefficient vanishes at infinity if and only if it tends to zero along every
sequence escaping compact subsets of
G
.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a weakly mixing unitary
representation. Let
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, and let
\varepsilon>0
. By
Lemma
6.1
, compact-open fixed vectors are dense in

\mathcal{H}
. Hence we can choose compact-open fixed vectors

\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{H}
such that

\|\xi-\xi_{0}\|\,\|\eta\|+\|\xi_{0}\|\,\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|<\varepsilon/2.

Then, for every
g\in G
,

\displaystyle\left|\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle-\langle\pi(g)\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\langle\pi(g)(\xi-\xi_{0}),\eta\rangle\right|+\left|\langle\pi(g)\xi_{0},\eta-\eta_{0}\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|\xi-\xi_{0}\|\,\|\eta\|+\|\xi_{0}\|\,\|\eta-\eta_{0}\|<\varepsilon/2.

uniformly for
g\in G
.

Let
U<G
be a compact open subgroup fixing both
\xi_{0}
and

\eta_{0}
. By Lemma
6.2
, there is
r\geq 0
such that
B_{r}\subset U
.
Thus
\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\in\mathcal{H}^{B_{r}}
. If
g_{n}\to\infty
in
G
,
then the assumption gives

P_{r}^{\pi}\pi(g_{n})P_{r}^{\pi}\to 0

weakly. Therefore

\langle\pi(g_{n})\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\rangle=\left\langle P_{r}^{\pi}\pi(g_{n})P_{r}^{\pi}\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\right\rangle\to 0.

Consequently there is a compact subset
C\subset G
such that

\left|\langle\pi(g)\xi_{0},\eta_{0}\rangle\right|<\varepsilon/2

for all
g\notin C
. For all such
g
, the uniform estimate above gives

\left|\langle\pi(g)\xi,\eta\rangle\right|<\varepsilon.

Hence every matrix coefficient of
\pi
vanishes at infinity. This proves
the proposition.
∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
g_{n}=b_{n}\dot{w}_{n}c_{n}
, where
b_{n},c_{n}\in B
and
w_{n}\in W
.
Then
g_{n}\to\infty
if and only if
\ell(w_{n})\to\infty
.

Proof.

For every
R
, the set
\bigcup_{\ell(w)\leq R}B\dot{w}B
is a finite
union of compact sets, hence compact. Therefore bounded Weyl
length prevents escape to infinity. Conversely, if
\ell(w_{n})\to\infty
,
then
g_{n}
eventually leaves every set of the above form, hence every
compact subset of
G
, since
B\backslash G/B=W
is discrete.
∎

Proof of Theorem A.

We first dispose of the rank-two case. If
|S|=2
, then
W
is the
infinite dihedral group and
X
is a locally finite bi-regular tree. Since the
building is thick, every vertex has valency at least
3
. Strong transitivity
on
X
is equivalent to
2
-transitivity on the boundary
\partial X
; see

[
8
, Theorem 1.1 and Corollary 3.6]
. Let

M=G^{(\infty)}
denote the monolith of
G
, that is, the intersection of
all non-trivial closed normal subgroups of
G
. By the structure theorem of
Burger–Mozes, in the form stated in
[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
,
the group
M
is compactly generated and topologically simple and acts

2
-transitively on
\partial X
. By Burger–Mozes
[
7
, Proposition 1.2.1]
, as recalled in

[
9
, Theorem 2.2]
, the quotient
G/M
is compact.

Let
\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a weakly mixing strongly continuous
unitary representation. We claim that
\pi|_{M}
has no non-zero
M
-fixed
vector. Indeed, since
M
is normal in
G
, the subspace
\mathcal{H}^{M}

is
G
-invariant. If
\mathcal{H}^{M}\neq 0
, then the corresponding
representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}^{M}
factors through the compact group

G/M
. By the Peter–Weyl theorem, every non-zero unitary representation of
a compact group contains a non-zero finite-dimensional subrepresentation. This
would give a non-zero finite-dimensional
G
-invariant subspace of

\mathcal{H}
, contradicting weak mixing. Hence, we obtain
\mathcal{H}^{M}=0
.

Since
M
is topologically simple and acts
2
-transitively on the boundary
of the bi-regular tree
X
, the results of Burger–Mozes
[
7
]
, see also Ciobotaru’s theorem
[
12
, Theorem 4.19]
, apply to
M
. Thus every matrix coefficient of

\pi|_{M}
vanishes at infinity on
M
.
It remains to pass from
M
to
G
. Since
G/M
is compact and
M
is
closed, there is a compact subset
K\subset G
such that
G=MK
. Let

(g_{i})
be a net in
G
tending to infinity. Write
g_{i}=m_{i}k_{i}
, with

m_{i}\in M
and
k_{i}\in K
. Passing to a subnet, we may assume

k_{i}\to k\in K
. Then
m_{i}\to\infty
in
M
; otherwise a subnet of

(m_{i})
would remain in a compact subset of
M
, and hence
(g_{i})
would
remain in a compact subset of
G
.
For
\xi,\eta\in\mathcal{H}
, we have

\langle\pi(g_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle=\langle\pi(m_{i})\pi(k_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle.

Since
k_{i}\to k
and
\pi
is strongly continuous,

\pi(k_{i})\xi\rightarrow\pi(k)\xi.

Hence

\displaystyle\bigl|\langle\pi(g_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle=\bigl|\langle\pi(m_{i})\pi(k_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\bigl|\langle\pi(m_{i})(\pi(k_{i})\xi-\pi(k)\xi),\eta\rangle\bigr|+\bigl|\langle\pi(m_{i})\pi(k)\xi,\eta\rangle\bigr|

\displaystyle\leq\|\pi(k_{i})\xi-\pi(k)\xi\|\,\|\eta\|+\bigl|\langle\pi(m_{i})\pi(k)\xi,\eta\rangle\bigr|.

The first term tends to
0
by strong continuity. The second term tends to

0
because
m_{i}\to\infty
in
M
and all matrix coefficients of

\pi|_{M}
vanish at infinity. Therefore

\langle\pi(g_{i})\xi,\eta\rangle\rightarrow 0.

Thus every matrix coefficient of
\pi
vanishes at infinity on
G
. This
proves Theorem
A
in the rank-two case.

We may therefore assume
|S|\geq 3
. Then every rank-two standard
parabolic subgroup is proper, hence spherical by minimal non-sphericity; in
particular
(W,S)
is
2
-spherical.

Let

\pi:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be a weakly mixing unitary
representation. We prove that all matrix coefficients of
\pi
vanish at
infinity.

By Proposition
4.5
, for every unitary
representation
\sigma:G\to\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{E})
with

\mathcal{E}^{G}=\{0\}
, chamber-spherical Hecke operators decay in operator
norm along escaping Weyl sequences. Indeed, by
Lemma
6.4
, every escaping sequence in
G
has a
decomposition

g_{n}=b_{n}\dot{w}_{n}c_{n},\qquad b_{n},c_{n}\in B,

with
\ell(w_{n})\to\infty
. For such a sequence,

P_{B}^{\sigma}\sigma(g_{n})P_{B}^{\sigma}=P_{B}^{\sigma}\sigma(\dot{w}_{n})P_{B}^{\sigma},

because
P_{B}^{\sigma}\sigma(b_{n})=P_{B}^{\sigma}
and

\sigma(c_{n})P_{B}^{\sigma}=P_{B}^{\sigma}
. Thus the decay
obtained for
\dot{w}_{n}
is exactly the chamber-spherical decay required
in Theorem
5.2
. Theorem
5.2
upgrades
this to finite-depth decay for
\pi
. Proposition
6.3

then shows that
\pi
is
C_{0}
.
∎

Proof of Corollary B.

Let
\pi
be a unitary representation of
G
with no non-zero

G
-fixed vector. If
\pi
had a non-zero finite-dimensional

G
-invariant subspace, the corresponding finite-dimensional unitary
representation has compact image by Caprace–Monod

[
11
, Corollary 3.1]
. Since
G
has no compact quotients by
hypothesis, this representation is trivial. This would give a non-zero

G
-fixed vector, a contradiction. Thus
\pi
is weakly mixing.
Theorem
A
implies that the matrix coefficients of
\pi
vanish at
infinity, so
G
has the Howe–Moore property.
∎

7.
Characters and invariant random subgroups

An
invariant random subgroup
(IRS) of a locally compact group
G

is a conjugation-invariant Borel probability measure on the space of closed
subgroups of
G
(equipped with the Chabauty topology). Equivalently, it
is the distribution of stabilizers for a probability-measure-preserving
action of
G
. We denote by
\operatorname{IRS}(G)
the space of all invariant random
subgroups of
G
, and by
\operatorname{IRS}_{\mathrm{erg}}(G)
the subspace of ergodic ones.

Recall also that a character of a countable group
\Gamma
is a
conjugation-invariant positive definite function normalized by

\chi(e)=1
; it is extremal if it is an extreme point of the convex set
of characters. If
\theta\in\operatorname{IRS}(\Gamma)
, then the associated
fixed-point character is given by

\chi_{\theta}(g)=\theta\bigl(\{H\leq\Gamma:g\in H\}\bigr).

If
\theta
is realized as the stabilizer distribution of a p.m.p.
action
\Gamma\curvearrowright(Y,\mu)
, then

\chi_{\theta}(g)=\mu(\{y\in Y:gy=y\})
.

In this section we apply the Howe–Moore property through the character
rigidity theorem of Creutz and Peterson. We use the following special case of
[
14
, Corollary 6.6]
.

Theorem 7.1

(Creutz–Peterson
[
14
, Corollary 6.6]
)
.

Let
\Gamma<L_{1}\times L_{2}
be an irreducible lattice, where
L_{1},L_{2}

are second countable, non-discrete, non-compact, topologically simple,
totally disconnected, locally compact, Kazhdan groups with the Howe–Moore property.
Assume that there is a compact open subgroup
K_{2}<L_{2}
such that

\Gamma_{1}=\pr_{1}\bigl(\Gamma\cap(L_{1}\times K_{2})\bigr)

is a square-integrable lattice in
L_{1}
and contains a non-torsion
element. Then every extremal character of
\Gamma
is either the regular
character or a finite-dimensional character. Equivalently, every finite
factor representation of
\Gamma
is either the regular representation or
finite-dimensional.

Proof of Theorem D.

Let
\Delta=\Delta_{+}\sqcup\Delta_{-}
be the twin building associated with

\Lambda
, and let
L_{\pm}
be the closures of the images of

\Lambda
in
\operatorname{Aut}(\Delta_{\pm})
. By Caprace–Rémy

[
10
]
, the group
\Gamma
is finitely presented, infinite
and simple, has property
(T)
, and embeds as an irreducible lattice in

L_{+}\times L_{-}
. Moreover
L_{+}
and
L_{-}
are second countable,
non-discrete, non-compact, topologically simple, totally disconnected
Kazhdan locally compact groups acting strongly transitively on

\Delta_{+}
and
\Delta_{-}
, respectively.

Each building
\Delta_{\pm}
has rank-three compact-hyperbolic
crystallographic type and all panels have thickness
q+1
. Since
topological simplicity and non-compactness imply that
L_{\pm}
have no
non-trivial compact quotient, Corollary
C
shows that both
L_{+}
and

L_{-}
have the Howe–Moore property.

Let
K_{-}<L_{-}
be the stabilizer of a chamber in
\Delta_{-}
, and put

\Gamma_{+}=\pr_{+}\bigl(\Gamma\cap(L_{+}\times K_{-})\bigr)<L_{+}.

This is, up to the finite central quotient, the stabilizer of the opposite
chamber in the diagonal action of
\Lambda^{\dagger}
. Rémy’s lattice
theorem
[
36
, Theorem 3]
identifies it as a non-uniform
lattice in
L_{+}
, and Rémy’s integrability theorem

[
37
]
gives the square-integrability required in
Theorem
7.1
.

It remains only to record the non-torsion condition. The Davis realization
of
\Delta_{+}
is a proper CAT
(-1)
space in the compact-hyperbolic
case, and the lattice
\Gamma_{+}
acts non-elementarily on it. Hence

\Gamma_{+}
contains a loxodromic isometry; see, for example,
de la Harpe
[
15
, Chapter III]
. Such an element has infinite
order. Thus
\Gamma_{+}
contains a non-torsion element.

Theorem
7.1
therefore applies to

\Gamma<L_{+}\times L_{-}
. Hence every extremal character of
\Gamma
is
either regular or finite-dimensional. Since
\Gamma
is finitely generated
and infinite simple, it has no non-trivial finite-dimensional unitary
representations: a non-trivial one would be faithful by simplicity, and
would make
\Gamma
a finitely generated linear group, hence residually
finite by Mal’cev
[
30
]
; an infinite simple group cannot be
residually finite. Thus the only finite-dimensional character is the
trivial character. This proves Theorem
D
.
∎

Proof of Corollary E.

Let
\theta\in\operatorname{IRS}_{\mathrm{erg}}(\Gamma)
, and realize it as the stabilizer
distribution of an ergodic probability-measure-preserving action

\Gamma\curvearrowright(Y,\mu)
. The action consequence of

[
14
, Corollary 6.6]
, applied in the proof of
Theorem
D
, says that this action is either essentially free or has finite
orbits. In the first case
\theta=\delta_{\{e\}}
.

In the finite-orbit case, almost every stabilizer has finite index in

\Gamma
. Since
\Gamma
is infinite and simple, it has no non-trivial
finite quotients, so every finite orbit is a singleton. Hence

\theta=\delta_{\Gamma}
. The statement for all invariant random subgroups
follows by ergodic decomposition.
∎

Remark 7.2
.

The same IRS conclusion
can be obtained directly from the stabilizer rigidity theorem of Creutz–Peterson

[
13
]
. In particular, it follows from this direct application that
\Gamma
satisfies the classical normal subgroup theorem: every normal subgroup of
\Gamma
is either finite or of finite index. Of course, this was already known for Kac–Moody lattices by the work of Caprace and Rémy in
[
10
]
as an intermediate step to the proof of simplicity, based on results by Bader–Shalom in
[
3
]
, but the present argument gives a new route to this intermediate result.
