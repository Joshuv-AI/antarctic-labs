Introduction

Chronic obstructive pulmonary disease (COPD) is a common respiratory disorder characterized by persistent airflow limitation and related respiratory symptoms caused by abnormalities in airway and/or alveolar function. The pathogenesis of COPD involves complex interactions between genetic predisposition and environmental risk factors [1]. FAM13A has been identified as one of the susceptibility genes for COPD [2]. Emerging evidence suggests that FAM13A may contribute to COPD susceptibility through multiple biological mechanisms[3–5]. Nevertheless, the association between FAM13A single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) and clinical phenotypes of COPD remains incompletely understood.

Matrix metalloproteinases (MMPs) play key roles in inflammation and lung tissue remodelling in COPD, among which MMP-9 and MMP-12 are particularly critical [6]. MMP-9 contributes to lung injury in COPD by degrading the extracellular matrix (ECM), recruiting neutrophils, and amplifying inflammatory responses [7, 8]. Previous studies have demonstrated that MMP-9 is associated with an increased risk of acute exacerbations in COPD patients [9], elevated MMP-9 levels are positively correlated with disease severity and inversely correlated with FEV1/FVC and FEV1 [10]. MMP-12 degrades elastin within the ECM [11] and promotes alveolar destruction and COPD progression by modulating macrophage activation [12]. Increased MMP-12 levels in the induced sputum of smoking COPD patients [13] are significantly associated with CT-measured emphysema severity and disease severity [14].

Small airway remodelling represents an early pathological feature of COPD, in which epithelial‒mesenchymal transition (EMT) plays a crucial role [15]. Our previous study demonstrated that FAM13A in small airway epithelial cells from COPD patients may promote EMT via the TGF-β1 signalling pathway, leading to upregulated MMP-9 expression [16]. In addition, Tam et al. [17] showed that FAM13A-deficient cells exhibited significantly increased MMP-2 expression under TGF-β1 stimulation. The protein encoded by FAM13A contains a Rho GTPase-activating protein domain, implying its potential function in regulating cytoskeletal dynamics through Rho GTPases, which have been reported to induce EMT [18]. Collectively, these findings support that FAM13A contribute to EMT, As key markers of EMT, MMP-9 and MMP-12 are critically involved in extracellular matrix degradation and remodelling [19].

Although several FAM13A SNPs have been reported, the relationships FAM13A SNP with, MMP-9, MMP-12, and clinical phenotypes in COPD remain to be fully elucidated. In the present study, targeted region capture sequencing was performed to cover the full length of FAM13A gene, Comprehensive clinical data were collected, including pulmonary function, chest HRCT-based quantitative parameters, routine blood test results, and serum levels of MMP-9 and MMP-12. We aimed to investigate the associations of FMA13A SNPs with COPD susceptibility, serum MMP-9 and MMP-12 levels, and related clinical phenotypes.

Material and methods

Study subjects

A total of 497 stable COPD patients were consecutively enrolled between January 1,2021,and May 31,2024, from three medical centers in Ningxia: the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University, the Fifth People’s Hospital of Ningxia, and the Second People’s Hospital of Shizuishan. All patients were clinically confirmed to have stable COPD and were no biological familial related to each other. The control group comprised 303 healthy individuals who underwent routine physical examination at Health Management Center of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University during the same study period. This study complied with the Declaration of Helsinki and was approved by the Ethics Committee of the General Hospital of Ningxia Medical University (No. 2020–786). It was retrospectively registered with the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry (ChiCTR2300071579). All participants provided written informed consent.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria

The COPD group criteria: presence of chronic cough, sputum production, dyspnea, and/or a history of exposure to risk factors for COPD; post-bronchodilator FEV1/FVC < 0.70 confirmed by pulmonary function tests; stable phase of COPD; age ≥ 40 years; Han Chinese ethnicity. The healthy control group criteria: no history of chronic or severe respiratory diseases; absence of respiratory symptoms; normal pulmonary ventilation function; no structural abnormalities on chest CT; and no severe systemic diseases. Exclusion Criteria: Patients who experienced an acute exacerbation of COPD within the past two weeks. Presence of other severe respiratory diseases, such as interstitial lung disease, active pulmonary tuberculosis, pleural effusion, pulmonary malignancy, or history of lung resection. Presence of severe systemic diseases, including heart failure, significant hepatic or renal dysfunction, or severe anemia. Mental or cognitive impairments. Inability to cooperate with medical history collection, pulmonary function testing, and chest CT examination.

Data collection

Following the data collection method in the previous study [20], the baseline information and clinical data of the study population were collected, including haematological data, pulmonary function, and imaging data. An LAA-950% ≥ 6% was defined as the emphysema phenotype, whereas an LAA-950% < 6% was considered the non-emphysema phenotype [21]. Never smokers were defined as individuals who had smoked fewer than 100 cigarettes in their lifetime. Former smokers were defined as those who had smoked ≥ 100 cigarettes in their lifetime but had abstained from smoking for at least 28 days before enrolment. Current smokers were defined as individuals who had smoked ≥ 100 cigarettes in their lifetime and had smoked within the past 28 days. Smoking pack-years were calculated as (Cigarettes per day/20) × years of smoking [1].

DNA extraction and SNP analysis

The standards for DNA extraction and screening of SNP sites of the FAM13A gene were the same as those in the previous study [20].

MMP-9 and MMP-12 measurement

A 3 mL venous blood sample was collected and centrifuged at 2500 r/min for 10 min to separate the serum, which was stored at −80 °C until analysis. Serum concentrations of MMP-9 and MMP-12 were measured using enzyme-linked immunosorbent assay (ELISA) kits provided by Thermo Fisher Scientific (Waltham, MA, USA). The double-antibody sandwich method was employed, and all procedures were performed according to the manufacturer's instructions. Each sample was analysed in duplicate to ensure the accuracy of the results. The ELISA kit demonstrated the following performance characteristics: Sensitivity: Minimum detectable concentration = 0.1ng/mL(MMP-9) and 0.05ng/mL(MMP-12), Specificity: No significant cross-reactivity with related MMPs. Detection Range:0.2-1000ng/mL (MMP-9) and 0.1-50ng/mL (MMP-12); Precison: Inter-assay coefficient of variation < 10%.

Statistical methods

Statistical analyses were performed using SPSS 24.0 software (Chicago, IL, USA). Continuous data were expressed as the means ± standard deviations (SDs) if normally distributed, or as the medians (interquartile ranges, IQR) if non-normally distributed, with normality tests conducted for all continuous variables. Categorical data were presented as percentages (%). For inter-group comparisons, the independent-sample t test was used for normally distributed data between two groups, whereas non-normally distributed data were compared using the Mann‒Whitney U test. For comparisons among multiple groups, normally distributed data with homogeneity of variance were analysed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA), followed by LSD post-hoc tests. Non-normally distributed data were analysed using the Kruskal‒Wallis test. 1:1 propensity score matching (age, BMI, smoking status) was applied to obtain matched COPD–control pairs for further analysis. Significant SNP loci were analysed using Plink software, and SNP visualisations were generated with the LocusZoom website (http://locuszoom.sph.umich). Binary logistic regression was employed to analyse the relationships between allelic frequencies and genotypes of SNP loci and disease susceptibility, adjusting for sex, age, BMI, and the smoking index. Multiple linear regression was used to assess the correlation between SNP genotypes and serum MMP-12 levels. A P value of < 0.05 was considered to indicate statistical significance.

For demographic features, the COPD group had a significantly higher mean age than the control group (67.41 ± 8.62 vs. 57.76 ± 9.88 years, P < 0.001) and a higher proportion of current smokers (32.39% vs. 20.46%, P < 0.001). No significant difference in sex distribution was observed between the two groups (P = 0.086), while the BMI of the COPD group was slightly lower (24.92 ± 3.83 vs.25.44 ± 3.39 kg/m2, P = 0.048).

In terms of pulmonary function, the COPD group exhibited significantly lower FEV1%pred (73.29 ± 22.26% vs.111.98 ± 19.30%), FEV1/FVC (58.53 ± 9.36% vs.81.08 ± 4.20%) and MMEF (0.84 ± 0.40 vs.3.11 ± 0.94L/s) (all P < 0.001), and a markedly higher RV/TLC ratio (47.59 ± 8.84% vs. 35.12 ± 6.09%, P < 0.001) compared with the control group. For haematological indicators, the COPD group had significantly elevated white blood cell counts (6.40 ± 1.85 × 10⁹/L vs. 6.05 ± 1.64 × 10⁹/L, P = 0.007) and neutrophil percentages (57.85 ± 10.53% vs.55.36 ± 8.78%, P = 0.001), as well as a higher median eosinophil count (0.13 × 10⁹/L vs. 0.11 × 10⁹/L, P = 0.039).

Regarding quantitative imaging parameters, the COPD group had significantly increased bronchial wall thickness (G3-WT: 1.38 ± 0.43 vs.1.15 ± 0.34 mm, P = 0.006) and bronchial wall area ratio (G3-WA%: 67.18 ± 7.94% vs.65.97 ± 5.75%, P = 0.021), alongside a notably higher median LAA-950% (3.17% vs. 0.65%, P < 0.001). Among the COPD cohort, the proportions of patients with GOLD 1, GOLD 2 and GOLD 3–4 disease were 37.22%, 48.29% and 14.49%, respectively.

Serum MMP-9 and MMP-12 levels

The serum levels of MMP-9 and MMP-12 were significantly higher in the COPD group than in the control group (both P < 0.001). Similarly, the median serum MMP-12 level was 0.65 ng/mL (IQR: 0.33–1.27 ng/mL) in the COPD group, whereas it was 0.54 ng/mL (IQR: 0.31–0.84 ng/mL) in the control group (P < 0.001). SNP screening

Targeted region capture sequencing was performed on the FAM13A gene for all participants, and variants were annotated on the basis of the reference genome. A total of 1485 SNP loci were identified. Quality control, which was performed using Plink software, identified the following SNPs for exclusion: 990 SNPs with a minor allele frequency (MAF) < 0.05, 7 SNPs not in Hardy‒Weinberg equilibrium (HWE, P < 0.05), and 12 multiallelic SNPs. After excluding these SNPs, 476 SNPs remained for further analysis. Among these 476 SNPs, allele frequencies were compared between COPD patients and healthy controls. To avoid false-negative results, the significance threshold was set at P < 0.05/number of LD quality controlled SNPs (169) = 2.96 × 10⁻4. After adjusting for covariates, such as age, sex, BMI, and smoking index, the SNP rs2869947 within the FAM13A gene was found to be significantly associated with COPD susceptibility. Association and stratified analyses between rs2869947 G > A and COPD susceptibility

Using logistic regression models adjusted for sex, age, BMI, and smoking index, the association between rs2869947 G > A and COPD risk was evaluated. In the codominant model, compared with the common allele GG genotype, the minor allele GA genotype was not significantly associated with COPD risk (GA vs. GG: OR = 1.169, 95% CI = 0.824–1.660, P = 0.382). However, individuals with the homozygous minor AA genotype had a significantly greater risk of COPD (AA vs. GG: OR = 2.040, 95% CI = 1.090–3.818, P = 0.026). According to the additive model, the risk of COPD increased by 27.3% with each additional A allele (OR = 1.314, 95% CI = 1.017–1.699, P = 0.037). The dominant model revealed no significant difference in COPD risk between individuals with the GA or AA genotypes and those with the GG genotype (GA + AA vs. GG: OR = 1.293, 95% CI = 0.928–1.803, P = 0.129). In the recessive model, individuals with the AA genotype had a significantly greater COPD risk than those with the GG or GA genotypes (AA vs. GG + GA: OR = 1.906, 95% CI = 1.037–3.504, P = 0.038). The details of the associations are shown in Table 5. Stratified analyses by sex, age, BMI, and smoking status revealed that compared with individuals with the GG or GA genotype, those with the AA genotype had a significantly greater COPD risk among males (OR = 2.494, 95% CI = 1.042–5.972, P = 0.040) and individuals with a BMI ≥ 24 (OR = 3.199, 95% CI = 1.300–7.872, P = 0.011). The details of the stratified analysis are provided in Supplement Table 2.

Analysis of the rs2869947 genotype and clinical indicators in COPD patients

The clinical characteristics of COPD patients with different rs2869947 genotypes (GG, GA, AA) were analysed, which revealed no significant differences among the three genotypes in terms of pulmonary function indices, including FEV1/FVC, FEV1%pred, MMEF, and RV/TLC (P > 0.05). The LAA-950% quantitative imaging parameter was significantly different among the genotypes (P = 0.049), with the highest value observed in patients carrying the AA genotype. There were no significant differences in the G3-WT and G3-WA% imaging parameters among the three genotypes (P > 0.05). Similarly, no significant differences in the haematological parameters, including white blood cell (WBC) count, neutrophil percentage (NEUT%), or absolute eosinophil (EOS) count, were detected among the three genotypes (P > 0.05). Discussion

This study explored the associations of FAM13A polymorphisms with COPD susceptibility, clinical phenotypes, and serum MMP-9, MMP-12 levels in Han Chinese population via targeted region capture sequencing. We identified FAM13A rs2869947 G > A as a COPD susceptibility locus, with the AA genotype correlated with elevated serum MMP-12 and higher LAA-950%.

Our findings extend the genetic evidence of FAM13A in Han Chinese COPD patients. Previous studies reported multiple FAM13A SNPs associated with COPD in diverse populations [22, 23], including Chinese cohorts [24–27]. The novel association of rs2869947 may result from population genetic heterogeneity, and comprehensive FAM13A locus coverage. Stratified analysis showed the AA genotype susceptibility effect was specific to males and individuals with BMI ≥ 24, Multiple large COPD cohort studies have demonstrated that the genetic effects of FAM13A are more remarkable in males, which is associated with higher smoking exposure and hormone-gene interactions in males [2]. FAM13A is highly expressed in adipose tissue and is mainly localized in mature adipocytes, compared with lean mice, the expression level of FAM13A in adipose tissue of obese mice is significantly decreased, suggesting that its abnormal expression is closely associated with obesity-related metabolic disorders [28].

The present study demonstrated that serum levels of MMP-9 and MMP-12 were significantly higher in patients with stable COPD than in healthy controls. Moreover, among COPD patients, emphysema-predominant subjects exhibited higher serum MMP-9 and MMP-12 concentrations compared with non-emphysematous patients. Consistent with our results, previous clinical studies have confirmed that circulating MMP-9 and MMP-12 levels are elevated in patients with stable COPD and are closely associated with the severity of emphysematous destruction and extracellular matrix degradation in the lung [29–31]. We further observed that the rs2869947 genetic polymorphism in the FAM13A gene was significantly correlated with circulating MMP-12 levels. Numerous studies have shown that both FAM13A and MMP12 are closely associated with susceptibility to COPD. As pointed out by Jiang et al. in their article "Connecting COPD GWAS Genes", FAM13A has a clear biological association with TGF-β2, with the underlying association pathway being FAM13A–AP3D1–CTGF–TGFβ2. Meanwhile, MMP12 also has biological associations with TGF-β2, and the specific regulatory pathways include TGFβ2–BGN–ELN–MMP12, TGFβ2–DCN–ELN–MMP12, and TGFβ2–FBN1–ELN–MMP12 [32]. Collectively, these findings suggest that a FAM13A-TGF-β2-ELN-MMP12 regulatory pathway may exist in the protein–protein interaction network of COPD.

Notably, the AA genotype was association with higher LAA-950%, whereas no significant associations were observed for pulmonary function indices (FEV1/FVC, FEV1%pred) or airway wall measurements (G3-WT, G3-WA%). This suggests rs2869947 specifically modulates the emphysema phenotype rather than general airflow limitation or small airway remodeling. Although direct functional validation of the rs2869947 AA genotype in humans is lacking, indirect evidence from animal studies supports a role for FAM13A in emphysema pathogenesis. FAM13A-/- mice were resistant to chronic cigarette smoke-induced emphysema compared with FAM13A +/+ mice, as complete loss of FAM13A activates compensatory Wnt/β-catenin mediated alveolar repair pathways [3], thereby providing protection against emphysema. FAM13A has also been implicated in the development of emphysema through energy metabolism pathways. Specifically, FAM13A regulates fatty acid oxidation, which contributes to airspace enlargement in mouse lungs [4]. These findings are consistent with our observation that the AA genotype correlates with higher LAA-950%.

This study has several notable limitations. First, based on our a priori sample size calculation(MAF = 0.33, OR = 1.3, α = 0.05, power = 0.80) required 320 cases and 320 controls, Although our total sample included 497 COPD patients and 303 controls (total > 640), the case–control ratio was unbalance, resulting in low actual statistical power of 41.8%. which may reduce the ability to detect weak genetic effects. Second, we did not fully incorporate other key environmental exposures contributing to COPD pathogenesis, such as inhalable particulate matter and toxic gases from household and ambient air pollution, as well as other environment and host-related factors. Instead, we focused primarily on cigarette smoking status, which may lead to an incomplete assessment of COPD-related risk factors. Third, mechanistic inferences were based only on indirect evidence, with no expermental validation at biological or function level in directly. Future large-scale, multi-centre studies with balanced case–control ratios and direct functional experiments are warranted to confirm these associations and further elucidate the underlying molecular mechanisms, with the ultimate goal of translating research findings into precision prevention and treatment strategies for COPD.

Conclusion

This study suggests that FAM13A SNP rs2869947 may be associated with COPD susceptibility in the Han Chinese population. The FAM13A AA genotype increased serum MMP-12 levels and correlated with emphysema phenotype.
