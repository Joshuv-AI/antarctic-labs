 Abstract (2400 characters max)

An integrated computational materials engineering (ICME) method is in development for rapid thermodynamic
experimental investigation and high-fidelity computational modeling of refractory multi-principal element alloys
(RMPEAs). These ultra-high-temperature (UHT) alloys are of interest for structural applications in extreme
environments, but deficiency of reliable data, especially melting temperatures, impedes the prediction of alloys with
favorable properties. The method leverages UHT capabilities and computational expertise of LLNL’s Materials Science
Division and the McCormack Lab’s UHT conical nozzle levitation (CNL) system to iteratively map the Nb-Ta-Zr phase
space, with focus on the liquidus surface, through targeted experiments selected by quantifying uncertainty in the
thermodynamic model fitting parameters. This method will reduce the time to map uncharted RMPEA phase space and
thereby accelerate discovery and development of advanced materials for applications in extreme environments.

Research Objective 1 is in-progress. The first phase of Milestone 1, to gather existing Nb-Ta-Zr data, construct a
thermodynamic database, and quantify uncertainty in the modeled phase diagram, is near completion. Subsequently,
model parameters will be updated and uncertainty re-quantified iteratively using data from uncertainty-driven,
targeted experiments.

Work for Milestones 2 and 3 focused on equipment training, sample preparation, preliminary testing, and code
development. CNL recalescence studies of pure elements and some randomly-selected alloy samples were conducted
to capture endmember melting points and investigate suitable testing parameters. Code modules to expedite sample
preparation and CNL data analysis were developed with further enhancements planned. Milestone 2 next entails
iterative measurement of alloy melting points and volume expansion using CNL coupled with synchrotron x-ray
diffraction (SXRD). Milestone 3 will focus on solid phase characterization through traditional annealing studies and
temperature-step CNL SXRD studies to chart phase boundaries.

Objective 2 will build on Objective 1 to investigate the effects of C impurities on the thermodynamics of the Nb-Ta-Zr
system. Carbon is unavoidable in manufacturing and many extreme environments, so understanding its impact on
candidate structural alloys is prerequisite to their utilization.

 Narrative (10,000 characters max)

Goals
The goals of this integrated computational and experimental project are to reduce time and cost to investigate the
thermodynamics of ultra-high-temperature refractory multi-principle element alloy systems; improve the fidelity of
thermodynamic databases (TDBs) and the accuracy of downstream model prediction; and ultimately accelerate the
discovery and development of new materials for advanced applications in aerospace, nuclear energy, and space
exploration (Aim 3) in support of LLNL’s Nuclear Enterprise Transformation mission focus and Advanced Materials
Manufacturing core competency. These are under pursuit through development of an integrated computational
materials engineering (ICME) method to iteratively map the Nb-Ta-Zr ternary phase space using the computational
CALPHAD (Calculation of Phase Diagrams) method, then applying uncertainty quantification (UQ) to the model
parameters to inform selection of key compositions for experimental investigations, with focus on mapping the
liquidus surface. The project focuses on two primary Objectives in support of three Aims: Objective 1. Conduct a
high-fidelity thermodynamic assessment of the Nb-Ta-Zr system to develop and evaluate the efficacy of the ICME
method for rapid phase diagram assessment (Aim 1), then Objective 2. Apply the ICME method to investigate effects
of carbon impurities on Nb-Ta-Zr alloy phase stability (Aim 2). These lay the foundation for accelerated materials
discovery and utilization through the method’s application to other ternary and higher-order systems.

Progress
Objective 1. High-Fidelity Thermodynamic Assessment of the Nb-Ta-Zr System
Status of in-progress Objective 1 milestones and minor changes are summarized below. No discontinuation or major
change is planned.

Milestone 1. CALPHAD Assessment

Initial computational modeling is nearing completion. The Nb-Ta-Zr ternary system has been modeled and a TDB
assembled from published assessments of binary subsystems. Data collection and formatting for UQ of the model
parameters to inform experimental investigations is in-progress and has prioritized liquidus data. Experience with
PyCalphad and ESPEI (Extensible Self-optimizing Phase Equilibria Infrastructure) has been gained via an online
workshop, a tutorial, and practice examples. Ad-hoc discussions with computational team members have been
important for gaining insight into and inspiration for further utilizing computational inputs and outputs and will become
regularly scheduled.

Once initial modeling is complete, iterative incorporation of new data and reassessment of the model parameters will
require less time. Modeling work in Year 2 will focus on thermodynamic analysis of the ternary system; selection of
compositions for targeted experimental studies; and method optimization through evaluation of factors most
influential on model parameters and uncertainty.

Literature review has required more time than estimated. Additional time has been needed to locate original sources
of experimental data, some of which had not been digitized and print copies of which are scarce; translate sources
from publication languages; and extract experimental data from plots when measurement values were not reported
elsewise, often requiring scrutiny of automated extraction or manual extraction from distorted figure scans.

Milestone 2. Iterative Liquidus Mapping
Rescalescence experiments to measure alloy melting points and map the Nb-Ta-Zr liquidus surface are underway.
Training to operate the Edmund Buehler MAM-1 plasma arc melter (PAM) has been completed. Proto-samples of a few
randomly selected alloy compositions were fabricated to build skills, intuition, and confidence operating the system.
Each material tends to behave differently in the levitator and under laser heating, so the proto-samples were then
utilized to gain baseline insight into suitable CNL system parameters for alloys of different elemental ratios, i.e. gas
flow rate, laser power output, and power ramp rate.

Some melting point data has been collected on pure element samples, including premiere measurements of pure Zr
using the McCormack Lab’s CNL system. Additional measurements are needed to ensure a statistically meaningful
data population. A brief oxidation study of pure element samples will be conducted to investigate the relationship
between observed melting point depression over subsequent sample heating cycles and sample oxidation in a related
study and mitigate the effect. Results of this study will inform duration and/or heating cycle quantity of future
experiments and if not sufficiently mitigated, used to help account for the effect in analysis.

Feedstock is prepared for mid- and quarter-point binary and ternary samples, but a process change requires training
prior to sample fabrication. Originally, all arc melting was to be carried out using the MAM-1, which is well-suited for
making small samples for CNL experiments. The need for a statistically meaningful data population with minimal
experimental uncertainty, however, calls for many CNL samples of identical composition. The feedstock volume needed
to make a sufficient quantity of samples from a single button exceeds the capacity of the MAM-1, so compositionally
homogeneous alloy buttons will be fabricated using the higher power, larger volume Arcast PAM, divided, and formed
into CNL samples using the MAM-1.

Computational tools to support experimental work are in development. Updates were made to the Python module for
CNL data processing originally developed by the PI in 2024, with planned enhancements to improve support for
multiple component samples. A separate Python module has been developed for easy calculation of alloy sample
feedstock masses based on constituent elements, target composition, target sample size, and desired CNL sample
quantity. Minor usability enhancements are planned.

Equipment downtime and commercial sample availability were two other obstacles encountered. Each equipment
needed for this milestone experienced at least one period of downtime lasting 2-6 weeks for various reasons. Since Nb
and Ta commercial beads have previously been assessed using the CNL system, extensive effort was made to acquire
Zr beads for comparison with fabricated samples, but these are not commercially available.

Annealing studies await results of the initial phase of Milestone 1. Training to operate the Apreo scanning electron
microscope (SEM) has been completed; likewise for sample hot mounting and polishing equipment. Insights into
challenges and considerations related to long-time annealing studies appropriate for refractory alloys have been
gained from LLNL team members.

Objective 2. Effects of C impurities on Nb-Ta-Zr Phase Stability (Year 3)
Application of the in-development ICME method to investigate the effects of carbon on the Nb-Ta-Zr system
thermodynamics awaits results of Objective 1. The proposed work is planned for Year 3 of the project and not
expected to begin until late Year 2 at the earliest, but some relevant literature has been collected alongside that for
Objective 1.

Future Research
Research activities associated with Objective 1 will continue through Year 2 until uncertainty in the CALPHAD model
parameters is reduced to within a reliable margin and the model parameters stabilize against new data incorporation.
Iterative tasks will be repeated a presently unknown number of times to achieve a high-fidelity thermodynamic phase
diagram and thus are scheduled to span the duration of Year 2. However, if high fidelity is achieved prior to the end of
Year 2, work on Objective 2 will proceed ahead of the originally proposed timeline. An estimated timeline of when this
work will be carried out is given.

- Training for Arcast PAM for button fabrication (Mar 26)
- Training and access at UC Berkeley (spring)
- Initial UQ of Calphad parameters from literature data (Mar 26)
- Selection and fabrication of alloy samples based on UQ (Apr, iterative)
- Recalescence experiments to measure melting point (Mar-Apr, iterative)
- Enhancement of CNL and alloy calculation code tools (spring-summer)
- Annealing studies to determine solid phase boundaries (spring, iterative)
- X-ray diffraction (XRD) to determine sample phases (iterative)
- SEM and EDS to characterize microstructures and inspect sample purity and homogeneity (iterative)
- Synchrotron XRD studies to determine solid phase boundaries and measure thermal volume expansion (autumn)
- Reassessment of Calphad parameters and UQ with new experimental data (iterative)
- Assembly of final TDB and construction of high-fidelity Nb-Ta-Zr phase diagram (Feb-Mar 27)
