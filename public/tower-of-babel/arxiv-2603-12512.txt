Byzantine-Robust Optimization under ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -Smoothness
Arman Bolatov, Samuel Horváth, Martin Takáč, Eduard Gorbunov

Abstract
We consider distributed optimization under Byzantine attacks in the presence of ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smoothness, a generalization of standard L L -smoothness that captures functions with state-dependent gradient Lipschitz constants. We propose Byz-NSGDM 1 1 1 During the preparation of the camera-ready version, we became aware of concurrent work by 54 that studies a closely related algorithmic framework and also derives an O ( K − 1 / 4 ) O(K^{-1/4}) convergence rate. We note that all theoretical and experimental results in our paper were obtained independently and prior to our awareness of this work. Their analysis is conducted under the L L -smoothness assumption and assumes homogeneous data across workers, whereas our results hold under the more general ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smoothness condition and accommodate bounded gradient heterogeneity among workers. , a normalized stochastic gradient descent method with momentum that achieves robustness against Byzantine workers while maintaining convergence guarantees. Our algorithm combines momentum normalization with Byzantine-robust aggregation enhanced by Nearest Neighbor Mixing (NNM) to handle both the challenges posed by ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smoothness and Byzantine adversaries. We prove that Byz-NSGDM achieves a convergence rate of O ( K − 1 / 4 ) O(K^{-1/4}) up to a Byzantine bias floor proportional to the robustness coefficient and gradient heterogeneity. Experimental validation on heterogeneous MNIST classification, synthetic ( L 0 , L 1 ) (L_{0},L_{1}) -smooth optimization, and character-level language modeling with a small GPT model demonstrates the effectiveness of our approach against various Byzantine attack strategies. An ablation study further shows that Byz-NSGDM is robust across a wide range of momentum and learning rate choices.

Assumptions and Problem Setup

Problem.

We consider a distributed optimization problem of the form

\min\limits_{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\left\{f(x):=\frac{1}{G}\sum\limits_{i\in\mathcal{G}}f_{i}(x)\right\},

where
\mathcal{G}
is a group of regular workers of size
G:=|\mathcal{G}|
connected with a server in a centralized way, and
f_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}
is the objective/loss function on worker
i
. However, in addition to the regular workers, some Byzantine workers take part in the training. More precisely, we assume that the total number of workers is
n
and
[n]:=\{1,\ldots,n\}=\mathcal{G}\cup\mathcal{B}
,
\mathcal{G}\cap\mathcal{B}=\varnothing
, where
\mathcal{B}
represents the set of Byzantine workers. We assume that
B:=|\mathcal{B}|\leq\delta n
for some
\delta\leq\nicefrac{{1}}{{2}}
. We do not make any further assumptions on the clients from
\mathcal{B}
, i.e., they can arbitrarily deviate from the optimization protocol, collude, and even know the updates that other clients send to the server.

Assumptions.

We state the standing assumptions and the robustness notion used throughout.

Assumption 1

(Lower boundedness)
.

We assume that
f
is lower bounded by
f^{*}>-\infty
and each local objective
f_{i}
is lower bounded by
f_{i}^{*}>-\infty
for any
i\in\mathcal{G}
.

Assumption 2

(
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness
[
58
,
57
,
12
]
)
.

For all
i\in\mathcal{G}
, the function
f_{i}
is
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth with
L_{0},L_{1}>0
: for all
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(y)\|\;\leq\;\Big(L_{0}+L_{1}\sup_{u\in[x,y]}\|\nabla f_{i}(u)\|\Big)\,\|x-y\|.

Assumption 3

(Unbiased oracle with bounded variance)
.

Each good worker
i\in\mathcal{G}
has access to an unbiased stochastic first-order oracle: for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, worker
i
can compute
\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(x)
such that

\mathbb{E}[\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(x)]\;=\;\nabla f_{i}(x),\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(x)\|^{2}\big]\;\leq\;\sigma^{2}.

Assumption 4

(Gradient heterogeneity)
.

The gradient heterogeneity across good workers is bounded for all
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\mathbb{E}_{i\sim\mathcal{G}}\big[\|\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f(x)\|^{2}\big]\;\leq\;\zeta^{2}.

(1)

Definition 1

(Byzantine-robust aggregator)
.

Let
\kappa\geq 0
. An aggregation rule
\texttt{ARAgg}:\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust if for any vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
with at most
B\leq\delta n
Byzantine workers (so
G=n-B\geq(1-\delta)n>\nicefrac{{n}}{{2}}
good workers) and
\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}v_{i}
,

\big\|\texttt{ARAgg}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})-\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}\big\|\;\leq\;\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\big\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}\big\|.

We refer to
\kappa
as the robustness coefficient.

The above definition is a modification of the one proposed by
5
. The key difference is in the usage of a linear (non-squared) bound on the right-hand side, which leads to different values of
\kappa
but is more natural for our analysis under
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness. Many standard Byzantine-robust aggregation rules satisfy this definition, including geometric median (RFA) and coordinate-wise median, as well as their composition with NNM. We provide explicit formulas for the robustness coefficient
\kappa
for these aggregators in Appendix
C
.

Related Work

Our work lies at the intersection of Byzantine-robust distributed optimization and optimization under generalized smoothness. We review the relevant literature in both areas.

Byzantine-robust distributed optimization.

The problem of Byzantine-robust distributed learning has received considerable attention. Early works established fundamental aggregation rules: Krum and Multi-Krum
[
9
,
15
]
select gradients based on distance metrics, geometric median (RFA)
[
42
]
provides a robust centroid estimator, and coordinate-wise median/trimmed mean
[
56
,
11
]
offer dimension-wise robustness. These methods provide statistical guarantees under i.i.d. data but suffer from bias terms that grow with data heterogeneity
[
56
,
11
]
. However, simply applying robust aggregation is insufficient: carefully crafted attacks defeat many standard defenses
[
7
,
53
]
, and
28
show that permutation-invariant schemes cannot guarantee convergence against arbitrary Byzantines without additional structure.

To address heterogeneity and improve robustness, several complementary approaches have emerged. Structural methods include
bucketing

[
29
]
, which partitions workers into homogeneous groups before aggregation, and
nearest neighbor mixing (NNM)

[
5
]
, which averages updates with nearest neighbors to contract heterogeneity. Recent theoretical analyses
[
6
,
5
]
characterize optimal error bounds and breakdown points under data heterogeneity. Crucially, achieving tight convergence guarantees requires refined algorithmic techniques beyond robust aggregation alone. Variance reduction methods
[
19
,
52
,
17
]
incorporate control variates to suppress stochastic noise that Byzantine workers exploit, achieving faster rates under weaker assumptions. Client momentum techniques
[
28
,
29
,
21
]
accumulate worker updates over time, reducing variance of honest clients and exposing Byzantine perturbations that are undetectable in single rounds but accumulate over iterations. These works demonstrate that stabilizing local estimators before applying robust aggregation is crucial for Byzantine resilience.

(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness in optimization.

The
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness assumption was introduced by
59
to capture the empirical observation that the local smoothness constant along training trajectories of deep learning models often scales linearly with the gradient norm. Formally, for twice differentiable functions, this condition requires
\|\nabla^{2}f(x)\|_{2}\leq L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f(x)\|
, which strictly generalizes classical
L
-smoothness (recovered when
L_{1}=0
). Functions satisfying
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness but not
L
-smoothness include
f(x)=\|x\|^{2n}
for
n\geq 2
,
f(x)=\exp(\langle a,x\rangle)
, and more generally, objectives with polynomially growing gradients
[
59
,
12
]
.

In the nonconvex setting,
59
showed that gradient clipping achieves complexity
O(\max\{L_{0}\Delta/\varepsilon^{2},(1+L_{1}^{2})\Delta/L_{0}\})
for finding
\varepsilon
-stationary points, where the dominant term is independent of
L_{1}
. This result was extended to momentum-based clipping
[
57
]
, normalized gradient descent
[
60
,
12
]
, SignGD
[
13
]
, and adaptive methods like AdaGrad and Adam
[
16
,
51
,
50
]
. In the convex case,
31
and
20
derived convergence rates for clipped gradient descent, accelerated methods, and adaptive stepsizes. Notably, recent works
[
20
,
49
]
provide bounds that avoid exponentially large factors in
L_{1}R_{0}
that plagued earlier analyses in the convex case. Normalized gradient descent with momentum
[
24
,
30
]
achieves stable convergence by controlling step magnitudes, making it well-suited for
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth problems.

However, all existing work on
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth optimization assumes benign (non-Byzantine) settings. Our work is the first to address Byzantine-robust optimization under
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness, combining normalized gradient descent with momentum and robust aggregation to handle both challenges simultaneously.

Convergence Analysis

Our main theoretical result establishes the convergence rate of
Byz-NSGDM
under Byzantine attacks. Full proofs are deferred to the supplementary materials.

Theorem 1

(Convergence of
Byz-NSGDM
)
.

Let Assumptions
1
–
4
hold and let the server use a
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust aggregator. Run Algorithm
1
with

\gamma_{k}\equiv\gamma=\frac{\gamma_{0}}{(K+1)^{3/4}},\qquad\eta_{k}\equiv\eta=\frac{1}{(K+1)^{1/2}},

where

\gamma_{0}\leq\min\left\{\frac{1}{2L_{1}},\frac{(K+1)^{1/4}}{\sqrt{32}L_{1}},\frac{1}{\sqrt{128(1+2\kappa)}L_{1}}\right\}.

(2)

Define
\Delta^{*}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})
and
V_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}\big[\|v_{i}^{0}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|\big]
. Then

\displaystyle\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|\big]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{1/2}\big(f(x^{0})-f^{*}\big)}{\gamma_{0}(K+1)^{1/4}}+\frac{2e^{1/2}(1+2\kappa)V_{0}}{(K+1)^{1/2}}+4\kappa\zeta

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2L_{0}\gamma_{0}}{(K+1)^{3/4}}+\frac{8(1+2\kappa)L_{0}\gamma_{0}}{(K+1)^{1/4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8L_{1}^{2}\Delta^{*}\gamma_{0}}{(K+1)^{3/4}}+\frac{32(1+2\kappa)L_{1}^{2}\Delta^{*}\gamma_{0}}{(K+1)^{1/4}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{2\sigma}{\sqrt{G}(K+1)^{1/4}}+\frac{4\kappa\sigma}{(K+1)^{1/4}}.

Remark 2

(Fixed-horizon schedule)
.

The parameters
\gamma
and
\eta
depend on the total number of iterations
K
but remain constant across iterations. This is a standard “fixed-horizon” choice common in convergence analyses. It can be converted to a diminishing schedule that does not require knowledge of
K
in advance via standard doubling tricks
[
22
]
.

Corollary 1
.

Under the conditions of Theorem
1
, all terms except
4\kappa\zeta
decay polynomially with
K
. Hence,

\min_{0\leq k\leq K}\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|\big]\;\leq\;O\!\left(\frac{1}{K^{1/4}}\right)+4\kappa\zeta.

The term
4\kappa\zeta
is the irreducible Byzantine bias floor—a fundamental limit arising from the interaction between Byzantine fraction (through
\kappa
) and data heterogeneity (
\zeta
). In homogeneous settings (
\zeta=0
), this bias vanishes entirely.

Proof roadmap.

Our analysis proceeds in three steps:

1.

Descent under normalization (Lemma
5
).
We show that the normalized update
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma_{k}\frac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}
yields a descent inequality:

f(x^{k+1})\leq f(x^{k})-\gamma_{k}\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|+2\gamma_{k}\|v^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})\|+O(\gamma_{k}^{2}),

where progress depends on
\|\nabla f(x^{k})\|
minus an error term from the momentum-to-gradient gap
\|v^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})\|
. The normalization ensures the step size is bounded by
\gamma_{k}
, preventing the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness from causing divergence.

2.

Bounding the momentum error (Lemmas
4
and
7
).
We decompose this gap into two parts: (i) the averaging error
\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k})\|
from honest workers’ momentum tracking (Lemma
4
), and (ii) the Byzantine-induced error
\|v^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|
controlled by the
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust aggregator (Lemma
7
).

3.

Telescoping.
Summing the descent inequalities with carefully chosen exponential weights absorbs the coupling between function values and momentum errors, yielding the final rate.

The key insight is that normalization step
x^{k+1}=x^{k}-\gamma_{k}\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}
bounds the step size by
\gamma_{k}
, preventing the
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness from causing divergence, while the robust aggregator limits Byzantine influence to a bias floor of
O(\kappa\zeta)
.

Remark 3
.

Following the analysis of
NSGDM
by
23
, our convergence proof can be extended to the setting of heavy-tailed stochastic noise
[
59
,
46
]
. In particular, Assumption
3
can be relaxed to the weaker

\mathbb{E}[\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(x)]=\nabla f_{i}(x),\qquad\mathbb{E}\big[\|\nabla f_{\xi_{i}}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(x)\|^{p}\big]\leq\sigma^{p},

for some
p\in(1,2]
.

Under this assumption, Lemma

must be adapted using arguments analogous to those in Lemma 16 of
23
. The remainder of the convergence analysis then proceeds along the same lines as in the current proof, with appropriate modifications to the stepsize
\gamma
and momentum parameter
\eta
to account for the heavier-tailed noise.

Experiments

We evaluate
Byz-NSGDM
on three distinct problems: a heterogeneous distributed learning task on MNIST, character-level language modeling, and a synthetic
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth optimization problem designed to validate our theoretical framework.

6.1
Attack Strategies

We evaluate our algorithm against several Byzantine attack strategies that represent different threat models in distributed learning, following common setups in prior work on non-IID Byzantine robustness
[
29
,
5
]
.

Bit/Sign Flipping (BF).

Byzantine workers send the negation of their computed gradient:
g_{i}^{k}=-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})
for
i\in\mathcal{B}
. This attack directly opposes the optimization direction and tests the algorithm’s ability to handle adversarial gradients
[
56
,
9
]
.

Label Flipping (LF).

For classification tasks, Byzantine workers flip the labels of their training data before computing gradients. Each sample with true label
y
is trained with label
(y+c)\bmod C
, where
C
is the number of classes. For MNIST with 10 classes, we use a shift of
c=5
. This creates consistent but misaligned gradient directions and is widely used to evaluate robustness under data poisoning and non-IID heterogeneity
[
29
]
.

Mimic Attack.

Byzantine workers exploit data heterogeneity by initially behaving honestly for a warmup period of 50 iterations, then strategically deviating. After warmup, they send
g_{i}^{k}=-2\bar{g}^{k}
, where
\bar{g}^{k}
is the average of honest gradients, aiming to reverse progress while avoiding early detection; see
[
5
]
for analysis of mixing defenses against such strategies.

ALIE (A Little Is Enough).

Byzantine workers craft gradients to maximize damage while avoiding outlier detection. They send
v_{i}^{k}=\bar{v}^{k}+z\cdot\sigma^{k}
, where
\bar{v}^{k}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}v_{i}^{k}
and
\sigma^{k}
is the vector of coordinate-wise standard deviations of
\{v_{i}^{k}\}_{i\in\mathcal{G}}

[
7
]
. We use
z=1
in our experiments.

Synthetic
(L_{0},L_{1})

We minimize the quartic objective:

7
Conclusion

In this work, we analyzed the behavior of Byzantine-robust normalized stochastic gradient descent with momentum (
Byz-NSGDM
) under the generalized
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smoothness assumption. While normalized momentum combined with robust aggregation has been studied under classical
L
-smoothness and homogeneous settings, its theoretical guarantees under state-dependent smoothness and gradient heterogeneity were previously not understood. Our results provide a convergence analysis in this more general regime.

We established a non-asymptotic convergence rate of
O(K^{-1/4})
up to an explicit Byzantine bias floor that depends on the robustness coefficient of the aggregation rule and the level of gradient heterogeneity. This characterization clarifies how generalized smoothness, heterogeneity, and adversarial corruption jointly affect the attainable accuracy.

Our empirical results on heterogeneous MNIST classification, synthetic
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth optimization, and character-level language modeling demonstrate that normalization combined with robust aggregation provides stable and competitive performance across diverse Byzantine attack strategies and aggregation rules. The experiments further highlight the practical robustness of the method across a wide range of hyperparameter choices.

Overall, our study bridges Byzantine-robust distributed optimization and optimization under generalized smoothness, and provides theoretical and empirical evidence that normalized momentum remains effective in adversarial and heterogeneous
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth settings. Future work includes extending the analysis to adaptive step-size schemes, tightening the bias floor characterization, and understanding optimal complexity limits under generalized smoothness and adversarial noise.

Lemma 1

(Lemma 1 from
30
)
.

Let
f_{i}
be
(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth for
i\in\mathcal{G}
. Then the following properties hold:

1.

Gradient Lipschitz bound:

\|\nabla f_{i}(x)-\nabla f_{i}(y)\|\leq(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f_{i}(y)\|)\exp(L_{1}\|x-y\|)\|x-y\|.

(3)

2.

Function value bound:

f_{i}(y)\leq f_{i}(x)+\left\langle\nabla f_{i}(x),y-x\right\rangle+\frac{L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|}{2}\exp(L_{1}\|x-y\|)\|y-x\|^{2}.

3.

Gradient norm bound:

\frac{\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|^{2}}{4(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|)}\leq f_{i}(x)-f_{i}^{*}.

(4)

4.

Average function smoothness:

f(y)\leq f(x)+\left\langle\nabla f(x),y-x\right\rangle+\frac{L_{0}+\frac{L_{1}}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|}{2}\exp(L_{1}\|x-y\|)\|y-x\|^{2}.

(5)

Lemma 2

(Lemma 2 from
30
)
.

Under Assumptions
1
and
2
, for any
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
:

\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|\leq 8L_{1}(f(x)-f^{*})+\frac{8L_{1}}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})+\frac{L_{0}}{L_{1}}.

(6)

Lemma 3
.

Let Assumptions
2
,
3
,
4
hold.
For each good worker
i\in\mathcal{G}
with momentum update

v_{i}^{k}=(1-\eta)v_{i}^{k-1}+\eta\nabla f_{\xi_{i}^{k-1}}(x^{k-1}),

consider normalized steps
\|x^{k}-x^{k-1}\|=\gamma
(i.e.,
\gamma_{k}\equiv\gamma>0
in Algorithm
1
) and a constant momentum parameter
\eta\in(0,1]
. Then:

1.

Bias.
For all
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[v_{i}^{k}\big|x^{k-1}\right]=(1-\eta)v_{i}^{k-1}+\eta\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1}).

2.

Individual momentum tracking error.
For all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|v_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})\|\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{k}\mathbb{E}\|v_{i}^{0}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|+\frac{2L_{0}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}

\displaystyle\quad+8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-1-t}\mathbb{E}\big[f(x^{t})-f^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}\big(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*}\big)+\sqrt{\eta}\sigma.

(7)

3.

Cross-worker momentum difference.
Let the expectation be over the stochastic gradients and an independent uniform draw of
i,j\in\mathcal{G}
. Then, for all
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|\big]

\displaystyle\leq 2\zeta+2(1-\eta)^{k}V_{0}+2\sqrt{\eta}\sigma+\frac{4L_{0}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}

\displaystyle\quad+16L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-1-t}\mathbb{E}\big[f(x^{t})-f^{*}\big]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{16L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}\Delta^{*},

(8)

where
V_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}\|v_{i}^{0}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|
and
\Delta^{*}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})
.

Proof.

The proof of these lemmas follows a very similar structure to that of the lemmas in
[
30
]
.

Part 1.
This follows directly from the unbiased oracle in Assumption
3
:

\mathbb{E}[\nabla f_{\xi_{i}^{k-1}}(x^{k-1})\mid x^{k-1}]=\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})
.

Part 2.

Define the tracking error
H_{i}^{k}:=v_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})
and the auxiliary terms

U_{i}^{k-1}:=\nabla f_{\xi_{i}^{k-1}}(x^{k-1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1}),\qquad G_{i}^{k}:=\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k}),

where we also set
x^{-1}:=0
, yielding
G_{i}^{0}=0
. From the momentum update
v_{i}^{k}=(1-\eta)v_{i}^{k-1}+\eta\nabla f_{\xi_{i}^{k-1}}(x^{k-1})
, we have

H_{i}^{k}=(1-\eta)H_{i}^{k-1}+(1-\eta)G_{i}^{k-1}+\eta U_{i}^{k-1}.

(9)

Unrolling gives

H_{i}^{k}=(1-\eta)^{k+1}H_{i}^{0}+\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k+1-t}G_{i}^{t}+\eta\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k-t}U_{i}^{t}.

Taking norms, triangle inequality, and expectations,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|H_{i}^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{k+1}\mathbb{E}\|H_{i}^{0}\|+\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k+1-t}\mathbb{E}\|G_{i}^{t}\|+\eta\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k-t}U_{i}^{t}\Big\|.

(10)

By Lemma
1
(see (
3
)) with
\|x^{t}-x^{t-1}\|=\gamma
,

\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t-1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\|\leq\big(L_{0}+L_{1}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{t-1})\|\big)e^{\gamma L_{1}}\gamma.

From Lemma
1
(see (
4
)) and the same algebra as Lemma 2 in
30
applied to each
f_{i}
,

\|\nabla f_{i}(x)\|\leq 8L_{1}\big(f_{i}(x)-f_{i}^{*}\big)+\frac{L_{0}}{L_{1}}\leq 8L_{1}\big(f(x)-f^{*}\big)+8L_{1}\big(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*}\big)+\frac{L_{0}}{L_{1}}.

We obtain

\mathbb{E}\|G_{i}^{t}\|\leq e^{\gamma L_{1}}\gamma\left(2L_{0}+8L_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}[f(x^{t})-f^{*}]+8L_{1}^{2}(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})\right).

Summing the geometric weights
\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k+1-t}\leq\eta^{-1}
yields the three deterministic terms in (
7
).

By Jensen and Cauchy–Schwarz,

\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k-t}U_{i}^{t}\Big\|\leq\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{2(k-t)}\mathbb{E}\|U_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\Big)^{1/2}.

The cross terms vanish since the oracle is unbiased and adapted to the filtration

\{\mathcal{F}_{t}\}
: for
s<t
,

\mathbb{E}\big[\langle U_{i}^{s},U_{i}^{t}\rangle\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle U_{i}^{s},\mathbb{E}[U_{i}^{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\rangle\big]=0.

Using
\mathbb{E}\|U_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\sigma^{2}
and summing the geometric series gives

\leq\sigma/\sqrt{2\eta-\eta^{2}}\leq\sigma/\sqrt{\eta}
, so the prefactor
\eta
in
(
10
) produces the term
\sqrt{\eta}\sigma
.
This finishes Part 2.

Part 3.

By the triangle inequality,

\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|\leq\|H_{i}^{k}\|+\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})-\nabla f_{j}(x^{k})\|+\|H_{j}^{k}\|.

Taking expectations, using (
1
) and Jensen gives

\mathbb{E}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k})-\nabla f_{j}(x^{k})\|\leq 2\zeta
; applying the bound of Part 2 to

H_{i}^{k}
and
H_{j}^{k}
and averaging over
i,j
replaces
(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})
by

\Delta^{*}
and
\mathbb{E}\|H_{i}^{0}\|
by
V_{0}
, yielding (
8
).
∎

Lemma 4

(Average momentum error)
.

Let
\bar{v}^{k}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}v_{i}^{k}
and assume the setting of Lemma
3
with constant
\eta
and normalized steps of size
\gamma
. Then for
k\geq 1
,

\displaystyle A_{k}:=\mathbb{E}\big[\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|\big]

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{k}V_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{\eta}\sigma}{\sqrt{G}}+\frac{2L_{0}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}

\displaystyle\quad+8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-1-t}\mathbb{E}[f(x^{t})-f^{*}]

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}\Delta^{*}.

(11)

Here
V_{0}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}\|v_{i}^{0}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{0})\|
and
\Delta^{*}:=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(f^{*}-f_{i}^{*})
.

Proof.

Let
H_{i}^{k}:=v_{i}^{k}-\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})
and define the averages

\bar{H}^{k}:=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}H_{i}^{k},\ \bar{G}^{t}:=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(\nabla f_{i}(x^{t-1})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})),\ \bar{U}^{t}:=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\big(\nabla f_{\xi_{i}^{t}}(x^{t})-\nabla f_{i}(x^{t})\big).

Averaging the per-worker recursion (
9
) gives

\bar{H}^{k}=(1-\eta)\bar{H}^{k-1}+(1-\eta)\bar{G}^{k-1}+\eta\bar{U}^{k-1}.

Unrolling, taking norms, and expectations as in Lemma
3
(Part 2),

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\|\bar{H}^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq(1-\eta)^{k}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{H}^{0}\|+\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-t}\mathbb{E}\|\bar{G}^{t}\|+\eta\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-1-t}\bar{U}^{t}\Big\|.

For
\mathbb{E}\|\bar{G}^{t}\|
, apply Lemma
1
(see (
3
)) and the same one-point bound used in Lemma
3
to get

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{G}^{t}\|\leq e^{\gamma L_{1}}\gamma\left(2L_{0}+8L_{1}^{2}\mathbb{E}[f(x^{t})-f^{*}]+8L_{1}^{2}\Delta^{*}\right).

For the noise term, independence across workers and unbiasedness give

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{U}^{t}\|^{2}=\mathbb{E}\Big\|\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i}U_{i}^{t}\Big\|^{2}=\frac{1}{G^{2}}\sum_{i}\mathbb{E}\|U_{i}^{t}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\sigma^{2}}{G}.

As in Part 2, cross terms in time vanish by the short argument

\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\bar{U}^{s},\bar{U}^{t}\rangle\big]=\mathbb{E}\big[\langle\bar{U}^{s},\mathbb{E}[\bar{U}^{t}\mid\mathcal{F}_{t}]\rangle\big]=0\quad(s<t),

so

\mathbb{E}\Big\|\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k-t}\bar{U}^{t}\Big\|\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{G}}\Big(\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}(1-\eta)^{2t}\Big)^{1/2}\leq\frac{\sigma}{\sqrt{G\eta}}.

Putting these together and using
\sum_{t=0}^{k}(1-\eta)^{k+1-t}\leq\eta^{-1}
yields

\mathbb{E}\|\bar{H}^{k}\|\leq(1-\eta)^{k}V_{0}+\frac{\sqrt{\eta}\sigma}{\sqrt{G}}+\frac{2L_{0}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}+8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}\sum_{t=0}^{k-1}(1-\eta)^{k-1-t}\mathbb{E}[f(x^{t})-f^{*}]+\frac{8L_{1}^{2}\gamma e^{\gamma L_{1}}}{\eta}\Delta^{*}.

Finally, note that
\bar{H}^{k}=\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})
, which gives (
11
).
∎

Lemma 5

(Descent Lemma for
Byz-NSGDM
)
.

Let
f(x)=\tfrac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}f_{i}(x)
with each
f_{i}

(L_{0},L_{1})
-smooth, and let the server update

x^{k}=x^{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}\frac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\qquad(\gamma_{k-1}>0).

Then

\displaystyle f(x^{k})

\displaystyle\leq f(x^{k-1})-\gamma_{k-1}\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|+2\gamma_{k-1}\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma_{k-1}^{2}}{2}\exp(\gamma_{k-1}L_{1})\left(L_{0}+\frac{L_{1}}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})\|\right).

Proof.

Apply Lemma
1
(average smoothness, (
5
)) with

x=x^{k-1}
and
y=x^{k}=x^{k-1}-\gamma_{k-1}\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}
:

\displaystyle f(x^{k})

\displaystyle\leq f(x^{k-1})+\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k-1}),-\gamma_{k-1}\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle+\frac{\gamma_{k-1}^{2}}{2}\exp(\gamma_{k-1}L_{1})\left(L_{0}+\frac{L_{1}}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|\nabla f_{i}(x^{k-1})\|\right).

(12)

It remains to lower bound the linear term
\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k-1}),\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle
.
Write

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k-1}),\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k},\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle+\left\langle v^{k},\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|+\|v^{k}\|\qquad\text{(Cauchy--Schwarz)}

\displaystyle\geq-\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|+\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|-\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|\quad\text{(triangle ineq.)}

\displaystyle=\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|-2\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|.

Therefore

-\gamma_{k-1}\left\langle\nabla f(x^{k-1}),\tfrac{v^{k}}{\|v^{k}\|}\right\rangle\leq-\gamma_{k-1}\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|+2\gamma_{k-1}\|\nabla f(x^{k-1})-v^{k}\|.

Substituting this bound into (
12
) gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 6

(Descent with curvature bound)
.

Let
\{x^{k}\}
be generated by Algorithm
1
and Assumption

2
hold. Then for
k\geq 1

\displaystyle f(x^{k})

Proof.

Lemma 7

(Byzantine Aggregation Error Decomposition)
.

Let
\bar{v}^{k}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}v_{i}^{k}
be the average momentum of good workers and
v^{k}=\texttt{ARAgg}(v_{1}^{k},\ldots,v_{n}^{k})
be the output of a
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust aggregator. Then for
k\geq 1
:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|v^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|]+\kappa\mathbb{E}_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|],

where
\mathbb{E}_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|]=\frac{1}{G^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|]
.

Proof.

Using the triangle inequality:

\|v^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|\leq\|v^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|+\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|.

Applying the
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust aggregator property:

\|v^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|.

For each
i\in\mathcal{G}
:

\displaystyle\|v_{i}^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|

\displaystyle=\left\|v_{i}^{k}-\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}}v_{j}^{k}\right\|=\left\|\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}}(v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k})\right\|\leq\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|.

Therefore:

\displaystyle\|v^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\frac{1}{G}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|=\frac{\kappa}{G^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|.

Taking expectation and combining with the triangle inequality:

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\|v^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}[\|v^{k}-\bar{v}^{k}\|]+\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{G^{2}}\sum_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}\mathbb{E}[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|]+\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|]

\displaystyle=\kappa\mathbb{E}_{i,j\in\mathcal{G}}[\|v_{i}^{k}-v_{j}^{k}\|]+\mathbb{E}[\|\bar{v}^{k}-\nabla f(x^{k-1})\|].

∎

C.1
Nearest Neighbor Mixing (NNM) and Robustness

NNM operator.

Given

Assumption 5

(Bounded leverage of the good cluster)

There exists a constant

\max_{j\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{j}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\frac{C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

in the subsequent analysis. In practice, when the good workers are well-clustered,

For a good pivot

\alpha_{i}:=\frac{|\mathcal{N}_{x_{i}}\setminus\mathcal{G}|}{G}=\frac{|\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{x_{i}}|}{G}\in[0,1/2).

Since at most

Lemma 8

.

For any pivot
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

\mathcal{N}_{\mu}
be the set of indices of the

Proof.

Since
|\mathcal{N}_{\mu}|=|\mathcal{G}|=G

|\mathcal{N}_{\mu}\setminus\mathcal{G}|=|\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mu}|

\mathcal{G}\setminus\mathcal{N}_{\mu}

Summing over all

yields (

∎

Lemma 9

(General Pivot Bound)
.

For any pivot
\mu\in\mathbb{R}^{d}

Write

Applying Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{4\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|,

(17)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|y_{i}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

(18)

Proof.

For (
17
), since
\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}=\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}y_{i}
, by triangle inequality:

\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|=\left\|\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}(y_{i}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}})\right\|\leq\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|y_{i}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

Applying Lemma
10
yields (
17
).

For (
18
), by the triangle inequality:

\|y_{i}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\|y_{i}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|+\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

Averaging over
i\in\mathcal{G}
and applying Lemma
10
and (
17
):

\frac{1}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|y_{i}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\frac{4\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|+\frac{4\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|=\frac{8\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

∎

Lemma 12

(Robustness after NNM)
.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
be
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust in the sense of Definition
1
.
Under Assumption
5
, for any
x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}
,

\big\|F\big(\texttt{NNM}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})\big)-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\big\|\leq\frac{\kappa^{\prime}}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|,

where
\kappa^{\prime}=(8\kappa+4)\alpha C
and
\alpha\leq B/(n-B)
.

Proof.

Let
(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})=\texttt{NNM}(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
. By the triangle inequality:

\|F(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\|F(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|+\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

By the
(\delta,\kappa)
-robustness of
F
(Definition
1
):

\|F(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|y_{i}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

Applying Lemma
11
:

\displaystyle\|F(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|y_{i}-\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}\|+\|\bar{y}_{\mathcal{G}}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{8\kappa\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|+\frac{4\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{(8\kappa+4)\alpha C}{G}\sum_{t\in\mathcal{G}}\|x_{t}-\bar{x}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

∎

Remark 6
.

The composition of NNM with a
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust aggregator
F
yields a robust aggregator with constant
\kappa^{\prime}=(8\kappa+4)\alpha C
, where
\alpha\leq B/(n-B)
measures the fraction of Byzantine contamination. When
B\ll n
, this additional factor is small, showing that NNM preprocessing preserves robustness up to a multiplicative constant depending on the Byzantine fraction and the leverage constant
C
.

C.2
Geometric Median

The Geometric Median of
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, denoted by
\text{GM}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, is defined as a vector that minimizes the sum of the
\ell_{2}
-distances to these vectors:

\text{GM}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\in\argmin_{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\|y-v_{i}\|.

Proposition 1

(Geometric Median Robustness)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
such that
B=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor<\frac{n}{2}
.
The Geometric Median is
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust with

\kappa=2\left(1+\frac{B}{n-2B}\right).

Proof.

The proof follows the same approach as in
[
5
]
but stops before squaring both sides to match our linear robustness definition.

Let
v^{*}:=\text{GM}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
be the geometric median of the input vectors. Consider any subset
S\subseteq[n]
of size
|S|=\lfloor(1-\delta)n\rfloor=n-B
. By the reverse triangle inequality, for any
i\in S
, we have

\|v^{*}-v_{i}\|\geq|\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\||.

Similarly, for any
i\in[n]\setminus S
, we obtain

\|v^{*}-v_{i}\|\geq|\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\||.

Summing over all input vectors and using the fact that
|[n]\setminus S|\leq B
:

\displaystyle\sum_{i\in[n]}\|v^{*}-v_{i}\|

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i\in S}(|\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\||)+\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus S}(|\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\||)

\displaystyle\geq(n-2B)\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|+\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|.

Rearranging terms:

\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\|\leq\frac{1}{n-2B}\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\|v^{*}-v_{i}\|+\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|\right).

By the definition of the geometric median:

\sum_{i\in[n]}\|v^{*}-v_{i}\|\leq\sum_{i\in[n]}\|\bar{v}_{S}-v_{i}\|.

Therefore:

\displaystyle\|v^{*}-\bar{v}_{S}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{n-2B}\left(\sum_{i\in[n]}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|+\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|-\sum_{i\in[n]\setminus S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{n-2B}\left(2\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|\right)=\frac{2}{n-2B}\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|

\displaystyle=\frac{2(n-B)}{(n-2B)|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|=2\left(1+\frac{B}{n-2B}\right)\frac{1}{|S|}\sum_{i\in S}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{S}\|.

This establishes the desired robustness property with
\kappa=2\left(1+\frac{B}{n-2B}\right)
.
∎

C.3
Coordinate-wise Median

For input vectors
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, their coordinate-wise median, denoted by
\text{CWMed}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})
, is defined as a vector whose
k
-th coordinate is:

\left[\text{CWMed}(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\right]_{k}:=\text{Median}\left([v_{1}]_{k},\ldots,[v_{n}]_{k}\right).

Lemma 13

(Coordinate-wise Robustness)
.

Assume that
F:\mathbb{R}^{d\times n}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a coordinate-wise aggregation function, i.e., there exist
d
real-valued functions
F_{1},\ldots,F_{d}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
such that for all
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\left[F(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})\right]_{k}=F_{k}([v_{1}]_{k},\ldots,[v_{n}]_{k})
.
If for each
k\in[d]
,
F_{k}
is
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust (in the sense of Definition
1
applied to scalars), then
F
is
(\delta,\kappa\sqrt{d})
-robust.

Proof.

Since each
F_{k}
is
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust, for any subset
\mathcal{G}
of good workers with
|\mathcal{G}|=G\geq(1-\delta)n
:

|[F(v_{1},\ldots,v_{n})]_{k}-[\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}]_{k}|\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}|[v_{i}]_{k}-[\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}]_{k}|.

Summing over all coordinates:

\sum_{k=1}^{d}|[F(v)]_{k}-[\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}]_{k}|\leq\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\sum_{k=1}^{d}|[v_{i}]_{k}-[\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}]_{k}|=\frac{\kappa}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}\|_{1}.

Using
\|a\|_{2}\leq\|a\|_{1}
on the left and
\|a\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{d}\|a\|_{2}
on the right:

\|F(v)-\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}\|\leq\frac{\kappa\sqrt{d}}{G}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{G}}\|v_{i}-\bar{v}_{\mathcal{G}}\|.

∎

Proposition 2

(Coordinate-wise Median Robustness)
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\delta<\frac{1}{2}
such that
B=\lfloor\delta n\rfloor<\frac{n}{2}
.
The Coordinate-wise Median is
(\delta,\kappa)
-robust with

\kappa=2\sqrt{d}\left(1+\frac{B}{n-2B}\right),

where
d
is the dimension of the input vectors.

Proof.

Since geometric median coincides with median for one-dimensional inputs, it follows from Proposition
1
that for one-dimensional inputs, median is
(\delta,\kappa_{0})
-robust with
\kappa_{0}=2\left(1+\frac{B}{n-2B}\right)
. Since coordinate-wise median is a coordinate-wise aggregation function, we apply Lemma
13
to obtain that the
d
-dimensional coordinate-wise median is
(\delta,\kappa_{0}\sqrt{d})
-robust, giving the stated bound.
∎

Remark 7
.

The
\sqrt{d}
factor arises from converting between
\ell_{1}
and
\ell_{2}
norms when combining per-coordinate bounds into a vector bound. This dimension dependence is a known limitation of coordinate-wise aggregation rules under linear robustness definitions. In contrast, the geometric median (Proposition
1
) achieves dimension-independent robustness because it operates directly on the full vectors.
