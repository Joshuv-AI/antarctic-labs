The Tentacles Landscape
Pablo Groisman

Abstract
Zhang and Strogatz [Phys. Rev. Lett. 127 , 194101 (2021)] used high-dimensional simulations to argue that basins of attraction in the Kuramoto ring are octopus-like: their volume scales as e − k ​ q 2 e^{-kq^{2}} in the winding number q q , nearly all of it concentrated in filamentary tentacles rather than near the attractor. They conjecture this geometry to be common in high dimensions but note that high-dimensional simulations are unreliable. We prove every feature of the octopus picture rigorously for identical oscillators on a ring coupled by any smooth odd function strictly increasing on ( − π , π ) (-\pi,\pi) .



Introduction

Basins of attraction are fundamental to the analysis of multistable dynamical systems
12
;
1
;
14
: they determine which initial conditions lead to which long-term outcomes. In low-dimensional systems, basins can often be visualized and their structure understood analytically. In high-dimensional systems, however, basic quantitative properties — the volumes of individual basins and the geometry of their boundaries — remain poorly understood. A central difficulty is that high-dimensional spaces come with many “curses”
3
: numerical sampling of state space becomes astronomically expensive and geometric approximations that work in low dimensions can fail catastrophically
20
. In addition, our mathematical intuition fails miserably
17
when the dimension is high.

The Kuramoto model on a cycle graph provides one of the cleanest and most
studied settings for this problem
19
;
20
;
2
.
For
n
identical oscillators with nearest-neighbor coupling,

\dot{\theta}_{i}=\sin(\theta_{i+1}-\theta_{i})+\sin(\theta_{i-1}-\theta_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

(1)

with periodic boundary conditions, the attractors are the
q
-twisted
states

\theta_{j}=2\pi jq/n+c
, in which the phases wind
q
times around
the ring. Understanding how the basins of these states partition the

n
-dimensional state space
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is a problem with direct implications in science and technology, including power grid stability
11
, training of deep neural networks
9
, and other networked
oscillator systems
4
.

In a foundational paper, Wiley, Strogatz, and Girvan
19

conjectured that the basin sizes follow a Gaussian law: the probability of
converging to the
q
-twisted state scales as
e^{-kq^{2}}
.
This was later challenged by Delabays, Tyloo, and Jacquod
2
,
who estimated basin sizes via local measurements near each attractor and
reported exponential scaling
e^{-k|q|}
instead.
Zhang and Strogatz
20
resolved this apparent contradiction
by demonstrating, through high-dimensional simulations, that the basins have an

octopus-like
geometry: nearly all their volume lies in long tentacles that
reach far from the attractor, which local measurements miss entirely.
Beyond settling the scaling debate, their paper reported a striking set of
further geometric properties—a universal master distribution for distances
between states and their attractors, the existence of rays that cross basin
boundaries many times, and the complete failure of hypercube
approximations to capture basin volume.
The authors themselves were careful to note, however, that all of these
observations rested on numerical simulations in high-dimensional spaces,
where reliable sampling is notoriously difficult, and that rigorous proofs
were lacking.

Analytical progress was made in
6
, which identified the
dynamical mechanism behind the Gaussian scaling: starting from a random
initial condition, the winding number stabilizes at a time
t\propto\log n
,
before long-range dependence between oscillators can develop.
Introducing the phase-difference variables
\eta_{i}=\theta_{i+1}-\theta_{i}
, a CLT argument for the winding number computed in terms of
\bm{\eta}=(\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n-1})
establishes the
e^{-kq^{2}}
scaling. This approach required trajectories to first enter the
invariant region
\mathcal{I}=\{|\eta_{i}|<\pi/2\text{ for all }i\}
,
which is only partially rigorous for the sine coupling; and
the rich geometric picture of
20
—the
master distance distribution, the tentacle structure, the failure of
hypercubes—was left entirely unaddressed.

In this Letter we remove these obstacles in one step. We replace
\sin

by a general coupling function
f
that is
C^{1}
, odd,
2\pi
-periodic,
and
strictly increasing on
(-\pi,\pi)
. This condition, which

\sin
narrowly fails to satisfy globally, has a decisive consequence:
the region
\mathcal{J}=\{|\eta_{i}|<\pi\text{ for all }i\}
is flow-invariant,
and the winding number
q
is therefore conserved for all
t\geq 0
.
Since random initial conditions satisfy
\eta_{i}\in(-\pi,\pi)
almost
surely, the winding number of the final state equals that of the initial
condition exactly—with no waiting time, no
\log n
bound, and no
approximation. From this single observation, all the geometric properties
reported numerically in
20
follow as rigorous theorems.

Specifically, we prove: (i) the
e^{-kq^{2}}
basin scaling (Theorem
3
); (ii) the master distance distribution and the origin of the constant
1.81
(Theorem
4
); (iii) the tentacle structure in a sharp form—almost every ray from a twisted state is equidistributed on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, visiting every basin with frequency equal to its measure (Theorem
6
); and (iv) the size of the basin’s “head”, both worst-case and typical (Theorem
8
). As a consequence of winding-number invariance and the gradient structure, we also find that all twisted states with
|q|<n/2
are stable (Corollary
2
), in contrast to the standard Kuramoto model where stability requires
|q|<n/4
.

The energy landscape studied here is an instance of a gradient system on a
compact space with a sum-of-potentials structure,

E(\bm{\theta})=\sum_{j}F(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})
,
where
F^{\prime}=f
. This structure is ubiquitous and has recently attracted attention in
deep learning: the self-attention dynamics of transformer networks
are a gradient flow of a structurally analogous
energy
5
;
13
, and the distribution of
tokens across metastable clusters in transformers is the counterpart, in a
different geometry, of basin sizes for twisted states. The rigorous landscape
picture developed here may offer tools and intuition for the still-open problem
of understanding transformer dynamics.

The model and winding number invariance

Throughout the paper we consider
n
identical oscillators on a cycle
graph evolving according to

\dot{\theta}_{i}=f(\theta_{i+1}-\theta_{i})+f(\theta_{i-1}-\theta_{i}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

(2)

with periodic boundary conditions
\theta_{n+1}=\theta_{1}
and coupling
function
f\colon\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

(H1)

f\in C^{1}(-\pi,\pi)
,

(H2)

f
is odd,
f(-x)=-f(x)
,

(H3)

f
is
2\pi
-periodic,

(H4)

f
is strictly increasing on
(-\pi,\pi)
, i.e.
f^{\prime}(x)>0

for all
x\in(-\pi,\pi)
.

Condition (H4) implies that
f
has simple jump discontinuities at
\pm\pi
after periodization; this
is harmless, as trajectories with typical initial
data never reach these points.

In the phase-difference variables
\eta_{i}=\theta_{i+1}-\theta_{i}
, the system reads

\dot{\eta}_{i}=f(\eta_{i+1})-2f(\eta_{i})+f(\eta_{i-1}),\quad i=1,\dots,n,

(3)

with
\eta_{n+1}=\eta_{1}
and
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The

q
-twisted states correspond to
\eta_{i}\equiv 2\pi q/n
for all
i
.
Whenever
\eta_{i}\neq\pm\pi
for all
i
, the winding number

q(t)=\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n}\eta_{i}(t)

(4)

is a well-defined integer. In view of this, and that
|\eta_{i}|/2\pi<1/2
, it can also be computed as

q(t)=\left[\frac{1}{2\pi}\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\eta_{i}(t)\right],

(5)

where
[x]
denotes the integer closest to
x
.
Let
\mathcal{J}=\{\bm{\eta}\in(-\pi,\pi)^{n}\colon\sum_{i}\eta_{i}\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z}\}

denote the phase space in the
\eta
variables, restricted to
configurations for which
q
is defined. Hypothesis (H1) makes the
right-hand side of (
3
)
C^{1}
on
\mathcal{J}
, so standard
ODE theory gives local existence and uniqueness of solutions.

Theorem 1

(Winding number invariance)
.

Under hypotheses (H1)–(H4), the region
\mathcal{J}
is positively invariant
under the flow of (
3
). Consequently, the winding
number
q(t)
is constant along every trajectory starting in
\mathcal{J}
.
Since the set
\{\bm{\eta}\in(-\pi,\pi]^{n}\colon\eta_{i}=\pi\text{ for some }i\}
has Lebesgue measure zero, for almost every
initial condition in state space the winding number is conserved for
all
t\geq 0
.

Proof.

Assume
\bm{\eta}(0)\in\mathcal{J}
and let
t_{0}>0
be the first time at which

\bm{\eta}(t_{0})\in\partial\mathcal{J}
. Then for some index
i
we have

\eta_{i}(t_{0})\in\{-\pi,\pi\}
; without loss of generality

\eta_{i}(t_{0})=\pi
. From (
3
), using

|\eta_{i\pm 1}(t_{0})|\leq\pi
and the monotonicity of
f
on

[-\pi,\pi]
,

\dot{\eta}_{i}(t_{0})=f(\eta_{i+1}(t_{0}))-2f(\pi)+f(\eta_{i-1}(t_{0}))\leq 0,

with strict inequality unless
\eta_{i+1}(t_{0})=\eta_{i-1}(t_{0})=\pi
.
If the inequality is strict we obtain a contradiction, since
\eta_{i}

must be non-decreasing at
t_{0}
to exit
\mathcal{J}
through
\pi
.
Otherwise,
\eta_{i\pm 1}(t_{0})=\pi
, and proceeding inductively along
the cycle either we find an index
j
at which
\dot{\eta}_{j}(t_{0})<0

(again a contradiction), or
\eta_{j}(t_{0})=\pi
for every
j
. The
latter is the unstable equilibrium in which all phase differences
equal
\pi
, which cannot be reached in finite time from any
configuration in
\mathcal{J}
by uniqueness of solutions. The symmetric
argument rules out
\eta_{i}(t_{0})=-\pi
. Hence no such
t_{0}
exists
and
\mathcal{J}
is positively invariant.
Invariance of the winding number inside
\mathcal{J}
follows immediately:
summing (
3
) over
i
and using the telescoping
structure of the discrete Laplacian of
f
,

\frac{d}{dt}\sum_{i}\eta_{i}(t)=0
,
so
q(t)
is constant in time.

\square

Remark 1
.

The corresponding statement for the sine coupling is weaker:
\sin

fails (H4), and the above maximum-principle argument yields only the
invariance of the smaller region
\mathcal{I}=\{|\eta_{i}|<\pi/2\}
, as
in
6
. In that setting, typical initial
conditions start outside
\mathcal{I}
, and one must analyze the entry
dynamics before the winding number is controlled.

A striking consequence of Theorem
1
combined with
the gradient structure of (
2
) is that every twisted
state with
|q|<n/2
is stable, and in fact is the only attractor
inside its basin.

Corollary 2

(Stability of twisted states)
.

Under hypotheses (H1)–(H4), for every integer
q
with
|q|<n/2
,
the
q
-twisted state is the unique attractor of (
2
)
within
\mathcal{K}_{q}
. In particular, all such twisted states are stable.

Proof.

The system (
2
) is the gradient flow of the energy

E(\bm{\theta})=\sum_{j=1}^{n}F(\eta_{j})
, with
F^{\prime}=f
and
F(0)=0
, where

F
is strictly convex on
(-\pi,\pi)
by (H4). By
Theorem
1
, every trajectory starting in
\mathcal{K}_{q}

remains in
\mathcal{K}_{q}
and preserves the constraint

\sum_{j=1}^{n}\eta_{j}=2\pi q
. Strict convexity of
F
together with
Lagrange multipliers gives a unique minimizer of
E
on
\mathcal{K}_{q}
, at
which all
\eta_{j}
are equal: this is the
q
-twisted state.
Since
E
is bounded below and
decreases along trajectories, LaSalle’s invariance principle

15
implies every trajectory in
\mathcal{K}_{q}
converges to it.

\square

In the standard Kuramoto model, by contrast, twisted states with

n/4\leq|q|<n/2
are equilibria but are
unstable
: the
linearization has a positive eigenvalue because
\cos(2\pi q/n)<0

in this range. Condition (H4) rules out this
instability entirely; the “instability gap” of the sine coupling is a special
feature of
\sin
near
\pm\pi/2
, not a generic property
of oscillator networks.

Theorem
1
is the master key of this paper: every
subsequent result is a consequence of the fact that
q
is determined
by the initial condition alone. The basin of attraction
of the
q
-twisted state consists, up to a null set, of initial
conditions
\bm{\theta}(0)
with winding number exactly
q
. Since

\bm{\theta}(0)
is sampled uniformly on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, which induces i.i.d. uniform distributions on
(-\pi,\pi]
for the phase differences

\eta_{i}
, the problem reduces to the distribution of
the sum of i.i.d. uniform random variables modulo
2\pi
.

Basin sizes

Let
\mathcal{K}_{q}\subset\mathbb{T}^{n}
denote the basin of attraction of the

q
-twisted state, and let
\mu
be the normalized uniform measure
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
.

Theorem 3

(Gaussian basin scaling)
.

Under hypotheses (H1)–(H4), as
n\to\infty
,

\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q})\;=\;\sqrt{\tfrac{6}{\pi n}}\,\exp\!\left(-\tfrac{6q^{2}}{n}\right)\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),

(6)

uniformly for
q=O(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

By Theorem
1
, for
\mu
-almost every

\bm{\theta}(0)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
the winding number is conserved:

q(\infty)=q(0)
. Hence, up to a null set,
\mathcal{K}_{q}
coincides with

\{\bm{\theta}(0)\in\mathbb{T}^{n}:q(0)=q\}
.
Sampling
\bm{\theta}(0)
uniformly on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
is equivalent to sampling

\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n-1}
i.i.d. uniformly on
(-\pi,\pi]
and
setting
\eta_{n}=-\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}\eta_{i}\pmod{2\pi}
. Writing

X_{i}=\eta_{i}/(2\pi)
, the variables
X_{i}
are i.i.d. uniform on

(-\tfrac{1}{2},\tfrac{1}{2}]
with mean
0
and variance
1/12
, and

q(0)=[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{i}]
, where
[\,\cdot\,]
denotes the
nearest integer. The Central Limit Theorem gives,
for integer
q=O(\sqrt{n})
,

\displaystyle\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q})

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}\!\left(\left[\sum_{i=1}^{n-1}X_{i}\right]=q\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi\,(n-1)/12}}\,e^{-\tfrac{6q^{2}}{n-1}}\,\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr),

which yields (
6
).

\square

The proof identifies the constant in the scaling law explicitly:

k=6/n
. Theorem
3
rigorously confirms the
conjecture of Wiley, Strogatz, and Girvan
19
and
the numerical evidence of Zhang and
Strogatz
20
.

Remark 2
.

The estimate (
6
) is sharp for

q=O(\sqrt{n})
, the regime that carries essentially all the mass.
For
|q|\gg\sqrt{n}
it gives
\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q})\to 0
;
the corresponding twisted states are still stable
(Corollary
2
), but their basins are exponentially small
in
q^{2}/n
. Even the volume of the union of all such basins
vanishes exponentially fast: although stable twisted states exist for

|q|<n/2
, only those with
|q|=O(\sqrt{n})
are seen by the system.

The geometry of the basins

Theorem
3
gives the volumes of the basins but says
nothing about their shapes. We use on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
the
\ell^{2}
distance on phase
differences normalized by
n
:

d(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{\prime})\;=\;\left[\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i=1}^{n}d_{i}^{2}\right]^{1/2},

(7)

where
d_{i}\in[0,\pi]
is the absolute phase difference between the

i
th coordinates of
\bm{\theta}
and
\bm{\theta}^{\prime}
on
S^{1}
, as
in
20
.

The master distance distribution

Let
\bm{\theta}^{(q)}
denote the
q
-twisted state. Zhang and Strogatz
observed numerically that for
\bm{\theta}
sampled uniformly from

\mathcal{K}_{q}
, the distance
d(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{(q)})
concentrates at
a value around
1.81
, the same for all
q
, and coinciding with the typical distance between two random points in

\mathbb{T}^{n}
. We prove this rigorously.

Theorem 4

(Master distance distribution)
.

Fix any point
\bm{\theta}^{*}\in\mathbb{T}^{n}
and sample
\bm{\theta}
uniformly
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. As
n\to\infty
,

d(\bm{\theta},\bm{\theta}^{*})\;\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\;\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3}\;\approx\;1.814,

(8)

with Gaussian fluctuations of order
n^{-1/2}
.
Conditioning on
\bm{\theta}\in\mathcal{K}_{q}
does not change the limit for
any
q
with
|q|=o(\sqrt{n})
.

Proof.

By rotational invariance of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, we may assume
\bm{\theta}^{*}=\bm{0}
.
The coordinate distances
d_{i}
are then i.i.d. uniform on
[0,\pi]
,
so
\mathbb{E}(d_{i}^{2})=\pi^{2}/3
and
\mathrm{Var}(d_{i}^{2})=4\pi^{4}/45
.
By the law of large numbers,

\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i}d_{i}^{2}\xrightarrow{\mathrm{a.s.}}\pi^{2}/3
;
the central limit theorem gives the Gaussian fluctuations.
For the conditional statement, for

|q|=o(\sqrt{n})
the conditioning on
q(\bm{\theta})=q
fixes
one linear function of the
\eta_{i}
at a value within its typical
fluctuation range; the conditional law of the
|\eta_{i}|

still satisfies a law of large numbers with the same limit; see

(
16
, Ch. VII)
.

\square

A striking corollary: as
n\to\infty
,
the basin of any
q
-twisted state with
|q|=o(\sqrt{n})
lies,
up to a set of vanishing measure, on the sphere of radius
1.814

centered at the attractor. The “head” of the basin carries no measure in the limit. All the
mass is at the typical distance
\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3}
, which is also
the typical distance to
any
other point; the basin is
a high-dimensional filamentary structure distributed across state
space like any other set of comparable measure. In fact, we show next that most of the space lies near basin boundaries.

Most of state space lies near basin boundaries

The boundaries of the sets
\mathcal{K}_{q}
are the points where the
winding number is ill-defined, i.e. where some

\eta_{i}
equals
\pi
(identifying
\pi
and
-\pi
on the circle).

Proposition 5

(Boundary proximity)
.

Let
\bm{\theta}
be uniform on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
and
\delta\in(0,\pi)
.
For each integer
k\geq 1
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\,\#\{i:|\eta_{i}-\pi|<\delta\}\geq k\,\bigr)\;\to\;1

(9)

as
n\to\infty
. Moreover, the number of such
\eta_{i}
is
Binomial
(n-1,\delta/\pi)
; if
\delta=\delta_{n}\to 0

with
n\delta_{n}\to\infty
, then with high probability at least

n\delta_{n}/2\pi
of the phase differences lie within
\delta_{n}
of

\pi
, a number that diverges with
n
.

Proof.

Each
\eta_{i}
is uniform on
(-\pi,\pi]
, so

\mathbb{P}(|\eta_{i}-\pi|<\delta)=\delta/\pi
. Since
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{n-1}
are
independent, the count is Binomial
(n-1,\delta/\pi)
, with mean

(n-1)\delta/\pi\to\infty
.

\square

A random point in state space thus lies arbitrarily close to the boundary of
some
\mathcal{K}_{q}
, and near many such boundaries simultaneously. Combined with
the master distance distribution, this explains why local measurements of
the basins cannot detect the bulk of the basin: the basin is not where local
measurements look.

The tentacle structure

We now establish a strong form of the tentacle picture: along almost every
ray emanating from a twisted state, the winding number changes infinitely
many times, and the ray visits each basin with frequency equal to that basin’s measure.

Theorem 6

(Equidistribution along random rays)
.

Fix
n\geq 3
and a
q
-twisted state
\bm{\theta}^{(q)}
. Let
\bm{v}

be a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
sampled uniformly on the sphere. Then for

\bm{v}
-almost every direction, the ray

\{\bm{\theta}^{(q)}+\lambda\bm{v}:\lambda\geq 0\}
is equidistributed
on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
with respect to
\mu
. In particular,
for every
q^{\prime}
with
\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}})>0
,

\lim_{T\to\infty}\frac{1}{T}\int_{0}^{T}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}}\!\bigl(\bm{\theta}^{(q)}+\lambda\bm{v}\bigr)\,d\lambda\;=\;\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}),

(10)

and the ray enters and exits each
\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
infinitely many times.

Proof.
We work directly on
\mathbb{T}^{n}=(\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z})^{n}
.
The ray
\lambda\mapsto\theta^{(q)}+\lambda v\pmod{2\pi}
is a linear
flow on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
, and by the Weyl-Kronecker equidistribution theorem

18
;
7
it is equidistributed with respect to
\mu

if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{n}k_{i}v_{i}\neq 0\quad\text{for every }(k_{1},\ldots,k_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}\setminus\{0\},

i.e.
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}
are rationally independent. The set of
v\in S^{n-1}
violating this condition is a countable union of codimension-one
subspheres, hence of spherical measure zero. For
v
-almost every direction
the orbit is therefore equidistributed on
\mathbb{T}^{n}
. Each basin

\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
has boundary contained in the null set
\bigcup_{i}\{\eta_{i}=\pi\}
; applying equidistribution to its indicator yields (
10
). For any
q^{\prime}
with
\mu(\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}})>0
,
(
10
) forces the ray to spend a set of
\lambda
-values
of infinite Lebesgue measure inside
\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
, and in particular to enter

\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
infinitely many times.
\square

Combining Theorem
6
with Theorem
3
, the
ray spends (approximately, as
n\to\infty
) a fraction
\sqrt{6/(\pi n)}\exp(-6q^{2}/n)
of its length inside
the original basin
\mathcal{K}_{q}
. The ray is, in a precise sense, a
statistical sample of
\mathbb{T}^{n}
; this is what the tentacles encode.

Corollary 7

(All basins are visited)
.

Under the hypotheses of Theorem
6
, for
\bm{v}
-almost
every direction and every
q^{\prime}
with
|q^{\prime}|<n/2
, the ray

\{\bm{\theta}^{(q)}+\lambda\bm{v}:\lambda\geq 0\}
enters the basin

\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
infinitely many times as
\lambda\to\infty
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2
, every twisted state with

|q^{\prime}|<n/2
is stable, so
\mathcal{K}_{q^{\prime}}
is open and of positive
measure; (
10
) then applies.

\square

Thus, starting from any attractor, almost every straight line
threads through every other basin infinitely many times; the tentacles of each
basin reach arbitrarily close to every other attractor.

The head of the octopus

Theorem
6
does not say at what distance the ray first
leaves the basin of the starting attractor — the size of the
“head” of the octopus. Two natural quantities capture this. The

worst-case

R_{q}\;=\;\sup\bigl\{r>0:B(\bm{\theta}^{(q)},r)\subset\mathcal{K}_{q}\bigr\},

is the radius of the largest Euclidean ball centered at the twisted
state and contained in
\mathcal{K}_{q}
. For
\bm{v}\in S^{n-1}
,

\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})\;=\;\inf\bigl\{\lambda>0:\bm{\theta}^{(q)}+\lambda\bm{v}\notin\mathcal{K}_{q}\bigr\},

is the distance from the attractor to the first basin boundary along
direction
\bm{v}
; note that

R_{q}=\inf_{\|\bm{v}\|=1}\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})
.

Theorem 8

(Head size)
.

Fix
q\in\mathbb{Z}
with
|q|=o(n)
and let
\bm{v}
be uniform on
S^{n-1}
.
Then as
n\to\infty
:

(i)

R_{q}=\pi/\sqrt{2}
exactly, independently of
n
and
q
,
so
R_{q}/\sqrt{n}=\pi/\sqrt{2n}\to 0
.

(ii)

\frac{\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})}{\sqrt{n/\log n}}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\tfrac{\pi}{2}
, (
\frac{\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})}{\sqrt{n}}\sim\frac{\pi}{2\sqrt{\log n}}
)
.

Here
\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}
denotes convergence in probability.
In particular, in the normalized distance (
7
) both
measures of head size vanish as
n\to\infty
, but the typical
first-crossing distance exceeds the inscribed-ball radius by a factor
of order
\sqrt{n/\log n}
.

Proof.

We treat
q=0
for simplicity; the extension to
|q|=o(n)
is
straightforward.
Let
A
be the cycle difference matrix whose rows are

\bm{e}_{i+1}-\bm{e}_{i}
(indices mod
n
), so that

\bm{\eta}=A\bm{\theta}
. A ray

\bm{\theta}(\lambda)=\lambda\bm{v}
from the origin corresponds to

\bm{\eta}(\lambda)=\lambda\bm{w}
with
\bm{w}=A\bm{v}
, and

\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})\;=\;\frac{\pi}{\|\bm{w}\|_{\infty}}.

(11)

(i)
Taking the infimum over
\bm{v}
in (
11
),

R_{0}=\pi/\|A\|_{2\to\infty}
; The norm in the denominator can be obtained as

\|A\|_{2\to\infty}=\max_{1\leq i\leq m}\|\bm{a}_{i}\|_{2},

where
\bm{a}_{i}
denotes the
i
-th row of
A
(thought of as a vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
). Since each row of
A
has one
+1
and one

-1
,
\|A\|_{2\to\infty}=\sqrt{2}
and
R_{0}=\pi/\sqrt{2}
.

(ii)
For
\bm{v}
uniform on
S^{n-1}
, the components
w_{i}=v_{i+1}-v_{i}

form a stationary sequence with
\mathrm{Var}(w_{i})=2/n+O(1/n^{2})
and
short-range dependence (only pairs at cyclic distance
1
are correlated,
with correlation of order
-1/2
). Classical extreme-value theory
for stationary sequences with asymptotically Gaussian marginals and weak
dependence
8
gives

\|\bm{w}\|_{\infty}/(2\sqrt{\log n/n})\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}1
.
Substituting into (
11
) yields

\lambda^{\ast}(\bm{v})/\sqrt{n/\log n}\xrightarrow{\mathbb{P}}\tfrac{\pi}{2}
.

\square

The basin
\mathcal{K}_{q}
has an inscribed ball of radius
\pi/\sqrt{2}
in
ambient Euclidean distance, a constant independent of
n
and so
vanishingly small in normalized distance. Yet along a typical ray
the basin extends to distance
\tfrac{\pi}{2}\sqrt{n/\log n}
,
larger by a factor of
\sqrt{n/\log n}
. The head is therefore
strongly anisotropic: narrow in a few adversarial directions but
thick in most generic ones — a star-shaped region, with tentacles
reaching out to distance
\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3}\approx 1.814
.

Discussion

Zhang and Strogatz
20
conjectured that the
octopus-like basin geometry they observed numerically in the
Kuramoto ring should be common in high-dimensional multistable
systems. Our results support this conjecture by exhibiting a large
natural family of models — identical oscillators on a ring coupled
by any smooth odd function strictly increasing on

(-\pi,\pi)
— for which every feature of the octopus picture holds
rigorously: basin volumes are Gaussian in the winding number, the
master distance concentrates at
\sqrt{\pi^{2}/3}
for every attractor,
almost every ray is equidistributed on state space, and the basin’s
head is tightly controlled. The picture is not a feature of any
particular coupling; it is a structural property of a whole class of
landscapes.

More broadly, the gradient-flow energy

E(\bm{\theta})=\sum_{j}F(\theta_{j+1}-\theta_{j})
is a member of a family
of landscapes with many local minima separated by low-dimensional
bottlenecks — a family that includes spin glasses, jammed sphere
packings
10
, and the self-attention
dynamics of transformer networks
5
;
13
.
The rigorous picture obtained here may serve as a
tractable model of what to expect in other high-dimensional
multistable systems, and as a template for the geometric statements
one might hope to prove elsewhere.
