Probing Heavy Neutral Higgs Bosons via Single Vector-Like Bottom Quark Production at the HL-LHC Preprint: IFJPAN-IV-2026-7
R. Benbrik 1, M. Berrouj 1, M. Boukidi 2, M. Ech-chaouy 1, K. Kahime 3, K. Salime 1

Abstract
We investigate the discovery prospects of a singly produced vector-like bottom quark in the Type-II Two-Higgs-Doublet Model extended by an S ​ U ​ ( 2 ) L SU(2)_{L} vector-like ( T , B ) (T,B) doublet. We focus on the non-standard decay chain B → ϕ ​ b B\to\phi b , followed by ϕ → t ​ t ¯ \phi\to t\bar{t} , where ϕ = H \phi=H or A A , leading to a final state with one charged lepton, missing transverse energy, and multiple b b -jets. We perform a full simulation of both signal and Standard Model backgrounds at s = 14 \sqrt{s}=14 TeV. We show that the exotic channels B → ϕ ​ b B\to\phi b can dominate over the conventional decay modes, reaching branching ratios of order 50 % 50\% for both neutral scalars in the alignment limit. A conventional cut-based analysis provides a 5 ​ σ 5\sigma discovery significance only at sufficiently high integrated luminosity. By contrast, an XGBoost-based multivariate analysis substantially improves the signal-background discrimination and extends the discovery reach up to m B ≃ 1.3 m_{B}\simeq 1.3 TeV with 600 ​ fb − 1 600~\mathrm{fb}^{-1} and up to m B ≃ 1.6 m_{B}\simeq 1.6 TeV with 3 ​ ab − 1 3~\mathrm{ab}^{-1} , even in the presence of systematic uncertainties as large as 15 % 15\% .

Z_{2}
symmetry, the scalar sector contains two complex
SU(2)_{L}
doublets,
\Phi_{1}
and
\Phi_{2}

38

59

\displaystyle V\left(\Phi_{1},\Phi_{2}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle m_{11}^{2}\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}+m_{22}^{2}\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}-m_{12}^{2}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}+\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\left(\Phi_{1}^{\dagger}\Phi_{1}\right)^{2}+\frac{\lambda_{2}}{2}\left(\Phi_{2}^{\dagger}\Phi_{2}\right)^{2}

Passing to the Higgs basis, only one linear combination of the two doublets acquires a nonzero vacuum expectation value,

\displaystyle H_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{c}G^{+}\\
\frac{v+\varphi^{0}_{1}+iG^{0}}{\sqrt{2}}\\

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}h\\

\end{array}\right),

and

\sin(\beta-\alpha)\to 1
, the lighter CP-even state

The possible VLQ representations under
SU(3)_{C}\times SU(2)_{L}\times U(1)_{Y}

\displaystyle T^{0}_{L,R},\quad B^{0}_{L,R}

\displaystyle(X,T^{0},B^{0})_{L,R},\quad(T^{0},B^{0},Y)_{L,R}

\displaystyle\text{(triplets)}\,.

The superscript

(T,B)

B

The presence of

d
,

, and
B

R_{b}

4
. The mixing between
b^{0}
and

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}b_{L,R}\\
B_{L,R}\\
\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\cos\theta_{L,R}^{d}&-\sin\theta_{L,R}^{d}e^{i\phi_{d}}\\

where

In the Higgs basis, the relevant Yukawa structure is

-\mathcal{L}_{Y}\supset y^{u}\bar{Q}^{0}_{L}\tilde{H}_{2}u^{0}_{R}+y^{d}\bar{Q}^{0}_{L}H_{1}d^{0}_{R}+M_{u}^{0}\bar{u}_{L}^{0}u_{R}^{0}+M_{d}^{0}\bar{d}_{L}^{0}d_{R}^{0}+\text{h.c.},

(22)

with
u_{R}^{0}=(u_{R},c_{R},t_{R},T_{R})
and
d_{R}^{0}=(d_{R},s_{R},b_{R},B_{R})
. The mass matrix in the vector-like bottom sector takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\text{mass}}=-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\bar{b}_{L}^{0}&\bar{B}_{L}^{0}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}y_{33}^{d}\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}&y_{34}^{d}\frac{v}{\sqrt{2}}\\

are Yukawa couplings. Diagonalization is performed through the bi-unitary transformation

U_{L}^{d}\mathcal{M}^{d}(U_{R}^{d})^{\dagger}=\mathcal{M}^{d}_{\text{diag}}.

(29)

The mixing angles satisfy

\displaystyle\tan 2\theta_{L}^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}|y_{34}^{d}|vM^{0}}{(M^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}(|y_{33}^{d}|^{2}+|y_{34}^{d}|^{2})}\quad\text{(singlets, triplets)},

\displaystyle\tan 2\theta_{R}^{d}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\sqrt{2}|y_{43}^{d}|vM^{0}}{(M^{0})^{2}-\frac{1}{2}v^{2}(|y_{33}^{d}|^{2}+|y_{43}^{d}|^{2})}\quad\text{(doublets)},

(30)

\displaystyle\tan\theta_{R}^{q}

\displaystyle\frac{m_{q}}{m_{Q}}\tan\theta_{L}^{q}\quad\text{(singlets, triplets)},

\displaystyle\frac{m_{q}}{m_{Q}}\tan\theta_{R}^{q}\quad\text{(doublets)}.

\displaystyle\Gamma(B\to\phi\,b)

\displaystyle=\frac{g^{2}}{128\pi}\,\frac{m_{B}}{M_{W}^{2}}\,\lambda^{1/2}(m_{B},m_{b},M_{\phi})

\displaystyle\times\left[(|Y^{L}_{\phi bB}|^{2}+|Y^{R}_{\phi bB}|^{2})(1+r_{b}^{2}-r_{\phi}^{2})\pm 4r_{b}\,\mathrm{Re}\!\left(Y^{L}_{\phi bB}Y^{R*}_{\phi bB}\right)\right],

\phi=H,A

\Gamma(B\to Ab)\approx\Gamma(B\to Hb)\approx\Gamma(B\to\phi b).

II.1
Experimental and theoretical constraints

We impose a set of theoretical and experimental constraints in order to identify viable regions of parameter space consistent with perturbativity, electroweak precision data, Higgs measurements, and direct collider bounds.

Theoretical constraints

•

Unitarity:
The
S
-wave amplitudes for scalar-scalar, scalar-gauge, and gauge-gauge scattering are required to satisfy perturbative unitarity at high energies
65
.

•

Perturbativity:
All quartic couplings in the scalar potential are required to obey
|\lambda_{i}|<8\pi
for
i=1,\dots,5

38
.

•

Vacuum stability:
The scalar potential must remain bounded from below in all field directions. This implies
53
;
16

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}>0,\quad\lambda_{2}>0,\quad\lambda_{3}>-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}},

\displaystyle\lambda_{3}+\lambda_{4}-|\lambda_{5}|>-\sqrt{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}}.

(33)

•

Electroweak precision observables:
The oblique parameters
S
and
T

58
are required to satisfy the 95% confidence-level bounds from the global electroweak fit, assuming
U=0

79
,

\displaystyle S=0.05\pm 0.08,\quad T=0.09\pm 0.07,\quad\rho_{ST}=0.92.

(34)

In the presence of VLQs, the total contribution is evaluated through
\chi^{2}(S_{\text{2HDM}}+S_{\text{VLQ}},\,T_{\text{2HDM}}+T_{\text{VLQ}})

To further assess possible overtraining, we compare the training and test samples using the Kolmogorov-Smirnov (KS) test
72
. The KS statistic measures the maximal difference between the cumulative distribution functions of two independent samples,

\displaystyle D=\max_{x}\left|F_{1}(x)-F_{2}(x)\right|,

(36)

where
F_{1}(x)
and
F_{2}(x)
denote the corresponding cumulative distributions. The test also provides a
p
-value, which quantifies the probability of obtaining a KS statistic at least as large as the observed one under the null hypothesis that the two samples are drawn from the same parent distribution. A small
p
-value therefore signals a statistically significant discrepancy.

If
D
is large and
p<0.05
, the two distributions are significantly different, indicating possible overtraining. Conversely, if
D
is small or
p\geq 0.05
, the training and test samples are statistically compatible. In Fig.
5
, we show the classifier outputs for signal and background in both samples, together with the corresponding KS statistics and
p
-values. In all cases, the KS statistic remains small and one finds
p>5\%
, indicating no significant evidence for overfitting.

V
Conclusion

We have analyzed the discovery potential of a singly produced vector-like bottom quark in the Type-II 2HDM extended by an
SU(2)_{L}

(T,B)
vector-like quark doublet, focusing on the non-standard decay chain
B\to\phi b
with
\phi=H,A
and
\phi\to t\bar{t}
. In the parameter region considered in this work, the exotic decays into heavy neutral Higgs bosons can dominate over the conventional SM channels, with
\mathrm{BR}(B\to Hb)
and
\mathrm{BR}(B\to Ab)
reaching values close to
50\%
in the alignment limit.

After imposing the relevant theoretical and experimental constraints, we identified viable benchmark configurations for which the signal process

pp\to B\bar{b}j\to\phi b\bar{b}j\to t\bar{t}b\bar{b}j\to W^{+}b\,\bar{t}\,b\bar{b}j\to\ell^{+}+\not{E}_{T}+3b+j+X

can be studied at the HL-LHC. Our cut-based analysis shows that the chosen kinematic selection suppresses the dominant SM backgrounds efficiently, but the resulting sensitivity remains limited once systematic uncertainties are included.

To improve the reach, we performed a multivariate analysis based on XGBoost. The classifier exhibits strong discriminating power, with high AUC values and no significant indication of overtraining according to the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test. Compared with the cut-based analysis, the multivariate approach leads to a substantial enhancement in the signal significance for all benchmark points considered.

For the benchmark scenarios studied here, the XGBoost analysis gives a
5\sigma
discovery reach up to about
m_{B}\simeq 1.3~\mathrm{TeV}
with
600~\mathrm{fb}^{-1}
and up to about
m_{B}\simeq 1.6~\mathrm{TeV}
with
3~\mathrm{ab}^{-1}
, even after including background systematic uncertainties of up to
15\%
. Our results therefore show that this channel can be relevant at the HL-LHC and can complement existing searches for heavy neutral Higgs bosons and vector-like quarks.
