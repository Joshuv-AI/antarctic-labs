Planck mass gravitinos in Einstein-Maxwell backgrounds
Artur Krawczyk

Abstract
Charged massive spin-3/2 fields have long been regarded as problematic, because their coupling to electromagnetism generically leads to loss of hyperbolicity, acausal propagation and loss of unitarity. At the same time, fractionally charged supermassive gravitinos play a central role in a recent proposal of two of the present authors, where they are the only fermionic degrees of freedom beyond the Standard Model and provide novel dark matter candidates. We address this apparent tension by revisiting the result of Deser and Waldron, who showed that the inclusion of gravity can compensate the electromagnetic source of the inconsistency. For a minimally coupled Rarita-Schwinger field in an Einstein-Maxwell background, consistency requires a lower bound on the mass in terms of the charge and the cosmological constant. What is usually a severe obstruction to low-energy charged spin-3/2 phenomenology becomes an independent indication that such particles must lie close to the Planck scale, as argued by two of the present authors on other grounds. We rederive the inequality from the solvability of the Rarita-Schwinger constraints and from the characteristic determinant, emphasizing how the Einstein-Maxwell stress tensor compensates the purely electromagnetic pathology. We then reformulate the system using a Stückelberg spinor, showing that the helicity-1/2 sector reproduces the same condition. This formulation is also essential for obtaining a renormalizable propagator. We discuss this propagator and the corresponding ghost system.

The notion of consistency relevant for massive spin-
\frac{3}{2}
fields has two complementary aspects. The first is classical: the constrained Rarita-Schwinger system must possess algebraic constraints that can be solved on the chosen background, and its characteristic hypersurfaces must lie inside, or on, the metric light cone.
This is the content of the bound reviewed below. The second aspect is perturbative: the massive spin-
\frac{3}{2}

In the ordinary massive Rarita-Schwinger formulation the propagator contains positive powers of momentum divided by powers of the mass, as in a unitary gauge of the massive vector field.

\frac{1}{2}

The price is the corresponding commuting spinor ghost system that we study using the BRST framework. Thus, the Stückelberg formulation provides both another derivation of the classical causality obstruction and a propagator suitable for standard perturbative power counting.
The main new contribution of the present work is the reinterpretation in the superheavy gravitino setting, together with a Stückelberg/BRST formulation that exposes the causality obstruction and yields a propagator suitable for perturbative power counting.

3
Stückelberg formulation

We can now reformulate the above analysis using an auxiliary Stückelberg field. This field isolates the sector that exhibits superluminal propagation. Additionally, it is required to obtain the propagator in a form that decays with momentum suitably for loop calculations.

First, we consider the gravitino-Stückelberg system in flat spacetime and in the absence of electromagnetic interactions. We do this first to study kinetic terms. This also serves as a foundation for quantum calculations around Minkowski vacuum, where all interaction terms are assumed to be small to be treated perturbatively.

The Lagrangian of the massless Rarita-Schwinger field in flat spacetime

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{m=0}=-i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}

(43)

possesses a symmetry
3
3

3

We consider the Dirac field, so there are two independent symmetry parameters
\epsilon,\bar{\epsilon}

\displaystyle\delta^{\prime}\psi_{\mu}=\partial_{\mu}\epsilon^{\prime},\qquad\delta^{\prime}\bar{\psi}_{\mu}=-\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime}\partial_{\mu}

(44)

with local spin-
\frac{1}{2}
fermions
\epsilon^{\prime},\bar{\epsilon}^{\prime}
. The quantization of this system was first described in

When

44

Throughout, expressions involving barred spinors are understood modulo total derivatives.
When an operator is written to the right of a barred spinor, it acts on everything to its right; equivalently,
the displayed formula is the integrated form obtained after moving derivatives off the barred field.

To restore symmetry we add a Stückelberg field

\displaystyle\delta\chi=m\epsilon,\qquad\delta\bar{\chi}=m\bar{\epsilon}.

(45)

For

the transformation rules for

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\mu}=\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\epsilon,\qquad\delta\bar{\psi}_{\mu}=-\bar{\epsilon}\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right).

(46)

and the invariant Lagrangian is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+m\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\chi}(i\not{\partial}+2m)\chi+\frac{3}{2}im(\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma\chi-\bar{\chi}\gamma\cdot\psi).

(47)

This Lagrangian can be derived using the invariant combination

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}=\psi_{\mu}-\frac{1}{m}\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\chi,\quad\bar{\psi}_{\mu}^{\prime}=\bar{\psi}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{m}\bar{\chi}\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right).

(48)

3.1
Causality of the Stückelberg system in Einstein-Maxwell background

The system (
48
) has the gauge symmetry which replaces usual constraints so the gamma-trace part of
\psi_{\mu}
is compensated by
\chi
. It is therefore natural to assume that the superluminal propagation of constraints will be reflected in equations for
\chi
. It was observed by Rahman
[
35
]
that this helicity-
\frac{1}{2}
mode probes the pathologies of minimal interactions found by Velo and Zwanziger.

The covariant derivative couples gravity and
A_{\mu}
to the Stückelberg field by

\displaystyle D_{\mu}\chi

\displaystyle=\partial_{\mu}\chi+\frac{1}{4}\omega_{\mu ab}\gamma^{ab}\chi+iqA_{\mu}\chi.

(49)

The Lagrangian is invariant under

\displaystyle\delta\psi_{\mu}=\left(D_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\epsilon,\qquad\delta\bar{\psi}_{\mu}=-\bar{\epsilon}\left(D_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right),\qquad\delta\chi=m\epsilon,\qquad\delta\bar{\chi}=m\bar{\epsilon}.

(50)

so the invariant combination is

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}^{\prime}=\psi_{\mu}-\frac{1}{m}\left(D_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right)\chi,\quad\bar{\psi}_{\mu}^{\prime}=\bar{\psi}_{\mu}+\frac{1}{m}\bar{\chi}\left(D_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right).

(51)

The Lagrangian now includes covariant derivatives and coupling to field strengths

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\sqrt{-g}}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+m\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}+\frac{3}{2}\bar{\chi}(i\not{D}+2m)\chi+\frac{3}{2}im(\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma\chi-\bar{\chi}\gamma\cdot\psi)+\mathcal{L}_{D^{2}}+\mathcal{L}_{D^{3}},

(52)

where terms with two and three commuted covariant derivatives are

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{D^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m}\left(i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\nu}D_{\rho}\chi-i\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\chi}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\nu}D_{\rho}\chi+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}\gamma_{\rho}\chi-\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}\chi\right)

(53)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2m}\left(qF_{\mu\nu}-\frac{i}{4}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\right)\left(-\bar{\psi}_{\rho}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\chi+\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\psi_{\rho}+i\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\chi\right)

(54)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{D^{3}}

\displaystyle=\frac{i}{m^{2}}\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}D_{\mu}D_{\nu}D_{\rho}\chi=-\frac{1}{2m^{2}}\bar{\chi}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\left(qF_{\mu\nu}-\frac{i}{4}R_{\mu\nu\alpha\beta}\gamma^{\alpha\beta}\right)D_{\rho}\chi=

(55)

\displaystyle=i\bar{\chi}\gamma_{\mu}\left(\frac{1}{2m^{2}}G^{\mu\nu}-\frac{q}{m^{2}}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\right)D_{\nu}\chi.

(56)

In the Stückelberg formulation, the problematic algebraic constraint is replaced by a gauge redundancy, so the relevant characteristic analysis is captured most transparently by the
\chi
sector.
Equations of motion for
\chi
are

\displaystyle\frac{3}{2}i\not{\partial}\chi+\frac{q}{m^{2}}\gamma_{\mu}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}\partial_{\nu}\chi+\frac{i}{2m^{2}}\gamma_{\mu}G^{\mu\nu}\partial_{\nu}\chi+\ldots=0,

(57)

where we included only the principal part.
The equation for
n_{\mu}
, the normal to the characteristic hypersurface, is obtained by setting the following determinant to zero:

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}

\displaystyle=\gamma_{\mu}\left(n^{\mu}+\frac{1}{3m^{2}}G^{\mu\nu}n_{\nu}+\frac{2q}{3m^{2}}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\gamma^{5}n_{\nu}\right).

(58)

The determinant vanishes when

\displaystyle\left(n^{\mu}+\frac{1}{3m^{2}}G^{\mu\nu}n_{\nu}\pm\frac{2q}{3m^{2}}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}n_{\nu}\right)^{2}=0\;,

(59)

which is the same equation as (
2.2
).

4
Propagator and gauge fixing

4.1
Unitary propagator at high momentum

In our discussion, we have considered the massive Rarita-Schwinger field as a gauge system together with an additional Stückelberg field
\chi
. This form is required to obtain the propagator that falls with momentum as
1/p
, which is needed for standard perturbative power counting.

We start with the free massive RS field in flat spacetime whose Lagrangian density is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=-i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+m\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu},

(60)

in the momentum space this is

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}=\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}p_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+m\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}.

(61)

The inverse of the kinetic operator is the propagator
[
15
]
4
4

4

The well known Van Dam-Veltman discontinuity for
m\rightarrow 0
is recovered by sandwiching this propagator between two conserved spinor currents
[
7
,
13
]
.

\displaystyle\left[\gamma^{\nu\rho\mu}p_{\rho}+m\gamma^{\nu\mu}\right]^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\not{p}+m}{p^{2}-m^{2}}\left[\eta^{\mu\nu}-\frac{1}{3}\gamma^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}+\frac{p^{\mu}\gamma^{\nu}}{3m}-\frac{\gamma^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{3m}-\frac{2p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{3m^{2}}\right].

(62)

This form is not appropriate for a quantum theory as it contains positive powers of momentum, which worsens the superficial degree of divergence and obscures standard power counting in loop calculations.

In the absence of the Stückelberg spinor
\chi
, i.e. when it is set to zero, this action takes the usual unitary gauge form whose propagator contains positive powers of momentum. To obtain a kinetic term with propagator
\sim 1/p
, we have to add a gauge-fixing term and include ghosts to preserve a proper number of propagating degrees of freedom.

4.2
Gauge fixing

Let us consider a gauge fixing condition that leads to a simple gauge-fixed Lagrangian:

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\gamma\cdot\psi+i\chi,\qquad\frac{\delta G}{\delta\epsilon}=i(-i\not{\partial}-m),

(63)

\displaystyle\bar{G}

\displaystyle=\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma-i\bar{\chi},\qquad\frac{\delta\bar{G}}{\delta\bar{\epsilon}}=-i(-i\not{\partial}-m),

(64)

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{4}\left(\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma-i\bar{\chi}\right)(i\not{\partial}-m)\left(\gamma\cdot\psi+i\chi\right),

(65)

where gauge fixing functions
G,\bar{G}
are chosen to generalize
\gamma\cdot\psi
gauge condition from the massless case.
This gauge choice was used for a massive gravitino in AdS in
[
13
]
and is analogous to the gauge
\frac{1}{4}\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma\not{\partial}\gamma\cdot\psi
from massless supergravity
[
4
,
37
]
.
Then the gauge fixed Lagrangian takes the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}+\frac{9}{4}\bar{\chi}(i\not{\partial}+m)\chi+\frac{3}{4}im(\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma\chi-\bar{\chi}\gamma\cdot\psi)-\frac{3}{4}(\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma\not{\partial}\chi-\bar{\chi}\not{\partial}\gamma\cdot\psi)+\frac{3}{4}\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma(i\not{\partial}-m)\gamma\cdot\psi

(66)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}+\left(\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma-\frac{3}{2}i\bar{\chi}\right)(i\not{\partial}+m)\left(\frac{1}{2}\gamma\cdot\psi+\frac{3}{2}i\chi\right)+\frac{1}{2}\bar{\psi}\cdot\gamma(i\not{\partial}-2m)\gamma\cdot\psi.

(67)

The Lagrangian (
67
) can be put into a diagonal form with Dirac kinetic terms. Consider the field redefinition
[
14
]

\displaystyle\psi_{\mu}=\phi_{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\gamma\cdot\phi,

(68)

which flips the sign of the
\gamma

\displaystyle\gamma\cdot\phi=-\gamma\cdot\psi.

(69)

Then

\displaystyle-i\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu\rho}\partial_{\nu}\psi_{\rho}+m\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\gamma^{\mu\nu}\psi_{\nu}=-\bar{\phi}_{\mu}(i\not{\partial}+m)\phi^{\mu}-\frac{1}{2}\bar{\phi}\cdot\gamma(i\not{\partial}-2m)\gamma\cdot\phi,

(70)

and the gauge-fixed Lagrangian takes a simple form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{gf}}

\displaystyle=-\bar{\phi}_{\mu}(i\not{\partial}+m)\phi^{\mu}+\bar{\xi}(i\not{\partial}+m)\xi,

(71)

where

\displaystyle\xi=\frac{1}{2}\gamma\cdot\psi+\frac{3}{2}i\chi=-\frac{1}{2}\gamma\cdot\phi+\frac{3}{2}i\chi.

(72)

The Lagrangian (
71
) leads to a very simple propagator structure for perturbation theory as

\displaystyle\langle\phi_{\mu}\bar{\phi}_{\nu}\rangle=-\eta_{\mu\nu}\frac{i\not{p}-m}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i0},\qquad\langle\xi\bar{\xi}\rangle=\frac{i\not{p}-m}{p^{2}-m^{2}+i0}.

(73)

However, we have to compensate the gauge freedom by introducing ghosts.

4.3
Ghost action

In this section, we derive the ghost action in an explicitly BRST-invariant way.
We introduce a complex ghost spinor
c
and its Dirac adjoint
\bar{c}
, associated with fermionic gauge parameter
\epsilon,
and its adjoint
\bar{\epsilon}
, so BRST transformations on the minimal set of fields

take the form

\displaystyle s\psi_{\mu}=\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right)c,\ s\bar{\psi}_{\mu}=-\bar{c}\left(\partial_{\mu}-i\frac{m}{2}\gamma_{\mu}\right),\ s\chi=mc,\ s\bar{\chi}=m\bar{c},\ sc=0,\ s\bar{c}=0,

(74)

and gauge-fixing functions transform as

\displaystyle sG=(\not{\partial}-im)c,\qquad s\bar{G}=\bar{c}(\not{\partial}-im).

(75)

Here
c,\bar{c}
are commuting spinor ghosts.
To the set of fields we add a complex antighost
c^{\prime},\bar{c}^{\prime}
and Nakanishi–Lautrup auxiliary anticommuting spinor fields
b,\bar{b}

\displaystyle sc^{\prime}=b,\quad s\bar{c}^{\prime}=\bar{b},\quad sb=0,\quad s\bar{b}=0.

(76)

The appearance of a dynamical Nakanishi–Lautrup field

is related to the fact that there is a kinetic
term
(i\not{\partial}-m)
between gauge fixing functions in (

5

The fields

\frac{3}{2}

For this reason we introduce the gauge-fixing fermion

\displaystyle\Xi

\displaystyle s\Xi

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{inv}}+s\Xi=\int d^{4}x\Big\{-\bar{\phi}_{\mu}(i\not{\partial}+m)\phi^{\mu}+\bar{\xi}(i\not{\partial}+m)\xi

\displaystyle S_{\mathrm{tot}}

\displaystyle=\int d^{4}x\sqrt{-g}\Big\{-\bar{\phi}_{\mu}(i\not{D}+m)\phi^{\mu}+\bar{\xi}(i\not{D}+m)\xi+\mathcal{L}_{D^{2}}+\mathcal{L}_{D^{3}}

\displaystyle-\bar{b}^{\prime}(i\not{D}-m)b^{\prime}+\bar{c^{\prime}}(i\not{D}-m)\left(\not{D}-im\right)c-\bar{c}(\not{D}-im)(i\not{D}-m)c^{\prime}\Big\},

The total BRST-invariant action (

M_{\mathrm{Pl}}

\epsilon

5
Conclusions

We have revisited the consistency conditions for a charged massive Rarita-Schwinger field in Einstein-Maxwell backgrounds from the perspective of a superheavy charged gravitino. The classical constraint and characteristic analyses lead to the same local condition: in the minimally coupled theory, any Einstein-Maxwell background is free of the Velo-Zwanziger obstruction provided the inequality (
2
) holds.
This bound is due to Deser and Waldron
[
9
]
but our interpretation differs: for ordinary low-energy phenomenology the Planckian scale of the bound is an obstruction, whereas for fractionally charged gravitino dark matter it is precisely the regime of interest.

One may ask whether the bound remains valid for a general effective theory of massive charged spin-
\frac{3}{2}
, in particular when the coupling is nonminimal. This changes equations of motion and hence also equations for characteristic surfaces. For hermitian nonminimal Pauli-like interactions of the form

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Non-min}}=\frac{iq}{m}\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\left(aF^{\mu\nu}+ib\gamma_{5}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\psi_{\nu},

(83)

the bound (
2
) is modified by factors of order
\mathcal{O}(a,b)
,
but the conclusion remains the same: causal propagation can be restored for Planck scale masses (see
[
6
,
9
]
for discussion of expressions obtained this way).
Interestingly, the BPS and causality bounds coincide for the theory with supergravity nonminimal coupling with Pauli term

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{SUGRA}}=\frac{iq}{m}\bar{\psi}_{\mu}\left(F^{\mu\nu}-i\gamma_{5}\tilde{F}^{\mu\nu}\right)\psi_{\nu}.

(84)

Deser and Waldron found that then the causality bound is
[
9
]

\displaystyle m^{2}>M_{\mathrm{SUGRA}}^{2}=8\pi\alpha_{e}\frac{q^{2}}{e^{2}}M_{\mathrm{Pl}}^{2}\;,

(85)

which is equal to the BPS bound (
4
).

We also reformulated the system using a Stückelberg spinor. In this language, the algebraic constraint of the massive Rarita-Schwinger field is traded for a fermionic gauge redundancy, and the dangerous helicity-
\frac{1}{2}
sector becomes explicit. The characteristic matrix of the Stückelberg spinor reproduces the same condition as the constrained Rarita-Schwinger analysis.

Finally, we discussed the propagator. The usual massive Rarita-Schwinger propagator has unitary-gauge high-momentum behavior and is not appropriate for standard perturbative power counting. The Stückelberg formulation allows a Feynman gauge in which the quadratic action is diagonal, has Dirac kinetic terms and leads to a renormalizable propagator.
