Effectiveness and Safety of Inebilizumab for IgG4-Related Disease in a Real-World Clinical Setting in Japan: Protocol for a Long-Term Prospective Observational Study (4SigHT Study)
Authors: Yoshiya Tanaka, Yuzo Kodama, Shingo Nakayamada, Atsushi Masamune, Hirokazu Sato, Akiko Takahashi, Junichiro Shibata, Yusuke Takeshima, Mitsuhiro Kawano
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
IntroductionIgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a systemic fibroinflammatory condition that can cause irreversible organ dysfunction and life-threatening complications. Although glucocorticoids are the standard first-line therapy, frequent flares necessitate prolonged use, which is in turn associated with toxicity. Inebilizumab, a CD19-targeted antibody approved in Japan in November 2025, demonstrated efficacy in the phase 3 MITIGATE trial. However, the trial’s eligibility criteria and controlled environment may not reflect the heterogeneity of the routine clinical setting or the long-term profile in the Japanese population. This study (4SigHT Study) aims to evaluate the long-term effectiveness and safety of inebilizumab in Japan.MethodsThis multicenter, prospective, observational study plans to enroll 100 patients with definite or probable IgG4-RD who are initiating inebilizumab at up to 40 sites in Japan. The study runs from March 1, 2026 to March 31, 2032. It includes up to 14 scheduled visits over a maximum duration of 312 weeks and reflects real-world decisions without mandated interventions. Comprehensive baseline assessments capture disease characteristics, comorbidities, vaccination history, and clinical history, including the frequency and nature of flares over the preceding 52 weeks. Longitudinal data are collected via an electronic data capture system. To ensure data robustness, the study employs a rigorous adjudication process. Suspected flares treated by investigators are adjudicated by an independent data and safety monitoring board using standardized organ-specific flare criteria.Planned OutcomesThe primary outcome is the proportion of participants with an adjudicated clinical flare through week 52. Secondary outcomes include treatment-free complete remission, glucocorticoid-free complete remission, time to clinical flare, annual clinical flare rate, proportion of patients who initiate additional treatment, time to treatment initiation for new or worsening symptoms, and cumulative glucocorticoid dose. Furthermore, the study evaluates the long-term safety of inebilizumab over 6 years.Trial RegistrationThe study is registered with the Japan Registry of Clinical Trials (jRCT1031250749).Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s40744-026-00870-1.

Introduction

Background

IgG4-related disease (IgG4-RD) is a systemic fibroinflammatory disease characterized by mass-forming lesions with dense lymphoplasmacytic infiltration and storiform fibrosis, encompassing previously distinct entities [1]. These lesions develop synchronously or metachronously in multiple organs, including the lacrimal and salivary glands, pancreas, kidneys, retroperitoneum, lungs, and bile ducts. Such involvement can lead to irreversible organ dysfunction and life-threatening complications [2, 3]. In Japan, a 2021 nationwide survey estimated the prevalence of autoimmune pancreatitis, a representative manifestation of IgG4-RD, at 13.3 per 100,000, a 25% increase from 2016 [4]. The disease predominantly affects middle-aged and elderly patients, with a male-to-female ratio of approximately 3:1, and affected patients often follow a severe clinical course and carry an increased risk of associated malignancy [3, 5].

Current treatment strategies rely primarily on glucocorticoids as first-line therapy. Although initial clinical responses are favorable, flare occurs frequently during de-escalation or discontinuation of glucocorticoid [6, 7], and there is a substantial risk of adverse events, particularly exacerbation of comorbidities such as diabetes mellitus and hypertension [8]. Immunosuppressants such as azathioprine, methotrexate, mycophenolate mofetil, and leflunomide are used in combination in some cases [9–12].

Given these limitations, there has been growing interest in molecularly targeted therapies directed at B cells. Although some studies have reported the efficacy of rituximab [13–15], a CD20-specific monoclonal antibody, it is not currently approved for this indication in Japan [16]. Moreover, CD19 is a more comprehensive therapeutic target. CD19-targeted antibodies may induce more extensive B cell depletion than CD20-targeted agents due to broader expression across B cell lineages [17, 18].

Inebilizumab is a monoclonal antibody that specifically targets the CD19 antigen. It obtained manufacturing and marketing approval in Japan in November 2025 for the treatment of IgG4-RD. It was first approved for neuromyelitis optica spectrum disorder (NMOSD) [19], and a phase 3 trial for generalized myasthenia gravis has also demonstrated its efficacy in improving function and reducing disease severity [20]. In the phase 3 MITIGATE trial [21], inebilizumab reduced the risk of flare and increased the rate of glucocorticoid-free complete remission compared with placebo. Regarding safety, while the overall adverse events were comparable between groups, the incidence of grade 3 or higher and serious adverse events was higher in the inebilizumab group (18% vs 12% and 18% vs 9%, respectively). Although the MITIGATE trial demonstrated the efficacy of inebilizumab, the Japanese subgroup was limited to only 20 cases in the treatment arm, receiving the study drug during the 52-week randomized controlled period. In addition, it remains uncertain whether the effectiveness observed in the trial will be replicated and sustained beyond 52 weeks in routine clinical practice in Japan. These limitations warrant the collection and evaluation of real-world data on effectiveness and safety in a broader patient population.

Objectives

To address the knowledge gap described above, we designed a prospective observational study (4SigHT Study) to evaluate the long-term effectiveness and safety of inebilizumab in a real-world clinical setting in Japan. The primary objective of this study is to describe the real-world use, effectiveness, and safety of inebilizumab using the IgG4-RD Responder Index [22] and organ-specific flare criteria as employed in the MITIGATE trial [21].

Methods

Study Design

This multicenter, prospective, non-interventional, observational study is designed to evaluate the long-term effectiveness and safety of inebilizumab in a real-world clinical setting in Japan. This study is scheduled to be conducted at up to 40 institutions across Japan. The study period is scheduled from March 1, 2026 (start of enrollment) to March 31, 2032 (end of observation), with enrollment ending on March 31, 2028 (Fig. 1). The observation schedule comprises 14 visits over up to 312 weeks per patient. Following enrollment and informed consent (visit 0), the clinical observation begins at visit 1 (week 0) with the first administration of inebilizumab. The primary outcome is evaluated at week 52 (visit 4), followed by observations every 26 weeks (visits 5–13) until the final assessment at week 312 (visit 14) or the study end date, whichever occurs first. In this observational study, all medical decisions, including inebilizumab initiation, are made by investigators as part of routine clinical practice. The study protocol was approved by the Non-Profit Organization MINS Institutional Review Board (IRB ID 12000060) and, where required, by the institutional ethics committees of each participating site. A complete list of all ethics committees and their reference numbers is provided in Supplemental Material 1. The study is conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and the Ethical Guidelines for Medical and Biological Research Involving Human Subjects (Japan). Written informed consent will be obtained from all participants prior to enrollment. Participants

Consistent with the approved product labeling for inebilizumab in Japan, the study enrolls patients with IgG4-RD who are scheduled to initiate inebilizumab, without imposing additional disease-specific restrictions beyond those specified in the labeling. Patients are eligible for inclusion if they meet all of the following criteria: (1) aged ≥ 18 years at the time of consent; (2) diagnosed with IgG4-RD as “definite” or “probable” according to the comprehensive diagnostic criteria (e.g., 2020 Revised Comprehensive Diagnostic (RCD) Criteria for IgG4-RD [24] or organ-specific diagnostic criteria [25–31] in effect at the time of consent); (3) scheduled to initiate inebilizumab treatment at a participating institution in Japan; (4) have available medical records covering at least the 52 weeks before the initiation of inebilizumab (or from disease onset if onset occurred within the past 52 weeks); and (5) have provided written informed consent.

Patients are excluded if they meet any of the following criteria: (1) lack the capacity to provide informed consent; (2) have contraindications to inebilizumab; (3) have received B cell-depleting therapy within 6 months prior to enrollment; or (4) have received molecular targeted agents other than inebilizumab within 6 months prior to enrollment. However, patients who completed the open-label period (OLP) of the MITIGATE trial [21] are eligible for enrollment regardless of prior inebilizumab exposure, while those who discontinued the MITIGATE trial (either the randomized controlled period or OLP) remain ineligible. Reflecting the observational design, no concomitant medications are mandated or prohibited beyond these exclusion criteria; all concomitant therapies are recorded in the electronic case report form (eCRF) throughout follow-up.

Treatment

In this real-world setting, inebilizumab (Uplizna® Intravenous Infusion 100 mg) is administered at the investigator’s discretion, typically in accordance with the dosage and administration guidelines specified in the approved product labeling in Japan. According to the labeling, the standard adult dosage is 300 mg of inebilizumab administered as an intravenous infusion at weeks 0 and 2, followed by maintenance doses of 300 mg every 6 months starting 6 months after the initial dose.

Measurements

Study Procedures and Enrollment

After obtaining written informed consent, investigators verify patient eligibility and formally enroll participants in the study. Although this is an observational study without strictly mandated visit dates, clinical observations are scheduled at specific time points to ensure longitudinal consistency. Assessments are performed at baseline (initiation of inebilizumab), at week 2, and every 6 months thereafter until the end of the observation period.

Baseline and Longitudinal Assessments

Baseline data are collected at enrollment (visit 0) and on the day of the first inebilizumab administration (visit 1). At visit 0, patient demographics, including age, sex, and race, are recorded. Regarding disease-specific characteristics, investigators will collect the initial diagnosis date and the total inclusion points according to the 2019 ACR/EULAR IgG4-RD classification criteria [2]. Fulfillment of either the Japanese comprehensive diagnostic criteria or the organ-specific diagnostic criteria in effect at the time of consent, and affected organs are also documented.

On the day of the first administration (visit 1), a comprehensive medical history review is conducted prior to dosing. This review includes viral infections (HBV, HCV, and HIV), co-existing autoimmune diseases, malignancies, and chronic conditions (e.g., diabetes, hypertension), as well as vaccination history (COVID-19, influenza, and pneumococcal vaccines). Furthermore, medication history, including glucocorticoid usage and other IgG4-RD treatments during the 52 weeks prior to enrollment, will be recorded.

Throughout the observation period (visits 2–14), investigators will evaluate disease activity and monitor safety through adverse event reporting and routine laboratory tests. A detailed schedule of all assessments is provided in Supplemental Material 2.

Disease Activity and Safety Assessments

A longitudinal evaluation is conducted at each study visit. Investigators assess disease activity using IgG4-RD Responder Index (copyright, Massachusetts General Hospital) [22] Scoring Sheet to evaluate the primary outcome. This index was originally developed in 2012 to address the need for a unified assessment across diverse organ manifestations [32]. The 2018 updated version established the current scoring system, which evaluates disease activity in 29 organs on a scale of 0 to 3, with the total score reflecting systemic disease activity. In this study, we use the certified Japanese version, validated in the 2018 update, and record the results in the eCRF.

During the study period, the investigator also reports information on treatments and interventions for IgG4-RD other than inebilizumab, including glucocorticoids and supportive care. Routine laboratory parameters, including serum IgG4 levels, are collected. Safety assessment involves the continuous collection of adverse events (AEs) throughout the study (Supplemental Material 3). Investigators will report all AEs, serious AEs (SAEs), AEs leading to discontinuation, and AEs of special interest (AESIs).

Flare Assessment and Adjudication

All organ-specific findings and suspected flares are subject to a rigorous, centralized adjudication process (Fig. 2 and Supplemental Material 4). The independent data and safety monitoring board (DSMB) comprises three external experts from institutions independent of the participating study sites: two physicians with extensive clinical experience in IgG4-RD and one board-certified radiologist with expertise in cross-sectional imaging review. None of the DSMB members are involved in the clinical care of any enrolled participant. This third-party committee independently adjudicates flare events, monitors safety signals, and may query investigators when additional information is needed. For any organ involvement, both the investigator and the DSMB evaluate the affected site using the standardized organ-specific flare criteria established in the MITIGATE trial protocol [33]. A suspected flare is defined as the emergence or worsening of signs and symptoms of IgG4-RD activity that meet at least one organ-specific criterion, in the absence of an alternative diagnosis or conflicting pathological findings. Throughout the observation period, investigators must evaluate any suspected flare based on clinical symptoms or findings, ideally before initiating new treatment. For each suspected event, the investigator is required to record detailed information in the eCRF, including assessment data, treatment initiated, and clinical outcomes.

According to this definition, a study-defined clinical flare is confirmed only when both of the following conditions are met: (1) the investigator has clinical suspicion and initiates additional treatment, and (2) the flare is adjudicated by the DSMB.

Planned Outcomes

Primary Outcome Measure

The primary outcome is the proportion of participants who experience a study-defined clinical flare of IgG4-RD at week 52 (visit 4) after receiving the first inebilizumab treatment.

Secondary and Long-term Outcomes Measures

Secondary outcomes include short-term assessments at week 52 (visit 4) and long-term evaluations through the end of the study. At week 52 (visit 4), secondary outcomes include the proportion of participants achieving complete remission without any additional treatment for IgG4-RD control, and the proportion of achieving complete remission without glucocorticoids. Complete remission is defined as no apparent disease activity (an IgG4-RD Responder Index score of 0 or no disease activity as determined by the investigator), no DSMB-adjudicated flare, and no relevant treatment for flare or disease control during the study period. Detailed definitions of complete remission and related outcome measures are provided in Supplemental Material 5.

Long-term secondary outcomes assessed through the end of the observation period include time to clinical flare, defined as the interval from the first administration to the date the investigator initiates necessary treatment (glucocorticoid initiation or dose increase, other immunotherapies, or interventions); the annual clinical flare rate, calculated as the incidence of adjudicated flares per year; the proportion of participants for whom investigators initiate treatment (medication or intervention) for new disease activity or worsening symptoms, regardless of DSMB flare adjudication, and the time to such initiation; and the cumulative glucocorticoid dose administered to control IgG4-RD. Other exploratory outcomes include the proportion of participants who did not receive glucocorticoids at week 8.

Safety Outcome Measures

Safety outcomes include the frequency and incidence rates of all AEs occurring after the initiation of inebilizumab, specifically SAEs, AESIs, and AEs leading to discontinuation. Clinical laboratory parameters will also be evaluated.

Sample Size and Power

The target sample size is 100 unique participants initiating inebilizumab. As this descriptive study does not test hypotheses via statistical inference, power calculations were not performed. The sample size was determined on the basis of feasibility, given the disease’s rarity. Participants who completed the OLP in the MITIGATE trial in Japan may be eligible for enrollment in this study. However, since these patients have already been treated with inebilizumab and do not represent de novo initiators, they are excluded from the primary analysis population, and they are not counted toward the target count.

Data Collection

Data management is conducted using an electronic data capture (EDC) system provided by TXP Medical Co., Ltd. (Tokyo, Japan). Investigators or authorized study personnel register the study in this system. Participants are assigned pseudonymized identification numbers. Data collection will continue for the full planned observation period, regardless of whether inebilizumab treatment is discontinued.

Data Analysis

A descriptive statistical approach will be used to summarize effectiveness and safety. Primary, secondary, and other outcome measures will be reported using appropriate summary statistics and visualization methods. Continuous variables will be presented as means with standard deviations or medians with interquartile ranges (IQR). Categorical variables will be presented as frequencies and proportions.

The primary populations include the full analysis set (≥ 1 dose plus post-baseline efficacy data) and the safety analysis set (≥ 1 dose). MITIGATE participants are excluded from these sets and are analyzed separately. Data will be locked annually commencing in 2026. Interim analyses will be conducted on the basis of monitoring of study progress. The final database lock is scheduled for 2032, immediately followed by the final comprehensive analysis.

Strengths and Limitations

Strengths

This multicenter, prospective, observational study has several strengths that support its clinical relevance and scientific value. First, this is Japan’s first multicenter prospective study initiated immediately after the introduction of inebilizumab for IgG4-RD. By recruiting patients in routine care, the study will systematically accumulate early real-world data on the long-term effectiveness and safety of inebilizumab. As a result, clinically relevant evidence can be provided promptly.

An additional strength of this study is its complementary relationship with the findings of the MITIGATE trial [21]. The MITIGATE trial, a randomized, double-blind, placebo-controlled trial of inebilizumab in IgG4-RD, achieved high internal validity. However, as previously discussed [34], its study population was limited to patients with recent flares, involvement of at least two organs, and discontinuation of all background immunosuppressive therapies. These criteria differ substantially from routine clinical practice, where immunosuppressants are commonly used in combination with glucocorticoids and treatment decisions must be made for patients with diverse disease severities. Moreover, the 52-week follow-up period is relatively short, leaving important questions about long-term B cell dynamics, infection risk, and the impact on irreversible organ fibrosis. The limited number of Japanese participants in the MITIGATE trial further raised concerns regarding its applicability to routine clinical practice in Japan. In contrast, this observational study captures a broader spectrum of patient characteristics through its less restrictive eligibility criteria, thereby improving our understanding of how inebilizumab performs across the diverse clinical scenarios encountered in daily practice.

Furthermore, this study enables evaluation of treatment patterns that are difficult to fully capture in randomized controlled trials. These include variations in glucocorticoid dose and duration, decisions regarding treatment of interruption or reinitiation, and the use of concomitant therapies. This study is expected to describe real-world treatment patterns, including patient profiles and the potential for glucocorticoid tapering, which may help inform future treatment strategies.

Another important strength of this study is its use of standardized and validated clinical assessment tools, including the IgG4-RD Responder Index [22] and organ-specific flare criteria [33]. As these outcome measures were also used in the MITIGATE trial, they facilitate comparison of clinical trial data with real-world evidence and clarify how trial findings can be applied in a routine clinical setting. Furthermore, the study employs the DSMB to centrally evaluate key outcomes, including flare, in a manner consistent with the MITIGATE trial [21, 33]. This centralized third-party assessment helps standardize outcome evaluation across institutions and strengthens the reliability of the real-world evidence generated.

In addition, this study will follow 100 patients with IgG4-RD for up to 6 years. As IgG4-RD is a rare disease, this study will provide a relatively large, long-term prospective cohort. The extended follow-up period will allow us to evaluate the durability of treatment effectiveness and long-term safety outcomes, including the risk of infection and changes in immunoglobulin levels associated with prolonged B cell depletion therapy. This is particularly important because IgG4-RD predominantly affects middle-aged and elderly individuals, for whom long-term immunosuppression raises substantial safety concerns. This long-term safety assessment will address a critical knowledge gap concerning the relationship between prolonged B cell depletion therapy and infection risk. Phase 3 trials of inebilizumab in NMOSD and long-term rituximab studies have shown gradual declines in serum IgG levels without a clear increase in infection rates [19, 35], whereas recent rheumatology data suggest that persistent B cell depletion may be associated with increased infection risk in some patients [36]. These conflicting findings indicate that the relationship between prolonged B cell depletion therapy and infection risk remains unclear. As IgG4-RD frequently affects older populations who are inherently at higher risk of infection [37], this study provides a valuable opportunity to generate long-term, disease-specific safety data on inebilizumab in a real-world clinical setting.

Limitations

Several limitations of this study should be acknowledged. First, because this is an observational study, it cannot establish a direct causal relationship between inebilizumab treatment and clinical outcomes.

Second, because the study does not include protocol-mandated treatment, it cannot fully control treatment discontinuation, dose modifications, or missing data from missed assessments. This may limit the interpretability of the results.

Third, the study is subject to several potential sources of bias. For example, selection bias may arise if patients initiating inebilizumab have a higher flare risk or glucocorticoid intolerance, resulting in a cohort that does not fully represent the broader IgG4-RD population. In addition, although flare and safety outcomes are centrally adjudicated by the DSMB, the underlying clinical information is derived from assessments made by treating investigators. As a result, residual evaluator bias cannot be completely ruled out.

Finally, the absence of a standardized imaging-monitoring protocol is a limitation common to IgG4-RD studies. Because this is a non-interventional study, the frequency, modality, and timing of imaging are determined by each site’s standard of care, which may affect the detection of subclinical organ involvement and the precision of flare adjudication. The MITIGATE trial similarly did not mandate a standardized radiological monitoring protocol [21, 33]. To partially mitigate this issue, all imaging data used to evaluate suspected flares are submitted to the DSMB for central review using uniform, organ-specific flare criteria. These limitations should be considered when interpreting the results, which should be evaluated alongside evidence from the MITIGATE trial.
