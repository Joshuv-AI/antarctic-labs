Generalized multi-dimensional conservation laws for stimulated Raman and Brillouin scattering in a density gradient
Vijay Patel, Sarah Chase, Frank S. Tsung, John P. Palastro, Denise E. Hinkel, Warren B. Mori

Abstract
Generalized local and multi-dimensional conservation laws of action, energy, momentum, and angular momentum are derived for stimulated Raman (SRS) and Brillouin backscattering (SBS) in a density gradient within the paraxial ray approximation. A Lagrangian density is given for which requiring the action be stationary reproduces the well known envelope equations for SRS and SBS in density gradients in the absence of damping. Using Noether’s theorem, the symmetries of the Lagrangian density are used to obtain local conservation laws for quantities that can easily be identified as the wave action, energy, and momentum. These multi-dimensional conservation laws reduce to the well known one dimensional Manley-Rowe relations, and frequency and wavenumber matching conditions. Additional symmetries of the action lead to conservation laws for new quantities that are identified as the orbital angular momentum and additional contributions to the energy and momentum of the wave from frequency and wavenumber shifts. Augmentation of the conservation laws in the presence of damping and extensions of the Lagrangian to include higher order corrections to the paraxial ray equation and nonlinear frequency shifts are also provided.

\displaystyle\partial_{t}(\frac{\omega_{\alpha}k_{\alpha,0}}{c_{\alpha}}|b_{\alpha}|^{2})+\partial_{z}(\frac{k_{\alpha,0}}{c_{\alpha}}{k_{\alpha}}|b_{\alpha}|^{2})+\nabla_{\perp}\cdot\frac{k_{\alpha,0}v_{\alpha}^{2}}{i2c_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})=-2\frac{\nu_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}k_{\alpha,0}\omega_{\alpha}|b_{\alpha}|^{2}.

(13)

In both cases, the E-L equations were augmented with the damping function. These conservation laws including loss from damping also reduce to their 1D counterparts when
\nabla_{\perp}
is set to zero.
The symmetries for action, quasi-energy, and quasi-momentum all arise from symmetries of the phases of the waves which are not linearly independent in which case the conservation laws are not linearly independent
[
41
]
.

2.1.2
Space Symmetries

In addition to internal symmetries that act only on the field variables themselves, there are also symmetries regarding transformations on space and time.
Since the action involves integration over time and space then these transformations
involve variations to the action arising from the end points.
If the Lagrangian depends explicitly on the space variables then the action cannot be invariant unless transformations are made to the part of the Lagrangian that depends explicitly on the space variable. If the symmetry is parameterized by
\epsilon
to one of the components of the four vector, or to a linear combination of these components , then the relevant conservation laws can be systematically obtained by differentiating the Lagrangian with respect to each space variable,

\displaystyle d_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}=\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{\partial L}}{\partial b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\epsilon}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\mathcal{\partial L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\epsilon}\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}+c.c.\bigg)+\partial_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}

(14)

Integrating by parts and using the augmented E-L equations that include damping
leads to the following generalized form for the conservation law,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{\partial L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\epsilon}b_{\alpha}+c.c.\bigg)-d_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\epsilon}\mathcal{L}+(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\epsilon}b_{\alpha}+c.c.\bigg)

(15)

where for
\partial_{\epsilon}
operating on
\mathcal{L}
the field variables
b_{\alpha}
and other spatial variables are kept fixed while for
d_{\epsilon}
and
\partial_{\mu}
only the other spatial variables are kept fixed.

Energy Conservation

As is well known from mechanics, energy and momentum conservation arises from the invariance of the action to
space and time translations. In this case

x^{\nu}\mapsto x^{\nu}+\epsilon
so that
d_{\epsilon}=\equiv\partial_{\nu}
where one is keeping the other spatial (spacetime) variables fixed but allowing the field variables to vary. We note that we are in flat space time so there is no distinction between covariant and contravariant variables. If the Lagrangian has both damping and an explicit spacetime dependence as in our case, then the energy-momentum tensor conservation equation is modified to,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}T^{\mu\nu}

\displaystyle=-\partial_{\epsilon=\nu}\mathcal{L}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\nu}b_{\alpha}+c.c.

(16)

where
T^{\mu\nu}=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\nu}b_{\alpha}+c.c.)-\delta^{\mu\nu}\mathcal{L}
is the energy-momentum tensor.
Applying Eqn.
16
with
\nu\rightarrow 0
(i.e.,
\nu\rightarrow t
) yields an energy conservation equation with the energy densities and fluxes given by,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=T^{00}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})-\mathcal{L}

(17)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{i}=T^{i0}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{i}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{i}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(18)

where for the SRS Lagrangian there is no explicit time dependence so
\partial_{\epsilon=t}\mathcal{L}
vanishes. When evaluated using the E-L equations, the Lagrangian takes the form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}|_{E-L}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4c_{\alpha}}\bigg(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}b_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}b_{\alpha}^{*}+2\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}\bigg)

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4c_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\perp}\cdot(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(19)

Inserting this into the expression for the energy density and energy flux (eqns.
17
and
18
) gives,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})

\displaystyle-\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4c_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\perp}\cdot(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(20)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{z}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}v_{g,\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-b_{\alpha}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(21)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{2c_{\alpha}}(\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha})

(22)

Since the second term for the energy density is a total divergence, we can absorb the time derivative of this into the energy flux, yielding a simplified form for the conserved quantities,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-b_{\alpha}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(23)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{z}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}v_{g,\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-b_{\alpha}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(24)

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4c_{\alpha}}(\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{t}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(25)

The full energy conservation equation can therefore be written as,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathcal{E}

\displaystyle+\partial_{z}\mathcal{S}^{z}+\nabla_{\perp}\cdot\mathcal{S}^{\perp}=-\frac{\omega_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}}{ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}-\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}b_{\alpha})

(26)

and importantly, this is distinct from the conservation of quasi-energy equation (Eqn. 12).

z
-momentum

The symmetry according to
z
-momentum invariance is parametrized by
\epsilon=z
so that
d_{\epsilon}=\partial_{z}
and
\partial_{\epsilon}=\partial_{\epsilon=z}
. In the inhomogeneous plasma case, this is not an exact symmetry of the Lagrangian; nevertheless, we can still write down an equation for how the
z
-momentum is modified based on Eqns.
15
and
16
,

\displaystyle\partial_{\mu}\bigg(\frac{\mathcal{\partial L}}{\partial\partial_{\mu}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}+c.c.\bigg)-\partial^{z}\mathcal{L}=\bigg(\frac{\partial F}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial^{z}b_{\alpha}+c.c.\bigg)

Hence, in this case we just need to add the term
-\partial_{\epsilon=z}\mathcal{L}=i(b_{0}b_{1}b_{2}^{*}e^{-i\phi(z)}-b_{0}^{*}b_{1}^{*}b_{2}e^{i\phi(z)})\phi^{\prime}(z)
to the RHS of the conservation equation.
The corresponding
z
-momentum density and current are given as follows,

\displaystyle p_{z}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(27)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{z}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*})-\mathcal{L}

(28)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(29)

which yields (when evaluated for the E-L eqns),

\displaystyle p_{z}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-b_{\alpha}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha})

(30)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{z}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\bigg(\frac{v_{g,\alpha}}{2i}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*})

\displaystyle-\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4\omega_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\perp}\cdot(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})\bigg)

(31)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\perp}=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{2c_{\alpha}}(\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle+\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha})

(32)

and importantly
\mathcal{P}^{z}
has no explicit
z
dependence. As was the case for the energy density, the transverse divergence in the momentum flux in the
z
direction
can again be absorbed into the
\perp
-flux,

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\frac{v_{g,\alpha}}{2i}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(33)

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{4c_{\alpha}}(\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}+\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(34)

It is now easily demonstrated from the E-L equations that the modification to the conservation of
z
-momentum in the presence of a density gradient and damping is given as follows,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{z}+\partial_{z}\mathcal{P}^{z}+\nabla_{\perp}\cdot\mathcal{P}^{\perp}

\displaystyle=i(b_{0}b_{1}b_{2}^{*}e^{-i\phi(z)}-b_{0}^{*}b_{1}^{*}b_{2}e^{i\phi(z)})\phi^{\prime}(z)+\frac{i\omega_{\alpha}\nu_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}-\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*}b_{\alpha})

It should be emphasized that while the damping term is always a momentum sink, the gradient term can either be a source or a sink depending on the sign of
\phi^{\prime}
. For instance, if
\phi^{\prime}(z)>0
, then the wavenumbers of the pump and plasma wave decrease at a faster rate than the rate at which the daughter light wave wavenumber increases due to scattering, leading to a net loss in forward momentum.

Total Energy and total Momentum

We can explain how the energy derived from the Lagrangian based in the invariance of the action due to time translation relates to the quasi-energy derived from conservation of action (phase symmetry). For simplicity, we address this question for the 1d case where there are no transverse gradients. In Fourier space, Eqn.
23
takes the following form,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\int wb_{\alpha}^{*}(w^{\prime})b_{\alpha}(w)\cos{(w-w^{\prime})t}dwdw^{\prime}

(35)

By averaging the above equation over timescales
\tau
much longer than the temporal variation of the envelope, that is
\tau\gg 1/\delta\omega_{\alpha}\gg 1/\omega_{\alpha}
, one obtains a useful form for the energy density,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}\int wb_{\alpha}^{*}(w)b_{\alpha}(w)dw

(36)

Thus in Fourier space, one has the following decomposition for the sum of the new expression for the energy and the quasi-energy,

\displaystyle u_{tot,\alpha}=u_{env}+u_{lag}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}(\omega_{\alpha}+\delta\omega)|b_{\alpha}(\omega_{\alpha}+\delta\omega,k_{\alpha})|^{2}

Heuristically, one can also interpret Eqn.
6
in real space by writing
b_{\alpha}=|b_{\alpha}|e^{i\theta_{\alpha}}

[
43
]
, where
\theta_{\alpha}
is the phase of the wave
envelope
. This leads to,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\propto\partial_{t}\theta_{\alpha}|b_{\alpha}|^{2}

and upon recognizing that
\partial_{t}\theta_{\alpha}=\delta\omega_{\alpha}
, we have
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}\propto\delta\omega_{\alpha}|b_{\alpha}|^{2}
so that,

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{\alpha,total}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}(\omega_{\alpha}+\partial_{t}\theta_{\alpha})|b_{\alpha}(\omega_{\alpha}+\partial_{t}\theta_{\alpha},k_{\alpha})|^{2}

The total energy density is the sum of the quasi-energy which is the energy stored in the wave packets themselves and the additional contribution from the temporal variation of the phase of the wave envelope. A similar interpretation holds for the total
z
-momentum density as well, where the frequencies are replaced with wavenumbers,

\displaystyle p_{z,tot,\alpha}=p_{z,env}+p_{z,action}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}(k_{\alpha}+\delta k)|b_{\alpha}(\omega_{\alpha},k_{\alpha}+\delta k)|^{2}

while in real space,

\displaystyle p_{z,tot,\alpha}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{c_{\alpha}}(k_{\alpha}+\partial_{z}\theta_{\alpha})|b_{\alpha}(\omega_{\alpha},k_{\alpha}+\delta k)|^{2}

\perp
-momentum conservation

The independence (symmetry) of the Lagrangian to the direction transverse to propagation gives us a momentum conservation law in the two orthogonal directions. The differential is
d_{\epsilon}=\partial_{a}
where
a=x,y
. The
\perp
-momentum density and momentum tensor are given as follows,

\displaystyle p_{\perp}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(37)

\displaystyle T^{ab}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}^{*})-\delta_{ab}\mathcal{L}

(38)

The momentum tensor, transverse momentum density, and
\perp
-momentum flux in the
z
-direction can then be simplified as follows,

\displaystyle p_{\perp}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(39)

\displaystyle T^{zb}=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}v_{g\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}

\displaystyle-\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(40)

\displaystyle T^{ab}=\frac{v_{\alpha}^{2}}{2c_{\alpha}}\bigg(\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}+\partial_{b}b_{\alpha}\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}^{*}

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\delta_{ab}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}b_{\alpha}+b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}^{2}b_{\alpha}^{*})-\delta_{ab}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*}\cdot\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}\bigg)

such that the
\perp
-momentum conservation equation (with damping) becomes,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}p_{\perp}+\partial_{z}T^{zb}+\partial_{a}T^{ab}

\displaystyle=-\sum\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\nabla_{\perp}b_{\alpha}^{*})

Angular Momentum Conservation

Finally, invariance under rotations around the axial
axis yields an angular momentum conservation law identical to OAM conservation. In this case
d_{\epsilon}=\partial_{\phi}
where
\partial_{\phi}=x\partial_{y}-y\partial_{x}
.
The angular momentum density and flux is given by:

\displaystyle\L_{z}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{t}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(42)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{z}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{z}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}^{*})

(43)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{a}

\displaystyle=(\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}+\frac{\partial\mathcal{L}}{\partial\partial_{a}b_{\alpha}^{*}}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}^{*})-(y\delta_{ax}-x\delta_{ay})\mathcal{L}

(44)

\displaystyle=\epsilon_{zbc}x^{b}T^{ac}

The density and fluxes can be written explicitly as,

\displaystyle L_{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha})

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{z}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega_{\alpha}v_{g,\alpha}}{2ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}^{*})

Finally, the damping rate that appears on the RHS of the conservation equation is,

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega_{\alpha}}{ic_{\alpha}}(b_{\alpha}^{*}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}-b_{\alpha}\partial_{\phi}b_{\alpha}^{*})

and the conservation law for OAM can now be written as,

\displaystyle\partial_{t}L_{z}+\partial_{z}\mathcal{M}^{z}+\partial_{a}\mathcal{M}^{a}=\mathcal{R}

We stress that such a conservation law holds for SRS or SBS growing from noise or from a seed. It also applies to a multi-speckled beam.

OAM for light waves

As described in
[
4
]
the above expressions for the OAM of a light wave can be derived from the definition of angular momentum in the electromagnetic fields,
\mathbf{L_{EM}}=\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{P}
where
\mathbf{P}=\frac{\mathbf{E}\times\mathbf{B}}{4\pi c}
.
Recall that in the slowly varying envelope approximation the vector potential in the Coulomb gauge for a linearly polarized light wave is written as:

\displaystyle\mathbf{A}(\mathbf{x})

\displaystyle=\mathbf{\hat{x}}u(x,y,z)e^{i(kz-\omega t)}

In terms of the envelopes, the momentum density for a light wave is
\mathbf{P}=\frac{1}{2}(\mathbf{E}^{*}\times\mathbf{B}+c.c.)
which in terms of the vector potential can be written as
\frac{i}{2}\omega(\nabla\mathbf{A}^{*}\cdot\mathbf{A}-c.c.)
. Rewriting this in terms of the envelope leads to
[
4
]

\displaystyle\mathbf{P}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{2i}(u^{*}\nabla u-u\nabla u^{*})+\omega k|u|^{2}\mathbf{\hat{z}}

The
z
-angular momentum density is then:

\displaystyle L_{z}

\displaystyle=(\mathbf{r}\times\mathbf{P})\cdot\mathbf{\hat{z}}=xP_{y}-yP_{x}

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{2i}(u^{*}\partial_{\phi}u-u\partial_{\phi}u^{*})

which agrees precisely with the angular momentum derived from the Lagrangian. We note that it is straightforward to extend this to allow for circularly polarized light and the associated spin angular momentum
[
4
]
.

OAM for plasma waves

The orbital angular momentum of the plasma waves requires a slightly more involved treatment in order to see its physical origin. Following
[
15
]
, the fluid equation describing the density perturbation reads, in the electrostatic approximation:

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\mathbf{v}_{e,\perp}

\displaystyle=\frac{e}{m_{e}}\nabla_{\perp}\varphi-\frac{\gamma_{e}v_{t}^{2}}{m_{e}}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}

where
n_{e}
is the electron density and
\gamma_{e}=3
is the coefficient in the adiabatic gas law for the equation of state for the plasma electrons. The electrostatic potential
\varphi
can be solved via Gauss’ law:

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\varphi=-k^{2}\varphi+\nabla_{\perp}^{2}\varphi=-\frac{4\pi e}{n_{e,0}}\tilde{n}_{e}

solving for
\varphi
and Taylor expanding to leading order in the transverse derivatives yields

\displaystyle\varphi\approx-\frac{4\pi e}{n_{e,0}}\tilde{n}_{e}

and inserting this into the fluid equation leads to,

\displaystyle-i\omega\mathbf{v}_{e,\perp}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\omega_{p}^{2}}{k^{2}}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}-\frac{\gamma_{e}v_{t}^{2}}{m_{e}}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{k^{2}}\omega^{2}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}

where we have identified
\omega^{2}=\omega_{p}^{2}+\frac{\gamma_{e}v_{t}^{2}}{m_{e}}k^{2}
.
The time-averaged perpendicular momentum density in normalized units is therefore,

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{P}}_{\perp,e}\equiv\frac{\langle\mathbf{P}_{\perp,e}\rangle}{n_{e,0}m_{e}c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4c}(\tilde{n}_{e}^{*}\mathbf{v}_{\perp,e}+\tilde{n}_{e}\mathbf{v}_{\perp,e}^{*})

\displaystyle=\frac{\omega}{4ick^{2}}(\tilde{n}_{e}^{*}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}-\tilde{n}_{e}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}^{*})

Recall that
\Gamma_{2}=c^{2}k^{2}/\omega_{p}^{2}
so we have:

\displaystyle\frac{\langle\mathbf{P}_{\perp,e}\rangle}{n_{e,0}m_{e}c}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4i\Gamma_{2}}\frac{\omega}{\omega_{p}}(\tilde{n}_{e}^{*}\frac{c}{\omega_{p}}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}-\tilde{n}_{e}\frac{c}{\omega_{p}}\nabla_{\perp}\tilde{n}_{e}^{*})

From this, one can see that the
z
-component of the angular momentum for the electrons agrees with the expression from the Lagrangian when written in normalized units.

2.1.3
OAM Index of an arbitrary wave

The conservation of OAM equation can be used to investigate under what circumstances the conservation of
L_{z}
can be described as an
\ell
matching condition, in analogy with how the conservation of energy and momentum are related to

\omega
and
k
matching conditions . In
[
4
]
it was shown that the OAM per photon of a pure Laguerre Gaussian mode, where the envelope is proportional to
e^{i\ell\phi}
, is

\ell\hbar
. We can use the conservation law for
\L_{z}
to define an effective OAM index for any envelope
[
15
]
.
The effective OAM index for wave
j
is the ratio of the
angular momentum density integrated over
d\mathbf{x}_{\perp}
to the total
action integrated over
d\mathbf{x}_{\perp}
,

\bar{\ell}_{j}=\frac{-\frac{i\omega_{j}}{2}\int\left(b_{j}^{*}\partial_{\phi}b_{j}-b_{j}\partial_{\phi}b_{j}^{*}\right)d\mathbf{x_{\perp}}}{\omega_{j}\int|b_{j}|^{2}d\mathbf{x_{\perp}}}.

(45)

In general, we can expand an arbitrary envelope,
b
, as a superposition of OAM states each with integer
\ell
,

b(\mathbf{x_{\perp}})=\sum_{\ell=-\infty}^{\infty}b_{\ell}(r)e^{i\ell\phi}

(46)

where the effective OAM index,
\bar{\ell}
, (equation
45
) is the weighted average,

\bar{\ell}=\frac{\sum_{\ell}\ell|b_{\ell}|^{2}}{\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}|b_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{2}}

(47)

For a pure Laguerre Gaussian envelope in vacuum there is a term
e^{i\ell_{0}\phi}
, the index
\ell_{0}
is recovered. Note that the action of the wave with frequency
\omega_{j}
is defined as
\mathbf{a}_{j}\equiv\omega_{j}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}|b_{\ell^{\prime}}|^{2}
so the OAM of the wave is
\bar{\ell}_{j}\mathbf{a}_{j}
.

In the literature the concept of
\ell
matching has been invoked with respect to SRS
[
39
]
. This can be easily argued if each wave is a pure OAM Laguerre-Gaussian mode. Consider
b_{0}\propto e^{il_{0}\phi}
,
b_{1}\propto e^{il_{1}\phi}
, and
b_{2}\propto e^{il_{2}\phi}
then in order for each wave to be resonantly driven then the RHS of each wave equation (Eqns.
1
to
3
) must have the same
\ell
as that on the LHS
\ell_{j}
, which leads directly to
\ell_{0}=\ell_{1}+\ell_{2}
.

However, this implies that the
\ell_{j}
number of each wave cannot change. The same assumption is true for
\omega
and
k
matching, where it is implicitly assumed that the
\omega_{j}
and
k_{j}
of each wave does not change. However, these quantities are not part of the envelope, while OAM is an intrinsic property of the envelope itself.
Thus, the OAM selection rule or
\ell
matching is more subtle than for
\omega
and
k
.

To clarify the above discussion, it is illustrative to see how
\omega
matching follows from the conservation of energy equation. The same arguments apply to
k
matching and the conservation of momentum equation. Consider the conservation of energy equation after integrating over
d\mathbf{x_{\perp}}
,

\partial_{t}\left(\omega_{0}\bar{a}_{0}+\omega_{1}\bar{a}_{1}+\omega_{2}\bar{a}_{2}\right)+\partial_{z}\left(v_{g0}\omega_{0}\bar{a}_{0}+v_{g1}\omega_{1}\bar{a}_{1}+v_{g2}\omega_{2}\bar{a}_{2}\right)=0

(48)

where
\bar{a}\equiv\int d\mathbf{x_{\perp}}\mathbf{a}
and
\mathbf{a}_{2}
is defined as
\frac{1}{\Gamma_{2}}|b_{2}|^{2}
. This equation can be rewritten as

\bar{a}_{0}(\partial_{t}+v_{g0}\partial_{z})\omega_{0}+\bar{a}_{1}(\partial_{t}+v_{g1}\partial_{z})\omega_{1}+\bar{a}_{2}(\partial_{t}+v_{g2}\partial_{z})\omega_{2}\\
+\omega_{0}(\partial_{t}\bar{a}_{0}+\partial_{z}v_{g0}\bar{a}_{0})+\omega_{1}(\partial_{t}\bar{a}_{1}+\partial_{z}v_{g1}\bar{a}_{1})+\omega_{2}(\partial_{t}\bar{a}_{2}+\partial_{z}v_{g2}\bar{a}_{2})=0

(49)

which can then be rewritten as

\bar{a}_{0}(\partial_{t}+v_{g0}\partial_{z})\omega_{0}+\bar{a}_{1}(\partial_{t}+v_{g1}\partial_{z})\omega_{1}+\bar{a}_{2}(\partial_{t}+v_{g2}\partial_{z})\omega_{2}\\
+(\omega_{0}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})(\partial_{t}\bar{a}_{0}+\partial_{z}v_{g0}\bar{a}_{0})=0

(50)

if the conservation of action equations are used. This equation is satisfied if
\omega
matching,
\omega_{0}-\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}=0
, and each frequency is constant. Thus,
\omega
matching follows from energy conservation only if each
\omega_{j}
does not change. Analogous conclusions can be made for
k
matching by combining the momentum and conservation of action equations.

Following the same arguments, we can combine conservation of OAM with the conservation of action equations leading to,

3
Extending the Lagrangian

To properly study some problems of interest the starting equations might need to be modified. For example, higher order corrections to the paraxial ray equations, including full temporal operators, and the addition of nonlinear frequency shifts to the EPW or IAW might be needed. In this section, we show how the Lagrangian can be straightforwardly modified to include some of these effects. We also mention how the conservation laws would be augmented for the full temporal operator.

Summary

A Lagrangian formulation of 3-wave coupled equations that describe SRS and SBS backscatter within the paraxial ray approximation that also accounts for a
slowly-varying density gradient is presented. Recasting the three-wave equations for the envelopes of each wave in terms of a Lagrangian provides a systematic procedure based on Noether’s theorem for identifying conservation laws for the system. We show how to augment the conservation laws to include sinks from damping. Some of these 3D conservation laws reduce to well known 1D versions of the Manley-Rowe relations (conservation of action) and conservation of energy and momentum. However, the 3D versions are new and additional conservation laws (even in 1D) are found for the energy, momentum, and OAM of the wave envelopes. These conservation laws hold for SRS and/or SBS arising from a diffraction limited or a multi-speckled pump with or without bandwidth, from noise, or from a well defined seed. Using the OAM equation, we define an OAM index for each wave and discuss under what conditions there is matching between the OAM indices. While interesting in their own right, these conservation laws also provide equations to test simulation codes, such as pF3D, that are based on the three coupled wave equations. Modifications to the Lagrangian to include strongly coupled SRS and SBS and nonlinear frequency shifts to the electrostatic waves are also given. Finally, we note that the Lagrangian itself can also have other uses. For example, trial functions that have well defined transverse properties/parameters that depend on (z,t) can be substituted into the action and then integration over the transverse variables can be carried out. Variation of this reduced Lagrangian provides 1D Euler-Lagrange equations for the parameters. In addition, trial functions that describe convective or absolute growth can be substituted into the Lagrangian to aid in determining thresholds. We leave these later ideas for future work.
