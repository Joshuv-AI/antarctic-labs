Quasiregular values from generalized manifold with controlled geometry
Deguang Zhong

Abstract
The main aim of this paper is to establish the Reshetnyak’s theorem for quasiregualr values from generalized n n -manifold with suitable controlled geometry to Euclidean space ℝ n . \mathbb{R}^{n}. This generalizes a previous result due to Kangasniemi and Onninen on the setting of Euclidean space [A single-point Reshetnyak’s theorem, Trans. Amer. Math. Soc., 𝟑𝟕𝟖 \mathbf{378} (2025): 3105-3128].



1
Introduction

In the 1960s
[
Res67
]
, Yu. G. Reshetnyak introduced the following concept of quasiregular maps or maps of bounded distortion in Euclidean space.

Definition 1.1
.

[
Res67
]

For a given constant
K\geq 1
and
n\geq 2.
Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open connected set. Then, a mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called quasiregular or a mapping of bounded distortion if it belongs to
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)

for almost every
x\in\Omega.

If the constant
K
in the Definition
1.1
is replaced by a measure function
K:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty),
then it leads to the definition of finite distortion mappings; c.f.
[
HK14
,
IM01
]
.

Within the framework of nonlinear elasticity, investigating the topological features of finite distortion mappings
f
in
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
with distortion
K
is of crucial importance. A deep theorem of Reshetnyak
[
Res67
]
states that each (locally non-constant) quasiregular map in a domain of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
is discrete, open, and sense-preserving. Here we say a mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is discrete if for every
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},
the set
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
consists of isolated points. A map
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is said to be open if
f(U)
is open set whenever
U
is any open set of
\Omega.
A continuous map is said to be sense-preserving if
{\deg}(y,f,D)>0
whenever
D
is a domain which compactly contained in
\Omega
and
y\in f(D)\setminus f(\partial D).
J. M. Ball
[
Bal77
,
Bal81
]
showed that the quasiregularity is too strong for some problems in nonlinear elasticity. Hence, it is natural to consider the case for
K\in L_{loc}^{p}
for some
p>0.
For existing results on this problem in Euclidean spaces, we refer the reader to
[
Bal81
,
IS93
,
HK93
,
VM98
,
Bjö
,
HM02
,
HR13
,
KOZ26
]
. For the non-Euclidean setting, we refer to
[
HH97
,
Zap08
,
Kir16
,
BV23
,
MR25
,
Zho251
]
.
In particular, recently Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO222
]
has extended Reshetnyak’s theorem to the case of quasiregular values in Euclidean spaces. Namely, they showed that a non-constant map
f\in W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfying the inequality

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)|f(x)-y_{0}|^{n},

where
K\geq 1
is a constant,
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{1+\varepsilon}(\Omega)
for some
\varepsilon>0,
then
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
is discrete, the local index
i(x,f)
is positive in
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
, and every neighborhood of every point of
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
is mapped to a neighborhood of
y_{0}.

The main goal of this paper is to generalizes the above result of Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO222
]
for quasiregualr values from generalized
n
-manifold with suitable controlled geometry to Euclidean space
\mathbb{R}^{n}.
It is read as follows.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

be a constant,
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

\mathcal{S}\subset\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a generalized

-manifolds with controlled geometry in the sense of subsection

\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
be a domain. Suppose that

is an nonconstant mapping satisfying the following inequality

\|apDf(x)\|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)|f-y_{0}|^{n}

(1)

for almost every
x\in\Omega.
If
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1,
then
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
is discrete, the local index
i(x,f)
is positive for every

2.1
Newtonian spaces

The objects of this paper is the generalized
n
-manifold, which in general does not admit a smooth structure. Consequently, the theory of Sobolev spaces in the smooth setting is no longer applicable. Instead, we employ the theory of Newtonian spaces on metric measure spaces. Newtonian spaces, introduced by N. Shanmugalingam in
[
Sha00
]
, provide an analogue of Sobolev spaces in the metric context. This theory, which relies on the concept of upper gradients, is applicable to metric spaces that are rich enough to contain sufficiently many rectifiable paths. The main references in this subsection are taken from
[
Sha00
,
HKST01
,
HKST15
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

Suppose that
(X,\mu)
is a metric measure space, where
\mu
is a Borel regular measure. Let
\Gamma
be collection of paths in
X
and
p\geq 1.
Then a Borel function

\rho:X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be admissible for
\Gamma
if the following inequality

\int_{\gamma}\rho ds\geq 1

holds for every locally rectifiable
\gamma\in\Gamma.
In this case, we donate
\rho\in{\rm Adm}\Gamma.
Then, the
p
-modulus of
\Gamma
is defined by

{\rm Mod}_{p}\Gamma:=\inf\int_{X}\rho(x)^{p}d\mu,

where the infimum is taken over all admissible functions
\rho\in{\rm Adm}\Gamma.

Definition 2.2
.

Suppose that
f:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
is a mapping from metric spaces
X
to
\overline{\mathbb{R}}.
Then, a Borel function
g:X\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is said to be an upper gradient of
f
if the following inequality

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq\int_{\gamma}gds

(2)

holds for every locally rectifiable path
\gamma:[0,1]\rightarrow X
with
\gamma(0)=x
and
\gamma(1)=y.
Moreover, we say
g
is a weak upper gradient of
f
if there exists a path family

\Gamma_{0},
such that
{\rm Mod}_{p}(\Gamma_{0})=0
and the inequality (
2
) holds for every
\gamma\notin\Gamma_{0}.

Definition 2.3
.

(Newtonian spaces)
[
Sha00
]

Suppose that
(X,\mu)
is a metric space and
p\geq 1.
Then, we say a real-value mapping
f:X\rightarrow\overline{\mathbb{R}}
belongs to
N^{1,p}(X)
if
f\in L^{p}(X)
and has
a
p
-integrable
p
-weak upper gradient. We say a mapping
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
belongs to
N^{1,p}(X,\mathbb{R}^{m})
if its every component function belongs to
N^{1,p}(X).

A real-value mapping
f\in N_{loc}^{1,p}(X)
if
f\in N^{1,p}(D)
for every compact

Further, we say

D\subset X.

Definition 2.4
.

(Weak Sobolev-Poincaré inequality)
[
HKST01
,
HKST15
]

Let
1\leq p<q
and
(X,\mu)
be a metric measure space. Then, the space
X
is said to support a weak Sobolev-Poincaré inequality if there exist constants
C>0
and

such that the following inequality

\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;\left|u-u_{B(x,r)}\right|^{q}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/q}\leq C{\rm diam}(B)\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;g^{p}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/p}

(3)

for every

and for every integrable functions

in
B(x,r)
and for all
p
-weak upper gradients

of

The case for

p
-Poincaré inequality.

2.2.7
Generalized
n
-manifolds with controlled geometry

By an oriented generalized
n
-manifold

with controlled geometry we mean an oriented generalized

-manifold

This class of spaces fits into the framework of controlled geometry in the sense of Heinonen–Koskela

]

]
states that if a metric measure space

supports a weak

Together with the local homological properties of a generalized manifold, these conditions ensure that analytic and topological tools—including proper maps, degrees, and Sobolev regularity—are well-defined and stable.

In addition, it should be noted that as

supports a weak

-Poincaré inequality for all

it implies from
[

, Theorem 8.3.2 and Corollary 9.1.36]
that

supports the following Sobolev-Poincaré inequality

\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;\left|u-u_{B(x,r)}\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C{\rm diam}(B)\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;\left|g\right|^{p}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/p}

(7)

for every

with

and for every integrable functions

in

and for all upper gradients

of

Combing this with the density of Lipschitz

and
[

\left(\int_{B(x,r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;\left|f_{j}-(f_{j})_{B(x,r)}\right|^{\frac{np}{n-p}}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-p}{np}}\leq C{\rm diam}(B)\left(\int_{B(x,\lambda r)}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\;\,\;\;\,\,\,\;\;\left|apDf\right|^{p}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/p}

2.3
Quasiregular values and values of finite distortion

As mentioned in the introduction, Yu. G. Reshetnyak
[
Res67
]
introduced the following concept of quasiregular maps or maps of bounded distortion in Euclidean space in the 1960s.

Definition 2.13
.

[
Res67
]

For a given constant
K\geq 1
and
n\geq 2.
Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is an open connected set. Then, a mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is called quasiregular or a mapping of bounded distortion if it belongs to
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)

for almost every
x\in\Omega.

[

,
HK14
,
IM01
,
Guo15
,
GGWX25
,
GW16
]
. For a given constant
K\geq 1.
Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n},n\geq 2,
is a domain and
\Sigma:\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is a measure function. Then, a mapping

belongs to the class of Sobolev space
W_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

|Df(x)|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)|f(x)|^{n}

(9)

for almost every

was introduced by Astala, Iwaniec and Martin
[

, Section 8.5]
. The inequality (

[

]
.
For the properties and applications for mappings relative to inequality (
9
), we refer the reader to
[

,
FS58
,
Har58
,
Sim77
,
AP06
]
. Recently, Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO222
]
give the following concept of quasiregular values.

Definition 2.14
.

[
KO222
, Definition 1.1]

For a given constant
K\geq 1.
Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain,

We refer the reader to

generalized this kinds of mappings satisfying inequality (
11
) to generalized finite distortion curves from Euclidean domain to Riemannian manifolds. Based on the developments for quasiregular maps and its generalizations discussed above, the concept for analogous mappings satisfying inequality (
11
) from generalized
n
-manifolds with controlled geometry to Euclidean space is natural to give; see Definition
2.16
.

Definition 2.15
.

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Then, by a result in
[
Kir14
, Corollary 2.17]
, we see that
f
is approximately differentiable
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-almost everywhere. This
approximate derivative
{\rm ap}Df(x):apTan(x,\mathcal{S})-x\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a linear mapping from the shifted approximate tangent plane
apTan(x,\mathcal{S});
c.f.
[
Fed69
, Definition 3.2.16]
.
Its Jacobian determinatant is defined by
J_{f}:={\rm det\,ap}Df(x).

Definition 2.16
.

We say that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
is a generalized finite distortion map if it satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)

(12)

for almost everywhere
x\in\Omega.
Here
K:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty)
and
\Sigma:\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are the measurable functions, and
\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|
is defined by

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|:=\sup_{|y|=1}|apDf(x)y|.

The following concept of values of finite distortion is analogical to the one in the setting of Euclidean space given by Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO25
]
.

Definition 2.17
.

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Then
f
is said to be having a
(K,\Sigma)
-finite distortion value at
y_{0}
if it satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)|f(x)-y_{0}|^{n}

(13)

for almost everywhere
x\in\Omega.
Here
K:\Omega\rightarrow[1,\infty)
and
\Sigma:\Omega\rightarrow[0,\infty)
are measurable functions.

Remark
.

It is worth noting that in Definitions
2.16
and
2.17
, it still allows for
\Omega
to have regions where
J_{f}
is negative.

3
Hölder regularity for generalized finite distortion and its applications

3.1
Hölder regularity for generalized finite distortion mappings

In this section, we will establish the Hölder regularity for generalized finite distortion. In order to do that, we first need the following lemma which obtained by Kirsilä
[
Kir16
, Theorem 3.1]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

[
Kir16
, Theorem 3.1]

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
is a relatively compact open set and
\eta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz mapping with

{\rm spt}\eta\subset\Omega.
Then the following equation

\int_{\Omega}\eta J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}=-\int_{\Omega}f_{j}J(f_{1},\cdots,f_{j-1},\eta,f_{j+1},\cdots,f_{n})d\mathcal{H}^{n}

(14)

holds for each
1\leq j\leq n.

Theorem 3.2
.

Suppose that
f
belongs to
N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)

(15)

for almost everywhere
x\in\mathcal{S}.
If
K\in L_{loc}^{p}(\mathcal{S})
for some
p>n
and
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})
for some
\varepsilon>0,
then
f
is locally Hölder continuous.

Proof.

By considering the mapping
g:=(f_{1}-(f_{1})_{2B},f_{2},\cdots,f_{n}),
we get according to Lemma
3.1
that

\displaystyle\left|\int_{2B}\eta J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right|\leq\frac{c(n)}{r}\int_{2B}\left|f_{1}-(f_{1})_{2B}\right|\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}K^{\frac{n-1}{n}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

(16)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(n)}{r}\left(\int_{2B}\left|f_{1}-(f_{1})_{2B}\right|^{n^{2}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{n^{2}}}\left(\int_{2B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{np}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{2B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(n-1)[(p-1)n+p]}{n^{2}p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(n)}{r}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{\frac{n^{2}}{n+1}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n+1}{n^{2}}}\left(\int_{2B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{np}}

\displaystyle\times\left(\int_{2B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(n-1)[(p-1)n+p]}{n^{2}p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(n)}{r}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)n+p}{n^{2}p}}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{np}}

\displaystyle\times

\displaystyle\left(\int_{2B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(n-1)[(p-1)n+p]}{n^{2}p}}\left(\int_{2B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{n-1}{np}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(n)}{r}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)n+p}{np}}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}.

Hence, we get

\displaystyle\int_{B}\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\int_{2B}\eta\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\int_{2B}\eta J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}+\int_{2B}\eta\frac{\Sigma}{K}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

(17)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{c(n)}{r}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)n+p}{np}}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\int_{2B}\eta\Sigma d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle c(n)\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)n+p}{np}}\left(\int_{2\lambda B}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}+\int_{2B}\eta\Sigma d\mathcal{H}^{n},

which implies that

\displaystyle\int_{B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,\,\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq

\displaystyle C(n,\lambda,\mathcal{K})\cdot\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\frac{np}{(p-1)n+p}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{(p-1)n+p}{np}}

(18)

\displaystyle+C(n,\lambda)\cdot\int_{2\lambda B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,\;\;\Sigma\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

If we donate
q=[(p-1)n+p]/np,r_{0}=(1+\varepsilon)q>q,g=(\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n}/K)^{1/q},f=\Sigma^{1/q},
then the inequality (
18
) gives us that

\int_{B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,g^{q}\leq C(n,\lambda,\mathcal{K})\cdot\left[\left(\int_{2\lambda B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,\,\,\,g\right)^{q}+\int_{2\lambda B}\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\!\;{}-{}\,\,\,\,\;f^{q}\right].

Since
p>n,
we see that
q>1.
As
f^{r_{0}}=\Sigma^{1+\varepsilon}\in L_{loc}^{1}(\Omega),
then by a Gehring’s lemma on metric measure space
[
Gol05
, Theorem 3.3]
, we see that there exists
\beta_{1}>1,
such that
\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n}/K\in L_{loc}^{\beta_{1}}(\Omega).
Without loss of generality, we assume that
\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n}/K\in L^{\beta_{1}}(\Omega).
We chose the constants
p_{1},q_{1}>0
such that

p=\frac{\beta_{1}p_{1}}{q_{1}}>n-1,1<q_{1}<\beta_{1}
and
1/p_{1}+1/q_{1}=1.
Those constants do exist. It is easy to see that they meet the inequalities

p_{1}>\max\{\frac{\beta_{1}+n-1}{\beta_{1}},\frac{\beta_{1}}{\beta_{1}-1}\}\,\,\,{\rm and}\,\,\,1<q_{1}<\beta_{1}.

Then, for every compact set
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega,

we get by using of Hölder inequality that

\int_{\Omega_{1}}(\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n})^{\frac{\beta_{1}}{q_{1}}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}\left(\frac{\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n}}{K}\right)^{\beta_{1}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{q_{1}}}\left(\int_{\Omega_{1}}K^{p}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{p_{1}}}<\infty.

This shows that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,\gamma}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
with
\gamma=n\beta_{1}/q_{1}>n.
By
[
HK00
, Theorem 5.1]
, we deduce that
f
is locally Hölder continuous.
This finishes the proof.
∎

3.2
Corollary I: Hölder regularity for values of finite distortion

According to Theorem
3.2
, we have the following corollary on Hölder regularity for values of finite distortion.

Proposition 3.3
.

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)\left|f(x)-y_{0}\right|^{n}

(19)

for almost everywhere
x\in\mathcal{S}
with
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
If
K\in L_{loc}^{p}(\mathcal{S})
for some
p>n
and
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})
for some
\varepsilon>0,
then
f
is locally Hölder continuous.

Proof.

We only need to prove that there exists a constant
Q\in(1,\infty),
such that
\Sigma(x)\left|f(x)-y_{0}\right|^{n}\in L_{loc}^{Q}(\mathcal{S}).
Without loss of generality, we may assume that
{\rm diam}\mathcal{S}<\infty,

f\in N^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n}),

K\in L^{p}(\mathcal{S})
and
\Sigma\in L^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})
for some
\varepsilon>0.
Then, we take a constant
Q
which satisfying
1<Q<1+\varepsilon.

Thus, for every compact subset
M\subset\mathcal{S},
we have by Hölder inequality

\displaystyle\int_{M}\left(\Sigma\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{n}\right)^{Q}d\mathcal{H}^{n}=

\displaystyle\int_{M}(\Sigma^{1+\varepsilon})^{\frac{Q}{1+\varepsilon}}\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{nQ}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

(20)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}\Sigma^{1+\varepsilon}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{Q}{1+\varepsilon}}\left(\int_{M}\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{m}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon-Q}{1+\varepsilon}}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\|\Sigma\|_{L^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})}^{Q}\left(\int_{M}\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{m}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon-Q}{1+\varepsilon}},

where
m=nQ(1+\varepsilon)/(1+\varepsilon-Q)>n.
If we let
f=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,f_{n}),
then we have that
f_{j}\in N^{1,n}(\mathcal{S})
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}.
Hence, by using of the Truinger inequality

[
HK00
, Theorem 6.1]
for Newtonian-Sobolev mappings we get that
f_{j}\in L_{loc}^{l}(\mathcal{S})
for all
j\in\{1,2,\ldots,n\}
and all
l\geq 1.
In addition, since

\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{m}\lesssim\left|f\right|^{m}+\left|y_{0}\right|^{m}\lesssim\left|y_{0}\right|^{m}+\sum_{i=1}^{n}f_{i}^{m},

we get

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}\left|f-y_{0}\right|^{m}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon-Q}{1+\varepsilon}}\lesssim

\displaystyle\left(\left|y_{0}\right|^{m}\mathcal{H}^{n}(M)+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\int_{M}f_{i}^{m}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1+\varepsilon-Q}{1+\varepsilon}}

(21)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle C(y_{0},{\rm diam}\mathcal{S},\varepsilon,Q,\|f\|_{L^{m}(\mathcal{S})})<\infty.

Therefore, according to Theorem
3.2
, we see that
f
is locally Hölder continuous. This completes the proof of Proposition
3.3
.
∎

3.3
Corollary II: Lusin’s condition (N) for values of finite distortion

3.3.1
Lusin’s condition (N) for values of finite distortion

A continuous mapping
f:(X,\mu_{1})\rightarrow(Y,\mu_{2})
is said to satisfy Lusin’s condition (N) if for each
E\subset X
with
\mu_{1}(E)=0,
we have
\mu_{2}(f(E))=0.

Suppose that
f\in N^{1,p}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),
where
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
is a domain. In the case that
p>n,
then by
[
HKST01
, Theorem 6.1]
and
[
Zap14
, Theorem 1]
, we see that
f
satisfies Lusin’s condition (N). In the case that
p<n,
the mapping
f
fails to satisfy Lusin’s condition (N); see for example in
[
Pon71
]
. In the case that

p=n,
there are lots of conditions posed on
f
which ensure that it satisfies Lusin’s condition (N). For example, Zapadinskaya
[
Zap14
, Theorem 1]
showed that if
f
is Hölder continuous, then it satisfies Lusin’s condition (N). Therefore, together with Theorem
3.2
and Proposition
3.3
, these yield the following results on Lusin’s condition (N).

Proposition 3.4
.

Suppose that
f
belongs to
N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)

(22)

for almost everywhere
x\in\mathcal{S}.
If
K\in L_{loc}^{p}(\mathcal{S})
for some
p>n
and
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})
for some
\varepsilon>0,
then
f
satisfies locally Lusin’s
condition (N).

Proposition 3.5
.

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\mathcal{S},\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq K(x)J_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)\left|f(x)-y_{0}\right|^{n}

(23)

for almost everywhere
x\in\mathcal{S}
with
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.
If
K\in L_{loc}^{p}(\mathcal{S})
for some
p>n
and
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{1+\varepsilon}(\mathcal{S})
for some
\varepsilon>0,
then
f
satisfies locally Lusin’s
condition (N).

Here we provide an alternative proof of Propositions
3.4
or
3.5
, which may be of independent interest. We address this in the subsection
3.3.2
.

3.3.2
Pseudomonotone mappings

We first recall the definition of a mapping to be
K
-pseudomonotone in Euclidean space. Suppose that
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
is a domain. In
[
MM95
]
, Malý and Martio
[
MM95
]
called a mapping
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}

is
K
-pseudomonotone,
K\geq 1,
if

{\rm diam}(fB(x,r))\leq K{\rm diam}(f\partial B(x,r))

(24)

whenever
B(x,r)
is compactly contained on
\Omega.
Then, they showed that a continuous mapping
f\in W^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies Lusin’s condition (N) if it is
K
-pseudomonotone.
This result was extended to the setting of metric measure spaces by Heinonen et al.
[
HKST01
, Theorem 7.2]
. In addition, by a result of Zapadinskaya
[
Zap14
, Theorem 1]
in the setting of metric space, we see that a Hölder mapping
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
also satisfies Lusin’s condition (N). Actually, the condition of Hölder continuity gives us that

{\rm diam}(fB(x,r))\leq Kr^{\alpha},0<\alpha<1,

(25)

whenever
B(x,r)
is compactly contained on
\Omega.
If we combing the conditions (
24
) with (
25
) in the setting of Euclidean space, one can also deduce the Lusin’s condition (N)
for
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}.
Indeed, this is a result due to Putten
[
Put03
, Theorem 3.1]
, which asserts that if
f\in W^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
satisfies the following inequality

{\rm diam}(fB(x,r))\leq K{\rm diam}(f\partial B(x,r))+K^{\prime}r^{\alpha}

(26)

for every
B(x,r)\subset\subset\Omega.
Here,
K,K^{\prime}
are positive constants depend only on
n
and
\Omega
and
0<\alpha\leq 1.
Then
f
satisfies the Lusin’s condition (N).

We next provide a version of this result in the setting of metric measure spaces. We recall that a function
P:[0,\infty]\rightarrow[0,\infty]
is to be a Young function if it is convex, non-constant in
(0,\infty)
with
P(0)=0.
Then, by compared with (
26
), we introduce the following concept.

Definition 3.1
.

Suppose that
\Omega
is a domain of
\mathcal{S}
and
P
is a Young function. Then, we say a mapping
\phi:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{n}
is
(K,P)
-pseudomonotone on
\Omega
if for each
x\in\Omega,
we have the following inequality

{\rm diam}(fB(x,r))\leq K{\rm diam}(f\partial B(x,r))+K^{\prime}\sqrt[n]{P(r)}

(27)

whenever
B(x,r)
is compactly contained on
\Omega.
Here,
K
and
K^{\prime}
are two positive constants depend only on
n
and
\Omega.

The main result of this subsection is the following theorem.

Theorem 3.6
.

Suppose that a continuous mapping
f\in N^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
is
(K,P)
-pseudomonotone on domain
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
with
P
satisfying that
P(2t)\lesssim P(t)
as
t\rightarrow 0^{+}.
Then
f
satisfies the Lusin’s condition (N).

Remark
.

Note that taking
P(r)=r^{\alpha}

,0<\alpha\leq n,
in (
27
) yields condition (
26
). We also observe that Theorem
3.6
is new even in Euclidean space. Indeed, there exists a Young function
P
which satisfying
r^{\beta}\lesssim P(r)
and
P(2t)\lesssim P(t)
for small
r>0
and any
\beta>0.
In addition, the proof of
[
Put03
, Theorem 3.1]
relies on the trace theorem for Sobolev mappings in Euclidean space. In contrast, the proof of Theorem
3.6
below does not require this result. Here, it also need to note that we use the
P
-Hausdroff measure of
E
defined by

\mathcal{H}^{P}(E):=\sup_{\delta>0}\inf\sum_{i}P(r_{i}),

where the infimum is taken over all the coverings of the set
E
by balls
B_{i}
with radius
r_{i}
not exceeding
\delta.

Proof.

The proof of this result is similar to
[
HKST01
, Theorem 7.2]
. We must show that
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U))=0
for every
U\subset\subset\Omega
with
\mathcal{H}^{P}(U)=0.
Without loss of generality, we may assume that

U\subset B(x_{1},R_{1})
and
g
is an upper gradient of
f
in some neighborhood of
U.
For every fix
\varepsilon>0,
we additionally assume that
\Omega\supset U
is a fix open domain with which satisfying

\int_{\Omega}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}<\varepsilon.

We consider the set

U_{1}=\left\{x\in U:\int_{B(x,10\lambda r)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}<2L\int_{B(x,r/5)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right\}

and denote
U_{2}=U\setminus U_{1}.
Then, we must show that
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U_{1}))=\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U_{2}))=0.

We first show that
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U_{1}))=0.
For every
x\in U_{1},
then we chose a
r_{x}>0
such that
B(x,10\lambda r_{x})\subset\Omega
and

\int_{B(x,10\lambda r_{x})}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}<2L\int_{B(x,r_{x}/5)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

Then, by
[
HK00
, Theorem 7.1]
we see that there exists a radius
r\in(r_{x},2r_{x}),
such that

\left|f(x_{0})-f(y_{0})\right|\leq c\left(\int_{B(x,10\lambda r_{x})}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right)^{1/n}

(28)

for every
x_{0},y_{0}\in\Omega
satisfying
d(x_{0},x)=d(y_{0},x)=r.
Now, by using of the
(K,P)
-pseudomonotone of
f,
we obtain

\displaystyle({\rm diam}f(B(x,r_{x})))^{n}\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}({\rm diam}f(\partial B(x,r)))^{n}+M_{2}P(r)

(29)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}\int_{B(x,10\lambda r_{x})}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}+M_{2}P(2r_{x})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}\int_{B(x,10\lambda r_{x})}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}+M_{2}P(r_{x}).

Hence, we get that
U_{1}\subset\cup_{x\in U_{1}}B(x,r_{x})
with
B(x,r_{x})
satisfying the inequality (
29
). By covering theorem
[
Hei01
, Chapter 1]
, we can chose a countable collection

\{B(x_{i},r_{i})\}
which covers
U_{1}
and
\{B(x_{i},r_{i}/5)\}
is pairwise disjoint.
By using this covering and inequality (
29
), we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i}({\rm diam}f(B(x_{i},r_{i})))^{n}

(30)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}\sum_{i}\int_{B(x_{i},10\lambda r_{i})}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}+M_{2}\sum_{i}P(r_{i})

\displaystyle<

\displaystyle M_{1}\sum_{i}\int_{B(x_{i},r_{i}/5)}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}+M_{2}\sum_{i}P(r_{i})

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle M_{1}\int_{\Omega}g^{n}\;d\mathcal{H}^{n}+M_{2}\sum_{i}P(r_{i})<(M_{1}+M_{2})\varepsilon,

which implies that
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U_{1}))=0
by letting
\varepsilon\rightarrow 0^{+}.

Next, we show that
\mathcal{H}^{n}(f(U_{2}))=0.
This can be done, according to
[
GO18
, Corollary 3.4 and Proposition 4.1.]
, by showing that for every

a\in U_{2},
there has

{\rm Lip}(a):=\limsup_{x\rightarrow a}\frac{|{f(x)-f(a)|}}{|{x-a|}}<\infty.

Since
a\in U_{2},
we see that there exists an integer
m>1,
such that for every
0<r<1/m,
there holds the following

2L\int_{B(a,r/5)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\leq\int_{B(a,10\lambda r)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

(31)

If we let
\omega(r)=\int_{B(a,r/5)}g^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n},
then the inequality (
31
) gives us that

\omega(r)\leq\omega(50\lambda r)/(2L).

(32)

Now, we take every

The totally disconnected

In this section, we will study the totally disconnected for values of finite distortion from generalized

-manifold with controlled geometry to Euclidean space.
Firstly, we need the following Caccioppoli-type estimate deduced from Lemma
3.1
. Estimates of this kind in Euclidean space have been established by Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO221
, Lemma 6.2]
and Onninen and Zhong

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose that
f\in N_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L_{loc}^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),

\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
is a relatively compact open set and
\eta:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is a Lipschitz mapping with

{\rm spt}\eta\subset\Omega.
If
\varphi:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
is piecewise
C^{1}
-smooth function with
\varphi^{\prime}
is locally bounded, then we have

\left|\int_{\Omega}\eta[n\varphi(\left|f\right|^{2})+2\varphi^{\prime}(\left|f\right|^{2})\left|f\right|^{2}]J_{f}\right|\lesssim\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\left|f\right|^{2})\left|f\right|\left|\nabla\eta\right|\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n-1}.

(33)

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\Omega
is an open and bounded domain such that
f\in N^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}),
and
\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}

are bounded. If by letting that
F_{i}=(f_{1},f_{2},\ldots,\varphi(|f|^{2})f_{i},\ldots,f_{n}),
then we have
F_{i}\in N^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).
To show this we must verify that

\varphi(|f|^{2})f_{i}\in N^{1,n}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega).
Since
f_{j}\in N^{1,n}(\Omega)\cap L^{\infty}(\Omega)
for all
1\leq j\leq n,
and

\varphi
and
\varphi^{\prime}
are bounded, we only verify that
|D(\varphi(|f|^{2})f_{j})|\in L_{loc}^{n}(\Omega).
This conclusion follows by

|D(\varphi(|f|^{2})f_{i})|\leq 2|\varphi^{\prime}(|f|^{2})||f_{i}|\sum_{j=1}^{n}|f_{i}||Df_{j}|+|\varphi(|f|^{2})||Df_{i}|<\infty.

Now, by virtue of Lemma
3.1
, we get

\displaystyle\left|\int_{\Omega}\eta[\varphi(\left|f\right|^{2})+2\varphi^{\prime}(\left|f\right|^{2})|f|^{2}]J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right|

(34)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\left|f\right|^{2})|f_{j}||J(f_{1},\cdots,f_{j-1},\eta,f_{j+1},\cdots,f_{n})|d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\varphi(\left|f\right|^{2})|f|\left|\nabla\eta\right|\|{\rm ap}Df\|^{n-1}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

This finishes the proof.

We also need the following basic fact.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
x\geq 0,n\geq 2
and
M,N
are two positive constants which are independent of

If
x\leq Mx^{\frac{n-1}{n}}+N,
then there exists a constant

such that
x\leq c(M,N,n).

Proof.

If
x=0,
then we can chose any positive constant
c
such that
x\leq c.
Now, we assume that
x>0
and consider the function

\mathcal{G}(x)=x^{1/n}-\frac{N}{x^{1-\frac{1}{n}}},x>0.

It is obvious that
\lim_{x\rightarrow 0^{+}}\mathcal{G}(x)=-\infty
and
\lim_{x\rightarrow+\infty}\mathcal{G}(x)=+\infty
and
\mathcal{G}
is strictly monotonically increasing when
x>0.
Hence, for any
t>0,
there exists
a unique
c=c(n,N,t)>0
such that
\mathcal{G}(x)=t.
So the desired result follows by letting

and
c(M,N,n)=\mathcal{G}^{-1}(M).

By virtue of Lemmas

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
be a bounded domain and

and

&0&&{\rm if}\;{x\in f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}}\end{aligned}\right.

(36)

and

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that

Since

\Omega_{1}=\Omega.
We also assume that

and and
\Sigma/K\in L^{q}(\Omega).
If we denote

u(x)=\log\log\frac{1}{|f(x)|},x\in\Omega,

then by
[
BB11
, Proposition 2.17]
, we see that

u\in N^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Now, we fix
\varepsilon>0
and consider the following mapping

\varphi_{\varepsilon}(t)=\frac{1}{2t^{\frac{n}{2}}}\int_{0}^{t}\frac{\phi_{\varepsilon}(s)}{s\log^{n}(\frac{1}{s})}ds,

where

\phi_{\varepsilon}(s)=\frac{1}{1+\varepsilon 2^{\frac{1}{s}}}.

For every fixed compact subset
\Omega_{1}\subset\Omega,
we take mappings
\varphi=\varphi_{\varepsilon}
and

Here
\eta_{1}

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n}\frac{J_{f}}{\left|f\right|^{n}\log^{n}(\frac{1}{\left|f\right|})}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(\left|f\right|)d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n-1}|\nabla\eta_{1}|\frac{\parallel apDf\parallel^{n-1}}{|f|^{n-1}\log^{n-1}(\frac{1}{|f|})}\left(\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|f|)\right)^{\frac{n-1}{n}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n}\frac{\parallel apDf\parallel^{n}}{K|f|^{n}\log^{n}(\frac{1}{|f|})}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|f|)d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}K^{n-1}|\nabla\eta_{1}|^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}.

Hence, by combining

and inequality (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n}\frac{\parallel apDf\parallel^{n}}{K|f|^{n}\log^{n}(\frac{1}{|f|})}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|f|)d\mathcal{H}^{n}

(38)

\displaystyle\lesssim

\displaystyle\left[\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n}\frac{\parallel apDf\parallel^{n}}{K|f|^{n}\log^{n}(\frac{1}{|f|})}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|f|)d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}\cdot\left[\int_{\Omega}K^{n-1}|\nabla\eta_{1}|^{n}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right]^{\frac{1}{n}}

\displaystyle C(n,\parallel K^{n-1}|\nabla\eta_{1}|^{n}\parallel_{L_{1}(\Omega)})\left[\int_{\Omega}\eta_{1}^{n}\frac{\parallel apDf\parallel^{n}}{K|f|^{n}\log^{n}(\frac{1}{|f|})}\varphi_{\varepsilon}(|f|)d\mathcal{H}^{n}\right]^{\frac{n-1}{n}}

Corollary II: A Jacobian-degree formula for values of finite distortion

The following Jacobian-degree formula for Newtonian-Sobolev mapping is similar to the one obtained by Kangasniemi and Onninen
[
KO222
]
on Euclidean space.

Lemma 4.9
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
be a domain and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that an nonconstant mapping
f
belongs to
N_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and has Lusin’s condition (N). If
f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}
is totally disconnected, then there
\varepsilon_{1}>0,
such that
U_{\varepsilon_{1}}\subset\subset\Omega
is a connected component of
f^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon_{1}))
and satisfies the following Jacobian-degree formula

{\rm deg}(f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}})=\frac{1}{\omega_{n}\varepsilon_{1}^{n}}\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}.

(49)

Proof.

According to Corollary
4.6
, we can chose a
\varepsilon_{1}>0
such that
U_{\varepsilon_{1}}\subset\subset\Omega
is a connected component of
f^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon_{1}))
satisfying that
f\in N^{1,n}(U_{\varepsilon_{1}},\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(U_{\varepsilon_{1}},\mathbb{R}^{n})
and having the Lusin’s condition (N) on
U_{\varepsilon_{1}}.

We first consider that
f
is Lipschitz map. Then, by
[
Kir16
, Lemma 3.5]
, we obtain that

{\rm deg}(y,f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}})=\sum_{x\in f^{-1}(y)\cap U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}{\rm sgn}J_{f}(x)

for
\mathcal{H}^{n}
-almost every
y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus f(\partial U_{\varepsilon_{1}}).
Combing this with coarea formula
[
Kir16
, Theorem 3.3]
, we obtain

\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm deg}(y,f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}})d\mathcal{H}^{n}(y).

(50)

Next, by
[
Kir14
, Lemma 4.3 ]
, we can select a sequence of locally Lipschitz maps
\{f_{j}\}_{j=1}^{\infty},
such that
f_{j}\rightarrow f
uniformly on compact sets.
Thus, for sufficiently large indices
j
there is a homotopy between
f_{j}
and
f,
such that
{\rm deg}(y,f_{j},U_{\varepsilon_{1}})={\rm deg}(y,f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}}).

In addition, according to
[
Kir14
, Corollary 2.31]
, we see that

\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f_{j}}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\rightarrow\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f}d\mathcal{H}^{n}\;\;{\rm as}\;\;j\rightarrow\infty.

So we get

\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}{\rm deg}(y,f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}})d\mathcal{H}^{n}(y)

(51)

when
f\in N^{1,n}(U_{\varepsilon_{1}},\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(U_{\varepsilon_{1}},\mathbb{R}^{n}).
Now, from
[
Kir16
, Lemma 2.7]
we deduce that

\int_{U_{\varepsilon_{1}}}J_{f}(x)d\mathcal{H}^{n}(x)=\int_{\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon_{1})}{\rm deg}(y,f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}})d\mathcal{H}^{n}(y)=\omega_{n}\varepsilon_{1}^{n}{\rm deg}(f,U_{\varepsilon_{1}}).

This finishes the proof of Lemma
4.9
.

Lemma 5.1

5.3
Local openness for quasiregular values

The proof of following lemma is based on
[
KO222
, Lemma 6.3]

Lemma 5.5
.

Let
K\geq 1
be a constant,
\Omega\subset\mathcal{S}
be a domain and
y_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Suppose that
f
belongs to
N_{loc}^{1,n}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})\cap\mathcal{C}(\Omega,\mathbb{R}^{n})
and satisfies the following inequality

\|{\rm ap}Df(x)\|^{n}\leq KJ_{f}(x)+\Sigma(x)\left|f(x)-y_{0}\right|^{n}

(72)

for almost everywhere
x\in\Omega.
If
\Sigma\in L_{loc}^{p}(\Omega)
for some
p>1,
then for every neighborhood
V
of every
x\in f^{-1}\{y_{0}\},
there is valid for
y_{0}\in{\rm int}f(V).

Proof.

Suppose that there is a
x_{0}\in f^{-1}\{y_{0}\},
such that there exists an open set
U_{0}
of
x_{0}
satisfying that
\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon)\setminus f(U_{0})\neq\emptyset
for all small
\varepsilon>0.

We chose a
\varepsilon_{0}>0,
such that
U_{\varepsilon_{0}}\subset\subset\Omega
and satisfies
\overline{U_{\varepsilon_{0}}}\cap f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}=x_{0}
and
{\rm deg}(f,U_{\varepsilon_{0}})>0.

Now, for any
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0},
we see that if denote
U_{\varepsilon}
is the
x_{0}
-connected component of
f^{-1}(\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon)),
then we have

\overline{U_{\varepsilon}}\cap f^{-1}\{y_{0}\}=x_{0}
and
{\rm deg}(f,U_{\varepsilon})>0.
So by Lemma
5.4
, we have that
{\rm deg}(y_{0},f,U_{\varepsilon})>0
for all

y_{0}\in\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon).
According to
[
Kir16
, Lemma 2.7]
, we have that
f\in f(U_{\varepsilon}).
Hence, we have
\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon)\subset f(U_{\varepsilon}).

In addition, it is obvious that
f(U_{\varepsilon})\subset\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon).
Therefore, we have
f(U_{\varepsilon})=\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon)
for all

\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.

From above, we see that
\mathbb{B}(y_{0},\varepsilon)\setminus f(U_{0})=f(U_{\varepsilon})\setminus f(U_{0})\neq\emptyset
for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.

So
U_{\varepsilon}\setminus U_{0}\neq\emptyset
and thus
\overline{U_{\varepsilon}}\setminus U_{0}\neq\emptyset
for all
\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}.
This is contradiction since

\bigcap_{\varepsilon<\varepsilon_{0}}\overline{U_{\varepsilon}}\setminus U_{0}=x_{0}\setminus U_{0}=\emptyset.
This finishes the proof of Lemma
5.5
.
∎
