EXECUTIVE SUMMARY
Project Electro-Nano-Pulsing (ENP) Processing of Advanced Materials established ENP as a high-intensity, short-
duration, field-assisted processing platform for advanced materials. The project was proposed as Tailoring Grain-
boundaries by Electro-Nano-Pulsing (ENP) and centered on using intense nanopulsed electric current to control
grain boundaries and interfaces through localized Joule heating, electric-field effects, and non-equilibrium
microstructure evolution. The resulting publications broadened this proposal focus into a wider ENP platform for

 Page 2

grain-boundary morphology engineering, defect and dislocation engineering, surface nanocoating, and
nanosecond-scale field-activated precipitation.
The project produced a functional ENP prototype capable of delivering high current density, ultrashort electrical
pulses, and high-frequency pulse sequences through conductive specimens. Published demonstrations show
current-density regimes on the order of 10^10-10^12 A/m2, pulse durations below 1 microsecond, and pulse
frequencies up to 100 kHz. This combination differentiates ENP from conventional electropulsing, spark plasma
sintering, flash sintering, and long-duration thermal treatment because ENP couples extreme intensity with near-
instantaneous processing time.
Major scientific accomplishments include localized grain-boundary roughening in Nichrome-80/Ni-based alloys;
formation of atomistic facets, nanoscale serrations, and step-like grain-boundary morphologies; quasi-
instantaneous formation of dense Cr2O3 / amorphous Si-Cr-O surface nanocoatings; defect reconfiguration and
high-density dislocation formation; and a nanosecond field-activated precipitation pathway for Ni5Ce intermetallic
precipitates in a Ce-microalloyed Ni-based alloy. The Ni5Ce work further connected ENP-induced microstructures
to improved corrosion resistance and increased ultimate tensile strength.
The project also produced dissemination and technology-transfer outputs: peer-reviewed publications in Materials
Today Advances, Scientific Reports, Journal of Alloys and Compounds, and Materials & Design; a formal SDSU
invention disclosure (D-0575); and PCT Patent Application No. PCT/US2024/061795 for Applications of Electric
Nano Pulsing (ENP) Technology. Collectively, these outputs establish ENP as both a fundamental research tool for
field-assisted materials science and a candidate platform for high-rate advanced manufacturing.

ACCOMPLISHMENTS
1. What are the major goals/objectives of the project?
The project organized the research around ENP experimentation, atomistic/multiscale modeling, and processing-
rule development for localized grain-boundary and interface control. The resulting publications demonstrated that
this same scientific framework could be expanded to surface engineering, defect engineering, and precipitation
control in Ni-based conductive model alloys.
Goal 1 - Develop and validate an ENP processing platform.
The first goal was to establish the experimental principles and hardware required for ENP processing: fast
capacitor discharge, intense current impulses, nanosecond-to-microsecond pulse duration, controlled ON/OFF
pulsing, and flexible sample coupling. The proposal envisioned high-current DC impulses as short as
approximately 100 ns and up to hundreds of microseconds, with the ability to impose intense current through
layered structures, microcrystalline materials, and particle/agglomerate interfaces. The conducted studies realized
this goal through a constructed ENP apparatus with high-voltage charging, an IPM/fast capacitor, solid-state
switching, low-inductance current delivery, pulse control, and electrical diagnostics.
Goal 2 - Tailor grain boundaries and interfaces by localized, non-equilibrium ENP effects.
The project focused on using high localized Joule heating and electric-field/current effects at grain boundaries and
interfaces to induce structural modifications that cannot be obtained through conventional thermal processing. The

 Page 4

published work realized this goal by showing localized GB roughening, atomistic faceting, nanoscale serration, and
step-like GB morphology in Nichrome-80/Ni-based alloys without large-scale alteration of adjacent grain interiors.
Goal 3 - Determine coupled thermal and non-thermal mechanisms.
The project sought to distinguish ordinary Joule heating from field-driven phenomena such as electron wind force,
electromigration, stress localization, and enhanced diffusion. The resulting publications advanced this objective
through finite-element electrothermal modeling, current-density/skin-depth analysis, electron-wind-force
calculations, diffusion analysis, and microstructural characterization at SEM/TEM/HRTEM length scales.
Goal 4 - Establish processing-structure-property relationships and ENP processing rules.
The proposal called for quantitative relationships between current strength, pulse duration, ON/OFF pattern, and
microstructure outcomes. The resulting work linked pulse duration, current density, and frequency to grain-
boundary morphology, surface nanocoating, dislocation density, Ni5Ce precipitation, corrosion response, and
tensile behavior.
Goal 5 - Extend ENP toward advanced manufacturing and technology transfer.
The final goal was to position ENP as a practical platform technology. The invention disclosure and PCT filing
emphasize the value of simultaneous ultra-high current density, ultra-short pulse duration, and high pulsing
frequency, as well as the potential for flexible electrodes, continuous processing, high-throughput treatment, and
processing of complex geometries.

2. What was accomplished under these goals?
ENP hardware and processing platform
A working ENP processing platform was designed, constructed, and validated. The published device architecture
includes high-voltage DC charging, integrated power module/fast capacitor energy storage, solid-state switching,
current shunt resistance, oscilloscope and voltage/current diagnostics, and two sample configurations: bus-bar and
through-hole. These configurations support fine wires, thin flakes, rods, and small conductive specimens, with
future extension to powders, inserts, and complex components.
FEA-guided localized grain-boundary heat localization
The project developed a modeling basis for why ENP can act locally at grain boundaries. Finite-element analysis
showed that, when intense nanopulses are applied on submicrosecond time scales, GB regions can experience
localized temperature elevation before heat fully homogenizes across the grain interior. This supports the proposal

 Page 5

hypothesis that nanosecond-to-microsecond pulses create localized non-equilibrium conditions inaccessible to
conventional long-duration heating.

Figure 3. FEA-guided basis for localized grain-boundary heating in Nichrome-80 under intense nanopulse current. Source: Xu et
 al., Materials Today Advances 2023.

Localized grain-boundary morphology engineering
Using Nichrome-80/Ni-based alloy model systems, ENP produced atomistic facets, nanoscale serrations, and
nanoscale step-like GB morphologies. These changes were localized at GB regions and did not require global
deformation, long annealing, or strong solute-segregation treatments. This accomplishment directly fulfills the
proposal objective of using ENP to tailor grain-boundary and interface structures.

Quasi-instantaneous processing without conventional grain growth
The resulting publications demonstrated that intense pulse sequences can generate extreme heating rates while
preserving much of the original grain structure. In contrast, conventional furnace heating near the melting point
produced much stronger grain growth and microstructural coarsening. This supports ENP as a localized, time-
compressed processing strategy rather than a conventional thermal exposure.

Defect reconfiguration and dislocation-density engineering
ENP was shown to reconfigure existing defects and generate new dislocation configurations. In the Scientific
Reports work, ENP produced jagged dislocations and ordered dislocation-wall structures in Nichrome alloy. This
early demonstration established ENP as a defect-reconfiguration technology rather than only a grain-boundary
treatment.

Surface nanocoating formation
ENP in air produced a dense, adherent, hierarchical surface nanocoating in Ni-Cr-Si/Nichrome alloy. TEM and
EDS results showed a double-layer structure consisting of an outer Cr2O3 layer and an inner amorphous Si-Cr-O
layer. This result demonstrates ENP as a surface engineering technique capable of producing protective
nanoscale coatings at extremely short time scales.

High-dislocation-density extension
The carried out work extended the defect-engineering result by demonstrating ultra-intense nanopulsing that
produced very high dislocation densities across multiple scales, including local values near 10^15/m2 and
atomistic-scale screw dislocation densities reaching approximately 10^16-10^18/m2. Higher pulsing frequency was
associated with denser dislocation distributions.

Figure 8. High dislocation-density formation at the atomic scale after ultra-intense nanopulsing current treatment. Source: Xu et

Field-activated precipitation and property improvement
The conducted studies extended ENP beyond GB morphology and defect engineering into precipitation control. A
single 330 ns pulse at 1.30 x 10^11 A/m2 induced thermally stable hexagonal Ni5Ce precipitates with semi-
coherent interfaces in a Ce-microalloyed Ni-based alloy. Comparative furnace, solution-aging, spark plasma
sintering, and lower-intensity electropulsing conditions did not reproduce the same field-activated precipitation
behavior, supporting the role of ENP-specific electromigration and ultrafast field effects.
The carried out work demonstrated property relevance: corrosion potential increased markedly, corrosion current
density decreased by orders of magnitude, and ultimate tensile strength increased from 709.3 MPa to 831.1 MPa.
These results show that ENP-induced microstructures can translate into measurable performance changes.

3. What opportunities for training and professional development has the project provided?
The project provided interdisciplinary training across materials science, pulsed-power hardware, microstructure
characterization, modeling, electrochemical testing, mechanical testing, and technology transfer. The involved
graduate students worked on modeling and experimentation; the resulting publication record demonstrates active
training in both experimental and computational aspects of ENP.
• High-voltage and pulsed-power experimentation: operation of DC power supplies, integrated power
 modules, solid-state switching, fast capacitor discharge, pulse diagnostics, current/voltage waveform
 interpretation, and safe sample-electrode setup.
• Advanced characterization: metallographic preparation, SEM-BSE imaging, SEM/EDS mapping, EBSD
 analysis, FIB lamella preparation, TEM/HRTEM imaging, selected-area diffraction, and
 defect/precipitate/coating interpretation.
• Modeling and data interpretation: finite-element electrothermal modeling, current-density and skin-depth
 analysis, thermal-stress estimation, electron-wind-force analysis, and diffusion/transport reasoning.
• Properties testing: electrochemical polarization, electrochemical impedance spectroscopy, tensile testing of
 fine wires, and interpretation of work-hardening and corrosion metrics.
• Professional development: peer-reviewed manuscript preparation, figure/data presentation, invention
 disclosure, patent documentation, and translation of fundamental materials science into potential advanced-
 manufacturing applications.

IMPACT
1. What is the impact on the development of the principal discipline(s) of the project?
The project advances field-assisted materials processing by defining an ENP regime in which electrical intensity,
pulse duration, and pulse frequency are simultaneously pushed beyond conventional electropulsing and current-
assisted sintering approaches. The central scientific impact is that electrical current is no longer treated only as a
heating aid; instead, ENP becomes an active microstructure-engineering stimulus capable of inducing localized,
non-equilibrium events on nanosecond-to-microsecond time scales.
For grain-boundary engineering, ENP provides a localized pathway to GB faceting, serration, and step formation
without extensive thermomechanical cycling or long-duration annealing. For defect engineering, ENP introduces a
route to high-density dislocation formation and defect reconfiguration that can exceed conventional cold-worked
limits at nanoscale volumes. For surface engineering, ENP enables fast formation of dense, adherent
nanocoatings. For precipitation science, the Ni5Ce result demonstrates that intense electric-current effects can
drive solute redistribution and intermetallic precipitation on time scales that conventional thermal treatments cannot
access.

2. What is the impact on the development of human resources?
The project trained personnel in a rare combination of materials science, high-voltage pulsed-power systems,
advanced microscopy, modeling, electrochemistry, mechanical testing, and innovation practice. Participants
gained experience spanning the full research cycle: proposal-driven scientific objectives, custom device
construction, ENP parameter selection, sample preparation, imaging and diffraction analysis, model-guided
interpretation, manuscript preparation, and invention/patent documentation.
This interdisciplinary training supports workforce development for advanced manufacturing, pulsed-power
materials processing, microstructural characterization, corrosion/mechanical testing, and technology

 Page 15

commercialization. The resulting expertise is directly relevant to emerging materials-processing sectors where
short processing time, localized modification, and property tailoring are essential.

3. What is the impact on technology transfer?
The project has a strong technology-transfer pathway because it produced both a working processing platform and
formal intellectual-property outputs. SDSU Invention ID D-0575 and PCT Patent Application No.
PCT/US2024/061795 establish a foundation for protecting and advancing ENP as a platform technology.
Technology-transfer opportunities include high-rate wire and small-component processing, localized grain-
boundary engineering, protective nanocoating, defect-density tuning, rare-earth precipitation control, and future
coupling with flash sintering, ultrafast high-temperature processing, capacitive discharge welding, and nanopowder
or porous-material processing. The ENP platform is especially attractive where conventional furnace treatment is
too slow, too global, or too damaging to the desired microstructure.

Overall impact statement
The project successfully transformed ENP from a proposal concept into a demonstrated materials-processing
platform with peer-reviewed validation, prototype hardware, mechanistic models, property data, and patent activity.
The most important outcome is the establishment of a new processing paradigm: using ultra-intense, ultrashort,
controlled electrical nanopulses to modify microstructure locally and rapidly, with direct implications for advanced
manufacturing and the design of next-generation structural and functional materials.
