 ABSTRACT

Discontinuous fiber-reinforced thermoplastic composites have gained considerable attention in
automotive, aerospace, and other industries, due to their high-rate of production combined with
their ability to attain complex and intricate shapes. Among other high-rate thermoplastic
manufacturing processes, injection-molding is one of the most common manufacturing methods
due to fast production and high surface finishing of complex geometries. Fiber orientation in
discontinuous fiber composites plays a pivotal role in determining the mechanical, electrical, and
thermomechanical properties, underscoring the necessity to comprehend fiber orientation in
injection molded parts. Among different fiber types, glass and carbon fibers are most common in
the composite industries. The recent trend of hybrid composites comprising both glass fiber (GF)
and carbon fiber (CF) is also gaining importance in the automotive industry. Hybrid fiber options
allow designers to optimize the balance between glass and carbon fibers by leveraging the high
durability and low cost of GF while the strength and lightweight properties of CF. Consequently,
comparing the fiber oriented distribution (FOD) of injection molded composites containing GF,
CF, and a hybrid of GF/CF is critical to investigating the local mechanical properties of intricate
structures for high-end applications.
 In Phase I of this project, FOD in injection-molded panels with respect to distance from the
gate was analyzed using X-ray computed tomography (X-CT) for GF, CF, and hybrid CF/GF
(CGF) reinforced nylon 66. To understand the reason behind the FOD with different fiber types,
computational fluid dynamics (CFD) and rheology were performed. Samples were extracted at
three locations: near the gate, center, and opposite end. Thickness of the layers of typical skin-
shell-core type FOD varies with fiber type and location. GF achieved flow direction alignment (in
shell) earlier than viscous CF and CGF near the gate, whereas CF showed the highest flow-
direction alignment at the center due to shear induced orientation. At the opposite end, GF
experienced more backflow than others indicating faster mold filling owing to its lower viscosity.
Hybrid CGF exhibited GF-dominated center and CF-dominated end region. The numerical model
used to obtain FOD and rheological predictions for the CF and GF composites served to
corroborate the trends observed in the experimental trials. The FOD responses across fiber types
and location were reflected in their longitudinal and transverse properties. Only GF showed higher
longitudinal modulus over transverse modulus near the gate attributed to rapid alignment, whereas
CF and CGF exhibited opposite trend. However, fountain flow enhanced the longitudinal modulus
over transverse modulus with the distance for all, particularly for CF. This study offers insights
into mold filling behavior of different fibers which are critical in optimizing injection molding
conditions for tailored final properties.

1. OBJECTIVES

The primary focus of the study is to understand the development of fiber orientation in the injection
molded panels with respect to fiber types, and locations which are critical to design a part with
precision achieving higher mechanical strength at lower weight. The overarching goal of this
proposal aligns well with the DOE mission to reduce the weight of structural parts used in
transportation industry.

 2. TECHNOLOGY DESCRIPTION

2.1 BACKGROUND

FOD is often defined by orientation tensors, which represent the probability of average fibers
alignment in a specified direction [1]. Experimental determination of FOD in a 3D space remains
a challenge. One frequently used method for investigating FOD involves the 2D microscopic
analysis of the elliptical cross-sections of fiber from a polished surface [2]. The ellipse-based
method possesses several limitations due to ambiguity in determining the positive and negative
orientation of fiber in 3D space relative to the symmetry plane [3, 4]. Apart from the ellipse-based
method, several non-destructive analyses have been reported for the fiber orientation
measurement, which includes Eddy current [5], Ultrasound [6], Vibration [7], thermal expansion
[8] etc. The common limitation of all these measurement methods is acquiring information on
average fiber orientation in bulk overlooking the microscopic details.
 The state-of-the-art method for FOD analysis in a 3D space involves X-ray micro-Computed
Tomography (X-CT) scans to understand through thickness orientation providing microscopic
information [4, 9-11]. The scan images obtained from the X-CT can be processed using different
image processing methods: the mean intercept length method (MIL) and the high-fidelity fiber
tracing method [4, 12]. Commercially available AVIZO software utilizes the high-fidelity fiber
tracing method to process the X-CT scans, which map the fiber orientation within the composites
[13]. A limited number of studies have been reported on the injection-molded discontinuous fiber-
reinforced composites using this method to investigate the distribution of shell and core layers
relative to the distance from the gate. Thi et al. investigated the effect of weld line on fiber
orientation of injection molded short GF/nylon 6 composites at different location of the mold [9].
Hamanaka et al. studied the effect of short GF content and mold thickness on the FOD of the
injection molded panel at different distances [14]. However, a comparative study on the effect of
fiber types on the along-the-thickness FOD has never been reported to the best of our knowledge.
With the different fiber types, the viscoelastic property of the melt alters, which is also a
determining factor of the fiber orientation. The incorporation of hybrid fibers combining GF and
CF makes the situation more complicated accounting detailed investigation to understand their
spatial arrangement within the molded parts.
 Computational fluid dynamics (CFD) with various theoretical models offers valuable insights
about fiber orientation by numerical analysis. The classical Isotropic Rotary Diffusion (IRD)
model [15] was developed by Folgar-Tucker; however, this model results in a thinner core region
compared to the experiments [16, 17]. This issue was handled by the reduced strain closure (RSC)
model [18] and the combined Anisotropic Rotary Diffusion (ARD)-RSC model [19] by using a
strain reduction factor to slow down the fiber orientation kinetics. However, the inlet condition
strongly influences the predictions of these models, making them difficult to implement for the
parts with complex geometries [20]. On the other hand, the parameters of the iARD-RPR
(improved Anisotropic Rotary Diffusion - Retarding Principal Rate) model [21] are found to be
flow-independent, i.e., the parameters can be optimized for a material and can then be used for any
flow conditions or geometry being manufactured [16]. The model combines the improved ARD
(iARD) model with the new retarding principal rate (RPR) model such that the former captures
the anisotropic characteristics of fiber orientation. At the same time, the latter controls the
orientation rate. The iARD-RPR model has been previously employed for predicting fiber
orientations in fiber-reinforced composites and has been incorporated in the commercial software

                                                                                             Page |3

Moldex3D (CoreTech System Co. Ltd), frequently used for understanding mold flow during
injection molding [22].
 The current work aims to understand the FOD in connection with the distance from gate
location and fiber type through X-CT measurement, followed by comparing the trend with the
CFD analysis. Previous studies in literature have used the iARD-RPR model to predict FOD in
GF and CF injection molded rectangular/circular disc plaques [23, 24], however their singular
objective was to optimize the model parameters and validate the models against the experimental
measurements. In the present work, validation of numerical model was performed as first step but
in addition to that, the contribution of the present numerical work is to provide insights into the
flow behavior along the plate length for the GF and CF fiber types. The influence of the flow
behavior on FOD trends is explained using viscosity and velocity profiles, measured
experimentally and through CFD simulation. Finally, the effect of FOD on the local mechanical
properties was discussed.

2.2 METHODOLOGY

2.2.1 Preparation of Injection Molded Panels

 Fiber orientation distribution was investigated with three injection molded end-gated plates
comprised of 50 wt.% GF, 50 wt.% CF and 50 wt.% hybrid CF and GF (CGF, 1:1 wt. ratio) in
combination with nylon 66 matrix. The pellets used in the injection molding process are
commercial grades procured from Polystruct, USA. The grades used in this work are proprietary,
so further information is not provided. These pellets were prepared in two steps: first, continuous
fibers were pultruded through molten nylon 66 under high pressure; second, the pultruded strands
were chopped into beads using a pelletizer. So, the initial length of the fiber is similar to the length
of the pellets which is 6 mm. During the injection molding, the melt and the mold temperature
were maintained at 293 and 93 °C, respectively. The dimensions of the plates were 25.4 cm
(length) × 15.4 cm (width) × 0.3 cm (thickness). Along the length, the long injection molded plate
can be divided into three different regions to compare the fiber orientation with respect to the
distance from the gate location (Figure 1a). Region-1, close to the gate location, was considered
up to the length of 7 cm (GF-1, CF-1, and CGF-1). Region-2 was the center location of the panel
within the length of 8 to 16 cm (GF-2, CF-2, and CGF-2). Region-3 was the panel section at the
far end of the gate location from 17 to 25 cm (GF-3, CF-3, and CGF-3).

2.2.2 X-CT Measurements

 X-CT scans of GF composites (GF-1, GF-2, and GF-3), CF composites (CF-1, CF-2, and CF-
3), and hybrid CGF composites (CGF-1, CGF-1, and CGF-3) were carried out by using Zeiss
Xradia Versa 520 X-ray Tomography Unit, USA. Square-shaped samples of area 1×1 cm2
extracted from the three different regions of the injection molded plates (GF, CF, and CGF) were
scanned (shown in Figure 1a). One X-CT scan was performed for each condition. The resolution
for the XCT was maintained at 3 µm for the GF and 1.5 µm for CF and hybrid CGF. A higher
magnification scan was conducted for carbon fiber in comparison to glass aiming to identify the
fibers, as the contrast was low in the case of carbon fiber (Figure 2). The scanned images for each
sample were stacked for investigating the FOD by AVIZO software in 3D space (Figure 1b). Fiber
tracking analysis using AVIZO software involved two successive steps: cylinder correlation lines
and trace correlation lines. Cylinder correlation lines require the inputs of cylinder outer diameter

                                                                                             Page |4

and inner diameter, cylinder length, and curvature, which were chosen based on GF and CF fibers
used for injection molding. Trace correlation lines were identified based on Minimum Seed
Correlation, Minimum Continuation, and Minimum Distance, selected based on the pixel intensity
obtained from Cylinder correlation. Individual fibers were tracked after the trace correlation
function, and the orientation of the fibers is expressed by color code (Figure 1c).

2.2.3 Characterization of the Injection Molded Parts

 The oscillatory shear rheology of the injection-molded plates was conducted using an ARES
G2 Rheometer with disc-shaped samples of 2.54 cm diameter extracted from the plates.
Rheological analysis was performed at 290 °C higher than the melting temperature of nylon 66.
TGA analysis was performed using TGA5500 TA instruments by heating the sample from 25 to
900 °C in air at a heating rate of 10 °C.min-1. A tensile test was carried out in longitudinal and
transverse directions using Test Resources 313 Series, USA, with a 2 mm.min-1 speed under
ambient temperature. Five specimens were tested for each condition. Fiber length distribution was
determined experimentally by burning off the resin. Each sample type was put in aluminum foil
pans and heated to 650 °C for 2 h in the controlled furnace environment (Thermo Scientific,
Linderg Blue M, Ahheville, NC). Following the burn-off process, each sample was placed in a
glass container filled with acetone and subjected to an ultrasonic bath (Fisher Scientific, Pittsburgh,
PA) for 2 min to disperse the fibers effectively. After that, fibers were placed in plain microscope
slides. Imaging of the fibers was obtained with a VHX-5000 digital microscope (Keyence
Corporation, Itasca, IL). The fiber lengths were measured from the images by using ImageJ
software. For each sample type, at least 2000 fibers were analyzed.

3. KEY RESULTS

 The fiber trajectory image (Figure 1c) clearly indicated the skin-shell-core morphology, as
reported in the literature. The thin “skin” layers in proximity to the mold surface contained
randomly oriented fibers mostly denoted by green lines, whereas shell and core regions comprised
predominantly aligned fibers along the YZ plane in the flow direction (blue line) and orthogonal
direction (red lines), respectively. An interface area was also noted between the shell and the core.
To understand the average shell and core thickness within the volume element, fiber orientation
vectors were measured and shown along the XZ plane in Figure 1d. The successive steps, as
depicted in Figure 1b-d, were followed for each volume element extracted from the different
regions of the same injection molded plates to determine the FOD with respect to the flow, and

                                                                                                 Page |7

further comparison was drawn between different injection molded samples composed of GF, CF,
and CGF.

3.5 INTERPRETATION OF FOD BY RHEOLOGICAL PROPERTIES

 The mechanism of FOD was investigated through rheological analysis by extracting the sample
from the injection molded panels. The goal of performing oscillatory rheology with the molded
part is to compare the viscosity of all the three types of composites across different regions during
injection molding. Figure 16a-c represents the oscillatory shear rheology of GF, CF and CGF. In
all three cases, shear thinning properties were discerned [25]. The viscosity of the CF composite
was higher than GF throughout the entire frequency range due to the larger volume fraction of CF.
The comparatively viscous melt of CF took longer time to orient the fibers in the flow direction
from the divergent flow that occurred while the melt was entering the mold cavity. Conversely,
less viscous GF-filled melt quickly oriented the fibers toward the flow direction by achieving the
fountain flow condition from the divergent flow. Thus, it displayed lower core thickness than CF
at Region-1. However, once the steady state condition was achieved at the middle section of the
plate (Region-2), fountain flow dominated the fiber orientation showing shear-induced alignment
of fibers in the flow direction (Figure 18). Viscous CF composites produced a more shearing effect
(higher shear storage modulus) (Figure 17a-c) leading to a higher elongational flow at the shell
region. Consequently, CF composite exhibited maximum longitudinal fiber fractions (orientation
angle 6-10°) as noted in the fiber angle distribution curve (18%) (Figure 11e) and lower core
thickness (0.88 mm) at Region-2 compared to the other two composites (GF and CGF) (Figure
11f).

3.6 EFFECT OF FOD ON THE LOCAL MECHANICAL PROPERTIES

Figure 20. Longitudinal and transverse modulus and their ratio for GF (a), CF(b) and CGF (c) at three
different regions, (d) comparison of calculated modulus of CGF using the rule of mixture with
experimentally determined longitudinal modulus.

 Variation in FOD with respect to distance altered the longitudinal and transverse tensile
modulus accordingly as shown in Figure 20a-c. The key alterations in the local mechanical
properties in connection with FOD are outlined below:
1. Only GF showed a higher longitudinal modulus (Figure 20a) than the transverse one near
the gate (GF-1), as it quickly aligned the fibers in the flow direction due to its lower viscosity. For
GF, from Region-1 to Region-2, an increase in the longitudinal modulus was noted followed by
exhibiting a consistent modulus from Region-2 to Region-3. The higher core thickness at the gate
followed by its gradual decrease clearly explains the increasing trend. While the dominant
backflow at the opposite end (Region-3) was responsible for marginal increase in longitudinal
modulus and a reduction in the ratio of longitudinal to transverse modulus; however, longitudinal
modulus was still higher as the higher shell thickness was maintained.
2. The transverse modulus was higher for CF at Region-1 than that of its longitudinal modulus
(Figure 20b), unlike GF. The significantly lower fraction of longitudinal fibers and higher core
thickness near the gate led the transverse modulus to dominate for CF at Region-1. However, the
longitudinal modulus exceeded at the middle of the plate (Region-2) supporting dominant shear
induced longitudinal fiber orientation once the melt reached fountain flow condition. From
Region-2 to Region-3, the longitudinal modulus and the ratio showed a gradual increase, indicating

an increase in shell thickness with distance. Unlike GF, the transverse modulus of CF did not show
a rise at Region-3, manifesting a lower extent of backflow as observed in the FOD analysis.
3. Similar to CF, the transverse modulus of hybrid CGF was initially higher at Region-1 and
after that longitudinal modulus surpassed its transverse modulus at Region-2 (Figure 20c), once
the streamline fiber fractions increased at the center. The modulus ratio also continuously
increased with the distance from the gate. However, at Region-2, the longitudinal modulus showed
a marginal increase compared to Region-1followed by a considerable increase at Region-3. In this
case, apart from the FOD, the CF to GF fibers ratio is also a determining factor in regulating the
modulus. We used the rule of mixture to predict the longitudinal modulus of CGF at three different
regions using the experimentally determined modulus of CF and GF (Figure 20d). Any deviation
between calculated longitudinal modulus and experimentally measured modulus was attributed to
alterations in GF and CF volume fractions from the ideal condition, as the basic orientation pattern
of GF and CF including the fiber length range did not change drastically in the hybrid system. The
calculated modulus of CGF was significantly higher at the center (Region-2) than that of
experimentally measured modulus, while conversely, it was lower than the experimental modulus
at Region-3. The result suggested that the middle section (Region-2) of the hybrid CGF panel
predominantly contained GF with comparatively lower modulus. The higher experimental
modulus at Region-3 indicated a higher fraction of CF being pushed to the opposite end in the case
of CGF panel, as supported by the rheological analysis (Figure 16f).

4. IMPACT

LFT (Long Fiber reinforced Thermoplastics) materials in general are a great option for metal
replacement, especially when it comes to lightweighting, high specific strength, and corrosion
resistance. Specifically in automotive and aerospace sectors, lightweighting is one of the key
requirements for energy efficiency. The lighter a product is, the less energy it takes to accelerate
or lift it. Between glass and carbon fiber, carbon is the more weight efficient material, but it has
its drawbacks compared to glass fibers, which include cost, and durability. Hybrid fiber options
allow designers to optimize the balance between glass and carbon fibers in order to have increase
durability compared to carbon, leveraging the weight reduction of carbon, while simultaneously
reducing the price of the material compared to a carbon reinforced resin formulation alone. The
work will shed light on the manufacturing of hybrid fiber composites using injection molding with
a focus on optimizing material properties and improving process efficiency. This will enable more
durable and cost-effective solutions for automotive and aerospace industries.

5. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK

 In summary, the current work comprehended the FOD of GF, CF and hybrid CGF based
injection molded panels as a function of distance from the gate and correlated with the rheology
and mechanical properties. The major contributions of this work are outlined below:
(i) Regardless of fiber types, the divergent flow near the gate induced orthogonal orientation
of fibers resulting in thicker core. With the distance from the gate, the divergent orthogonal flow
converted into fountain flow promoting longitudinal fiber orientation in the shell layers with a
gradual reduction in core thickness. This trend was also predicted by the CFD model. At the
opposite end, backflow further adversely impacted the longitudinal orientation.
(ii) In the case of GF, fibers quickly attained the fountain flow showing more longitudinally
aligned fibers and lower core thickness near gate as compared to viscous CF and CGF.
Consequently, only GF manifested higher longitudinal modulus than its transverse modulus near
the gate location. In contrary, the transverse modulus was dominating for both CF and CGF closer
to the gate location.
(iii) Higher viscosity of CF facilitated the shear induced alignment after attaining the fountain
flow at the center of the panel and thereby exhibiting highest longitudinal fiber fraction. Thus, its
longitudinal modulus surpassed the transverse modulus at the center. Irrespective of fiber type, a
gradual increase in the longitudinal modulus was observed with the distance due to more shear
induced orientation. The extent of backflow for CF was also considerably lower than GF at the
opposite end.
(iv) Similar to CF, the longitudinal modulus of CGF exceeded its transverse modulus at the
center of the panel due to enhanced shear induced orientation of viscous melt. In this case, more
CF were being pushed to the opposite end during mold filling attributing to the GF dominating
center region while the higher fraction of CF at opposite end. Consequently, the viscosity and
longitudinal modulus of CGF showed a subsequent rise at opposite end of the mold.
(v) The FOD predictions obtained from the CFD model showed good correlation with the
experimental measurements. The flow behavior along the plate length that influenced the FOD
morphology was explained with viscosity, and velocity profiles. These profiles helped to explain
the shell-core thickness variation along length for the GF and CF fibers.

The discrepancy in the simulation predictions compared to the experiments can be attributed to the
following reasons. First, the fiber length variation in the melt during the injection molding process,
as recorded in the experimental trials, was not implemented in the CFD model. The variation in
fiber length can significantly influence the flow behavior and, subsequently, the FOD morphology,
as understood by literature. Second, the material cards used for modeling were the ones available
in the material database. It is acknowledged that for accurate flow modeling, the thermal and
capillary rheological properties of the material systems under study must be characterized
thoroughly. The above-mentioned shortcomings of the current CFD model will be addressed in
our future work.
