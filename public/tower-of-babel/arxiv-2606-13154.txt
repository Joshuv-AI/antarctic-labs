Computing Joins in the Weak Order of Type B B Coxeter Groups: an Algorithmic Approach
Riccardo Biagioli, Lorenzo Perrone

Abstract
We present an algorithm for computing the join of two elements in the weak order of the Coxeter group of type B B . This extends Markowsky’s algorithm for computing joins of standard permutations to signed permutations, and allows us to confirm a conjecture of Dyer concerning a geometric interpretation of these joins.

Introduction

In a very influential paper, Dyer
[
5
]
proposed several conjectures regarding the weak order of Coxeter groups. One of these conjectures, which will be discussed in detail in the following sections, has been the primary motivation for the present study.

It is well known that the Coxeter system of type

\sigma(-a)=-\sigma(a)
for every

and
[\pm n]=\{-n,\ldots,-1,1,\ldots n\}.

We refer to

3

for any undefined notation.

i\neq\pm j\in[\pm n]

\left(i\ j\right)\in S^{B}_{n}

\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pti\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt

Through this combinatorial interpretation, we take as set of Coxeter generators for

S_{B}=\{s_{0}\!=\!\left(1\ \kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt\right),s_{1}\!=\!\left(1\ 2\right),\ldots,s_{n-1}\!=\!\left(n-1\ n\right)\}

In what follows, we will use both the
complete notation
of a signed permutation

\sigma=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(n)

Recall that, given a Coxeter system
(W,S)
, its set of reflections is
T=\bigcup_{w\in W}wSw^{-1}
and the
left-reflection set
of an element

is
T_{L}(w)=\{t\in T\mid\ell(tw)<\ell(w)\}
.
The
(right) weak order
on
(W,S)
is a partial order defined by the prefix property: for any
u,v\in W
, we have
u\leq_{R}v
if and only if a reduced expression for
u
is a prefix of a reduced expression for
v
. It can also be characterized in terms of left-reflection sets, namely

u\leq_{R}v\ \text{ if and only if }\ T_{L}(u)\subseteq T_{L}(v).

(1)

The poset
(W,\leq_{R})
is a meet-semilattice, and in particular it is a lattice whenever the group
W
is finite. On the other hand, it is never a lattice when
W
is infinite.
For further details on
(W,\leq_{R})
, we refer the reader to
[
3
, Chapter 3]
.

In the combinatorial description
(S^{B}_{n},S_{B})
, the set of reflections corresponds to the set of transpositions
T=\{\left(i\ j\right)\mid i\neq j\in[\pm n]\}
introduced above. Moreover, in this interpretation, it is useful to introduce the following well known statistics of a signed permutation
\sigma\in S^{B}_{n}
:

•

\mathrm{Inv}(\sigma)=\{(i,j)\in[n]\times[n]\mid i<j,\sigma(i)>\sigma(j)\}
, the set of
inversions
of
\sigma
;

•

\mathrm{Neg}(\sigma)=\{i\in[n]\mid\sigma(i)<0\}
, the set of
negative entries
of
\sigma
;

•

\mathrm{Nsp}(\sigma)=\left\{\{i,j\}\subseteq[n]\mid i\neq j,\sigma(i)+\sigma(j)<0\right\}
, the set of
negative sum pairs
of
\sigma
.

The computation of these statistics provides a direct formula for the Coxeter length:

\ell(\sigma)=|\mathrm{Inv}(\sigma)|+|\mathrm{Neg}(\sigma)|+|\mathrm{Nsp}(\sigma)|.

This identity reflects a decomposition of the left-reflection set of
\sigma
into three disjoint subsets:

T_{L}(\sigma)=\left\{\left(i\ j\right)\mid(i,j)\in\mathrm{Inv}(\sigma^{-1})\right\}\sqcup\left\{\left(i\ \kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pti\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt\right)\mid i\in\mathrm{Neg}(\sigma^{-1})\right\}\sqcup\left\{\left(i\ \kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptj\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt\right)\mid\{i,j\}\in\mathrm{Nsp}(\sigma^{-1})\right\}.

(2)

A proof of last equality can be found in
[
3
, Chapter 8]
and
[
9
]

It is important to observe that the left reflection set
T_{L}(w)
uniquely identifies the element
w
. Therefore, to determine the join of two elements in
(W,\leq_{R})
, it suffices to compute its left reflection set. For instance, Theorem 10-3.25 of
[
7
]
gives two descriptions of the left reflection set of the join of two elements in terms of closure operations. As we will see below, the conjecture that motivated our work is formulated in a similar way.

In
[
5
]
, Dyer introduced the extended weak order, a partial order generalizing the weak order, and conjectured that it is a lattice even when the underlying Coxeter group is infinite. In the same paper, he formulated a conjectural description of the join of two elements in this poset. It is not yet known whether this conjecture holds even for finite Coxeter systems. It has been proved for Coxeter groups of types
I
and

[

H_{3}

Bruhat preclosure
recently introduced by Dermenjian in
[

For each Coxeter system
(W,S)
, we recall that the Bruhat graph
B(W)
is the directed graph having
W
as vertex set, and where for any
u,v\in W
there is an arrow
u\xrightarrow{t}v
whenever

and
\ell(u)<\ell(v)

Definition 1
(
A
-Bruhat path)

Let
W
be a Coxeter group; consider the Bruhat graph

associated with
W
and
A\subseteq T
. An

is any directed path in
B(W)
starting from the identity element

of

, whose edges are labeled by elements in the set
A
.
If a set

can be written as a union of two left-reflection sets, i.e.
A=T_{L}(u)\cup T_{L}(v)
for some
u,v\in W
, then we use the expression
(u,v)
-Bruhat paths instead of
A
-Bruhat paths.

Definition 2
(Bruhat preclosure)

Given a set of reflection
A\subseteq T
; its Bruhat preclosure is

\overline{A}=\{t\in T\mid\text{there is an $A$-Bruhat path from }e\text{ to }t\}.

If
A=\overline{A}
we say it is
preclosed

Observe that this operation is not a closure operator: in general,

\overline{\overline{A}}\neq\overline{A}
. Indeed, counterexamples were found by Dermenjian in Coxeter groups of types

and

; see
[

Consequently, the following conjecture is of a rather different nature from Theorem 10-3.25, where the operations involved are proper closure operators.

Conjecture 3

Let
W
be a finite Coxeter group and
u,v\in W
. Then

T_{L}(u\vee_{R}v)=\overline{T_{L}(u)\cup T_{L}(v)}.

In general, computing the join of two elements in the poset

Conjecture

-Bruhat paths.

Algorithm

In this section, we describe an algorithm to compute the join of two elements in
(S^{B}_{n},\leq_{R})
. Note that when restricted to standard permutations this coincides with the algorithm in
[
6
, §2]
; therefore it can be considered a generalization of it.
For simplicity and readability in the definition of the algorithm, we use the complete notation for the two input signed permutations.

Definition 4
(
n
-cut)

The
n
-cut of
\sigma\in S^{B}_{n}
is the pair
(B_{\sigma},B^{*}_{\sigma})
, where
B_{\sigma}
and
B^{*}_{\sigma}
are the strings made of all entries different from
\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt
, respectively, on the left and on the right of the entry
n
in the complete notation of
\sigma
.
More precisely, if
k:=|\sigma^{-1}(n)|
, we have:

1.

if
\sigma^{-1}(n)<0
, then

\begin{split}&B_{\sigma}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk+1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt),\\
&B_{\sigma}^{*}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(k-1)\sigma(k+1)\cdots\sigma(n);\end{split}

2.

if
\sigma^{-1}(n)>0
, then

\begin{split}&B_{\sigma}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk+1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(k-1),\\
&B_{\sigma}^{*}=\sigma(k+1)\cdots\sigma(n).\end{split}

Moreover, in case 1, we write
B^{*}_{\sigma}=B^{*^{\prime}}_{\sigma}B^{*^{\prime\prime}}_{\sigma}
, where

B^{*^{\prime}}_{\sigma}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(k-1),\qquad B^{*^{\prime\prime}}_{\sigma}=\sigma(k+1)\cdots\sigma(n),

while in case 2, we write
B_{\sigma}=B^{\prime}_{\sigma}B^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma}
, where

B^{\prime}_{\sigma}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk+1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt),\qquad B^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma}=\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptk-1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\cdots\sigma(\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt)\sigma(1)\cdots\sigma(k-1).

Thus, in case 1, we express the complete notation of the signed permutation as
\sigma=B_{\sigma}nB^{*^{\prime}}_{\sigma}\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5ptB^{*^{\prime\prime}}_{\sigma}
, while in case 2, we can write
\sigma=B^{\prime}_{\sigma}\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5ptn\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5ptB^{\prime\prime}_{\sigma}nB^{*}_{\sigma}
.

Given

H^{\sigma}_{i}=\{a\in[\pm(i-1)]\mid\sigma^{-1}(a)>\sigma^{-1}(i)\}.

Observe that the underlying set of the string

coincides with

Algorithm 1

Consider

.

1.

Set

\pi_{1}=\begin{cases}\kern 0.5pt\overline{\kern-0.5pt1\kern-0.5pt}\kern 0.5pt1,\quad&\emph{if}\sigma^{-1}(1)>0\text{ and }\tau^{-1}(1)>0,\\

(a)

(b)

(c)

The main result of this extended abstract is the following theorem, which shows that Algorithm
1
correctly computes the join of two signed permutations in
(S^{B}_{n},\leq_{R})

Theorem 13

Let
\sigma,\tau\in S^{B}_{n}
; then

J(\sigma,\tau)=\sigma\vee_{R}\tau.

(4)

We conclude this section by sketching our proof of Theorem
13
. We prove by induction on
i
that
\pi_{i}=\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i}\vee_{R}\rho_{\tau}^{n-i}\in S^{B}_{i}
; in particular, we show that

\displaystyle\mathrm{Inv}((\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i})^{-1})\cup\mathrm{Inv}((\rho_{\tau}^{n-i})^{-1})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{Inv}(\pi_{i}^{-1}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Neg}((\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i})^{-1})\cup\mathrm{Neg}((\rho_{\tau}^{n-i})^{-1})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{Neg}(\pi_{i}^{-1}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Nsp}((\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i})^{-1})\cup\mathrm{Nsp}((\rho_{\tau}^{n-i})^{-1})

\displaystyle\subseteq\mathrm{Nsp}(\pi_{i}^{-1}),

getting
\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i},\rho_{\tau}^{n-i}\leq_{R}\pi_{i}
, which implies
\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i}\vee_{R}\rho_{\tau}^{n-i}\leq_{R}\pi_{i}.

Then, in order to obtain
\pi_{i}\leq_{R}\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i}\vee_{R}\rho_{\tau}^{n-i}
, it is sufficient to prove that for any
\beta\in S^{B}_{i}
such that
\rho_{\sigma}^{n-i},\rho_{\tau}^{n-i}\leq_{R}\beta

\pi_{i}\leq_{R}\beta
. This is done by using Lemma

and Proposition

on

The case

The main importance of Theorem

Theorem 14

Let
\sigma,\tau\in S^{B}_{n}

We conclude this extended abstract with a related open problem. As noted above, our algorithm generalizes the one defined by Markowsky for computing the join of two permutations in the weak order of the symmetric group
S_{n}
.
Indeed,
S_{n}
is isomorphic to the subgroup of
S^{B}_{n}
consisting of signed permutations with no negative entries.
Furthermore,

is a sublattice of
(S^{B}_{n},\leq_{R})
and Algorithm

Within

In general, however, Algorithm

does not compute the join of two signed permutations in the poset

As a Coxeter system,
