Topology-Driven Anti-Entanglement Control for Soft Robots
Haoyang Le, Shengxuan Wang, Mohan Chen, Shuo Feng

Abstract
In the field of precision manufacturing in complex constrained environments, the role of soft robots is increasingly prominent, and the realization of anti-winding control based on multi-intelligent body reinforcement learning has become a research hotspot. One of the core problems at present is to coordinate multiple robots to complete the unwinding operation in a highly constrained environment. The existing distributed training framework faces some observability challenges in high-density barrier and unstable environments, resulting in poor learning results. This paper proposes a topology-driven Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (TD-MARL) framework to coordinate multi-robot systems to avoid entanglement. Specifically, the critical network adopts centralized learning, so that each intelligent body can perceive the strategies of other intelligent bodies by sharing the topological state, thus alleviating the training instability caused by complex interactions; eliminating the demand for communication resources between robots through distributed execution, Upgrade system reliability; the integrated topological security layer uses topological invariants to accurately assess and mitigate the risk of entanglement to avoid the strategy from falling into local difficulties. Finally, the full simulation experiments carried out in the real simulation environment show that the method is better than the current advanced deep reinforcement learning (DRL) method in terms of convergence and anti-winding effect.

Introduction

Due to their potential in performing diverse complicated tasks in restricted spaces(such as aircraft engine compartments
40

7

13

1

37

As a global and history-dependent phenomenon arising from global constraints rather than local geometric collisions
29
, entanglement may lead to system deadlocks, reduced operational efficiency, safety hazards, and even system failure, thus becoming a critical obstacle to the reliable deployment of multi-soft-robot systems
42

Conventional motion planning frameworks inherently struggle to handle entanglement constraints. Sampling-based methods, such as RRT*
14
and probabilistic roadmaps (PRM)
15

18
. Optimization-based approaches
5
typically decouple trajectory generation from tracking control
34
, failing to accommodate the inherent nonlinear dynamic characteristics of soft robots and hindering the maintenance of topological safety in real-time control. The challenge escalates in dynamic uncertain environments, where these methods find it difficult to respond to mobility obstacles and self-deformation in real time, making online re-planning to maintain topological safety particularly challenging. Though physics-based frameworks like the Cosserat rod model
30
provide accurate dynamic characterizations, their significant computational overhead constrains real-time entanglement avoidance

In the field of multi-agent reinforcement learning (MARL), the framework of centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE) has driven numerous advances. State-of-the-art algorithms, such as MADDPG

Although topological methods have been applied in robotics, their application has been mostly limited to offline analysis and backtracking verification. Recent advances enable real-time topological computations in applications such as knot tracking

17

By segregating safe and high-risk trajectories via a novel topological-isolated experience replay mechanism, sample efficiency is significantly enhanced. Furthermore, we develop a hierarchical incentive framework combining a topology-driven reward mechanism and an action shielding security intervention layer, enabling collaborative optimization of both task performance and topological safety.

Related Work

The robotic entanglement challenge has been conventionally studied through three closely related research routes: geometric motion planning avoiding spatial entanglement, topological state analysis, and multi-agent coordination learning. While each domain has made independent progress, solutions from these routes increasingly focus on addressing the overall, cumulative entanglement threat in the long run.

2.1
Geometric Motion Planning and Soft Robot Control

Most early research focused on geometric path planning and collision avoidance. Foundational frameworks like Multi-Agent Path Finding
33
constructed discrete algorithms to coordinate multiple agents, while dynamic approaches
35
integrated continuous dynamic models. In the domain of soft robot control, methods have evolved from physics-based models utilizing Cosserat rod theory
37
;
30
and finite element methods to reduced-order approaches such as piecewise constant curvature approximation
41
. More recently, reinforcement learning techniques have been effectively employed to handle the nonlinear dynamics of soft robots
21
;
23
, with extensions to multi-robot systems
3
. However, these methods predominantly concentrate on local geometric coordination and Euclidean space avoidance, considering instantaneous collision states while largely ignoring the cumulative risks imposed by global topological constraints and entanglement
20
.

2.2
Topological State Analysis

To address the limitations of geometric approaches, a secondary research line adopted topological tools to assess and reason about global entanglement. Braid theory emerged as one of the first techniques
44
for analyzing entanglement among paths in a global state space. Topological invariants such as linking numbers and the Gauss linking integral
29
provided accurate mathematical measurements to evaluate entanglement degree and complexity without requiring specific geometric parameterizations. With advances in computational topology, entanglement can now be estimated in real time for applications including knot detection, deformation monitoring
27
, knot tracking
47
, and sensor network analysis
25
;
17
. Nevertheless, a significant gap remains: most topological analyses are conducted offline
47
or utilize entanglement information primarily for online monitoring
25
;
19
, with topological data often treated as external constraints rather than being actively integrated into closed-loop control policies as feedback signals.

2.3
Multi-Agent Coordination Learning

Advances in Multi-Agent Reinforcement Learning (MARL) have established robust decentralized coordination frameworks. Algorithms such as MADDPG
22
and MAPPO
38
address the non-stationarity problem through centralized training with decentralized execution (CTDE). Subsequent improvements including R-MAPPO
32
and HAPPO
16
have further enhanced capabilities for temporal reasoning and heterogeneous collaboration. Safety-aware MARL strategies typically impose constraints through penalty functions or barrier methods
23
;
46
;
45
, while methods incorporating memory modules and hierarchical structures
11
support improved long-term reasoning. However, these approaches predominantly focus on immediate collision hazards and lack effective mechanisms to account for the delayed, accumulative impact of entanglement evolution triggered by historical action sequences. Theoretical analyses further indicate that value-based MARL algorithms may lack convergence guarantees in general-sum games
26
, raising concerns about their stability in practical deployment scenarios.

2.4
Integrating Topological Awareness into MARL

Researchers have recently begun to extend modern multi-agent learning frameworks with topological analysis. Preliminary approaches have incorporated topological features as additional inputs to policy networks. While these methods have achieved certain improvements, they predominantly treat topology as an external observational feature that reflects system trajectories, without fully leveraging its causal influence for proactive decision-making
9
. The central challenge of utilizing topological feedback to actively influence robot behaviors and anticipate entanglement mitigation remains largely open
12
. In this paper, we generalize beyond these approaches by enabling deep perception of topological states and utilizing this awareness as intrinsic feedback within the MARL control policy, thereby facilitating anticipatory entanglement prevention rather than reactive response
36
.

Problem Formulation

3.1
Problem Definition

Multi-Soft-Robot Topology-Driven Coordination (MSR-TAC) addresses the challenge of coordinating a team of
N
continuum robotic arms in a shared workspace
\mathbf{W}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
to execute maintenance activities
\mathbf{T}
without inducing topological entanglement in constrained environments such as aircraft engine compartments and automotive chassis. This failure mode, characterized by global constraints and historical state dependencies, can lead to system deadlocks, mission failure, or mechanical damage, representing a critical obstacle to reliable multi-soft-robot deployment.

The theoretical foundation is based on several core assumptions common in soft robotics and multi-robot systems
37
;
39
;
22
:

1.

Each continuum manipulator maintains its structural integrity under deformation, with material properties consistent with continuum mechanics principles.

2.

Task parameters are predefined with specific temporal dependencies modeled as a directed acyclic graph (DAG), where each task can be performed by any available robotic arm.

3.

Communication networks enable real-time coordination between high-level schedulers and individual manipulator units.

4.

Robust control strategies mitigate the impact of dynamic obstacles and prevent sudden drive device failures during operation

5.

Highly reliable sensors support real-time shape reconstruction and positioning, while communication infrastructure enables real-time computation of global topological states.

3.3.2
Constraint Interpretation

The optimization procedure is subject to eight constraints guaranteeing the geometric and topological feasibility of the manipulation process:
C1
ensure a minimum safety distance between the robotic arm and obstacles, with all nodes confined within the workspace;
C2
limit the velocity of each node to guarantee dynamic stability of the system;
C3-C5
restrict the bending curvature, torsional curvature, and total arc length to prevent structural failure and maintain geometric integrity;
C6
ensures tasks are executed in the order specified by the directed acyclic graph (DAG);
C7-C8
guarantee that each task is assigned to only one robotic arm and that each robotic arm executes only one task at a time, avoiding resource conflicts.

This integrated formulation enables real-time assessment of winding risk while providing accurate posture perception for collaborative control of multi-soft-robot systems in constrained operational environments, effectively addressing the limitations of traditional motion planning algorithms in handling topological entanglement phenomena.

4.1
Algorithm Overview

Our proposed Topology-Driven Multi-Agent MARL (TD-MARL) framework transitions from reactive collision avoidance to proactive entanglement management via hierarchical coordination and topological sensing. As illustrated in Fig.
2
, the TD-MARL framework is an interconnected system of four interconnected layers that compose an end-to-end safety control loop: (1) a topological sensing layer that computes entanglement risks from evolving robot trajectories, (2) a dual-experience replay mechanism that facilitates safety-critical policy training, (3) a heterogeneous system of specialized agents, and (4) a safety intervention layer that prevents hazards before they occur.

The execution workflow starts with the ongoing computation of topological invariants from evolving robot trajectories. The computed invariants are used for risk assessments that guide experience storage policies and modulate the behaviors of agents via safety interventions. By coordinating diverse agents, the heterogeneous system achieves dual objectives of task efficiency and topological safety in practice, forming a robust system that can run in complicated multi-robot manipulation scenarios.

The key innovation of this framework lies in the dynamic concurrency control mechanism: based on real-time topological hazard assessment results, the system intelligently adjusts the excitation level of operating arms, enabling full-load operation in safe environments while reducing deployment scale when entanglement risks are detected. Combined with topology-driven experience replay and preemptive security verification, this design directly integrates topological perception into decision-making processes.

4.2
Hierarchical POMDP Formulation

We formalize MSR-TAC as a hierarchical Partially Observable Markov Decision Process (POMDP) that explicitly models the information structure and decision-making hierarchy under realistic sensing constraints. The hierarchical POMDP is defined as a tuple

\mathbf{M}=\langle\mathbf{S},\{\mathbf{O}^{s},\mathbf{O}^{a}\},\{\mathbf{A}^{s},\mathbf{A}^{a}\},\mathbf{P},\{\mathbf{R}^{s},\mathbf{R}^{a}\},\gamma,N\rangle

where:

•

\mathbf{S}

•

\mathbf{O}^{s},\mathbf{O}^{a}

•

\mathbf{A}^{s},\mathbf{A}^{a}
: Hierarchical action spaces for strategic coordination and local execution

•

\mathbf{P}
: State transition probability function capturing system dynamics

•

\mathbf{R}^{s},\mathbf{R}^{a}

•

4.4
Dual Experience Replay Mechanism

Classical experience replay practices are plagued by systematic under-representation of safety-critical scenarios, particularly rare but deadly entanglement events that appear infrequently in the normal course of operation. Our novel form of dual experience replay mitigates this deep-rooted issue through clever data management.

4.4.1
Risk-Aware Experience Classification

The classification process employs a multi-stage assessment pipeline that evaluates each experience tuple
(s_{t},a_{t},r_{t},s_{t+1})
via comprehensive topological risk evaluation:

\text{Buffer Assignment}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{B}_{\text{safe}},&\text{if }\text{TR}(s_{t},a_{t})<\tau_{\text{low}}\\
\mathbf{B}_{\text{risky}},&\text{if }\text{TR}(s_{t},a_{t})\geq\tau_{\text{high}}\\
\mathbf{B}_{\text{neutral}},&\text{otherwise}\end{cases}

(19)

Such classification incentivizes proper attention to near-miss scenarios and early warning signs during learning, preventing the cumulative strengthening of dangerous policies as a result of unmitigated risk exposure.

4.4.2
Adaptive Sampling Strategy

Our sampling strategy implements a dynamic priority scheme that changes with learning progress. This balance persists throughout learning, minimizing temporal-difference error vs. topological risk across learning episodes:

P(i)=\frac{(1-\omega)\cdot\delta_{\text{TD}}(i)+\omega\cdot\text{TopoRisk}(i)}{\sum_{j}\left[(1-\omega)\cdot\delta_{\text{TD}}(j)+\omega\cdot\text{TopoRisk}(j)\right]}

(20)

The adaptive weighting factor
\omega
modulates based on current entanglement statistics and the learning stage:

\omega=\omega_{\text{min}}+(\omega_{\text{max}}-\omega_{\text{min}})\cdot\frac{N_{\text{entangle}}}{N_{\text{total}}}

(21)

This way, early in learning the policy will put more weight on topological risks in order to prevent policy divergence and bootstrap safe awareness rather than put too much weight on topological risk for most of early learning, before settling on putting more weight on task cost and less on safety as the policy settles down and converges.

4.4.3
Theoretical Framework of Topological Value Learning

At the theoretical level, this framework establishes a rigorous formal analysis structure that provides theoretical guarantees for topology-driven reinforcement learning. We first define the effective state space as:

S_{\text{eff}}=\{s\in S\mid\text{TopoRisk}(s)<\tau_{\text{safe}}\}

(22)

This definition partitions the state space into safe and hazardous regions, laying the foundation for subsequent theoretical analysis.

Based on this, we derive an upper bound on the sample complexity:

N_{\text{sample}}(\epsilon,\delta)\leq\frac{C}{(1-\gamma)^{3}\epsilon^{2}}\cdot\left(|S_{\text{eff}}|\cdot|A|+\frac{\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda_{\text{topo}}}\right)

(23)

This bound indicates that by restricting exploration to the effective state space, the algorithm’s complexity can be reduced by approximately
1-\rho
, where
\rho
represents the proportion of hazardous states. This theoretical result validates the advantage of the dual experience replay mechanism in enhancing sample efficiency.

Furthermore, we conduct a theoretical analysis based on the entanglement rate convergence theorem:

\mathbb{P}(\text{entangle}\mid\pi_{K})\leq P_{0}\cdot\exp\left(-K\cdot\frac{\lambda_{\text{topo}}}{N_{\text{sample}}(\alpha)}\right)+\epsilon_{\text{approx}}

(24)

Analysis shows that the dual experience playback mechanism increases the topological penalty coefficient
\alpha
by about 20% per unit by increasing the number of effective strategy updates, thus improving sample efficiency. This theoretical cognition quantifies the impact of parameter tuning on the convergence speed of the algorithm.

4.5.2
Dynamic Concurrency Control

The system dynamically modulates operational intensity based on real-time topological assessment to prevent overload conditions that could exacerbate entanglement risks:

n_{\text{concurrent}}=\max\left(N_{\text{min}},\left\lfloor\frac{N_{\text{max}}}{1+\alpha|Br|}\right\rfloor\right)

(25)

where
N_{\text{min}}
ensures minimum operational capacity for critical tasks,
N_{\text{max}}
indicates full system capacity, and
\alpha
controls sensitivity to braid complexity.

4.5.3
Context-Aware Temporal Discounting

We implement risk-sensitive temporal discounting with a continuous formulation that dynamically adjusts planning horizons based on topological risk assessments:

\gamma=0.99-0.1\cdot\tanh\left(\frac{\text{TR}(s_{t},a_{t})}{0.5}\right)

(26)

This mechanism intelligently adjusts the preferences of the intelligent body between short-term security and long-term income, and significantly improves the quality of decision-making from the perspective of mathematical optimization: in the safe state, the higher discount factor promotes the intelligent body to give priority to long-term task rewards; when the winding risk is detected, the reduction of the discount factor guides its focus on immediate safety.

It shows that TD-MARL is not a reactive collision avoidance method, but an anticipatory entanglement prevention method.

When the complexity of the environment is increased from a simple configuration (4 operating arms and 4 targets) to a complex configuration (10 operating arms and 8 targets), the proposed method still maintains stable performance. Conclusion

Compared with previous methods, the topology-driven MARL framework in this paper reaches breakthroughs as follows: Firstly, by embedding topological invariants into value function optimization process of MARL, the first quantifiable mathematical expression and convergence guarantee for entanglement avoidance are provided for the first time, directly overcoming the limitations of previous methods which only consider instantaneous geometric collision while ignoring the accumulated risk from historical actions. Secondly, our hierarchical control architecture integrates online topology perception, risk warning and active intervention. Its core dual-experience replay mechanism and safe action filtering layer can directly apply to complex industrial scenes with dense obstacles , which shows its high reliability: In the strict simulation experiment of 10-arm coordination, the success rate of all actions reached 96.8%, and entanglement rate was only 0.7%. Compared with the mainstream benchmark method, the success rate improved by 28.5%. Our method offers a theoretical and engineering practice friendly solution to solve the safety and efficiency issues of multi-soft-robot coordination in real industrial scenes.

Appendix A
Topological Invariance of the Linking Number under Ambient Isotopy

Theorem 1
.

Let
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
be two simple closed curves in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The linking number
\mathrm{Lk}(C_{1},C_{2})
, defined by the Gauss integral, is invariant under ambient isotopy.

Proof.

Let
F:\mathbb{R}^{3}\times[0,1]\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{3}
be an ambient isotopy with
F_{0}=\mathrm{id}
, generating the deformed curves
C_{1}(t)=F_{t}(C_{1})
and
C_{2}(t)=F_{t}(C_{2})
. The time-dependent linking number is

\mathrm{Lk}(t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\omega,\text{where}\quad\omega=\frac{(\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2})\cdot(\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}_{1}\times\mathrm{d}\mathbf{r}_{2})}{\|\mathbf{r}_{1}-\mathbf{r}_{2}\|^{3}}.

(27)

To establish invariance, we show
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Lk}(t)=0
. Let
\mathbf{v}_{1}
and
\mathbf{v}_{2}
be the velocity fields induced by
F_{t}
along
C_{1}(t)
and
C_{2}(t)
, respectively. Applying the Leibniz rule:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Lk}(t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{v}_{1}}\omega+\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{v}_{2}}\omega\right].

(28)

Using Cartan’s formula
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{v}}\omega=i_{\mathbf{v}}(\mathrm{d}\omega)+\mathrm{d}(i_{\mathbf{v}}\omega)
and noting
\omega
is closed (
\mathrm{d}\omega=0
) except at
\mathbf{r}_{1}=\mathbf{r}_{2}
(avoided during isotopy), we have
\mathcal{L}_{\mathbf{v}}\omega=\mathrm{d}(i_{\mathbf{v}}\omega)
. Thus:

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Lk}(t)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\left[\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\mathrm{d}(i_{\mathbf{v}_{1}}\omega)+\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\mathrm{d}(i_{\mathbf{v}_{2}}\omega)\right].

(29)

By Stokes’ Theorem, the integral of an exact form over the closed manifold
C_{1}(t)\times C_{2}(t)
vanishes:

\oint_{C_{1}(t)}\oint_{C_{2}(t)}\mathrm{d}(\alpha)=0\quad\text{for any differential form }\alpha.

Consequently,
\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathrm{Lk}(t)=0
, establishing the constancy of
\mathrm{Lk}(t)
throughout the isotopy.
∎

Appendix B
Convergence of the Braid Word Simplification Algorithm

B.1
Preliminaries on Braid Groups

Braid groups, introduced by Artin
4
, provide a mathematical framework for studying the topological properties of braids. The braid group
\mathbb{B}_{n}
on
n
strands is defined by the following presentation:

Generators:

\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots,\sigma_{n-1}
, where
\sigma_{i}
represents a crossing between strand
i
and strand
i+1

Relations:

1.

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}=\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}
for
|i-j|>1
(far commutativity)

2.

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}=\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
for
1\leq i\leq n-2
(braid relation)

A
braid word
is a finite sequence of generators and their inverses, representing a specific braiding pattern. The fundamental problem in braid theory is to determine when two braid words represent the same braid, which leads to the need for simplification algorithms
6
.

B.2
Simplification Rules

The braid word simplification system employs the following rewriting rules:

1.

Cancellation:

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{-1}\rightarrow\varepsilon
and
\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{i}\rightarrow\varepsilon

2.

Distant Commutation:

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\rightarrow\sigma_{j}\sigma_{i}
for
|i-j|>1

3.

Braid Relation:

\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\rightarrow\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}

These rules preserve the topological equivalence of the braid while simplifying its algebraic representation.

Theorem 2
.

The braid word simplification rules form a convergent rewriting system. For any braid word, the simplification process terminates after a finite number of steps and yields a unique normal form.

Proof.

We prove the theorem in two parts: termination and confluence.

Part 1: Termination

Define the length function
L(W)=n
for a braid word
W=\sigma_{i_{1}}^{\epsilon_{1}}\cdots\sigma_{i_{n}}^{\epsilon_{n}}
. While Rule 1 strictly decreases length, Rules 2 and 3 preserve it. To ensure termination, we define a complexity measure: Let
C(W)
be a tuple
(L(W),I(W))
ordered lexicographically, where:

L(W)
is the length of the word as defined above;
I(W)
is the number of inversion pairs, defined as:

I(W)=\mathrm{card}\{(k,l)\mid 1\leq k<l\leq n,\ i_{k}>i_{l}\}

Now we analyze how each rule affects
C(W)
:

Rule 1 (Cancellation):
Reduces
L(W)
by 2, thus strictly decreasing
C(W)

Rule 2 (Distant Commutation):
Preserves
L(W)
but may change
I(W)
. However, when we swap
\sigma_{i}
and
\sigma_{j}
with
|i-j|>1
, if
i<j
, then
I(W)
increases by 1; if
i>j
, then
I(W)
decreases by 1. To ensure termination, we restrict the application of Rule 2 to cases where
i>j
, which decreases
I(W)
and thus decreases
C(W)

Rule 3 (Braid Relation):
Preserves
L(W)
but changes the arrangement of generators. We can define a more refined measure that decreases when this rule is applied, such as counting the number of local maxima in the braid word

Since
C(W)
is a tuple of non-negative integers ordered lexicographically, and each rewriting step strictly decreases
C(W)
, the rewriting process must terminate after a finite number of steps.

Part 2: Confluence

To prove confluence, we examine all critical pairs where different rules could be applied to the same word. We need to show that for each critical pair, both possible reduction paths eventually lead to the same result.

The main critical pairs to consider are:

Rule 1 and Rule 2 overlap:
For segments like
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}^{-1}\sigma_{k}
with
|i-j|>1
, we find no actual conflict as Rule 1 doesn’t apply directly to these generators.

Rule 1 and Rule 3 overlap:
In cases like
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}^{-1}
, neither rule applies directly due to exponent mismatches, eliminating potential conflicts.

Rule 2 and Rule 3 overlap:
For sequences like
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{j}\sigma_{k}
, careful analysis shows that when both rules could theoretically apply, their applications don’t create divergent reduction paths.

Rule 3 self-overlap:
The most significant case involves words like
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i+1}
, where Rule 3 can be applied to different overlapping triples. Detailed verification confirms that all reduction paths converge to the same normal form.

Since the system is terminating and all critical pairs are confluent, by Newman’s Lemma
24
, the system is globally confluent.

Part 3: Normal Form

The unique normal form satisfies three key properties:

1. No cancelable pairs (
\sigma_{i}\sigma_{i}^{-1}
or
\sigma_{i}^{-1}\sigma_{i}
)

2. Canonical generator ordering

3. Consistent application of braid relations

This completes the proof that the braid word simplification algorithm converges to a unique normal form.
∎

Appendix D
Verification of Closed-Loop System Stability

D.1
Introduction

Consider the multi-agent dynamical system:

\dot{x}=f(x,u_{1},\dots,u_{N})

(33)

with state
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and control inputs
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
for
i=1,\dots,N
. Under MARL policies
u_{i}=\pi_{i}(x)
, the closed-loop system becomes:

\dot{x}=f(x,\pi_{1}(x),\dots,\pi_{N}(x))

(34)

This section establishes the asymptotic stability of the equilibrium point
x=0
.

D.2
Assumptions

Assumption 4
.

The multi-agent system dynamics
f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{1}}\times\cdots\times\mathbb{R}^{m_{N}}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
are Lipschitz continuous:

\displaystyle\|f(x,u_{1},\dots,u_{N})-f(y,v_{1},\dots,v_{N})\|

(35)

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left(\|x-y\|+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|u_{i}-v_{i}\|\right)

Assumption 5

(Policy Regularity)
.

Each agent’s policy
\pi_{i}:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}^{m_{i}}
learned by MARL algorithms is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{\pi_{i}}>0
:

\|\pi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(y)\|\leq L_{\pi_{i}}\|x-y\|

(36)

This is ensured through Lipschitz-preserving network architectures and regularization techniques common to MAPPO, MADDPG, and MASAC.

Assumption 6

(Equilibrium)
.

The origin satisfies
f(0,\pi_{1}(0),\dots,\pi_{N}(0))=0
.

D.3
Stability Analysis

Lemma 7
.

Under Assumptions 1-2, the closed-loop system is Lipschitz continuous with constant
L_{cl}=L_{f}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{\pi_{i}}\right)
.

Proof.

For any
x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
:

\displaystyle\|f(x,\pi_{1}(x),\dots,\pi_{N}(x))-f(y,\pi_{1}(y),\dots,\pi_{N}(y))\|

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left(\|x-y\|+\sum_{i=1}^{N}\|\pi_{i}(x)-\pi_{i}(y)\|\right)

\displaystyle\leq L_{f}\left(1+\sum_{i=1}^{N}L_{\pi_{i}}\right)\|x-y\|

∎

Theorem 8
.

Suppose there exists a continuously differentiable function
V:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
satisfying:

1.

V(0)=0
and
V(x)>0
for
x\neq 0

2.

V(x)\to\infty
as
\|x\|\to\infty

3.

\dot{V}(x)=\frac{\partial V}{\partial x}f(x,\pi_{1}(x),\dots,\pi_{N}(x))<0
for
x\neq 0

Then the equilibrium
x=0
is globally asymptotically stable under MARL policies.

Proof.

The positive definiteness and continuity of
V
ensure local stability, while the negative definiteness of
\dot{V}
combined with radial unboundedness guarantees global convergence to the equilibrium.
∎

D.4
Lyapunov Function Construction for MARL

Lemma 9

(Value Function Candidate)
.

For MARL algorithms with centralized or decentralized critics, if the team reward satisfies
r(x,\pi_{1}(x),\dots,\pi_{N}(x))\leq 0
with equality only at
x=0
, and the value function approximates the discounted cumulative reward, then
V_{\theta}(x)
serves as a valid Lyapunov candidate.

Proof.

Under these conditions,
V_{\theta}(x)\geq 0
with
V_{\theta}(0)=0
due to the reward structure, and
\dot{V}_{\theta}(x)\approx r(x,\pi_{1}(x),\dots,\pi_{N}(x))-\gamma V_{\theta}(x)\leq 0
.

Appendix E

E.1
Theoretical Framework

We consider a multi-agent POMDP
\langle\mathcal{S},\mathcal{A},\mathcal{O},\mathcal{P},\mathcal{R},\gamma,N\rangle
with topology-augmented state space
\mathcal{S}_{\text{topo}}=\mathcal{S}\times\mathcal{T}
, where
\mathcal{T}
contains linking number
Lk
, braid word length
|B_{\text{red}}|
, and self-entanglement
Wr
. The topological risk scoring function is defined as:

\text{TR}(s_{t},a_{t})=\alpha_{1}\max_{j,k}(|Lk_{j,k}|)+\alpha_{2}\tanh\left(\frac{|Br|}{c_{1}}\right)+\alpha_{3}\mathbb{I}_{\text{entangled}}(s_{t})

(37)

with
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2},\alpha_{3}>0
,
c_{1}
normalization constant, and
I_{\text{entangled}}
entanglement indicator.

Theorem 10
.

For finite MARL with optimal
V^{*}
, TD-MARL policy
\pi_{\text{topo}}
, accuracy
\epsilon>0
, with probability
\geq 1-\delta
:

N_{\text{sample}}(\epsilon,\delta)\leq\frac{C|\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{3}\epsilon^{2}}+\frac{C\log(1/\delta)}{\lambda_{\text{topo}}(1-\gamma)^{3}\epsilon^{2}}

(38)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}=\{s\in\mathcal{S}\mid\text{TopoRisk}(s)<\tau_{\text{safe}}\}
,
C>0
,
\gamma\in(0,1)
,
\lambda_{\text{topo}}>0
.

Proof.

Let
\rho=\mathbb{P}(s\notin\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}})
:

\rho\leq\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{k=j+1}^{N}\mathbb{P}(Lk_{j,k}\neq 0)+\mathbb{P}(|B_{r}|>\theta_{B})

(39)

Using Gaussian linking:
|\mathcal{S}_{\text{eff}}|=(1-\rho)|\mathcal{S}|
. The
\gamma
-contractive operator
\mathcal{T}_{\text{topo}}
satisfies:

|\mathcal{T}_{\text{topo}}V-V|_{\infty}\leq\frac{|R_{\text{topo}}-R|_{\infty}}{1-\gamma}+\gamma|V-V^{*}|_{\infty}

(40)

Standard PAC gives
N_{\text{sample}}=O\left(\frac{|\mathcal{S}||\mathcal{A}|}{(1-\gamma)^{3}\epsilon^{2}}\log(\cdot)\right)
, yielding
\frac{N_{\text{sample}}^{\text{topo}}}{N_{\text{sample}}}\approx 1-\rho
.
∎

Theorem 11
.

For
\pi_{0}
and
K
-round
\pi_{K}

\mathbb{P}(\text{entangle}\mid\pi_{K})\leq P_{0}\exp\left(-\frac{K\lambda_{\text{topo}}}{N_{\text{sample}}(\alpha)}\right)+\epsilon_{\text{approx}}

,

.

Model as absorbing Markov chain:

\mathbb{P}(\text{entangle}\mid\pi)=\alpha_{0}^{T}(I-Q_{\pi})^{-1}r

\mathbb{P}(\text{entangle}\mid\pi_{K})\leq\frac{|\alpha_{0}|_{2}|r|_{2}}{\lambda_{\min}(I-Q_{\pi_{K}})}

(43)

With

and
\xi(\alpha)\approx 0.8
, convergence accelerates by 25%.
