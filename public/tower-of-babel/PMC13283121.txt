Design oriented modeling of squeezed MHD convective flow of hybrid nanoliquid over a sensor surface with chemical reaction advanced sensor technology using artificial neural network

ABSTRACT
This design-oriented methodology has significant uses in industrial process optimization, and advanced sensor technologies. The same framework is also relevant to mining and mineral-processing environments, where sensor-assisted monitoring is required for ore-slurry transport, chemically reactive leaching systems, tailings process streams, and mine-water treatment units operating under coupled thermal and mass-transfer conditions. Hybrid nanofluid increase sensitivity and response time under convective and magnetic control, which helps improve the thermal and mass transfer performance of chemical and biosensors. To improve the analysis and prediction accuracy of such fluid flows, we used a supervised neural network with back-propagated Levenberg–Marquardt technique (SNN-BLM). The model is helpful in biomedical engineering for creating drug delivery systems, lab-on-a-chip systems, and bio-reactors, where microorganism bioconvection promotes efficient reaction rates and uniform mixing. It facilitates the improvement of chemical reactors, coating procedures, and micro-cooling devices in energy and manufacturing systems, allowing for improved control of heat, flow stability, and reaction efficiency through the use of magnetic fields and nanofluid characteristics. This study presents a computational analysis of squeezed bioconvection hybrid nanofluid flow over a sensor surface, incorporating a heat source. The interplay between bioconvection, hybrid nanoparticles and chemical reaction is of utmost importance in fluid dynamics and heat and mass transmission. The result is gained using the Homotopy analysis method (HAM). The velocity profile increases by enhancing the values of magnetic parameter and squeezed flow index parameter. The temperature profile decreases by enhancing the values of suction/injection parameter and squeezed flow index parameter. Introduction

The novel artificial intelligence technique in fluid mechanics encircles deep learning, which covers neural networks. Artificial neural networks (ANNs) are new tools applied mathematics, like data classification in various minor sets, curve analysis, self-driving vehicles, and prediction in data set for stock market predictions and trading. Artificial neural networks offer a computational framework for handling unprocessed data. Much like how biological neurons work in the human nervous system, the artificial neurons found in neural networks rely on mathematical operations to capture relationships between different physical variables. To give some perspective, the human brain is thought to contain roughly 10 billion neurons, and each neuron typically connects with about 10,000 others. These biological neurons take in raw information and, after carrying out suitable mathematical steps, produce meaningful outputs. In a similar spirit, ANNs have grown in popularity largely because of their usefulness in fluid dynamics research. Their strength lies in learning complex patterns and generalizing links between inputs and outputs, which makes them highly applicable to a wide variety of fluid flow modeling problems. [2] explored the ANN predictive model for heat transfer rate in MHD Cattaneo–Christov heat flux flow over a stretching cylinder. The enhancing mass transfer in hybrid nanofluid with motile microbes and LTNE effects using ANN is explored by Aoudia et al. [3]. Sharma et al. [4] explored the low variational magnetic field between two rotating disks on sodium alginate-based hybrid nanofluid flow: An ANN-based study. Liaqat et al. [12] turn to machine learning—specifically a linear regression approach—to study three-dimensional magnetohydrodynamics Casson fluid flow with Cattaneo–Christov heat flux, highlighting its relevance to engineering science and technology. Ben Hamida et al. [13] investigated thermal analysis in bioconvection flow of CNTs/water hybrid nanofluid mixed with microbes, relying on an artificial neural network for their analysis. Working fluids are less appropriate for processes requiring rapid and efficient heat transfer due to their fundamentally low thermal conductivity as compared with solids. The rapid growth of nanotechnology has resulted in nanofluids, which can carry heat over microscopic temperature gradients. A novel type of heat transmission liquids known as nanofluids was created by dispersing metallic or non-metallic nanopowders with 1 to 100 nm in the pedestal liquid, such as water, kerosene, ethylene, etc. After Chu [17] presented the perception of nanofluids, numerous researchers continued to investigate nanoscale fluids containing microbes. Hybrid nanofluids (HNFs) with two different types of Nps in a base fluid have better properties than standard Nfs that only contain one type of Np. HNFs have enhanced viscosity, better thermal and electrical conductivity, and other characteristics due to their complementing qualities. The enhancement of the heat transfer rate in a eigen oil based hybrid nanofluid flow over a extandable cylinder, utilizing the extended Yamada–Ota and Xue models is examined by Fatima et al. [26]. Noreen et al. [27] present a water-based ternary-hybrid nanofluid numerical analysis having homogeneous/heterogeneous reactions, incorporating thermal conductivity, over a wedge. Using a Taguchi-based sensitivity approach, Kumar et al. [31] analyzed the effect of linear boundary conditions with low magnetic field and chemical reactions on ternary nanofluids confined within a conical gap.

Bioconvection refers to the movement of microscopic organisms, like, algae and bacteria. The bioconvection flow is found in some essential applications of ecofriendly systems, fuel cells, biocells, pharmaceutical schemes, and so forth. Gyrotactic bacteria are essential for bioconvection because they organize group motions that have a major impact on ecological dynamics. The spatial distribution of microorganisms in aquatic environments is influenced by the transport of nutrients, which is facilitated by their capacity to navigate to nutrient-rich regions. The resulting bioconvection patterns promote the biological pump by permitting the uphill transfer of nutrients and organic materials. Additionally, the capacity of motile microorganisms to retort the light gradients progresses their perception and understanding of their surroundings. The study of these microbes provides important insights into bio-Microsystems, thermo-bioconvection, biofuels, microbial augmentation, and other bioengineering systems in addition to ecological implications. Das and Patgiri [32] discussed bioconvection flow in a non-Darcian regime. Iqbal et al. [33] considered the bioconvection flow of a nanofluid. Ahmed et al. Noreen et al. [38] studied the simulation of casson nanofluid flow carrying microbes over non-linear surface.

The inclusion of chemical reactions in the regime of flow fields emerges as a powerful way for controlling heat and mass transportation. In the medical and drug industries, this approach is applied in numerous applications, playing a substantial role in various critical processes. Jubair et al. [39] numerically analyzed the consequence of chemical reaction on HNF flow across an inclined plate. Venkateswarlu et al. Recently, a lot of attention has been paid to sensor surface by researchers due to its widespread usage in chemical innovations. The micro cantilever is an excellent illustration since it can be recycled to precisely feel a variety of harmful or bio-warfare proxies while depleting the capacity to distinguish a variety of diseases. Basha [47] conducted a study flow over a sensor surface on the dynamics of MHD nanofluid. A couple stress squeezed flow of NF is deliberated by Salahuddin and Awais concluded a sensor surface [48]. Riaz et al. [49] presented a study to discuss the flow of magnetized nanolubricant completed over a sensor surface. Salahuddin et al. [50] analyzed the flow of Maxwell NF across a sensor surface. The flow of HNF concluded a radiative sensor surface were presented by Ullah et al. [51]. In addition to biomedical and chemical sensing applications, sensor surfaces are also increasingly relevant to mining and mineral-processing operations, where real-time monitoring of slurry properties, chemically reactive extraction streams, tailings transport, and mine-water quality is essential. In such environments, coupled heat transfer, mass diffusion, magnetic effects, and reaction kinetics influence both sensing reliability and overall process efficiency.

This study presents a novel examination of squeezed bioconvective HNF flow a sensor surface, combined the results of chemical reactions, nanofluid hybridization, and bio-convection. Unlike previous studies that primarily focus on conventional nanofluids or single-species flows, the present work integrates multiple mechanisms, including squeezing effects, hybrid nanoparticle interactions, and chemical reaction dynamics, within a unified computational framework. The analysis captures the complex interplay between fluid mechanics, thermal transport, and bioconvection, while providing practical insights relevant to the design of advanced sensor surfaces and biomedical devices. This is the first study that comprehensively models such a coupled system, offering a deeper understanding of how hybrid nanofluids behave under squeezing flows with chemical reactions over functionalized surfaces.

Solution methodology

In this work, the governing equations describing the squeezed bioconvective HNF flow with chemical reaction (CR) over a sensor surface are changed into a non-dimensional set of differential equations using suitable transformations. The nonlinear operators are carefully constructed, and suitable initial guesses and auxiliary linear operators are chosen to guarantee convergence of the homotopy series. HAM offers a great deal of flexibility through the auxiliary parameters and convergence-control functions, enabling stable and quickly convergent series solutions. By comparing the limiting instances with before published results, the accurateness of the generated HAM solutions for velocity, temperature, nanoparticle concentration, and microbe density is confirmed. These solutions are analyzed under various governing factors.

4-Supervised neural networks technique

Machine Learning Algorithms (MLAs) have been developed to address real-world problems in various fields, including engineering, physics, and mathematics. MLAs are frequently classified into three main categories according on their training methods: Among these are Supervised Learning Algorithms (SLAs), which, like people, learn by practicing on known tasks or datasets. To ensure that the correct output is known in advance, SLAs train the model using labeled data. Internal parameters, sometimes called weights, are modified throughout the training phase based on how the model’s predictions and the intended results compare. The teaching signal that guides this weight alteration is typically a training sample or dataset.

The efficacy and computational efficiency of the supervised neural network trained using the back-propagated Levenberg–Marquardt technique (SNN-BLMS) are demonstrated by the training curves’ consistent and smooth convergence over all epochs. The graphical results and the continuously declining mean squared error (MSE) in Table 1 demonstrate the model’s improved performance over the training period. This consistent decrease in MSE demonstrates that the improved generalization capacity and higher prediction accuracy of the SNN-BLMS model are linked to lower error levels. Furthermore, the consistent and well-behaved convergence patterns show how resilient the training process is, ensuring reliable and optimal learning. Table 2 and 3 shows the strong correlation between higher precision and prediction quality and lower MSE values. The fitness evaluation charts in Fig. 2 and 3 provide a detailed comparison between the computed solutions and the reference data. The remarkable ability of the model to precisely represent and reproduce the fundamental behavior of the fluid dynamic system is confirmed by the close agreement between the two curves. This agreement supports the suggested neural network model’s structural soundness and predictive ability. For training, testing, and validation datasets, regression analysis shows the link between predicted and desired outcomes (Fig. 4). Conclusions

In this work, we have presented a computational study of bioconvective HNF in squeezed circumstances over a sensor surface, incorporating the influences of motile microorganisms. The governing PDEs are describing the fluid motion, nanoparticle concentration, heat transfer, and microorganism distribution were transformed to nonlinear ODEs through appropriate similarity transformations. To obtain reliable analytical approximations, HAM was used. This framework provides a robust mathematical foundation for examining the coupled transport processes within the squeezed configuration. The results have been shown in graphical and tabular forms. In addition to advanced sensor and biomedical technologies, the present framework is also applicable to mining and mineral-processing environments, where sensor-based analysis of hybrid-fluid transport can support monitoring of ore slurries, chemically reactive leaching systems, tailing streams, and mine-water treatment operations. In this sense, the study contributes to the mining priority as an enabling modeling tool for process sensing, transport analysis, and operational optimization. Limitations and future scope

The present model considers laminar squeezed flow, which may not capture turbulent or transient effects in practical systems. Unlike this, future research should extend the analysis to unsteady 3D flows with variable physical properties. Additionally, experimental validation of the predicted heat and mass transfer rates is recommended for real-world sensor applications.
