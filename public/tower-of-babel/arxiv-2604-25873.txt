Abstract
. We provide new quantitative results on the embedding of the Muckenhoupt class A ∞ A_{\infty} into A p A_{p} with the correct asymptotic behavior when the Fujii–Wilson constant [ w ] A ∞ [w]_{A_{\infty}} is close to 1, namely that the parameter p p goes to 1 when the weight is nearly constant. As intermediate steps towards the result, we obtain quantitative estimates on the weighted and unweighted BMO norms of log ⁡ w \log w for an A ∞ A_{\infty} weight w w . As a consequence, we show that a precise quantitative weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequality can be proved for weights with small [ w ] A ∞ [w]_{A_{\infty}} that recovers the classical Sobolev exponent p ∗ = n ​ p n − p p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p} when [ w ] A ∞ → 1 + [w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1^{+} .

1.
Introduction and Main Results

The purpose of this article is to provide new, improved quantitative estimates for the embedding of
A_{\infty}
weights into
A_{p}
classes. For
1<p<\infty
, the Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
classes of weights are defined by the condition

[w]_{A_{p}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)\,dy\right)\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)^{1-p^{\prime}}\,dy\right)^{p-1}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all the cubes
Q
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with sides parallel to the coordinate axes. The relevance of this class of weights comes from the very well-known fact that they characterize the weighted
L^{p}
boundedness of maximal and singular operators like the Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator and Calderón–Zygmund operators.
The limiting case of when
p=1
defines the class
A_{1}
; that is, the set of weights
w
such that

[w]_{A_{1}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(y)\,dy\right)\underset{Q}{\operatorname{ess}\sup}\left(w^{-1}\right)<+\infty.

At the other endpoint we have the
A_{\infty}
class defined as the union of
A_{p}
classes, namely
A_{\infty}:=\bigcup_{p\geq 1}A_{p}.

The membership in
A_{\infty}
can also be characterized in terms of the finiteness of the so-called
A_{\infty}
constant defined as

[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\right)\exp\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\log w^{-1}\right).

This
A_{\infty}
constant was originally introduced by Hruščev
[
Hru84
]
(see also
[
GCRdF85
]
). In recent years, a new definition was introduced that is more adequate to express quantitative norm inequalities for maximal and singular operators, namely the one defined as

[w]_{A_{\infty}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)\,dx,

where
M
stands for the Hardy–Littlewood maximal function,

Mf(x)=\sup_{Q\ni x}\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(y)|\,dy.

This is sometimes called the “Wilson constant”, as it was implicitly introduced by Wilson a long time ago (see
[
Wil87
,
Wil89
,
Wil08
]
) with a different notation, and was also implicitly introduced by Fujii
[
Fuj78
]
. Its explicit formulation appears in
[
HP13
]
. This constant is more relevant since it is known that
[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}<\infty\iff[w]_{A_{\infty}}<\infty
, but there are examples of weights
w\in A_{\infty}
so that
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
is much smaller than

[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}
(interesting examples can be found in
[
HP13
]
, where it was shown that
[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}
can be exponentially larger than
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
).

An easy result is that the
A_{p}
classes are increasing in
p
and
open
, in the sense that if
w\in A_{p}
with
p>1
then there exists an
\varepsilon>0
such that
w\in A_{p-\varepsilon}
, with an explicit control of the
A_{p-\varepsilon}
constant
[w]_{A_{p-\varepsilon}}
in terms of
[w]_{A_{p}}
. The relevant question here is to determine, given the weight
w\in A_{\infty}
with a certain constant
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
, what is the best possible
p\geq 1
such that
w\in A_{p}
. Moreover, it is interesting to find a quantitative connection between this optimal
p\geq 1
and the constant
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
.

Work in this direction can be found in
[
HP16
]
, where, as a consequence of their study of quantitative weighted Solyanik estimates, the authors prove that if
w
is an
A_{\infty}
weight in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, then
w\in A_{p}
for
p>e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}
with
[w]_{A_{p}}\leq e^{e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}
, where
c_{n}>1
is a dimensional constant. This seems to be the best available result in the literature on this question, but a simple observation is that both these estimates fall short of what they should be in the particular case of weights with
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
. Notice that since
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1
is equivalent to
w
being constant almost everywhere (see for instance
[
PR18
, Proposition 2.1]
), it makes sense to refer to this asymptotic analysis as the case of
flat weights
. The main contribution in this article is to provide a sharp asymptotic estimate for the
A_{\infty}\hookrightarrow A_{p}
embedding for flat weights. That is, denoting by
p([w]_{A_{\infty}})
the index such that
w\in A_{p([w]_{A_{\infty}})}
we show that
p([w]_{A_{\infty}})\to 1
when
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
, with explicit quantitative estimates for the
A_{p}
constant. The remarkable aspect is that we indeed need to restrict ourselves to weights with small
A_{\infty}
constants, a condition that seems to be common in these types of results. We do not know whether similar estimates can be proved for
all

A_{\infty}
weights.

We now state the main result of the paper.

Theorem 1.1
.

There exist dimensional constants
c_{n},\tau_{n},\tau_{n}^{\prime}>0
such that if
w\in A_{\infty}
with
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
then

w\in A_{p}\qquad\text{with}\qquad p=1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1),

and moreover,

[w]_{A_{p}}\leq\exp(\tau_{n}^{\prime}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)).

Remark 1.2
.

We observe that under the smallness condition
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
of the preceding theorem, the exponential upper bound can be controlled linearly. As a result, the quantities
p
and
[w]_{A_{p}}
exhibit the same asymptotic behavior, yielding the relation

p\simeq_{n}[w]_{A_{p}}\simeq_{n}1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1).

A key intermediate step towards the proof of Theorem
1.1
is an estimate on the BMO seminorm of
\log w
, where
w
is an
A_{\infty}
weight. The fact that
\log w
belongs to BMO when
w\in A_{\infty}
is not new; even some quantitative estimates are known. A nice result in this area was provided by Korey
[
Kor98
]
related to the
[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}
constant. Among other results, Korey proved that

\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq\log\left(2[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}\right).

There is also an asymptotic estimate of the form

\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq C\sqrt{\log\left([w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}\right)}\qquad\text{as}\qquad[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1.

Results like these were later used by Pattakos and Volberg in
[
PV12
]
to study the interesting problem of the continuity of norm estimates in weighted inequalities for Calderón–Zygmund operators.

In that direction, we provide here a sharper estimate for the BMO seminorm in terms of a mixed combination of the Hruščev and Fujii–Wilson
A_{\infty}
constants. This estimate should be compared with the result in
[
HP13
, Proposition 1.21]
.

Theorem 1.3
.

If
w\in A_{\infty}
, then
\log w\in\operatorname{BMO}
with

\left\|\log w\right\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq c_{n}\log(2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime})([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1),

for some dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
.

As a final but important contribution, we mention that the sharp embedding theorem for flat weights produces an interesting improvement of the recent Poincaré-Sobolev type inequalities obtained by the first author in
[
Cla25
]
.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
n\geq 2
and let
1\leq p<n
. Let
w\in A_{\infty}
and consider the exponent
p^{*}_{w}
defined by the relation

\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{p^{*}_{w}}=\frac{1}{n}\frac{1}{1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}.

There exists
c_{n,p}>0
such that if
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n,p}
, then we have

\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f-f_{Q}|^{p^{*}_{w}}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p^{*}_{w}}}\leq c_{n}p^{*}(1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1))^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every cube
Q
. Moreover, we have

\left\|f-f_{Q}\right\|_{L^{p^{*}_{w},p}\left(Q,\frac{w(x)dx}{w(Q)}\right)}\leq c_{n}p^{*}(1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1))^{\frac{1}{p}}\ell(Q)\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\nabla f|^{p}w\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

for every cube
Q
.

Although the previous result is restricted to weights with a small
A_{\infty}
constant, it has the remarkable consequence of asymptotically recovering the classical
Sobolev exponent when
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
. That is, when
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
, we have
p^{*}_{w}\to p^{*}=\frac{np}{n-p}
.

To put this result into perspective, it is worth noting that the weighted Poincaré inequality does not hold for the entire class
A_{\infty}
, as shown in
[
PR19
, Theorem 1.26]
.
Consequently, the Poincaré-Sobolev inequality does not hold either. In contrast, our result shows that if the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
is not too large, that is, if
w
is not a highly degenerate weight, then a weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequality can be established. In other words, the failure of a Poincaré inequality for the entire
A_{\infty}
class comes precisely from weights with large
A_{\infty}
constant, namely, the most singular weights in the class
A_{\infty}
.

Outline of the paper

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
we present a new result on doubling properties of
A_{\infty}
weights, which has the advantage of being asymptotically correct for flat weights. In Section
3
we present the proofs of the intermediate result in Theorem
1.3
. Finally, Section
4
contains the proof of our main contribution in Theorem
1.1
, while Section
5
details its consequences for Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities as presented in Theorem
1.4
.

2.
From
A_{\infty}
to doubling

We include in this section a result that can be seen as a byproduct of the approach to studying properties of flat weights. We show that if a weight
w
belongs to
A_{\infty}
, then the measure
w\,dx
is doubling. Moreover, we obtain an explicit estimate of the doubling constant in terms of the Fujii–Wilson
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
. The novelty here is that the doubling constant shows the correct asymptotic behavior for flat weights.

We will use the following reverse Hölder inequality from
[
PR18
]
, which is asymptotically sharp for flat weights. Let us mention that, in the case of
A_{1}
weights, the first sharp reverse Hölder inequality was proved by Kinnunen
[
Kin94
]
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
with Lebesgue measure, and later extended to general measures in
[
LPR17
]
.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
, then for each
0\leq\varepsilon\leq\frac{1}{2^{n+1}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}
,

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{1+\varepsilon}\leq 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\right)^{1+\varepsilon}

for every cube
Q
.

Remark 2.2
.

The previous result is not exactly the same as the one stated in
[
PR18
]
. Here we slightly weaken the range of admissible
\varepsilon
, but in exchange we obtain a much more manageable constant, namely
2[w]_{A_{\infty}}
, in the right-hand side.

As a consequence, using the standard techniques, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.3
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
, then for each cube
Q
and each measurable subset
E\subset Q
, we have

\frac{w(E)}{w(Q)}\leq 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(\frac{|E|}{|Q|}\right)^{\frac{1}{1+2^{n+1}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}}.

From Theorem
2.1
we also obtain a left-openness property of the
A_{p}
classes. More precisely, given
w\in A_{p}
, we deduce that
w\in A_{p-\varepsilon}
for some
\varepsilon>0
depending on
[\sigma]_{A_{\infty}}
, where
\sigma:=w^{1-p^{\prime}}
is the dual weight of
w
. Compared with the result in
[
HPR12
]
, the value
p-\varepsilon
that we obtain here is slightly smaller, but on the other hand, the constant is controlled by a mixed-type constant. Furthermore, this estimate captures the correct asymptotic behavior for flat weights, as we have
p-\varepsilon\to 1
when
[\sigma]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
w\in A_{p}
for some
1<p<\infty
. If we consider

(2.1)

\varepsilon=\frac{p-1}{2^{n+1}([\sigma]_{A_{\infty}}-1)+1},

then
w\in A_{p-\varepsilon}
with

[w]_{A_{p-\varepsilon}}\leq(2[\sigma]_{A_{\infty}})^{p-1}[w]_{A_{p}}.

We next show that if a weight belongs to
A_{\infty}
, then the associated measure
w\,dx
is doubling, and we provide an explicit estimate of the doubling constant in terms of the Fujii–Wilson
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
. To the best of our knowledge, this type of quantitative result has not appeared in the literature before. Related estimates have been obtained using Hruščev’s exponential
A_{\infty}
constant (see, for instance,
[
Gra14
]
).

Theorem 2.5
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
, then the measure
w\,dx
is doubling, i.e.,

w(2Q)\leq D_{w}w(Q)

for every cube
Q
, where

D_{w}\leq\exp\left(\kappa\,n(4[w]_{A_{\infty}})^{1+2^{n+1}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}\right),

and
\kappa
is a dimension-free constant.

Remark 2.6
.

Using the same argument together with the reverse Hölder inequality from
[
HPR12
]
produces the bound
D_{w}\lesssim\exp(\exp(c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}))
.

Proof.

Let
Q
be a cube and let
\lambda>1
. Applying Corollary
2.3
to the cube
\lambda Q
and the subset
E=\lambda Q\setminus Q
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{w(\lambda Q\setminus Q)}{w(\lambda Q)}\leq

\displaystyle 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(\frac{|\lambda Q\setminus Q|}{|\lambda Q|}\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(1-\frac{|Q|}{|\lambda Q|}\right)^{\theta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(1-\lambda^{-n}\right)^{\theta},

where
\theta=\frac{1}{1+2^{n+1}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}\in(0,1)
. Hence,

\displaystyle w(\lambda Q)=

\displaystyle w(Q)+w(\lambda Q\setminus Q)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle w(Q)+2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(1-\lambda^{-n}\right)^{\theta}w(\lambda Q).

We now choose
\lambda>1
such that

2[w]_{A_{\infty}}\left(1-\lambda^{-n}\right)^{\theta}\leq\frac{1}{2},

and consequently,

w(\lambda Q)\leq 2w(Q).

The previous inequality holds for any
1<\lambda\leq\lambda_{0}
, where

\lambda_{0}=\frac{1}{\left(1-\left(4[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\right)^{\frac{1}{n}}}.

Let
m\in N
be the smallest integer such that
\lambda_{0}^{m}\geq 2
. Then, we have

w(2Q)\leq w(\lambda_{0}^{m}Q)\leq 2^{m}w(Q).

We observe that
m\simeq\frac{\log 2}{\log\lambda_{0}}
, so it remains to estimate
\log\lambda_{0}
. Since

\log\lambda_{0}=-\frac{1}{n}\log(1-s)

where
s=(4[w]_{A_{\infty}})^{-\frac{1}{\theta}}\in(0,\frac{1}{4}]
, we can use the elementary inequality

s\leq-\log(1-s)\leq\frac{s}{1-s}\leq 2s,

which gives
\log\lambda_{0}\simeq\frac{s}{n}
. Therefore,

\displaystyle D_{w}\leq

\displaystyle 2^{m}\simeq 2^{\frac{\log 2}{\log\lambda_{0}}}\simeq\exp\left(\frac{\log 2}{\log_{2}e}\frac{n}{s}\right)=\exp\left(\kappa\,n\left(4[w]_{A_{\infty}}\right)^{1+2^{n+1}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}\right).

∎

Remark 2.7
.

In the case of constant weights (
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1
), the doubling constant obtained above depends exponentially on the dimension, which is expected. Moreover, we observe that not only for constant weights, but also for flat weights of the form
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1+\delta
with
\delta\to 0
, we observe the correct asymptotic behavior
D_{w}\sim e^{4\kappa n}
.

3.
Logarithm of an
A_{\infty}
weight

In this section, we prove that if
w\in A_{\infty}
, then
\log w\in\operatorname{BMO}
. Moreover, we provide an explicit bound for the
\operatorname{BMO}
seminorm of
\log w
in terms of the
A_{\infty}
constant of
w
. For this purpose, we introduce the following logarithmic
A_{\infty}
constant to be used throughout the section,

[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(1+\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)\right)w(x)\,dx,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
, and
w_{Q}=\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w(x)dx
.

Remark 3.1
.

It is shown in
[
HP13
]
that
[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}\leq 2^{n+1}[w]_{A_{\infty}}
. In addition, by
[
Ste69
]
we have the reverse inequality,

\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)dx\leq 3^{n}\int_{Q}\left(1+\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)\right)w(x)\,dx

for each cube
Q
. In conclusion, we have
[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}\simeq_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}
.

As a first step, we show that
\log w
belongs to the weighted
\operatorname{BMO}
space,
\mathrm{BMO}_{w}
. This space, which is usually denoted as
\mathrm{BMO}_{w,w}
in the literature (see, for instance,
[
OPRRR20
]
), is equipped with the seminorm

\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}:=\sup_{Q}\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q,w}|w(x)\,dx,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q
, and
f_{Q,w}=\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}f(x)w(x)\,dx
is the weighted average of
f
on the cube
Q
.

Proposition 3.2
.

If
w\in A_{\infty}
, then
\log w\in\operatorname{BMO}_{w}
and

(3.1)

\left\|\log w\right\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}\leq 8([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1).

Proof.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
and let
Q
be a cube. Since
\log w-(\log w)_{Q,w}
has zero integral on the cube
Q
with respect to the weighted measure
w\,dx
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\log w-(\log w)_{Q,w}|w=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(\log w-(\log w)_{Q,w}\right)^{+}w

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(\log w-\log w_{Q}\right)^{+}w

\displaystyle+2\left(\log w_{Q}-(\log w)_{Q,w}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle I_{1}+I_{2}.

where
a^{+}=\max(a,0)
. We can easily estimate the first term

\displaystyle I_{1}=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(1+\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)\right)w(x)\,dx-1\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1).

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(\log w)_{Q,w}-\log w_{Q}=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}(\log w(x)-\log w_{Q})w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}(\log w(x)-\log w_{Q})^{+}w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}(\log w_{Q}-\log w(x))^{+}w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle P-N.

We estimate
P
and
N
separately. The bound for
P
is analogous to that for
I_{1}
,

\displaystyle P=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(1+\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)\right)w(x)\,dx-1

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1.

To estimate
N
, we use the elementary inequality
\log t\leq t-1
,

\displaystyle N=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(\log\left(\frac{w_{Q}}{w(x)}\right)\right)^{+}w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{\{w\leq w_{Q}\}}\left(\frac{w_{Q}}{w(x)}-1\right)w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(w_{Q}-w(x)\right)^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|w(x)-w_{Q}|\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}(w(x)-w_{Q})^{+}\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{\{w\geq w_{Q}\}}w_{Q}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}-1\right)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{\{w\geq w_{Q}\}}w_{Q}\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\log\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)\,dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)w(x)\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1),

where in the third inequality we use
t-1\leq t\log t
for
t\geq 1
. Therefore,

\displaystyle I_{2}=

\displaystyle 2\left(\log w_{Q}-(\log w)_{Q,w}\right)^{+}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|\log w_{Q}-(\log w)_{Q,w}|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2|(\log w)_{Q,w}-\log w_{Q}|

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2|P-N|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2P+2N

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1)+4([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 6([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1).

Combining the previous estimates, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\log w-(\log w)_{Q,w}|w\leq 8([w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1).

for every cube
Q
. Taking the supremum over all cubes
Q
completes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.3
.

A closer inspection of the argument shows that we have, in fact, proved a stronger statement. For each cube
Q
, we have

\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|\log w(x)-(\log w)_{Q,w}|w(x)\,dx\leq\frac{8}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)w(x)\,dx.

The estimate obtained above has the correct asymptotic behavior for flat weights with respect to the logarithmic
A_{\infty}
constant
[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}
, in the sense that as
[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}\to 1
, both sides of the inequality go to zero. However, if we simply used Lemma 6.2 from
[
HP13
]
, which states that
[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}\leq 2^{n+1}[w]_{A_{\infty}}
, we would lose this property. Indeed, such a bound would yield
\left\|\log w\right\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}\leq c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}-1
, which clearly does not vanish as
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
. Therefore, we need to refine this result to obtain an inequality that preserves the correct asymptotic behavior for flat weights.

Lemma 3.4
.

For any cube
Q
and any measurable function
w
, we have

\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w(x)}{w_{Q}}\right)w(x)\,dx\leq 2^{n}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)\,dx-1\right).

Hence, if
w\in A_{\infty}
,

(3.2)

[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\log}-1\leq 2^{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1).

Proof.

The estimate follows from the reverse weak type (1,1) inequality: if
w
is nonnegative and

\frac{1}{t}\int_{\{x\in Q:w(x)>t\}}w\,dx\leq 2^{n}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|.

By the layer-cake formula applied to
s\log^{+}(\tfrac{s}{w_{Q}})
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w}{w_{Q}}\right)w=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{w_{Q}}^{\infty}\frac{1}{t}\left(\int_{\{x\in Q:w(x)>t\}}w\,dx\right)\,dt

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{w_{Q}}^{\infty}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{\infty}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle-\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{w_{Q}}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})(x)\,dx

\displaystyle-\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{w_{Q}}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|\,dt.

We note that since
w_{Q}\leq M(w\chi_{Q})(x)
for each
x\in Q
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{w_{Q}}

\displaystyle\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>t\}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}\int_{0}^{w_{Q}}\left|\{x\in Q:M(w\chi_{Q})(x)>w_{Q}\}\right|\,dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{2^{n}}{w(Q)}|Q|\int_{0}^{w_{Q}}dt

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}\frac{|Q|}{w(Q)}w_{Q}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2^{n}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\left(1+\log^{+}\left(\frac{w}{w_{Q}}\right)\right)w\,dx-1=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\log^{+}\left(\frac{w}{w_{Q}}\right)w\,dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle 2^{n}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}M(w\chi_{Q})\,dx-1\right),

and we conclude the proof by taking the supremum over
Q
.
∎

Theorem 3.6

There exists a dimensional constant
c_{n}>0

\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq c_{n}\log(2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime})\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}},

Fix a cube

Next, by the characterization of

On the other hand, by the definition of the Luxemburg norm, any measurable function

\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}\exp\left(\frac{|h(x)|}{\|h\|_{\exp L(Q,w)}}\right)w(x)\,dx\leq 2.

We now apply these two facts with

g(x)=\frac{|f(x)-f_{Q,w}|}{\|f-f_{Q,w}\|_{\exp L(Q,w)}},

and
h(x)=f(x)-f_{Q,w}

\exp\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\frac{|f(x)-f_{Q,w}|}{\|f-f_{Q,w}\|_{\exp L(Q,w)}}\,dx\right)\leq 2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime}.

Taking logarithms gives

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q,w}|\,dx\leq\log(2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime})\,\|f-f_{Q,w}\|_{\exp L(Q,w)}.

Finally, we apply the John–Nirenberg theorem for non-doubling measures due to Mateu, Mattila, Nicolau, and Orobitg
[
MMNO00

1

. It follows that there exists a dimensional constant

Combining the previous estimates, we conclude that

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|\,dx\leq 2c_{n}\log(2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime})\,\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}.

Taking the supremum over all cubes

4.
Proof of Theorem
1.1
and its consequences

Using the results established in the previous section, we now proceed with the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Our approach follows the technique of
[
Pol95
]
(see also
[
Kor98
,
Mit05
]
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The goal is to obtain control on the
A_{p}
condition for
w\in A_{\infty}
by assuming some quantitative control on
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
. Let’s start by considering
r\geq 1
to be chosen later and connect the
A_{p}
condition
with previous estimates on the BMO norm of
\log w
.

Let
Q
be a cube. By Jensen’s inequality and the elementary inequality
\exp(t)\leq\exp(|t|)
we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{r\log w}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{-r\log w}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{r(\log w-(\log w)_{Q})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{-r(\log w-(\log w)_{Q})}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{|r||\log w-(\log w)_{Q}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{|-r||\log w-(\log w)_{Q}|}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}e^{r|\log w-(\log w)_{Q}|}\right)^{\frac{2}{r}}.

This last quantity is exactly what we know how to control by using the BMO estimates for
\log w
. More precisely, by Theorem
1.3
we have
\log w\in\operatorname{BMO}
, and by John-Nirenberg inequality we can obtain that

|\{x\in Q:|\log w(x)-(\log w)_{Q}|>t\}|\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha_{n}t}{\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}}\right)|Q|.

Hence,

(4.1)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}\exp\left(r|\log w(x)-(\log w)_{Q}|\right)\,dx\leq 3,

where

r=\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}}.

We use estimate in (
4.1
) to control the last integral in our previous estimate to obtain

(4.2)

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{-r}\right)^{\frac{1}{r}}\leq 3^{\frac{2}{r}}.

Two things remain to be proved: on one hand, we need to show that
r
above can be chosen such that
r\geq 1
. This will be a consequence of the estimates on
\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}
. On the other hand, we need to exploit this result to obtain an explicit expression for
p([w]_{A_{\infty}})
, the best possible index for which
w\in A_{p}
.

For the first one, we simply need to restrict to weights with small constant,
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1+\delta
with
\delta
small enough such that

\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}
. To that end, recall that we have the estimate from Theorem
1.3
, namely

\left\|\log w\right\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq c_{n}\log(2[w]_{A_{\infty}}^{\prime})([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1).

Since our standing assumption is that
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1+\delta
with a small
\delta
, we may assume that
[w]_{A_{\infty}}
is bounded by, let’s say, 2. From that, we want to obtain that
[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}
is controlled as well. This can be found in
[
BR14
, Theorem 1.3]
, where the inequality

[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}\leq C\frac{e^{e^{[w]_{A_{\infty}}}+1}}{e^{[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}

is proved in dimension 1. For the higher dimensional case in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,
n\geq 2
, the result is from
[
HP16
, Corollary 5.6]
where the inequality

[w]^{\prime}_{A_{\infty}}\leq e^{e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}

has been proved. Hence, under the assumption
[w]_{A_{\infty}}=1+\delta
with
\delta\leq 1
, we have

\left\|\log w\right\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq C_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1).

We now choose
\delta\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2C_{n}}
to get the desired estimate
\|\log w\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq\frac{\alpha_{n}}{2}
.

To finish with the proof we now consider
p-1=\frac{1}{r}
, i.e.
p=1+\frac{1}{r}
. This yields

\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w\left(\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}w^{1-p^{\prime}}\right)^{p-1}\leq 3^{2(p-1)}.

We have proved
[w]_{A_{p}}\leq 3^{2(p-1)}
with
p=1+\frac{2C_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)
. Using this expression for
p
, we have

[w]_{A_{p}}\leq 3^{2(p-1)}=3^{\frac{4C_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)},

which is the desired estimate with
\tau_{n}=\frac{2C_{n}}{\alpha_{n}}
and
\tau_{n}^{\prime}=2\log 3\,\tau_{n}
. This concludes the proof.

∎

The only result of this type, a quantitative embedding of
A_{\infty}
into some
A_{p}
class, that we are aware of in the literature is the following theorem from
[
HP16
]
.

Theorem 4.1
.

If
w\in A_{\infty}
then
w\in A_{p}
with
p=e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}
and
[w]_{A_{p}}\leq e^{e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}
for some dimensional constant

.

Remark 4.2
.

We note that the corresponding result in
[
HP16
]
is originally formulated with the strict inequality
p>e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}
. However, one can readily obtain the statement with equality,
p=e^{c_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}
, by replacing

with a slightly larger dimensional constant.

Our result improves this estimate in the case
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
, yielding the correct asymptotic behavior for flat weights. That is, when
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\to 1
we have
p\to 1
and
[w]_{A_{p}}\to 1
. Putting together our result with the embedding of
[
HP16
]
, we obtain the following quantitative general embedding.

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
w\in A_{\infty}
. Then
w\in A_{p}
with

p=\begin{cases}1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)&\text{ if }[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n},\\
e^{C_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}&\text{ if }[w]_{A_{\infty}}>1+c_{n}.\end{cases}

for some dimensional constant
c_{n}>0
. In addition,

[w]_{A_{p}}\leq\begin{cases}e^{\tau_{n}^{\prime}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)}&\text{ if }[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n},\\
e^{e^{C_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}}}&\text{ if }[w]_{A_{\infty}}>1+c_{n}.\end{cases}

Remark 4.4
.

The restriction
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
is natural in our approach. It is required to guarantee that the integrability exponent
r
obtained from the John-Nirenberg inequality is large enough (specifically,
r\geq 1
), which is a necessary condition for our estimates. However, one may conjecture that even for large
A_{\infty}
constants, the embedding could still hold with
p
of the order
C_{n}[w]_{A_{\infty}}
.

Corollary 4.5
.

There exist dimensional constants
c_{n},C_{n}>0
such that if
w\in A_{\infty}
with
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
, then

\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}}\leq C_{n}\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}.

Proof.

Let
c_{n},\tau_{n}
the dimensional constants given by Theorem
1.1
, hence if
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
we have
w\in A_{p}
with
p=1+\tau_{n}([w]_{A_{\infty}}-1)
, and
[w]_{A_{p}}\leq C_{n}
. We observe that since
[w]_{A_{\infty}}\leq 1+c_{n}
then
D_{w}\leq e^{e^{c_{n}}}
(see Remark
2.6
). Let
Q
be a cube, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{|Q|}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|dx\leq

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}\left(\frac{1}{w(Q)}\int_{Q}|f(x)-f_{Q}|^{p}w(x)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle[w]_{A_{p}}^{\frac{1}{p}}C_{n}D_{w}p\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C_{n}\|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}_{w}},

where in the second inequality we use
[
MPRRR23
, Theorem 1.1]
, and in the last inequality we use that

,
[w]_{A_{p}}
and

are bounded by a dimensional constant.
∎

5.
Applications to weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities

In this section, we present some applications to weighted Poincaré-Sobolev inequalities, motivated by
[

. The first one is a direct consequence of the open property of Muckenhoupt weights as presented in Corollary

The result from
[

]
is the following.

Theorem 5.1

(
[

]

Let

Theorem 5.2

Under the same hypothesis as in Theorem
