A Definitive Determination of the Interstellar Carbon Abundance toward ρ \rho Ophiuchi A and B Facilities: HST(STIS) ISMOD ( Y. Sheffer et al. 2008 )
Adam M. Ritchey, S. R. Federman, Daniel E. Welty, Adolf N. Witt

Abstract
We present the results of an effort to derive interstellar gas-phase C II abundances along the lines of sight toward ρ \rho Oph A and B. Our analysis is based on high-resolution NUV and FUV archival spectra acquired with the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph on the Hubble Space Telescope. Column densities of C II are derived both from fits to the weak C II] λ ​ 2325 \lambda 2325 intersystem transition and from fits to the damping wings of the strong C II λ ​ 1334 \lambda 1334 line. We find that the results from the weak-line and strong-line determinations agree with each other remarkably well for both sight lines, demonstrating the reliability of the f f -values of the C II transitions. Furthermore, the gas-phase C abundance that we obtain for ρ \rho Oph A is in very good agreement with previous determinations of interstellar C abundances from measurements of the weak C II] λ ​ 2325 \lambda 2325 line. By demonstrating the reliability of the damping wing fitting technique for the C II λ ​ 1334 \lambda 1334 line, our analysis opens the door to future surveys of interstellar C abundances using the same methodology.

I
Introduction

The gas-phase interstellar carbon abundance is a crucial parameter that bears on the ionization balance in neutral diffuse interstellar clouds and on the abundance of carbonaceous dust grains in the interstellar medium (ISM). Determinations of interstellar C II abundances (C
+
being the dominant ionization state of C in diffuse clouds) have typically followed one of three approaches. The most secure determinations are derived from observations of the weak C II]
\lambda 2325
intersystem transition in absorption against background stars
(
J. A. Cardelli et al. 1996
;
U. J. Sofia et al. 2004
)
. This line is optically thin along diffuse and translucent sight lines so that the column density is directly proportional to the equivalent width. However, the line is typically extremely weak (with equivalent widths of
\sim
1
-
2 mÅ or less) and therefore requires high resolution, very high signal-to-noise (S/N) ratio NUV spectra to be detected. Another method for deriving C II abundances involves fitting the damping wings of the strong, typically heavily saturated C II
\lambda 1334
transition
(
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
;
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012
)
. However, this approach can yield unreliable results if the underlying continuum is poorly defined or if there are low column density C II absorption components at moderately high velocity that obscure the Lorentzian damping wings of the main low-velocity interstellar C II component. A third approach involves examining absorption or emission from the forbidden [C II] 158
\mu
m line
(
E. Dwek et al. 1997
;
M. Gerin et al. 2015
;
C. J. R. Clark et al. 2025
)
. In this case, uncertainties arise from the assumptions being made about the excitation conditions in the interstellar gas and from attempts to disentangle absorption and emission along complicated lines of sight.

Prior to 2011, most measurements of interstellar C abundances were derived using the weak intersystem transition at 2325.4 Å. With the exception of a few outliers, these earlier measurements are generally consistent with an average gas-phase carbon abundance of C/H
\sim

1.6\times 10^{-4}

(
U. J. Sofia et al. 2004
, e.g.,)
. However, the total number of sight lines with secure detections of the weak C II]
\lambda 2325
feature is small (
\sim
13) and the prospects for expanding the sample are limited by the need for high-resolution, very high S/N ratio NUV spectra, which generally require long exposure times with the only instrument currently capable of obtaining such data, the Space Telescope Imaging Spectrograph (STIS) on the Hubble Space Telescope (HST). Thus,
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
explored using the strong C II
\lambda 1334
transition to derive C abundances via fits to the Lorentzian damping wings.
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
reported C II column densities (using their strong-line method) for the same six sight lines that
U. J. Sofia et al. 2004
had analyzed via the weak intersystem line. Unfortunately, the results did not agree, with the strong-line determinations yielding column densities that were
\sim
40% smaller than those from the weak-line measurements. Based on these results,
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
suggested that the oscillator strength of the intersystem transition was in need of revision.

In this Letter, we revisit the comparison between column densities of C II derived from fits to the damping wings of the allowed C II
\lambda 1334
transition and from profile fits to the weak, semi-forbidden C II]
\lambda 2325
line.

Our analysis is based on high-resolution FUV and NUV HST/STIS spectra of
\rho

\rho

II
Observations and Data Processing

HST/STIS observations of
\rho
Oph A and B were obtained in 2015 under program GO 13365 (PI: Martin Cordiner). The observations employed both the E140H grating, with the wavelength setting at 1271 Å, and the E230H grating, with the wavelength setting at 2263 Å, allowing coverage of both C II transitions at high-resolution (
R\sim 120,000
). The sight lines to
\rho
Oph A and B were chosen for this study because (1) the component structure along the two lines of sight is relatively simple, with
\sim
90% of the column density in the ‘main’ low-velocity component (as determined from the weak O I
\lambda 1355
line), (2) the total hydrogen column density is quite large (
10^{21.7}
cm
-2
toward
\rho
Oph A), meaning that the weak C II
\lambda 2325
line is easily detectable toward both
\rho
Oph A and
\rho
Oph B, and (3) the stars are part of the same binary system, which means we can compare column densities from the strong and weak C II transitions for two closely spaced sight lines as an additional check on the accuracy of our results.

The pipeline-calibrated STIS spectra were retrieved from the Mikulski Archive for Space Telescopes (MAST). An IDL procedure was used to coadd multiple exposures of a given target obtained with the same echelle grating and to combine adjacent echelle orders into a single FUV or NUV spectrum for each sight line.
2
2

2

The IDL procedure (stack_echelle_fits) was written by Edward B. Jenkins.
When combining multiple overlapping spectra, the procedure weights the individual flux values by the inverse square of the associated uncertainties. It is particularly important to properly combine the overlapping portions of the echelle orders when analyzing the strong C II
\lambda 1334
line, which typically appears in two adjacent orders of the E140H grating. As described below, our analysis of the C II
\lambda 2325
feature utilizes the O I
\lambda 1355
line as a profile template. The O I
\lambda 1355
line also appears in two adjacent orders of the E140H grating, which are near the edge of the spectral format when the wavelength setting at 1271 Å is chosen. The standard CALSTIS pipeline procedure extracts only one of the orders containing O I
\lambda 1355
. Thus, to improve the overall S/N ratio in this line, we manually extracted both orders containing O I
\lambda 1355
from the calibrated 2D spectral images obtained from MAST, following the procedure described in
A. M. Ritchey et al. 2011
, and then coadded the orders to produce final O I
\lambda 1355
spectra.

III.2
Weak-line Measurements

Our analysis of the C II
\lambda 2325

\rho
Oph A and
\rho
Oph B followed the methodology we have adopted in previous analyses of weak interstellar absorption lines
(
A. M. Ritchey et al. 2011
;
A. M. Ritchey et al. 2018
)
. Small segments of spectra (
\sim
2 Å wide) centered on the C II
\lambda 2325
(and O I
\lambda 1355
) lines were isolated and then continuum-normalized by fitting low-order polynomials to regions free of interstellar absorption. The S/N ratio (per pixel) near the C II
\lambda 2325
lines is
\sim
90 for both sight lines. As anticipated, the C II
\lambda 2325
(and O I
\lambda 1355

\lambda 2325

Y. Sheffer et al. 2008

III.3
Strong-line Determinations

We chose the sight lines to
\rho
Oph A and B for our analysis, not only because the weak C II
\lambda 2325
lines are unambiguously detected, but also because the Lorentzian damping wings in the strong C II
\lambda 1334
transition are clearly visible in both directions. A major uncertainty in deriving C II column densities from fits to the damping wings of the
\lambda 1334
transition is the shape of the underlying continuum onto which the interstellar absorption features are superimposed. In the case of
\rho
Oph A and B (which have spectral types B1 V and B2 V), the interstellar C II
\lambda 1334
lines (along with the nearby interstellar C II*
\lambda 1335
features) are superimposed onto very broad stellar C II absorption lines. (The FWHM of the stellar C II line is
\sim
500 km s
-1
for both stars.) Previous efforts to derive C II abundances from fits to the damping wings
(
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
;
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012
)
employed synthetic stellar models to fit the continuum near the interstellar C II lines. While such an approach may be appealing from a theoretical standpoint, the problem is that the synthetic spectrum invariably does not provide a perfect match to the actual observed spectrum. The continuum fitting procedure adopted in
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
, for example, required seven free parameters to fine-tune the modelled continuum, and even then there are cases where the synthetic spectrum does not appear to match the observed spectrum particularly well.

For our analysis, we adopt a more direct approach to fitting the continuum. First, we isolate the interstellar C II
\lambda 1334
line within a small spectral window with a width of 2 Å. (A width of 2 Å at 1334.5 Å corresponds to a velocity width of 450 km s
-1
.) We then fit a low-order polynomial to regions very far from the line and well outside the obvious damping wings. (Essentially, we choose continuum points at the very edges of the spectral window.) For both sight lines, we found that a first-order cubic spline function provided a good representation of the underlying continuum. Our continuum fits for the C II
\lambda 1334
lines toward
\rho
Oph A and B are shown in Figure
2
. We also explored how changes in the order of the polynomial, and differences in the placement of the continuum points, affected the derived column densities. These alternate continuum fits are also shown in Figure
2
. For
\rho
Oph A, the alterations in the fitting parameters did not significantly change the resulting continuum fit. For
\rho
Oph B, however, subtle differences in the structure of the noise in the continuum regions led to more significant variations in the alternate continuum fits.

Once the data were continuum-normalized, we used our multi-component Voigt profile fitting routine ISMOD to fit the C II
\lambda 1334
absorption lines.
4
4

4

We make no attempt to fit the C II*
\lambda 1335
absorption features. The column density of C II in the excited fine-structure level is generally too small to create significant damping wings (i.e., the lines are on the flat portion of the curve of growth). Any attempt to derive column densities from these features would be accompanied by exceptionally large uncertainties.
For the
\rho
Oph A and B sight lines, the damping wings are dominated by a single low-velocity absorption component (i.e., the ‘main’ component seen in the O I
\lambda 1355
profile). However, excess absorption on the blue side of the C II profile requires that additional low column density components be included in the fit. The component structure cannot be directly evaluated using the heavily saturated C II profile. Thus, we used a combination of the O I
\lambda 1355
line, which probes the highest column density components, and the S II
\lambda 1250
line, which is useful for examining the relative strengths of the lower column density components at higher velocity. An initial prediction for the component structure in the C II line was obtained from the results of ISMOD fits to the O I and S II profiles (adjusting for differences in the cosmic abundances of the elements and in the expected amount of dust depletion).

Ultimately, we fit the C II
\lambda 1334
absorption profiles with six components, the two prominent components seen in the O I
\lambda 1355
line, and four additional components at
-30.7
km s
-1
,
-26.8
km s
-1
,
-18.8
km s
-1
, and +0.0 km s
-1
. In these fits, we held the relative velocities among the components fixed, but all other parameters were permitted to vary. We stress that the total C II column density obtained with this method is almost entirely determined by the strength of the main absorption component. The inclusion of the additional velocity components is necessary in order to properly fit the observed spectrum. However, the contribution from those components to the total C II column density is minimal (i.e.,
\sim
13%, which is less than the associated uncertainties). Since the component structure cannot be determined directly from the saturated C II profile, the individual column densities of those minor additional components are not well constrained in the fit. However, this has very little impact on the outcome for the total C II column density.

Our Voigt profile fits to the C II
\lambda 1334
lines toward
\rho
Oph A and B are presented in Figure
3
. The total C II column densities derived from these fits are given in Table
2
. There are two major sources of uncertainty in the column densities derived from the strong C II transition. First, there are the statistical uncertainties in the measured intensities that affect the Voigt profile fit. The quality of the fit may be judged by the degree to which the damping wings from the synthetic absorption profile match the observed spectrum. To examine the effect of statistical uncertainties, we increased and decreased the total column density of the synthetic profile to match the fluctuations in the intensity points in the wings of the profile. In particular, the wing regions closest to the core of the line are the most important in the fitting process. Small changes in the synthetic profile in these regions lead to large variations in the fit residuals. For both sight lines, we found that the allowed range in column density due to statistical uncertainties corresponded to
\pm 0.06
dex. (This range in column density is indicated by the dotted red lines in Figure
3
.)

In addition to the statistical uncertainties, there are systematic effects related to the placement of the continuum. This is particularly important for strong-line determinations where the strength of the damping wings can be greatly impacted by the choice of continuum. For each of the alternate continuum fits described above (see Figure
2
), we employed the same Voigt profile fitting procedure as for our nominal continuum fit to derive the total C II column density. We then evaluated the standard deviation of the resulting values. For
\rho
Oph A, the range of plausible continuum fits was small and the resulting spread in the derived column densities was
\pm 0.02
dex. For
\rho
Oph B, the variation in continuum fits was larger and the spread in column densities corresponded to
\pm 0.06
dex. These systematic uncertainties were added in quadrature with the statistical uncertainties to arrive at the values given in Table
2
.

IV
Discussion and Conclusions

The main conclusion from our analysis is that the C II column densities derived from the weak C II
\lambda 2325
line and the strong C II
\lambda 1334
feature agree with each other remarkably well for both sight lines toward
\rho
Oph A and B (Table
2
). Furthermore, the column densities we find toward
\rho
Oph A are indistinguishable from those toward
\rho
Oph B, as would be expected given the binary nature of the targets and the similarity in the absorption profiles. We also note that the uncertainties associated with the strong-line determinations are smaller than those from the weak-line measurements, indicating that, in cases where the damping wings are clearly visible, Voigt profile fitting of the C II
\lambda 1334
feature can be a reliable method for deriving C II abundances.

Taking the weighted mean of the results from the strong-line and weak-line determinations, we find total C II column densities of
\log N(
C II
)=17.95\pm 0.06
for
\rho
Oph A and
17.94\pm 0.07
for
\rho
Oph B.

5

\lambda 1335

\sim
6% or less for both sight lines. This would correspond to a logarithmic increase in
\log N(

)
of
\lesssim 0.03
dex.

\log N(

\rho

221\pm 102
parts per million of H (ppm).

The gas-phase C abundance we derive toward
\rho
Oph A is entirely consistent with previous determinations of C abundances derived from analyses of the weak intersystem transition
(e.g.,
U. J. Sofia et al. 2004
, and references therein)

(
D. E. Welty et al. 1999
)

<
C/H
>
=
(1.70\pm 0.14)\times 10^{-4}
. However, our results are not consistent with more recent attempts to obtain C II abundances from the strong
\lambda 1334
transition
(
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
;
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012
)
.
U. J. Sofia et al. 2011
obtained C II column densities that were consistently below those derived from the weak transition measured along the same lines of sight, leading those authors to suggest that the
f
-value of the intersystem line was in need of revision. We find nearly identical C II column densities from analyses of the
\lambda 1334
and
\lambda 2325
transitions toward
\rho
Oph A and B, demonstrating that there is no tension between the
f
-values of these transitions. Moreover, as shown in Table
1
, there is consensus among theoretical and experimental efforts that the oscillator strengths of the C II transitions are secure.

The C II column densities derived by
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012

In many directions, there are weaker C II absorption components at higher velocities that tend to obscure the damping wings from the main low-velocity component. This appears to be the case for many of the sight lines analyzed by
V. S. Parvathi et al. 2012

\lambda 1334
line can be a reliable method for obtaining gas-phase C abundances, the sight lines must be carefully chosen to avoid these uncertainties.

In summary, we examined high-resolution NUV and FUV HST/STIS spectra for the sight lines toward
\rho
Oph A and B in an effort to derive C II abundances from both the weak C II
\lambda 2325
intersystem transition and the strongly saturated C II
\lambda 1334
line. Our main findings are that the C II column densities derived from the two transitions agree with each other remarkably well for both sight lines, demonstrating that the oscillator strengths of the two C II transitions are reliable and well established. The C abundance obtained for the line of sight to
\rho
Oph A is consistent with many previous determinations of C abundances derived from the weak intersystem transition, providing further clarity on the gas-phase abundance of C in the ISM. By demonstrating the reliability of the damping wing fitting technique for analyses of the C II
\lambda 1334
line, our analysis opens the door to future surveys of interstellar C abundances using the same methodology, with the caveat that the sample of sight lines must be carefully selected to avoid the systematic uncertainties that seem to have plagued previous investigations.
