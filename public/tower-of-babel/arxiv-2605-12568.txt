Non-asymptotic quantisation of spherically symmetric distributions
Luc Pronzato, Anatoly Zhigljavsky

Abstract
Zador’s celebrated theorem is a cornerstone of optimal quantisation, establishing both the weak limit of the empirical distribution of an n n -point optimal quantiser in ℝ d \mathds{R}^{d} and the decay rate of the associated L s L_{s} -mean quantisation error. However, for large dimensions d d , observing this asymptotic behaviour demands an astronomically large sample size n n , which grows super-exponentially with d d . Through a detailed analysis of the quantisation problem for spherically symmetric distributions, we demonstrate that for moderate n n random quantisers uniformly distributed on a sphere of suitable radius R R achieve exceptional performance. The expected distortion, expressed as a triple integral, can be computed with arbitrary precision, and the optimal radius R R can be efficiently determined numerically. Leveraging results from extreme-value theory, we derive approximations for R R , particularly in scenarios where n n scales with d d . Depending on the growth rate of n n , R R may either converge to zero or approach a limiting value R ∞ R_{\infty} that is independent of s s .



keywords:
quantisation, distortion, spherically symmetric distribution, Zador’s theorem, extreme-value theory

Remark 2.1

) can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left\{\|X-\mathbf{u}\|\leq t\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }t\leq|r-a|\,,\\
I_{\upsilon}(\delta,\delta)&\mbox{if }|r-a|\leq t\leq a+r\,,\\
1&\mbox{if }a+r\leq t\,,\end{array}\right.

where
r=\|\mathbf{u}\|
and
\upsilon=\upsilon(t,a,r)
is given by (
11
).

2.4
F_{n}(t;\mathbb{P}^{[n]},\mu)
and
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]})
when
\mathbb{P}
is spherically symmetric

From (
3
) and (
4
), the mean distance c.d.f.
F_{n}(\cdot\,;\mathbb{P}^{[n]},\mu)
can be calculated explicitly as

\displaystyle F_{n}(t;\mathbb{P}^{[n]},\mu)=1-\mathsf{E}_{\mu}\left\{\bigg(1-\mathbb{P}\left\{\|X-U\|\leq t\right\}\bigg)^{n}\right\}\,,

(16)

where
U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mu
,
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{P}
, and
X
and
U
are independent.
When
\mathbb{P}
is spherically symmetric,
\mathbb{P}\left\{\|X-\mathbf{u}\|\leq t\right\}
is given by (
10
) which only depends on
\mathbf{u}
through
r=\|\mathbf{u}\|
, and the expectation with respect to
U
in (
16
) is reduced to an expectation with respect to the distribution of
\|U\|
. The calculation of
F_{n}(t;\mathbb{P}^{[n]},\mu)
thus amounts to a double integration, with respect to the distributions of
\|X\|
and
\|U\|
.
From (
2
), the calculation of
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]})
requires an additional integration with respect to
t
,

\displaystyle D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\int_{t\geq 0}t^{s-1}\mathsf{E}_{\mu}\left\{\bigg(1-\mathbb{P}\left\{\|X-U\|\leq t\right\}\bigg)^{n}\right\}\,\mbox{\rm d}t\,,

(17)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle s\int_{t\geq 0}t^{s-1}\int_{r\geq 0}H(r,t;\Phi,n)\,\mbox{\rm d}\Psi(r)\,\mbox{\rm d}t\,,

where

\displaystyle H(r,t;\Phi,n)=\bigg(1-\int_{\rho\geq 0}I_{\upsilon}(\delta,\delta)\,\mbox{\rm d}\Phi(\rho)\bigg)^{n}\,,

with
\upsilon=\upsilon(t,\rho,r)
given by (
11
),
\Psi(\cdot)
the c.d.f. of
\|U\|
for
U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mu
and
\Phi(\cdot)
the c.d.f. of
\|X\|
for
X\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{P}
.

The integrals required to compute
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]})
can be evaluated with arbitrary precision. When
\mathbb{P}
is appropriately parameterised, a high-performance random quantiser can be obtained by numerically minimising the distortion with respect to its defining parameters. For the remainder of this paper, we focus on quantisers defined by a distribution
\mathbb{P}
that depends on a single scalar parameter: the radius of a sphere (Section
3.1
), the radius of a ball (Section
3.3
), or the variance of a multivariate normal distribution (Section
3.4
). The optimisation is performed using a derivative-free line-search method, specifically the golden-section algorithm.

Remark 2.2
.

The expression (
17
) gives the expected
(\mu,s)
-distortion for random quantisers. Therefore, according to the paradigm of the so-called “probabilistic method” (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
), for any
\mathbb{P}
there exists at least one non-random
n
-point set
\mathbf{X}_{n}
with
(\mu,s)
-distortion
E_{\mu,s}^{s}(\mathbf{X}_{n})\leq D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]})
. See Section
3.2
for an illustration involving full factorial designs.

Exact, non-asymptotic, results

Throughout this section, three emblematic cases of spherically symmetric measures are investigated. Nevertheless, the results remain valid for any measure
\nu
, regardless of its symmetr, so long as the radial component
r=\|U\|
follows the same distribution under
\nu
as under
\mu
. For instance, in Section
3.1
(
\mu
uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)
),
\nu
can be a point mass
\delta_{\mathbf{u}}
for any unit vector
\mathbf{u}
; in Sections
3.3
(
\mu
uniform in
{\mathscr{B}}_{d}(1)
) and
3.4
(
\mu
normal),
\nu
may be supported on a line segment or a line, respectively, provided the distribution of
r
is preserved.

3.1
\mu
is uniform on the unit sphere
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)

Here
\mu
is the spherical measure (uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)
), and the distribution of
\|U\|
for
U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mu
is the delta measure at
r=1
. When
n=1
and
\mathbf{X}_{n}=\{\mathbf{0}_{d}\}
, the distance c.d.f.
F(t;\mathbf{X}_{n},\mu)
equals 0 for
t<1
and 1 for
t\geq 1
. For any other
\mathbf{X}_{n}
it has a density
f(\cdot\,;\mathbf{X}_{n},\mu)
on
[0,\mathsf{CR}(\mathbf{X}_{n})]
.

We first consider random quantisers with distribution
\mathbb{P}=\mathbb{P}_{a}
uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(a)
and minimise
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]}_{a})
with respect to
a\in[0,1]
. The expression (
2.3
) can be simplified as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}_{a}\left\{\|X-\mathbf{u}\|\leq t\right\}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}0&\mbox{if }t\leq 1-a\,,\\
I_{\upsilon}(\delta,\delta)&\mbox{if }1-a\leq t\leq 1+a\,,\\
1&\mbox{if }1+a\leq t\,,\end{array}\right.

where
\upsilon=[t^{2}-(1-a)^{2}]/(4a)
. Formula (
17
) then gives

\displaystyle D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]}_{a})=(1-a)^{s}+s\int_{1-a}^{1+a}t^{s-1}\left[1-I_{\upsilon}(\delta,\delta)\right]^{n}\,\mbox{\rm d}t\,.

(19)

This exact expression for the expected
(\mu,s)
-distortion depends on

and
a
; the value
a^{*}=a^{*}(d,n,s)
that minimises
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]}_{a})
with respect to
a
can be obtained numerically with arbitrary precision for any given
d,n
and
s
. In the special case
d=3
,
\delta=1
and

for

, so that

can be calculated explicitly for
s
even. It is given by a polynomial in
a
of degree
s
, with for example
D_{\mu,2}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]}_{a})=(1-a)^{2}+4\,a/(n+1)
and
D_{\mu,4}(\mathbb{P}^{[n]}_{a})=(1-a)^{4}+8\,a[n(1-a)^{2}+2+2\,a^{2}]/[(n+1)(n+2)]
, which gives
a^{*}(3,n,2)=(n-1)/(n+1)
and
a^{*}(3,n,4)
as the root of a cubic equation, which satisfies
a^{*}(3,n,4)=1-4/n+16/n^{2}+{\mathcal{O}}(1/n^{3})
as

.

3.2
Comparison between random quantisers uniform on a sphere and optimised full factorial
2^{d}
designs

Let
n=2^{d}
and
\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)
be the full factorial
2^{d}
design with points
\mathbf{x}_{i}
having coordinates
\pm b
,
b\in(0,1]
: the
\mathbf{x}_{i}
are vertices of the cube
[-b,b]^{d}
; they also belong to the sphere
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(a)
with
a=\sqrt{d}\,b
. For
{\mathscr{X}}={\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)
, the Voronoi regions
\mathscr{V}(\mathbf{x}_{i})=\{\mathbf{x}\in{\mathscr{X}}:\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{i}\|=\min_{\mathbf{x}_{j}\in\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)}\|\mathbf{x}-\mathbf{x}_{j}\|\}
are all identical up to a permutation of coordinates and do not depend on the value of
b
.

Consider first the case
s=2
. For
\mu
uniform on
{\mathscr{X}}={\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)
,
E_{2,\mu}^{2}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]=\mathsf{E}\{\|V-\mathbf{b}\|^{2}\}
where
\mathbf{b}=(b,\ldots,b)\in\mathds{R}^{d}
and
V=(V_{1},\ldots,V_{d})
is uniform in
\mathscr{V}(\mathbf{b})={\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)\cap\mathds{R}_{+}^{d}
. Therefore

\displaystyle E_{2,\mu}^{2}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]=\mathsf{E}\{\|V\|^{2}-2\,b\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}+db^{2}\}=1-2\,bd\,\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}\}+db^{2}\,.

Minimisation of
E_{2,\mu}^{2}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]
with respect to
b
yields
b_{2}^{*}=\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}\}
and the optimal
(\mu,2)
-distortion of a
2^{d}
factorial design is
E_{2,\mu}^{2}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b_{2}^{*})]=1-d(b_{2}^{*})^{2}
.
For
U^{(d)}=(U_{1},\ldots,U_{d})\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mu
, the first component
U_{1}
has the density
\varphi_{1}(\cdot)
of Lemma
2.1
on
[-1,1]
, therefore,
V_{1}
has the density
2\,\varphi_{1}(\cdot)
on
[0,1]
, so that

\displaystyle b_{2}^{*}=\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma((d+1)/2)}\,,

\displaystyle E_{2,\mu}^{2}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b_{2}^{*})]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1-\frac{d}{\pi}\left(\frac{\Gamma(d/2)}{\Gamma((d+1)/2)}\right)^{2}\,.

More generally, the analytic expression for the
(\mu,s)
-distortion
E_{\mu,s}^{s}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]
can be derived for any even
s
, but the calculations become more complicated as
s
increases. For
s=4
we obtain

\displaystyle E_{4,\mu}^{4}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathsf{E}\{\|V-\mathbf{b}\|^{4}\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 1+4\,b^{2}\,\mathsf{E}\left\{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i}\right)^{2}\right\}+d^{2}b^{4}+2\,db^{2}-4\,bd\,\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}\}(1+db^{2})\,.

Using
\mathsf{E}\{(\sum_{i=1}^{d}V_{i})^{2}\}=1+d(d-1)\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}V_{2}\}
, where
\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}V_{2}\}=2/(\pi d)
from
\varphi_{2}(\cdot,\cdot)
of Lemma
2.1
, together with the expression above for
\mathsf{E}\{V_{1}\}
, we can express
E_{4,\mu}^{4}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]
as a fourth-degree polynomial in
b
with coefficients depending on
d
. The optimal value
b_{4}^{*}
can be obtained explicitly.
The values of
b_{2}^{*}
and
b_{4}^{*}
are very close for large
d
as
a_{j}^{*}=\sqrt{d}\,b_{j}^{*}=\sqrt{2/\pi}+{\mathcal{O}}(1/d)
for
j=2,4
.

For
s=\infty
,
E_{\infty,\mu}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b_{\infty}^{*})]=\mathsf{CR}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)]
, the covering radius of
\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)
, with
(\mathsf{CR}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b)])^{2}=1+d\,b^{2}-2\,b
being minimum for
b_{\infty}^{*}=1/d
, which gives

\mathsf{CR}[\mathbf{X}_{n}(b_{\infty}^{*})]=\sqrt{1-1/d}
.

4
Extreme-value approximations

In this section, extreme-value theory is used to derive approximations of the
(\mu,s)
-distortion
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]})
, where
\mathbb{P}_{a}
is uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(a)
(Section
4.1
).
In Section
4.2
, three asymptotic regimes are identified that govern the limiting behaviour of
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]})
when
n
increases with
d
.
The case where
\mu
is uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(1)
is considered first (Section
4.3
): we show that to each asymptotic regime is associated a limiting value of the optimal radius
a^{*}
that does not depend on
s
. In Section
4.4
, these results are extended to general spherically symmetric measures
\mu
satisfying a norm-concentration property, with the uniform distribution in
{\mathscr{B}}_{d}(1)
and the multivariate normal distribution as illustrative examples.

4.1
Uniform quantisers on a sphere

Consider random quantisers
\mathbf{R}_{n}
with distribution
\mathsf{P}=\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]}
where
\mathbb{P}_{a}
is uniform on
{\mathcal{S}}_{d-1}(a)
. For large
n
, the distribution of
\zeta(n,d)
in (
9
) can be approximated using standard results from extreme-value theory. Here we assume
d\geq 3
to avoid singularity in beta distributions.

Lemma 4.1
.

Assume that
d\geq 3
and
\mathsf{P}=\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]}
. For any fixed
\mathbf{u}\in\mathds{R}^{d}
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4\,a\,\|\mathbf{u}\|\,\kappa_{n,d}}\left[d^{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_{n})-(\|\mathbf{u}\|-a)^{2}\right]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\rm d}}{{\rightarrow}}\xi_{d}\,,\ n\to\infty\,,

(24)

where
\xi_{d}
is a random variable with Weibull c.d.f.
F_{d}(t)=1-\exp(-t^{\delta})
,
t\geq 0
, and moments
\mathsf{E}\{\zeta_{d}^{k}\}=\Gamma(1+k/\delta)
with
\delta=(d-1)/2
, and where
\kappa_{n,d}
is the
1/n
quantile of
\beta_{\delta,\delta}
, defined by
I_{\kappa_{n,d}}(\delta,\delta)=1/n
.

Proof.

From standard extreme-value theory (see, e.g.,
[
5
, p. 59]
), the random variable

\zeta(n,d)/\kappa_{n,d}
in (
9
) converges in distribution to the random variable
\xi_{d}
.
∎

Remark 4.1
.

Lemma
4.1
yields the following approximations for the
s
-moment of
d(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_{n})
for large
n
and any given
\mathbf{u}
:

\displaystyle M_{s}(d,n,a,r)=\int_{\zeta\geq 0}\left[(r-a)^{2}+4\,a\,r\,\kappa_{n,d}\,\zeta\right]^{s/2}\,\mbox{\rm d}F_{d}(\zeta)\,,

with
r=\|\mathbf{u}\|
.
Explicit expressions are easily obtained for even
s
. Assuming that
\|\mathbf{u}\|=1
, this gives in particular the approximations

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}_{2}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 16\,a^{2}\,\kappa_{n,d}^{2}\,\mathsf{var}\{\xi_{d}\}=16\,a^{2}\,\kappa_{n,d}^{2}\left[\Gamma(1+2/\delta)-\Gamma^{2}(1+1/\delta)\right]\,,

\displaystyle\mathsf{var}_{4}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 64\,a^{2}\,\kappa_{n,d}^{2}\,\left\{(1-a)^{4}\,\mathsf{var}\{\xi_{d}\}+a^{2}\,\kappa_{n,d}^{2}\,\mathsf{var}\{\xi_{d}^{2}\}\right.

\displaystyle\left.+(1-a)^{2}\,a\,\kappa_{n,d}\,\left[\Gamma(1+3/\delta)-\Gamma(1+1/\delta)\Gamma(1+2/\delta)\right]\right\}\,.

for
\mathsf{var}_{s}=\mathsf{var}\{d^{s}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_{n})\}
when
s=2
and
s=4
, respectively.

\triangleleft

The random variables
\zeta(n,d)
in (
9
) depend on
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{R}_{n}
, but (
24
) shows that the dependence in
\mathbf{R}_{n}
vanishes asymptotically, so that
\xi_{d}
only depends on
\mathbf{u}/\|\mathbf{u}\|
. We hence obtain the following extreme-value approximation of
D_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]})
for
\mu
spherically symmetric.

Definition 4.1
.

Assume that
d\geq 3
and that
\mu
is spherically symmetric. For any
\mathbf{R}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]}
, the extreme-value approximation of the
(\mu,s)
-distortion

is defined by

\displaystyle\widehat{E}_{\mu,s}^{s}(n;a)=\int_{r\geq 0}\int_{\zeta\geq 0}\left[(r-a)^{2}+4\,a\,r\,\kappa_{n,d}\,\zeta\right]^{s/2}\,\mbox{\rm d}F_{d}(\zeta)\,\mbox{\rm d}\Psi(r)\,,

(25)

where

with

with

is the

quantile of
\beta_{\delta,\delta}
defined by
I_{\kappa_{n,d}}(\delta,\delta)=1/n

\widehat{D}_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]})
of

\widehat{D}_{\mu,s}(\mathbb{P}_{a}^{[n]})=\widehat{E}_{\mu,s}^{s}(n;a)
.

The approximation (
25
) is derived as follows. Since
\mu

U\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{\sim}}r\cdot U^{(d)}
where

is uniformly distributed on

and
r

\Psi(\cdot)
. Since

in (

and has the c.d.f.

(r-a)^{2}+4\,a\,r\,\kappa_{n,d}\,\zeta(U^{(d)})
for

in

where

For
s

4.2
Three asymptotic regimes for
n
growing with
d

In view of Lemma
4.1
, the asymptotic behaviour of
\kappa_{n,d}
is the key element in understanding that of
d^{2}(\mathbf{u},\mathbf{R}_{n})
. For
d=3
,
\beta_{1,1}
is uniform on
[0,1]
and we simply have
\kappa_{n,3}=1/n
. For
d>3
there is no explicit formula for
\kappa_{n,d}
, but it can easily be computed numerically by solving
I_{\kappa_{n,d}}(\delta,\delta)=1/n
. For fixed
t\leq 1/2
,
I_{t}(\delta,\delta)
decreases as
\delta
increases. Therefore,
\kappa_{n,d}
is an increasing function of
d
for any fixed
n\geq 2
(it is also a decreasing function of
n
for any fixed
d
). When
n
tends to infinity with
d
we have the following properties.

Proposition 4.1
.

For fixed
d\geq 3
, the
1/n
quantile
\kappa_{n,d}
of
\beta_{\delta,\delta}
with
\delta=(d-1)/2
tends to zero as
n
tends to infinity. When
d\to\infty
and
n=n(d)
grows with
d
, we have the following three cases:

(i) if
n^{1/d}\to\infty
, then
\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{n,d}=0
;

(ii) if
n=C\lambda^{d}\,(1+o(1))
with
\lambda>1
and
C>0
, then

\displaystyle\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{n,d}=\frac{1}{2}\left[1-\sqrt{1-1/\lambda^{2}}\right]\,;

(29)

(iii) if
(\log n)/d\to 0
, then
\lim_{d\to\infty}\kappa_{n,d}=\frac{1}{2}
.

Proof.

Since
x^{\delta-1}(1-t)^{\delta}\leq x^{\delta-1}(1-t)^{\delta-1}\leq x^{\delta-1}(1-x)^{\delta-1}
for
\delta\geq 1
and all
t\in[0,1)
and
x\in[0,t]
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{t^{\delta}(1-t)^{\delta}}{\delta B(\delta,\delta)}\leq I_{t}(\delta,\delta)\,.

(30)

The derivation of an exploitable upper bound is more delicate.
Assume that
d\geq 3
and let

\delta^{\prime}=\lfloor\delta\rfloor
, the largest integer smaller than or equal to
\delta
.
As
n\geq 2
, we are only interested in values of
t
less than
1/2
. Since
\delta^{\prime}\leq\delta
, we have
I_{t}(\delta,\delta)\leq I_{t}(\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})
, which
can be calculated explicitly by successive integration by parts. Direct calculations yield the following special case of the well-known relation between the c.d.f. of the beta and binomial distributions:

\displaystyle I_{t}(\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{k=0}^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\binom{2\delta^{\prime}-1}{k}t^{2\delta^{\prime}-1-k}(1-t)^{k}\,,

(31)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t^{\delta^{\prime}}(1-t)^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\sum_{k=0}^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\binom{2\delta^{\prime}-1}{k}\left(\frac{t}{1-t}\right)^{\delta^{\prime}-1-k}\,.

(32)

Using the property
t/(1-t)\leq 1
for
t\leq 1/2
, with
\sum_{k=0}^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\binom{2\delta^{\prime}-1}{k}=4^{\delta^{\prime}-1}
, we thus obtain

\displaystyle I_{t}(\delta,\delta)\leq I_{t}(\delta^{\prime},\delta^{\prime})\leq t^{\delta^{\prime}}[4\,(1-t)]^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\leq\frac{1}{2}\,[4\,t(1-t)]^{\delta^{\prime}-1}\,,\ t\leq 1/2\,.

(33)

Together with (
30
), this implies, for
n\geq 2
and
d\geq 3
,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\left[1-\sqrt{1-(2/n)^{1/(\delta^{\prime}-1)}}\right]\leq\kappa_{n,d}\leq\frac{1}{2}\left[1-\sqrt{(1-4\,c_{d}\,n^{-1/\delta})_{+}}\right]\,,

(34)

where

5
Conclusions

We have demonstrated that, for a spherically symmetric measure
\mu
in
\mathds{R}^{d}
, unless the sample size
n
is extremely large, a random quantiser uniformly distributed on a sphere of suitable radius can significantly outperform a random quantiser whose distribution follows the asymptotic limit predicted by Zador’s theorem. The optimal radius can be determined numerically by minimising a triple integral, which can be evaluated with arbitrary precision. Additionally, an approximation of the optimal radius is available, derived from extreme-value theory. Since the variability of the distortion for such random quantisers rapidly diminishes as
n
increases, this approach is highly practical for constructing high-performance quantisers.

While the restriction to spherically symmetric distributions may appear limiting, it provides a robust foundation for broader applications. For instance, quantising the uniform measure on the
d
-dimensional hypercube
[-1,1]^{d}
presents a compelling and challenging problem, particularly in space-filling design for computer experiments (see, e.g.,
[
10
]
). While greedy quantisation offers an attractive approach for the incremental construction of designs in low dimensions
[
6
,
7
]
, it becomes computationally infeasible for large
d
. In such cases, recent results from
[
8
]
allow the uniform measure to be approximated by a spherically symmetric distribution. Similarly, one can consider random quantisers distributed according to a product measure, which can itself be approximated by a spherically symmetric distribution. Preliminary findings then suggest that quantisers distributed on the vertices of a smaller hypercube exhibit promising performance, paving the way for further advances in this direction.
