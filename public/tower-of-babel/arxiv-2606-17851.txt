A homotopy-type-theoretic generalization of neurosymbolic inference
Fernando Zhapa-Camacho, Robert Hoehndorf

Abstract
A wide range of neurosymbolic (NeSy) systems compute one functional: a belief-weighted sum of a logical quantity over a space of σ \sigma -structures, of which weighted model counting, fuzzy logic, and probabilistic logic are special cases. This account is built on sets , and a set deliberately forgets two things that are important for NeSy: when two σ \sigma -structures are the same up to a symmetry of the theory, and how many distinct proofs witness a query. Replacing the underlying sets by types , in the sense of homotopy type theory, preserves this information, and turns this functional into a belief-weighted homotopy cardinality, a notion of size that counts each object in inverse proportion to its symmetries. We develop the framework from scratch for NeSy systems, prove a conservativity theorem that recovers the classical functional when symmetries are trivial, and show that the symmetry our framework exposes is exactly the one behind reasoning shortcuts. The payoff is concrete: the shortcut-aware concept posterior that recent methods reach by ensembling or expressive density estimation is the only symmetry-invariant point of the confusion-set simplex, computable in closed form by averaging a single model over the symmetry group. On MNIST reasoning-shortcut benchmarks this single-model wrapper is better calibrated than a diversity-trained ensemble, while leaving label accuracy and identifiable concepts untouched. Code is freely available at https://github.com/bio-ontology-research-group/hott-nesy .

Introduction

Much of neurosymbolic AI can be read as a single recipe: a neural
network proposes weights over possibilities, a logical theory says
which possibilities are admissible, and inference aggregates the
weights of the admissible ones
(
6
)
. Fixing a
space
\Omega
of
\sigma
-structures
(assignments of values to
the symbols of a logical language), a
logic function

\ell

that assigns a value to a sentence
\varphi
in a

\sigma
-structure, and a neural
belief

b_{\theta}
over

\sigma
-structures, neurosymbolic inference can be defined as the
belief-weighted aggregate

F_{\theta}(\varphi)\;=\;\int_{\Omega}\ell(\varphi,\omega)\,b_{\theta}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}m(\omega).

(1)

With a counting measure this is weighted model counting (WMC); over a
continuous domain it is weighted model integration. Through the lens
of algebraic model counting
(
10
)
, varying the
arithmetic used to combine values recovers probabilistic, fuzzy,
tropical (MaxSAT/Viterbi), and provenance inference; varying the
belief recovers the semantic loss
(
25
)
, DeepProbLog

(
12
)
, logic
tensor networks
(
1
)
, Scallop

(
8
)
, and more. Equation (
1
) is
therefore a common denominator for large parts of the NeSy field.

A space of
\sigma
-structures is a
set
, and a set is a
deliberately forgetful object: it records which elements it contains
and nothing else – neither
(i)
that two

\sigma
-structures are “the same up to a symmetry” of the theory,
nor
(ii)
how many distinct derivations witness that a query
holds. Both are central to relational and structured NeSy. If two
individuals are interchangeable (no evidence distinguishes them), the

\sigma
-structures obtained by swapping them are not different ones,
yet Eqn. (
1
) counts them as two; if a query is provable
in several ways, Eqn. (
1
) collapses this to a single
number. In the terminology of category theory

(
2
)
, the functional is
decategorified
: it
projects a structured situation down to bare numbers. The first
symmetry is not
an exotic concern: it is exactly the structure behind
reasoning
shortcuts

(
15
)
, a central failure mode of
NeSy learning.

We investigate what Eqn. (
1
) becomes if its underlying sets
are replaced by
types
, in the sense of homotopy type theory
(HoTT)
(
20
)
. A type is like a set that additionally
remembers, for any two elements, the ways in which they may be
identified; from this it recovers each element’s symmetry group and
each proof’s identity. The correct
notion of
size
for such an object is not cardinality but

homotopy cardinality

(
2
;
11
)
,
which counts each element weighted by the reciprocal of its
symmetries. Carrying Eqn. (
1
) through this change of
foundations (Section
3
) yields a belief-weighted homotopy
cardinality that retains this information while specializing back to
Eqn. (
1
) when all symmetries are trivial.

We keep the development self-contained and assume no background in
type theory: Section
2.3
introduces every notion we use,
and Appendix
A
expands it in logic and knowledge-graph
terms. Our contributions are:

•

lifting each ingredient of Eqn. (
1
) one level,
exposing two design parameters the classical functional cannot name:
a

and a
truncation level
;

•

a
conservativity theorem
(Section
3.1
),
verified in Lean 4 with Mathlib
(
19
)
, identifying the
belief-weighted homotopy cardinality with a symmetry-corrected
weighted count and recovering Eqn. (
1
) when the
symmetry is trivial or the semiring idempotent;

•

we identify (Section
3.2
) the symmetry parameter with the
mechanism behind reasoning shortcuts, leading to a closed-form,
single-model method for shortcut-aware concept posteriors
(
orbit-averaging
);

•

experiments (Section
4
) on MNIST reasoning-shortcut
tasks where the wrapper is better calibrated than a five-model

Bears
ensemble

(

, with accuracy and identifiable concepts

•

–

and of the route to the continuous side through cohesion.

The neurosymbolic inference functional

(2)

Example 1

.

Let two individuals

\sigma
-structure is a function

\omega\colon\{1,2\}\to\{\bot,\top\}

A single algebraic generalization underlies the field’s diversity:

(

, fuzzy

2.2
Reasoning shortcuts

A NeSy model trains its perception – which predicts the latent
symbols, or
concepts
, of each input – with supervision only on
the logical output, and this under-determination has a well-studied
failure mode. Write
\beta
for the map sending a concept assignment to
the program output it produces. A
reasoning shortcut
is a
perception that minimizes the training loss while assigning its
concepts the wrong meaning, induced by a relabeling
\alpha
of the
vocabulary that leaves the output unchanged,
\beta(\alpha(c))=\beta(c)

(
15
;
4
)
. The concept
assignments reachable from a
given one by such output-preserving relabelings form its

confusion set

(
24
)
, and, under
mild assumptions, the
loss-minimizing perceptions are the convex combinations of the
resulting deterministic optima
(
15
)
. Under the
common
independence assumption
, that the perception factorizes
over concepts
(
22
)
, the model is driven
to one deterministic assignment per input and commits to a single
element of each confusion set with no evidence to prefer it.

The concepts within a confusion set are
unidentifiable
from the
data, so a predictor should be
uncertain
across them rather than
commit to one shortcut
(
14
)
; the desired

shortcut-aware
concept posterior is the maximum-entropy (uniform)
distribution on each confusion set. Existing methods reach it by
training a diversity-encouraged ensemble (
Bears
,

14
), by regularizing toward a uniform target

(
24
)
, or by fitting an expressive
joint distribution over concepts
(
23
)
.
Section
3.2
recovers this target in closed form by reading
confusion sets as symmetry orbits.

2.3
Types, identifications, and symmetry

A type is a set that also remembers how its elements can be
identified. For neurosymbolic inference the elements are

\sigma
-structures, and an identification is a renaming of
individuals carrying one
\sigma
-structure to another that respects
the theory – an isomorphism of
\sigma
-structures, or an
automorphism when the two coincide.

For a type
A
and elements
a,b:A
there is an
identity type

\mathrm{Id}_{A}(a,b)
whose elements are
identifications
of
a
with

b
. Take
A
to be the
\sigma
-structures of a theory

T
. An identification of
\sigma
-structure

a
with
\sigma
-structure
b
is then a renaming of the domain
individuals that carries
a
to
b
and respects
T
. There may be
none (the two are genuinely different models), exactly one (the
situation in an ordinary set of
\sigma
-structures), or several. The
self-identifications of a single
\sigma
-structure, the renamings of
individuals that leave it unchanged, compose and invert, so they form
a group: the
automorphism group

\mathrm{Aut}(a)
. This is the information a set discards: a set
of
\sigma
-structures is the degenerate case in which the only
renaming fixing any
\sigma
-structure is the identity.

In the smokers program of Example
1
, if no evidence
distinguishes individuals
1
and
2
, then swapping them identifies
the
\sigma
-structure “only
1
smokes” with “only
2
smokes”.
The same swap leaves “nobody smokes” and “everybody smokes”
unchanged, so it is a nontrivial automorphism of each. The type of

\sigma
-structures records this relabeling symmetry of the program;
the set of
\sigma
-structures cannot.

2.4
Truncation: propositions, sets, groupoids

Types are stratified by how much identification structure they carry,
the
truncation level
. A
(-1)
-type
, or

proposition
, has at most one element: a yes/no fact, such as
whether a ground atom holds or whether
\varphi
is entailed. A

0
-type
, or
set
, has at most one identification between
any two elements: the
\sigma
-structures
\Omega=D^{\sigma}
form a

0
-type, recording no symmetry. A
1
-type
, or

groupoid
, may have nontrivial automorphism groups but no higher
structure;
\sigma
-structures carrying their renaming symmetries live
here.

2.5
Dependent sums and homotopy cardinality

If to each
a:A
we assign a type
P(a)
, the
dependent sum

\sum_{a:A}P(a)
is the type of pairs
(a,p)
with
p:P(a)
. We take

P(\omega)
to be the
derivations
that witness the query in

\sigma
-structure
\omega
: a Boolean once we forget which, or the
several distinct proof trees that provenance records. The dependent
sum bundles each
model with a derivation, and its identifications combine renamings of
models with identifications of the derivations they carry, so
symmetries of models and of proofs are tracked together.

Ordinary counting is the wrong measure of
size
for a groupoid
of
\sigma
-structures, because it ignores symmetry: a

\sigma
-structure fixed by a nontrivial renaming should count as less
than a whole one. The right notion is
homotopy cardinality

(
2
;
11
)
, which counts each element in
inverse proportion to its symmetries,

\lvert X\rvert\;=\;\sum_{[x]}\frac{1}{\lvert\mathrm{Aut}(x)\rvert},

(3)

the sum running over
\sigma
-structures up to renaming. A set of
k

\sigma
-structures has
\lvert X\rvert=k
;
a single model with symmetry group
G
counts as
\tfrac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}
.

To connect this to a group of renamings, we use three standard notions
from group actions. When a finite group
G
acts on a finite set
X

of
\sigma
-structures, the
orbit

G\cdot x=\{g\cdot x:g\in G\}
collects the structures
indistinguishable from
x
up to renaming,
the
stabilizer

\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)=\{g\in G:g\cdot x=x\}
collects the
renamings fixing
x
, and each orbit has size

\tfrac{\lvert G\rvert}{\lvert\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)\rvert}
(the

orbit–stabilizer theorem
). The
action
groupoid

X/\!\!/G
(a double slash, distinguishing it from the
orbit set
X/G
, which forgets the stabilizers) adds an
identification
x\to g\cdot x

for every
g\in G
; combining the two
preceding facts, its homotopy cardinality is

\lvert X/\!\!/G\rvert=\sum_{\text{orbits}}\frac{1}{\lvert\mathrm{Stab}_{G}(x)\rvert}=\frac{\lvert X\rvert}{\lvert G\rvert}.

(4)

Example 2

(Models up to relabeling)
.

Take the four
\sigma
-structures of Example
1
with
renaming group
\mathrm{Sym}(2)=\{\mathrm{id},\text{swap}\}
. “Nobody” and
“everybody” are each fixed by the swap (stabilizer of size
2
); the
two “exactly one” models form one orbit with trivial stabilizer.
Then Eqn. (
3
) gives
\tfrac{1}{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}+1=2=\tfrac{4}{2}
,
the symmetry-weighted count, not the number
3
of
models-up-to-relabeling.

Homotopy cardinality requires finiteness (finitely many isomorphism
classes, finite automorphism groups; “
\pi
-finiteness”).

3.2
Reasoning shortcuts as residual symmetry

The symmetry parameter is not an abstraction, but the structure behind
reasoning shortcuts (Section
2.2
), and turning it into a
measure yields a practical method. The output-preserving relabelings of the
vocabulary are invertible and closed under composition, so they form a
group, the symmetry parameter
G\leq\mathrm{Aut}(T)
instantiated by
“preserves the belief”. A confusion set is then exactly an orbit

G\cdot x
of a
\sigma
-structure under this group, and two facts the
literature states set-theoretically become orbit–stabilizer
arithmetic: the number of distinct shortcuts through a

\sigma
-structure is the orbit size

\tfrac{\lvert G\rvert}{\lvert\mathrm{Stab}\rvert}
, and the symmetry-corrected
functional
\lvert X/\!\!/G\rvert_{b}
is invariant under this group
by construction (Theorem
4
). The independence assumption
is the trivial case
G=\mathbf{1}
: every grounding is distinct, and
the loss minima are the disconnected deterministic choices of
Section
2.2

(
22
)
.

The shortcut-aware posterior is the unique symmetry-invariant point:
the concept posteriors form the probability simplex on a confusion
set, that is on an orbit, the group action permutes its vertices,
and the single invariant point is the uniform distribution,
the maximum-entropy target of
Section
2.2
. Here it is forced: for any
belief the symmetry parameter fixes, the orbit-conditional posterior
is uniform,
\tfrac{b(y)}{\sum_{z}b(z)}=\tfrac{1}{\lvert G\cdot x\rvert}
, the same
invariant measure the functional integrates against (proof in Lean,
Appendix
C
).

This response lives only in a non-idempotent semiring: reading the same
confusion set in an idempotent one, such as the
(\max,+)
of
most-probable-model inference, collapses its tied values to one
number (Proposition
5
), so the aware uniform posterior
and an overconfident commitment to one shortcut become
indistinguishable; awareness needs the counting semiring.

Orbit-averaging realizes this invariant point as a closed-form,
inference-time method that requires neither an ensemble nor a density
model:
symmetrize
any base perception

with
the group-averaging operator over the output-preserving group

\bar{p}(c\mid x)\;=\;\frac{1}{\lvert G\rvert}\sum_{g\in G}p_{\theta}(g^{-1}\!\cdot c\mid x).

(6)

Proposition 6

(Orbit-averaging is closed-form shortcut awareness)
.

For an output-preserving
G
, the symmetrized posterior in
Eqn. (
6
)
(i)
leaves the induced label
distribution unchanged, so label accuracy is preserved exactly;

(ii)
equals the uniform (maximum-entropy) distribution on

(iii)

is computed in one forward pass plus a sum over

\tfrac{\lvert G\rvert}{\lvert\mathrm{Stab}\rvert}

Part (i) holds because each

within itself

statement above. So orbit-averaging keeps confident, correct
predictions on identifiable concepts (orbit size

calibrated-uncertain exactly on the confusable ones, from a single
model. The group

is read off the knowledge as the

(

)
or saucy

(

)
. The closed form is exact when

where it is only partial, an ensemble
(

)
or

(

represents the rest of the simplex. To

Discussion

5.1
A generating-function consequence

The categorified reading has an analytic consequence already in the
simplest relational model. Generalizing
Example
1
to
n
individuals with one unary predicate,
a
\sigma
-structure is a subset of
\{S(1),\dots,S(n)\}
,

\mathrm{Aut}(T)=\mathrm{Sym}(n)
, and the exchangeable belief

b(\omega)=\beta^{\lvert\omega\rvert}
is
\mathrm{Sym}(n)
-invariant, so the
symmetry parameter is maximal,
G=\mathrm{Sym}(n)
. Orbits are indexed by the
number

\binom{n}{m}
and stabilizer of
order
m!(n-m)!
, giving

\textstyle\sum_{\omega}b(\omega)=(1+\beta)^{n},\qquad\lvert\Omega/\!\!/\mathrm{Sym}(n)\rvert_{b}=\frac{(1+\beta)^{n}}{n!},

(7)

in agreement with Theorem
4
(
\lvert G\rvert=n!
); the
closed form is machine-checked for every

(Appendix
C
).

Corollary 7

(Inference generating functions)
.

For the exchangeable model, the flat functional (
2
)
assembles into the
ordinary
generating function

\sum_{n}(1+\beta)^{n}x^{n}=\frac{1}{1-(1+\beta)x}
, while the belief-weighted
homotopy cardinality (
5
) assembles into the

exponential
generating function

\sum_{n}\frac{(1+\beta)^{n}}{n!}x^{n}=e^{(1+\beta)x}
.

In the theory of combinatorial
species
(
9
)
, a species’ exponential generating
function is the homotopy cardinality of its associated groupoid

(
2
)
; our model is the species “subsets of the
domain”. Because
species compose, the homotopy-cardinality reading
composes correctly when relational models are built from parts, while
the flat count overcounts relabelings; the correct generating function
follows from the inference functional itself, without a separate
exchangeability argument.

5.2
Comparison of types and sets

Two further payoffs are worth
naming. The first is proof-relevance: a set-based truth value cannot
distinguish
“provable” from “provable in three symmetric ways”, whereas the
truncation level of
P
places
satisfaction checking, derivation counting, and symmetric-derivation
counting on one axis.

The second payoff is representation invariance: HoTT’s univalence
axiom
(
20
)
lets equivalent types be treated as equal
(Appendix
A
), so inference computed in one symbolic
encoding equals inference in any equivalent one, with no
separate proof; in set-based foundations such invariance must be
re-established by hand. The finite case is machine-checked
(
groupoidCard_transport
,
Appendix
C
); Appendix
B
spells out the
consequence for description-logic NeSy systems.

5.3
The continuous side, and an open problem

Plain homotopy type theory supplies identifications but neither a
metric nor a gradient.
Real-cohesive
homotopy type
theory
(
18
)

equips every type with a synthetic notion of continuity
alongside its identifications; its
shape modality
collapses a
continuous type to its discrete content, a synthetic symbol-grounding
map.

One substantial gap remains: homotopy cardinality is a counting
invariant for
\pi
-finite types (Section
2.5
), and a
synthetic measure compatible with cohesion that lets
Eqn. (
5
) range over a continuous
\Omega
does not
yet exist – the central open problem the framework raises.

6
Related work

Our value object refines established
machinery: algebraic model counting
(
10
)

already reads probabilistic, fuzzy, tropical, and provenance inference
as one semiring sum, and we recover it as decategorifications of the
dependent sum, with Scallop
(

)
the set-valued
case and lifted inference

(

;
17
)
the orbit

. Reasoning shortcuts

(

)
, the independence assumption

(

(

7
Conclusion

Read over types rather than sets, neurosymbolic inference becomes
a belief-weighted homotopy cardinality that
remembers structure symmetries and proof identity and recovers the
classical functional when symmetries are trivial
(Theorem
4
, machine-checked). The symmetry it exposes is
the one behind reasoning shortcuts, and orbit-averaging, the
closed-form single-model method it yields, matches or beats a
five-model ensemble.
