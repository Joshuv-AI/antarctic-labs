Non-abelian Extensions of Lie algebras with derivations
Jun Jiang, Kanghe Xu

Abstract
. In this paper, we investigate non-abelian extensions of Lie algebras with derivations using several different approaches. We show that the theory of non-abelian extensions of a Lie algebra with a derivation can be characterized by means of the second non-abelian cohomology, the Deligne groupoid, the homotopy category of strict Lie 2 2 -algebras with strict derivations, and the notion of a ( 𝔤 , D ) (\mathfrak{g},D) -kernel, respectively. Moreover, within this unified framework, we address the following existence problem: given a non-abelian extension of Lie algebras 0 → 𝔥 → i 𝔤 ^ → p 𝔤 → 0 , \begin{CD}0@>{}>{}>\mathfrak{h}@>{i}>{}>\hat{\mathfrak{g}}@>{p}>{}>\mathfrak{g}@>{}>{}>0,\end{CD} let ( K , D ) ∈ Der ⁡ ( 𝔥 ) × Der ⁡ ( 𝔤 ) (K,D)\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}) be a pair of derivations of 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} and 𝔤 \mathfrak{g} respectively. When does there exist a derivation D ^ \hat{D} of 𝔤 ^ \hat{\mathfrak{g}} such that D ^ | 𝔥 = K \hat{D}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=K and D ∘ p = p ∘ D ^ . D\circ p=p\circ\hat{D}. We provide an obstruction class for the existence of such a lift.

In this paper, we solve the above problem by using non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs. We also investigate non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs through four different approaches. In Section
2
, we introduce the non-abelian cohomology for LieDer pairs and apply it to classify non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs. In Section
3
, we construct a differential graded Lie algebra, whose set of Maurer-Cartan elements corresponds bijectively to the set of non-abelian
2
-cocycles. Furthermore, the set of connected components of the Deligne groupoid are used to characterize non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs. In Section
4
, we investigate the relationship between non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and the homotopy category of strict Lie
2
-algebras with strict derivations. More precisely, we show that the hHom set between strict Lie 2-algebras with strict derivations is isomorphic to the non-abelian cohomology of LieDer pairs. We also prove that
(\mathfrak{g},D)\mapsto{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
is a representable functor in a suitable category. In Section
5

(\mathfrak{g},D)

(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel. We also obtain cohomology groups of a LieDer pairs via a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel, then use the second cohomology group to describe the set of equivalent non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs. We use the third cohomology group to study when a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel comes from a non-abelian extension of LieDer pairs. In Section
6
, we end this paper by characterizing the extensibility of derivations of Lie algebras within established framework. We also give the obstruction class of the extensibility of derivations of Lie algebras.

Throughout the paper, all the Lie algebras are over
\mathbb{C}
and finite-dimensional.

Z^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
. Then, to prove

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}))\cong H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)\cong\mathbf{Ext_{nab}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K),

we only need to prove that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}})}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is non-empty iff
[\mu]=[\mu^{\prime}]
, where

\mu=(\varrho,\omega,\chi),\mu^{\prime}=(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})\in Z^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
.

If
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}})}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
, then there exists
(\tau,0)\in\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}_{0}
such that,

(28)

\mu=e^{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}\mu^{\prime}+\frac{{\rm{Id}}-e^{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}}{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}(d(\tau,0)).

By (
3
), we obtain that
[\tau,\omega^{\prime}]=0,\ [\tau,\chi^{\prime}]=0
and
[\tau,[\tau,\varrho^{\prime}]]=0
, which implies

\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)(\mu^{\prime})=[(\tau,0),(\omega^{\prime}+\varrho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})]=([\tau,\omega^{\prime}]+[\tau,\varrho^{\prime}],[\tau,\chi^{\prime}])=([\tau,\varrho^{\prime}],0),

and

\mathrm{ad}^{2}(\tau,0)(\mu^{\prime})=[(\tau,0),([\tau,\varrho^{\prime}],0)]=([\tau,[\tau,\varrho^{\prime}]],0)=0.

Hence, we have

\displaystyle e^{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}\mu^{\prime}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{n!}\mathrm{ad}^{n}(\tau,0)(\mu^{\prime})=\big({\rm{Id}}+\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)+\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{ad}^{2}(\tau,0)+\cdots\big)(\mu^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\big({\rm{Id}}+\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)\big)(\mu^{\prime})=(\omega^{\prime}+\varrho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})+([\tau,\varrho^{\prime}],0)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(\omega^{\prime}+\varrho^{\prime}+[\tau,\varrho^{\prime}],\chi^{\prime}).

By (
3
), we also get

[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]]=0,\quad[\tau,[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]]]=0,\quad[\tau,[\tau,D]]=0,\quad[\tau,[\tau,K]]=0,

thus

\displaystyle[(\tau,0),d(\tau,0)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(\tau,0),([\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]+[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau],-[\tau,D]-[\tau,K])]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]]+[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],-[\tau,[\tau,D]]-[\tau,[\tau,K]])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle([\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],0),

and

\mathrm{ad}^{2}(\tau,0)(d(\tau,0))=[(\tau,0),[(\tau,0),d(\tau,0)]]=[(\tau,0),([\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],0)]=([\tau,[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]]],0)=0,

which implies that

\displaystyle\frac{{\rm{Id}}-e^{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}}{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}(d(\tau,0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{1}{(n+1)!}\mathrm{ad}^{n}(\tau,0)(d(\tau,0))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(d(\tau,0)+\frac{1}{2}[(\tau,0),d(\tau,0)]+\frac{1}{6}[(\tau,0),[(\tau,0),d(\tau,0)]]+\cdots\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\Big(([\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]+[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau],-[\tau,D]-[\tau,K])+\frac{1}{2}([\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],0)\Big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-([\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]+[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]+\frac{1}{2}[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],-[\tau,D]-[\tau,K]).

We can rewrite equation (
28
) and regroup the result according to the correspondening components, we obtain the following equations

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\omega=\omega^{\prime}+[\tau,\varrho^{\prime}]-[\pi_{\mathfrak{g}},\tau]-\frac{1}{2}[\tau,[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau]],

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g}\otimes\mathfrak{h},\mathfrak{h})

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\varrho=\varrho^{\prime}-[\pi_{\mathfrak{h}},\tau],

\displaystyle\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})

\displaystyle:

\displaystyle\chi=\chi^{\prime}+[\tau,D]+[\tau,K].

The above three equations give us the equalities (
6
) (
5
) and (
7
) respectively. Hence, two non-abelian 2-cocycles
(\varrho,\omega,\chi)
and
(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
are equivalent through
\tau\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})
.

If
(\varrho,\omega,\chi)
and
(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
are two equivalent non-abelian 2-cocycles, i.e., there exists a linear map
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
, such that the equations (
5
), (
6
) and (
7
) are satisfied. Then by direct calculation, one can see that
\mu:=(\omega+\varrho,\chi)
and
\mu^{\prime}:=(\omega^{\prime}+\varrho^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
are equivalent through
e^{\mathrm{ad}(\tau,0)}
, which means that
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}})}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})\neq\emptyset
.
Therefore,
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}})}(\mu,\mu^{\prime})
is non-empty iff
[\mu]=[\mu^{\prime}]
, which implies that

\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Del}(\mathcal{L}^{\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h}}))\cong H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)\cong\mathbf{Ext_{nab}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K).

We finish the proof.
∎

4.
Non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and the homotopy category of strict Lie 2-algebras with strict derivations

In this section, we investigate the relationship between non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and hHom sets between strict Lie 2-algebras with strict derivations. We prove that
(\mathfrak{g},D)\mapsto{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
is a representable functor in a suitable category. We recall some definitions of strict Lie
2
-algebras firstly.

Definition 4.1
.

(
[
3
]
)

A
strict Lie 2-algebra
is a graded vector space
\mathfrak{g}=\mathfrak{g}_{0}\oplus\mathfrak{g}_{1}
with the following maps

•

a linear map
d_{\mathfrak{g}}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{0}
,

•

skew-symmetric bilinear maps
l_{2}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\wedge\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{0}
and
\mathfrak{g}_{0}\wedge\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, also denoted as
[\cdot,\cdot]
,

which satisfy

d_{\mathfrak{g}}([x,a])=[x,d_{\mathfrak{g}}(a)],\quad[d_{\mathfrak{g}}(a),b]=[a,d_{\mathfrak{g}}(b)],

[x,[y,z]]+[y,[z,x]]+[z,[x,y]]=0,\quad[x,[y,a]]+[y,[a,x]]+[a,[x,y]]=0,

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},\ a,b\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
. We also denote a strict Lie 2-algebra as a quadruple
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])
.

Definition 4.2
.

(
[
3
]
)

Let
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
\mathfrak{h}=(\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{h}})
be two strict Lie 2-algebras. A
homomorphism

\phi:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
is a triple
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
of linear maps where
\phi_{0}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}_{0},\ \phi_{1}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}_{1}
and
\phi_{2}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}_{1}
, such that

(29)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\phi_{1},

(30)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\phi_{0}(x),\phi_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{h}}(\phi_{2}(x,y)),

(31)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}([x,a]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\phi_{0}(x),\phi_{1}(a)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\phi_{2}(x,d_{\mathfrak{g}}(a)),

(32)

\displaystyle[\phi_{0}(x),\phi_{2}(y,z)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi_{0}(y),\phi_{2}(z,x)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle[\phi_{0}(z),\phi_{2}(x,y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=\phi_{2}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}},z)

\displaystyle+

\displaystyle\phi_{2}([y,z]_{\mathfrak{g}},x)+\phi_{2}([z,x]_{\mathfrak{g}},y),

for all
x,y,z\in\mathfrak{g},\ a\in\mathfrak{h}
.

Example 4.3
.

Given a Lie algebra
(\mathfrak{g},[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we have the following strict Lie 2-algebras.

(1)
Let
(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])=(\mathfrak{g},0,0,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
. Then
(\mathfrak{g},0,0,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
is a strict Lie 2-algebra.

(2)
Let
\mathfrak{g}_{0}=\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),\ \mathfrak{g}_{1}=\mathfrak{g}
and

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathrm{ad}

\displaystyle:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{g}}(x)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{ad}(x),

\displaystyle[\cdot,\cdot]

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\wedge\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),

\displaystyle[D,D^{\prime}]

\displaystyle=D\circ D^{\prime}-D^{\prime}\circ D,

\displaystyle[\cdot,\cdot]

\displaystyle:\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})\wedge\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g},

\displaystyle[D,x]

\displaystyle=D(x),

for all
D,D^{\prime}\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus
(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),\mathfrak{g},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a strict Lie 2-algebra.

Definition 4.4
.

(
[
13
]
)

Let
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g}_{0},\mathfrak{g}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{g}},[\cdot,\cdot])
be a strict Lie 2-algebra. A
strict derivation
on
\mathfrak{g}
is a pair of linear maps
\mathcal{D}=(\mathcal{D}_{0},\mathcal{D}_{1})
, where
\mathcal{D}_{0}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{0},\ \mathcal{D}_{1}:\mathfrak{g}_{1}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}_{1}
, such that

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{g}}\circ\mathcal{D}_{1},

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}([x,y])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{D}_{0}(x),y]+[x,\mathcal{D}_{0}(y)],

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{1}([x,a])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{D}_{0}(x),a]+[x,\mathcal{D}_{1}(a)],

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0},\ a\in\mathfrak{g}_{1}
.
We denote a strict Lie 2-algebra with a strict derivation by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D})
, called a
Lie2Der
pair.

Definition 4.5
.

(
[
14
]
)

A
homomorphism

(\phi,\theta_{\phi}):(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D})\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{K})
between two
Lie2Der
pairs is a Lie 2-algebra homomorphism
\phi=(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2}):\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
with a linear map
\theta_{\phi}:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}_{1}
such that

(33)

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\circ\mathcal{D}_{0}-\mathcal{K}_{0}\circ\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\theta_{\phi},

(34)

\displaystyle\phi_{1}\circ\mathcal{D}_{1}-\mathcal{K}_{1}\circ\phi_{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\phi}\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}},

(35)

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{1}(\phi_{2}(x,y))-\phi_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{0}x,y)-\phi_{2}(x,\mathcal{D}_{0}y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\theta_{\phi}(x),\phi_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+[\phi_{0}(x),\theta_{\phi}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\theta_{\phi}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
.

Definition 4.6
.

(
[
14
]
)

Let
(\phi,\theta_{\phi}),(\psi,\theta_{\psi}):(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D})\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{h},\mathcal{K})
be two homomorphisms of
Lie2Der
pairs. A
2-homomorphism

\vartheta:(\phi,\theta_{\phi})\Longrightarrow(\psi,\theta_{\psi})
is a linear map
\vartheta:\mathfrak{g}_{0}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}_{1}
satisfying

(36)

\displaystyle\psi_{0}-\phi_{0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\vartheta,\quad\psi_{1}-\phi_{1}=\vartheta\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}},

(37)

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x,y)-\phi_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vartheta([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\phi_{0}(x),\vartheta(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[\vartheta(x),\psi_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}},

(38)

\displaystyle\vartheta\circ\mathcal{D}_{0}-\mathcal{K}_{1}\circ\vartheta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\psi}-\theta_{\phi}.

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}_{0}
. We call two homomorphisms are
homotopic
if there exists a 2-homomorphism between them.

Clearly, the homotopy relation between homomorphisms of Lie2Der pairs is an equivalence relation and compositible with composition of homomorphisms. This allows us to define the homotopy category of the category of Lie2Der pairs. See
[
16
]
for more details of the homotopy category of a category.

Definition 4.7
.

Denote by
\mathtt{LD}
the category of
Lie2Der
pairs, and denote by
\mathtt{hLD}
the corresponding homotopy category.

Example 4.8
.

Given a
LieDer
pair
(\mathfrak{g},D)
, by direct calculation,
(D,0)
is a strict derivation on
(\mathfrak{g},0,0,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
, we denote this
Lie2Der
pair by
(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D})
.

Let
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be a LieDer pair. By Example
4.3
,
(\mathfrak{h}_{0},\mathfrak{h}_{1},d_{\mathfrak{h}},[\cdot,\cdot])=(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])
is a strict Lie 2-algebra. Define
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}=(\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}):(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])\,\rightarrow\,(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])
by
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}=\mathrm{ad}K,~~\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}=K
, then we have

\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}\circ\mathrm{ad}(x)=\mathrm{ad}K(\mathrm{ad}(x))=[K,\mathrm{ad}(x)]=\mathrm{ad}(K(x))=\mathrm{ad}\circ\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}(x),

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}([K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[K,[K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}]]=[[K,K^{\prime}],K^{\prime\prime}]+[K,[K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}]]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}(K^{\prime}),K^{\prime\prime}]+[K^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}(K^{\prime\prime})],

and

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}([K^{\prime},x])

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(K^{\prime}(x))=[K,K^{\prime}](x)+K^{\prime}(K(x))=[[K,K^{\prime}],x]+[K^{\prime},K(x)]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}(K^{\prime}),x]+[K^{\prime},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}(x)]

for all
K^{\prime},K^{\prime\prime}\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
and
x\in\mathfrak{h}
.
Thus, we have the following result.

Proposition 4.9
.

With the above notations,
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}=(\mathrm{ad}K,K)
is a strict derivation on

(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])
. Denote this
Lie2Der
pair by
(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}})
.

By Theorem
2.7
, non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
are classified by the second non-abelian cohomology
H_{nab}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
. At the end of this section, we will show the relationship between non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and hHom sets between strict Lie 2-algebras with strict derivations via
H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
.

Proposition 4.10
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be two
LieDer
pairs. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between the set of non-abelian 2-cocycles
Z^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
and
\rm{Hom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}))
.

Proof.

Suppose
(\varrho,\omega,\chi)\in Z^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
, define
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\theta_{\phi})=(\varrho,0,-\omega,-\chi)
, we have

\phi_{0}\circ d_{\mathfrak{g}}=\phi_{0}\circ 0=0\circ\mathrm{ad}=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\phi_{1}.

Thus, (
29
) is satisfied. By (
2
), we obtain

\displaystyle\phi_{0}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\phi_{0}(x),\phi_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\varrho(x),\varrho(y)]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}=\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-\varrho(x)\varrho(y)+\varrho(y)\varrho(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{ad}\omega(x,y)=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\phi_{2}(x,y),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, which means that (
30
) is satisfied. By the fact that
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi_{0}
are zero maps, equations (
31
) and (
32
) are satisfied automatically. Therefore, the triple
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
is a Lie 2-algebra homomorphism between
\mathfrak{g}=(\mathfrak{g},0,0,[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathfrak{g}})
and
(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h}),\mathfrak{h},\mathrm{ad},[\cdot,\cdot])
.

By equation (
3
), we have

\displaystyle\phi_{0}\circ\mathcal{D}_{0}(x)-\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},0}\circ\phi_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho(D(x))-[K,\varrho(x)]=\varrho(D(x))-K\circ\varrho(x)+\varrho(x)\circ K

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{ad}\chi(x)=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\theta_{\mathfrak{h}}(x),

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus equation (
33
) is satisfied. Also by the fact that the maps
d_{\mathfrak{g}}
and
\phi_{0}
are zeros, the condition (
34

4

\displaystyle\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der},1}(\phi_{2}(x,y))-\phi_{2}(\mathcal{D}_{0}(x),y)-\phi_{2}(x,\mathcal{D}_{0}(y))-[\theta_{\phi}(x),\phi_{0}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-[\phi_{0}(x),\theta_{\phi}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}+\theta_{\phi}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(-\omega(x,y))-(-\omega(D(x),y))-(-\omega(x,D(y)))-[-\chi(x),\varrho(y)]-[\varrho(x),-\chi(y)]+(-\chi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}))

\displaystyle-\big(K\omega(x,y)-\omega(D(x),y)-\omega(x,D(y))-[\chi(x),\varrho(y)]-[\varrho(x),\chi(y)]+\chi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\big(K\omega(x,y)-\omega(D(x),y)-\omega(x,D(y))+\varrho(y)\chi(x)-\varrho(x)\chi(y)+\chi([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})\big)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, which means that (
35

(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\theta_{\phi})=(\varrho,0,-\omega,-\chi):(\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D})\,\rightarrow\,(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}})

Conversely, given a homomorphism

. Similarly as above, by direct calculation, one can check that

is a non-abelian 2-cocycle of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
with values in
(\mathfrak{h},K)
.

The two processes mentioned above are inverse to each other, in other words, we obtain a one-to-one correspondence between
Z^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
and
\mathrm{Hom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}))
.
∎

Theorem 4.11
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be two
LieDer
pairs. Then there is a one-to-one correspondence between
H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
and
\rm{hHom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}))

{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)\cong\rm{hHom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}})).

Proof.

Suppose that
(\varrho,\omega,\chi)
and
(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
are two equivalent non-abelian 2-cocycles, and the equivalence is given by
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}

4.10
, the maps
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\theta_{\phi})=(\varrho,0,-\omega,-\chi)

(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\theta_{\psi})=(\varrho^{\prime},0,-\omega^{\prime},-\chi^{\prime})

5

\psi_{0}(x)-\phi_{0}(x)=\varrho(x)-\varrho^{\prime}(x)=\mathrm{ad}\circ\tau(x)=d_{\mathfrak{h}}\circ\vartheta(x),

for all

, thus the first equation of (
36
) holds. The second of (
36
) holds automatically as
d_{\mathfrak{g}},\psi_{1}
and
\phi_{1}
are zero maps. By equation (
6

\displaystyle\psi_{2}(x,y)-\phi_{2}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(-\omega)(x,y)-(-\omega^{\prime})(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\varrho^{\prime}(x)\tau(y)-\varrho^{\prime}(y)\tau(x)+[\tau(x),\tau(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\tau([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\vartheta([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})-[\phi_{0}(x),\vartheta(y)]-[\vartheta(x),\psi_{0}(y)],

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, which means that equation (
37

(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2})
and
(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\psi_{2})
are homotopic as homomorphisms of Lie 2-algebras. By (
7

\displaystyle\vartheta\circ\mathcal{D}_{0}-\mathcal{K}_{1}\circ\vartheta

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tau\circ D-K\circ\tau=(-\chi^{\prime})-(-\chi)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\theta_{\psi}-\theta_{\phi}.

Hence
\vartheta=\tau
is a 2-homomorphism from
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\theta_{\phi})
to

(\psi_{0},\psi_{1},\psi_{2},\theta_{\psi})
. In other words,
(\phi_{0},\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\theta_{\phi})
and

H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})\cong\rm{hHom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}))
. By Theorem
2.7
,

{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)\cong\rm{hHom}_{\mathtt{LD}}((\mathfrak{g},\mathcal{D}),(\mathfrak{h}_{\mathrm{Der}},\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{Der}}))
.
∎

According to Proposition
4.10
and Theorem
4.11
, we have the following result.

Theorem 4.12
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be two
LieDer

\mathtt{LieDer}
the category of

\mathtt{LieDer}\,\rightarrow\,\mathtt{Set},\quad(\mathfrak{g},D)\mapsto{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K),

of

5.
Non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and

-kernels

In this section, we introduce the notion of the

-kernel, which is used to construct the cohomology groups of LieDer pairs, and then employed to study the non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs. We recall the representation and cohomology of LieDer pairs firstly.

Definition 5.1
.

(
[
25
]
)

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
be a
LieDer
pair. A
representation
of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
on a vector space
V

(\rho,T)
, where
\rho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(V)
is a representation of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
on the vector space
V
and
T\in\mathfrak{gl}(V)
such that the following equation holds:

(39)

T(\rho(x)u)=\rho(D(x))u+\rho(x)T(u),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},u\in V.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
be a LieDer pair and
(\rho,T)
be a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
on a vector space
V
. Denote by
\mathrm{d}_{\rho}^{CE}:\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n+1}\mathfrak{g},V)
the Chevalley-Eilenberg coboundary operator of the Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
with coefficients in the representation
(V,\rho)
. Define the
1
-cochains
C^{1}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},V)
to be
\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},V)
. For
n\geq 2
, define the space of
n
-cochains

C^{n}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},V)=\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)\oplus\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},V).

For
n\geq 1
, define an operator
\delta:C^{n}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},V)\,\rightarrow\,C^{n}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},V)
by

(40)

\delta(\omega_{n})=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\omega_{n}\circ({\rm{Id}}\otimes\cdots\otimes D\otimes\cdots\otimes{\rm{Id}})-T\circ\omega_{n}.

For
n=1
, define the coboundary operator
\partial_{\rho}:C^{1}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g};V)\,\rightarrow\,C^{2}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g};V)
by

\partial_{\rho}(\omega_{1})=(\mathrm{d}_{\rho}^{CE}\omega_{1},-\delta(\omega_{1})).

For
n\geq 2
, define the coboundary operator
\partial_{\rho}:C^{n}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g};V)\,\rightarrow\,C^{n+1}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g};V)
by

(41)

\partial_{\rho}(\omega_{n},\chi_{n-1})=\Big(\mathrm{d}_{\rho}^{CE}\omega_{n},\mathrm{d}_{\rho}^{CE}\chi_{n-1}+(-1)^{n}\delta(\omega_{n})\Big)

where
\omega_{n}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n}\mathfrak{g},V)
and
\chi_{n-1}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{n-1}\mathfrak{g},V)
. Then
(C^{*}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g};V),\partial_{\rho})
is a complex

25
]
.

.

(

]

5.1.
The relation between non-abelian extensions of LieDer pairs and
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels

Let
\mathfrak{g}
be a Lie algebra. Denote by
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})=\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})/\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
the set of outer derivations of
\mathfrak{g}
, then
(\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})})
is a Lie algebra as
\mathrm{ad}(\mathfrak{g})
is the ideal of
(\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g}),[\cdot,\cdot]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})})
. Assume
D\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
, denote by
\bar{D}
the equivalence class of
D
in
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
.

Definition 5.3
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be two
LieDer
pairs. A
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel
for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
is a Lie algebra homomorphism
\mathfrak{k}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
such that

(42)

[\overline{K},\mathfrak{k}(x)]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})}=\mathfrak{k}(D(x)),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\overline{K}
is the outer derivation induced by
K
.

Denote by
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
the center of a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{h}
. It is known that any derivation
K:\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
preserves
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
. Let
\nu\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
and choose
T\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
such that
\overline{T}=\nu
. Then the linear map
\widetilde{\nu}:\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
given by

\widetilde{\nu}(u)=T(u),\quad~~\forall~~u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),

is independent on the choice of
T
. Therefore, without ambiguity,
\nu\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
can be regarded as a linear map on
\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
.
Given a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel
\mathfrak{k}
for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
, then
\mathfrak{k}(x)\in\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
, which means that we can obtain a linear map
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
given by

\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)(u)=\mathfrak{k}(x)(u),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},\ u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}).

Proposition 5.4
.

With the above notations,
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
on
(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)
. The representation
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}
is called the induced representation of the
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel
\mathfrak{k}
.

Proof.

Since

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}([x.y]_{\mathfrak{g}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathfrak{k}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})(u)=[\mathfrak{k}(x),\mathfrak{k}(y)]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})}(u)=\mathfrak{k}(x)(\mathfrak{k}(y)(u))-\mathfrak{k}(y)(\mathfrak{k}(x)(u))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)(\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(y)u)-\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(y)(\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)u),

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},\ u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
, then
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a Lie algebra representation. By equation (
42
), we have

\displaystyle[\overline{K},\mathfrak{k}(x)]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})}(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)u)-\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)K(u)=\mathfrak{k}(D(x))(u)=\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(D(x))u,

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g},\ u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
. Thus
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
on
(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)
.
∎

Let
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
be a non-abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
. By Theorem
2.7
, there is a
[(\varrho,\omega,\chi)]\in H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
corresponding
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
. For the linear map
\varrho:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
, define a linear map
\upsilon:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
by

\upsilon(x)=\overline{\varrho(x)},\quad~~\forall x\in\mathfrak{g},

where
\overline{\varrho(x)}
the equivalence class of
\varrho(x)
in
\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{g})
. Moreover, if
[(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})]=[(\varrho,\omega,\chi)]
, then there exists a linear map
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that
\varrho-\varrho^{\prime}=\mathrm{ad}\tau
, which implies that
\overline{\varrho(x)}=\overline{\varrho^{\prime}(x)}
for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, the linear map
\upsilon:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
is well defined.

Proposition 5.5
.

With the above notations, the map
\upsilon:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
is a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
. Moreover, if
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
and
(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})
are two isomorphic non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
, then the corresponding
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
are the same.

Proof.

By (
2.5
), (
2
) and (
2
), we obtain the following equations

\displaystyle\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})=[\varrho(x),\varrho(y)]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})}-\mathrm{ad}\omega(x,y),\quad\text{and}\quad K\circ\varrho(x)=\varrho(Dx)+\varrho(x)\circ K+\mathrm{ad}\chi(x),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, we have

\displaystyle\upsilon([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})}=\overline{[\varrho(x),\varrho(y)]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}-\mathrm{ad}\omega(x,y)}=\overline{[\varrho(x),\varrho(y)]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}}=[\overline{\varrho(x)},\overline{\varrho(y)}]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\upsilon(x),\upsilon(y)]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})},

and

\displaystyle\upsilon(D(x))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\varrho(D(x))}=\overline{K\circ\varrho(x)-\varrho(x)\circ K+\mathrm{ad}\chi(x)}=\overline{K\circ\varrho(x)-\varrho(x)\circ K}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{K}\circ\upsilon(x)-\upsilon(x)\circ\overline{K}=[\overline{K},\upsilon(x)]_{\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})},

which means that
\upsilon
is a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
.

Denote by
(\varrho,\omega,\chi)
and
(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})
the corresponding non-abelian 2-cocycles of
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
and
(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})
respectively. Then there exists a linear map
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

\varrho^{\prime}(x)-\varrho(x)=\mathrm{ad}\tau(x),

which means

\overline{\varrho^{\prime}(x)}=\overline{\varrho(x)},

for all
x\in\mathfrak{g}
. Thus, the corresponding
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels are the same.
∎

For a given
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel
\mathfrak{k}
for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
, denote by
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
the set of isomorphic classes of non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
such that
\mathfrak{k}
is the corresponding
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel. The set
{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
of all isomorphic classes of non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
is the family of
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
parametrized by
\mathfrak{k}
. Hence, the classification of all non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
can be reduced to the following two questions.

Question 5.6
.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)
be
LieDer
pairs.

•

(i)
:
For a fixed
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel
\mathfrak{k}
for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
, give a parameterization of
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

•

(ii)
:
Find a method to decide for which
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels the set
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
is nonempty.

Definition 5.7
.

Let
\mathfrak{k}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
be a
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
. We say that
\mathfrak{k}
is
integrable
if
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}\neq\emptyset
.

Let
\mathfrak{k}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Out}(\mathfrak{h})
be an integrable
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel for
(\mathfrak{h},K)
. By Proposition
5.4
, we have that
\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a representation of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
on
(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)
, where

\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}(x)u=\mathfrak{k}(x)u,\quad\forall u\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),~~x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Denote by
H_{{\rm LieDer}}^{n}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
the
n
-th cohomology group of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
with coefficients in the representation
(\rho_{\mathfrak{k}},K)
. Therefore, for the first problem, we have the following answer.

Theorem 5.8
.

With the above notations, the set
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
is isomorphic to
H^{2}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
. Moreover, we have

{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)=\bigsqcup_{\text{Integrable}~~(\mathfrak{g},D)\text{-kernels}}H^{2}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}.

Proof.

Fixing an element
[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})]
in
\rm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
, it means that
[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})]
is the isomorphic class of
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
, where
(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})
is a non-abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)

such that
\mathfrak{k}
is the corresponding
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernel.
By Theorem
2.7
, we know that the non-abelian extensions of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
are classified by the second non-abelian cohomology
H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
. Denote by
[(\varrho,\omega,\chi)]
the element in
H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
corresponding to
[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})]
. Let
[(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})]\in{\rm Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
and
[(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})]\neq[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})]
. Denote by
[(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\chi})]
the element in
H^{2}_{nab}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)
corresponding to
[(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})]
. Then
\overline{\varrho}=\overline{\tilde{\varrho}}=\mathfrak{k}
, which means that there exists a linear map
\tau:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

\varrho(x)-\tilde{\varrho}(x)=\mathrm{ad}\tau(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Define linear maps
\omega^{*}:\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
and
\chi^{*}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
by

(43)

\displaystyle\omega^{*}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}(x,y)+\tilde{\varrho}(x)\tau(y)-\tilde{\varrho}(y)\tau(x)+[\tau(x),\tau(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\tau([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

(44)

\displaystyle\chi^{*}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}(x)+K(\tau(x))-\tau(D(x)),

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
. Then we have
[(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\chi})]=[(\varrho,\omega^{*},\chi^{*})]
. By (
1
) and (
3
), it implies that

[\omega(x,y)-\omega^{*}(x,y),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,

and

[\chi(x)-\chi^{*}(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}=0,

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}
,
which means that the map
\eta=\omega-\omega^{*}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\wedge^{2}\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
and
\theta=\chi-\chi^{*}\in\mathrm{Hom}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
. By (
2
) and (
4
), we have

\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\eta)=0,\quad\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\theta)+\delta(\eta)=0,

thus,
\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\eta,\theta)=0
, i.e.,
(\eta,\theta)
is a
2
-cocycle of the complex
(C^{*}_{\mathrm{LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})),\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}})
.

If
[(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{\prime},\chi^{\prime})]=[(\varrho,\omega,\chi)]
and
[(\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime},\tilde{\omega}^{\prime},\tilde{\chi}^{\prime})]=[(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\chi})]
such that
\overline{\varrho}=\overline{\varrho^{\prime}}=\overline{\tilde{\varrho}}=\overline{\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{k}
, there exists linear maps
\tau^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h},\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}

and
\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varrho(x)u-\varrho^{\prime}(x)u&=&[\tau^{\prime}(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}},\\
\omega(x,y)-\omega^{\prime}(x,y)&=&\varrho^{\prime}(x)\tau^{\prime}(y)-\varrho^{\prime}(y)\tau^{\prime}(x)+[\tau^{\prime}(x),\tau^{\prime}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\tau^{\prime}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\\
\chi(x)-\chi^{\prime}(x)&=&K(\tau^{\prime}(x))-\tau^{\prime}(D(x)),\end{array}\right.

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\tilde{\varrho}(x)u-\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(x)u&=&[\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}},\\
\tilde{\omega}(x,y)-\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(x,y)&=&\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(x)\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(y)-\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(y)\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(x)+[\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(x),\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\\
\tilde{\chi}(x)-\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}(x)&=&K(\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(x))-\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}(D(x)),\end{array}\right.

for all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g},u\in\mathfrak{h}
, and

\varrho^{\prime}(x)-\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(x)=\mathrm{ad}\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x),\quad\forall x\in\mathfrak{g}.

Then
[(\varrho^{\prime},\omega^{*}{{}^{\prime}},\chi^{*}{{}^{\prime}})]=[(\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime},\tilde{\omega}^{\prime},\tilde{\chi}^{\prime})]
, where

(47)

\displaystyle\omega^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\omega}^{\prime}(x,y)+\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(x)\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(y)-\tilde{\varrho}^{\prime}(y)\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x)+[\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x),\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),

(48)

\displaystyle\chi^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\tilde{\chi}^{\prime}(x)+K(\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x))-\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(D(x)).

Moreover, the maps
\eta^{\prime}=\omega^{\prime}-\omega^{*}{{}^{\prime}}
and
\theta^{\prime}=\chi^{\prime}-\chi^{*}{{}^{\prime}}
satisfy
\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\eta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=0
. By (
43
), (
44
), (
47
) and (
48
), it implies that

\displaystyle(\eta-\eta^{\prime})(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\omega(x,y)-\omega^{\prime}(x,y)-\omega^{*}(x,y)+\omega^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x,y)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\tau^{\prime}+\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}-\tilde{\tau}{{}^{\prime}}-\tau)(x,y),

and

\displaystyle(\theta-\theta^{\prime})(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\chi(x)-\chi^{\prime}(x)-\chi^{*}(x)+\chi^{*}{{}^{\prime}}(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\delta(\tau^{\prime}+\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}-\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}-\tau)(x),

which means that
(\eta,\theta)-(\eta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})=\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\tau^{\prime}+\tau^{*}{{}^{\prime}}-\tilde{\tau}^{\prime}-\tau)
, i.e.,
[(\eta,\theta)]=[(\eta^{\prime},\theta^{\prime})]\in H^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))_{\mathfrak{k}}
. Therefore the map

\Xi:{\rm Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}\,\rightarrow\,H_{{\rm LieDer}}^{2}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
defined by

\Xi([(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})])=[(\eta,\theta)],

is well defined, and
\Xi([(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})])=0
.

Assume that
\Xi([(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})])=0,
there exists a linear map
l:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})
such that
\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(l)=(\eta,\theta)
. Then

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\varrho(x)u-\tilde{\varrho}(x)u&=&[\tau(x)+l(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}},\\
\omega(x,y)-\tilde{\omega}(x,y)&=&\tilde{\varrho}(x)(\tau+l)(y)-\tilde{\varrho}(y)(\tau+l)(x)+[(\tau+l)(x),(\tau+l)(y)]_{\mathfrak{h}}-(l+\tau)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}),\\
\chi(x)-\tilde{\chi}(x)&=&K((\tau+l)(x))-(\tau+l)(D(x)),\end{array}\right.

which means that
[(\varrho,\omega,\chi)]=[(\tilde{\varrho},\tilde{\omega},\tilde{\chi})]
, i.e.,
[(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D})]=[(\hat{\mathfrak{g}},\hat{D})]
. Thus
\Xi
is injective.

For any
[(\eta,\theta)]\in H^{2}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
, i.e.,
\partial_{\rho_{\mathfrak{k}}}(\eta,\theta)=0
. By direct calculation,
(\varrho,\omega-\eta,\chi-\theta)
is a non-abelian
2
-cocycle, denote by
(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},\bar{D})
the non-abelian extension of
(\mathfrak{g},D)
by
(\mathfrak{h},K)
corresponding to
(\varrho,\omega-\eta,\chi-\theta)
. We have
\Xi([(\bar{\mathfrak{g}},\bar{D})])=[(\eta,\theta)]
, which means that
\Xi
is surjective. Thus,
the set
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
is isomorphic to
H^{2}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{g}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
, which implies

{\rm{\bf Ext_{nab}}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)=\bigsqcup_{\text{Integrable}(\mathfrak{g},D)\text{-kernels}}H^{2}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}.

We finish the proof.
∎

Remark 5.9
.

By the above Theorem, we see that if
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
is nonempty, then
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{h},K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
is an affine space with the associated vector space
H^{2}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))_{\mathfrak{k}}
.

5.2.
Integrable
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels and third cohomology groups

In this subsection, we investigate the second question in Question
5.6
: Find a method to decide for which
(\mathfrak{g},D)
-kernels the set
\mathrm{Ext}(\mathfrak{g},\mathfrak{h})_{\mathfrak{k}}
is nonempty.

Let
(\mathfrak{g},D)
and
(\mathfrak{h},K)

\displaystyle=

Definition 5.10

6.
Applications: Extensibility of derivations of Lie algebras and the obstruction class

In this section, we use the non-abelian extensions of Lie algebras with derivations to study the following question:

•

Given a non-abelian extension of Lie algebras
0\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,0
, let
(K,D)\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
be a pair of derivations of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
respectively. When does there exist a derivation
\hat{D}
of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\hat{D}|_{\mathfrak{h}}=K,\quad\text{and}\quad D\circ p=p\circ\hat{D}.

If such a
\hat{D}
exists, we say that the derivations
(K,D)
of
\mathfrak{h}
and
\mathfrak{g}
is
extensible
.

Let
0\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}\,\rightarrow\,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,0
be a non-abelian extension of
\mathfrak{g}
by
\mathfrak{h}
. Denote by
\mathrm{Der}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
the set of all the derivations of
\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
that preserve
\mathfrak{h}
i.e., those
\hat{D}\in\mathrm{Der}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})
for which
\hat{D}|_{\mathfrak{h}}\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})
.
Choose a section
s:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
of the non-abelian extension, then we can define a map

\Gamma:\mathrm{Der}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{g})
by

\Gamma(\hat{D})=(\hat{D}|_{\mathfrak{h}},p\circ\hat{D}\circ s),\quad\forall~~\hat{D}\in\mathrm{Der}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}}),

where
p:\hat{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is the projection. Note that for two different sections
s:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
and
s^{\prime}:\mathfrak{g}\,\rightarrow\,\hat{\mathfrak{g}}
, we have

p\circ\hat{D}\circ(s-s^{\prime})=0,

then the definition of
\Gamma
is independent on the choice of the section
s
. Denote by Im
(\Gamma)
the image of
\Gamma
.

Proposition 6.1
.

With the above notations, we have that
Im
(\Gamma)\subset\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
and
\Gamma
is a Lie algebra homomorphism. In other words,
\Gamma:\mathrm{Der}_{\mathfrak{h}}(\hat{\mathfrak{g}})\,\rightarrow\,\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})\times\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
is a Lie algebra homomorphism.

Proof.

For all
x,y\in\mathfrak{g}
, since

\displaystyle[(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)(x),y]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[x,(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)(x),(p\circ s)(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[(p\circ s)(x),(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}-(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p([\hat{D}(s(x)),s(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}}+[s(x),\hat{D}(s(y))]_{\mathfrak{g}})-(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s)([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p(\hat{D}[(s(x)),s(y)]_{\mathfrak{g}})-p\hat{D}(s([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle p\hat{D}([s(x),s(y)]_{\hat{\mathfrak{g}}}-s([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}}))=0,

then
p\circ\hat{D}\circ s\in\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{g})
.

By the fact that,

p\big(\hat{D}(sx)-s(p\circ\hat{D}\circ s(x))\big)=p\hat{D}s(x)-ps(p\hat{D}s(x))=p\hat{D}s(x)-p\hat{D}s(x)=0,

i.e.,
\mathrm{Im}\big(({\rm{Id}}-s\circ p)\circ\hat{D}\circ s\big)\in\mathfrak{h}

for all

Denote by
\tilde{\omega}=q^{*}(\omega)
and
\tilde{\chi}=q^{*}(\chi)
, by direct calculation we have

q^{*}(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\omega)=\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega},\quad\quad q^{*}(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\chi+\delta(\omega))=\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}),

where
\delta
given by (
40
).

If we want to prove that
(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\omega,\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\chi+\delta\omega)
is a 3-cocycle, it suffices to show that the pullback
q^{*}(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\omega,\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\chi+\delta\omega)=(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega},\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}))\in C^{3}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
is a
3
-cocycle as
q:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{g}
is surjective.

Since

\overline{\tilde{\varrho}(x,K^{\prime})-\tilde{\rho}(x,K^{\prime})}=\overline{\varrho(x)-K^{\prime}}=\mathfrak{k}(x)-\overline{K^{\prime}}=0,\quad\forall(x,K^{\prime})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},

there exists a linear map
\tilde{\varepsilon}:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{h}
such that

(64)

\tilde{\varrho}=\tilde{\rho}+\mathrm{ad}\circ\tilde{\varepsilon}:\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}\,\rightarrow\,\mathfrak{gl}(\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h})).

For any
(x,K^{\prime})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}
and
u\in\mathfrak{h}
, we have

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\chi}(x,K^{\prime})\Big)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\chi(x),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}=K\Big(\tilde{\varrho}(x,K^{\prime})u\Big)-\tilde{\varrho}(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))u-\tilde{\varrho}(x,K^{\prime})K(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K\Big((\tilde{\rho}+\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon})(x,K^{\prime})u\Big)-\Big((\tilde{\rho}+\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon})(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))\Big)u-(\tilde{\rho}+\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon})(x,K^{\prime})K(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K\Big(\tilde{\rho}(x,K^{\prime})\Big)u-\tilde{\rho}(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))u-\tilde{\rho}(x,K^{\prime})K(u)

\displaystyle+K(\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime})u)-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))u-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime})K(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle K(\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime})u)-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))u-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime})K(u)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}K(\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime}))u-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(\tilde{D}(x,K^{\prime}))u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\mathrm{ad}\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime}))u.

Then
\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon})\in\mathrm{Hom}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
.
By (
64
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\omega}+[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\omega}]_{\smile},\quad\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}=\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\chi}+[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\chi}]_{\smile}.

Since

\displaystyle[\tilde{\omega}((x,K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime}))),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\omega(x,y),u]_{\mathfrak{h}}=[\varrho(x),\varrho(y)]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}(u)-\varrho([x,y]_{\mathfrak{g}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\varrho}(x,K^{\prime}),\tilde{\varrho}(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}(u)-\tilde{\varrho}([(x.K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle[\tilde{\rho}(x,K^{\prime})+\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime}),\tilde{\rho}(y,K^{\prime\prime})+\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\mathrm{Der}(\mathfrak{h})}(u)-\tilde{\rho}([(x.K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})u

\displaystyle-\mathrm{ad}\tilde{\varepsilon}([(x.K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{ad}\Big(\tilde{\rho}(x,K^{\prime})\tilde{\varepsilon}(y,K^{\prime\prime})-\tilde{\rho}(y,K^{\prime\prime})\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime})-\tilde{\varepsilon}([(x.K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\tilde{\mathfrak{g}}})+[\tilde{\varepsilon}(x,K^{\prime}),\tilde{\varepsilon}(y,K^{\prime\prime})]_{\mathfrak{h}}\Big)u

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\Big(\mathrm{ad}\circ(\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile})((x,K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime}))\Big)u,

for all
(x,K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime})\in\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},u\in\mathfrak{h}
, which implies that

\tilde{\omega}((x,K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime}))-(\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile})((x,K^{\prime}),(y,K^{\prime\prime}))\in\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}).

Furthermore, by (
54
) and
[[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile}=0
, we obtain

[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\omega}]_{\smile}=\big[\tilde{\varepsilon},\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\varepsilon}+\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile}\big]_{\smile}=[\tilde{\varepsilon},\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile}=-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile}.

Hence,
\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega}=\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\omega}+[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\omega}]_{\smile}=\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile}=\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}(\tilde{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile})
, which means that
\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega}
is a coboundary. We have
\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega})=0
, that is,
\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega}\in Z^{3}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}))
.

By equations (
55
) and (
57
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega})-[\tilde{\varepsilon},\delta(\tilde{\varepsilon})]_{\smile}=\mathrm{d}^{CE}_{\tilde{\rho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega})+[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\chi}]_{\smile}=\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}),

which means that
\partial_{\tilde{\rho}}(\tilde{\omega}-\frac{1}{2}[\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{\varepsilon}]_{\smile},\tilde{\chi})=(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega},\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}))
. Thus,
(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega},\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}))
is coboundary, which implies that
(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\omega},\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\tilde{\varrho}}\tilde{\chi}+\delta(\tilde{\omega}))\in Z^{3}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\tilde{\mathfrak{g}},\tilde{D};\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
. Thus
(\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\omega,\mathrm{d}^{F}_{\varrho}\chi+\delta(\omega))\in Z^{3}_{{\rm LieDer}}(\mathfrak{g},D;\mathfrak{z}(\mathfrak{h}),K)_{\mathfrak{k}}
. We finish the proof.
