Characterization of the complete chloroplast genome of Cyclea hypoglauca (Schauer) Diels, 1910 (Menispermaceae)
Authors: Bao-Ngoc Mach, Kanal Aing, Shadi Basiri, Haimei Chen, Liangqiang Huang, Xinmin Pan, Jingling Li
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractCyclea hypoglauca (Schauer) Diels, 1910, is a wild climber of the Menispermaceae family, which has been exploited in traditional medicine for centuries. This study aimed to characterize the chloroplast (cp) genome of C. hypoglauca and to improve understanding of phylogeny and evolution in Menispermaceae. The complete cp genome of C. hypoglauca is 159,596 bp in length and exhibits the typical quadripartite structure. In total, 129 genes were identified, including 84 protein-coding genes, 37 tRNAs, and eight rRNAs. These results provide the first complete cp genome of C. hypoglauca, contributing to comparative genomic and phylogenetic studies of Menispermaceae.Keywords: Cyclea hypoglauca, menispermaceae, chloroplast genome, phylogenetic analysis

Introduction

Cyclea hypoglauca (Schauer) Diels, 1910 (Figure 1), is a tropical perennial climbing vine in the family Menispermaceae. The plant is native to Southeast Asia, traditionally used as a medicinal plant in southern China and Vietnam, where it is commonly known as ‘fen ye lun huan teng’ and ‘sâm lá mốc’ (https://powo.science.kew.org/taxon/urn:lsid:ipni.org:names:26916-1). Despite its long history of use, only a few studies have evaluated the pharmaceutical properties and genetics of C. hypoglauca (Xu et al., 2022). At the generic level, Cyclea is one of the largest genera of Menispermaceae, with 31 accepted species mainly distributed in tropical Asia, yet studies on its genetics remain limited (Selvaraj et al., 2012; Valsan et al., 2025).

Over the past four decades, chloroplast genomes (cp genomes) have become a powerful tool for resolving land plant phylogeny and evolution because of their conserved structure and moderate substitution rates (Daniell et al., 2016). In the last twenty years, evolutionary and phylogenetic studies in Menispermaceae have relied mainly on a limited set of plastid markers such as matK, rbcL, and trnL, together with geometric morphometrics tools (Hoot et al., 2009; Wefferling et al., 2013). More recently, complete cp genomes have been reported for a few genera in this family, including Sinomenium, Menispermum, Tinospora, and Stephania (Chen et al., 2025; Song et al., 2023), but data for Cyclea remain under‑represented. Currently, only two Cyclea species have their complete cp genome sequences deposited in public databases (Sayers et al., 2022). This limits comparative analyses within the genus and hinders their inclusion in broader Menispermaceae studies. By characterizing the first complete cp genome of C. hypoglauca, this study aims to provide a foundation for future work on species identification, systematics, and the evolution in Menispermaceae.

Materials and methods

Plant material, DNA extraction, sequencing, and cp genome assembly

Fresh leaves of C. hypoglauca were sampled in Guangxi, China (24°00’53.0ʺN, 110°05′21.5ʺE). Voucher number Y072 can be used to track these specimens at the Institute of Medicinal Plant Development. Leaf samples were kept at 20 °C after being dehydrated by silica gel packets. Genomic DNA extraction was performed using the DNeasy Plant Mini Kit (Cat. 69104, Qiagen) following the manufacturer’s recommended protocol. The extracted total DNA was prepared for sequencing on the Illumina platform, utilizing the 150 bp paired-end mode. This process generated 49,821,698 paired‑end reads, corresponding to approximately 14.9 GB of data. The cp genome was assembled with GetOrganelle v1.7.7.1 (Jin et al., 2020), and assembly quality was evaluated by plotting sequencing depth across the cp genome. Gene annotation was executed by CPGAVAS2 (Shi et al., 2019) and GeSeq (Tillich et al., 2017), and tRNA genes were annotated by using tRNAscan-SE v2.0 (Chan et al., 2021). To illustrate the cp genome map of C. hypoglauca, the OGDRAW v1.3.1 (Greiner et al., 2019) was utilized. Genes containing introns were outlined using the CPGView (Liu et al., 2023).

Chloroplast genome structural variation assessment

Junction sites of the cp genomes were visualized using CPJSdraw v0.0.1 (Li et al., 2023) for comparison of gene distribution and regional size variation among eight Menispermaceae species. To evaluate cp genome structural variation among nine Ranunculales species, their complete cp genome sequences were aligned using the progressiveMAUVE (Darling et al., 2010) algorithm and alignment viewer with locally collinear block (LCB) computation and full alignment enabled.

Phylogenetic analysis

The verified, complete cp genome sequences of C. hypoglauca and 22 individuals from the Ranunculales order were obtained from the GenBank database (Table S1). Protein-coding genes (PCGs) shared by all taxa (78 genes) were extracted and concatenated by species in PhyloSuite (Zhang et al., 2020), resulting in one concatenated PCG sequence per taxon. The 22 concatenated sequences were aligned using the MAFFT alignment plugin v7.490 (Katoh & Standley, 2013) in PhyloSuite under default settings and poorly aligned regions were trimmed. The concatenated PCG alignment was treated as a single data partition and subjected to model selection in jModelTest v2.1.10 (Posada, 2008) to identify the optimal evolutionary model. Phylogenetic trees were reconstructed under the selected Transversion Model with Invariable Sites and Gamma-distributed rates (TVM+I + G) using both Bayesian Inference (BI) and Maximum Likelihood (ML). BI analyses were performed in MrBayes v3.2 (Ronquist et al., 2012) with two independent Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) runs of 1,000,000 generations each, sampling every 1,000 generations and discarding the first 25% of samples as burn‑in. Calculations were terminated when the standard deviation of split frequencies dropped below 0.001. ML analyses were conducted in IQ-TREE v2.4.0 (Minh et al., 2020) in ultrafast bootstrap mode with 1,000 bootstrap replicates to assess branch support. The phylogram, displaying Bayesian posterior probabilities (BPP) and bootstrap scores (BS) values from BI and ML, respectively, was visualized using FigTree v1.4.4 (https://github.com/rambaut/figtree/).

Results

The average coverage depth and the minimal depth were 1,850.28× and 534×, respectively (Figure S1), indicating sequencing data were qualified for cp genome assembly. The complete cp genome of C. hypoglauca is a circular DNA molecule of 159,596 bp in length, consisting of a Large Single-Copy (LSC) region (90,331 bp), a Small Single-Copy (SSC) region (20,483 bp), and two Inverted Repeat (IR) regions of 24,391 bp each (Figure 2). The overall mean GC content was 38%. A total of 129 genes were identified, including 113 unique genes: 79 PCGs, 30 tRNAs, and four rRNAs. Among them, 22 genes contained introns, comprising 21 cis-splicing genes and one trans-splicing gene (rps12). Of the cis-splicing genes, two PCGs and two tRNAs were duplicated in the IRs. The cp gene content of C. hypoglauca was described in Table S2, and the structure of cis- and trans-splicing PCGs was demonstrated in Figure S2. A comparison of junction sites among eight Ranunculales species showed conserved gene distribution and no significant IR expansion or contraction in C. hypoglauca (Figure S3). Whole cp genome alignment of nine Menispermaceae species revealed six LCBs, with no apparent deletion or inversion in the cp genome of C. hypoglauca (Figure S4). The BI and ML analyses produced consistent topologies, supporting a close relationship between Cyclea and Stephania, with most branches receiving high BPP and BS support (Figure 3).

Discussion and conclusion

This study is the first to characterize the complete cp genome of C. hypoglauca. Its genome size and regional structure fall within the typical range reported in Menispermaceae, with no major structural variation detected. The gene content comprises 79 PCGs, 30 tRNAs, and four rRNAs, similar to C. sutchuenensis and Stephania species (Wang et al., 2024). Phylogenetic analysis showed that Cyclea species formed a distinct clade with strong BPP and BS support and were most closely related to Stephania. This finding is in agreement with previous studies based on different lines of evidence, including morphological, fossil, and molecular data (Chen et al., 2025; Lian et al., 2025; Wang et al., 2024; Wefferling et al., 2013). These results expand genomic resources for Cyclea and Menispermaceae. However, additional genomic data is necessary to clarify phylogenetic relationships within Cyclea and to broaden our understanding of evolution in this genus.
