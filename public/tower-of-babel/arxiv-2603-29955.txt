Finiteness of Hadamard ranks
Dario Antolini, Edoardo Ballico, Alessandro Oneto

Abstract
. The Hadamard rank of a point with respect to a projective variety is, if it exists, the minimum number of points of the variety whose coordinate-wise product is the given point. We classify the projective varieties for which the Hadamard rank is finite for any point. As a by-product we obtain the finiteness of the Hadamard rank with respect to varieties of tensors, such as Grassmannians, Chow varieties, varieties of reducible forms and their secant varieties, complementing previous known results on secant varieties of Segre-Veronese varieties. We prove sharp upper bounds on the maximum Hadamard rank for certain families of algebraic varieties: this is a consequence of a result on the lower semi-continuity of the Hadamard rank for curves that do not contain points with at least two zero coordinates.

1.
Introduction

In
[
CMS10
]
, the authors introduced the definition of
Hadamard product
of algebraic varieties to study statistical models known as
Restricted Boltzmann Machines
by means of algebraic and tropical geometry. Such approach was continued and generalized in
[
CTY10
,
MM15
,
Mon18
]
.

Tensors encoding the joint probabilities that lie on Discrete Restricted Boltzmann Machines can be expressed as entry-wise product of low-rank tensors. This interpretation has been considered in
[
SM18
,
OV26
]
. A general geometric framework for such decompositions of tensors was formalized in
[
AMO25
]
where the definition of
Hadamard ranks
was introduced with respect to any algebraic variety. This notion can be regarded as a multiplicative version of the classical additive notion of rank with respect to any algebraic variety which is geometrically studied through secant varieties, see
[
OV25
]
for a recent survey.

Notation
.

Let
V
be a
(N+1)
-dimensional
\mathbb{C}
-vector space and fix a basis
\{e_{0},\ldots,e_{N}\}
. For any vector
p=p_{0}e_{0}+\cdots+p_{N}e_{N}
, we denote by
(p_{0}:\cdots:p_{N})\in\mathbb{P}V=\mathbb{P}^{N}
the corresponding projective point.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
p=(p_{0}:\cdots:p_{N}),\ q=(q_{0}:\cdots:q_{N})\in\mathbb{P}V
. Their
Hadamard product
is defined, if it exists, as the coordinate-wise product
p\star q=(p_{0}q_{0}:\cdots:p_{N}q_{N})
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
be any irreducible projective variety.

Definition 1.2
.

The
Hadamard-
X
-rank
of a point
q\in\mathbb{P}V
is defined as

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(q)=\min\{m\mid q=p_{1}\star\cdots\star p_{m},\ p_{i}\in X\},

with the convention that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(q)=\infty
if the set of such decompositions is empty.

The question that we address in this paper, which naturally arises from this definition, is the following.

Question
.

For which
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
and
q\in\mathbb{P}V
the Hadamard-
X
-rank of
q
is finite? For which
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
the Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for every point of
\mathbb{P}V
?

Having in mind applications to tensor decompositions, in
[
AMO25
]
, the authors focused on toric varieties, such as Segre-Veronese varieties, and their secant varieties. Since toric varieties are Hadamard-idempotent, see e.g.,
[
FOW17
, Proposition 4.7]
, the Hadamard-rank with respect to toric varieties is infinite for every point outside the variety: in particular, that is the case for Segre-Veronese varieties. At the same time, it was proven in
[
AMO25
, Corollary 3.8]
that the Hadamard-rank with respect to secant varieties of toric varieties is finite everywhere. In the case of secants of Segre-Veronese varieties, this translates to the following: for any
r\geq 2
, any (partially-symmetric) tensor can be expressed as entry-wise product of finitely many (partially-symmetric) tensors of rank at most
r
.

In the literature of tensor decompositions, there are several other interesting algebraic varieties, for example, Grassmannians, Chow varieties and varieties of reducible forms. All these are not toric varieties and the results of
[
AMO25
]
about finiteness of Hadamard ranks do not apply.

In
[
AMO25
, Corollary 3.40]
, it was shown that the
generic
Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite if and only if the variety
X
is
concise
(i.e., it is not contained in any coordinate hyperplane) and it is not contained in a
binomial hypersurface
. However, the finiteness of the generic Hadamard-
X
-rank does not guarantee that the Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for
any
point, see
[
AMO25
, Example 3.10]
.

Main results.

In this paper, we give a complete classification of the irreducible varieties for which the Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for
any
point of the ambient space.

Notation
.

We write
H_{i}=\{x_{i}=0\}
for the
i
th coordinate hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
i\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
.

Theorem A
.

The Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for every point if and only if
X
is strongly concise, namely, for any
i\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
,
(X\cap H_{i})\not\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}H_{j}
.

In
Section

3.1
, we will use
A
to show the finiteness of the Hadamard rank with respect to important varieties of tensors in the literature, such as Grassmannians, Chow varieties, varieties of reducible forms and tangential varieties. These results complement
[
AMO25
, Corollary 3.9]
on finiteness of Hadamard ranks with respect to secant varieties of Segre-Veronese varieties, justifying and supporting a new research direction on the study of different Hadamard ranks of tensors and their properties.

For example, once we know that the maximum Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite, it is natural to look for bounds. We prove that under certain geometric conditions the upper bound is given by the dimension of the ambient space. We also note that such upper bound is sharp (
Example

4.9
).

Theorem B
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible variety of dimension
n\leq N/2
such that
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}=\emptyset
. Then,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq N
for any
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

The latter result is a corollary of a result on the lower semi-continuity of the Hadamard rank with respect to algebraic curves that do not contain points with at least two coordinates equal to zero (
Theorem

4.6
): this generalizes an analogous result for straight lines from
[
BCK16
]
. In
Example

4.1
, we show that the condition about the containment of points with two zero-coordinates is needed: indeed, we provide an example of a conic in
\mathbb{P}^{2}
containing a coordinate point and for which the Hadamard rank is not lower semi-continuous. Such example justifies the definition of
Hadamard border ranks
, see
Definition

4.2
.

2.
Hadamard products of algebraic varieties

We first recall the basic definitions on Hadamard products of algebraic varieties. For a general reference, we refer to the recent book
[
BC24
]
.

The Hadamard product of points defined in
Definition

1.1
induces a rational map
h\colon\mathbb{P}V\times\mathbb{P}V\dashrightarrow\mathbb{P}V
. This can be regarded as the composition of the Segre embedding
\mathbb{P}V\times\mathbb{P}V\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}(V\otimes V)
with the linear projection onto the diagonal coordinates. This allows us to define the Hadamard product of algebraic varieties.

Definition 2.1
.

Given two algebraic varieties
X,Y\subset\mathbb{P}V
, their
Hadamard product
is the Zariski closure of the image of
X\times Y
through the Hadamard product map, i.e.,

(1)

X\star Y:=\overline{h(X\times Y)}=\overline{\{p\star q~|~p\in X,\ q\in Y,\ p\star q\text{ exists}\}.}

Given an algebraic variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
, we define its
Hadamard powers
iteratively as:

X^{\star 0}:=(1:\cdots:1)\quad\text{ and }\quad X^{\star s}:=X\star X^{\star(s-1)}.

Remark 2.2
.

If
X,Y\subset\mathbb{P}V
are irreducible, then
X\star Y\subset\mathbb{P}V
is irreducible, since it is (the closure of) the image of a rational map.

The notion of Hadamard rank with respect to an algebraic variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
is strictly related to the Hadamard powers of
X
. If we look at the variety of points with bounded Hadamard-
X
-rank

\eta_{m}(X)=\overline{\{p\in\mathbb{P}V\mid\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq m\}},

then,

\eta_{m}(X)=X\cup X^{\star 2}\cup\cdots\cup X^{\star m}.

Note that if
(1:\cdots:1)\in X
, then
X^{\star i}\subset X^{\star(i+1)}
for any
i
, and, in particular,
\eta_{m}(X)=X^{\star m}
. Anyway, we always have that
\dim X^{\star i}\leq\dim X^{\star(i+1)}
and thus
\dim\eta_{m}(X)=\dim X^{\star m}
. This allows us to interpret the
generic Hadamard-
X
-rank
, which is defined as

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}=\min\{r\mid\text{there exists a Zariski dense $U\subset X$ such that }\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)=r\text{ for all }p\in U\},

in terms of Hadamard powers of
X
. Indeed, the generic point
p\in\mathbb{P}V
has Hadamard rank
m
with respect to
X
if and only if
m
is the minimum integer
k
for which
X^{\star k}
fills the ambient space, namely,

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}=\min\{m\ |\ \eta_{m}(X)=\mathbb{P}V\}=\min\{m\ |\ X^{\star m}=\mathbb{P}V\},

with the convention that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}=\infty
if
X^{\star m}
does not fill the ambient space for any
m
.

The generic Hadamard-
X
-rank is not always finite, as the following example shows.

Example 2.3

(Conciseness)
.

Let
H_{i}=\{x_{i}=0\}
be the
i
th coordinate hyperplane in
\mathbb{P}^{N}
, where
i=0,\ldots,N
. Notice that if
X\subset H_{i}
for some
i
, then
X^{\star m}\subset H_{i}^{\star m}=H_{i}
for all
m\geq 1
, and in particular
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}=\infty
. In other words, if
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}<\infty
then
X
is
concise
, i.e.,
X
is not contained in any coordinate hyperplane.

A more general obstruction to the finiteness of the generic Hadamard rank is being contained in an
Hadamard-idempotent variety
, i.e. a variety
Y\subset\mathbb{P}V
such that
Y^{\star 2}=Y
.

Example 2.4
.

An example of concise varieties that are Hadamard-idempotent are embedded toric varieties, i.e. varieties parametrized by monomial maps, or, equivalently, such that their defining ideal has a generating set made by polynomials of the form
{\bf x}^{\alpha}-{\bf x}^{\beta}
.
1
1

1

We use the compact notation
{\bf x}^{\alpha}=x_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots x_{N}^{\alpha_{N}}
for
{\bf x}=(x_{0},\ldots,x_{N})\in\mathbb{C}^{N+1}
and
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{N})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N+1}
.

However, that is not the only case. Indeed, it is possible to have that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}=\infty
even if the Hadamard powers of
X
are not all contained in some specific proper variety, as we see in the following example.

Notation
.

In what follows, by a binomial we mean a polynomial of the form
a{\bf x}^{\alpha}+b{\bf x}^{\beta}
for some
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\{0\}
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{N+1}
. By a binomial hypersurface we mean an hypersurface defined by a binomial equation.

Example 2.5
.

Let
c\in\mathbb{C}
be a non-zero complex number which is not a root of unity. For all integers
N\geq 2
and
d\geq 1
, we consider the binomial hypersurface
X_{d,N}=\{x_{1}^{d}-cx_{0}^{d-1}x_{N}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
. Then,
X_{d,N}^{\star m}=\{x_{1}^{d}-c^{m}x_{0}^{d-1}x_{N}=0\}
, see
[
BC22
, Proposition 4.5]
. For all
m\geq 1
, let
p_{m}=(1:\cdots:1:c^{-m})
. Since
p_{m}=p_{1}^{\star m}
and
p_{1}\in X_{d,N}
, we deduce
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X_{d,N}}(p_{m})\leq m
. Actually, equality holds: if, by contradiction, we had
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X_{d,N}}(p_{m})=r<m
, then
p_{m}\in X_{d,N}^{\star r}
, and hence
1-c^{r}c^{-m}=0
. This is a contradiction since
r-m\neq 0
and
c
is not a root of unity. In particular,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X_{d,N}}(p_{m})=m
for all
m
.

In
[
AMO25
]
, it was shown using tropical geometry that concise irreducible Hadamard-idempotent varieties must be contained in a binomial hypersurface. As a corollary, the following characterization of the varieties
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
for which the generic Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite was obtained.

Theorem 2.6

(
[
AMO25
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
be an irreducible variety. The following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}<\infty
, i.e. the Hadamard-
X
-rank of the generic point
p\in\mathbb{P}V
is finite

(2)

X
is concise and not contained in any binomial hypersurface

(3)

the vanishing ideal of
X
does not contain any variable and any binomial.

The goal of the next section is to prove a classification of the irreducible varieties for which the Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for
every
point, i.e. not only for the general one.

3.
Finiteness of the maximum rank

Given an irreducible variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
, we denote the
maximum Hadamard-
X
-rank
as

\operatorname{Hrk}^{\max}_{X}=\min\{r\mid\forall p\in\mathbb{P}V,\ \operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq r\}

with the convention that
\operatorname{Hrk}^{\max}_{X}=\infty
if there exists at least a point
p\in\mathbb{P}V
for which
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)=\infty
.

Note that even if we require the finiteness of the generic Hadamard-
X
-rank, it is not guaranteed that the maximum Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite, as shown in the following example.

Example 3.1

(
[
AMO25
]
)
.

Consider the quadric curve
Q=\{x_{0}x_{1}+x_{0}x_{2}+x_{1}x_{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Then,
Q^{\star 2}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
and so
\operatorname{Hrk}_{Q}^{\circ}=2
. However, every point
(a:b:0)
such that
ab\neq 0
cannot be written as a product of points on
Q
. Hence,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{Q}((a:b:0))=\infty
for all
a,b\in\mathbb{C}\smallsetminus\{0\}
.

However, in the previous example, the problem arises for points with some coordinate equal to zero. This motivated to look at points
with all non-zero coordinates
, and the following bound, inspired by the analogous result
[
BT15
, Theorem 1]
for additive
X
-ranks, was obtained.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
AMO25
]
)
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a concise irreducible variety not contained in any binomial hypersurface. Then,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq 2\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}
for all
p=(p_{0}:\cdots:p_{N})\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
such that
\prod_{i}p_{i}\neq 0
.

If we look again at
Example

3.1
, we realize that the intersection of
Q
with any coordinate line
H_{0},H_{1},H_{2}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
yields only coordinate points, i.e. points having always at least two coordinates equal to zero. Since we want to avoid such a situation, we give the following definition.

Definition 3.3
.

A variety
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is called strongly concise if, for each
i=0,\ldots,N
, the intersection
X\cap H_{i}
is not contained in a union of coordinate hyperplanes different from
H_{i}
, i.e.,
(X\cap H_{i})\not\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}H_{j}
.

Remark 3.4
.

Clearly, if
X
is strongly concise then
X
is concise, as defined in
Example

2.3
. Moreover, notice that binomial hypersurfaces are not strongly concise: in particular, if
X
is strongly concise, then the generic Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite by
Theorem

2.6
.

Remark 3.5
.

From an algebraic point of view, saying that
X
is strongly concise is equivalent to say that the vanishing (radical) ideal of
X
, denoted by
I(X)
, does not contain a polynomial of the form
x_{i}F+{\bf x}^{\alpha}
where
{\bf x}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{N}]
is a monomial not involving
x_{i}
. Indeed, if such a polynomial is identically zero on
X
, then
(X\cap H_{i})\subset\{{\bf x}\mid{\bf x}^{\alpha}=0\}\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}H_{j}
. Vice versa, assume that
X
is not strongly concise. Then, for some
i\in\{0,\ldots,N\}
,
(X\cap H_{i})\subset\bigcup_{j\neq i}H_{j}
, and so
I\Big(\bigcup_{j\neq i}H_{j}\Big)\subset I(X\cap H_{i})
. The ideal on the left is generated by
\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}
, while the ideal on the right is
\sqrt{I(X)+(x_{i})}
. By the inclusion, there exists
r\geq 1
such that
\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{r}\in I(X)+(x_{i})
, from which
\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{r}=G-x_{i}F
for some
G\in I(X)
. Hence, the ideal
I(X)
contains the polynomial
G=\prod_{j\neq i}x_{j}^{r}+x_{i}F
.

We now prove
A
by separately proving the two implications.

The necessary condition is a generalization of
Theorem

3.2
.

Proposition 3.6
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a strongly concise irreducible variety. Then, for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
,

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq 2\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}+z(p),

where
z(p)
denotes the number of zero coordinates of
p
. In particular,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\max}<\infty
.

Proof.

Let
p=(p_{0}:\cdots:p_{N})\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
and write
z(p)=z
. Since
X
is strongly concise, for all
i=0,\ldots,N
, there exists
q_{i}\in X
such that if
q_{i}=(q_{i0}:\cdots:q_{iN})
, then
q_{ii}=0
and
\prod_{j\neq i}q_{ij}\neq 0
. Let
i_{1},\ldots,i_{z}
be all the indices such that
p_{i_{j}}=0
and let
q=q_{i_{1}}\star\cdots\star q_{i_{z}}
. Since
q
and
p
have the same number of zero coordinates, there exists
p^{\prime}\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
with all non-zero coordinates such that
p=p^{\prime}\star q
. Since
X
is strongly concise,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}<\infty
(
Remark

3.4
) and by
Theorem

3.2
we know that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p^{\prime})\leq 2\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}
. At the same time, by construction,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(q)\leq z
. Hence,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p^{\prime})+\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(q)\leq 2\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\circ}+z
and we conclude.
∎

Now, we prove the opposite direction which concludes the proof of
A
.

Proposition 3.7
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a variety and assume that the maximum Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite. Then,
X
is strongly concise.

Proof.

Suppose by contradiction that
X
is not strongly concise. Then, there exists
i=0,\ldots,N
such that
(X\cap H_{i})\subset\bigcup_{k\neq i}H_{k}
. In other words, for any point
y=(y_{0}:\cdots:y_{N})\in X
with
y_{i}=0
, we have
y_{k}=0
for some other
k\neq i
. Let
p=(p_{0}:\cdots:p_{N})\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
be any point in the ambient space with
p_{i}=0
and
\prod_{j\neq i}p_{j}\neq 0
. Since the maximum Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite,
p=q_{1}\star\cdots\star q_{m}
for some
q_{1},\ldots,q_{m}\in X
. Since
p_{i}=0
, then, for some
j=1,\ldots,m
, the point
q_{j}=(q_{j0}:\cdots:q_{jN})\in X
satisfies
q_{ji}=0
. Since
q_{j}\in X
and
(X\cap H_{i})\subset\bigcup_{k\neq i}H_{k}
, we have that
q_{jk}=0
for some
k\neq i
. It follows that
p_{k}=0
for some
k\neq i
, a contradiction.
∎

Corollary 3.8
.

For any
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
irreducible variety, the following are equivalent:

(1)

\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\max}<\infty
, i.e. the Hadamard-
X
-rank is finite for any point
p\in\mathbb{P}V

(2)

X
is strongly concise

(3)

the vanishing ideal of
X
does not contain a polynomial of the form
x_{i}F+{\bf x}^{\alpha}
, where the monomial
{\bf x}^{\alpha}\in\mathbb{C}[x_{0},\ldots,\widehat{x_{i}},\ldots,x_{N}]
does not involve the variable
x_{i}
.

Proof.

The proof follows from
Remark

3.5
,
Propositions

3.6
and
3.7
.
∎

3.1.
Varieties of tensors

In what follows, we show that some families of varieties relevant for tensor decompositions are strongly concise. By
A
, this will imply that the Hadamard decompositions associated to them will always exist for any point in the ambient space.

Recall that the case of secant varieties of Segre-Veronese varieties was already considered in
[
AMO25
]
.

3.1.1.
Grassmannians

We begin with skew-symmetric tensor decompositions.

Lemma 3.9
.

The Grassmannian of
k
-dimensional linear subsets of
\mathbb{C}^{n}
is strongly concise in its Plücker embedding
\mathbb{G}(k,n)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

Proof.

It is well known that a point in
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
is determined by the equivalence class of a full rank
k\times n
matrix. The Plücker embedding takes any matrix in the equivalence class into its
k\times k
maximal minors. Hence, the strongly conciseness of
\mathbb{G}(k,n)
in this embedding is equivalent to the following statement:

There exists a
k\times n
matrix with exactly one
k\times k
minor equal to zero.

We prove the statement in the following equivalent geometric reformulation.

Claim
.

For
k\leq n
, there exist
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
such that
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\}
do not span
\mathbb{C}^{k}
, but any other subset
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k}}\}
spans
\mathbb{C}^{k}
.

Proof of Claim.

For all
k\geq 1
, we prove the theorem by induction on
n\geq k+1
. By construction, the points
v_{1},\ldots,v_{k}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
span an hyperplane
H\cong\mathbb{C}^{k-1}
. In the base case
n=k+1
, we can just choose
v_{k+1}
to be not in
H
. For the inductive step, we can choose
v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1}\in\mathbb{C}^{k}
with the desired property. For all subsets
\{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}\}\subset\{1,\ldots,n-1\}
, denote by
H_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
the hyperplane spanned by
\{v_{i_{1}},\ldots,v_{i_{k-1}}\}
. Since
\bigcup_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}}H_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}}\neq\mathbb{C}^{k}
, we can choose
v_{n}\not\in H_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}}
for all
i_{1},\ldots,i_{k-1}
. Then,
\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{n-1},v_{n}\}\subset\mathbb{C}^{k}
have the desired property.
∎

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

We now turn to the case of symmetric tensors (or, homogeneous polynomials) with particular structures.

3.1.2.
Tangential variety to Veronese varieties

Definition 3.10
.

For all
d,n\geq 1
, the
tangential variety
to the Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in degree
d
is:

\tau_{d,n}=\{[L^{d-1}M]:L,M\in(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})^{\vee}\}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.

This is the Zariski closure of the union of all tangent spaces to the embedding
\mathbb{P}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

Lemma 3.11
.

The tangential variety
\tau_{d,n}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is strongly concise.

Proof.

Write
L=a_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}x_{n}
and
M=b_{0}x_{0}+\cdots+b_{n}x_{n}
for some
a_{0},\ldots,a_{n},b_{0},\ldots,b_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
. Then,
L^{d-1}M=\sum_{\alpha}c_{\alpha}{\bf x}^{\alpha}
, where
\alpha=(\alpha_{0},\ldots,\alpha_{n})
runs over all the multi-indices such that
\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=d
and

c_{\alpha}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k=0\\
\alpha_{k}\neq 0\end{subarray}}^{n}b_{k}\binom{d-1}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{k}-1\cdots\alpha_{n}}a_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots a_{k}^{\alpha_{k}-1}\cdots a_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}

where the multinomial coefficient is
\binom{m}{m_{0}\cdots m_{n}}=\frac{m!}{m_{0}!\cdots m_{n}!}
. In other words,
c_{\alpha}
is the scalar product between the vector
{\bf b}=(b_{0},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
and the vector
{\bf v}_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
with components

({\bf v}_{\alpha,{\bf a}})_{k}=\begin{cases}\binom{d-1}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{k}-1\cdots\alpha_{n}}a_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots a_{k}^{\alpha_{k}-1}\cdots a_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}\qquad&\text{if }\alpha_{k}>0,\\
0&\text{if }\alpha_{k}=0,\end{cases}

for
k=0,\ldots,n
, where we use the notation
{\bf a}=(a_{0},\ldots,a_{n})\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
. For all
\alpha
, let
H_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\subset\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
be the hyperplane orthogonal to
{\bf v}_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
.

Claim
.

If
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
is generic, then for all
\alpha
the set
H_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{\beta\neq a}H_{\beta,{\bf a}}
is non-empty.

Before proving the Claim, we see how it implies the statement. If the Claim holds, then we can choose
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
to be generic and, for all
\alpha
, we can choose
{\bf b}_{\alpha}\in H_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\smallsetminus\bigcup_{\beta\neq\alpha}H_{\beta,{\bf a}}
. Denote by
L,M_{\alpha}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})^{\vee}
the associated linear forms. Then, the coefficient of
{\bf x}^{\alpha}
in
L^{d-1}M_{\alpha}
is zero but all the others are non-zero.

Proof of the Claim.

We prove that the set of vectors
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
for which the Claim fails is Zariski closed. Suppose that the Claim is false. Then, there exists a multi-index
\alpha
such that
H_{\alpha,{\bf a}}\subset\bigcup_{\beta\neq\alpha}H_{\beta,{\bf a}}
. Hence,
H_{\alpha,{\bf a}}=H_{\beta,{\bf a}}
for some
\beta\neq\alpha
. In particular,
{\bf v}_{\alpha,{\bf a}}={\bf v}_{\beta,{\bf a}}
, that is

\binom{d-1}{\alpha_{0}\cdots\alpha_{k}-1\cdots\alpha_{n}}a_{0}^{\alpha_{0}}\cdots a_{k}^{\alpha_{k}-1}\cdots a_{n}^{\alpha_{n}}=\binom{d-1}{\beta_{0}\cdots\beta_{k}-1\cdots\beta_{n}}a_{0}^{\beta_{0}}\cdots a_{k}^{\beta_{k}-1}\cdots a_{n}^{\beta_{n}}

for all
k\in\{0,\ldots,n\}
. Since
\alpha\neq\beta
and
\alpha_{0}+\cdots+\alpha_{n}=\beta_{0}+\cdots+\beta_{n}=d\geq 1
, at least one of the above is a non-trivial polynomial equation vanishing on
{\bf a}
. Hence, the set of
{\bf a}\in\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
for which the statement is false is cut out by some polynomial equations, therefore it is Zariski closed.
∎

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

3.1.3.
Chow varieties and varieties of reducible forms

The tangential variety to the Veronese embedding is contained, for example, in the Chow variety and the variety of reducible forms. This will be useful because of the following remark.

Remark 3.12
.

Let
X\subset Y
be two algebraic varieties. If
X
is strongly concise, then
Y
is strongly concise. In particular, if
X
is strongly concise, then all its secant varieties are strongly concise.

Summing-up, we got the following.

Corollary 3.13
.

For each of these varieties and their secant varieties, the associated Hadamard rank is finite for any point in the ambient space.

•

The Grassmannian
\mathbb{G}(k,n)\hookrightarrow\mathbb{P}\bigwedge^{k}\mathbb{C}^{n}
in its Plücker embedding.

•

The tangential variety
\tau_{d,n}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}
to the Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in degree
d
.

•

the Chow variety

\mathbb{X}_{d,n}=\{[L_{1}\cdots L_{d}]:L_{i}\in(\mathbb{C}^{n+1})^{\vee}\}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}.

•

the variety of reducible forms

\mathcal{R}_{{\bf d},n}=\{[F_{1}\cdots F_{k}]:F_{i}\in\mathrm{Sym}^{d_{i}}\mathbb{C}^{n+1}\}\subset\mathbb{P}\mathrm{Sym}^{d}\mathbb{C}^{n+1},

for any fixed
{\bf d}=(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
with
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=d
.

Proof.

The first two points are implied by
A
together with
Lemma

3.9
and
Lemma

3.11
respectively. Since the tangential variety to the Veronese embedding of
\mathbb{P}^{n}
in degree
d
is contained in the Chow variety
\mathbb{X}_{d,n}
and in the variety of reducible forms
\mathcal{R}_{{\bf d},n}
with
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=d
, the last two points are a consequence of
A
,
Lemma

3.11
and
Remark

3.12
.
∎

Remark 3.14
.

We can rephrase the above result in terms of decompositions of tensors.

•

For any
r\geq 1
, every skew-symmetric tensor can be written as a coefficient-wise product of finitely many skew-symmetric tensors of skew-symmetric rank at most
r
.

•

For any
r\geq 1
, every homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
can be written as a coefficient-wise product of finitely many polynomials of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_{i}^{d-1}M_{i}
with
\deg(L_{i})=\deg(M_{i})=1
.

•

For any
r\geq 1
, every homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
can be written as a coefficient-wise product of finitely many polynomials of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{r}L_{i,1}\cdots L_{i,d}
with
\deg(L_{i,j})=1
.

•

For any
r\geq 1
and fixed degrees
(d_{1},\ldots,d_{k})
with
d_{1}+\cdots+d_{k}=d
, every homogeneous polynomial of degree
d
can be written as a coefficient-wise product of finitely many polynomials of the form
\sum_{i=1}^{r}F_{i,1}\cdots F_{i,k}
with
\deg(F_{i,j})=d_{j}
.

4.
Border Hadamard rank

Contrarily to the classical notion of matrix rank, it is well known that the additive notion of
X
-rank fails often to be lower semi-continuous: a first example is given by Rational Normal Curves in
\mathbb{P}^{3}
.

In
Example

3.1
it is given a family of points contained in a Hadamard power
X^{\star m}
but with infinite Hadamard-
X
-rank. That is not the only pathology against lower semi-continuity of Hadamard ranks: in the following example, we give a point contained in
X^{\star 2}
but with Hadamard-
X
-rank equal to
3
.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the conic
C=\{x_{0}(x_{1}+x_{2})+(x_{1}-x_{2})^{2}=0\}\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
. Note that
C
is strongly concise, thus the Hadamard rank is finite for every point by
A
. Also, note that
C
is not binomial, thus
C^{\star 2}=\mathbb{P}^{2}
by
Theorem

2.6
. The conic
C
is tangent to the line
H_{0}=\{x_{0}=0\}
at the point
(0:1:1)
. We compute the Hadamard-
C
-rank of all points in
H_{0}
.

•

(0:1:1)
has Hadamard-
C
-rank equal to
1
. This is the only one in
H_{0}
since
C\cap H_{0}=\{(0:1:1)\}
.

•

(0:s:t)
with
(s:t)\not\in\{(1:1),(1:-1)\}
have Hadamard-
C
-rank equal to
2
. Indeed, we can write

(0:s:t)=(0:1:1)\star(-(s-t)^{2}:s(s+t):t(s+t)).

•

(0:1:-1)
has Hadamard-
C
-rank equal to
3
. It cannot be written as an Hadamard product of two points in
C
: indeed, such a decomposition needs to involve the point
(0:1:1)
in order to get the zero in the first coordinate, but
C
does not contain any point of the form
(u:1:-1)
. At the same time, we have

(0:1:-1)=(0:1:1)\star(-1:6:3)\star(9:1:-2).

We have already observed that
C^{\star 2}
fills the ambient space, thus
(0:1:-1)
is a limit of a
1
-parameter family of points with Hadamard-
C
-rank equal to
2
. More explicitly, we can write

(0:1:-1)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}~(0:1+\epsilon:-1+\epsilon)=\lim_{\epsilon\rightarrow 0}~(0:1:1)\star(-2:\epsilon(1+\epsilon):\epsilon(-1+\epsilon)).

The latter example justifies the following definition.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}V
be an algebraic variety and
p\in\mathbb{P}V
. The border Hadamard-
X
-rank of
p
is

\underline{\operatorname{Hrk}}_{X}(p)=\min\{m\ |\ p\in X^{\star m}\}.

with the convention that if
p\not\in X^{\star m}
for all
m\geq 1
, then
\underline{\operatorname{Hrk}}_{X}(p)=\infty
.

We now show that for varieties whose points do not contain too many zeroes, border rank equals rank in the low-rank regime.

We recall the following notation from
[
BC24
]
.

Notation
.

For all
i=0,\ldots,N
, we denote by
\Delta_{i}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
the variety of points with at most
i+1
non-zero coordinates. Hence

\Delta_{0}=\{(1:0:\cdots:0),\ldots,(0:\cdots:0:1)\}\subset\Delta_{1}\subset\cdots\subset\Delta_{N-1}=H_{0}\cup\cdots\cup H_{N}\subset\Delta_{N}=\mathbb{P}^{N}.

In particular,
\Delta_{N-k}\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
is the variety of points having at least
k
coordinates equal to zero.

Remark 4.3
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible variety of dimension
n
. Since a linear space of dimension at least
N-n
intersects
X
, one has that
X\cap\Delta_{i}\neq\emptyset
for all
i\geq N-n
. If
n>N/2
, then
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}\neq\emptyset
.

Remark 4.4
.

A curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
with
C\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
is strongly concise because, by definition,
C\cap(H_{i}\cap H_{j})=\emptyset
for all
i\neq j
.

We begin here to investigate the difference between rank and border rank.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
X\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible variety of dimension
n\leq N/2
such that
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}=\emptyset
. Then, for all
r\geq 2
such that
rn\leq N
, the points
p\in\eta_{r}(X)\smallsetminus\eta_{r-1}(X)
satisfy
\underline{\operatorname{Hrk}}_{X}(p)=\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)=r
.

Proof.

Since
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}=\emptyset
, the points of
X
have at most
n
coordinates equal to zero. A product of
r
points in
X
has at most
rn
coordinates equal to zero: thus, by numerical assumption
rn\leq N
, the restriction
h\colon X^{r}\dashrightarrow X^{\star r}
of the Hadamard map to
X^{r}
is a morphism. Since morphisms of projective varieties are closed, we conclude that

X^{\star r}=\{p_{1}\star\cdots\star p_{r}:p_{i}\in X\}.

This implies the statement.
∎

Theorem 4.6
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible curve such that
C\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
. Then:

(1)

for all
1\leq r\leq N
,
\eta_{r}(C)
has dimension
r
;

(2)

for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
,
\underline{\operatorname{Hrk}}_{C}(p)=\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)\leq N
and equality holds when
p
is generic.

Proof.

Since (1) implies that
\eta_{N}(C)=\mathbb{P}^{N}
, (2) follows from (1) and
Lemma

4.5
. We prove (1).

By assumption,
C
contains a point
p
with all non-zero coordinates, thus, without loss of generalities, we can assume that
(1:\cdots:1)\in C
. Indeed, let
q=p^{\star(-1)}
and consider
q\star X
. Clearly,
(1:\cdots:1)=q\star p\in q\star X
. Moreover, by
[
AMO25
, Lemma 3.13]
,
(q\star X)^{\star r}=q^{\star r}\star X^{\star r}
: in particular, the dimensions of all Hadamard powers of
X
, thus of
\eta_{r}(X)
, are preserved by the diagonal change of coordinates given by the Hadamard product with
q
. Thus, it is enough to prove (1) under the assumption
(1:\cdots:1)\in C
.

As mentioned in
Section

2
, if
(1:\cdots:1)\in C
, then
\eta_{r}(C)=C^{\star r}
. Suppose by contradiction that there exists
2\leq s\leq N
such that
\dim C^{\star s}\leq s-1
but
\dim C^{\star r}=r
for
r\leq s-1
. Then, since
C^{\star(s-1)}\subset C^{\star s}
, we have that
s-1\leq\dim C^{\star s}\leq s-1
, from which
\dim C^{\star s}=s-1=\dim C^{\star(s-1)}
. Since Hadamard powers are irreducible (
Remark

2.2
), we have
C^{\star s}=C^{\star(s-1)}
. This implies that
C^{\star(s+i)}=C^{\star(s-1)}
for all
i\geq 0
: in particular,
C^{\star m}\neq\mathbb{P}^{N}
for all
m\geq 1
, hence
\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}^{\circ}=\infty
. This is a contradiction: indeed, by
Remark

4.4
,
C
is strongly concise and, in particular, it has finite generic Hadamard rank by
A
.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

For a given irreducible curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
and a general linear transformation
g\in GL_{N}
, it holds
g(C)\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
, hence
Theorem

4.6
applies to
g(C)
.

Remark 4.8
.

Note that the assumption
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}=\emptyset
in
Lemma

4.5
, and thus the assumption
C\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
in
Theorem

4.6
, is needed: indeed,
Example

4.1
provides a counterexample to the equality between rank and border rank since the curve
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{2}
contains the coordinate point
(1:0:0)
.

The bound in
Proposition

3.6
gives as a consequence that if
X
is strongly concise, then
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\max}\leq 3N
. However, if the variety
X
has not too many zeroes, we can show that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}^{\max}\leq N
. This is the content of
B
, of which we now
give a proof.

Proof of
B
.

The case
n=1
is
Theorem

4.6
. If
n\geq 2
, let
L\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be a general linear space of codimension
n-1
. By the theorem of Bertini (see
[
Laz04
, Theorem 3.3.1]
or
[
Jou83
, Section I.6]
),
C=X\cap L
is an irreducible curve and
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
.

We notice that
C\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
. In fact, suppose by contradiction that
C\cap(H_{i}\cap H_{j})=(X\cap L)\cap(H_{i}\cap H_{j})\neq\emptyset
for
i\neq j
. We recall that
\Delta_{N-n-1}=\bigcup_{k_{1},\ldots,k_{n+1}}(H_{k_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{k_{n+1}})
. Since
L
is generic, by semicontinuity:

\dim(X\cap H_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap H_{k_{1}}\cap\cdots\cap H_{k_{n-1}})\geq\dim(X\cap H_{i}\cap H_{j}\cap L)=\dim(C\cap H_{i}\cap H_{j})\geq 0

for some distinct
i,j,k_{1},\ldots,k_{n-1}
, hence
X\cap\Delta_{N-n-1}\neq\emptyset
, a contradiction.

In conclusion,
Theorem

4.6
applies to
C
, and then
\operatorname{Hrk}_{X}(p)\leq\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)\leq N
for all
p\in\mathbb{P}^{N}
.
∎

We notice that the bound in
B
is sharp.

Example 4.9
.

Let
C\subset\mathbb{P}^{N}
be an irreducible curve such that
C\cap\Delta_{N-2}=\emptyset
. Let
p=(1:0:\cdots:0)
or any other coordinate point. By
Theorem

4.6
, we have that
\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)\leq N
. However, since each point of
C
has at most one coordinate equal to zero,
\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)\geq N
, hence
\operatorname{Hrk}_{C}(p)=N
.
