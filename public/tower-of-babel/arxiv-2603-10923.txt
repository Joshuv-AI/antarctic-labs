Abstract.
We study the long-time dynamics of a bulk–surface convective Cahn–Hilliard system describing phase separation processes with bulk-surface interaction. The presence of convection terms leads to a non-autonomous dynamical system and prevents the associated free energy from being a Lyapunov functional, which makes the analysis of the asymptotic behavior considerably more challenging.
First, we establish an instantaneous regularization property for weak solutions. Next, interpreting the evolution as a continuous two-parameter process, we prove the existence of a minimal pullback attractor. Finally, under suitable decay assumptions on the velocity fields, we show that every solution converges as
t\to\infty
to a single steady state.
The proof of this convergence relies on the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality combined with customized decay estimates that compensate for the lack of a monotone energy functional.

Keywords:
convective Cahn–Hilliard equation, bulk-surface interaction, dynamic boundary conditions, propagation of regularity, pullback-attractor, convergence to equilibrium.

1  Introduction

The Cahn–Hilliard equation was originally introduced to describe spinodal decomposition in binary alloys. Subsequently, it was recognized that the equation also provides an effective framework for modeling later stages of phase separation processes, such as Ostwald ripening. Today, the Cahn–Hilliard equation has become one of the most widely used diffuse-interface models, with applications spanning materials science, life sciences, and image processing.

In its classical formulation in a bounded domain, the Cahn–Hilliard system is typically supplemented with homogeneous Neumann boundary conditions for both the phase-field variable and the chemical potential
when studying the associated initial-boundary value problem. However, both mathematicians and physicists (see, e.g.,
[
3
,
14
,
15
,
35
]
) have observed that such boundary conditions lead to significant limitations when an accurate description of the dynamics near the boundary is required.

A key drawback is that the homogeneous Neumann condition for the phase-field variable forces diffuse interfaces between the two components to intersect the boundary at a right angle. In many practical situations, this assumption is unrealistic, since the contact angle generally deviates from ninety degrees and may even evolve dynamically over time. Moreover, the homogeneous Neumann condition for the chemical potential prevents any exchange of material between the bulk and the boundary. While this constraint is appropriate in settings where total bulk mass must be conserved, it becomes inadequate when processes such as adsorption or chemical reactions occur at the boundary, where mass transfer between bulk and surface must be taken into account.

Motivated by these limitations of the classical formulation and by the need to more accurately capture short-range interactions between bulk and surface, several Cahn–Hilliard models with dynamic boundary conditions have been proposed and studied in the literature. In particular, dynamic boundary conditions have attracted considerable attention in recent years (see, e.g.,
[
18
,
21
,
19
,
24
,
47
,
10
,
9
,
16
,
50
,
39
,
53
,
45
]
), as they allow for a consistent description of coupled bulk-surface dynamics, including variables contact angles and the exchange of material between bulk and boundary.
For more information on the Cahn–Hilliard equation with and without dynamic boundary conditions, we refer to the recent review paper
[
56
]
and the book
[
49
]
.

Main results, difficulties, and novelties of this paper.

In this paper, we investigate the long-time dynamics of system (
1.1
).
On the one hand, this question is of interest since insights gained for (
1.1
) may provide information on the long-time behavior of more involved models in which (
1.1
) appears as a subsystem, such as the two-phase flow models studied in
[
29
,
42
,
54
,
12
,
22
]
.
On the other hand, the problem is also appealing from the viewpoint of mathematical analysis.

Remarkably, the long-time behavior of bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard models in the non-convective case is by now well understood, and an extensive literature is available (see, e.g.,
[
25
,
50
,
44
,
47
,
53
,
43
,
46
]
).
We also refer to
[
8
,
17
,
26
,
52
]
for the long-time analysis of further Cahn–Hilliard models with dynamic boundary conditions.
In contrast, only very few results are available for convective Cahn–Hilliard systems.
In
[
11
]
, the authors study a viscous, convective Cahn–Hilliard system with dynamic boundary conditions. It is shown there that the
\omega
-limit set of this model is non-empty and that each of its elements is a solution to the corresponding stationary problem. We also mention the contribution
[
57
]
, which studies the long-time dynamics of a convective Cahn–Hilliard model in two dimensions. However, the convective term considered there has a very specific structure and is substantially different from the one appearing in system (
1.1
). We finally remark that we expect the new results presented in the recent contribution
[
33
]
could be extended to the bulk-surface Cahn–Hilliard equation, which will be the objective of future work.

The main difficulty of the long-time analysis of convective Cahn–Hilliard models such as (
1.1
) is that the underlying dynamical system is non-autonomous. Therefore, unless the velocity fields are constant in time, it cannot be described as a semigroup. For this reason, we need to move from the framework of semigroups and global attractors to the more general theory of two-parameter processes and pullback attractors.
Moreover, due to the presence of the additional convection terms, the total free energy
E_{\mathrm{free}}
is in general no longer decreasing along solutions of (
1.1
). As a consequence, establishing convergence to a single equilibrium becomes significantly more challenging, since, unlike in the non-convective case,
E_{\mathrm{free}}
is not a Lyapunov functional.

The main results of this paper are as follows:

(I)

Instantaneous regularization of the weak solution.

In Section 3, after recalling from
[
41
,
30
]
some well-posedness and regularity results, we establish a new instantaneous regularization property for weak solutions. Namely, the unique weak solution instantly regularizes at times strictly greater than the initial time due to parabolic smoothing.

(II)

Existence of a minimal pullback attractor.

In Section 4, we show that the underlying dynamical system of system (
1.1
) can be interpreted as a continuous two-parameter process. We further observe that in the non-convective case (i.e.,
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\equiv(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
), the corresponding dynamical system is autonomous, and the continuous process can be reduced to a continuous semigroup. Finally, using abstract results from the theory of non-autonomous dynamical systems, we prove that the continuous process associated with (
1.1
) actually admits a minimal pullback attractor.

At this point, we also want to mention the work
[
34
]
, where the existence of a minimal pullback attractor to a non-autonomous Cahn–Hilliard–Darcy system with mass source was established by employing similar techniques.

(III)

Convergence to a steady state.

In Section 5, we finally show that each unique solution to system (
1.1
) converges as
t\to\infty
to a single steady state. In other words, this means that the
\omega
-limit set is actually a singleton.
Our proof relies on a

Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality
and the fact that stationary solutions are
uniformly strictly separated
from pure phases.
As our dynamical system is non-autonomous and since the energy functional
E_{\mathrm{free}}
is not necessarily decreasing along solutions, we need to devise customized decay estimates for the weak solution to prove its convergence to a steady state. In this context, it is essential that the velocity fields
(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})
fulfill a certain decay property for large times in a suitable norm (see
(D5)
).

To the best of our knowledge, this is the first work establishing convergence to equilibrium as
t\to\infty
for a convective Cahn–Hilliard model where prescribed velocity fields cause the system to be non-autonomous.
In this sense, this technique may have the potential to be applied to a variety of related situations and models, including even the case of multi-component phase-field systems (see, e.g.,
[
5
,
20
,
32
]
) or problems on evolving surfaces like
[
4
]
.

We also refer to
[
34
]
, where convergence to equilibrium was established for a two-dimensional Cahn–Hilliard–Darcy model with mass source. However, the situation considered there somewhat differs from the one in the present paper since the external quantity rendering the dynamical system non-autonomous enters the Cahn–Hilliard equation through a source term on the right-hand side.

\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. By means of the Riesz representation theorem, this product can be extended to a duality pairing on
(\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1})^{\prime}\times\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}
, which will also be denoted as
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1}}
.

(P3)

Let
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
be real numbers. We define for
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}
the generalized bulk-surface mean

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)\coloneqq\frac{\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\beta,1\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}}{\beta^{2}|\Omega|+|\Gamma|},

which reduces to

\displaystyle\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=\frac{\beta|\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}}{\beta^{2}|\Omega|+|\Gamma|}

if
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}
. We then define the closed linear subspaces

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}:\textnormal{mean}\left(\phi,\psi\right)=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L=\infty.\end{cases}

Note that these subspaces are Hilbert spaces with respect to the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
and its induced norm.
For any
s>1
, we further set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{V}}_{L,\beta}^{s}\coloneqq\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{1}\cap\mathcal{H}^{s}.

(P4)

Let
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
. We define a bilinear form on
\mathcal{H}^{1}\times\mathcal{H}^{1}
by

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}\coloneqq

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\nabla\phi\cdot\nabla\zeta\;\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\Gamma}\nabla_{\Gamma}\psi\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\xi\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt+\chi(L)\int_{\Gamma}(\beta\psi-\phi)(\beta\xi-\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Moreover, we set

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta}\coloneqq\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}^{1/2}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}
. The bilinear form
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L,\beta}
defines an inner product on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
, and
\|\cdot\|_{L,\beta}
defines a norm on
\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
that is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}
(see
[
38
, Corollary A.2]
). Moreover, the space
\big(\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta},\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)_{L,\beta},\|\cdot\|_{L,\beta}\big)
is a Hilbert space.

(P5)

For any
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\beta\in\mathbb{R}
, we define the space

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}:\beta|\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in(\mathcal{H}^{1})^{\prime}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L=\infty.\end{cases}

Using the Lax-Milgram theorem, one can show that for any
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}
, there exists a unique weak solution
\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)=\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\big)\in\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta}
to the following elliptic problem with bulk-surface coupling:

\displaystyle-\Delta\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\phi\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{in }\Omega,

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\Gamma}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}+\beta\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=-\psi

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma,

\displaystyle L\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}

\displaystyle=\beta\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}-\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}\hskip 20.00003pt

\displaystyle\text{on }\Gamma.

This means that
\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)
satisfies the weak formulation

\displaystyle\big(S_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{L,\beta}=-\langle\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\rangle_{\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}}

for all test functions
\left(\xi,\zeta\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta}
. Consequently, we have

\displaystyle\|\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}\leq C\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}}

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
, for a constant
C\geq 0
depending only on
\Omega
,
L
and
\beta
. We can thus define the solution operator

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}:\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}\rightarrow\mathcal{V}^{1}_{L,\beta},\hskip 10.00002pt\left(\phi,\psi\right)\mapsto\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi)=\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Omega}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}^{\Gamma}(\phi,\psi)\big),

as well as an inner product and its induced norm on
\mathcal{V}^{-1}_{L,\beta}
by

\displaystyle\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\big)_{L,\beta,\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\phi,\psi),\mathcal{S}_{L,\beta}(\zeta,\xi)\big)_{L,\beta},

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{L,\beta,\ast}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\big(\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\phi,\psi\right)\big)_{L,\beta,\ast}^{1/2},

for
\left(\phi,\psi\right),\left(\zeta,\xi\right)\in\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}
. This norm is equivalent to the norm
\|\cdot\|_{(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}}
on
\mathcal{V}_{L,\beta}^{-1}
. For the case
L\in(0,\infty)
, we refer the reader to
[
38
, Theorem 3.3 and Corollary 3.5]
for a proof of these statements. In the other cases, the results can be proven analogously.

(P6)

Let
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
,
K,L\in[0,\infty]
as well as
m\in\mathbb{R}
if
L\in[0,\infty)
and
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
if
L=\infty
. We define

\displaystyle\mathcal{W}_{K,L,m}^{1}\coloneqq\begin{cases}\big\{(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{H}_{K,\alpha}^{1}\,\big|\,\beta|\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=m\big\}&\text{if}\;L\in[0,\infty),\\[4.30554pt]
\left\{(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{H}_{K,\alpha}^{1}\,\middle|\,\begin{aligned} |\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}&=m_{1},\\
|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}&=m_{2}\end{aligned}\right\}&\text{if}\;L=\infty,\end{cases}

which is a closed subset of
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. Hence, it is a complete metric space with respect to the norm-induced metric on
\mathcal{H}^{1}
. For any
s>1
, we further set

\displaystyle{\mathcal{W}_{K,L,m}^{s}}\coloneqq\mathcal{W}_{K,L,m}^{1}\cap\mathcal{H}^{s}.

Additionally, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}\coloneqq\big\{(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{W}_{K,L,m}^{1}:\|\phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega)}\leq 1,\;\|\psi\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma)}\leq 1\big\}.

The metric
\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}}
is defined as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}}\big((\phi,\psi),(\zeta,\xi)\big)

\displaystyle\coloneqq\|(\phi,\psi)-(\zeta,\xi)\|_{\mathcal{H}^{1}}+\left|\int_{\Omega}F_{1}(\phi)\;\mathrm{d}x-\int_{\Omega}F_{1}(\zeta)\;\mathrm{d}x\right|

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\left|\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}(\psi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma-\int_{\Gamma}G_{1}(\xi)\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\right|,

and
\big(\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m},\mathrm{d}_{\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}}(\cdot,\cdot)\big)
is thus a complete metric space.

We first state a
bulk-surface Poincaré inequality

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
K\in[0,\infty)
and
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{R}
with
\alpha\beta|\Omega|+|\Gamma|\neq 0
be arbitrary. Then there exists a constant
C_{P}>0
depending only on
K,\alpha,\beta
and
\Omega
such that

\displaystyle\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\leq C_{P}\|\left(\phi,\psi\right)\|_{K,\alpha}

(2.2)

for all
\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{H}^{1}_{K,\alpha}
satisfying
\beta|\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0

Furthermore, we recall the following variant of Gronwall’s lemma, which is sometimes referred to as the
Uniform Gronwall Lemma
. It can be found, e.g., in
[
55
, Chapter III., Lemma 1.1]
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
g,h,y
be three non-negative locally integrable functions on
[t_{0},\infty)
such that
y^{\prime}
is locally integrable on
(t_{0},\infty)
and which satisfy

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y(t)

\displaystyle\leq g(t)y(t)+h(t)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for a.e.\penalty\ }t\geq t_{0},

and

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+r}g(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq a_{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{t}^{t+r}h(s)\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq a_{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{t}^{t+r}y(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq a_{3}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all\penalty\ }t\geq t_{0},

where
r,a_{1},a_{2},a_{3}
are positive constants. Then it holds

\displaystyle y(t+r)\leq\left(\frac{a_{3}}{r}+a_{2}\right)\mathrm{e}^{a_{1}}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all\penalty\ }t\geq t_{0}.

We conclude this section by stating another variant of Gronwall’s lemma, which follows from
[
28
, Lemma 2.5]
, see also
[
34
, Corollary A.1]
.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
g,h,y
be three non-negative locally integrable functions on
[t_{0},\infty)
which satisfy, for some
\gamma>0
,

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}y(t)+\gamma y(t)\leq g(t)y^{\frac{1}{2}}(t)+h(t)\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for a.e.\penalty\ }t\geq t_{0}.

Additionally, assume that there exist positive constants
A_{1}
and
A_{2}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{t}^{t+1}g(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq A_{1},\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{t}^{t+1}h(s)\;\mathrm{d}s\leq A_{2}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all\penalty\ }t\geq t_{0}.

Then it holds that

\displaystyle y(t)\leq 2y(t_{0})\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma(t-t_{0})}+Q(\gamma,A_{1},A_{2}),

where

\displaystyle Q(\gamma,A_{1},A_{2})=\Big(\frac{\mathrm{e}^{\frac{\gamma}{2}}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\frac{\gamma}{2}}}A_{1}\Big)^{2}+\frac{2\mathrm{e}^{\gamma}}{1-\mathrm{e}^{-\gamma}}A_{2}.

3  Well-posedness and instantaneous regularization

In the remainder of this paper, we will require the following assumptions on the initial data and the velocity fields:

(D1)

If
L\in[0,\infty)
, let
m\in\mathbb{R}
satisfy

\displaystyle\beta m,\;m\in(-1,1),

while in the case
L=\infty
, let
m=(m_{1},m_{2})\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
be given such that

\displaystyle m_{1},\;m_{2}\in(-1,1).

(D2)

The velocity fields have the regularities

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{tan}}(\Gamma)).

(D3)

At least one of the following conditions is fulfilled:

(D3.1)

\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
have the regularities

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{6/5}(\Omega))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{3}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{1+\omega}(\Gamma))\cap L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2+\omega}(\Gamma))

for some
\omega>0
.

(D3.2)

\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
have the regularities

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega))\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Omega)),

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Gamma))\cap L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{H}^{1}(\Gamma)),

and it holds that

K\in[0,\infty]
and
L\in[0,\infty]
. Additionally, if
K=0
, it holds

\displaystyle\mathbf{v}|_{\Gamma}=\mathbf{w}\hskip 20.00003pt\text{a.e.\penalty\ on\penalty\ }\Gamma\times\mathbb{R}.

The assumptions
(D1)
and

are imposed to ensure the well-posedness of system (

3.3

3.4

Definition 3.1

(Weak solution of system (

(A1)
–
(A2)

(S1)

if the following properties hold:

(i)

The functions
\phi
,
\psi
,
\mu

\displaystyle(\phi,\psi)

\displaystyle\in H^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\tau,\infty;(\mathcal{H}_{L,\beta}^{1})^{\prime})\cap L^{\infty}(\tau,\infty;\mathcal{H}_{K,\alpha}^{1}),

(3.1b)

(ii)

\phi

(3.2)

(iii)

The functions
\phi

\mu

(3.3b)

a.e. on

for all test functions

(iv)

The functions

(3.4)

for all

\phi

,
\mu

Lemma 3.2
.

Suppose that
(A1)
–
(A2)
,
(S1)
–
(S3)

(D1)

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\textnormal{div}}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{2}_{\mathrm{tan}}(\Gamma))

(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)

1.1

\displaystyle E_{\mathrm{free}}\big(\phi(t),\psi(t)\big)

\displaystyle\leq E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi_{\tau},\psi_{\tau})+\frac{1}{2}\int_{\tau}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s,

(3.6a)

\displaystyle\int_{\tau}^{t}\|(\mu(s),\theta(s))\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq 2E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi_{\tau},\psi_{\tau})+\int_{\tau}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}s,

4  Existence of a minimal pullback attractor

In contrast to previous works on the long-time dynamics of non-convective Cahn–Hilliard systems with or without dynamic boundary conditions (see, e.g.,
[
1
,
25
,
44
,
33
]
), additional challenges in our model arise because of the additional convection terms. As the prescribed velocity fields
\mathbf{v}
and
\mathbf{w}
may be time-dependent, the underlying dynamical system is non-autonomous. If the velocity fields are not constant in time, the dynamical system can no longer be interpreted as a semigroup, and the discussion of a possible attractor becomes more interesting.
Therefore, the non-autonomous nature of our model requires moving from the framework of semigroups and global attractors to the more general setting of two-parameter processes and pullback attractors.

To prove the existence of a pullback attractor, we introduce an additional assumption:

(D4)

The velocity fields have the additional regularities

(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\in L^{2}_{\mathrm{uloc}}(\mathbb{R};\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Omega)\times\mathbf{L}^{6}(\Gamma)),

and if
K=0
, we demand that
\alpha=\beta
.

4.1  Formulation as a process

We first introduce the concept of a two-parameter continuous process, which generalizes the notion of semigroups.

Definition 4.1

(Continuous process)
.

A
continuous process
on a metric space
X
is a family
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
of continuous mappings
S(t,\tau):X\to X
with the following properties:

(i)

For all
\tau\in\mathbb{R}
and
x\in X
, it holds
S(\tau,\tau)x=x
.

(ii)

For all
t\geq s\geq\tau
, it holds
S(t,\tau)=S(t,s)\,S(s,\tau)
.

(iii)

The mapping

\{(t,\tau)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}\,:\,t\geq\tau\}\times X\ni(t,\tau,x)\mapsto S(t,\tau)x\in X

is continuous.

The following lemma shows that the dynamical system associated with (
1.1
) can actually be formulated as a process.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose that
(A1)
–
(A2)
,
(S1)
–
(S3)

(D1)

The dynamical system is well-defined by Proposition

Continuity with respect to

\displaystyle\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\phi^{K,L}(t_{k}),\psi^{K,L}(t_{k}))\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}-\tfrac{1}{2}\|(\phi_{\tau},\psi_{\tau})\|_{K,\alpha}^{2}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt=E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi^{K,L}(t_{k}),\psi^{K,L}(t_{k}))-E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi_{\tau},\psi_{\tau})

\displaystyle\underset{k\to\infty}{\limsup}\;E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi^{K,L}(t_{k}),\psi^{K,L}(t_{k}))\leq E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi_{\tau},\psi_{\tau}).

4.2  Existence of a unique pullback attractor

Now, we are going to investigate the long-time behavior of the non-autonomous dynamical system
\big(S_{m}^{K,L}(t,\tau),\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}\big)
through the theory of pullback attractors. As a preliminary step, we first recall the fundamental framework, including the basic definitions and a key theorem concerning pullback attractors, whose assumptions will be verified for our system in the subsequent analysis. In the following, for any metric space
X
, we write
\mathcal{P}(X)
to denote the family of all non-empty subsets of
X
.

We start by recalling the main concepts related to pullback dynamics (see, e.g.,
[
48
]
), which will be used to establish our main result.

Definition 4.4

(Pullback absorbing sets)
.

Let
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
be a continuous process on a metric space
X
.
A family

\widehat{D}_{0}=\{D_{0}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of non-empty sets is called

pullback absorbing
for the process
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
, if for any bounded set
{D}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
and any
t\in\mathbb{R}
,
there exists a time
\tau_{0}(t,D)\leq t
such that

\displaystyle S(t,\tau)D\subset D_{0}(t)\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all }\tau\leq\tau_{0}(t,{D}).

In this case,
\tau_{0}(\cdot,\cdot)
is referred to as the corresponding
pullback absorption time
for the absorbing family
\widehat{D}_{0}
.

Definition 4.5

(Pullback attractor)
.

Let
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
be a continuous process on a metric space
X
.
A family
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
of non-empty subsets of
X

is called a
pullback attractor

for the process
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}

in
X
if

(i)

\mathcal{A}(t)
is compact in
X
for every
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

(ii)

\mathcal{A}
is invariant, i.e.,
S(t,\tau)\mathcal{A}(\tau)=\mathcal{A}(t)
for all
t\geq\tau
,

(iii)

\mathcal{A}
is pullback attracting, i.e., for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
and any bounded set
D
it holds

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\textnormal{dist\,}_{X}(S(t,\tau)D,\mathcal{A}(t))=0.

A pullback attractor
\mathcal{A}=\{\mathcal{A}(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
is referred to as
minimal
, if for every family
\widehat{C}=\{C(t)\}_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\in\mathcal{P}(X)
of closed sets, which satisfies

\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\textnormal{dist\,}_{X}(S(t,\tau)D,C(t))=0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all bounded sets $D$,}

it holds that
\mathcal{A}(t)\subset C(t)
for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
.

Remark 4.6
.

We point out that in the case of autonomous systems, the definition of the global attractor is simply that of a compact invariant and attracting set.
Indeed, these two properties are enough to conclude that the global attractor is minimal closed attracting (i.e., the smallest closed attracting set) and maximal invariant (i.e., the largest invariant set).
For non-autonomous systems, these properties become more subtle. Specifically, unlike in autonomous dynamical systems, the global pullback attractor defined here is neither the maximal invariant set attracting all bounded sets nor the minimal closed attracting set.
In particular, the definition above does not guarantee the crucial property that the attractor attracts itself, meaning that

\displaystyle\lim_{\tau\to-\infty}\textnormal{dist\,}(S(t,\tau)\mathcal{A}(\tau),\mathcal{A}(t))=0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{for all $t\in\mathbb{R}$}.

This explains why the minimality property must be assumed a priori in the definition above (leading to the minimal pullback attractor), as it does not follow from the other properties. This assumption guarantees the uniqueness of
\mathcal{A}
.
In order for the minimal and maximal properties to follow from the other assumptions, the attracting property must hold not just for bounded sets independent of time, but also for time-dependent families of sets belonging to a specific universe. This results in alternative definitions of pullback attractors (see, e.g.,
[
48
]
), which can be larger than the ones considered in the present paper.

We will use the following proposition to prove the existence of a pullback attractor for the process
S_{m}^{K,L}
(see
[
48
, Theorem 7]
and
[
23
, Proposition 3.7]
).

Proposition 4.7
.

Let
\{S(t,\tau)\}_{t\geq\tau}
be a continuous process on a metric space
X
.
Assume that there exists a compact attracting family of sets

-\Delta_{\Gamma}\xi+G^{\prime}(\xi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\zeta\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\geq|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\zeta,\xi)-E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})|^{1-\varpi^{\ast}}

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{K,L,m}
satisfying
\|(\zeta-\phi_{\infty},\xi-\psi_{\infty})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\leq b^{\ast}
.
Here,
\mathbf{P}_{L}
denotes the projection of
\mathcal{L}^{2}
onto

\begin{cases}\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}:\beta|\Omega|{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}+|\Gamma|{\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L\in[0,\infty),\\
\{\left(\phi,\psi\right)\in\mathcal{L}^{2}:{\langle\phi\rangle}_{\Omega}={\langle\psi\rangle}_{\Gamma}=0\}&\text{if\penalty\ }L=\infty.\end{cases}

For
K=0
,
L\in[0,\infty]
and
\alpha=1
, the result can be found in
[
44
, Lemma 5.2]
, and for
K\in[0,\infty)
,
L=\infty
and
\alpha=1
, it was established in
[
47
, Lemma 5.2]
. For general
K\in[0,\infty]
and
\alpha\in[-1,1]
as specified by
(A1)
, the statement can be verified analogously.

Theorem 5.9

(Convergence to an equilibrium)
.

Suppose that the assumptions
(A1)
–
(A2)
,
(S1)
-
(S3)
and
(D1)
-
(D3)
are fulfilled. Moreover, suppose that
(D5)
holds with
a>0
.
In addition, we assume that
F_{1},G_{1}
are real analytic on
(-1,1)
and
F_{2},G_{2}
are real analytic on
\mathbb{R}
.
Let
(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\in\mathcal{X}_{K,L,m}
, and let
(\phi,\psi,\mu,\theta)
denote the corresponding weak solution to (
1.1
) on
[0,\infty)
.

Then there exists
(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\in\mathcal{N}^{K,L}_{m}
such that

\displaystyle\lim_{t\rightarrow\infty}\|(\phi(t),\psi(t))-(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\|_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}=0.

(5.15)

Since
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma}_{K,L,m}
with
\sigma\in(\frac{d}{2},2)
(see Lemma
5.3
), the embedding
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma}_{K,L,m}\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})\times C(\Gamma)
implies that
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is also a compact subset of
C(\overline{\Omega})\times C(\Gamma)
.
Then, as
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\subset\mathcal{N}_{m}^{K,L}
and any pair
(\phi,\psi)\in\mathcal{N}^{K,L}_{m}
(see Lemma
5.6
) is strictly separated from the pure phases (see Lemma
5.7
), we can argue by contradiction (see, for instance,
[
1
]
or
[
20
, Proof of Lemma 3.11]
) to show that there exists
\delta\in(0,1)
such that

\displaystyle\|\phi_{*}\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})}\leq 1-\delta,\hskip 10.00002pt\|\psi_{*}\|_{C(\Gamma)}\leq 1-\delta\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all\penalty\ }(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})\in\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}).

Here, it is crucial that the width of the separation layer
\delta
is independent of
(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})\in\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
.

Moreover, we know from Lemma
5.3
that

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist\,}_{\mathcal{W}^{\sigma}_{K,L,m}}((\phi(t),\psi(t)),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0}))\to 0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as\penalty\ }t\to\infty,

(5.16)

with
\sigma\in\big(\frac{d}{2},2\big)
.

As the embedding
\mathcal{W}^{s}_{K,L,m}\hookrightarrow C(\overline{\Omega})\times C(\Gamma)
is compact, there
T_{S}>0
such that

\displaystyle\|(\phi(t),\psi(t))\|_{C(\overline{\Omega})\times C(\Gamma)}\leq 1-\frac{\delta}{2},\hskip 20.00003pt\text{for all\penalty\ }t\geq T_{S}.

(5.17)

Thanks to this uniform separation property, we can apply Corollary
3.6
to infer that

\displaystyle\|(\phi,\psi)\|_{L^{\infty}(T_{S},\infty;\mathcal{H}^{3})}\leq C.

(5.18)

In particular, this entails that
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}^{3}
.

Now, to show that
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a singleton, we need to make use of the additional assumption
(D5)
. Using Remark
5.1
, we deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1-2\varpi}{1-\varpi}}\;\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\left(\int_{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{as}\big\|\big(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s)\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1-2\varpi}{1-\varpi}}\left(\int_{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{-a\frac{1-2\varpi}{\varpi}s}\;\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{\varpi}{1-\varpi}}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\Big(\frac{\varpi}{a(1-2\varpi)}\Big)^{\frac{\varpi}{1-\varpi}}\left(\int_{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{as}\big\|\big(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s)\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}s\right)^{\frac{1-2\varpi}{1-\varpi}}

for all
\varpi\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
.
This entails that

\displaystyle\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{L^{\frac{1-2\varpi}{1-\varpi}}(T_{\mathrm{dec}},\infty;\mathcal{L}^{2})}\leq\Big(\frac{\varpi}{a(1-2\varpi)}\Big)^{\frac{\varpi}{1-2\varpi}}\int_{T_{\mathrm{dec}}}^{\infty}\mathrm{e}^{as}\big\|\big(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s)\big)\big\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\;\mathrm{d}s

(5.19)

for all
\varpi\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
.
Notice that the assumptions
(D3)
and
(D5)
readily imply that
\mathbf{v}(t)\to\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Omega)
and
\mathbf{w}(t)\to\mathbf{0}
in
\mathbf{L}^{2}(\Gamma)
as
t\to\infty
.

Now, thanks to (
5.16
), (
5.18
) and the compact embedding
\mathcal{H}^{3}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{2}
, we have

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist\,}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big((\phi(t),\psi(t)),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\big)\to 0\hskip 20.00003pt\text{as\penalty\ }t\rightarrow\infty.

(5.20)

Moreover, as
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{W}^{\sigma}_{K,L,m}
(see Lemma
5.3
) and a bounded subset of
\mathcal{H}^{3}
, we conclude by interpolation that
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
.

Now, for any
(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})\in\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
, let
\varpi^{*}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
and
b^{*}>0
be as obtained by Lemma
5.8
. Then, we obviously have

\displaystyle\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\subset\bigcup_{(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})\in\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})}B_{b^{*}}(\phi_{*},\psi_{*}).

where
B_{b^{*}}(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})
denotes the open ball in
\mathcal{H}^{2}
with center
(\phi_{*},\psi_{*})
and radius
b_{*}
.
As
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})
is a compact subset of
\mathcal{H}^{2}
, there exist finitely many points
\{(\phi^{*}_{i},\psi^{*}_{i})\}_{i=1,...,M}
with corresponding radii
\{b^{*}_{i}\}_{i=1,...,M}

such that

\displaystyle\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\subset\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}B_{b^{*}_{i}}(\phi^{*}_{i},\psi^{*}_{i})

and the Łojasiewicz–Simon inequality is fulfilled on each of these balls with an exponent
\varpi_{i}^{*}\in(0,\tfrac{1}{2})
. This means that for every
i\in\{1,...,M\}
, there exists a constant
C_{i}>0
such that

\displaystyle C_{i}\left\|{\mathbf{P}_{L}}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta\zeta+F^{\prime}(\zeta)\\
-\Delta_{\Gamma}\xi+G^{\prime}(\xi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\zeta\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\geq|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\zeta,\xi)-E_{*}|^{1-\varpi^{*}_{i}}

(5.21)

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in B_{b^{*}_{i}}(\phi^{*}_{i},\psi^{*}_{i})
. Now, we define

\displaystyle C\coloneqq\underset{i=1,...,M}{\max}\,C_{i}\,>0\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt\varpi^{*}\coloneqq\underset{i=1,...,M}{\min}\,\varpi^{*}_{i}\in\big(0,\tfrac{1}{2}\big).

As the balls
B_{b^{*}_{i}}(\phi^{*}_{i},\psi^{*}_{i})
,
i=1,...,M
, are open, there further exists

\displaystyle 0<b<\underset{i=1,...,M}{\min}b^{*}_{i}

such that for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{K,L,m}
, it holds that

\displaystyle(\zeta,\xi)\in\bigcup_{i=1}^{M}B_{b^{*}_{i}}(\phi^{*}_{i},\psi^{*}_{i})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if}\hskip 10.00002pt\textnormal{dist\,}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big((\zeta,\xi),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\big)\leq b.

Hence, in view of (
5.21
), we conclude that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&C\left\|{\mathbf{P}_{L}}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta\zeta+F^{\prime}(\zeta)\\
-\Delta_{\Gamma}\xi+G^{\prime}(\xi)+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\zeta\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\geq|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\zeta,\xi)-E_{*}|^{1-\varpi^{*}}\end{split}

(5.22)

for all
(\zeta,\xi)\in\mathcal{W}^{2}_{K,L,m}
with

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist\,}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big((\zeta,\xi),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\big)\leq b\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\zeta,\xi)-E_{*}|\leq 1.

Recalling Lemma
5.6
and the convergence (
5.20
), we conclude that there exists a time
T_{F}>\max\{T_{S},T_{\mathrm{dec}}\}
such that

\displaystyle\textnormal{dist\,}_{\mathcal{H}^{2}}\big((\phi(t),\psi(t)),\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})\big)\leq b\hskip 10.00002pt\text{and}\hskip 10.00002pt|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))-E_{*}|\leq 1

for all
t\geq T_{F}
.
Thus, employing (
5.22
), we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))-E_{*}|^{1-\varpi^{\ast}}&\leq C\left\|{\mathbf{P}_{L}}\begin{pmatrix}-\Delta\phi(t)+F^{\prime}(\phi(t)),\\
-\Delta_{\Gamma}\psi(t)+G^{\prime}(\psi(t))+\alpha\partial_{\mathbf{n}}\phi(t)\end{pmatrix}\right\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}\\[4.30554pt]
&\leq C\|(\mu(t),\theta(t))\|_{L,\beta}.\end{split}

(5.23)

for all
t\geq T_{F}
. Now, using
[
13
, Lemma 7.1]
, the assertion can be established by following the line of argument in
[
33
]
. To this end, let
s,t\geq T_{F}
be arbitrary. Then, recalling (
5.17
), we use the energy inequality (
(v)
) to deduce that

\displaystyle\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(s),\psi(s))-E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Omega}\phi\mathbf{v}\cdot\nabla\mu\;\mathrm{d}x\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s}^{t}\int_{\Gamma}\psi\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla_{\Gamma}\theta\;\mathrm{d}\Gamma\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v}(\tau),\mathbf{w}(\tau))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(s),\psi(s))-E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r+\frac{1}{2}\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r.

After rearranging some terms and elevating to the power
2(1-\varpi^{\ast})>0
, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq\left(E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(s),\psi(s))-E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))+\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(s),\psi(s))-E_{*}|^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}+2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))-E_{*}|^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 20.00003pt+2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{t}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}.

Now, we send
t\to\infty
. Recalling that
E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(t),\psi(t))\to E_{*}
as
t\to\infty
, we apply (
5.23
) to infer that

\displaystyle\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}|E_{\mathrm{free}}(\phi(s),\psi(s))-E_{*}|^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}+2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}C\|(\mu(s),\theta(s))\|^{2}_{L,\beta}+2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}.

Since
T_{F}>T_{\mathrm{dec}}
, the mapping
(T_{F},\infty)\ni t\mapsto\|(\mathbf{v}(t),\mathbf{w}(t))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}
is non-increasing according to assumption
(D5)
. Thus, using the estimate (
5.19
), we deduce that

\displaystyle 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}\left(\|(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1}{1-\varpi^{\ast}}}\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{\frac{1-2\varpi^{\ast}}{1-\varpi^{\ast}}}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq 2^{2(\frac{1}{2}-\varpi^{\ast})}C\|(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Hence, we conclude that

\displaystyle\left(\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L,\beta}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r+\int_{s}^{\infty}\|(\mathbf{v},\mathbf{w})\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\;\mathrm{d}r\right)^{2(1-\varpi^{\ast})}

\displaystyle\hskip 10.00002pt\leq C\left(\|(\mu(s),\theta(s))\|^{2}_{L,\beta}+\|(\mathbf{v}(s),\mathbf{w}(s))\|_{\mathcal{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)

for almost all
s\geq T_{F}
. According to
[
13
, Lemma 7.1]
, this yields

\displaystyle\|(\mu,\theta)\|_{L^{1}(T_{*},\infty)}\leq C

for some
T_{*}>0
.
Recalling that
(\mathbf{v}(t),\mathbf{w}(t))\to(\mathbf{0},\mathbf{0})
in
\mathcal{L}^{2}
as
t\to\infty
,
we infer from (
1.1a
) and (
1.1c
) that

\displaystyle(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\in L^{1}(T_{*},\infty;(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}).

Hence, using the fundamental theorem of calculus, we conclude that, as
t\to\infty
,

\displaystyle(\phi(t),\psi(t))\to(\phi(T_{*}),\psi(T_{*}))+\int_{T_{*}}^{\infty}(\partial_{t}\phi,\partial_{t}\psi)\;\mathrm{d}r=:(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\hskip 10.00002pt\text{in $(\mathcal{H}^{1}_{L,\beta})^{\prime}$}.

This allows us to identify
\omega^{K,L}(\phi_{0},\psi_{0})=\{(\phi_{\infty},\psi_{\infty})\}
, which completes the proof, since (
5.15
) directly comes from (
5.20
).
∎
