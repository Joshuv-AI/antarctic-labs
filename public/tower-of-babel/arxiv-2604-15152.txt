The Multinomial Allocation Model and the Random Box Load
Serik Sagitov

Abstract
We revisit the random allocation model in which n n balls are independently placed into N N boxes with probabilities q 1 , … , q N q_{1},\ldots,q_{N} . A classical asymptotic result due to Kolchin, Sevastyanov, and Chistyakov for the expectations, variances, and covariances of the occupancy counts is reformulated in a compact and transparent form in terms of the load of a randomly selected box. We further derive explicit two-sided bounds for the associated remainder terms, obtained under weaker assumptions than those previously required.



Keywords:
Occupancy problem; multinomial allocation; two-sided bounds.

1
Introduction

The classical occupancy problem is a cornerstone of probability theory and combinatorics
[
2
]
, with deep connections to statistics, computer science, and population biology. In its simplest form,
n
balls are independently placed into
N
boxes, each selected with equal probability
1/N
. Fundamental quantities of interest include the number of empty boxes
\hat{N}_{0}
, the number
\hat{N}_{r}
of boxes containing exactly
r
balls, and the maximum occupancy; see
[
3
]
for a recent treatment of the latter.

A natural generalization replaces the equiprobable allocation by the
multinomial allocation model

[
4
,
1
]
. In this setting, each box
k
is chosen with its own probability
q_{k}
, where

q_{1}\geq q_{2}\geq\cdots\geq q_{N}>0,\qquad q_{1}+\cdots+q_{N}=1.

Thus, the vector
(q_{1},\ldots,q_{N})
describes how the load is distributed across boxes, allowing for heterogeneous box weights.

In this paper, we revisit Theorem 5 in
[
4
, Ch. III.1]
, which analyzes the multinomial allocation model in the so-called
central region
. To make the result more transparent, we restate it as Theorem A in terms of the empirical proportions

\hat{q}_{r}:=\frac{\hat{N}_{r}}{N},\qquad r=0,1,\ldots,n.

Here,
\hat{q}_{r}
is the proportion of boxes containing exactly
r
balls; in particular,
\hat{q}_{0}
is the proportion of empty boxes. These quantities form a probability distribution on
\{0,\ldots,n\}
, since

\sum_{r=0}^{n}\hat{q}_{r}=1,

and their first moment satisfies

\sum_{r=1}^{n}r\hat{q}_{r}=\alpha,\qquad\alpha:=\frac{n}{N},

where
\alpha
is the average number of balls per box (the
average load
).

Our reformulation is based on a simple randomization device. Let

\xi=nq_{X},\qquad X\sim\mathrm{Uniform}\{1,\ldots,N\},

so that
X
selects a box uniformly at random. The random variable
\xi
can be interpreted as the load of a randomly chosen box. By construction,

\mathrm{E}[\xi]=\alpha.

Moreover,

0<\alpha\leq\beta,\qquad\beta:=nq_{1},

where
\beta
is the maximal possible value of
\xi
. The case
\alpha=\beta
characterizes the equiprobable allocation model, in which
\xi\equiv\alpha
is deterministic.

Theorem A

(
[
4
]
)
.

Assume that, as
n\to\infty
, there exist positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, independent of
n
, such that

C_{1}\leq\alpha\leq\beta\leq C_{2}.

(1)

For all non-negative integers
r
, define

p_{r}(x):=\frac{x^{r}}{r!}e^{-x}.

Then, for any fixed
r\neq t
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]+O(n^{-1}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\alpha\mathrm{E}\!\left[p_{r}(\xi)\bigl(1-p_{r}(\xi)\bigr)\right]-n^{-1}\!\left(\mathrm{E}\!\left[p_{r}(\xi)(\xi-r)\right]\right)^{2}+O(n^{-2}),

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[\hat{q}_{r},\hat{q}_{t}]

\displaystyle=-n^{-1}\alpha\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)p_{t}(\xi)]-n^{-1}\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)(\xi-r)]\,\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)(\xi-t)]+O(n^{-2}).

Our main result, presented in the next section, extends the approximations in Theorem A by allowing the parameters of the model
(N,q_{1},\ldots,q_{N})
to depend on
n
, without imposing the restriction (
1
).

Main result

Theorem 1
.

For
0\leq r,t\leq n
and
r\neq t
, the following three approximation formulas hold

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]+(2n)^{-1}\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)(r-(\xi-r)^{2})]+n^{-2}R_{1}(n,r),

(2)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\alpha\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)(1-p_{r}(\xi))]-n^{-1}(\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)(\xi-r)])^{2}+n^{-2}(R_{2}(n,r,r)+\alpha R_{1}(n,r)),

(3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}[\hat{q}_{r},\hat{q}_{t}]

\displaystyle=-n^{-1}\alpha\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)p_{t}(\xi)]-n^{-1}\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)(\xi-r)]\,\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)(\xi-t)]+n^{-2}R_{2}(n,r,t),

(4)

where provided
q_{1}\leq 1/4
,

\displaystyle-(r!)^{-1}\beta^{r}(r^{3}2^{r-1}+4)

\displaystyle\leq R_{1}(n,r)\leq(r!)^{-1}\beta^{r}r^{2}(2r^{2}+4\beta^{2}),

(5)

\displaystyle-(r!t!)^{-1}\beta^{2u}L_{2}(u,\beta)

\displaystyle\leq R_{2}(n,r,t)\leq(r!t!)^{-1}\beta^{2u}K_{2}(u,\beta),

(6)

with
u=\max(r,t)
and

\displaystyle L_{2}(r,\beta)

\displaystyle=12+2\beta+5(r^{3}+\beta)2^{r}+\beta^{2}(r^{2}+1),

\displaystyle K_{2}(r,\beta)

\displaystyle=8+12r^{3}2^{r}+4\beta r+\beta^{2}2^{2r+6}r^{2}.

Corollary 1
.

For the proportion of empty boxes
\hat{q}_{0}
, we get

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{0}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[e^{-\xi}]-(2n)^{-1}\mathrm{E}[\xi^{2}e^{-\xi}]+n^{-2}R_{1}(n),\qquad\qquad\qquad\qquad

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\hat{q}_{0}]

\displaystyle=n^{-1}\alpha\mathrm{E}[e^{-\xi}(1-e^{-\xi})]-n^{-1}(\mathrm{E}[\xi e^{-\xi}])^{2}+n^{-2}R_{2}(n),

with

-4\leq R_{1}(n)\leq 0,\qquad-\beta^{2}-7\beta-4\alpha-12\leq R_{2}(n)\leq 8.

Example.

Turning to the equiprobable allocation, we obtain

\displaystyle-4n^{-2}\leq\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{0}]-e^{-\alpha}+\tfrac{1}{2}n^{-1}\alpha^{2}e^{-\alpha}\leq 0,

(7)

\displaystyle-(\alpha^{2}+11\alpha+12)n^{-2}\leq\;

\displaystyle\mathrm{Var}[\hat{q}_{0}]-n^{-1}\bigl(\alpha e^{-\alpha}-\alpha e^{-2\alpha}-\alpha^{2}e^{-2\alpha}\bigr)\leq 8n^{-2}.

\tfrac{1}{2}n^{-1}\alpha^{2}e^{-\alpha}

n>\tfrac{1}{8}\alpha^{-2}e^{\alpha}.

Auxiliary results

Lemma 1
.

Define

\Phi_{n}(r,t)=\binom{n}{r,t,n-r-t}\Big(\mathrm{E}\!\left[q_{X}^{r}q_{Y}^{t}(1-q_{X}-q_{Y})_{+}^{\,n-r-t}\right]-N^{-1}\mathrm{E}\!\left[q_{X}^{r+t}(1-2q_{X})_{+}^{\,n-r-t}\right]\Big)-\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{t}],

where
q_{Y}
is an independent copy of
q_{X}
, and
(a)_{+}:=\max(a,0)
.
Then for all
r\neq t
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r}\,\mathrm{E}\!\left[q_{X}^{r}(1-q_{X})^{n-r}\right],\qquad\mathrm{Var}[\hat{q}_{r}]=\Phi_{n}(r,r)+N^{-1}\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}],\qquad\mathrm{Cov}[\hat{q}_{r},\hat{q}_{t}]=\Phi_{n}(r,t).

Proof.

According to formulas (8), (9), and (10) in
[
4
, Ch. III.1]
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{N}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r}\sum_{k=1}^{N}q_{k}^{r}(1-q_{k})^{\,n-r},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{N}_{r}(\hat{N}_{r}-1)]

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r,r,n-2r}\sum_{k\neq l}q_{k}^{r}q_{l}^{r}(1-q_{k}-q_{l})^{\,n-2r},

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{N}_{r}\hat{N}_{t}]

\displaystyle=\binom{n}{r,t,n-r-t}\sum_{k\neq l}q_{k}^{r}q_{l}^{t}(1-q_{k}-q_{l})^{\,n-r-t},\quad r\neq t.

Observe that for any functions
F(\cdot)
and
G(\cdot,\cdot)
,

\displaystyle N^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{N}F(q_{k})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[F(q_{X})],

\displaystyle N^{-2}\sum_{k,l=1}^{N}G(q_{k},q_{l})

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[G(q_{X},q_{Y})].

Hence,

N^{-2}\sum_{k\neq l}G(q_{k},q_{l})=\mathrm{E}[G(q_{X},q_{Y})]-N^{-1}\mathrm{E}[G(q_{X},q_{Y})],

which yields the stated expressions and completes the proof.
∎

Remark.
Note that if
q_{1}\leq\tfrac{1}{2}
, then

(1-q_{X}-q_{Y})_{+}=1-q_{X}-q_{Y},\qquad(1-2q_{X})_{+}=1-2q_{X}.

We impose the stronger condition
q_{1}\leq\tfrac{1}{4}
in order to apply Lemma
3
below.

Lemma 2
.

For
0\leq r\leq n
,
n\geq 1
, define
\varphi_{n}(\cdot)
by

\frac{n!}{(n-r)!}=n^{r}(1-n^{-1}\varphi_{n}(r)).

Then

0\leq\varphi_{n}(r)\leq\binom{r}{2},

and moreover,
\varphi^{*}_{n}(r)
defined by

\varphi_{n}(r)=\binom{r}{2}-n^{-1}\varphi^{*}_{n}(r),

satisfies

0\leq\varphi^{*}_{n}(r)\leq r^{4},\quad 0\leq r\leq n.

Proof.

Since

\varphi_{n}(r)=\frac{n^{r}-n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}{n^{r-1}}=\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}kn^{1-k}(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1),

we get

0\leq\varphi_{n}(r)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}k=\binom{r}{2}.

It remains to observe that

\displaystyle n^{-1}\varphi^{*}_{n}(r)

\displaystyle=\binom{r}{2}-\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}kn^{1-k}(n-1)\cdots(n-k+1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}k\Big(1-\big(1-\tfrac{1}{n}\big)\cdots\big(1-\tfrac{k-1}{n}\big)\Big)\leq n^{-1}\sum_{k=1}^{r-1}k^{3}\leq n^{-1}r^{4}.

∎

Lemma 3
.

For
n\geq 1
and
0\leq r\leq n
, define
\delta_{n}(r,x)
by

\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n-r}=e^{-x}+n^{-1}\delta_{n}(r,x).

If
0\leq x\leq\frac{n}{2}
, then

-2\leq\delta_{n}(r,x)\leq r2^{r-1},

(9)

and moreover,
\delta^{*}_{n}(r,x)
defined by

\delta_{n}(r,x)=e^{-x}x(r-x/2)+n^{-1}\delta^{*}_{n}(r,x),

satisfies

-4-r\leq\delta^{*}_{n}(r,x)\leq x^{2}r(r+1)2^{\,r+1}.

(10)

Proof.

We use the representation

\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{n-r}=\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{-r}e^{\,n\ln(1-x/n)}

and repeatedly apply Taylor’s theorem with Lagrange remainder. By

\ln(1-y)=-y-\frac{y^{2}}{2(1-\theta y)^{2}},\qquad\theta\in[0,1],

we have

-y-2y^{2}\leq\ln(1-y)\leq-y,\quad 0\leq y\leq 1/2.

With
y=\frac{x}{n}
, it follows that

-x-\frac{2x^{2}}{n}\leq n\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)\leq-x,\quad 0\leq x\leq n/2.

Exponentiating and using the inequality
e^{-t}\geq 1-t
for
t\geq 0
, we obtain

e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{2x^{2}}{n}\right)\leq e^{\,n\ln(1-x/n)}\leq e^{-x}.

On the other hand,

1\leq\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{-r}\leq 1+\frac{xr}{n}2^{\,r}.

Combining the bounds, we find

-2x^{2}e^{-x}\leq\delta_{n}(r,x)\leq e^{-x}xr2^{\,r},

which yields (
9
) in view of
e^{-x}x<1/2
and
e^{-x}x^{2}<1
.

The higher order Taylor’s expansion,

\ln(1-y)=-y-\frac{y^{2}}{2}-\frac{y^{3}}{3(1-\theta y)^{3}},\qquad\theta\in[0,1],

yields

-x-\frac{x^{2}}{2n}-\frac{8x^{3}}{3n^{2}}\leq n\ln\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)\leq-x-\frac{x^{2}}{2n},\quad 0\leq x\leq n/2.

Exponentiating and using

e^{-t}=1-t+\frac{t^{2}}{2}e^{-\theta^{\prime}t},\quad t\geq 0,\qquad\theta^{\prime}\in[0,1],

we obtain

e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{2n}-\frac{8x^{3}}{3n^{2}}\right)\leq e^{\,n\ln(1-x/n)}\leq e^{-x}\left(1-\frac{x^{2}}{2n}\right)+\frac{x^{4}}{4n^{2}}.

On the other hand,

\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{-r}=1+\frac{xr}{n}+\frac{r(1+r)x^{2}}{2n^{2}}\left(1-\frac{\theta^{\prime\prime}x}{n}\right)^{-r-2},\qquad\theta^{\prime\prime}\in[0,1],

yields

1+\frac{rx}{n}\leq\left(1-\frac{x}{n}\right)^{-r}\leq 1+\frac{rx}{n}+\frac{r(1+r)x^{2}}{n^{2}}2^{r+1},

Combining these bounds, and using
e^{-x}x^{3}<3/2
, we establish (
10
).
∎

Lemma 4
.

Denote

Q_{n}(r,t):=(r!t!)^{-1}\mathrm{E}\Big[\xi^{r}\eta^{t}(1-q_{X}-q_{Y})^{n-r-t}\Big],

where
\eta=nq_{Y}
.
Then

Q_{n}(r,t)=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)]+n^{-1}(r!t!)^{-1}\mathrm{E}\Big[\xi^{r}\eta^{t}\delta_{n}(r+t,\xi+\eta)\Big],

(11)

\displaystyle Q_{n}(r,t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)]+n^{-1}\Big((r+t)\big(\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)\xi]+\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)\xi]\big)-\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)\xi]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)\xi]\Big)

\displaystyle-(2n)^{-1}\Big(\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)\xi^{2}]+\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)\xi^{2}]\Big)+n^{-2}(r!t!)^{-1}\mathrm{E}\Big[\xi^{r}\eta^{r}\delta_{n}^{*}(r+t,\xi+\eta)\Big].

(12)

Proof.

Relation (

) follows from

(1-q_{X}-q_{Y})^{n-r-t}=e^{-\xi-\eta}+n^{-1}\delta_{n}(r+t,\xi+\eta).

On the other hand, in terms of the function

, we have

\displaystyle Q_{n}(r,t)

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]\mathrm{E}[p_{t}(\xi)]+n^{-1}\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)p_{t}(\eta)(\xi+\eta)(r+t-2^{-1}(\xi+\eta))]

\displaystyle+n^{-2}(r!t!)^{-1}\mathrm{E}\Big[\xi^{r}\eta^{r}\delta_{n}^{*}(r+t,\xi+\eta)\Big],

leading to (

).
∎

Lemma 5
.

Assuming
q_{1}\leq 1/4
, define
R_{0}(n,r)
by

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]+n^{-1}R_{0}(n,r).

Then for
0\leq r\leq n
and
n\geq 1

-(r!)^{-1}\beta^{r}(r^{2}+2)\leq R_{0}(n,r)\leq(r!)^{-1}r(2\beta)^{r}.

Using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle={n\choose r}\mathrm{E}[q_{X}^{r}(1-q_{X})^{n-r}]=(r!)^{-1}\Big(1-n^{-1}\varphi_{n}(r)\Big)\mathrm{E}[\xi^{r}(1-n^{-1}\xi)^{n-r}].

Further, using Lemma

\displaystyle\mathrm{E}[\hat{q}_{r}]

\displaystyle=\mathrm{E}[p_{r}(\xi)]+(r!n)^{-1}\mathrm{E}[\xi^{r}\delta_{n}(r,\xi))]-(r!n)^{-1}\varphi_{n}(r)\mathrm{E}[\xi^{r}(1-n^{-1}\xi)^{n-r}].

and the stated upper and lower bounds follow from Lemmas
