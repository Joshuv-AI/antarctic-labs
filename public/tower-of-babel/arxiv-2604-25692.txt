Interlacing of zeros of polynomials completed with two additional points
Kerstin Jordaan, Vikash Kumar

Abstract
We investigate completed interlacing of zeros for pairs of polynomial sequences that fail to interlace by exactly two points. Using a general mixed recurrence relation, we identify a quadratic polynomial whose zeros serve as the two extra points required to achieve complete interlacing. We determine the precise positions of these two extra points relative to the zeros of the higher-degree polynomial, thereby establishing full interlacing results. The theory is applied to several classical families of orthogonal polynomials. In the Jacobi case, we improve earlier results by giving explicit extra points that complete the interlacing of P n ( α , β ) P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)} and P n + 1 ( α + 1 , β + 1 ) P_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)} . Second, we address an open question regarding the interlacing of zeros for Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials of consecutive degree with parameter increased by one. Finally, we establish new interlacing results for Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials.

Introduction

Let
\mu
be a positive Borel measure on the real line
\mathbb{R}
whose support, denoted by
supp(\mu)
, contains infinitely many points. Let
P_{n}(x)=x^{n}+\dots
be a monic polynomial of exact degree
n
and we say that
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n\in\mathcal{I}}
forms an orthogonal polynomials sequence with respect to
\mu
if

\displaystyle\int_{supp(\mu)}P_{n}(x)P_{m}(x)d\mu(x)=\kappa_{n}\delta_{n,m},\quad\text{for all }n,m\in\mathcal{I},

(1.1)

where
\kappa_{n}>0
. The index set
\mathcal{I}
consists of all non-negative integers
n
for which the moment
m_{n}=\int_{supp(\mu)}x^{n}d\mu(x)
is finite. Specifically,
\mathcal{I}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
if all moments exist, or
\mathcal{I}=\{0,1,...,N\}
if only a finite number of moments exist. For instance, the index set
\mathcal{I}
for Jacobi and Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials is
\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, whereas for Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials, the index set is finite
[
15
]
. The well-known properties of the orthogonality relation are that for each
n\in\mathcal{I}
, the zeros of orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)
are all real, distinct, and simple. These zeros lie within the interior of the support of the measure
\mu

[
12
]
. The orthogonality also ensures that the zeros of consecutive degree polynomials satisfy a specific pattern known as the interlacing property (or separation theorem)
[
3
]
.

The zeros of any two polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}
interlace, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{n}\prec\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}
, if

\displaystyle x_{1,n}<y_{1,n-1}<x_{2,n}<y_{2,n-1}<\cdots<y_{n-1,n-1}<x_{n,n},

(1.2)

where
\{x_{k,n}\}_{k=1}^{n}
and
\{y_{k,n-1}\}_{k=1}^{n-1}
denotes the zeros of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{Q}_{n-1}
respectively in ascending order, while we say that the zeros of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
alternate the zeros of
\mathcal{Q}_{n}
, denoted
\mathcal{P}_{n}\prec\mathcal{Q}_{n}
, if

\displaystyle x_{1,n}<y_{1,n}<x_{2,n}<y_{2,n}<\cdots<y_{n-1,n}<x_{n,n}<y_{n,n}.

(1.3)

The computational relevance of such zero configurations comes in part from Gaussian quadrature, since the zeros of orthogonal polynomials are the quadrature nodes, and they are commonly computed through the eigenvalues of the associated Jacobi matrix (cf.
[
11
,
10
]
). Interlacing is therefore useful in the analysis and comparison of quadrature nodes arising from related polynomial families (cf.
[
7
]
).

The interlacing property of two orthogonal polynomials,
P_{n}
and
P_{m}
, from the same family (where
m-n>1
) was first studied by Stieltjes. He showed (cf.
[
16
, page 46]
) that for
m>n+1
, at least one zero of
P_{m}
lies between any two consecutive zeros of
P_{n}
. This result is commonly referred to as Stieltjes interlacing. This was further extended in
[
2
,
4
]
by identifying a polynomial
d_{m-n-1}
of degree
m-n-1
that helps to complete the interlacing property as
d_{m-n-1}P_{n}\prec P_{m}
, provided that
P_{n}
and
P_{m}
do not have common zero. The polynomials
d_{m-n-1}
were initially introduced as dual polynomials by de Boor and Saff
[
4
]
and subsequently identified as the associated polynomials of
P_{m}
in the work of Vinet and Zhedanov
[
17
]
. Beyond the case of a single sequence of orthogonal polynomials, a polynomial
d_{k}
of degree
k
can also complete the interlacing of zeros between two different systems of orthogonal polynomials. For instance, in
[
8
]
a first-degree polynomial
d_{1,t}
was identified such that
d_{1,t}L_{n-1}^{(\alpha+t)}\prec L_{n+1}^{(\alpha)}
for
t\in\{0,1,2,3,4\}
, where
L_{n}^{(\alpha)}
denotes the Laguerre polynomial of degree
n
with parameter
\alpha>-1
. Similarly,
[
9
]
identified a second-degree polynomial
d_{2,t}
such that
d_{2,t}C_{n-2}^{(\lambda+t)}\prec C_{n+1}^{(\lambda)}
for
t\in\{0,1,2,3\}
, where
C_{n}^{(\lambda)}
represents the ultraspherical (Gegenbauer) polynomial of degree
n
and parameter
\lambda>-1/2
.

It is possible to relax the constraint of orthogonality to identify a polynomial
d_{l}
of degree
l
that completes the interlacing of zeros between two arbitrary polynomials from different sequences, (cf.
[
5
,
13
,
14
]
).

Let
\mathcal{P}_{i}
,
\mathcal{G}_{j}
, and
\mathcal{Q}_{k}
denote polynomials of degrees
i
,
j
, and
k
, respectively, whose zeros are real and lie in an interval
(a,b)
. Assume that
\mathcal{Q}_{k}\prec\mathcal{G}_{j}
whenever
k=j+1
or
\mathcal{G}_{j}\prec\mathcal{Q}_{k}
whenever
k=j-1
. These polynomials satisfy the general mixed recurrence relation

\displaystyle A(x)\mathcal{P}_{i}(x)=B(x)\mathcal{G}_{j}(x)+d_{l}(x)\mathcal{Q}_{k}(x),

(1.4)

where
A(x)
,
B(x)
, and
d_{l}(x)
are continuous functions on

.

By applying the mixed recurrence relation (
1.4
) with

, and
d_{1}(x)=x-E
,
[
13
, Theorem 2.1]
uses

to complete the interlacing of zeros of
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
and
\mathcal{G}_{n}(x)
and applies the result to Meixner-Pollaczek, pseudo-Jacobi and continuous Hahn polynomials. Extending this framework,
[
14
]
develops various general results for completing interlacing by identifying the degree one polynomials. These general results are applied to several orthogonal families (Laguerre, Jacobi, Krawtchouk and Meixner polynomials), as well as a class of non-orthogonal polynomials (Narayana polynomials), to establish new completed interlacing results using degree one polynomials.

Motivated by these results, a natural question arises regarding completed interlacing of two distinct polynomial sequences that fail to fully interlace by exactly two points. Specifically, we aim to explicitly identify two additional points (zeros of quadratic polynomials) crucial for completing the interlacing.

To achieve this, we prove the complete interlacing results for two distinct general polynomials by utilizing the general mixed recurrence relation with
i=n
,
j=n+1
,
k=n
, and
d_{2}(x)=\mp(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2})
. This methodology enables us to refine existing results and resolve open problems in the literature. On the one hand, we improve upon
[
1
, Theorem 2]
by establishing the complete interlacing of the zeros of Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}
and
P_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}
using two explicitly determined extra points. On the other hand, we answer the open question posed in
[
13
]
for Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials by identifying the precise conditions under which the zeros of
P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)
and
P_{n+1}^{(\lambda+1)}(x;\phi)
interlace. Finally, we establish an analogous result concerning completing interlacing of the zeros of Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}(x;a,b)
and
P_{n+1}(x;a+1,b)
by explicitly identifying two extra points.

The manuscript is organized as follows: In Section
2
, we prove general results for completing the interlacing of arbitrary polynomials
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}
by identifying two additional points from a degree-two polynomial
d_{2}
. These results are then applied to various orthogonal families, specifically Jacobi polynomials (§
3.1
), Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials (§
3.2
), and Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials (§
3.3
), to establish new completed interlacing results with the help of two extra points.

Remark 2.1
.

In Theorem
2.1
, the points
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
play a central role in completing the interlacing between the zeros of
\mathcal{P}_{n}
and
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}
. The proof, in particular, that
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
cannot lie simultaneously in the same interval among
(-\infty,y_{1,n+1})
,
(y_{k,n+1},y_{k+1,n+1})
,
k=1,\dots,n
, or
(y_{n+1,n+1},\infty)
. In general, both points are needed to complete the interlacing described in Theorem
2.1
. However, for a fixed
n
, the proof also identifies configurations in which one of the two points already suffices. More precisely:

a)

If
E_{1}<y_{1,n+1}
and
y_{k,n+1}<E_{2}<y_{k+1,n+1}
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x)\prec(x-E_{2})\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)
.

b)

If
E_{2}>y_{n+1,n+1}
and
y_{k,n+1}<E_{1}<y_{k+1,n+1}
for some
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}
, then
(x-E_{1})\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)\prec\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x)
.

Thus, one extra point may be sufficient in these particular configurations, whereas the use of both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
provides a uniform formulation of the completed interlacing in Theorem
2.1
.

Theorem 2.2
.

\displaystyle A(x)\,\mathcal{P}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=B(x)\,\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x)+(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2})\,\mathcal{Q}_{n}(x).

Proof.

Note that

Evaluating (

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{n}(y_{n+1,n+1})=\frac{(y_{n+1,n+1}-E_{1})(y_{n+1,n+1}-E_{2})}{A(y_{n+1,n+1})}\,\mathcal{Q}_{n}(y_{n+1,n+1}).

(2.6)

Since

.

3.1
Jacobi polynomials

The monic Jacobi polynomials, denoted by
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
, form an orthogonal sequence on
(-1,1)
with respect to the measure
d\mu(x)=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}\,dx
, where
\alpha,\beta>-1

[
3
]
. They satisfy the three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle P_{n+1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=(x-c^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1})P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)-\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x),

(3.1)

with
P_{-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=0
and
P_{0}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)=1
, where the recurrence coefficients
c^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}
and
\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}
are given by

\displaystyle\lambda^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{4n(n+\alpha)(n+\beta)(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)^{2}(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)(2n+\alpha+\beta-1)}>0,

\displaystyle c^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{\beta^{2}-\alpha^{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}\in\mathbb{R}.

(3.2)

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
be a monic Jacobi polynomial of degree
n
with
\alpha>-1,\beta>-1
. Let
\{y_{k,n+1}\}_{k=1}^{n+1}
denote the zeros of the polynomial
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
. Assume that
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
and
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
have no common zero. Then,
(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2})P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
, where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
, given in (
3.7
) and (
3.8
). In particular, if both
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
lie outside the extreme zeros of
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
, namely
E_{1}<y_{1,n+1}
and
E_{2}>y_{n+1,n+1}
, then
P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)\prec P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
.

Proof.

Using (cf.
[
6
, eq. (8)]
), the monic Jacobi polynomial
P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
satisfies the following mixed three-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle\frac{n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}P^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

\displaystyle=-\left(x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}\right)P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n-1}(x)+\frac{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}P^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n}(x)

(3.3)

for
n\in\mathbb{N}
and
\alpha,\beta>-1
. Evaluating the recurrence (
3.1
) at the shifted parameters
(\alpha+1,\beta+1)
and substituting the expression for
\mathcal{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)
from (
3.3
), followed by algebraic simplification, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\,\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}}{n}\;{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}\right){P}_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)-T(x)\;{P}_{n}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x),

(3.4)

where

\displaystyle T(x)=\left(x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}\right)\left(x-c_{n+1}^{\alpha+1,\beta+1}\right)-\frac{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}{n}\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}.

(3.5)

Equivalently,
T(x)
admits the factored representation

\displaystyle T(x)=(x-E_{n,\alpha,\beta})(x-F_{n,\alpha,\beta}),

(3.6)

with roots

\displaystyle E_{1}:=E_{n,\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2(n+1)(\alpha-\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+4)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}+\sqrt{\Delta}\right],

(3.7)

\displaystyle E_{2}:=F_{n,\alpha,\beta}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{2(n+1)(\alpha-\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+4)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}-\sqrt{\Delta}\right],

(3.8)

and discriminant

\Delta=\left(\frac{2(n+1)(\alpha-\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+4)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}\right)^{2}\\
+\frac{4}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)^{2}}\left[\frac{(\alpha-\beta)^{2}(\alpha+\beta+2)}{2n+\alpha+\beta+4}+\frac{4(n+\alpha+1)(n+\beta+1)(n+\alpha+\beta+2)}{2n+\alpha+\beta+3}\right].

(3.9)

Now, (
3.4
) reads as

\displaystyle\frac{(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\,\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}}{n}\;{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=\left(x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}\right){P}_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)-(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2})\;{P}_{n}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x).

(3.10)

Let us define

\mathcal{G}_{n+1}(x):={P}_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)
,

\mathcal{Q}_{n}(x):={P}_{n}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)
,
and
\mathcal{P}_{n}(x):={P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
.
We also set

A(x):=\frac{(n+\alpha+\beta+1)\,\lambda_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}}{n}>0

and

B(x):=x-\frac{\alpha-\beta}{2n+\alpha+\beta+2}
. From (
3.5
), it is immediate that
B(E_{1})\neq 0
and
B(E_{2})\neq 0
.
Furthermore, for any
\alpha,\beta>-1
, the zeros of
{P}_{n}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)
and
{P}_{n+1}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}(x)
are strictly interlacing. Thus, the result follows directly from Theorem
2.1
.
∎

Remark 3.2
.

In
[
1
, Theorem 2 and Remark 6]
, the authors investigated the relative location of the zeros of

{P}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)
and
{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

by using the mixed three-term recurrence relation satisfied by the non-monic Jacobi polynomials

[
1

and evaluating it at two consecutive zeros of

{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)
(see
[
1
, eq. (15)]
).
As a result, the positions of four zeros of

{P}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)

and some of their possible configurations were discussed based on numerical observations
(see
[
1

).

In contrast, in Corollary
3.1
,

(

), and evaluate it at two consecutive zeros of

{P}^{(\alpha+1,\beta+1)}_{n+1}(x)

{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

,
leaving only two zeros undetermined in general. We have analysed all possible configurations for these two zeros
and shown that the positions of the points

and

determine their locations.
Consequently, this yields the precise interlacing of the zeros of

and
{P}^{(\alpha,\beta)}_{n}(x)

in certain cases, depending on the positions of
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
for suitable values of
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\alpha>-1
, and
\beta>-1
.
Overall, the present result extends and improves

[
1
, Theorem 2 and Remark 6]

by providing a clearer analytical framework for describing the interlacing pattern.

3.2
Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials

The monic Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials
[
15
, Section 9.7]
, denoted as
P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)
, are defined by

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)

\displaystyle=i^{n}(2\lambda)_{n}\left(\frac{e^{2i\phi}}{e^{2i\phi}-1}\right)^{n}\,_{2}F_{1}\left(-n,\lambda+ix;2\lambda;1-\frac{1}{e^{2i\phi}}\right)

(3.11)

These polynomials are orthogonal with respect to
d\mu(x)=|\Gamma(\lambda+ix)|^{2}e^{(2\phi-\pi)x}dx
on
(-\infty,\infty)
, where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda>0
, and
0<\phi<\pi
. Here,
\Gamma(z)
represents the Gamma function

\displaystyle\Gamma(z)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}t^{z-1}e^{-t}\,dt,

and the Gauss hypergeometric series
{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)
is defined as

{}_{2}F_{1}\left(a,b;c;z\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(a)_{k}(b)_{k}}{(c)_{k}}\frac{z^{k}}{k!}.

(3.12)

The Pochhammer symbol
(a)_{n}
is defined as follows

\displaystyle(a)_{0}=1~\text{for}~a\neq 0,~(a)_{n}

\displaystyle=a(a+1)(a+2)\cdots(a+n-1)~\text{for}~n\geq 1.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda>0
, and
\phi\in(0,\pi)
. Then the polynomial

\displaystyle T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)=(1-\cos 2\phi)x^{2}+(n+1)\sin(2\phi)x+(\lambda^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+n\lambda+\lambda)\cos 2\phi),

(3.13)

has only real zeros whenever

\displaystyle\phi\in\left(0,\frac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}\right)\right)\cup\left(\pi-\frac{1}{2}\arccos\left(\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}\right),\pi\right).

(3.14)

Proof.

To determine the conditions on
\phi
under which the polynomial

T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)
has only real zeros, it suffices to show that
its discriminant is positive. Denoting by

D:=\mathrm{Disc}_{x}\!\left(T_{n,\lambda,\phi}\right)

the discriminant of
T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)
, we have

\displaystyle D=(n+1)^{2}\sin^{2}(2\phi)-4(1-\cos(2\phi))(\lambda^{2}-(\lambda^{2}+n\lambda+\lambda)\cos 2\phi).

(3.15)

The zeros of
T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)
are

\displaystyle x=\frac{-(n+1)\sin 2\phi\pm\sqrt{D}}{2(1-\cos 2\phi)}.

To simplify (
3.15
), we denote
\cos(2\phi)=y,n+1=A
and
B=\lambda^{2}+n\lambda+\lambda=\lambda^{2}+A\lambda
. We can write (
3.15
) as

\displaystyle D

\displaystyle=A^{2}(1-y^{2})-4(1-y)(\lambda^{2}-By)

\displaystyle=-(A+2\lambda)^{2}y^{2}+4\lambda(A+2\lambda)y+A^{2}-4\lambda^{2}

\displaystyle=-(A+2\lambda)[\underbrace{(A+2\lambda)y^{2}-4\lambda y-(A-2\lambda)}_{f(y)}].

To ensure that
D>0
, it suffices to determine conditions under which the
quadratic polynomial
f(y)
appearing in the bracketed term
[\cdot]
in
the above expression is negative. The polynomial
f(y)
vanishes at

\displaystyle y

\displaystyle=\frac{4\lambda\pm\sqrt{(4\lambda)^{2}+4(A^{2}-4\lambda^{2})}}{2(A+2\lambda)}=\frac{2\lambda\pm A}{A+2\lambda}.

Thus, the zeros of
f(y)
are
y=1
and

y=1-\frac{2A}{A+2\lambda}<1
.
Since
A+2\lambda>0
, the graph of
f(y)
opens upward, and hence

f(y)<0
for
y\in\left(\frac{2\lambda-A}{A+2\lambda},\,1\right).

Consequently,
D>0
provided that

\displaystyle\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}<\cos 2\phi<1.

Since
\arccos
is decreasing on
(-1,1)
, it follows that

\displaystyle 0<\arccos(\cos 2\phi)<\arccos\!\left(\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}\right).

Using the identity

\displaystyle\arccos(\cos 2\phi)=\begin{cases}2\phi,&\text{if }\phi\in(0,\frac{\pi}{2})\\
2\pi-2\phi,&\text{if }\phi\in(\frac{\pi}{2},\pi)\end{cases},

we obtain (
3.14
). Also, the polynomial
T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)
can be factorised as

\displaystyle T_{n,\lambda,\phi}(x)=(1-\cos 2\phi)(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2}),

(3.16)

where

\displaystyle E_{1}:=E_{n,\lambda,\phi}=\frac{-(n+1)\sin 2\phi+\sqrt{(n+1+2\lambda)(1-\cos 2\phi)\left(\cos 2\phi-\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}\right)}}{2(1-\cos 2\phi)},

(3.17)

and

\displaystyle E_{2}:=F_{n,\lambda,\phi}=\frac{-(n+1)\sin 2\phi-\sqrt{(n+1+2\lambda)(1-\cos 2\phi)\left(\cos 2\phi-\frac{2\lambda-n-1}{2\lambda+n+1}\right)}}{2(1-\cos 2\phi)}.

(3.18)

This completes the proof.
∎

It is well known that, for any
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
\lambda>0
, and

\phi\in(0,\pi)
,
P_{n+1}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)\prec P_{n}^{(\lambda)}(x;\phi)
. In contrast, such an

and

Nevertheless, it was shown in

that these zeros interlace in the special case

and

. In the following result, we demonstrate

\phi
to

and
\lambda>0
. A key role in

and

, defined in
(
3.17
) and
(
3.18
), respectively.

.

, and

3.3
Pseudo Jacobi polynomials

For real parameters
a
and
b
, the monic Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials, denoted by
P_{n}(x;a,b)
, are expressed in terms of the hypergeometric function as
[
15
]
:

P_{n}(x;a,b)=\frac{2^{n}(a+ib+1)_{n}}{i^{n}(2a+n+1)_{n}}\,{}_{2}F_{1}\left(\left.\begin{matrix}-n,2a+n+1\\
a+ib+1\end{matrix}\,\right|\,\frac{1-ix}{2}\right),

(3.21)

where
n
is a non-negative integer. These polynomials satisfy an orthogonality relation on the real line with respect to the measure
d\mu(x)=(1+x^{2})^{a}e^{2b\arctan x}dx
, provided that
b\in\mathbb{R}
and
a<-n
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
n\in\mathbb{N},a<-n-\frac{3}{2}
and
b\in\mathbb{R}
. The quadratic polynomial defined by

R_{n,a,b}(x)=x^{2}-\frac{b(n+1)}{(a+n+1)(a+n+2)}x+\frac{(a+n+1)(a+n+2)(2a+n+2)+b^{2}(n+1)}{(a+n+1)(a+n+2)(2a+2n+3)}

(3.22)

has only real zeros whenever

a<\frac{-3n-5-\sqrt{n^{2}+2n+5}}{4}~\text{with}~a\neq-n-2,

(3.23)

and

|b|\geq\sqrt{\frac{4(a+n+1)^{2}(a+n+2)^{2}(2a+n+2)}{(n+1)\left|4(a+n+1)^{2}-(2n-2)(a+n+1)-(n+1)\right|}}.

(3.24)

Proof.

To simplify the algebraic manipulation, we denote
u=a+n+1
. Given the range
a<-n-1.5
, the range for
u
becomes
u<-0.5
. Now, (
3.22
) reads as

\displaystyle P(x)=x^{2}-\frac{b(n+1)}{u(u+1)}x+\frac{u(u+1)(2u-n)+b^{2}(n+1)}{u(u+1)(2u+1)}.

The zeros of the quadratic polynomial are real if and only if the discriminant
\Delta:=Disc_{x}(P_{n,a,b})\geq 0
.

\displaystyle\Delta

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{b(n+1)}{u(u+1)}\right)^{2}-4\left(\frac{u(u+1)(2u-n)+b^{2}(n+1)}{u(u+1)(2u+1)}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{b^{2}(n+1)\overbrace{\left[-4u^{2}+(2n-2)u+(n+1)\right]}^{K(u)}-4u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u-n)}{u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u+1)}.

Thus, we can write

\Delta=\frac{b^{2}(n+1)K(u)-4u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u-n)}{u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u+1)}.

(3.25)

Since the denominator term
u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u+1)
is negative. We need the numerator must be negative, in order to make
\Delta\geq 0
. i.e.,

b^{2}(n+1)K(u)-4u^{2}(u+1)^{2}(2u-n)\leq 0.

(3.26)

To achieve (
3.26
), we need
K(u)=-4u^{2}+(2n-2)u+(n+1)<0
. To do that, we find the roots of
K(u)=0
,

u=\frac{n-1\mp\sqrt{n^{2}+2n+5}}{4}.

The only possible value of
u
is

u<\frac{n-1-\sqrt{n^{2}+2n+5}}{4}.

Hence (
3.26
) holds, when
a
and
b
satisfies the condition (
3.23
) and (
3.24
) respectively.
∎

The quadratic polynomial
R_{n,a,b}(x)
can be written in factorized form as

R_{n,a,b}(x)=(x-E_{n,a,b})(x-F_{n,a,b}),

(3.27)

where
E_{1}:=E_{n,a,b}
and
E_{2}:=F_{n,a,b}
are real and given by

E_{1}=\frac{b(n+1)+\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{N}}{2a+2n+3}}}{2(a+n+1)(a+n+2)},

(3.28)

and

E_{2}=\frac{b(n+1)-\sqrt{\frac{\mathcal{N}}{2a+2n+3}}}{2(a+n+1)(a+n+2)},

(3.29)

where
\mathcal{N}
is given by

\mathcal{N}=b^{2}(n+1)\Big[-4(a+n+1)^{2}+(2n-2)(a+n+1)+(n+1)\Big]\\
-4(a+n+1)^{2}(a+n+2)^{2}(2a+n+2).

The monic Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials satisfy the following mixed recurrence relation, which is very useful for establishing interlacing properties between different sequences of these polynomials

\displaystyle\frac{2(2a+n+1)_{2}\left((a+n+1)^{2}+b^{2}\right)}{(n+a+1)(2a+2n+1)_{3}}P_{n}(x;a;b)

\displaystyle=-\left(x-\frac{b}{n+a+1}\right)P_{n+1}(x;a+1;b)+(x-E_{1})(x-E_{2})P_{n}(x;a+1;b),

(3.30)

where
E_{1}
and
E_{2}
are given by (
3.28
) and (
3.29
). This relation can be verified by comparing the coefficients of
x^{n}
.

4
Conclusion

This study establishes a systematic framework for completing the interlacing of zeros in polynomial sequences where interlacing fails by exactly two points. We have identified a specific degree-two polynomial whose zeros provide the necessary points to restore a complete interlacing structure. Beyond identifying these points, we have determined their precise positioning relative to the zeros of the higher-degree polynomial, thereby offering a full characterization of the interlacing behavior.

We have shown the utility of this framework by applying it to several orthogonal families. This allowed us to refine the existing interlacing results for Jacobi polynomials, address an open question regarding the interlacing of Meixner-Pollaczek polynomials of consecutive degree and parameter shifted by one, and established new interlacing properties for Pseudo-Jacobi polynomials of consecutive degree and parameter shifted by one. While this work focuses on identifying the degree-two polynomial using general mixed recurrence relation given in
1.4
, it is worth noting that extending this approach to degree-three polynomials (three extra points) or more is extremely tedious and computationally challenging for general polynomial sequences. Nevertheless, the unified approach presented here represents a clear advancement in the field, offering a practical tool for researchers to investigate similar interlacing gaps in both orthogonal and non-orthogonal polynomial families.
