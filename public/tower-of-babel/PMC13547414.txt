Physics-informed learning–based fault-tolerant control for robust wireless power transfer in electric vehicles
Authors: Muhammad Haris Saleem, Arslan Ahmed Amin, Turki Alsuwian
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Wireless Power Transfer-based electric vehicles (EVs) emerge as a promising technology that can drastically reduce carbon emissions worldwide. Conventional ICE-based transportation has led to a significant increase in greenhouse gas emissions and exacerbated climate change. On the contrary, WPT-based EVs can offer extended range, contactless charging, enhanced safety, and reduced vehicle costs. However, the complex design of WPT is vulnerable to power converter failure and coil misalignment. These faults can significantly deteriorate the performance of the WPT-based EVs. A power converter failure can degrade WPT performance, and this paper introduces redundant switches to ensure smooth vehicle operation. This paper presents a Physics-informed Neural Network (PINN) that incorporates physical laws to observe signals from power converters and control the Active Fault-Tolerant Controller (AFTC). The AFTCS consists of the FDI block, which is responsible for detecting and isolating faults in real time. The PINN-based observer system monitors the signal in real time. The results clearly illustrate that the proposed framework provides accurate current tracking and ensures the residual signal remains in the threshold even in parameter variations and achieves a reduced settling time of 0.22s. Furthermore, the steady-state error is reduced to 0.3 A, outperforming the other control methods and ensuring the stability of the system in the event of faults. Thus, a PINN-based FTC is introduced to ensure the resilience and reliable operation of WPT-based EVs in case of power converter failure and coil misalignment.

Introduction

ICE-based transportation is the primary source of excessive gas worldwide. Results in drastic climate change and unpredictable weather patterns, pushing the world toward renewable energy sources. Hybrid Electric Vehicles (HEVs) offer an opportunity to reduce reliance on ICE engines, thereby decreasing carbon footprints1. However, the global imperative to reduce carbon emissions has driven the development of new technologies and the adoption of full EV-based transportation. The significant issues with EVs are range anxiety, safety concerns, and battery costs. This problem has paved the way for a new technology to emerge: Wireless Power Transfer (WPT) for charging electric vehicles2. WPT-based technology offers benefits such as extended range, enhanced safety, and reduced costs. However, the complex architecture of the WPT has specific vulnerabilities that can significantly undermine the performance of WPT-based transportation. These include coil misalignment and power converter failures, which affect the power output for charging the EV3.

Various studies have proposed control frameworks to improve the performance of WPT-based systems in the event of coil misalignment or converter faults. The paper4 discusses a partial power control approach to enhance WPT performance in the presence of coil misalignment. By optimizing power losses, EVs can be safely charged. However, this paper only considers coil misalignment and does not address other faults, leaving the implementation gap unresolved.

Similarly, the conventional observer-based model, like the Extended Kalman Filter and Levenberg-Marquardt method, requires an accurate mathematical model5,6. However, in the case of WPT architecture, the performance of these observers lags in a practical scenario as the system is operating at a higher frequency. Furthermore, the computational overhead and sensitivity to external parameters like coil misalignment, EMI interference, and sensor noise can trigger false FTC systems, which can degrade the performance of the overall system7,8.

With the emergence of AI, different data-driven techniques are used for the estimation and diagnosis of faults in critical systems. The Artificial Neural Network (ANN) and Convolutional Neural Network (CNN) are used in fault diagnosis to enhance the system performance in the event of a fault9. These techniques study the pattern recognition to identify the faults, and do not require rigorous mathematical modelling. However, these methods don’t integrate the physical constraints that can result in transient and unrealistic responses from the model. Furthermore, these methods require a large dataset to train the model to detect the fault, which is a constraint in a practical scenario10.

Similarly, the paper11 introduced a dual-phase shift (DPS) technique, which is efficient in controlling the current stress introduced by the power converters in the WPT-architecture. This ensures the efficient transfer of power from the transmitter to the receiver end. However, results are limited to specific conditions and don’t include the other factors. Furthermore, the paper12 introduced a 500 W WPT-based prototype that uses a series-series resonant framework to significantly increase the flux coefficient and power output. However, this paper provides a complex control system for the practical scenario13.

The paper14 uses the optimal control method to enhance the voltage regulation at the receiving side of the WPT-based system. An LQR controller is deployed to increase the voltage regulation and fast response in case of coil misalignment. However, the controller required an exact model, and narrow performance criteria were considered15. Similarly, the paper16 introduced an MFC-based control scheme for dynamic WPT to control the pulsating output voltage at the receiving side. It uses an adaptive extended state observer and an online parameter estimation method to deal with the faults.

Various papers in the literature have focused on fault-tolerant control to enhance the robustness of WPT-vehicles in the presence of actuator, sensor, or communication faults17. The paper18 introduces a concurrent data-driven control system to handle the system’s nonlinearity. Furthermore, the paper19,20 used an SFPC-based framework to address output-voltage fluctuations in WPT-based EVs. This technique improves the performance of the EV, but it considers only limited faults21.

However, the PINN-based observer model integrates KCL and KVL constraints into the neural network to provide a robust model output. The model estimates the actual WPT framework and provides the result bounded by electromagnetic principles. Therefore, this paper distinguishes itself from the existing literature by offering a data-driven FTC that enhances the reliability and resilience of the WPT system in the event of a fault. A Physics-informed Neural Network (PINN) is used to estimate faults across the power converter, detect them in real time, and compensate for the overall system. Fault analysis in WPT environment

The fault in the WPT environment can significantly deteriorate the performance and efficiency of EV. The faults are the abnormal conditions that can degrade the performance parameter of WPT, like resonance, safety, and range cruise of the EV. Furthermore, the complex architecture of WPT has unconventional faults that require sophisticated solutions to control the efficient power transfer between the primary and secondary coils. These faults include resonance issues, inverter switches breakdown, rectifier faults, and compensation degradation, which can undermine the potential of wireless power transfer25,26. The inverter is the most fault-prone component in WPT architecture due to the high switching stress and the required precise PWM signals for the IGBTs. In this research paper, we focused on open-circuit faults, short-circuit faults, sensor noise, and gate driver faults. The OCF and SCF can deteriorate the waveform and performance of the inverter, which results in low magnetic coupling of power from the primary to the secondary coil.

In the open circuit faults, the voltage across the switches exceeds the operational level and can result in undermining the performance of the system. Additionally, the short circuit fault results in high current, which can cause high losses in the primary and secondary side inductors. The misalignment of the coil can degrade the magnetic coupling, which results in a change of impedance for the inverter.18\documentclass[12pt]{minimal} \usepackage{amsmath} \usepackage{wasysym} \usepackage{amsfonts} \usepackage{amssymb} \usepackage{amsbsy} \usepackage{mathrsfs} \usepackage{upgreek} \setlength{\oddsidemargin}{-69pt} \begin{document}$$\:{Z}_{Ref}=\:\frac{{\omega\:}^{2}{M}^{2}}{{R}_{s,eq}}$$\end{document}

During the high magnetic coupling with a high value of M, it can result in frequency bifurcation, which changes the Zero Voltage Switching (ZVS) for the inverter. This ZVS can cause high dv/dt, which increases thermal stress on the IGBT switches. PINN architecture for WPT-based EV

The complex design of WPT-based EV requires a robust and resilient framework to ensure the reliable performance of the vehicle. The conventional neural network required accurate modelling and extensive data to learn the pattern, which is not feasible for the real-time control of EVs. Thus, the Physics-Informed Neural Network provides an advanced framework, which deploys a Multi-layer Perceptron (MLP), that integrates a physics constraint surrogate model of the inverter to provide an accurate prediction of the actual model27.

The PINN internal structure basically consists of six layers with 128 neurons. This six-layer-based NN is effective in capturing the dynamic response of the inverter under high-frequency stress. The NN integrated with the circuit physics is capable of measuring the transient response of the inverter in real-time. The higher harmonics introduced during faults required a high-layer network to observe the response of the inverter with minimal divergence. The activation function for this environment is the hyperbolic tangent (tanh) function, which provides high-order differentiability and zero-centred output, which allows the algorithm to converge faster compared to the other ReLU functions28. The output layer provides ÎINV, which is used to mimic the actual inverter in the WPT architecture.

The distinguishing feature of PINN is a composite loss function, which basically includes the physics-based equation into the loss function to ensure that it doesn’t violate the physics laws. Conclusion

In conclusion, this paper presents a physics-informed learning-based FTC framework for the wireless charging of EVs that enhances the robustness, reliability, and operation resilience of the overall system. As high-frequency power converters experience high switching stress, parameter uncertainties, and inverter faults, a PINN-based observer is developed to continuously estimate the dynamic states of the WPT system under the operating conditions. By embedding the KVL constraint into the learning process, it enhances the physical consistency, fault estimation capability, and system adaptability of the PINN-based WPT system. In the event of a fault occurrence, the proposed observer rapidly diagnoses the faults and performs isolation and activates the FTC framework to ensure the stable power transfer operation in the event of faults. The proposed framework enhances the fault resilience, efficiency, and system reliability under the abnormal fault conditions and serves as a vital safety-critical solution for the next-generation EV charging infrastructure. The results are validated in simulation, in which PINN-based FTC measures the sensor error estimation error in 0.22 s and provides a fast, smooth exponential convergence response, which is far better than the rest of the methods. Therefore, the PINN-based FTC improves the resilience of the WPT architecture, which is crucial for the reliability of the vehicle’s performance in real-time. The future research directions include validation of this technique using hardware-in-the-loop (HIL), ensuring the security of cyber-physical space, a hybrid PINN-transformer model, and dynamic coupling and misalignment robustness.
