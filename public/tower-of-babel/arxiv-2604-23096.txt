Introduction

Let
j
be the elliptic modular function defined on the complex upper half-plane

\mathbb{H}=\{\tau\in\mathbb{C}~|~\mathrm{Im}(\tau)>0\}

with Fourier expansion

j(\tau)=\frac{1}{q}+744+196884q+21493760q^{2}+\cdots\quad(q=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}\tau},~\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

It is a weakly holomorphic (i.e., holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
) modular function
for the modular group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
(see
[
14
,
\lx@sectionsign
3.3]
).
Weber
[
19
]
proved that if
\Phi_{p}(x,\,y)
is the modular polynomial for a prime
p
,
then it satisfies the so-called
Kronecker congruence relation

\Phi_{p}(x,\,y)\equiv(x^{p}-y)(x-y^{p})\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathbb{Z}[x,\,y]).

(1)

See also
[
8
, Theorem 11.18]
.

On the other hand, let
K
be an imaginary quadratic field,
H_{K}
its Hilbert class field, and

\mathfrak{a}
a nonzero ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
In view of the theory of complex multiplication, Hasse
[
9
]
further
showed that for all but finitely many prime ideals
\mathfrak{p}
of
\mathcal{O}_{K}
such that

\mathfrak{p}\neq\overline{\mathfrak{p}}
, the congruence

j(\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})\equiv j(\mathfrak{a})^{p}\ (\textup{mod}\ \mathfrak{P})\quad\textrm{with}~p=\mathrm{N}_{K/\mathbb{Q}}(\mathfrak{p})

(2)

holds for any prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{H_{K}}

lying above
\mathfrak{p}
.
Here,
j(\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\mathfrak{a})
and
j(\mathfrak{a})
denote
the invariants of the elliptic curves

\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{p}^{-1}\mathfrak{a}
and
\mathbb{C}/\mathfrak{a}
, respectively.

Later, Chen-Yui
[
4
]
, Cais-Conrad
[
2
]
, Cho
[
5
,
6
]
, and others
used Hasse’s
q
-expansion principle to show that certain Hauptmoduln of genus zero other than
j
satisfy
the Kronecker congruence relation (
1
) for special primes
p
.

For a meromorphic modular function
f
of level
N
, define a function
f_{p}
on
\mathbb{H}
by

f_{p}(\tau)=f\left(\frac{\tau}{p}\right)\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

For a positive integer
n
, let
\zeta_{n}=e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}/n}
be a primitive
n
th root of unity.
Recently, Jung et al.
[
11
]
attempted to generalize
the congruence (
1
) to functions on modular curves
of higher level and genus, and proved the following result.

Theorem A

(
[
11
, Theorem 7.1]
)
.

Let
f
be a weakly holomorphic modular function for
\Gamma_{1}(N)

with rational Fourier coefficients which is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.
If
p
is a prime such that
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
, then

\prod_{\delta\in R_{N,\,p}}\bigg\{(f_{p}^{p}-f)(f_{p}-f^{p})\bigg\}\circ\delta\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{N,\,p}).

Here,
R_{N,\,p}
is a set of representatives for the
right cosets of
\Gamma_{1}(N)\cap\Gamma^{0}(p)
in
\Gamma_{1}(N)
,
and

\mathcal{O}_{N,\,p}

is the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}[j]
in the
field of meromorphic modular functions for
\Gamma_{1}(N)
with Fourier coefficients
in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Np})
.

However, their argument relies on the class field theoretic control of singular values,
the resulting congruence is obtained only after taking a product over suitable Galois conjugates, rather than in a direct form for the single function

F=\frac{1}{p}(f_{p}^{p}-f)(f_{p}-f^{p}).

Accordingly, they conjectured that
F
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

In this paper, we show that the above conjecture holds.
Let
\mathcal{F}_{N}
be the field of meromorhic modular functions of level
N

whose Fourier coefficients lie in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
.
As is well known,
\mathcal{F}_{N}
is a Galois extension of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
whose
Galois group is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})/\langle-I_{2}\rangle

(see
\lx@sectionsign
2
).

Theorem B

(Theorem
8.1
)
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N}
.
Suppose that
f
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
. Then,
for every prime
p
such that
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
, the function

F=\frac{1}{p}\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)

is also integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
. Consequently,
we obtain a Kronecker-type congruence

\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{Np}}).

Here,
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{Np}}
denotes
the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}[j]
in the field
\mathcal{F}_{Np}
.
Our approach is based on a direct analysis of Fourier expansions and integrality
over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
without relying on class field theory, and is closely related to valuation-theoretic ideas in the sense of Tate
[
17
]
and Serre
[
15
]

as well as Katz’s
q
-expansion principle
[
12
]
.

2
Fields of meromorphic modular functions

We briefly review Galois extensions of modular function fields.

For
\mathbf{v}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{1}&v_{2}\end{bmatrix}\in M_{1,\,2}(\mathbb{Q})\setminus M_{1,\,2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
the Fricke function
f_{\mathbf{v}}
on
\mathbb{H}
is defined by

f_{\mathbf{v}}(\tau)=-2^{7}3^{5}\frac{g_{2}(\tau)g_{3}(\tau)}{\Delta(\tau)}\wp(v_{1}\tau+v_{2};\,[\tau,\,1])\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H})

where
\wp(z;\,[\tau,\,1])
(
z\in\mathbb{C})
is the Weierstrass
\wp
-function relative to the lattice
[\tau,\,1]

and

g_{2}(\tau)=g_{2}([\tau,\,1]),\quad g_{3}(\tau)=g_{3}([\tau,\,1]),\quad\Delta(\tau)=g_{2}(\tau)^{3}-27g_{3}(\tau)^{2}.

For a positive integer
M
, let
\mathcal{F}_{M}
be the field given by

\mathcal{F}_{M}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}\mathbb{Q}(j)&\textrm{if}~M=1,\\
\mathbb{Q}\left(j,\,f_{\mathbf{v}}~|~\mathbf{v}\in\frac{1}{M}M_{1,\,2}(\mathbb{Z})\setminus M_{1,\,2}(\mathbb{Z})\right)&\textrm{if}~M\geq 2.\end{array}\right.

It is well known that the field
\mathcal{F}_{M}
is a Galois extension of
\mathcal{F}_{1}
whose
Galois group is isomorphic to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})/\langle-I_{2}\rangle

(see
[
16
, Theorem 6.6 (1), (2)]
). Furthermore,
\mathcal{F}_{M}
coincides
with the field of meromorphic modular funcitons for the principal
congruence subgroup

\Gamma(M)=\{\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})~|~\alpha\equiv I_{2}\ (\textup{mod}\ NM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))\}

whose Fourier coefficients
belong to the
M
th cyclotomic field
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
(see
[
16
, Proposition 6.9 (1)]
).

For an integer
d
relatively prime to
M
, let

\sigma_{M,\,d}
denote the automorphism of
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
given by
\zeta_{M}^{\sigma_{M,\,d}}=\zeta_{M}^{d}
.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
and write its Fourier expansion with respect to
q^{1/M}
as

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n/M}~\textrm{with}~a_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

Let
\gamma\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})/\langle-I_{2}\rangle

(
\simeq\mathrm{Gal}(\mathcal{F}_{M}/\mathcal{F}_{1})
)
.

(i)

If
\gamma
is represented by
\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&d\end{bmatrix}
for some
integer

relatively prime to
M
, then

g^{\gamma}(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}^{\sigma_{M,\,d}}q^{n/M}.

(ii)

If
\gamma\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z})/\langle-I_{2}\rangle
, then

g^{\gamma}=g\circ\alpha\quad\textrm{for any}~\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})~\textrm{lifting $\gamma$}.

Proof..

See
[
16
, Theorem 6.6 (3) and Proposition 6.21 (3)]
.
∎

Lemma 2.2
.

Let

be a positive integer and let

, the function
f_{p}
lies in

3
Lemmas on integrality over
\mathbb{Z}[j]

In this section, we recall
well-known facts about integrality over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
g
be a meromorphic modular function for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
If
g
is weakly holomorphic, then it is a polynomial in
j
with coefficients
in the
\mathbb{Z}
-module generated by the Fourier
coefficients of
g
.

Proof..

See
[
14
, Theorem 2 in Chapter 5]
.
∎

Note that the group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{R})=\{\alpha\in M_{2}(\mathbb{R})~|~\det(\alpha)>0\}
acts on
\mathbb{H}

by fractional linear transformations. See
[
16
,
\lx@sectionsign
1.2]
.

Lemma 3.2
.

If
\alpha\in M_{2}^{+}(\mathbb{Z})
, then
j\circ\alpha
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

Proof..

See
[
14
, Corollary to Theorem 3 in Chapter 5]
.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
for a positive integer
M
.
If
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
, then
g
is weakly holomorphic.

Proof..

Since
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
, it satisfies a monic polynomial equation

g^{m}+A_{m-1}(j)g^{m-1}+\cdots+A_{1}(j)g+A_{0}(j)=0

for some positive integer
m
and
A_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
(
i=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1
).
Dividing both sides by
g^{m}
, we get

1+A_{m-1}(j)\frac{1}{g}+\cdots+A_{1}(j)\frac{1}{g^{m-1}}+A_{0}(j)\frac{1}{g^{m}}=0.

(3)

Suppose on the contrary that
g
is not weakly holomorphic. Then

g
must have a pole at some point
\tau_{0}\in\mathbb{H}
.
Since
j
is holomorphic on
\mathbb{H}
,
each
A_{i}(j(\tau_{0}))
is finite (
i=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1
).
Taking the limit as
\tau\rightarrow\tau_{0}
in (
3
), we obtain

1=0
, a contradiction.
Therefore we conclude that
g
is weakly holomorphic.
∎

Remark 3.4
.

Geometrically,
the integrality of
g
over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
implies that
g
defines a regular function on the interior of
the moduli stack
\mathcal{M}_{1,\,1,\,\mathbb{Z}}
,
and hence has no poles on
\mathbb{H}
.
For further details on the arithmetic of
\mathcal{M}_{1,\,1,\,\mathbb{Z}}
,
see
[
13
]
or
[
7
]
.

4
Boundedness of Fourier coefficients

For completeness, we record a boundedness property for the denominators
of Fourier coefficients of a weakly holomorphic modular function.
This property is well known and is often used implicitly in the literature
(see, for example,
[
1
]
).

Let
M
be a positive integer, and let
k
be an integer.
We denote by
S_{k}(\Gamma(M))
the vector space of cusp forms of level
M

over
\mathbb{C}
.

Proposition 4.1
.

If
S_{k}(\Gamma(M))
is nontrivial, then it has a basis
consisting of cusp forms with Fourier coefficients in
\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof..

See
[
16
, Theorem 3.52]
.
∎

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
. If
g
is weakly holomorphic, then there is
a nonzero algebraic integer
c
in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
for which
all Fourier coefficients of
cg
are algebraic integers.

Proof..

Let
\widetilde{\Delta}
be a function on
\mathbb{H}
given by

\widetilde{\Delta}(\tau)=\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{12}}\,\Delta(\tau)=q\prod_{n=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{n})^{24}\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}),

which is a cusp form for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
of weight
12

with integer Fourier coefficients (see
[
16
, Theorem 2.9]
).
Since
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
is weakly holomorphic,

\widetilde{\Delta}^{k}g
belongs to
S_{12k}(\Gamma(M))
for
a suitably large positive integer
k
.
By Proposition
4.1
, there
is a basis
\{g_{1},\,g_{2},\,\ldots,\,g_{m}\}
for
S_{12k}(\Gamma(M))
over

\mathbb{C}
in which every
g_{i}
has integer
Fourier coefficients (
i=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,m
).
Then we have

\widetilde{\Delta}^{k}g=c_{1}g_{1}+c_{2}g_{2}+\cdots+c_{m}g_{m}\quad\textrm{for some unique}~c_{1},\,c_{2},\,\ldots,\,c_{m}\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})

because Fourier coefficients of
g
lie in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
.
It follows that

g=c_{1}(g_{1}\widetilde{\Delta}^{-k})+c_{2}(g_{2}\widetilde{\Delta}^{-k})+\cdots+c_{m}(g_{m}\widetilde{\Delta}^{-k}).

(4)

Here, we note that every
\widetilde{\Delta}^{-k}g_{i}
has integer Fourier coefficients
(
i=1,\,2,\,\ldots,\,m
). Thus (
4
) implies
that there is an algebraic integer
c
in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
so that

cg
has algebraic integer Fourier coefficients.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

(i)

The boundedness of the denominators of
the Fourier coefficients of the cusp form
\widetilde{\Delta}^{k}g
in Lemma
4.2

can be also established by applying Katz’s
q
-expansion principle
[
12
, Theorem 1.6.1]
.
Since
S_{12k}(\Gamma(M))
admits a canonical integral structure over
the base ring
\mathbb{Z}[1/M,\zeta_{M}]
,
the Fourier coefficients of
\widetilde{\Delta}^{k}g
necessarily have bounded denominators,
which in turn implies the same for
g
.

(ii)

Recently, Calegari, Dimitrov and Tang
[
3
]
settled the
unbounded denominators conjecture
, which asserts that any modular form with bounded denominators is necessarily a modular form for a congruence subgroup.

5
Exponential valuations on weakly holomorphic modular functions

Following the
q
-expansion valuation framework of Tate
[
17
]
and Serre
[
15
]
,
we introduce exponential valuations on weakly holomorphic modular functions.

Let
M
be a positive integer and let
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
.
For each nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
,
let
v_{\mathfrak{P}}:L\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the
usual exponential valuation on
L
with respect to
\mathfrak{P}
. That is,

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(0):=\infty
, and for
x\in L\setminus\{0\}
,
v_{\mathfrak{P}}(x)
is defined by
the relation

x(\mathcal{O}_{L})_{\mathfrak{P}}=\pi^{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(x)}(\mathcal{O}_{L})_{\mathfrak{P}}

where
\pi
is a uniformizer of the localization
(\mathcal{O}_{L})_{\mathfrak{P}}
.
Let
\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}

denote the integral closure of
\mathbb{Z}[j]
in the field
\mathcal{F}_{M}
.

Definition 5.1
.

We define a map

w_{\mathfrak{P}}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}

by

w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n})~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

where
g\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}
has the Fourier expansion

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n/M}~\textrm{with}~a_{n}\in\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

Remark 5.2
.

By Lemmas
3.3
and
4.2
,
w_{\mathfrak{P}}
is well defined.
We have
w_{\mathfrak{P}}(0)=\infty
.

Lemma 5.3
.

The function
w_{\mathfrak{P}}
satisfies the following properties
:

(i)

w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g+h)\geq\min\{w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g),\,w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)\}

for all
g,\,h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}
.

(ii)

w_{\mathfrak{P}}(gh)=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)

for all
g,\,h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}
.

Proof..

Let
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
, and
let
g,\,h\in\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{M}}
with Fourier expansions

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n/M},~h(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}b_{n}q^{n/M}\quad(a_{n},\,b_{n}\in L,~\tau\in\mathbb{H}),

respectively.

(i)

We see that

\displaystyle w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g+h)

\displaystyle=w_{\mathfrak{P}}\left(\sum_{n\gg-\infty}(a_{n}+b_{n})q^{n/M}\right)

\displaystyle=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n}+b_{n})~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n}),\,v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})\}~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

since
v_{\mathfrak{P}}
is an exponential valuation on
L

\displaystyle=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n}),\,v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle=\min\{w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g),\,w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)\}.

(ii)

The assertion is trivial if
g=0
or
h=0
.

Assume that
g,\,h\neq 0
, so that
w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g),\,w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)<\infty
.
Letting

(gh)(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/M}~\textrm{with}~c_{n}\in L\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}),

we deduce
from the fact
v_{\mathfrak{P}}
is an exponential valuation on
L

that

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{P}}(c_{t})

\displaystyle=v_{\mathfrak{P}}\left(\sum_{\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{l}(m,\,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}\\
\textrm{such that}~m+n=t\end{array}}a_{m}b_{n}\right)\quad(t\in\mathbb{Z})

(5)

\displaystyle\geq\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{m}b_{n})~|~m+n=t\}

\displaystyle=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{m})+v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})~|~m+n=t\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{m})~|~m\in\mathbb{Z}\}+\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}

\displaystyle=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h).

On the other hand, let

r=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}~|~v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n})=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)\}\quad\textrm{and}\quad s=\min\{n\in\mathbb{Z}~|~v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)\}.

Then we observe that

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n})>w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)~\textrm{for all}~n<r,

(6)

\displaystyle v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})>w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h)~\textrm{for all}~n<s.

(7)

Express
c_{r+s}
as

c_{r+s}=a_{r}b_{s}+\sum_{\footnotesize\begin{array}[]{l}(m,\,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}~\textrm{such that}\\
(m,\,n)\neq(r,\,s)~\textrm{and}~m+n=r+s\end{array}}a_{m}b_{n}.

(8)

We see that

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{r}b_{s})=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{r})+v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{s})=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h).

For
(m,\,n)\in\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{Z}
such that
(m,\,n)\neq(r,\,s)

and
m+n=r+s
, we have

m<r\quad\textrm{or}\quad n<s,

and hence we get by (
6
),
(
7
) and Definition
5.1
that

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{m}b_{n})=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{m})+v_{\mathfrak{P}}(b_{n})>w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h).

Thus we achieve from (
8
) that

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(c_{r+s})=v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{r}b_{s})=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h).

(9)

Therefore we conclude by (
5
) and (
9
) that

w_{\mathfrak{P}}(gh)=\min\{v_{\mathfrak{P}}(c_{n})~|~n\in\mathbb{Z}\}=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)+w_{\mathfrak{P}}(h).

∎

Remark 5.4
.

Lemma
5.3
(ii) can be viewed as a formal extension of the Gauss Lemma to Laurent series,
following the framework established by
Tate
[
17
]
in the context of rigid geometry. This property is also consistent with
the valuation defined by Serre
[
15
]
for
p
-adic modular forms.

6
A criterion on integrality over
\mathbb{Z}[j]

In this section, we establish a criterion for a meromorphic modular function
to be integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
by utilizing the exponential valuations introduced in the preceding section.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
M
be a positive integer and let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
.
If
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
, then
its Fourier coefficients are all algebraic integers in
\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
.

Proof..

Let
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
, and write the Fourier expansion of
g
as

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n/M}~\textrm{with}~a_{n}\in L\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

Suppose on the contrary that
a_{n}
is not an algebraic integer for some
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
Then there exists a nonzero prime ideal
\mathfrak{P}
of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
such that

v_{\mathfrak{P}}(a_{n})<0
, which implies

w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)<0.

(10)

Since
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
, it satisfies a monic polynomial equation

g^{m}+A_{m-1}(j)g^{m-1}+\cdots+A_{1}(j)g+A_{0}(j)=0

(11)

for some positive integer
m
and
A_{i}(x)\in\mathbb{Z}[x]
(
i=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1
).
By applying the properties of the valuation
w_{\mathfrak{P}}
established in Lemma
5.3
, we have

\displaystyle m\cdot w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)

\displaystyle=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g^{m})

\displaystyle=w_{\mathfrak{P}}(-A_{m-1}(j)g^{m-1}-\cdots-A_{1}(j)g-A_{0}(j))\quad\textrm{by (\ref{gequation})}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{w_{\mathfrak{P}}(-A_{k}(j)g^{k})~|~k=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1\}

\displaystyle=\min\{w_{\mathfrak{P}}(-A_{k}(j))+k\cdot w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)~|~k=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1\}

\displaystyle\geq\min\{k\cdot w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)~|~k=0,\,1,\,\ldots,\,m-1\}\quad\textrm{since $-A_{k}(j)$ has integer Fourier coefficients}

\displaystyle=(m-1)w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)\quad\textrm{by (\ref{wg<0})}.

It then follows that
w_{\mathfrak{P}}(g)\geq 0
, which contradicts (
10
).
Therefore we conclude that
the Fourier coefficients of
g
must be all algebraic integers in
L
.
∎

Proposition 6.2
.

Let
M
be a positive integer and let

g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
. Then,

g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
if and only if
g
is weakly holomorphic and

g\circ\alpha
has algebraic integer Fourier coefficients for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Proof..

Assume that
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
. Then
g
is weakly holomorphic by Lemma
3.3
.
Since
j
is modular for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
,
g\circ\alpha
is also integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]

for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Consequently, Lemma
6.1
implies that the Fourier coefficients of
g\circ\alpha

are all algebraic integers for all
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Conversely, assume that
g
is weakly holomorphic
and
g\circ\alpha
has algebraic integer Fourier coefficients for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Let
\{\alpha_{1}=I_{2},\,\alpha_{2},\,\ldots,\,\alpha_{t}\}
be a complete set
of representatives for the (right) cosets of
\Gamma(M)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
Consider the polynomial

G(x)=\prod_{i=1}^{t}(x-g\circ\alpha_{i})~\in\mathcal{F}_{M}[x],

which has
g
as a root.
By assumption, every coefficient of
G(x)

is a weakly holomorphic modular function for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
with
algebraic integer Fourier coefficients.
Thus each coefficient of
G(x)
is a polynomial in
j

with algebraic integer coefficients by Lemma
3.1
,
and hence
g
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.
∎

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
. If
p
is a prime not dividing
N
, then

c^{p}\equiv c^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K})\quad\textrm{for all}~c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}.

Proof..

If
c\in\mathcal{O}_{K}
, then we get by Lemma
7.1
that

c=A(\zeta_{N})\quad\textrm{for some}~A(x)=\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}x^{n}\in\mathbb{Z}[x].

We then derive that

\displaystyle c^{p}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}^{p}\zeta_{N}^{pn}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K})\quad\textrm{by the binomial theorem}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}\zeta_{N}^{pn}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K})\quad\textrm{by Fermat's little theorem
because $a_{n}\in\mathbb{Z}$}

\displaystyle\equiv\left(\sum_{n=0}^{m}a_{n}\zeta_{N}^{n}\right)^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K})

\displaystyle\equiv c^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K}).

∎

Lemma 7.3
.

Let
M
be a positive integer and
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{M})
.
Let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{M}
have the Fourier expansion

g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}q^{n/M}\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H})

such that
a_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{L}
for all
n\in\mathbb{Z}
.
For any prime
p
, we have

g(\tau)^{p}\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}a_{n}^{p}q^{pn/M}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/M})\!)).

(12)

Proof..

Let
t=\mathrm{ord}_{q^{1/M}}(g)
. For any positive integer
k
such that
k>t
, we derive by
using the binomial theorem that

\displaystyle g(\tau)^{p}

\displaystyle=q^{pt/M}\left(\sum_{n=t}^{k}a_{n}q^{(n-t)/M}+q^{(k+1-t)/M}\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{(n-(k+1))/M}\right)^{p}

\displaystyle\equiv q^{pt/M}\left\{\left(\sum_{n=t}^{k}a_{n}q^{(n-t)/M}\right)^{p}+\left(q^{(k+1-t)/M}\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{(n-(k+1))/M}\right)^{p}\right\}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/M})\!))

\displaystyle\equiv q^{pt/M}\left\{\sum_{n=t}^{k}a_{n}^{p}q^{p(n-t)/M}+q^{p(k+1-t)/M}\left(\sum_{n=k+1}^{\infty}a_{n}q^{(n-(k+1))/M}\right)^{p}\right\}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/M})\!))

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n=t}^{k}a_{n}^{p}q^{pn/M}+q^{p(k+1)/M}T(q^{1/M})\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/M})\!))\quad\textrm{for some}~T(x)\in\mathcal{O}_{L}[\![x]\!].

By letting
k\rightarrow\infty
, we arrive at the desired congruence.
∎

The proof of Lemma
7.3

essentially relies on the properties of the

acting on the power series ring over
\mathcal{O}_{L}/p\mathcal{O}_{L}

of characteristic
p

8
A generalization of the Kronecker congruence relation

In this section,
we establish our main result, which provides a generalization of the classical Kronecker congruence relation to meromorphic modular functions of higher level.

Theorem 8.1
.

Let
N
be a positive integer and
let
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N}
.
Suppose that
f
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
. Then,
for every prime
p
such that
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
, the function

F=\frac{1}{p}\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)

is also integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
. Consequently,
we obtain a Kronecker-type congruence

\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{Np}}).

Proof..

Let
K=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{N})
and
L=\mathbb{Q}(\zeta_{Np})
. We first
observe the following:

(i)

Both
f_{p}
and
f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}
belong to
\mathcal{F}_{Np}

and their Fourier coefficients lie
in
K
by Proposition
2.1
(i) and Lemma
2.2
, since
f\in\mathcal{F}_{N}
.

(ii)

For
every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, both
f_{p}\circ\alpha
and

f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha
are integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
by
Proposition
2.1
(i) and
Lemma
3.2
because
f
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.

(iii)

For every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
, the Fourier coefficients of

f_{p}\circ\alpha
and

f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha

lie in
\mathcal{O}_{L}

by (i), (ii) and Lemma
6.1
.

By (ii) and Lemma
3.3
,

pF
is weakly holomorphic.
According to Proposition
6.2
with (iii),
to establish the theorem,
it suffices to show that

(pF)\circ\alpha=\left\{\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)\right\}\circ\alpha

has Fourier coefficients in
p\mathcal{O}_{L}
for every
\alpha\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.

Let
\alpha=\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
.
There are two possible cases:

p\,|\,a\quad\textrm{or}\quad p\nmid a.

Let
\mathfrak{P}
be a prime ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{L}
lying above
p
,
and let
w_{\mathfrak{P}}:\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{F}_{Np}}\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\infty\}
be the exponential valuation defined in Definition
5.1
(with
M=Np
).
Write

(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}~\textrm{with}~c_{n}\in\mathcal{O}_{K}\quad(\tau\in\mathbb{H}).

(13)

Case 1.

Assume
p\,|\,a
.
We have

(f_{p}\circ\alpha)(\tau)=f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{p(c\tau+d)}\right)=f\left(\frac{(a/p)(p\tau)+b}{c(p\tau)+pd}\right)=\left(f\circ\begin{bmatrix}a/p&b\\
c&pd\end{bmatrix}\right)(p\tau).

(14)

The matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a/p&b\\
c&pd\end{bmatrix}
belongs to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})
because

p\,|\,a
, and satisfies

\begin{bmatrix}a/p&b\\
c&pd\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\alpha\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}~\textrm{or}~-\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\alpha\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\ (\textup{mod}\ NM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

(15)

since
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
.
We find that

\displaystyle~~~~\left(\left((f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}-f_{p}\right)\circ\alpha\right)(\tau)

\displaystyle=(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)^{p}-\left(f\circ\begin{bmatrix}a/p&b\\
c&pd\end{bmatrix}\right)(p\tau)\quad\textrm{by
(\ref{ffff})}

\displaystyle=(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]\alpha\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}(p\tau)

\displaystyle\hskip 28.45274pt\textrm{by
Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (ii) and (\ref{apa})
since}~\mathrm{Gal}(\mathcal{F}_{N}/\mathcal{F}_{1})\simeq\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z}/N\mathbb{Z})/\langle-I_{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)^{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}(p\tau)\quad\textrm{again by
Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (ii)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\right)^{p}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}q^{pn/N}\quad\textrm{by (\ref{facq}) and Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (i)}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}^{p}q^{pn/N}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}q^{pn/N}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{pLaurent}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}(c_{n}^{p}-c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}})q^{pn/N}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{K}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{cpc}},

which shows that
\left((f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}-f_{p}\right)\circ\alpha

has Fourier coefficients in
p\mathcal{O}_{K}
.
It follows by (iii) that
(pF)\circ\alpha=\left\{\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)\right\}\circ\alpha
has Fourier coefficients in
p\mathcal{O}_{L}
.

Case 2.

Now, assume
p\nmid a
. We find that

(f_{p}\circ\alpha)(\tau)=f\left(\frac{a\tau+b}{p(c\tau+d)}\right)=\left(f\circ\begin{bmatrix}a&b\\
pc&pd\end{bmatrix}\right)(\tau)=\left(f\circ\begin{bmatrix}a&(b-ak)/p\\
pc&d-ck\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\right)(\tau)

(16)

where
k
is an integer satisfying
ak\equiv b\ (\textup{mod}\ p)
.
The matrix
\begin{bmatrix}a&(b-ak)/p\\
pc&d-ck\end{bmatrix}
belongs to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{Z})

and satisfies

\begin{bmatrix}a&(b-ak)/p\\
pc&d-ck\end{bmatrix}\equiv\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\alpha\begin{bmatrix}1&-k\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\ (\textup{mod}\ NM_{2}(\mathbb{Z}))

(17)

because
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
. We derive that

\displaystyle~~~~((f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})\circ\alpha)(\tau)

\displaystyle=(f_{p}\circ\alpha)(\tau)^{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)

\displaystyle=\left(f\circ\begin{bmatrix}a&(b-ak)/p\\
pc&d-ck\end{bmatrix}\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}\right)(\tau)^{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)\quad\textrm{by (\ref{ffffk})}

\displaystyle=f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]\alpha\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&-k\\
0&1\end{smallmatrix}\right]\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\left(\begin{bmatrix}1&k\\
0&p\end{bmatrix}(\tau)\right)^{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)\quad\textrm{by (\ref{abcdk}) and Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (ii)}

\displaystyle=\left((f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)\circ\begin{bmatrix}1&-k\\
0&1\end{bmatrix}\right)^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\left(\frac{\tau+k}{p}\right)^{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\circ\alpha)(\tau)\quad\textrm{again by Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (ii)}

\displaystyle=g^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\left(\frac{\tau+k}{p}\right)^{p}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\quad\textrm{where}~g(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}\zeta_{N}^{-nk}q^{n/N},~\textrm{by (\ref{facq})}

\displaystyle=h\left(\frac{\tau+k}{p}\right)^{p}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\quad\textrm{where}~h(\tau)=\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}\zeta_{N}^{-pnk}q^{n/N},~\textrm{by Proposition \ref{Galoisaction} (i)}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}\zeta_{N}^{-pnk}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}n(\tau+k)/pN}\right)^{p}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}(c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}})^{p}\zeta_{N}^{-p^{2}nk}e^{2\pi\mathrm{i}n(\tau+k)/N}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))\quad\textrm{by Lemma \ref{pLaurent}}

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}(c_{n}^{\sigma_{N,\,p}})^{\sigma_{N,\,p}}q^{n/N}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))

by Lemma
7.2
and the fact
p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)
, so
p^{2}\equiv 1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)

\displaystyle\equiv\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}-\sum_{n\gg-\infty}c_{n}q^{n/N}\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/N})\!))

because

\sigma_{N,\,p}\circ\sigma_{N,\,p}
is the identity on
K
due to

p\equiv 1
or
-1\ (\textup{mod}\ N)

\displaystyle\equiv 0\ (\textup{mod}\ p\mathcal{O}_{L}(\!(q^{1/N})\!)).

This proves that
(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})\circ\alpha
has Fourier coefficients in
p\mathcal{O}_{L}
,
and hence

(pF)\circ\alpha=\left\{\left(f_{p}^{p}-f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]}\right)\left(f_{p}-(f^{\left[\begin{smallmatrix}1&0\\
0&p\end{smallmatrix}\right]})^{p}\right)\right\}\circ\alpha
has Fourier coefficients in
p\mathcal{O}_{L}
by (iii).

This completes the proof that
F
is integral over
\mathbb{Z}[j]
.
∎
