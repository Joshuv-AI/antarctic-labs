Maximal compatibility of disklike G G -transfer systems
David DeMark, Michael A. Hill, Yigal Kamel, Nelson Niu, Kurt Stoeckl, Danika Van Niel, Guoqi Yan

Abstract
. Transfer systems are a combinatorial model for N ∞ N_{\infty} -operads, which encode commutative structures in equivariant homotopy theory. Blumberg–Hill and Chan gave criteria for when two transfer systems are a compatible pair, meaning they encode the additive transfers and multiplicative norms of a ring-type structure. In this paper, given a transfer system encoding an additive structure, we give explicit formulae for the maximal transfer system it is compatible with. Our formulae simplify for disklike transfer systems, which typically encode additive structures. Further, we prove that (maximal) compatibility is functorial with respect to the inflation map induced by a quotient of groups, letting us compute maximal compatible transfer systems as inflations of connected transfer systems.

1.
Introduction

Ring spectra are fundamental in algebraic topology, and highly structure ring spectra are central to the field. Here we consider associative or commutative ring spectra where the associativity and commutativity are encoded by specifying coherent homotopies witnessing these properties. In the commutative case, the multiplication is structured by an
E_{\infty}
operad, and May showed that all such
E_{\infty}
operads are equivalent
[
13
]
.

There is a similar story in the equivariant setting over a finite group
G
, with some subtleties. Here we can intertwine the action of
G
with the permutation action on the coordinates. Classically, this is no new structure: the defining feature of “commutativity” is that the order does not matter. In the homotopical context, since commutativity is extra structure encoded by explicit homotopies, we are no longer guaranteed to be able to do this. Unpacking these ideas and codifying these led to the notion of an
N_{\infty}
operad by Blumberg and the second author
[
5
]
.

The combined work of many authors,

[
5
,
3
,
7
,
11
,
14
,
15
]
, show that the
\infty
-category of
G
-
N_{\infty}
operads is equivalent to a poset, the category of
G
-transfer systems. This allows us to study these complex
N_{\infty}
operads using tangible combinatorial tools.

When viewed additively, the
N_{\infty}
operads structure connective
G
-spectra with certain transfers prescribed by the operad. When viewed multiplicatively, they parameterize ring spectra with certain norm maps, the algebraic shadow of which is the corresponding incomplete Tambara functors. Allowing both to vary introduces compatibility conditions arising from
G
-twisted versions of the distributive law of multiplication over addition, described by Blumberg and the second author and refined by Chan to transfer systems
[
6
,
8
]
. Moreover, for any fixed additive transfer system, there is a maximal compatible multiplicative transfer system.

In this paper, we characterize compatible pairs
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
of transfer systems by devising formulae for the maximal compatible transfer system
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}_{a}})
for any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}_{a}
.
This characterizes all compatible pairs, as Blumberg–Hill observe that a pair of
G
-transfer systems
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
are compatible, if, and only if,

is contained by the unique maximal compatible transfer system

[

.
Our formulae simplify for

2.
Background

2.1.
Transfer Systems

We will denote the subgroup lattice of
G
as
\Sub(G)
, which has a natural partial order
\leq
defined by the subgroup inclusion.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
G
be a finite group.
A
G
-transfer system

\mathcal{O}
is a
binary relation refining subgroup inclusion on
\Sub(G)
,
consisting of
transfers
denoted by
\to
,
that satisfies the following additional conditions:

(1)

(Reflexivity)
H\to H
for all
H\leq G
,

(2)

(Conjugation)
K\to H
implies
gKg^{-1}\to gHg^{-1}
for all
g\in G
,

(3)

(Restriction)
K\to H
and
L\leq H
implies
(K\cap L)\to(H\cap L)
, and

(4)

(Composition)
L\to K
and
K\to H
implies
L\to H
.

We denote the set of
G
-transfer systems by
\tr(G)
.
Transfer systems may also be defined on an arbitrary lattice by ignoring condition (2).
We call these
categorical transfer systems
for clarity on the rare occasions they come up.
We denote the set of categorical transfer systems on
\mathcal{P}
by
\tr(\mathcal{P})
.

Throughout this paper, a
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
will be represented as a directed graph whose vertices comprise
\Sub(G)
and whose edges represent the partial order relation.

Notation 2.2
.

We use arrows to denote transfers in
\mathcal{O}
.
We sometimes employ dotted lines to denote subgroup inclusions which may not be transfers in
\mathcal{O}
for additional clarity.
For conciseness, we omit arrows that represent the reflexivity condition in both
G
-transfer systems and other binary relations known to be reflexive.

See the leftmost and rightmost diagrams of
Example

2.9
for two examples of transfer systems.

There are two fundamental transfer systems for any group
G
, which are maximal and minimal among all
G
-transfer systems when ordered by inclusion.

Definition 2.3
.

The
complete
G
-transfer system
has
H\to K
if and only if
H\leq K
.

The
trivial
G
-transfer system
only has the transfers
H\to H
for all
H\leq G
.

The collection of all transfer systems for a given group forms a lattice under refinement.

Proposition 2.4

(
[
16
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

The set of all
G
-transfer systems
\Tr(G)
is a lattice ordered by containment.
If
\mathcal{O}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
are two
G
-transfer systems, their
meet
is
\mathcal{O}\land\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is defined by intersection
\mathcal{O}\cap\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
, and their
join

\mathcal{O}\lor\mathcal{O}^{\prime}
is the meet of all transfer systems which contain both.

One can also generate a transfer system from a binary relation.

Definition 2.5
.

Let
B
be a binary relation refining inclusion on
\Sub(G)
. The
transfer system generated by
B
, denoted
T(B)
, is defined to be the intersection of all
G
-transfer systems containing
B
.
We shall also say that
B

generates

T(B)
.

An equivalent perspective which we find more useful in our later calculations is closing the binary relation under reflexivity, conjugation, restriction, and then composition. Meaning, we first add in all missing reflexive arrows, conjugate arrows, restriction arrows, and then composition arrows.

Definition 2.6

(
[
15
, Construction A.1]
)
.

Let
B
be a binary relation on
\Sub(G)
that refines inclusion. We have the following definitions.

(1)

The
reflexive closure
of
B
is

\Refl(B)=B\cup\bigcup_{H\leq G}\{H\rightarrow H\}.

(2)

The
conjugate closure
of
B
is

\Conj(B)=\bigcup_{K\to H\in B}\{gKg^{-1}\to gHg^{-1}\,|\,g\in G\}.

(3)

The
restriction closure
of
B
is

\Res(B)=\bigcup_{K\to H\in B}\{L\cap K\to L|L\leq H\}.

(4)

The
composition closure
of
B
is

\displaystyle\Comp(B)=\{H_{0}\to H_{n}\,|\,H_{0}\leq\dots\leq H_{n}\text{ with each }H_{i-1}\to H_{i}\in B\}.

Proposition 2.7

(
[
15
, Theorem A.2]
)
.

Let
B
be a binary relation refining inclusion on
\Sub(G)
.
Then
T(B)
is the closure of
B
under, reflexivity, conjugation, restriction, then composition; or equivalently one may commute restriction and conjugation:

T(B)=\Comp(\Res(\Conj(\Refl(B))))=\Comp(\Conj(\Res(\Refl(B)))).

One may also commute
\Refl(-)
with all other operators.

Remark 2.8
.

Although we treat reflexivity explicitly, one can also safely ignore it and just always assume all binary relations have reflexive edges.

This process allows us to consider generating sets of edges which give a transfer system.

There are many useful properties that are observable in a transfer system. We will now define saturated and disklike transfer systems which as mentioned in the introduction have applications to certain varieties of ring spectra. Additionally, these properties have implications when we discuss compatible pairs of transfer systems in the next section and throughout this article.

Definition 2.10
.

We say that a
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
is

if for every sequence of subgroups
L\leq K\leq H\leq G
whenever the transfer
L\to H
is in
\mathcal{O}
then

is also in
\mathcal{O}
. This can be visualized as the following:

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces K}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces H.}

If

\mathcal{O}

.

Note that in the above diagram, since

is a transfer system, then

is in
\mathcal{O}
by restriction.
If a transfer system is not saturated, then one can remedy the saturation failures to generate a saturated transfer system.

Definition 2.11
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
G
-transfer system.
The
hull
of
\mathcal{O}
, denoted
\hull(\mathcal{O})
, is the intersection of all saturated
G
-transfer systems which contain
\mathcal{O}
.

It can be shown that
\hull(\mathcal{O})
can also be constructed by adding exactly the transfers which cause a saturation failure in
\mathcal{O}
.

Definition 2.12
.

A
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
is said to be

if
\mathcal{O}
can be generated by a set of transfers all with target
G
.

We will refer to any such set of transfers to
G
which generate a disklike transfer system, say
\mathcal{O}

for

.
We say a set of disklike generators is

.
We say a set of disklike generators is

if it is equal to the complexity of the

-transfer system it generates.
Within a disklike transfer system

2.2.
Compatible Pairs

Let us recall the definition of a compatible pair of transfer systems.

Definition 2.16

(
[
8
, Definition 4.6]
)
.

A pair of
G
-transfer systems
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is

if the following two conditions hold:

(1)

\mathcal{O}_{m}\subseteq\mathcal{O}_{a}
, and

(2)

for any

, if
K\to H
is in
\mathcal{O}_{m}
, and
K\cap J\to K
is in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
, then
J\to H
is in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
.

Condition

can be visualized as

\mathcal{O}_{m}
(and consequently

), and the thin edge is in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
, then the dashed edge must be in
\mathcal{O}_{a}
to be compatible.

(2.1)

The most basic examples of compatible pairs are pairings with the complete and trivial transfer systems, see

Proposition 2.17
.

Let

and

be the complete and trivial
G
-transfer systems respectively. For any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
,
(C,\mathcal{O})
and

Remark 2.18
.

A transfer system is not always compatible with itself. In fact,
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O})
is a compatible pair if, and only if,
\mathcal{O}
is saturated.

Compatibility is preserved under joins in the multiplicative slot.

Proposition 2.19

(
[
6
, Proposition 7.84]
)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m}
and
\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{m}
be
G
-transfer systems.
If
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
and
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{m})
are
both compatible, then
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m}\lor\mathcal{O}^{\prime}_{m})
is also compatible.

This shows that for any
\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{a}
, there is a maximal compatible
\mathcal{O}_{m}
: the join of all compatible ones.

Proposition 2.20

(
[
6
, Corollary 7.85]
)
.

For any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
, there exists a unique transfer system
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
such that
(\mathcal{O},\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}}))
is maximally compatible.

Given a transfer system
\mathcal{O}
,

(\mathcal{O},-)
.

Proposition 2.21

(
[

, Remark 7.86]
)
.

A pair of
G
-transfer systems
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible if, and only if,
\mathcal{O}_{m}\subseteq\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}_{a}})
.

3.
Characterizing the Maximal Compatible Transfer System

3.1.
Maximal Compatible Formulae

Proposition 3.1
.

For any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
, a transfer
K\xrightarrow{e}H
is in
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
if and only if

\big(\mathcal{O},T(e)\big)

is compatible.

Proof.

If
f\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
then
T(f)\subseteq\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
so
(\mathcal{O},T(f))
must be compatible.
For readability, let
S=\{e\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,(\mathcal{O},T(e))\text{ is compatible}\}
.
In the other direction, we observe that

\displaystyle S\subseteq\underset{e\in S}{\bigvee}T(e).

Since
Proposition

2.19
shows that compatibility is closed under joins, then we know that
(\mathcal{O},\underset{e\in S}{\bigvee}T(e))
is a compatible pair, therefore
S\subseteq\underset{e\in S}{\bigvee}T(e)\subseteq\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.
∎

When checking compatibility with
T(e)
, it actually suffices to check a much smaller collection of edges.

.

For any

, if

is compatible with

, then so is any conjugate of

.

Proof.

Assume

is compatible with
\mathcal{O}_{a}
, and let

. Conjugating the diagram

\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces gsg^{-1}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{a}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{O}_{a}}

by

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{a}
and
\mathcal{O}_{m}
be
G
-transfer systems, and

a binary relation such that
\mathcal{O}_{m}=T(B_{m})
. The pair
(\mathcal{O}_{a},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible if and only if
\Res(B_{m})
is compatible with
\mathcal{O}_{a}
.

Note that
\Res(B_{m})
is not a transfer system in general.

Proof.

One direction is immediate. For the other, we need to show that

\Refl\big(\Comp\big(\Conj\big(\Res(B_{m})\big)\big)\big)

is compatible with
\mathcal{O}_{a}
. The reflexive condition is immediate. Lemma

and then Lemma

then show this is compatible with
\mathcal{O}_{a}
if

\Res(B_{m})\big),

as desired.

∎

We can use this lemma to obtain the following improved formulae.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
G
-transfer system.
The maximal compatible transfer system can be written as the following set:

(3.2)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\{K\to H\in\mathcal{O}\,\,|&\text{ for any }I<J\leq H,\\
&\text{ if }K\cap I\to K\cap J\in\mathcal{O}\text{ then }I\to J\in\mathcal{O}\}.\end{split}

Its complement,
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}\backslash\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}}),
can be written as:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}=\{K\to H\in\mathcal{O}\,\,|&\text{ there exist }I<J\leq H\text{, such that }\\
&K\cap I\to K\cap J\in\mathcal{O}\text{ and }I\to J\notin\mathcal{O}\}.\end{split}

This proposition tells us that whenever we have a diagram of the following form in

,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces J}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K\cap J}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{K\cap I}}
\scriptstyle{\color[rgb]{1,0,0}\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\not\exists}

all of the bold edges are in

, as they all restrict into a compatibility failure.
See

for a concrete realization of this diagram in the group

.

Proof.

From

, we know that

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\{K\overset{e}{\to}H\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,(\mathcal{O},T(e))\text{ is a compatible pair}\}.

Lemma

allows us to check the compatibility of

by checking if

is compatible with

.
Thus,

\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\{K\to H\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,

and observe that if

.
The complement can then be computed directly.
∎

Corollary 3.6
.

For any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
, if all restrictions of
e\in\mathcal{O}
are reflexive, then
e\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

Proof.

Suppose there exists an transfer
e\colon K\to H\in\mathcal{O}
such that all restrictions of
e
are reflexive, and consider subgroups
I<J\leq H
.
As
e
only admits reflexive restrictions, then
K\cap I=I
and
K\cap J=J
.
This implies that
I,J\leq K
.
By
Equation

3.2
, the condition for
e
to be in
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
is that if
K\cap I=I\to K\cap J=J\in\mathcal{O}
, then
I\to J\in\mathcal{O}
, and this is vacuously true.
Therefore
e\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.
∎

Given the number of subgroups present in these formulae, we now introduce some notation that simplifies their presentation.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
r
and
e
be transfers in a
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
.
If
e
restricts onto
r
, then we write
r\leq e
.
If
e
non-reflexively restricts into
r
, then we write
r<e
.
If
r<e
is a covering relation of the restriction poset, we write
r\prec e
. If
e:K\to H
, and
r:K\cap J\to J
are transfers in
\mathcal{O}
such that
r\leq e
, then we say that
r\leq e
is a

•

compatibility success
, denoted
r{{\leq}^{S}}e
, if
K\cap J\to K\in\mathcal{O}
implies
J\to H\in\mathcal{O}
,

•

compatibility failure
, denoted
r{{\leq}^{F}}e
, if
K\cap J\to K\in\mathcal{O}
and
J\to H\not\in\mathcal{O}
,

•

covered compatibility success
, denoted
r{{\prec}^{S}}e
, if
r{{\leq}^{S}}e
and
r\prec e
, and

•

covered compatibility failure
, denoted
r{{\prec}^{F}}e
, if
r{{\leq}^{F}}e
and
r\prec e
.

For example, the expressions of
Proposition

3.5
simplify to

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})

\displaystyle=\{e\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,\forall r\leq e,\text{if }r^{\prime}<r\text{ then $r^{\prime}<^{S}r$}\},\text{ and}

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}

\displaystyle=\{e\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,\exists r\leq e,\text{ such that }\exists r^{\prime}<r\text{ with $r^{\prime}<^{F}r$}\}.

We now refine our characterization of the maximal compatible transfer system in a manner that lets us recursively construct it using
Corollary

3.6
.
That is to say, starting with those transfers which restrict into no non-trivial transfers, and hence are trivially in the maximal compatible transfer system, we can construct the maximal transfer system from the bottom up.
Note that this recursion does terminate as
Equations

3.3
and
3.4
contain strict inequalities, and every strict chain of inequalities in a finite poset terminates.

Theorem 3.8
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a
G
-transfer system.
The maximal compatible transfer system can be written as the following set
built by a recursion on
e
starting with the transfers in
\mathcal{O}
which do not restrict to any non-trivial transfers:

(3.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\{e\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,\forall r<e,r\in M(\mathcal{O})\text{ and }r<^{S}e\},

where every restriction is in
\mathcal{O}
.
Its complement,
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}\coloneqq\mathcal{O}\backslash\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}}),
can be written as:

(3.6)

\displaystyle\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}=\{e\in\mathcal{O}\,|\,\exists r<e,r\not\in M(\mathcal{O})\text{ or }r<^{F}e\}.

Proof.

Let
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
be the formula of
Equation

3.3
, and let
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
be the formula of
Equation

3.5
.
We will show these formulae coincide, which yields the equality of their complements by negation.
We first observe that
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})\subseteq\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
as the condition defining
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
is a priori weaker that of
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

In the other direction, let
e\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
, and let
r^{\prime},r
be arbitrary, non-reflexive restrictions in
\mathcal{O}
such that
r^{\prime}<r\leq e
.
If there exists no such non-trivial
r^{\prime}
then
e
is vacuously in
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
by
Corollary

3.6
.
Since
r^{\prime}
and
r
are restrictions of
e
then
r,r^{\prime}\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
, or else
e\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
by definition of
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
.
Then
r^{\prime}<^{S}r
, as
r^{\prime}<r
and
r\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}
.
Therefore
e\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
by
Equation

3.3
, and
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{\prime}=\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
. ∎

3.2.
Consequences and Examples

We now characterize maximal compatible transfer systems, and deduce further properties using the formulae of the previous section.

Proposition 3.9
.

For any
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
,
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
is always saturated.

Proof.

If
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
is not saturated, then it contains a transfer

such that there exists a subgroup

with

If

is not in
\mathcal{O}
then
(\mathcal{O},\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}}))
is not compatible, to see this consider

for the case when
K\cap J=K
.
Therefore
J\to H
is in
\mathcal{O}
and not in
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
, and therefore
J\to H\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}
.

Proposition

3.5
then implies that

must restrict into a compatibility failure.
However, we observe in the following diagram of edges in

that this implies

as it also restricts into a compatibility failure, a contradiction.

3.3.
Disklike Maximal Compatible Formulae

Recall from
Definition

3.7
that if
r^{\prime}<r
is a covering relation of the restriction poset, we write
r^{\prime}\prec r
.
We will show that if
\mathcal{O}
is a disklike transfer system we may refine the formulae of
Theorem

3.8
to check only covering relations instead of all relations.

Lemma 3.18
.

If there is a transfer
s\in\mathcal{O}
such that
s\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
, then there exist transfers
e\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
and
r\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
such that
r<^{F}e\leq s
.

Proof.

Since
s\in\mathcal{O}
and
s\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
, then
s\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})^{c}
.
Meaning, there exists a transfer
s_{1}<s
in
\mathcal{O}
such that
s_{1}\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
or
s_{1}<^{F}s
.
If
s_{1}\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
, then this implies we must have
s_{1}<^{F}s
, thus we can let
r=s_{1}
and
e=s
and we are done.
If
s_{1}\not\in\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
, then given
s_{1}<s
, we can repeat this argument to find a transfer
s_{2}<s_{1}
such that
s_{2}\notin\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
or
s_{2}<^{F}s_{1}
.
This induction argument will finish because
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
contains all transfers in
\mathcal{O}
which have no non-trivial restrictions.
Therefore there exists some
n
such that
s_{n}<s_{n-1}<\ldots<s_{1}<s_{0}
, where

Disklike Consequences and Examples

Theorem

3.19
immediately yields an algorithm for computing the maximal compatible transfer system.
We observe that the initialization
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})\coloneqq\min\mathcal{O}
can be done as transfers in
\mathcal{O}
which only restrict to trivial transfers are trivially in
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
(
Corollary

3.6
), and the reason we may work with conjugacy classes of transfers,
[m]
, is because
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
is closed under conjugation.

Algorithm 3.21

Given a disklike
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
computes
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

Initialize
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})\coloneqq\min\mathcal{O}
under the
\leq
order, and
Q\coloneqq\mathcal{O}\setminus\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

while

Q
is non-empty
do

Let
m
be a minimal element of
Q
under the
\leq
order, and delete
[m]
from
Q
.

if

\forall r\in\mathcal{O}
such that
r\prec m
, we have that
r\in\mathrm{M}(\mathcal{O})
and
r{{\prec}^{S}}m

then

Add
[m]
to
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

return

\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
.

We now analyze the performance of
Algorithm

3.21
.
First, we bound the number of non-reflexive transfers in a disk-like transfer system
\mathcal{O}
, denoted
|\mathcal{O}|
, by the number of cover relations in the restriction poset of
\mathcal{O}
, denoted
C_{\mathcal{O}}
.
Note, most disklike transfer systems have many more cover relations than non-reflexive transfers.

Lemma 3.22
.

Every disklike
G
-transfer system,
\mathcal{O}
, satisfies
|\mathcal{O}|\leq 2C_{\mathcal{O}}+1
.

Proof.

Let
D
be the maximal set of disklike generators of
\mathcal{O}
.
Then we have the partition
\mathcal{O}=D\sqcup(\mathcal{O}\setminus D)
.
We observe that every transfer in
e\in\mathcal{O}\setminus D
has a disklike generator, and hence has at least one cover relation above it
e\prec q
.
Thus there is a surjection from cover relations to
\mathcal{O}\setminus D
, given by
(e\prec q)\mapsto e
,
implying
|\mathcal{O}\setminus D|\leq C_{\mathcal{O}}
.
We shall show that
|D|\leq|\mathcal{O}\setminus D|+1
, and thus conclude that
|\mathcal{O}|\leq 2|\mathcal{O}\setminus D|+1\leq 2C_{\mathcal{O}}+1
.

Suppose that
\mathcal{O}
has a single disklike generator, then
|D|=1\leq 1
.
If
\mathcal{O}
has more than one disklike generator, then it has a unique minimal disklike generator
N_{\mathcal{O}}\to G
where
N_{\mathcal{O}}
is meet of all subgroups
H
such that
H\to G\in D
, (see
Lemma

4.15
).
By restriction, there are transfers
N_{\mathcal{O}}\to H
for every every other disklike generator
H\to G\in D
.
Thus we have an injection from the set of non-minimal disk like generators to
\mathcal{O}\setminus D
, given by
H\to G\mapsto N_{\mathcal{O}}\to H
.
This implies
|D|-1\leq|\mathcal{O}\setminus D|
as desired.
∎

We now analyze the worst case asymptotic performance of
Algorithm

3.21
.
Recall that if
f,g:A\to\mathbb{N}
, then in big O notation we say
f=O(g)
if there exists a constant
K\in\mathbb{N}
such that
\forall a\in A,f(a)\leq Kg(a)
.
Thus an algorithm is said to have worst case time complexity
O(g)
if a function
f
measuring the number of operations of the algorithm satisfies
f=O(g)
.

Proposition 3.23
.

Algorithm

3.21
has a
O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
worst case time complexity across all transfer systems across all groups, where
C_{\mathcal{O}}
is the number of cover relations in the restriction poset of
\mathcal{O}
.
Moreover,
Algorithm

3.21
is optimal among all algorithms which must compute the restriction poset of
\mathcal{O}

Proof.

We shall show that the initialization time and the main while loop of the algorithm both have a worst case time complexity of
O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
.
We note that in the worst case, our group is abelian, and thus we cannot improve performance via clever use of conjugacy classes.

The initialization cost is
O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
.
To initialize our queue and to determine minimal transfers in
\mathcal{O}
under the restriction order
\leq
, we just need to topologically sort the transfers of
\mathcal{O}
under
\leq
, i.e. pick a total ordering of the transfers of
\mathcal{O}
which is compatible with
\leq
.
Optimal algorithms for topologically sorting a graph with
|V|
vertices and
|E|
edges have a worst case time complexity of
O(|V|+|E|)
(see for example
[
9
]
), thus our topological sort has a time complexity of
O(|\mathcal{O}|+C_{\mathcal{O}})
.
However,
Lemma

3.22
says that in a disklike transfer system
|\mathcal{O}|\leq 2C_{\mathcal{O}}+1
, and thus
O(|\mathcal{O}|+C_{\mathcal{O}})=O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
(i.e. the asymptotic growth of the sum of two things depends solely on the larger).
The main while loop of the algorithm also has a worst case time complexity of
O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
, as in this particular case the algorithm inspects every single cover relation, yielding the claimed total complexity
O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
.

Finally, we can see that this worst case time complexity is optimal amongst all algorithms which must construct the graph of the restriction poset, as this graph has
|\mathcal{O}|
vertices, and
C_{\mathcal{O}}
edges, and thus takes
O(|\mathcal{O}|+C_{\mathcal{O}})=O(C_{\mathcal{O}})
time to construct.
∎

Remark 3.24
.

Hypothetically, the best possible algorithm for computing the maximal compatible transfer system would have a time complexity of

Given that

-transfer system,

However, it seems likely that

G
-transfer system, i.e., every general algorithm must check some linear proportion of cover relations.

is not in
p^{-1}\mathcal{O}
.
Applying
Lemma

4.10
twice reveals
(I\cap H)N\to(J\cap H)N
is equal to
I\to J
.
Since
(I\cap H)N\to(J\cap H)N\notin p^{-1}\mathcal{O}
, then by
Lemma

4.3
we have that
I\cap H\to J\cap H\notin p^{*}\mathcal{O}
.
Therefore
K\to H
restricts into a compatibility failure, and
K\to H\in\mathrm{M}({p^{*}\mathcal{O}})^{c}
.∎

This result then lets us extend
Proposition

4.11
to the case when
\mathcal{O}_{m}
is not saturated.

Corollary 4.13

(
Theorem

1.2
(3))
.

Let
N\trianglelefteq G
be a normal subgroup. Let
\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}_{m}
be two
G/N
-transfer systems, and
p:G\twoheadrightarrow G/N
the projection.
If
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible, then so is
(p^{*}\mathcal{O},p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{m})
.

Proof.

Recall that
(\mathcal{O},\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible if, and only if,
\mathcal{O}_{m}\subseteq\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
(
Proposition

2.21
).
Thus as
p^{*}
is monotone (
Lemma

4.8
), and preserves maximal compatibility (
Proposition

4.12
), this implies that
p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{m}\subseteq p^{*}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\mathrm{M}({p^{*}\mathcal{O}})
.
Which implies
(p^{*}\mathcal{O},p^{*}\mathcal{O}_{m})
is compatible.
∎

4.3.
Consequence and Counterexamples

In this section we discuss a powerful consequence of
Theorem

1.2
, namely
Corollary

4.16
. This uses the right adjoint to the inflation functor, the
N
-fixed points.

Definition 4.14

(
[
16
, Section 5.1]
,
[
5
, Appendix B]
)
.

If
\mathcal{O}
is a
G
-transfer system, let
\mathcal{O}^{N}
be the
G/N
-transfer system given by restricting
\mathcal{O}
to
\Sub(G/N)\subseteq\Sub(G)
.

Rubin showed that the
N
-fixed points is right adjoint to the pull back
[
16
]
, so for any normal subgroup
N
, we always have a map of
G
-transfer systems

p^{\ast}\mathcal{O}^{N}\to\mathcal{O},

and when we look at the part of
\Sub(G)
coming from
\Sub(G/N)
, this map is just the identity.

Recall now that for any
\mathcal{O}
, there is a minimal subgroup
N_{\mathcal{O}}
which transfers to
G
. Minimality guarantees also that
N_{\mathcal{O}}
is normal.

Lemma 4.15
.

For a disklike
G
-transfer system
\mathcal{O}
, we have

p^{\ast}\mathcal{O}^{N}\cong\mathcal{O}.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{O}
is disklike, it is generated by transfers of the form
H\to G
. The minimality of
N_{\mathcal{O}}
shows that any such
H
contains
N_{\mathcal{O}}
, so all of the generating transfers already sit in the image of
\Sub(G/N)
in
\Sub(G)
.
∎

A consequence of
Theorem

1.2
(4) and
Lemma

4.3
is that

\mathrm{M}({p^{*}\mathcal{O}^{N}})=p^{*}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}^{N}})=\Res(p^{-1}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}^{N}})).

Combining these with
Lemma

4.15
gives a significant simplification in the computation of the maximal compatible transfer system for a disklike transfer system.

Corollary 4.16
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a disklike
G
-transfer system and let
p:G\to G/N_{\mathcal{O}}
be the associated quotient map.
Then, the computation of
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})
reduces to the computation of
\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}^{N}})
in
\sub(G/N_{\mathcal{O}})
:

\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}})=\Res(p^{-1}\mathrm{M}({\mathcal{O}^{N}})).

This simplification is powerful.
For example, we can use this corollary to simplify the computation of the maximal compatible transfer system for the
C_{q^{3}r^{3}}
-transfer system of
Example

4.5
.
We observe that
N_{\mathcal{O}}=C_{qr}
, and that our computation reduces to the
C_{q^{2}r^{2}}
-transfer system of
Example

3.21
.
In general, the smaller the interval
[N_{\mathcal{O}},G]
is in comparison to all of
\sub(G)
, the more effective this simplification is.
