 ABSTRACT
The neutron resonance parameters for 19 F were evaluated using transmission, capture, and inelastic data
in the resonance region from thermal energies up to 1 MeV. The analyses were carried out with the code
SAMMY. A key feature of this evaluation is the inclusion of a high-resolution capture cross-section mea-
surement performed at the Oak Ridge Electron Linear Accelerator (Lewis 1976) in the energy range from
10 to 700 keV. In addition, new inelastic cross-section measurements conducted at the Geel Electron Lin-
ear Accelerator (Mondelaers and Schillebeeckx 2006) were incorporated into the evaluation. The reduced
R-matrix (Reich–Moore) formalism implemented in the SAMMY code was used to analyze the data. This
formalism was also applied to fit the inelastic data and determine resonance parameters for the first two
inelastic levels of 19 F, occurring at 109.9 and 197.2 keV, respectively. The evaluation also produced the
corresponding resonance parameter covariance matrices.
The objective of this work is to describe the experimental database used in the evaluation, outline the pro-
cedures employed to analyze the data, and present the resulting resonance parameters together with their
application to benchmark calculations.

 1. INTRODUCTION
Fluorine is an important nuclide that appears in a wide range of nuclear applications due to its unique
chemical stability and neutron interaction properties. For instance, it is a constituent of UF6 , which is used
in the gaseous diffusion and gas centrifuge processes for the separation of two naturally occurring ura-
nium isotopes, 235 U and 238 U. Fluorine is also a key component in molten salt reactor technologies, where
fluoride-based salts such as LiF-BeF2 (FLiBe) or LiF-NaF-KF (FLiNaK) serve as both coolants and fuel
carriers, providing excellent thermal stability, chemical inertness, and favorable heat-transfer characteris-
tics.
From a nuclear physics perspective, fluorine is an intermediate-mass nuclide whose neutron interaction
behavior significantly influences neutron moderation and absorption in reactor systems. Its relatively low
atomic mass enables partial slowing down of fast neutrons into the epithermal energy range, while its non-
negligible neutron capture cross section can impact reactivity and spectrum shaping in systems containing
fluorine-bearing materials. Consequently, accurate nuclear data for 19 F are essential for reactor design, fuel
cycle analysis, and criticality safety assessments involving both structural and chemical compounds that
include fluorine.
Resonance parameters for fluorine were determined through a self-consistent analysis of relevant experi-
mental data, namely high-resolution transmission, capture, and inelastic cross sections. Thermal total and
capture cross sections were also evaluated. The multilevel R-matrix code SAMMY was used for the se-
quential analysis of the data. Two distinctive features of the 19 F analysis with SAMMY are the fitting of
the new Geel Electron Linear Accelerator (GELINA) inelastic cross-section data and the inclusion of the
Oak Ridge Electron Linear Accelerator (ORELA) capture cross-section measurements. In the energy re-
gion below 1 MeV, there are two inelastic levels at 109.9 and 197.2 keV. The Reich–Moore formalism was
originally developed to accurately describe fission cross sections by accounting for interference effects in
fission channels. Nevertheless, this formalism can also be applied to other reactions, such as the inelas-
tic cross section considered in this work. In the capture cross-section analysis, multiple-scattering effects
were incorporated into the SAMMY evaluation. The results of the SAMMY analysis are presented, and
the cross sections calculated from the evaluated resonance parameters are compared with the experimental
data.

 2. EXPERIMENTAL DATABASE
The experimental data used in the SAMMY evaluation of the 19 F cross sections are as follows:
 1. Three neutron transmission measurements by Larson et al. (1976) were performed at ORELA over
 the neutron energy range from 5 eV to 20 MeV. The thick and thin samples, with atomic densities
 of 0.13093 and 0.016886 at/b, respectively, were measured at an 80 m flight path, while the medium
 sample, with an atomic density of 0.024184 at/b, was measured at a 200 m flight path. The thick
 and thin sample transmission data were analyzed in the energy region from 5 eV to 200 keV. The
 medium sample transmission data were analyzed in the energy region from 10 keV to 1 MeV. Both
 the medium and the thin samples were contaminated with carbon, which was accounted for in the
 SAMMY evaluation. The sequential SAMMY analysis of the experimental transmission data was
 performed by allowing a search on the normalization and background correction parameters and us-
 ing the sequence SAMMAR of the SAMMY code. A constant background correction was used. For
 the thin sample, the normalization and background corrections were 0.97183 and 0.00749, respec-
 tively. For the medium sample, the normalization and background were 0.97903 and 0.00013, re-
 spectively, and for the thick sample, the results were 0.99795 and −0.00128, respectively. The thick
 sample data were used as the reference data for two reasons: (a) the sample was not contaminated
 with carbon, and (b) the effective cross section extracted from the transmission data was well nor-
 malized with the existing ENDF/B evaluation.
 2. A set of total cross-section measurements, obtained at the 5 MeV Van de Graaff accelerator at Ar-
 gonne National Laboratory by Hibdon (1964) with a 10 m flight path, was included in the evaluation
 below 300 keV.
 3. One capture cross-section measurement by Guber et al. (2002) was performed at the ORELA 40 m
 flight path in the energy range from 10 to 700 keV. The normalization and background of the capture
 data calculated with the SAMMY-SAMMAR code were 0.99885 and 0.00009, respectively.
 4. Experimental data below 5 eV available in the literature were used to constrain the low-energy be-
 havior of the evaluated cross sections. The total cross-section data of Rainwater et al. (1948), cover-
 ing the energy range from 0.0205 to 37.1 eV, were used for fitting the total cross sections.
 5. The only dedicated thermal capture measurement available was that performed by Raman et
 al. (1996) at the 8 MW Omega West reactor (Los Alamos National Laboratory) and obtained
 through γ-spectroscopy of the 20 F levels. The reported thermal capture cross section is 0.0096 ±
      0.0003 b at 0.0253 eV.
 6. Inelastic cross-section data for the 110 and 197 keV excited levels, measured at ORELA by Mor-
 gan and Dickens (1976) using a 47.3 m flight path, were included in the evaluation. These measure-
 ments played an important role in constraining the inelastic scattering contributions and improving
 the overall consistency of the evaluation in this energy region.
 7. Recent experimental work by Knijpstra et al. (2025) provided new high-precision data for the
 19 F(n, n′ γ) reaction. The measurements were performed at the GELINA time-of-flight facility of

 the European Commission’s Joint Research Centre using monoenergetic pulsed-neutron beams and
 high-resolution γ-ray spectroscopy. The study was designed to obtain accurate partial γ-production
 cross sections associated with the first excited states of 19 F, thereby improving the knowledge of in-
 elastic scattering processes in fluorine.

 2

 The experiment covered neutron energies from the threshold region up to several megaelectron volts
 and employed a combination of high-purity germanium and LaBr3 detectors for γ-ray detection. The
 authors report detailed angular-distribution and energy-dependent cross sections for prominent γ
 transitions, including those corresponding to the 109.9 and 197.2 keV levels in 19 F. Absolute nor-
 malization was achieved through reference reactions and well-characterized neutron flux monitor-
 ing, resulting in reduced systematic uncertainties compared with earlier measurements. The experi-
 ment was performed using the GAINS (Germanium Array for Inelastic Neutron Scattering) setup at
 a flight-path length of approximately 100 m.
 These new data constitute an important benchmark for nuclear-reaction modeling and evaluation of
 fluorine isotopes. In particular, they help constrain inelastic scattering channels in the intermediate
 energy ranges that are relevant for dosimetry, shielding, and reactor physics applications. The re-
 sults of Knijpstra et al. (2025) complement the classic transmission and capture datasets obtained
 at ORELA (Morgan and Dickens 1976), providing an updated experimental foundation for future
 resonance and continuum-region evaluations of fluorine.

 3. SAMMY ANALYSIS AND RESONANCE PARAMETERS
To initiate a data fitting using the code SAMMY, in addition to the experimental data analyzed, two inputs
were needed: an input file containing the initial guess of the resonance parameters and an input file con-
taining spin-group information. The initial set of Reich–Moore resonance parameters for 19 F up to 1 MeV
was constructed as follows: (a) using resonance parameters listed in the Atlas of Neutron Resonances
(Mughabghab 2018), which consisted of resonance energy Er , neutron width Γn , gamma width Γγ , reso-
nance spin J, and angular momentum l; (b) using the peak search option of the RSAP code (Sayer 2003)
to identify resonance energies and neutron widths from experimental total cross-section data; and (c) using
the SUGGEL computer code (Oh and Leal 2001) to identify the orbital angular momentum l of the reso-
nance level. For the two inelastic channels, which open at the energies 109.9 and 197.2 keV, an arbitrary
initial width value of 1,000 meV was initially assigned.
Natural fluorine is 100% 19 F, which has ground state spin and parity I π = 1/2+ . Given that the neutron
spin and parity are iπ = 1/2+ , there are two possible channel spins, namely sc = 0 and sc = 1. The spin
and parity J π of the excited states for the n + 19 F reaction are shown in Table 2 for orbital angular momenta
l = 0, 1, 2, and 3 (s-, p-, d-, f -waves, respectively).
Table 2 shows the possibility of more than one entrance channel for distinct values of the angular momen-
tum l. As an example, for l = 1 and l = 3, there are two possible entrance channels with resonance spin
and parity J π = 2− . The SAMMY code can accommodate this feature, and the ENDF (Cullen et al. 2009)
format can also represent this information.

The two outgoing inelastic channels also require spin information: the channel at an excitation energy of
109.9 keV has spin and parity J π = 1/2− , and the channel at 197.2 keV has J π = 5/2+ . The spin assign-
ments for these outgoing channels are determined based on the conservation of total angular momentum
and parity—that is, the total angular momentum and parity for the entrance channel must equal that for
the corresponding outgoing channel. Tables 3 and 4 show the possible spin combinations for the first and
second inelastic channels, respectively.
A close examination of Tables 2 to 4 reveals that, based on the conservation of total angular momentum,
multiple combinations of entrance and outgoing channels are possible. For instance, for J π = 2− , there are
two possible entrance channels, two outgoing channels for the 109.9 keV inelastic level, and three outgo-
ing channels for the 197.2 keV level. All of this information is provided to the SAMMY code as input for
fitting the experimental data.

 4. RESULTS OF THE SAMMY ANALYSIS
The experimental data presented in Table 1 were analyzed using the SAMMY code through an iterative
least-squares fitting procedure. Resonance parameters were varied systematically until a reasonable and
physically consistent representation of the data was obtained. The final fit reproduced the experimental
trends and magnitudes across the relevant energy range within the stated experimental uncertainties. The
analysis incorporated full covariance treatment to propagate both experimental and model uncertainties to
the fitted parameters.

4.1 TRANSMISSION (TOTAL CROSS SECTION)
The data presented in Table 1, comprising three sets of transmission measurements by Larson et al. (1976)
and total cross-section data by Hibdon (1964), were analyzed using the SAMMY code up to 1 MeV. In
Figure 1, the black continuous line represents the SAMMY prediction based on the evaluated resonance
parameters, while the red symbols correspond to the experimental data. The Larson et al. transmission
data, measured at the 200 m flight path with an atomic density of 0.024184 at/b, were used to generate
resonance parameters up to 1 MeV and are presented in the form of effective total cross sections extracted
from the measured transmission. The total cross-section data of Hibdon, shown at the bottom of the figure,
are also included for comparison. Comparisons between the experimental data and the SAMMY calcula-
tions based on the evaluated resonance parameters are shown in Figure 1, demonstrating very good agree-
ment.

4.2 CAPTURE CROSS SECTION
Figure 2 shows comparisons of the SAMMY-calculated capture cross section with the experimental data
of Guber et al. (2002). The agreement between the calculations and the measurements is very good across
the energy range considered, indicating that the evaluated resonance parameters provide an adequate rep-
resentation of the capture process. It should be noted that Figure 2 presents the total capture cross section,
which includes both the resonance and direct capture components; Section 4.5 provides further discussion
of these contributions. The magnitude of the capture cross section is significantly smaller than that of the
total cross section, typically by a factor of about 1,000. As a result, some resonances that are clearly visi-
ble in the capture data do not appear in the total cross section. This difference highlights the sensitivity of
capture measurements to specific resonance features and underscores the importance of including capture
data in the evaluation to properly constrain the resonance parameters.

 region up to 700 keV.

4.3 INELASTIC CROSS SECTION
For the inelastic cross-section analysis, experimental data covering the first and second inelastic scat-
tering channels were used. Below 1 MeV, two inelastic channels are open in 19 F, occurring at 109.9 and
197.2 keV, which correspond to the first and second excited states, respectively. The primary dataset con-
sisted of the measurements by Morgan and Dickens (1976), which were obtained at ORELA using a 47.3
m flight path. Also included were more recent high-resolution measurements performed at the GELINA
facility by Knijpstra et al. (2025) at a flight path of approximately 100 m, and these measurements pro-
vided detailed information on the inelastic cross-section shape. These complementary datasets span a
broad energy range and provide important constraints on the inelastic scattering cross sections associated
with both channels.
The inclusion of these experimental data enabled the identification of resonance parameters that provide
a consistent and accurate description of the inelastic measurements. This work represents the first appli-
cation of the Reich–Moore formalism to the simultaneous fitting of inelastic scattering data of this type,
extending its use beyond its traditional applications. Figures 3 and 4 present the SAMMY results for the
data of Morgan and Dickens (1976) and Knijpstra et al. (2025), respectively. The high-resolution data of
Knijpstra et al., measured at a longer flight path, were used as the primary basis for the SAMMY fitting,
enabling determination of resonance energy parameters with better accuracy.

4.4 THERMAL CROSS SECTION
The experimental data of Rainwater et al. (1948) were used for the fitting of the total cross section in the
energy region from thermal to 5 eV. The Rainwater et al. total cross-section data were in agreement with
the effective total cross section derived from Oak Ridge National Laboratory’s thick sample transmission
data. No data normalization was needed for the Rainwater et al. total cross section. With the exception of
the capture cross-section measurement of Raman et al. (1996) at thermal energy (0.0253 eV), there are no
other experimental capture cross-section data available in the literature. To fit the capture cross-section
data at low energy, the ENDF/B-VIII.1 19 F capture cross-section evaluation was used. The ENDF capture
data were normalized at thermal to the value suggested by Raman et al. Figure 5 shows a comparison of

4.5 DIRECT CAPTURE CROSS SECTION
The R-matrix formalism parameterizes cross sections in the resolved resonance energy region, in which
the incident particle and the target nucleus combine to form a (many-body) compound nucleus, which then
decays to yield the outgoing particle and the residual nucleus. On the other hand, some physical processes
do not proceed via formation of a compound nuclear state but are attributed to direct (single-particle) re-
actions whose parameterization has only recently been introduced into the R-matrix formalism (Arbanas
et al. 2023) and is yet to be implemented into the SAMMY code.
In this work, an extant feature of the SAMMY code was employed to read the direct neutron capture cross
section calculated using the CUPIDO code (Dietrich and Arbanas 1995) and to add it to the R-matrix cross
section, thus neglecting interference effects. A direct-semidirect (DSD) capture cross section was calcu-
lated using the CUPIDO code and listed in tabular format as input to the SAMMY code. The SAMMY
code then calculated the corresponding resonance part (σres γ ) of the capture cross section and added to it

the direct capture component (σγ ) multiplied by a scalar that was treated as a parameter to be optimized
(i.e., evaluated) simultaneously with the R-matrix parameters. Due to the uncertainty in the absolute mag-
nitude of the model calculations, the effect of direct reaction processes on the total capture cross section
cannot be well known. At present, SAMMY applies Bayesian evaluation to the coefficient α multiplying
the external direct cross section.

 5. COVARIANCE GENERATION
In addition to the resonance-parameter evaluation, this work also provides resonance-parameter covari-
ance obtained from the SAMMY fitting of experimental data, including both statistical and systematic
uncertainty information. The study focuses on the generation and testing of covariance data for 19 F in
the resonance region, with particular emphasis on the propagation of resonance-parameter covariance to
multigroup cross-section uncertainties. The covariance information was represented in ENDF format us-
ing the LCOMP=1 option, which explicitly provides the full resonance-parameter covariance matrix. The
resonance-parameter covariance was obtained from the SAMMY fitting of experimental data and therefore
incorporated both the statistical and systematic uncertainties associated with the measurements. This rep-
resentation was then processed with AMPX/PUFF to obtain cross-section covariance data in the SCALE
56-neutron-group structure. The resonance-parameter covariance was processed with PUFF using a flat-
flux weighting spectrum to generate multigroup cross-section covariance data.
A key aspect of this work is that the resonance-parameter covariance treatment was extended to inelas-
tic threshold reactions. Traditionally, resonance-parameter covariance is most commonly associated with
elastic, capture, fission, and total cross sections in the resolved resonance region. Applying this approach
to threshold inelastic channels is therefore a new and technically important step because it tests whether
resonance-parameter uncertainty information can be propagated consistently to reactions that open only
above threshold.
The significance of this study lies in demonstrating that resonance-parameter covariance data can be prop-
agated to multigroup cross-section uncertainty for 19 F, including inelastic threshold reactions. Using the
ENDF LCOMP=1 representation enabled the full resonance-parameter covariance matrix to be retained
and correlations among resonance parameters to be carried into the processed covariance data. Because the
covariance originated from the SAMMY analysis of experimental measurements, the resulting uncertain-
ties reflect both experimental systematic effects and statistical fluctuations embedded in the fitting process.
The resulting covariance matrices in the SCALE 56-neutron-group structure provide a practical format for
sensitivity and uncertainty analysis in criticality safety applications. This work is particularly relevant be-
cause the use of resonance-parameter covariance for inelastic threshold reactions has not traditionally been
part of standard covariance-generation workflows. The covariance results were visualized using the COVR
module of NJOY. Five covariance plots are presented for the principal reaction channels of 19 F, namely the
total cross section, elastic scattering, capture, and the two inelastic scattering channels corresponding to
the 110 and 197 keV excited states, respectively. Figures 7 to 11 show the covariance matrices for the to-
tal, elastic scattering, capture, and inelastic scattering cross sections corresponding to the 110 and 197 keV
channels.

 6. BENCHMARK RESULTS
6.1 HEU-SOL-THERM-039
The International Criticality Safety Benchmark Evaluation Project (ICSBEP) benchmark HEU-SOL-
THERM-039 (HST-039-001) was selected to assess the impact of the revised 19 F resonance evaluation
on thermal-spectrum criticality calculations involving fluorine-bearing systems. This benchmark consists
of a spherical Monel tank containing a homogeneous solution of UF6 dissolved in hydrofluoric acid (HF).
The uranium enrichment was approximately 93 wt % in 235 U, providing a highly enriched thermal system
with significant sensitivity to the neutron interaction properties of fluorine and hydrogen.
The spherical vessel was surrounded by a large cylindrical water tank filled with hot water maintained
at temperatures ranging from approximately 75◦ C to 95◦ C. Several configurations were measured with
hydrogen-to-uranium atomic ratios varying from 5.7 to 82, corresponding to uranium concentrations be-
tween approximately 975 and 155 g/L. These variations provided a broad range of moderation conditions,
enabling the evaluation of the fluorine resonance treatment under different neutron spectra.
The experiments were performed during the 1960s at the Commissariat à l’Énergie Atomique (CEA) Val-
duc facility using “Apparatus C.” The UF6 employed in the experiments was supplied by the Union Car-
bide Oak Ridge Y-12 plant in 1964. Due to the presence of fluorine in both UF6 and HF, this benchmark
provides a valuable integral test for validating the 19 F resonance parameters and their influence on neutron
multiplication calculations.

6.2 TETHYS CRITICAL EXPERIMENT
In addition to the historical ICSBEP benchmark, the forthcoming Tethys critical experiment was consid-
ered as an important application for the revised 19 F evaluation. The Tethys experiment is planned for exe-
cution in 2027 using the Hypatia critical experiment platform and is intended to support the validation of
molten salt reactor materials and advanced reactor concepts employing FLiBe (2LiF:BeF2 ) as a coolant
and/or moderator material.
The experiment is designed around a central stack of 6 in. diameter components composed of depleted
uranium, highly enriched uranium (HEU) metal fuel plates, beryllium, graphite, polyethylene, and con-
tainerized solid FLiBe. The assembly will be reflected by beryllium blocks to achieve the desired neutron
economy and spectral characteristics. By incorporating FLiBe directly into the experimental configuration,
the benchmark will provide valuable integral data for validating neutron interaction data for both lithium
and fluorine in reactor-relevant environments.
Because fluorine is a major constituent of FLiBe, the Tethys experiment is expected to exhibit sensitivity
to the 19 F cross sections, particularly in the epithermal and intermediate energy ranges relevant to molten
salt systems. Consequently, this experiment will represent an important future benchmark for assessing the
performance of the revised 19 F resonance evaluation in advanced reactor applications.

6.3 BENCHMARK CALCULATIONS
Benchmark calculations were performed to assess the impact of the revised 19 F resonance evaluation on
integral criticality predictions. Three major evaluated nuclear data libraries were considered in this study:
ENDF/B-VIII.1, JEFF-4.0, and JENDL-5. For each library, the original and revised 19 F resonance eval-
uations were incorporated while maintaining the remaining isotopic data from the ENDF/B-VIII.1 eval-
uation. This approach allowed the effects of the fluorine evaluation to be isolated and directly compared
across the different library environments.

 20

All criticality calculations were performed using the continuous-energy version of the SCALE Code Sys-
tem with the CE-KENO Monte Carlo transport sequence. Continuous-energy cross section libraries com-
patible with SCALE were generated using the AMPX nuclear data processing system. The processed li-
braries included the modified 19 F resonance parameters embedded within each of the three evaluated data
libraries considered in this work.
It should be emphasized that only the 19 F evaluation was modified between the libraries investigated in this
study. All other isotopes present in the benchmark models, including uranium, hydrogen, oxygen, beryl-
lium, carbon, and structural materials, were taken from the ENDF/B-VIII.1 library, including the appro-
priate thermal scattering law (TSL) data for hydrogen bound in HF for HST-039-001. Additionally, the
fluorine TSL for FLiBe was not used based on the physical properties of the FLiBe used in the Tethys
experiment. Consequently, differences observed in the benchmark results can be primarily attributed to
changes associated with the 19 F resonance evaluation rather than variations in the underlying data for other
nuclides.
The benchmark models were simulated using identical CE-KENO input configurations and calculation
parameters to ensure a consistent comparison between the different 19 F evaluations. The resulting effective
neutron multiplication factors (keff ) were then compared against benchmark reference values to quantify
the influence of the revised fluorine resonance treatment on the predicted system reactivity.
The benchmark calculation results obtained with the original and revised 19 F resonance evaluations are
summarized in Table 9. The simplified model keff with uncertainty for HST-039-001 is 1.0000 ± 0.0057 and
Tethys is expected to be approximately 1.0000. As such, only the Monte Carlo keff and uncertainty values
are provided for the two experimental cases.

 8. CONCLUSION AND FUTURE WORK
The revised 19 F resonance evaluation produced a systematic reduction in the calculated keff values for all
benchmark systems investigated in this work. This behavior was consistently observed for calculations
performed using ENDF/B-VIII.1, JEFF-4.0, and JENDL-5 based libraries in which only the 19 F evaluation
was modified while all remaining isotopic evaluations were retained from ENDF/B-VIII.1. The observed
changes therefore primarily reflect differences associated with the fluorine resonance representation.
The reduction in system reactivity suggests that the revised resonance parameters increase the effective
neutron removal associated with fluorine through modifications to the scattering and capture cross sections
in the resolved and unresolved resonance regions. Because fluorine is present in both the HF and UF6 con-
stituents of the HEU-SOL-THERM-039 benchmark as well as in the FLiBe coolant salt composition, the
systems exhibit measurable sensitivity to the 19 F nuclear data.
For the HEU-SOL-THERM-039 benchmark, the revised evaluation improves agreement with the bench-
mark reference value of 1.0000 ± 0.0057. Although the original evaluations already produced reasonably
accurate predictions, the revised resonance treatment consistently reduced the positive reactivity bias ob-
served in all libraries considered. The largest correction was observed for the JENDL-5 based calculations,
indicating a stronger sensitivity of that library to the revised fluorine resonance structure.
A similar reduction in calculated reactivity was observed for the FLiBe critical experiment model designed
for future execution at NCERC using the Hypatia platform. Although experimental benchmark uncertain-
ties are not yet available for this system, the consistency between the FLiBe trends and the validated HEU-
SOL-THERM-039 benchmark results suggests that the revised 19 F evaluation may improve predictive ca-
pabilities for fluoride-salt systems relevant to advanced reactor technologies.
The TSUNAMI uncertainty propagation analysis demonstrated that uncertainties associated with the 19 F
covariance data contribute measurably to the uncertainty in the predicted benchmark reactivity. The covari-
ance matrices generated using the PUFF module of AMPX provide a first-order estimate of the impact of
fluorine nuclear data uncertainties on integral system behavior. These results indicate that improved res-
onance parameter covariance information for 19 F is important for reducing uncertainties in criticality and
reactor physics calculations involving fluoride-bearing materials.
The present study focused exclusively on modifications to the 19 F evaluation while maintaining all remain-
ing isotopic data from ENDF/B-VIII.1. Consequently, the results isolate the impact of fluorine nuclear data
but do not account for potential correlations with uncertainties in other isotopes present in the benchmark
systems. In addition, validation was limited to a relatively small set of fluorine-sensitive benchmark con-
figurations.
Despite the improvements, the calculated keff values remain approximately 1000 pcm above the benchmark
reference value, suggesting that additional sources of bias remain. One potential contributor is the thermal
scattering representation for hydrogen and fluorine in hydrogen fluoride, for which experimental validation
data remain limited. To address this need, future work will include thermal neutron scattering measure-
ments at the Spallation Neutron Source (SNS) to provide experimental constraints on the HF thermal scat-
tering law. These measurements will support the development and validation of improved TSL evaluations
and help determine whether deficiencies in the current bound-atom scattering treatment contribute to the
remaining benchmark discrepancy. The combination of new differential measurements, improved nuclear
data evaluations, and integral benchmark validation is expected to further reduce uncertainties in the pre-
diction of fluoride-bearing systems relevant to advanced reactor technologies and nuclear criticality safety
applications.

 25

Future work will include the validation of the revised 19 F evaluation against additional integral benchmark
experiments, extension of the covariance analysis to continuous-energy representations, and application
to molten salt reactor systems employing FLiBe and related fluoride salts. The forthcoming Tethys exper-
iment is expected to provide particularly valuable integral data for further assessment of fluorine nuclear
data performance in reactor-relevant environments.
