1.
Introduction and statement of the results

In the series of papers
[
1
,
2
,
3
]
Hardy proved the next two inequalities.

Suppose that
p>1
,
f(x)\geq 0
; that
f
is integrable over any finite interval
(0,x)
and
f^{p}
is integrable over
(0,\infty)
. Then the next inequality

(1.1)

\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{x}\int_{0}^{x}f(t)dt\right)^{p}\,dx\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}\,\int_{0}^{\infty}f^{p}(x)dx.

holds.
This is the original Hardy’s integral inequality.

The discrete version of Hardy’s inequality reads as

(1.2)

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{p}\leq\left(\frac{p}{p-1}\right)^{p}\,\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}{a_{k}^{p}},\ \ \ \ a_{k}\geq 0,\quad k\in\mathbb{N}.

Initially, in his 1920 paper
[
2
]
Hardy derived (
1.2
) with the
larger constant
p^{2}/(p-1)
which appeared on the right-hand side of (
1.2
). Later, Landau
in the letter
[
4
]
dated 1921, published later in
[
5
]
,
was the first to establish ((
1.2
)) with the exact constant
(p/(p-1))^{p}
in the sense that
there is no smaller one for which (
1.2
) holds for every sequence of non-negative numbers
a_{k}
.
In the same letter Landau pointed out that equality in (
1.2
) occurs only for the trivial sequence, that is,
when
a_{k}=0
for every
k\in\mathbb{N}
.

The lack of nontrivial extremal sequence motivates one to consider the“finite version” of (
1.2
)

(1.3)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{p}\leq d_{n}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{a_{k}^{p}},\ \ \ \ a_{k}\geq 0,\quad k=1,2,...,n.

The natural questions are: what are the best constant
d_{n}
and the corresponding extremal sequence.

The behaviour of the constant
d_{n}
in (
1.3
) was studied extensively - see, for instance,
[
6
]
,
[
7
]
,
[
8
]
,
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
.
In
[
8
]
Herbert S. Wilf established the exact rate of convergence of
the constant
d_{n}

d_{n}=4-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{\ln^{2}n}+O\left(\frac{\ln\ln n}{\ln^{3}n}\right).

In
[
11
]
F. Stampach gave slightly better estimation, i.e.

d_{n}=4-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{\ln^{2}n}+\frac{32\pi^{2}(\gamma+6\log 2)}{\log^{3}n}+O\left(\frac{1}{\ln^{4}n}\right).

In
[
12
]
we also studied the asymptotic behaviour of the constant
d_{n}
. It was proved there that
d_{n}
can be expressed in terms of the smallest zero of a continuous dual Hahn polynomial of degree
n
(see
[
13
]
), for a specific choice of the parameters, in terms of which these polynomials are defined. Despite that nice interpretation of
d_{n}
, it was only proved in
[
12
, Theorem 1.1]
that the next inequalities are true for every natural
n\geq 3

(1.4)

4\Bigg(1-\frac{4}{\ln n+4}\Bigg)\leq d_{n}\leq 4\Bigg(1-\frac{8}{(\ln n+4)^{2}}\Bigg).

In all proofs of the above mentioned estimations for the constant
d_{n}
, the authors substantially used the special properties of the space
l_{2}
.
In
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
we applied a different approach
which allowed us to give a simpler proof of some of
the mentioned estimations and to find an almost extremal sequence. We proved the next theorem.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

a_{k}=\int_{k}^{k+1}h(x)dx,

where

(1.5)

h(x)=x^{-1/2}\left(2\alpha\cos(\alpha\log x)+\sin(\alpha\log x)\right),\quad 1\leq x\leq n+1,

and
\alpha
is the only solution of the equation

\tan(\alpha\log(n+1))+2\alpha=0\ \ \mathrm{in\ the\ interval}\ \ \left(\frac{\pi}{2\log(n+1)},\frac{\pi}{\log(n+1)}\right).

Then

(1.6)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{2}\geq\frac{4}{1+4\alpha^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{a_{k}^{2}}.

Since

\frac{4}{1+4\alpha^{2}}\geq 4-16\alpha^{2}>4-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{\log^{2}(n+1)}

we obtain

(1.7)

d_{n}\geq 4-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{\log^{2}(n+1)}.

Combining the above mentioned results with the latter we obtain very sharp estimates for
d_{n}
for every fixed
n
:

Theorem 1.2
.

The inequalities

(1.8)

4-\frac{16\pi^{2}}{[\log(n+1)]^{2}}\leq d_{n}\leq 4-\frac{32}{[\log n+4]^{2}}

hold for every natural
n\geq 3
.

In
[
14
]
we also established the exact rate of convergence of
d_{n}
and full asymptotic expansion of
d_{n}
in terms of the negative powers of
\log n
.

Theorem 1.3
.

(1.9)

d_{n}\sim 4-\frac{16\,\pi^{2}}{4\,\pi^{2}+(\gamma+6\log 2+\log n)^{2}}\ \ \mathrm{as}\ \ n\rightarrow\infty,

where
\gamma
is the Euler constant, and the following full asymptotic expansion of
d(n,0)
in terms of the negative powers of
\log n
holds: For every fixed
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
m\geq 2
,

(1.10)

d_{n}=4-\sum_{k=2}^{m}c_{k}\ \frac{1}{[\log n]^{k}}\,+\,O((\log n)^{-m-1}),\ \ \ n\to\infty,

with

(1.11)

c_{k}=16\pi^{2}\left(4\pi^{2}+(\gamma+6\log 2)^{2}\right)^{(k-2)/2}\ U_{k-2}\left(-\frac{\gamma+6\log 2}{\sqrt{4\pi^{2}+(\gamma+6\log 2)^{2}}}\right),

where
U_{k-2}
denotes the Chebyshev polynomial of the second kind of degree
k-2
.

The weighted generalised version of (
1.3
) reads as

(1.12)

\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big|\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big|^{q}u_{k}\right)^{1/q}\leq d(n,p,q)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{n}|a_{k}|^{p}\,v_{k}\right)^{1/p}

where
u_{k}
,
v_{k}
are given weight sequences with positive components.

There are many papers investigating different aspects and applications of Hardy’s inequality (
1.12
) ;
see for instance
[
16
]
,
[
17
]
and the bibliography of
[
18
]
. In most of them
the authors are trying to determine the conditions on the parameters
p
,
q
and on the weight sequences
u_{k}
,
v_{k}
under
which the Hardy inequality (
1.12
) holds for some classes of sequences.

In the present paper we investigate a different aspect of the Hardy’s inequality (
1.12
). In the special case when
p=q=2
,
v_{k}=\epsilon
,
u_{k}=k^{\epsilon-2}
,
0\leq\epsilon<1
, i.e. the inequality (
1.12
) reads as

(1.13)

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{2}k^{\epsilon}\leq d(n,\epsilon)\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{a_{k}^{2}}\,k^{\epsilon},\quad

The questions we are trying to answer are: what is
the smallest possible constant
d(n,\epsilon)
for which (
1.13
) holds and what is the extremal sequence for (
1.13
).

In this paper we establish for
0\leq\epsilon<1
the exact rate of convergence of
d(n,\epsilon)
and find an almost extremal sequence. Our main result is the next theorem.

Theorem 1.4
.

Let
0\leq\epsilon<1
. Then the inequality (
1.13
) holds, i.e.

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{2}k^{\epsilon}\leq d(n,\epsilon)\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{a_{k}^{2}}\,k^{\epsilon}

where

(1.14)

\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}\leq d(n,\epsilon)\leq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}\left(8+(1-\epsilon)^{2}\right)}\frac{1}{\ln^{2}(n+1)}

and
\alpha
is the only solution of the equation

\tan(\alpha\ln(n+1))+\frac{2\alpha}{1-\epsilon}=0\ \ \mathrm{in\ the\ interval}\ \ \left(\frac{\pi}{2\ln(n+1)},\frac{\pi}{\ln(n+1)}\right).

Also, the sequence

(1.15)

a_{k}=\int_{k}^{k+1}h_{\epsilon}(x)dx,

where

(1.16)

h_{\epsilon}(x)=x^{-(1+\epsilon)/2}\left(\frac{2\alpha}{1-\epsilon}\cos(\alpha\ln x)+\sin(\alpha\ln x)\right)

is the ”‘almost extremal”’ sequence, i.e. the next inequality holds

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\Big(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\Big)^{2}k^{\epsilon}\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}\,\sum_{k=1}^{n}{a_{k}^{2}}k^{\epsilon}.

Remark 1
.

The constant
\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}\left(8+(1-\epsilon)^{2}\right)}
in (
1.14
)
is by no means the best one. It could be significantly improved in a lot of ways but that would have made the proof longer and much more complicated. Our goal was to keep the proof as simple as possible.

Corollary 1.5
.

d(n,\epsilon)\sim\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{c}{\ln^{2}(n+1)},

i.e. there exist constants
c_{1}=c_{1}(\epsilon)>0
and
c_{2}=c_{2}(\epsilon)>0
such that

\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{c_{1}}{\ln^{2}n}\leq d(n,\epsilon)\leq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{c_{2}}{\ln^{2}n}.

Indeed, it follows from

\displaystyle\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\frac{1}{1+\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}}\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\left(1-\frac{4\alpha^{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}}-\frac{16\pi}{(1-\epsilon)^{4}\ln^{2}(n+1)}.

For the function
h_{\epsilon}(x)
defined in (
1.16
), we have

0<h_{\epsilon}(x)<x^{-(1+\epsilon)/2}\alpha\left(\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}+|\ln x|\right)<\frac{\pi}{\ln(n+1)}x^{-(1+\epsilon)/2}\left(\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}+|\ln x|\right)

and consequently

a_{k}\leq\frac{\pi}{\ln(n+1)}k^{-(1+\epsilon)/2}\left(\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}+\ln(k+1)\right),\quad 1\leq k\leq n.

Then it is obvious, as Landau pointed in his letter to Hardy, that if we let
n\rightarrow\infty
the almost extremal sequence
a_{k},\,k=1,2,...
defined in the Theorem
1.4
goes to the zero sequence, i.e.
to the sequence
a_{k}=0
for all
k
.

2.
Proof of Theorem
1.4

Cauchy’s inequality yields

\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{2}\right)\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{\mu_{i}^{2}}\right)

for every pair of sequences
a_{i}
and
\mu_{i}
, such that
\mu_{i}\neq 0
for every
i=1,2,...,n
.
By multiplying both sides of the latter inequality by
k^{-2+\epsilon}
, summing from 1 to
n
and
changing the order of summation we get

\displaystyle\sum_{k=1}^{n}k^{-2+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}a_{i}\right)^{2}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left[k^{-2+\epsilon}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{2}\right)\right]\left[\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{a_{i}^{2}}{\mu_{i}^{2}}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}a_{i}^{2}i^{\epsilon}

where

M_{i}=i^{-\epsilon}\mu_{i}^{-2}M_{i}^{*},\quad M_{i}^{*}=\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-2+\epsilon}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{2}.

Obviously,

d(n,\epsilon)\leq\max_{1\leq i\leq n}M_{i}.

Let us denote for brevity
(1-\epsilon)/2=\beta,\,\,0<\beta\leq 1/2
. Then

M_{i}=i^{2\beta-1}\mu_{i}^{-2}M_{i}^{*},\quad M_{i}^{*}=\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-(2\beta+1)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}\mu_{j}^{2}.

Now we minimize

\max_{1\leq i\leq n}M_{i}

over the sequences
\mu_{i}\neq 0,\,\,i=1,...,n
, i.e. find

\min_{\mu_{i}\neq 0}\,\max_{1\leq i\leq n}M_{i}.

Let

\mu_{i}=\left(i^{\beta-1}-\frac{1}{A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\int_{i}^{i+1}\frac{\ln^{2}x}{x^{1-\beta}}dx\right)^{1/2}

where
A=A(\epsilon)
is a constant which does not depend on
n
or
\{a_{k}\}_{1}^{n}
and will be chosen later.

There exists
\eta_{i}\in[i,i+1]
such that

(2.1)

i^{1-2\beta}\mu_{i}^{2}=i^{-\beta}-\frac{i^{1-2\beta}}{A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\frac{\ln^{2}\eta_{i}}{\eta_{i}^{1-\beta}}\geq i^{-\beta}-\frac{\ln^{2}\eta_{i}}{A\ln^{2}(n+1)\eta_{i}^{\beta}}.

\displaystyle M_{i}^{*}=\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-(1+2\beta)}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{\beta-1}-\frac{1}{A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\int_{1}^{k+1}\frac{\ln^{2}x}{x^{1-\beta}}dx\right)=M_{1}-M_{2}.

\displaystyle M_{1}=\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-(1+2\beta)}\sum_{j=1}^{k}j^{\beta-1}\leq\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-(1+2\beta)}\left(1+\int_{1}^{k}x^{\beta-1}\right)=\frac{1}{\beta}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\frac{k^{\beta}+\beta-1}{k^{1+2\beta}}

For
x\geq 1
the function
g(x)=\frac{x^{\beta}+\beta-1}{x^{1+2\beta}}
is decreasing and consequently

\displaystyle\beta M_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{n}\frac{k^{\beta}+\beta-1}{k^{1+2\beta}}\leq g(i)+\int_{i}^{n}g(x)dx

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{i^{1+\beta}}+\frac{1}{\beta i^{\beta}}+(\beta-1)\left(\frac{1}{i^{1+2\beta}}+\frac{1}{2\beta i^{2\beta}}\right)-\frac{1}{\beta}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}-\frac{1-\beta}{2n^{2\beta}}\right).

But

\displaystyle\frac{1}{i^{1+\beta}}+(\beta-1)\left(\frac{1}{i^{1+2\beta}}+\frac{1}{2\beta i^{2\beta}}\right)=\frac{1}{i^{1+2\beta}}\left(i^{\beta}+\beta-1+\frac{\beta-1}{2\beta}i\right)\leq 0

because the function
g_{1}(x)=x^{\beta}+\beta-1+\frac{\beta-1}{2\beta}x
is decreasing for
x\geq 1
.
Then

(2.2)

M_{1}\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}i^{\beta}}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{n^{\beta}}-\frac{1-\beta}{2n^{2\beta}}\right).

Remark 2
.

It is easy to see that for
\mu_{i}=i^{(\beta-1)/2}
we obtain (
1.13
) with

d(n,\epsilon)=\beta^{-2}=\left(\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\right)^{2}
.

\displaystyle A\ln^{2}(n+1)M_{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{-(1+2\beta)}\int_{1}^{k+1}\frac{\ln^{2}x}{x^{1-\beta}}dx

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}(k+1)-2\beta^{-1}\ln(k+1)+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{k^{1+2\beta}}.

The function
x^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}x-2\beta^{-1}\ln x+2\beta^{-2}\right)
is increasing, so

\displaystyle\frac{(k+1)^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}(k+1)-2\beta^{-1}\ln(k+1)+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{k^{1+2\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{x^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}x-2\beta^{-1}\ln x+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{x^{1+2\beta}}

and

\displaystyle A\ln^{2}(n+1)M_{2}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{-1}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\int_{k}^{k+1}\frac{x^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}x-2\beta^{-1}\ln x+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{x^{1+2\beta}}

\displaystyle=\beta^{-1}\int_{i}^{n+1}\frac{x^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}x-2\beta^{-1}\ln x+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{x^{1+2\beta}}

\displaystyle\geq\beta^{-1}\int_{\eta_{i}}^{n+1}\frac{x^{\beta}\left(\ln^{2}x-2\beta^{-1}\ln x+2\beta^{-2}\right)-2\beta^{-2}}{x^{1+2\beta}}

(2.3)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln^{2}\eta_{i}+2\beta^{-2}-\beta^{-2}\eta_{i}^{-\beta}}{\beta^{2}\eta_{i}^{\beta}}-\frac{\ln^{2}(n+1)+2\beta^{-2}-\beta^{-2}(n+1)^{-\beta}}{\beta^{2}(n+1)^{\beta}}.

Then from (
2.2
) and (
2
) we obtain

\displaystyle M_{i}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{i^{\beta}}-\frac{\ln^{2}\eta_{i}}{A\eta_{i}^{\beta}\ln^{2}(n+1)}\right)-\frac{1}{\beta^{4}\eta_{i}^{\beta}A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\left(2-\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{\beta}}\right)-F(n)

where

F(n)=\frac{1}{\beta^{2}n^{\beta}}\left(1-\frac{1-\beta}{2n^{\beta}}\right)-\frac{1}{A\beta^{2}(n+1)^{\beta}}\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta^{2}\ln^{2}(n+1)}-\frac{1}{\beta^{2}(n+1)^{\beta}\ln^{2}(n+1)}\right).

By taking
A=A(\beta)
big enough we can make
F(n)\geq 0
.
Indeed, for
n\geq 3
we have

\displaystyle F(n)\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}n^{\beta}}\left[1-\frac{1-\beta}{2n^{\beta}}-\frac{1}{A}\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta^{2}\ln^{2}(n+1)}\right)\right]\geq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}n^{\beta}}\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{A}\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\right)\right)

so, it is enough to take
A=2\left(1+\frac{2}{\beta^{2}}\right)
.

Then

M_{i}^{*}\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{i^{\beta}}-\frac{\ln^{2}\eta_{i}}{A\eta_{i}^{\beta}\ln^{2}(n+1)}\right)-\frac{1}{\beta^{4}\eta_{i}^{\beta}A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\left(2-\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{\beta}}\right)

and from (
2.1
) we obtain

\displaystyle M_{i}\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}-\frac{i^{\beta}}{\beta^{4}\eta_{i}^{\beta}A\ln^{2}(n+1)}\left(2-\frac{1}{\eta_{i}^{\beta}}\right)\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}\beta^{2}(2+\beta^{2})\ln^{2}(n+1)}

since
\eta_{i}/i\in[1,2]
. Consequently,

d(n,\epsilon)\leq\frac{1}{\beta^{2}}-\frac{c(\beta)}{\ln^{2}(n+1)}=\left(\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\right)^{2}-\frac{c(\epsilon)}{\ln^{2}(n+1)}

where

c(\epsilon)=\frac{4\sqrt{2}}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}\left(8+(1-\epsilon)^{2}\right)}.

In order to prove the left inequlity in (
1.14
) we write the left-hand side of (
1.13
) in the following way

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\right)^{2}k^{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}\left(i^{-\epsilon}a_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\left(k^{\epsilon-2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\right)\right)a_{i}^{2}i^{\epsilon}=\sum_{i=1}^{n}M_{i}a_{i}^{2}i^{\epsilon}

where

M_{i}=i^{-\epsilon}a_{i}^{-1}\sum_{k=i}^{n}\left(k^{\epsilon-2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\right).

Obviously

\sum_{k=1}^{n}\left(\frac{1}{k}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\right)^{2}k^{\epsilon}\geq\left(\min_{1\leq i\leq n}M_{i}\right)\sum_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{2}k^{\epsilon}.

Since the function
h_{\epsilon}(x)
is continuous there exists a point
\eta_{i}\in[i,i+1]
such that

a_{i}=h_{\epsilon}(\eta_{i})
and consequently

(2.4)

i^{\epsilon}a_{i}\leq\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}h_{\epsilon}(\eta_{i})

For the sequence
a_{k}
, defined in (
1.15
) we have

\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}=\int_{1}^{k+1}h_{\epsilon}(x)\,dx=\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}(k+1)^{(1-\epsilon)/2}\sin(\alpha\ln(k+1)).

The function
x^{(1-\epsilon)/2}\sin(\alpha\ln x)
is increasing so

\displaystyle\sum_{k=i}^{n}\left(k^{\epsilon-2}\sum_{j=1}^{k}a_{j}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\sum_{k=i}^{n}k^{\epsilon-2}(k+1)^{(1-\epsilon)/2}\sin(\alpha\ln(k+1))

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\int_{i}^{n+1}x^{(\epsilon-3)/2}\sin(\alpha\ln x)\,dx

\displaystyle\geq\frac{2}{1-\epsilon}\int_{\eta_{i}}^{n+1}x^{(\epsilon-3)/2}\sin(\alpha\ln x)\,dx=\frac{4\eta_{i}^{\epsilon}h_{\epsilon}(\eta_{i})}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}.

From this and (
2.4
) we obtain

M_{i}\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}},\quad i=1,...,n,\quad\mbox{i.e.}\quad\min_{1\leq i\leq n}M_{i}\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}

and consequently

d(n,\epsilon)\geq\frac{4}{(1-\epsilon)^{2}+4\alpha^{2}}.

The proof of Theorem
1.4
is complete.
