Introduction

For a given graph
F
, a graph
G
is said to be
F
-free
if it does not contain
F
as a subgraph.
A classical problem in extremal combinatorics is to determine the maximum number of edges in an
F
-free graph on
n
vertices.
The maximum number of edges is called the
Turán number
for
F
and is denoted by
\ex(n,F)
.
Let
K_{p}
denote the complete graph on
p
vertices.
The study of Turán-type problems dates back to the early 20th century, when Mantel
[
14
]
determined the Turán number of
K_{3}
.
Turán
[
18
]
generalized this result by determining
\ex(n,K_{p})
for all
n
and
p
.
Later, Erdős, Stone and Simonovits
[
7
,
8
]
proved that
\ex(n,F)
is asymptotically determined by the chromatic number
\chi(F)
of
F
, when
\chi(F)\geq 3
. Specifically they proved

\ex(n,F)=\frac{1}{2}\left(1-\frac{1}{\chi(F)-1}\right)n^{2}+o(n^{2}).

(1)

However (
1
) only shows that
\ex(n,F)=o(n^{2})
when
F
is bipartite.
In general it is difficult to determine the exact value, or even the order of magnitude, of the Turán number of a bipartite graph.
Indeed, there are relatively few bipartite graphs for which the order of magnitude of the Turán number is known.
Two fundamental classes of bipartite graphs that have been extensively studied are even cycles and complete bipartite graphs.
For the even cycle
C_{2k}
, Bondy and Simonovits
[
2
]
gave an upper bound
\ex(n,C_{2k})=O(n^{1+\frac{1}{k}})
.
For the complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
, a well-known result of Kővári, Rényi and Sós
[
13
]
showed that
\ex(n,K_{s,t})\leq\frac{(t-1)^{\frac{1}{s}}}{2}(1+o(1))n^{2-\frac{1}{s}}
for
s\leq t
when
n
is sufficiently large.
We refer readers who are interested in the Turán number of bipartite graphs to the survey of Füredi and Simonovits
[
10
]
.

Turán-type problems for the Cartesian product of two bipartite graphs, particularly two trees, have attracted much attention.
Let
P_{t}
be the path with
t
vertices.
Bradač, Janzer, Sudakov and Tomon
[
3
]
prove that there exist positive real numbers
c=c(t)
and

such that

cn^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\ex(n,P_{t}\mathbin{\square}P_{t})\leq Cn^{\frac{3}{2}}.

They also prove a more general version, that is, there exist positive real numbers
c=c(T,t)
and
C=C(T,t)
such that

cn^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\ex(n,P_{t}\mathbin{\square}T)\leq Cn^{\frac{3}{2}}

for every tree

and every positive integer

.

In particular, Gao, Janzer, Liu and Xu
[
11
]
improve the upper bound of
\ex(n,P_{t}\mathbin{\square}P_{t})
by proving that for any given
t
and sufficiently large

\ex(n,P_{t}\mathbin{\square}P_{t})\leq 5t^{\frac{3}{2}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

In addition, they considered the Cartesian product of an even cycle and an edge, and show that

\ex(n,K_{2}\mathbin{\square}C_{2l})=\Theta(n^{\frac{3}{2}})

for any integer
l\geq 4
.

In this paper we mainly focus on the graphs
B_{t}\coloneq K_{2}\mathbin{\square}S_{t}
, equivalently
t
four-cycles sharing a common edge (see Figure

). The order of magnitude of the extremal number is

by
[

]
, so we focus on the coefficient of

.
Note that when
t=1
, we have
B_{1}=C_{4}
.
The upper bound for
\ex(n,C_{4})
is given by Kővári-Sós-Turán theorem
[
13
]
.
Combining the lower bound constructions given independently by Erdős, R’enyi and S’os
[
6
]
and Brown
[

, we have

\ex(n,C_{4})=\frac{1}{2}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

We establish bounds for general
t
, and in the case of
B_{2}
we obtain more precise results. We also give an improvement for the bipartite Turán number of
K_{2}\mathbin{\square}T
. Our main results are the following.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
t\geq 1
and sufficiently large
n
, we have

\ex(n,B_{t})\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

and

\ex(n,B_{t})\geq\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{\left\lfloor(t+1)/2\right\rfloor}}{2}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}},&\text{ if $t$ is odd, }\\
\frac{2s(s+1)(\sqrt{4s^{2}+5s+1}-2s-1)}{(\sqrt{4s^{2}+5s+1}-s-1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}},&\text{ if $t=2s$. }\end{cases}

By calculation, the above result implies that

\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{\ex(n,B_{t})}{\sqrt{t}}\leq\frac{1}{2}.

Next, we will give a better upper bound in the case of
B_{2}
.
Theorem
1.1
gives the bound

\frac{4(\sqrt{10}-3)}{(\sqrt{10}-2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\ex(n,B_{2})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Where
\frac{4(\sqrt{10}-3)}{(\sqrt{10}-2)^{\frac{3}{2}}}\approx 0.518
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\approx 0.707
.
In the following result, we further improve the coefficient of the upper bound of

from
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\approx 0.707
to
\frac{2}{\sqrt{11}}\approx 0.603
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For sufficiently large
n
, we have

\ex(n,B_{2})\leq\frac{2}{\sqrt{11}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Let
F
be a bipartite graph.
The bipartite Turán number of
F
, denoted by
\ex_{bip}(n,F)
, is the maximum number of edges in an
n
-vertex
F
-free bipartite graph.
The function
\ex_{bip}(n,F)
has been extensively studied, see
[

,

]
for recent progress.
For the bipartite Turán number of
C_{4}
, the upper bound is established by Reiman
[
16
]
, while the lower bound is given by the incidence graph of a projective plane.
Thus, we have

\ex_{bip}(n,C_{4})=\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

For the bipartite case, we get a more general result.

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
T
be a tree with
t
vertices. For sufficiently large
n
, we have

\ex_{bip}(n,K_{2}\mathbin{\square}T)\leq\frac{\sqrt{t-1}}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Specializing to the tree
T=K_{1,t}
we obtain an upper bound

\ex_{bip}(n,B_{t})\leq\frac{\sqrt{t}}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

The following result provides a lower bound for
\ex_{bip}(n,B_{t})
.

Theorem 1.4
.

For any
t\geq 1
and sufficiently large
n
, we have

\ex_{bip}(n,B_{t})\geq\begin{cases}\frac{\sqrt{\left\lfloor(t+1)/2\right\rfloor}}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}},\text{ if $t$ is odd,}\\
\frac{1}{4}\frac{t(t+2)}{(t+1)^{\frac{3}{2}}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}},\text{ if $t$ is even}.\end{cases}

The above results implies that

\frac{1}{4}\leq\lim_{t\to\infty}\frac{ex_{bip}(n,B_{t})}{\sqrt{t}}\leq\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2}}.

When
t=2
, by Theorem
1.3
and
1.4
, we have

\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\ex_{bip}(n,B_{2})\leq\frac{1}{2}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Where
\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}\approx 0.385
.
In the following result, we further improve the upper bound of
\ex_{bip}(n,B_{2})
from
\frac{1}{2}
to
0.468
.

Theorem 1.5
.

For sufficiently large
n
, we have

\frac{2}{3\sqrt{3}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}\leq\ex_{bip}(n,B_{2})\leq 0.468(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

2.1
Notation

For a graph

, we use
V(G)
and

to denote the vertex set and edge set of

, respectively.
We use

and

to denote the number of vertices and edges of

, respectively.
For a subset

, we use

(or

If

, we write

and denote

.
We use

to denote the codegree of

.
When

,

is the degree of

.
For two sets of vertices

and

, let

be the number of edges with one endpoint in

and the other endpoint in
B
.
We say a vertex set

is

if

.
For a graph
G
, denote by
N(C_{4},G)
the number of copies of
C_{4}
(denoted by

) for which there exists a good set
S
of size
t
such that
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq S
or
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\subseteq S
.
For bipartite graph

with parts
V_{1}
and

, denote by

the number of copies of
C_{4}
(denoted by

) for which there exists a good set

of size

such that

.
Let

immediately before deleting
xy
. Then among the
d-1
neighbors of
y
in
N(y)\setminus\{x\}
, at least
(d-1)-(t-1)=d-t
vertices
z
satisfy
d(\{x,z\})\leq t-1
. For each Type 2 deletion of an edge
xy
we record all of the triples
(x,z,y)
where
d(\{x,z\})\leq t-1
. Let
\mathcal{S}
be the set of these triples.

Observe that at each Type 2 deletion of an edge
xy
, the codegree of each pair
x
and
z
is reduced by exactly one. The initial codegree of a pair
x
and
z
(the codegree at the first moment a pair
\{x,z\}
is recorded) considered is at most
t-1
, thus the total number of times
x
and
z
can occur together in a triple from
\mathcal{S}
(so either as
(x,z,y)
or
(z,x,y)
) is at most
t-1
. Thus,

|\mathcal{S}|\leq(t-1)\binom{|X|}{2}.

(10)

We will now obtain a lower bound on
|\mathcal{S}|
. Since
G^{\prime}
is empty and vertices
y
are only deleted when their degree is at most
t-1
, it must happen that the degree of each vertex
y
of degree at least
t
is reduced from
d_{G}(y)
to
t-1
through Type 2 deletions of edges incident to
y
. We have seen that when
y
has degree
d
such a step adds at least
d-t
new triples to
\mathcal{S}
. Summing, we obtain that the number of triples
(x,z,y)
involving
y
with
d_{G}(y)\geq t
is at least

(d_{G}(y)-t)+(d_{G}(y)-t-1)+\cdots+0\;=\;\binom{d_{G}(y)-t+1}{2},

and
0
if
d_{G}(y)\leq t-1
. Thus,

|\mathcal{S}|\geq\sum_{y\in Y}\binom{\max\{d_{G}(y)-t+1,0\}}{2}.

(11)

Now set
a_{y}\coloneqq\max\{d_{G}(y)-t+1,0\}
.
Then
\binom{a_{y}}{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}(a_{y}^{2}-a_{y})
, so (
11
) implies

|\mathcal{S}|\;\geq\;\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in Y}a_{y}^{2}\;-\;\frac{1}{2}\sum_{y\in Y}a_{y}.

(12)

Clearly
\sum_{y}a_{y}\leq\sum_{y}d_{G}(y)=m
. On the other hand,

\sum_{y}a_{y}\;\geq\;\sum_{y}d_{G}(y)-(t-1)|Y|\;=\;m-(t-1)|Y|,

and also
\sum_{y}a_{y}\geq 0
. By the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality

\sum_{y\in Y}a_{y}^{2}\;\geq\;\frac{(\sum_{y\in Y}a_{y})^{2}}{|Y|}\;\geq\;\frac{(\max\{m-(t-1)|Y|,0\})^{2}}{|Y|}.

If
m\leq(t-1)|Y|
, then
m=O(n)
. So we assume
m>(t-1)|Y|
. Plugging the above estimates into (
12
) we get

|\mathcal{S}|\;\geq\;\frac{\bigl(m-(t-1)|Y|\bigr)^{2}}{2|Y|}\;-\;\frac{m}{2}.

(13)

Combining (
10
) and (
13
) yields

\frac{(m-(t-1)|Y|)^{2}}{2|Y|}\;-\;\frac{m}{2}\;\leq\;(t-1)\binom{|X|}{2}.

Using the well-known result that forbidding a bipartite graph implies
m=o(n^{2})
(or the stronger estimate
m=O(n^{\frac{3}{2}})
from
[
3
]
) and the fact that
|X|
and
|Y|
are at most
n
, we obtain

m^{2}\;\leq\;(t-1)|Y||X|^{2}+o(n^{3}).

By our assumption that
|X|\leq|Y|
, it follows that
|Y||X|^{2}\leq\frac{n^{3}}{8}
and thus

m\;\leq\;\frac{\sqrt{t-1}}{2\sqrt{2}}n^{\frac{3}{2}}+o(n^{\frac{3}{2}}).\qed

With a simple modification of the embedding procedure from Lemma
4.1
we can obtain the following stronger lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
F
be a bipartite graph with parts
A
and
B
constructed in the following way. Beginning with an edge, apply any sequence of operations of the following type until
|B|=t
:

(Operation) Take any edge
ab
with
a\in A
and
b\in B
created so far, and add two new vertices
c\in A
,
d\in B
and the edges
bc
,
cd
and
ad
. Then add any number
r\geq 0
vertices
f\in B
and the edges
af
,
fc
.

Let
G
be a bipartite graph with parts
X
and
Y
and at least one edge. Assume that for every edge
xy\in E(G)
with
x\in X
and
y\in Y
the vertex
y
has at least
t
neighbors
z
with
d(\{x,z\})\geq t
. Then
G
contains
F
as a subgraph.

From Lemma
4.2
we obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
F
be a bipartite graph constructed as in Lemma
4.2
. Then

\ex_{bip}(n,F)\leq\frac{\sqrt{t-1}}{2\sqrt{2}}(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}.

Proof
.

The proof is exactly the same as the proof of Theorem
4.1
, using Lemma
4.2
in place of Lemma
4.1
.
∎

The graphs
K_{2}\mathbin{\square}T

Proof of Theorem

In this section, we will give the proof of Theorem

.
Recall that the statement of Theorem

\ex_{bip}(n,B_{2})\leq 0.468(1+o(1))n^{\frac{3}{2}}

In fact, for any given

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
G
be a
B_{2}
-free bipartite graph on
n
vertices.
If
\delta(G)\geq cn^{\frac{1}{2}}
for a constant
c
, then for every

we have

e(G)\leq\max\left\{\sqrt{\frac{4}{27}+2\epsilon},\sqrt{\frac{1}{4}-4c^{2}\epsilon}\right\}n^{\frac{3}{2}}+o(n^{\frac{3}{2}}).

Proof
.

Let
G
be a
B_{2}
-free bipartite graph with parts
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
, where
|V_{1}|=n_{1}
,
|V_{2}|=n_{2}
and
n=n_{1}+n_{2}
.
Without loss of generality, assume that
|V_{1}|\leq|V_{2}|
.
Choose an edge
e=v_{1}v_{2}
where
v_{i}\in V_{i}
for

.
Let
\mathcal{C}_{i}
be the collection of
C_{4}
(denoted by

) such that
C\cap V_{i}
is a good set.
Then
N_{i}(C_{4},G)=|\mathcal{C}_{i}|
.
The number of copies of
C_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
containing

is at most
d(v_{i})
.
Then for all edges
e
containing
v_{i}
, we sum up all the
C_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
containing
e
, and have
\sum_{u\in N(v_{i})}d(v_{i})=d(v_{i})^{2}
.
Summing over all vertices in
V_{i}
, each
C_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}_{i}
is counted four times. Thus, we have

4N_{i}(C_{4},G)\leq\sum_{v\in V_{i}}d(v)^{2}.

Now we improve the estimate of the upper bound for
N_{i}(C_{4},G)
.
Notice that if there exists a good set
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}
in
V_{1}
, then for every
v\in N(u_{1})\cap N(u_{2})
, and every
w\in V_{2}\setminus\{v\}
with
\{v,w\}
good,
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\cap(N(v)\cap N(w))=\emptyset
.
Otherwise, there exists a
B_{2}
in
G
.
As a result, there is no
C_{4}
in
\mathcal{C}_{2}
containing either
\{v,u_{1}\}
or
\{v,u_{2}\}
.
Thus there are
2\sum_{v\in N(u_{1})\cap N(u_{2})}d(v)\geq 2cn^{\frac{1}{2}}d(u_{1},u_{2})

in
\mathcal{C}_{2}
.
A fixed edge
vu

with
v\in\{u,u^{\prime}\}
.
Otherwise, if
vu
is in two good sets
\{u,u^{\prime}\}

.
Hence, we have

4N_{2}(C_{4},G)\leq\sum_{v\in V_{2}}d(v)^{2}-2cn^{\frac{1}{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\subseteq V_{1},\\
\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\text{ is good}\end{subarray}}d(u_{1},u_{2}).

(14)

If

It implies that

Then we have

As a result,

Then, by Equation (

Proof of Theorem

.
