On zero-sum Ramsey numbers of complete bipartite graphs
Cheng Chi, Jialin He

Abstract
For an integer q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 and a graph F F satisfying q | e ⁡ ( F ) q\mid e(F) , the zero-sum Ramsey number R ⁡ ( F , ℤ q ) R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{q}) is the least integer n n such that every edge-labeling w : E ⁡ ( K n ) → ℤ q w\colon E(K_{n})\to\mathbb{Z}_{q} contains a copy of F F whose edge-label sum is zero in ℤ q \mathbb{Z}_{q} . Write K s , t K_{s,t} for the complete bipartite graph with s s vertices on one side and t t vertices on the other side. We prove that for every integer q ≥ 2 q\geq 2 , there is an explicit threshold S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) such that R ⁡ ( K s , q ​ k , ℤ q ) = s + q ​ k R(K_{s,qk},\mathbb{Z}_{q})=s+qk for all s ≥ S ⁡ ( q ) s\geq S(q) and all k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 . We also determine the zero-sum Ramsey number of K s , 3 ​ k K_{s,3k} over ℤ 3 \mathbb{Z}_{3} completely. We show that R ⁡ ( K s , 3 ​ k , ℤ 3 ) = s + 3 ​ k R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=s+3k , except when s = 2 s=2 and k ≥ 1 k\geq 1 , or when s ∈ { 3 , 4 , 5 , 7 } s\in\{3,4,5,7\} and k = 1 k=1 . In these exceptional cases, R ⁡ ( K s , 3 ​ k , ℤ 3 ) = s + 3 ​ k + 1 R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=s+3k+1 . In particular, this implies that the threshold S ⁡ ( q ) S(q) is best possible for q = 3 q=3 .

1
Introduction

Zero-sum Ramsey theory is a group-labelled analogue of classical Ramsey theory.
Instead of seeking a monochromatic copy of a prescribed graph, one labels the edges of a complete graph by elements of a finite abelian group and asks for a copy whose total edge-label is the identity.
This area was initiated by Bialostocki and Dierker
[
3
,
4
]
, motivated in part by the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem
[
19
]
and the Cauchy–Davenport theorem
[
14
,
18
]
; see Caro’s survey
[
10
]
for background.

Throughout the paper we work with the cyclic group
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
, where
q\geq 2
.
For a graph
G
, the
zero-sum Ramsey number

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{q})
is the least integer
n
such that every edge-labeling
w\colon E(K_{n})\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
contains a copy
G^{\prime}
of
G
with
\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}w(e)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
The divisibility condition
q\mid e(G)
is necessary, since the constant labeling
w(e)=1
gives a nonzero sum on every copy of
G
.
Conversely, if
q\mid e(G)
, then
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{q})
is finite by the ordinary multicolor Ramsey theorem
[
25
]
, because any monochromatic copy of
G
is zero-sum.
A related bipartite-host version was studied earlier by Caro
[
8
]
. In that setting, one colors the edges of a complete bipartite host graph and asks for a zero-sum copy of a prescribed bipartite graph. This differs from the present parameter, where the host graph is complete and the target graph is
K_{s,t}
.

Determining exact values of
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{q})
is difficult beyond a few special cases.
For
q=2
, Caro gave a complete
characterization of
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{2})
for every graph
G
with an even number of edges
[
9
]
.
For
q\geq 3
, no comparable general theory is known.
Even for complete graphs, substantial work of Alon and Caro
[
1
]
, Caro
[
6
,
11
]
, and Harborth and Piepmeyer
[
20
,
21
]
leaves some cases open, including residue classes over
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
; see
[
12
]
.
Other exact results are known for stars and matchings
[
4
,
7
]
.
Trees and forests have also received considerable attention.
Caro and Roditty posed a conjecture concerning the zero-sum Ramsey number of trees
[
13
]
.
More recently, Caro and Mifsud initiated a systematic study over
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, proving sharp
n+O(1)
-type bounds for forests
[
12
]
.
Subsequently, Alvarado, Colucci and Parente determined the exact
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-Ramsey numbers of forests without isolated vertices
[
2
]
.

For graphs containing cycles, much less is known.
Cycles are the most basic family in this direction.
Related zero-sum cycle-existence results have been obtained for group-labelled complete digraphs
[
24
]
.
For ordinary cycles of prescribed length, several ranges of zero-sum Ramsey numbers are known, but the general picture remains incomplete.
For example, Caro
[
6
,
10
]
conjectured that
R(C_{q},\mathbb{Z}_{q})=4q-3
for every odd
q\geq 5
, a problem that remains open in general.
See
[
15
,
22
]
for recent progress on cycles and wheels.

Recent general results show that zero-sum Ramsey numbers are often close to the vertex-count lower bound for sparse or locally sparse graphs. Katz, Lian, Malekshahian and Shapiro proved linear bounds for bounded-degree graphs over finite abelian groups
[
23
]
; for prime moduli, sharper
n+O_{p}(1)
-type bounds have been obtained for forests
[
17
]
, degenerate graphs under suitable hypotheses
[
26
]
, and graphs with large
2
-packings
[
22
]
. Complete bipartite graphs
K_{s,t}
, however, fall outside these frameworks when both parts grow: they have unbounded maximum degree and degeneracy, diameter two, and no large
2
-packing. Their natural lower bound is simply

R(K_{s,t},\mathbb{Z}_{q})\geq s+t
.

Our first contribution is to show that for every integer
q\geq 2
, this trivial lower bound is sharp for complete bipartite graphs, once
s
is sufficiently large as a function of
q
.
For an integer
q\geq 2
, let
g=\gcd(q,2)
,
d=q/g
and
M(q)=R(K_{q},\mathbb{Z}_{d})
.
When
d=1
, we regard
\mathbb{Z}_{1}
as the trivial group and set
R(K_{m},\mathbb{Z}_{1})=m
.
Let
S(q)=gM(q)-q-g+1
.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
q\geq 2
be an integer.
For every
k\geq 1
and every
s\geq S(q)
, we have

R(K_{s,qk},\mathbb{Z}_{q})=s+qk.

Theorem
1.1
may be compared with the following
elementary upper bound due to Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem
[
19
]
. For all integers
q\geq 2
,
s\geq 1
, and
k\geq 1
,

R(K_{s,qk},\mathbb{Z}_{q})\leq s+qk+q-1.

Indeed, let
N=s+qk+q-1
, and let

w\colon E(K_{N})\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be an arbitrary edge-labeling. Fix an
s
-set

A=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{s}\}\subseteq V(K_{N})
, and let

C=V(K_{N})\setminus A
. Then
|C|=qk+q-1
. For each
v\in C
, define

a_{v}=\sum_{u\in A}w(uv)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

By the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem, the sequence

(a_{v})_{v\in C}
contains a subsequence indexed by a set

B\subseteq C
with
|B|=qk
and
\sum_{v\in B}a_{v}=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Hence,
\sum_{v\in B}\sum_{u\in A}w(uv)=0
in
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
.
Thus the complete bipartite subgraph with parts
A
and
B
is a zero-sum copy of
K_{s,qk}
. This gives a general upper bound which is only
q-1

above the vertex-count lower bound
s+qk
. Theorem
1.1

shows that, once
s\geq S(q)
, these extra
q-1
vertices are unnecessary.

Particularly, we study the case
q=3
.
Our second contribution is a complete determination of
R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})
, including all exceptional small cases.
The proof uses a lifting method different from the one used to prove
Theorem

1.1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

For all integers
s\geq 2
and
k\geq 1
,

R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=\begin{cases}s+3k+1,&s=2,\ k\geq 1,\ \text{or}\ s\in\{3,4,5,7\},\ k=1,\\
s+3k,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

For
q=3
, the EGZ bound above gives

R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})\leq s+3k+2
. Hence
Theorem
1.2
improves this general bound by two vertices in all non-exceptional cases and by one vertex in the exceptional cases.
Moreover, the threshold
S(q)
in Theorem
1.1
is best possible for
q=3
.
Indeed, Lemma
2.1
gives
M(3)=R(K_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=11
, and hence
S(3)=8
.
Moreover,
Theorem

1.2
gives
R(K_{7,3},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=11>10=7+3
, while
R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=s+3k
for all
s\geq 8
and
k\geq 1
.
Thus the condition
s\geq S(3)
cannot be improved.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows.

Section

2
contains the notation and some general auxiliary lemmas.

Section

3
proves
Theorem

1.1
.
The proof of
Theorem

1.2
begins in
Section

4
and continues with the proofs of the base lemmas in
Section

5
.

2
Definitions and lemmas

Throughout the paper, all edge-label sums are taken in the relevant cyclic group.
For a subgraph
H
of a labeled complete graph, write
w(H)=\sum_{e\in E(H)}w(e)
.
The subgraph
H
is
zero-sum
if
w(H)=0
.
If
A
and
B
are disjoint vertex sets, define

w(A,B)=\sum_{a\in A}\sum_{b\in B}w(ab).

Thus a copy of
K_{s,m}
with sides
A
and
B
is zero-sum if
|A|=s
,
|B|=m
, and
w(A,B)=0
.

Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
with edge-labeling
w
.
For a vertex
x\in V
, define its
weighted degree
by
\tilde{d}_{w}(x)\coloneqq\sum_{y\neq x}w(xy)
.
For
X\subseteq V
, define

\tilde{e}_{w}(X)\coloneqq\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq X}w(xy)\text{ and }\partial_{w}(X)\coloneqq w(X,V\setminus X).

When the context is clear, we write
\tilde{d}(x)
,
\tilde{e}(X)
and
\partial(X)
.

For the proof of
Theorem

1.2
, we shall use the following result on edge-colored complete graphs proved by Chung and Graham
[
16
]
.

Lemma 2.1

(Chung and Graham, Theorem 1 in
[
16
]
)
.

We have
R(K_{3},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=11
. Equivalently, every edge-coloring of
K_{11}
with three colors contains a triangle which is not exactly two-colored, while there exists a three-coloring of
K_{10}
in which every triangle is exactly two-colored.

Next, we state a lifting lemma showing that
R(K_{s+t,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})
is controlled by
R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})+t
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
s\geq 1
,
k\geq 1
, and
t\geq 0
. If
3\nmid s
, then
R(K_{s+t,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})\leq R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})+t
.

Proof
.

Set
n=R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})+t
. Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
with
|V|=n
, and let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}
be an arbitrary edge-labeling. Choose a
t
-set
Z\subseteq V
and let
X=V\setminus Z
.
For
x\in X
, define
h(x)=\sum_{z\in Z}w(zx)
.
Define an auxiliary edge-labeling
w^{\prime}
on the pairs from
X
by

w^{\prime}(xy)=w(xy)+s(h(x)+h(y)).

Since
|X|=R(K_{s,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})
, there are disjoint sets
A,B\subseteq X
with
|A|=s
,
|B|=3k
, and
w^{\prime}(A,B)=0
. Expanding this equality gives

0=w(A,B)+s|B|\sum_{a\in A}h(a)+s|A|\sum_{b\in B}h(b).

(2.1)

Now
|B|=3k=0
and
s|A|=s^{2}=1
in
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, since
3\nmid s
, so (
2.1
) becomes
0=w(A,B)+\sum_{b\in B}h(b)
. Since
\sum_{b\in B}h(b)=w(Z,B)
, we get
w(A\cup Z,B)=0
. Thus
K
contains a zero-sum
K_{s+t,3k}
, which implies that
R(K_{s+t,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})\leq n
, proving the desired result.
∎

3
Proof of Theorem
1.1

The first step is an elementary cut-sum identity.
It measures how the cut sum changes when a set of new vertices is inserted into one side of a cut.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
, and let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be an edge-labeling.
Let
U\subseteq V
, write
X=V\setminus U
, and for each
x\in X
let

b_{x}=\left(\sum_{y\in X\setminus\{x\}}w(xy)-\sum_{u\in U}w(ux)\right)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

Then, for every
Q\subseteq X
,

\partial(U\cup Q)-\partial(U)=\left(\sum_{x\in Q}b_{x}-2\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}w(xy)\right)\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(3.1)

Proof
.

By the definition of the cut sum,

\displaystyle\partial(U\cup Q)-\partial(U)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in Q\\
y\in X\setminus Q\end{subarray}}w(xy)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U\\
x\in Q\end{subarray}}w(ux).

Also,

\displaystyle\sum_{x\in Q}b_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in Q\\
y\in X\setminus Q\end{subarray}}w(xy)+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x,y\in Q\\
x\neq y\end{subarray}}w(xy)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U\\
x\in Q\end{subarray}}w(ux)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in Q\\
y\in X\setminus Q\end{subarray}}w(xy)+2\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}w(xy)-\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}u\in U\\
x\in Q\end{subarray}}w(ux).

Subtracting the internal-edge term gives (
3.1
).
∎

Using this proposition, we prove the following lemma.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
q\geq 2
, let
g=\gcd(q,2)
,
d=q/g
, and set
M(q)=R(K_{q},\mathbb{Z}_{d})
.
Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
, and let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be an edge-labeling.
Suppose that
U\subseteq V
, set
X=V\setminus U
, and assume that
\partial(U)=0
.
If
|X|\geq gM(q)-g+1
, then there exists a subset
Q\subseteq X
such that
|Q|=q
and
\partial(U\cup Q)=0
.

Proof
.

The goal is to choose
q
vertices
Q\subseteq X
so that the cut increment in (
3.1
) vanishes.
We first find a subset on which the linear terms
b_{x}
lie in a common residue class modulo
g
, and then apply the definition of
M(q)
to an auxiliary
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
-edge-labeling.

For each
x\in X
, let

b_{x}=\sum_{y\in X\setminus\{x\}}w(xy)-\sum_{u\in U}w(ux)\in\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

By Proposition
3.1
, it suffices to find
Q\subseteq X
with
|Q|=q
such that

\sum_{x\in Q}b_{x}-2\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}w(xy)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

Partition
X
according to the residue of
b_{x}
modulo
g
.
There are
g
residue classes.
Since
|X|\geq gM(q)-g+1=g(M(q)-1)+1
, one of these classes contains at least
M(q)
vertices.
Thus there are a residue
\epsilon\in\mathbb{Z}_{g}
and a subset
Y\subseteq X
with
|Y|\geq M(q)
such that
b_{x}\equiv\epsilon\pmod{g}
for every
x\in Y
.
We also write
\epsilon
for its representative in
\{0,\ldots,g-1\}
.
For each
x\in Y
, there is a unique
a_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
such that

b_{x}=\epsilon+ga_{x}\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(3.2)

Here
ga_{x}
denotes the image of
a_{x}\in\mathbb{Z}_{d}
under the natural homomorphism
\mathbb{Z}_{d}\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
,
a\mapsto ga
.

Let
\overline{w}(xy)
denote the reduction of
w(xy)
modulo
d
, and let
c=2/g
.
Thus
c=2
when
q
is odd and
c=1
when
q
is even.
We define an auxiliary edge-labeling
\psi
on the pairs from
Y
as follows.
If
d=1
, let
\psi\equiv 0
.
If
d>1
, then
q\equiv 0\pmod{d}
, so
q-1\equiv-1\pmod{d}
and
q-1
is invertible in
\mathbb{Z}_{d}
; define

\psi(xy)=-c\,\overline{w}(xy)+(q-1)^{-1}(a_{x}+a_{y})\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{d}.

For every
Q\subseteq Y
with
|Q|=q
, we have

\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\psi(xy)=\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}-c\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\overline{w}(xy)\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{d}.

(3.3)

Indeed, for
d=1
, (
3.3
) is trivial since
\mathbb{Z}_{1}
contains the identity element only.
For
d>1
, fix
x\in Q
. In the sum

\sum_{\{u,v\}\subseteq Q}(a_{u}+a_{v})
, the term
a_{x}
appears once for each edge of
K_{Q}
incident with
x
. Since
|Q|=q
, there are
q-1
such edges.
Hence

\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}(a_{x}+a_{y})=(q-1)\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}.

This implies

\displaystyle\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\psi(xy)

\displaystyle=-c\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\overline{w}(xy)+(q-1)^{-1}\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}(a_{x}+a_{y})

\displaystyle=-c\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\overline{w}(xy)+(q-1)^{-1}(q-1)\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}-c\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\overline{w}(xy).

Since
|Y|\geq M(q)=R(K_{q},\mathbb{Z}_{d})
, the edge-labeling
\psi
contains a zero-sum copy of
K_{q}
.
Thus there exists
Q\subseteq Y
such that
|Q|=q
and

\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\psi(xy)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{d}.

(3.4)

For this
Q
, combining (
3.3
) and (
3.4
) gives

\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}-c\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}\overline{w}(xy)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{d}.

Multiplying by
g
and using
gc=2
, we obtain

g\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}-2\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}w(xy)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(3.5)

The replacement of
\overline{w}(xy)
by
w(xy)
in (
3.5
) is valid because
\overline{w}(xy)
is the reduction of
w(xy)
modulo
d
, and
q=gd
divides
2d
.

By (
3.2
) and
|Q|=q
,

\sum_{x\in Q}b_{x}=q\epsilon+g\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}=g\sum_{x\in Q}a_{x}\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

(3.6)

Equations (
3.5
) and (
3.6
) give

\sum_{x\in Q}b_{x}-2\sum_{\{x,y\}\subseteq Q}w(xy)=0\text{ in }\mathbb{Z}_{q}.

Therefore (
3.1
) yields
\partial(U\cup Q)-\partial(U)=0
.
Since
\partial(U)=0
, we have
\partial(U\cup Q)=0
, as required.
∎

Now we are ready to prove
Theorem

1.1
.

The lower bound
R(K_{s,qk},\mathbb{Z}_{q})\geq s+qk
holds trivially since a complete graph on
s+qk-1
vertices cannot contain a copy of
K_{s,qk}
.
It remains to prove the upper bound.
Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
with
|V|=s+qk
, and let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{q}
be an arbitrary edge-labeling.

We construct nested sets
U_{0}\subset U_{1}\subset\cdots\subset U_{k}\subseteq V
such that, for every
0\leq i\leq k
,
|U_{i}|=iq
and
\partial(U_{i})=0
.
Start with
U_{0}=\emptyset
.
Suppose that
U_{i}
has been constructed for some
0\leq i<k
, and write
X_{i}=V\setminus U_{i}
.
By the choice of
s
,

|X_{i}|=s+q(k-i)\geq s+q\geq gM(q)-g+1.

Hence Lemma
3.2
gives a set
Q_{i}\subseteq X_{i}
with
|Q_{i}|=q
and
\partial(U_{i}\cup Q_{i})=0
.
Let
U_{i+1}=U_{i}\cup Q_{i}
.
Then
|U_{i+1}|=(i+1)q
and
\partial(U_{i+1})=0
, so the induction continues.

After
k
steps,
|U_{k}|=qk
and
\partial(U_{k})=0
.
Let
B=U_{k}
and
A=V\setminus U_{k}
.
Then
|B|=qk
,
|A|=s
, and

w(A,B)=\partial(B)=\partial(U_{k})=0.

The complete bipartite subgraph with parts
A
and
B
is therefore a zero-sum copy of
K_{s,qk}
.
Since
w
was arbitrary, every
\mathbb{Z}_{q}
-edge-labeling of
K_{s+qk}
contains a zero-sum
K_{s,qk}
. This proves the desired upper bound and completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
. ∎

4
Proof of Theorem
1.2

In this section, we first isolate three base results and then use them to prove Theorem
1.2
. The proofs of these results are deferred to
Section

5
.
In Subsection
4.1
, we establish the lower bounds for the exceptional
K_{s,3}
cases by constructing explicit edge-labelings.
The proof of Theorem
1.2
is given in Subsection
4.2
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For every
k\geq 1
,
R(K_{2,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=3k+3
.

Lemma 4.2
.

R(K_{3,6},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=9
.

Lemma 4.3
.

R(K_{4,6},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=10
.

4.1
Lower-bound constructions for the exceptional
K_{s,3}
values

In this subsection, for
s\in\{3,4,5,7\}
, we show that
R(K_{s,3},\mathbb{Z}_{3})\geq s+4
by constructing an edge-labeling of
K_{s+3}
without a zero-sum

5.1

We first prove the lower bound. Let

to every edge of

R(K_{2,3k},\mathbb{Z}_{3})\geq 3k+3

We now prove the upper bound. Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
with
|V|=3k+3
, and let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}
be an arbitrary edge-labeling. For distinct vertices
x,y,z\in V
, define

p(x,y;z)=\sum_{u\in V\setminus\{x,y,z\}}\bigl(w(xu)+w(yu)\bigr).

This is the weight of the copy of
K_{2,3k}
with two-vertex side
\{x,y\}
and whose
3k
-vertex side is
V\setminus\{x,y,z\}

(5.1)

For
v\in V
, recall the weighted degree
\tilde{d}(v)=\sum_{u\neq v}w(vu)
. Adding a fixed constant

to every edge does not change the weight of any copy of

has
6k

. Thus we may normalize the edge-labeling so that, for a fixed vertex

Let

Since

B\coloneqq\sum_{y\in R}b_{y}=\sum_{y\in R}r_{y}

We next translate the assumption (
5.1

and

For distinct

(5.2)

The two forbidden values differ by

. Therefore, if

is the third element of

, namely

, then the two forbidden values coincide. Define

\delta_{yz}=0\text{ if }r_{y}\neq r_{z}\text{ and }\delta_{yz}\in\{1,2\}\text{ if }r_{y}=r_{z}.

(5.3)

Indeed, in the case

Now fix

. Using

, the degree equation at

Since

, this simplifies to

\sum_{z\in R\setminus\{y\}}\delta_{yz}=r_{y}+B.

(5.4)

The condition

gives

\delta_{yz}-b_{y}-b_{z}+r_{y}+r_{z}\neq 0.

(5.5)

Finally, for distinct

, the condition

gives

\delta_{yz}+b_{y}+b_{z}-b_{t}-\delta_{yt}-\delta_{zt}\neq 0.

(5.6)

Thus any counterexample to the upper bound yields the abstract structure described in the following lemma.

Proof of Lemma
4.2

We first prove a counting lemma which will also be used in the next subsection.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
K
be a complete graph on vertex set
V
, let
w\colon E(K)\to\mathbb{Z}_{3}
be an edge-labeling, and let
B,Q\subseteq V
be disjoint. Let
L=V\setminus(B\cup Q)
. If
|L|\geq 7
, then for every
\rho\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
,

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq L,\ |T|\equiv\rho,\\
\partial(B\cup T)=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|T|}=0\ \text{in}\ \mathbb{Z}_{3}.

(5.14)

Proof
.

Write

, and encode a subset

by a vector

For

For a
0
-
1

, the factor

\chi_{u}(x)+\chi_{v}(x)-2\chi_{u}(x)\chi_{v}(x)

is
1
exactly when precisely one of

is contained in

, and is
0
otherwise. Hence

F_{2}(x)=\partial(B\cup T)

on
0
-
1

\deg F_{1}\leq 1
and
\deg F_{2}\leq 2
.

For
a\in\mathbb{Z}_{3}
, the element
1-a^{2}
is equal to
1
if
a=0
, and is equal to
0
otherwise. Hence

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},\\
F_{1}(x)=F_{2}(x)=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\ell}}=\sum_{x\in\{0,1\}^{\ell}}(-1)^{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\ell}}\bigl(1-F_{1}(x)^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-F_{2}(x)^{2}\bigr).

The polynomial

\bigl(1-F_{1}^{2}\bigr)\bigl(1-F_{2}^{2}\bigr)

has degree at most
2\deg F_{1}+2\deg F_{2}\leq 2(1+2)=6<\ell
. It therefore suffices to check the signed sum of a monomial of degree less than
\ell

. Summing first over

gives

Thus every monomial contributes

. Therefore, we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in\{0,1\}^{\ell},\\
F_{1}(x)=F_{2}(x)=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{x_{1}+\cdots+x_{\ell}}=0.

Translating back from

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}T\subseteq L,\ |T|\equiv\rho,\\
\partial(B\cup T)=0\end{subarray}}(-1)^{|T|}=0,

∎

K_{10}
contains a zero-sum copy of
K_{4,6}
. Hence

R(K_{4,6},\mathbb{Z}_{3})=10.
