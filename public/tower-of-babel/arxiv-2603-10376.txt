Abstract
. In [ 5 ] , the authors introduced multiple Eisenstein series of arbitrary rank in positive characteristic and the q q -shuffle algebra ℰ \mathcal{E} associated with them. In the present paper, we establish a class of linear independence results for multiple Eisenstein series. We also prove that the q q -shuffle algebra ℛ \mathcal{R} of multiple zeta values embeds into the inverse limit of the spaces of multiple Eisenstein series with respect to the rank r r , and that ℰ \mathcal{E} is isomorphic to the tensor square of ℛ \mathcal{R} . As an application, we show that ℰ \mathcal{E} is an associative algebra, thereby verifying the conjecture proposed in [ 5 ] .

1.1.
The

Fix a prime

\mathbb{F}_{p}
be the prime field of order

\mathbb{F}_{q}
be a finite extension of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
containing
q

Let
A\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\mathbb{F}_{q}[\theta]
denote the polynomial ring in the variable

We denote by

\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
the completion of a fixed algebraic closure of

.
We also let

be the set of all monic polynomials in

.

Let
\mathbb{N}
be the set of positive integers. For
r\in\mathbb{N}
, let

denote the Drinfeld symmetric space of rank
r
, defined as the set of points

whose coordinates are

-linearly independent.
As established in
[
8
, Proposition 6.1]
,
\Omega^{r}
carries a rigid analytic structure.
Under the normalization
z_{r}=1
, we view
\Omega^{r}
as the subset of
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}
given by

\Omega^{r}=\{(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r})\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}^{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}z_{1},\dots,z_{r},\text{ are }K_{\infty}\text{-linearly independent and }z_{r}=1\}.

We denote by
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
the algebra of rigid analytic functions on
\Omega^{r}
.

We now recall the partial order on
A

[
5

, which generalizes the definition provided in
[
7
]
for rank two case.
For
\bm{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})\in A^{r}
and
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
, we put

\langle\bm{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=1}^{r}f_{i}z_{i}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
r\geq 1
,
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1},z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
and
\bm{f},\bm{g}\in A^{r}
.
Write
\bm{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{r})
and
\bm{g}=(g_{1},\ldots,g_{r})
.
We define a partial order
\succ
on

as follows:

(i)

\langle\bm{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle\succ 0
if
f_{1}=\cdots=f_{i-1}=0
and

for some

.

(ii)

For
\langle\bm{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle,\langle\bm{g},\mathbf{z}\rangle\succ 0
, we write
\langle\bm{f},\mathbf{z}\rangle\succ\langle\bm{g},\mathbf{z}\rangle
if one of the following holds:

(a)

There exists
1\leq i\leq r
such that

f_{1}=\cdots=f_{i-1}=g_{1}=\cdots=g_{i-1}=0
and

f_{i},g_{i}\in A_{+}
with
\deg f_{i}>\deg g_{i}
.

(b)

There exists
1\leq i\leq r
such that

f_{1}=\cdots=f_{i-1}=g_{1}=\cdots=g_{i-1}=0
and

f_{i}\in A_{+},g_{i}=0
.

For convenience, let

\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\bigcup_{m=1}^{\infty}\mathbb{N}^{m}\cup\{\varnothing\}

denote the set of all indices. For a non-empty index
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}
, we define its
depth
and
weight
by

\operatorname{dep}(\underline{a})=m\quad\text{and}\quad\operatorname{wt}(\underline{a})=a_{1}+\cdots+a_{m},

respectively.
For the empty index
\varnothing
, we put
\operatorname{dep}(\varnothing)=0=\operatorname{wt}(\varnothing)
.
For each
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we further denote the subset of indices whose weight is at most
w
by

\mathcal{I}_{\leq w}=\{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{wt}(\underline{a})\leq w\}.

We now recall the rank
r
multiple Eisenstein series introduced in
[
5
, Definition 1.4]
, which serve as a generalization of the single and double Eisenstein series on
\Omega^{2}
defined by Chen
[
7
]
. It is also worth noting that Pellarin
[
15
]
previously proposed vector-valued versions of multiple Eisenstein series in the rank two case.

Definition 1.2

(Multiple Eisenstein series)
.

Let
r\geq 1
.
For any non-empty index
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}
, we define the
rank
r
multiple Eisenstein series
on
\Omega^{r}
by

E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\bm{f}_{1},\ldots,\bm{f}_{m}\in A^{r}\\
\langle\bm{f}_{1},\mathbf{z}\rangle\succ\cdots\succ\langle\bm{f}_{m},\mathbf{z}\rangle\succ 0\end{subarray}}\frac{1}{\langle\bm{f}_{1},\mathbf{z}\rangle^{a_{1}}\cdots\langle\bm{f}_{m},\mathbf{z}\rangle^{a_{m}}}.

By convention, we put
E_{r}(\varnothing;\mathbf{z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
.

It is shown in
[
5
, Proposition 2.1]
that the rank
r
multiple Eisenstein series are rigid analytic functions on

, i.e.,
E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
.
In addition, we remark that when

, they become Thakur’s multiple zeta values
[
17
]
.

It is well-known from
[
19
]
that the product of Thakur’s multiple zeta values can be written as an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear combination of multiple zeta values of the same weight, commonly known as the
q
-shuffle relation
.
The explicit formula is given by the
q
-shuffle algebra
\mathcal{R}
, whose algebra structure was first observed by Yamamoto based on an explicit
q
-shuffle formula of Chen
[
6
, Theorem 3.1]
, and was subsequently formalized by Shi in her PhD thesis
[
16
]
. This
q
-shuffle algebra
\mathcal{R}
is defined as follows:

Definition 1.3
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be the free monoid generated by the set
\{x_{k}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}k\in\mathbb{N}\}
and let
\mathcal{R}
be the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space generated by
\mathcal{S}
. For a non-empty index
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}
, we define
x_{\underline{a}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{a_{1}}\cdots x_{a_{m}}
, and for
\underline{a}=\varnothing
, we define
x_{\varnothing}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1
.
An element
x_{\underline{a}}
is called a
word of depth
m
.
When
m\geq 2
, we put
x_{\underline{a}^{-}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{a_{2}}\cdots x_{a_{m}}
, and when
m=1
, we put
x_{\underline{a}^{-}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{\varnothing}=1
.
We define the
q
-shuffle product

\ast
on
\mathcal{R}
inductively on the sum of depths as follows:

(i)

For the empty word
x_{\varnothing}=1
and any
\mathfrak{a}\in\mathcal{R}
, define

1*\mathfrak{a}=\mathfrak{a}*1=\mathfrak{a}.

(ii)

For non-empty indices
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}
and
\underline{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{n})\in\mathcal{I}
, define

x_{\underline{a}}*x_{\underline{b}}=x_{a_{1}}(x_{\underline{a}^{-}}*x_{\underline{b}})+x_{b_{1}}(x_{\underline{a}}*x_{\underline{b}^{-}})+x_{a_{1}+b_{1}}(x_{\underline{a}^{-}}*x_{\underline{b}^{-}})\\
+\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i+j=a_{1}+b_{1}\\
q-1\mid j\end{subarray}}\Delta^{i,j}_{a_{1},b_{1}}x_{i}((x_{\underline{a}^{-}}*x_{\underline{b}^{-}})*x_{j})

where

(a)

\Delta^{i,j}_{a,b}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(-1)^{a-1}\binom{j-1}{a-1}+(-1)^{b-1}\binom{j-1}{b-1}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
.

(b)

x\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}x_{i}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i}\epsilon_{i}(xx_{i})
for
\epsilon_{i}\in\mathbb{F}_{p}
and
x,x_{i}\in\mathcal{S}
.

(iii)

Expand the product
\ast
to the
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-vector space
\mathcal{R}
by the distributive law.

In this way,
\mathcal{R}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra with respect to the
q
-shuffle product
\ast
.
We mention that throughout this paper, algebras are not necessarily assumed to be associative.

In
[
5
]
, we showed that the multiple Eisenstein series satisfy the same
q
-shuffle relations as Thakur’s multiple zeta values do, thereby extending Shi’s result
[
16
]
for the rank one case to arbitrary rank. More precisely, we have the following theorem:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
5
, Theorem 1.6]
)
.

For
r\geq 1
, we define
\widehat{E}_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
to be the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map satisfying

\widehat{E}_{r}(1)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=1\quad\text{and}\quad\widehat{E}_{r}(x_{\underline{a}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}).

Then
\widehat{E}_{r}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism, i.e.,

\widehat{E}_{r}(x_{\underline{a}}\ast x_{\underline{b}})=E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})E_{r}(\underline{b};\mathbf{z}).

\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}

x_{\underline{a}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=x_{a_{1}}\cdots x_{a_{m}}

We define the

(i)

For the empty word

(ii)

Remark 1.14
.

Shi
[
16
, Conjecture 3.2.11]
proposed a conjectural Hopf algebra structure on

, and a proof of Shi’s conjecture was announced by Im–Kim–Le–Ngo Dac–Pham

We note that Theorem

[

In the classical setting, multiple Eisenstein series were first introduced by Gangl, Kaneko, and Zagier
[
9
]
in the depth two case, and later generalized to arbitrary depth by Bachmann
[
3
]
.
Subsequently, Bachmann and Tasaka
[
2
]
showed
that multiple Eisenstein series satisfy various linear relations arising from shuffle regularization, known as the
restricted double shuffle relations
.
This result was further generalized in the recent work of Bachmann and Kanno
[
1
]
.

In contrast, in §
2.3
we establish a class of linear independence results for multiple Eisenstein series in positive characteristic, which lead to the main theorems mentioned above.
Our key idea is to consider multiple Eisenstein series of varying ranks simultaneously, for instance through the
q
-shuffle algebra
\mathcal{E}
and the inverse limit
\varprojlim\limits_{r\in\mathbb{N}}\mathcal{Z}^{(r)}
.
There seems to be no similar notion of such consideration in the existing classical literature.

2.
t
-expansion of multiple Eisenstein series and applications

In this section, we first recall the definitions of Goss polynomials, multiple Goss sums, and Goss expansions.
We then compute a lower bound for the vanishing order of the
t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}
-expansion of multiple Goss sums.
The main result of this section is to establish a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-linear independence result of multiple Eisenstein series.

2.1.
Multiple Goss sums and Goss expansions

Fix a positive integer
r
.
Given any
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1},z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
, we let
\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}
be the
A
-lattice spanned by the coordinates of
\mathbf{z}
.
That is,

\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=Az_{1}+\cdots+Az_{r-1}+Az_{r}\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}.

We define

\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=X\prod_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}\setminus\{0\}}\left(1-\frac{X}{\lambda}\right)

as the associated exponential function.
Following
[
4
, Proposition 15.3]
, we can write

\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{z})X^{q^{n}},

where each
\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{z})
is a rigid analytic function on
\Omega^{r}
, i.e.,
\alpha_{n}(\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
.
We further put

t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\frac{1}{\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)}=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}\frac{1}{X+\lambda}.

Associated to each lattice in
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
, Goss constructed a family of polynomials known as
Goss polynomials
.

Definition 2.1

(
[
12
]
,
[
10
]
)
.

The
Goss polynomials
are the unique polynomials

G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})[X],\quad n\geq 1,

defined recursively as follows.

(i)

G_{1}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)=X
.

(ii)

For
n>1
,

G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)=X\sum_{i=0}^{\lceil\log_{q}n\rceil-1}\alpha_{i}(\mathbf{z})G_{n-q^{i}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X),

where
\lceil x\rceil\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}n\geq x\}
for
x\in\mathbb{R}
.

Furthermore, Goss polynomials satisfy the following properties (see
[
12
]
and also
[
10
]
).

Proposition 2.2
.

For each positive integer
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have the following properties.

(i)

G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X))=\sum_{\lambda\in\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}1/(X+\lambda)^{n}
.

(ii)

For
1\leq n\leq q
,
G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)=X^{n}
.

(iii)

G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)
is a monic polynomial of degree
n
.

(iv)

X
divides
G_{n}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)
.

Recall that when
r\geq 2
, we set
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}=(z_{2},\ldots,z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r-1}
for
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
.
As before,
\mathcal{I}
denotes the set of all indices, including the empty index.

Definition 2.3

(
[
5
, Definition 2.4]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
. For any non-empty index
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}

rank

G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in A_{+}\\
\deg f_{1}>\cdots>\deg f_{m}\geq 0\end{subarray}}G^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}_{a_{1}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{1}z_{1}))\cdots G^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}_{a_{m}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{m}z_{1})).

By convention, we put

Recall the multiple Eisenstein series

.
Multiple Goss sums arise naturally in the study of the expansion of

More precisely, we have the following

Proposition 2.4

(
[

, Proposition 2.3]

Let

For any non-empty index

2.2.
t
-expansion of multiple Goss sums and multiple Eisenstein series

For each

\Gamma_{r}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\left\{\left(\begin{array}[]{c|c}1&*\\

Remark 2.5
.

The following examples will be frequently used in the subsequent discussions.

(i)

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r-1})

\displaystyle\;\hookrightarrow\;

\displaystyle\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}

\displaystyle F

\displaystyle\;\mapsto\;

\displaystyle F\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=(\mathbf{z}\mapsto F(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})).

Throughout this paper, we identify

(ii)

For

, the function
H\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathbf{z}\mapsto t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(X)|_{X=fz_{1}}

Since

\displaystyle(\gamma\cdot H)(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle=H(\mathbf{z}\cdot\gamma^{\operatorname{tr}})

\displaystyle=H(z_{1}+a_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+a_{r}z_{r},z_{2},\ldots,z_{r})

\displaystyle=t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}+fa_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+fa_{r}z_{r})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})+\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fa_{2}z_{2}+\cdots+fa_{r}z_{r})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})}=t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})=H(\mathbf{z}).

We next recall the

-expansion of

-invariant rigid analytic functions in the arbitrary rank setting developed in

]

[

]

Proposition 2.6

(
[

, Proposition 5.4]
,
[

, (7.12.1)]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
.
For each
F\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}
, there is a unique sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r-1})

F

F(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}F_{n}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{n},

valid for
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1},1)\in\Omega^{r}
in a neighborhood of infinity.

Definition 2.7

(
[
4
, Definition 5.12]
)
.

Let
r\geq 2
.
For each
F\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}
with the
t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}
-expansion given in Proposition
2.6
, we define

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\inf\{n\in\mathbb{Z}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}F_{n}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\neq 0\}\in\mathbb{Z}\cup\{\pm\infty\}.

We note that for any
F_{1},F_{2}\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}
, we have

(1)

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{1}F_{2})=\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{1})+\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{2})

and

(2)

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{1}+F_{2})\geq\min\{\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{1}),\operatorname{ord}_{r}(F_{2})\}.

To obtain a lower bound for
\operatorname{ord}_{r}(G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))
, we require the theory of Drinfeld
A
-modules, originally introduced in
[
8
]
.
We briefly review the necessary background as follows.

Definition 2.8

(
[
13
, Definition 4.4.2]
)
.

Let
r\geq 1
. A
rank
r
Drinfeld
A
-module

\phi
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-algebra homomorphism

\displaystyle\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}A

\displaystyle\;\rightarrow\;

\displaystyle\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\mathbb{G}_{a}(\mathbb{C}_{\infty}))

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle\;\mapsto\;

\displaystyle\phi_{f}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g_{f,0}\operatorname{id}+g_{f,1}\tau^{1}+\cdots+g_{f,r\deg f}\tau^{r\deg f}

where for each
f\in A
, we require:

(i)

g_{f,i}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
for
0\leq i\leq r\deg f
.

(ii)

g_{f,0}=f
.

(iii)

g_{f,r\deg f}\neq 0
.

Here,
\tau\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}x\mapsto x^{q}
denotes the
q
-th power Frobenius endomorphism on
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-valued points of the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}
.
Furthermore, we let

\phi_{f}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=g_{f,0}X+g_{f,1}X^{q}+\cdots+g_{f,r\deg f}X^{q^{r\deg f}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}[X]

be the associated
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear polynomials.

Now, let
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1},z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
and consider the associated lattice

\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}=Az_{1}+Az_{2}+\cdots+Az_{r-1}+Az_{r}.

It follows from Drinfeld’s uniformization theorem (see
[
13
, § 4]
) that there exists a unique rank
r
Drinfeld
A
-module
\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}
over
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that for all
f\in A
,

\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(fX)=\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}_{f}(\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}(X)).

For each
f\in A
, we write

\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}}}_{f}(X)\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=\sum_{i=0}^{r\deg f}g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z})X^{q^{i}},

and view the coefficient forms
g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z})
as functions on
\Omega^{r}
. Then it is known by
[
4
, Proposition 15.12]
that for each
0\leq i\leq r\deg f
, we have

g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
and
g_{f,r\deg f}(\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\times}
.
Here, we denote the group of units of
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
by
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\times}
.

In the following lemma, we provide a lower bound for
\operatorname{ord}_{r}(G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))
.

Lemma 2.9
.

Let
r\geq 2
.
For any index
\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}
, we have that
G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}
and

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))\geq\frac{q^{(r-1)\operatorname{dep}(\underline{a})}-1}{q^{r-1}-1}.

Proof.

For the empty index, the result is clear.
We may assume
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}
is non-empty.
By Remark
2.5
, the function
\mathbf{z}\mapsto t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})
is
\Gamma_{r}
-invariant on
\Omega^{r}
for each
f\in A_{+}
.
Thus, one sees from Definitions
2.1
and
2.3
that

G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}.

To obtain the desired lower bound for
\operatorname{ord}_{r}(G_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))
, by Definition
2.3
and (
2
), it suffices to show that for all
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in A_{+}
with
\deg f_{1}>\cdots>\deg f_{m}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{1}z_{1}))\cdots G_{a_{m}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{m}z_{1}))\right)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{q^{(r-1)m}-1}{q^{r-1}-1}.

For each
\mathbf{z}=(z_{1},\ldots,z_{r-1},z_{r}=1)\in\Omega^{r}
, we denote by
\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}
the Drinfeld
A
-module corresponding to the lattice
\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}
and write

\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}_{f}(X)=\sum_{i=0}^{(r-1)\deg f}g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})X^{q^{i}}.

We note that by the discussion above,
g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r-1})
for
0\leq i\leq(r-1)\deg f
and
g_{f,(r-1)\deg f}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r-1})^{\times}
.
It follows that for
f\in A_{+}

\displaystyle t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}_{f}(\exp_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1}))}=\frac{1}{\phi^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}_{f}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{-1})}

\displaystyle=\left(g_{f,(r-1)\deg f}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\cdot t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{-q^{(r-1)\deg f}}+\sum_{i=0}^{(r-1)\deg f-1}g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\cdot t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{-q^{i}}\right)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{q^{(r-1)\deg f}}}{g_{f,(r-1)\deg f}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}\cdot\left(1+\sum_{i=0}^{(r-1)\deg f-1}\frac{g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{g_{f,(r-1)\deg f}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}\cdot t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{q^{(r-1)\deg f}-q^{i}}\right)^{-1}.

Since

1+\sum_{i=0}^{(r-1)\deg f-1}\frac{g_{f,i}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}{g_{f,(r-1)\deg f}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})}\cdot X^{q^{(r-1)\deg f}-q^{i}}\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r-1})[\![X]\!]^{\times},

we see that

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}))=q^{(r-1)\deg f}.

By Proposition
2.2
, for each
a\in\mathbb{N}
,
X
divides
G_{a}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(X)
, so we have

(3)

\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(G_{a}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}))\right)\geq q^{(r-1)\deg f}.

By (
1
), for
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}\in A_{+}
with
\deg f_{1}>\cdots>\deg f_{m}\geq 0
, we have

\displaystyle\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{1}z_{1}))\cdots G_{a_{m}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{m}z_{1}))\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(G_{a_{j}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(f_{j}z))\right)

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{j=1}^{m}q^{(r-1)\deg f_{j}}\geq\sum_{j=0}^{m-1}q^{(r-1)j}=\frac{q^{(r-1)m}-1}{q^{r-1}-1},

and hence the proof is completed.
∎

We now recall that
\mathcal{Z}^{(r)}_{L}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
denotes the

-algebra generated by all rank

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subalgebra

given in Definition

.
The following proposition enables us to consider the inverse limit of
\mathcal{Z}^{(r)}_{L}
in §

.

Proposition 2.10
.

For any
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-subalgebra
L\subseteq\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
and
r\geq 1
, there is a unique
L
-algebra homomorphism

\pi_{r+1,L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Z}^{(r+1)}_{L}\to\mathcal{Z}^{(r)}_{L}

such that for all
\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}
,

\pi_{r+1,L}(E_{r+1}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))=E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}).

Proof.

First, we recall from Proposition
2.4
that for each
r\geq 1
and any non-empty index
\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}
, we have the following Goss expansion

E_{r+1}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})=E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})+\sum_{i=1}^{\operatorname{dep}(\underline{a})}E_{r}(\underline{a}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})G_{r+1}(\underline{a}_{(i)};\mathbf{z}).

By Remark
2.5
and Lemma

.
It follows that

E_{r+1}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\in\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r+1})^{\Gamma_{r+1}}

and hence

\mathcal{Z}_{L}^{(r+1)}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r+1})^{\Gamma_{r+1}}.

Now, given
F\in\mathcal{Z}_{L}^{(r+1)}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r+1})^{\Gamma_{r+1}}
, by Proposition
2.6
, there is a unique sequence
\{F_{n}\}_{n\in\mathbb{Z}}
in
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})
such that

admits the

-expansion

F(\mathbf{z})=\sum_{n=-\infty}^{\infty}F_{n}(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1})^{n}.

Define the map
\pi_{r+1,L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Z}^{(r)}_{L}\to\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})

Next, we calculate the constant term of

.
By Lemma

and (

\operatorname{ord}_{r+1}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\operatorname{dep}(\underline{a})}E_{r}(\underline{a}^{(i)};\mathbf{z}^{\prime})G_{r+1}(\underline{a}_{(i)};\mathbf{z})\right)\geq 1,

and hence the constant term of

with respect to the

That is,

\pi_{r+1,L}(E_{r+1}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}))=E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z}).

\pi_{r+1,L}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{Z}_{L}^{(r+1)}\to\mathcal{Z}_{L}^{(r)}

2.3.
Linear independence of multiple Eisenstein series

In this section, we investigate linear independence results of multiple Eisenstein series.
We recall that the set

\mathcal{I}_{\leq w}=\{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{wt}(\underline{a})\leq w\}

consists of all indices of weight at most
w\geq 0
.

In view of the following theorem, any finite set of multiple Eisenstein series is
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-linearly independent, provided that the rank in question is sufficiently large

Theorem 2.11
.

Suppose that
w\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
.
Then for each
r>(w+1)+\log_{q}(w+1)
,

\{E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\leq w}\}\subseteq\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})

is a
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-linearly independent subset of
\mathcal{O}(\Omega^{r})^{\Gamma_{r}}
.

Proof.

We proceed by induction on
w
.
When
w=0
, the only possible index is the empty index.
Then for
r>0+1+\log_{q}(0+1)=1
, we have
E_{r}(\varnothing;\mathbf{z})=1
, which is clearly
\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
-linearly independent.
This proves the base case
w=0
.

Let
w_{0}
be a non-negative integer and assume that the assertion holds for
w=w_{0}
.
Now we let
w=w_{0}+1
and fix any integer
r>(w+1)+\log_{q}(w+1)
.
Assume that there exists
g_{\underline{a}}\in\mathbb{C}_{\infty}
such that

(4)

\sum_{\underline{a}\in\mathcal{I}_{\leq w}}g_{\underline{a}}E_{r}(\underline{a};\mathbf{z})=0.

Let
\underline{a}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{m})\in\mathcal{I}_{\leq w}
be any index of
\operatorname{wt}(\underline{a})\leq w
.
By Lemma
2.9
, for
2\leq i\leq\operatorname{dep}(\underline{a})
,

(5)

\operatorname{ord}_{r}(G_{r}(\underline{a}_{(i)};\mathbf{z}))\geq\frac{q^{(r-1)i}-1}{q^{r-1}-1}\geq\frac{q^{2(r-1)}-1}{q^{r-1}-1}=q^{r-1}+1>q^{r-1},

where
\underline{a}_{(i)}=(a_{1},\ldots,a_{i})
is defined in Definition
1.9
.
On the other hand,

G_{r}(\underline{a}_{(1)};\mathbf{z})=\sum_{f\in A_{+}}G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}))=G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(z_{1}))+\sum_{1\neq f\in A_{+}}G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1})).

By (
3
), for
f\in A_{+}
and
f\neq 1
, we have

\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}))\right)\geq q^{(r-1)\deg f}\geq q^{r-1}.

It follows from (
2
) that

(6)

\operatorname{ord}_{r}\left(\sum_{1\neq f\in A_{+}}G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(t_{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(fz_{1}))\right)\geq q^{r-1}.

By Proposition
2.2
(iii),
G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(X)
is monic and

\deg_{X}G_{a_{1}}^{\Lambda_{\mathbf{z}^{\prime}}}(X)=a_{1}\leq\operatorname{wt}(\underline{a})\leq w<(w+1)q^{w}=q^{w+\log_{q}(w+1)}<q^{r-1},

so we have

(7)

Remark 2.13
.

As a consequence of Proposition
2.12
, we obtain that for
r\geq 2
, the functions

and

are

-linearly independent.

Recall that for

Theorem 3.13
.

Let
(\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
be any Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure on

. There is a unique Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure

on

such that the inclusion map
\iota\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{E}
and
\widehat{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E}
are Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphisms.
Moreover, endow the Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure on
\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}
via the structure induced by the product
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\times_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group schemes.
Then the unique
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-linear map

\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E},\quad x_{\underline{a}}\otimes x_{\underline{b}}\mapsto x_{\underline{a}}*\widehat{e}(x_{\underline{b}})

defined in Theorem

becomes an isomorphism of Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}

In other words, we have

\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\times_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\simeq\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{E}

as
\mathbb{F}_{p}

Proof.

Fix a Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure
(\Delta,\varepsilon,S)
on
(\mathcal{R},\ast)
, we obtain an
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group scheme structure on
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}
.
This induces a natural
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group scheme structure on the product
\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\times_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}
and hence
\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}
is endowed an Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure
(\tilde{\Delta},\tilde{\varepsilon},\tilde{S})
.
Moreover, let
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\times_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}\to\operatorname{Spec}\mathcal{R}
be the first (resp. second) projection.
Then
\operatorname{pr}_{1},\operatorname{pr}_{2}
are both
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-group scheme homomorphisms.
In other words,
i_{1},i_{2}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}
defined by
i_{1}(\mathfrak{a})=\mathfrak{a}\otimes 1
and
i_{2}(\mathfrak{b})=1\otimes\mathfrak{b}
are both Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphisms.

For the existence part, by Theorem
3.9
, the map
\phi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E}
defined by
\phi(x_{\underline{a}}\otimes x_{\underline{b}})=x_{\underline{a}}*\widehat{e}(x_{\underline{b}})

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras, so

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure

(\Delta^{\mathrm{e}},\varepsilon^{\mathrm{e}},S^{\mathrm{e}})\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}=((\phi\otimes\phi)\circ\tilde{\Delta}\circ\phi^{-1},\varepsilon\circ\phi^{-1},\phi\circ\tilde{S}\circ\phi^{-1})

on
(\mathcal{E},\ast)
and
\phi
becomes a Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra isomorphism.
Since
\iota=\phi\circ i_{1}
and
\widehat{e}=\phi\circ i_{2}
,
\iota
and
\widehat{e}
are Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism.

Finally, we prove the uniqueness part.
Assume that
(\Delta^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime},S^{\prime})
is another Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra structure on
(\mathcal{E},\ast)
such that
\iota,\widehat{e}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E}
are both Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphisms.

\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras, there is a unique Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra homomorphism
\psi\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}\mathcal{R}\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{p}}\mathcal{R}\to\mathcal{E}

.

\mathbb{F}_{p}

\iota=\phi\circ i_{1}
and
\widehat{e}=\phi\circ i_{2}
.
By the universal property of the tensor product, it follows that
\psi=\phi
, which is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebras by Theorem

.
Thus,
\psi=\phi
is a Hopf
\mathbb{F}_{p}
-algebra isomorphism.
We obtain

\displaystyle(\Delta^{\prime},\varepsilon^{\prime},S^{\prime})

\displaystyle=((\psi\otimes\psi)\circ\tilde{\Delta}\circ\psi^{-1},\varepsilon\circ\psi^{-1},\psi\circ\tilde{S}\circ\psi^{-1})
