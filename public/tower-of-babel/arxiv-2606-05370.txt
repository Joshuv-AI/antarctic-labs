Novel 𝓝 = 𝟐 \mathcal{N}=2 higher-spin supercurrents
Nikita Zaigraev

Abstract
We study cubic interactions of 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 massless integer-spin gauge supermultiplets in harmonic superspace. We construct the complete class of abelian ( 𝐬 , 𝐬 𝟏 , 𝐬 𝟐 ) (\mathbf{s},\mathbf{s_{1}},\mathbf{s_{2}}) cubic vertices with the minimal number of space-time derivatives. Such vertices exist only for 𝐬 ≥ 𝐬 𝟏 + 𝐬 𝟐 \mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{s_{1}}+\mathbf{s_{2}} and universally take the form of the gauge prepotential coupled to the conserved higher-spin supercurrent. For 𝐬 𝟏 ≠ 𝐬 𝟐 \mathbf{s_{1}}\neq\mathbf{s_{2}} , we find the novel complex principal supercurrent, whose real and imaginary parts generate the parity-invariant and the parity-breaking interactions, respectively. The supercurrents are constructed from gauge-invariant 𝒩 = 2 \mathcal{N}=2 higher-spin Weyl supertensors associated with the spin- 𝐬 𝟏 \mathbf{s_{1}} and spin- 𝐬 𝟐 \mathbf{s_{2}} gauge multiplets. These supertensors are defined in terms of unconstrained higher-spin analytic prepotentials. We also derive the complete set of conserved component higher-spin currents associated with the ( s , s 1 , s 2 ) (s,s_{1},s_{2}) vertices, including both traceless currents and currents with the non-vanishing trace. Four-derivative
(3,2,1)
vertex and its
\mathcal{N}=2
superfield generalization

We start with the example of
the
(3,2,1)
vertex with four derivatives. Such an interaction can be constructed as

S_{(3,2,1)}=\int d^{4}x\;\left(\Phi^{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}\,J_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}+\Phi^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}T_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\right),

(2.1)

where the spin-
3
current and the current trace must be constructed from the gauge-invariant linearized Weyl tensor and the Faraday tensor (electromagnetic field tensor). Moreover, the current and the current trace must be real. The requirement of the invariance under gauge transformations,

\delta\Phi^{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}=\partial^{(\alpha(\dot{\alpha}}\xi^{\alpha(2))\dot{\alpha}(2))},\qquad\delta\Phi^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\partial_{\beta\dot{\beta}}\xi^{(\alpha\beta)(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})},

(2.2)

leads to the current conservation equation:

\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}J_{(\alpha(2)\beta)(\dot{\alpha}(2)\dot{\beta})}+\partial_{(\alpha(\dot{\alpha}}T_{\alpha)\dot{\alpha})}\approx 0.

(2.3)

In the spinor formalism, Weyl-like tensors naturally decompose into (anti-)self-dual parts.
Let
h_{\alpha(2)\dot{\alpha}(2)}
and
A_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}

be the fields of spin-2 and spin-1 correspondingly. Then
spin-2 and spin-1 self-dual tensors are given by:

C_{\alpha(4)}=(\partial^{2})^{\dot{\beta}(2)}_{(\alpha(2)}h_{\alpha(2))\dot{\beta}(2)},\qquad C_{\alpha(2)}=\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}A_{\alpha)\dot{\beta}}.

(2.4)

The anti-self-dual parts are obtained by the complex conjugation. On the equations of motion, Weyl-like tensors satisfy

\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\beta}C_{\beta\alpha(3)}\approx 0,\qquad\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\beta}C_{\beta\alpha}\approx 0.

(2.5)

Using these spin-tensors, one can construct a complex spin-
3
current with four derivatives as:

\mathbf{J}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}=C_{\alpha(3)\beta}\partial^{\beta}_{(\dot{\alpha}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2))}.

(2.6)

In this order of derivatives, it is impossible to construct the current trace, meaning that
T_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=0
. Consequently, the trace partner
\Phi^{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}

completely decouples from this interaction, and the vertex is governed solely by the first term in (
2.1
).
This current (
2.6
) satisfies the conservation equation:

\partial^{\rho\dot{\rho}}\mathbf{J}_{(\rho\alpha(2))(\dot{\rho}\dot{\alpha}(2))}=\left(\partial^{\rho\dot{\rho}}C_{\rho\alpha(2)\beta}\right)\partial^{\beta}_{(\dot{\rho}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2))}+C_{\rho\alpha(2)\beta}\,\partial^{\rho\dot{\rho}}\left(\partial^{\beta}_{(\dot{\rho}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2))}\right)\approx 0.

(2.7)

The current (
2.6
) is complex, so it cannot be directly used to construct the cubic vertex (
2.1
). However, one can consider the real and imaginary parts of this complex spin-3 current:

\begin{split}&\text{Re}\,\mathbf{J}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}=\frac{1}{2}\left\{C_{\alpha(3)\beta}\partial^{\beta}_{(\dot{\alpha}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2))}+\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}C_{\alpha(2))}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(3)\dot{\beta}}\right\},\\
&\text{Im}\,\mathbf{J}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}=\frac{1}{2i}\left\{C_{\alpha(3)\beta}\partial^{\beta}_{(\dot{\alpha}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2))}-\partial_{(\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}C_{\alpha(2))}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(3)\dot{\beta}}\right\}.\end{split}

(2.8)

Each of these currents defines a consistent cubic vertex
(3,2,1)
.
Note that the vertex constructed from the imaginary part of the current is parity-breaking.

We proceed to the
\mathcal{N}=2
generalization of this vertex. According to the general construction, we seek the vertex
(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})
in the following form:

S_{(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})}=\int d^{4}xd^{8}\theta du\left(\Psi^{-(\alpha\beta)\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{D}^{+}_{\beta}+\bar{\Psi}^{-(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\beta}}\right)\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},

(2.9)

where
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
is a real principal supercurrent. Gauge transformations of the Mezincescu spin-
3
prepotential are

\begin{split}\delta\Psi^{-}_{(\alpha\beta)\dot{\alpha}}=\;&\mathcal{D}^{++}K^{(-3)}_{(\alpha\beta)\dot{\alpha}}+\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}B^{--}_{\beta)\dot{\alpha}}+\mathcal{D}^{+\rho}B^{--}_{(\alpha\beta\rho)\dot{\alpha}}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}B^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}B^{--}_{(\alpha\beta)},\end{split}

(2.10)

and they imply that the principal supercurrent for vertex consistency must satisfy

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\approx 0,\\
\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\approx 0,\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\approx 0.\end{cases}

(2.11)

Analogously to the previous example, to find its explicit structure, we first introduce the
complex
\mathcal{N}=2
principal spin-
3
supercurrent
, using the chiral
\mathcal{N}=2
super-Weyl tensor
\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}
and the chiral
\mathcal{N}=2
Maxwell superfield strength
\mathcal{W}
(see Appendix
A
for their definition):

\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}.

(2.12)

This supercurrent satisfies the following equations:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=0,\\
\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\approx 0,\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\approx 0.\end{cases}

(2.13)

To verify the conservation law (
2.13
), one can explicitly apply the spinor covariant derivatives to the supercurrent and use properties of
\mathcal{N}=2
super-Weyl tensors presented in Appendix
A
. For example,

\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\left(\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\right)\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}+\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\bar{\mathcal{W}}\right)\approx 0.

(2.14)

Similarly, for the conjugate spinorial derivative, the relation holds due to the off-shell chirality of the super-Weyl tensor (
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}=0
) and the on-shell equations of motion for the Maxwell multiplet:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\left(\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}\right)=\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}\right)\approx 0.

(2.15)

The expression
\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}
vanishes on-shell, which physically corresponds to the Dirac equation for the photino component of the vector multiplet.

As in the bosonic example, one can also construct the interaction using the real or imaginary parts of the complex spin-
\mathbf{3}
supercurrent. Accordingly, they will be responsible for the
\mathcal{N}=2
supersymmetrization of the parity-invariant and the parity-breaking
(3,2,1)
cubic interactions:

\begin{split}&\text{Re}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}+\partial_{\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{W}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}\right),\\
&\text{Im}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\mathcal{W}_{(\alpha\beta)}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}-\partial_{\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{W}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{(\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta})}\right).\end{split}

(2.16)

To see how the conservation equations (
2.13
) guarantee the gauge invariance of the cubic vertex, one performs an integration by parts in the superfield action (
2.9
) under the gauge shifts of the prepotentials. For instance, the variation with respect to the harmonic parameter
K^{(-3)}_{(\alpha\beta)\dot{\alpha}}
vanishes due to the harmonic constraint
\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}=0
. Similarly, the shifts proportional to the gauge parameters
B^{--}
translate directly into the requirement for the supercurrent to be killed by the spinor covariant derivatives
\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}
and
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\alpha}}
.

Since both
\text{Re}\,\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
and
\text{Im}\,\mathscr{J}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}
dynamically satisfy the full set of these constraints on-shell, we conclude that (
2.9
) provides a consistent, gauge-invariant
\mathcal{N}=2
superfield generalization of the four-derivative
(3,2,1)
vertices.

3
General higher-spin currents

Let us now proceed to construct general component higher-spin currents. As in the example above, for the consistency of the vertex, the higher-spin current must satisfy a conservation condition:

\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}J_{(\beta\alpha(s-1))(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-1))}+\partial_{(\alpha(\dot{\alpha}}T_{\alpha(s-2))\dot{\alpha}(s-2))}\approx 0.

(3.1)

The main building block for the currents is the Weyl-like tensor, defined by

C_{\alpha(2s)}\coloneqq(\partial^{s})^{\dot{\beta}(s)}_{(\alpha(s)}\Phi_{\alpha(s))\dot{\beta}(s)}.

(3.2)

This is the only tensor that does not vanish on the equations of motion. On-shell, it satisfies

\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\beta}C_{\beta\alpha(2s-1)}\approx 0.

(3.3)

We also use the condensed notation
C_{s}
for the spin-
s
Weyl-like tensor.

The general gauge-invariant ansatz for a complex spin-
s
current involving
s+s_{1}-s_{2}
derivatives has the form

\mathbf{J}_{\alpha(s)\dot{\alpha}(s)}=\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}}a_{p}\left(\partial^{p}C_{s_{1}}\right)_{\alpha(p+s_{1}+s_{2})\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p}\bar{C}_{s_{2}}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p)\dot{\alpha}(s-p)}.

(3.4)

Here
a_{p}
are arbitrary complex coefficients.
In the first and second terms, full symmetrization is carried out over all dotted and undotted indices.
The upper limit of the sum is

s-s_{1}-s_{2}
, because the second term must contain at least
s_{1}-s_{2}
derivatives to match the index structure.

The non-zero trace of the current exists only when
s-s_{1}-s_{2}\geq 2
. The general ansatz is given by

\begin{split}\mathbf{T}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}-2}b_{p}&\left(\partial^{p+1}C_{s_{1}}\right)_{\alpha(p+s_{1}+s_{2})\beta(s_{1}-s_{2}+1)(\dot{\alpha}(p)\dot{\beta})}\\
&\qquad\times\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p-1}\bar{C}_{s_{2}}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2}+1)\dot{\beta}}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-p-2)}.\end{split}

(3.5)

In total, the ansatz for the current trace contains
s-s_{1}-s_{2}-1
independent complex parameters
b_{p}
.

The conservation equation (
3.1
) yields the recurrence relations for coefficients:

a_{p}\cdot\frac{s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p}{s}\cdot\frac{s-p}{s}+a_{p+1}\cdot\frac{s_{1}+s_{2}+p+1}{s}\cdot\frac{p+1}{s}+b_{p}+b_{p-1}=0.

(3.6)

In this equation, we assume the following boundary conditions:

\begin{split}&a_{p}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p<0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;p>s-s_{1}-s_{2},\\
&b_{p}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p<0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;p>s-s_{1}-s_{2}-2.\end{split}

(3.7)

The solutions of this equation depend on the choice of complex coefficients
b_{p}
.

\bullet
In the
b_{p}=0
case, we obtain a unique solution:

a^{(0)}_{p}=(-1)^{p}\frac{\binom{s}{s_{1}+s_{2}+p}\binom{s}{p}}{\binom{s}{s_{1}+s_{2}}}.

(3.8)

This solution exists for any
s\geq s_{1}\geq s_{2}
provided that
s\geq s_{1}+s_{2}
.
Corresponding traceless higher-spin current is given by the following expression:

\begin{split}\mathbf{J}^{(0)}_{\alpha(s)\dot{\alpha}(s)}=\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}}(-1)^{p}\frac{\binom{s}{s_{1}+s_{2}+p}\binom{s}{p}}{\binom{s}{s_{1}+s_{2}}}&\left(\partial^{p}C_{s_{1}}\right)_{\alpha(p+s_{1}+s_{2})\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\\
&\times\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p}\bar{C}_{s_{2}}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p)\dot{\alpha}(s-p)}.\end{split}

(3.9)

\bullet
Solutions with
b_{p}\neq 0
are
given by:

a_{p}=a_{p}^{(0)}\left[\frac{a_{0}}{a_{0}^{(0)}}+s^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{p-1}\frac{b_{j}+b_{j-1}}{(s-2s_{2}-j)(s-j)a_{j}^{(0)}}\right],\qquad p=1,2,\dots,s-2s_{2}.

(3.10)

In total, if
s-s_{1}-s_{2}\geq 2
, there are
s-s_{1}-s_{2}
complex conserved currents; if
s-s_{1}-s_{2}=1
or
s-s_{1}-s_{2}=0
, there is only one traceless complex current.

Example:

In this case, there are two complex currents. The traceless current is given by:

\mathbf{J}^{(0)}_{\alpha(5)\dot{\alpha}(5)}=\;\bigl(C_{2}\bigr)_{\alpha(3)\beta}\bigl(\partial^{3}\bar{C}_{1}\bigr)^{\beta}_{\alpha(2)\dot{\alpha}(5)}-\frac{5}{2}\;\bigl(\partial C_{2}\bigr)_{\alpha(4)\beta\dot{\alpha}}\bigl(\partial^{2}\bar{C}_{1}\bigr)^{\beta}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}(4)}+\;\bigl(\partial^{2}C_{2}\bigr)_{\alpha(5)\beta\dot{\alpha}(2)}\bigl(\partial\bar{C}_{1}\bigr)^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}(3)}.

(3.11)

The current with a nontrivial trace has the form:

\mathbf{J}^{(1)}_{\alpha(5)\dot{\alpha}(5)}=-b_{0}\frac{5^{2}}{4}\left(\partial C_{2}\right)_{\alpha(4)\beta\dot{\alpha}}\left(\partial^{2}\bar{C}_{1}\right)^{\beta}_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}(4)},

(3.12a)

\mathbf{T}^{(1)}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}=b_{0}\left(\partial C_{2}\right)_{\alpha(3)\beta(2)\dot{\beta}}\left(\partial^{2}\bar{C}_{1}\right)^{\beta(2)\dot{\beta}}_{\dot{\alpha}(3)}.

(3.12b)

After taking the real and imaginary parts of these currents, we obtain four real currents (two from each complex one). Not all of them give independent nontrivial cubic vertices, since some of vertices reduce to the “fake” interactions. In this example, the "fake"
(5,2,1)
vertices can be constructed as the real and imaginary parts of a complex gauge-invariant Lagrangian:

\mathcal{L}^{\text{fake}}_{(5,2,1)}=\mathcal{R}^{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}_{(s=5)}C_{\alpha(3)\beta}\partial^{\beta}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{C}_{\dot{\alpha}(2)}.

(3.13)

Here
\mathcal{R}^{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}_{(s=5)}
is the spin-

Zaigraev:2026jvu

\begin{split}\mathcal{R}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}:=\;&\Box\Phi_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}+2\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\Phi_{(\alpha(2)\beta)(\dot{\alpha}(2)\dot{\beta})}-\frac{50}{9}\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\partial^{\gamma\dot{\gamma}}\Phi_{(\alpha(3)\beta\gamma)(\dot{\alpha}(3)\dot{\beta}\dot{\gamma})}.\end{split}

(3.14)

Since the linearized Ricci tensor vanishes on-shell,
\mathcal{R}_{\alpha(3)\dot{\alpha}(3)}\approx 0

To construct

(4.1)

;

\begin{split}\delta_{\lambda,b}\Psi^{-}_{\alpha(s-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\;&\mathcal{D}^{++}K^{(-3)}_{\alpha(s-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}+\mathcal{D}^{+}_{(\alpha}B^{--}_{\alpha(s-2))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\\
&+\mathcal{D}^{+\beta}B^{--}_{(\alpha(s-1)\beta)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}+\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}B^{--}_{\alpha(s-1)(\dot{\alpha}(s-2)\dot{\beta})}\\
&+\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{(\dot{\alpha}}B^{--}_{\alpha(s-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}.\end{split}

(4.2)

The superfield
\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}
is the real
principal
\mathcal{N}=2
supercurrent
, defined by the following equations:

\begin{cases}\widetilde{\mathcal{J}}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)},\\
\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,\\
\mathcal{D}^{+\beta}\mathcal{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{J}_{\alpha(s-2)(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}\approx 0.\end{cases}

(4.3)

If these conditions are satisfied, then the cubic interaction (
4.1
) is consistent at the leading order.

Following the construction of the spin-
\mathbf{3}
supercurrent, we introduce a
complex principal
\mathcal{N}=2
supercurrent

\mathscr{J}
that satisfies the same equations (
4.3
), except for the reality condition:

\begin{cases}\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,\\
\mathcal{D}^{+\beta}\mathscr{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}\approx 0.\end{cases}

(4.4)

Then, extracting the real and imaginary parts from the complex supercurrent

\begin{split}&\text{Re}\,\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}+\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\right),\\
&\text{Im}\,\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\frac{1}{2i}\left(\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}-\tilde{\mathscr{J}}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\right),\end{split}

(4.5)

we obtain the parity-even and the parity-odd real
\mathcal{N}=2
supercurrents and the corresponding cubic vertices (
4.1
).

Before specifying the explicit form of the supercurrent, let us note that system (
4.4
) inherently implies several general consequences; in particular:

\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathscr{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,

(4.6a)

\mathcal{D}^{-\beta}\mathscr{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0,

(4.6b)

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-\dot{\beta}}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}\approx 0,

(4.6c)

\partial^{\beta\dot{\beta}}\mathscr{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}\approx 0.

(4.6d)

The last equation ensures that all component fields are traceless conserved currents (see Section
2
and eq. (
3.9
) above).

We proceed to construct a general spin-
\mathbf{s}
complex
\mathcal{N}=2
principal supercurrent linear in the spin-
\mathbf{s_{1}}
and spin-
\mathbf{s_{2}}
super-Weyl tensors. Such a construction is possible provided that
\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{s_{1}}+\mathbf{s_{2}}
.
We start with an ansatz for the
\mathcal{N}=2
complex principal supercurrent. For definiteness, we will consider
\mathbf{s_{1}}\geq\mathbf{s_{2}}
.

To systematically solve the constraints, we structure the ansatz for the complex principal supercurrent by grouping terms according to the number of supersymmetric covariant derivatives. Specifically, the coefficients
a_{p}
,
b_{p}
, and
c_{p}
parameterize contributions containing zero, one, and two spinor derivatives acting on the super-Weyl tensor, respectively.
The most general ansatz then has the form:

\begin{split}&\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}}a_{p}\left(\partial^{p}\mathcal{W}\right)_{\alpha(s_{1}+s_{2}+p-2)\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p)\dot{\alpha}(s-2s_{2}-p)}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}\\
&+\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}-1}b_{p}\Bigg[\left(\partial^{p}\mathcal{D}^{+}\mathcal{W}\right)_{\alpha(s_{1}+s_{2}+p-1)\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p-1}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-2s_{2}-p-1)}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}\\
&\qquad\qquad\qquad-\left(\partial^{p}\mathcal{D}^{-}\mathcal{W}\right)_{\alpha(s_{1}+s_{2}+p-1)\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p-1}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p-1)\dot{\alpha}(s-2s_{2}-p-1)}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}\Bigg]\\
&+\sum_{p=0}^{s-s_{1}-s_{2}-2}c_{p}\left(\partial^{p}\mathcal{D}^{+}\mathcal{D}^{-}\mathcal{W}\right)_{\alpha(s_{1}+s_{2}+p)\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})\dot{\alpha}(p)}\left(\partial^{s-2s_{2}-p-2}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\alpha(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2s_{2}-p-2)}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}.\end{split}

(4.7)

Here, we use spin-
\mathbf{s_{1}}
and spin-
\mathbf{s_{2}}
Weyl-like supertensors
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s_{1}-2)}
and
\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}
.
In each term, we assume full symmetrization over all dotted and undotted indices.

This ansatz automatically satisfies the harmonic independence condition off-shell:

\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=0.

(4.8)

Imposing the remaining conservation equations (
4.4
) on the introduced ansatz leads to the set of coupled algebraic recurrence relations for coefficients. In particular, the undotted spinor derivative constraint yields

\mathcal{D}^{+\beta}\mathscr{J}_{(\beta\alpha(s-3))\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}\approx 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\begin{cases}a_{p}(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p)-4i\,b_{p}(s_{1}+s_{2}+p-1)=0,\\
b_{p}(s-s_{1}-s_{2}-p-1)+4i\,c_{p}(s_{1}+s_{2}+p)=0.\end{cases}

(4.9)

The action of the conjugate derivative gives additional constraints:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+\dot{\beta}}\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)(\dot{\beta}\dot{\alpha}(s-3))}\approx 0\quad\Rightarrow\quad\begin{cases}a_{p}\,p+4i\,b_{p-1}(s-p-1)=0,\\
b_{p}\,p-4i\,c_{p-1}(s-p-2)=0.\end{cases}

(4.10)

In addition, the general ansatz requires boundary conditions to be imposed on the coefficients:

\begin{split}&a_{p}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p<0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;p>s-s_{1}-s_{2},\\
&b_{p}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p<0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;p>s-s_{1}-s_{2}-1,\\
&c_{p}=0\quad\text{for}\quad p<0\;\;\;\text{or}\;\;\;p>s-s_{1}-s_{2}-2.\end{split}

(4.11)

Solving the resulting equations (
4.9
) and (
4.10
), we obtain the unique solution:

a_{p}=(-1)^{p}\frac{\binom{s-2}{p}\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}+p-2}}{\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}-2}},\qquad p=0,1,2\dots,s-s_{1}-s_{2},

(4.12a)

\qquad b_{p}=\frac{(-1)^{p}}{4i}\frac{\binom{s-2}{p}\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}+p-1}}{\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}-2}},\qquad p=0,1,2\dots,s-s_{1}-s_{2}-1,

(4.12b)

\qquad\quad c_{p}=\frac{(-1)^{p}}{16}\frac{\binom{s-2}{p}\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}+p}}{\binom{s-2}{s_{1}+s_{2}-2}},\quad\quad p=0,1,2\dots,s-s_{1}-s_{2}-2.

(4.12c)

We conclude this section by listing some features of the constructed
\mathcal{N}=2
complex principal supercurrents.

•

In the saturated spin limit
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{s_{1}}+\mathbf{s_{2}}
, the structure of the supercurrent simplifies significantly, as the boundary conditions force the coefficients
b_{p}
and
c_{p}

to vanish identically, leaving only the single non-vanishing series parameterized by
a_{0}
:

\mathscr{J}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)}=\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(s_{1}+s_{2}-2)\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}\left(\partial^{s_{1}-s_{2}}\right)^{\beta(s_{1}-s_{2})}_{\dot{\alpha}(s_{1}-s_{2})}\bar{\mathcal{W}}_{\dot{\alpha}(2s_{2}-2)}.

(4.13)

•

In the equal-spin limit
\mathbf{s_{1}}=\mathbf{s_{2}}
, we reproduce the
\mathcal{N}=2
principal supercurrents previously obtained in
Zaigraev:2024ryg
. For odd
\mathbf{s}
, these supercurrents are purely imaginary, while for even
\mathbf{s}
, they are real.

•

The conditions (
4.4
) defining the complex principal supercurrent lead to the conclusion that all component currents are conserved (
4.6d
). This does not mean that after component reduction all resulting vertices will be constructed from the traceless currents. The reason for this lies in the fact that some of fields in the Wess–Zumino gauge correspond to traces of the higher-spin Lorentz connection and lead to the appearance of trace-type terms. For details, see
Zaigraev:2026jvu
.

5
Conclusions

In this article, we have systematically investigated cubic interaction vertices for
\mathcal{N}=2
higher-spin gauge supermultiplets in harmonic superspace and corresponding
\mathcal{N}=2
supercurrents. As a simple example, we constructed the four-derivative
(\mathbf{3},\mathbf{2},\mathbf{1})
superfield vertex, which provides the
\mathcal{N}=2
extension of the component
(3,2,1)
interaction.

Besides the supersymmetric analysis, we carried out the complete classification of the corresponding bosonic higher-spin currents, associated with the abelian
(s,s_{1},s_{2})
interactions. Solving the current conservation equations, we obtained the complete family of complex currents, including currents with the non-vanishing trace, and identified the corresponding trivial (“fake”) vertices removable by local field redefinitions. After factoring out such vertices, a single nontrivial complex current remains in the generic case
s_{1}\neq s_{2}
, generating the parity-even and the parity-odd cubic interactions. We also analyzed the special case
s_{1}=s_{2}
, where additional reality conditions reduce the number of independent conserved currents.

For arbitrary
\mathcal{N}=2
spins, we constructed the most general gauge-invariant ansatz for the complex principal supercurrent built from spin-
\mathbf{s_{1}}
and spin-
\mathbf{s_{2}}
Weyl-like supertensors with the minimal number of derivatives. Solving the supercurrent conservation equations, we found the unique exact solution, which exists provided the bound
\mathbf{s}\geq\mathbf{s_{1}}+\mathbf{s_{2}}
is satisfied. In the saturated case
\mathbf{s}=\mathbf{s_{1}}+\mathbf{s_{2}}
, the supercurrent reduces to a particularly simple form.

Finally, we demonstrated that for
\mathbf{s_{1}}\neq\mathbf{s_{2}}
the principal supercurrent is intrinsically complex and generates two independent cubic vertices whose component expansions contain the parity-even and the parity-odd
(s,s_{1},s_{2})
interactions. In the equal-spin limit
\mathbf{s_{1}}=\mathbf{s_{2}}
, the supercurrent becomes purely imaginary for odd
\mathbf{s}
and purely real for even
\mathbf{s}
, reproducing the results of
Zaigraev:2024ryg
;
Zaigraev:2026jvu
.

Appendix A
\mathcal{N}=2
Weyl-like higher-spin tensors

The only gauge-invariant object that does not vanish on the higher-spin equations of motion is the Weyl-like tensor. In this appendix, we present the main details of its construction in the harmonic approach
Ivanov:2024gjo
;
Ivanov:2025gvs
;
Ivanov:2025mld
;
Zaigraev:2026jvu
. For clarity, we will divide the construction into several stages.

1.

Supersymmetry-invariant prepotentials

It is convenient to define superfields that are invariant under supersymmetry transformations. To this end, we consider the expansion of the spin-
\mathbf{s}
differential operator (
1.3
) in the basis of covariant derivatives:

\hat{\mathcal{H}}^{++}_{(s)}=G^{++\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)M}\mathcal{D}_{M}\partial^{s-2}_{\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)},\qquad\delta_{\epsilon}G^{++\alpha(s-2)\dot{\alpha}(s-2)M}=0.

(A.1)

Here, the covariant derivatives are given by

\mathcal{D}_{M}=\left\{\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}},\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha},\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}},\partial_{5}\right\}
. The
\mathcal{D}^{-}
covariant derivatives are defined by:

\begin{split}\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha}&=-\partial^{-}_{\alpha}+4i\bar{\theta}^{-\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-2i\theta^{-}_{\alpha}\partial_{5},\\
\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}&=-\partial^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}-4i\theta^{-\alpha}\partial_{\alpha\dot{\alpha}}-2i\bar{\theta}^{-}_{\dot{\alpha}}\partial_{5}.\end{split}

2.

Composite superfields
\mathcal{H}^{\pm\pm}_{\alpha(2s-2)}

Using supersymmetry-invariant superfields
G^{++}
, we can construct the composite superfield:

\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\alpha(2s-2)}:=\partial_{(\alpha_{1}}^{\;\;\dot{\beta}_{1}}\dots\partial_{\alpha_{s-2}}^{\;\;\dot{\beta}_{s-2}}\left(\mathcal{D}^{-}_{\alpha_{s-1}}G^{+++}_{\alpha_{s}\dots\alpha_{2s-2})(\dot{\beta}_{1}\dots\dot{\beta}_{s-2})}+\partial_{\alpha_{s-1}}^{\;\;\dot{\beta}_{s-1}}G^{++}_{\alpha_{s}\dots\alpha_{2s-2})(\dot{\beta}_{1}\dots\dot{\beta}_{s-1})}\right).

(A.2)

The key property possessed by this superfield is the “half-analyticity” condition
Ivanov:2024gjo
:

\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=0.

(A.3)

It completely fixes the relative coefficient in (
A.2
).
Using the harmonic zero-curvature condition, one can construct a superfield with a negative harmonic charge:

\mathcal{D}^{++}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=\mathcal{D}^{--}\mathcal{H}^{++}_{\alpha(2s-2)}.

(A.4)

Thus, the obtained superfields have a simple gauge transformation law with the composite “half-analytic” parameter:

\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{H}^{\pm\pm}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}\Lambda_{\alpha(2s-2)},\qquad\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}_{\dot{\alpha}}\Lambda_{\alpha(2s-2)}=0.

(A.5)

3.

Higher-spin Weyl-like supertensor

We define the gauge-invariant superfield:

\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}:=\left(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+}\right)^{2}\mathcal{H}^{--}_{\alpha(2s-2)},\qquad\delta_{\lambda}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=0.

(A.6)

Gauge invariance is ensured by the “half-analyticity” condition of the gauge parameter. Moreover, “half-analyticity” implies that the constructed superfield satisfies the conditions of harmonic independence and chirality:

\mathcal{D}^{\pm\pm}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=0,\qquad\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{\pm}_{\dot{\alpha}}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}=0.

(A.7)

In components, this superfield contains the higher-spin Weyl-like tensors, so it is natural to identify it as the
\mathcal{N}=2
spin-
\mathbf{s}
Weyl-like supertensor. For more details on the component structure see
Zaigraev:2026jvu
.

On-shell, the supertensor satisfies many useful properties. We will present the main ones:

\mathcal{D}^{\pm\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}\approx 0,\qquad\partial_{\dot{\alpha}}^{\;\alpha}\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}\approx 0,\qquad\Box\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}\approx 0.

(A.8)

Since for
s=1
the superfield
\mathcal{W}_{\alpha(2s-2)}

completely loses its spinor indices, this case requires separate consideration.
The superstrength is defined as
\mathcal{W}:=(\bar{\mathcal{D}}^{+})^{2}V^{--}
and on-shell satisfies

(\mathcal{D}^{+})^{2}\mathcal{W}\approx 0,\qquad\mathcal{D}^{+\alpha}\partial_{\alpha}^{\dot{\beta}}\mathcal{W}\approx 0.

(A.9)
