Fusions of One-Variable First-Order Modal Logics
Roman Kontchakov, Dmitry Shkatov, Frank Wolter

Abstract
We investigate preservation results for the independent fusion of one-variable first-order modal logics. We show that, without equality, Kripke completeness and decidability of the global and local consequence relation are preserved, under both expanding and constant domain semantics. By contrast, Kripke completeness and decidability are not preserved for fusions with equality and non-rigid constants (or, equivalently, counting up to one), again for the global and local consequence and under both expanding and constant domain semantics. This result is shown by encoding Diophantine equations. Even without equality, the finite model property is only preserved in the local case. Finally, we view fusions of one-variable modal logics as fusions of propositional modal logics sharing an S5 modality and provide a general sufficient condition for transfer of Kripke completeness and decidability (but not of finite model property).

Introduction

The study of combined modal logics has been an active area of research for many years

A central example of such a robust combination is fusion: a basic method in which the component logics are combined without introducing axioms that mix their modal operators, and, correspondingly, without imposing any semantic constraints that relate the distinct accessibility relations. In fact, properties such as Kripke completeness, decidability, finite model property, and Craig interpolation are all preserved under forming the fusion of two (or more) classical propositional modal logics, for both the local and global consequence relations

first-order modal logics and corresponding many-dimensional modal logics. We focus in this paper on one-variable first-order modal logics, either without equality or with equality and non-rigid constants, and on corresponding commutators and products of propositional modal logics. Theorem A

(Equality-Free Fusion)

Kripke completeness and decidability are preserved under fusions of Kripke complete one-variable first-order modal logics, for both the local and global consequence and expanding and constant domain semantics.
The finite model property is preserved for the local consequence but not for global consequence. Moreover, the finite model property fails for global consequence for all non-trivial fusions under both expanding and constant domain semantics.

The proof builds on the cactus model construction

For one‑variable first‑order modal logics with equality, the presence of equality can only have a substantial impact if one also allows non‑rigid constants or, equivalently, counting quantifiers such as

Theorem B

Decidability and recursive axiomatisability are not preserved for fusions of one-variable first-order modal logics with equality, neither for the local nor global consequence and neither for expanding nor constant domain semantics.
Moreover, the global consequence is undecidable for all non-trivial fusions for constant domain semantics.

, as required.

Let
\chi
be any formula. Then one can easily show by induction that
\mathfrak{M},w\models\chi
iff
\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa^{\prime}},(w,n)\models\chi
for all
n\in\kappa^{\prime}
. It follows that
\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa^{\prime}}\not\models\varphi
and

\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is an
(E,\kappa^{\prime})
-homogeneous model. This finishes the proof of the claim and also the proof for logics of the form
[L,\textbf{S5}]^{\textit{xd}}
.

Now assume a logic of the form
[L,\mathbf{S5}]^{\textit{cd}}
is given and Kripke complete. The construction of
\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
is the same, using a model
\mathfrak{M}=(\mathfrak{F},V)
with
\mathfrak{F}
also satisfying
(\textit{rcom}_{R,E})
. So it suffices to show that
\mathfrak{M}_{\kappa^{\prime}}
then also satisfies
(\textit{rcom}_{R,E})
: assume
(w,n)R^{\prime}(w^{\prime},m)
and
(w^{\prime},m)E^{\prime}(w^{\prime\prime},k)
. By definition,
n=m
,
wRw^{\prime}
, and
w^{\prime}Ew^{\prime\prime}
.
Then, as
(W,R,E)
satisfies
(\mathit{rcom}_{R,E})
, we have a
v^{\prime}
with
wEv^{\prime}
and
v^{\prime}Rw^{\prime\prime}
. But then
(w,n)E^{\prime}(v^{\prime},k)
and
(v^{\prime},k)R^{\prime}(w^{\prime\prime},k)
, as required.
∎

We obtain the following transfer result for (semi)commutators.

Theorem 5.6
.

Let
d\in\{\textit{xd},\textit{cd}\}
and
L_{1}
and
L_{2}
be consistent propositional modal logics such that
[L_{i},\textbf{S5}]^{d}
is
(
globally
)
Kripke complete, for both

. Then

•

[L_{1},\textbf{S5}]^{d}\otimes_{E}[L_{2},\textbf{S5}]^{d}
is
(
globally
)
Kripke complete
;

•

[L_{1},\textbf{S5}]^{d}\otimes_{E}[L_{2},\textbf{S5}]^{d}
is
(

)

[L_{1},\textbf{S5}]^{d}

[L_{2},\textbf{S5}]^{d}

(

)

6
Discussion

We have established preservation and non‑preservation results for one‑variable first‑order modal logics and for related propositional modal logics. Many questions remain open:

•

The behaviour of fusions of full first‑order modal logic is still largely
unexplored. A transfer theorem for canonicity is given in
[
30
]
, but, for example, the transfer of
completeness is open.

•

Monodic fragments extend one‑variable modal logics by allowing more expressive first‑order fragments while restricting modal operators to formulas with at most one free variable

[
23
,
1
]
.
We have shown that decidability does not transfer from the one‑variable
fragment with equality to the monodic fragment over the two‑variable fragment
of FO with counting. Positive preservation results are known
[
14
]
,
and it is straightforward to adapt our proofs to, for instance, the monodic fragments based on the two-variable fragment of
\mathbf{FO}
without equality or description logics such as
ALC
,
ALCI
, and
SHI
with the universal role
[
3
,
23
]
. However, a systematic study of fusions of monodic fragments is still missing.

•

It would be valuable to identify sufficient conditions under which Kripke completeness and decidability transfer to fusions of one‑variable modal logics with equality. We note that many standard polymodal fusions with equality such
as polymodal
\mathbf{K}
and
\mathbf{S5}
are in fact decidable
[
1
]
.

•

We have seen that, in many cases, completeness and decidability are preserved under fusions of propositional modal logics sharing an
S5
operator. Can this be extended to other modal logics?

•

Our analysis focused on Kripke complete modal logics. Since first‑order
modal logics are often incomplete under standard Kripke semantics, it would be interesting to obtain preservation results that do not presuppose Kripke
completeness. Techniques from
[
34
,
2
]
may be helpful here.
