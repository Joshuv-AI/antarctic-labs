Meson Octet in a Uniform Magnetic Field
Prabal Adhikari

Abstract
Chiral perturbation theory is utilized to construct the renormalized magnetic masses and decay constants of the meson octet at next-to-leading order. While the neutral pion mass decreases identically to two-flavor chiral perturbation theory, the neutral kaon mass remains unaltered by the magnetic field. The renormalized magnetic masses for the charged mesons change identically. Meson decay constants in the axial and vector channels are constructed. Each of the decay constants increase monotonically in the magnetic background. Low-energy theorems – Gell-Mann-Oakes-Renner relations for the neutral mesons and their generalization for the charged mesons through the pseudoscalar coupling – are constructed and provide non-trivial crosschecks.

II
Chiral Perturbation Theory Ingredients

Construction of the meson magnetic octet mass and decay constants in three-flavor chiral perturbation theory
Gasser and Leutwyler 1985
requires accounting for loop effects. We work up to
{\mathcal{O}}(p^{4})
, which includes the anomalous Wess-Zumino-Witten (WZW) contribution to the chiral Lagrangian
Wess and Zumino 1971
;
Witten 1983
. A scalar-pseudoscalar source,
\chi

A vector source is required to account for the magnetic field while a left source is required to construct the left current. The WZW Lagrangian encodes a vector current that couples the meson to the vacuum only in a background magnetic field.

III
Meson Octet Magnetic Mass

III.1
Green’s Function,
G_{Q}(x^{\prime},x)

The construction of the meson octet magnetic mass requires the
Euclidean
Green’s function,
G_{Q}(x^{\prime},x)
. For neutral mesons (
Q=0
), it is translationally invariant. However, the charged Green’s function (
Q=\pm
), as is evident from the defining relation,

\displaystyle(-D^{\prime}_{\mu}D^{\prime}_{\mu}+{\mathring{m}}_{\varphi^{Q}}^{2})G_{Q}(x^{\prime},x)=\delta^{(4)}(x^{\prime}-x)\,.

(22)

is not. Since the the
{\delta}
-function and the neutral Green’s operator is translationally invariant, the neutral Green’s function is translationally invariant,
G_{0}(x^{\prime},x)=G_{0}(x^{\prime}-x)
. However, the covariant derivative due to the gauge potential, Eq. (
13
), is not translationally invariant. Therefore, the charged Green’s function,
G_{\pm}(x^{\prime},x)
must also be translationally variant to ensure translational invariant of the product. Nevertheless, the charged Green’s function is separable into a translationally invariant and variant contributions

\begin{split}G_{Q}(x^{\prime},x)&=e^{ieB\Delta x_{1}{\overline{x}}_{2}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{ds}{(4\pi)^{2}s^{2}}\frac{eBs}{\sinh eBs}e^{-{\mathring{m}}_{\varphi}^{2}s}\\
&\times\exp\left[-\tfrac{eB\left(\Delta x_{1}^{2}+\Delta x_{2}^{2}\right)}{4\tanh eBs}-\tfrac{\Delta x_{0}^{2}+\Delta x_{3}^{2}}{4s}\right]\ ,\end{split}

(23)

with
\Delta x_{\mu}=x^{\prime}_{\mu}-x_{\mu}
and
{\overline{x}}_{\mu}=\tfrac{1}{2}(x^{\prime}_{\mu}+x_{\mu})
. The Schwinger integral is notably translationally invariant with all translational variance encoded in the Schwinger phase.

Tadpole contributions to the renormalized mass require the coincident limit of the Green’s function,

\displaystyle G_{Q}(x,x)\equiv\mathcal{D}_{Q}({\mathring{m}}_{\varphi}^{2})=\mathcal{D}_{0}({\mathring{m}}_{\varphi}^{2})+\mathcal{D}_{B}({\mathring{m}}_{\varphi}^{2})\,.

(24)

Despite the absence of translational invariance of the charged Green’s function, it is coordinate independent. The ultraviolet divergence in the coincident Green’s function is handled using dimensional regularization in
d=4-2{\varepsilon}
dimensions. Using the
{\overline{\rm MS}}
scheme with scale
\Lambda
, the
B
-independent contribution to the Green’s function is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{0}(m^{2})

\displaystyle=-\frac{m^{2}}{(4\pi)^{2}}\left[\frac{1}{\epsilon}+1+\log\frac{\Lambda^{2}}{m^{2}}\right]\ .

(25)

The magnetic field dependent contribution to the tadpole

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}_{B}(m^{2})=\frac{eB}{(4\pi)^{2}}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{m^{2}}{eB})\,,

(26)

is a function of the dimensionless Schwinger integral
{\mathcal{I}}(z)
. It is finite and admits the closed form expression

\displaystyle{\mathcal{I}}(z)=2\log\Gamma(\tfrac{1+z}{2})+z(1-\log\tfrac{z}{2})-\log(2\pi)\,,

(27)

in terms of the
\log\Gamma
function. In the zero field limit, the function vanishes, as required.

III.2
Two-point Function & Meson Mass

The meson octet magnetic mass is extracted from the renormalized two-point function,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{G}}_{Q}(x^{\prime},x)

IV
Meson Octet Decay Constants

The meson decay constant for
\varphi^{Q}
in a zero magnetic field is defined by the meson-to-vacuum matrix element

\displaystyle\left.\langle\,0\,|\,J_{L{\mu}}(x)\,|\,\varphi^{Q}(p)\,\rangle\right|_{x=0}=iF_{\varphi^{Q}}\,p_{{\mu}}\ .

(48)

The left current,
J_{L{\mu}}(x)
, is defined as the derivative with respect to left-current components,
L^{\mu}=\sum_{a=1}^{8}L^{\mu a}{\lambda}^{a}
,

\displaystyle J_{L\mu}^{a}=\left.\frac{\partial{\mathcal{L}}}{\partial L^{\mu a}}\right|_{L_{\mu}=R_{\mu}=-e{\mathcal{Q}}A_{\mu}}\,.

(49)

The right-hand side of the matrix element can be deduced (up to a proportionality factor) by matching the Lorentz structure. Only the four-momentum of the meson field,
p_{{\mu}}
, provides indices with the factor,
F_{\varphi^{Q}}
, identified as the meson decay constant.

However, in a background magnetic field, four-momentum ceases to be a good quantum number for the charged mesons. It is, therefore, necessary to handle matrix elements in position space for the charged mesons – naturally the method is also applicable for the neutral mesons. To this end, consider the correlation function

\displaystyle\langle\,0\,|\,J^{\varphi^{Q}}_{L{\mu}}(x^{\prime})\,\varphi^{Q}(x)\,|\,0\,\rangle\,,

(50)

for which Lorentz structure is supplied by the covariant derivative,
D_{\mu}
, Eq. (
12
). The magnetic field, either through the field strength tensor
F_{\mu\nu}
or its dual field
{\widetilde{F}}_{\mu\nu}=\tfrac{1}{2}{\varepsilon}_{\mu\nu{\alpha}{\beta}}F^{{\alpha}{\beta}}
provides further Lorentz indices.

In addition to Lorentz index matching, parity matching aids the construction of the correlation function. Since the left-current contains both parity odd and even contributions, it is preferable to consider the parity sectors separately. For the axial current, the matrix element is

\begin{split}\hskip-10.0pt&\langle\,0\,|\,J^{\varphi^{Q}}_{A{\mu}}(x)\varphi^{Q}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle=\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ -\left[F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{Q}}D_{\mu}-ieF_{\varphi^{Q}}^{(A2)}F_{\mu\nu}D^{\nu}+\cdots\right]\\
&\ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \ \times\langle\,0\,|\,\varphi^{Q}(x)\varphi^{Q}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle\,.\end{split}

(51)

F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{Q}}
first appears at tree level while
F^{(A2)}_{\varphi^{\pm}}
at next-to-leading order.
Since the correlation function is parity odd, the contributions on the right-hand-side must be proportional to the parity odd covariant derivative. Each term may contain one or more of the parity even field strength tensors. More generally, the axial pion decay constant
F^{(An)}_{\varphi^{Q}}
for
n\geq 1
is associated with the term containing
n-1
field strength tensors.

The parity even contribution to the correlation function, Eq. (
50
), arises through the vector current,

\begin{split}\langle\,0\,|\,&J^{\varphi^{Q}}_{V{\mu}}(x)\,\varphi^{Q}(\,0\,)\,|\,0\,\rangle\\
=&-\left[eF_{\varphi^{Q}}^{(V)}{\widetilde{F}}^{\mu\nu}D_{\mu}+\cdots\right]\langle\,0\,|\,\varphi^{Q}(x)\varphi^{Q}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle\end{split}

(52)

Since the dual field is parity odd for a magnetic background, the right-hand-side is parity even in spite of the covariant derivative.

IV.1
Axial Meson Decay Constant,
F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{Q}}

The axial decay constant,
F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{Q}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{eB}{(4{\pi}F)^{2}}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\{{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{{\pi}}^{2}}{eB})+\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{K}^{2}}{eB})\right\}\right]

(54)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\frac{F^{(A1)}_{K^{\pm}}(B)}{F_{K}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{eB}{(4{\pi}F)^{2}}\left[\tfrac{1}{2}\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{{\pi}}^{2}}{eB})+{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{K}^{2}}{eB})\right\}\right]

(55)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\frac{F^{(A1)}_{K^{0}}(B)}{F_{K}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{eB}{(4{\pi}F)^{2}}\left[\tfrac{1}{4}\left\{{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{{\pi}}^{2}}{eB})+{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{K}^{2}}{eB})\right\}\right]

(56)

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\frac{F^{(A1)}_{\eta}(B)}{F_{\eta}}

IV.3

The contribution to the vector meson decay constant,

\displaystyle F^{(V)}_{K^{0}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{16{\pi}^{2}F}\,.

\displaystyle F^{(V)}_{{\pi}^{0}}=F^{(V)}_{\eta}=\frac{1}{8{\pi}^{2}F}\,.

(64)

V
Low Energy Theorems

Low energy theorems provide non-trivial cross-checks for the derived renormalized magnetic masses and decay constants (in the axial channel). Explicit chiral symmetry breaking for the neutral mesons is evident from the divergence of the respective axial current components

\displaystyle\partial^{{\mu}}J_{A\mu}^{\varphi^{0}}=F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{0}}(B)m_{\varphi^{0}}^{2}(B)\,\varphi^{0}\,.

(65)

The corresponding relationship in QCD for the neutral mesons is

\displaystyle\partial^{{\mu}}\left({\overline{q}}{\gamma}_{{\mu}}{\gamma}_{5}\tfrac{1}{2}{\lambda}_{a}q\right)=i{\overline{q}}{\gamma}_{5}\left\{\tfrac{1}{2}{\lambda}_{a},s\right\}q\,,

(66)

where
s
is the quark mass matrix and
q(x)
is the quark field
q^{T}(x)=\big(\,u(x),d(x),s(x)\,\big)
.
Comparison of the correlation function in chiral perturbation theory

\begin{split}\langle\,0\,|\,\partial^{{\mu}}J_{A\mu}^{\varphi^{0}}(x)&\varphi^{0}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle\\
=&F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{0}}(B)m_{\varphi^{0}}^{2}(B)\langle\,0\,|\,\varphi^{0}(x)\varphi^{0}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle\end{split}

(67)

with the corresponding relation in QCD leads to the low energy theorems

\displaystyle F_{{\pi}^{0}}(B)m_{{\pi}^{0}}^{2}(B)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{u}+m_{d})G_{{\pi}^{0}}(B)

(68)

\displaystyle F_{K^{0}}(B)m_{K^{0}}^{2}(B)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(m_{d}+m_{s})G_{K^{0}}(B)\,.

(69)

For compactness, we have adopted the notation
F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{0}}(B)=F_{\varphi^{0}}(B)
.
6
6

6

An analogous relation for the octet eta

\displaystyle F^{(A1)}_{\eta}(B)m_{\eta}^{2}(B)

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{6}(m_{u}+m_{d}+4m_{s})G_{\eta}(B)\,,

(70)

does not appear to hold in chiral perturbation theory. Unlike the magnetic corrections to the masses of the neutral pion and the neutral kaons, the magnetic corrections to the octet eta mass (squared) is not the product of the zero field mass (squared) and a magnetic tadpole. Amusingly, however, in the limit of equal quark masses Eq. (
47
) reduces to

\begin{split}m_{\eta}^{2}(B)=m_{\eta}^{2}\left[1+\frac{eB}{(4{\pi}F)^{2}}\left\{-\tfrac{1}{3}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{{\pi}}^{2}}{eB})+\tfrac{2}{3}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{K}^{2}}{eB})\right\}\right]\,.\end{split}

(71)

{\mathcal{I}}

\displaystyle\frac{G_{\eta}(B)}{G_{\eta}}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{eB}{(4{\pi}F)^{2}}\left[\tfrac{1}{3}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{{\pi}}^{2}}{eB})+\tfrac{1}{12}{\mathcal{I}}(\tfrac{{\mathring{m}}_{K}^{2}}{eB})\right]\,.

\displaystyle\hskip-5.0pt\langle\,0\,|\,P_{\varphi^{0}}(x)\varphi^{0}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle=G_{\varphi^{0}}(B)\langle\,0\,|\,\varphi^{0}(x)\varphi^{0}(0)\,|\,0\,\rangle\,,

VI
Conclusion

In this paper, we have characterized the impact of magnetic fields on the meson octet masses and decay constants using three-flavor chiral perturbation theory. There are some surprises – at next-to-leading order the magnetic mass for the neutral pion is identical to the result in two-flavor chiral perturbation theory and the magnetic field does not modify the mass of the neutral kaon and finite volume corrections in the neutral kaon polarizability is absent at this order. The magnetic mass of the octet eta decreases monotonically with the magnetic field. For the charged mesons, the corrections to the renormalized mass and pole mass are identical. The first axial decay constant,
F^{(A1)}_{\varphi^{Q}}
, monotonically increases for each of the mesons with the neutral pion exhibit the most significant increase, followed by the charged pions, charged kaons, neutral kaons and the octet eta. The second axial decay constant,
F^{(A2)}_{\varphi^{\pm}}
, is non-zero for the charged mesons and degenerate. The vector decay constant,
F^{(V)}_{\varphi^{Q}}
is identical for each of the mesons with the exception of the neutral kaon for which it is smaller by a factor of two.

For the neutral mesons, Gell-Mann-Oakes relations provide non-trivial tests of the validity of the magnetic masses and decay constants – their alteration must be consistent with the enhancement of the corresponding quark condensates. Additionally, the relations generalize for the charged mesons with the replacement of the magnetic mass by the renormalized mass at zero field.

The renormalized magnetic masses and decay constants are inputs in the construction of magnetic decay widths, which may be explored further in future work, particularly for the kaons. For the pions, decay width modifications were constructed in two-flavor chiral perturbation theory in Ref.
Adhikari and Tiburzi 2024
for the anomalous channel,
{\pi}^{0}\rightarrow{\gamma}{\gamma}
and the non-anomalous leptonic channel
{\pi}^{+}\rightarrow\ell^{+}{\nu}_{\ell}
. Among the inputs required for the analysis, only
F^{(A1)}_{{\pi}^{\pm}}(B)
is modified in three-flavor chiral perturbation theory. The ratio of the three-flavor axial decay constant to its two-flavor counterpart
