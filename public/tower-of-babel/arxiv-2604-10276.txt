Geronimus transformation and Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
N. Neha †

Abstract
. Iterated Geronimus transformations generate Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials from classical families. We establish a direct equivalence between a Sobolev inner product involving point evaluation and the first derivative at a a (located outside the support of the original measure) and two successive Geronimus transformations. Explicit three-term and five-term recurrence relations are derived for the double Geronimus polynomials, revealing their underlying algebraic structure. Connection formulas linking the Sobolev-orthogonal polynomials Q n M , N ​ ( x ) Q_{n}^{M,N}(x) with both the original polynomials and the transformed Geronimus polynomials are obtained through Christoffel–Darboux kernels and determinantal representations. In the Jacobi case, a detailed asymptotic analysis shows that the ratios of derivatives and norms converge to explicit constants independent of the perturbation parameters M M and N N . These results provide a unified framework connecting spectral transformation theory with Sobolev orthogonality.

1.
Introduction

Orthogonal polynomials and their spectral transformations lie at the heart of approximation theory, integrable systems, and quantum mechanics. While classical orthogonal polynomial families (Jacobi, Laguerre, Hermite) are well-characterized through their weight functions and recurrence relations, modern applications in spectral theory
[
16
]
and Sobolev orthogonal polynomials
[
18
]
demand controlled perturbations of their orthogonality structures. Among such perturbations, the Geronimus transformation – first introduced in 1943
[
10
]
but overlooked until its rediscovery in integrable lattice systems
[
20
]
– has emerged as a fundamental tool for constructing new orthogonal polynomial sequences through measure modification.

Iterated Geronimus transformations applied to classical orthogonal polynomials generate Sobolev-type orthogonal families by systematically incorporating derivative terms into the inner product through layered perturbations. A single Geronimus transformation at a point
a\notin\text{supp}(\mu)=:E
modifies the measure as
d\mu_{g}(x)=\frac{1}{x-a}d\mu(x)+M\delta(x-a
), altering orthogonality conditions but not involving derivatives.
To account for derivatives, the transformation must be iterated at the same point
a
. This repeated process builds rational factors of the form
\frac{1}{(x-a)^{k}}
and adds multiple Dirac masses, ultimately leading to an inner product that includes discrete evaluations of the functions and their higher-order derivatives at
a
.

A double Geronimus transformation
[
9
]
addresses this limitation by inducing a Sobolev inner product of the form

(1.1)

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{gg}=\int_{E}p(x)q(x)d\mu_{gg}(x)+\mathbf{p}(a)^{\top}\mathbf{M}_{1}\mathbf{q}(a),

where
\textbf{p}(a)=\begin{bmatrix}p(a)&p^{\prime}(a)\end{bmatrix}^{T}
and
\mathbf{M}_{1}
encodes masses and moments as

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}b&c\\
c&d\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\int_{E}d\mu_{gg}(x)&\int_{E}xd\mu_{gg}(x)\\
\int_{E}xd\mu_{gg}(x)&\int_{E}x^{2}d\mu_{gg}(x)\end{bmatrix}.

The subtraction of the moment terms in
\mathbf{M}_{1}
guarantees that orthogonality is preserved, even though the inner product combines an integral part with discrete evaluations at the point
a
. Crucially, the transformed measure satisfies
(x-a)^{2}d\mu_{gg}=d\mu
, maintaining algebraic consistency with the original measure
\mu
. The resulting orthogonal polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
exhibit the relation in terms of
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
, which are orthogonal with respect to
\mu
,

\displaystyle P_{n}^{gg}(x)=P_{n}(x)+B_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-2}(x),\;n\geq 1,\;C_{n}\neq 0\;\text{for}\;n\geq 2.

This structure reflects the non-diagonal Sobolev inner product, inducing higher-order recurrence relations distinct from classical three-term recursions
[
2
,
14
]
. Furthermore, from (
1.1
), we have
\langle(x-a)^{2}p,q\rangle_{gg}=\langle p,q\rangle_{0}:=\int_{E}p(x)q(x)d\mu(x)
. These types of families are well explored in the literature (see
[
2
,
14
]
and reference therein). For further iterations of the Geronimus transformation, see the results presented in
[
1
,
17
]
. The Geronimus introduced the transformation in
[
10
,
11
]
as a method for constructing new sequences of orthogonal polynomials from existing families of known sequences. It has also been examined in connection with different schemes of mechanical quadratures (see
[
19
]
). Further references on the Geronimus transformation can be found in
[
5
]
and
[
6
]
. The case of multiple Geronimus transformations is studied in
[
8
]
, where it is shown that such iterations lead to discrete Sobolev-type inner products.

Sobolev orthogonality often arises from spectral transformations (e.g., Darboux or Geronimus) that perturb classical measures. For instance, the Krall-Jacobi polynomials, arising from iterated Darboux transformations of classical Jacobi polynomials
[
15
]
, exemplify how spectral methods generate Sobolev orthogonal systems. These polynomials satisfy higher-order differential equations, reflecting the action of commutative algebras of operators, a structure mirrored in Sobolev inner products that incorporate derivatives as perturbations. The iterated Christoffel transformations
[
13
]
modify moments in ways equivalent to introducing point masses or derivative couplings, as seen in coherent pairs
[
18
]
. These systems reflect a unifying structure where spectral perturbations (point masses, derivatives) generate commutative algebras of differential operators, as seen in bispectral frameworks
[
12
]
. This synthesis underscores a spectral-theoretic duality: perturbations via spectral transformations (Geronimus, Darboux, Uvarov) inherently encode derivative terms into the orthogonality structure, as demonstrated by Krall-type systems
[
3
]
. The Sobolev orthogonality characteristic of these systems reveals a unifying spectral framework, wherein perturbations manifest through point masses or modifications of moments, thereby introducing derivative components
[
2
,
14
]
.

In this manuscript, we examine the relationship between iterated Geronimus transformations and Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials generated by inner products that include point evaluations and derivatives. In particular, we consider the monic polynomial sequence
\{Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
that is orthogonal with respect to the Sobolev inner product

(1.2)

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{S}=\int_{E}p(x)q(x)d\mu(x)+Mp(a)q(a)+Np^{\prime}(a)q^{\prime}(a),\quad a\notin\text{supp}(\mu),

defined on the space
\mathbb{P}
of polynomials with real coefficients. This inner product introduces a double perturbation at
x=a
, combining both function values and first derivatives—a structure that naturally arises in problems involving spectral data modifications or boundary conditions.

Building on foundational work from
[
4
]
, we observe that the polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
can be expressed in terms of the original orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)
associated with
\mu
, analogous to the explicit relations derived for classical orthogonal polynomials under iterated Geronimus transformations (see
[
9
]
). We show that applying two successive Geronimus transformations to the polynomial sequence
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
produces the Sobolev orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
, thereby establishing a direct connection between discrete spectral transformations and Sobolev inner products involving derivatives. The primary aim of this work is to analyze the interplay between the Sobolev-modified polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
and their iteratively transformed counterparts
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
at the critical point
x=a
. Unlike previous studies, which primarily focus on single-step transformations, our work explores the recurrence relations and asymptotic properties of orthogonal systems generated through iterated Geronimus transformations.

The explicit formula and matrix representation of the double Geronimus polynomials were presented in
[
9
]
. This manuscript extends the study by exploring additional properties of the double Geronimus polynomials and examining how successive Geronimus transformations influence the structure and asymptotic behavior of Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials.
The remainder of this manuscript is as follows: In Section
2
, we derive recurrence relations for the double Geronimus polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}
, including both three-term and higher-order forms induced by the quadratic factor
(x-a)^{2}
, revealing their algebraic and orthogonality structure. Section
3
introduces a Sobolev inner product perturbed by point evaluations and derivatives, establishes explicit connection formulas between the Sobolev-orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
and
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
through kernel polynomials and determinant representations. These results clarify how discrete spectral modifications at
x=a
influence the Sobolev framework. Section
4
presents the asymptotic behavior of the Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
and the associated Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
. It includes detailed comparisons of norms and the study of their behavior under successive transformations.

2.
Relations for double Geronimus transformation polynomials

As we know, orthogonal polynomials
[
7
]
satisfy a three-term recurrence relation given by

(2.1)

\displaystyle xP_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+\beta_{n}P_{n}(x)+\gamma_{n}P_{n-1}(x),\quad n\geq 0.

Since the polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}
are also orthogonal, it is natural to explore the recurrence relation they satisfy and how their recurrence coefficients depend on
\beta_{n}
and
\gamma_{n}
. We also derive alternative recurrence relations satisfied by
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}
.

Theorem 2.1
.

The double Geronimus polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfy the three-term recurrence relation

(2.2)

\displaystyle P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)=(x-a-\sigma_{n,n}^{gg})P_{n}^{gg}(x)-\sigma_{n,n-1}^{gg}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

where

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{gg}=B_{n}-B_{n+1}+(\beta_{n}-a)\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad\sigma_{n,n-1}^{gg}=\frac{C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n-1}\lVert P_{n-3}\rVert^{2}_{0}}.

Proof.

Since
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
forms a standard orthogonal sequence, we can write

\displaystyle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg}(x)=P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n,n}^{gg}P_{n}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n,n-1}^{gg}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x),

with coefficients given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n,n}^{gg}=\frac{\langle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}{\langle P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad\sigma_{n,n-1}^{gg}=\frac{\langle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}{\langle P_{n-1}^{gg}(x),P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}.

From the expansion

P_{n}^{gg}(x)=P_{n}(x)+B_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-2}(x),

we compute

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg},P_{n}^{gg}\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)(x-a)P_{n}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+B_{n}\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)(x-a)P_{n-1}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+C_{n}\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)(x-a)P_{n-2}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x).

Using the recurrence relation (
2.1
)

(x-a)P_{n}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+(\beta_{n}-a)P_{n}(x)+\gamma_{n}P_{n-1}(x),

we get

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg},P_{n}^{gg}\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)P_{n+1}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)+(\beta_{n}-a)\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)P_{n}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+B_{n}\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)P_{n}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)\left(P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)-B_{n+1}P_{n}(x)-C_{n+1}P_{n-1}(x)\right)d\mu_{gg}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+(B_{n}+\beta_{n}-a)\|P_{n}^{gg}\|^{2}_{gg}

\displaystyle=(B_{n}-B_{n+1}+\beta_{n}-a)\|P_{n}^{gg}\|^{2}_{gg}.

Similarly,

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)(x-a)P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)=\|P_{n}^{gg}\|^{2}_{gg}.

Moreover, the norm satisfies

(2.3)

\displaystyle\|P_{k}^{gg}\|^{2}_{gg}=\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{k-2}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}=\langle P_{k-2}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{0}=C_{k}\|P_{k-2}\|^{2}_{0}.

Hence, we achieve the required result.
∎

For the Jacobi measure with
a=-1
, the double Geronimus-modified measure becomes

d\mu_{gg}(x)=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta-2}.

Let
\{P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
denote the monic Jacobi polynomials orthogonal with respect to
d\mu(x)\\
=(1-x)^{\alpha}(1+x)^{\beta}
, and let
\{P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
be orthogonal with respect to
d\mu_{gg}(x)
.
Then the relationship between these polynomials is

\displaystyle P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)+\frac{4n(\alpha+n)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta-2)(2n+\alpha+\beta)}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{4n(n-1)(\alpha+n)(\alpha+n-1)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta-3)(2n+\alpha+\beta-2)^{2}(2n+\alpha+\beta-1)}P_{n-2}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x).

For the monic Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
with
a=-1
, the recurrence relation (
2.2
) simplifies to

\displaystyle xP_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)=P_{n+1}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)+\frac{(\beta-2)^{2}-\alpha^{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta-2)(2n+\alpha+\beta)}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{4n(\alpha+n)(n+\beta-2)(n+\alpha+\beta-2)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta-3)(2n+\alpha+\beta-2)^{2}(2n+\alpha+\beta-1)}P_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x),\quad\beta>1,\;\alpha>-1.

Proposition 2.1
.

The double Geronimus polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
satisfy the five-term recurrence relation

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n+1}^{gg}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n}^{gg}P_{n}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n-1}^{gg}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{n+1,n-2}^{gg}P_{n-2}^{gg}(x),

where the coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n+1}^{gg}=\frac{C_{n+1}B_{n}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}+B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n+1}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}},\quad\alpha_{n+1,n}^{gg}=\frac{\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}+B_{n}^{2}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}+C^{2}_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n-1}^{gg}=\frac{B_{n}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}+B_{n-1}C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n-1}\lVert P_{n-3}\rVert^{2}_{0}},\quad\alpha_{n+1,n-2}^{gg}=\frac{C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n-2}\lVert P_{n-4}\rVert^{2}_{0}}.

Proof.

Since
(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n+2
and the family
\{P_{k}^{gg}(x)\}_{k\geq 0}
forms a basis, we can express

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x)=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\alpha_{n+1,k}^{gg}P_{k}^{gg}(x),

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,k}^{gg}=\frac{\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}{\langle P_{k}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}.

By orthogonality,
\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}=0
for
k\leq n-3
, reducing the expansion to

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n+1}^{gg}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n}^{gg}P_{n}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n-1}^{gg}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{n+1,n-2}^{gg}P_{n-2}^{gg}(x).

We now compute the numerators in the coefficients using the structure of
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
in terms of the original orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg},P_{n+1}^{gg}\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\int_{E}(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x)P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)\,d\mu_{gg}(x)=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)\,d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=\int_{E}P_{n}^{gg}(x)\left(P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n+1}P_{n}(x)+C_{n+1}P_{n-1}(x)\right)d\mu(x)

\displaystyle=B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert_{0}^{2}+C_{n+1}B_{n}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert_{0}^{2}.

Similarly, we compute the remaining inner products:

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\lVert P_{n}\rVert_{0}^{2}+B_{n}^{2}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert_{0}^{2}+C_{n}^{2}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=B_{n}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert_{0}^{2}+B_{n-1}C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert_{0}^{2},

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}^{gg}(x),P_{n-2}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert_{0}^{2}.

Substituting these expressions into the definition of
\alpha_{n+1,k}^{gg}
yields the desired recurrence coefficients.
∎

3.
Connection formulas

Building on the recurrence structure established for the double Geronimus polynomials
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
in Section
2
, we now employ the connection formulas derived in
[
13
]
to express the Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
in terms of the classical Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
. In particular, by leveraging the Christoffel-Darboux kernel and the determinantal representations introduced in
[
13
]
, we establish a bridge between the recursive framework of
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
and the perturbed orthogonality structure of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
, adapting these analytical tools to the iterated Geronimus-Sobolev context.

The Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
admit the following representation in terms of the orthogonal polynomials
P_{n}(x)
(see
[
13
, Lemma 1]
)

(3.1)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)=P_{n}(x)-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)K_{n-1}(x,a)-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)K_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(x,a),

where
K_{n}(x,y)
denotes the Christoffel–Darboux kernel, given by

\displaystyle K_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\frac{P_{k}(x)P_{k}(y)}{\lVert P_{k}(x)\rVert^{2}_{0}}.

To compute the unknown values
Q_{n}^{M,N}(a)
and
(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)
, we proceed by evaluating equation (
3.1
) at
x=a
, and then differentiating it with respect to
x
, followed by evaluation at
x=a
,

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{M,N}(a)

\displaystyle=P_{n}(a)-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)K_{n-1}(a,a)-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)K_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a),

\displaystyle(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=P_{n}^{\prime}(a)-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(a,a)-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(-1)K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a).

Solving this system of equations yields closed-form expressions for the evaluation and derivative of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
at
x=a
,

(3.2)

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{M,N}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\begin{vmatrix}P_{n}(a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)\\
P_{n}^{\prime}(a)&1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a)\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}1+MK_{n-1}(a,a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)\\
MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(a,a)&1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a)\end{vmatrix}},

(3.3)

\displaystyle(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)

\displaystyle=\frac{\begin{vmatrix}1+MK_{n-1}(a,a)&P_{n}(a)\\
MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(a,a)&P_{n}^{\prime}(a)\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}1+MK_{n-1}(a,a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)\\
MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(a,a)&1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a)\end{vmatrix}}.

Substituting these expressions into (
3.1
) yields the determinantal formula for
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
in terms of the classical polynomials

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)=\frac{\begin{vmatrix}P_{n}(x)&MK_{n-1}(x,a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(x,a)\\
P_{n}(a)&1+MK_{n-1}(a,a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)\\
P_{n}^{\prime}(a)&MK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)&1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a)\end{vmatrix}}{\begin{vmatrix}1+MK_{n-1}(a,a)&NK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)\\
MK_{n-1}^{(0,1)}(a,a)&1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(a,a)\end{vmatrix}}.

The following theorem establishes a critical connection between the Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
\{Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)\}
and the iteratively transformed Geronimus-Jacobi polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

The Sobolev-type orthogonal polynomials
\{Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
and the double Geronimus polynomials
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
satisfy the recurrence relation

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n+1}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n}P_{n}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n-1}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{n+1,n-2}P_{n-2}^{gg}(x),

where the coefficients
\alpha_{n+1,k}
are defined as follows

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n+1}

\displaystyle=\frac{B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}-C_{n+1}(MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)P_{n-1}(a)+N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)P_{n-1}^{\prime}(a))}{C_{n+1}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}},

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}\big(\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}-\big(MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)P_{n-1}(a)+N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)P^{\prime}_{n-1}(a)\big)B_{n}

\displaystyle-\big(MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)P_{n-2}(a)+N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)(P_{n-2})^{\prime}(a)\big)C_{n}\big),

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n-1}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C_{n-1}\lVert P_{n-3}\rVert^{2}_{0}}\big(-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)(P_{n-1}(a)+B_{n-1}P_{n-2}(a)-C_{n-1}P_{n-3}(a))

\displaystyle-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)(P^{\prime}_{n-1}(a)+B_{n-1}P_{n-2}^{\prime}(a)-C_{n-1}P_{n-3}^{\prime}(a))\big),

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,n-2}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{C_{n-2}\lVert P_{n-4}\rVert^{2}_{0}}\big(-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)(P_{n-2}(a)+B_{n-2}P_{n-3}(a)-C_{n-2}P_{n-4}(a))

\displaystyle-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)(P^{\prime}_{n-2}(a)+B_{n-2}(P_{n-3})^{\prime}(a)-C_{n-2}(P_{n-4})^{\prime}(a))\big).

Proof.

We begin by expressing the expansion of
(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
in terms of the orthogonal basis
\{P_{k}^{gg}(x)\}

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\alpha_{n+1,k}P_{k}^{gg}(x),

where

\displaystyle\alpha_{n+1,k}=\frac{\langle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}{\langle P_{k}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}.

We now use the Sobolev inner product (
1.2
) associated with the measure
\mu_{gg}

\displaystyle\langle p,q\rangle_{S}=\int_{E}p(x)q(x)(x-a)^{2}d\mu_{gg}(x)+Mp(a)q(a)+Np^{\prime}(a)q^{\prime}(a).

Substituting
p(x)=Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
and
q(x)=(x-a)^{2}P_{k}^{gg}(x)
, we get

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle\!=\!\langle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),(x-a)^{2}P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}\!=\!\langle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),(x-a)^{2}P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{S}

\displaystyle=0\quad{\mbox{for}}\quad k\leq n-3.

Thus, we achieve

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n+1}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n}P_{n}^{gg}(x)+\alpha_{n+1,n-1}P_{n-1}^{gg}(x)

\displaystyle+\alpha_{n+1,n-2}P_{n-2}^{gg}(x).

To compute the coefficients, we proceed term by term. For
\alpha_{n+1,n+1}
, we use the structure of
P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)

P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)=P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n+1}P_{n}(x)+C_{n+1}P_{n-1}(x),

and since
\langle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{n+1}(x)\rangle_{0}=0
, we find

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}+C_{n+1}\langle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{n-1}(x)\rangle_{0}.

Using the known structure of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
and kernel identities, we compute

\langle Q_{n}^{M,N}(x),P_{n-1}(x)\rangle_{0}=-MQ_{n}^{M,N}(a)P_{n-1}(a)-N(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(a)P_{n-1}^{\prime}(a),

from which
\alpha_{n+1,n+1}
follows directly.

The computations for
\alpha_{n+1,n},\alpha_{n+1,n-1},\alpha_{n+1,n-2}
proceed analogously using the recurrence relations and inner product identities. Each involves combining the orthogonality of
P_{n}(x)
with the structure of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
, and evaluating via the values and derivatives at
x=a
.
∎

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
be the sequence of orthogonal polynomials associated with the double Geronimus transformation of the measure, and let
\{P_{n}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
be the standard orthogonal polynomial sequence. Then, the following connection formula holds

(3.4)

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n+1,n+1}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n+1,n}P_{n}^{gg}(x),\quad n\geq 0,

where the connection coefficients are given by

\displaystyle\sigma_{n+1,n+1}=\frac{B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n+1}\lVert P_{n-1}\rVert^{2}_{0}}\quad{\mbox{and}}\quad\sigma_{n+1,n}=\frac{\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}}{C_{n}\lVert P_{n-2}\rVert^{2}_{0}}.

Proof.

Since
(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x)
is a polynomial of degree
n+2
and
\{P_{n}^{gg}(x)\}_{n\geq 0}
forms a complete basis, we may expand

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\sum_{k=0}^{n+1}\sigma_{n+1,k}P_{k}^{gg}(x),

where the coefficients are given by the orthogonality relation

\displaystyle\sigma_{n+1,k}=\frac{\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}{\langle P_{k}^{gg}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}}.

Using the structure relation for
P_{k}^{gg}(x)

P_{k}^{gg}(x)=P_{k}(x)+B_{k}P_{k-1}(x)+C_{k}P_{k-2}(x),

and orthogonality of
\{P_{n}(x)\}
, we have

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{n}(x),P_{k}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{0}=\langle P_{n}(x),P_{k}(x)+B_{k}P_{k-1}(x)+C_{k}P_{k-2}(x)\rangle_{0}

\displaystyle=0\quad{\mbox{for}}\quad k\leq n-1,

it follows that

\displaystyle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x)=P_{n+2}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n+1,n+1}P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)+\sigma_{n+1,n}P_{n}^{gg}(x).

Computing the non-zero numerators explicitly

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x),P_{n+1}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle\!=\!\langle P_{n}(x),P_{n+1}(x)+B_{n+1}P_{n}(x)+C_{n+1}P_{n-1}(x)\rangle_{0}\!=\!B_{n+1}\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0},

\displaystyle\langle(x-a)^{2}P_{n}(x),P_{n}^{gg}(x)\rangle_{gg}

\displaystyle=\langle P_{n}(x),P_{n}(x)+B_{n}P_{n-1}(x)+C_{n}P_{n-2}(x)\rangle_{0}=\lVert P_{n}\rVert^{2}_{0}.

The result follows by substituting into the definition of
\sigma_{n+1,k}
.
∎

For the special case of the monic Jacobi polynomials
P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
and the double Geronimus transformation applied at
a=-1
, identity (
3.4
) becomes

\displaystyle(1+x)^{2}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)

\displaystyle=P_{n+2}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)+\frac{4(n+\beta)(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta)(2n+\alpha+\beta+2)}P_{n+1}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x)

\displaystyle+\frac{4(n+\beta-1)(n+\beta)(n+\alpha+\beta-1)(n+\alpha+\beta)}{(2n+\alpha+\beta-1)(2n+\alpha+\beta)^{2}(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta-2)}(x).

This identity reveals how the multiplication by
(1+x)^{2}
induces a shift in the Jacobi weight exponent
\beta\mapsto\beta-2
, expressed explicitly in terms of the transformed orthogonal basis.

This section establishes key asymptotic results for Sobolev-Jacobi polynomials

Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
and
P_{n}^{gg}(x)
, derived from iterated Geronimus transformations. These results are critical for understanding the stability and convergence of spectral methods in Sobolev Orthogonal polynomials.

Lemma 4.1
.

(Gamma function Scaling).
For
k,l\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{n^{k-l}\Gamma(n+l)}{\Gamma(n+k)}=1.

Lemma 4.2
.

(Derivative and norm of Jacobi polynomials).
For
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
, the scaled monic Jacobi polynomials
\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x):=\frac{(n+\alpha+\beta+1)_{n}}{2^{n}(\alpha+1)_{n}}P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
satisfy

(4.2)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}(-1)^{n}\frac{(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(k)}(-1)}{n^{-\alpha+\beta+2k}}=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{(-2)^{k}\Gamma(\beta+k+1)},

and

(4.3)

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}n^{2\alpha+1}\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}_{\mu}=2^{\alpha+\beta}(\Gamma(\alpha+1))^{2}.

Proof.

Compute the
kth
derivative of
\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)
and evaluate it at
x=-1
.

\displaystyle(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(k)}(-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1+k)}{2^{k}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1)}\tilde{P}_{n-k}^{(\alpha+k,\beta+k)}(-1)

\displaystyle=\frac{n!}{(\alpha+1)_{n}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1+k)}{2^{k}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1)}(-1)^{n-k}\binom{n+\beta}{n-k}

\displaystyle=\frac{\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1+k)}{2^{k}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1)}\frac{(-1)^{n-k}\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)\Gamma(\beta+k+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(-2)^{k}}\frac{\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+k+1)}\bigg(\frac{(-1)^{n}}{n^{\alpha-\beta-2k}}\frac{n^{\alpha}\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\frac{n^{-k}\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1+k)}{\Gamma(\alpha+\beta+n+1)}

\displaystyle\times\frac{n^{-k-\beta}\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n-k+1)}\bigg).

Using (
4.1
), we obtain the required result (
4.2
).

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}_{\mu}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}}{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}\frac{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{n!\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}\bigg(\frac{n!}{(\alpha+1)_{n}}\bigg)^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}\Gamma(n+1)\Gamma(n+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)(\Gamma(\alpha+1))^{2}}{(2n+\alpha+\beta+1)\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)(\Gamma(n+\alpha+1))^{2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}(\Gamma(\alpha+1))^{2}}{n^{2\alpha+1}}\frac{n^{\alpha+\beta}\Gamma(n+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+\beta+1)}\frac{n^{\alpha-\beta}\Gamma(n+\beta+1)}{\Gamma(n+\alpha+1)}\frac{n}{2n+\alpha+\beta+1}

By using (
4.1
), we arrive at the desired outcome stated in (
4.3
).
∎

Lemma 4.3
.

For
k,s\geq 0
, the derivatives of the Christoffel-Darboux kernel for Jacobi polynomials at
x=a=-1
satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{K_{n-1}^{(k,s)}(-1,-1)}{n^{2\beta+2k+2s+2}}=\frac{(-1)^{k+s}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+k+s+1}(\beta+k+s+1)\Gamma(\beta+k+1)\Gamma(\beta+s+1)}.

This scaling reflects the dominance of high-order terms in Sobolev norms as
n
grows.

Proof.

Apply Stolz’s criterion to convert the limit into a difference quotient

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{K_{n-1}^{(k,s)}(-1,-1)}{n^{2\beta+2k+2s+2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{K_{n-1}^{(k,s)}(-1,-1)-K_{n-2}^{(k,s)}(-1,-1)}{n^{2\beta+2k+2s+2}-(n-1)^{2\beta+2k+2s+2}}.

Apply the Christoffel–Darboux formula to simplify the numerator, and use the binomial expansion to reduce the denominator.

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{K_{n-1}^{(k,s)}(-1,-1)}{n^{2\beta+2k+2s+2}}

\displaystyle=\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\dfrac{(\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(k)}(-1)(\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(s)}(-1)}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}_{\mu}}}{(2\beta+2k+2s+2)n^{2\beta+2k+2s+1}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2\beta+2k+2s+2}\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(k)}(-1)}{n^{-\alpha+\beta+2k}}\frac{(\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(s)}(-1)}{n^{-\alpha+\beta+2s}}\frac{n^{-2\alpha-1}}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n-1}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}_{\mu}}.

By employing (
4.2
) and (
4.3
), we arrive at the desired outcome.
∎

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
j
be a non-negative integer. Then, we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{(j)}(-1)}{(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)}=\frac{j(j-1)}{(\beta+j+1)(\beta+j+2)}

Proof.

Using value of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
from (
3.1
), we have

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{(j)}(-1)}{(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)}

\displaystyle\!=\!\lim_{n\to+\infty}1\!-\!\frac{M\!\left(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)(1\!+\!NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,\!-1))\!-\!N(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)\!K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,\!-1)\right)\!K_{n-1}^{(0,j)}(-1,-1)}{\mathcal{D}\cdot(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)}

\displaystyle-\frac{N\left((1+MK_{n-1}(-1,-1))(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)-MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)\right)K_{n-1}^{(1,j)}(-1,-1)}{\mathcal{D}\cdot(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)},

\displaystyle\text{where }\mathcal{D}=1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)+MK_{n-1}(-1,-1)+MNK_{n-1}(-1,-1)K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle\hskip 9.24994pt-MN\left(K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\right)^{2}.

We denote the expression

M\left(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)\left(1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)\right)-N\left(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\right)^{\prime}(-1)K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\right)K_{n-1}^{(0,j)}(-1,-1)

as
\mathcal{T}_{1}
, and the expression

N\left(\left(1+MK_{n-1}(-1,-1)\right)\left(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\right)^{\prime}(-1)-MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)\right)K_{n-1}^{(1,j)}(-1,-1)

as
\mathcal{T}_{2}
.
Now, we analyze the asymptotic behavior term by term using Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
.

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)^{n}n^{-\alpha+\beta}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{\Gamma(\beta+1)},\quad(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)\sim\frac{(-1)^{n}n^{-\alpha+\beta+2}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{(-2)\Gamma(\beta+2)},

\displaystyle(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)^{n}n^{-\alpha+\beta+2j}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{(-2)^{j}\Gamma(\beta+j+1)},\quad K_{n-1}(-1,-1)\sim\frac{n^{2\beta+2}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+1}(\beta+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)^{2}},

\displaystyle K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)n^{2\beta+4}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+2}(\beta+2)\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(\beta+2)},

\displaystyle K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{n^{2\beta+6}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+3}(\beta+3)\Gamma(\beta+2)^{2}},

\displaystyle K_{n-1}^{(0,j)}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)^{j}n^{2\beta+2j+2}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+j+1}(\beta+j+1)\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(\beta+j+1)}\quad\text{and}

\displaystyle K_{n-1}^{(1,j)}(-1,-1)

\displaystyle\sim\frac{(-1)^{j+1}n^{2\beta+2j+4}}{2^{\alpha+\beta+j+2}(\beta+j+2)\Gamma(\beta+j+1)\Gamma(\beta+2)}.

The dominant term in
\mathcal{D}
is

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\sim MN\left(K_{n-1}(-1,-1)K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)-\left(K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\right)^{2}\right),

which simplifies to

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\sim MN\frac{n^{4\beta+8}}{\left(2^{\alpha+\beta+2}\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(\beta+2)\right)^{2}}\left(\frac{1}{(\beta+1)(\beta+3)}-\frac{1}{(\beta+2)^{2}}\right).

\displaystyle\mathcal{D}\cdot(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{(j)}(-1)\sim\frac{MN(-)^{j}(-1)^{n}n^{-\alpha+5\beta+2j+8}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2^{j}\left(2^{\alpha+\beta+2}\Gamma(\beta+1)\Gamma(\beta+2)\right)^{2}(\beta+1)(\beta+3)(\beta+2)^{2}\Gamma(\beta+j+1)}.

Substituting the asymptotic expansions into
\mathcal{T}_{1}
and
\mathcal{T}_{2}

\displaystyle\mathcal{T}_{1}

\displaystyle\sim-\frac{MN(-1)^{j}(-1)^{n}n^{-\alpha+5\beta+2j+8}\Gamma(\alpha+1)}{2^{2\alpha+2\beta+j+4}\left(\Gamma(\beta+1)\right)^{2}(\Gamma(\beta+2))^{2}(\beta+2)(\beta+3)(\beta+j+1)\Gamma(\beta+j+1)},

Proposition 4.2
.

(Norm asymptotics).
The Sobolev norm satisfy

\displaystyle\lim_{n\to+\infty}\frac{\lVert Q_{n}^{(M,N)}\rVert_{S}}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert_{\mu}}=1

Proof.

The squared Sobolev norm of
Q_{n}^{(M,N)}(x)
is

\displaystyle\lVert Q_{n}^{(M,N)}\rVert^{2}_{S}

\displaystyle=\!\langle Q_{n}^{(M,N)}(x),\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(x)\rangle\!+\!MQ_{n}^{(M,N)}(-1)\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)\!+\!N(Q_{n}^{(M,N)})^{\prime}(-1)(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1).

Dividing by
\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert Q_{n}^{(M,N)}\rVert^{2}_{S}}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}}=1+M\frac{Q_{n}^{(M,N)}(-1)\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}}+N\frac{(Q_{n}^{(M,N)})^{\prime}(-1)(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)}{\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}}.

From the construction of
Q_{n}^{M,N}(x)
, we use

\displaystyle Q_{n}^{M,N}(-1)=\frac{\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)(1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1))-N(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)}{\mathcal{D}},

\displaystyle(Q_{n}^{M,N})^{\prime}(-1)=\frac{(1+MK_{n-1}(-1,-1))(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)-MK_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)}{\mathcal{D}},

\displaystyle\text{where }\mathcal{D}=1+NK_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)+MK_{n-1}(-1,-1)+MN\left(K_{n-1}K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}-\left(K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}\right)^{2}\right).

Using Lemmas
4.2
and
4.3
, substitute the leading asymptotic terms

\displaystyle\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}(-1)\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{-\alpha+\beta}),

\displaystyle\quad(\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)})^{\prime}(-1)\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{-\alpha+\beta+2})

\displaystyle K_{n-1}(-1,-1)\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{2\beta+2}),

\displaystyle\quad K_{n-1}^{(1,0)}(-1,-1)\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{2\beta+4}),

\displaystyle K_{n-1}^{(1,1)}(-1,-1)\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{2\beta+6}),

\displaystyle\quad\lVert\tilde{P}_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{-2\alpha-1}),

where
\mathcal{O}
is Big O notation. The leading term in
\mathcal{D}
is
\mathcal{D}\sim\mathcal{O}(n^{4\beta+8})
. Substituting these asymptotics into (
4.4
)

\displaystyle\frac{\lVert Q_{n}^{(M,N)}\rVert^{2}_{S}}{\lVert P_{n}^{(\alpha,\beta)}\rVert^{2}}=1+\frac{1}{\mathcal{O}(n)}+\frac{1}{\mathcal{O}(n)}.

We obtain the required result as
n\to\infty
.
∎
