Rational curves on cubic hypersurfaces in positive characteristic
Natsume Kitagawa

Abstract
. We study the moduli spaces of rational curves on cubic hypersurfaces in characteristic ≠ 2 , 3 \neq 2,3 . As a result, we prove that for every integer d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 the Kontsevich moduli space of stable maps on a smooth cubic hypersurface X X of degree d d is irreducible if the dimension of X X is greater than or equal to 4 4 .



1.
Introduction

Study of rational curves on Fano varieties is an interesting subject. Rational curves play an important role in classification theory, such as the study of rational connectedness or the minimal model program.
In this paper, we are interested in the irreducibility of the moduli spaces of rational curves on Fano varieties in positive characteristic.
We study cubic hypersurfaces of dimension
\geq 3
, defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2,3
. In the case that the dimension is greater than or equal to
4
, we obtain the following theorem:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 4
defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2,3
. Then for every integer
d\geq 1
, the Kontsevich moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
is irreducible and generically parametrizes free curves.

This result extends
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
which treats the case of characteristic
0
. Generalizing the theorem by Coskun and Starr to positive characteristic presents another difficulty: the existence of inseparable maps. Because of this, we cannot deduce the existence of free rational curves solely from the dominance of the evaluation maps.
To overcome this difficulty, we use the dimension counting argument as in
[
4
]
that calculates the upper bound of the dimension of the non-free locus on
\overline{M}(X,d)
inductively. The result in
[
10
]
on the dimension of fibres of evaluation maps enables us such an argument. Once the existence of free curves is proved, we can employ the proof of
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
and we obtain the above result.

By almost the same dimension counting argument, we also obtain the following result about cubic threefolds.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic threefold defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
\neq 2,3
. Then for every integer
d\geq 1
, the Kontsevich moduli space
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
generically parametrizes free curves.

In characteristic
0
,
Geometric Manin’s Conjecture
, proposed in
[
14
]
, predicts the structure of moduli spaces of rational curves on Fano varieties; namely, the number of irreducible components of a given degree, the dimension of the irreducible components, and so on.

The moduli spaces of rational curves on del Pezzo surfaces in positive characteristic are studied in
[
2
]
from the viewpoint of Geometric Manin’s Conjecture. Let
S
be a del Pezzo surface, and
\overline{\Rat}(S)
be the union of the irreducible components of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(S)
which generically parametrize stable maps from irreducible domains.
For a smooth cubic surface
S
,
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
states that, if
S
is not the Fermat cubic surface defined over a field of characteristic
2
, every dominant component of
\overline{\Rat}(S)
is separable and generically parametrizes free rational curves. Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
partially generalize this result to higher dimensional cases, and give new examples supporting Geometric Manin’s Conjecture in positive characteristic.

Remark 1.3
.

In
[
2
, Remark 1.4]
it is pointed out that the exceptions in
[
2
, Theorem 1.1]
coincide with non-globally F-regular del Pezzo surfaces if the degree of del Pezzo surface is
\geq 2
(See also
[
15
, Section 3]
). We remark on globally F-regularity of smooth cubic hypersurfaces and freeness of lines on non-globally F-regular ones.

By
[
12
, Theorem B]
, a smooth cubic hypersurface is not globally F-regular only if the characteristic of the ground field is
2
. Combining Fedder’s criterion and the argument in the proof of
[
8
, Theorem 4.4]
, we see that a smooth cubic hypersurface which is not globally F-regular is projectively equivalent to the Fermat cubic hypersurface.

Let
X
be the Fermat cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 3
defined over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
2
. Then any line on
X
is non-free (See
[
9
, Proposition 1.2]
for
n=3
and
[
8
, Proof of Theorem 0.4]
for
n\geq 4
). In particular, Theorem
1.1
and Theorem
1.2
do not hold for the Fermat cubic hypersurfaces in characteristic
2
.

1.1.
Related works

In characteristic
0
, there are numerous results about moduli spaces of rational curves on Fano varieties. The work most relevant to the present paper is
[
6
]
, which studied the space of rational curves on smooth cubic hypersurfaces of dimension
\geq 4
defined over
\mathbb{C}
.

[
17
]
extended the results of
[
6
]
to the case of del Pezzo varieties.

Despite the progress in characteristic
0
, there are relatively few results in positive characteristic. The moduli spaces of rational curves on del Pezzo surfaces were studied in
[
2
]
. For affine hypersurfaces of low degree,
[
5
]
proved the irreducibility of the moduli spaces using the circle method.
[
10
]
showed that, for smooth cubic hypersurfaces or smooth complete intersections of two quadrics of dimension
\geq 3
, the moduli spaces of rational curves of a given degree on them have the expected dimensions.
[
16
]
studied the moduli spaces of rational curves on smooth cubic hypersurfaces over finite fields.
[
3
]
showed the separable rational connectedness of low degree Fano hypersurfaces by proving the existence of free lines with essentially sharp assumptions.

Regarding Manin’s conjecture and Geometric Manin’s conjecture,
[
18
]
is a good reference. Also,
[
15
]
provides a nice description of formulations and developments of the conjectures in positive characteristic.

2.
Free lines

We work over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2,3
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 3
. Let
\Lambda_{1}\subset\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
be the locus parametrizing non-free lines. Then
\dim\Lambda_{1}\leq n-2
.

Proof.

This is proved in
[
11
, Chapter 2, Lemma 2.12]
. We give the proof for the convenience of the reader.

Fix a line
L
on
X
. In some coordinate
x_{0},\ldots,x_{n+1}
on
\mathbb{P}^{n+1}
,
L
is written as
L=\{x_{1}=\cdots=x_{n+1}=0\}
. Consider the exact sequence of normal bundles:

0\rightarrow N_{L/X}\rightarrow N_{L/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\rightarrow N_{X/\mathbb{P}^{n+1}}\rightarrow 0.

This is isomorphic to

0\rightarrow N_{L/X}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)^{\oplus n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(3)\rightarrow 0.

Tensoring
\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1)
, we have

0\rightarrow N_{L/X}(-1)\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(2)\rightarrow 0.

Let
\alpha:\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus n}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{L}(2)
be the surjective morphism in the above. Then by
[
7
, Corollary 6.16]
and Euler’s formula,
\alpha
is written as

\alpha=\left(\left.\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}\right|_{L}\right).

Recall that
L
is free (as a rational curve on
X
) if and only if
H^{1}(L,N_{L/X}(-1))=0
. This is equivalent to the condition that the induced map
\alpha:H^{0}(L,\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus n})\rightarrow H^{0}(L,\mathcal{O}_{L}(2))
is surjective.

Since
X
is smooth, the rank of
\alpha:H^{0}(L,\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus n})\rightarrow H^{0}(L,\mathcal{O}_{L}(2))
is at least
2
. Now consider the Gauss map
\gamma_{X}:X\rightarrow X^{*}
and the image
\gamma_{X}(L)
, where
X^{*}
is the dual variety of
X
. If the rank of
\alpha
is
3
(i.e.,
\alpha
is surjective), the image
\gamma_{X}(L)
is a smooth conic and
\gamma_{X}|_{L}:L\rightarrow\gamma_{X}(L)
is injective. If the rank of
\alpha
is
2
,
\gamma_{X}|_{L}:L\rightarrow\gamma_{X}(L)
is a double covering of a line on
X^{*}
.

Let
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}\subset\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,1)
be the family of non-free lines. Since
\gamma_{X}:X\rightarrow X^{*}
is birational, the image
\ev(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1})\subset X
is a proper closed subset, where
\ev:\overline{M}_{(0,1)}(X,1)\rightarrow X
is the evaluation map. Hence the dimension of
\ev(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1})
is at most
n-1
. So it suffices to show that the restriction of the evaluation map
q:=\ev|_{\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}}:\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}\rightarrow\ev(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1})\subset X
is generically finite.

For any
L\in\Lambda_{1}
, let
\iota_{L}:L\rightarrow L
be the covering involution for the restriction of the Gauss map
\gamma_{X}|_{L}:L\rightarrow X^{*}
. Consider a map
\iota_{x}:q^{-1}(x)\rightarrow X;L\rightarrow\iota_{L}(x)
for
x\in\ev(\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1})
. Since
\gamma_{X}(\iota_{x}(L))=\gamma_{X}(x)
for any
L\in q^{-1}(x)
, the image of
\iota_{x}
is a finite set as the Gauss map is a finite morphism.

If
x\neq\iota_{L}(x)
for a line
L\in q^{-1}(x)
, then
L
is the unique line through
x
and
\iota_{L}(x)
. Therefore
\iota_{x}
is injective on the subset of lines
L
in
q^{-1}(x)
satisfying
x\neq\iota_{L}(x)
, and thus this set is finite. Note that, fiberwise with respect to the forgetful map
\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,1)\rightarrow\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
, the set of points
(L,x)\in\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}
satisfying
x=\iota_{L}(x)
is of codimension
1
. Thus, for a general point
x\in X
the dimension of
q^{-1}(x)
cannot be greater than
0
. Therefore
q
is generically finite, and thus the dimension of
\widetilde{\Lambda}_{1}
is at most
n-1
.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 3
. Then the space
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
of lines on
X
is smooth, irreducible and generically parametrizes free lines.

Proof.

The smoothness of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
follows from
[
1
, Corollary 1.12]
. However, in our situation we can prove it in an easier way; let
L
be a line on
X
. By a normal bundle calculation,
N_{L/X}
is isomorphic to either of the following:

\mathcal{O}_{L}^{\oplus 2}\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)^{n-3,},\quad\text{or}\quad\mathcal{O}_{L}(-1)\oplus\mathcal{O}_{L}(1)^{n-2}.

In either case, we obtain
H^{1}(L,N_{L/X})=0
. This implies that
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
is smooth.

By
[
1
, Theorem 1.16 (i)]
,
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
is connected. Since it is smooth, we see that
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
is irreducible.
The last statement follows from Lemma
2.1
since the expected dimension of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,1)
is equal to
2n-4>n-2
.
∎

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 4
. Then a general fibre of the evaluation map
\ev:\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,1)\rightarrow X
is irreducible.

Proof.

For a general point
x\in X
, the proof of
[
10
, Proposition 4.2]
implies that
\ev^{-1}(x)
is a complete intersection. Hence it is connected if
n\geq 4
. By Lemma
2.1
, the union of non-free lines on
X
is a proper closed subset. Therefore by
[
13
, 3.5.4 Corollary]
,
\ev^{-1}(x)
is smooth. Therefore the general fibre is irreducible.
∎

3.
Free curves and the irreducibility

We use the following result proved in
[
10
]
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 3
. For an integer
d\geq 1
, let
\ev_{d}:\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d)\rightarrow X
be the evaluation map. Then there exists a finite set
K\subset X
such that

\displaystyle\dim\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)=\begin{cases}(n-1)d-2\qquad\text{if}\quad x\notin K,\\
(n-1)d-1\qquad\text{if}\quad x\in K.\end{cases}

Proof.

This is proved in
[
10
, Proof of Theorem 1.3]
.
∎

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 4
and let
K\subset X
be a subset as in Proposition
3.1
. If
p\notin K
, the fibre
\ev_{d}^{-1}(p)
generically parametrizes curves with irreducible domains.

Proof.

Using Proposition
3.1
, the proof of
[
6
, Proposition 2.5]
works.
∎

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
X
be a smooth cubic hypersurface of dimension
n\geq 4
. Then for any positive integer
d
, the locus in
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
parametrizing stable maps with at least one non-free component has dimension at most
d(n-1)
. In particular, every component of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
generically parametrizes free curves.

Proof.

We mimic the proof of
[
4
, Theorem 5.1]
.

The proof proceeds by induction on the degree
d
. The case of
d=1
follows from Lemma
2.1
.
Suppose that
d\geq 2
and the assertion holds for every degree less than
d
. Let
\Lambda_{d}\subset\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
be a closed subscheme generically parametrizing stable maps with at least one non-free component. If
\dim\Lambda_{d}\leq 2n-2
, there is nothing to prove. Hence we assume
\dim\Lambda_{d}>2n-2
. Let
Y
be the subvariety of
X
swept out by the image of maps parametrized by
\Lambda_{d}
. Then
\dim Y\leq n
, and thus there are two distinct points of
Y
and at least
1
-dimensional family of stable maps parametrized by
\Lambda_{d}
through these two points. Therefore we can apply the bend and break lemma; we see that the locus
\Lambda^{\prime}_{d}\subset\Lambda_{d}
parametrizing stable maps with reducible domain has codimension at least
1
in
\Lambda_{d}
, and thus there exists a positive integer
e<d
such that the locus of stable maps which decompose as a degree
e
stable map with at least one non-free component glued at a point to a degree
d-e
stable map has dimension at least
\dim\Lambda_{d}-1
.

By induction hypothesis and Proposition
3.1
, the locus
\Lambda^{\prime}_{d}
of glued map has dimension at most

\{e(n-1)\}+\{(d-e)(n-1)-2\}+1=d(n-1)-1,

and thus

\dim\Lambda_{d}\leq\dim\Lambda^{\prime}_{d}+1\leq d(n-1).

So we obtain the desired result for
d
.
∎

Before the proof of Theorem
1.1
, we recall a notion introduced in
[
14
, Definition 5.6]
, which we will use in the proof. Let
M^{\prime\prime}_{i}
be an irreducible component of
\overline{M}_{0,2}(X)
for
i=1,\ldots,r-1
, and
M^{\prime}_{r}
be an irreducible component of
\overline{M}_{0,1}(X)
. Consider the fibre product

M^{\prime\prime}_{1}\times_{X}M^{\prime\prime}_{2}\times_{X}\cdots\times_{X}M^{\prime\prime}_{r-1}\times_{X}M^{\prime}_{r}.

A
main component
is any component of the product which dominates every parameter spaces
M^{\prime\prime}_{i}
and
M^{\prime}_{r}
under each projection map.

Proof of Theorem 1.1.

By Lemma
3.3
, every irreducible component of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
generically parametrizes free curves. In particular, the restriction of the evaluation map to each component is dominant. Moreover, by Lemma
2.3
a general fibre of
\ev:\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,1)\rightarrow X
is irreducible. Then, using Proposition
3.1
we can apply the argument as in
[
6
, Proof of Theorem 1.1]
. We give the proof for completeness.

The case of
d=1
follows from Proposition
2.2
. Let
\ev_{d}:\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d)\rightarrow X
be the evaluation map. Since every component of
\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d)
dominates
X
, it suffices to show the irreducibility of the general fibre of
\ev_{d}
. We prove it by induction on
d
. The case of
d=1
is proved in Lemma
2.3
. Suppose
d\geq 2
and the assertion holds for
e<d
. Let
\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
be a divisor on
\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d)
parametrizing stable maps that decompose as a degree
e
stable map with a marked point and a degree
d-e
stable map glued at a point. Take a general point
x\in X
and let
\pi:\overline{M}_{0,2}(X,e)\rightarrow\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,e)
be the forgetful morphism of second marked points. We show the following claim:

Claim 3.4
.

The main component of
\pi^{-1}(\ev_{e}^{-1}(x))\times_{X}\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d-e)
is unique. Moreover,
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)\cap\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
is irreducible.

Proof.

The general fibre of the first projection

\pi_{1}:\pi^{-1}(\ev_{d}^{-1}(x))\times_{X}\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d-e)\rightarrow\pi^{-1}(\ev_{d}^{-1}(x))

is irreducible by the induction hypothesis. This means the dominant component of
\pi_{1}
is unique.
By Proposition
3.2
, the general map in
\ev_{e}^{-1}(x)
has irreducible domain. Hence
\pi^{-1}(\ev_{e}^{-1}(x))
is irreducible in
\overline{M}_{0,2}(X,e)
since
\ev_{e}^{-1}(x)
is irreducible by the induction hypothesis. Consider the restriction of the glueing map to
\pi^{-1}(\ev_{e}^{-1}(x))\times_{X}\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d-e)
. Note that its image coincides with
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)\cap\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
. By Proposition
3.1
, we conclude that
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)\cap\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
is irreducible.
∎

We now return to the proof of Theorem
1.1
. Let
M
be an irreducible component of
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)
for a general point
x\in X
. By the bend and break lemma,
M
must contain stable maps with reducible domains in codimension
1
. By Claim
3.4
,
M
must contain the entire intersection of at least one boundary component
\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
with
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)
for some
e<d
. Now we show that every component
M
must contain chains of free lines.

Take a general stable map
(f_{2}:C_{2}\rightarrow X,p)\in\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d-e)
where
p\in X
is a marked point. By Proposition
3.1
, for any stable map
(f_{1}:C_{1}\rightarrow X,x,p)\in\pi^{-1}(\ev_{e}^{-1}(x))\subset\overline{M}_{0,2}(X,e)
, the fibre
\pi_{1}^{-1}((f_{2},p))
contains all stable maps consisting of
(f_{1},x,p)
and
(f_{2},p)
glued at
p
. Take
(f_{1},x,p)
such that
f_{1}:L\cup C_{1}^{\prime}\rightarrow X
where
L
is a general line through
x
and
f_{1}|_{C_{1}^{\prime}}
is a degree
e-1
stable map whose image contains
x
and
p
. Then we see that
M
contains a stable map
(f:L\cup C_{1}^{\prime}\cup C_{2}\rightarrow X,x)
.

Consider the projection

\pi_{\{1,d-1\},1}:\ev_{1}^{-1}(x)\times\overline{M}_{0,1}(X,d-1)\rightarrow\ev_{1}^{-1}(x).

By the induction hypothesis, the general fibre of
\pi_{\{1,d-1\},1}
is irreducible. Since the above
L\in\ev_{1}^{-1}(x)
is general, the fibre
\pi_{\{1,d-1\},1}^{-1}(L)
contains the stable map
(f:L\cup C_{1}^{\prime}\cup C_{2}\rightarrow X,x)
. Moreover, it is contained in the unique main component. This implies the intersection of
\Delta_{\{1,\{1\}\},\{d-1,\emptyset\}}
with
M
is nonempty. By Claim
3.4
again,
M
must contain the entire intersection of at least one boundary component
\Delta_{\{e,\{1\}\},\{d-e,\emptyset\}}
with
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)
. In particular, it contains chains of free lines. Since a point representing a chain of free curves is a smooth point in
M
, we obtain the irreducibility of
\ev_{d}^{-1}(x)
. Hence
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
is irreducible.
∎

4.
Free curves on cubic threefolds

Let
X
be a smooth cubic threefold defined over an algebraically closed field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2,3
. By the same argument as in Lemma
3.3
, we can prove that
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
generically parametrizes free rational curves for any
d\geq 1
. However, by a dimension reason, we have to check the case of the space of conics
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,2)
directly.

Lemma 4.1
.

The space
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,2)
consists of two irreducible components
R_{2}
,
N_{2}
, where
R_{2}
generically parametrizes birational stable maps from irreducible curves to smooth conics and
N_{2}
parametrizes degree
2
covers from
\mathbb{P}^{1}
to lines. Both of them generically parametrize free curves.

Proof.

By
[
14
, Proof of Proposition 7.4]
,
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,2)
consists of two components
R_{2}
,
N_{2}
. Clearly
N_{2}
parametrizes free curves. Glueing two free lines, we see that
R_{2}
generically parametrizes free curves.
∎

Proof of Theorem 1.2.

As in Lemma
3.3
, it suffices to show that the locus in
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,d)
parametrizing stable maps with at least one non-free component has dimension at most
2d-1
.
The base case of induction is
d=2
because we cannot use the bend and break lemma for the space of conics by a dimension reason. By Lemma
4.1
, each component of
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,2)
generically parametrizes free curves. Hence the locus parametrizing stable maps with at least one non-free component in
\overline{M}_{0,0}(X,2)
has dimension at most the expected dimension minus
1
, which is equal to
3=2\cdot(3-1)-1
, thus the assertion holds. The rest is the same as the proof of Lemma
3.3
.
∎
