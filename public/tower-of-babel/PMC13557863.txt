Association between oxidative stress index and lung cancer risk in idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis: an exploratory retrospective analysis
Authors: Kiyofumi Shimoji, Tetsu Hirakawa, Kakuhiro Yamaguchi, Shinjiro Sakamoto, Yasushi Horimasu, Takeshi Masuda, Taku Nakashima, Hironobu Hamada, Hiroshi Iwamoto
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundIdiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a significant risk factor for lung cancer. However, early detection is often hindered by background fibrotic changes. This study assessed whether the oxidative stress index (OSI), which reflects the balance between systemic oxidative stress and antioxidant capacity, is associated with lung cancer development in patients with IPF.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 90 patients with newly diagnosed IPF. Serum levels of derivatives of reactive oxygen metabolites (d-ROMs) and biological antioxidant potential (BAP) were measured at diagnosis to calculate the OSI (100 × d-ROMs/BAP). Using a 1-year landmark approach (n=83), the primary endpoint was lung cancer development during the follow-up period.ResultsFifteen patients (18.1%) developed lung cancer. Although individual d-ROMs showed no significant differences between cohorts, the baseline OSI was significantly higher in the lung cancer group than in the non-lung cancer group (P<0.001). Receiver operating characteristic analysis demonstrated high discriminative capacity of the OSI (area under the curve: 0.857), with an optimal cut-off of 14.39. Multivariate Cox regression analysis indicated an independent association between the OSI and lung cancer development, even after adjusting for antifibrotic use and macrophage proportion in bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF). Notably, the OSI did not significantly predict all-cause mortality, suggesting its relative specificity for carcinogenesis in this population.ConclusionsBaseline OSI may serve as a potential biomarker associated with future lung cancer development in patients with IPF, independent of antifibrotic agent use and BALF macrophage proportion. Further large-scale prospective studies are needed to validate whether OSI-guided risk stratification can help optimize surveillance strategies for this high-risk population.

Highlight box

Key findings

• The oxidative stress index (OSI), calculated from derivatives of reactive oxygen metabolites (d-ROMs) and biological antioxidant potential (BAP), was associated with future lung cancer development in patients with idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF). This finding suggests that systemic redox imbalance may provide biological information related to carcinogenesis risk in this population.

What is known and what is new?

• Patients with IPF have a significantly higher risk of developing lung cancer than those without IPF. However, early diagnosis is notoriously difficult because fibrotic structural distortions (e.g., honeycombing) obscure nodules on conventional imaging, and existing tumor markers lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity.

• Our findings suggest that OSI, an integrated marker of oxidative stress and antioxidant capacity, may help identify patients with IPF who are at increased risk of developing lung cancer. Unlike imaging-based assessment, OSI provides systemic biological information that is not directly affected by fibrotic structural distortion.

What is the implication, and what should change now?

• OSI may serve as a minimally invasive blood-based marker for identifying patients with IPF who are at increased risk of lung cancer. Given the exploratory nature of this single-center retrospective study, large-scale, multicenter prospective studies are needed before OSI can be incorporated into routine clinical practice. If validated, OSI-guided risk stratification could help optimize surveillance strategies and facilitate earlier detection of lung cancer in this high-risk population.

Introduction

Background

Idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis (IPF) is a refractory disease characterized by progressive pulmonary fibrosis due to chronic alveolar epithelial damage and abnormal repair. Median survival is approximately 3–5 years, with a 5-year survival rate of 20–40% (1,2). IPF directly affects life expectancy and is a potent risk factor for primary lung cancer development, with an incidence substantially higher than that observed in patients without IPF (3,4). Furthermore, management of lung cancer associated with IPF is often constrained by the high-risk of acute exacerbations of the underlying fibrosis, limiting indications for surgical resection, radiotherapy, and chemotherapy (5-7). Consequently, disease progression is often faster in IPF cases than in non-IPF cases (8).

To date, several clinical risk factors for lung cancer in patients with IPF, such as a history of heavy smoking and emphysema, have been well documented (9). Furthermore, IPF and lung cancer share pathological pathways, including telomere abnormalities, MUC5B promoter polymorphisms, and p53 mutations, suggesting a strong biological link (3). Despite these shared biological mechanisms, early detection of lung cancer in IPF remains clinically challenging. The characteristic structural distortions inherent to IPF, such as progressive fibrosis and honeycombing, often obscure small malignant nodules on conventional imaging, causing diagnostic delays (10). Moreover, existing tumor markers lack sufficient sensitivity and specificity for early diagnosis or cancer onset prediction (11). Consequently, reliable, minimally invasive biomarkers that enable early detection or prediction of lung cancer independent of the background fibrotic changes are urgently needed.

Excessive reactive oxygen species (ROS) production by chronic inflammation directly destroys lung tissue and promotes fibrosis (12,13) while driving carcinogenesis by inducing DNA damage (14). Derivatives of reactive oxygen metabolites (d-ROMs) and biological antioxidant potential (BAP) tests enable simple, objective evaluation of systemic oxidative stress and antioxidant capacity using micro-volume blood samples (15). Previous studies have suggested that serum oxidative stress assessed by d-ROMs may reflect disease severity and progression in IPF (16,17). In patients with lung cancer, oxidative stress measured using d-ROMs has been associated with lung cancer stage or nodal involvement (18), whereas perioperative redox balance assessed using both d-ROMs and BAP has been associated with long-term postoperative outcomes (19). However, the clinical utility of these redox-related biomarkers remains investigational.

Rationale and knowledge gap

Whether alterations in this redox balance can predict future lung cancer development in patients with IPF remains unclear.

Objective

We hypothesized that an imbalance between IPF-derived oxidative stress and systemic antioxidant capacity may reflect lung cancer risk. The aim of this study was to investigate whether the oxidative stress index (OSI), that is, the balance between d-ROMs and BAP, can serve as a biomarker for lung cancer development in patients with IPF. We present this article in accordance with the STROBE reporting checklist (available at https://tlcr.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/tlcr-2026-0585/rc).

Methods

Patients and study design

This retrospective study used data from a prospectively maintained institutional interstitial lung disease registry at Hiroshima University Hospital. Consecutive patients diagnosed with IPF between January 2003 and December 2020 were screened for eligibility. Baseline clinical data, including patient characteristics, laboratory findings, chest high-resolution computed tomography (HRCT) images, and pulmonary function test (PFT) results, were collected at initial diagnosis. Among 135 consecutive patients identified from the registry, 90 met the following inclusion criteria and were enrolled in the present study: (I) written informed consent for participation in the registry and availability of stored serum samples, (II) no history or evidence of primary lung cancer at IPF diagnosis, (III) no history of acute exacerbation of IPF before sample collection, and (IV) complete PFT data (Figure 1). For both lung cancer development and all-cause mortality analyses, a 1-year landmark approach was used to minimize the effect of occult lung cancer at IPF diagnosis or early mortality. Considering the typical lung cancer doubling time in patients with interstitial lung disease (20), a 1-year landmark approach was used to reduce the potential influence of pre-existing subclinical lung cancer at baseline. After excluding 7 patients who developed lung cancer, died, or dropped out within the first year, 83 patients who survived without lung cancer for at least 1 year after initial IPF diagnosis were analyzed (Figure 1). The 1-year landmark was defined as time zero for longitudinal analyses. IPF diagnosis was established by two respiratory specialists based on multidisciplinary analyses of clinical, radiological, and pathological findings in accordance with the ATS/ERS/JRS/ALAT clinical practice guidelines (1,2). Corticosteroids, immunosuppressant, or antifibrotic agent use was defined as the initiation of these therapies within the 1-year landmark period. Bronchoalveolar lavage was performed during the initial diagnostic process. Among the 83 patients in the 1-year landmark analysis, bronchoalveolar lavage fluid (BALF) data were available for 71 patients (12 in the lung cancer development (LC) and 59 in the non-lung cancer development (non-LC) group). BALF differential cell counts were used to determine the proportion of macrophages, neutrophils, lymphocytes, and eosinophils. The study was conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. The study was approved by the Ethics Committee of Hiroshima University Hospital (Approval No. E2004-0326), and informed consent was obtained from all individual participants.

Patient selection flowchart. Of the 135 patients screened, 27 were excluded because stored blood samples were unavailable, and 18 were excluded because of acute exacerbation of IPF around the time of sample collection or missing pulmonary function data. Seven patients were excluded from the cohort eligible for the 1-year landmark analysis: three who developed lung cancer within the first year, three who died within 1 year, and one who dropped out during the initial year. Consequently, the final analysis included 83 patients for assessment of lung cancer development. IPF, idiopathic pulmonary fibrosis.

Measurement of d-ROMs and BAP

At initial IPF diagnosis, serum samples were centrifuged at 300 ×g for 5 min at 4 °C and stored at –80 °C until use. d-ROMs and BAP levels were measured using a free radical elective evaluator system (REDOXLIBRA; Wismerll Co., Ltd., Tokyo, Japan). Briefly, for d-ROMs measurement, 20 µL of serum was added to 1 mL of assay reagent (Wismerll Co., Ltd.) and incubated at 37 °C for 1 min. Hydroperoxides were subsequently measured spectrophotometrically at 505 nm. Results were expressed in Carratelli units (U.CARR), where 1 U.CARR equals 0.08 mg/100 mL H2O2. For BAP measurement, 10 µL of serum was added to 1 mL of reagent (Wismerll Co., Ltd.), and reduced trivalent iron was measured over 5 min in µmol/L. To evaluate the balance between oxidative stress and antioxidant capacity, the OSI was calculated as follows: OSI = 100 × d-ROMs/BAP.

Statistical analyses

Continuous variables are presented as medians and interquartile ranges (IQR) and were compared between groups using the Mann-Whitney U test. Categorical variables were compared using Pearson’s chi-squared test. Correlations between parameters were evaluated using Spearman’s rank correlation coefficient. Cox regression analyses were used to evaluate factors associated with lung cancer development or all-cause mortality. In lung cancer analyses, patients were censored if they expired without lung cancer or were lost to follow-up. In all-cause mortality analyses, patients were censored only if alive at study end or lost to follow-up. Optimal cutoff values for lung cancer prediction were determined using receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curves. Cumulative incidence rates of lung cancer were estimated using the Kaplan-Meier method and were compared between groups using the log-rank test. Statistical analyses were performed using JMP (version 18.2.1; SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA), with P<0.05 considered significant.

Results

Patient characteristics

In the 1-year landmark analysis, 15 patients developed lung cancer and 68 did not. The median follow-up period after the 1-year landmark was 49 months (IQR, 22–70 months). Baseline patient characteristics at initial IPF diagnosis—including age, sex, medical treatments (corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or antifibrotic agents), pulmonary function, and serum biomarker levels—showed no significant differences between LC and non-LC groups except for smoking history (pack-years) and BALF neutrophil proportion (Table 1). HRCT findings, including emphysema presence and usual interstitial pneumonia (UIP) pattern distribution, did not significantly differ (definite or probable vs. indeterminate UIP pattern) between groups. Notably, all 15 lung cancer cases arose within pre-existing fibrotic areas. Comparison of oxidative stress markers and correlation analysis

Baseline oxidative stress marker levels were assessed in the 1-year landmark analysis. The median d-ROM level was 313.0 (IQR, 269.0–384.0) U.CARR in the LC group and 301.5 (IQR, 253.5–347.7) U.CARR in the non-LC group, with no significant difference between groups (P=0.45; Figure 2A). The median BAP level was significantly lower in the LC group than in the non-LC group [1,920.0 (IQR, 1,605.0–2,052.0) vs. 2,224.5 (IQR, 2,003.7–2,912.0) µmol/L, P<0.001], whereas the median OSI level was significantly higher in the LC group [17.1 (IQR, 15.4–20.2) vs. 12.4 (IQR, 10.3–14.6), P<0.001; Figure 2B,2C]. Furthermore, a weak but significant positive correlation was observed between d-ROMs and BAP levels (rs=0.39, P<0.001; Figure 2D). OSI showed no significant correlations with baseline characteristics, including age, smoking history, pulmonary function, or other laboratory data (Table 2). Moreover, baseline OSI levels did not differ significantly regardless of sex, smoking history, medical treatment, emphysema, UIP pattern, or pulmonary function (Table 3).

Discriminative capacity of OSI for lung cancer development and cumulative incidence analysis

ROC curve analysis demonstrated the discriminative potential of OSI for lung cancer development, with an area under the curve of 0.857 (Figure 3A). At the optimal cutoff value of 14.39, the sensitivity and specificity were 93.3 and 75.0%, respectively. Lung cancer incidence was significantly higher in the high-OSI group (OSI ≥14.39) than in the low-OSI group [OSI <14.39; 45.1% (14/31 cases) vs. 1.7% (1/52 cases), P<0.001; Figure 3B]. Kaplan-Meier analysis revealed a significantly higher cumulative lung cancer incidence in the high-OSI group than in the low-OSI group (log-rank test, P<0.001; Figure 3C). Additional analyses were performed to evaluate the discriminative ability of BAP alone. ROC curve analysis showed that BAP predicted lung cancer development with an AUC of 0.803 and an optimal cutoff value of 1973.00 µmol/L, yielding a sensitivity of 73.3% and a specificity of 79.4% (Figure S2A). Patients with low BAP levels (BAP <1,973.00 µmol/L) exhibited a significantly higher incidence of lung cancer than those with high BAP levels (BAP ≥1973.00 µmol/L) [41.6% (10/24 cases) vs. 8.4% (5/59 cases), P<0.001; Figure S2B], and Kaplan-Meier analysis confirmed a significantly higher cumulative incidence of lung cancer in the low-BAP group (log-rank test, P <0.001, Figure S2C).

Association of OSI with lung cancer risk adjusted for potential confounders

In our exploratory models, multivariate Cox regression analysis showed that OSI remained significantly associated with lung cancer development, even after adjusting for potential confounders such as antifibrotic agent use and BALF macrophage proportion [hazard ratio (HR) 1.10, 95% confidence interval (CI): 1.03–1.17, P <0.001; HR 1.07, 95% CI: 1.00–1.15, P=0.02, respectively; Table 4]. During a follow-up after the 1-year landmark, 53 of 83 patients (63.9%) died. Univariate Cox regression analysis identified antifibrotic agent use, percent forced vital capacity (%FVC), percent vital capacity (%VC), and BALF neutrophil and lymphocyte proportions as factors significantly associated with all-cause mortality (Table S1). However, OSI was not significantly associated with mortality. Multivariate Cox regression analysis for all-cause mortality included antifibrotic agent use, %FVC, and BALF neutrophil proportion as covariates. However, none of these factors showed a significant independent association with mortality. Furthermore, OSI was evaluated for its potential association with all-cause mortality by stratifying patients into high- and low-OSI groups based on the optimal cutoff value (14.39) derived from the ROC analysis for lung cancer development. Kaplan-Meier analysis and the log-rank test revealed no significant survival difference between these groups (P=0.62; Figure S3).

Discussion

Key findings

The primary finding of this exploratory study is that the OSI at initial diagnosis in patients with IPF is potentially associated with lung cancer development. Our results suggest that disruption of redox balance in patients with IPF might be linked to carcinogenesis risk.

Strengths and limitations

A primary strength of this study is its direct focus on the clinical challenges of early lung cancer detection in patients with IPF. Current diagnostic modalities are often insufficient; while conventional imaging is frequently obscured by structural distortions like honeycombing, existing tumor markers also lack the necessary sensitivity and specificity for reliable early diagnosis or prediction of lung cancer onset. By utilizing d-ROMs and BAP tests, our approach enables an objective and minimally invasive evaluation of systemic oxidative stress. This approach may provide biological information that complements imaging and traditional biomarkers in this high-risk population.

This study had several limitations that warrant consideration. First, this single-center retrospective study with a small sample size may be subject to statistical overfitting, necessitating multicenter validation to confirm the generalizability of these findings. Second, the analysis relied on a single baseline measurement of oxidative stress markers at IPF diagnosis, leaving the impact of longitudinal changes on predictive performance unexamined. Similarly, reliable evaluation of longitudinal changes in pulmonary function parameters, including FVC and VC, was not feasible because serial PFTs were not performed at standardized intervals in this retrospective cohort.

Third, owing to the retrospective nature of this study, the frequency and intervals of chest computed tomography scans for lung cancer surveillance were not strictly standardized and left to the discretion of individual attending physicians. This variability in monitoring intensity may have affected the precise timing of lung cancer detection. However, patients underwent regular chest X-ray examinations during outpatient visits, providing a clinical framework that facilitated detection of lung cancer events. Fourth, antioxidant capacity may be affected by exogenous factors such as diet, supplements, and medications beyond corticosteroids, immunosuppressants, or antifibrotic agents. These variables were excluded from the multivariate analysis owing to difficulties in comprehensive data collection.

Comparison with similar research

Previous studies have evaluated redox-related biomarkers in both IPF and lung cancer. In patients with IPF, serum oxidative stress assessed by d-ROMs has been reported to reflect disease severity and progression (16,17). However, these studies primarily focused on fibrotic disease activity or clinical outcomes in IPF rather than on predicting lung cancer development. Similarly, studies in patients with lung cancer have suggested that oxidative stress markers, mainly assessed by d-ROMs, may be associated with lung cancer stage or nodal involvement (18), whereas perioperative redox balance assessed using both d-ROMs and BAP may be associated with postoperative outcomes (19). However, these studies did not address whether baseline redox status could predict subsequent lung cancer development in patients with underlying IPF. In contrast, the present study specifically investigated whether redox balance, expressed as the OSI using both d-ROMs and BAP at the time of IPF diagnosis, was associated with subsequent lung cancer development. Therefore, our findings extend previous observations by suggesting that systemic redox imbalance may serve not only as a marker of IPF disease activity or lung cancer progression but also as a potential biological indicator of carcinogenesis risk in patients with IPF.

Individual oxidative stress assessment showed no significant difference in d-ROM levels, whereas BAP levels differed significantly between the LC and non-LC groups. BAP alone also showed good discriminative ability for lung cancer development, suggesting that reduced antioxidant capacity may have substantially contributed to the association between redox imbalance and lung cancer risk in this cohort. However, redox status is defined by the dynamic equilibrium between oxidative and antioxidant capacities (21,22). Accordingly, OSI may provide an integrated assessment of redox imbalance by incorporating both oxidative stress burden and antioxidant capacity, rather than evaluating either component alone. Consistent with this rationale, OSI demonstrated a higher AUC and higher sensitivity than BAP alone, supporting its potential value as an integrated marker of carcinogenesis-related redox imbalance. These findings suggest that by integrating the interplay between both components, OSI may better reflect the oxidative stress state that potentially acts as the “common soil” for carcinogenesis in IPF.

Heavy smoking and comorbid emphysema have been recognized as clinical risk factors for lung cancer development in patients with IPF (9). In this cohort, emphysema prevalence did not differ between the groups, whereas smoking history (pack-years) differed significantly. However, smoking history (pack-years) was not significantly associated with lung cancer development in the Cox regression analysis. This may reflect the high overall smoking prevalence (approximately 89%) among study participants, which likely homogenized baseline risk. Although these established clinical factors are useful for risk assessment, they primarily reflect demographic or structural disease characteristics. By contrast, OSI may provide a biological assessment of carcinogenesis-related redox imbalance, thereby complementing established clinical risk factors.

Furthermore, the potential of antifibrotic agents such as nintedanib to suppress lung cancer development remains debatable (23), whereas pirfenidone possesses direct ROS-scavenging capabilities (24), and corticosteroids modulate oxidative stress via inflammatory pathways (25,26). However, in this study, these medical interventions did not significantly affect baseline OSI levels or lung cancer risk. These results suggest that baseline redox balance measured by OSI reflects the inherent carcinogenesis risk in IPF, independent of antifibrotic therapy. This indicates that the observed OSI variation in this study reflects persistent redox imbalance inherent to IPF rather than transient pharmacological fluctuations.

Explanations of findings

Analysis of the relationship between OSI and various clinical parameters (Tables 2,3) reinforces that OSI is an independent biological indicator rather than a surrogate for existing factors. OSI remained independent of primary markers of disease severity (%FVC), age, and cumulative smoking history. Notably, preserved lung function (%FVC and %VC ≥80%) was not a significant risk factor for lung cancer development. However, preserved lung function (%FVC ≥80% or %VC ≥80%) was associated with better survival in the univariate analyses. This paradox likely reflects competing risks between respiratory failure and malignancy in IPF. Patients with preserved lung function are less likely to die from early respiratory complications, remaining at risk for lung cancer over a longer clinical course of IPF. Furthermore, less severe fibrotic changes in these patients may facilitate earlier detection of neoplastic nodules on computed tomography imaging, which are often obscured by extensive honeycombing in advanced disease. These findings emphasize that even in patients with relatively mild physiological impairment, a high baseline OSI identifies a subgroup at increased risk of carcinogenesis. Notably, the OSI was not significantly associated with all-cause mortality (Table S1). Similarly, lung cancer development itself was not significantly associated with all-cause mortality in this cohort. This may be partly attributable to the high competing mortality risk inherent to IPF. Among the 53 deaths in the 1-year landmark cohort, most were caused by acute exacerbation of IPF, chronic respiratory failure, or pneumonia, whereas only four deaths were directly attributed to lung cancer. Therefore, the predominance of IPF-related and respiratory causes of death may have attenuated the apparent impact of lung cancer development on all-cause mortality, particularly given the small number of lung cancer events. Accordingly, the OSI may more specifically reflect the risk of subsequent lung cancer development rather than overall survival or general disease progression.

Implications and actions needed

As fibrotic lesion changes in IPF often hinder early cancer detection on imaging (10), OSI, a blood-based marker, may help identify patients with IPF who are at increased risk of lung cancer and may benefit from closer surveillance. Nevertheless, the present findings are exploratory and should not be interpreted as sufficient evidence to support the immediate incorporation of OSI into routine clinical practice. Further large-scale, multicenter prospective studies are needed to validate these findings and clarify the clinical utility of OSI-guided surveillance strategies in patients with IPF.

Conclusions

Our exploratory study suggests that integrated baseline assessment of redox balance using the OSI may serve as a potential biomarker for predicting future lung cancer development in patients with IPF. These findings support the need for further large-scale, multicenter prospective studies to validate the predictive performance of OSI and to determine whether OSI-guided risk stratification can optimize surveillance strategies for lung cancer in IPF.
