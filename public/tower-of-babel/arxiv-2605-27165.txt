Weighted Riesz–Kolmogorov criterion and multilinear extrapolation of compactness on variable Lebesgue spaces
Spyridon Kakaroumpas, Stefanos Lappas

Abstract
. This paper addresses a novel weighted Riesz–Kolmogorov theorem and the extrapolation of multilinear compact operators in the context of weighted variable Lebesgue spaces. We establish the latter result via our Riesz–Kolmogorov theorem which yields a weighted interpolation theorem for multilinear compact operators in the variable Lebesgue setting. In proving this, we also show a weighted interpolation theorem in mixed-norm variable Lebesgue spaces. By means of our extrapolation result, we obtain new weighted compactness estimates for the commutators of multilinear ω \omega -Calderón–Zygmund operators, multilinear fractional integrals and multilinear Fourier multipliers on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces. Our work generalizes several recent ones, including but not limited to those of Cao, Olivo and Yabuta in the setting of multilinear operators acting on the classical weighted Lebesgue spaces as well as the previous result by the authors in the setting of bilinear operators and variable Lebesgue spaces.

1.
Introduction

This paper focuses on two new compactness results: the weighted Riesz–Kolmogorov characterization of total boundedness (or equivalently precompactness/relatively compactness) theorem and the extrapolation theorem for multilinear compact operators in the variable exponent setting. Let us begin by first recalling some historical facts related to the Riesz–Kolmogorov compactness criteria.

The theory of variable Lebesgue spaces has seen substantial progress over the past few years, with systematic treatments provided in
[
18
,
14
]
. One important aspect of this theory concerns the characterization of totally bounded sets. In the classical constant
L^{p}
Lebesgue spaces, relative compactness is characterized by the Riesz–Kolmogorov theorem, see, for instance,
[
30
,
50
,
47
,
53
,
49
]
. We remark that this criterion was further extended (when
0<p<1
) in
[
52
]
. A weighted
L^{p}(v)
version of Riesz–Kolmogorov theorem for
p>1
was studied in
[
11
]
, and was further extended to the case
0<p<1
(see
[
54
,
9
]
). In recent years, Riesz–Kolmogorov type theorems have also been generalized to variable exponent
L^{p(\cdot)}
Lebesgue spaces (see
[

]
in the Euclidean setting and
[

,
1
]
in the setting of metric measure spaces), to variable exponent

Morrey spaces on a bounded doubling metric measure space (see
[

]
), to Banach/quasi-Banach function spaces (see
[

]
and
[

,

[

In this paper, we will focus in proving a weighted variable
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
Riesz–Kolmogorov theorem. To state it we need the following definition of variable Muckenhoupt
\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}

1.1 Definition

(
[
13
]
, (1.3) in page 365)
.

Given an exponent
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}
and weight
w
, we say that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
if

[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}}:=\sup_{Q}|Q|^{-1}\|w\chi_{Q}\|_{p(\cdot)}\|w^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}<\infty,

where the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Notice that
w\in\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
if and only if
u=w^{p(\cdot)}\in A_{p(\cdot)}
, with
[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}}=[u]_{A_{p(\cdot)}}
. Here, we define
[u]_{A_{p(\cdot)}}:=\sup_{Q}|Q|^{-1}\|u^{\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}}\chi_{Q}\|_{p(\cdot)}\|u^{-\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}}\chi_{Q}\|_{p^{\prime}(\cdot)}<\infty
and this is the non-symmetric definition of the variable Muckenhoupt
\mathcal{A}_{p(\cdot)}
class (see
[
13
, page 364]
). By choosing
p(\cdot)=p=\text{constant}
in the latter definition, one recovers the classical Muckenhoupt
A_{p}
class of weights introduced in
[
43
]
.

Our first main result is the following new weighted version of the Riesz–Kolmogorov criterion in the variable exponent setting (see Section
2
for a detailed description of the definitions and notations used).

1.2 Theorem
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}
,
\widetilde{q}\in(0,p_{-})
,
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
w^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{p(\cdot)}{\widetilde{q}}}
. Then, the family
\mathcal{F}\subset L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
is totally bounded (or equivalently precompact/relatively compact) if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(i)

\mathcal{F}
is uniformly bounded, i.e.,

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\infty

or equivalently, there exists
M>0
such that for all
f\in\mathcal{F}

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\;w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq M;

(ii)

\mathcal{F}
is
\widetilde{q}
-equicontinuous, i.e.,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(\cdot,r)}|f(\cdot)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}=0

or equivalently,

\lim_{r\rightarrow 0}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=0;

(iii)

\mathcal{F}
uniformly vanishes at infinity, i.e.,

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R)}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}=0

or equivalently,

\lim_{R\rightarrow\infty}\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R)}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\;w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=0.

.

(1)

By choosing
\widetilde{q}=1

(2)

Let us briefly outline the core mechanism behind the proof of Theorem
1.2
, which will be presented in Section
3

[

and relies on three fundamental ingredients: Lemma

will imply an interpolation of compactness result (see Theorem

Having stated our first main result, we now proceed with the main extrapolation result for multilinear compact operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces. We first provide a brief historical overview of this topic.

One of the fundamental tools for proving weighted
L^{p}
boundedness results in harmonic analysis is the extrapolation theorem of Rubio de Francia
[
48
]
. A systematic study of this theory for linear and multilinear operators on weighted Lebesgue spaces is presented in
[
16
]
and
[
21
,
15
,
45
,
35
,
34
]
, respectively. There is now a well-developed extrapolation theory for variable exponent spaces in both the linear and multilinear settings; see
[
17
,
8
,
44
]
. Nevertheless, it remains an open problem whether one can establish the precise linear and multilinear counterparts of Rubio de Francia’s extrapolation of boundedness on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces (see
[
33
, Remark 1.7]
).

In the past few years, the study of extending extrapolation results to compact operators has been the focus of considerable research activity. In the linear setting, the first work addressing this problem was obtained by Hytönen and the second named author in
[
26
]
. Subsequently, several extensions have been developed in different settings, including weighted Morrey spaces
[
32
]
, two-weight inequalities
[
37
]
, Banach function spaces
[
39
]
(see also
[
33
]
, where an alternative proof for the compactness of linear operators is provided in the framework of weighted variable Lebesgue spaces), and one-sided situations
[
41
]
. Furthermore, the first extrapolation results for multilinear compact operators in the context of classical weighted Lebesgue spaces were obtained by Cao, Olivo, and Yabuta in
[
9
]
(see also
[
27
]
by Hytönen and the second named author). The latter was further extended to bilinear operators acting on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces by us
[
28
]
. Motivated by the aforementioned works, our second main result is the following abstract extrapolation of compactness principle for multilinear operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces.

1.4 Theorem
.

Let
\Theta
be a collection of pairs
(\vec{Y},Y)
with
\vec{Y}=(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
a
m
-tuple of Banach spaces and
Y
a quasi-Banach space. Let
T
be a
m
-linear operator defined and

\text{bounded}\quad T:Y_{1}\times\ldots\times Y_{m}\to Y\quad\text{for {all}}\quad(\vec{Y},Y)\in\Theta

as well as

\text{compact}\quad T:X_{1}\times\ldots\times X_{m}\to X\quad\text{for {some}}\quad(\vec{X},X)\in\Theta.

Then
T
is

\text{compact}\quad T:Z_{1}\times\ldots\times Z_{m}\to Z\quad\text{for {all}}\quad(\vec{Z},Z)\in\Theta,

in each of the following cases:

(1)

There exist fixed constants
t\in(0,\infty)
and
\gamma\in\left[0,\infty\right)
such that
\Theta
consists of all pairs
(\vec{Y},Y)
of the form

\displaystyle Y_{j}=L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle Y=L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}}),

where
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{1},\infty)
is a proper
m
-admissible quadruple with

t<\min\{(p_{j})_{-}:\penalty\ j=1,\ldots,m\}

and

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma,

and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
is a
m
-tuple of weights such that
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
and
\vec{w}^{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{\vec{p}(\cdot)}{t},\frac{q(\cdot)}{t}}
.

(2)

There exist a fixed constant
\gamma\in\left[0,\infty\right)
, a fixed
m
-tuple
\vec{r}\in[1,\infty)^{m}
and a fixed constant
s\in(0,\infty]
such that
\Theta
consists of all pairs
(\vec{Y},Y)
of the form

\displaystyle Y_{j}=L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle Y=L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}}),

where
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
is a proper
m
-admissible quadruple with

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma,

and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
is a
m
-tuple of weights such that
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}
.

1.5 Remark
.

In contrast to
[
28
]
, we do not restrict ourselves to bilinear operators, and moreover, we explicitly allow that our operators map into quasi-Banach spaces rather than just Banach ones. Furthermore, setting
t=1
and taking all variable exponents to be constant in Theorem
1.4
, allows us to recover and extend several results of
[

,

,

]
.

The main ingredients needed for the proof of Theorem
1.4
are three-fold. First, we rely on our Riesz–Kolmogorov type Theorem
1.2
. Second, we need to understand the interpolation of compactness for multilinear operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces, see Theorem
4.28
. This is achieved through delicate technical work in Section
4
, see, especially, the weighted interpolation of boundedness Theorems
4.1
and
4.22
in weighted variable Lebesgue spaces and mixed-norm weighted variable Lebesgue spaces, respectively. The fact that we explicitly allow quasi-Banach spaces in the range of our operators considerably complicates matters. In particular, we cannot apply one of the classical abstract interpolation schemes that can be found in standard references on the subject such as
[
6
]
. To overcome this difficulty, we adapt the strategy from
[
9
]
, which concerned a similar setting to ours but with constant exponents. However, the presence of variable exponents necessitates much more involved arguments and new ideas. Finally, we use the factorization results for multilinear variable Muckenhoupt classes from the paper
[
28
]
of the authors, which themselves relied in turn on the factorization results for linear variable Muckenhoupt classes proved by the second named author and Oikari in
[
33
]
.

As applications of Theorem
1.4
, we establish new compactness results for the multilinear commutators of
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund operators (see Subsection

), fractional integrals (see Subsection

The subsequent sections of this paper are structured as follows. In Section
2

1.2

4.28

(see Lemmata

2.
Preliminaries

As this paper primarily concerns multilinear compact operators, we briefly review their definition here for the convenience of the reader. Let
A_{1},\dots A_{m}
be normed spaces and
B
be a quasi normed space. We say that a multilinear operator

T:A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}\rightarrow B
is compact if the set
\{T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}):\;\|a_{i}\|_{A_{i}}\leq 1,\;1\leq i\leq m\}
is precompact in
B
. It is easy to check that
[
5
, Proposition 1]
also holds for multilinear operators and thus it provides several equivalent ways of formulating the compactness of
T
.

2.A.
Lebesgue spaces in the variable exponent setting

We begin by gathering some basic results about variable Lebesgue spaces. We point out that the references
[
14
]
and
[
18
]
contain most of the properties for these spaces.

Throughout the paper, all sets denoted by notations such as
\Omega,\Omega_{i},\widetilde{\Omega},
or similar will be assumed to be measurable subsets of some
\mathbb{R}^{n}
of positive measure, unless explicitly otherwise stated. Notice that the implicit
n
might be different from set to set, unless explicitly otherwise stated.

2.A.1.
Notation for functions with variable exponents

For any measurable function
p(\cdot):\Omega\to[0,\infty]
and any set
U\subset\Omega
, we define

p_{-}(U)=\essinf_{x\in U}p(x),\qquad p_{+}(U)=\esssup_{x\in U}p(x),

and if
U=\Omega
, then we will abbreviate
p_{\pm}(\Omega)
to
p_{\pm}
.

In the sequel, we will focus on certain notable classes of measurable functions
p(\cdot):\Omega\to[0,\infty]
. These are defined as

\mathscr{P}_{0}:=\{p(\cdot):\penalty\ 0<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty\}

and

\mathscr{P}:=\{p(\cdot):\penalty\ 1<p_{-}\leq p_{+}<\infty\}.

Given
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}
, its dual exponent
p^{\prime}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}
is defined by the relation
1/p(\cdot)+1/p^{\prime}(\cdot)=1.

2.A.2.
Luxemburg norm of
L^{p(\cdot)}
spaces

Given
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
, define the modular associated with
p(\cdot)
by

(2.1)

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f):=\int_{\Omega}|f(x)|^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x.

The variable Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
, or for simplicity just
L^{p(\cdot)}
, consists of all measurable functions
f:\Omega\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
such that

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\|f\|_{{p(\cdot)}}:=\inf\{\lambda\in(0,\infty):\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f/\lambda)\leq 1\}<\infty.

It follows from
[
14
, Proposition 2.12]
that
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(\Omega)
if and only if
\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)<\infty
.

2.A.3.
Weighted Lebesgue spaces in the variable exponent setting

A weight is a measurable function
w
on
\Omega
such that
0<w(x)<\infty
for almost every
x\in\Omega
. Given a weight
w
, we define
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
to be the weighted variable Lebesgue space with norm

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}:=\|fw\|_{p(\cdot)}<\infty.

We will often exploit the fact that the weighted variable Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
is a quasi-Banach function space for
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
; when
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}
, it becomes a Banach function space. This can be found in
[
44
, page 42]
.

2.A.4.
Log-Hölder continuity conditions

For technical purposes, the variable exponent functions we will be considering on the whole of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
will possess both local and asymptotic Hölder continuity. The relevant definitions are provided below.

2.2 Definition
.

Let
p(\cdot):\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a function. We say that
p(\cdot)

(1)

is locally log-Hölder continuous, denoted by
p(\cdot)\in\mathrm{LH}_{0},
if there exists
C_{0}>0
such that

|p(x)-p(y)|\leq\frac{C_{0}}{-\log(|x-y|)}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n},\;\;\text{whenever}\;\;|x-y|<\tfrac{1}{2};

(2)

is log-Hölder continuous at infinity, denoted by
p(\cdot)\in\mathrm{LH}_{\infty},
if there exist
p_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}
and
C_{\infty}>0
such that

|p(x)-p_{\infty}|\leq\frac{C_{\infty}}{\log(e+|x|)}\;\;\text{for all}\;\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

We then define the class of globally log-Hölder continuous functions as
\mathrm{LH}:=\mathrm{LH}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}_{\infty}
. The corresponding log-Hölder constant is defined as
C_{\log}(p):=\max\{C_{0},C_{\infty}\}
.

2.A.5.
Auxiliary results

The following results were originally established for exponents in
\mathscr{P}
. In case of Lemma
2.3
, the same proof remains valid for exponents in
\mathscr{P}_{0}
. For the rest (except Lemma
2.6
), the same proofs remain valid for exponents in

2.3 Lemma

(
[
14
]
, Proposition 2.18)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Then, it holds that
\||f|^{s}\|_{p(\cdot)}=\|f\|^{s}_{sp(\cdot)},
for all constants
s\in(0,\infty)
.

2.4 Lemma

(
[
14
]
, Corollary 2.23)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
and
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}
. If
\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}>1
, then

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}\leq\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}}.

If
0<\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq 1
, then

\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}}\leq\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}.

Summarizing we have

\min\left\{\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}\right\}\leq\|f\|_{p(\cdot)}\leq\max\left\{\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},\rho_{p(\cdot)}(f)^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}\right\}.

2.5 Lemma

(
[
1
]
, Lemma 1.2)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Then

\|f+g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\leq\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}(\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}+\|g\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}),

for all
f,g\in L^{p(\cdot)}
.

2.6 Lemma

(
[
14
]
, Corollary 2.30)
.

Let
p_{1}(\cdot),\dots,p_{m}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}
satisfy

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}=1,

where
j=1,\dots,m
. Then, for any
f_{j}\in L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
we have

\int_{\Omega}|f_{1}(x)\cdots f_{m}(x)|\,\mathrm{d}x\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}}\cdots\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}},

where the implicit constant depends only on
m
and
p_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,p_{m}(\cdot)
.

2.7 Lemma

(
[
51
]
, Lemma 6.2)
.

Let
p(\cdot),p_{1}(\cdot),\dots,p_{m}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
satisfy

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)},

where
j=1,\dots,m
. Then, for any
f_{j}\in L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
we have
f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\in L^{p(\cdot)}
, and in fact

\|f_{1}\cdots f_{m}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}\lesssim\|f_{1}\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}}\cdots\|f_{m}\|_{L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}},

where the implicit constant depends only on

p_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,p_{m}(\cdot)

2.A.6.
Convergence theorems

The following monotone and dominated convergence theorems for variable Lebesgue spaces were originally established for exponents in
\mathscr{P}
. We state them directly for exponents in
\mathscr{P}_{0}
, since the same proofs remain valid in this more general setting. Alternatively, one can use Lemma
2.3
to reduce back exponents in
\mathscr{P}
.

2.8 Proposition

(
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.59)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Let
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an increasing sequence of nonnegative functions in
L^{p(\cdot)}
converging pointwise a.e. to a function
f
. Then, we have
\lim\limits_{k\to\infty}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}
.

2.9 Proposition

(
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.62)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Let
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions in
L^{p(\cdot)}
converging pointwise a.e. to a function
f
. Assume that there exists a nonnegative function
g\in L^{p(\cdot)}
such that
|f_{k}|\leq g
a.e., for all
k=1,2,\ldots
. Then, we have
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}
and
f_{k}\to f
as
k\to\infty
in
L^{p(\cdot)}
.

Recall that a sequence of measurable functions
f_{k}:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
,
k=1,2,\ldots

converges in measure
to a measurable function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
if

\lim_{k\to\infty}|\{|f_{k}-f|\geq\varepsilon\}|=0,\quad\forall\varepsilon>0.

Recall also that if
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges in measure to
f
, then one can find a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})^{\infty}_{k=1}
converging pointwise a.e. to
f
.

2.10 Proposition

(
[
14
]
, Theorem 2.66 and remarks after its proof)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Let
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions in
L^{p(\cdot)}
such that
f_{k}\to f
as
k\to\infty
in
L^{p(\cdot)}
. Then,
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
converges in measure to
f
. In particular, there exists a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})^{\infty}_{k=1}
converging pointwise a.e. to
f
.

Proposition
2.10
will be useful later to prove a.e. equality of functions that arise as limits of the same sequence of functions in two different modes of convergence. For future purposes, we will also need a result of this type that concerns
mixed
norms.

2.11 Proposition
.

Let
p_{1}(\cdot),p_{2}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}(\Omega)
and
q_{1},q_{2}\in(0,\infty)
. Let
f_{k}:\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
,
k=1,2,\ldots
be a sequence of measurable functions and let
f,g:\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}\to\mathbb{C}
be measurable functions such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\|f_{k}(x,y)-f(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{y}}\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}_{x}}=0

and

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\|f_{k}(x,y)-g(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{y}}\|_{L^{p_{2}(\cdot)}_{x}}=0.

Then, it holds
f=g
a.e. on
\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}
.

Proof.

Consider the measurable set

E:=\{f\neq g\}\subset\Omega\times\widetilde{\Omega}

and define

E_{x}:=\{y\in\widetilde{\Omega}:\penalty\ (x,y)\in E\},\quad x\in\Omega.

By the theorem of Tonelli–Fubini we have

|E|=\int_{\Omega}|E_{x}|\,\mathrm{d}x.

Therefore, in order to show that
|E|=0
, it suffices to show that
|E_{x}|=0
for a.e.
x\in\Omega
.

Since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\|f_{k}(x,y)-f(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{y}}\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}_{x}}=0,

by Proposition
2.10
we deduce that there exist a subsequence
(f_{m_{k}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a measurable set
A\subset\Omega
with
|\Omega\setminus A|=0
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f_{m_{k}}(x,y)-f(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{1}}_{y}}=0,\quad\forall x\in A.

Then, since

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|\|f_{m_{k}}(x,y)-g(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{y}}\|_{L^{p_{2}(\cdot)}_{x}}=0,

by Proposition
2.10
we deduce that there exist a further subsequence
(f_{m_{\ell_{k}}})_{k=1}^{\infty}
and a measurable set
B\subset\Omega
with
|\Omega\setminus B|=0
, such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|f_{m_{\ell_{k}}}(x,y)-g(x,y)\|_{L^{q_{2}}_{y}}=0,\quad\forall x\in B.

Set
C:=A\cap B
. Then,
C\subset\Omega
is a measurable set with
|\Omega\setminus C|=0
. We will show that
|E_{x}|=0
, for every
x\in C
. Indeed, for every
x\in C
we have

f_{m_{\ell_{k}}}(x,y)\to f(x,y)\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{q_{1}}_{y}

and

f_{m_{\ell_{k}}}(x,y)\to g(x,y)\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{q_{2}}_{y},

which yields that

f(x,y)=g(x,y)\quad\text{for a.e. }y\in\widetilde{\Omega},

that is
|E_{x}|=0
. This concludes the proof.
∎

2.B.
Multilinear variable Muckenhoupt conditions

In this subsection we describe the classes of the weights that feature in the results of this paper. The definitions and facts that follow have already appeared in
[
28
]
, where we refer the reader for more details.

2.12 Definition
.

(1)

We say that a quadruple
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
is
m
-admissible
if all of the following conditions are satisfied:

•

\vec{r}=(r_{1},\ldots,r_{m})\in(0,\infty)^{m}
is a
m
-tuple of constants.

•

s\in(0,\infty]
is a constant.

•

\vec{p}(\cdot)=(p_{1}(\cdot),\ldots,p_{m}(\cdot))\in\mathscr{P}_{0}^{m}
is a
m
-tuple of variable exponents with

r_{j}<(p_{j})_{-},\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,m.

•

q(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
is a variable exponent with
q_{+}<s
.

•

There is a constant
\gamma\in[0,\infty)
such that

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma,

where the variable exponent
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
is defined via

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}.

In this case, we define the constant
r\in(0,\infty)
via

\frac{1}{r}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{r_{j}}.

(2)

We say that a
m
-admissible quadruple
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
is
proper
if

p_{j}(\cdot)\in\mathrm{LH},\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,m

and

q(\cdot)\in\mathrm{LH}.

Notice that in this case,
p(\cdot)\in\mathrm{LH}
.

2.13 Definition
.

Let
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
be a
m
-admissible quadruple with

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma\in[0,\infty).

A vector of weights
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\dots,w_{m})
is said to satisfy the
m
-linear
\mathcal{A}_{({\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}),(\vec{r},s)}
condition (or
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{({\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}),(\vec{r},s)}
) if

(2.14)

[\vec{w}]_{\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}}:=\sup_{Q}|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}<\infty.

Here, the supremum is taken over all cubes
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
\nu_{\vec{w}}:=\prod_{j=1}^{m}w_{j}
.

If
\gamma=0
, we denote

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),(\vec{r},s)}:=\mathcal{A}_{({\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}),(\vec{r},s)}.

Moreover, if
r_{j}=1
and
s=\infty
, then we denote

\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}:=\mathcal{A}_{({\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}),(\vec{r},s)}.

An equivalent characterization for these multilinear variable exponent Muckenhoupt classes in terms of linear ones was proved in
[
28
, Lemma 3.5]
. Since this principle will be important for us, we state it below and refer the reader to
[
28
, Lemma 3.5]
for its proof.

2.15 Lemma

(
[
28
]
, Lemma 3.5)
.

Let
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
be a
m
-admissible quadruple. Then
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{({\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}),(\vec{r},s)}
if and only if

(2.16)

\begin{cases}w_{j}\in\mathcal{A}_{p_{j}(\cdot),(r_{j},\sigma_{j})},&\text{with}\quad\frac{1}{\sigma_{j}}=\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\big(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\big)\quad\text{for all}\quad j=1,\dots,m,\\
\nu_{\vec{w}}\in\mathcal{A}_{(p(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(r,s)}.\end{cases}

3.
Characterization of total boundedness in the variable exponent setting

In this section, we establish the Riesz–Kolmogorov criterion for total boundedness in the weighted variable Lebesgue space
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
for
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}
. We begin by gathering several key results that will be needed later.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
and
w
be a weight such that
w^{p(\cdot)}
is integrable over the ball
B(x_{0},R)
.
Then, we denote by
L^{0}(B(x_{0},R),w^{p(\cdot)})
the space of measurable functions on
B(x_{0},R)
equipped with the metric

d_{0}(f,g):=\int_{B(x_{0},R)}\phi(f(x)-g(x))w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x,

where

\phi(t):=\frac{|t|}{1+|t|},\quad t\in\mathbb{C}.

It is a well-known fact that this space is a complete metric space with respect to this metric and the convergence in this metric is equivalent to the convergence in measure. In what follows, we denote by
w(A)
for
A\subset B(x_{0},R)
the weighted Lebesgue measure
w(A):=\int_{A}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x
. The following result is a special case of
[
1
, Theorem 1.3]
(see also
[
31
]
):

3.1 Lemma

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 1.3)
.

Any almost uniformly bounded and almost equicontinuous subset of
L^{0}(B(x_{0},R),w^{p(\cdot)})
is totally bounded. This means that a subset
\mathcal{F}
of
L^{0}(B(x_{0},R),w^{p(\cdot)})
is totally bounded if for any
\varepsilon>0
, there exist
\delta>0
and
\lambda>0
such that, for any function
f\in\mathcal{F}
, there exists a measurable subset
E(f)\subset B(x_{0},R)
satisfying the following properties

(1)

w(E(f))<\varepsilon;

(2)

|f(x)-f(y)|<\varepsilon,\quad\text{for any}\;\;x,y\in B(x_{0},R)\setminus E(f)\;\;\text{satisfying}\;\;|x-y|<\delta;

(3)

|f(x)|\leq\lambda\quad\text{for}\;\;x\in B(x_{0},R)\setminus E(f).

We recall a particular form of the Lebesgue–Vitali compactness-type theorem, corresponding to a special case of
[
1
, Theorem 2.1]
:

3.2 Lemma

(
[
1
]
, Theorem 2.1)
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
. Then, a subset
\mathcal{F}
of
L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x_{0},R),w)
is totally bounded if and only if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\mathcal{F}
is totally bounded in
L^{0}(B(x_{0},R),w^{p(\cdot)})
;

(2)

the family
\mathcal{F}
is
L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x_{0},R),w)
-equi-integrable, i.e., for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists
\delta>0
, such that

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\varepsilon\;(\text{or equivalently},\;\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon)

for all
E\subset B(x_{0},R)
satisfying
w(E)<\delta
.

We will also rely on the following result in later proofs.

3.3 Lemma
.

Let
p(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}
,
\widetilde{q}\in(0,p_{-})
and
w^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{p(\cdot)}{\widetilde{q}}}
. Then

\|M_{\widetilde{q}}(f)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\leq C\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)},

where
M_{\widetilde{q}}(f)(x):=\sup_{r>0}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\;\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}
is the
\widetilde{q}
-Hardy–Littlewood maximal operator and
C
is a positive constant that depends on
n
,
p(\cdot)
,
\widetilde{q}
,
C_{\log}(p)
and the weight
w
.

3.4 Remark
.

Notice that the proof of Lemma
3.3
follows by combining Lemma
2.3
with
[
12
, Theorem 1.5]
(see also
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
).

Now, we are in a position to present the proof of the weighted Riesz–Kolmogorov Theorem
1.2
in the variable exponent setting.

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Let us first prove the necessity. We fix
0<\varepsilon<1
such that
\widetilde{\varepsilon}:=\frac{\varepsilon}{C}<1
, where
C>0
is the constant that appears in Lemma
3.3
. Since
\mathcal{F}
is totally bounded in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
, there exists a finite
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
-net
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}
in
\mathcal{F}
, that is, there exists a finite number of functions
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{F}
such that

\mathcal{F}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B(f_{k},\widetilde{\varepsilon}).

Let
f\in\mathcal{F}
be an arbitrary function. Then, we can fix
k\in\{1,\dots,N\}
satisfying

(3.5)

\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\widetilde{\varepsilon}<1.

By Lemma
2.4
, we have

(3.6)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x<\widetilde{\varepsilon}^{p_{-}}.

Set
M_{1}:=\sup_{k=1,\dots,N}\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\infty
.
Then, condition
(i)
is satisfied since by Lemma
2.5
and (
3.5
) we have

\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\leq\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}(\|f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}+\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)})\leq\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}(M_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon})=M,

where
M:=\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}(M_{1}+\widetilde{\varepsilon})>0
.

By the triangle inequality and the elementary inequality

(3.7)

(a+b)^{\alpha}\leq\max\{1,2^{\alpha-1}\}(a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha})\leq 2^{\alpha}(a^{\alpha}+b^{\alpha}),

where
a,b\geq 0
and
\alpha>0
, we deduce the following estimates

(3.8)

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\Bigg(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f_{k}(x)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+4^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(y)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle=:2^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\mathcal{I}_{1}+4^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\mathcal{I}_{2}+4^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\mathcal{I}_{3}.

Next, we provide the bounds for
\mathcal{I}_{1}
,
\mathcal{I}_{2}
and
\mathcal{I}_{3}
. By (
3.6
) it is immediate that
\mathcal{I}_{1}<\widetilde{\varepsilon}^{p_{-}}
.

Now, by the triangle inequality, Lemmata
2.4
and
3.3
, and (
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}}\left(|f_{k}(x)|^{\widetilde{q}}+\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}\left(1+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}\right)-1}\left(|f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}+\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}\left(1+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}\right)-1}\left(|f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}+[M_{\widetilde{q}}f_{k}(x)]^{p(x)}\right)\in L^{1}(w^{p(\cdot)}).

Notice that
f_{k}\in L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
and
w^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{p(\cdot)}{\widetilde{q}}}
imply that
|f_{k}|^{\widetilde{q}}\in L^{\frac{p(\cdot)}{\widetilde{q}}}(w^{\widetilde{q}})\subset L^{1}_{\operatorname{loc}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Hence, by the Lebesgue differentiation theorem for classical Lebesgue spaces we obtain that

\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(x)-f_{k}(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\rightarrow 0\;\text{as}\;r\rightarrow 0^{+},\;\text{a.e.}\;x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Thus, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem for classical Lebesgue spaces that
\mathcal{I}_{2}<\varepsilon
for sufficiently small
r>0
.

Lastly, to deal with
\mathcal{I}_{3}
, we have

\|M_{\widetilde{q}}(|f_{k}-f|)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\leq C\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\leq C\widetilde{\varepsilon}=\varepsilon<1,

where we used Lemma
3.3
(since
w^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{p(\cdot)}{\widetilde{q}}}
) and (
3.5
). Therefore, this and Lemma
2.4
imply that

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}_{3}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f_{k}(y)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}(M_{\widetilde{q}}(|f_{k}-f|)(x))^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\|M_{\widetilde{q}}(|f_{k}-f|)\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}^{p_{-}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{p_{-}}\|f_{k}-f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}^{p_{-}}

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon^{p_{-}}.

Combining (

) with the preceding estimates, we conclude that condition
(ii)
holds.

Fix a point
x_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Since
f_{k}\in L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
, it follows from the dominated convergence theorem for classical Lebesgue spaces that we can fix sufficiently large

such that

(3.9)

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R_{k})}|f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon,\;k=1,\dots,N.

Then, for
R:=\max\{R_{k}:\;k=1,\dots,N\}
, by the triangle inequality, (
3.6
), (
3.7
) and (

) we have

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R_{k})}|f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}}\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|f(x)-f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x+\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R_{k})}|f_{k}(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)

This shows that condition
(iii)
holds. Therefore, we have shown that conditions
(i)
–
(iii)
are satisfied, which completes the proof of necessity.

Let us next prove the sufficiency. Suppose that conditions
(i)
,
(ii)
and

hold. Then by condition

, for any fixed

, there exists

(3.10)

At this point, we introduce the following Lipschitz cut-off function with the Lipschitz constant
\frac{1}{R}

\varphi_{R}(x):=\begin{cases}2-\frac{|x-x_{0}|}{R},&\text{if}\quad x\in B(x_{0},2R)\setminus B(x_{0},R),\\
1,&\text{if}\quad x\in B(x_{0},R),\\
0,&\text{if}\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},2R).\end{cases}

Note that
0\leq\varphi_{R}\leq 1
.
In addition, we define the following set
\mathcal{F}_{R}
by

\mathcal{F_{R}}:=\{f\varphi_{R}:\;f\in\mathcal{F}\}.

Our goal is to show that
\mathcal{F_{R}}
is totally bounded in
L^{p(\cdot)}(B(x_{0},R),w)
. The proof first relies on Lemmata
3.12
,
3.14
, and
3.20
, which will be stated and proved below. Observe that Lemma
3.12
is used only in the proofs of Lemmata

and
3.20
. Then, the latter two lemmata guarantee that conditions
(1)
and
(2)
of Lemma

are satisfied which in turn implies that

is totally bounded in

.

Thus, we can choose

in
\mathcal{F}
such that

is an
\varepsilon
-net in
\mathcal{F}_{R}
, that is, there exists a finite number of functions

such that

\mathcal{F}_{R}\subset\bigcup_{k=1}^{N}B(f_{k}\varphi_{R},\varepsilon).

Let
g\in\mathcal{F}_{R}
be an arbitrary function. Then,
g
can be written in the form
g=f\varphi_{R}
with
f\in\mathcal{F}
and we can fix

satisfying

(3.11)

\|g-g_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\varepsilon<1,

where

with
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{F}
. We will show that
\{f_{k}\}_{k=1}^{N}\subset\mathcal{F}
is an
\widetilde{\varepsilon}
-net set in
\mathcal{F}
, where

0<\widetilde{\varepsilon}:=\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}\left(\max\left\{2^{1+\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{1+\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}\kappa^{\frac{1}{p_{+}}}+\varepsilon\right)<1.

For any

, by invoking Lemmata
2.4
and
2.5

) and (

), and applying the properties of the cut-off function

\displaystyle\|f-f_{k}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}

\displaystyle\leq\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}\Bigg(\max\left\{2^{\frac{1}{p_{-}}},2^{\frac{p_{+}}{p_{-}}}\right\}\big[\|f\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R)}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}

\displaystyle\qquad+\|f_{k}\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(x_{0},R)}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\big]+\|(f-f_{k})\varphi_{R}\chi_{B(x_{0},R)}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\Bigg)

\displaystyle<\widetilde{\varepsilon},

which shows that

is totally bounded in

3.14 Lemma
.

is

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{R}}\|f\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\varepsilon\;(\text{or equivalently},\;\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{R}}\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x<\varepsilon)

Proof.

Fix

and

. By the triangle inequality and (

), we have

|f(x)|^{\widetilde{q}}\leq 2^{\widetilde{q}}(|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}+|f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}),

for any

and

. Averaging (

) with respect to

Next, we raise the preceding inequality to the power

and integrate the resulting expression over the measurable set

. By the Hölder’s inequality (Lemma

) and (

), we have

(3.16)

\displaystyle\int_{E}|f(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\Bigg(\int_{E}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{E}\left(\frac{1}{|B(x,r)|}\int_{B(x,r)}|f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x\Bigg)

\displaystyle\leq 2^{p_{+}+\frac{p_{+}}{\widetilde{q}}}\int_{E}\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,r)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{p(x)/\widetilde{q}}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

) that

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|

\displaystyle=\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,\delta)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{1+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}}\left(\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,\delta)}|f(x)-f(z)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}+\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,\delta)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right).

Since
B(x,\delta)\subset B(y,2\delta)
and using the property of the Lebesgue measure that
|B(x,2\delta)|=2^{n}|B(x,\delta)|
, the previous inequality implies that for any
x,y\in B(x_{0},R)
with
|x-y|<\delta

|f(x)-f(y)|\leq 2^{1+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}}2^{\frac{n}{\widetilde{q}}}\left(\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,2\delta)}|f(x)-f(z)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}+\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(y,2\delta)}|f(y)-f(z)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}z\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right).

Let
\lambda>1
. By applying Markov’s inequality and invoking condition
(a)
of Lemma
3.12
, we deduce

\displaystyle w\left(\left\{x\in B(x_{0},R):\;|f(x)|>\lambda\right\}\right)

\displaystyle=w\left(\left\{x\in B(x_{0},R):\;|f(x)|^{p(x)}>\lambda^{p(x)}\right\}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda^{p_{-}}}\int_{B(x_{0},R)}|f(x)|^{p(x)}w(x)^{p(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\widetilde{M}}{\lambda^{p_{-}}}.

Thus, if
\lambda>1
is large enough then

\sup_{f\in\mathcal{F}_{R}}w\left(\left\{x\in B(x_{0},R):\;|f(x)|>\lambda\right\}\right)<\varepsilon.

We define

\displaystyle E(f)

\displaystyle:=\left\{x\in B(x_{0},R):\;|f(x)|>\lambda\right\}

\displaystyle\qquad\cup\left\{x\in B(x_{0},R):\;\left(\displaystyle\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(x,2\delta)}|f(x)-f(y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}>\varepsilon\;\right\}\subset B(x_{0},R).

Then,

\displaystyle w(E(f))<2\varepsilon,\quad\text{as long as}\;\;\lambda>1\;\;\text{is large enough},

\displaystyle|f(x)|\leq\lambda,\quad\text{for}\;\;x\in B(x_{0},R)\setminus E(f),

\displaystyle|f(x)-f(y)|\leq 2^{2+\frac{1}{\widetilde{q}}}2^{\frac{n}{\widetilde{q}}}\varepsilon,\quad\text{for}\;\;x,y\in B(x_{0},R)\setminus E(f),\;|x-y|<\delta.

Hence, it follows from Lemma
3.1
that
\mathcal{F}_{R}
is totally bounded in
L^{0}(B(x_{0},R),w^{p(\cdot)})
. This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

4.
Interpolation of compactness for multilinear operators in the variable exponent setting

4.A.
Some terminology and notation

In this section, we will establish the weighted interpolation for multilinear compact operators within the framework of variable exponent spaces, see Theorem
4.28
below. To streamline the presentation, we divide the proof of this result into several subsections below. Now, we begin by defining two important concepts that will be used subsequently.

By a
generalized simple function
we mean a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{E_{i}}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\subset\Omega
are measurable sets. By a
simple function
we mean a function
f:\Omega\to\mathbb{C}
of the form

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{E_{i}}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}
and
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\subset\Omega
are measurable sets of
finite
measure.

4.B.
Interpolation of boundedness I

The first key step is to provide the proof of the following counterpart of
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
in the variable exponent setting.

4.1 Theorem
.

Let
p_{0,j}(\cdot),p_{1,j}(\cdot)\in(\mathscr{P}\cap\mathrm{LH})(\Omega_{j})
,
j=1,\ldots,m
and
q_{0}(\cdot),q_{1}(\cdot)\in(\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH})(\Omega)
. Let
w_{0,j},w_{1,j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
be weights on
\Omega_{j}
and let also
v_{0},v_{1}
be weights on
\Omega
. Let
T
be a
m
-linear operator such that

T:L^{p_{0,1}(\cdot)}(w_{0,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{0,m}(\cdot)}(w_{0,m})\to L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})

and

T:L^{p_{1,1}(\cdot)}(w_{1,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{1,m}(\cdot)}(w_{1,m})\to L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(v_{1})

boundedly. For each
i=0,1
, let
M_{i}\in(0,\infty)
with

(4.2)

\|T(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q_{i}(\cdot)}(v_{i})}\leq M_{i}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{i,j}(\cdot)}(w_{i,j})},

for all
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in L^{p_{i,1}(\cdot)}(w_{i,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{i,m}(\cdot)}(w_{i,m})
. Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and define

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}:=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)},\quad j=1,\ldots,m

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}:=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}(\cdot)},

as well as

\displaystyle w_{j}:=w_{0,j}^{1-\theta}w_{1,j}^{\theta},\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle v:=v_{0}^{1-\theta}v_{1}^{\theta}.

Then, we have

T:L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})\to L^{q(\cdot)}(v)

boundedly and it holds

\|T(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},

for all
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})\in L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})
.

To prove Theorem
4.1
, we adapt the strategy of the proof of
[
9
, Theorem 3.1]
. However, several steps are significantly more complicated in the present variable exponent setting.

The lengthy proof of Theorem
4.1
begins with a couple of reductions.

4.3 Lemma
.

Assume the same setup as in Theorem
4.1
. For each
j=1,\ldots,m
define

A_{j}:=\{f_{j}\text{ simple functions on }\Omega_{j}:\penalty\ \|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{i,j}(\cdot)}(w_{i,j})}<\infty\text{ for all }i=0,1\}.

Then, to prove Theorem
4.1
, it suffices to show that

(4.4)

\|T(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in\vec{A}:=A_{1}\times\cdots\times A_{m}.

Proof.

Assume that (
4.4
) has been shown. Note that it follows from Lemma
4.6
below that

A_{j}\subset L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad\forall j=1,\ldots,m.

Since
T
is
m
-linear, an application of the well known extension theorem for bounded multilinear operators on quasi-normed vector spaces yields that there exists a unique
m
-linear operator

\widetilde{T}:L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})\to L^{q(\cdot)}(v)

such that

\widetilde{T}|_{\vec{A}}=T|_{\vec{A}}.

It remains to check that
\widetilde{T}
agrees with
T
on the entirety of the intersection of the respective domains of definitions. So let
i\in\{0,1\}
and

\vec{f}\in(L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m}))\cap(L^{p_{i,1}(\cdot)}(w_{i,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{i,m}(\cdot)}(w_{i,m}))

be arbitrary. Then, by Lemma
4.6
we have that for all
j=1,\ldots,m
there exists a sequence
(f_{j,k})^{\infty}_{k=1}
in
A_{j}
such that

f_{j,k}\to f_{j}\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\text{ in }L^{p_{i,j}(\cdot)}(w_{i,j})

and

f_{j,k}\to f_{j}\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}).

By the multilinearity of
T
and (
4.4
), we conclude that

T(\vec{f}_{k})\to T(\vec{f})\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{q_{i}(\cdot)}(v_{i})

and

\widetilde{T}(\vec{f}_{k})\to\widetilde{T}(\vec{f})\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\text{ in }L^{q(\cdot)}(v),

that is

T(\vec{f}_{k})v_{i}\to T(\vec{f})v_{i}\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{q_{i}(\cdot)}

and

\widetilde{T}(\vec{f}_{k})v\to\widetilde{T}(\vec{f})v\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\text{ in }L^{q(\cdot)}.

Then, by Proposition
2.10
we have that there exists a subsequence
(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})^{\infty}_{k=1}
such that

T(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})v_{i}\to T(\vec{f})v_{i}\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{a.e. pointwise on }\Omega

and

\widetilde{T}(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})v\to\widetilde{T}(\vec{f})v\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{a.e. pointwise on }\Omega,

and thus

T(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})\to T(\vec{f})\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{a.e. pointwise on }\Omega

and

\widetilde{T}(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})\to\widetilde{T}(\vec{f})\quad\text{ as }k\to\infty\quad\text{a.e. pointwise on }\Omega,

Since
T(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})=\widetilde{T}(\vec{f}_{m_{k}})
a.e., for all
k=1,2,\ldots
, we conclude that
T(\vec{f})=\widetilde{T}(\vec{f})
a.e. This completes the proof.
∎

4.5 Lemma
.

Let
p(\cdot),q(\cdot)\in(\mathscr{P}\cap\mathrm{LH})(\Omega)
and let
w,v
be weights on
\Omega
.

(1)

Define

A:=\{f\text{ simple function on }\Omega:\penalty\ \|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\infty\text{ and }\|f\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}<\infty\}.

Then, we have

A=\mathrm{Span}(\{\chi_{E}:\penalty\ E\text{ measurable subset of }\Omega\text{ with }|E|<\infty\text{ and }\chi_{E}\in L^{p(\cdot)}(w)\cap L^{q(\cdot)}(v)\}).

(2)

Let
f\in L^{p(\cdot)}(w)\cap L^{q(\cdot)}(v)
. Then, there exists a sequence
(f_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}
of simple functions in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)\cap L^{q(\cdot)}(v)
, such that

f_{n}\to f\quad\text{as }n\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{p(\cdot)}(w)

and

f_{n}\to f\quad\text{as }n\to\infty\quad\text{in }L^{q(\cdot)}(v).

Proof.

(1)

First of all, it is clear that
A
is a vector subspace of
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)\cap L^{q(\cdot)}(v)
and
\chi_{E}\in A
, for every measurable subset of
E
of
\Omega
with
|E|<\infty
,
\|w\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}<\infty
and
\|v\chi_{E}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}}<\infty
. This shows the inclusion
\supset
.

We now prove the inclusion
\subset
. Let
f\in A
be arbitrary. We can write

f=\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\chi_{E_{i}}

where
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}\in\mathbb{C}\setminus\{0\}
and
E_{1},\ldots,E_{n}\subset\Omega
are pairwise disjoint measurable sets of finite measure. Then, for all
i=1,\ldots,n
we have

\|\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}\leq\frac{1}{|a_{i}|}\||f|\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}=\frac{1}{|a_{i}|}\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}<\infty.

Similarly we obtain
\|\chi_{E_{i}}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}<\infty
, concluding the proof.

(2)

It is well known that there exists a sequence
(h_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}
of generalized simple functions on
\Omega
such that
|h_{n}|\leq|f|
, for all
n=1,2,\ldots
and
h_{n}\to f
pointwise on
\Omega
. Write

\Omega=\bigcup_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{n},

where
(\Omega_{n})^{\infty}_{n=1}
is an increasing sequence of measurable subsets of
\Omega
of finite measure, and let
g_{n}:=h_{n}\chi_{\Omega_{n}}
,
n=1,2,\ldots
. Then
g_{1},g_{2},\ldots
are simple functions,
|g_{n}|\leq|f|
, for all
n=1,2,\ldots
and
g_{n}\to f
pointwise on
\Omega
.

Since
|g_{n}|\leq|f|
, we deduce
g_{n}\in L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
, for all
n=1,2,\ldots
. Moreover, since

|g_{n}w|\leq|fw|,\quad\forall n=1,2,\ldots

and
fw\in L^{p(\cdot)}
, by Proposition
2.9
we deduce that
g_{n}w\to fw
as
n\to\infty
in
L^{p(\cdot)}
, that is
g_{n}\to f
as
n\to\infty
in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
. The exact same argument shows that
g_{n}\to f
as
n\to\infty
in
L^{q(\cdot)}(v)
, concluding the proof.

∎

The following is an analogue of
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
.

4.6 Lemma
.

Let
p_{0}(\cdot),p_{1}(\cdot)\in(\mathscr{P}\cap\mathrm{LH})(\Omega)
. Let
w_{0},w_{1}
be weights on
\Omega
. Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and define

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}:=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1}(\cdot)}

as well as

w:=w_{0}^{1-\theta}w_{1}^{\theta}.

Define

A:=\{f\text{ simple function on }\Omega:\penalty\ \|f\|_{L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(w_{i})}<\infty\text{ for all }i=0,1\}.

(1)

The family
A
is dense in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
.

(2)

Let
i\in\{0,1\}
be arbitrary. Then, for all
f\in L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(w_{i})\cap L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
, there exists a sequence
(f_{k})_{k=1}^{\infty}
in
A
such that
f_{k}\to f
as
k\to\infty
in
L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}(w_{i})
and
f_{k}\to f
as
k\to\infty
in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
.

Proof.

We adapt the arguments from the proof of
[
9
, Lemma 3.3]
. We will be suppressing the dependence of implicit constants on
p_{0}(\cdot),p_{1}(\cdot)
and
\theta
from the notation.

(1)

First of all, let us note that
A
is a subset of
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
. Indeed, for all
f\in A
, by Lemma
2.3
and Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.7
, we have

\displaystyle\|f\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}

\displaystyle=\|fw\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\||fw_{0}|^{1-\theta}|fw_{1}|^{\theta}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\||fw_{0}|^{1-\theta}\|_{L^{p_{0}(\cdot)/(1-\theta)}}\||fw_{1}|^{\theta}\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)/\theta}}

\displaystyle=\||fw_{0}|\|_{L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}}^{1-\theta}\||fw_{1}|\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}}^{\theta}=\|f\|_{L^{p_{0}(\cdot)}(w_{0})}^{1-\theta}\|f\|_{L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})}^{\theta}<\infty.

To prove that
A
is dense in
L^{p(\cdot)}(w)
, by Lemma
4.5
we deduce that it suffices to prove that for all measurable subsets
E
of
\Omega
with
|E|<\infty
and
\|w\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}<\infty
and for all
\varepsilon>0
we can find

with

. Since

\|\cdot\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle\|\chi_{E}-\chi_{E\setminus F_{N}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}(w)}=\|w\chi_{F_{N}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}=\|w\chi_{E}\chi_{F_{N}}\|_{L^{p(\cdot)}}<\varepsilon,

(2)

The proof is similar to the second part of the proof of part

\Omega
with

we can find

\|w_{i}\chi_{E}\|_{L^{p_{i}(\cdot)}}<\infty

of

4.7 Lemma
.

Assume the same setup as in Lemma
4.3
. Then, in proving (
4.4
) we may assume without loss of generality that

.

Proof.

By assumption, we can write

\Omega=\bigcup_{k=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{k},

where
(\Omega_{k})^{\infty}_{k=1}
is an increasing sequence of measurable subsets of
\Omega
with finite measure. For each

\lim_{k\to\infty}\|g\chi_{\Omega_{k}}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}=\lim_{k\to\infty}\||gv\chi_{\Omega_{k}}|\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}}=\||gv|\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}}=\|g\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(v)}.

for all measurable functions

. Therefore, in proving (

) we may assume without loss of generality that
|\Omega|<\infty
.
∎

.

4.3
. Moreover, assume that

4.16 Lemma
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. It holds that

\Phi_{\ell,k}(z)\to\Phi_{\ell}(z)

as
k\to\infty
uniformly on compact sets for
z\in\bar{S}
.

Proof.

Fix a compact subset
K
of
\bar{S}
. It is well known that for all
a\in(0,\infty)
, the function

z\mapsto a^{z}=\exp((\ln a)z)

in continuous on
\mathbb{C}
and thus uniformly continuous on every compact subset of
\mathbb{C}
. It is then easy to see that

\lim_{k\to\infty}F_{z,k,j}(\omega)\to F_{z,j}(\omega)\quad\text{uniformly for }(\omega,z)\in\Omega_{j}\times K.

Since
w_{0,j}
is bounded and
|\Omega_{j}|<\infty
, it follows that

F_{z,k,j}\to F_{z,j}\quad\text{as }k\to\infty\text{ in }L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})

uniformly for
z\in K
. Moreover, it easy to see that

\sup_{z\in K}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\esssup|F_{z,k,j}|,\esssup|F_{z,j}|\right)<\infty.

Thus, by the same token as before we have

\sup_{z\in K}\left(\sup_{k\in\mathbb{N}}\|F_{z,k,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})},\|F_{z,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}\right)<\infty.

Therefore, using assumption (
4.2
) for
i=0
and the multilinearity of
T
, we deduce

T(\vec{F}_{z,k})\to T(\vec{F}_{z})\quad\text{as }k\to\infty\text{ in }L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})

uniformly for
z\in K
. An easy computation shows that there exists some constant
0<C<\infty
such that for all
z\in K
and all
k\in\mathbb{N}
we have

|U_{\ell,k}(z,\cdot)-U_{\ell}(z,\cdot)|\leq C|T(\vec{F}_{z,k})(\cdot)-T(\vec{F}_{z})|\quad\text{a.e.\penalty\ on }\Omega.

Therefore, since
s<1
, for each
z\in K
and
k\in\mathbb{N}
we can compute

\displaystyle|\Phi_{\ell,k}(z)-\Phi_{\ell}(z)|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\Omega}|U_{\ell,k}(z,\omega)-U_{\ell}(z,\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq C^{s}\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z,k})(\omega)-T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=C^{s}\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z,k})(\omega)-T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{-s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq C^{s}C_{1}\||T(\vec{F}_{z,k})-T(\vec{F}_{z})|^{s}v_{0}^{s}\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)/s}}\cdot\|v_{0}^{-s}\|_{L^{(q_{0}(\cdot)/s)^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle\leq C^{s}C_{1}C_{2}\|T(\vec{F}_{z,k})-T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}^{s}.

In the transition from the third to the fourth line we have used Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.6
, so that

depends only on
q_{0}(\cdot)
and
s
. Moreover, we have used the fact that, since
v_{0}
is a.e. bounded from below by a positive constant and

, we have

C_{2}:=\|v_{0}^{-s}\|_{L^{(q_{0}(\cdot)/s)^{\prime}}}<\infty.

This completes the proof.
∎

4.17 Lemma
.

Fix
\ell,k\in\mathbb{N}
. The function
\Phi_{\ell,k}
is continuous on

Proof.

It is clear that one can write

is a measurable subset of

of finite measure. It follows that for all

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}t_{j}\in\{1,\ldots,N_{j}\}\\
j=1,\ldots,m\end{subarray}}\exp\left(\dfrac{z^{2}-1}{s\ell}\right)M_{0}^{z-1}M_{1}^{-z}v_{0}(\omega)^{1-z}v_{1}(\omega)^{z}G_{z}(\omega)

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}(a_{t_{j},j}^{z}b_{t_{j},j}e^{ic_{t_{j},j}})T(\chi_{A_{t_{1},1}},\ldots,\chi_{A_{t_{m},m}})(\omega):=R(z,\omega)

4.18 Lemma
.

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. Then, there holds

\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\Phi_{\ell}(z)=0.

Proof.

It suffices to prove that there exists a constant

and
0<\mathrm{Re}(z)\leq 1
, for all

We begin by estimating

\Phi_{\ell}(z)=\left|\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}-1}{\ell}\right)\right|\int_{\Omega}|M_{0}^{z-1}M_{1}^{-z}T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)v_{0}(\omega)^{1-z}v_{1}(\omega)^{z}G_{z}(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega.

Next, we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|M_{0}^{z-1}M_{1}^{-z}T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)v_{0}(\omega)^{1-z}v_{1}(\omega)^{z}G_{z}(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}M_{0}^{s\mathrm{Re}(z)-s}M_{1}^{-s\mathrm{Re}(z)}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{s-s\mathrm{Re}(z)}v_{1}(\omega)^{\mathrm{Re}(z)}g(\omega)^{\mathrm{Re}(t(z,\omega))}\chi_{\{g\neq 0\}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

whenever

, for

\mathrm{Re}(t(z,\omega))=r^{\prime}(\omega)-sr^{\prime}(\omega)\left[\frac{1-\mathrm{Re}(z)}{q_{0}(\omega)}+\frac{\mathrm{Re}(z)}{q_{1}(\omega)}\right].

As in the proof of Lemma
4.16
we estimate

\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq C_{2}\|T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}^{s}.

Assumption (
4.2
) for

\displaystyle\|T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}\leq C_{3}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|F_{z,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}

Now, we have

\displaystyle|F_{z,j}(\omega)|=|\widetilde{f}_{j}(\omega)|^{\mathrm{Re}(p_{j}(\omega)m_{z,j}(\omega))}\chi_{\{\widetilde{f}_{j}\neq 0\}}(\omega)w_{0,j}(\omega)^{\mathrm{Re}(z)-1}w_{1,j}(\omega)^{-\mathrm{Re}(z)}

Thus, since

\mathrm{Re}((p_{j}m_{z,j})(\omega))=\frac{p_{j}(\omega)(1-\mathrm{Re}(z))}{p_{0}(\omega)}+\frac{p_{j}(\omega)\mathrm{Re}(z)}{p_{1}(\omega)},

we deduce

\sup_{z\in\bar{S}}\esssup|F_{z,j}|<\infty.

Thus, as in the proof of Lemma

\sup_{z\in\bar{S}}\|F_{z,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}<\infty,

yielding the desired result.

0\leq\Phi_{\ell}(1+iy)\leq 1.

Proof.

Let us prove (

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|M_{0}^{z-1}M_{1}^{-z}T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)v_{0}(\omega)^{1-z}v_{1}(\omega)^{z}G_{z}(\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}M_{0}^{s\mathrm{Re}(z)-s}M_{1}^{-s\mathrm{Re}(z)}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{s-s\mathrm{Re}(z)}v_{1}(\omega)^{\mathrm{Re}(z)}g(\omega)^{\mathrm{Re}(t(z,\omega))}\chi_{\{g\neq 0\}}(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle=\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{s}g(\omega)^{r^{\prime}(\omega)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\omega,

where
r_{0}(\cdot):=\frac{q_{0}(\cdot)}{s}
. Using Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.6

2.3
, we get

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|T(\vec{F}_{z})(\omega)|^{s}v_{0}(\omega)^{s}g(\omega)^{r^{\prime}(\omega)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\omega

\displaystyle\lesssim\||T(\vec{F}_{z})|^{s}v_{0}^{s}\|_{L^{r_{0}(\cdot)}}\|g(\cdot)^{r^{\prime}(\cdot)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}\|_{L^{r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}}

\displaystyle=\|T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}^{s}\|g(\cdot)^{r^{\prime}(\cdot)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}\|_{L^{r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}}.

We compute

\displaystyle\rho_{r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}(g(\cdot)^{r^{\prime}(\cdot)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)})=\int_{\Omega}g(\omega)^{r^{\prime}(\omega)}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq 1,

since
\|g\|_{L^{r^{\prime}(\cdot)}}\leq 1
. By Lemma
2.4
this implies that

\displaystyle\|g(\cdot)^{r^{\prime}(\cdot)/r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}\|_{L^{r_{0}^{\prime}(\cdot)}}\leq 1.

Finally, using assumption (
4.2
) for
i=0
we compute

\displaystyle\|T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}\leq M_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|F_{z,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}=M_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|F_{z,j}w_{0,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}}.

\displaystyle\rho_{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(F_{z,j}w_{0,j})=\rho_{p_{j}(\cdot)}(\widetilde{f_{j}})=\rho_{p_{j}(\cdot)}(f_{j}w_{j})\leq 1

by Lemma
2.4

\|f_{j}w_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}}\leq 1
, and therefore

\displaystyle\|F_{z,j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}\leq 1.

We conclude that

\|T(\vec{F}_{z})\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(v_{0})}\leq M_{0}.

Thus

\displaystyle 0\leq\Phi_{\ell}(z)\leq\left|\exp\left(\frac{z^{2}-1}{\ell}\right)\right|=\exp(-(y^{2}+1)/\ell)\leq 1.

The proof of (

∎

4.C.3.
Applying the maximum principle

Fix
\ell\in\mathbb{N}
. In view of the continuity of
\Phi_{\ell}
on the whole of
\bar{S}
and the facts that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell}(z)\geq 0,\quad\forall z\in\bar{S},

\displaystyle\lim_{|z|\to\infty}\Phi_{\ell}(z)=0,

which we proved above in Lemmata
4.15
,
4.18
and
4.19
, we deduce that
\Phi_{\ell}
attains a maximum value on
\bar{S}
.

Since
\Phi_{\ell}
is subharmonic in
S
by Lemma
4.15
, the maximum principle yields that it cannot attain a global maximum on
S
, unless it is constant on
S
and hence by continuity on the whole of
\bar{S}
. In view of the facts that

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell}(iy)\leq 1,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

\displaystyle\Phi_{\ell}(1+iy)\leq 1,\quad\forall y\in\mathbb{R},

we conclude finally that

\Phi_{\ell}(z)\leq 1,\quad\forall z\in\bar{S}.

4.C.4.
Concluding the proof

In particular, we obtain

|\Phi_{\ell}(\theta)|\leq 1,\quad\forall\ell=1,2,\ldots.

Fatou’s lemma for classical Lebesgue spaces yields

\int_{\Omega}\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}|U_{\ell}(\theta,\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}\int_{\Omega}|U_{\ell}(\theta,\omega)|^{s}\,\mathrm{d}\omega=\liminf_{\ell\to\infty}|\Phi_{\ell}(\theta)|\leq 1.

Observe that

U_{\ell}(\theta,\omega)=\exp\left(\frac{\theta^{2}-1}{s\ell}\right)M_{0}^{\theta-1}M_{1}^{-\theta}T(f_{1}w_{1}^{-1},\ldots,f_{m}w_{m}^{-1})(\omega)v(\omega)g(\omega)^{1/s},

therefore

\lim_{\ell\to\infty}U_{\ell}(\theta,\omega)=M_{0}^{\theta-1}M_{1}^{-\theta}T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(\omega)v(\omega)g(\omega)^{1/s}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|T(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(\omega)v(\omega)|^{s}g(\omega)\,\mathrm{d}\omega\leq M_{0}^{s(1-\theta)}M_{1}^{s\theta},

completing the proof.

p_{j}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}
. Let us show that
(p_{j})_{-}>r_{j}
. Pick
t\in(r_{j},\min\{(p_{0,j})_{-},(p_{1,j})_{-}\})
. Then, we have that

\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}<\frac{1-\theta}{t}+\frac{\theta}{t}=\frac{1}{t},

therefore
(p_{j})_{-}\geq t>r_{j}
.

Similarly,
q(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}\cap\mathrm{LH}
and
q_{+}<s
. Finally, we compute

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{p_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}\right)+\theta\left(\frac{1}{p_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}\right)=(1-\theta)\gamma+\theta\gamma=\gamma.

This concludes the proof.

(2)

Let
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any cube. Observe that

\frac{1}{\frac{1}{(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}}+\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\theta\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}

and

\frac{1}{\frac{1}{(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}\right)}}+\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\theta\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}\right)}}=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}},\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Thus, using Lemma
2.3
and Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.7
, we obtain

\displaystyle|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}^{1-\theta}\chi_{Q}\cdot\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}^{\theta}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{0,j}^{-(1-\theta)}\chi_{Q}\cdot w_{1,j}^{-\theta}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle\lesssim|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}^{1-\theta}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}^{\theta}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\theta\left(\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{0,j}^{-(1-\theta)}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{(1-\theta)\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}\right)}}\qquad\cdot\|w_{1,j}^{-\theta}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\theta\left(\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}\right)}}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}^{1-\theta}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{0,j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}}}^{1-\theta}\cdot\|w_{1,j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}}}^{\theta}

\displaystyle=\left(|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{0,j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}}}\right)^{1-\theta}

\displaystyle\qquad\cdot\left(|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{1,j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}}}\right)^{\theta}.

This yields immediately the desired result.

∎

4.26 Lemma
.

Let
(p(\cdot),q(\cdot),r,s)
be a proper 1-admissible quadruple with

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma.

Let
w
be a weight. Then, we have

w\in\mathcal{A}_{(p(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(r,s)}\Leftrightarrow w^{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{t(\cdot)},

where

a:=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma}>0,\quad t(\cdot)=\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}.

In fact,

[w]_{\mathcal{A}_{(p(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(r,s)}}=[w^{a}]_{\mathcal{A}_{t(\cdot)}}^{1/a}.

Proof.

First of all, we observe that
t_{-}>1
. Indeed, pick
x\in(r,p_{-})
. Then, we have

0<\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}=\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\gamma-\frac{1}{s}<\frac{1}{x}-\gamma-\frac{1}{s}

and therefore

t(\cdot)>\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma}{\frac{1}{x}-\gamma-\frac{1}{s}}>1.

Let now
Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be any cube. It holds

at(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}.

Moreover, we compute

t^{\prime}(\cdot)=\frac{t(\cdot)}{t(\cdot)-1}=\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma}{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}+\frac{1}{s}}=\frac{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}-\gamma}{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}},

therefore

at^{\prime}(\cdot)=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}}.

Thus, using Lemma
2.3
we have

\displaystyle|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|w\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\|w^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-1/a}\|w\chi_{Q}\|_{at(\cdot)}\|w^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{at^{\prime}(\cdot)}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{-1/a}\|w^{a}\chi_{Q}\|_{t(\cdot)}^{1/a}\|w^{-a}\chi_{Q}\|_{t^{\prime}(\cdot)}^{1/a}

\displaystyle=(|Q|^{-1}\|w^{a}\chi_{Q}\|_{t(\cdot)}\|w^{-a}\chi_{Q}\|_{t^{\prime}(\cdot)})^{1/a}.

This yields immediately the desired result.
∎

4.27 Lemma
.

Let
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
be a proper
m
-admissible quadruple. Let
\vec{w}
be a
m
-tuple of weights. Then, we have

\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}\Rightarrow\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},\infty)}

and in fact,

[\vec{w}]_{\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},\infty)}}\lesssim[\vec{w}]_{\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}},

where the implicit constant depends only on
q(\cdot)
and
s
.

Proof.

It is clear that
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},\infty)
is also a proper
m
-admissible quadruple. Let now
Q
be any cube. Noticing that

\frac{1}{s}+\frac{1}{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}=\frac{1}{q(\cdot)},

using Hölder’s inequality, Lemma
2.7
, we compute

\displaystyle|Q|^{\gamma-\left(\frac{1}{r}-\frac{1}{s}\right)}\|\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{r}}\|\chi_{Q}\|_{s}\|\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{s}}}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle\gtrsim|Q|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{r}}\|\chi_{Q}\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{q(\cdot)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}

\displaystyle=|Q|^{\gamma-\frac{1}{r}}\|\nu_{\vec{w}}\chi_{Q}\|_{q(\cdot)}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|w_{j}^{-1}\chi_{Q}\|_{\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r_{j}}-\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}}}.

This yields immediately the desired result.
∎

The following is the main result of this subsection and Section
4
. It can be thought of as a counterpart of
[
9
, Corollary 3.7]
in the variable exponent setting.

4.28 Theorem
.

Let
\gamma\in[0,\infty)
and let
(\vec{p}_{i}(\cdot),q_{i}(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
,
i=0,1
be proper
m
-admissible quadruples with

\frac{1}{p_{0}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q_{0}(\cdot)}=\frac{1}{p_{1}(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q_{1}(\cdot)}=\gamma.

Let

\displaystyle\vec{w}_{0}\in\mathcal{A}_{(p_{0}(\cdot),q_{0}(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)},

\displaystyle\vec{w}_{1}\in\mathcal{A}_{(p_{1}(\cdot),q_{1}(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}.

Let
\theta\in(0,1)
and define
p_{j}(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
,
q(\cdot)\in\mathscr{P}_{0}
by

\displaystyle\frac{1}{p_{j}(\cdot)}:=\frac{1-\theta}{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{p_{1,j}(\cdot)},\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}:=\frac{1-\theta}{q_{0}(\cdot)}+\frac{\theta}{q_{1}(\cdot)}

as well as the vector of weights
\vec{w}
by

w_{j}:=w_{0,j}^{1-\theta}w_{1,j}^{\theta},\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Note that by Lemma
4.25
we have that
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
is a proper
m
-admissible quadruple with

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma

and that
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)}
.

Let
T
be a
m
-linear operator such that

T:L^{p_{0,1}(\cdot)}(w_{0,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{0,m}(\cdot)}(w_{0,m})\to L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})

boundedly and

T:L^{p_{1,1}(\cdot)}(w_{1,1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{1,m}(\cdot)}(w_{1,m})\to L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}})

compactly. Then, we have

T:L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})\to L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})

compactly.

Proof.

We adapt the strategy of
[
9
, Theorem 3.6]
.

Notice that Theorem
4.1
implies already that

T:L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})\to L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})

boundedly. Define

\widetilde{q}:=\frac{1}{\frac{1}{r}-\gamma}.

Observe that by Lemma
4.25
we obtain

\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},s)},

which in view of Lemma
2.15
implies

\nu_{\vec{w}}\in\mathcal{A}_{(p(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(r,s)}.

Therefore, by Lemma
4.27
we get

\nu_{\vec{w}}\in\mathcal{A}_{(p(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(r,\infty)},

which with Lemma
4.26
gives

\nu_{\vec{w}}^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{q(\cdot)/\widetilde{q}}.

Therefore, to show that this operator
T
is compact, by Theorem
1.2
it suffices to show the following:

(1)

\sup_{\vec{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\|T(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}<\infty;

(2)

\lim_{r\to 0}\sup_{\vec{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|T(\vec{f})(x)-T(\vec{f})(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}=0;

(3)

\lim_{R\to\infty}\sup_{\vec{f}\in\mathcal{F}}\|T(\vec{f})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}=0,

where

\mathcal{F}:=\{\vec{f}\in L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m}):\penalty\ \|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}{(w_{j})}}=1,\penalty\ j=1,\ldots,m\}.

Define
L^{\vec{p}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}):=L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})
. Since
T
is multi-homogeneous, conditions
(1)
to
(3)
are obviously equivalent to the following:

(1’)

There exists
M>0
such that

\|T(\vec{f})\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}\leq M\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\vec{w});

(2’)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
r_{0}=r_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
, such that for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
we have

\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|T(\vec{f})(x)-T(\vec{f})(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}\leq\varepsilon\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\vec{w});

(3’)

For all
\varepsilon>0
there exists
R_{0}=R_{0}(\varepsilon)>0
, such that for all
R\in(R_{0},\infty)
we have

\|T(\vec{f})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}\leq\varepsilon\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\vec{w}).

Condition
(1’)
says just that
T:L^{\vec{p}(\cdot)}(\vec{w})\to L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})
is bounded, which follows by Theorem
4.1
, as noted earlier in the proof. Therefore, we only need to show
(2’)
and
(3’)
.

Let
\varepsilon>0
be arbitrary. Set

M_{0}:=\|T\|_{L^{\vec{p}_{0}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}_{0})\to L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}<\infty.

For all
r>0
, we consider

N_{0}(g,r):=\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|g(x)-g(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}_{x}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}.

By combining Lemma
2.15
with Lemmata
4.26
and
4.27
we obtain

(4.29)

\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{q_{0}(\cdot)/\widetilde{q}}.

We claim that

(4.30)

N_{0}(g,r)\leq C_{0}\|g\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}.

Therefore, by the triangle inequality, (
3.7
) and using Lemma
3.3
(since
\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}}^{\widetilde{q}}\in\mathcal{A}_{q_{0}(\cdot)/\widetilde{q}}
by (
4.29
)) we estimate

\displaystyle N_{0}(g,r)

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|g(x)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}+\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|g(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}_{x}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}\left\||g|+M_{\widetilde{q}}(g)\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}\leq C_{2}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}+\left\|M_{\widetilde{q}}(g)\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left\|g\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}.

This proves (
4.30
).

Now, pick
\delta>0
such that

M_{0}^{1-\theta}\delta^{\theta}<\varepsilon\quad\text{and}\quad(C_{0}M_{0})^{1-\theta}\delta^{\theta}<\varepsilon.

Since
T:L^{\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot)}(\vec{w})\to L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}})
is compact, by Theorem
1.2
we have that there exists
r_{0}>0
such that for all
r\in(0,r_{0})
we have

(4.31)

\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|T(\vec{f})(x)-T(\vec{f})(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}_{x}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}})}\leq\delta\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}(w_{1,j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}_{1}),

and furthermore that there exists
R_{0}>0
such that for all
R\in(R_{0},\infty)
we have

(4.32)

\|T(\vec{f})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}\|_{L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}})}\leq\delta\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}(w_{1,j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}_{1}).

Let
r\in(0,r_{0})
be arbitrary. We claim that

(4.33)

\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|T(\vec{f})(x)-T(\vec{f})(x+y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}\leq\varepsilon\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}).

Indeed, we consider the operator
S
defined by

S(\vec{f})(x,y):=T(\vec{f})(x)-T(\vec{f})(x+y),\quad x,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Observe that
S
is
m
-linear. For all
\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}_{0}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}_{0})
, by (
4.30
) we compute

(4.34)

\displaystyle\left\|\left(\mathop{}\mkern-3.0mu\mathchoice{\hbox to0.0pt{$\displaystyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\textstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\textstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.83331pt{\hss$\scriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to5.1215pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}{\hbox to0.0pt{$\scriptscriptstyle\vbox{\hbox to4.82639pt{\hss$\scriptscriptstyle{-}$\hss}}$\hss}}\mkern-3.0mu\int_{B(0,r)}|S(\vec{f})(x,y)|^{\widetilde{q}}\,\mathrm{d}y\right)^{1/\widetilde{q}}\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}_{x}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}=N_{0}(T(\vec{f}),r)

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\left\|T(\vec{f})\right\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}\leq C_{0}M_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})}.

Therefore, combining (
4.34
) and (
4.31
) with Theorem
4.22
we obtain (
4.33
).

Finally, let
R\in(R_{0},\infty)
be arbitrary. We claim that

(4.35)

\|T(\vec{f})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}\|_{L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})}\leq\varepsilon\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}}(\vec{w}).

Indeed, it is clear that the operator
\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}T
is still
m
-linear and

(4.36)

\|T(\vec{f})\chi_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B(0,R)}\|_{L^{q_{0}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{0}})}\leq M_{0}\prod_{j=1}^{m}\|f_{j}\|_{L^{p_{0,j}(\cdot)}(w_{0,j})},\quad\forall\vec{f}\in L^{\vec{p}_{0}(\cdot)}(\vec{w}_{0}).

Thus, combining (
4.36
) and (
4.32
) with Theorem
4.1
we deduce (
4.35
). This concludes the proof.
∎

Proof of abstract extrapolation of compactness principle

This section is devoted to an abstract extrapolation of compactness principle for multilinear operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces, namely Theorem
1.4
. To establish it, we employ the weighted interpolation of compactness Theorem
4.28
as well as the following two factorization results that can be found in
[
28
]
.

5.1 Lemma

(
[
28
]
, Lemma 4.1)
.

Let
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
and
(\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot),q_{1}(\cdot),\vec{r},s)
be proper

6.
Applications

In this section, we describe several applications of Theorem
1.4
. Since much of the preparatory work has already been carried out in Section 7 of the paper
[
28
]
by the authors, we will be briefer at the corresponding places and refer the reader to the aforementioned work for details.

6.A.
Notation for multilinear commutators

We employ some standard notation for commutators of multilinear operators. Namely, given some
m
-linear operator
T
accepting as arguments
m
-tuples
\vec{f}=(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})
of appropriate functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and some appropriately smooth function
b
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we define for each
j=1,\ldots,m
the commutator

[T,b]_{e_{j}}(\vec{f})(x):=b(x)T(\vec{f})(x)-T(f_{1},\ldots,bf_{j},\ldots,f_{m})(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}.

Moreover, given a
m
-tuple
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
of appropriately smooth functions on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
, we consider the
sum commutator

[T,\vec{b}]_{\Sigma}(\vec{f}):=\sum_{j=1}^{m}[T,b_{j}]_{e_{j}}(\vec{f}).

6.B.
The symbol of the commutators

Typically, the symbol
b
of our commutators will belong to the class

\operatorname{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\overline{C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}^{\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})},

where
C_{c}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of smooth functions with compact supports and
\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is the space of bounded mean oscillation functions defined by

\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n}):=\Big\{f:\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{C}\ \Big|\ \|f\|_{\operatorname{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}:=\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}}\langle|f-\langle f\rangle_{Q}|\rangle_{Q}<\infty\Big\}.

It turns out that this is the most natural choice in order to be able to apply our abstract extrapolation Theorem
1.4
.

6.C.
Multilinear
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund operators

Let
\omega:[0,\infty)\rightarrow[0,\infty)
be a modulus of continuity, namely,
\omega
is increasing, subadditive and satisfies
\omega(0)=0
. Consider a function
K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m+1}\setminus\{x=y_{1}=\ldots=y_{m}\}
. We say that
K
is an
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund kernel
if it fulfills the following
size and smoothness conditions
:

•

|K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x-y_{j}|)^{mn}},

•

|K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})-K(x^{\prime},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x-y_{j}|)^{mn}}\omega\bigg(\frac{|x-x^{\prime}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x-y_{j}|}\bigg),

whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{j=1,\ldots,m}|x-y_{j}|
, and for each
i=1,\dots,m,

•

|K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{i},\ldots,y_{m})-K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{i}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{m})|\lesssim\frac{1}{(\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x-y_{j}|)^{mn}}\omega\bigg(\frac{|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|}{\sum_{j=1}^{m}|x-y_{j}|}\bigg),

whenever
|y_{i}-y_{i}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{j=1,\ldots,m}|x-y_{j}|
.

Now, we say that the
m
-linear operator
T:\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times\mathcal{S}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\rightarrow\mathcal{S}^{\prime}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is an
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund operator if it extends to a bounded multilinear operator from
L^{q_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{q_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
to
L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
for some
\frac{1}{q}=\frac{1}{q_{1}}+\cdots+\frac{1}{q_{m}}
with
1<q_{1},\dots,q_{m}<\infty
, and there exists an
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund kernel
K
such that

T(\vec{f})(x):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}K(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})f(y_{1})\cdots f(y_{m})\,\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\cdots\,\mathrm{d}y_{m},

for all
x\notin\cap_{j=1}^{m}\operatorname{supp}f_{j}
,
j=1,\dots,m
.

We say that a modulus of continuity
\omega
satisfies the Dini condition, denoted by
\omega\in
Dini, if

\|\omega\|_{\text{Dini}}:=\int_{0}^{1}\omega(t)\frac{\,\mathrm{d}t}{t}<\infty.

We refer the reader to
[
55
,
22
,
40
]
for further discussions concerning the Dini condition.

In
[
9
, Theorem 5.1]
, it was shown that commutators of
m
-linear
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund operators are compact on constant exponent weighted Lebesgue spaces. Additionally, the authors of
[
28
, Theorem 7.5]
obtained the compactness of commutators of bilinear
\omega
-Calderón–Zygmund operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces and under the restriction that the target space is a Banach space. Theorem
1.4
allows us to extend this to the multilinear setting and remove the restriction on the target space.

To achieve this, we first need the following boundedness result for the commutators obtained by the authors in
[
28
, Theorem 7.4]
, which had built upon ideas from
[
10
, Theorem 2.4]
.

6.1 Theorem

(
[
28
]
, Theorem 7.4)
.

Let

. Let

. Let

. Then, for all proper

-admissible quadruples

.

Proof.

We apply part
(1)
of Theorem
1.4
. The class
\Theta
we will be considering consists of all pairs

\displaystyle Y_{j}=L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle Y=L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}}),

where

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma=0,

and

is a

and

Fix

. First of all, observe that the sum of multilinear compact operators remains compact per

Fix

Pick now a constant

is satisfied for the operator

6.D.
Multilinear fractional Calderón–Zygmund operators

Let
m\geq 2
and
\alpha\in(0,mn)
. Consider a function
K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})
defined on
(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m+1}\setminus\{x=y_{1}=\ldots=y_{m}\}
. We say that
K_{\alpha}
is an
m
-linear
\alpha
-fractional Calderón–Zygmund kernel
if there exist constants
A,B>0
, such that the following
size
and
smoothness
estimates are satisfied:

•

|K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\leq\frac{A}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn-\alpha}},

•

|K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})-K_{\alpha}(x^{\prime},y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})|\leq\frac{B|x-x^{\prime}|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn-\alpha+1}},

whenever
|x-x^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{j=1,\ldots,m}|x-y_{j}|
,

•

For each
j=1,\ldots,m
, one has

|K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{j},\ldots,y_{m})-K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{j}^{\prime},\ldots,y_{m})|\leq\frac{B|y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime}|}{(|x-y_{1}|+\cdots+|x-y_{m}|)^{mn-\alpha+1}},

whenever
|y_{j}-y_{j}^{\prime}|\leq\frac{1}{2}\max_{k=1,\ldots,m}|x-y_{k}|
.

As noticed in
[
4
]
in the special case
m=2
, given such a kernel
K_{\alpha}
, the
m
-linear operator
T_{\alpha}
defined by

T_{\alpha}(\vec{f})(x):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}K_{\alpha}(x,y_{1},\ldots,y_{m})f_{1}(y_{1})\cdots f_{m}(y_{m})\,\,\mathrm{d}y_{1}\cdots\,\mathrm{d}y_{m},\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

is well defined whenever
f_{j}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
are for example bounded functions with compact support on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Combining results from
[
29
,
4
]
, one can see that
T_{\alpha}
extends to a bounded
m
-linear operator
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\to L^{q}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
whenever
1<p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
and
\frac{\alpha}{n}=\frac{1}{p}-\frac{1}{q}
, where
\frac{1}{p}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}}
.

The compactness of commutators of
T_{\alpha}
on unweighted and constant exponent weighted Lebesgue spaces had already been studied in
[
4
,
9
]
. Another related result appeared in
[
28
, Theorem 7.9]
, where the compactness of commutators of bilinear fractional Calderón–Zygmund operators on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces was established under the assumption that the target space is Banach. Theorem
1.4
extends the latter result to the multilinear setting without imposing any restriction on the target space.

The first ingredient for doing so is the general boundedness result for the commutators established by the authors in
[

, Theorem 7.8]
, which had adapted itself ideas from
[

.

(
[

]

-admissible quadruples

The second ingredient concerns compactness on unweighted constant exponent Lebesgue spaces. Such a result is the unweighted version of

of the form

\displaystyle Y_{j}=L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle Y=L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}}),

where
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{1},\infty)
is a proper
m
-admissible quadruple with

t<\min\{(p_{j})_{-}:\penalty\ j=1,\ldots,m\}

and

\frac{1}{p(\cdot)}-\frac{1}{q(\cdot)}=\gamma=\frac{\alpha}{n},

and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
is a
m
-tuple of weights such that
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)}
and
\vec{w}^{t}\in\mathcal{A}_{\frac{\vec{p}(\cdot)}{t},\frac{q(\cdot)}{t}}
.

Fix
(\vec{Z},Z)\in\Theta
. Similarly to the proof of Theorem
6.3
, we only have to show that for all
j=1,\ldots,m
,
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
maps
Z_{1}\times\ldots\times Z_{m}
into
Z
.

Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The boundedness assumption of part
(1)
of Theorem
1.4
for the operator
[T_{\alpha},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
is clearly just the conclusion of Theorem
6.4
.

Pick now a constant

d\in\left(t,\frac{m}{\gamma}\right).

Such a choice is possible, because
\gamma<\frac{m}{t}
by assumption. Then, Theorem
6.5
yields that the compactness assumption of part
(1)
of Theorem
1.4
is satisfied for the operator
[T_{\alpha},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
with the particular choices

\displaystyle X_{j}=L^{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}(w_{1,j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle X=L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}),

where

\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot):=(d,\ldots,d),\quad q_{1}(\cdot):=\frac{1}{\frac{m}{d}-\gamma},\quad\text{and}\quad\vec{w}_{1}:=(1,\ldots,1).

Therefore, applying part
(1)
of Theorem
1.4
we conclude the proof.
∎

6.E.
Multilinear Fourier multipliers

Let
s\in\mathbb{N}
and
m\geq 2
. Let
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{C}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm}\setminus\{0\})
be a bounded function and let
\Phi
be a Schwarz function on
\mathbb{R}^{nm}
satisfying the conditions

\mathrm{supp}(\Phi)\subset\left\{(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}:\penalty\ \frac{1}{2}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}|\xi_{j}|\leq 2\right\}

and

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\Phi(2^{-j}\xi)=1,\quad\forall\;\xi=(\xi_{1},\ldots,\xi_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{nm}\setminus\{0\}.

We consider the usual Sobolev space
W^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})
equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{W^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})}:=\left(\int_{\mathbb{R}^{nm}}(1+|\xi|^{2})^{s}|\widehat{f}(\xi)|^{2}\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi\right)^{1/2},

where
\widehat{f}
denotes the Fourier transform of
f
on all the variables in
\mathbb{R}^{nm}
. Define

\mathfrak{m}_{k}(\xi):=\Phi(\xi)\mathfrak{m}(2^{k}\xi),\quad\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{nm}

for each
k\in\mathbb{Z}
. We say that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})
if

\|\mathfrak{m}\|_{\mathcal{W}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})}:=\sup_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\|\mathfrak{m}_{k}\|_{W^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})}<\infty.

Now, let
T_{\mathfrak{m}}
denote the
m
-linear Fourier multiplier associated to the symbol
\mathfrak{m}
, defined by

T_{\mathfrak{m}}(f_{1},\ldots,f_{m})(x):=\int_{(\mathbb{R}^{n})^{m}}\mathfrak{m}(\xi)e^{2\pi ix\cdot(\xi_{1}+\cdots+\xi_{m})}\widehat{f}_{1}(\xi_{1})\cdots\widehat{f}_{m}(\xi_{m})\,\,\mathrm{d}\xi,\quad x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}

for Schwarz functions
f_{1},\ldots,f_{m}
on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The compactness properties of the commutators
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b]_{e_{j}}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
on constant exponent weighted Lebesgue spaces were studied in
[
25
,
9
]
.
Moreover, the compactness of commutators of bilinear Fourier multipliers on weighted variable Lebesgue spaces was obtained in
[
28
, Theorem 7.15]
, provided that the target space is Banach. Theorem
1.4
generalizes this to the multilinear setting without requiring the target space to be Banach.

To do this, we first need the general boundedness result for the commutators established by the authors in
[
28
, Theorem 7.13]
, which had adapted in turn some ideas from
[
36
]
.

6.7 Theorem

(
[
28
]
, Theorem 7.13)
.

Let
b\in\mathrm{BMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume
\frac{mn}{2}<s\leq mn
and
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})
. Then, there exists a constant
p_{0}\in\left(\frac{mn}{s},\infty\right)
depending only on
m,n
and
s
, such that the following holds. For all
r_{0}\in(p_{0},\infty)
, for all proper
m
-admissible quadruples
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},\infty)
with
q(\cdot)=p(\cdot)
and
r_{j}:=r_{0}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, and for all
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),(\vec{r},\infty)}
, we have that
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b]_{e_{j}}
maps
L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})
into
L^{p(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})
boundedly, for all
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Second, we need some information about the compactness on unweighted constant exponent Lebesgue spaces. Such a result is the unweighted version of
[
9
, Theorem 5.9]
.

6.8 Theorem

(
[
9
, Theorem 5.9]
)
.

Assume
\frac{mn}{2}<s\leq mn
and
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{nm})
. Let
1\leq t_{1},\ldots,t_{m}<2
with

\frac{s}{n}=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{t_{j}}.

Let
b\in\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Then, for all
1<p_{1},\ldots,p_{m}<\infty
with
t_{j}<p_{j}
for each
j=1,\ldots,m
and

\frac{1}{p}:=\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{p_{j}},

we have that
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b]_{e_{j}}
maps
L^{p_{1}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})\times\cdots\times L^{p_{m}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
into
L^{p}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
compactly for each
j=1,\ldots,m
.

Now, we can state and prove the main result of this subsection.

6.9 Theorem
.

Let
\vec{b}=(b_{1},\ldots,b_{m})
be a
m
-tuple of function in
\mathrm{CMO}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
. Assume
\frac{mn}{2}<s\leq mn
and
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathcal{W}^{s}(\mathbb{R}^{mn})
. Consider the constant
p_{0}\in\left(\frac{mn}{s},\infty\right)
depending only on
m
,
n
and
s
from Theorems
6.7
. Then, the following holds. For all
r_{0}\in(p_{0},\infty)
, for all proper
m
-admissible quadruples
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},\infty)
with
q(\cdot)=p(\cdot)
,
r_{j}:=r_{0}
,
j=1,\ldots,m
, and for all
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),(\vec{r},\infty)}
, we have that
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
maps
L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\ldots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})
into
L^{p(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})
compactly, for all
j=1,\ldots,m
. In particular,
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},\vec{b}]_{\Sigma}
maps
L^{p_{1}(\cdot)}(w_{1})\times\ldots\times L^{p_{m}(\cdot)}(w_{m})
into
L^{p(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}})
compactly.

Proof.

We apply part
(2)
of Theorem
1.4
. Fix
r_{0}\in(p_{0},\infty)
. The class
\Theta
we will be considering consists of all pairs
(\vec{Y},Y)
of the form

\displaystyle Y_{j}=L^{p_{j}(\cdot)}(w_{j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle Y=L^{q(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}}),

where
(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot),\vec{r},\infty)
is a proper
m
-admissible quadruple with
q(\cdot)=p(\cdot)
,

r_{j}:=r_{0}>p_{0}>\frac{mn}{s}\geq 1,\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

and
\vec{w}=(w_{1},\ldots,w_{m})
is a
m
-tuple of weights such that
\vec{w}\in\mathcal{A}_{(\vec{p}(\cdot),q(\cdot)),(\vec{r},\infty)}=\mathcal{A}_{\vec{p}(\cdot),(\vec{r},\infty)}
. Observe that in the notation of part
(2)
of Theorem
1.4
, for this particular class we have
\gamma=0
.

Fix
(\vec{Z},Z)\in\Theta
. Similarly to the proofs of Theorems
6.3
and
6.6
, we only have to show that for all
j=1,\ldots,m
,
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
maps
Z_{1}\times\ldots\times Z_{m}
into
Z
.

Fix
j\in\{1,\ldots,m\}
. The boundedness assumption of part
(2)
of Theorem
1.4
for
[T_{\mathfrak{m}},b]_{e_{j}}
is clearly just the content of Theorem
6.7
.

We check the compactness assumption of part
(2)
of Theorem
1.4
. Set

t_{j}:=\frac{mn}{s},\quad j=1,\ldots,m.

Then, we have
1\leq t_{j}<2
,
j=1,\ldots,m
and

\sum_{j=1}^{m}\frac{1}{t_{j}}=\frac{s}{n}.

Pick now
d>r_{0}
.
Then, Theorem
6.8
yields that the compactness assumption of Theorem
1.4
holds for the operator
[T_{m},b_{j}]_{e_{j}}
with the particular choices

\displaystyle X_{j}=L^{p_{1,j}(\cdot)}(w_{1,j}),\quad j=1,\ldots,m,

\displaystyle X=L^{q_{1}(\cdot)}(\nu_{\vec{w}_{1}}),

where

\vec{p}_{1}(\cdot):=(d,\ldots,d),\quad q_{1}(\cdot):=\frac{d}{m},\quad\text{and}\quad\vec{w}_{1}:=(1,\ldots,1).

Therefore, applying part
(2)
of Theorem
1.4
we conclude the proof.
∎
