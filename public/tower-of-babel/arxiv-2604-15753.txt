The truncation property and continuity for the long-range contact process on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d}
Stein Andreas Bethuelsen, Frank Namugera

Abstract
We consider a general class of contact processes on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} with potentially long-range interactions. By adapting well established renormalization arguments to the long-range setting we extend by now classical results for finite-range processes to this more general setting. Particularly, we provide general conditions on the decay of the interactions under which a supercritical process remains supercritical after truncation of the interaction parameter at a sufficiently large distance. Further, for the family of parameters satisfying this latter truncation property, we conclude that the probability of the process to never recover is continuous. AMS-MSC 2020 : 60K35; 82B43 Key Words : contact process, long-range, renormalization, truncation, continuity. 1 Introduction and main results Introduction. The ordinary, nearest neighbour, contact process is one of the classical interacting particle systems covered in the defining books [ 25 , 26 ] by Liggett. On the d d -dimensional integer lattice, it is the Markov process on the state space Ω ≔ { 0 , 1 } ℤ d \Omega\coloneqq\{0,1\}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}} where the state at a location x ∈ ℤ d x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d} transitions from 1 1 to 0 0 at rate δ ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) \delta\in(0,\infty) , and, if in state 1 1 , transmits its state to a neighbour y ∼ x y\sim x in state 0 0 at rate λ ∈ ( 0 , ∞ ) \lambda\in(0,\infty) . Motivation for studying this dynamic process stems among others from the modelling of infection spread in a population, where one interprets the nodes of the network as individuals of the population and these are healthy if in state 0 0 and infected if in state 1 1 . Another interpretation is that nodes in state 1 1 as occupied by an individual and nodes in state 0 0 as vacant, providing a simplified model for the evolution of a population. It also has its origin stemming from high energy physics. Today it forms a fundamental building block, and reference model, for a wealth of agent based models, see e.g. [ 24 ] . Although the framework of interacting particle systems was rather early defined for general interactions, by the time of Liggett’s first book [ 25 ] , most results for the ordinary contact process were restricted to the one-dimensional lattice. First with the groundbreaking work of Bezuidenhout and Grimmett in [ 4 ] the ordinary contact process on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 2 d\geq 2 , obtained a detailed and deep mathematical analysis, which not much later was extended to a broader class of (finite range) processes [ 5 ] . Since then the study of the ordinary contact process has witnessed a flourishing development to cover more general, and realistic, modelling settings, as partly summarized in [ 26 ] and in the recent books [ 24 , 34 ] . Our main results concern the LRCP on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} , d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 , and provide general conditions under which it is, in a certain sense, non-sensitive with respect to local changes of the parameter within the supercritical regime. In principle, one could also consider how sensitive the process is with respect to changes in the graph. Results in this direction have previously been obtained in [ 4 , 5 ] for finite range models. There it is shown that, if ϕ ⁡ ( λ , δ ) > 0 \phi(\lambda,\delta)>0 for this model on ℤ d + 1 \mathbb{Z}^{d+1} , d ≥ 1 d\geq 1 , then as concluded in [ 5 , Theorem 2.8] , the same holds true when restricted to a sufficiently thick one-dimensional slab [ − k , k ] d × ℤ [-k,k]^{d}\times\mathbb{Z} . In fact, by monotonicity this result immediately extends to the LRCP when λ ∈ Λ \lambda\in\Lambda is resilient since it then, by definition, satisfies the truncation property. For other graphs than ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} the only other continuity results we are aware of are for the ordinary contact process on regular trees [ 29 ] , but see also the discussion in [ 34 ] for related results in the setting of the contact process on finite (and random) graphs. Such questions have, however, received more attention for ordinary percolation, see in particular [ 11 , 15 , 16 ] . It would be very interesting to explore similar extensions for the LRCP. Extension to other attractive interacting particle systems. In this paper we focus solely on the LRCP, but our arguments extends to a much wider class of processes on ℤ d \mathbb{Z}^{d} after relatively minor modifications. Particularly, the work of [ 5 ] included a general class of continuous-time and discrete-time attractive spin-flip dynamics with finite-range dependence. In their general setting, the finite range assumption appears to be crucial. However, the extension presented in this paper can presumably be adapted without much effort to the sub-class of additive spin-flip dynamics for which there is a graphical representation akin to that in Section (see e.g. [ 25 , Chapter 3.6] ) and of which the LRCP is a key example. Renormalisation arguments reminiscent to those of [ 4 , 5 ] have also been successfully applied to various variants of the contact process, e.g. with aging and other random growth models [ 12 , 13 ] , in a dynamic evolving environment [ 32 , 31 ] or a two-level contact process [ 27 ] .
