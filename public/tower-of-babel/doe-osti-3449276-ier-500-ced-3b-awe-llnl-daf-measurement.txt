Executive Summary
This report summarizes the experiments in FY25 to support IER-500, a series of subcritical experiments
conducted by Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL) and the Atomic Weapons Establishment
(AWE). The experiments used radiation test objects (RTO) in various configurations that include plutonium.
The campaign occurred over the course of two weeks beginning on February 25, 2025, and ending on March
6, 2025. This was the first and only visit by AWE for FY25. The first week of the measurement campaign
involved setup of the detector systems to be used the following week. Additional time was utilized to
perform source checks as well as collect background data. Over the course of the second week, a total of
three unique configurations of LLNL-RTO-016 were measured with a combination of neutron and gamma-
ray diagnostic systems. No equipment failures or safety/security issues were encountered. Experiment
participants included: Nigel Kelsall (AWE), Lawrence Jones (AWE), Chris Ryden (AWE), Paul Yap-
Chiongco (LLNL), James Sevier (LLNL), and Samuel Varghese (LLNL). AWE is planning another trip for
mid to late FY26. LLNL is in the process of procuring and building new diagnostics equipment, software,
SNM, and moderators and reflectors.

Introduction
The Atomic Weapons Establishment (AWE) has continued access to the fissile material held at the Device
Assembly Facility (DAF), Nevada, to underwrite current and develop future radiometric assay tools used
to support Counter Terrorism (CT) activities (JOWOG 29). This offer has been supported in the US by both
the National Criticality Safety Program (NCSP) and the Lawrence Livermore National Laboratory (LLNL)
Global Security (GS) Directorate for several years and has permitted access to fissile materials that are not
readily provisioned for at AWE. The NCSP provides the technical staff and facility support staff for this
work along with granting facility time.
Initial motivation for early measurement campaigns concentrated upon the collection of benchmarking data
for geometries of varying multiplication to allow new UK neutron detection assets to be tested. Various
visits provided a relevant range of data to allow the UK to underwrite the revised initial deployable neutron
detector (ND)2 and provided experimental data to justify the replacement of further neutron detection
equipment to replace the aging neutron correlation systems. Further campaigns beyond this have been used
to extend the collected data set in order to include Pu oxide as well as HEU.
Recent campaigns have shifted to collect data from bulk fissile items through a variety of mediums and
barriers (moderators and attenuators) with the aim of understanding the response of UK deployable
response assets. These measurements have been used to further develop the understanding and experience
of UK CT responders. Measurements moving forward are expected to expand the degree of realism in the
measurement item and will aim to better represent threat items of concern to the UK in the hope of providing
further aid in the learning and development of responders. To this end, it is the desire of AWE to further
strengthen collaboration through the UK design and manufacturing of test geometries which can be used
with US material for measurements at the DAF.

Experiment Description and Measurements
AWE equipment was successfully delivered to the DAF prior to the beginning of measurement activities
and was in the facility ready for use upon arrival. The equipment included:

 • Four (ND)2 He-3 Neutron Detectors with all accompanying electronics and support equipment
 • One 110%-efficient liquid nitrogen cooled gamma-ray detector and associated counting electronics
 • One Detective-X
 • One identiFINDER
 • One Fission Meter and associated Fission Meter controller.

The goal of this experimental campaign was to collect data on a “realistic” pseudo threat item with extant
UK detection equipment following current UK deployment methodologies. This is to provide a realistic
data set that can be used to bolster the training of UK responders. Presently, responder training is heavily
reliant upon synthetic data. In addition, AWE would like to collect the data set to provide another benchmark
set to allow continued development of UK neutron analysis theory. These measurements are particularly
important for the treatment of neutrons due to the multiplying effect of the material and other interactions
which may be present (i.e., alpha n, etc).

AWE successfully completed several measurements with a single assembly surrounded by a varying degree
of reflector and moderator material. Measurements were taken with the bare SNM item, the pseudo threat

configuration of material, and the pseudo threat configuration of material in a blind box arrangement. As
previously noted, the goal of this set of measurements was to provide data which could ultimately be used
to further develop the understanding and experience of UK CT responders. Measurements followed a fairly
standardized emergency response approach. The handheld identiFINDER was utilized for hotspot mapping
while the (ND)2, Fission Meter, and gamma-ray detection systems were utilized to perform passive data
collection of both neutron and gamma-rays. This data will be processed over the course of FY25 and FY26
and compared against an MCNP model which provides a set of simulated data.

Conclusions and Future Work
Overall, this AWE visit was quite successful. There were no delays to the receipt of equipment and its
transfer to the DAF for measurements. Over the course of the second week, several high precision data sets
were collected for a variety of configurations which utilized the same underlying assembly. AWE continues
to work on developing the inject data and simulated comparisons to the data collected over the course of
the campaign. Towards the conclusion of the trip, discussions were held regarding the next steps and future
trips. Tentatively, AWE is set to return mid to late next calendar year to continue measurements geared
towards generating inject data for higher fidelity items of concern to the UK. The US continues to develop
new RTOs with increasing degrees of fidelity and hopes to be able to manufacture and provide RTOs which
better align with the technical touchstones of the UK.
