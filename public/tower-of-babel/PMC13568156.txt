Introduction

Preoperative fasting is a long-standing practice aimed at reducing the risk of pulmonary aspiration and respiratory complications during anaesthesia. The rationale is based on minimising gastric content volume and thereby lowering the likelihood of regurgitation and aspiration.1–3

The actual duration of preoperative fasting in routine clinical practice often far exceeds current preoperative fasting guidelines.4 Prolonged fasting has been associated with reduced patient well-being and an increased incidence of postoperative symptoms such as thirst, anxiety, postoperative nausea and vomiting, and fatigue.5–14 Increased concern regarding the consequences of over fasting has led to a growing trend towards adopting more liberal fasting protocols, specifically allowing patients to consume clear fluids less than two hours before anaesthesia.6,9,13,15 Preliminary data suggest that this approach can improve patient well-being without a meaningful increase in the risk of aspiration;6,7,9,13,15–17 however, the absence of large-scale randomised controlled trials and the lack of systematic evaluation limit this conclusion.

Another growing area of research is the use of bedside gastric ultrasound to assess stomach content. This non-invasive technique might be helpful in identifying patients at higher risk of aspiration and in guiding clinical decisions, especially when gastric emptying is delayed or fasting has been shorter than recommended.8,18–32

Objectives

The objective of this guideline is to provide evidence-based recommendations on the duration of preoperative fasting for clear fluids before anaesthesia. Additionally, it intends to provide recommendations on the use of ultrasound to evaluate gastric content during preoperative evaluation before elective procedures.

Methods

Guidelines development group

The guideline development group will be composed of the Task Force (TF), which includes content experts and patient representatives, and a methods team with methodologists. The guideline development process will be overseen by an external auditor, who will act as a nonvoting member to ensure that the process adheres to GRADE methodology. A steering group and an oversight committee will also be established to provide strategic direction and independent supervision of the process. The roles, composition and responsibilities of these bodies will be explicitly defined. Members of the steering group and/or oversight committee, as well as the methodologists, will not have voting privileges in formulating recommendations.

The European Society of Anaesthesiology and Intensive Care (ESAIC) will support the guideline development by providing logistical assistance through its staff and by financing the project, including travel expenses and honoraria for the systematic reviews. All participating members will declare any conflicts of interest, which will be analysed by ESAIC. Participants with significant intellectual or financial conflicts of interest may take part in discussions of the evidence and may offer comments or opinions. However, they will not be permitted to participate in discussions that determine the direction or strength of the recommendations. Additionally, they will not be allowed to vote should a formal vote be required. All methodologists must be free from financial and intellectual conflicts of interest in order to participate in the development of the systematic review and guideline. Authorship of the guideline and any accompanying manuscripts will be determined in accordance with the criteria established by the International Committee of Medical Journal Editors (ICMJE).

This guideline will be developed in collaboration with the European Association for Endoscopic Surgery (EAES). The EAES will contribute two representatives to the guideline development panel to ensure the multidisciplinary nature of the group and to incorporate additional perspectives into the development process.

Guideline development process

Key questions

The ESAIC guidelines committee chair, in collaboration with the co-chairs, suggested two main questions to be developed in this guideline. During the first kick-off meeting, the questions were discussed and approved by the panel members.

(1)Should adults follow a liberal fasting approach for clear fluids (0–2 h) or standard fasting times (2–4 h) before elective anaesthesia?

(2)Should adult patients undergo routine ultrasound to assess gastric content before elective anaesthesia?

Should adult patients undergo routine ultrasound to assess gastric content before elective anaesthesia?

The task force will generate a comprehensive list of potential outcomes, clearly define them and rate each as critical, important or not important for decision-making. For question one, prespecified outcomes include aspiration, mortality, pneumonia, length of stay, thirst, nausea, vomiting and combined nausea and vomiting.

Systematic reviews and additional evidence

The Evidence Synthesis Team (EST) will conduct two systematic reviews following methods outlined in the Cochrane Handbook for Systematic Reviews of Interventions. Searches will be conducted in CENTRAL, Embase and MEDLINE, with no language or date restrictions. Both published and unpublished studies will be sought, including through forward citation searching (snowballing) and backward citation searching (reference list checking).

The systematic review for question one will include both adult and paediatric patients, given the anticipated low number of studies and the similarity of fasting practices across these populations; a prespecified subgroup analysis will compare adults and paediatric patients, with results reported separately if a statistically significant difference is identified (P < 0.05). The intervention is defined as liberal fasting (0–2 h) for clear fluids, encompassing any type of clear liquid, compared to standard fasting (2–4 h). Excluded study designs include case series, case reports, cross-sectional studies and nonprimary literature (systematic reviews, meta-analyses, editorials, letters and opinion articles); studies on fasting for solids or comparing fasting durations beyond 4 h for clear fluids will also be excluded.

Additionally, systematic searches will be conducted to identify studies on: patient values (typically reported as utility values for different health states) and barriers and facilitators affecting the acceptability of the intervention among different stakeholders, as well as its feasibility. Given that this literature is typically scarce, no restrictions will be applied regarding the population or study design.

During this process, existing systematic reviews will also be identified; where a recent, high-quality review addressing one of the key questions is found, it will be adopted rather than duplicated.

Data synthesis and analysis

When possible, the evidence will be synthesised using the Hartung–Knapp–Sidik–Jonkman (HKSJ) method, with between-study variance (τ2) estimated using the Restricted Maximum Likelihood (REML) approach. Effect estimates will be reported as risk differences for dichotomous outcomes and mean differences for continuous outcomes, each accompanied by their corresponding 95% confidence intervals. In addition, a 95% prediction interval will be calculated using a t-distribution. For diagnostic test accuracy studies, meta-analyses will be conducted using a bivariate random-effects model. If the available data are not suitable for meta-analysis, a narrative summary will be developed.

The risk of bias of the included studies will be assessed using a structured approach: RoB 2.0 for randomised controlled trials,33 ROBINS-I for nonrandomised studies of interventions34 and QUADAS-2 for diagnostic test accuracy studies.35 We will also assess the certainty of the evidence following the GRADE approach. Two methodologists will assess the domains of study limitations, inconsistency, indirectness, imprecision and dissemination bias and will rate the certainty of the evidence for each outcome as high, moderate, low or very low following GRADE guidance.36

We will consider all predefined outcomes during the evidence-to-decision process. During this process, our external auditor will provide their expert guidance on our methodology but will not be permitted to make judgements about evidence-to-decision domains or final recommendations. During the meeting, TF members will reach agreement on the wording, the direction and strength of the recommendation(s) through discussion, with the opportunity to suggest revisions or additional recommendations in alignment with GRADE methodology. Voting will be conducted only if necessary.

Publication and dissemination

This clinical practice guideline will be published in the ESAIC official journal, European Journal of Anaesthesiology (EJA), along with the systematic review(s). The recommendations will be presented at the annual ESAIC meeting, Euroanaesthesia. The guidelines group will also work for broader dissemination via infographics, promotion, social media dissemination, etc.

Target users and implications

This guideline is intended for anaesthesiologists, surgeons, nurses and other healthcare professionals involved in the preoperative management of adults. Most importantly, this guideline can help inform patients about the best preoperative fasting practices.

The systematic review, meta-analysis and evidence-to-decision framework will further identify evidence gaps for future areas of research.

Peer-review

The guidelines and the infographics will be available for ESAIC members for 4 weeks to receive comments before submission to the EJA. Important and relevant comments will be incorporated by the Task Force into the guidelines.

Strengths and limitations

We will develop this clinical practice guideline according to robust methodological standards outlined by GRADE. We will develop patient-centred recommendations, informed by the evidence and the perspectives of a diverse multidisciplinary panel.

Limitations will include general challenges such as the element of subjectivity present with interpreting evidence, which we will aim to mitigate by adhering to robust methodology. Furthermore, patient partners may not reflect the opinions of all patients, and this will be considered during the evidence-to-decision framework. Finally, this guideline will not be intended to dictate clinical practice, and clinicians should use this guideline as a guide while making patient-tailored decisions.

Funding

The ESAIC will support financially the development of this clinical practice guideline.

Conclusion

This clinical practice guideline will provide rigorous, evidence-informed recommendations to guide healthcare professionals and other key stakeholders in making patient-centred decisions regarding preoperative fasting for clear fluids before anaesthesia in adults, and the use of gastric ultrasound to assess gastric content during the preoperative evaluation prior to elective procedures.
