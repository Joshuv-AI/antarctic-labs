The Clinical, Genetic, Psychosocial, and Care Landscape of X-Linked Dystonia-Parkinsonism in Filipino Men: A Scoping Review
Authors: Mary Dioise Ramos, Jennifer Manning, Jolie Harris, Marie Adorno, Rowan Marye, Lorraine Evangelista
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundX-linked dystonia-parkinsonism (XDP) is a hereditary movement disorder predominantly affecting Filipino males from Panay Island ancestry. Existing research emphasizes molecular and motor aspects, neglecting diagnostic, therapeutic, and psychosocial impacts.ObjectivesTo map the literature on XDP in Filipino men, including its genetic basis, clinical features, diagnostic methods, treatment options, and psychosocial effects.MethodsWe conducted a systematic search following the Joanna Briggs Institute (JBI) methodology and the Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) principles.ResultsA total of 27 studies were included. Symptoms commenced from localized to widespread dystonia. Parkinsonism manifested within one to two years. Most examined patients exhibited the TAF1 genetic mutation, and diagnosis takes years. Deep-brain stimulation reduced dystonia severity. Pharmacological therapies produced some benefits. Qualitative analysis revealed stigma and identity loss, caregiver burden, financial hardship, and coping and resilience.ConclusionsXDP exhibits a rapid and severe growth trend; however, access to diagnostic tests, modern medicines, and supportive care remains unequal. To address these disparities, it is essential to improve access to genetic and clinical treatments and incorporate psychosocial support systems, including stigma reduction, caregiver assistance, and community networks, into a comprehensive care model.Keywords: X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism, XDP, Filipino men, neurogenetics, deep brain stimulation, rare disease, caregiving, psychosocial burden, Philippines, scoping review

1. Introduction

X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism (XDP) is a rare genetic neurological movement condition that mostly affects Filipino men, particularly those with Panay Island ancestry in the Philippines (Bragg et al., 2019; Domingo et al., 2015). X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism, formerly known as “Lubag syndrome,” is now recognized as a distinct disorder resulting from a retrotransposon insertion into the TAF1 gene on the X chromosome (Deng et al., 2008; Kawarai et al., 2017). The mutation causes a biphasic clinical course, beginning with focal dystonia in early to middle adulthood and progressing to generalized dystonia, as well as Parkinsonian symptoms, including bradykinesia, stiffness, and postural instability (Evidente et al., 2002; Rosales, 2010).

Despite the increasing clarity of genetic and clinical characteristics, healthcare systems continue to inadequately recognize XDP, particularly in rural regions of the Philippines, where access to specialists is challenging (Alcachupas et al., 2020; De Roxas & Jamora, 2019). Most research has focused on either genetic findings (Reyes et al., 2021) or surgical outcomes in restricted cohorts, leading to considerable gaps in understanding diagnostic pathways, treatment accessibility, and psychological implications (Alonto & Jamora, 2024; Brüggemann et al., 2019). The impact of XDP extends beyond patients to include families, caregivers, and the broader community. It impacts professional responsibilities, social interactions, and psychological well-being. Geographic and financial barriers, along with the inequitable distribution of genetic testing and neurosurgical services, lead to substantial inequities in healthcare delivery (Alcachupas et al., 2023).

This scoping review systematically outlines the literature on XDP in Filipino men, thereby addressing extant gaps. The following specific objectives are: 1) outline clinical and neuropsychiatric manifestations as well as disease progression; 2) assess existing diagnostic methodologies and the challenges related to timely validation; 3) consolidate pharmacological, surgical, and supportive management strategies; and 4) integrate evidence concerning psychosocial effects and lived experience. This review aims to integrate genetic, clinical, and sociocultural perspectives to advocate for a more personalized and equitable approach to XDP care.

2. Methods

This scoping study was conducted using the Joanna Briggs Institute methodology (Pearson et al., 2020), which is a systematic enhancement of Arksey and O'Malley’s basic framework. The Preferred Reporting Items for Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses Extension for Scoping Reviews (PRISMA-ScR) checklist was also adhered to (Arksey & O'Malley, 2005; Peters et al., 2020; Tricco et al., 2018). We sought to outline the breadth of evidence for XDP in Filipino men by first developing specific research questions, then employing a thorough search strategy that utilized pre-existing inclusion and exclusion criteria. We collected and organized relevant data and then combined the quantitative and qualitative results into a unified narrative.

To capture the full scope of published literature evidence, we searched six electronic databases: PubMed, Embase, Scopus, and Web of Science, as well as relevant conference proceedings (including abstracts from the International Congress of Parkinson’s Disease and Movement Disorders) and major Philippine university thesis repositories. The search spanned January 2014 through August 2025, with the final search conducted on August 18, 2025. There were no language restrictions at the retrieval stage; however, only records with English abstracts or translations were retained for full review. We constructed search strings (see Table 1) for each database we included.

The focus of our inclusion criteria was primary research on Filipino male XDP patients. Both qualitative and quantitative research methods, including case series, cohort studies, interviews, and focus groups. The review included publications that covered genetic discoveries, clinical features and progression, diagnostic techniques, pharmaceutical and surgical therapies, and supportive interventions, including case series, cohort studies, interviews, focus groups, and psychosocial outcomes. We excluded narrative evaluations that lacked original data, animal or in vitro studies, and any research that did not specifically address Filipino populations. Two independent reviewers screened all titles, abstracts, and full texts; discrepancies were resolved through discussion or, when necessary, by a third reviewer. In a 10% random sample of records, inter-rater agreement was substantial (Cohen’s κ = 0.82 for title/abstract screening; κ = 0.87 for full-text review). We developed and executed a pilot test of a standardized data-extraction form in Microsoft Excel, which documented publication details, study design, sample demographics, clinical and genetic findings, diagnostic procedures, treatment modalities, and psychological variables. Descriptive statistics, including medians, ranges, frequencies, and percentages, were employed to summarize quantitative variables, whilst thematic analysis was utilized for qualitative data, with two analysts independently coding the extracts. We consolidated findings into three primary themes through an iterative consensus process. Our conclusive narrative synthesis integrates these quantitative summaries and qualitative themes to demonstrate the convergence of biological causes, clinical trajectories, and psychosocial realities in Filipino men with XDP.

Most studies originated from Metro Manila (n = 10) and the provinces of Panay Island (n = 7), highlighting an ethnogeographic concentration. International groups conducted the remaining studies or drew from broader Philippine cohorts. 3.2. Clinical Trajectory and Diagnostic Challenges

According to Acuña et al. (2023) and Lee et al. (2002), symptoms of XDP in males from the Philippines typically appear between the ages of 30 and 40. However, they can begin anywhere from late adolescence to middle life. There are two stages to the disorder. The first is focal dystonia, which typically impacts the muscles in the jaw, oromandibular area, and neck. Over four to five years, the disorder progresses from focal dystonia, which typically affects the muscles in the neck, jaw, and oromandibular region, to generalized dystonia (Brüggemann et al., 2019).

Despite a well-established clinical course, prompt diagnosis remains challenging. Most individuals exhibiting symptoms must wait one to two years before receiving a definitive diagnosis. This delay frequently arises from geographic and socioeconomic constraints. Genetic testing for the TAF1 SVA retrotransposon insertion, present in over 95% of verified cases, demonstrates high accuracy and sensitivity (Domingo et al., 2015; Pasco et al., 2015). However, due to the predominance of testing sites in urban areas, individuals from distant locations, such as Panay Island, must undertake extensive travel to access care. The delays are exacerbated by the substantial cost of testing, limited insurance coverage, and the absence of established referral pathways (Acuña et al., 2023).

Neuroimaging modalities, including MRI and transcranial sonography, facilitate diagnosis by revealing progressive striatal atrophy, particularly in the caudate and putamen (Hanssen et al., 2023). Contemporary imaging remains difficult to access outside of major hospitals. Emerging technologies, such as phase-sensitive optical coherence tomography, appear beneficial for surgical planning; however, they remain challenging to obtain (Jamora et al., 2021). These diagnostic issues are particularly critical due to the rapid progression of motor symptoms. Failure to utilize early intervention may result in enduring problems and diminished quality of life. The interplay between clinical deterioration and systemic access limitations stresses the need for decentralized genetic services, portable diagnostic devices, and streamlined referral systems to ensure timely and equitable care.

3.3. Treatment Outcomes and Therapeutic Limitations

Pharmacological treatments for XDP primarily aim to relieve symptoms, with their effectiveness varying depending on the specific medication used (Rosales, 2010; Wadia et al., 2010). Anticholinergics and benzodiazepines may provide minor relief for focal dystonia, whereas levodopa and dopamine agonists typically have limited success in treating Parkinsonian symptoms (Wadia et al., 2010). Botulinum toxin injections are commonly used for cranio-cervical dystonia, but barriers to access and high costs, especially in rural areas, limit their widespread use (De Roxas & Jamora, 2019; Wadia et al., 2010). Deep brain stimulation (DBS), particularly targeting the globus pallidus internus (GPi), has demonstrated long-term benefits in motor function and quality of life for some patients, although outcomes vary depending on the disease stage and electrode placement (Wadia et al., 2010; Donlon et al., 2025; Rasche, 2022).

No existing treatment can slow disease progression, and the therapeutic benefits are frequently limited or short-lived (Rosales, 2010). Cost, geographic accessibility, and the need for specialized neurosurgical expertise significantly hinder individuals’ access to surgery (De Roxas & Jamora, 2019). 3.4. Psychosocial and Supportive Care

Equally important are the non-motor and psychosocial aspects of XDP, such as cognitive decline, emotional distress, stigma, and loss of social roles. As shown in Table 7, these challenges revolve around four main themes: stigma and identity loss, caregiver burden, financial hardship, and coping and resilience. Though often overlooked in clinical practice, these factors are vital to patient well-being. Incorporating psychosocial interventions, peer support, and community resources into clinical care is essential for delivering patient-centered management.

Likewise, multidisciplinary supportive care is essential for maintaining individuals’ functionality and enhancing their quality of life (Alcachupas et al., 2022). The primary objectives of physiotherapy and occupational therapy are to maintain mobility, diminish contractures, and facilitate the performance of everyday activities (Alcachupas et al., 2022). The Parkinson’s Wellness Program at the University of California Irvine Health exemplifies a community-based initiative that provides group exercise and educational resources (Domingo et al., 2014b). Nevertheless, culturally adapted services are scarce for XDP (Alcachupas et al., 2023). The integration of culturally appropriate care methods remains a primary focus for enhancing outcomes in Filipino populations (Alcachupas et al., 2023).

4. Discussion

This review reveals a clear and concerning reality: XDP in Filipino men is both genetically distinct and systematically overlooked. Rather than merely enumerating its biphasic dystonia-to-parkinsonism progression and molecular underpinnings, it is imperative to interpret these patterns as an indication to modify both therapeutic approaches and research paradigms.

4.1. Interpretation of Key Findings

This review emphasizes the urgent need for early diagnosis and prompt treatment of XDP. This unique neurodegenerative disorder often remains undetected within the Philippine healthcare system (Rosales, 2010). Its rapid progression from localized dystonia to generalized dystonia and eventually Parkinsonism over five to eight years emphasizes the need and opportunity to slow down its development and reduce the burden on caregivers and the economy (Hanssen et al., 2023; Lee et al., 2002).

Scientific advances, such as PCR-based molecular diagnostics (Evidente et al., 2004; Pasco et al., 2015; Plummer et al., 2005) and DBS (Brüggemann et al., 2019), have broadened the options for diagnosing and managing XDP. However, these benefits are not equitably available. People in rural areas—particularly on Panay Island—face long trips to urban centers, high out-of-pocket costs, and extended wait times for testing and surgery (Acuña et al., 2023; De Roxas & Jamora, 2019).

The rapid change from focal to generalized dystonia and the subsequent emergence of Parkinsonian features stress the importance of early recognition: delayed diagnosis accelerates disability. It amplifies the burden on families and health systems (Alcachupas et al., 2023). Although PCR assays and DBS expand treatment options, rural populations frequently face travel distances exceeding 100 km, high costs, and long wait times—inequities that exacerbate the social and economic impacts of XDP (Bragg et al., 2017). Patients from urban areas are more likely to access confirmatory genetic testing, neurosurgical procedures, and ongoing follow-up care, whereas those in remote areas often remain underserved.

Non-motor and psychosocial issues, such as cognitive slowing, emotional distress, stigma, and social role loss, are frequently overlooked in standard care but are vital for patient well-being (Alcachupas et al., 2023; Alinsangao et al., 2018). As summarized in Table 7, these challenges cluster around four cross-cutting themes: stigma & identity loss, caregiver burden, financial hardship, and coping & resilience. Therefore, integrating psychological support, peer networks, and community-based services with medical and surgical treatments is crucial for a genuinely patient-centered XDP care approach.

4.2. Implications for Policy and Practice

Enhancing the accessibility of PCR-based genetic testing is crucial. Tele-genetics platforms and collaborations with local laboratories can reduce the expenses and distance associated with obtaining a confirmatory diagnosis (Pasco et al., 2015). Initiatives must ensure that insurance coverage includes both molecular diagnostics and subsequent rehabilitative therapy, thereby mitigating a substantial financial obstacle for patients and their families (De Roxas & Jamora, 2019). Standardizing referral pathways in primary care can create a more efficient process from initial doctor visits to specialist appointments, improving overall access (Rosales, 2010).

Targeted educational campaigns and anti-stigma efforts can help increase public understanding of XDP’s main features and the importance of seeking help early at the community level. Emphasizing early intervention is crucial in reducing the social isolation frequently associated with severe movement disorders (Alcachupas et al., 2023; Alinsangao et al., 2018). Developing a nationwide XDP registry is a vital step; a detailed epidemiological database would support tracking genetic differences, disease progression, and quality of life, thereby guiding clinical practices and research. The team in the Collaborative Center for X-linked Dystonia Parkinsonism at Massachusetts General Hospital is dedicated to furthering the understanding of this rare genetic disorder.

The expansion and decentralization of DBS services are crucial for reducing disparities in neurosurgical treatments. These centers should ideally include sliding-scale subsidies to improve access to this established treatment for marginalized populations (Brüggemann et al., 2019; Donlon et al., 2025). Investing in community-focused psychosocial programs, such as culturally appropriate support groups, organized caregiver training, and easily accessible mental health services, can help reduce the non-motor burden of XDP (Alcachupas et al., 2023; Alinsangao et al., 2018). Ultimately, targeted research on mobile clinics, telemedicine systems, and innovative pharmaceuticals will yield the evidence necessary to develop equitable and scalable care pathways for XDP across the Philippines (Yu et al., 2021).

4.3. Strengths and Limitations

This review’s strengths lie in its systematic and clear approach to combining clinical, genetic, and psychosocial perspectives. It is imperative to recognize several limitations. First, relying on English-language publications and indexed studies introduces a selection bias that may exclude grey literature, regional dissertations, and community reports in Filipino languages. Second, the variety of study designs prevented a meta-analysis from being performed. Lastly, the lack of longitudinal psychological data makes it challenging to understand how stigma, coping mechanisms, and caregiver burden change over time. Future research should incorporate regional archives, Filipino-language studies, and longitudinal qualitative or cohort studies to develop a more comprehensive evidence base.

5. Conclusion

X-linked dystonia-parkinsonism in Filipino males advances rapidly and severely. Access to diagnosis, treatment, and supportive care remains inequitable. Patients living in remote regions experience prolonged waiting times for treatment and possess limited options for care. The psychological impacts on families are predominantly overlooked. To address these disparities, programs and services must focus on reducing the cost of genetic testing, decentralizing surgical and rehabilitative therapies, and incorporating psychosocial support as a standard component of care. The establishment of a nationwide XDP registry and community-based interventions is a crucial measure for advancing a more equitable and patient-centered healthcare system.
