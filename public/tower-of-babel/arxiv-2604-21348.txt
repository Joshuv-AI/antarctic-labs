Ghost Degrees of Freedom Without Quantum Runaway: Exact Moment Bounds from an Operator Conservation Law
Christopher Ewasiuk, Stefano Profumo

Abstract
We prove an exact quantum conservation law for a harmonic oscillator coupled to a ghost degree of freedom: a second classical conserved quantity lifts to a quantum operator that commutes with the Hamiltonian with no ℏ \hbar corrections, yielding a rigorous, state-independent upper bound on the mean squared phase-space radius for all time and every quantum state with finite initial second moments. The proof uses only canonical commutation relations and the Leibniz rule; it requires no confining potential, no spectral assumptions, and no perturbative expansion. The interaction studied here is bounded and vanishes at large separations, the generic situation in effective field theory, yet this suffices to guarantee quantum stability in the sense of bounded second moments. Three independent numerical frameworks (Heisenberg picture, Schrödinger picture, and Fock-space diagonalization) confirm wavepacket confinement below the analytic bound, a real energy spectrum, and Poisson level statistics numerically consistent with an integrable structure. The absence of a confining potential means the proof is silent on spectral discreteness and the existence of a ground state; those questions, addressed for polynomial confining interactions in concurrent work 12 ; 11 , remain open for the interaction class studied here and represent the sharpest targets for future work. Ghost quantum instability is therefore not an inevitable consequence of a wrong-sign kinetic term but depends critically on the interaction structure.

Introduction

Ghost fields, degrees of freedom with a wrong-sign kinetic term, appear across

One class of scalar field models that accommodates

For the class of models where a ghost

We emphasize that our proof is quantum-mechanical rather

Recent work by Deffayet, Mukohyama, and

II
Model

We study the two degree of freedom quantum system (natural units
\hbar=\omega=m=1
)

\hat{H}=\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2}\right)-\tfrac{1}{2}\!\left(\hat{p}_{y}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}\right)+V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y}),

(1)

where
\hat{x},\hat{p}_{x}
are the normal (positive-energy) oscillator operators,

\hat{y},\hat{p}_{y}
are the ghost operators (note the overall minus sign on the ghost
sector), and

V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y})=\lambda\!\left[\!\left(\hat{x}^{2}-\hat{y}^{2}-1\right)^{\!2}+4\hat{x}^{2}\right]^{-1/2}.

(2)

The potential
V_{I}
(the subscript denotes the interaction term) satisfies

0<|V_{I}|\leq|\lambda|
everywhere, since the expression under the square root
is bounded below by unity, as shown by the geometric identity (
5
)
below. Canonical commutation relations hold within each sector; operators
from different sectors commute. In the classical limit
\hbar\to 0
,
Eq. (
1
) reduces to the integrable system of Ref.
13
,
whose Lyapunov stability was established in
Refs.
9
.

The Hamiltonian (
1
) requires care with respect to the
standard
L^{2}
inner product, because the ghost kinetic term
flips the sign of the
\hat{p}^{2}_{y}
contribution. As shown in
Theorem
1
,
\hat{H}
is nevertheless self-adjoint on

\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
via the bounded perturbation theorem, since
V_{I}
is a bounded
self-adjoint multiplication operator. Stone’s theorem then guarantees a strongly
continuous unitary group
U(t)=e^{-it\hat{H}}
, making the bound (
7
) rigorously
applicable in the continuum. Throughout, we work with the truncated Fock space and
discrete position grid as concrete computational frameworks.

III
Exact quantum conservation law

III.1
From Classical to Quantum: The conserved operator

The classical system
13
possesses, in addition to
H
, a second
conserved quantity

C=K^{2}+\left(p_{x}^{2}+x^{2}\right)-\left(x^{2}-y^{2}-1\right)V_{I}(x,y),

(3)

where
K=p_{y}x+p_{x}y
is the hyperbolic boost generator. We promote this to
the quantum operator

\hat{C}=\hat{K}^{2}+\left(\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2}\right)-\left(\hat{x}^{2}-\hat{y}^{2}-1\right)V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y}),

(4)

with
\hat{K}=\hat{p}_{y}\hat{x}+\hat{p}_{x}\hat{y}
. Since
[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{y}]=[\hat{y},\hat{p}_{x}]=0
,

\hat{K}
is Hermitian. There is no operator-ordering ambiguity in
\hat{C}
: both

V_{I}
and
(\hat{x}^{2}-\hat{y}^{2}-1)
are functions of commuting position operators.

The key geometric identity

(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}-(y^{2}-x^{2})^{2}=2x^{2}+2y^{2}+1\geq 1,

(5)

which holds for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, yields the pointwise bound

\left|(y^{2}-x^{2})\right|V_{I}(x,y)<|\lambda|\quad\forall\,(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2},

(6)

which promotes to the operator inequality

-|\lambda|\hat{1}<(\hat{y}^{2}-\hat{x}^{2})V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y})<|\lambda|\hat{1}
.
We show in the next section that
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
exactly for all

\hbar>0
, with no operator-ordering remainder, and that this conservation law
yields the main result for all
t_{b}>0
:

\bigl\langle\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}+\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{p}_{y}^{2}\bigr\rangle(t_{b})\;\leq\;\bigl\langle\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}+\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{p}_{y}^{2}\bigr\rangle(0)\;+\;4|\lambda|.

(7)

This is a statement about second moments of the quantum state, not its
spatial support: the wavefunction can in principle develop tails outside any
compact region while
\langle\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}\rangle
remains bounded.
Subject to this qualification, the bound holds for every quantum state, pure
or mixed, coherent or Fock; independently of
\hbar
, the wavefunction, and
the energy. It reduces to the classical bound of Ref.
13

as
\hbar\to 0
, since no
\hbar
-dependent terms appear in Eq. (
7
).

IV
Operator-theoretic formulation and conserved moment bound

We place the model on the Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})
,
with canonical operators

x,\,y\ \text{(multiplication)},\qquad p_{x}=-i\partial_{x},\quad p_{y}=-i\partial_{y},

defined on the usual Sobolev domains. Throughout we set
\hbar=1
.
Define the free operator

H_{0}:=\frac{1}{2}(p_{x}^{2}+x^{2})-\frac{1}{2}(p_{y}^{2}+y^{2}),

(8)

the interaction
V_{I}(x,y)=\lambda\big[(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}\big]^{-1/2}
,
the boost operator
K:=xp_{y}+yp_{x}
, the conserved operator

C:=K^{2}+(p_{x}^{2}+x^{2})-(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)V_{I}(x,y),

(9)

the positive semi-definite operator

\Sigma:=K^{2}+\tfrac{1}{2}(p_{x}^{2}+x^{2})+\tfrac{1}{2}(p_{y}^{2}+y^{2})\geq 0,

(10)

and the conserved combination
E:=C-H
. The finite Hermite span

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fin}}:=\mathrm{span}\{h_{n}(x)h_{m}(y):n,m\geq 0\}

is invariant under
x,y,p_{x},p_{y}
, and hence under
H
,
K
, and
C
.

Theorem 1 (Self-adjointness).

The Hamiltonian

H:=H_{0}+V_{I}

is self-adjoint on
D(H_{0})
and essentially self-adjoint on

\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fin}}
.

Proof.

Let
A:=\frac{1}{2}(-\partial_{t}^{2}+t^{2})
denote the one-dimensional harmonic
oscillator on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
, with domain
D(A)
. Then

H_{0}=A\otimes I-I\otimes A

on
\mathcal{H}\cong L^{2}(\mathbb{R})_{x}\otimes L^{2}(\mathbb{R})_{y}
.
Since
A\otimes I
and
I\otimes A
strongly commute,
H_{0}
is self-adjoint on

D(H_{0})=D(A\otimes I)\cap D(I\otimes A)
.
The identity
(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}\geq 1
implies
|V_{I}(x,y)|\leq|\lambda|

for all
(x,y)\in\mathbb{R}^{2}
, so
V_{I}
is a bounded self-adjoint multiplication
operator. Hence
H
is self-adjoint on
D(H_{0})
by the bounded perturbation
theorem. Essential self-adjointness on
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fin}}
follows
because
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fin}}
is a core for
H_{0}
and is invariant
under
V_{I}
.∎

By Stone’s theorem,
H
generates a strongly continuous unitary group

U(t)=e^{-itH}
, making the bound (
7
) rigorously applicable
in the continuum.

Theorem 2 (Conserved moment bound).

Let
\psi_{0}\in D(\Sigma^{1/2})
, and define
\psi_{t}:=e^{-itH}\psi_{0}
.
Then for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
,

\langle\psi_{t},\Sigma\,\psi_{t}\rangle\leq\langle\psi_{0},\Sigma\,\psi_{0}\rangle+2|\lambda|.

(11)

Consequently, Eq. (
7
) holds.

Proof.

On
\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{fin}}
, all operators are well-defined and all
commutators reduce using the canonical relations

[x,p_{x}]=i
,
[y,p_{y}]=i
, with all cross-commutators vanishing, together with

[p_{x},f]=-i\,\partial_{x}f
and
[p_{y},f]=-i\,\partial_{y}f

for smooth multiplication operators
f(x,y)
.
Since
C
is at most quadratic in momenta, every term in
[C,H]
involves at
most first derivatives of
V_{I}
via the Leibniz rule, and the correspondence

[\hat{A},\hat{B}]=i\hbar\{A,B\}_{\mathrm{PB}}
is exact with no higher-order
remainder. A direct computation then gives
[C,H]=0
by exact cancellation,
corresponding to the classical identity
\{C,H\}_{\mathrm{PB}}=0

(Ref.
13
); the explicit operator-ordered derivation,
including the step-by-step cancellation enforced by the geometric
identity (
5
), is given in the Supplemental
Material
1
.
Standard approximation arguments extend this to all of
D(H_{0})
, giving

\langle E\rangle_{t}=\langle E\rangle_{0}
under unitary evolution.

A direct algebraic computation yields

E=\Sigma+(y^{2}-x^{2})V_{I}(x,y)
.
Since
(y^{2}-x^{2})V_{I}
is a bounded self-adjoint operator with norm
\leq|\lambda|

by Eq. (
6
), we have the quadratic-form inequalities

\Sigma\leq E+|\lambda|\,I,\qquad\Sigma\geq E-|\lambda|\,I.

(12)

Applying these at times
0
and
t
,

\langle\Sigma\rangle_{t}\leq\langle E\rangle_{t}+|\lambda|=\langle E\rangle_{0}+|\lambda|\leq\langle\Sigma\rangle_{0}+2|\lambda|.

Finally, since
\hat{K}^{2}\geq 0
implies

x^{2}+y^{2}+p_{x}^{2}+p_{y}^{2}\leq 2\Sigma
as quadratic forms,
setting
t_{a}=0
gives Eq. (
7
). ∎

Consequently,

\tfrac{d}{dt}\langle\hat{C}\rangle=\tfrac{1}{i\hbar}\langle[\hat{C},\hat{H}]\rangle=0

for every quantum state, provided the time evolution is unitary;
we return to this point in the
Discussion
.

VII
Discussion and conclusions

The central result of this Letter is analytic and exact: the operator

\hat{C}
defined in Eq. (
4
) satisfies

[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
with no
\hbar
corrections, as a direct
consequence of canonical commutation relations and the Leibniz rule alone.
From this single fact, elementary operator algebra yields
the bound (
7
): the mean squared phase-space radius

\langle\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}+\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{p}_{y}^{2}\rangle
is bounded
for all time, for every quantum state with finite initial second moments,
at all coupling strengths, provided the time evolution is unitary.

We now state precisely what the bound does and does not establish.

What is proven.
The bound (
7
) is a rigorous statement
about second moments of the quantum state. It holds for every pure or mixed
state, coherent or Fock, and reduces to the classical bound of
Ref.
13
in the limit
\hbar\to 0
, since no
\hbar
-dependent
terms appear in Eq. (
7
). There are no operator-ordering
ambiguities in the proof and no perturbative remainder: the commutator

[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
is exact for all
\hbar>0
.

What is not proven.
The bound does not imply
spectral stability, the existence of a ground state, or
spatial localization of the wavefunction beyond the control
of second moments: in principle the wavefunction can develop
tails outside any compact region while
\langle\hat{x}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2}\rangle
remains bounded. The question of whether

\hat{H}
admits a self-adjoint extension on
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

is resolved affirmatively in Theorem
1
, which
establishes that unitary evolution holds in the continuum and
that the bound (
7
) applies literally. What remains open is the construction of the positive-definite
\mathcal{CPT}
inner product from the
eigenstates of
\hat{H}
, and the extension of these results
to ghost-coupled field theories including Lee-Wick
models
18
;
19
and the
Pais-Uhlenbeck oscillator
23
.
Concurrent work
12
;
11

resolves two of the questions left open here, establishing
a unique vacuum and a discrete (non-dense) spectrum for
ghost systems with polynomial confining interactions.
Whether spectral discreteness accompanies bounded second
moments for the bounded, vanishing interaction
V_{I}
of
Eq. (
2
) is the sharpest open question left by
the present work: the operator
\hat{C}
predicts Poisson
inter-multiplet statistics (confirmed numerically in
Fig.
4
), but a proof that the spectrum is not dense for
non-confining interactions remains out of reach with
current methods.

Relation to the Ostrogradsky theorem.
The most natural objection to
the preceding is that the Ostrogradsky theorem guarantees the Hamiltonian is
unbounded below, and our spectrum confirms this. The theorem does not,
however, preclude bounded phase-space moments, and our result shows
explicitly that the two can coexist. Ostrogradsky instability and bounded
second moments are statements about different properties of the dynamics,
and the interaction structure of
V_{I}
is precisely what allows them to be
simultaneously satisfied.

Structural relevance to Lee-Wick and Pais-Uhlenbeck.
The boundedness
of
V_{I}
and the existence of
\hat{C}
both depend on the specific
hyperbolic-boost-covariant combination
(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}
, not on the
wrong-sign kinetic term alone. Lee-Wick models
18
;
19

and the Pais-Uhlenbeck oscillator
23
acquire their
ghost sector from higher-derivative kinetic terms that, after reduction to a
two-oscillator form, do not generically share this symmetry structure; whether physically motivated interactions in those theories
can be arranged to preserve an analogous conserved operator
is an open question that the present construction makes sharp.
A natural intermediate step toward the field-theoretic
extension is the classical field theory program of
Refs.
10
;
17
, which establishes
small-data global stability for ghostly field configurations
in
(N+1)
dimensions: the dynamical decoupling mechanism
responsible for classical stability in the mechanical model
persists in the field-theoretic setting, lending support to
the conjecture that an analogous quantum operator bound
may survive the passage to infinitely many degrees of freedom.
The key obstacles specific to field theory, namely
renormalization, the absence of a conserved
\hat{C}
in
the continuum limit, and the divergence of the naive
vacuum decay rate, remain open and represent the sharpest
targets for future work.

Relation to prior benign-ghost and pseudo-Hermiticity approaches.

Two bodies of work have previously argued that ghost-coupled systems can be
well-behaved. Smilga and collaborators
24
;
8

identified classes of “benign ghost” mechanical
systems whose classical motion remains bounded and whose quantum evolution
is unitary, typically by exhibiting specific interaction forms or by
restricting to islands of stability in phase space. Mostafazadeh and
others in the pseudo-Hermitian /
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric quantum mechanics
program
20
obtain a stable,
unitary Pais-Uhlenbeck quantization by redefining the Hilbert-space inner
product, replacing the standard
L^{2}
metric with a positive-definite

\mathcal{CPT}
-like inner product with respect to which the Hamiltonian
is Hermitian. Our result is logically distinct from both approaches.
The Hamiltonian studied here is self-adjoint on the standard
L^{2}(\mathbb{R}^{2})

inner product (Theorem
1
), so unitarity is inherited
from the bounded-perturbation theorem rather than from a redefinition of
the inner product; the bound (
7
) then follows from an exact
operator identity
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
rather than from exact solvability
or confinement to a stability island. To our knowledge, this is the first
rigorous, state-independent, non-perturbative upper bound on the mean
squared phase-space radius for a ghost-coupled quantum system that is not
exactly solvable, obtained without modifying the inner product.

Role of the numerics.
With continuum unitarity established
analytically (Theorem
1
), the numerical results play

the analytic bound (
7

\hat{C}
are reflected in the actual dynamics — at the Ehrenfest,
wavepacket, and spectral levels, respectively.

Together these results sharpen the message of
Refs.
13

9

consequence of a wrong-sign kinetic term, but depends critically on the
interaction structure. The present work provides the first rigorous
quantum-mechanical demonstration of this fact, extending the classical
Lyapunov result to an exact operator bound with no
\hbar

perturbative remainder.

Open questions for future work include: whether

VIII
Supplemental Material:

Ghost Degrees of Freedom Without Quantum Runaway —
Explicit Operator-Ordered Verification of
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0

This Supplemental Material carries out the explicit operator-ordered
verification that
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
with no
\hbar
remainder, where

\displaystyle\hat{H}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2})-\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{p}_{y}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2})+V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y}),

(17)

\displaystyle\hat{C}

\displaystyle=\hat{K}^{2}+(\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2})-(\hat{x}^{2}-\hat{y}^{2}-1)V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y}),

(18)

\displaystyle\qquad\hat{K}=\hat{x}\hat{p}_{y}+\hat{y}\hat{p}_{x},

(19)

\displaystyle V_{I}(x,y)=\lambda\bigl[(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}\bigr]^{-1/2}.

(20)

Throughout we set
\hbar=1
and use
[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{x}]=[\hat{y},\hat{p}_{y}]=i
,
with all cross-sector commutators vanishing. In Secs. I–V we carry out the
operator algebra to reduce
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]
to a sum of terms of definite
momentum degree. In Sec. VI we show that each such term vanishes
identically by direct substitution of Eq. (
20
) and the algebraic
identity

(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}-(y^{2}-x^{2})^{2}=2(x^{2}+y^{2})+1,

(21)

equivalently

\Delta\equiv(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)^{2}+4x^{2}=(x^{2}-y^{2})^{2}+2(x^{2}+y^{2})+1.

(22)

Notation.

We abbreviate

s=x^{2}+y^{2},\qquad d=x^{2}-y^{2},\qquad\Delta=d^{2}+2s+1,

(23)

so
V_{I}=\lambda/\sqrt{\Delta}
. We also use

\hat{B}=\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2},\quad\hat{D}=\hat{p}_{y}^{2}+\hat{y}^{2},\quad\hat{M}=(\hat{x}^{2}-\hat{y}^{2}-1)V_{I}(\hat{x},\hat{y}),

(24)

so that
\hat{H}=\tfrac{1}{2}(\hat{B}-\hat{D})+V_{I}
and

\hat{C}=\hat{K}^{2}+\hat{B}-\hat{M}
.

I. Preliminary commutator identities

For any smooth
f(\hat{x},\hat{y})
:

\displaystyle[\hat{\hat{p}_{x}},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=-i\,\partial_{x}f,

\displaystyle[\hat{\hat{p}_{y}},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=-i\,\partial_{y}f,

(25)

\displaystyle[\hat{\hat{p}_{x}^{2}},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=-2i(\partial_{x}f)\,\hat{p}_{x}-\partial_{x}^{2}f,

\displaystyle[\hat{\hat{p}_{y}^{2}},\hat{f}]

\displaystyle=-2i(\partial_{y}f)\,\hat{p}_{y}-\partial_{y}^{2}f.

(26)

Equation (
26
) follows from (
25
) and

\hat{p}_{x}(\partial_{x}f)=(\partial_{x}f)\hat{p}_{x}-i\partial_{x}^{2}f
.
Our convention throughout is to place position-operator functions to the
left of the remaining momenta.

We also record

\displaystyle[\hat{x}^{2},f]

\displaystyle=[\hat{y}^{2},f]=0,

\displaystyle[\hat{p}_{x},\hat{p}_{y}]

\displaystyle=0,

\displaystyle[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{y}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{y},\hat{p}_{x}]=0.

(27)

II. Decomposition of
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]

Using the decomposition above,

\displaystyle[\hat{C},\hat{H}]

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{K}^{2},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]+[\hat{K}^{2},V_{I}]

\displaystyle\quad+\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{B},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]+[\hat{B},V_{I}]

\displaystyle\quad-\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{M},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]-[\hat{M},V_{I}].

(28)

Two terms vanish immediately:
[\hat{B},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]=0
(because

[\hat{B},\hat{D}]=0
since the
x
- and
y
-sectors commute), and

[\hat{\hat{M}},\hat{V_{I}}]=0
(both are functions of commuting position
operators). We show next that
[\hat{K}^{2},{\hat{B}-\hat{D}}]
also
vanishes identically. This leaves only three nontrivial contributions,
which we evaluate in Secs. IV–V.

III. The identity
[\hat{K}^{2},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]=0

A direct calculation gives

\displaystyle[\hat{K},\hat{B}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{x}\hat{p}_{y}+\hat{y}\hat{p}_{x},\,\hat{p}_{x}^{2}+\hat{x}^{2}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{x},\hat{p}_{x}^{2}]\hat{p}_{y}+\hat{y}[\hat{p}_{x},\hat{x}^{2}]

\displaystyle=2i\hat{p}_{x}\hat{p}_{y}-2i\hat{x}\hat{y}=2i(\hat{p}_{x}\hat{p}_{y}-\hat{x}\hat{y}),

(29)

where we used
[\hat{\hat{x}},\hat{\hat{p}_{x}^{2}}]=2i\hat{p}_{x}
,

[\hat{\hat{p}_{x}},\hat{\hat{x}^{2}}]=-2i\hat{x}
, and the fact that cross-sector
operators commute. An entirely analogous computation gives

[\hat{\hat{K}},\hat{\hat{D}}]=-2i\hat{x}\hat{y}+2i\hat{p}_{x}\hat{p}_{y}=2i(\hat{p}_{x}\hat{p}_{y}-\hat{x}\hat{y}).

(30)

Therefore

[\hat{K},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]=0,

(31)

and consequently

\displaystyle[\hat{K}^{2},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]

\displaystyle=\hat{K}[\hat{K},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]+[\hat{K},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]\hat{K}

\displaystyle=0.

(32)

The decomposition (
28
) reduces to

\boxed{\ [\hat{C},\hat{H}]=[\hat{K}^{2},V_{I}]+[\hat{B},V_{I}]-\tfrac{1}{2}[\hat{M},\hat{B}-\hat{D}].\ }

(33)

IV. Evaluation of
[\hat{B},V_{I}]
and
[\hat{M},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]

Since
V_{I}
and
\hat{M}
are multiplication operators, from
(
26
) together with
[\hat{\hat{x}^{2}},\hat{V_{I}}]=0
:

\displaystyle[\hat{B},V_{I}]

\displaystyle=[\hat{p}_{x}^{2},V_{I}]=-2i(\partial_{x}V_{I})\hat{p}_{x}-\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I}.

(34)

Similarly,

\displaystyle[\hat{M},\hat{B}-\hat{D}]

\displaystyle=2i(\partial_{x}M)\hat{p}_{x}+\partial_{x}^{2}M

\displaystyle\quad-2i(\partial_{y}M)\hat{p}_{y}-\partial_{y}^{2}M.

(35)

With
M=(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)V_{I}
,

\displaystyle\partial_{x}M

\displaystyle=2xV_{I}+(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)\partial_{x}V_{I},

(36)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}M

\displaystyle=-2yV_{I}+(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)\partial_{y}V_{I},

(37)

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}M

\displaystyle=2V_{I}+4x\,\partial_{x}V_{I}+(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I},

(38)

\displaystyle\partial_{y}^{2}M

\displaystyle=-2V_{I}-4y\,\partial_{y}V_{I}+(x^{2}-y^{2}-1)\partial_{y}^{2}V_{I}.

(39)

V. Evaluation of
[\hat{K}^{2},V_{I}]

Using
[\hat{\hat{K}},\hat{V_{I}}]=\hat{x}[\hat{\hat{p}_{y}},\hat{V_{I}}]+\hat{y}[\hat{\hat{p}_{x}},\hat{V_{I}}]=-iu
, where

u(x,y)\equiv x\,\partial_{y}V_{I}+y\,\partial_{x}V_{I},

(40)

we obtain
[\hat{\hat{K}^{2}},\hat{V_{I}}]=\hat{K}[\hat{\hat{K}},\hat{V_{I}}]+[\hat{\hat{K}},\hat{V_{I}}]\hat{K}=-i(\hat{K}u+u\hat{K})
. Expanding the anticommutator with
position-ordered ordering:

\displaystyle\hat{K}u+u\hat{K}

\displaystyle=\hat{x}(\hat{p}_{y}u+u\hat{p}_{y})+\hat{y}(\hat{p}_{x}u+u\hat{p}_{x})

\displaystyle=2\hat{x}u\hat{p}_{y}+2\hat{y}u\hat{p}_{x}-i(\hat{x}\partial_{y}u+\hat{y}\partial_{x}u),

(41)

where we used
\hat{p}_{y}u=u\hat{p}_{y}-i\partial_{y}u
etc. Hence

[\hat{\hat{K}^{2}},\hat{V_{I}}]=-2i(\hat{x}u\hat{p}_{y}+\hat{y}u\hat{p}_{x})-(\hat{x}\partial_{y}u+\hat{y}\partial_{x}u).

(42)

Substituting the definition of
u
:

\displaystyle\hat{x}u

\displaystyle=\hat{x}^{2}\partial_{y}V_{I}+\hat{x}\hat{y}\partial_{x}V_{I},

(43)

\displaystyle\hat{y}u

\displaystyle=\hat{x}\hat{y}\partial_{y}V_{I}+\hat{y}^{2}\partial_{x}V_{I},

(44)

\displaystyle\hat{x}\partial_{y}u+\hat{y}\partial_{x}u

\displaystyle=\hat{x}^{2}\partial_{y}^{2}V_{I}+\hat{y}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I}

\displaystyle\quad+2\hat{x}\hat{y}\,\partial_{x}\partial_{y}V_{I}+\hat{x}\partial_{x}V_{I}+\hat{y}\partial_{y}V_{I},

(45)

so that

\displaystyle[\hat{K}^{2},V_{I}]

\displaystyle=-2i\bigl[\hat{x}^{2}(\partial_{y}V_{I})\hat{p}_{y}+\hat{x}\hat{y}(\partial_{x}V_{I})\hat{p}_{y}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+\hat{x}\hat{y}(\partial_{y}V_{I})\hat{p}_{x}+\hat{y}^{2}(\partial_{x}V_{I})\hat{p}_{x}\bigr]

\displaystyle\quad-\bigl[\hat{x}^{2}\partial_{y}^{2}V_{I}+\hat{y}^{2}\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I}

\displaystyle\qquad\quad+2\hat{x}\hat{y}\,\partial_{x}\partial_{y}V_{I}+\hat{x}\partial_{x}V_{I}+\hat{y}\partial_{y}V_{I}\bigr].

(46)

VI. Explicit cancellation, order by order in momenta

Summing Eqs. (
34
), (
35
), and (
46
) according to
Eq. (
33
) yields

[\hat{\hat{C}},\hat{\hat{H}}]=\mathcal{A}_{x}\,\hat{p}_{x}+\mathcal{A}_{y}\,\hat{p}_{y}+\mathcal{A}_{0},

(47)

where the coefficient functions are

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{x}

\displaystyle=-i\bigl[(1+x^{2}+y^{2})\partial_{x}V_{I}+2xy\,\partial_{y}V_{I}+2xV_{I}\bigr],

(48)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{y}

\displaystyle=-i\bigl[(1+x^{2}+y^{2})\partial_{y}V_{I}+2xy\,\partial_{x}V_{I}+2yV_{I}\bigr],

(49)

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}

\displaystyle=-2V_{I}-3x\,\partial_{x}V_{I}-3y\,\partial_{y}V_{I}

\displaystyle\quad\quad-\tfrac{1+x^{2}+y^{2}}{2}(\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I}+\partial_{y}^{2}V_{I})

\displaystyle\hskip 60.00009pt-2xy\,\partial_{x}\partial_{y}V_{I}.

(50)

The coefficients of
\hat{p}_{x}
,
\hat{p}_{y}
, and the identity are independent
operator structures, so
[\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0
is equivalent to the three
scalar identities
\mathcal{A}_{x}\equiv 0
,
\mathcal{A}_{y}\equiv 0
, and

\mathcal{A}_{0}\equiv 0
. We verify each in turn.

A. Derivatives of
V_{I}

From
V_{I}=\lambda\Delta^{-1/2}
and
\Delta=d^{2}+2s+1
with
d=x^{2}-y^{2}
,

s=x^{2}+y^{2}
,

\partial_{x}\Delta=4x(d+1),\qquad\partial_{y}\Delta=4y(1-d),

(51)

giving

\partial_{x}V_{I}=-\frac{2\lambda x(d+1)}{\Delta^{3/2}},\qquad\partial_{y}V_{I}=-\frac{2\lambda y(1-d)}{\Delta^{3/2}}.

(52)

B. Vanishing of
\mathcal{A}_{x}

Multiplying Eq. (
48
) through by
\Delta^{3/2}/\lambda
and using
(
52
):

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{3/2}}{\lambda}\,\frac{\mathcal{A}_{x}}{-i}

\displaystyle=-2x(1+s)(d+1)-4xy^{2}(1-d)+2x\Delta

\displaystyle=2x\bigl[\Delta-(1+s)(d+1)-2y^{2}(1-d)\bigr].

(53)

Expanding the bracket using
(1+s)(d+1)=d+1+sd+s
,

2y^{2}(1-d)=2y^{2}-2y^{2}d
, and
\Delta=d^{2}+2s+1
:

\displaystyle\Delta-(1+s)(d+1)-2y^{2}(1-d)

\displaystyle\quad=d^{2}+s-d-sd-2y^{2}+2y^{2}d.

(54)

Substituting
2y^{2}=s-d
:

\displaystyle\quad=d^{2}+s-d-sd-(s-d)+(s-d)d

\displaystyle\quad=d^{2}+s-d-sd-s+d+sd-d^{2}=0.

(55)

Hence
\mathcal{A}_{x}\equiv 0
.

C. Vanishing of
\mathcal{A}_{y}

Multiplying Eq. (
49
) through by
\Delta^{3/2}/\lambda
:

\displaystyle\frac{\Delta^{3/2}}{\lambda}\,\frac{\mathcal{A}_{y}}{-i}

\displaystyle=-2y(1+s)(1-d)-4x^{2}y(d+1)+2y\Delta

\displaystyle=2y\bigl[\Delta-(1+s)(1-d)-2x^{2}(d+1)\bigr].

(56)

Expanding with
(1+s)(1-d)=1-d+s-sd
and
2x^{2}(d+1)=2x^{2}d+2x^{2}
,
substituting
2x^{2}=s+d
:

\displaystyle\Delta-(1+s)(1-d)-2x^{2}(d+1)

\displaystyle\quad=d^{2}+s+d+sd-sd-d^{2}-s-d=0.

(57)

Hence
\mathcal{A}_{y}\equiv 0
.

D. Vanishing of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
: second derivatives

We need the second derivatives of
V_{I}
. Differentiating
(
52
) and using
\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta=12x^{2}-4y^{2}+4
,

\partial_{y}^{2}\Delta=12y^{2}-4x^{2}+4
,
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\Delta=-8xy
:

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta+\partial_{y}^{2}\Delta

\displaystyle=8(s+1),

(58)

\displaystyle(\partial_{x}\Delta)^{2}+(\partial_{y}\Delta)^{2}

\displaystyle=16\bigl[x^{2}(d+1)^{2}+y^{2}(1-d)^{2}\bigr]

\displaystyle=16\bigl[d^{2}(s+2)+s\bigr]

\displaystyle=16\bigl[\Delta(s+2)-2(s+1)^{2}\bigr],

(59)

where the last step uses
d^{2}=\Delta-2s-1
. Then

\displaystyle\partial_{x}^{2}V_{I}+\partial_{y}^{2}V_{I}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\lambda(\partial_{x}^{2}\Delta+\partial_{y}^{2}\Delta)}{2\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{3\lambda\bigl[(\partial_{x}\Delta)^{2}+(\partial_{y}\Delta)^{2}\bigr]}{4\Delta^{5/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{4\lambda(2s+5)}{\Delta^{3/2}}-\frac{24\lambda(s+1)^{2}}{\Delta^{5/2}}.

(60)

For the cross term,

\partial_{x}\Delta\cdot\partial_{y}\Delta=16xy(1-d^{2})=16xy\bigl[2(s+1)-\Delta\bigr]
,
and
\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\Delta=-8xy
, giving

\partial_{x}\partial_{y}V_{I}=-\frac{\lambda\partial_{x}\partial_{y}\Delta}{2\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{3\lambda\,\partial_{x}\Delta\,\partial_{y}\Delta}{4\Delta^{5/2}}=-\frac{8\lambda xy}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{24\lambda xy(s+1)}{\Delta^{5/2}}.

(61)

E. Vanishing of
\mathcal{A}_{0}
: assembly

Substituting (
52
), (
60
), and
(
61
) into (
50
), and factoring
\lambda
:

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}/\lambda

\displaystyle=-\frac{2}{\Delta^{1/2}}+\frac{6x^{2}(d+1)}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{6y^{2}(1-d)}{\Delta^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{2(s+1)(2s+5)}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{12(s+1)^{3}}{\Delta^{5/2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{16x^{2}y^{2}}{\Delta^{3/2}}-\frac{48x^{2}y^{2}(s+1)}{\Delta^{5/2}}

\displaystyle=\frac{-2\Delta+6(d^{2}+s)-2(s+1)(2s+5)+16x^{2}y^{2}}{\Delta^{3/2}}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{12(s+1)^{3}-48x^{2}y^{2}(s+1)}{\Delta^{5/2}}.

(62)

Now observe that
x^{2}(d+1)+y^{2}(1-d)=d^{2}+s
, so the
\Delta^{-3/2}

numerator in Eq. (
62
) is

\displaystyle-2\Delta+6(d^{2}+s)-2(s+1)(2s+5)+16x^{2}y^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=-2d^{2}-4s-2+6d^{2}+6s-4s^{2}-14s-10+16x^{2}y^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad=4d^{2}-12s-12-4s^{2}+16x^{2}y^{2}.

(63)

Using
d^{2}=s^{2}-4x^{2}y^{2}
(which is equivalent to

(x^{2}-y^{2})^{2}=(x^{2}+y^{2})^{2}-4x^{2}y^{2}
),

4d^{2}=4s^{2}-16x^{2}y^{2}
, so

4d^{2}-4s^{2}+16x^{2}y^{2}=0,

(64)

and the numerator of the
\Delta^{-3/2}
piece collapses to
-12s-12=-12(s+1)
.

Therefore

\mathcal{A}_{0}/\lambda=-\frac{12(s+1)}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{12(s+1)\bigl[(s+1)^{2}-4x^{2}y^{2}\bigr]}{\Delta^{5/2}}.

(65)

The key algebraic fact is

(s+1)^{2}-4x^{2}y^{2}=s^{2}+2s+1-4x^{2}y^{2}=d^{2}+2s+1=\Delta,

(66)

using again
s^{2}-4x^{2}y^{2}=d^{2}
. Hence the
\Delta^{-5/2}
term equals

12(s+1)\Delta/\Delta^{5/2}=12(s+1)/\Delta^{3/2}
, and

\mathcal{A}_{0}/\lambda=-\frac{12(s+1)}{\Delta^{3/2}}+\frac{12(s+1)}{\Delta^{3/2}}=0.

(67)

F. Conclusion

Combining Secs. VI.B–E, all three coefficient functions in
Eq. (
47
) vanish identically:

\mathcal{A}_{x}\equiv 0,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{y}\equiv 0,\qquad\mathcal{A}_{0}\equiv 0,

(68)

and therefore, as an operator identity on the common invariant core

\mathcal{D}_{\rm fin}=\mathrm{span}\{h_{n}(\hat{x})h_{m}(\hat{y}):n,m\geq 0\}
,

\boxed{\ [\hat{C},\hat{H}]=0.\ }

(69)

Because
\hat{C}
is at most quadratic in momenta, the operator computation
above truncates: no higher than first derivatives of
V_{I}
enter the
momentum-linear pieces
\mathcal{A}_{x}\hat{p}_{x}+\mathcal{A}_{y}\hat{p}_{y}
, and
no higher than second derivatives enter the
\mathcal{A}_{0}
piece, with all
three pieces vanishing exactly. There is no
\hbar
-dependent remainder:
Eq. (
69
) holds as an exact operator identity, not as a leading
semiclassical approximation.

Standard approximation arguments, using the self-adjointness of
\hat{H}
on

\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
established in Theorem 1 of the main text, extend
Eq. (
69
) to all of
\mathcal{D}(H_{0})
. Hence, under the unitary
evolution
U(t)=e^{-it\hat{H}}
generated by Stone’s theorem,

\langle\psi_{t},\hat{C}\psi_{t}\rangle=\langle\psi_{0},\hat{C}\psi_{0}\rangle

for every
\psi_{0}\in\mathcal{D}(\Sigma^{1/2})
.
