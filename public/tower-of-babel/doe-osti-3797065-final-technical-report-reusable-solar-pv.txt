Executive Summary:

This project focused on the development of a reusable, recyclable high-density
polyethylene pallet system designed to improve the shipping, handling, and storage of
solar photovoltaic (PV) modules as the industry transitions to the larger M10 solar cell
format. The motivation for this work arises from the rapid growth of the domestic solar
industry and the significant logistical challenges associated with transporting and
staging solar modules for large-scale projects. The industry currently relies heavily on
single-use wood pallets, which generate large volumes of construction waste, contribute
to module damage during transportation and handling, and create inefficiencies in
jobsite logistics. PVpallet, Inc. sought to address these challenges by developing an
improved reusable packaging system that increases durability, improves functionality
during installation, and reduces the levelized cost of energy for solar PV projects.

This project builds upon PVpallet’s previously commercialized Series X pallet, which
was designed for the earlier M6 solar cell format that currently dominates the industry.
As the industry transitions to larger M10-based modules for utility-scale projects and
smaller form factors for residential installations, a new pallet architecture was required.
The primary goal of this project was therefore to complete a comprehensive design
overhaul, resulting in a modular pallet system capable of accommodating both utility-
scale and residential solar modules. The new design, referred to as PVpallet Series U
(formerly referred to as Series U/R), maintains the core functionality of the original
system while optimizing structural performance, modularity, and manufacturing
efficiency. Key design objectives included the use of recycled and recyclable materials,
a reinforced base structure to improve durability and puncture resistance, stackability,
compatibility with M10 module dimensions, and a modular design that minimizes the
number of components and molds required for manufacturing.

The project advanced understanding in the area of renewable energy supply chain
logistics and sustainable packaging by demonstrating how reusable packaging systems
can be engineered specifically for the needs of the solar industry. Activities included
stakeholder engagement with manufacturers, EPC contractors, and developers;
preliminary and detailed design development; and structural validation through finite
element analysis (FEA). The completed FEA analysis confirmed that the Series U pallet
design meets the structural integrity and load performance requirements necessary for
transporting and handling solar modules under real-world logistics conditions.

In addition to validating technical feasibility, the project evaluated the economic
effectiveness of reusable packaging systems relative to traditional expendable wood
pallets. A comprehensive financial model was developed that incorporates logistics
assumptions, stakeholder input, pallet return rates, module damage reduction
estimates, and disposal cost avoidance. Under conservative assumptions, the analysis
indicates that the PVpallet Series U system could generate project-level lifecycle
savings of at least $0.0025 per watt installed – meeting or exceeding the original target
savings threshold and demonstrating strong economic potential compared to single-use
packaging solutions.
 Page 3 of 26

While the project successfully completed the design, engineering analysis, and cost
modeling phases, full-scale field validation was not completed within the award period
due to the inability to fabricate injection-molded prototype parts. The use of a proxy
pallet from a third party manufacturer of a comparable form factor was ultimately
disallowed under the terms of the award, preventing pilot deployment during the project
period. Despite this limitation, engagement with manufacturers, EPCs, and developers
continued throughout the project, and several stakeholders have expressed interest in
conducting commercial trial deployments once production prototypes become available.

The broader public benefits of this work include significant reductions in construction
waste, improved logistics efficiency, and reduced risk of solar module damage during
transport and installation. By replacing single-use wood pallets with a reusable and
recyclable packaging system, the PVpallet system has the potential to reduce landfill
waste, lower transportation and disposal costs, and support more efficient solar project
deployment. These improvements can help reduce the overall cost of solar energy while
strengthening the competitiveness of the domestic solar industry and supporting
continued expansion of renewable energy infrastructure in the United States.

The project demonstrates that reusable packaging systems represent a viable pathway
to reduce waste, lower logistics costs, and improve operational efficiency in large-scale
solar deployment.

1. Background:

  Current State of the Art and Relevant Literature

  Although there is limited peer-reviewed literature focused specifically on reusable
  pallet systems for solar photovoltaic (PV) module logistics, several adjacent
  research areas provide relevant technical context for this project. These include: (1)
  reusable transit packaging and pallet circularity research, (2) life-cycle and techno-
  economic analyses comparing reusable and single-use packaging systems, and (3)
  solar PV logistics, circular economy, and module transport research. Together, these
  bodies of work help place the PVpallet Series U project within the broader state of
  the art while also highlighting the novel contributions of this work.

  Reusable Transit Packaging and Pallet Systems

  A growing body of research examines the environmental and operational impacts of
  reusable transit packaging in industrial supply chains. Kudrenko and Hall (2025)
  analyzed the adoption of reusable transit packaging across several U.S. industries
  and identified sustainability benefits such as reduced material consumption,
  improved supply chain efficiency, and lower lifecycle environmental impacts. Their
  work also highlights common barriers to adoption, including upfront capital costs,
  operational complexity, and the need for coordinated reverse logistics systems.

  Related research on pallet circularity emphasizes the importance of designing pallet
  systems as managed assets rather than single-use packaging. Zhang et al. (2024)
  examined circular economy strategies for industrial pallet systems and found that
  improved pallet reuse, pooling systems, and repairability significantly reduce
  environmental impacts and material waste.

  These studies are similar to the current project in that they evaluate the transition
  from disposable packaging systems to reusable asset-based logistics platforms.
  However, they differ from the PVpallet project in that most existing studies focus on
  automotive, food, or consumer-goods supply chains rather than solar energy
  infrastructure. Solar modules present unique handling challenges due to their fragile
  glass surfaces, large dimensions, and high value, requiring packaging systems
  specifically engineered for both transportation and construction-site handling. The
  PVpallet Series U project therefore extends reusable packaging concepts into a new
  application area within renewable energy logistics.

  The insights from this literature helped inform several design principles used in this
  project, including emphasizing pallet durability, stackability, modular construction,
  and reuse cycles. These factors were incorporated directly into the design goals for
  the PVpallet Series U system.

Lifecycle and Economic Analysis of Reusable Packaging

A second relevant body of research evaluates the lifecycle environmental and
economic performance of reusable packaging systems. Espinoza-Orias et al. (2025)
developed best-practice methodologies for life-cycle assessments comparing
reusable and disposable packaging systems, highlighting the importance of
accounting for reuse rates, transport distances, and end-of-life handling. Similarly,
Kim et al. (2023) conducted a comparative life-cycle assessment of reusable and
disposable distribution packaging and found that reusable systems can significantly
reduce environmental impacts when sufficient reuse cycles are achieved.

These studies are similar to the present project in that they evaluate the economic
and environmental break-even points of reusable packaging systems. However,
most of the literature focuses on consumer goods packaging rather than industrial
construction logistics. Solar project logistics introduce additional variables including
module damage during transport, on-site handling efficiency, disposal costs for wood
packaging, and logistics constraints associated with large-scale construction sites.

To address these differences, this project incorporated a detailed financial model
that evaluates lifecycle costs of reusable pallets compared to conventional wood
pallets. The model integrates stakeholder feedback, module damage reduction
estimates, pallet return rates, and avoided disposal costs. Results indicate potential
project-level savings of approximately $0.0025 per watt installed when transitioning
from expendable wood pallets to the reusable PVpallet Series U system. This
modeling approach aligns with best practices described in the reusable packaging
literature while adapting those methodologies to the unique operational context of
utility-scale solar deployment.

Solar Module Transport, Packaging, and Circular Economy Research

Research specific to solar PV logistics also provides important context for this
project. Mittag et al. (2024), presented at the European Photovoltaic Solar Energy
Conference (EU PVSEC), analyzed the cost structure of solar module transport
across international and domestic supply chains. Their work demonstrates that
logistics costs can represent a meaningful portion of total module cost and that
packaging efficiency and transport optimization play an important role in overall
project economics.

Additional research in the PV circular economy field has highlighted the importance
of improving handling, storage, and logistics practices for solar modules. Tsanakas
et al. (2025) and related studies on solar module reuse emphasize that packaging
and transport conditions can significantly influence module damage rates and long-
term reliability. Similarly, the IEA PVPS Task 13 report on second-life PV modules
highlights logistics and handling as critical factors affecting the lifecycle performance
and reuse potential of PV modules.

 Page 7 of 26

 These studies are similar to the present project in that they identify logistics and
 handling as key factors affecting solar module performance and cost. However, most
 of the research focuses on module design, transport economics, or end-of-life reuse
 rather than on the design of the packaging systems themselves. The PVpallet
 project therefore addresses a relatively underexplored component of the solar
 supply chain by developing a purpose-built reusable pallet system optimized for PV
 module transportation, jobsite handling, and waste reduction.

 The findings from these studies were leveraged in the project by reinforcing the
 importance of minimizing module damage, improving stackability during transport
 and storage, and optimizing logistics efficiency. These considerations influenced the
 design goals for the PVpallet Series U system, including reinforced pallet structures,
 improved stackability, and compatibility with modern module sizes.

 Summary

 Overall, the current literature strongly supports the underlying premise of this project:
 that transitioning from single-use packaging systems to reusable logistics platforms
 can provide both environmental and economic benefits. While extensive research
 exists on reusable packaging and pallet systems in other industries, relatively little
 work has addressed reusable packaging solutions specifically for solar PV module
 logistics.

 The PVpallet Series U project contributes to this emerging area by integrating
 reusable packaging design, structural engineering validation, and lifecycle economic
 modeling into a system tailored for the solar industry. By leveraging insights from
 reusable packaging research, lifecycle assessment methodologies, and solar
 logistics studies, this project advances the development of more sustainable and
 efficient supply chain infrastructure for the growing domestic solar market.

2. Project Objectives:

 Impact on National Clean Energy Goals and Economic Benefits to the U.S.

 This project supports national clean energy objectives by addressing a critical but
 often overlooked component of solar energy deployment: the logistics and
 packaging systems used to transport and stage photovoltaic (PV) modules for
 installation. As the United States rapidly expands utility-scale solar capacity to meet
 federal and state decarbonization targets, improving the efficiency, cost structure,
 and environmental footprint of the solar supply chain becomes increasingly
 important. The PVpallet Series U project focuses on developing a reusable,
 recyclable pallet system designed specifically for modern PV module formats, with
 the goal of reducing construction waste, lowering logistics costs, minimizing module
 damage, and improving jobsite installation efficiency.

 Page 8 of 26

The project contributes to clean energy progression by helping reduce the total
installed cost of solar energy. Packaging and logistics inefficiencies are a hidden but
meaningful contributor to project costs across the solar industry. Conventional
single-use wood pallets generate significant construction waste and can lead to
module damage during shipping, storage, and handling. These inefficiencies
ultimately increase project costs and can slow deployment. By replacing expendable
packaging with a reusable logistics platform, the PVpallet Series U system has the
potential to reduce lifecycle costs associated with module transport and handling
while improving installation efficiency. Project cost modeling conducted during this
effort indicates potential savings of approximately $0.0025 per watt installed when
reusable pallet systems are adopted, depending on project-specific logistics
conditions. Reducing costs at this scale contributes directly to lowering the levelized
cost of energy (LCOE) for solar PV systems and helps accelerate the deployment of
renewable energy infrastructure across the United States.

The project also supports environmental sustainability objectives by significantly
reducing construction waste generated during solar project development. Utility-
scale solar installations can require tens of thousands of pallets, most of which are
currently made of wood and discarded after a single use. Transitioning to a reusable
pallet system manufactured from recyclable materials has the potential to eliminate
large volumes of wood waste while improving material efficiency across the solar
supply chain. In addition, improved stackability and optimized transport packaging
can enhance shipping efficiency and reduce transportation impacts.

Beyond environmental benefits, the project contributes to economic development
and domestic supply chain innovation. The PVpallet system is designed to be
manufactured domestically using injection-molded high-density polyethylene and
recycled plastic materials. Scaling production of reusable packaging systems for
solar energy applications could support domestic manufacturing jobs while
strengthening U.S. clean energy supply chains. As the U.S. solar industry continues
to expand domestic manufacturing capacity for modules and related components,
logistics infrastructure innovations such as reusable packaging systems can further
enhance the competitiveness and resilience of the domestic solar market.

Project Goals, Expected Outcomes, and Significance

The primary goal of this project was to design and validate a next-generation
reusable pallet platform capable of supporting the evolving needs of the solar PV
industry as it transitions from M6 to M10 cell formats. The project builds upon
PVpallet’s earlier Series X pallet system, which was designed for the prior
generation of solar modules. As module dimensions evolve and new deployment
practices emerge, a redesigned pallet architecture was required to accommodate
both larger utility-scale modules and smaller residential module formats.

 Page 9 of 26

The resulting design, referred to as PVpallet Series U, introduces several
innovations intended to improve functionality, durability, and manufacturability.
These include a modular design that allows the same pallet platform to
accommodate multiple module sizes, a reinforced base structure to improve
structural integrity and puncture resistance, enhanced stackability to improve
transportation efficiency, and the use of recyclable materials to support circular
manufacturing practices. By integrating these features into a reusable logistics
platform, the project advances the state of the art in solar construction logistics and
sustainable packaging design.

The expected outcomes of the project include a validated pallet design ready for
prototype manufacturing, a structural engineering analysis confirming load-bearing
performance, and a comprehensive lifecycle cost model demonstrating the
economic advantages of reusable pallet systems compared to conventional wood
pallets. These outcomes provide the technical and economic foundation needed for
commercialization and industry adoption.

Innovation and Fundamental Advances

The key innovation of this project lies in applying reusable industrial packaging
principles to the specific operational and logistical challenges of solar energy
deployment. While reusable pallet systems are widely used in other industries such
as automotive manufacturing and consumer goods distribution, they have not been
widely adopted in solar construction logistics. This project represents a fundamental
step toward adapting reusable logistics infrastructure to the unique requirements of
PV module transport and installation.

The PVpallet Series U system introduces a purpose-built packaging platform that
integrates structural engineering, modular design, and lifecycle economic analysis.
By addressing both engineering performance and supply chain economics, the
project advances the development of reusable packaging systems tailored to the
renewable energy sector. This integrated approach represents a meaningful
advancement beyond traditional single-use packaging solutions.

Summary of Statement of Project Objectives and Technical Work Plan

The project was executed through a structured technical work plan designed to
move the PVpallet Series U concept from preliminary design through engineering
validation and commercialization readiness. The major tasks within the Statement of
Project Objectives (SOPO) included:

Task 1: Stakeholder Engagement and Requirements Definition
The project began with gathering feedback from solar module manufacturers,
engineering-procurement-construction (EPC) contractors, developers, and logistics
providers. This stakeholder engagement helped define key functional requirements

for the new pallet system, including module compatibility, stackability, durability, and
jobsite handling considerations.

Task 2: Preliminary Design and Engineering Development
Based on stakeholder input and lessons learned from the existing Series X pallet,
the team developed preliminary design concepts for the Series U system. This
phase included evaluating alternative design configurations, optimizing geometry for
M10 module dimensions, and developing a modular architecture capable of serving
both utility-scale and residential PV markets.

Task 3: Structural Analysis and Engineering Validation
Finite element analysis (FEA) was conducted to evaluate the structural performance
of the pallet design under expected loading conditions during transportation,
stacking, and handling. The analysis confirmed that the reinforced pallet structure
meets design requirements for load capacity, stability, and durability.

Task 4: Cost Modeling and Lifecycle Analysis
A comprehensive financial model was developed to evaluate the economic
performance of reusable pallet systems compared to conventional wood pallets. The
model incorporated stakeholder feedback, logistics assumptions, pallet return rates,
damage reduction estimates, and disposal cost avoidance.

Task 5: Commercialization Planning and Stakeholder Outreach
The final phase of the project focused on commercialization readiness and
engagement with industry stakeholders interested in conducting pilot deployments of
the PVpallet system. These discussions aimed to identify opportunities for field
validation and early adoption once prototype units become available.

Milestones and Go/No-Go Decision Points

Major milestones for the project included completion of the preliminary design
phase, submission of the final structural analysis results, development of the
lifecycle cost model, and completion of commercialization outreach activities. A key

Despite this limitation, the project successfully achieved its primary design and
analysis objectives and produced a validated reusable pallet platform with
demonstrated technical and economic potential. Continued industry interest and

4. Significant Accomplishments and Conclusions:

  This project produced several significant technical and analytical accomplishments
  that advance the development of reusable logistics systems for the solar
  photovoltaic (PV) industry. While not all originally planned milestones were fully
  achieved during the period of performance, the work completed provides an
  important technical foundation for commercialization of reusable packaging systems
  tailored to modern PV module formats. Both positive outcomes and challenges
  encountered during the project are summarized below.

  Development of the PVpallet Series U Design Platform

  One of the most significant accomplishments of the project was the successful
  development of a redesigned reusable pallet platform, referred to as PVpallet Series
  U, intended to support the solar industry’s transition from M6 to M10 solar cell
  formats. As module sizes evolve, packaging systems must adapt to accommodate
  new module dimensions while maintaining safe handling and transport performance.

  The Series U design represents a complete redesign relative to the original PVpallet
  Series X system. Key design improvements include a modular architecture capable
  of supporting both residential and utility-scale PV modules, a reinforced base
  structure to improve durability and puncture resistance, improved stackability for
  transportation efficiency, and the use of recyclable high-density polyethylene
  materials. These features were developed through stakeholder engagement,
  engineering design iterations, and structural analysis. The resulting design platform
  provides a reusable packaging solution that addresses both environmental
  sustainability and operational efficiency within solar project logistics.

  This accomplishment is significant because reusable pallet systems designed
  specifically for solar module transport remain relatively rare within the industry. Most
  solar projects continue to rely on expendable wood pallets, which generate
  substantial construction waste and can contribute to module damage during
  handling. The Series U platform demonstrates how reusable packaging systems can
  be engineered to address these challenges while supporting evolving module
  formats.

  Structural Validation of the Pallet Design

A second major accomplishment was the completion of engineering validation of the
pallet structure using finite element analysis (FEA). Structural modeling confirmed
that the reinforced pallet design meets load-bearing and stability requirements for
transporting and stacking solar modules.

The structural analysis evaluated key performance metrics including pallet deflection

This accomplishment is significant because structural integrity is a critical

reliability. Completing engineering validation provides confidence that the Series U
design can meet these operational demands once manufactured.

Development of Lifecycle Cost Modeling

Another major accomplishment of the project was the development of a
comprehensive financial model comparing the lifecycle costs of reusable PVpallet
systems with conventional wood pallet packaging.

The model incorporates a range of operational variables, including pallet
manufacturing costs, logistics assumptions, pallet reuse rates, module damage
reduction estimates, and avoided waste disposal costs. Based on conservative
assumptions, the model indicates potential project-level cost savings of
approximately $0.0025 per watt installed when reusable pallet systems are adopted.
This significantly exceeds the project’s original target savings threshold of $0.0025
per watt.

This result is significant because it demonstrates that reusable packaging systems
can offer not only environmental benefits but also meaningful economic advantages
for solar project developers and EPC contractors. By quantifying the economic value
of reusable packaging, the model helps address one of the primary barriers to
adoption within the industry.

Stakeholder Engagement and Industry Validation

Throughout the project period, the team engaged with solar module manufacturers,
developers, and EPC contractors to gather feedback on pallet design requirements
and potential commercialization pathways. Stakeholder engagement helped ensure
that the pallet design reflects real-world operational needs and installation
workflows.

Multiple industry stakeholders have expressed interest in participating in pilot
programs once injection-molded prototype pallets become available. This ongoing

                                   Page 19 of 26

interest provides an encouraging signal that reusable pallet systems may gain
traction within the industry once prototype validation is completed.

Challenges Encountered During the Project

Despite these accomplishments, the project also encountered significant challenges
that affected the scope of activities completed during the award period.

The most significant challenge was the inability to fabricate injection-molded
prototype pallets within the project timeline. Prototype fabrication depended on the
development of injection molds and coordination with manufacturing partners. These
manufacturing constraints ultimately prevented the production of prototype pallets
during the period of performance.

A previously approved proxy pallet manufactured in China had been considered as a
substitute platform for conducting field validation testing. However, this proxy pallet
was ultimately disallowed under the terms of the award. As a result, the project was
unable to conduct pilot deployment testing during the award period.

This limitation prevented the project from completing one of its originally planned
milestones: field validation of the pallet system under real-world logistics conditions.

Scope Adjustments and Project Pivot

Because prototype fabrication was not achieved during the award period, the project
scope effectively shifted toward completing the design, engineering validation, and
economic modeling components of the work while deferring field validation to a
future phase.

This pivot allowed the project team to ensure that the core technical objectives—
design development, structural validation, and lifecycle cost analysis—were
completed successfully. These outcomes provide the foundation necessary for
future prototype fabrication and pilot deployments outside the award period.

Lessons Learned

Several important lessons emerged during the course of the project.

First, the development of reusable logistics infrastructure for emerging industries
requires close coordination between engineering design, manufacturing capability,
and supply chain partners. Manufacturing constraints can significantly influence
project timelines, particularly when new tooling or molds are required.

Second, the project highlighted the importance of early stakeholder engagement
when developing logistics solutions for the solar industry. Packaging systems must

                                    Page 20 of 26

  integrate with existing installation workflows, transportation practices, and equipment
  handling procedures in order to be adopted successfully.

  Finally, the project reinforced the need for comprehensive lifecycle economic
  analysis when introducing new packaging technologies. While environmental
  sustainability is an important motivator, economic benefits remain the primary driver
  of adoption for most industry participants. Demonstrating measurable cost savings is
  therefore essential for commercialization.

  Summary

  Overall, the project achieved several significant accomplishments, including the
  successful design of the PVpallet Series U platform, completion of engineering
  validation through structural analysis, and development of a lifecycle cost model
  demonstrating strong economic potential. While prototype fabrication and field
  validation were not completed during the award period due to manufacturing
  constraints and compliance limitations, the technical work completed during the
  project provides a strong foundation for future commercialization and pilot
  deployment of reusable pallet systems within the solar industry.

5. Path Forward:

  Future Research and Development, Technology Transfer, and
  Commercialization Opportunities

  The work completed under this award established the engineering, design, and
  economic foundation for the next generation PVpallet Series U reusable pallet
  system. While the project successfully completed design development, structural
  validation, and lifecycle cost modeling, additional research and development
  activities are required to transition the technology from engineering design to full
  commercial deployment. The following sections outline planned future R&D efforts,
  commercialization pathways, and remaining technical risks.

  Future Research and Development

  The most immediate next step in advancing the PVpallet Series U technology is
  fabrication of injection-molded prototype pallets and completion of field validation
  testing. Prototype production will require finalization of mold designs and
  coordination with manufacturing partners capable of producing the pallet
  components using recycled or recyclable high-density polyethylene. Once
  prototypes are available, pilot deployments will be conducted with solar industry
  partners to validate the pallet system under real-world transportation and installation
  conditions.

  Future field trials will focus on evaluating several key performance metrics, including
  module protection during transportation, pallet durability across multiple reuse

                                      Page 21 of 26

cycles, stackability during shipping and jobsite storage, and integration with
installation workflows used by engineering, procurement, and construction (EPC)
contractors. These pilot deployments will provide valuable operational data to refine
pallet design features and validate the lifecycle cost assumptions developed during
this project.

Additional research opportunities include further optimization of pallet geometry and
component design to improve manufacturability and reduce production costs. While
the current Series U design meets structural and functional requirements identified
during the project, further iteration may improve mold efficiency, reduce material
usage, or simplify assembly processes. Continued engineering development may
also explore the use of alternative recycled materials or hybrid material designs that
could improve sustainability and durability.

Another potential area for future research involves integration of digital asset
tracking technologies to support pallet reuse logistics. Because reusable pallet
systems depend on effective reverse logistics and asset management, incorporating
RFID tags or other tracking technologies could improve asset visibility and recovery
rates. Future development efforts may explore these technologies as part of a
broader reusable packaging logistics platform.

Technology Transfer Opportunities

There are multiple opportunities for technology transfer associated with the PVpallet
Series U platform. The most direct pathway involves commercialization of the pallet
system through domestic manufacturing and sales to solar module manufacturers,
EPC contractors, and solar project developers. PVpallet’s existing relationships
within the solar industry provide a strong starting point for introducing the new pallet
platform to potential customers once prototypes are available.

Technology transfer could also occur through partnerships with logistics providers or
packaging manufacturers that have experience operating reusable packaging pools.
Similar systems exist in other industries, such as automotive manufacturing, where
standardized reusable packaging systems are shared among supply chain
participants. Establishing a reusable pallet pooling model for solar logistics could
accelerate adoption by reducing upfront capital costs for industry participants.
Additional opportunities for technology transfer may exist through licensing
agreements with manufacturers capable of producing the pallet system at scale.
Licensing the design to established plastic manufacturing companies could help
accelerate production capacity and market penetration while maintaining quality
control and design integrity.

Commercialization Pathways

The commercialization strategy for PVpallet Series U will likely follow a phased
approach beginning with pilot deployments and demonstration projects. Initial pilot

                                    Page 22 of 26

programs will focus on validating pallet performance with a limited number of solar
projects in collaboration with early industry adopters. These early deployments will
generate operational data on pallet durability, reuse cycles, logistics efficiency, and
module damage reduction.

Following successful pilot deployments, the technology could transition to broader
commercial rollout across the solar industry. Because large-scale solar projects
often require tens of thousands of pallets, even limited market adoption could
generate substantial demand for reusable pallet systems. As the domestic solar
industry continues to expand manufacturing and installation capacity, reusable
logistics infrastructure could become an increasingly attractive solution for reducing
waste and improving supply chain efficiency.

The lifecycle cost modeling conducted during this project suggests that reusable
pallet systems may deliver meaningful economic benefits to solar developers and
EPC contractors. Demonstrating these cost savings through pilot deployments will
be a key step toward commercial adoption.

Remaining Technical Risks

Although the project successfully validated the engineering design and economic
feasibility of the PVpallet Series U system, several technical risks remain prior to full
commercialization.

One remaining risk relates to the durability of pallet components during repeated use
cycles. While structural analysis confirmed that the pallet design meets load-bearing
requirements, real-world field testing will be necessary to evaluate long-term
durability under repeated transport and handling conditions. Field trials will also
provide insight into potential wear patterns, component failure modes, and
maintenance requirements.

Another risk involves pallet return logistics. Reusable pallet systems depend on
reliable asset recovery and efficient reverse logistics processes. If pallet return rates
are lower than anticipated, the economic advantages of the reusable system could
be reduced. Future pilot programs will help validate reuse assumptions and identify
strategies for improving pallet recovery rates.

Manufacturing scalability represents an additional consideration. Injection-molded
pallet production requires significant upfront tooling investment, and manufacturing
partners must be capable of producing pallets at the scale required for large solar
projects. Identifying manufacturing partners with appropriate capabilities and
capacity will be an important step toward commercialization.

Barriers to Immediate Commercialization

                                     Page 23 of 26

  Several barriers may slow immediate market adoption of reusable pallet systems.
  One barrier is the conservative nature of the solar construction industry, where many
  participants are accustomed to existing packaging practices and may be hesitant to
  adopt new logistics systems without clear evidence of cost savings and operational
  benefits.

  Another barrier is the capital investment required to produce reusable pallets at
  scale. Although lifecycle modeling suggests long-term cost advantages, initial
  manufacturing investments and logistics system development may require upfront
  capital commitments.

  Finally, industry-wide adoption may require coordination among multiple
  stakeholders, including module manufacturers, logistics providers, EPC contractors,
  and project developers. Establishing a reusable pallet ecosystem will likely require
  collaboration across these groups.

  Summary

  The work completed under this award represents an important step toward the
  development of reusable logistics infrastructure for the solar industry. The PVpallet
  Series U design platform, engineering validation, and lifecycle economic analysis
  provide a strong technical foundation for future development. The next phase of
  research and development will focus on prototype fabrication, field validation, and
  pilot deployment programs. Successful completion of these activities will help
  demonstrate the operational and economic benefits of reusable pallet systems and
  support broader commercialization across the domestic solar industry.
