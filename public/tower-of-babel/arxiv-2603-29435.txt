On the combinatorics of the refined 1-leg DT/PT correspondence
Davide Accadia, Danilo Lewański, Sergej Monavari

Abstract
. We provide a new proof of a result of Bessenrodt on the relation among the generating series of reversed plane partitions and skew plane partitions, motivated by the geometric DT/PT wallcrossing formula for local curves recently proved by the third author. This also recovers a result of Sagan. We moreover establish various new closed formulas for the weighted enumeration of reversed and skew plane partitions, proving a result dual to a theorem by Gansner, we find a new identity on the generating series counting internal and external hooks of a given Young diagram, and we combine the latter with Bessenrodt’s theorem. Finally, we interpret our results as identities in the Fock space via the bosonic/fermionic formalism.

1.2.
Bessenrodt’s result and thin partitions

Let
\lambda
be a Young diagram. Denote by
\mathcal{H}(\lambda)
the set of
internal
hooks of
\lambda
, and by
\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\lambda)
the set of
external
hooks of
\lambda
. We remark that an internal (resp. external) hook of
\lambda
is equivalent to a

(resp.
\square\notin\lambda

\lambda

[
2
]
established the following bijection.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
2
, Thm. 3.2]
)
.

Let
\lambda
be a Young diagram. There is a bijection of sets

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\lambda)\longleftrightarrow\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\varnothing)\cup\mathcal{H}(\lambda),

which preserves hook types.

Bessenrodt’s original strategy for proving
Theorem

1.1
is to carefully keeping track of the relation between addable and removable hooks in
\lambda
of given hook and arm length. The proof exploits the representation of partitions as Maya diagrams and it admits a natural interpretation in terms of bosonic operators in the Fock space (cf. also
[
6
]
).

Our main result is an alternative proof of
Theorem

1.1
for the case of
thin partitions
(cf.
Theorem

3.5
), a class of partitions which we introduce in
Definition

3.1
. Our argument is based on a certain subdivision of the Young diagram, in which the constituent rectangles, called tectonic plates, are shifted in a careful and controlled way, that we call tectonic movement. This tectonic movement preserves hook types and makes the underlying combinatorial structure transparent, allowing the desired equality to emerge naturally.

1.3.
Hook–to–strip

For
d\geqslant\ell>0
, consider the following two collections of pairs of partitions and hooks

\displaystyle S_{d,\ell}\coloneqq\Big\{(\lambda,h)\Big|\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d},h\text{ internal hook of }\lambda,\text{ of hook length }\ell\Big\},

\displaystyle S^{\prime}_{d,\ell}\coloneqq\Big\{(\lambda,h)\Big|\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d},h\text{ external hook of }\lambda,\text{ of hook length }\ell\Big\}.

These sets are empty whenever
d
is negative. We prove the following new combinatorial identity relating sets of internal and external hooks.

Theorem 1.2

(
Theorem

4.1
)
.

Let
d\geqslant\ell>0
. There exists a bijection of sets

S_{d,\ell}\longleftrightarrow S^{\prime}_{d-\ell,\ell},

which preserves the positions of both hands and feet of the hooks, in particular preserving both content sets and hook types.

1.4.
Wall–crossing

The bijection of
Theorem

1.1

2.1

Theorem 1.3
.

Let
\lambda

\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{1-x^{a(\square)+1}y^{\ell(\square)}}=\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\varnothing)}\frac{1}{1-x^{a(\square)+1}y^{\ell(\square)}}\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{1-x^{a(\square)+1}y^{\ell(\square)}}.

Specialising

[

Corollary 1.4

Let
\lambda

\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{1-q^{h(\square)}}=\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}^{\prime}(\varnothing)}\frac{1}{1-q^{h(\square)}}\prod_{\square\in\mathcal{H}(\lambda)}\frac{1}{1-q^{h(\square)}}.

In other words, Bessenrodt’s theorem
[

(

Let

\prod_{(\lambda,\square)\in S_{d,\ell}}\frac{1}{(1-x^{a_{\lambda}(\square)+1}y^{\ell_{\lambda}(\square)})}=\prod_{(\lambda^{\prime},\square^{\prime})\in S^{\prime}_{d-\ell,\ell}}\frac{1}{(1-x^{a_{\lambda}(\square^{\prime})+1}y^{\ell_{\lambda}(\square^{\prime})})}.

2.2.
Generating series

Let
\lambda
be a Young diagram and
\bf{n}
be either a reverse plane partition, or a skew plane partition of shape
\lambda
. Let
(q_{\square})_{\square}
be an infinite set of variables, indexed by
\square\in{\mdmathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}
, and set the multivariable

{\bf{q}}^{\bf{n}}=\prod_{\square\in{\mdmathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}}q_{\square}^{n_{\square}}.

Let

be an infinite set of variables, and for a box

, set the multivariable

(2.1)

\displaystyle\mathbf{p}_{\square,\lambda}=\prod_{(i,j)\in H_{\lambda}(\square)}q_{j-i}.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\lambda
be a Young diagram, and for each
\square=(i,j)\in{\mdmathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}
set
q_{\square}=q_{j-i}
. Then

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf{n}\in\rpp(\lambda)}{\mathbf{q}}^{\bf{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\square\in\lambda}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{p}_{\square,\lambda}},

\displaystyle\sum_{\bf{n}\in\spp(\lambda)}{\bf{q}}^{\bf{n}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\square\notin\lambda}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{p}_{\square,\lambda}}.

Proof.

The first identity was proved by Gansner
[
5
, Thm. 5.1]
. The second identity follows dually by the first one. In fact, let
N>0
be an integer large enough so that
\lambda\subset[0,N]\times[0,N]
. Consider the subcollection
\spp^{N}(\lambda)\subset\spp(\lambda)
consisting of skew plane partitions whose boxes with non-zero entries are contained in
[0,N]\times[0,N]
. Consider the Young diagram

\lambda^{D}=\set{(N-i,N-j)\in{\mdmathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}}{(i,j)\in[0,N]\times[0,N]\setminus\lambda}\subset{\mdmathbb{Z}}^{2}_{\geqslant 0}.

Clearly, to each skew plane partition
\bf{n}
of shape
\lambda
in
\spp^{N}(\lambda)
uniquely corresponds a reverse plane partition
\bf{n}^{D}
of shape
\lambda^{D}
.

To each box
\square=(i,j)\notin\lambda
corresponds a box
\square^{D}=(N-i,N-j)\in\lambda^{D}
. In particular, we have that

\mathbf{p}_{\square,\lambda}=\mathbf{p}_{\square^{D},\lambda^{D}}.

Therefore, by the first identity we have that

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf{n}}\in\spp^{N}(\lambda)}{\bf{q}}^{\bf{n}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{{\bf{n}}^{D}\in\rpp(\lambda^{D})}{\bf{q}}^{{\bf{n}}^{D}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\square^{D}\in\lambda^{D}}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{p}_{\square^{D},\lambda^{D}}}

\displaystyle=\prod_{\square\in[0,N]\times[0,N]\setminus\lambda}\frac{1}{1-\mathbf{p}_{\square,\lambda}}.

Taking the limit for
N\rightarrow\infty
concludes the proof.
∎

This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎

5.
Fock space interpretation of
Theorem

4.1

We briefly recall the definition of the fermionic and bosonic Fock spaces and their most used operators. We refer to
[
18
]
for more details.

Let
V
be an infinite-dimensional
\mdmathbb{C}
-vector space with basis
\left\{\underline{s}\mid s\in\mdmathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}\right\}
. The
semi-infinite wedge space
, denoted by
\mathcal{V}=\Lambda^{\frac{\infty}{2}}V
, has a basis defined by

v_{S}:=\{\underline{s_{1}}\wedge\underline{s_{2}}\wedge\underline{s_{3}}\wedge\cdots\mid s_{1}>s_{2}>s_{3}>\cdots\},

where
S=\{s_{1}>s_{2}>\cdots\}\subset\mdmathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}
is such that the sets

S_{+}=S\setminus\left(\mdmathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\quad\text{and}\quad S_{-}=\left(\mdmathbb{Z}_{\leqslant 0}-\tfrac{1}{2}\right)\setminus S

are finite. We equip
\mathcal{V}
with the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
defined by the elements
v_{S}
being orthonormal.

There exists a unique
c\in\mdmathbb{Z}
such that
s_{k}+k-1/2=c
for
k
sufficiently large; this constant
c
is called the
charge
. The charge-zero subspace, denoted
\mathcal{V}_{0}\subset\mathcal{V}
, is spanned by semi-infinite wedge products of the form

\underline{\lambda_{1}-\tfrac{1}{2}}\wedge\underline{\lambda_{2}-\tfrac{3}{2}}\wedge\underline{\lambda_{3}-\tfrac{5}{2}}\wedge\cdots,

indexed by partitions
\lambda\in\mathcal{P}
. The basis element in
\mathcal{V}_{0}
corresponding to the empty partition,

v_{\varnothing}=\underline{-\tfrac{1}{2}}\wedge\underline{-\tfrac{3}{2}}\wedge\underline{-\tfrac{5}{2}}\wedge\cdots,

is called the
vacuum vector
and plays a special role. Similarly, the dual of the vacuum vector with respect to the inner product
\left(\cdot,\cdot\right)
is called the
covacuum vector
.

We also define the following operators that will be used in the rest of the paper.

Definition 5.1
.

For
k\in\mdmathbb{Z}+\tfrac{1}{2}
, the
fermionic operator

k
is defined by

{}_{k}v_{S}=\underline{k}\wedge v_{S}.

The operator
{}_{k}^{*}
is defined to be the adjoint of
k
with respect to the inner product. The
normally ordered product
is defined by

:{}_{i}{}_{j}^{*}:\ =\begin{cases}{}_{i}{}_{j}^{*},&\text{if }j>0,\\
-{}_{j}^{*}{}_{i},&\text{if }j<0.\end{cases}

Definition 5.2
.

For a non-negative integer
r
, define the operator

\mathcal{F}_{r}:=\sum_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\frac{k^{r}}{r!}:{}_{k}{}_{k}^{*}:

The operator
\mathcal{F}_{0}
is called the
charge operator
. We say that an operator
\mathcal{O}
acting on
\mathcal{V}
has charge
c\in\mdmathbb{Z}
if

[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{F}_{0}]=c\,\mathcal{O}.

The operators
\mathcal{F}_{r},:{}_{i}{}_{j}^{*}:
have charge zero, while the operators
i
and
{}_{j}^{*}
have charge equal to
1
and
-1
, respectively.
The operator
\mathcal{F}_{1}
is called the
energy operator
. We say that an operator
\mathcal{O}
acting on
\mathcal{V}
has
energy

E\in\mdmathbb{Z}
if

[\mathcal{O},\mathcal{F}_{1}]=E\,\mathcal{O}.

The operators
:{}_{i}{}_{j}^{*}:
have energy
j-i
. Operators with positive energy annihilate the vacuum, while operators with negative energy are annihilated by the covacuum.

Definition 5.3
.

The
free boson operators
are defined as

\alpha_{E}\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\,:{}_{k-E}{}_{k}^{*}:

These operators have energy
E
, zero charge, and satisfy the bosonic commutation relation

[\alpha_{E},\alpha_{F}]=E\delta_{E+F}.

We now enrich slightly the free boson operators to form the generating series we are interested in. The
\bf{q}
-twisted
free boson operators
are defined as

\alpha_{E}^{\bf{q}}\coloneqq\sum_{k\in\mdmathbb{Z}+\frac{1}{2}}\,\left(\prod_{j=k+1/2}^{k-E-1/2}q_{j}\right)\vdots{}_{k-E}{}_{k}^{*}\vdots

where the symbol
\vdots\cdots\vdots
stands for the usual normal ordering
:\cdots:
, which moreover applies the absolute value to the coefficient of the application to each individual Maya diagram.

Now we are armed to giver a completely equivalent statement of
Theorem

4.1
in terms of Fock space operators.

Proposition 5.4
.

For
\ell>0
we have

\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\\
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{d-\ell}\end{subarray}}\Big\langle\mu|\alpha_{\ell}^{\bf{q}}|\lambda\Big\rangle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\lambda\in\mathcal{P}_{d}\\
\mu\in\mathcal{P}_{d-\ell}\end{subarray}}\Big\langle\lambda|\alpha_{-\ell}^{\bf{q}}|\mu\Big\rangle

Proof.

It follows from the action of the free bosons via the Murnagham-Nakayama rule as removing strips of size equal to the energy in all possible ways. The rest follows by the proof of
Theorem

4.1
. This concludes the proof of the proposition.
∎
