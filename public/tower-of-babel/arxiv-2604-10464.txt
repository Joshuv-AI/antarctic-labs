On the converse of the Shimorin–Peláez–Rättyä–Wick theorem
Yuerang Li, Zipeng Wang

Abstract
. We establish a converse of the Shimorin–Peláez–Rättyä–Wick theorem. Specifically, we obtain necessary and sufficient conditions for a Shimorin kernel to be the kernel of a radial, logarithmically subharmonic weighted Bergman space.



1991 Mathematics Subject Classification
Primary 46E22, 30H20.

1.
Introduction and main results

For a nonnegative measurable function
\sigma
on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
, the weighted Bergman space
L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)
consists of all holomorphic functions on
\mathbb{D}
that are square-integrable with respect to
\sigma\mathrm{d}A
. That is,

L_{a}^{2}(\sigma):=\left\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}\sigma(z)\mathrm{d}A(z)<\infty\right\},

where
\mathrm{d}A(z)=\frac{dxdy}{\pi}
denotes the normalized area measure on the complex plane.

The space
L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space if it is a Hilbert space and the point evaluation functionals at all points of the unit disk are bounded. In that case, for each
z\in\mathbb{D}
, there exists a unique element
K_{\sigma}(\cdot,z)\in L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)
such that

f(z)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}f(\lambda)\overline{K_{\sigma}(\lambda,z)}\,\sigma(\lambda)\,\mathrm{d}A(\lambda)

for all
f\in L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)
. The function
K_{\sigma}(\cdot,z)
is the reproducing kernel, and it is called the Bergman kernel of
L_{a}^{2}(\sigma)
at the point
z
.

A particularly important class of weighted Bergman spaces is the one induced by a logarithmically subharmonic weight that reproduces the origin; that is, those
\sigma
for which
\log\sigma
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
and

p(0)=\int_{\mathbb{D}}p(z)\sigma(z)\mathrm{d}A(z)

for every polynomial
p
. This weight induces a natural hyperbolic Riemannian metric on the unit disk and also serves as a starting point for proving the positivity of the Green function for the weighted biharmonic operator (see
[
Shi02
]
for more details).

For a logarithmically subharmonic weight, the Shimorin problem in kernel representation theory asks whether its reproducing kernel is of Bergman-type, i.e., whether the kernel admits a Bergman-type integral representation. In
[
Shi02
]
, Shimorin proved that every radial logarithmically subharmonic weight admits such a representation. He proved that for a radial logarithmically subharmonic weight, there exists a measure
\nu
on
[0,1]
such that its kernel
K_{\sigma}
admits a Bergman-type integral representation

K_{\sigma}(z,\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-z\bar{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-rz\bar{\lambda}}\,d\nu(r),\qquad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

Let
\nu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
. We call the Bergman-type integral

S_{\nu}(z,\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-z\bar{\lambda}}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-rz\bar{\lambda}}\,\mathrm{d}\nu(r)

a Shimorin-type kernel on the unit disk.

Recall that a weight
\sigma
on the unit disk is radial if
\sigma(z)=\sigma(|z|)
for all
z\in\mathbb{D}
.
Among radial weights, an important class is that of
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight. For example, this class characterizes those radial weights for which the weighted Bergman projection is bounded from
L^{\infty}
onto the Bloch space. We recall that a finite positive Borel measure
\omega
on
[0,1)
is said to belong to

\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that

\widehat{\omega}(r):=\omega([r,1))\leq C\widehat{\omega}\left(\frac{1+r}{2}\right),\qquad r\in[0,1).

One can consult
[
PR14
]
,
[
PR15
]
,
[
PR21
]
and
[
PRW19
]
for more details.

In 2019, Peláez, Rättyä, and Wick
[
PRW19
]
systematically developed a Shimorin-type integral representation for Bergman kernels. In particular, they established a surprising and interesting connection between logarithmically subharmonic weights and
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weights. Precisely, they showed that for any finite measure
\nu
on
[0,1]
satisfying

(1.1)

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\mathrm{d}\nu(r)}{1-r}=\infty,

the Shimorin-type kernel is the reproducing kernel of a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weighted Bergman space.

Shimorin–Peláez–Rättyä–Wick Theorem.
Every radial logarithmically subharmonic weight is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight, and its reproducing kernel has a Shimorin-type representation.

From its definition, we only know that a Shimorin-type kernel is positive definite; it is not necessarily a Bergman kernel. For example, when
\nu=\delta_{0}
, the Dirac measure at
0
, we have

S_{\nu}(z,\lambda)=\frac{1}{1-z\bar{\lambda}},

which is the reproducing kernel of the Hardy space. It is therefore natural to ask when a Shimorin-type kernel represents a Bergman kernel. Moreover, this occurs when the weight is logarithmically subharmonic. Precisely, we study the following question concerning the converse of the Shimorin–Peláez–Rättyä–Wick theorem.

Question 1
.

Let
\nu
be a positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
and let
S_{\nu}
denote the associated Shimorin-type kernel on the unit disk. Under what conditions is
S_{\nu}
the kernel of a radial, logarithmically subharmonic weighted Bergman space?

The following result, essentially due to Shimorin, Peláez, Rättyä, and Wick, characterizes when a Shimorin-type kernel is a Bergman kernel.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
. The Shimorin-type kernel
S_{\nu}
is a Bergman kernel if and only if the measure
\nu
satisfies the Peláez–Rättyä–Wick condition, i.e.,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\infty.

We now state our main result, which answers Question
1
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
satisfying the Peláez–Rättyä–Wick condition.
The Shimorin-type kernel
S_{\nu}
is the kernel of a radial logarithmically subharmonic weighted Bergman space if and only if there exists a logarithmically convex function

h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

such that

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

and

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty

for some constant
T>0
.

2.
The proof of Theorem
1.1

The main goal of this section is to prove Theorem
1.1
, which follows from Theorem
2.1
. Note that a radial weight on the unit disk
\mathbb{D}
is determined by a measure on
[0,1)
. More precisely, let
\omega
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1)
.
The associated radial measure
\omega\otimes m
on
\mathbb{D}
is defined by

\mathrm{d}(\omega\otimes m)(re^{i\theta})=r\mathrm{d}\omega(r)\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\pi},

where
m=\frac{\mathrm{d}\theta}{\pi}
is the normalized Haar measure on the unit circle
\mathbb{T}
. Then the weighted Bergman space is

L_{a}^{2}(\omega):=\left\{f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D}):\|f\|_{L_{a}^{2}(\omega)}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{D}}|f(z)|^{2}\mathrm{d}(\omega\otimes m)(z)<\infty\right\}.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
, and let
S_{\nu}
be the corresponding Shimorin-type kernel.
There exists a finite positive Borel measure
\omega
on
[0,1)
with
\omega([r,1))>0
for all
0<r<1
such that

K_{\omega}(z,\lambda)=S_{\nu}(z,\lambda),\quad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

if and only if

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\infty.

Moreover,
\omega
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight.

Remark 2.1
.

We emphasize once again that this result is due to Shimorin, Peláez, Rättyä, and Wick. Based on recent work by Berg and Durán
[
BD05
]
, we present a self-contained proof that is of independent interest.

Remark 2.2
.

For any radial weight
\omega
, the Bergman space
L^{2}_{a}(\omega)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space if and only if
\omega([r,1))>0
for all
0<r<1
.

We begin with the following two useful results of Berg and Durán.

Lemma 2.2

(Theorem 1.1 in
[
BD05
]
)
.

Let
(a_{n})_{n}
be a Hausdorff moment sequence of a nonzero measure
\nu
. Then
the sequence
(b_{n})_{n}
defined by

b_{n}=\frac{1}{a_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}}

is a Hausdorff moment sequence, and its associated measure

\mu

satisfies
\mu(\{0\})=0
and

(2.2)

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{z+1}}{1-t}\mathrm{d}\nu(t)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{1}t^{z}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)\right)=1,\qquad\text{for }\operatorname{Re}z\geq 0.

Lemma 2.3

(Theorem 4.1 in
[
BD05
]
)
.

Let
\nu
be a nonzero measure on
[0,1]
and write

\nu=a\delta_{0}+b\delta_{1}+\widetilde{\nu}

with
a=\nu(\{0\}),b=\nu(\{1\}),\widetilde{\nu}=\nu|_{(0,1)}.

Let
f
be the nonzero Bernstein function given by

f(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{s}}{1-t}\mathrm{d}\nu(t)=a+bs+\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{s}}{1-t}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\nu}(t),\quad s>0.

Then the measure

\mu

determined by Lemma
2.2

is given by

\mathrm{d}\mu(x)=x\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\kappa}(x),

where
\widetilde{\kappa}
is the potential kernel of the corresponding product convolution semigroup satisfying

\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\kappa}(t)=\frac{1}{f(s)},\quad s>0.

Moreover, the moment sequence

b_{n}=\frac{1}{a_{0}+\cdots+a_{n}}

of
\mu
is given by

b_{n}=\frac{1}{f(n+1)},\qquad n\geq 0.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
, and let
S_{\nu}
be the corresponding Shimorin-type kernel.
Suppose that

S_{\nu}(z,\lambda)=K_{\omega}(z,\lambda),\quad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

for some measure
\omega
on
[0,1)
with
\omega([r,1))>0
for all
0<r<1
.
Note that

S_{\nu}(z,\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)(z\bar{\lambda})^{n},

and

K_{\omega}(z,\lambda)=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{1}{2\omega_{2n+1}}(z\bar{\lambda})^{n},

where
\omega_{2n+1}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\mathrm{d}\omega(r)
.
Then we have

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\frac{1}{2\omega_{2n+1}}.

By the Lebesgue dominated convergence theorem,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\omega_{2n+1}=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\mathrm{d}\omega(r)=0.

Hence, by the monotone convergence theorem,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\frac{1}{2\omega_{2n+1}}=\infty.

Conversely, consider the Hausdorff moment sequence of the measure
\nu

v_{k}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{k}\mathrm{d}\nu(r),\quad k\in\mathbb{N},

by Lemma
2.2
, there exists a finite positive Borel measure
\mu
on
[0,1]
such that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\cdot\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=1,\quad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Let
\psi(x)=\sqrt{x}
. By a change of variables,

\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2n}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2n+1}\frac{1}{t}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t).

Then we can define a measure
\omega
on
[0,1]
by

(2.3)

\omega(E)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{E}\frac{1}{t}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\psi^{-1}(E)}r^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}\mu(r),

where
E\subset[0,1]
is a Borel set. Hence we have

2\omega_{2n+1}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{2n+1}\frac{1}{t}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t)=\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\frac{1}{\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)}.

To complete the proof, it remains to show that

•

\omega
is a finite measure on
[0,1]
, i.e.,
\int_{0}^{1}r^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)<\infty
;

•

\omega(\{1\})=0
, that is,
\omega
is concentrated on
[0,1)
;

•

For all
0<r<1
,
\omega([r,1))>0
;

•

\omega
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight.

Let
f
be the nonzero Bernstein function given by

f(s)=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-t^{s}}{1-t}\mathrm{d}\nu(t).

By Lemma
2.3
, there is a
\sigma
-finite positive Borel measure
\widetilde{\kappa}
on
[0,1]
such that

\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=t\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\kappa}(t),\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\kappa}(t)=\frac{1}{f(s)},\ s>0.

Hence,

\int_{0}^{1}r^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\int_{0}^{1}t^{1/2}\mathrm{d}\widetilde{\kappa}(t)=\frac{1}{f(1/2)}<\infty.

Next, to show
\omega(\{1\})=0
, we only need to show
\mu(\{1\})=0
(Indeed,
\omega(\{1\})=\frac{1}{2}\mu(\{1\})
). If
\mu(\{1\})\neq 0
, then

\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)>\mu(\{1\})>0.

Since
\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\infty
, we have

\lim_{n\to\infty}\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\infty.

This contradicts the fact that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=1.

Finally, we show that for all
0<r<1
,

\mu([r,1))=\int_{r}^{1}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)>0.

Otherwise, if there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that
\mu([r_{0},1))=0
, then

\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)=\int_{0}^{r_{0}}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)\leq r_{0}^{n}\mu([0,1)),

and note that

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\int_{0}^{1}r^{k}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\leq(n+1)\nu([0,1]).

Hence,

\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\mu(r)\leq(n+1)r_{0}^{n}\mu([0,1))\nu([0,1]).

The right-hand side of the inequality converges to zero as
n\to\infty
, which is a contradiction.

Hence,

\omega([r,1))=\frac{1}{2}\int_{[r,1)}t^{-1}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{[r^{2},1)}s^{-1/2}\mathrm{d}\mu(s)\geq\frac{1}{2}\mu([r^{2},1))>0.

It remains to show that
\omega
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight.
This is equivalent to showing
[
PRW19
]
that there exists a finite positive constant
C
such that

\omega_{n}\leq C\omega_{2n}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, where
\omega_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}r^{n}\mathrm{d}\omega(r)
.

The case
n=0
is trivial. Let
n\geq 1
. By the definition (
2.3
) of the measure
\omega
and a change of variables, we have

\omega_{n}=\int_{0}^{1}t^{n}\mathrm{d}\omega(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}t^{n-1}\mathrm{d}(\psi_{*}\mu)(t)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}t^{(n-1)/2}\mathrm{d}\mu(t).

Here
\psi(x)=\sqrt{x}
.
By Lemma
2.3
, for every
s\geq 0
,

\int_{0}^{1}t^{s}\mathrm{d}\mu(t)=\frac{1}{f(s+1)}.

Therefore, for every
n\geq 1
,

\omega_{n}=\frac{1}{2f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)},\qquad\omega_{2n}=\frac{1}{2f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}.

Thus

\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{2n}}=\frac{f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}.

It remains to prove that

f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq 2f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right),\qquad n\geq 1.

For
k\geq 0
, write
\nu_{k}:=\int_{0}^{1}r^{k}\mathrm{d}\nu(r).

Since
\nu
is a finite positive measure on
[0,1]
, the sequence
(\nu_{k})_{k\geq 0}
is decreasing, and
f
is increasing. We now distinguish two cases.

Case 1:
n=2m-1
for
m\geq 1
.

Then

f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)=f(2m-\frac{1}{2})\leq f(2m)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m-1}\nu_{k},\qquad f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)=f(m)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}.

Since
(\nu_{k})
is decreasing, we have

\sum_{k=m}^{2m-1}\nu_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}.

Hence

f(2m)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\sum_{k=m}^{2m-1}\nu_{k}\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}=2f(m).

Therefore,

f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq 2f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right).

Case 2:
n=2m
for
m\geq 1
.

Since
f
is increasing, we get

f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)=f\left(2m+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq f(2m+1)=\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\nu_{k}.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)=f\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{m+1/2}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\int_{0}^{1}r^{m}\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r).

Since, for every
r\in(0,1)
,

\frac{1-r^{1/2}}{1-r}=\frac{1}{1+r^{1/2}}\geq\frac{1}{2},

we have

f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)\geq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{1}r^{m}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{m}.

Moreover, using again that
(\nu_{k})
is decreasing, we have

\sum_{k=m+1}^{2m}\nu_{k}\leq\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}.

Therefore,

\displaystyle f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{2m}\nu_{k}=\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\nu_{m}+\sum_{k=m+1}^{2m}\nu_{k}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\nu_{m}=2\left(\sum_{k=0}^{m-1}\nu_{k}+\frac{1}{2}\nu_{m}\right)

\displaystyle\leq 2f\left(m+\frac{1}{2}\right)=2f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right).

Combining the two cases, we obtain

f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)\leq 2f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right),\qquad n\geq 1.

Hence, for every
n\geq 1
,

\frac{\omega_{n}}{\omega_{2n}}=\frac{f\left(n+\frac{1}{2}\right)}{f\left(\frac{n+1}{2}\right)}\leq 2.

Together with the trivial case
n=0
, this shows that

\omega_{n}\leq 2\omega_{2n},\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

Therefore
\omega
is a
\widehat{\mathcal{D}}
-weight. This completes the proof.
∎

3.
The proof of Theorem
1.2

The following theorem characterizes those Shimorin-type kernels that arise from
radial logarithmically subharmonic weights. In particular,
Theorem
1.2
is an immediate consequence of
Theorem
3.1
together with
Theorem
1.1
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\nu
be a finite positive Borel measure on
[0,1]
satisfying the Peláez–Rättyä–Wick condition.
Let
S_{\nu}
be the corresponding Shimorin-type kernel.
Then there exists a radial logarithmically subharmonic weight on the unit disk

\omega(z)=\omega(|z|)

such that

\int_{0}^{1}r\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r<\infty

and

K_{\omega}(z,\lambda)=S_{\nu}(z,\lambda),\qquad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D},

if and only if there exists a logarithmically convex function

h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)

such that

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

and such that there exists
T>0
with

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty.

For the proof of Theorem
3.1
, we need some results on potential theory.

Lemma 3.2

(
[
Ran95
, Corollary 2.5.2]
)
.

Let
u
be a subharmonic function on a domain
D\subset\mathbb{C}
, and assume that

u\not\equiv-\infty.

If
\{z:|z-w|\leq\rho\}\subset D
, then

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u\left(w+\rho e^{i\theta}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta>-\infty.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
\varphi
be a radial logarithmically subharmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
with
\varphi\not\equiv 0
. Set

u(z):=\log\varphi(z),\qquad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
u
is allowed to take the value
-\infty
. Then the function

g(t):=u(e^{-t/2})=\log\varphi(e^{-t/2}),\qquad t>0,

is convex on
(0,\infty)
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is logarithmically subharmonic, the function

u=\log\varphi

is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
and is not identically
-\infty
. Since
\varphi
is radial,

u
is radial as well. Hence there exists a function, still denoted by
u
, such that

u(z)=u(|z|),
for any
z\in\mathbb{D}.

We first claim that

u(r)>-\infty,\qquad 0<r<1.

Suppose, to the contrary, that there exists
r_{0}\in(0,1)
such that

u(r_{0})=-\infty.

Since
u
is radial, we have

u(z)=u(|z|)=-\infty,\qquad|z|=r_{0}.

Hence

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta=-\infty.

On the other hand,
u
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
and
u\not\equiv-\infty
. Applying Lemma
3.2
with
D=\mathbb{D}
,
w=0
, and
\rho=r_{0}
, we obtain

\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}u\left(r_{0}e^{i\theta}\right)\mathrm{d}\theta>-\infty,

which is a contradiction. Therefore,
for every

r\in(0,1)
, we have

u(r)>-\infty.

We now prove that
g
is convex. Fix
t_{1},t_{2}>0
and
\lambda\in(0,1)
, and set

t:=(1-\lambda)t_{1}+\lambda t_{2}.

Let

r_{1}:=e^{-t_{1}/2},\qquad r_{2}:=e^{-t_{2}/2},\qquad r:=e^{-t/2}.

Then

r=r_{1}^{1-\lambda}r_{2}^{\lambda}.

Without loss of generality, assume
t_{1}<t_{2}
, so that
r_{1}>r_{2}
.

Consider the annulus

A:=\{z\in\mathbb{C}:\ r_{2}<|z|<r_{1}\}.

Define

h(z):=\frac{\log(r_{1}/|z|)}{\log(r_{1}/r_{2})}u(r_{2})+\frac{\log(|z|/r_{2})}{\log(r_{1}/r_{2})}u(r_{1}),\qquad z\in A.

Since
\log|z|
is harmonic on
A
, the function
h
is harmonic on
A
.
Moreover,
h
is radial and satisfies

h(\zeta)=u(r_{1})\quad\text{for }|\zeta|=r_{1},\qquad h(\zeta)=u(r_{2})\quad\text{for }|\zeta|=r_{2}.

Now
u-h
is subharmonic on
A
, because
u
is subharmonic and
h
is harmonic.
Also, for every boundary point
\zeta\in\partial A
, we have

\limsup_{A\ni z\to\zeta}(u(z)-h(z))\leq 0.

Indeed, if
|\zeta|=r_{1}
, then by radiality,

\limsup_{A\ni z\to\zeta}u(z)\leq u(r_{1})=h(\zeta),

and similarly, if
|\zeta|=r_{2}
, then

\limsup_{A\ni z\to\zeta}u(z)\leq u(r_{2})=h(\zeta).

Hence, by the maximum principle for subharmonic functions,

u(z)\leq h(z),\qquad z\in A.

In particular, for every
z
with
|z|=r
,
u(r)\leq h(r)
.

Since
r=r_{1}^{1-\lambda}r_{2}^{\lambda},
we get

\log\frac{r_{1}}{r}=\log\frac{r_{1}}{r_{1}^{1-\lambda}r_{2}^{\lambda}}=\lambda\log\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}},

and

\log\frac{r}{r_{2}}=\log\frac{r_{1}^{1-\lambda}r_{2}^{\lambda}}{r_{2}}=(1-\lambda)\log\frac{r_{1}}{r_{2}}.

Therefore

h(r)=\lambda u(r_{2})+(1-\lambda)u(r_{1}).

Thus

u(r)\leq(1-\lambda)u(r_{1})+\lambda u(r_{2}).

Recalling that
r=e^{-t/2}
,
r_{1}=e^{-t_{1}/2}
, and
r_{2}=e^{-t_{2}/2}
, we obtain

u(e^{-t/2})\leq(1-\lambda)u(e^{-t_{1}/2})+\lambda u(e^{-t_{2}/2}),

that is,

g\big((1-\lambda)t_{1}+\lambda t_{2}\big)\leq(1-\lambda)g(t_{1})+\lambda g(t_{2}).

Hence
g
is convex on
(0,\infty)
.
∎

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be such that
\log h
is convex on
(0,\infty)
.
Define

\varphi(z)=\varphi(|z|):=\frac{h(-2\log|z|)}{|z|^{2}},\qquad 0<|z|<1.

Then
\varphi
is logarithmically subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
.

If, in addition,
\varphi
is bounded above in a neighborhood of
0

(for example, if there exists
T>0
such that

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty),

then
\varphi
extends to a logarithmically subharmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
.

Proof.

Since
h>0
, the function

k(t):=\log h(t),\qquad t>0,

is well defined. By assumption,
k
is convex on
(0,\infty)
.

Define

g(t):=k(t)+t=\log h(t)+t,\qquad t>0.

Since the sum of a convex function and an affine function is convex,
g
is convex on

(0,\infty)
.

Now consider the right half-plane

\mathbb{H}:=\{w\in\mathbb{C}:\operatorname{Re}w>0\},

and define

U(w):=g(\operatorname{Re}w),\qquad w\in\mathbb{H}.

Then
U
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{H}
.

Next fix
z_{0}\in\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Choose a simply connected neighborhood

V\subset\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
of
z_{0}
on which a holomorphic branch of the logarithm
exists; denote it by
\mathrm{Log}z
. Define

F(z):=-2\mathrm{Log}z,\qquad z\in V.

Then
F
is holomorphic on
V
, and

\operatorname{Re}F(z)=\operatorname{Re}(-2\mathrm{Log}z)=-2\log|z|>0,\qquad z\in V,

because
0<|z|<1
. Hence
F(V)\subset\mathbb{H}
.

Since
U
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{H}
and
F
is holomorphic, the composition
U\circ F
is subharmonic on
V
.
On the other hand, for
z\in V
,

(U\circ F)(z)=g(\operatorname{Re}F(z))=g(-2\log|z|).

By the definition of
g
,

g(-2\log|z|)=\log h(-2\log|z|)-2\log|z|.

Since

\varphi(z)=\frac{h(-2\log|z|)}{|z|^{2}},

it follows that

(U\circ F)(z)=\log\varphi(z).

Therefore
\log\varphi
is subharmonic on
V
. Since
z_{0}
was arbitrary, we conclude that
\log\varphi

is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. Equivalently,
\varphi
is logarithmically
subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
.

For the last assertion, assume that
\varphi
is bounded above in a neighborhood of
0
.
Then
\log\varphi
is bounded above in a punctured neighborhood of
0
and is subharmonic
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
. By the removable singularity theorem for subharmonic functions,
the function

\widetilde{u}(z):=\begin{cases}\log\varphi(z),&z\neq 0,\\[4.0pt]
\limsup\limits_{\zeta\to 0}\log\varphi(\zeta),&z=0,\end{cases}

is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
. Setting

\widetilde{\varphi}(z):=e^{\widetilde{u}(z)},

we obtain a logarithmically subharmonic extension of
\varphi
to the whole disk.
This completes the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
3.1
.

Necessity.

Assume that there exists a radial logarithmically subharmonic weight

\omega
on
\mathbb{D}
such that

\int_{0}^{1}r\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r<\infty

and

K_{\omega}(z,\lambda)=S_{\nu}(z,\lambda),\qquad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

By the coefficient formulas for the kernels
K_{\omega}
and
S_{\nu}
, it follows that
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(2\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r\right)=1.

Define the function
h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
by

h(t):=e^{-t}\omega(e^{-t/2}),\qquad t>0.

By Lemma
3.3
, the function
t\mapsto\log\omega(e^{-t/2})
is convex on
(0,\infty)
. Hence

\log h(t)=\log\omega(e^{-t/2})-t

is also convex on
(0,\infty)
, because subtracting an affine function preserves convexity.
Thus
h
is logarithmically convex.

Next, by the change of variables
r=e^{-t/2}
, we obtain for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}e^{-t}\omega(e^{-t/2})\mathrm{d}t=2\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r.

Therefore

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N}.

It remains to verify the growth condition. Since
\log\omega
is subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}
,
it is locally bounded above near
0
. Hence
\omega
is bounded above in some neighborhood
of
0
. Therefore, for all sufficiently large
t
,

e^{t}h(t)=\omega(e^{-t/2})

is bounded, that is, there exists
T>0
such that

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty.

Sufficiency.

Assume that there exists a logarithmically convex function

h:(0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
such that

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t\right)=1,\qquad n\in\mathbb{N},

and such that

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty

for some
T>0
.

Define

\omega(z):=\frac{h(-2\log|z|)}{|z|^{2}},\qquad 0<|z|<1.

By Lemma
3.4
, the function
\omega

is logarithmically subharmonic on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
, and the growth condition

\sup_{t\geq T}e^{t}h(t)<\infty

implies that
\omega
is bounded above near
0
. Hence
\omega
extends to a
logarithmically subharmonic function on
\mathbb{D}
. Since the original function
on
\mathbb{D}\setminus\{0\}
is radial, the extension is radial as well.
Since
h(t)>0
for all
t>0
, we have
\omega(r)>0
for all
0<r<1
.
Hence
\omega([r,1))>0
for every
0<r<1
, so
L_{a}^{2}(\omega)
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.
We next show that

\int_{0}^{1}r\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r<\infty.

Taking
n=0
, we get

\nu([0,1])\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\mathrm{d}t=1.

Since
\nu([0,1])>0
, it follows that

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{\nu([0,1])}<\infty.

Using again the change of variables
t=-2\log r
, we obtain

\int_{0}^{1}r\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r=\int_{0}^{1}\frac{h(-2\log r)}{r}\mathrm{d}r=\frac{1}{2}\int_{0}^{\infty}h(t)\mathrm{d}t=\frac{1}{2\nu([0,1])}<\infty.

Finally, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle 2\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=2\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n-1}h(-2\log r)\mathrm{d}r

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-nt}h(t)\mathrm{d}t.

Hence, for each
n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left(\int_{0}^{1}\frac{1-r^{n+1}}{1-r}\mathrm{d}\nu(r)\right)\left(2\int_{0}^{1}r^{2n+1}\omega(r)\mathrm{d}r\right)=1.

By the coefficient formulas for
K_{\omega}
and
S_{\nu}
, this implies

K_{\omega}(z,\lambda)=S_{\nu}(z,\lambda),\qquad z,\lambda\in\mathbb{D}.

This completes the proof.
∎
