A New Fractional Step Structure Preserving Method for The Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert Equation
Changjian Xie

Abstract
In this paper, we propose a structure preserving method using a Crank-Nicolson’s type method with an implicit Gauss-Seidel fractional iteration. Such a method is of first-order accuracy in time and second-order accuracy in space, stable and length preserving. Such kind of method brings great benefits for the theoretical analysis. The numerical accuracy, norm preserving and stability are verified for 1D and 3D tests.

Introduction

The Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation
[
6
,
3
]
incorporates two fundamental terms governing magnetization dynamics: a gyromagnetic term, responsible for energy conservation, and a damping term, which models energy dissipation.
The LLG equation constitutes a vector-valued, nonlinear system characterized by a point-wise constant magnitude constraint on the magnetization vector. The model without damping is given by

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{t}=-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}.

Significant research efforts have been dedicated to devising efficient and numerically stable methods for micromagnetic simulations, as summarized in review articles such as
[
5
,
2
]
.
Such a equation can be solved with Crank-Nicolson’s method (midpoint type) below,

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{2}\times\Delta_{h}\left(\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{2}\right),

which is a nonlinear scheme with great stability. However, this paper focus on the linearity, efficiency, stability and structure preservation.
A notable limitation persists: the implicit Crank-Nicolson’s method
[
4
]
involves a nonlinear system with the norm preserving property. In this paper, we use such a method and such property to construct a structure preserving method based on implicit Gauss-Seidel iteration.

section

Concluding remarks and potential future research directions are provided in
section

.

2
The governing equation and numerical scheme

The Landau-Lifshitz-Gilbert (LLG) equation forms the fundamental basis of micromagnetics, providing a rigorous description of the spatiotemporal evolution of magnetization in ferromagnetic materials by incorporating two key physical phenomena: gyromagnetic precession and dissipative relaxation
[
6
,
1
]
. In nondimensional form, this governing equation is expressed as

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{t}=-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}-\alpha\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$})

(1)

subject to the homogeneous Neumann boundary condition

\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial{\bm{\nu}}}\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=0,

(2)

where
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
(
d=1,2,3
) represents the bounded domain of the ferromagnetic material, and
\bm{\nu}
is the unit outward normal vector on the boundary
\partial\Omega
. This boundary condition ensures no magnetic surface charge, a physically appropriate assumption for isolated ferromagnetic systems.

The magnetization field
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
is a three-dimensional vector field satisfying the pointwise constraint
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|=1
, stemming from the quantum mechanical alignment of electron spins in ferromagnets. The first term on the right-hand side of
eq.

1
describes gyromagnetic precession, where magnetic moments precess around the exchange field
\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}
. The second term represents dissipative relaxation, with
\alpha>0
being the dimensionless Gilbert damping coefficient that governs the rate of energy dissipation into the lattice.

For the construction of the structure preserving method, we set
\alpha=0
for
eq.

1
, we have the LLG equation below,

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{t}=-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$}.

(3)

It is obvious that
eq.

3
has a length-preserving property during the evolution process. To see this, we do scalar multiplication of
eq.

3
with
m
:

\displaystyle\frac{\partial\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}{\partial t}\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=-(\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\Delta\mbox{\boldmath$m$})\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$m$}=0.

Then we have

\displaystyle\frac{\partial|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}|^{2}}{\partial t}=0,

which implies
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},t)|
is constant for all
t
and
x
. And we assume that
|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(\mbox{\boldmath$x$},0)|=1
. Let
Let
E(\mathbf{m}(\mathbf{x},t))
be an energy defined by
E(\mathbf{m}(t)):=\|\nabla\mathbf{m}(t)\|_{L^{2}(\Omega)}^{2}
. By taking an inner product of
eq.

3
with
\Delta\mathbf{m}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial t}\cdot\Delta{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}=-({\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\times\Delta{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}})\cdot\Delta{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}=0.

(4)

Using homogeneous Neumann or periodic boundary conditions, from
eq.

4
we have

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} 0&=\int_{\Omega}\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial t}\cdot\Delta{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\,dx=\int_{\partial\Omega}\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial t}\cdot\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}}\,ds-\int_{\Omega}\nabla\frac{\partial{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}}{\partial t}:\nabla{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}\,dx\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\frac{dE({\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(t))}{dt},\end{aligned}

which implies that
E({\mbox{\boldmath$m$}}(t))
is constant and this problem has an energy conservation property. Here,
n
is a unit normal vector to
\partial\Omega
and the operator ‘:’ is defined as
A:B=\sum_{ij}a_{ij}b_{ij}
.

If we consider the simple linear vectorial equation

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{t}=-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$},

(5)

where
\mbox{\boldmath$a$}^{T}=(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
is a constant vector. We use the Crank-Nicolson method to
eq.

5
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{2}\times\mbox{\boldmath$a$},

(6)

which is norm preserving, since that if
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}
do the inner product for both sides, leading to

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}\|_{2}=\|\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}\|_{2}.

After rearranging the compact form for
eq.

6
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}\\
\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{2}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n+1}\\
m_{2}^{n+1}\\
m_{3}^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(a_{2}m_{3}^{n}-a_{3}m_{2}^{n})\\
m_{2}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(a_{3}m_{1}^{n}-a_{1}m_{3}^{n})\\
m_{3}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(a_{1}m_{2}^{n}-a_{2}m_{1}^{n})\end{pmatrix}

We then have another form

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n+1}\\
m_{2}^{n+1}\\
m_{3}^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}\\
\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{2}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}1&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}\\
\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{3}&1&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{2}&\frac{1}{2}\Delta ta_{1}&1\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n}\\
m_{2}^{n}\\
m_{3}^{n}\end{pmatrix}=A\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n}\\
m_{2}^{n}\\
m_{3}^{n}\end{pmatrix}

where

\displaystyle A=\frac{1}{S}\begin{pmatrix}1+\beta^{2}a_{1}^{2}&-2\beta a_{3}+\beta^{2}a_{1}a_{2}&2\beta a_{2}+\beta^{2}a_{1}a_{3}\\
2\beta a_{3}+\beta^{2}a_{1}a_{2}&1+\beta^{2}a_{2}^{2}&-2\beta a_{1}+\beta^{2}a_{2}a_{3}\\
-2\beta a_{2}+\beta^{2}a_{1}a_{3}&2\beta a_{1}+\beta^{2}a_{2}a_{3}&1+\beta^{2}a_{3}^{2}\end{pmatrix}

where
S=\det(A)=1+\beta^{2}(a_{1}^{2}+a_{2}^{2}+a_{3}^{2})
and
\beta=\frac{\Delta t}{2}
. The spectral analysis for the matrix
A
is given by our previous work.

For

3

\displaystyle\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}}{2}\times\Delta_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h},

(7)

where

. To be specific, we propose

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{3}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{2}\\
-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{3}&1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{1}\\

m_{2}^{n+1}\\
m_{3}^{n+1}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}m_{1}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(\Delta_{h}g_{2}m_{3}^{n}-\Delta_{h}g_{3}m_{2}^{n})\\
m_{2}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(\Delta_{h}g_{3}m_{1}^{n}-\Delta_{h}g_{1}m_{3}^{n})\\
m_{3}^{n}+\frac{1}{2}\Delta t(\Delta_{h}g_{1}m_{2}^{n}-\Delta_{h}g_{2}m_{1}^{n})\end{pmatrix}

The trick is to handling the

or
t_{n+1}
. If
g
evaluate at

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}1&\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{3}^{n}&-\frac{1}{2}\Delta t\Delta_{h}g_{2}^{n}\\

\displaystyle|\gamma_{1}|=1,\quad|\gamma_{2}|=|\gamma_{3}|=\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+a_{n+1}^{2}+b_{n+1}^{2}+c_{n}^{2}}}\leq 1.

Here
a_{n+1},b_{n+1},c_{n}
is small compared to
1
.

Remark 3
.

We first define explicit and implicit methods using the general form of time integration for a differential equation:

\frac{du}{dt}=f(t,u),\quad u(t_{0})=u_{0}

where
u(t)
is the solution variable,
t
is time, and
f(\cdot)
is the right-hand side (RHS) function.

Explicit methods compute the next time step
u_{n+1}
using only known values from previous steps (
u_{n},u_{n-1},\dots
):

u_{n+1}=u_{n}+\Delta t\cdot F_{\text{explicit}}(t_{n},u_{n})

The most common example is the
Forward Euler Method
:

u_{n+1}=u_{n}+\Delta t\cdot f(t_{n},u_{n})

where
\Delta t=t_{n+1}-t_{n}
is the time step size.

Implicit methods compute
u_{n+1}
using unknown values at the next time step (
u_{n+1}
):

u_{n+1}=u_{n}+\Delta t\cdot F_{\text{implicit}}(t_{n+1},u_{n+1})

The classic example is the
Backward Euler Method
:

u_{n+1}=u_{n}+\Delta t\cdot f(t_{n+1},u_{n+1})

This requires solving an algebraic equation (linear/nonlinear) for
u_{n+1}
at each step.

Stability is the most critical advantage of implicit methods. We illustrate this with the linear test problem:

\frac{du}{dt}=\lambda u,\quad\lambda\in\mathbb{C},\text{Re}(\lambda)<0

Applying Forward Euler gives:

u_{n+1}=(1+\lambda\Delta t)u_{n}

For stability (
|u_{n+1}|\leq|u_{n}|
), we require:

|1+\lambda\Delta t|\leq 1

This imposes a strict upper bound on
\Delta t
(e.g., for
\lambda=-10
,
\Delta t<0.2
). For stiff problems (large
|\lambda|
),
\Delta t
must be extremely small.

Applying Backward Euler gives:

u_{n+1}=\frac{1}{1-\lambda\Delta t}u_{n}

For stability:

\left|\frac{1}{1-\lambda\Delta t}\right|\leq 1

This holds for any positive
\Delta t
when
\text{Re}(\lambda)<0
(A-stable). Implicit methods have unconditional stability for stiff problems, while explicit methods have conditional stability.

For our step 3, the matrix

\displaystyle A=\begin{pmatrix}1&c&-b\\
-c&1&a\\
b&-a&1\end{pmatrix}

Here are more comparison details for stability:

1.

For Crank-Nicolson’s scheme,
a,b,c
directly depends on
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}^{n+1}
, so the implicit way gives great stability (A-stability). However, it has to be a nonlinear system.

2.

If
a,b,c
directly depends on
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}^{n}
, we get an explicit scheme with CFL-type conditional stability, even though the lower order term is implicit.

3.

If
a,b,c
indirectly depends on
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}^{n}
, say implicit treatment by solving a heat diffusion equation, we get a slightly better CFL-condition.

4.

Our method design a Gauss-Seidel type iteration to update the
m^{n+1}
, thus
a,b,c
can be formed as a mixed way to combine
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}^{n}
and
\mbox{\boldmath$m$}^{n+1}
. Such a method gives unconditionally stable results.

3.3
Convergence of our proposed scheme

By the Lax equivalence theorem, consistency and stability imply convergence. As
\Delta t,h\to 0
,

\max_{0\leq n\leq T/\Delta t}\|\bm{m}^{n}-\bm{m}(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}}\to 0,

with first-order accuracy in time and second-order accuracy in space:

\|\bm{m}^{n}-\bm{m}(t^{n})\|_{L^{2}}=O(\Delta t+h^{2}).

Considering the implicit scheme
eq.

23
, we denote
e_{h}^{n}=\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}^{n}-\mbox{\boldmath$m$}_{h}(t_{n})
, and have

\displaystyle\frac{e_{h}^{n+1}-\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}}{\Delta t}=-\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}}{2}\times\Delta_{h}\mbox{\boldmath$g$}_{h}^{n+1}-\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t_{n+1})+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t_{n})}{2}\times\Delta_{h}(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+O(\Delta t+h^{2})

Taking the inner product with
\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}
, we have

\displaystyle\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}\|^{2}-\|\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\|^{2}=-\Delta t\langle\frac{\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t_{n+1})+\mbox{\boldmath$m$}(t_{n})}{2}\times\Delta_{h}(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1},\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n+1}+\mbox{\boldmath$e$}_{h}^{n}\rangle+O(\Delta t+h^{2}).

Indeed, we have

\|e_{h}^{n}\|_{L^{2}}\leq C(\Delta t+h^{2}),\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{Z}

which will be proved in the future work.

Lemma 1

(Boundedness of Elliptic Operator)
.

For any
\Delta t>0
and mesh size
h>0
, the operator
(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}
is uniformly bounded in
L^{2}(\Omega)
:

\|(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}},

where
C
is a constant independent of
\Delta t
and
h
. Moreover,

\|\Delta_{h}(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}u\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{2}}.

Proof.

Since
-\Delta_{h}
is positive definite with spectral bound
\sigma(-\Delta_{h})\leq Ch^{-2}
, we have

\|(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}\|=\sup_{\|u\|=1}\frac{1}{\|(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})u\|}\leq\frac{1}{1+\Delta t\cdot\inf\sigma(-\Delta_{h})}\leq C.

The boundedness of
\Delta_{h}(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}
follows from
\|\Delta_{h}(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}\|=\|-(I-\Delta t\Delta_{h})^{-1}(-\Delta_{h})\|\leq C
.
∎

5
Conclusions and discussions

In this paper, we propose a structure preserving method with the first order accuracy in time and the second order accuracy in space. Such a method use a Crank-Nicolson’s implicit method with the Gauss-Seidel iteration. Such a method preserves the norm constraint. Such a method is constructed only based on the equation, not using a projection step. Implicit Crank-Nicolson’s method involves a nonlinear system, however, our proposed method is linear and stable. In the future work, we will analyze the stability of such a method and apply for micromagnetics simulations. We will modify such a method to be a finite element methods for the space discretization. The proposed method is stable, length preserving, accurate. Meanwhile, the convergence analysis and stability analysis for the proposed method along with the normalizing step can be proved. The implicit Crank-Nicolson’s method has great theoretical value, such as stability, norm preserving, however, our approach is linear and expected to have some of the good properties of the implicit Crank-Nicolson’s method.
