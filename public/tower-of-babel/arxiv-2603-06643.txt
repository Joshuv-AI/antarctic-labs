On some signatures of Lie-Hamilton System in Quantum Hamilton Jacobi Equation
Arindam Chakraborty

Abstract
The general forms of Quantum Hamilton Jacobi Equation for a particle of constant mass, position dependent effective mass and non-Hermitian Effective mass Swanson model have been considered. It has been found that the said equations can be recast in the form of Cayley-Klein Riccati equations which admit a Lie-Hamilton structure. The possible expressions of Lie symmetry and Lie Integral have also been considered.

: Lie-Hamilton, Quantum Hamilton-Jacobi, non-Hermitian, Lie symmetry, Lie integral, Riccati Equation, symplectic, Poisson structure.

Quantum Hamilton Jacobi (QHJ) Equation and Cayley-Klein Riccati (CKR) System

Case-I : Constant Mass

In conventional quantum mechanics of a particle of constant mass moving in a potential
V(x)
, the spectral problem is given by the time-independent Schrödinger equation

\displaystyle H\Psi=E\Psi\>\>{\rm{or}}\>\>(T+V(x))\Psi=E\Psi

\displaystyle-\frac{\hbar^{2}}{2m}\frac{d^{2}\Psi(x)}{dx^{2}}+V(x)\Psi(x)=E\Psi(x)

(1)

where
T
is the kinetic energy operator and
E
being the eigenvalue. In Quantum Hamilton-Jacobi (QHJ) framework the so called
quantum action

S\dot{=}S(x,E)

[
1

\Psi=e^{iS(x,E)/\hbar},\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>S(x,E)=-i\hbar\ln\Psi,\>\>\>\>\>\>\>\>i=\sqrt{-1}

Therefore in terms of

2

\frac{1}{2m}\left(\frac{dS}{dx}\right)^{2}-\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\frac{d^{2}S}{dx^{2}}+V(x)-E=0

(3)

It is to be noted that as
\hbar\rightarrow 0

3

[
35
]
. Equation-

Quantum Hamilton Jacobi (QHJ
) Equation viewed as the quantum deformation of the classical Hamilton Jacobi Equation. The quantity

\wp(x,E)=\frac{dS}{dx}=\frac{-i\hbar}{\Psi}\frac{d\Psi}{dx}

which is a complex function for obvious reason and in view of equation-

\frac{\wp^{2}}{2m}-\frac{i\hbar}{2m}\wp^{\prime}+V(x)-E=0.

(5)

A single ’prime’ over any variable implies first order derivative with respect to
x

In conventional QHJ method QMF is considered a complex function and expanded in Laurrent series. The residue at a moving pole is substituted in the action integral to achieve energy quantization. The detail calculations are given in
[
1

In the present discussion instead of quantization we investigate an underlying Lie Hamiltonian structure corresponding to a system involving a single particle of constant mass
m
. Inorder to make things amenable to CKR equation we employ the following transformation

\displaystyle p(x)=\frac{\alpha(x)\wp(x)+\beta(x)}{\gamma(x)\wp(x)+\delta(x)}\>\>\>{\rm{with}}\>\;\>\alpha\delta-\beta\gamma\neq 0

(6)

and choose

. Here,

-ip^{\prime}-a_{1}(x)+ia_{2}(x)p+a_{3}(x)p^{2}=0

(7)

where,

\displaystyle a_{1}(x)=W(\sigma,\alpha)(\alpha\delta)^{-1}-\alpha\delta^{-1}f(x)+\sigma^{2}(\alpha\delta)^{-1}

\displaystyle a_{2}(x)=-(2\sigma\alpha^{-1}+W(\delta,\alpha)(\delta\alpha)^{-1})

\displaystyle a_{3}(x)=1/\alpha(x)

\displaystyle f(x)=2m(V(x)-E)

\displaystyle p_{1}^{\prime}=a_{2}(x)p_{1}+2a_{3}(x)p_{1}p_{2}

\displaystyle p_{2}^{\prime}=a_{1}(x)+a_{2}(x)p_{2}+a_{3}(x)(p_{2}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})

3
Lie-Hamilton System and QHJ equations

The idea of Lie system is closely related to the existence of so called superposition rule for a system of first order ordinary differential equations. Let
\mathcal{M}
be a real manifold with
{\rm{dim}}\>\>\mathcal{M}=n
and a local coordinate system
\{p_{1}\cdots p_{n}\}
.

Definition 1

A system of first order ordinary differential equations on a manifold
\mathcal{M}
of the form

\displaystyle\frac{dp_{i}}{dx}=X_{i}(x,\mathbf{p}),\>\>\>\>\>i=1\cdots n

(24)

is said to admit a
global superposition principle
if there exists an
x
-independent map
\Omega:\mathcal{M}^{m}\times\mathcal{M}\rightarrow\mathcal{M}
such that

\displaystyle p(x)=\Omega(p_{1}(x)\cdots p_{m}(x);\alpha)

(25)

where,
p_{1}(x)\cdots p_{m}(x)
is a generic family of particular solutions to
X
and
\alpha=\alpha_{1}\cdots,\alpha_{n}\in N
are constants that appear due to initial conditions of
X
.

The so called
Lie-Scheiffer Theorem
ensures that such superposition principle exists iff

X(x,p)=\sum_{\mu=1}^{r}a_{\mu}(x)\chi_{\mu}(p)

(26)

where, the vector fields
\{\chi_{\mu}:\mu=1,\cdots,r\}
spanning an
r
-dimensional Lie algebra called
Vessiot-Guldberg Lie Algebra (VGLA)
of
X
denoted by
V^{X}
.

Definition 2

A
Lie System
is a system of equations mentioned in equation-
24
along with the condition-
26
. A Lie system isomorphic to a Lie algebra
\mathfrak{g}
is called
\mathfrak{g}
-Lie System.

In this section we demonstrate an
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
-Lie system is possible to be constructed out of equation-
2
and equation-
2
.
Let us define the following vector fields

\chi_{1}=\partial_{p_{2}},\>\>\>\>\chi_{2}=p_{1}\partial_{p_{1}}+p_{2}\partial_{p_{2}}\>\>\>{\rm{and}}\>\>\>\chi_{3}=2p_{1}p_{2}\partial_{p_{1}}+(p_{2}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})\partial_{p_{2}}

(27)

as generators of where,
\partial_{p_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial p_{j}}
. It is easy to verify the following commutation relations :

[\chi_{1},\chi_{2}]=\chi_{1},\>\>\>[\chi_{2},\chi_{3}]=\chi_{3}\>\>\>{\rm{and}}\>\>[\chi_{1},\chi_{3}]=2\chi_{2}

(28)

The above algebra is isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})
for obvious reason. It is to be mentioned that Lie-Scheffers theorem relates the dimension of the VGLA with the number of particular solutions of the superposition rule and the
dimension of the manifold in which the Lie system is defined.

3.1
Poisson Structure and Lie-Hamilton System

Definition 3

A pair
(\mathcal{M},\omega)
is called a
symplectic manifold
if
\omega
is a non-degenerate closed
2
-form defined over the manifold
\mathcal{M}

Proposition 1

The vector fields
\{\chi_{i}:i=1,2,3\}
called
symplectic vector fields
admit a family of
Hamiltonian functions

\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{3}\}=\{-p_{1}^{-1},-p_{2}p_{1}^{-1},-(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})p_{1}^{-1}\}
relative to the symplectic form
\omega=p_{1}^{-2}dp_{2}\wedge dp_{1}
. The Poisson bracket
\{\mathcal{H}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{j}\}_{\omega}=\omega(\chi_{i},\chi_{j})
defines a
Lie-Hamilton Algebra
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})

Proof :

In order to construct the relevant Hamiltonian functions
\{\mathcal{H}_{j}:j=1,2,3\}
we consider the
2
-form
\omega=F(p_{1},p_{2})dp_{2}\wedge dp_{1}
. For a VGLA the local Hamiltonians relative to
\omega
, demands the conditions that the Lie derivatives
\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{i}}\omega=0\>\>\forall\>\>i
. Using the homotopy relation
[
19

\mathcal{L}_{\chi_{i}}\omega=d\iota_{\chi_{i}}\omega+\iota_{\chi_{i}}d\omega\>\>{\rm{with}}\>\>\iota_{\chi_{i}}(\theta\wedge\alpha)=(\iota_{\chi_{i}}\theta)\wedge\alpha+(-1)^{\nu}\theta\wedge(\iota_{\chi_{i}}\alpha)

(29)

where,

\theta
and
\iota_{\chi_{i}}
is the so called contraction and applying the condition

we find

\displaystyle\partial_{p_{2}}F=0,\>\>p_{1}\partial_{p_{1}}F+p_{2}\partial_{p_{2}}F+2F=0\>\>{\rm{and}}\>

\displaystyle 2p_{1}p_{2}\partial_{p_{1}}F+(p_{2}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})\partial_{p_{2}}F+4p_{2}F=0

The first two equations give

and hence
\omega=p_{1}^{-2}dp_{2}\wedge dp_{1}
which is a closed non-degenerate
2

\mathbb{R}^{2}_{p_{1}\neq 0}=\{p_{1},p_{2}\}\in\mathbb{R}^{2}(p_{1}\neq 0)

The
Hamiltonian functions

\{\mathcal{H}_{i}\in C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}):i=1,2,3\}

being the function ring, are given by the relations
\{d\mathcal{H}_{i}=\iota_{\chi_{i}}\omega:i=1,2,3\}

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}_{1}=-p_{1}^{-1},\>\>\mathcal{H}_{2}=-p_{2}p_{1}^{-1},\>\>\>\mathcal{H}_{3}=-(p_{1}^{2}+p_{2}^{2})p_{1}^{-1}

(31)

The Poisson Bracket relative to
\omega
as

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{i},\mathcal{H}_{j}\}_{\omega}=\omega(\chi_{i},\chi_{j})=p_{1}^{-2}dp_{2}\wedge dp_{1}(\chi_{i},\chi_{j})

\displaystyle=p_{1}^{-2}\{dp_{2}(\chi_{i})dp_{1}(\chi_{j})-dp_{1}(\chi_{i})dp_{2}(\chi_{j})\}

(32)

This gives the following relations of
Lie Hamilton algebra

\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{3}:\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega}\}
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{sl}(2,\mathbb{R})

\displaystyle\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{2}\}_{\omega}=-\mathcal{H}_{1},\>\>\{\mathcal{H}_{2},\mathcal{H}_{3}\}_{\omega}=-\mathcal{H}_{3},\>\>\{\mathcal{H}_{1},\mathcal{H}_{3}\}_{\omega}=-2\mathcal{H}_{2}\>\>\>\square

Remark 2

It is to be mentioned that the bracket

(C^{\infty}(\mathcal{M}),\cdot,\{\cdot,\cdot\}_{\omega})
as a natural consequence of the existence of a symplectic manifold (here

For example, in the present example

Proposition 2

Let

Definition 7

Conclusion

The above discussion deals with the existence of a
Lie-Hamilton System
for
Quantum Hamilton Jacobi (QHJ)
equation in one dimension in various circumstances like conventional potential problem to variable mass Schrödinger problem and even to the case of non-Hermitian Swanson Hamiltonian. The prime objectives of quantum mechanics like finding out eigenvalues or eigen states have not been envisaged here, rather we assume their existence a priori for a bound state problem. We, in turn, explore the QHJ equation from the perspective of differential equation (here
Cayley-Klein Riccati equation
)to glean out signatures of Lie-Hamilton System, signatures which are relevant for geometric understanding of the problem. The differential geometric features like symplectic form, Poisson bi-vector, Lie derivative, Lie Integral, Lie symmetry have been our concern. It has come out at the end that sometimes, if not most of the cases differential geometric features seem to be more general than the precise classification of the physical problem to be supposedly held categories like Classical or Quantum Mechanical. This view is in consonance with various efforts ever made to view Classical Mechanics as the limiting case of Quantum Formalisms. Similar consideration for Lie-Dirac system is under preparation. Extension of the method employed here to higher dimension, for general non-Hermitian potential and for particle with spin may be suggested as some interesting areas of further investigation.
