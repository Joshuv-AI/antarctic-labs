Ultracontractivity of Heat semigroups in L 2 ​ ( Ω ) \mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right) with non-local Robin boundary conditions using Nash’s inequality
Christoph Schwerdt

Abstract
We study heat equations ∂ u ∂ t − div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 \frac{\partial u}{\partial t}-\operatorname{div}\left(A\nabla u\right)=0 on bounded Lipschitz domains Ω \Omega in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} for d > 2 d>2 , where − div ( A ∇ ⋅ ) -\operatorname{div}\left(A\nabla\cdot\right) is a second-order uniformly elliptic operator with generalised Robin boundary conditions. These boundary conditions are formally given by ν ⋅ A ∇ u + B u = 0 \nu\cdot A\nabla u+Bu=0 where ν \nu is the outer unit normal on ∂ Ω \partial\Omega and B ∈ ℒ ⁡ ( L 2 ​ ( ∂ Ω ) ) B\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\partial\Omega\right)\right) is a general operator which is allowed to destroy the positivity preserving property of the solution semigroup. Ultracontractivity of the solution semigroup is shown by using Nash’s inequality on the Sobolev space H 1 ​ ( Ω ) H^{1}(\Omega) .

(\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}})_{t\geq 0}
be the
associated semigroup to the auxiliary form
\tilde{a}(B)^{\ast}
.
Furthermore let
u\in D(\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast})
and
t>0
be arbitrary but fixed. We define

v(t)\ =\ \mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right).

Remember that
D(\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast})
is a dense subset in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
since
the operator
\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}
is densely defined as a generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup in

\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
. Also
v(t)
and
u
are contained
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
since
\Omega
is bounded. Hence

\left\|v(t)\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\ \leq\ \mathrm{e}^{-t\alpha}\ \left\|\mathrm{e}^{-tL(B)^{\ast}}\right\|_{1}\ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}\leq\ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}

is implied by Corollary
2.12
where we used

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}\ =\ \mathrm{e}^{-t\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-tL(B)^{\ast}}
.
We start the argumentation with

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle=2\Re\langle v^{\prime}(t),v(t)\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}=-2\Re\langle\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}v(t),v(t)\rangle_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}

\displaystyle=-2\Re\tilde{a}(B)^{\ast}(v(t),v(t))\leq-2\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}

by (
13
). Now we use Nash’s inequality (
15
) to conclude to

\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\ \leq\ -2\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}^{2}\ \leq\ -\frac{2}{C_{d}}\frac{\|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}}{\|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{d}}}.

By using the chain rule we argue that

\displaystyle\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\left(\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)^{-\frac{2}{d}}\

\displaystyle\geq\ \left(-\frac{2}{d}\right)\left(\|v(t)\|^{2}_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\right)^{-\frac{2}{d}-1}\left(-\frac{2}{C_{d}}\right)\frac{\|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}}{\|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{d}}}

\displaystyle=\ \frac{4}{dC_{d}}\ \|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\ \geq\ \frac{4}{dC_{d}}\ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}

is implied. Hence we conclude to

\displaystyle\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\

\displaystyle=\ \|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\ =\ \int_{0}^{t}\frac{\partial}{\partial t}\ \|v(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}\,\mathrm{d}t+\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}

\displaystyle\geq\ \frac{4}{dC_{d}}\ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}^{-\frac{4}{d}}t

and end up with

\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\ \leq\ \left(\frac{dC_{d}}{4}\right)^{d/4}\ t^{-d/4}\left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}

(14)

for any function
u\in D(\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast})
.

iii)

Note that
D(\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast})
is a dense subset in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
due to

\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}
being a generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
.
Furthermore
\Omega
is bounded and therefore the domain
D(\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast})
is even a dense subset
in
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
with respect to
\|\cdot\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}
.
Finally we argue that there exists an unique extension of
\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{B}^{\ast}}
to a continuous operator from

\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
to
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
satisfying

\exists C>0\ \forall u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)\ :\ \|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)}\ \leq\ Ct^{-d/4}\ \left\|u\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)}.

∎

4.2
A duality argumenation

From Lemma
4.1
we conclude that there exists an adjoint operator

T(t)\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right),\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)\right)
to

\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right),\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)\right)

satisfying

i)

\langle\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}u,v\rangle=\langle u,T(t)v\rangle

for every
u\in\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
and
v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)

ii)

\|T(t)\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}=\|\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}\to\mathrm{L}^{2}}\leq Ct^{-d/4}
.

On the other hand
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}

is the adjoint operator of
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)}
in
\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
by Lemma

A.4
. Furthermore

\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
is a subset of
\mathrm{L}^{1}\left(\Omega\right)
since
\Omega
is bounded
and therefore we conclude

\langle\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)}u,v\rangle=\langle u,\left(\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)}\right)^{\ast}v\rangle=\langle u,\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)^{\ast}}v\rangle=\langle T(t)u,v\rangle

is true for every
u,v\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
.
Hence
\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}(B)}u=T(t)u
is implied
for every
u\in\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right)
. Finally we end up with

\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{B}}=\mathrm{e}^{t\alpha}\mathrm{e}^{-t\tilde{L}_{B}}\in\mathcal{L}\left(\mathrm{L}^{2}\left(\Omega\right),\mathrm{L}^{\infty}\left(\Omega\right)\right).

In particular we end up with

\left\|\mathrm{e}^{-tL_{B}}\right\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}\to\mathrm{L}^{\infty}}\leq C\mathrm{e}^{t\alpha}t^{-\frac{d}{4}}

for any
t>0
which finally proves Theorem
3.1
.

Appendix A
Useful results on forms and
C_{0}
-semigroups

Let us collect some known facts from
[
2
]
as a guideline to our own argumentation. Let
H
be
a Hilbert space over
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{C}
or
\mathbb{R}
and
a\colon D(a)\times D(a)\to\mathbb{K}
be a sesquilinear form.

Definition A.1
.

i.)

We call
a
densely defined if
D(a)
is dense in
H
.

ii.)

We call
a
accretive if
\Re a(u,u)\geq 0
holds for every
u\in D(a)
.

iii.)

We call
a
continuous if
a
is accretive and there exists a constant
M\geq 0
such that

\left|a(u,v)\right|\leq M\|u\|_{a}\|v\|_{a}

holds for every
u,v\in D(a)
where
\|u\|_{a}=\left(\Re a(u,u)+\|u\|\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}
.

iv.)

We call
a
a closed form if
\left(D(a),\|\cdot\|_{a}\right)
is a complete space.

v.)

The adjoint form
a^{\ast}
of
a
is defined by

a^{\ast}(u,v)=\overline{a(v,u)}

for
u,v\in D(a^{\ast})=D(a)
.

Now, let the form
a\colon D(a)\times D(a)\to\mathbb{K}
be densely defined, accretive, coninuous and closed.
Then we define a subspace

D(A)=\left\{u\in D(a)\ |\ \exists v\in H:a(u,\varphi)=\langle v,\varphi\rangle\ \text{ holds for every }\varphi\in D(a)\right\}

as the domain of an unbounded operator
A\colon D(A)\subset H\to H
defined by the equation

a(u,\varphi)=\langle Au,\varphi\rangle

for
u\in D(A)
and every
\varphi\in D(a)
. We call
A
the associated operator to the form
a
.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
A
be the associated operator to the form
a
. Then
D(A)
is dense in
H
and for every
\lambda>0
the operator

\lambda+A
is invertible such that

\left\|\lambda\left(\lambda+A\right)^{-1}u\right\|\leq\|u\|

holds for every
u\in H
. Hence
-A
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tA}\right)_{t\geq 0}

of contractions in
H
.

Definition A.3
.

Let
A\colon D(A)\subset H\to H
be a densely defined operator in
H
. Then the adjoint operator
A^{\ast}
to
A
is defined
by

D(A^{\ast})=\left\{u\in H\ |\ \exists v\in H:\langle A\varphi,u\rangle=\langle\varphi,v\rangle\ \text{ for every }\varphi\in D(A)\right\}

and
\langle A\varphi,u\rangle=\langle\varphi,A^{\ast}u\rangle
for
u\in D(A^{\ast})
and every
\varphi\in D(A)
.

Lemma A.4
.

The adjoint operator
A^{\ast}
of
A
is the associated operator to the adjoint form
a^{\ast}
of
a
. Furthermore

-A^{\ast}
is the generator of a
C_{0}
-semigroup
\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tA^{\ast}}\right)_{t\geq 0}
in
H
such that

\mathrm{e}^{-tA^{\ast}}=\left(\mathrm{e}^{-tA}\right)^{\ast}

holds for every time
t\geq 0
.

Appendix B
Nash’s inequality on
H^{1}(\Omega)

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
for
d>2
be a bounded Lipschitz domain. We will present the details in the argumentation of Nash’s inequality which reads

\exists C_{d}>0\ \forall u\in H^{1}(\Omega)\ :\ \|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}\leq C_{d}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{d}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}^{2}

(15)

where we keep in mind that
H^{1}(\Omega)\subset\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)
is also contained in

\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)
since
\Omega
is bounded. We start to prove (
15
) for

u\in C^{1}(\Omega)
and use Hölder inequalities to conclude to

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x=\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\frac{4}{d+2}}|u|^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d-2}{d+2}}.

Due to
\emptyset\neq\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
being a Lipschitz domain, we use a generalization of the
Sobolev embedding theorem to infer that

\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|^{\frac{2d}{d-2}}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d-2}{2d}}\leq C_{d}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

is true where
C_{d}>0
is a constant independent of
u
. We conclude to

\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\leq C_{d}^{\frac{2d}{d+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|u|\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{4}{d+2}}\left(\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}\,\mathrm{d}x\right)^{\frac{d}{d+2}}

which implies

\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)}^{2+\frac{4}{d}}\leq\tilde{C}_{d}\|u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{1}(\Omega)}^{\frac{4}{d}}\|\nabla u\|_{\mathrm{L}^{2}(\Omega)^{d}}^{2}

for
\tilde{C}_{d}=C_{d}^{2}>0
. Due to a density argumentation we have shown (
15
) on
H^{1}(\Omega)
.
