Investigating the Interplay between Contextual and Parametric Chain-of-Thought Faithfulness under Optimization
Jingyi Sun Qianli Wang Pepa Atanasova Nils Feldhus Isabelle Augenstein

Abstract
Chain-of-Thought (CoT) faithfulness, i.e., whether CoTs genuinely reflect large language models’ (LLM) underlying behavior, is typically evaluated under two disjoint paradigms: contextual faithfulness , measured by perturbing the input or CoT trace, and parametric faithfulness , assessed by intervening on a model’s parametric knowledge. Yet prior work compares them only descriptively. We fill this gap by proposing FaithMATE , a unified preference-alignment interface for optimizing models towards either faithfulness paradigm. 1 1 1 The code will be released upon publication. It enables us to investigate the interplay between the two paradigms, examining whether and to what extent faithfulness gains generalize within and across paradigms. Across three models, two datasets, and six faithfulness metrics, we find that the two paradigms are positively coupled, yet asymmetric: optimizing towards parametric faithfulness yields consistent gains across both paradigms, whereas the contextual counterpart delivers more variable gains. Within the contextual paradigm, faithfulness gains on one metric do not consistently transfer to others, implying that existing contextual metrics capture disjoint facets of faithfulness and exposing inherent trade-offs. These findings imply that CoT faithfulness is not a monolithic objective and therefore requires multifaceted optimization and evaluation.

Existing work evaluates CoT faithfulness primarily through two paradigms.
Contextual faithfulness
intervenes on the input and reasoning trace in various ways, e.g., by inserting an error
13

25
;
5

Parametric faithfulness
, in contrast, intervenes on a model’s parameters rather than its context: it deems a CoT step faithful if unlearning the knowledge underlying that step changes the answer
(
26
)
. While this perspective targets model internals directly, it is limited to the knowledge explicitly verbalized by the CoT, neglecting information that shapes CoT generation but remains unexpressed.

Although the two paradigms have largely been studied in isolation,
they can yield divergent faithfulness conclusions for the same CoTs
31
, raising the question of whether they capture a shared notion of faithfulness or reward different properties of CoTs. As methods for improving CoT faithfulness proliferate, observational comparisons on fixed CoTs cannot reveal what each faithfulness paradigm rewards under optimization or whether and to what extent optimizing one transfers to the other.

To this end, we introduce
FaithMATE
(

It constructs faithfulness-targeted preference pairs, allowing models to be optimized towards either contextual or parametric faithfulness (Figure

).
This unified optimization interface enables an optimization-based investigation of the interplay between the two paradigms, while also allowing us to compare what each rewards under optimization. Experiments on three models, two datasets, and six faithfulness metrics reveal the following findings.

First, across faithfulness paradigms, contextual and parametric faithfulness exhibit a positively coupled, yet asymmetric relationship (§
5.1
): optimizing for parametric faithfulness delivers more consistent improvements across both paradigms, while contextual optimization, although occasionally larger in magnitude, is less consistent.
This positions parametric faithfulness as the more reliable target for broad generalization.

Second, within the contextual faithfulness paradigm, faithfulness gains from optimizing one contextual metric do not consistently transfer to others, e.g., between Adding Mistake and Paraphrasing (§
5.2
). This suggests that contextual faithfulness metrics are better understood as a family of related but distinct properties, rather than interchangeable measurements of a single latent quantity.
Thus, we call attention to potential tensions among contextual faithfulness metrics.

Additional experiments show that task-arithmetic merging of models, each optimized for a specific faithfulness target, can partially mitigate this inconsistency and amplify the faithfulness improvements.

Third, we qualitatively characterize what different metrics actually reward under optimization (§
5.4
). We find that contextual metric-shared gains are typically driven by improved alignment between reasoning and final answer, whereas contextual metric-specific gains are mostly prompted by the mitigation of post-hoc rationalization

Corruption-based.

\hat{z}_{i}
and
\hat{z}_{i}^{{}^{\prime}}
, respectively. The faithfulness score is then calculated as
(\hat{z}_{i}-\hat{z}_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})\in[-1,1]
.
6
6

6

For Paraphrasing, we use

-(\hat{z}_{i}-\hat{z}_{i}^{{}^{\prime}})
.
See App.
D.1

Larger positive margins indicate a bigger drop in the original answer’s probability after corruption, and, thus, a more faithful CoT.

Attribution-based.

The main idea of attribution-based contextual faithfulness metrics is that a truly consistent CoT should depend on the same key input tokens that drive the prediction
17
;
1
. A representative instantiation of this idea is CC-SHAP
17
7
7

7

Due to exceptional high computational cost, we only employ CC-SHAP as

, which uses Shapley values to measure each input token’s contribution to the predicted answer and to the generated CoT separately, and then computes the cosine similarity between the two contribution distributions. This assesses how well the importance of inputs for the answer aligns with their importance in the CoT,

4.3.2
Parametric Faithfulness

In contrast, parametric faithfulness evaluates CoT faithfulness through interventions on models’ parametric knowledge.
Specifically, given an input
x
, the target CoT
c
is first decomposed into
T
reasoning steps
c=(s_{1},...,s_{T})
.
\mathrm{ff}_{\mathrm{hard}}
provides a binary answer as to whether a CoT is faithful by measuring whether unlearning the knowledge used by models in any CoT step causes models to change their output prediction
26
8
8

8

Implementation details are provided in Appendix
D.2
.
:

\displaystyle\mathrm{ff}_{\mathrm{hard}}=\mathbb{I}[\exists\,s_{i}\text{ such that }y\neq y^{(i)*}]

(5)

where
s_{i}
is the
i

y^{(i)*}

To enable more fine-grained evaluation, we further propose

\displaystyle\mathrm{ff}_{\mathrm{cont}}=\frac{\sum_{i=1}^{T}\mathbb{I}[s_{i}\text{ such that }y\neq y^{(i)*}]}{T}

\mathrm{ff}_{\mathrm{cont}}

Does Model Merging Help?

Motivation and Setup.

Optimizing towards a single contextual faithfulness metric yields gains that do not consistently transfer across other contextual metrics (§
5.2

complementary

partial

Model merging is well-suited to this goal: it can combine the parameters of multiple specialized models into a single model without additional training, potentially integrating their respective advantages
23
;
21

Conclusion

We presented
FaithMATE
, a unified framework for CoT faithfulness optimization that enables a controlled investigation of the connection between contextual and parametric faithfulness. Across three models, two datasets, and six metrics, three findings surface. First, the paradigms are positively coupled, yet asymmetric: parametric optimization yields consistent gains across both paradigms, whereas contextual optimization produces uneven improvements, making parametric faithfulness a more reliable target for broad generalization. Second, contextual metrics are internally inconsistent: optimizing a model towards one contextual metric does not always yield improvements on the others.
Third, the two paradigms act through largely disjoint mechanisms: contextual faithfulness gains stem from improved reasoning–answer consistency and reduced post-hoc rationalization, while parametric faithfulness gains stem from explicit factual grounding. We hope
FaithMATE
serves as a foundation for developing faithfulness objectives that are both parametrically grounded and contextually robust, and enables more precisely scoped claims about individual faithfulness metrics.

Limitations

We acknowledge several limitations, stated as follows.

Lack of User Study.

Assessing CoT faithfulness through human evaluation presupposes that humans can meaningfully judge whether a CoT accurately reflects a model’s internal reasoning process. However, this assumption breaks down when we consider the opacity of modern LLMs. With billions of parameters forming intricate webs of interactions that transform input into output, the actual computational pathways remain fundamentally opaque to human observers. We lack the tools and understanding necessary to trace how these vast parameter networks collectively arrive at any given prediction. Consequently, human evaluation of CoT faithfulness, while intuitively appealing, may be methodologically flawed given our current limited insight into LLM internals, consistent with
10
;
17
.

Online vs. Offline Learning.

Given the high inference time required for almost all faithfulness metrics, as indicated in Section
3.4
, only offline learning is feasible in our setting. Consequently, online-learning approaches such as GRPO
20
are not applicable. Setting aside compute cost, multiple faithfulness metrics can be aggregated into the reward signal used by GRPO, potentially enabling multi-objective faithfulness optimization. Though worth exploring, it is currently out of the scope of this paper.

Scope of Experiments.

Although we conducted experiments with two datasets, three models, and six faithfulness metrics, extending the scope of these experiments, i.e., including additional datasets and models, is non-trivial, given the large number of possible configurations and exceptionally high inference time required to assess CoT faithfulness using the selected faithfulness metrics (§
4.3
). Furthermore, our experimental setup is limited to English language Multiple-Choice Question Answering (MCQA) tasks. Due to the inherent limitations of parametric faithfulness, code and math reasoning is conceptually challenging
26
, while current faithfulness metrics are only applicable to closed-ended reasoning. Therefore, consistent with
26
;
30
, we focus on two widely used MCQA datasets in our experiments.

Metrics as Proxies for Faithfulness.

The metrics we employ are proxies for faithfulness rather than direct measures of it. In the absence of a ground truth for faithfulness, no degree of agreement among metrics can establish their validity for measuring CoT faithfulness. This challenge is partly mechanistic: a model’s answer may depend on latent or unspoken factors that are not fully expressed in the CoT, and it remains unclear whether LLMs have a single explicit reasoning process that any textual trace could recover. The metrics studied here are therefore best viewed as proxies, each capturing useful but partial facets of faithfulness.
FaithMATE
contributes by making the relationships among these proxies explicit, especially regarding where gains transfer, and why multi-metric evaluation is necessary. Stronger validation may require mechanistic-interpretability-based ground truth or counterfactual metrics that probe decision-relevant but unspoken factors; absent such ground truth, CoT faithfulness may be better treated as a family of related but distinct properties rather than as one universal score.

For the parametric faithfulness metric, we follow

and implement FUR with NPO+KL unlearning. We segment each generated CoT into reasoning steps, unlearn one step at a time with KL regularization on a retain set sampled from other CoT steps in the same dataset, and then measure whether the model’s answer changes.

=

F.3
Summary

This qualitative case study suggests different surface signatures for contextual and parametric faithfulness improvements.
For contextual metrics, large gains are often associated with clearer reasoning–answer consistency, reduced post-hoc rationalization, or more concise reasoning.
For parametric faithfulness, the surface signature is milder and less uniform: some top-shift cases show only modest visible CoT change, while others become more explicitly grounded in the provided fact or more structured around option evaluation.
Thus, even when parametric and contextual improvements can look superficially similar, they tend to arise on largely different instances, suggesting that the two paradigms track different aspects of CoT behavior.
This interpretation also resonates with prior work showing that parametric faithfulness is only weakly aligned with human judgments of supportive-ness, indicating that internal reliance on reasoning steps and human-perceived plausibility are distinct properties
(

Appendix G
Why Parametric
\Leftrightarrow
Contextual?

As discussed in Section
5.1
, when parametric faithfulness is optimized, contextual faithfulness can be enhanced, and vice versa. To better understand the bidirectional cross-paradigm transfer, we analyze the training dataset and distinguish the datasets used for contextual and parametric faithfulness optimization:
\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}^{f_{j}}=\{(x,y_{\text{cont}})_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
and
\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}=\{(x,y_{\text{para}})_{i}\}_{i=1}^{N}
, where
f_{j}\in\mathcal{F}_{\text{train}}
is one of the corruption-based contextual metrics that we aim to optimize (§
4.3.1
).

Contextual
\Rightarrow
Parametric.

For each CoT from
\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}^{f}
, we select
y_{\text{cont}}
as the CoT achieving the highest faithfulness score within the sampled CoT set
\mathcal{C}
, as defined in Eq. (
3
). We further compute the overlap ratio
r_{cp}
, defined as the proportion of CoTs in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}
that also appear in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}
:

\displaystyle r_{cp}=\frac{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}\cap\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}|}{|\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}|}

(12)

Figures
3
and
4
show that
r_{cp}
is 15-25% across four contextual faithfulness metrics, indicating that the two faithfulness optimization objectives are not in conflict. Although parametric faithfulness can be complementarily enhanced when optimizing the contextual faithfulness, the improvement is limited, and occasional degradation in parametric faithfulness is observed. This phenomenon can be attributed to the dominance of contextually
faithful
but parametrically
unfaithful
CoTs in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}
. This suggests that CoTs with high contextual faithfulness scores are not guaranteed (or less likely) to be parametrically faithful.

Parametric
\Rightarrow
Contextual

In comparison, Figures
3
and
4
demonstrate that the average contextual faithfulness scores of CoTs in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}
are above 0, while close to 0 for Paraphrasing, and in most cases they show higher or comparable values than that of CoTs in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{cont}}
. Moreover, Figures
3
and
4
illustrate that on average 95% of CoTs from
\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}
are contextually faithful. Taken together, most CoTs in
\mathcal{D}_{\text{para}}
are
parametrically
and
contextually
faithful at the same time. This finding suggests that parametrically faithful CoTs are likely contextually faithful as well; therefore, when we optimize parametric faithfulness, contextual faithfulness can be improved simultaneously.

H.2
Similarity Analysis

To better understand the relationships among the task vectors produced by different faithfulness objectives under
FaithMATE
, we perform a similarity study. Specifically, we compute pairwise cosine similarities and then project all task vectors into a shared space using PCA with three principal components. As shown in Figure
5
, the task vectors for Early Answering and Filler Token exhibit moderate similarity and lie along a broadly aligned direction in the 3D principal component space, consistent with their strong synergy when merged. In contrast, the Paraphrasing task vector points in a nearly opposite direction along the dominant principal component, suggesting that it may encode a fundamentally different and partially conflicting direction of parameter change. This directional opposition helps explain why incorporating the Paraphrasing vector into the merged models tends to cancel out gains from other faithfulness objectives rather than composing them additively.
