Boundary null-controllability for the beam equation with classical structural damping.
Sergei Avdonin, Julian Edward

Abstract
Let Δ \Delta be the Dirichlet Laplacian on the interval ( 0 , π ) (0,\pi) , and let T > 0 T>0 . We prove a well-posedness results for the structurally damped beam equation u t ​ t + Δ 2 ​ u − ρ ​ Δ ​ u t = 0 , x ∈ ( 0 , π ) , t > 0 u_{tt}+\Delta^{2}u-\rho\Delta u_{t}=0,x\in(0,\pi),t>0 with various boundary conditions including u ⁡ ( 0 , t ) = u x ​ x ​ ( 0 , t ) = 0 ; u ⁡ ( π , t ) = f ⁡ ( t ) , u x ​ x ​ ( π , t ) = 0 , u(0,t)=u_{xx}(0,t)=0;u(\pi,t)=f(t),u_{xx}(\pi,t)=0, and f ∈ H 0 2 ​ ( 0 , T ) f\in H_{0}^{2}(0,T) and appropriate initial conditions. Introduction

Let
\Delta
be the Laplacian:
\Delta=\partial_{x}^{2}
on the interval
(0,\pi)
.
We will denote the Dirichlet Laplacian by
A_{D}=-\Delta

with operator domain either
H^{2}(0,\pi)\cap H^{1}_{0}(0,\pi)
,
and the Neumann Laplacian by
A_{N}=-\Delta
with operator domain

\{u\in H^{2}(0,\pi):u^{\prime}(0)=u^{\prime}(\pi)=0\}.

It is well known that

are self-adjoint with non-negative spectra. In what follows, we will denote by

or
A_{N}
when there is no need for distinction. Denote by

the operator domain of

We will study boundary control problems for the equation

u_{tt}+A_{j}^{2}u+\rho A_{j}u_{t}=0,\ x\in(0,\pi),\ t>0,\ j=\mbox{D or N.}

(1.1)

with positive constant

Throughout this paper, controllability will always mean
the ability of steering any initial state
(u(x,0),u_{t}(x,0))
to zero over a finite time by some appropriate
input function
f
(i.e. exact controllability to zero or null controllability).
The boundary controls will be either

\displaystyle u(0,t)=u_{xx}(0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\displaystyle u(\pi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(t),

\displaystyle u_{xx}(\pi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(1.2)

\displaystyle u_{x}(0,t)=u_{x}(\pi,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle g(t),

\displaystyle u_{xxx}(\pi,t)=u_{xxx}(0,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

(1.3)

for the Neumann Laplacian.

The term

models a specific dissipative effect, known as structural damping. To the best of our knowledge, this was introduced in
[
11
]
:
“The basic property of structural damping, which is said to be consistent with empirical studies,
is that the amplitudes of the normal modes of vibration are attenuated at rates which are proportional to the oscillation frequencies.” This model was also studied under the name “proportional
damping” (cf.
[
7
]

as “classical” structural damping, but one can also consider

for
\alpha\in(0,2]

The case

is known as “Kelvin–Voigt” damping. For a distributive control
and the damping term being

in
[
15
]
,
[
23
]
, see also
[
1
]
. The case of boundary control for

[

and
[

3
Conclusions

We considered the Euler-Bernouilli beam, for either Dirichlet or Neumann control applied at one end. To prove continuous dependance of the velocity on time, we assume the control and energy spaces are regular. For these spaces, we prove well-posedness. For small damping parameter,
\rho\leq 2
, we then prove null-controllability for Dirichlet control; for Neumann control, we must assume the energy spaces are orthogonal constants. For
\rho>2
, we apply number theoretic results to prove that
null controllability holds in some cases, but not others.

We list several possible extensions to this work. First, it should be possible to extend this study to higher dimensions. Second, one can consider the beam equation with a spectral fractional damping term,
A^{\alpha}u_{t}
, with
\alpha\in(0,1)
; this will be addressed in
[
4
]
,
[
5
]
. Third, it is natural to consider the beam equation with non-constant coefficients, but this will require different methods. Finally, the methods in this work should also apply to higher order controls such as moment control,
u_{xx}(\pi,t)=f(t)
, and shear control,
u_{xxx}(\pi,t)=f(t)
.
