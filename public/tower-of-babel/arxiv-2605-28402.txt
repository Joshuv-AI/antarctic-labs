On the Smallest Eigenvalues and Quantum Chromatic Numbers of Hamming Graphs and Generalizations
Yu Ning, Jack H. Koolen, Xiande Zhang

Abstract
The smallest eigenvalues of (distance‑ j j ) Hamming graphs with distance parameter j j at least half the length were completely determined by Brouwer et al. (2018). In the present work, we address the complementary regime, namely distances j j strictly less than half the length, and derive asymptotic lower bounds on the smallest eigenvalue of binary Hamming graphs. For certain natural generalizations, specifically Cayley graphs defined over quaternary vector spaces, we asymptotically determine the smallest eigenvalue as well. As an application, we obtain lower bounds on the quantum chromatic number of these graphs. In particular, for the aforementioned Cayley graphs over quaternary vectors, our lower bounds for the quantum chromatic number coincide with known upper bounds. Keywords: Hamming graph, Cayley graph, smallest eigenvalue, quantum chromatic number

\lambda_{\min}(H(n,2))=\begin{cases}K_{2}(n/2)=-n/2,&n\text{ even},\\
K_{2}((n+1)/2)=K_{2}((n-1)/2)=-(n-1)/2,&n\text{ odd}.\end{cases}

When

is a polynomial of degree

, and
reaches the minimum at

Also note that

In particular,

\lambda_{\min}(H(n,4))\geq K_{4}(x_{0})=K_{4}(x_{1})=-n^{2}/4+3n/4-2/3

\lambda_{\min}(H(n,4))
is close to

Our goal is to give lower bounds on

for

and even

.

3.1
Roadmap

In this subsection, we explain the approach to the lower bounds on
K_{j}(w)
for

w\in[0,n]
and even
j<n/2
. We will use some notions from
the theory of distance-regular graphs
[

,

]
.

A connected graph

with diameter
D
is called

if
there are constants

, the so-called

,
such that for all

and all vertices
u
and
v
at distance
i
,
among the neighbors of
v
, there are
c_{i}
at distance
i-1
from
u
,
a_{i}
at
distance
i
, and
b_{i}
at distance
i+1
. Then
H(n,1)
is a distance-regular
graph of diameter
n
and of intersection numbers

\text{$c_{i}=i$, $a_{i}=0$ and $b_{i}=n-i$ with $i\in[0,n]$}.

In the following, we always refer to
a_{i},b_{i}
and
c_{i}
as the above values.

Let
A_{j},j\in[1,n]
be the adjacency matrix of

H(n,j)
, and

be the identity matrix of size
2^{n}
. Then

A_{j}
satisfies the following recursion
[
25
, Page 13]

[
5
, Section 4.1]
,

A_{1}A_{j}=c_{j+1}A_{j+1}+b_{j-1}A_{j-1}.

((15))

In accordance to
Eq. (15)
, for
j\in[0,n]
, we consider
polynomials

defined by the recursion,

\begin{cases}xq_{j}(x)=c_{j+1}q_{j+1}(x)+b_{j-1}q_{j-1}(x),\\
q_{0}(x)=1,q_{1}(x)=x.\end{cases}

((16))

So
q_{j}(x)
is a polynomial of degree

satisfying
q_{j}(A_{1})=A_{j}
, for all

[

, Page 13]
[
5
, Section 4.1]
.
We note that

is related to

as in the
following lemma.

Lemma 3.1
.

For all

,

q_{j}(n-2x)=K_{j}(x)

Proof.

For

, by

and

,

Note that

for all integers

. As

and

are both polynomials of degree

and share the same value at

for all

.
∎

By

, finding the smallest

for

is

((17))

Next, we compute all coefficients of

. Before that, we need the following notation.

of integers
with

Let

With these notations, we determine the coefficients of

below.

Theorem 3.2

For any

in

\frac{(-1)^{i/2}}{j!}\sum_{I\in\mathcal{I}(i/2,j)}R(I),&i\equiv 0\pmod{2}.\end{cases}

((18))

The proof of

From now on, we assume that

Let

As

((21))

Next, we estimate

3.2
Lower Bounds on the Smallest Eigenvalue

The lower bounds are derived by considering the magnitude of

and
the coefficients of

. First, we consider the regime when

Theorem 3.3
.

For fixed even

\absolutevalue{q_{j}(x)}=O(n^{j/2})
when

Proof.

Since each element in

∎

The second regime we consider is

.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
j=\alpha n
be even with

. Then for

\absolutevalue{q_{j}(x)}=\frac{(C_{\alpha}n)^{j}}{j!}\cdot\quantity(\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{j+1}}{2\sqrt{2}}+o(1)),

Proof.

For each

. Second,
for

Since
{\left|{\mathcal{I}(k,j)}\right|}=\binom{j-k}{k}
,
we have
\absolutevalue{L_{2k}(j)}\leq\binom{j-k}{k}\quantity(C_\alpha n)^{2k}/j!

We conclude that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{q_{j}(x)}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{j/2}{\absolutevalue{L_{2k}(j)}}\cdot\absolutevalue{x}^{j-2k}\leq\frac{(2C_{\alpha}n)^{j}}{j!}\sum_{k=0}^{j/2}\binom{j-k}{k}\quantity(\frac{1}{4})^{k}

((22))

\displaystyle=\frac{(2C_{\alpha}n)^{j}}{j!}\cdot\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{j+1}-(1-\sqrt{2})^{j+1}}{2^{j+1}\sqrt{2}}

((23))

\displaystyle=\frac{(C_{\alpha}n)^{j}}{j!}\cdot\quantity(\frac{(1+\sqrt{2})^{j+1}}{2\sqrt{2}}+o(1)),

((24))

where

follows from

in

Appendix A

∎

Corollary 3.5
.

Let

be even and let

|\lambda_{\min}(H(n,j))|=\begin{cases}O\left(n^{j/2}\right),&j=O(1),\\

Proof.

By
Eq. (19)
, we have
|\lambda_{\min}(H(n,j))|\leq\max_{r_{0}<x<r_{1}}\absolutevalue{q_j(x)}
. The result follows immediately from

Theorem 3.3
,
Theorem 3.4
, and
Stirling’s approximation
[

]
.
∎

There is a trivial bound

.

3.5
provides a great improvement on this

. We compare them for

For

((25))

Lemma 3.6

Next, we consider the case

Lemma 3.7

4
The Smallest Eigenvalue of Cayley Graphs over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}

In this section, we asymptotically determine the smallest eigenvalue of

\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},(r,s,r,s))
, the Cayley graph over
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
generated by vectors
in
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
of type
(r,s,r,s)
with
n=2(r+s)
(see

Section 2.1
). For simplicity of notations, we write
G(r,s)\triangleq\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},(r,s,r,s))
and
S\triangleq\mathbb{Z}_{4}(r,s,r,s)
.

4.1
The Expression of Eigenvalues of
G(r,s)

In this subsection, we express all the eigenvalues of
G(r,s)
with weight
enumerators.

For any
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
, the corresponding eigenvalue of
G(r,s)
is

\lambda(\mathbf{v})=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in S}\zeta_{4}^{\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}}
by
Eq. (6)
. We partition
S
into four disjoint
subsets,

S(\mathbf{v},a)\triangleq{\left\{{\mathbf{b}\in S}\;\colon\;{\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}=a}\right\}},~a\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}.

Note that there is a bijection
S(\mathbf{v},1)\to S(\mathbf{v},3),\;\mathbf{x}\mapsto-\mathbf{x}.
So,

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in S}\zeta_{4}^{\mathbf{b}\cdot\mathbf{v}}={\left|{S({\mathbf{v}},0)}\right|}+{\left|{S({\mathbf{v}},1)}\right|}\zeta_{4}+{\left|{S({\mathbf{v}},2)}\right|}\zeta_{4}^{2}+{\left|{S({\mathbf{v}},3)}\right|}\zeta_{4}^{3}

((32))

\displaystyle={\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}-{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},2)}\right|}=2{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}-\beta({\mathbf{v}}),

((33))

where
\beta(\mathbf{v})\triangleq{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}+{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},2)}\right|}
.
We will determine
\beta({\mathbf{v}})
and
{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}
separately.

For
\mathbf{v}=(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
, we write
\overline{\mathbf{v}}\triangleq(\overline{v_{1}},\dots,\overline{v_{n}})\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
as the image of

\mathbf{v}
through the quotient map
\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}
, i.e.,

\overline{v_{i}}\triangleq v_{i}\pmod{2}\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
for
v_{i}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}
.

Lemma 4.1
.

For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
with
\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{v})=(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
, we have

\beta(\mathbf{v})=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}}\quantity(\frac{1}{2}(x+y)^n + \frac{1}{2}(x+y)^{2r}(x-y)^{2s})[t_{0}+t_{2},t_{1}+t_{3}].

((34))

Proof.

First, we show that

\beta(\mathbf{v})=A_{\langle\overline{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{2}^{\bot}}(x,y)[2r,2s]\binom{2r}{r}\binom{2s}{s}.

((35))

For
\mathbf{b}\in S({\mathbf{v}},0)\cup S({\mathbf{v}},2)
, it is clear that

\overline{\mathbf{b}}\cdot\overline{\mathbf{v}}=0\in\mathbb{Z}_{2}
. That is, for
each
\mathbf{b}\in S({\mathbf{v}},0)\cup S({\mathbf{v}},2)
with

\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{b})=(r,s,r,s)
, we have
\mathbf{t}(\overline{\mathbf{b}})=(2r,2s)
and
\overline{\mathbf{b}}
is a codeword of type
(2r,2s)
in

\langle\overline{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{2}^{\bot}
. Conversely, for each

\overline{\mathbf{b}}\in\langle\overline{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{2}^{\bot}
of
type
(2r,2s)
, there are
\binom{2r}{r}\binom{2s}{s}
of preimages

\mathbf{b}\in S(\mathbf{v},0)\cup S(\mathbf{v},2)
of type
(r,s,r,s)
. This
number is obtained by replacing in
\overline{\mathbf{b}}
half of the
1
with

3
, and half of the
0
with
2
. So
Eq. (35)
is established.

As
\mathbf{t}(\overline{\mathbf{v}})=(t_{0}+t_{2},t_{1}+t_{3})

By

, we have

\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}A_{\langle\overline{\mathbf{v}}\rangle_{2}^{\bot}}[2r,2s]=\binom{n}{2r}A_{(2r,2s)^{\bot}}[t_{0}+t_{2},t_{1}+t_{3}].

For different

A_{{(2r,2s)}}(x,y)=\begin{cases}x^{n},&s=0,\\
x^{n}+x^{n-2s}y^{2s},&s\neq 0.\end{cases}

((36))

By the MacWilliams identity (
Eq. (10)
),

A_{(2r,2s)^{\bot}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}(x+y)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}(x+y)^{2r}(x-y)^{2s}.

((37))

Note that
\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}=\binom{n}{2r}\binom{2r}{r}\binom{2s}{s}
. Then

Eq. (34)
follows.
∎

Next, we give an expression of
{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}
.

Lemma 4.2
.

For
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
with
\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{v})=(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})

{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{4\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}}\big((x+y+z+w)^{n}+2((x+z)^{2}-(y+w)^{2})^{r}((x-z)^{2}+(y-w)^{2})^{s}\\
+(x+y+z+w)^{2r}(x-y+z-w)^{2s}\big)[t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}].

Proof.

By definition and
2.1
,

{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}=A_{(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})^{\bot}}[r,s,r,s]=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}}A_{(r,s,r,s)^{\bot}}[t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}].

Note that

A_{(r,s,r,s)}(x,y,z,w)=\begin{cases}x^{n}+2x^{r}y^{s}z^{r}w^{s}+x^{2r}z^{2s},&s\neq 0,\\

By the MacWilliams identity (

A_{(r,s,r,s)^{\bot}}(x,y,z,w)=\frac{1}{4}(x+y+z+w)^{n}+\frac{1}{2}((x+z)^{2}-(y+w)^{2})^{r}((x-z)^{2}+(y-w)^{2})^{s}\\
+\frac{1}{4}(x+y+z+w)^{2r}(x-y+z-w)^{2s}.

((39))

∎

Now we are able to give an expression of

Theorem 4.3

((40))

Proof.

By
4.1
,
4.2
and

,

\displaystyle\lambda(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=2{\left|{S(\mathbf{v},0)}\right|}-\beta(\mathbf{v})

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}}\big(((x+z)^{2}-(y+w)^{2})^{r}((x-z)^{2}+(y-w)^{2})^{s}

\displaystyle\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad\quad+\frac{1}{2}(x+y+z+w)^{2r}(x-y+z-w)^{2s}\big)[t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}]

\displaystyle\quad-\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{2\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}}\quantity(
(x+y)^{2r}(x-y)^{2s})[t_{0}+t_{2},t_{1}+t_{3}].

((41))

We show that

Let

.
Note that
\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}=\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}\binom{t_{0}+t_{2}}{t_{0}}\binom{t_{1}+t_{3}}{t_{1}}

We first compute

. Since there are
2r
terms
(x+y+z+w)

and
2s
terms
(x-y+z-w)
in

j
-th

(x-y+z-w)

((43))

where the summand

∎

4.2
The Smallest Eigenvalue of
G(r,s)

In this section, we asymptotically determine the smallest eigenvalue of
G(r,s)=\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},(r,s,r,s))
with
n=2(r+s)
for even
r\geq s
in suitable
regimes.

First, we list some observations and notations that are convenient for the
discussion. By
Theorem 4.3
, for
\mathbf{v}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
with

\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{v})=(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
, the eigenvalue

\lambda(\mathbf{v})
of

depends only on the type of
\mathbf{v}
, so
we write
\lambda(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\triangleq\lambda(\mathbf{v})
. As the
polynomial in
Eq. (40)
for the expression of

\lambda(\mathbf{v})
is symmetric with respect to the transposition of
x,z

and the transposition of
y,w
, we know that
\lambda(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
is
independent of the order of
t_{0},t_{2}
and the order of
t_{1},t_{3}
. So, we may
assume
t_{0}\leq t_{2}
and
t_{1}\leq t_{3}
. For
\mathbf{b}\in S\triangleq\mathbb{Z}_{4}(r,s,r,s)

\mathbf{1}\cdot\mathbf{b}=2r\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}
, where
\mathbf{1}\in\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n}
is the all-one vector. Thus,

\lambda(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{1})=\sum_{\mathbf{b}\in S}\zeta_{4}^{(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{1})\cdot\mathbf{b}}=(-1)^{r}\lambda(\mathbf{v}).

((45))

As
\mathbf{t}(\mathbf{v}+\mathbf{1})=(t_{3},t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})
, if
r
is even, we have

\lambda(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\lambda(t_{3},t_{0},t_{1},t_{2})
, and we can further assume
that
t_{0}\leq t_{1}
. Note that the largest eigenvalue of

is

\lambda_{\max}(G(r,s))=\lambda(0,0,0,n)=\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}

In summary, we can always assume that
t_{0}\leq t_{2}
,
t_{0}\leq t_{1}\leq t_{3}
, and

(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})\notin\{(0,0,0,n),

(0,0,n,0)\}
in the following.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
n=2(r+s)
with
r\geq s
and
r
be even.
The smallest eigenvalue of

is given by

\lambda_{\min}(G(r,s))=\lambda(0,1,n-2,1)=-\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{n-1},

((46))

in both of the following two regimes

(R1).

s\geq 2
is fixed and
n
is large enough, i.e.
s=O(1)
;

(R2).

s=\alpha\cdot\frac{n}{2}
and
n
is large enough, where

.

The proof of Theorem
4.4
will be given in the following steps.

•

The expression for

in
Eq. (
(46)
) is verified in
4.5
.

•

When

or

for the two regimes

•

When

and

.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
n=2(r+s)
with

. For

\lambda(0,1,n-2,1)=-\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{n-1}.

Proof.

By

\displaystyle\lambda(0,1,n-2,1)

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{0,1,n-2,1}}\quantity(((x+z)^2 - (y+w)^2)^r((x-z)^2+(y-w)^2)^s)[0,1,n-2,1]

\displaystyle=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{0,1,n-2,1}}\quantity((z^2-2yw)^r(z^2-2yw)^s)[0,1,n-2,1]

,

or

∎

In

, we only prove the case where

by

Lemma 4.6

Let

\displaystyle=(-1)^{r}\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{0,t_{1},0,t_{3}}}\quantity((y+w)^{2r}(y-w)^{2s})[0,t_{1},0,t_{3}]

In the following, assume

Lemma 4.7
.

Let

and
(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})
be a non-negative partition of

.
Suppose

,

\absolutevalue{\lambda(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3)}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{n-1}=\absolutevalue{\lambda(0,1,n-2,1)}.

Proof.

By
Theorem 4.3
,

\absolutevalue{\lambda(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3)}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}}((x+z)^{2}+(y+w)^{2})^{r+s}[t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}].

We expand the polynomial

\displaystyle((x+z)^{2}+(y+w)^{2})^{n/2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}\binom{n/2}{k}(x+z)^{2k}(y+w)^{n-2k}

((52))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}\binom{n/2}{k}\sum_{l=0}^{2k}\quantity(
\sum_{l=0}^{2k} \binom{2k}{l} x^{2k-l}{z^l}
)x^{2k-l}{z^{l}}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2k}\quantity(
\sum_{m=0}^{n-2k} \binom{n-2k}{m} y^{n-2k-m}w^m
)y^{n-2k-m}w^{m}

((53))

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{n/2}\sum_{l=0}^{2k}\sum_{m=0}^{n-2k}\binom{n/2}{k}\binom{2k}{l}\binom{n-2k}{m}x^{2k-l}z^{l}y^{n-2k-m}w^{m}.

. So, we assume that

\absolutevalue{\lambda(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3)}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{\binom{n}{t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3}}}f(t_{0},t_{1},t_{2},t_{3})=\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}\binom{n/2}{(t_{0}+t_{2})/2}}{\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}}.

\absolutevalue{\lambda(t_0,t_1,t_2,t_3)}\leq\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{n-1}=\absolutevalue{\lambda(0,1,n-2,1)}
, it suffices to show that

\binom{n}{t_{0}+t_{2}}\geq(n-1)\binom{n/2}{(t_{0}+t_{2})/2}.

As
(t_{0}+t_{2})\notin{\left\{{0,n}\right\}}
and

is even, we have
(t_{0}+t_{2})\in[2,n-2]
.

\forall~m\in[1,n/2-1],\,\binom{n}{2m}\geq(n-1)\binom{n/2}{m}.

((55))

We note the following inequality

\binom{n}{2m}=\sum_{j=0}^{2m}\binom{n/2}{j}\binom{n/2}{2m-j}\geq\binom{n/2}{m}\binom{n/2}{m}.

As
\binom{n/2}{m}\geq\binom{n/2}{2}
for all

, and

\binom{n/2}{2}\geq n-1
for all even

, we have

\binom{n}{2m}\geq\binom{n/2}{m}\binom{n/2}{m}\geq\binom{n/2}{2}\binom{n/2}{m}\geq(n-1)\binom{n/2}{m}.

for all even
n\geq 10
and
m\in[2,n/2-2]
.
For the rest case where

,
Eq. (55)
holds trivially.
∎

5
Quantum Chromatic Numbers

We first introduce some related works on quantum chromatic numbers.

5.1
Related Works

The precise
definition of quantum coloring and the quantum chromatic number
\chi_{q}(G)
of a
graph
G
, can be found in
Section 1
.

For the lower bound of
\chi_{q}(G)
, we have the following spectral lower bound in

[
15
]

\chi_{q}(G)\geq 1-\frac{\lambda_{\max}(G)}{\lambda_{\min}(G)}.

((56))

By this bound and
Eq. (14)
we have (also see

[
9
, Theorem 1.3]
),

\chi_{q}(H(n,j))\geq\begin{cases}\frac{2j}{2j-n},&\text{ even }j>n/2,\\
n,&j=n/2\text{ and $4\mid n$}.\end{cases}

((57))

However, there is no lower bound of
\chi_{q}(H(n,j))
for even
j<n/2
. When
j

is odd, we have
\chi_{q}(H(n,j))=2

[
8
]
.

The upper bounds of
\chi_{q}(G)
can be derived from flat orthogonal
representations of
G

[
21
]
. Orthogonality graphs (also known as
Hadamard graphs)
[
22
]
constitute a family of graphs
naturally equipped with a flat orthogonal representation. Let
O_{n,p}
be the

orthogonality graph
, whose vertices are vectors in
\mathbb{C}^{n}
with entries
consisting of only the
p
-th roots of unity, and two vertices are adjacent in

O_{n,p}
if and only if they are orthogonal. This representation of vertices
yields
\chi_{q}(O_{n,p})\leq n

[
21
]
. There is a natural isomorphism
between
O_{n,p}
and the Cayley graphs
\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{p}^{n},(l,l,\dots,l))
when

n=lp
and
p
is a prime. So, this determines
\chi_{q}(O_{n,2})=\chi_{q}(H(n,n/2))=n
for
n
divided by
4
. Recently, Cao
et al.

[
9
]
extended this result to
\chi_{q}(O_{lp,p})=lp
for any integer
p\geq 2
and
l
large enough such that
l(p-1)
is even. All these exact values
are obtained by the upper bound from orthogonal representations and the spectral
lower bound.

The upper bounds of
\chi_{q}(G)
can also be derived from graph homomorphisms

[
8
]
. If there is a graph homomorphism
G\to H
,
then

[
8
]

\chi_{q}(G)\leq\chi_{q}(H).

((58))

For example, when
j>n/2
, there is a graph homomorphism

H(n,j)\to H(2j,j)
whose underlying map on the vertices is given by

\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{n}\to\mathbb{Z}_{2}^{2j},\quad(v_{1},\dots,v_{n})\mapsto(v_{1},\dots,v_{n},0,\dots,0).

Thus, for even
j>n/2
, we have (also see

Eq. (3)
and
[
9
, Theorem 1.1]
)

\chi_{q}(H(n,j))\leq\chi_{q}(H(2j,j))=2j.

For
p=4
and even
n
, note that

O_{n,4}\cong\bigcup_{r=0}^{n/2}G(r,n/2-r).

In particular,
G(r,s)
is a subgraph of

6
Conclusion

We provide asymptotic lower bounds for the smallest eigenvalue and the quantum
chromatic number of the distance-
j
Hamming graph
H(n,j)
for even
j<n/2
.
In the regime
j=O(1)
, we have
|\lambda_{\min}(H(n,j))|=O(n^{j/2})
, which
implies that
\chi_{q}(H(n,j))=\Omega(n^{j/2})
. The regime
j=\Theta(n)
is
also considered, and exponential lower bounds on
\lambda_{\min}(H(n,j))
and

\chi_{q}(H(n,j))
are derived. By these lower bounds on the smallest eigenvalue
of
H(n,j)
, we also showed that
\lambda_{\min}(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},(r,s,r,s)))=-\frac{\binom{n}{r,s,r,s}}{n-1}
and
\chi_{q}(\cay(\mathbb{Z}_{4}^{n},(r,s,r,s)))=n
with

n=2(r+s)
, for fixed
s\geq 2
or
s=\frac{1}{2}\alpha n
with
0<\alpha\leq 0.17
, where
r\geq s
is even and large enough.

There are many related problems left. The following ones might be interesting
for future study.

(1)

Find an upper bound for
\chi_{q}(H(n,j))
in the regime
j=O(1)
. The case

j=2
has been considered in
[
20
]
(also see
Table 1
).

(2)

The approximation in
Theorem 3.4
is rough. Can this be refined
to provide better lower bounds on
\lambda_{\min}(H(n,j))
?

(3)

There is still a gap between the lower bound of
\chi_{q}(H(n,j))
in this paper
and the upper bound in
Eq. (3)
, in the regime

j=\Theta(n)
. A solution to Problem (2) might narrow this gap.

(4)

We believe that
Theorem 4.4
holds for all

n=2(r+s)
with even
r\geq s
, not just the regimes considered in this
paper. If this is true, it will imply that
\chi_{q}(G(r,s))=n
with
n=2(r+s)
for all
even
r\geq s
.

(5)

Finally, what is the smallest eigenvalue of the graph
H(n,j)
for even
j<n/2
?

Fibonacci Polynomials

We collect some results on the Fibonacci polynomial

[
28
]
, which are used in the proof of

Theorem 3.4
. The Fibonacci polynomial
F_{n}(x)
is defined by
the recursion
F_{n+1}(x)=xF_{n}(x)+F_{n-1}(x)
with initial values
F_{1}(x)=1
and
F_{2}(x)=x
. It is known that

[
28
]

F_{n}(x)=\sum_{k=0}^{{\left\lfloor{(n-1)/2}\right\rfloor}}\binom{n-1-k}{k}x^{n-1-2k}=\frac{\quantity(x+\sqrt{x^2+4})^{n}-\quantity(x-\sqrt{x^2+4})^{n}}{2^{n}\sqrt{x^{2}+4}}.

((61))

The following related polynomial is directly used in the proof of

Theorem 3.4
,

f_{n}(x)\triangleq\sum_{k=0}^{{\left\lfloor{n/2}\right\rfloor}}\binom{n-k}{k}x^{k}.

((62))

By
Eq. (61)
,
f_{n}(x)=\sqrt{x}^{n}F_{n+1}(1/\sqrt{x})
,
and we have

f_{n}(x)=\frac{(1+\sqrt{1+4x})^{n+1}-(1-\sqrt{1+4x})^{n+1}}{2^{n+1}\sqrt{1+4x}}.

((63))
