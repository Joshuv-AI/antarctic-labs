Bertrand Legendre curves in the unit tangent bundle over Euclidean plane
Nozomi Nakatsuyama, Masatomo Takahashi

Abstract
We investigate not only the associated curves of regular plane curves, but also those of Legendre curves. As associated curves, we consider Bertrand regular plane curves and Bertrand Legendre curves. These curves contain parallel, evolute and involute curves, as well as evolutoid and involutoid curves. Since associated curves may have singular points even if the original curve is regular, Legendre curves provide a suitable framework for investigating the properties of such curves. We give existence conditions of Bertrand regular plane curves and Bertrand Legendre curves. Moreover, we give an inverse operation for Bertrand Legendre curves. Furthermore, we define a mapping between sets of Legendre curves using Bertrand Legendre curves and prove that this mapping is bijective up to equivalence relations.

Introduction

The Bertrand and Mannheim curves are classical objects in differential geometry (
[
1
,
3
,
4
,
5
,
7
,
10
,
14
,
15
,
17
,
19
,
22
,
24
]
).
The Bertrand (respectively, Mannheim) curve is a space curve whose principal normal line is the same as the principal normal (respectively, bi-normal) line of another curve.
We investigated Bertrand type curves as general cases in Euclidean 3-space (cf.
[
20
,
21
]
).
In this paper, we apply the idea to the plane curves.
We investigate not only the associated curves of regular plane curves, but also those of Legendre curves.
As associated curves, we consider Bertrand regular plane curves and Bertrand Legendre curves.
These curves contain parallel, evolute and involute curves, as well as evolutoid and involutoid curves.
Since associated curves may have singular points even if the original curve is regular, Legendre curves provide a suitable framework for investigating the properties of such curves.
A Legendre curve in the unit tangent bundle over Euclidean plane is a plane curve with a moving frame.
Then we can define the (Legendre) curvature of the Legendre curve (cf.
[
8
]
).
The curvature is a complete invariant for Legendre curves up to congruence (Euclidean motion) like as the curvature of regular plane curves.
The existence and uniqueness theorems for the curvature are valid (cf.
[
8
]
).
The definitions of the evolutoid of regular plane curves and frontals have already investigated in
[
11
,
16
]
, and the definition of the involutoid (tanvolute) of regular plane curves has already investigated in
[
2
]
.
Moreover, the evolutoid and involutoid of spherical Legendre curves investigated in
[
18
]
.
However, as far as we know, the explicit form of the definition of the involutoid of regular plane curves and of Legendre curves (frontals) in the unit tangent bundle over Euclidean plane cannot be found.

We give the conditions for the existence of Bertrand regular plane curves and Bertrand Legendre curves (Theorems
3.2
and
4.2
).
Then we give the explicit form of the involutoid.
We also give new correspondences which connects between evolutes and involutes of Legendre curves.
Moreover, we give an inverse operation for Bertrand Legendre curves (Theorem
4.13
).
Furthermore, we define a mapping between sets of Legendre curves using Bertrand Legendre curves and prove that this mapping is bijective up to equivalence relations (Theorem
4.15
).

We shall assume throughout the whole paper that all maps and manifolds are
C^{\infty}
unless the contrary is explicitly stated.

J

2.1
Regular plane curves

Let
I
be an interval of
\mathbb{R}
and let
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a regular plane curve, that is,
\dot{\gamma}(t)\not=0
for all
t\in I
, where
\dot{\gamma}(t)=(d\gamma/dt)(t)
.
If we take the arc-length parameter
s
, that is,
|\gamma^{\prime}(s)|=1
for all
s
, then the tangent vector, the normal vector are given by
\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)=\gamma^{\prime}(s),\ \mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)=J(\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)),
where
\gamma^{\prime}(s)=(d\gamma/ds)(s)
.
Then
\{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)\}
is a moving frame of
\gamma(s)
and we have the Frenet formula:

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\boldmath$t$}^{\prime}(s)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}^{\prime}(s)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\kappa(s)\\
-\kappa(s)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)\end{array}\right),

where
\kappa(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$t$}^{\prime}(s)\cdot\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s).

If we take a general parameter
t
, then the tangent vector, the normal vector are given by

\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(t)={\dot{\gamma}(t)}/{|\dot{\gamma}(t)|},\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)=J(\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(t)).

Then
\{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(t),\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\}
is a moving frame of
\gamma(t)
and we have the Frenet formula:

\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(t)\\
\dot{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\kappa(t)\\
-|\dot{\gamma}(t)|\kappa(t)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(t)\\
\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(t)\end{array}\right),

where

\kappa(t)={{\rm det}(\dot{\gamma}(t),\ddot{\gamma}(t))}/{|\dot{\gamma}(t)|^{3}}.

2.2
Legendre curves

Let
(\gamma,\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be a smooth mapping.

Definition 2.1

We say that
(\gamma,\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
is a
Legendre curve
if
\dot{\gamma}(t)\cdot\nu(t)=0
for all
t\in I
.
We also say that
\gamma
is a
frontal
if there exists a smooth map
\nu:I\to S^{1}
such that
(\gamma,\nu)
is a Legendre curve.

We denote
\mu:I\to S^{1}
by
\mu(t)=J(\nu(t))
.
Then
\{\nu(t),\mu(t)\}
is a moving frame of
\gamma(t)
.
Therefore, we have the following Frenet formula:

\displaystyle\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\dot{\nu}(t)\\
\dot{\mu}(t)\end{array}\right)=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&\ell(t)\\
-\ell(t)&0\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{c}\nu(t)\\
\mu(t)\end{array}\right),\ \dot{\gamma}(t)=\beta(t)\mu(t),

where
\ell(t)=\dot{\nu}(t)\cdot\mu(t),\beta(t)=\dot{\gamma}(t)\cdot\mu(t)
.
The mapping
(\ell,\beta):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is called the (Legendre) curvature of
(\gamma,\nu)
.
By definition,
t\in I
is a singular point of
\gamma
, that is,
\dot{\gamma}(t)=0
if and only if
\beta(t)=0
.
We say that
t\in I
is an
inflection point
of
\gamma
(or,
(\gamma,\nu)
) if
\ell(t)=0
.

Let
(\gamma,\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be a Legendre curve with curvature
(\ell,\beta)
.
Then
(\gamma,-\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
is also a Legendre curve with curvature
(\ell,-\beta)
.

Obviously, a regular curve is a frontal.
We give a relation between curvatures of Legendre curves and of regular curves.

Proposition 2.2

(1)
Let
(\gamma,\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be a Legendre curve with
(\ell,\beta)
.
If
\gamma
is a regular plane curve with curvature
\kappa
, then we have
\ell=\kappa|\beta|
.

(2)

If
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a regular plane curve with curvature
\kappa
, then
(\gamma,\mbox{\boldmath$n$}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
is a Legendre curve with curvature
(\ell,\beta)=(|\dot{\gamma}|\kappa,-|\dot{\gamma}|)
.

We say that a smooth plane curve
\gamma:(I,t_{0})\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
at
t_{0}
is a
j/i
-cusp (or,
(i,j)
-cusp), where
(i,j)=(2,3),(2,5)
,
(3,4)
and
(3,5)
if
\gamma
is
\mathcal{A}
-equivalent to the germ
t\mapsto(t^{i},t^{j})
at the origin.
For curves with
j/i
-cusps in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
, the criteria are known in
[
6
,
23
]
.

Proposition 2.3

Let
(\gamma,\nu):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be a Legendre curve with
(\ell,\beta)
.

(1)

\gamma
has a
3/2
-cusp at
t_{0}
if and only if
\beta(t_{0})=0
,
\dot{\beta}(t_{0})\neq 0
and
\ell(t_{0})\neq 0
.

(2)

\gamma
has a
5/2
-cusp at
t_{0}
if and only if
\beta(t_{0})=\ell(t_{0})=0
,
\dot{\beta}(t_{0})\neq 0
and
\ddot{\ell}(t_{0})\dot{\beta}(t_{0})-\dot{\ell}(t_{0})\ddot{\beta}(t_{0})\neq 0
.

(3)

\gamma
has a
4/3
-cusp at
t_{0}
if and only if
\beta(t_{0})=\dot{\beta}(t_{0})=0
,
\ddot{\beta}(t_{0})\neq 0
and
\ell(t_{0})\neq 0
.

(4)

\gamma
has a
5/3
-cusp at
t_{0}
if and only if
\beta(t_{0})=\dot{\beta}(t_{0})=\ell(t_{0})=0
,
\ddot{\beta}(t_{0})\neq 0
and
\dot{\ell}(t_{0})\neq 0
.

3
Bertrand regular plane curves

Let
\gamma,\overline{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be regular plane curves and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}:I\to S^{1}
be smooth curves.

Definition 3.1

We say that
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-regular mates
if there exists a smooth function
\lambda:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(t)
for all
t\in I
.
We also say that
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand regular (plane) curve
if there exists a regular plane curve
\overline{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
such that
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
are
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-regular mates.

We clarify the notation
\lambda\not\equiv 0
.
Throughout this paper,
\lambda\not\equiv 0
means that
\{t\in I|\lambda(t)\not=0\}
is a dense subset of
I
.
Then
\lambda
is not identically zero for any non-trivial subintervals of
I
.
It follows that
\gamma
and
\overline{\gamma}
are different plane curves for any non-trivial subintervals of
I
.
Note that if
\lambda
is constant, then
\lambda\not\equiv 0
means that
\lambda
is a non-zero constant.

We give a characterization of Bertrand regular curves.
Let
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a regular plane curve with curvature
\kappa
.
By a parameter change, we may assume that
s
is the arc-length parameter of
\gamma
.
Note that
s
is not the arc-length parameter of
\overline{\gamma}
.
Since
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}:I\to S^{1}
are smooth curves, there exist smooth functions
\theta,\tau:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)=\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)
and
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)=\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)+\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)
.

Theorem 3.2

Let
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}:I\to S^{1}
be smooth curves and
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be a regular plane curve with curvature
\kappa
.
Then
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand regular curve if and only if there exist smooth functions
\lambda,\theta,\tau:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that

\displaystyle(\cos\theta(s)+{\lambda}^{\prime}(s))\sin\tau(s)-(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)({\theta}^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\cos\tau(s)=0,

(1)

\displaystyle(\cos\theta(s)+{\lambda}^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)({\theta}^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)\not=0

(2)

for all
s\in I
.

Proof.
Suppose that
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand regular curve.
Then there exists a smooth function
\lambda:I\to\mathbb{R}
with
\lambda\not\equiv 0
such that
\overline{\gamma}(s)=\gamma(s)+\lambda(s)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)
and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)
for all
s\in I
, where there exist smooth functions
\theta,\tau:I\to\mathbb{R}
such that
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)=\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)
and
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)=\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)+\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)
.
By differentiating
\overline{\gamma}(s)=\gamma(s)+\lambda(s)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)
, we have

\displaystyle|\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(s)|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)=

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s)(\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s))

\displaystyle+\lambda(s)(-(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)).

By the above notations, we have

\begin{pmatrix}J({\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}(s))\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$v$}}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\theta(s)&-\sin\theta(s)\\
\sin\theta(s)&\cos\theta(s)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)\\
{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)\end{pmatrix},\ \begin{pmatrix}J(\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s))\\
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\cos\tau(s)&-\sin\tau(s)\\
\sin\tau(s)&\cos\tau(s)\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)\\
\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)\end{pmatrix}.

Moreover, since
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)=\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)
, we have
J(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s))=J(\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s))
, that is,

\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)-\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)=\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)-\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle|\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(s)|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)

\displaystyle=(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)-(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))J(\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s))

\displaystyle=(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))(\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)+\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s))

\displaystyle\quad-(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))(\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)-\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s))

\displaystyle=\left((\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)\right)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)

\displaystyle\quad+\left((\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\sin\tau(s)-(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\cos\tau(s)\right)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s).

It follows that we have equations (
1
) and (
2
).

Conversely, suppose that equations (
1
) and (
2
) satisfy.
Let
\overline{\gamma}:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
be
\overline{\gamma}(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)
, where
\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s)=\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)
.
By a direct calculation, we have

\displaystyle\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(s)=

\displaystyle\left((\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)\right)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)\neq 0.

It follows that
\overline{\gamma}
is a regular plane curve.
Moreover, since

\displaystyle|\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(s)|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)

\displaystyle=(1+\lambda^{\prime}(s)\cos\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\sin\theta(s))\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)

\displaystyle\quad+(\lambda^{\prime}(s)\sin\theta(s)+\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\cos\theta(s))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s),

then we have

\displaystyle|\dot{\overline{\gamma}}(s)|\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ (1+\lambda^{\prime}(s)\cos\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\sin\theta(s))\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)

\displaystyle-(\lambda^{\prime}(s)\sin\theta(s)+\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\cos\theta(s))\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s).

Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ \frac{1+\lambda^{\prime}(s)\cos\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\sin\theta(s)}{(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)}\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)

\displaystyle+\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(s)\sin\theta(s)+\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\cos\theta(s)}{(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s),

\displaystyle\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)=

\displaystyle\ -\frac{\lambda^{\prime}(s)\sin\theta(s)+\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\cos\theta(s)}{(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)}\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)

\displaystyle+\frac{1+\lambda^{\prime}(s)\cos\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s))\sin\theta(s)}{(\cos\theta(s)+\lambda^{\prime}(s))\cos\tau(s)+(\sin\theta(s)-\lambda(s)(\theta^{\prime}(s)+\kappa(s)))\sin\tau(s)}\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s).

By a direct calculation, we have

\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}(s)=\cos\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$t$}}(s)+\sin\tau(s)\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$n$}}(s)=\cos\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$t$}(s)+\sin\theta(s)\mbox{\boldmath$n$}(s)=\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(s).

It follows that
\gamma:I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}
is a
(\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}})
-Bertrand regular curve.

\Box

Remark 3.3

The condition (

By Theorem

4
Bertrand Legendre curves

Let
(\gamma,\nu),(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\nu}):I\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be Legendre curves and
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}:I\to S^{1}
be smooth curves.
Since
\mbox{\boldmath$v$},\overline{\mbox{\boldmath$w$}}:I\to S^{1}

Example 4.17

Let
(\gamma,\nu):[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}^{2}\times S^{1}
be

\displaystyle\gamma(t)=\left(\cos^{3}t,\sin^{3}t\right),\ \nu(t)=\left(\sin t,\cos t\right).

Then
\gamma
is an astroid.
By a direct calculation,
\mu(t)=(-\cos t,\sin t)
and
(\gamma,\nu)
is a Legendre curve with the curvature
(\ell(t),\beta(t))=(-1,3\cos t\sin t)
.

If we take
\theta(t)=0
,
\tau(t)=\pi/2
, a smooth function
\lambda:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by
\lambda(t)=3\cos t\sin t
, then
\beta(t)+\lambda(t)\ell(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi)
.
Therefore, the Legendre curve
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\nu})
such that
\overline{\gamma}
is an evolute of
(\gamma,\nu)
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)=\left(\cos^{3}t+3\cos t\sin^{2}t,\sin^{3}t+3\cos^{2}t\sin t\right),

\displaystyle\overline{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=-\mu(t)=\left(\cos t,-\sin t\right).

If we take
\theta(t)=\pi/2
,
\tau(t)=0
and a smooth function
\lambda:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by
\lambda(t)=(3\cos 2t)/4+c
, where
c
is a constant, then
\beta(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi)
.
Therefore, the Legendre curve
(\overline{\gamma},\overline{\nu})
such that
\overline{\gamma}
is an involute of
(\gamma,\nu)
is given by

\displaystyle\overline{\gamma}(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mbox{\boldmath$v$}(t)=\left(-\frac{\cos^{3}t}{2}+\frac{3\cos t}{4}-c\cos t,-\frac{\sin^{3}t}{2}+\frac{3\sin t}{4}+c\sin t\right),

\displaystyle\overline{\nu}(t)

\displaystyle=\mu(t)=\left(-\cos t,\sin t\right).

If we take a constant
\theta
,
\tau(t)=\pi/2
and a smooth function
\lambda:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
,
\lambda(t)=3\cos t\sin t\cos\theta
,
then
\beta(t)\cos\theta+\lambda(t)\ell(t)=0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi)
.
By Definition
4.7
, the evolutoid
\mathcal{E}v[\theta](\gamma,\nu)
of
(\gamma,\nu)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}v[\theta](\gamma,\nu)(t)

\displaystyle=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)(\cos\theta\nu(t)+\sin\theta\mu(t))

\displaystyle=\left(\cos^{3}t+3\cos t\sin t\cos\theta\sin(t-\theta),\sin^{3}t+3\cos t\sin t\cos\theta\cos(t-\theta)\right).

If we take
\theta(t)=\pi/2
, a constant
\tau
with
\cos\tau=0
and a smooth function
\lambda:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by
\lambda(t)=0
, then
(\beta(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\cos\tau-\lambda(t)\ell(t)\sin\tau=0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi)
.
By Definition
4.7
, the involutoid
\mathcal{I}nv[\tau](\gamma,\nu)
of
(\gamma,\nu)
is given by
\mathcal{I}nv[\tau](\gamma,\nu)(t)=\gamma(t)+\lambda(t)\mu(t)=\left(\cos^{3}t,\sin^{3}t\right)
.
Moreover, if we take
\theta(t)=\pi/2
, a constant
\tau
with
\cos\tau\neq 0
and a smooth function
\lambda,:[0,2\pi)\to\mathbb{R}
by

\lambda(t)=-\frac{3\cos\tau(\sin 2t\sin\tau-2\cos 2t\cos\tau)}{2(\sin^{2}\tau+4\cos^{2}\tau)}+ce^{-(\tan\tau)t},

where
c
is a constant, then
(\beta(t)+\dot{\lambda}(t))\cos\tau-\lambda(t)\ell(t)\sin\tau=0
for all
t\in[0,2\pi)
.
By Definition
4.7
, the involutoid
\mathcal{I}nv[\tau](\gamma,\nu)
of
(\gamma,\nu)
is given by

\displaystyle\mathcal{I}nv[\tau](\gamma,\nu)(t)

\displaystyle=\biggl(\cos^{3}t+\frac{3\cos t\cos\tau(\sin 2t\sin\tau-2\cos 2t\cos\tau)}{2(\sin^{2}\tau+4\cos^{2}\tau)}-ce^{-(\tan\tau)t}\cos t,

\displaystyle\qquad\sin^{3}t-\frac{3\sin t\cos\tau(\sin 2t\sin\tau-2\cos 2t\cos\tau)}{2(\sin^{2}\tau+4\cos^{2}\tau)}+ce^{-(\tan\tau)t}\sin t\biggr).
