Flow diversion of a distal M3 segment middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm in a pediatric patient: illustrative case

Intracranial pseudoaneurysms are a known yet rare complication of cranial surgery.1 These lesions carry a high rate of morbidity and mortality attributable to the risk of pseudoaneurysm rupture or thrombosis and consequential subarachnoid hemorrhage, intraparenchymal hemorrhage, epidural hematoma, carotid-cavernous fistula formation, transient ischemic events, or thromboembolic phenomenon dependent on their size and location.1,2 There are no clear data regarding the time frame from vessel injury to pseudoaneurysm development.2,3 However, several reports have described their inception as early as hours to weeks following vessel injury.2,4 Likewise, the time course between pseudoaneurysm development and potential associated sequelae is generally unknown.2,3 Given the morbidity and potential mortality associated with rupture of an intracranial pseudoaneurysm, early treatment is imperative; yet the best modality of treatment remains debated.1 The advent of flow diversion has shifted the treatment paradigm of these lesions from vessel sacrifice to vessel reconstruction. However, literature on flow diversion of distal intracranial pseudoaneurysms remains scarce.

Illustrative Case

A 14-year-old female underwent an uncomplicated left temporal craniotomy for resection of a pleomorphic xanthoastrocytoma. There was no evidence of vascular injury at the time of surgery. Routine follow-up MRI at 3 months postoperatively demonstrated an avidly enhancing lesion superficial to the left superior temporal gyrus along the course of the distal middle cerebral artery vessels, suspicious for a pseudoaneurysm (Fig. 1). Subsequent CT angiography demonstrated similar findings, prompting a formal angiography study (Fig. 2). Diagnostic cerebral angiography confirmed the presence of an 8-mm pseudoaneurysm in the distal left M3 segment middle cerebral artery (Fig. 3). As such, flow diversion of the parent vessel was recommended pending the ability to track a microsystem into the distal cerebrovasculature during the procedure. In the case that endovascular intervention was deemed not feasible, open surgical wrapping with Gore-Tex membrane would be pursued the same day. As such, dual antiplatelet therapy was not initiated preoperatively. A right femoral approach was utilized with a system consisting of a Navien 072 guide catheter (Medtronic Neurovascular), Phenom 21 microcatheter (Medtronic Neurovascular), and Aristotle 14 standard microwire (Scientia Vascular). The distal left M3 segment middle cerebral artery vessels were accessible with a microsystem. A 180-μg/kg bolus of eptifibatide was administered. Next, a 2.5 × 12–mm Pipeline Vantage embolization device (Medtronic Neurovascular) was successfully deployed across the parent left M3 segment middle cerebral artery containing the pseudoaneurysm. Completion angiography demonstrated early contrast stasis within the pseudoaneurysm with excellent wall apposition of the flow diverter. The patient tolerated the procedure well and was transitioned to aspirin and ticagrelor postoperatively for 6 months. Follow-up angiography at 6 months demonstrated no evidence of residual pseudoaneurysm with a patent Pipeline Vantage embolization device without evidence of any significant in-stent stenosis (Fig. 4).

Discussion

Observation

Intracranial pseudoaneurysms represent a rare yet difficult lesion to treat due to their location and the fragility of the pseudoaneurysm wall.1,2 Historically, treatment has been limited to open cranial surgery encompassing microsurgical clipping, wrapping, ligation of the parent vessel, and trapping with or without extracranial-intracranial bypass.1 Numerous endovascular treatments of intracranial pseudoaneurysms have been reported with varying degrees of safety and efficacy.1,5 Primary coil embolization of these lesions has been associated with high failure rates due to pseudoaneurysm growth and recanalization.1,5 Endovascular occlusion of the parent artery has been suggested for the treatment of distal pseudoaneurysms supplying noneloquent brain parenchyma; however, this may lead to cerebral ischemia dependent on collateral blood flow.1,5 Flow diversion represents an alternative treatment option for these lesions focused on parent vessel reconstruction. Several studies and case reports have demonstrated flow diversion as a viable, off-label treatment for intracranial pseudoaneurysms.5–7 Chen et al. investigated the safety and efficacy of the Pipeline embolization device (Medtronic Neurovascular) in 19 patients undergoing flow diversion of an intracranial pseudoaneurysm.5 Of the 18 patients with available follow-up imaging, 14 patients (78%) demonstrated complete pseudoaneurysm occlusion, and 2 patients (11%) achieved near-complete pseudoaneurysm occlusion.5 Importantly, no complications were reported secondary to placement of the device.5 Notably, 2 patients (11%) exhibited significant pseudoaneurysm progression on follow-up imaging and required internal carotid artery sacrifice.5 The authors concluded that the use of flow diversion in the treatment of intracranial pseudoaneurysm may be a feasible alternative treatment to parent vessel sacrifice.5 Sami et al. evaluated clinical outcomes and follow-up angiograms in 7 patients with 8 intracranial pseudoaneurysms.6 Of the 6 patients with available follow-up imaging, 5 (83%) demonstrated complete pseudoaneurysm occlusion and 1 (17%) achieved near-complete pseudoaneurysm occlusion.6 One patient experienced a distal middle cerebral artery rupture during the flow diversion procedure, which resulted in death.6 The authors concluded that the use of flow diversion for the treatment of intracranial pseudoaneurysms can achieve good, persistent occlusion rates with an acceptable complication rate.6 However, the majority of pseudoaneurysms treated within these two studies arose from the internal carotid artery, with only 1 pseudoaneurysm arising from the middle cerebral artery.5,6

Although several studies have evaluated the safety and efficacy of flow diversion of internal carotid artery pseudoaneurysms, few have investigated this treatment in the distal cerebrovasculature.5,6,8 In fact, to the best of our knowledge, Aljuboori et al. described the only case of flow diversion of a distal middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm to date.8 The authors reported the case of a 19-year-old male who sustained a gunshot wound to the face.8 Diagnostic cerebral angiography demonstrated a small left internal carotid artery pseudoaneurysm and a left M2 superior division middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm.8 The patient underwent successful primary coil embolization of the middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm; however, repeat follow-up angiography 2 weeks later demonstrated recurrence.8 As such, the authors elected to proceed with flow diversion of both aneurysms via two telescoping Pipeline Flex embolization devices (Medtronic Neurovascular).8 Six-month follow-up imaging demonstrated aneurysm obliteration with parent vessel reconstruction.8

Here, we present the first case of a distal M3 segment middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm treated with flow diversion in a pediatric patient. We suspect that vessel injury occurred during the craniotomy; however, there was no evidence of hemorrhage to suspect vascular insult at the time of surgery. The parent vessel may have developed spasm following injury, which concealed intraoperative identification of the injury. Following resolution of spasm, the pseudoaneurysm may have then developed. We opted for treatment of the pseudoaneurysm given the size and location of the lesion. Open cerebrovascular as well as endovascular techniques were considered. Surgical clipping was decided against, given the fragility of the pseudoaneurysm wall. Extracranial-intracranial bypass was not considered, as the superficial temporal artery was sacrificed during the initial procedure. Primary coil embolization was not pursued due to the known high recanalization rates associated with this technique.1 Parent artery occlusion was decided against, given that the parent vessel may be supplying the left superior temporal gyrus and eloquent speech area. As such, we opted for flow diversion of the left M3 segment middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysm via a Pipeline Vantage embolization device. Although flow diversion has been proven to be a safe and efficacious treatment option for true aneurysms, further studies are needed to validate these findings in the setting of pseudoaneurysms. Nevertheless, this case demonstrates that flow diversion is a safe, feasible, and efficacious treatment option for distal M3 segment middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysms.

Lessons

Lesions suspicious for intracranial pseudoaneurysms on noninvasive imaging should be confirmed via formal angiography. Flow diversion of distal M3 segment middle cerebral artery pseudoaneurysms is a safe, feasible, and efficacious treatment option; however, further studies are necessary to explore the long-term outcomes of flow diversion of pseudoaneurysms arising in the distal cerebrovasculature.
