A result on the generic Picard number of surfaces in fake weighted projective 3 3 -spaces
Julius Giesler

Abstract
We give a criterion for certain generic nondegenerate surfaces in a fake weighted projective 3 3 -space to have Picard number > 1 >1 . These algebraic surfaces are of general type. We do this by considering degenerations (along an edge), keeping track of the geometric genus, and using vanishing cohomology classes to construct a rational Picard class on the surface not proportional to the canonical divisor.

Degenerations and the geometric genus

Definition 2.1
.

A degeneration of algebraic surfaces is a proper flat morphism
p:\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
, where
B\subset\mathbb{C}
is the unit disc, such that
X_{t}:=p^{-1}(t)
is a smooth projective surface for

is a Kähler manifold. The degeneration is called semistable if we may write
X_{0}=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}V_{i}
, where
V_{i}
are smooth and the irreducible components of
X_{0}
, the
V_{i}
intersect transversally and locally
p
is defined by

t=x_{1}\cdot...\cdot x_{k}

(Subdivison of a simplex along an edge)

\displaystyle\Delta_{i,i+1}:=\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{i+1}.

Let

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\Delta
be a
3
-dimensional lattice simplex with
l^{*}(\Delta)>0
and an edge
e
wit
l^{*}(e)>0
. Choose the subdivision

\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup...\cup\Delta_{r},\quad\Delta_{i,i+1}:=\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{i+1}

along
e
with hypersurfaces
Z_{\Delta}
,
V_{i}:=Z_{\Delta_{i}}
and double curves
C_{i,i+1}:=Z_{\Delta_{i}}\cap Z_{\Delta_{i+1}}
. Then

p_{g}(X_{t})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}p_{g}(Z_{\Delta_{i}})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}g(C_{i,i+1}).

As a consequence for all additional components
V_{r+1},...,V_{l}
in a semistable degeneration and all other double curves
C_{ij}
we have

p_{g}(V_{i})=0,\quad g(C_{ij})=0.

Proof.

We have
p_{g}(X_{t})=l^{*}(\Delta)
and
p_{g}(Z_{\Delta_{i}})=l^{*}(\Delta_{i})
for
i=1,...,r
by
[

]
. Further
g(C_{i,i+1})=l^{*}(\Delta_{i,i+1})
and

\displaystyle l^{*}(\Delta)=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}l^{*}(\Delta_{i})+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}l^{*}(\Delta_{i,i+1}).

(2)

Let
\mathcal{X}\rightarrow B
be a semistable degeneration one obtains by the semistable reduction Theorem. Then by (
[
Pers77
, Prop.2.7.3]
) we have

p_{g}(X_{t})=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{l}p_{g}(V_{i})+\sum\limits_{i\neq j}g(C_{ij})+h^{2}(\Gamma).

where
\Gamma
denotes the dual graph of
X_{0}
. But all nonzero terms are already contained in
p_{g}(Z_{\Delta_{i}})
and
g(C_{i,i+1})
. Thus
h^{2}(\Gamma)=0
and the result follows.
∎

2.1
Vanishing cohomology classes and Picard classes

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
\Delta
be a
3
-dimensional simplex having an edge
e
with
l^{*}(e)>0
and
\dim\,F(\Delta)\in\{2,3\}
. Let
f\in U_{reg}(\Delta)
be sufficiently nondegenerate and consider the subdivision

\Delta=\Delta_{1}\cup...\cup\Delta_{r},\quad\Delta_{i,i+1}:=\Delta_{i}\cap\Delta_{i+1}

from above. If

\displaystyle\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r-1}l^{*}(\Delta_{i,i+1})>\sum\limits_{i=1}^{r}l^{*}(\Delta_{i}),

(6)

then
\rho(Z_{\Delta,f})>1
, that is not all Picard classes of
Z_{\Delta,f}
come from
\mathbb{P}_{\Delta}
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{X}\overset{p}{\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}
be the total space of the semistable degeneration.
K_{X_{t}}
defines a nontrivial Picard class in
\ker(N)
since
K_{X_{t}}=K_{\mathcal{X}|{X_{t}}}
by the adjunction formula since
X_{t}
is just a fiber of
p
. The vanishing cohomology classes lie in
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})
and the space
H^{2,0}(X_{t})\oplus H^{0,2}(X_{t})
intersects
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})
in a
\leq p_{g}
-dimensional vector subspace. Thus by
6
and construction
2.8
there is a vanishing cohomology class
V
with

V\notin H^{2,0}(X_{t})\oplus H^{0,2}(X_{t}).

Further
V.K_{X_{t}}=0
since
V
is orthogonal on
\ker(N)
. By construction
V
projects nontrivially under

\pi:H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})\rightarrow H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})\rightarrow H^{1,1}(X_{t}).

We show that
w:=\pi(V)\in H^{1,1}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})=\Pic_{\mathbb{Q}}(X_{t})
(the last equality follows from the Lefschetz
(1,1)
-theorem): Write
V=\alpha+w+\overline{\beta}
, where
w\neq 0
. Let
v_{1},...,v_{k}
be a basis of
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})
such that the nondegenerate intersection product on
H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{C})
is given by
v_{i}.v_{j}=d_{i}\cdot\delta_{ij}
, where
d_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}\setminus\{0\}
. Write

\displaystyle V=\sum a_{i}\cdot v_{i},\quad\alpha+\overline{\beta}=\sum b_{i}\cdot v_{i},\quad w=\sum c_{i}\cdot v_{i}\quad a_{i}\in\mathbb{Q},b_{i},c_{i}\in\mathbb{R}.

Then
a_{i}=b_{i}+c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
. Define
\tilde{a_{i}}:=a_{i}\cdot\sqrt{d_{i}}\in\mathbb{C}
and analogously
\tilde{b_{i}}
and
\tilde{c_{i}}
. We have
H^{1,1}(X_{t})\perp\big(H^{2,0}(X_{t})\oplus H^{0,2}(X_{t})\big)
. Thus

\displaystyle 0=w.(\alpha+\overline{\beta})=\sum\tilde{b_{i}}\cdot\tilde{c_{i}}.

Use
\tilde{a_{i}}^{2}=(\tilde{b_{i}}+\tilde{c_{i}})^{2}
to get

\displaystyle\sum\tilde{a_{i}}^{2}\cdot v_{i}=\sum(\tilde{b_{i}}^{2}+\tilde{c_{i}}^{2})\cdot v_{i}

Thus
\tilde{b_{i}}\cdot\tilde{c_{i}}=0
by linear independence and either
b_{i}=0
or
c_{i}=0
since
d_{i}\neq 0
, that is
b_{i},c_{i}\in\mathbb{Q}
and
w\in H^{2}(X_{t},\mathbb{Q})
defines a rational Picard class. But

w.K_{X_{t}}=V.K_{X_{t}}=0,

and
K_{X_{t}}^{2}>0
, thus
w
defines an additional Picard class not proportional to
K_{X_{t}}
.
∎

Remark 2.11
.

Of course the property
K_{X_{t}}^{2}>0
is important: The generic Picard number of a K3 surface is
1
. But there is a semistable degeneration of K3 surfaces into a chain of rational and ruled elliptic surfaces

, thus
p_{g}(V_{i})=0
, such that
C_{i,i+1}:=V_{i}\cap V_{i+1}
is an elliptic curve, that is
g(C_{i,i+1})=1
(type II degeneration).
