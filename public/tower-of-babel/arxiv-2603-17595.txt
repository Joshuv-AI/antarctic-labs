Pretty good plus state transfer in cycles
Sarojini Mohapatra, Hiranmoy Pal

Abstract
We investigate fractional revival in graphs with respect to the adjacency, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices. We observe that, under certain conditions, fractional revival is preserved under graph complementation. Then we establish a connection between fractional revival in a graph and in its double cover, and obtain a complete characterization of pretty good plus state transfer in cycles and their complements. This leads to characterizations of pretty good vertex state transfer in weighted paths with potential. Keywords: Spectra of graphs, Circulant graph, Continuous-time quantum walk, Fractional revival, Pretty good plus state transfer. MSC: 15A16, 05C50, 81P45.

Introduction

The transfer of quantum states in quantum spin networks is a fundamental problem in quantum information theory.

Continuous-time quantum walks
, introduced by Farhi and Gutmann
farhi
, provide a useful framework for analyzing such quantum transport phenomena.
A continuous-time quantum walk on a graph
G
is governed by the transition matrix

U_{M(G)}(t)=\exp{(itM(G))}=\sum_{k\geq 0}\frac{(it)^{k}}{k!}(M(G))^{k},\quad\text{where $t\in\mathbb{R},$}

and M(G) denotes the Hamiltonian associated with
G.
In this context, we consider
M(G)
to be the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix, or the signless Laplacian matrix of
G.
Whenever the context is clear, we write

U(t)
instead of

U_{M(G)}(t).
Let
G(V(G),E(G),w)
be a weighted undirected graph with vertex set
V(G)
and edge set
E(G),
where the weight function
w\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E(G)\to\mathbb{R}
assigns a real number to each edge of
G.
The
adjacency matrix

A(G)
is defined by
A(G)_{j,k}=w(j,k)
for
(j,k)\in E(G),
and
0
otherwise. A
potential on
G
is a diagonal matrix
\Delta,
where
\Delta_{j,j}
is the potential at the vertex
j.
For a graph
G
with potential, the Hamiltonian
M(G)
is considered as
A(G)+\Delta.

Unless otherwise specified, we assume that
G
is finite, simple, and undirected. In the case of a simple graph, each edge has weight
1,
and the potential
\Delta
is the zero matrix. The
Laplacian
and
signless Laplacian
matrices of a simple graph are defined by
L(G)=D(G)-A(G),
and
Q(G)=D(G)+A(G),
respectively, where
D(G)
is the degree matrix of
G.
Let
\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d}
be the distinct eigenvalues of
M(G)
with corresponding eigenprojection matrices
E_{\lambda_{1}},E_{\lambda_{2}},\ldots,E_{\lambda_{d}}.
Then the spectral decomposition of
U_{M(G)}(t)
is given by

U_{M(G)}(t)=\sum_{j=1}^{d}\exp{(it\lambda_{j})}E_{\lambda_{j}}.

A
real pure state
is represented by a unit vector in
\mathbb{R}^{n}

god7
. Let
a
and
b
be two vertices in a graph
G.
The characteristic vector
{\mathbf{e}}_{a}
is called the
vertex state
associated with
a.
For a non-zero real number
s,
a real pure state of the form
\frac{1}{\sqrt{1+s^{2}}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+s{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
is called an
s
-pair state

kim
. In particular, when
s=-1,
the state is called a
pair state
and for
s=1,
it is called a
plus state
.
The

of a state
{\bf u}
relative to

is the set

\{\lambda_{j}\mathrel{\mathop{\mathchar 58\relax}}E_{\lambda_{j}}{\bf u}\neq 0\}.

A state
{\bf u}
is called a fixed state if and only if
{\bf u}
is an eigenvector of
M(G)
associated with the lone eigenvalue in the eigenvalue support of
{\bf u}

.
A graph
G
is said to exhibit

between two linearly independent real pure states

and
{\bf v}
if there exists a

\tau
and a phase factor

U_{M(G)}(\tau){\bf u}=\gamma{\bf v}.

(1)

If
{\bf u}={\bf v},
then the state

is said to be periodic in

When
{\bf u}
and
{\bf v}
in (
1
) are both vertex states, pair states, or plus states, then the corresponding PST is called
vertex PST, pair PST
, or
plus PST
, respectively. PST in quantum spin networks was first introduced by Bose

and has attracted considerable attention over the past two decades, with extensive research devoted to characterizing vertex PST.
A fundamental result due to Godsil

establishes that for any integer
k>0,
there is a finite number of connected graphs of maximum degree
k
with vertex PST. This result has prompted the investigation of more general notions of state transfer beyond vertex states,
such as pair (plus) PST
che1
;
jia
;
ojha1
;
pal10
,
s
-pair PST
kim
, PST between real pure states
god8
;
god7
;
pal11
.

A relaxation to vertex PST known as
pretty good state transfer (PGST)
was introduced in
god1
;
vin
. Extension of this notion to more general states, including pair states and real pure states, have also been investigated

;
pal11
;
ojha1
;
pal10
. A graph
G
is said to exhibit

between two linearly independent real pure states
{\bf u}
and

if there exists a sequence of real number

and a complex number
\gamma
of unit modulus such that

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right){\bf u}=\gamma{\bf v}.

(2)

As in vertex PST,

shows that plus PST with respect to the adjacency, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices is a rare phenomenon. This motivates us to investigate plus PGST in graphs. In particular, we study plus PGST in cycles and their complements.

Fractional revival (FR)
is another generalization of PST, which is relevant for quantum entanglement generation. FR occurs whenever a continuous-time quantum walk maps

In a path

In this work, we consider the initial state to be a real pure state. Let

{\bf u}
and
{\bf v}
be two linearly independent states in a graph

The graph

is said to exhibit

from

to
{\bf v}

at time

if there exist complex scalars
\alpha,\beta
with

U_{M(G)}(\tau){\bf u}=\alpha{\bf u}+\beta{\bf v}.

(3)

In this case, we also say that

to

at

In particular, if

and

If

A non-trivial relation between FR from a vertex state and a pair state is established in

A relaxation to FR called as

We consider PGFR from a real pure state

to

where

and

are linearly independent.
A graph

is said to exhibit PGFR from
{\bf u}
to
{\bf v}
if there is a sequence of time

such that for some

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right){\bf u}=\alpha{\bf u}+\beta{\bf v}.

Remark 1
.

If
G
is a regular graph, then the transition matrices governed by the adjacency matrix, the Laplacian matrix, or the signless Laplacian matrix differ only by a global phase. Consequently, the state transfer
properties are identical under all such choices of

Throughout the paper,
\mathbf{1}
denotes the all-ones vector,

the identity matrix,

the all-ones square matrix, and

The article is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we include the effect of certain graph automorphisms on the existence of PGST from plus and vertex states. In Section
3
, we study FR from a real pure state in the complement of a graph with respect to the adjacency, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices. Section
4

2
Algebraic properties

An
automorphism

f
of a graph
G
is a bijection on the vertex set
V(G)
such that vertices

a
and
b
are adjacent in
G
if and only if
f(a)
and
f(b)
are adjacent in
G.

If
P
is the permutation matrix corresponding to the automorphism
f,
then
P
commutes with
M(G).
Since the transition matrix
U_{M(G)}(t)
is a polynomoal in
M(G),
the matrix
P
commutes with
U_{M(G)}(t)
as well. The automorphism
f
is said to
fix
a vertex
a
in
G
if
P{\mathbf{e}}_{a}={\mathbf{e}}_{a}.

In the following, we observe that the existence of certain automorphisms in a graph
G
with plus PGST guarantees the existence of pair PGST in
G.

Theorem 1
.

Let a graph
G
admit pretty good plus state transfer between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right).
Then the graph
G
admits pretty good pair state transfer if there exists an automorphism of
G
with permutation matrix

P
satisfying one of the following conditions.

1.

P{\mathbf{e}}_{a}={\mathbf{e}}_{a}
and
P{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\neq{\mathbf{e}}_{b},

2.

P{\mathbf{e}}_{a}={\mathbf{e}}_{b}
and
P{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\neq{\mathbf{e}}_{a}.

Proof.

Suppose the graph
G
admits plus PGST between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right).
Then by (
2
), we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)=\gamma\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right).

(4)

Suppose condition-1 holds. Then
multiplying
P
on both sides of (
4
) and subtracting the resulting equation from (
4
), yields

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right)\left({\mathbf{e}}_{b}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)=\gamma\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{c}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right).

(5)

Since
U_{M(G)}(t)
is unitary, (
5
) implies that
\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{b}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)\right\|=\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{c}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right)\right\|.
This equality is possible only if
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{c}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right)
represents a pair state. Hence,
G
admits pair PGST.
Similarly, if condition-2 holds, an analogous argument shows that

G
admits pair PGST.
∎

Next, we observe that certain graph automorphisms prevent the existence of PGST between a vertex state and a plus state.

Theorem 2
.

Let
a,b
and
c
be vertices of a graph
G.
If there exists an automorphism of
G
that fixes
b
but not
a,
then there is no pretty good state transfer between

{\mathbf{e}}_{a}
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{b}+{\mathbf{e}}_{c}\right).

Proof.

Suppose
G
admits PGST between
{\mathbf{e}}_{a}
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{b}+{\mathbf{e}}_{c}\right).
Then by (
2
), we have

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right){\mathbf{e}}_{a}=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{b}+{\mathbf{e}}_{c}\right).

(6)

Let
P
be the permutation matrix of the automorphism of
G
that fixes
b
but not
a.
Multiplying
P
on both sides of (
6
) and subtracting the resulting equation from (
6
), gives

\lim_{k\to\infty}U_{M(G)}\left(t_{k}\right)({\mathbf{e}}_{a}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{a})=\frac{\gamma}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{c}\right),

which is a contradiction as
\left\|{\mathbf{e}}_{a}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{a}\right\|\neq\left\|\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}-P{\mathbf{e}}_{c}\right)\right\|.

Hence, the result follows.
∎

3
State transfer in graph complement

The
complement
of a graph
G,
denoted by
\overline{G},
is the graph on the vertex set of
G,
where two distinct vertices are adjacent in
\overline{G}
if and only if they are not adjacent in
G.

The matrix
M(\overline{G})
corresponding to the adjacency, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices of the complement
\overline{G}
on
n
vertices is given by

M(\overline{G})=\delta J+\zeta I-M(G),

where
\delta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1,&\mbox{if}&M=L\\
1,&\mbox{if}&M\in\{A,Q\},\end{array}\right.

and
\zeta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1,&\mbox{if}&M=A,\\
n,&\mbox{if}&M=L,\\
n-2,&\mbox{if}&M=Q.\end{array}\right.

The preservation of vertex PST under graph complementation was established in
(
god1
, Lemma 15.3)
and
(
alv
, Theorem 2)
for the adjacency matrix of regular graphs and for the Laplacian matrix, respectively. Moreover, the preservation of Laplacian FR between two vertices under graph complementation was shown in
(
chan3
, Theorem 9)
. The observations extend to FR from real pure states as well. We include the proof for convenience.

Theorem 3
.

Let
\mathbf{1}
be an eigenvector of a graph
G
on
n
vertices. If
G
exhibits fractional revival from a state
{\bf u}
to
{\bf v}
at time
\tau
with respect to
M(G)
and
n\tau\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z},
then the complement
\overline{G}
exhibits fractional revival from
{\bf u}
to
{\bf v}
relative to
M(\overline{G}).

Proof.

Since
\mathbf{1}
is an eigenvector of
G
and
J=\mathbf{1}\mathbf{1}^{T},
it follows that
M(G)
commutes with
J.
Therefore, for all
t\in\mathbb{R},

U_{M(\overline{G})}(t)=\exp{(itM(\overline{G}))=\exp{(it\zeta)}\exp{(it\delta J)}}\exp{(-itM(G))}.

The spectral decomposition of
J
gives

\exp{(it\delta J)}=[\exp{(itn\delta)}-1]\frac{1}{n}J+I.

If
n\tau\in 2\pi\mathbb{Z},
then we obtain
U_{M(\overline{G})}(-\tau){\bf u}=\exp{(-i\tau\zeta)}U_{M(G)}(\tau){\bf u},
and the result follows.
∎

.

The observation in Theorem

Theorem 4
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices. For a vector
{\bf u}\in\mathbb{R}^{n},

if
\mathbf{1}
is orthogonal to the set of vectors
\left\{{\bf u},M(G){\bf u},\ldots,M(G)^{n-1}{\bf u}\right\},
then

U_{M(\overline{G})}(t){\bf u}=\exp{(it\zeta)}U_{M(G)}(-t){\bf u},

where

and

are the transition matrices of

\overline{G}
, respectively, and the constant
\zeta
is as defined earlier for

Proof.

Since
G
is a graph of order

the matrix
M(G)^{k}
can be expressed as a linear combination of

for all non-negative integers

As
\mathbf{1}
is orthogonal to the subspace generated by
\left\{{\bf u},M(G){\bf u},\ldots,M(G)^{n-1}{\bf u}\right\},
it follows that
JM(G)^{k}{\bf u}=\mathbf{0}.
Now the result follows from
M(\overline{G})^{k}{\bf u}=(\delta J+\zeta I-M(G))^{k}{\bf u}=(\zeta I-M(G))^{k}{\bf u}.

∎

4
Double covers

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be graphs on the same vertex set. The graph
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
is defined by the adjacency matrix

\begin{bmatrix}A(X_{1})&A(X_{2})\\
A(X_{2})&A(X_{1})\end{bmatrix}.

If
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
have disjoint edge sets, then
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
is called a double cover of the graph with adjacency matrix
A(X_{1})+A(X_{2}).

Let
D(X_{1})
and
D(X_{2})
be the degree matrices of graphs
X_{1}
and
X_{2},
respectively. Consider
D=D(X_{1})+D(X_{2}).
Then the matrix
M\in\{A,L,Q\}
associated with the graph
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
becomes

M(X_{1}\ltimes X_{2})=\begin{bmatrix}\eta D+\delta A(X_{1})&\delta A(X_{2})\\
\delta A(X_{2})&\eta D+\delta A(X_{1})\end{bmatrix},\quad\text{where}

\eta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0,&\mbox{if}&M=A,\\
1,&\mbox{if}&M\in\{L,Q\},\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\delta=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}-1,&\mbox{if}&M=L,\\
1,&\mbox{if}&M\in\{A,Q\}.\end{array}\right.

We extend
(
cou
, Lemma 5.1)
to obtain a unified expression for the transition matrix of
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
with respect to the adjacency, Laplacian, and signless Laplacian matrices.

Theorem 5
.

Let
X_{1}
and
X_{2}
be graphs on the same vertex set
V.

The transition matrix of
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
is given by

U_{M({X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}})}(t)=\frac{1}{2}\begin{bmatrix}U_{M(G_{+})}(t)+U_{M(G_{-})}(t)&U_{M(G_{+})}(t)-U_{M(G_{-})}(t)\\
U_{M(G_{+})}(t)-U_{M(G_{-})}(t)&U_{M(G_{+})}(t)+U_{M(G_{-})}(t)\end{bmatrix},

where
t\in\mathbb{R},
and
M(G_{\pm})=\eta D+\delta(A(X_{1})\pm A(X_{2})).

Proof.

Let
A(K_{2})
be the adjacency matrix of a complete graph on two vertices, then the matrix
M\in\{A,L,Q\}
associated with the graph
X_{1}\ltimes X_{2}
can be written as

M(X_{1}\ltimes X_{2})=I_{2}\otimes(\eta D+\delta A(X_{1}))+A(K_{2})\otimes(\delta A(X_{2})).

Note that
I_{2}
and
A(K_{2})
commute and can be simultaneously diagonalized by the matrix

H=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}1&1\\
1&-1\end{bmatrix}.

Since
(H\otimes I_{n})^{-1}=H\otimes I_{n},
it follows from

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{\left(it_{k}W\right)}=-1.

This contradicts (

) as

implying no plus PGST between

and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{2}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b+\frac{n}{2}}\right)
in

.
∎

Combining Lemma

and Lemma

Theorem 9
.

A cycle
C_{n}
admits pretty good plus state transfer if and only if

with

Moreover, every plus state in

exhibits pretty good plus state transfer.

Next, we investigate plus PGST in
\overline{C_{n}}
whenever

Lemma 7
.

There is no pretty good plus state transfer in the complement
\overline{C_{n}},
whenever
n=mp,
where

is a positive integer and

is an odd prime.

Proof.

If
m
is odd, then the result follows from Lemma
1
. Thus, assume that
m
is even.
The spectral decomposition of the adjacency matrix of

\overline{C_{n}}
gives
A(\overline{C_{n}})=\displaystyle\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\mu_{l}E_{\mu_{l}},
where

E_{\mu_{l}}=\frac{1}{n}{\bf x}_{l}{\bf x}_{l}^{*},
and
{\bf x}_{l}(j)=\omega_{n}^{lj},
for
0\leq j\leq n-1,
as given in (
12
).

Case I

(a-b\neq\frac{n}{2})
: If
\overline{C_{n}}
admits plus PGST from
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right),
where
a
and

b
are non-antipodal vertices, then by Lemma

, it occurs between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a+\frac{n}{2}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b+\frac{n}{2}}\right).
Without loss of generality, let
a=0
and
0<b<\frac{n}{2}.

Let
P
be the permutation matrix corresponding to the automorphism of
\overline{C_{n}}
defined by
P{\mathbf{e}}_{j}={\mathbf{e}}_{j+\frac{n}{2}}.
Using a similar approach as in the proof of Lemma
6
, we obtain

E_{\mu_{l}}(e_{0}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b})=(-1)^{l}E_{\mu_{l}}\left(e_{\frac{n}{2}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b+\frac{n}{2}}\right).

(16)

For an odd prime
p,
using (
14
) and the fact that
\lambda_{0}=n-\mu_{0}-1,
and
\lambda_{l}=-1-\mu_{l},
for
1\leq l\leq n-1,
we have

(\mu_{2}-\mu_{1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\mu_{mr+2}-\mu_{mr+1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\mu_{mr-2}-\mu_{mr-1})=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}2p,&\mbox{if}&m=2,\\
0,&\mbox{if}&m\geq 3.\end{array}\right.

(17)

If
\overline{C_{n}}
admits plus PGST between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{0}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{2}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b+\frac{n}{2}}\right),
then a similar argument as in the proof of Lemma
6
gives
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{(it_{k}\mu_{l})}=(-1)^{l}\gamma.

It follows that

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{(it_{k}(\mu_{l+1}-\mu_{l}))}=-1.
Let
W^{\prime}
be the expression on the left-hand side of (
17
). Then

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{\left(it_{k}W^{\prime}\right)}=-1,
which contradicts (
17
) for
m\geq 3
as
W^{\prime}=0.

Next we consider the case
m=2.
Since
\mu_{p}=1,
it follows that
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{(it_{k})}=-\gamma.
Since
W^{\prime}=2p,
we have
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp(it_{k}2p)=-1,
and consequently
\gamma^{2p}=-1.
For the eigenvalues
\mu_{1}
and
\mu_{p-1},
we obtain
\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp(it_{k}(\mu_{1}+\mu_{p-1}))=-\gamma^{2}.
Since
\mu_{1}+\mu_{p-1}=-2,
we have
\gamma^{4}=-1,
which is a contradiction to
\gamma^{2p}=-1
as
p
is an odd prime.

Case II

(a-b=\frac{n}{2})
: If
\overline{C_{n}}
admits plus PGST from
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right),
where
a
and
b
are antipodal vertices, then by Lemma

,
n
is divisible by

and plus PGST occurs between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a+\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b+\frac{n}{4}}\right).
Without loss of generality, let

and

Let
P^{\prime}
be the permutation matrix corresponding to the automorphism of
\overline{C_{n}}
defined by
P^{\prime}{\mathbf{e}}_{j}={\mathbf{e}}_{j+\frac{n}{4}}.

Therefore, we have

(P^{\prime}{\bf x}_{l})(j)={\bf x}_{l}\left(j+\frac{n}{4}\right)=\omega_{n}^{l\left(j+\frac{n}{4}\right)}=(i)^{l}{\bf x}_{l}(j).

This shows that
P^{\prime}{\bf x}_{l}=(i)^{l}{\bf x}_{l},
and consequently,
P^{\prime}E_{\mu_{l}}=(i)^{l}E_{\mu_{l}}.

Since
P^{\prime T}=P^{\prime-1}
and
{\bf x}_{l}^{*}P^{\prime T}=(i)^{-l}{\bf x}_{l}^{*},
we have
{\bf x}_{l}^{*}P^{\prime}=(i)^{l}{\bf x}_{l}^{*}.
It follows that
P^{\prime}E_{\mu_{l}}=E_{\mu_{l}}P^{\prime}
and

E_{\mu_{l}}\left(e_{0}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)=P^{\prime}E_{\mu_{l}}\left(e_{\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{3n}{4}}\right)=(i)^{l}E_{\mu_{l}}\left(e_{\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{3n}{4}}\right).

(18)

From the proof of
(
pal4
, Lemma 11)
, we observe that for an odd prime
p,

1+2\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\cos{\left(\frac{2r\pi}{p}\right)}=0.

(19)

Multiplying both sides of (

) by

and

yields

\lambda_{1}+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lambda_{mr+1}+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lambda_{mr-1}=0,\quad\text{and}\quad\lambda_{3}+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lambda_{mr+3}+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}\lambda_{mr-3}=0,

respectively. Consequently, we have

(\lambda_{3}-\lambda_{1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\lambda_{mr+3}-\lambda_{mr+1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\lambda_{mr-3}-\lambda_{mr-1})=0.

(20)

Substituting
\lambda_{l}=-1-\mu_{l}
in (

), we obtain

(\mu_{3}-\mu_{1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\mu_{mr+3}-\mu_{mr+1})+\sum_{r=1}^{\frac{p-1}{2}}(\mu_{mr-3}-\mu_{mr-1})=0.

(21)

Suppose
\overline{C_{n}}
admits plus PGST between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{0}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{3n}{4}}\right),
then there exists a sequence

and a complex number

of unit modulus such that

\gamma\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}E_{\mu_{l}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{3n}{4}}\right)=\gamma\left({\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{4}}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{3n}{4}}\right)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\sum_{l=0}^{n-1}\exp{(it_{k}\mu_{l})}E_{\mu_{l}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{0}+{\mathbf{e}}_{\frac{n}{2}}\right).

(22)

Multiplying both sides of (

) by

and using (

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{(it_{k}\mu_{l})}=(-i)^{l}\gamma.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\lim_{k\to\infty}\exp{(it_{k}(\mu_{l+2}-\mu_{l}))}=-1.
Denoting the term on the left-hand side of (

) as

we arrive at a contradiction

.

and

6
PGST in weighted paths with potential

Let
P_{n}
be a path on
n
vertices with vertex set
\{1,2,\ldots,n\},
where two vertices
j
and
k
are adjacent if and only if
|j-k|=1.

We study the existence of vertex PGST in certain weighted paths with potential using equitable partitions. A partition
\Pi
of the vertex set of a graph
G
with disjoint cells
V_{1},V_{2},\ldots,V_{d}
is said to be
equitable
if each vertex in
V_{j}
is adjacent to exactly
c_{jk}
vertices in
V_{k},
where

c_{jk}
is a constant depending only on the cells
V_{j}
and
V_{k}
.
The graph with the
d
cells of
\Pi
as its vertices having adjacency matrix
A(G/\Pi),
where
A(G/\Pi)_{j,k}=\sqrt{c_{jk}c_{kj}},
is called the
symmetrized quotient graph
of
G
over
\Pi,
and is denoted by
G/\Pi.
A relation between vertex PGST in
G/\Pi
and plus PGST in
G
is observed in
(
god8
, Remark 5)
, which implies that for
V_{1}=\{a,b\}
and
V_{2}=\{c,d\},
PGST occurs between
V_{1}
and
V_{2}
in
G/\Pi
if and only if PGST occurs between
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{a}+{\mathbf{e}}_{b}\right)
and
\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left({\mathbf{e}}_{c}+{\mathbf{e}}_{d}\right)
in
G.

It is well known that PST occurs only on the paths
P_{2}
and
P_{3}
with respect to the adjacency matrix
god1
. This raises a question of whether PST could be induced between the endpoints of
a path of arbitrary length by placing a suitable potential on the endpoints. Kempton et al.
kem1
showed that there is no potential that induces PST between the end
points of a path of length at least
4.
However, for a path
P_{n}
of any length, there is some choice of potential such that by
placing the potential on each endpoint of
P_{n},
there is PGST between the endpoints
kem2
.
Further, it is observed in
kirk5
that for paths on more than two vertices, the set of weights
w\geq 1
assigned to the end vertices, for which the resulting path does not admit PGST, forms a dense subset of
[1,\infty).

Here, we present a few observations on the existence of PGST on weighted paths with potentials.
