The Yang-Mills equation near instanton-anti-instanton configurations
Alex Waldron, Hao Yin

Abstract
. We study the question of whether a sequence of non-instanton Yang-Mills connections can limit to a bubbling configuration composed only of instantons. In the case that the Uhlenbeck limit and the bubbles are of opposite charge, we determine an obstruction coming from deformations of the Uhlenbeck limit. As an application, we prove that instantons are the only solutions of the SU ⁡ ( 2 ) {\mathrm{SU}}(2) Yang-Mills equation on ℝ 4 {\mathbb{R}}^{4} with energy less than 4 ​ π 2 ​ ( | κ | + 2 ) + ε κ , 4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa|+2\right)+{\varepsilon}_{\kappa}, where κ \kappa is the charge. We also prove discreteness of the energy spectrum on the trivial SU ⁡ ( 2 ) {\mathrm{SU}}(2) -bundle in the range [ 0 , 16 ​ π 2 ) . \left[0,16\pi^{2}\right).



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Background

Let
(M,g)
be a closed, oriented Riemannian manifold of dimension four and
E\to M
a vector bundle equipped with a metric.
For metric-compatible connections
A
on
E,
we define the Yang-Mills functional

\mathcal{YM}(A)=\frac{1}{2}\int_{M}\left|F_{A}\right|^{2}dV_{g}.

Its critical points satisfy the Yang-Mills equation

(1.1)

D_{A}^{*}F_{A}=0

and are referred to as Yang-Mills connections.

Let
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots
be a sequence of Yang-Mills connections on
E\to M
with uniformly bounded Yang-Mills energy. The renowned compactness theory of Uhlenbeck
[
25
,
26
]
provides an essentially complete picture of the limiting behavior of such a sequence in dimension four: after passing to a subsequence and changing gauges,
A_{k}
converges smoothly away from a finite set of points
S=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m}
to a limiting Yang-Mills connection
A_{\infty}
on a possibly different bundle
E_{\infty}\to M.

Moreover, after rescaling near each point
z_{i}
and applying Uhlenbeck’s theorems inductively, one can obtain a “bubble tree” of Yang-Mills connections
\{B_{i,j}\}_{i=1,}^{m}{}_{j=1}^{n_{i}}

accounting for all of the concentrated energy. See §
1.2
and Theorem
3.1
below for a statement of the standard bubble-tree compactness theory.

This paper is concerned with the following “converse” question to Uhlenbeck’s theory.

Question.
Given a Yang-Mills connection
A_{\infty}
on
E_{\infty}\to M,
a finite collection of points
S=\{z_{i}\}_{i=1}^{m},
and a collection of bubble Yang-Mills connections
\{B_{i,j}\},
does there exist a sequence of smooth Yang-Mills connections
A_{1},A_{2},\ldots
on
E\to M
that converges to this limit in the bubble-tree sense?

Our goal is to prove negative results on this question generalizing those recently obtained by the second-named author
[
28
]
. In other words, we will show that certain types of bubbling configurations cannot arise as Uhlenbeck limits of smooth solutions to the Yang-Mills equations.

Recall that in dimension four, the Hodge star operator decomposes the space of 2-forms into the direct sum of self-dual (SD) and anti-self-dual (ASD) forms. A connection
A
is called (A)SD if its curvature
F_{A}
is (A)SD, and in either case,
A
is called an instanton. These are special Yang-Mills connections in the sense that their Yang-Mills energy achieves the minimum value allowed by the topology of the bundle.

Because of their significance both in physics and in Donaldson theory, instantons have been studied intensively over the past five decades. In particular, the gluing problem for the ASD equations was the focus of the foundational work of Taubes, the main thrust of which is as follows. Supposing that
A_{\infty}
and
\{B_{i,j}\}
are all ASD, the answer to the above question is positive if either:
A_{\infty}
is flat and
M
is negative-definite and simply-connected
[
20
]
;
m
is sufficiently large and the points
z_{i}
are chosen judiciously
[
22
]
; or,
M
has a generic metric and
A_{\infty}
is not flat, with structure group
G={\mathrm{SU}}(2)
(the last result combines Taubes’s work with the so-called Freed-Uhlenbeck Theorem, see
[
10
]
or
[
5
, Th. 3.17]
).

Certain negative results on the above question for the ASD equations have also appeared in Taubes’s and Donaldson’s work
[
22
,
6
]
. These take the form of
obstructions
to gluing together a given ASD bubble configuration to obtain a smooth ASD connection, and are necessary to establish partial compactness for certain moduli spaces of ASD instantons.

The gluing problem for the full Yang-Mills equation (
1.1
) has been studied much less than the gluing problem for the ASD equation. However, another thread of Taubes’s work is indirectly concerned with the above question, in that it involves analyzing neighborhoods of bubble-tree configurations from a Morse-theoretic point of view
[
23
]
. Taubes’s most successful results in this direction
[
21
,
24
]
required analyzing configurations in which both
A_{\infty}
and the bubbles are instantons but the bubbles have the
opposite
charge from
A_{\infty}
. We continue this thread in the present paper.

1.2.
Statement of results

To state our main obstruction, we need the following details of the standard bubbling analysis (see §
3
).

Let
z_{i}\in S
be a bubble point and
r_{i}>0
a sufficiently small radius. For each
x\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i}),
we use parallel transport along radial geodesics to identify
(T_{x}M,g_{x})
isometrically with
(T_{z_{i}}M,g_{z_{i}}).
We consider the exponential map

\exp_{x}:T_{z_{i}}M\cong T_{x}M\to M

as a map from the fixed space
T_{z_{i}}M
into
M
with
\exp_{x}(0)=x.

Let
x^{i}_{k}\in B_{r_{i}}(z_{i})
and
0<\lambda^{i}_{k}<r_{i}
be any sequence of points and scales such that
x^{i}_{k}\to z_{i}
and
\lambda^{i}_{k}\searrow 0
as
k\to\infty
, and define

(1.2)

\begin{split}\varphi_{k}:B_{\frac{r_{i}}{\lambda_{k}}}(0)\subset T_{z_{i}}M&\to B_{2r_{i}}(z_{i})\subset M\\
\varphi_{k}(y)&=\exp_{x_{k}}(\lambda_{k}y).\end{split}

We may pass to a subsequence such that the connections
\varphi_{k}^{*}A_{k}
converge in the Uhlenbeck sense to a Yang-Mills connection on
T_{z_{i}}M.
It is possible to choose
x^{i}_{k}
and
\lambda^{i}_{k}
corresponding to an “outer” bubble scale—see Theorem
3.1
—and we denote the corresponding (possibly flat) Uhlenbeck limit by
B_{i,1}.

According to Uhlenbeck’s removable singularity theorem,
B_{i,1}
extends to a Yang-Mills connection on a bundle
E_{i,1}
over the one-point compactification:

E_{i,1}\to\widehat{T_{z_{i}}M}\cong S^{4}.

Moreover, the converging connections
A_{k}
naturally induce an isomorphism

\hat{\rho}_{i}:(E_{i,1})_{\infty}\to(E_{\infty})_{z_{i}}

which we call the “attaching map,” see Definition/Lemma
3.2
. This procedure can be continued to obtain a full bubble tree
\{B_{i,j}\}
at
z_{i}.

Let
\iota
denote inversion in the unit sphere in
T_{z_{i}}M.

Its extension
\hat{\iota}
to
\widehat{T_{z_{i}}M}
is reflection across the equator, whose derivative

d\hat{\iota}_{0}:T_{0}\widehat{T_{z_{i}}M}\cong T_{z_{i}}M\longrightarrow T_{\infty}\widehat{T_{z_{i}}M}

is an orientation-reversing linear isometry. Combined with
\hat{\rho}_{i},

d\hat{\iota}_{0}^{*}
induces a linear isometry

d\hat{\iota}_{0}^{*}\otimes\hat{\rho}_{i}:\Lambda^{2,+}T_{\infty}^{*}\widehat{T_{z_{i}}M}\otimes{\text{End}}(E_{i,1})_{\infty}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{2,-}T^{*}_{z_{i}}M\otimes{\text{End}}(E_{\infty})_{z_{i}},

which we will again denote by
\hat{\rho}_{i}.
After applying this map, the self-dual curvature of
B_{i,1}
at
\infty
becomes an
{\text{End}}(E_{\infty})_{z_{i}}
-valued self-dual 2-form at
z_{i}.

Theorem 1.1
.

Assume that the Uhlenbeck limit
A_{\infty}
is anti-self-dual and unobstructed, i.e.
H^{2,+}_{A_{\infty}}=\left\{0\right\},
and that all bubble connections are anti-self-dual except possibly at
z_{1}\in S.

For each
a\in{\rm Ker}D_{A_{\infty}}^{+},
we must have

(1.3)

\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right),\left(D^{-}_{A_{\infty}}a\right)(z_{1})\right\rangle=0.

A similar result holds after reversing the roles of SD and ASD forms.

Theorem
1.1
can be compared with recent work of Ozuch
[
15
]
and LeBrun-Ozuch
[
13
]
, where deformations of the limiting orbifold lead to obstructions to the existence of smooth bubbling sequences of noncollapsed 4D Einstein metrics.

An important application of the obstruction described in Theorem
1.1
is to rule out certain bubbling configurations for sequences of Yang-Mills connections on the 4-sphere. From
[
28
]
, we know that there is no sequence of
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-Yang-Mills connections on
S^{4}
that splits into a 1-instanton and a 1-anti-instanton. (A further comparison between that obstruction and (
1.3
) is given in Section
8
.) Using the obstruction (
1.3
), we are able to prove the following generalization.

Theorem 1.2
.

Let
\left\{A_{k}\right\}_{k=1}^{\infty}
be an Uhlenbeck-convergent sequence of
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-Yang-Mills connections on
S^{4}
. Let
\mathcal{B}=\{A_{\infty},B_{i,j}\},

and suppose that
\mathcal{B}
contains at least two non-flat connections. It is impossible that
\mathcal{B}
contains a single SD instanton of unit charge while the rest are ASD.

In addition to the obstruction of Theorem
1.1
, the proof of Theorem
1.2
needs the explicit formula for instantons on
S^{4}
as given in Atiyah’s book
[
1
]
for a good choice of the deformation
a
. Moreover, the proof must deal with a general bubble tree, which may involve so-called “ghost” bubbles.

In
[
12
]
, the authors proved that: on an
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-bundle of charge
\kappa
on
S^{4}
, any Yang-Mills connection with energy strictly less than
4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa|+2\right)
is an instanton (see
[
4
, Remark 2.1]
for a comparison of normalizations). As a corollary of Theorem
1.2
, we can obtain the following improvement.

Corollary 1.3
.

On an
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-bundle of charge
\kappa
on the 4-sphere, there exists
{\varepsilon}_{\kappa}>0
such that any Yang-Mills connection with energy less than
4\pi^{2}\left(|\kappa|+2\right)+{\varepsilon}_{\kappa}
is an instanton.

In the case of the trivial bundle, by combining Theorem
1.2
with the standard Łojasiewicz inequality for a Yang-Mills connection, we can also prove the following result.

Corollary 1.4
.

The set of critical values less than
16\pi^{2}
of the Yang-Mills functional on the trivial
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-bundle over
S^{4}
is discrete.

We note that discreteness of the energy spectrum of the Yang-Mills functional on any compact Riemannian 4-manifold (as on
S^{4}/{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
) is a well-known open problem. We also note that the 2-equivariant Yang-Mills connection constructed by Sibner, Sibner, and Uhlenbeck
[
19
]
has energy between
8\pi^{2}
and
16\pi^{2}.
It is reasonable to conjecture that this is the only nonzero critical value below
16\pi^{2}
on the trivial
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
-bundle.

In the situation of (

can then be accomplished using the following elementary formula.

Lemma 1.6
.

Let
A
be a connection on
E
and
a\in\Omega^{1}\left({\mathfrak{g}}_{E}\right).
For
\Omega\Subset M,
we have

(1.6)

\int_{\partial\Omega}\operatorname{Tr}F_{A}^{+}\wedge a=\int_{\Omega}\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D_{A}^{*}F_{A},a\right\rangle-\left\langle F_{A}^{+},D_{A}^{+}a\right\rangle\,dV.

Proof.

We calculate

(1.7)

\begin{split}d(\operatorname{Tr}F_{A}^{+}\wedge a)&=\operatorname{Tr}\left(D_{A}F_{A}^{+}\wedge a+F_{A}^{+}\wedge D_{A}a\right).\end{split}

By the Bianchi identity, we have

2D_{A}F_{A}^{+}=D_{A}\left(F_{A}+*F_{A}\right)=D_{A}*F_{A}=*D_{A}^{*}F_{A}.

This gives

\begin{split}\operatorname{Tr}D_{A}F^{+}_{A}\wedge a&=-\operatorname{Tr}a\wedge D_{A}F^{+}_{A}\\
&=-\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{Tr}a\wedge*D_{A}^{*}F_{A}\\
&=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle a,D_{A}^{*}F_{A}\right\rangle.\end{split}

Returning to (
1.7
), we get

\begin{split}d(\operatorname{Tr}F_{A}^{+}\wedge a)&=\frac{1}{2}\left\langle D_{A}^{*}F_{A},a\right\rangle\,dV-\left\langle F_{A}^{+},D_{A}^{+}a\right\rangle dV.\end{split}

The result now follows by integrating over
\Omega
and applying Stokes’s Theorem.
∎

For the proof of Theorem
1.1
(§
5
), we take
\Omega=M\setminus\cup_{i}B(z_{i},R)
with

sufficiently small,

and

1.6
) is
O(\lambda_{k}^{2})
a priori while the RHS is
o(\lambda_{k}^{2}).
The leading-order term on the LHS must therefore vanish, implying (
1.3
).

2.
Refined curvature estimate on a cylinder

2.1.
Statement of results

Let

C=C_{T}=\left[-T,T\right]\times S^{3}.

We endow
C
with a metric of the form

(2.1)

\bar{g}=dt^{2}+h(t),

where
h(t)
is a
t
-dependent Riemannian metric on
S^{3}
and we assume

(2.2)

|h(t)-h_{round}|+|{\partial}_{t}h|+|\nabla_{\theta}h|\leq\alpha_{1}e^{-\kappa_{0}\left(t+T\right)}+\alpha_{2}e^{\kappa_{0}\left(t-T\right)}.

Here
0<\kappa_{0}\leq 2
and
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}\leq\alpha_{0},
a small constant which will depend only on
\kappa_{0}.

Note that by Gauss’s Lemma, any smooth metric on a ball takes this form (with
\kappa_{0}=2
and
\alpha_{1}=0
) after making the conformal change

\bar{g}=\frac{g}{r^{2}}

and setting
t=\log r.

Since the Yang-Mills equation in dimension four is conformally invariant, we are free to work on the cylinder instead of on an annulus. We shall also exploit the fact that
A
is Yang-Mills if and only if the self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of the curvature are individually harmonic, as a consequence of the Bianchi identity.

We will first give a simple proof of the following decay estimate which essentially dates back to Uhlenbeck
[
26
]
; see also Donaldson-Kronheimer
[
5
, Prop. 7.3.3]
, Råde
[
18
]
, and Groisser-Parker
[
11
]
. We follow the convention that constants appearing in statements of theorems are universal unless stated otherwise, while a constant appearing in the proof of the corresponding theorem is allowed to have the same dependence as in the statement. Constants will be allowed to increase from line to line during a proof.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
A
be any Yang-Mills connection on
C_{T+1}
with

\sup_{-T\leq t\leq T}\mathcal{YM}\left(\left[t-1,t+1\right]\times S^{3},A\right)\leq{\varepsilon}^{2}<{\varepsilon}^{2}_{0},

where
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
is a universal constant.
Let

{\varepsilon}_{1}=\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{L^{2}\left(\left[-T-1,-T+1\right]\times S^{3}\right)},\qquad{\varepsilon}_{2}=\|F_{A}^{+}\|_{L^{2}\left(\left[T-1,T+1\right]\times S^{3}\right)}.

We have

(2.3)

|F_{A}^{+}(t)|\leq C_{\eqref{F+estimate:estimate}}\left({\varepsilon}_{1}e^{-2(t+T)}+{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2(t-T)}\right).

A similar result holds for the anti-self-dual curvature.

Next, we wish to refine this result by isolating the leading-order terms. Write
*_{t}=*_{h(t)}
for the Hodge star operator on
S^{3}
defined by
h(t),
and
*_{\theta}
for the corresponding operator with

Notice that for a metric
\bar{g}
of the form (
2.1
) on
C,
any self-dual
2
-form
\omega
still takes the form

(2.4)

\omega=dt\wedge\alpha(t)+*_{t}\alpha(t)

for
\alpha(t)\in\Omega^{1}_{S^{3}}.

Now, observe that the operator

*_{\theta}d_{\theta}:\Omega_{cocl}^{1}(S^{3})\to\Omega_{cocl}^{1}(S^{3})

is self-adjoint and squares to the Hodge Laplacian, whose first eigenvalue on coclosed forms is
4.
The spectrum of
*_{\theta}d_{\theta}
on coclosed 1-forms therefore begins with
\pm 2
and has all eigenvalues of norm at least

In fact, by Folland
[

on coclosed 1-forms are
\pm m

and we denote the

\left|\tau\left(F_{A}^{+}\right)-(c+\lambda^{2}\iota^{*}(d))\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon_{1}\frac{\lambda^{3}}{|x|^{5}}+\varepsilon_{2}\frac{|x|}{r_{0}}+\left({\varepsilon}_{1}\left({\varepsilon}+C_{M}r_{0}^{2}\right)+{\varepsilon}_{2}{\varepsilon}\right)\frac{\lambda^{2}}{|x|^{2}r_{0}^{2}}\right)

on
U.

2.2.
First-order harmonic self-dual forms in cylindrical coordinates

Let
\omega
be a
constant
self-dual 2-form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}.
Viewed in cylindrical coordinates (
r=e^{t}
),
\omega
takes the form

(2.9)

\omega=e^{2t}\left(dt\wedge\alpha+*_{\theta}\alpha\right),

where
\alpha\in\Omega^{1}_{S^{3}}
is independent of
t.

We have

d\omega=0=e^{2t}\left(dt\wedge\left(-d_{\theta}\alpha+2*_{\theta}\alpha\right)+d_{\theta}*_{\theta}\alpha\right).

Since
*_{\theta}^{2}=1,
we see that
\alpha
is coclosed and belongs to
\Theta_{2}.

Conversely, given
\alpha\in\Theta_{2},
we may define a harmonic self-dual 2-form by (
2.9
). Changing back to
{\mathbb{R}}^{4},
this remains harmonic and is bounded at the origin, hence smooth. Since the coefficients are bounded and harmonic on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4},
they are constant.

The eigenspace
\Theta_{-2}
arises in the same way from the
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}
at
\infty,
where
r=e^{-t}.
Given
\beta\in\Theta_{-2},
we have a harmonic self-dual 2-form given by

(2.10)

\omega=e^{-2t}\left(dt\wedge\beta+*_{\theta}\beta\right).

Note that anti-self-dual harmonic 2-forms can be obtained by applying the inversion map
\iota:(t,\theta)\mapsto(-t,\theta).

2.3.
The equation in cylindrical coordinates

We will study the inhomogeneous linear equation

(2.11)

*d\omega=\eta

on
C,
where
\omega\in\Omega^{2,+}_{C}
is a self-dual 2-form,
\eta\in\Omega^{1}_{C}
is a 1-form, and
*=*_{\bar{g}}
for a metric
\bar{g}
of the form (
2.1
).
A good reference for first-order linear analysis on cylinders is Donaldson
[
7
, Ch. 3]
, although the estimates there are not directly relevant to (
2.11
).

For a self-dual form
\omega
as in (
2.4
),
we calculate

d\omega=dt\wedge\left(-d_{\theta}\alpha+*_{t}\frac{{\partial}\alpha}{{\partial}t}+\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}*_{t}\right)\alpha\right)+d_{\theta}*_{t}\alpha

and

\begin{split}*d\omega&=\frac{{\partial}\alpha}{{\partial}t}-*_{t}d_{\theta}\alpha+*_{t}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}*_{t}\right)\alpha-dt*_{t}d_{\theta}*_{t}\alpha\\
&=\frac{{\partial}\alpha}{{\partial}t}-*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha+d_{\theta}^{*}\alpha\,dt+E_{1}+E_{2}dt,\end{split}

where

E_{1}=*_{t}\left(\frac{\partial}{\partial t}*_{t}\right)\alpha+(*_{\theta}-*_{t})d_{\theta}\alpha

and

E_{2}=\left(d_{t}^{*}-d_{\theta}^{*}\right)\alpha.

We split
\alpha
into closed and coclosed parts on
S^{3}
with respect to the round metric:

\alpha=\alpha_{cocl}+\alpha_{cl}.

Also write

(2.12)

\begin{split}\eta&=\beta+\rho dt,\\
\tilde{\beta}&=\beta-E_{1},\\
\tilde{\rho}&=\rho-E_{2}.\end{split}

Then the equation (
2.11
) is equivalent to the following system of three equations:

(2.13)

\frac{{\partial}\alpha_{cocl}}{{\partial}t}-*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha_{cocl}=\tilde{\beta}_{cocl},

(2.14)

\frac{{\partial}\alpha_{cl}}{{\partial}t}=\tilde{\beta}_{cl},

and

(2.15)

d_{\theta}^{*}\alpha_{cl}=\tilde{\rho}.

Note that (
2.14
) and (
2.15
) are overdetermined.

We further decompose

\alpha_{cocl}=\alpha_{+}+\alpha_{-}

according to the positive and negative eigenspaces of
*_{\theta}d_{\theta}
on coclosed forms. We have

(2.16)

\int_{S^{3}}\left\langle\alpha_{+},*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha_{+}\right\rangle\geq 2\|\alpha_{+}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S^{3})},\qquad\int_{S^{3}}\left\langle\alpha_{-},*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha_{-}\right\rangle\leq-2\|\alpha_{-}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(S^{3})}.

Write
\alpha_{\pm m}\in\Theta_{\pm m}
for the
\pm m
-th eigenmode of
\alpha,
and

\alpha_{\geq m}(t)=\sum_{i=m}^{\infty}\left(\alpha_{i}(t)+\alpha_{-i}(t)\right).

2.4.
Estimates on the system (
2.13
-
2.15
)

The main point is to estimate the equation (
2.13
) for the coclosed part of
\alpha.
We shall henceforth abbreviate

\|\cdot\|_{L^{2}\left(S_{round}^{3}\right)}=\|\cdot\|.

Lemma 2.4
.

Fix
m\geq 2.

Let
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)
be
t
-dependent coclosed 1-forms on
S^{3}
with

(2.17)

\frac{{\partial}\alpha}{{\partial}t}-*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha=\beta.

(a) Let
\alpha^{h}(t)
denote the solution on the cylinder of the homogeneous equation

(2.18)

\frac{{\partial}\alpha^{h}}{{\partial}t}-*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha^{h}=0,\qquad\alpha^{h}_{+}(T)=\alpha_{+}(T),\qquad\alpha^{h}_{-}(-T)=\alpha_{-}(-T).

We then have

(2.19)

\|\alpha_{\geq m}(t)-\alpha_{\geq m}^{h}(t)\|\leq\int_{-T}^{T}\|\beta(s)\|\,e^{-m|t-s|}\,ds.

(b) For
t,t_{0}\in\left[-T,T\right],
we have

(2.20)

\|\alpha_{-m}(t)-e^{-m(t-t_{0})}\alpha_{-m}(t_{0})\|\leq\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\beta(s)\|\,e^{-m(t-s)}\,ds\right|

and

(2.21)

\|\alpha_{m}(t)-e^{m(t-t_{0})}\alpha_{m}(t_{0})\|\leq\left|\int_{t_{0}}^{t}\|\beta(s)\|\,e^{-m(s-t_{0})}\,ds\right|.

Proof.

To prove (
2.19
), replacing
\alpha
by
\alpha_{\geq m},
we may assume without loss of generality that
\alpha
is orthogonal to
\Theta_{i}
for
|i|<m.
We claim that

(2.22)

\|\alpha_{-}(t)-\alpha_{-}^{h}(t)\|\leq\int_{-T}^{t}\|\beta_{-}(s)\|e^{m(s-t)}\,ds

and

(2.23)

\|\alpha_{+}(t)-\alpha_{+}^{h}(t)\|\leq\int_{t}^{T}\|\beta_{+}(s)\|e^{m(t-s)}\,ds.

Further replacing
\alpha_{-}(t)
by
\alpha_{-}(t)-\alpha_{-}^{h}(t),
we may assume without loss of generality that
\alpha_{-}(-T)=0.

From (
2.17
) and (
2.16
), we have

(2.24)

\begin{split}\frac{d\|\alpha_{-}(t)\|}{dt}&=\frac{\int\left\langle\alpha_{-},-*_{\theta}d_{\theta}\alpha_{-}+\beta_{-}\right\rangle\,dV_{\theta}}{\|\alpha_{-}\|}\\
&\leq-m\|\alpha_{-}\|+\|\beta_{-}\|.\end{split}

We obtain

(2.25)

\begin{split}\frac{d\left(e^{mt}\|\alpha_{-}\|\right)}{dt}&\leq e^{mt}\|\beta_{-}\|.\end{split}

Integrating from
-T
to
t
yields the desired result (
2.22
). The estimate (
2.23
) is proved similarly, and the result (
2.19
) follows by combining (
2.22
-
2.23
).

To prove (
2.21
), note that on the
\pm m
’th eigenmode, (
2.25
) becomes the ODE

(2.26)

\begin{split}\frac{d\left(e^{\pm mt}\alpha_{\mp m}\right)}{dt}&=e^{\pm mt}\beta_{\mp m}.\end{split}

Integrating from
t_{0}
to
t
gives the result.
∎

Lemma 2.5
.

Let
\alpha(t)
and
\beta(t)
be coclosed forms solving (
2.17
) as above. Let
m\in\{2,3,\ldots\}
and fix constants

0\leq\gamma_{1}\leq\cdots\leq\gamma_{max}<m<\kappa_{1}\leq\kappa_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\kappa_{max}.

For nonnegative constants
A_{i}^{\pm},B_{j}^{\pm},
let

\mu(t)=\sum_{i}\left(A^{-}_{i}e^{-\kappa_{i}\left(t+T\right)}+A^{+}_{i}e^{\kappa_{i}\left(t-T\right)}\right)+\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t-T\right)}\right).

(a) Assume that

(2.27)

\begin{split}\|\beta(t)\|&\leq\mu(t).\end{split}

We then have

(2.28)

\begin{split}\|\alpha_{\geq m}(t)\|&\leq\|\alpha_{\geq m}(-T)\|e^{-m(t+T)}+\|\alpha_{\geq m}(T)\|e^{m(t-T)}\\
&\quad+C\left(\left(\sum_{i}A^{-}_{i}\right)e^{-m\left(t+T\right)}+\left(\sum_{i}A^{+}_{i}\right)e^{m\left(t-T\right)}+\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t-T\right)}\right)\right).\end{split}

Here
C
depends on
m,

\kappa_{i},
and
\gamma_{j}.

(b) There exist (
t
-independent) coclosed 1-forms

c_{\pm m}\in\Theta_{\pm m}

such that

(2.29)

\begin{split}\|\alpha_{\pm m}(t)-c_{\pm m}e^{\pm m(t\mp T)}\|&\leq C\mu(t).\end{split}

Here
C
again depends on
m,

\kappa_{i},
and
\gamma_{j}.

Proof.

It is easy to see that

\int_{-T}^{T}e^{\mp\kappa_{i}(s\pm T)}e^{-m|t-s|}\,ds\lesssim e^{\mp m\left(t\pm T\right)}

and

\int_{-T}^{T}e^{\mp\gamma_{j}(s\pm T)}e^{-m|t-s|}\,ds\lesssim e^{\mp\gamma_{j}\left(t\pm T\right)}.

Lemma
2.4
a
therefore gives

\|\alpha_{\geq m}(t)-\alpha_{\geq m}^{h}(t)\|\leq C\left(\left(\sum_{i}A^{-}_{i}\right)e^{-m\left(t+T\right)}+\left(\sum_{i}A^{+}_{i}\right)e^{m\left(t-T\right)}+\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t-T\right)}\right)\right),

which implies (
2.28
).

We prove (
2.29
) for
-m,
since the
+m
case is similar.

Choose
t_{0}\in\left[-T,T\right]
to be the least number such that

\sum_{i}A^{-}_{i}e^{-\kappa_{i}\left(t_{0}+T\right)}\leq\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t_{0}+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t_{0}-T\right)}\right)+\sum_{i}A^{+}_{i}e^{\kappa_{i}\left(t_{0}-T\right)}.

We then have

(2.30)

\mu(t)\leq 2\begin{cases}\sum_{i}A^{-}_{i}e^{-\kappa_{i}\left(t_{0}+T\right)}&t<t_{0}\\
\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t_{0}+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t_{0}-T\right)}\right)+\sum_{i}A^{+}_{i}e^{\kappa_{i}\left(t_{0}-T\right)}&t\geq t_{0}.\end{cases}

Now define

c_{-m}:=e^{m(t_{0}+T)}\alpha_{-m}(t_{0}).

To prove (
2.29
), it suffices to establish that

(2.31)

\|\alpha_{-m}(t)-e^{-m(t-t_{0})}\alpha_{-m}(t_{0})\|\lesssim\mu(t).

We use Lemma
2.4
b
on each term.

For
t<t_{0},
we have

\int_{t}^{t_{0}}e^{-\kappa_{i}\left(s+T\right)}e^{-m(t-s)}\,ds=e^{-\kappa_{i}T-mt}\int_{t}^{t_{0}}e^{(m-\kappa_{i})s}\,ds\lesssim e^{-\kappa_{i}(t+T)},

where we have used
\kappa_{i}>m.

Applying Lemma
2.4
b
in view of (
2.30
), we get (
2.31
) for
t<t_{0}.

For
t_{0}\leq t,
we calculate

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(s+T\right)}e^{-m(t-s)}\,ds=e^{\gamma_{j}T-mt}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(m-\gamma_{j})s}\,ds\lesssim e^{-\gamma_{j}(t+T)}.

Simliarly, we have

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(s-T\right)}e^{-m(t-s)}\,ds=e^{-\gamma_{j}T-mt}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(\gamma_{j}+m)s}\,ds\lesssim e^{\gamma_{j}(t-T)}.

and

\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{\kappa_{i}\left(s-T\right)}e^{-m(t-s)}\,ds=e^{-\kappa_{i}T-mt}\int_{t_{0}}^{t}e^{(\kappa_{i}+m)s}\,ds\lesssim e^{\kappa_{i}(t-T)}.

Applying Lemma
2.4
b
in view of (
2.30
), we get (
2.31
) for
t_{0}\leq t.
This completes the proof of (
2.31
), and with it (
2.29
), for
-m.
The proof for
+m
is similar.
∎

Given these estimates on the coclosed part, we can easily deduce the following estimate on

Proposition 2.6
.

and
\tilde{\rho}

(a) Assume that

\|\tilde{\beta}(t)\|+\|\tilde{\rho}(t)\|\leq\mu(t).

We then have

\begin{split}\|\alpha(t)\|&\leq\left(\|\alpha(-T)\|+C\sum_{i}A^{-}_{i}\right)e^{-2(t+T)}+\left(\|\alpha(T)\|+C\sum_{i}A^{+}_{i}\right)e^{2(t-T)}\\
&\quad+C\sum_{j}\left(B^{-}_{j}e^{-\gamma_{j}\left(t+T\right)}+B_{j}^{+}e^{\gamma_{j}\left(t-T\right)}\right).\end{split}

Here

and

(b) Suppose

Decompose

Moreover, there exist (

-independent) coclosed 1-forms

) that the closed part of

2.34
), and (

To prove (

2.5.
Split
{\varepsilon}
-regularity

We shall use the following well-known
{\varepsilon}
-regularity-type estimates repeatedly below.

Lemma 2.7
.

There exists a universal constant
{\varepsilon}_{0}>0
as follows.

(a) Let
A
be a Yang-Mills connection on
B_{r}(x)\subset M,
with
r
sufficiently small, and suppose that

(2.37)

\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x))}<{\varepsilon}_{0}

(with no assumption on
F^{-}_{A}
).
We then have

\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r/2}(x))}\leq\frac{C\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x))}}{r^{2}}.

(b) Let
A
be a Yang-Mills connection on
U=\left(t-1,t+1\right)\times S^{3}
with respect to a metric
\bar{g}
of the form (
2.1
), let
U^{\prime}=\left(t-\frac{1}{2},t+\frac{1}{2}\right)\times S^{3}.
Suppose that

(2.38)

\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(U)}<{\varepsilon}_{0}

(with no assumption on
F^{-}_{A}
).
We then have

\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{\infty}(U^{\prime})}\leq C\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(U\setminus U^{\prime})}.

Similar results hold for the anti-self-dual curvature.

Proof.

These estimates follow from the split Bochner formula

(2.39)

\nabla^{*}\nabla F^{+}=\mathrm{Rm}\#F^{+}+F^{+}\#F^{+}

and the standard
{\varepsilon}
-regularity argument, see
[
5
, p. 74]
or
[
26
, Theorem 3.5]
.
∎

Lemma 2.8
.

(a) Let
A
be as in Lemma
2.7
a
and suppose that the full curvature satisfies

\|F_{A}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r})}<{\varepsilon}_{0}.

We then have

\|\nabla_{A}F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{\infty}\left(B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x)\right)}\leq\frac{C\|F^{+}_{A}\|_{L^{2}(B_{r}(x)\setminus B_{\frac{r}{2}}(x))}}{r^{3}}.

(b) Let

and

we see that the full curvature

.

Proof of Theorem

.

We prove (
2.3
) simultaneously for the self-dual and anti-self-dual parts of the curvature, using the continuity method on

The result for

follows directly from Lemma
2.7
b.
To prove the theorem for general

in place of

To this end, assume (
2.3
) for both the SD and ASD curvature, so that in particular the full curvature satisfies

|F_{A}|\leq 2C_{\eqref{F+estimate:estimate}}{\varepsilon}e^{2(|t|-T)}.

According to Lemma
2.9
, assuming

is sufficiently small, there exists a gauge

on

|\tau(A)(t)|\leq CC_{\eqref{F+estimate:estimate}}{\varepsilon}e^{2(|t|-T)}.

In this gauge, the Yang-Mills equation is

Multiplying (

) by the hypothesis (

), we get

|\eta|\leq CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}\left({\varepsilon}_{1}e^{-4(t+T)}+{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{4(t-T)}+\left({\varepsilon}_{1}+{\varepsilon}_{2}\right)e^{-4T}\right).

From (

) and Lemma

\begin{split}|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|&\leq CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}_{1}\left(\alpha_{1}e^{-(2+\kappa_{0})(t+T)}+\alpha_{2}e^{-(2-\kappa_{0})t-(2+\kappa_{0})T}\right)\\

Overall, we have

(2.46)

\begin{split}|E_{1}|+|E_{2}|+|\eta|&\leq CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}_{1}\left({\varepsilon}\left(e^{-4(t+T)}+e^{-4T}\right)+\alpha_{1}e^{-(2+\kappa_{0})(t+T)}+\alpha_{2}e^{-(2-\kappa_{0})t-(2+\kappa_{0})T}\right)\\
&\,\,+CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}_{2}\left({\varepsilon}\left(e^{4(t-T)}+e^{-4T}\right)+\alpha_{2}e^{(2+\kappa_{0})(t-T)}+\alpha_{1}e^{(2-\kappa_{0})t-(2+\kappa_{0})T}\right).\end{split}

Applying Proposition

\begin{split}\|\Phi_{A}^{+}(t)\|&\leq C\left({\varepsilon}_{1}e^{-2(t+T)}+{\varepsilon}_{2}e^{2(t-T)}\right)\\
&+CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}_{1}\left({\varepsilon}\left(e^{-2(t+T)}+e^{-4T}\right)+\alpha_{1}e^{-2(t+T)}+\alpha_{2}e^{-(2-\kappa_{0})t-(2+\kappa_{0})T}\right)\\
&\,\,+CC^{2}_{(\ref{F+estimate:estimate})}{\varepsilon}_{2}\left({\varepsilon}\left(e^{2(t-T)}+e^{-4T}\right)+\alpha_{2}e^{2(t-T)}+\alpha_{1}e^{(2-\kappa_{0})t-(2+\kappa_{0})T}\right).\end{split}

Notice that

and

Also note that

So (

\int_{S^{3}_{R}(z_{1})}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+})\wedge a+\sum_{i>1}\int_{S^{3}_{R}(z_{i})}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+})\wedge a=\frac{1}{2}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\langle\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+}),D_{\rho_{k}(A_{k})}^{+}a\rangle.

Theorem
1.1
then follows from the following three claims:

(5.1)

\lim_{R\searrow 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-2}\int_{S^{3}_{R}(z_{1})}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+})\wedge a=\frac{1}{2}\pi^{2}\langle\hat{\rho}(F_{B_{j,1}}^{+}(\infty)),D_{A_{\infty}}a(z_{1})\rangle

for the distinguished bubble point
z_{1},

(5.2)

\lim_{R\searrow 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-2}\int_{S^{3}_{R}(z_{i})}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+})\wedge a=0

for any ASD bubble point
z_{i}
with
i>1,

and

(5.3)

\lim_{k\to\infty}\lambda_{k}^{-2}\int_{\Omega_{R}}\langle F_{k}^{+},D_{A_{k}}^{+}a\rangle=0.

The equation (
5.2
) follows from the bound (
4.9
), which gives

\lambda_{k}^{-2}\int_{S^{3}_{R}(z_{i})}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}(F_{A_{k}}^{+})\wedge a=O(R^{3}).

The equation (
5.3
) follows by writing

D_{\rho_{k}(A_{k})}=D_{A_{\infty}}+\left(\rho_{k}(A_{k})-A_{\infty}\right)\#,

where the remainder term tends to zero, and using

\|F^{+}_{A_{k}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\Omega_{R})}\lesssim{\varepsilon}^{\prime}_{k}\lesssim\lambda_{k}^{2}

by Theorem
1.5
b
and Lemma
2.7
.

It remains to prove (
5.1
). We work in the radial gauge determined by
A_{\infty}
and view
\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)
as a constant
{\text{End}}(E_{\infty})_{z_{1}}
-valued ASD 2-form on
{\mathbb{R}}^{4}\cong T_{z_{1}}M.
We denote the
{\text{End}}(E_{\infty})_{z_{1}}
-valued 1-form
a
in this gauge by the same letter. For
\lambda_{k}\leq r\leq 1,
Theorem
1.5
b
gives

\exp_{x^{i}_{k}}^{*}\rho_{k}(F^{+}_{A_{k}})=\lambda_{k}^{2}\left(\iota^{*}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)+\gamma_{k}\right),

where

|\gamma_{k}|\lesssim\frac{|\delta_{k}|}{r^{4}}+\frac{\lambda^{i}_{k}}{|x|^{5}}+\frac{{\varepsilon}^{i}_{k}+K\lambda_{k}^{2}}{|x|^{2}}.

Note that

\left.\iota^{*}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\right|_{S^{3}_{R}}=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\left.\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\right|_{S^{3}_{R}}.

Since the integral (
5.1
) is over
S^{3}_{R},
we obtain

(5.4)

\begin{split}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\int_{S^{3}_{R}}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}\left(F^{+}_{A_{k}}\right)\wedge a&=\int_{S_{R}^{3}}\operatorname{Tr}\left(\iota^{*}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)+\gamma_{k}\right)\wedge a.\\
&=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\int_{S_{R}^{3}}\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\wedge a+\int_{S_{R}^{3}}\gamma_{k}\wedge a.\end{split}

Since
\delta_{k},\lambda^{i}_{k}\to 0
as
k\to\infty,
we conclude that

(5.5)

\begin{split}\lim_{k\to\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}^{2}}\int_{S^{3}_{R}}{\rm Tr}\rho_{k}\left(F^{+}_{A_{k}}\right)\wedge a&=\frac{1}{R^{4}}\int_{S_{R}^{3}}\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\wedge a+O(R).\end{split}

To calculate the leading term, we use Stokes’s theorem over
B_{R}:

(5.6)

\begin{split}\int_{S_{R}^{3}}\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\wedge a&=\int_{B_{R}}d\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\wedge a\\
&=\int_{B_{R}}\operatorname{Tr}\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right)\wedge da\\
&=\frac{\pi^{2}}{2}R^{4}\left(\left\langle\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)\right),D^{-}_{A_{\infty}}a(z_{1})\right\rangle+O(R)\right).\end{split}

Returning to (
5.5
), we may now pass to the limit
R\to 0
to obtain the desired result (
5.1
). ∎

6.
Proof of Theorem
1.2

Let
A_{k}
be the sequence of Yang-Mills connections in Theorem
1.2
. Based on Uhlenbeck’s compactness theory, one can construct a bubble tree structure (see “the bubble tree diagram” in Figure 2 of
[
17
]
). The vertices of the tree correspond to
\mathcal{B},
and
A_{\infty}
is the root. As assumed in Theorem
1.2
, exactly one vertex represents the unique SD bubble, which we denote by
B_{+}
below. Other vertices represent either non-trivial ASD connections, or ghost bubbles. The first step for the proof of Theorem
1.2
is to pullback
A_{k}
by some conformal transformation of
S^{4}
(depending on
k
) such that in the bubble tree mentioned above for the new sequence, the root
A_{\infty}
is a nontrivial ASD connection, and all vertices (other than
A_{\infty}
and
B_{+}
) on the unique path connecting
A_{\infty}
and
B_{+}
represent ghost bubbles.

By modifying the above sequence of conformal transformations if necessary, we may further assume that

•

B_{+}
is obtained from a scaling of
A_{k}
by scales
\lambda_{k}\searrow 0
centered at the north pole;

•

there is no energy concentration at the south pole.

Let
\varphi
be the stereographic projection from
S^{4}\setminus\left\{\text{south pole}\right\}
to
\mathbb{R}^{4}
(that takes the equator to the unit sphere in
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and the north pole to the origin). For the proof of Theorem
1.2
, it suffices to consider the sequence
\tilde{A}_{k}:=(\varphi^{-1})^{*}A_{k}
of Yang-Mills connection on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. Denote the weak limit by
\tilde{A}_{\infty}.

By some abuse of notation, we still denote by
B_{+}
the only SD bubble in the bubble tree for the sequence
\tilde{A}_{k}.

It is not hard to see that the sequence described above (using Parker’s algorithm or any equivalent one) satisfies the following properties, which are sufficient for our purposes.

(1)

\tilde{A}_{k}
converges smoothly locally (up to gauge transformation) away from a finite set
S
(with
0\in S
) to the weak limit
\tilde{A}_{\infty}
, which is a nontrivial ASD instanton;

(2)

there exists a sequence
\mu^{(0)}_{k}\searrow 0
such that, under the scaling map
\varphi_{k}(y)=\mu^{(0)}_{k}y
of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, the Uhlenbeck limit of
\varphi_{k}^{*}(\tilde{A}_{k})
is the
only
SD bubble
B_{+}
;

(3)

for any sequence
\mu_{k}
satisfying

\mu_{k}\searrow 0\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\searrow 0,

if
\psi_{k}(y)=\mu_{k}y
, then the Uhlenbeck limit of
\psi_{k}^{*}(\tilde{A}_{k})
is a trivial connection. Note that for the sequence
\psi^{*}_{k}(\tilde{A}_{k})
,
0
is always an energy concentration point;

(4)

there are
l
sequences of positive numbers
\left\{(\mu_{k}^{(j)})\right\}_{j=1}^{l}
satisfying

\frac{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}\searrow 0,\quad\text{for }j=1,\dots,l\quad\text{and}\quad\mu^{(l)}_{k}\searrow 0,

as follows;

(5)

let
\psi^{(j)}_{k}(y)=\mu^{(j)}_{k}y
, then for the sequence
(\psi^{(j)}_{k})^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}
, there are energy concentration points other than
0
. In this case, the trivial Uhlenbeck limit of
(\psi^{(j)}_{k})^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}
is called a ghost bubble;

(6)

these are the only ghost bubbles between
\tilde{A}_{\infty}
and
B_{+}
in the sense that if for some other sequence
\mu_{k}\searrow 0
there are more than one energy concentration points (for the sequence
\psi^{*}_{k}(\tilde{A}_{k})
where
\psi_{k}
is defined in (
3
)), then there exists some
j
in
1,\dots,l
such that
\mu_{k}
is equivalent to
\mu^{(j)}_{k}
, i.e.

\sup_{k}\left(\frac{\mu_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}+\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu_{k}}\right)<+\infty.

The proof of Theorem
1.2
consists of the following steps, each taking a subsection:

(1)

use Corollary
2.3
to find an expansion of
F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}
in each neck region and introduce the leading coefficients
c^{(j)}_{k}
and
d^{(j)}_{k}
;

(2)

obtain an upper bound for
F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}
, which allows us to extract a scaled limit of
F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}
on each ghost bubble domain; this limit will be shown to be the inversion of an
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued constant two-form on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, denoted by
\xi^{(j)}_{\infty}
;

(3)

define what we mean by a
standard curvature tensor
and show that the limit of
d^{(j)}_{k}
as
k\to\infty
is such a tensor;

(4)

complete the proof using an argument analogous to that of Theorem
1.1
together with a formula from the ADHM construction.

6.1.
Expansion on the Necks

There are
l+1
necks connecting the chain of ghost bubbles between
B_{+}
and
\tilde{A}_{\infty}
. By setting
\mu^{(l+1)}_{k}\equiv 1
, they are given by

\hat{N}^{(j)}:=B_{\delta\mu^{(j)}_{k}}\setminus B_{\delta^{-1}\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}

for
j=1,\dots,l+1
. Here
\delta
is a small positive constant that depends on the sequence
A_{k}
but not on
k
. We also need the scaling of these domains: recalling that
\psi^{(j)}_{k}(y)=\mu_{k}^{(j)}y
, set

N^{(j)}=(\psi^{(j)}_{k})^{-1}(\hat{N}^{(j)})=B_{\delta}\setminus B_{\delta^{-1}\lambda^{(j)}_{k}}

where

\lambda^{(j)}_{k}=\frac{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}.

Set

A^{(j)}_{k}:=(\psi^{(j)}_{k})^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}

for
j=0,1,\dots,l+1
. In particular, the limit of
A^{(0)}_{k}
is the unique SD bubble and the limit of
A^{(l+1)}_{k}
is the weak limit
\tilde{A}_{\infty}
(on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
).

Applying the Euclidean version of Theorem
2.1
, we get on
N^{(j)}
for each
j=1,\dots,l+1
:

\left|x\right|^{2}\left|F_{A^{(j)}_{k}}^{+}\right|\leq C\left(\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\left|x\right|^{2}+\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}\frac{(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{2}}{\left|x\right|^{2}}\right),

where

\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}=\left\|F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{2\lambda^{(j)}_{k}\delta^{-1}}\setminus B_{\lambda^{(j)}_{k}\delta^{-1}})}\quad\text{and}\quad\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}=\left\|F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{\delta}\setminus B_{\delta/2})}.

By Lemma
2.9
, there exist gauges
\tau^{(j)}_{k}
on
N^{(j)}
(and hence on
\hat{N}^{(j)}
) such that the connection forms of
A^{(j)}_{k}
in these gauges satisfy condition (
2.8
) in Corollary
2.3
, which implies the following expansion for
F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}
on
N^{(j)}
:

(6.1)

\begin{split}\left|\tau^{(j)}_{k}(F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}})-\left(c^{(j)}_{k}+(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{2}\iota^{*}d^{(j)}_{k}\right)\right|&\leq C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\left|x\right|+C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{3}\left|x\right|^{-5}\\
&+C(\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}+\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2})(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{2}\left|x\right|^{-2},\end{split}

where
c^{(j)}_{k}
and
d^{(j)}_{k}
are constant self-dual and anti-self-dual two-forms, respectively, on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
, satisfying

\left|c^{(j)}_{k}\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\quad\text{and}\quad\left|d^{(j)}_{k}\right|\leq C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}.

6.2.
Upper bound for
F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}

For the sequence
\tilde{A}_{k}
, the bubble tree structure could be complicated. A vital assumption in Theorem
1.2
is that
B_{+}
is the
only
SD bubble. This assumption combined with Lemma
2.7
gives us a uniform upper bound for
F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}
that is valid across neck regions and bubble domains.

Recall that
\iota
denotes the inversion map in the unit sphere of
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. By Uhlenbeck’s removable singularity theorem,
\iota^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}
is a sequence of Yang–Mills connections on
\mathbb{R}^{4}
. After inversion, assumption (
2
) stated at the beginning of this section implies that

\left\|F^{+}_{\iota^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}}\right\|_{L^{2}(B_{(\mu^{(0)}_{k})^{-1}\delta})}\leq\varepsilon_{0}

(for sufficiently large
k
). Here
\varepsilon_{0}
is given in Lemma
2.7
and
\delta
is small depending on
\varepsilon_{0}
and
B_{+}
.
By Lemma
2.7
, we get

(6.2)

\sup_{B_{(\mu^{(0)}_{k})^{-1}\delta/2}}\left|F^{+}_{\iota^{*}\tilde{A}_{k}}\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{0}(\mu^{(0)}_{k})^{2}.

Since the inversion
\iota
is a conformal map, we obtain

(6.3)

\left|x\right|^{2}\left|F^{+}_{\tilde{A}_{k}}\right|(x)\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\left|x\right|}\right)^{2}

for all
x
with
\left|x\right|>2\delta^{-1}\mu^{(0)}_{k}
.

As a direct corollary of (
6.3
), we have

Corollary 6.1
.

\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}\right)^{2},\quad\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}\right)^{2}

and

\left|c^{(j)}_{k}\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}\right)^{2},\quad\left|d^{(j)}_{k}\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}\right)^{2}.

As another corollary of the upper bound (
6.3
), the following lemma shows the existence of some scaled limit of
F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}
on each ghost bubble.

Lemma 6.2
.

Let
j=1,\dots,l
.
For the sequence
A^{(j)}_{k}
defined above, let
S^{(j)}
denote the finite energy concentration set. There exists a sequence of gauges
\eta^{(j)}_{k}
defined on
\Omega^{(j)}_{k}:=B_{k}\setminus B_{1/k}(S^{(j)})
such that

\left\|\eta^{(j)}_{k}(A^{(j)}_{k})\right\|_{C^{2}(\Omega_{k}^{(j)})}\to 0.

Let
\xi^{(j)}_{k}=\left(\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}\eta^{(j)}_{k}(F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}})
, then
\xi^{(j)}_{k}
converges in
C^{1}_{loc}
on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus S^{(j)}
to
\iota^{*}(\xi^{(j)}_{\infty})
, where
\xi^{(j)}_{\infty}
is an
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-valued constant anti-self-dual two-form.

Proof.

Since the Uhlenbeck limit of
A^{(j)}_{k}
is the trivial connection, the claim on the existence of
\eta^{(j)}_{k}
is trivial. By definition,
A^{(j)}_{k}
is a scaling of
\tilde{A}_{k}
by
\mu^{(j)}_{k}
. Then it follows from (
6.3
) that on
\Omega_{k_{0}}^{(j)}
(for any
k_{0}
fixed),

\left|x\right|^{2}\left|F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}\right|(x)\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left(\frac{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}\right)^{2}\left|x\right|^{-2}.

This gives an upper bound for
\xi^{(j)}_{k}
on
\Omega_{k_{0}}^{(j)}
:

(6.4)

\left|x\right|^{2}\left|\xi^{(j)}_{k}\right|(x)\leq C\varepsilon_{0}\left|x\right|^{-2}.

Note that
\xi^{(j)}_{k}
is the representation of an
A^{(j)}_{k}
-harmonic two-form in the gauge
\eta^{(j)}_{k}
and the connection form of
A^{(j)}_{k}
in the same gauge vanishes in
C^{2}
topology in the limit. Hence, (
6.4
) implies that
\xi^{(j)}_{k}
converges in
C^{1}_{loc}
in
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus S^{(j)}
to a self-dual harmonic two form, denoted by
\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty}
.

Being a harmonic form bounded near
S^{(j)}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
(due to (
6.4
)),
\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty}
can be smoothly extended to be defined on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Using again the conformal invariance of harmonic two-form, we find that
\iota^{*}(\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty})
is an anti-self-dual harmonic two-form defined on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
, but (
6.4
) implies that

\left|\iota^{*}\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty}\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{0}

on
\mathbb{R}^{4}\setminus\left\{0\right\}
. Therefore by the Liouville theorem for harmonic functions, we conclude that
\iota^{*}(\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty})
is a constant two-form. The proof of the lemma is done by setting
\xi^{(j)}_{\infty}=\iota^{*}(\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty})
.
∎

6.3.
Standard curvature tensor

The
\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{+}}(\infty)\right)
in (
1.3
) is a tensor in the space
\Lambda^{2,-}T_{z_{1}}^{*}M\otimes{\rm End}(E_{\infty})_{z_{1}}
. Identifying
T_{z_{1}}^{*}M
with
\mathbb{R}^{4}
and choosing a trivialization of
(E_{\infty})_{z_{1}}
, it becomes a tensor in

\Lambda^{2,-}\mathbb{R}^{4}\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2).

The same holds for the coefficient
d^{(j)}_{k}
in (
6.1
). The following definition characterizes a property of such tensors that is important for the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Definition 6.3
.

A tensor
F^{-}
in
\Lambda^{2,-}\otimes\mathfrak{su}(2)
is defined to be a
standard curvature tensor
, if for some choice of basis
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})
of
\mathfrak{su}(2)
, there exists an orthonormal basis
(e_{1},e_{2},e_{3})
of
\Lambda^{2,-}
such that

F^{-}=\lambda\left(e_{1}\otimes\mathbf{i}+e_{2}\otimes\mathbf{j}+e_{3}\otimes\mathbf{k}\right)

for some
\lambda\in\mathbb{R}
.

Notice that the above definition is independent of the choice of
(\mathbf{i},\mathbf{j},\mathbf{k})
and hence makes sense for the anti-self-dual part of a curvature tensor (such as
\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{+}}(\infty)\right)
mentioned above) even without a choice of trivialization.

In light of Corollary
6.1
, we define

\tilde{d}^{(j)}_{k}=\left(\frac{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}d^{(j)}_{k},\quad\tilde{c}^{(j)}_{k}=\left(\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}c^{(j)}_{k}

and let
d^{(j)}_{\infty}
denote the limit of
\tilde{d}^{(j)}_{k}
as
k\to\infty.

The main result of this subsection is

Lemma 6.4
.

d^{(j)}_{\infty}
is a nonvanishing standard curvature tensor for
j=1,\dots,l+1
.

Proof.

By the proof of Theorem
1.5
, there is a linear isometry
\beta
from
\mathbb{R}^{r}
to
E_{0}
(see (
3.15
)) such that

\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{+}}(\infty)\right)=\beta_{*}(d^{(1)}_{\infty}),

where
\beta_{*}
is the induced isomorphism between
\mathfrak{su}(2)\subset\mathbb{R}^{r}\times\mathbb{R}^{r}
and
{\rm End}(E_{0})
.
Since
B_{+}
is a one-instanton, then explicit formula shows that
\hat{\rho}_{1}\left(F^{+}_{B_{+}}(\infty)\right)
is a nonvanishing standard curvature tensor. Hence, so is
d^{(1)}_{\infty}
.

The proof of the lemma is then reduced to the following claim: if
d^{(j)}_{\infty}
is a nonvanishing standard curvature tensor, then so is
d^{(j+1)}_{\infty}
for
j=1,2,\dots,l
.

The proof of the claim consists of two steps.

Step 1.
Fix
R<\delta
. On
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}\subset N^{(j)}
, (
6.1
) implies that

(6.5)

\begin{split}\left|\tau^{(j)}_{k}(F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}})-\left(c^{(j)}_{k}+(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{2}\iota^{*}d^{(j)}_{k}\right)\right|&\leq C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\left|x\right|+C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}(\lambda_{k}^{(j)})^{3}\left|x\right|^{-5}\\
&+C(\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}+\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2})(\lambda^{(j)}_{k})^{2}\left|x\right|^{-2}.\end{split}

By the definitions of
\eta^{(j)}_{k}
and
\xi^{(j)}_{k}
in Lemma
6.2
, multiplying the above inequality by
\left(\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}
on both sides, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\tau^{(j)}_{k}(\eta^{(j)}_{k})^{-1}\xi^{(j)}_{k}-\left(\tilde{c}^{(j)}_{k}+\iota^{*}\tilde{d}^{(j)}_{k}\right)\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}\left|x\right|+C\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}\left(\frac{\mu^{(j)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}(\lambda_{k}^{(j)})^{3}\left|x\right|^{-5}

\displaystyle+C(\varepsilon^{(j)}_{1}+\varepsilon^{(j)}_{2})\left(\frac{\mu^{(j-1)}_{k}}{\mu^{(0)}_{k}}\right)^{2}\left|x\right|^{-2}.

By Corollary
6.1
, taking the limit
k\to\infty
, we get on
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}

\left|\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau^{(j)}_{k}(\eta^{(j)}_{k})^{-1}\xi^{(j)}_{k}-\iota^{*}d^{(j)}_{\infty}\right|\leq CR+C+CR^{-2}.

Recall that
\xi_{k}^{(j)}
converges to
\hat{\xi}^{(j)}_{\infty}=\iota^{*}\xi^{(j)}_{\infty}
, which is of order
R^{-4}
on
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
. The same estimate holds for
\iota^{*}d^{(j)}_{\infty}
. Compared with
R^{-4}
, the right-hand side is negligible as
R\to 0
.

Note that by the definition of ghost bubble, the connection form of
A^{(j)}_{k}
in gauge
\eta^{(j)}_{k}
over
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
converges to zero as
k\to\infty
and by the construction of
\tau^{(j)}_{k}
(see Lemma
2.9
), the connection form of the same connection in gauge
\tau^{(j)}_{k}
over
B_{2R}\setminus B_{R}
converges to zero by passing
k\to\infty
and then
R\to 0
. Hence,
\lim_{R\searrow 0}\lim_{k\to\infty}\tau^{(j)}_{k}(\eta^{(j)}_{k})^{-1}
is a constant linear transformation, which implies that if
d^{(j)}_{\infty}
is a nonvanishing standard curvature tensor, so is
\xi^{(j)}_{\infty}
.

Step 2.
We derive the following on
B_{\delta(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{-1}}\setminus B_{\delta^{-1}}
from (
6.1
):

(6.6)

\begin{split}\left|\tau^{(j+1)}_{k}(F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}})-\left((\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{2}c^{(j+1)}_{k}+\iota^{*}d^{(j+1)}_{k}\right)\right|&\leq C\varepsilon^{(j+1)}_{2}(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{3}\left|x\right|+C\varepsilon^{(j+1)}_{1}\left|x\right|^{-5}\\
&+C(\varepsilon^{(j+1)}_{1}+\varepsilon^{(j+1)}_{2})(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{2}\left|x\right|^{-2}.\end{split}

In fact, we consider the pullback of (
6.1
) by the map
\psi:B_{\delta(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{-1}}\setminus B_{\delta^{-1}}\to N^{(j+1)}
defined by
\psi(y)=\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k}y
. (
6.6
) follows from the following facts:

•

\psi^{*}F^{+}_{A^{(j+1)}_{k}}=F^{+}_{A^{(j)}_{k}}
;

•

\psi^{*}(c^{(j+1)}_{k})=(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{2}c^{(j+1)}_{k}
;

•

\psi^{*}\iota^{*}d^{(j+1)}_{k}=(\lambda^{(j+1)}_{k})^{-2}\iota^{*}d^{(j+1)}_{k}
;

•

for any two form
\alpha
on
N^{(j+1)}
,

Lemma 6.7
.

Let
(B,\lambda)
be a pair (as above) satisfying the ADHM condition (A1) and (A2). If
(\lambda_{t})
(for

Proof.

Define the map

\Psi(B,\lambda)={\rm Im}(B^{*}B+\lambda^{*}\lambda),

where

extracts the imaginary part of each entry. The condition (A1) is equivalent to

The condition (A2) implies (in particular, when

has full column rank. Hence, for any column vector

to

\dim_{\mathbb{H}}({\rm Ker}(B))\leq 1.

The proof of the lemma uses the implicit function theorem. First, we observe that the image of
\Psi
is the space of
\kappa\times\kappa

V
. Our goal is then to show the (partial) linearization

We then discuss the rank of

However,

. Set

a=\frac{d}{dt}A_{t}.

Since
F_{A_{t}}^{+}=0
for all
t
, it follows that

D_{A_{\infty}}^{+}a=0.

Case 1:
X
is scaling
.

\displaystyle(D^{-}_{A_{\infty}}a)(z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}F^{-}_{A_{t}}(z_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\tilde{\varphi}_{t}^{*}F_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1}).

Since
\varphi_{t}(z_{1})\equiv z_{1}
for any
t
, the lift
\tilde{\varphi}_{t}
is irrelevant for the above compution. Moreover,

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}(\varphi_{t})_{*,z_{1}}
is the identity map from
T_{z_{1}}S^{4}
to itself. Therefore,

(D^{-}_{A_{\infty}}a)(z_{1})=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\tilde{\varphi}_{t}^{*}F_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1})=2F_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1}).

Thus, the obstruction given by (
1.3
) is

\langle\hat{\rho}_{1}(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)),F_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1})\rangle=0.

This is equation (1.10) in Theorem 1.4 of
[
28
]
.

Case 2:
X
is rotation
. Again,
\varphi_{t}
leaves
z_{1}
invariant and for some
\sigma^{\prime}\in\mathfrak{so}(4)
:

\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}(\varphi_{t})_{*,z_{1}}=\sigma^{\prime}

as a map from
T_{z_{1}}S^{4}
to itself.
\sigma^{\prime}
induces a
\sigma\in\mathfrak{su}(2)
such that

(D^{-}_{A_{\infty}}a)(z_{1})=\frac{d}{dt}|_{t=0}\tilde{\varphi}_{t}^{*}F_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1})=ad_{\sigma}(F_{A_{\infty}}).

The obstruction in Theorem
1.1
becomes

\forall\sigma\in\mathfrak{su}(2)\quad\langle\hat{\rho}_{1}(F^{+}_{B_{1,1}}(\infty)),ad_{\sigma}(F_{A_{\infty}})(z_{1})\rangle=0,

which is equation (1.9) in Theorem 1.4 of
[
28
]
.

For (2), i.e., deformation caused by gauge group action, let
\xi
be a section of
{\rm End}(E)
and take
a=D_{A_{\infty}}\xi
. Since

D_{A_{\infty}}^{-}a(z_{1})=[F^{-}_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1}),\xi(z_{1})],

the obstruction in Theorem
1.1
becomes

\langle[F^{-}_{A_{\infty}}(z_{1}),\xi(z_{1})],\hat{\rho}_{1}(F_{B_{1,1}}^{+}(\infty))\rangle=0.

If the structure group is
{\mathrm{SU}}(2)
, then this is the same obstruction that was obtained for the rotation case above. However, for general compact structure group, Theorem
1.1
does provide new obstructions.

In case (3), the obstruction aligns more closely with that found by Biquard for the degeneration of Einstein four-manifolds
[
2
]
(see also
[
14
]
and
[
15
]
). By comparison with
[
15
]
, it is natural to ask whether the assumption
H^{2,+}_{A_{\infty}}=\{0\}
can be removed from Theorem
1.1
.
