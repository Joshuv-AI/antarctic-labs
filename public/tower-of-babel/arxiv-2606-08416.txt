1.
Introduction

The
abc
-conjecture, introduced by Joseph Oesterlé
[
24
]
and David Masser
[
19
]
in the 1980s, has remained an active conjecture in number theory. The conjecture asserts an asymptotic result on what we denote the
quality

q(\cdot)
of triples
(a,b,c)
with
a+b=c
, for coprime
a,b,c
; such triples are denoted
abc
-triples. The significance of the
abc
-conjecture extends far beyond an isolated statement; a proof would immediately imply Fermat’s Last Theorem for
n\geq 6

[
35
,
34
]
, the Fermat–Catalan and Beal conjectures, and Szpiro’s conjecture on elliptic curves
[
34
]
, while also shedding light on the distribution of prime gaps. Outside pure mathematics, deeper understanding of prime structure underlies cryptographic primitives (e.g. RSA), efficient integer factorization (the General Number Field Sieve depends on the abundance of smooth numbers
[
4
]
), and even biological phenomena – such as the prime-numbered life cycles of cicadas.

Several recent works have established theoretical results that provide insight towards the conjecture itself
[
12
]
and developed computer algorithms to find high-quality triples quickly
[
8
,
21
,
22
]
. Nitaj characterized several of the relevant methodologies for triple searches
[
23
]
; van der Horst introduced an algorithm using elliptic curves
[
32
]
; and Elkies illustrated connections to other unsolved problems in number theory
[
10
]
. Given the intractable nature of determining high-quality triples under the standard
quality metric
to evaluate the suitability of
abc
-triples as measured by the conjecture, a natural strategy is to pursue
alternative
quality metrics that preserve the conjecture’s qualitative content while being more tractable from analytic standpoints. This approach is the focus of the ensuing manuscript; we note that previous variants of the quality used in the
abc
-conjecture can be found in ratios involving conductors and discriminants of associated elliptic curves, as well as minor algebraic variations
[
34
,
32
,
17
]
. Our metrics, which are based on the Doubly Geometric Mean of the primes dividing
abc
for each such
abc
-triple (
a,b,c
), assume a distinct approach by directly penalizing the number
\omega
of distinct prime divisors of
abc
.

The main contributions of this work are as follows:

(1)

We define several new quality metrics (Definitions
3.2
,
4.1
) in the same spirit as the standard quality for the
abc
-conjecture, which (i) penalize the number of distinct prime divisors of
abc
, and (ii) incorporate roundness of an
abc
-triple.

(2)

We analyze all variant quality metrics by constructing families of high-quality triples and investigating asymptotic growth rates (Theorems
3.5
,
3.6
,
3.8
.) We develop an analogue of the
abc
-conjecture (Theorem
3.9
) and prove it conditionally on the infinitude of twin primes or Mersenne primes (Theorem
3.10
), and
unconditionally
through Chen’s theorem (Theorem
3.12
).

(3)

We also prove results on analogues of transition phases with respect to asymptotic behavior for parametrized families of quality metrics in Theorems
4.4
,
4.7
, and
4.8
– and develop connections to elliptic curves and the
abc
-conjecture itself in Theorems
4.10
,
4.13
,
4.15
, and
4.16
.

(4)

We show an asymptotic improvement on the brute force algorithm (Algorithm
1
) via constructive enumerations of high-quality triples (Algorithms
3
,
4
), enabling scalable, sublinear-time computation of high-quality triples under our alternative quality metrics.

2.
Background

We first offer a brief description of the frame of reference from which we approach the
abc
-conjecture, as a manner of motivating our discussions in Section
3
.

2.1.
From Mason-Stothers to the Standard Quality

The
abc
-conjecture arises as the integer analogue of the Mason–Stothers Theorem
[
18
,
29
]
. The latter result asserts that if there exist nonconstant polynomials
x(t),y(t),z(t)\in\mathbb{C}[t]
sharing no common roots, such that
x(t)+y(t)=z(t)
, then
\max\{\deg x,\deg y,\deg z\}<|\{\mu\in\mathbb{C}:(xyz)(\mu)=0\}|
. The integer analogue of this conjecture thereby replaces the degree
\deg
by the absolute value, and the count of distinct roots
\mu
of
(xyz)(t)
by the
radical
of a given triple. The following definitions arise.

Definition 2.1

(
abc
-triple)
.

A triple
(a,b,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{3}
is an
abc
-triple
if, and only if,
a+b=c
and
\gcd(a,b)=\gcd(b,c)=\gcd(a,c)=1
.

Definition 2.2

(Radical)
.

The
radical
of an integer
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
is given by
\mathrm{rad}(n):=\prod_{p\mid n,\,p\in\mathbb{P}}\,p
.

A first attempt at a quality metric is therefore of the form
q_{\text{test}}(a,b,c)=c/\mathrm{rad}(abc)
, which measures the
roundness
of the triple
(a,b,c)
as per the size of the largest entry,
c
. However, since the family
(1,2^{p(p-1)n}-1,2^{p(p-1)n})
for
p\in\mathbb{P}
,
n\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
has
\mathrm{rad}(abc)<2c/p
(by Fermat’s Little Theorem), it follows that
q_{\text{test}}
may become arbitrarily large. To produce a more analytically interesting metric, one might reduce the growth rate of the quality by introducing a logarithmic term in the numerator, developing the following.
3
3

3

We designate this quality as the
standard quality
since it has been analyzed extensively and is the focal point of the conjecture itself
[
34
,
8
,
32
,
17
]
. We restrict to
abc
-triples (with
a,b,c
coprime); otherwise triples of the form
(2^{h},2^{h},2^{h+1})
have unbounded quality.

Definition 2.3

(
Standard Quality
)
.

For an
abc
-triple
(a,b,c)
, the standard quality is

q_{s}(a,b,c)=\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln(\mathrm{rad}(abc))}.

To our knowledge, the tightest uniform bound on the asymptotics of the quality
q_{s}(\cdot)
for large triples is the following.

Theorem 2.4

(Stewart and Yu, 2001
[
28
]
)
.

There exists some fixed
C_{0}
such that

c<\exp\bigl(C_{0}\sqrt[3]{\mathrm{rad}(abc)}\,(\ln\mathrm{rad}(abc))^{3}\bigr)

for all
abc
-triples.

Taking exponentials reveals the bound
q_{s}(a,b,c)\leq C_{0}(\mathrm{rad}(abc))^{1/3}(\ln(\text{rad}(abc))^{1/3}
, which implies that the quality is bounded above by a sublinear function in the radical
\text{rad}(abc)
.

Only 241 triples with
q_{s}>1.4
are known
[
8
]
, with the highest quality obtained being
q_{s}\approx 1.6299
(due to Ressyat, per continued fractions
[
34
,
32
]
). See Table
1
for a few such examples. We denote triples with quality
q_{s}>1
to be
abc
-hits
; those with
q_{s}>1.4
are
high-quality
, per the classification given by the standard quality. We note, additionally, that the triple with fifth-highest known quality,
q_{s}
, is of the form
(7^{3},3^{10},2^{11}\cdot 29)
; this triple utilizes a remarkably smaller number of primes than the other highest-ranking triples, under the standard quality
[
9
]
. This principle of prioritizing few primes in the coupled factorization of
abc
motivates our alternative metrics in Section
3

We may also view the standard quality metric as an effective geometric mean of the primes dividing
abc
, when each prime is provided with the corresponding weight.

For instance, we may write the prime factorization of
abc
with distinct primes
p_{1},\dots,p_{\omega}
, such that
\omega
is the number of primes dividing
abc
. Manipulation yields

(1)

\displaystyle q_{s}(a,b,c)=\frac{\sum_{p_{i}\mid c}e_{i}\ln(p_{i})}{\sum_{i=1}^{\omega}\ln(p_{i})},

where we write
c=\prod p_{i}^{e_{i}}
for all such primes
p_{i}
dividing
c
. Recognizing the resulting
\omega
-th root as a geometric mean, we observe

(2)

\displaystyle q_{s}(a,b,c)=\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln\!\bigl(\,\sqrt[\omega]{\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}p_{i}^{\omega}}\,\bigr)}=\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln\!\bigl(\mathrm{GM}\{p_{i}^{\omega}\}\bigr)},

where
\mathrm{GM}(\cdot)
denotes the geometric mean of the given entities. Thus, it follows that the standard quality is a comparison of the benchmark size measure
\ln(c)
to the size measure provided by the geometric mean of the primes dividing
abc
, each weighted by the corresponding
\omega
. Thus, one might seek to define a quality metric that seeks to penalize the
number
of primes much more harshly than their size, effectively selecting
against
smoothness of
abc
-triples. This effort is the task of the following section.

3.
DGM Quality Metric

3.1.
Motivation and Definition

Observe, in Table
1
, that many high-quality triples (for instance, the third highest-quality triple of
(19\cdot 1307,7\cdot 29^{2}\cdot 31^{8},2^{8}\cdot 3^{22}\cdot 5^{4})
) use significantly more primes than those of the first, fifth, and sixth highest quality triples. Notably, the characterization of the standard quality in Definition
2.3
guarantees that quality metrics that may use a large number of primes, yet ensure that the radical
\text{rad}(abc)
remains small through comparatively large exponents on primes of small magnitude, might be prioritized over triples that utilize a small number of (possibly large) primes. Thus, we seek a metric that aggressively privileges a small
overall
number of primes within the factorization of
abc
.
4
4

4

For instance, the cardinality of the solution set to some Diophantine equations may be bounded specifically with respect to a function of the number of prime factors of a given integer in consideration. Explicitly, the Thue equation
F(x,y)=m
(for a homogeneous form
F
, irreducible over
\mathbb{Q}
with degree
d
greater than
3
) has solutions bounded by
O(d^{\omega(m)+1})
, where
\omega(m)
is the number of primes in the factorization of
m
. If
m
represents
abc
in our case, an analogy may be drawn.

Our solution invokes the doubly geometric mean of such primes dividing
abc
in each
abc
-triple. As motivation, consider the sequence of means with progressively stronger
taming
of large values; that is, if we write the sequence of means as

\displaystyle\mathrm{AM}(x_{i})

\displaystyle:=\tfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}x_{i},

\displaystyle\mathrm{GM}(x_{i})

\displaystyle:=\exp\bigl(\mathrm{AM}\{\ln x_{i}\}\bigr),

\displaystyle\mathrm{DGM}(x_{i})

\displaystyle:=\exp\bigl(\exp(\mathrm{AM}\{\ln\ln x_{i}\})\bigr),

then each step progressively adds an inner logarithmic factor and an outer exponential, which rescales such that all three means agree when the
x_{i}
are all identical. For instance, on the set
\{2,3,5,7,1009\}
, the arithmetic mean
205.2
is dominated by the single large prime
1009
(for removing it drops the AM to
4.25
), while the GM is
24
and the DGM is
5.04\approx 5
. Thus, the DGM rewards triples that avoid many primes in their factorizations with lower prioritization of size, which isolates
\omega
(the number of primes dividing
abc
) as the dominant parameter, to a greater extent than the partial role of
\omega
in the standard quality metric
[
9
]
. Explicitly, we have the following.

Definition 3.1

(Doubly Geometric Mean)
.

For a set
S=\{x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}_{>0}
, we evaluate

\mathrm{DGM}(S):=\exp\Big(\exp\Big(\tfrac{1}{k}\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ln\ln(x_{i})\Big)\Big).

Using the DGM metric, the resulting
DGM Quality
may be defined in the natural extension of equation (
2
).

Definition 3.2

(DGM Quality)
.

Over all
(a,b,c)\in(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{3}
an
abc
-triple, and
\omega
being the number of primes dividing
abc
, with
P_{\omega}=\{p^{\omega}:p\in\mathbb{P},p\mid abc\}
, we define the quality
q_{DGM}:(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{3}\mapsto\mathbb{R}^{+}
by

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c):=\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln(\mathrm{DGM}(P_{\omega}))}.

Using Definition
3.1
, the DGM quality may be rewritten in a suggestive manner. For a given
abc
-triple
(a,b,c)
with corresponding set
P_{\omega}
as given in Definition
3.2
above, we have

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(c)}{\exp\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\omega}\sum_{i}\ln\ln(p_{i}^{\omega})\bigr)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(c)}{\exp\!\bigl(\ln\omega+\tfrac{1}{\omega}\sum_{i}\ln\ln p_{i}\bigr)},

which may be rewritten as

(3)

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)=\frac{\ln(c)}{\omega\cdot\sqrt[\omega]{\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\ln(p_{i})}}.

We remark, upon comparing equation (
3
) with that of (
1
), that while both effectively involve a geometric mean of the prime divisors of
abc
as a
penalty
for each triple, the quality
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
includes an explicit multiplicative contribution of
1/\omega
. Thus, the DGM quality penalizes triples with many prime divisors overall with a linearly dependent penalty on
\omega
. Additionally, the contribution of each prime towards the geometric mean is
logarithmic
, rather than a contribution of
p_{i}^{\omega}
within the standard quality. Thus, the DGM quality directly prioritizes triples with a smaller overall number of primes in their factorizations.

3.2.
Elementary Bounds and Families of High-Quality Triples

We begin by establishing a series of straightforward bounds on the DGM Quality, to contextualize later results on asymptotics. We begin by establishing the following lemma.

Figure 1
.
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
evaluated on all
abc
-triples
(a,b,c)
with
c\leq 100
. Red “x”s are DGM-hits, and black “x”s give the highest-quality triples for a valid
abc
-triples upon a given value of
c
.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any fixed integer
s\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
with distinct prime divisors of the form
p_{1},\dots,p_{\omega_{s}}
, we must have that

\sum_{i=1}^{\omega_{s}}\ln(p_{i})\leq\ln(s)
, with equality if, and only if,
s
is a squarefree integer.

Proof.

Write the prime factorization of
s
as
\prod_{i=1}^{\omega_{s}}p_{i}^{e_{i}}
, with all exponents
e_{i}
satisfying
e_{i}\geq 1
. Then, it follows that
\ln(s)=\sum_{i=1}^{\omega_{s}}e_{i}\ln(p_{i})
. Hence, we must have that
\ln s=\sum e_{i}\ln p_{i}\geq\sum\ln p_{i}
since all the
e_{i}\geq 1
.
∎

Therefore, if we are able to convert the expression in equation (
3
) to one involving a
sum
of the logarithm of the primes dividing
abc
, the above result guarantees an upper bound on the denominator. The AM-GM inequality provides precisely this tool.

Theorem 3.4
.

For every
abc
-triple,
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)>\tfrac{1}{3}
.

Proof.

Let
(a,b,c)
an
abc
-triple, with
\omega
defined as before. We apply the AM-GM inequality on equation (
3
):

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\ln(c)}{\omega\cdot\frac{1}{\omega}\sum_{i=1}^{\omega}\ln(p_{i})}\geq\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln(abc)},

where the second inequality invokes Lemma
3.3
. Since
a,b<c
, we have

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)>\frac{\ln(c)}{\ln(c^{3})}=\frac{1}{3}.

∎

As in the case for the standard quality, we state that an
abc
-triple is a
DGM hit
if we have
q_{\text{DGM}}(a,b,c)>1
; per Definition
3.2
, this is equivalent to the condition
\mathrm{DGM}(P_{\omega})<c
. Figure
1
shows
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
evaluated on all
abc
-triples with
c\leq 100
, with DGM hits isolated.

For any positive integer
c
, we define the sequence
s_{c}:=\max\{q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c):(a,b,c)\text{ is an }abc\text{-triple}\}
at fixed
c
. We now seek to investigate
\limsup s_{c}
via various families of triples; this is suggested by certain subsets of triples in Figure
1
that appear to ascend roughly logarithmically in the size of
c
.

Upon inspection many of the high-quality triples
(a,b,c)
in Figure
1
utilize components in
a,b
with maximally simple factorization – for instance, powers of
2
or
3
. For instance, we may set
a=2^{i}
and
b=3^{j}
for indices
i,j
; if we have that
c=a+b
is prime, then
\omega=3
. Consequently, equation (
3
) immediately gives

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(2^{i},3^{j},c)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln c}{3({\ln 2\cdot\ln 3\cdot\ln c})^{1/3}}

\displaystyle=(\ln c)^{2/3}\cdot\frac{1}{3\sqrt[3]{\ln 2\cdot\ln 3}},

which diverges asymptotically as a function of
c
. We designate these triples to constitute the
power of 2, 3
family; the asymptotic distribution of triples of this form influences the behavior of the DGM quality, as follows.

Theorem 3.5
.

If there exist infinitely many pairs
(i,j)\subset(\mathbb{Z}^{+})^{2}
such that
c=2^{i}+3^{j}
is prime, then
\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
N\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, and pick
c_{0}>\exp(3N\sqrt{3N})
. Then, by the above bound,
s_{c}\geq q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)>\frac{1}{3}\cdot(\ln(c_{0}))^{2/3}>N
for all
power of 2, 3
triples
(a,b,c)
with
c>c_{0}
.
∎

This method may be extended to establish the following theorem in greater generality, with a slight modification of the relevant bound.

Theorem 3.6
.

For distinct primes
p,q
, if there are infinitely many pairs
(i,j)
with
c=p^{i}+q^{j}
prime, then

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(p^{i},q^{j},c)=(\ln c)^{2/3}/\sqrt[3]{3\ln p\ln q}\to\infty
, such that
\limsup s_{c}=\infty
.

The previous two constructions are special cases of the following general theorem, which allows the integers
a,b
in an
abc
-triple to be more general products of powers over
fixed
sets of primes.

Theorem 3.7

(Fixed sequence of primes)
.

For fixed primes
a_{1},\dots,a_{n}
and
b_{1},\dots,b_{m}
and exponents
i_{1},\dots,i_{n},j_{1},\dots,j_{m}
in
\mathbb{Z}^{+}
, let
a=\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{i_{k}}
,
b=\prod_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}^{j_{k}}
,
c=a+b
. If there are infinitely many tuples
(i_{1},\dots,i_{n},j_{1},\dots,j_{m})
such that
c
is prime, then
\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}=\infty
.

Proof.

Let
\left(\prod_{k=1}^{n}a_{k}^{i_{k}},\prod_{k=1}^{m}b_{k}^{j_{k}},c\right)
be such a valid
abc
-triple. For such triples, we have
\omega=m+n+1
, and equation (
3
) gives

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln c}{(m\!+\!n\!+\!1)\left(\ln(c)\cdot{\prod_{i=1}^{m+n}(\ln(p_{i}))}\right)^{1/{m+n+1}}}

\displaystyle=(\ln c)^{1-\frac{1}{m+n+1}}\cdot K,

where
K=\frac{1}{m+n+1}\bigl(\prod_{i=1}^{m+n}\ln p_{i}\bigr)^{-1/(m+n+1)}
is independent of the choice of exponents
i_{1},\dotsc,i_{n},j_{1},\dotsc,j_{m}
. Since
1-1/(m+n+1)>0
, the logarithmic term, and hence
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}\to\infty
, diverge along the infinite family of
abc
-triples as
c\rightarrow\infty
.
∎

Theorems
3.5
and
3.6
thereby give the cases
m=n=1
for the above result, with the rate of growth decreasing as more primes are admitted (and
m,n
grow).

Such methods of searching for triples with concise factorizations also yield other families of the form
(1,c-1,c)
for various special choices of
c
. For instance, for a given Fermat prime
p=2^{n}+1
, a
Fermat triple
of the form
(1,2^{n},2^{n+1})
may be formed, such that
\omega=2
. An analogous derivation to Thm.
3.5
for this value of
\omega
gives
q_{DGM}(1,2^{n},2^{n+1})\geq\sqrt{n}/2
for all such triples. Hence, divergent asymptotic behavior occurs, conditionally on the infinitude of Fermat primes, but only five such primes are known
[
31
]
).

3.2.1.
Mersenne Triples

A natural extension of this approach, however, is to invoke Mersenne primes (of the form
2^{n}-1
) rather than Fermat primes. For all primes of the form
2^{n}-1
, we must have that
n
itself is prime, creating
Mersenne triples
of the form
(1,2^{n}-1,2^{n})
for suitable prime
n,2^{n}-1

[
5
]
. There are 51 known Mersenne primes; the largest known prime overall is Mersenne, with
n=82{,}589{,}933

[
13
]
. In an analogous manner to the Fermat triple case, a bound on
q_{\text{DGM}}
may be established for Mersenne triples.

Theorem 3.8
.

For all Mersenne triples
(1,2^{n}-1,2^{n})
,
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)=\sqrt{n}/2
.

Proof.

Let
(1,2^{n}-1,2^{n})
be a Mersenne triple, such that
n,2^{n}-1
are both prime. Then, we must have that
\omega=2
, and hence, applying (
3
),

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}=\frac{\ln c}{2\sqrt{\ln 2\cdot\ln c}}

\displaystyle=\sqrt{n\ln 2}\cdot\frac{1}{2\sqrt{\ln 2}}=\frac{\sqrt{n}}{2}.\qed

Thus, for the largest known Mersenne prime, which corresponds to
n_{m}=82{,}589{,}933
, we have

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(1,\,2^{n_{m}}-1,\,2^{n_{m}})=\frac{\sqrt{82{,}589{,}933}}{2}\approx 4543.95,

which is the triple corresponding to the highest DGM quality that we found using the Mersenne method.

Therefore, upon the Lenstra-Pomerance-Wagstaff conjecture that there exist an infinitude of Mersenne primes (with distribution such that the number of such primes below exponent
n
is approximated by
\exp(\gamma)\cdot\log(\log(n)))
for Euler-Mascheroni constant
\gamma
), it follows from Theorem
3.8
that
\limsup_{c\rightarrow\infty}s_{c}=\infty

[
25
]
. Conversely, if
s_{c}
is bounded above, then it follows that the conjecture that there exist infinitely many Mersenne primes fails.

These results motivate the following analogue of the
abc
-conjecture for the DGM quality; although this analogue is not quite as interesting as the conjecture itself, since we establish that the quality is
unbounded
asymptotically, it nonetheless lends itself to modifications that will be of interest in Section
4
.

Theorem 3.9

(DGM Quality Asymptotics)
.

We have that
\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}=\infty
.

We remark that Theorem
3.9
follows from either (i) the infinitude of Mersenne primes, (ii) the infinitude of Fermat primes, (iii) the infinite of primes of the form
p^{i}+q^{j}
for fixed primes
p,q
, or (iv) any case covered by Theorem
3.7
. These observations follow from Theorems
3.5
,
3.6
,
3.7
, and
3.8
.

3.2.2.
Alternative Conditional Proof through Twin Primes

The constructions in Theorems
3.5
-
3.8
all share the structural property that at most one of the entries of the
abc
-triple
(a,b,c)
remains a prime, such that
\omega(abc)
remains invariant over the resulting families. We now give a distinct conditional route in which two such primes grow with the size of
c
, such that the constraint is provided by twin primes. This yields a new family of triples whose existence is not subsumed by Theorems
3.5
–
3.8
, since two entries of the triple vary (rather than being drawn from a fixed prime set).
5
5

5

While this method is less computationally efficient, it motivates the result in section
3.2.3
that gives an unconditional proof of Theorem
3.9
.

Theorem 3.10

(Twin-prime approach to Theorem
3.9
)
.

All triples of the form
(a,b,c)=(2,p,p+2)
for
p
prime satisfy

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(2,p,p+2)\in\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\ln c)^{1/3}}{3\,(\ln 2)^{1/3}}\right)

as
c\rightarrow\infty
, and hence if the twin prime conjecture holds,
\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}=\infty
, establishing Theorem
3.9
.

Proof.

Accordingly, all such triples
(a,b,c)=(2,p,p+2)
are
abc
-triples, since
p
is odd and both
p,p+2
are primes. Thus,
\omega=3
results, with all three primes being distinct since
p\geq 3
is required of a twin prime pair.

Thus, applying equation (
3
), we have

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(2,p,p+2)=\frac{\ln(p+2)}{3\left(\ln 2\cdot\ln(p)\cdot\ln(p+2)\right)^{1/3}}.

For large
p
, we may invoke the approximation that
\ln(p+2)=\ln p+\ln(1+2/p)=\ln p+\mathcal{O}(1/p)
, and hence
\ln p\cdot\ln(p+2)=(\ln p)^{2}(1+o(1))
, yielding the result that

({\ln 2\cdot\ln p\cdot\ln(p+2)})^{1/3}=(\ln 2)^{1/3}(\ln p)^{2/3}(1+o(1)).

Substitution yields

\displaystyle q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(c)}{3\,(\ln 2)^{1/3}(\ln(c))^{2/3}(1+o(1))}

\displaystyle\in\mathcal{O}\left(\frac{(\ln(c))^{1/3}}{3\,(\ln 2)^{1/3}}\right)

for triples of this form. Therefore, up to constant scaling, the DGM quality grows proportionately to
(\ln(c))^{1/3}
along this family; thus, if the twin-prime family is infinite, then there are infinite such triples of the form
(2,p,p+2)
, and we have
\limsup_{c\rightarrow\infty}s_{c}=\infty
.
∎

We remark that Theorem
3.10
is structurally distinct from the strategy of Mersenne primes or fixed families of primes, since the twin prime direction yields a quality metric that grows slightly
slower
in
\ln(c)
asymptotically, and harnesses an entirely disjoint family of triples, yet nonetheless gives a divergent result for
s_{c}
.
6
6

6

By the Hardy-Littlewood prime
k
-tuples heuristic (which remains unproven), the density of twin primes below
N
asymptotically approaches
2C_{2}N/(\ln N)^{2}
with
C_{2}
the twin-prime constant, so this family is heuristically far denser than the Mersenne family, which may be advantageous for future computational methods on the DGM quality
[
15
]
.

3.2.3.
Implications of Chen’s Theorem

All preceding routes to establish Theorem
3.9
are conditional on conjectures (for instance, the Twin-Prime or Lenstra-Pomerance-Wagstaff conjectures). We now demonstrate that, upon coupling the twin-prime construction provided by Theorem
3.10
with a slight weakening involving numbers that are the products of exactly two primes (
P_{2}
numbers), then the theorem holds unconditionally. To establish this, we invoke the following result
[
6
]
.

Theorem 3.11

(Chen
[
6
]
)
.

There exist infinitely many primes
p
such that
p+2
is either (i) prime, or (ii) a product of two primes – i.e., a
P_{2}
number.

Let the set
S
be the set of primes
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{+}
such that
p+2
lies in
\mathbb{P}\cup P_{2}
. Then, by the above theorem, we know
|S|=\infty
. Harnessing this result gives the following.

Theorem 3.12

(Unconditional proof of Theorem
3.9
)
.

We have
\limsup_{c\rightarrow\infty}s_{c}=\infty
per the following family: for all
p\in S
, the triple
(a,b,c)=(2,p,p+2)
satisfies

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)\;\geq\;\frac{(\ln(c))^{1/4}}{4\,(\ln(2)/4)^{1/4}}.

Proof.

Fix some
p\in S
with
p\geq 3
; Theorem
3.11
guarantees infinitely many such
p
. Note that either
p+2
lies in
\mathbb{P}
, or it is a
P_{2}
number. In either case, since
p,p+2
are odd, the elements
2,p,p+2
are pairwise coprime, such that
(a,b,c):=(2,p,p+2)
is an
abc
-triple. We now split into two cases.

The first case is when
p+2
is prime. In this case, we simply apply the argument of Theorem
3.10
since
\omega=3
in this case, we have

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)\geq\frac{(\ln c)^{1/3}}{3\,(\ln(2))^{1/3}}\geq\frac{\ln(c)^{1/4}}{4(\ln(2)/4)^{1/4}},

where the latter inequality holds for sufficiently large
c
.

In the latter case, we have that
p+2\in P_{2}
, and hence
p+2=qr
for
q,r
prime and, without loss of generality,
q\leq r
. Note that since
p+2
is odd, both
q,r
are odd; additionally, we have
p\nmid p+2
, since
p\geq 3
, and hence
p
does not lie in the set
\{q,r\}
. Therefore, the primes dividing
abc=2pqr
are exactly
\{2,p,q,r\}
, which give
\omega=4
. We note that the subcase
q=r
collapses to
\omega=3
and gives an even stronger bound than the twin prime case above, so we assume
q<r
for the remainder of the derivation. Applying (
3
) gives

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)=\frac{\ln c}{4({\ln 2\cdot\ln p\cdot\ln q\cdot\ln r})^{1/4}}.

Noting that
\ln(q)+\ln(r)=\ln(qr)=\ln(c)
, AM-GM applied to
\ln(q),\ln(r)
gives
\sqrt{\ln(q)\ln(r)}\leq\ln(c)/2
. Hence, together with
p<c
, we have

\ln(2)\cdot\ln(p)\cdot\ln(q)\cdot\ln(r)\;\leq\;\frac{\ln(2)}{4}\,(\ln(c))^{3}.

Therefore, taking fourth roots,

q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)\;\geq\;\frac{\ln(c)}{4\,(\ln(2)/4)^{1/4}(\ln(c))^{3/4}}\;=\;\frac{(\ln(c))^{1/4}}{4\,(\ln(2)/4)^{1/4}},

and the desired inequality holds in either case. Since
|S|=\infty
,
c=p+2
is unbounded along such triples corresponding to
p\in S
, so the right-hand side tends to
\infty
. Thus,
\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}=\infty
and Theorem
3.9
holds.
∎

We remark briefly on the exponent in this proof. Our exponent on the logarithmic term is
1/4
, which derives from the AM-GM usage; however, the proof of Chen’s theorem implies that the small prime factor
q
in the proof above satisfies that
\log(q)/\log(p+2)
is bounded above by
1/2+o(1)
.
7
7

7

This follows from asymptotics under the requirement
p^{1/10}<q\leq p^{1/2}\leq r\leq p^{0.9}

[
6
]
.
Under such size control of
q
, the bound improves to
q_{\text{DGM}}(a,b,c)\sim(\ln(c))^{1/3-\varepsilon}
along this family, which matches those of the twin prime family. We leave a fully unconditional refinement of the critical exponent of divergence of the DGM quality as an open problem.
8
8

8

One might define a
running average

r(c_{0}):=\sum_{x\in A}q_{DGM}(x)/|A|
, where
A=\{\text{triples\ }(a,b,c):c\leq c_{0}\}
; this experimentally tends to a constant near
0.528
as
c\rightarrow\infty
.

4.
DGM Quality Class

4.1.
Motivation and Definition

Noting that the DGM Quality asymptotically tends to infinity, while the standard quality conjecturally tends to
1
in a
\lim\sup
fashion, we produce tunable variants of the DGM quality using parameters
\alpha,\beta
, as follows.

Definition 4.1

(DGM Quality Class)
.

For specific
\alpha\geq 0
,
\beta>0
and
U=\{p_{i}^{\omega^{\alpha}}:p_{i}\mid abc,p_{i}\in\mathbb{P}\}
, we set

q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta):=\frac{\ln(c)}{\bigl(\ln(\mathrm{DGM}(U))\bigr)^{\beta}}.

Note that
q_{C}(\cdot;1,1)
reduces to
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
, and hence
\alpha
corresponds to a finer influence of
\omega
itself, whereas
\beta
controls overall growth of the denominator.

Fix
\alpha,\beta
as above. By noting that

\displaystyle\ln(\mathrm{DGM}(U))

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\omega}\textstyle\sum_{i}\ln\ln(p_{i}^{\omega^{\alpha}})\bigr)

\displaystyle=\exp\bigl(\alpha\ln\omega+\tfrac{1}{\omega}\textstyle\sum_{i}\ln\ln p_{i}\bigr)

\displaystyle=\omega^{\alpha}\cdot\sqrt[\omega]{\textstyle\prod_{i}\ln p_{i}},

it follows that

(4)

\displaystyle q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\ln c}{\bigl(\omega^{\alpha}\cdot\sqrt[\omega]{\prod_{i=1}^{\omega}\ln p_{i}}\bigr)^{\beta}}

over all
abc
-triples (
a,b,c
), per a similar rewriting of our DGM quality. Therefore, an analogous result to Theorem
3.4
may be established.

Theorem 4.2
.

For all
abc
-triples and
\alpha\in[0,1]
,
\beta>0
,

q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta)>\frac{\ln(c)}{(\omega^{\alpha-1}\ln(abc))^{\beta}}\geq\frac{1}{3(\omega^{\alpha-1})^{\beta}}>\frac{1}{3}.

Proof.

We apply AM-GM, such that
\sqrt[\omega]{\prod\ln p_{i}}\leq\tfrac{1}{\omega}\sum\ln p_{i}
; Lemma
3.3
gives that
q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta)>\ln(c)/((\omega^{\alpha-1}\ln(abc))^{\beta})
. This bound holds independent of the restriction on
\alpha
. Additionally, substituting
\ln(abc)<3\ln(c)
and
\omega^{\alpha-1}\leq 1
for
\alpha\leq 1
, we have the desired inequality.
∎

We may also establish a few elementary results with respect to the variation of
\alpha
while
\beta
is fixed. Indeed, for
0\leq\alpha_{1}<\alpha_{2}
and some fixed
\beta>0
, we have that
q_{C}(\cdot;\alpha_{1},\beta)>q_{C}(\cdot;\alpha_{2},\beta)
, since the denominator of equation (
4
) is smaller for
\alpha_{1}
while
\beta
is held constant. Indeed, for the extreme case
\alpha=0
, we observe
q_{C}(a,b,c;0,1)=\omega\cdot q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
. In this case, we observe that the quality
q_{C}(a,b,c;0,1)
diverges (as a consequence of Theorem
3.9
) and is bounded below by
1
(independently resolving the cases
\omega\geq 3
, where we apply Theorem
3.4
;
\omega=2
, where we note that Theorem
4.2
with
\alpha=0
yields
q_{C}(a,b,c;0,1)>\omega/2=1
; and
\omega=1
, which gives a single triple). Additionally, a natural extension of Theorem
3.8
gives
q_{C}(a,b,c;0,1)\geq\sqrt{n}
for Mersenne triples, and similarly for triples of the form in Theorem
3.12
, such that divergence is guaranteed.

We may also consider the variation of
\beta
under fixed values of
\alpha
. We note that the increase of
\beta
slows growth of the quality metric monotonically. That is,
\beta=2
keeps
q_{C}(\cdot;1,2)\leq 2
for all examined triples; the high-quality triples for
\beta=2
thereby form a refined subset of those for
\beta=1
. Thus, the asymptotic control of
\beta
lends itself to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4.3
.

For every
\alpha\geq 0
, there exists
\beta=\beta(\alpha)>0
such that

\limsup_{c\to\infty}\max\{q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta):(a,b,c)\text{ abc-triple}\}=L

for some finite, nonzero
L
.

4.2.
Phase Transitions in DGM Parameter Regimes

Conjecture
4.3
asserts that, for each
\alpha
, there exists some
\beta
giving a finite, nonzero limit. We investigate partial justifications of this claim through variants with respect to specific values of
\beta
that give
phase transitions
in the asymptotic behavior of the DGM quality class. We harness explicit families from Section
3.2
, identifying a
critical

\beta
from which the asymptotic quality transitions from divergence to convergence to a finite value.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a family of
abc
-triples such that
\omega(abc)=\omega
is constant (motivated by Section
4

k
of the
\omega

\Theta(c)

\omega-k

(\omega,k)

(\omega,k)=(2,1)

(\omega,k)=(3,1)

Theorem 4.4

(Phase transition for

Suppose there exists an infinite family
\mathcal{F}
of

(\omega,k)

\omega,k

\beta_{0}=\omega/k
, such that

\lim_{c\to\infty,\,(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{F}}q_{C}\;=\;\begin{cases}\infty&\beta<\omega/k,\\[2.0pt]
\dfrac{1}{\omega^{\alpha\omega/k}\cdot K^{1/k}}&\beta=\omega/k,\\[6.0pt]
0&\beta>\omega/k,\end{cases}

for a finite constant
K
.

Proof.

Let
(a,b,c)\in\mathcal{F}
, and write the primes dividing
abc
as
\{p_{1},\dots,p_{\omega-k},q_{1},\dots,q_{k}\}
, such that the primes
p_{i}
are all bounded and we have that
\ln(q_{j})=\ln(c)\cdot(1+o(1))
for each
j\in\{1,\dotsc,k\}
. Set
K:=\prod_{i=1}^{\omega-k}\ln p_{i}
, which is constant with respect to
(a,b,c)
. Applying (
4
) gives

q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta)=\frac{\ln(c)}{\bigl(\omega^{\alpha}\sqrt[\omega]{\prod_{i}\ln(p_{i})\cdot\prod_{j}\ln(q_{j})}\bigr)^{\beta}}.

Since
\ln q_{j}=\ln c\cdot(1+o(1))
for
j=1,\dots,k
, the inner product factors as
\textstyle\prod_{i}\ln(p_{i})\prod_{j}\ln(q_{j})=K\cdot(\ln(c))^{k}\cdot(1+o(1))
. Substitution gives

\displaystyle q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle=\frac{\ln(c)}{\omega^{\alpha\beta}\,\left(\prod_{i=1}^{\omega-k}\ln p_{i}\right)^{\beta/\omega}(\ln(c))^{k\beta/\omega}(1+o(1))}

\displaystyle=\omega^{-\alpha\beta}K^{-\beta/\omega}(\ln(c))^{1-k\beta/\omega}(1+o(1)).

Noting that all terms but the
\ln(c)
term are constant in the resulting expression, it follows that
\mathrm{sgn}(1-k\beta/\omega)
determines the asymptotic behavior of
q_{C}(\cdot;\alpha,\beta)
on such triples. Indeed, at
\beta=\omega/k
,
\ln(c)
vanishes and the limit equals
\omega^{-\alpha\omega/k}K^{-1/k}
, as desired. Note that, assuming this result, the resulting
\lim\sup q_{C}(a,b,c_{0})
over triples
(a,b,c_{0})
, as
c_{0}\rightarrow\infty
, must tend to
\infty
for
\beta<\omega/k
and must be bounded below by
\omega^{-\alpha\omega/k}K^{-1/k}
at
\beta=\omega/k
.
∎

Theorem 4.6

(
\omega=2

For
\alpha\geq 0

\limsup_{c\to\infty}s_{c}^{(2)}(\alpha,\beta)\;=\;\begin{cases}+\infty&\beta<2\\
\frac{1}{2^{2\alpha}\,\ln 2}&\beta=2\\
0&\beta>2;\end{cases}

the cases are conditional on the infinitude of Mersenne primes (or, equivalently, of Fermat primes).

Proof.

We first discuss the structure of triples with
\omega=2
. If
(a,b,c)
is such a triple, then let the two primes be of the form
\{p_{1},p_{2}\}
,
p_{1}\neq p_{2}
. Since
a,b
are coprime, we may write
a=p_{1}^{i}
and
b=p_{2}^{j}
for some
i,j\geq 0
up to relabeling. Hence, we have
c=p_{1}^{i}+p_{2}^{j}
, which must be of the form
c=p_{1}^{m}p_{2}^{n}
for some
m,n\geq 0
. Additionally, the coprime constraint guarantees that either
c=1
, which is impossible (as
a,b\geq 1
), or that one of
a,b
must be
1
. In the latter case, assume that
a=1
, forcing the equation
1+p_{2}^{j}=p_{1}^{m}p_{2}^{n}
. Thus, either
j=0
, giving the triple
(1,1,2)
, or
n=0
by coprimality. In the
n=0
situation, we have
b=p_{1}^{m}-1=p_{2}^{j}
, such that
p_{2}^{j}+1=p_{1}^{m}

. Substitution into the standard quality in Def.

gives

q_{s,n}=\frac{\ln(c_{n})}{\ln(N_{n})}=\frac{q_{C}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n};\alpha,1)\cdot\omega_{n}^{\alpha}\cdot G_{n}}{\omega_{n}\cdot A_{n}}

Hence, substitution of the packing efficiency
\eta_{n}
immediately gives

\displaystyle q_{s,n}=q_{C}(\cdot;\alpha,1)\cdot\omega_{n}^{\alpha-1}\cdot\eta_{n}.

In the critical boundary, we assume that the prime count scales as
\omega_{n}=\delta(\ln c_{n})^{\gamma}\bigl(1+o(1)\bigr)
, such that
\alpha=1/\gamma
. By Theorem
4.10
, the limit supremum of
q_{C}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n};1/\gamma,1)
converges exactly to the boundary constant
\frac{1}{\delta^{1/\gamma}\ln 2}
. Hence, we have

\limsup_{n\to\infty}q_{s,n}\leq\left(\frac{1}{\delta^{1/\gamma}\ln 2}\right)\cdot\limsup_{n\to\infty}\left(\omega_{n}^{1/\gamma-1}\cdot\eta_{n}\right),

as desired.

As an extension, if, additionally, the largest prime factor

P_{n}:=P(N_{n})
satisfies
\ln P_{n}\in O(\ln c_{n})
(i.e.,
P_{n}\sim c_{n}^{\kappa}

for some
\kappa>0
), then by Lemma
4.12
, we have
\eta_{n}=\mathcal{O}\!\left((\ln c_{n})^{1/\omega_{n}-1}\right),
and hence
\omega_{n}^{\frac{1-\gamma}{\gamma}}\cdot\eta_{n}=\mathcal{O}\left((\ln c_{n})^{1/\omega_{n}-\gamma}\right)\rightarrow 0
as
n\to\infty
, since
\gamma>0
. Consequently, we must have
\limsup_{n\to\infty}\,q_{s,n}=0.

∎

Hence, our phase transitions build towards a correspondence between the
standard
quality and the DGM quality class, mediated by the primes appearing in the factorization of families of triples.

4.4.
Sharp Packing Bounds

The factorization of Theorem
4.13
reduces at
\alpha=1
to the exact identity

q_{s}=\eta\,q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
, with
\eta=G/A
the packing efficiency of Lemma
4.12
. Since
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
diverges (Theorem
3.9
) while
q_{s}

is bounded
conjecturally
, the resulting comparison is governed by the decay of
\eta
; thus, extracting analytic content from this bound requires a lower bound on
\eta
– which, in principle, serves as a reverse AM-GM inequality. We supply such a bound via the Specht ratio, which gives a restatement of the
abc
-conjecture in terms of the DGM quality.

Lemma 4.14

(Specht Implications)
.

Let
(a,b,c)
be an
abc
-triple with
\omega\geq 2
and maximal prime divisor
P
,
and set

and
S(h)=\frac{(h-1)\,h^{1/(h-1)}}{e\ln h}
as the Specht ratio, for
h>1
; we define
S(1)=1
. Then,
q_{s}(\cdot)=\eta\,q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(\cdot)
with
1/S(h)\leq\eta\leq 1
, and therefore

q_{s}(a,b,c)\;\leq\;q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(a,b,c)\;\leq\;S(h)\,q_{s}(a,b,c).

Observe
S(h)=\frac{h}{e\ln h}\cdot(1+o(1))
for large
h
.

Proof.

The identity
q_{s}=\eta\,q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
is the case
\alpha=1
of Theorem
4.13
, since
q_{C}(\cdot;1,1)=q_{\mathrm{DGM}}(\cdot)
. The upper
bound
\eta\leq 1
is given by the AM-GM inequality, as noted in Lemma

4.12
. For the lower bound, we apply Specht’s inequality to the
positive reals
\ell_{i}:=\ln p_{i}
, which lie in
(\ln 2,\ln P)
with maximal comparative ratio being bounded by
\ln(P)/\ln(2)=h
by monotonicity
[
27
]
. The arithmetic (
A
) and geometric (
G
) means of the
\ell_{i}
satisfy

, such that
\eta=G/A\geq 1/S(h)
. Substituting
\eta\in(1/S(h),1)
into
q_{s}=\eta\,q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
gives
q_{s}\leq q_{\mathrm{DGM}}\leq S(h)\,q_{s}
.
∎

Theorem 4.15

(Packing-efficiency formulation of
abc

.

The

-conjecture (Conjecture

) is equivalent to the assertion that, for every

for all but finitely many

Proof.

By Lemma

,
q_{s}=\eta\,q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
exactly; thus, the inequalities

and

are equivalent over each

, which establishes the equivalence. Now, let us assume

, and using

from Lemma

with the asymptotic result following from the bound on

for large

. Since
P\leq c

follows.
∎

4.5.
Power-Mean Interpolation

As a last piece of analysis on the DGM quality itself, we note that the standard quality
q_{s}
and the DGM quality
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
are the endpoints of a one-parameter family that is obtained by replacing the geometric mean in the denominator of
q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
with a generalized power mean (effectively, a
p
-norm). Indeed, for
t\in[0,1]
, let us set
\kappa=1-t
and let
M_{\kappa}
be the power mean of the set
\{\ln p_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\omega}
, such that

M_{\kappa}(\{\ln p_{i}\})=\begin{cases}\bigl(\tfrac{1}{\omega}\sum_{i}(\ln p_{i})^{\kappa}\bigr)^{1/\kappa}&0<\kappa\leq 1\\[2.0pt]
\sqrt[\omega]{\prod_{i}\ln p_{i}}&\kappa=0,\end{cases}

such that

q_{t}(a,b,c)\;=\;\frac{\ln c}{\omega\cdot M_{1-t}(\{\ln p_{i}\})}.

satisfies that
q_{0}=q_{s}
and
q_{1}=q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
(since
M_{1}
recovers the arithmetic mean, and
M_{0}
recovers the geometric mean). By Maclaurin’s inequality,
M_{\kappa}
is non-decreasing in
{\kappa}
, hence
q_{t}
is non-decreasing in
t
on every triple; this establishes
q_{s}\leq q_{\mathrm{DGM}}
pointwise.

We note, briefly, that for our
(\omega,k)
-families, as defined in Section
4.2
, specific bounds may be generated on the limiting value of
q_{t}
on these families. Indeed, for
t\in[0,1)
, the same methods as in Section
4.2
give that
q_{t}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\rightarrow 2^{1/(1-t)}/2
on Mersenne triples
a_{n},b_{n},c_{n}
(which have
(\omega,k)=(2,1)
), and
q_{t}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\rightarrow(3/2)^{1/(1-t)}/3
on twin prime triples (which have
(\omega,k)=(3,2)
). In general, we have
q_{t}(a_{n},b_{n},c_{n})\rightarrow(\omega/k)^{1/(1-t)}/\omega
for all such
\omega,k,t
, and corresponding families.
12
12

12

Explicitly, this occurs since we have
k
primes growing as
\Theta(\ln(c))
while the other
\omega-k
primes are bounded, such that
(M_{\kappa}(\ln(\{p_{i}\}))^{\kappa}=1/\omega(k(\ln(c))^{\kappa}+O(1))
, and
M_{\kappa}(\ln(\{p_{i}\}))\sim(k/\omega)^{1/\kappa}\ln(c)

yields the limiting form.
Hence, divergence only occurs as

from below; this occurs since, for

, except at

.

One might consider the question of when

is attainable for specific

and choices of triples. To attain this, we may consider triples
(1,p-1,p)
, such that the number of primes

and

is bounded by

for some function

, the formulation of

gives

q_{t}(1,p-1,p)\;=\;\frac{\omega^{1/(1-t)-1}}{\bigl((\omega-1)\delta(p)^{1-t}+1\bigr)^{1/(1-t)}(1+o(1))};

thus, if
\delta

\mathcal{O}(\omega^{-1/(1-t)})
, we have
q_{t}\sim(\ln\ln p)^{t/(1-t)}
, which diverges as

. The former condition requires
\delta(p)\rightarrow 0
at the rate

\delta(p)=(\ln\ln p)^{-1/(1-t)}(1+o(1))

P(p-1)\leq p^{0.5366}
with
\omega
bounded

2

.

On the other hand, the
abc
-conjecture itself gives
upper

q_{t}
, for fixed

Theorem 4.16
.

Assuming the
abc
-conjecture, for every
\varepsilon>0
,
t\in[0,1)
,
\omega_{0}\geq 2
, and

Fix

.

5.5.
Parametric Hulls over the DGM Class

As extensional material, we observe that the DGM class from Section
4
provides a continuum of metrics indexed by
(\alpha,\beta)
, such that the highest-quality triples depend on
where
in this continuum of metrics the current choice of
q_{C}(\cdot;\alpha,\beta)

For instance, a triple (

with

\beta=1

\beta=2
, as the nested record sets of Tables

5

Hence, rather than rescanning the pool of triples at each parameter value, we determine high-quality triples using a structural feature of the DGM class. We write
G=(\prod_{i}\ln p_{i})^{1/\omega}
as in Section
4
and invoke equation (
4

\ln(q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta))=\ln(\ln(c))-\beta\ln(G)-(\alpha\beta)\ln\omega,

which, in the coordinates
(u,v):=(\beta,\alpha\beta)
, is a bilinear map in
(\alpha,\beta)
with intercept
\ln(\ln(c))
and gradient
(-\ln G,-\ln\omega)
. Thus, for a fixed triple
(a,b,c)
(which fix
\omega,G

\alpha,\beta
over
\alpha\geq 0,\beta>0
gives a plane determined the corresponding parameters
\{u>0,\,v\geq 0\}
(the image of the set
\{\beta>0,\,\alpha\geq 0\}
under the bijection
\alpha\leftarrow v/u,\beta\leftarrow u
). If such a plane is embedded in a space homeomorphic to
\mathbb{R}^{3}
with the
height
of a point on the plane corresponding to the value of
\ln(q_{C}(a,b,c;\alpha,\beta))
for fixed
(a,b,c)
and varying
\alpha,\beta

(\alpha,\beta)
amounts to comparing the vertical heights of the resulting planes.

Determining
optimal
choices of triples for each set of parameters
(\alpha,\beta)
follows from this geometric construction. Since maximizing
q_{C}
is functionally equivalent to maximizing

\ln(q_{C})
, the highest attainable log-quality over a set
\mathscr{T}:=\{T_{i}\}
of
n

abc
-triples
(a_{i},b_{i},c_{i})
is the pointwise maximum

F(u,v):=\max_{i}\bigl(\ln(\ln(c_{i}))-u\ln G_{i}-v\ln\omega_{i}\bigr),

which is precisely the upper envelope of the
n
planes. The triple that realizes such a maximum at the point
(u,v)
is therefore a
record-setter
for the DGM quality (i.e., its quality is higher than that of any triple with smaller
c
) for such an
(\alpha,\beta)
pair. A triple whose plane lies below the envelope everywhere is never optimal, since if the triple
T_{i}
is maximized at no choice of parameter, then for every

(\alpha,\beta)
some

\ln(q_{C}(T_{j};\alpha,\beta))>\ln(q_{C}(T_{i};\alpha,\beta))

T_{i}
is dominated
within the class. The record-setting triples are therefore exactly
those contributing a face to the broad envelope; equivalently, such triples form the vertices of the lower convex hull of the points
(\ln G_{i},\ln(\omega_{i}),\ln\ln c_{i})
. This method generates a complete set of record-setters that supersedes the case-by-case analysis exhibited in scenarios such as Tables
4
–
5

Moreover we observe that projecting such a record-setting envelope onto the
(u,v)
-plane partitions the parameter region into convex domains, one per record-setting triple, across which the

(\alpha,\beta)

We defined novel quality metrics that prioritize triples with small

3.2

3

4

We extended our quality metrics via a parametrization through the DGM quality class in Sec.
4
to modulate asymptotic behavior. Upon establishing phase transition results that determined asymptotics with respect to to parameter
\beta

4.4

4.6
,
4.7

4.8
