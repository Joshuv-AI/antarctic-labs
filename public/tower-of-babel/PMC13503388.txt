Safety and effectiveness of etonogestrel implant in Chinese women requiring contraception: a multicenter, prospective, observational study
Authors: Xinyan Liu, Xiaoye Wang, Xiao’ou Xu, Yanfei Zhou, Wenxia Ma, Li Jiang, Qiwei Qi, Jian Li, Tinghui Yu, John W Grunden, Chengliang Xiong
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractObjectiveTo assess the overall safety and effectiveness of the etonogestrel implant radiopaque in real-world clinical settings in China.MethodsA multicenter, prospective, single-arm, observational study was conducted in women (aged ≥ 18 years) across 31 centers in China (March 2016–July 2021). The implant was prescribed as part of routine clinical practice. Primary outcomes included overall safety and topical (local) safety profile; secondary outcomes included participant and physician satisfaction and contraceptive effectiveness of the etonogestrel implant.ResultsOf 1,901 women using the etonogestrel implant, 45.5% completed the 3-year study. The mean (SD) age of the women was 32.1 (5.6) years and the mean (SD) implant exposure duration was 783.7 (388.7) days. Overall, 47.9% reported drug-related adverse events (AEs; excluding topical AEs), 1.1% reported serious AEs, and 10.4% reported topical AEs after insertion. Vaginal bleeding (15.6%) was the most frequent drug-related AE. At 36 months after insertion or upon removal, most women were satisfied (38.9%) or very satisfied (23.7%) with the implant. Among 96 physicians who completed questionnaires following the first insertion, 57.3% were very satisfied and 38.5% were satisfied. Most (98.9%) of the physicians reported satisfaction following their first removal procedure. Of them, 65.3% were very satisfied and 33.7% were satisfied. One (1.1%) physician was neither satisfied nor dissatisfied. One (0.05%) in-treatment pregnancy was reported.ConclusionSafety findings from this study in China’s real-world clinical setting were consistent with the clinical development program without unexpected safety signals. Observed contraceptive effectiveness was consistent with established evidence for the implant, with general satisfaction reported among Chinese women.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1007/s00404-026-08433-z.Keywords: Contraceptive implant, Effectiveness, Etonogestrel, Safety, Satisfaction

What does this study add to the clinical work?

This study provides real-world evidence supporting the continued use of the etonogestrel long-acting contraceptive implant as a reliable contraceptive option, with no new safety concerns and high satisfaction reported among women and clinicians.

Introduction

Contraception is an important part of women’s health for preventing unplanned pregnancies. According to the 2019 Global Burden of Disease Study, 160 million women of reproductive age have unmet contraceptive needs worldwide [1]. Between 2015 and 2019, about 121 million unplanned pregnancies occurred annually. Of these, 61% ended in abortion, amounting to 73.3 million abortions per year [2].

In China, condoms, oral contraceptives, and emergency contraceptives account for most contraceptive practices [3]. Unfortunately, user compliance is often low with short-acting hormonal contraceptives, such as pills, due to user-dependent behavior [4–6]. User dependence may contribute to an unintended pregnancy, which may also result from imprecise dosage recommendations or even inaccessibility [7–9]. In recent years, various long-acting reversible contraception (LARC) methods have become popular and essential due to their high contraceptive effectiveness, acceptable safety profile, longer duration of action, independence of user compliance, and no requirement of frequent re-supply as compared to short-acting contraception methods. Subdermal hormonal implants are commonly used as LARC methods, owing to their unique drug delivery mode and ability to provide long contraceptive protection [10, 11].

The etonogestrel implant was first marketed in 1998 in Indonesia. This implant is a single rod (4-cm long and 2-mm wide) with a solid ethylene vinyl acetate (EVA) core containing 68 mg of etonogestrel, the active metabolite of desogestrel, a progestin widely used in hormonal contraceptives. Since 2018, > 3.3 million implants have been distributed globally [12]. In China, a new etonogestrel implant received regulatory approval in 2013. This implant offered enhanced usability and safety compared to the original product, featuring the inclusion of barium sulfate to achieve radiological visibility (radiopacity) and a new insertion device to simplify insertion and localization for practitioners. This approval was based on the demonstration of bioequivalence between the new proprietary implant (Implanon radiopaque, Implanon NXT or Nexplanon elsewhere) and the reference product (Implanon non-radiopaque) [13]. However, China’s regulatory agency requested to conduct a real-world, observational, single-arm phase 4, post-authorization safety study in routine clinical practice as a part of the Sponsor’s post-marketing commitments. Therefore, this study aimed to collect the data for overall and topical (local) safety (adverse events [AEs] and serious adverse events [SAEs]), participant and physician satisfaction, and overall contraceptive effectiveness of the etonogestrel implant in routine clinical practice in China.

Material and methods

Study design

This multicenter, prospective, single-arm, post-marketing, observational study was conducted across 31 sites in China (March 29, 2016–July 28, 2021). The study consisted of a screening period, a 3-year observation period starting on the day of implant insertion and ending on the day of implant removal, and a 1-month follow-up assessment after implant removal. The implant was prescribed as part of routine clinical practice by physicians who believed the implant was an appropriate choice for their patients, not as an intervention designed for clinical study purposes. Women had to decide to use the implant as their contraceptive method prior to joining the study.

As this was a real-world observational post-marketing safety study conducted in routine clinical practice, women were not required to remain in the study for the entire 3‑year duration. Implant removal at any time was allowed and marked the end of observation for that participant.

This study was conducted in compliance with local laws and regulations and in accordance with the guidelines and regulations of the China National Medical Products Administration. The study protocol was approved by the Independent Ethics Committee of each site before the start of the study. All women provided a written or witnessed oral statement. The study is registered with chinadrugtrials.org.cn (CTR20160241) and HMA-EMA (catalogues.ema.europa.eu; EUPAS11131).

Participants

Adult women (≥ 18 years) seeking contraception, capable of answering the questionnaire, and consenting to use the etonogestrel contraceptive implant were included. The pregnancy status was checked before insertion. There were no other specific exclusion criteria.

Study objectives and endpoints

The primary objective was to evaluate the overall safety profile and topical safety profile of the etonogestrel contraceptive radiopaque implant. Secondary objectives were to assess participant and physician satisfaction and estimate the overall contraceptive effectiveness of the etonogestrel contraceptive implant. The overall safety profile included all non-serious AEs considered by investigators to be related to the implant and all SAEs, regardless of assessed relationship to the implant. AEs documented by investigators during routine clinical practice were coded using the Medical Dictionary for Regulatory Activities (MedDRA) system organ class and preferred terms for analysis. A topical AE referred to any AE in the insert arm proximate to the insertion site or, based on the investigator’s assessment, was potentially a topical AE related to the implant. The investigator instructed the participant to observe the insertion site and report any AEs.

Participants completed a participant satisfaction questionnaire with the investigator by telephone at Day 10 and 6 months after implant insertion and at the time of implant removal. The investigator also completed the physician satisfaction questionnaire in the clinic after completing the insertion and after the removal of the first and the fifth participants’ implants.

Contraceptive effectiveness was assessed based on the pregnancies with an estimated conception date occurring between implant insertion and removal (in-treatment pregnancies). Pregnancies conceived before insertion or after removal were not included in the contraceptive effectiveness outcome. A pregnancy test was performed if a pregnancy was suspected outside the scheduled visits.

Statistical methods

All data were analyzed using descriptive statistics. Continuous variables were expressed as mean (± standard deviation [SD]) and median (minimum, maximum). Categorical variables were expressed as frequencies or proportions. Safety endpoints (drug-related AEs, SAEs, and discontinuation due to drug-related AE or SAE) and the frequency of overall and individual topical AEs were summarized as counts, point estimates, and 95% confidence intervals (CI). All two-sided 95% CIs were calculated using the Wilson score method.

Participant and physician satisfaction scores were expressed as counts and percentages for each question. Contraceptive effectiveness was evaluated by summarizing the pregnancies that occurred during the treatment period with radiopaque implant contraception. For contraceptive effectiveness, the Pearl Index (PI; pregnancies per 100 woman-years) was calculated for in-treatment pregnancies, with point estimates and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) derived using the Poisson distribution. All analyses were performed using SAS Version 9.4 (SAS Institute Inc., Cary, NC, USA).

Results

Participant disposition and baseline characteristics

A total of 1,909 women were screened, and 1,901 were included in the study. Of these, 45.5% of women completed the 3-year study period, while 54.5% discontinued the study before reaching the 3-year duration of use (Fig. 1). The mean (SD) age was 32.1 (5.6) years, and the mean (SD) body mass index was 21.7 (3.0) kg/m2; 7.9% of lactating women were exposed to the implant; 15.8% reported using at least one concomitant medication while on the implant. The most frequently reported (> 1%) concomitant medications were mifepristone (3.0%), drospirenone and ethinylestradiol (1.8%) for bleeding, tranexamic acid (1.3%), and callicarpa nudiflora leaf (1.1%). The mean (SD) implant exposure duration was 783.7 (388.7) days (Table S1).

Primary outcome

Overall safety

Of 1,901 women, 47.9% reported drug-related AEs (excluding topical AEs), 10.4% reported at least one topical AE, and 0.4% reported at least one drug-related SAE. No deaths were reported (Table 1). Most drug-related AEs (excluding topical AEs) were mild (81.7%) or moderate (17.0%). The most frequently reported (≥ 5% of women) drug-related AEs (excluding topical AEs) were vaginal bleeding (15.6%), heavy menstrual bleeding (8.0%), amenorrhea (6.7%), menstrual disorder (6.3%), and weight gain (5.9%; Tables S2 and S3). SAEs were reported by 1.1% of women, including 0.4% with drug-related SAEs. The most frequently reported (> 0.1% of women) SAEs were abnormal uterine bleeding (0.3%) and uterine leiomyoma (0.2%; Table S4). Among women who reported > 1 AE (47.9%), 39.1% had mild and 8.2% had moderate severity. Drug-related AE leading to implant removal accounted for 34.0% and the most frequently reported was vaginal bleeding (10.5%). Additionally, cumulative discontinuation rates with the implant due to bleeding irregularities were 14.5% at the end of the first year, 25.5% after the second year, and 30.0% after the third year (data not shown). The most common drug-related AE and SAE involving bleeding irregularities was vaginal bleeding (15.6%) and abnormal uterine bleeding (0.3%), respectively (Table 1). Bleeding irregularities were reported by 41.6%, including 0.3% with SAEs (data not shown).

Topical AE

Topical AEs were reported by 10.4% of women. The most frequently reported (> 2% of women) topical AEs by preferred term were implant site bruising (4.6%), pruritus (4.2%), and pain (2.5%; Table S5). No topical SAEs were reported.

Secondary outcomes

Participant satisfaction

Of 1,890 women who completed the initial satisfaction questionnaire at Day 10, 64.3% and 34.3% reported no pain or mild pain during the insertion procedure, respectively. Only 0.2% reported severe pain. Most (91.9%) women reported that the insertion procedure was less painful than or as painful as expected (Fig. 2A, Table S6).

A total of 1,755 women completed the removal satisfaction questionnaires, with 50.8% having implant removal before Month 36 and 49.2% having removal at Month 36. About 99.1% finished the pain assessment questions. Most women (98.1%) reported no pain (63.7%) or mild pain (34.4%) during the removal procedure, and the majority (73.3%) of them reported that the removal procedure was less painful than expected and 19.1% reported that the removal procedure was as expected (Fig. 2A). About 47.4% were willing to continue to use the implant as their method of contraception (Table S6).

A total of 1,704 women completed the general satisfaction questionnaire 6 months after insertion, and 1,740 women completed it 36 months after insertion or upon removal. At 6 months after insertion, 22.3% and 53.3% of women were very satisfied and satisfied, respectively. At 36 months after insertion or upon removal, 23.7% and 38.9% of women were very satisfied and satisfied, respectively (Fig. 2B, Table S6).

Physician satisfaction

All the physicians (n = 96) completed the questionnaire following their first insertion and 82 physicians completed the fifth insertion satisfaction questionnaire: 90.2% of physicians completed the questionnaire after their fifth insertion procedure, and 9.8% of physicians completed it with < 5 insertion procedures. Most physicians (95.8%) were satisfied following their first insertion procedure, of whom 57.3% were very satisfied and 38.5% were satisfied. The majority (92.7%) of physicians did not report any issues during their first insertion (Fig. 3). The most common difficulty was removing the protection cap from the applicator (4.2%; Table S7).

All the physicians (n = 95) completed the questionnaires following their first implant removal procedures. Overall, 98.9% of physicians reported satisfaction following their first removal procedure, of whom 65.3% were very satisfied, 33.7% were satisfied, and only one (1.1%) physician reported being neither satisfied nor dissatisfied (Fig. 3). Satisfaction after the 5th implant removal was evaluated in 86.9% of physicians who completed ≥ 5 removal procedures and 13.1% of physicians who completed < 5. All physicians (100.0%) were satisfied. No issues were reported with their 5th removal procedure (Fig. 3, Table S7).

Contraceptive effectiveness

During the 3-year observational period, of the 1,901 women with implant insertion, one (0.05%) pregnancy was conceived during the treatment period (Table S8). The Pearl Index for in-treatment pregnancy was 0.0265 per 100 woman-years (95% CI 0.00, 0.15). The conception date was estimated to be 4 months after implant insertion. The pregnancy was terminated, and the implant was removed on the same day.

Discussion

This study evaluated the safety, participant and physician satisfaction, and contraceptive effectiveness of the radiopaque etonogestrel implant at the end of the 3-year recommended usage period in Chinese women seeking contraception in routine clinical practice. Safety and effectiveness during the study period were as expected, with no new safety findings, and the majority of women and physicians were satisfied with the contraceptive implant and its insertion and removal. Most women were satisfied with the etonogestrel implant in terms of ease of insertion and removal procedures and were willing to recommend it to others. Similarly, physician satisfaction with insertion and removal procedures was high, and few reported complications. The implant was effective, with one pregnancy reported while the implant was in place (conception date estimated to be 4 months after implant insertion). The pregnancy was terminated and the implant was removed on the same day. The results were similar to another study, which reported six pregnancies after etonogestrel implant removal during the first 14 days [14].

The etonogestrel implant is a well tolerated and safe method of long-acting hormonal contraception for women [15]. However, there are common drug-related AEs associated with its use, as is the case among users of progestin-only and combined estrogen-progestin contraceptives, and symptom acceptability varies widely [14]. Modesto et al. showed that in etonogestrel implant users, the discontinuation rate due to menstrual bleeding irregularities was 17% at 1 year and 62% at 2 years [16]. Therefore, it becomes important to counsel the users prior to choosing this method and help them understand the impact of the implant on the possible changes in bleeding patterns and anticipated risks.

In this study, the cumulative discontinuation rates owing to bleeding irregularities were 14.5% after 1 year, 25.5% after 2 years, and 30.0% after 3 years. The removal rate was the highest in the first year and subsequently decreased in later years, which aligned with the bleeding pattern transition. The next-generation applicator is designed to facilitate correct subdermal insertions, and it is easy to use with a one-handed action [17]. Detailed training on insertion and removal operations was provided before the study to ensure strict adherence. Physicians reported few issues in the operation procedure. No topical SAEs were reported. Among 6% of the physicians, the only consistent issue encountered was “Difficulty removing protection cap”.

Overall, the current study demonstrated the contraceptive effectiveness of the radiopaque etonogestrel implant for up to 3 years in real-world settings, which was consistent with the global data. With > 99% efficacy in preventing pregnancy, the results are aligned with previous reports of high contraceptive efficacy of the implant [14, 18]. During 9 years of marketing experience involving 923 women, no in-treatment or pretreatment pregnancies were reported for a duration of 2 years, while 50 post-treatment pregnancies were observed (6 within 14 days of implant removal), confirming that the implant achieves reversible contraceptive protection exceeding 99% [18].

The current study mirrored routine clinical practice without influencing the physicians’ treatment decisions, thereby providing real-world evidence supporting the safety, effectiveness, and satisfaction of both participants and physicians with the etonogestrel implant insertion and removal procedures. This study has limitations inherent to a single‑arm observational design intended to capture real‑world experience with the implant. As there was no comparator group, causal inference cannot be made. AEs were first documented by investigators as part of routine clinical practice and later MedDRA‑coded, which may introduce variability in reporting across centers. In addition, no missing-data imputation or censoring was performed, which is appropriate given the descriptive nature of the analytic approach. Since the study was conducted during the COVID-19 pandemic, there is a possibility of missing or incomplete data, inadequate reporting by the participants, and potential physician/selection bias. Nonetheless, the large sample size and > 3 years of follow-up support the study findings. This study addressed an important gap as prior research has not evaluated the safety and effectiveness of the etonogestrel implant in such a large cohort in China, especially in real‑world clinical settings. As a populous country, China has a great demand for contraception, and this study provides additional evidence of the favorable benefit-risk profile of this long-acting contraceptive implant.

Conclusion

This post-authorization safety study in Chinese women treated in routine clinical practice provides real-world evidence consistent with the established overall safety and effectiveness of the etonogestrel contraceptive implant for 3 years, as described in the product label and core company datasheet. There were no new safety signals, and the type and incidence of topical and general AEs observed were consistent with those reported during the clinical development of the contraceptive implant. Most women were very satisfied or satisfied and willing to recommend the implant to others. Similarly, most physicians were satisfied with implant insertion and removal procedures, with few complications reported.
