Induction therapy of pralsetinib followed by conversion surgery for unresectable locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer with the rearranged during transfection (RET) fusion gene mutation: protocol for a single-arm phase II prospective trial (the SALVAGE-P trial)
Authors: Han-Yu Deng, Xiao-Ming Qiu, Lu Li, Xiao-Jun Tang, Qinghua Zhou
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
BackgroundSelective rearranged during transfection (RET) inhibitors have been approved as first-line treatment for locally advanced non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC) harboring RET fusion gene mutations. Growing evidence shows that loco-regional therapy including salvage surgery may improve the prognosis of locally advanced NSCLC after induction therapy. Therefore, we initiated the SALVAGE-P trial to assess the safety and feasibility of induction selective RET inhibitors followed by conversion surgery in treating initially unresectable locally advanced NSCLC.MethodsThis is a single-arm, phase II prospective study. Patients with treatment-naïve, unresectable locally advanced NSCLC (clinical stage IIIA, IIIB, and IIIC) harboring RET fusion gene mutations will be eligible for our study (N=25). Patients will receive induction therapy of pralsetinib (400 mg QD) for up to 6 months. If the primary tumor becomes resectable, participants will undergo surgical resection (lobectomy with systematic lymph node dissection). After surgery, participants will receive 4 cycles of pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) plus carboplatin [area under the curve (AUC) =5] every three weeks as adjuvant therapy. The primary endpoints are objective response rate (ORR) and R0 resection rate. Secondary endpoints include major pathological response (MPR) and pathological complete response rates, event-free survival (EFS), overall survival (OS) and treatment related adverse effects.DiscussionThe SALVAGE-P trial aims to assess the safety and feasibility of induction selective RET inhibitors followed by conversion surgery, which will begin in January 2026, with primary completion anticipated in December 2028.Trial RegistrationThis trial has been registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry with a registration number of ChiCTR2600116415.

Introduction

Lung cancer remains the most common type of malignant tumor, accounting for 12.4% of all cancers, and has the highest cancer-related death rate (approximately 1.8 million deaths) worldwide (1). Lung cancers are generally divided into small cell lung cancers and non-small cell lung cancer (NSCLC), with the latter accounting for about 80% of cases (2). Lung cancers are often diagnosed at an advanced stage owing to difficulties in early diagnosis and treatment, and patients with NSCLC have a poor long-term prognosis, with a 5-year survival rate of 26.4% (3). Despite rapid advances in new drugs for treating lung cancer, the prognosis of locally advanced or advanced NSCLC remains poor, indicating that novel therapeutic strategies are urgently needed.

Recently, targeted therapies such as epidermal growth factor receptor (EGFR) and anaplastic lymphoma kinase (ALK) tyrosine kinase inhibitors (TKIs) have been widely confirmed to be promising therapies for locally advanced or advanced NSCLC harboring common oncogenic driver gene mutations (4). As a less common gene mutation in NSCLC, the rearranged during transfection (RET) gene fusion accounts for approximately 1–2% of all gene mutations (5). RET also acts as an oncogene in NSCLC when aberrantly activated and can serve as a targetable gene for novel drugs. NSCLCs with RET gene mutations respond poorly to chemotherapy and immunotherapy; they may not benefit from immune checkpoint inhibitors (6) or multikinase nonselective RET inhibitors (7). Recently, two selective RET inhibitors, selpercatinib (8) and pralsetinib (9), have been approved for advanced NSCLC with RET fusion mutations because of their high efficacy. However, despite their effectiveness in advanced NSCLC with high overall response rate, the reported progression-free survival (PFS) of NSCLC patients treated with selpercatinib and pralsetinib in the first-line setting was only 22 months (10) and 13 months (11) respectively, and drug resistance inevitably develops during treatment. Therefore, novel treatment modalities are still required to improve the prognosis of patients with RET fusion-positive NSCLC. Previously, we explored the therapeutic strategy of salvage surgery for locally advanced or advanced NSCLC after induction therapy of targeted EGFR TKI (12) and mesenchymal-epithelial transition (MET) TKI (13), and demonstrated that salvage surgery after induction targeted therapy is a feasible and effective treatment modality for advanced NSCLC patients with oncogene-driven gene mutations. Moreover, a previous study reported a case of an advanced NSCLC patient treated with induction therapy of pralsetinib followed by salvage surgery who achieved complete pathological response (pCR) (14), suggesting that induction therapy of RET inhibitors followed by salvage surgery is feasible for advanced NSCLC. In our previous unpublished retrospective study, we explored the feasibility and safety of salvage surgery after induction therapy of selective RET inhibitors, and found that salvage surgery after induction therapy of selective RET inhibitors yielded significantly higher 3-year overall survival (OS) and PFS rates, and appeared to be a feasible and effective treatment strategy for improving the prognosis of locally advanced or advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. Therefore, we initiated this single-arm, phase II prospective study (the SALVAGE-P trial) to evaluate the efficacy and safety of induction therapy of pralsetinib followed by conversion surgery in patients with unresectable locally advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. We present this article in accordance with the SPIRIT reporting checklist (available at https://jtd.amegroups.com/article/view/10.21037/jtd-2026-0703/rc).

Methods

Study design

The SALVAGE-P trial is an open-label, single-arm, phase II prospective study. A total of 25 patients diagnosed with clinically unresectable locally advanced NSCLC (clinical stage IIIA/IIIB/IIIC) will be recruited for the trial. The objective of the trial is to evaluate both efficacy and safety of induction therapy of pralsetinib followed by conversion surgery in patients with unresectable locally advanced RET fusion-positive NSCLC. Eligibility criteria

Eligible patients, who must be previously untreated, must be ≥18 years old, with unresectable locally advanced NSCLC (clinical stage IIIA, IIIB and IIIC disease, cT3-4N1-2, or cT1-4N2-3) according to the 9th edition of TNM stage for lung cancer (15). Next generation sequencing reports must reveal RET fusion gene mutations without other common oncogenic driven gene mutations (such as EGFR, ALK, ROS1, BRAF, etc.). Patients must have good cardiopulmonary function and provide informed consent. The key inclusion and exclusion criteria are listed in Table 1.

Treatment and follow-up

Study participants will receive induction therapy of pralsetinib (400 mg QD) for up to 6 months and will be evaluated every 6 weeks in the multidisciplinary team (MDT, consisting of thoracic surgeons, medical oncologists, and radiation oncologists) discussion for surgical resectability. The resectability is evaluated with pre- and post-treatment imaging criteria with cardiopulmonary functions, and is defined as complete response, partial response, or stable disease using the Response Evaluation Criteria in Solid Tumors (RECIST 1.1) guidelines (16). If the primary tumor is resectable, participants will undergo surgical resection (lobectomy with systematic lymph node dissection under video-assisted thoracoscopic surgery (VATS) or open thoracotomy depending on MDT discussion, with VATS preferred). If the primary tumor still remains unresectable, continued pralsetinib for stable disease or switching into chemotherapy with radiotherapy for progression disease will be offered for the participants. After surgery, the participants will receive 4 cycles of chemotherapy {pemetrexed (500 mg/m2) plus carboplatin [area under the curve (AUC) =5]} every three weeks as adjuvant therapy.

During the induction therapy period, we will perform follow-up for each participant every 2 months with contrast-enhanced cervical, chest and abdominal CT and lung cancer tumor biomarkers. For participants undergoing surgery, the first postoperative follow-up will be at 30 days postoperatively for safety analysis. The total follow-up duration will be 5 years for each participant. Regular follow-up will be performed every 3 months for the first 2 years with chest CT and lung cancer tumor biomarkers; investigators will determine whether to perform contrast-enhanced or non-contrast-enhanced CT. Non-contrast-enhanced CT and lung cancer tumor biomarkers will be performed every 6 months during the following 3–5 years.

Patient recruitment and allocation

After eligibility assessment, a signed informed consent form will be obtained from each participant, and each participant will be registered. Registration will be captured online with the electronic case report form (eCRF) for assigning patients with a patient number, which will be used for pseudonymised identification throughout the study. Patient recruitment will begin in January 2026 and the study is expected to continue for the next three years.

Study endpoints

The primary endpoints of the study are objective response rate (ORR) and R0 resection rate. ORR will be evaluated according to the RECIST 1.1 guidelines (16). The secondary key endpoints are major pathological response (MPR) and pCR rate in participants undergoing radical surgery, based on multidisciplinary recommendations for pathologic assessment of lung cancer resection specimens after neoadjuvant therapy by IASLC (17), event-free survival (EFS, time from induction therapy to progression or death), OS (time from induction therapy to death) and treatment-related adverse effects (TRAEs, graded by were graded by investigators, according to the National Cancer Institute Common Terminology Criteria for Adverse Events version 5.0) in all participants. The safety profile also includes major postoperative complication and 30-day mortality rates.

Statistical analysis

The primary endpoints are ORR and R0 resection rates. We calculated the sample size based on ORR in the trial. Previously, the reported ORR for pralsetinib in advanced NSCLC was 72% (11). Therefore, in the SALVAGE-P trial, the anticipated ORR for locally advanced NSCLC is approximately 75%. A Simon’s 2-stage design will be used. In the first stage, 11 patients will be accrued. The study will be stopped if no more than 6 patients with a response are observed. Otherwise, 14 additional patients will be accrued for a total of 25, considering potential drop-out rate. The sample size was calculated with α=0.05 (one-tailed) and the power of test (1-β) =80%.

We will perform the statistical analysis using SPSS 22.0 software (SPSS Corp., Chicago, IL, USA). Data will be presented as the mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables or number (%) for categorical data. Kaplan-Meier analysis with survival curves will be used to estimate EFS and OS.

Patients or public involvement

Patients and the public would not be involved in the research’s design, conduct, reporting or dissemination plans. All participants will sign the informed consent and be involved in the follow-up as we previously described (18).

Ethics and dissemination

This SALVAGE-P trial was approved by the ethics committee of West China Hospital, Sichuan University on December 30, 2025 (No. 2025-2316), and has been registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Registry, with a registration number of ChiCTR2600116415 (https://www.chictr.org.cn/showproj.html?proj=304054). The SALVAGE-P trial will be conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki and its subsequent amendments. All participants will sign the informed consent form. The ethics committee of our hospital will regularly review all research data every 12 months, including research data, medical records and electronic CRFs. If important protocol modifications are required, we will report to the ethics committee. The investigators will guarantee the participants’ right to withdraw from this trial in an emergency to ensure their safety without medical care being impacted. We will carefully store all these data and make them only available for the researchers and monitoring panels. The final results of the SALVAGE-P trial will be published in peer-reviewed journals or presented at academic conferences.

Discussion

Despite the excellent anti-tumor effects of selective RET inhibitors in treating NSCLC with RET fusion gene mutations, drug resistance inevitably occurs, posing a great challenge to prolonging survival in these patients. Local-regional consolidative therapy including salvage surgery may be indicated for patients who do not progress after induction therapy, potentially prolonging both PFS and OS (19). For locally advanced or advanced NSCLC, salvage surgery following the induction of targeted therapy for those harboring targetable gene mutations or immune checkpoint inhibitor, has been increasingly reported and proven feasible for patients who respond well to induction therapy (20). However, whether patients locally advanced NSCLC harboring RET fusion gene mutations could benefit from conversion surgery remains unclear. Therefore, the SALVAGE-P trial is designed to assess the safety and feasibility of induction selective RET inhibitors followed by conversion surgery, which may contribute to the growing body of evidence on the application of conversion surgery in locally advanced NSCLC after induction therapy. Patient enrolment is expected to start in January 2026, with primary completion anticipated in December 2028.
