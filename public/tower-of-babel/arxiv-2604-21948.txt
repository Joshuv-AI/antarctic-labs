Numerical Semigroups with a e = 2 ​ g + 1 a_{e}=2g+1
Michael Hellus, Reinhold Hübl, Anton Rechenauer

Abstract
. This article discusses numerical semigroups having a generator which is as large as possible. This turns out to be 2 ​ g + 1 2g+1 , where g g is the genus of the semigroup. We will show that these semigroups are closely related to symmetric semigroups and have interesting symmetry properties themselves. Furthermore we will show that Wilf’s question has a positive answer for these semigroups and some semigroups derived thereof.

1.
Introduction

Numerical semigroups have been studied intensely since their first
appearance in
[
10
]
, nevertheless many questions about them are still
wide open. One particularly intriguing problem is a question of Wilf concerning
the ratio of gaps and sporadic elements of such a semigroup (cf.
[
11
]
):

For each such numerical semigroup
S
let
F(S)
be its Frobenius element,
i.e. the largest integer not in
S
, let
g(S)
be its genus, i.e. the number of
positive integers not in
S
(called the gaps of
S
) and let
e(S)
be the
number of minimal generators of
S
(the embedding dimension of
S
).

Wilf’s Question
.

For each numerical semigroup
S\neq\mathbb{N}
, is it true that

\frac{g(S)}{F(S)+1}\leq\frac{e(S)-1}{e(S)}\,\,?

If
\sigma(S)
denotes the number of integers
n<F(S)
that
are in
S
(called the sporadic elements of
S
), then the question
may be reformulated as

Wilf’s Question
.

For each numerical semigroup
S\neq\mathbb{N}
, is it true that

F(S)+1\leq e(S)\cdot\sigma(S)\,\,?

As
F(S)+1=\sigma(S)+g(S)
, this provides a relation between
the number of gaps and the number of sporadic elements of a
numerical semigroup. Though this question has a positive answer
in a number of cases, it is still wide open in general
(see
[
2
]
for a survey).

In this note we study semigroups having a generator “as large as
possible” which turns out to be
2\cdot g(S)+1
. We show that
these classes of semigroups have interesting symmetry properties and
are closely related to symmetric semigroups, though with the exception
of the multiplicity
2
case they are not symmetric themselves. We also
show that for these semigroups and some semigroups derived from them
the answer to Wilf’s question is positive.

Numerical semigroups in general are closely related to curve singularities
as they naturally appear as value semigroups of unibranch curve
singularities. The semigroups studied here bear a close relationship
to the classification of curve singularities by parametrization as
curve singularities whose value semigroups satisfy
a_{e}=2g+1
show
that the bounds on the
\mathcal{L}
–determinacy, obtained
in
[
6
]
, are sharp in general.

Parts of the research for this paper were done while the second author
was visiting Purdue University. He expresses his gratitude to this
institution for its support and hospitality.

2.
Maximal possible generator

Throughout this paper
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle

will be a numerical semigroup with
a_{1}<a_{2}<\cdots<a_{e}
,
embedding dimension
e
(so that
a_{1}
,
\ldots
,
a_{e}
are
the minimal generators of
S
) and multiplicity
m=a_{1}
. By

g=g(S)
we denote its genus and by
f=F(S)
its Frobenius
element.

Lemma 2.1
.

In the above situation

a_{e}\leq 2g+1

Proof.

It suffices to show that any
\lambda\in\mathbb{N}
with
\lambda\geq 2\cdot g+2
is
contained in
S_{+}+S_{+}
.

Let
t=\lfloor\frac{\lambda}{2}\rfloor
. Then
t\geq g+1
, and we have

t
pairwise distinct presentations

\begin{array}[]{l c l c l}\lambda&=&1&+&(\lambda-1)\\
\lambda&=&2&+&(\lambda-2)\\
&\vdots&&&\\
\lambda&=&t&+&(\lambda-t)\\
\end{array}

As
t>g
for at least one equation

\lambda=i+(\lambda-i)

neither
i
nor
\lambda-i
can be gaps of
S
, i.e. both
i
and
\lambda-i
are in

S_{+}
, proving the lemma.
∎

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a numerical
semigroup of genus
g
. Then the following are equivalent:

(1)

a_{e}=2g+1
.

(2)

The assignment
\varphi
given by
\varphi(n)=2g+1-n

induces a well defined bijection
\varphi:\{1,\ldots,2g\}\cap S\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,2g\}\setminus S
.

Proof.

First assume
a_{e}=2g+1
. We get
g
equations

\begin{array}[]{l c l c l}a_{e}&=&1&+&(a_{e}-1)\\
a_{e}&=&2&+&(a_{e}-2)\\
&\vdots&&&\\
a_{e}&=&g&+&(a_{e}-g)\\
\end{array}

As
a_{e}\notin S_{+}+S_{+}
in each equation at least one
summand must be not in
S
.
As there are only
g
gaps, the existence and bijectivity
of
\varphi
follows.

Assume now that a
bijection
\varphi:\{1,\ldots,2g\}\cap S\longrightarrow\{1,\ldots,2g\}\setminus S

is given by the assignment
\varphi
. Then
for each
1\leq n\leq g
in the representation

2g+1=n+(2g+1-n)
exactly
one summand is in
S
and one is not. Thus
2g+1\notin S_{+}+S_{+}
. On the other hand, all
g
gaps of
S
are
contained in
\{1,\ldots,2g\}
by the bijectivity of

\varphi
, hence
2g+1\in S
. Thus it is a minimal
generator of
S
, and by Lemma
2.1

necessarily the last one.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup of genus
g
. Then

a_{e}=2g+1
if and only if
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{a_{e}\}
is a symmetric semigroup.

Moreover in case
a_{e}=2g+1
we have:
If
e=2
, then
a_{1}=2
and
S^{\prime}=\langle 2,a_{e}+2\rangle
and if

e>2
, then
S^{\prime}=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e-1}\rangle
. In both cases we have

m(S^{\prime})=m(S)
.

Proof.

Assume
a_{e}=2g+1
.
As
a_{e}
is a minimal generator of
S
, the set
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{a_{e}\}
certainly
is a numerical semigroup with
g(S^{\prime})=g+1
and
F(S^{\prime})=2g(S^{\prime})-1=2g+1
. Hence

g(S^{\prime})=\frac{F(S^{\prime})+1}{2}
, implying that
S^{\prime}
is symmetric.

Conversely suppose the
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{a_{e}\}
is symmetric. Then

F(S^{\prime})=2g(S^{\prime})-1=2g+1
.
If
a_{e}\neq F(S^{\prime})
, then
2g+1=F(S^{\prime})=F(S)\leq 2g
, a contradiction.

If
e=2
,
S=\langle m,l\rangle
with
l\geq m+1
, then
2g+1=(m-1)\cdot(l-1)+1
by the formula for the Frobenius element in
numerical semigroups generated by two elements, hence
2g+1>l

except in case
m=2
in which case
2g+1=l

and
S^{\prime}=\langle a_{1},l+2\rangle=\langle 2,a_{e}+2\rangle
,
and in case
e>2
the additional statement is a
consequence of the following lemma.
∎

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
S^{\prime}
be a symmetric numerical semigroup with multiplicity
m\geq 3
. Then

S^{\prime}
is generated by its elements less than
F(S^{\prime})
.

Proof.

As
S^{\prime}
is symmetric,
F(S^{\prime})+1=2g(S^{\prime})
. Thus by Lemma
2.1

any element
s>F(S^{\prime})+2
cannot be part of a minimal generating set of
S^{\prime}
. But by
symmetry (and as
m>2
)
F(S^{\prime})+1-m=F(S^{\prime})-(m-1)\in S^{\prime}
and
F(S^{\prime})+2-m=F(S^{\prime})-(m-2)\in S^{\prime}
, hence all generators are smaller than
F(S^{\prime})
.
∎

Let

F(S)=a_{e}-m=2g+1-m

Proposition 2.7
.

Let
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup with multiplicity

m=a_{1}
, genus
g=g(S)
and with Frobenius element
f=F(S)
. Then the
following are equivalent:

i)

a_{e}=2g+1
.

ii)

m+\operatorname{RG}(f,S)=\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
.

iii)

a_{e}=f+m
and
|\operatorname{RG}(f,S)|=m-2
.

Proof.

First assume that
a_{e}=f+m
. Then the following holds

(1)

The map
\iota:\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)\longrightarrow\operatorname{RG}(f,S)
given by
\iota(L)=L-m
is well–defined (and injective).

In fact, if
L\in\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)
, then
f+m-L\notin S
, hence

f+m-L\leq f
, implying
L\geq m
. As
L
is a gap, necessarily
L>m
and
therefore
L-m\in\{1,\ldots,f-1\}
. Certainly
L-m
itself is a gap as
L
is, and so is

f-(L-m)=f+m-L
. Hence
L-m\in\operatorname{RG}(f,S)
.

(2)

L\in\operatorname{RG}(f,S)\setminus\operatorname{im}(\iota)
if and only
if
L+m\in\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
.

If
L\in\operatorname{RG}(f,S)
but
L+m\notin\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)
,
then
L
is a gap and
f-L=(f+m)-(L+m)
is a gap of
S
, hence necessarily

L+m
is not a gap of
S
, thus
L+m\in\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
.

Conversely if
L+m\in\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
, then

L+m\in S
but
L\notin S
. Furthermore
x=f-L=(f+m)-(L+m)\notin S
, as
otherwise
f+m=x+(L+m)\in S_{+}
contradicting the fact that
a_{e}=f+m
is a minimal
generator. Thus
L\in\operatorname{RG}(f,S)\setminus\operatorname{im}(\iota)
.

(3)

a_{e}=2g+1
if and only if
\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)=\emptyset

This is immediate by Proposition
2.2
as
a_{e}=f+m
.

With this we are able to complete the proof of the proposition:

Assume i). If
a_{e}=2g+1
, then
a_{e}=f+m
by
Corollary
2.6
, hence

\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)=\emptyset
by (3), and therefore

m+\operatorname{RG}(f,S)=\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}

by (2), thus ii) holds.

Now assume ii), i.e. assume that
m+\operatorname{RG}(f,S)=\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
.
As
\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}
has
m-2

elements, it remains to show that
a_{e}=f+m
.

Assume otherwise. Then
a_{e}<f+m
(as any
s>f+m
is in
S_{+}+S_{+}
) and

f+m=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{e}n_{i}\cdot a_{i}

with
n_{i}\geq 0
and
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{e}n_{i}\geq 2
. As
f\notin S
,
necessarily
n_{1}=0
, i.e.
f+m=\sum\limits_{i=2}^{e}n_{i}\cdot a_{i}
, and as

a_{e}<f+m
,

a_{2},\ldots,a_{e}\in\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}

Let
i_{0}=\operatorname{min}\{i\,|\,\,n_{i}>0\}
and write

f+m=a_{i_{0}}+s

with

s=\left(n_{i_{0}}-1\right)\cdot a_{i_{0}}+\sum\limits_{i=i_{0}+1}^{e}n_{i}\cdot a_{i}\in S_{+}

As
a_{i_{0}}-m\in\operatorname{RG}(f,S)

is a reflected gap of
S
with respect to
f
,

s=f+m-a_{i_{0}}=f-\left(a_{i_{0}}-m\right)\quad\notin S

a contradiction. Hence
a_{e}=f+m
and
|\operatorname{RG}(f,S)|=m-2

and iii) is true.

If iii) holds, i.e. if
a_{e}=f+m
and
|\operatorname{RG}(f,S)|=m-2
,
then by (2) we have that

m+\operatorname{RG}(f,S)\setminus\operatorname{im}(\iota)=\operatorname{Ap}(S)\setminus\{0,f+m\}

has
m-2
elements. Hence
\operatorname{im}(\iota)=\emptyset
, thus

\operatorname{RG}(f+m,S)=\emptyset
, and therefore
a_{e}=2g+1
by (3),
hence we get i).

4.
Estimating the number of gaps and Wilf’s inequality

For numerical semigroups
S
with
a_{e}=2g+1
the answer to
Wilf’s question is positive.

Theorem 4.1

(Wilf’s inequality)
.

Let
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup with

a_{e}=2g+1
, genus
g(S)
and Frobenius element
F(S)
. Then

(1)

\frac{g(S)}{F(S)+1}\leq\frac{e-1}{e}

Proof.

As
S
has type
t(S)=e-1
, we get from
[
3
, Theorem 20]
that

e\cdot(F(S)+1-g(S))\geq F(S)+1

hence the claim.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

In the situation of theorem
4.1
, we have
by corollary
2.6
,

F(S)=2\cdot g(S)+1-m(S)

hence (
1
) becomes

(2)

\frac{g(S)}{2\cdot g(S)+2-m(S)}\leq\frac{e-1}{e}

which is equivalent to

(3)

e\cdot g(S)\leq 2\cdot e\cdot g(S)+2\cdot e-e\cdot m(S)-2\cdot g(S)-2+m(S)

hence to

(4)

0\leq(e-2)\cdot g(S)-(e-1)\cdot(m(S)-2)

i.e. to

(5)

(m(S)-2)\cdot(e-1)\leq(e-2)\cdot g(S)

resp., if
e>2
, to

(6)

g(S)\geq(m(S)-2)\cdot\frac{e-1}{e-2}

Now let
S^{\prime}=S\setminus\{a_{e}\}
be the symmetric semigroup associated with
S
.
Then, excluding the case

, by Proposition

we have
m(S^{\prime})=m(S)
and
e(S^{\prime})=e-1
,
and obviously

. Thus equation (

(7)

g(S^{\prime})\geq 1+(m(S^{\prime})-2)\cdot\frac{e(S^{\prime})}{e(S^{\prime})-1}

for any symmetric semigroup

(the case

being obvious).

Estimate (
7
) is actually valid for any numerical semigroup

satisfying the inequality
F(T)>m(T)\geq 2
.

In fact for any such
T
, all integers

with

are either gaps
or minimal generators, thus

We first treat the case

. Then

\displaystyle(2\cdot m(T)-e(T)-2)\cdot(e(T)-1)

\displaystyle-(m(T)-2)\cdot e(T)

\displaystyle=(e(T)-2)\cdot(m(T)-e(T)-1)

\displaystyle\geq 0

and we conclude

(2\cdot m(T)-e(T)-2)\cdot(e(T)-1)\geq(m(T)-2)\cdot e(T)

(8)

g(T)\geq 2\cdot m(T)-e(T)-1\geq 1+(m(T)-2)\cdot\frac{e(T)}{e(T)-1}

In case

we have

\begin{array}[]{l c l}m(T)\cdot(e(T)-1)&=&m(T)\cdot(m(T)-1)\\
&>&m(T)^{2}-m(T)-1\\
&=&(m(T)-1)+(m(T)-2)\cdot m(T)\\
&=&(e(T)-1)+(m(T)-2)\cdot e(T)\\
\end{array}

and thus, as

g(T)\geq m(T)>1+(m(T)-2)\cdot\frac{e(T)}{e(T)-1}

(even with strict inequality). Note however that in the remaining case

,
i.e.

, inequality (

not hold for

It is not clear to us whether estimate (
8
) has any relation to
Wilf’s question for nonsymmetric
T
.

5.
Closing the largest gap

A well established technique to study numerical semigroups is deriving them from
well understood semigroups by closing suitable gaps. In section
2
we
have seen that all numerical semigroups
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
with

a_{e}=2g+1
arise from a symmetric semigroup by closing its largest gap which
provided the basis of most of the results obtained so far. In this section we will
pick up this theme by closing the largest gap of
S
(i.e. the two largest gaps of
a symmetric semigroup). Before doing so, we recall some facts and arguments,
communicated to us by the late Rolf Waldi, that seem to be well known to experts;
we include them here for lack of a suitable reference.

Let
S
be an arbitrary numerical semigroup. A set
D
of gaps of
S
is called
distinguished if for any gap
a
of
S

s\in S
with

. We note that any distinguished set
D
of

necessarily contains
the set
\operatorname{PF}(S)
of all the Pseudo–Frobenius elements of
S
, and
that
\operatorname{PF}(S)

Lemma 5.1
.

If
D
is a distinguished set of gaps of
S
, containing
d
elements, then

\frac{g(S)}{F(S)+1}\leq\frac{d}{d+1}

Proof.

This is immediate by
[
3
, Theorem 20]
, as
d\geq t
, the type of
S
.

An easy direct argument goes as follows: Set
D=\{g_{1},\ldots,g_{d}\}
and let

\{s_{1},\ldots,s_{n}\}
be the set of sporadic elements of
S
, i.e. the elements

s\in S
with
s<F(S)
. Then by the very
definition of distinguished, the gaps of
S
are contained in the set

M=\bigcup_{i=1}^{d}\{g_{i}-s_{1},\ldots,g_{i}-s_{n}\}

which contains at most
d\cdot n
elements. Thus

\frac{F(S)+1-g(S)}{g(S)}\geq\frac{n}{n\cdot d}=\frac{1}{d}

which is equivalent to the claim.
∎

From now on for the remainder of this section let

S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a nontrivial numerical semigroup
with

and let

a_{e}-a_{1}=F(S)
is the largest gap of

Lemma 5.2
.

If
a_{e}>2\cdot a_{1}
, then
T
is minimally generated by
a_{1},\ldots,a_{e-1},a_{e}-a_{1}
. In particular
T
has embedding dimension

Proof.

First note that

T\cap\left(\{0,1,\ldots,a_{e}\}\setminus\{a_{e}-a_{1}\}\right)=S\cap\left(\{0,1,\ldots,a_{e}\}\setminus\{a_{e}-a_{1}\}\right)

hence any element
0<a<a_{e}-a_{1}
is a minimal generator of
S
if and only if it
is a minimal generator of
T
.

The element
a_{e}-a_{1}
is a minimal generator of
T
as it is a gap of
S
, whereas

a_{e}\in T_{+}+T_{+}
is not.

Now let
a_{e}-a_{1}<a<a_{e}
. Clearly if
a\in S_{+}+S_{+}
, then
a\in T_{+}+T_{+}
.
Conversely if
a\in T_{+}+T_{+}
,
a=t_{1}+t_{2}
, then
t_{1}\geq a_{1}

as
a_{e}>2\cdot a_{1}
, hence
t_{2}<a_{e}-a_{1}
and vice versa, implying that
t_{1},t_{2}\in S_{+}
, hence
a\in S_{+}+S_{+}
.

Finally we note that by Lemma
2.1
resp. its proof any
a\in T

with
a\geq a_{e}=2g(S)+1=2g(T)+3
cannot be a minimal generator of

,
completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Theorem 5.3

(Wilf’s inequality)
.

Let
S=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be a numerical semigroup
with
a_{e}=2g+1
and let
T=S\cup\{a_{e}-a_{1}\}
. Then
T

satisfies Wilf’s inequality

(9)

\frac{g(T)}{F(T)+1}\leq\frac{e(T)-1}{e(T)}

Proof.

If
e=1
, then
T=S=\mathbb{N}
, and there is nothing to show. Thus we may assume
e\geq 2
.
If
a_{e}<2\cdot a_{1}
, then by Proposition
2.3

S=\langle a_{1},a_{1}+1,\dots,2a_{1}-1\rangle

hence

T=\langle a_{1}-1,a_{1},\dots,2a_{1}-3\rangle

and (
9
) is true as equality. Hence we may assume
a_{e}>2\cdot a_{1}
.
Then
e(T)=e
by Lemma
5.2
, thus by Lemma
5.1
it
suffices to show that

D=\{a_{e}-2a_{1},a_{e}-a_{2},\ldots,a_{e}-a_{e-1}\}

is a distinguished set of gaps of
T
.

By Lemma
5.2
the elements of
D
certainly are gaps of
T
.

If
t
is a gap of
T
, then it is one of
S
as well. Thus
a_{e}-t\in S
by
Proposition
2.2
and
0<a_{e}-t<a_{e}
. Hence

a_{e}-t=\sum\limits_{i=1}^{e-1}n_{i}\cdot a_{i}

for some
n_{i}\geq 0
with
\sum\limits_{i=1}^{e-1}n_{i}>0
. If
n_{i}=0
for all

i>1
, then necessarily
n_{1}\geq 2
(as otherwise

which is not a gap),
hence
t+(n_{1}-2)\cdot a_{1}=a_{e}-2\cdot a_{1}\in D
, and if

, then

, showing that

∎

6.
Value-Semigroups of Curve Singularities

Numerical semigroups are closely related to the local rings
of curves and appear naturally as value semigroups
of unibranch curves singularities. In
[
4
]
parametrizations

\varphi:P=k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]]\longrightarrow k[[t]]

(or more generally
\varphi:P=k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]]\longrightarrow k[[t_{1}]]\times\cdots\times k[[t_{r}]]
) of
completions of local rings of curves are studied.
Two such parametrizations

\varphi
and
\psi
are called
left-equivalent
or

\mathbf{\mathcal{L}}
-equivalent
if there
exists a
\sigma\in\mathrm{Aut}_{k}(P)
such
that
\varphi\circ\sigma^{-1}=\psi
.
A parameterization
\varphi:P=k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]]\longrightarrow k[[t_{1}]]\times\cdots\times k[[t_{r}]]
is called

j
-
\mathcal{L}
-
determined

if any other parameterization
\psi:P=k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{N}]]\longrightarrow k[[t_{1}]]\times\cdots\times k[[t_{r}]]

with
\varphi=\psi\pmod{\overline{\mathfrak{m}}^{j+1}}

is
\mathcal{L}
-equivalent to
\varphi
.

In
[
4
, Corollary 12]
it was shown that any parametrization

\varphi
is
4\cdot\delta(R)-1
-
\mathcal{L}
-determined,
a bound which was improved in
[
6
, Corollary 3.3]
to

2\cdot\delta(R)+1=2g+1
for unibranch curve
singularities, where

\delta(R)=\operatorname{lg}\left(\raisebox{0.2pt}{$\overline{R}$}\mkern-3.0mu\diagup\mkern-3.0mu\raisebox{-0.2pt}{$R$}\right)

denotes the singularity-degree of
R
and where
g=g(S)

is the genus of the value semigroup of
S
. This bound is sharp
as a general bound and value semigroups
S
of singularities
attaining this bound satisfy
a_{e}=2g+1
. Conversely for any
such
S
its numerical semigroup ring is an example of a
singularity attaining this bound. Part of the motivation for
this paper stems from these studies and the question to
understand those singularities for which the bound is sharp.
Understanding these singularities then helps improving the
bound for other classes. The following result
generalizes
[
8
, Theorem 2.1]
, where it is
shown that a plane curve singularity is

(c(R){-}1)
–
\mathcal{L}
–determined, where
c(R)
is
the conductor–degree of
R
, for unibranch singularities.

Theorem 6.1
.

If
(R,\mathfrak{m},k)
is a complete reduced and irreducible
Gorenstein–curve–singularity with multiplicity
m\geq 3
and
conductor degree
c(R)
, then any parametrization

\varphi:k[[X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}]]\longrightarrow\overline{R}
is

(c(R){-}1)
–
\mathcal{L}
–determined (hence also

(c(R){-}1)
–left–right–determined in the sense of
[
4
]
).

Proof.

Let
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n}
be the Herzog–Kunz sequence of
R

(as defined in
[
5
]
) and let
V(R)=\langle a_{1},\ldots,a_{e}\rangle
be the value semigroup of
R
,
which is symmetric as
R
is Gorenstein.
By
[
5
, Corollary 2.5]
we know
b_{n}\leq a_{e}
and by Lemma
2.4
,

a_{e}<F(V(R))<c(R)

As by
[
6
]
2.2 any parametrization
\varphi
of

is

(d_{R}{-}1)
–
\mathcal{L}
–determined where

∎

Remark 6.2
.

That a curve singularity

\mathcal{L}
–determined
