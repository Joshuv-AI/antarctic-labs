A novel prognostic index predicts outcome of diffuse large B-cell lymphoma patients treated with R-CHOP immunochemotherapy
Authors: Zhiya Wu, Yuxin Du, Yue Teng, Ying Sui, Jianqiu Wu
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
The International Prognostic Index (IPI) and IPI related prognostic indexes are widely used for risk-stratification in lymphoma. However, identifying poor prognostic group patients remains a significant objective. This study aimed to develop a predictive prognostic model for DLBCL treated with R-CHOP immunochemotherapy. A cohort comprising 167 individuals newly diagnosed with DLBCL between January 2016 and June 2021 at Jiangsu cancer hospital, Nanjing Medical University were enrolled for investigation. Univariate and multivariate Cox analysis were used for variable selection. The Akaike information criterion (AIC) guided the selection of factors for constructing the nomograms, along with a novel prognostic index for assessing both progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS). Internal validation was performed with the bootstrap method(B = 1000). Age, Lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), stage, extra-nodal sites and absolute CD4 + T cell counts (ACD4C) were associated with both PFS and OS. These discerned prognostic factors were subsequently employed in constructing nomograms for PFS and OS, respectively. The C-indexes of Internal validation performed with the Bootstrap method were 0.76 (PFS: 95%CI 0.67–0.80),0.81(OS:95%CI 0.72–0.85), respectively. The calibration plots, alongside internal bootstrap resampling, demonstrated commendable consistency between predictions and observations. For enhanced clinical applicability, we devised a novel immune prognostic index, categorizing DLBCL patients into four distinct risk groups: low, low-intermediate, high-intermediate, and high risk, corresponding to 0, 1–2, 3, 4–5 risk factors respectively. Kaplan-Meier analysis underscored the superior discriminatory capacity of the immune index in assessing the prognosis across various risk groups. The proposed immune index is a useful tool to predict the prognosis of DLBCL patients treated with R-CHOP immunochemotherapy in this study.

Introduction

Diffuse large B-cell lymphoma (DLBCL) is the most prevalent subtype of aggressive lymphomas, accounting for approximately one-third of all newly diagnosed non-Hodgkin lymphoma (NHL) cases annually [1]. This disease demonstrates marked heterogeneity, reflected in diverse clinical manifestations and variable prognostic outcomes [2].The majority of patients with DLBCL can achieve curative outcomes with the standard first-line regimen R-CHOP (rituximab plus cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vincristine, and prednisone) [3, 4].Unfortunately, around 30%−40% of patients would face the challenge of refractoriness to initial treatment or experience relapse even after achieving complete remission(CR), leading to a poor clinical prognosis [5]. Therefore, optimizing the identification of high-risk patients and developing strategies to overcome treatment resistance remain critical priorities in improving DLBCL management.

Several clinical prognostic indices, such as the International Prognostic Index (IPI), the revised IPI (R-IPI), and the National Comprehensive Cancer Network International Prognostic index (NCCN-IPI), are widely utilized for risk stratification in DLBCL patients. The IPI originated in the pre-rituximab era, incorporates five clinical parameters: age, Eastern Cooperative Oncology Group (ECOG) performance status (PS), stage, extranodal sites and serum lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) [6]. The integration of rituximab into CHOP chemotherapy has significantly enhanced the survival outcomes for DLBCL patients [7]. The R-IPI provides a more clinically relevant prognostic assessment for patients treated with R-CHOP compared with the original IPI [8]. Nonetheless, its prognostic efficacy has been controversial in recent years [9]. The NCCN-IPI refining categorization of two known continuous prognostic variables, age and LDH, enhanced the capacity to discriminate risk groups [10]. In the rituximab era, there was still lack of a clinical risk index that could identify a special patient subgroup with a 5-year OS rate below 50% [11].

The implementation of risk-stratification to identify patients with extremely poor prognostic outcomes remains a challenging endeavor, primarily attributable to the lack of universally sensitive biomarkers. Over the last decade, multiple clinical studies have been made to the exploration of effective biomarkers, aimed at enhancing predictive accuracy [12–14]. Among these endeavors, blood samples are most favored by clinicians as a noninvasive and efficient test. Recent studies have demonstrated that the quantification of circulating immune cells could serve as a reliable predictor of tumor prognosis [15], emerging as a promising alternative marker for immune tumor microenvironment (TME) [16].

Flow cytometry represents a cornerstone in the diagnostic and therapeutic evaluation of patients with NHL, providing objective, quantitative, and reproducible assessment of immune and tumor cell populations that directly supports clinical decision‑making. Beyond immunophenotypic characterization of malignant clones, quantitative evaluation of CD4 + T‑cell count and percentage, CD8 + T‑cell count and percentage, and the CD4+/CD8 + ratio has emerged as a valuable tool in risk stratification, treatment selection, and monitoring of therapeutic response in specific NHL settings. These immune markers reflect host immune competence, tumor microenvironment immune status, and treatment‑related immune perturbations, all of which influence outcomes and toxicity profiles. As highlighted in previous studies, precise flow cytometric analysis enhances diagnostic accuracy and helps guide personalized antineoplastic strategies, underscoring its essential role in the modern management of NHL [17].

We integrated several basic and vital clinical variables alongside baseline circulating subsets of blood lymphocytes to establish a novel immune clinical index, offering a comprehensive assessment of survival probability in patients with newly diagnosed DLBCL treated with R-CHOP regimen.

Methods

Patient selection and data collection

We retrospectively and continuously collected the clinical data of 378 DLBCL patients from January 2016 to June 2021 at Jiangsu cancer hospital, Nanjing Medical University. The inclusion criteria were as follows (Fig. 1): (i)newly diagnosed DLBCL patients by two pathologists according to the 2016 World Health Organization (WHO) classification of lymphoid neoplasms; (ii) accomplished at least two cycles of R-CHOP; (iii)more than 18-year-old. We excluded patients who had incomplete follow-up data and clinical information, combined with other malignant tumors and autoimmune disease. Patiens who diagnosed with primary central nervous system, high-grade B-cell lymphoma, thansformed lymphoma, primary mediastinal DLBCL were also excluded. At last,167 patients were included for model construction and internal validation. We followed up all the patients until December 2022 through review of medical records and telephone interviews. The study was approved by the research Ethics Committee of the Jiangsu cancer hospital (Approval No.: KY-2024-024).Fig. 1Study diagram of patient selection process

Study diagram of patient selection process

Baseline variables collected for each patient included age, gender, Ann Arbor stage, B symptoms, ECOG performance status, lactate dehydrogenase (LDH) level, absolute lymphocyte count (ALC), the number of extranodal sites, bone marrow involvement, peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets, IPI score, R-IPI score, NCCN-IPI score, relapse and survival.

LDH elevated was considered as>245U/L in plasma. The treatment response was evaluated according to the WHO response criteria.

Construction and validation of the model

Risk factors were selected from clinical features that have been demonstrated to be prognostic of DLBCL previously and peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets. Univariate analysis was used to select potential prognostic variables corrected to OS or PFS. Only variables with significance(P<0.05) in the univariate analysis were entered into the multivariate survival analysis. The stepwise regression based on Akaike information criteria (AIC) was used to select the variables contained in the nomogram. Internal validation was performed with the bootstrap method(B = 1000). Time-dependent receiver operating characteristic (ROC) curve (AUC) was used for PFS and OS at different time points. A concordance index (C-index) was calculated by analyzing the area under the AUC. A calibration plot was used to evaluate the consistency between predicted and observed probabilities for survival. Each negative prognostic factor was assigned one point to build a novel immune prognostic index (NIPI).

Comparison with other models

To evaluate the predictive performance of the NIPI, we compared the C-index, decision curve analysis (DCA) and Kaplan-Meier survival with IPI, R-IPI, and NCCN-IPI models. DCA was employed to evaluate the clinical utility and net benefit of each model.

Statistical analysis

PFS was defined as the time from the date of diagnosis to the time of disease progression, relapse, death, or last follow-up. OS was defined as the time from the date of diagnosis to the time of death, or last follow-up. We used multiple interpolation methods to deal with missing data. The determination of the optimal cut-off value of peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets was determined by X-tile (version 3.6.1). The survival analysis was calculated by Kaplan-Meier method and compared by the log-rank test. Bootstraps with 1000 resamples were used to calculate C-index. The nomogram was formulated using the rms and survival ROC packages in R version 4.2.2(R Core Team, Vienna, Austria, http://www.r-project.org/). All statistical analysis were two-sided. Unless otherwise stated, P value <0.05 was statistically significant.

The planned relative dose intensity of each chemotherapy component (rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, vinblastine, and prednisone) was maintained at a high level throughout the treatment period. Discussion

In this retrospectively study, we retrieved severalbaseline clinical characteristics, peripheral blood lymphocyte subsets, therapeutic strategies and clinical outcomes of 167 DLBCL patients treated with R-CHOP regimenBased on these data, we developed the nomograms to predict the 1-,3-,5-year PFS rate and 1-,3-,5-year OS rate of DLBCL patients, and proposed a novel immune prognostic index for individual prognosis evaluation. This model has been validated as a reliable tool for predicting survival in DLBCL patients and shown to have a higher C-index than the IPI, R-IPI, and NCCN-IPI. Both PFS and OS were used as our study endpoints. According to the minimal AIC, we used the stepwise regression to select variables, which finally output five identical variables included in the nomograms, including age, stage, ACD4C, extranodal sites, LDH. In the multivariate analyses, the most important factor associated with PFS was ACD4C, and the most important factor associated with OS was age. Patients were stratified into four risk groups, the low-risk group, the intermediate-low risk group, the high-intermediate risk group and the high-risk group based on the NIPI. In Kaplan-Meier survival analysis, we found more pronounced differences in survival rate between subgroups in NIPI than that in other models.

The heterogeneity of the DLBCL is related to tumor cells and tumor microenvironment (TME) [18]. TME T cells have been reported as good tools for the prediction of DLBCL outcome [19, 20]. High density of TME T cells may indicate a pre-existing antitumor immune response. Especially in rituximab-containing regimens, TME T cells were further stimulated to participate in the immune response against tumor [16]. CD4 + T cells are key players in adaptive immune response against lymphoma, with regulatory T-cells (Treg) playing an important role in the maintenance of immune homeostasis and body immune tolerance by suppressing the activation of other T-cell subsets [21]. Rituximab promoted cellular immune response that resulted in durable antitumor protection [22]. It has been showed that peripheral CD4 + T cell counts were significantly increased in the chemosensitive patients compared to chemoresistant patients in DLBCL [16].

Ann Arbor stages I-II non-bulky disease was conventionally considered as limited-stage DLBCL(LS-DLBCL). LS-DLBCL patients generally have excellent prognosis, with at least 70% to 90% of 5-year OS rate [23, 24]. Advanced-stage disease was often associated with the progression and systemic involvement of lymphoma. In a retrospective analysis of a large national cohort of DLBCL patients with validated SEER-Medicare set, older age and advanced stage were both demonstrated worse OS [25]. Our research also came to the same conclusion. Approximately one-third of DLBCL patients present with extranodal primary sites, most commonly in the gastrointestinal tract, head/neck, or skin/soft tissue [26]. Advanced-stage DLBCL can invade extranodal organs and the distinction of outcome between primary extranodal DLBCL and secondary extranodal DLBCL was hard to identify, which often depended on the specific part. NCCN-IPI redefined the identification of disease involvement at specific extranodal sites including bone marrow, CNS, liver/gastrointestinal tract, or lung, holding the idea that lymphomatous involvement of major organs would be a stronger predictor than merely the number of extranodal sites, such as IPI and R-IPI [10]. However, some studies have pointed out that number of extranodal sites ≥ 2 conferred progressively worse outcome [27].In our study, we selected involvement of ≥ 2 extranodal sites as a prognostic variable. Prognosis in DLBCL patients has been historically predicted by LDH both in IPI, R-IPI and NCCN-IPI. Consistent with previous studies, LDH levels above the upper limit of normal (ULN) was proved to be an adverse independent factor in DLBCL and incorporated into the NIPI.

The accuracy and generalizability of our prognostic model are dependent on the quality and completeness of baseline whole-body imaging, which serves as the foundation for precise disease staging and extranodal involvement mapping—two critical determinants of outcome in lymphoma. FDG‑PET/contrast‑enhanced CT represents the gold‑standard baseline imaging modality, as it reliably identifies occult subdiaphragmatic disease and splenic involvement in Hodgkin lymphoma that alters staging and directly dictates the need for extended anthracycline‑based chemotherapy and consolidative splenic radiation [28]. Moreover, prospective data confirm that modern hybrid imaging such as PET/MRI provides equivalent staging performance to PET/CT, further supporting the role of integrated metabolic/anatomic whole‑body imaging for uniform, accurate baseline assessment [29]. Incomplete or non‑standardized imaging may lead to understaging, misclassification of extranodal disease, and biased estimation of model‑derived risk. Therefore, the routine use of high‑quality fused FDG‑PET/contrast‑enhanced CT at diagnosis is not only essential for correct staging and treatment selection but also critical to preserving the prognostic performance and clinical validity of our model across real‑world populations.

We performed a novel model to predict the outcome of patients receiving R-CHOP immunochemotherapy and assess the population of patients who can benefit from this classic first-line treatment regimen or have a high-risk of treatment failure, which differed from previous studies. The ICPI has several advantages, in which the five factors (age, stage, extranodal sites, LDH and ACD4C) are all objective and available. Unlike IPI, ECOG PS is a fluctuant factor, which easily affected by the subjective judgment of the observer. We also included DLBCL patients only treated with R-CHOP therapy in order to eliminate the influence of different therapeutic factors. So far, the widely used model IPI and other derived models have failed to identify a true high-risk population. While the NIPI showed the ability to identify the patients with 5-year OS rate of less than 50% in this study.

The limitations were as follows. The performance and clinical utility of our prognostic model have not been explained in the context of modern frontline therapies combined with new drugs, especially high-dose liposomal anthracycline treatment regimens and immunochemical therapies containing polatuzumab and vedotin. Picardi et al. [30]. reported promising preliminary results with frontline liposomal doxorubicin supercharge regimens in patients with advanced‑stage diffuse large B‑cell lymphoma, showing favorable response and tolerability profiles that may alter baseline risk stratification and modify the prognostic separation achieved by conventional or model‑derived predictors. Similarly, frontline Pola‑R‑CHP (polatuzumab vedotin combined with rituximab, cyclophosphamide, doxorubicin, and prednisone) has emerged as a new standard of care in untreated diffuse large B‑cell lymphoma, significantly improving progression‑free survival compared with standard R‑CHOP. These modern regimens may reduce event rates in higher‑risk subgroups, dampen the prognostic impact of traditional variables, and require recalibration or validation of our model in cohorts treated with these novel frontline approaches to maintain robust risk stratification and clinical applicability. This was a single-center, retrospective study, with a relatively small sample size in China. Although the bootstrap method was used for internal validation, large samples of multi-center data are still needed for external validation. Considering that the population we included in the study was for patients who were only treated with R-CHOP, the predictive accuracy of the NIPI to the populations who were not use rituximab and some special types which need to receive central nervous system (CNS) prophylaxis are inappropriate. CD4⁺ T cell count and lymphocyte subset analysis are not included in the routine workup for DLBCL in general clinical settings. Accordingly, the practical application of our novel prognostic index will be limited in medical institutions lacking such testing capabilities. Because the median follow-up time in this study was approximately 3 years, future model predictions of 5-year PFS and OS rates still need to be further validated by extending the follow-up time.

Conclusion

In conclusion, this is an effective model for individualized risk prediction of DLBCL patients treated with R-CHOP regimen when compared with other clinical indexes, composed of four objective variables in the IPI score and peripheral CD4 + T cell counts. In our study, it could identify the extremely poor risk group with an OS rate of less than 50% to a certain extent. Due to the limitations of retrospective study, prospective multicenter studies are still needed to further validate the model before the model can be used in routine clinical practice.
