Vitiligo Information Resource database v3
Authors: Abhishek Sengupta, Vishal Singh, Muskan Syed, Alakto Choudhury, Payal Gupta, Somesh Gupta, Priyanka Narad
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Vitiligo is a multifaceted autoimmune skin disease characterized by the loss of epidermal melanocytes, yet its molecular basis remains poorly understood. To address the need for a specialized systems biology interface, we present VIRdb v3.0, an integrative database combining curated transcriptomics-based datasets with differentially expressed genes, a protein–protein interaction network highlighting regulatory hubs, and an enriched library of natural compounds and FDA-approved drugs targeting vitiligo-related proteins. The platform supports pathway enrichment, molecular docking, and cross-disease comparisons with autoimmune disorders to identify shared molecular signatures. Backend redevelopment using the Django framework enables scalability and real-time data integration. The updated platform now also supports z-score normalization of expression data, multi-gene pathway enrichment queries, and comparative gene–gene interaction networks for vitiligo and its comorbid autoimmune conditions. VIRdb v3.0 facilitates hypothesis generation, biomarker discovery, and therapeutic exploration, advancing vitiligo systems biology. The database is freely accessible at https://virdb.sbdaresearch.in/.

Introduction

The intricate nature of complex disorders poses significant challenges to clinical research due to their multifactorial etiologies involving genetic susceptibility, dysregulation of the immune system, and the environment1. They differ from monogenic diseases, whose etiologies are heterogeneous in nature, and hence identifying their causative mechanisms and drug targets becomes a challenge2. With the recent advances in multi-omics spanning from genomics, transcriptomics, proteomics, and epigenomics, research related to such diseases has been substantially revolutionized by facilitating systems-level insight into pathogenic mechanisms3. Furthermore, artificial intelligence (AI)-powered computational methods have facilitated researchers to integrate and analyze high-dimensional biological data to identify novel disease-related pathways and biomarkers4. This transition has ushered in more accurate therapeutic strategies and personalized treatment protocols, paving the way for a revolutionary period in the treatment of complex diseases5.

Vitiligo, a chronic autoimmune depigmentation disease with melanocyte destruction, is a classic example of the multifactorial nature of diseases6. It occurs in about 1–1.5% of the world’s population, with some studies indicating a rising prevalence of 5–9% in certain groups7. While autoimmunity is recognized as the prime etiology of melanocyte destruction, mechanisms involved in oxidative stress, impaired melanogenesis, and disruption of cell adhesion also contribute to disease progression8. The psychosocial consequence of vitiligo is profound, on the mood, self-concept, and overall quality of life of patients9. Notwithstanding decades of intensive study, however, the true molecular pathways leading to vitiligo continue to elude discovery10. Single-gene-based biochemical pathway concepts characterized the initial study of the topic, but the development of high-throughput sequencing methodologies opened new realms for research, as one may pursue an integrative overview of vitiligo’s genetic and epigenetic components11. Yet, the area remains undermined without an encompassing, easy-to-use database that complements multi-omics information with interactive analysis tools and databases specially designed for vitiligo research12.

To address this complexity, the Vitiligo Information Resource database (VIRdb) was previously developed as a dedicated systems biology platform for vitiligo research. VIRdb v1.0 provided a foundational repository integrating differentially expressed genes, protein targets, natural compounds, and molecular docking results, enabling early-stage therapeutic exploration. Subsequently, VIRdb v2.0 expanded this framework by incorporating co-expression networks and comorbidity-focused analyses, particularly across autoimmune disorders, thereby offering initial insights into shared molecular signatures. While these versions represented important advances, they were constrained by limited transcriptomic depth, the absence of standardized expression normalization across conditions, restricted immune-specific analyses, and a lack of cross-disease network integration.

Building upon the earlier efforts, Vitiligo Information Resource database v3.0 (VIRdb) now offers a major advancement in vitiligo research, integrating heterogeneous data with innovative computational analysis to allow for a deeper understanding of the molecular basis of the disease as indicated in (Fig. 1)13. Key features of VIRdb v3.0 include: (1) Comprehensive data exploration: Users can browse all available datasets, including differentially expressed genes (DEGs), natural leads, protein targets, and interactive DEG analysis, facilitating in-depth research on vitiligo. (2) Pathway and network analysis: Equipped with specialized tools for pathway enrichment, network visualization, and interaction analysis, VIRdb v3.0 enables the exploration of gene–disease associations and functional annotations. (3) Advanced data processing tools: Features include a heatmap generator, a boxplot generator, and gene information retrieval, allowing users to analyze expression profiles and visualize molecular interactions efficiently. (4) Biomarker and drug discovery: By integrating curated natural compounds and known protein targets, VIRdb 3.0 supports the identification of novel therapeutic candidates for vitiligo treatment. (5) User-friendly interface: Designed with interactive visualization tools, researchers can customize queries, generate graphical insights, and analyze multi-dimensional data with ease. A guided video walkthrough of the website’s features and usage is also available for users15.

By acting as an open, centrally accessible database, VIRdb v3.0 enables hypothesis-driven research, biomarker identification, and verification of in silico experiments of highly advanced vitiligo-related research16. Through its massive database structuring and high-end analytical integration abilities, VIRdb v3.0 enables scientists to discover new insights into pathogenic processes and opens the doors to improved diagnosis and treatment regimens17. VIRdb v3.0 is also designed to support global scientific collaborations, promoting the systematic exploration of vitiligo and its relationship with broader autoimmune and inflammatory disorders18. Results

VIRdb v3.0 introduces four major analytical enhancements: (i) a redesigned heatmap module implementing gene-level z-score normalization for comparing gene expression profiles across conditions; (ii) an integrated boxplot analysis enabling simultaneous comparative analysis of multiple genes and phenotypes across patient cohorts; (iii) a comparative functionality for analyzing gene expression array-based studies of autoimmune disorders; and (iv) systems biology modules for constructing and interpreting protein–protein interaction (PPI) and gene–gene interaction (GGI) networks based on disease-relevant targets. These integrated tools facilitate a systems-level understanding of immune dysregulation, pathway convergence, and interaction landscapes, moving beyond static differential expression outputs. Application of these analytical capabilities to vitiligo and multiple sclerosis datasets demonstrates the platform’s utility for comprehensive autoimmune research.

Use Case 1: Heatmap analysis reveals differential expression of immune regulatory genes in vitiligo

The heatmap of gene expression provides a visual overview of the relative expression patterns of CTLA4 and CD28 across 36 skin biopsy samples, stratified into 9 healthy, 9 lesional, 9 non-lesional, and 9 peri-lesional groups. To enable meaningful intra-gene comparisons, z-score normalization was applied across samples for each gene independently, thereby highlighting relative expression shifts within a gene across conditions, while avoiding direct comparison between genes. The analysis, performed using the preloaded vitiligo dataset, reveals distinct condition-specific variation. For instance, CD28, a costimulatory receptor involved in T-cell activation, displays generally higher relative expression (z-scores ≥ 0) in most lesional and peri-lesional samples (e.g., Lesional_5, Peri Lesional_6), consistent with increased T-cell co-stimulation activity in affected tissue. In contrast, CTLA4, an inhibitory checkpoint receptor, exhibits more variable expression, including lower z-score values (e.g., ≤−1 in Peri Lesional_5 and Lesional_4) in certain affected samples, suggesting localized dampening of immune inhibition in specific regions. Notably, healthy samples such as Healthy_5 show relatively elevated CTLA4 expression (z-score > +2), underscoring the heterogeneity across patient samples. While absolute expression magnitudes differ between genes and are not directly comparable, these within-gene trends support the notion that a context-specific imbalance in immune activation and regulation may contribute to the autoimmune landscape of vitiligo. CTLA4 showed the greatest variability in expression among the three genes explored, with a number of samples showing significant upregulated expression (bright yellow), particularly amongst Healthy and some Lesional samples. The heterogeneity in expression for this immune checkpoint inhibitor may reflect differences in the level of regulatory T-cell activity regulating the immune suppression, or the mechanisms behind the immune suppressive effect. Although these expression changes were normalized via z-score transformation and therefore are useful for relative comparisons, immune gene expression profiles seen in vitiligo are unique to the individual, instead of conforming to a uniform disease-associated signature. This heterogeneity is likely associated with the varied clinical presentation and treatment responses in vitiligo and underlines the need for assessment of personalized therapy.

Use Case 2: Boxplot analysis reveals gene expression profiles and pathway implications in vitiligo and multiple sclerosis

Differential expression analyses between different sample groups indicated different regulatory processes, especially in immune and metabolic pathways. The analysis compared gene expression patterns and pathway implications in vitiligo and multiple sclerosis (MS) to reveal transcriptional changes involved in disease development.

Gene expression analysis was conducted using the updated version of the boxplot analysis module, which enables visualization across both genes and sample conditions. Across-gene analysis for the Vitiligo cohort revealed differential expression patterns of CTLA4 and CD28, with CD28 exhibiting significantly higher median expression levels (~6.1) compared to CTLA4 (~4.8). Furthermore, condition-specific analysis of CTLA4 expression across healthy, lesional, non-lesional, and peri-lesional groups (Fig. 3) demonstrated nuanced dysregulation. While healthy controls showed moderately consistent expression (median ~4.8), lesional samples exhibited reduced levels (median ~4.6), suggestive of suppressed immune checkpoint signaling in affected tissues. Interestingly, non-lesional samples displayed elevated expression (median ~5.0), hinting at compensatory regulation or pre-lesional immune priming. Peri-lesional tissues exhibited the widest variability with a median expression of ~4.5 and the presence of outliers. These dynamic expression shifts in CTLA4 and CD28 are biologically relevant as both are critical regulators of T-cell co-stimulation and immune tolerance. Such variations may reflect localized immune activation, exhaustion, or remodeling across disease-affected zones in vitiligo. In the Across Genes mode, expression profiles of CTLA4, CD28, and PDCD1 were examined across 29 PBMC samples from MS patients and healthy controls. As shown in Fig. 4a, CD28 exhibited the highest median expression (~8.3), followed by PDCD1 (~6.8), while CTLA4 showed a relatively lower expression range (~4.5–4.9). The observed interquartile ranges indicate consistent expression of CD28 across samples, while CTLA4 exhibited minor variability. These differences support known immunoregulatory roles of these checkpoint and co-stimulatory molecules in MS pathogenesis. To further dissect the expression of CTLA4, we used the Across Conditions mode to compare MS samples with healthy controls (Fig. 4b). The boxplot revealed a marginal increase in median expression levels of CTLA4 in MS samples (~4.7) compared to healthy controls (~4.6), with similar variability. Although modest, this elevation suggests a potential dysregulation of immune checkpoint activity in MS. The tool’s flexibility allows such gene-level comparisons across disease states and could assist in identifying candidate immunoregulatory biomarkers. Pathway enrichment of highly expressed genes (including CD28 and PDCD1) is indicative of activation of T-cell co-stimulation, neuroinflammation, and NF-κB signaling pathways. The protein–protein interaction (PPI) network was constructed using a manually curated list of vitiligo-associated therapeutic protein targets, comprising both experimentally validated differentially expressed genes (DEGs) and additional targets derived from prior literature31. The second cluster, with the hub protein SMAD2, a master effector molecule of TGF-β pathways, including proteins such as BLU and XRCC5YWHAQ, indicates this module’s function in transcriptional processes and DNA damage response mechanisms, with implications for an interface between immune regulation and melanocyte loss. HSPB1, the small heat shock protein, and SMAD4, a TGF-β pathway mediator molecule, and PRKDC constituted the third group, reflecting cross-talk between stress adaptation mechanisms, signal cascades, and DNA repair machinery. The absence of intermediary genes suggests the direct functional interaction between these modules, emphasizing their reciprocal function in suppressing cellular stress and melanocyte integrity. The over-representation of stress-response proteins and DNA repair molecules is consistent with the oxidative stress model of vitiligo, while interactions implicating TGF-β/SMAD pathways imply new targets for modulating immune-mediated melanocyte death. Future perspectives and hypothesis-generating applications of VIRdb v3.0

While the present study primarily aims to demonstrate the analytical scope and functionality of VIRdb v3.0 as a community resource, the platform is inherently designed to support hypothesis generation rather than formal hypothesis testing. The representative analyses showcased here serve as illustrative use cases, highlighting how integrated transcriptomic, network, and systems-level tools can be applied to explore immune regulatory axes, stress-response pathways, and molecular interaction modules in vitiligo and related autoimmune conditions. These examples are intended to guide users in leveraging the database for exploratory investigations rather than to assert definitive biological conclusions. Future applications of VIRdb v3.0 may involve the formulation and rigorous evaluation of specific biological hypotheses through experimental validation, incorporation of longitudinal and single-cell datasets, and integration with clinical phenotypes, thereby extending its utility toward hypothesis-driven and translational research.

Discussion

VIRdb v3.0 represents a useful contribution to vitiligo research, offering a customized approach to a multi-dimensional database that integrates genetic, proteomic, and cheminformatic data to allow comprehensive analysis of vitiligo pathogenesis and therapeutic discovery. Among its strengths is the inclusion of differentially expressed genes from multiple vitiligo subtypes, selected from publicly available high-throughput transcriptomic datasets. By integrating experimentally confirmed vitiligo-related protein targets and virtually screening natural products from the NPASS database, VIRdb v3.0 offers a solid platform for scientists to discover new therapeutic candidates. One of the most significant advantages of the platform is its interactive network visualization, which employs D3-Force algorithms to visualize complex interactions among genes, proteins, and bioactive compounds. This feature enables researchers to identify top regulatory nodes and mechanistic pathways in vitiligo and thereby give hints for potential intervention strategies. Moreover, VIRdb v3.0 provides useful data analysis functionalities, including interactive heatmap and boxplot generators, which allow scientists to explore patterns of gene expression across multiple datasets. These tools improve hypothesis generation, enabling better experimental design and validation studies. In addition to its data consolidation and visualization features, VIRdb v3.0 is also an active knowledge-sharing system, incorporating recent literature resources, blog entries, and research news. This supports an interactive research environment that closes the gap between computational biologists, dermatologists, and experimental scientists.

VIRdb v3.0 is thus a vitiligo-focused bioinformatics platform designed to advance vitiligo research through comprehensive transcriptomic and molecular interaction analyses. By integrating high-throughput RNA sequencing data with functional enrichment tools, it enables researchers to decipher critical molecular pathways involved in vitiligo pathogenesis. The database provides a suite of tools and datasets for analyzing molecular interactions, pathway associations, and gene expression patterns, allowing users to explore detailed analysis of differentially expressed genes, protein targets, molecular docking results, and network relationships. The revised platform features enhanced normalization, robust gene-pathway tools, and clearer UI for vitiligo-specific analysis. These enhancements, including z-score normalization, improved gene/pathway analysis, and support for cross-disease comparison, make VIRdb v3.0 a more robust and user-oriented platform for vitiligo research. Future uses of VIRdb v3.0 extend beyond vitiligo because its architecture can be adapted to explore other autoimmune and pigmentary conditions. The ability of the database to accept new datasets and the future possibility of including multi-omics levels of epigenomic and metabolomic data will ensure that it remains an important precision medicine tool. Further, the inclusion of AI-based predictive modeling may streamline drug repurposing approaches, speeding up the discovery of high-therapeutic-potential candidate molecules.

In summary, VIRdb v3.0 is an integrated resource in vitiligo research, providing comprehensive data integration, sophisticated computational capabilities, and a collaborative environment for researchers and clinicians. Through the facilitation of streamlined identification of disease-linked molecular targets and candidate therapeutics, the platform not only enhances our knowledge of the molecular mechanisms of vitiligo but also expedites the creation of targeted, individualized interventions, ultimately leading to better patient outcomes. While VIRdb v3.0 currently focuses on transcriptomic analysis and pathway exploration, its future development aims to incorporate multi-omics data, including proteomics and metabolomics, to refine disease modeling and biomarker identification. Additionally, planned advancements such as AI-driven predictive modeling and deep learning algorithms will enhance disease classification and therapeutic response predictions. As computational and AI-driven methodologies become integral to dermatology, VIRdb v3.0 is set to become a cornerstone resource, bridging gaps in vitiligo research and fostering innovative strategies for diagnosis, prognosis, and treatment. By continuously evolving and providing a centralized, accessible database, VIRdb v3.0 empowers researchers and clinicians to drive significant advancements in understanding vitiligo, ultimately improving patient outcomes and guiding future breakthroughs in dermatological research.

Data acquisition repositories and database content

Systematic literature search of scientific databases such as PubMed, Scopus, Web of Science, and Google Scholar was conducted for all the applicable studies addressing gene expression in vitiligo, protein interactions, metabolic pathways, and potential therapeutic targets32. In order to enhance the effectiveness of the search, a list of keyword combinations using Boolean operators (“AND,” “OR,” and “NOT”) was established to facilitate the retrieval of pertinent publications, as illustrated in (Fig. 7). The articles retrieved were subsequently filtered systematically based on predetermined inclusion and exclusion criteria, ultimately reducing the selection to 76 highly relevant studies33. For ease of data extraction, a text-mining script was also formulated and executed in the RStudio platform to additionally facilitate automated data collection of literature in CSV format34. The selected studies provided valuable information on pathogenesis-associated genes, protein–protein interactions, genetic variations, and their relationships with other diseases. GSE12991 is included for its relevance to miRNA expression data associated with melanogenesis and pigmentation genes associated pathways that are evolutionarily conserved, despite being from zebrafish. GSE188603 provides in vitro transcriptomic insights into the response of PIG3V vitiligo melanocytes to hydroxychloroquine treatment, offering a controlled model to study pigmentation recovery and immune modulation mechanisms relevant to vitiligo pathophysiology. GSE198956 captures non-coding RNA profiles from plasma samples of vitiligo patients, supporting the identification of circulating RNA signatures for potential use as non-invasive biomarkers, thereby expanding the database’s translational utility beyond tissue-level expression data. Deployment of database architecture

The VIRdb v3.0 database, which manages vitiligo-related data, operates on a hybrid cloud infrastructure for high availability, security, and scalability. The backend system is constructed based on a relational database management system (PostgreSQL) for the storage of structured data and MongoDB for semi-structured datasets, including gene-disease relationships and pathway interactions96. The application is hosted on a load-balanced server environment with caching and load-balancing mechanisms to enhance query execution and data retrieval performance. Nginx is used as the main web server, handling HTTP requests and content delivery efficiently97.

Static content and database files are stored and retrieved in a systematic manner using an integrated cloud storage mechanism, avoiding loss of data and providing redundancy98. The frontend is developed with the latest web technologies like React.js to enable smooth user interaction with heavy filtering and search capabilities for vitiligo gene and protein-related data99. The backend is run on a Python-based Django platform, offering a solid API layer to enable fast data querying and visualization. Security aspects involve SSL/TLS encryption for safe transmission of data, role-based access control (RBAC) for restricted user privileges, and automated recovery and backup mechanisms in case of data loss100. System performance is monitored and logged with metrics like Prometheus and Grafana, and automated patching and updating via a CI/CD pipeline minimize downtime101. Logging and monitoring are done in a modular way to enable scalability and integration with other bioinformatics tools in the future, as demonstrated in Fig. 10.
