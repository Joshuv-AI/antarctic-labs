Tuberculous pyosalpinx associated with serous tubal intraepithelial lesion: a case report on evidence of early tubal neoplastic transformation
Authors: Akkamahadevi C. Hiremath, Vandana N. Kumar, Brunda Muniswamy, Archana Shivamurthy, Vivek Tubagere Gururaj, Padmasri Ramalingappa
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
Serous tubal intraepithelial lesion (STIL) is an uncommon precursor lesion in the spectrum of tubal serous neoplasia, characterised by atypical epithelial proliferation, aberrant p53 expression and a low proliferative index. Chronic inflammatory conditions such as tuberculosis may contribute to carcinogenesis through persistent epithelial injury. This report describes the case of a 29-year-old woman with previously treated tubercular endometritis who presented with persistent pelvic pain and bilateral pyosalpinx. Diagnostic laparoscopy revealed grossly enlarged fallopian tubes with torsion and purulent intraluminal contents, necessitating bilateral salpingectomy. Histopathological examination demonstrated chronic granulomatous salpingitis with focal epithelial atypia. Immunohistochemistry showed diffuse abnormal p53 overexpression (80%) and a low Ki-67 proliferation index (<10%), consistent with STIL. Marked cytological atypia and high proliferative activity were absent, favouring STIL over serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma. This case highlights a rare association between chronic tuberculous inflammation and early tubal neoplastic transformation. It emphasises the importance of meticulous histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation of salpingectomy specimens.

Introduction

Serous tubal intraepithelial lesion (STIL) represents an early precursor lesion in the continuum of tubal serous carcinogenesis, bridging the transition between benign tubal epithelium and serous tubal intraepithelial carcinoma (STIC). STIC is widely recognised as a precursor to high-grade serous carcinoma (HGSC), particularly arising from the fimbrial end of the fallopian tube [1], [2].

Chronic inflammation is a well-established contributor to carcinogenesis. Persistent infectious and inflammatory processes may lead to repeated cycles of epithelial injury and repair, promoting oxidative DNA damage, genomic instability and dysregulated cellular proliferation [3], [4].

Pelvic tuberculosis is a chronic granulomatous disease that commonly involves the fallopian tubes, leading to fibrosis, adhesions and structural distortion. Although chronic inflammation has been implicated in malignant transformation, its role in early tubal epithelial precursor lesions remains poorly understood.

This report describes a case involving the rare coexistence of tuberculous salpingitis and STIL, highlighting the possible association between chronic granulomatous inflammation and early tubal neoplastic transformation.

Case Presentation

A 29-year-old nulligravida presented with acute abdominal pain predominantly involving the right iliac fossa. She initially sought treatment at a local clinic, where she received symptomatic management with analgesics. Pelvic ultrasonography performed at that time revealed bilateral hydrosalpinx with tubal diameters measuring more than 3.5 cm, more prominent on the right side, with internal echogenic contents suggestive of inflammatory debris. As she had been married for 2 years without conception, conservative management was advised.

Despite analgesic therapy, she continued to experience persistent dull aching lower abdominal pain and subsequently presented to another institute for further evaluation. There was no history suggestive of pulmonary tuberculosis. However, she reported prolonged close household exposure to a family member with chronic cough, fever and weight loss suggestive of pulmonary tuberculosis while living in an overcrowded household.

On examination, the patient was afebrile with a body mass index of 17.4 kg/m2. Abdominal examination revealed a palpable tender cystic pelvic mass corresponding to approximately 18–20 weeks gravid uterine size. There was no clinical evidence of ascites or peripheral lymphadenopathy. In view of the bilateral tubo-ovarian pathology and suspicion of genital tuberculosis, an endometrial biopsy was performed for cartridge-based nucleic acid amplification test (CBNAAT) and histopathological examination. Although CBNAAT was negative for Mycobacterium tuberculosis, histopathology demonstrated granulomatous endometritis suggestive of genital tuberculosis.

The patient subsequently received standard 6-month antitubercular therapy (ATT). However, she continued to experience intermittent dull aching abdominal pain during treatment. Follow-up ultrasonography demonstrated persistent bilateral adnexal tubular cystic lesions. The left adnexal lesion measured 9 × 3.4 cm, while the right-sided tubular echoic structure with incomplete septations measured 9.4 × 4.4 cm, suggestive of persistent complex hydrosalpinx/pyosalpinx (Fig.1a, b). MRI revealed bilateral dilated tubular adnexal structures, with diameters measuring up to 4.7 cm on the right side and 4.0 cm on the left side. The lesions demonstrated T2 hyperintense and T1 hyperintense luminal signals with internal debris and fluid layering, suggestive of thick purulent contents. In view of persistent symptoms and progressive tubo-ovarian disease, diagnostic laparoscopy was planned. The patient was counselled preoperatively regarding the probable need for bilateral salpingectomy because of grossly diseased fallopian tubes and the possible future requirement for assisted reproductive techniques, including in vitro fertilisation and embryo transfer. Informed written consent was obtained.

Diagnostic laparoscopy revealed grossly enlarged bilateral fallopian tubes with torsion of the right tube (Fig. 3a). Aspiration yielded purulent fluid from the right tube, while thick caseous material was obtained from the left tube (Fig. 3c, d). Features suggestive of Fitz-Hugh-Curtis syndrome were also noted (Fig. 3b). No obvious peritoneal tubercles were identified intraoperatively. The postoperative period was uneventful. At 9-month follow-up, the patient remained asymptomatic with complete resolution of abdominal pain. She was counselled regarding future fertility options, including assisted reproductive techniques such as in vitro fertilisation and embryo transfer.

Discussion

Female genital tuberculosis is usually secondary to hematogenous or lymphatic dissemination from a primary focus, most commonly pulmonary tuberculosis, which may remain clinically silent or undiagnosed. In the present case, the patient had no overt pulmonary symptoms but reported prolonged close household exposure to a family member with symptoms suggestive of pulmonary tuberculosis while living in overcrowded conditions, which may have contributed to occult genital infection.

The diagnosis of genital tuberculosis was supported by histopathological evidence of granulomatous endometritis with epithelioid granulomas, Langhans giant cells and focal necrosis. Although CBNAAT was negative, female genital tuberculosis is typically paucibacillary, limiting the sensitivity of microbiological investigations including CBNAAT, acid-fast bacilli staining and culture. Histopathology therefore remains an important diagnostic modality, particularly in endemic regions.

STIL is recognised as an early precursor lesion in the spectrum of tubal serous carcinogenesis, intermediate between benign tubal epithelium and STIC. STIL is characterised by mild to moderate cytological atypia, abnormal p53 expression and low proliferative activity [1], [7]. The fallopian tube, particularly the fimbrial end, is increasingly recognised as the site of origin for many high-grade serous carcinomas [4], [5], [6].

Chronic inflammation is an established contributor to carcinogenesis through mechanisms including oxidative DNA damage, cytokine-mediated cellular proliferation and impaired apoptosis [8]. Persistent granulomatous inflammation in pelvic tuberculosis may similarly create a pro-inflammatory microenvironment capable of promoting epithelial dysplasia and early neoplastic transformation. Although a direct causal association between tuberculous salpingitis and STIL has not been established, the present case raises the possibility of inflammation-associated early tubal epithelial neoplasia.

An important diagnostic challenge in this case was differentiating STIL from reactive epithelial atypia and pseudocarcinomatous hyperplasia secondary to chronic inflammation. Histopathological findings of epithelial stratification and nuclear atypia initially raised concern for reactive proliferative changes. However, diffuse abnormal p53 overexpression involving more than 80% of epithelial nuclei together with a low Ki-67 proliferation index (<10%) supported the diagnosis of STIL rather than STIC, which typically demonstrates marked atypia and higher proliferative activity. Immunohistochemistry therefore played a crucial role in distinguishing reactive from premalignant epithelial alterations.

Reports describing the coexistence of chronic tuberculous salpingitis and STIL remain extremely limited in the literature. This case highlights the importance of meticulous histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation of salpingectomy specimens obtained for chronic inflammatory tubo-ovarian disease, as clinically significant precursor lesions may otherwise remain undetected.

Conclusion

Chronic pelvic tuberculosis may contribute to early epithelial neoplastic alterations within the fallopian tube, including STIL, through persistent granulomatous inflammation and repeated epithelial injury. Although STIL is not an invasive malignancy, it represents an important precursor lesion in the spectrum of tubal serous carcinogenesis. This case highlights the rare coexistence of tuberculous pyosalpinx and STIL, suggesting a possible association between chronic inflammation and early tubal neoplastic transformation. It also underscores the importance of meticulous histopathological and immunohistochemical evaluation of salpingectomy specimens, even in apparently infectious tubo-ovarian disease, to identify clinically significant precursor lesions that may otherwise remain undetected.
