Search for a new heavy scalar resonance decaying to a pair of \PZ bosons in the four-lepton final state in proton-proton collisions at s = 13 ​ \TeV \sqrt{s}=13\TeV


Abstract
A search for a new heavy scalar resonance decaying to two \PZ bosons, each subsequently decaying to a pair of electrons or muons, is presented. The results are based on a proton-proton collision data set collected by the CMS experiment at the LHC at a center-of-mass energy of 13 \TeV , corresponding to an integrated luminosity of 138 \fbinv . The search is performed over a wide range of resonance masses from 130 \GeV to 3 \TeV , considering both narrow- and broad-width scenarios, and considering the gluon fusion and vector boson fusion production processes. For the broad-width scenario, the interference between the new resonance, the 125 \GeV Higgs boson production, and the continuum background is taken into account. No significant excess with respect to the standard model background expectation is observed in the examined phase space. Upper limits at the 95% confidence level are set on the product of the heavy scalar resonance production cross section and the branching fraction for its decay into two \PZ bosons. The exclusion limits range from 0.05–0.1 \unit pb in the low-mass region to 0.005 \unit pb in the high-mass region.

Introduction

In 2012, the ATLAS and CMS Collaborations discovered a boson
[
7
,
25
,
26
]
with a mass close to 125
\GeV
and with properties consistent with those expected for the Higgs boson (
\PH
) predicted by the standard model of particle physics (SM).
This discovery represents a major breakthrough for the field of particle physics and a significant validation of the SM, which postulates the existence of this particle as an excitation of the Higgs field.

The SM, however, presents several limitations. For example, it does not include a description of gravity or dark matter and does not explain the origin of the matter-antimatter asymmetry in the universe.
These issues could be addressed by a range of theories beyond the SM (BSM). In many models describing such BSM effects, additional scalar resonances are predicted,
\eg
, additional Higgs bosons in two-Higgs doublet models
[
14
]
and radions in models with warped extra dimensions
[

,
60
,
20
]
.

Previous searches for a new boson decaying to a pair of
\PZ
bosons have been performed by the CMS
[
28
,
38
]
and ATLAS
[
8
,
9
]
Collaborations
using data collected in Run 1 (2011–2012) and Run 2 (2015–2018) of the CERN LHC. In these searches, no evidence for a signal was observed.

In this paper, a search for a heavy scalar resonance
\PX
is presented in the decay channel
\PX\to\PZ\PZ\to 4\Pell
where the leptons
\Pell
are electrons (
\Pe

\PGm

Since the mass of the new resonance is not known, the search is performed over a wide mass range from 130
\GeV
to 3
\TeV
. The resonance can be produced via gluon fusion (ggF) or vector boson fusion (VBF). The numerical results are provided in HEPData
[
1
]
.

The CMS detector

The CMS apparatus
[
23
,
48
]
is a multipurpose, nearly hermetic detector, designed to trigger on
[
41
,
34
,
49
]
and identify electrons, muons, photons, and charged and neutral hadrons
[
43
,
37
,
27

\unit

\unit

\eta

More detailed descriptions of the CMS detector, together with a definition of the coordinate system used and the relevant kinematic variables, can be found in Refs.
[
23
,
48
]
.

Events of interest are selected using a two-tiered trigger system. The first level (L1), composed of custom hardware processors, uses information from the calorimeters and muon detectors to select events at a rate of around 100
\unit
kHz within a fixed latency of 4
\mus

[
41
]

49

Data and simulated samples

The search is performed using the
\Pp\Pp
collision data set collected by the CMS experiment in 2016–2018
[
45
,
35
,
39
]
, with a total integrated luminosity of 138
\fbinv
.

Events are selected by the online trigger system before undergoing the reconstruction described in Section
0.4
. The online selection is based on dielectron, dimuon, and electron-muon trigger algorithms. Additional triggers, requiring three leptons with low transverse momentum (
\pt
) thresholds and no isolation criteria, as well as single-lepton triggers, are also used to increase the selection efficiency.
The applied
\pt

\GeV
for single-lepton triggers, from 8 to 23
\GeV
for double-lepton triggers, and from 5 to 16
\GeV

The trigger efficiency measurement follows the strategy adopted in Ref.
[
44

, using a “tag-and-probe” method
[
24
]
. The efficiency is greater than 99% in most of the phase space and is compatible between the observed data and simulation within statistical uncertainties.

Monte Carlo (MC) simulated samples are used to model the signal, background, and interference processes.

Signal samples are generated at next-to-leading order (NLO) in perturbative quantum chromodynamics (pQCD) with the
\POWHEG
2.0
[
3
,
75
,
57
]
generator for the ggF and VBF production mechanisms. The decays of the BSM resonances to four leptons are modeled with
JHUGen
7.0.2
[
59
,
12
,
5
,
65
]
, including corrections for the
\PZ\PZ
branching fraction and angular correlations among the fermions. Signal samples are generated for a wide range of masses of the
\PX
resonance (

\GeV
to 3
\TeV

\Gamma_{\PX}
) set to the values predicted by the complex pole scheme (CPS)
[

Events in ggF production samples generated with
\POWHEG

[
68

(NNLOPS). These weights are obtained from the ratio of the production cross section at NNLOPS accuracy to the production cross section obtained with the

The dominant background process originates from the production of two
\PZ

\PQq\PAQq\to\PZ\PZ\to 4\Pell

Another important background is the loop-induced production of two

The background of
\PQq\PQq\to\PQq\PQq\PZ\PZ

\MCFM
7.0. It is referred to as VBF
\PZ\PZ

Samples of triboson events (
\PW\PW\PZ
,

\MGvATNLO

Additional samples of

All simulated samples are interfaced with

0.4
Event reconstruction and selection

The particle-flow (PF) algorithm
[
33
]
is used to reconstruct and identify individual particles in the event with an optimized combination of the raw information from each subdetector. The reconstructed particles are referred to as PF candidates. The energy of photons is measured in the ECAL. Electrons are reconstructed by associating the reconstructed tracks in the inner tracker with the ECAL clusters, the latter additionally considering the bremsstrahlung photon showers geometrically compatible with the electron track. The electron energy is obtained from the combination of the track momentum at the primary vertex (PV) and the cluster energy, where the PV is taken to be the vertex corresponding to the hardest scattering in the event, evaluated using tracking information alone, as described in Ref.
[
29
]
. The muon momentum is measured from the track momenta in both the inner tracker and the muon detectors. The energy of charged hadrons is determined by combining tracks in the inner tracker with the associated energy deposits in both the ECAL and HCAL, with corrections to take into account the imperfect linear energy response of calorimeters. Finally, the energy of neutral hadrons is determined from the energy deposits in the ECAL and HCAL.

Electrons with transverse momenta
\pt^{\Pe}>7\GeV
are reconstructed within the detector acceptance region, corresponding to a pseudorapidity range of
\abs{\eta^{\Pe}}<2.5

[
43
]
.
For each electron, the impact parameter significance, defined as the ratio of the three-dimensional (3D) impact parameter with respect to the PV to its uncertainty, is required to be
<4
. This ensures the suppression of nonprompt leptons from hadron decays or photon conversions.
Electrons are identified using a boosted decision tree algorithm based on the
XGBoost
framework
[
22
]
, including electron isolation information. The algorithm was developed for the analysis in Ref.
[
44
]
, with an updated training performed for Ref.
[
46
]
.

Muons with
\pt^{\PGm}>5\GeV
are reconstructed within the detector acceptance region
\abs{\eta^{\PGm}}<2.4
, combining the information from the silicon tracker and the muon system
[
37
]
.

Cases where tracks in the tracker are matched to hits in only one or two muon planes are used to recover very low
\pt
muons that do not traverse the entire detector.
The muons considered in this analysis are selected from the muon track candidates by applying loose requirements on the track in the muon system and the inner tracker.

For muons with
\pt^{\PGm}>200\GeV
, dedicated identification requirements are applied to improve the efficiency
[
37
]
.
The same impact parameter requirements as for electrons are applied.

To distinguish the muon candidates from the
\PZ
boson decay from those from hadronic decays within jets, a requirement on the relative isolation
\mathcal{I}^{\PGm}<0.35
is imposed, which was optimized in Ref.
[
30
]
. The relative isolation is defined as:

\mathcal{I}^{\PGm}=\frac{1}{\pt^{\PGm}}\left(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{charged}\\
\text{hadrons}\end{subarray}}\pt+\max\left[\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{neutral}\\
\text{hadrons}\end{subarray}}\pt+\sum_{\text{photons}}\pt-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\text{charged}\\
\text{hadrons}\end{subarray}}\pt^{\text{PU}},\,0\GeV\right]\right)

(1)

where the sums are performed over PF candidates
i
within a cone of
\Delta R(\PGm,i)<0.3
, with
\Delta R(\PGm,i)=\sqrt{\smash[b]{(\eta^{\PGm}-\eta^{i})^{2}+(\phi^{\PGm}-\phi^{i})^{2}}}
, where
\phi
is the azimuthal angle in radians. The first sum involves charged hadrons originating from the PV, and the second term involves neutral hadrons and photons. The contribution of neutral particles from PU vertices, estimated as half the contribution of charged hadrons from PU, is subtracted. The total contribution from neutral particles is guaranteed to be non-negative by the max function.

Photons originating through final-state radiation (FSR) from the selected leptons are recovered to improve the accuracy of the
\PZ
boson candidate mass reconstruction. Particle-flow photons satisfying
\pt^{\PGg}>2\GeV
,
\abs{\eta^{\PGg}}<2.4
, and relative isolation
\mathcal{I}^{\PGg}<1.8
are considered as FSR candidates. This selection was optimized in Ref.
[
25
]
. Each candidate is associated to the closest lepton in the event and is required to have
\Delta R(\PGg,\Pell)/E_{\text{T},\PGg}^{2}<0.012/\GeV^{2}
and
\Delta R(\PGg,\Pell)<0.5
, where
E_{\text{T},\PGg}
is the transverse energy of the photon. For each lepton, only the photon with the lowest
\Delta R(\PGg,\Pell)/E_{\text{T},\PGg}^{2}
is retained. The FSR candidates are excluded from the computation of the muon isolation.

Lepton reconstruction and selection efficiencies are measured with a tag-and-probe method, selecting
\PZ
boson decays both in data and in simulation, separately for electrons and muons in bins of
\pt
and
\eta
. All MC samples are corrected by the ratio of the efficiency in data to that in simulation.
The lepton energy scale and resolution are calibrated using known resonances decaying to two leptons in bins of
\pt
and
\eta
. Detailed descriptions of this calibration are given in Refs.
[
43
,
37
]
.

Hadronic jets are clustered from PF candidates using the infrared- and collinear-safe anti-
\kt
algorithm
[
16
]
with a distance parameter of 0.4. The jet energy is computed from the vectorial sum of all PF candidates clustered inside it.
Since the reconstructed jet energy is on average 5–10% larger than the true jet energy in simulation, particles from PU vertices are discarded from the jet composition, and residual corrections are applied. The corrections are derived from dijet,
\PGg
+ jets,
\PZ
+ jets, and multijet events
[
32
]
.
In this analysis, hadronic jets are required to have
\pt^{\text{jet}}>30\GeV
and
\abs{\eta^{\text{jet}}}<4.7
. Requirements are made on their energy fractions of different types of particles, and they must have
\Delta R>0.4
with respect to all selected leptons and associated photons. For jets with
\pt^{\text{jet}}<50\GeV
, a dedicated identification algorithm is used to reject jets from PU
[
42
]
.

The jet flavor is identified using the
DeepCSV
algorithm
[
36
,
31
,
47
]
, a deep neural network based algorithm to tag heavy-flavor jets. Jets that pass the working point with a 68% tagging efficiency for
\PQb
jets, a 10–20% mistagging probability for
\PQc
jets, and a 1% mistagging probability for light flavor jets are considered to be
\PQb
tagged. Since most jets from VBF stem from light-flavor quarks, vetoing
\PQb
-tagged jets suppresses backgrounds containing
\PQb
jets and improves the sensitivity to the VBF production process.

The described PF objects are used as input to the event selection, which targets events with at least four identified and isolated leptons originating from the PV. The event selection follows that employed in Ref.
[
44
]
and is summarized below.

Pairs of leptons with opposite charge and same flavor, whose invariant mass must be
12<m_{\PZ}<120\GeV
, are used to build
\PZ
boson candidates. Then,
\PZ\PZ
candidates are formed by selecting two of the
\PZ
boson candidates. The
\PZ
boson candidate with invariant mass closest to the nominal
\PZ
boson mass
m_{\PZ}^{\text{nom}}=91.1876\GeV

[
76
]
is defined as
\PZ_{1}
, and the other is defined as
\PZ_{2}
.
Three mutually exclusive final states are defined on the basis of the flavors of the four leptons in the event:
4\PGm
,
4\Pe
, and
2\Pe 2\PGm
.
The
\PZ\PZ
candidates must satisfy the following requirements:

•

Any two of the four leptons must have
\Delta R(\Pell_{1},\Pell_{2})>0.02
.

•

The
\PZ_{1}
is required to have
m_{\PZ_{1}}>40\GeV
.

•

Among the four leptons, the one with the highest (second highest)
\pt
must have
\pt>20\,(10)\GeV
.

•

Any two of the four leptons with opposite charge must have
m_{\Pell^{+}\Pell^{-}}>4\GeV
, to suppress QCD multijet events.

•

In the
4\PGm
and
4\Pe
final states, background processes containing a
\PZ
boson and a low-mass resonance such as
\PJGy
can pass the above selections by swapping one lepton between the two resonances. To avoid selecting such events, given a
\PZ\PZ
candidate, two alternative
\PZ
boson candidates are built by swapping the two positively charged leptons. The alternative
\PZ
boson candidate with the invariant mass closer to
m_{\PZ}^{\text{nom}}
is denoted as
\PZ_{\text{a}}
, the other as
\PZ_{\text{b}}
. The
\PZ\PZ
candidate is discarded if
\abs{m_{\PZ_{\text{a}}}-m_{\PZ}^{\text{nom}}}<\abs{m_{\PZ_{1}}-m_{\PZ}^{\text{nom}}}
and
m_{\PZ_{\text{b}}}<12\GeV
.

•

The invariant mass of the
\PZ\PZ
candidate is required to be greater than 70
\GeV
.

The signal region is composed of all events with at least one
\PZ\PZ
candidate. If more than one
\PZ\PZ
candidate satisfies the selection, the candidate with the highest value of a discriminant to distinguish signal and background processes, which will be defined in Section
0.5
, is selected.

0.5
Observables

A new resonance would appear as a peak in the distribution of the reconstructed four-lepton mass
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
. The mass resolution at 125
\GeV
is around 1
\GeV
, reaching about 30, 60, and 90
\GeV
in the
4\Pe
,
2\Pe 2\PGm
, and
4\PGm
final states at a mass of 3
\TeV
, respectively.

This is mainly because the electron energy resolution worsens at high energies due to shower containment and ECAL calibration limitations, while the muon momentum resolution degrades more strongly due to reduced track curvature and increased sensitivity to alignment effects. The left panel of Fig.
1
shows the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
distributions for various signal hypotheses, the background processes, and the observed data. All signal and background distributions are estimated from MC simulation, except the background involving jets misidentified as leptons, labelled
\PZ
+ jets, which is estimated from data using the method described in Section
0.8.2
. The remaining backgrounds include triboson production (
\PW\PZ\PZ,\PW\PW\PZ
) and
\PQt\PAQt
associated with vector bosons (
\PQt\PAQt\PZ
,
\PQt\PAQt\PZ\PZ
,
\PQt\PAQt\PW\PW
), which are collectively referred to as “other” backgrounds.

Signal processes with different masses and produced via different production mechanisms are shown, normalized to the Higgs boson production cross section expected in the SM. The various signal processes present a narrow peak for low masses, and can be prominent above backgrounds if they have sufficiently large production cross sections. The width of the distribution increases at higher resonance masses, as expected for SM-like Higgs bosons.

In addition to
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
, a kinematic discriminant
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
is considered in the analysis. This observable is computed using MELA.

It is defined as a function of the likelihood ratio:

D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}=\left[1+\frac{\mathcal{P}^{\PQq\PAQq}_{\text{bkg}}\left(\vec{\Omega}^{\PH\to 4\Pell}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}\right)}{\mathcal{P}^{\Pg\Pg}_{\text{sig}}\left(\vec{\Omega}^{\PH\to 4\Pell}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}\right)}\right]^{-1}.

(2)

In this equation,
\mathcal{P}^{\PQq\PAQq}_{\text{bkg}}
corresponds to the likelihood of the event to have originated from
\PQq\PAQq\to 4\Pell
, and is calculated using the MEs from
\MCFM
;
\mathcal{P}^{\Pg\Pg}_{\text{sig}}
is calculated with the MEs from
JHUGen
and defines the likelihood of the event to have originated from the production of a 125
\GeV
Higgs boson via ggF; and
\vec{\Omega}^{\PH\to 4\Pell}
refers to the angular phase space of the
4\Pell
system plus the masses of the two
\PZ
bosons. As
m_{4\Pell}
is not used in the computation, and the angular correlations are similar for the Higgs boson decays and the spin-0 X resonance decays,
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
is shown to remain applicable for high mass signal processes.

0.6
Event categorization

To improve the sensitivity of the analysis to the VBF production process, VBF-enriched regions are defined using the topology of the hadronic jets. The criteria used to categorize events are the same as those used in Ref.
[
38
]
. Two mutually exclusive categories are defined: the VBF-tagged one, containing events satisfying the criteria described below, and the untagged one, containing the remaining events, which are primarily produced via ggF.

The VBF tagging criteria include the following requirements, as a VBF signal event typically contains one or two reconstructed energetic jets, which are not likely to be
\PQb
jets:

•

There is no additional electron or muon that satisfies the lepton selection discussed in Section
0.4
in the event.

•

There must be at least two jets that satisfy the jet selection described in Section
0.4
.

•

If there are two or three jets that satisfy the jet selection requirements, the number of
\PQb
-tagged jets must be
\leq 1
; otherwise, there must be no
\PQb
-tagged jets in the event. This condition is a compromise between rejecting the
\PQt\PAQt
background which usually contains
\PQb
jets and retaining VBF signals that contain light jets mistagged as
\PQb
jets.

Requirements are also placed on the discriminant
D_{\text{2jet}}^{\text{VBF}}
, computed with MELA and defined as:

D_{\text{2jet}}^{\text{VBF}}=\left[1+\frac{\mathcal{P}_{\text{HJJ}}\left(\vec{\Omega}^{\PH+JJ}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}\right)}{\mathcal{P}_{\text{VBF}}\left(\vec{\Omega}^{\PH+JJ}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}\right)}\right]^{-1},

(3)

where
\mathcal{P}_{\text{HJJ}}
is the probability for the event topology to be similar to that of an SM Higgs boson produced via ggF in association with two jets, and
\mathcal{P}_{\text{VBF}}
is the probability for the event topology to correspond to that of an SM Higgs boson produced via VBF. The angular phase space of the
4\Pell
system and the two most energetic jets is denoted as
\vec{\Omega}^{\PH+JJ}
. These probabilities are calculated using
JHUGen
MEs. Similar to
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
,
D_{\text{2jet}}^{\text{VBF}}
is defined with respect to Higgs boson production in the SM, but it can also be used to discriminate VBF signals at any mass.

Figure
2
shows the
D_{\text{2jet}}^{\text{VBF}}

0.7
Signal modeling

To test a large number of signal hypotheses without simulating all of them, a parametric approach is used to model the different signals. The approach introduced in Ref.
[
38
]
is followed.

The signal model is constructed from parameterized signal templates obtained using MC simulation.
Given the mass
m_{\PX}
and decay width
\Gamma_{\PX}
of the resonance, the probability density function (pdf) of the signal process is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{\text{sig}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}},D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{\PX},\Gamma_{\PX})=\{[\mathcal{M}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}|m_{\PX},\Gamma_{\PX})\epsilon(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})]\otimes\mathcal{R}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})\}\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}).

(4)

We have:

•

The
\otimes
denotes a convolution.

•

The
\mathcal{M}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}|m_{\PX},\Gamma_{\PX})
describes the theoretical lineshape of the signal, with
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
being the
4\Pell
invariant mass at generator level.

•

The
\epsilon(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})
is the overall signal efficiency, which accounts for the probability of the signal to fall within the detector acceptance, as well as the reconstruction, selection, and categorization efficiencies.

•

The
\mathcal{R}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})
is the signal resolution function, which converts the distribution from generator level to detector level, to account for the finite energy resolution of the detector.

•

The
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
is the template of
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
in bins of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
, extending the formula to two dimensions.

The theoretical lineshape of the signal can be described as the differential production cross section as a function of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
, and is based on Refs.
[
70
,
61
]
. It is parameterized as

\mathcal{M}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}|m_{\PX},\Gamma_{\PX})=\sigma(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})\frac{2m_{\PX}m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}\Gamma_{\PX}}{[(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})^{2}-m_{\PX}^{2}]^{2}+m_{\PX}^{2}\Gamma_{\PX}^{2}}.

(5)

The spin-0 propagator is described by the Breit–Wigner function; the other factors contributing to the production cross section, including the proton PDFs, the MEs, and the kinematic phase space, are incorporated in the cross section
\sigma(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})
, which is calculated with the
\MCFM
generator
[
17
,
18
,
19
]
. Such a parameterization has been confirmed to give the same lineshape as that directly generated by
\MCFM

[
38
]
.

The signal efficiency
\epsilon(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})
is defined as the number of events within the detector acceptance, satisfying the online and offline selections, and falling in a certain category, divided by the total number of generated events. The efficiency is computed for each final state, data-taking period, and production mechanism. It is estimated from MC simulation as a function of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
, and parameterized with an empirical function that is the product of a polynomial function and an error function. The
\chi^{2}
divided by the number of degrees of freedom of the fits is in the range 0.6–1.3.
Figure
3
shows the signal efficiency evaluated from simulation for the 2018 data-taking period. In general, the efficiency is higher in the final states with muons. Signal processes have an overall efficiency of 14–80%. More than 90% of ggF signal events are categorized into the untagged category.
For VBF signal events, as the mass grows, the fraction of VBF-tagged events decreases. This is because the two VBF jets tend to be less energetic at higher masses, as most of the energy of the partons is needed to produce the heavy resonance. The signal efficiencies are similar for the other data-taking periods.

The signal resolution
\mathcal{R}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})
is described by a double Crystal Ball (DCB) function of
\Delta m_{4\Pell}=m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}-m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
defined as

\text{DCB}(\Delta m_{4\Pell}|\mu_{\text{res}},\sigma_{\text{res}},\alpha_{1},n_{1},\alpha_{2},n_{2})=\begin{cases}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{\abs{\alpha_{1}}}\right)^{n_{1}}\re^{-\frac{\alpha_{1}^{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{n_{1}}{\abs{\alpha_{1}}}-\abs{\alpha_{1}}-\frac{\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}}}{\sigma_{\text{res}}}\right)^{-n_{1}},\ \frac{\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}}}{\sigma_{\text{res}}}\leq-\alpha_{1}&\\[8.0pt]
\re^{-\frac{(\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}})^{2}}{2\sigma_{\text{res}}^{2}}},\ -\alpha_{1}<\frac{\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}}}{\sigma_{\text{res}}}<\alpha_{2}&\\[8.0pt]
\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\abs{\alpha_{2}}}\right)^{n_{2}}\re^{-\frac{\alpha_{2}^{2}}{2}}\left(\frac{n_{2}}{\abs{\alpha_{2}}}-\abs{\alpha_{2}}+\frac{\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}}}{\sigma_{\text{res}}}\right)^{-n_{2}},\ \frac{\Delta m_{4\Pell}-\mu_{\text{res}}}{\sigma_{\text{res}}}\geq\alpha_{2}&\end{cases}

(6)

with all parameters
\mu_{\text{res}},\sigma_{\text{res}},\alpha_{1},n_{1},\alpha_{2},n_{2}
dependent on
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
.

To compute the dependence on
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
, all signal samples are merged with equal normalization and divided into different
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
bins. The distribution of
\Delta m_{4\Pell}
is then extracted and fitted with the DCB function to obtain the values of the six function parameters. The dependence of each parameter
p
on
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
is fitted with the following function:

f_{p}(x)=a_{p,0}+a_{p,0.5}x^{0.5}+a_{p,1}x+a_{p,1.5}x^{1.5}+a_{p,2}x^{2}+a_{p,2.5}x^{2.5},

(7)

where
x=m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
,
p=\mu_{\text{res}},\sigma_{\text{res}},\alpha_{1},n_{1},\alpha_{2},n_{2}
. Several functional forms were studied, including polynomial functions and functions with the same form but a different order. Among these functions,
f_{p}
was found to have the best fit performance with stable convergence and without evident overfitting effects. A simultaneous fit is then performed over all
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
bins, taking the
a_{p,i}
as the parameters to be fitted. The method is applied individually for each production mechanism, in each final state, and for each data-taking period.

Following the above steps, the parametric model is built as a function of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
. Examples are shown in Fig.

, with various mass and width assumptions, for the two considered signal production mechanisms. The integral of each shape is normalized to 1. For
\Gamma_{\PX}=4\MeV
,
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
is mostly distributed around
m_{\PX}
; when the width is large, a large fraction of off-shell production is expected, especially for the signals with
m_{\PX}=130\GeV
and 3000
\GeV
.

To build the 2D pdf, the conditional pdf
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
is obtained from the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
versus
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}

bin, the distribution is normalized to unity. The distributions are similar for the two production mechanisms, since

only depends on the kinematic properties of the

The signal model is validated by comparing it with the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
distribution extracted from the MC simulation. In general, they are compatible within statistical fluctuations, except in the tails of the

), and

, with which the simulation is performed. This is taken into account by adding an additional systematic uncertainty in the signal shapes.

0.8
Background modeling

0.8.1
Irreducible backgrounds

The dominant background contribution comes from the
\PQq\PAQq\PZ\PZ
process, with the next-largest background arising from
\Pg\Pg\PZ\PZ
production. Another irreducible background is VBF
\PZ\PZ
. Other backgrounds, including triboson production (
\PW\PZ\PZ,\PW\PW\PZ
) and
\PQt\PAQt
plus vector boson production (
\PQt\PAQt\PZ
,
\PQt\PAQt\PZ\PZ
,
\PQt\PAQt\PW\PW
), are also taken into account. All these backgrounds are estimated from simulation.

The pdfs for different backgrounds are built with different methods. For
\PQq\PAQq\PZ\PZ
and other backgrounds, the pdf is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{\text{bkg}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}},D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}})=\mathcal{P}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}),

(8)

where the one-dimensional pdfs
\mathcal{P}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
are empirical functions obtained from the simulated
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
distributions, and the conditional pdfs
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
are obtained from the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
versus
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
distributions in simulation.

For
\Pg\Pg\PZ\PZ
and VBF
\PZ\PZ
, the one-dimensional pdf is built using
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
(instead of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
), which will be used to deduce interferences at the generator level. The pdf is convolved with the energy resolution function introduced in Section
0.7
to build the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
pdf. It is then multiplied by the conditional pdf
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
extracted from MC simulation.

The production of a 125
\GeV
Higgs boson is also an irreducible background to this analysis; this process is modeled using the same approach as for the signals, by setting
m_{\PX}=125\GeV
and
\Gamma_{\PX}=4.1\MeV
in Eq. (
5
).

The expected yields of all the backgrounds described above are estimated from simulation.

0.8.2
Reducible backgrounds

The reducible backgrounds involve at least one selected lepton originating from the misidentification of a light jet, or from nonprompt decays of mesons or heavy-flavor jets. They are dominated by the Drell–Yan process, and also arise from other processes including
\PQt\PAQt
,
\PW\PZ
,
\PW\PW
, and QCD multijet events. These background processes are labelled
\PZ
+ jets.
The
\PZ
+ jets contribution is estimated from dedicated control regions in data, using the misidentification rate method of Refs.
[
44
,
46
]
. The
\PZ
+ jets yield in the signal region is evaluated by multiplying the yields extracted in the control regions by the estimated lepton misidentification rates, denoted as
f_{\Pe}
and
f_{\PGm}
. These rates are defined as the fraction of leptons satisfying all the lepton selection criteria in Section
0.4
except the identification and isolation requirements, to that of leptons also satisfying the identification and isolation requirements. The values of
f_{\Pe}
and
f_{\PGm}
are measured in another control region containing events with one
\PZ
candidate composed of two leptons satisfying all the selection criteria, plus exactly one additional lepton (
\Pe
or
\PGm
) without the identification and isolation requirements. This region is denoted as
\PZ+\Pell
. The misidentification rates are measured as functions of
\pt^{\Pell}
, separately for the barrel and endcap regions.

Two methods are used to estimate the
\PZ
+ jets contribution: the opposite-sign (OS) and same-sign (SS) methods.
In the OS method, the
\PZ
candidate in the
\PZ+\Pell
region is required to have
\abs{m_{\PZ}-m_{\PZ}^{\text{nom}}\ \text{GeV}}<7\GeV
to improve purity; the control region used to estimate the event yield requires one
\PZ
candidate satisfying all the selection criteria, together with two additional OS leptons, at least one of which fails the identification and isolation requirements.
In the SS method, the mass window considered in the
\PZ+\Pell
region is not constrained, but a dedicated approach is used to estimate the contribution of photon conversions; the control region used to estimate the event yield is defined by one
\PZ
candidate satisfying all the selections, together with two additional SS leptons, both without the identification and isolation requirements.
The estimated yields from the two methods are compatible within uncertainties; the final yield estimate is taken as the weighted average between the two estimates, for each final state and data-taking period.
The differential distributions for the variables used in the analysis are obtained from the SS method, because the OS method introduces much larger statistical fluctuations.

The pdf for the
\PZ
+ jets background is built similarly to Eq. (
8
), where
\mathcal{P}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
is the empirical function built from the Landau distribution
[
71
]
, fitted to the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
distribution from the SS method. The conditional probability
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
is obtained from the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
versus
D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}
distribution from the SS method.

0.9
Interference modeling

In this analysis, the broad-width hypothesis is considered in addition to the narrow-width assumption (NWA). Therefore, the interference of the signal with other processes needs to be considered, as it can be significant when the signal width is large.
The modeling of the interference follows the approach used in Ref.
[
38
]
.
For the ggF (VBF) production mode, three possible interferences among the ggF (VBF) signal, ggF (VBF) 125
\GeV
Higgs boson production, and the
\Pg\Pg\PZ\PZ
(VBF
\PZ\PZ
) background are considered.
The
\Pg\Pg\PZ\PZ
and VBF
\PZ\PZ
backgrounds will be referred to as nonresonant backgrounds in what follows. Interferences are added to the signal and background models in the final statistical analysis. In particular, the interference between the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson production and nonresonant backgrounds is treated as a background since it is not related to signal processes. The pdf of each source of interference is built as

\mathcal{P}_{\text{int}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}},D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}})=\\
[\mathcal{P}_{\text{int}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}|\mathcal{P}_{\text{sig}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}),\mathcal{P}_{\text{bkg}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}),\text{ME phases})\otimes\mathcal{R}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}})]\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}).

(9)

The interference lineshape
\mathcal{P}_{\text{int}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}|\mathcal{P}_{\text{sig}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}),\mathcal{P}_{\text{bkg}}(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}),\text{ME phases})
contains contributions from both the amplitudes and phases of the two interfering processes. The amplitudes are extracted from the lineshapes of the signals, SM Higgs boson production, and nonresonant backgrounds. The phases of the scalar propagators (signals and 125
\GeV
Higgs boson) are computed from the kinematical properties, whereas the phases in other parts of the MEs are derived from the corresponding generators.

The resolution functions are the same as those for the signals introduced in Section
0.7
, which are universal for any process with four genuine leptons.

The backgrounds are normalized assuming their cross sections in the SM, signals are normalized such that their cross sections are
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)=0.1\unit{pb}
, and the interference contributions are normalized accordingly. The interference between the 125

m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}>150\GeV
, as shown in Fig.

The conditional pdf
\mathcal{P}(D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}}|m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}})
for the interference terms is obtained from the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
versus

distribution extracted from MC samples reweighted by MELA variables to simulate the interference processes. By weighting simulated samples of off-shell Higgs boson production and the signal samples with the variables computed by MELA, the simulation of the interference among the signal process, the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson, and the nonresonant backgrounds is obtained.

The interference model is validated by comparing the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
shape from the interference model to the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
distribution obtained from MC simulation. Some discrepancies are observed for the interference between signals and nonresonant backgrounds, as well as between the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson and nonresonant backgrounds, stemming from the possible imperfect estimation of the phases used in Eq. (

Correction factors are computed as the ratios between the distribution from simulation and the interference model. These correction factors range from 1 to 1.3 and are applied to the interference model. Additional systematic uncertainties are estimated to cover the residual model-simulation difference, ranging from 9.5% to 11% for ggF production and 13% to 18% for VBF production.

0.10
Systematic uncertainties

In this analysis, more than 90% of the total uncertainty arises from statistical fluctuations in the data. However, it is still important to have a proper estimation and treatment of systematic uncertainties.

Systematic uncertainties are divided into experimental and theoretical uncertainties. Experimental uncertainties arise from imperfect knowledge of the detector and experimental measurements; theoretical uncertainties originate from the limited precision of the theoretical computations. They can affect the normalization or the shapes of each signal, background, and interference process.

0.10.1
Experimental uncertainties

The integrated luminosities are measured individually in the 2016, 2017, and 2018 data-taking periods
[
45
,
35
,
39
]
. The uncertainties range from 1% to 3%, and are partially correlated among the different years, leading to an overall 1.6% uncertainty. They affect the normalization of all processes estimated from simulation,
\ie
, all except
\PZ
+ jets.

The lepton efficiency uncertainties arise from all the selection steps, and include trigger, reconstruction, and selection efficiencies. These uncertainties are dominated by the uncertainties in the lepton identification and isolation efficiency, and affect the yields of all the processes estimated from MC simulation. The lepton efficiency is measured with a tag-and-probe method, in both observed data and simulation. The same method as in Ref.
[
46
]
is used to extract the uncertainties. Electrons and muons are treated as uncorrelated, because the selection of these objects is different. For electrons, the uncertainty ranges from 3% to 11% in the
4\Pe
final state, and from 2% to 6% in the
2\Pe 2\PGm
final state. For muons, the uncertainty ranges from 0.2% to 2% in the
4\PGm
final state, and from 0.2% to 1.4% in the
2\Pe 2\PGm
final state.

The uncertainties are computed individually for each process. For the ggF and VBF signals, the uncertainty values depend on the resonance masses. A parametrization is therefore built as a function of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
, and used to compute the uncertainty for any signal mass.

The lepton energy scale and resolution impact the shapes of processes convolved with the energy resolution function, including signal processes, interference contributions, and interfering backgrounds. Uncertainties for electrons and muons are considered uncorrelated. The uncertainties are estimated by comparing the
\PZ\to\Pell\Pell
invariant mass distributions in the simulation with those from the observed data. The energy scale uncertainty is 0.15% (0.03%) for electrons (muons), and it affects the final states containing electrons (muons) by shifting the
\mu_{\text{res}}
in Eq.
6
. The energy resolution uncertainty is 10% (3%) for electrons (muons), and it affects the final states containing electrons (muons) by modifying the
\sigma_{\text{res}}
in Eq.
6
. The quoted values are taken from Ref.
[
52
]
.

The jet energy scale and resolution do not affect the total signal or background efficiency, but affect the categorization, since the VBF tagging criteria include jet selections. These uncertainties affect all processes except
\PZ
+ jets. Jet energy scale uncertainties originate from various sources, and they are partially correlated among data-taking periods, while the uncertainty in the jet energy resolution is considered uncorrelated among all final states and data-taking periods. In general, these uncertainties amount to around 0.1% for the untagged category and 1% to 4% for the VBF-tagged category.

The jet
\PQb
tagging efficiency uncertainties also affect the VBF categorization efficiency, similarly to the jet energy scale and resolution. To estimate them, the
\PQb
tagging efficiencies are varied by their uncertainties, the number of jets that satisfy the
\PQb
tagging requirements is recounted, and the shifted categorization is performed. These uncertainties are estimated to be smaller than 0.1%.

Simulated samples of events are corrected for the timing shift of the L1 trigger in 2016, and for mismodeling of PU distributions. The corresponding uncertainties affect the normalization of all processes, except
\PZ
+ jets. These uncertainties are typically smaller than 1%, and are treated as uncorrelated between data-taking periods.

For the signal processes, uncertainties related to jets, PU, and the L1 timing shift effect are averaged over all mass points, since no mass dependence is observed.

The
\PZ
+ jets estimation uncertainties take into account the statistical fluctuations in control regions. Owing to differences in the composition between the control regions where the misidentification rates are computed and where they are applied, an additional systematic uncertainty is considered. The differences are estimated using simulation, and lead to an uncertainty of around 30% on the yield of
\PZ
+ jets in the SR. The uncertainty is treated as uncorrelated among final states and data-taking periods.

0.10.2
Theoretical uncertainties

Theoretical uncertainties are correlated across all data-taking periods.

The uncertainty in the branching fraction of
\PH\to\PZ\PZ\to 4\Pell
affects the normalization of the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson production processes. The uncertainty amounts to around 2%
[
72
]
.

The uncertainty in the renormalization and factorization scales (
\mu_{\text{R}},\mu_{\text{F}}
) is determined by simultaneously varying these two scales between 0.5 and 2 times their nominal value. This uncertainty affects all processes except the
\PZ
+ jets background, and it ranges from 1% to 15% depending on the process, final state, and category. For the
\PQq\PQq\PZ\PZ
background, this uncertainty affects the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
shape in addition to the yield.

The uncertainty in the PDFs follows the PDF4LHC recommendations, taking the root-mean-square of variations with different replicas of the default NNPDF set
[
15
]
. The uncertainty in the strong coupling constant
\alpS
is estimated by varying its value by 1 standard deviation (SD). The uncertainties range from 0.1% to 7%. For the
\PQq\PQq\PZ\PZ
background, the uncertainty in the PDFs affects the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
shape, just as for the renormalization and factorization scales.

For the signal processes, the uncertainties in the renormalization and factorization scales, PDFs, and
\alpS
are parameterized as a function of
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{gen}}
. For a given signal process, its lineshape is weighted by the parameterized function, and the uncertainty is computed as the difference in normalization between the weighted and nominal lineshapes.

The initial-state radiation (ISR) and FSR scales are used by
\PYTHIA

[
80
]
to model the underlying event, as well as the fragmentation and hadronization of partons. They affect all simulated processes. The uncertainties are estimated by varying the ISR and FSR scales from 4 to 0.25 times the nominal values. The uncertainty ranges from 0.4% to 10%. For the signal processes, the values are averaged over all mass points.

An uncertainty is added to the signal and interference processes to account for potential differences in the lineshapes obtained from different generators. It ranges from 2% to 10%.
Additionally, the uncertainties accounting for the interference term mismodeling introduced in Section
0.9
are taken into account. They range from 9.5% to 11% for ggF production, and from 13% to 18% for VBF production.

A 10% uncertainty in the determination of the
K
factor used for the
\Pg\Pg\to\PZ\PZ
prediction is applied.
For the
\PQq\PAQq\to\PZ\PZ
background, the uncertainties affect the
m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}}
shape, ranging from 0.1% at low mass to 30% at high mass.

All systematic uncertainties are treated as nuisance parameters in the fit. They are summarized in Table

.

Results

To build the complete statistical model, the signal strength is defined as

r=\frac{\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)}{\sigma_{\text{model}}(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)},

(10)

where
\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)
refers to the production cross section of the resonance multiplied by the branching fraction for
\PX\to\PZ\PZ
;
\sigma_{\text{model}}(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)
is the reference value set in the statistical model. The fraction of the VBF signal is defined as the VBF production cross section divided by the total production cross section:

f_{\text{VBF}}=\frac{\sigma(\PQq\PQq\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)}{\sigma(\Pp\Pp\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)}=\frac{\sigma(\PQq\PQq\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)}{\sigma(\Pg\Pg\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)+\sigma(\PQq\PQq\to\PX\to\PZ\PZ)},

(11)

such that the signal strengths for ggF production and VBF production are

r_{\text{ggF}}=r(1-f_{\text{VBF}}),\ r_{\text{VBF}}=rf_{\text{VBF}}.

(12)

By setting
f_{\text{VBF}}=0
(1), the pure ggF (VBF) production mechanism hypothesis is tested.

The sum of the pdf of each process multiplied by its predicted yield is written as:

\mathcal{P}(\vec{x}|r,\vec{\theta})=\sum_{\text{s}=\text{ggF},\text{VBF}}[r_{\text{s}}S_{\text{s}}(\vec{\theta})\mathcal{P}_{\text{s}}(\vec{x}|\vec{\theta})+\sqrt{r_{\text{s}}}I_{\text{s}}(\vec{\theta})\mathcal{P}_{\text{s,int}}(\vec{x}|\vec{\theta})]+\sum_{\text{b}}B_{\text{b}}(\vec{\theta})\mathcal{P}_{\text{b}}(\vec{x}|\vec{\theta}).

(13)

The first sum considers the signal and interference processes, and the second sum considers all backgrounds and the 125
\GeV
Higgs boson-background interference. The discriminating variables are
\vec{x}=(m_{4\Pell}^{\text{reco}},D_{\text{bkg}}^{\text{kin}})
. The expected yields for the signal, interference, and background processes are given by
S_{\text{s}}
,
I_{\text{s}}
, and
B_{\text{b}}
, respectively.
The nuisance parameters
\vec{\theta}
are used to take into account systematic uncertainties. Both the integrated yield and the differential distribution of each process can depend on the nuisance parameters.

Summary

A search for a spin-0 resonance decaying to a pair of
\PZ
bosons in the four-lepton final state, where the leptons are muons or electrons, is performed at the CMS experiment. The data set used was collected in 2016–2018 and corresponds to an integrated luminosity of 138
\fbinv
. The searched-for resonance can be produced via gluon fusion or vector boson fusion. The mass of the sought resonance is scanned over a range from 130
\GeV
to 3
\TeV
, and different decay width assumptions are tested.

No significant excess over the standard model background expectation is observed. The largest fluctuation is seen at a mass of 137.8
\GeV
under the narrow-width assumption, reaching a global significance of 1.8 standard deviations. Upper limits at 95% confidence level on the production cross section multiplied by the decay branching fraction of
\PX\to\PZ\PZ
are set for various masses, decay widths, and production mechanisms. The exclusion limits range from 0.05–0.1
\unit
pb in the low-mass region to 0.005
\unit
pb in the high-mass region.
