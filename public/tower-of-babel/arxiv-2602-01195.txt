Graph-null sets
M. Laczkovich, A. Máthé

Abstract
We say that a plane set A A is graph-null, if there is a function g : [ 0 , 1 ] → ℝ g\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}} such that λ 2 ​ ( A + graph ​ g ) = 0 \lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,g)=0 . A plane set A A has the translational Kakeya property if, for every translated copy A ′ A^{\prime} of A A and for every ε > 0 \varepsilon>0 , there is a finite sequence of vertical and horizontal translations bringing A A to A ′ A^{\prime} such that the area touched during the horizontal translations is less than ε \varepsilon . These properties are equivalent if A A is compact. We show that the graph of every absolutely continuous function is graph-null. Also, the graph of a typical continuous function is graph-null. Therefore, there are nowhere differentiable continuous functions whose graphs are graph-null. Still, we show that there exists a continuous function whose graph is not graph-null.

Introduction and main results

We say that a set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
has the

Kakeya property
(shortly property (K)), if there exists a set
A^{\prime}\neq A

such that
A
can be continuously moved to
A^{\prime}
within a set of
arbitrarily small area. Clearly, every segment and every circular arc has
property (K). A set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
is said to have the
strong Kakeya
property
(shortly property
{\rm(K^{s})}
) if, whenever
A^{\prime}
is an arbitrary
congruent copy of
A
, then
A
can be continuously moved to
A^{\prime}
within a set
of arbitrarily small area. It is well known that line segments have
property
{\rm(K^{s})}
; this follows from Besicovitch’ solution of
the classical Kakeya problem
[
1
]
,
[
5
, Theorem 7.6]
. Some circular
arcs also have property
{\rm(K^{s})}
. For short enough arcs this was proved
in
[
6
]
, and for circular arcs shorter than the half circle in
[
3
]
.
It is not known whether or not every proper circular arc has property

{\rm(K^{s})}
. On the other hand, if a continuum has property (K), then it
must be a segment or a circular arc
[
4
]
.
In this note we investigate a related notion.

Definition 1.1
.

A plane set
A
has the
translational Kakeya property

{\rm(K^{t})}

if, for every translated copy
A^{\prime}
of
A
and for every

\varepsilon>0
, there is a finite sequence of vertical and horizontal translations
bringing
A
to
A^{\prime}
such that the area touched during the
horizontal
translations is less than
\varepsilon
.

The most interesting cases are the continuous curves.
We will see that property
{\rm(K^{t})}
is shared by the graphs of most
continuous functions and by most absolutely continuous curves.
As paradoxical as it may seem, every circle and every polygon without
vertical sides has property
{\rm(K^{t})}
(see Remark
1.7
.1).

First we give an equivalent formulation of property
{\rm(K^{t})}
.
We denote the
d
dimensional Lebesgue outer measure by
\lambda_{d}
.
When a set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
is translated by the vector
(a,0)
, the set of points
touched is
A+([0,a]\times\{0\})
. From this observation it is clear
that
a set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
has property
{\rm(K^{t})}
if and only if, for
every
\varepsilon>0
there is a simple function
h\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)<\varepsilon
.

(A function
h\colon[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is
simple
, if there is a partition

a=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{k}=b
such that
h
is constant in
(x_{i-1},x_{i})

for every
i=1,\ldots,k
.)

If the projection of a set
A
onto the
y
axis is of measure zero, then

A
has property
{\rm(K^{t})}
. Indeed, translating
A

horizontally, the area touched has two dimensional measure zero.

In the other direction, let
E_{A}
denote the set of numbers
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
such
that
((0,\varepsilon)\times\{y\})\cap A\neq\emptyset
for every
\varepsilon>0
. If

A
has property
{\rm(K^{t})}
, then
E_{A}
must be of measure zero. Indeed,
if
h\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is a simple function, and
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{k}=1

is a partition such that
h(x)=c_{i}
if
(x_{i-1}<x<x_{i})
for every
i=1,\ldots,k
,
then
A+{\rm graph}\,h
contains the set
(x_{i-1},x_{i})\times(E_{A}+c_{i})
for every

i=1,\ldots,k
, and thus

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)\geq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{1}(E_{A})\cdot(x_{i}-x_{i-1})=\lambda_{1}(E_{A}).

We also consider the following variant:

Definition 1.2
.

A plane set
A
is
graph-null,
if there is a function

g\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,g)=0
.

If the projection of the set
A
onto a nonhorizontal line
e
is of linear
measure zero, then
A
is graph-null. Indeed, in this case
\lambda_{2}(A+f)=0

for every line
f
perpendicular to
e
.

For example, the set
B=\{(x,y)\colon y-x\in{\mathbb{Q}}\}
is graph-null. On the
other hand,
B
does not have property
{\rm(K^{t})}
, since
((0,\varepsilon)\times\{y\})\cap B\neq\emptyset
for every
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in{\mathbb{R}}
. Therefore, the
graph-null property does not imply
{\rm(K^{t})}
. In Remark
1.7
.2 we
will see that the graph-null property does not imply
{\rm(K^{t})}
even
for the graphs of functions that are continuous everywhere except one point.
One can also show that the
{\rm(K^{t})}
property does not imply the
graph-null property even for
G_{\delta}
sets (see
[
8
]
). Therefore,

{\rm(K^{t})}
and the graph-null property are independent.

We show, however, that for compact sets property
{\rm(K^{t})}

is equivalent to the graph-null property, moreover, it is equivalent to an
even more restrictive property defined as follows.

Let
K([0,1]^{2})
denote the family of nonempty compact subsets of
[0,1]^{2}
.
It is well known that the Hausdorff metric

d(K_{1},K_{2})=\max\{{\rm dist}\,(x,K_{1}),{\rm dist}\,(y,K_{2})\colon x\in K_{2},\ y\in K_{1}\}

(K_{1},K_{2}\in K([0,1]^{2}))
makes
K([0,1]^{2})
a compact metric space

[
7
, Theorem 4.26, p. 26]
. Let
{\cal K}=\{K\in K([0,1]^{2})\colon{\rm pr}_{x}K=[0,1]\}
, where
{\rm pr}_{x}K
denotes the projection of
K
onto
the
x
axis. It is easy to check that
{\cal K}
is a closed, hence compact subset
of
K([0,1]^{2})
.

Definition 1.3
.

We say that a set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
is
typically graph-null
, if

\lambda_{2}(A+K)=0
holds for every
K
belonging to a comeager subset of
{\cal K}
.

Since
{\rm pr}_{x}\,K=[0,1]
for every
K\in{\cal K}
, it follows that every element of
{\cal K}

contains the graph of a function defined on
[0,1]
. Therefore, every typically
graph-null set is graph-null.

Theorem 1.4
.

For every compact set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
, the following are equivalent.

(i)

A
has property
{\rm K}^{t}
.

(ii)

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is a function
g\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,g)<\varepsilon
.

(iii)

A
is graph-null.

(iv)

A
is typically graph-null.

We will prove Theorem
1.4
in Section
2
.
As we mentioned already, the graphs of most continuous functions share
the properties listed in Theorem
1.4
. First we show that smooth functions
have this property.

Theorem 1.5
.

If
f\colon[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuously differentiable, then

{\rm graph}\,f
is graph-null.

Proof.
The statement is a consequence of the following theorem of E. Sawyer

[
11
]
:
there exists a function
\phi\colon{\mathbb{R}}\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
whenever
f\colon[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is continuously differentiable, then the
range of
\phi|_{[a,b]}-f
is of measure zero.

The function
\phi
constructed by Sawyer is everywhere continuous except at
the points of a countable set
C
. It follows that
{\rm graph}\,\phi
is a Borel
subset of
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
.

Let
f\colon[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be continuously differentiable. Since

{\rm graph}\,f
is compact,
({\rm graph}\,f)\times({\rm graph}\,\phi)

is a Borel subset of the product space
{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}\times{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
. Then the set

E=({\rm graph}\,f)+({\rm graph}\,\phi)
, being a continuous image of
({\rm graph}\,f)\times({\rm graph}\,\phi)
is analytic, hence Lebesgue measurable. We have

E={\rm graph}\,f+{\rm graph}\,\phi=\{(x_{1}+x_{2},f(x_{1})+\phi(x_{2}))\colon x_{1}\in[a,b],\ x_{2}\in{\mathbb{R}}\}.

Then, for every
c\in{\mathbb{R}}
we have

E_{c}=\{y\colon(c,y)\in E\}=\{f(c-x)+\phi(x)\colon c-x\in[a,b]\},

which is the range of the function
\phi|_{[c-b,c-a]}-g
, where
g(x)=-f(c-x)

(x\in[c-b,c-a])
. Since
g
is continuously differentiable, it follows
from Sawyer’s theorem that
\lambda_{1}(E_{c})=0
for every
c
. Then, by Fubini’s
theorem, we have
\lambda_{2}(E)=0
, proving that
{\rm graph}\,f
is
graph-null.
\square

Corollary 1.6
.

If a set
A\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
can be covered by countably many graphs of
continuously differentiable functions, then
A
is graph-null.

Indeed, we have
\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,\phi)=0
, where
\phi
is Sawyer’s function.

Remarks 1.7
.

1.
It is clear that every circle can be covered by countable many graphs of
continuously differentiable functions. Therefore,
every circle is
graph-null and, consequently, has property
{\rm K^{t}}
(by Theorem
1.4
).
The same is true for polygons without vertical sides.

2. Let
f(x)=\sin(1/x)
if
x\in(0,1]
, and let
f(0)=0
. Then
{\rm graph}\,f

is graph-null, as it can be covered by countable many graphs of continuously
differentiable functions. On the other hand,
{\rm graph}\,f
does not have
property
{\rm K}^{t}
, since
{\rm graph}\,f
intersects
(0,\varepsilon)\times\{y\}

for every
\varepsilon>0
and
y\in[-1,1]
. This example shows that the graph-null
property does not imply
{\rm K}^{t}
even for graphs of functions that are
continuous everywhere except one point.

3. It is clear that if a set
A
has any of the properties (i)-(iv) listed
in Theorem
1.4
, then every subset of
A
has the same property. Thus we
may ask whether or not the family of sets having any of these properties form
an ideal, or even a
\sigma
-ideal.

The answer is positive in the case of property (iv). The family of
typically graph-null sets is a
\sigma
-ideal, since the intersection of countable
many comeager subsets of
{\cal K}
is also comeager in
{\cal K}
.

The family of sets having property
{\rm K}^{t}
is not a
\sigma
-ideal. Indeed,
let
f
be as in Remark 2 above, and let
f_{n}
be the restriction of
f

to the interval
[1/n,1]
. Then
{\rm graph}\,f_{n}
has property
{\rm K}^{t}
by
Theorem
1.5
for every
n=2,3,\ldots
. On the other hand,

\bigcup_{n=2}^{\infty}{\rm graph}\,f_{n}=({\rm graph}\,f)\setminus\{(0,0)\}
does not have
property
{\rm K}^{t}
.

It is not clear if the family of sets having property
{\rm K}^{t}
is an ideal
or not. It is also open whether the sets having properties (ii) and (iii) of
Theorem
1.4
form ideals.

The following theorem extends Theorem
1.5
to absolutely continuous curves.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
\gamma=(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})\colon[a,b]\to{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
be a curve such that

\gamma_{1}
and
\gamma_{2}
are absolutely continuous functions on
[a,b]
. If

\gamma^{\prime}_{1}(t)\neq 0
for a.e.
t\in[a,b]
, then
\gamma([a,b])
is graph-null.

Corollary 1.9
.

If
f\colon[a,b]\to{\mathbb{R}}
is absolutely continuous, then its graph is
graph-null.

The proof of Theorem
1.8
, provided in Section
3
, does not use
Sawyer’s theorem.

The statement that
the graph of “most continuous functions” is graph-null is made precise
by the following theorem.

Theorem 1.10
.

The set of continuous functions
f\in C[a,b]
such that
{\rm graph}\,f
is
graph-null is comeager in
C[a,b]
.

Proof.
It is easy to see that the set
U_{\varepsilon}=\{(f,K)\in C[a,b]\times{\cal K}\colon\lambda_{2}(({\rm graph}\,f)+K)<\varepsilon\}
is open in
C[a,b]\times{\cal K}
for every

\varepsilon>0
. Since the set of continuously differentiable functions is dense in

C[a,b]
, and every such function is typically graph-null by Theorems
1.4

and
1.5
, it follows that
U_{\varepsilon}
is everywhere dense in
C[a,b]\times{\cal K}
for every
\varepsilon>0
. Therefore, the set

U=\{(f,K)\in C[a,b]\times{\cal K}\colon\lambda_{2}(({\rm graph}\,f)+K)=0\}=\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}U_{1/n}

is a dense
G_{\delta}
set in
C[a,b]\times{\cal K}
. Then, by the Kuratowski-Ulam
theorem
[
7
, Theorem 8.41, p. 53]
, there is a comeager subset
V\subset C[a,b]

such that, for every
f\in V
,
\{K\in{\cal K}\colon\lambda_{2}(({\rm graph}\,f)+K)=0\}

is comeager in
{\cal K}
.
\square

Since a typical continuous function is nowhere differentiable, we obtain that

there are nowhere differentiable continuous functions such that their
graph is graph-null.
We show, however, that the graph-null property, although
is typical among continuous functions, is not shared by all continuous
functions.

Theorem 1.11
.

There exists a continuous function
f\in C[0,1]
such that
{\rm graph}\,f

is not graph-null.

Theorem
1.11
will be proved in Section
4
.

Let
M[a,b]
denote the set of functions that are continuous and increasing
on
[a,b]
. Then
M[a,b]
is a closed subspace of
C[a,b]
and, consequently,
it is a Polish space under the supremum metric.

Theorem 1.12
.

The set of increasing continuous functions
f\in C[a,b]
such that
{\rm graph}\,f

is graph-null is comeager in
C[a,b]
.

The proof is the same as that of Theorem
1.10
. It is known that
a typical element of
M[a,b]
is singular; that is, its derivative is
zero a.e. Therefore, we find that
there are singular, increasing and
continuous functions such that their graph is graph-null.

We conclude this section with the following open question:

Is the graph of every monotonic and continuous function graph-null?

Proof of Theorem
1.4

We denote by
{\rm cl}\,A
the closure of the set
A
.

Lemma 2.1
.

If a bounded set
A
has property
{\rm K}^{t}
, then for every
\varepsilon>0
there
exists a simple function
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,\varepsilon]
such that

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)<\varepsilon
.

Proof.

For
x\in{\mathbb{R}}
and
\varepsilon>0
we define

x\bmod\varepsilon=x-\varepsilon\lfloor x/\varepsilon\rfloor,

so that
(x\bmod\varepsilon)\in[0,\varepsilon)
. Let
\Phi:{\mathbb{R}}^{2}\to{\mathbb{R}}^{2}
be the function
given by

\Phi(x,y)=(x,\,y\bmod\varepsilon).

We show that
\lambda_{2}(\Phi(E))\leq\lambda_{2}(E)
for every
E\subset{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}
. Put

S_{i}=\{(x,y)\colon x\in{\mathbb{R}},\ y\in[i\varepsilon,(i+1)\varepsilon)\}

for every
i\in{\mathbb{Z}}
. Clearly,
\Phi(E)=\bigcup_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}((E\cap S_{i})-(0,i\varepsilon))
. Since the sets
S_{i}
are measurable and disjoint, we have

\lambda_{2}(E)=\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{2}(E\cap S_{i})
, and thus
\lambda_{2}(\Phi(E))\leq\sum_{i\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\lambda_{2}(E\cap S_{i})=\lambda_{2}(E)
.

As
A
is bounded, there is a positive integer
N
such that

A\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times[-N\varepsilon,\,N\varepsilon).

Since
A
has property
{\rm K}^{t}
, there exists a simple function

g\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,g)<\varepsilon/(2N+1)
.
Let
h\colon[0,1]\to[0,\varepsilon)
be the (simple) function given by
h(x)=g(x)\bmod\varepsilon
.

Notice that

A+{\rm graph}\,h\subset{\mathbb{R}}\times[-N\varepsilon,\,(N+1)\varepsilon),

hence
2N+1
many translates of
\Phi(A+{\rm graph}\,h)=\Phi(A+{\rm graph}\,g)
cover
A+{\rm graph}\,h
.
By translation invariance and subadditivity of Lebesgue outer measure, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)

\displaystyle\leq(2N+1)\lambda_{2}(\Phi(A+{\rm graph}\,g))

\displaystyle\leq(2N+1)\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,g)

\displaystyle<(2N+1)\frac{\varepsilon}{2N+1}=\varepsilon.

∎

Lemma 2.2
.

If a bounded set
A
has property
{\rm K}^{t}
, then for every simple function

g\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
and for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists a simple function

h\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
such that
|g-h|<\varepsilon
, and

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)<\varepsilon
.

Proof.
Let
0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{n}=1
be a partition of
[0,1]
such that
g

is constant in the interval
(x_{i-1},x_{i})
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
.
By Lemma
2.1
, there is a simple function
f\colon[0,1]\to[0,\varepsilon]
such
that
\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,f)<\varepsilon/n
. Let
h=g+f
. Then
h
is a simple function,
and
|h-g|<\varepsilon
.

The set
A+{\rm graph}\,h
is covered by the sets
A+({\rm graph}\,h|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})})

(i=1,\ldots,n)
, and by a finite number of translated copies of
A
. Therefore,
we have

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)\leq\sum_{i=1}^{n}\lambda_{2}\left(A+({\rm graph}\,h|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})})\right).

Since
A+({\rm graph}\,h|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})})
is a translated copy of

A+({\rm graph}\,f|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})})\subset A+{\rm graph}\,f
, we have

\lambda_{1}(A+({\rm graph}\,h|_{(x_{i-1},x_{i})}))<\varepsilon/n
for every
i=1,\ldots,n
. Therefore,

\lambda_{2}(A+{\rm graph}\,h)<\varepsilon
.
\square

We say that
K\in{\cal K}
is
m
-simple
(
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
), if there is a partition

0=x_{0}<x_{1}<\ldots<x_{n}=1
and there are closed segments

of

such that

, and

.

.

For every
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
, the family of
m
-simple sets is everywhere dense in

{\cal K}
.

Proof.
Let
m\in{\mathbb{R}}
,

and
\varepsilon>0
be given. We show that there

m
-simple set

such that

. Put
\eta=\varepsilon/\max(1,|m|)
.

Using the fact that

. Moving some points of

slightly if necessary, we may assume that the

, where

,

, and

.
For every

.

, there is an
m

with

such that

k\geq 1
, and suppose that
(10)
k-1
holds for every
j,x,y
satisfying
the conditions stated in the lemma. Since
A_{k-1}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{k-1}}R_{j}^{k-1}
,
we have

A_{k-1}\cap\left(A_{k-1}+(x,y)\right)=\bigcup_{j_{1},j_{2}=1}^{N_{k-1}}\left(R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\cap(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}+(x,y))\right)

and

\begin{split}\big|(R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\cap A_{k})\cap\big((R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}\cap A_{k})&+(x,y)\big)\big|=\\
&\left|\left(R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{1}}}\cap\left(\left(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{2}}}+(x,y)\right)\right|\end{split}

(11)

for every
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
. Suppose
x\geq 1/N_{k}
. We prove

\begin{split}\bigg|\left(R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{1}}}\cap&\left(\left(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{2}}}+(x,y)\right)\bigg|<\\
&\tfrac{1}{4}\cdot\left|R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\cap(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}+(x,y))\right|+2^{-2k-2}\cdot|R^{k-1}_{j_{1}}|\end{split}

(12)

for every
1\leq j_{1},j_{2}\leq N_{k-1}
. If
j_{2}>j_{1}
, then (
12
) is true,
because its left hand side is zero.

Suppose
j_{2}<j_{1}
. Since
R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}
is a translated copy of
R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}
,

(R_{j_{1}}^{k-1})^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{1}}}
is a translated copy of
(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1})^{\alpha^{k}_{j_{2}}}
,
and we may apply Lemma
4.2
with
R=R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}
,
n=n_{k}
,
\alpha=\alpha^{k}_{j_{2}}
and
\beta=\alpha^{k}_{j_{1}}
. Since the cardinality of each of
the sets listed in (
9
) is less than
((v-u)/4)+\varepsilon_{k}N_{k}
, and

\varepsilon_{k}\cdot N_{k}=(\varepsilon_{k}\cdot N_{k-1})\cdot n_{k}
, it follows that the
conditions of Lemma
4.2
are satisfied with
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{k}\cdot N_{k-1}
.
By the choice of
\varepsilon_{k}
, (
12
) is true.

If
j_{1}=j_{2}
, then we can argue as follows. Suppose
j_{1}=j_{2}=j
, and

\alpha=\alpha^{k}_{j}=(\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{k}})
. Since
x\geq 1/N_{k}
, we have

\left(R_{j}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha}\cap\left(\left(R_{j}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha}+(x,y)\right)\subset\left(R_{j}^{k-1}\right)^{\alpha}\cap\left(\left(R_{j}^{k-1}\right)^{\beta}+(x^{\prime},y)\right),

where
x^{\prime}=x-(1/N_{k})\geq 0
and
\beta=(1,\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n_{k}-1})
.
Put
\varepsilon=\varepsilon_{k}\cdot N_{k-1}
. If
0\leq m\leq n_{k}
, then

\displaystyle|\{1\leq i\leq n-m\colon

\displaystyle\alpha_{i+m}=\beta_{i}=1\}|\leq

\displaystyle 1+((n-m)/4)+\varepsilon n_{k}<((n-m)/4)+2\varepsilon n_{k},

as
n_{k}>1/\varepsilon_{k}\geq 1/\varepsilon
. We get the same estimate for the cardinality
of the sets
V_{2},V_{3},V_{4}
of Lemma
4.2
. Applying the lemma, we obtain
(
12
) in this case as well.

Now we add (
12
) for every
j_{1}
and
j_{2}
. Since the (possibly
degenerate) rectangles
R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\cap(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}+(x,y))

are pairwise nonoverlapping and
\sum_{j=1}^{N_{k-1}}|R^{k-1}_{j}|=|A_{k-1}|
,
we obtain

\left|A_{k}\cap\left(A_{k}+(x,y)\right)\right|<\tfrac{1}{4}\cdot\left|A_{k-1}\cap\left(A_{k-1}+(x,y)\right)\right|+2^{-2k-1}\cdot|A_{k-1}|.

(13)

(Note that for every
j_{1}
there are at most two
j_{2}
such that

|R_{j_{1}}^{k-1}\cap(R_{j_{2}}^{k-1}+(x,y))|>0
.) By
|A_{k-1}|=2^{-k+1}
, the
right hand side of (
13
) is at most
2^{-k-1}+2^{-2k-1}\cdot 2^{-k+1}\leq 2^{-k}-2^{-3k}
. Thus (10)
k
is proved if
x\geq 1/N_{k}
.

If
x\geq 1/N_{j}
, where
j<k
, then (10)
k-1
holds by
the induction hypothesis, and thus the left hand side of (
13
) is less than

\frac{1}{4}\cdot\left(2^{j-2k+2}-2^{-3k+3}\right)+2^{-2k-1}\cdot 2^{-k+1}\leq 2^{j-2k}-2^{-3k}.

\square

Now we prove that
A
does not have property
{\rm(K^{t})}
. Let
h\colon[0,1]\to{\mathbb{R}}
be a simple function, and let
B=A+{\rm graph}\,h
.
We prove that
|B|\geq 1/9
. Let
H
be the closure of
{\rm graph}\,h
, then

H\setminus{\rm graph}\,h
is finite. Since
|A|=0
, it is enough to show that

|A+H|\geq 1/9
. Since
A
and
H
are compact, we have

A+H=\bigcap_{k=0}^{\infty}(A_{k}+H),

and thus it is enough to prove that
|A_{k}+H|\geq 1/9
for every
k
.

Let
k
be fixed, and put
C_{i}=A_{k}+\{(i/N_{k},h(i/N_{k}))\}
and
f_{i}=\chi_{C_{i}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,N_{k}
. Then
C_{i}\subset A_{k}+H
for every
i
, and

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}f_{i_{1}}f_{i_{2}}\,d\lambda_{2}=|C_{i_{1}}\cap C_{i_{2}}|=\left|A_{k}\cap(A_{k}+((i_{2}-i_{1})/N_{k},y))\right|,

where
y=h(i_{2}/N_{k})-h(i_{1}/N_{k})
. By Lemma
4.3
, we obtain

\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}f_{i_{1}}f_{i_{2}}\,d\lambda_{2}<2^{j-2k}
whenever
i_{2}-i_{1}\geq N_{k}/N_{j}
.
Let
I_{j}
denote the set of pairs
(j_{1},j_{2})
such that
1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq N_{k}
and
N_{k}/N_{j}\leq i_{2}-i_{1}<N_{k}/N_{j-1}
. Then we have, for every

j=1,\ldots,k
,

\displaystyle\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\sum_{(i_{1},i_{2})\in I_{j}}f_{i_{1}}f_{i_{2}}\,d\lambda_{2}

\displaystyle<|I_{j}|\cdot 2^{j-2k}<N_{k}\cdot\frac{N_{k}}{N_{j-1}}\cdot 2^{j-2k}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{N_{k}^{2}}{4^{j-1}}\cdot 2^{j-2k}=4N_{k}^{2}\cdot 2^{-j-2k}

by
N_{j}\geq 4^{j}
. Therefore, we obtain

\begin{split}\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}f_{i}\right)^{2}\,d\lambda_{2}=&\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}f^{2}_{i}\,d\lambda_{2}+2\cdot\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\sum_{1\leq i_{1}<i_{2}\leq N_{k}}f_{i_{1}}f_{i_{2}}\,d\lambda_{2}<\\
&N_{k}\cdot 2^{-k}+2\cdot\sum_{j=1}^{k}4N_{k}^{2}\cdot 2^{-j-2k}<9N_{k}^{2}\cdot 2^{-2k}.\end{split}

Put
C=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{k}}C_{i}
and
f=\chi_{C}
. Then
C\subset A_{k}+H
. We have

\begin{split}N_{k}\cdot 2^{-k}=&\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}f_{i}\,d\lambda_{2}=\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}f_{i}\right)\cdot f\,d\lambda_{2}\leq\\
&\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N_{k}}f_{i}\right)^{2}\,d\lambda_{2}\right)^{1/2}\cdot\left(\int_{{{\mathbb{R}}}^{2}}f^{2}\,d\lambda_{2}\right)^{1/2}<\\
&3N_{k}\cdot 2^{-k}\cdot\sqrt{|C|},\end{split}

and thus
|C|>1/9
. Then
|A_{k}+H|\geq 1/9
for every
k
,
and
|A+H|\geq 1/9
. Since
A\setminus({\rm graph}\,g)
is countable,

H\setminus({\rm graph}\,h)
is finite and
|H|=0
, we obtain
|({\rm graph}\,g)+({\rm graph}\,h)|\geq 1/9
. This proves that
{\rm graph}\,g
does not have property

{\rm(K^{t})}
.

Finally, we show that a slight modification of the construction of the sets

A_{k}
gives a continuous function
f
not having property
{\rm(K^{t})}
.
We fix a sequence of numbers
q_{k}
such that
q_{k}>1
for every
k=0,1,\ldots
,
and
\prod_{k=0}^{\infty}q_{k}<2
.

We have
A_{k}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{k}}R_{j}^{k}
, where the rectangles
R_{j}^{k}

(j=1,\ldots,N_{k})
are nonoverlapping. Let
{\tilde{R}}_{j}^{k}
be a rectangle
homothetic to
R_{j}^{k}
, covered by the interior of
R_{j}^{k}
, and such that

|{\tilde{R}}_{j}^{k}|>|R_{j}^{k}|/q_{k}
. We put

{\tilde{A}}_{k}=\bigcup_{j=1}^{N_{k}}{\tilde{R}}_{j}^{k}
for every
k
, and

\tilde{A}=\bigcap_{k}^{\infty}{\tilde{A}}_{k}
. Then we have
|{\tilde{A}}_{k}|>2^{-k-1}
for every
k
.

The section
\{y\colon(x,y)\in\tilde{A}\}
is either empty or a singleton for
every
x
, and thus
\tilde{A}={\rm graph}\,f_{0}
, where
f_{0}
is a continuous function
defined on the projection
D
of
\tilde{A}
onto
[0,1]
.

Let
h
and
H
be as above, let
k
be fixed, and put
{\tilde{C}}_{i}={\tilde{A}}_{k}+\{(i/N_{k},h(i/N_{k}))\}
,
\tilde{C}=\bigcup_{i=1}^{N_{k}}{\tilde{C}}_{i}
and
{\tilde{f}}_{i}=\chi_{{\tilde{C}}_{i}}
for every
i=1,\ldots,N_{k}
.
Since
|{\tilde{A}}_{k}|=2^{-k-1}
for every
k
, the computation
of (
4
) gives

N_{k}\cdot 2^{-k-1}<3N_{k}\cdot 2^{-k}\cdot\sqrt{|{\tilde{C}}|},

and thus
|{\tilde{C}}|>1/36
. This proves that
{\rm graph}\,f_{0}
does not have
property
{\rm(K^{t})}
. Since
D
is compact,
f_{0}
can be extended to
[0,1]
as
a continuous function
f
. It is clear that
{\rm graph}\,f
does not have
property
{\rm(K^{t})}
either.
\square
