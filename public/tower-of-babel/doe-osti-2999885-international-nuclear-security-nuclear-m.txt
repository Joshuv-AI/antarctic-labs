 Rev. 2

 This procedure provides guidance for reporting and resolving Nuclear Material Accounting and
 Control (NMAC) irregularity.

2. SCOPE
 This procedure applies to all personnel in facilities that contain Accountable Nuclear Material or
 Nuclear Material Control Systems.

 3.1 Roles and Responsibilities
 3.1.1 Facility Personnel
 Facility Personnel are responsible for:
 • Reporting NMAC irregularities or suspected irregularities to facility management or
 the Material Balance Area (MBA) Custodian immediately.
 • Maintaining control of the impacted area/item/container/material until secured or the
 irregularity is resolved.
 • Promptly performing any actions required by this procedure

 3.1.2 Operations Management
 Operations Management is responsible for:
 • Ensuring the MBA Custodian or NMAC Manager is notified of NMAC irregularity or
 suspected irregularity
 • Ensuring necessary immediate corrective actions, if any, are implemented.

 3.1.3 Material Balance Area (MBA) Custodian
 The MBA Custodian is responsible for:
 • Ensuring necessary immediate corrective actions, if any, are implemented.
 • Reporting NMAC irregularities to the NMAC Manager without delay
 • Documenting irregularities and irregularity resolutions for the NMAC Manager.

 3.1.4 Nuclear Material Accounting and Control (NMAC) Manager
 The NMAC Manager is responsible for:
 • Ensuring the Officially Designated Authority, Security Services Line Management,
 Security Program Manager and Operations Management are notified.
 • Advising facility personnel of corrective actions, if any, are required to resolve the
 irregularity.
 • Ensuring suspected or confirmed Security Incidents are reported in accordance with
 designated procedure
 • Ensuring status updates are provided to the Security Program Manager, when
 applicable

 2

 Rev. 2

 • Determining compensatory measures with input from Physical Security, Vulnerability
 Analysis, and Operations Management
 • Ensuring all irregularities are entered into the NMAC irregularity database.

 3.1.5 Nuclear Material Accounting and Control (NMAC) Personnel
NMAC Personnel are responsible for:
  • Providing the content, revision, and review of this procedure
  • Advising facility personnel of corrective actions, if any, are required to resolve the
 irregularity.
  • Could include the MBA Custodian and/or NMAC Manager

 3.2 Implementation
 3.2.1 General Requirements
 1. Discovering Personnel: shall promptly report all NMAC confirmed or suspected
 irregularities or incidents of security concern to Operations Management and the
 MBA Custodian.

 2. Discovering Personnel: maintain control of the area/item/container/material
 UNTIL otherwise secured,

 OR the irregularity is resolved,

 OR the personnel is relieved.

 3. Operations Management: ensure the MBA Custodian is notified immediately of the
 irregularity or suspected irregularity.

 4. MBA Custodian: shall promptly report confirmed or suspected irregularities to the
 NMAC Manager.

 5. NMAC Manager: notify the Facility Manager, Security Services Line Management,
 and Security Program Manager, as applicable.

Note: Attachment 4.1: Nuclear Material Accounting and Control Irregularity Examples provides
NMAC Loss Detection Elements and examples of typical irregularities associated with each
element.

 6. NMAC and Facility Personnel: attempt to resolve the irregularity using the
 guidance provided in Attachment 4.2, Irregularities and Suggested Responses.

Note: Attachment 4.2 provides the initial steps to assist in irregularity resolution. Additional steps
may be required to resolve the irregularity or confirm incident.

 7. IF the irregularity is determined to be an incident of security concern,

 THEN NMAC Manager: ensure irregularity resolution status is provided to the
 Security Program Manager.

 8. MBA Custodian and NMAC Personnel: ensure details of the irregularity are
 recorded in the NMAC Irregularity Database by completing Attachment 4.3,
 Irregularity Investigation.

Note: An irregularity is considered resolved when all specified actions are complete.

 9. WHEN the irregularit(ies) are considered resolved,

 THEN Responsible organizations/programs: confirm the completion of all actions
 (e.g. NMAC, Vulnerability Assessment/Physical Security, Operations, etc.)

 10. NMAC Manager: notify the Officially Designated Authority, Security Services Line
 Management, and Security Program Manager when the irregularity is resolved.

performing periodic item monitoring on nuclear material.

Item monitoring is used in conjunction with other nuclear material accounting and control (NMAC)
processes and practices to verify the presence and integrity of selected nuclear material (NM)
items on a periodic basis. As described in IAEA NSS 32-T, the objective of item monitoring is for
detection of irregularities in a timely manner while also improving reliability and conﬁdence in the
employed NMAC system. Item monitoring provides the timely detection of irregularities, especially
important when items are vulnerable during processing and in between inventory periods, including
unauthorized removal. It is not necessary for operational activities to be paused for item monitoring
activities unless an irregularity is discovered during the course of this procedure.

 1. At least two facility operators with authorized access to the area containing nuclear
 material
 2. MBA Custodian
 3. Electronic Balance
 4. Item Weight Measurement Procedure
 5. Relevant Forms
 a. Location Inventory List
 b. Item Monitoring List Generation Datasheet
 c. Item Monitoring Activity Sheet

Item monitoring is a program that involves periodic checking of a random sample of items
between physical inventory takings. Doing so increases the likelihood of quickly detecting
unauthorized removal of nuclear material or other irregularity. During Item monitoring only
a sampling of items are checked. The sample is selected using statistical techniques that
are discussed in Section 2.3.

 overseeing the veriﬁcation process and maintaining control of all material information and

 10697, Item Monitoring.
NMAC Manager:
  • Upon request, the NMAC Manager is responsible for supplying information or further
 guidance in the event of an irregularity.

 B. IMPLEMENTATION

Gross weight measurement plays a critical role in nuclear material control and accounting. It is
used to verify inventory records, support measurement systems (such as NDA or DA), and conﬁrm
the accuracy of transfers, receipts, and removals.

In most cases, determining the net weight of nuclear material requires subtracting the known
container tare weight from the measured gross weight. For this reason, accurately weighing both
full and empty containers is essential.

To ensure the reliability of all measurements, scales must be routinely calibrated using certiﬁed
calibration weights. These weights serve as traceable references that conﬁrm the scale is
operating within acceptable tolerances. Prior to use, the scale’s performance should be checked
and recorded on the Scale Standard Measurement Control Sheet. This sheet can be used to track
the scale’s limits over time. Incorporating these practices ensures that all weight data is accurate
and repeatable.

 3.1 PREPARING THE SCALE
 A. ROLES AND RESPONSIBLITIES
Scale Users must be current in at least Training ID# 10855, Scale Operator as well as any additional
training requirements, such as handling and movements, as necessary to perform the activities
below with nuclear material.

 1. Conﬁrm that the scale is still in its calibration range by comparing the calibration
 expiration date on the Certiﬁcate of Calibration with today’s date.
 2. IF today’s date is after the listed expiration date on the Certiﬁcate of Calibration for
 the scale,
 THEN the scale may not be used for measurements
 UNTIL the scale undergoes successful calibration
 OR another scale in calibration is located for use.
 3. Press the applicable button on the scale to turn it on, such as ON/ZERO Off.
 4. Zero (tare) the scale in accordance with the model in use.

Note: Some scales will automatically zero (tare) when the scale turns on, such as the example
scale shown in Figure 1.

 5. Select the appropriate calibration weight.

Note 1: Each material type and conﬁguration is subject to differing calibration weight and bounds of
acceptance. These values are determined and provided before starting any relevant activity.

Note 2: In some instances, multiple calibration weights may be necessary if a variety of gross
material weights are anticipated during performance of 3.2 Weighing an Item.

Note 3: Care should be taken to ensure the scale is within the calibrated weight range for the
anticipated and measured item’s weight.

 6. Record the following information for the item on the Record of Weighing Log:
 a. Measurement Date
 b. Item ID
 c. Item Description
 d. Declared Gross Weight
 7. Gently place the calibration weight in the center of the steel platform.

 3.2 WEIGHING AN ITEM
 A. ROLES AND RESPONSIBLITIES
While handling nuclear material, the weighing procedure shall be performed by a team of at least
two trained facility personnel. At no time may the two-person rule be violated during the process.

Scale Users must be current in, at a minimum, Training ID# 10855, Scale Operator as well as any
additional training requirements, such as handling and movements, necessary to perform the
activities below if measuring nuclear material.

This section applies to both of the following instances:

• Items containing nuclear material: to determine or verify item gross weight
• Empty containers: to determine the starting container tare weight of new empty containers or to
 verify all previous nuclear material has been removed from the newly empty container

 1. Locate the item to be weighed.
 2. Move the item to the workspace, as required.
 3. Record the following information for the item on the Record of Weighing Log:
 a. Measurement Date
 b. Item ID
 c. Item Description
 d. Declared Gross Weight
 i. IF the item has a Declared Gross Weight measured previously,
 THEN use the previous Declared Gross Weight on the form for recording and
 subsequent difference measurement
 ii. IF the item contains nuclear material AND there is no Declared Gross Weight
 yet,
 THEN record ‘New Item’ in the Declared Gross Weight portion of the Record
 of Weighing Log
 AND ensure the overall material balance in which the item was created
 equates and is logical throughout this procedure.
 iii. IF the item is an empty container,
 THEN use the relevant option below:
 1. IF the empty container is new AND has no previous container tare
 weight,
 THEN record ‘new empty container’ in the Declared Gross Weight
 portion of the Record of Weighing Log.
 2. IF the empty container has been used previously AND has a
 recorded container tare weight,
 THEN use the previously recorded container tare weight in the
 Declared Gross Weight portion of the Record of Weighing Log.

 6

 Rev. 2
Note: For measuring nuclear material, the speciﬁc material and processes in use will determine
how the calculated material balance for Step 3.c.ii is determined. There is an expectation that no
signiﬁcant material is lost or gained.

 4. Gently place the item in the center of the steel platform.
 5. Record the result of gross weight on the Record of Weighing Log and any other
 documentation, as required.

Note: For newly created items or new empty containers, Step 6-8 will not be applicable.

 6. Subtract the Declared Gross Weight of the item from the Measured Gross Weight
 obtained in Step 5. This value is the Gross Weight Difference.
 7. Record the Difference calculated in Step 6 on the Gross Weight Difference
 (Measured – Declared) on the Record of Weighing Log.

Note 1: Only the gross weight can be measured, this includes the weight of all components of the
item, such as container, lid, label(s), TID etc.

Note 2: The difference should be used immediately here and tracked over time to help in various
detections, including but not limited to theft, equipment trends and process monitoring contributing
values.

Note 3: The difference in existing empty containers for the container tare weight provides indication
if all material previously housed in the empty container was fully removed or not.

 8. Check and note on the Record of Weighing Log whether the item passes or fails the
 measurement uncertainty of the respective material.
 a. IF the item is outside of the allowable range for the material,
 THEN pause work
 AND contact the NMAC Manager for guidance.
 b. IF the item is within the allowable range for the speciﬁc material,
 THEN continue to Step 9.

Note: The allowable material range for each material should be established in advance and
provided for reference for all material types, such as isotope, and characteristics, such as
enrichment and form, combinations in use within the facility.

 9. Both Scale Users: Print and sign the Record of Weighing Log, verifying the accuracy
 of the information entered.
 10. Repeat Steps 1-9 until all items are weighed.

Physical Inventory Taking (PIT) activities, in conjunction with other NMAC program elements,
ensures accountability of nuclear material (NM) and discrepancies between the book inventory
and the physical inventory are detected and resolved. PITs are conducted at the frequency
established with the regulatory body and include planning, preparation, conduct, and
reconciliation. During the PIT, it is recommended that operations in all affected areas are paused to
ensure no material handling or operations are performed until the PIT is successfully completed.

The PIT activity may also be prompted by the discovery or the suspicion of an irregularity in the
facility. In this instance, the extent of condition and/or assurance of irregularity resolution may be
determined by fully completing the procedure below. It is required to both document the reasons
and findings of the unscheduled PIT on the appropriate documentation and in the areas denoted
throughout this procedure.

 An effective tamper indicating device (TID) and its implementing program can provide
 confidence in the unopened state of an object, such as container, door, cabinet, cage etc.
 Proper use of a TID promotes confidence in the continuity of knowledge of the object’s
 contents. Assurance of TID effectiveness and applicability for the object and the associated
 environment before facility use is essential to ensure the longevity and continued effectiveness
 of not only the applied TID but also the facility TID program as a whole. TIDs should be applied
 in parallel with effective surveillance methods and practices in order to assess the state of the
 applied TIDs. TIDs not only serve as an indicator of attempt of or access to the object, but also
 as a deterrent to such actions from a potential insider.

 Further information on their forms and functions as well as more information about TID
 program requirements themselves can be found through relevant IAEA documentation, NSS.
   25-G and NSS. 32-T.

 3.3 TID REMOVAL

The removal shall be performed by an authorized TID User and verified by the TID Verifier. The TID
Custodian (or Alternate) may not remove or void TID(s) in their MBA, however they can act as a TID
Verifier.

The TID User and TID Verifier must be up to date in either Training ID# 10331, Tamper Indicating
Device Facility Operator or Training ID# 10335, Tamper Indicating Device NMAC Department.

CAUTION: If the TID is found broken or damaged as a result of improper application or wear, the TID
should be VOIDED and an irregularity investigation may need to be launched.

 1. TID User (remover) and TID Verifier: Verify the TID number and container/item
 number against provided documentation, such as:

 a. TID logbook

 b. Relevant entry in the TID Activity Form

 c. NMAC accounting system records

 d. Shipper/Receiver Log

 2. IF the TID is suspected to have been intentionally interfered with or there exists any
 other discrepancies,

 THEN pause the process

 AND contact responsible NMAC representative.

 THEN TID User: Record following information on a TID Activity Form:

 a. Date of Activity

 b. TID ID Number

 c. TID Type

 d. Container/Item ID

 e. Location (MBA)

 4. TID User: Remove the TID.

 5. TID User: Destroy and discard the TID in the applicable TID discard bin such that

This procedure outlines the standardized instructions for accessing, securing, and resealing a
nuclear material vault. The procedure ensures the integrity and safety of the vault contents while
maintaining strict accountability.

 1. Vault
 2. 2 qualified Vault Users
 3. Vault Access Log
 4. TID(s), if applicable
 5. Tamper Indicating Device Procedure, if applicable

 It is recommended to store material that is not currently in use in a vault or other types of
 containment room/area that can be locked. A vault type room (VTR) is one structural
 storage arrangement commonly employed in facilities to promote enhanced material
 security and continuity of knowledge. In addition to continuity of knowledge assistance,
 utilizing VTRs for material storage and containment can both reduce the time for detection
 of missing material and serve as a deterrence for theft or sabotage through access control,
 added surveillance measures and control measures.
 See IAEA NSS. 25-G Use of Nuclear Material Accounting and Control for Nuclear Security Purposes at
 Facilities for both source and additional information.

 3.1 VAULT OPENING

Vault opening must be performed by a team of at least two Vault Users with up-to-date training in
Training ID# 10554, Vault Access and, if applicable, either Training ID# 10331, Tamper Indicating
Device Facility Operator or Training ID# 10335, Tamper Indicating Device NMAC Department.

The two-person rule may not be violated at any time.

Follow the approach outlined in this section when accessing a vault that contains nuclear material
with or without a TID.

CAUTION 1: A minimum of two qualified Vault Users must accompany the open vault at all times.

CAUTION 2: If appropriate personnel cannot accompany the vault at all moments during the period
the vault door is open, the vault must be closed following Vault Closing section of this procedure
and reopen it only when sufficient personnel return.

CAUTION 3: Upon discovery of any discrepancies such as a compromised TID or discrepancies in
the Vault Access Log), immediately pause work and refer to the Irregularity Response Procedure.

 1. Ensure the VTR has a posted Vault Access Log.

 2. Ensure the following information on the Vault Access Log is current and
 accurate:

 a. Building
 b. MBA
 c. Vault Number

 3. IF the VTR door(s) requires removing a TID(s) for access,

 THEN perform the following actions to remove each TID required for entry:

 a. Ensure that the physical TID matches the TID number recorded in the Vault
 Access Log.
 b. Ensure the TID is integral and does not have any signs of tampering.
 c. GO TO Tamper Indicating Device Procedure to remove and discard the TID
 AND RETURN TO this procedure Step 4.

 4. Open the vault door(s).

 5. Complete the Vault Opening section of the Vault Access Log.

 6. Enter the VTR.

 3.2 VAULT CLOSING

Vault opening must be performed by a team of at least two Vault Users with up-to-date training in
Training ID# 10554, Vault Access and, if applicable, either Training ID# 10331, Tamper Indicating
Device Facility Operator or Training ID# 10335, Tamper Indicating Device NMAC Department.

The two-person rule may not be violated at any time.

 A. IMPLEMENTATION

Follow the approach outlined in this section when closing a vault that contains nuclear material
with or without a TID.

 1. Ensure inside of vault is as expected.

 2. Exit the vault.

 3. Close the vault door(s).

 4. IF the VTR door requires an application of a TID,

 THEN perform the following actions to apply the TID:

 a. GO TO Tamper Indicating Device Procedure to request and apply the TID
 AND RETURN TO this procedure Step 4.e
 b. Ensure the TID is applied appropriately on the vault door such that opening
 without indication is impossible.
 c. Record the TID number on the Vault Access Log.

Note: Step 4.e. may be done earlier in the same day to have a TID ready and on hand for vault
securing. However, the TID must be issued and used within the same workday in accordance with
the Tamper Indicating Device Procedure.

 5. Complete the Vault Closing section of the Vault Access Log.
