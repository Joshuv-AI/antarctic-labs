Quasi-Static Control of Discrete Cosserat Rod Thanks:
Srishti Siddharth, Domenico Campolo, Ravi Banavar

Abstract
In this paper, we design feedback control laws for soft robots modelled using the Cosserat rod, which is spatially discretised using the Piecewise Constant Strain (PCS) approach. The PCS approach transforms the nonlinear PDEs describing the Cosserat rod to a system of nonlinear ODEs. This simplification results in a model describing soft robots which is similar to the serial rigid-link manipulators. We design feedback control laws for the quasi-static PCS model by using external wrenches as control inputs. The control laws are designed based on state-feedback linearisation in strain and task spaces. An extensive set of numerical results demonstrates the performance of the control laws for end-effector trajectory tracking and shape control of soft robots.



I
Introduction

In many engineering and scientific applications, geometrically exact rods are used to obtain accurate models for highly deformable slender rods. Such flexible structures find applications in continuum robots
[
7
,
4
]
, DNA strands experiencing various mechanical forces
[
6
]
and flexible microrobots for endovascular surgery
[
2
]
. The geometrically exact rod
[
1
]
or the Cosserat rod provides a mathematically elegant and high-fidelity model for such slender rods undergoing large deformations, unlike Euler-Bernoulli and Timoshenko beam theories, which account for small deformations.

The variational formulation of the geometrically exact rod and its numerical aspects were presented in the extensive work of Simo et al. given in
[
19
]
and
[
20
]
.
The book by Antman
[
1
]
presents the theory of planar and spatial geometrically exact rods. The modern treatment of geometrically exact rods is directed towards algorithm design and implementation based on the special Cosserat rod theory, which describes the motion of the rod using the positions of material points lying on its centerline and the orientations of rigid cross-sections attached at each material point.

The soft robotics community has proposed various numerical models
based on the Cosserat rod theory. The Piecewise Constant Curvature (PCC) model
[
5
,
8
]
assumes that a continuum robot can be represented by a series of connected circular arcs, which is not valid in the case of out-of-plane external loads. The Piecewise Constant Strain (PCS) model introduced by Renda et al.
[
17
,
18
,
16
]
, assumes that a soft robot consists of multiple sections, each with constant linear and angular strains. The resulting geometric structure of the model is similar to the traditional rigid-link manipulators, thus unifying the theory of rigid-link and soft robots
[
12
,
14
]
. This allows us to extend the control design theory developed for rigid-link manipulators to soft robots. Other strain based models include the Geometric Variable Strain (GVS) model
[
3
]
and the Piecewise Linear Strain (PLS) model
[
11
]
, which include discrete statics and dynamics models of the Cosserat rod based on strain basis functions.

Several model-based controllers have been proposed for soft robots, which are inherently underactuated.
Lyapunov function-based control laws have been developed in
[
9
]
and
[
13
]
for dynamics of soft robots. An inverse kinematics-based controller for the GVS model has been developed in
[
15
]
. Finally, a robust control law for the PLS static model has been proposed in
[
10
]
. For the sake of conciseness, we do not provide an extensive literature survey on control of soft robots. In this paper, we will focus on design of control laws for the quasi-static PCS model. Thus, the end-effector trajectory tracking and shape control tasks will be achieved by considering slow motion of the robot to satisfy the conditions for quasi-static operation.

Contributions:
The contribution of the paper is to primarily develop the model-based control theory of soft robots. This is in contrast to the modern development of soft robots which focuses on learning-based control strategies. We formulate the inverse kinematics for soft robots, which provides various strains for a given end-effector pose of the robot. We then derive simple and effective control schemes based on state-feedback linearisation in strain and task spaces, while remaining within the framework of the Cosserat rod theory. We present three numerical experiments to validate the control approaches.

II
Statics of the Cosserat rod

II-A

Kinematics of the Rod

The Cosserat rod theory accounts for stretching, shearing, bending and twisting strains of a rod. Let
{L}
be the length of the undeformed rod and let
{S}\in[0,{L}]
be the arc-length parameter which is used to identify the material points on the centerline of the rod. The configuration of the rod is described with respect to an inertial frame
\{e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}\}
, where
e_{1},e_{2},e_{3}
are the standard unit vectors in
{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
. The undeformed or reference configuration of the rod is assumed to be the one in which the rod is lying in a straight configuration along the
e_{1}
axis. The configuration of the rod at any time instant is completely described by the position of each material point lying on the centerline, denoted by
{x}({S},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and the orientation of the cross-section attached to each point, denoted by
{R}({S},t)\in{\mathrm{SO(3)}}
. Thus, the configuration of the rod at a time instant
t
, defined by the map
{g}:[0,{L}]\to SE(3)
, is given as

{g}({S},t)=\begin{bmatrix}{R}({S},t)&{x}({S},t)\\
0^{T}&1\end{bmatrix}

(1)

for all
{S}\in[0,{L}]
. In addition to the configuration described in (
1
), the kinematics of the rod is described by its strains and velocities. The velocity twist, expressed in the body-fixed frame (or the material frame) of the rod is given by

\widehat{{\eta}}({S},t)={g}^{-1}({S},t)\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial t}({S},t)={g}^{-1}({S},t)\dot{{g}}({S},t),

(2)

where

{\eta}({S},t)=\begin{bmatrix}{\omega}^{T}({S},t)&{\gamma}^{T}({S},t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6},

(3)

\widehat{(\cdot)}:{\mathbb{R}}^{6}\to{\mathfrak{se}(3)}
is the hat map,

{\gamma}({S},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
{\omega}({S},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are the linear and angular velocities, respectively.

Similarly, the strain twist, expressed in the material frame, is given by

\widehat{{\xi}}({S},t)={g}^{-1}({S},t)\frac{\partial{g}}{\partial{S}}({S},t)={g}^{-1}({S},t){g}^{\prime}({S},t),

(4)

where

{\xi}({S},t)=\begin{bmatrix}{\kappa}^{T}({S},t)&{V}^{T}({S},t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6},

(5)

{V}({S},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
and
{\kappa}({S},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are the linear and angular strains, respectively. The first component of
{V}({S},t)
represents the stretching strain along
e_{1}
, and the remaining components represent the shearing strains along
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
axes, respectively. Similarly, the first component of
{\kappa}({S},t)
represents the twisting strain about the
e_{1}
axis, and the remaining components represent bending strains about
e_{2}
and
e_{3}
axes, respectively.

For the sake of concise notation, the space and time dependence of the quantities described in (
1
) - (
5
) will be sometimes ignored.

The relationship between the velocity in (
3
) and the strain in (

) is given by

{\eta}^{\prime}=\dot{{\xi}}-{\text{ad}}_{{\xi}}{\eta}

(6)

due to the equality of the mixed partial derivatives, i.e.,
(\dot{{g}})^{\prime}=\dot{({g}^{\prime})}
. Here,
{\text{ad}}_{{\xi}}
is the adjoint map for the strain twist vector described in
[

]
.

II-B

Statics of the rod

The strain potential energy is stored in the rod due to various modes of deformation. The strain potential energy of the rod at time instant
t
is given as

\mathcal{U}(t)=\int_{0}^{{L}}\frac{1}{2}({\xi}({S})-{\xi}_{0})^{T}\Sigma({S})({\xi}({S})-{\xi}_{0})\,d{S},

(7)

where
{\xi}_{0}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&1&0&0\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is the strain for the reference configuration and
\Sigma({S})=\text{diag}(GJ_{x}({S}),EI_{y}({S}),EI_{z}({S}),EA({S}),GA({S}),GA({S}))
is the stiffness matrix of the rod,
E
is the Young’s modulus and
G
is the shear modulus of the rod. The relation between the moduli is given by
G=\frac{E}{2(1+\nu)}

where
\nu
is the Poisson’s ratio of the rod
[
1
]
.

The cross-sectional area of the rod at
{S}
is denoted by
A({S})
and the second moment of inertia is given by
{\mathcal{I}}({S})=\text{diag}(J_{x}({S}),J_{y}({S}),J_{z}({S}))
. For a circular cross-section, we have
{\mathcal{I}}({S})=\text{diag}(2,1,1)\frac{A^{2}({S})}{4\pi}
.
Assuming a linear visco-elastic constitutive relation, the material stress, also called the wrench of internal elastic forces, is given as

F_{i}({S},t)={\Sigma}({S})({\xi}({S},t)-{\xi}_{0})+{\Gamma}\dot{{\xi}}({S},t),

(8)

where
{\Gamma}=\text{diag}(J_{x},3J_{y},3J_{z},3A,A,A){v_{s}}
and
{v_{s}}
is the shear viscosity modulus. The internal elastic wrench can be written as
F_{i}=\begin{bmatrix}M_{i}^{T}&N_{i}^{T}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
, where
M_{i},N_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are the stresses, expressed in the material frame, due to angular and linear strains, respectively. Let
F_{e}=\begin{bmatrix}m^{T}_{e}&n^{T}_{e}\end{bmatrix}^{T}
be the external distributed wrench on the rod, where
m_{e},n_{e}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3}
are the external distributed moment and force, respectively, expressed in the material frame. Thus, the quasi-static equation of motion of the rod, derived by applying the Lagrange-D’Alembert principle, is given as

F_{i}^{\prime}+{\text{ad}}_{{\xi}}^{*}F_{i}+F_{e}=0,

(9)

where the coadjoint map
{\text{ad}}_{{\xi}}^{*}
for the strain twist vector defined in
[
16
]
.
In this paper, we consider the external distributed load due to gravity. Thus, the distributed wrench due to gravity is given as
F_{e}={M}{\text{Ad}}^{-1}_{{g}}{\mathcal{G}}

where
{M}=\text{diag}(J_{x},J_{y},J_{z},A,A,A)\rho
is the screw inertia matrix,
\rho
is the mass density of the rod,
{\mathcal{G}}=\begin{bmatrix}0&0&0&0&0&-9.81\end{bmatrix}^{T}
is the gravity twist with respect to the inertial frame and
Ad
is the Adjoint map for
{\mathrm{SE(3)}}
defined in
[
16
]
.

III
Discrete model of the Static Cosserat rod: PCS approach

In this section, we will consider the Piecewise Constant Strain (PCS) approach to discretise the Cosserat rod
[
16
]
. The rod is spatially discretised into sections with constant strains, which results in analytical integration of the equations describing the continuous space-time equation of motion of the Cosserat rod.

III-A

Discrete kinematics of the Rod

Let us consider that the rod of undeformed length
{L}
has been discretised into
{N}
sections. Thus,
{S}\in[0,{L}]
is divided into
{N}
sections:
[0,{L}_{1}],[{L}_{1},{L}_{2}],...,[{L}_{{N}-1},{L}_{{N}}]
, where
{L}_{{N}}={L}
. According to the PCS approach, each section is assumed to have a constant strain, denoted by
{\xi}_{n}
for
{n}=1,2,...,{N}
. The spatial evolution of the configuration of the rod at time instant
t
is described by

{g}^{\prime}={g}\widehat{{\xi}}.

(10)

Based on the PCS approach, the solution of (
10
) for
{S}\in[{L}_{{n}-1},{L}_{{n}}]
can be written as

{g}({S})={g}({L}_{{n}-1})e^{({S}-L_{{n}-1})\widehat{{\xi}}_{n}}={g}({L}_{{n}-1}){g}_{n}({S}),

(11)

where the closed-form expression for
{g}_{n}({S}):=e^{({S}-L_{{n}-1})\widehat{{\xi}}_{n}}
is given in
[
16
]
.

The pose at
{S}\in[{L}_{n-1},{L}_{n}]
can be found recursively from (
11
), which gives us the product of exponentials (PoE) formula for the PCS model as

{g}({S})={g}(0)e^{{L}_{1}\widehat{{\xi}}_{1}}e^{({L}_{2}-{L}_{1})\widehat{{\xi}}_{2}}...e^{({S}-{L}_{{n}-1})\widehat{{\xi}}_{{n}}}.

(12)

The PoE formula (
12
) allows us to represent the Cosserat rod by
{\xi}_{n}
for
{n}=1,...,{N}
, which can be thought of as joint twists. Thus, the joint or the strain vector for a discrete rod is given by

{q}=\begin{bmatrix}{\xi}^{T}_{1}{\xi}^{T}_{2}...{\xi}^{T}_{{N}}\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}}.

(13)

The velocity of the cross-section at
{S}\in[0,{L}]
can be computed by integrating (
6
) for a section
[{L}_{{n}-1},{L}_{{n}}]
, i.e., by integrating

{\eta}^{\prime}({S})=\dot{{\xi}}_{n}-{\text{ad}}_{{\xi}_{{n}}}{\eta}({S}),\quad{S}\in[{L}_{{n}-1},{L}_{{n}}].

(14)

Since
{\xi}_{n}
is constant at time instant
t
, therefore, (
14
) is a nonhomogeneous, linear matrix differential equation with constant coefficient, and it’s solution is given by

{\eta}({S})={\text{Ad}}^{-1}_{{g}_{{n}}({S})}({\eta}({L}_{{n}-1})+T_{{g}_{{n}}({S})}\dot{{\xi}}_{{n}}),

(15)

where the closed-form expressions for
{\text{Ad}}_{{g}_{{n}}({S})}
and
T_{{g}_{{n}}({S})}
are provided in
[
16
]
.

Assumption 1

In this paper, we will consider a cantilever rod. Thus, the boundary conditions at the base of the rod are given by

{g}(0,t)=I_{4},\quad{\eta}(0,t)=0\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\quad\forall t\geq 0.

(16)

Remark 2

I_{k}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{k\times k}
denotes the
k
dimensional identity matrix.

On computing
{\eta}({S})
recursively from (
15
), we get

{\eta}({S})={J}({S},{q})\dot{{q}},

(17)

where the closed-form expression for the geometric Jacobian,
{J}({S},{q})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6\times 6{N}}
is given in
[
16
]
.

III-B

Discrete statics of the rod

The discrete statics can be obtained by converting (
9
) to the weak form and applying the piecewise constant strain assumption

D\dot{{q}}+K({q}-{q}^{*})=\bar{{J}}^{T}({q})\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{ext}(t)+\mathcal{N}({q}){\mathcal{G}},

l_{{n}}
for
{n}=1,...,{N}

{n}
,
\bar{{J}}(q)=\begin{bmatrix}{J}^{T}({L}_{1},q)&{J}^{T}({L}_{2},q)&...&{J}^{T}({L},q)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}\times 6{N}}

\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{ext}(t)=\begin{bmatrix}\mathcal{F}_{ext}({L}_{1},t)&\mathcal{F}_{ext}({L}_{2},t)&...&\mathcal{F}_{ext}({L},t)\end{bmatrix}^{T}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}\times 1}
and
\mathcal{N}(q)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}\times 6}
is the gravitational matrix
[
16

. The strain potential energy of the discrete rod is given by

\displaystyle\begin{split}\mathcal{U}_{d}(t)=\frac{1}{2}({q}-{q}^{*})^{T}K({q}-{q}^{*}),\end{split}

(19)

which can be obtained by applying the PCS assumption to (

[

V
Quasi-Static control of the discrete Cosserat rod

The shape regulation and end-effector position tracking for the rod can be achieved by formulating the control problems in the strain space or the task space. We will discuss both control approaches in this section.

V-A

Strain space control

Let us consider a new strain vector
\bar{{q}}={q}-{q}^{*}
. Thus, the discrete statics of the rod, given in (
18
), can be written as

\dot{\bar{{q}}}=A\bar{{q}}+{D^{-1}}u(\bar{{q}},t)+{D^{-1}}\mathcal{N}(\bar{{q}}){\mathcal{G}},

(21)

where
A=-D^{-1}K
and
u(\bar{{q}},t)=\bar{{J}}^{T}(\bar{{q}})\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{ext}(t)
.

Remark 3

It should be noted that
A
is a diagonal, Hurwitz matrix since the diagonal matrices
D^{-1}
and
K
contain strictly positive elements. Therefore, in the absence of any external wrenches, the rod converges to the reference configuration.

Theorem 1

Consider the desired strain vector of the rod, denoted by
{q}_{d}(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}}
. Given a symmetric, positive definite matrix
\bar{K}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6{N}\times 6{N}}
, the feedback control law

u(\bar{{q}},t)=D(-\bar{K}\bar{e}-A\bar{e}-A\bar{{q}}_{d}+\dot{\bar{{q}}}_{d})-\mathcal{N}(\bar{{q}}){\mathcal{G}},

(22)

applied to (
21
), renders the desired trajectory locally asymptotically stable. Here,
\bar{e}(t)=\bar{{q}}(t)-\bar{{q}}_{d}(t)
is the strain error.

Proof:
From (
21
),
the error dynamics are given by

\dot{\bar{e}}=A\bar{e}+D^{-1}u(\bar{{q}},t)+D^{-1}\mathcal{N}(\bar{{q}}){\mathcal{G}}+A\bar{{q}}_{d}-\dot{\bar{{q}}}_{d}.

(23)

Consider the Lyapunov function

V(\bar{e})=\frac{1}{2}\bar{e}^{T}\bar{e}.

(24)

Thus,
V(\bar{e})>0
for
\bar{{q}}\neq\bar{{q}}_{d}
and
V(\bar{e})=0
for
\bar{{q}}=\bar{{q}}_{d}
.

On differentiating (
24
) with respect to time and substituting the statics given in (
21
) and the feedback control law in (
22
), we get

\dot{V}(\bar{e})=-\bar{e}^{T}\bar{K}{\bar{e}}.

(25)

Thus,
\dot{V}(\bar{e})<0
for
\bar{{q}}\neq\bar{{q}}_{d}
and
\dot{V}(\bar{e})=0
for
\bar{{q}}=\bar{{q}}_{d}
, which ensures that the strain error converges to the origin asymptotically. The external wrench vector is given by

\bar{\mathcal{F}}_{ext}(t)=(\bar{{J}}(\bar{q}))^{-T}u(\bar{{q}},t).

(26)

Remark 4

For set-point control,
\dot{\bar{{q}}}_{d}=0
.

V-B

Task space control

The end-effector position tracking control can be designed by transforming the quasi-static system in (
18

Let us consider the minimal coordinates
y(t)=(r(t),{x}({L},t))\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6}

r(t)=a(t)\theta(t)
(
a(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{3},\theta(t)\in{\mathbb{R}}

{S}={L}
and
{x}({L},t)

\dot{y}=\begin{bmatrix}\dot{r}\\
\dot{x}({L})\end{bmatrix}=T(r,{x}({L}),{R}({L}))\begin{bmatrix}{\omega}({L})\\
{\gamma}({L})\end{bmatrix},

(27)

where

(28)

\tilde{(\cdot)}:{\mathbb{R}}^{3}\to{\mathfrak{so}(3)}

On substituting

\bar{B}(\bar{{q}})=T(r,{x}({L}),{R}({L})){J}({L},\bar{{q}})D^{-1}{J}^{T}({L},\bar{{q}})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{6\times 6}

VII
Conclusions and Future Work

In this paper, we developed feedback control laws in the strain and task spaces for the discrete Cosserat rod. The Piecewise Constant Strain (PCS) discretisation approach presents a simplified model of the Cosserat rod, which is described by a set of nonlinear PDEs evolving on
{\mathrm{SE(3)}}
. The IK algorithm and the corresponding numerical test illustrated the strain redundancy of the Cosserat rod. Shape regulation of the rod was achieved by implementing the feedback control in the strain space. The performance of the proposed feedback control laws employed for end-effector position tracking of the rod was also demonstrated.

Future work will focus on the development of an IK algorithm which provides a
solution with minimum strain potential energy and an optimal control framework for cable-actuated soft robots, along with the incorporation of buckling analysis in the control design.
