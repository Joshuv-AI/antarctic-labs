Magnetic moments of open bottom–charm molecular pentaquark octets
Halil Mutuk, Xian-Wei Kang

Abstract
We present a comprehensive theoretical investigation of the magnetic moments of open heavy-flavor molecular pentaquarks with quark compositions b ​ c ¯ ​ q ​ q ​ q b\bar{c}qqq and c ​ b ¯ ​ q ​ q ​ q c\bar{b}qqq (where q = u , d , s q=u,d,s ). Employing a molecular picture in which the pentaquarks are treated as S-wave bound states of a heavy baryon and a meson, we systematically construct the complete spin–flavor wavefunctions for the two distinct SU(3) f octet representations, 8 1 ​ f 8_{1f} and 8 2 ​ f 8_{2f} , arising from symmetric and antisymmetric light-diquark configurations, respectively. Within the framework of the constituent quark model, we calculate the magnetic moments of spin-parity configurations, J P = 1 2 − ​ ( 1 2 + ⊗ 0 − ) J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-}(\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}) and J P = 1 2 − , 3 2 − ​ ( 1 2 + ⊗ 1 − ) J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-},\frac{3}{2}^{-}(\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}) , for each member of the b ​ c ¯ b\bar{c} and c ​ b ¯ c\bar{b} octets. Our results reveal a striking hierarchy: in the 8 2 ​ f 8_{2f} representation, the 1 2 + ⊗ 0 − \frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-} states exhibit near-universal magnetic moments ( μ ≈ − 0.062 ​ μ N \mu\approx-0.062\,\mu_{N} for b ​ c ¯ ​ q ​ q ​ q b\bar{c}qqq and μ ≈ + 0.362 ​ μ N \mu\approx+0.362\,\mu_{N} for c ​ b ¯ ​ q ​ q ​ q c\bar{b}qqq ), as a direct consequence of the spin-singlet light-diquark that suppresses light-quark contributions. In contrast, the 8 1 ​ f 8_{1f} representation shows a broad spectrum of values with frequent sign changes, reflecting the active role of the symmetric light-diquark. The clear differences between the b ​ c ¯ b\bar{c} and c ​ b ¯ c\bar{b} families demonstrate explicit heavy-quark flavor symmetry breaking in electromagnetic observables. These predictions provide a detailed set of electromagnetic benchmarks that can serve as discriminants for the internal flavor structure and spin configuration of future experimentally observed open heavy-flavor pentaquarks, offering valuable guidance for ongoing and future searches at facilities such as LHCb and Belle II.

The landscape of hadron spectroscopy has been profoundly reshaped by the discovery of exotic multiquark states, challenging the traditional meson and baryon configurations and offering new insights into the nonperturbative regime of Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD). Building upon this discovery, the LHCb Collaboration performed an updated amplitude analysis in 2019, revealing three distinct hidden-charm pentaquark states:

The magnetic moment of a hadron stands out as a fundamental electromagnetic property that encodes direct information about the distribution of charge and spin among its constituents. For pentaquark states, the magnetic moment depends critically on the relative alignment of heavy-quark and light-quark spins, the orbital angular momentum configuration, and the overall color-flavor symmetry of the wavefunction. Consequently, magnetic moment predictions can vary significantly across different structural models, even for states with similar masses and quantum numbers. This sensitivity makes the magnetic moment a powerful discriminant for probing the internal architecture of exotic hadrons. In the context of bottom-charm pentaquarks, the magnetic moment receives contributions from two different heavy quarks and from the three light quarks, offering a rich interplay that is absent in single-heavy-flavor systems. Calculating and comparing these moments across different models can therefore reveal characteristic signatures that may be tested against future experimental measurements.

Spectroscopic and electromagnetic properties together with strong and weak decays maintain valuable information for future experimental and lattice QCD studies. Furthermore, theoretical and phenomenological studies may be helpful to get insights into the underlying structure and dynamics of these particles. From an experimental perspective, the search for bottom-charm pentaquarks is entering a promising era. The upgraded LHCb experiment, with its enhanced trigger and tracking capabilities, is poised to accumulate unprecedented statistics in heavy-flavor decays. Similarly, the Belle II experiment is collecting large datasets in
e^{+}e^{-}
collisions, which may provide complementary access to bottom and charm hadrons. The rich environment at these facilities offers multiple potential production mechanisms for bottom-charm pentaquarks, such as fragmentation in high-energy collisions or photoproduction reactions. Promising discovery channels involve searching for final states that contain a
B_{c}
meson accompanied by light hadrons, or signatures like
J/\psi\Lambda_{b}
and
D^{(*)}\Sigma_{b}
, which balance reconstruction feasibility with expected branching fractions. Although no bottom-charm pentaquark has been reported to date, the experimental tools and datasets now available might make their discovery a realistic near-term goal. Theoretical guidance on their expected masses, widths, and distinguishing properties—such as magnetic moments—is essential to sharpen these searches and, should a candidate be found, to elucidate its internal structure.

In this work, we focus specifically on bottom–anticharm (and charm–antibottom) pentaquarks within the molecular model, concentrating on the two distinct SU(3)

8_{1f}

This framework provides a well-defined and computationally tractable approach for constructing pentaquark wave functions while respecting color confinement and Pauli statistics. In this picture, open bottom–charm pentaquarks are described as hadronic molecules composed of a singly heavy baryon and heavy–light meson, namely
(bqq)(\bar{c}q)

b\bar{c}

(cqq)(\bar{b}q)
for the
c\bar{b}

II
Wave Functions

The electromagnetic structure of a hadron is determined by the internal configuration of its constituent quarks. For the open–flavor molecular
pentaquarks considered in this work, the total wave function factorizes into flavor, spin, color, and spatial components,

\displaystyle\Psi=\psi_{\mathrm{flavor}}\otimes\chi_{\mathrm{spin}}\otimes\xi_{\mathrm{color}}\otimes\eta_{\mathrm{space}}.

(2)

Fermi-Dirac statistics require the full wave function to be antisymmetric under exchange of identical light quarks. In the molecular picture, this
constraint is already satisfied within each constituent hadron. The baryon
(Qqq)
contains two identical light quarks (in some flavor configurations),
and its color-flavor-spin wavefunction is appropriately antisymmetrized. The meson
(q\bar{Q}^{\prime})
contains no identical quarks. When these two
color-singlet hadrons bind to form a molecule, there is no additional antisymmetrization required across different hadrons, as the quarks are
confined to distinct spatial regions. The spatial part
\eta_{\mathrm{space}}
for the ground-state S-wave molecule is symmetric, and the color
wavefunction
\xi_{\mathrm{color}}
is simply the product of the two individual color singlets, which is overall symmetric.

In the molecular interpretation, open–bottom–charm pentaquarks arise from a singly heavy baryon and a heavy–light meson of the form

(Qqq)(\bar{Q}^{\prime}q)
, with
(Q,Q^{\prime})=(b,c)
or
(c,b)
. The flavor structure of the singly heavy baryon is governed by the SU(3) flavor symmetry of
the two light quarks. Symmetric light–diquark configurations,

\displaystyle\{q_{1}q_{2}\}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q_{1}q_{2}+q_{2}q_{1}),

(3)

belong to the sextet representation
6_{f}
, while antisymmetric diquarks,

\displaystyle[q_{1}q_{2}]=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(q_{1}q_{2}-q_{2}q_{1}),

(4)

belong to the antitriplet representation
\bar{3}_{f}
. Combining the baryon with a heavy antimeson
(q\bar{Q}^{\prime})
in the
3_{f}
representation generates

\displaystyle 6_{f}\otimes 3_{f}

\displaystyle=10_{f}\oplus 8_{1f},

(5)

\displaystyle\bar{3}_{f}\otimes 3_{f}

\displaystyle=8_{2f}\oplus 1_{f},

(6)

yielding the overall three–light–quark decomposition

\displaystyle 3\otimes 3\otimes 3=1\oplus 8_{1}\oplus 8_{2}\oplus 10.

(7)

Thus, two distinct SU(3) flavor octets arise:
8_{1f}
built from symmetric diquarks and
8_{2f}
built from antisymmetric diquarks. These
octets provide the dominant classification for open–flavor molecular pentaquarks.

8_{2f}

([ds]c\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow-\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow))(s\bar{b}\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\uparrow\downarrow-\downarrow\uparrow))

The spin structure follows from the quantum numbers of the constituents. Baryons built from a symmetric light-diquark configuration exist with
both
J_{B}^{P_{B}}=\frac{1}{2}^{+}
and
\frac{3}{2}^{+}
, whereas those built from an antisymmetric light-diquark configuration are restricted to

J_{B}^{P_{B}}=\frac{1}{2}^{+}
. The heavy-light mesons
(q\bar{Q}^{\prime})
appear with spin-parity
J_{M}^{P_{M}}=0^{-}
and
1^{-}
. For S–wave
molecules, the total spin–parity is obtained from

\displaystyle J=S_{B}\oplus S_{M},\qquad P=P_{B}P_{M},

(8)

leading to the lowest configurations

\displaystyle J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-}(\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}),\qquad J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-}(\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}),\qquad J^{P}=\frac{3}{2}^{-}(\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}).

(9)

The SU(3)
f
spin–flavor wave functions of the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
pentaquark octets are listed in Tables
1
and
2
. Each state is presented both at the hadronic level, as a molecular combination of a baryon and a meson, and at the quark level, where the structure is expressed in terms of the symmetric
\{q_{1}q_{2}\}
and antisymmetric
[q_{1}q_{2}]
light-diquark configurations defined above. The origin of the two octets can be understood from SU(3)
f
decomposition. The
8_{1f}
representation is obtained from the
combination
{6}_{f}\otimes{3}_{f}
, corresponding to symmetric light-diquark configurations, while the
8_{2f}
representation arises from
{\bar{3}}_{f}\otimes{3}_{f}
, associated with antisymmetric light-diquark structures. All wave functions are constructed consistently within this framework,
and the orthogonality of the octet states follows directly from the underlying Clebsch–Gordan algebra.

The molecular assignment adopted in this work is supported by dynamical calculations in the literature. Within the coupled-channel unitary framework based on the local hidden-gauge approach, Ref.
Lin et al. 2024
demonstrated that meson–baryon interactions in the
\bar{c}bqqq
and
b\bar{c}qqq
systems can dynamically generate bound states near the relevant baryon–meson thresholds, providing direct theoretical evidence for loosely bound molecular states in precisely the configurations studied here. Independently, Ref.
Wang and Long 2025
identified fourteen S-wave molecular
pentaquark candidates in the
QQqqs
systems using the extended local hidden-gauge formalism, including states with bottom–charm content, and found their predicted masses to lie in close proximity to the corresponding baryon–meson thresholds. The near-threshold nature of these states is the standard criterion for a molecular interpretation, and the present work takes these dynamical results as motivation for treating the open bottom–charm pentaquarks as S-wave hadronic molecules in computing their electromagnetic properties. Prior to the LHCb observation, the possibility of molecular states composed of a heavy baryon and a heavy meson had already been predicted within the one-boson-exchange model in Ref.
Yang et al. 2012
.

III
Magnetic Moments

In this section we compute the magnetic moments of the open–bottom–anticharm (
b\bar{c}
) and open–charm–antibottom (
c\bar{b}
) molecular pentaquarks within the constituent quark model. This approach has been widely applied to describe electromagnetic properties of heavy hadronic molecules, providing a transparent and intuitive framework for estimating magnetic moments from quark degrees of freedom. In general, the magnetic moment of a hadron receives contributions from both the intrinsic spin of its constituent quarks and from any orbital motion:

\mu=\mu_{\mathrm{spin}}+\mu_{\mathrm{orbital}}.

(10)

For the S-wave molecular pentaquarks considered here, the baryon and meson constituents are in a relative
L=0
state; consequently the orbital
magnetic moment vanishes and only the spin contribution remains.

At the quark level, the spin magnetic moment operator is

\hat{\mu}_{\mathrm{spin}}=\sum_{i}\frac{Q_{i}}{2M_{i}}\,\hat{\sigma}_{i},

(11)

where
Q_{i}
,
M_{i}
and
\hat{\sigma}_{i}
denote the electric charge (in units of
e
), constituent mass and Pauli spin operator of the
i
th
quark. The elementary quark magnetic moments are given by the diagonal matrix elements

\displaystyle\langle q\uparrow|\hat{\mu}_{z}|q\uparrow\rangle

\displaystyle=+\frac{Q_{q}}{2M_{q}},

(12)

\displaystyle\langle q\downarrow|\hat{\mu}_{z}|q\downarrow\rangle

\displaystyle=-\frac{Q_{q}}{2M_{q}}.

(13)

Here
\hat{\mu}_{z}
, the
z
-component of this operator, yields the magnetic moment
\mu=\langle\hat{\mu}_{z}\rangle
when evaluated in a
state with maximum spin projection.

In the molecular picture, an S-wave pentaquark is expressed as a coherent superposition of baryon–meson channels. Its magnetic moment is
therefore the sum of the magnetic moments of the constituent hadrons:

\hat{\mu}=\hat{\mu}_{B}+\hat{\mu}_{M},

(14)

where
\hat{\mu}_{B}
and
\hat{\mu}_{M}
are the magnetic moment operators of the baryon and meson, respectively. Hence, once the magnetic moments of the individual singly-heavy baryons and anti-heavy mesons are known, the magnetic moments of the complete pentaquark octets follow directly.

To illustrate the quark-model calculation, we consider the antisymmetric bottom baryon
\Lambda_{b}^{0}\equiv[ud]b
with
J^{P}=\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}
. Its spin–flavor wave function for
S_{z}=+\tfrac{1}{2}
is

|\Lambda_{b}^{0},\,S_{z}=+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(ud-du)b\otimes\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\bigl(\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow-\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow\bigr).

(15)

Applying the spin magnetic moment operator

\hat{\mu}_{z}=\sum_{i}(Q_{i}/2M_{i})\,\sigma_{z}^{(i)}
to this state yields

\displaystyle\mu_{\Lambda_{b}^{0}}

\displaystyle=\langle\Lambda_{b}^{0},\,+\tfrac{1}{2}|\hat{\mu}_{z}|\Lambda_{b}^{0},\,+\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\Bigl[(\mu_{u}+\mu_{d}-\mu_{b})\bigl(\langle\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow|\sigma_{z}^{(1)}|\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\rangle+\langle\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow|\sigma_{z}^{(2)}|\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\rangle+\langle\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow|\sigma_{z}^{(3)}|\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow\rangle\bigr)

\displaystyle\qquad+\;\text{contributions from the other spin component}\Bigr].

(16)

Evaluating the spin matrix elements gives the compact result

\mu_{\Lambda_{b}^{0}}=\mu_{b},

(17)

where
\mu_{q}\equiv Q_{q}/(2M_{q})
is the magnetic moment of a quark with flavor
q
. This reflects the fact that in an antisymmetric light-diquark
configuration
[ud]
, the two light quarks couple to spin zero, so the baryon magnetic moment is carried entirely by the heavy
b
quark.

As a concrete illustration, consider the neutral open bottom–charm molecular pentaquark belonging to the
8_{1f}
representation. Its
quark-level flavor wave function reads

P_{b\bar{c}}^{2}(8_{1f})=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,\{ud\}b\,d\bar{c}-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,\{dd\}b\,u\bar{c}.

(18)

For the S-wave configuration with
J^{P}=\tfrac{1}{2}^{-}(\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-})
, the meson carries zero spin and the total angular momentum is entirely provided by the baryon. Using the explicit quark spin wave functions given in Table
1
, the spin–flavor state with

M=+\tfrac{1}{2}
can be written as

\displaystyle\left|P^{2}_{b\bar{c}}(8_{1f}),\,J=\tfrac{1}{2},\,M=+\tfrac{1}{2}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sqrt{\frac{1}{3}}\,\Big[(\{ud\}b)\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow+\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow-2\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow)\Big]\otimes\Big[(d\bar{c})\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\uparrow\downarrow-\downarrow\uparrow)\Big]

\displaystyle\quad-\sqrt{\frac{2}{3}}\,\Big[(\{dd\}b)\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{6}}(\uparrow\downarrow\uparrow+\downarrow\uparrow\uparrow-2\uparrow\uparrow\downarrow)\Big]\otimes\Big[(u\bar{c})\otimes\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{2}}(\uparrow\downarrow-\downarrow\uparrow)\Big].

(19)

The magnetic moment is obtained from the expectation value of
\hat{\mu}_{z}=\sum_{i}\hat{\mu}_{i}
, where the sum runs over all constituent quarks and antiquarks. In this configuration, the meson does not contribute through total spin, and its contribution reduces to that of the constituent antiquark.

Evaluating the operator on the explicit spin basis above, one finds

\displaystyle\mu\bigl[P_{b\bar{c}}^{2}(8_{1f})\bigr]

\displaystyle=\frac{4}{3}\mu_{d}-\frac{1}{3}\mu_{b}+\frac{1}{3}\mu_{d}+\frac{2}{3}\mu_{u}+\mu_{\bar{c}},

(20)

in agreement with the expression used in the numerical analysis. Substituting the constituent quark magnetic moments (in units of the
nuclear magneton
\mu_{N}
) yields the numerical prediction for this state.

IV
Results and Discussion

We use the following constituent quark masses for numerical analysis:
m_{u}=m_{d}=361.8
MeV,
m_{s}=540.4
MeV,
m_{c}=1724.8
MeV, and

m_{b}=5052.9
MeV
Wu et al. 2017
. The results are presented in Tables
3
for
b\bar{c}
octet states and
4
for
c\bar{b}
octet states. The magnetic moments of the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
octet molecular pentaquark families exhibit a remarkably rich and systematic structure. When analyzed in the context of the two distinct SU(3)
f
flavor representations,
8_{1f}
and
8_{2f}
, a clear narrative emerges that links the electromagnetic properties directly to the underlying quark dynamics, spin coupling, and the ordering of the heavy quarks. This comprehensive dataset reveals that the magnetic moment is not merely a static property but a highly sensitive probe capable of discriminating between different internal configurations and symmetry realizations.

IV.1
Pseudoscalar channel:
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}
,
J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-}

The
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}
configuration demonstrates a fundamental dichotomy between the two flavor representations. In the
8_{2f}

representation, a near-perfect universality is observed. Every
b\bar{c}
state from
P_{b\bar{c}}^{1}
through
P_{b\bar{c}}^{8}
possesses an identical
magnetic moment of
\mu=-0.062\,\mu_{N}
, and every
c\bar{b}
state shares the value
\mu=+0.362\,\mu_{N}
. This constancy is a direct and powerful consequence of the antisymmetric nature of the light-diquark in the
8_{2f}
wave function. In this configuration, the two light quarks are forced into a spin-singlet state, effectively silencing their contribution to the magnetic moment. Furthermore, the pseudoscalar meson
partner carries zero spin. Consequently, the magnetic moment of the entire molecular system is almost entirely dictated by the single heavy quark
(or antiquark) residing within the baryon component. The sign reversal between the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
families encodes the change in the
dominant heavy constituent electric charge, while the magnitude difference reflects the inverse mass scaling: the charm quark larger magnetic
moment due to its lower mass dominates in the
c\bar{b}
case. This provides an exceptionally clean electromagnetic signature for any state
belonging to the
8_{2f}
molecular multiplet, making these predictions particularly robust and testable.

The behavior of the same
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}
configurations in the
8_{1f}
representation is markedly different. Here the light diquark
is in a symmetric spin-triplet configuration, allowing the light quarks to contribute actively to the total magnetic moment. As a result, the predicted values span a broad range including both positive and negative magnetic moments that depend sensitively on the flavor composition of each
state. For the
b\bar{c}
sector, the values vary from large positive
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{1})=+1.749\,\mu_{N}
and
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{3})=+1.812\,\mu_{N}
,
to moderate positive
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{4})=+0.372\,\mu_{N}
, and to negative values
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{2})=-0.555\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{6})=-1.068\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{7})=-0.238\,\mu_{N}
, and
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{8})=-0.814\,\mu_{N}
.
The state
P_{b\bar{c}}^{5}
yields a comparatively small value
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{5})=-0.077\,\mu_{N}
, reflecting a partial cancellation
among contributions from different quark components. A similar pattern appears in the
c\bar{b}
sector:
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{1})=+1.607\,\mu_{N}
,

\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{3})=+1.671\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{4})=+0.231\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{2})=-0.697\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{6})=-1.210\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{7})=-0.380\,\mu_{N}
,
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{8})=-0.956\,\mu_{N}
, and
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{5})=-0.218\,\mu_{N}
. This wide spread of values originates from the interplay among the magnetic moments of the
u
,
d
, and
s
quarks in the symmetric light-diquark configuration together with the heavy quark contributions. Since
\mu_{q}=Q_{q}/(2m_{q})
, the different masses and charges of the
b
and
c
quarks directly produce quantitative differences between the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
sectors, and the
8_{1f}
representation consequently displays a much richer structure than the nearly universal pattern of the
8_{2f}
case.

IV.2
Vector channel:
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}
,
J^{P}=\frac{1}{2}^{-}

The introduction of a vector meson through the
\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}
configuration significantly enriches the observed patterns and highlights the critical role of spin coupling. The magnetic moments show substantial deviations from their pseudoscalar counterparts, often involving sign changes that reflect destructive interference between the baryon and vector-meson spin contributions.

A representative example is the state
P_{b\bar{c}}^{1}
. Its magnetic
moment changes from

\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{1})=+1.749\,\mu_{N}

in the
\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 0^{-}
channel to

\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{1})=-0.824\,\mu_{N}

in the
\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}
channel within the
8_{1f}

representation. In contrast, in the
8_{2f}
representation the same
configuration yields

\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{1})=+0.931\,\mu_{N}
.
This demonstrates that the underlying flavor symmetry can qualitatively alter, and even reverse, the interference pattern that governs the magnetic moment. Such large shifts and sign reversals are a generic feature across both the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
octets.

A comparison of corresponding states further illustrates the interplay between flavor structure and spin coupling. For the pair
P_{b\bar{c}}^{2}
and
P_{c\bar{b}}^{2}
, one finds
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{2})=+0.519\,\mu_{N}
and
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{2})=+0.273\,\mu_{N}
for the
J=\tfrac{1}{2}
states in the
8_{1f}
representation, indicating similar constructive spin alignment. However, their
J=\tfrac{3}{2}
moments differ noticeably:
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{2})=-0.054\,\mu_{N}
, while
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{2})=-0.635\,\mu_{N}
, showing that the heavy-quark content significantly affects higher-spin configurations.

The pair
P_{b\bar{c}}^{3}
and
P_{c\bar{b}}^{3}
exhibits a consistent pattern across both spin sectors. For
J=\tfrac{1}{2}
, the magnetic moments are negative,
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{3})=-0.719\,\mu_{N}
and
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{3})=-0.965\,\mu_{N}
, while for
J=\tfrac{3}{2}
they become positive,

\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{3})=+1.640\,\mu_{N}
and
\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{3})=+1.671\,\mu_{N}
. This behavior indicates a common spin-coupling mechanism in which the higher-spin configuration favors constructive alignment of the constituent magnetic moments despite the interchange of heavy quarks. The states
P_{b\bar{c}}^{6}
and
P_{c\bar{b}}^{6}
are distinguished by their large magnitudes across different configurations, reflecting a
flavor structure that enhances additive contributions. In particular, the
J=\tfrac{3}{2}
state of
P_{b\bar{c}}^{6}
reaches
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{6})=-2.104\,\mu_{N}
in the
8_{1f}
representation, one of the largest values in the spectrum.

IV.3
Vector channel:
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}
,
J^{P}=\frac{3}{2}^{-}

The
J^{P}=\tfrac{3}{2}^{-}
states, originating from maximal spin alignment in the
\tfrac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes 1^{-}
coupling, systematically exhibit the
largest magnetic moments in magnitude across both SU(3)
f
representations and both heavy-quark sectors. This enhancement follows
from the coherent addition of spin contributions from the baryon and the vector meson.

The effect is particularly pronounced in the
c\bar{b}
sector, where the charm quark carries a comparatively larger intrinsic magnetic moment.
A representative example is the state
P_{c\bar{b}}^{1}
, for which

\displaystyle\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{1})=+2.533\,\mu_{N}

(21)

in the
8_{1f}
representation for
J=\tfrac{3}{2}
, making it one of the largest magnetic moments in the spectrum.

This behavior is generic, indicating that the
J=\tfrac{3}{2}
states are the most electromagnetically enhanced configurations and therefore the
most favorable candidates for observation in electromagnetic processes. The dependence on the flavor representation remains significant. For
instance, the state
P_{c\bar{b}}^{6}
shows a pronounced variation between
the two octets:

\displaystyle\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{6})

\displaystyle=-0.957\,\mu_{N}\quad(8_{1f}),

(22)

\displaystyle\mu(P_{c\bar{b}}^{6})

\displaystyle=+2.153\,\mu_{N}\quad(8_{2f}).

(23)

The large magnitude difference and the sign reversal clearly demonstrate the sensitivity of magnetic moments to the underlying flavor structure.
Such behavior provides a powerful diagnostic tool for distinguishing between the
8_{1f}
and
8_{2f}
assignments of experimentally observed
states.

IV.4
Theoretical implications

The comprehensive set of results carries several important theoretical implications. First, the dramatic and systematic differences between the
predictions of the
8_{1f}
and
8_{2f}
representations establish the magnetic moment as a particularly useful observable for distinguishing
between different flavor structures in molecular pentaquarks. A sufficiently reliable measurement could therefore provide direct
information on the underlying spin–flavor wave function. Second, the nontrivial differences between the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
families
across all spin-parity configurations constitute explicit evidence of heavy-quark flavor symmetry breaking in electromagnetic observables.
Since
\mu\propto Q/m
, interchanging the roles of the
b
and
c
quarks between the baryon and meson components modifies the relative
contributions of each quark sector and changes the interference pattern among spin and flavor components in a way not captured by simple symmetry arguments. The sign change of the universal
8_{2f}
pseudoscalar moment between families — from
\mu\approx-0.062\,\mu_{N}
for
b\bar{c}
to
\mu\approx+0.362\,\mu_{N}
for
c\bar{b}
— is the cleanest manifestation of this breaking, encoding directly the difference in sign
and magnitude of
\mu_{b}
and
\mu_{c}
arising from their different charges and masses. Third, the consistent and sizable splitting between the

J=1/2
and
J=3/2
magnetic moments originating from the same hadronic configuration provides a potentially useful indicator for spin
determination. If an electromagnetic observable related to the magnetic moment can be constrained experimentally, it may serve as a complementary
tool to angular distribution analyses
Jacob and Wick 1959
;
Aaij et al. 2025
;
Kang et al. 2010
;
Kang et al. 2011
;
Charles et al. 2010
in assigning the spin of a newly discovered pentaquark state. Fourth, the near-vanishing magnetic moment of
P_{b\bar{c}}^{5}
in
the
8_{1f}
pseudoscalar channel,
\mu(P_{b\bar{c}}^{5})=-0.077\,\mu_{N}
, reflects a fine balance between the light- and heavy-quark contributions
within the adopted constituent quark mass scheme.

From an experimental perspective, while the direct measurement of a static magnetic moment for a short-lived pentaquark state is currently
beyond reach, several indirect avenues are conceivable. The most direct connection is through radiative decay widths: the partial width for a
radiative transition between two pentaquark states of different hadronic configuration is proportional to the cube of the photon energy and the
square of the transition magnetic moment
Wang et al. 2019
. We note that the diagonal magnetic moments tabulated here are insufficient for
this purpose, as radiative widths require off-diagonal transition matrix elements between states of different spin-parity configuration; a
dedicated calculation of these transition moments is left for future work. Additionally, the production mechanisms of pentaquarks in high-energy
collisions, particularly in polarized processes, could exhibit asymmetries sensitive to their electromagnetic structure. Future high-luminosity
experiments at LHCb, Belle II, and the forthcoming Electron-Ion Collider will be essential for pursuing these challenging measurements. The
predictions tabulated here provide a concrete and detailed set of benchmarks against which such future data can be compared, offering a
clear roadmap for testing the molecular nature and internal symmetry structure of these exotic hadrons.

V
Final Remarks

The systematic calculation of magnetic moments for the open heavy–flavor molecular pentaquark octets in the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
sectors provides a rich and coherent set of theoretical predictions that illuminate the intricate interplay between flavor symmetry, spin coupling,
and heavy–quark dynamics. Within the constituent quark model framework and employing explicitly constructed SU(3)
f
wave functions for the two distinct representations,
8_{1f}
and
8_{2f}
, we have identified several robust and physically transparent patterns that can serve as valuable benchmarks for future theoretical and experimental studies.

One of the most striking outcomes of this work is the pronounced dichotomy between the two flavor representations. In the
8_{2f}
representation, the magnetic moments of all
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\otimes\,0^{-}
states display near–universal values,
\mu=-0.062\,\mu_{N}
for the entire
b\bar{c}
octet and
\mu=+0.362\,\mu_{N}
for the
c\bar{b}
octet. This universality is a direct manifestation of the antisymmetric light–diquark structure: the light quarks couple to a spin–singlet configuration and therefore contribute negligibly, leaving the magnetic moment dominated by the heavy quark (or antiquark) residing in the baryon. In contrast, the
8_{1f}
representation, built from symmetric light–diquarks, exhibits a broad
distribution of magnetic moments spanning both positive and negative values.

The inclusion of vector meson components through
\frac{1}{2}^{+}\!\otimes\!1^{-}
configurations introduces pronounced spin–dependent effects.
In many cases, these lead to large deviations and even sign reversals relative to the pseudoscalar configurations. A particularly robust feature is that the
J^{P}=3/2^{-}
states systematically possess the largest magnetic moments in magnitude. This behavior can be traced to the coherent alignment of baryon and vector–meson spins in the maximal–spin configuration, making the high–spin states especially promising candidates for
experimental access via electromagnetic processes such as radiative transitions.

The theoretical implications of these findings are multifaceted. First, the stark contrast between the
8_{1f}
and
8_{2f}
predictions establishes the magnetic moment as a powerful discriminator for probing the internal flavor structure of molecular pentaquarks. Second, the nontrivial differences observed between the
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
families provide clear evidence of heavy–quark flavor symmetry breaking in electromagnetic observables. Since magnetic moments scale inversely with the constituent mass, the interchange of heavy quark roles between the baryon and meson components leads to distinct interference patterns that are not captured by simple symmetry arguments. Moreover, the consistent splitting between the
J=1/2
and
J=3/2
magnetic moments arising from the same hadronic configuration suggests a novel and potentially valuable pathway for experimental spin determination.

From an experimental standpoint, although direct measurements of static magnetic moments for short–lived pentaquark states remain extremely challenging, several indirect avenues are conceivable. Radiative decay widths, which are proportional to the square of the transition magnetic moment, as well as production asymmetries in polarized collisions, may offer access to these electromagnetic properties. The upgraded LHCb experiment, Belle II, and the future Electron–Ion Collider are well positioned to accumulate the high–precision data required for such analyses.
In this context, the comprehensive set of predictions presented in this work provides concrete and quantitative benchmarks against which future measurements can be compared.

In conclusion, the magnetic moments of
b\bar{c}
and
c\bar{b}
molecular pentaquarks are far more than numerical byproducts of a specific model.
They encode fundamental information about spin–flavor correlations, heavy–quark dynamics, and the composite structure of exotic hadrons.
As experimental searches for open heavy–flavor pentaquarks intensify, the results reported here will serve as an essential diagnostic tool for unraveling the internal anatomy of these systems and for advancing our understanding of nonperturbative QCD in the heavy–quark sector.
