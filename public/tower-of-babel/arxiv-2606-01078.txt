Non-Vacuous Certification of Transport MCMC via Oscillation-Controlled Normalizing Flows
Jun Hu junhu22@whut.edu.cn

Abstract
Transport MCMC trains a normalizing flow to precondition Metropolis–Hastings proposals, achieving high empirical efficiency on challenging posteriors; yet no prior work produces a numerically non-vacuous, rigorous spectral-gap bound for such samplers. We establish the first such bounds. For independence MH on the banana family we certify γ ∗ = 0.828 \gamma^{*}=0.828 at D = 2 D=2 (covering in the original space) and γ ∗ ≥ × 10 − 4 \gamma^{*}\geq 7.6\!\times\!10^{-4} at D = 5 D=5 (covering in an analytically unwarped Gaussian space with a grid-certified gradient bound under the stated numerical Lipschitz certification), both rigorous at 95 % 95\% confidence. The framework rests on three pillars: (i) spectral normalization with reduced scale clips constrains the flow Lipschitz constant from 10 47 10^{47} to 10 4 10^{4} ; (ii) a coverage-based empirical oscillation bound replaces the vacuous analytical bound with a data-dependent certificate; and (iii) oscillation-regularised training cuts the empirical oscillation by 60 60 – 90 % 90\% at no cost to density fit, extending practical certificates through D = 20 D=20 ( γ ∗ ≥ × 10 − 4 \gamma^{*}\geq 1.7\!\times\!10^{-4} ). Tests on four further targets (Gaussian mixture, shear-building, Neal’s funnel, Bayesian logistic regression) identify three precise barriers: boundary curvature, target stiffness, and tail-coverage mismatch. An affine-vs-spline comparison shows that simpler architectures yield tighter certificates at identical NLL, inverting the usual expressiveness hierarchy.

Introduction

Markov chain Monte Carlo (MCMC) remains the dominant paradigm for
Bayesian computation in science and engineering, yet its practical
efficiency hinges on how quickly the chain explores the target
distribution
\pi
. When
\pi
is high-dimensional, multimodal, or
strongly correlated, standard random-walk proposals mix slowly and
reliable convergence diagnostics become difficult to
obtain
[
Roberts and Rosenthal 2004
]
.

A promising remedy is
transport MCMC
: one trains a normalizing
flow
T_{\phi}
to approximate
\pi
, then uses
T_{\phi}
to precondition
the Metropolis–Hastings proposal, effectively straightening the
geometry of the target so that a simple random walk in the latent
space translates into an efficient proposal in the original
space
[
Parno and Marzouk 2018
,
Hoffman et al. 2019
,
Gabrié et al. 2022
,
Marzouk et al. 2016
]
. Empirically,
transport-preconditioned samplers achieve high acceptance rates and
low autocorrelation even on challenging posteriors; theoretically,
however,
no existing work has produced a non-vacuous,
numerically meaningful bound on the spectral gap
of such a sampler.

The spectral gap
\gamma
of an MCMC kernel controls its geometric
convergence rate: after
t
steps the total variation distance from
stationarity decays as
(1-\gamma)^{t}
. For transport MCMC, the
spectral gap depends on the
oscillation
of the log-density
ratio
\log(\pi/q_{\phi})
, where
q_{\phi}
is the flow-induced proposal
density
[
Tierney and Mira 1999
]
. Bounding this oscillation requires
bounding the Lipschitz constant of the composite map
T_{\phi}
—a
product of per-layer Lipschitz constants that, for unconstrained
normalizing flows, grows exponentially with depth and is
astronomically large in practice (e.g.,
>10^{47}
for a six-layer
RealNVP; see Section
4
). This renders all
existing analytical bounds trivially infinite.

In this paper we attack the problem from two complementary
directions.

Contribution 1: Lipschitz-constrained normalizing flows.

We apply spectral normalization
[
Miyato et al. 2018
]
to every
linear layer in the scale and shift sub-networks of
RealNVP
[
Dinh et al. 2017
]
, combined with a soft scale clip

s(x)=c\tanh(\cdot)
. We prove a per-layer Lipschitz bound
(Theorem
3
) showing that the forward-map
Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})
drops from
O(10^{47})
to

O(10^{2\text{--}4})
—an improvement of over forty orders of
magnitude—with negligible impact on transport quality (acceptance
rate, effective sample size).

Contribution 2: Diagnosis of the analytical bound.

Despite this dramatic reduction, we show that the analytical
oscillation bound from
Hu 2026
remains vacuous on every tested
target. We trace the cause to a structural bottleneck: the bound
contains the multiplicative term

L_{U}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})\cdot 2R
, where
L_{U}
is the Lipschitz
constant of the target score and
R
is the support radius. Even at

\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})=1
(the theoretical minimum for a non-trivial flow),
this term alone exceeds
25
for a two-dimensional banana target and

3{,}000
for an eight-dimensional shear building—far too large for
the exponentiated bound
\delta^{*}=\exp(\cdot)
to be finite.
We provide a complete decomposition table quantifying each term’s
contribution (Section
4
).

Contribution 3: Empirical oscillation bounds.

To break through the analytical barrier, we develop a

coverage-based empirical oscillation bound

(Theorem
13
).
Given
n
posterior samples forming a probabilistic
\varepsilon
-net
of the HPD credible set (whose smooth boundary we exploit via the
implicit function theorem), we bound the true oscillation by the
sample oscillation plus a Lipschitz correction
2M_{\mathcal{K}}\varepsilon^{*}
.
At
D=2
, the covering radius
\varepsilon^{*}
satisfies the
curvature condition
c_{2}\,\kappa_{\max}\,\varepsilon^{*}=0.523<1
,
making the bound
fully rigorous
:
\gamma^{*}=0.828
at

95\%
confidence under the independence MH kernel
(Theorem
1
), with
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
and
M_{\mathcal{K}}

independently certified and
\pi_{\min}
taken from the
banana-analytic density floor (exact, since
Z=(2\pi)^{d/2}
).
At
D=5
, an analytic shear chart maps the banana HPD set to a
Gaussian ball, yielding a charted, grid-certified rigorous certificate
(
\gamma^{*}\geq 7.6\!\times\!10^{-4}
) under the stated numerical
Lipschitz certification via the same covering framework applied in
the unwarped space (Proposition
19
).

Contribution 4: Oscillation-regularised training.

We propose adding the batch oscillation of
\log(\pi/q_{\phi})
as a
penalty during flow training. This reduces the empirical oscillation
by
60
–
90\%
at no cost to density-fit quality, extending
practical (non-rigorous) certificates through
D=20
.

Contribution 5: Architecture comparison and barrier
taxonomy.

A comparison of RealNVP and neural spline flows reveals that simpler
architectures yield tighter certificates despite identical NLL,
inverting the usual expressiveness hierarchy. Experiments on five
target families identify three barriers to non-vacuous certification:
boundary curvature (dimension), target stiffness (
L_{U}
), and
tail-coverage mismatch.

Background

2.1
Transport-preconditioned MCMC

Let
\pi(x)\propto\exp(-U(x))
be a target density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
potential
U
. A normalizing flow
T_{\phi}:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a
learnable diffeomorphism
[
Rezende and Mohamed 2015
,
Papamakarios et al. 2021
]

that maps a simple base distribution

p_{Z}
(typically
\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
) to an approximation
q_{\phi}
of
\pi

via the change-of-variables formula

q_{\phi}(x)=p_{Z}\bigl(T_{\phi}(x)\bigr)\,|\!\det J_{T_{\phi}}(x)|,

(1)

where
J_{T_{\phi}}(x)=\partial T_{\phi}/\partial x
is the Jacobian.
One trains
\phi
by minimising the reverse
KL divergence
\mathrm{KL}(q_{\phi}\|\pi)
or, equivalently,
maximising the evidence lower bound on samples from
\pi
.

In
transport MCMC

[
Parno and Marzouk 2018
]
, the trained flow serves
as a proposal mechanism for a Metropolis–Hastings (MH) kernel.
In the
independence
variant, the sampler proposes
z^{\prime}\sim p_{Z}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})
independently of the current state, maps to the
data space
x^{\prime}=T_{\phi}^{-1}(z^{\prime})
, and accepts or rejects via the
MH ratio

\alpha(x_{t},x^{\prime})=\min\!\left(1,\;\frac{\pi(x^{\prime})\,q_{\phi}(x_{t})}{\pi(x_{t})\,q_{\phi}(x^{\prime})}\right)=\min\!\left(1,\;\exp\!\bigl(h(x_{t})-h(x^{\prime})\bigr)\right),

(2)

where
h(x)=\log\pi(x)-\log q_{\phi}(x)
is the log-density ratio.
When the flow is exact (
q_{\phi}=\pi
),
h
is constant, every
proposal is accepted, and the chain produces i.i.d. samples.
A random-walk variant (propose
z^{\prime}=z_{t}+\eta
,

\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
) is also common; we focus on the
independence kernel because it admits a self-contained spectral gap
bound (Theorem
1
below).

2.2
Spectral gap and oscillation

The convergence rate of a reversible MH chain is governed by the
spectral gap
\gamma
of its transition kernel
P
:

\gamma\;\mathrel{:=}\;1-\sup_{\begin{subarray}{c}f\in L^{2}(\pi)\\
\mathrm{Var}_{\pi}(f)>0\end{subarray}}\frac{\langle f,Pf\rangle_{\pi}}{\mathrm{Var}_{\pi}(f)}.

(3)

A positive spectral gap guarantees geometric ergodicity:

\|P^{t}(x,\cdot)-\pi\|_{\mathrm{TV}}\leq C(x)(1-\gamma)^{t}

for some function
C(x)
.

For transport-preconditioned independence MH, the spectral gap is
controlled by the oscillation of
h
over the effective support.
Specifically, let
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a compact set with

\pi(\mathcal{K})\geq 1-\alpha
, and define

\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\;\mathrel{:=}\;\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}h(x)-\inf_{x\in\mathcal{K}}h(x).

(4)

The following classical result connects oscillation to the spectral
gap of an independence sampler.

Theorem 1

(
Mengersen and Tweedie 1996
)
.

Let
P
be the transition kernel of the independence
Metropolis–Hastings sampler on
\mathcal{K}
with proposal density

q_{\phi}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{K})
(induced by the normalizing flow
T_{\phi}
)
and target
\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal{K})
. Then the spectral gap satisfies

\gamma\;\geq\;\frac{2}{1+\exp\!\bigl(\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\bigr)}.

(5)

Proof.

Define the importance weight
w(x)=\pi(x)/q_{\phi}(x)
and let

m=\inf_{x\in\mathcal{K}}w(x)
,
M=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}w(x)
.
The independence MH acceptance probability satisfies

\alpha(x,x^{\prime})=\min\!\left(1,\,\frac{w(x^{\prime})}{w(x)}\right)\geq\frac{m}{M}

for all
x,x^{\prime}\in\mathcal{K}
, since
w(x^{\prime})\geq m
and
w(x)\leq M
.
Therefore the kernel admits a
uniform minorization
:

P(x,A)=\int_{A}\alpha(x,x^{\prime})\,q_{\phi}(x^{\prime})\,dx^{\prime}\geq\frac{m}{M}\,q_{\phi}(A)\quad\text{for all }x\in\mathcal{K},\;A\subseteq\mathcal{K}.

(6)

Setting
\beta=m/M
and
\nu=q_{\phi}(\cdot\mid\mathcal{K})
,
the spectral gap of a uniformly minorized reversible chain
satisfies
\gamma\geq 2\beta/(1+\beta)

[
Liu 1996
, Theorem 2.1]
. Substituting

\beta=m/M
gives

\gamma\;\geq\;\frac{2\,m/M}{1+m/M}\;=\;\frac{2m}{m+M}\;=\;\frac{2}{1+M/m}.

Since
\log(M/m)=\sup_{\mathcal{K}}\log w-\inf_{\mathcal{K}}\log w=\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)
, we obtain (
5
).
∎

The independence MH sampler proposes
z^{\prime}\sim p_{Z}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{d})

independently of the current state, maps to

x^{\prime}=T_{\phi}^{-1}(z^{\prime})
, and accepts or rejects via the MH
ratio (
2
). When
q_{\phi}=\pi
,
\mathrm{osc}(h)=0

and
\gamma=1
(the chain produces i.i.d. samples).
The bound degrades exponentially with
\mathrm{osc}(h)
, so
even moderate oscillation (say,
\mathrm{osc}>20
) renders the bound
vacuous.

Remark 2

(RWMH variant)
.

The random-walk variant (propose

z^{\prime}=z+\eta
with
\eta\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\sigma^{2}I_{d})
) admits the
weaker perturbation bound

\gamma_{\phi}\geq e^{-3\,\mathrm{osc}(h)}\cdot\gamma_{0}

[
Hu 2026
]
, where
\gamma_{0}
is the spectral gap of an oracle
chain sampling from
\pi
directly. The independence-MH
bound (
5
) is self-contained (no
\gamma_{0}
)
and tighter, making it the natural choice for certification.
All spectral gap bounds reported in this paper use the independence
MH kernel.

2.3
Analytical oscillation bound from
Hu 2026

Hu 2026
derive an upper bound on
\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)
in terms of
the global Lipschitz constant of the flow:

\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)\;\leq\;L_{U}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})\cdot 2R\;+\;L_{dc}\;+\;\tfrac{R^{2}}{2},

(7)

where:

•

L_{U}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}}\|\nabla U(x)\|
is the Lipschitz
constant of the target score,

•

\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})
is the forward-map
Lipschitz constant (product of per-layer Lipschitz constants),

•

R
is the radius of the compact support
\mathcal{K}
, and

•

L_{dc}=L\cdot d_{B}\cdot c
is the Jacobian log-determinant
bound, with
L
layers,
d_{B}
the transformed-partition dimension,
and
c
the scale clip.

For a RealNVP flow with
L
affine coupling layers,

\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})=\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})
grows exponentially with
depth. Without architectural constraints, the per-layer Lipschitz
constants
\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})
are determined by the unconstrained spectral
norms of the sub-network weight matrices, which can be arbitrarily
large.

For completeness, we note that (
7
) follows from
the standard decomposition

h(x)=\log\pi(x)-\log p_{0}(T_{\phi}^{-1}(x))+\log|\!\det J_{T_{\phi}}(T_{\phi}^{-1}(x))|

together with subadditivity of
\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}
: the score term contributes
at most
L_{U}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})\cdot 2R
on a ball of radius
R

(post-composition with
T_{\phi}^{-1}
scales the diameter by

\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})
); the base log-density contributes at most
R^{2}/2
for
a standard Gaussian base on
\mathcal{K}
; and the log-Jacobian contributes
at most
L_{dc}
since each clipped affine coupling layer contributes
at most
d_{B}c
to
|\!\log\det J_{f_{l}}|
.

Hu 2026
acknowledge that this product is

“numerically vacuous”
for their six-layer, eight-dimensional
configuration:
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})>10^{47}
, yielding

\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h)>10^{50}
and
\gamma\approx 0
. They identify
spectral normalization and reduced scale clips as the path forward
—a direction we formalise and quantify in this paper.

2.4
RealNVP architecture

RealNVP
[
Dinh et al. 2017
]
constructs
T_{\phi}
as a composition
of
L
affine coupling layers. Each layer
f_{l}
partitions the input

x=(x_{A},x_{B})
and applies an element-wise affine transformation to

x_{B}
conditioned on
x_{A}
:

f_{l}(x)=\bigl(x_{A},\;x_{B}\odot\exp(s_{l}(x_{A}))+t_{l}(x_{A})\bigr),

(8)

where
s_{l},t_{l}:\mathbb{R}^{d_{A}}\to\mathbb{R}^{d_{B}}
are neural networks
(the
scale
and
shift
sub-networks, respectively) and

\odot
denotes element-wise multiplication. The partition alternates
between layers. The Jacobian is lower-triangular with diagonal

\exp(s_{l}(x_{A}))
, giving the tractable log-determinant

\log|\!\det J_{f_{l}}|=\sum_{j}[s_{l}(x_{A})]_{j}
.

The scale output is typically clipped:

s_{l}(x_{A})=c\cdot\tanh(\hat{s}_{l}(x_{A}))
for a clip bound
c>0
,
ensuring each diagonal entry lies in
[\exp(-c),\exp(c)]
. This
clip directly controls the Jacobian log-determinant bound

L_{dc}=L\cdot d_{B}\cdot c
appearing in (
7
).

2.5
Spectral normalization

Spectral normalization
[
Miyato et al. 2018
]
constrains the spectral
norm of a weight matrix
W
to a target value
\sigma_{\max}

(typically
1
) by reparametrising

W_{\mathrm{SN}}=\frac{\sigma_{\max}}{\sigma_{1}(W)}\,W,

(9)

where
\sigma_{1}(W)
is the largest singular value, estimated on-line
via power iteration. Applied to every linear layer in a
K
-layer
MLP with Lipschitz-
1
activations (e.g., tanh, ReLU), spectral
normalization ensures

\mathrm{Lip}(\text{MLP})\;\leq\;\sigma_{\max}^{K}.

(10)

Setting
\sigma_{\max}=1
gives
\mathrm{Lip}\leq 1
regardless of
depth—a property we exploit in Section
3
to
control the per-layer Lipschitz constants of RealNVP.

3
Lipschitz-Constrained RealNVP

We now show how spectral normalization, combined with a soft scale
clip, yields per-layer Lipschitz bounds that are both tight and
computable. The main result (Theorem
3
)
provides an analytical formula for
\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})
in terms of three
controllable quantities: the spectral norm target
\sigma_{\max}
,
the scale clip
c
, and the diameter of the transformed partition on
the compact support.

3.1
Architecture

We modify the standard RealNVP coupling layer (
8
)
in two ways:

1.

Spectral normalization.
Every
nn.Linear

layer in both the scale network
s_{l}
and the shift network
t_{l}

is spectrally normalized with target
\sigma_{\max}=1.0
, using
one power-iteration step during training and ten during final
bound evaluation.

2.

Soft scale clip.
The scale output uses

s_{l}(x_{A})=c\cdot\tanh(\hat{s}_{l}(x_{A}))
, where
\hat{s}_{l}
is
the raw (spectrally-normalized) sub-network. Since
\tanh
has
Lipschitz constant
1
, the composite scale network satisfies

\mathrm{Lip}(s_{l})\leq c\cdot\sigma_{\max}^{K}=c
when

\sigma_{\max}=1
.

The shift network has no output nonlinearity, so

\mathrm{Lip}(t_{l})\leq\sigma_{\max}^{K}=1
. All other architectural
choices (hidden widths, depth
K
, number of coupling layers
L
,
alternating masks) remain unchanged from the baseline.

3.2
Per-layer Lipschitz bound

Theorem 3

(Per-layer Lipschitz constant)
.

Let
f_{l}
be an affine coupling layer (
8
) with
scale clip
c
,
\mathrm{Lip}(s_{l})\leq\lambda_{s}
, and

\mathrm{Lip}(t_{l})\leq\lambda_{t}
. Suppose the input
x_{B}
component
satisfies
\|x_{B}\|\leq B
on the compact support
\mathcal{K}
. Then

\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})\;\leq\;\sqrt{1+e^{2c}+(e^{c}B\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{t})^{2}}.

(11)

Under the LCNF configuration
(
\sigma_{\max}=1
, soft clip
c\cdot\tanh
), this specialises to

\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l})\;\leq\;\sqrt{1+e^{2c}+(e^{c}Bc+1)^{2}}.

(12)

Proof.

Write
y=f_{l}(x)
with
y_{A}=x_{A}
and

y_{B}=x_{B}\odot\exp(s_{l}(x_{A}))+t_{l}(x_{A})
.
For two inputs
x,x^{\prime}
in
\mathcal{K}
, set

a=\|x_{A}-x^{\prime}_{A}\|
and
b=\|x_{B}-x^{\prime}_{B}\|
, so that

\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}=a^{2}+b^{2}
.

A
-partition.

\|y_{A}-y^{\prime}_{A}\|=a
.

B
-partition.

Adding and subtracting
x^{\prime}_{B}\odot e^{s_{l}(x_{A})}
:

y_{B}-y^{\prime}_{B}=\underbrace{(x_{B}-x^{\prime}_{B})\odot e^{s_{l}(x_{A})}}_{(I)}+\underbrace{x^{\prime}_{B}\odot\bigl(e^{s_{l}(x_{A})}-e^{s_{l}(x^{\prime}_{A})}\bigr)}_{(II)}+\underbrace{t_{l}(x_{A})-t_{l}(x^{\prime}_{A})}_{(III)}.

(13)

Term (I):

Since
|[s_{l}]_{j}|\leq c
, each entry of
e^{s_{l}}
is at most
e^{c}
,
so
\|(I)\|\leq e^{c}b
.

Term (II):

By the mean value theorem component-wise,

|e^{u}-e^{v}|\leq e^{c}|u-v|
for
u,v\in[-c,c]
.
Therefore
\|(II)\|\leq e^{c}\|x^{\prime}_{B}\|\cdot\|s_{l}(x_{A})-s_{l}(x^{\prime}_{A})\|\leq e^{c}B\lambda_{s}\,a
.

Term (III):

\|(III)\|\leq\lambda_{t}\,a
.

Combining by the triangle inequality:

\|y_{B}-y^{\prime}_{B}\|\;\leq\;e^{c}b+(e^{c}B\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{t})\,a\;\eqqcolon\;\beta\,b+\gamma\,a,

(14)

where
\beta=e^{c}
and
\gamma=e^{c}B\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{t}
.

Full bound.

By Cauchy–Schwarz,

(\beta b+\gamma a)^{2}\leq(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})(a^{2}+b^{2})
, so

\displaystyle\|f_{l}(x)-f_{l}(x^{\prime})\|^{2}

\displaystyle=a^{2}+\|y_{B}-y^{\prime}_{B}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq a^{2}+(\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})(a^{2}+b^{2})

\displaystyle\leq(1+\beta^{2}+\gamma^{2})\,\|x-x^{\prime}\|^{2}.

(15)

Substituting
\beta=e^{c}
and

\gamma=e^{c}B\lambda_{s}+\lambda_{t}

gives (
11
). Setting
\lambda_{s}=c
,

\lambda_{t}=1
yields (
12
).
∎

3.3
Forward-map Lipschitz constant

The full flow
T_{\phi}=f_{L}\circ\cdots\circ f_{1}
has Lipschitz
constant bounded by the product of per-layer bounds:

\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})\;\leq\;\prod_{l=1}^{L}\mathrm{Lip}(f_{l}).

(16)

Example 4

(Magnitude comparison)
.

Consider a six-layer RealNVP with
d=8
(
d_{B}=4
), two-hidden-layer
sub-networks (
K=3
linear layers), and
R=2.08

Remark 6

(Related work on Lipschitz flows)
.

Several works have studied Lipschitz control in normalizing

5
Empirical Oscillation with Concentration Guarantees

Sections 3 and 4 established that spectral normalization reduces the
forward-map Lipschitz constant
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})
by tens of orders of magnitude,
yet the analytical oscillation bound from
Hu 2026
remains vacuous due to
the multiplicative structure
L_{U}\cdot\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})\cdot 2R
. In this
section we take a fundamentally different approach: we
estimate
the
oscillation of the log-density ratio from posterior samples and provide a
high-probability certificate that the true oscillation exceeds the
empirical estimate by at most a controlled correction term.

5.1
Setup and notation

Let
\pi(x)=\exp(-U(x))/Z
denote the target density on
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
known normalising constant
Z
, and let

q_{\phi}
be the density induced by the trained normalizing flow
T_{\phi}
with
standard normal base
p_{Z}
. Define the log-density ratio

h(x)\;\mathrel{:=}\;\log r_{\phi}(x)\;=\;\log\pi(x)-\log q_{\phi}(x).

(17)

The oscillation of
h
on a set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
is

\mathrm{osc}_{S}(h)=\sup_{x\in S}h(x)-\inf_{x\in S}h(x)
.
The spectral gap of the
T_{\phi}
-preconditioned Metropolis–Hastings kernel
is controlled by
\delta^{*}=\exp(\mathrm{osc}_{\mathcal{K}}(h))
via the bound

\gamma\geq 2/(1+\delta^{*})

[
Mengersen and Tweedie 1996
]
.

We require three assumptions.

Assumption 7

(HPD credible set)
.

For
\alpha\in(0,1)
, define the highest posterior density (HPD)
level set

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:U(x)\leq u_{\alpha}\},

(18)

where
u_{\alpha}
is the
(1-\alpha)
-quantile of
U(X)
under

X\sim\pi
, so that
\pi(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})=1-\alpha
.
Assume
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is compact with diameter

D\mathrel{:=}\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})<\infty
.
The density floor on
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is

\pi_{\min}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\frac{\exp(-u_{\alpha})}{Z}\;>\;0.

(19)

Assumption 8

(Smooth HPD boundary)
.

The potential satisfies

\nabla U(x)\neq 0
for every
x
with
U(x)=u_{\alpha}
.

Assumption 9

(Local regularity)
.

The log-density ratio
h
is continuously differentiable on an
open neighbourhood of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, with local Lipschitz constant

M_{\mathcal{K}}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\sup_{x\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}}\|\nabla h(x)\|\;<\;\infty.

(20)

Assumption
7
defines
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
as the HPD
level set of
\pi
, which need not be convex; for the banana target,
the nonlinear shear
x_{2}\mapsto x_{2}-\kappa(x_{1}^{2}-1)
makes the
level sets non-convex. The density floor
\pi_{\min}
is exact
(not estimated) since
Z
is assumed known; for the banana family
used here,
Z=(2\pi)^{d/2}
.
Assumption
8
ensures by the implicit
function theorem that
\partial\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}=\{U=u_{\alpha}\}
is a
smooth
(d{-}1)
-dimensional manifold; it holds whenever
u_{\alpha}

is not a critical value of
U
, which is generic by Sard’s theorem
and easily verified for specific targets.
Assumption
9
holds whenever
U
and
T_{\phi}
are

C^{1}
, which is guaranteed for RealNVP with tanh activations.

Remark 10

(Certification of
u_{\alpha}
and
\pi_{\min}
)
.

The quantile
u_{\alpha}
can be certified from
n
samples via order
statistics. Let
U_{(1)}\leq\cdots\leq U_{(n)}
be the ordered
potential values. Setting
k
such that

P(\mathrm{Bin}(n,1{-}\alpha)\leq k)\geq 1-\delta_{q}
gives a
conservative upper bound
u_{\alpha}^{+}=U_{(k)}
: with probability

\geq 1-\delta_{q}
,
\pi(\{U\leq U_{(k)}\})\geq 1-\alpha
.
For
n=10{,}000
,
\alpha=0.01
,
\delta_{q}=0.01
:
k=9920

suffices. The resulting
\pi_{\min}=\exp(-U_{(9920)})/Z
is a
certified lower bound on the density floor, and differs negligibly
from the empirical
99
th-percentile density because
U_{(9920)}

and
U_{(9900)}
are neighbouring order statistics.

5.2
Covering lemma

The key geometric ingredient is that
n
independent draws from
\pi

form an
\varepsilon
-net of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
with high probability.
Unlike classical covering arguments for convex bodies, we do not
assume convexity of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
; instead, we exploit the smooth
boundary (Assumption
8
).

Lemma 11

(Probabilistic covering with curvature correction)
.

Let
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
satisfy
Assumptions
7
–
8

with
\pi_{\min}>0
,
D=\mathrm{diam}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})
, and maximum
principal curvature
\kappa_{\max}
of
\partial\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
.
Define
c_{d}=V_{d-1}(d{+}1)/(V_{d}\cdot d)
(the spherical-cap
coefficient;
c_{2}\approx 0.95
,
c_{10}\approx 1.42
).
Let
X_{1},\dots,X_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi
and define
the effective sample count

n_{\mathrm{eff}}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\left\lfloor n(1-\alpha)-\sqrt{\tfrac{n}{2}\ln\tfrac{3}{\delta}}\right\rfloor.

(21)

For any
\varepsilon>0
satisfying

c_{d}\kappa_{\max}\varepsilon<1
, define the
curvature-corrected half-ball volume

\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\;\mathrel{:=}\;\frac{V_{d}}{2}\,\bigl(1-c_{d}\,\kappa_{\max}\,\varepsilon\bigr).

(22)

Then

\mathbb{P}\bigl(\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon)^{c}\bigr)\;\leq\;\frac{\delta}{3}\;+\;\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha},\varepsilon)\cdot\bigl(1-\pi_{\min}\,\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{d}\bigr)^{n_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(23)

where
\mathcal{E}(\varepsilon)=\{\forall x\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha},\,\min_{i}\|x-X_{i}\|\leq\varepsilon\}
and

\mathcal{N}(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha},\varepsilon)\leq(D/\varepsilon+1)^{d}
.

Proof.

Part (i): effective sample count.

Let
N_{\alpha}=|\{i:X_{i}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\}|
. By Hoeffding’s
inequality,
\mathbb{P}(N_{\alpha}<n_{\mathrm{eff}})\leq\delta/3
.

Part (ii): covering given
N_{\alpha}\geq n_{\mathrm{eff}}
.

Condition on
N_{\alpha}\geq n_{\mathrm{eff}}
and let

\{Y_{j}\}_{j=1}^{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}
be the samples in
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
,
i.i.d. from
\pi(\cdot\mid\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})
.
Let
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{N}\}
be a minimal
\varepsilon
-covering.

For each centre
c_{j}\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, we lower-bound

\mathrm{Vol}(B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})
.

Interior centres

(
\mathrm{dist}(c_{j},\partial\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})\geq\varepsilon
):

B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\subset\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, volume
=V_{d}\varepsilon^{d}\geq\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{d}
.

Boundary-adjacent centres

(
\mathrm{dist}(c_{j},\partial\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})<\varepsilon
):
By Assumption
8
and the implicit function
theorem,
\partial\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is locally the graph of a
C^{1}

function with principal curvatures bounded by
\kappa_{\max}
.
The intersection
B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
contains at
least a half-ball minus a spherical cap of height

\kappa_{\max}\varepsilon^{2}/2
. The cap volume is at most

c_{d}\,\kappa_{\max}\,\varepsilon\cdot(V_{d}/2)\,\varepsilon^{d}

(a standard estimate; see, e.g.,
Vershynin 2018
), giving

\mathrm{Vol}\bigl(B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\bigr)\;\geq\;\frac{V_{d}\,\varepsilon^{d}}{2}\bigl(1-c_{d}\,\kappa_{\max}\,\varepsilon\bigr)\;=\;\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{d},

(24)

provided
c_{d}\,\kappa_{\max}\,\varepsilon<1
.

In both cases,

\pi(B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})\geq\pi_{\min}\cdot\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\cdot\varepsilon^{d}
.
By independence:

\mathbb{P}(Y_{i}\notin B(c_{j},\varepsilon)\;\forall\,i)\leq(1-\pi_{\min}\,\omega_{d}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{d})^{n_{\mathrm{eff}}}
.
Summing over
j
and combining with Part (i)
gives (
23
).
∎

be a geometrically ergodic Markov chain
targeting
\pi
with mixing time
\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
and geometric
rate
\rho<1
. Given a chain of length
N
, thin by factor

k\geq 2\tau_{\mathrm{mix}}
to obtain

n=\lfloor N/k\rfloor
approximately independent samples.
Then Theorem
13
and both corollaries hold with

\varepsilon^{*}
evaluated at the thinned count
n
, provided the
confidence is adjusted to
\delta^{\prime}=\delta-n\rho^{k}
.

Proof sketch.

By geometric ergodicity, the total variation distance between the
joint law of the thinned samples and
\pi^{\otimes n}
is at most

n\rho^{k}
(
[
Levin and Peres 2017
, Proposition 21.1]
).
All events in Theorem
13
are measurable under
the joint law; their probabilities differ by at most
n\rho^{k}
.
For our transport-preconditioned chains, ESS
/N>0.02
even at

D=10
, so
k\leq 50
and
n>200
, with minor impact on

\varepsilon^{*}
.
∎

5.5
Geometry-aware certification via analytic charting

The curvature condition
c_{d}\kappa_{\max}\varepsilon^{*}<1
in
Corollary
14
is purely geometric: it asks whether
the boundary of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is mild enough that a half-ball of
radius
\varepsilon^{*}
at any boundary point fits inside

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
up to a controlled cap-volume correction.
For a non-convex, banana-shaped
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
the boundary curvature
grows with dimension and the feasibility window

\varepsilon<1/(c_{d}\kappa_{\max})
shrinks below the
cover-radius requirement once
d\geq 5

(Table
4
, “infeasible” rows).

For target families that admit an analytic description, however,

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
can often be re-coordinatised into a region with much
nicer geometry — e.g. a Euclidean ball. We formalise this as
follows.

Proposition 19

(Charted oscillation guarantee)
.

Suppose there is a
C^{1}
diffeomorphism

\Psi:\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}\to\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{y}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with

|\det J_{\Psi}(x)|=1
for all
x\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, and such that

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{y}
is bounded with diameter
D^{y}
, smooth boundary
with maximum principal curvature
\kappa_{\max}^{y}
, and density
floor

\pi_{\min}^{y}=\min_{y\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{y}}\pi_{x}(\Psi^{-1}(y))
.
Define the charted log-ratio gradient supremum

M_{\mathcal{K}}^{y}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\sup_{y\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{y}}\bigl\|J_{\Psi}(\Psi^{-1}(y))^{-T}\,\nabla_{x}h(\Psi^{-1}(y))\bigr\|.

Then Theorem
13
and
Corollary
14
hold with the tuple

(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha},D,\pi_{\min},\kappa_{\max},M_{\mathcal{K}})
replaced by
its
y
-space counterpart

(\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{y},D^{y},\pi_{\min}^{y},\kappa_{\max}^{y},M_{\mathcal{K}}^{y})

and with
n
i.i.d. certification samples drawn from

\pi_{y}(y)=\pi_{x}(\Psi^{-1}(y))
.

Proof sketch.

Because
|\det J_{\Psi}|=1
, the change of variables is volume
preserving and
\pi_{y}(y)=\pi_{x}(\Psi^{-1}(y))
. The log-ratio is
invariant:
h(x)=\log\pi_{x}(x)-\log q_{\varphi}(x)=\log\pi_{y}(y)-\log\tilde{q}_{\varphi}(y)
where

\tilde{q}_{\varphi}(y):=q_{\varphi}(\Psi^{-1}(y))
. Drawing

y_{1},\dots,y_{n}\overset{\mathrm{iid}}{\sim}\pi_{y}
and applying
Theorem
13
in
y
-coordinates uses

D^{y},\pi_{\min}^{y},\kappa_{\max}^{y}
for the covering
inequality and
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{y}
for the Lipschitz correction; the
chain rule

\nabla_{y}h_{y}(y)=J_{\Psi}(\Psi^{-1}(y))^{-T}\nabla_{x}h(\Psi^{-1}(y))

gives the form of
M_{\mathcal{K}}^{y}
.
∎

Banana shear chart.

For the banana family with curvature
c
we use the shear

\Psi:x\mapsto y
defined by
y_{1}=x_{1}
,

y_{2}=x_{2}-c(x_{1}^{2}-1)

y_{j}=x_{j}
for

Its Jacobian is lower-triangular with unit diagonal, hence

\kappa_{\max}^{y}=1/\sqrt{\chi^{2}_{d,1-\alpha}}

6
Oscillation-Regularised Training

The empirical oscillation framework of Section
5

shows that the spectral gap bound is ultimately limited by
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}

and
\widehat{M}_{n}
—both properties of the trained flow, not of the
architecture. This suggests a natural algorithmic improvement: add
the empirical oscillation as a regulariser during training, so the flow
learns to minimise not only the density-fit loss but also the
pointwise variation of
\log(\pi/q_{\phi})
.

6.1
Algorithm

We augment the standard reverse-KL training objective with an
oscillation penalty. Given a mini-batch
\{x_{i}\}_{i=1}^{B}
drawn from
the target
\pi
(or a close approximation), define the batch
oscillation

\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\max_{1\leq i\leq B}\log r_{\phi}(x_{i})\;-\;\min_{1\leq i\leq B}\log r_{\phi}(x_{i}),

(34)

where
\log r_{\phi}(x)=\log\pi(x)-\log q_{\phi}(x)
is computed
in the forward pass (
\log\pi
from the target,
\log q_{\phi}
from
the flow). The regularised loss is

\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}\;=\;\mathrm{NLL}(\phi)\;+\;\lambda\,\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B},

(35)

where
\mathrm{NLL}(\phi)=-\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i}\log q_{\phi}(x_{i})
is
the negative log-likelihood and
\lambda\geq 0
controls the
regularisation strength.

Warmup schedule.

To avoid interfering with the initial density-fitting phase, we
ramp
\lambda
linearly from
0
to its target value over the first

100
epochs of training. The NLL loss stabilises during this warmup,
after which the oscillation penalty gradually takes effect. Early
stopping monitors the combined loss
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}
with patience
80

epochs.

Computational cost.

The oscillation term (
34
) requires only the
max and min of log-ratio values already computed in the NLL loss;
the additional overhead is negligible. Training time increases by
roughly
2\times
(from
\sim\!180
to
\sim\!350
–
600
epochs)
because the regulariser prevents early convergence of NLL alone,
but each epoch has the same cost.

6.4
Theoretical analysis

We provide a simple condition under which oscillation regularisation
does not degrade the density fit.

Proposition 20

(NLL preservation under small
\lambda
)
.

Let
\phi^{*}_{0}
be a stationary point of the NLL loss

\mathrm{NLL}(\phi)
, and let
\phi^{*}_{\lambda}
be a stationary
point of
\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=\mathrm{NLL}+\lambda\,\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}
.
If the Hessian
\nabla^{2}\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{0})
has minimum
eigenvalue
\mu_{\min}>0
and
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}
is

G
-Lipschitz in
\phi
, then

\|\phi^{*}_{\lambda}-\phi^{*}_{0}\|\;\leq\;\frac{\lambda\,G}{\mu_{\min}},\qquad\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})-\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{0})\;\leq\;\frac{\lambda^{2}G^{2}}{2\mu_{\min}}.

(36)

Proof.

By the implicit function theorem, near
\phi^{*}_{0}
the gradient
condition
\nabla_{\phi}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=0
gives

\nabla\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})=-\lambda\,\nabla\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})
.
Since
\mathrm{NLL}
is
\mu_{\min}
-strongly convex near

\phi^{*}_{0}
:

\mu_{\min}\,\|\phi^{*}_{\lambda}-\phi^{*}_{0}\|\;\leq\;\|\nabla\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})\|=\lambda\,\|\nabla\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})\|\leq\lambda G.

The NLL increase follows from a second-order Taylor expansion
around
\phi^{*}_{0}
(where
\nabla\mathrm{NLL}=0
):

\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})-\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{0})\leq\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla^{2}\mathrm{NLL}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\,\|\phi^{*}_{\lambda}-\phi^{*}_{0}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}G^{2}}{2\mu_{\min}}
,
using
\|\nabla^{2}\mathrm{NLL}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\mu_{\min}^{-1}\cdot\mu_{\min}^{2}=\mu_{\min}
only if the
Hessian is well-conditioned. More generally, bounding with the
Hessian spectral norm
\mu_{\max}
gives

\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{\lambda})-\mathrm{NLL}(\phi^{*}_{0})\leq\frac{\lambda^{2}G^{2}\mu_{\max}}{2\mu_{\min}^{2}}
.
∎

The bound (
36
) quantifies the NLL cost of
regularisation: it scales as
\lambda^{2}
, so for small
\lambda

the density fit is barely affected. In our experiments, the
measured NLL shift is
<0.002
across all banana dimensions
(Table
6
), consistent with the
\lambda^{2}

scaling at
\lambda=0.02
.

Remark 21

(Gradient orthogonality)
.

The bound in Proposition
20
is worst-case.
In practice, the NLL gradient and the oscillation gradient are
nearly orthogonal: NLL penalises the
mean
of
\log r_{\phi}
,
while the oscillation penalises its
range
. A flow that
uniformly shifts
\log r_{\phi}
by a constant changes NLL but not
oscillation; a flow that redistributes
\log r_{\phi}
variation
without changing its mean changes oscillation but not NLL. This
near-orthogonality explains why the empirical NLL cost is
even smaller than the
\lambda^{2}
bound predicts.

8.3
Why certification fails: three barriers

The vacuous targets reveal three distinct, structurally different
barriers to non-vacuous certification. These are not architectural
failures of the flow but fundamental limitations of current
worst-case credible-set certification.

Mode 1: Target stiffness (shear building).

The shear building posterior has
L_{U}=765
—two orders of
magnitude larger than the banana targets. Even the
analytical

bound at
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})=1
gives

L_{U}\cdot 2R=765\times 4.17=3186
, far too large.
The empirical oscillation
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}=22
confirms that
the flow does not fit this target well: the affine coupling layers
cannot capture the anisotropic, high-condition-number posterior
geometry. Preconditioning the target (e.g., via a Laplace
approximation) before flow training would reduce both
L_{U}
and

\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
.

Mode 2: Tail mismatch (funnel).

The funnel has
R=299
and
L_{U}=4459
: the conditional variance

e^{x_{1}}
spans nine orders of magnitude. Training samples are
drawn from a reference distribution that under-samples the extreme
tails (
|x_{1}|>6
), so the flow is optimised for the bulk but
incurs large
\log r_{\phi}
excursions in the tails—exactly where
the MCMC chain occasionally visits. Oscillation regularisation
worsens the problem (Table
7
) because
the training-batch oscillation proxy does not see the tail misfit.
A solution would be MCMC-in-the-loop training: periodically
re-drawing training batches from the current transport chain to
align the training and evaluation supports.

Mode 3: High dimension + limited data (BayesLR).

With
D=25
and only
20{,}000
training samples (
n/d=800
,
compared to
n/d\geq 2500
for the banana targets), the flow
underfits:
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}=30
even without regularisation.
Additionally,
\varepsilon^{*}
would be enormous at
d=25

(the half-ball volume
\omega_{25}\approx 10^{-10}
), making
the covering correction vacuous regardless of
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
.
More training data, deeper architectures, or dimension-reduction
techniques (e.g., active subspaces) are needed.

Near-miss: GMM.

The Gaussian mixture (
D=5
) is the closest to non-vacuous
among the failing targets:
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}=2.63
with osc-reg,

\widehat{M}_{n}=2.70
, and
\varepsilon^{*}\approx 4.1
.
The v3 practical bound gives

\mathrm{osc}_{\text{bound}}=2.63+2\times 2.70\times 4.1=24.8
,
which is vacuous (
\delta^{*}\approx 6\times 10^{10}
).
The bottleneck is the covering correction: even though the
oscillation itself is modest, the gradient supremum
\widehat{M}_{n}

is
4\times
larger than for the banana at the same dimension
(2.70 vs 0.70), inflating
2\widehat{M}_{n}\varepsilon^{*}
.
The higher
\widehat{M}_{n}
reflects the bimodal structure: the flow’s

\log r_{\phi}
gradient is steep near the saddle region between modes.

9
Discussion

The rigorous claim is deliberately narrow—a single target family
at
D\in\{2,5\}
—but the framework is general, and the
negative results on harder targets (GMM, shear building, funnel,
Bayesian logistic regression) are themselves a contribution: they
identify the precise barriers that future theoretical advances must
overcome.

Summary of contributions.

This paper establishes the first rigorous non-vacuous spectral gap
bound for a learned-transport independence Metropolis sampler
(
\gamma^{*}=0.828
on banana
D=2
, fully certified covering
with verified curvature condition). The certification framework
rests on three pillars:
(i) spectral normalization reduces
\mathrm{Lip}(T_{\phi})
by over
40

orders of magnitude (Theorem
3
);
(ii) a coverage-based empirical oscillation bound bypasses the
structurally vacuous analytical bound
(Theorem
13
);
(iii) oscillation-regularised training pushes practical certificates
to
D=20
at no cost to density-fit quality
(Section
6
).
A geometry-aware charting technique
(Proposition
19
, Section
5.5
)
extends rigorous certification from
D=2
to
D=5
by
transforming the banana target’s HPD set from a curved
non-convex region to a convex Gaussian ball.
The comparison with neural spline flows
(Section
7
) reveals that simpler
architectures produce tighter certificates, establishing a

certification hierarchy
distinct from the usual
expressiveness hierarchy.

When do the bounds work?

Non-vacuous bounds require two conditions:
(i) the flow fits the target well enough that
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}

is small (
\lesssim 2
); and
(ii) the credible set is covered densely enough that

2\widehat{M}_{n}\varepsilon^{*}
is moderate.
Condition (i) depends on the target and the flow:
well-conditioned targets (banana, GMM) satisfy it;
ill-conditioned targets (shear8 with
L_{U}=765
) or heavy-tailed
ones (funnel with
R=299
) do not.
Condition (ii) is geometric:
\varepsilon^{*}
grows with
d
via the
volumetric curse. Oscillation regularisation helps with (i)
(reducing
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
by
60
–
90\%
) but cannot
address (ii).

The gap between practical and rigorous bounds.

The practical bound (Corollary
15
) approximates

M_{\mathcal{K}}
by
\widehat{M}_{n}
; the rigorous bound
(Corollary
14
) adds a Hessian correction that
collapses the bound at
D\geq 5
. Closing this gap is the most
impactful open problem: it would convert our practical bounds into
fully certified guarantees. Dimension-free concentration tools
(log-Sobolev inequalities) or local Lipschitz certification
(interval bound propagation, semidefinite
relaxation
[
Fazlyab et al. 2019
]
) are promising directions.

Limitations.

The coverage-based bound inherits the curse of dimensionality: the
covering radius

\varepsilon^{*}\propto(\pi_{\min}\omega_{d})^{-1/d}
grows steeply
because
\omega_{d}=V_{d}/2^{d}
collapses exponentially with
d
.
Oscillation regularisation is not target-agnostic: it fails when
training samples do not cover the MCMC evaluation support
(Section
6.5
).
The non-vacuous results are limited to banana-type targets; more
challenging posteriors (multimodal, funnel, high-dimensional
regression) remain out of reach.

Future directions.

Several extensions target the barriers identified above:
(i)
dimension-free covering
: replacing the volumetric

\varepsilon
-net with transport-adapted or Voronoi-mass arguments
that avoid the
V_{d}
collapse;
(ii)
certified
M_{\mathcal{K}}
: closing the gap between practical
and rigorous certificates via interval bound propagation on the
RealNVP Jacobian;
(iii)
MCMC-in-the-loop regularisation
: computing

\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{B}
on transport-chain mini-batches to extend
oscillation regularisation to heavy-tailed targets (addressing the
funnel barrier);
(iv)
posterior-adapted preconditioning
: using the empirical
posterior covariance (rather than the Laplace Hessian, which we found
inadequate for the shear building; see
Appendix
A
) to reduce
L_{U}
and
R
;
(v)
oscillation-aware architectures
: designing coupling
layers whose residual
\log(\pi/q_{\phi})
is smooth by construction,
rather than relying on post-hoc oscillation control.

Reproducibility.

Code and reproducibility materials will be released at

https://github.com/junhu22/LCNF
.

A.5
Certification details for
D=2

Every numerical input in the headline
\gamma^{*}=0.828
for the

D=2
banana has an independent certification. This appendix
documents the three pieces (training/certification split,
grid-based
M_{\mathcal{K}}
, and banana-analytic
\pi_{\min}
) and a
multi-seed stability study.

Training / certification split.

The banana target admits exact i.i.d. sampling:

x_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)
,

x_{2}\mid x_{1}\sim\mathcal{N}(\kappa(x_{1}^{2}-1),\,1)

with
\kappa=0.1
. We draw two independent samples:

•

Training set
:
50{,}000
i.i.d. samples (seed
42
),
used to fit the osc-regularised flow.

•

Certification set
:
10{,}000
i.i.d. samples (seed

20{,}260{,}521
), disjoint from the training set, used to
compute
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
.

On the certification set we obtain

\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}=0.273
and a support radius
R=3.80
. The
density floor on
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is taken from the banana-analytic
level set, which is exact because
Z=(2\pi)^{d/2}
in closed form
and
U(X)\sim\tfrac{1}{2}\chi^{2}_{d}
:

\pi_{\min}=\exp(-\tfrac{1}{2}\chi^{2}_{d,1-\alpha})/(2\pi)^{d/2}=1.59\!\times\!10^{-3}

at
d=2
,
\alpha=0.01
. No empirical sample-based correction
is needed for the banana family; the generic sample-based
order-statistic / DKW procedure is described in
Remark
10
for use on non-analytic targets.

Grid-certified
M_{\mathcal{K}}
.

M_{\mathcal{K}}
enters the bound via the Lipschitz correction

2M_{\mathcal{K}}\,\varepsilon^{*}
but is otherwise treated empirically
elsewhere in this paper. At
D=2
we replace

\widehat{M}_{n}
with a grid-certified upper bound obtained by gridding.
We evaluate
\|\nabla h\|
and
\|\nabla^{2}h\|_{\text{op}}
on all
grid nodes inside the
\Delta
-enlargement

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}\;\mathrel{:=}\;\{x:\mathrm{dist}(x,\mathcal{K}_{\alpha})\leq\Delta\},

where
\Delta
is the grid half-diagonal. This guarantees that
every
x\in\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
lies within distance
\Delta
of at least
one evaluated node, so the certified bound is

M_{\mathcal{K}}\;\leq\;\max_{y\in\mathrm{Grid}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}}\|\nabla h(y)\|\;+\;\Bigl(\max_{y\in\mathrm{Grid}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}}\|\nabla^{2}h(y)\|_{\text{op}}\Bigr)\cdot\Delta.

1.

Build a
100\!\times\!100
uniform grid on the bounding box of

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
, namely

[-3.33,\,3.33]\times[-3.43,\,3.23]
(from the cert sample

\alpha
-quantile, padded by
0.3
). Cell spacing

\Delta_{x_{1}}=\Delta_{x_{2}}=0.0673
and half-diagonal

\Delta=0.0476
.

2.

Identify
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}
on the grid as the 8-connected
dilation of the node set
\{y:\log\pi(y)\geq\log\pi_{\alpha}\}
:

6{,}743
of
10{,}000
nodes (compared with
6{,}379
strictly
inside
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
). Each grid cell that meets
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}

contributes its four corners to
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}
, so every
point of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
is within
\Delta
of an evaluated node.

3.

Compute
\|\nabla h(y)\|
analytically at every node
(the banana score
\nabla\log\pi
is closed-form;

\nabla\log q_{\varphi}
is one autograd call) and take

M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\text{grid}}:=\max_{y\in\mathrm{Grid}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}}\|\nabla h(y)\|=0.0387
.

4.

Bound the local Lipschitz of
\nabla h
by the
operator-norm of its Hessian, evaluated on the same nodes by
double autograd against the analytic banana Hessian; we obtain

L_{\nabla h}:=\max_{y\in\mathrm{Grid}\cap\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}}\|\nabla^{2}h(y)\|_{\text{op}}=0.0966
.

5.

Add the Lipschitz correction:

M_{\mathcal{K}}\leq M_{\mathcal{K}}^{\text{grid}}+L_{\nabla h}\,\Delta=0.0387+0.0046=0.0433
.

This is
2.8\!\times
tighter than
\widehat{M}_{n}=0.122
from
the in-sample IMH chain, because the IMH chain visits the

\alpha
-fraction of probability mass that lives outside

\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
(where
\|\nabla h\|
tends to be largest), whereas
the certified bound only needs to cover
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}^{\Delta}
,
which is a
\Delta
-thickening of
\mathcal{K}_{\alpha}
and excludes the
heavy tail entirely.

Multi-seed stability.

To confirm that the headline numbers are not seed-specific, we
retrained the osc-regularised flow with five independent seeds

\{42,123,456,789,1024\}
and re-evaluated each under the IMH
kernel.
Across the five runs the training NLL is reproducible to four
significant figures
(
2.847\pm 0.013
at
D=2
,
14.164\pm 0.046
at
D=10
) and
the IMH acceptance rate and ESS vary by less than half a percentage
point (
98.9\%\pm 0.25\%
AR and
9{,}169\pm 611
ESS at
D=2
;

96.0\%\pm 0.43\%
AR and
8{,}521\pm 289
ESS at
D=10
). The
empirical oscillation has a coefficient of variation of
20\%

(
0.478\pm 0.095
at
D=2
;
1.470\pm 0.191
at
D=10
) and

\widehat{M}_{n}
of
12\%

(
0.735\pm 0.088
at
D=2
;
1.129\pm 0.379
at
D=10
),
well within the safety margin of the rigorous bound at
D=2
.
Source:
results/multi_seed_stability.csv
.

Sensitivity of
\gamma^{*}
to cert inputs.

With the certified inputs above, the curvature-corrected
covering equation

(D/\varepsilon+1)^{2}\cdot(1-\pi_{\min}\,\omega_{2}(\varepsilon)\,\varepsilon^{2})^{n_{\text{eff}}}\leq\delta/3

admits
\varepsilon^{*}=0.863
with

c_{2}\kappa_{\max}\varepsilon^{*}=0.523
, well below the curvature
failure threshold of
1
. The osc bound is

\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}+2M_{\mathcal{K}}\,\varepsilon^{*}=0.273+0.075=0.348
, giving

\gamma^{*}=2/(1+e^{0.348})=0.828
.
For comparison, the same calculation with the in-sample IMH
estimates (
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}=0.071
,
\widehat{M}_{n}=0.122
,

\pi_{\min}=1.39\!\times\!10^{-3}
) yields a slightly larger

\gamma^{*}
: the certified
\widehat{\mathrm{osc}}_{n}
rises
(
0.071\to 0.273
) while the certified
M_{\mathcal{K}}
falls
(
0.122\to 0.043
), and
\gamma^{*}
shifts by only
-1.9\%

relative to the uncertified estimate—confirming that the
certification machinery does not materially weaken the headline
gap at
D=2
.

Unwarping map.

With curvature

\Psi:(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})\mapsto(x_{1},\,x_{2}-c(x_{1}^{2}-1),\,x_{3},x_{4},x_{5})

|\det J_{\Psi}|=1
. In
y
-coordinates the target is exactly

\pi_{y}=\mathcal{N}(0,I_{5})
.

y
-space geometric parameters (analytic).

With
\alpha=0.01
and

•

,

D^{y}=2R^{y}=7.768
.

•
