Inequalities between Dirichlet and Neumann eigenvalues in large dimensions
N. D. Filonov

Abstract
Let Ω \Omega be a bounded domain in ℝ d \mathbb{R}^{d} . Denote by λ k \lambda_{k} (resp. μ k \mu_{k} ) the eigenvalues of the Laplace operator in Ω \Omega with Dirichlet (resp. Neumann) boundary conditions. Denote by Ψ = Ψ ⁡ ( d , k , Ω ) \Psi=\Psi(d,k,\Omega) the shift of indices in the inequality μ k + Ψ ⩽ λ k \mu_{k+\Psi}\leqslant\lambda_{k} . We are interested to describe the behaviour of Ψ \Psi for large d d . We prove that a) Ψ ⁡ ( d , 1 , Ω ) ⩾ C ​ ( e / 2 ) d \Psi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant C(e/2)^{d} for all domains Ω \Omega ; and b) Ψ ⁡ ( d , k , Ω ) ⩾ C ​ ( e / 2 ) d \Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant C(e/2)^{d} for all k k and all convex domains Ω \Omega . 1 1 1 Keywords: Laplace operator, Dirichlet problem, Neumann problem, inequalities for eigenavlues, convex domains

Introduction

Let
d\geqslant 2
,
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain
such that the embedding
W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)\subset L_{2}(\Omega)
is compact,
where
W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)
is the Sobolev space.
We consider Dirichlet and Neumann problems for the Laplace operator in
\Omega
:

\begin{cases}-\Delta\varphi_{k}=\lambda_{k}\varphi_{k},\\
\left.\varphi_{k}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0,\end{cases}\qquad\begin{cases}-\Delta\psi_{k}=\mu_{k}\psi_{k},\\
\left.\frac{\partial\psi_{k}}{\partial\nu}\right|_{\partial\Omega}=0.\end{cases}

Spectra of these two problems are discrete,
the eigenvalues form two sequences

0<\lambda_{1}<\lambda_{2}\leqslant\lambda_{3}\leqslant\dots,\qquad\lambda_{k}\to+\infty,

0=\mu_{1}<\mu_{2}\leqslant\mu_{3}\leqslant\dots,\qquad\mu_{k}\to+\infty.

We take multiplicity into account.
It is clear from the min-max principle that
\mu_{k}\leqslant\lambda_{k}
always.
Many papers are devoted to the improvements of this inequality,
see
[
18
,
17
,
1
,
14
,
9
,
5
,
20
]
.
In particular, the inequality

\mu_{k+d}\leqslant\lambda_{k}\qquad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N},\ \text{for all convex domains}\ \Omega

(1.1)

is proved in
[
14
]
.
The last sentence of this paper says

perhaps (
1.1
) can be replaced by a better inequality of the form

\mu_{\Phi(d,k)}<\lambda_{k}

for convex
d
-dimensional domains.

We show that in large dimensions this inequality indeed can be improved,
see Theorem
1.4
below.

Introduce notations

\Phi(d,k,\Omega):=\#\left\{j:\mu_{j}(\Omega)\leqslant\lambda_{k}(\Omega)\right\},

\Psi(d,k,\Omega):=\Phi(d,k,\Omega)-k

in such a way that the inequality

\mu_{k+\Psi(d,k,\Omega)}\leqslant\lambda_{k}

holds true by definition.
In these terms Friedlander’s inequality

\mu_{k+1}<\lambda_{k}
(see
[
9
,
5
]
) means

\Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant 1

(1.2)

for all
k
and all domains.
Levine-Weinberger’s inequality means
\Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant d

for all
k
and all
convex
domains.

Our first result concerns the cases
k=1
and
k=2
.

Theorem 1.1
.

The inequalities

\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant\frac{(j_{d/2-1})^{d}}{(d+2)2^{d-1}\Gamma(d/2+1)^{2}}

(1.3)

and

\Phi(d,2,\Omega)\geqslant\frac{(j_{d/2-1})^{d}}{(d+2)2^{d-2}\Gamma(d/2+1)^{2}}.

hold true for all domains
\Omega
.
Here and everywhere below we denote by
j_{\nu}
the first positive root
of the Bessel function
J_{\nu}
.

Corollary 1.2
.

There is an absolute constant
C_{1}>0
such that

\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant C_{1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{d}\qquad\forall\ \Omega.

(1.4)

Corollary 1.3
.

For all domains
\Omega\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
we have

\mu_{3}<\lambda_{1}\qquad\text{if}\ \ d\geqslant 7,

and

\mu_{4}<\lambda_{2}\qquad\text{if}\ \ d\geqslant 6.

See also the table at the end of the proof of Corollary
1.3
below.

Note also the elegant paper by L. Hatcher
[
10
]
.
Answering the question posed in
[
3
]
on the estimates of
\Phi(d,1,\Omega)

in geometric terms, he showed that

c_{1}(d)\,\frac{\operatorname{mes}_{d-1}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{\operatorname{mes}_{d}(\Omega)^{d-1}}\leqslant\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\leqslant c_{2}(d)\,\frac{\operatorname{mes}_{d-1}(\partial\Omega)^{d}}{\operatorname{mes}_{d}(\Omega)^{d-1}}

for all convex domains
\Omega
.
The constants
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
depend only on
d
.
However,
\log c_{1}(d)
is of order of
d^{2}
for large
d

which is too large for us.

P. Freitas and M. Gama in
[
8
]
showed that
\Psi(d,k,\Omega)

grows as a power of
k
as
k\to\infty
.
We are interested in another regime,
namely what happens if
d\to\infty
.

Let us formulate our main result.

Theorem 1.4
.

There is an absolute constant
C_{conv}>0
such that

\Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant C_{conv}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{d}

for all natural
k
and for all bounded convex domains
\Omega
.

Theorem
1.4
together with Corollary
1.2
invite us to make the following

Conjecture 1.5
.

There exist two absolute positive constants
C_{*}
,
\alpha_{*}
such that

\Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant C_{*}e^{\alpha_{*}d}\qquad\forall\ k\in\mathbb{N},\quad\forall\ \Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}.

It is natural to consider the examples of multi-dimensional cubes and balls.
It is shown in
[
3
]
that a) in the case of a ball the function
\Phi(d,1,B)

grows faster than any power of
d
(Theorem 3 in
[
3
]
);
and b) in the case of a cube it grows exponentially,

\alpha_{1}d\leqslant\log\Phi(d,1,Q)\leqslant\alpha_{2}d

with some positive constants
\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2}
(formula (10) in
[
3
]
).
We refine these results by explicitly computing
the asymptotics of
\log\Phi(d,1)
in these cases.

Theorem 1.6
.

Let
B
be a
d
-dimensional ball.
Then

\log\Phi(d,1,B)=\alpha_{ball}d+O(d^{1/3}),\qquad d\to+\infty.

Here

\alpha_{ball}=\sqrt{2}\log(\sqrt{2}+1)-\log 2\approx 0,55.

Remark 1.7
.

Moreover, one can show that

\log\Phi(d,1,B)=\alpha_{ball}d+\hat{\alpha}d^{1/3}+O(\log d),\qquad d\to+\infty,

see (
4.10
) below.

Note that this Theorem shows that if the Conjecture
1.5
holds true
then
\alpha_{*}\leqslant\alpha_{ball}
.

Theorem 1.8
.

Let
Q
be a
d
-dimensional cube.
Then the asymptotics

\log\Phi(d,1,Q)=\alpha_{cube}d+O(d^{1/3}),\qquad d\to+\infty

holds true,
\alpha_{cube}\approx 1,04
.

We prove this Theorem in a separate paper.

For further references introduce the counting functions
for the spectra of the Dirichlet and Neumann problems,

N_{\cal D}(\Omega,\Lambda):=\#\{j:\lambda_{j}\leqslant\Lambda\},\qquad N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\Lambda):=\#\{j:\mu_{j}\leqslant\Lambda\}.

In these terms

\Phi(d,k,\Omega)=N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{k}).

(1.5)

If
\lambda_{k}
is a simple eigenvalue of the Dirichlet problem then

\Psi(d,k,\Omega)=N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{k})-N_{\cal D}(\Omega,\lambda_{k}).

In general case,

\Psi(d,k,\Omega)\geqslant N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{k})-N_{\cal D}(\Omega,\lambda_{k}).

(1.6)

In the next section we prove Theorem
1.1
and Corollaries
1.2
and
1.3
.
In §3 we prove Theorem
1.4
.
In §4 we prove Theorem
1.6
.

2
Bounds for
\Phi(d,1,\Omega)
and
\Phi(d,2,\Omega)
for arbitrary domains

We use three following famous inequalities.

Theorem 2.1

(Kröger,
[
13
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain such that
the embedding
W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)\subset L_{2}(\Omega)
is compact.
Then the Neumann counting function satisfies the bound

N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda)\geqslant\frac{2}{d+2}\cdot\frac{\omega_{d}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega}{(2\pi)^{d}}\ \lambda^{d/2}.

Here and in what follows we denote by
\omega_{d}

the volume of the unit ball in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\omega_{d}=\frac{\pi^{d/2}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)}.

Theorem 2.2

(Faber-Krahn,
[
4
,
11
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain.
Then

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geqslant\lambda_{1}(B_{R}),

where
B_{R}
is a ball of the same volume,
\operatorname{mes}_{d}B_{R}=\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega
.

Theorem 2.3

(Krahn-Szego,
[
12
]
)
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded domain.
Then

\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\geqslant\lambda_{1}(B_{\tilde{R}}),

where
B_{\tilde{R}}
is a ball such that
\operatorname{mes}_{d}B_{\tilde{R}}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega
.

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The first eigenfunction and the first eigenvalue
of the Dirichlet problem in a ball of radius
R
are well known:

\varphi_{1}(x)=|x|^{1-d/2}J_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\left(\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}|x|}{R}\right),\qquad\lambda_{1}(B_{R})=\left(\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}}{R}\right)^{2}.

(2.1)

The equalities
\operatorname{mes}_{d}B_{R}=\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega
and
\operatorname{mes}_{d}B_{\tilde{R}}=\frac{1}{2}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega

imply

R=\frac{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{1/d}}{\omega_{d}^{1/d}},\qquad\tilde{R}=\frac{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{1/d}}{(2\omega_{d})^{1/d}}.

Therefore,

\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geqslant\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{2}\omega_{d}^{2/d}}{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{2/d}},\qquad\lambda_{2}(\Omega)\geqslant\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{2}2^{2/d}\omega_{d}^{2/d}}{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{2/d}}.

Now, Theorem
2.1
yields the bounds

\displaystyle\Phi(d,1,\Omega)=N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{1})\geqslant\frac{2}{d+2}\cdot\frac{\omega_{d}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega}{(2\pi)^{d}}\ \lambda_{1}(\Omega)^{d/2}

\displaystyle\geqslant\frac{2\omega_{d}^{2}\left(j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\right)^{d}}{(d+2)(2\pi)^{d}}=\frac{\left(j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}\right)^{d}}{(d+2)2^{d-1}\Gamma(d/2+1)^{2}},

and in the same manner,

\displaystyle\Phi(d,2,\Omega)\geqslant\frac{2}{d+2}\cdot\frac{\omega_{d}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega}{(2\pi)^{d}}\ \lambda_{2}(\Omega)^{d/2}\geqslant\frac{(j_{\frac{d}{2}-1})^{d}}{(d+2)2^{d-2}\Gamma(d/2+1)^{2}}.\quad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

Proof of Corollary
1.2
.

Taking a logarithm of inequality (
1.3
) we obtain

\log\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant d\log j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}-d\log 2-2\log\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)+O(\log d),\quad d\to\infty.

(2.2)

The asymptotics of the zeros of the Bessel functions

j_{\nu}=\nu+|a_{1}|2^{-1/3}\nu^{1/3}+O\left(\nu^{-1/3}\right),\qquad\nu\to+\infty,

is well known, see for example
[
19
]
.
Here
a_{1}
is the first zero of the Airy function
Ai
,
a_{1}\approx-2,34
.
Therefore,

j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}=\frac{d}{2}+|a_{1}|2^{-2/3}d^{1/3}+O(1),\quad d\to\infty,

(2.3)

\log j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}=\log d-\log 2+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}+O(d^{-1}),\quad d\to\infty.

(2.4)

We use also Stirling’s formula

\log\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)=\frac{d}{2}\left(\log d-\log 2-1\right)+O(\log d),\quad d\to\infty.

(2.5)

Substituting (
2.4
) and (
2.5
) into (
2.2
) we get

\log\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant(1-\log 2)d+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{1/3}+O(\log d)\geqslant(1-\log 2)d

for sufficiently large
d
.
This yields the existence of such a constant
C_{1}>0
that

\Phi(d,1,\Omega)\geqslant C_{1}\left(\frac{e}{2}\right)^{d}\qquad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

For the proof of Corollary
1.3
we use

Lemma 2.4
.

The function

f_{0}(x)=\log(1+x)-\frac{2x}{3}

is positive on the interval
(0,1]
.

Proof.

We have
f_{0}^{\prime\prime}(x)=\frac{-1}{(1+x)^{2}}
, thus,
the function
f_{0}
is concave.
Next,

f_{0}(0)=0,\qquad f_{0}(1)=\log 2-\frac{2}{3}>0.

Therefore,

f_{0}(x)>0
if
0<x\leqslant 1
.

Denote the right hand side of (
1.3
) by

f_{1}(d):=\frac{(j_{d/2-1})^{d}}{(d+2)2^{d-1}\Gamma(d/2+1)^{2}}.

From
[
19
]
we know that

j_{\nu}\geqslant\nu+|a_{1}|2^{-1/3}\nu^{1/3},

and therefore

j_{\nu-1}\geqslant(\nu-1)\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{-1/3}\nu^{-2/3}\right).

It is known also that

\Gamma(x+1)=x\Gamma(x)<x^{x+1/2}e^{-x}\sqrt{2\pi}\,e^{\frac{1}{12x}},

so,

\Gamma\left(\frac{d}{2}+1\right)<\sqrt{2\pi}\left(\frac{d}{2}\right)^{\frac{d+1}{2}}e^{-\frac{d}{2}+\frac{1}{6d}}.

Combining all together we obtain

f_{1}(d)\geqslant\frac{(d-2)^{d}\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)^{d}e^{d}}{\pi(d+2)2^{d-1}d^{d+1}e^{\frac{1}{3d}}}=:f_{2}(d).

Lemma 2.5
.

The function
f_{2}(d)
is increasing for
d\geqslant 8
.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle\log f_{2}(d)=d\log(d-2)+d\log\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)+d

\displaystyle-\log\pi-\log(d+2)-(d-1)\log 2-(d+1)\log d-\frac{1}{3d},

\displaystyle\left(\log f_{2}(d)\right)^{\prime}=\frac{d}{d-2}+\log(d-2)+\log\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)-\frac{|a_{1}|2^{4/3}}{3\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)d^{2/3}}

\displaystyle+1-\frac{1}{d+2}-\log 2-\frac{d+1}{d}-\log d+\frac{1}{3d^{2}}.

Furthermore,

\frac{d}{d-2}-\frac{1}{d+2}-\frac{d+1}{d}=\frac{6d+4}{d(d^{2}-4)}>0,

\log\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)-\frac{|a_{1}|2^{4/3}}{3\left(d^{2/3}+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}\right)}>\log\left(1+|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\right)-\frac{|a_{1}|2^{4/3}}{3d^{2/3}}>0

by virtue of Lemma
2.4
as we have
|a_{1}|2^{1/3}d^{-2/3}\leqslant 1

if
d\geqslant\sqrt{2}|a_{1}|^{3/2}\approx 5,06
.
Thus,

\left(\log f_{2}(d)\right)^{\prime}\geqslant\log(d-2)+1-\log 2-\log d=\log\left(\frac{(d-2)e}{2d}\right)>0,

whenever

d>\frac{2e}{e-2}\approx 7,57
.

Proof of Corollary
1.3
.

The preceding Lemma yields

f_{1}(d)\geqslant f_{2}(d)\geqslant f_{2}(11)\approx 2,37\qquad\text{if}\quad d\geqslant 11.

(2.6)

For any
\varepsilon\in(0,1)
denote by
\tilde{\varepsilon}(\Omega)
the number

\tilde{\varepsilon}(\Omega)=\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{2}\omega_{d}^{2/d}}{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{2/d}}\left(1-(1-\varepsilon)^{2/d}\right)>0.

Then repeating the arguments from the proof of Theorem
1.1
for
the counting function at the point
(\lambda_{1}(\Omega)-\tilde{\varepsilon}(\Omega))
one obtain

N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\varepsilon}(\Omega))\geqslant\frac{2}{d+2}\cdot\frac{\omega_{d}\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega}{(2\pi)^{d}}\left(\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{2}\omega_{d}^{2/d}}{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{2/d}}-\tilde{\varepsilon}(\Omega)\right)^{\frac{d}{2}}=f_{1}(d)(1-\varepsilon).

For sufficiently small
\varepsilon
the inequality (
2.6
) implies

N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\varepsilon})\geqslant 2,3\quad\Rightarrow\quad\mu_{3}\leqslant\lambda_{1}-\tilde{\varepsilon}<\lambda_{1}

whenever
d\geqslant 11
.
In the same manner,

\mu_{5}<\lambda_{2}\qquad\text{whenever}\quad d\geqslant 11.

d
we calculate the function

explicitly:

3
Convex domains

In this section we prove Theorem
1.4
.

3.1
Estimate for the difference
N_{\cal N}(\lambda)-N_{\cal D}(\lambda)

via the exponentials

The following Lemma is proved in
[
6
]
.
We give a proof for the sake of completeness.

Lemma 3.1
.

Let
\lambda>0
.
Introduce the subspace

H_{\cal D}(\lambda)=\operatorname{Lin}\left\{\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{k}\right\}_{\lambda_{k}\leqslant\lambda},

(3.1)

where
\varphi_{j}
is the Dirichlet eigenfunction corresponding to
\lambda_{j}
.
Let
F\subset W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)
be a subspace such that

F\cap H_{\cal D}=\{0\},\qquad-\Delta v=\lambda v,\qquad\|\nabla v\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2}\leqslant\lambda\|v\|_{L_{2}(\Omega)}^{2},\quad\forall\ v\in F.

Then

N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda)-N_{\cal D}(\Omega,\lambda)\geqslant\dim F.

Proof.

Let us consider the subspace
H_{\cal D}+F\subset W_{2}^{1}(\Omega)
.
By assumptions,

\dim\left(H_{\cal D}+F\right)=N_{\cal D}(\Omega,\lambda)+\dim F.

Pick a function
w
from this subspace,

w=u+v,\quad u\in H_{\cal D},\quad v\in F.

Then we have

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(|\nabla u|^{2}+|\nabla v|^{2}+2\,\operatorname{Re}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle\right)dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u|^{2}dx\leqslant\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u|^{2}dx,\quad\int_{\Omega}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\leqslant\lambda\int_{\Omega}|v|^{2}dx,

\displaystyle\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u,\nabla v\rangle=-\int_{\Omega}u\Delta\overline{v}\,dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}u\overline{v}\,dx.

Therefore,

\int_{\Omega}|\nabla w|^{2}dx\leqslant\lambda\int_{\Omega}\left(|u|^{2}+|v|^{2}+2\operatorname{Re}(u\overline{v})\right)dx=\lambda\int_{\Omega}|w|^{2}dx.

Now, the variational principle implies

N_{\cal N}(\Omega,\lambda)\geqslant\dim\left(H_{\cal D}+F\right).\qquad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

Taking into account this Lemma and the inequality (
1.6
),
our goal now is to construct a such subspace
F
for
\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1}(\Omega)
.

3.2
Minimal width

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain.
The distance between two supporting hyperplanes of
\Omega

orthogonal to a vector
\vec{e}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
is called the width
of
\Omega
in the direction
\vec{e}
.
We denote by
w_{\min}(\Omega)
the smallest width of
\Omega

over all directions.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain.
For each
k=1,\dots,d
, there exists a
k
-dimensional plane
H_{k}
,

\dim H_{k}=k
, such that

\operatorname{mes}_{k}(P_{k})\geqslant\frac{w_{\min}(\Omega)^{k}}{k!},\qquad\text{where}\quad P_{k}=\Omega\cap H_{k}.

(3.2)

Proof.

We prove this Lemma by induction in
k
.

Clearly, one can find two points
x_{1}^{(1)},x_{2}^{(1)}\in\overline{\Omega}

such that
|x_{1}^{(1)}-x_{2}^{(1)}|\geqslant w_{\min}(\Omega)
.
Now, take the line passing through these two points as
H_{1}
.
Thus, the claim in the case
k=1
is proven.

Assume that
H_{k}
satisfies (
3.2
).
Choose a unit vector
\vec{e}_{k+1}
orthogonal to the plane
H_{k}
.
We take the linear hull of
H_{k}
and
\vec{e}_{k+1}
as the next plane
H_{k+1}
,

H_{k+1}=\left\{x=y+t\vec{e}_{k+1},\ y\in H_{k},t\in\mathbb{R}\right\}.

By definition of the minimal width of
\Omega
,
there are two points
x_{1}^{(k+1)},x_{2}^{(k+1)}\in\overline{\Omega}
such that

x_{1}^{(k+1)}=y_{1}+t_{1}\vec{e}_{k+1},\quad x_{2}^{(k+1)}=y_{2}-t_{2}\vec{e}_{k+1},\quad y_{1},y_{2}\in H_{k},\quad t_{1},t_{2}\geqslant 0,\quad t_{1}+t_{2}\geqslant w_{\min}(\Omega).

Then the convex set
\overline{P_{k+1}}=\overline{\Omega}\cap H_{k+1}

contains the convex hull of the set
P_{k}
and of both points
x_{1}^{(k+1)}
,
x_{2}^{(k+1)}
.
The measure of this convex hull is at least

\frac{t_{1}\operatorname{mes}_{k}P_{k}}{k+1}+\frac{t_{2}\operatorname{mes}_{k}P_{k}}{k+1}.

Now, due to (
3.2
)

\operatorname{mes}_{k+1}P_{k+1}\geqslant\frac{(t_{1}+t_{2})\operatorname{mes}_{k}P_{k}}{k+1}\geqslant\frac{w_{\min}(\Omega)^{k+1}}{(k+1)!},

which is (
3.2
) for the index
k+1
.

Choosing
k=d
in (
3.2
) we obtain

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded convex domain.
Put

w\equiv w(\Omega):=\left(d!\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega\right)^{1/d}.

(3.3)

Then

w_{\min}(\Omega)\leqslant w(\Omega).

(3.4)

Remark 3.4
.

We provided the proof of Theorem
3.3
for the sake of completeness.
The question about minimizing the volume of convex domain of given minimal width in
\mathbb{R}^{d}

is known as Kakeya-Pál problem.
The answer is known
[
16
]
for the case
d=2

3.3
Functions
u_{\eta}

In the sequel in this section we assume

x=(x_{1},y)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},\qquad x_{1}\in\mathbb{R},\quad y\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},

\Omega\subset\left\{x=(x_{1},y):|x_{1}|\leqslant\frac{w}{2}\right\},

where the number

is defined by the formula (

Lemma 3.6
.

Let
\delta>0
.
Then

\int_{\Omega}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)dx\geqslant\left(1-\frac{w^{2}\delta^{2}}{2}\right)\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega.

Proof.

The inequality

0\leqslant 1-\cos z\leqslant\frac{z^{2}}{2}\qquad\forall\ z\in\mathbb{R}

implies the bound

\left|\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega-\int_{\Omega}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)dx\right|\leqslant\int_{\Omega}\left|1-\cos(2x_{1}\delta)\right|dx\leqslant\int_{\Omega}2x_{1}^{2}\delta^{2}dx\leqslant\frac{w^{2}\delta^{2}}{2}\,\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega.\quad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

Let

\lambda\geqslant\delta^{2},\qquad\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\qquad|\eta|^{2}+\delta^{2}=\lambda.

Introduce the function

u_{\eta}(x)=\cos(x_{1}\delta)\,e^{i\langle y,\eta\rangle},\qquad\text{where}\quad x=(x_{1},y).

(3.5)

It is clear that

-\Delta u_{\eta}=\lambda u_{\eta}.

(3.6)

Lemma 3.7
.

Let

\eta,\zeta\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1},\qquad|\eta|=|\zeta|=\sqrt{\lambda-\delta^{2}}.

Then

\lambda\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}\,\overline{u_{\zeta}}\,dx-\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{\eta},\nabla u_{\zeta}\rangle dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\delta^{2}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)+\frac{|\eta-\zeta|^{2}}{2}\cos^{2}(x_{1}\delta)\right)e^{i\langle y,\eta-\zeta\rangle}dx.

Proof.

We have

\int_{\Omega}u_{\eta}\,\overline{u_{\zeta}}\,dx=\int_{\Omega}\cos^{2}(x_{1}\delta)e^{i\langle y,\eta-\zeta\rangle}dx,

\int_{\Omega}\langle\nabla u_{\eta},\nabla u_{\zeta}\rangle dx=\int_{\Omega}\left(\delta^{2}\sin^{2}(x_{1}\delta)+\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle\cos^{2}(x_{1}\delta)\right)e^{i\langle y,\eta-\zeta\rangle}dx.

Taking into account the identity

\frac{|\eta-\zeta|^{2}}{2}=\lambda-\delta^{2}-\langle\eta,\zeta\rangle,

we obtain the result.

Lemma
3.6
and Lemma
3.7
imply the following

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\eta\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}
,
|\eta|=\sqrt{\lambda-\delta^{2}}
.
Then

\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{\eta}|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\eta}|^{2}dx\geqslant\delta^{2}\left(1-\frac{w^{2}\delta^{2}}{2}\right)\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega.

In the sequel we take

\delta=\frac{1}{w(\Omega)}.

Then

\lambda\int_{\Omega}|u_{\eta}|^{2}dx-\int_{\Omega}|\nabla u_{\eta}|^{2}dx\geqslant\frac{\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega}{2w(\Omega)^{2}}.

(3.7)

Remark 3.9
.

In the sequel we consider

\lambda\geqslant\lambda_{1}(\Omega)\geqslant\frac{j_{\frac{d}{2}-1}^{2}\omega_{d}^{2/d}}{(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega)^{2/d}},\qquad\text{and}\qquad\delta^{2}=\frac{1}{\left(d!\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega\right)^{2/d}}.

so the condition

is fulfilled.

Lemma 3.12
.

The estimates

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dx\right|^{2}d\theta\leqslant(2\pi)^{d-1}w(\Omega)\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\left|\int_{\Omega}\cos^{2}(x_{1}\delta)e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dx\right|^{2}d\theta\leqslant(2\pi)^{d-1}w(\Omega)\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega

hold true.

Proof.

Given
x_{1}
denote by
\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})
a cross-section of the domain
\Omega

with the corresponding hyperplane,

\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})=\left\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d-1}:(x_{1},y)\in\Omega\right\}.

Then

\left\|\int_{\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})}e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dy\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}^{2}=\int_{\mathbb{R}^{d-1}}\left|\int_{\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})}e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dy\right|^{2}d\theta=(2\pi)^{d-1}\operatorname{mes}_{d-1}\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1}),

as the Fourier transform preserves
L_{2}
-norm.
Therefore,

\displaystyle\left\|\int_{\Omega}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dx\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}=\left\|\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}\cos(2x_{1}\delta)dx_{1}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})}e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dy\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}

\displaystyle\leqslant\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}dx_{1}\left\|\int_{\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})}e^{i\langle y,\theta\rangle}dy\right\|_{L_{2}(\mathbb{R}^{d-1})}=\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}dx_{1}(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\left(\operatorname{mes}_{d-1}\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\leqslant(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{w}\left(\int_{-w/2}^{w/2}dx_{1}\operatorname{mes}_{d-1}\Omega^{\prime}(x_{1})\right)^{1/2}=(2\pi)^{\frac{d-1}{2}}\sqrt{w}\left(\operatorname{mes}_{d}\Omega\right)^{1/2}.

The proof of the second inequality is similar.

Lemma 3.13
.

Let
m\geqslant 2
,
a,b\geqslant 0
,
G\in C(\mathbb{R}^{m})
,

0\leqslant G(\theta)\leqslant a\quad\forall\ \theta\in\mathbb{R}^{m},\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}G(\theta)\,d\theta\leqslant b.

Then

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{G(\theta)\,d\theta}{|\theta|}\leqslant\frac{m}{m-1}\left(\omega_{m}ab^{m-1}\right)^{1/m}.

Proof.

Let us consider the function

h(r)=\int_{|\theta|\geqslant r}\frac{G(\theta)\,d\theta}{|\theta|}.

Clearly, the integral
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{G(\theta)\,d\theta}{|\theta|}
converges,
thus, the function
h
is well defined, non-negative and continuous on
[0,\infty)
.
Moreover,

rh(r)\leqslant\int_{|\theta|\geqslant r}G(\theta)\,d\theta,\quad\text{so}\quad rh(r)\mathop{\longrightarrow}\limits_{r\to\infty}0.

Next,

h^{\prime}(r)=-\frac{1}{r}\int_{|\theta|=r}G(\theta)\,dS(\theta),

(3.13)

therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}G(\theta)\,d\theta=\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\int_{|\theta|=r}G(\theta)\,dS(\theta)=-\int_{0}^{\infty}rh^{\prime}(r)\,dr=\int_{0}^{\infty}h(r)\,dr,

and

\int_{0}^{\infty}h(r)\,dr\leqslant b.

(3.14)

Furthermore, (
3.13
) implies

h^{\prime}(r)\geqslant-m\omega_{m}ar^{m-2},

so,

h(r)=h(0)+\int_{0}^{r}h^{\prime}(t)\,dt\geqslant h(0)-m\omega_{m}a\int_{0}^{r}t^{m-2}dt=h(0)-\frac{m\omega_{m}a}{m-1}\,r^{m-1}.

Denote

r_{0}=\left(\frac{(m-1)h(0)}{m\omega_{m}a}\right)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}.

Then by virtue of (
3.14
)

\displaystyle b\geqslant\int_{0}^{\infty}h(r)\,dr\geqslant\int_{0}^{r_{0}}\left(h(0)-\frac{m\omega_{m}a}{m-1}\,r^{m-1}\right)dr

\displaystyle=h(0)r_{0}-\frac{\omega_{m}ar_{0}^{m}}{m-1}=\frac{m-1}{m}\,r_{0}h(0)=\left(\frac{(m-1)h(0)}{m}\right)^{\frac{m}{m-1}}\frac{1}{(\omega_{m}a)^{\frac{1}{m-1}}},

and therefore,

\left(\omega_{m}ab^{m-1}\right)^{1/m}\geqslant\frac{m-1}{m}\,h(0).\qquad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

0,\quad&|\theta|\geqslant 1,\end{cases}

then

a=1,\quad b=\omega_{m}\quad\text{and}\quad\int_{\mathbb{R}^{m}}\frac{G(\theta)\,d\theta}{|\theta|}=\frac{m\omega_{m}}{m-1}.

3.6

Given a Hermitian matrix

of a subspace of

Lemma 3.19
.

Let

B=B^{*}\in\operatorname{Mat}(\mathbb{C},N\times N),\qquad B=B_{0}+B_{1},

where
B_{0}
is a diagonal part of
B
and
B_{1}
is an off-diagonal part of
B

B_{0}\geqslant aI_{N\times N}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|B_{1}\|_{S_{2}}^{2}\leqslant b(N^{2}-N),

N_{+}(B)\geqslant N-\frac{b(N^{2}-N)}{a^{2}}.

Proof.

The second condition (
3.18

\#\left(\operatorname{spec}(B_{1})\cap(-\infty,-a]\right)\leqslant\frac{b(N^{2}-N)}{a^{2}}.

Therefore,

\#\left(\operatorname{spec}(B)\cap(0,\infty)\right)\geqslant N-\frac{b(N^{2}-N)}{a^{2}}.\quad\vrule height=7.0pt,width=5.0pt,depth=0.0pt

Lemma 3.20

Fix

satisfying the conditions of Lemma
3.19
for each

Proof.

We take as
N
a nearest integer to the number
\frac{a^{2}+b}{2b}

Then we use Lemma
