Sharp Capacity Scaling of Spectral Optimizers in Learning Associative Memory
Juno Kim, Eshaan Nichani, [0.5em] Denny Wu, Alberto Bietti, Jason D. Lee, [0.5em] UC Berkeley

Abstract
Spectral optimizers such as Muon have recently shown strong empirical performance in large-scale language model training, but the source and extent of their advantage remain poorly understood. We study this question through the linear associative memory problem, a tractable model for factual recall in transformer-based models. In particular, we go beyond orthogonal embeddings and consider Gaussian inputs and outputs, which allows the number of stored associations to greatly exceed the embedding dimension. Our main result sharply characterizes the recovery rates of one step of Muon and SGD on the logistic regression loss under a power law frequency distribution. We show that the storage capacity of Muon significantly exceeds that of SGD, and moreover Muon saturates at a larger critical batch size. We further analyze the multi-step dynamics under a thresholded gradient approximation and show that Muon achieves a substantially faster initial recovery rate than SGD, while both methods eventually converge to the information-theoretic limit at comparable speeds. Experiments on synthetic tasks validate the predicted scaling laws. Our analysis provides a quantitative understanding of the signal amplification of Muon and lays the groundwork for establishing scaling laws across more practical language modeling tasks and optimizers.

Introduction

Large language models (LLMs) with billions of parameters are typically trained using adaptive first-order optimization algorithms. The workhorse of modern neural network optimization has long been the Adam optimizer and its variants
[
24
,
33
]
. However, there has been growing interest in matrix-based or
spectral
optimizers
[
35
,
16
,
53
,
21
]
, which explicitly utilize the matrix structure of neural network parameters. Among these methods, Muon
[
21
]
has shown strong empirical performance in large-scale pretraining studies
[
32
]
, even outperforming Adam at sufficiently large batch sizes
[
58
,
47
]
.
Muon updates each weight matrix in the approximate direction of the polar factor, or spectral orthogonalization, of the negative gradient. Ignoring accumulation, this can also be interpreted as steepest descent with respect to the spectral norm
[
2
]
. However, it remains unclear which aspects of modern language model training make this update particularly effective.

To investigate this question, we analyze the dynamics of Muon versus stochastic gradient descent (SGD) on the task of learning linear
associative memory
. The associative memory task, introduced in
6
,
38
, provides a simple model of factual recall in language models
[
43
,
1
]
, and captures the ability of transformer-based models to store factual knowledge within the self-attention matrices. The goal is to store a collection of atomic associations (i.e., facts), expressed as
N
pairs of input and output embeddings
\{(v_{i},u_{i})\}_{i\in[N]}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, using a weight matrix
{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
so that
u_{i}\approx{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}
. We train
{\mathbf{W}}
by casting this task as a multiclass logistic regression problem with logits given by
u_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}
and optimizing the cross-entropy loss. A formal description of the problem is given in Section
3
.

Recent work has studied the benefit of spectral optimizers on related associative memory tasks
[
56
,
30
]
, but these results rely on an orthogonality assumption on the embeddings
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
. While this assumption simplifies the optimization analysis, it also requires the embedding dimension
d
to be larger than the number of stored items
N
. In contrast, we study the regime in which
u_{i}
and
v_{i}
are drawn i.i.d. from an isotropic Gaussian distribution, so that the number of stored items can greatly exceed the embedding dimension (
N\gg d
). This captures the ability of language models to store items, or features, in
superposition

[
10
]
, where the total number of features is far greater than the ambient dimension. Indeed, under this random-embedding model, it is information-theoretically possible for
{\mathbf{W}}
to store up to
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{2})
items
[
38
]
. At the same time, removing orthogonality makes the learning dynamics substantially more intricate
[
52
]
and they remain poorly understood.

Motivated by Zipf’s law for language modeling, we assume that the
i
th item appears with power-law frequency
p_{i}\sim i^{-\alpha}
, parallel to previous theoretical analyses on scaling laws
[
37
,
5
,
31
,
40
,
42
,
27
]
. We also consider the minibatch versions of SGD and Muon, where at each timestep a new batch of size
B
is sampled with replacement.
Under this setting, our main result sharply characterizes the one-step recovery of Muon, showing that Muon outperforms SGD and stores significantly more items than in the orthogonal case.

Theorem 1

(Informal version of Theorems

,

)
.

Let
d
be the embedding dimension and
B
be the batch size, and suppose the
i

p_{i}\propto i^{-\alpha}
for
\alpha>1
. One step of Muon on the associative memory task recovers the top
\widetilde{\Theta}(\min\{d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\})
most frequent items, while one step of SGD recovers the top
\widetilde{\Theta}(\min\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\})
most frequent items.

This further implies that the
critical batch size
, beyond which increasing batch size does not yield performance gains, is much larger for Muon compared to SGD. The capacity exponents and batch size saturation predicted by our theory are empirically verified by our experiments (Figure

).

We also study the multi-step trajectories of Muon and SGD under a simplifying thresholded gradient update, and show the following scaling laws for the recovery rate (for brevity, we only state the population version below).

Theorem 2

(Informal version of Theorems
9
,
10
)
.

Under the thresholded update,
t
steps of Muon recover the top
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{2-(1-\frac{1}{2a})^{t}})
items. In contrast,
t
steps of SGD recover the top
d_{t}
items where
d_{t}
is given by the recursion
d_{t+1}=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{t})
if
d_{t}\lesssim d
, and
d_{t+1}=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}})
if
d_{t}\gtrsim d
.

The main takeaways from our analysis are as follows.

(1)

Muon improves storage efficiency.
In the population regime
B\rightarrow\infty
, one step of Muon is already able to recover the top
d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
items, while one step of SGD only recovers the top
d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
(see Figure
1(a)
). Surprisingly, a single step of Muon is able to store more than
d
items, which is the maximum when embeddings are constrained to be orthogonal. In other words, Muon is able to effectively store more features than dimensions via superposition.

(2)

The benefit of Muon comes at larger batch sizes.
When the batch size
B
is small, Muon and SGD both recover the top
B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
items. However, the performance of SGD saturates at a batch size of
B\asymp\sqrt{d}
, while Muon saturates at the much larger batch size of
B\asymp d^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}
(see Figure
1(b)
). This provides evidence that Muon has the ability to handle much larger batch sizes, and aligns with empirical observations that Muon significantly outperforms non-spectral optimizers only at large batch sizes
[
58
]
.

(3)

Muon accelerates early in training.
The SGD exponent initially exhibits a slow linear scaling and requires
\lceil 2\alpha\rceil
steps to reach
d_{t}\gtrsim d
, while Muon exceeds this in a single step. However, once SGD enters this regime, both updates obey the same recursion
d_{t+1}\sim d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
with the same convergence rate to the optimal
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{2})
capacity. Thus, the main benefits of Muon (with appropriate batch size) appear earlier in training when gradients are strongly anisotropic, explaining the short-term gains observed in
[
47
]
.

The rest of the paper is organized as follows. In Section
3
, we formally define the associative memory task and the family of spectral optimizers considered in our analysis. Section
4
contains our main results on the scaling of a single step of Muon and SGD, as well as an argument that Muon is the asymptotically optimal one-step update. In Section
5
, we extend our scaling analysis to multiple steps along the Muon and SGD trajectories. We conclude in Section
6
with simulations verifying our predicted scaling laws and batch size analysis, as well as experiments with transformers on a more sophisticated in-context recall task.

2
Related Work

Associative memory and factual recall.

Associative memory has a long history in neural computation
[
59
,
26
,
18
]
. Recent work has shown that transformer weights can be viewed as associative memories storing input-output mappings between pairs of concepts
[
14
,
4
,
6
,
20
]
. This perspective is especially useful for modeling factual recall
[
1
]
, where such mechanisms encode factual knowledge directly in the weights of a transformer
[
36
,
13
,
38
]
.

Our most relevant points of comparison are
56
,
30
, which analyze Muon on similar associative memory tasks. Their analysis assumes that the embeddings
\{u_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}
and
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}
are pairwise orthogonal, greatly simplifying the study of the polar map. However, this assumption also limits the model capacity to at most
N\leq d
stored associations. By contrast, because
{\mathbf{W}}
has
d^{2}
parameters, the information-theoretically optimal capacity is
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{2})

[
38
]
, which requires storing embeddings in superposition. In this regime, we show that a single Muon step already recovers
\widetilde{\Theta}\!\left(d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}\right)
items, far beyond what is possible under orthogonality. We further derive scaling laws for the multi-step Muon and SGD dynamics, and show that both updates indeed approach the optimal capacity.

Theoretical analyses of Muon.

A number of recent works have sought to rigorously characterize the benefits of Muon and other matrix-based optimizers over SGD. One line of work derives convergence guarantees using tools from convex optimization and online learning.
48
,
8
,
22
prove convergence guarantees for Muon that depend on smoothness in the spectral norm or the spectral norm of the weight matrix itself.
19
derive regret bounds and corresponding non-convex optimization rates. Beyond Muon,
60
develop a framework for analyzing convergence rates of a broad class of matrix preconditioners on smooth convex problems, while
29
adopt a structure-aware preconditioning perspective to introduce a new family of matrix-based optimizers.

A second line of work studies the loss reduction after a single descent step.
9
show that Muon achieves a larger one-step loss reduction than SGD when the gradient rank exceeds the activation rank.
49
introduces an “isotropic curvature model” based on a single optimization step and show that Muon is optimal in certain regimes. However,
15
demonstrate that such single-step arguments can fail to predict full end-to-end convergence rates.

Finally, other works compare Muon and SGD on specific problem classes.
11
show that, for separable classification, Muon converges to the solution maximizing the spectral-norm margin. For matrix-valued linear regression,
55
characterize the risk of Muon on isotropic data, while
50
show faster convergence than SGD under imbalanced covariates.
34
further show that, in matrix factorization, Muon attains a convergence rate faster than SGD and independent of the condition number.

Adaptivity to heavy-tailed data.

Our results show that Muon is particularly effective when the fact distribution is power-law distributed with heavy tail. Similar advantages have also been observed for other adaptive optimizers.
28
,
61
show that Adam and its limiting variant SignSGD outperform SGD when the class distribution follows a power law.
27
further prove that SignSGD outperforms SGD for learning a bigram model, while
23
derive scaling laws for SignSGD in the power-law random features model.

3
Setting: Associative Memory

Linear associative memory.

The goal of the associative memory task is to store a collection of atomic associations, or
facts
. Let
[N]
be the input and output vocabulary. A set of facts is defined by a bijection
f^{*}:[N]\rightarrow[N]
, where the input token
i
is mapped to the output token
f^{*}(i)
. Each token is assigned an embedding vector
v_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
and an unembedding vector
u_{i}\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
, sampled i.i.d. from the distribution
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
. Without loss of generality, we will assume that
f^{*}(i)=i
for all
i\in[N]
. As an illustrative example, consider the set of countries
\mathcal{S}=\{\text{USA, France, Japan,}\dots\}
and the set of capitals
\mathcal{A}=\{\text{Washington~D.C., Paris, Tokyo}\dots\}
, with the goal being to store the mapping between each country and its capital. The embeddings of the countries
USA, France, Japan,
\dots
are
v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\dots
respectively, and the embeddings of the capitals
Washington D.C., Paris, Tokyo,
\dots
are
u_{1},u_{2},u_{3},\dots
respectively.

We consider training a linear associative memory model, given by a weight matrix
{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, to store the fact dataset as the following multi-class classification problem. The score prediction for the unembedding token
u_{j}
associated to
v_{i}
is defined as

\displaystyle\hat{p}_{{\mathbf{W}}}(j\mid i):=\frac{\exp(u_j^\top\bW v_i)}{\sum_{k\in[N]}\exp(u_k^\top\bW v_i)},\quad\forall j\in[N].

Let
p\in\Delta^{N}
denote the vector of probabilities of each item in the dataset. The population cross-entropy loss is then defined as

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{W}}):=\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[-\log p_{{\mathbf{W}}}(i\mid i)]=-\sum_{i\in[N]}p_{i}\Bigg(u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}-\log\Bigg(\sum_{j \in[N]} \exp(u_j^\top\bW v_i)\Bigg)\Bigg).

(1)

We assume that
p
follows a power law,
p_{i}\sim i^{-\alpha}
with exponent
\alpha>1
. This condition is motivated by Zipf’s law in statistical linguistics, which states that the frequency of a word decays approximately as a power of its rank, indicating that such heavy-tailed structure arises naturally in language
[
41
]
. Such a power law source condition is common in prior analyses of scaling laws
[
7
,
37
,
5
,
31
,
40
,
42
,
27
,
30
]
.

We will consider optimizing
{\mathbf{W}}
via the minibatch variant of Muon and SGD. Let
B
be the batch size. A
minibatch

\mathcal{B}
is defined as a collection of tokens
\mathcal{B}:=\{i_{1},\dots,i_{B}\}
, where each token is sampled i.i.d from
p
. The loss on a minibatch
\mathcal{B}
is defined by

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{W}};\mathcal{B}):=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{i\in\mathcal{B}}-\log\hat{p}_{{\mathbf{W}}}(i\mid i)=-\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}\Bigg(u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}-\log\Bigg(\sum_{j \in[N]} \exp(u_j^\top\bW v_i)\Bigg)\Bigg),

where
q_{i}:=\frac{1}{B}\sum_{j\in\mathcal{B}}\mathbf{1}_{\{i=j\}}
are the empirical frequencies of each token in the batch
\mathcal{B}
. The negative gradient at some
{\mathbf{W}}
is thus

\displaystyle-\nabla_{\mathbf{W}}L({\mathbf{W}};\mathcal{B})=\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}\Bigg(u_{i}-\sum_{j\in[N]}u_{j}\hat{p}_{\mathbf{W}}(j\mid i)\Bigg)v_{i}^{\top}.

(2)

Muon.

The Muon optimizer
[
21
]
directly operates on weight matrices. Let
{\mathbf{G}}=-\nabla_{{\mathbf{W}}}L({\mathbf{W}};\mathcal{B})
be the negative gradient of the loss (we will omit momentum in our treatment). Denote by
{\mathbf{G}}={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
the singular value decomposition (SVD) of
{\mathbf{G}}
. The polar map is defined as
\pol({\mathbf{G}}):={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
; if
{\mathbf{G}}
is full rank, then
\pol({\mathbf{G}})={\mathbf{G}}({\mathbf{G}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}})^{-1/2}
. The Muon update is

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}\leftarrow{\mathbf{W}}+\eta\cdot\pol({\mathbf{G}}).

In practice, rather than computing the exact SVD, one instead approximates
\pol({\mathbf{G}})
via a constant number of
Newton–Schulz
iterations. Let
\varphi(z)
be a quadratic or higher-order polynomial. A single Newton–Schulz iteration computes the mapping

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}\mapsto{\mathbf{G}}\varphi({\mathbf{G}}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}})={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{S}}\varphi({\mathbf{S}}^{2}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}.

The output of multiple steps of Newton–Schulz is thus of the form
{\mathbf{U}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
, where
h(z)
is the function obtained by composing
z\mapsto z\varphi(z^{2})
with itself multiple times.
\varphi
is typically chosen so that
h(z)\approx 1
. This motivates a broad class of
spectral optimizers
: given a function
h:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
satisfying
h(0)=0
, one can define the spectral map
h({\mathbf{G}})={\mathbf{U}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
and update the weight matrix as
{\mathbf{W}}\leftarrow{\mathbf{W}}+\eta h({\mathbf{G}})
. Within this scheme, gradient descent corresponds to
h(z)=z
, while exact Muon corresponds to
h(z)=\sgn(z)
.

In this work, we will focus on a stabilized approximation to Muon:
h_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}
for a hyperparameter
\lambda
(see right), which as we will see determines the ‘resolution’ of the singular spectrum. The limit
\lambda\to 0^{+}
recovers the exact polar map. Given schedules
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
,
\{\lambda_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
, the Muon updates
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
are defined by

\displaystyle{\mathbf{W}}_{t+1}={\mathbf{W}}_{t}+\eta_{t}{\mathbf{G}}_{t}({\mathbf{G}}_{t}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}_{t}+\lambda_{t}^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})^{-1/2},\quad{\mathbf{G}}_{t}:=-\nabla_{{\mathbf{W}}}L({\mathbf{W}}_{t};\mathcal{B}_{t})

(3)

initialized at
{\mathbf{W}}_{0}=0_{d\times d}
. We also denote the estimated scores by
\hat{p}_{t}=\hat{p}_{{\mathbf{W}}_{t}}
.

Remark
.

We consider the stabilized approximation
h_{\lambda}
primarily for technical convenience. In Section
4.3
, we give a heuristic argument that this update is asymptotically optimal for sufficiently small
\lambda
, and moreover the exact polar map should attain the same rates. A similar smoothed approximation is considered in
19
, and, unlike Muon, is shown to converge on non-smooth, non-convex problems. Relatedly,
15
demonstrate problems where using a constant number of Newton-Schulz iterations outperforms the exact polar map.

4
One Step of Muon

4.1
One-step recovery of Muon and SGD

We say that the weight matrix
{\mathbf{W}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

recovers
or
stores
item
i
if
\argmax_{j\in[N]}\hat{p}(j\mid i)=i
, i.e., the diagonal or
signal
logit (
j=i
) dominates all off-diagonal or
interaction
logits (
j\neq i
):

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}>\max_{j\neq i}u_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}v_{i}.

(4)

Our main result sharply characterizes the set of recovered items after one Muon update.

Theorem 3

(one-step recovery of Muon)
.

Let
u_{i},v_{i}
for
i\in[N]
be i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
vectors. Let
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}=-\nabla_{{\mathbf{W}}}L({\mathbf{W}}_{0};\mathcal{B})
be the negative gradient at initialization of the empirical loss on a minibatch
\mathcal{B}
of size
B
, or the population loss (equivalently
B=\infty
). Suppose
N=\poly(d)
,
N\gtrsim d^{2\alpha+1}
and set

\displaystyle\lambda\asymp\max\left\{\frac{(\log d)^{2\alpha+2}}{d^{\alpha}},\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{B}\right\}.

Then with high probability, the one-step Muon update
{\mathbf{W}}_{1}\propto h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})
recovers all items up to

\displaystyle i\lesssim\min\left\{i^{\star},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\},\quad i^{\star}\asymp d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(\log d)^{-2-\frac{5}{\alpha}}.

This bound is tight (up to polylog factors) in the sense that for items
i\gg i^{\star}
, the signal and interaction terms in Eq. (
4
) will be of the same order, so recovery cannot be guaranteed; moreover, items
i\gg B^{1/\alpha}
have vanishing probability to even be observed in the minibatch
\mathcal{B}
, and hence will only be learned sporadically.
From this, we see that the
critical batch size
, beyond which increasing
B
no longer yields gains in recovery, is

\displaystyle B_{\Muon}^{\star}=\widetilde{\Theta}((i^{\star})^{\alpha})=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}}),

and this allows us to recover
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}})
items. We also note that the condition
N\gtrsim d^{2\alpha+1}
can be removed by considering correlation loss.

As a corollary, we obtain the following guarantee for the loss decrease after one step.

Corollary 4
.

Taking the learning rate
\eta\asymp(\log d)^{-4}\sqrt{d}
in the setting of Theorem
3
, the one-step update
{\mathbf{W}}_{1}=\eta h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})
achieves loss

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{W}}_{1})\leq\widetilde{O}\quantity(\max\left\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}+\frac 12-\alpha}, B^{\frac{1}{\alpha} -1}\right\})

and moreover no item is significantly misclassified, that is,
\hat{p}_{1}(i\mid i)\geq(1-o(1))\sup_{j\neq i}\hat{p}_{1}(j\mid i)
for all
i\in[N]
.

The proof of Theorem
3
is developed throughout Appendices
A
,
B
. A sketch of the main ideas is provided in Section
4.2
. We start by observing from Eq. (
2
) that the gradient of the cross-entropy loss
L({\mathbf{W}})
at initialization is roughly
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}\approx\sum_{i}p_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
. If the embeddings
u_{i},v_{i}
were orthogonal, Muon would then output
{\mathbf{W}}_{1}\propto h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})\approx\sum_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
which already classifies all items correctly; however this constrains the number of items
N\leq d
, far less than the information-theoretic optimum
\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{2})
. In our non-orthogonal setting,
N
can be much larger, but we must now account for the correlations between embeddings.

Let us now focus on each signal term
u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}_{1}v_{i}
. The main contribution comes from the aligned rank-one spike
p_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
in the gradient
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}
. At the same time, a large fraction of the singular values of
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}
lie below
d^{-\alpha}
. The map
h_{\lambda}
amplifies these by a factor of
\lambda^{-1}\sim d^{\alpha}
, which also boosts the spike magnitude
p_{i}
by the same factor, allowing us to recover items with lower frequencies. Thus,
\lambda
effectively acts as a
scale of resolution
for the singular spectrum; the implications of this is discussed in Section
4.3
.

In contrast, we show the following bound on the number of recovered items for vanilla gradient descent on the same objective.

Theorem 5

(one-step recovery of SGD)
.

In the setting of Theorem
3
and
N\gtrsim d
, the number of items recovered by the one-step SGD update
{\mathbf{W}}_{1}^{\SGD}=\eta{\mathbf{G}}_{0}
is

\displaystyle\widetilde{\Theta}\quantity(\min\left\{d^\frac{1}{2\alpha},B^\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\})

with high probability. In addition, for any choice of learning rate
\eta
, the loss is lower bounded as

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{W}}_{1}^{\SGD})\geq\widetilde{\Omega}\quantity(\max\left\{ d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac 12}, B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\right\}).

The intuitive reason for this threshold is that an item beyond this has signal
p_{i}\ll 1/\sqrt{d}
, which is drowned out by the noise of order
p_{j}/\sqrt{d}
from the
j\lesssim\log d
most frequent items, and so is unlikely to have the highest score.
Thus, the number of items recovered by Muon greatly improves upon that of SGD by a factor of
d
. Furthermore, the critical batch size for SGD is
B_{\SGD}^{\star}=\widetilde{\Theta}(\sqrt{d})
, which is much smaller compared to
B_{\Muon}^{\star}=\widetilde{\Theta}(d^{\alpha+\frac{1}{2}})
.

4.2
Proof sketch of Theorem
3

We start by approximating the gradient as a sum of independent rank-one terms:

\displaystyle-\nabla_{{\mathbf{W}}}L({\mathbf{W}}_{0};\mathcal{B})=\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}(u_{i}-\bar{u})v_{i}^{\top}\approx\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}=:{\mathbf{G}}

and defining the logits
\gamma_{ij}:=u_{j}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}})v_{i}
(omitting
\eta
). We analyze the signal and interaction terms separately.

Lower bounding signal logits (Appendix
A
).

Denote the leave-one-out gradient
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}:={\mathbf{G}}-q_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
, so that
u_{i}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i})v_{i}
is random with size
\widetilde{O}(1/\sqrt{d})
. We study how the
i
th logit behaves as we gradually add the
u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
term back in via the auxiliary function

\displaystyle\phi(q)=u_{i}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i}+qu_{i}v_{i}^{\top})v_{i},\quad q\geq 0.

(5)

By definition,
\gamma_{ij}=\phi(q_{i})
, which we analyze via Taylor expansion. The slope
\phi^{\prime}(0)
can be computed explicitly via the Daleckii–Krein formula (Proposition
11
), which computes the Fréchet derivative of a matrix function along a specified perturbation direction. Let
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}
be the SVD with singular values
{\mathbf{S}}=\diag(s_{1},\cdots,s_{d})
in decreasing order and
a={\mathbf{A}}^{\top}u_{i}
,
b={\mathbf{B}}^{\top}v_{i}
. Then
\phi^{\prime}(0)
is given as the sum of nonnegative terms

\displaystyle\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\neq\ell}\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{k})+h_{\lambda}(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}+s_{\ell}}(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}+\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{k})-h_{\lambda}(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}-s_{\ell}}(a_{k}b_{\ell}+a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}+\sum_{k}h_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s_{k})a_{k}^{2}b_{k}^{2}.

We focus on the first term. As
a,b
are i.i.d. Gaussian conditioned on
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}
,
(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}\approx 1/d^{2}
. Since
z\mapsto h_{\lambda}(z)/z
is decreasing, the sum is dominated by small singular values. We then show that the ‘bulk’ singular value
s_{d/2}=\widetilde{O}(d^{-\alpha})
with high probability (Lemmas
14
,
17
). Hence choosing
\lambda
at the same scale,

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)\gtrsim\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{d/2})}{s_{d/2}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{d/2}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}\gtrsim\lambda^{-1}.

(6)

Moreover, we show that
\phi
is nondecreasing and
|\phi^{\prime\prime}(0)|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
. Taylor expanding
\phi
around zero and optimizing the radius yields the lower bound

\displaystyle\gamma_{ii}=\phi(q_{i})\gtrsim\min\left\{\frac{q_{i}}{\lambda},1\right\}-\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.

Upper bounding interaction logits (Appendix
B
).

The interaction logits turn out to be much more challenging to control. A naive approach is to use that
h_{\lambda}
is
\lambda^{-1}
-Lipschitz with respect to operator norm (Proposition
26
), so
(u_{j},v_{j})\mapsto\gamma_{ij}=u_{j}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-j}+q_{j}u_{j}v_{j}^{\top})v_{i}
is a Lipschitz mapping of Gaussians and so exhibits good concentration. This argument works when either
q_{i}
or
q_{j}\ll\lambda
, but fails to bound ‘large’ interactions where both
i,j\leq r
for a threshold
r\approx d
. For these terms, we first invoke a block resolvent integral representation amenable to series expansion, then develop a nonasymptotic perturbative analysis reminiscent of moment methods in random matrix theory. A technical overview is provided in Appendix
B.1
for the interested reader. In the end, we show:

\displaystyle|\gamma_{ij}|\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}\quad\forall j\neq i.

We have thus proved that
\gamma_{ii}>\max_{j\neq i}\gamma_{ij}
if
q_{i}/\lambda\gtrsim 1/\sqrt{d}
(ignoring log factors). In the population regime, from
p_{i}\asymp i^{-\alpha}
we conclude that all items
i\lesssim d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
are recovered w.h.p. In the minibatch setting, we incur an additional information-theoretic threshold: items
i\gg B^{1/\alpha}
, that is
p_{i}\ll 1/B
, are unlikely to be observed in the minibatch at all, and thus will not be learned.

4.3
Optimality of Muon

A natural follow-up question to Theorem
3
is: is the
d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
one-step recovery rate optimal for the linear associative memory task, or can it be improved by choosing a different estimator
h({\mathbf{G}}_{0})
of
{\mathbf{W}}
? For example, our analysis used
\lambda\sim d^{-\alpha}
while the limit
\lambda\to 0
recovers the exact polar map. Can choosing such a sharper resolution improve our results?

In this section, we give a negative answer to this question by providing a heuristic argument for the one-step optimality of our stabilized variant of Muon. We first show that the Bayes optimal estimator must be spectrally equivariant, that is for the SVD of the gradient
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
, it holds that
h({\mathbf{G}}_{0})={\mathbf{U}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
and
h({\mathbf{S}})
is diagonal.

Proposition 6
.

Let
\Spec(d)
denote the set of bi-orthogonally equivariant measurable maps
h:\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that
\sup\lVert h\rVert_{\F}<\infty
, that is,
h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top})={\mathbf{U}}h({\mathbf{X}}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
for all
{\mathbf{X}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
and
{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}\in O(d)
. The Bayes optimal update rule w.r.t.
L
is in
\Spec(d)
, that is, for the Bayes risk
\mathcal{R}(h):=\E_{(u_{i},v_{i})_{i\in[N]},\mathcal{B}}[L(h({\mathbf{G}}_{0}))]
it holds that

\displaystyle\inf_{h:\sup\lVert h\rVert_{\F}<\infty}\mathcal{R}(h)=\inf_{h\in\Spec(d)}\mathcal{R}(h).

This is essentially a corollary of the Hunt–Stein theorem on minimax tests of invariant statistical problems. Any bi-orthogonal conjugate
h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{X}}):={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}){\mathbf{V}}
of
h
will have the same Bayes risk due to rotation invariance. Then the estimator
\bar{h}\in\Spec(d)
constructed by averaging
h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}
over Haar measure
{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}\sim O(d)\times O(d)
satisfies
\mathcal{R}(\bar{h})\leq\mathcal{R}(h)
due to convexity of
L
.

We remark that
h\in\Spec(d)
does not preclude nonseparable maps where each diagonal entry
h({\mathbf{S}})_{ii}
can depend on the entire spectrum
{\mathbf{S}}
. Nonetheless, such maps are in general difficult to implement as they require computing the full SVD, which Muon (with Newton–Schulz iterations) is designed to avoid. Thus, we restrict our attention to separable maps
h({\mathbf{S}})=\diag(h(s_{i}))
for a scalar-valued function
h
. Since the inputs to
h
are bounded by
\lVert{\mathbf{G}}_{0}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)
w.h.p., we can always rescale
h
to have bounded outputs. We also assume a mild monotonicity property:

Assumption 7
.

h:\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\to[0,1]
is
C^{1}
and
h(z)/z
is nonincreasing.

Intuitively, this means that smaller singular values are blown up by a larger multiplicative factor. Note that we do not require
h
itself to be monotonic. For example, the classical cubic Newton–Schulz iteration
h(z)=\frac{3}{2}z-\frac{1}{2}z^{3}

[
3
]
satisfies this assumption, as well as its higher-order iterates on the interval of convergence (see right figure) — see Appendix
C.4
for details.

We now show that any
h
satisfying Assumption
7

cannot
improve the signal
\gamma_{ii}
. As in Eq. (
5
), we compute the strength of the signal via a first-order approximation of the auxiliary map
\phi
, and the slope is given via the singular values of the leave-one-out gradient
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}=\sum_{j\neq i}q_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
as

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\asymp\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k,\ell}\frac{h(s_{k})+h(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}+s_{\ell}}+\frac{h(s_{k})-h(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}-s_{\ell}}+\frac{1}{d^{2}}\sum_{k}h^{\prime}(s_{k})\lesssim\frac{1}{d}\sum_{k}\frac{h(s_{k})}{s_{k}},

where the inequality follows from
\frac{h(s_{k})+h(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}+s_{\ell}}\leq\frac{h(s_{k})}{s_{k}}+\frac{h(s_{\ell})}{s_{\ell}}
,
\frac{h(s_{k})-h(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}-s_{\ell}}\leq\min\{\frac{h(s_{k})}{s_{k}},\frac{h(s_{\ell})}{s_{\ell}}\}
and
h^{\prime}(s_{k})\leq\frac{h(s_{k})}{s_{k}}
under Assumption
7
. That is, the signal strength is roughly determined by
how much the average singular value is blown up
by
h
. If
h
is Lipschitz, this is uniformly bounded by
\lVert h\rVert_{\Lip}
(thus Eq. (
6
) is tight for
h_{\lambda}
). However, even without Lipschitz control, this is fundamentally limited by the average scale of the singular spectrum as we prove below.

Lemma 8
.

Let
s_{1}\geq\cdots\geq s_{d}
be the singular values of the leave-one-out gradient
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}
. It holds w.h.p. that
s_{d}\gtrsim d^{-\alpha-1}(\log d)^{-1}
and
\sum_{k=1}^{d}s_{k}^{-1}\lesssim d^{\alpha+1}(\log d)^{2}
.

As such, we must have
\phi^{\prime}(0)\lesssim d^{\alpha}
regardless of the choice of
h
, therefore the signal
\gamma_{ii}=\phi(q_{i})
is upper bounded (ignoring higher-order terms) as
\phi(0)+\phi^{\prime}(0)q_{i}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}+q_{i}d^{\alpha}
. In contrast, the noise and interaction terms
\phi(0)
and
\gamma_{ij}
are generally of size
\widetilde{\Theta}(1/\sqrt{d})
. As a consequence, we indeed require
q_{i}\gg d^{-\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}
, equivalently
i\ll d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
in the population regime to ensure recovery, matching the rate obtained in Theorem
3
.

For example, Lemma
8
implies that taking the resolution as
\lambda\ll d^{-\alpha-1}
instead of
\lambda\sim d^{-\alpha}
in Theorem
3
essentially gives the exact polar map
h(z)\approx\sgn(z)
, as this scale will never be ‘seen’ by the singular values. Nonetheless, even in this regime the average blowup of the singular values is of order
d^{\alpha}
, and so we will not see any improvement from using the polar map. In fact, from this argument we expect roughly the same recovery rate, which is indeed what we observe in our experiments in Section
6
.

5
Multiple Steps of Muon

We now turn our attention to the entire update trajectory of Muon. To study the macroscopic scaling behavior of this process, we will adopt a simplifying heuristic. At step
t
, suppose all items
i=1,\cdots,d_{t}
have been recovered with
\hat{p}_{t}(i\mid i)\approx 1
. We presume all items
i>d_{t}
have not been recovered at all, i.e.
\hat{p}_{t}(i\mid i)\ll 1
, and approximate the current gradient as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{t}\approx\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}^{(t)}(1-\hat{p}_{t}(i\mid i))u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}\approx\sum_{i>d_{t}}q_{i}^{(t)}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}=:\bar{{\mathbf{G}}}_{t}

(7)

where
q^{(t)}
is the frequency vector of the
t
th minibatch. This can be viewed as a
deflation
process: starting from all items
{\mathbf{G}}_{0}\approx\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
, the already-recovered items are continually removed from the gradient after each update. Under this simplification, we recursively derive the
recovery threshold

\{d_{t}\}_{t\geq 1}
after each Muon update, which yields the following sharp scaling law.

Theorem 9

(multi-step recovery of Muon)
.

Let
d_{0}=0
,
T\in\mathbb{N}
and

\displaystyle d_{t}=\widetilde{\Theta}\quantity(\min\{d^{2-(1-\frac{1}{2\alpha})^t}, B^\frac{1}{\alpha}\}),\quad\lambda_{t}=\widetilde{\Theta}\quantity(d_{t+1}^{-\alpha} \sqrt{d}),\quad\eta\asymp(\log d)^{-4}\sqrt{d}.

Then for sufficiently large
d
, the iterates
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined as
{\mathbf{W}}_{0}=0
,
{\mathbf{W}}_{t+1}={\mathbf{W}}_{t}+\eta h_{\lambda_{t}}(\bar{{\mathbf{G}}}_{t})
recover all items
i=1,\cdots,d_{t}
at all steps
t\leq T
, and moreover
L({\mathbf{W}}_{t})\leq\widetilde{O}(d_{t}^{1-\alpha})
.

Thus for sufficiently large
B
, the recovery exponent
2-(1-\frac{1}{2\alpha})^{t}
converges exponentially to the information-theoretic maximum with a fixed learning rate. In comparison, for multiple steps of SGD, we show a strictly suboptimal scaling law for
any
learning rate schedule.

Theorem 10

(multi-step recovery of SGD)
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{N}
and
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
be any learning rate schedule. Suppose the SGD iterates
\{{\mathbf{W}}_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}
defined as
{\mathbf{W}}_{0}=0
,
{\mathbf{W}}_{t+1}={\mathbf{W}}_{t}+\eta_{t}\bar{{\mathbf{G}}}_{t}
recover all items
i=1,\cdots,d_{t}
with constant probability at all steps
t\leq T
. Then it must hold that

\displaystyle d_{t+1}\lesssim\begin{cases}\min\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{t},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}&d_{t}\lesssim d,\\
\min\{d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}&d_{t}\gtrsim d.\end{cases}

Moreover, this rate is achieved (up to polylog factors) by taking
\eta_{t}=\widetilde{\Theta}(d_{t+1}^{\alpha})
.

In words, for the first
\lceil 2\alpha\rceil
steps, the recovery exponent of SGD increases linearly until
d_{t}\gtrsim d
; note that Muon already achieves this with a single update. After this point, however, the improvement recursion
d_{t+1}\sim d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{t}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
matches that of Muon. Hence Muon accelerates recovery earlier in training, but the convergence behavior for large
t
is comparable to that of SGD. This aligns with the empirical observations in
[
47
]
, where preconditioned optimizers such as Muon and SOAP
[
53
]
are found to outperform AdamW on shorter runs, but the gap narrows over longer horizons.

We give a brief intuition for this phenomenon. In the non-orthogonal setting, each item
i>d_{t}
to be recovered must compete with noise from the top individual unclassified items
j\sim d_{t}
with large frequencies, as well as the aggregate fluctuation from the bulk of the unclassified items, which leads to the two thresholds in Theorem
10
. Hence, Muon can be interpreted as effectively removing the first threshold by amplifying the bulk (but not top) singular directions. Once
d_{t}>d
, the gradient becomes relatively more isotropic and so the effect of orthogonalization is less pronounced; the second threshold becomes the limiting factor for both optimizers.

Remark
.

The rates in Theorem
9
and Theorem
10
are given for a constant (or at most logarithmically diverging) horizon
T
, and hence can achieve
\Omega(d^{2-\epsilon})
recovery rate for any
\epsilon>0
. As in Section
4.3
, this scaling is likely near-optimal in this regime. However when
T
grows further, we expect that the batch size dependency should more accurately scale as
(TB)^{1/\alpha}
, as this is the information-theoretic upper bound on the set of all observed items.

We also emphasize that the approximation in Eq. (
7
) is only a heuristic. In actuality, after
t
steps of Muon, while items
i\geq\bar{d}_{t}:=d_{t}\polylog(d)
do have near-uniform logits, for items
d_{t}\leq i\leq\bar{d}_{t}
the predicted scores
\hat{p}_{t}(i\mid i)
can range anywhere between
\frac{1}{N}
and
1
. Moreover, these scores depend in a complicated manner on all embeddings
\{u_{j},v_{j}\}_{j\in[N]}
, so that the arguments used to prove Theorem
3
cannot be directly applied. Theoretically, this issue can be bypassed by assuming the existence of gaps in the power-law spectrum as in
30
, however we do not pursue this approach. Instead, we leave the precise end-to-end guarantee as a conjecture stated below, and empirically validate the predictions of Theorem
9
in Figure
4
(on the exact Muon iterates).

Conjecture
.

The recovery and convergence rates of Theorem
9
also hold for the exact Muon iterates
{\mathbf{W}}_{t+1}={\mathbf{W}}_{t}+\eta h_{\lambda_{t}}({\mathbf{G}}_{t})
.

6.2
In-context Recall with Transformers

We next consider a simple associative recall task that can be solved by a two-layer transformer via the
induction head
mechanism
[
39
]
. An induction head is a circuit composed of two attention heads that enables the model to copy a bigram from context, for example by predicting
{\boldsymbol{b}}
after observing
[\ldots,{\boldsymbol{a}},{\boldsymbol{b}},\ldots,{\boldsymbol{a}}]
. As shown by
4
, this mechanism can be implemented using a small number of associative memory matrices, making it a natural testbed for understanding how our perspective may extend to richer architectures such as multilayer transformers.

Data distribution.

To study how optimizers interact with heavy-tailed data, we consider a variant of the synthetic model from
4
in which selected tokens follow power-law distributions. Specifically, we introduce two disjoint vocabularies
\mathcal{Q}
and
\mathcal{V}
, each of size
N
, together with three power-law distributions: the
trigger
distribution
p^{(t)}
, supported on
\mathcal{Q}
; the
output
distribution
p^{(o)}
, supported on
\mathcal{V}
; and the
noise
distribution
p^{(n)}
, also supported on
\mathcal{V}
but potentially with a different frequency ordering from
p^{(o)}
.
Each sequence is generated by first sampling
K
triggers
q_{1},\ldots,q_{K}\in\mathcal{Q}
from
p^{(t)}
without replacement, and
K
outputs
o_{1},\ldots,o_{K}\in\mathcal{V}
from
p^{(o)}
with replacement. The resulting bigrams
(q_{k},o_{k})
are then inserted into
M
random positions in a sequence of length
T
. All remaining positions are filled with noise tokens
n_{j}\in\mathcal{V}
sampled from
p^{(n)}
. For instance, when
K=1
,
M=2
, and
T=8
, a sequence takes the form

\displaystyle[n_{1},q_{1},o_{1},n_{2},n_{3},q_{1},o_{1},n_{4}].

In this example, the second occurrence of
o_{1}
is perfectly predictable from the preceding context, and we train the two-layer transformer using cross-entropy loss only on such predictable output tokens. In all experiments, we set
T=24
and
K=M=2
. For simplicity, we take
p^{(n)}
to be uniform over
\mathcal{V}
, while
p^{(t)}
and
p^{(o)}
follow power laws with exponents
\alpha_{t}
and
\alpha_{o}
.

, while for AdamW we set

Conclusion

In this work, we study the storage capacities of Muon and SGD in a linear associative memory model with random embeddings and power-law item frequencies. We sharply characterize the one-step recovery rate of both optimizers and show that Muon recovers substantially more items than SGD. This gap also implies a much larger critical batch size for Muon, helping explain why its advantage is most visible in large-batch training. We further show that Muon’s gains are concentrated early in training, whereas over longer horizons its recovery dynamics become comparable to those of SGD. Overall, our analysis opens the door towards establishing scaling laws for spectral optimizers in realistic language modeling settings. Promising directions for future work include studying anisotropic embeddings, momentum
[
12
]
, and more complex language-modeling tasks such as compositional and multi-hop reasoning
[

,
57
]
.

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{s_i\qty(\bB_q^\top D\xi(\bT_q)[\ddot\bT_q]\bB_q)_{ij}}\lesssim\Big(\sup_{i,j}s_{i}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(1)}(s_i^2,s_j^2)}\Big)\absolutevalue{b_i b_j}.

Next, we have from the triangle inequality,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{s_i\qty(\bB_q^\top D^2\xi(\bT_q)[\dot\bT_q,\dot\bT_q]\bB_q)_{ij}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{k=1}^{d}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\cdot\absolutevalue{s_i(s_i a_i b_k + s_k a_k b_i)(s_j a_j b_k + s_k a_k b_j)}

\displaystyle\leq\Big(\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}^{2}s_{j}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\Big)\absolutevalue{a_i a_j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}b_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}^{2}s_{k}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\Big)\absolutevalue{a_i b_j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\absolutevalue{a_k b_k}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}s_{j}s_{k}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\Big)\absolutevalue{a_j b_i}\sum_{k=1}^{d}\absolutevalue{a_k b_k}

\displaystyle\qquad+\Big(\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}s_{k}^{2}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\Big)\absolutevalue{b_i b_j}\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\Big(\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}(s_{i}+s_{k})(s_{j}+s_{k})\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}\Big)\quantity(\abs{a_i a_j} + \abs{a_i b_j} + \abs{b_i a_j} + \abs{b_i b_j}).

Squaring and summing over
i,j
gives

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{S}}_{q}{\mathbf{B}}_{q}^{\top}\ddot{}{\mathbf{R}}_{q}{\mathbf{B}}_{q}\rVert_{\F}\lesssim\sup_{i,j}s_{i}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(1)}(s_i^2,s_j^2)}+\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}(s_{i}+s_{k})(s_{j}+s_{k})\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}.

Altogether, we have

\displaystyle\lVert\ddot{}{\mathbf{Y}}_{q}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\sup_{i,j}s_{i}\absolutevalue{\xi^{(1)}(s_i^2,s_j^2)}+\sup_{i,j,k}s_{i}(s_{i}+s_{k})(s_{j}+s_{k})\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}.

Finally, we evaluate this bound with our choice of
h_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}
, which corresponds to
\xi(z)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{z+\lambda^{2}}}
. Computing the first divided difference directly gives

\displaystyle\xi^{(1)}(x,y)=\frac{\xi(x)-\xi(y)}{x-y}=-\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}}(\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}})},

which is valid when
x=y
by continuity, and plugging in
s_{i}^{2},s_{j}^{2}
yields

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{s_i\xi^{(1)}(s_i^2,s_j^2)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}\times\frac{s_{i}}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}\leq\lambda^{-2}

for all
i,j
. Also, for the second divided difference, we obtain

\displaystyle\xi^{(2)}(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=\frac{\xi^{1}(x,z)-\xi^{(1)}(y,z)}{x-y}

\displaystyle=\frac{\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{z+\lambda^{2}}}{(\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}})(\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{z+\lambda^{2}})(\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{z+\lambda^{2}})}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{1}{\sqrt{x+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{y+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{z+\lambda^{2}}}.

It follows that for all
i,j,k
,

\displaystyle s_{i}(s_{i}+s_{k})(s_{j}+s_{k})\absolutevalue{\xi^{(2)}(s_i^2,s_j^2,s_k^2)}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{s_{i}}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\frac{s_{i}+s_{k}}{\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{k}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}\frac{s_{j}+s_{k}}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{k}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}

\displaystyle\qquad\times\frac{\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{k}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{k}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}(\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{k}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}+\frac{1}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}(\sqrt{s_{i}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}+\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{j}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}})}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\lambda^{-2}.

Therefore for this choice of
\xi
, we conclude that
\lVert\ddot{}{\mathbf{Y}}_{q}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
and thus

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\phi''(q)}=|u^{\top}\ddot{}{\mathbf{Y}}_{q}v|\lesssim\lambda^{-2}

for all
q\in[0,1]
.
∎

A.2
Minibatch concentration

Here, we collect concentration inequalities for the minibatch frequencies
q
which will be needed in later sections.

Lemma 14
.

Define the empirical covariance matrix

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}:=\sum_{j\in[N]}q^{2}_{j}u_{j}u_{j}^{\top}.

It holds with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
over sampling of
q
that

\displaystyle\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}})\lesssim\begin{cases}d^{-2\alpha}(\log d)^{2}&B\gtrsim d^{\alpha},\\
0&B\lesssim d^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Hereafter, we use
M
to denote any sufficiently large constant exponent that allows for union bounding over (say)
N^{2}=\poly(d)
items, and often omit the qualifying high probability statements.

Proof.

Let
N
be the number of examples satisfying
i\geq\frac{d}{4}
in a minibatch of size
B
.
N
is distributed as
\Bin(B,\rho)
where
\rho=\sum_{i\geq d/4}p_{i}\asymp d^{1-\alpha}
. From the multiplicative Chernoff bound, it holds that for all
\epsilon>0
,

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(N\ge(1+\eps)B\rho)\leq\exp\quantity(-\frac{\eps^2 B\rho}{2+\eps}).

If
B\lesssim d^{\alpha}
so that
B\rho\leq\frac{d}{8}
, by taking
\epsilon=\frac{d}{4B\rho}-1
we have

\displaystyle\Pr(N\ge\frac{d}{4})\leq\exp\quantity(-\frac{(d-4B\rho)^2}{4(d+4B\rho)})\leq e^{-\Omega(d)}.

Hence with high probability,
N<\frac{d}{4}
so that the total number of nonzero
q_{i}
is less than
\frac{d}{2}
. It follows that
\rank(\bM)<\frac{d}{2}
and so
\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}})=0
.

Now suppose
B\gtrsim d^{\alpha}
. Choose a positive integer
K\asymp\frac{1}{d}B^{1/\alpha}
and define the sets
I_{k}:=\{(k-1)d+\frac{d}{2},\cdots,kd+\frac{d}{2}-1\}
for
k\geq 1
. Consider the decomposition

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}=\underbrace{\sum_{i=1}^{d/2-1}q_{i}^{2}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}}_{=:{\mathbf{M}}_{0}}+\sum_{k\in[K]}\underbrace{\sum_{i\in I_{k}}q_{i}^{2}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}}_{=:{\mathbf{M}}_{k}}+\underbrace{\sum_{i=(K+1/2)d}^{N}q_{i}^{2}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}}_{=:{\mathbf{M}}_{\tail}}.

Since
\rank(\bM_0)<\frac{d}{2}
, we have
\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}}_{0})=0
. By Weyl’s inequality,

\displaystyle\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}})\leq\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}}_{0})+\norm{\sum_{k\in[K]} \bM_k + \bM_{\tail}}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{k\in[K]}\norm{\bM_k}_{\mathrm{op}}+\norm{\bM_{\tail}}_{\mathrm{op}}.

(13)

We first control the bulk sum. Since
|I_{k}|\leq d
, it follows from
51
that

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{i\in I_k} u_iu_i^\top}_{\mathrm{op}}=O(1)\quad\implies\quad\norm{\bM_k}_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\max_{i\in I_{k}}q_{i}^{2}

with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
. To bound this quantity, set
\bar{p}_{k}:=\max_{i\in I_{k}}p_{j}\asymp(k-\frac{1}{2})^{-\alpha}d^{-\alpha}
and note that
p_{i}\geq 3^{-\alpha}\bar{p}_{k}
for all
i\in I_{k}
. By the Chernoff bound for
Bq_{i}\sim\Bin(B,p_{i})
,

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(q_i \ge(1+\eps)p_i)\leq\exp\quantity(-\frac{Bp_i\eps^2}{2+\eps})

(14)

and so union bounding over
i\in I_{k}
, we have

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\max_{i\in I_k} q_i \ge(1+\eps) \bar{p}_k)

\displaystyle\leq d\exp\quantity(-\frac{3^{-\alpha}B\bar{p}_k\eps^2}{2+\eps})\lesssim\frac{1}{d^{M}}

by taking
\epsilon\gtrsim\frac{\log d}{B\bar{p}_{k}}\vee\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{B\bar{p}_{k}}}
. Hence for all
k\in[K]
we have

\displaystyle\max_{i\in I_{k}}q_{i}\lesssim(1+\epsilon)\bar{p}_{k}\lesssim\bar{p}_{k}+\frac{\log d}{B}.

For the tail sum, we exploit the sparsity of the frequencies
q_{i}
. Define the set of indices

\displaystyle I_{\tail}:=\left\{i:\quantity(K+\frac 12)d\leq i\leq N,\;q_{i}>0\right\}.

N_{\tail}:=|I_{\tail}|
is distributed as
\Bin(B,\rho_{\tail})
where
\rho_{\tail}=\sum_{i\geq(K+1/2)d}p_{i}\asymp B^{(1-\alpha)/\alpha}
, so that
N_{\tail}\asymp B\rho_{\tail}\asymp B^{1/\alpha}
with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
. Moreover for each
i\in I_{\tail}
, it holds that
p_{i}\lesssim(Kd)^{-\alpha}\asymp 1/B
and so

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(Bq_i\ge r)\leq\binom{B}{r}p_{i}^{r}\leq\quantity(\frac{eBp_i}{r})^{r}=d^{-\omega(1)}

by taking
r\asymp\log d
, hence
q_{i}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{B}
. It follows from
51
that

\displaystyle\norm{\bM_{\tail}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{i\in I_{\tail}}q_{i}^{2}\cdot\norm{\sum_{i\in I_{\tail}} u_iu_i^\top}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}\frac{N_{\tail}}{d}\quantity(1+ \sqrt{\frac{d}{N_{\tail}}} + \frac{d}{N_{\tail}})

\displaystyle\lesssim\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}\frac{B^{1/\alpha}}{d}

since
B\gtrsim d^{\alpha}
. We conclude from Eq. (
13
):

\displaystyle\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}})\lesssim\sum_{k\in[K]}\quantity(\bar{p}_k + \frac{\log d}{B})^{2}+\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}\frac{B^{1/\alpha}}{d}\lesssim d^{-2\alpha}(\log d)^{2},

where we have used that
\sum_{k\geq 1}\bar{p}_{k}^{2}\asymp\sum_{k\geq 1}(kd)^{-2\alpha}\asymp d^{-2\alpha}
.
∎

Lemma 15

(concentration of tail frequencies)
.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a randomly sampled minibatch with empirical frequencies
q
. Let
r
be any integer and denote
q_{>r}=(q_{r+1},\cdots,q_{N})
. Then it holds with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
that

\displaystyle\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-\alpha}+\frac{\log d}{B}

and

\displaystyle\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\begin{cases}r^{1/2-\alpha}\log d&B\gtrsim r^{\alpha},\\
\displaystyle\sqrt{\frac{r^{1-\alpha}}{B}}\log d&B\lesssim r^{\alpha}.\end{cases}

Proof.

We first control
\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty}
. Let the index
i>r
so that
p_{i}\lesssim r^{-\alpha}
. Recalling the Chernoff bound Eq. (
14
) for
Bq_{i}\sim\Bin(B,p_{i})
, we may choose

\displaystyle\epsilon\gtrsim 1+\frac{\log d}{Bp_{i}}

such that

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(q_i \ge(1+\eps)p_i)\leq\exp\quantity(-\frac{Bp_i\eps^2}{2+\eps})\leq\exp\quantity(-\frac{Bp_i\eps}{3})\leq\frac{1}{d^{M}},

which implies

\displaystyle q_{i}\lesssim\quantity(1+\frac{\log d}{Bp_i})p_{i}\lesssim r^{-\alpha}+\frac{\log d}{B}

for all
r<i\leq N
.

For
\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}
, we repeat the analysis from the proof of Lemma
14
. If
B\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
, thresholding at
B^{1/\alpha}
gives
q_{i}\lesssim p_{i}+\frac{\log d}{B}
for items with
r<i\leq B^{1/\alpha}
, and
q_{i}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{B}
for the
N_{\tail}\asymp B^{1/\alpha}
items with
i>B^{1/\alpha}
. Combining, we obtain

\displaystyle\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{i=r+1}^{B^{1/\alpha}}\quantity(p_i + \frac{\log d}{B})^{2}+N_{\tail}\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{1-2\alpha}+B^{1/\alpha-2}(\log d)^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{1-2\alpha}(\log d)^{2}.

Finally, if
B\lesssim r^{\alpha}
, we may treat all items
i>r
as in the tail, so that
N_{\tail}\sim\Bin(B,\rho_{\tail})
with
\rho_{\tail}=\sum_{i>r}p_{i}\asymp r^{1-\alpha}
and
N_{\tail}\asymp B\rho_{\tail}\asymp Br^{1-\alpha}
. Hence

\displaystyle\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim N_{\tail}\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}\lesssim\frac{r^{1-\alpha}}{B}(\log d)^{2},

as was to be shown.
∎

As a corollary, we prove the following lemma which will be used in Section
B
.

Lemma 16
.

Let
r\asymp\frac{d}{(\log d)^{2}}
. There exists

\displaystyle\lambda\asymp\max\left\{\frac{(\log d)^{2\alpha+2}}{d^{\alpha}},\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{B}\right\}

(15)

such that the event

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{q}

\displaystyle\,:\,\max\left\{\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty},\frac{\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}\right\}\leq\lambda\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}}\,

(16)

satisfies
\Pr(\cE_q)\geq 1-O(d^{-M})
.

Proof.

By Lemma
15
, we have that when
B\gtrsim r^{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty},\frac{\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-\alpha}+\frac{\log d}{B}+\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}}\cdot r^{-\alpha}\log d\lesssim d^{-\alpha}(\log d)^{2\alpha+1}

and when
B\lesssim r^{\alpha}
,

\displaystyle\max\left\{\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty},\frac{\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}\right\}

\displaystyle\lesssim r^{-\alpha}+\frac{\log d}{B}+\sqrt{\frac{r^{1-\alpha}}{Bd}}\log d\lesssim\frac{\log d}{B}.

Therefore by choosing
\lambda
as in Eq. (
15
) with an appropriate proportionality constant, we can ensure that
\mathcal{E}_{q}
occurs with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
.
∎

A.3
Lower bounding the signal

We now consider the stabilized sign map
h_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}
. Recall from Lemma
12
that

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\neq\ell}\quantity( \frac{h_\lam(s_k) + h_\lam(s_\ell)}{s_k + s_\ell} (a_kb_\ell- a_\ell b_k)^2 + \frac{h_\lam(s_k) - h_\lam(s_\ell)}{s_k - s_\ell} (a_kb_\ell+ a_\ell b_k)^2)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k}h_{\lambda}^{\prime}(s_{k})a_{k}^{2}b_{k}^{2}.

Since
\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{k})-h_{\lambda}(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}-s_{\ell}}
is always positive by the mean value theorem and
h_{\lambda}(z)/z
is decreasing, we may lower bound
\phi^{\prime}(0)
as

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{4}\sum_{k\neq\ell}\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{k})+h_{\lambda}(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}+s_{\ell}}(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{d/2\leq k<\ell}\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{k})+h_{\lambda}(s_{\ell})}{s_{k}+s_{\ell}}(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{d/2})}{2s_{d/2}}\sum_{d/2\leq k<\ell}(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}.

(17)

Note that
a,b
are Gaussian conditioned on
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}
. Let
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}
be the vectors consisting of the last
\frac{d}{2}
coordinates of
a,b
, respectively. It holds that
a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d/2})
and

\displaystyle\sum_{d/2\leq k<\ell}(a_{k}b_{\ell}-a_{\ell}b_{k})^{2}=\norm{a'}^{2}\norm{b'}^{2}-\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle^{2}.

(18)

Standard concentration bounds give
\norm{a'}^{2},\norm{b'}^{2}=\Theta(1)
while
\langle a^{\prime},b^{\prime}\rangle^{2}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{d}
with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
, hence Eq. (
18
) is lower bounded by a constant.

It thus suffices to control the ‘bulk’ singular value
s_{d/2}
. By the lemma below, this can be reduced to controlling the bulk eigenvalues of the weighted covariance matrix

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}:=\sum_{j\in[N]}q^{2}_{j}u_{j}u_{j}^{\top}.

(19)

Lemma 17
.

It holds that
s_{k}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i})\lesssim\lambda_{k}({\mathbf{M}})^{1/2}
for all
i,k
with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
.

Proof.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}_{-i}:=\sum_{j\neq i}q^{2}_{j}u_{j}u_{j}^{\top}
. The
k
th column of
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}
is
\sum_{j\neq i}q_{j}u_{j}v_{jk}
, which for each
k
is an i.i.d. sample from
\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{M}}_{-i})
conditioned on
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}
. Therefore

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}{\mathbf{M}}_{-i}^{1/2}{\mathbf{Z}},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathbf{Z}}_{k\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\;\;\text{i.i.d.}

It holds that
[
45
, Eq. 2.3]

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\norm{\bZ}_{\op} \le 3\sqrt{d})\geq 1-2e^{-d/2}

and thus

\displaystyle s_{k}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i})\leq\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}s_{k}\quantity(\bM_{-i}^{1/2})\norm{\bZ}_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\lambda_{k}({\mathbf{M}}_{-i})^{1/2}\leq\lambda_{k}({\mathbf{M}})^{1/2}

since
\lambda_{k}(\cdot)
respects Loewner order.
∎

Then by Lemma
14
, we have
\lambda_{d/2}({\mathbf{M}})\lesssim d^{-2\alpha}(\log d)^{2}
and so
s_{d/2}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i})\lesssim d^{-\alpha}\log d\lesssim\lambda
. We conclude from Eq. (
17
),

\displaystyle\phi^{\prime}(0)\geq\frac{h_{\lambda}(s_{d/2})}{2s_{d/2}}\sum_{k<l\leq d/2}(a_{k}b_{l}+a_{l}b_{k})^{2}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\sqrt{\smash[b]{s_{d/2}^{2}}+\lambda^{2}}}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\lambda}.

We have also shown that
\sup_{q\in[0,1]}\absolutevalue{\phi''(q)}\lesssim\lambda^{-2}
in Lemma
13
. In addition, since
u_{i},v_{i}
are independent of
{\mathbf{G}}_{-i}
and
\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-i})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
from Proposition
26
, a standard concentration bound for subexponential sums
[
51
, Lemma 2.8.6 and Corollary 2.9.2]
gives that with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\phi(0)}=\absolutevalue{u_i^\top h_\lam(\bG_{-i}) v_i}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.

Since
\phi
is increasing by Lemma
12
, we can therefore Taylor expand
\phi
to obtain

\displaystyle\phi(q)\geq\phi(t)\geq\phi(0)+t\phi^{\prime}(0)-\frac{1}{2}t^{2}\sup_{0\leq s\leq t}|\phi^{\prime\prime}(s)|\gtrsim\phi(0)+\frac{t}{\lambda}-\frac{t^{2}}{\lambda^{2}}.

Finally, taking the supremum over
t\in[0,q]
gives

\displaystyle\gamma_{ii}=\phi(q_{i})\gtrsim\min\left\{\frac{q_{i}}{\lambda},1\right\}-O\quantity(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}).

(20)

A.4
Putting things together

In Section
B
, we analyze the interaction terms and show that under
\mathcal{E}_{q}
(Proposition
18
),

\displaystyle|\gamma_{ij}|\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}},\quad\forall i\neq j.

Combining with Eq. (
20
), the uncentered logit gap is thus lower bounded as

\displaystyle\gamma_{ii}-\max_{j\neq i}\gamma_{ij}\gtrsim\min\left\{\frac{q_{i}}{\lambda},1\right\}-O\quantity(\frac{(\log d)^3}{\sqrt{d}}).

We now show that centering does not affect the computation. The mean vector is distributed as
\bar{u}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{Nd}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
so that
\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{2}\lesssim 1/\sqrt{N}
, and moreover
\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{2},\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}\lesssim 1
for all
i\in[N]
with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
. It follows that

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{G}}_{0}-{\mathbf{G}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{i\in[N]}q_{i}\lVert\bar{u}\rVert_{2}\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}.

(21)

By Proposition
26
, for all
i,j
,

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})v_{i}-u_{j}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}})v_{i}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})-h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert{\mathbf{G}}_{0}-{\mathbf{G}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\frac{d^{\alpha}}{\sqrt{N}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}.

Thus we also have

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})v_{i}-\max_{j\neq i}u_{j}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})v_{i}\gtrsim\min\left\{\frac{q_{i}}{\lambda},1\right\}-O\quantity(\frac{(\log d)^3}{\sqrt{d}}).

We conclude that item
i
will be recovered (regardless of the scaling
\eta
) if

\displaystyle q_{i}\gtrsim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}\lambda\asymp\max\left\{\frac{(\log d)^{2\alpha+5}}{d^{\alpha+1/2}},\frac{(\log d)^{5}}{B\sqrt{d}}\right\}.

In the population regime (
B=\infty
), taking
q_{i}=p_{i}\asymp i^{-\alpha}
, we hence recover all items up to

\displaystyle i\leq i^{\star}\asymp d^{1+\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(\log d)^{-2-\frac{5}{\alpha}}.

If
q_{i}
are obtained from a minibatch of size
B
, we have from the Chernoff lower bound that
\Pr(q_i \le\frac 12 p_i)\leq\exp(-\frac 12 Bp_i)\leq d^{-M}
for
i
such that
p_{i}\gtrsim B^{-1}\log d
, which also ensures

\displaystyle q_{i}\geq\frac{p_{i}}{2}\gtrsim\max\left\{\frac{(\log d)^{2\alpha+5}}{d^{\alpha+1/2}},\frac{(\log d)^{5}}{B\sqrt{d}}\right\}

for all
i\lesssim i^{\star}
. Therefore with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
, we recover all items up to

\displaystyle i\lesssim\min\left\{i^{\star},\quantity(\frac{B}{\log d})^{1/\alpha}\right\}.

(22)

A.5
Proof of Corollary
4

Let

Appendix B
Analysis of Interaction Terms

B.1
Overview

In this section, we show the following result for the interaction terms.

Proposition 18
.

Fix a threshold
r\asymp\frac{d}{(\log d)^{2}}
. Under the event

\displaystyle\mathcal{E}_{q}

\displaystyle\,:\,\max\left\{\norm{q_{>r}}_{\infty},\frac{\norm{q_{>r}}_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}\right\}\leq\lambda\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}}\,,

it holds with probability
1-d^{-\omega(1)}
that for all pairs
i\neq j
of distinct indices,

\displaystyle|\gamma_{ij}|

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}.

We have verified
\mathcal{E}_{q}
occurs with high probability by a judicious choice of
\lambda
in Lemma
16
, and assume this for fixed
q
throughout the section. When either
q_{i}
or
q_{j}\ll\lambda
, the interaction terms can be bounded by a simple operator Lipschitz concentration argument, which we provide in Section
B.8
. The main challenge arises when controlling the leading
r\times r
block, where any operator norm bound fails to capture the correct scale. The analysis for these ‘large’ interactions requires a much more involved perturbative approach, and will be developed throughout Sections
B.2
-
B.7
. For the readers’ convenience, we provide a sketch of the argument here.

Gather the top
r\asymp\frac{d}{(\log d)^{2}}
items into
{\mathbf{U}}=[u_{1}\;\cdots\;u_{r}]
,
{\mathbf{V}}=[v_{1}\;\cdots\;v_{r}]
and
{\mathbf{Q}}=\diag(q_{1},\cdots,q_{r})
. We need to bound the off-diagonal entries of
{\mathbf{K}}:={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}){\mathbf{V}}
, where the gradient
{\mathbf{G}}
is split into

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}+{\mathbf{Z}},\quad{\mathbf{Z}}:=\sum_{\ell=r+1}^{N}q_{\ell}u_{\ell}v_{\ell}^{\top}.

In the limiting regime
r/d\to 0
, we can ensure that the Gram matrices
{\mathbf{G}}_{u}={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}{\mathbf{U}}
,
{\mathbf{G}}_{v}={\mathbf{V}}^{\top}{\mathbf{V}}
are approximately identity (Section
B.2
). Utilizing equivariance of
h_{\lambda}
and isotropicity of the tail
{\mathbf{Z}}
given
{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}
, we rewrite
{\mathbf{K}}
in this near-orthonormal basis as

\displaystyle{\mathbf{K}}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}&0\end{bmatrix}h_{\lambda}\quantity(\bmat{\bG_u^{1/2} \bQ\bG_v^{1/2} &\\&0}+\bZ)\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{1/2}\\
0\end{bmatrix},

which is a perturbation of the top
r\times r
block of
h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{Q}})
. We then invoke the resolvent representation
{\mathbf{X}}^{-1/2}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}({\mathbf{X}}+s{\mathbf{I}}_{d})^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s
to get rid of the inverse square root in
h_{\lambda}
, and expand all fractional powers and inverses in terms of the error matrices
{\mathbf{E}}_{u}={\mathbf{G}}_{u}-{\mathbf{I}}_{r}
,
{\mathbf{E}}_{v}={\mathbf{G}}_{v}-{\mathbf{I}}_{r}
and
\tilde{{\mathbf{Z}}}=\lambda^{-1}{\mathbf{Z}}
(Section
B.3
). This yields the expression (omitting series truncations, which are controlled in Section
B.4
)

\displaystyle{\mathbf{K}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}&0\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\sum_{k\geq 0}\quantity(\bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2})^{k}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

(23)

where
{\mathbf{D}}_{s}=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Q}}^{2}&\\
&0\end{bmatrix}+(\lambda^{2}+s){\mathbf{I}}_{d}
is diagonal dependent on
s
and
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
are perturbations, e.g., the expansion for
{\mathbf{H}}
is

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Q}}+{\mathbf{E}}_{u}{\mathbf{Q}}&\\
&0\end{bmatrix}+\lambda\sum_{k,\ell\geq 0}\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{E}}_{u}&\\
&0\end{bmatrix}^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{E}}_{v}&\\
&0\end{bmatrix}^{\ell}.

We further expand Eq. (
23
) entrywise over all summed factors in
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
(recorded as symbols
\mu,\nu
) and also over all valid index paths
\iota
, into products
{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
of entries of
{\mathbf{E}}_{u},{\mathbf{E}}_{v},\tilde{{\mathbf{Z}}}
. Integrating out
s
in the coefficients gives the complete expansion
{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}=\sum\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
. Along the way, we prove two crucial results: (1) all integrated coefficients
|\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}|\leq 1
(Section
B.5
); and (2) every pair of monomials
{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu},{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}
are nonnegatively correlated (Section
B.6
). This lets us strip away all coefficients to construct an
isotropic
perturbation
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}:=\sum{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
which upper bounds
{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}
:

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}^{2}]=\sum\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}\theta_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\E[{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}]\leq\sum\E[{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}]=\E[\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}^{2}].

This new object
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}
is essentially equivalent to removing all scalar coefficients of
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
and factors of
{\mathbf{D}}_{s}
in the computation of Eq. (
23
). Importantly, unlike
{\mathbf{K}}
, the off-diagonal entries of
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}
are now distributionally invariant. Furthermore, its higher moments can be controlled (after sorting by degree) using standard moment methods, i.e., Gaussian hypercontractivity and decay estimates for
{\mathbf{E}}_{u},{\mathbf{E}}_{v},\tilde{{\mathbf{Z}}}
(Section
B.7
). This finally yields the desired upper bound

\displaystyle|\gamma_{ij}|=|{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}|\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}.

), we obtain the expression

\displaystyle{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}&0\end{bmatrix}({\mathbf{H}}+{\mathbf{R}}_{h})({\mathbf{D}}_{s}+\boldsymbol{\Delta}+{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s})^{-1}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Compare to the quantity obtained by ignoring the truncation errors
{\mathbf{R}}_{h},{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s}
and expanding the inverse using the (again truncated) Neumann series:

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}&0\end{bmatrix}\underbrace{{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\sum_{k=0}^{K}\quantity(-\bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2})^{k}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}}_{=:\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{s}({\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta})}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

(31)

We justify this expansion in Lemma
22
and show
\lVert{\mathbf{K}}-\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=d^{-\omega(1)}
in Lemma
23
. Hence it suffices to bound the off-diagonal entries of
\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}
.

B.4
Truncation error bounds

Here, we show that the errors from truncating the series for
{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2},{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2},{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1}
and the Neumann series in Eq. (
31
) are ignorable.

Lemma 22

(Neumann series stability)
.

Under the event
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{op}}
, there exists a constant
C
such that for every
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq C\rho.

Proof.

Define the diagonal matrices

\displaystyle{\mathbf{A}}_{s}:=({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+\beta_{s}^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{r})^{-1/2}{\mathbf{Q}},\quad{\mathbf{B}}_{s}:=\beta_{s}({\mathbf{Q}}^{2}+\beta_{s}^{2}{\mathbf{I}}_{r})^{-1/2}

so that
\norm{\bA_s}_{\mathrm{op}},\norm{\bB_s}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
. We bound each of the four terms in (
30
) separately. For the first term,

\displaystyle\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\db{\bQ\bE_u\bQ}\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}=\norm{\bA_s\bE_u\bA_s}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\rho.

For the second term, noting that the sum starts from
k=1
,

\displaystyle\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2} \qty(\beta_s^2 \sum_{k=1}^K \db{-\bE_v}^k) \bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\norm{\bB_s\bE_v\bB_s}_{\mathrm{op}}^{k}\leq\sum_{k=1}^{K}\rho^{k}\leq\frac{\rho}{1-\rho}.

For the third term, we have

\displaystyle\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\db{\bQ}\db{\bE_u}^k}_{\mathrm{op}}=\norm{\bA_s\bE_u^k}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\rho^{k},

\displaystyle\norm{\db{\bE_v}^\ell\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}=\beta_{s}^{-1}\norm{\db{\bE_v}^\ell\bB_s}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\beta_{s}^{-1}\rho^{\ell}.

Then noting that
\lambda\leq\beta_{s}
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\lambda\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\quantity(\lam\sum_{k,\ell=0}^K \binom{\frac 12}{k}\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell} \db{\bQ} \db{\bE_u}^k \tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell)\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\qty(\lam\sum_{k,\ell=0}^K \binom{\frac 12}{k}\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell} \db{\bQ} \db{\bE_u}^k \tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell)\bD_s^{-1/2}}\llbracket{\mathbf{Q}}\rrbracket\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\db{\bQ}\db{\bE_u}^k \tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda\beta_{s}^{-1}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\rho^{k+\ell+1}\leq\frac{\rho}{(1-\rho)^{2}}

and similarly for the transposed term. Finally for the fourth term,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\lambda^{2}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\quantity(\lam^2\sum_{k,\ell=0}^K \binom{-\frac 12}{k}\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell} \db{\bE_v}^k \tilde\bZ^\top\tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell)\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\qty(\lam^2\sum_{k,\ell=0}^K \binom{-\frac 12}{k}\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell} \db{\bE_v}^k \tilde\bZ^\top\tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell)\bD_s^{-1/2}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2} \db{\bE_v}^k \tilde\bZ^\top\tilde\bZ\db{\bE_v}^\ell\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\lambda^{2}\beta_{s}^{-2}\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\rho^{k+\ell+2}\leq\frac{\rho^{2}}{(1-\rho)^{2}}.

Combining the errors concludes the proof.
∎

Lemma 23

(truncation error bound)
.

Suppose the decay factor satisfies
\rho\lesssim\frac{1}{\log d}
and the truncation threshold
K\gtrsim\log d
. Under the event
\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{op}}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{K}}-\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=d^{-\omega(1)}.

Proof.

First we control the errors
{\mathbf{R}}_{h},{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s}
. For
{\mathbf{R}}_{h}
, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{h}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}-\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}\rrbracket+\llbracket{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}-\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{u}^{1/2}\rrbracket{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}.

Then by Lemma
21
,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{R}}_{h}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\bG_u^{1/2}\bZ}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{\bG_v^{-1/2} - \bar\bG_v^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}+\norm{\bG_u^{1/2} - \bar\bG_u^{1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{\bZ\bar\bG_v^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(2\sqrt{1+\rho} +\rho^{K+1})\lambda\rho\cdot\rho^{K+1}=d^{-\omega(1)}.

For
{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s}
, we have that

\displaystyle{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s}

\displaystyle=\beta_{s}^{2}\llbracket{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1}-\check{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\qquad+\llbracket{\mathbf{Q}}\rrbracket\quantity(\bmat{\bG_u^{1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} \bZ\bmat{\bG_v^{-1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} - \bmat{\bar\bG_u^{1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} \bZ\bmat{\bar\bG_v^{-1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}})

\displaystyle\qquad+\quantity(\bmat{\bG_v^{-1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} \bZ^\top\bmat{\bG_u^{1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} - \bmat{\bar\bG_v^{-1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}} \bZ^\top\bmat{\bar\bG_u^{1/2}&\\&\bI_{d-r}})\llbracket{\mathbf{Q}}\rrbracket

\displaystyle\qquad+\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}-\begin{bmatrix}\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}\begin{bmatrix}\bar{}{\mathbf{G}}_{v}^{-1/2}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}

and we can similarly bound, using that
\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
and
\lambda=\poly(d^{-1})
,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{R}}_{\delta,s}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\beta_{s}^{2}\cdot d^{-\omega(1)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\bR_{\delta,s}\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}=d^{-\omega(1)}

(32)

uniformly over
s
. Now define

\displaystyle{\mathbf{N}}_{1}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\bI_d + \bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2} + \bD_s^{-1/2}\bR_{\delta,s}\bD_s^{-1/2})^{-1},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{N}}_{2}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\bI_d + \bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2})^{-1},

\displaystyle{\mathbf{N}}_{3}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\quantity(-\bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2})^{k}.

We have that
\lVert{\mathbf{N}}_{1}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert{\mathbf{N}}_{2}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=1+o(1)
due to Lemma
22
and Eq. (
32
). Moreover,

\displaystyle\norm{\bN_1 - \bN_2}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\norm{\bN_2(\bN_2^{-1}-\bN_1^{-1})\bN_1}_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\norm{\bD_s^{-1/2}\bR_{\delta,s}\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}=d^{-\omega(1)}

and by the Neumann series,

\displaystyle\norm{\bN_2-\bN_3}_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\sum_{k=K+1}^{\infty}(C\rho)^{k}=d^{-\omega(1)}.

Thus from

\displaystyle{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}&0\end{bmatrix}({\mathbf{H}}+{\mathbf{R}}_{h}){\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}{\mathbf{N}}_{1}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}&0\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}{\mathbf{N}}_{3}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\\
0\end{bmatrix}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,

and
\norm{\bH}_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim 1
, it follows that

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{K}}-\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\norm{(\bH+\bR_h) \bD_s^{-1/2}\bN_1\bD_s^{-1/2} - \bH\bD_s^{-1/2} \bN_3 \bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\norm{\bR_h \bD_s^{-1/2}\bN_1\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\qquad+\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\norm{\bH\bD_s^{-1/2}(\bN_1-\bN_3)\bD_s^{-1/2}}_{\mathrm{op}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\beta_{s}^{-2}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\cdot\quantity(\norm{\bR_h}_\op\norm{\bN_1}_\op+ \norm{\bH}_\op\nnorm{\bN_1-\bN_3}_\op)

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}d^{-\omega(1)}=d^{-\omega(1)},

as was to be shown.
∎

B.5
Complete perturbative expansion

We will now fully multiply out
\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{s}({\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta})
in Eq. (
31
) by plugging in Eq. (
29
) and Eq. (
30
) into each instance of
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
and further expanding all matrix products entrywise. Since there are many different types of terms, we will keep track of all terms and coefficients by introducing
symbols

\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
and
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
for
{\mathbf{H}}
and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
, respectively.

For the rest of the section, we set

\displaystyle\tilde{q}=(\tilde{q}_{1},\cdots,\tilde{q}_{d}):=(q_{1},\cdots,q_{r},0,\cdots,0)

so that
\llbracket{\mathbf{Q}}\rrbracket=\diag(\tilde{q})
. We will also denote by
\mathcal{I}^{m}
the set of length
m
index sequences or
paths

\iota=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{m})\in[d]^{m}
, and by
\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{m}
the set of augmented paths
\iota=(i_{0},\cdots,i_{m+1})\in[d]^{m+2}
with the restriction that
i_{0}=i
and
i_{m+1}=j
.

For
{\mathbf{H}}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\mu}:=\{1,2\}\cup\left\{(3,k,\ell,\iota):0\leq k,\ell\leq K,\;\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k+\ell}\right\}.

From Eq. (
29
), we can decompose

\displaystyle{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}a_{ij}^{\mu}{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{\mu}

(33)

where

(1)

\mu=1
:
(a_{ij}^{1},{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{1})=(\tilde{q}_{j},\llbracket{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\rrbracket_{ij})

(2)

\mu=2
:
(a_{ij}^{2},{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{2})=(\tilde{q}_{j},\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{ij})

(3)

\mu=(3,k,\ell,\iota)
: recalling
\iota=(i_{1},\cdots,i_{k+\ell})\in[d]^{k+\ell}
,

\displaystyle(a_{ij}^{\mu},{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{\mu})

\displaystyle=\quantity(\binom{\frac 12}{k}\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell} \lam, \prod_{m=1}^k\db{\bE_u}_{i_{m-1}i_{m}} \tilde\bZ_{i_ki_{k+1}}\prod_{m=1}^\ell\db{\bE_v}_{i_{k+m}i_{k+m+1}})\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda,\prod_{m=1}^{k}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i_{k}i_{k+1}}\prod_{m=1}^{\ell}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{i_{k+m}i_{k+m+1}},

here with the convention that
i_{0}=i
,
i_{m+1}=j
depending on the pair
(i,j)
being expanded.

For
\boldsymbol{\Delta}
, let

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\nu}:=\{1\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(2,k,\iota):1\leq k\leq K,\;\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k-1}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(3,k,\ell,\iota):0\leq k,\ell\leq K,\;\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k+\ell}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(4,k,\ell,\iota):0\leq k,\ell\leq K,\;\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k+\ell}\right\}

\displaystyle\cup\left\{(5,k,\ell,\iota):0\leq k,\ell\leq K,\;\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k+\ell+1}\right\}.

From Eq. (
30
), we can decompose
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}
with coefficients in the following bilinear form:

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}}b_{i}^{\nu}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu}c_{j}^{\nu}

(34)

where

(1)

\nu=1
:

\displaystyle(b_{i}^{1},c_{j}^{1},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{1})=(\tilde{q}_{i},\tilde{q}_{j},\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{ij})

(2)

\nu=(2,k,\iota)
:

\displaystyle(b_{i}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu})=\quantity((-1)^k\beta_s,\beta_s,\prod_{m=1}^k \db{\bE_u}_{i_{m-1}i_m})

(3)

\nu=(3,k,\ell,\iota)
:

\displaystyle(b_{i}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu})=\quantity(\binom{\frac 12}{k}\tq_i,\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell}\lam, \prod_{m=1}^k\db{\bE_u}_{i_{m-1}i_{m}} \tilde\bZ_{i_ki_{k+1}}\prod_{m=1}^\ell\db{\bE_v}_{i_{k+m}i_{k+m+1}})\tilde{q}_{i},\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda,\prod_{m=1}^{k}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i_{k}i_{k+1}}\prod_{m=1}^{\ell}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{i_{k+m}i_{k+m+1}}

(4)

\nu=(4,k,\ell,\iota)
:

\displaystyle(b_{i}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu})=\quantity(\binom{-\frac 12}{\ell}\lam, \binom{\frac 12}{k}\tq_j, \prod_{m=1}^\ell\db{\bE_v}_{i_{m-1}i_{m}} \tilde\bZ_{i_{\ell+1}i_\ell}\prod_{m=1}^k \db{\bE_u}_{i_{\ell+m}i_{\ell+m+1}})\lambda,\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}\tilde{q}_{j},\prod_{m=1}^{\ell}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i_{\ell+1}i_{\ell}}\prod_{m=1}^{k}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{i_{\ell+m}i_{\ell+m+1}}

(5)

\nu=(5,k,\ell,\iota)
:

\displaystyle(b_{i}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu},\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu})=\Bigg(\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{k}\lambda,\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda,\prod_{m=1}^{k}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{i_{m-1}i_{m}}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i_{k+1}i_{k}}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{i_{k+1}i_{k+2}}\prod_{m=2}^{\ell+1}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{i_{k+m}i_{k+m+1}}\Bigg).

Observe that every
{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{\mu}
and
\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu}
are purely products of entries of
\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket
or
\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}
, without any numerical coefficients.

Returning to Eq. (
31
), fix a pair of indices
i,j\in[r]
, so that

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{s}({\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta})_{ij}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

(35)

By expanding each power
\big(-{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\boldsymbol{\Delta}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\big)^{k}
along paths
\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}
and plugging in Eq. (
33
) and Eq. (
34
), we obtain

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Psi}_{s}({\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta})_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\left[{\mathbf{H}}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\quantity(-\bD_s^{-1/2}\bDelta\bD_s^{-1/2})^{k}{\mathbf{D}}_{s}^{-1/2}\right]_{ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\frac{{\mathbf{H}}_{i_{0}i_{1}}}{\sqrt{d_{i_{1},s}}}\quantity(\prod_{\ell=1}^k \frac{\bDelta_{i_\ell i_{\ell+1}}}{\sqrt{d_{i_\ell,s}d_{i_{\ell+1},s}}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i_{k+1},s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}\frac{a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}{\mathbf{H}}_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{d_{i_{1},s}}}\quantity(\prod_{\ell=1}^k \sum_{\nu_\ell\in\cS_\nu} \frac{b_{i_\ell}^{\nu_\ell} \bDelta_{i_\ell i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_\ell} c_{i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_\ell}}{\sqrt{d_{i_\ell,s}d_{i_{\ell+1},s}}} )\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i_{k+1},s}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k=0}^{K}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}}\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu},

(36)

where we have defined for each
\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
and
\nu=(\nu_{1},\cdots,\nu_{k})\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}
(
k
being implicit),

\displaystyle\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}:=\frac{a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{d_{i_{1},s}}}\quantity(\prod_{\ell=1}^k \frac{b_{i_\ell}^{\nu_\ell} c_{i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_\ell}}{\sqrt{d_{i_\ell,s}d_{i_{\ell+1},s}}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i_{k+1},s}}}

and

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}:={\mathbf{H}}_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{i_{\ell}i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_{\ell}}.

Note that
{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
is also a product of a number of entries of
\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket
or
\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}
. We denote this number by the
degree

n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
; the degree of
{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
as a polynomial of Gaussians
u_{ij},v_{ij}
is
2n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
(however, this polynomial is nonhomogeneous due to the presence of the
-{\mathbf{I}}_{r}
terms in
{\mathbf{E}}_{u},{\mathbf{E}}_{v}
). From the definition of
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
, we can check that

\displaystyle 0\leq n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}\leq(2K+1)+K(2K+2)\leq CK^{2}.

(37)

The coefficients
\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}
further satisfy the following uniform bound.

Lemma 24
.

For all
\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k}
and symbols
\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
,
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}
, there exists an index
m=m(\iota,\mu,\nu)\in\{1,\cdots,k+1\}
such that for all
s\geq 0
,

\displaystyle|\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}|\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{m},s}}.

Proof.

Denote the projection of the symbols
\mu
and
\nu
to the integer-valued first coordinate as
\pi(\mu)\in\{1,2,3\}
and
\pi(\nu)\in\{1,2,3,4,5\}
, respectively.

First suppose
k=0
. When
\pi(\mu)\in\{1,2\}
, we have
a_{ij}^{\mu}=\tilde{q}_{j}
. When
\pi(\mu)=3
, we have
|a_{ij}^{\mu}|\leq\lambda
. Hence

\displaystyle|\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}|=\frac{|a_{ij}^{\mu}|}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{j}\vee\lambda}{\tilde{q}_{j}^{2}+\beta_{s}^{2}}.

Now let
k\geq 1
. We first claim that for all
i,j
and all symbols
\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
,
\nu,\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}
, it holds that
|a_{ij}^{\mu}b_{j}^{\nu}|\leq d_{j,s}
and
|b_{j}^{\nu}c_{j}^{\nu^{\prime}}|\leq d_{j,s}
. Indeed, for each
i,j
,

\displaystyle a_{ij}^{\mu}

\displaystyle\in\{\tilde{q}_{j}\}\cup\left\{\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda:k,\ell\leq K\right\},

\displaystyle b_{j}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\in\left\{\tilde{q}_{j},\beta_{s},-\beta_{s}\right\}\cup\left\{\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}\tilde{q}_{j}:k\leq K\right\}\cup\left\{\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda:\ell\leq K\right\}.

By Eq. (
26
) and
\lambda\leq\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+s}=\beta_{s}
, we have

\displaystyle|a_{ij}^{\mu}b_{j}^{\nu}|,\,|b_{j}^{\nu}c_{j}^{\nu^{\prime}}|\leq(\tilde{q}_{j}\vee\beta_{s})^{2}\leq\tilde{q}_{j}^{2}+\beta_{s}^{2}=d_{j,s}

(38)

as claimed. Now rewrite

\displaystyle\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}}{\sqrt{d_{i_{1},s}}}\quantity(\prod_{\ell=1}^k \frac{b_{i_\ell}^{\nu_\ell} c_{i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_\ell}}{\sqrt{d_{i_\ell,s}d_{i_{\ell+1},s}}})\frac{1}{\sqrt{d_{i_{k+1},s}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}b_{i_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}}{d_{i_{1},s}}\quantity(\prod_{\ell=2}^{k} \frac{c_{i_\ell}^{\nu_{\ell-1}} b_{i_\ell}^{\nu_\ell}}{d_{i_\ell,s}})\frac{c_{i_{k+1}}^{\nu_{k}}}{d_{i_{k+1},s}}

to consolidate the denominators. We divide into the following cases.

(1)

\pi(\nu_{k})\neq 2
: we have

\displaystyle c_{i_{k+1}}^{\nu_{k}}=c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}\in\left\{\binom{\frac{1}{2}}{k}\tilde{q}_{j}:k\leq K\right\}\cup\left\{\binom{-\frac{1}{2}}{\ell}\lambda:\ell\leq K\right\}

so that
|c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}|\leq\tilde{q}_{j}\vee\lambda
. Thus by Eq. (
38
),

\displaystyle|\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}|=\frac{|a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}b_{i_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}|}{d_{i_{1},s}}\prod_{\ell=2}^{k}\frac{|c_{i_{\ell}}^{\nu_{\ell-1}}b_{i_{\ell}}^{\nu_{\ell}}|}{d_{i_{\ell},s}}\cdot\frac{|c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}|}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{|c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}|}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{j}\vee\lambda}{d_{j,s}}.

(2)

\pi(\nu_{k})=2
and
\pi(\nu_{1})\notin\{1,3\}
: we have
c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}=\beta_{s}
and
|b_{i_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}|\leq\beta_{s}
, as well as
|a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}|\leq\tilde{q}_{i_{1}}\vee\lambda
regardless of
\mu
. Then

\displaystyle|\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}|\leq\frac{|a_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}|}{d_{i_{1},s}}\cdot\frac{|b_{i_{1}}^{\nu_{1}}c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}|}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{1}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{1},s}}\cdot\frac{\beta_{s}^{2}}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{1}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{1},s}}.

(3)

\pi(\nu_{k})=2
and
\pi(\nu_{1})\in\{1,3\}
: let
m\in\{2,\cdots,k\}
be the smallest index such that
\pi(\nu_{m})\notin\{1,3\}
, so
c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}=\beta_{s}
and
|b_{i_{m}}^{\nu_{m}}|\leq\beta_{s}
. Since
\pi(\nu_{m-1})\in\{1,3\}
, we also have either
|c_{i_{m}}^{\nu_{m-1}}|\leq\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}
or
|c_{i_{m}}^{\nu_{m-1}}|\leq\lambda
. Then

\displaystyle|\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}|\leq\frac{|c_{i_{m}}^{\nu_{m-1}}|}{d_{i_{m},s}}\cdot\frac{|b_{i_{m}}^{\nu_{m}}c_{j}^{\nu_{k}}|}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{m},s}}\cdot\frac{\beta_{s}^{2}}{d_{j,s}}\leq\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{m},s}}.

This concludes the proof of the lemma.
∎

B.6
Positive path correlation and graded recombination

Substituting Eq. (
36
) and integrating out
s
in Eq. (
35
) thus gives

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}}\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}

(39)

where the coefficients are given as

\displaystyle\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\zeta_{\iota,s}^{\mu,\nu}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Importantly,
\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
are uniformly bounded: by Lemma
24
, there exists an index
m
such that

\displaystyle|\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}s^{-1/2}\cdot\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}\vee\lambda}{d_{i_{m},s}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s=\frac{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}\vee\lambda}{\sqrt{\tilde{q}_{i_{m}}^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}\leq 1.

With Eq. (
37
) in mind, we further introduce a gradation in Eq. (
39
) according to degree; this is necessary to correctly apply Gaussian hypercontractivity later.

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}=\sum_{n=0}^{CK^{2}}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n},\quad{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{K}(-1)^{k}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}}\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=n\}}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}.

(40)

We now present a key insight which allows us to remove the coefficients
\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}
when computing moments of
{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}
.

Lemma 25

(positive path correlation)
.

Let
k,k^{\prime}\geq 0
. It holds for all paths
\iota\in\mathcal{I}^{k}
,
\iota^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}^{k^{\prime}}
and symbols
\mu,\mu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}
,
\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}
,
\nu^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k^{\prime}}
that

\displaystyle\E\left[{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\right]\geq 0,

where the expectation is taken over all
u_{1},\cdots,u_{N}
and
v_{1},\cdots,v_{N}
.

Proof.

{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu},{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}
are products of indices of
\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket
or
\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}
, so we may write

\displaystyle{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}=\prod_{(i,j)}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket_{ij}\prod_{(i,j)}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket_{ij}\prod_{(i,j)}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{ij}

(41)

where the products range over multisets of index pairs. We can remove the double brackets by restricting to
(i,j)\in[r]\times[r]
for
{\mathbf{E}}_{u},{\mathbf{E}}_{v}
, otherwise the product will be identically zero. Further expand each entry as

\displaystyle({\mathbf{E}}_{u})_{ij}

\displaystyle=u_{i}^{\top}u_{j}-\delta_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\quantity(u_{i\ell}u_{j\ell} - \frac{\delta_{ij}}{d}),

\displaystyle({\mathbf{E}}_{v})_{ij}

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{\top}v_{j}-\delta_{ij}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\quantity(v_{i\ell}v_{j\ell} - \frac{\delta_{ij}}{d}),

\displaystyle\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}_{ij}

\displaystyle=\lambda^{-1}\sum_{\ell=r+1}^{N}q_{\ell}u_{\ell i}v_{\ell j},

then Eq. (
41
) decomposes into a sum of terms with positive coefficients of the form

\displaystyle\prod_{\gamma}\quantity(u_\gamma^2-\frac 1d)\prod_{\gamma}\quantity(v_\gamma^2-\frac 1d)\prod_{\gamma}u_{\gamma}\prod_{\gamma}v_{\gamma}

where
\gamma\in[N]\times[d]
denote index pairs. Rescale
\tilde{u}_{\gamma}=\sqrt{d}u_{\gamma}
so that
\tilde{u}_{\gamma}
is i.i.d.
\mathcal{N}(0,1)
, then by symmetry it suffices to show

\displaystyle{\mathbf{Y}}=\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}}(\tilde{u}_{\gamma}^{2}-1)\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{B}}\tilde{u}_{\gamma}

has nonnegative expectation for arbitrary multisets
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{B}
. Denote multiset union by
\sqcup
. By Isserlis’ theorem,

\displaystyle\E[{\mathbf{Y}}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}(-1)^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\\
|\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|=m\end{subarray}}\E\left[\prod_{\gamma\in\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{B}}\tilde{u}_{\gamma}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{m\geq 0}(-1)^{m}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\subseteq\mathcal{A}\\
|\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|=m\end{subarray}}\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{B})

(42)

where
\mathcal{P}(\mathcal{C})
counts the number of ways to partition
\mathcal{C}
into pairs of equal index pairs. Then by inclusion–exclusion, Eq. (
42
) exactly counts the number of ways to partition
\mathcal{A}\sqcup\mathcal{A}\sqcup\mathcal{B}
into pairs which do not contain any of the
(\gamma,\gamma)
pairs arising from each of the
\tilde{u}_{\gamma}^{2}-1
factors, as fixing
m
such pairs in
\mathcal{A}\sqcup\mathcal{A}
yields a subset of ‘free’ index pairs
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\sqcup\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
where
|\mathcal{A}\setminus\mathcal{A}^{\prime}|=m
. Hence
\E[{\mathbf{Y}}]
is a count and thus nonnegative.
∎

To utilize this result, define the ‘coefficientless’ recombined version
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}
of
\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}
and its gradation
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}
analogously to Eq. (
40
),

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}:=\sum_{n=0}^{CK^{2}}\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n},\quad[\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}]_{ij}=\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}:=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\sum_{\iota\in\mathcal{I}_{ij}^{k}}\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}^{k}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=n\}}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}.

Then we can bound using Lemma
25
and
|\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}|,|\theta_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}|\leq 1
,

\displaystyle\E\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{k,\iota,\mu,\nu}\sum_{k^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}(-1)^{k+k^{\prime}}\theta_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}\theta_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=n\}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}=n\}}\E\left[{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k,\iota,\mu,\nu}\sum_{k^{\prime},\iota^{\prime},\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=n\}}\boldsymbol{1}_{\{n_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}=n\}}\E\left[{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota^{\prime}}^{\mu^{\prime},\nu^{\prime}}\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right].

(43)

Furthermore, define the ‘coefficientless’ versions of
{\mathbf{H}},\boldsymbol{\Delta}
as

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{H}}

\displaystyle:=\llbracket{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\rrbracket+\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket+\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell},

(44)

\displaystyle\hat{}\boldsymbol{\Delta}

\displaystyle:=\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket+\sum_{k=1}^{K}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{k}+\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}(\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell}+\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket^{k})

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{k,\ell=0}^{K}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{k}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket^{\ell}.

(45)

These correspond to removing precisely the coefficients
a_{ij}^{\mu}
(resp.
b_{i}^{\nu},c_{j}^{\nu}
) in the entrywise decompositions Eq. (
33
), Eq. (
34
), yielding the relations

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}=\sum_{\mu\in\mathcal{S}_{\mu}}{\mathbf{H}}_{ij}^{\mu},\quad\hat{}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}=\sum_{\nu\in\mathcal{S}_{\nu}}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{ij}^{\nu}

and

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}=\sum_{k,\iota,\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{T}}_{\iota}^{\mu,\nu}=\sum_{k,\iota,\mu,\nu}{\mathbf{H}}_{i_{0}i_{1}}^{\mu}\prod_{\ell=1}^{k}\boldsymbol{\Delta}_{i_{\ell}i_{\ell+1}}^{\nu_{\ell}}=\left[\sum_{k=0}^{K}\hat{}{\mathbf{H}}\smash[t]{\hat{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}^{k}\right]_{ij}.

Thus we obtain the recombined expression

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}=\sum_{k=0}^{K}\hat{}{\mathbf{H}}\smash[t]{\hat{\boldsymbol{\Delta}}}^{k}.

(46)

Since an
n
-fold product of matrices expands entrywise into a sum of
n
-fold products of entries,
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}
is precisely the grading of
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}
according to (polynomial) degree. In particular, we may express
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{:n}={\mathbf{F}}_{n}(\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket,\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}})
for some homogeneous matrix polynomial
{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
of degree
n
.

Next, let
\boldsymbol{\Pi}
be any
r\times r
permutation matrix and let

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}:=\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Pi}&\\
&{\mathbf{I}}_{d-r}\end{bmatrix}.

By symmetry,
({\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}({\mathbf{U}}\boldsymbol{\Pi},{\mathbf{V}}\boldsymbol{\Pi})
and independently

\displaystyle(u_{r+1},\cdots,u_{N},v_{r+1},\cdots,v_{N})\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}(\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}u_{r+1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}u_{N},\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}v_{r+1},\cdots,\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}v_{N}),

which implies

\displaystyle\quantity(\db{\bE_u},\db{\bE_v},\tilde\bZ)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}\quantity(\db{\bPi^\top\bU^\top\bU\bPi- \bI_r}, \db{\bPi^\top\bV^\top\bV\bPi- \bI_r}, \bPi_+^\top\tilde\bZ\bPi_+)

\displaystyle=\quantity(\bPi_+^\top\db{\bE_u}\bPi_+, \bPi_+^\top\db{\bE_v}\bPi_+, \bPi_+^\top\tilde\bZ\bPi_+).

Then for each
n
it holds that

\displaystyle\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}

\displaystyle={\mathbf{F}}_{n}\quantity(\db{\bE_u},\db{\bE_v},\tilde\bZ)

\displaystyle\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}{\mathbf{F}}_{n}\quantity(\bPi_+^\top\db{\bE_u}\bPi_+, \bPi_+^\top\db{\bE_v}\bPi_+, \bPi_+^\top\tilde\bZ\bPi_+)

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}{\mathbf{F}}_{n}\quantity(\db{\bE_u},\db{\bE_v},\tilde\bZ)\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}^{\top}\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}

since
{\mathbf{F}}_{n}
is a matrix polynomial, therefore
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{:n}
is also distributionally invariant under the permutation
\boldsymbol{\Pi}_{+}
. In particular, the second moment of
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{ij:n}
is equal for any pair of distinct indices
i,j\leq r
, and so

\displaystyle\E\left[\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\E\left[\sum_{i,j\leq r,i\neq j}\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{r(r-1)}\E\left[\lVert\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\rVert_{\F}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{d}{r(r-1)}\E\left[\lVert\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\right].

(47)

B.7
Graded tail bounds and hypercontractivity

We proceed to bound each
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}
. We remark that we only need to control products up to at most polylogarithmic degree since
n\leq CK^{2}\lesssim(\log d)^{2}
, otherwise the expectation would suffer superexponential blowup in
d
. In addition,
\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:0}=\llbracket{\mathbf{I}}_{r}\rrbracket
is diagonal (as is
{\mathbf{K}}_{:0}
) and does not affect Eq. (
47
), so we only consider
n\geq 1
.

Expanding all products in Eq. (
46
), the number of summed monomials in the expression
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{:n}={\mathbf{F}}_{n}(\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket,\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}})
can be upper bounded as follows. Each monomial is an
n
-fold product of
\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rrbracket,\llbracket{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rrbracket,\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}
which we write as a length
n
sequence; there are at most
3^{n}
possible sequences. This is further partitioned into
k+1
consecutive subsequences which simultaneously determine the power
k\geq 0
of
\hat{}\boldsymbol{\Delta}
, and which factor of
\hat{}{\mathbf{H}}
or
\hat{}\boldsymbol{\Delta}
each subsequence originated from. Since all terms in Eq. (
44
) are distinct, and all terms in Eq. (
45
) are also distinct, this information uniquely specifies each term in
\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{:n}
. As a partition can be specified by choosing a subset of points in the sequence as break points, the total number of such partitioned sequences is at most
3^{n}\times 2^{n}=6^{n}
.

The discussion thus far implies that

\displaystyle\lVert\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 6^{n}\max\left\{\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}},\lVert\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\right\}^{n}

and so

\displaystyle\E\left[\lVert\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 6^{2n}\quantity(\E\left[\nnorm{\bE_u}_\op^{2n}\right] + \E\left[\nnorm{\bE_v}_\op^{2n}\right] + \E\left[\nnorm{\tilde\bZ}_\op^{2n}\right])\left[\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{u}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}\right]+\E\left[\lVert{\mathbf{E}}_{v}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}\right]+\E\left[\lVert\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}\right].

(48)

We now bound each moment in turn.

For
{\mathbf{E}}_{u}
and
{\mathbf{E}}_{v}
, recall from Lemma
19
that

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\norm{\bE_u}_\op> C\max\left\{ \frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}}, \qty(\frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}})^2\right\})\leq 2e^{-t^{2}}.

Applying the tail integral formula and integrating by parts, we have that

\displaystyle\E\left[{\norm{\bE_u}_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}}\right]

\displaystyle=\int_{0}^{\infty}2ns^{2n-1}\Pr(\norm{\bE_u}_\op>s)\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n}+\int_{0}^{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{r}}2n\quantity(C\frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}})^{2n-1}2e^{-t^{2}}\cdot\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\qquad+\int_{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{r}}^{\infty}2n\quantity(C\qty(\frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}})^2)^{2n-1}2e^{-t^{2}}\cdot\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t.

The second term is bounded, using the inequality
(a+b)^{n}\leq 2^{n-1}(a^{n}+b^{n})
, as

\displaystyle\frac{4nC}{\sqrt{d}}\int_{0}^{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{r}}\quantity(C\frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}})^{2n-1}e^{-t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\frac{2^{2n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\quantity(C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n-1}\int_{0}^{\infty}e^{-t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t+\frac{2^{2n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\quantity(\frac{Ct}{\sqrt{d}})^{2n-1}e^{-t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2^{2n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\quantity(C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n-1}+\frac{2^{2n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\quantity(\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}})^{2n-1}\Gamma(n)

\displaystyle\lesssim\quantity(2C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n-1},

where we have used that
\Gamma(n)\lesssim n^{n-1/2}\ll\sqrt{r}^{2n-1}
and
n\lesssim(\log d)^{2}
.

Similarly, the third term is bounded as

\displaystyle\frac{4nC}{\sqrt{d}}\int_{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{r}}^{\infty}\quantity(C\qty(\frac{\sqrt{r}+t}{\sqrt{d}})^2)^{2n-1}e^{-t^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{2^{4n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\quantity(C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{4n-2}+\frac{2^{4n}nC}{\sqrt{d}}\quantity(\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}})^{4n-2}\Gamma\quantity(2n+\frac 12)

\displaystyle\lesssim\quantity(2C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{4n-2}.

We thus have

\displaystyle\E\left[{\norm{\bE_u}_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}}\right]=\E\left[{\norm{\bE_v}_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}}\right]\lesssim\quantity(2C\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n-1}.

For
\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}
, we have from Lemma
20
and Eq. (
16
) that

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\nnorm{\tilde\bZ}_\op> t\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})\leq e^{Cd(t_{0}-t)},\quad\forall t\geq t_{0}

for constants
C,t_{0}
. Then, substituting
s=Cd(t-t_{0})
,

\displaystyle\E\left[{\lVert\tilde{}{\mathbf{Z}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2n}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(t_0\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n}+2n\quantity(\frac{r}{d})^{n}\int_{t_{0}}^{\infty}t^{2n-1}e^{Cd(t_{0}-t)}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}t

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(t_0\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n}+\frac{2^{2n-1}nr^{n}}{Cd^{n+1}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\quantity(\qty(\frac{s}{Cd})^{2n-1} + t_0^{2n-1})e^{-s}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=\quantity(t_0\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n}+\frac{2^{2n-1}nr^{n}}{Cd^{n+1}}\quantity(\frac{\Gamma(2n)}{(Cd)^{2n-1}} + t_0^{2n-1})

\displaystyle\lesssim\quantity(2t_0\sqrt{\frac{r}{d}})^{2n}.

Recalling that
\rho\asymp\sqrt{r/d}
, we have shown that Eq. (
48
) is bounded as
(C\rho)^{2n-1}
for some constant
C
. Combining Eq. (
43
) and Eq. (
47
), it follows that

\displaystyle\E\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]\leq\E\left[\hat{{\mathbf{K}}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]\leq\frac{d}{r(r-1)}\E\left[\lVert\hat{}{\mathbf{K}}_{:n}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\right]\lesssim\frac{d}{r^{2}}(C\rho)^{2n-1}.

Now fix an integer
L\asymp\log d
such that
C\rho L\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Observe that each
{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}
is a multilinear polynomial of degree at most
2n
in the entries
u_{k\ell},v_{k\ell}
, thus by Gaussian hypercontractivity,

\displaystyle\E\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{L}\right]^{1/L}\leq(L-1)^{n}\E\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{2}\right]^{1/2}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{dL}}{r}(C\rho L)^{n-1/2}=:\frac{t}{\sqrt{L}}.

By Markov’s inequality,

\displaystyle\Pr(|\bK_{ij:n}| > t)\leq t^{-L}\E\left[{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}^{L}\right]\lesssim L^{-L/2}=d^{-\omega(1)}.

Therefore, union bounding over all
1\leq i,j\leq d
with
i\neq j
and
n\lesssim(\log d)^{2}
, we conclude:

\displaystyle|\tilde{}{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}|\leq\sum_{n=1}^{CK^{2}}|{\mathbf{K}}_{ij:n}|\lesssim\sum_{n=1}^{CK^{2}}\frac{L\sqrt{d}}{r}(C\rho L)^{n-1/2}\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}

and hence

\displaystyle|{\mathbf{K}}_{ij}|\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{3}}{\sqrt{d}}

(49)

with probability
1-d^{-\omega(1)}
.

B.8
Lipschitz concentration for tail logits

We now bound the magnitude of the interactions
\gamma_{ij}
when either
q_{i}
or
q_{j}\ll\lambda
, which is true when
\max\{i,j\}>r
under
\mathcal{E}_{q}
. Here, we only provide the argument for when
q_{j}
is small. We first show that
h_{\lambda}
is
\lambda^{-1}
-Lipschitz w.r.t. operator norm.

Proposition 26

(operator Lipschitz bound)
.

For
\lambda>0
,
h_{\lambda}(z)=\frac{z}{\sqrt{z^{2}+\lambda^{2}}}
and arbitrary
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, it holds that
\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
and

\displaystyle\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})-h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{B}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lambda^{-1}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}-{\mathbf{B}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

(50)

We remark that in general, matrix functions do not inherit the Lipschitz constant of the underlying scalar function in operator norm (although this is true in Frobenius norm; see
25
). For this particular result, we rely on a uniform integral representation of
h_{\lambda}
.

Proof.

The first claim
\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 1
holds since the range of
h_{\lambda}
is contained in
[-1,1]
. We now prove the main claim. We first show Eq. (
50
) for symmetric
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}
; note that since
h_{\lambda}
is odd,
h_{\lambda}
is equal to the usual functional calculus when applied to symmetric matrices. Consider the integral representation

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(t)=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{\infty}\frac{t}{t^{2}+\delta_{s}^{2}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s,\quad\delta_{s}:=\sqrt{\lambda^{2}+s^{2}}.

For a real symmetric matrix
{\mathbf{A}}
, define the map

\displaystyle h_{\lambda,R}({\mathbf{A}}):=\frac{2}{\pi}\int_{0}^{R}{\mathbf{A}}\quantity(\bA^2+\delta_s^2 \bI_d)^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s

so that
h_{\lambda,R}({\mathbf{A}})\to h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})
as
R\to\infty
. Note that

\displaystyle\quantity(\bA+\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\bA-\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)\quantity(\bA^2+\delta_s^2 \bI_d)^{-1},

\displaystyle\quantity(\bA-\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}

\displaystyle=\quantity(\bA+\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)\quantity(\bA^2+\delta_s^2 \bI_d)^{-1},

so we may express

\displaystyle h_{\lambda,R}({\mathbf{A}})=\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\quantity(\bA+\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}+\quantity(\bA-\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s.

Denoting the spectrum of
{\mathbf{A}}
by
\sigma({\mathbf{A}})
, it holds that

\displaystyle\norm{\qty(\bA\pm\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}}_{\mathrm{op}}=\max_{\mu\in\sigma({\mathbf{A}})}\frac{1}{\absolutevalue{\mu\pm\mathrm{i} \delta_s}}=\max_{\mu\in\sigma({\mathbf{A}})}\frac{1}{\sqrt{\mu^{2}+\delta_{s}^{2}}}\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{s}}.

Hence for all real symmetric
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}
,

\displaystyle\norm{\qty(\bA\pm\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1} - \qty(\bB\pm\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle=\norm{\qty(\bA\pm\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1} (\bB-\bA) \qty(\bB\pm\mathrm{i}\delta_s \bI_d)^{-1}}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\delta_{s}^{2}}\norm{\bA-\bB}_{\mathrm{op}}

and so

\displaystyle\lVert h_{\lambda,R}({\mathbf{A}})-h_{\lambda,R}({\mathbf{B}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{\pi}\int_{0}^{R}\frac{2}{\lambda^{2}+s^{2}}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}-{\mathbf{B}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\mathop{}\!\mathrm{d}s\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}-{\mathbf{B}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

Eq. (
50
) follows by taking
R\to\infty
.

Now for general
{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}
, define the symmetric dilations

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{A}}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&{\mathbf{A}}\\
{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d},\quad\tilde{\mathbf{B}}:=\begin{bmatrix}0&{\mathbf{B}}\\
{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{2d\times 2d}.

Let the SVD of
{\mathbf{A}}
be
{\mathbf{A}}={\mathbf{U}}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}
and define the
2d\times 2d
orthogonal matrix

\displaystyle{\mathbf{O}}:=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\begin{bmatrix}{\mathbf{U}}&{\mathbf{U}}\\
{\mathbf{V}}&-{\mathbf{V}}\end{bmatrix}.

Then by a simple computation,
\tilde{\mathbf{A}}
can be diagonalized as

\displaystyle\tilde{\mathbf{A}}={\mathbf{O}}\begin{bmatrix}\boldsymbol{\Sigma}&0\\
0&-\boldsymbol{\Sigma}\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{O}}^{\top}

so that

\displaystyle h_{\lambda}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})={\mathbf{O}}\begin{bmatrix}h_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})&0\\
0&-h_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\Sigma})\end{bmatrix}{\mathbf{O}}^{\top}=\begin{bmatrix}0&h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})\\
h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})^{\top}&0\end{bmatrix}.

Since operator norm is preserved under dilation, we conclude:

\displaystyle\lVert h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{A}})-h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{B}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\lVert h_{\lambda}(\tilde{\mathbf{A}})-h_{\lambda}(\tilde{\mathbf{B}})\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert\tilde{\mathbf{A}}-\tilde{\mathbf{B}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}=\frac{1}{\lambda}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}-{\mathbf{B}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

∎

We now show that (a truncated version of) each logit is a centered Lipschitz function of the pair
(u_{j},v_{j})
.

Lemma 27
.

Let
u,v\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\mathbf{I}}_{d}/d)
i.i.d. Define the maps

\displaystyle F:(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}\to\mathbb{R},\quad F(u,v):=u^{\top}h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-j}+q_{j}uv^{\top})v_{i},

\displaystyle\iota:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d},\quad\iota(u)=\frac{u}{1\vee\frac{1}{2}\norm{u}_{2}}.

Then the map
(u,v)\mapsto F(\iota(u),\iota(v))
is centered and
(2+16\lambda^{-1}q_{j})
-Lipschitz.

Proof.

Since
(u,v)\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}(-u,-v)
and

\displaystyle F(\iota(-u),\iota(-v))=F(-\iota(u),-\iota(v))=-F(\iota(u),\iota(v)),

we have
\E[F(\iota(u),\iota(v))]=0
by symmetry. Also note that
\iota
is a projection to an
L^{2}
-ball and thus
1
-Lipschitz. For
(u,v),(u^{\prime},v^{\prime})\in(\mathbb{R}^{d})^{2}
, let

\displaystyle H:=h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-j}+q_{j}\iota(u)\iota(v)^{\top}),\quad H^{\prime}:=h_{\lambda}({\mathbf{G}}_{-j}+q_{j}\iota(u^{\prime})\iota(v^{\prime})^{\top}).

Then by Proposition
26
and
\norm{\iota(u)}_{2}\leq 2
,

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{F(\iota(u),\iota(v))-F(\iota(u'),\iota(v'))}

\displaystyle\leq\norm{\iota(u)-\iota(u')}_{2}\norm{H}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{\iota(v_i)}_{2}+\norm{\iota(u')}_{2}\norm{H-H'}_{\mathrm{op}}\norm{\iota(v_i)}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq 2\norm{u-u'}_{2}+\frac{4q_{j}}{\lambda}\norm{\iota(u)\iota(v)^\top- \iota(u')\iota(v')^\top}_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(2+\frac{8q_j}{\lam})\norm{u-u'}_{2}+\frac{8q_{j}}{\lambda}\norm{v-v'}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(2+\frac{16q_j}{\lam})\norm{(u,v)-(u',v')}_{2}.

This proves the assertion.
∎

By Lemma
27
and concentration of Lipschitz functions of Gaussians
[
54
, Theorem 2.26]
, it follows that

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\abs{F(\iota(u),\iota(v))} \ge t)\leq 2\exp\quantity(-\frac{dt^2}{2(2+16\lam^{-1}q_j)^2})

where the extra
d
factor comes from the variance scaling of
u,v
. Moreover we have
\norm{u_k}_{2},\norm{v_k}_{2}\leq 2
for all
k\in[N]
with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
, so that
\iota(u_{j})=u_{j}
,
\iota(v_{j})=v_{j}
and

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\gamma_{ij}}=\absolutevalue{F(u_j,v_j)}\lesssim\quantity(1+\frac{q_j}{\lam})\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}.

(51)

Under
\mathcal{E}_{q}
, we further have
q_{j}\leq\lVert q_{>r}\rVert_{\infty}<\lambda
, hence
\absolutevalue{\gamma_{ij}}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}
if
j>r
. A similar argument applies when
i>r
. We remark that while Eq. (
51
) holds for all
i,j\in[N]
, we still need the more involved argument for the leading block since our guarantee for the signal in Eq. (
20
) is upper bounded by
\widetilde{O}(1)
.

C.1
Proof of Theorem

Item

is recovered by the SGD update

iff

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}_{0}v_{i}>\max_{j\neq i}u_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}_{0}v_{i}.

(52)

The lower bound amounts to comparing the signal and noise magnitudes of the top
d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
items. For the upper bound, we will show that items
i\gtrsim d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
are unlikely to be recovered due to the large random noise from the top
\Theta(\log d)
competitors.

First note that

\displaystyle\max_{i\neq j}\left\{\absolutevalue{\langle u_i,u_j \rangle},|\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}-1|,\absolutevalue{\langle v_i,v_j \rangle},|\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}-1|\right\}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}

(53)

with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
, due to the usual concentration bounds. The difference between the centered and uncentered logits can then be bounded as follows:

Lemma 28
.

It holds with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
that

\displaystyle\max_{i,j\in[N]}|u_{j}^{\top}({\mathbf{G}}_{0}-{\mathbf{G}})v_{i}|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{d}.

This improves upon the uniform control in Eq. (
21
) as we can explicitly use the inner product structure of the logits in the SGD case.

Proof.

Let
\bar{u}_{-i}:=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{j\neq i}u_{j}
, then
\bar{u}_{-i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{N-1}{N^{2}d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
and so
|\langle u_{i},\bar{u}_{-i}\rangle|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{Nd}}
for all
i\in[N]
with probability
1-O(d^{-M})
. Hence,

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{\top}({\mathbf{G}}_{0}-{\mathbf{G}})v_{i}|

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{\sum_{k\in[N]} -q_k\langle u_i,\bar{u}\rangle\langle v_k,v_i\rangle}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k\in[N]}q_{k}|\langle u_{i},\bar{u}_{-i}\rangle||\langle v_{k},v_{i}\rangle|+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{k\in[N]}q_{k}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}|\langle v_{k},v_{i}\rangle|\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{Nd}}+\frac{1}{N}.

The result follows by noting that
N\gtrsim d
.
∎

Furthermore, we have from Eq. (
53
)

\displaystyle|u_{i}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}v_{i}-q_{i}|

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{q_i \qty(\nnorm{u_i}_2^2 \nnorm{v_i}_2^2 -1) + \sum_{k\ne i} q_k \langle u_i,u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle}

\displaystyle\lesssim q_{i}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+\frac{\log d}{d}\sum_{k\neq i}q_{k}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{d}

and

\displaystyle|u_{j}^{\top}{\mathbf{G}}v_{i}-q_{j}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle|

\displaystyle=\absolutevalue{q_i \langle u_i,u_j\rangle\nnorm{v_i}_2^2 + q_j\qty(\nnorm{u_j}_2^2 -1) \langle v_i,v_j\rangle+ \sum_{k\ne i,j} q_k \langle u_j,u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle}

\displaystyle\lesssim q_{i}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}+q_{j}\frac{\log d}{d}+\frac{\log d}{d}\sum_{k\neq i}q_{k}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{d}.

Combining these bounds, we see that Eq. (
52
) is implied by

\displaystyle q_{i}\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}>\max_{j\neq i}q_{j}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle+O\quantity(\frac{\log d}{d})

(54)

where the second bound follows from
q_{j}\leq 1
and Eq. (
53
). By the Chernoff bound, for items satisfying
p_{i}\gtrsim\frac{\log d}{B}
it holds w.h.p. that
q_{i}\asymp p_{i}
:

\displaystyle\Pr(|q_i-p_i| \ge\frac{p_i}{2})\leq 2\exp(-\frac{Bp_i}{3})\lesssim\frac{1}{d^{M}},

(55)

so the first inequality in Eq. (
54
) holds if

\displaystyle p_{i}\gtrsim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\quad\text{and}\quad p_{i}\gtrsim\frac{\log d}{B}.

Therefore items
i\lesssim\min\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}
are always recovered, proving the lower bound.

Conversely, Eq. (
52
) implies

\displaystyle q_{i}>\max_{j\neq i}q_{j}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle-O\quantity(\frac{\log d}{d}).

(56)

First suppose that
B\gtrsim\sqrt{d}(\log d)^{\alpha+1}
and
p_{i}\gtrsim\frac{\log d}{B}
.
For each
j\leq L\log d
, it holds that
p_{j}\geq d^{-o(1)}
so that the Chernoff bound Eq. (
55
) holds for index
j
as well. Then
j\leq d^{-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}i
so that
p_{j}\gtrsim\sqrt{d}p_{i}
and so
q_{j}\gtrsim\sqrt{d}q_{i}
. We also have that
q_{j}\geq\frac{1}{2}p_{j}\gtrsim d^{-o(1)}
. Thus Eq. (
56
) further implies

\displaystyle\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\leq\frac{1}{q_{j}}\quantity( q_i + O\qty(\frac{\log d}{d}))\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}

for some constant
C
(independent of
L
). If
p_{i}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{B}
, we instead use

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(Bq_i\ge m)\leq\binom{B}{m}p_{i}^{m}\leq\quantity(\frac{eBp_i}{m})^{m}\lesssim\frac{1}{d^{M}}

for sufficiently large
m\asymp\log d
, so
q_{i}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{B}
and
q_{j}\gtrsim p_{j}\gtrsim(L\log d)^{-a}
again implies

\displaystyle\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\lesssim\frac{1}{q_{j}}\cdot\frac{\log d}{\min\{B,d\}}\leq\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}}.

(57)

Now since
\sqrt{d}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle

\mathcal{N}(0,\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2})
conditioned on
v_{i}
and
\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}\geq\frac{1}{2}
, this probability can be bounded as

\displaystyle\Pr(\max_{j\le L\log d}\langle v_i,v_j\rangle\le\frac{C}{\sqrt{d}})\leq\Pr(\cN(0,1)\le 2C)^{L\log d}\lesssim\frac{1}{d^{M}}

by taking

in Eq. (

Finally, if

15
shows that we sample at most

and so Eq. (
56

, hence the same conclusion as above holds.

We have thus shown that items

\displaystyle i>i_{\SGD}^{\star}\asymp\min\left\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(\log d)^{1+\frac{1}{\alpha}},B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\right\}

are not recovered with high probability. It follows that
\hat{p}_{1}(i\mid i)\leq\frac{1}{2}
for these items, and hence the cross-entropy loss is lower bounded as

\displaystyle L({\mathbf{W}}_{1}^{\SGD})=\mathbb{E}_{i\sim p}[-\log p_{{\mathbf{W}}}(i\mid i)]\geq\sum_{i>i_{\SGD}^{\star}}p_{i}\log 2\geq\widetilde{\Omega}\quantity(\max\left\{ d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}-\frac 12}, B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}-1}\right\}),

as was to be shown.

C.2
Proof of Proposition
6

We first show that
\Spec(d)
is equal to the set of (bounded) spectral estimators
h
which maps an SVD
{\mathbf{X}}={\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}
to
h({\mathbf{X}})={\mathbf{A}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{B}}^{\top}
where
h({\mathbf{S}})
is diagonal. Clearly, any such estimator is bi-orthogonally invariant: for any
{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}\in O(d)
,

\displaystyle h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top})=h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top})={\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{A}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{B}}^{\top}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}={\mathbf{U}}h({\mathbf{X}}){\mathbf{V}}^{\top}.

Conversely, let
h\in\Spec(d)
. Since
h({\mathbf{X}})={\mathbf{A}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{B}}^{\top}
by equivariance, it suffices to show that the matrix
h({\mathbf{S}})
is diagonal. Let
{\mathbf{D}}=\diag(\pm 1,\dots,\pm 1)\in O(d)
be any diagonal sign matrix.
Since
{\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{D}}={\mathbf{S}}
, equivariance yields

h({\mathbf{S}})=h({\mathbf{D}}{\mathbf{S}}{\mathbf{D}})={\mathbf{D}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{D}}.

Looking at each entry, this implies for
i\neq j
that
h({\mathbf{S}})_{ij}=({\mathbf{D}}h({\mathbf{S}}){\mathbf{D}})_{ij}={\mathbf{D}}_{ii}{\mathbf{D}}_{jj}h({\mathbf{S}})_{ij}
. Choosing
{\mathbf{D}}
with
{\mathbf{D}}_{ii}=1
and
{\mathbf{D}}_{jj}=-1
forces
h({\mathbf{S}})_{ij}=0
as desired.

We proceed to prove minimax optimality of
\Spec(d)
. Let
\nu_{d}
denote the normalized Haar measure on
O(d)
and let
({\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}})\sim\nu_{d}\otimes\nu_{d}
. Given any measurable
h
, define its conjugation

\displaystyle h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{X}}):={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}){\mathbf{V}},

and also define its bi-orthogonal symmetrization

\displaystyle\bar{h}({\mathbf{X}}):=\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{X}})]=\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[{\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}){\mathbf{V}}].

The map
\bar{h}
is well-defined since
h
is measurable and
\lVert h\rVert_{\F}

{\mathbf{A}},{\mathbf{B}}\in O(d)

\displaystyle\bar{h}({\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top})

\displaystyle=\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[{\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}){\mathbf{V}}]

\displaystyle={\mathbf{A}}\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{A}})^{\top}h({\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{A}}{\mathbf{X}}{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}){\mathbf{V}}{\mathbf{B}}]{\mathbf{B}}^{\top}={\mathbf{A}}\bar{h}({\mathbf{X}}){\mathbf{B}}^{\top},

{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}\in O(d)

\displaystyle u_{i}^{\prime}={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}u_{i},\quad v_{i}^{\prime}={\mathbf{V}}^{\top}v_{i},\quad{\mathbf{W}}^{\prime}={\mathbf{U}}^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}{\mathbf{V}}

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(h)

\displaystyle=\E\left[L({\mathbf{U}}^{\top}h({\mathbf{G}}_{0}){\mathbf{V}};({\mathbf{U}}^{\top}u_{i},{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}v_{i})_{i\in[N]},\mathcal{B})\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[L(h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{G}}_{0}^{\prime});(u_{i}^{\prime},v_{i}^{\prime})_{i\in[N]},\mathcal{B})\right]

\displaystyle=\mathcal{R}(h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}).

For the last inequality, we have used that

due to isotropy of the Gaussian distribution. Also note that the map
{\mathbf{W}}\mapsto L({\mathbf{W}})
is convex due to convexity of log-sum-exp. Taking expectations over
({\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}})\sim\nu_{d}\otimes\nu_{d}
and applying Jensen’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\mathcal{R}(h)=\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[\mathcal{R}(h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}})]

\displaystyle=\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}\left[\E\left[L(h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{G}}_{0}))\right]\right]

\displaystyle\geq\E\left[L(\E_{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}[h^{{\mathbf{U}},{\mathbf{V}}}({\mathbf{G}}_{0})])\right]=\mathcal{R}(\bar{h}).

Therefore, the infimum must be attained by a spectral estimator.

Proof of Lemma
8

We present the proof for the full uncentered gradient
{\mathbf{G}}=\sum_{i\in[N]}p_{i}u_{i}v_{i}^{\top}
; the leave-one-out case follows similarly. As in the proof of Lemma

, we have

\displaystyle{\mathbf{G}}\mathrel{\stackrel{{\scriptstyle d}}{{=}}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}{\mathbf{M}}^{1/2}{\mathbf{Z}},\quad\text{where}\quad{\mathbf{Z}}_{k\ell}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,1)\;\;\text{i.i.d.}

where
{\mathbf{M}}
is the weighted covariance matrix of
u_{i}
from Eq. (

{\mathbf{G}}
, i.e., the nuclear norm of
{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}
. By submultiplicativity,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{G}}^{-1}\rVert_{*}=\sqrt{d}\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}{\mathbf{M}}^{-1/2}\rVert_{*}\leq\sqrt{d}\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}\rVert_{*}\lVert{\mathbf{M}}^{-1/2}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

(58)

We now bound each term. For
\lVert{\mathbf{M}}^{-1/2}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
, we have from
51
that for a sufficiently large constant
C
,

\displaystyle{\mathbf{M}}\succeq\sum_{i=d}^{Cd}p_{i}^{2}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}\succeq(Cd)^{-2\alpha}\sum_{i=d}^{Cd}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}\succeq(Cd)^{-2\alpha}\cdot\Theta(1){\mathbf{I}}_{d}

and so
\lVert{\mathbf{M}}^{-1/2}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim d^{\alpha}
with probability
1-e^{-\Omega(d)}
.

To bound
\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}\rVert_{*}
, we require more precise control on the singular spectrum of
{\mathbf{Z}}
. Denote the
d\times k
matrix consisting of the first
k
columns of
{\mathbf{Z}}
as
{\mathbf{Z}}_{1:k}
. By the Courant–Fisher theorem,

\displaystyle s_{k}({\mathbf{Z}})^{2}=\lambda_{k}({\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}})=\max_{\dim E=k}\min_{x\in E,\norm{x}=1}x^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}^{\top}{\mathbf{Z}}x\geq\min_{\norm{x}=1}\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}_{1:k}x\rVert_{2}^{2}=s_{\min}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1:k})^{2}

so that
s_{k}({\mathbf{Z}})\geq s_{\min}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1:k})
for all

. Moreover by Theorem 1.1 of
44
, we have for all

,

\displaystyle\Pr(s_{\min}(\bZ_{1:k}) \le t(\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{k-1}))\leq(Ct)^{d-k+1}+e^{-\Omega(d)}

for some constant
C
. Taking

and union bounding,

\displaystyle\Pr(s_{\min}(\bZ_{1:k}) \le\frac{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{k-1}}{\log d}:\forall k)\leq\sum_{k=1}^{d}\quantity(\frac{C}{\log d})^{d-k+1}+e^{-\Omega(d)}\lesssim\frac{1}{\log d}.

This further implies

\displaystyle s_{k}({\mathbf{Z}})\geq s_{\min}({\mathbf{Z}}_{1:k})\geq\frac{\sqrt{d}-\sqrt{k-1}}{\log d}\geq\frac{d-k+1}{2\sqrt{d}\log d}

for all
k
, hence

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}^{-1}\rVert_{*}=\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{1}{s_{k}({\mathbf{Z}})}\leq 2\sqrt{d}\log d\sum_{k=1}^{d}\frac{1}{d-k+1}\lesssim\sqrt{d}(\log d)^{2}.

We conclude from Eq. (
58

C.4
Properties of the Cubic Newton–Schulz Iteration

Lemma 29
.

Let
h(z)=\frac{3}{2}z-\frac{1}{2}z^{3}
be the cubic Newton–Schulz map and let
h^{(k)}
denote its
k
-fold iterate. Then the maps
z\mapsto h^{(k)}(z)/z
are nonincreasing and
\lim_{k\to\infty}h^{(k)}(z)=1=\sgn(z)
for all
z\in(0,\sqrt{3})
.

In other words, the line segment connecting the origin and the point
(z,h^{(k)}(z))
on the graph of
h^{(k)}
becomes flatter as
z
increases, which is clear from visual inspection.

Proof.

Since
h
maps
[0,\sqrt{3}]
onto
[0,1]
, it holds that
0\leq h^{(k)}\leq 1
on
[0,\sqrt{3}]
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
. We prove by induction that
h^{(k)}(z)/z
is nonincreasing on
[0,\sqrt{3}]
, equivalently

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{h^{(k)}(z)}{z})^{\prime}=\frac{z(h^{(k)})^{\prime}(z)-h^{(k)}(z)}{z^{2}}\leq 0\quad\Leftrightarrow\quad z(h^{(k)})^{\prime}(z)\leq h^{(k)}(z).

(59)

For
k=1
, the claim is clear. Assume Eq. (
59
) holds, then

\displaystyle z(h^{(k+1)})^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=zh^{\prime}(h^{(k)}(z))(h^{(k)})^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{2}\quantity(1-h^{(k)}(z)^2)z(h^{(k)})^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{3}{2}\quantity(1-h^{(k)}(z)^2)h^{(k)}(z).

Thus,

\displaystyle h^{(k+1)}(z)=h(h^{(k)}(z))=\quantity(\frac 32-\frac{1}{2}h^{(k)}(z)^2)h^{(k)}(z)\geq z(h^{(k+1)})^{\prime}(z).

This proves the first claim. For the pointwise limit, note that the first iterate
h(z)\in[0,1]
for any
z\in(0,\sqrt{3})
and also
x\leq h(x)\leq 1
for all
x\in[0,1]
, so the sequence of iterates
\{h^{(k)}(z)\}_{k\geq 1}
is monotone increasing. Hence it must converge to a positive fixed point of
h
, the only solution being
1
.
∎

We first collect some necessary concentration inequalities.

Lemma 30
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{N\times m}
be fixed and let
{\mathbf{Z}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}
have i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries. Then for every
t\geq 0
,

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(\nnorm{\bZ\bA}_{\op} \ge\nnorm{\bA}_{\F} + (\sqrt d + t)\nnorm{\bA}_{\op})\leq 2e^{-ct^{2}}

for some universal constant
c
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{T}:=\mathbb{S}^{m-1}\times\mathbb{S}^{d-1}
and define the Gaussian processes

\displaystyle X_{u,v}:=\langle{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{A}}u,v\rangle,\quad Y_{u,v}:=\langle g,{\mathbf{A}}u\rangle+\norm{\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}\langle h,v\rangle,

where
g\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\mathbf{I}}_{N})
and
h\sim\mathcal{N}(0,{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
are independent, so that

\displaystyle\norm{\bZ\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}=\sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}}X_{u,v}.

(60)

We compare the increments of
X,Y
. For
(u,v),(w,z)\in\mathcal{T}
,

\displaystyle\E\left[(X_{u,v}-X_{w,z})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\langle{\mathbf{Z}},v({\mathbf{A}}u)^{\top}-z({\mathbf{A}}w)^{\top}\rangle_{\F}^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\lVert v({\mathbf{A}}u)^{\top}-z({\mathbf{A}}w)^{\top}\rVert_{\F}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{\bA u}_{2}^{2}+\norm{\bA w}_{2}^{2}-2\langle{\mathbf{A}}u,{\mathbf{A}}w\rangle\langle v,z\rangle.

On the other hand,

\displaystyle\E\left[(Y_{u,v}-Y_{w,z})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=\norm{\bA(u-w)}_{2}^{2}+\norm{\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}\norm{v-z}_{2}^{2}

\displaystyle=\norm{\bA u}_{2}^{2}+\norm{\bA w}_{2}^{2}-2\langle{\mathbf{A}}u,{\mathbf{A}}w\rangle+2\norm{\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}^{2}(1-\langle v,z\rangle).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\E\left[(Y_{u,v}-Y_{w,z})^{2}\right]-\E\left[(X_{u,v}-X_{w,z})^{2}\right]

\displaystyle=2(1-\langle v,z\rangle)\quantity(\norm{\bA}_{\op}^2 - \langle\bA u,\bA w\rangle)\geq 0.

Hence by the Sudakov–Fernique inequality and Eq. (
60
), we obtain

\displaystyle\E[\norm{\bZ\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}]=\E\left[\sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}}X_{u,v}\right]\leq\E\left[\sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}}Y_{u,v}\right].

Since
g,h
are independent, the right-hand side is further bounded as

\displaystyle\E\left[\sup_{(u,v)\in\mathcal{T}}Y_{u,v}\right]

\displaystyle=\E\left[\sup_{u\in\mathbb{S}^{m-1}}\langle g,{\mathbf{A}}u\rangle\right]+\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\E\left[\sup_{v\in\mathbb{S}^{d-1}}\langle h,v\rangle\right]

\displaystyle=\E\lVert{\mathbf{A}}^{\top}g\rVert_{2}+\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\E\norm{h}_{2}

\displaystyle\leq\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\F}+\sqrt{d}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

Now for the tail estimate, the map
{\mathbf{Z}}\mapsto\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}
is
\norm{\bA}_{\mathrm{op}}
-Lipschitz with respect to the Frobenius norm, thus by Gaussian concentration we have

\displaystyle\Pr\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\geq\quantity(
\nnorm{\bZ\bA}_{\op}
\ge\E[\nnorm{\bZ\bA}_{\op}] + t\nnorm{\bA}_{\op}
)[\lVert{\mathbf{Z}}{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}]+t\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq 2e^{-ct^{2}}.

Combining this with the expectation bound yields the claimed bound.
∎

Lemma 31
.

Let
{\mathbf{A}}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
be fixed and
u,v\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})
i.i.d. Then with probability

\displaystyle|u^{\top}{\mathbf{A}}v|\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{d}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\F}+\frac{\log d}{d}\lVert{\mathbf{A}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

\sigma_{1},\cdots,\sigma_{d}\geq 0

\displaystyle\Pr\quantity(|u^\top\bA v| \ge\tau)\leq 2\exp\quantity(-C\min\left\{\frac{d^2\tau^2}{\sum_i\sigma_i^2}, \frac{d\tau}{\max_i \sigma_i}\right\})

from which the statement follows.
∎

Lemma 33
.

Let
{\mathbf{M}}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}q_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}
where
u_{i}\sim\mathcal{N}(0,\frac{1}{d}{\mathbf{I}}_{d})

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\frac{\lVert q\rVert_{1}}{d}+\lVert q\rVert_{\infty},\quad\lVert{\mathbf{M}}\rVert_{\F}\lesssim\frac{\lVert q\rVert_{1}}{\sqrt{d}}+\lVert q\rVert_{2}.

Proof.

Let

{\mathbf{Z}}=\sqrt{d}\begin{bmatrix}u_{1}\;\cdots\;u_{N}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times N}

We will make use of the following concentration phenomenon for Gaussian maxima.

Lemma 34

(superconcentration of Gaussian maxima)
.

Let
Z_{1},\cdots,Z_{n}
be i.i.d standard Gaussian. There exists a constant
C>0
such that

\displaystyle\Pr(\max_{i\in[n]} Z_i \le\sqrt{2\log 2n} - \frac{C\log\log n}{\sqrt{\log n}})=n^{-\omega(1)}.

Proof.

The Gaussian cumulative distribution function satisfies

\displaystyle\Pr(Z \le a)\leq 1-\frac{a}{a^{2}+1}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\exp(-\frac{a^2}{2}),\quad\forall a>0.

Let

\displaystyle a_{n}=\sqrt{2\log 2n}-\frac{C\log\log n}{\sqrt{2\log 2n}}

so that
a_{n}^{2}\leq 2\log 2n-2C\log\log n+1
for sufficiently large
n
.
It follows that

\displaystyle\Pr(\max_{i\in[n]} Z_i \le a_n)

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(1-\frac{a_n}{a_n^2+1} \frac{1}{\sqrt{2\pi}} \exp(-\frac{a_n^2}{2}))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(1-\frac{1}{2\sqrt{2\pi} a_n} \exp(-\log 2n + C\log\log n -\frac 12))^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\quantity(1-\frac{e^{C\log\log n}}{8n\sqrt{\pi e \log 2n}})^{n}

\displaystyle\leq\exp(-\frac{(\log n)^C}{8\sqrt{\pi e \log 2n}})

\displaystyle=n^{-\omega(1)}

for
C>1.5
, where we have used
1-z\leq e^{-z}
for the last inequality.
∎

\eta_{t}\asymp d_{t+1}^{\alpha}(\log d)^{2}
. It is straightforward to check for both cases of Eq. (
66
) that

\displaystyle\quantity(\frac{d_{t+1}}{d_t})^{\alpha}\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{d}}{(\log d)^{5}},\quad d_{t+1}^{\alpha}d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\lesssim\frac{d}{(\log d)^{5}}.

(67)

Fix an item
d_{\tau-1}<i\leq d_{\tau}
so that
B\gtrsim d_{\tau-1}^{\alpha}\log d
, and fix
j\neq i
with
d_{\tau^{\prime}-1}<j\leq d_{\tau^{\prime}}
. We control the bulk of the gradient as follows.

Lemma 36
.

It holds for all
i,j
and
t\leq\tau-1
that

\displaystyle\norm{\sum_{k>d_t,k\ne i,j} q_k^{(t)}u_k v_k^\top}_{\F}\lesssim d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\log d.

Proof.

As in Section
B.2
, gather all appearing terms into matrices

\displaystyle{\mathbf{U}}

\displaystyle=\begin{bmatrix}u_{d_{t}+1}\;\cdots\;u_{N}\end{bmatrix},{\mathbf{V}}=\begin{bmatrix}v_{d_{t}+1}\;\cdots\;v_{N}\end{bmatrix}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times\Theta(N)},\quad{\mathbf{Q}}=\diag\quantity(q_{d_t+1}^{(t)},\cdots,q_N^{(t)}).

Since
\sqrt{d}{\mathbf{U}}
has i.i.d. standard Gaussian entries, by Lemma
30
,

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\F}\leq\sqrt{d}\,\lVert{\mathbf{U}}{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\F}+\sqrt{d}\,\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\sqrt{d}\,\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}.

Applying Lemma
30
again to
\sqrt{d}{\mathbf{V}}
gives

\displaystyle\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}{\mathbf{V}}^{\top}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\lesssim\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}\rVert_{\F}+\lVert{\mathbf{Q}}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\frac{\lVert q_{>d_{t}}^{(t)}\rVert_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}+\lVert q_{>d_{t}}^{(t)}\rVert_{\infty}.

Since
B\gtrsim d_{\tau-1}^{\alpha}\log d\geq d_{t}^{\alpha}\log d
, we have that
\norm{q_{>d_t}}_{2}\lesssim d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}\log d
and
\lVert q_{>d_{t}}\rVert_{\infty}\leq d_{t}^{-\alpha}
w.h.p. by Lemma
15
. Plugging in above gives the desired result.
∎

Combining Lemma
31
and Lemma
36
, we have that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\sum_{k>d_t,k\ne i,j} q_k^{(t)} \langle u_j,u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{d}\,\norm{\sum_{k>d_t,k\ne i,j} q_k^{(t)}u_kv_k^\top}_{\F}\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{d}d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}.

(68)

Now we bound the interaction logits at step
\tau
as

\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau,ij}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}-1}\eta_{t}q_{j}^{(t)}\lVert u_{j}\rVert_{2}^{2}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}\sum_{k>d_{t},k\neq i,j}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{j},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}+\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}-1}\eta_{t}q_{j}^{(t)}+\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{d}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\eta_{\tau-1}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\quantity(p_i + \frac{\log d}{B})+\eta_{\tau\wedge\tau^{\prime}-1}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\quantity(p_j + \frac{\log d}{B})+\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{d}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim(\log d)^{2}\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}}\quantity(\frac{d_\tau^\alpha}{d_{\tau-1}^\alpha} + \frac{d_{\tau'}^\alpha}{d_{\tau'-1}^\alpha})+\frac{(\log d)^{4}}{d}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}d_{t+1}^{\alpha}d_{t}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{1}{\log d},

where we have used Eq. (
68
), the usual Chernoff bounds with
B\gtrsim d_{\tau-1}^{\alpha}\log d
, and Eq. (
67
) for the last inequality. Next, for the signal logit,

\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau,ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau-1}\eta_{t}\sum_{k>d_{t},k\neq i}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{i},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\eta_{\tau-1}q_{i}^{(\tau-1)}-O\quantity(\frac{1}{\log d})\gtrsim(\log d)^{2},

where we have again used Eq. (
68
) with
i=j
and
\eta_{\tau-1}q_{i}^{(\tau-1)}\gtrsim d_{\tau}^{\alpha}(\log d)^{2}p_{i}\gtrsim(\log d)^{2}
. Therefore item
i
is recovered as in Eq. (
65
).

We now prove the upper bound. Let
\{\eta_{t}\}_{t\geq 0}\subset\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be any learning rate schedule and suppose
T=o(\sqrt{\log d})
. We will recursively show that the largest item recovered by
{\mathbf{W}}_{t+1}
must satisfy w.h.p.

\displaystyle d_{\tau+1}\lesssim\begin{cases}\min\{d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{\tau}(T\log d)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},(TB)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}&d_{\tau}\lesssim d(\log d)^{-4},\\
\min\{d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{\tau}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(T\log d)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}},(TB)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\}&d_{\tau}\gtrsim d(\log d)^{-4}.\end{cases}

(69)

Case I:
d_{\tau}\lesssim d(\log d)^{-4}
and
B\gtrsim d^{\frac{1}{2}}d_{\tau}^{\alpha}\log d
.

The argument for this regime is a slightly more involved version of Theorem
5
. Consider a fixed item
i\asymp d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{\tau}
so that
i\lesssim B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
and competitors
j
with
d_{\tau}<j\leq 2d_{\tau}
. Then
q_{j}^{(t)}/q_{i}^{(t)}\asymp p_{j}/p_{i}\asymp\sqrt{d}
for all
t\leq\tau
and

\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau+1,ij}-\gamma_{\tau+1,ii}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}\langle u_{i},u_{j}\rangle\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}q_{j}^{(t)}\lVert u_{j}\rVert_{2}^{2}\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\sum_{k>d_{t},k\neq i,j}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{j},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\qquad-\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}\lVert u_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}-\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\sum_{k>d_{t},k\neq i}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{i},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\quantity(q_j^{(t)} \langle u_j-u_i,u_j\rangle\langle v_i,v_j\rangle+ q_i^{(t)} \langle u_i,u_j-u_i\rangle\nnorm{v_i}_2^2 + \sum_{k>d_\tau,k\ne i,j} q_k^{(t)} \langle u_j-u_i,u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle)

\displaystyle\qquad+(u_{j}-u_{i})^{\top}\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\sum_{d_{t}<k\leq d_{\tau}}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle u_{k}}_{=:w}.

(70)

Here, we have separated into terms involving items
k>d_{\tau}
(including the signal and competitor items
i,j
), which can be controlled stepwise, and terms involving items
k\leq d_{\tau}
arising from previous gradients, which we control as a group. Let us first examine the terms in the brackets. We have that

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{q_j^{(t)} \langle-u_i,u_j\rangle\langle v_i,v_j\rangle+ q_i^{(t)} \langle u_i,u_j-u_i\rangle\nnorm{v_i}_2^2}\lesssim\frac{\log d}{d}p_{j}+p_{i}\lesssim d^{-\frac{1}{2}}d_{\tau}^{-\alpha}

and also by Eq. (
68
)

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\sum_{k>d_\tau,k\ne i,j} q_k^{(t)} \langle u_j-u_i,u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle}\lesssim\frac{(\log d)^{2}}{d}d_{\tau}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha},

which is dominated by the previous upper bound under
d_{\tau}\lesssim d(\log d)^{-4}
. Hence we may choose
C=\Theta(1)
so that
\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\geq C/\sqrt{d}
implies for all
t\leq\tau
,

\displaystyle q_{j}^{(t)}\langle u_{j}-u_{i},u_{j}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle+q_{i}^{(t)}\langle u_{i},u_{j}-u_{i}\rangle\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}+\sum_{k>d_{\tau},k\neq i,j}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{j}-u_{i},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle

\displaystyle\gtrsim\frac{Cq_{j}^{(t)}}{\sqrt{d}}-\Theta(d^{-\frac{1}{2}}d_{\tau}^{-\alpha})>0.

Then conditioned on
v_{i}
satisfying
\lVert v_{i}\rVert=\Theta(1)
,
\langle v_{i},v_{j}\rangle\geq C/\sqrt{d}
holds with constant probability independently for each
d_{\tau}<j\leq 2d_{\tau}
, so the set
\mathcal{J}
of such items
j
has size
\Theta(d_{\tau})
w.h.p.

Now conditioning on variables
v_{1},\cdots,v_{N}
and
u_{1},\cdots,u_{d_{\tau}}
(and thus
w
and
\mathcal{J}
), the scalars
u_{i}^{\top}w
and
u_{j}^{\top}w
for
j\in\mathcal{J}
are i.i.d. Gaussian, hence the largest among them is
not

u_{i}^{\top}w
with probability
1-\Theta(d_{\tau}^{-1})
. It follows from Eq. (
70
) that

\displaystyle\max_{j\neq i}\gamma_{\tau+1,ij}-\gamma_{\tau+1,ii}>\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}(u_{j}-u_{i})^{\top}w>0

and thus item
i
cannot be recovered with probability
1-\Theta(d_{\tau}^{-1})
, showing that
d_{\tau+1}\lesssim d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{\tau}
.

Case II:
d_{\tau}\gtrsim d(\log d)^{-4}
and
B\gtrsim dd_{\tau}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\log d
.

Fix an item
i\asymp d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{\tau}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}
with
i\lesssim B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
, noting that
i\gg d
, and a competitor
j\in\mathcal{J}=\{i+1,\cdots,i+\sqrt{d}\}
. Decompose

\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau+1,ij}-\gamma_{\tau+1,ii}

\displaystyle=(u_{j}-u_{i})^{\top}\underbrace{\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\quantity(\sum_{d_t<k\le d_\tau} q_k^{(t)} \langle v_i,v_k\rangle u_k + \sum_{k>d_\tau, k\notin\cJ\cup\{i\}} q_k^{(t)} \langle v_i,v_k\rangle u_k)}_{=:w}

(71)

\displaystyle\qquad+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}\sum_{k\in\mathcal{J}}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle u_{j}-u_{i},u_{k}\rangle\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle+\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}q_{i}^{(t)}\langle u_{j}-u_{i},u_{i}\rangle\lVert v_{i}\rVert_{2}^{2}.

(72)

The competing fluctuations will come from Eq. (
71
). Rewrite

\displaystyle w=\sum_{k\leq d_{\tau}}\sum_{t:d_{t}<k}\eta_{t}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle u_{k}+\sum_{k>d_{\tau},k\notin\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}}\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}q_{k}^{(t)}\langle v_{i},v_{k}\rangle u_{k}.

In particular,
w
is isotropic Gaussian conditioned on all
\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}
, so
\lVert w\rVert_{2}^{2}
concentrates as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert w\rVert_{2}^{2}\mid\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}\right]

\displaystyle=v_{i}^{\top}\quantity(\sum_{k\le d_\tau} \sum_{t: d_t<k} \eta_t^2 (q_k^{(t)})^2 v_kv_k^\top+ \sum_{k>d_\tau, k\notin\cJ\cup\{i\}} \sum_{t=0}^\tau\eta_t^2 (q_k^{(t)})^2 v_kv_k^\top)v_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}^{2}\cdot v_{i}^{\top}\underbrace{\quantity(\sum_{k>d_t, k\notin\cJ\cup\{i\}} (q_k^{(t)})^2 v_k v_k^\top)}_{=:\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{t}}v_{i}.

Denote by
\tilde{q}^{(t)}\in\mathbb{R}^{N-d_{t}-d-1}
the vector consisting of all
(q_{k}^{(t)})^{2}
with
k>d_{t}
,
k\notin\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}
for each
t\leq\tau
. The number of items
k>i+\sqrt{d}
in the minibatch is
O(Bi^{-\alpha})
by the Chernoff bound. We have that

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{q}^{(t)}\rVert_{1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{k>d_{t},k\notin\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}}(q_{k}^{(t)})^{2}=\sum_{k>d_{t},k\notin\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}}p_{k}^{2}\pm O\quantity(Bi^{-\alpha}\qty(\frac{\log d}{B})^2)\asymp d_{t}^{1-2\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{q}^{(t)}\rVert_{2}

\displaystyle\lesssim\sum_{d_{t}<k<i}p_{k}^{4}+\sum_{k>i+\sqrt{d}}(q_{k}^{(t)})^{4}\lesssim d_{t}^{1-4\alpha}+Bi^{-\alpha}\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{4}\lesssim d_{t}^{1-4\alpha}+\frac{d_{\tau}^{2-4\alpha}\log d}{d^{4}}\lesssim d_{t}^{1-4\alpha},

\displaystyle\lVert\tilde{q}^{(t)}\rVert_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{k>d_{t},k\notin\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}}p_{k}^{2}+\quantity(\frac{\log d}{B})^{2}\lesssim d_{t}^{-2\alpha}.

Then
\Tr(\bOmega_t)\asymp\lVert\tilde{q}^{(t)}\rVert_{1}
and by the Hanson-Wright inequality and Lemma
33
, we have w.h.p.

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{v_i^\top\bOmega_t v_i - \frac{\Tr(\bOmega_t)}{d}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{d}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{t}\rVert_{\F}+\frac{\log d}{d}\lVert\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{t}\rVert_{\mathrm{op}}

\displaystyle\lesssim\frac{\sqrt{\log d}}{d}\quantity(\frac{d_t^{1-2\alpha}}{\sqrt{d}} + d_t^{1-4\alpha})+\frac{\log d}{d}\quantity(\frac{d_t^{1-2\alpha}}{d} + d_t^{-2\alpha})=o\quantity(\frac{d_t^{1-2\alpha}}{d}).

Defining
\bar{\eta}_{t}:=\max_{0\leq s\leq t}\eta_{s}
, noting that
p_{i}\asymp d^{-1}d_{\tau}^{\frac{1}{2}-\alpha}
, we thus have

\displaystyle\lVert w\rVert_{2}^{2}\asymp\mathbb{E}\left[\lVert w\rVert_{2}^{2}\mid\{v_{i}\}_{i\in[N]}\right]\asymp\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}^{2}\cdot v_{i}^{\top}\boldsymbol{\Omega}_{t}v_{i}\gtrsim\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}^{2}\cdot\frac{d_{t}^{1-2\alpha}}{d}\gtrsim d\bar{\eta}_{\tau}^{2}p_{i}^{2}.

Also, for Eq. (
72
), noting that items
k\in\mathcal{J}
also satisfy
k\lesssim B^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}(\log d)^{-\frac{1}{\alpha}}
so that
q_{k}^{(t)}\asymp p_{i}
, we may directly bound

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\sum_{t=0}^\tau\eta_t \sum_{k\in\cJ} q_k^{(t)} \langle u_j-u_i, u_k\rangle\langle v_i,v_k\rangle}\lesssim\sum_{t=0}^{\tau}\eta_{t}p_{i}\quantity(\sqrt{\frac{\log d}{d}} + |\cJ|\frac{\log d}{d})\lesssim\frac{\log d}{\sqrt{d}}\tau\bar{\eta}_{\tau}p_{i}

and

\displaystyle\absolutevalue{\sum_{t=0}^\tau\eta_t q_i^{(t)} \langle u_j-u_i,u_i\rangle\nnorm{v_i}_2^2}\lesssim\tau\bar{\eta}_{\tau}p_{i}.

Defining the i.i.d. standard Gaussian variables
Z_{k}:=\sqrt{d}u_{k}^{\top}\frac{w}{\norm{w}_{2}}
for
k\in\mathcal{J}\cup\{i\}
, we have thus shown that

\displaystyle\gamma_{\tau+1,ij}-\gamma_{\tau+1,ii}\geq\langle u_{j}-u_{i},w\rangle-O(\tau\bar{\eta}_{\tau}p_{i})\geq\frac{\norm{w}_{2}}{\sqrt{d}}(Z_{j}-Z_{i}-O(T)).

If item
i
was recovered at step
\tau+1
, it follows that
Z_{i}\geq Z_{j}-O(T)
for all
j\in\mathcal{J}
. On the other hand, by Gaussian superconcentration (Lemma
34
) it holds that
\max_{j\in\mathcal{J}}Z_{j}=\sqrt{2\log 2|\mathcal{J}|}+o(1)
. By Mill’s inequality, supposing
T=o(\sqrt{\log d})
,

\displaystyle\Pr(Z_i \ge\sqrt{2\log 2|\cJ|} - O(T))\lesssim\frac{e^{-\frac{1}{2}\quantity(\sqrt{2\log 2|\cJ|}-O(T))^{2}}}{\sqrt{2\log 2|\mathcal{J}|}}\lesssim\frac{e^{O\quantity(T\sqrt{\log|\cJ|})}}{|\mathcal{J}|\sqrt{\log|\mathcal{J}|}}=\frac{o(\poly(d))}{\sqrt{d}}.

Hence item
i
cannot be recovered with constant probability among competitors
\mathcal{J}
(in fact, superconcentration is not needed to show an
o(1)
bound for each step
\tau
, but we elect to demonstrate the stronger near-uniform bound).

Case III: batch size threshold.

Items
i\asymp(TB)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
have a constant probability of not being sampled in any minibatch,
q_{i}^{(0)}=\cdots=q_{i}^{(T)}=0
so that
{\mathbf{W}}_{\tau+1}
is independent of
u_{i},v_{i}
. Fixing
(\log d)^{2}
such items and comparing to item
1
, it holds that
\gamma_{\tau+1,i1}-\gamma_{\tau+1,ii}=(u_{i}-u_{1})^{\top}{\mathbf{W}}_{\tau+1}v_{i}
has probability
\frac{1}{2}
of being positive independently for each
i
, and so at least one of these items will not be recovered w.h.p.

Therefore, if
d_{\tau}\gtrsim d(\log d)^{-4}
but
B\lesssim dd_{\tau}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\log d
, then
d_{\tau+1}\lesssim(TB)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\lesssim d^{\frac{1}{2\alpha}}d_{\tau}(T\log d)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
; and if
d_{\tau}\gtrsim d(\log d)^{-4}
but
B\lesssim dd_{\tau}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\log d
, then
d_{\tau+1}\lesssim(TB)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}\lesssim d^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}d_{\tau}^{1-\frac{1}{2\alpha}}(T\log d)^{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. Combining with the previous cases concludes Eq. (
69
).
