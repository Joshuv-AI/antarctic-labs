Perturbations of Dirac Operators
Steffen Schmidt

Abstract
. We study perturbations of relative cubic Dirac operators for basic classical Lie superalgebras within the uniform formalism of the colour quantum Weil algebra. This perspective leads to three complementary classes of perturbations and resulting invariants. First, we define semisimple perturbations that assign to each finite-dimensional simple supermodule a finite collection of semisimple orbits, together with canonically defined vector spaces measuring the degree of atypicality. Second, we introduce nilpotent perturbations parametrized by the self-commuting variety of a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra; the resulting family of cohomology theories combines Dirac cohomology and Duflo–Serganova cohomology. Third, we deform the cubic Dirac operator by a Weil-covariant differential built from the universal 1 1 -form in the colour quantum Weil algebra and the Weil differential, producing a Chern-type invariant that assigns to each finite-dimensional module a natural class in the cohomology of the Weil complex.



1.
Introduction

1.1.
Vue d’Ensemble

Symmetry serves as a guiding principle in mathematics and physics. The language of symmetry groups and their actions
organizes a wide range of phenomena. In this setting, Dirac operators form a
natural bridge between quantum mechanics, geometry, and representation theory.

In representation theory, Dirac operators provide conceptual and computational tools
for addressing basic problems concerning a (semisimple) Lie group
G
: they furnish explicit constructions,
give effective criteria for unitarizability, contribute to classification results, and
relate the topology of locally symmetric spaces to representation-theoretic data.
A foundational instance is Parthasarathy’s use of a representation-theoretic Dirac
operator in the study of discrete series representations
[
24
]
.

Interest in this circle of ideas intensified in the late 1990s, following Kostant’s
introduction of the
cubic Dirac operator

[
19
]
and the
subsequent development of
Dirac cohomology
, including Huang–Pandžić’s proof
of Vogan’s conjecture
[
13
]
. More generally, Kostant defined cubic Dirac
operators
D_{{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{u}}
for a pair consisting of a quadratic Lie algebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
and a quadratic Lie subalgebra
\mathfrak{u}
. When
{\mathfrak{g}}
is
semisimple and
\mathfrak{u}
has equal rank, these operators yield, among other
consequences, generalizations of the Bott–Borel–Weil theorem and of the Weyl character
formula. In
[
1
]
, Alekseev and Meinrenken placed this construction
into a uniform framework by characterizing cubic Dirac operators as the unique elements
of the quantum Weil algebra whose induced differential is compatible with contractions
and Lie derivatives.

In parallel with the representation-theoretic developments, (cubic) Dirac operators have
also been studied from a geometric viewpoint. Following Freed–Hopkins–Teleman
[
11
]
, one attaches to a representation a family of Dirac-type operators, and the resulting Dirac family encodes the representation in K-theoretic terms. This construction ties in beautifully with Kirillov’s orbit method: for an irreducible representation the associated K-class localizes on (essentially) a single coadjoint orbit, together with its prequantum line bundle (and corresponding twisting data). Meinrenken reformulated this observation for unitarizable modules over reductive Lie algebras (see
[
20
, Section 8.6]
).

On the analytic side, Quillen introduced the notion of a
superconnection
as a
framework for a local family index theorem for Dirac operators
[
26
]
,
a program that was subsequently realized by Bismut
[
2
]
. In Quillen’s principle, Dirac
operators are a quantization of the theory of connections, and the supertrace of the heat
kernel of the square of a Dirac operator quantizes the Chern character of the corresponding
connection. Superconnections have since proved fruitful in further contexts, including the
definition of Chern characters on non-compact manifolds and for infinite-dimensional vector
bundles.

We use these ideas as a guiding principle throughout the present article, where we consider extensions of the above theory of Dirac operators to Lie superalgebras.

1.2.
Dirac Operators and Lie Superalgebras

Huang and Pandžić introduced Dirac operators and Dirac cohomology for quadratic Lie superalgebras relative to the even subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}

and proved that Dirac cohomology determines the infinitesimal character of a simple module
[
14
]
. Further aspects and the relation to unitarity were studied by Xiao
[
34
]

and by the author
[
29
]
. Kostant-type cubic Dirac operators for quadratic
Lie superalgebras were developed by Kang–Chen
[
18
]
and Meyer
[
21
]
,
and the corresponding Dirac cohomology was treated in
[
23
]
; in particular,
much of the classical theory extends to the super setting.

Despite these developments, the existing results are not yet organized within a uniform
formalism in which cubic Dirac operators appear as canonical objects. The present article uses such a framework by importing the
Alekseev–Meinrenken viewpoint: cubic Dirac operators are realized as distinguished
elements of the colour quantum Weil algebra. We implement this construction for quadratic Lie
superalgebras and use it as a uniform language for the ensuing Dirac-theoretic statements.

We now turn to the precise algebraic setting in which our construction takes place. Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Lie superalgebra equipped with a non-degenerate invariant supersymmetric bilinear form
B
(a
quadratic
Lie superalgebra). Let
{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
be a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra with
B_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
; we call
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
a
quadratic pair
. Then

(1.1)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp},

where
{\mathfrak{p}}
is
\operatorname{ad}({\mathfrak{l}})
-stable and
B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is non-degenerate.

Relative to a quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
, we study the cubic Dirac operator in the uniform framework of the colour quantum Weil algebra, extending the approach of Meinrenken and Alekseev
[
1
]
to Lie superalgebras. This formalism provides a natural construction of relative Dirac operators and yields a streamlined proof that the cubic Dirac operator has a well-behaved square. The colour quantum Weil algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product

(1.2)

{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}),

where
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
carry the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading induced by the parity on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. We equip
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-bigrading determined by
placing the generators
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)
in bidegree
(\bar{0},p(x))
and the generators
1\otimes x
in bidegree
(\bar{1},p(x))
.
The bracket on
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
is defined as the graded commutator with respect to the associated symmetric bicharacter. The algebra
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
is naturally endowed with contractions
\iota_{x}
and Lie derivatives
L_{x}
for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Inside
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
there is a distinguished element
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, the
cubic Dirac operator
. For a homogeneous basis
\{e_{a}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
B
-dual basis
\{e^{a}\}
it is

(1.3)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\coloneqq\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{a,b,c}(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(e_{b})+p(e_{c})}f_{abc}\,e^{a}e^{b}e^{c}.

It induces the unique differential

d^{{\mathcal{W}}}\coloneqq[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}

on
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
compatible with contractions and Lie derivatives. Moreover, its square is central:

(1.4)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\frac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\!\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\bigr)(1\otimes 1),

where
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is the quadratic Casimir. The construction has a relative formulation in terms of a quadratic subalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
, which is viewed as an element of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
under the canonical embedding
j:{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. The
relative cubic Dirac operator
is

(1.5)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}-j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}).

It lies in the subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{l}}
-basic elements
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
and squares to a central element. This places
[
18
,
21
]
into a common framework.

In this article we focus on the case where
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a basic classical Lie superalgebra.
Let
\overline{M}(\mathfrak{p})
be the oscillator supermodule of
{\operatorname{Cl}}(\mathfrak{p})
.
For a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, we consider the induced action of

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
on
M\otimes\overline{M}(\mathfrak{p})
. The associated Dirac cohomology is

(1.6)

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\coloneqq\ker(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})\Big/\Bigl(\ker(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})\cap\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})\Bigr).

It is a fundamental invariant: if
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\neq 0
, then
the central character of any simple
{\mathfrak{l}}
-submodule occurring in

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
determines the central character of
M
.
Applications of Dirac cohomology in the super setting are developed systematically
in
[
23
,
28
]
; for instance,
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)

vanishes unless
M
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, and in that case it
embeds into Kostant’s cohomology, with explicit examples computed in loc. cit.

1.3.
Results

The main results are organized into three parts, corresponding to three families of deformations of the relative cubic Dirac operator associated with a quadratic pair. Throughout,
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
denotes a quadratic pair, and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
the corresponding relative cubic Dirac operator. Unless stated otherwise,
{\mathfrak{g}}
is assumed to be basic classical.

1.3.1.
Semisimple perturbations

The semisimple perturbations extend to Lie superalgebras the localization ideas of Freed–Hopkins–Teleman
[
11
]
, and single out the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents of finite-dimensional supermodules. Comparing the resulting Laplace family with the value at the origin yields a natural energy operator; together with the semisimple perturbations it detects atypicality, and the degree of atypicality at each
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent.

Let
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
be the absolute cubic Dirac operator and let
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
be the real span of the root system. Using
B
to identify
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\cong{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, we obtain a semisimple perturbation family

(1.7)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\longrightarrow{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})},\qquad\xi\longmapsto\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi},\qquad\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}.

If
G_{\bar{0}}
denotes the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, then these families are
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
-invariant and
G_{\bar{0}}
-equivariant (coadjoint action on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
, adjoint action on
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
).

Fix a positive system in
\Delta
and let
M
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. The semisimple perturbations (
1.7
) act on
M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
and lead to a family of Laplace operators whose kernel behaviour is the main object of study. In the super case the form
B
on the real span of roots is typically indefinite, so the resulting Laplace equations do not localize a unique parameter. We therefore pass to the corresponding Laplace families for the even subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, which recover a rigid parameter set and thereby detect the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition of
M
.

Since
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is reductive, it decomposes into simple and abelian factors, and for each factor one has the corresponding family of Dirac and Laplace operators. This permits the construction of a modified family of non-negative operators, denoted
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
, whose kernel is the intersection of the kernels of the factorwise Laplace operators.

The main result is that this modified family detects precisely the
G_{\bar{0}}
-coadjoint orbit attached to a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module. The following theorem is Theorem
54
in the main text.

Theorem 1
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
such that
L(\Lambda)\big|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\bigoplus_{\mu}L_{0}(\mu)^{n(\mu)}
. Then

\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow\xi\in\bigcup_{\mu\,:\,n(\mu)\neq 0}\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}).

If
\xi=-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}
, then the kernel is
L(\Lambda)^{\mu}\otimes S^{\rho_{\bar{0}}}\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})
with
S:=S^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-}}
.

To detect atypicality, we compare the Laplace family with its value at the origin. This defines a canonical
energy
family
T(\eta)
, parametrized by isotropic directions in
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
and thus adapted to the defect of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. For a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
, we then consider the joint family
(T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))
, parametrized by
(\eta,\xi)\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}\times{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, acting on the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-submodule of
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
generated by
L(\Lambda)\otimes S\otimes 1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}
with
S:=S^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-}}
. The even parameter
\xi
localizes at the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents of
L(\Lambda)
, while the isotropic parameter
\eta
detects atypicality at each such constituent; in particular, the joint kernel is non-zero precisely on the corresponding product of isotropic constraints and
G_{\bar{0}}
-orbits. The following theorem is Theorem
63
in the main text.

Theorem 2
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then

\ker((T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))=\{0\}
unless
both

a)

\xi=-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}
, where
\mu
is the highest weight of a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
,

b)

\eta\in X_{\mu+\rho}\coloneqq\{\eta^{\prime}\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}:B(\mu+\rho,\eta^{\prime})=0\}
.

In particular,

\ker(T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow(\eta,\xi)\in\bigcup_{\mu\,:\,n(\mu)\neq 0}X_{\mu+\rho}\times\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}).

1.3.2.
Nilpotent Perturbations

For a basic classical Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
there are two complementary constructions attached to a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule: Dirac cohomology, which controls the infinitesimal character, and Duflo–Serganova cohomology, a symmetric monoidal functor preserving the superdimension. We construct a single family of relative cubic Dirac operators whose cohomology combines these two theories.

Fix a quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
and assume
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{l}}
and
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{1}}\neq\{0\}
. Set

(1.8)

{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{1}}:\ [x,x]=2x^{2}=0\},

the self-commuting variety of
{\mathfrak{l}}
. In contrast with the reductive (purely even) case, the bigrading of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
permits square-zero perturbations of the relative cubic Dirac operator while leaving its square unchanged. Let
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
be the relative cubic Dirac operator. For
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
define

(1.9)

\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}+j\bigl(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)\bigr)\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}).

This element is odd, invariant under the centralizer

C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)\coloneqq\{y\in{\mathfrak{l}}:\ [x,y]=0\},

and it is
L_{\bar{0}}
-equivariant in the sense that

\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})=\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(x)}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}

for any
g\in L_{\bar{0}}
.
Here,
L_{\bar{0}}
denotes the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{0}}
. Moreover, the square is independent of
x
:

(1.10)

(\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}.

For a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
we define the cohomology of
\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
by

(1.11)

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\coloneqq\ker\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\big/\bigl(\ker\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\bigr).

Our aim is to describe
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
in terms of Dirac cohomology (Section
3.5
) and Duflo–Serganova cohomology (Section
5.1
). For the morphisms

(1.12)

\upeta_{x}^{\ast}:\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}_{x}),{\mathbb{C}}\bigr)\longrightarrow\operatorname{Hom}\bigl(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathbb{C}}\bigr),

as well as the homomorphism
\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}:{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}\to\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
, which arise naturally in Duflo–Serganova cohomology and Dirac cohomology, we establish the following result in the unitarizable case (
cf.
Theorem
73
in the main text):

Theorem 3
.

Assume that
M
is a unitarizable highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then for every
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
one has

(1.13)

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)=\operatorname{DS}_{x}\!\bigl({\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\bigr).

In particular,
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
is an
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
-supermodule. If
V
is an
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
-supermodule occurring in
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
with infinitesimal character
\chi_{\nu}^{{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}}
, then

(1.14)

\chi_{\nu}^{{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}}\in(\upeta_{x}^{\ast})^{-1}\bigl(\chi_{\Lambda}\circ\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\bigr).

If
M
is not unitary, having a spectral sequence argument would lead to the following conjecture.

Conjecture 4
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. If
\operatorname{DS}_{x}\bigl({\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\bigr)=0
, then

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)=0.

1.3.3.
Bismut–Quillen Superconnection

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a semisimple complex Lie algebra and let
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
be a quadratic pair. For a finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
, consider the finite-dimensional
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
-supermodule
E\coloneqq M\otimes S
, where
S
is the spin module associated with the pair. A naive Chern–Weil-type expression formed directly from the relative cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
vanishes identically. To obtain a non-trivial invariant, we therefore replace
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
by a Bismut–Quillen-type superconnection
{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)
, obtained by perturbing
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
by a canonical term involving the universal connection
1
-form of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
and the Weil differential. This yields an odd,
{\mathfrak{l}}
-equivariant element of
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)
, and we define

(1.15)

\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)\coloneqq\operatorname{str}_{E}\!\bigl(e^{-{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)^{2}}\bigr)\in\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}).

We prove that
\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)
determines a cohomology class independent of
t
. The following theorem appears in the main text as Theorem
91
.

Theorem 5
.

For any finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
the class

(1.16)

[\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)]\in{\operatorname{H}}\bigl(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\bigr)

is independent of
t
.

We finally introduce the appropriate replacement in the super setting. Since the oscillator module is infinite-dimensional, the supertrace on
E
is not available in general. For unitarizable
M
, however, the “heat operator”
e^{-t\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}}
localizes as
t\to\infty
on
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
, and the argument producing the class
[\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)]
in the finite-dimensional case applies whenever a supertrace is available. This leads us to define a canonical substitute for
\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)
in the super case.

1.4.
Conventions and Notation

The ground field is
{\mathbb{C}}
. Let
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\coloneqq{\mathbb{Z}}/2{\mathbb{Z}}
be the ring of integers modulo
2
, and denote by
\bar{0}
and
\bar{1}
the residue classes of even and odd integers, respectively. For a super vector space
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
, the parity of an element
x\in V
is written
p(x)\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. Expressions of the form
(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}
, for general
v,w\in V
, are understood by restricting first to homogeneous elements, inserting their parities in the exponent, and extending linearly.

For any two super vector spaces
V
and
W
, let
\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)
denote the space of all parity–preserving linear maps. The elements of
\operatorname{Hom}(V,W)
are called morphisms of super vector spaces.

For any two super vector spaces
V
and
W
, define the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
–graded tensor product
V\otimes W
by

(V\otimes W)_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}}),\quad(V\otimes W)_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq(V_{\bar{0}}\otimes W_{\bar{1}})\oplus(V_{\bar{1}}\otimes W_{\bar{0}}).

The assignment
(V,W)\mapsto V\otimes W
is additive in each variable. Moreover,
\otimes
is associative, and the map
V\otimes W\to W\otimes V
,
v\otimes w\mapsto(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}w\otimes v
, is an isomorphism. If
V
and
W
are superalgebras, the product in
V\otimes W
is
(v_{1}\otimes w_{1})(v_{2}\otimes w_{2})=(-1)^{p(w_{1})p(v_{2})}(v_{1}v_{2}\otimes w_{1}w_{2})
for any homogeneous
v_{1},v_{2}\in V
and
w_{1},w_{2}\in W
.

1.5.
Leitfaden

The paper is organized as follows. Section
2
collects the necessary background on basic classical Lie superalgebras, highest weight supermodules, infinitesimal characters, atypicality, and unitarizable supermodules. In Section
3
we develop the cubic Dirac operator in the required generality. After introducing the colour quantum Weil algebra, we define the cubic and relative cubic Dirac operators, discuss the oscillator supermodule, and formulate the corresponding Dirac cohomology, with particular emphasis on the unitary setting. Section
4
studies semisimple perturbations of the cubic Dirac operator and introduces the associated family of Laplace operators, culminating in a detecting family. Section
5
treats nilpotent perturbations parametrized by the self-commuting variety of a quadratic subalgebra and relates Duflo–Serganova cohomology to Dirac cohomology for unitarizable supermodules. Finally, Section
6
places these perturbations in the framework of Bismut–Quillen superconnections, first for semisimple Lie algebras and then for basic classical Lie superalgebras.

2.
Preliminaries

This section fixes conventions and notation used throughout the article. We recall basic classical Lie superalgebras, highest weight supermodules, infinitesimal characters and atypicality, and then formulate unitarity.

2.1.
Basic Classical Lie Superalgebra

Throughout, we work with quadratic Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
i.e.
, Lie superalgebras admitting a non-degenerate invariant supersymmetric consistent bilinear form
B
. Consistency means
B(x,y)=0
whenever
p(x)\neq p(y)
, invariance means
B([x,y],z)=B(x,[y,z])
for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, and supersymmetry means
B(x,y)=(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}B(y,x)
for all homogeneous
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Our main examples are basic classical Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
:
{\mathfrak{g}}
is simple,
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is reductive, and
{\mathfrak{g}}
carries such a form
B
, unique up to a nonzero scalar.

According to Kac
[
15
]
, the basic classical Lie superalgebras comprise the complex simple Lie algebras together with

(2.1)

A(m|n),\ B(m|n),\ C(n),\ D(m|n),\ F(4),\ G(3),\ D(2,1;\alpha).

We assume throughout that
\alpha\in{\mathbb{R}}
for
D(2,1;\alpha)
, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}
is contragredient, unless otherwise stated.

For the simple Lie algebras we take the usual Killing form
B
, while for the basic classical Lie superalgebras
A(m|n)
with
m\neq n
,
B(m|n)
,
C(n+1)
,
D(m|n)
with
m\neq n+1
,
F(4)
, and
G(3)
, we take as
B(\cdot,\cdot)
the
super Killing form

(2.2)

B(x,y)\coloneqq\operatorname{str}(\operatorname{ad}_{x}\circ\operatorname{ad}_{y}),\qquad x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

For the remaining cases, the super Killing form vanishes identically; an alternative invariant consistent form is then chosen as in
[
22
, Section 5.4]
, and will again be referred to simply as the super Killing form.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
be a Cartan subalgebra and let
\Delta\coloneqq\Delta({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}})
be the set of roots. Then

(2.3)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha},\qquad{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha}\coloneqq\{X\in{\mathfrak{g}}:[H,X]=\alpha(H)X\ \text{for all }H\in{\mathfrak{h}}\}.

Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is basic classical, one has
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. Moreover, we fix some positive system
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
and define the
Weyl vector
to be

(2.4)

\rho\coloneqq\rho_{\bar{0}}-\rho_{\bar{1}},\qquad\rho_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{0}}}\alpha,\qquad\rho_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}_{\bar{1}}}\alpha.

Associated with
\Delta^{+}
,
{\mathfrak{g}}
has a
triangular decomposition

(2.5)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+},\qquad{\mathfrak{n}}^{\pm}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\pm\alpha}.

The Weyl group
W
is the Weyl group of the Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. The associated fundamental system
\Uppi
is the set of roots in
\Delta^{+}
not expressible as sums of two positive roots; its elements are called
simple roots
.

2.2.
Highest Weight Supermodules

The main class of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules considered in this article is the class of highest weight supermodules, which we now define. Fix a positive system
\Delta^{+}
. This choice determines a triangular decomposition
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
and the corresponding Borel subsuperalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
. In what follows,
\Delta^{+}
is kept fixed and omitted from the notation.

Definition 6
.

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
is called a
highest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
with respect to a positive system
\Delta^{+}
if there exists a nonzero vector
v_{\Lambda}\in M
with
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
such that the following holds:

a)

Xv_{\Lambda}=0
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
,

b)

Hv_{\Lambda}=\Lambda(H)v_{\Lambda}
for all
H\in{\mathfrak{h}}
, and

c)

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})v_{\Lambda}=M
.

The vector
v_{\Lambda}
is referred to as the
highest weight vector
of
M
, and
\Lambda
is referred to as the
highest weight
.

Every simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule is the unique simple quotient of a universal highest weight supermodule, the
Verma supermodule
. Since
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
is a right
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})
-supermodule by right multiplication, for
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
we set

(2.6)

M_{{\mathfrak{b}}}(\lambda)\coloneqq\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes_{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{b}})}{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda},

where
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
is the one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{b}}
-supermodule on which
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
acts trivially and
{\mathfrak{h}}
acts by
\lambda
.
Since
{\mathfrak{b}}
is fixed, we omit the subscript. Then
M(\lambda)
is a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\lambda
and highest weight vector
[1_{\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})}\otimes 1]
, and
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})[1_{\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})}\otimes 1]=M(\lambda)
. Moreover, if
M
is any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule and
v\in M
has weight
\lambda
with
{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}v=0
, there is a unique
{\mathfrak{g}}
-morphism
M(\lambda)\to M
sending
[1_{\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})}\otimes 1]
to
v
.

The following properties of highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are immediate from their realization as quotients of Verma supermodules.

Lemma 7
.

Let
M
be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
.

a)

M
is a
weight supermodule
, that is,
M
is
{\mathfrak{h}}
-semisimple.

b)

For all weights
\lambda
of
M
, we have
\dim(M^{\lambda})<\infty
, while
\dim(M^{\Lambda})=1
.

c)

Any nonzero quotient of
M
is again a highest weight supermodule.

d)

M
has a unique maximal subsupermodule and a unique simple quotient.

e)

Any two simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules with highest weight
\Lambda
are isomorphic.

In what follows, we denote the unique simple quotient of a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\Lambda
by
L(\Lambda)
.

An important class of highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is formed by the finite-dimensional ones. For our fixed Borel subalgebra

{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{1}}
with
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
, the finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are parametrized by dominant integral weights
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
,
i.e.
,

(2.7)

B(\lambda+\rho_{\bar{0}},\alpha)>0\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}.

Let
P^{++}
denote the set of such weights (with respect to our fixed
{\mathfrak{b}}
). For
\lambda\in P^{++}
, let
L(\lambda)
be the
simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of highest weight
\lambda
with even highest weight vector. Then the full set of finite-dimensional
simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is

(2.8)

\{\,L(\lambda),\ \Pi L(\lambda)\mid\lambda\in P^{++}\,\}.

2.3.
Infinitesimal Characters and Atypicality

Any highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule admits an
infinitesimal character
,
i.e.
, an algebra homomorphism

\chi:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
. We describe these via the Harish–Chandra homomorphism. By PBW,

(2.9)

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\cong{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\oplus\bigl({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})+\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\bigr),

and we let
p:\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})
be the corresponding projection. Its restriction

p|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}:\ \mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{h}})\cong\operatorname{S}

is an algebra homomorphism. Define the twist
\zeta:\operatorname{S}\to\operatorname{S}
by

(2.10)

\lambda(\zeta(f))=(\lambda-\rho)(f)\qquad(f\in\operatorname{S},\ \lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}),

and set
\operatorname{HC}\coloneqq\zeta\circ p|_{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. The map
\operatorname{HC}:\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\to\operatorname{S}
is an injective ring homomorphism. Moreover, its image can be described as follows. Let
\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}\coloneqq\{f\in\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}}):w(\lambda)(f)=\lambda(f)\,\text{for all }w\in W,\,\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}\}
and, for any
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, define

A_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Delta_{1}^{+}:(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0\}.

Then, the image of
HC
is given by (
[
17
,
32
,
33
]
):

\operatorname{im}(\text{HC})=\bigl\{f\in\operatorname{S}({\mathfrak{h}})^{W}:(\lambda+t\alpha)(f)=\lambda(f)\ \text{for all }t\in{\mathbb{C}},\,\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast},\,\alpha\in A_{\lambda-\rho}\bigr\}.

For
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
define
\chi_{\lambda}(z)\coloneqq(\lambda+\rho)\bigl(\operatorname{HC}(z)\bigr)
for
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Definition 8
.

A
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M

has infinitesimal character
if
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
acts on
M
via
\chi_{\lambda}
for some

\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. In this case,
\chi_{\lambda}
is the infinitesimal character of
M
.

Examples include highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
M
with highest weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. They have infinitesimal character
\chi_{\Lambda}
.

As a direct consequence of the description of
\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{HC})
, for any
\lambda,\lambda^{\prime}\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
one has

\chi_{\lambda}=\chi_{\lambda^{\prime}}
if and only if

\lambda^{\prime}=w\biggl(\lambda+\rho+\sum_{i=1}^{k}t_{i}\alpha_{i}\biggr)-\rho,

where
w\in W
,
t_{i}\in{\mathbb{C}}
, and
\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}\in A_{\lambda}\coloneqq\{\alpha\in\Delta_{1}^{+}:(\lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0,\ B(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}

are linearly independent odd isotropic roots. This leads to the definition of typicality and atypicality.

Definition 9
.

A weight
\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
is called
typical
if
A_{\Lambda}=\emptyset
, that is,

(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)\neq 0
for
all
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. Otherwise,
\Lambda
is called
atypical
. The

degree of atypicality
of
\Lambda
, denoted by
{\operatorname{at}}(\Lambda)
, is the maximal number of
linearly independent mutually orthogonal positive odd isotropic roots

\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}

such that
(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
. In brief,
{\operatorname{at}}(\Lambda)
is the dimension of a maximal
isotropic subspace of
\operatorname{span}_{\mathbb{C}}(A_{\Lambda})\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. We call a highest weight

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
with highest weight
\Lambda

typical
if
{\operatorname{at}}(\Lambda)=0
, and
otherwise
atypical
.

Remark 10
.

The degree of atypicality is independent of the choice of positive root system.

The
defect
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, denoted
\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})
, is the dimension of a maximal isotropic subspace in the
{\mathbb{R}}
-span of
\Delta
.
For
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
A(m-1|n-1)
,
B(m|n)
, or
D(m|n)
(so that
\Delta_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong{\mathbb{R}}^{m,n}
), one has

\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})=\min(m,n)
. For
{\mathfrak{g}}
of type
C(n)
,
\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})=1
. A simple Lie algebra and
{\mathfrak{osp}}(1|2n)
have defect
0
.
In all cases,

(2.11)

0\leq{\operatorname{at}}(\lambda)\leq\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})

for any
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
.

2.4.
Unitarizable Supermodules

In this subsection, we define unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, which are formulated relative to real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Our notation follows

[
3
,
5
,
8
]
.

Let
V=V_{\bar{0}}\oplus V_{\bar{1}}
be a complex super vector space. A
super Hermitian form
on
V
is a sesquilinear map

{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}:V\times V\to{\mathbb{C}}
, linear in the first variable and conjugate-linear in the second, satisfying

(2.12)

{\langle}v,w{\rangle}=(-1)^{p(v)p(w)}\,\overline{{\langle}w,v{\rangle}}

for all homogeneous
v,w\in V
, and we assume it is consistent,
i.e.
,
{\langle}v,w{\rangle}=0
whenever
p(v)\neq p(w)
.

A super Hermitian form
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
decomposes as
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}={\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{0}}+i{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{1}}
, where

(2.13)

{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{s}}\coloneqq(-1)^{s}\,{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}|_{V_{\bar{s}}\times V_{\bar{s}}},\qquad\bar{s}\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}.

Then
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{0}}
and
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{1}}
are ordinary Hermitian forms on
V_{\bar{0}}
and
V_{\bar{1}}
, respectively. We call
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}

non-degenerate
(resp.
positive definite
) if both
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{0}}
and
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{1}}
are non-degenerate (resp. positive definite).
We say that
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
is
super positive definite
if
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{0}}
is positive definite and
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{\bar{1}}
is negative definite; in this case,

{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
is a
Hermitian product
on
V
.

For
T\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V)
, we define the
adjoint

T^{\dagger}
by

(2.14)

{\langle}Tv,w{\rangle}=(-1)^{p(v)p(T)}\,{\langle}v,T^{\dagger}w{\rangle}

for all homogeneous
v,w\in V
, and extend by linearity.

Unitarity for
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules is defined relative to real forms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which we now set up. For
r\in\{2,4\}
, let

{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,r}^{{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
be the set of automorphisms
\theta
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
viewed as a real super vector space such that

\theta|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}}
and

(2.15)

\theta^{2}\big|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}}=\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}},\qquad\theta^{2}\big|_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}}=\begin{cases}\ \operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}},&r=2,\\
-\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{1}}},&r=4.\end{cases}

We set

(2.16)

\displaystyle{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\theta\in{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}^{{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathfrak{g}}):\theta\ \text{is}\ {\mathbb{C}}\text{-linear}\},

\displaystyle\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,r}({\mathfrak{g}})

\displaystyle\coloneqq\{\theta\in{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,r}^{{\mathbb{R}}}({\mathfrak{g}}):\theta\ \text{is}\ \text{conjugate-linear}\}.

A
real structure
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
is a conjugate-linear Lie superalgebra morphism
\phi:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\phi\in\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,2}({\mathfrak{g}})
,
i.e.
,
\phi
is a conjugate-linear involution. The fixed-point subalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\phi}
is a
real form
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Real structures in
\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,2}({\mathfrak{g}})
are related to elements of
{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})
as follows.

Proposition 11

(
[
8
]
)
.

There exists a unique
\omega\in\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})
, up to inner automorphisms of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, together with a positive system

\Delta^{+}
and root vectors
e_{\pm\alpha}
for
\alpha\in\Delta^{+}
such that

\omega(e_{\pm\alpha})=-e_{\mp\alpha}\ \text{for all even simple }\alpha,\qquad\omega(e_{\pm\alpha})=\pm e_{\mp\alpha}\ \text{for all odd simple }\alpha.

Moreover:

a)

\omega
induces a bijection

\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,2}({\mathfrak{g}})\setminus\{\theta:\theta|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\omega|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\}\ \longrightarrow\ {\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\theta\longmapsto\omega^{-1}\circ\theta.

b)

For the Killing form
B(\cdot,\cdot)
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, one has
\overline{B(X,Y)}=B(\omega(X),\omega(Y))
for all
X,Y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,
and
B(\cdot,\omega(\cdot))
is positive definite.

Remark 12
.

The positive system
\Delta^{+}
is the
distinguished positive system
, meaning that there is exactly one odd positive root that cannot be expressed as the sum of two other positive roots.

As a consequence, there is a one-to-one correspondence, up to equivalence
[
30
,
25
,
8
,
6
]

(2.17)

\{\text{real forms }{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}\text{ of }{\mathfrak{g}}\}\ \longleftrightarrow\ \{\theta\in{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})\}.

Here a real form is realized as the fixed-point subspace of a suitable involution, and two real forms are identified whenever they are isomorphic (equivalently, whenever the corresponding involutions are conjugate by an automorphism of
{\mathfrak{g}}
).

Fix a real form
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
of a basic classical Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
,
i.e.
,
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}={\mathfrak{g}}^{\theta}
for some

\theta\in{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Set
\sigma\coloneqq\omega\circ\theta\in\overline{{\operatorname{aut}}}_{2,2}({\mathfrak{g}})
, the corresponding conjugate-linear
involution (
cf.
Proposition
11
). We call
\theta
a
Cartan automorphism
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
(or of

{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
) if

(2.18)

B_{\theta}(\cdot,\cdot)\coloneqq-B(\cdot,\theta(\cdot))

is an inner product on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
. For each
\theta\in{\operatorname{aut}}_{2,4}({\mathfrak{g}})
there is a unique real form
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
for which

\theta
restricts to a Cartan automorphism, and conversely each real form admits a unique Cartan automorphism
[
6
, Theorem 1.1]
.
Hence, we may assume throughout that the chosen
\theta
is Cartan.

With the notion of a real form established, we can now define unitarizable supermodules.

Definition 13
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and let
{\mathcal{H}}
be a complex
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-supermodule. The supermodule
{\mathcal{H}}
is called a
unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
(or
unitarizable

{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-supermodule
) if there exists a Hermitian product
{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}
on
{\mathcal{H}}
such that
X^{\dagger}=-X
for all
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
. Explicitly, this means:

\langle Xv,w{\rangle}=-(-1)^{p(v)p(X)}{\langle}v,Xw{\rangle}

for all
v,w\in{\mathcal{H}}
and
X\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
.

The following standard result is central.

Proposition 14
.

Unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are completely reducible, that is, the orthogonal complement of any
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-submodule is again a
{\mathfrak{g}}^{{\mathbb{R}}}
-submodule.

3.
The Cubic Dirac Operator

We introduce the cubic Dirac operator, its relative version, and Dirac cohomology for quadratic Lie superalgebras in the language of the colour quantum Weil algebra.

Throughout this section, let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a quadratic Lie superalgebra with non-degenerate invariant supersymmetric bilinear form
B
. Via
B
, we identify
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
by the musical isomorphisms

(3.1)

\flat:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*},\quad x\mapsto x^{\flat},\quad x^{\flat}(y)\coloneqq B(x,y),\qquad\sharp:{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}\to{\mathfrak{g}},\quad\alpha\mapsto\alpha^{\sharp},\quad B(\alpha^{\sharp},y)=\alpha(y)

for all
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. This identification will be used without further comment.

3.1.
Colour Quantum Weil Algebra

The cubic Dirac operators and their perturbations take values in the colour quantum Weil algebra, which we now introduce.

3.1.1.
Exterior and Clifford Algebra over
{\mathfrak{g}}

We briefly recall the definition and properties of the
exterior
and
Clifford algebra
over
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
T({\mathfrak{g}})
be the tensor algebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with unit
1_{T({\mathfrak{g}})}
. It carries the canonical
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading

(3.2)

T({\mathfrak{g}})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}T^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad T^{0}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathbb{C}},\quad T^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})=\mathrm{span}\{x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k}:x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}\}.

Reducing the degree mod
2
yields a superalgebra structure with
T({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{0}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}}T^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
T({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{1}}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{k\in 2{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}+1}T^{k}({\mathfrak{g}}).
Thus
T({\mathfrak{g}})
carries two
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-gradings, hence two superalgebra structures: one from tensor degree modulo
2
, and one from the intrinsic parity of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. In this article we use the latter, that is, we regard
T({\mathfrak{g}})
as the superalgebra induced by the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-parity.

Exterior Algebra over
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Let
I_{\wedge}\subset T({\mathfrak{g}})
be the homogeneous ideal generated by

(3.3)

x\otimes y+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}\,y\otimes x,\qquad x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

The quotient
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq T({\mathfrak{g}})/I_{\wedge}
is the
exterior algebra
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, with unit
1_{\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})}
and product
\wedge
(exterior multiplication). Equivalently, it is generated by
{\mathfrak{g}}
subject to

(3.4)

x\wedge y+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}\,y\wedge x=0,\qquad x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

The
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading of
T({\mathfrak{g}})
descends to
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
, so that
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})=\bigoplus_{k\geq 0}\bigwedge^{\!k}({\mathfrak{g}})
, where

\bigwedge^{\!k}({\mathfrak{g}})
is spanned by exterior products of
k
elements of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Reducing the degree mod
2
gives the natural superalgebra grading

\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})=\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{0}}\oplus\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{1}}
. We also have the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading induced by the intrinsic parity of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we fix this grading from now on.

On
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
we use the standard operators
\epsilon
,
\iota
, and
L
.
For
v\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, let
\epsilon(v)
denote
left exterior multiplication
by
v
.
Define a derivation
\iota_{v}
on
T({\mathfrak{g}})
by

(3.5)

\iota_{v}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{l})\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{k-1}(-1)^{p(v)(p(x_{1})+\cdots+p(x_{k-1}))}\,B(v,x_{k})\,x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes\widehat{x}_{k}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{l}.

Then
\iota_{v}(I_{\wedge})\subset I_{\wedge}
(
cf.

[
4
, Proposition 4.5]
), hence
\iota_{v}
descends to

\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
; it is called
contraction
, has degree
-1
with respect to the
{\mathbb{Z}}
-grading, and parity
p(v)
.
Finally, for homogeneous
T\in\operatorname{End}({\mathfrak{g}})
define a degree-
0
derivation
L_{T}
on
T({\mathfrak{g}})
by

(3.6)

L_{T}(x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{l})\coloneqq\sum_{k=1}^{l}(-1)^{p(T)(p(x_{1})+\cdots+p(x_{k-1}))}\,x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes T(x_{k})\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{l}.

It preserves
I_{\wedge}
and therefore induces a degree-
0
derivation on
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
, the
Lie derivative
.

Moreover,
B
induces a non-degenerate bilinear form on
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
. For
k\geq 0
and
x_{i},y_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, define on
T^{k}({\mathfrak{g}})

(3.7)

\langle x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k},\,y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{k}\rangle_{T^{k}}\coloneqq\prod_{i=0}^{k-1}B(x_{k-i},\,y_{1+i}),

and set

\langle x_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{k},\,y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{k}\rangle_{\wedge^{k}}\coloneqq\sum_{\sigma\in S_{k}}p(\sigma;x_{1},\ldots,x_{k})\,\langle x_{\sigma(1)}\otimes\cdots\otimes x_{\sigma(k)},\,y_{1}\otimes\cdots\otimes y_{k}\rangle_{T^{k}},

Then

\bigwedge^{\!k}({\mathfrak{g}})

\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{\wedge}

Lemma 15

(
[
4

The form

We focus on

Lemma 16
.

For
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}

\operatorname{ad}_{x}\in{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Proof.

For
y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}

B(\operatorname{ad}_{x}y,z)=B([x,y],z)=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}B(y,[x,z])=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}B(y,\operatorname{ad}_{x}z).\qed

With respect to
\operatorname{ad}:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{g}})

\operatorname{str}\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{x}\circ\mu(y,z)\bigr)=B([x,y],z)\qquad x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}},

where

{\mathfrak{g}}

\mu(x,y)(z)=-\iota_{z}(x\wedge y)=-B(z,x)y+(-1)^{p(z)p(x)}B(z,y)x\qquad x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

For

Proposition 17

(

)

\mu:\bigwedge^{2}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{g}})

\{e_{a}\}

\lambda(T)\coloneqq\mu^{-1}(T)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a}T(e^{a})\wedge e_{a}.

Via the identification

Lemma 18
.

Under
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}

\lambda(T)=\omega_{T}
for all
T\in{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{g}})

Proof.

Let
\flat:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}
be given by

and extend to

\bigwedge^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}\to\bigwedge^{2}{\mathfrak{g}}^{*}

(u\wedge v)^{\flat}=u^{\flat}\wedge v^{\flat}

For homogeneous
y,z
we compute, using the convention of Section
1.4

\begin{split}(2\lambda(T))^{\flat}(y\wedge z)&=-\sum_{a}(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(y)}\Bigl(B(Te^{a},y)\,B(e_{a},z)-(-1)^{p(y)p(z)+p(T)p(y)}B(Te^{a},z)\,B(e_{a},y)\Bigr)\\
&=-(-1)^{p(y)p(z)}\sum_{a}\Bigl(B(Te^{a},y)\,B(e_{a},z)-(-1)^{p(y)p(z)}B(Te^{a},z)\,B(e_{a},y)\Bigr).\end{split}

Using

(-1)^{p(y)p(z)}(2\lambda(T))^{\flat}(y\wedge z)=-\,B(Tz,y)+(-1)^{p(y)p(z)}B(Ty,z).

Since

Hence

For any

Clifford Superalgebra.

3.1.2.
Colour Quantum Weil Algebra

With the notation fixed, we introduce the colour quantum Weil algebra. We regard
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
as
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded algebras, with grading induced by that of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Definition 22
.

The
colour quantum Weil algebra
associated with
{\mathfrak{g}}
is the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product

\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}).

We take as generators of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
the elements
1\otimes x
and
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)\coloneqq x\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(x))
, where
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Recall that
\gamma^{\prime}(x)\coloneqq\gamma^{\prime}(\operatorname{ad}_{x})=q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))
for any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. This choice is natural, since the map
x\mapsto x\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(x))
defines an embedding of
{\mathfrak{g}}
into
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. We endow
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading by setting, for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

(3.17)

{\operatorname{bideg}}(1\otimes x)=(\bar{1},p(x)),\qquad{\operatorname{bideg}}(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))=(\bar{0},p(x)).

Remark 23
.

Another common choice of generators is given by the tensor product generators

u^{x}\coloneqq x\otimes 1,\qquad\theta^{x}\coloneqq 1\otimes x,\qquad x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

They are used only in Section
6
. They are of bidegree
{\operatorname{bideg}}(u^{x})=(\bar{0},p(x))
and
{\operatorname{bideg}}(\theta^{x})=(\bar{1},p(x))
.

For
\alpha=(\bar{a},\bar{b})
and
\beta=(\bar{c},\bar{d})
in

{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
we use the standard symmetric bicharacter

(3.18)

\chi(\alpha,\beta)\coloneqq(-1)^{\bar{a}\bar{c}+\bar{b}\bar{d}}.

For homogeneous
A,B\in{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
of bidegrees
\alpha
and
\beta
, we set

(3.19)

[A,B]_{{\mathcal{W}}}\coloneqq AB-\chi(\alpha,\beta)\,BA,

and extend
{\mathbb{C}}
-linearly. The total degree is the sum of the two components of the bidegree in
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
. In particular, if
A\in{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
has total degree

\bar{1}
, then
A^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}[A,A]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
. This endows
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with the structure of a colour Lie algebra (
cf.

[
27
]
), which we call the
colour quantum Weil algebra
.

Lemma 24
.

For
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
one has

\begin{gathered}{}_{{\mathcal{W}}}=2B(x,y)(1\otimes 1),\qquad[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}),\\
[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\,1\otimes y]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=1\otimes[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}.\end{gathered}

Proof.

Since
{\operatorname{bideg}}(1\otimes x)=(\bar{1},p(x))
and
{\operatorname{bideg}}(1\otimes y)=(\bar{1},p(y))
, we have

\chi({\operatorname{bideg}}(1\otimes x),{\operatorname{bideg}}(1\otimes y))=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}
. Hence

\begin{split}[1\otimes x,1\otimes y]_{{\mathcal{W}}}&=(1\otimes x)(1\otimes y)+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}(1\otimes y)(1\otimes x)\\
&=1\otimes\bigl(xy+(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}yx\bigr)=2B(x,y)\,(1\otimes 1),\end{split}

by the Clifford relation. Next,

\begin{split}[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),1\otimes y]_{{\mathcal{W}}}&=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)(1\otimes y)-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}(1\otimes y)\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)\\
&=-\tfrac{1}{2}\Bigl((1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(x))(1\otimes y)-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}(1\otimes y)(1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(x))\Bigr)\\
&=-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1\otimes(\gamma^{\prime}(x)y-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}y\gamma^{\prime}(x))\bigr)\\
&=1\otimes(-\tfrac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\prime}(x),y)]_{{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})})\\
&=1\otimes L_{x}(y)=1\otimes[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\end{split}

using Lemma
21
in the last steps. The identity

[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
is proved similarly.
∎

On
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
there exist two canonical operations. The first is the contraction
\iota_{x}
, an odd derivation characterized by the relations

(3.20)

\iota_{x}(1\otimes y)=B(x,y)(1\otimes 1),\qquad\iota_{x}(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y))=-\frac{1}{2}(1\otimes[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}),

for
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. The second is the Lie derivative, which is a combination of the Lie derivative
L^{U}
on
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
L^{C}
on
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
:

(3.21)

L_{x}(y\otimes z)\coloneqq L_{x}^{U}(y)\otimes z+y\otimes L_{x}^{C}(z)

With these definitions, the following lemma is immediate by comparison with Lemma
24
.

Lemma 25
.

For
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
one has:

a)

\iota_{x}=\tfrac{1}{2}[1\otimes x,\,\cdot\,]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
.

b)

L_{x}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\,\cdot\,]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
.

Proof.

a) This is immediate from the definition of
\iota_{x}
and the commutator (
3.19
).
b) It suffices to check the identity on the generators of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. For
y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
,

\begin{split}L_{x}(1\otimes y)&=1\otimes L^{C}_{x}y=1\otimes\Bigl(-\tfrac{1}{2}[\gamma^{\prime}(x),y]_{{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})}\Bigr)=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),1\otimes y]_{{\mathcal{W}}},\end{split}

using
[x\otimes 1,1\otimes y]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0
and Lemma
24
. Moreover,

\begin{split}L_{x}(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y))&=L_{x}\Bigl(y\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(y))\Bigr)\\
&=[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1\otimes L_{x}\gamma^{\prime}(y)\bigr)=[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\tfrac{1}{4}\bigl(1\otimes[\gamma^{\prime}(x),\gamma^{\prime}(y)]_{{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})}\bigr)\\
&=[x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\bigr)=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}},\end{split}

where we used Lemma
21
in the penultimate step and
Lemma
24
in the last step. Since the generators span
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
as an algebra, this
implies
L_{x}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
.
∎

3.2.
Cubic Dirac Operator

We construct the cubic Dirac operator for quadratic Lie superalgebras, including, in particular, all basic classical Lie superalgebras. Recall that any
quadratic Lie superalgebra

{\mathfrak{g}}
is equipped with a non-degenerate invariant supersymmetric bilinear form
B
. In what follows, we fix such a pair
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
. Then the cubic Dirac operator is a distinguished element of the colour quantum Weil algebra
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
, obtained by adding to the canonical quadratic element the Clifford quantization of the structure-constants tensor associated with
({\mathfrak{g}},B)
. The resulting element is invariant under the diagonal adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and encodes the full Lie superalgebraic structure of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

3.2.1.
Structure Constants Tensor

Let
\{e_{a}\}
be a homogeneous basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
B
-dual basis
\{e^{a}\}
, that is,
B(e_{a},e^{b})=\delta_{ab}
. The bilinear form
B
identifies
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
and defines the Cartan
3
-form

(3.22)

\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(x,y,z)\coloneqq-\tfrac{1}{2}B\bigl(x,[y,z]\bigr),\qquad x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

By invariance and supersymmetry of
B
, the form
\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant and totally skew-supersymmetric. Via the identification
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
(
cf.

20
),
\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
determines a canonical cubic element
\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in\bigwedge^{3}({\mathfrak{g}})_{\bar{0}}
characterized by

(3.23)

(\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}},x\wedge y\wedge z)_{\wedge}=-\tfrac{1}{2}B([x,y],z)

for all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Here
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\wedge}
denotes the non-degenerate supersymmetric bilinear form on
\bigwedge({\mathfrak{g}})
introduced in Section
3.1.1
. The element
\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is called the
structure constants tensor
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
{\mathfrak{g}}
is clear from the context, we omit the subscript
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Lemma 26
.

For all
x,y,z\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, the following hold:

a)

2\iota_{x}\phi=-\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x})
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

b)

\iota_{x}\iota_{y}\phi=B([x,y],\cdot)
. In particular, under the identification
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
,
\iota_{x}\iota_{y}\phi=[x,y]
.

c)

In the homogeneous basis,

\phi=-\frac{1}{12}\sum_{a,b,c}(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(e_{b})+p(e_{c})}f_{abc}e^{a}\wedge e^{b}\wedge e^{c}.

d)

\phi
is invariant under the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

a)
For homogeneous
x,y,z
, we obtain using Lemma
18

(\iota_{x}\phi)(y\wedge z)=\phi(x,y,z)=-\tfrac{1}{2}B([x,y],z)=-\tfrac{1}{2}\omega_{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}(y,z)=-\tfrac{1}{2}(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))^{\flat}(y\wedge z).

Hence
2(\iota_{x}\phi)^{\flat}=-(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))^{\flat}.

Using the
B
-identification
\bigwedge^{2}({\mathfrak{g}})\cong\bigwedge^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast})
, we finally obtain the statement.

b) One has by definition:

\displaystyle\iota_{x}\iota_{y}\phi(z)

\displaystyle=(\iota_{x}\iota_{y}\phi,z)_{\wedge}=(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}(\iota_{y}\phi,x\wedge z)_{\wedge}=(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}(\phi,y\wedge x\wedge z)_{\wedge}

\displaystyle=-(-1)^{p(x)p(y)}B([y,x],z)=B([x,y],z).

c) is a straightforward computation and will be omitted.

d) One has
\phi(x,y,z)=-\tfrac{1}{2}B([x,y],z)
. For homogeneous
w,x,y,z
,
the Lie derivative on forms gives

(L_{w}\phi)(x,y,z)=\phi([w,x],y,z)+(-1)^{p(w)p(x)}\phi(x,[w,y],z)+(-1)^{p(w)(p(x)+p(y))}\phi(x,y,[w,z]).

Hence, using invariance of
B
, one has for any
w\in{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle-2(L_{w}\phi)(x,y,z)

\displaystyle=B([[w,x],y],z)+(-1)^{p(w)p(x)}B([x,[w,y]],z)+(-1)^{p(w)(p(x)+p(y))}B([x,y],[w,z])

\displaystyle=B([w,x],[y,z])+(-1)^{p(w)p(x)}B(x,[[w,y],z])+(-1)^{p(w)(p(x)+p(y))}B(x,[y,[w,z]])

\displaystyle=(-1)^{p(w)p(x)}\Big(-B(x,[w,[y,z]])+B(x,[[w,y],z])+(-1)^{p(w)p(y)}B(x,[y,[w,z]])\Big).

By the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded Jacobi identity

[w,[y,z]]=[[w,y],z]+(-1)^{p(w)p(y)}[y,[w,z]]
,
the parentheses vanish. Therefore
L_{w}\phi=0
for all
w
,
i.e.
,
\phi
is invariant under the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.
∎

We are interested in the quantization of
\phi
, that is,

(3.24)

\phi^{\prime}\coloneqq q(\phi)\in{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}).

Theorem 27

(
[
18
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

The square of
\phi^{\prime}
is a constant given by

(\phi^{\prime})^{2}=\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})).

Whenever
{\mathfrak{g}}
is basic classical, it is useful to rewrite the proposition using the Freudenthal–de Vries formula (see
[
21
]
). Fix a positive root system of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and denote by
\rho
the associated Weyl vector, that is the half sum of the positive roots. Then, the Freudenthal–de-Vries formula is

(3.25)

\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}))=B(\rho,\rho),

and the square of
\phi^{\prime}
equals
B(\rho,\rho)
.

3.2.2.
Cubic Dirac Operator

Let
\{e_{a}\}
be a homogeneous basis of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
B
-dual basis
\{e^{a}\}
. Note that
p(e_{a})=p(e^{a})
for all
a
. The
cubic Dirac operator

{\mathfrak{g}}

(3.26)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\coloneqq\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}+1\otimes\phi^{\prime}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}).

Lemma 28
.

The cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
satisfies the following properties:

a)

The definition of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is independent of the choice of a basis.

b)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant, that is,
L_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=0
.

c)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
has bidegree
(\bar{1},\bar{0})
,
i.e.
, it is an odd element of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
relative to the total degree.

Proof.

a) We fix two bases
\{e_{a}\}
and
\{f_{a}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
B
-dual bases
\{e^{a}\}
and
\{f^{a}\}
, respectively. We can express any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
by
x=\sum_{a}B(x,f^{a})f_{a}=\sum_{a}B(f_{a},x)f^{a}
for any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
such that

\displaystyle\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b,c}B(f_{b},e^{a})B(e_{a},f^{c})\,f^{b}\otimes f_{c}=\sum_{b,c}B\!\left(\sum_{a}B(f_{b},e^{a})e_{a},\,f^{c}\right)f^{b}\otimes f_{c}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b,c}B(f_{b},f^{c})\,f^{b}\otimes f_{c}=\sum_{b}f^{b}\otimes f_{b}.

In particular, the definition
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is independent of the choice of a basis.

b) One has for any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}

\displaystyle L_{x}\left(\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}\right)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}[x,e^{a}]\otimes e_{a}+\sum_{a}(-1)^{p(x)p(e_{a})}e^{a}\otimes[x,e_{a}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}B(e_{b},[x,e^{a}])e^{b}\otimes e_{a}+\sum_{a,b}(-1)^{p(x)p(e_{a})}B([x,e_{a}],e^{b})e^{a}\otimes e_{b}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}B(e_{b},[x,e^{a}])e^{b}\otimes e_{a}-\sum_{a,b}B(e_{a},[x,e^{b}])e^{a}\otimes e_{b}

\displaystyle=0,

where we used invariance and supersymmetry of
B
. Moreover,
\phi^{\prime}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant by Lemma
26
and Theorem
20
. This completes the proof of b).

c) By Lemma
26
and the definition of
q
(
cf.
Theorem
20
), it is immediate that
\phi
has bidegree
(\bar{1},\bar{0})
and total degree
\bar{1}
. Moreover, parity is additive on simple tensors, e.g.

p(e^{a}\otimes e_{a})=p(e^{a}\otimes 1)+p(1\otimes e_{a})
, so
\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}
has bidegree
(\bar{1},\bar{0})
and hence total degree
\bar{1}
. Therefore
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, being a sum of homogeneous elements of total degree
\bar{1}
, is itself odd.
∎

The cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
has a particularly simple square. This theorem also appears in
[
21
]
. We give an alternative proof using the quantum Weil algebra.

Theorem 29
.

The square of the cubic Dirac operator satisfies

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\tfrac{1}{24}\,\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\!\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\bigr)(1\otimes 1)

where
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
denotes the quadratic Casimir element of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

We first show that
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}\in{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes 1
. It suffices to check
\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=0
for all

x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Indeed,

\begin{split}\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}&=\tfrac{1}{2}[\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=-\tfrac{1}{4}[\gamma^{\prime}(x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}L_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=0,\end{split}

since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant.

Next we identify
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}
inside
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Let
\pi:{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
be the projection

\operatorname{id}_{{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})}\otimes\epsilon
, where
\epsilon:{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})={\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1\oplus{\operatorname{Cl}}^{>0}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}\cdot 1
. For brevity write

s(x)\coloneqq x\otimes 1
. By Proposition
27
,

\begin{split}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}\bmod\ker\pi&=\sum_{a,b}(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(e_{b})}s(e^{a})s(e^{b})\,e_{a}e_{b}+(\phi^{\prime})^{2}\bmod\ker\pi\\
&=\sum_{a,b}(-1)^{p(e_{b})}s(e^{a})s(e^{b})\,B(e_{a},e_{b})+\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\!\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\bigr)\bmod\ker\pi\\
&=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\!\bigl(\operatorname{ad}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\bigr)\bmod\ker\pi,\end{split}

where we used the graded product in
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and the Clifford identity

e_{a}e_{b}=\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(e_{a}e_{b}+(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(e_{b})}e_{b}e_{a}\bigr)+\tfrac{1}{2}[e_{a},e_{b}]=B(e_{a},e_{b})+\tfrac{1}{2}[e_{a},e_{b}]\quad\text{in }{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}),

together with
\sum_{a}B(e_{a},e^{b})\,e_{a}=e_{b}
. This completes the proof.
∎

Remark 30
.

Using the Freudenthal–de Vries formula (
3.25
), for a basic classical Lie superalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
with Weyl vector
\rho
one can rewrite the square as

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+B(\rho,\rho).

The following lemma will be used in subsequent constructions.

Lemma 31
.

In
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
, the following commutation relations hold:

\displaystyle{}_{{\mathcal{W}}}

\displaystyle=2(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+B(\rho,\rho)(1\otimes 1)),

\displaystyle[\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}

\displaystyle=0

for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. In addition, if
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
is even, one has

[1\otimes x,\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=2\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(x).

Proof.

The cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
has bidegree
(\bar{1},\bar{0})
, that is, total degree
\bar{1}
. Hence

[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=2\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=2\bigl(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+B(\rho,\rho)(1\otimes 1)\bigr).

The commutation relation
[\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
follows from the identity

L_{x}=[\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(x),\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}

in
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
(see Lemma
25
). Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant, the claim follows. Next, for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
the element
1\otimes x
is odd with respect to the total degree. Since both
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
1\otimes x

are odd, we obtain

\begin{split}[1\otimes x,\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}&=2\,\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=2\bigl(\iota_{x}(\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a})+1\otimes q(\iota_{x}\phi)\bigr)\\
&=2\Bigl(x\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1\otimes q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))\bigr)\Bigr)=2\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\end{split}

where we used

\iota_{x}\Bigl(\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes e_{a}\Bigr)=\sum_{a}(-1)^{p(e_{a})p(x)}\,e^{a}\otimes B(x,e_{a})=\sum_{a}(-1)^{p(e_{a})}\,B(x,e_{a})\,e^{a}\otimes 1=x\otimes 1,

since
B(x,e_{a})=0
unless
p(x)=p(e_{a})
.
∎

3.3.
Relative Cubic Dirac Operator and Weil Differential

We define relative cubic Dirac operators with respect to quadratic Lie subsuperalgebras
{\mathfrak{l}}
of basic classical Lie superalgebras
{\mathfrak{g}}
. This generalizes the constructions in the literature in the case where
{\mathfrak{l}}
is a Levi subsuperalgebra; see
[
14
,
23
]
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra and let
{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}

be a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra, with non-degenerate supersymmetric invariant bilinear
form
B_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. We refer to
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
as a

quadratic pair
. For a quadratic pair
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
, there is an orthogonal decomposition

(3.27)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp},

with respect to
B
. The restriction
B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is non-degenerate. In particular, one can form the Clifford superalgebra
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})\coloneqq{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}};B_{{\mathfrak{p}}})
. For simplicity, assume that
\dim{\mathfrak{p}}=2(p_{0}+p_{1})
is even, where
2p_{0}=\dim{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}
and
2p_{1}=\dim{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}}
. By invariance of
B
, one has
[{\mathfrak{l}},{\mathfrak{p}}]\subseteq{\mathfrak{p}}
.

As above, denote by
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})
the cubic Dirac operators associated with
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{l}}
, respectively. The relative cubic Dirac operator is obtained by combining these into a single operator via a natural embedding

j\colon\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}),

induced by the adjoint action of
{\mathfrak{l}}
on
{\mathfrak{p}}
.

For any
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
,
\operatorname{ad}_{x}\in{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{g}})
decomposes as the sum of
\operatorname{ad}_{x}^{{\mathfrak{l}}}\in{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{l}})
and
\operatorname{ad}_{x}^{{\mathfrak{p}}}\in{\mathfrak{osp}}({\mathfrak{p}})
. Set
\gamma_{{\mathfrak{l}}}^{\prime}(x)\coloneqq-\tfrac{1}{2}q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}^{{\mathfrak{l}}}))
and
\nu_{\ast}(x)\coloneqq\gamma_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{\prime}(x)\coloneqq-\tfrac{1}{2}q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}^{{\mathfrak{p}}}))\in{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
. The latter defines a Lie superalgebra homomorphism
\nu_{\ast}:{\mathfrak{l}}\to{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
. This map has an explicit description. As the dimension of
{\mathfrak{p}}
is even, we can decompose
{\mathfrak{p}}
in isotropic super subspaces
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}
compatible with the choice of a fixed positive system, that is
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
and
{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}\subset{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
. Let
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\subset\Delta
denote the associated root system, and set
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\cap\Delta^{+}
. The weights of
{\mathfrak{u}}
and
{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}
then belong to
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+}\coloneqq\Delta^{+}\cap\Delta\setminus\Delta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{-}\coloneqq\Delta^{-}\cap\Delta\setminus\Delta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Set
\Delta^{+}_{{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{0}}\coloneqq\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{0}}
and
\Delta^{+}_{{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{1}}\coloneqq\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}}}^{+}\cap\Delta_{\bar{1}}
. Finally, set
\rho^{{\mathfrak{u}}}\coloneqq\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{0}}^{+}}\alpha-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{\alpha\in\Delta_{{\mathfrak{p}},\bar{1}}^{+}}\alpha
.

Lemma 32
.

The map
\nu_{\ast}:{\mathfrak{l}}\to{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
is a Lie superalgebra homomorphism. In particular, if
u_{1},\ldots,u_{p}
is a basis of
{\mathfrak{u}}
and
\overline{u}_{1},\ldots,\overline{u}_{p}
is a basis of
\overline{{\mathfrak{u}}}
, then

\nu_{\ast}(X)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{j,k=1}^{p}(-1)^{p(u_{j})}B(X,[\overline{u}_{j},u_{k}])\overline{u}_{k}u_{j}+\begin{cases}\rho^{{\mathfrak{u}}}(X),\qquad&\text{if}\ X\in{\mathfrak{h}},\\
0,\qquad&\text{else}.\end{cases}

Proof.

The proof is verbatim the proof of Lemma 2.3.7 in
[
23
]
.
∎

To distinguish the generators of
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
from those of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
, we write
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}
instead of
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}
when necessary. Consider the natural embedding
j:\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
defined on generators by

(3.28)

j(1\otimes x)=1\otimes x,\qquad j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(x))=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\qquad x\in{\mathfrak{l}}.

Proposition 33
.

The following hold:

a)

The map
j:\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
is a Lie superalgebra homomorphism.

b)

j:\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
intertwines with contractions and Lie derivatives, that is,

\iota_{x}\circ j=j\circ\iota_{x},\qquad L_{x}\circ j=j\circ L_{x}\qquad\forall x\in{\mathfrak{l}}.

Proof.

a) This is immediate from
[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})
for
x,y\in{\mathfrak{g}}
(Lemma
24
).

b) We prove the statement for Lie derivatives
L_{x}
for
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. It is enough to prove the statement on the generators. One has

(L_{x}\circ j)(1\otimes y)=L_{x}(1\otimes y)=1\otimes L_{x}(y)=(j\circ L_{x})(1\otimes y).

Moreover, using that
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}
and
\nu_{*}
are Lie superalgebra homomorphisms, and that
L_{x}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
by Lemma
25
, one obtains with Lemma
24
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
:

(L_{x}\circ j)(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(y))=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(y))]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})=(j\circ L_{x})(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(x)).

That
j
intertwines with contractions is another direct calculation and will be omitted.
∎

Having fixed the notation, define the
relative cubic Dirac operator
by

(3.29)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}-j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}})\in{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}.

In
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
, set

\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\coloneqq(\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}}))^{{\mathfrak{l}}},

the
{\mathfrak{l}}
-basic subalgebra of the colour quantum Weil algebra, that is, the subalgebra consisting of all elements annihilated by
L_{x}
and
\iota_{x}
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Theorem 34
.

The following hold:

a)

The operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
lies in
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
.

b)

One has

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}-j(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{l}}})+\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}})-\tfrac{1}{24}\operatorname{str}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(\Omega_{{\mathfrak{l}}}).

Proof.

a) Note
\gamma^{\prime}(x)=-\tfrac{1}{2}q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))
, so that
\gamma^{\prime}(x)=\gamma^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)+\gamma^{\prime}_{{\mathfrak{p}}}(x)
. One first verifies that

\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x).

Indeed,

\displaystyle\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}e^{a}\otimes(-1)^{p(x)p(e_{a})}B(x,e_{a})+1\otimes\iota_{x}\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(-1)^{p(e_{a})}B(x,e_{a})\,e^{a}\otimes 1+q(\iota_{x}\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}})

\displaystyle=x\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}\bigl(1\otimes q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x}))\bigr),

where the identity
2\iota_{x}\phi_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=-\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{x})
from Lemma
26
is used.

By Lemma
33
, the Lie superalgebra homomorphism

j\colon\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
intertwines contractions and Lie derivatives. Hence, by definition of
j
,

\displaystyle\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}

\displaystyle=\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}-j(\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}})=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)-j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(x))=0.

Moreover,
L_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
follows directly from the
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariance of the cubic Dirac operators; see Lemma
28
.

b) In a), we have proven that
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
lies in
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
. In particular, it commutes with the image of
j
since

[j(1\otimes x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=2\iota_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=0,\qquad[j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})}(x)),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=L_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=0

where we used
\iota_{x}=\tfrac{1}{2}[1\otimes x,\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
by the Clifford relations, and Lemma
25
.
Thus,
[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}})]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0
. As
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is an odd element in
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
, we conclude

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}-\tfrac{1}{2}j([\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}})=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}-j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}).

The statement now follows with Theorem
29
.
∎

3.3.1.
Weil Differential

We grade
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
by the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading of
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
,
so that
(\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}}))_{\bar{s}}\coloneqq\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})_{\bar{s}}
. Via the diagonal embedding

{\mathfrak{l}}\to\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
, the adjoint action makes
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
into an

{\mathfrak{l}}
-supermodule. The
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariants then coincide with
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
. The space
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
is endowed with the obvious
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}\times{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading from
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
, making it into a colour Lie algebra.

Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
has a simple square, it induces a differential on
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
. For homogeneous

A\in\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
set

(3.30)

(d^{\prime})^{{\mathcal{W}}}(A)\coloneqq[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}},A]=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}A-(-1)^{p(A)}A\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}.

As
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariant,
(d^{\prime})^{{\mathcal{W}}}
preserves
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
and restricts to

d^{{\mathcal{W}}}:{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\to{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}).
This operator is nilpotent,
(d^{{\mathcal{W}}})^{2}=0
(see
[
18
]
), and compatible with the Lie derivative and contraction of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
defined in (
3.20
) and (
3.21
).

Lemma 35
.

The differential
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
is compatible with
L_{x}
and
\iota_{x}
for any
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
, that is, on
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})

d^{\mathcal{W}}L_{x}-(-1)^{p(x)}L_{x}d^{\mathcal{W}}=0,\qquad d^{{\mathcal{W}}}\iota_{x}+(-1)^{p(x)}\iota_{x}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}=L_{x}.

Moreover,
d^{\mathcal{W}}
is unique with this property.

Proof.

We note that
L_{x}=[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\cdot]_{{\mathcal{W}}}
. If we write
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}=\operatorname{ad}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}^{{\mathcal{W}}}
and
L_{x}=\operatorname{ad}_{\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)}^{{\mathcal{W}}}
, then

d^{{\mathcal{W}}}L_{x}-(-1)^{p(x)}L_{x}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}=\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)}-(-1)^{p(x)}\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)}\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}=\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}}=\operatorname{ad}^{{\mathcal{W}}}_{L_{x}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}=0

where we used
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariance of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
in the last equality. Similarly, one proves
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}\iota_{x}+(-1)^{p(x)}\iota_{x}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}=L_{x}.

∎

Since
(d^{{\mathcal{W}}})^{2}=0
, we may form its cohomology

\ker d^{{\mathcal{W}}}/\operatorname{im}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
. Recall that
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
denotes the center of the universal enveloping subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{l}}
. Let
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}_{\Delta})
denote the image of
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
in
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
under the
diagonal embedding. Then:

Theorem 36

(
[
18
, Theorem 6.2]
)
.

One has
\ker d^{{\mathcal{W}}}=\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}_{\Delta})\oplus\operatorname{im}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
. Equivalently, the cohomology of
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
is
isomorphic to
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}_{\Delta})\cong\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
.

3.4.
Oscillator Supermodule

At this stage the Dirac operator is only a ”​
Dirac element
“
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
in the Weil algebra

{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. To extract representation-theoretic information, we view it as an odd endomorphism of a suitable
representation space. For this, one must choose a
{\operatorname{Cl}}(\mathfrak{p})
-supermodule on which the Clifford
generators act. The
oscillator supermodule
provides the canonical choice (after fixing a
polarization
\mathfrak{p}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}
), realizing the Clifford action by creation and annihilation
operators. We briefly introduce it and refer to
[
23
]
for all details.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra. We adapt the notation from Section
3.3
and fix a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra

({\mathfrak{l}},B_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{l}}})\subset({\mathfrak{g}},B)
such that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
,
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{l}}
and
{\mathfrak{p}}
is even-dimensional. Since we work over
{\mathbb{C}}
and
B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
is non-degenerate,
there exist subsuperspaces
{\mathfrak{u}},{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}\subset{\mathfrak{p}}
with
{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}\subset{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}
and
{\mathfrak{u}}\subset{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
such that

(3.31)

{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{u}}\oplus{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}},\qquad{\mathfrak{u}}={\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}^{\perp},\qquad{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}={\mathfrak{u}}^{\perp},

with respect to
B_{{\mathfrak{p}}}
,
i.e.
,
{\mathfrak{u}}
and
{\overline{\mathfrak{u}}}
are complementary Lagrangian subsuperspaces.
Moreover,
{\mathfrak{p}}
decomposes into its even and odd parts as
{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}}
, and this
splitting is compatible with the polarization:

(3.32)

Lemma 37

The spin module

We may choose the dual bases

The following holds:

\partial_{k}

over

M({\mathfrak{p}})\coloneqq M({\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}})\otimes{S^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}},\qquad\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})\coloneqq\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}_{\bar{1}})\otimes{\overline{S}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}

-supermodules, with the

3.5.
Dirac Cohomology

We define Dirac cohomology for the relative cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
, where
{\mathfrak{l}}
is the quadratic subalgebra from Section
3.3
, and we record the main results of
[
23
]
. Although stated there for parabolic subalgebras, the arguments carry over verbatim to our setting.

Given an endomorphism
T\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(V)
of a super vector space
V
, we define its cohomology by

(3.42)

{\operatorname{H}}(T;V)\coloneqq\ker T/(\operatorname{im}T\cap\ker T).

We apply this to the (relative) cubic Dirac operator. Let
M
be a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule and let
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
be the oscillator
supermodule from Section
3.4
. On the
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{p}})
-supermodule

, the operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
acts componentwise.
Moreover,
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
supercommutes with
{\mathfrak{l}}
, so
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is naturally an
{\mathfrak{l}}
-supermodule.

Definition 40
.

The
Dirac cohomology
of a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule

(with respect to
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
) is the
{\mathfrak{l}}
-supermodule

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\coloneqq{\operatorname{H}}(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}};\,M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}))=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\big/\bigl(\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\bigr).

We are particularly interested in admissible
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
-supermodules,
i.e.
,
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules that are
{\mathfrak{l}}
-semisimple. For these supermodules, the Dirac cohomology is
{\mathfrak{l}}
-semisimple by Section

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
commutes with the action of
{\mathfrak{l}}
.

Lemma 41

Let
M
be an admissible
({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
-supermodule. Then
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
is a semisimple
{\mathfrak{l}}
-supermodule, that is,
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
is completely reducible as an
{\mathfrak{l}}
-supermodule.

Given the square formula of Theorem
34

infinitesimal character. The following super-analogue of the Casselman–Osborne theorem is due to

]
.

Theorem 42

(
[

]
)

Let

denote the Harish–Chandra monomorphism and let

be restriction. Then there exists an algebra homomorphism

\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}:{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}\to\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
such that for every

one can write

z\otimes 1=\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(z)+\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\!A+A\!\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{S}^{W^{{\mathfrak{g}}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\operatorname{S}^{W^{{\mathfrak{l}}}}}

3.6.
The Unitary Case

Of particular interest are the unitarizable highest weight supermodules over basic classical Lie superalgebras. As shown in

over Hermitian superspaces. For such supermodules the Dirac operator is selfadjoint, and the resulting Dirac cohomology is
especially simple. We record the main statements and refer to

for proofs. We retain the notation introduced above.

Theorem 43
.

Let
({\mathcal{H}},{\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle}_{{\mathcal{H}}})
be a unitarizable
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule. Then:

is selfadjoint with respect to

4.
Semisimple Perturbations

We introduce a family of cubic Dirac operators and associated Laplace operators, parametrized by elements of the real span of the root system, extending the standard cubic Dirac operator. For each finite-dimensional supermodule, the Laplace operator associated to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
determines a finite collection of
G_{\bar{0}}
-orbits encoding the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition. On the other hand, comparison of a family of Laplace operators for
{\mathfrak{g}}
with the square of the cubic Dirac operator gives energy operators detecting the degree of atypicality. Together, these operators detect both the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition and the atypicality.

4.1.
Family of Cubic Dirac Operators and Laplace Operators

We construct a family of cubic Dirac operators parametrized by the real span of the root system and study their properties. This family specializes to the ordinary cubic Dirac operator at the origin. We begin with the case in which
\mathfrak{g}
is a complex simple Lie algebra. Our results generalize those of
[
9
,
11
,
10
]
. Next, we define analogous families for basic classical Lie superalgebras with nontrivial odd part, as well as for the even part of basic classical Lie superalgebras. We study these families as elements in the color quantum Weil algebra
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
and operators acting on

M\otimes\overline{M}(\mathfrak{n}^{-}),

where
M
is a finite-dimensional simple (super)module.

4.1.1.
Complex Simple Lie Algebras

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra; that is,
{\mathfrak{g}}
is isomorphic to

\mathfrak{sl}(n;\mathbb{C})
,
\mathfrak{so}(2n;\mathbb{C})
,
\mathfrak{sp}(2n;\mathbb{C})
,

\mathfrak{so}(2n+1;\mathbb{C})
, or one of the exceptional Lie algebras
\mathfrak{g}_{2},\mathfrak{f}_{4},\mathfrak{e}_{6},\mathfrak{e}_{7},\mathfrak{e}_{8}
.
We adapt the notation from above. In particular,
B
denotes the Killing form on
{\mathfrak{g}}
. It restricts to a non-degenerate form on
{\mathfrak{h}}
, and allows us to identify
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. The induced non-degenerate bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
will be denoted by the same symbol by abuse of notation.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
denote the real span of
\Delta
. The induced form
B
is positive definite on
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. Using
B
, we identify
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
with
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
and denote the induced positive definite form on
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
by the same symbol. In particular, for
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, we write
h_{\xi}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
for the corresponding element, and conversely.

Let
G
be the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Then
G
acts naturally on
{\mathfrak{g}}
by the adjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}
and on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
by the coadjoint representation
\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}
. The musical isomorphism
\sharp:{\mathfrak{g}}\to{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
intertwines the adjoint and coadjoint representations, that is,

(4.1)

(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}x)^{\sharp}=\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}(x^{\sharp}),\qquad g\in G,\ x\in{\mathfrak{g}}.

This identification yields a canonical bijection between adjoint and coadjoint orbits.

Define

(4.2)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}.

Via the identification
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cong{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, this gives a
family of cubic Dirac operators
parametrized by
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. For
\xi=0
, one recovers the cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, which we shall refer to as the absolute cubic Dirac operator.

Proposition 44
.

For any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}

a)

The element
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is
{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant. In particular, it preserves all weight spaces.

b)

For every
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is an odd element in
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
.

c)

One has

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}+h_{\xi}\otimes 1+2\bigl(1\otimes\nu_{\ast}(h_{\xi})\bigr)+B(\xi,\xi)(1\otimes 1).

Proof.

a) The cubic Dirac operator
D_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant, so in particular
{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant. The statement now follows as
{\mathfrak{h}}
is abelian; so in particular
[h,\xi]=0
for all

.

By definition of the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading on
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
, the element

is odd for every

; see (

is odd. Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is odd by Lemma

, it follows that
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is odd.

c) Using b), we compute

\displaystyle\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}+[D_{{\mathfrak{g}}},1\otimes h_{\xi}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\tfrac{1}{2}[1\otimes h_{\xi},1\otimes h_{\xi}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}

\displaystyle=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}+2\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(h_{\xi})+B(\xi,\xi)(1\otimes 1),

where we used the Clifford relations for
[1\otimes h_{\xi},1\otimes h_{\xi}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=2B(h_{\xi},h_{\xi})(1\otimes 1)=2B(\xi,\xi)(1\otimes 1)
and Lemma
31
.
∎

Fix a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule

. Such supermodules are highest weight modules, parametrized by dominant integral weights

; see Section

for some Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}
. From now on, we fix some
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
and omit the superscript.

Recall the oscillator module
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
. Since
{\mathfrak{g}}
is even, one has
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})\cong\overline{S}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}}
; see (
3.34
). As the positive system is fixed, we write
S\coloneqq\overline{S}^{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}}
. Then the operators

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
define a
family of cubic Dirac operators

(4.3)

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\to\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\bigl(L(\Lambda)\otimes S\bigr),\qquad\xi\mapsto\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi}.

Our object of study is the subset

\{\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\mid\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\neq 0\}.

In general,
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is difficult to analyze directly, whereas Proposition
44
gives effective control of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}
. For
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, one may also use unitarity: every finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module is unitarizable, and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is selfadjoint on
L(\Lambda)\otimes S
. This usual argument is not available for
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
, since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is not selfadjoint in general.

Lemma 45
.

Let
{\mathfrak{r}}
be the compact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. If
h_{\xi}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cap{\mathfrak{r}}
, then
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is selfadjoint. In particular,

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}.

In general,
h_{\xi}\notin{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cap{\mathfrak{r}}
. Accordingly, one considers
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}
instead of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
. This is reflected in the notation

(4.4)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}\bigl(L(\Lambda)\otimes S\bigr),\qquad\xi\longmapsto\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}.

Throughout the remainder of this article, the family
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is referred to as the
family of Laplace operators
.

We compute the kernel of
{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}
on
L(\Lambda)
. The sets of weights of
L(\Lambda)
and of
S
are contained in
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. Since the restriction of
B
to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
is positive definite, we set
\|\mu\|\coloneqq B(\mu,\mu)
for
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
.

Lemma 46
.

{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}
acts on any weight space
(L(\Lambda)\otimes S)^{\mu}
of weight
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
as the scalar

\lVert\Lambda+\rho\rVert^{2}-\lVert\mu\rVert^{2}+\lVert\mu+\xi\rVert^{2}.

Proof.

By Proposition
44
,

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}+2\bigl(h_{\xi}\otimes 1+1\otimes\nu_{\ast}(h_{\xi})\bigr)+B(\xi,\xi)(1\otimes 1).

By Theorem
29
,

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1+B(\rho,\rho)(1\otimes 1)
, and
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}

acts on
L(\Lambda)
by the scalar

B(\Lambda+\rho,\Lambda+\rho)-B(\rho,\rho)
. Hence
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}
acts on

L(\Lambda)\otimes S
by
\|\Lambda+\rho\|^{2}
. Let
\mu=\mu_{1}+\mu_{2}
with

\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
such that
L(\Lambda)^{\mu_{1}}\neq 0
and

S^{\mu_{2}}\neq 0
. Then
h_{\xi}
acts on
(L(\Lambda)\otimes S)^{\mu}
by

B(\mu_{1},\xi)
and
\nu_{\ast}(h_{\xi})
by
B(\mu_{2},\xi)

(Proposition
39
). Consequently,

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}=\|\Lambda+\rho\|^{2}+2B(\mu,\xi)+B(\xi,\xi)=\|\Lambda+\rho\|^{2}-\|\mu\|^{2}+\|\mu+\xi\|^{2}.\qed

Theorem 47
.

The kernel of
{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}
is trivial unless
\xi=-\Lambda-\rho
. In this case the kernel is one-dimensional and given by
L(\Lambda)^{\Lambda}\otimes S^{\rho}
.

Proof.

Using Lemma
46
, the kernel is the direct sum of all weight spaces
(L(\Lambda)\otimes S)^{\mu}
such that

\lVert\Lambda+\rho\rVert^{2}-\lVert\mu\rVert^{2}+\lVert\mu+\xi\rVert^{2}=0.

We first note that
\lVert\mu+\xi\rVert^{2}\geq 0
since
\mu+\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
by assumption. Moreover,
\lVert\Lambda+\rho\rVert^{2}-\lVert\mu\rVert^{2}\geq 0
and equality holds precisely when
\mu=\Lambda+\rho

[
16
, Proposition 11.4]
. Thus, the weight space belongs to the kernel precisely when
\mu=\Lambda+\rho
and
\lVert\Lambda+\rho+\xi\rVert^{2}=0
. Since
B
is positive definite, we conclude
\xi=-\Lambda-\rho
. As
\Lambda
is the highest weight of
L(\Lambda)
and
\rho
is the highest weight of
S
, the weight space is one-dimensional and precisely given by
L(\Lambda)^{\Lambda}\otimes S^{\rho}
.
∎

The adjoint action
\operatorname{Ad}\colon G\to{\mathrm{GL}}({\mathfrak{g}})
extends canonically to actions
\operatorname{Ad}^{U}
on
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and
\operatorname{Ad}^{C}
on
{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
; the latter uses the invariance of
B
. This induces an action

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}
of
G
on
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
:

(4.5)

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}(x\otimes y)=\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{U}(x)\otimes\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{C}(y).

View
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
as subspaces of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
respectively, such that
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}})\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
or
\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast})\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
. Although our family was defined only on
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, it may be viewed as the restriction of a more general family on
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
. More precisely, regarding
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
as a subspace of
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
, one has families

(4.6)

{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\xi\mapsto\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\qquad\text{and}\qquad\xi\mapsto{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)},

whose restrictions to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
recover the families considered above.

Lemma 48
.

The maps

\operatorname{D}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\xi),{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}:\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}\longrightarrow\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{g})

are
G
-equivariant with respect to the coadjoint action
\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}
on

\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}
and the adjoint action
\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}
on

\mathcal{W}(\mathfrak{g})
,
i.e.
,

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}\bigl(\operatorname{D}_{\mathfrak{g}}(\xi)\bigr)=\operatorname{D}_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl(\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\xi\bigr),\quad\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}\bigl({\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}\bigr)=\Updelta_{\mathfrak{g}}\bigl(\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\xi\bigr)\qquad g\in G,\ \xi\in\mathfrak{g}^{\ast}.

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant, it is fixed by
\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}
.
Hence

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}\bigl(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\bigr)=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(h_{\xi}).

Under the identification
{\mathfrak{g}}\cong{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
via
B
, the adjoint action of
G

corresponds to the coadjoint action
\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}
, so
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(h_{\xi})=h_{\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}\xi}
. Analogously, for the Laplace operator one has

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}({\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)})=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+2(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(h_{\xi})\otimes 1+1\otimes q(\lambda(\operatorname{ad}_{\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}h_{\xi}})))+B(h_{\xi},h_{\xi})(1\otimes 1)=\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi),

where we used
G
-equivariance of
q
and
\lambda
and invariance of
B
, that is,

B(h_{\xi},h_{\xi})=B(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(h_{\xi}),\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(h_{\xi}))=B(h_{\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi},h_{\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi}).\qed

Since
G
is connected and simply connected with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
, any finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
integrates to a representation
\pi_{M}\colon G\to{\mathrm{GL}}(M)
whose derived representation coincides with the given
{\mathfrak{g}}
-action. Let
\pi_{\Lambda}
and
\pi_{S}
denote the corresponding representations on
L(\Lambda)
and
S
. Then
L(\Lambda)\otimes S
carries the representation

(4.7)

\pi\colon G\to{\mathrm{GL}}\bigl(L(\Lambda)\otimes S\bigr),\qquad\pi(g)\coloneqq\pi_{\Lambda}(g)\otimes\pi_{S}(g).

Using Lemma
48
and the compatibility of the

{\mathfrak{g}}
- and
G
-module structures on
L(\lambda)\otimes S
, one obtains the
following.

Lemma 49
.

For
g\in G
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
,

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)=\pi(g)\bigl(\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\bigr),\qquad\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)=\pi(g)(\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)).

In particular, for all
g\in G
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, there are vector space isomorphisms

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)\cong\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi),\qquad\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)\cong\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi).

For
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
, denote by
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(x)\subset{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
its coadjoint orbit. A coadjoint orbit is called
semisimple
if it contains a semisimple element,
i.e.
, an element lying in the dual of a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. Equivalently,
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(x)\cap{\mathfrak{t}}^{\ast}\neq\{0\}
for any Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
. A semisimple coadjoint orbit is called
{\mathbb{R}}
-split
if
\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(x)\cap{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\neq\{0\}
. Note that for any other Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{t}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
with corresponding real span of roots

{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, the spaces
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
and
{\mathfrak{t}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
are conjugate under the adjoint
action. In particular, for
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, the coadjoint orbit

\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(\mu)
is
{\mathbb{R}}
-split and semisimple.

Combining Lemma
49
with Theorem
47
, one obtains the following result.

Theorem 50
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module. Then
\ker{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}=\{0\}
unless
\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(-\Lambda-\rho)
. In particular, this assignment associates to each finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module a unique
{\mathbb{R}}
-split semisimple coadjoint orbit.

4.1.2.
Basic Classical Lie Superalgebra

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
. Fix the super Killing form
B
on
{\mathfrak{g}}
, which identifies
{\mathfrak{h}}
with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
; in particular,
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. We denote by the same symbol the non-degenerate form on
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
. As above, let
{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
denote the real span of the root system
\Delta
. Unlike the finite-dimensional simple complex Lie algebra case, the restriction of
B
to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is not positive definite. As an example, consider
{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
and
\mu\coloneqq(1,0|0,-1)\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
, which is isotropic, that is,
B(\mu,\mu)=0
. The even subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is reductive; let
G_{\bar{0}}
be the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.

As in the complex simple even case, define a family of cubic Dirac operators and Laplace operators by

(4.8)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\xi\mapsto\begin{cases}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi},\\
{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}.\end{cases}

By the same argument as in the proof of Proposition
44
, one obtains the following.

Proposition 51
.

For any
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, the following hold:

a)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
is
{\mathfrak{h}}
-invariant.

b)

For every
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, the operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
has bidegree
(\bar{1},\bar{0})
; in particular, it is odd with respect to the total degree.

c)

{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}+h_{\xi}\otimes 1-\tfrac{1}{2}(1\otimes\nu_{\ast}(h_{\xi}))+B(\xi,\xi)(1\otimes 1).

d)

\operatorname{Ad}^{\mathcal{W}}_{g}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)
for all
g\in G_{\bar{0}}
.

e)

\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\mathcal{W}}{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}=\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)
for all
g\in G_{\bar{0}}
.

As in the simple complex even case, the natural question is whether, for each finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, the kernel of
{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}
determines a unique
G_{\bar{0}}
-coadjoint orbit. Recall from Section
2.2
that such supermodules are parametrized by dominant integral weights
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
with respect to a Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}\oplus{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{1}}
, that is,
\lambda
satisfies

(4.9)

B(\lambda+\rho_{\bar{0}},\alpha)>0\qquad\text{for all }\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+},

where
\Delta^{+}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{+}\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
. We fix a
\Lambda\in P^{++}
for a fixed Borel
{\mathfrak{b}}
and consider the finite-dimensional simple supermodule
L(\lambda)
with even highest weight vector such that we view the family as

(4.10)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\to\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})),\qquad\xi\mapsto\begin{cases}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi},\\
{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2}.\end{cases}

Since
B
restricted to
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
is not positive definite, we cannot conclude as in the proof of Theorem
47
. Indeed, we only have the following partial result which is proved as above:

Lemma 52
.

The following hold:

a)

For all
g\in G_{\bar{0}}
and
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
,

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)\cong\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi),\qquad\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\operatorname{Ad}_{g}^{\ast}\xi)\cong\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)

as super vector spaces.

b)

{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}
acts on any weight space
(L(\Lambda)\otimes S)^{\mu}
of weight
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
as the scalar

B(\Lambda+\rho,\Lambda+\rho)-B(\mu,\mu)+B(\mu+\xi,\mu+\xi).

For a fixed weight
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
of
L(\Lambda)\otimes S
, the expression

(4.11)

B(\Lambda+\rho,\Lambda+\rho)-B(\mu,\mu)+B(\mu+\xi,\mu+\xi)

may admit many solutions in
\xi
as
B
is indefinite. To overcome this, we consider appropriate modifications of families of Laplace operators defined with respect to the even subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, using Theorem
47
. This allows one to detect the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition of a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

4.1.3.
Even Lie Subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
.
The Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
is reductive.
Consequently,
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
admits a decomposition

(4.12)

{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{r},

where
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}
is either trivial or an abelian Lie algebra, and
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
is simple for all

i=1,\ldots,r
. The possible cases are summarized in Table
1
.

\begin{array}[]{|c|c|c|}\hline\cr{\mathfrak{g}}&{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}&\text{Type of }{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}\\
\hline\cr\mathfrak{sl}(m|n),\ m\neq n&\mathfrak{sl}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(n)\oplus\mathbb{C}&\text{reductive, not semisimple}\\
\hline\cr\mathfrak{psl}(n|n),\ n\geq 2&\mathfrak{sl}(n)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(n)&\text{semisimple}\\
\hline\cr\mathfrak{osp}(2|2n)&\mathfrak{so}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(2n)\cong\mathbb{C}\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(2n)&\text{reductive, not semisimple}\\
\hline\cr\mathfrak{osp}(m|2n),\ m\neq 2&\mathfrak{so}(m)\oplus\mathfrak{sp}(2n)&\text{semisimple}\\
\hline\cr D(2,1;\alpha),\ \alpha\neq 0,-1&\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2)&\text{semisimple}\\
\hline\cr F(4)&\mathfrak{so}(7)\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2)&\text{semisimple}\\
\hline\cr G(3)&{\mathfrak{g}}_{2}\oplus\mathfrak{sl}(2)&\text{semisimple}\\
\hline\cr\end{array}

Table 1
.
Even Lie subalgebras of basic classical Lie superalgebras

The summands
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
are mutually orthogonal with respect to restriction of the super Killing form
B
. Moreover, for any
i=1,\ldots,r
, the restriction of
B
to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
is a nonzero scalar multiple of the Killing form of

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
, and the restriction to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}
is non-trivial.
We denote by
B_{i}
the induced bilinear form on
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
and
({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i})^{\ast}
, respectively.

Fix a Cartan subalgebra
{\mathfrak{h}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
; then
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. Accordingly,
{\mathfrak{h}}
decomposes compatibly with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
as

(4.13)

{\mathfrak{h}}={\mathfrak{h}}^{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{r},

where
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
is a Cartan subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
for
i\neq 0
, and
{\mathfrak{h}}^{0}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}
. The restriction of
B_{i}
to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
induces a
non-degenerate symmetric bilinear form on
({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})^{\ast}
, defined analogously
to the form obtained by restricting
B
to
{\mathfrak{h}}
; by abuse of notation, this form
is again denoted by
B_{i}
. Consequently,

{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}=({\mathfrak{h}}^{0})^{\ast}\oplus({\mathfrak{h}}^{1})^{\ast}\oplus\cdots\oplus({\mathfrak{h}}^{r})^{\ast},

and for
i\neq 0
the space
({\mathfrak{h}}^{i})^{\ast}
is identified with
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
via

B_{i}
. With respect to this decomposition, each
\lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
admits a unique decomposition

\lambda=(\lambda^{0},\lambda^{1},\ldots,\lambda^{r})
, where
\lambda^{i}
is the
restriction of
\lambda
to
{\mathfrak{h}}^{i}
. For
\lambda,\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}^{\ast}
, the bilinear
form
B
satisfies

(4.14)

B(\lambda,\mu)=\sum_{i=0}^{r}B_{i}(\lambda^{i},\mu^{i}).

In the sequel, the subscript
i
is omitted whenever no ambiguity arises.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
denote the real span of
\Delta
with dual space
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
.
Then
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}={\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{r}.
In addition, let
G_{\bar{0}}
be the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
Then

(4.15)

G_{\bar{0}}=G_{\bar{0}}^{0}\times G_{\bar{0}}^{1}\times\cdots\times G_{\bar{0}}^{r},

where
G_{\bar{0}}^{i}
is the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
. We have a natural coadjoint action of
G_{\bar{0}}
on
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}
.

Let
\Delta
be the root system of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with respect to
{\mathfrak{h}}
, and let
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
denote the root
system of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
The root systems
\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{1},\ldots,\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{r}
are identified with subsets of
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
such that
\Delta_{\bar{0}}=\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{1}\sqcup\ldots\sqcup\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{r}
. A choice of positive system
\Delta^{+}\subset\Delta
induces positive systems on each

\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{i}
.
With this choice, the positive system of
\Delta_{\bar{0}}
is the disjoint union of the positive systems of the

\Delta_{\bar{0}}^{i}
.
The even Weyl vector
\rho_{\bar{0}}
is defined accordingly, and decomposes as

\rho_{\bar{0}}=(0,\rho^{1}_{\bar{0}},\ldots,\rho^{r}_{\bar{0}}).

Fix a Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
.
Then
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
decomposes as

{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}={\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}\oplus{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{1}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{r}
with
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}={\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}
.
In particular, the set of dominant integral weights parameterizing finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-modules is

(4.16)

P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}}=P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}}\times\ldots\times P^{++}_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{r}},

where
P_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}}^{++}
is identified with
({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0})^{\ast}\coloneqq\operatorname{Hom}_{{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{0},{\mathbb{C}})
and each
P_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}^{+}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
is described in Section
2.2
.

Let
L_{0}(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module.
Then
L_{0}(\Lambda)
is a highest weight module with respect to
{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}
with highest weight
\Lambda\in P_{{\mathfrak{b}}_{\bar{0}}}^{++}
.
Moreover,
L_{0}(\Lambda)
decomposes as an outer tensor product

(4.17)

L_{0}(\Lambda)=L(\Lambda^{0};{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0})\boxtimes L(\Lambda^{1};{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{1})\boxtimes\cdots\boxtimes L(\Lambda^{r};{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{r}),

where each

{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}
.

Since each
({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i},B_{i})
is a quadratic Lie algebra, there is a cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i})
. As above, this defines families

(4.18)

({\mathfrak{h}}^{i}_{{\mathbb{R}}})^{\ast}\to\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}),\qquad\xi\mapsto\begin{cases}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}+1\otimes h_{\xi^{i}},\\
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})^{2}.\end{cases}

For
i\neq j
, the elements
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})
and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{j}}(\xi^{j})
anti-commute in
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
, via the embedding (
3.28
).
Hence, for
\xi=(\xi^{0},\ldots,\xi^{r})\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, we define

(4.19)

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{r}\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}),

which equals
\bigl(\sum_{i=0}^{r}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\bigr)^{2}
.

We are interested in the action of
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
on
L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S
, where now
S\coloneqq\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{0}}^{-})
. In general, the kernel of
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
does not coincide with the intersection of the kernels of the operators
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})
. As a consequence, Theorem
50
is not directly applicable. We address this by introducing a modified family of operators, using that
S
is unitarizable and that
L_{0}(\Lambda)
is finite-dimensional.

Since
L_{0}(\Lambda)
is finite-dimensional, we can fix a Hermitian inner product on
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)}
on
L_{0}(\Lambda)
. Let
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}\coloneqq\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)}\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{S}
be the induced Hermitian inner product on
L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S
. With respect to this product, we denote by
(\cdot)^{\dagger}
the adjoint of an endomorphism. We then define

(4.20)

\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\sum_{i=0}^{r}(\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i}))^{\dagger}\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S).

Lemma 53
.

The operator
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
satisfies the following properties:

a)

\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
is positive semi-definite, that is,

\langle\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)v,v\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}\geq 0\qquad\text{for all }v\in L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S.

b)

One has

\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)=\bigcap_{i=0}^{r}\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i}).

Proof.

For
v\in L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S
, one has

\langle\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)v,v\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\bigl\langle(\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i}))^{\dagger}\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})v,v\bigr\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}=\sum_{i=0}^{r}\bigl\langle\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})v,\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})v\bigr\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S},

hence

\langle\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)v,v\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}\geq 0.

This proves a). For b), positivity of the Hermitian inner product implies that

v\in\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\langle\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)v,v\rangle_{L_{0}(\Lambda)\otimes S}=0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})v=0\text{ for all }i=0,\ldots,r,

which is equivalent to

v\in\bigcap_{i=0}^{r}\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i}).

∎

Theorem 54
.

Let
L_{0}(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-module with highest weight

\Lambda\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. Then
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
has trivial kernel unless

\xi=-\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{0}}
. In particular,

\operatorname{ker}\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\neq\{0\}\iff\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G}^{\ast}(-\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{0}})\coloneqq\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\Lambda-\rho_{\bar{0}}).

Proof.

By Lemma
53
is the kernel of
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
the intersection of the kernels of

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})
. By
Theorem
47
,

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})
has trivial kernel unless

\xi^{i}=-\Lambda^{i}-\rho_{\bar{0}}^{i}
for
i=1,\ldots,r
, and

\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi^{i})\neq\{0\}
iff

\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(-\Lambda^{i}-\rho_{\bar{0}}^{i})
. For the abelian
factor
{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}
, one has
\rho_{\bar{0}}^{0}=0
and

L(\Lambda^{0};{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0})
is one-dimensional, with

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{0}}(\xi^{0})
acting by the scalar

B_{0}(\Lambda^{0}+\xi^{0},\Lambda^{0}+\xi^{0})
. Since
B_{0}
is non-degenerate,
this scalar vanishes only for
\xi^{0}=-\Lambda^{0}
. The claim follows.
∎

Remark 55
.

Let
{\mathfrak{r}}
be the compact real form of
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. If
h_{\xi}\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}\cap{\mathfrak{r}}
, then
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi)

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}_{\bar{0}}^{i}}(\xi)

54
remains valid with
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
replaced by
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
.

We now apply the preceding result to finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. We thus embed
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}})
into
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Fix a
finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
with respect to a fixed
positive Borel
{\mathfrak{b}}
. Upon restriction to the even subalgebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
,
the module

decomposes as a finite direct sum of simple

-modules,

(4.21)

L(\Lambda)\Big|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}=\bigoplus_{\mu}L_{0}(\mu)^{n(\mu)},

where the sum runs over the highest weights
\mu
of the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituents and

, the modified family

\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
is viewed as a family in
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}

acting on
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
by

(4.22)

\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\otimes 1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}((L(\Lambda)\otimes S)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})),

where we use the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product decomposition

and identify

with

. This family then detects precisely the

-constituents of

via their highest

.

Corollary 56
.

Let

be a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with highest weight

such that

. Then

\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow\xi\in\bigcup_{\mu\,:\,n(\mu)\neq 0}\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}).

If

, then the kernel is
L(\Lambda)^{\mu}\otimes S^{\rho_{\bar{0}}}\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})
.

Example 57
.

We consider the projective special linear Lie superalgebra
\mathfrak{psl}(2|2)
. Fix the distinguished positive system such that the Weyl vector takes the form
\rho=(1,0|0,-1)
. We consider
L(\Lambda)
with highest weight
\Lambda=(1,0|0,-1)
. Let
\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, where
{\mathfrak{h}}
is the Cartan subalgebra of diagonal matrices. Then

\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow-\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(X+\rho_{\bar{0}}),\quad X=\{\Lambda,(0,0|1,-1),(1,-1|0,0),(0,-1|1,0)\}.

4.2.
A Detecting Family

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}\oplus{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
, and retain the notation introduced above. In the previous section, a family of cubic Dirac operators

(4.23)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi},\qquad\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast},

was introduced; its invariance properties were established; and the kernel of the associated family of Laplace operators
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)=\operatorname{D}^{2}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)
was analyzed. When restricted to
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
, a resulting Laplace family
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
detects the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-decomposition of a finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule, but does not provide further information.

For
{\mathfrak{g}}
with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
, the representation theory differs from the classical case because of the presence of isotropic odd roots. This is reflected in Kac’s distinction between typical and atypical simple finite-dimensional supermodules, defined in terms of the relation between the highest weight and the isotropic roots; see Section
2.3
. Typical representations retain many of the features familiar from ordinary semisimple Lie algebras, whereas atypical representations do not. In particular, the even subalgebra no longer determines all relevant structure. This leads to additional invariants, and in this paper we introduce an energy operator for the detection of atypicality.

Recall that a weight
\Lambda
is
typical
if
A_{\Lambda}=\emptyset
and
atypical
otherwise, where
A_{\Lambda}=\{\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}:B(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0,\ B(\alpha,\alpha)=0\}
. The
degree of atypicality
of
\Lambda
, denoted by
{\operatorname{at}}(\Lambda)
, is the maximal cardinality of a linearly independent set of mutually orthogonal isotropic roots
\alpha\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}^{+}
such that
B(\Lambda+\rho,\alpha)=0
.

To detect this information, we introduce an additional family of operators measuring the deviation from the Laplace operator
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
, called
energy operators
. These encode atypicality and will be combined with
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
below. We recall that

(4.24)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}+1\otimes h_{\xi},\qquad{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\xi)^{2},\qquad\xi\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}.

Definition 58
.

Let
\eta\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. The difference of the family
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\eta)
and the Laplace operator
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is called
energy operator
, that is,

T(\eta)\coloneqq\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(\eta)-\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=2\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(h_{\eta})+B(\eta,\eta).

Remark 59
.

The operator
T(\eta)
is called
energy operator
, since it generates the Lie derivative in
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
by Lemma
31
. Note that
T(0)=0
.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}}\subset{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}
be a maximal isotropic subspace. Using the form
B
, we identify
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
with its dual
({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}
. The latter is spanned by pairwise distinct, mutually orthogonal odd isotropic roots of
{\mathfrak{g}}
. In particular, by definition, the dimension of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}}
coincides with the defect of
{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Since
B
is invariant, it is preserved under the adjoint action of
G_{\bar{0}}
. In particular, for
\lambda\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}
and
g\in G_{\bar{0}}
, the element
\operatorname{Ad}^{\ast}_{g}(h)\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
is isotropic.

The operators
T(\eta)
are regarded as a
family of energy operators
parametrized by
({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}
, defined by

(4.25)

({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}\rightarrow\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\eta\mapsto T(\eta)\coloneqq 2\gamma^{\mathcal{W}}(h_{\eta}),

where we note that
B(\eta,\eta)=0
since
\eta\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}
.
If we fix a Borel subalgebra
{\mathfrak{b}}
and a finite-dimensional simple supermodule
L(\Lambda)
with
\Lambda\in P^{++}
, we consider

(4.26)

({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\mathrm{iso}})^{\ast}\longrightarrow\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})),\qquad\eta\longmapsto T(\eta).

The set of weights of
L(\Lambda)
is
\mathcal{P}_{L(\Lambda)}=\{\Lambda-{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}[A]:A\subset\Delta^{+}\}
and the set of weights of
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
is

\mathcal{P}_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})}=\{\rho-{\mathbb{Z}}_{+}[B]:B\subset\Delta^{+}\}
. Both are subsets of
{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
. A general weight of
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
takes the form
\Lambda-\mu_{1}+\rho-\mu_{2}
such that
L(\Lambda)^{\Lambda-\mu_{1}}
and
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})^{\rho-\mu_{2}}
are non-trivial, and
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}
are positive sums of positive roots. As in the proof of Lemma
46
, one obtains the following.

Lemma 60
.

For any weight
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, the operator
T(\eta)
acts on the weight space

(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}))^{\mu}
by the scalar
2B(\mu,\eta)
. In particular,

\ker T(\eta)=\ker T(\eta)^{2}.

Any element
\eta\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}
can be written as

(4.27)

\eta=a_{1}\alpha_{1}+\cdots+a_{r}\alpha_{r},\qquad a_{i}\in{\mathbb{R}},

where
\alpha_{i}\in\Delta_{\bar{1}}
are pairwise orthogonal odd isotropic roots. The integer

0\leq r\leq\operatorname{def}({\mathfrak{g}})
is called the
rank
of
\eta
and is denoted by
{\operatorname{rk}}(\eta)
.
For fixed
\mu\in{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}
, define the real vector subspace

(4.28)

X_{\mu}\coloneqq\{\eta\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}\mid B(\mu,\eta)=0\},

called the
isotropic annihilator
of
\mu
.

Corollary 61
.

Let
\mu=\Lambda-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}+\rho
be a weight of
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
and

\eta\in({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}
. If
(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}))^{\mu}\subset\ker T(\eta)
, then
\Lambda-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
is atypical of degree at least
{\operatorname{rk}}(\eta)
. Conversely,
if
\Lambda-\mu_{1}-\mu_{2}
is atypical of degree
k
, then

(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}))^{\mu}\subset\ker T(\eta)
for all
\eta\in X_{\mu}
. In
particular,

\ker T(\eta)\big|_{(L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-}))^{\mu}}\neq\{0\}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\eta\in X_{\mu}.

For a finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
L(\Lambda)
, Corollary
61
shows that the kernel of
T
on
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
is too large to yield a useful localization. It is therefore natural to replace
L(\Lambda)\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
by a smaller subspace. By construction (
cf.
(
3.41
)) the oscillator supermodule
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}^{-})
is a highest weight module with highest weight vector
1_{S}\otimes 1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}
. Set
E_{\Lambda}:=L(\Lambda)\otimes S
and write
1:=1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}
. We then consider the
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-submodule
\langle E_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
, where parity is disregarded and
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
acts diagonally. This submodule already suffices for the application of the Dirac operator to the Dirac inequality; see
[
28
]
.

Lemma 62

(
[
28
]
)
.

If
L(\Lambda)\big|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mu}L_{0}(\mu)^{n(\mu)}
, then

\langle E_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mu}L_{0}(\mu+\rho)^{n(\mu)},

and any
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-highest weight vector is of the form
v_{\mu}\otimes 1_{S}\otimes 1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}
for some
v_{\mu}\in L(\Lambda)^{\mu}
.

By Lemma
62
, both
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
and
T(\eta)
act on
\langle E_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}
. For
T(\eta)
, this is immediate from the definition and the
{\mathfrak{h}}
-semisimplicity of the module. We combine these operators into a single map

(4.29)

(T,\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}})\colon({\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\text{iso}})^{\ast}\times{\mathfrak{h}}_{{\mathbb{R}}}^{\ast}\to\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\bigl(\langle E_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}},(\langle E_{\Lambda}\otimes 1\rangle_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}})^{\oplus 2}\bigr),

called the
detecting family
.

Theorem 63
.

Let
L(\Lambda)
be a finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with
L(\Lambda)|_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}\cong\bigoplus_{\mu}L_{0}(\mu)^{n(\mu)}
. Then

\ker((T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))=\{0\}
unless both of the following conditions hold:

a)

\xi=-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}
, where
\mu
is the highest weight of a
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
-constituent of
L(\Lambda)
,

b)

\eta\in X_{\mu+\rho}
.

In particular,

\ker(T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))\neq\{0\}\Leftrightarrow(\eta,\xi)\in\bigcup_{\mu\,:\,n(\mu)\neq 0}X_{\mu+\rho}\times\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}).

Proof.

By construction,

\ker(T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))=\ker T(\eta)\cap\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi).

It therefore suffices to determine
\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
and the action of
T(\eta)
on this space. By (
4.22
), Lemma
62
, and Theorem
54
, the kernel of
\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)
is non-zero precisely for

\xi\in\bigcup_{\mu\,:\,n(\mu)\neq 0}\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}}),

and in this case

\ker\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi)\cong\bigoplus_{\mu\,:\,\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}})}(L_{0}(\mu)^{\mu}\otimes S^{\rho_{\bar{0}}}\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})^{-\rho_{\bar{1}}})^{\oplus n(\mu)}.

Here
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})^{-\rho_{\bar{1}}}={\mathbb{C}}(1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})})
, so the summand indexed by
\mu
has dimension
n(\mu)
. Now
T(\eta)
preserves each such summand, and it acts on

(L(\Lambda)^{\mu}\otimes S^{\rho_{\bar{0}}})\otimes 1_{\overline{M}({\mathfrak{n}}_{\bar{1}}^{-})}
by the scalar
2B(\mu+\rho,\eta)
. Consequently, the joint kernel

\ker(T(\eta),\widetilde{\Updelta}_{{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}}(\xi))

is obtained by restricting the above direct sum to those
\mu
for which both conditions hold:

\xi\in\operatorname{Ad}_{G_{\bar{0}}}^{\ast}(-\mu-\rho_{\bar{0}})\qquad\text{and}\qquad B(\mu+\rho,\eta)=0.

The assertion follows from Corollary
61
.
∎

5.
Nilpotent Perturbations

In this section, let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra with
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}\neq\{0\}
. Two standard tools for studying
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules are:

(1)

Dirac cohomology
, which detects the infinitesimal character.

(2)

Duflo–Serganova cohomology
(DS cohomology), a symmetric monoidal functor preserving the superdimension of finite-dimensional supermodules.

We construct a family of relative cubic Dirac operators
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}
, parametrized by the self-commuting variety, whose cohomology interpolates between Dirac cohomology and Duflo–Serganova cohomology. We start by recalling DS cohomology.

5.1.
Duflo–Serganova Cohomology

The
Duflo–Serganova functor
(DS functor), introduced by Duflo and Serganova
[
7
]
, is a symmetric monoidal tensor functor associated with an odd square-zero element.

In what follows, we briefly recall the DS functor, mainly following

[
7
,
12
]
; see also
[
31
]
.

The DS functor depends on a choice of element in the
self-commuting variety

(5.1)

{\mathcal{Y}}\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}:[x,x]=0\}.

Let
G_{\bar{0}}
be the connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{0}}}
. It acts on
{\mathcal{Y}}
by the adjoint action, and
{\mathcal{Y}}
is a
G_{\bar{0}}
-stable Zariski-closed cone in
{\mathfrak{g}_{\bar{1}}}
. Its
G_{\bar{0}}
-orbits are in bijection with
W
-orbits of subsets of mutually orthogonal, linearly independent, odd isotropic roots; in particular,
{\mathcal{Y}}
has finitely many
G_{\bar{0}}
-orbits. Concretely, fix
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}
. Then there exist
g\in G_{\bar{0}}
and mutually orthogonal, linearly independent isotropic roots

\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{k}
such that

(5.2)

\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(x)=x_{1}+\cdots+x_{k},\qquad x_{i}\in{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha_{i}}.

The integer
{\operatorname{rk}}(x)\coloneqq k
is independent of the choices and is called the
rank
of
x
; equivalently, it is the rank of
x
in the standard representation.

To each
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}
we associate a Lie superalgebra

(5.3)

{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}\coloneqq\ker(\operatorname{ad}_{x})/\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ad}_{x}),

which is well-defined since
\operatorname{im}(\operatorname{ad}_{x})=[x,{\mathfrak{g}}]
is an ideal in
\ker(\operatorname{ad}_{x})
. Moreover,
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
has Cartan subalgebra

(5.4)

{\mathfrak{h}}_{x}\coloneqq\Bigl(\bigcap_{i=1}^{k}\ker(\alpha_{i})\Bigr)\Big/\bigl({\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha_{1}}\oplus\cdots\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha_{k}}\bigr),\qquad{\mathfrak{h}}^{\alpha}\coloneqq[{\mathfrak{g}}^{\alpha},{\mathfrak{g}}^{-\alpha}],

and the corresponding root system is

(5.5)

\Delta_{x}=\{\alpha\in\Delta:B(\alpha,\alpha_{i})=0\ \text{for }i=1,\dots,k,\ \alpha\neq\alpha_{i}\}.

We now define the
DS functor
. Given a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
, each
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}
acts on
M
by an endomorphism
x_{M}\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(M)
with
x_{M}^{2}=0
. Define

(5.6)

M_{x}\coloneqq\ker(x_{M})/\operatorname{im}(x_{M}).

Then
M_{x}
is naturally a
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
-supermodule, since
\ker(x_{M})
is
\ker(\operatorname{ad}_{x})
-stable and

[x,{\mathfrak{g}}]\cdot\ker(x_{M})\subseteq\operatorname{im}(x_{M})
. Thus
M\mapsto M_{x}
defines a functor from

{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules to
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
-supermodules, denoted

(5.7)

\operatorname{DS}_{x}(M)\coloneqq M_{x}.

This is the
Duflo–Serganova functor
(DS functor). For finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules, it has the following basic properties.

Lemma 64

(
[
12
, Section 2]
)
.

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
-smod
denote the category of finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules. For any
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}
, the functor
\operatorname{DS}_{x}:{\mathfrak{g}}\textbf{-smod}\to{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}\textbf{-smod}
is additive and symmetric monoidal. Moreover,
\operatorname{DS}_{x}
preserves superdimension.

For the remainder of this section, fix a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
. We recall a construction from

[
7
, Section 6]
(see also
[
31
]
).
Let
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}
be the subsuperalgebra of
\operatorname{ad}_{x}
-invariants in
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and set

I_{x}\coloneqq[x,\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})]\subset\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}}).

Consider
\phi=\pi\circ\iota
in the sequence

(5.8)

{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x})\xrightarrow{\ \iota\ }\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\xrightarrow{\ \pi\ }\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}/\bigl(I_{x}\cap\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\bigr),

where
\iota
is the inclusion and
\pi
the projection. Both are morphisms of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
-supermodules (adjoint action), and
\phi
is an isomorphism of super vector spaces
[
7
, Lemma 6.6]
. Define

(5.9)

\upeta\coloneqq\phi^{-1}\circ\pi:\ \mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}\to{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x}).

Then
u\cdot m=\upeta(u)\cdot m
for
u\in\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}
and
m\in M_{x}
, since
\ker(x_{M})
is

\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}
-stable and
I_{x}\ker(x_{M})\subseteq\operatorname{im}(x_{M})
.

Let
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x})
be the center of
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x})
. Since
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\subset\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{g}})^{\operatorname{ad}_{x}}
and
\upeta

is a morphism of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
-supermodules, we have
\upeta\bigl(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})\bigr)\subset\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x}),

and the dual map

(5.10)

\upeta^{*}:\ \operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}_{x}),{\mathbb{C}})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}}),{\mathbb{C}})

is injective
[
7
, Theorem 6.11]
.

Let
\chi
be the infinitesimal character of
M
. For
z\in\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
and

,

(5.11)

\chi(z)m=zm\equiv\upeta(z)m\pmod{xM},

so if

contains a submodule with infinitesimal character

, then
\upeta^{*}(\xi)=\chi
. Moreover,

(5.12)

{\operatorname{at}}\bigl(\upeta^{*}(\xi)\bigr)={\operatorname{at}}(\xi)+{\operatorname{rk}}(x),

and hence
{\operatorname{at}}(\xi)={\operatorname{at}}(\chi)-{\operatorname{rk}}(x)
. Thus we obtain:

Theorem 65

(
[
31
, Theorem 2.1]
)
.

Let
M
be a simple highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule with infinitesimal character
\chi
.
Then
M_{x}
decomposes as a direct sum of
{\mathfrak{g}}_{x}
-supermodules whose generalized infinitesimal characters lie in

(\upeta^{*})^{-1}(\chi)
, and each
\xi\in(\upeta^{*})^{-1}(\chi)
satisfies
{\operatorname{at}}(\xi)={\operatorname{at}}(\chi)-{\operatorname{rk}}(x)
.

Corollary 66
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}\setminus\{0\}
have rank
l
, and let

be a highest weight
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule of atypicality

.
If

, then
\operatorname{DS}_{x}(M)=0
.

5.2.
A Family of Nilpotent Perturbations

Fix a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
such that
B_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
is non-degenerate and

(5.13)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp},

where the decomposition is orthogonal with respect to
B
. In what follows, we embed
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
into
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
using the embedding (
3.28
). Assume
{\mathfrak{h}}\subset{\mathfrak{l}}
and
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{1}}\neq\{0\}
. Let

(5.14)

{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\{x\in{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{1}}:\ [x,x]=2x^{2}=0\}

be the self-commuting variety of
{\mathfrak{l}}
. If
L_{\bar{0}}
is a connected simply connected Lie group with Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{0}}
, then
{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}

is an
L_{\bar{0}}
-stable Zariski-closed cone in
{\mathfrak{l}}_{\bar{1}}
. We will define a family of cubic Dirac operators parametrized by

x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

Recall that the quantum Weil algebra
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
is generated by
1\otimes x
and
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)=x\otimes 1+1\otimes\nu_{*}(x)
,
with bidegrees
(\bar{1},p(x))
and
(\bar{0},p(x))
(hence total degrees
p(x)+\bar{1}
and
p(x)
, respectively). We write
{\operatorname{deg}}

for the total degree on
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
. Unlike the reductive (purely even) case, this grading allows square-zero perturbations of the
relative cubic Dirac operator while keeping a nice square.

Definition 67
.

For
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
set

\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}+j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))\in{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}.

Remark 68
.

Since
j:{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
is a Lie superalgebra homomorphism, we have
j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))^{2}=0
for all

x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Indeed,
x
is odd, hence
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)
is odd for the total degree, and therefore

j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,[j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)),j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}\,j([\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)]_{{\mathcal{W}}})=\tfrac{1}{2}\,j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,x]_{{\mathfrak{g}}}))=0.

Let
C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)\coloneqq\{y\in{\mathfrak{l}}:[x,y]=0\}
be the
commutant
of
x
in
{\mathfrak{l}}
. The following lemma summarizes the main properties
\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

Lemma 69
.

For
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
the following hold:

a)

{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
is odd in
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
with respect to the total degree.

b)

{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
is
C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)
-invariant.

c)

For
g\in L_{\bar{0}}
, one has
\operatorname{Ad}_{g}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})=\operatorname{D}^{\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(x)}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

d)

({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
.

Proof.

a) Both
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))
have total degree
\bar{1}
, hence so does
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
.
b) Let
y\in C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)
. Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariant, it commutes with
y
under the embedding
{\mathfrak{l}}\hookrightarrow{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
.
Moreover,

[\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x),\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(y)]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}([x,y]_{{\mathfrak{g}}})=0,

so
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
is
C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)
-invariant.
c) This follows from
\operatorname{Ad}
-equivariance of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
and of
j\circ\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}
.
d) Since
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
is odd,

({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2}=\tfrac{1}{2}[{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}},{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=\tfrac{1}{2}[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}},\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}]_{{\mathcal{W}}}+[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}},j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))]_{{\mathcal{W}}}+\tfrac{1}{2}[j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)),j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))]_{{\mathcal{W}}}.

The middle term vanishes by
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariance of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
, and the last term is zero by
Remark
68
. Hence
({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
.
∎

Fix a highest weight simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
such that we can consider the family

(5.15)

{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\to\operatorname{End}(M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})),\qquad x\mapsto{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}.

Recall that we assigned to any morphism
T
of a super vector space
V
a cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}(T;V)
(Section
3.5
), which yields the Dirac cohomology
{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
for
T=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
V=M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
. Analogously, we define

(5.16)

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)\coloneqq{\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}))=\ker{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}/(\ker{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}\cap\operatorname{im}{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}).

Our goal is to describe this cohomology. First, by Lemma
69
, we have the following lemma

Lemma 70
.

For any
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
is
{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)
is a
C_{{\mathfrak{l}}}(x)
-supermodule.

Computing the cohomology on
M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
reduces to the Laplacian

\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
. We need:

Lemma 71
.

The following hold:

a)

M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})\cong\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\oplus\operatorname{im}\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

b)

{\operatorname{H}}_{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\coloneqq{\operatorname{H}}(\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}};\,M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}))=\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

Proof.

Part a) is proved in
[
23
, Proposition 4.1.2]
. Part b) follows immediately from a).
∎

Lemma 72
.

One has

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,\ker({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2})={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2})={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,{\operatorname{H}}_{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)).

Proof.

By definition,
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}+j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))
, and Lemma
69
gives
({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2}=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}=\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Together with Lemma
71
, this yields

M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})\cong\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\oplus\operatorname{im}\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}.

Decompose
M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
into eigenspaces of
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Since
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
commutes with
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
and with

j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))
, it commutes with
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
, hence
\ker{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
,
\operatorname{im}{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}
, and
{\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}))
split as direct sums over these eigenspaces.

Let
\lambda\neq 0
and let
V_{\lambda}
be the
\lambda
-eigenspace of
\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
. It suffices to show that

\ker({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}|_{V_{\lambda}})\subset\operatorname{im}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}|_{V_{\lambda}})
. Take
v\in V_{\lambda}
with
{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}v=0
. Then

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v=-j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))v
, and hence

\begin{split}{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}\,\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v&=(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}+j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x)))\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v\\
&=\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}v+j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v\\
&=\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v-j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))^{2}v\\
&=2\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v=2\lambda v,\end{split}

where we used
j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))^{2}=0
and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))=-j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
(both are odd).
Thus
v=\frac{1}{2\lambda}{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}v)\in\operatorname{im}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}|_{V_{\lambda}})
, proving the claim. Therefore only the

\lambda=0
eigenspace contributes,
i.e.
,
\ker({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}})^{2}=\ker\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}={\operatorname{H}}_{\Updelta_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
, and the stated equalities follow.
∎

For a unitarizable highest weight supermodule
M
(Section
2.4
), the previous lemma yields an explicit description of
{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)

in terms of Dirac cohomology (Section
3.5
) and Duflo–Serganova cohomology
(Section
5.1
). Recall
\upeta_{x}
from (
5.9
) and its dual injective map

\upeta^{*}_{x}:\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}_{x}),{\mathbb{C}})\to\operatorname{Hom}(\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}}),{\mathbb{C}})
for
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. Also let

\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}:{\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})}\to\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{l}})
be the homomorphism from Theorem
42
. We then have:

Theorem 73
.

Assume
M
is a unitarizable highest weight supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then for any
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
one has

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)=\operatorname{DS}_{x}({\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)).

In particular,
{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)
is a
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
-supermodule and decomposes as a direct sum of
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
-supermodules. If
V
is a
{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}
-supermodule with infinitesimal character
\chi_{\nu}^{{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}}
, then

\chi_{\nu}^{{\mathfrak{l}}_{x}}\in(\upeta_{x}^{\ast})^{-1}(\chi_{\Lambda}\circ\eta_{{\mathfrak{l}}}).

Proof.

If
M
is unitarizable, then
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
by Lemma
43
and

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
by unitarity. Hence, by
Lemma
72
,

\begin{split}{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)&={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}}))={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}};\,\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2})={\operatorname{H}}(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}+j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x));\,\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})\\
&={\operatorname{H}}(j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x));\,\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}).\end{split}

The action of
{\mathfrak{l}}
on
M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
is induced by its embedding into
{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
via
\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}
. Hence, by the embedding (
3.28
), one has

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)=\operatorname{DS}_{x}(\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}})=\operatorname{DS}_{x}\bigl({\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\bigr).

This proves the first part of the theorem. The remaining claims follow by combining Theorem
42
with Theorem
65
.
∎

Remark 74
.

The proof of Theorem
73
relies on the identity

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2},

which holds for unitarizable
M
. In general, this is equivalent to each of the following conditions:

(a)

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
;

(b)

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\cap\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}=\{0\}
;

(c)

M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
;

(d)

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
.

Thus, the assumption is equivalent both to the direct sum decomposition in c) and to the identification of Dirac cohomology with the full kernel in d).

The following corollary is immediate.

Corollary 75
.

Let
M
be a unitarizable highest weight supermodule with highest weight
\Lambda
. Then:

a)

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)=0
if
{\operatorname{rk}}(x)>{\operatorname{at}}(\Lambda)
.

b)

For
g\in L_{\bar{0}}
, there is a canonical isomorphism

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)\cong{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{\!\operatorname{Ad}_{g}(x)}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M).

If
M
is not unitarizable, we can only formulate a vanishing conjecture. By Lemma
72
,

{\operatorname{H}}_{{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}}}(M)={\operatorname{H}}({\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{x}},\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}).

Moreover, on
V\coloneqq\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
, both
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
and
j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))
are anti-commuting differentials, since

[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}},j(\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(x))]_{{\mathcal{W}}}=0.

This suggests the following vanishing statement from the perspective of spectral sequences.

Conjecture 76
.

Let
x\in{\mathcal{Y}}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
. If
\operatorname{DS}_{x}\bigl({\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)\bigr)=0
, then

{\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}^{x}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)=0.

6.
Bismut–Quillen’s Superconnection

We perturb the relative cubic Dirac operator by the covariant Weil differential associated to a finite-dimensional (super)module
M
, obtained from the universal connection
1
-form of the colour Weil algebra. The resulting operator defines an element of
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}({\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}\otimes E)
, where
E\coloneqq M\otimes S
, and admits a well-defined exponential after completing
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. Taking the relative supertrace yields a cohomology class in the completed colour Weil algebra, thereby assigning to each finite-dimensional module a canonical class. We first consider reductive complex Lie algebras, and then adapt the construction to finite-dimensional supermodules over basic classical Lie superalgebras. Moreover, Section
6.1.4
contains an explicit example of the construction in the case of the complex simple Lie algebra
\mathfrak{sl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
.

6.1.
Semisimple Complex Lie Algebra

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a finite-dimensional semisimple complex Lie algebra with Killing form
B
. In the following, we use
B
to identify
{\mathfrak{g}}
and
{\mathfrak{g}}^{\ast}
. Fix a Cartan subalgebra
\mathfrak{h}\subset{\mathfrak{g}}
with root system
\Delta
, and choose a positive system
\Delta^{+}
.
This yields a triangular decomposition

(6.1)

{\mathfrak{g}}=\mathfrak{n}^{-}\oplus\mathfrak{h}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{+},\qquad\mathfrak{n}^{\pm}\coloneqq\bigoplus_{\alpha\in\Delta^{+}}{\mathfrak{g}}^{\pm\alpha}.

Fix a basis
\{e_{a}\}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
and its
B
-dual basis
\{e^{a}\}
. We consider the quantum Weil algebra
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
, and fix the tensor product generators (
cf.
Remark
23
)

(6.2)

u^{a}\coloneqq e^{a}\otimes 1\in\mathcal{W}^{2}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\theta^{a}\coloneqq 1\otimes e^{a}\in\mathcal{W}^{1}({\mathfrak{g}}),

where the
u^{a}
are even and the
\theta^{a}
are odd. We define the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-valued quantum Weil algebra
to be
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
, and we equip it with the Lie bracket

(6.3)

[A,B]\coloneqq\sum_{i,j}(a_{i}b_{j})\otimes[x_{i},y_{j}]_{{\mathfrak{g}}},

where
A=\sum_{i}a_{i}\otimes x_{i},B=\sum_{j}b_{j}\otimes y_{j}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
.
We regard
{\mathfrak{g}}
as purely even; this induces a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading on

\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}
from
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
.

Henceforth, let
(\rho_{M},M)
be a fixed finite-dimensional simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module; when
M
is clear from context, we suppress
\rho_{M}
.
Recall the spin module
S
from Section
3.4
. Note that
S\coloneqq\bigwedge\mathfrak{n}^{-}
carries a natural
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading

S=S_{\bar{0}}\oplus S_{\bar{1}}
, making it a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule,
i.e.
, the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-action preserves the grading.
Set

(6.4)

E\coloneqq M\otimes S.

Equipped with the induced grading from
S
, the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
(\rho_{E},E)
becomes a
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule.

Recall that
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
is
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded. Using the
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-graded tensor product
\otimes
, the space
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E
is a super vector space. Hence
\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E)
inherits a
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading: its even part consists of parity-preserving endomorphisms and its odd part of parity-reversing endomorphisms. With the canonical supercommutator
[\cdot,\cdot]_{\operatorname{End}}
, it is a Lie superalgebra.

We have moreover a natural action of
{\mathfrak{g}}
on
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E
:

(6.5)

\upalpha(x)(v\otimes w)\coloneqq L_{x}v\otimes w+v\otimes\rho_{E}(x)w,\qquad x\in{\mathfrak{g}},\ v\otimes w\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E,

where
L_{x}
denotes the Lie derivative (
3.21
) on
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
.

6.1.1.
The Weil Covariant Differential

The Bismut–Quillen superconnection may be viewed as a perturbation of the cubic Dirac operator by the
Weil covariant differential

\nabla^{M}
associated with
M
, which we now define.

The
universal connection
1
-form
is the odd element

(6.6)

\Theta\coloneqq\sum_{a}\theta^{a}\otimes e_{a}\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\mathfrak{g}}.

It is independent of the choice of basis. Intrinsically,
\Theta
is the canonical element
corresponding to
\operatorname{id}_{\mathfrak{g}}
under the identification

\mathfrak{g}^{*}\otimes\mathfrak{g}\simeq\operatorname{End}(\mathfrak{g})
, viewed in

\mathcal{W}^{1}(\mathfrak{g})\otimes\mathfrak{g}
. Moreover, it is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariant for the diagonal action
L_{x}
on the first factor and
\operatorname{ad}_{x}
on the second factor.

Lemma 77
.

One has for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}

(L_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{x})\Theta=0,\qquad(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)\Theta=x.

Proof.

We have by definition

\displaystyle(L_{x}\otimes 1)\Theta

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}L_{x}\theta^{a}\otimes e_{a}=\sum_{a}(1\otimes[x,e^{a}])\otimes e_{a}=\sum_{a}(1\otimes\sum_{b}B([x,e^{a}],e_{b})e^{b})\otimes e_{a}

\displaystyle=\sum_{b}\theta^{b}\otimes\sum_{a}B([x,e^{a}],e_{b})e_{a}=-\sum_{b}\theta^{b}\otimes\sum_{a}B([x,e_{b}],e^{a})e_{a}=-(1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{x})\Theta,

where we used invariance of
B
, that is,
B([x,e^{a}],e_{b})=B(x,[e^{a},e_{b}])=-B(x,[e_{b},e^{a}])=-B([x,e_{b}],e^{a})
. The second property
(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)\Theta=x
follows by definition.
∎

Furthermore,
\Theta
satisfies a Maurer–Cartan curvature type equation. The following lemma is a straightforward computation.

Lemma 78
.

One has

(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)\Theta+\tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta,\Theta]=\sum_{a}u^{a}\otimes e_{a}.

The universal connection
1
-form
\Theta
induces a
\mathbb{C}
-linear endomorphism

(6.7)

\Theta^{M}\coloneqq\sum_{a}\theta^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}(e_{a})\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E),

acting on
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E
componentwise.

Lemma 79
.

The following hold:

a)

\Theta^{M}\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E)_{\bar{1}}
.

b)

\Theta^{M}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-equivariant, that is,
[\upalpha(x),\Theta^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

c)

(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)\Theta^{M}+\tfrac{1}{2}[\Theta^{M},\Theta^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}=F^{M}
with
F^{M}\coloneqq\sum_{a}u^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}(e_{a})
.

d)

(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)\Theta^{M}=\rho_{E}(x)
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

Proof.

a) follows from the definition of the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-grading. For b), a direct calculation gives

[\upalpha(x),\Theta^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}=\sum_{a}L_{x}\theta^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}(e_{a})+\sum_{a}\theta^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}([x,e_{a}]).

Using Lemma
77
and applying
\rho_{E}
to the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-factor, one gets

\sum_{a}(L_{x}\theta^{a})\otimes\rho_{E}(e_{a})+\sum_{a}\theta^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}([x,e_{a}])=0,

hence b) follows.
Finally, c) and d) follow from Lemma
77
and Lemma
78
by applying
1\otimes\rho_{E}
.
∎

Let
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
be the cubic Dirac operator of
{\mathfrak{g}}
, and let
d^{\mathcal{W}}\coloneqq[\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\cdot]
denote the associated Weil differential on
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
. The
Weil covariant differential
combines both,
\Theta^{M}
and
d^{\mathcal{W}}
into a single operator acting on
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E
.

Definition 80
.

The
Weil covariant differential
is

\nabla^{M}\coloneqq d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1+\Theta^{M}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E).

The following lemma summarizes the main properties of
\nabla^{M}
.

Lemma 81
.

The following hold:

a)

\nabla^{M}\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E)_{\bar{1}}
.

b)

\nabla^{M}
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-equivariant, that is,
[\upalpha(x),\nabla^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

c)

\nabla^{M}
and
1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
anti-commute, that is,
[\nabla^{M},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
.

d)

(\nabla^{M})^{2}=F^{M}
with
F^{M}\coloneqq\sum_{a}u^{a}\otimes\rho_{E}(e_{a})
.

e)

For any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
one has

(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)\nabla^{M}=-d^{\mathcal{W}}(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)+L_{x}\otimes 1+1\otimes\rho_{E}(x).

Proof.

a) follows from Lemma
28
, the definition of
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
in Section
3.3.1
and Lemma
79
. Next, b) follows if we show that
[\upalpha(x),d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1]_{\operatorname{End}}
=0 since
[\upalpha(x),\Theta^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
by Lemma
79
. However, by Lemma
35
, one has

[\upalpha(x),d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1]_{\operatorname{End}}=(L_{x}d^{\mathcal{W}}-(-1)^{p(x)}d^{{\mathcal{W}}}L_{x})\otimes 1=0.

This proves b). For c), we compute

\displaystyle{}_{\operatorname{End}}=[d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1,1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}+[\Theta^{M},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}=\sum_{a}\theta^{a}\otimes[\rho_{E}(e_{a}),\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]=0

by the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariance of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
. Next, we prove d). Since
\nabla^{M},d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1
and
\Theta^{M}
are odd, one has

\begin{split}(\nabla^{M})^{2}&=\tfrac{1}{2}[\nabla^{M},\nabla^{M}]\\
&=(d^{\mathcal{W}})^{2}\otimes 1+\Theta^{M}(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)+(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)\Theta^{M}+(\Theta^{M})^{2}\\
&=\Theta^{M}(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)+(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)\Theta^{M}+(\Theta^{M})^{2},\end{split}

where we used
(d^{\mathcal{W}})^{2}=0
.
The statement follows by computing the three appearing summands on any elementary tensor
w\otimes v
by linearity since the action is componentwise. One has:

\displaystyle(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)\Theta^{M}(w\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}d^{\mathcal{W}}(\theta^{a}w)\otimes v=\sum_{a}((d^{\mathcal{W}}\theta^{a})w-\theta^{a}d^{\mathcal{W}}w)\otimes e_{a}v

\displaystyle\Theta^{M}(d^{\mathcal{W}}\otimes 1)(w\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a}(\theta^{a}d^{\mathcal{W}}w)\otimes e_{a}v,

\displaystyle(\Theta^{M})^{2}(w\otimes v)

\displaystyle=\sum_{a,b}\theta^{a}\theta^{b}w\otimes e_{a}e_{b}v=\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{a,b}\theta^{a}\theta^{b}w\otimes[e_{a},e_{b}]v

Now, the statement follows by using

d^{\mathcal{W}}\theta^{a}=u^{a}-\tfrac{1}{2}\sum_{b,c}B([e_{b},e_{c}],e^{a})\theta^{b}\theta^{c}.

Finally, e) follows by
[\iota_{x},d^{\mathcal{W}}]_{\mathcal{W}}=\iota_{x}d^{\mathcal{W}}+d^{\mathcal{W}}\iota_{x}=L_{x}
and Lemma
79
.
∎

Remark 82
.

The properties of
\nabla^{M}
justify the name covariant Weil differential.

6.1.2.
Bismut–Quillen Superconnection

The Bismut–Quillen superconnection couples the cubic Dirac operator acting on
E
to the quantum Weil algebra
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
using the Weil covariant differential.

Definition 83
.

The
Bismut–Quillen superconnection
associated with
M
is

\mathbb{A}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{M}(t)\coloneqq\nabla^{M}+\sqrt{t}(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}})\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E),\qquad t>0.

The following proposition collects important properties of the Bismut–Quillen superconnection.

Proposition 84
.

The following hold:

a)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E)_{\bar{1}}
.

b)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)
is
{\mathfrak{g}}
-equivariant, that is,
[\upalpha(x),\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
.

c)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)
has square

(\mathbb{A}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{\!M}(t))^{2}=F^{M}+t(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}).

Proof.

Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
is an odd operator on
E
and
\nabla^{M}
is odd, the Bismut–Quillen superconnection
\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)
is odd. This proves a). Statement b) is a direct consequence of the
{\mathfrak{g}}
-invariance of
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and Lemma
81
. It remains to compute the square. Since
\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t),1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
\nabla^{M}
are odd, one has

\displaystyle(\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t))^{2}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}[\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t),\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}

\displaystyle=\tfrac{1}{2}[\nabla^{M},\nabla^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}+\tfrac{\sqrt{t}}{2}[\nabla^{M},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}+\tfrac{\sqrt{t}}{2}[1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},\nabla^{M}]_{\operatorname{End}}+\tfrac{t}{2}[1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}

\displaystyle=(\nabla^{M})^{2}+\sqrt{t}[\nabla^{M},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}+t(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2})

\displaystyle=F^{M}+t(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{2}),

where we used in the last equality that
[\nabla^{M},1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
and
F^{M}\coloneqq(\nabla^{M})^{2}
.
This finishes the proof.
∎

Next, we refine the definition by involving certain quadratic subalgebras yielding a relative version of the Bismut–Quillen superconnection. As in Section
3.3
, fix a quadratic Lie subalgebra
{\mathfrak{l}}
of
{\mathfrak{g}}
with non-degenerate supersymmetric invariant bilinear form
B_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\coloneqq B|_{{\mathfrak{l}}}
such that we have an orthogonal decomposition

(6.8)

{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}},\qquad{\mathfrak{p}}={\mathfrak{l}}^{\perp},

with respect to
B
. Moreover, assume
\mathfrak{h}\subset{\mathfrak{l}}
. Let
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}\in{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{l}})
denote the associated cubic Dirac operator.

Any
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module is in particular a
{\mathfrak{l}}
-module. Under the natural embedding
j:\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{l}})\hookrightarrow\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})
defined in (
3.28
), we consider
j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}})
as an endomorphism of
E
.

Definition 85
.

The
{\mathfrak{l}}
-relative Bismut–Quillen superconnection
is

\mathbb{A}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)\coloneqq\mathbb{A}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}^{\!M}(t)-\sqrt{t}(1\otimes j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}}))=\nabla^{M}+\sqrt{t}(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}).

Recall that the space of
{\mathfrak{l}}
-basic elements is the subalgebra
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
of elements annihilated by
L_{x}
and
\iota_{x}
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
. Inside
\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E
, we consider the space of
{\mathfrak{l}}
-invariant elements

(6.9)

(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)_{\text{basic}}\coloneqq\{v\otimes w\in\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E:\upalpha(x)(v\otimes w)=0\ \text{for all}\ x\in{\mathfrak{l}}\}.

The
{\mathfrak{l}}
-relative Bismut–Quillen superconnection
\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
has the following properties:

Proposition 86
.

The following hold:

a)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}(\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes E)_{\bar{1}}
.

b)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
is
{\mathfrak{l}}
-equivariant, that is,
[\upalpha(x),\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
.

c)

{\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
restricts to an endomorphism of
({\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)_{\text{basic}}
.

d)

\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
has square

(\mathbb{A}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}=F^{M}+t(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}).

Proof.

a) follows from Lemma
81
and Proposition
84
and b) follows from Lemma
28
and Proposition
84
. Moreover, c) is a direct consequence of b). Finally, d) follows by a similar line of argument as in Proposition
84
using that
\nabla^{M}
,
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}}}
and
j(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{l}}})
anti-commute.
∎

6.1.3.
Chern Character-Type Invariant

Using the Bismut–Quillen superconnection, we assign to any finite-dimensional module
M
over a complex semisimple Lie algebra
{\mathfrak{g}}
a cohomology class. We use the notation from above.

We first introduce a suitable completion of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
. Consider the canonical
embedding
\iota:{\mathfrak{g}}\hookrightarrow{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. There exists a unique unital algebra
morphism
\epsilon:{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\to{\mathbb{C}}
, the
augmentation
, satisfying

\epsilon(\iota(x))=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
. Its kernel

I\coloneqq\ker(\epsilon)={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}}){\mathfrak{g}}

is the
augmentation ideal
of
{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. For
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}={\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, define

\epsilon_{{\mathcal{W}}}\coloneqq\epsilon\otimes\operatorname{id}_{{\operatorname{Cl}}}:{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}\to{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
and set

J\coloneqq\ker(\epsilon_{{\mathcal{W}}})
. Then
J=I\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. For
n\geq 1
, let
J^{n}

denote the
n
-th power of the two-sided ideal
J
; explicitly,

J^{n}=I^{n}\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}})
for all
n\geq 1
.

The
J
-adic completion of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
is defined as the projective limit

(6.10)

\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}/J^{n},

where the transition maps are induced by the canonical surjections

{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}/J^{n+1}\to{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}/J^{n}
. In particular,

(6.11)

\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\cong\widehat{{\mathfrak{U}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\otimes{\operatorname{Cl}}({\mathfrak{g}}),\qquad\widehat{{\mathfrak{U}}}({\mathfrak{g}})\coloneqq\varprojlim_{n\geq 1}{\mathfrak{U}}({\mathfrak{g}})/I^{n}.

The differential
d^{{\mathcal{W}}}
of
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
naturally induces a differential on
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
, denoted by the same symbol, since it preserves the
J
-adic filtration. We denote by
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
the subalgebra of
{\mathfrak{l}}
-basic elements.

For any
x\in{\mathfrak{g}}
, identified with its image in
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
, the exponential series
\exp(x)\coloneqq\sum_{n\geq 0}\frac{x^{n}}{n!}

converges in
\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}})
. Since
E
is finite-dimensional, algebraic
exponentials of endomorphisms of
E
are well-defined. Hence:

Lemma 87
.

One has

e^{-(\mathbb{A}^{M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{k}\bigl((\mathbb{A}^{M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}\bigr)^{k}}{k!}\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}\bigl((\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)_{\mathrm{basic}}\bigr).

Since
E
is finite-dimensional, we can form the relative supertrace
\operatorname{str}_{E}
.

Lemma 88
.

One has

\operatorname{str}_{E}(e^{-{\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)^{2}})\in\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}).

Proof.

Consider a general element
T\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}((\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)_{\text{basic}})
. In particular,
(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)T=0
and
\upalpha(x)T=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
. We show that
\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)\in\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
, that is, it suffices to prove that
\iota_{x}\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)=0
and
L_{x}\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)=0
for all
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
. Then the statement follows.

Fix
x\in{\mathfrak{l}}
. By linearity, it suffices to assume that
T
is a simple tensor of the form
T=v\otimes w
. Then, using
(\iota_{x}\otimes 1)T=0
, one has

\iota_{x}\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)=\iota_{x}(v\operatorname{str}_{E}(w))=(\iota_{x}v)\operatorname{str}_{E}(w)=\operatorname{str}_{E}((\iota_{x}v)\otimes w)=\operatorname{str}_{E}((\iota_{x}\otimes 1)T)=0.

Next, using
\upalpha(x)T=0
, one has

\operatorname{str}_{E}((L_{x}\otimes 1)T)=-\operatorname{str}_{E}((1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{\rho_{E}(x)})T).

Consequently,

L_{x}\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)=(L_{x}v)\operatorname{str}_{E}(w)=\operatorname{str}_{E}((L_{x}\otimes 1)T)=-\operatorname{str}_{E}((1\otimes\operatorname{ad}_{\rho_{E}(x)})T)=v\operatorname{str}_{E}([\rho_{E}(x),w])=0.

This finishes the proof.
∎

This leads to the following definition:

(6.12)

\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t):=\operatorname{str}_{E}\bigl(e^{-({\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}}\bigr)\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}).

In general,
\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)
is non-zero, although
\operatorname{str}_{E}(e^{-\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}})=0
(
cf.
Section
6.1.4
). Since
E
is finite-dimensional,
\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)
is well defined for every
t>0
by Proposition
86
and Lemma
87
. We will show that
\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)
is independent of
t
and determines a class in

(6.13)

{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})\cong\widehat{\mathfrak{Z}}({\mathfrak{l}}),

where
\widehat{\mathfrak{Z}}({\mathfrak{l}})
denotes the completion of the center of
\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{l}})
.

Lemma 89
.

For any
T\in\operatorname{End}_{\mathbb{C}}((\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})\otimes E)_{\text{basic}})
one has

d^{\mathcal{W_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}}\operatorname{str}_{E}(T)=\operatorname{str}_{E}([\nabla^{M},T]_{\operatorname{End}})

Proof.

By linearity of
\operatorname{str}_{E}
, it suffices to consider
T=v\otimes w
for
v\in\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}})
and
w\in\operatorname{End}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(E)
. Then

\displaystyle\operatorname{str}_{E}([\nabla^{M},v\otimes w]_{\operatorname{End}})

\displaystyle=\operatorname{str}_{E}(d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}v\otimes w+\sum_{a}(\theta^{a}v\otimes[\rho_{E}(e_{a}),w]_{\operatorname{End}}))

\displaystyle=(d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}v)\operatorname{str}_{E}(w)+\sum_{a}(\theta^{a}v)\operatorname{str}_{E}([\rho_{E}(e_{a}),w]_{\operatorname{End}})

\displaystyle=(d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}v)\operatorname{str}_{E}(w),

since
\operatorname{str}_{E}([\rho_{E}(e_{a}),w])=0
for all
a
by cyclicity of the supertrace.
∎

Lemma 90
.

For all
t>0
, one has

d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)=0.

Proof.

By Lemma
89
, one has

d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\operatorname{str}_{E}(e^{-({\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}})=\operatorname{str}_{E}([\nabla^{M},e^{-({\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t))^{2}}]_{\operatorname{End}})=\operatorname{str}_{E}(0)=0

since
\nabla^{M}
commutes with
(\nabla^{M})^{2}
and
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
, and
{\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)^{2}=(\nabla^{M})^{2}+t(1\otimes\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2})
by Proposition
86
.
∎

Theorem 91
.

For any finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module
M
one has

[\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)]\in{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}).

Moreover, the class
[\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)]
is independent of
t
.

Proof.

By
{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})={\operatorname{H}}^{0}(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})\cong\widehat{\mathfrak{Z}}(\mathfrak{U}({\mathfrak{l}}))
, and Lemma
90
, it follows that
[\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)]\in H(\widehat{\mathcal{W}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})
. It remains to show that it is independent of
t
.

For this purpose, we consider
{\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
as a smooth family of endomorphisms in the variable
t
. Then, using that
{\mathbb{A}}^{\!M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
is odd for all
t
, one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)^{2})={\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)+\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t){\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)=[{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}

and thus

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2})e^{-(1-s)({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}\mathrm{d}s

\displaystyle=-\int_{0}^{1}e^{-s({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}[{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}e^{-(1-s)({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}\mathrm{d}s.

Applying the supertrace and using
[{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t),e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}]_{\operatorname{End}}=0
, one has

\displaystyle\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\operatorname{ch}_{M}(t)

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{str}_{E}([{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t)]_{\operatorname{End}}e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{str}_{E}([{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t),\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}]_{\operatorname{End}})

\displaystyle=-\operatorname{str}_{E}([\nabla^{M},(\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}]_{\operatorname{End}})

\displaystyle=-d^{\mathcal{W}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\operatorname{str}_{E}((\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}{\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))e^{-({\mathbb{A}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{M}(t))^{2}}).

This finishes the proof.
∎

Altogether, we have assigned to each finite-dimensional simple module a cohomology class in the colour quantum Weil algebra, which can be seen as a coarse Chern–Weil type invariant of
{\mathfrak{g}}
-modules. We close this section with an example.

6.1.4.
Example:
\mathfrak{sl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
and the Bismut–Quillen Superconnection

We consider
{\mathfrak{g}}\coloneqq\mathfrak{sl}(2,{\mathbb{C}})
. Fix a standard
{\mathfrak{g}}
-triple
(e,f,h)
with

(6.14)

[h,e]=2e,\qquad[h,f]=-2f,\qquad[e,f]=h.

Let
{\mathfrak{h}}\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}}h
be the Cartan subalgebra and let
B(X,Y)\coloneqq\operatorname{tr}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\operatorname{ad}_{X}\operatorname{ad}_{Y})
be the Killing form (standard normalization). Set

{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}}e
and
{\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\coloneqq{\mathbb{C}}f
, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{n}}^{-}\oplus{\mathfrak{h}}\oplus{\mathfrak{n}}^{+}
.

For
n\in{\mathbb{Z}}_{\geq 0}
, let
V_{n}
be the simple
{\mathfrak{g}}
-module of highest weight
n
. It has basis

v_{0},\dots,v_{n}
with action

hv_{k}=(n-2k)v_{k},\qquad fv_{k}=\begin{cases}v_{k+1},&0\leq k<n,\\
0,&k=n,\end{cases}\qquad ev_{k}=\begin{cases}0,&k=0,\\
k(n-k+1)v_{k-1},&1\leq k\leq n.\end{cases}

Thus the weights are
n,n-2,\dots,-n
, each with multiplicity one. The center
\mathfrak{Z}({\mathfrak{g}})
of the universal enveloping algebra is generated by the quadratic Casimir element

(6.15)

\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}=ef+fe+\tfrac{1}{2}h^{2},

which acts on
V_{n}
by the scalar
\tfrac{n(n+2)}{2}
.

Let
S:=\bigwedge\mathfrak{n}^{-}
be the
\rho
-shifted spin module for the Clifford algebra of
\mathfrak{p}:=\mathfrak{n}^{+}\oplus\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, with respect to the pairing induced by
B
. It is generated by
s_{\bar{0}}:=1\in\bigwedge^{0}\mathfrak{n}^{-}
and
s_{\bar{1}}:=f\in\bigwedge^{1}\mathfrak{n}^{-}
, of parity
\bar{0}
and
\bar{1}
, respectively. The action of
\mathfrak{g}
is given by

e\cdot s_{\bar{0}}=s_{\bar{1}},\qquad e\cdot s_{\bar{1}}=0,\qquad f\cdot s_{\bar{0}}=0,\qquad f\cdot s_{\bar{1}}=s_{\bar{0}},\qquad h\cdot s_{\bar{0}}=s_{\bar{0}},\qquad h\cdot s_{\bar{1}}=-s_{\bar{1}}.

Hence
S_{\bar{0}}
has weight
-\rho
and
S_{\bar{1}}
has weight
\rho
. Therefore
S
is the simple
\mathfrak{g}
-module of highest weight
\rho
, that is,
S\cong V_{1}
. Its
{\mathbb{Z}}_{2}
-grading is
S_{\bar{0}}=\bigwedge^{0}\mathfrak{n}^{-}={\mathbb{C}}s_{\bar{0}}
and
S_{\bar{1}}=\bigwedge^{1}\mathfrak{n}^{-}={\mathbb{C}}s_{\bar{1}}
.

As a quadratic subalgebra we take
{\mathfrak{l}}\coloneqq{\mathfrak{h}}
. It has quadratic Casimir
\Omega_{{\mathfrak{h}}}=\tfrac{1}{8}h^{2}
. It acts on a one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
(where
h
acts by
\lambda
) by
\tfrac{\lambda^{2}}{8}
.

As an
{\mathfrak{h}}
-supermodule,

V_{n}\otimes S
decomposes into one-dimensional weight spaces, with parity determined by
the
S
-factor:

(6.16)

(V_{n}\otimes S)_{\bar{0}}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}\,(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{0}}),\qquad(V_{n}\otimes S)_{\bar{1}}=\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}\,(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{1}}),

and

(6.17)

h(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{0}})=(n-2k+1)(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{0}}),\qquad h(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{1}})=(n-2k-1)(v_{k}\otimes s_{\bar{1}}).

Equivalently, writing
{\mathbb{C}}_{\lambda}
for the one-dimensional
{\mathfrak{h}}
-module of weight
\lambda
,

(6.18)

V_{n}\otimes S\cong\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}_{\,n-2k+1}\right)_{\bar{0}}\oplus\left(\bigoplus_{k=0}^{n}{\mathbb{C}}_{\,n-2k-1}\right)_{\bar{1}}.

Thus the odd weights are
n-1,n-3,\dots,-n-1
, while the even weights are

n+1,n-1,\dots,-n+1
.

Let
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}
be the
{\mathfrak{h}}
-relative cubic Dirac operator. It squares to

(6.19)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}=\Omega_{{\mathfrak{g}}}\otimes 1-\frac{1}{8}(h\otimes 1+1\otimes\gamma^{{\mathcal{W}}}(h))-\frac{1}{8}(1\otimes 1).

Consequently, if
{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu}
is a
{\mathfrak{h}}
-weight space of
V_{n}\otimes S
, it acts as scalar multiplication by

(6.20)

\frac{(n+1)^{2}-\mu^{2}}{8}.

We conclude that
{\mathbb{C}}_{\mu}
belongs to
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
iff
\mu=\pm(n+1)
. Because each finite-dimensional highest weight module
V_{n}
carries a
\mathfrak{su}(2)
-invariant positive Hermitian form (so the compact real form acts by skew-Hermitian operators), the basis-independent cubic Dirac operator
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}
is therefore selfadjoint, and for a selfadjoint operator one has
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
. Hence

(6.21)

\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}={\mathbb{C}}_{n+1}\oplus{\mathbb{C}}_{-(n+1)}.

Note that
{\mathbb{C}}_{n+1}
is spanned by
v_{0}\otimes s_{\bar{0}}
and
{\mathbb{C}}_{-(n+1)}
is spanned by
v_{n}\otimes s_{\bar{1}}
, that is,
{\mathbb{C}}_{n+1}
is even while
{\mathbb{C}}_{-(n+1)}
is odd. In particular,
\operatorname{sdim}_{{\mathbb{C}}}(\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}})=1-1=0
.

A naive construction of an invariant would be

(6.22)

\operatorname{str}_{V_{n}\otimes S}(e^{-\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},\mathfrak{h}}^{2}}).

However, this is always trivial. Indeed, by Lemma
71
, we have

V_{n}\otimes S\cong\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}\oplus\operatorname{im}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
, and since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
is positive,
all its eigenvalues are positive. Let
\lambda>0
be an eigenvalue, and set
E\coloneqq V_{n}\otimes S
. Then

(6.23)

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}:\ker(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}-\lambda)\cap E_{\bar{0}}\to\ker(\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}-\lambda)\cap E_{\bar{1}}

defines an isomorphism. We conclude

(6.24)

\operatorname{str}_{E}(e^{-\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}})=\operatorname{str}_{E}(\operatorname{id}_{\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}})=\operatorname{sdim}({\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}})=1-1=0.

If, instead, we couple
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}
to the covariant Weil differential coming from the universal connection
1
-form, the resulting class is non-trivial.

For the standard triple
\{e,f,h\}
, denote the corresponding even and odd
generators in
{\mathcal{W}({\mathfrak{g}})}
by
u^{e},u^{f},u^{h}
and

\theta^{e},\theta^{f},\theta^{h}
. Then

(6.25)

\nabla^{V_{n}}=\tfrac{1}{4}\theta^{e}\otimes\rho_{E}(f)+\tfrac{1}{4}\theta^{f}\otimes\rho_{E}(e)+\tfrac{1}{8}\theta^{h}\otimes\rho_{E}(h),\quad F^{V_{n}}=\tfrac{1}{4}u^{e}\otimes\rho_{E}(f)+\tfrac{1}{4}u^{f}\otimes\rho_{E}(e)+\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}\otimes\rho_{E}(h).

The
{\mathfrak{g}}
-action on
E
is given by

(6.26)

\rho_{E}(e)=\rho_{V_{n}}(e)\otimes 1,\quad\rho_{E}(f)=\rho_{V_{n}}(f)\otimes 1,\quad\rho_{E}(h)=\rho_{V_{n}}(h)\otimes 1+1\otimes\gamma^{\prime}(h),

where the last formula is specified in (
6.17
).

If
n=0
, so that
V_{0}\cong{\mathbb{C}}
is trivial, then

\rho_{V_{0}}(e)=\rho_{V_{0}}(f)=\rho_{V_{0}}(h)=0
, hence

(6.27)

\nabla^{V_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}\otimes\rho_{E}(h),\quad F^{V_{0}}=\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}\otimes\rho_{E}(h),\quad\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}=0,

and therefore

(6.28)

\operatorname{ch}_{V_{0}}(t)=\operatorname{str}_{S}(e^{-F^{V_{0}}}).

Using
\rho_{E}(h)s_{\bar{0}}=s_{\bar{0}}
and

\rho_{E}(h)s_{\bar{1}}=-s_{\bar{1}}
, one obtains

(6.29)

\operatorname{ch}_{V_{0}}(t)=e^{\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}}-e^{-\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}}=2\sinh\!\bigl(\tfrac{1}{8}u^{h}\bigr)\in\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),

which defines a class in

{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[[h]]
.

For
n\geq 1
, equip
V_{n}
with a Hermitian form such that

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}
is selfadjoint and

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
is non-negative. Decomposing
E
into

\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}
-eigenspaces
E(\lambda)
gives

(6.30)

e^{-t\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}}\xrightarrow{t\to\infty}\begin{cases}1,&\lambda=0,\\
0,&\lambda\neq 0.\end{cases}

Since
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}
and
\nabla^{V_{n}}
commute and
[\operatorname{ch}_{V_{n}}(t)]
is independent of
t
,

(6.31)

[\operatorname{ch}_{V_{n}}(t)]=[\operatorname{str}_{E}(e^{-F^{V_{n}}}e^{-t\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}^{2}}]=[\operatorname{str}_{\operatorname{ker}\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}}\bigl(e^{-F^{V_{n}}}\bigr)],

and by (
6.21
),

(6.32)

[\operatorname{ch}_{V_{n}}(t)]=\bigl[e^{\tfrac{n+1}{8}u^{h}}-e^{-\tfrac{n+1}{8}u^{h}}\bigr]=\bigl[2\sinh\!\bigl(\tfrac{n+1}{8}u^{h}\bigr)\bigr]\in{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}),d^{{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{h}}}})\cong{\mathbb{C}}[[h]].

6.2.
A Word on Basic Classical Lie Superalgebras

Let
{\mathfrak{g}}
be a basic classical Lie superalgebra and let
{\mathfrak{l}}\subseteq{\mathfrak{g}}
be a quadratic Lie subsuperalgebra as in Section
3.3
, so that
{\mathfrak{g}}={\mathfrak{l}}\oplus{\mathfrak{p}}
. For every finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodule
M
we may form the Bismut–Quillen superconnection
{\mathbb{A}}^{M}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}(t)
introduced above; with the usual sign conventions, the construction and its formal properties extend to the super setting. In contrast to the case of a complex semisimple Lie algebra, the oscillator module
\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
is infinite-dimensional, and hence the supertrace on
E\coloneqq M\otimes\overline{M}({\mathfrak{p}})
is not a priori available. Nevertheless, for unitarizable finite-dimensional
{\mathfrak{g}}
-supermodules
M
(Section
2.4
) one can extract a canonical substitute. In this case
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is selfadjoint, hence
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
is positive. Writing
E=\bigoplus_{\lambda\geq 0}E(\lambda)
for the
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}
-eigenspace decomposition, one has on
E(\lambda)

e^{-t\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}}\xlongrightarrow[t\to\infty]{}\begin{cases}\operatorname{id}_{E(0)},&\lambda=0,\\
0,&\lambda\neq 0.\end{cases}

Moreover
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}^{2}=\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}={\operatorname{H}}_{\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}(M)
and
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
is finite-dimensional by
[
23
]
. Hence, whenever a relative supertrace on
E
can be defined so that the transgression argument applies, the class
[\chi_{M}(t)]
is independent of
t
, and letting
t\to\infty
yields the substitute

(6.33)

\widetilde{\operatorname{ch}}_{M}\coloneqq\operatorname{str}_{\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}}\!\bigl(e^{F^{M}}\bigr).

Since
\nabla^{M}
commutes with
\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
, the curvature term
F^{M}
preserves
\ker\operatorname{D}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}
, so this expression is well-defined. In particular, it determines a class
[\widetilde{\operatorname{ch}}_{M}]\in{\operatorname{H}}(\widehat{{\mathcal{W}}}({\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}),d^{{\mathcal{W}}_{{\mathfrak{g}},{\mathfrak{l}}}})
.
