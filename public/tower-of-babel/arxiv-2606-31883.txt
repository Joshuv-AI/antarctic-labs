Possibly Relevant Translations
Søren Brinck Knudstorp

Abstract
We develop translations from relevant logics into normal modal logics, and use them to clarify structural connections between relevant and modal logic, obtain a few corollary results, and raise questions for future work.



The past century has witnessed the flourishing of many a new logical tradition, each developing along its own lines, yet with analogies identified and formalized through translation.

A classic example is the Gödel–McKinsey–Tarski translation of intuitionistic propositional logic into classical modal logic, which has fostered a rich exchange of ideas, results, and research questions
[
7
]
; its original intent of demonstrating how intuitionistic logic can be interpreted classically now seems the least of its impact.

The present paper pursues a similar, though more modest, aim for another pair of traditions: relevant logic as conceived in the Anderson–Belnap lineage and modal logic as studied in the theory of normal modal logic.

Our main contributions are full and faithful translations from relevant logics into normal modal logics. We leave aside discussion of philosophical differences and motivations, though we hope that the translations shall as well be conceptually clarifying. We also use the translations to sample a few corollary results and raise some questions, but leave a fuller analysis for future work.

The paper proceeds by developing a series of translations, starting with the relevant vocabulary
\{\land,\lor,\to\}
and gradually adding
\mathsf{t},\circ,
and
\neg
.

1.2
Proof outline

The first goal is to define a translation
⋆
such that for all formulas
\varphi,\psi
of the positive relevant language,

\displaystyle\vdash_{\mathbf{B}^{+}}\varphi\to\psi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\vdash_{\mathbf{K}_{2}}\varphi^{\star}\supset\psi^{\star}.

(3)

Afterwards, we extend the translation across further relevant logics and then to arbitrary formulas in the positive language (not only those with main connective
\to
). In a later section, we will also translate relevant logics in languages with additional relevant vocabulary.

We proceed to outline the proof strategy but defer most proof steps to the appendix. The proof will be carried out semantically by defining a function
F
that takes
pointed Kripke models
(model-world pairs
\mathbb{M},w
) and returns
pointed Routley–Meyer models
(model-point pairs
\mathfrak{M},x
), subject to the following adjunction pattern:

\displaystyle F(\mathbb{M},w)

\displaystyle\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\mathbb{M},w\vDash\alpha^{\star}.

(4)

This ensures left-to-right of (
3
) (fullness of the translation): if
\mathbb{M},w\vDash\varphi^{\star}
, then
F(\mathbb{M},w)\Vdash\varphi
, whence
\vdash_{\mathbf{B}^{+}}\varphi\to\psi
(and soundness for
\mathbf{B}^{+}
) imply
F(\mathbb{M},w)\Vdash\psi
(cf. (
1
)), so we have
\mathbb{M},w\vDash\psi^{\star}
, which suffices by completeness for
\mathbf{K}_{2}
.

For the converse direction (faithfulness, or conservativity, of the translation), it suffices to show that
F
is essentially surjective, up to satisfaction equivalence; i.e., that for all pointed Routley–Meyer models
(\mathfrak{M},x)
, there is a pointed Kripke model
(\mathbb{M},w)
such that for all
\alpha
,

\mathfrak{M},x\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad F(\mathbb{M},w)\Vdash\alpha.

It will, however, be more convenient (and equivalent) to directly define a function
G
that takes pointed Routley–Meyer models and returns pointed Kripke models, subject to the following:

\displaystyle\mathfrak{M},x

\displaystyle\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad G(\mathfrak{M},x)\vDash\alpha^{\star}.

(5)

Then, whenever
\mathfrak{M},x\Vdash\varphi
, we also have
G(\mathfrak{M},x)\vDash\varphi^{\star}
, so from
\vdash_{\mathbf{K}_{2}}\varphi^{\star}\supset\psi^{\star}
(and soundness for
\mathbf{K}_{2}
) we get
G(\mathfrak{M},x)\vDash\psi^{\star}
, hence
\mathfrak{M},x\Vdash\psi
, which, by (
1
) and completeness for
\mathbf{B}^{+}
, is as required.

In sum, we shall define a triple consisting of a translation of formulas
⋆
and two model transformations
F:\{(\mathbb{M},w)\}\rightleftarrows\{(\mathfrak{M},x)\}:G
fulfilling (
4
) and (

).

\displaystyle\vDash p\subset\supset\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$\cr\raisebox{0.21529pt}{\scalebox{0.8}[1]{$\neg$}}\cr}}\vtop{\halign{#\cr$\Box$\cr\raisebox{0.21529pt}{\scalebox{0.8}[1]{$\neg$}}\cr}}p

Proof.

All three formulas are Sahlqvist; see the appendix for a proof of the last correspondent (

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(W,R,V)
be a Kripke model. For
0\notin W
, define

R^{\prime}\mathrel{:=}R\cup\{(0,x,x)\mid x\in W\cup\{0\}\}.

Then
(W\cup\{0\},R^{\prime},\{0\},V)
is a Routley–Meyer model and for all
x\in W
and formulas
\alpha
of the positive relevant language,

x\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad x\vDash\alpha^{\star}.

Proof.

It is readily verified that
(W\cup\{0\},R^{\prime},\{0\},V)
is a Routley–Meyer model. The biimplication is proven by induction. The only non-trivial case is the inductive step for implication. There, we have the following for all
x\in W
:

\displaystyle x\Vdash\beta\to\gamma

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z\in W\cup\{0\}$: if $R^{\prime}xyz$ and }y\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{(i)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z\in W$: if $Rxyz$ and }y\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(IH)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z\in W$: if $Rxyz$ and }y\vDash\beta^{\star},\text{ then $z\vDash\gamma^{\star}$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash\beta^{\star}\to\gamma^{\star}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash(\beta\to\gamma)^{\star}.

Here, (i) holds because when
x\in W
, then
R^{\prime}xyz
implies
y,z\in W
and
Rxyz
.
∎

Lemma A.2
.

Let
(\mathfrak{F},V)
with
\mathfrak{F}=(K,R,N)
be a Routley–Meyer model. Then
(K,R,V)
is a Kripke model and for all
x\in K
and formulas
\alpha
of the positive relevant language,

x\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad x\vDash\alpha^{\star}.

Proof.

(K,R,V)
is evidently a Kripke model. We prove the biimplication by induction on
\alpha
. The base case is by definition, and the inductive steps for conjunction and disjunction are immediate by the induction hypothesis. Lastly, for the case where
\alpha=\beta\to\gamma
, we have the following:

\displaystyle x\Vdash\beta\to\gamma

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rxyz$ and }y\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(IH)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rxyz$ and }y\vDash\beta^{\star},\text{ then $z\vDash\gamma^{\star}$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash\beta^{\star}\to\gamma^{\star}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash(\beta\to\gamma)^{\star},

which shows the required equivalence.
∎

Lemma A.3
.

Let
(W,R,V)
be a Kripke model. For
0\notin W
, define

R^{\prime}\mathrel{:=}R\cup\{(0,x,x)\mid x\in W\cup\{0\}\}.

(W\cup\{0\},R^{\prime},\{0\},V)
is a Routley–Meyer model and for all
x\in W

\alpha

Proof.

The proof is by induction on
\alpha
. The base case holds as
0\notin V(p)
for all propositional letters
p
. The inductive cases for
\land,\lor
are immediate by the induction hypothesis. Lastly, for the case where
\alpha=\beta\to\gamma
, let

\displaystyle 0\Vdash\beta\to\gamma

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z\in W\cup\{0\}$: if $R^{\prime}0yz$ and }y\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $z\in W\cup\{0\}$: if }z\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{}{\Rightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $z\in W$: if }z\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{\ref{Kripke-Routley}}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\;\overset{}{\Rightarrow}

for all
y,z\in W
: if
Rxyz
,
then
z\vDash\beta^{\star}\supset\gamma^{\star}

\displaystyle\;\overset{}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z\in W$: if $Rxyz$ and }y\vDash\top,\text{ then $z\vDash\beta^{\star}\supset\gamma^{\star}$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash\Box_{\to}(\beta^{\star}\supset\gamma^{\star})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle x\vDash(\beta\to\gamma)^{\ast}.

Lemma A.4
.

Let
(\mathfrak{F},V)
with
\mathfrak{F}=(K,R,N)
be a Routley–Meyer model. Then
(K,R,V)
is a Kripke model and for all
n\in N

Once again, the proof is by induction on

\displaystyle n\Vdash\beta\to\gamma

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rnyz$ and }y\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{(i)}}{\Leftarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rnyz$ and }z\Vdash\beta,\text{ then $z\Vdash\gamma$}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rnyz$ and }z\vDash\beta^{\star},\text{ then $z\vDash\gamma^{\star}$}

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\;\overset{}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle\text{for all $y,z$: if $Rnyz$ and }y\vDash\top,\text{ then $z\vDash\beta^{\star}\supset\gamma^{\star}$}

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle n\vDash\Box_{\to}(\beta^{\star}\supset\gamma^{\star})

\displaystyle\overset{\text{(def)}}{\Leftrightarrow}

\displaystyle n\vDash(\beta\to\gamma)^{\ast},

where (i) followed from persistence in Routley–Meyer models:

Proposition 2.1

(Restated)
.

Let

be a Kripke frame. Then

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash p\supset\mathsf{t}\circ p

iff

\displaystyle\leq\text{ is reflexive},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\supset\mathsf{t}\circ p

iff

\displaystyle\leq\text{ is transitive},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ\mathsf{t}\supset\mathsf{t}

\displaystyle\text{$N$ is an $\leq$-upset},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\circ q\supset p\circ q

\displaystyle\text{ $Rxyz,x^{-}\leq x\Rightarrow Rx^{-}yz$},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash p\circ(\mathsf{t}\circ q)\supset p\circ q

\displaystyle\text{ $Rxyz,y^{-}\leq y\Rightarrow Rxy^{-}z$},

\displaystyle\mathbb{F}

\displaystyle\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ(p\circ q)\supset p\circ q

\displaystyle\text{ $Rxyz,z\leq z^{+}\Rightarrow Rxyz^{+}$}.

\mathbb{F}=(W,R,N)
is a Routley–Meyer frame iff it validates the listed axioms.

We prove the second and the fourth item and leave the rest as an exercise.

First, suppose

and
x\leq y\leq z
; i.e., that there are
m,n\in N
s.t.
Rmxy
and
Rnyz
. We are to find
l\in N
such that
Rlxz
. Set

. Then

, so since
\mathbb{F}\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\supset\mathsf{t}\circ p
, we have
z\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ p
, which precisely shows that there is
l\in N
with
Rlxz
.

Conversely, assume
\leq
is transitive, and let
V,z
be arbitrary with
z\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ(\mathsf{t}\circ p)
. We are to show that
z\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ p
. Since
z\vDash\mathsf{t}\circ(\mathsf{t}\circ p)
, there are
m,n\in N
and
x,y\in W
with
Rmxy,Rnyz,x\vDash p
. Thus,
x\leq y\leq z
, so by transitivity
x\leq z
, hence there is
l\in N
with
Rlxz
, as desired.

Second, suppose
\mathbb{F}\vDash(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\circ q\supset p\circ q
and
Rxyz,x^{-}\leq x
; i.e., there is
n\in N
s.t.
Rnx^{-}x
. We are to show that
Rx^{-}yz
. Set
V(p)\mathrel{:=}\{x^{-}\}
,
V(q)\mathrel{:=}\{y\}
. Then
z\vDash(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\circ q
, so

, whence

Conversely, assume that for all

:
Rxyz
and
x^{-}\leq x
imply
Rx^{-}yz
. Let
V,z
be arbitrary with
z\vDash(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\circ q
. We are to show that

. Since
z\vDash(\mathsf{t}\circ p)\circ q
, there are

and

Theorem 2.5

(Restated)
.

For all formulas
\varphi\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\circ,\mathsf{t}}
,

\displaystyle\vdash_{\mathbf{B}}\varphi\qquad\text{iff}\qquad\vdash_{\mathbf{Mod.B^{+}}}\mathsf{t}\supset\varphi^{\circ}.

Proof.

For right-to-left, assume
\vdash_{\mathbf{Mod.B^{+}}}\mathsf{t}\supset\varphi^{\circ}
and let
(\mathfrak{F},V)
be an arbitrary Routley–Meyer model. Then
(\mathfrak{F},V)
is a
\mathbf{Mod.B^{+}}
model as well, cf. Proposition

. By Lemma

, for all

x\Vdash p\qquad\overset{\text{(def)}}{\text{iff}}\qquad x\vDash p\qquad\overset{\text{\ref{lm:upsets}}}{\text{iff}}\qquad x\vDash p^{\circ}.

An induction readily extends it to all formulas
\alpha\in\mathcal{L}^{+}_{\circ,\mathsf{t}}
; i.e., for all
x\in\mathfrak{F}
,

x\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad x\vDash\alpha^{\circ}.

Therefore, if
n
is a normal point, then

, so by soundness for
\mathbf{Mod.B}^{+}
, we get

, hence

, which shows that
\vdash_{\mathbf{B}}\varphi
by completeness for
\mathbf{B}
.

Conversely, assume
\vdash_{\mathbf{B}}\varphi
and let
(W,R,N,V)
be an arbitrary
\mathbf{Mod.B^{+}}
model. By completeness of
\mathbf{Mod.B^{+}}
for the frames of Proposition

, it is enough to show that for all normal points

,

. From Proposition

it also follows that
(W,R,N)
is a Routley–Meyer frame. We define a valuation

over

by setting

V^{\prime}(p)\mathrel{:=}\|p^{\circ}\|.

This yields a well-defined Routley–Meyer model by Lemma

. So by assumption and soundness for
\mathbf{B}
, for all normal points
n\in N
,
n\Vdash\varphi
. An easy induction then shows that for all formulas

and

,

x\Vdash\alpha\qquad\text{iff}\qquad x\vDash\alpha^{\circ},

which completes the proof.
∎
