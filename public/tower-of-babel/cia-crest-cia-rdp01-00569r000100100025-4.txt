Dear Dean Williams:

This is in response to your letter of 20 December 1983,
received in this office on 29 December, in which you reguested,
under the Freedom of Information Act (FOIA), information on the
Klaus Fuchs case. We apologize for the delay in acknowledging
your request.

This Agency has in the past received similar requests on
such notables as Guy Burgess, Donald Maclean and Kim Philby in
conjunction with Klaus Fuchs. We are enclosing copies of
documents released previously. You will note that there is an
i/ obvious lack of information released in the past on Fuchs. If
you wish, you may submit a request specifically for documents
on Mr . Fuchs .

As you know, the FOIA authorizes federal agencies to
collect fees for records services. We are enclosing,
therefore, a fee schedule for your guidance. You will note
II that processing charges involve search fees, including computer
/( time where indices are computerized, and copying costs for
\ * documents deemed releasable.

In accordance with section (e) of the schedule, fees are
assessable even if no records are found, or, if found, they are
determined to be not releasable. This means you will be^

| charged even if our search results are negative or if it is
I determined that no information is releasable under the
1 exemptions of the FOIA.

Before we can begin any processing of your request, we must
receive your commitment to pay all fees incurred and under the
conditions stated above. The search fees for each item in a
K request are usually about $100; a more precise fee estimate for
|\ your particular request can be obtained if you wish.

Dear Dean Williams:

This is in regard to your 27 March 1984 letter in which
you provided a fee commitment of $200 for this Agency to
conduct document searches in response to your Freedom of
Information Act (FOIA) request for records on Klaus Fuchs.

We have accepted your request and will begin our searches
for responsive documents in existence as of and through the
date of this acceptance letter.

We believe you also should be informed that one of the
systems to be searched in response to your request is our most
backlogged system, which consists predominately of sensitive
internal operational correspondence and unevaluated intelligence
reporting. Because of their sensitive and fragmented nature,
tLe review of records located within this system is very time
consuming. Furthermore, over half of all the requests we
receive require a search of this system. When records from
this system are located, requests often take up to two or more
years to process. We very much regret this, but our large
backlog of cases and the number of requests we continue to
receive have frustrated our attempts to improve the situation.

The heavy volume of some 3200 information requests such
as yours currently being handled by the Agency has created pro-
cessing backlogs. Also, we process all requests on a first— in,
first— out basis. Since we will not be able to respond within
the 10 working days stipulated by the Freedom of Information
Act, it is your right to construe this as a denial, subject to
appeal to the CIA Information Review Committee. It would seem
