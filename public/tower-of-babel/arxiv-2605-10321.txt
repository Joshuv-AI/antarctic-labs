Parity Nonconservation in Hydrogen Induced by Low-Mass Vector-Boson Exchange
V. A. Dzuba, V. V. Flambaum, G. K. Vong

Abstract
Parity-nonconserving (PNC) effects in atoms produced by Z Z -boson exchange between the electron and the nucleus grow rapidly with the nuclear charge Z Z . If a hypothetical additional Z ′ Z^{\prime} boson is light, however, its contribution does not exhibit the same strong enhancement with Z Z . As a result, the ratio of the low-mass Z ′ Z^{\prime} contribution to the Standard Model Z Z -boson contribution increases rapidly with decreasing Z Z , in fact faster than 1 / Z 2 1/Z^{2} . Hydrogen has a further important advantage: its theoretical description is substantially cleaner than that of heavy atoms, allowing a more accurate interpretation of experimental results. For these two reasons, hydrogen and deuterium PNC experiments may provide an especially favorable setting in which to disentangle a possible Z ′ Z^{\prime} contribution from the Standard Model background. In this paper we calculate the ratio of the Z ′ Z^{\prime} -boson contribution, for arbitrary Z ′ Z^{\prime} mass, to the Standard Model Z Z -boson contribution to parity violation in hydrogen and deuterium, including both nuclear-spin-independent (NSI) and nuclear-spin-dependent (NSD) interactions.



I
Introduction

Parity nonconservation (PNC) in atoms provides one of the most precise low-energy tests of the electroweak interaction and a sensitive probe of new neutral-current physics (see e.g. reviews

Dzuba and Flambaum 2012
;
Roberts et al. 2015
).
The classic observation is that atomic transitions of opposite parity are weakly mixed by parity-violating electron–nucleus interactions, thereby generating otherwise forbidden electric-dipole amplitudes. Because these effects can be calculated in the Standard Model and measured with high precision, atomic PNC has long been used to test electroweak theory and to constrain extensions of it, including extra neutral gauge bosons
Bouchiat and Fayet 2005
;
Diener et al. 2012
;
Dzuba et al. 2017
.

Hydrogen occupies a special place in this program. In contrast to heavy many-electron atoms, hydrogen is free from many-body electronic-structure uncertainties and is sensitive in a particularly transparent way to proton neutral-current couplings. The possibility of studying parity violation in hydrogen was recognized long ago by Cahn and Kane
Cahn and Kane 1977
. Later analyses developed the formalism in detail for hydrogen and deuterium, emphasized the extraction of the NSI weak coupling constant
C_{1p}
and the NSD weak coupling constant
C_{2p}
, and clarified the experimental advantages of level crossings and metastable states
Dunford and Holt 2007
;
Dunford and Holt 2011
. More recently, laser-based and quantum-control strategies for hydrogen PNC have been revisited, reinforcing the case that hydrogen can provide a clean determination of the proton weak charge and complementary information on weak neutral currents
Rasor and Yost 2020
;
Li et al. 2024
.

At the same time, parity-violating electron scattering has developed into a powerful and conceptually clean probe of semileptonic neutral-current interactions. The proton weak charge is especially sensitive to physics beyond the Standard Model because its Standard Model value is accidentally small
Erler et al. 2003
. The final Qweak result for parity-violating elastic
ep
scattering is in agreement with the Standard Model and places strong constraints on new semileptonic parity-violating interactions
Androić et al. 2018
. In the heavy-boson limit these constraints admit a model-independent effective-field-theory interpretation in terms of contact operators, with the review of Ref.
Carlini et al. 2019
providing the most useful summary of present bounds. Ongoing and future parity-violating electron-scattering programs further extend the sensitivity to extra neutral gauge bosons over a broad mass range
Dev et al. 2021
;
Thomas et al. 2022
;
Thomas et al. 2025
.

The case of an additional neutral boson
Z^{\prime}
is therefore of renewed interest. For a sufficiently heavy
Z^{\prime}
, exchange of the boson reduces at low energy to a local four-fermion interaction. In atomic language, its contribution can then be absorbed into shifts of the conventional weak couplings or weak charge. This short-range regime underlies most model-independent discussions of heavy
Z^{\prime}
constraints from parity-violation experiments
Diener et al. 2012
;
Carlini et al. 2019
. However, if the boson mass is small enough that its Compton wavelength becomes comparable to or larger than the atomic length scale, the interaction is no longer local. One must retain the full Yukawa propagator, and the atomic matrix element depends on the detailed spatial overlap of electronic wave functions and the nuclear Yukawa-type PNC potential rather than only on contact densities.

This finite-range regime has been studied in our papers Refs.
Dzuba et al. 2017
;
Dzuba et al. 2025
, where the parity-violating potentials generated by a vector boson of arbitrary mass have been applied to several heavy atoms and ions. This work provides the general framework needed to interpolate continuously between the contact and long-range limits. Yet, to the best of our knowledge, the hydrogen case has not been treated explicitly for a
Z^{\prime}
boson of arbitrary mass, despite the fact that hydrogen is arguably the cleanest system in which to isolate the dependence on the boson mass and on the underlying axial-electron/vector-proton and vector-electron/axial-proton coupling structures. An additional advantage of hydrogen is that the ratio of the low-mass
Z^{\prime}
contribution to the Standard Model
Z
-boson contribution increases rapidly with decreasing
Z
, faster than
1/Z^{2}

Dzuba et al. 2017
.

The purpose of the present work is to fill this gap. We investigate parity violation in hydrogen induced by exchange of a
Z^{\prime}
boson of arbitrary mass, including both nuclear-spin-independent (NSI) and nuclear-spin-dependent (NSD) interactions.
Our aim is not merely to reinterpret the standard weak interaction in a different notation, but to consider the hydrogen PNC effects in the regime where the finite range of the new interaction is essential and
the interaction cannot be reduced to a redefinition of the Standard Model
Z
-boson constants. Hydrogen is then especially attractive because the required matrix elements can be derived analytically or semi-analytically with controlled accuracy, making the dependence on
m_{Z^{\prime}}
transparent.

The analysis is also timely from the phenomenological point of view. Heavy-atom PNC, parity-violating electron scattering, and hydrogen spectroscopy probe related but not identical combinations of couplings
Erler et al. 2003
;
Androić et al. 2018
;
Carlini et al. 2019
.

We use atomic units
\hbar=e=m_{e}=1
,
c=1/\alpha
.

Interaction Lagrangian for
Z
and
Z^{\prime}
bosons.

The neutral-current
Z
Lagrangian is

\mathcal{L}_{Z}=-\frac{1}{4}Z_{\mu\nu}Z^{\mu\nu}+\frac{1}{2}m_{Z}^{2}c^{2}Z_{\mu}Z^{\mu}-\frac{g}{2\cos\theta_{W}}Z_{\mu}J_{Z}^{\mu},

(1)

with

J_{Z}^{\mu}=\sum_{f}\bar{f}\gamma^{\mu}\bigl(g_{f}^{V}-g_{f}^{A}\gamma_{5}\bigr)f.

(2)

II.1
Nuclear-spin-independent PV interaction

The parity-violating nuclear-spin-independent (NSI) part of the interaction comes from the electron axial current and the proton vector current. For the SM
Z
-boson (without radiative corrections)

g_{e}^{A}=-\frac{1}{2},\qquad g_{p}^{V}=\frac{1}{2}-2\sin^{2}\theta_{W},

(3)

Proton weak charge is equal to

Q_{W}^{p}=-4g_{e}^{A}g_{p}^{V}=1-4\sin^{2}\theta_{W}.

(4)

Inclusion of radiative corrections gives
Q_{W}^{p}=0.0705

Tanabashi et al. 2018
. Corrections produced by the dispersive parity violating interaction may also be included into the definition of the weak charge giving
Q_{W}^{p}=0.0719

Flambaum and Samsonov 2026
.

Then we may write the NSI part of the
Z
-exchange parity-violating Hamiltonian as

V_{Z}(r)=-\frac{G_{F}}{2\sqrt{2}\,c}\,Q_{W}^{p}\,m_{Z}^{2}c^{2}\,\Phi(m_{Z},r)\,\gamma_{5},

(5)

where
G_{F}\approx 2.2225\times 10^{-14}
a.u.
is the Fermi constant of the weak interaction,

\Phi(m,r)=\frac{1}{4\pi}\frac{e^{-\mu r}}{r}.

(6)

In atomic units
\mu=mc=m/\alpha m_{e}
.
Finite nuclear radius must be introduced for a heavy atom case since the Dirac relativistic electron wave functions in
s_{1/2}
and
p_{1/2}
waves tend to infinity for the point-like nucleus. However, the non-relativistic approximation is sufficiently accurate for hydrogen, so the finite proton size correction is
\sim 10^{-5}
and may be neglected.
In the case of the large mediator mass,
m\to\infty
,

\Phi(m,r)=\frac{1}{\mu^{2}}\delta^{3}(r)\,.

(7)

For a generic parity-violating
Z^{\prime}
boson we absorb the model-dependent gauge coupling and charges into two effective couplings
g_{eA}^{\prime}
and
g_{pV}^{\prime}
and present NSI interaction potential as

V_{Z^{\prime}}(r)=g_{eA}^{\prime}g_{pV}^{\prime}\,\Phi(m_{Z^{\prime}},r)\,\gamma_{5}\,.

(8)

II.2
Z^{\prime}
boson matrix elements

We write the Dirac orbital as

\psi_{n\kappa m}(\mathbf{r})=\frac{1}{r}\begin{pmatrix}g_{n\kappa}(r)\,\Omega_{\kappa m}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})\\
if_{n\kappa}(r)\,\Omega_{-\kappa m}(\hat{\mathbf{r}})\end{pmatrix},

(9)

so that for
ns_{1/2}
and
np_{1/2}
states,

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\equiv\langle ns_{1/2}|V_{Z^{\prime}}|np_{1/2}\rangle

\displaystyle=ig_{eA}^{\prime}g_{pV}^{\prime}\int_{0}^{\infty}dr\,\bigl(g_{ns}f_{np}-f_{ns}g_{np}\bigr)\,\Phi_{m}(r).

(10)

In the nonrelativistic limit,

g_{ns}(r)=rR_{ns}(r),\qquad g_{np}(r)=rR_{np}(r),

(11)

where
R
are the non-relativistic hydrogen radial wave functions, while the small components follow from the Dirac radial equations,

\displaystyle f_{ns}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c}\left(\frac{d}{dr}-\frac{1}{r}\right)g_{ns}(r),

\displaystyle f_{np}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2c}\left(\frac{d}{dr}+\frac{1}{r}\right)g_{np}(r).

(12)

PNC effects in the transitions
1s-2s
,
2s-2p_{1/2}
,
2s-3s
and
2s-4s
have been discussed in the literature. Therefore, we need matrix elements of the weak interaction between close levels
2s-2p_{1/2}
,
3s-3p_{1/2}
and
4s-4p_{1/2}
.
Specific matrix elements may be experimentally separated by reducing interval between opposite parity levels
ns-np_{1/2}
to the close to zero value by magnetic field.
The hydrogenic integrals give

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{2}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=i\,\frac{g_{eA}^{\prime}g_{pV}^{\prime}}{96\pi c}\,\sqrt{3}\,\frac{3\mu+1}{(\mu+1)^{3}},

(13)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{3}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=i\,\frac{g_{eA}^{\prime}g_{pV}^{\prime}}{27\pi c}\,\sqrt{2}\,\frac{81\mu^{3}+54\mu^{2}+30\mu+4}{(3\mu+2)^{5}},

(14)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{4}^{Z^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=i\,\frac{g_{eA}^{\prime}g_{pV}^{\prime}}{3840\pi c}\,\sqrt{15}\,\frac{960\mu^{5}+800\mu^{4}+640\mu^{3}+192\mu^{2}+42\mu+3}{(2\mu+1)^{7}}.

(15)

II.4
Nuclear-spin-dependent PV interaction

The Standard-Model Hamiltonian describing the nuclear-spin-dependent (NSD) parity-violating electron-proton interaction may be written as

H_{\rm SD}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}c}\frac{\varkappa_{p}}{I}\,{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\bm{I}}\,m_{Z}^{2}c^{2}\,\Phi(m_{Z},r),

(26)

where
\varkappa_{p}=0.043
is the dimensionless NSD coupling constant
Dunford and Holt 2007
.
In the contact (
m_{Z}\to\infty
) approximation,

H_{\rm SD}^{\rm contact}=\frac{G_{F}}{\sqrt{2}c}\frac{\varkappa_{p}}{I}\,{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\bm{I}}\,\rho({\bm{r}}).

(27)

For a generic parity-violating
Z^{\prime}
boson we absorb the model-dependent gauge coupling and charges into two effective couplings
g_{eV}^{\prime}
and
g_{pA}^{\prime}
and write the NSD interaction potential as

V_{Z^{\prime}}(r)=\frac{g_{eV}^{\prime}g_{pA}^{\prime}}{I}\,{\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\bm{I}}\,\Phi(m_{Z^{\prime}},r).

(28)

For hydrogen we take the proton to be nonrelativistic and at rest, and consider the stretched hyperfine state with maximal projections
I_{z}=J_{z}=1/2

F=F_{z}=1

({\bm{\alpha}}\cdot{\bm{I}})/I\to\alpha_{z}
and the matrix element reduces to

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{n}^{Z^{\prime},\rm SD}\equiv

\displaystyle\langle ns_{1/2},m_{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}|V_{Z^{\prime}}|np_{1/2},m_{j}=\tfrac{1}{2}\rangle

\displaystyle g_{eV}^{\prime}g_{pA}^{\prime}\!\int d^{3}r\,\psi_{ns}^{\dagger}(\mathbf{r})\,\alpha_{z}\,\psi_{np}(\mathbf{r})\,\Phi(m_{Z^{\prime}},r).

12
