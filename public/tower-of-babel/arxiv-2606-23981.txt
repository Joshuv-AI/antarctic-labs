Monge-Ampère-type equation for forms of positive degree and Demailly’s transcendental Morse inequality
Mathew George

Abstract
. The Monge-Ampère-type equation for forms of positive degree was introduced by Dinew and Popovici to prove the qualitative part of an analytic version of Demailly’s transcendental Morse inequality for higher cohomology classes, conditional on the solvability of this nonlinear PDE. In this paper, we show that this can be proven unconditionally. We first show that the originally proposed Laplacian trace condition, Λ m − 2 ​ Δ ​ u = 0 \Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=0 , is analytically too rigid to permit solutions and hence introduce a general gauge-fixing that leads to the ( a , b ) (a,b) Monge-Ampère-type equation. By deriving a priori estimates, we establish the solvability of this class of equations for different parametric values ( a , b ) (a,b) . As a geometric application, for b = 0 b=0 this framework reduces to the classical complex Monge-Ampère equation, which yields the qualitative Demailly’s inequality for higher-degree forms. Further, we prove that the uniqueness of these solutions can be significantly strengthened under the kernel condition ∂ ¯ ∗ ​ u = 0 \bar{\partial}^{*}u=0 .

1.
Introduction

A central theme in complex geometry is the detection of strictly positive currents using purely cohomological volume thresholds. Demailly’s transcendental Morse inequalities
[
2
]
provide such a criterion bypassing explicit constructions of positive currents. Dinew and Popovici
[
3
]
introduced Monge-Ampère-type equation for forms of positive degree to extend these bigness criteria to higher-degree
(m,m)
-forms. Specifically, on an
n
-dimensional compact Kähler manifold
(M,\omega)
, for
1<m<n
the Monge-Ampère-type equation for forms of positive degree can be written as

(1.1)

\left[\star_{\omega}\left((\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u)\wedge\omega_{n-m-1}\right)\right]^{n}=dV,

for an
(m,m)
form
\alpha>0
and
dV
a smooth volume form on
M
. The classical version
[
1
,
8
]
of Demailly’s transcendental Morse inequality for
(1,1)
forms states that if
\alpha
and
\beta
are nef classes on a compact
n
-dimensional Kähler manifold, the difference
[\alpha]-[\beta]
contains a strictly positive Kähler current, given that the following volume threshold holds

\int_{M}\alpha^{n}-n\int_{M}\alpha^{n-1}\wedge\beta>0.

Originally stated in
[
1
, Conjecture
10.1
,
(ii)
]
, weaker versions were shown in
[
1
]
and
[
12
]
. The qualitative part of the conjecture was resolved by Popovici
[
8
]
. This inequality and its general version are crucial ingredients for extending the fundamental duality between the pseudo-effective cone
\mathcal{E}
and the cone of movable classes
\mathcal{M}
on projective manifolds to compact Kähler manifolds. In
[
3
]
, this is generalized to higher-degree
(m,m)
-forms assuming the solvability of the Monge-Ampère-type equation for forms of positive degree under some gauge conditions. Specifically, it was shown that for a strictly strongly positive closed
(m,m)
-form
\beta
, the class
[(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}-\beta]_{BC}
contains a strictly positive current if the integral inequality

\frac{1}{n!}\int_{M}\alpha_{\omega}^{n}-\frac{1}{(n-m)!}\int_{M}\alpha_{\omega}^{n-m}\wedge\beta>0

holds, assuming that
\alpha
is a
d
-closed
(m,m)
form satisfying
\Delta\alpha_{\omega}=0
. To construct this current, the solvability of the Monge-Ampère-type equation (
1.1
) is assumed under

\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u>0,\quad u\in\mbox{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\mbox{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*}),\quad\text{ and }\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=0.

This set of conditions is over-constraining and cannot produce solutions except in the trivial case when
dV
is a constant multiple of
(\alpha_{\omega})^{n}
. In this paper, we replace
\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=0
by more general condition of the form

(1.2)

(m-1)\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=(1+a)i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u+(1+b)\Lambda^{m-1}\Delta u\;\omega.

for any two real constants
a
and
b
satisfying
a+nb=m-n-2
. This is a natural gauge-fixing for equation (
1.1
) as it constrains the relationship between the three
(1,1)
forms that govern the equation: the complex Hessian
i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u
, the form
(\Lambda^{m-1}\Delta u)\;\omega
and the
(1,1)
part of the Laplacian
\Delta u
.

Denote the primitive coordinates of the solution
u\in C^{\infty}_{m-1,m-1}(M,\mathbb{R})
of (
1.1
) by
\psi_{k}
, for
0\leq k\leq m-1
. Then it can be shown that equation (
1.1
) depends only on the first two primitives
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
. The condition
\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=0
would imply that
\psi_{0}
and
\psi_{1}
are harmonic. So
\psi_{0}
is constant and
\Delta\psi_{1}=0
, which is incompatible with (
1.1
).

Similar to
[
3
]
, we begin our study of the PDE under condition
(1)
: the solution
u\in\mbox{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\mbox{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*})
. Under
(1)
, it was shown that if the primitives of all orders greater than
0
are equal, any two solutions are unique up to a factor of
c\omega^{n}
. In this paper, we show that the condition
(1)
yields a stronger uniqueness theorem which is stated in Section
2
. This result relies on a cascade of relations between successive primitives (
2.9
) of the solution
u
coming from the condition
\partial^{*}u=0
.

Independent of the geometric constraints required by the transcendental Morse inequality, we establish
a priori
second-order and gradient estimates for this generalized class of Monge-Ampère-type equations. The following theorem can be stated as a consequence.

Theorem 1.1
.

For any
(a,b)
with
a+nb=m-n-2
satisfying condition
I.
from Theorem
1.2
, there exists a form

and a unique constant
c>0
satisfying

\left[\star_{\omega}\left((\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u)\wedge\omega_{n-m-1}\right)\right]^{n}=c\cdot dV,

with

\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u>0,\quad u\in\mbox{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\mbox{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*}),

and

(m-1)\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u=(1+a)i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u+(1+b)\Lambda^{m-1}\Delta u\;\omega.

If
(a,b)
and
\alpha
satisfies condition
II.
in Theorem
1.2
, with

, then there exists a constant

such that if

, then for some

for this system.

The solution

are unique up to harmonic forms. Further uniqueness of higher order primitives could only be established under additional assumptions as in Theorem

for

) can now be proven.

Independent of the geometric application, we study this equation for other parametric values

. In Section

(a,b)
Monge-Ampère-type equation

\left(\star_{\omega}(\alpha\wedge\omega_{n-m-1})+\frac{n!}{(n-m+1)!}\left[ai\partial\bar{\partial}f+b\Delta f\omega\right]\right)^{n}=dV.

We prove the following.

Theorem 1.2
.

For any pair
(a,b)\neq(0,0)
, consider the equation

(1.3)

\log\det(\tilde{g}_{f})=c.\psi(z),\text{ with }\;\;\;\tilde{g}_{f}>0.

where

\tilde{g}_{f}=X+\frac{n!}{(n-m+1)!}\left[ai\partial\bar{\partial}f+b\Delta f\omega\right],

with
X
a positive
(1,1)
form and
\psi(z)\in C^{\infty}(M)
a positive function. Then the following statements are true.

I.

If
a+b\geq 0
and
b\geq 0
,
or
if
a+b\leq 0
and
b\leq 0
, then for some
c>0
, there exists a solution

for (
1.3
) unique up to constants.

II.

If
a+b<0
,
b>0

or

a+b>0
,
b<0
, and
\kappa(X)<\dfrac{|a|}{n|b|}
, then there exists a constant
\epsilon>0
such that if

there exists a solution

for (

In Section
2
, we establish the strengthened uniqueness theorem under the Kernel condition and also prove that the original Laplace Trace condition is too rigid. In Section
3
, we introduce the
(a,b)

The Kernel Condition and Uniqueness

The following notations will be used in this paper. For any
(m-1,m-1)
form
\alpha
and any
(1,1)
form
\beta

•

\alpha_{\omega}=\star_{\omega}(\alpha\wedge\omega_{n-m-1})
defines a
(1,1)
form.

•

\beta_{k}=\dfrac{\beta^{k}}{k!}.

•

The
\partial
-Laplacian of the Kähler metric
\omega
will simply be written
\Delta
which is defined by
\partial\partial^{*}+\partial^{*}\partial
.

•

\Lambda
and

denote the Trace operator and the Lefschetz operator for the metric
\omega

We recall the definition of strong positivity.

Definition 2.1
.

An
(m,m)
form
\alpha\in C^{\infty}_{m,m}(M,\mathbb{R})
is called
strongly positive
if it can be decomposed as

\alpha=\sum_{s}c_{s}\,(i\alpha^{s}_{1}\wedge\bar{\alpha^{s}}_{1})\wedge\dots\wedge(i\alpha^{s}_{m}\wedge\bar{\alpha^{s}}_{m})

for
c_{s}>0
and
\alpha^{s}_{i}
a
(1,0)
form. We denote this by
\alpha>0
. If
c_{s}\geq 0
, then
\alpha
is strongly semi-positive, denoted by
\alpha\geq 0
.

Given an
\alpha\in C^{\infty}_{m,m}(M,\mathbb{R})
with
\alpha>0
, the Monge-Ampère equation for forms of positive degree for
u\in C_{m-1,m-1}^{\infty}(M,\mathbb{R})
can be written as

(2.1)

\left[\star_{\omega}\left((\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u)\wedge\omega_{n-m-1}\right)\right]^{n}=dV

assuming
\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u>0
.

The Kernel Condition
u\in\text{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\text{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*})
:

Let
u\in C^{\infty}_{m-1,m-1}(M,\mathbb{R})
be a real form in
\mbox{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\text{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*})
. Write the Lefschetz decomposition of
u
as

(2.2)

\displaystyle u=f\omega^{m-1}+\psi_{1}\wedge\omega^{m-2}+\sum\limits_{k=2}^{m-1}\psi_{k}\wedge\omega^{m-1-k}

where
f\in C^{\infty}(M)
and
\psi_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{k,k}(M)
are the primitive coordinates of
u
that satisfy
\Lambda(\psi_{k})=0
, or equivalently
\psi_{k}\wedge\omega^{n-2k+1}=0
for each
1\leq k\leq m-1
. Then the Kernel condition becomes a system of first-order equations for primitives as follows. Kähler identity
[L,\bar{\partial}^{*}]=-i\partial
implies the formula
\bar{\partial}^{*}(v\wedge\omega^{p})=\bar{\partial}^{*}v\wedge\omega^{p}+p\;i\partial v\wedge\omega^{p-1}
, so that

(2.3)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{*}u=

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{*}f\wedge\omega^{m-1}+(i(m-1)\partial f+\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{1})\wedge\omega^{m-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{m-1}((m-k)i\partial\psi_{k-1}+\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k})\wedge\omega^{m-k-1}.

This is not a primitive decomposition since
\partial\psi_{k}
is not a primitive, although
\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}
is. Let

(2.4)

\partial\psi_{k}=(\partial\psi_{k})_{\text{prim}}+\eta_{k}\wedge\omega

be the primitive decomposition of
\partial\psi_{k}
, for
\eta_{k}\in C^{\infty}_{k,k-1}(M)
a primitive form. The fact that
\eta_{k}
is primitive follows from the following. The commutation relation
[\Lambda,\partial]=i\bar{\partial}^{*}
implies
\Lambda(\partial\psi_{k})=i\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}
. So
\Lambda(\partial\psi_{k})
is primitive. If

\eta_{k}=\eta_{k}^{0}+\eta_{k}^{1}\wedge\omega+\dots

is a primitive decomposition, then applying
\Lambda
to (
2.4
) shows

(2.5)

\displaystyle\Lambda(\partial\psi_{k})=c_{0}\eta_{k}^{0}+c_{1}\eta_{k}^{1}\wedge\omega+\dots{}

for non-zero constants
c_{i}
. This means
\eta_{k}^{i}=0
for
i\geq 2
and
\eta^{0}_{k}
is primitive.

We also have

(2.6)

\displaystyle i\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\partial\psi_{k})

\displaystyle=\Lambda(\eta_{k}\wedge\omega)=(n-2k+1)\eta_{k}.

Hence

(2.7)

\displaystyle(\partial\psi_{k})_{\text{prim}}=\partial\psi_{k}-\frac{i\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}\wedge\omega}{n-2k+1}.

Plug this into (
2.3
) and using
\bar{\partial}^{*}f=0
we get the primitive decomposition of
\bar{\partial}^{*}u
.

(2.8)

\displaystyle\bar{\partial}^{*}u

\displaystyle=(\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{1}+i(m-1)\partial f+(m-2)i\eta_{1})\wedge\omega^{m-2}

\displaystyle+\sum_{k=2}^{m-1}\left[(m-k)i(\partial\psi_{k-1})_{prim}+\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}+(m-k-1)i\eta_{k}\right]\wedge\omega^{m-k-1}

\displaystyle=\left[\frac{n-m+1}{n-1}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{1}+i(m-1)\partial f\right]\wedge\omega^{m-2}+\sum_{k=2}^{m-1}\Big[(m-k)i\partial\psi_{k-1}

\displaystyle+\frac{m-k}{n-2k+3}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k-1}\wedge\omega+\frac{n-m-k+2}{n-2k+1}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}\Big]\wedge\omega^{m-k-1}

The Kernel condition
\bar{\partial}^{*}u=0
now gives the following general identity relating primitives of
u
.

(2.9)

(m-k)i\partial\psi_{k-1}+\frac{m-k}{n-2k+3}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k-1}\wedge\omega+\frac{n-m-k+2}{n-2k+1}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}=0

for each
1\leq k\leq m-1
, assuming
\psi_{0}=f
. For
k=1

(2.10)

\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{1}=-\frac{i(m-1)(n-1)}{n-m+1}\partial f.

Lemma 2.2

If

\partial\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}=0
for all

Proof.

Applying
\bar{\partial}^{*}
to (
2.9

(2.11)

\displaystyle(m-k)i\bar{\partial}^{*}\partial\psi_{k-1}+\frac{(m-k)}{(n-2k+3)}i\partial\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k-1}=0

∎

We recall the Dolbeault Green’s operator
G_{\partial}:C^{\infty}_{p,q}(M)\to C^{\infty}_{p,q}(M)

\partial

\alpha=\partial(G_{\partial}\partial^{*}\alpha)+\partial^{*}(G_{\partial}\partial\alpha)+\alpha_{H},

\displaystyle\psi_{k-1}=G_{\partial}\partial^{*}\left(\frac{i}{n-2k+3}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k-1}\wedge\omega+\frac{i(n-m-k+2)}{(m-k)(n-2k+1)}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{k}\right)+\gamma

\displaystyle=\frac{(m-1)n}{n-m+1}i\partial\bar{\partial}f+\frac{m-1}{n-m+1}\Delta f\;\omega+\frac{i(n-m)}{(n-3)(m-2)}\partial^{*}\bar{\partial}^{*}\psi_{2}.

) and (

(2.23)

For

will be absent in the above formula. Now the PDE is expressed in terms of

and the primitive
\psi_{2}
. If

\Lambda_{\rho}(Q(u_{1}-u_{2}))=0

which follows from the binomial expansion of

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\Lambda_{\rho}\left(\frac{n(m-n)}{n-m+1}\Delta(f_{1}-f_{2})\;\omega+\frac{n(m-n)}{n-m+1}i\partial\bar{\partial}(f_{1}-f_{2})\right),

since

Theorem 3.1
.

Let

. Suppose

are such that

(1)

,

.

(2)

\beta\geq C\omega_{m}
for some constant

\partial\bar{\partial}\beta=0

(3)

\dfrac{1}{n!}\int_{M}\alpha_{\omega}^{n}-\dfrac{1}{(n-m)!}\int_{M}\alpha_{\omega}^{n-m}\wedge\beta>0

Then there exists a real
(m,m)
current

such that

T\geq\delta(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}\text{ for some constant }\delta>0,\hskip 14.22636pt\partial\bar{\partial}T=0,\text{ and }\hskip 14.22636ptT\in[(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}-\beta]_{A}.

If in addition,

,

can be found such that

and

lies in
[(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}-\beta]_{BC}
. Here

stands for the Bott-Chern cohomology and
[.]_{A}
stands for the Aeppli cohomology class.

Proof.

By Lamari-type duality lemma
[
7
,
3
]
, to prove that there exists a real current
S
and a constant
\delta>0
such that
T=(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}-\beta+i\partial\bar{\partial}S
satisfies the Theorem, it is enough to show that there exists a constant
\delta>0
such that

(1-\delta)\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega\geq\int_{M}\beta\wedge\Omega,

for any
\Omega\in C^{\infty}_{n-m,n-m}(M,\mathbb{R})
satisfying
\partial\bar{\partial}\Omega=0
and
\Omega>0
weakly. Assume for contradiction that there is a sequence of forms
\Omega_{k}
and
\delta_{k}\to 0
such that

(3.5)

(1-\delta_{k})\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega_{k}<\int_{M}\beta\wedge\Omega_{k}.

By assumption

, it is possible to normalize the sequence
\Omega_{k}
so that
\int_{M}\beta\wedge\Omega_{k}=1
for each
k
. This also implies

\int_{M}\omega_{m}\wedge\Omega_{k}\leq\frac{1}{C},

so that

weakly. Taking limits of (

Let

be a solution of

\frac{1}{n!}\left[\star_{\omega}\Big((\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u_{k})\wedge\omega_{n-m-1}\Big)\right]^{n}=c_{k}\beta\wedge\Omega_{k}

such that

(3.7)

\tilde{\alpha}_{k}=\alpha+i\partial\bar{\partial}u_{k}>0\quad\text{and}\quad u_{k}\in\mbox{Ker}(\partial^{*})\cap\mbox{Ker}(\bar{\partial}^{*}),

and

(3.8)

(m-1)\Lambda^{m-2}\Delta u_{k}=(m-n-1)i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u_{k}+\Lambda^{m-1}\Delta u_{k}\;\omega.

which exists by Theorem
1.1
.

By Hölder’s inequality and the pointwise inequality
\dfrac{\rho_{m}\wedge\Omega}{\omega_{n}}\cdot\dfrac{\rho_{n-m}\wedge\beta}{\rho_{n}}\geq\dfrac{\beta\wedge\Omega}{\omega_{n}}
for any
(1,1)
form
\rho>0
(See
[
3
, p. 25]
)

\displaystyle\left(\int_{M}((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega_{k}\right)\cdot\left(\int_{M}((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{n-m}\wedge\beta\right)\geq c_{k}\left(\int_{M}\beta\wedge\Omega_{k}\right)^{2}=c_{k}.

Note that

. By (
3.8
)

\displaystyle(\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega}

\displaystyle=\star_{\omega}\left(\alpha\wedge\omega_{n-m-1}\right)+Q(u_{k})

\displaystyle=\alpha_{\omega}+\frac{m-n-2}{(m-1)!}\left(i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u_{k}\right).

It follows that for any

((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{p}-(\alpha_{\omega})_{p}=i\partial\bar{\partial}\Lambda^{m-1}u_{k}\wedge\Gamma,

for a
d
-closed form

. Note that
\alpha_{\omega}
is harmonic and hence
d
-closed. Now applications of the Stokes’ theorem using

and

gives

\int_{M}((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega_{k}=\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega_{k},

\int_{M}((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{n-m}\wedge\beta=\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{n-m}\wedge\beta,

and

\int_{M}((\tilde{\alpha}_{k})_{\omega})_{n}=\int_{M}({\alpha}_{\omega})_{n}.

Combining the above equations with
\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{m}\wedge\Omega_{\infty}\leq 1
, we have

\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{n-m}\wedge\beta\geq\int_{M}(\alpha_{\omega})_{n}

contradicting the assumption
3

∎

Theorem 4.1
.

Any solution

Proof.

The largest eigenvalue

[
11

10
,
9

\varphi(t)=-\log\left(1-\frac{t}{2(1+\sup|\nabla f|^{2})}\right).

Consider a local holomorphic coordinate system that diagonalizes the Hessian at the maximum point
z_{0}
of

such that

and

. Differentiating the PDE by

(4.3)

\displaystyle\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\nabla_{\bar{1}}X_{i\bar{j}}+G^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\nabla_{\bar{1}}f_{i\bar{j}}-\tilde{g}^{i\bar{l}}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{j}}\nabla_{\bar{1}}\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\tilde{g}_{k\bar{l}}=\psi_{1\bar{1}},

and using the curvature formula

\nabla_{1}\nabla_{\bar{1}}f_{i\bar{j}}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{1\bar{1}}+\sum_{p}(R_{1\bar{1}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{j}}-R_{1\bar{j}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{1}}),

(4.4)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{1\bar{1}}=

\displaystyle\psi_{1\bar{1}}-G^{i\bar{j}}\sum_{p}(R_{1\bar{1}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{j}}-R_{1\bar{j}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{1}})+\tilde{g}^{i\bar{l}}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{j}}\nabla_{\bar{1}}\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\tilde{g}_{k\bar{l}}

\displaystyle-\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\nabla_{\bar{1}}X_{i\bar{j}}.

The eigenvalue derivative formulae are given by

\nabla_{i}\theta_{1}=\nabla_{i}f_{1\bar{1}},

\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}\theta_{1}=\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{1\bar{1}}+\sum_{p>1}\frac{\nabla_{i}f_{1\bar{p}}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{p\bar{1}}}{\theta_{1}-\theta_{p}}.

So we have

(4.5)

\displaystyle G^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{i}\nabla_{\bar{j}}P(f)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}\left(\psi_{1\bar{1}}-G^{i\bar{j}}\sum_{p}(R_{1\bar{1}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{j}}-R_{1\bar{j}i\bar{p}}f_{p\bar{1}})+\tilde{g}^{i\bar{l}}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{j}}\nabla_{\bar{1}}\tilde{g}_{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\tilde{g}_{k\bar{l}}\right)

\displaystyle-G^{i\bar{j}}\frac{\nabla_{i}\theta_{1}\nabla_{\bar{j}}\theta_{1}}{\theta_{1}^{2}}+\varphi^{\prime}G^{i\bar{i}}\theta_{i}^{2}+\varphi^{\prime}G^{i\bar{j}}\sum_{k}\nabla_{i}f_{k}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{\bar{k}}

\displaystyle+\varphi^{\prime\prime}G^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{i}|\nabla f|^{2}\nabla_{\bar{j}}|\nabla f|^{2}-\frac{1}{\theta_{1}}\tilde{g}^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{1}\nabla_{\bar{1}}X_{i\bar{j}}+\sum_{p>1}G^{i\bar{j}}\frac{\nabla_{i}f_{1\bar{p}}\nabla_{\bar{j}}f_{p\bar{1}}}{\theta_{1}(\theta_{1}-\theta_{p})}

\displaystyle-C|\nabla f|(1+\sum\tilde{g}^{i\bar{i}})

\displaystyle\geq-C\sum_{i}\tilde{g}^{i\bar{i}}+\min\{(a+b),b\}\;\varphi^{\prime}\sum_{k}\tilde{g}^{k\bar{k}}\theta_{1}^{2}

\displaystyle+(\varphi^{\prime\prime}-(\varphi^{\prime})^{2})G^{i\bar{j}}\nabla_{i}|\nabla f|^{2}\nabla_{\bar{j}}|\nabla f|^{2}

\displaystyle>0.

In the final inequality we used

,

(4.6)

and

at the maximum point

Let

Proof.

By differentiating the PDE by

c
. In addition,

|\kappa(\tilde{g}_{f})-\kappa(X)|_{C^{0}(M)}\leq C_{\delta}\;|\log\det\tilde{g}_{f}-\log\det X|_{C^{0,\gamma}(M)},

and

|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq 2\;C_{1}|\psi-\log\det(X)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}

for uniform positive constants
C_{1}
and
C_{\delta}
.

Proof.

This is a quantitative version of the implicit function theorem proved using the contraction mapping theorem. Denote the linearization of the PDE at
0
by
\mathcal{L}
, so that

\mathcal{L}f=a\Delta_{X}f+b\;\mbox{tr}_{X}(\omega)\Delta_{\omega}f.

By the assumption on
X
, this is uniformly elliptic with kernel being constant functions. Hence by Fredholm theory, the kernel of the adjoint
L^{*}
is
1
-dimensional. Let
\rho(x)
span this kernel and
\int_{M}\rho\;\omega^{n}=1
. Then define

C_{0}^{k,\gamma}=\{f\in C^{k,\gamma}(M)|\int_{M}f\rho\;\omega^{n}=0\},

\Omega^{k,\gamma}=\{f\in C_{0}^{k,\gamma}|\;\;\tilde{g}_{f}>0\},

and

\Omega_{R^{\prime}}=\{f\in C_{0}^{2,\gamma}|\;\;|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}<R^{\prime}\}

where
R^{\prime}
is defined below. Then
\mathcal{L}:C_{0}^{2,\gamma}\to C_{0}^{0,\gamma}
has zero kernel, and is invertible in this domain by Fredholm alternative. Let
C_{1}=\|\mathcal{L}^{-1}\|
be the operator norm. Define the projection
\pi:C^{k,\gamma}\to C_{0}^{k,\gamma}
by

\pi(f)=f-\int_{M}f\rho\omega^{n}.

Note that
|\pi(f)|_{C^{0}}<2|f|_{C^{0}}
. To write (
4.1
) as a fixed-point equation, define
G(f)=\pi\circ\log\det(X+ai\partial\bar{\partial}f+b\Delta f\omega)
and the map
\Phi:\Omega_{R^{\prime}}\to\Omega_{R^{\prime}}
by

\Phi(f)=f+\mathcal{L}^{-1}\left[\pi\circ\psi-(G(0)+\mathcal{L}(f)+\mathcal{R}(f))\right]

with

\mathcal{R}(f)=G(f)-G(0)-\mathcal{L}(f).

Both
\mathcal{R}
and
G
are defined from
\Omega^{2,\gamma}\to C_{0}^{0,\gamma}
. By choosing
\epsilon
small we ensure that
\Phi
maps to
\Omega_{R^{\prime}}
.
\mathcal{R}(f)
denotes the quadratic error in the Taylor approximation of
G(f)
near
f=0
, and can be estimated as follows.

|\mathcal{R}(f)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq\int_{0}^{1}(1-t)|D^{2}G(tf)(f,f)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}dt,

where the Fréchet derivative
D^{2}G(tf)(f,f)
can be evaluated as

D^{2}G(tf)(f,f)=-\pi(\tilde{g}_{tf}^{i\bar{l}}\tilde{g}_{tf}^{k\bar{j}}(V_{f})_{k\bar{l}}(V_{f})_{i\bar{j}}),

for
V_{f}=ai\partial\bar{\partial}f+b\Delta f\omega
. Let
R_{0}=\dfrac{(a+n|b|)^{-1}\lambda_{min}(X)}{2}
. In the ball
B_{R_{0}}(0)
,
\tilde{g}_{tf}
is uniformly equivalent to
X
, and consequently

|D^{2}G(tf)(f,f)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq C^{\prime}_{2}|V_{f}|_{C^{0,\gamma}}^{2}\leq C_{2}|f|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}}.

So
|\mathcal{R}(f)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq C_{2}|f|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}}.
Similarly for any
h
,

|D\mathcal{R}(f)h|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq 2C_{2}R^{\prime}|h|_{C^{2,\gamma}}.

This is sufficient to show that
\Phi
is a contraction mapping in the ball
B_{R^{\prime}}(0)
, where

R^{\prime}<\min\left\{R_{0},\frac{1}{2C_{1}C_{2}}\right\}.

Indeed, we have

\Phi(f)=\mathcal{L}^{-1}[\tilde{\Psi}-\mathcal{R}(f)]

with
\tilde{\Psi}=\pi\circ\psi-G(0)
, and

|\Phi(f_{2})-\Phi(f_{1})|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq C_{1}|\mathcal{R}(f_{2})-\mathcal{R}(f_{1})|_{C^{0,\gamma}}\leq 2C_{1}C_{2}R^{\prime}|f_{2}-f_{1}|_{C^{2,\gamma}}.

By the choice of
R^{\prime}
this is a contraction. So by Banach contraction mapping theorem, there is a unique fixed point
f\in B_{R^{\prime}}(0)
for
\Phi
. Clearly the fixed-point solution of
\Phi
satisfies
G(f)=\pi\circ\psi
. We also get that

(4.13)

\displaystyle|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}

\displaystyle=\;|\mathcal{L}^{-1}[\tilde{\Psi}-\mathcal{R}(f)]|_{C^{2,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq\;C_{1}|\tilde{\Psi}|_{C^{0,\gamma}}+C_{1}C_{2}|f|^{2}_{C^{2,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{1}|\tilde{\Psi}|_{C^{0,\gamma}}+\frac{1}{2}|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}

Hence
|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq 2C_{1}|\tilde{\Psi}|_{C^{0,\gamma}}
. By local Lipschitz continuity of the condition number

(4.14)

\displaystyle|\kappa(\tilde{g}_{f})-\kappa(X)|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa}|\tilde{g}-X|_{C^{0}}

\displaystyle\leq C_{\kappa}(a+n|b|)|f|_{C^{2,\gamma}}\leq C^{\prime}_{\delta}|\tilde{\Psi}|_{C^{0,\gamma}}

\displaystyle\leq 2C^{\prime}_{\delta}|\log\det\tilde{g}_{f}-\log\det X|_{C^{0,\gamma}(M)}.

For
\epsilon
small, this shows that
\tilde{g}_{f}\in\mathcal{G}_{\delta}
. Here
C_{\kappa}
is the local Lipschitz constant of
\kappa
near
X
.

Set
R=R^{\prime}
to complete the proof with
C_{\delta}=2C^{\prime}_{\delta}
.

∎

Theorem
4.4
implies that the operator remains uniformly elliptic in this range and gives
C^{2,\gamma}
estimates for
f
in
B_{R}(0)
. Now applying the Schauder theorem gives
C^{\infty}
estimates for
f
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.2
.

Open Question:
It would be desirable to remove the small data assumption
|\psi-\log\det(X)|_{C^{0,\gamma}}<\epsilon
in Regime
2
. But it is unclear how to ensure uniform ellipticity along the continuity path. It might be fruitful to consider other paths that can achieve this. The advantage in Regime
2
is that
C^{2}
estimates for the solution are immediate from the cone condition:
\mu_{1}>0
implies that the ratio
\dfrac{\lambda_{1}}{\lambda_{n}}
is bounded above. This combined with the PDE, uniformly bounds all
\lambda_{i}
. Possibly one could search for structural properties of the PDE that avoid the boundary of the elliptic cone, similar to how the Monge-Ampère equation avoids the boundary hyperplanes
\{\lambda_{i}=0\}
.
