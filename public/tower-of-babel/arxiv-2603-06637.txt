Klein–Gordon oscillator with linear–fractional deformed Casimirs in doubly special relativity
Abdelmalek Boumali, Nosratollah Jafari

Abstract
We study the Klein–Gordon (KG) oscillator in a doubly special relativity (DSR) framework where the mass-shell condition is deformed through a linear–fractional (Möbius-type) modification of the Casimir invariant induced by a nonlinear map from physical momenta p μ p^{\mu} to auxiliary Lorentz-covariant variables π μ \pi^{\mu} . In ( 1 + 1 ) (1+1) dimensions the deformation is controlled by a constant covector a μ a_{\mu} , and inequivalent realizations arise depending on whether a μ a_{\mu} is timelike, spacelike, or lightlike. Implementing the KG oscillator through a reverted-product nonminimal coupling, we obtain exact closed-form spectra for both particle and antiparticle branches and explicit eigensolutions in all three geometries. Timelike and lightlike deformations produce identical spectra characterized by a Planck-suppressed additive displacement of both branches; this breaks the exact E ↔ − E E\leftrightarrow-E symmetry via a term linear in E E and can be interpreted as a branch-independent reparametrization of the energy origin. In contrast, the spacelike deformation is strictly isospectral to the undeformed oscillator but generates complex-shifted wavefunctions and a non-Hermitian spatial operator. We provide a compact 𝒫 ​ 𝒯 \mathcal{PT} -symmetric and pseudo-Hermitian formulation by constructing an explicit similarity map to a Hermitian oscillator, deriving the metric operator η = 𝒮 † ​ 𝒮 \eta=\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}\mathcal{S} , and establishing η \eta -orthogonality and biorthonormal relations for the shifted states. Finally, we compare quantitatively with the Magueijo–Smolin (DSR2) model: the squared-denominator invariant leads to a larger Planck-suppressed displacement at fixed m / E p m/E_{\mathrm{p}} , highlighting the role of the denominator power in controlling spectral shifts. A dimensionless parametrization and representative plots are presented to illustrate dependence on the deformation ratio, oscillator strength, and excitation level.

Introduction

The possibility that special-relativistic kinematics may be modified near an observer-independent high-energy scale

E_{\mathrm{p}}
(equivalently, a short length scale
\ell_{\mathrm{p}}=1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
in units
\hbar=c=1

phenomenology. Doubly special relativity (DSR) implements this idea while preserving the relativity principle by
realizing Lorentz transformations
nonlinearly
on momentum space and inducing modified dispersion relations (MDRs)

A complementary line of development connects DSR with

spacetime structures

Constraints on deviations from Lorentz-invariant propagation are typically extremely strong, yet the MDR framework remains a
useful theoretical laboratory and continues to motivate precision searches

11

Exactly solvable relativistic bound-state systems provide a controlled setting for tracking how a deformed Casimir affects
(i) the spectrum, (ii) degeneracy patterns, and (iii) the analytic structure of eigenfunctions. This is particularly useful
in DSR because distinct nonlinear realizations may agree to leading order in
1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
in free-particle kinematics while still
producing qualitatively different operator representations once nonminimal couplings are introduced.
The KG oscillator is an especially convenient benchmark: it retains exact solvability, admits explicit (closed-form)
eigenfunctions, and keeps the two-branch (particle/antiparticle) structure of relativistic spectra transparent.
It is also closely related to the Dirac oscillator, introduced via a nonminimal substitution linear in both position
and momentum

;
19
. In many settings, squaring the Dirac oscillator yields
KG-type oscillator sectors (plus spin-dependent couplings), which further motivates the KG oscillator as a clean starting
point for deformed-kinematics studies.

In this work we solve the KG oscillator subject to a family of
linear–fractional
deformed Casimir constraints.
The deformation is generated by a single constant covector
a_{\mu}
, whose causal character leads to three inequivalent
DSR geometries (timelike, spacelike, lightlike). Importantly, although the free-particle dispersion relations differ only by
which momentum component appears in the deformation denominator, the oscillator implementation makes the differences
manifest: timelike/lightlike deformations displace the spectrum through a linear-in-
E
term, whereas the spacelike case
produces a non-Hermitian (but
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric) operator with complex-shifted eigenfunctions.

Objectives and outline.

The objectives of this paper are threefold.
First, we formulate and solve the Klein–Gordon oscillator under a linear–fractional (first-power) DSR deformation of the
Casimir invariant generated by the nonlinear map introduced in Sec. 2, and we provide exact closed-form expressions for both
energy branches in the three inequivalent geometric realizations (timelike, spacelike, and lightlike).
Second, we clarify the qualitative differences between these geometries at the operator level: we show that timelike and
lightlike choices induce an additive Planck-suppressed displacement of the spectrum and break the exact particle/antiparticle
symmetry through a term linear in
E
, whereas the spacelike choice is exactly isospectral but leads to complex-shifted
eigenfunctions and a non-Hermitian representation of the spatial operator.
Third, we provide a constructive
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric and pseudo-Hermitian treatment of the spacelike sector (and of the
wavefunction sector of the lightlike geometry), explicitly exhibiting the similarity transformation to a Hermitian oscillator,
the associated metric operator, and the resulting orthogonality structure.

In addition, we present a systematic comparison with the Magueijo–Smolin DSR model, emphasizing how the power of the
denominator in the deformed invariant controls the magnitude and functional form of Planck-suppressed spectral shifts at fixed

m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
. To make parameter dependence transparent we introduce a dimensionless formulation and provide representative plots
illustrating the dependence on the deformation ratio
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
, the oscillator strength
\omega/m
, and the excitation level
n

The paper is organized as follows. Section 2 introduces the linear–fractional Casimir deformation and its classification into

\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric construction. Section 5 compares the results with Magueijo–Smolin DSR.
Section 6 presents the dimensionless analysis and numerical illustrations. Section 7 discusses the relation to the Dirac

II
Linear–Fractional Deformed Casimir and Geometric Classification

II.1
Nonlinear map and the first-power Casimir

Let
p^{\mu}
denote physical momenta and let
\pi^{\mu}
be auxiliary variables transforming linearly under Lorentz
transformations,
\pi^{\prime\mu}=\Lambda^{\mu}{}_{\nu}\pi^{\nu}
. Consider the nonlinear map

\pi^{\mu}=\frac{p^{\mu}}{\sqrt{\,1+\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,a_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}\,}},

(1)

where
a_{\mu}
is a constant covector and
\ell_{\mathrm{p}}=1/E_{\mathrm{p}}
.
Imposing the standard auxiliary mass-shell condition

\eta_{\mu\nu}\pi^{\mu}\pi^{\nu}=-m^{2}

(2)

and using (
1
) yields the
linear–fractional
(first-power) deformed Casimir:

\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{1+\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,a_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}}=-m^{2}.

(3)

Equivalent MDR form and small-
\ell_{\mathrm{p}}
expansion.

Multiplying (
3
) by the denominator gives

\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}+m^{2}=-\,m^{2}\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,(a\cdot p),

(4)

which makes explicit that the first-power deformation introduces a
linear
correction in the momentum component
selected by
a_{\mu}
. In the perturbative regime
\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,|a\cdot p|\ll 1
, the deformation is Planck-suppressed and the map is
approximately the identity:

\pi^{\mu}=p^{\mu}\left[1-\frac{\ell_{\mathrm{p}}}{2}(a\cdot p)+O(\ell_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})\right].

(5)

Although (
4
) is algebraically simple, the geometric content is richer because different causal classes of

a_{\mu}
cannot be mapped into each other by Lorentz transformations.

Physical domain.

The map (
1
) is well-defined in the region where
1+\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,a\cdot p>0
. In timelike realizations this is commonly
interpreted as restricting energies away from a pole at
E\simeq E_{\mathrm{p}}
, whereas in spacelike/lightlike realizations the
restriction involves spatial momentum or light-cone combinations. In the bound-state setting we will choose parameters such
that the plotted levels remain in the regular domain and the deformation can be treated consistently within the stationary
ansatz.

II.2
Timelike, spacelike, and lightlike choices of
a_{\mu}

In
(1+1)
dimensions one may choose representatives so that the denominator depends on
E
,
p
, or
E+p
:

timelike:

\displaystyle a_{\mu}=(-1,0),\qquad a_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}=-E,

(6)

spacelike:

\displaystyle a_{\mu}=(0,-1),\qquad a_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}=-p,

(7)

lightlike:

\displaystyle a_{\mu}=(-1,-1),\qquad a_{\alpha}p^{\alpha}=-(E+p).

(8)

Then (
3
) yields the three inequivalent first-power MDRs

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=m^{2},

(9)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{1-p/E_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=m^{2},

(10)

\displaystyle\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{1-(E+p)/E_{\mathrm{p}}}

\displaystyle=m^{2}.

(11)

These reduce to
E^{2}-p^{2}=m^{2}
as
E_{\mathrm{p}}\to\infty
. The causal classification is closely aligned with how time-, space-, and
lightlike
\kappa
-deformations arise in quantum-group approaches to deformed kinematics

12
;
14
;
8
;
9
.

Geometric meaning.

Because
a_{\mu}
is a fixed covector, it selects a preferred direction in momentum space
only
in the sense that the
nonlinear realization is written with respect to that direction; the relativity principle is maintained because the
auxiliary variables
\pi^{\mu}
transform linearly and the deformation is encoded in the nonlinear map between
p^{\mu}
and

\pi^{\mu}
. In
(1+1)
dimensions the three causal classes (timelike/spacelike/lightlike) exhaust the inequivalent cases,
which is why the oscillator problem naturally splits into three sectors with distinct spectral and operator-theoretic
behavior.

II.3
Relation to the Magueijo–Smolin (MS) DSR invariant

In the Magueijo–Smolin realization (often called DSR2), the invariant can be written as

\frac{\eta_{\mu\nu}p^{\mu}p^{\nu}}{(1-\ell_{\mathrm{p}}\,p_{0})^{2}}=-m^{2},

(12)

which in
(1+1)
becomes

\frac{E^{2}-p^{2}}{(1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}})^{2}}=m^{2}.

(13)

The key structural distinction with (
9
) is the
power
of the denominator. This changes the way the
pole at
E=E_{\mathrm{p}}
enters the mass shell and, consequently, changes the scale of Planck-suppressed spectral displacements in
bound-state problems. In particular, expanding at large
E_{\mathrm{p}}
gives

first-power:

\displaystyle E^{2}-p^{2}=m^{2}\left(1-\frac{E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)+O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-2}),

(14)

MS (squared):

\displaystyle E^{2}-p^{2}=m^{2}\left(1-\frac{2E}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)+O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-2}),

(15)

anticipating that (at fixed
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
) MS produces an approximately doubled leading displacement compared with the first-power
timelike/lightlike model.

III
Klein–Gordon Oscillator and Operator Realization

III.1
Reverted-product nonminimal coupling

The KG oscillator is introduced by a nonminimal substitution in the spatial momentum sector. A particularly transparent
ordering is the reverted-product operator

\hat{P}^{2}_{\mathrm{rev}}=(\hat{p}+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)(\hat{p}-\mathrm{i}m\omega x)=\hat{p}^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}-m\omega,

(16)

where
\hat{p}=-\mathrm{i}\,\mathrm{d}/\mathrm{d}x
and
[x,\hat{p}]=\mathrm{i}
. The operator
\hat{p}^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}
has eigenvalues

(2n+1)m\omega
, hence

\hat{P}^{2}_{\mathrm{rev}}\psi_{n}=\lambda_{n}\psi_{n},\qquad\lambda_{n}=2n\,m\omega,\qquad n=0,1,2,\ldots

(17)

We use the stationary ansatz
\Psi(t,x)=e^{-\mathrm{i}Et}\psi(x)
, so that
\hat{E}\to E
.

Number-operator representation (useful for interpretation).

Defining the standard ladder operators

a=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m\omega}}\left(m\omega x+\mathrm{i}\hat{p}\right),\qquad a^{\dagger}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{2m\omega}}\left(m\omega x-\mathrm{i}\hat{p}\right),

(18)

one finds
\hat{P}^{2}_{\mathrm{rev}}=2m\omega\,a^{\dagger}a
, so that the reverted-product ordering corresponds directly to
the nonnegative number operator eigenvalues
2m\omega\,n
. This makes the ground state
\lambda_{0}=0
especially transparent
and keeps constant shifts explicit when comparing different deformed Casimirs.

Remark on ordering.

The reverted-product choice (
16
) is not merely cosmetic: it preserves exact solvability and produces the clean
eigenvalue reduction (
17
). Other orderings differ by constant shifts (effectively
n\to n+1
) and may obscure
direct quantitative comparisons between distinct MDR models, especially when Planck-suppressed additive shifts are also
present.

III.2
Promoting the deformed Casimir to an operator constraint

In each geometry we promote the dispersion relation to an operator equation by substituting
p^{2}\to\hat{P}^{2}_{\mathrm{rev}}

and treating
E
as a spectral parameter in the stationary sector. Where a linear
p
appears (spacelike/lightlike), we
implement the oscillator consistently by using the same nonminimal structure in the linear term:

p\ \longrightarrow\ (\hat{p}+\mathrm{i}m\omega x),

(19)

which is the natural companion to (
16
). This choice keeps the deformation aligned with the oscillator’s
creation/annihilation structure and is crucial for enabling the explicit similarity transformation used below.

On operator ordering and physical observables.

In deformed kinematics, different operator realizations may be related by similarity transformations or may correspond to
different prescriptions for coupling the MDR to interactions. Our aim here is to adopt a minimal and analytically tractable
realization that (i) reduces to the usual KG oscillator as
E_{\mathrm{p}}\to\infty
, (ii) preserves exact solvability, and (iii) makes
the causal distinction of
a_{\mu}
operational at the spectral and wavefunction level. The pseudo-Hermitian construction in
Sec. 4 further guarantees a consistent inner-product structure whenever non-Hermitian operators occur.

IV
Exact Eigensolutions in the Three Geometries

IV.1
Timelike geometry

From (
9
) with
p^{2}\to\hat{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{2}
:

\frac{E^{2}-\hat{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{2}}{1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}}}=m^{2}\quad\Longrightarrow\quad E^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}E-\left(\hat{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{2}+m^{2}\right)=0.

(20)

Acting on
\psi_{n}
and using (
17
) yields

E^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}E-\left(m^{2}+2n\,m\omega\right)=0,

(21)

so that the two branches are

E_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{TL}}=-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega+\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}.

(22)

Branch structure and displacement.

Equation (
22
) makes two basic effects explicit:
(i) the spectrum remains real and discrete, and (ii) both branches are shifted by the same additive amount

-m^{2}/(2E_{\mathrm{p}})
, breaking the exact particle–antiparticle symmetry
E_{n,-}=-E_{n,+}
.
A simple diagnostic is the branch sum,

E_{n,+}^{\mathrm{TL}}+E_{n,-}^{\mathrm{TL}}=-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}},

(23)

which vanishes in the undeformed oscillator but is fixed here by the deformation scale.

Perturbative regime and admissible levels.

In the regime
E_{\mathrm{p}}\gg m
one finds

E_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{TL}}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega}\;-\;\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}+O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-2}).

(24)

The timelike model contains a pole at
E=E_{\mathrm{p}}
. A conservative condition for remaining in the regular domain is

E_{n,+}/E_{\mathrm{p}}<1
, which (at leading order) suggests

n\lesssim\frac{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}-m^{2}}{2m\omega}.

(25)

In practical illustrative plots (Sec. 6) we choose parameters so that all plotted positive-branch levels satisfy this
constraint comfortably.

IV.1.1
Energy reparametrization viewpoint for timelike/lightlike sectors

A useful way to interpret the linear term in
E
is to complete the square in (
21
). Writing

\left(E+\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}=m^{2}+2n\,m\omega+\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}},

(26)

we introduce the shifted variable

\widetilde{E}:=E+\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}.

(27)

In terms of
\widetilde{E}
the spectrum becomes symmetric:

\widetilde{E}_{n,\pm}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega+\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}},

(28)

while the
physical
energies
E_{n,\pm}=\widetilde{E}_{n,\pm}-m^{2}/(2E_{\mathrm{p}})
inherit an overall displacement.
Thus, one may regard the timelike/lightlike deformation as implementing a universal Planck-suppressed reparametrization
of the energy origin: the two branches remain opposite in
\widetilde{E}
, but are both shifted in
E
.
This viewpoint is convenient when comparing deformations: at leading order, the first-power deformation yields

\Delta E\simeq-m^{2}/(2E_{\mathrm{p}})
, whereas MS yields
\Delta E\simeq-m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}
(cf. Sec.
V
).

Wavefunctions.

The spatial eigenproblem remains the standard reverted-product oscillator; thus one may take

\psi_{n}^{\mathrm{TL}}(x)=\phi_{n}(x)
, with
\phi_{n}
given explicitly in Appendix
A
. The deformation therefore
affects the timelike sector
only
through the energy eigenvalues, not through spatial localization properties at the
level of
\psi(x)
.

IV.2
Spacelike geometry

Starting from (
10
) and implementing the oscillator in the linear term according to (
19
) gives

E^{2}\psi=\left[\hat{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{2}+m^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\,(\hat{p}+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)\right]\psi.

(29)

Using (
16
), one finds

\displaystyle E^{2}\psi

\displaystyle=\biggl[\left(\hat{p}^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\hat{p}\right)+\left(m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{m^{3}\omega}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}x\right)+m^{2}-m\omega\biggr]\psi.

(30)

While (
30
) is non-Hermitian in the standard
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
inner product, it remains exactly solvable
and admits a consistent
\eta
-inner product (pseudo-Hermiticity).

Completing squares and isospectrality.

Completing the square in momentum and position gives

\displaystyle\hat{p}^{2}-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\hat{p}

\displaystyle=\left(\hat{p}-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}-\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}},

(31)

\displaystyle m^{2}\omega^{2}x^{2}-\mathrm{i}\frac{m^{3}\omega}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}x

\displaystyle=m^{2}\omega^{2}\left(x-\mathrm{i}\frac{m}{2\omega E_{\mathrm{p}}}\right)^{2}+\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}},

(32)

so the constants cancel and one obtains

E^{2}\psi=\left[(\hat{p}^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}\omega^{2}(x^{\prime})^{2}+m^{2}-m\omega\right]\psi,\qquad\hat{p}^{\prime}=\hat{p}-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}},\quad x^{\prime}=x-\mathrm{i}\frac{m}{2\omega E_{\mathrm{p}}}.

(33)

Hence

E^{2}=m^{2}+2n\,m\omega,\qquad\Rightarrow\qquad E_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{SL}}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega}.

(34)

The spacelike deformation is therefore
isospectral
: it changes eigenfunctions but not eigenvalues.

IV.2.1
Explicit eigenfunctions,
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry, and pseudo-Hermiticity

Similarity map (consistent operator statement).

Define the deformation-induced shifts

\delta=\frac{m}{2\omega E_{\mathrm{p}}},\qquad\kappa=\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}.

(35)

Introduce the (generally non-unitary) similarity operator

\mathcal{S}=e^{-\delta\hat{p}}\,e^{-\mathrm{i}\kappa x},

(36)

so that

\mathcal{S}^{-1}x\,\mathcal{S}=x-\mathrm{i}\delta,\qquad\mathcal{S}^{-1}\hat{p}\,\mathcal{S}=\hat{p}-\kappa.

(37)

Then the non-Hermitian operator in (
30
) is similar to a Hermitian oscillator operator
h
:

H=\mathcal{S}^{-1}h\,\mathcal{S},\qquad h^{\dagger}=h,

(38)

implying isospectrality and guaranteeing a real spectrum (unbroken
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry).

Eigenfunctions.

If
\phi_{n}
denotes the standard harmonic-oscillator eigenfunction (Appendix
A
),

\phi_{n}(x)=\mathcal{N}_{n}\exp\!\left(-\frac{m\omega}{2}x^{2}\right)H_{n}\!\left(\sqrt{m\omega}\,x\right),

(39)

then a corresponding eigenfunction of (
30
) is

\psi_{n}^{\mathrm{SL}}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\kappa x}\,\phi_{n}(x-\mathrm{i}\delta).

(40)

The spatial profile is therefore a complex translation of the Hermite-Gaussian, accompanied by a plane-wave phase.

Why the spectrum remains real.

With parity
\mathcal{P}:x\to-x,\ \hat{p}\to-\hat{p}
and time reversal

\mathcal{T}:\mathrm{i}\to-\mathrm{i},\ \hat{p}\to-\hat{p}
, the combination
-\mathrm{i}x
is
\mathcal{PT}
-even. Consequently the
imaginary linear term in
x
in (
30
) is
\mathcal{PT}
symmetric. More strongly, the similarity map
(
36
) yields pseudo-Hermiticity and the associated metric operator and orthogonality relations,
summarized in Appendix
B

4
;
17
;
18
.

Physical inner product and observables (interpretive note).

Because
H
is non-Hermitian in the standard inner product, expectation values should be computed with the
\eta
-inner
product
(\cdot,\cdot)_{\eta}
induced by the metric
\eta=\mathcal{S}^{\dagger}\mathcal{S}
. In this framework, the spectrum
is real and the eigenfunctions form an
\eta
-orthonormal basis, so the spacelike deformation defines a consistent quantum
theory despite the non-Hermitian appearance of (
30
).

IV.3
Lightlike geometry

From (
11
) one obtains, after operator promotion,

\displaystyle E^{2}+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}E-\left(\hat{P}_{\mathrm{rev}}^{2}+m^{2}\right)+\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}(\hat{p}+\mathrm{i}m\omega x)=0.

(41)

The last term has the same structure as in the spacelike sector and is removed by the same similarity map; consequently,
one again obtains (
21

E_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{LL}}=-\frac{m^{2}}{2E_{\mathrm{p}}}\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega+\frac{m^{4}}{4E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}.

(42)

Thus, the lightlike deformation shares the
timelike
spectrum but inherits spacelike-type complex-shifted eigenfunctions:

\psi_{n}^{\mathrm{LL}}(x)=e^{\mathrm{i}\kappa x}\,\phi_{n}(x-\mathrm{i}\delta),

(43)

with
\kappa,\delta
as in (
35
). The energy-reparametrization interpretation of
Sec.
IV.1.1
applies equally to this sector.

Interpretation.

The lightlike case can be viewed as combining (i) the timelike-type linear term in
E
that displaces both branches and
(ii) the spacelike-type linear momentum contribution that produces a non-Hermitian but
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric spatial
operator. This makes the lightlike realization a useful “hybrid” laboratory for disentangling which aspects of the
deformation affect spectral positions versus wavefunction structure.

V
Comparison with Magueijo–Smolin DSR

For MS DSR, the dispersion relation is (
13
). Implementing the oscillator via

p^{2}\to\hat{P}^{2}_{\mathrm{rev}}
and acting on
\psi_{n}
gives

\frac{E^{2}-\lambda_{n}}{(1-E/E_{\mathrm{p}})^{2}}=m^{2},\qquad\lambda_{n}=2n\,m\omega,

(44)

which leads to

\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)E^{2}+\frac{2m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}E-(m^{2}+2n\,m\omega)=0.

(45)

Hence, writing the result in a column-safe form,

\displaystyle E_{n,\pm}^{(\mathrm{MS})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{1-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}}\biggl[-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}\pm\sqrt{\,m^{2}+2n\,m\omega\!\left(1-\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2}}\right)}\biggr].

(46)

For
E_{\mathrm{p}}\gg m
,

E_{n,\pm}^{(\mathrm{MS})}=\pm\sqrt{m^{2}+2n\,m\omega}\;-\;\frac{m^{2}}{E_{\mathrm{p}}}+O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-2}).

(47)

Quantitative comparison and role of the denominator power.

Both models break the exact
E\leftrightarrow-E
symmetry by shifting both branches by the same additive amount, but the
magnitude differs: MS gives a leading displacement twice that of the first-power timelike/lightlike model
(
24
). Beyond leading order, MS also introduces a prefactor

(1-m^{2}/E_{\mathrm{p}}^{2})^{-1}
that produces additional
O(E_{\mathrm{p}}^{-2})
distortions; hence MS is not reducible to a pure energy-origin
shift once higher-order terms are kept. This makes the power of the denominator a genuinely model-defining input in
DSR-inspired bound-state spectra.

Pole structure and admissible parameters.

Both timelike first-power and MS contain a pole at
E=E_{\mathrm{p}}
. For phenomenologically realistic masses
\epsilon=m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
is tiny,
so the pole is far above oscillator energies unless
\omega
or
n
are taken extremely large. In illustrative plots (Sec. 6)
we nevertheless enforce a conservative “below-pole” criterion to avoid spurious near-pole artifacts.

VI
Parameter Dependence and Numerical Illustration

VI.1
Dimensionless form

Introduce the dimensionless ratios

\Omega=\frac{\omega}{m},\qquad\epsilon=\frac{m}{E_{\mathrm{p}}},\qquad e_{n,\pm}=\frac{E_{n,\pm}}{m}.

(48)

The special-relativistic spectrum reads

e_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{SR}}=\pm\sqrt{1+2n\Omega}.

(49)

For the first-power Casimir model:

spacelike:

\displaystyle e_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{SL}}=\pm\sqrt{1+2n\Omega},

(50)

timelike/lightlike:

\displaystyle e_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{TL}}=e_{n,\pm}^{\mathrm{LL}}=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}\pm\sqrt{1+2n\Omega+\frac{\epsilon^{2}}{4}}.

(51)

For MS:

e_{n,\pm}^{(\mathrm{MS})}=\frac{-\epsilon\pm\sqrt{1+2n\Omega(1-\epsilon^{2})}}{1-\epsilon^{2}}.

(52)

Interpretation of dimensionless parameters.

\Omega=\omega/m
measures the oscillator strength relative to rest mass, while
\epsilon=m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
controls the magnitude of
DSR corrections. In realistic particle-physics settings
\epsilon\ll 1
, so the DSR-induced spectral displacement is tiny
and essentially
n
-independent at leading order. In contrast, in quantum-mechanical analogue contexts or in purely
theoretical explorations, larger
\epsilon
can be used to visualize the structure of the deformation while keeping the
interpretation of the Planck scale as an effective cutoff.

VI.2
Leading-order interpretation

For
\epsilon\ll 1
:

\displaystyle e_{n,+}^{\mathrm{TL}}-e_{n,+}^{\mathrm{SR}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{\epsilon}{2}+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(53)

\displaystyle e_{n,+}^{(\mathrm{MS})}-e_{n,+}^{\mathrm{SR}}

\displaystyle=-\epsilon+O(\epsilon^{2}),

(54)

while spacelike is exactly isospectral. The qualitative distinction between SR/spacelike and timelike-type deformations is
that the linear-in-
E
term yields an additive displacement of both branches, breaking
e_{n,-}=-e_{n,+}
.

Large-
n
behavior (fixed
\Omega
, small
\epsilon
).

At fixed
\Omega
and
\epsilon\ll 1
, the undeformed growth is
|e_{n,\pm}^{\rm SR}|\sim\sqrt{2n\Omega}
, while the
leading DSR shifts remain approximately constant,
\Delta e\sim-\epsilon/2
(first-power) or
\Delta e\sim-\epsilon
(MS).
Thus, for sufficiently large
n
the relative correction
|\Delta e|/|e_{n,+}^{\rm SR}|
decreases as
n^{-1/2}
even though
the absolute displacement is
n
-independent at leading order.

VII
Relation to the Dirac Oscillator and Outlook

The Dirac oscillator is a canonical relativistic oscillator model obtained from the Dirac equation by a nonminimal
substitution linear in position
16
;
19
. It has deep connections with quantum optics
through exact mappings to Jaynes–Cummings-type models
5
and has even been realized
experimentally in analogue platforms
6
. In
(1+1)
dimensions, the squared Dirac oscillator
reduces to KG-type oscillator sectors with comparatively simple spin structure, suggesting that extensions of the present
analysis to spinorial DSR deformations are tractable.

From a DSR perspective, two directions are particularly natural.
First, one can introduce the same linear–fractional Casimir deformation at the Dirac level, then analyze how the resulting
linear-in-
E
(or linear-in-
p
) terms reorganize the usual Dirac-oscillator ladder structure and spinor component coupling.
Because the KG oscillator already isolates the purely orbital sector, the present results provide a baseline for predicting
which deformations primarily cause
energy-origin shifts
(timelike/lightlike) and which primarily cause
non-Hermitian
representations
(spacelike-type).

Second, it is interesting to combine the present MDR deformation with deformed phase-space structures motivated by

\kappa
-Poincaré symmetry. In such settings, not only the dispersion relation but also the canonical commutators and
operator realizations may be modified, and the interplay with pseudo-Hermiticity becomes nontrivial. In particular, it would
be valuable to determine when
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry remains unbroken and to identify the corresponding metric operators
systematically for oscillator-type models.

More broadly, the analytic solvability of the present
(1+1)
system suggests several immediate extensions:
(i) higher dimensions (with possible degeneracy lifting),
(ii) external fields (electric or magnetic) that compete with the oscillator scale,
and (iii) finite-temperature or thermal-field-theory versions where branch asymmetry can affect thermodynamic quantities.
Each of these extensions may be sensitive to the denominator power in the Casimir and therefore provide additional criteria
for discriminating DSR realizations in effective models.

VIII
Conclusion

We solved the KG oscillator for a family of linear–fractional (first-power) deformed Casimir invariants and classified the
results by timelike, spacelike, and lightlike momentum-space geometries.

Main spectral outcomes.

Timelike and lightlike deformations yield identical closed-form spectra with a Planck-suppressed additive shift that breaks
the exact particle/antiparticle symmetry:
both branches are displaced by
-m^{2}/(2E_{\mathrm{p}})
at leading order, while the branch separation remains governed primarily by the
usual oscillator scale
\sqrt{m^{2}+2nm\omega}
. The spacelike deformation is exactly isospectral, so the entire discrete set of
oscillator energies is unchanged compared to special relativity.

Operator-theoretic and wavefunction outcomes.

Although the spacelike spectrum remains real and undeformed, the associated spatial operator becomes non-Hermitian in the
standard
L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
inner product and the eigenfunctions acquire a complex translation and a phase factor. We showed
explicitly that this is an example of an unbroken
\mathcal{PT}
-symmetric sector and provided a constructive pseudo-Hermitian
formulation: an explicit similarity map to a Hermitian oscillator, the corresponding metric operator, and the resulting

\eta
-orthogonality/biorthonormal structure. The lightlike realization combines the timelike-type spectral displacement with
the spacelike-type wavefunction shift, making it a useful hybrid testing ground for separating “energy-shift” effects from
“state-deformation” effects.

Comparison with MS and significance.

Comparison with the Magueijo–Smolin DSR model highlights the role of the denominator power in the deformed Casimir: the MS
squared invariant produces a leading displacement twice as large as the first-power model at fixed
m/E_{\mathrm{p}}
, and also induces
additional higher-order distortions not reducible to a pure energy-origin shift. This shows that, in solvable bound-state
problems, the detailed functional form of the MDR (not merely the existence of a Planck scale) controls observable spectral
differences in effective models.

Outlook.

The present analysis provides a controlled benchmark for studying DSR-inspired deformations in interacting relativistic
systems. Extensions to Dirac oscillators, higher dimensions, and external fields appear particularly promising, especially
for clarifying when pseudo-Hermiticity and
\mathcal{PT}
symmetry protect spectral reality and for determining how different
nonlinear realizations of DSR reorganize operator algebras beyond the free-particle sector.
