Optimal Vector Balancing for Zonotopes
Victor Reis

Abstract
A zonotope is a linear image of the cube [ − 1 , 1 ] m [-1,1]^{m} for some m ∈ ℕ m\in\mathbb{N} . We show that there is a universal constant C > 0 C>0 such that, for every zonotope Z ⊂ ℝ d Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{d} and vectors v 1 , … , v n ∈ Z v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in Z , there are signs x 1 , … , x n ∈ { − 1 , 1 } x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\{-1,1\} with ∑ i = 1 n x i ​ v i ∈ C ​ d ​ Z . \sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}\in C\sqrt{d}\,Z. This resolves a 2002 question of Schechtman and generalizes Spencer’s six standard deviations theorem, which corresponds to the case Z = [ − 1 , 1 ] d Z=[-1,1]^{d} .



1
Introduction

Spencer’s
six standard deviations theorem

[
Spe85
]
states that for every choice of vectors
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\in[-1,1]^{d}
, there are signs
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\in\{-1,1\}
so that

\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}v_{i}\Big\|_{\infty}\leq 6\sqrt{d}.

In 2002, Schechtman asked whether a
C\sqrt{d}
bound holds for vectors in an
arbitrary
zonotope

Z=A^{\top}[-1,1]^{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This question appears as the first problem in
Open problems on embeddings
of finite metric spaces

[
MN03
, Problem 2.1]
; see
also
[
AIM07
, Problem 1.1]
. Our main theorem answers it affirmatively.

Theorem 1
.

There is a universal constant
C>0
such that the following holds. Let

Z\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a zonotope. Then, for every integer
n\in[1,d]
and all

v_{1},\ldots,v_{n}\in Z
, there exist polynomial-time computable signs

x_{i}\in\{-1,1\}
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}\in C\sqrt{n\log\Big(\frac{2d}{n}\Big)}\,Z.

Previously, the best known bound for
n=d
was
O(\sqrt{d}\,\log\log\log d)

[
HRR23
]
. For the cube
Z=[-1,1]^{d}
, this recovers Spencer’s bound and is optimal up to universal constants for every
n
and
d

[
Rei23
, Theorem 71]
. A standard linear-algebraic argument shows that for
n>d
, the bound is the same as the
n=d
bound up to a factor of two
[
Rei23
, Theorem 3]
.

There is a natural route to this statement through zonotope sparsification.
Talagrand proved that there exists a zonotope
\widetilde{Z}=\tilde{A}^{\top}[-1,1]^{\widetilde{m}}
with
\widetilde{m}=O(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log d)
and
\widetilde{Z}\subseteq Z\subseteq(1+\varepsilon)\widetilde{Z}

[
Tal90
]
. A 1987 problem of Schechtman
[
Sch87
, Problem 7]
(see also
[
AIM07
, Problem 1.12]
) asks whether the logarithmic factor can be removed: does every

d
-dimensional zonotope admit such an approximation with
\widetilde{m}=d\cdot C(\varepsilon)
?

Spencer
[
Spe85
]
proved more generally that for vectors
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in[-1,1]^{m}
, there are signs
x_{1},\dots,x_{n}\in\{-1,1\}
so that

\Big\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}u_{i}\Big\|_{\infty}\leq 11\sqrt{n\log\Big(\frac{2m}{n}\Big)}.

Thus assuming an approximation
\widetilde{Z}
with
\widetilde{m}=d\cdot C(\varepsilon)
exists for constant
\varepsilon
, given
v_{1},\dots,v_{d}\in Z
, we may write
v_{i}=(1+\varepsilon)\tilde{A}^{\top}u_{i}
and apply Spencer’s theorem to
u_{i}\in[-1,1]^{\widetilde{m}}
, yielding
\|\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}u_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 11\sqrt{d\log\Big(\frac{2\widetilde{m}}{d}\Big)}=11\sqrt{1+\log C(\varepsilon)}\sqrt{d}
and consequently
\sum_{i=1}^{d}x_{i}v_{i}\in 11(1+\varepsilon)\sqrt{1+\log C(\varepsilon)}\sqrt{d}Z
. Therefore, this would yield an affirmative answer to Schechtman’s zonotope vector balancing question. Alas, the bound
\widetilde{m}=O(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log d)
due to Talagrand remains the state of the art for zonotope sparsification, so this approach currently only leads to an
O(\sqrt{d\log\log d})
vector balancing bound.

1.1
Proof overview

We reduce the problem to a volumetric bound on quotients of sections of the
\ell_{1}
ball. In Section 2, we recall the
Lewis position
of the polar of a zonotope, which we denote by
K_{1}
, along with other concepts that will be needed later in the proof. In Section 3, we apply
Gordon’s escape theorem
to find sections of
K_{1}
with small radius. In Section 4, we generalize this to sections of linear images
MK_{1}
. For this, we will need to interpolate between
K_{1}
and the Euclidean ball. To deal with the linear maps, we use the fact that linear maps preserve coverings, and bound entropy numbers via
Carl’s inequality
. In Section 5, we pass from the Lewis position to arbitrary sections of the
\ell_{1}
ball, apply Carl’s inequality again to bound entropy numbers of quotients of sections, and derive the volumetric estimates.
Finally, in Section 6 we identify the convex body associated with balancing vectors on a zonotope with the quotient of a section of the
\ell_{1}
ball and construct a partial coloring, which can be iterated to achieve the optimal vector balancing bound.

We use
A\lesssim B
to denote that there exists a universal constant
C
with
{A\leq C\cdot B}
. For
p\in[1,\infty]
,
B_{p}^{d}

is the unit ball of
\ell_{p}^{d}
, and
\log
denotes the binary logarithm.

1.2
Other related work

Theorem
1
was previously known in the special case where
Z=B^{d}_{p}
,
p\in[2,\infty]
, for which the tight bound is
\Theta(\sqrt{n\min\{p,\log(2d/n)\}})

[
RR22
]
. While these are not strictly zonotopes for
p<\infty
, they are
zonoids
, which can be approximated arbitrarily well by a zonotope, so Theorem
1
still applies.

Suppose the vectors satisfy
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in\{0,1\}^{d}
with at most
t
ones each. Beck and Fiala
[
BF81
]

proved, using a linear-algebraic argument, that there are signs with
\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq 2t
and conjectured an
O(\sqrt{t})
bound. A recent work of Bansal and Jiang
[
BJ25
]
resolved the Beck–Fiala conjecture for
t\geq\log^{2}n
and obtained a bound of
(\sqrt{t}+\sqrt{\log n})\cdot(\log\log n)^{O(1)}
for smaller values of
t
; when
t\leq\sqrt{\log n}
, the best bound is still
2t-\log^{*}(t)

[
Buk16
]
.

A stronger conjecture is due to Komlós, who asked whether for
v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\in B_{2}^{d}
there exist signs with
\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}\|_{\infty}\leq O(1)
. This would also generalize Spencer’s theorem, and the best known bound of
(\log^{1/4}n)\cdot(\log\log n)^{O(1)}
is also due to Bansal and Jiang
[
BJ25
]
.

Another open generalization of Spencer’s theorem is the
Matrix Spencer conjecture

[
Zou12
,
Mek14
]
which asks whether for symmetric matrices
A_{1},\ldots,A_{n}\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times n}
with eigenvalues in
[-1,1]
, there are
signs
x\in\{-1,1\}^{n}
so that the maximum singular value of
\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}A_{i}
is at most
O(\sqrt{n})
. This conjecture has only been proved under the additional assumption that the matrices are
block-diagonal with constant-size blocks
[
DJR22
]
, or have rank at most
\frac{n}{\log^{3}n}

[
BJM23
]
.

Regarding the zonotope sparsification problem, Cohen and Peng
[
CP15
]
made Talagrand’s
O(\frac{d}{\varepsilon^{2}}\log d)
sparsity bound algorithmic. Linear-size sparsification has only recently been obtained for
O(1)
-modular zonotopes
[
ERRS26
]
, and Talagrand’s bound remains the state of the art in general.

2
Preliminaries

A symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
is a compact convex set with nonempty interior so that
K=-K
. Its associated norm is
\|x\|_{K}:=\inf\{t>0:x\in tK\}
, its polar is
K^{\circ}:=\{y\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:|\langle x,y\rangle|\leq 1\forall x\in K\}
, and
\|y\|_{K^{\circ}}:=\sup_{x\in K}|\langle x,y\rangle|
is the polar norm. When a symmetric convex set
K
is contained in a proper subspace, its norm and its polar are also considered to be taken inside that subspace.

2.1
Lewis position, John decomposition, and volume ratio

Throughout, fix a full-dimensional zonotope
Z=A^{\top}B^{m}_{\infty}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{m\times d}
has rank
d
, and all rows
A_{1},\ldots,A_{m}
are nonzero. We normalize the polar of
Z
so that the zonotope is in
Lewis position

[
Lew78
]
. Observe that

Z^{\circ}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}|\langle x,A_{i}\rangle|\leq 1\Big\}.

Theorem 2

(
[
Bal91
, Lemma 9]
)
.

There are unit vectors

u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\in\partial B_{2}^{d}
, weights
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}>0
, and an invertible linear map
T:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{d}
such that

K_{1}:=TZ^{\circ}=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|\leq 1\Big\},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\,u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}=I_{d}.

(
\dagger
)

In Sections 3 and 4, we put
Z
in Lewis position and denote
K_{1}:=Z^{\circ}
.

This normalization is slightly different from the one in
[
HRR23
]
, where
normalized zonotopes
are defined using Lewis weights. One can check that if a zonotope
Z
is in the above Lewis position, then for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a normalized zonotope
\widetilde{Z}
with

(1-\varepsilon)\widetilde{Z}\subset Z\subset(1+\varepsilon)\widetilde{Z}
. We will not use this, as the Lewis position is more convenient.

For
p\in[1,2]
, define the interpolation bodies

K_{p}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|^{p}\leq 1\}.

Thus
K_{2}=B_{2}^{d}
. The next two lemmas follow directly from Hölder’s inequality.

Lemma 3
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
, and put
q=p/(p-1)
. Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|x\|_{K_{1}}\leq d^{1/q}\|x\|_{K_{p}}.

Lemma 4
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
, and put
q=p/(p-1)
. Then, for every
x\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
,

\|x\|_{K_{p}}\leq\|x\|_{K_{1}}^{1-2/q}\|x\|_{2}^{2/q}.

We also use the following inclusions:

Lemma 5
.

One has
\frac{1}{\sqrt{d}}B_{2}^{d}\subseteq K_{1}\subseteq B_{2}^{d}
.

Proof.

The first inclusion follows from Lemma
3
with
p=2
. Take
x\in K_{1}
. By (

†

‣
2
),

\|x\|_{2}^{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|^{2}.

Since
u_{i}\in\partial B_{2}^{d}
, we have
|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|\leq\|x\|_{2}
, so

\|x\|_{2}^{2}\leq\|x\|_{2}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|\leq\|x\|_{2}.

Then
\|x\|_{2}\leq 1
, so
K_{1}\subseteq B_{2}^{d}
.
∎

For a different convex body, we also use the symmetric John position
[
Joh48
]
.

Theorem 6
.

Let
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a symmetric convex body whose
maximal-volume inscribed ellipsoid is
B_{2}^{n}
. There are unit vectors

u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\in K\cap K^{\circ}\cap\partial B_{2}^{n}
and weights

c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}=I_{n}.

For a full-dimensional convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, let

\pazocal E_{K}
denote the maximal-volume ellipsoid contained in
K
, and define

\vr(K):=\left(\frac{\vol_{n}(K)}{\vol_{n}(\pazocal E_{K})}\right)^{1/n}
.
This quantity is invariant under invertible linear transformations, and so it will later be useful to assume John position when bounding the volume ratio of a convex body.

2.2
Entropy and Gelfand numbers

For convex bodies
K,L\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
, let
N(K,L)
be the least number of translates of
L
needed to cover
K
, and define the
k
th dyadic
entropy number
by

e_{k}(K,L):=\inf\{t>0:N(K,tL)\leq 2^{k-1}\}.

We abbreviate
e_{k}(K):=e_{k}(K,B_{2}^{d})
and use this quantity to upper bound volume. We in turn upper bound entropy numbers in terms of
section radii
known in the operator theory literature as Gelfand numbers:

Definition 7
.

Let
K,L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be symmetric convex bodies and denote

H:=\spanop K
. For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, define

r_{k}(K,L):=\inf\left\{\sup_{x\in K\cap F}\|x\|_{L}:F\subseteq H\text{ a subspace},\ \codim_{H}F<k\right\}.

We abbreviate
r_{k}(K):=r_{k}(K,B_{2}^{d})
. Clearly,
r_{k}(K,L)
is decreasing in
k
, and if
k>\dim H
, then
r_{k}(K,L)=0
, since one may take
F=\{\mathbf{0}\}
. The infimum is attained: for each
k
the corresponding
Grassmannian in
H
is compact, and the objective is continuous on
that Grassmannian.

Carl’s inequality
upper bounds entropy numbers in terms of section radii; see
[
Car81
]
and Pisier’s textbook
[
Pis99
, Chapter 5]
. We state the general version, although we only need it for
\alpha=1
and
L=B^{d}_{2}
.

Theorem 8
.

For every
\alpha>0
there is a constant
C_{\alpha}
such that the following
holds. Let
K,L
be symmetric convex bodies in
\mathbb{R}^{d}
, and let
k\geq 1
be an
integer. Then

\max_{j\in[1,k]}j^{\alpha}e_{j}(K,L)\leq C_{\alpha}\max_{j\in[1,k]}j^{\alpha}r_{j}(K,L).

We use
Gordon’s escape theorem

[
Gor88
]
to construct sections of small radii. For a bounded set
S\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, its
(Gaussian) width
is
w(S):=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{g\sim N(0,I_{n})}\sup_{x\in S}\langle g,x\rangle
.

Theorem 9

(
[
Ver18
, Theorem 9.3.4]
)
.

Let
S\subseteq\partial B_{2}^{n}
be any set, and let
G
be an
m\times n
random matrix with independent standard Gaussian entries.

If
m\gtrsim w(S)^{2}
, then the random subspace
F:=\ker G
satisfies
S\cap F=\emptyset
with probability at least
1-2\exp(-\Omega(m))
.
In particular, there exists a universal constant
m_{\mathrm{E}}\in\mathbb{N}
so that for

m\geq m_{\mathrm{E}}\max\{1,w(S)^{2}\}
one can find a subspace

F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
\codim F\leq m
and
S\cap F=\emptyset
.

We now turn Gaussian width into a section radius bound. This is the
geometric form of Carl–Pajor’s Gelfand-number estimate
[
CP88
, Lemma 2.1]
,
also recorded in Pisier’s textbook
[
Pis99
, Chapter 5, Theorem 5.8]
. A self-contained proof using Theorem
9
is included in Appendix
A
.

Proposition 10
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a symmetric convex body and
k\in\mathbb{N}
. Then

r_{k}(K)\lesssim\frac{w(K)}{\sqrt{k}}.

2.3
Covariance domination and Gaussian width

In this subsection, we translate tools from operator theory to convex geometric language. For completeness, we include the short proofs in Appendix
A
.

For a convex symmetric body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
, define
\pazocal M(K):=\conv\{yy^{\top}:y\in K\}
and

\pazocal{B}_{n}(K):=\left\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{n\times d}:M^{\top}M\preceq A\text{ for some }A\in\pazocal M(K^{\circ})\right\}.

Here
B\preceq A
means that
A-B
is positive semidefinite.
\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)
is the unit ball of the
absolutely
2
-summing norm introduced by
Pietsch
[
Pie79
]
; see
[
Pis99
, Chapter 1]
.

Lemma 11
.

\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)
is convex for any symmetric convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Definition 12
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a symmetric convex body. Define

\Gamma(K):=\sup_{A\in\pazocal M(K^{\circ})}\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{g\sim N(0,A)}\|g\|_{K^{\circ}}.

The supremum is attained since
\pazocal M(K^{\circ})
is compact. In the language of operator theory, this is referred to as the Gaussian type-
2
constant
[
LT91
, Chapter 9]
.

Lemma 13
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a symmetric convex body, and let

M:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. Then

r_{1}(MK)\leq\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)}.

The next lemma bounds the width of a linear image
[
DMT81
, Lemma 1]
.

Lemma 14
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a symmetric convex body, and let
M:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be
linear. Then

w(MK)\leq\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)}\Gamma(K).

Finally, we use a bound on the Gaussian width from a cover.

Lemma 15
.

Let
K\subseteq rB_{2}^{n}
be bounded, and let
L\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be symmetric and convex. If

N(K,tL)\leq 2^{k-1},

then

w(K)\lesssim r\sqrt{k}+t\,w(L).

2.4
Partial coloring from hereditary volume bounds

For
S\subseteq[n]
, write
\mathbb{R}^{S}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}:x_{i}=0\ \forall i\notin S\}
. For a convex body
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, define
K_{S}:=K\cap\mathbb{R}^{S}
.
We regard
K_{S}
as a convex body in the coordinate space
\mathbb{R}^{S}
and use

\vol_{|S|}
for volume in that space. We also use the convention

\vol_{0}(K_{\emptyset})=1
.

Let
\gamma_{n}
denote the standard Gaussian measure on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
. We use two results of Reis and Rothvoss. The first,
[
RR22
, Theorem 7]
, says that hereditary lower bounds on coordinate-section volumes imply a Gaussian measure lower bound.

Theorem 16
.

For every symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\gamma_{n}(K)^{1/n}\gtrsim\left(\min_{S\subseteq[n]}\vol_{|S|}(K_{S})\right)^{1/n}.

The second produces a shifted partial coloring
[
RR22
, Theorem 6]
.

Theorem 17
.

For every
\alpha,\beta,\eta>0
there is a constant

C=C(\alpha,\beta,\eta)>0
such that the following holds. Let

K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a symmetric convex body with
\gamma_{n}(K)\geq e^{-\alpha n}
. Let
y\in[-1,1]^{n}
, and let
H\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a subspace with

\dim(H)\geq\beta n
. Then there is
x\in CK\cap H
such that

x+y\in[-1,1]^{n}
and
\left|\{i\in[n]:(x+y)_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\}\right|\geq(\beta-\eta)n
. Moreover, it may be constructed in polynomial time.

We use the following consequence. See also
[
DNTT18
, Lemma 8]
.

Corollary 18
.

Let
n\geq 1
, and let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a symmetric convex body such that

\vol_{|S|}(K_{S})\geq 1\qquad\text{for every }S\subseteq[n].

Then for every
y\in[-1,1]^{n}
there is a polynomial-time computable
y^{\prime}\in[-1,1]^{n}
such that
\left|\{i\in[n]:y^{\prime}_{i}\in\{\pm 1\}\}\right|\geq\frac{n}{2}

and
\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{K}\lesssim 1
.

Proof.

The volume assumption and Theorem
16
imply
\gamma_{n}(K)^{1/n}\gtrsim 1
. Apply Theorem
17
to obtain
x\in CK
such that
y^{\prime}:=x+y
belongs to
[-1,1]^{n}
and has at least
n/2
coordinates
in
\{\pm 1\}
. Then
\|y-y^{\prime}\|_{K}=\|x\|_{K}\lesssim 1
.
∎

Finally, we use the inverse Santaló inequality of Bourgain and Milman
[
BM87
]
.

Theorem 19
.

For every symmetric convex body
K\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

\vol_{n}(K)^{1/n}\vol_{n}(K^{\circ})^{1/n}\gtrsim\frac{1}{n}.

A section radius bound for
K_{1}

We derive an upper bound on the section radii
r_{k}(K_{1})
of the body defined in (

†

‣
2
) via Gordon’s escape theorem (Theorem
9
). First, we need to upper bound its width.

Proposition 20
.

For every
r\geq 0
,

w\bigl(K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d}\bigr)\lesssim\sqrt{\log(1+dr^{2})}.

Proof.

Let
x\in K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d}
. For
g\sim N(0,I_{d})
, (

†

‣
2
) gives

\langle g,x\rangle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\langle g,u_{i}\rangle\langle x,u_{i}\rangle.

Let
q\geq 2
be chosen later and put
p=q/(q-1)
. Then
Lemma
4
gives

\|x\|_{K_{p}}\leq r^{2/q}.

Thus Hölder’s inequality yields

|\langle g,x\rangle|\leq\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle x,u_{i}\rangle|^{p}\right)^{1/p}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle g,u_{i}\rangle|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\leq r^{2/q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|\langle g,u_{i}\rangle|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

By concavity of
t\mapsto t^{1/q}
, this gives

w(K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d})=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sup_{x\in K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d}}|\langle g,x\rangle|\leq r^{2/q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\,\mathop{\mathbb{E}}|\langle g,u_{i}\rangle|^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

For
u_{i}\in\partial B_{2}^{d}
,
\langle g,u_{i}\rangle\sim N(0,1)
, so

(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}|\langle g,u_{i}\rangle|^{q})^{1/q}\lesssim\sqrt{q}
. Since
\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}=d
from (

†

‣
2
),

w(K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d})\lesssim\sqrt{q}\,(dr^{2})^{1/q}.

If
t:=dr^{2}<4
, take
q=2
and use
t\lesssim\log(1+t)
on this range. If

t\geq 4
, take
q=\log t
, so
t^{1/q}=2
. In either case, the above inequality yields

w(K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d})\lesssim\sqrt{\log(1+dr^{2})}.\qed

Corollary 21
.

For
k\in[1,d]
and
\lambda\geq 1
, take
r^{2}=\lambda\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}
. Then

w\bigl((\tfrac{1}{r}K_{1})\cap\partial B_{2}^{d}\bigr)^{2}\lesssim\frac{k\log(2\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Proof.

By Proposition
20
and homogeneity,

w\bigl((\tfrac{1}{r}K_{1})\cap\partial B_{2}^{d}\bigr)^{2}=w\bigl(r^{-1}(K_{1}\cap r\partial B_{2}^{d})\bigr)^{2}\leq w\bigl(r^{-1}(K_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{d})\bigr)^{2}\lesssim\frac{\log(1+dr^{2})}{r^{2}}.

Put
\alpha:=\log(\tfrac{2d}{k})\geq 1
. By assumption,
r^{2}=\tfrac{\lambda\alpha}{k}
, so

w\bigl((\tfrac{1}{r}K_{1})\cap\partial B_{2}^{d}\bigr)^{2}\lesssim\frac{\log(1+dr^{2})}{r^{2}}=\frac{k}{\lambda}\cdot\alpha^{-1}\log\Big(1+\tfrac{\lambda d\alpha}{k}\Big).

Moreover, since
\lambda\geq 1
and
\alpha\geq 1
,

\alpha^{-1}\log\Big(1+\tfrac{\lambda d\alpha}{k}\Big)=\alpha^{-1}\log(1+\lambda 2^{\alpha-1}\alpha)\leq\alpha^{-1}\log(2\lambda\cdot 2^{\alpha-1}\alpha)\lesssim\log(2\lambda).\qed

Theorem 22
.

For every integer
k\in[1,d]
,

r_{k}\bigl(K_{1}\bigr)\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Equivalently, there is a subspace
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\codim F<k
such
that

\sup_{x\in K_{1}\cap F}\|x\|_{2}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Proof.

We apply Gordon’s escape theorem. If

k\leq m_{\mathrm{E}}
, then combining Proposition
10
(
r_{1}(K_{1})\lesssim w(K_{1})
), Lemma
5
(
K_{1}\subseteq B^{d}_{2}
) and Proposition
20
for
r=1
gives

r_{k}(K_{1})\leq r_{1}(K_{1})\lesssim w(K_{1})=w(K_{1}\cap B^{d}_{2})\lesssim\sqrt{\log(1+d)}\leq\sqrt{\log(2d)}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Assume
k\geq m_{\mathrm{E}}+1
. Take
r:=\sqrt{\lambda\tfrac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}

where
\lambda
is a constant to be determined and let
S:=(\tfrac{1}{r}K_{1})\cap\partial B_{2}^{d}
. Corollary
21

yields a constant
C>0
so that

w(S)^{2}\leq C\frac{k\log(2\lambda)}{\lambda}.

Taking
\lambda
large enough so that
\frac{C\log(2\lambda)}{\lambda}\leq\frac{1}{2m_{\mathrm{E}}}
, we obtain

k-1\geq m_{\mathrm{E}}\max\{1,w(S)^{2}\}.

Theorem
9
gives
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\codim F\leq k-1<k

and
S\cap F=\emptyset
.
If
K_{1}\cap F
contained a point
x
with
\|x\|_{2}\geq r
, then by convexity of
K_{1}
, the
point
\frac{r}{\|x\|_{2}}x
would lie in
K_{1}\cap r\partial B^{d}_{2}\cap F=r(S\cap F)=\emptyset
, absurd.
Thus

r_{k}(K_{1})\leq\sup_{x\in K_{1}\cap F}\|x\|_{2}\leq r\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.\qed

4
A section radius bound for linear images of
K_{1}

Next, we derive an upper bound on the radii
r_{k}(MK_{1})
. We again apply Gordon’s escape theorem, but the localized width bound is more subtle; first we need to interpolate between
K_{1}
and the Euclidean ball.

Lemma 23
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
, and put
q=p/(p-1)
. For every integer
k\in[1,d]
,

r_{k}\bigl(K_{1},K_{p}\bigr)\lesssim\Big(\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}\Big)^{1/q}.

Proof.

Theorem
22
yields a subspace

F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
with
\codim F<k
such that for
x\in F
,

\|x\|_{2}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}\|x\|_{K_{1}}.

Lemma
4
gives

\|x\|_{K_{p}}\leq\|x\|_{K_{1}}^{1-2/q}\|x\|_{2}^{2/q}\leq\Big(\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}\Big)^{1/q}\|x\|_{K_{1}}.

Taking the supremum over

gives the claim.
∎

Lemma 24
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
, and put
q=p/(p-1)
. For every integer
k\in[1,d]
,

e_{k}\bigl(K_{1},K_{p}\bigr)\lesssim\Big(\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}\Big)^{1/q}.

Proof.

Since
1<p\leq 2
, we have
q\geq 2
. By Lemma
23
, for every
1\leq s\leq k
,

r_{s}(K_{1},K_{p})\lesssim\left(\frac{\log(2d/s)}{s}\right)^{1/q}.

Carl’s inequality (Theorem
8
) for
\alpha=1
gives

ke_{k}(K_{1},K_{p})\lesssim\max_{s\in[1,k]}s\left(\frac{\log(2d/s)}{s}\right)^{1/q}.

Writing
u=k/s\geq 1
, the last expression is

k^{1-1/q}u^{-(1-1/q)}(\log(2d/k)+\log u)^{1/q}.

Since
\log(2d/k)\geq 1
and
q\geq 2
,

u^{-(1-1/q)}(\log(2d/k)+\log u)^{1/q}\lesssim\log(2d/k)^{1/q},

uniformly for
u\geq 1
. Therefore

ke_{k}(K_{1},K_{p})\lesssim k^{1-1/q}\log(2d/k)^{1/q},

and hence

e_{k}(K_{1},K_{p})\lesssim\left(\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}\right)^{1/q}.\qed

Lemma 25
.

Let
p\in(1,2]
, and put
q=p/(p-1)
. Then

\Gamma\bigl(K_{p}\bigr)\lesssim\sqrt{q}.

Proof.

Let
Ux=(\langle x,u_{1}\rangle,\ldots,\langle x,u_{m}\rangle)
and define the norm

\|v\|:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}|v_{i}|^{p}\right)^{1/p}
.
Then
\|x\|_{K_{p}}=\|Ux\|
. Its dual norm is given by

\|z\|_{*}:=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}|z_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}
.
We may write the polar body as

K_{p}^{\circ}=\left\{U^{\top}z:\|z\|_{*}\leq 1\right\}=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{m}z_{i}u_{i}:\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}|z_{i}|^{q}\leq 1\right\}.

Take
P\in\pazocal M(K_{p}^{\circ})
attaining
\Gamma(K_{p})
. By Carathéodory’s theorem, we may write

P=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}y_{\ell}y_{\ell}^{\top},\qquad y_{\ell}=U^{\top}z^{(\ell)},\qquad\|z^{(\ell)}\|_{*}\leq 1,

where
\lambda_{\ell}\geq 0
and
\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}=1
. Let

g_{1},\ldots,g_{N}
be independent standard Gaussians. Then
G\sim N(0,P)
has the same
distribution as

\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}g_{\ell}y_{\ell}=U^{\top}v
, where we denote
v_{i}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\sqrt{\lambda_{\ell}}g_{\ell}z_{i}^{(\ell)}
. Also denote its covariance by
\sigma_{i}^{2}:=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}(z_{i}^{(\ell)})^{2}
.
The vector

U^{\top}v
belongs to
\|v\|_{*}K_{p}^{\circ}
, so that

\|U^{\top}v\|_{K_{p}^{\circ}}\leq\|v\|_{*}.

Therefore, by concavity of
t\mapsto t^{1/q}
and the Gaussian moment estimate,

\Gamma(K_{p})=\mathop{\mathbb{E}}_{G\sim N(0,P)}\|G\|_{K_{p}^{\circ}}\leq\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\|v\|_{*}\leq\left(\mathop{\mathbb{E}}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}|v_{i}|^{q}\right)^{1/q}\lesssim\sqrt{q}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}\sigma_{i}^{q}\right)^{1/q}.

Since

\sigma_{i}^{2}=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}(z_{i}^{(\ell)})^{2}
,
the triangle inequality in the
\ell_{q/2}
norm yields

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}\sigma_{i}^{q}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}\left|\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}(z_{i}^{(\ell)})^{2}\right|^{q/2}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}^{1-q}|z_{i}^{(\ell)}|^{q}\right)^{2/q}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{N}\lambda_{\ell}\|z^{(\ell)}\|_{*}^{2}\leq 1.

We conclude
\Gamma(K_{p})\lesssim\sqrt{q}
, as claimed.
∎

Proposition 26
.

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. For every integer
k\in[1,d]
and every
r\geq 0
,

w(MK_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{n})\lesssim r\sqrt{k}+\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}\sqrt{\log(2d/k)}.

Proof.

Put
q:=2\log(2d/k)\geq 2,p:=\frac{q}{q-1}\in(1,2]
. By Lemma
24
, for

one has

N(MK_{1}\cap rB^{n}_{2},tMK_{p})\leq N(MK_{1},tMK_{p})\leq N(K_{1},tK_{p})\leq 2^{k-1},

where we have used that
N(MK,ML)\leq N(K,L)
for any convex bodies
K,L
as we may simply apply
M
to any cover.
Lemma
15

, gives

w(MK_{1}\cap rB^{n}_{2})\lesssim r\sqrt{k}+t\,w(MK_{p}).

By Lemma

,
K_{p}\subseteq d^{1/q}K_{1}
. Thus

, so
\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})\subseteq d^{1/q}\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{p})
, and therefore

\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{p})}\leq d^{1/q}\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}

Therefore

∎

Theorem 27
.

Let
M:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. For every integer

,

r_{k}\bigl(MK_{1}\bigr)\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

13

Fix an absolute constant

for which Proposition

gives

w(MK_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{n})\leq C_{0}\left(r\sqrt{j}+\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}\sqrt{\log(2d/j)}\right)

and

be the constant in Theorem

. Choose universal constants

,

\displaystyle w(S)

\displaystyle\leq r^{-1}w(MK_{1}\cap rB_{2}^{n})

\displaystyle\leq C_{0}\sqrt{j}+C_{0}\frac{\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}\sqrt{\log(2d/j)}}{r}.

Since

yields a subspace

Hence

r_{k}(MK_{1})\leq\sup_{x\in MK_{1}\cap F}\|x\|_{2}\leq r\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K_{1})}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.\qed

5
From section radii to volume bounds

Theorem

is invariant under linear transformations, which implies the following corollary for arbitrary quotients of sections of the
\ell_{1}

Corollary 28
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
d
-dimensional and let
M:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. Then,
for every integer

r_{k}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Take a linear isomorphism
A:\mathbb{R}^{d}\to F
and set

.
Thus
AK=B_{1}^{m}\cap F
. Theorem
2
gives an invertible

with

to

r_{k}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))=r_{k}(MAT^{-1}TK)\lesssim\left\|MAT^{-1}\right\|_{\pazocal B_{n}(TK)}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

It is straightforward to check that by definition

\left\|MAT^{-1}\right\|_{\pazocal B_{n}(TK)}=\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal B_{n}(AK)}
.
∎

Now we are ready to start moving towards volumetric bounds. We first pass to entropy numbers via Carl’s inequality, then bound volume through covering numbers.

Corollary 29
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be
d
-dimensional and let
M:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. Then,
for every integer

e_{k}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)}\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Proof.

By Corollary
28
, for every
j\in[1,k]
, we have

jr_{j}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)}\sqrt{j\log(2d/j)}.

Since
j\mapsto j\log(2d/j)
attains its maximum either at
j=k
or at one of the integers closest to
2d/e
if
k>2d/e
, it follows that
j\log(2d/j)\lesssim k\log(2d/k)
. Therefore, by Theorem
8
with
K=M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)
,

L=B_{2}^{n}
, and
\alpha=1
,

ke_{k}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))\lesssim\max_{j\in[1,k]}jr_{j}(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))\lesssim\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)}\sqrt{k\log(2d/k)}.\qed

Lemma 30
.

Let
K\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a symmetric convex set with span
H
, and let
M:\mathbb{R}^{m}\to\mathbb{R}^{n}
be linear. Assume that
MK
is
full-dimensional in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
and that its maximal-volume inscribed ellipsoid is

B_{2}^{n}
. Then

\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)}\leq\sqrt{n}.

Proof.

By Theorem
6
, there exist points
u_{1},\ldots,u_{N}\in(MK)^{\circ}\cap\partial B_{2}^{n}
and weights

c_{1},\ldots,c_{N}>0
such that

\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}u_{i}u_{i}^{\top}=I_{n}.

Taking traces gives
\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}=n
. For each
i\in[N]
,
M^{\top}u_{i}\in K^{\circ}
, because

\sup_{x\in K}\left|\langle x,M^{\top}u_{i}\rangle\right|=\sup_{z\in MK}\left|\langle z,u_{i}\rangle\right|\leq 1.

Therefore

P:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\frac{c_{i}}{n}(M^{\top}u_{i})(M^{\top}u_{i})^{\top}\in\pazocal M(K^{\circ})
,
and

M^{\top}M=M^{\top}I_{n}M=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}(M^{\top}u_{i})(M^{\top}u_{i})^{\top}=nP.

Thus
M^{\top}M=(\sqrt{n})^{2}P
with
P\in\pazocal M(K^{\circ})
, so that
\left\|M\right\|_{\pazocal{B}_{n}(K)}\leq\sqrt{n}
.
∎

We now prove a volume-ratio bound for quotients of sections of
B_{1}^{m}

Theorem 31
.

Let
F\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
be a
d
-dimensional subspace. Let

be a linear map with

. Assume that

is full-dimensional

\vr\bigl(M(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)\bigr)\lesssim\sqrt{\log\Big(\frac{2d}{n}\Big)}.

Proof of Theorem

.

We may assume without loss of generality that the John ellipsoid of

is

, otherwise apply an invertible linear transformation. By Lemma

,

Let

By Theorem

\pazocal E

(

6
Balancing vectors in any zonotope

We now proceed towards the proof of Theorem
1
. Let

Z=A^{\top}B_{\infty}^{m}\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a zonotope, which we assume without loss of generality is full-dimensional (otherwise we may reduce
d
after an invertible linear transformation) so that

has rank
d
, and set

Let

, where

, and take

u_{1},\ldots,u_{n}\in B_{\infty}^{m}
with
v_{i}=A^{\top}u_{i}
for

Lemma 33

For every nonempty

K_{S}=\bigl(M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)\bigr)^{\circ}.

Proof.

The polar of

Since
A
has rank

identifies

with
B_{1}^{m}\cap F
.

For

\displaystyle\left\|V_{S}^{\top}y\right\|_{Z}

\displaystyle=\sup_{x\in Z^{\circ}}\left|\langle V_{S}^{\top}y,x\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x:\left\|Ax\right\|_{1}\leq 1}\left|\langle y,V_{S}x\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{x:\left\|Ax\right\|_{1}\leq 1}\left|\langle y,M_{S}Ax\rangle\right|

\displaystyle=\sup_{b\in B_{1}^{m}\cap F}\left|\langle y,M_{S}b\rangle\right|.

Thus
y\in K_{S}
iff
\left|\langle y,z\rangle\right|\leq 1
for every
z\in M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)
, i.e.
y\in\bigl(M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)\bigr)^{\circ}
.
∎

Proposition 34
.

For every nonempty

S\subseteq[n]
,
k:=|S|
, one has

\vol_{k}(K_{S})^{1/k}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\sqrt{k\log(2d/k)}}.

Proof.

Fix
S\subseteq[n]
and put
k=|S|
. If
M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)
is lower-dimensional, then Lemma
33
implies that

K_{S}
is unbounded, and the claim is trivial. Now assume that
M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F)
is full-dimensional. By Lemma
33
we have
K_{S}=(M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))^{\circ}
.
By Corollary
32
, since
u_{1},\dots,u_{n}\in B_{\infty}^{m}
,

\vol_{k}(M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))^{1/k}\lesssim\sqrt{\frac{\log(2d/k)}{k}}.

Therefore, the inverse Santaló inequality (Theorem
19
) yields

\vol_{k}(K_{S})^{1/k}=\vol_{k}((M_{S}(B_{1}^{m}\cap F))^{\circ})^{1/k}\gtrsim\frac{1}{\sqrt{k\log(2d/k)}}.\qed

We now convert Proposition
34
into a partial coloring.

Lemma 35
.

For every
y\in[-1,1]^{n}
, there exists a polynomial-time computable
y^{\prime}\in[-1,1]^{n}
such that at least
n/2
coordinates of
y^{\prime}
lie in

\{\pm 1\}
and

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}(y_{i}-y^{\prime}_{i})v_{i}\right\|_{Z}\lesssim\sqrt{n\log(2d/n)}.

Proof.

Apply Proposition
34
and Corollary
18
after scaling
K
by
\sqrt{n\log(2d/n)}
.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1
.

Set
y^{(0)}=\mathbf{0}
. Given
y^{(j)}
, let
I_{j}
be the set of coordinates not yet in

\{\pm 1\}
and put
n_{j}=|I_{j}|
. If
n_{j}>0
, apply
Lemma
35
to the active vectors
(v_{i})_{i\in I_{j}}
and the
point
y^{(j)}|_{I_{j}}
. Keeping the fixed coordinates unchanged gives

y^{(j+1)}\in[-1,1]^{n}
with
n_{j+1}\leq n_{j}/2
and

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(y_{i}^{(j)}-y_{i}^{(j+1)}\bigr)v_{i}\right\|_{Z}\lesssim\sqrt{n_{j}\log(2d/n_{j})}.

Let
t
be the first index with
n_{t}=0
. Such a
t
exists since the active
set size is at least halved at each step. Then all coordinates of

y^{(t)}
are signs; write
x_{i}=y_{i}^{(t)}
.

By the triangle inequality and symmetry of
Z
,

\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}x_{i}v_{i}\right\|_{Z}\leq\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\left\|\sum_{i=1}^{n}\bigl(y_{i}^{(j+1)}-y_{i}^{(j)}\bigr)v_{i}\right\|_{Z}\lesssim\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\sqrt{n_{j}\log(2d/n_{j})}.

Since
n_{j}\leq n2^{-j}
for
j\in[0,t)
,

n_{j}\log(2d/n_{j})\lesssim n2^{-j}\log(2d2^{j}/n)=n2^{-j}(\log(2d/n)+j)\leq n\log(2d/n)\,2^{-j}(j+1).

Thus the sum may be upper bounded by

\sum_{j=0}^{t-1}\sqrt{n_{j}\log(2d/n_{j})}\lesssim\sqrt{n\log(2d/n)}\sum_{j\geq 0}2^{-j/2}\sqrt{j+1}\lesssim\sqrt{n\log(2d/n)}.

Hence the signed sum lies in

C\sqrt{n\log(2d/n)}\,Z
for a universal constant
C
.
∎

Proof of Lemma

.

Proof of Lemma

.

Let

Proof of Lemma

.
