Post-Jamming Mechanics of Feedback-Regulated Budding-Cell Packings
Pawel Gniewek

Abstract
Budding-cell packings jam before all buds are mechanically constrained. The post-jamming state is therefore set by both the pressure P P and the fraction u u of buds that remain unconstrained. We develop a mean-field theory for this regime. A modified Maxwell count gives the post-jamming coordination, a depletion law for the unconstrained buds predicts the crossover density ϕ 2 \phi_{2} at which that reservoir is exhausted, and a flux-partition argument explains why strong growth feedback can markedly increase rigidity while generating little internal pressure.



Confined microbial populations can jam under their own growth
Delarue2016
. In budding yeast, experiments showed that the contact pressures generated in this way feed back on cell growth
Delarue2016
, and simulations showed that the same feedback can raise the contact number and strongly stiffen the packing while adding little pressure during further growth
Gniewek2019
. Ordinary jamming by external compression does not have this feature
OHern2003
. Jamming transitions have since been documented in other biological settings, including the fluid-to-solid transition that sculpts the vertebrate body axis
Mongera2018
, and growth-induced mechanical stress is increasingly recognized as a generic feature of spatially confined cell populations
Delarue2025
. More broadly, proliferating active matter—in which replication injects both biomass and new degrees of freedom—has emerged as a distinct class of driven systems with collective phenomena that have no counterpart in fixed-particle-number active matter
Hallatschek2023
. In budding-cell populations, however, jamming occurs while a finite fraction
u_{J}
of buds is still mechanically unconstrained, which makes the post-jamming regime different. Those unconstrained buds leave residual growth modes at the jamming point, so pressure alone does not specify the post-jamming state. Here we develop a mean-field theory for that regime. The theory identifies the unconstrained-bud fraction
u
as the additional state variable, predicts how that population is depleted above jamming, and explains why strong feedback can rigidify the packing without building up much internal pressure.

Model.—
We use the two-dimensional budding-cell model of Ref.
Gniewek2019
. Each cell consists of a rigid mother lobe and a growing bud [Fig.
1
(
A

\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{0,i}\exp\!\left[-\frac{P_{\mathrm{bud},i}}{P_{0}}\right],

(1)

where
\gamma_{0,i}
is the innate zero-pressure growth rate assigned at cell birth,
P_{\mathrm{bud},i}
is the pressure on bud
i
, and
P_{0}
is the feedback pressure scale [Fig.
1
(
B
)]. Smaller values of
P_{0}
correspond to stronger feedback. Simulation details are given in the Supplemental Material
SM
.

(2)

counts contacts twice. The post-jamming theory uses pressure

Modified counting.—
In doubled-contact counting, the finite-size isostatic relation is

N_{c}^{\mathrm{iso}}=6N_{\mathrm{nr}}-2N_{u}-2.

(3)

Note that removing the two global translations would by itself give
6N_{\mathrm{nr}}-2N_{u}-4
. A periodic packing with only that many counted contacts is marginal but cannot support a finite pressure or have a positive bulk modulus
B>0
. Therefore, one additional physical contact is needed to sustain compression, just as is observed in finite-size jamming of sphere packings
Goodrich2012
. In doubled-contact counting that extra constraint contributes
+2
to the count, which yields Eq. (
3
). Dividing by
N_{\mathrm{nr}}
gives

Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(u)=6-2u-\frac{2}{N_{\mathrm{nr}}}\simeq 6-2u.

(4)

This is the budding-cell analogue of the reduced isostatic coordination found in other nonspherical packings
Mailman2009
;
Schreck2010
. A general theory of hypostatic jamming has since placed such systems in a common universality class
Brito2018
, and detailed studies of dimer and other composite-particle packings confirm that proper identification of unconstrained internal modes—rotational rattlers in dimer packings play an analogous role to the unconstrained buds here—restores isostaticity at jamming
VanderWerf2018
;
Ikeda2021
. The relevant distance from marginality is therefore

\Delta Z\equiv Z-Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(u).

(5)

Each time a previously unconstrained bud becomes mechanically complete,
Z
increases by four counted contacts while
Z_{\mathrm{iso}}
increases by only two. The first new physical contact removes the free relative rotation of the bud. The second is the extra contact needed to maintain a load-bearing packing. Hence

\Delta Z(P,u)=\Delta Z_{0}(P)+2(u_{J}-u),

(6)

and

Z(P,u)=6+2u_{J}-4u+\Delta Z_{0}(P).

(7)

Eq. (
6
) is tested in Figure
2
(
A
) over a broad set of
(P_{0},\Delta\phi)
values. The relation captures the overall trend over 3 orders of magnitude in the system compaction
\Delta\phi
, and more than 4 orders of magnitude in the feedback strength. Small deviations are expected and are attributed to finite-size effects that decay as
\sim N^{-1}
, or at the largest
\Delta\phi
, to cell divisions that begin to repopulate the unconstrained sector.

Bud depletion.—
To follow the free-bud reservoir above jamming, we label the buds that are unconstrained at
\phi_{J}
. For each such bud
\alpha
, let
a_{\alpha}^{\ast}
be the added bud area required for mechanical completion. We define

H(a)=\Pr(a^{\ast}\leq a),\qquad S(a)=1-H(a),

(8)

where
S(a)
is the survival function of these completion thresholds. In the interval before new divisions matter, the surviving unconstrained-bud fraction is

u(a)=u_{J}S(a).

(9)

In Figure
2
(
B
) this depletion is observed directly. For populations with no feedback or very weak feedback, growth occurs in both the compressed and free sectors, and free buds become constrained over a broad range of compactions. That trend quickly reverses as stress feedback on bud growth becomes stronger. In these cases we observe a sharper drop in the population of unconstrained buds, and the drop occurs at lower densities than before.

To analyze this qualitatively, let

\chi_{c}(a;P_{0})\equiv\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-P_{\mathrm{bud}}/P_{0}}\right\rangle_{c}

(10)

be the mean growth factor of the compressed buds. Approximating the nonrattler density by its jamming value
n_{J}
over this narrow interval, the density increment obeys

\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}a}=n_{J}\bigl[u(a)+(1-u(a))\chi_{c}(a;P_{0})\bigr].

(11)

If
\chi_{c}
varies only weakly during depletion, we replace it by its interval average
\bar{\chi}(P_{0})
and obtain

\phi(a;P_{0})-\phi_{J}=n_{J}\left[\bar{\chi}(P_{0})a+\bigl(1-\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr)u_{J}\int_{0}^{a}S(a^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}\right].

(12)

We define the depletion density operationally by

u(\phi_{2},P_{0})=\varepsilon,

(13)

with
\varepsilon\ll 1
. If
u_{J}S(a_{\varepsilon})=\varepsilon
, then

\phi_{2}(P_{0})-\phi_{J}=n_{J}\left[\bar{\chi}(P_{0})a_{\varepsilon}+\bigl(1-\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr)u_{J}\int_{0}^{a_{\varepsilon}}S(a)\,\mathrm{d}a\right].

(14)

Thus stronger feedback moves
\phi_{2}
closer to
\phi_{J}
—the same degree of bud completion is reached after a smaller increase in packing fraction because a smaller fraction of growth is spent on the compressed sector. Figure
2
(
C
) supports this prediction overall. In the simulations, the measured depletion density (defined as the packing fraction at which the last bud that was free at jamming becomes complete) agrees well with the values estimated from Eq. (
14
), although the latter show a small systematic overestimate. Since the survival function develops an increasingly sharp edge in the asymptotic limit
P_{0}\rightarrow 0^{+}
, it is natural to consider a sharp-edge limit for
S(a)

S(a)\approx\Theta(a_{c}-a),

(15)

This reduces the depletion density to

\phi_{2}(P_{0})\approx\phi_{J}+n_{J}a_{c}\bigl[u_{J}+(1-u_{J})\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr].

(16)

Threshold broadening in Eq. (
15
) and injection of new unconstrained buds by later divisions change the picture only modestly and are treated in the Supplemental Material
SM
.

Mechanical consequences.—
The same two-sector perspective explains the anomalous mechanics observed in Ref.
Gniewek2019
. For a mechanically arrested packing, the instantaneous response to an externally imposed strain has the effective-medium form

\displaystyle B_{\mathrm{ext}}

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{8}\,Z,

(17)

\displaystyle G

\displaystyle=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}M_{4}\,\Delta Z,

(18)

where
\ell_{c}
is the mean contact length and
M_{4}\equiv\left\langle n_{x}^{2}n_{y}^{2}\right\rangle=1/8
for an isotropic contact network. The key point is that
B_{\mathrm{ext}}
depends on the full contact number, whereas
G
depends on the excess coordination
\Delta Z

Wyart2005EPL
;
Ellenbroek2006
;
Goodrich2012
. The derivations of Eqs. (
17
) and (
18
) are given in the Appendix and the Supplemental Material
SM
.

During a small increment
\mathrm{d}a
of bud area, the unconstrained sector contributes
n_{J}u\,\mathrm{d}a
to
\mathrm{d}\phi
and the compressed sector contributes
n_{J}(1-u)\chi_{c}\,\mathrm{d}a
. The fraction of the total density increment entering the compressed sector is therefore

\lambda_{c}(\phi,P_{0})=\frac{(1-u)\chi_{c}}{u+(1-u)\chi_{c}}.

(19)

Only that compressed-sector increment directly loads the pre-existing force network. Hence the growth bulk response is

B_{\mathrm{grow}}(\phi,P_{0})\equiv\phi\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial\phi}\right)_{\mathrm{grow}}=\lambda_{c}(\phi,P_{0})\,B_{\mathrm{ext}}(\phi,P_{0}).

(20)

This is the central mechanical result of this Letter. In Figure
3
(
A
) the bulk response experienced by the growing population is broadly captured by this relation. Unconstrained buds grow freely into void space, while only the population of buds that are part of the load-bearing contact network has to perform mechanical work to expand. This is the primary mechanism that organizes the colony growth and determines the internal mechanical stress experienced by the cells. Even though feedback controls growth-generated pressure through
\lambda_{c}
, it is the structural term
\Delta Z
that controls rigidity. The two need not track one another. To see that, note that at the depletion density, the pressure buildup is

P_{2}(P_{0})=\int_{\phi_{J}}^{\phi_{2}(P_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\phi}\,\lambda_{c}(\phi,P_{0})B_{\mathrm{ext}}(\phi,P_{0})

(21)

and packing stiffness is

G_{2}(P_{0})=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}M_{4}\Bigl[2(u_{J}-\varepsilon)+\Delta Z_{0}\!\bigl(P_{2}(P_{0})\bigr)\Bigr].

(22)

As
P_{0}\to 0
,
\lambda_{c}
also tends to zero, but the structural term
2(u_{J}-\varepsilon)
remains finite. The consequences of this can be seen in Figures
3
(
B
) and (
C
). In Figure
3
(
B
), the internal pressure at the depletion point,
P_{2}
, decreases strongly as the feedback strengthens. That decrease is closely mirrored by the depletion-interval mean compressed-sector flux fraction,
\bar{\lambda}_{c}
. This supports the view that
B_{\rm ext}
varies only weakly over that compression interval and therefore suggests the approximate form
P_{2}\propto\exp[-P^{\dagger}/P_{0}]
, with
P^{\dagger}
a typical internal pressure over the
\phi_{2}-\phi_{J}
interval. Thus
P_{2}
is driven toward zero, whereas, as shown in Figure
3
(
C
),
G_{2}
remains finite and is consistent with approaching the plateau
G_{2}(0^{+})=n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}M_{4}u_{J}
at
P_{0}\to 0
. This is the trend observed in the simulations. Equivalently, the ratio
G_{2}/P_{2}
grows rapidly as feedback strengthens
SM
. It is worth noting that at the depletion point, the shear modulus
G_{2}
is substantial and of the same order as the internal pressure of the compacted packing at full confluency, i.e.
G_{2}/P_{\max}\approx 1
. Analytical details can be found in the Supplemental Material
SM
.

The mean-field theory developed in this Letter provides a clear picture of the link between the growth mechanism and self-rigidification. Budding cells jam with a finite population of mechanically incomplete buds. Compressive stress feeds back on growth and suppresses it in the compressed sector, thereby routing growth into the remaining underconstrained sector. Each completed bud adds more counted contacts than the modified isostatic count requires, so the packing moves away from marginality even though the force network sees only a small fraction of the added volume. This is the origin of the increased shear rigidity while growth-generated pressure stays small. Quantitatively, the presented theory depends on system-specific measured inputs
\Delta Z_{0}(P)
,
S(a)
, and
\bar{\chi}(P_{0})
. But once those are fixed, it predicts the post-jamming coordination, the depletion density, and the arrest values of the pressure and moduli without additional fitting. The same mechanism should matter quite generally in growing jammed matter that retains an underconstrained sector at jamming. This Letter identifies growth as a distinct form of activity that can steer a jammed packing away from marginality by depleting residual underconstrained degrees of freedom, extending proliferating active-matter ideas into the jamming and rigidity physics of dense packings
Weady2024
;
Hallatschek2023
.

Appendix A
Finite-size Maxwell counting and contact budget

For the nonrattler cells, let
N_{\mathrm{nr}}=N-N_{f}
. A budding cell has three rigid-body degrees of freedom in two dimensions. If
N_{u}
buds are unconstrained, one rotational mode per such bud remains free, so the number of constrained degrees of freedom is
3N_{\mathrm{nr}}-N_{u}
. Removing the two global translations gives the naive zero-pressure Maxwell count

N_{c}^{(0)}=2\bigl(3N_{\mathrm{nr}}-N_{u}-2\bigr)=6N_{\mathrm{nr}}-2N_{u}-4,

(23)

where
N_{c}
counts contacts twice.

A periodic packing with the count in Eq. (
23
) is marginal but cannot sustain a finite pressure or have a positive bulk modulus. As in finite-size jamming of sphere packings
Goodrich2012
, one additional physical contact is needed to create the state of self stress that supports compression. In doubled-contact counting this adds
+2
, so

N_{c}^{\mathrm{iso}}=N_{c}^{(0)}+2=6N_{\mathrm{nr}}-2N_{u}-2,

(24)

which is the relation used in the simulations. Dividing by
N_{\mathrm{nr}}
gives

Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(u)=6-2u-\frac{2}{N_{\mathrm{nr}}},\qquad u\equiv\frac{N_{u}}{N_{\mathrm{nr}}}.

(25)

The structural relation in the Letter follows from the same budget. When an unconstrained bud becomes mechanically complete, the actual coordination number increases by two physical contacts, or four counted contacts. By contrast, the modified isostatic count increases by only two counted contacts. Thus,

\mathrm{d}Z=-4\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad\mathrm{d}Z_{\mathrm{iso}}=-2\,\mathrm{d}u,

(26)

which implies

\mathrm{d}(\Delta Z)=-2\,\mathrm{d}u,\qquad\Delta Z\equiv Z-Z_{\mathrm{iso}}(u).

(27)

Integrating from jamming, where the unconstrained-bud fraction is
u_{J}
, yields

\Delta Z(P,u)=\Delta Z_{0}(P)+2(u_{J}-u).

(28)

The coefficient
2
is fixed by counting and is not a fit parameter.

Appendix B
Effective-medium mechanics

Bulk modulus.—
For a small isotropic areal strain
\eta\equiv\delta\phi/\phi
, the strain tensor in two dimensions is
\bm{\epsilon}=-(\eta/2)\mathbf{I}
. A contact of pre-strain length
\ell
and unit normal
\bm{n}
therefore undergoes the affine normal strain

\frac{\delta\ell}{\ell}=\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\bm{n}=-\frac{\eta}{2},

(29)

so the corresponding contact energy is
E_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{aff}}=k\ell^{2}\eta^{2}/8
. With contact density
\rho_{c}=n_{J}Z/2
, and replacing
\ell^{2}
by its contact-length average
\ell_{c}^{2}
, the affine energy density is
\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bulk}}=(n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}/16)Z\,\eta^{2}
. Identifying
\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bulk}}=\tfrac{1}{2}B_{\mathrm{ext}}\eta^{2}
gives

B_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{8}\,Z.

(30)

Shear modulus.—
For simple shear, with displacement field
\bm{u}=(\gamma y,0)
, the affine normal strain of the same contact is

\frac{\delta\ell}{\ell}=\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\bm{n}=\gamma n_{x}n_{y},

(31)

so the affine contact energy is
(k\ell^{2}/2)\gamma^{2}n_{x}^{2}n_{y}^{2}
. Using the orientational average
M_{4}\equiv\left\langle n_{x}^{2}n_{y}^{2}\right\rangle=1/8
for an isotropic contact network, and replacing the total contact density by the excess density
n_{J}\Delta Z/2
because only excess states of self stress survive the nonaffine relaxation near marginality, one obtains

G=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}M_{4}\,\Delta Z.

(32)

Growth bulk response.—
During a small increment
\mathrm{d}a
of bud area, the unconstrained and compressed sectors contribute
n_{J}u\,\mathrm{d}a
and
n_{J}(1-u)\chi_{c}\,\mathrm{d}a
to the density change, so

\mathrm{d}\phi=n_{J}\bigl[u+(1-u)\chi_{c}\bigr]\mathrm{d}a.

(33)

The fraction of that increment entering the compressed sector is therefore

\lambda_{c}=\frac{(1-u)\chi_{c}}{u+(1-u)\chi_{c}}.

(34)

Approximating only that sector as directly loading the pre-existing force network gives

\mathrm{d}P=\frac{B_{\mathrm{ext}}}{\phi}\,\lambda_{c}\,\mathrm{d}\phi,

(35)

and hence

B_{\mathrm{grow}}\equiv\phi\left(\frac{\partial P}{\partial\phi}\right)_{\mathrm{grow}}=\lambda_{c}B_{\mathrm{ext}}.

(36)

(S1)

where

N_{u}

counts contacts twice. Let
N_{\mathrm{nr}}=N-N_{f}
and
u=N_{u}/N_{\mathrm{nr}}
. The direct Maxwell count for a periodic system gives

(S2)

, which yields Eq. (

Appendix D
Depletion beyond the sharp-threshold limit

D.1
Threshold broadening

Depletion of the buds that are unconstrained at jamming is modeled by introducing the completion-threshold survival function
S(a)
, where
a
is the added bud area measured from
\phi_{J}
. In the no-injection limit, the surviving unconstrained-bud fraction is

u(a)=u_{J}S(a).

(S7)

If the compressed-sector mean growth factor varies slowly over the depletion interval, the density increment obeys

\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\mathrm{d}a}=n_{J}\left[u(a)+(1-u(a))\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\right],

(S8)

where
n_{J}
is the nonrattler density at jamming and
\bar{\chi}(P_{0})
is the depletion-interval average of the compressed-sector growth factor. Integrating Eq. (
S8
) gives

\phi(a;P_{0})-\phi_{J}=n_{J}\left[\bar{\chi}(P_{0})a+\bigl(1-\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr)u_{J}\int_{0}^{a}S(a^{\prime})\,\mathrm{d}a^{\prime}\right].

(S9)

The main text defines
\phi_{2}
operationally through
u(\phi_{2},P_{0})=\varepsilon
, with
\varepsilon
a small cutoff. To quantify how a finite threshold width broadens that point, write

a=a_{c}+\sigma_{a}x,\qquad S(a)=\mathcal{S}(x),

(S10)

where
a_{c}
is the mean completion area and
\sigma_{a}
is the edge width. Substituting into Eq. (
S9
) and expanding around the solution
x_{2}
of
u_{J}\mathcal{S}(x_{2})=\varepsilon
yields

\phi-\phi_{2}\simeq n_{J}\bigl[\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr]\sigma_{a}(x-x_{2}),

(S11)

so the density broadening is

\delta\phi_{2}\sim n_{J}\bigl[\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr]\sigma_{a}.

(S12)

A finite edge width in the threshold distribution therefore maps directly onto a finite width in the approach to
\phi_{2}
.

If instead the survival function decays to zero like a power law,

S(a)\sim C(a_{c}-a)^{\beta},\qquad a\to a_{c}^{-},

(S13)

then near
a_{c}
one has
u(a)=u_{J}C(a_{c}-a)^{\beta}
. For the finite-cutoff definition used in the Letter,
u(\phi_{2},P_{0})=\varepsilon
gives

\phi_{2}-\phi\simeq n_{J}\bigl[\varepsilon+(1-\varepsilon)\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\bigr](a_{2}-a),

(S14)

with
a_{2}
fixed by
u(a_{2})=\varepsilon
. Hence

u(\phi)-\varepsilon\propto\phi_{2}-\phi,\qquad\Delta Z_{2}-\Delta Z(\phi)\propto\phi_{2}-\phi,

(S15)

when
\bar{\chi}>0
. By contrast, the formal exact-depletion limit
\varepsilon\to 0
recovers

u(\phi)\propto(\phi_{\mathrm{dep}}-\phi)^{\beta},\qquad\Delta Z_{\mathrm{dep}}-\Delta Z(\phi)\propto(\phi_{\mathrm{dep}}-\phi)^{\beta},

(S16)

where
\phi_{\mathrm{dep}}
is the corresponding exact-depletion density. The singular
\bar{\chi}=0
limit gives a different scaling and is not pursued here.

D.2
Injection of new degrees of freedom by continued division

The Letter focuses on the no-injection regime, in which free buds are only depleted by mechanical completion. Continued division can be included by adding a source term for newly created unconstrained buds,

\frac{\mathrm{d}u}{\mathrm{d}a}=-h(a)u+I_{\mathrm{div}}(a,P_{0}),\qquad h(a)\equiv-\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}a}\ln S(a),

(S17)

where
h(a)
is the completion hazard associated with the no-injection survival function
S(a)
and
I_{\mathrm{div}}
is the injection rate.

A simple solvable limit is

h(a)=h_{0},\qquad I_{\mathrm{div}}(a,P_{0})=I_{0}(P_{0}),

(S18)

for which
S(a)=\mathrm{e}^{-h_{0}a}
. With the initial condition
u(0)=u_{J}
, Eq. (
S17
) gives

u(a)=u_{J}\mathrm{e}^{-h_{0}a}+\frac{I_{0}(P_{0})}{h_{0}}\bigl(1-\mathrm{e}^{-h_{0}a}\bigr).

(S19)

At large
a
, the unconstrained-bud fraction saturates at

u_{a\gg 0}=\frac{I_{0}(P_{0})}{h_{0}}.

(S20)

If
I_{0}/h_{0}\ll\varepsilon
, the cutoff
u=\varepsilon
is still reached and the no-injection theory remains accurate up to small corrections. If
I_{0}/h_{0}
is comparable to
\varepsilon
,
u(a)
saturates before exact depletion is reached, and
\phi_{2}
is replaced by a rounded crossover.

Appendix E
Extended effective-medium mechanics

E.1
Bulk modulus

We use an effective-medium estimate for the instantaneous bulk response of a mechanically arrested packing. Consider a small isotropic areal strain
\eta\equiv\delta\phi/\phi
, so that the strain tensor in two dimensions is
\bm{\epsilon}=-(\eta/2)\mathbf{I}
. For a contact of pre-strain length
\ell
and unit normal
\bm{n}
, the affine normal strain is

\frac{\delta\ell}{\ell}=\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\bm{n}=-\frac{\eta}{2}.

(S21)

The elastic energy stored in one contact is therefore

E_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{aff}}=\frac{k}{2}(\delta\ell)^{2}=\frac{k\ell^{2}}{8}\eta^{2}.

(S22)

With contact density
\rho_{c}=n_{J}Z/2
, and replacing
\ell^{2}
by its contact-averaged value
\ell_{c}^{2}
, the affine energy density becomes

\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bulk}}=\rho_{c}\left\langle E_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{aff}}\right\rangle=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{16}Z\,\eta^{2}.

(S23)

Identifying
\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{bulk}}=\tfrac{1}{2}B_{\mathrm{ext}}\eta^{2}
gives

B_{\mathrm{ext}}=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{8}\,Z.

(S24)

E.2
Shear modulus

For simple shear, write the contact normal as
\bm{n}=(\cos\theta,\sin\theta)
. The orientational fourth moment relevant to shear is

M_{4}\equiv\left\langle n_{x}^{2}n_{y}^{2}\right\rangle=\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}\cos^{2}\theta\sin^{2}\theta\,\mathrm{d}\theta=\frac{1}{8}.

(S25)

Consider a central-force contact of length
\ell
under a small simple shear strain
\gamma
, with displacement field
\bm{u}=(\gamma y,0)
. The symmetric strain tensor is

\bm{\epsilon}=\frac{1}{2}\bigl(\nabla\bm{u}+\nabla\bm{u}^{\mathsf{T}}\bigr)=\frac{\gamma}{2}\begin{pmatrix}0&1\\
1&0\end{pmatrix}.

(S26)

The affine normal extension of the contact is then

\frac{\delta\ell}{\ell}=\bm{n}\cdot\bm{\epsilon}\cdot\bm{n}=\gamma n_{x}n_{y},

(S27)

so the affine elastic energy stored in one contact is

E_{\mathrm{cont}}^{\mathrm{aff}}=\frac{k}{2}(\delta\ell)^{2}=\frac{k\ell^{2}}{2}\gamma^{2}n_{x}^{2}n_{y}^{2}.

(S28)

Near marginality, nonaffine relaxation removes the contribution of isostatic contacts to the shear modulus. The remaining energy is carried by the excess states of self stress, whose number scales with
\Delta Z
in central-force packings
Wyart2005EPL
;
Ellenbroek2006
;
Goodrich2012
. Using the excess contact density

\rho_{c}^{\mathrm{exc}}=\frac{n_{J}}{2}\Delta Z,

(S29)

the shear energy density becomes

\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{shear}}=\frac{k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}\gamma^{2}M_{4}\rho_{c}^{\mathrm{exc}}=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{4}M_{4}\Delta Z\,\frac{\gamma^{2}}{2}.

(S30)

Identifying
\delta\mathcal{E}_{\mathrm{shear}}=\tfrac{1}{2}G\gamma^{2}
gives

G=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}M_{4}\Delta Z.

(S31)

E.3
Flux partition, arrest pressure, and plateau moduli

During a small interval
\mathrm{d}t
, let each unconstrained bud gain added area
\mathrm{d}a
. Approximating the nonrattler density by
n_{J}
, the unconstrained sector contributes
n_{J}u\,\mathrm{d}a
to
\mathrm{d}\phi
, while the compressed sector contributes
n_{J}(1-u)\chi_{c}\,\mathrm{d}a
. Summing the two gives

\mathrm{d}\phi=n_{J}\bigl[u+(1-u)\chi_{c}\bigr]\mathrm{d}a.

(S32)

The fraction of the total density increment that enters the compressed sector is therefore

\lambda_{c}=\frac{(1-u)\chi_{c}}{u+(1-u)\chi_{c}}.

(S33)

Because unconstrained buds grow into available void space without loading existing contacts, only the compressed-sector increment directly loads the force-bearing network. Writing
\mathrm{d}\phi_{c}=\lambda_{c}\,\mathrm{d}\phi
and using the bulk modulus from Eq. (
S24
),

\mathrm{d}P=\frac{B_{\mathrm{ext}}}{\phi}\mathrm{d}\phi_{c}=\frac{B_{\mathrm{ext}}}{\phi}\lambda_{c}\,\mathrm{d}\phi.

(S34)

Integrating from
\phi_{J}
to
\phi_{2}
gives the arrest pressure

P_{2}(P_{0})=\int_{\phi_{J}}^{\phi_{2}(P_{0})}\frac{\mathrm{d}\phi}{\phi}\,\lambda_{c}(\phi,P_{0})B_{\mathrm{ext}}(\phi,P_{0}).

(S35)

In the sharp-threshold limit, the compressed sector receives the density increment

\delta\phi_{c}\simeq n_{J}a_{c}(1-u_{J})\bar{\chi}(P_{0}).

(S36)

If
B_{\mathrm{ext}}
varies slowly over that interval,

P_{2}(P_{0})\simeq\frac{B_{2}(0^{+})}{\phi_{J}}\,n_{J}a_{c}(1-u_{J})\bar{\chi}(P_{0}).

(S37)

If the compressed-sector pressure is concentrated near a typical value
P^{\dagger}
, then

\bar{\chi}(P_{0})\simeq\exp\!\left[-\frac{P^{\dagger}}{P_{0}}\right],\qquad P_{2}(P_{0})\propto\exp\!\left[-\frac{P^{\dagger}}{P_{0}}\right].

(S38)

At the depletion point
u(\phi_{2},P_{0})=\varepsilon
, the contact number and excess coordination are

Z_{2}=6+2u_{J}-4\varepsilon+\Delta Z_{0}(P_{2}),\qquad\Delta Z_{2}=2(u_{J}-\varepsilon)+\Delta Z_{0}(P_{2}),

(S39)

which give

B_{2}(P_{0})=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{8}\Bigl[6+2u_{J}-4\varepsilon+\Delta Z_{0}(P_{2})\Bigr],

(S40)

G_{2}(P_{0})=\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{2}M_{4}\Bigl[2(u_{J}-\varepsilon)+\Delta Z_{0}(P_{2})\Bigr].

(S41)

In the low-pressure strong-feedback limit, these reduce to

B_{2}(0^{+})\simeq\frac{n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}}{8}\bigl(6+2u_{J}\bigr),

(S42)

G_{2}(0^{+})\simeq n_{J}k\ell_{c}^{2}M_{4}u_{J},

(S43)

up to the small cutoff and no-feedback compression corrections.

In the strong-feedback limit,
G_{2}
approaches the finite plateau of Eq. (
S43
) while
P_{2}
vanishes exponentially as in Eq. (
S38
). The ratio
G_{2}/P_{2}
therefore diverges as
P_{0}\to 0
. Figure
S1
(
B
) shows this divergence in the simulation data.

The confluency pressure scale used in the Letter can be estimated by affinely compressing a monodisperse packing from
\phi_{J}\simeq 0.84
to
\phi=1
. This gives

\frac{P_{\max}}{k}\simeq\frac{2}{\pi}(1-\phi_{J})\approx 0.1.

(S44)

Appendix F
Growth-rate crossover

F.1
Distribution and heterogeneity

The normalized growth rate
g\equiv\gamma/\gamma_{0}
has a two-component distribution. If
\rho_{c}(p)
is the compressed-sector pressure distribution, then for
g=\exp(-p/P_{0})
, the compressed-sector distribution follows from the change of variables
p=-P_{0}\ln g
and
|\mathrm{d}p/\mathrm{d}g|=P_{0}/g
, i.e.

\Pi_{c}(g)=\frac{P_{0}}{g}\rho_{c}(-P_{0}\ln g),\qquad 0<g\leq 1.

(S45)

Unconstrained buds contribute a peak at
g=1
, so
\Pi_{u}(g)=\delta(g-1)
. Adding these two terms, for an unconstrained-bud fraction
u
, gives:

\Pi(g\mid\phi,P_{0})=u\,\delta(g-1)+(1-u)\frac{P_{0}}{g}\rho_{c}\!\left(-P_{0}\ln g\right),\qquad 0<g\leq 1.

(S46)

The compressed-sector contribution is centered near
g_{\ast}=\exp(-P_{\ast}/P_{0})
if the pressure distribution is concentrated near a characteristic value
P_{\ast}
. The distribution is therefore bimodal when

P_{0}\ll P_{\ast},

(S47)

and becomes unimodal once
P_{0}\sim P_{\ast}
, when the compressed sector overlaps the unconstrained-bud peak near
g=1
.

A convenient scalar summary is the normalized growth-rate heterogeneity

h_{\gamma}\equiv\frac{\mathrm{Var}(g)}{\left\langle g\right\rangle^{2}}.

(S48)

The moments of the full distribution are

\left\langle g\right\rangle=u+(1-u)\chi_{c},\qquad\chi_{c}\equiv\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-p/P_{0}}\right\rangle_{c},

(S49)

\left\langle g^{2}\right\rangle=u+(1-u)\chi_{c,2},\qquad\chi_{c,2}\equiv\left\langle\mathrm{e}^{-2p/P_{0}}\right\rangle_{c},

(S50)

so

h_{\gamma}=\frac{u+(1-u)\chi_{c,2}-\bigl[u+(1-u)\chi_{c}\bigr]^{2}}{\bigl[u+(1-u)\chi_{c}\bigr]^{2}}.

(S51)

Note that
h_{\gamma}
is not an independent state variable; it is a derived moment ratio of the same two-sector distribution.

F.2
Numerical evidence

At fixed
\Delta\phi=3\times 10^{-3}
, we measure both the normalized heterogeneity
h_{\gamma}
and the full histogram
\Pi(g)
. Equation (
S46
) predicts that decreasing
P_{0}
should drive the compressed-sector contribution toward smaller
g
while leaving the unconstrained-bud spike pinned at
g=1
.

The increase in

F.3
Order-of-magnitude estimate for the crossover scale

The crossover scale
P^{\ast}
in Eq.
S47
can be estimated by combining the growth law with the low-pressure mechanics. Strong suppression of compressed-sector growth begins once the growth rate drops by an order of magnitude

\exp\!\left[-\frac{p_{\mathrm{bud}}}{P_{0}}\right]\sim 0.1,

(S52)

which implies

P^{\ast}\sim\frac{p_{\mathrm{bud}}^{\mathrm{typ}}}{\ln 10}.

(S53)

A characteristic compressed-bud pressure can be estimated as

p_{\mathrm{bud}}^{\mathrm{typ}}\sim B_{2}(0^{+})\,\varepsilon_{c},

(S54)

where
\varepsilon_{c}
is the characteristic local strain needed to complete a compressed bud. If that strain is estimated from the characteristic added completion area
a_{c}
,

\varepsilon_{c}\sim\frac{a_{c}}{\ell_{c}^{2}},

(S55)

then, with
\ell_{c}
the same contact-length scale that enters the elastic moduli, Eq. (
S42
) gives

\frac{P^{\ast}}{k}\sim\frac{1}{\ln 10}\frac{B_{2}(0^{+})}{k}\frac{a_{c}}{\ell_{c}^{2}}\sim\frac{n_{J}(6+2u_{J})}{8\ln 10}\,a_{c}.

(S56)

Using estimates from the dataset with
L=15D_{0}
, in the strong-feedback regime,
n_{J}\simeq 0.775
and
u_{J}\simeq 0.23
, together with completion thresholds in the range
a_{c}\sim 4.7\times 10^{-3}
to
1.6\times 10^{-2}
, Eq. (
S56
) gives

\frac{P^{\ast}}{k}\sim 1.3\times 10^{-3}\ \text{to}\ 4.2\times 10^{-3}.

(S57)

This matches the order of magnitude at which Fig.
S2
shows the onset of clear bimodality. The estimate is only at the order-of-magnitude level because the compressed-sector pressure distribution is broad and the completion thresholds are not perfectly sharp, but it supports the interpretation that
P^{\ast}
is set by the low-pressure contact network rather than by an independent fitting scale.

Appendix G
Simulation protocol

The simulations follow the cell-based model introduced in Ref.
Gniewek2019
. Cells are represented as mother–bud dumbbells with repulsive harmonic lobe–lobe interactions. Growth proceeds by quasistatic increments of bud size under the local law

\gamma_{i}=\gamma_{0,i}\exp\!\left[-\frac{P_{\mathrm{bud},i}}{P_{0}}\right],

(S58)

with complete overdamped mechanical relaxation after each increment.

The primary jamming point
\phi_{J}
is identified as the first state with nonzero population pressure and a system-spanning force-bearing contact network. The main-text theory is tested only over the subsequent pre-division window used in the depletion analysis; outside that window, late-division effects should be treated as corrections. External bulk moduli are obtained by imposing a small isotropic areal strain on a mechanically arrested configuration and measuring the pressure response. Shear moduli are obtained analogously from small simple-shear deformations of the same arrested configurations.

These measurements probe the timescale window
\tau_{\rm mech}\ll\tau_{\rm probe}\ll\tau_{\rm grow}
, where
\tau_{\rm mech}
is the overdamped mechanical relaxation time,
\tau_{\rm probe}
is the duration of the strain step, and
\tau_{\rm grow}
is the growth time. In this regime the packing responds as a structurally frozen contact network. If instead
\tau_{\rm probe}\gtrsim\tau_{\rm grow}
, growth can create or redistribute contacts during the deformation itself and the response becomes a coupled growth–mechanics problem rather than the instantaneous elastic modulus discussed in the Letter.
