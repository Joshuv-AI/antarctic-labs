Degree-Based Weighted Adjacency Matrices: Spectra, Integrality, and Edge Deletion Effects
Bilal Ahmad Rather and Hilal Ahmad Ganie, [2mm] School of Mathematics, Statistics, Shandong University of Technology, Zibo 255049, China bilalahmadrr@gmail.com

Abstract
The article presents weighted adjacency spectrum of complete multipartite graphs, characterize its families with three distinct eigenvalues and identifies integral matrices. Also, we observe that for almost all weighted matrices, the energy and the spectral radius of a complete graph decreases upon edge deletion, thereby correcting and refining earlier published results in [Bilal and Munir, Int. J. Quantum Chem. (2024)]. Furthermore, we give counter examples related to I ​ S ​ I \displaystyle ISI energy decrease of regular tripartite graph by edge deletion and give its correct I ​ S ​ I \displaystyle ISI spectrum and I ​ S ​ I \displaystyle ISI energy and settle an open problem related to I ​ S ​ I \displaystyle ISI energy change of the multipartite graph. Also, we calculate the weighted adjacency spectrum of crown multipartite graph and discuss its integral spectral weighted spectrum.

Theorem 2.1

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a connected graph with vertex set
\displaystyle V(G)=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{n}\}
and let
\displaystyle S=\{v_{1},v_{2},\dots,v_{\alpha}\}
be an independent subset of
\displaystyle G
such that
\displaystyle N(v_{i})=N(v_{j})
, for all
\displaystyle i,j\in\{1,2,\dots,\alpha\}
. Then
\displaystyle 0
is the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
with multiplicity at least
\displaystyle\alpha-1
.

Similar to Theorem
2.1
, we have the following result.

Theorem 2.2

(
[
28
]
)
.

Let
\displaystyle G
be a connected graph with vertex set
\displaystyle V(G)=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{n}\}
and let
\displaystyle S=\{v_{1},\dots,v_{\alpha}\}
be a clique of
\displaystyle G
such that
\displaystyle N(v_{i})\setminus\{v_{j}\}=N(v_{j})\setminus\{v_{i}\}
, for all
\displaystyle i,j\in\{1,\dots,\alpha\}
. Then
\displaystyle-\phi(d_{v_{i}},d_{v_{i}})
is the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
with multiplicity at least
\displaystyle\alpha-1
, where
\displaystyle d_{v_{1}}=\dots=d_{v_{\alpha}}.

Consider a
\displaystyle l\times l
matrix

B=\begin{pmatrix}b_{1,1}&b_{1,2}&\cdots&b_{1,l}\\
b_{2,1}&b_{2,2}&\cdots&b_{2,l}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
b_{l,1}&b_{l,2}&\cdots&b_{l,l}\\
\end{pmatrix},

where rows and columns are partitioned according to some partition
\displaystyle P=\{P_{1},P_{2},\dots,P_{l}\}
of index set
\displaystyle N.
The quotient matrix
\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}
of
\displaystyle B
is the
\displaystyle l\times l
matrix whose
\displaystyle(i,j)
-th entry is the average row sums of the block
\displaystyle b_{i,j}
of
\displaystyle P
, see
[
7

\displaystyle b_{i,j}
of
\displaystyle B
has a constant column (or row) sum, we say that the partition
\displaystyle P

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

\displaystyle B

\displaystyle\mathcal{Q}

Theorem 2.3
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong K_{p_{1},p_{2},\dots,p_{t}}
with
\displaystyle t\geq 2
and
\displaystyle p_{1}\geq p_{2}\geq\cdots\geq p_{t}\geq 1
. Let
\displaystyle d_{i}:=n-p_{i}
be the common degree of every vertex in the
\displaystyle i
-th part. Then the weighted adjacency spectrum of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
consists of the eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
with
multiplicity
\displaystyle n-t
and the remaining
\displaystyle t
eigenvalues are exactly the eigenvalues of the

\displaystyle t\times t
matrix
\displaystyle M
defined by

M=(m_{ij})_{t\times t},\qquad m_{ii}=0,\quad m_{ij}=p_{j}\,\phi(d_{i},d_{j})\ \ (i\neq j).

(2.2)

Equivalently,
\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))=\{0^{[\,n-t\,]}\}\ \cup\ \operatorname{Spec}(M).

Proof.

Let the partite sets be
\displaystyle V_{i}=\{v_{i1},v_{i2},\dots,v_{ip_{i}}\},
for
\displaystyle i=1,2,\dots,t.
In a complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph, every vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to all vertices outside
\displaystyle V_{i}

and to no vertex inside
\displaystyle V_{i}
. Hence, each vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}
has common degree
\displaystyle d(v_{ij})=n-p_{i}=:d_{i}
for
\displaystyle 1\leq j\leq p_{i}.
With the above ordering, the matrix
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is a block matrix with
\displaystyle t\times t
blocks, where the
\displaystyle(i,i)
-block is the
\displaystyle p_{i}\times p_{i}
zero matrix (no edges inside
\displaystyle V_{i}
),
and for
\displaystyle i\neq j
the
\displaystyle(i,j)
-block corresponds to all edges between
\displaystyle V_{i}
and
\displaystyle V_{j}
.
Since every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{i}
is adjacent to every vertex of
\displaystyle V_{j}
and all such edges carry the same
weight
\displaystyle\phi(d_{i},d_{j})
, the off-diagonal block is
\displaystyle\phi(d_{i},d_{j})J_{p_{i}\times p_{j}}
. Thus, we have

\dot{A}(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{p_{1}}&\phi(d_{1},d_{2})J_{p_{1}\times p_{2}}&\cdots&\phi(d_{1},d_{t})J_{p_{1}\times p_{t}}\\
\phi(d_{2},d_{1})J_{p_{2}\times p_{1}}&0_{p_{2}}&\cdots&\phi(d_{2},d_{t})J_{p_{2}\times p_{t}}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\phi(d_{t},d_{1})J_{p_{t}\times p_{1}}&\phi(d_{t},d_{2})J_{p_{t}\times p_{2}}&\cdots&0_{p_{t}}\end{pmatrix}.

(2.3)

For
\displaystyle i\in\{1,\dots,t\}
, consider the subspace

U_{i}:=\Big\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}:\ \mathrm{supp}(x)\subseteq V_{i},\ \sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0\Big\}.

Then, it is clear that
\displaystyle\dim(U_{i})=p_{i}-1
. We claim that
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)x=0,
for every
\displaystyle x\in U_{i}
.
As
\displaystyle\mathrm{supp}(x)\subseteq V_{i}
and the
\displaystyle(i,i)
-block of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is
\displaystyle 0_{p_{i}}
,
we have
\displaystyle(\dot{A}(G)x)_{u}=0
, for all
\displaystyle u\in V_{i}
.
For a vertex
\displaystyle w\in V_{j}
with
\displaystyle j\neq i
, we have

(\dot{A}(G)x)_{w}=\sum_{u\sim w}\phi(d(w),d(u))\,x_{u}=\sum_{u\in V_{i}}\phi(d_{j},d_{i})\,x_{u}=\phi(d_{j},d_{i})\sum_{u\in V_{i}}x_{u}=0,

since
\displaystyle w
is adjacent to all vertices in
\displaystyle V_{i}
, and
\displaystyle x
sums to
\displaystyle 0
over
\displaystyle V_{i}
.
Hence, we obtain
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)x=0
. Therefore
\displaystyle 0
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
with multiplicity at least

\sum_{i=1}^{t}\dim(U_{i})=\sum_{i=1}^{t}(p_{i}-1)=n-t.

Let
\displaystyle\Pi=\{V_{1},\dots,V_{t}\}
be the partition of
\displaystyle V(G)
.
From the block form (
2.3
), the row sum from any vertex in
\displaystyle V_{i}

into the block
\displaystyle V_{j}
(with
\displaystyle i\neq j
) is constant and equals

\sum_{u\in V_{j}}\phi(d_{i},d_{j})=p_{j}\,\phi(d_{i},d_{j}),

and inside each
\displaystyle V_{i}
, it is
\displaystyle 0
.
Thus
\displaystyle\Pi
is an
equitable partition
for
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
, and the associated quotient matrix is
exactly
\displaystyle M
in (
2.2
).

It is standard that, if
\displaystyle y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{t})^{\top}\in\mathbb{R}^{t}
and for
\displaystyle x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}
with
\displaystyle x_{u}=y_{i}
for all
\displaystyle u\in V_{i}
, then we have

(\dot{A}(G)x)_{w}=\sum_{j\neq i}\sum_{u\in V_{j}}\phi(d_{i},d_{j})\,y_{j}=\sum_{j\neq i}p_{j}\phi(d_{i},d_{j})\,y_{j}=(My)_{i}\quad\text{for all }w\in V_{i}.

Hence, if
\displaystyle My=\lambda y
, then
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)x=\lambda x
. So,
\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))
.
The subspaces
\displaystyle U_{1},\dots,U_{t}
are mutually orthogonal and their direct sum has dimension
\displaystyle n-t
,
and every vector
\displaystyle x
that is constant on each partite set, that is,
\displaystyle x_{u}=y_{i}
on
\displaystyle V_{i}
lies in a
\displaystyle t
-dimensional subspace

W:=\{x:\ x\text{ is constant on each }V_{i}\}\cong\mathbb{R}^{t}.

Moreover, we have

\mathbb{R}^{V(G)}=\Big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}U_{i}\Big)\ \oplus\ W\quad\text{and}\quad\dim\Big(\bigoplus_{i=1}^{t}U_{i}\Big)=n-t,\ \dim(W)=t.

As we proved above that
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
acts as
\displaystyle 0
on
\displaystyle\bigoplus U_{i}
and acts as
\displaystyle M
on
\displaystyle W

via the identification
\displaystyle W\cong\mathbb{R}^{t}
. Hence, the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
are
exactly those coming from
\displaystyle 0
on
\displaystyle\bigoplus U_{i}
(giving
\displaystyle 0^{[n-t]}
) together with those from
\displaystyle M
on
\displaystyle W
.
Thus, we obtain
\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))=\{0^{[\,n-t\,]}\}\ \cup\ \operatorname{Spec}(M).

The following consequence is immediate from the above theorem and is concerned with the minimum number of distinct eigenvalue for the matrix
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G).

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong K_{p_{1},\dots,p_{t}}
be a complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph with
\displaystyle t\geq 3
and
\displaystyle n>t
Then
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
has exactly
three
distinct eigenvalues if and only if
\displaystyle G
is regular, that is,
\displaystyle p_{1}=\cdots=p_{t}
.

Proof.

Assume that
\displaystyle G
is regular. Then
\displaystyle p_{1}=\cdots=p_{t}=p\geq 2
and

\displaystyle d_{1}=\cdots=d_{t}=d=n-p=p(t-1)
. Hence,
\displaystyle\phi(d_{i},d_{j})=\phi(d,d)
for all
\displaystyle i\neq j
and
the quotient matrix becomes

M\;=\;p\,\phi(d,d)\,(J_{t}-I_{t}).

Since
\displaystyle J_{t}
has eigenvalues
\displaystyle t
(multiplicity
\displaystyle 1
) and
\displaystyle 0
(multiplicity
\displaystyle t-1
), it follows that

\displaystyle J_{t}-I_{t}
has eigenvalues
\displaystyle t-1
(multiplicity
\displaystyle 1
) and
\displaystyle-1
(multiplicity
\displaystyle t-1
). Therefore, we have

\operatorname{Spec}(M)=\Big\{(t-1)p\phi(d,d),\;\;\big(-p\phi(d,d)\big)^{[t-1]}\Big\}.

By Theorem
2.3
,
\displaystyle 0
is an eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)

with multiplicity
\displaystyle n-t=t(p-1)\geq 1
, and the remaining
\displaystyle t
eigenvalues are precisely
\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(M)
.
Hence
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
has exactly three distinct eigenvalues:

\{\,0,\;-p\phi(d,d),\;(t-1)p\phi(d,d)\,\}.

Conversely, assume now that
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
has exactly three distinct eigenvalues.
Since
\displaystyle n>t
, Theorem
2.3
gives that
\displaystyle 0
is an eigenvalue
of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n-t\geq 1
. Hence the remaining eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
,
namely the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle M
, can take at most
two
distinct values. Therefore
\displaystyle M
has
exactly two distinct eigenvalues, say
\displaystyle\lambda
with multiplicity
\displaystyle 1
and
\displaystyle\mu
with multiplicity
\displaystyle t-1
. As
\displaystyle M
is real symmetric, it is diagonalizable, and since
\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(M)=0
, so we have

\lambda+(t-1)\mu=0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\mu=-\frac{\lambda}{t-1}.

Let
\displaystyle v
be a unit eigenvector for
\displaystyle\lambda
. Since
\displaystyle M
has only the two eigenvalues
\displaystyle\lambda,\mu
,
its spectral decomposition is

M\;=\;\mu I_{t}+(\lambda-\mu)\,vv^{\top}.

(2.4)

Now, since the diagonal of
\displaystyle M
is zero, so for each
\displaystyle i
, we have

0=M_{ii}=\mu+(\lambda-\mu)v_{i}^{2}.

Thus,
\displaystyle v_{i}^{2}
is constant (independent of
\displaystyle i
). Since
\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{t}v_{i}^{2}=1
, we get
\displaystyle v_{i}^{2}=\tfrac{1}{t}
for all
\displaystyle i
. Replacing
\displaystyle v
by
\displaystyle-v
if necessary, we may assume
\displaystyle v_{i}=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}
, for all
\displaystyle i
, that is,
\displaystyle v=\tfrac{1}{\sqrt{t}}\mathbf{1}
, where
\displaystyle\mathbf{1}
is the all-ones vector.
Substituting into (
2.4
), we obtain

M=\mu I_{t}+(\lambda-\mu)\frac{1}{t}J_{t}\;=\;\frac{\lambda}{t-1}(J_{t}-I_{t}).

In particular, every off-diagonal entry of
\displaystyle M
is the
same
constant

\displaystyle c:=\tfrac{\lambda}{t-1}
, that is, for all
\displaystyle i\neq j
, we have

M_{ij}=c.

(2.5)

However, by using the explicit form of
\displaystyle M
derived from Theorem
2.3
, for
\displaystyle i\neq j
, we have

M_{ij}=p_{j}\,\phi(d_{i},d_{j}),\qquad\text{and}\qquad M_{ji}=p_{i}\,\phi(d_{j},d_{i})=p_{i}\,\phi(d_{i},d_{j}).

Combining this with (
2.5
), we obtain

c=p_{j}\phi(d_{i},d_{j})=p_{i}\phi(d_{i},d_{j}).

Since
\displaystyle\phi(d_{i},d_{j})\neq 0
, for adjacent degrees, it follows that
\displaystyle p_{i}=p_{j}
, for every
\displaystyle i\neq j
.
Hence all part sizes are equal, that is,
\displaystyle G
is regular.

Remark 2.5
.

The hypotheses
\displaystyle t\geq 3
and
\displaystyle n>t
are essential for the statement three distinct eigenvalues if and only if regular. For instance, if
\displaystyle t=2
(complete bipartite graphs), then
\displaystyle M
is
\displaystyle 2\times 2
with eigenvalues
\displaystyle\pm\phi(d_{1},d_{2})\sqrt{p_{1}p_{2}}
, and (when
\displaystyle n>2
) one always has

\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))=\{0^{[n-2]},\pm\phi(d_{1},d_{2})\sqrt{p_{1}p_{2}}\}
, which already gives three distinct
eigenvalues even if
\displaystyle p_{1}\neq p_{2}
.
Also, if
\displaystyle n=t
(that is, all
\displaystyle p_{i}=1
), then
\displaystyle G\cong K_{t}
is regular but
\displaystyle 0
is not an eigenvalue,
so
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
has only two distinct eigenvalues.

The spectrum of regular multipartite graph is given in the following consequence.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong K_{p,\dots,p}
be a complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph with
\displaystyle t\geq 2
,
\displaystyle p\geq 2
,
and degree
\displaystyle d=n-p=p(t-1)
. Then

\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))=\Big\{\,0^{[\,n-t\,]},\ \big(-p\phi(d,d)\big)^{[\,t-1\,]},\ (t-1)p\phi(d,d)\,\Big\}.

Proof.

As in the proof of Corollary
2.4
,
the quotient matrix is
\displaystyle M=p\phi(d,d)(J_{t}-I_{t})
, whose eigenvalues are

\displaystyle(t-1)p\phi(d,d)
(multiplicity
\displaystyle 1
) and
\displaystyle-p\phi(d,d)
(multiplicity
\displaystyle t-1
).
Theorem
2.3
gives
\displaystyle 0^{[\,n-t\,]}
as the remaining part
of the spectrum.

The next consequences is concerned with integral eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G).

Corollary 2.7
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong K_{p,\dots,p}
be a complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph with
\displaystyle t\geq 2
,
\displaystyle p\geq 2
and degree
\displaystyle d
.
Then
\displaystyle\operatorname{Spec}(\dot{A}(G))
is integral if and only if
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

By Corollary
2.6
, all eigenvalues are integer multiples
of
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
(namely
\displaystyle 0
,
\displaystyle-p\phi(d,d)
and
\displaystyle(t-1)p\phi(d,d)
). Thus, they are all integers
if and only if
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
is an integer.

For the standard choices of
\displaystyle\phi
, the following consequences discuss their integral spectra.

Corollary 2.8
.

Let
\displaystyle G\cong K_{p,\dots,p}
be a complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph with
\displaystyle t\geq 2
,
\displaystyle p\geq 2
and degree
\displaystyle d
.

1.

For
\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=1
(adjacency),
\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\dfrac{x+y}{2\sqrt{xy}}
(AG),

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\dfrac{2\sqrt{xy}}{x+y}
(GA),
\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=x+y
(first Zagreb), and

\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=xy
(second Zagreb), the spectrum of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is always integral.

2.

For
\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\dfrac{xy}{x+y}
(ISI), the spectrum of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is integral if and only if

\displaystyle d
is even.

Proof.

In each case, we evaluate
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
and then use Corollary
2.7
.
For
\displaystyle x=y=d
, we have

\phi(d,d)=1,\qquad\frac{d+d}{2\sqrt{d^{2}}}=1,\qquad\frac{2\sqrt{d^{2}}}{d+d}=1,\qquad d+d=2d\in\mathbb{Z},\qquad d^{2}\in\mathbb{Z},

so
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)\in\mathbb{Z}
in all these cases. For ISI case, we have

\phi(d,d)=\frac{d\cdot d}{d+d}=\frac{d}{2},

which is an integer exactly
\displaystyle d
is even. So, the claim follows.

3
Weighted adjacency energy of some special families of graphs

It is known that the weighted adjacency spectra of
\displaystyle K_{n}
is

\Big\{(n-1)\phi(d_{u},d_{u}),(-\phi(d_{u},d_{u}))^{n-1}\Big\},

as
\displaystyle d_{u}=d_{v}=d_{n}
for each
\displaystyle u,v\in V(K_{n}).
It is easy to see that

\dot{E}_{\phi}(K_{n})=(2n-2)\phi(d_{n},d_{n}).

Let
\displaystyle\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3},\dots,v_{n}\}
be the vertex labelling of
\displaystyle K_{n}
so that by edge deletion
\displaystyle e=v_{1}v_{2}
we obtain a new graph
\displaystyle K_{n}-e.
Also, let
\displaystyle d_{v_{1}}=d_{v_{2}}=n-2=d_{1}
and
\displaystyle d_{3}=\dots=d_{n}=n-1=d_{n}
. Then the weighted adjacency matrix of
\displaystyle G\cong K_{n}-e
is

\dot{A}(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{2}&\phi(d_{1},d_{n})J_{2\times n-2}\\
\phi(d_{1},d_{n})J_{n-2\times 2}&\phi(d_{n},d_{n})(J_{n-2}-I_{n-2})\end{pmatrix}.

By Theorems
2.1
and
2.2
, it is easy to check that the eigenvalue of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
are:
\displaystyle-\phi(d_{n},d_{n})
with multiplicity
\displaystyle n-3
, simple eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
, and the eigenvalues of the matrix

C=\begin{pmatrix}0&(n-2)\phi(d_{1},d_{n})\\
2\phi(d_{1},d_{n})&(n-3)\phi(d_{n},d_{n})\end{pmatrix}.

The eigenvalues of the matrix
\displaystyle C
are

\frac{(n-3)\phi(d_{n},d_{n})\pm\sqrt{(n-3)^{2}\phi^{2}(d_{n},d_{n})+8(n-2)\phi^{2}(d_{1},d_{n})}}{2}.

Therefore, the energy of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is

\dot{E}_{\phi}(G)=(n-3)\phi(d_{n},d_{n})+\sqrt{(n-3)^{2}\phi^{2}(d_{n},d_{n})+8(n-2)\phi^{2}(d_{1},d_{n})}.

Now, if
\displaystyle\dot{E}_{\phi}(K_{n})\leq\dot{E}_{\phi}(G)
, then we have

(n+1)\phi(d_{n},d_{n})\leq\sqrt{(n-3)^{2}\phi^{2}(d_{n},d_{n})+8(n-2)\phi^{2}(d_{1},d_{n})}.

After simplifying the above inequality we get

\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}\leq\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}<1,

(3.6)

where
\displaystyle d_{n}=n-1
and
\displaystyle d_{1}=n-2.

Now, if
\displaystyle\phi(d_{x},d_{y})=\frac{d_{x}d_{y}}{d_{x}+d_{y}}
, that is, the expression for the
\displaystyle ISI
index and we have

\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}=\frac{n-1}{2}\times\frac{2n-3}{(n-1)(n-2)},

which is clearly greater than
\displaystyle 1.
Thus, the
\displaystyle ISI
energy
\displaystyle\dot{E}_{ISI}
of
\displaystyle K_{n}
does not increase upon edge deletion, which contradicts Theorem 2 presented by Bilal and Munir
[
2
]
. In particular, for
\displaystyle n=23,
we have

\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}=\frac{2n-3}{2n-4}=\frac{43}{42}>1.

The
\displaystyle ISI
spectrum of
\displaystyle K_{23}
is
\displaystyle\{242,(-11)^{[22]}\}
and that of
\displaystyle K_{23}-e
is
\displaystyle\{240.186,0,(-11)^{[20]},-20.1859\}

and
\displaystyle\dot{E}_{ISI}(K_{23})=484>480.372=\dot{E}_{ISI}(K_{23}-e).

In fact, under the given circumstances, the spectral radius of
\displaystyle K_{n}
increases upon edge deletion if
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<1
and decreases otherwise. Thus, it follows that the spectral radius of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(K_{n})
decreases if
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<1.
We make these observations precise in the following result.

Theorem 3.1
.

The following holds for the weighted adjacency matrix of a complete graph.

1.

The spectral radius of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(K_{n})
decreases if
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

2.

The energy of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(K_{n})
decreases if
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Theorem
3.1
is useful in the sense that we do not need to calculate the spectrum and energy of
\displaystyle K_{n}
and
\displaystyle K_{n}-e,
rather we only need to calculate the ratio
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})},
which is a function of degrees and is known if
\displaystyle\phi
is fixed. In case,
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<1
, then we need to verify inequality
\displaystyle\frac{\phi(d_{n},d_{n})}{\phi(d_{1},d_{n})}<\sqrt{\frac{n-2}{n-1}}.

Conjecture 1
.

The energy of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(K_{n})
is strictly greater than
\displaystyle\dot{A}(K_{n}-e)
for almost all functions
\displaystyle\phi(x,y).

Bilal and Munir
[
2
]
also proved that the
\displaystyle ISI
energy of a regular tripartite graph
\displaystyle K_{p,p,p}
decreases upon edge deletion (see Theorems 6 and 7 of
[

\displaystyle ISI
spectrum and energy of
\displaystyle K_{p,p,p}-e
and give several counter examples.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\displaystyle p\geq 2
and let
\displaystyle K_{p,p,p}
have partite sets
\displaystyle V_{1}=\{v_{11},\dots,v_{1p}\},V_{2}=\{v_{21},\dots,v_{2p}\},
and
\displaystyle V_{3}=\{v_{31},\dots,v_{3p}\}.
Let
\displaystyle e=v_{11}v_{21}
be an edge of
\displaystyle K_{p,p,p}
, and let
\displaystyle G:=K_{p,p,p}-e
. Then the ISI-spectrum of
\displaystyle G
consists of the eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
with multiplicity
\displaystyle 3p-5
, the two simple eigenvalues

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{p\left(5p-1-4p^{2}\pm\sqrt{16p^{4}+24p^{3}-95p^{2}+70p-15}\right)}{2(4p-1)},

and the three zeros of the cubic polynomial

\displaystyle\displaystyle q_{p}(x)=\frac{1}{(4p-1)^{2}}

\displaystyle\displaystyle\Big(\left(-16p^{2}+8p-1\right)x^{3}+\left(16p^{4}-24p^{3}+9p^{2}-p\right)x^{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle+\left(32p^{6}-46p^{4}+26p^{3}-4p^{2}\right)x+32p^{7}-64p^{6}+40p^{5}-8p^{4}\Big).

Proof.

In
\displaystyle K_{p,p,p}
every vertex has degree
\displaystyle 2p
. After deleting the edge
\displaystyle e=v_{11}v_{21}
,
only the endpoints lose one neighbor, hence
\displaystyle d(v_{11})=d(v_{21})=2p-1,
and
\displaystyle d(u)=2p
for every
\displaystyle u\in V(G)\setminus\{v_{11},v_{21}\}.
For the ISI-weight
\displaystyle\phi(x,y)=\dfrac{xy}{x+y}
, we let

b:=\phi(2p,2p)=\frac{(2p)^{2}}{4p}=p,\qquad a:=\phi(2p-1,2p)=\frac{(2p-1)(2p)}{(2p-1)+2p}=\frac{2p(2p-1)}{4p-1}.

For the equitable partition, define the cells as

C_{1}=\{v_{11}\},\quad C_{2}=\{v_{21}\},\quad C_{3}=V_{1}\setminus\{v_{11}\},\quad C_{4}=V_{2}\setminus\{v_{21}\},\quad C_{5}=V_{3},

so that
\displaystyle|C_{3}|=|C_{4}|=p-1
and
\displaystyle|C_{5}|=p
.
Order the vertices as
\displaystyle(C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4},C_{5})
. Since, there are no edges inside any part, and that the deleted edge is precisely between
\displaystyle C_{1}
and
\displaystyle C_{2}
, we obtain

ISI(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0_{1\times(p-1)}&aJ_{1\times(p-1)}&aJ_{1\times p}\\
0&0&aJ_{1\times(p-1)}&0_{1\times(p-1)}&aJ_{1\times p}\\
0_{(p-1)\times 1}&aJ_{(p-1)\times 1}&0_{p-1}&bJ_{p-1}&bJ_{(p-1)\times p}\\
aJ_{(p-1)\times 1}&0_{(p-1)\times 1}&bJ_{p-1}&0_{p-1}&bJ_{(p-1)\times p}\\
aJ_{p\times 1}&aJ_{p\times 1}&bJ_{p\times(p-1)}&bJ_{p\times(p-1)}&0_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

The sets
\displaystyle C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}
are independent, and every two vertices inside each of these sets have the
same neighborhood in
\displaystyle G
, hence also the same rows in
\displaystyle ISI(G)
. Therefore, by
Theorem
2.1
, the eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
occurs with multiplicity at least

(|C_{3}|-1)+(|C_{4}|-1)+(|C_{5}|-1)=(p-2)+(p-2)+(p-1)=3p-5.

Concretely, for
\displaystyle C_{3}
one may take vectors supported on
\displaystyle C_{3}
whose coordinates sum to
\displaystyle 0
, and similarly
for
\displaystyle C_{4}
and
\displaystyle C_{5}
, these give us
\displaystyle 3p-5
linearly independent eigenvectors for eigenvalue
\displaystyle 0
. Now
\displaystyle\dim(\mathbb{R}^{V(G)})=3p
and the partition into the five cells
\displaystyle(C_{1},\dots,C_{5})
will produce
five further eigenvalues (counting multiplicity) coming from the corresponding equitable quotient. Hence, the multiplicity of
\displaystyle 0
is exactly
\displaystyle 3p-5
. The partition
\displaystyle\Pi=\{C_{1},C_{2},C_{3},C_{4},C_{5}\}
is equitable for
\displaystyle ISI(G)
, since each block
between
\displaystyle C_{i}
and
\displaystyle C_{j}
has constant row sum. Therefore, the eigenvalues of the quotient matrix
\displaystyle Q=Q(\Pi)
are eigenvalues of
\displaystyle ISI(G)
, and they account for the remaining
\displaystyle 5
eigenvalues. Computing constant row sums between the cells, we have the following equitable quotient matrix

Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&a(p-1)&ap\\
0&0&a(p-1)&0&ap\\
0&a&0&bp(p-1)&bp\\
a&0&b(p-1)&0&bp\\
a&a&b(p-1)&b(p-1)&0\end{pmatrix}.

With
\displaystyle b=p
and
\displaystyle bp=p^{2}
,
\displaystyle b(p-1)=p(p-1)
, the above matrix can be written as

Q=\begin{pmatrix}0&0&0&a(p-1)&ap\\
0&0&a(p-1)&0&ap\\
0&a&0&p(p-1)&p^{2}\\
a&0&p(p-1)&0&p^{2}\\
a&a&p(p-1)&p(p-1)&0\end{pmatrix}.

(3.7)

Next, we decomposition
\displaystyle Q
, and try to find its eigenvalues. Let
\displaystyle P
be the permutation matrix that swaps indices
\displaystyle 1\leftrightarrow 2
and
\displaystyle 3\leftrightarrow 4
, and fixes
\displaystyle 5
. From (
3.7
), it is clear that
\displaystyle PQ=QP
, and hence
\displaystyle\mathbb{R}^{5}
splits into the direct sum of the
\displaystyle(-1)
-eigenspace and
\displaystyle(+1)
-eigenspace of
\displaystyle P
as

\displaystyle W_{-}=\mathrm{span}\{u_{1},u_{2}\},u_{1}=e_{1}-e_{2},\ u_{2}=e_{3}-e_{4},

\displaystyle W_{+}=\mathrm{span}\{v_{1},v_{2},v_{3}\},

\displaystyle v_{1}=e_{1}+e_{2},\ v_{2}=e_{3}+e_{4},\ v_{3}=e_{5},
and both
\displaystyle W_{-}
and
\displaystyle W_{+}
are
\displaystyle Q
-invariant. Now, computing the action of
\displaystyle Q
on
\displaystyle W_{-}
, we have

Qu_{1}=Q(e_{1}-e_{2})=(\text{col}_{1}-\text{col}_{2})=-a(e_{3}-e_{4})=-a\,u_{2},

Qu_{2}=Q(e_{3}-e_{4})=(\text{col}_{3}-\text{col}_{4})=-a(p-1)(e_{1}-e_{2})-p(p-1)(e_{3}-e_{4})=-a(p-1)u_{1}-p(p-1)u_{2}.

Thus, in the basis
\displaystyle(u_{1},u_{2})
the restriction
\displaystyle Q|_{W_{-}}
is represented by

A_{-}=\begin{pmatrix}0&-a(p-1)\\
-a&-p(p-1)\end{pmatrix}.

Hence the eigenvalues on
\displaystyle W_{-}
are the roots of

\lambda^{2}+p(p-1)\lambda-a^{2}(p-1)=0,

that is,

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{-p(p-1)\pm\sqrt{p^{2}(p-1)^{2}+4a^{2}(p-1)}}{2}.

With
\displaystyle a=\dfrac{2p(2p-1)}{4p-1}
and simplifying, we obtain

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{p\left(5p-1-4p^{2}\pm\sqrt{16p^{4}+24p^{3}-95p^{2}+70p-15}\right)}{2(4p-1)}.

These are two
simple
eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q
, and hence of
\displaystyle ISI(G)
.

Now, to compute the restriction of
\displaystyle Q
to
\displaystyle W_{+}
in the basis
\displaystyle(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, we use columns of (
3.7
) as

\displaystyle\displaystyle Qv_{1}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=Q(e_{1}+e_{2})=(\text{col}_{1}+\text{col}_{2})=a(e_{3}+e_{4})+2ae_{5}=av_{2}+2av_{3},

\displaystyle\displaystyle Qv_{2}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=Q(e_{3}+e_{4})=(\text{col}_{3}+\text{col}_{4})=a(p-1)(e_{1}+e_{2})+p(p-1)(e_{3}+e_{4})+2p(p-1)e_{5}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=a(p-1)v_{1}+p(p-1)v_{2}+2p(p-1)v_{3},

\displaystyle\displaystyle Qv_{3}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=Qe_{5}=\text{col}_{5}=ap(e_{1}+e_{2})+p^{2}(e_{3}+e_{4})=ap\,v_{1}+p^{2}v_{2}.

Therefore,
\displaystyle Q|_{W_{+}}
is represented by the
\displaystyle 3\times 3

M=\begin{pmatrix}0&a(p-1)&ap\\
a&p(p-1)&p^{2}\\
2a&2p(p-1)&0\end{pmatrix}=\left(\begin{array}[]{ccc}0&\dfrac{2p(2p-1)(p-1)}{4p-1}&\dfrac{2p^{2}(2p-1)}{4p-1}\\
\dfrac{2p(2p-1)}{4p-1}&p(p-1)&p^{2}\\
\dfrac{4p(2p-1)}{4p-1}&2p(p-1)&0\end{array}\right).

(3.8)

Thus, the remaining three eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q
(hence of
\displaystyle ISI(G)
) are the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle M
, that is, the zeros of
\displaystyle\det(xI_{3}-M)
. A direct determinant computation gives

\displaystyle\displaystyle\det(xI_{3}-M)=

\displaystyle\displaystyle\frac{1}{(4p-1)^{2}}\Big(\left(-16p^{2}+8p-1\right)x^{3}+\left(16p^{4}-24p^{3}+9p^{2}-p\right)x^{2}

A crown graph
\displaystyle C_{p,p}
is a graph obtained from the complete bipartite graph
\displaystyle K_{p,p}
by removing edges
\displaystyle\{v_{i},u_{i}\}
for
\displaystyle 1\leq i=j\leq p
, where
\displaystyle v_{i}
and
\displaystyle u_{i}
is the vertex labelling of partite sets. That is,
\displaystyle C_{p,p}
is a graph obtained from
\displaystyle K_{p,p}
by removing a perfect matching (in a graph, a perfect matching is the set of independent edges which covers each of vertex of the graph). The degree of each vertex in
\displaystyle C_{p,p}
is
\displaystyle d=p-1.

The weighted adjacency matrix of
\displaystyle G\cong C_{p,p}
can be written as

\dot{A}(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{p}&\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})\\
\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})&0_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

It is easy to see that
\displaystyle\pm\phi(d,d)
is the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
with corresponding eigenvectors

\displaystyle\displaystyle X_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Big(-1,x_{i2},x_{i3},\dots,x_{ip},-1,y_{i2},y_{i3},\dots,y_{ip}\Big)

\displaystyle\displaystyle Y_{i}

\displaystyle\displaystyle=\Big(1,z_{i2},z_{i3},\dots,z_{ip},-1,w_{i2},w_{i3},\dots,w_{ip}\Big),

where
\displaystyle x_{ij}=\delta_{ij}=y_{ij}
and
\displaystyle z_{ij}=w_{ij}=-\delta_{ij}
with
\displaystyle\delta_{ij}
being the Kronecker delta function with
\displaystyle 2\leq i\leq p
.
The other two eigenvalue count from the following quotient matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&(p-1)\phi(d,d)\\
(p-1)\phi(d,d)&0\end{pmatrix}.

The eigenvalues of above matrix are
\displaystyle\pm(p-1)\phi(d,d).

The weighted adjacency energy of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is

\dot{E}_{\phi}(G)=2(p-1)\phi(d,d).

Let
\displaystyle V_{i}=\{u_{i1},u_{i2},\dots,u_{ip}\}
be the vertex labelling of the complete
\displaystyle t
-partite graph
\displaystyle K_{p,\dots,p}
. The
\displaystyle p
-crown multipartite graph
\displaystyle C_{p,\dots,p}
of order
\displaystyle n=pt
is obtained from
\displaystyle K_{p,\dots,p}
by deleting edges of the form
\displaystyle\{u_{ik},u_{jk}\}
with
\displaystyle 1\leq k\leq t
and
\displaystyle 1\leq i=j\leq p.
Clearly,
\displaystyle C_{p,\dots,p}
is a regular graph with each vertex of degree
\displaystyle d=(t-1)p-(t-1)=(t-1)(p-1).
Proceeding with the above idea, the weighted adjacency matrix of
\displaystyle G\cong C_{p,\dots,p}
can be written as

\dot{A}(G)=\begin{pmatrix}0_{p}&\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})&\dots&\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})\\
\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})&0_{p}&\dots&\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})&\phi(d,d)(J_{p}-I_{p})&\dots&0_{p}\end{pmatrix}.

With the eigenvalue technique used as above, the eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
consists of the eigenvalues
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
with multiplicity
\displaystyle(t-1)(p-1)
and the eigenvalues of the following quotient matrix

\displaystyle\displaystyle Q(\dot{A}(G))=\begin{pmatrix}0&(p-1)\phi(d,d)&\dots&(p-1)\phi(d,d)\\
(p-1)\phi(d,d)&0&\dots&(p-1)\phi(d,d)\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
(p-1)\phi(d,d)&(p-1)\phi(d,d)&\dots&0\end{pmatrix}_{t}.

The eigenvalues of
\displaystyle Q(\dot{A}(G))
are
\displaystyle-(p-1)\phi(d,d)
with multiplicity
\displaystyle t-1
and the eigenvalue
\displaystyle(p-1)(t-1)\phi(d,d)
. With this analysis, one eigenvalue is missing. Let it be
\displaystyle\lambda
and keeping in view the multiplicities of above eigenvalues along with the fact that sum of eigenvalues of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is
\displaystyle 0
. It follows that
\displaystyle\lambda=-(t-1)\phi(d,d)
with multiplicity
\displaystyle p-1.
Thus, the spectrum of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(G)
is completely determined. The weighted adjacency energy of
\displaystyle G
is

\dot{E}_{\phi}(G)=2(t-1)(p-1)\phi(d,d)+2(p-1)(t-1)\phi(d,d)=4(p-1)(t-1)\phi(d,d).

Form the above theory, it is evident that if
\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
is an integer then its corresponding matrix is integral. So, we have the following results.

Theorem 3.5

Let
\displaystyle d
be the degree of
\displaystyle C_{p,\dots,p}.
Then, the spectrum of
\displaystyle\dot{A}(C_{p,\dots,p})

\displaystyle\phi(d,d)
is integer.

4
Conclusion

Results of energy change due to edge deletion in specific graph families are presented in the paper along with the weighted adjacency matrices that go with them. For almost all weighted adjacency matrices, the energy of the complete graph increases by edge deletion, correcting a finding from
[
2
]
.
Additionally, it is demonstrated that deleting edge from a regular complete multipartite graph increases its energy, which solves problem in
[
2
]
.
Additionally, spectral property results for the crown multipartite graphs are shown. It is challenging to determine if energy increases or decreases when edges are removed from a weighted matrix of a graph.
