1.
Introduction

A commutation relation characterizes the disparity between different orders of operation of given operators
X
and
Y
. For notation, we use the commutator
[X,Y]:=XY-YX
to describe this relation. Note that, if
X
and
Y
commute (that is,
XY=YX
), then the commutator vanishes. There are many examples for noncommutative settings. In particular, matrices, quaternions, Lie algebras, etc., provide many instances. Noncommutative structures play a central role in discrete mathematics and physics (e.g., harmonic oscillator in quantum mechanics). The
Weyl algebra
can be considered as the complex unital algebra generated by two operators
X
and
Y
satisfying the commutation relation

(1)

[X,Y]=I,

where
I
denotes the identity operator. A concrete representation of the generators is given as operators acting on (smooth) functions of a single variable:
X\rightsquigarrow D=\frac{d}{dx}
is the derivative operator with respect to
x
, and
Y\rightsquigarrow M
is the multiplication operator with
x
(i.e.,
(Mf)(x)=xf(x)
). Thus, from a mathematical point of view, the Weyl algebra is the simplest example of a ring of differential operators and has been studied thoroughly due to its rich structure. From a physical point of view, a realization is given by
X\rightsquigarrow\hat{a}
and
Y\rightsquigarrow\hat{a}^{{\dagger}}
, i.e., the annihilation and creation operator of the harmonic oscillator. A word in the letters
X
and
Y
is in
normal ordered form
if all letters
Y
stand to the left of all the letters
X
. It is an old result due to Scherk from 1823 (see
[
20
]
for some history) that normal ordering
(YX)^{n}
(in the concrete representation mentioned above) involves the Stirling numbers of the second kind,

(2)

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{k=1}^{n}S(n,k)Y^{k}X^{k}.

In the physics community, this result was rediscovered by Katriel in 1974 (in the realization with the creation and annihilation operators). Many generalizations have been considered, see, e.g.,
[
5
,
20
]
.

In the past years, many “
q
-deformed” structures have been studied intensively by many authors. By a
q
-deformation
we mean introducing a parameter
q
into defining relations such that by taking the limit
q\rightarrow 1
the undeformed structure is recovered. For instance, the
q
-deformed Weyl algebra
is defined by the commutation relation
XY-qYX=I
, and it is clear that, for
q\rightarrow 1
, we obtain (
1
). The
quantum plane

[
16
]
is the algebra generated by two variables
X
and
Y
satisfying the
q
-commutation relation

(3)

XY=qYX.

The consequences of (
3
) have been studied in the mathematical literature and there exists a deep connection to identities for
q
-special functions, see, e.g.,
[
14
,
15
]
. In the physical literature, many
q
-deformed models have been investigated. As in the undeformed case, the commutation relation of the
q
-deformed Weyl algebra is realized by the
q
-deformed annihilation and creation operators. Furthermore, one has a
q
-analog to (
2
) involving the
q
-deformed Stirling numbers of the second kind. In more recent times, some of the
q
-deformed structures have also been
(p,q)
-deformed
, i.e., a further deformation parameter
p
has been introduced into the defining relations such that, for
p\rightarrow 1
, the
q
-deformed structure is recovered. There exist mathematical as well as physical motivations to study such
(p,q)
-deformations. For example, the
q
-deformed Weyl algebra has been further deformed from a mathematical motivation in
[
10
,
13
]
, and the commutation relation of the
q
-deformed annihilation and creation operator has been extended to a
(p,q)
-deformation, leading in particular to
(p,q)
-deformed Stirling numbers upon normal ordering
[
12
]
and to
(p,q)
-special functions in analogy to the
q
-special functions mentioned above
[
7
,
11
]
. The
(p,q)
-deformed Stirling numbers were also discussed around this time in connection with a
(p,q)
-statistics for rook placements on the staircase board
[
31
]
. Since then, many further aspects and generalizations have been studied. For more background and some history concerning normal ordering, see
[
5
,
20
,
28
]
and the references therein.

Let us describe the contents of this paper. We consider variables
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
satisfying the following
(p,q)
-commutation relations,

(4)

\displaystyle XY-qYX

\displaystyle=hf(Y)Z_{p},

(5)

\displaystyle Z_{p}Y

\displaystyle=pYZ_{p},

(6)

\displaystyle XZ_{p}

\displaystyle=pZ_{p}X,

(7)

\displaystyle Z_{1}

\displaystyle=I,

where

is a polynomial function. For a word
\omega
in letters
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
, we say it is in

if all the letters

stand to the left of all the letters

which stand to the left of all the letters

. That is,
\omega
has the form
\omega=\sum_{k,l,m}a_{k,l,m}Y^{k}Z_{p}^{l}X^{m}
for some coefficients

. For the special case where

is a monomial – where we call the algebraic structure generated by
X,Y
and

the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
– we derive the normal ordered form of arbitrary words and give a combinatorial interpretation of the normal ordering coefficients in terms of generalized Stirling numbers. We discuss many special cases by specializing the parameters

, thereby recovering several results given in literature as well as deriving new ones. For example, for
p=1
, we obtain the
q
-commutation relation

studied in
[

,
19
]
. Letting furthermore

[
2
,
3
,
4
]
. On the other hand, specializing to

, the
(p,q)
-derivative operator,

the operator of multiplication with
x
, and
Z_{p}
the Fibonacci operator
[
21
]
, one gets the
(p,q)
-commutation relations investigated in
[
23
]
.

This paper is the first part of a series of 3 papers. In a sequel
[
17
]
, we introduce
(p,q)
-rook numbers and give an interpretation of the normal ordering coefficients of words in the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra in terms of these

-deformed generalized Weyl algebra.

The structure of the present paper is as follows. In Section

2.
Basic results on normal ordering

In this section, we present basic normal ordering results for
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
satisfying (
4
) - (
7
). Before we do this, we fix some notations that will be used throughout the paper. Let
p
and
q
be two indeterminates. We define the
twin-basic numbers
for
n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}:=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
by

[n]_{p,q}=\frac{p^{n}-q^{n}}{p-q}.

We regard them as a
(p,q)
-deformation of the natural numbers. Clearly, specializing to
p=1
, one recovers the conventional
q
-deformed numbers,
[n]_{1,q}=\frac{1-q^{n}}{1-q}=[n]_{q}
. One has
[n]_{p,q}=p^{n-1}+p^{n-2}q+\cdots+pq^{n-2}+q^{n-1}
, reducing for
p=1
to the well-known identity
[n]_{q}=1+q+q^{2}+\cdots+q^{n-1}
. The corresponding factorials and binomial coefficients are given by

[n]_{p,q}!=[n]_{p,q}[n-1]_{p,q}\cdots[2]_{p,q}[1]_{p,q},\,\,\,{n\choose k}_{p,q}=\frac{[n]_{p,q}!}{[n-k]_{p,q}![k]_{p,q}!},

together with
[0]_{p,q}!=1
. One may introduce a
(p,q)
-derivative

D_{p,q}
by letting it act as

(D_{p,q}f)(x):=\frac{f(px)-f(qx)}{(p-q)x}.

For
p=1
, one recovers the
Jackson-derivative

D_{q}
, i.e.,
(D_{1,q}f)(x)=\frac{f(x)-f(qx)}{(1-q)x}=(D_{q}f)(x)
. The action of
D_{p,q}
on monomials is given, for
n\in\mathbb{N}
, by

D_{p,q}x^{n}=[n]_{p,q}x^{n-1}.

See
[
24
]
for more information on
D_{p,q}
and some references.

2.1.
The normal ordered form of
X^{m}Y

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying (
4
) - (
7
), such that
f
is a polynomial function in (
4
). In this section, we present an identity for normal ordering
X^{m}Y
. For instance, it is clear that from (
4
), one obtains

(8)

X^{2}Y=q^{2}YX^{2}+qhf(Y)Z_{p}X+hXf(Y)Z_{p}.

Hence, we have to consider the normal ordered form of the last term on the right-hand side in (
8
). For that, we need the following result.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying (
4
) - (
7
). For
k\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

(9)

XY^{k}=q^{k}Y^{k}X+h[k]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k-1}Z_{p}.

Proof.

The proof will be by induction on
k
. For
k=1
, (
9
) holds true by assumption. By the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle XY^{k+1}

\displaystyle=q^{k}Y^{k}XY+h[k]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k-1}Z_{p}Y

\displaystyle=q^{k}Y^{k}(qYX+hf(Y)Z_{p})+h[k]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k-1}Z_{p}Y

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}Y^{k+1}X+q^{k}hY^{k}f(Y)Z_{p}+h[k]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k-1}Z_{p}Y

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}Y^{k+1}X+q^{k}hf(Y)Y^{k}Z_{p}+ph[k]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k}Z_{p}

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}Y^{k+1}X+h(q^{k}+p[k]_{p,q})f(Y)Y^{k}Z_{p}

\displaystyle=q^{k+1}Y^{k+1}X+h[k+1]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{k}Z_{p},

where in the last equation we used that
[k+1]_{p,q}=q^{k}+p[k]_{p,q}
.
∎

Similarly, one can generalize the above lemma to a polynomial in
Y
as follows.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying (
4
) - (
7
). For any polynomial
L\in\mathbb{C}[Y]
, we have the following identity,

(10)

XL(Y)=L(qY)X+hf(Y)D_{p,q}L(Y)Z_{p}.

Proof.

Let
L(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}y^{i}
be a polynomial. By virtue of Lemma
2.1
, and using the linearity of the
(p,q)
-derivative operator
D_{p,q}
, one has

\displaystyle XL(Y)

\displaystyle=X\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}Y^{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}\big(q^{i}Y^{i}X+h[i]_{p,q}f(Y)Y^{i-1}Z_{p}\big)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}(qY)^{i}X+hf(Y)\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}[i]_{p,q}Y^{i-1}Z_{p}

\displaystyle=L(qY)X+hf(Y)D_{p,q}L(Y)Z_{p},

as requested.
∎

Now, letting
L\equiv f
in Proposition
2.2
, we obtain

Xf(Y)=f(qY)X+hf(Y)D_{p,q}f(Y)Z_{p}.

Using this in (
8
) yields the normal ordered form of
X^{2}Y
,

(11)

X^{2}Y=q^{2}YX^{2}+h\big(qf(Y)+pf(qY)\big)Z_{p}X+h^{2}f(Y)D_{p,q}f(Y)Z_{p}^{2}.

To describe the general case
X^{m}Y
, let us first introduce some basic tools about partitions from
[
19
]
which we will use in the following. Recall that a
partition

\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{k})\equiv\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\ldots\lambda_{k}
is a weakly decreasing sequence of positive integers. We denote the sum of the parts of
\lambda
by
|\lambda|
, that is,
|\lambda|=\sum_{i=1}^{k}\lambda_{i}
. For a partition
\lambda
, the
Young diagram

Y_{\lambda}
of shape
\lambda
is a left-justified diagram of
|\lambda|
boxes, with
\lambda_{i}
black boxes in the
i
th column. We denote the set of all Young diagrams that are contained in a
\ell\times k
box by
{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell,k}
. Define
{\mathcal{I}}_{m}=\bigcup_{k=0}^{m}{\mathcal{I}}_{m-k,k}
. Note that
{\mathcal{I}}_{m-k,k}
contains
\binom{m}{k}
elements, hence
|{\mathcal{I}}_{m}|=2^{m}
. In the following, we will denote the empty partition in
{\mathcal{I}}_{m-k,k}
also by
\emptyset
(instead of, e.g.,
000\in{\mathcal{I}}_{1,3}
or
00\in{\mathcal{I}}_{2,2}
, see Figure
1
).

.

\mathcal{I}_{4}
containing 16 partitions is given in Figure

.

Definition 2.4
.

Let
f(y)=\sum_{k=0}^{s}\alpha_{k}y^{k}
be a polynomial. For a partition
\lambda
with
d\geq 1
parts, we define

\displaystyle g_{d}(\lambda;Y)

\displaystyle\equiv g_{d}(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d};Y)

\displaystyle:=\sum_{i_{1},i_{2},\ldots,i_{d}=0}^{s}\Big(q^{\sum_{j=1}^{d}i_{j}\lambda_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{i_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}[i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}+1-j]_{p,q}\Big)Y^{i_{1}+i_{2}+\cdots+i_{d}+1-d}.

Moreover, for
d=0
, we set

g_{0}(\emptyset;Y):=Y.

Note that
g_{d}(\lambda;Y)
depends on the polynomial
f
, but to simplify the notation, we do not indicate this dependency explicitly. It is clear that, for
d=1
, we obtain, for
k\geq 0
,

g_{1}(k;Y)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}q^{ki}\alpha_{i}Y^{i}=f(q^{k}Y).

Example 2.5
.

Let us give the explicit values of
g_{d}(\lambda;Y)
for small
d
.

•

g_{1}(\emptyset;Y)=f(Y)
,

•

g_{1}(1;Y)=f(qY)
,

•

g_{1}(2;Y)=f(q^{2}Y)
,

•

g_{2}(\emptyset;Y)=f(Y)D_{p,q}f(Y)
,

•

g_{2}(1;Y)=D_{p,q}f(qY)
,

•

g_{2}(10;Y)=f(Y)D_{p,q}f(qY)
,

•

g_{2}(11;Y)=f(qY)D_{p,q}f(qY)
,

•

g_{3}(\emptyset;Y)=f(Y)\bigl(f(pY)D^{2}_{p,q}f(Y)+q^{-1}D_{p,q}f(qY)D_{p,q}f(Y)\bigr)
.

Following
[
19
]
, for any partition
\lambda=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d})
we define
\lambda^{*}
to be the partition

\lambda^{*}=(\lambda_{1}+1,\lambda_{2}+1,\ldots,\lambda_{d}+1),

and
\lambda_{*}
to be the partition

\lambda_{*}=(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2},\ldots,\lambda_{d},0).

Thus,
Y_{\lambda^{*}}
is the Young diagram that is obtained from
Y_{\lambda}
by adding a filled row before the first row of
Y_{\lambda}
, and
Y_{\lambda_{*}}
is the Young diagram that is obtained from
Y_{\lambda}
by adding an empty column on the right side of
Y_{\lambda}
. Thus, if
Y_{\lambda}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell,k}
, then
Y_{\lambda^{*}}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell+1,k}
and
Y_{\lambda_{*}}\in{\mathcal{I}}_{\ell,k+1}
.

Lemma 2.6
.

Let
\lambda
be a partition with
d\geq 0
parts. Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying (
4
) - (
7
), where
f(y)=\sum_{i=0}^{s}\alpha_{i}y^{i}
is a polynomial. Then
g_{d}(\lambda;Y)
satisfies the following recursive formula,

(12)

Xg_{d}(\lambda;Y)=q^{1-d}g_{d}(\lambda^{*};Y)X+hg_{d+1}(\lambda_{*};Y)Z_{p}.

Proof.

The proof is the same as for
p=1
given in
[
19
]
. By virtue of Definition
2.4
, we obtain

\displaystyle Xg_{d}(\lambda;Y)

\displaystyle=Xg_{d}(\lambda_{1},\ldots,\lambda_{d};Y)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\geq 0}\Big(q^{\sum_{j=1}^{d}i_{j}\lambda_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{i_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}[i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}+1-j]_{p,q}\Big)XY^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{d}+1-d}

\displaystyle=q^{1-d}\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d}\geq 0}\Big(q^{\sum_{j=1}^{d}i_{j}\lambda^{*}_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{i_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d-1}[i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}+1-j]_{p,q}\Big)Y^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{d}+1-d}X

\displaystyle\qquad+h\sum_{i_{1},\ldots,i_{d+1}\geq 0}\Big(q^{\sum_{j=1}^{d}i_{j}\lambda_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}\alpha_{i_{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{d}[i_{1}+\cdots+i_{j}+1-j]_{p,q}\Big)Y^{i_{1}+\cdots+i_{d}-d}Z_{p}

\displaystyle=q^{1-d}g_{d}(\lambda^{*};Y)X+hg_{d+1}(\lambda_{*};Y)Z_{p},

where we used Lemma
2.1
in the second equality.
∎

Now, we are in the position to present the normal ordered form of
X^{m}Y
.

Theorem 2.7
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying (
4
) - (
7
), where
f(y)=\sum_{j=0}^{s}\alpha_{j}y^{j}
is a polynomial. Then the normal ordered form of
X^{m}Y
is given by

(13)

X^{m}Y=\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j}\Big(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}g_{j}(\lambda;Y)\Big)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m-j}.

Proof.

We use induction on
m
. Obviously, for
m=0
, the left-hand side of (
13
) gives
Y
, and the right-hand side is given by

\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{0,0}}q^{0-0-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}g_{0}(\lambda;Y)=g_{0}(\emptyset;Y)=Y.

Assume that (
13
) holds for
m\geq 0
. Then, by the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle X^{m+1}Y

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}\big(Xg_{j}(\lambda;Y)\big)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}\Big(q^{1-j}g_{j}(\lambda^{*};Y)X+hg_{j+1}(\lambda_{*};Y)Z_{p}\Big)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j+1-\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\lambda_{i}+1)}p^{|\lambda|}g_{j}(\lambda^{*};Y)XZ_{p}^{j}\right)X^{m-j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j+1}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}g_{j+1}(\lambda_{*};Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j+1}X^{m-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m-j,j}}q^{m-j+1-\sum_{i=1}^{j}(\lambda_{j}+1)}p^{|\lambda|+j}g_{j}(\lambda^{*};Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{*}\in\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}}q^{m+1-j-|\lambda_{*}|}p^{|\lambda_{*}|}g_{j}(\lambda_{*};Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m+1-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda^{*}\in\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}}q^{m+1-j-|\lambda^{*}|}p^{|\lambda^{*}|}g_{j}(\lambda^{*};Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m+1-j}

\displaystyle\qquad\qquad+\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda_{*}\in\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}}q^{m+1-j-|\lambda_{*}|}p^{|\lambda_{*}|}g_{j}(\lambda_{*};Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m+1-j}

\displaystyle=\sum_{j=0}^{m+1}h^{j}\left(\sum_{\lambda\in\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}}q^{m+1-j-|\lambda|}p^{|\lambda|}g_{j}(\lambda;Y)\right)Z_{p}^{j}X^{m+1-j},

where the second equality follows from Lemma
2.6
. The sum over
\lambda_{*}
is understood as the sum over all Young diagrams in
\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}
, where the last column is empty (by convention, we define
\mathcal{I}_{k,-1}
to be the empty set), while the sum over
\lambda^{*}
is understood as the sum over all Young diagrams
\mathcal{I}_{m+1-j,j}
such that the first row is filled.
∎

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}[n+j]_{p,q}=[k]_{q}!{n+k-1\choose k}_{p,q}
, we can write this equivalently as

(31)

X^{m}Y^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigg(h^{k}q^{n(m-k)}{n+k-1\choose k}_{p,q}\frac{[m]_{p,q}!}{[m-k]_{p,q}!}\bigg)Y^{n+k}Z_{p}^{k}X^{m-k}.

Specializing further to
p=1
, this reduces to

X^{m}Y^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\bigg(h^{k}q^{n(m-k)}{n+k-1\choose k}_{q}\frac{[m]_{q}!}{[m-k]_{q}!}\bigg)Y^{n+k}X^{m-k},

a well-known result for the
q
-deformed Jordan plane (see
[
20
, Proposition 7.61]
and references therein), as expected. Using (
31
), we can show the following analog to Proposition
2.16
and Proposition
2.20
.

.

3.
Normal ordering arbitrary words in the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra

In this section, we consider normal ordering arbitrary words
\omega
in
X
and
Y
which satisfy the commutation relations (
16
) of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
A_{s;h|p,q}
. Such a word can be written in the form

(34)

\omega=X^{m_{r}}Y^{n_{r}}\cdots X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}

with suitable exponents
m_{k},n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}=\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
. The resulting normal oderering coefficients can be interpreted as generalized Stirling numbers and have a connection to rook numbers.

3.1.
Normal ordering arbitrary words

To start, we consider normal ordering
X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}
. This can be done in two steps: First commuting
X^{m_{1}}
and
Y^{n_{1}}
using (
18
), then commuting
X^{m_{2}}
and
Y^{n_{1}+*}
using again (
18
). Let us abbreviate for the following

a_{s;p,q}(n,k):=\prod_{j=0}^{k-1}[n+(s-1)j]_{p,q}.

The above-mentioned first step yields

X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\bigg(h^{k_{1}}q^{n_{1}(m_{1}-k_{1})}{m_{1}\choose k_{1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}a_{s;p,q}(n_{1},k_{1})\bigg)X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1}}Z_{p}^{k_{1}}X^{m_{1}-k_{1}}.

Now, as second step we have to commute
X^{m_{2}}
and
Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1}}
using (
18
),

\displaystyle X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{m_{2}}\bigg(h^{k_{2}}q^{(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1})(m_{2}-k_{2})}{m_{2}\choose k_{2}}_{p,q^{s-1}}a_{s;p,q}(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1},k_{2})\bigg)

\displaystyle Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{2}}X^{m_{2}-k_{2}}.

Inserting this, one obtains

\displaystyle X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}

\displaystyle X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{m_{2}}\bigg\{h^{k_{1}+k_{2}}q^{n_{1}(m_{1}-k_{1})+(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1})(m_{2}-k_{2})}{m_{1}\choose k_{1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{m_{2}\choose k_{2}}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle a_{s;p,q}(n_{1},k_{1})a_{s;p,q}(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1},k_{2})\bigg\}Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{2}}X^{m_{2}-k_{2}}Z_{p}^{k_{1}}X^{m_{1}-k_{1}}.

Using
X^{m_{2}-k_{2}}Z_{p}^{k_{1}}=p^{k_{1}(m_{2}-k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{1}}X^{m_{2}-k_{2}}
, one finds

Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{2}}X^{m_{2}-k_{2}}Z_{p}^{k_{1}}X^{m_{1}-k_{1}}=p^{k_{1}(m_{2}-k_{2})}Y^{n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}X^{m_{1}+m_{2}-(k_{1}+k_{2})}.

To introduce some further abbreviations, let us write
{\bf n}=(n_{2},n_{1})
and denote
|{\bf n}|=n_{1}+n_{2}
, and, similarly, for
{\bf m}
and
{\bf k}
. Denoting also

{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}={m_{2}\choose k_{2}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{m_{1}\choose k_{1}}_{p,q^{s-1}},

we can write the above result more briefly as

\displaystyle X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}=

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{m_{2}}\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{k_{1}(m_{2}-k_{2})}q^{n_{1}(m_{1}-k_{1})+(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1})(m_{2}-k_{2})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle a_{s;p,q}(n_{1},k_{1})a_{s;p,q}(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1},k_{2})\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|}.

We can iterate this to see how a recursive structure appears in the coefficients,

\displaystyle X^{m_{3}}Y^{n_{3}}

\displaystyle X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{m_{2}}\bigg\{h^{k_{1}+k_{2}}q^{n_{1}(m_{1}-k_{1})+(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1})(m_{2}-k_{2})}{m_{1}\choose k_{1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{m_{2}\choose k_{2}}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle a_{s;p,q}(n_{1},k_{1})a_{s;p,q}(n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)k_{1},k_{2})\bigg\}X^{m_{3}}Y^{n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}Z_{p}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}X^{m_{2}+m_{1}-(k_{1}+k_{2})}.

Now, we have to commute
X^{m_{3}}
and
Y^{n_{3}n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}
using (
18
),

\displaystyle X^{m_{3}}

\displaystyle Y^{n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2})}=\sum_{k_{3}=0}^{m_{3}}\bigg(h^{k_{3}}q^{(n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2}))(m_{3}-k_{3})}{m_{3}\choose k_{3}}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle a_{s;p,q}(n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2}),k_{3})\bigg)Y^{n_{3}+n_{2}+n_{1}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3})}Z_{p}^{k_{3}}X^{m_{3}-k_{3}}.

Inserting this, using
X^{m_{3}-k_{3}}Z_{p}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}=p^{(k_{1}+k_{2})(m_{3}-k_{3})}Z_{p}^{k_{1}+k_{2}}X^{m_{3}-k_{3}}
, and using the notation introduced above (extended from
r=2
to arbitrary
r
), we obtain for the right-hand side:

\displaystyle\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{1}}\sum_{k_{2}=0}^{m_{2}}\sum_{k_{3}=0}^{m_{3}}\bigg\{

\displaystyle h^{|{\bf k}|}q^{n_{1}(m_{1}-k_{1})+(n_{1}+n_{2}+(s-1)k_{1})(m_{2}-k_{2})+(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2}))(m_{3}-k_{3})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle p^{k_{1}(m_{2}-k_{2})+(k_{1}+k_{2})(m_{3}-k_{3})}a_{s;p,q}(n_{1},k_{1})a_{s;p,q}(n_{1}+n_{2}+(s-1)k_{1},k_{2}))

\displaystyle a_{s;p,q}(n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}+(s-1)(k_{1}+k_{2}),k_{3})\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|}.

Let us introduce some further notations to get less clumsy formulas. Let us first abbreviate
({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}
to be the set of all
{\bf k}=(k_{1},\ldots,k_{r})\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{r}
such that
0\leq k_{j}\leq m_{j}
for
j=1,\ldots,r
. Furthermore, let us introduce

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r}(m_{j}-k_{j})\left\{\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}+(s-1)\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}k_{l}\right\},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k}):=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{r-1}(m_{j+1}-k_{j+1})\sum_{l=1}^{j}k_{l},

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k}):=

\displaystyle\prod_{j=1}^{r}a_{s;p,q}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}+(s-1)\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}k_{l},k_{j}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}+(s-1)\sum_{l=1}^{j-1}k_{l}+(s-1)\ell\right]_{p,q}.

Then we can write the above result compactly as

\displaystyle X^{m_{3}}Y^{n_{3}}X^{m_{2}}Y^{n_{2}}X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}=\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{3}}\bigg\{

\displaystyle h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{3}({\bf m},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{3|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{3|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|}.

Thus, it is natural to conjecture that the above structure holds in general.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying the commutation relations (
16
) of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
A_{s;h|p,q}
. Then the normal ordered form of an arbitrary word
\omega=X^{m_{r}}Y^{n_{r}}\cdots X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}
,
m_{k},n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is given by

(35)

\omega=\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}}\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|}.

Proof.

We give a proof by induction in
r\in\mathbb{N}
. Clearly, for
r=1,2,3
the above formula holds true, as was discussed above. So it remains to show the formula for
r+1
, assuming it holds true for
r
. Thus, let us consider
X^{m_{r+1}}Y^{n_{r+1}}X^{m_{r}}Y^{n_{r}}\cdots X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}
. Using the induction hypothesis, this equals

\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}}\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})\bigg\}X^{m_{r+1}}Y^{n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|},

where the bold vectors still have
r
components. Now, one has to use (
18
) to find

\displaystyle X^{m_{r+1}}

\displaystyle Y^{n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}=\sum_{k_{r+1}=0}^{m_{r+1}}\bigg(h^{k_{r+1}}q^{(n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|)(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})}{m_{r+1}\choose k_{r+1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle\prod_{j=0}^{k_{r+1}-1}[n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|+(s-1)j]_{p,q}\bigg)Y^{n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|+(s-1)k_{r+1}}Z_{p}^{k_{r+1}}X^{m_{r+1}-k_{r+1}}.

Inserting this, using
X^{m_{r+1}-k_{r+1}}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}=p^{|{\bf k}|(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{m_{r+1}-k_{r+1}}
, and collecting terms, one finds

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}}\sum_{k_{r+1}=0}^{m_{r+1}}

\displaystyle\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|+k_{r+1}}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})+|{\bf k}|(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})+(n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|)(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}

\displaystyle{m_{r+1}\choose k_{r+1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})a_{s;p,q}(n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|,k_{r+1})\bigg\}

\displaystyle Y^{n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)(|{\bf k}|+k_{r+1})}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|+k_{r+1}}X^{|{\bf m}|+m_{r+1}-(|{\bf k}|+k_{r+1})}

Let us denote the vectors with
r+1
components by
{\bf m}^{\prime}=(m_{r+1},m_{r},\ldots,m_{1})=(m_{r+1},{\bf m})
, and, similarly, for
{\bf n}^{\prime}
and
{\bf k}^{\prime}
. The above definitions of the auxiliary functions show that

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{r+1|s}({\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf n}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})+(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})\left\{\sum_{l=1}^{r+1}n_{l}+(s-1)\sum_{l=1}^{r}k_{l}\right\}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})+(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})(n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|),

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{r+1}({\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})+(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})\sum_{l=1}^{r}k_{l}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})+(m_{r+1}-k_{r+1})|{\bf k}|,

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{r+1|s;p,q}({\bf n}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})a_{s;p,q}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{r+1}n_{l}+(s-1)\sum_{l=1}^{r}k_{l},k_{r+1}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})a_{s;p,q}\left(n_{r+1}+|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|,k_{r+1}\right).

Observing
|{\bf n}^{\prime}|=|{\bf n}|+n_{r+1}
(and, similarly, for
|{\bf m}^{\prime}|
and
|{\bf k}^{\prime}|
), and using

{\bf m^{\prime}\choose\bf k^{\prime}}_{p,q^{s-1}}={m_{r+1}\choose k_{r+1}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}},

we can, therefore, write the preceding sum as

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf k}^{\prime}\in({\bf 0}^{\prime},{\bf m}^{\prime})_{r+1}}

\displaystyle\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}^{\prime}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r+1}({\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r+1|s}({\bf m}^{\prime},{\bf n}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})}{\bf m^{\prime}\choose\bf k^{\prime}}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r+1|s;p,q}({\bf n}^{\prime},{\bf k}^{\prime})\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}^{\prime}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}^{\prime}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}^{\prime}|}X^{|{\bf m}^{\prime}|-|{\bf k}^{\prime}|},

as requested.
∎

By splitting the sum, we can write (
35
) equivalently as

(36)

\omega=\sum_{k=0}^{|{\bf m}|}h^{k}\mathscr{J}_{p,q,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},k)Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)k}Z_{p}^{k}X^{|{\bf m}|-k},

where we introduced the coefficients

(37)

\mathscr{J}_{p,q,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},k):=\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}\atop|{\bf k}|=k}\bigg\{p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})\bigg\}.

Thus, from (
36
) we can infer that the variables in the normal ordered form appear in the combinations
Y^{|{\bf n}|}X^{|{\bf m}|},Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)}Z_{p}X^{|{\bf m}|-1},\ldots,Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf m}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf m}|}
, with coefficients given by (
37
). Note that the summand for
k=0
is given by
\mathscr{J}_{p,q,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},0)Y^{|{\bf n}|}X^{|{\bf m}|}
where

\mathscr{J}_{p,q,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},0)=p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf 0})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf 0})}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf 0}).

From the definitions, one finds

{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf 0})=0,\,\,\,{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf 0})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l},\,\,\,{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf 0})=1.

Let us define for any word
\omega=X^{m_{r}}Y^{n_{r}}\cdots X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}
its
index

{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)
by

{\mathcal{I}}(\omega):=\sum_{j=1}^{r}m_{j}\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l},

see
[
20
, Section 7.1.1]
. If
X
and
Y
were
q
-commuting, it would count the number of times one has to use the commutation relation in order to bring
\omega
into normal ordered form. Thus,
{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf 0})={\mathcal{I}}(\omega)
, and, consequently,
\mathscr{J}_{p,q,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},0)=q^{{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)}
. Therefore, we can write in analogy to (
19
) the expansion

(38)

\omega=q^{{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)}Y^{|{\bf n}|}X^{|{\bf m}|}+{\mathcal{O}}(h).

Corollary 3.2
.

For
s=1
(corresponding to the

-deformed shift algebra, see Section

{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|1}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})=\sum_{j=1}^{r}(m_{j}-k_{j})\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l},\,\,{\mathcal{A}}_{r|1;p,q}({\bf n},{\bf k})=\prod_{j=1}^{r}a_{1;p,q}\left(\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}\right)=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\prod_{\ell=0}^{k_{j}-1}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}\right]_{p,q}.

Hence, (

) reduces to

\omega=\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}}\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf m},{\bf k})}q^{\sum\limits_{j=1}^{r}(m_{j}-k_{j})\sum\limits_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\left[\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}\right]_{p,q}^{k_{j}}\bigg\}Y^{|{\bf n}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|}.

If we specialize furthermore to
p=1
, we obtain the following extension of (

),

\omega=Y^{|{\bf n}|}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Biggl(q^{\sum\limits_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}}X+h\left[\sum_{l=1}^{j}n_{l}\right]_{q}\Biggr)^{m_{j}}.

Corollary 3.3
.

Recall that letting
h=0
in (
4
) – (
7
) means considering
q
-commuting variables
X
and
Y
, i.e.,
XY=qYX
. For
h=0
, (
35
) reduces to
\omega=q^{{\mathcal{I}}(\omega)}Y^{|{\bf n}|}X^{|{\bf m}|}
, where we used the index of a word introduced above. This is a well-known result for
q
-commuting variables (see
[
20
, Section 7.1.1]
). Comparing with (
38
), we see that this is the lowest order term in an expansion in
h
.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying the commutation relations (
16
) of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
A_{s;h|p,q}
. Then the normal ordered form of the word
\omega=(XY)^{r},r\in\mathbb{N}
, is given by

(40)

(XY)^{r}=\sum_{k=0}^{r}h^{k}\bigg\{\sum_{{\bf k}\in\{0,1\}^{r}\atop|{\bf k}|=k}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}({\bf 1},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf k})}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}({\bf 1},{\bf k})\bigg\}Y^{r+(s-1)k}Z_{p}^{k}X^{r-k}.

Proof.

Using the fact that, for all
{\bf k}\in\{0,1\}^{r}
,

(41)

{\bf 1\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}=1,

this follows immediately from (
35
) since in the case at hand
{\bf m}={\bf n}=(1,1,\ldots,1)
of length
r
.
∎

In Theorem
3.1
, the normal ordered form of a word
\omega=w_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=X^{m_{r}}Y^{n_{r}}\cdots X^{m_{1}}Y^{n_{1}}
,
m_{k},n_{k}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, is given. What about considering the letter
Z_{p}
on the left-hand side? Since
Z_{p}
almost commutes with
X
and
Y
we can pull all letters
Z_{p}
to the right (or, alternatively, to the left), yielding some powers of
p
and something of the form
\omega^{\ast}Z_{p}^{n}
, where
\omega^{\ast}
is a word in
X
and
Y
only. This can be normal ordered and then the factor
Z_{p}^{n}
be brought to its correct position. Let us consider this in more detail and let

(42)

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}=Z_{p}^{u_{2r}}Y^{n_{r}}Z_{p}^{u_{2r-1}}X^{m_{r}}\cdots Z_{p}^{u_{4}}Y^{n_{2}}Z_{p}^{u_{3}}X^{m_{2}}\cdot Z_{p}^{u_{2}}Y^{n_{1}}Z_{p}^{u_{1}}X^{m_{1}}\cdot Z_{p}^{u_{0}}

be an arbitrary word in
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
. Except for the right-most factor
Z_{p}^{u_{0}}
we can group the factors as
r
“blocks” of the form
Z_{p}^{u_{2k}}Y^{n_{k}}Z_{p}^{u_{2k-1}}X^{m_{k}}
, where
k=1,\ldots,r
. Commuting
Z_{p}^{u_{2k-1}}
to the right yields a factor
p^{u_{2k-1}(\sum_{j=1}^{k-1}n_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{j})}
, whereas commuting
Z_{p}^{u_{2k}}
to the right yields a factor
p^{u_{2k}(\sum_{j=1}^{k}n_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{k}m_{j})}
. Together, we get as exponent

u_{2k-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{2k-1}{2}\rfloor}n_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{2k}{2}\rfloor}m_{j}\right)+u_{2k}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{2k}{2}\rfloor}n_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{2k+1}{2}\rfloor}m_{j})\right).

Thus, pulling all letters
Z_{p}
of
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}
to the right, we get a factor of
p^{\mathscr{L}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u})}
, where

(43)

\mathscr{L}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{2r}u_{\ell}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell}{2}\rfloor}n_{j}-\sum_{j=1}^{\lfloor\frac{\ell+1}{2}\rfloor}m_{j}\right).

Denoting by
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}^{\ast}
the
reduced word of
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}
where all letters
Z_{p}
are deleted from
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}
, we can write (using
|{\bf u}|=u_{2r}+u_{2r-1}+\cdots+u_{1}+u_{0}
)

(44)

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}=p^{\mathscr{L}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u})}\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}^{\ast}Z_{p}^{|{\bf u}|}.

Now, we can use (
35
) for
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}^{\ast}
to obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying the commutation relations (
16
) of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
A_{s;h|p,q}
. Then the normal ordered form of an arbitrary word
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}=Z_{p}^{u_{2r}}Y^{n_{r}}Z_{p}^{u_{2r-1}}X^{m_{r}}\cdots Z_{p}^{u_{2}}Y^{n_{1}}Z_{p}^{u_{1}}X^{m_{1}}Z_{p}^{u_{0}}
is given by

(45)

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}=\sum_{{\bf k}\in({\bf 0},{\bf m})_{r}}{\mathfrak{N}}_{p,q;h,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}|{\bf k})\,Y^{|{\bf n}|+(s-1)|{\bf k}|}Z_{p}^{|{\bf u}|+|{\bf k}|}X^{|{\bf m}|-|{\bf k}|},

where we introduced the abbreviation

{\mathfrak{N}}_{p,q;h,s}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}|{\bf k}):=\bigg\{h^{|{\bf k}|}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{r}^{\ast}({\bf m},{\bf k})+|{\bf u}|(|{\bf m}|-k)+\mathscr{L}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}^{\ast}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf k})}{\bf m\choose\bf k}_{p,q^{s-1}}{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}^{\ast}({\bf n},{\bf k})\bigg\}

with
\mathscr{L}({\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u})
defined in (
43
) and the functions
{\mathcal{P}}_{r}^{\ast},{\mathcal{Q}}_{r|s}^{\ast}
and
{\mathcal{A}}_{r|s;p,q}^{\ast}
have been labeled with the asterisk “
\ast
” to indicate that they are evaluated for the reduced word
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n},{\bf u}}^{\ast}
.

3.2.
The connection to generalized Stirling numbers

Let us recall that in this context
generalized Stirling numbers

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
were introduced as normal ordering coefficients of
(YX)^{n}
in
A_{s;h|p,q}
as follows (see
[
23
, Definition 3.3]
),

(46)

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)\,Y^{s(n-k)+k}Z_{p}^{n-k}X^{k}.

They satisfy the recurrence relation
[
23
, Theorem 3.5]

(47)

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n+1,k|p,q)=p^{n-k+1}q^{s(n-k+1)+k-1}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k-1|p,q)+h[s(n-k)+k]_{p,q}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q).

Example 3.6
.

Let
s=0
. Then (
46
) reduces to

(48)

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{0;h}(n,k|p,q)\,Y^{k}Z_{p}^{n-k}X^{k}.

Comparing this with
[
22
, Proposition 3]
, we can identify
\mathfrak{S}_{0;1}(n,k|p,q)=S_{p,q}(n,k)
with the
(p,q)
-Stirling numbers of the second kind discussed in
[
22
]
. One has
S_{p,q}(0,0)=1
and

(49)

S_{p,q}(n,k)=p^{n-k}q^{k-1}S_{p,q}(n-1,k-1)+[k]_{p,q}S_{p,q}(n-1,k),

following also from (
47
) for
s=0
and
h=1
.

Since
(YX)^{n}=Y(XY)^{n-1}X
, we can use Corollary
3.4
for
r=n-1
to obtain

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{k=0}^{n-1}h^{k}\bigg\{\sum_{{\bf k}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\atop|{\bf k}|=k}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{n-1}({\bf 1},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{n-1|s}({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf k})}{\mathcal{A}}_{n-1|s;p,q}({\bf 1},{\bf k})\bigg\}Y^{n+(s-1)k}Z_{p}^{k}X^{n-k}.

Letting
\kappa=n-k
, this gives

(YX)^{n}=\sum_{\kappa=1}^{n}h^{n-\kappa}\bigg\{\sum_{{\bf k}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\atop|{\bf k}|=n-\kappa}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{n-1}({\bf 1},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{n-1|s}({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf k})}{\mathcal{A}}_{n-1|s;p,q}({\bf 1},{\bf k})\bigg\}Y^{s(n-\kappa)+\kappa}Z_{p}^{n-\kappa}X^{\kappa}.

Comparing this with (
46
), one obtains the following result.

Proposition 3.7
.

The generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
can be expressed as follows,

(50)

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)=h^{n-k}\sum_{{\bf k}\in\{0,1\}^{n-1}\atop|{\bf k}|=n-k}p^{{\mathcal{P}}_{n-1}({\bf 1},{\bf k})}q^{{\mathcal{Q}}_{n-1|s}({\bf 1},{\bf 1},{\bf k})}{\mathcal{A}}_{n-1|s;p,q}({\bf 1},{\bf k}).

Note that from (
50
) it is obvious that
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)=h^{n-k}\mathfrak{S}_{s;1}(n,k|p,q)
(
[
23
, Corollary 3.6]
). If we define the corresponding
(p,q)
-generalized Bell polynomials by

(51)

\mathfrak{B}^{(s;h)}_{n|p,q}(x):=\sum_{k=0}^{n}\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)x^{k},

this translates into

(52)

\mathfrak{B}^{(s;h)}_{n|p,q}(x)=h^{n}\,\mathfrak{B}^{(s;1)}_{n|p,q}(x/h).

The generalized Stirling numbers
\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(n,k|p,q)
are defined as normal ordering coefficients of the particular word
(YX)^{n}
, see (
46
). Following
[
9
]
, we consider the normal ordering coefficients of arbitrary words.

Definition 3.8
.

Let
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
be variables satisfying the commutation relations (
16
) of the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra
A_{s;h|p,q}
. The
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]
are defined as normal ordering coefficients of the string
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=Y^{n_{r}}X^{m_{r}}\cdots Y^{n_{1}}X^{m_{1}}
, i.e., by

(53)

\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=\sum_{k=m_{1}}^{|{\bf m}|}S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]Y^{|{\bf n}|-(|{\bf m}|-k)(1-s)}Z_{p}^{|{\bf m}|-k}X^{k}.

For the special case
{\bf m}={\bf 1}_{r}=(\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\underbrace{1,1\ldots 1}_{r\ \text{times}})$}
and
{\bf n}={\bf 1}_{r}=(\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\underbrace{1,1\ldots 1}_{r\ \text{times}})$}
we have

(54)

(YX)^{r}=\sum_{k=1}^{r}S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf 1}_{r},{\bf 1}_{r}}[k]Y^{s(r-k)+k}Z_{p}^{r-k}X^{k},

which by comparison with (
46
) shows that
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf 1}_{r},{\bf 1}_{r}}[k]=\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(r,k|p,q)
, for
r\geq 1
. The expression of the following proposition for the numbers
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]
provides an extension of
[
9
, Equation (7)]
(where
p=1
) to arbitrary
p
.

Proposition 3.9
.

The numbers
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]
are given by

(55)

S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]=h^{|{\bf m}|-k}\sum_{k_{1}+k_{2}+\cdots+k_{r-1}=|{\bf m}|-k}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\Psi_{s;p,q}\left[k_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{i}n_{j}+(s-1)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},m_{i+1}\right],

where

\Psi_{s;p,q}[j,k,l,m]=p^{(l-j)m}q^{k(l-j)}{l\choose j}_{p,q^{s-1}}\prod_{\ell=0}^{j-1}[k+\ell(s-1)]_{p,q}.

Proof.

By virtue of Theorem
3.1
, the string
\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}
can be written as

(56)

\displaystyle\mathrm{w}_{{\bf m},{\bf n}}=\sum_{k_{1}=0}^{m_{2}}

\displaystyle\cdots\sum_{k_{r-1}}^{m_{r}}h^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}}\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\Psi_{s;p,q}\left[k_{i},\sum_{j=1}^{i}n_{j}+(s-1)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},m_{i+1}\right]

\displaystyle Y^{|{\bf n}|-(s-1)(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1})}Z_{p}^{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}}X^{|{\bf m}|-(k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1})}.

By comparing the coefficient of
X^{k}
in (
53
) and (
56
), we obtain (
55
).
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

For the special case
{\bf m}={\bf 1}_{r}=(\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\underbrace{1,1\ldots 1}_{r\ \text{times}})$}
and
{\bf n}={\bf 1}_{r}=(\raisebox{0.0pt}[0.0pt][0.0pt]{$\underbrace{1,1\ldots 1}_{r\ \text{times}})$}
we have
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf 1}_{r},{\bf 1}_{r}}[k]=\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(r,k|p,q)
, hence

\mathfrak{S}_{s;h}(r,k|p,q)=h^{r-k}\sum_{k_{1}+\cdots+k_{r-1}=r-k}\quad\prod_{i=1}^{r-1}\Psi_{s;p,q}[k_{i},i+(s-1)\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},\sum_{j=1}^{i-1}k_{j},1].

Generalizing (
51
), we can define the
(p,q)
-generalized Bell polynomials
B_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}(x)
and
(p,q)
-generalized Bell numbers
B_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}
associated to
S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]
in a straightforward fashion,

B_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}(x):=\sum_{k=m_{1}}^{|{\bf m}|}S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k]x^{k},\,\,\,\,B_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}:=B_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}(1)=\sum_{k=m_{1}}^{|{\bf m}|}S_{s,h;p,q}^{{\bf m},{\bf n}}[k].

4.
Conclusion

In this paper, we considered variables
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
satisfying the
(p,q)
-commutation relation
XY-qYX=hf(Y)Z_{p}
with
XZ_{p}=pZ_{p}X
and
Z_{p}Y=pZ_{p}Y
for a polynomial
f
. Due to the variable
Z_{p}
there are three variables which do not commute. However,
Z_{p}
“almost” commutes with
X
and
Y
, so the situation is “close” to the one where only
X
and
Y
exist and satisfy
XY-qYX=hf(Y)
. For most of the results we considered the special case where
f
is a monomial,
f(Y)=Y^{s}
with
s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
. In this case, the algebraic structure generated by
X,Y
and
Z_{p}
satisfying the above commutation relations was called
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra. We presented the normal ordering of arbitrary words in the letters
X,Y,Z_{p}
and related aspects in the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra. In particular, the relation to
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Stirling numbers was discussed in detail.

In a sequel
[
17
]
, we introduce
(p,q)
-rook numbers and give an interpretation of the normal ordering coefficients of words in the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra in terms of these
(p,q)
-rook numbers. In further work
[
18
]
, the binomial formula for
(X+Y)^{n}
is studied in the
(p,q)
-deformed generalized Weyl algebra.
