A priori error analysis of a mass-lumped midpoint finite element method with a structure-preserving solver for the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation with Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction
Agus L. Soenjaya

Abstract
. The Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert (LLG) equation is a fundamental model in micromagnetics for the magnetisation dynamics of ferromagnets at temperatures well below the Curie temperature. In chiral magnetic materials, the bulk Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI) contributes a first-order term to the effective field, induces a natural chiral boundary condition, and plays an important role in the dynamics of skyrmions. In this paper, we analyse a mass-lumped midpoint finite element scheme for the LLG equation with exchange interaction and bulk DMI. The fully discrete scheme preserves the nodal unit-length constraint exactly and satisfies a discrete energy law. Under suitable regularity assumptions on the exact solution, we prove an optimal-order convergence estimate in the energy norm, first order in space and second order in time. To our knowledge, this is the first such a priori error estimate for the mass-lumped midpoint finite element method, even in the exchange-only case. Since the midpoint scheme is nonlinear, its practical implementation requires an algebraic solver that is compatible with the geometric structure of the method. To this end, we propose a structure-preserving fixed-point iteration which preserves the nodal unit-length constraint at every iterate. We further prove that, under an appropriate residual stopping criterion, the resulting inexact scheme retains the same convergence rate as the ideal midpoint scheme, up to the contribution of the solver tolerance. Numerical experiments support the theoretical results and illustrate the robustness and structure-preserving properties of the method.

1.
Introduction

The Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert (LLG) equation
[
17
,
39
]
is a fundamental continuum model for the magnetisation dynamics of ferromagnetic materials at temperatures well below the Curie temperature. It plays a central role in micromagnetics
[
38
]
and underpins many technological applications, including magnetic sensors, spintronic devices, and magnetic recording media
[
36
,
44
,
46
]
. A defining feature of the LLG equation is the pointwise modulus constraint on the magnetisation. This non-convex geometric constraint, together with the strongly nonlinear structure of the equation, makes the design and rigorous analysis of accurate structure-preserving numerical methods a challenging problem in computational micromagnetics
[
45
]
.

In chiral magnetic materials, an important additional mechanism is the Dzyaloshinskii–Moriya interaction (DMI), an antisymmetric exchange interaction arising in systems without inversion symmetry
[
27
,
43
]
. The DMI favours magnetic configurations with a preferred handedness and is a key mechanism behind chiral spin textures such as helices, domain walls, and skyrmions
[
32
]
. These textures are of significant interest in spintronics and magnetic-memory applications, including skyrmion-based information storage and racetrack-type devices
[
32
,
37
,
49
]
.

1.1.
Problem introduction

In this paper, we consider the LLG equation with short-range exchange interaction and bulk DMI. The exchange interaction gives the leading second-order contribution to the effective field
[
31
]
, while the bulk DMI contributes a first-order term and induces the natural chiral boundary condition
[
32
]
. Thus, although the modulus constraint remains unchanged, the DMI modifies the variational structure, boundary behaviour, and energy balance of the model. These features make the construction and analysis of stable and accurate finite element schemes for the LLG equation with DMI particularly delicate.

We now introduce the model considered in this paper. Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, be a bounded domain with outward unit normal vector
\boldsymbol{n}
, and let
T>0
. The dynamics of the magnetisation
\boldsymbol{m}:[0,T]\times\Omega\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
, under the influence of an effective field
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})
and at temperatures far below the Curie temperature, is governed by the Landau–Lifshitz–Gilbert equation:

(1.1a)

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}=\alpha\boldsymbol{m}\times\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}-\gamma\boldsymbol{m}\times\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.1b)

\displaystyle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})=\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\Delta\boldsymbol{m}-2\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\nabla\times\boldsymbol{m}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{in }(0,T)\times\Omega,

(1.1c)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{m}(0,\boldsymbol{x})=\boldsymbol{m}^{0}(\boldsymbol{x})\qquad

\displaystyle\text{for }\boldsymbol{x}\in\Omega,

(1.1d)

\displaystyle\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{m}+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{m}\times\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}\qquad

\displaystyle\text{on }(0,T)\times\partial\Omega.

Here,
\alpha>0
is the Gilbert damping coefficient,
\gamma>0
is the gyromagnetic ratio,
\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}>0
is the exchange stiffness constant, and
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\in\mathbb{R}
measures the strength of the DMI. The vector
\boldsymbol{n}
is the outward-pointing unit normal vector. The initial datum is assumed to satisfy the pointwise constraint
|\boldsymbol{m}^{0}|=1
. The LLG dynamics formally preserves this constraint, so that

(1.2)

|\boldsymbol{m}(t,\boldsymbol{x})|=1\qquad\text{for }(t,\boldsymbol{x})\in[0,T]\times\Omega.

The boundary condition in (
1.1d
) is the natural chiral boundary condition associated with the exchange and bulk DMI energy.

The effective field
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})
in (
1.1b
) is obtained as the negative variational derivative of the micromagnetic energy, which we assume to consist of the exchange energy and the DMI energy:

(1.3)

\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m})=\mathcal{E}_{\rm{exc}}(\boldsymbol{m})+\mathcal{E}_{\rm{DMI}}(\boldsymbol{m}):=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{m},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{m}\right\rangle.

Indeed, under the natural boundary condition (
1.1d
), one has

\frac{\mathrm{d}}{\mathrm{d}t}\mathcal{E}\big(\boldsymbol{m}(t)\big)=-\left\langle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m}),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}\right\rangle.

The energy-dissipation structure of the LLG equation is then obtained by testing
(
1.1a
) with
\boldsymbol{m}\times\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}
. Since

|\boldsymbol{m}|=1
implies
\boldsymbol{m}\cdot\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}=0
, we obtain

\gamma\left\langle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m}),\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}\right\rangle=\alpha\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

Consequently, sufficiently smooth solutions formally satisfy the energy identity

(1.4)

\mathcal{E}\big(\boldsymbol{m}(t)\big)+\frac{\alpha}{\gamma}\int_{0}^{t}\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(s)}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\,\mathrm{d}s=\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m}^{0}),\qquad\forall t\in[0,T].

Although additional lower-order contributions, such as applied fields, uniaxial anisotropy, or magnetostatic effects, can also be incorporated into the effective field, we restrict the analysis in this paper to the exchange and bulk DMI contributions. This choice allows us to focus on the main analytical difficulties associated with the higher-order terms.

1.2.
Literature review

We next review existing analytical and numerical results in the literature that are relevant to the present paper.

The analytical theory of the LLG equation is by now rather well developed, especially for effective fields consisting of the exchange contribution. The seminal works
[
47
,
9
]
established the existence of weak solutions and revealed the possible nonuniqueness of such solutions. Existence and uniqueness results for more regular solutions were subsequently obtained in
[
18
]
. For sufficiently small initial data, global existence of arbitrarily smooth solutions to the LLG equation with natural boundary conditions was proved in
[
30
]
. More recently, the weak-strong uniqueness principle was established in
[
23
]
: any weak solution coincides with a strong solution as long as both solutions emanate from the same initial datum and coexist on the same time interval. Compared with the exchange-only model, the inclusion of the bulk DMI introduces a first-order chiral contribution to the effective field and changes the natural boundary condition from the homogeneous Neumann condition to the chiral boundary condition in (
1.1d
). Existence of weak solutions in this case was shown in
[
34
]
. Mathematical analysis of micromagnetic models involving DMI, particularly in thin-film regimes, has been studied in
[
22
,
35
]
.

From the numerical point of view, a central difficulty in the approximation of the LLG equation is the preservation of the pointwise modulus constraint (
1.2
), while retaining a discrete counterpart of the energy law (
1.4
). Since the present paper is concerned with a finite element approximation, we first recall the main finite element strategies developed for the LLG equation and highlight their treatment of the length constraint, energy stability, and convergence. In the following,
h
and
k
denote the spatial mesh size and time-step size, respectively, and
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}
denotes the finite element approximation of
\boldsymbol{m}
at time level
t_{j}
.

(1)
Tangent-plane schemes with sphere projection
[
6
,
7
,
40
]
.

In this class of methods, one first computes an approximation
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{j+1}
of
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}
in the discrete tangent space at
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}
. An intermediate value

\widehat{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{j+1}=\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}+k\boldsymbol{v}_{h}^{j+1}
is then normalised nodally to produce
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j+1}
. These schemes are attractive because they lead to linear systems at each time step and enforce the nodal unit-length constraint after normalisation. Their stability analysis is typically based on a discrete energy inequality, but the proof may require geometric conditions on the mesh
[
8
]
. Moreover, the treatment of additional first-order contributions such as the DMI may introduce further conditional stability requirements
[
34
]
. Convergence to weak solutions has been proved in
[
6
,
8
]
; see also
[
40
]
for coupling with the quasi-static Maxwell equations. Error estimates under sufficient regularity assumptions have been obtained more recently in
[
11
]
. A formally almost second-order modification of this approach was proposed in
[
25
]
.

(2)
Projection-free tangent-plane schemes
[
1
,
13
]
.

These schemes modify the tangent-plane approach by avoiding the nodal normalisation step. This removes the mesh restrictions associated with the projection and leads to a simpler stability mechanism. The price is that the unit-length constraint is no longer imposed exactly at the nodes; instead, it is recovered only asymptotically as the discretisation parameters tend to zero. Convergence to weak solutions has been proved in
[
1
,
13
]
, while a priori error estimates were established in
[
29
,
14
]
. Higher-order and BDF-type variants, including convergence results and error estimates, have been developed in
[
2
]
and more recently in
[
3
,
4
]
.

(3)
Mass-lumped midpoint schemes
[
15
,
20
]
.

The mass-lumped midpoint method is a formally second-order-in-time implicit geometric scheme in which the mass-lumped inner product is used to enforce the unit-length constraint at the nodes. A key advantage of this method is that it preserves the nodal modulus constraint exactly and satisfies a discrete energy law without imposing geometric restrictions on the mesh. Its main drawback is that the resulting algebraic problem is nonlinear, so that a suitable structure-preserving nonlinear solver is required at each time step
[
15
,
20
]
. Convergence to weak solutions was proved in
[
15
]
, while the effect of inexact nonlinear solvers, at the level of subsequential weak convergence, has been investigated in
[
20
,
24
]
. To the best of our knowledge, however, a priori error estimates for the mass-lumped midpoint finite element scheme are not available even for the exchange-only effective field, and no rate estimates are known when solver errors are included.

(4)
Other schemes for the LLG equation.

Several other discretisations of the LLG equation have also been proposed. Linearly implicit finite element schemes based on alternative formulations of the equation were studied, for instance, in
[
10
,
19
,
33
]
. These methods are computationally attractive, but the unit-length constraint is typically satisfied only asymptotically, and they may not preserve the energy structure. Higher-order linearly implicit BDF methods were analysed in
[
2
,
48
]
; these schemes provide high-order accuracy and rigorous error estimates under suitable regularity assumptions, but again do not impose the unit-length constraint exactly at the nodes. A generalised SAV-type method preserving the length constraint was recently proposed in
[
42
]
; however, the dissipated quantity is a modified energy, and the analysis in that work is carried out at the time-semidiscrete level. Its extension to a fully discrete finite element scheme retaining the same favourable structural properties appears to be nontrivial.

(5)
Numerical methods for LLG with DMI.

Numerical methods for the LLG equation with DMI have also been considered, although rigorous finite element error estimates in this setting remain scarce. A finite difference scheme for the bulk-DMI model was proposed in
[
41
]
. In the finite element context, convergent tangent-plane integrators for chiral ferromagnets were analysed in
[
34
]
, while the mass-lumped midpoint method with DMI was investigated computationally in
[
24
]
. The latter work also highlights the suitability of the midpoint scheme for energy-sensitive skyrmion dynamics, providing further motivation to study this scheme in the presence of DMI. Nevertheless, a priori error estimates for the mass-lumped midpoint finite element approximation of the LLG equation with bulk DMI appear to be unavailable in the literature.

1.3.
Contributions of the paper

The goal of this paper is to provide a quantitative error analysis for the mass-lumped midpoint finite element approximation of the LLG equation with bulk DMI. This complements the existing convergence theory for structure-preserving LLG discretisations
[
15
,
34
]
, where the mass-lumped midpoint method is known to preserve the nodal length constraint and to satisfy a discrete energy law, but where a priori convergence rates appear to be unavailable even for the exchange-only model.

The first contribution of this paper is the formulation and analysis of a fully discrete mass-lumped midpoint finite element scheme for the LLG equation with exchange and bulk DMI under the natural chiral boundary condition. The DMI term is incorporated through the discrete exchange–DMI energy, so that the resulting scheme is compatible with the chiral boundary condition at the variational level. We prove that the scheme preserves the nodal unit-length constraint exactly and satisfies a discrete counterpart of the continuous energy identity. The stability estimates derived from this structure provide the foundation for the subsequent error analysis.

The second and main contribution is an a priori error estimate for the ideal nonlinear midpoint scheme. Under suitable regularity assumptions on the exact solution, we prove that the fully discrete approximation converges with the rate

\max_{0\leq j\leq J}\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}(t_{j})-\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left(k\sum_{j=0}^{J-1}\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{j+1/2})-d_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq C(h+k^{2}),

up to the precise form stated in Theorem
3.7
. The proof combines the geometric cancellations of the midpoint method and careful estimates of the first-order DMI contribution and the chiral boundary condition. A notable feature of the ideal scheme is that its stability and convergence analysis does not require a mesh-dependent time-step restriction. This is in contrast to some existing structure-preserving schemes, where stability and convergence are obtained under additional assumptions such as an angle condition on the triangulation and a CFL-type condition; see, for example,
[
34
]
.

The third contribution concerns the practical solution of the nonlinear algebraic system arising at each time step. We propose a structure-preserving fixed-point iteration for the midpoint scheme; see Algorithm
4.1
. Unlike a generic nonlinear solver, the proposed iteration preserves the nodal unit-length constraint at every iterate and satisfies an inexact discrete energy law, with an additional defect term measuring the solver error. This makes the iteration compatible with the geometric and dissipative structure of the underlying midpoint scheme.

Finally, we prove an
a priori
error estimate for the fully discrete method with the solver error taken into account. More precisely, under a certain CFL-type condition, we show that if the fixed-point iteration is terminated according to a suitable residual criterion, then the numerical solution produced by the inexact solver retains the same convergence rate as the exact nonlinear midpoint solution up to the contribution of the prescribed solver tolerance; see Theorem
4.8
. This result provides a rigorous justification of the practical algorithm and shows that the structure-preserving nonlinear iteration does not destroy the stability and accuracy properties of the midpoint discretisation. These theoretical findings
are supported by numerical experiments illustrating convergence, constraint
preservation, energy dissipation, and the qualitative effect of the DMI-induced
chiral boundary condition; see Section
5
.

2.
Preliminaries

In this section, we collect the notation and finite element tools used throughout
the paper.

2.1.
Notations

Let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
,
d=1,2,3
, be an open and bounded convex
polytopal domain. For
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\geq 0
, we write

\mathbb{L}^{p}:=\mathbb{L}^{p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\qquad\mathbb{W}^{s,p}:=\mathbb{W}^{s,p}(\Omega;\mathbb{R}^{3}).

We also set

\mathbb{H}^{s}:=\mathbb{W}^{s,2},\qquad\mathbb{W}^{0,p}:=\mathbb{L}^{p}.

The differential operators
\nabla
and
\Delta
are understood componentwise when acting on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
-valued functions. For the curl operator, we use the standard
three-dimensional curl when
d=3
. In two space dimensions, for

\boldsymbol{v}=(v_{1},v_{2},v_{3})
, we use the convention

\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v}=\left(\partial_{2}v_{3},\,-\partial_{1}v_{3},\,\partial_{1}v_{2}-\partial_{2}v_{1}\right)^{\top},

and in one space dimension,

\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v}=\left(0,\,-\partial_{1}v_{3},\,\partial_{1}v_{2}\right)^{\top}.

The outward unit normal is embedded in
\mathbb{R}^{3}
in the natural way in the
cases
d=1,2
.

If
X
is a Banach space, then
L^{p}(0,T;X)
and
W^{s,p}(0,T;X)
denote the
usual Lebesgue–Bochner and Sobolev–Bochner spaces of functions from
(0,T)

into
X
. Throughout the paper, the scalar product in a Hilbert space
H
is
denoted by
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle_{H}
, with associated norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{H}
.
When
H=\mathbb{L}^{2}
, we simply write

\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle:=\int_{\Omega}\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{v}\,d\boldsymbol{x},\qquad\left\|{\boldsymbol{u}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}:=\sqrt{\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{u}\right\rangle}.

We use the same notation
\left\langle\cdot,\cdot\right\rangle
for the
\mathbb{L}^{2}
inner
product of vector-valued and matrix-valued functions; the meaning will always
be clear from the context.

Finally,
C
denotes a generic positive constant which may take different values
at different occurrences, but is independent of the discretisation parameters

h
and
k
. When the dependence on a parameter is relevant, it is indicated
explicitly, for example by writing
C(T)
.

2.2.
Finite element approximation

Let
\{\mathcal{T}_{h}\}_{h>0}
be a family of shape-regular and quasi-uniform triangulations of
\Omega
with maximal mesh-size
h>0
. Denote by
\mathcal{N}_{h}
the set of vertices of
\mathcal{T}_{h}
. We use the lowest-order conforming Lagrange finite element space

(2.1)

\mathbb{V}_{h}:=\left\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3}):\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{1}(K;\mathbb{R}^{3}),\;\,\forall K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}\right\}\subset\mathbb{H}^{1}.

Let
\{\varphi_{z}\}_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}
be the scalar nodal basis of the
underlying scalar
P_{1}
finite element space, and we denote by
I_{h}:C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3})\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
the nodal interpolation operator. For continuous vector fields
\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3})
, we define the reduced-integration, or mass-lumped, inner product by

(2.2)

\left\langle\boldsymbol{u},\boldsymbol{v}\right\rangle_{h}:=\int_{\Omega}I_{h}(\boldsymbol{u}\cdot\boldsymbol{v})\,\mathrm{d}x=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\beta_{z}\,\boldsymbol{u}(z)\cdot\boldsymbol{v}(z),\quad\text{where }\;\beta_{z}:=\int_{\Omega}\varphi_{z}\,\mathrm{d}x.

We write

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{h}^{2}:=\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

The norm
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{h}
is uniformly equivalent to the standard
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-norm on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
, namely

(2.3)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq 5\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h};

see
[
12
, Lemma 3.9]
.

First, we recall the following standard approximation and inverse estimates
[
16
,
28
]
. There is a constant
C
independent of
h
, such that for every
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
,

(2.4)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{2}}.

More generally, if
p\in(d,\infty)
, then

(2.5)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,p}}.

For every
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,
s\in\{0,1\}
, and
1\leq q\leq p\leq\infty
, the inverse estimate holds:

(2.6)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}}\leq Ch^{-d\left(\frac{1}{q}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,q}}.

Furthermore, we shall make use of the following quadrature estimate
[
15
]
: for all
\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(2.7)

\big|\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle\big|\leq Ch\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}.

We also need a stability estimate for the nodal interpolant applied to piecewise smooth functions
[
14
]
. If
\boldsymbol{v}\in C^{0}(\overline{\Omega};\mathbb{R}^{3})
and
\boldsymbol{v}|_{K}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}(K)
for every
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, then

(2.8)

\left\|{I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+Ch\left\|{\mathrm{D}_{h}^{2}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}},

where
\mathrm{D}_{h}^{2}
denotes the elementwise Hessian.

Let
P_{h}:\mathbb{L}^{2}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
denote the usual
\mathbb{L}^{2}
-orthogonal projection onto
\mathbb{V}_{h}
,
defined by

(2.9)

\left\langle P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle=0,\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

On quasi-uniform triangulations,
P_{h}
extends to a bounded operator on

\mathbb{L}^{p}
and is stable in
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}

[
21
,
26
]
; more precisely, for any
p\in[1,\infty]
and
s\in\{0,1\}
, there exists a constant
C
independent
of
h
such that

(2.10)

\left\|{P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{s,p}}.

Moreover, for any
p\in(1,\infty)
and
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{W}^{2,p}
,

(2.11)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}+h\left\|{\nabla(\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{p}}\leq Ch^{2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{2,p}}.

We now introduce the discrete effective field operator corresponding to the effective field
\mathcal{H}
in (
1.1b
).
Define the bilinear form
\mathfrak{a}:\mathbb{H}^{1}\times\mathbb{H}^{1}\to\mathbb{R}
by

(2.12)

\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}):=\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{v},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle.

If
\boldsymbol{v}
is sufficiently smooth and satisfies the chiral boundary condition

(2.13)

\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\partial_{\boldsymbol{n}}\boldsymbol{v}+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\boldsymbol{v}\times\boldsymbol{n}=\boldsymbol{0}\qquad\text{on }\partial\Omega,

then integration by parts gives

(2.14)

\left\langle\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{\chi}\right\rangle=-\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}),\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}.

The form
\mathfrak{a}
given by (
2.12
) is symmetric and continuous on
\mathbb{H}^{1}\times\mathbb{H}^{1}
. For fixed
\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}>0
and
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\in\mathbb{R}
, the form
\mathfrak{a}
satisfies the Gårding inequality: there exist constants
C_{0},C_{1}>0
such that

(2.15)

\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{v})+C_{0}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}\geq C_{1}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{1}.

To mimic (
2.14
) at the discrete level, we define the (unshifted) discrete effective field operator
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
by

(2.16)

\left\langle\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}),\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

In the pure exchange case
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0
, this reduces to
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}=\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
, where
\Delta_{h}:\mathbb{V}_{h}\to\mathbb{V}_{h}
is the discrete Laplacian
[
15
]
defined by

\left\langle\Delta_{h}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=-\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle,\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Since
\mathfrak{a}
is only Gårding coercive in the sense of (
2.15
), to simplify our analysis we introduce a fixed shift parameter
\lambda>0
. We choose
\lambda
sufficiently large such that the shifted discrete bilinear form

(2.17)

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}):=\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})+\lambda\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}

is uniformly coercive on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
, namely

(2.18)

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\geq c_{\lambda}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Indeed, (
2.15
) and the norm equivalence
(
2.3
) imply

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h})\geq C_{1}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+(\lambda-C_{0})\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2},

so (
2.18
) follows by choosing
\lambda\geq C_{0}
.

Moreover, we define the shifted continuous and discrete effective fields by

(2.19)

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}):=\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{v})-\lambda\boldsymbol{v},\qquad\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}:=\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}-\lambda\boldsymbol{v}_{h}.

Then, by (
2.16
),

(2.20)

\left\langle\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}),\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

For
\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{H}^{2}
satisfying (
2.13
), we define the elliptic projection
R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
by

(2.21)

\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}=P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}).

Equivalently, since (
2.21
) is an equality in
\mathbb{V}_{h}
represented through the mass-lumped inner product,

(2.22)

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(R_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})=-\left\langle P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

By (
2.18
), this elliptic projection is well-defined for every admissible
\boldsymbol{v}
.

Remark 2.1
.

Replacing
\mathcal{H}
by
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
does not change the LLG equation at the continuous level, since
\boldsymbol{m}\times\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m})=\boldsymbol{m}\times\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})
. Similarly, replacing
\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}
by
\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
at the discrete level will also not change the ideal midpoint scheme to be defined later.

Other technical estimates used in the
a priori
error analysis in the next section are proved in Appendix
A
. They concern the consistency of the midpoint approximation, projection errors, and bounds related to the mass-lumped inner product and the discrete effective field. We will refer to these results when needed in order to keep the main argument focused.

3.
The ideal midpoint scheme and its error analysis

Fix
N\in\mathbb{N}
and let
\mathbb{V}_{h}
be the finite element space defined in (
2.1
). Let
k:=T/N
be the time step size and write
t_{i}:=ik
for
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N\}
. For a sequence
\{\boldsymbol{v}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
, we write

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{v}^{i+1}:=\frac{\boldsymbol{v}^{i+1}-\boldsymbol{v}^{i}}{k},\qquad\overline{\boldsymbol{v}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{v}^{i+1}+\boldsymbol{v}^{i}).

For the exact solution, we use the notation

\boldsymbol{m}^{i}:=\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i}),\qquad\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\qquad\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}),

and

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}+\boldsymbol{m}^{i}).

We first present the fully implicit midpoint scheme in its ideal form, in which the nonlinear algebraic system arising at each time step is assumed to be solved exactly. This idealised scheme is the object of the stability and error analysis in the next sections, and it also serves as the reference scheme for the inexact solver considered later.

Algorithm 3.1

(Ideal fully implicit midpoint scheme)
.

Set
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0}:=I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}
.

For

i=0
to
N-1
, given
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,
do
:

Find
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that, for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(3.1)

\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

Output
: a sequence of approximations
\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
.

Since we have

(3.2)

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h}^{0}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

the scheme (
3.1
) is identical to the unshifted midpoint scheme (
\lambda=0
). When
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0
, this scheme was considered in
[
15
]
, where convergence towards a weak solution (along a subsequence and without rate) was shown. This scheme is well-posed by the same argument as in
[
15
,
24
]
.

For fixed
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}
, the scheme (
3.1
) is a finite-dimensional nonlinear algebraic system for the unknown
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}
. The nonlinearity comes from the precession term

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

where both factors depend on the unknown midpoint value. The Gilbert term is comparatively simpler, since

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}=\frac{1}{k}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}.

Thus, at each time step, one has to solve a nonlinear system, for instance by fixed-point iteration, Newton’s method, or variants thereof; see, e.g.,
[
15
,
24
]
for detailed discussions. We now study the idealised scheme (
3.1
), assuming that this nonlinear system is solved exactly. The effect of an inexact nonlinear solve is addressed later in Section
4
, where we propose a structure-preserving fixed-point iteration and incorporate the resulting solver error into the analysis.

The following regularity assumption is made to accommodate our
a priori
error analysis.

Assumption 3.2

(Regularity of the exact solution)
.

Let
p>\max\{d,2\}
be fixed such that

d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\leq 1.

In particular, when
d=3
we take
p\in(3,6]
. We assume that the exact solution
\boldsymbol{m}
of (
1.1
) satisfies

(3.3)

\boldsymbol{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4}\cap\mathbb{W}^{3,p})\cap W^{1,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2}\cap\mathbb{W}^{1,p})\cap W^{2,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{3})\cap W^{3,\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{1}).

Moreover,
\boldsymbol{m}(t)
satisfies the chiral boundary condition (
1.1d
)
for every
t\in[0,T]
in the sense required for the elliptic projection
R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}(t)
.

The existence of arbitrarily regular solutions to the LLG equation, at least for sufficiently small initial data and with
\mathcal{H}(\boldsymbol{m})
consisting of only the exchange field, was shown in
[
30
]
.

Remark 3.3
.

Assumption
3.2
is a convenient sufficient condition and is not intended to be minimal. The requirement
\boldsymbol{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{4})

ensures that
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{H}^{2})
,
which is used to estimate the projection defect
P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m})-\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m})
by (
2.11
).

The requirement
\boldsymbol{m}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{3,p})

ensures that

\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m})\in L^{\infty}(0,T;\mathbb{W}^{1,p})
,
which is needed for the bound

\left\|{P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}\leq C.

The regularity of
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}
gives (
A.10
),
while the assumptions on
\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}
and
\partial_{ttt}\boldsymbol{m}
give the midpoint consistency estimates (
A.9
), to be proven in Lemma
A.3
.
These estimates are essential for our
a priori
error analysis.

At each time step, the solution of (
3.1
) belongs to the set

(3.4)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{h}:=\{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}:\left|{\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}}\right|=1,\quad\forall\boldsymbol{z}\in\mathcal{N}_{h}\},

a property which we refer to as the nodal length preservation.
The following lemma proves this property and the energy stability of the scheme.

Lemma 3.4

(Nodal length preservation and energy stability)
.

Suppose that
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}
solves (
3.1
). Then for any
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
,

(3.5)

|\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}(z)|=|\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}(z)|,\qquad\forall z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}.

Equivalently,
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
for all
i
if
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
Moreover, we have the discrete energy stability

(3.6)

\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})\right]+k\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}=0.

Consequently, it holds that

(3.7)

\max_{0\leq j\leq N}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}^{2}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\right)+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C,

where the constant
C
may depend on
\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}
, but is independent of
n
,
h
, or
k
.

Proof.

Since the test function in (
3.1
) is arbitrary and the mass-lumped inner product is nodal, the scheme is equivalent to a system of nodal equations. Testing the nodal equation at
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
with

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}(z)
gives

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}(z)\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}(z)=0,

since each cross-product term is orthogonal to

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}(z)
. Therefore

|\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}(z)|^{2}-|\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}(z)|^{2}=2k\,\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}(z)\cdot\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}(z)=0,

which proves (
3.5
).

We now prove (
3.6
). Taking

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}
in (
3.1
) gives

(3.8)

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

Next, taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}

in (
3.1
), we obtain

(3.9)

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

By adding (
3.8
) and (
3.9
), the field-cross term cancels. We note that by (
2.20
),

\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}\right).

Furthermore, since
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
is symmetric,

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}\right)=\frac{1}{2k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})\right].

This proves (
3.6
).

Finally, since
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}
is piecewise affine, it is a convex combination of its nodal values on each element. Hence, (
3.5
), together with the fact that
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0}=I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}
and
|\boldsymbol{m}^{0}|=1
, implies
\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1
. Bounds on the rest of the terms in (
3.7
) follow from (
3.6
) and the coercivity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
.
∎

We next derive the consistency equation. Define

(3.10)

\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}:=\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

and

(3.11)

\boldsymbol{E}_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})-\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}).

By Lemmas
A.1
and
A.3
, as well as the approximation properties of
P_{h}
in (
2.11
), we have

(3.12)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{E}_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C(h+k^{2}).

Furthermore, Lemma
A.2
and the stability of
P_{h}
in (
2.10
) imply

(3.13)

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}+\left\|{P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}\leq C.

These estimates will be repeatedly used in the sequel.

Next, we record in the following lemma the projected exact equation and a consistency estimate.

Lemma 3.5

(Projected exact equation and consistency estimate)
.

For each
i\in\{0,1,\ldots,N-1\}
, the projected exact solution satisfies

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

(3.14)

\displaystyle\qquad+\gamma\left\langle R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, where the consistency residual

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-I_{h}\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\quad-\alpha I_{h}\left(R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right)

(3.15)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma I_{h}\left(R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})\right).

Moreover, we have the estimate

(3.16)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C(h+k^{2}).

Proof.

The exact solution satisfies the pointwise identity

\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\alpha\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\gamma\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})=\boldsymbol{0}.

Applying
I_{h}
and testing with
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
in the mass-lumped product gives

(3.17)

\displaystyle\left\langle I_{h}\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle I_{h}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle I_{h}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

Subtracting (
3.17
) from the left-hand side of (
3.14
), and using (
2.21
) gives precisely the identity (
3.15
).

It remains to prove (
3.16
). From (
3.15
), by adding and subtracting the relevant terms, we can write

\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}=\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0,h}^{i+1}-\alpha\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1,h}^{i+1}+\gamma\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2,h}^{i+1},

where, using the notations in (
3.10
) and (
3.11
),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0,h}^{i+1}

\displaystyle:=\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}+\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-I_{h}\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1,h}^{i+1}

\displaystyle:=I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}+\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}\right),

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2,h}^{i+1}

\displaystyle:=I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})+\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{E}_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

For the term
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0,h}^{i+1}
, we have by (
3.12
) and (
2.4
),

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{0,h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq\left\|{\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left\|{\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-I_{h}\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C(h+k^{2}).

Next, with the aim of estimating
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1,h}^{i+1}
and
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2,h}^{i+1}
, we set

\displaystyle\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle:=R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\delta_{t}^{i+1}

\displaystyle:=\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-I_{h}\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

\displaystyle\delta_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle:=P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})-I_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}).

Then
\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\delta_{t}^{i+1},\delta_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
and, by
(
3.12
) and the interpolation estimates (
2.4
) and (
2.11
),

(3.18)

\left\|{\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left\|{\delta_{t}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}+\left\|{\delta_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C(h+k^{2}).

Moreover, thanks to the fact that
I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}
vanishes at all nodes for any sufficiently smooth
\boldsymbol{v}
, we observe that

I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}\right)=I_{h}\left(\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}\right),

I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{E}_{t}^{i+1}\right)=I_{h}\left(I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\delta_{t}^{i+1}\right),

and similarly

I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{E}_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})\right)=I_{h}\left(\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})\right),

I_{h}\left(\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{E}_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right)=I_{h}\left(I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\delta_{H}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right).

Therefore, by Lemma
A.4
and (
3.18
), as well as the bound
(
3.13
) and the estimate (
2.5
), we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{1,h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\delta_{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}+C\left\|{\delta_{t}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\left\|{I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}

\displaystyle\leq C(h+k^{2}).

Similar estimate also holds for
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{2,h}^{i+1}
.
Hence, (
3.16
) follows.
∎

In the error analysis, it is convenient to compare the numerical solution not directly with
\boldsymbol{m}^{i}
, but with its elliptic projection
R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i}
. Thus we introduce
the discrete errors:

(3.19)

\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i}:=\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i},\qquad\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\qquad\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}:=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}-\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}.

Notice that
\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i}
is not the full error. Instead,

(3.20)

\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}=\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i}+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}),

where the second term is controlled by the elliptic projection estimate in
Lemma
A.1
. Hence it remains to estimate
\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i}
in a
suitable energy norm.

We now record the discrete error equation in the following lemma, obtained by subtracting the projected exact equation
(
3.14
) from the numerical scheme
(
3.1
). Its particular form is chosen so that, when tested
with
\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}
and

-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}
,
the leading precession terms cancel and the increment of

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})
appears.

Lemma 3.6

(Discrete error equation)
.

For every
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, the discrete error defined in (
3.19
) satisfies

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

(3.21)

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h},

where
\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}
is the consistency residual term given in (
3.15
).

Proof.

We subtract the projected exact equation (
3.14
) from the numerical scheme (
3.1
). For the damping term, using

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},

we have

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}-R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}+\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}.

Similarly, using the linearity of
\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
and the defining identity (
2.21
), namely

\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),

we get

\displaystyle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}).

Inserting these two identities into the subtracted equation gives (
3.21
).
∎

We are now in a position to prove an error estimate in the energy norm for the ideal scheme given by Algorithm
3.1
.

Theorem 3.7

(Energy-norm error estimate for the ideal scheme)
.

Let Assumption
3.2
hold, and let

\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be a sequence satisfying the ideal midpoint scheme (Algorithm
3.1
). Then, for
k>0
sufficiently small,

(3.22)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+(h+k^{2})^{2}\right).

Consequently, we have an
a priori
error estimate

(3.23)

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C\left(h+k^{2}\right),

where
C
is a constant depending on
T
, but is independent of
N
,
h
, and
k
.

Proof.

Fix
i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
. Taking

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}
in the discrete error equation
(
3.21
) gives

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}

(3.24)

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}.

Next, taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}
in (
3.21
), we obtain

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}

(3.25)

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha}\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}.

Adding (
3.24
) and
(
3.25
), we observe that the terms containing
\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}
cancel.
Furthermore, note that by (
2.20
),

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right)

(3.26)

\displaystyle=\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right].

Therefore, after adding (
3.24
) and
(
3.25
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right]

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}-\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle\quad-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha}\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}+\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}

(3.27)

\displaystyle=:J_{1}+J_{2}+\ldots+J_{6}.

We now estimate each term on the right-hand side of (
3.27
). Firstly, by Hölder’s inequality, the Sobolev embedding
\mathbb{W}^{1,p}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{L}^{\infty}
,
the bound (
3.13
), the norm equivalence (
2.3
), and Young’s inequality, we obtain for any
\varepsilon>0
,

\displaystyle|J_{1}|+|J_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\alpha\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}+\gamma\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})}\right\|_{h}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}+C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}\left\|{P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}

(3.28)

\displaystyle\leq\varepsilon\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{2}}^{2}.

For the term
J_{3}
, by (
3.16
) and Young’s inequality,

(3.29)

|J_{3}|\leq\varepsilon\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq\varepsilon\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+C_{\varepsilon}(h+k^{2})^{2}.

To estimate the terms
J_{4}
and
J_{5}
,
we note the following identity. Since

\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=\left\langle I_{h}\Big(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\Big),\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h},

we have by the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
in (
2.20
),

\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},I_{h}\Big(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\Big)\right).

Applying this identity with
\boldsymbol{z}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}
and

\boldsymbol{z}_{h}=P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})
, then using the discrete product estimate (
A.12
), we obtain

(3.30)

\displaystyle|J_{4}|+|J_{5}|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\left(\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}+\left\|{P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}})}\right\|_{\mathbb{W}^{1,p}}\right)\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Finally, noting that

\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}\right),

we have by (
3.16
),

(3.31)

|J_{6}|\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C(h+k^{2})^{2}.

Now, we substitute these estimates back into (
3.27
).
Choosing
\varepsilon>0
sufficiently small in
(
3
) and (
3.29
) and rearranging the terms, we obtain

(3.32)

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ck(h+k^{2})^{2}.

By the coercivity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
, we have the estimate

\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq C\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})+\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right].

Therefore, (
3.32
) implies

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right]

(3.33)

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})+\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right]+Ck(h+k^{2})^{2}.

For
k>0
sufficiently small, the term

Ck\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})

on the right-hand side can be absorbed into the left-hand side. This yields

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+c\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})\leq(1+Ck)\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})+Ck(h+k^{2})^{2},

where
c>0
is independent of
h
and
k
. In particular, we have

(3.34)

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\leq Ck\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})+Ck(h+k^{2})^{2}.

Applying the discrete Gronwall lemma to (
3.34
)
yields

(3.35)

\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\leq C\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0})+(h+k^{2})^{2}\right].

To estimate the accumulated time-difference term, we return to
(
3.33
) and sum it over
i=0,1,\ldots,j-1
. The energy
increments telescope, and thus by applying (
3.35
) and
jk\leq T
,

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0})\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1})+\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i})\right]+Cjk(h+k^{2})^{2}

(3.36)

\displaystyle\leq C\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0})+(h+k^{2})^{2}\right].

Dropping the nonnegative term

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{j})
from the left-hand side of
(
3
), we conclude that

(3.37)

k\sum_{i=0}^{j-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0},\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0})+(h+k^{2})^{2}\right].

Combining (
3.35
) and
(
3.37
), and again utilising the equivalence between

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
and
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}
on

\mathbb{V}_{h}
, proves (
3.22
).

Finally, recall that we set
\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0}=I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}
. By the approximation properties of
I_{h}
and
R_{h}
in (
2.4
) and (
A.1
), we have

(3.38)

\displaystyle\left\|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}^{0}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}^{0}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq Ch^{2}.

This, together with (
3.22
), the error decomposition (
3.20
), and (
A.1
), yields (
3.23
).
∎

4.
The inexact midpoint scheme and its error analysis

At each time step, the ideal midpoint scheme (
3.1
) requires the solution of a nonlinear algebraic system. A generic nonlinear solver need not preserve the geometric structure or the energy stability inherited from the midpoint discretisation. We therefore introduce in Algorithm
4.1
a constraint-preserving fixed-point iteration, formulated in terms of the midpoint unknown

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}+\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}),

which is designed to preserve the nodal unit-length constraint and to satisfy an inexact discrete energy law.

Since

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}=\frac{2}{k}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\right),

the exact midpoint scheme (
3.1
) is equivalent to finding

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that, for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(4.1)

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\frac{\gamma k}{2}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=\left\langle\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

After that, we set

\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i+1}=2\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}.

Inspired by
[
20
,
24
]
, we now define a practical inexact solver to solve (
4.1
) in the following algorithm. Recall that
\mathcal{M}_{h}
is the set defined by (
3.4
).

Algorithm 4.1

(Constraint-preserving inexact midpoint solver)
.

Set
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0}:=\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0}=I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}
.

For

i=0
to
N-1
, given
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
and a prescribed tolerance
\varepsilon_{i+1}>0
,
do
:

(1)

Set

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,0}:=\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}.

(2)

For
\ell=0,1,2,\ldots
, given

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, compute

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
such that, for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(4.2)

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\frac{\gamma k}{2}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=\left\langle\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

(3)

Stop at the first index
\ell_{\ast}\geq 0
satisfying

(4.3)

\left\|{I_{h}\left[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}}\right)\right]}\right\|_{h}\leq\varepsilon_{i+1}.

(4)

Define

(4.4)

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1},\qquad\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}:=2\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}.

Output
: a sequence of approximations
\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\}_{1\leq i\leq N}
.

For the analysis, we also define the defect quantities:

(4.5)

\boldsymbol{r}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}:=\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}}\right),\qquad\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}:=I_{h}\left[\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{r}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right].

By the stopping criterion (
4.3
) and the
definition (
4.4
), we immediately have

(4.6)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq\varepsilon_{i+1}.

Remark 4.2
.

The shifted operator
\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
is still used here to align the
fixed-point solver with the shifted formulation used in the error analysis in
Section
3
. The exact midpoint scheme is unchanged by
this shift due to (
3.2
).

At the level of the inexact inner iteration, however, the shift is not
invisible. Indeed,
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell+1}\times\lambda\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell}

does not vanish in general, since
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell+1}
and

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell}
need not coincide. Thus the value of
\lambda
changes the
algebraic path of the fixed-point iteration and enters the defect term in the
inexact discrete energy law through

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell_{\ast}+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell_{\ast}+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}^{\ell_{\ast}}\right).

In particular, the constants in the contraction and error estimates may
depend on the fixed shift parameter
\lambda
, but remain independent of

h
and
k
.

We first verify that the inner iteration is well-defined and that the update
preserves the nodal unit-length constraint exactly. This is the key structural
advantage of the iteration defined by Algorithm
4.1
.

Lemma 4.3

(Well-posedness and nodal constraint of the inner iteration)
.

Assume that
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
.
Then, for every
\ell\geq 0
, the linear problem
(
4.2
) admits a unique solution

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
. Moreover,

(4.7)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{L}^{\infty}}\leq 1.

If the iteration is stopped according to
(
4.3
), then the update
(
4.4
) satisfies
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
.

Proof.

For fixed
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}
, the left-hand side of
(
4.2
) defines a bilinear form in

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}
and
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}
. Taking

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}
, the cross-product terms vanish
nodally.
Therefore, the bilinear form is coercive with respect to
\left\|{\cdot}\right\|_{h}
,
and the Lax–Milgram theorem yields existence and uniqueness.

Next, let
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
. Testing
(
4.2
) with
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\varphi_{z}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}(z)
gives

\beta_{z}|\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}(z)|^{2}=\beta_{z}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z)\cdot\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}(z),

Noting that
|\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z)|=1
, we obtain
\left|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}(z)}\right|\leq 1
for all
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}
is affine on each element,
(
4.7
) follows.

It remains to prove that the update belongs to
\mathcal{M}_{h}
. Since

\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}(z)=2\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}(z)-\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z),

we compute

\displaystyle|\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}(z)|^{2}

\displaystyle=4|\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}(z)|^{2}-4\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}(z)\cdot\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z)+|\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z)|^{2}=|\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}(z)|^{2}=1.

This proves
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\in\mathcal{M}_{h}
, thus completing the proof of the lemma.
∎

Before deriving the perturbed midpoint equation satisfied by the stopped iterate,
we first show that the inner fixed-point iteration is contractive under the usual
condition
k=O(h^{2})
with a sufficiently small constant. The proof follows a similar structure as the contraction argument for the constraint-preserving midpoint
iteration in
[
15
,
20
,
24
]
, adapted here to the shifted discrete field

\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
.

Lemma 4.4

(Contraction of the inner iteration)
.

There exist constants
h_{0}>0
,
c_{\mathrm{FL}}>0
, and
q\in(0,1)
,
independent of
h,k,i,\ell
, such that, for all
h\in(0,h_{0})
, if

(4.8)

k\leq c_{\mathrm{FL}}h^{2},

then

(4.9)

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+2}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq q\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}}\right\|_{h},\qquad\forall\ell\geq 0.

Consequently, for every
\varepsilon_{i+1}>0
, the stopping criterion
(
4.3
) is satisfied after finitely many
iterations.

Proof.

Set
\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell}:=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}
.
Subtracting (
4.2
) at the iterations

\ell+1
and
\ell
gives, for all
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\alpha\left\langle\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\frac{\gamma k}{2}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

(4.10)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{\gamma k}{2}\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

Choosing
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}
, the two cross-product terms on
the left-hand side vanish nodally. Therefore,

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq Ck\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{h}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}}\right\|_{h},

where we used the nodal bound (
4.7
).
We next use the inverse estimate (see
[
15
]
):

(4.11)

\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{h}\leq Ch^{-2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{h},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}

to infer that

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq Ckh^{-2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\delta}_{h}^{\ell}}\right\|_{h}.

By choosing
c_{\mathrm{FL}}>0
sufficiently small in (
4.8
),
we obtain
Ckh^{-2}\leq q<1
, and thus
(
4.9
) follows.

It remains to prove that the stopping criterion is reached in finitely many
steps. Since the lumped norm is nodal, we have

\displaystyle\left\|{I_{h}\left[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell})\right]}\right\|_{h}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\beta_{z}\left|\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}(z)\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell})(z)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\beta_{z}\left|\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell})(z)\right|^{2}

\displaystyle=\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell})}\right\|_{h}^{2},

where we again used (
4.7
).
Thanks to (
4.11
), we obtain

\left\|{I_{h}\left[\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell})\right]}\right\|_{h}\leq Ch^{-2}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}}\right\|_{h}.

By (
4.9
),

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell}}\right\|_{h}\leq q^{\ell}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,0}}\right\|_{h}.

Thus the left-hand side of (
4.3
)
tends to zero geometrically as
\ell\to\infty
. Since

\varepsilon_{i+1}>0
, the stopping criterion is reached after finitely
many iterations.
∎

Having shown that the inner iteration terminates under the time-step condition
in Lemma
4.4
, we next identify the
equation satisfied by the stopped iterate. This equation is the ideal midpoint
scheme perturbed by an algebraic residual which is controlled directly by the
stopping criterion.

Lemma 4.5
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be generated by
Algorithm
4.1
. Then, for each
i\in\{0,\ldots,N-1\}
and every
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
,

(4.12)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h},

where
\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}
is defined by (
4.5
), satisfying (
4.6
).

Proof.

By (
4.4
),

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1},\qquad\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}=\frac{2}{k}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}-\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\right).

Hence, we have

\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}=-\frac{2}{k}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}.

Multiplying (
4.2
) with

\ell=\ell_{\ast}
by
2/k
then gives

(4.13)

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=0.

Adding and subtracting the fully implicit field contribution gives

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

(4.14)

\displaystyle\qquad=\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}+1}-\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i,\ell_{\ast}}\right),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

Using the notations in (
4.5
), and using the fact that the mass-lumped inner product is nodal,

\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\boldsymbol{r}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}=\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}.

Therefore (
4.14
) implies
(
4.12
), as required.
∎

The identity above shows that the stopped iterate solves the ideal midpoint
scheme up to the explicitly controlled residual

\gamma\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}
. We now derive the corresponding energy
identity, which is the starting point for the stability and error estimates.

Lemma 4.6

(Perturbed energy identity)
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be generated by
Algorithm
4.1
. Then, for each

i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]

(4.15)

\displaystyle\qquad=\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha}\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}.

In particular, the energy identity (
3.6
) is recovered whenever

\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}=\boldsymbol{0}
.

Proof.

By Lemma
4.5
, the stopped iterate satisfies
(
4.12
). Taking

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}
in (
4.12
) gives

(4.16)

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}=\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}.

Next, taking
\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}

in (
4.12
) gives

\displaystyle-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}

(4.17)

\displaystyle\qquad=-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha}\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}.

We now add (
4.16
) and
(
4.17
) to observe that the leading precession terms cancel.
Moreover, by the definition of
\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}
,

\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right).

Since
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
is symmetric,

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right)=\frac{1}{2k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right].

This proves (
4.15
).
∎

The identity (
4.15
) is no longer a pure
dissipation law because of the terms involving

\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}
. However, the stopping criterion gives

\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq\varepsilon_{i+1}
, which is enough to
recover an
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-stability estimate under a mild smallness condition on the
accumulated solver tolerances.

Lemma 4.7

(
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-stability of the inexact scheme)
.

Let
\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be generated by
Algorithm
4.1
. Assume that

(4.18)

k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\leq C_{\mathrm{st}}.

Then, for
k>0
sufficiently small,

(4.19)

\max_{0\leq j\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{j}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C_{\mathrm{st}}\right).

In particular, for a uniform tolerance

\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{\rm fp}
, condition
(
4.18
) is satisfied if
\varepsilon_{\rm fp}=O(h)
.

Proof.

We estimate the two residual terms in the perturbed energy identity
(
4.15
). First, by the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality and (
4.6
),

\gamma\left|\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}\right|\leq\frac{1}{4}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+C\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

For the second residual term, since

\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\in\mathbb{V}_{h}
, the identity (
2.20
) gives

\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right).

We note that by the inverse estimate (
2.6
),

\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq Ch^{-1}\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq Ch^{-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}.

Hence, by the continuity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
and Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\varepsilon_{i+1}\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ch^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

Substituting these estimates into
(
4.15
) and multiplying by
k
yields

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]\leq Ck\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ckh^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

Now, we aim to estimate the terms on the right-hand side.
By the coercivity and continuity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
,

\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}\leq C\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})+\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right].

Therefore, we infer that

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]

(4.20)

\displaystyle\qquad\leq Ck\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})+\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]+Ckh^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

For
k>0
sufficiently small, the next-step energy term on the right-hand
side of (
4.20
) can be absorbed into the left-hand side. By a similar argument as in (
3.34
) and applying the discrete Gronwall lemma, we obtain

\max_{0\leq j\leq N}\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{j},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{j})\leq C\left(\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0})+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\right).

Summing (
4.20
) over
i=0,\ldots,j-1
, using the
telescoping of the energy increments, and applying the preceding bound, we
also obtain

k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C\left(\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0},\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0})+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\right).

Finally, using the equivalence between

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\cdot,\cdot)
and the
\mathbb{H}^{1}
-norm on
\mathbb{V}_{h}
,
and then using (
4.18
), proves
(
4.19
).

If
\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{\rm fp}
, then
k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{\rm fp}^{2}=Th^{-2}\varepsilon_{\rm fp}^{2}
.
Thus, (
4.18
) is satisfied whenever

\varepsilon_{\rm fp}=O(h)
. This completes the proof.
∎

With the stability estimate in hand, we are now ready to derive an
a priori
error bound for the practical inexact scheme. As in the exact-solver case, we compare the numerical solution with the elliptic projection of the exact solution. Define

(4.21)

\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}:=\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}-R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i},\qquad\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}:=\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-R_{h}\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}.

Theorem 4.8

(Energy-norm error estimate for the practical scheme)
.

Let Assumption
3.2
hold, and suppose that

k\leq c_{\mathrm{FL}}h^{2}
, where
c_{\mathrm{FL}}
is chosen as in
Lemma
4.4
. Let

\{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}\}_{i=0}^{N}
be generated by
Algorithm
4.1
. Then, for
k>0

sufficiently small,

(4.22)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{0}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+(h+k^{2})^{2}+k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\right).

Consequently,

(4.23)

\displaystyle\max_{0\leq i\leq N}\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\leq C\left(h+k^{2}+\left(k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\right).

In particular, if

k\sum_{i=0}^{N-1}h^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}\leq C(h+k^{2})^{2},

then the practical midpoint scheme preserves the same error rate as the ideal scheme; see (
3.23
). For a uniform tolerance

\varepsilon_{i+1}=\varepsilon_{\rm fp}
, it is sufficient to take
\varepsilon_{\rm fp}\leq Ch(h+k^{2})
.

Proof.

Subtracting the projected exact equation from
(
4.12
), analogously to (
3.21
), we obtain the perturbed discrete error equation

\displaystyle\left\langle\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\alpha\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\gamma\left\langle\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\times P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}),\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}

(4.24)

\displaystyle\quad-\left\langle\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}+\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}.

Testing (
4
) first with

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}
and then with

\boldsymbol{\phi}_{h}=-\frac{\gamma}{\alpha}\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}
,
and adding the two identities, gives the same cancellation as in the exact
solver case (Theorem
3.7
), with two additional terms. More precisely,

\displaystyle\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha k}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\rho}_{h}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+\varepsilon_{0}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}

(4.25)

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}-\frac{\gamma^{2}}{\alpha}\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h},

where
\varepsilon_{0}>0
can be chosen arbitrarily small. It remains to estimate the last two terms on the right-hand side of (
4.25
). The first residual term is estimated by

\left|\gamma\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right\rangle_{h}\right|\leq C\varepsilon_{i+1}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}\leq\varepsilon_{0}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+C\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

For the second solver term, we use the identity (
2.20
) to obtain

\displaystyle\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}},\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}\right).

Therefore, by the continuity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
, inverse
estimate (
2.6
), the norm equivalence (
2.3
), and the bound (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\mathcal{H}_{h,\lambda}\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}\right\rangle_{h}\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{s}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-1}\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}\varepsilon_{i+1}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ch^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2},

where we also used Young’s inequality.
Choosing
\varepsilon_{0}>0
sufficiently small and using (
3.16
), we get

\displaystyle\frac{k}{2}\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1}}\right\|_{h}^{2}+\frac{\gamma}{2\alpha}\left[\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1},\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+1})-\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i},\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i})\right]

\displaystyle\leq Ck\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{e}}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{1}}^{2}+Ck(h+k^{2})^{2}+Ckh^{-2}\varepsilon_{i+1}^{2}.

The remainder of the proof is now identical to the exact-solver case in Theorem
3.7
, leading to
(
4.22
). Finally,
(
4.23
) follows from the splitting

\boldsymbol{m}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}=\boldsymbol{e}_{h,\varepsilon}^{i}+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i}),

as well as Lemma
A.1
and an argument analogous to (
3.38
). The remaining statements of the theorem follows immediately as a consequence of (
4.23
). This completes the proof.
∎

5.
Numerical experiments

In this section, we present numerical experiments illustrating the convergence behaviour and structure-preserving properties of the proposed inexact midpoint scheme. All computations are performed on
FEniCS

[
5
]
using continuous piecewise affine finite elements. The nonlinear systems are solved by the constraint-preserving fixed-point iteration in Algorithm
4.1
.
The detail of each experiment is specified below.

5.1.
Spatial and temporal convergence rates

We first verify the convergence rates predicted by the
a priori
error estimates.
Since no closed-form solution is available for the problem with the chiral
boundary condition, the errors are computed against refined numerical reference
solutions. Let
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,k,\varepsilon}^{N}
denote the numerical solution at
final time
T=t_{N}
, and let
\boldsymbol{m}_{\rm ref}
be the corresponding reference
solution. For
s=0,1
, we define

e_{s}(h,k):=\left\|{I_{h_{\rm ref}}\boldsymbol{m}_{h,k,\varepsilon}^{N}-\boldsymbol{m}_{\rm ref}}\right\|_{\mathbb{H}^{s}}.

Here
\mathbb{H}^{0}=\mathbb{L}^{2}
, and
I_{h_{\rm ref}}
denotes interpolation onto the
reference finite element space.

For two consecutive refinement levels with parameters

\rho_{j}
and
\rho_{j+1}
, where
\rho=h
in the spatial convergence test and

\rho=k
in the temporal convergence test, the observed convergence rate in the

\mathbb{H}^{s}
norm is computed by

\operatorname{rate}_{s}:=\frac{\log\!\left(e_{s}(\rho_{j})/e_{s}(\rho_{j+1})\right)}{\log\!\left(\rho_{j}/\rho_{j+1}\right)}.

In this uniform refinement test,
\rho_{j+1}=\rho_{j}/2
, so this reduces to

\operatorname{rate}_{s}=\log_{2}\!\left(\frac{e_{s}(\rho_{j})}{e_{s}(\rho_{j+1})}\right).

We now describe the first experiment. Let
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
. The parameters are chosen as

\alpha=0.5,\qquad\gamma=1,\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}=1,\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0.2,\qquad\lambda=0,

and the final time is
T=2\times 10^{-3}
. The fixed-point tolerance is set to
\varepsilon_{i}=10^{-10}
for all time-steps. The initial datum is

\boldsymbol{m}^{0}(x,y)=\frac{\bigl(a\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y),a\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y),1\bigr)}{\left|\bigl(a\sin(\pi x)\sin(\pi y),a\cos(\pi x)\sin(\pi y),1\bigr)\right|},\qquad\text{where }\,a=0.35.

This gives a smooth, unit-length, non-equilibrium initial configuration.

For the spatial convergence test, we use a coupled refinement strategy: the mesh
is refined uniformly and the time step is chosen according to
k=0.05h^{2}
.
This choice is compatible with the contraction condition for the fixed-point
iteration; see Lemma
4.4
. It also
ensures that the temporal contribution in the estimate is of higher order than
the leading spatial contribution. Indeed, the error estimate predicts

e_{1}(h,k)\lesssim h+k^{2}
, and hence, under the choice
k=0.05h^{2}
, the temporal
term satisfies
k^{2}=O(h^{4})
. Thus the observed
\mathbb{H}^{1}
rate is expected to
reflect the spatial convergence rate. The reference solution is computed on a
fine
200\times 200
triangulation using the same time-step rule. The errors

e_{0}
and
e_{1}
are shown in Figure
1(a)
. The observed

\mathbb{H}^{1}
rate is close to first order, while the
\mathbb{L}^{2}

higher rate.

For the temporal convergence test, the spatial mesh is fixed to a

64\times 64
triangulation, and the time step is successively halved over four
refinement levels. Let
k_{\min}
denote the smallest, that is, the finest,
time step among these four tested values. The reference solution is computed on
the same spatial mesh with
k_{\rm ref}=k_{\min}/6
.
Since the spatial mesh is fixed, the comparison isolates the temporal discretisation
error. The corresponding errors are shown in
Figure
1(b)
, and the observed rates are close to
second order.

5.2.
Influence of the fixed-point tolerance

We next examine the influence of the stopping tolerance in the constraint-preserving fixed-point iteration. This experiment is designed to illustrate the solver-error contribution in Theorem
4.8

h

k

\varepsilon_{\rm fp}/h
.

To test this behaviour, we fix the same parameters as before, fix the spatial mesh and the time step, and vary only the fixed-point tolerance. More precisely, we use a
64\times 64
triangulation and choose
k=0.05h^{2}
.
A reference solution
\boldsymbol{m}_{h,k,\varepsilon_{\rm ref}}^{N}

\varepsilon_{\rm fp}>\varepsilon_{\rm ref}

\delta_{s}(\varepsilon_{\rm fp}):=\left\|{\boldsymbol{m}_{h,k,\varepsilon_{\rm fp}}^{N}-\boldsymbol{m}_{h,k,\varepsilon_{\rm ref}}^{N}}\right\|_{H^{s}},\qquad s=0,1.

Since the mesh and time step are fixed throughout this test, the quantities
\delta_{s}(\varepsilon_{\rm fp})
measure only the effect of stopping the fixed-point iteration at different tolerances.

Figure

and

Nevertheless, the observed behaviour is consistent with the solver-error contribution in Theorem

5.3.
Skyrmion relaxation and structure preservation

We next test the robustness of the proposed scheme on a physically relevant chiral configuration. The purpose of this experiment is to illustrate the
behaviour of the inexact midpoint scheme when applied to a localised
skyrmion-type texture, and to verify the preservation of the geometric and
energetic structures during its dissipative relaxation.

Let
\Omega=(0,1)^{2}
. We consider a Bloch-type skyrmion initial state. Set

r:=\sqrt{\left(x-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}+\left(y-\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}}\quad\text{and}\quad\theta(r):=\frac{\pi}{2}\left(1-\tanh\left(\frac{r-R}{w}\right)\right),

with
R>0
and
w>0
to be specified later.
For
r>0
, the initial magnetisation is given by

\boldsymbol{m}^{0}(x,y)=\left(-\left[\frac{y-\frac{1}{2}}{r}\right]\sin\theta(r),\,\left[\frac{x-\frac{1}{2}}{r}\right]\sin\theta(r),\,\cos\theta(r)\right),

while at the centre of the skyrmion we set

\boldsymbol{m}^{0}\left(\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)=(0,0,-1).

In the experiment, we take
R=0.18
and
w=0.04
.
This initial condition describes a localised Bloch-type skyrmion texture, namely the magnetisation points approximately downward near the centre of the domain, upward away from the core, and rotates tangentially in the transition layer between the two regions.

The parameters are chosen as

\alpha=0.5,\qquad\gamma=1,\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}=1,\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=2\pi,\qquad\lambda=0.

We use a
64\times 64
triangulation and choose
k=0.02h^{2}
and
T=0.2
.
The fixed-point tolerance is set to
\varepsilon_{i}=10^{-10}
for all time-steps.
To assess the structure-preserving behaviour of the method, we monitor the nodal length defect

(5.1)

\mathrm{d}(t_{i}):=\max_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\left||\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}(z)|-1\right|,

and the discrete energy

(5.2)

\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})=\frac{\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}}{2}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\left\langle\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}\right\rangle.

The results are shown in Figures
3
and
4
. The numerical solution preserves the nodal
unit-length constraint up to round-off error throughout the simulation. The
discrete energy decreases over time, confirming the dissipative behaviour of
the scheme in this chiral setting. The snapshots show that the localised
skyrmion core persists during the relaxation: the central region remains
oriented approximately downward while the surrounding magnetisation remains
oriented upward. This persistence is consistent with the robustness usually
associated with skyrmion-type textures, whose localised reversed core and
chirality are important features in spintronic applications.

5.4.
Bubbling experiment with chiral boundary condition

We finally test the proposed method on a high-gradient benchmark inspired by the bubbling experiment of Bartels and Prohl
[
15
]
. The purpose of this experiment is twofold. First, in the exchange-only case
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0
, it provides a reference test in which the maximum gradient is known numerically to develop a rapid growth. Second, for
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\neq 0
, it allows us to examine how the bulk DMI term and the corresponding chiral natural boundary condition affect the concentration and possible unwinding of the bubble.

Here, we take
\Omega=\left(-\frac{1}{2},\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}
.
To describe the initial data, let
\boldsymbol{x}=(x_{1},x_{2})
,
r=|\boldsymbol{x}|
, and
A(r)=\frac{1}{s}(1-2r)^{4}
.
We then set

\boldsymbol{m}^{0}(\boldsymbol{x})=\begin{cases}(0,0,-1),&r\geq\frac{1}{2},\\[5.0pt]
\displaystyle\frac{\bigl(2x_{1}A(r),\,2x_{2}A(r),\,A(r)^{2}-r^{2}\bigr)}{A(r)^{2}+r^{2}},&r<\frac{1}{2}.\end{cases}

The discrete initial condition is given by
I_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{0}
.
The parameters of the problem are chosen as

\alpha=1.0,\qquad\gamma=2.0,\qquad\kappa_{\mathrm{e}}=1,\qquad\lambda=0,\qquad s=4.

We compare the five DMI strengths

\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0,\quad\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=\pm 1,\quad\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=\pm 2.

The parameter
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=0
corresponds to an effective field consisting of only the exchange interaction considered in
[
15
]
.
In this experiment, a
32\times 32
triangulation is fixed throughout. We choose
k=0.05h^{2}
and run the experiment up to final time
T=0.1
.
The fixed-point tolerance is set to
\varepsilon_{i}=10^{-10}
for all time-steps.

We monitor six diagnostic quantities: the nodal length defect (
5.1
), the discrete energy change
\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})-\mathcal{E}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{0})
, the exchange energy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{exc}}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})
, the DMI energy
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{DMI}}(\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i})
, the maximum gradient seminorm
G_{\infty}^{i}
, and the number of fixed-point iterations at each time-step. Here,
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{exc}}
and
\mathcal{E}_{\rm{DMI}}
were defined in (
1.3
), and

G_{\infty}^{i}:=\max_{K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{m}_{h}^{i}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}.

Figures
5
and
6
show snapshots of the magnetisation for
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=-2
and
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=+2
, respectively. When
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=-2
, the initially localised bubble undergoes a rapid concentration near the origin, producing a strongly localised structure. In contrast, when
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}=+2
, the bubble appears to unwind smoothly.

The corresponding diagnostic quantities are shown in Figure
7
. The nodal length defect remains close to machine precision throughout the computation, confirming that the fixed-point iteration preserves the nodal unit-length constraint. The discrete energy decreases in time as shown. The plot of
G_{\infty}^{i}
exhibits the high-gradient behaviour, indicating a possible gradient blow-up when
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}\leq 0
. On the other hand, in the tested range, positive values of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
appear to delay the gradient concentration or suppress it altogether. The fixed-point iteration count remains moderate, although it becomes more demanding near the time interval where the gradient becomes large.

A notable feature of the DMI comparison is the asymmetry with respect to the sign of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
. In the present experiment, negative values of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
accelerate the growth of
G_{\infty}^{i}
, while positive values of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
tend to suppress the concentration and promote relaxation. This behaviour appears to be consistent with the chiral nature of the DMI interaction. The initial bubble has a prescribed handedness, and changing the sign of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
changes whether the DMI contribution favours or penalises this chirality. In particular, the chiral boundary condition may either assist the concentration of the bubble or facilitate its unwinding, depending on the sign of
\kappa_{\mathrm{d}}
. A systematic investigation of this is beyond the scope of the present work, but appears to be an interesting topic for further study.

Appendix A
Auxiliary estimates

We prove several auxiliary estimates which are essential for the error analysis.

Lemma A.1

(Elliptic projection estimate)
.

For every
\boldsymbol{v}\in{H}^{2}
satisfying (
2.13
), the projection
R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}
defined by (
2.21
) satisfies

(A.1)

\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}\leq Ch\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{{H}^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\right),

where
\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}
was defined in (
2.19
) and
C
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

Set
\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}:=I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}
and

\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}:=R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}
.
Then

(A.2)

R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}+\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}.

Subtracting
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(I_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})
from (
2.22
), and using (
2.17
), gives

(A.3)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})

\displaystyle=-\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})-\lambda\left\langle\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle+\lambda\big(\left\langle I_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle-\left\langle I_{h}\boldsymbol{v},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}\big)-\delta_{h}(\boldsymbol{v};\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}),

where
\delta_{h}
represents the quadrature defect term:

\delta_{h}(\boldsymbol{v};\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}):=\left\langle P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle_{h}-\left\langle P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v}),\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}\right\rangle.

By the interpolation estimate, the continuity of
\mathfrak{a}
, the quadrature estimate (
2.7
), and the
L^{2}
-stability of
P_{h}
, we obtain from (
A.3
),

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}(\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h},\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}+C\left\|{\boldsymbol{\eta}_{h}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}

\displaystyle\quad+Ch\left\|{I_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}+Ch\left\|{P_{h}\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}

(A.4)

\displaystyle\leq Ch\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{{H}^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\right)\left\|{\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}.

Choosing
\boldsymbol{\chi}_{h}=\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}
in (
A.4
) and using the coercivity of
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
in (
2.18
) gives

\left\|{\boldsymbol{\theta}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}\leq Ch\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{{H}^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{{L}^{2}}\right).

The estimate (
A.1
) then follows by (
A.2
), (
2.4
), and the triangle inequality.
∎

Lemma A.2

(Smooth-data bounds on elliptic projection)
.

Let
R_{h}
be the elliptic projection defined by (
2.21
). Define

(A.5)

p^{\ast}:=\begin{cases}\infty,&d=1,2,\\
6,&d=3.\end{cases}

Then, for every
p\in[1,p^{\ast}]
and every
\boldsymbol{v}\in{W}^{1,p}\cap{H}^{2}
satisfying (
2.13
),

(A.6)

\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}+\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right),

where
C
is a constant independent of
h
.

Proof.

We split
R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}=P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}+(R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v})
.
The term
P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}
can be bounded by (
2.10
).
Moreover,

R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}=(R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-\boldsymbol{v})+(\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}).

Therefore, by Lemma
A.1
and (
2.11
),

(A.7)

\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ch\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right).

Now, if
1\leq p\leq 2
, then
H^{1}\hookrightarrow W^{1,p}
on bounded domains, and hence

(A.8)

\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{1}}.

Combining (
A.8
) with (
A.7
) gives the desired estimate for
p\in[1,2]
.

If
2<p\leq p^{\ast}
, the inverse estimate gives

\displaystyle\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{-d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{1}}

\displaystyle\leq Ch^{1-d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)}\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right).

By the definition of
p^{\ast}
, we have
1-d\left(\frac{1}{2}-\frac{1}{p}\right)\geq 0
,
and hence, for
h\leq 1
,

\left\|{R_{h}\boldsymbol{v}-P_{h}\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\boldsymbol{v})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right),

thus implying (
A.6
).
This completes the proof of the lemma.
∎

Lemma A.3

(Midpoint consistency and projected coefficient bound)
.

Let
p\in[1,p^{\ast}]
, where
p^{\ast}
were defined in (
A.5
). Assume that the exact solution
\boldsymbol{m}
satisfies the regularity (
3.3
) and the chiral boundary condition (
2.13
) for all
t\in[0,T]
.
Then, for every
i=0,1,\ldots,N-1
,

(A.9)

\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{H^{3}}+\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ck^{2}.

Moreover,

(A.10)

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C.

Here,
C
is a constant which depends on the regularity of the exact solution in (
3.3
), but is independent of
h
and
k
.

Proof.

Set
\tau:=k/2
. We first prove the midpoint consistency estimate (
A.9
). By Taylor’s formula with integral remainder, for
s\in[-\tau,\tau]
,

\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+s)=\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}+s\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}+\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

Taking
s=\tau
and
s=-\tau
, adding the two identities, and dividing by
2
, we obtain

\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\int_{0}^{\tau}(\tau-r)\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}\big(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+r\big)\,\mathrm{d}r+\int_{0}^{-\tau}(-\tau-r)\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}\big(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+r\big)\,\mathrm{d}r\right].

Hence, we infer that

\left\|{\overline{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}-\boldsymbol{m}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{H^{3}}\leq Ck^{2}\left\|{\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(t_{i},t_{i+1};H^{3})}.

Next, observe that

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}=\frac{1}{k}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s=\frac{1}{k}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+s)\,\mathrm{d}s.

Therefore, we have

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{1}{k}\int_{-\tau}^{\tau}\left[\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+s)-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})\right]\mathrm{d}s.

Using Taylor’s formula for
\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}
around
t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}
, we obtain

\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+s)-\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})=s\partial_{tt}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}})+\int_{0}^{s}(s-r)\partial_{ttt}\boldsymbol{m}(t_{i+\frac{1}{2}}+r)\,\mathrm{d}r.

The first term integrates to zero over
[-\tau,\tau]
. Hence,

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}-\dot{\boldsymbol{m}}^{i+\frac{1}{2}}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq Ck^{2}\left\|{\partial_{ttt}\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(t_{i},t_{i+1};H^{1})}.

This proves (
A.9
).

It remains to prove (
A.10
). Since the elliptic projection
R_{h}
is linear,
\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}=R_{h}(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1})
.
Moreover, because
\boldsymbol{m}(t)
satisfies the chiral boundary condition for every
t
, the difference quotient
\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}
also satisfies the same boundary condition. Thus, Lemma
A.2
gives

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}R_{h}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\leq C\left(\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}+\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{H^{2}}+\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\right).

By writing

\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}=\frac{1}{k}\int_{t_{i}}^{t_{i+1}}\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}(s)\,\mathrm{d}s,

we infer that

\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}+\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(t_{i},t_{i+1};W^{1,p}\cap H^{2})}.

Furthermore, we have

\left\|{\mathcal{H}_{\lambda}(\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1})}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|{\mathrm{d}_{t}\boldsymbol{m}^{i+1}}\right\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\left\|{\partial_{t}\boldsymbol{m}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(t_{i},t_{i+1};H^{2})}.

Combining the preceding estimates proves (
A.10
).
∎

Lemma A.4

(Discrete product estimates)
.

Let
p>\max\{d,2\}
and let
\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\in V_{h}
. Then, for all
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\in V_{h}
,

(A.11)

\left\|{I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{{W}^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}.

Furthermore, the bilinear form
\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}
defined in (
2.17
) satisfies

(A.12)

\left|\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\right)\right|\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{{W}^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{{H}^{1}}^{2}.

Here,
C
is a constant independent of
h
and
\lambda
.

Proof.

Let
q\in[2,\infty)
be chosen such that
\frac{1}{2}=\frac{1}{p}+\frac{1}{q}
.
Since
p>d
, the Sobolev embeddings
W^{1,p}\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}
and
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{q}
hold.

We first prove (
A.11
). On each element
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
, set
\boldsymbol{q}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}
.
Since
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{h}
are affine on
K
, we have
\boldsymbol{q}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(K;R^{3})
. Let
\widehat{K}
be the reference simplex.
The restriction of
I_{h}
to the finite-dimensional
space
\mathcal{P}_{2}(K;R^{3})
is uniformly stable after scaling to the
reference element. More precisely, by affine equivalence and norm equivalence
on
\mathcal{P}_{2}(\widehat{K};R^{3})
, shape-regularity implies

(A.13)

\displaystyle\left\|{I_{h}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)},

(A.14)

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla I_{h}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

Indeed, (
A.13
) follows from the boundedness of the linear map

I_{\widehat{K}}:\mathcal{P}_{2}(\widehat{K};R^{3})\to\mathcal{P}_{1}(\widehat{K};R^{3})

in
{L}^{2}(\widehat{K})
and scaling.
For (
A.14
), observe
that the map
\boldsymbol{q}\mapsto\left\|{\nabla I_{\widehat{K}}\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{K})}

vanishes on constants. Hence, it defines a seminorm on the quotient space

\mathcal{P}_{2}(\widehat{K};R^{3})/R^{3}
. On this quotient,
\boldsymbol{q}\mapsto\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{K})}

is a norm. Since the quotient space is finite-dimensional, there exists

C>0
such that

\left\|{\nabla I_{\widehat{K}}\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{K})}\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{q}}\right\|_{L^{2}(\widehat{K})},\qquad\forall\boldsymbol{q}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(\widehat{K};R^{3}).

Scaling back to
K
gives (
A.14
), with constants depending
only on the shape-regularity of the mesh.

Using (
A.13
)–(
A.14
), the product rule elementwise, and Hölder’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left\|{I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})}\right\|_{H^{1}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq C\left(\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}(K)}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}(K)}+\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}(K)}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{q}(K)}\right).

Summing over
K\in\mathcal{T}_{h}
and using the embedding
{W}^{1,p}\hookrightarrow{L}^{\infty}
proves (
A.11
).

Next, we prove (
A.12
). Since
I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})(z)=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(z)\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}(z)
for all
z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}
, the shifted mass-lumped contribution vanishes exactly:

\displaystyle\lambda\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\right\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=\lambda\sum_{z\in\mathcal{N}_{h}}\beta_{z}\,\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(z)\cdot\bigl(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}(z)\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}(z)\bigr)=0.

Therefore,

\mathfrak{a}_{h,\lambda}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\right)=\mathfrak{a}\left(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},I_{h}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h})\right),

and all constants in subsequent argument are independent of
\lambda
.
Now, set
\boldsymbol{q}_{h}:=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{r}_{h}:=I_{h}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}-\boldsymbol{q}_{h}
.
Then

\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},I_{h}\boldsymbol{q}_{h})=\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{q}_{h})+\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{r}_{h}).

We estimate the non-interpolated term first. By the product rule, we have

\displaystyle\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\right\rangle

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left\langle\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\right\rangle+\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left\langle\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\right\rangle=\sum_{\ell=1}^{d}\left\langle\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\partial_{\ell}\boldsymbol{z}_{h}\right\rangle,

Hence, by Hölder’s inequality and the embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{q}
,

\left|\left\langle\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\right\rangle\right|\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{q}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

The DMI terms are first order and are bounded in the same way:

\displaystyle\left|\left\langle\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\nabla\times\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\right\rangle\right|+\left|\left\langle\nabla\times\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{q}_{h}\right\rangle\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Consequently,

(A.15)

\left|\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{q}_{h})\right|\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

It remains to control the interpolation defect
\boldsymbol{r}_{h}
. Since

\boldsymbol{q}_{h}|_{K}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(K;R^{3})
, the standard local interpolation
estimate for the nodal
P_{1}
interpolant gives

\left\|{\boldsymbol{r}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}+h_{K}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{r}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq Ch_{K}^{2}\left\|{\mathrm{D}_{h}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}.

Since
\boldsymbol{v}_{h}
and
\boldsymbol{z}_{h}
are affine on
K
, the elementwise
second derivatives of
\boldsymbol{q}_{h}=\boldsymbol{v}_{h}\times\boldsymbol{z}_{h}
consist only of
products of first derivatives. Thus

\left\|{\mathrm{D}_{h}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{q}(K)}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}(K)}.

Furthermore, by the inverse estimate (
2.6
), we have

\displaystyle\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{r}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{K}\left\|{\mathrm{D}_{h}^{2}\boldsymbol{q}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{K}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{q}(K)}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}(K)}

\displaystyle\leq Ch_{K}^{1-d/p}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}(K)}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}(K)}.

Since
p>d
, the factor
h_{K}^{1-d/p}
is uniformly bounded. Hence, after summing over
K
,

\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{r}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}}\leq C\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{p}}\left\|{\nabla\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{L^{2}}.

The corresponding
{L}^{2}
estimate follows in the same way, with the factor

h_{K}^{2-d/p}
.
Therefore, by the continuity of
\mathfrak{a}
, we obtain

(A.16)

\displaystyle\left|\mathfrak{a}(\boldsymbol{v}_{h},\boldsymbol{r}_{h})\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{r}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}\leq C\left\|{\boldsymbol{z}_{h}}\right\|_{W^{1,p}}\left\|{\boldsymbol{v}_{h}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{2}.

Combining (
A.15
) and
(
A.16
) proves (
A.12
).
∎
