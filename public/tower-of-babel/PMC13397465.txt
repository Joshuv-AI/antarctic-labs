Safety of a feed additive consisting of potassium ferrocyanide for all animal species (K+S Kali GmbH)
Authors: EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), Roberto Edoardo Villa, Giovanna Azimonti, Eleftherios Bonos, Henrik Christensen, Mojca Durjava, Birgit Dusemund, Ronette Gehring, Boet Glandorf, Maryline Kouba, Marta López‐Alonso, Francesca Marcon, Giovanna Martelli, Carlo Nebbia, Alena Pechová, Miguel Prieto‐Maradona, Katerina Theodoridou, Noël Dierik, Matteo L Innocenti, Jordi Ortuño, Jordi Tarrés‐Call, Maria Vittoria Vettori
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractFollowing a request from the European Commission, EFSA was asked to deliver a scientific opinion on the safety of a feed additive consisting of potassium ferrocyanide for all animal species. In a previous assessment the FEEDAP Panel concluded that, in the absence of a margin of safety, the use of potassium chloride supplemented with the additive at the proposed conditions of use (150 mg ferrocyanide anion (from potassium ferrocyanide)/kg KCl) was not considered to be safe for chickens for fattening, laying hens, turkeys, piglets, veal calf, cattle for fattening, dairy cows, horses, rabbits and cats. Furthermore, the available data did not allow the FEEDAP Panel to conclude on the safety of the additive for the soil and the marine environment. In the present assessment the applicant proposes to modify the conditions of use of the additive for all species, setting them at 60 mg ferrocyanide anion/kg KCl and submitted additional information in support of the safety for the target species and the environment. Based on the new information provided the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the supplementation of potassium chloride with 60 mg ferrocyanide anion (from potassium ferrocyanide)/kg KCl is safe for all animal species and the environment.Keywords: anticaking agents, potassium ferrocyanide, safety, technological additives

Description of the additive.

On 11.05.2023, the EFSA Panel on Additives and Products or Substances used in Animal Feed (FEEDAP), in its opinion on the safety and efficacy of a feed additive consisting of potassium ferrocyanide as a technological additive for all animal species, concluded that, in the absence of a margin of safety, the use of potassium chloride according to the proposed conditions of use is not considered to be safe for chickens for fattening, laying hens, turkeys, piglets, veal calf, cattle for fattening, dairy cows, horses, rabbits and cats. In the absence of information on the use of potassium chloride in the diets for any other animal species, no conclusion on a potentially safe level of potassium chloride, supplemented with 150 mg ferrocyanide/kg KCl, can be made. Furthermore, the available data did not allow the FEEDAP Panel to conclude on the safety of the additive for the soil and the marine environment.

The European Commission gave the possibility to the applicant to submit supplementary information and data to complete the assessment and to allow a revision of the EFSA opinion.

The supplementary information and data have been transmitted by the applicant using the E‐Submission Food Chain Platform (application number FEED‐2025‐36514). 1

In view of the above and in accordance with Article 29(1)(a) of Regulation (EC) No 178/2002, the European Commission requests EFSA to deliver a new scientific opinion on a feed additive consisting of potassium ferrocyanide as a technological additive for all animal species under the conditions of Regulation (EC) No 1831/2003, based on the supplementary information and data submitted by the applicant.

DATA AND METHODOLOGIES

Data

The present assessment is based on data submitted by the applicant in the form of supplementary information 2 to a previous application on the same product. 3 The dossier was received on 11.11.2015 and the general information and supporting documentation are available on Open.EFSA at https://open.efsa.europa.eu/questions/EFSA‐Q‐2025‐00633.

The FEEDAP Panel used the data provided by the applicant together with data from other sources, such as previous risk assessments by EFSA or other expert bodies, peer‐reviewed scientific papers, other scientific reports and experts' (elicitation) knowledge, to deliver the present output.

Methodologies

The approach followed by the FEEDAP Panel to assess the safety and the efficacy of Potassium ferrocyanide is in line with the principles laid down in Regulation (EC) No 429/2008 4 and the relevant guidance documents: Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the target species (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2017), Guidance on the assessment of the safety of feed additives for the environment (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2019).

ASSESSMENT

Potassium ferrocyanide was intended to be supplemented to potassium chloride at a proposed maximum concentration of 150 mg ferrocyanide anion (from potassium ferrocyanide)/kg KCl. In the opinion adopted in 2023 (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023), the additive was characterised and the Panel identified maximum safe concentrations for the ferrocyanide ions from potassium ferrocyanide in complete feed for the target species. The Panel concluded also that (i) the supplementation of potassium chloride with potassium ferrocyanide at the proposed conditions of use is safe for pigs for fattening and lactating sows, sheep, goats, salmon and dogs; (ii) in the absence of a margin of safety, the use of potassium chloride according to the proposed conditions of use is not considered to be safe for chickens for fattening, laying hens, turkeys, piglets, veal calf, cattle for fattening, dairy cows, horse, rabbit and cats and (iii) in the absence of information on the use of potassium chloride in the diets for any other animal species, no conclusion on a potentially safe level of potassium chloride, supplemented with 150 mg ferrocyanide/kg, can be made.

Regarding the safety for the environment the Panel could not conclude on the safety of the additive for the soil and the marine environment.

The FEEDAP Panel concluded that the use of the feed additive potassium ferrocyanide incorporated in potassium chloride (providing a maximum of 150 mg ferrocyanide/kg KCl) does not raise concerns on the safety for the consumers; it is not irritant to skin and eye but should be considered as a dermal and respiratory sensitiser.

Potassium ferrocyanide was considered to be efficacious as an anticaking agent when included in potassium chloride at the proposed use levels.

In the present assessment the applicant proposes to modify the conditions of use of the additive for all species, setting them at 60 mg ferrocyanide anion/kg KCl for all animal species and submitted additional information in support of the safety for the target species and the environment.

Safety

Safety for the target species

In 2023, the FEEDAP Panel concluded that ‘The maximum safe concentration of ferrocyanide ions from potassium ferrocyanide in complete feed corresponds to: 0.45 mg/kg for turkey for fattening; 0.33 mg/kg chickens for fattening and other poultry species for fattening or reared for laying/breeding; 0.50 mg/kg for laying hens and other laying/breeding birds; 0.60 mg/kg for piglets; 0.72 mg/kg for pigs for fattening and minor porcine species for fattening; 0.88 mg/kg for sows and minor porcine species for reproduction/breeding; 1.40 mg/kg for veal calves (milk replacer); 0.86 mg/kg for dairy cows and other dairy ruminants (except for sheep/goats); 1.32 mg/kg for cattle for fattening and other ruminants for fattening or for rearing, sheep/goats and horses; 0.53 mg/kg for rabbits; 1.51 mg/kg for salmonids and other fin fish, 1.58 mg/kg for dogs; 1.32 mg/kg for cats and 5.87 mg/kg for ornamental fish’.

Regarding the supplementation of KCl with potassium ferrocyanide, the Panel concluded that: ‘The use of potassium ferrocyanide is safe, when added to potassium chloride at a maximum content of 150 mg ferrocyanide anions (anhydrous)/kg for pigs for fattening and lactating sows, sheep, goats, salmon and dogs. In the absence of a margin of safety, the use of potassium chloride according to the proposed conditions of use is not considered to be safe for chickens for fattening, laying hens, turkeys, piglets, veal calf, cattle for fattening, dairy cows, horse, rabbit and cats. In the absence of information on the use of potassium chloride in the diets for any other animal species, no conclusion on a potentially safe level of potassium chloride, supplemented with 150 mg ferrocyanide/kg, can be made’.

In the present assessment, the applicant proposes to modify the conditions of use of the additive for all animal species by setting a maximum level of 60 mg ferrocyanide anion (from potassium ferrocyanide)/kg KCl and recalculated the margins of safety (Table 2) based on this proposed level, following the approach applied in the previous opinion (EFSA FEEDAP Panel, 2023). 5 Margins of safety above 1 were calculated for all species, with the exception of dairy cows and rabbits, for which values were below 1 (0.8).

The FEEDAP Panel notes that the calculations of the margin of safety were based on the worse case assumption that all the requirements of potassium are met with the supplementation of KCl. This assumption did not consider the natural background content of potassium in the feed materials, which is particularly present in forages. The Panel considers it reasonable that the supplementation of the diets with KCl to cover the requirements will be adjusted to the potassium content of the feed. Therefore, the maximum proposed concentration of 60 mg ferrocyanide anion/kg KCl is considered safe also for dairy cows and rabbits; this conclusion is extrapolated to all animal species.

The Panel reiterates also the concept that ‘the above assessment is based on the assumption that potassium chloride is the only dietary source of ferrocyanide anions, since the estimate for maximum content of supplemented potassium chloride is derived from the ferrocyanide safety. Any other source of ferrocyanide would reduce the margin of safety given for the use of supplemented potassium chloride in the different animal species/categories. The only figure which is independent from the use of any other feed material containing or supplemented with ferrocyanide is the maximum dietary concentration of ferrocyanide in complete feed’.

Safety for the environment

In 2023 the FEEDAP Panel concluded that ‘The available data do not allow the FEEDAP Panel to conclude on the safety of the additive for the soil and the marine environment, while the use of the additive in land‐based aquaculture according to the proposed conditions of use is considered of no concern’.

The applicant submitted updated calculations of the predicted environmental concentrations (PECs) considering the proposed new conditions of use of 60 mg ferrocyanide anion/kg KCl, 6 a literature search on the concentration of the additive in the groundwater/porewater, 7 considerations on the marine PECsediment and aquatic ecotoxicity studies.

The PECsoil calculated in Phase I exceed the threshold of 10 μg/kg for dairy cow (12.4 μg/kg), cattle for fattening (10.3 μg/kg) and veal calf (10.5 μg/kg). The PEC of groundwater/porewater and the PECmarinesediment exceed their respective thresholds of 0.1 μg/L and 10 μg/kg.

The Panel considers that the same worst‐case assumption described above (see Section 3.1.1) applies also to the assessment of the safety for the environment. The literature search submitted by the applicant indicates that ferrocyanide is expected to undergo a natural biodegradation process that would keep environmental cyanide releases associated with its use as a feed additive insignificant compared to natural cyanide sinks and cycles. It would also not significantly alter the concentration and/or distribution of the substance in the receiving environment. In addition, the use of the ferrocyanide ion as a low‐level anticaking agent in KCl would not exceed its naturally occurring concentrations in some feed sources (i.g. linseed). Due to the high solubility in water, accumulation in marine sediment is not expected.

The ecotoxicity studies submitted (72‐h algal growth inhibition study, 8 48‐h acute immobilisation study in Daphnia magna, 9 96‐h acute toxicity study in Cyprinus carpio, 10 3‐h activated sludge respiration inhibition test 11 ) are considered as supporting evidence for the safety of the additive for the environment and are described in Appendix A.

Based on the above the FEEDAP Panel concludes that the use of the additive under the proposed conditions of use is safe for the environment.

CONCLUSIONS

The FEEDAP Panel concludes that the supplementation of potassium chloride with 60 mg ferrocyanide anion (from potassium ferrocyanide)/kg KCl is safe for all animal species and the environment.

RECOMMENDATION

The FEEDAP Panel recommends to include in the authorisation of potassium ferrocyanide the maximum safe concentrations of total ferrocyanide anions in complete feed of: 0.45 mg/kg for turkey for fattening; 0.33 mg/kg chickens for fattening and other poultry species for fattening or reared for laying/reproduction; 0.50 mg/kg for laying hens and other laying/reproduction birds; 0.60 mg/kg for piglets; 0.72 mg/kg for pigs for fattening and minor porcine species for fattening; 0.88 mg/kg for sows and minor porcine species for reproduction; 1.40 mg/kg for veal calves (milk replacer); 0.86 mg/kg for dairy cows and other dairy ruminants (except for sheep/goats); 1.32 mg/kg for cattle for fattening and other ruminants for fattening or for rearing, sheep/goats and horses; 0.53 mg/kg for rabbits; 1.51 mg/kg for salmonids and other fin fish, 1.58 mg/kg for dogs; 1.32 mg/kg for cats and 5.87 mg/kg for ornamental fish; 0.33 for all other animal species.

The above maximum safe concentrations of ferrocyanide anions in complete feed are independent from the use of any feed material containing or supplemented with ferrocyanide, and should therefore be applicable to all ferrocyanide sources authorised as feed additives (i.e. potassium (1i536) and sodium (1i535) ferrocyanide).

Ecotoxicity studies

Effects on aquatic primary producers (algae)

A 72‐h algal growth inhibition study with sodium ferrocyanide was conducted according to OECD Guideline 201. 12 Pseudokirchneriella subcapitata was exposed under static conditions to nominal concentrations of 0.10, 0.32, 1.0, 3.2, 10 and 32 mg/L, alongside a control. As test concentrations decreased over the exposure period, effect concentrations were expressed based on measured average exposure concentrations. The algae were exposed to time‐weighted average measured concentrations of 0.028, 0.055, 0.51, 2.0, 5.8 and 27 mg/L. The decline in concentrations was considered to be related to interaction with the test system, including adsorption to algal biomass and/or other non‐biological processes. Based on measured concentrations, the 72h‐ErC50 (growth rate) was 3.1 mg/L (95% CI: 0.80–12 mg/L) and the 72h‐No Observed Effect Concentration (NOEC) for growth rate inhibition was 0.055 mg/L.

Effects on aquatic invertebrates (Daphnia magna)

A 48‐h acute immobilisation study with sodium ferrocyanide was conducted according to OECD Guideline 202. 13 D. magna were exposed under static conditions to nominal concentrations of 0.10, 1.0, 10 and 100 mg/L, alongside a control. Analytical measurements confirmed that test concentrations remained stable throughout the exposure period (100%–104% of nominal), and therefore nominal concentrations were used for effect assessment. No immobilisation was observed at any of the tested concentrations up to 100 mg/L after 48 h of exposure, resulting in a 48h‐EC50 > 100 mg/L.

Effects on fish (Cyprinus carpio)

A 96‐h acute toxicity study with sodium ferrocyanide was conducted according to OECD Guideline 203. 14 C. carpio were exposed under static conditions to a nominal concentration of 100 mg/L, alongside a control, as part of a limit test. Analytical measurements confirmed that test concentrations remained stable throughout the exposure period (85%–101% of nominal) and therefore nominal concentrations were used for effect assessment. No mortality or visible effects were observed at 100 mg/L after 96 h of exposure, resulting in a 96h‐LC50 > 100 mg/L.

Effects on microorganisms in sewage treatment plants

In a 3‐h activated sludge respiration inhibition test conducted according to OECD Guideline 209, 15 sodium ferrocyanide was tested at nominal concentrations of 10, 100 and 1000 mg/L. The study was performed as a combined limit/range‐finding test with activated sludge from a municipal sewage treatment plant. No statistically significant inhibition of respiration was observed up to the highest concentration tested. The 3h‐EC50 was > 1000 mg/L.
