1
Introduction

Let
G=(V,E)
be a finite graph and let
h\geq 1
be an integer. An integer-valued function
f:V\to\mathbb{Z}
is called
h
-Lipschitz if

|f(u)-f(v)|\leq h\qquad\text{for every }uv\in E.

If
G
has connected components
C_{1},\ldots,C_{k}
, fix one root
r_{i}\in C_{i}
in each component and require
f(r_{i})=0
. Let
N_{G}(h)
be the number of such rooted functions. Korsky, Saffat and Aiylam observed that
N_{G}(h)
is an Ehrhart polynomial of degree
|V|-k
and defined
c(G)
by

c(G)^{|V|-k}:=\lim_{h\to\infty}\frac{N_{G}(h)}{h^{|V|-k}}.

(1)

Equivalently,
c(G)^{|V|-k}
is the volume of the rooted Lipschitz polytope

P_{G}:=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V}:x(r_{i})=0\text{ for all }i,\ |x(u)-x(v)|\leq 1\text{ for all }uv\in E\}.

(2)

This note concerns the random graph
G(n,d/n)
in the regime where
n\to\infty
first and
d\to\infty
afterwards. The previous bounds from
[
5
]
are

\frac{1}{2d}+O(d^{-2})\leq\log c(G(n,d/n))\leq\frac{4\log^{2}d}{d}+O(d^{-1})

(3)

with high probability. We prove the following first-order asymptotic.

Theorem 1.1
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
d_{0}=d_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that, for every
d\geq d_{0}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\left|\log c(G(n,d/n))-\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}\right|\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{d}\right)=1.

Equivalently,

c(G(n,d/n))=1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o_{d}(d^{-1})

with high probability.

The lower bound uses a logistic profile which is essentially uniform on an interval of length one and has boundary layers of width
d^{-1}
. For the random graph, we average first over the independent graph edges and then use bounded differences to pass from the annealed volume to high probability. The gain in profile entropy is
\pi^{2}/(3d)
, while the edge-forbidden cost is
\pi^{2}/(6d)
, leaving
\pi^{2}/(6d)
.

The upper bound is more delicate but has a short final form. The flatness lemma from
[
5
]
implies that every Lipschitz function has almost all vertices in some interval of length one. After anchoring that interval as
[0,1]
, the first upper tail is
(1,2]
and the first lower tail is
[-1,0)
. For fixed tail sizes
r,s
, the expected tail volume is bounded by

\frac{q^{rs+r}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}},\qquad q=1-d/n,

up to
\exp(o_{d}(n/d))
and harmless polynomial factors from the choice of the anchor. Since
q^{r}\leq 1
, summing over
r,s
and using the
q
-binomial theorem gives one
q
-Pochhammer product,

\sum_{r,s}\frac{q^{rs+r}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\sum_{r,s}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\exp(o_{d}(n/d))\cdot\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

Finally,

-\log{(q;q)_{\infty}}=-\frac{n}{d}\int_{0}^{\infty}\log{(1-e^{-t})}\,dt+o(n/d)=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}\cdot n+o(n/d).

This is the source of the sharp constant.

The same logistic profile also gives the lower bound for the hypercube, but the proof is separate: it integrates over one bipartition class and then uses the available intervals on the other class. The upper bound for the hypercube is different; it uses the homomorphism comparison theorem of Galvin and Tetali
[
4
]
. This yields an auxiliary result,

\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o(d^{-1})\leq\log c(Q_{d})\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}+o(1)\right)\frac{\log d}{d}.

Typical flatness of Lipschitz functions on expanders was studied by Peled, Samotij and Yehudayoff
[
7
]
. Krueger, Li and Park recently extended this line of work to weak expanders and larger Lipschitz constants
[
6
]
. The present paper is different in emphasis: it studies the leading Ehrhart-volume growth constant on sparse random graphs.

Throughout,
o_{d}(1)
denotes a quantity tending to zero as
d\to\infty
, after the limit
n\to\infty
has been taken. We write
p=d/n
and
q=1-p
.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Volume Normalization

For a connected graph
H
and a root
r\in V(H)
, set

P_{H}(r):=\{x\in\mathbb{R}^{V(H)}:x_{r}=0,\ |x(u)-x(v)|\leq 1\text{ for all }uv\in E(H)\}.

The volume of
P_{H}(r)
is independent of the chosen root, since changing the root amounts to changing coordinates in the quotient space
\mathbb{R}^{V(H)}/\langle\mathbf{1}\rangle
by a determinant-one linear map. Hence, for connected
H
,

\Vol(P_{H}(r))=c(H)^{|V(H)|-1}.

For a disconnected graph, the total rooted volume is the product over connected components.

We shall use the following standard facts about
G(n,d/n)
: for fixed
d>1
, with high probability there is a unique giant component
C_{1}
; its complement has
(\varrho_{d}+o(1))n
vertices, where
\varrho_{d}
is the solution of
\varrho=e^{-d(1-\varrho)}
; in particular
|V\setminus C_{1}|=e^{-d+o_{d}(d)}n
as
d\to\infty
. We also use
|E(G)|=(d/2+o(1))n
with high probability. See, for example,
[
3
, Chapters 5–6]
.

2.2
Two Analytic Estimates

The following elementary integral occurs in both the lower and upper bounds.

Lemma 2.1
.

-\int_{0}^{\infty}\log{(1-e^{-t})}\,dt=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

Proof.

Expand
\log(1-e^{-t})=-\sum_{k\geq 1}e^{-kt}/k
and integrate term by term.
∎

We also need the asymptotics of the
q
-Pochhammer product. Write

(q;q)_{r}:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-q^{j}),\qquad(q;q)_{\infty}:=\prod_{j=1}^{\infty}(1-q^{j}).

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
q=1-d/n
, where
d
is fixed and
n\to\infty
. Then

-\log{(q;q)_{\infty}}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}\cdot n+o(n/d).

The error is uniform for all
d
in any range with
d=o(n)
.

Proof.

Put
p=d/n
,
q=1-p
, and
\lambda=-\log q
. Then
\lambda=p+O(p^{2})
, and
q^{j}=e^{-\lambda j}
. Hence

-\log(q;q)_{\infty}=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}-\log(1-q^{j})=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(\lambda j),

where
f(t):=-\log(1-e^{-t})
. The function
f
is positive and decreasing on
(0,\infty)
, so the elementary integral comparison

\int_{\lambda}^{\infty}f(t)\,dt\leq\lambda\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(\lambda j)\leq\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,dt

gives

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(\lambda j)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,dt+O\left(\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\lambda}f(t)\,dt\right).

Near zero,
f(t)=-\log t+O(t)
, and therefore

\int_{0}^{\lambda}f(t)\,dt=O(\lambda\log(1/\lambda)).

Thus

\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}f(\lambda j)=\frac{1}{\lambda}\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,dt+O(\log(1/\lambda)).

By Lemma
2.1
,

\int_{0}^{\infty}f(t)\,dt=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}.

Since
\lambda=p+O(p^{2})
, we have
1/\lambda=1/p+O(1)
, and hence

-\log(q;q)_{\infty}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6p}+O(\log(1/p)).

Finally
p=d/n
, so
1/p=n/d
, and
O(\log(1/p))=o(n/d)
. Therefore

-\log(q;q)_{\infty}=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}\cdot n+o(n/d),

as claimed.
∎

2.3
Concentration Estimates

We shall use the following standard bounded-differences inequalities in two places.

Lemma 2.3

(Bounded differences)
.

Let
Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m}
be independent random variables, and let
F=F(Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{m})
be a real-valued function. Suppose that changing only the
i
-th coordinate can change
F
by at most
c_{i}
. Then, for every
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}(|F-\mathbb{E}\left[F\right]|\geq t)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{\sum_{i}c_{i}^{2}}\right).

Moreover,

\log\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}\left[F\right]+\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}c_{i}^{2}.

Proof.

Let
M_{i}=\mathbb{E}(F\mid Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i})
be the Doob martingale. Its martingale differences satisfy
|M_{i}-M_{i-1}|\leq c_{i}
. The two tail estimates are the Azuma–Hoeffding, or McDiarmid, bounded-differences inequality. For the exponential-moment estimate, Hoeffding’s lemma gives

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{M_{i}-M_{i-1}}\mid Y_{1},\ldots,Y_{i-1}\right]\leq\exp(c_{i}^{2}/8).

Multiplying these conditional estimates over
i
gives

\mathbb{E}\left[e^{F-\mathbb{E}\left[F\right]}\right]\leq\exp\left(\frac{1}{8}\sum_{i}c_{i}^{2}\right),

which is the stated bound.
∎

3
The Lower Bound

The lower bound uses the density

\rho_{d}(x):=\frac{1-e^{-d}}{(1+e^{-dx})(1+e^{d(x-1)})},\qquad x\in\mathbb{R}.

(4)

Its distribution function is

F_{d}(x)=\int_{-\infty}^{x}\rho_{d}(t)\,dt=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\log\left(\frac{1+e^{dx}}{1+e^{d(x-1)}}\right).

(5)

In particular,
\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho_{d}(x)\,dx=1
. The density is close to
1
on
[0,1]
, has a logistic left boundary layer near
0
, and has a logistic right boundary layer near
1
.

For a probability density
\rho
on
\mathbb{R}
, define

H(\rho):=-\int_{\mathbb{R}}\rho(x)\log\rho(x)\,dx,\qquad Q(\rho):=\iint_{|x-y|>1}\rho(x)\rho(y)\,dx\,dy.

(6)

Here
H(\rho)
is the continuous entropy of the profile and
Q(\rho)
is the probability that a random pair of profile values would violate a
1
-Lipschitz edge constraint.

Lemma 3.1

(Logistic boundary layers)
.

The density (
4
) satisfies

\displaystyle H(\rho_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3d}+O(d^{-2}),

(7)

\displaystyle Q(\rho_{d})

\displaystyle=\frac{\pi^{2}}{3d^{2}}+O(d^{-3}).

(8)

Consequently,

H(\rho_{d})-\frac{d}{2}\cdot Q(\rho_{d})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+O(d^{-2}).

(9)

Proof.

Near the right endpoint, put
x=1+t/d
. Then

\rho_{d}(1+t/d)=\frac{1}{1+e^{t}}+O(e^{-d/3})

uniformly for
|t|\leq d^{2/3}
. Near the left endpoint, put
x=t/d
. Then

\rho_{d}(t/d)=\frac{1}{1+e^{-t}}+O(e^{-d/3})

in the same range. Outside the two
d^{-1}
-scale boundary layers, the density is exponentially close either to
1
or to
0
. Hence

H(\rho_{d})=-\frac{2}{d}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{t}}\right)\log\left(\frac{1}{1+e^{t}}\right)\,dt+O(d^{-2}).

With
u=(1+e^{t})^{-1}
, the integral is

-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\log u}{1-u}\,du=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6},

which proves (
7
).

For
Q(\rho_{d})
, by symmetry it is enough to compute
\mathbb{P}(X-Y>1)
and double it. Put
X=1+t/d
. By (
5
), the left-tail mass below
t/d
is

F_{d}(t/d)=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\log\left(\frac{1+e^{t}}{1+e^{t-d}}\right)=\frac{1}{d}\cdot\log(1+e^{t})+O(d^{-2})

uniformly over the contributing range. Therefore

\mathbb{P}(X-Y>1)=\frac{1}{d^{2}}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}\frac{\log(1+e^{t})}{1+e^{t}}\,dt+O(d^{-3})=\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d^{2}}+O(d^{-3}).

Doubling gives (
8
).
∎

We next give a random-graph lower-bound lemma. For a compact interval

I=I_{d,T}:=[-T/d,1+T/d],

write

Z_{I}(G):=\Vol\{x\in I^{[n]}:|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq 1\text{ for every }ij\in E(G)\}.

This is an unrooted volume. Rooting is handled by slicing at the end.

Lemma 3.2

(Random-graph profile lower bound)
.

Let
T=T(d)\to\infty
with
T^{2}=o(d)
, and let
I=I_{d,T}
. For fixed
d
, as
n\to\infty
, with high probability,

\log Z_{I}(G(n,d/n))\geq n\left(H(\rho_{d})-\frac{d}{2}\cdot Q(\rho_{d})+o_{d}(d^{-1})\right).

Here
\rho_{d}
may be replaced by its truncation to
I
and renormalization; this changes
H(\rho_{d})
by
o_{d}(d^{-1})
and
Q(\rho_{d})
by
o_{d}(d^{-2})
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the truncation and renormalization of
\rho_{d}
to
I
, and put

H=H(\rho),\qquad Q=Q(\rho),\qquad W(x)=-\log\rho(x).

For
x\in I^{n}
, let

B(x):=\#\{\{i,j\}:|x_{i}-x_{j}|>1\}.

Averaging over the random graph gives

\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[Z_{I}(G)\right]=\int_{I^{n}}q^{B(x)}\,\mathrm{d}x=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{i=1}^{n}W(X_{i})\right)q^{B(X)}\right],

where
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
are independent with density
\rho
.

For each fixed
d
, the law of large numbers gives

\sum_{i=1}^{n}W(X_{i})=nH+o_{d}(n/d)

with probability tending to one. Also,

\mathbb{E}\left[B(X)\right]=Q\binom{n}{2}.

Since
B(X)
is a sum of bounded pair functions, all summands indexed by disjoint pairs are independent. Hence only
O(n^{3})
pairs of summands can have nonzero covariance, and

\operatorname{Var}B(X)=O_{d}(n^{3}).

Chebyshev’s inequality therefore gives

B(X)=Q\binom{n}{2}+o_{d}(n^{2}/d^{2})

with probability tending to one, because
n^{3/2}=o(n^{2}/d^{2})
for each fixed
d
. Since
\log q=-d/n+O_{d}(n^{-2})
, on the intersection of these two events we have

q^{B(X)}\geq\exp\left(-\frac{d}{n}\cdot Q\binom{n}{2}-o_{d}(n/d)\right)=\exp\left(-\frac{d}{2}\cdot Qn-o_{d}(n/d)\right).

Since the intersection of the two typical events has probability tending to one, its logarithm is
o_{d}(n/d)
. Therefore

\log\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[Z_{I}(G)\right]\geq nH-\frac{d}{2}\cdot Qn-o_{d}(n/d).

(10)

It remains to pass from the mean to high probability. Generate
G(n,d/n)
by independent vertex-exposure blocks, where the block at vertex
i
consists of the edges from
i
to earlier vertices. Let
F(G):=\log Z_{I}(G)
. We claim that changing one exposure block changes
F
by at most
CT/d
.

Indeed, suppose that only the block at
i
is changed. Fix all coordinates except
x_{i}
. The constraints not involving
i
are unchanged. The constraints involving
i
restrict
x_{i}
to

I\cap\bigcap_{j\in J}[x_{j}-1,x_{j}+1]

for some set
J
of neighbors, including the fixed later-neighbor constraints and whichever earlier-neighbor constraints are present. Since every
x_{j}\in I=[-T/d,1+T/d]
, this interval has length between
1-2T/d
and
1+2T/d
. Hence the two possible fiber lengths, before and after the block change, differ pointwise by a factor at most

\frac{1+2T/d}{1-2T/d}.

Integrating over the other coordinates gives

|F(G)-F(G^{\prime})|\leq\log\left(\frac{1+2T/d}{1-2T/d}\right)\leq C\cdot\frac{T}{d}

for all sufficiently large
d
.

Applying Lemma
2.3
with
n
exposure blocks and
c_{i}=CT/d
gives, for every
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}\left(|F(G)-\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[F(G)\right]|\geq t\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{nC^{2}T^{2}/d^{2}}\right).

Taking
t=\eta n/d
and then letting
n\to\infty
for each fixed
d
gives

F(G)=\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[F(G)\right]+o_{p}(n/d).

The exponential-moment part of Lemma
2.3
gives

\log\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[e^{F(G)}\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[F(G)\right]+O\left(\frac{nT^{2}}{d^{2}}\right).

Since
e^{F(G)}=Z_{I}(G)
and
T^{2}=o(d)
, the error term is
o_{d}(n/d)
. Combining this with (
10
) yields

\mathbb{E}_{G}\left[F(G)\right]\geq nH-\frac{d}{2}\cdot Qn-o_{d}(n/d).

Using the concentration estimate for
F(G)
once more gives the claimed high-probability lower bound.
∎

Theorem 3.3

(Random-graph lower bound)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
d_{0}=d_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that, for every
d\geq d_{0}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\log c(G(n,d/n))\geq\frac{\pi^{2}/6-\varepsilon}{d}\right)=1.

Proof.

Choose
T=T(d)\to\infty
with
T^{2}=o(d)
, for instance
T=\log d
, and let
I=I_{d,T}
. By Lemma
3.2
and Lemma
3.1
, with high probability,

Z_{I}(G)\geq\exp\left(n\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o_{d}(d^{-1})\right)\right).

This is an unrooted volume of valid assignments lying in
I^{n}
.

Let
k
be the number of connected components of
G
. With high probability,
k\leq 1+|V\setminus C_{1}|=e^{-d+o_{d}(d)}n+1
. Slicing the unrooted set by the root coordinate in each component loses at most a factor
|I|^{k}
. Since

\log|I|^{k}=k\log(1+2T/d)=o_{d}(n/d),

some rooted slice has volume at least

\exp\left(n\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o_{d}(d^{-1})\right)\right).

Translating each component separately sends that slice into the rooted Lipschitz polytope. Finally,
|V|-k=n-o_{d}(n/d)
on the same exponential scale, so (
1
) gives the claimed lower bound.
∎

4
The Upper Bound

4.1
Anchored Flatness

The upper bound begins with the expansion input already used in
[
5
]
. We include the short proof for completeness.

Lemma 4.1

(Flatness)
.

There is an absolute constant
K
such that the following holds. Put

\alpha=\frac{K\log d}{d},\qquad R=\lceil 2\alpha n\rceil.

With high probability, every
1
-Lipschitz function
x
on the giant component
C_{1}
of
G(n,d/n)
has a vertex
v\in C_{1}
such that, after subtracting
x_{v}
, all but at most
R
vertices of
C_{1}
have values in
[0,1]
.

Proof.

Let
m=\lceil\alpha n\rceil
. Let
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
be the event that every two disjoint subsets
A,B\subseteq[n]
with
|A|=|B|=m
have at least one edge between them. For fixed such
A,B
,

\mathbb{P}(E(A,B)=\varnothing)=(1-d/n)^{m^{2}}\leq\exp(-d\alpha^{2}n+O(d\alpha)).

The number of possible pairs is at most

\binom{n}{m}^{2}\leq\exp(2h_{\rm bin}(\alpha)n+o(n)),

where
h_{\rm bin}
is the binary entropy function. Since

h_{\rm bin}(\alpha)=\alpha\log(1/\alpha)+O(\alpha)=K\cdot\left(\frac{\log^{2}d+O(\log d\log\log d)}{d}\right)

and

d\alpha^{2}=K^{2}\cdot\frac{\log^{2}d}{d},

choosing
K
sufficiently large gives
\mathbb{P}(\mathcal{E}_{\alpha})\to 1
.

Assume
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
. Let
x:C_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
be
1
-Lipschitz. Let
a
be the
m
-th smallest value of
x
on
C_{1}
, and choose
v\in C_{1}
with
x_{v}=a
. Then at least
m
vertices have value at most
a
, and at most
m-1
vertices have value below
a
. If at least
m
vertices had value greater than
a+1
, then the set of vertices with value at most
a
and the set of vertices with value greater than
a+1
would be two disjoint sets of size at least
m
with no edge between them, contradicting
\mathcal{E}_{\alpha}
. Hence at most
m-1
vertices have value greater than
a+1
. Therefore all but at most
2m-2\leq R
vertices lie in
[a,a+1]
.
∎

4.2
The One-Tail Integral

The main enumeration step is a Gaussian-binomial identity. For
0<q<1
and integers
N,r\geq 0
, define

A_{N,r}(q):=\int_{[0,1]^{N}}\int_{[0,1]^{r}}q^{\#\{(i,u):y_{u}>t_{i}\}}\,dy\,dt.

The Gaussian binomial coefficient is

\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N+r}{r}_{q}:=\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1-q^{N+j}}{1-q^{j}}.

Lemma 4.2

(One-tail
q
-binomial identity)
.

For all
N,r\geq 0
,

A_{N,r}(q)=\binom{N+r}{r}^{-1}\genfrac{[}{]}{0.0pt}{}{N+r}{r}_{q}.

(11)

Consequently,

A_{N,r}(q)\leq\binom{N+r}{r}^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1-q^{j}}.

(12)

Proof.

Order the
N+r
real variables
t_{1},\ldots,t_{N},y_{1},\ldots,y_{r}
. Each relative order is equally likely under Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{N+r}
. The statistic
\#\{(i,u):y_{u}>t_{i}\}
is the inversion number of the corresponding binary word with
N
letters of one type and
r
letters of the other type. The generating function of this inversion statistic is the Gaussian binomial coefficient; see, for example,
[
2
, Chapter 1]
or
[
8
, Section 1.7]
. Dividing by the number
\binom{N+r}{r}
of binary words gives (
11
). The inequality follows from
1-q^{N+j}\leq 1
.
∎

The following correlation observation lets the two tails decouple before the cross-tail edge penalty is applied.

Lemma 4.3

(Opposite monotonicities)
.

Fix
N,r,s\geq 0
and
0<q<1
. For
t\in[0,1]^{N}
, set

B_{r}(t):=\int_{[0,1]^{r}}q^{\#\{(i,u):y_{u}>t_{i}\}}\,dy,

and

C_{s}(t):=\int_{[0,1]^{s}}q^{\#\{(i,w):z_{w}>1-t_{i}\}}\,dz.

Then

\int_{[0,1]^{N}}B_{r}(t)C_{s}(t)\,dt\leq A_{N,r}(q)A_{N,s}(q).

Proof.

The function
B_{r}(t)
is increasing in each coordinate
t_{i}
, while
C_{s}(t)
is decreasing in each coordinate. Under product Lebesgue measure on
[0,1]^{N}
, increasing and decreasing functions are negatively correlated. Applying this elementary form of the FKG/Chebyshev correlation inequality gives the claim.
∎

4.3
Summing the Two Tails

We now prove the summation bound which produces the constant. Recall that

(q;q)_{r}=\prod_{j=1}^{r}(1-q^{j}).

Lemma 4.4

(
q
-Pochhammer summation)
.

Let
q=1-d/n
and let
R=O(n\log d/d)
. Then

\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq R}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\exp(o_{d}(n/d))\cdot\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

(13)

Consequently,

\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq R}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\exp\left(\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+o_{d}(1)\right)\cdot\frac{n}{d}\right).

(14)

Proof.

We use the
q
-binomial theorem in the form

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{z^{s}}{(q;q)_{s}}=\frac{1}{(z;q)_{\infty}},\qquad|z|<1.

(15)

For
r\geq 1
, take
z=q^{r}
. Then

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{s}}=\frac{1}{(q^{r};q)_{\infty}}.

Therefore

\sum_{s=0}^{\infty}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{r}(q^{r};q)_{\infty}}=\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}(1-q^{r})}.

Since
1-q^{r}\geq 1-q=d/n
, summing over
1\leq r\leq R
gives

\sum_{1\leq r\leq R}\sum_{s=0}^{R}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\frac{R}{1-q}\cdot\frac{1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

The row
r=0
is bounded by

\sum_{s=0}^{R}\frac{1}{(q;q)_{s}}\leq\frac{R+1}{(q;q)_{\infty}}.

Since
R=O(n\log d/d)
, the logarithm of the prefactor is
o(n/d)
. This proves (
13
). Equation (
14
) follows from Lemma
2.2
.
∎

4.4
The Anchored Volume Bound

We next define the random variable whose expectation will bound the volume of all anchored flat functions. Fix an anchor
a\in[n]
. For a vector
x\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
with
x_{a}=0
, partition the vertices into

\displaystyle S(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i:x_{i}\in[0,1]\},

\displaystyle U(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i:x_{i}\in(1,2]\},

\displaystyle W(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i:x_{i}\in[-1,0)\},

\displaystyle D(x)

\displaystyle:=\{i:x_{i}>2\text{ or }x_{i}<-1\}.

We shall only count vectors with
a\in S(x)
and
|U(x)|+|W(x)|+|D(x)|\leq R
.

A vector arising from a Lipschitz function on the giant component has the following additional property: every vertex of
D(x)
is connected, through vertices outside
S(x)
, to
U(x)\cup W(x)
. Indeed, no edge from
D(x)
to
S(x)
can be Lipschitz, so a path from a deep vertex to the central set must first pass through the first upper or lower layer. To overcount such vectors, we allow a directed forest: every vertex of
D
chooses a parent in
D\cup U\cup W
, and repeated parent maps eventually reach
U\cup W
. There are at most
n^{|D|}
such parent maps.

Let
Z_{R}(G)
be the sum, over anchors
a
, over all choices of disjoint sets
S,U,W,D
with
a\in S
and
|U|+|W|+|D|\leq R
, and over all such directed forests from
D
to
U\cup W
, of the volume of vectors satisfying:

(i)

x_{a}=0
;

(ii)

x_{i}\in[0,1]
for
i\in S
,
x_{i}\in(1,2]
for
i\in U
, and
x_{i}\in[-1,0)
for
i\in W
;

(iii)

for every forest edge
ij
,
|x_{i}-x_{j}|\leq 1
and the graph edge
ij
is present;

(iv)

every non-forest graph edge
ij
with
|x_{i}-x_{j}|>1
is absent.

The definition overcounts the actual anchored Lipschitz volume, which is useful for an upper bound.

Lemma 4.5

(Expected anchored flat volume)
.

Let
R=O(n\log d/d)
. Then

\mathbb{E}\left[Z_{R}(G(n,d/n))\right]\leq\exp\left(\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+o_{d}(1)\right)\frac{n}{d}\right).

(16)

Proof.

Fix an anchor
a
and fixed disjoint sets
S,U,W,D
with
a\in S
. Write

N=|S|,\qquad r=|U|,\qquad s=|W|,\qquad m=|D|.

First take
m=0
. For
i\in S\setminus\{a\}
, write
x_{i}=t_{i}\in[0,1]
; for
u\in U
, write
x_{u}=1+y_{u}
with
y_{u}\in[0,1]
; for
w\in W
, write
x_{w}=-z_{w}
with
z_{w}\in[0,1]
.

An edge
ui
with
u\in U
and
i\in S\setminus\{a\}
is forbidden precisely when
y_{u}>t_{i}
. An edge
wi
with
w\in W
and
i\in S\setminus\{a\}
is forbidden precisely when
z_{w}>1-t_{i}
. Every edge between
U
and
W
is forbidden, except on a measure-zero boundary, because

(1+y_{u})-(-z_{w})=1+y_{u}+z_{w}>1.

The anchor contributes asymmetrically: every edge from
a
to
U
is forbidden, except on a measure-zero boundary, while every edge from
a
to
W
is compatible. Therefore the expected volume for these fixed sets is at most

q^{rs+r}\int_{[0,1]^{N-1}}\int_{[0,1]^{r}}\int_{[0,1]^{s}}q^{\#\{(i,u):y_{u}>t_{i}\}+\#\{(i,w):z_{w}>1-t_{i}\}}\,\mathrm{d}z\,\,\mathrm{d}y\,\,\mathrm{d}t.

By Lemma
4.3
and Lemma
4.2
, the integral is at most

q^{rs+r}\binom{N-1+r}{r}^{-1}\binom{N-1+s}{s}^{-1}\prod_{j=1}^{r}\frac{1}{1-q^{j}}\prod_{j=1}^{s}\frac{1}{1-q^{j}}.

(17)

Now sum over choices of the anchor and over choices of
S,U,W
with
m=0
and fixed
r,s
. The number of choices after the anchor is fixed is

\frac{(n-1)!}{(N-1)!r!s!}.

Multiplying by the two binomial denominators in (
17
) and then summing over the
n
possible anchors leaves the factor

n\cdot\frac{(n-1)!}{(N-1)!r!s!}\binom{N-1+r}{r}^{-1}\binom{N-1+s}{s}^{-1}=\frac{n!(N-1)!}{(N+r-1)!(N+s-1)!}.

Since
r,s\leq R=O(n\log d/d)
, Stirling’s formula gives

\log\left(\frac{n!(N-1)!}{(N+r-1)!(N+s-1)!}\right)=O\left(\log n+\frac{(r+s)^{2}}{n}\right)=o_{d}(n/d).

Thus the total contribution with
m=0
is at most

\exp(o_{d}(n/d))\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq R}\frac{q^{rs+r}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}\leq\exp(o_{d}(n/d))\sum_{0\leq r,s\leq R}\frac{q^{rs}}{(q;q)_{r}(q;q)_{s}}.

(18)

By Lemma
4.4
, this is at most the right-hand side of (
16
).

It remains to check that deep vertices do not change the exponential rate. Fix
D
with
|D|=m
. Since each vertex in
D
is at distance more than one from every central vertex in
S
, all edges between
D
and
S
must be absent. This gives a factor

q^{mN}\leq\exp(-(1-o_{d}(1))dm),

because
N\geq n-R-m\geq(1-o_{d}(1))n
for the terms under consideration. The directed forest from
D
to
U\cup W
has at most
n^{m}
choices and its edges are present with probability
p^{m}
, giving a factor at most
d^{m}
. Given the non-deep coordinates and the forest, the deep coordinates are parametrized by the forest edge increments, each lying in an interval of length at most
2
; hence their volume is at most
2^{m}
.

After also choosing the set
D
, the total deep multiplier is bounded by

\sum_{m\leq R}\binom{n}{m}(2d)^{m}\exp(-(1-o_{d}(1))dm).

(19)

Let

a_{d}=(2d)\exp(-(1-o_{d}(1))d).

Then (
19
) is at most

\sum_{m=0}^{n}\binom{n}{m}a_{d}^{m}=(1+a_{d})^{n}.

Thus its logarithm is at most

na_{d}=n\exp(-d+O(\log d))=o_{d}(n/d).

Multiplying this by the non-deep estimate (
18
) proves (
16
).
∎

4.5
Completing the Upper Bound

Theorem 4.6

(Upper bound)
.

For every
\varepsilon>0
there is
d_{0}=d_{0}(\varepsilon)
such that, for every
d\geq d_{0}
,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{P}\left(\log c(G(n,d/n))\leq\frac{\pi^{2}/6+\varepsilon}{d}\right)=1.

Proof.

Let
C_{1}
be the giant component. On the flatness event of Lemma
4.1
, every
1
-Lipschitz function on
C_{1}
is covered by one of the anchored classes counted by
Z_{R}(G)
: choose the anchor
v
supplied by Lemma
4.1
, subtract
x_{v}
, and place all vertices outside
C_{1}
arbitrarily in the central interval
[0,1]
, which has volume one in each coordinate. Changing from a fixed root to the chosen anchor is a determinant-one coordinate change in the quotient by constant functions. Thus the rooted volume of
C_{1}
is bounded by the anchored flat volume counted by
Z_{R}(G)
, and

\Vol(P_{C_{1}})\leq Z_{R}(G)

(20)

on the flatness event.

By Lemma
4.5
and Markov’s inequality, with high probability

Z_{R}(G)\leq\exp\left(\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n}{d}\right)

for all sufficiently large
d
. Hence

\Vol(P_{C_{1}})\leq\exp\left(\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\right)\frac{n}{d}\right)

with high probability.

The components outside
C_{1}
contain
e^{-d+o_{d}(d)}n
vertices with high probability. Since every connected component on
m
vertices has rooted Lipschitz volume at most
2^{m-1}
, the total contribution of the non-giant components is

\exp(o_{d}(n/d)).

Combining this with (
20
) gives

\Vol(P_{G})\leq\exp\left(\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6}+\varepsilon\right)\frac{n}{d}\right)

with high probability. Since
|V|-k=n-o_{d}(n/d)
in the exponent scale relevant here, (
1
) gives

\log c(G(n,d/n))\leq\frac{\pi^{2}/6+\varepsilon}{d}

with high probability.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

The lower bound is Theorem
3.3
and the upper bound is Theorem
4.6
. Since
e^{x}=1+x+O(x^{2})
for
x=O(d^{-1})
, the logarithmic statement is equivalent to

c(G(n,d/n))=1+\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o_{d}(d^{-1}).

∎

5
Hypercubes

Let
Q_{d}
denote the
d
-dimensional hypercube. We record a lower bound from the logistic profile and an upper bound from the Galvin–Tetali homomorphism comparison theorem
[
4
]
.

Lemma 5.1

(Neighbor extreme values)
.

Let
T=T(d)\to\infty
with
T^{2}=o(d)
, let

I=[-T/d,1+T/d],

and let
\rho
be the truncation and renormalization of
\rho_{d}
to
I
. Let
X_{1},\ldots,X_{d}
be independent samples from
\rho
, and set

M=\max_{i}X_{i},\qquad m=\min_{i}X_{i},\qquad R=m+1-M.

Then

\mathbb{E}\left[R\right]=o_{d}(d^{-1}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[R^{2}\right]=O(d^{-2}),

and consequently

\mathbb{E}\left[\log(2-(M-m))\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[\log(1+R)\right]\geq-o_{d}(d^{-1}).

Proof.

First use the untruncated density
\rho_{d}
, and let
\widetilde{M}
be the maximum of
d
independent samples. For fixed
t\in\mathbb{R}
, the exact distribution function (
5
) gives

F_{d}(1+t/d)=1-\frac{\log(1+e^{-t})}{d}+o(d^{-1}).

Hence

\mathbb{P}\bigl(d(\widetilde{M}-1)\leq t\bigr)=F_{d}(1+t/d)^{d}\longrightarrow\exp(-\log(1+e^{-t}))=\frac{1}{1+e^{-t}}.

The same formula gives exponential tail bounds. Indeed, for
t\geq 0
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(d(\widetilde{M}-1)>t\bigr)\leq d\left(1-F_{d}(1+t/d)\right)\leq Ce^{-t},

and, for
0\leq t\leq d/2
,

\mathbb{P}\bigl(d(\widetilde{M}-1)<-t\bigr)=F_{d}(1-t/d)^{d}\leq Ce^{-t},

while the remaining range
t>d/2
is exponentially small in
d
. Thus
d(\widetilde{M}-1)
converges in
L^{2}
to the standard logistic law, which has mean zero and finite second moment.

The probability that one of the
d
samples lies outside
I
is
O(e^{-T})
, by the same tail estimates. Since
T\to\infty
, truncating and renormalizing does not change the
L^{2}
limit. Therefore, under the truncated law,

d(M-1)\to Y\quad\text{in }L^{2},

where
Y
is standard logistic. The truncated density is symmetric under
x\mapsto 1-x
, so
dm
has the same distribution as
-d(M-1)
. Since
\mathbb{E}\left[Y\right]=0
and
\mathbb{E}\left[Y^{2}\right]<\infty
,

\mathbb{E}\left[R\right]=\mathbb{E}\left[m+1-M\right]=o_{d}(d^{-1}),\qquad\mathbb{E}\left[R^{2}\right]=O(d^{-2}).

Finally
R\in[-2T/d,1]
, so for large
d
we have
R\geq-1/2
. The elementary inequality

\log(1+r)\geq r-r^{2}\qquad(-1/2\leq r\leq 1)

gives

\mathbb{E}\left[\log(1+R)\right]\geq\mathbb{E}\left[R\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[R^{2}\right]=-o_{d}(d^{-1}).

∎

Theorem 5.2

(Hypercube bounds)
.

As
d\to\infty
,

\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o(d^{-1})\leq\log c(Q_{d})\leq\left(\frac{3}{4}+o(1)\right)\frac{\log d}{d}.

Proof.

We first prove the lower bound. Let
N=2^{d}
, and write the bipartition of
Q_{d}
as
A\cup B
, with
|A|=|B|=N/2
. By symmetry we may root later at a vertex in
A
. Choose
T=T(d)\to\infty
with
T^{2}=o(d)
, for instance
T=\log d
, and let
\rho
be the truncation and renormalization of
\rho_{d}
to

I=[-T/d,1+T/d].

For
x\in I^{A}
and
b\in B
, define

\ell_{b}(x):=\left|\bigcap_{a\sim b}[x_{a}-1,x_{a}+1]\right|.

Since all values
x_{a}
lie in an interval of length
1+2T/d<2
, the interval above is nonempty and

1-2T/d\leq\ell_{b}(x)\leq 2.

Thus

Z_{Q}:=\int_{I^{A}}\prod_{b\in B}\ell_{b}(x)\,\mathrm{d}x_{A}

is an unrooted volume of valid Lipschitz assignments on
Q_{d}
: after choosing the values on
A
, each vertex
b\in B
may be chosen independently in the interval of length
\ell_{b}(x)
.

Let
X_{a}
,
a\in A
, be independent with density
\rho
, and put
W=-\log\rho
. Then

Z_{Q}=\mathbb{E}_{\rho}\left[\exp\left(\sum_{a\in A}W(X_{a})\right)\prod_{b\in B}\ell_{b}(X)\right].

Since
W
is bounded by
O(T)
under the truncated law, changing one variable
X_{a}
changes
\sum_{a\in A}W(X_{a})
by at most
CT
. Lemma
2.3
gives

\mathbb{P}\left(\sum_{a\in A}W(X_{a})-|A|H(\rho)\leq-t\right)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{(N/2)C^{2}T^{2}}\right).

Taking
t=\gamma_{d}N/d
with
\gamma_{d}\downarrow 0
and
\gamma_{d}^{2}N/(d^{2}T^{2})\to\infty
gives

\sum_{a\in A}W(X_{a})\geq|A|H(\rho)-o_{d}(N/d)

(21)

with probability tending to one.

We next control the product of interval lengths. Fix
b\in B
, and let
M_{b}
and
m_{b}
be the maximum and minimum of the
d
neighboring values
X_{a}
. Then

\ell_{b}=2-(M_{b}-m_{b}).

By Lemma
5.1
,

\mathbb{E}\left[\log\ell_{b}\right]\geq-o_{d}(d^{-1}).

(22)

Now set

S(X):=\sum_{b\in B}\log\ell_{b}(X).

Linearity of expectation and (
22
) give

\mathbb{E}\left[S\right]\geq-o_{d}(N/d).

Changing one coordinate
X_{a}
affects only the
d
terms with
b\sim a
. Each term
\log\ell_{b}
lies between
\log(1-2T/d)
and
\log 2
, so changing one coordinate can change
S
by at most
Cd
. Applying Lemma
2.3
with
|A|=N/2
variables and
c_{a}=Cd
gives, for every
t>0
,

\mathbb{P}(S-\mathbb{E}\left[S\right]\leq-t)\leq\exp\left(-\frac{2t^{2}}{(N/2)C^{2}d^{2}}\right).

Choose any
\eta_{d}\downarrow 0
with
\eta_{d}^{2}N/d^{4}\to\infty
. Taking
t=\eta_{d}N/d
gives

S(X)\geq-o_{d}(N/d)

(23)

with probability tending to one.

On the intersection
\mathcal{E}
of the high-probability events (
21
) and (
23
),

\exp\left(\sum_{a\in A}W(X_{a})\right)\prod_{b\in B}\ell_{b}(X)\geq\exp\left(|A|H(\rho)-o_{d}(N/d)\right).

Since
\mathbb{P}_{\rho}(\mathcal{E})\to 1
, its logarithm is
o_{d}(N/d)
. Therefore

Z_{Q}\geq\mathbb{P}_{\rho}(\mathcal{E})\exp\left(|A|H(\rho)-o_{d}(N/d)\right)=\exp\left(|A|H(\rho)-o_{d}(N/d)\right).

Since
|A|=N/2
,
H(\rho)=H(\rho_{d})+o_{d}(d^{-1})
, and Lemma
3.1
gives
H(\rho_{d})=\pi^{2}/(3d)+O(d^{-2})
, we obtain

Z_{Q}\geq\exp\left(N\left(\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o(d^{-1})\right)\right).

Slicing by the coordinate of a root in
A
loses only a factor
|I|=1+o_{d}(1)
, so

\log c(Q_{d})\geq\frac{\pi^{2}}{6d}+o(d^{-1}).

We prove the upper bound. Let
N=2^{d}
, fix a root
o\in V(Q_{d})
, and let
h
be a positive integer. Choose a fixed integer
L\geq 5
and set
M=Lh
. Let
T_{M,h}
be the graph with vertex set
\mathbb{Z}/M\mathbb{Z}
, with a loop at every vertex, and with an edge between two vertices when their cyclic distance is at most
h
.

We first observe that rooted
h
-Lipschitz functions on
Q_{d}
are in bijection with homomorphisms
\varphi:Q_{d}\to T_{M,h}
satisfying
\varphi(o)=0
. Reduction modulo
M
gives one direction. Conversely, given such a homomorphism, every oriented edge
uv
has a unique lift increment
a_{uv}\in[-h,h]\cap\mathbb{Z}
satisfying

\varphi(v)-\varphi(u)\equiv a_{uv}\pmod{M},

because
M>2h
. Around every
4
-cycle, the sum of the four lifted increments is congruent to
0
modulo
M
and has absolute value at most
4h<M
, so it is actually
0
. Since the cycle space of the hypercube is generated by its
4
-cycles, the lifted increments have zero sum around every cycle. Integrating them from the root gives a unique rooted integer-valued
h
-Lipschitz function.

The target
T_{M,h}
is vertex-transitive. Hence the number
N_{Q_{d}}(h)
of rooted
h
-Lipschitz functions on
Q_{d}
is

N_{Q_{d}}(h)=\frac{\Hom(Q_{d},T_{M,h})}{M}.

(24)

By the Galvin–Tetali theorem, for every finite
d
-regular bipartite graph
G
and every finite target graph
H
with loops allowed,

\Hom(G,H)\leq\Hom(K_{d,d},H)^{|V(G)|/(2d)}.

Applying this with
G=Q_{d}
and
H=T_{M,h}
gives

\Hom(Q_{d},T_{M,h})\leq\Hom(K_{d,d},T_{M,h})^{N/(2d)}.

(25)

The same lifting argument applies to
K_{d,d}
, whose cycle space is also generated by
4
-cycles. Therefore

\Hom(K_{d,d},T_{M,h})=MN_{K_{d,d}}(h)=LV_{d}h^{2d}+O_{d}(h^{2d-1}),

where

V_{d}:=\Vol(P_{K_{d,d}})

with one vertex of
K_{d,d}
rooted. Combining this with (
24
) and (
25
), then taking the leading coefficient of
h^{N-1}
, gives

c(Q_{d})^{N-1}\leq L^{-1}(LV_{d})^{N/(2d)}.

(26)

It remains to estimate
V_{d}
.

Root one vertex in the left part of
K_{d,d}
. If the values on the left part have range
r\leq 2
, then every vertex in the right part has an available interval of length
2-r
. The volume of the set of left-part values with root fixed and range at most
r
is
dr^{d-1}
; differentiating in
r
gives

V_{d}=d(d-1)\int_{0}^{2}r^{d-2}(2-r)^{d}\,dr=2^{2d-1}d(d-1)B(d-1,d+1).

(27)

By Stirling’s formula,

V_{d}\sim\sqrt{\pi}\,d^{3/2}.

Taking logarithms in (
26
) and using
N=2^{d}
yields

\log c(Q_{d})\leq\frac{\log V_{d}+O(1)}{2d}+o(1/d)=\left(\frac{3}{4}+o(1)\right)\frac{\log d}{d}.

This proves the theorem.
∎

Remark 5.3
.

The hypercube should not be viewed as a random-graph analogue with the same proof. In the Erdos–Renyi case the sharp upper bound comes from the abundance and near-independence of potential edges between separated level sets. This mechanism is absent in
Q_{d}
, whose edges have rigid product structure. In particular,
Q_{d}
admits
1
-Lipschitz functions of large range, so one cannot first prove a global flatness statement of the kind used for random graphs. The estimates in this section therefore use a different, comparison-based argument. By contrast, the lower bound uses the logistic profile through the bipartite structure of the cube.
