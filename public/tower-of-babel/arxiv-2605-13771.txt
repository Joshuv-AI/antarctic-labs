Upper Bounds for Symmetric Approximate Bounded Indistinguishability
Christopher Williamson

Abstract
A pair of probability distributions over { 0 , 1 } n \{0,1\}^{n} is said to be ( k , δ ) (k,\delta) -wise indistinguishable if all of the size k k marginals are within statistical distance at most δ \delta . Previous works introduced this concept and study when and how well one can distinguish between such a pair of symmetric distributions by observing t t bits. We use a simple hypergeometric smoothing approach and Hahn polynomials to obtain new upper bounds that apply across a wider range of parameters and improve previously available bounds in several regimes. In particular, prior works left open the basic question of whether there exist constants 0 < c 1 < c 2 < 1 0<c_{1}<c_{2}<1 and a pair of ( c 1 ​ n , 0 ) (c_{1}n,0) -wise indistinguishable distributions such that the c 2 ​ n c_{2}n -wise marginals have statistical distance Ω ⁡ ( 1 ) \Omega(1) . One application of our new bounds is to rule this out for all c 1 , c 2 c_{1},c_{2} and to show that the c 2 ​ n c_{2}n -wise marginals must in fact be exponentially close. Another application in this setting is to show that the c 2 ​ n c_{2}n -wise marginals must be super-polynomially close even if the c 1 ​ n c_{1}n -wise marginals are allowed to have statistical distance δ \delta for any δ ≤ exp ⁡ ( − ω ⁡ ( n ​ log ⁡ n ) ) \delta\leq\exp\left({-\omega(\sqrt{n\log{n}})}\right) . Our bounds also yield new results in other regimes, for example when k k is sublinear or when t / n t/n tends to 1.



1
Introduction

We consider pairs of distributions (
\mu,\nu
) over
\{0,1\}^{n}
.
The distributions
\mu
and
\nu
are said to be
(k,\delta)
-wise
indistinguishable if for any subset
S\subseteq[n]
of size at most
k
,
the marginal distributions
\mu_{S}
and
\nu_{S}
over indices in
S
are
of statistical distance at most
\delta
. The distributions are
t
-wise reconstructible with advantage
\epsilon
if there exists a set
S\subseteq[n]
of indices of size
t
and a
statistical test
T:\{0,1\}^{t}\to\{0,1\}
such that

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}[T(X_{S})]-\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\nu}[T(Y_{S})]\right|\geq\epsilon,

where
X_{S}
is the restriction of random variable
X
to the bits located
at the indices in
S
. The distributions are symmetric if
\mu
and

\nu
are invariant under permutation (see Section
2.2
); for
such distributions the size of
S
is relevant but not
the choice of indices.

Cryptographic motivation.

Work of Bogdanov et al.
[
1
,
3
]
considered this notion of
indistinguishability as a way to capture cryptographic secret sharing
schemes in a minimal setting. Their observation was that a single bit secret
can be shared by sampling
n
bits from
\mu
or from
\nu
, depending on
the secret:
(k,0)
-wise indistinguishability of the distributions provides
a security guarantee that any size
\leq k
coalition of colluding parties
learns nothing about the secret from their joint shares. The secret
reconstruction function for the scheme is a test
T
applied over the shares
of sufficiently many (possibly all) parties. Their work proves the existence of distributions over
\Sigma^{n}
where
\Sigma
consists of bit strings of length polynomial in
n
. In this work we only consider distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
.

The special case of
T=\mathrm{OR}
captures the notion of visual
cryptography, introduced by Naor and Shamir
[
7
]
in 1994. Their work
considered
(k,0)
-wise indistinguishable distributions and attempted to
maximise the reconstruction advantage of
\mathrm{OR}
taken over
k+1

bits. They gave a tight bound for all
k
in the special case
k=n-1
; they
also derived a non-tight result for general
k
and
n
. Krause and
Simon
[
6
]
gave improved upper and lower bounds for general
k
and
n
, but these bounds are exponentially far apart when
k=\Omega(n)
. In
this work, we consider the statistical distance between the distributions, which must capture the power of general tests
T
.

Prior work.

Bogdanov et al.
[
2
]
and Huang and Viola
[
5
]
considered
the extent to which symmetric
k
-wise indistinguishable distributions must
have statistically close
t
-wise marginals for
t>k
. In particular,

[
2
]
shows that if
\mu
and
\nu
are symmetric over
n
-bit
strings and perfectly
k
-wise indistinguishable, then the statistical
distance between
t
-wise marginals is at most

O(k^{3/2})\cdot e^{-k^{2}/1156t}

for all
k<t\leq n/64
. The analogous result in
[
5
]
gives a similar
bound and also applies to
t
at most some (unspecified) constant fraction
of
n
.
In the sharp reconstruction setting where
t=k+1
,
[
8
]
gives the upper
bound

n^{O(1)}\cdot\frac{(n-t)^{(n-t)/2}(n+t)^{(n+t)/2}}{2^{t}\,n^{n}}.

We observe that these results leave open the question of whether there exist constants
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<1
and a pair of symmetric distributions
\mu,\nu
over
\{0,1\}^{n}
that are
c_{1}n
-wise indistinguishable yet
c_{2}n
-wise reconstructible with advantage
e^{-o(n)}
or even
\Omega(1)
.

Our contribution.

We study both the regimes of
(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishability for
\delta\neq 0
and without requiring that reconstruction parameter
t=k+1
. We extend prior results, which do not provide upper bounds on the reconstruction advantage of distributions for all
t
and
k
. Our bound is strong enough to answer the question above in the negative.
Our main contribution is the following theorem, from which more easily interpretable corollaries easily follow.

Theorem 1
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be symmetric distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
which are

(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishable. Then, for
integers
0\leq s\leq k<t<n
, the reconstruction advantage over
t
bits is at most

\sqrt{n+1}\,\exp{\left(-\frac{(n-t)(s+1)(s+2)}{2n(t+s+2)}\right)}+\delta\,\sqrt{2(s+1)}\cdot 2^{s}.

(1)

By setting
\delta=0
and
s=k
we immediately obtain:

Corollary 1
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be symmetric distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
which are
(k,0)
-wise indistinguishable. Set integers
0\leq k<t\leq n
.
Then, the reconstruction advantage over
t
bits is at most

\sqrt{n+1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n-t)(k+1)(k+2)}{2n(t+k+2)}\right).

This is already a more general result than previously known. In the parameter regime in which
t
is at most some fixed constant fraction of
n
, we recover the bound as in
[
2
]
in which the rate of decay in the exponent is governed (ignoring constants) by
-k^{2}/t
. In the case that
k
is at least some fixed constant fraction of
n
, we see that the behaviour is governed (ignoring constants) by the exponent
-(n-t)
.

In the setting that
\delta\neq 0
, we are interested in fixing
k
and then maximising
\delta
so that the statistical distance over
t
-wise marginals still must be superpolynomially small. We select two different choices of
s
and apply Theorem
1
to obtain two upper bounds for approximate bounded indistinguishability. These corollaries extend results in
[
5
]
, which only consider reconstruction by all
n
bits and require
\delta
to be smaller than our bounds. Our two corollaries are stated in Section
5
and handle the cases in which
t
is at most a fixed constant fraction of
n
and in which
t
approaches
n
, respectively. To gain intuition for now, we state just one result which is less general than what we prove in Section
5
.

Corollary 2
.

Fix constants
0<c_{1}<c_{2}<1
and let
k=\lfloor c_{1}n\rfloor
and
t=\lfloor c_{2}n\rfloor
.

Suppose
\mu,\nu
are symmetric
(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishable
distributions on
\{0,1\}^{n}
, and suppose

\delta\leq\exp\!\bigl(-\omega(\sqrt{n\log n})\bigr).

Then, the
t
-wise reconstruction advantage is at most
n^{-\omega(1)}
.

Techniques and roadmap.

The established technique to develop indistinguishability upper bounds for
symmetric distributions is to provide low-degree polynomial approximations to any candidate statistical test. Some works, such as
[
2
]
, employ Chebyshev polynomials to construct approximations. However, these polynomials are best when approximating functions over continuous domains. The use of discrete Chebyshev polynomials, as in
[
8
]
, enabled sharper bounds but in a more constrained parameter setting, considering that these polynomials are harder to work with. The key method of this work is to notice and exploit the fact that local statistical tests over
t
bits behave like strongly damped global statistical tests, and that this can be made quantitative by expressing statistical tests in a Hahn polynomial basis. We describe this technique in more detail in Section
3
after preliminaries in Section
2
. In Section
4
we prove the main technical theorem and in Section
5
we present the corollaries.

2
Preliminaries

2.1
Rising and falling factorials

For
x\in\mathbb{R}
and
r\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
, we define the falling factorial as

x^{\underline{r}}:=x(x-1)\cdots(x-r+1),\qquad x^{\underline{0}}:=1

and use Pochhammer’s symbol, given by

(x)_{r}:=x(x+1)\cdots(x+r-1),\qquad(x)_{0}:=1

2.2
Symmetric functions and distributions

Let
f:\{0,1\}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a function.
We say that
f
is symmetric if the output of
f
depends only on the Hamming weight of its input.
A probability distribution
\mu
is symmetric if the corresponding probability mass function mapping inputs to probabilities is a symmetric function.

2.3
Characterisations of statistical distance

We use the standard notion of statistical distance (total variation distance). For distributions over bit strings, the statistical distance
\Delta
between
\mu
and
\nu
is

\frac{1}{2}\|\mu-\nu\|_{1}
, or

\Delta(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\sum_{z\in\{0,1\}^{n}}\left|\Pr_{X\sim\mu}[X=z]-\Pr_{Y\sim\nu}[Y=z]\right|.

We also consider the statistical distance between marginal distributions of
\mu
and
\nu
. If
S\subseteq[n]
, let
\mu_{S}
and

\nu_{S}
denote the corresponding marginals on the coordinates in
S
. For

0\leq k\leq n
, define

\Delta_{k}(\mu,\nu):=\max_{\begin{subarray}{c}S\subseteq[n]\\
|S|=k\end{subarray}}\Delta(\mu_{S},\nu_{S}),

where
\mu_{S}
is the marginal distribution of
\mu
over the indices in
S
. In this paper,
\mu
and
\nu
will always be symmetric, in which case
\Delta(\mu_{S},\nu_{S})
is the same regardless of the choice of
S
.

When
\mu
and
\nu
are symmetric distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
, the marginals over any set of indices
S
are also symmetric and the best function with which to distinguish between
\mu_{S}
and
\nu_{S}
may be assumed symmetric (see, for example, Facts 5 and 6 in
[
8
]
). It follows that

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\left|\mathbb{E}[f(A_{X})]-\mathbb{E}[f(A_{Y})]\right|,

(2)

where the supremum is over functions
f:\{0,1,\dots,t\}\to[-1,1]
and where
A_{X}
(resp.
A_{Y}
) is the Hamming weight of the first
t
positions of a sample
X\sim\mu
(resp.
Y\sim\nu
).

2.4
Bounded indistinguishability

We say that
\mu
and
\nu
are
(k,\delta)
-wise
indistinguishable if
\Delta_{k}(\mu,\nu)\leq\delta
and we write
k
-wise indistinguishable as shorthand for

(k,0)
-wise indistinguishable.

In this paper,
\mu
and
\nu
will always be symmetric, in which
case
\Delta(\mu_{S},\nu_{S})
depends only on
|S|
, not on the
particular choice of
S
. Thus, for symmetric distributions, one may
take
S=\{1,\dots,k\}
in the definition of
\Delta_{k}(\mu,\nu)
.

2.5
Hahn polynomials

The Hahn polynomials are a family of orthogonal polynomials over a discrete set. We will use them to create approximations to any candidate statistical test. Here we provide definitions and some basic properties of these polynomials. For an integer
n\geq 0
and
0\leq r\leq n
, define

Q_{r}^{(n)}(x):=Q_{r}(x;0,0,n),

where
Q_{r}(x;\alpha,\beta,n)
denotes the Hahn polynomial as described in the Digital Library of Mathematical Functions
[
4
]
. We have, also following the conventions of DLMF
[
4
, §18.20(ii),(18.20.5)]
,

Q_{r}^{(n)}(x)={}_{3}F_{2}\!\left(\begin{matrix}-r,\ r+1,\ -x\\
1,\ -n\end{matrix};1\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}\frac{(-r)_{\ell}(r+1)_{\ell}(-x)_{\ell}}{(1)_{\ell}(-n)_{\ell}}\frac{1}{\ell!}.

This finite sum is well-defined for every
0\leq r\leq n
, since

(-n)_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}n^{\underline{\ell}}\neq 0\qquad\text{for }0\leq\ell\leq r\leq n.

We derive a further expression for
Q_{r}^{(n)}
, using:

(-r)_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}\frac{r!}{(r-\ell)!},\qquad(r+1)_{\ell}=\frac{(r+\ell)!}{r!},\qquad(-x)_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}x^{\underline{\ell}},

and

(-n)_{\ell}=(-1)^{\ell}n^{\underline{\ell}},\qquad(1)_{\ell}=\ell!,

to obtain

Q_{r}^{(n)}(x)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{r}{\ell}\binom{r+\ell}{\ell}\frac{x^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}.

(3)

2.6
Orthogonality

We have that (cf.
[
4
, §18.19]
)

\sum_{x=0}^{n}Q_{r}^{(n)}(x)Q_{s}^{(n)}(x)=h_{n,r}\delta_{rs},

(4)

where

h_{n,r}=\frac{(n+r+1)!(n-r)!}{(2r+1)(n!)^{2}}.

(5)

and
\delta_{rs}
is the Kronecker delta.

We define an inner product with:

\langle f,g\rangle_{n}:=\frac{1}{n+1}\sum_{x=0}^{n}f(x)g(x),\qquad\|f\|_{n}^{2}:=\langle f,f\rangle_{n}.

With respect to this inner product, the polynomials
Q_{0}^{(n)},Q_{1}^{(n)},\dots,Q_{n}^{(n)}
are mutually orthogonal. To obtain an orthonormal basis, we compute:

H_{n,r}:=\|Q_{r}^{(n)}\|_{n}^{2}=\frac{h_{n,r}}{n+1}=\frac{(n+r+1)!(n-r)!}{(2r+1)(n!)^{2}(n+1)}=\frac{1}{2r+1}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{n+j+2}{n-j},

(6)

where the final equality follows from the following short calculation:

\displaystyle\frac{(n+r+1)!(n-r)!}{(2r+1)(n!)^{2}(n+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r+1}\cdot\frac{(n+r+1)!}{(n+1)n!}\cdot\frac{(n-r)!}{n!}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r+1}\cdot\frac{(n+2)(n+3)\cdots(n+r+1)}{n(n-1)\cdots(n-r+1)}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2r+1}\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{n+j+2}{n-j}.

2.7
Orthonormality

We write
\phi_{r}^{(n)}
to denote the orthonormal version of the
Q_{r}^{(n)}
:

\phi_{r}^{(n)}(x):=\frac{Q_{r}^{(n)}(x)}{\sqrt{H_{n,r}}},\qquad 0\leq r\leq n.

(7)

Then

\langle\phi_{r}^{(n)},\phi_{s}^{(n)}\rangle_{n}=\delta_{rs}.

Since there are
n+1
mutually orthonormal functions on the

(n+1)
-point set
\{0,\dots,n\}
, the family
\left\{\phi_{0}^{(n)},\phi_{1}^{(n)},\dots,\phi_{n}^{(n)}\right\}

is an orthonormal basis for all real-valued functions on
\{0,\dots,n\}
.
For use later, we define

\lambda_{n,t,r}=\sqrt{\frac{H_{n,r}}{H_{t,r}}},

(8)

which is legitimate because
H_{t,r}
is never zero. We end this section with two short lemmas for use later, each of which is proven in the appendix.

Lemma 1
.

Suppose
0\leq t<n
. Then

\lambda_{n,t,r+1}<\lambda_{n,t,r}\qquad\text{for }0\leq r<t.

Lemma 2
.

For
0\leq s\leq t
,

\max_{0\leq r\leq s}\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}\leq 2^{2s+1}.

3
Hypergeometric smoothing

Let
t\leq n
. For a function

f:\{0,1,\dots,t\}\to\mathbb{R}
, define the hypergeometric sampling
operator
T_{n,t}
such that

T_{n,t}f:\{0,1,\dots,n\}\to\mathbb{R}

where

(T_{n,t}f)(k):=\sum_{a=0}^{t}f(a)\frac{\binom{k}{a}\binom{n-k}{t-a}}{\binom{n}{t}}.

(9)

Equivalently, let
\mu
be some distribution over
\{0,...,n\}
, take a sample
X\leftarrow\mu
and consider
k=|X|
as a Hamming weight. Then, if we apply a random permutation to
1^{k}0^{n-k}
, and define random variable
A
as the Hamming weight observed over the first
t
indices, we have that

(T_{n,t}f)(k)=\mathbb{E}[f(A)\mid|X|=k].

(10)

We establish a lemma about the distribution of the observed Hamming weight over the first
t
bits. In a subsequent lemma, we will use this to establish a relationship showing how the operator
T_{n,t}
acts on the polynomials
\phi_{r}^{(n)}
.

Lemma 3
.

Let
0\leq t\leq n
. Fix
k\in\{0,1,\dots,n\}
. Let
W\subseteq[n]
be a fixed set of size
k
, choose
a subset
S\subseteq[n]
of size
t
uniformly at random, and define

A:=|S\cap W|.

Then, for every
0\leq\ell\leq t
,

\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\underline{\ell}}\right]=\frac{t^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}k^{\underline{\ell}}.

Proof.

The case
\ell=0
is immediate because both sides equal 1. We now assume
\ell\geq 1
.

The random variable
\binom{A}{\ell}
counts size
\ell
subsets of

S\cap W
. For each
U\subseteq W
with
|U|=\ell
, let
\mathbf{1}_{U\subseteq S}
be an indicator random variable for the event that
U\subseteq S
.
Then

\binom{A}{\ell}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subseteq W\\
|U|=\ell\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{U\subseteq S}.

Taking expectations and using linearity,

\mathbb{E}\left[\binom{A}{\ell}\right]=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}U\subseteq W\\
|U|=\ell\end{subarray}}\Pr[U\subseteq S].

For a fixed
\ell
-set
U
, since
S
is a uniformly random

t
-subset of
[n]
,

\Pr[U\subseteq S]=\frac{\binom{n-\ell}{t-\ell}}{\binom{n}{t}}=\frac{t^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}.

There are
\binom{k}{\ell}
choices of
U
, so

\mathbb{E}\left[\binom{A}{\ell}\right]=\binom{k}{\ell}\frac{t^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}.

Multiplying both sides by
\ell!
and using the identity
m^{\underline{\ell}}=\ell!\binom{m}{\ell}
, yields that
\mathbb{E}\left[A^{\underline{\ell}}\right]=k^{\underline{\ell}}\frac{t^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}
.
∎

We are now in a position to show how
T
acts on the orthonormal Hahn polynomials.

Lemma 4
.

For every
0\leq r\leq t
,

T_{n,t}\phi_{r}^{(t)}=\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)},

where the
\lambda_{n,t,r}
are defined in Equation
8
.

Proof.

Using (
10
), (
3
), and
Lemma
3
,

\displaystyle(T_{n,t}Q_{r}^{(t)})(k)

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[Q_{r}^{(t)}(A)\mid|X|=k\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\!\left[\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{r}{\ell}\binom{r+\ell}{\ell}\frac{A^{\underline{\ell}}}{t^{\underline{\ell}}}\,\middle|\,|X|=k\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell=0}^{r}(-1)^{\ell}\binom{r}{\ell}\binom{r+\ell}{\ell}\frac{k^{\underline{\ell}}}{n^{\underline{\ell}}}

\displaystyle=Q_{r}^{(n)}(k).

Then, linearity of the operator
T
and dividing by the normalizing
constants gives

T_{n,t}\phi_{r}^{(t)}=\frac{Q_{r}^{(n)}}{\sqrt{H_{t,r}}}=\sqrt{\frac{H_{n,r}}{H_{t,r}}}\frac{Q_{r}^{(n)}}{\sqrt{H_{n,r}}}=\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)}.

∎

3.1
Quantifying the hypergeometric smoothing

In this section, we derive an upper bound on the scaling factors
\lambda_{n,t,r}
.

Lemma 5
.

Let
1\leq r\leq t<n
. Then

\lambda_{n,t,r}\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n-t)r(r+1)}{2n(t+r+1)}\right).

(11)

Proof.

For
0\leq j\leq r-1
, we have

\frac{(t-j)(n+j+2)}{(n-j)(t+j+2)}=1-\epsilon_{j},

where

\epsilon_{j}:=\frac{2(n-t)(j+1)}{(n-j)(t+j+2)}.

We also have directly from Equation
8
that
\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}(1-\epsilon_{j})=\lambda^{2}_{n,t,r}
.
The value
\epsilon_{j}
is non-negative because
n-t\geq 0
and
n-j>0
and the other terms are positive. It is also at most 1 because
n-j\geq n-t
and
t+j+2\geq 2j+2
.
It is trivial that

\epsilon_{j}\geq\frac{2(n-t)(j+1)}{n(t+r+1)}.

It follows that

\displaystyle\frac{(t-j)(n+j+2)}{(n-j)(t+j+2)}

\displaystyle=1-\epsilon_{j}

\displaystyle\leq 1-\frac{2(n-t)(j+1)}{n(t+r+1)}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{2(n-t)(j+1)}{n(t+r+1)}\right),

where we used
1-x\leq e^{-x}
.

Multiplying this estimate over
j=0,\dots,r-1
, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{n,t,r}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\exp\!\left(-\frac{2(n-t)}{n(t+r+1)}\sum_{j=0}^{r-1}(j+1)\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\left(-\frac{2(n-t)}{n(t+r+1)}\cdot\frac{r(r+1)}{2}\right)

\displaystyle=\exp\!\left(-\frac{(n-t)r(r+1)}{n(t+r+1)}\right).

Taking square roots gives (
11
).
∎

4
Proof of main theorem

Distributions
\mu,\nu
over
\{0,1\}^{n}
are assumed to be symmetric and
(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishable. For brevity, we introduce notation to capture an important quantity related to the upper bound from Lemma
5
. For
0\leq s<t\leq n
, define

E_{n,t,s}:=\frac{(n-t)(s+1)(s+2)}{2n(t+s+2)}.

We will consider statistical tests that observe
t>k
bits. In a nutshell, we have two steps: first, we argue that the high degree terms of such statistical tests are smoothed into irrelevance. This is Lemma
6
, which shows that smoothed tests over
t
bits are well approximated by smoothed tests over
<t
bits. Second, in Lemma
7
, we show how to control the distinguishing power of local tests in terms of their infinity norm. Finally, we put the pieces together in Theorem
1
.

4.1
Approximating general tests

We establish an approximation-theoretic result before translating into the language of approximate bounded indistinguishability.

Lemma 6
.

Let
0\leq s<t<n
, and let

f:\{0,1,\dots,t\}\to[-1,1].

Then there exists a function
h_{f}:\{0,1,\dots,s\}\to\mathbb{R}

such that
\|h_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2(s+1)}\cdot 2^{s}
and

\|T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\,e^{-E_{n,t,s}}.

(12)

Proof.

Expand
f
in the orthonormal Hahn basis on
\{0,\dots,t\}
:

f=\sum_{r=0}^{t}a_{r}\phi_{r}^{(t)},

and recall that from Lemma
4
we have
T_{n,t}\phi_{r}^{(t)}=\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)}
and the implication
T_{n,t}f=\sum_{r=0}^{t}a_{r}\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)}
.
Since
|f|\leq 1
, and by orthogonality of the
\phi_{r}^{(t)}
,

\sum_{r=0}^{t}a_{r}^{2}=\|f\|_{t}^{2}=\frac{1}{t+1}\sum_{a=0}^{t}f(a)^{2}\leq 1.

Then, we define

h_{f}:=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}\frac{\lambda_{n,t,r}}{\lambda_{n,s,r}}\phi_{r}^{(s)},

which is legitimate because
\lambda_{n,s,r}
is nonzero as it is a ratio of positive values.
Linearity of the operator
T
gives

T_{n,s}h_{f}=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}\frac{\lambda_{n,t,r}}{\lambda_{n,s,r}}T_{n,s}\phi_{r}^{(s)}=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}\frac{\lambda_{n,t,r}}{\lambda_{n,s,r}}\lambda_{n,s,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)}=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)},

where the result is just the degree
\leq s
portion of
T_{n,t}f
.
The remainder is

R_{f}:=T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f}=\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}a_{r}\lambda_{n,t,r}\phi_{r}^{(n)}.

By orthonormality on
\{0,\dots,n\}
,
\|R_{f}\|_{n}^{2}=\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}a_{r}^{2}\lambda_{n,t,r}^{2}
.
For every
r>s
, Lemma
1
gives that
\lambda_{n,t,r}\leq\lambda_{n,t,s+1}
and by Lemma
5

\lambda_{n,t,s+1}\leq e^{-E_{n,t,s}}.

Therefore

\|R_{f}\|_{n}^{2}\leq e^{-2E_{n,t,s}}\sum_{r=s+1}^{t}a_{r}^{2}\leq e^{-2E_{n,t,s}}.

Hence
\|R_{f}\|_{n}\leq e^{-E_{n,t,s}}
. Every function
g
on
\{0,\dots,n\}
satisfies

\|g\|_{\infty}^{2}\leq\sum_{x=0}^{n}g(x)^{2}=(n+1)\|g\|_{n}^{2}
,
and thus
\|g\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\,\|g\|_{n}
.
From this, we obtain

\|T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f}\|_{\infty}=\|R_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{n+1}\,e^{-E_{n,t,s}}.

Finally, we compute an upper bound on
||h_{f}||_{\infty}
. Directly from the definitions of
\lambda_{n,m,r}
and
h_{f}
, we have:

h_{f}=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}\sqrt{\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}}\phi_{r}^{(s)}.

By orthonormality on
\{0,\dots,s\}
,

\displaystyle\|h_{f}\|_{s}^{2}=\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}^{2}\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\max_{0\leq r\leq s}\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}\right)\sum_{r=0}^{s}a_{r}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{0\leq r\leq s}\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}

\displaystyle\leq 2^{2s+1},

where the final inequality is from Lemma
2
.
Since
\|h_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{s+1}\,\|h_{f}\|_{s}
,
we have

\|h_{f}\|_{\infty}\leq\sqrt{2(s+1)}\cdot 2^{s}.

∎

4.2
Approximate bounded indistinguishability

We begin by stating a simple lemma about the distinguishing power of tests that observe
s\leq k
bits. A proof of this lemma appears in the Appendix.

Lemma 7
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be symmetric distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
which are

(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishable. Let
s\leq k
, and let
h:\{0,1,\dots,s\}\to\mathbb{R}
.
Then

\left|\mathbb{E}_{X\sim\mu}[(T_{n,s}h)(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}_{Y\sim\nu}[(T_{n,s}h)(|Y|)]\right|\leq 2\delta\|h\|_{\infty}.

We now combine Lemmas
6
and
7
to obtain our main result.

Theorem
1
(restated)
.

Let
\mu,\nu
be symmetric distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
which are

(k,\delta)
-wise indistinguishable. Then, for integers
0\leq s\leq k<t<n
,
one has

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)\leq\sqrt{n+1}\,e^{-E_{n,t,s}}+\delta\,\sqrt{2(s+1)}\cdot 2^{s}.

Proof.

Let

A_{X}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}X_{i},\qquad A_{Y}:=\sum_{i=1}^{t}Y_{i}.

By symmetry and Equation
2
,

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)=\frac{1}{2}\sup_{\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1}\left|\mathbb{E}[f(A_{X})]-\mathbb{E}[f(A_{Y})]\right|,

where the supremum is over functions
f:\{0,1,\dots,t\}\to[-1,1]
.
Fix such an
f
. Apply Lemma
6
to
obtain
h_{f}
satisfying the conditions of that lemma. From Equation
10
, we have
\mathbb{E}[f(A_{X})]=\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f)(|X|)]
and
\mathbb{E}[f(A_{Y})]=\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f)(|Y|)]
.
Thus,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[f(A_{X})]-\mathbb{E}[f(A_{Y})]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f)(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f)(|Y|)]

\displaystyle=\Big(\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f})(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f})(|Y|)]\Big)

\displaystyle+\Big(\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h_{f})(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h_{f})(|Y|)]\Big).

The first term in the sum is bounded as follows:

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f})(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f})(|Y|)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\|T_{n,t}f-T_{n,s}h_{f}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sqrt{n+1}\,e^{-E_{n,t,s}}.

For the second term, use Lemma
7
. Since
s\leq k
,

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h_{f})(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h_{f})(|Y|)]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\|h_{f}\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\,\sqrt{2(s+1)}\cdot 2^{s}.

Combining the two estimates, taking the supremum over all
\|f\|_{\infty}\leq 1
, and dividing by two (matching the constant in Equation
2
) gives the theorem.
∎

5
Applications of the main theorem

We state our corollaries of the main theorem, which follow straightforwardly from Theorem
1
and proper selection of the parameter
s
. We have two corollaries; the first is relevant for reconstruction by
t
bits for
t
up to some fixed constant fraction of
n
and the second is relevant for
t
of the form
t=n-q
for some sublinear
q
.

Corollary 3

(Approximate indistinguishability for small
t
)
.

Fix a constant
0<c<1
. Let
k=k(n)
and
t=t(n)
be integers satisfying
k<t
,
1\leq t\leq(1-c)n
, and
k=\omega\!\left(\sqrt{t\log n}\right)
.
Suppose
\mu,\nu
are symmetric
(k,\delta_{n})
-wise indistinguishable
distributions on
\{0,1\}^{n}
, where

\delta_{n}\leq\exp\!\left(-\omega\!\left(\sqrt{t\log n}\right)\right).

Then,
\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)=n^{-\omega(1)}
.

Proof.

Fix
D>0
. Let
d>0
be a constant to be chosen later, depending
only on
D
and
c
, and set

s:=\left\lceil d\sqrt{t\log n}\right\rceil.

By definition of
s
and the premises of the corollary, we have
s\leq k
and can apply Theorem
1
:
we have

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)\leq\sqrt{n+1}e^{-E_{n,t,s}}+\delta_{n}\sqrt{2(s+1)}\,2^{s},

where

E_{n,t,s}=\frac{(n-t)(s+1)(s+2)}{2n(t+s+2)}.

We first lower-bound
E_{n,t,s}
. Using
t\leq(1-c)n
and
t+s+2\leq 3t
, we have

E_{n,t,s}\geq\frac{cs^{2}}{6t}\geq\frac{cd^{2}}{6}\log n.

One can then choose
d
large enough so that for all sufficiently large
n
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{n+1}e^{-E_{n,t,s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n+1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{cd^{2}}{6}\log n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-D}.

For the second term, since
s=O(\sqrt{t\log n})
, we have
\sqrt{2(s+1)}2^{s}=\exp{(O(\sqrt{t\log{n}})})
.
By assumption,
\delta_{n}\leq\exp(-\omega(\sqrt{t\log{n}}))
and therefore

\delta_{n}\sqrt{2(s+1)}\,2^{s}=\exp(-\omega(\sqrt{t\log{n}}))\leq\exp(-\omega(\log{n}))\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-D},

where the first inequality uses
t=\omega(\log n)
, which follows from the premises of the corollary.
Combining the two estimates gives

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)\leq n^{-D}

for all sufficiently large
n
, proving the corollary since
D>0
is arbitrary.
∎

Corollary 4

(Approximate indistinguishability for large
t
)
.

Let
q=q(n)
and set
t=n-q
and
k<t
.
Suppose

k(n)=\omega\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right).

If
\mu,\nu
are symmetric
(k,\delta_{n})
-wise indistinguishable
distributions over
\{0,1\}^{n}
, where

\delta_{n}\leq\exp\!\left(-\omega\!\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right)\right).

Then
\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)=n^{-\omega(1)}
.

Proof.

Fix
D>0
. Let
d>0
be a constant to be chosen later, depending
only on
D
, and set

s:=\left\lceil dn\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right\rceil.

By the premises of the corollary, we have
s\leq k
and can apply Theorem
1
.
Since
t=n-q
, we have

E_{n,t,s}=\frac{q(s+1)(s+2)}{2n(t+s+2)}.

We first lower-bound
E_{n,t,s}
. Using
t+s+2\leq 3n
we have

E_{n,t,s}\geq\frac{qs^{2}}{6n^{2}}\geq\frac{d^{2}}{6}\log n.

One can then choose
d
large enough so that for all sufficiently large

n
,

\displaystyle\sqrt{n+1}e^{-E_{n,t,s}}

\displaystyle\leq\sqrt{n+1}\exp\!\left(-\frac{d^{2}}{6}\log n\right)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-D}.

For the second term in Theorem
1
, since
s=O\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right)
, we have
\sqrt{2(s+1)}2^{s}=\exp\left(O\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right)\right)
.
By assumption,
\delta_{n}\leq\exp\left(-\omega\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right)\right)
, and therefore

\delta_{n}\sqrt{2(s+1)}\,2^{s}=\exp\left(-\omega\left(n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}\right)\right)\leq\exp(-\omega(\log n))\leq\frac{1}{2}n^{-D},

where the first inequality used
n\sqrt{\frac{\log n}{q}}=\omega(\log n)
, which follows from
q\leq n
.
Combining the two
estimates gives

\Delta_{t}(\mu,\nu)\leq n^{-D}

for all sufficiently large
n
, proving the corollary since
D>0
is
arbitrary.
∎

Appendix A
Technical lemmas

We record here proofs of three technical lemmas.

A.1
Proof of Lemma
1

Proof.

Because
\lambda_{n,t,r}
is always positive, it suffices to show that
\lambda_{n,t,r+1}^{2}<\lambda_{n,t,r}^{2}
. Immediately from Equations
6
and
8
we have that

\lambda_{n,t,r}^{2}=\prod_{j=0}^{r-1}\frac{(t-j)(n+j+2)}{(n-j)(t+j+2)},

where the empty product is interpreted as
1
and as such we have that

\frac{\lambda_{n,t,r+1}^{2}}{\lambda_{n,t,r}^{2}}=\frac{(t-r)(n+r+2)}{(n-r)(t+r+2)}<1,

where the final inequality follows from

\displaystyle(n-r)(t+r+2)-(t-r)(n+r+2)

\displaystyle=2(n-t)(r+1)>0.

∎

A.2
Proof of Lemma
2

Proof.

Since

\frac{H_{t,r}}{H_{s,r}}=\lambda_{t,s,r}^{2}

and Lemma
1
states that the factors
\lambda_{t,s,r}
are decreasing in
r
, the
ratio
H_{s,r}/H_{t,r}
is increasing in
r
. Therefore

\max_{0\leq r\leq s}\frac{H_{s,r}}{H_{t,r}}=\frac{H_{s,s}}{H_{t,s}}.

Directly from Equation
6

H_{s,s}=\frac{1}{s+1}\binom{2s}{s}\leq\frac{4^{s}}{s+1}.

Also, from the product formula

H_{t,s}=\frac{1}{2s+1}\prod_{j=0}^{s-1}\frac{t+j+2}{t-j},

and each factor in the product is at least
1
. Hence

H_{t,s}\geq\frac{1}{2s+1}.

Thus

\frac{H_{s,s}}{H_{t,s}}\leq\frac{2s+1}{s+1}4^{s}\leq 2^{2s+1}.

∎

A.3
Proof of Lemma
7

Proof.

Let

A_{X}:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}X_{i},\qquad A_{Y}:=\sum_{i=1}^{s}Y_{i}

be the Hamming weights observed on the first
s
coordinates of a sample
X\sim\mu
(resp.
Y\sim\nu
). Since
\mu
and
\nu
are symmetric, we condition on
|X|=j
, and from Equation
10
, have that

\mathbb{E}[h(A_{X})]=\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h)(|X|)].

Similarly,

\mathbb{E}[h(A_{Y})]=\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h)(|Y|)].

Define the
s
-bit symmetric test

H(z):=h(|z|),\qquad z\in\{0,1\}^{s}.

Then

\|H\|_{\infty}=\|h\|_{\infty}.

Because
s\leq k
and
\mu,\nu
are
(k,\delta)
-wise
indistinguishable,
\Delta_{s}(\mu,\nu)\leq\delta
and therefore

\displaystyle\left|\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h)(|X|)]-\mathbb{E}[(T_{n,s}h)(|Y|)]\right|

\displaystyle=\left|\mathbb{E}[H(X_{\{1,\dots,s\}})]-\mathbb{E}[H(Y_{\{1,\dots,s\}})]\right|

\displaystyle\leq 2\delta\|H\|_{\infty}

\displaystyle=2\delta\|h\|_{\infty},

where the inequality is via Equation
2
.
∎
