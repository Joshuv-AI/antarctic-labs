Target-Side Paraphrase Augmentation for Sign Language Translation with Large Language Models
Pedro Dal Bianco, Jean Paul Nunes Reinhold, Oscar Stanchi, Facundo Quiroga, Franco Ronchetti, Ulisses Brisolara Corrêa

Abstract
Sign language translation (SLT) remains constrained by limited paired sign-video/text corpora and heavy-tailed target vocabularies. We study target-side augmentation in which GPT-4o generates controlled paraphrase variants of reference sentences while the sign input remains unchanged. A Signformer-style pose-based Transformer is trained under a two-stage schedule: pre-training on the augmented corpus followed by fine-tuning on the original references. We evaluate on three datasets spanning complementary challenges: PHOENIX14T (German Sign Language), with moderate lexical diversity; GSL (Greek Sign Language), with highly controlled, repetitive recordings; and LSA-T (Argentinian Sign Language), with severe long-tail sparsity. On PHOENIX14T, augmentation improves BLEU-4 from 9.56 to 10.33. The near-saturated GSL baseline and extremely sparse LSA-T setting reveal the limits of the approach. To our knowledge, this is the first study to apply LLM-generated target-side paraphrases and LLM-as-a-Judge evaluation to SLT. The semantic evaluation reveals gains in fidelity that lexical overlap metrics understate.

Introduction

Sign language translation (SLT) maps sign-language video to spoken-language text, sitting at the intersection of computer vision and NLP. Despite substantial recent progress
4
;
5
, robust translation remains difficult due to the multimodal complexity of signed utterances, the scarcity of large parallel corpora
3
, and heavy-tailed target vocabularies where many words appear only a handful of times.

These constraints motivate compact, pose-based models. We adopt a Signformer-inspired architecture
17
using MediaPipe-derived pose features, so that the effect of data augmentation can be studied within a controlled and resource-efficient setup.

Data augmentation is standard in low-resource MT
13
;
8
, but in SLT it has mostly targeted the sign side
11
;
15
. We instead augment the
text side
: each reference sentence is rewritten into semantically faithful variants via an LLM, exposing the decoder to multiple acceptable surface realizations of the same signed content. To our knowledge, LLM-generated target-side paraphrase augmentation has not previously been studied for SLT, although LLMs have appeared in other SLT roles
16
.

We evaluate on PHOENIX14T
4
, GSL
1
, and LSA-T
2
. We also conduct a semantic evaluation using an LLM-as-a-Judge protocol (Section
5
).

Our contributions: (1) we introduce LLM-based target-side paraphrase augmentation for SLT and release three augmented datasets covering DGS, GSL, and LSA; (2) we present a three-dataset study showing that the effect depends strongly on corpus characteristics; and (3) we conduct a semantic evaluation using LLM-as-a-Judge on PHOENIX14T and GSL, revealing gains that lexical overlap metrics understate. Related Work

Sign Language Translation.

Early SLT systems used a two-stage pipeline: sign language recognition predicted glosses, which were then translated to text
4
. More recent work has shifted toward
gloss-free
SLT, mapping video directly to text
5
;
6
. Modern systems rely on transformer architectures; Sign2GPT
16
combines CLIP with a GPT-style decoder, while Signformer
17
achieves competitive performance with a lightweight transformer. Our model follows this efficiency-oriented line using body-pose sequences.

Data Augmentation in SLT.

Prior SLT augmentation has focused on the sign side through production models
14
or generative methods
15
. Target-side augmentation has received less attention:
11
used heuristic paraphrase rules for gloss-to-text data. In NLP more broadly, LLMs have been used for paraphrase generation in low-resource tasks
7
. Our work brings that idea to SLT, keeping sign input fixed while rewriting target references.

Methodology

3.1
Model Architecture

Our baseline follows the Signformer encoder–decoder Transformer
17
(Figure
1
), adapted to accept skeleton keypoints. MediaPipe Holistic
10
extracts 2D coordinates for 33 body landmarks, 21 per hand, and a facial subset, concatenated into a per-frame feature vector projected into the model’s embedding space through a linear layer.

This pose-based representation emphasizes articulatory motion over appearance, suppressing background and lighting variation. While pose-based approaches may lag behind image-based models
19
, the reduced dimensionality simplifies the pipeline and the relative comparisons with and without augmentation remain informative within this setting.

3.2
LLM-Based Data Augmentation

For each video–sentence pair
(V,T)
, we use GPT-4o to generate
N=3
paraphrases
T_{1}^{\prime},T_{2}^{\prime},T_{3}^{\prime}
that preserve the meaning of
T
while allowing controlled lexical and syntactic variation. The prompt requires structured JSON and explicitly preserves tense, register, and propositional content. During training, these are materialized as four examples
(V,T)
,
(V,T^{\prime}_{1})
,
(V,T^{\prime}_{2})
,
(V,T^{\prime}_{3})
. Figure
2
summarizes the procedure, and Table
1
provides examples.

To reduce semantic drift, we filter paraphrases using the mean
\bar{s}(T,T_{i}^{\prime})
of four surface-form similarity measures (character-level Jaccard, word-level Jaccard, normalized Levenshtein, trigram overlap), retaining variants only if

0.3\leq\bar{s}(T,T_{i}^{\prime})\leq 0.95,

discarding exact duplicates regardless. The lower bound rejects meaning-altering variants; the upper bound removes near-copies.

3.3
Training Schedule

We compare two conditions:

•

Baseline:
trained on the original, non-augmented training set.

•

+Augmentation:

–

Stage 1:
pre-train on the augmented corpus (originals plus three paraphrases per instance).

–

Stage 2:
fine-tune on originals only, re-aligning the decoder with reference phrasing. All hyperparameters and early stopping remain identical across conditions.

The two-stage schedule is central: pre-training broadens the decoder distribution, while fine-tuning re-centers it on the reference style without discarding the broader lexical exposure. Both conditions use teacher forcing with cross-entropy loss, warm-up-and-decay learning rate, label smoothing, and early stopping on validation loss.

Datasets and Evaluation

We evaluate on three SLT datasets differing in linguistic diversity, recording conditions, and lexical structure.

PHOENIX14T

4
contains weather-broadcast recordings in DGS paired with German text. Its domain-specific phrasing is relatively formulaic, but natural recording conditions introduce visual variation across signers.

GSL

1

LSA-T

2

PHOENIX14T shows +0.77 BLEU: paraphrastic exposure helps the decoder generalize beyond memorized templates. GSL starts from 94.38 BLEU; augmentation slightly reduces it to 92.22, as the decoder produces semantically valid but lexically different outputs that single-reference BLEU penalizes. LSA-T remains at 1.2 BLEU in both conditions, as target-side augmentation cannot address sign-side data sparsity.

Why BLEU can understate gains.

Augmentation encourages the model to accept multiple valid realizations, but BLEU rewards overlap with only one reference. A semantically correct variant differing lexically from the reference is penalized, creating a methodological mismatch that explains why modest BLEU gains can correspond to larger semantic improvements.

Results.

Table
4
reports semantic fidelity. On PHOENIX14T, augmentation raises fidelity from 2.51 to 3.65 (+45%), fluent-but-wrong translations drop from 54.8% to 35.5%, and pairwise preferences favor augmentation 52.9% vs. 13.1%. On GSL, fidelity improves from 7.72 to 8.77 (+13.6%), confirming semantic gains where lexical metrics are near-saturated.

Limitations.

GPT-5.2 served as judge while GPT-4o generated augmented targets. Although architecturally distinct, shared training lineage may introduce subtle alignment; semantic judging should be treated as complementary evidence, ideally validated with human assessment.

6
Conclusion

Target-side LLM augmentation is not uniformly beneficial for SLT: its value depends on where a corpus sits along the spectrum from formulaic to long-tail sparse. On PHOENIX14T, paraphrastic exposure improves both BLEU and semantic fidelity. On GSL, the near-perfect baseline leaves no room for lexical variation to help. On LSA-T, target-side rewriting cannot address sign-side sparsity.

The semantic evaluation confirms gains exceeding what BLEU captures. Promising directions include combining target-text with sign-side augmentation, evaluating with multiple references, and replacing GPT-4o with open-source alternatives to clarify whether gains stem from the augmentation principle or the specific generator.
