On quiver skew braces, their ideals and products
Davide Ferri

Abstract
. Quiver skew braces or skew bracoids are equivalent to braided groupoids, that is, groupoids with a constraint of abelianity. They are the quiver-theoretic version of skew braces, an increasingly studied structure lying in the intersection of group and ring theory. In this paper, we define ideals and quotients for quiver skew braces, with respect to two notions of morphisms. Following the track of a previous work of ours (2025), we define a classical semidirect product à la Brown, and a categorical semidirect product à la Bourn and Janelidze, for the category of quiver skew braces. It is known that connected groupoids can be expressed as the datum of a group of loops and a set of vertices. We demonstrate how no such decomposition holds for quiver skew braces, which makes their theory richer than the theory of groupoids.

A

is a group

r\colon G\times G\to G\times G
, called a

, that satisfies properties analogous to the canonical flip

A set is a quiver with a single vertex. Following this idea, the philosophy of ‘oidification’ is concerned with turning set-theoretic (i.e., one-vertex) concepts into quiver-theoretic (multiple-vertices) ones. It is natural to ask for an oidification of braided groups. A
braided groupoid
is a groupoid
\mathscr{G}
with a morphism of quivers
r\colon\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}
, also called a

[
1
]
. Here

\mathscr{G}^{0}

[
20
]
. Interestingly, though, this time there is no sensible notion of an ‘abelian groupoid’ to generalise: if
a
and
b
are consecutive arrows,
b
and
a
need not be, so

is generally undefined. For the same reason, the canonical flip on a groupoid is, usually, not even a map of quivers. It is easy to get convinced that the canonical flip on
\mathscr{G}
is a map of quivers if and only if
\mathscr{G}
is a group, and that an abelian groupoid is forced to be just an abelian group. The notion of braiding is thus, in some sense, ‘more natural’ than the notion of commutativity—at least in the context of quivers.

Braided groups are equivalent to
skew braces
. A skew brace is a set with two group operations, satisfying a compatibility
[
16
,
23
]
. These objects have been classically perceived as having the flavour of a ring (radical rings yield, in fact, examples of skew braces
[
23
]
), hence they are often handled through ring-theoretic techniques.

Likewise, braided groupoids are equivalent to what we call
quiver skew braces
, which are an oidification of skew braces. These are groupoids

with an extra group structure on each outgoing star
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
. They were introduced by Sheng, Tang, and Zhu
[
24

with the name

. Since a distinct object named

[
19

In this paper, we investigate the basic theory of braided groupoids, or equivalently quiver skew braces, in line with what was done for groupoids in
[

,
14
,

Two notions of ‘semidirect product’ have been studied for groupoids. One, which we call Brown’s

semidirect products
[
8
]
, makes more sense geometrically. The other one, described in
[
14

As observed by Brown
[
7
]
, a (connected) groupoid
\mathscr{G}
can be simply identified with the datum of a set and a group: the set of vertices
\mathscr{G}^{0}
, and any of the (isomorphic) isotropy groups
\mathscr{G}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
. The theory of braided groupoids or quiver skew braces, however, is significantly more complex in this aspect. The datum ‘set of vertices and isotropy group’ is promoted to the datum ‘heap of vertices and isotropy skew brace’: this time, though, not all quiver skew braces are obtained from the datum of a heap and a skew brace. In
Section

4.1
, we provide a counterexample.

In other words, it is not immediate to rewrite quiver skew brace theory in a way that overrides the quiver-theoretic aspect. This prompts the study of decompositions of quiver skew braces into geometrically simpler objects, and the notion of an
unprunable
quiver skew brace—that is, simple with respect to ideal bundles.

1.
Groupoids and braided groupoids

1.1.
Groupoids

A
groupoid
is a quiver
\mathscr{G}=\left(\mathscr{G}^{1}\,\underset{\mathfrak{t}}{\overset{\mathfrak{s}}{\rightrightarrows}}\,\mathscr{G}^{0}\right)
endowed with a multiplication
m\colon\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}
,
m(x\otimes y)=xy
, defined only for consecutive arrows, and with a family
\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}=\{1_{\lambda}\mid\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}\}
of loops, such that:

(
i
)

m(\mathrm{id}\otimes m)=m(m\otimes\mathrm{id})
(associativity);

(
ii
)

1_{\lambda}
is a loop on
\lambda
, satisfying
x1_{\lambda}=x
and
1_{\lambda}y=y
for all
x\in\mathscr{G}(\mathscr{G}^{0},\lambda)
and
y\in\mathscr{G}(\lambda,\mathscr{G}^{0})
(unit loops);

(
iii
)

for all
x\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
there exists
x^{-1}\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
such that
xx^{-1}=1_{\mathfrak{s}(x)}
and
x^{-1}x=1_{\mathfrak{t}(x)}
(inverses).

A morphism of groupoids
f=(f^{1},f^{0})\colon\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
is a morphism of quivers satisfying
f^{1}(xy)=f^{1}(x)f^{1}(y)
for all
x\otimes y\in(\mathscr{G}\otimes\mathscr{G})^{1}
. A morphism of groupoids is strong (over
\Lambda=\mathscr{G}^{0}=\mathscr{H}^{0}
) if
f^{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\Lambda}
.

1.2.
Heaps

Our approach in this paper pivots around the notion of
heap
, originated from the work of Prüfer
[
22
]
(abelian case) and Baer
[
3
]
. Despite heaps containing roughly the same information as groups, the language of heaps can be more effective than the language of groups—or vice versa—for specific purposes.

We follow the exposition of Brzeziński
[
9
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

A
heap
is a set
X
equipped with a ternary operation
\left<\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}}\right>
satisfying the
associativity condition

\left<\left<a,b,c\right>,d,e\right>=\left<a,b,\left<c,d,e\right>\right>

and the
Mal’tsev conditions

\left<a,b,b\right>=a,\quad\left<a,a,b\right>=b

for all
a,b,c,d,e\in X
.

It is often said that heaps are the ‘affine version’ of groups. The following well-known fact explains this viewpoint.

Proposition 1.2
.

Let
(G,+)
be a group (not necessarily abelian), which we denote additively. Then
\left<a,b,c\right>=a-b+c
is a heap structure.

Let
(G,\left<\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}}\right>)
be a heap. For all
b\in G
, then,
a+_{b}c:=\left<a,b,c\right>
is a group operation, with neutral element
b
.

The two above connections are mutually inverse.

Definition 1.4

2.
Ideals and quotients

In analogy to skew braces, we give notions of ideals and quotients for quiver skew braces. We give two notions, termed
ideals
and
ideal bundles
, which work well in
{\sf QSKB}
and in
{\sf QSKB}_{\Lambda}
respectively.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
(\mathscr{G},\{+_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda\in\Lambda},\cdot)
be a quiver skew brace over
\Lambda
. An
ideal
of
\mathscr{G}
is a normal subgroupoid
\mathscr{I}
of
\mathscr{G}
such that
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)
is an additive subgroup of
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{I}^{0}
, and satisfying

(8)

\displaystyle a+_{\mathfrak{s}(a)}\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{s}(a))=\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{s}(a))+_{\mathfrak{s}(a)}a\text{ (normality)},

(9)

\displaystyle a\rightharpoonup\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{t}(a))\subseteq\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{s}(a))\text{ (invariance)},

for all
a\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
. An ideal
\mathscr{I}
is an
ideal bundle
if it is moreover a subgroup bundle.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a quiver skew brace, and
\mathscr{I}
be an ideal of
\mathscr{G}
. Then, the quotient
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
inherits a unique quiver skew brace structure such that the canonical projection
\pi\colon\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
is a morphism of quiver skew braces.

This is moreover a strong morphism over
\mathscr{G}^{0}
if and only if
\mathscr{I}
is an ideal bundle.

Proof.

Since
\mathscr{I}
is normal in
\mathscr{G}
, the operation
\cdot
induces a groupoid structure on
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
(denoted again by
\cdot
) such that
\pi
is a morphism of groupoids. Moreover, (
8
) states that
(\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda),+_{\lambda})
is a normal subgroup of
(\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda),+_{\lambda})
for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
. We need to check that two arrows
x,y\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
are equivalent in
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
if and only if they are equivalent in the quotient group
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)/\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)
. Let
x,y\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
be equivalent in
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
, thus
x=yi
for some
i\in\mathscr{G}(\mathfrak{t}(y),\mathfrak{t}(x))
. We search for an element
j\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
such that
x=y+j
, and then we prove that
j
lies in
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)
. By the definition of
\rightharpoonup
one easily has
y+j=y(y^{-1}\rightharpoonup j)
, thus the request that
x
be equal to
y+j
is equivalent to the condition
j=y\rightharpoonup(y^{-1}x)=y\rightharpoonup i
. Finally,
y\rightharpoonup i\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)
holds by (
9
). This proves that the quotient is well-defined.

It is immediate to notice that the compatibility (
5
) passes to the quotient, hence
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
is a quiver skew brace, and the canonical projection is clearly a morphism.

If
\mathscr{I}
is an ideal bundle, the quotient
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
is a quiver over
\mathscr{G}^{0}
, and
\pi^{0}
is the identity map on
\mathscr{G}^{0}
. Conversely if
\pi^{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}
then there is no arrow
x\in\mathscr{I}^{1}
with
\mathfrak{s}(x)\neq\mathfrak{t}(x)
, otherwise
\mathfrak{s}(x)
and
\mathfrak{t}(x)
would be identified by
\pi^{0}
: therefore
\mathscr{I}
is a bundle of loops, and hence an ideal bundle.
∎

Proposition 2.3
.

The ideals of
\mathscr{G}
are exactly the kernels of morphisms
f\colon\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
in
{\sf QSKB}
. The ideal bundles are exactly the kernels of morphisms in
{\sf QSKB}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}
.

Proof.

Every ideal
\mathscr{I}
is the kernel of the projection
\pi\colon\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{I}
. By
Section

2
,
\pi
is a morphism in
{\sf QSKB}
, and in case
\mathscr{I}
is an ideal bundle, the same projection is a morphism in
{\sf QSKB}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}
.

Conversely, let
f\colon\mathscr{G}\to\mathscr{H}
be a morphism in
{\sf QSKB}
, and
\mathscr{I}
be its kernel. For every
\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
, the morphism
f
induces a homomorphism between the additive groups
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
and
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{H}}(f^{0}(\lambda))
, whose kernel
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)=\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\lambda)
is a normal subgroup: thus
\mathscr{I}
satisfies (
8
). As for (
9
), for
a\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
and
k\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{t}(a))
one has

f^{1}(a\rightharpoonup k)=f^{1}(-a+ak)=-f^{1}(a)+f^{1}(a)1=1,

thus
a\rightharpoonup k\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{I}}(\mathfrak{s}(a))
. If moreover
f
is a morphism in
{\sf QSKB}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}
, then its kernel is a bundle of loops, and hence an ideal bundle.
∎

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
\mathscr{I}
be an ideal in
\mathscr{G}
. Then
\mathscr{I}
is invariant under
\leftharpoonup
.

Proof.

Let
\mathscr{I}
be the kernel of
f
. Let
i\in\mathscr{I}^{1}
and
g\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
such that
i\otimes g
is defined. Then

\displaystyle f^{1}((i\rightharpoonup g)(i\leftharpoonup g))=(f^{1}(i)\rightharpoonup f^{1}(g))f^{1}(i\leftharpoonup g)

\displaystyle=(1\rightharpoonup f^{1}(g))f^{1}(i\leftharpoonup g)=f^{1}(g)f^{1}(i\leftharpoonup g),

while on the other hand

f^{1}((i\rightharpoonup g)(i\leftharpoonup g))=f^{1}(ig)=f^{1}(i)f^{1}(g),

whence
f^{1}(i\leftharpoonup g)=1
.
∎

3.
Products

3.1.
Classical semidirect products
à la
Brown

Given a group
G
, a set
\Lambda
, and an action
\triangleright
of the groupoid
\widehat{\Lambda}
on
G
, we recall e.g. from Brown
[
6
,
7
]
that the set
G\times\widehat{\Lambda}^{1}
can be given a groupoid structure, which is denoted by
G\rtimes\widehat{\Lambda}
in
[
14
]
, by setting

\displaystyle\mathfrak{s}\Big(g\times[a,b]\Big)=a,\qquad\mathfrak{t}\Big(g\times[a,b]\Big)=b,

\displaystyle\Big(g\times[a,b]\Big)\Big(h\times[b,c]\Big)=g([a,b]\triangleright h)\times[a,c].

Using the same nomenclature as in
[
14
]
, we also say ‘
\mathscr{G}
-module’ meaning a quiver
Q
with a semistrong action of the groupoid
\mathscr{G}
on
Q
. We say that a small category
\mathscr{M}
is a ‘
\mathscr{G}
-module algebra’ if the quiver
\mathscr{M}
is a
\mathscr{G}
-module with the action
\triangleright
, and moreover
g\triangleright(ab)=(g\triangleright a)(g\triangleright b)
for
g\in\mathscr{G}^{1}
,
a,b\in\mathscr{M}^{1}
.

We can define a skew brace-by-heap semidirect product, giving rise to a quiver skew brace. We do not prove the following proposition immediately, since it will be a special case of
Section

3.1
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
(G,+,\cdot)
be a skew brace,
(\Lambda,\left<\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}}\right>)
be a heap, and
\triangleright
be an action of the groupoid
\widehat{\Lambda}
on
G
. On the groupoid
(G,\cdot)\rtimes\widehat{\Lambda}
define the operation

(g\times[a,b])+(h\times[a,c])=(g([a,b]\triangleright h))\times[a,\left<b,a,c\right>].

This restricts to group operations for all stars
\mathrm{St}_{G\rtimes\widehat{\Lambda}}(a)=G\times\mathrm{St}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}(a)
. It makes
G\rtimes\widehat{\Lambda}
into a quiver skew brace if and only if
(G,+)
is a
\widehat{\Lambda}
-module algebra.

When
\triangleright
is the trivial action, we call this a
direct product
, and denote it by
G\times\widehat{\Lambda}
.

The skew brace-by-heap semidirect product can be slightly generalised, to a quiver skew brace-by-heap semidirect product. This will lead us to finding a quiver skew brace that has nontrivial ideals but only trivial ideal bundles (
Section

4.1
).

Let
\mathscr{G}
be a quiver skew brace, and
(\Lambda,\left<\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}}\right>
) be a heap. Let
\triangleright
be an action of the groupoid
\widehat{\Lambda}
on
\mathscr{G}
. Consider the semidirect product of groupoids
\mathscr{G}\rtimes_{\triangleright}\widehat{\Lambda}
as in
[
14
, Definition 4.6]
, with underlying quiver

\displaystyle(\mathscr{G}^{1}\times\widehat{\Lambda}^{1})\,\underset{\mathfrak{T}}{\overset{\mathfrak{S}}{\rightrightarrows}}\,(\mathscr{G}^{0}\times\Lambda)

\displaystyle\mathfrak{S}(g\times[a,b])=\mathfrak{s}(g)\times a,\qquad\mathfrak{T}(g\times[a,b])=([b,a]\triangleright\mathfrak{t}(g))\times b,

and with multiplication and units

(g\times[a,b])\cdot(h\times[b,c])=g([a,b]\triangleright h)\times[a,c],\qquad 1_{\lambda,a}=1_{\lambda}\times 1_{a}.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\mathscr{G},\Lambda,\triangleright
be as above. On the semidirect product
\mathscr{G}\rtimes_{\triangleright}\widehat{\Lambda}
, define an additive structure as

(g\times[a,b])+_{a}(h\times[a,c])=(g+_{\lambda}h)\times[a,\left<b,a,c\right>]

for
\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
,
g,h\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
. This is a quiver skew brace structure if and only if the bundle of additive star-groups
\bigsqcup_{\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}}\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
is a
\widehat{\Lambda}
-module algebra.

Proof.

We check that
+
is associative:

\displaystyle\big((g\times[a,b])+(h\times[a,c])\big)+(k\times[a,d])

\displaystyle=(g+h+k)\times[a,\left<\left<b,a,c\right>,a,d\right>]

\displaystyle=(g+h+k)\times[a,\left<b,a,\left<c,a,d\right>\right>]

\displaystyle=(g\times[a,b])+\big((h\times[a,c])+(k\times[a,d])\big).

Clearly
1\times[a,a]
is a neutral element for
\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}\rtimes\widehat{\Lambda}}(a)
, and the additive inverse of
g\times[a,b]
is
(-g)\times[a,\left<a,b,a\right>]
. We check the compatibility between
\cdot
and
+
: on one hand

\displaystyle(g\times[a,b])\cdot\Big(h\times[b,c]+k\times[b,d]\Big)

\displaystyle=g([a,b]\triangleright(h+k))\times[a,\left<c,b,d\right>].

On the other hand,

\displaystyle(g\times[a,b])(h\times[b,c])-(g\times[a,b])+(g\times[a,b])(k\times{b,d})

\displaystyle=\Big(g([a,b]\triangleright h)-g+g([a,b]\triangleright k)\Big)\times[a,\left<\left<c,a,\left<a,b,a\right>\right>,a,d\right>]

\displaystyle=\Big(g([a,b]\triangleright h)-g+g([a,b]\triangleright k)\Big)\times[a,\left<\left<\left<c,a,a\right>,b,a\right>,a,d\right>]

\displaystyle=\Big(g([a,b]\triangleright h)-g+g([a,b]\triangleright k)\Big)\times[a,\left<\left<c,b,a\right>,a,d\right>]

\displaystyle=\Big(g([a,b]\triangleright h)-g+g([a,b]\triangleright k)\Big)\times[a,\left<c,b,d\right>]

\displaystyle=\big(g([a,b]\triangleright h+[a,b]\triangleright k)\big)\times[a,\left<c,b,d\right>].

The first entries are equal for all
g\in\mathscr{G}(\Lambda,[a,b]\triangleright\mathfrak{s}(h))=\mathscr{G}(\Lambda,[a,b]\triangleright\mathfrak{s}(k))
if and only if

[a,b]\triangleright(h+k)=[a,b]\triangleright h+[a,b]\triangleright k

holds for all
\lambda\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
,
h,k\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\lambda)
, which is the module algebra condition on
\triangleright
.
∎

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
\mathscr{G}\rtimes_{\triangleright}\widehat{\Lambda}
be a semidirect product of quiver skew braces. Then
\mathscr{G}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\bigsqcup_{a\in\Lambda}\mathscr{G}\times[a,a]
is an ideal. In particular, it is a sub-quiver skew brace, isomorphic to
\bigsqcup_{a\in\Lambda}\mathscr{G}
.

Proof.

Since
\left<a,a,a\right>=a
for all
a
, it is clear that every star of
\mathscr{G}\rtimes\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
is closed under the sum. Moreover, one has

(g\times[a,a])\cdot(h\times[a,a])=g([a,a]\triangleright h)\times[a,a]=gh\times[a,a]

because every unit
[a,a]=1_{a}
in
\widehat{\Lambda}
must act trivially (see e.g.
[
14
, Lemma 4.2 (
ii
)]
), hence
\mathscr{G}\rtimes\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
is a subgroupoid. Observe that, since every
1_{a}
acts trivially, as a sub-quiver skew brace one has indeed
\mathscr{G}\rtimes\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}=\mathscr{G}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}\cong\bigsqcup_{a\in\Lambda}\mathscr{G}
.

We finally observe that
\mathscr{G}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
is stable under
\rightharpoonup
. One has

\displaystyle-(g\times[a,b])+(g\times[a,b])(h\times[b,b])

\displaystyle=(-g)\times[a,\left<a,b,a\right>]+g([a,b]\triangleright h)\times[a,b]

\displaystyle=(-g+g([a,b]\triangleright h))\times[a,\left<\left<a,b,a\right>,a,b\right>]

\displaystyle=(-g+g([a,b]\triangleright h))\times[a,a],

which lies in
\mathscr{G}\times\mathbbm{1}_{\widehat{\Lambda}}
. ∎

3.2.
Categorical semidirect products
à la
Bourn and Janelidze

The categorical notion of semidirect product, in the sense of Bourn and Janelidze
[
5
]
, is a universal object corresponding to split epimorphisms. In the category
{\sf SKB}
of skew braces, the semidirect product was defined by Facchini and Pompili
[
12
]
. In the category
{\sf Gpd}
of groupoids, a categorical semidirect product was described in
[
14
]
, where it was named ‘crossed product’ to avoid confusion with the classical (and more geometrically motivated) notion of semidirect product defined by Brown
[
8
]
. A semidirect product in
{\sf QSKB}
should truss the two respective semidirect products of
{\sf SKB}
and of
{\sf Gpd}
. This is what we pursue here.

Definition 3.4
.

A digroup
(A,+,\cdot)
is the datum of two group structures on the same set, with same unit
1
.

Skew braces are in particular digroups.

Definition 3.5

(Facchini and Pompili
[
12
]
)
.

Let
(N,+,\cdot)
and
(H,+,\cdot)
be digroups. Suppose given:

(
i
)

a right action
\triangleleft
of
(H,\cdot)
on
N
, by group automorphisms of
(N,\cdot)
;

(
ii
)

a right action
\varphi
of
(H,+)
on
N
, by group automorphisms of
(N,+)
;

(
iii
)

a morphism of pointed sets
\gamma\colon(H,1)\to(\Aut_{{\sf Set}^{*}}(N,1),\mathrm{id})
,
h\mapsto\gamma_{h}
, where
{\sf Set}^{*}
is the category of pointed sets.

The
semidirect product

H\ltimes N
is defined as the set
H\times N
, with group operations

\displaystyle(h,a)\cdot(k,b)

\displaystyle=(hk,a(b\triangleleft h)),

\displaystyle(h,a)+(k,b)

\displaystyle=(h+k,\gamma_{h+k}^{-1}(\varphi_{k}\gamma_{h}(a)+\gamma_{k}(b))).

It can be proven that this is a digroup
[
12
, Theorem 4.1]
, and it is the semidirect product in the category of digroups
[
12
, Theorem 3.1]
. If
N
and

are skew braces, then

is moreover a skew brace if and only if
\gamma\colon(N,\cdot)\to\Aut_{{\sf Gp}}(H,+)
is a group homomorphism with image in the additive automorphisms of
H
, and the additional compatibility

(10)

\begin{split}&\gamma_{k_{1}k_{2}-k_{1}+k_{1}k_{3}}^{-1}\!\Big(\!\varphi_{-k_{1}+k_{1}k_{3}}\big(\!\gamma_{k_{1}k_{2}}(\!(a_{1}\!\triangleleft\!k_{2})a_{2})-\gamma_{k_{1}}\!(a_{1})\!\big)+\gamma_{k_{1}k_{3}}(\!(a_{1}\!\triangleleft\!k_{3})a_{3})\!\Big)\\
&=(a_{1}\triangleleft(k_{2}+k_{3}))\gamma_{k_{2}+k_{3}}^{-1}\Big(\varphi_{k_{3}}\gamma_{k_{2}}(a_{2})+\gamma_{k_{3}}(a_{3})\Big)\end{split}

holds for all
a_{i}\in N
,
k_{i}\in H

[
12

. The condition (

Section

and
Section

3.2
).

Hereafter, we assume that both

and

is a homomorphism from

into the additive automorphisms of

.

Remark 3.6
.

The inverses of
(h,a)
with respect to the two group operations of
H\ltimes N
are, respectively:

\displaystyle(h,a)^{-1}

\displaystyle=(h^{-1},a^{-1}\triangleleft h^{-1}),

\displaystyle-(h,a)

\displaystyle=\big(-h,\,-\gamma_{-h}^{-1}\left(\varphi_{h}^{-1}\gamma_{h}(a)\right)\big).

The former is simply the inverse of an element in a semidirect product of groups, while the latter is verified to be the additive inverse via an immediate computation.

For the groupoidal setting, using a heap-theoretic formulation of these semidirect products will be inevitable, as we shall see in
Section

3.2
. Thus we first rewrite the additive side of Facchini and Pompili’s semidirect product in the heap-theoretic form.

Lemma 3.7
.

The heap structure induced by the group
(H\ltimes N,+)
reads as follows:

\displaystyle\left<(h_{1},a_{1}),(h_{2},a_{2}),(h_{3},a_{3})\right>=

\displaystyle\Big(\left<h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\right>,\;\gamma_{\left<h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}\right>}^{-1}\left(\left<\varphi_{h_{3}}\varphi_{h_{2}}^{-1}\gamma_{h_{1}}(a_{1}),\;\varphi_{h_{3}}\varphi_{h_{2}}^{-1}\gamma_{h_{2}}(a_{2}),\;\gamma_{h_{3}}(a_{3})\right>\right)\Big),

where we denote with the same brackets

also the heap structure of

.

Proof.

Using the definition of
(H\ltimes N,+)
twice, it is immediate to compute

\displaystyle(k_{1},b_{1})+(k_{2},b_{2})+(k_{3},b_{3})=

\displaystyle\Big(k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3},\;\gamma^{-1}_{k_{1}+k_{2}+k_{3}}\big(\varphi_{k_{3}}\varphi_{k_{2}}\gamma_{k_{1}}(b_{1})+\varphi_{k_{3}}\gamma_{k_{2}}(b_{2})+\gamma_{k_{3}}(b_{3})\big)\Big).

One concludes by substituting

,
(k_{2},b_{2})=-(h_{2},a_{2})
,
(k_{3},b_{3})=(h_{3},a_{3})
.
∎

Lemma 3.8
.

The compatibility (

\begin{split}&\mathrm{T}_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}}\Big((m\triangleleft h_{2}^{-1}h_{1})b_{1},m,(m\triangleleft h_{2}^{-1}h_{3})b_{3})\Big)\\
&=(m\triangleleft\left<h_{2}^{-1}h_{1},1,h_{2}^{-1}h_{3}\right>)\mathrm{T}_{h_{2}^{-1}h_{1},1,h_{2}^{-1}h_{3}}(b_{1},1,b_{3})\end{split}

Lemma 3.9

Under the assumption of (

\mathrm{T}_{kh_{1},kh_{2},kh_{3}}=\mathrm{T}_{h_{1},h_{2},h_{3}}

\displaystyle\mathrm{T}_{kh_{1},kh_{2},kh_{3}}(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})

Completely prunable quiver skew braces

Let
\mathscr{G}
be connected and completely prunable. This means that
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}
is also a quiver skew brace, and hence
\mathscr{G}^{0}
has a heap structure
\left<\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}},\raisebox{-2.0pt}{\text{---}}\right>
.

Lemma 4.6
.

If
\mathscr{G}
is completely prunable and connected, then the short exact sequence of quiver skew braces

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbbm{1}_{\mathscr{G}^{0}}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathscr{G}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces 1,}

splits.

Proof.

Let
\zeta
be a chosen vertex in
\mathscr{G}^{0}
. For each
a\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
, we choose an arrow
x_{\zeta,a}\colon\zeta\to a
. This choice can easily be extended to a section of groupoids
s\colon\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}\to\mathscr{G}
, as it is well known, by setting
s^{1}([\zeta,a])=x_{\zeta,a}
and
s^{1}([a,b])=x_{a,b}=[\zeta,a]^{-1}[\zeta,b]
. Let
\mathscr{U}
be the coarse subgroupoid of
\mathscr{G}
thus obtained.

We define
x_{\zeta,a}+_{\zeta}x_{\zeta,b}=x_{\zeta,\left<a,\zeta,b\right>}
. We now prove that there is a unique way of extending
+_{\zeta}
to
\{+_{a}\mid a\in\mathscr{G}^{0}\}
so that
\mathscr{U}
becomes a quiver skew brace. The request

\displaystyle x_{\zeta,a}(x_{a,b}+_{a}x_{a,c})

\displaystyle=x_{\zeta,a}x_{a,b}-_{\zeta}x_{\zeta,a}+_{\zeta}x_{\zeta,a}x_{a,c}

\displaystyle=x_{\zeta,b}-_{\zeta}x_{\zeta,a}+_{\zeta}x_{\zeta,c}

\displaystyle=x_{\zeta,\left<b,\zeta,\left<\left<\zeta,a,\zeta\right>,\zeta,c\right>\right>}

\displaystyle=x_{\zeta,\left<b,a,c\right>}

implies
x_{a,b}+_{a}x_{a,c}=x_{\zeta,a}^{-1}x_{\zeta,\left<b,a,c\right>}=x_{a,\left<b,a,c\right>}
. This is the only way to extend
+_{\zeta}
to a quiver skew brace structure.

We now need to check that this extension is indeed a quiver skew brace structure. First, we observe that
+_{a}
is a group operation on
\{x_{a,b}\mid b\in\mathscr{G}^{0}\}
: this follows immediately from the fact that the operation
\cdot_{a}
on
\mathscr{G}^{0}
, given by
b\cdot_{a}c=\left<b,a,c\right>
, is a group operation. Finally, we observe that the quiver skew brace compatibility holds everywhere, and not just when the starting vertex is
\zeta
: this verification is immediate, and analogous to the computations done above for
\zeta
.
∎

From now on, we fix a section
s
and we simply identify
x_{a,b}
with
[a,b]
.

Remark 4.7
.

Fix
\zeta\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
, so that
\mathscr{G}^{0}
becomes a group with
a\cdot b=\left<a,\zeta,b\right>
; and let
G=\mathscr{G}_{\zeta}
. Every
x\in\mathrm{St}_{\mathscr{G}}(\zeta)
can be written uniquely as
g[\zeta,a]
for
g\in G
and
a\in\mathscr{G}^{0}
: indeed for
x\colon\zeta\to a
one has that
x[\zeta,a]^{-1}=g\in G
is a loop on
\zeta
.

Since
\mathscr{G}
is connected and we have chosen a coarse subgroupoid
\mathscr{U}
, every isotropy group
\mathscr{G}_{a}
is canonically isomorphic to
G
via

\mathscr{G}_{a}\to\mathscr{G}_{\zeta},\quad x\mapsto[\zeta,a]x[\zeta,a]^{-1}.

Thus, with a similar trick as above, every
x\in\mathscr{G}(a,b)
is entirely determined by the datum of
[a,b]\in\mathscr{U}
and
g\in G
, where
g=[\zeta,a]x[a,b]^{-1}[\zeta,a]^{-1}=[\zeta,a]x[b,\zeta]
. This yields a bijection between
\mathscr{G}^{1}
and
G\times\mathscr{G}^{0}\times\mathscr{G}^{0}
.

Corollary 4.8
.

If
\mathscr{G}
is a completely prunable quiver skew brace, then
\mathscr{G}
is isomorphic to
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}\mathop{}\mathopen{\vphantom{{\blacktriangleright\kern-3.0pt<}}}\kern-0.5pt{{\blacktriangleright\kern-3.0pt<}}\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}
as quiver skew braces.

Proof.

It follows from
Section

3.2
and the above considerations.
∎

As it happens for
{\sf Gpd}
, the isomorphism
\mathscr{G}\cong\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}\mathop{}\mathopen{\vphantom{{\blacktriangleright\kern-3.0pt<}}}\kern-0.5pt{{\blacktriangleright\kern-3.0pt<}}\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}
is not canonical, and it depends on the choice of a section
\mathscr{G}/\mathscr{G}^{\circlearrowright}\to\mathscr{G}
, that is, of a maximal Schurian sub-quiver skew brace of
\mathscr{G}
.
