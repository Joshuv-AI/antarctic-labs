 1. Executive Summary

 The rapid growth in energy demand, driven by advancements in AI technology, renewable
energy integration, and increased industrial activity, highlights the need for innovative solutions
to improve the resiliency and efficiency of the power grid. This report details the fire testing of
GridWrap’s Composite WiRe Wrap, to evaluate the wrap's ability to enhance conductor
performance under wildfire-simulated conditions. Conductors were prepared and tested in a
controlled environment at 400°C for 18 minutes, during which sag and temperature data were
recorded. The results demonstrated that the unwrapped conductor sagged 3/16ths of an inch,
while the WiRe Wrapped conductor sagged only 1/16th of an inch under identical conditions,
indicating a significant improvement in mechanical performance. This project, conducted as part
of a collaborative effort between BEA (Idaho National Laboratory’s managing contractor) and
GridWrap, Inc., demonstrates the potential of grid-enhancing technologies like WiRe Wrap to
reduce sag, increase grid resiliency, and support the future energy landscape. By validating the
performance of such technologies in real-world scenarios, this work provides critical insights for
industry adoption, benefiting both grid operators and U.S. taxpayers through improved
reliability, reduced emissions, and enhanced energy delivery.

 2. Fire Testing Process

Conductor Preparation and End Attachment:
 This testing utilized conductor made by Southwire, specifically Southwire’s ACSR Turkey.
The preparation process began by uncoiling the conductor and ensuring it was straightened after
transport. Once straightened, the WiRe Wrap was installed by clamping it evenly along the
length of the conductor. The clamps were tightened securely to hold the wrap in place. Dead-end
clamps were then attached and tightened to the ends of the conductor, and the conductor was
placed onto the fire table test stand. The fire table test stand was housed inside a test chamber
designed to ensure maximum ventilation and centralized heat control for safety. Tensioning
weights, each weighing 120 pounds (equivalent to 10% of the conductor's Rated Breaking
Strength), were attached to the dead ends using chains and carabiners. Finally, the conductor was
positioned as close as possible to the thermocouples on the fire table to accurately measure the
heat generated by the fire.

Camera Placement and Set Up:
 A Basler Ace 2 A2A5060 high-speed camera was used to capture the sag of the conductor
during exposure to high temperatures. To facilitate the sag measurement, a ruler was bolted to
the fire table stand next to the thermocouples and behind the conductor. Challenges with ruler
visibility and fluctuating light from the flames were resolved through two adjustments: an
additional light source was added inside the test chamber that was removed from direct heat of
the fire but could point directly at the conductor and ruler and a red powder-coated ruler with
white hash marks was utilized, which provided better visibility against the bright glow of the
flame and didn’t allow for any reflective-ness on the red surface.
The camera and tripod were set up outside the test chamber, positioned to view the conductor
through a dual-pane window with a new sheet of plexiglass for optimal clarity. The tripod was
elevated to align the camera with the conductor. The camera was connected to the Pylon Viewer
program, where the focus, aperture, and other settings were adjusted and locked for the duration
of the test. Trial runs were conducted to refine the settings, ensuring that the images could be
captured in full quality without overloading the camera’s internal cache. Once the ideal settings
were applied, the system was prepared for the next step.
The temperature logging program was initiated. Care was taken to ensure that the correct
thermocouples were being recorded to achieve accurate results and consistent temperature
readings throughout the conductor. With all systems in place to measure, record, and monitor the
process, the fire test could commence.

Fire Test:
 The fire testing was conducted in a controlled environment designed to replicate the extreme
conditions that conductors might encounter during a wildfire. The test chamber was equipped with
a fire testing table, which featured six burners, a 2 x 4 burn area, adjustable height for flame
exposure, and thermocouples for recording temperature.

Once the conductors were positioned, the fire testing commenced by igniting the burners to
produce flames that simulated wildfire conditions. For the purposes of testing the WiRe Wrap by
GridWrap, the temperature of 400°C was selected and a duration of 18 minutes, allowing sufficient
time to observe the effects of heat on the conductors while collecting data on temperature and
material sag. Data was recorded continuously throughout the duration of the test. After the test was
completed, the specimens were allowed to cool before being removed from the chamber.

A total of three tests were conducted. The first was an initial trial that served as a learning
experiment. During this first test, a silver ruler was used for measurement but deemed unsuitable
due to its reflective surface, which made it difficult to read the sag. Additionally, this trial provided
an opportunity to refine the camera settings to ensure optimal performance in future tests.

Following the initial trial, two definitive tests were conducted under identical conditions: one with
an unwrapped conductor and one with a WiRe Wrapped conductor.

Results:
 Two fire tests were conducted to evaluate the sag performance of GridWrap’s WiRe Wrap
under wildfire-simulated conditions. In the first test, the ACSR Turkey conductor without
GridWrap's WiRe Wrap was subjected to a temperature of 400°C for a duration of 18 minutes.
During this test, the conductor sagged 3/16ths of an inch. In the second test, the ACSR Turkey
conductor wrapped with GridWrap's WiRe Wrap was subjected to the same temperature and
duration. Under these identical conditions, the conductor with the WiRe Wrap sagged 2/16ths of
an inch. These results demonstrate a 1/16ths reduction in sag when the conductor was equipped
with GridWrap's WiRe Wrap, highlighting its potential to improve conductor performance under
extreme heat conditions. Photos of the sag can be seen in the appendix.
