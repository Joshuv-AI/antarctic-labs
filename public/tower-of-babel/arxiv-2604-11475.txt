Introduction and Overview

Let
I
be an ideal in a commutative Noetherian ring
R
. Then a prime ideal
\mathfrak{p}\subset R
is an
associated prime
of
I
if there exists
an element
u
in
R
such that
\mathfrak{p}=(I:_{R}u)
, where
(I:_{R}u)=\{r\in R\mid ru\in I\}
. The
set of associated primes
of
I
, denoted by
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I)
, is the set of all prime ideals associated to
I
. In
[
4
]
, Brodmann proved that the sequence
\{\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I^{n})\}_{n\geq 1}
of associated prime ideals is stationary for large
n
, i.e., there exists a positive integer
n_{0}
such that
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I^{n})=\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I^{n_{0}})
for all
n\geq n_{0}
. The minimal such
n_{0}
is called the
index of stability
of
I
and
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I^{n_{0}})
is called the
stable set

of associated prime ideals of
I
, which is denoted by
\mathrm{Ass}^{\infty}(I).

This paper has two main sections. Section
2
is concerned with the study of the notion of the strong persistence index of an ideal.
Assume that
I
is an ideal in a commutative Noetherian ring
R
. We say that a positive integer
\ell_{0}
is the
strong persistence index
of
I
if
\ell_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
(I^{\ell+1}:_{R}I)=I^{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. The ideal
I
is said to have the
strong persistence property
if
\ell_{0}=1
. The concept of the strong persistence property of ideals has been investigated over the last decade, for more details and information consult
[
1
,
3
,
7
,
8
,
9
,
11
,
12
]
.
In this section, by means of Example
2.3
and Lemmata
2.7
and
2.10
, we show that there exist infinitely many monomial ideals in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
n\geq 3
, that have the same strong persistence index
\ell_{0}\geq 2
, cf. Theorem
2.11
.
We conclude this section with some results on the strong persistence index of ideals in commutative Noetherian rings.

Section
3
is devoted to exploring the second topic of our study. We say that an ideal
I
in a commutative Noetherian
R

has the phenomenon of
fluctuation in colon powers

if there exist positive integers
a<b<c
such that at least one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

(I^{a}:I)=I^{a-1},\quad(I^{b}:I)\neq I^{b-1},\quad\text{but}\quad(I^{c}:I)=I^{c-1}.

(ii)

(I^{a}:I)\neq I^{a-1},\quad(I^{b}:I)=I^{b-1},\quad\text{but}\quad(I^{c}:I)\neq I^{c-1}.

A natural question is whether there exist infinitely many monomial ideals in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
n\geq 2
, whose colon powers exhibit fluctuation. This question is answered in the affirmative in Theorem
3.4
.

Throughout this paper,
\mathcal{G}(I)
denotes the unique minimal set of monomial generators of a monomial ideal
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where

R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring over a field
K
. Furthermore, the
support
of a monomial
u\in R
, denoted by
\mathrm{supp}(u)
, is the set of variables that divide
u
. Moreover, for a monomial ideal
I
, we set
\mathrm{supp}(I)=\bigcup_{u\in\mathcal{G}(I)}\mathrm{supp}(u)
.

2.
On the strong persistence index of monomial ideals

First, recall that for an ideal
I
in a commutative Noetherian ring
R
, we say that a positive integer
\ell_{0}
is the
strong persistence index
of
I
if
\ell_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
(I^{\ell+1}:_{R}I)=I^{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. Suppose now that
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a polynomial ring
over a field
K
. It is natural to ask whether there exists a monomial ideal
I\subset R
with
\mathrm{supp}(I)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
that has the
strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
. We provide a positive answer to this question in Theorem
2.11
. To do this, we start with
Lemma
2.2
and Example
2.3
. Next, we focus on Lemmata
2.7
and
2.10
.

Before establishing Lemma
2.2
, we recall the following proposition.

Proposition 2.1
.

(
[
10
, Proposition 4.2]
)
Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
over a field
K
with
\mathcal{G}(I)=\{u_{1},\ldots,u_{m}\}
and
\mathrm{Ass}_{R}(R/I)=\{\mathfrak{p}_{1},\ldots,\mathfrak{p}_{s}\}
. Then, the following statements hold.

(i)

If
x_{i}|u_{t}
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq n
, and
for some
t
with
1\leq t\leq m
, then there exists
j
with
1\leq j\leq s,

such that
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
.

(ii)

If
x_{i}\in\mathfrak{p}_{j}
for some
i
with
1\leq i\leq n
, and for some
j
with
1\leq j\leq s
, then there exists
t
with

1\leq t\leq m
, such that
x_{i}|u_{t}
.

Especially,
\bigcup_{j=1}^{s}\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{j})=\bigcup_{t=1}^{m}\mathrm{supp}(u_{t})
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
I\subset R=K[x,y,z]
be a monomial ideal,
\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,z)
, and
I=Q\cap L
be a minimal primary decomposition of
I
such that

\sqrt{Q}\in\{(x,y),(x,z),(y,z)\}
and
\sqrt{L}=\mathfrak{m}
. Also, let
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t})
for some
t\geq 2
.
Then
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{\alpha})
for all
\alpha\geq t
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t+1})
. Suppose, on the contrary, that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t+1})
.
According to
\mathrm{Min}(I)=\{\sqrt{Q}\}
, we can deduce that
\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t+1})=\{\sqrt{Q},\mathfrak{m}\}
. It is well-known that

I^{t+1}
is generated by

\{uv\mid u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\text{ and }v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t})\}.

Without loss of generality, assume that
\sqrt{Q}=(x,y)
.
Let
f=uv
with
u\in\mathcal{G}(I)
and
v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t})
. In what follows, we prove that there exists
f_{1}\in I^{t+1}

with
z\nmid f_{1}
such that
f_{1}\mid f
. Since
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t})
and
\mathrm{Min}(I)=\{\sqrt{Q}\}
, it follows that

\mathrm{Ass}(R/I^{t})=\{\sqrt{Q}\}
, and by virtue of
z\notin(x,y)
, we can conclude from Proposition
2.1
that
z\nmid v
. If
z\nmid u
, then
z\nmid uv
, and so the argument is done. Hence, let
z\mid u
. Since
v\in\mathcal{G}(I^{t})
, hence there exist
g_{1},\ldots,g_{t}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
v=g_{1}\cdots g_{t}
. As
ug_{2}\cdots g_{t}\in I^{t}
, we derive that there exist
m_{1},\ldots,m_{t}\in\mathcal{G}(I)
such that
z\nmid m_{1}\cdots m_{t}
and
m_{1}\cdots m_{t}\mid ug_{2}\cdots g_{t}
. Set
f_{1}:=m_{1}\cdots m_{t}g_{1}
. On account of
f_{1}\mid ug_{1}g_{2}\cdots g_{t}
,
f_{1}\in I^{t+1}
, and
z\nmid f_{1}
, this finishes the proof.
∎

As an application of Lemma
2.2
, we present the following example. Notably, the construction of this example draws from
[
6
, Theorem 4.1]
.

Example 2.3
.

Assume that
I=(x^{m+3},y^{m+3},x^{m+2}y,xy^{m+2},x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
with
m,r\geq 1
is a monomial ideal in
R=K[x,y,z]
and

\mathfrak{m}=(x,y,z)
. We show that

\mathrm{Ass}(I^{s})=\begin{cases}\{(x,y),\mathfrak{m}\}&\text{if }1\leq s\leq m\\
\{(x,y)\}&\text{if }s\geq m+1.\\
\end{cases}

We first note that

I=(x,y^{m+3})\cap(x^{m+3},y)\cap(x^{m+1},y^{m+2})\cap(x^{m+2},y^{2})\cap(x^{m+2},y^{m+2},z^{r}).

This yields that
\mathrm{Ass}(I)=\{(x,y),(x,y,z)\}
. In view of
\mathrm{Min}(I)=\{(x,y)\}
,
we get
\{(x,y)\}\subseteq\mathrm{Ass}(I^{t})\subseteq\{(x,y),\mathfrak{m}\}
for all
t\geq 1
.

In what follows, we assume
1\leq s\leq m
, and our aim is to show that
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Ass}(I^{s})
. To do this, put
h:=x^{m+1}y^{s(m+2)-1}z^{r-1}
. Since
s\leq m
, we obtain the following

(1)

xh=x^{m+2}y^{s(m+2)-1}z^{r-1}
. Due to
(x^{m+2}y)(y^{m+3})^{s-1}\mid xh
, this leads to
xh\in I^{s}
.

(2)

yh=x^{m+1}y^{s(m+2)}z^{r-1}
. Because
(xy^{m+2})^{s}\mid yh
, we have
yh\in I^{s}
.

(3)

zh=x^{m+1}y^{s(m+2)-1}z^{r}
. Since
(y^{m+3})^{s-1}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})\mid zh
, this gives that
zh\in I^{s}
.

Consequently, we get
(x,y,z)\subseteq(I^{s}:h)
for all
1\leq s\leq m
. Here, we show that
h\notin I^{s}
. On the contrary, assume that
h\in I^{s}
. Hence, there exist nonnegative integers
a,b,c,d,
and
e
with
a+b+c+d+e=s
such that

(x^{m+3})^{a}(y^{m+3})^{b}(x^{m+2}y)^{c}(xy^{m+2})^{d}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})^{e}\mid h.

This gives the following conditions:

(i)

(m+3)a+(m+2)c+d+(m+1)e\leq m+1
;

(ii)

(m+3)b+c+(m+2)d+2e\leq s(m+2)-1
;

(iii)

re\leq r-1
;

(iv)

a+b+c+d+e=s
, where
a,b,c,d,e\geq 0
.

It follows from (iii) that
e=0
, and by (iv), we get
a+b+c+d=s
. If
a\geq 1
or
c\geq 1
, this contradicts (i). Therefore, we must have
a=0
and
c=0
.
Since
a+b+c+d=s
, we gain
d=s-b
. Now, the left hand side of (ii) is
ms+2s+b
, while the right hand side of (ii) is
ms+2s-1
. Because
b\geq 0
, this
leads to a contradiction. Accordingly,
h\notin I^{s}
, and so
\mathfrak{m}\in\mathrm{Ass}(I^{s})
for all
1\leq s\leq m
.

To complete our argument, we need to verify that
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(I^{s})
for all
s\geq m+1
. From now on, our strategy is to rely on
Lemma
2.2
. For this purpose, it is sufficient for us to show
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(I^{m+1})
.
We claim if
v\in I^{m+1}
, then there exists
f\in I^{m+1}
with
z\nmid f
such that
f\mid v
. Pick

(
\star
)

v=(x^{m+3})^{a}(y^{m+3})^{b}(x^{m+2}y)^{c}(xy^{m+2})^{d}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})^{e}\in I^{m+1},

with
a\geq 0,

b\geq 0,

c\geq 0,

d\geq 0
,
e\geq 1
, and
a+b+c+d+e=m+1
. Because
(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})^{m+1}=(x^{m+2}y)^{m}(xy^{m+2})z^{r(m+1)}
,
this permits us to assume that
1\leq e\leq m
. We first show the claim holds when
e=1
. Let
m=1
.
Then we have
I=(x^{4},y^{4},x^{3}y,xy^{3},x^{2}y^{2}z^{r})
, and we may meet the following cases:

\bullet

y^{4}(x^{3}y)\mid(xy^{3})(x^{2}y^{2}z^{r})
, where
y^{4}(x^{3}y)\in\mathcal{G}(I^{2})
.

\bullet

x^{4}(xy^{3})\mid(x^{3}y)(x^{2}y^{2}z^{r})
, where
x^{4}(xy^{3})\in\mathcal{G}(I^{2})
.

\bullet

(xy^{3})^{2}\mid y^{4}(x^{2}y^{2}z^{r})
, where
(xy^{3})^{2}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{2})
.

\bullet

(x^{3}y)^{2}\mid x^{4}(x^{2}y^{2}z^{r})
, where
(x^{3}y)^{2}\in\mathcal{G}(I^{2})
.

Now, let
m\geq 2
. One can easily see the following statements:

(1)

x^{m+3}(x^{m+2}y)^{2}\mid(x^{m+3})^{2}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(2)

y^{m+3}(x^{m+2}y)^{2}\mid x^{m+3}y^{m+3}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(3)

x^{m+3}(y^{m+3})^{2}\mid(xy^{m+2})^{2}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(4)

(x^{m+2}y)^{3}\mid x^{m+3}(x^{m+2}y)(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(5)

x^{m+3}y^{m+3}(x^{m+2}y)\mid x^{m+3}(xy^{m+2})(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(6)

y^{m+3}(x^{m+2}y)^{2}\mid(x^{m+2}y)(xy^{m+2})(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(7)

(y^{m+3})^{2}(x^{m+2}y)\mid y^{m+3}(xy^{m+2})(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
.

(8)

(x^{m+3})^{m-\alpha}(xy^{m+2})^{\alpha+1}\mid(y^{m+3})^{\alpha}(x^{m+2}y)^{m-\alpha}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
, where

0\leq\alpha\leq m
.

By considering the statements above, we can deduce that the claim is true when
e=1
and
m\geq 2
.
Now, we use induction on
m
. Let
m=1
. Then
e=1
. The argument above states that our claim holds in case
m=1
. Now, suppose, inductively, that
m\geq 2
and that the result has been proved for all values less than
m
. Write
v=v_{1}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
. It follows from (
\star
) that

v_{1}\in I^{m}
, and so we can conclude from the inductive hypothesis that there exists a monomial
f_{1}\in I^{m}
with
z\nmid f_{1}
such that
f_{1}\mid v_{1}
. Furthermore, in view of the statements (1)–(8), we can derive that there exists a monomial
f_{2}\in I^{m+1}
with
z\nmid f_{2}
such that
f_{2}\mid f_{1}(x^{m+1}y^{2}z^{r})
, and hence

. This completes the inductive step, and thus the claim is proved by induction.

Since
\mathfrak{m}\notin\mathrm{Ass}(I^{m+1})
, based on Lemma
2.2
, this gives rise to

In the sequel, we recall the definition of the weighting operation, beginning with the following definition.

Definition 2.5
.

A weight over a polynomial ring
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
is a function
W:\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
such that
w_{i}=W(x_{i})
is called the weight of the variable
x_{i}
. For a monomial ideal
I\subset R
and a weight
W
, we define the weighted ideal, denoted
by
I_{W}
, to be the ideal generated by
\{h(u):u\in\mathcal{G}(I)\}
, where
h
is the unique homomorphism
h:R\rightarrow R
given by

h(x_{i})=x_{i}^{w_{i}}
.

As an example, assume
R=K[x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}]
and the monomial ideal
I=(x_{1}^{2}x_{3}x_{4}^{5},x_{2}^{4}x_{4}^{3}x_{5}^{2},x_{1}x_{3}^{2},x_{4}x_{5}^{3})
in
R
.
Also, let
W:\{x_{1},x_{2},x_{3},x_{4},x_{5}\}\rightarrow\mathbb{N}
be a weight over
R
with
W(x_{1})=1
,
W(x_{2})=4
,
W(x_{3})=2
,
W(x_{4})=3
, and
W(x_{5})=2
. It is easy to check that the weighted ideal
I_{W}
is given by

I_{W}=(x_{1}^{2}x_{3}^{2}x_{4}^{15},x_{2}^{16}x_{4}^{9}x_{5}^{4},x_{1}x_{3}^{4},x_{4}^{3}x_{5}^{6}).

(I\cap J)_{W}=I_{W}\cap J_{W}

(iv)

(I:_{R}J)_{W}=(I_{W}:_{R}J_{W})
.

We are now in a position to state and prove Lemma

, which is essential for formulating Theorem

Lemma 2.7
.

Let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal and
\ell_{0}\geq 1
. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

I
has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
if and only if
I^{*}
has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
,
where

denotes the expansion of

(ii)

I
has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
if and only if
I_{W}
has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
,
where
I_{W}
denotes the weighted ideal.

Proof.

(i) We first observe that for any two monomial ideals

and
H
, Lemma
2.4
(i) implies that

if and only if
H^{*}=F^{*}
.
Now, we assume that

has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
. This implies that
\ell_{0}
is the smallest integer such that
(I^{\ell+1}:_{R}I)=I^{\ell}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. According to Lemma
2.4
(v), we have
(I^{\ell+1}:_{R}I)^{*}=((I^{\ell+1})^{*}:_{R^{*}}I^{*})
, and hence

((I^{\ell+1})^{*}:_{R^{*}}I^{*})=(I^{\ell})^{*}
for all
\ell\geq\ell_{0}
. It follows from Lemma

(iii) that

(I^{\ell+1})^{*}=(I^{*})^{\ell+1}

((I^{*})^{\ell+1}:_{R^{*}}I^{*})=(I^{*})^{\ell}

((I^{*})^{\ell^{\prime}+1}:_{R^{*}}I^{*})=(I^{*})^{\ell^{\prime}}

that

, which contradicts the minimality of
\ell_{0}
.
Consequently, we get

has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
.
By a similar argument, we can show the converse.

(ii) Note that for any two monomial ideals

and

, we have

if and only if

. Now, the claim can be established by combining Lemma
2.6
and mimicking the proof of part (i).

To formulate Theorem

(L^{s}:_{S}L)\subseteq L^{s-1}
, and so
(L^{s}:_{S}L)=L^{s-1}
.
Accordingly, we deduce that
L
has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}=m+1
.
Now, let
\mathfrak{p}_{1}:=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{a}})
,
\mathfrak{p}_{2}:=(x_{i_{a+1}},\ldots,x_{i_{b}})
, and
\mathfrak{p}_{3}:=(x_{i_{b+1}},\ldots,x_{i_{c}})
with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})\cap\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{j})=\emptyset
for all
1\leq i<j\leq 3
and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{3}\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{i})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
We can promptly deduce from Lemma
2.7
(i) that
L^{*}
in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}
. Put
I:=(L^{*})_{W}
with
W(x_{i})=\alpha_{i}
such that
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
It follows from Lemma
2.7
(ii) that
I
also has the strong persistence index
\ell_{0}\geq 2
. Accordingly, we can conclude that there exist
infinitely many such monomial ideals in
R
that have the same strong persistence index
\ell_{0}\geq 2
. This finishes the proof.
∎

We conclude this section with the following results on the strong persistence index of ideals in commutative Noetherian rings.

Proposition 2.12
.

Let
R
be a commutative Noetherian ring and let
I
be an ideal of
R
whose strong persistence index is
\ell_{0}
. Then

(I^{r}:I^{s})=I^{r-s}\quad\text{for all }r-s\geq\ell_{0}.

Proof.

Take
m\in(I^{r}:I^{s})
. Then
mI^{s}\subseteq I^{r}
. Take any
h\in I^{s-1}
and
g\in I
. Since
hg\in I^{s}
, it follows that

(mh)g=m(hg)\in I^{r}\quad\text{for all }g\in I.

Thus
mh\in(I^{r}:I)
for all
h\in I^{s-1}
. Since
r\geq\ell_{0}+1
, we have

(I^{r}:I)=I^{r-1},

and hence
mh\in I^{r-1}
for all
h\in I^{s-1}
. This implies that

m\in(I^{r-1}:I^{s-1}).

Repeating this argument inductively
s-1
times, we obtain

m\in(I^{r-(s-1)}:I^{s-(s-1)})=(I^{r-s+1}:I).

Since
r-s+1\geq\ell_{0}+1
, it follows that
(I^{r-s+1}:I)=I^{r-s}.

Therefore,
m\in I^{r-s}
, and the proof is complete.
∎

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative Noetherian ring
R
. Then

\ell_{0}(I^{k})<\frac{\ell_{0}(I)}{k}+1

for all integers
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

Fix
k\geq 1
and set
\ell_{0}^{\prime}:=\ell_{0}(I^{k})
. By definition, we can deduce that

((I^{k})^{\ell_{0}^{\prime}+1}:I^{k})=(I^{k})^{\ell_{0}^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad((I^{k})^{\ell_{0}^{\prime}}:I^{k})\neq(I^{k})^{\ell_{0}^{\prime}-1}.

Equivalently,

(I^{k(\ell_{0}^{\prime}+1)}:I^{k})=I^{k\ell_{0}^{\prime}}\quad\text{and}\quad(I^{k\ell_{0}^{\prime}}:I^{k})\neq I^{k(\ell_{0}^{\prime}-1)}.

Suppose, on the contrary, that
k\ell_{0}^{\prime}-k\geq\ell_{0}(I)
. Then, according to Proposition
2.12
, we get

(I^{k\ell_{0}^{\prime}}:I^{k})=I^{k\ell_{0}^{\prime}-k},
which contradicts the minimality of
\ell_{0}^{\prime}
. Hence, we must have

k\ell_{0}^{\prime}-k<\ell_{0}(I),
and therefore

\ell_{0}^{\prime}<\frac{\ell_{0}(I)}{k}+1.

∎

Proposition 2.14
.

Let
I
be an ideal of a commutative Noetherian ring
R
. Then

\ell_{0}(I^{k})\leq\ell_{0}(I)

for all integers
k\geq 1
.

Proof.

From Proposition
2.13
, we have the following inequalities

\ell_{0}(I^{k})<\frac{\ell_{0}(I)}{k}+1\leq\ell_{0}(I)+1.

Since
\ell_{0}(I^{k})
is an integer, it follows that
\ell_{0}(I^{k})\leq\ell_{0}(I)
, as desired.
∎

3.
On the phenomenon of fluctuations in colon powers of monomial ideals

This section is devoted to exploring the second topic of our study.
Suppose that
I
is a monomial ideal in a polynomial ring
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
.
Another natural line of study in this context concerns the behavior of colon ideals of powers of
I
as follows:

Definition 3.1
.

Let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal. We say that
I
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers
if there exist positive integers
a<b<c
such that at least one of the following cases occurs:

(i)

(I^{a}:I)=I^{a-1},\quad(I^{b}:I)\neq I^{b-1},\quad\text{but}\quad(I^{c}:I)=I^{c-1}.

(ii)

(I^{a}:I)\neq I^{a-1},\quad(I^{b}:I)=I^{b-1},\quad\text{but}\quad(I^{c}:I)\neq I^{c-1}.

The following example demonstrates that the behavior described in Definition
3.1
may occur for certain monomial ideals.

Example 3.2
.

(i)

Let the monomial ideal
I=(x^{6},y^{6},xy^{5},x^{5}y,x^{4}y^{4})
in
R=K[x,y]
.
Using Macaulay2
[
5
]
, we deduce that

(I^{2}:I)=I,\quad(I^{3}:I)\neq I^{2},\quad(I^{4}:I)\neq I^{3},\quad(I^{5}:I)=I^{4}.

This means that
I
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers.

(ii)

Consider the monomial ideal
J=(x^{7},y^{7},x^{2}y^{5},x^{5}y^{2})\subset R=K[x,y]
. By means of Macaulay2
[
5
]
, we obtain that

(J^{2}:J)\neq J,\quad(J^{3}:J)=J^{2},\quad(J^{4}:J)\neq J^{3},\quad(J^{5}:J)=J^{4}.

We can now immediately deduce that
J
exhibits the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers.

(iii)

Assume the following monomial ideal in
R=K[x,y,z]
,

\displaystyle L=(

\displaystyle x^{8}y^{5},\,x^{7}y^{9},\,x^{6}y^{10},\,x^{4}y^{11},\,x^{13}y^{3}z^{2},\,x^{8}y^{4}z^{2},\,x^{6}y^{9}z^{2},

\displaystyle x^{13}y^{2}z^{3},\,x^{4}y^{9}z^{3},\,x^{4}y^{4}z^{5},\,y^{5}z^{7},\,x^{13}z^{8}).

Using Macaulay2
[
5
]
, we deduce that

(L^{2}:L)=L,\quad(L^{3}:L)\neq L^{2},\quad(L^{4}:L)=L^{3},\quad(L^{5}:L)=L^{4}.

Thus,
L
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers.

It is natural to ask whether there exist infinitely many monomial ideals in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, where
n\geq 2
, that exhibit the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers.
We provide a positive answer to this question in Theorem
3.4
. To achieve this, we first establish the following auxiliary lemma.

Lemma 3.3
.

Let
I\subset R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a monomial ideal. Then the following statements hold.

(i)

I
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers if and only if
I^{*}
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers,
where
I^{*}
denotes the expansion of
I
.

(ii)

I
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers if and only if
I_{W}
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers,
where
I_{W}
denotes the weighted ideal.

Proof.

(i) Recall that for any two monomial ideals
A
and
B
, it follows from Lemma
2.4
(i) that
A=B
if and only if
A^{*}=B^{*}
.
Now, the claim can be shown by parts (iii) and (v) of Lemma
2.4
.

(ii) Remember that for any two monomial ideals
A
and
B
, we always have
A=B
if and only if
A_{W}=B_{W}
. Hence, the assertion can be verified
by parts (ii) and (iv) of Lemma
2.6
.
∎

We are now ready to state and prove the main result of this section in the following theorem.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
with
n\geq 2
be a polynomial ring in
n
variables with coefficients in a field
K
.
Then there exist infinitely many monomial ideals in
R
that have the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers.

Proof.

Let
J
be a monomial ideal in
R=K[x,y]
exhibiting the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers
(see Example
3.2
(ii), which guarantees the existence of such a monomial ideal).
Here, we consider

\mathfrak{p}_{1}:=(x_{i_{1}},\ldots,x_{i_{a}})\quad\text{and}\quad\mathfrak{p}_{2}:=(x_{i_{a+1}},\ldots,x_{i_{b}}),

with
\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cap\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=\emptyset
and

\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{1})\cup\mathrm{supp}(\mathfrak{p}_{2})=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
.
It follows at once from Lemma
3.3
(i) that
J^{*}
in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]

has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers. Now, set
I:=(J^{*})_{W}
with
W(x_{i})=\alpha_{i}
such that
\alpha_{i}\geq 1
for all
i=1,\ldots,n
.
According to Lemma
3.3
(ii), we can conclude that
I
has the phenomenon of fluctuation in colon powers as well.
This implies that there exist infinitely many monomial ideals in
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
that have the phenomenon of
fluctuation in colon powers, as claimed.
∎
