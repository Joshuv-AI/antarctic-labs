Asymptotic properties of solutions to the characteristic problem for the ultrahyperbolic equation
Maxim N. Demchenko

Abstract
The paper concerns the problem for the ultrahyperbolic equation in the Euclidean space with data on a characteristic hyperplane. Smoothness and asymptotics of the solution along characteristic lines transversal to the initial hyperplane are investigated. Keywords: ultrahyperbolic equation, asymptotic behavior of a solution at the infinity, scattering problem.



1
Introduction

We consider the problem for the ultrahyperbolic equation

(\partial^{2}_{ts}+\partial_{x_{1}}^{2}+\ldots+\partial_{x_{d}}^{2}-\partial_{y_{1}}^{2}-\ldots-\partial_{y_{n}}^{2})v=0,

(1.1)

where the solution
v
is a function of variables

(t,s,x_{1},\ldots,x_{d},y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2},\hskip 10.00002ptN=d+n,\hskip 10.00002ptd,n\geqslant 1,

subject to the following condition

v|_{t=0}=v_{0}.

(1.2)

The latter is imposed on the hyperplane
\{t=0\}
, which,
as will be seen later, is a
characteristic
one for equation (
1.1
).
We will consider solutions
v
from the class of
L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
-valued continuous functions of variable
t\in{\mathbb{R}}
.
The meaning of condition (
1.2
) for such functions and data
v_{0}\in L_{2}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
is obvious.
We will demand that equation (
1.1
) be satisfied in the whole space
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
in the sense of distributions.
This also makes sense, as solutions from the class in question can be considered as elements of
L_{2,\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+2})
.

Problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) was first studied in
[
1
]
.
The main result obtained there was a representation of the solution as a convolution of the data
v_{0}
with a certain distribution
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}
.
The latter was given in terms of analytic continuation of a family of distributions with respect to a complex parameter.
The present paper concerns the issues of smoothness of the solution and its asymptotic behavior for large
t
.
We will use the representation of the solution obtained in
[
2
]

(where the well-posedness of the problem was also established).

Our interest in the characteristic problem for equation (
1.1
) is motivated by well-posedness of such a problem
in standard (non-analytic) classes of functions,
in contrast to the Cauchy problem with data on a noncharacteristic hypersurface.
As is well known, in the case of a hyperbolic equation, the Cauchy problem is well-posed exactly when
the initial data are specified on a
spacelike
hypersurface.
However, the latter has no counterparts in the case of ultrahyperbolic equation,
which, in essence, is the reason of ill-posedness of the corresponding Cauchy problem.

The following conservation law for problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) was established in
[
1
]
:

\|v(t,\cdot)\|_{L_{2}}=\|v_{0}\|_{L_{2}},\hskip 10.00002ptt\in{\mathbb{R}}.

This feature, together with the well-posedness,
demonstrates the similarity of the problem with classical evolution problems
for hyperbolic equations.
In particular, it seems to be achievable to develop an analogue of stationary scattering theory for such a problem
considering
t
as a time parameter.
One of the corner stones of such a theory would be the study of the asymptotic behavior of solutions for large
t
,
which is the subject of the present paper.

The majority of results in this field either concern corresponding fundamental solutions
(we mention the pioneering work
[
3
]
and more recent ones
[
4
,
5
]
)
or generalize Asgeirsson’s theorem
[
6
,
7
]
.
In the well-posed problems for ultrahyperbolic equations known so far, the data are specified on a characteristic hypersurface or at the infinity
(the scattering data).
Besides the results of
[
1
]
mentioned above,
we also mention the paper
[
8
]
,
where the well-posedness of the problem in Euclidean space with data on the characteristic cone was established.
Problems with data at the infinity were considered in
[
9
]
.
In the case of hyperbolic equations, such kind of problems were studied first in
[
10
,
11
]

and later in
[
12
,
13
,
14
]
.
As was mentioned above, the Cauchy problem for the ultrahyperbolic equation is ill-posed in general.
However, there are positive results in the case, when the solution is assumed to be symmetric with respect to a part of variables
[
15
]

or the initial data belong to a special class of functions
[
16
]
.
Among other nonclassical problems close to the one considered in the present paper,
the most complete investigation was carried out for the problems connected with
pseudohyperbolic
equations
[
17
]
.

2
Smoothness and asymptotics of the solution to problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
)

The result of the paper concerns the smoothness of the solution to problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) for
t\neq 0

and its behavior for large
t
.
As in the case of hyperbolic equations
[
10
,
11
]
,
asymptotics of solutions along characteristic lines transversal to the initial hyperplane will be established.

To formulate the result,
it is convenient to pass from variables
t,s
to

x_{0}=t+s,\hskip 10.00002pty_{0}=t-s.

Put

\displaystyle\overline{x}=(x_{1},\ldots,x_{d})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d},\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{y}=(y_{1},\ldots,y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n},

\displaystyle x=(x_{0},\overline{x})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1},\hskip 10.00002pty=(y_{0},\overline{y})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}.

Since

\partial_{t}=\partial_{x_{0}}+\partial_{y_{0}},\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{s}=\partial_{x_{0}}-\partial_{y_{0}},\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{t}\partial_{s}=\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}-\partial_{y_{0}}^{2},

the function

u(x,y)=v\left(t,s,\overline{x},\overline{y}\right)

(2.1)

satisfies the following equation

\displaystyle(\Delta_{x}-\Delta_{y})u=0,

(2.2)

\displaystyle\Delta_{x}=\partial_{x_{0}}^{2}+\ldots+\partial_{x_{d}}^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\Delta_{y}=\partial_{y_{0}}^{2}+\ldots+\partial_{y_{n}}^{2}.

In coordinates
(x,y)
, the condition
\{t=0\}
determines the set

\{(x,y)\,|\,x_{0}+y_{0}=0\},

(2.3)

which is a characteristic hyperplane for equation (
2.2
).
Therefore, the set
\{t=0\}
is a characteristic hyperplane for equation (
1.1
).

The result will be given in terms of the function
u
related to the solution
v
by equality (
2.1
).
We will need a description of lines that are characteristic with respect to equation (
2.2
) and transversal to hyperplane (
2.3
).
Such lines consist of points
(x,y)
of the form

\displaystyle x=X+\tau\theta,\hskip 10.00002pty=Y+\tau\omega,

(2.4)

\displaystyle(X,Y,\theta,\omega)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{d+1}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n+1}\times S^{d}\times S^{n}

(
S^{m}
is the unit sphere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{m+1}
),
where
\tau
is a real parameter.
The transversality condition above reads

\theta_{0}+\omega_{0}\neq 0.

(2.5)

In view of the last constraint, we may assume that the “initial point”
(X,Y)
in (
2.4
) (which corresponds to
\tau=0
)
lies on the hyperplane (
2.3
),
which is equivalent to condition

X_{0}+Y_{0}=0.

(2.6)

Under this assumption, a point
(x,y)
corresponding to
\tau\neq 0
lies outside of this hyperplane.
Observe that condition (
2.5
) ensures the point
(x,y)
with large
\tau

corresponds to the point
(t,s,\overline{x},\overline{y})
with large
t
.

Further we will need some estimates locally uniform with respect to
(x,y)
outside of hyperplane (
2.3
).
Any such point together with its small neighborhood can be represented in the form (
2.4
)
with fixed
\theta,\omega
satisfying (
2.5
) and parameters
\tau
,
X,Y
that vary within small neighborhoods
and satisfy conditions (
2.6
) and
\tau\neq 0
.
Thus, in locally uniform estimates, constants may depend arbitrarily on
\theta,\omega,R

providing that

|X|+|Y|\leqslant R.

(2.7)

We will study the function
u(x,y)
and its partial derivatives with multiindex
\beta
:

\partial^{\beta}u=\partial_{x_{0}}^{\beta_{0}}\ldots\partial_{x_{d}}^{\beta_{d}}\,\partial_{y_{0}}^{\beta_{d+1}}\ldots\partial_{y_{n}}^{\beta_{N+1}}u.

The formulation of the result involves the polynomial

P^{\beta}(\theta,\omega)=(i\theta_{0})^{\beta_{0}}\ldots(i\theta_{d})^{\beta_{d}}(-i\omega_{0})^{\beta_{d+1}}\ldots(-i\omega_{n})^{\beta_{N+1}},

and the Fourier transform of the function
v_{0}(s,\overline{x},\overline{y})

defined as follows

\widetilde{v}_{0}(\lambda,\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta})=2\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+1}}e^{-i(s\lambda+\overline{x}\,\overline{\xi}-\overline{y}\,\overline{\eta})}v_{0}(s,\overline{x},\overline{y})\,dsd\overline{x}d\overline{y},\hskip 10.00002pt(\lambda,\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta})\in{\mathbb{R}}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{d}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}.

Here and further expression
wz
for
w,z\in{\mathbb{R}}^{m}
denotes the inner product,

w^{2}:=ww
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
v_{0}(s,\overline{x},\overline{y})
belong to Schwartz class
\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
.
Then the function
u(x,y)
related to the solution of problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) via (
2.1
)
is
C^{\infty}
-smooth outside of hyperplane (
2.3
).
Under assumptions (
2.5
), (
2.6
), (
2.7
), for any point
(x,y)
of the form (
2.4
)
with
\tau\geqslant\tau_{0}>0
, we have

\displaystyle\left|\partial^{\beta}u(x,y)-\tau^{-N/2}F^{\beta}(\theta,\omega,p)\right|\leqslant C_{R,\tau_{0},\theta,\omega,\beta,v_{0}}\tau^{-(N+1)/2},

(2.8)

\displaystyle F^{\beta}(\theta,\omega,p):=P^{\beta}(\theta,\omega)|\theta_{0}+\omega_{0}|\int_{\mathbb{R}}e^{irp}G^{\beta}_{d,n}(r)\widetilde{v}_{0}(r(\theta_{0}+\omega_{0}),r\overline{\theta},r\overline{\omega})dr,

\displaystyle G^{\beta}_{d,n}(r):=\frac{e^{i({\rm sgn}\,r)\pi(n-d)/4}({\rm sgn}\,r)^{|\beta|}|r|^{N/2+|\beta|}}{2(2\pi)^{N/2+1}},\hskip 10.00002ptp:=X\theta-Y\omega.

Note that the integral in the relation for the coefficient
F^{\beta}(\theta,\omega,p)
is absolutely convergent
due to condition (
2.5
) and rapid decay of the function
\tilde{v}_{0}
at the infinity.

Asymptotics (
2.8
) provides a full description of a solution in the following sense. The coefficient
F^{\beta}(\theta,\omega,p)
is defined on the manifold
S^{d}\times S^{n}\times{\mathbb{R}}
of the dimension
N+1
.
This coefficient turns out to be sufficient to determine the initial data
v_{0}
(which are defined on the hyperplane of the same dimension) and thus the solution
v
in the entire space
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}

[
18
]
.

3
The Fourier transform of the function
u(x,y)

It was shown in
[
2
]
that for
v_{0}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
,
the solution to problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
) and thus the function
u(x,y)
defined by (
2.1
)
are tempered distributions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
.
For the generalized Fourier transform of
u(x,y)
defined as

\hat{u}(\xi,\eta)=\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}}e^{-i(x\xi-y\eta)}u(x,y)dxdy,

the following formula was obtained

\hat{u}(\xi,\eta)=a(\xi,\eta)\,\delta\left(\xi^{2}-\eta^{2}\right),\hskip 10.00002pta(\xi,\eta)=2\pi|\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}|\,\widetilde{v}_{0}(\xi_{0}+\eta_{0},\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta}).

(3.1)

In order to describe the precise meaning of the r.h.s. of the first equality in (
3.1
),
as well as for further use,
we introduce the following submanifolds of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
:

\displaystyle\Sigma=S^{d}\times S^{n},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}=\left\{(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}\setminus\{0\}\,|\,\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)=0\right\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{1}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}\setminus\{0\}\,|\,\,\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)=\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)=|\overline{\xi}|=|\overline{\eta}|=0\},

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}\setminus\{0\}\,|\,\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)=\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)=0,\,|\overline{\xi}|=|\overline{\eta}|\neq 0\},

where

\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)=\xi^{2}-\eta^{2},\hskip 10.00002pt\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)=\xi_{0}+\eta_{0},

Note that in the definitions of
\mathcal{C}_{0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, the condition
|\overline{\xi}|=|\overline{\eta}|
is redundant and is given
for clarity only.
Clearly,
\mathcal{C}_{0},\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset\mathcal{C}
.

We will use the following diffeomorphism

\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}\to\mathcal{C},\hskip 10.00002pt(\zeta,\sigma,r)\mapsto(r\zeta,r\sigma).

(3.2)

Introduce the volume form
\frac{1}{2}r^{N-1}dS_{\zeta}dS_{\sigma}dr
on
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
where
dS_{\zeta}
,
dS_{\sigma}
are the standard volume forms on spheres
S^{d}
,
S^{n}
, respectively.
Let
dS_{\xi,\eta}
be the counterpart of this form induced on
\mathcal{C}
by diffeomorphism (
3.2
).
For further references, we record this formally as follows

dS_{\xi,\eta}=\frac{1}{2}r^{N-1}dS_{\zeta}dS_{\sigma}dr.

(3.3)

Action of the distribution
\hat{u}(\xi,\eta)
of the form (
3.1
)
on a test function
\varphi(\xi,\eta)
is defined by the equality

\langle\hat{u},\varphi\rangle=\int_{\mathcal{C}}(a\varphi)(\xi,\eta)\,dS_{\xi,\eta}.

(3.4)

In the proof of Theorem
1
, formula (
3.1
) will be used.
Formal inversion of the Fourier transform in the latter yields

u(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-N-2}\int_{\mathcal{C}}a(\xi,\eta)e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}dS_{\xi,\eta}\\
=(2\pi)^{-N-1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}|\,\tilde{v}_{0}(\xi_{0}+\eta_{0},\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta})e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}dS_{\xi,\eta}.

For a point
(x,y)
of the form (
2.4
), the last relation reads

u(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-N-1}\int_{\mathcal{C}}|\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}|\,\tilde{v}_{0}(\xi_{0}+\eta_{0},\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta})e^{i(X\xi-Y\eta)}e^{i\tau\Phi(\xi,\eta)}dS_{\xi,\eta},

(3.5)

where

\Phi(\xi,\eta)=\theta\xi-\omega\eta.

Note that the integral here is not absolutely convergent.
The reason is that the arguments of the function
\tilde{v}_{0}
in the integrand may stay bounded when the point
(\xi,\eta)

tends to infinity along
\mathcal{C}_{1}\subset\mathcal{C}
.
The integral converges due to oscillations of the exponential in the neighborhood of this set.
Besides, the integrand is nonsmooth at
\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}=0
, i.e. on the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}\cup\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
This complicates the analysis of smoothness and asymptotic behavior of the solutions,
as compared to the case of hyperbolic equations.

4
Littlewood-Paley decomposition of the function
u(x,y)

In our study of the distribution
u(x,y)
, we will consider its Littlewood-Paley decomposition,
which is defined in terms of the Fourier transform
\hat{u}(\xi,\eta)
.
Let a function
\tilde{\chi}(\rho)
satisfy

\tilde{\chi}\in C_{0}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}_{+}),\hskip 10.00002pt\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\tilde{\chi}(2^{-j}\rho)=1,\hskip 10.00002pt\rho>0

(4.1)

(for any positive
\rho
, the series on the l.h.s. of the last equality contains finite number of nonzero terms).
Introduce the following functions

\chi(\xi,\eta)=\tilde{\chi}\left(\sqrt{(\xi^{2}+\eta^{2})/2}\right),\hskip 10.00002pt\chi_{j}(\xi,\eta)=\chi(2^{-j}\xi,2^{-j}\eta),\hskip 10.00002ptj\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

The restrictions
\chi_{j}|_{\mathcal{C}}
are compactly supported functions in
\mathcal{C}

satisfying, by properties (
4.1
), the following relation

\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\chi_{j}(\xi,\eta)=1.

Since the amplitude
a(\xi,\eta)
is a bounded function,
this equality implies that
formula (
3.4
) can be written as the following relation

\left\langle\hat{u},\varphi\right\rangle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\chi_{j}a\varphi)(\xi,\eta)\,dS_{\xi,\eta},

which essentially is the Littlewood-Paley decomposition of the distribution
u(x,y)
.
This can be generalized on the partial derivatives
\partial^{\beta}u
as follows

\left\langle\widehat{\partial^{\beta}u},\varphi\right\rangle=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\chi_{j}a^{\beta}\varphi)(\xi,\eta)\,dS_{\xi,\eta},

(4.2)

where

a^{\beta}(\xi,\eta)=(P^{\beta}a)(\xi,\eta)

(4.3)

(the polynomial
P^{\beta}
was defined in the formulation of Theorem
1
).
Observe that the terms of the series in (
4.2
) are still well-defined,
if a test function
\varphi(\xi,\eta)
is replaced by the function

(2\pi)^{-N-2}e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}

for arbitrary
(x,y)
.
As will be shown in sec.
7

(which will be the principal part of the proof of Theorem
1
),
such a series is absolutely convergent locally uniformly with respect to
x,y
outside of the hyperplane (
2.3
).
This implies that outside this hyperplane,
the sum of this series gives exactly the value of
the function
\partial^{\beta}u(x,y)
:

\partial^{\beta}u(x,y)=(2\pi)^{-N-2}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\chi_{j}a^{\beta})(\xi,\eta)e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}\,dS_{\xi,\eta}.

(4.4)

To see this, consider an arbitrary test function
\psi(x,y)
with a compact support outside of the hyperplane (
2.3
).
By substituting the expression for its inverse Fourier transform

\varphi(\xi,\eta)=(2\pi)^{-N-2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}}e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}\psi(x,y)\,dxdy

into the r.h.s. of (
4.2
), we obtain

\left\langle\partial^{\beta}u,\psi\right\rangle=\left\langle\widehat{\partial^{\beta}u},\varphi\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{-N-2}\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}}dxdy\,\psi(x,y)\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\chi_{j}a^{\beta})(\xi,\eta)e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}\,dS_{\xi,\eta}

(we have changed the order of integration with respect to
x,y
and
\xi,\eta
).
This is equivalent to

\left\langle\partial^{\beta}u,\psi\right\rangle=(2\pi)^{-N-2}\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}}dxdy\,\psi(x,y)\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\chi_{j}a^{\beta})(\xi,\eta)e^{i(x\xi-y\eta)}\,dS_{\xi,\eta},

since the function
\psi(x,y)
is compactly supported, and the series under the integral sign
converges uniformly with respect to
(x,y)\in{\rm supp}\,\psi
.
This implies equality (
4.4
).

It is convenient to transform the terms of the series (
4.4
) as follows.
By taking
2^{-j}\xi
,
2^{-j}\eta
as new variables of integration
and invoking the definition of the volume form (
3.3
),
we arrive at the equivalent relation

\partial^{\beta}u(x,y)=\sum_{j\in{\mathbb{Z}}}(2\pi)^{-N-2}\,2^{Nj}I^{\beta}_{j}(x,y),

(4.5)

where

I^{\beta}_{j}(x,y)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}a^{\beta}_{j}(\xi,\eta)e^{i2^{j}(x\xi-y\eta)}\,dS_{\xi,\eta},\hskip 10.00002pta^{\beta}_{j}(\xi,\eta)=\chi(\xi,\eta)a^{\beta}(2^{j}\xi,2^{j}\eta).

(4.6)

Remarks concerning the integral (
3.5
) made in the end of sec.
3

are relevant
to the integrals
I^{\beta}_{j}
:
the integrand does not decay for large
j
and
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
and has a singularity at
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}\cup\mathcal{C}_{1}
.
For this reason, we will decompose
I^{\beta}_{j}
to the sum of the integrals of the form

I^{\beta}_{\varkappa,j}(x,y)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\varkappa a^{\beta}_{j})(\xi,\eta)e^{i2^{j}(x\xi-y\eta)}dS_{\xi,\eta}

with smooth cut-off functions
\varkappa
.
We will consider the cases when these functions are supported near
\mathcal{C}_{0}
,
\mathcal{C}_{1}
,
and conclude that the corresponding integrals do not contribute to the principal term of asymptotics (
2.8
).
We will see that the latter is determined by stationary points
of the restriction of the phase function
\Phi(\xi,\eta)
to
\mathcal{C}
.
These points constitute the rays

\mathcal{C}_{\rm st}=\{(\pm r\theta,\pm r\omega)\,|\,r>0\}\subset\mathcal{C}\setminus(\mathcal{C}_{0}\cup\mathcal{C}_{1})

(4.7)

(the signs
\pm
are assumed to be equal).
The last inclusion follows from condition (
2.5
).

5
Auxiliary assertions

Note that the differentials
d\Upsilon
,
d\Upsilon_{0}
are nonzero everywhere in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}\setminus\{0\}
.
Besides, they are linearly independent at points
(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}
,
since the contrary means that
d\Upsilon=\alpha d\Upsilon_{0}
,
whence

\overline{\xi}=\frac{1}{2}\partial_{\overline{\xi}}\Upsilon=\frac{\alpha}{2}\partial_{\overline{\xi}}\Upsilon_{0}=0,

which contradicts the definition of the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

Thus the system of equations
\Upsilon=\Upsilon_{0}=0
,
which locally determines the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, is nondegenerate,
and so
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a smooth submanifold of
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
of codimension two.
We will need, however, the fact that
\mathcal{C}_{0}
is a smooth submanifold of
\mathcal{C}
of codimension one.
To verify this, we use the representation

\mathcal{C}_{0}=\{(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}\,|\,\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)=0\},

(5.1)

which means that the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
, considered as a subset of
\mathcal{C}
, is locally determined
as a level set of the restriction
\Upsilon_{0}|_{\mathcal{C}}
.
Therefore, it suffices to check that

T_{(\xi,\eta)}^{*}\mathcal{C}\ni d(\Upsilon_{0}|_{\mathcal{C}})(\xi,\eta)\neq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}.

(5.2)

The contrary to this implies

d\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)\big|_{T_{(\xi,\eta)}\mathcal{C}}=d(\Upsilon_{0}|_{\mathcal{C}})(\xi,\eta)=0.

Then the differential
d\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta)

vanishes on the subspace
T_{(\xi,\eta)}\mathcal{C}\subset T_{(\xi,\eta)}{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
of codimension one.
Thus, by the definition of the manifold
\mathcal{C}
,
it is proportional to the differential
d\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)
.
We have seen, however, that these two differentials are linearly independent
on the set
\mathcal{C}_{0}
.

Lemma 1
.

Under the assumption (
2.5
),
the function
\Phi(\xi,\eta)
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
satisfies

\displaystyle d\Phi(\xi,\eta)\big|_{T_{(\xi,\eta)}\mathcal{C}_{0}}\neq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}_{0}.

(5.3)

\displaystyle d\Phi(\xi,\eta)\big|_{T_{(\xi,\eta)}\mathcal{C}}\neq 0,\hskip 10.00002pt(\xi,\eta)\in\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{\rm st}.

(5.4)

Proof.

Elements of the space
T_{(\xi,\eta)}^{*}{\mathbb{R}}^{N+2}
that vanish on
T_{(\xi,\eta)}\mathcal{C}_{0}

form a two-dimensional subspace.
As was shown before, the differentials
d\Upsilon
,
d\Upsilon_{0}
form a basis in the latter.
So the contrary to relation (
5.3
) means that for some
\alpha,\alpha_{0}\in{\mathbb{R}}
, we have

d\Phi(\xi,\eta)=\alpha d\Upsilon(\xi,\eta)+\alpha_{0}d\Upsilon_{0}(\xi,\eta).

Then, in view of equalities

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{0}}\Phi=\theta_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\eta_{0}}\Phi=-\omega_{0},

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{0}}\Upsilon=2\xi_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\eta_{0}}\Upsilon=-2\eta_{0},

\displaystyle\partial_{\xi_{0}}\Upsilon_{0}=1,\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\eta_{0}}\Upsilon_{0}=1,

we deduce that

\theta_{0}=2\alpha\xi_{0}+\alpha_{0},\hskip 10.00002pt-\omega_{0}=-2\alpha\eta_{0}+\alpha_{0}.

Taking into account the equality
\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}=0
satisfied on
\mathcal{C}_{0}
,
we come to a contradiction with assumption (
2.5
).

Now turn to the proof of (

The contrary to the latter means that

This can be written in the form of the equalities

\partial_{\xi}\Phi(\xi,\eta)=2\alpha\xi,\hskip 10.00002pt\partial_{\eta}\Phi(\xi,\eta)=-2\alpha\eta,

which are equivalent to

\theta=2\alpha\xi,\hskip 10.00002pt\omega=2\alpha\eta.

Hence

(\xi,\eta)
belongs to
\mathcal{C}_{\rm st}

which contradicts the condition of Lemma.

Lemma 2
.

For
(\zeta,\sigma,r)\in\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, we have

|\partial_{r}^{k}(a^{\beta}_{j}(r\zeta,r\sigma))|\leqslant C_{k}2^{j(1+|\beta|)},\hskip 10.00002ptk\geqslant 0,\hskip 10.00002ptj\in{\mathbb{Z}}.

(5.5)

Proof.

Since
P^{\beta}(2^{j}r\zeta,2^{j}r\sigma)=(2^{j}r)^{|\beta|}P^{\beta}(\zeta,\sigma)
,
by definitions (
4.6
), (
4.3
), (
3.1
), we have

a^{\beta}_{j}(r\zeta,r\sigma)=2\pi(2^{j}r)^{1+|\beta|}P^{\beta}(\zeta,\sigma)\tilde{\chi}(r)|\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}|\,\tilde{v}_{0}(2^{j}r(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}),2^{j}r\overline{\zeta},2^{j}r\overline{\sigma}).

(5.6)

Hence it suffices to show that

|(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0})\,\partial_{r}^{k}(\tilde{v}_{0}(2^{j}r(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}),2^{j}r\overline{\zeta},2^{j}r\overline{\sigma}))|\leqslant C_{k},\hskip 10.00002ptk\geqslant 0,\hskip 10.00002ptr\in{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}.

(5.7)

The expression under the modulus sign equals

\sum_{\alpha+|\nu|+|\gamma|=k}C_{\alpha,\nu,\gamma}2^{kj}(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0})^{\alpha+1}\,\overline{\zeta}^{\nu}\,\overline{\sigma}^{\gamma}\left(\partial_{\lambda,\overline{\xi},\overline{\eta}}^{\alpha,\nu,\gamma}\,\tilde{v}_{0}\right)(2^{j}r(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}),2^{j}r\overline{\zeta},2^{j}r\overline{\sigma}).

We have

|(\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0})^{\alpha+1}\,\overline{\zeta}^{\nu}\,\overline{\sigma}^{\gamma}|\leqslant C_{k}|\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}|^{\alpha+1}\cdot|\overline{\zeta}|^{|\nu|}\cdot|\overline{\sigma}|^{|\gamma|}\leqslant C_{k}\rho^{k+1}\leqslant C_{k}\rho^{k},

where
\rho:=|\zeta_{0}+\sigma_{0}|+|\overline{\zeta}|+|\overline{\sigma}|
.
At the same time, since
\tilde{v}_{0}\in\mathcal{S}({\mathbb{R}}^{N+1})
,
the partial derivative in the expression above is majorized by

C_{k,M}\left(1+2^{j}\rho\right)^{-M},\hskip 10.00002ptM\geqslant 0

(we took into account that the variable
r\in{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}
is separated from zero).
Letting
M=k
here, we estimate the whole expression by

\frac{C_{k}2^{kj}\rho^{k}}{\left(1+2^{j}\rho\right)^{k}}\leqslant C_{k}.

This implies estimate (
5.7
) and thus (
5.5
) holds true.
∎

Lemma 3

,

According to definitions (

\leqslant\frac{C_{\alpha,M}2^{|\alpha|j}}{(1+2^{j}(|\xi_{0}+\eta_{0}|+|\overline{\xi}|+|\overline{\eta}|))^{M}},

so the maximum over

Then, since

Lemma 4
.

Under the assumptions of Theorem
1
,
every point
(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})\in\mathcal{C}_{1}
has a neighborhood
V\subset\mathcal{C}
, depending on
\theta,\omega,\tau_{0}
, and

such that (
6.1
) holds true for arbitrary function
\varkappa\in C_{0}^{\infty}(V)

Proof.

According to (
2.4
), we have

x\xi-y\eta=X\xi-Y\eta+\tau(\theta\xi-\omega\eta).

(6.2)

It follows from condition (
2.6
) that

X\xi-Y\eta=X_{0}\xi_{0}-Y_{0}\eta_{0}+\overline{X}\,\overline{\xi}-\overline{Y}\overline{\eta}=X_{0}(\xi_{0}+\eta_{0})+\overline{X}\,\overline{\xi}-\overline{Y}\overline{\eta}.

Be definition of
\mathcal{C}_{1}
, we have

\displaystyle\xi^{*}_{0}=-\eta^{*}_{0}\neq 0,

(6.3)

\displaystyle\overline{\xi^{*}}=0=\overline{\eta^{*}}.

(6.4)

Hence the preceding expression vanishes at the point
(\xi,\eta)=(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})
,
and does not exceed
4R(|\xi-\xi^{*}|+|\eta-\eta^{*}|)
in absolute value at arbitrary point.
Therefore, for arbitrary
\varepsilon>0
, the estimate

|X\xi-Y\eta|\leqslant\tau_{0}\varepsilon

(6.5)

holds in a sufficiently small neighborhood of the point
(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})
.

Now turn to the term
\tau(\theta\xi-\omega\eta)
in (
6.2
).
In view of equality (
6.3
), we have

\theta\xi-\omega\eta=(\theta_{0}+\omega_{0})\xi_{0}^{*}+\theta_{0}(\xi_{0}-\xi_{0}^{*})-\omega_{0}(\eta_{0}-\eta_{0}^{*})+\overline{\theta}\,\overline{\xi}-\overline{\omega}\,\overline{\eta}.

According to (
6.4
), the sum of the last three terms here
vanishes at the point
(\xi,\eta)=(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})
,
and thus does not exceed
2\varepsilon
in absolute value in a sufficiently small neighborhood of this point.
Now letting

\varepsilon=|(\theta_{0}+\omega_{0})\xi^{*}_{0}|/4,

(
\varepsilon>0
in view of conditions (
2.5
) and (
6.3
)),
we obtain the estimate
|\theta\xi-\omega\eta|\geqslant 2\varepsilon
.
Combined with estimate (
6.5
) and condition
\tau\geqslant\tau_{0}
, this implies that

|x\xi-y\eta|\geqslant\tau\varepsilon,\hskip 10.00002pt(\xi,\eta)\in V,

(6.6)

where
V
is a sufficiently small neighborhood of the point
(\xi^{*},\eta^{*})
.

Introduce the differential operator

L=(x\zeta-y\sigma)^{-1}\partial_{r},

which is well-defined on the part of the manifold
\Sigma\times{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
, in which the denominator of the expression is nonzero.
The counterpart of the operator
L
induced on
\mathcal{C}
by diffeomorphism (
3.2
)
will be denoted by the same symbol.
By (
6.6
), for
(\xi,\eta)=(r\zeta,r\sigma)\in V
, we have

|x\zeta-y\sigma|=r^{-1}|x\xi-y\eta|\geqslant r^{-1}\varepsilon\tau.

(6.7)

This quantity is positive,
so the operator
L
is well-defined in
V
.
Let
L_{T}
denote the differential operator formally adjoint to
L

with respect to the volume form (
3.3
).
We have

L_{T}^{k}=\frac{(r^{1-N}\partial_{r}(r^{N-1}\cdot))^{k}}{(y\sigma-x\zeta)^{k}},\hskip 10.00002ptk\geqslant 1.

(6.8)

Observe that

e^{i2^{j}(x\xi-y\eta)}=(i2^{j})^{-k}L^{k}e^{i2^{j}(x\xi-y\eta)},

whence

|I_{\varkappa,j}(\tau)|\leqslant 2^{-kj}\int_{\mathcal{C}}\left|\left(L_{T}^{k}(\varkappa a^{\beta}_{j})\right)(\xi,\eta)\right|dS_{\xi,\eta}.

The integrand is nonzero at a point
(\xi,\eta)=(r\zeta,r\sigma)
only if
r\in{\rm supp}\tilde{\chi}\subset{\mathbb{R}}_{+}
,
which implies that
r
must be bounded and separated from zero.
Then by formula (
6.8
) and estimate (
6.7
), we have

\left|\left(L_{T}^{k}(\varkappa a^{\beta}_{j})\right)(r\zeta,r\sigma)\right|\leqslant C_{\varkappa,k}\,\tau^{-k}\max_{0\leqslant\alpha\leqslant k}|\partial_{r}^{\alpha}(a^{\beta}_{j}(r\zeta,r\sigma))|.

According to (
5.5
), the last expression is bounded by
C_{\varkappa,k}\tau^{-k}2^{(1+|\beta|)j}
, whence

|I_{\varkappa,j}(\tau)|\leqslant C_{\varkappa,k}\,\tau^{-k}2^{(1+|\beta|-k)j}.

(6.9)

Now taking
k=N+1+|\beta|+{\rm sgn}j
, we arrive at estimate (
6.1
).
∎

It is convenient to write the integral

I^{\beta}_{\varkappa,j}(\tau)=\int_{\mathcal{C}}(\varkappa b^{\beta}_{j})(\xi,\eta)e^{i\tau 2^{j}\Phi(\xi,\eta)}dS_{\xi,\eta},

where

(6.10)

The estimate of the function

analogous to (

) will be required.
To derive it, observe that for the function

of variables

the functional

Then, in view of (

|b^{\beta}_{j}|_{\xi,\eta,k}\leqslant C_{k}(1+2^{kj})|a^{\beta}_{j}|_{\xi,\eta,k}.

Now applying estimate (
5.10
) gives

|b^{\beta}_{j}|_{\xi,\eta,k}\leqslant\frac{C_{k,M,\mathcal{K}}2^{(1+|\beta|)j}}{(1+2^{j})^{M}},

(6.11)

as soon as
(\xi,\eta)
belongs to a compact set
\mathcal{K}\subset\mathcal{C}\setminus\mathcal{C}_{1}

Lemma 5

Note that condition (
