Executive Summary
Organic mixed ionic electronic conductors (OMIECs) are critical materials for next-generation
technologies including high-capacity batteries, bio-electronic interfaces, and neuromorphic
computing devices. This project addressed a fundamental challenge associated to the optimization
of electronic and ionic conduction mechanisms in these materials, which often involves conflicting
design parameters and is directly coupled to molecular design of polymers and of the formulation
of blends from these polymers.
Our research focused on exploring a new family of structured OMIEC materials with long-range
order that could potentially decouple electronic and ionic conduction optimization. These materials
are produced by formulating mixtures of ion-conducting block copolymers and electronic
conducting polymers, where the self-assembly propensity of ionic-conducting block copolymers
guides the assembly of conjugated components, which provide the electronic conductive pathway.
To achieve this work, the team developed and used advanced methods of artificial intelligence
coupled to neutron and x-ray scattering experiments to aid in the autonomous development of
phase diagrams for polymer nanostructures. The methods are generally useful and can be applied
to a wide variety of materials.

Key Technical Achievements:
 • Demonstrated successful formation of structured OMIEC blends using PEO-PPO-PEO
 triblock copolymers with conjugated polymers (PPBT)
 • Achieved shear-alignment of OMIEC materials into single-crystalline oriented structures
 using rheo-SANS
 • Developed and validated autonomous phase-mapping algorithms (autophasemap) for high-
 throughput analysis
 • Established high-throughput electrochemical characterization using automated robotic
 systems
 • Created contrast variation SANS methodologies for unambiguous structural
 characterization
 • Developed mechanochemical synthesis of a family of unique high-c block copolymers
 with fluoropolymer blocks and polyether blocks and created OMIEC blends with
 conjugated polymers

 • Analyzed structure in solution and as coated films for OMIEC blends and applied
 automated high-throughput organic electrochemical transistor (OECT) device testing with
 the PolyBot framework at Argonne National Laboratory.

Impact on Understanding: This work advanced understanding of structure-property relationships
in complex polymer blend systems and demonstrates how AI-driven autonomous experimentation
can accelerate materials discovery in neutron and X-ray scattering facilities.

Project Goals and Objectives
Major Goals
The overarching goal was to uncover the relationship between structure and function for structured
OMIEC materials having long-range order and potential to decouple optimization of electronic
and ionic conduction mechanisms.
Objective 1: Identify and formulate structure-property relationships for 3D structured
OMIEC blends using HT neutron and x-ray scattering, rheological and electrochemical
analysis.
 • Use small angle neutron and X-ray scattering techniques, including contrast variation, to
 evaluate relationships between polymer molecular parameters, formulation conditions,
 processing, structure, and performance
 • Implement electrochemical analysis (impedance spectroscopy and organic electrochemical
 transistors) to probe key metrics for bio-electronics and neuromorphic computing
 applications
Objective 2: Develop infrastructure for AI-driven, high-throughput scattering-aided
molecular design of OMIEC blends with target structure and properties.
 • Achieve significant advances in artificial intelligence (AI) and adaptive designs of
 experiments (DOE) to accelerate analysis of phase behavior and properties
 • Develop AI-driven autonomous experimentation capabilities integrating robotic sample
 synthesis and advanced algorithms for phase map delineation to minimize the need for
 human intervention
 • Evaluate and demonstrate the powerful role of neutron and X-ray scattering facilities in
 autonomous experimentation using the development of complex polymer blends as a
 practical system of interest

Comparison of Actual Accomplishments with Project Goals
Objective 1 Accomplishments vs. Goals
Goal: Establish structure-property relationships for 3D structured OMIEC blends
Accomplishments:
 • Successfully demonstrated formation of composite polymer mesophases in PEO-PPO-
 PEO/PPBT blends with preference for elongated phases (cylindrical micelles)
 • Completed rheo-SANS experiments demonstrating shear-alignment capability from
 powder-like to single-crystalline domains
 • Performed contrast variation SANS at HFIR to unambiguously identify conjugated
 polymer locations
 • Developed crosslinking methodologies for aqueous electrolyte compatibility
 • Established high-throughput electrochemical characterization using PolyBot system at
 Argonne National Laboratory

 • Extended work to custom-synthesized fluorinated block copolymer systems (DEGFy,
 PEGFy series) using mechanochemical synthesis methods
Goal: Characterize electrochemical properties
Accomplishments:
 • Demonstrated automated OECT fabrication and testing from OMIEC blends using the
 PolyBot facility in collaboration with staff at Argonne National Laboratory
 • Showed enhanced performance with addition of ion gel layers
 • Initiated high-throughput autonomous electrochemical campaigns
 • (Partial Achievement) In absence of ion gel layers, materials were had limited ionic and
 electronic transport.

Objective 2 Accomplishments vs. Goals
Goal: Develop AI and high-throughput tools suitable for autonomous SANS/SAXS analysis
Accomplishments:
 • Developed and published autophasemap algorithm for autonomous phase mapping
 • Demonstrated shape-based clustering for rapid dataset analysis
 • Integrated Pedersen micelle models into SasView for community use and modified them
 to account for partial solvation of the micelle core
 • Developed activephasemap algorithm for iterative sampling campaigns using generative
 autoencoders (pending publication currently under review)
 • Extended generalized phase mapping algorithms to multiple spectroscopic techniques
 beyond scattering
 • Established high-throughput robotic formulation workflows for OMIEC material
 formulation, suitable for integrated SANS and SAXS analysis

Experimental Approaches Used
 • High-throughput SAXS: Created custom 48-well plates suitable for SAXS/SANS and
 integrated with automated sample synthesis, formulation and measurements. Design is
 openly shared in a repository for community use.
 • Contrast Variation SANS: Used CV-SANS to outline details of the organization of
 complexes integrating conjugated polymers and block copolymers.
 • Rheo-SANS: Demonstrated shear-alignment of OMIEC materials using concentric
 cylinder shear cell with in-situ SANS structural characterization.
 • Robotic formulation: Applied automated liquid handling with statistical design of
 experiments (DOE) algorithms to explore systematic influence of molecular and
 formulation parameters.
 • Electrochemical testing: Pursued OECT fabrication, and performance evaluation (trans
 conductance) with PolyBot system at Argonne National Laboratory.
Problems Encountered and Resolutions
 • Unstable OMIEC Films: Some OMIEC formulations were unstable in film form, which
 made it difficult to fabricate good electrochemical devices. Resolution: Formulated new
 block copolymers to produce blends with reduced solubility.
 • Electronic Conduction: Lower than expected electronic transport in hydrated systems.
 Resolution: Investigated alternative polymer blend architectures and processing conditions

 to achieve superior performance. Extended analysis to donor-acceptor conjugated
 polymers.
 • SANS Access: Limited neutron facility access in the US due to facility shut down (NIST,
 HFIR). Resolution: Leveraged international facilities (ANSTO) to secure beamtime.

Software and Open-Source Tools
 1. autophasemap Algorithm: Shape-based clustering tool for scattering data analysis
 o GitHub repository: https://github.com/pozzo-research-
 group/papers/tree/main/autophasemap
 o Enables rapid clustering based on shape similarity metrics

 o Applicable to SAXS, SANS, UV-Vis, and other spectroscopic data
 2. activephasemap Algorithm: Active-sampling generative phase mapping algorithm for
 functional data analysis (SAXS, SANS, spectroscopy)
 o GitHub repository: https://github.com/pozzo-research-
 group/papers/tree/main/autophasemap
 o Iterative active sampling algorithm for differentiable phase mapping of functional
 data
 o Enables in-silico retrosynthesis through optimization via the differentiable model
 3. Modified Pedersen Micelle Models: Integrated into SasView package
 o GitHub repository: https://github.com/pozzo-research-group/micelles
 o Spherical and cylindrical micelle models for community use
 o Modified formulation to account for solvation of the micelle core
 o Numerical accuracy validated against SASFit
 4. High-throughput Automation Hardware: For SAXS/SANS analysis using open-source
 automation systems
 o Custom SAXS/SANS sample environments for 48 unique sample compositions
 using Kapton windows
 o Integrated with robotic liquid handling protocols

Future Work and Recommendations
Immediate Next Steps
 1. Structural Optimization: Complete thin-film characterization using SAXS to correlate
 solution and solid-state structures

 2. Electronic Enhancement: Explore different conjugated polymer architectures and
 processing conditions to eliminate the need to use ion-gel while still achieving good
 electrochemical performance
 3. Autonomous Implementation: Demonstrate closed-loop autonomous phase mapping at
 synchrotron facilities with active sample formulation
Long-term Directions
 1. Material Expansion: Extend methodology to other OMIEC chemistries and applications
 2. Device Integration: Scale up to practical device fabrication and testing
 3. AI Infrastructure: Broaden autonomous experimentation capabilities across neutron and
 X-ray facilities
