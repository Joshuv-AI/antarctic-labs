Global-in-time strong solutions for the 2D and 3D generalized compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system with arbitrarily large initial data
Yongteng Gu, Xiangdi Huang, Weili Meng, Huitao Zhou a. State Key Laboratory of Mathematical Sciences, Academy of MathematicsSystems Science,Chinese Academy of Sciences, Beijing 100190, China;

Abstract
In 1901, Korteweg formulated a constitutive equation for the Cauchy stress tensor to provide a continuum mechanical model for capillarity within fluids. Dunn and Serrin [Arch. Ration. Mech. Anal. 88(2):95-133,1985] in 1985 further modified the system of compressible fluids based on the Korteweg theory of capillarity. Since then, for the 2D and 3D compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system, the global existence of strong solutions with arbitrarily large initial data have remained a challenging open problem. In this paper, we provide an affirmative answer to this longstanding open problem. Specifically, under the assumption that the viscosity coefficients satisfy a BD-type algebraic relation of the form μ ⁡ ( ρ ) = ν ​ ρ α \mu(\rho)=\nu\rho^{\alpha} and λ ⁡ ( ρ ) = 2 ​ ν ​ ( α − 1 ) ​ ρ α \lambda(\rho)=2\nu(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha} , and that the Korteweg stress tensor complies with a generalized Bohm identity of the form κ ⁡ ( ρ ) = ε 2 ​ α 2 ​ ρ 2 ​ α − 3 \kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3} , we establish the global existence of strong solutions for the 2D and 3D systems in torus with arbitrarily large regular initial data. The analysis is carried out in the intermediary non-dispersive regime, characterized by the condition that the capillarity coefficient constant ε \varepsilon does not exceed the viscosity constant ν \nu . This result provides the first proof of the global-in-time existence of strong solutions for the 3D general Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system with arbitrarily large initial data in the non-dispersive regime. Keywords: compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system; density-dependent viscosity; global strong solutions; large initial data. Introduction

In this paper, we consider the following compressible Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\div(\rho u)=0,\\
(\rho u)_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho u\otimes u)+\nabla P=\div(2\mu(\rho)\mathbb{D}u)+\nabla(\lambda(\rho){\rm div}u)+\div\mathbb{K},\end{array}\right.

(1)

where
t
is time,
x
is the spatial coordinate, and
\rho,u=(u_{1},\dots,u_{N})^{t}(N=2,3),
and
P=\rho^{\gamma}(\gamma\geq 1)
represent respectively the fluid density, velocity and pressure;
\mathbb{D}u
is the deformation tensor given by

\displaystyle\mathbb{D}u=\frac{\nabla u+(\nabla u)^{t}}{2}.

The density-dependent viscosity coefficients
\mu(\rho)
and
\lambda(\rho)
satisfy physical constraints:

\displaystyle\mu(\rho)\geq 0,\quad 2\mu(\rho)+N\lambda(\rho)\geq 0.

The capillarity term
\mathbb{K}
is defined as

\displaystyle\mathbb{K}=\Big(\rho\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho)-\frac{\rho\kappa^{\prime}(\rho)-\kappa(\rho)}{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Big)\mathbb{I}-\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho,

where
\mathbb{I}
is the identity matrix, so that its divergence is given by

\displaystyle\operatorname{div}\mathbb{K}=\nabla\left(\rho\kappa(\rho)\Delta\rho+\frac{\kappa(\rho)+\rho\kappa^{\prime}(\rho)}{2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)-\operatorname{div}(\kappa(\rho)\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho).

The system (
1
) originates from the work of
[
17
]
, who in 1901 introduced a stress tensor incorporating density gradients to model capillarity, a framework further refined by Dunn and Serrin
[
8
]
. Within this framework, if we consider the situation where
\kappa(\rho)=0
, system (
1
) degenerates into the compressible Navier-Stokes equations with density-dependent viscosities. For such systems, provided the viscosity coefficients satisfy the Bresch-Desjardins (BD) relation
\lambda(\rho)=2\rho\mu^{\prime}(\rho)-2\mu(\rho)
, a critical mathematical entropy estimate can be obtained
[
2
,
3
]
. Subsequently, the global existence of weak solutions in multiple dimensions for flows obeying the BD relation has been extensively studied. This includes the work of Guo–Jiu–Xin
[
10
]
on spherically symmetric finite-energy weak solutions, Vasseur–Yu
[
20
]
for the viscous Saint-Venant model (
\mu(\rho)=\rho,\lambda(\rho)=0
), and Li–Xin
[
18
]
for general viscosities
\mu(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha},\lambda(\rho)=2(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha}
. Further developments were achieved by Bresch–Vasseur–Yu
[
6
]
in the context of a general physical symmetric viscous stress tensor, and by Huang–Meng–Zhang
[
14
]
for spherically symmetric weak solutions with higher regularity, incorporating the phenomenon of vacuum vanishing in such weak solutions. Despite these advancements, the global existence of strong or smooth solutions with arbitrarily large initial data in multiple dimensions has long remained a challenging open problem. Only recently has significant progress been made. Zhang
[
22
]
pioneered the field by proving that for
\alpha<1
(subject to certain exponent constraints), arbitrarily large spherically symmetric initial data give rise to a unique global classical solution. Huang–Meng–Zhang
[
14
]
subsequently improved this result via a parameter continuation argument. They also extend the validity to the critical case
\alpha=1
in two dimensions (the viscous Saint-Venant equations). Concurrently, Chen–Zhang–Zhu
[
7
]
established the unique global regular solution for large spherically symmetric data to the viscous Saint-Venant equations using a distinct approach. Gu–Huang
[
9
]
further extended the
\alpha=1
result from
[
14
]
to the three-dimensional case and generalized the framework to non-isentropic flows involving entropy transport. Notably, in all these recent breakthroughs, the solutions are established for arbitrarily large initial data without requiring smallness assumptions. However, the global existence of smooth or strong solutions for general (non-symmetric) large initial data when
\alpha\leq 1
, or even for spherically symmetric data when
\alpha>1
, remains an open question to date.

In the case where

\displaystyle\mu(\rho)=\nu\rho,\quad\lambda(\rho)=0,\quad\kappa(\rho)=\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\rho},

system (
1
) reduces to the quantum Navier-Stokes equations. We focus particularly on recent results regarding the case
\nu=\varepsilon
. Regarding the one-dimensional quantum Navier–Stokes system, Jüngel
[
16
]
proved the global existence of smooth solutions on
\mathbb{R}
with strictly positive particle densities, valid for arbitrarily large initial data. In higher dimensions, Yu-Wu
[
21
]
established a blow-up criterion based on the lower bound of the density. However, the global existence of strong/smooth solutions for arbitrarily large initial data remained unproven. This challenge was recently addressed by Huang-Meng-Zhang
[
15
]
, who employed a specialized Moser iteration to construct a control relationship between the effective velocity
v
and the density’s lower bound. They proved that the density has a positive lower bound, thereby establishing the global existence of strong solutions for arbitrarily large initial data in periodic domains. Furthermore, Huang-Gu-Lei
[
13

proved the global strong solutions for large initial data in the whole space by utilizing precise density truncation combined with a refined De Giorgi iteration. It is worth noting that both results establish the global existence of large-data solutions far from vacuum in general domains, achieved without imposing any symmetry or special geometric assumptions on the initial data.

As for the general Navier-Stokes-Korteweg system, there is also a substantial body of literature available. Regarding the one-dimensional case, for system (
1
) featuring density-dependent viscosity and capillarity coefficients, Germain-LeFloch
[
11
]
established the global existence of finite-energy weak solutions for the Cauchy problem, further proving their convergence toward Euler system entropy solutions. Additionally, the vanishing capillarity limit was investigated by Burtea-Haspot
[
5

[
1

obtained global weak solutions for the periodic problem with large data, provided that the viscosity
\mu(\rho)=\rho^{\alpha}
and capillarity coefficient
\kappa(\rho)=\rho^{\iota}
satisfy certain prescribed conditions. For higher dimensions, Haspot
[
12
]
studied the existence of both local and global strong solutions under the assumption of small initial data, considering various types of density-dependent viscosity and capillarity coefficients.

As previously noted, for the case where
\mu(\rho)=\nu\rho
,
\lambda(\rho)=0,\kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}/\rho

\nu=\varepsilon

[
13

15

1

\displaystyle\mu(\rho)=\nu\rho^{\alpha},\quad\lambda(\rho)=2\nu(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha},\quad\kappa(\rho)=\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3},\quad\nu\geq\varepsilon>0,

(2)

2

\displaystyle\rho(x,0)=\rho_{0}(x),\quad u(x,0)=u_{0}(x),\quad x\in\mathbb{T}^{N},

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
N\in\{2,3\}
and
\beta=\sqrt{1-\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{\nu^{2}}}
. Assume that
(\alpha,\gamma,\beta)
satisfies

\displaystyle N=2,\quad\alpha\in\Big(\frac{\sqrt{5}-1}{2},1\Big),\quad\gamma\in[1,\infty),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta_{2}^{-}(\alpha));

(4)

\displaystyle N=3,\quad\alpha\in\left(\frac{9\sqrt{3}-4\sqrt{2}}{9\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{2}},\,1\right),\quad\gamma\in\Big[1,\frac{15\alpha-7}{3}\Big),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta^{-}_{3}(\alpha)),

(5)

where
\beta_{N}^{-}(x)
is the positive root of the following quadratic equation:

\displaystyle(\beta_{N}^{-}(x))^{2}+\frac{2(1-x)(Nx^{2}+\sqrt{N}x+1-N)}{x(1-\sqrt{N}(1-x))^{2}}\beta_{N}^{-}(x)+\frac{(1-x)(-Nx^{2}-Nx+2N-2)}{x(1-\sqrt{N}(1-x))^{2}}=0.

Moreover, assume that the initial data
(\rho_{0},u_{0})
satisfy

\displaystyle 0<\underline{\rho_{0}}\leq\rho_{0}\leq\overline{\rho_{0}},\quad\rho_{0}\in H^{3},\quad u_{0}\in H^{2},

(6)

where
\underline{\rho_{0}}
and
\overline{\rho_{0}}
are positive constants. Then the problem (
1
)–(
3
) admits a unique global strong solution
(\rho,u)
satisfying for any
0<T<\infty
and
(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T]
,

\displaystyle\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}(C(T))^{-1}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq C(T),\\
\rho\in C([0,T];H^{3})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}),\ \rho_{t}\in C([0,T];H^{1})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{2}),\\
u\in C([0,T];H^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}),\ u_{t}\in L^{\infty}(0,T;L^{2})\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{1}),\end{array}\right.

where the constant
C(T)>0
depends on the initial data and
T
.

Remark 1.1
.

It is worth noting that in the endpoint case
\alpha=1
, the system reduces to the quantum Navier-Stokes equations. Recently, if additionally
\nu=\varepsilon
, i.e.,
\beta=0
, Huang-Meng-Zhang
[
15
]
and Huang-Gu-Lei
[
13
]
established global strong solutions for the endpoint case
\alpha=1
on periodic domains
\mathbb{T}^{N}
and in the whole space
\mathbb{R}^{N}
, respectively. The main theorem of this paper generalizes their results to the more general case of
\alpha
, where the viscosity coefficient
\nu
also differs from the capillarity coefficient constant
\varepsilon
. Due to the inherent differences between the dissipative and dispersive structures, the methods and techniques developed herein are not applicable to the endpoint case, and vice vesa. To overcome the challenges posed by the general framework, we are required to develop novel Nash-Moser iteration schemes to establish both upper and lower bounds for the density. Furthermore, we need to enhance the regularity of the fast diffusion equation with convection term satisfied by the density, which present additional difficulties of a fundamentally new nature.

Remark 1.2
.

It is necessary to clarify the primary sources of the constraints on the indices

,

, and

and

Due to the physical constraint

2
Main strategy

When
\alpha=1
and
\nu=\varepsilon>0
, it was proved in
[
15
]
that problem (
1
)–(
3
) admits a unique global strong solution. They obtained an upper bound for the density by applying the De Giorgi iteration technique to the linear parabolic equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
satisfied by
\rho
. Then they established a precise relationship between the
L^{\infty}
estimate of the effective velocity and the lower bound of the density, and subsequently used a Nash–Moser iteration method on equation (
85
) for
\rho^{-1}
to derive a lower bound for the density. Finally, they showed that the upper and lower bounds of the density allow one to close the estimates for higher-order derivatives of
(\rho,v)
, thereby yielding a global strong solution.

However, when
\alpha<1
, the nonlinearity introduces many new difficulties. The density equation is transformed into a fast diffusion equation with convection term. First, when estimating the upper and lower bounds of the density, the equations
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
and (
85
) satisfied by
\rho
and
\rho^{-1}
become nonlinear parabolic equations, so the method developed in
[
15
]
for estimating the density bounds is no longer applicable. To overcome this, we develop an original modified Nash–Moser iteration method to obtain both the upper and lower bounds for the density. Moreover, the nonlinearity in equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
caused by
\alpha<1
gives rise to nonlinear terms when estimating higher-order derivatives; how to handle these nonlinear terms is another major difficulty we face.

In this section, we will demonstrate how we use the modified Nash–Moser method to obtain the upper and lower bounds for the density, and how we overcome the difficulties brought by nonlinear terms in the estimates for higher-order derivatives.

2.1
Upper bounds for the density

In what follows, we will highlight some key points of our approach to obtaining an upper bound for the density.

•

Prerequisite of the iteration: Regularity of
v

From the nonlinear parabolic equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
satisfied by the density, it can be seen that improving the regularity of
\rho
from
L^{p}
with finite
p
to
L^{\infty}
requires some regularity of the coefficient
v
. Indeed, in the iteration process we make use of the integrability of
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}
, where
\hat{q}_{N}>0
is to be determined.

•

Core of the iteration: Reverse Hölder’s inequality

Using equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
together with the density-weighted
L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}
estimate for
v
to perform
L^{p}
estimates for
\rho
, we obtain a constant
C_{0}>0
, independent of
p
, such that for any
p>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{0}(p+2)^{2}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{l}_{T}L^{l\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}}}^{l}+C_{0}\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(14)

where
l=p+3-\alpha-\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}
. The two energies on the left-hand side of the above inequality can be interpolated to yield an energy that shares the same form as the first term on the right-hand side, thereby constructing a reverse Hölder inequality. More precisely, we take
r
such that
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
, with
r
to be chosen later. Then,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{s}_{T}L^{\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}s}}

\displaystyle\leq\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{\xi}\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{r}}^{\eta},

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&s=(p+2)\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p+\alpha+1,\\
&\eta=\frac{2}{(p+2)\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p+\alpha+1},\hskip 9.24994pt\xi=\frac{2\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)}{(p+2)\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p+\alpha+1}.\end{split}

By the Sobolev embedding, there exists a constant
C_{r}>0
such that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{s}_{T}L^{\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}s}}&\leq C_{r}^{\eta}\left(C_{0}(p+2)^{2}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{l}_{T}L^{l\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}}}^{l}+C_{0}\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\xi+\eta}{2}}.\end{split}

(15)

This is precisely the core of the iteration—the reverse Hölder inequality.

•

Procedure of the iteration: On the choice of
r

A natural way to proceed with the iteration is to take
p_{0}
sufficiently large, and denote the corresponding quantities
l,s,\xi,\eta
determined by
p_{0}
as
l_{0},s_{0},\xi_{0},\eta_{0}
. Then we set
s_{0}=l_{1}
, let
p_{1}
be the value of
p
determined by
l_{1}
, and denote the corresponding quantities associated with
p_{1}
as
l_{1},s_{1},\xi_{1},\eta_{1}
. Continuing this process indefinitely… Based on the following two observations, we are able to use the reverse Hölder inequality (
15
) to initiate the iteration process and obtain an upper bound for the density. On the one hand, to ensure that this iteration scheme can reach
L^{\infty}
, we need
l_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. In fact, we know that

\displaystyle\frac{s}{l}

\displaystyle\to 1+\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r},\text{ as }p\to\infty.

This indicates that we need to choose
r
such that
\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}>0
holds, in order to ensure that
l_{k}\to\infty
as
k\to\infty
. The existence of
r
such that
\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}>0
and
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
is guaranteed if

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{N}>N-2.

(16)

On the other hand, to prevent the energy from blowing up during the iteration, the following condition is required:

\displaystyle\frac{\xi+\eta}{2}l\leq 1,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha\geq\frac{\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)\hat{q}_{N}+2\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\Big(\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)(\hat{q}_{N}+2)+2\hat{q}_{N}+4}.

Such an
r
exists if

\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}.

(17)

•

On the estimate of
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\hat{q}N+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}

From
(
16
)
and
(
17
)
, it can be seen that for any
\alpha>\frac{N-1}{N}
, obtaining an upper bound for the density requires the boundedness of
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\hat{q}N+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}
, where
\hat{q}_{N}>N-2
can be taken sufficiently small. The dissipative nature of the parabolic equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
for
v
implies that the achievable integrability of
v
is influenced by the closeness of
\alpha
to
1
and
\beta
to
0
. To be more precise, the closer
\alpha
gets to
1
and the closer
\beta
gets to
0
, the higher the level of integrability that
v
can attain.

In fact, multiplying
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v
and integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
yields that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx+\nu\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\nu\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx\\
&\quad+(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{t}:\nabla(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v{\rm div}(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx\\
&\leq C\int\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|dx.\end{split}

Directly using the facts that
|\nabla|v||\leq|\nabla v|
and
|\operatorname{div}v|\leq\sqrt{N}|\nabla v|
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx+\hat{\delta}_{0}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\leq C\int\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|dx,\end{split}

(18)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{0}=(2\nu-c)(1+N(\alpha-1))-\frac{(c-\nu+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(2\nu-c))^{2}}{4\nu}\hat{q}_{N}.

Note that
\hat{\delta}_{0}>0
if

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{N}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(1+(\alpha-1)N)}{(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}}.

(19)

Therefore, such a
\hat{q}_{N}
exists if

\displaystyle N-2<\frac{4(1-\beta)(1+(\alpha-1)N)}{(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}}.

This gives rise to the conditions on
\alpha
and
\beta
in
(
39
)
and
(
42
)
. In the three-dimensional case, handling the right-hand side term in
(
18
)
imposes an upper bound on
\gamma
, as shown in
(
42
)
.

•

Additional remarks

In addition to the key points mentioned above, various difficulties also arise during the iteration process. For instance, obtaining a bound for
\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}
in
(
14
)
presents a challenge. Another example is how to design a suitable iteration scheme from the reverse Hölder inequality (
15
) that also reflects the influence of the initial data, which presents a further challenge. Finally, establishing the boundedness of the starting point of the iteration, i.e.,
\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{l_{0}}_{T}L^{\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}l_{0}}}
, poses yet another difficulty. We refer to Proposition
4.2
for more details.

2.2
Positive lower bounds for the density

As for the derivation of a positive lower bound for the density, the method is essentially the same as that for the upper bound, with only minor modifications. It is particularly worth pointing out that the reason why the method used for the upper bound is also applicable to the lower bound is that
\alpha<1
. Specifically, from
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\partial_{t}\tau-c\alpha{\rm div}(\tau^{1-\alpha}\nabla\tau)+2c\alpha\tau^{-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}+v\cdot\nabla\tau-\tau{\rm div}v=0,

(20)

where
\tau=\rho^{-1}
. It can be seen that
\tau
also satisfies a parabolic equation, which makes it possible to use the modified Nash–Moser-type iteration method to raise the regularity of
\tau
from
L^{p}
with finite
p
to
L^{\infty}
, provided that we have certain regularity of
v
, namely
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\check{q}_{N}+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\check{q}_{N}+2}}
for some
\check{q}_{N}
.

We now discuss the main differences in the iteration for the lower bound of the density compared to that for the upper bound, and explain why the iteration works only for
\alpha<1
.

•

Iteration scheme:
L^{q}
to
L^{\infty}

In fact, by performing standard
L^{p}
estimates on
\tau
using
(
20
)
, we can obtain a constant
C_{3}\geq 1
, independent of
p
, such that

\displaystyle\left\|\tau^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\tau^{\frac{p-\alpha+3}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{3}\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}(p+3)^{2}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{l}_{T}L^{l\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}}}^{l}+C_{3}\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}\left\|\tau_{0}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(21)

where

l=p+1+\alpha+\frac{2}{\check{q}_{N}+2}
and
\Theta_{T}=\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}}+1
. As in the iteration procedure for the upper bound of the density, we take
r
such that the Sobolev embedding
H^{1}\hookrightarrow L^{r}
holds. Consequently,

\displaystyle\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{s}_{T}L^{\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}s}}\leq\left\|\tau^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{\xi}\left\|\tau^{\frac{p+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{r}}^{\eta},

where

\displaystyle\begin{split}&s=(p+2)\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p-\alpha+3,\\
&\eta=\frac{2}{(p+2)\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p-\alpha+3},\hskip 9.24994pt\xi=\frac{2\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)}{(p+2)\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)+p-\alpha+3}.\end{split}

Therefore, using the Sobolev embedding, we obtain the reverse Hölder inequality,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{s}_{T}L^{\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}s}}&\leq\tilde{C}_{r}^{\eta}\left(C_{3}\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}(p+3)^{2}\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{l}_{T}L^{l\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}}}^{l}+C_{3}\Theta_{T}^{1-\alpha}\left\|\tau_{0}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)^{\frac{\xi+\eta}{2}}.\end{split}

On the one hand, to ensure that the iteration reaches
L^{\infty}
, we need

\displaystyle\frac{s}{l}\to 1+\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}>1\quad\text{as }p\to\infty.

This requires
\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}>0
, where
r<\frac{2N}{N-2}
. Consequently, we need

\displaystyle\check{q}_{N}>N-2.

(22)

On the other hand, to prevent the energy accumulation from blowing up during the iteration, we need

\displaystyle\frac{\xi+\eta}{2}l\leq 1,

which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha\leq\frac{\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)\check{q}_{N}+2\check{q}_{N}+2}{\Big(\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}-\frac{2}{r}\Big)(\check{q}_{N}+2)+2\check{q}_{N}+4}.

Such a
\check{q}_{N}
exists provided that

\displaystyle\alpha<\frac{\check{q}_{N}+1}{\check{q}_{N}+2},

(23)

which is the opposite of the integrability condition
(
17
)
imposed on the effective velocity in the iteration for the upper bound of the density.
This is precisely why our iteration procedure requires
\alpha<1
. In light of these two observations, we can improve the regularity of
\tau
from a finite regularity to
L^{\infty}
.

•

Starting point of the iteration:
L^{q}
regularity of
\tau

As shown above, the upper bound of
\tau
follows once we prove the boundedness of the starting point of the iteration. To the end, we obtain from (
20
) that, for any
q>0
,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\tau^{q+2}dx+\frac{c(q+3)\alpha}{2}\int\tau^{q+1-\alpha}|\nabla\tau|^{2}dx\leq C\left(\int\tau^{l_{q}\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}}dx\right)^{{}^{\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}}},\end{split}

where
l_{q}=q+1+\alpha+\frac{2}{\check{q}_{N}+2}
. Since (
23
) holds, we get
l_{q}<q+3-\alpha
. Thus, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}C\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{l_{q}\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}}}^{l_{q}}&\leq C\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{(q+3-\alpha)\frac{\check{q}_{N}+2}{\check{q}_{N}}}}^{l_{q}}=C\left\|\tau^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\frac{2(\check{q}_{N}+2)}{\check{q}_{N}}}}^{\frac{2l_{q}}{q+3-\alpha}}\\
&\leq C\left\|\tau^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{H^{1}}^{\frac{2l_{q}}{q+3-\alpha}}\\
&\leq C\left\|\nabla\tau^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{\frac{2l_{q}}{q+3-\alpha}}+C\left\|\tau^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{1}}^{\frac{2l_{q}}{q+3-\alpha}}\\
&\leq\delta\left\|\nabla\tau^{\frac{q+3-\alpha}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C+C\left(\int\tau^{q+2}dx\right)^{\frac{l_{q}}{q+2}},\end{split}

where
\delta>0
is a sufficiently small constant. Then,

\displaystyle\frac{1}{q+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\tau^{q+2}dx\leq C+C\left(\int\tau^{q+2}dx\right)^{\frac{l_{q}}{q+2}}.

In order to obtain an estimate for
\left\|\tau\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{q+2}}
via Grönwall’s inequality, we need
l_{q}\leq q+2
, which is equivalent to

\displaystyle\alpha<\frac{\check{q}_{N}}{\check{q}_{N}+2}.

(24)

One can readily observe that condition
(
24
)
is more restrictive than
(
23
)
.

•

On the estimate of
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\check{q}N+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\check{q}_{N}+2}}

Owing to the dissipation in the parabolic equation for
v
, it follows from
(
19
)
that obtaining the integrability of
\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\check{q}N+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\check{q}_{N}+2}}
requires

\displaystyle\check{q}_{N}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(1+(\alpha-1)N)}{(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}},

(25)

which, together with
(
22
)
through
(
24
)
, implies that we need
\check{q}_{N}>N-2
and

\displaystyle\frac{2\alpha}{1-\alpha}<\check{q}_{N}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(1+(\alpha-1)N)}{(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}}.

To ensure the existence of such
\check{q}_{N}
, it naturally imposes restrictions on
\alpha
and
\beta
as given in
(
87
)
and
(
91
)
. Observe that the effective velocity requires higher integrability in the process of estimating the lower bound of the density compared to that for the upper bound. Therefore, the requirements on the parameters for closing the lower bound of the density are more stringent than those for the upper bound.

2.3
Derivation of higher-order estimates

In what follows, we explain the key points of our approach to the higher-order estimates.

•

Prerequisite of the higher-order argument: The upper and lower bounds of the density only freeze the coefficients, but do not yet close the strong estimates

Once the upper and lower bounds of
\rho
have been established, the coefficients
\rho^{\alpha}
,
\rho^{\alpha-1}
and
\rho^{-\alpha}
become uniformly bounded from above and below. This allows us to regard the momentum equation satisfied by
v
as a Lamé-type system with constant principal part and variable lower-order terms. In particular, one may hope to recover
\nabla^{2}v
from
v_{t}
,
\nabla\rho
,
\left|\nabla v\right|
and the transport terms. However, this is still far from sufficient for closing the higher-order estimates. The essential reason is that, when
\alpha<1
, the diffusion in the continuity equation acts naturally on
\rho^{\alpha}
rather than on
\rho
itself. Therefore, even after the density has been bounded away from vacuum and concentration, differentiating the equation for
\rho
still produces nonlinear terms involving both the derivatives of the coefficient
\rho^{\alpha-1}
and the convection
{\rm div}(\rho v)
. In particular, closing the first-level estimate requires simultaneous control of the density and velocity quantities at the same differential level, including
\nabla^{2}\rho
,
\nabla v
,
\nabla\rho_{t}
,
v_{t}
,
\nabla^{2}v
, together with the diffusion quantity
\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})
. Once this first-level control is available, one can then recover
\nabla^{3}\rho
and subsequently estimate
\rho_{t}
directly from the continuity equation. This is the basic reason why the upper and lower bounds for the density serve only as a starting point in the higher-order analysis.

•

Core difficulty in the continuity equation: the dissipation is generated at the level of
\rho^{\alpha}
, while the nonlinear error terms are expressed in terms of
\rho

If one rewrites the continuity equation and tests it by
\Delta\rho_{t}
, then in the linear case
\alpha=1
the diffusion contributes the quadratic energy term

\frac{c}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

and the corresponding control of
\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
is recovered through the identity

\rho_{t}+\div(\rho v)=c\Delta\rho.

By contrast, when
\alpha<1
, testing the rewritten continuity equation by
\Delta\rho_{t}
produces the weighted energy

\frac{c\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx

together with the dissipation
\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, while the quantity
\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
is subsequently controlled through the identity

\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho v)=c\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})

and added to the preceding estimate. At the same time, differentiating the coefficient
\rho^{\alpha-1}
gives rise to the difficult term

\int(\rho^{\alpha-1})_{t}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx,

while one encounters nonlinear products such as

|v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2},\qquad|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2},\qquad|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2},\qquad|\nabla\rho|^{6}.

These terms represent the main nonlinear obstructions at the first level: the last two are intrinsic to the fast-diffusion structure, while the mixed terms involving
v
arise from the convection.Our strategy is not to force the equation into a form resembling the linear heat equation. Instead, we keep the good quantity
\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})
generated by the diffusion, and estimate the coefficient term and the convection term by integration by parts. In two dimensions, this allows the dangerous contributions to be absorbed by a small multiple of
\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
plus lower-order nonlinear terms after rewriting in terms of
z=\rho^{\alpha}
; in three dimensions, one must further use the additional positive quantity

A(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx

to control the critical terms. Thus the first-level diffusion is organized around
\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})
rather than directly around
\nabla^{3}\rho
.

•

Coupling mechanism with the momentum equation: recovering
\nabla^{2}v
without losing derivatives

The continuity estimate alone is not closed, because differentiating
{\rm div}(\rho v)
at the first level yields terms coming from the expansion of
\nabla^{2}(\rho v)
, in particular

\rho\nabla^{2}v,\qquad\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla v,\qquad v\nabla^{2}\rho.

Therefore, the momentum equation must be estimated simultaneously at the same level.To this end, we test the equation for
v
by
v_{t}
, which yields a coercive first-order energy inequality with leading term given by the weighted quadratic functional

\int\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx.

On the other hand, thanks to the upper and lower bounds of
\rho
, the momentum equation can be rewritten as a constant-coefficient Lamé-type elliptic system

\nu\Delta v+a_{0}\nabla{\rm div}v=F,

where
F
contains
\rho v_{t}
,
\rho u\cdot\nabla v
,
\nabla P
, and lower-order commutator terms involving
\nabla\rho
and
\nabla v
.This allows us to derive an elliptic estimate for
\nabla^{2}v
in terms of
v_{t}
and lower-order quantities. The importance of this step is that it prevents the convection from causing a loss of derivatives: the second derivatives of
v
needed in the continuity equation are recovered from the momentum equation, while the nonlinear terms in the momentum equation can in turn be estimated by the quantities appearing in the continuity estimate. Thus the continuity and momentum estimates are coupled at the first level, with an additional auxiliary
L^{4}
estimate for
\nabla\rho
required in the three-dimensional case.

•

Two-dimensional closure: rewriting the highest-order density term in terms of
z=\rho^{\alpha}

In two dimensions, once the first-level differential inequalities for the continuity equation and the momentum equation have been obtained, the remaining nonlinear terms can still be treated perturbatively. Indeed, the basic energy estimate, together with the upper and lower bounds of the density, yields an
L^{\infty}_{t}L^{2}_{x}
bound for
\nabla(\rho^{\alpha})
, and in dimension two the Gagliardo–Nirenberg and Ladyzhenskaya inequalities are strong enough to control the troublesome terms
|\nabla\rho|^{6}
and
|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}
after rewriting them in terms of
z=\rho^{\alpha}
. More precisely, after setting
z=\rho^{\alpha}
, the upper and lower bounds of
\rho
allow us to express
\nabla\rho
and
\nabla^{2}\rho
in terms of
\nabla z
and
\nabla^{2}z
. Consequently, the troublesome terms

\int|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx,\qquad\int|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx

can be bounded by a small multiple of
\|\nabla^{3}z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\nabla\Delta z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
plus a lower-order quantity depending on
\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}
. This is sufficient to absorb the dangerous part into the dissipation and then apply Grönwall’s inequality. Hence, in two dimensions, the fast diffusion changes the algebra of the estimates, but it does not destroy the perturbative structure of the first-level energy method. The remaining mixed terms involving
v
, such as
|v|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}
,
|v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}
, and
|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}
, are then treated by combining the
L^{q}
-bound for
v
with two-dimensional interpolation.

•

Genuinely three-dimensional difficulty: the terms
|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}
and
|\nabla\rho|^{6}
are no longer lower-order

The three-dimensional case is substantially more delicate.
At the level of the first-order differential inequalities, the nonlinear terms

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx\qquad\text{and}\qquad\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx

can no longer be treated perturbatively by using only the first-level dissipation

\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\qquad\text{and}\qquad\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

To close the estimate, one has to derive an additional evolution inequality for

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
, whose diffusion produces the positive quantity

A(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Using the upper and lower bounds of
\rho
, this quantity controls

\int|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx
; moreover, a sharp algebraic estimate
shows that the weighted cubic term

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx

is also bounded by
A(t)
. This is precisely the mechanism that makes the
three-dimensional higher-order analysis more difficult than the two-dimensional one
and is also the point where the additional restriction on
\alpha
arises. To overcome this difficulty, we derive an additional evolution estimate for

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
by differentiating the continuity equation and testing the
resulting equation by
c^{-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho
. The crucial point is that the
diffusion contribution first produces a quadratic form
D(t)
, which, after a direct
algebraic decomposition, satisfies

D(t)\geq\alpha A(t)-C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx.

Using the sharp estimate

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq C\,A(t),

we obtain a coercive lower bound
D(t)\geq\varepsilon_{2}A(t)
for some

\varepsilon_{2}>0
. Consequently, the auxiliary
L^{4}
-estimate for
\nabla\rho

provides precisely the extra dissipation needed to control the critical density terms
that cannot be treated perturbatively in the three-dimensional first-level
higher-order estimate. Moreover, a sharp algebraic estimate shows that the cubic term

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx

can also be controlled by
A(t)
. As a consequence, the auxiliary evolution estimate
for
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
becomes coercive provided that

\varepsilon_{2}=\alpha\left(1-\frac{243(1-\alpha)^{2}}{8(2-\alpha)^{2}}\right)>0.

This is exactly the step at which an additional lower-bound restriction on
\alpha

enters the three-dimensional first-level higher-order estimate: one needs the
dissipation generated by the fast diffusion to dominate the cubic density interaction.

•

Closure of the first-level estimate and propagation to higher levels.

After the second-order continuity estimate, the first-order momentum estimate, and, in the three-dimensional case, the auxiliary
L^{4}
estimate for
\nabla\rho
, we combine the corresponding differential inequalities with suitable large coefficients. In two dimensions, after rewriting the highest-order density terms in terms of
z=\rho^{\alpha}
, the dangerous contributions can be absorbed perturbatively, while the remaining mixed terms are handled by the
L^{q}
-bound for
v
together with two-dimensional interpolation. In three dimensions, the additional quantity
A(t)
supplied by the auxiliary
L^{4}
estimate for
\nabla\rho
absorbs the critical first-level terms that cannot be treated perturbatively. In both cases, we finally obtain a closed differential inequality for an energy functional equivalent to

\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

together with
\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}
when
N=3
. Since the lower-order spacetime bounds have already been obtained from the basic energy estimate and from the upper and lower bounds of the density, Grönwall’s inequality yields the first-level higher-order estimate. Once the first-level differential inequality has been closed, one first recovers
\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}
from the chain-rule relation between
\rho
and
\rho^{\alpha}
: in two dimensions through
\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
together with the lower-order
z
-terms, and in three dimensions through
\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
combined with the control of
A(t)
. One then estimates
\rho_{t}
directly from the continuity equation. After that, the subsequent higher-order estimates can be carried out in a much more standard way.
In this sense, the heart of the argument lies in the first-level estimate, where one must make the fast diffusion, the convection, and the Lamé structure interact in a compatible way.

3
Energy estimates

Throughout the remainder of this paper, unless otherwise indicated, we shall always assume that
N=2
or
3
,
\alpha\in\left(\frac{N-1}{N},1\right)
,
\gamma\in[1,\infty)
and
\beta\in[0,1)
. By a standard argument, system (
13
) with periodic boundary conditions and initial data
(\rho_{0},v_{0})
admits a unique local strong solution on
\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T^{*})
for some
T^{*}>0
. We prove that
(\rho,v)
is global in time by contradiction, i.e., we assume that
T^{*}<\infty
. Then, for any
T\in(0,T^{*})
,
(\rho,v)
is well-defined on
\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T]
.

We start with the standard energy estimate and define

\displaystyle E_{0}=\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho_{0}|u_{0}|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho_{0}^{\gamma}+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}|\nabla\rho_{0}^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\right)dx.

Proposition 3.1
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon
, and
E_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\left(\rho|u|^{2}+\rho^{\gamma}+|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\right)dx+\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(26)

Proof.

Our analysis is devoted to the case
\gamma>1
, the proof for
\gamma=1
being entirely similar. Multiplying
\eqref{Equ1}_{2}
by
u
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and using integration by parts together with
\eqref{Equ1}_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)&dx+2\nu\int\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dx\\
&+2\nu(\alpha-1)\int\rho^{\alpha}({\rm div}u)^{2}dx=\int{\rm div}\mathbb{K}\cdot udx.\end{split}

Using Lemma
8.1
and
\eqref{Equ1}_{1}
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int{\rm div}\mathbb{K}\cdot udx&=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\int\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}{\rm div}(\rho u)dx\\
&=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\int\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}\rho dx\\
&=\frac{4\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\int\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\partial_{t}\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}dx\\
&=-\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}\frac{d}{dt}\int|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}dx.\end{split}

Therefore,

\displaystyle\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|u|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}+\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{(2\alpha-1)^{2}}|\nabla\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}|^{2}\right)dx

\displaystyle+2\nu\int\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dx+2\nu(\alpha-1)\int\rho^{\alpha}({\rm div}u)^{2}dx=0.

For
\alpha\in\big(\frac{N-1}{N},1\big)
, integrating the above equality over time and invoking the basic estimate
|\operatorname{div}u|^{2}\leq N|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}
, we obtain
(
26
)
.
∎

Next, we prove that the system satisfies the general entropy inequality.

Proposition 3.2
.

There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon
, and
E_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho|v|^{2}dx+\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx+\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(27)

Proof.

As before, we treat the case
\gamma>1
; the case
\gamma=1
can be handled similarly. Multiplying
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
v
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and using integration by parts together with
\eqref{Equ1}_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)dx+c\gamma\alpha\int\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx\\
&+\nu\int\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v:(\nabla v)^{t}dx+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}({\rm div}v)^{2}dx=0.\end{split}

(28)

Since
|{\rm div}v|^{2}\leq N|\nabla v|^{2}
,
\alpha<1
, and
c\in(\nu,2\nu)
, we have

\displaystyle(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}({\rm div}v)^{2}dx\geq N(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dx

(29)

and

\displaystyle(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v:(\nabla v)^{t}dx\geq(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dx.

(30)

Substituting
(
29
)
and
(
30
)
into
(
28
)
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{d}{dt}\int\left(\frac{1}{2}\rho|v|^{2}+\frac{1}{\gamma-1}\rho^{\gamma}\right)dx+c\gamma\alpha\int\rho^{\gamma+\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx\\
&+(2\nu-c)(1+N(\alpha-1))\int\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\leq 0.\end{split}

(31)

Integrating the above inequality over time, we obtain
(
27
)
, thanks to the facts that
\alpha>\frac{N-1}{N}
and
c<2\nu
.

∎

Moreover, we discuss the higher regularity estimates for the density resulting from the Korteweg term in the three-dimensional case, which will be used to relax the restriction on the exponent
\gamma
in three dimensions.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
N=3
. There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon
, and
E_{0}
such that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\big|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}}\big|^{4}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(32)

Proof.

By Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla v|^{2}dxdt=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(u+c\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)|^{2}dxdt\\
&=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt+c^{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)|^{2}dxdt+2c\alpha\int\rho^{\alpha}\nabla u:\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)dxdt\\
&=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt+c^{2}\alpha^{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)|^{2}dxdt+2c\alpha\int\rho^{\alpha}\mathbb{D}u:\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)dxdt\\
&\geq\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt+\frac{c^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)|^{2}dxdt-C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\mathbb{D}u|^{2}dxdt,\end{split}

where the third line holds because
\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)
is a symmetric matrix. This, together with
(
26
)
and
(
27
)
, implies that

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla u|^{2}dxdt+\frac{c^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)|^{2}dxdt\leq C.

(33)

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{|3\alpha-2|^{2}}{4|\alpha-1|^{2}}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla(\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1})|^{2}dxdt\\
&=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\otimes\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}+\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&=\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&\quad+2\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\cdot\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}dxdt.\end{split}

(34)

Next, we focus on handling the last term on the right-hand side. A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad 2\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\frac{\alpha}{2}}\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\cdot\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}dxdt\\
&=-\alpha\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\cdot\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}dxdt\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}{\rm div}(\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho)|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{Q_{T}}{\rm div}(\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla\rho^{\alpha-1})|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&=\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{Q_{T}}\nabla\rho^{1-\alpha}\cdot\nabla\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt+\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{1-\alpha}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&\geq\frac{\alpha}{2(\alpha-1)}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{1-\alpha}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\\
&\geq-\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt-C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt,\end{split}

(35)

where the last inequality follows from Young’s inequality. Substituting
(
35
)
into
(
34
)
yields

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla\rho^{-\frac{\alpha}{2}}|^{2}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt+\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt\leq C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{\alpha}|\nabla^{2}\rho^{\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt,

which, together with
(
33
)
, implies
(
32
)
.
∎

With the preceding properties established, we are now in a position to derive the following corollary.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
N=3
. There exists a constant
C>0
depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0}
and
T^{*}
such that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}H^{2}}\leq C.

(36)

Proof.

By virtue of
(
26
)
and the fact that
\alpha-\frac{1}{2}<\gamma
, we obtain, using the standard Sobolev embedding, the boundedness of
\left\|\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6}}
, and consequently the boundedness of
\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{6\alpha-3}}
.

For the term
\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{2}}
, applying Young’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{3\alpha-2}dxdt\leq\int_{Q_{T}}\big(\rho^{6\alpha-3}+1\big)dxdt\leq C.

(37)

For the term
\left\|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}L^{2}}
, we obtain from (
32
), Young’s inequality, and (
37
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\int_{Q_{T}}|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}|^{2}dxdt&\leq C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{3\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dxdt\\
&\leq C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{3\alpha-6}|\nabla\rho|^{4}dx+C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{3\alpha-2}dxdt\\
&\leq C\int_{Q_{T}}\big|\nabla\rho^{\frac{3\alpha-2}{4}}\big|^{4}dxdt+C\int_{Q_{T}}\rho^{3\alpha-2}dxdt\\
&\leq C.\end{split}

(38)

A combination of
(
37
)
,
(
38
)
, and
(
32
)
leads to
(
36
)
, thus concluding the proof of Corollary
3.1
.
∎

4
Upper bound for the density

In this section, we apply a modified Nash-Moser-type iteration technique to obtain an upper bound for the density. From the parabolic equation
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
satisfied by
\rho
, the key to this method for improving the regularity of the density lies in obtaining certain integrability estimates for
v
. Indeed, in the iteration process, we need density-weighted
L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p}
estimates for
v
with
p
greater than the dimension.

To achieve this, we introduce the following notation:

\displaystyle\begin{split}\beta^{+}_{2}(x)&=1,\ x\in(0.5,1);\\
\beta^{+}_{3}(x)&=-\frac{-3x^{2}+(-\sqrt{3}+12)x+\sqrt{3}-7}{(1-\sqrt{3}(1-x))^{2}}\\
&\hskip 9.24994pt+\frac{1}{2}\sqrt{\frac{(-6x^{2}+(-2\sqrt{3}+24)x+2\sqrt{3}-14)^{2}}{(1-\sqrt{3}(1-x))^{4}}-4\frac{3x^{2}-18x+11}{(1-\sqrt{3}(1-x))^{2}}},\ x\in\Big(\frac{9-\sqrt{48}}{3},1\Big).\end{split}

Here
\beta^{+}_{3}(x)
is the positive root of the following quadratic equation

\displaystyle\beta^{+}_{3}(x)^{2}+\frac{-6x^{2}+(-2\sqrt{3}+24)x+2\sqrt{3}-14}{(1-\sqrt{3}(1-x))^{2}}\beta^{+}_{3}(x)+\frac{3x^{2}-18x+11}{(1-\sqrt{3}(1-x))^{2}}=0,

which is equal to

\displaystyle 1=\frac{4(1-\beta_{3}^{+}(x))(3x-2)}{(\beta_{3}^{+}(x)+\sqrt{3}(1-x)(1-\beta_{3}^{+}(x)))^{2}}.

We now discuss the selection of integrability exponents for the effective velocity that leads to an upper bound for the density, treating the cases
N=2
and
N=3
separately.

•

Case I: The two-dimensional case

For any
(\alpha,\gamma,\beta)
satisfying

\displaystyle N=2,\quad\alpha\in(0.5,1),\quad\gamma\in[1,\infty),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta_{2}^{+}(\alpha)),

(39)

we choose
\hat{q}_{2}>0
sufficiently small, depending on
\alpha
and
\beta
, such that the following condition holds:

\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{\hat{q}_{2}+1}{\hat{q}_{2}+2},

(40)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{2}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(2\alpha-1)}{(\beta+\sqrt{2}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}}.

(41)

Such a
\hat{q}_{2}>0
satisfying both
(
40
)
and
(
41
)
exists because
\alpha\in(0.5,1)
and
\beta\in[0,\beta_{2}^{+}(\alpha))
.

•

Case II: The three-dimensional case

For any
(\alpha,\gamma,\beta)
satisfying

\displaystyle N=3,\quad\alpha\in\Big(\frac{9-\sqrt{48}}{3},1\Big),\quad\gamma\in\Big[1,\frac{15\alpha-7}{3}\Big),\quad\beta\in[0,\beta_{3}^{+}(\alpha)),

(42)

we choose
\hat{q}_{3}>1
sufficiently small, depending on
\alpha,\beta
and
\gamma
, such that the following condition holds:

\displaystyle\alpha>\frac{\hat{q}_{3}+1}{\hat{q}_{3}+2},

(43)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{3}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(3\alpha-2)}{(\beta+\sqrt{3}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}},

(44)

\displaystyle\gamma<3\alpha-1+\frac{6\alpha-4}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}.

(45)

It is worth noting that there exists a
\hat{q}_{3}>1
fulfilling
(
43
)
to
(
45
)
simultaneously. Indeed,
(
43
)
is guaranteed by
\alpha>\frac{2}{3}
,
(
44
)
holds due to
\alpha\in\big(\frac{9-\sqrt{48}}{3},1\big)
together with
\beta\in[0,\beta_{3}^{+}(\alpha))
, and
(
45
)
is satisfied for
\gamma\in\big[1,\frac{15\alpha-7}{3}\big)
.

We now proceed to establish the
\rho
-weighted
L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}
integrability of
v
.

Proposition 4.1
.

Assume that
(
39
)
or
(
42
)
holds. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{*},\beta,N,\hat{q}_{N}
, and
\left\|\rho_{0}^{1/(\hat{q}_{N}+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}
, such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\leq C.

(46)

Proof.

Multiplying
\eqref{Equ2}_{2}
by
|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and using integration by parts, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx+\nu\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\nu\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx\\
&\quad+(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{t}:\nabla(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v{\rm div}(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx\\
&\leq C\int\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|dx.\end{split}

(47)

Since
\nu<c
, we have

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{t}:\nabla(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx\\
&\geq(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+(\nu-c)\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v||\nabla|v||dx.\end{split}

(48)

Moreover, since
c<2\nu
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v{\rm div}(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx\\
&\geq(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}({\rm div}v)^{2}dx+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|{\rm div}v||\nabla|v||dx\\
&\geq N(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx+\sqrt{N}(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v||\nabla|v||dx,\end{split}

(49)

where the last inequality follows from
|{\rm div}v|\leq\sqrt{N}|\nabla v|
.
Adding
(
48
)
and
(
49
)
together with Young’s inequality gives

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad(\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{t}:\nabla(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\int\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v{\rm div}(|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}v)dx\\
&\geq\Big(\nu-c+N(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\Big)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\\
&\quad+\hat{q}_{N}\Big(\nu-c+\sqrt{N}(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\Big)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v||\nabla|v||dx\\
&\geq\Big(\nu-c+N(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\Big)\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\\
&\quad-\nu\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla|v||^{2}dx-\frac{(c-\nu+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(2\nu-c))^{2}}{4\nu}\hat{q}_{N}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx,\end{split}

(50)

Substituting
(
50
)
into
(
47
)
yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx+\hat{\delta}_{0}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\leq C\int\rho^{\gamma}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|dx,\end{split}

(51)

where

\displaystyle\hat{\delta}_{0}:=(2\nu-c)(1+N(\alpha-1))-\frac{(c-\nu+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(2\nu-c))^{2}}{4\nu}\hat{q}_{N}>0,

due to

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{N}<\frac{4(1-\beta)(1+(\alpha-1)N)}{(\beta+\sqrt{N}(1-\alpha)(1-\beta))^{2}},

which is guaranteed by
(
41
)
and
(
44
)
. Thus, applying Young’s inequality yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx&+\frac{\hat{\delta}_{0}}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}|\nabla v|^{2}dx\leq C\int\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}}dx.\end{split}

(52)

Next, we proceed to estimate the right-hand side of the above inequality. When
N=2
, it follows from Young’s inequality that

\displaystyle C\int\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{2}}dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{2}+2}dx+C\int\rho^{\Big(2\gamma-\alpha-\frac{\hat{q}_{2}}{\hat{q}_{2}+2}\Big)\frac{\hat{q}_{2}+2}{2}}dx.

(53)

Combining
(
26
)
with the fact that
\gamma>\alpha-\frac{1}{2}
, we obtain from the Sobolev embedding the boundedness of
\left\|\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p}}
for any
1\leq p<\infty
, which in turn yields the boundedness of
\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{p}}
for any
1\leq p<\infty
. Consequently, it follows from
(
52
)
and
(
53
)
that

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{2}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{2}+2}dx\leq C\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{2}+2}dx+C,

which together with Grönwall’s inequality implies (
46
).

When
N=3
, we use (
52
) and Hölder’s inequality to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{3}+2}dx&\leq C\int\rho^{2\gamma-\alpha}|v|^{\hat{q}_{3}}dx\\
&\leq C\left(\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{3}+2}dx\right)^{\frac{\hat{q}_{3}}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}\left(\int\rho^{\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(\hat{q}_{3}+2)-\hat{q}_{3}}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}.\end{split}

(54)

To handle the right-hand side of the above inequality, we use the Gagliardo-Nirenberg inequality, Hölder’s inequality, (
26
), and (
36
) to obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L_{T}^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}\zeta}L^{\zeta}}&\leq C\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{\frac{12\alpha-6}{3\alpha-2}}}^{\theta}\left\|\rho^{\frac{3}{2}\alpha-1}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}W^{1,6}}^{1-\theta}\leq C,\end{split}

where

\displaystyle\theta=\frac{(2\alpha-1)\hat{q}_{3}+16\alpha-8}{(5\alpha-3)\hat{q}_{3}+22\alpha-12},\quad\zeta=\frac{(5\alpha-3)\hat{q}_{3}+22\alpha-12}{3\alpha-2}.

This implies that

\displaystyle\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}\zeta^{\prime}}_{T}L^{\zeta^{\prime}}}\leq C,

(55)

where

\displaystyle\zeta^{\prime}=\frac{(5\alpha-3)\hat{q}_{3}+22\alpha-12}{2}.

Consequently, it follows from (
54
) and (
45
) that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{d}{dt}\left\|\rho^{\frac{1}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}v\right\|_{L^{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}^{2}&\leq C\left(\int\rho^{\frac{(2\gamma-\alpha)(\hat{q}_{3}+2)-\hat{q}_{3}}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}\\
&=C\left(\int\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}\rho^{\frac{(2\gamma-6\alpha+2)\hat{q}_{3}+4\gamma-24\alpha+12}{2}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int(\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}+1)dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}\\
&\leq C\left(\int\rho^{\zeta^{\prime}}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{3}+2}}+C,\end{split}

which, after integrating in time and employing
(
55
)
, leads to (
46
).

Therefore, we have completed the proof of Proposition
4.1
.
∎

We are now ready to establish the upper bound for the density. Denote that

\displaystyle M_{0}=\int\rho_{0}dx.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
(
39
)
or
(
42
)
holds. Then there exists a constant
C>0
, depending on
\gamma,\alpha,\nu,\varepsilon,E_{0},T^{*},\beta,N,\hat{q}_{N},\left\|\rho_{0}^{1/(\hat{q}_{N}+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}},M_{0}^{-1}
, and
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}}
such that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C.

(56)

Proof.

Step 1: Preparation for the iteration

We claim that there exists a universal constant
C_{0}\geq 1
, depending on the quantities stated in Proposition
4.2
but independent of
p
, such that for any
p>0
,

\displaystyle\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{\infty}_{T}L^{2}}^{2}+\left\|\rho^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}_{T}H^{1}}^{2}\leq C_{0}(p+2)^{2}\left\|\rho\right\|_{L^{l}_{T}L^{l\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}}}^{l}+C_{0}\left\|\rho_{0}^{\frac{p+2}{2}}\right\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

(57)

where

l=p+3-\alpha-\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}=\frac{p(\hat{q}_{N}+2)+(3-\alpha)(\hat{q}_{N}+2)-2}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}
.

Indeed, for any
p>0
, multiplying
\eqref{Equ2}_{1}
by
\rho^{p+1}
, integrating the resulting equation over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and using integration by parts together with Young’s inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{p+2}dx+c\alpha(p+1)\int\rho^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx\\
&=\int\rho v\cdot\nabla\rho^{p+1}dx\\
&\leq\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx+C(p+1)\int\rho^{p-\alpha+3}|v|^{2}dx\\
&\leq\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx+C(p+1)\left(\int\rho|v|^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}dx\right)^{\frac{2}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}\left(\int\rho^{l\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}}dx\right)^{\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}.\end{split}

(58)

Consequently, it follows from
(
46
)
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{1}{p+2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{p+2}dx+\frac{c\alpha(p+1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha+p-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}dx\\
&\leq C(p+1)\left(\int\rho^{l\frac{\hat{q}_{N}+2}{\hat{q}_{N}}}dx\right)^{\frac{\hat{q}_{N}}{\hat{q}_{N}+2}}.\end{split}

(59)

Integrating the above inequality over time yields

&\leq\int_{0}^{T}\left(\int\rho^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{p+2}}\left(\int 1dx\right)^{\frac{1-\alpha}{p+2}}dt\\
&\leq T\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{p+\alpha+1}{p+2}}\\
&\leq T^{*}\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{\alpha-1}{p+2}}\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho^{p+2}dx.\end{split}

\displaystyle=\int\rho_{0}dx=\int\rho(x,t)dx\leq\left(\int\rho^{p+2}(x,t)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2}}\left(\int 1dx\right)^{\frac{p+1}{p+2}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int\rho^{p+2}(x,t)dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2}}\leq\left(\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int\rho^{p+2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{p+2}},

6
Higher-order estimates

With the established upper and lower bounds for the density at hand, we now turn to estimates for higher-order derivatives of
(\rho,v)
, which satisfy the following system:

\left\{\begin{array}[]{l}\rho_{t}+\div(\rho v)-c\Delta\rho^{\alpha}=0,\\
\rho\dot{v}+\nabla P=\nu{\rm div}(\rho^{\alpha}\nabla v)+(\nu-c){\rm div}(\rho^{\alpha}(\nabla v)^{t})+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\nabla(\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v).\end{array}\right.

(117)

Since the density equation takes the form of a porous medium equation with fast diffusion, difficulties arise from the nonlinear terms when performing the first level of higher-order estimates. The following Proposition
6.1
addresses these challenges for the two-dimensional and three-dimensional cases, respectively.

By applying Hölder’s inequality and standard proof techniques, we readily obtain Lemma
6.1
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
q>0
. Assume that

\rho(x,t)\geq\underline{\rho}>0\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T],

and that for some constant
C_{q}>0
,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\rho|v|^{q+2}\,dx\leq C_{q}.

Then,
\forall t\in[0,T]
,

\left(\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}|v(t)|^{q}\,dx\right)^{\frac{1}{q}}\leq C_{q}^{\frac{1}{q+2}}|\mathbb{T}^{N}|^{\frac{2}{q(q+2)}}\underline{\rho}^{-\frac{1}{q+2}}.

Next, we show the elliptic estimate for the effective velocity
v
.

Lemma 6.2
.

Assume that

0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T].

Set

a_{0}:=(\nu-c)+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c).

Then, by the second equation in
\eqref{0}_{2}
,
v
satisfies the constant–coefficient elliptic system

\nu\Delta v+a_{0}\nabla{\rm div}v=F,

(118)

where

\begin{split}F=\frac{1}{\rho^{\alpha}}\Big(&\rho v_{t}+\rho u\cdot\nabla v+\nabla P\\
&-\nu\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla v-(\nu-c)\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\cdot(\nabla v)^{t}-(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)\nabla\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}v\Big).\end{split}

(119)

Then there exists a constant

\varepsilon_{1}=\varepsilon_{1}\!\left(\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,N,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho}\right)>0

such that

\varepsilon_{1}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}|\nabla^{2}v|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\rho|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}\Big(|v|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Big)\,dx.

(120)

Proof.

All integrals are taken over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
.
By the second equation in
\eqref{0}_{2}
and the product rule, one collects all second–order terms to the left to obtain (
118
)–(
119
).

Set
\mathcal{L}v:=\nu\Delta v+a_{0}\nabla{\rm div}v
and

m_{0}:=\min\{\nu,\nu+a_{0}\}=\min\{\nu,\alpha(2\nu-c)\}>0.

For
\xi\neq 0
, the Fourier symbol of
\mathcal{L}
is

\sigma_{\mathcal{L}}(\xi)=\nu|\xi|^{2}I+a_{0}\,\xi\otimes\xi,

whose eigenvalues are
\nu|\xi|^{2}
(with multiplicity
N-1
) and
(\nu+a_{0})|\xi|^{2}
. Hence

|\sigma_{\mathcal{L}}(\xi)w|\geq m_{0}|\xi|^{2}|w|\qquad\text{for all }w\in\mathbb{C}^{N},

and therefore, by Parseval’s identity,

\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{m_{0}^{2}}\,\|F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(121)

It remains to bound
\|F\|_{L^{2}}
. Since
\underline{\rho}\leq\rho\leq\overline{\rho}
, all weights
\rho^{\theta}
appearing below are bounded in
L^{\infty}
by constants depending only on
\alpha,\gamma,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho}
. Using also

u=v-\alpha c\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho,\qquad\nabla P=\gamma\rho^{\gamma-1}\nabla\rho,\qquad\rho^{-\alpha}\nabla\rho^{\alpha}=\alpha\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho,\qquad|{\rm div}v|\leq\sqrt{N}\,|\nabla v|,

we infer

\|F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\int\rho|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+C\int\Big(|v|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla v|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Big)\,dx,

where
C=C(\alpha,\gamma,\nu,\varepsilon,N,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho})
(recall
c=c_{+}(\varepsilon)
is fixed).
Combining with (
121
) and choosing
\varepsilon_{1}:=m_{0}^{2}/(4C)
yield (
120
).
∎

The following two Lemmas gives some basic control on two intrinsic nonlinear terms
|\nabla\rho|^{6}
and
|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}
from the fast-diffusion structure.

Lemma 6.3
.

Assume that
N=3
,
\alpha<1
, and

0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T].

Then, for every fixed
t\in[0,T]
,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq\frac{27}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(122)

Consequently,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq\frac{27\,\overline{\rho}^{\,3-\alpha}}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(123)

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
and write all integrals over
\mathbb{T}^{3}
. Set

J:=\int\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx.

Since
\partial_{i}(\rho^{\alpha-2})=(\alpha-2)\rho^{\alpha-3}\partial_{i}\rho
, an integration by parts gives

(2-\alpha)J=\int\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Bigl(4\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}(\partial_{ij}\rho)(\partial_{i}\rho)(\partial_{j}\rho)+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Delta\rho\Bigr)\,dx.

On
\{|\nabla\rho|\neq 0\}
, let

e:=\frac{\nabla\rho}{|\nabla\rho|},\qquad H:=\nabla^{2}\rho.

Then

4\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}(\partial_{ij}\rho)(\partial_{i}\rho)(\partial_{j}\rho)+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Delta\rho=|\nabla\rho|^{2}(4e\otimes e+I):H,

while the identity is trivial on
\{|\nabla\rho|=0\}
. Since
|e|=1
and
N=3
,

|4e\otimes e+I|^{2}=16+8+3=27.

Hence

\left|4\sum_{i,j=1}^{3}(\partial_{ij}\rho)(\partial_{i}\rho)(\partial_{j}\rho)+|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Delta\rho\right|\leq\sqrt{27}\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|.\qquad\text{a.e.}

Therefore,

(2-\alpha)J\leq\sqrt{27}\int\Bigl(\rho^{\frac{\alpha-3}{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{3}\Bigr)\Bigl(\rho^{\frac{\alpha-1}{2}}|\nabla\rho||\nabla^{2}\rho|\Bigr)\,dx.

Young’s inequality with parameter
2-\alpha
yields

J\leq\frac{27}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx,

which is exactly (
122
). Since

|\nabla\rho|^{6}=\rho^{3-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\leq\overline{\rho}^{\,3-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6},

(
123
) follows immediately from (
122
).
∎

Remark 6.1
.

The same argument shows that (

) remains valid for every
\alpha\neq 2
: one simply replaces
2-\alpha
by
|2-\alpha|
in the previous proof. Accordingly, under the sole assumption

one has

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq\frac{27}{(2-\alpha)^{2}}\max_{\underline{\rho}\leq s\leq\overline{\rho}}s^{3-\alpha}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, the constant
27
is optimal within this pointwise argument. Indeed, for any unit vector

,

\sup_{H=H^{\top},\;H\neq 0}\frac{|(4e\otimes e+I):H|^{2}}{|H|^{2}}=|4e\otimes e+I|^{2}=27.

Hence the coefficient in the pointwise estimate above, and therefore the constant in (
122
) produced by this route, cannot be improved.

Lemma 6.4
.

Let
N=2
and
\alpha\neq 0
. For some fixed
t\in[0,T]
, assume that

0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty\qquad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{2},

and set

z:=\rho^{\alpha}.

Assume further that

\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\|\nabla z(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C_{0}.

If, at this time
t
, one has
z(\cdot,t)\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{2})
, then there exists a constant

C>0
, depending only on

\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho},C_{0}
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

(124)

Moreover, for any
\eta>0
, there exists a constant
C_{\eta}>0
, depending only on

\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho},C_{0},\eta
, such that

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

(125)

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
and suppress the dependence on
t
. Since
z=\rho^{\alpha}
and
\alpha\neq 0
, the chain rule gives

\nabla\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha}\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla z,\qquad\nabla^{2}\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha}\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla^{2}z+\frac{1-\alpha}{\alpha^{2}}\rho^{1-2\alpha}\nabla z\otimes\nabla z.

In particular, in view of
\underline{\rho}\leq\rho\leq\overline{\rho}
,

|\nabla\rho|\leq C|\nabla z|,\qquad|\nabla^{2}\rho|\leq C\bigl(|\nabla^{2}z|+|\nabla z|^{2}\bigr),

with
C=C(\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho})
.

Since each component of
\nabla z
has zero mean on
\mathbb{T}^{2}
, the two-dimensional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality yields

\|\nabla z\|_{L^{6}}^{6}\leq C\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}\leq CC_{0}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

Therefore,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla z|^{6}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

which proves (
124
).

Next, by the preceding pointwise bounds,

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla z|^{2}|\nabla^{2}z|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla z|^{6}\,dx.

The second term is already controlled by (
124
) applied to
\rho=z^{1/\alpha}
, equivalently by the previous estimate on
\|\nabla z\|_{L^{6}}^{6}
. For the first term, Hölder’s inequality gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla z|^{2}|\nabla^{2}z|^{2}\,dx\leq\|\nabla z\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{4}}^{2}.

Again by the two-dimensional Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequality,

\|\nabla z\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{4}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}z\|_{L^{2}}.

Hence

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{2}}|\nabla z|^{2}|\nabla^{2}z|^{2}\,dx\leq C\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla^{3}z\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4},

where we used
\|\nabla z\|_{L^{2}}\leq C_{0}
. Combining the last two estimates yields (
125
).
∎

Lemma 6.5
.

Let
N\in\{2,3\}
and
\alpha\neq 0
. For some fixed
t\in[0,T]
, assume that

\rho(\cdot,t)\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\qquad 0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty\quad\text{on }\mathbb{T}^{N},

and set

z:=\rho^{\alpha}.

Then there exists constants
C_{\rho,z},C>0
, depending only on

\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho},N
, such that

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\rho,z}\|\nabla\Delta z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx+C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{N}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx.

(126)

In particular:

(i)

If
N=3
and
\alpha\neq 2
, then there exists a constant
C_{\rho,z,3}>0
, depending only on

\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho}
, such that

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\rho,z,3}\|\nabla\Delta z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\rho,z,3}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(127)

(ii)

If
N=2
and

\sup_{0\leq s\leq T}\|\nabla z(s)\|_{L^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{2})}\leq C_{0},

then there exists constants
C_{\rho,z,2},C>0
, depending only on

\alpha,\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho},C_{0}
, such that

\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{\rho,z,2}\|\nabla\Delta z\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{2}z\|_{L^{2}}^{4}.

(128)

Proof.

Fix
t\in[0,T]
and omit the time dependence. Since
\alpha\neq 0
and
\underline{\rho}\leq\rho\leq\overline{\rho}
, smooth composition yields
z=\rho^{\alpha}\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
.

Starting from

\Delta z=\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\Delta\rho+\alpha(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2},

we differentiate once and solve for
\nabla\Delta\rho
:

\nabla\Delta\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha}\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla\Delta z-(\alpha-1)\rho^{-1}\nabla\rho\,\Delta\rho-(\alpha-1)\rho^{1-\alpha}\nabla\bigl(\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\bigr).

Since
\rho
is bounded away from
0
and
\infty
,

|\Delta\rho|\leq\sqrt{N}\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|,\qquad\bigl|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2})\bigr|\leq C\bigl(|\nabla\rho|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|+|\nabla\rho|^{3}\bigr),

we infer that

|\nabla\Delta\rho|\leq C\bigl(|\nabla\Delta z|+|\nabla\rho|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|+|\nabla\rho|^{3}\bigr).

Squaring and integrating, and using
\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}=\|\nabla\Delta\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}
, we obtain (
126
).

If
N=3
, then

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{1-\alpha}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Moreover, if
\alpha\neq 2
, Remark
6.1
gives

\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Substituting these two bounds into (
126
), we arrive at (
127
).

If
N=2
, Lemma
6.4
, together with
(
126
)
, yields
(
128
)
.

∎

The following lemma provides the dissipation estimates for the
L^{4}
estimate, which in turn characterizes the range of the exponent
\alpha
.

Lemma 6.6
.

Let
T\in(0,T^{*})
, let
N=3
, and let
(\rho,v)
be a strong solution to system (
13
)
on
\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T]
.
Assume that

0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty,\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{3}\times[0,T],

as well as

\alpha\in\left(\frac{9\sqrt{3}-4\sqrt{2}}{9\sqrt{3}-2\sqrt{2}},\,1\right).

Then there exists a constant

\varepsilon_{2}:=\alpha\left(1-\frac{243(1-\alpha)^{2}}{8(2-\alpha)^{2}}\right)>0,

(129)

such that

\frac{1}{4c}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}A(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla(\rho v)|^{2}\,dx,

(130)

where

A(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx.

Here
C>0
depends only on

\alpha,c^{-1},\underline{\rho}^{-1},\overline{\rho}.

Proof.

Throughout the proof,
C
denotes a constant depending only on the quantities listed in the statement.

Differentiating (
13
)
1
and testing the resulting equation by

c^{-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho
, we obtain, after integration by parts,

\frac{1}{4c}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+D(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla(\rho v)|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx,

where

D(t):=\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,\nabla^{2}\rho:\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha})\,dx+2\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|\,\nabla|\nabla\rho|\cdot\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha})\cdot\nabla\rho\,dx.

We next estimate
D(t)
from below. Writing
z=\rho^{\alpha}
, one has

\nabla^{2}z=\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla^{2}\rho+\alpha(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho.

On
\{|\nabla\rho|>0\}
, set

e:=\frac{\nabla\rho}{|\nabla\rho|},\qquad H:=\nabla^{2}\rho,\qquad s:=(1-\alpha)\frac{|\nabla\rho|^{2}}{\rho}.

Then
\nabla|\nabla\rho|=He
, and a direct computation gives

D(t)=\alpha\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\Bigl(|H|^{2}+2|He|^{2}-3s\,e^{\top}He\Bigr)\,dx.

Since
|He|^{2}\geq(e^{\top}He)^{2}
,

|H|^{2}+2|He|^{2}-3s\,e^{\top}He\geq|H|^{2}+2(e^{\top}He)^{2}-3s\,e^{\top}He\geq|H|^{2}-\frac{9}{8}\,s^{2}.

Hence

D(t)\geq\alpha A(t)-\frac{9\alpha(1-\alpha)^{2}}{8}\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}\rho^{\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{6}\,dx.

Invoking (
122
), we infer that

D(t)\geq\varepsilon_{2}A(t),

where
\varepsilon_{2}
is given by (
129
); in particular,
\varepsilon_{2}>0
by the assumption on
\alpha
. Therefore,

\frac{1}{4c}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\varepsilon_{2}A(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla(\rho v)|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx.

By Young’s inequality,

C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla(\rho v)|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|\,|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx\leq\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}A(t)+C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla(\rho v)|^{2}\,dx.

Therefore, by the previous two displayed inequalities,

\frac{1}{4c}\frac{d}{dt}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\frac{\varepsilon_{2}}{2}A(t)\leq C\int_{\mathbb{T}^{3}}|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla(\rho v)|^{2}\,dx,

which is exactly (
130
). This completes the proof.
∎

Based on these lemmas, we now give the first higher-order estimate as follows.

Proposition 6.1
.

Assume that (
87
) or (
91
) holds. Let

T\in(0,T^{*})
, and let
(\rho,v)
be a strong solution to system (
117
)

\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T]
, where

Set

When

Moreover, when

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left\|\nabla\rho(t)\right\|_{L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{3})}^{4}\leq C.

Proof.

We divide the proof into six steps.

Step 1. Preliminary bounds.

By Proposition
4.2
, Proposition
5.2
, Proposition

(134)

Hence, by Lemma

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|v(t)\|_{L^{q}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\leq C.

(135)

Further, the definition of
\check{q}_{N}
in (

(136)

By the basic energy inequality (
26

\nabla\bigl(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr)=\Bigl(\alpha-\frac{1}{2}\Bigr)\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\nabla\rho,\qquad\nabla(\rho^{\alpha})=\frac{\alpha}{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\rho^{\frac{1}{2}}\nabla\bigl(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}\bigr),

(137)

Similarly,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C.

In addition, (

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

(139)

We next show

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{2}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

(140)

Indeed, differentiating

\nabla\rho=\frac{1}{\alpha-1}\rho^{2-\alpha}\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})

once more gives

whence

\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-1})\|_{L^{4}}^{4}.

(141)

If

(

139
). If

and
(
32
)
. In both cases (
140
) follows from (
141
).

We also need

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

(142)

Since

Now (

) if

Step 2. A second-order estimate for the continuity equation.

Rewrite (

)

as

\rho_{t}+\div(\rho v)-c\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\Delta\rho-c\alpha(\alpha-1)\rho^{\alpha-2}|\nabla\rho|^{2}=0.

Testing by

\displaystyle\frac{c\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx+\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\Bigl|\int\nabla\div(\rho v)\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}\,dx\Bigr|

\displaystyle\quad+C\int\Bigl(|\nabla\rho||\nabla^{2}\rho|+|\nabla\rho|^{3}\Bigr)|\nabla\rho_{t}|\,dx

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{c\alpha}{2}\Bigl|\int(\rho^{\alpha-1})_{t}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx\Bigr|.

Hence, by Young’s inequality,

\displaystyle\frac{c\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\qquad\leq C\|\nabla^{2}(\rho v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\Bigl(|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{6}\Bigr)\,dx+\frac{c\alpha}{2}\Bigl|\int(\rho^{\alpha-1})_{t}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx\Bigr|.

(143)

Moreover, by (

)

\frac{c^{2}}{8}\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla{\rm div}(\rho v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(144)

\displaystyle=-\frac{c\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}{\rm div}(\rho v)|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx

Hence, by
(

\displaystyle|K_{1}|

\displaystyle\leq C\int|v|\,|\nabla\rho|\,|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx+C\int|v|\,|\Delta\rho|\,|\nabla\Delta\rho|\,dx

\displaystyle\leq C\int|v|\,|\nabla\rho|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}\,dx+C\int|v|\,|\nabla^{2}\rho|\,|\nabla^{3}\rho|\,dx

\displaystyle K_{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{c^{2}\alpha(\alpha-1)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle=-\frac{c^{2}\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-3}\nabla(\rho^{\alpha})\cdot\nabla\rho\,|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle|K_{2}|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{c^{2}}{32}\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\Bigl(|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{6}\Bigr)\,dx.

(146)

Substituting (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\frac{c\alpha}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\Delta\rho|^{2}\,dx+\frac{1}{4}\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{c^{2}}{16}\|\nabla\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\\
&\qquad\leq C\|\nabla^{2}(\rho v)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\int\Bigl(|v|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2}|\nabla^{2}\rho|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{6}\Bigr)\,dx\\

(147)

Step 3. A first-order energy estimate for the momentum equation.

Define

\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v):=\nu|\nabla v|^{2}+(\nu-c)\,\nabla v:(\nabla v)^{t}+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)({\rm div}v)^{2}.

(148)

Since

, we obtain

\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\geq(2\nu-c)(N\alpha-N+1)|\nabla v|^{2}

(149)

(150)

Multiplying (

)

by

and integrating by parts, we get

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha}\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx+\int\rho|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx

\displaystyle\qquad=-\int\rho\,u\cdot\nabla v\cdot v_{t}\,dx-\int\nabla P\cdot v_{t}\,dx+\frac{1}{2}\int(\rho^{\alpha})_{t}\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx.

(151)

Now

\displaystyle\left|\int\rho\,u\cdot\nabla v\cdot v_{t}\,dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\rho|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+C\int(|v|^{2}+|\nabla\rho|^{2})|\nabla v|^{2}\,dx,

(152)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{4}\int\rho|v_{t}|^{2}\,dx+C\int|\nabla\rho|^{2}\,dx.

(153)

Finally, we get from

\displaystyle\frac{1}{2}\int(\rho^{\alpha})_{t}\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle=\frac{c\alpha}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}\Delta(\rho^{\alpha})\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx-\frac{\alpha}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}{\rm div}(\rho v)\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)\,dx

\displaystyle=:L_{1}+L_{2}.

For

, we use the fact that

is quadratic in

|\nabla\mathcal{Q}_{+}(\nabla v)|\leq C\,|\nabla v|\,|\nabla^{2}v|.

We then integrate by parts so as to shift one derivative falling on

are treated in the same way.

We next estimate the coercive part on the left-hand side of (
187
). Since
a_{0}<0
and, for every matrix

, it follows that

\nu|M|^{2}+a_{0}(M^{t}:M)\geq(\nu+a_{0})|M|^{2}=\alpha c_{-}|M|^{2}.

Therefore, there exists
\varepsilon_{3}>0
depending only on
\nu,\alpha,c
and the upper bound of
\rho
such that

(188)

Now define

\Psi(t):=1+\|\nabla\rho(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

By (
185
) together with the basic energy estimate, we have

\int_{0}^{T}\Psi(t)\,dt\leq C.

(189)

Moreover, by Sobolev embeddings and Gagliardo–Nirenberg inequalities on
\mathbb{T}^{N}
(
N=2,3
),

\|f\|_{L^{6}}\leq C\|f\|_{H^{1}},\qquad\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2},

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}},\qquad\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2},

and, using also the basic energy estimate,

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Psi(t),\qquad\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\rho\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Psi(t).

Hence, using Young’s inequality, we obtain that for some sufficiently small
\eta>0
to be determined,

\displaystyle C\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|v_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t)\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|v_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\Psi(t)\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t)\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{6}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|v_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+C\|\rho_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle C\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{3}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Psi(t)\bigl(1+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr),

\displaystyle C\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Psi(t)\bigl(1+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr).

Combining these estimates with (
187
) and (
188
), and letting
\eta<\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{12}
, we infer that

\frac{d}{dt}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{2}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq 2\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t)\bigl(1+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr).

(190)

Step 2. Elliptic estimate for
\nabla^{3}v
.

Rewriting

as a constant-coefficient elliptic system, we have

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta v-a_{0}\nabla\operatorname{div}v=\rho^{1-\alpha}\Big\{

(191)

\displaystyle+\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\cdot\bigl[\nu\nabla v+(\nu-c)(\nabla v)^{t}+(\alpha-1)(2\nu-c)(\operatorname{div}v)\mathbb{I}\bigr]\Big\}.

Differentiating

\displaystyle\|\nabla F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Big(

\displaystyle\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}

Now, by the definition of
\Psi(t)
and (

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{4}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{4}}^{4}+\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Psi(t).

Moreover,

\|v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|v\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t),

\|\nabla v\|_{L^{4}}^{4}\leq\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t),

\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{\infty}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t),

\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}\leq C\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}\leq\eta\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t).

Therefore, choosing

sufficiently small, we deduce that

\|\nabla^{3}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C_{1}\|\nabla v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\Psi(t)\bigl(1+\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\bigr)

(192)

for some constant

.

(193)

Letting
\eta<\frac{\varepsilon_{3}}{16C_{1}}
and using
(

)
together with Gronwall’s inequality, we obtain

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|v_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\|\nabla v_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla^{3}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)\,dt\leq C.

(194)

Step 3. Uniform estimate of
\nabla^{2}v
.

Applying the
L^{2}
elliptic estimate to (
191
), we obtain

\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\|F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}.

(195)

Using the explicit form of

\displaystyle\|\nabla^{2}v\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\Big(\|v_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{6}}^{2}\|\nabla v\|_{L^{3}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\Big)

which implies

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\nabla^{2}v(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C.

(196)

Finally, combining (
185
), (

), and (

), we conclude that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|v(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\int_{0}^{T}\|v(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

Together with (

Proposition 6.3
.

Under the assumptions of Proposition
6.1
, there exists a constant
C>0
, depending only on the quantities appearing in Proposition
6.1
, such that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\right)+\int_{0}^{T}\left(\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\right)dt\leq C.

(197)

Proof.

Throughout the proof, all integrals are taken over
\mathbb{T}^{N}
, and
C
denotes a generic positive constant depending only on the quantities appearing in the statement of the proposition.

From Proposition
6.1
, Proposition
6.2
, and (

), we already know that

\displaystyle\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{2}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\Bigr)\leq C,

(198)

\displaystyle\int_{0}^{T}\Bigl(\|\nabla^{3}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|\nabla\rho_{t}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v_{t}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Bigr)\,dt\leq C,

and

0<\underline{\rho}\leq\rho(x,t)\leq\overline{\rho}<\infty,\qquad(x,t)\in\mathbb{T}^{N}\times[0,T].

(199)

In particular, since
\mathbb{T}^{N}
has finite measure and (
199
) holds,

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}\leq C,

so that

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C.

Since

, we also have the Sobolev embeddings

H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\qquad H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{6}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),\qquad H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})\hookrightarrow L^{\infty}(\mathbb{T}^{N}),

and the Gagliardo–Nirenberg and Poincaré’s inequalities

\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{2}\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2},\qquad\|\Delta\rho\|_{L^{3}}\leq C\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{1/2}.

(200)

We shall also use repeatedly that

is an algebra and that, for

,

\|fg\|_{H^{m}}\leq C\Bigl(\|f\|_{H^{m}}\|g\|_{H^{2}}+\|f\|_{H^{2}}\|g\|_{H^{m}}\Bigr).

(201)

Furthermore, if
F\in C^{\infty}([\underline{\rho},\overline{\rho}])

\|F(\rho(t))\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\qquad\text{for all }t\in[0,T].

(202)

Step 1. Estimate of

and

Taking the time derivative of

, we obtain

\displaystyle\rho_{tt}

\displaystyle-\alpha(\alpha-1)c\,\rho^{\alpha-2}\rho_{t}\Delta\rho-\alpha c\,\rho^{\alpha-1}\Delta\rho_{t}

(203)

\displaystyle-\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)c\,\rho^{\alpha-3}\rho_{t}|\nabla\rho|^{2}-2\alpha(\alpha-1)c\,\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}

\displaystyle=-\bigl(\operatorname{div}(\rho v)\bigr)_{t}.

Multiplying (

) by
\rho_{tt}
and integrating by parts, we find

\displaystyle\frac{\alpha c}{2}\frac{d}{dt}\int\rho^{\alpha-1}|\nabla\rho_{t}|^{2}\,dx+\|\rho_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

\displaystyle=\frac{\alpha(\alpha-1)c}{2}\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\rho_{t}|\nabla\rho_{t}|^{2}\,dx

(204)

\displaystyle+\alpha(\alpha-1)c\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\rho_{t}\Delta\rho\rho_{tt}dx

\displaystyle+\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)c\int\rho^{\alpha-3}\rho_{t}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\rho_{tt}\,dx

\displaystyle+\alpha(\alpha-1)c\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla\rho_{t}\,\rho_{tt}\,dx

\displaystyle-\int\bigl(\operatorname{div}(\rho v)\bigr)_{t}\,\rho_{tt}\,dx.

\displaystyle\leq\eta\|\nabla^{2}\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C_{\eta}\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2},

\displaystyle\left|\alpha(\alpha-1)c\int\rho^{\alpha-2}\rho_{t}\Delta\rho\rho_{tt}dx\right|

\displaystyle\leq C\|\rho_{t}\|_{L^{6}}\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{3}}\|\rho_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\|\rho_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C\|\rho_{t}\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\|\nabla^{2}\rho\|_{L^{2}}\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{8}\|\rho_{tt}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+C(\|\nabla^{3}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+1)(1+\|\nabla\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}),

\displaystyle\left|\alpha(\alpha-1)(\alpha-2)c\int\rho^{\alpha-3}\rho_{t}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\rho_{tt}\,dx\right|

and

\displaystyle\|\rho^{1-\alpha}\operatorname{div}(\rho v)\|_{H^{2}}

\displaystyle\leq C\|\operatorname{div}(\rho v)\|_{H^{2}}\leq C\|\rho v\|_{H^{3}}

\displaystyle\leq C\Bigl(\|\rho\|_{H^{3}}\|v\|_{H^{2}}+\|\rho\|_{H^{2}}\|v\|_{H^{3}}\Bigr)\leq C\bigl(1+\|v\|_{H^{3}}\bigr).

Substituting these bounds into (
219
), we infer that

\|\nabla^{4}\rho\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\leq C\Bigl(1+\|\nabla^{2}\rho_{t}\|_{L^{2}}^{2}+\|v\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\Bigr).

(220)

Integrating (
220
) over
(0,T)
and using (
213
) and (
184
), we obtain

\int_{0}^{T}\|\nabla^{4}\rho(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

(221)

Step 4. Conclusion.

By (
198
), (
214
), (
218
), (
199
), and (
221
), we have

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{3}}^{2}\leq C,\qquad\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{H^{1}}^{2}\leq C,

and

\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{4}}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{t}(t)\|_{H^{2}}^{2}\,dt+\int_{0}^{T}\|\rho_{tt}(t)\|_{L^{2}}^{2}\,dt\leq C.

This proves the proposition.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction, assuming that the maximal existence time
T^{*}
is finite, i.e.,
T^{*}<\infty
.

Step 1: A Priori Estimates and Uniform Boundedness.

The uniform estimates established in Propositions
6.1
-
6.3
ensure that the solution remains bounded within the periodic domain
\mathbb{T}^{N}
. Specifically, for any
T\in(0,T^{*})
, there exists a constant
C
depending on
\nu,c,T^{*},E_{0},\gamma,\alpha,N,\hat{q}_{N}
,
\left\|\rho_{0}^{1/(\hat{q}_{N}+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\hat{q}_{N}+2}},

M_{0}^{-1},\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\left\|\tau_{0}\right\|_{L^{\infty}},\check{q}_{N}
,
\left\|\rho_{0}^{1/(\check{q}_{N}+2)}v_{0}\right\|_{L^{\check{q}_{N}+2}}
,
\left\|\rho_{0}\right\|_{H^{3}},
and
\left\|v_{0}\right\|_{H^{2}}
such that:

\sup_{0\leq t\leq T}\left(\|\rho(t)\|_{H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}+\|v(t)\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})}\right)\leq C<\infty.

(222)

A critical feature of this bound is that
C
is independent of
T
as it approaches the limit
T^{*}
.

Step 2: Regularity and Temporal Continuity.

By considering the integrability of the temporal derivatives derived from the system, we find that:

\displaystyle\rho

\displaystyle\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{4}(\mathbb{T}^{N})),

\displaystyle v

\displaystyle\in H^{1}(0,T;H^{1}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))\cap L^{2}(0,T;H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N})).

These inclusions guarantee that the solution trajectories are continuous in time with values in the high-order Sobolev spaces
C([0,T];H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))
and
C([0,T];H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N}))
, respectively. Consequently, the state at the maximal time
T^{*}
is rigorously defined by the limit:

(\rho(T^{*}),v(T^{*})):=\lim_{t\to T^{*}}(\rho(t),v(t)).

(223)

Step 3: Extension of the Solution and Contradiction.

The limit
(\rho(T^{*}),v(T^{*}))
inherits the full regularity of the preceding states, ensuring
\rho(T^{*})\in H^{3}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
and
v(T^{*})\in H^{2}(\mathbb{T}^{N})
. Furthermore, since the estimate
\|\tau(t)\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq C
holds uniformly for
t<T^{*}
, the density remains strictly positive and bounded away from vacuum at
T^{*}
.

By utilizing
(\rho(T^{*}),v(T^{*}))
as new initial data, the local existence theory allows us to extend the solution to a larger interval
[0,T^{*}+\delta)
for some
\delta>0
. This provides a direct contradiction to the maximality of
T^{*}
. Consequently, we must have
T^{*}=\infty
.

The proof of uniqueness for the system is established through standard arguments and is therefore omitted.
∎

The lemmas in this section are intended to facilitate the transformation of system (

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
N=2
or
N=3
. For any
\alpha>\frac{N-1}{N}
, it holds that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\nabla\left(\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-2}\Delta\rho+\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}(\alpha-1)\rho^{2\alpha-3}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\right)-\div(\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\otimes\nabla\rho)\\
=\frac{2\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}{2\alpha-1}\rho\nabla(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\Delta\rho^{\alpha-\frac{1}{2}}).\end{split}

(224)

Proof.

A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{\text{LHS of }\eqref{A-1}}{\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}\\
&=(2\alpha-2)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\Delta\rho+\rho^{2\alpha-2}\nabla\Delta\rho+(\alpha-1)(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho\\
&\quad+2(\alpha-1)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho-(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho-\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\Delta\rho-\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho\\
&=(\alpha-2)(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho+(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho+\rho^{2\alpha-2}\nabla\Delta\rho\\
&\quad+(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\Delta\rho.\end{split}

(225)

On the other hand, a direct computation also yields

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\frac{\text{RHS of }\eqref{A-1}}{\varepsilon^{2}\alpha^{2}}\\
&=\rho\nabla\big(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\operatorname{div}(\rho^{\alpha-\frac{3}{2}}\nabla\rho)\big)\\
&=\Big(\alpha-\frac{3}{2}\Big)\rho\nabla\big(\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\big)+\rho\nabla\big(\rho^{2\alpha-3}\Delta\rho\big)\\
&=(\alpha-2)(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho+(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho+\rho^{2\alpha-2}\nabla\Delta\rho\\
&\quad+(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\Delta\rho.\end{split}

(226)

This completes the proof of Lemma
8.1
.

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
N=2
or
N=3
and
\alpha>\frac{N-1}{N}

(

)
, system (

)-(

Proof.

We first prove that
\eqref{Equ2'}_{1}
holds. From (

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho v_{\pm})-c_{\pm}\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\\
&=\rho_{t}+{\rm div}(\rho u)+c_{\pm}\alpha{\rm div}(\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla\rho)-c_{\pm}\Delta\rho^{\alpha}\\
&=0.\end{split}

Next, we prove that
\eqref{Equ2'}_{2}
holds. A direct computation shows that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\frac{D}{Dt}v_{\pm}=\rho\frac{D}{Dt}u+\rho\frac{D}{Dt}(c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho),\end{split}

(227)

where
\frac{D}{Dt}=\partial_{t}+u\cdot\nabla
. It follows from
\eqref{Equ1}_{2}
that

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\frac{D}{Dt}u+\nabla P&=\nu\rho^{\alpha}\Delta u+\nu\rho^{\alpha}\nabla{\rm div}u+\nu\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u+\nu\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\\
&\quad+2(\alpha-1)\nu\nabla\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}u+2(\alpha-1)\nu\rho^{\alpha}\nabla{\rm div}u+{\rm div}\mathbb{K}\\
&=\nu\rho^{\alpha}\Delta u+(2\alpha-1)\nu\rho^{\alpha}\nabla{\rm div}u+\nu\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\cdot\nabla u+\nu\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho^{\alpha}\\
&\quad+2(\alpha-1)\nu\nabla\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}u+{\rm div}\mathbb{K}.\end{split}

(228)

Using
\eqref{Equ1}_{1}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\begin{split}\rho\frac{D}{Dt}(c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho)&=c_{\pm}\alpha\rho\frac{D}{Dt}\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho+c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\frac{D}{Dt}\nabla\rho\\
&=c_{\pm}\alpha\rho\frac{D}{Dt}\rho^{\alpha-2}\nabla\rho+c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla\frac{D}{Dt}\rho-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho\\
&=-c_{\pm}\alpha(\alpha-2)\rho^{\alpha-1}{\rm div}u\nabla\rho-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla(\rho{\rm div}u)-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho\\
&=c_{\pm}(-\alpha^{2}+\alpha)\rho^{\alpha-1}{\rm div}u\nabla\rho-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha}\nabla{\rm div}u-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha-1}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho\\
&=c_{\pm}(1-\alpha)\nabla\rho^{\alpha}{\rm div}u-c_{\pm}\alpha\rho^{\alpha}\nabla{\rm div}u-c_{\pm}\nabla u\cdot\nabla\rho^{\alpha}.\end{split}

(229)

)

)

\displaystyle\begin{split}&\quad\rho\frac{D}{Dt}v_{\pm}+\nabla P\\

Therefore,

\displaystyle\begin{split}\frac{{\rm div}\mathbb{K}}{\varepsilon^{2}}&=\alpha^{2}(\alpha-2)(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-4}|\nabla\rho|^{2}\nabla\rho+\alpha^{2}(2\alpha-3)\rho^{2\alpha-3}\nabla\rho\cdot\nabla^{2}\rho\\
