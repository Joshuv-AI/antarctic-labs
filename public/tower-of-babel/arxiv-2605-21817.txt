A linear upper bound on the ℤ p \mathbb{Z}_{p} -Ramsey number of graphs with sufficiently large 2 2 -packing
Emily Heath, Andrew Simmons

Abstract
Given a positive integer k k and graph G G , the ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -Ramsey number R ⁡ ( G , ℤ k ) R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k}) is the least N N (if it exists) such that every coloring f : E ⁡ ( K N ) → ℤ k f:E(K_{N})\rightarrow\mathbb{Z}_{k} contains a copy G ′ G^{\prime} of G G such that ∑ e ∈ E ⁡ ( G ′ ) f ⁡ ( e ) = 0 \sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}f(e)=0 . Motivated by a question of Caro and Mifsud, we study the ℤ k \mathbb{Z}_{k} -Ramsey number of graphs with a sufficiently large 2-packing, i.e. a set of vertices S ⊆ V ⁡ ( G ) S\subseteq V(G) such that N ⁡ [ u ] ∩ N ⁡ [ v ] = ∅ N[u]\cap N[v]=\emptyset for all distinct u , v ∈ S u,v\in S . In particular, we prove that R ⁡ ( G , ℤ p ) ≤ n + 6 ​ p − 9 R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+6p-9 for all n n -vertex graphs G G and all primes p p such that p p divides e ⁡ ( G ) e(G) , the minimum degree of G G is at least 1 1 , and there exists a 2 2 -packing of G G with size p − 1 p-1 . Introduction

The Erdős-Ginzburg-Ziv theorem states that every sequence of
2k-1
integers contains a subsequence of
k
integers whose sum is congruent to
0
modulo
k
. Bialostocki and Dierker
2
initiated the study of a graph-theoretic analogue of the theorem, establishing some of the first results in zero-sum Ramsey theory. While classical Ramsey theory involves coloring combinatorial structures and investigating the appearance of monochromatic substructures, zero-sum Ramsey theory involves interchanging colors with elements from a group (often
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
) and instead focusing on zero-sum substructures.

More precisely, given a graph
G
and positive integer
k
, the
Ramsey number

R(G,k)
is the least
N
for which every
k
-coloring

f:E(K_{N})\to\left\{1,\ldots,k\right\}
contains a monochromatic copy of
G
; its existence is guaranteed by Ramsey’s Theorem
9
. If
\Gamma
is a finite abelian group, then the
zero-sum Ramsey number

R(G,\Gamma)
is the least
N
(if it exists) for which every
\Gamma
-coloring

f:E(K_{N})\to\Gamma
contains a
zero-sum copy
of
G
, i.e. a copy
G^{\prime}
of
G
such that
\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}f(e)=0_{\Gamma}
. The
exponent
of any finite group
\Gamma
, denoted
\exp(\Gamma)
, is the smallest positive integer
r
such that
rg=0
for all
g\in\Gamma
. Note that
R(G,\Gamma)
exists if and only if
\exp(\Gamma)
divides
e(G)
. If the divisibility condition holds, then monochromatic copies of
G
are zero-sum, and therefore
R(G,\Gamma)\leq R\left(G,\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert\right)
. If the divisibility condition does not hold, then for all positive integers
N
, there exists a constant
\Gamma
-coloring of
K_{N}
that fails to contain a zero-sum copy of
G
. In our work, we restrict our focus to the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-Ramsey number

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})
, which exists if and only if
\exp(\mathbb{Z}_{k})=k
divides
e(G)
.

Few exact values for the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-Ramsey number are known, even for small values of
k
. For example, the
\mathbb{Z}_{2}
-Ramsey numbers were completely determined in all cases by Caro
4
, but there are still cases that remain open for
k=3
. Recently, Alvarado, Colucci, and Parente
1
made significant progress by completely determining the
\mathbb{Z}_{3}
-Ramsey number of forests.

However, it is known that the zero-sum Ramsey number is linear for a large family of graphs. In particular, Katz, Lian, Malekshahian, and Shapiro
8
showed that a linear upper bound of the form
R(G,\Gamma)\leq Cn
holds for all
n
-vertex graphs of bounded maximum degree and finite abelian groups
\Gamma
such that
\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert
divides
e(G)
.

In many cases for the
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-Ramsey number, the upper bound can be improved to the form
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})\leq n+C
.
For example, Colucci and D’Emidio
6
proved that
R(F,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+9p-12
for all primes
p
and all
n
-vertex forests
F
with
p
dividing
e(F)
, minimum degree at least
1
, and
n\geq 3p^{2}-12p+11
. Shapiro
10
then generalized this result to
d
-degenerate graphs and gave a slight improvement in the case of forests (which are 1-degenerate graphs). A
d
-degenerate
graph is a graph in which every subgraph has at least one vertex of degree at most
d
. Shapiro
10
showed that
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+(3+3d)p
for all
d
-degenerate
n
-vertex graphs
G
and all primes
p
dividing
e(G)
such that
2d<p\leq e(G)/(2d(d+1)^{2})
.

We now discuss our main result. Caro and Mifsud
3
asked whether
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{k})\leq n+k-1
for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
containing at least
k-1
vertices of degree
1
with pairwise distances of at least
3
. They proved this result for the case
k=3
. We answer their question in the affirmative for all primes and in fact prove a much more general result for graphs containing a
2
-packing of size
k-1
.
A
2
-packing
of a graph
G
is a subset
S\subseteq V(G)
such that
N[u]\cap N[v]=\emptyset
for all distinct
u,v\in S
, where
N[u]
denotes the closed neighborhood
N(u)\cup\{u\}
.

Theorem 1
.

Let
p
be prime. Suppose
G
is an
n
-vertex graph such that
p
divides
e(G)
, the minimum degree of
G
is at least
1
, and there exists a
2
-packing
S
of
G
with
\left\lvert S\right\rvert=p-1
. Let

s_{0}=\left\lvert\left\{v\in S:\deg(v)\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)\right\}\right\rvert,

and

c=\begin{cases}0&\mbox{if $V(G)\neq\bigcup_{v\in S}N[v]$ or $\deg(v)=1$ for all $v\in S$,}\\
1&\mbox{otherwise.}\\
\end{cases}

Then

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq\begin{cases}n+p-1+c&\mbox{if $s_{0}=0$,}\\
n+5p-9+s_{0}+c&\mbox{if $s_{0}\neq 0$.}\\
\end{cases}

Moreover, equality holds in the case that
s_{0}=c=0
if there exists an integer
d\not\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
such that
\deg(v)\equiv d\ (\mathrm{mod}\ p)
for all
v\in V(G)
.

Note that in all cases of the hypothesis for Theorem
1
, we have
s_{0}+c\leq p
. Therefore the upper bound can be stated more simply as
R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+6p-9
for all
n
-vertex graphs
G
such that
p
divides
e(G)
, the minimum degree of
G
is at least
1
, and there exists a
2
-packing of
G
with size
p-1
.

Moreover, we obtain an improved linear upper bound for the
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-Ramsey number of graphs of bounded maximum degree when

is prime. Indeed, if
\Delta
is a positive integer, then any
n
-vertex graph

with maximum degree at most
\Delta
contains a
2
-packing of size
\left\lfloor\frac{n}{1+\Delta+\Delta(\Delta-1)}\right\rfloor=\left\lfloor\frac{n}{\Delta^{2}+1}\right\rfloor
. Therefore Theorem

applies for such

when
p
divides
e(G)
, the minimum degree of
G
is at least
1
, and
n\geq(p-1)(\Delta^{2}+1)
. Lastly, note the condition on minimum degree can now be dropped since
R(G\cup K_{1},\Gamma)\leq R(G,\Gamma)+1
for any graph

and finite abelian group
\Gamma
such that
\left\lvert\Gamma\right\rvert
divides
e(G)
.

Corollary 1
.

Suppose
p
is prime and
\Delta
is a positive integer. Then there exists a constant
C=C(p,\Delta)
for which

R(G,\mathbb{Z}_{p})\leq n+C

for all
n
-vertex graphs

such that
p

and the maximum degree of

. In particular, it suffices to choose

The family of graphs for which Theorem
1
applies has substantial overlap with yet another well known family of graphs. Indeed, the equality case in the theorem determines the exact

-Ramsey number of most cycles when

Corollary 2
.

If

such that

n
, then
R(C_{n},\mathbb{Z}_{p})=n+p-1
.

During completion of this manuscript, we learned that Chi and He

independently obtained this same result for

for all odd

. More generally, they proved that
R(C_{n},\mathbb{Z}_{k})\leq\max\{R(C_{2k},\mathbb{Z}_{k}),n+k-1\}
for all

. They also determined for wheel graphs

Preliminaries

We will use a generalized version of the Cauchy-Davenport theorem.

Theorem 2
.

Let
A_{1},\ldots,A_{l}
be nonempty subsets of
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
for
p
prime. Then

\left\lvert A_{1}+\cdots+A_{l}\right\rvert\geq\min\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lvert A_{i}\right\rvert-l+1,\ p\right\}.

In addition, we will use a weighted analogue of the Erdős–Ginzburg–Ziv theorem proved by Grynkiewicz
7
.

Theorem 3
.

Let
n,k\geq 2
be positive integers. Suppose
a_{1},\ldots,a_{n}
is a sequence of integers such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}a_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ k)
and suppose
b_{1},\ldots,b_{n+k-1}
is another sequence of integers. Then there exists a rearranged subsequence
b_{j_{1}},\ldots,b_{j_{n}}
such that
\sum_{i=1}^{n}b_{j_{i}}a_{i}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ k)
.

The following definition allows us to create a correspondence between special subgraphs of a
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-colored “host” graph and additive sets, so that we may use the Cauchy–Davenport theorem.

Definition 1
.

Let
p\geq 2
be prime. Let
H
be a complete graph and
f:E(H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. Call the pair
(S,T)
a
switch
if
S,T\subseteq V(H)
such that
\left\lvert S\right\rvert\geq 2
,
\left\lvert T\right\rvert\geq 1
,
S\cap T=\emptyset
, and
\left\lvert\left\{\sum_{t\in T}f(st):s\in S\right\}\right\rvert=\left\lvert S\right\rvert
. In other words, the stars with center in
S
and
T
as the set of leaves have distinct edge sums. We say that two switches
(S_{1},T_{1})
and
(S_{2},T_{2})
are
disjoint
if
S_{1}
,
S_{2}
,
T_{1}
, and
T_{2}
are pairwise disjoint.

Figure 1:
By Lemma
1
, finding a zero-sum copy of the graph on the left in a
\mathbb{Z}_{5}
-coloring reduces to finding a subgraph like the graph on the right. In every thick–dashed pair of stars, the stars must have distinct edge sums. The edge sum of the gray edges can be any value.

If we find an appropriate set of switches in a “host” graph, then we can find copies of the “target” graph with as many different edge sums as the number of elements in the sum set of the corresponding additive sets. In particular, if the corresponding sum set is the whole group
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
, then one of the copies is guaranteed to be zero-sum. See Figure
1
for an example. The following lemma makes this precise.

Lemma 1
.

Let
p
be prime. Suppose
G
is an
n
-vertex graph with a
2
-packing
S=\left\{v_{1},\ldots,v_{l}\right\}
. Let
H=K_{n+p-1}
and
f:E(H)\to\mathbb{Z}_{p}
. If
H
contains a family of pairwise disjoint switches
\left\{(S_{i},T_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{l}
such that
\left\lvert T_{i}\right\rvert=\deg(v_{i})
for all
i
and
\sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lvert S_{i}\right\rvert=p+l-1
, then
H
contains a zero-sum copy of
G
.

Proof.

Assume that
H
contains such a family of switches
\left\{(S_{i},T_{i})\right\}_{i=1}^{l}
. For each
i
, let
A_{i}=\left\{\sum_{t\in T_{i}}f(st):s\in S_{i}\right\}
. We describe how to embed a zero-sum copy of
G
. For each
i
, map
N(v_{i})
injectively into
T_{i}
, but otherwise arbitrarily. Embed
G\setminus\left(S\cup\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}N(v_{i})\right)
in
H\setminus\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}(S_{i}\cup T_{i})
arbitrarily; there are exactly enough vertices to do so. Let
c
denote the edge sum of the resulting copy of
G\setminus S
. We may complete an embedding of
G
by choosing for each
v_{i}\in S
an image in
S_{i}
. Note that
|A_{i}|=|S_{i}|
by definition of switches. Thus, Theorem
2
yields

\left\lvert A_{1}+\cdots+A_{l}\right\rvert\geq\min\left\{p,\ \sum_{i=1}^{l}\left\lvert S_{i}\right\rvert-l+1\right\}=p.

In particular, it is possible to choose images for the
v_{i}
so that the resulting copy of the bipartite graph with parts
S
and
\bigcup_{i=1}^{l}N(v_{i})
has edge sum
-c
. As a result, the total edge sum of the copy of
G
is
0
, as desired.
∎

Monochromatic copies of the “target” graphs that we consider (graphs with size divisible by the number of colors) are zero-sum. Hence, the next lemma will allow us to always assume that our
\mathbb{Z}_{p}
-colored “host” graphs contain small switches which are sufficient for embedding vertices of degree 1.

Lemma 2
.

Let

be an integer. Let

-vertex graph with

Note that we may assume

for some

, then we may replace

with another non-monochromatic path containing an edge of color

.
Since we must have either

It must be the case that at least one of

,

, or

is equal to

The next lemma allows us to compute edge sums easily for a specific family of

-colorings when

Lemma 3
.

Let
k\geq 3
be odd. Suppose
K
is a
\mathbb{Z}_{k}
-colored complete graph and there exists a partition of
V(K)
into sets
V_{1},\ldots,V_{m}
such that
\left\lvert V_{i}\right\rvert\geq 2
for each
i
, each clique

is monochromatic in some color

, and the edges between cliques

and

are colored with
\overline{2}^{-1}(c_{i}+c_{j})
. Then for any subgraph
G

\overline{2}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{i}}\deg_{G}(v)

Proof.

For any subgraph
G

K

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\left\lvert E(G)\cap K[V_{i}]\right\rvert+\sum_{i<j}\overline{2}^{-1}(c_{i}+c_{j})\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{i}}\left\lvert N_{G}(v)\cap V_{j}\right\rvert

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\left(\frac{1}{2}\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{i}}\left\lvert N_{G}(v)\cap V_{i}\right\rvert\right)+\sum_{i<j}\overline{2}^{-1}\left(c_{i}\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{i}}\left\lvert N_{G}(v)\cap V_{j}\right\rvert+c_{j}\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{j}}\left\lvert N_{G}(v)\cap V_{i}\right\rvert\right)

\displaystyle=\overline{2}^{-1}\sum_{i=1}^{m}c_{i}\sum_{v\in V(G)\cap V_{i}}\deg_{G}(v).

∎
