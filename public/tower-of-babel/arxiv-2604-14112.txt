Gravitational Sommerfeld Effects: Formalism, Renormalization, and Perturbation to 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 10 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{10})
Chih-Hao Chang, Chia-Hsien Shen, Zihan Zhou

Abstract
In the effective field theory (EFT) description of binary inspirals, the radiated gravitational waveform receives universal corrections from the curved background, the so-called “tail effects”, that resum into the so-called “Sommerfeld factor”. We develop a systematic framework for computing this gravitational Sommerfeld factor for scalar perturbations with the presence of tidal effects on the system. Using the worldline EFT, we recast the diagrammatic resummation as a solution to the d d -dimensional wave equation with a localized source, and derive a closed-form expression for the Sommerfeld factor in terms of the EFT connection matrix. We prove that the phase of the Sommerfeld factor is exactly the same as elastic Compton scattering phase shift when there is no tidal dissipation. By combining the renormalization techniques in EFT with the Mano–Suzuki–Takasugi method in black hole perturbation theory, we analytically solve the Sommerfeld factor for both the magnitude and phase to 𝒪 ⁡ ( G 10 ) \mathcal{O}(G^{10}) for the ℓ = 0 , 1 , 2 \ell=0,1,2 partial waves. We further establish a new renormalization group equation for the radiative multipole moments, whose exact solution yields an improved resummation of the waveform beyond the universal tail logarithms. These high-precision data and exact relations pave the way for future waveform resummation.

The waveform from a generic binary system contains a universal sector as a consequence of Birkhoff’s theorem: outside of any spherically symmetric source, the gravitational field is described by the Schwarzschild metric regardless of the source’s internal structure. As the wave leaves the binary, it propagates under the Schwarzschild background leading to the so-called “tail” effects

The rest of the waveform is non-universal which depends on system’s finite-size structures.
To study such effects, we need a formalism that incorporates tidal deformations to improve the resummation for the waveform.

The Wave Equation with a Source.
—
We will study the waveform of a scalar
\phi
emitted from a spinless compact system with mass
M
, which creates a
d
-dimensional Schwarzschild background

\displaystyle ds^{2}

\displaystyle=-fdt^{2}+f^{-1}dr^{2}+r^{2}d\Omega_{d-2}^{2},

(7)

where

\displaystyle f

\displaystyle=1-\frac{2GMn_{d}\mu^{4-d}}{r^{d-3}},\quad n_{d}\equiv\frac{4\pi^{\frac{3-d}{2}}\Gamma\left(\frac{d-1}{2}\right)}{d-2}\,.

(8)

We have used dimensional regularization to promote
d=4-2\epsilon
and introduced
\mu
as the renormalization scale.
The source for
\phi
is encoded by a series of multipole moments
Q^{L}(\tau)
determined by the short-distance dynamics of the compact system. The action reads

\displaystyle S_{\text{source}}=\sum_{\ell}\mu^{\frac{4-d}{2}}\int\differential{\tau}Q^{L}(\tau)\nabla_{L}\phi\,,

(9)

where we use
L
to denote a collection of
\ell
Lorentz indices. The derivative
\nabla_{L}
is a
\ell
-th order spatial derivative projected to the symmetric and trace-free (STF) part in the rest frame of the worldline.
The full action is then

\displaystyle S

\displaystyle=S_{\text{bulk}}+S_{\text{Love}}+S_{\text{source}},

(10)

where
S_{\text{bulk}}
describes
\phi
minimally coupled to gravity and
S_{\text{Love}}
introduces the tidal interaction on the worldline

\displaystyle S_{\text{Love}}=\sum_{n,\ell}\frac{\mu^{4-d}}{\ell!}C_{\ell,n}\int\differential{\tau}\nabla_{L}\phi_{+}\nabla^{L}\partial^{n}_{\tau}\phi_{-},

(11)

with
C_{\ell,n}
being the Love number.
To account for dissipative tidal effects described by odd in
n
terms in Eq. (
11
),
we use the in-in formalism to split the field as
\phi_{\pm}
and the equation of motion for the physical
\phi_{+}
is given by
\delta S/\delta\phi_{-}=0
. We will drop the
+
subscript from now on.
The wave equation is then a Klein-Gordon equation of
\phi
in the Schwarzschild spacetime with tidal interaction and sources on the worldline.
We render the complete action and the wave equation in the Supplemental Material.

Due to the rotational symmetry, we can decompose the scalar field as

\displaystyle\phi(t,{\bf\it r})=

\displaystyle\sum_{L}e^{-i\omega t}R_{L}(r)Y_{L}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})

(12)

where
Y_{L}
is the
d
-dimensional spherical harmonics in the STF basis.
Defining
R_{L}=u_{L}/r^{1-\epsilon}
, the wave equation becomes one dimensional

\displaystyle\left(\derivative[2]{r}+\omega^{2}\!-\!\frac{(\ell-\epsilon)(\ell-\epsilon+1)}{r^{2}}-\!V(r)\!\right)u_{L}=\rho_{L}\,,

(13)

where the potential
V(r)=V_{\rm grav}(r)+V_{\rm Love}(r)
is sum of the long-range
V_{\rm grav}(r)
and the short-range
V_{\rm Love}(r)
from tidal interaction that depends on the Love number through the tidal response function
F_{\ell}\equiv\sum_{n}C_{\ell,n}(i\omega)^{n}
.
The new ingredient compared to
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
is the source term

\displaystyle\rho_{L}=\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}(-1)^{\ell+1}\mu^{\epsilon}r^{1-\epsilon}Q^{L^{\prime}}(\omega)\!\int_{\Omega}Y^{*}_{L}\nabla_{L^{\prime}}\delta({\bf\it r})

(14)

where
Q^{L^{\prime}}(\omega)
is the multipole moment in the frequency space and
\int_{\Omega}
integrates over the
d-2
sphere. We will drop the argument of
Q^{L^{\prime}}
when there is no confusion.
We put the details on wave equation and spherical harmonics in the Supplemental Material.

Derivation of the Sommerfeld Factor
—
In the presence of the source, one can solve the waveform using Green’s function. But to recycle the known homogeneous solutions, we observe that the source is localized at
r=0
; therefore, the waveform are still given by the same homogeneous solutions. The only role of this source is to modify the boundary conditions at
r=0
. Both the Green’s function and the modified boundary conditions yield the same result. We only present the derivation from the latter and see more details in the Supplemental Material.

As explained in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
, we can span the waveform in terms of the two independent solutions,
u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}
and
u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
,

\displaystyle u_{L}=

\displaystyle B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}+B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}

(15)

\displaystyle\rightarrow

\displaystyle\mu^{-\epsilon}B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}\,r^{\ell+1-\epsilon}+\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{2\ell+1-2\epsilon}B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}\,r^{-\ell+\epsilon}+\dots\,,

where
B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}
and
B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}
are two unknown coefficients, and we show the near-zone (NZ) behavior near
r\rightarrow 0
in the second line
1
1

1

We choose a different
\mu
dependence from
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
but it does not affect the Sommerfeld factors and renormalization group equations.
.
A crucial insight from
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
is that the tidal potential
V_{\rm Love}(r)
provides a boundary condition on
B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}
and
B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}
. This is relatively simple to see for
\ell=0
since

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\frac{r^{\epsilon}}{r^{1-\epsilon}}=-\frac{4\pi^{\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{1}{2}-\epsilon\right)}\delta^{3-2\epsilon}({\bf\it r})\,,

(16)

such that the
\nabla^{2}
acting on
u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
yields a delta function that must be matched to the one in
V_{\rm Love}(r)
.
For our case, the source itself is also an
\ell
-th derivative on the delta function. Therefore, the presence of the source modifies the NZ boundary condition as

\displaystyle\frac{B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}}{c_{\ell}}-F_{\ell}B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}=Q_{L}\,,\,c_{\ell}=\frac{2^{\ell}\,\Gamma\left(\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon+\ell\right)}{2\pi^{\frac{3}{2}-\epsilon}}

(17)

whose detailed derivation is given in the Supplemental Material. The boundary condition in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
is then the source-free limit of Eq. (
17
).

Once we fix the NZ boundary condition, the waveform is then fixed by imposing another boundary condition in the far zone (FZ),
\omega r\gg 1
.
We choose the two independent solutions as the outgoing
u^{+}_{\ell}
and the incoming
u^{-}_{\ell}
, which satisfy large-
r
behavior

\displaystyle u^{\pm}_{\ell}(r)\xrightarrow{r\to\infty}(\mp i)^{\ell+1}e^{\pm i\omega\big(r+2GM\log(\bar\mu_{\rm IR} r)\big)},

(18)

where
\bar{\mu}_{\rm IR}
is the IR cutoff originating from the
1/r
potential.
The full waveform is

\displaystyle u_{L}=A^{+}_{L}u^{+}_{\ell}+A^{-}_{L}u^{-}_{\ell}\,.

(19)

For the waveform emission, we assume that there is no incoming wave, and thus we impose
A^{-}_{L}=0
.

To combine the boundary conditions in the NZ and FZ,
we note that the wave equation is of second order. The bases in far and near zones must be linearly related,
(u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell},u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell})=(u^{+}_{\ell},u^{-}_{\ell})\,{\bf\it W}
, where
{\bf\it W}
is the
2\times 2

connection matrix
that only depends the long-range
V_{\rm grav}
. Since
V_{\rm grav}
is real, the two solutions
(u^{+}_{\ell},u^{-}_{\ell})
must be related by complex conjugation, implying
W_{2i}=W_{1i}^{*}
.
By equating Eqs. (
15
) and (
19
), we find

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}A^{+}_{L}\\
A^{-}_{L}\end{pmatrix}={\bf\it W}\begin{pmatrix}B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}\\[3.0pt]
B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}\end{pmatrix}\,.

(20)

Solving
A^{+}_{L}
from Eqs. (
17
), (
20
), and
A^{-}_{L}=0
, we arrive at the main formula of our paper

\displaystyle\phi\xrightarrow{r\to\infty}

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell}\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\frac{ic_{\ell}\,\omega^{\ell}}{(2\ell+1)!!}\,\frac{e^{-i\omega t}u^{+}_{\ell}}{r^{1-\epsilon}}Q^{L}Y_{L}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})

\displaystyle\mathcal{S}_{\ell}=

\displaystyle\frac{(2\ell+1)!!}{2\omega^{\ell+1}(W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22})}

(21)

where
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
is the Sommerfeld factor in Eq. (
1
) that is normalized to unity in the free theory limit in
d=4
.
At this stage, the bare connection matrix
W_{ij}
may be UV divergent and therefore requires renormalization, which we will discuss later.

One can compare the Sommerfeld effect with the Compton scattering, in which there is an incoming wave but no source.
The corresponding
S
-matrix
\hat{S}_{\ell}\equiv A^{+}_{L}/A^{-}_{L}
is given by
Caron-Huot et al. 2025

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{\ell}=

\displaystyle\frac{A^{+}_{L}}{A^{-}_{L}}=\frac{W_{11}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{12}}{W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22}}

(22)

An important case is the conservative system where all tidal interactions have even numbers of time derivatives, which leads to a real
F_{\ell}
.
Combining this conservative condition with
W_{2i}=W_{1i}^{*}
, we find

\displaystyle\textrm{Arg}\big(\mathcal{S}_{\ell}\big)=\frac{1}{2}\,\textrm{Arg}\big(\hat{S}_{\ell}\big)\,.

(23)

This is an exact relation between the phase in the Sommerfeld factor and the phase shift in the conservative Compton scattering.
Through the EFT setup, this relation allows us to import the rich results in Compton scattering to the waveform emission
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
;
Bautista et al. 2026
;
Bjerrum-Bohr et al. 2026
;
Ivanov et al. 2026
.

Near Zone and Renormalization
—
Armed with the general formula for the Sommerfeld factor, the task is to compute the connection matrix. As laid out in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
, one can calculate this using the Born series. This is particularly straightforward in the NZ since the perturbative expansion of
u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}
and
u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
are polynomials in
r
which are simple to integrate in general
d
.

However, the point-particle limit we take in the EFT leads to ultraviolet (UV) divergence at
r=0
at
d=4
.
Our formula (
21
) is at the bare level. The physical waveform requires a renormalization procedure.
We start the renormalization as in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
by constructing the renormalized NZ coefficients as
(\bar{B}^{\textrm{reg}}_{L},\bar{B}^{\textrm{irr}}_{L})^{T}=Z(B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L},B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L})^{T}
. Demanding the renormalization-scale independence of the bare parameters then gives
(d/{d\log\mu}+{\bf\it\gamma})(\bar{B}_{\ell}^{\text{reg}},\bar{B}_{\ell}^{\text{irr}})^{T}=0
, where the anomalous dimension
\gamma
is reviewed in Eq. (
S78
).
Using the renormalized boundary condition (
17
), we find

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\log\mu}\bar{F}_{\ell}(\omega)

\displaystyle=-\frac{\gamma_{21}}{c_{\ell}}+(\gamma_{11}-\gamma_{22})\bar{F}_{\ell}(\omega)+c_{\ell}\gamma_{12}\bar{F}^{2}_{\ell}(\omega)

\displaystyle\frac{d}{d\log\mu}\bar{Q}_{L}(\omega)

\displaystyle=\Big(\gamma_{11}+\gamma_{12}c_{\ell}\bar{F}_{\ell}(\omega)\Big)\bar{Q}_{L}(\omega)\,,

(24)

where the second equation is new: it gives the full RG equation of linearized source with the full tidal-response dependence.
We will discuss the structures and analytic solutions to the RG equations later.

Exact Renormalization Group of Multipole Moments
—
The synergy between EFT and BHPT further illuminates the structures of the RG running.
Up to
\order{G^{2\ell+2}}

\gamma_{11}
in Eq. (

As

To cultivate the complete RG structure of

and

In addition, we can connect

We start from the the RG equation of the EFT NZ basis,

\left({d}/{d\log\mu}-{\bf\it\gamma}^{T}\right)(\bar{R}^{\text{reg}}_{\ell},\bar{R}^{\text{irr}}_{\ell})^{T}=0

We notice that the NZ solutions in BHPT take the form

\displaystyle R^{C}_{\nu}

\displaystyle=e^{(\nu-\ell)\log(2\omega r)}((2\omega r)^{\ell}+(2\omega r)^{-\ell-1}+\cdots)

(29)

\displaystyle R^{C}_{-\nu-1}

\displaystyle=e^{-(\nu-\ell)\log(2\omega r)}((2\omega r)^{\ell}+(2\omega r)^{-\ell-1}+\cdots)\,,

Resummation Proposal
— The exact solutions of the RG equations (
24

Ivanov et al. 2025
, the Sommerfeld factor
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}
coincides with the tail resummation factor

in the factorized MPM waveform. The RG running of the radiative multipoles efficiently resums the UV tail logs and captures the wave-amplitude correction due to scatterings against the curved background. Continuing from

Cipriani et al. 2026a

|\mathcal{S}_{\ell}|=|\mathcal{S}_{\rm IR}|\times|\mathcal{S}_{\rm run}|\times|\mathcal{S}_{\rm rem}|~,

(33)

where each factor isolates a distinct physical mechanism. The infrared factor

|\mathcal{S}|_{\rm IR}=\left|\frac{\Gamma(\nu(\omega)+1+2iGM\omega)\Gamma(2\ell+2)}{\Gamma(2\nu(\omega)+2)\Gamma(\ell+1)}\right|e^{\pi GM\omega}

captures the enhancement due to infrared loagirhtms. The running factor reads

|\mathcal{S}_{\rm run}|=\left|\frac{\bar{F}_{\ell,-}(\omega)-\bar{F}_{\ell,+}(\omega)}{\bar{F}_{\ell,-}(\omega)-\bar{F}_{\ell,0}(\omega)+\bigl(\bar{F}_{\ell,0}(\omega)-\bar{F}_{\ell,+}(\omega)\bigr)(\omega r_{\rm orb})^{2(\nu(\omega)-\ell)}}\right|(\omega r_{\rm orb})^{(\nu(\omega)-\ell)}~,

(35)

where

This factor improves
Ivanov et al. 2025

\nu(\omega)-\ell
to all orders in

Chang et al. 2026
.

Conclusion
—
We have developed a systematic framework for computing the gravitational Sommerfeld factor of scalar perturbation from a generic compact source including tidal effects.
We derived a closed-form expression for the Sommerfeld factor in Eq. (
21
).
We further streamlined the hybridization with BHPT, enabling the analytic computation of perturbative data to
\mathcal{O}(G^{10})
.
Along the way, we established the RG equation for radiative multipoles, including its mixing with tides, whose solution paves the way for improving future waveform models.

Our work opens several promising directions for future research. A key priority is the extension to spin-2 gravitational perturbations, which would enable a direct connection between the Sommerfeld factor and the gravitational waveform modes
h_{\ell m}
used in data analysis.
This generalization will require addressing important subtleties, including recoil effects on the worldline
Cheung et al. 2024a
;
Kosmopoulos and Solon 2024
;
Cheung et al. 2024b
and gauge dependence in spin-2 perturbations.
Beyond this, realistic binary systems carry orbital angular momentum, leading to mixing between different quantum numbers
\ell
.
Capturing these effects naturally calls for a treatment in a Kerr background.
Finally, a systematic investigation of different resummation proposals would be highly valuable.
Overall, our approach provides a natural starting point for developing a closer synergy between EFT and BHPT for precision gravitational-wave physics.

Details on the Equation of Motion

In this appendix, we provide the explicit form of the wave equation and the potentials appearing therein. We work in
d=4-2\epsilon
dimensions with the Schwarzschild metric (
7
) and use the in-in formalism in the Schwinger–Keldysh basis. The complete action is
S=S_{\text{Bulk}}+S_{\text{Love}}+S_{\text{Source}}
, where

\displaystyle S_{\text{Bulk}}

\displaystyle=-\int\differential[d]{x}\sqrt{-g}\,(\partial\phi_{-})(\partial\phi_{+})\,,

(S1)

\displaystyle S_{\text{Love}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell,n}\frac{\mu^{2\epsilon}}{\ell!}C_{\ell,n}\int_{\gamma}\differential{\tau}\,\nabla_{L}\phi_{+}\nabla^{L}\partial^{n}_{\tau}\phi_{-}\,,

\displaystyle S_{\text{Source}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\ell}\mu^{\epsilon}\int_{\gamma}\differential{\tau}\,Q^{L}(\tau)\nabla_{L}\phi_{-}\,.

The equation of motion for
\phi\equiv\phi_{+}
follows from
\delta S/\delta\phi_{-}=0
:

\displaystyle\bigg(\Box_{\text{Sch}}+\mu^{2\epsilon}\sum_{n,\ell}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+n}}{\ell!}C_{\ell,n}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\nabla^{L}\partial_{t}^{n}\bigg)\phi(x)=\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L}(t)\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\,,

(S2)

where
\Box_{\text{Sch}}
is the scalar d’Alembertian in the Schwarzschild background,

\displaystyle\Box_{\text{Sch}}=-\frac{1}{f}\partial_{t}^{2}+\frac{1}{r^{d-2}}\partial_{r}\big(r^{d-2}f\,\partial_{r}\big)+\frac{\Delta_{S^{d-2}}}{r^{2}}\,~,

(S3)

where

\displaystyle f(r)=1-\frac{2GM\mu^{2\epsilon}n_{d}}{r^{d-3}}\quad,\quad\text{with}\quad n_{d}=\frac{4\pi^{\frac{3-d}{2}}\Gamma(\frac{d-1}{2})}{d-2}~,

(S4)

and
\Delta_{S^{d-2}}
is the Laplacian on the unit
(d-2)
-sphere. The source term on the right-hand side encodes the multipole moments of the compact object, while the tidal potential on the left-hand side captures the response of the compact object to external perturbations.

Radial equation.

To derive Eq. (
13
) in the main text, we first decompose
\phi
into spherical harmonics:

\phi(r,\Omega)=e^{-i\omega t}\sum_{\ell,m}R_{\ell m}(r)Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)\equiv e^{-i\omega t}\sum_{\ell,m}\frac{u_{\ell m}(r)}{r^{(d-2)/2}}Y_{\ell m}(\Omega)\,.

(S5)

Then, the equation of motion reduces to a radial equation for
u_{\ell m}(r)

\displaystyle r^{-1+\epsilon}f(r)u_{\ell m}^{\prime\prime}(r)+r^{-1+\epsilon}f^{\prime}(r)u_{\ell m}^{\prime}(r)+r^{-1+\epsilon}\frac{\omega^{2}}{f(r)}u_{\ell m}(r)+r^{\epsilon-3}\Big(r(\epsilon-1)f^{\prime}(r)-(\epsilon-1)\epsilon f(r)-\ell(\ell-2\epsilon+1)\Big)u_{\ell m}(r)

(S6)

\displaystyle=V_{\rm Love}(r)R_{\ell m}(r)+\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L}(\omega)\int_{\Omega}Y_{\ell m}^{*}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\,,

where the tidal potential
V_{\rm Love}(r)
is given by

V_{\rm Love}(r)=-\sum_{\ell,n}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}C_{\ell,n}\mu^{2\epsilon}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\nabla^{L}(i\omega)^{n}\equiv-\mu^{2\epsilon}\sum_{\ell}F_{\ell}(\omega)\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}(\mathbf{r})\nabla_{L}~,

(S7)

with the tidal response function
F_{\ell}(\omega)=\sum_{n}C_{\ell,n}(i\omega)^{n}
. To clean up second-derivative term in the equation, we divide both sides by
r^{-1+\epsilon}f(r)
and find

\displaystyle u_{\ell m}^{\prime\prime}(r)+\frac{f^{\prime}(r)}{f(r)}u_{\ell m}^{\prime}(r)+\frac{\omega^{2}}{f(r)^{2}}u_{\ell m}(r)+\frac{1}{r^{2}f(r)}\Big(r(\epsilon-1)f^{\prime}(r)-(\epsilon-1)\epsilon f(r)-\ell(\ell-2\epsilon+1)\Big)u_{\ell m}(r)

(S8)

\displaystyle=r^{1-\epsilon}\frac{V_{\rm Love}(r)}{f(r)}R_{\ell m}(r)+r^{1-\epsilon}\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{f(r)}\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L}(\omega)\int_{\Omega}Y_{\ell m}^{*}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\,.

Since the tidal response function and the source term are localized at the origin, we can further simplify the equation by replacing

with
1
on the right-hand side. This leads to the final form of the radial equation:

\displaystyle\left[\frac{d^{2}}{dr^{2}}-\frac{(\ell-\epsilon)(\ell-\epsilon+1)}{r^{2}}+\omega^{2}\right]u_{\ell m}(r)=V_{\rm Grav}(r)u_{\ell m}(r)+r^{1-\epsilon}\Big(V_{\rm Love}(r)R_{\ell m}(r)+\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L}(\omega)\int_{\Omega}Y_{\ell m}^{*}\nabla_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\Big)\,.

(S9)

with the effective gravitational potential defined as
Caron-Huot et al. 2025

\displaystyle V_{\text{Grav }}(r)=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\left(\frac{2GMn_{d}\mu^{2\epsilon}}{r^{1-2\epsilon}}\right)^{n}\left[\frac{2\epsilon-1}{r}\frac{d}{dr}+\frac{\ell^{2}+\ell+1-\epsilon(3+2\ell)+2\epsilon^{2}}{r^{2}}-(n+1)\omega^{2}\right]~.

(S10)

Once transformed into the STF basis, we recover Eq. (

II
Spherical Harmonic Basis and STF Basis

In the previous appendix, the radial equation was written in the spherical harmonic basis. However, the localized tidal Love term and the multipole source are more naturally expressed in terms of STF tensors. In this appendix, we therefore collect the identities that convert between the spherical harmonic basis and the STF basis, and then show the derivative formulas needed for deriving the near-zone boundary condition.

We begin by defining the scalar spherical harmonics
Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})
on the unit sphere
S^{d-2}
as an orthonormal basis of eigenfunctions of the spherical Laplacian,

\displaystyle\Delta_{S^{d-2}}Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})=-\ell(\ell+d-3)\,Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}}),

(S11)

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})Y^{(d)}_{\ell^{\prime}{\bf\it m}^{\prime}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})=\delta_{\ell\ell^{\prime}}\delta_{{\bf\it m}{\bf\it m}^{\prime}}.

(S12)

For fixed
\ell
, the label
{\bf\it m}
runs over a basis of the degenerate eigenspace. A convenient choice is the basis adapted to the multiplicity-free branching chain

\displaystyle SO(d-1)\supset SO(d-2)\supset\cdots\supset SO(3)\supset SO(2),

(S13)

with

{\rm Res}_{SO(N)}^{SO(N-1)}V_{\ell_{n}}=\bigoplus_{\ell_{n-1}=0}^{\ell_{n}}V_{\ell_{n-1}}~,

(S14)

where
V_{\ell_{n}}
is the irreducible representation for
SO(N)
and
V_{\ell_{n-1}}
is the irreducible representation for
SO(N-1)
. Therefore, it is convenient to introduce a set of quantum numbers

\displaystyle{\bf\it m}\equiv(\ell_{d-3},\ell_{d-4},\dots,\ell_{2},m)

(S15)

to efficiently capture the multiplicity-free branching chain structure with

\displaystyle\ell\geq\ell_{d-3}\geq\ell_{d-4}\geq\cdots\geq\ell_{2}\geq|m|,

(S16)

where
\ell_{j}\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
for
j=2,\dots,d-3
and
m\in\mathbb{Z}
. In
d=4
, this reduces to the familiar
SO(3)\supset SO(2)
labeling by a single magnetic quantum number
m=-\ell,-\ell+1,\dots,\ell
. We only need one representation-theoretic fact here: this eigenspace furnishes the rank-
\ell
symmetric trace-free irrep of
SO(d-1)
. Equivalently, it is in one-to-one correspondence with rank-
\ell
STF tensors
T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell}}
, which has the properties,

\displaystyle T_{i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell}}=T_{(i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell})},\qquad\delta^{i_{1}i_{2}}T_{i_{1}i_{2}i_{3}\cdots i_{\ell}}=0.

(S17)

For convenience, we denote indices collectively by the notation
L\equiv i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell}
. The degeneracy of scalar harmonics with angular momentum
\ell
is therefore the dimension of this irreducible representation,

\displaystyle\Tr 1_{\ell}=\frac{(d+2\ell-3)\Gamma(d+\ell-3)}{\Gamma(d-2)\Gamma(\ell+1)}\,,

(S18)

where
1_{\ell}
denotes the identity operator on the
\ell
-th irreducible representation.

Thus the spherical harmonic basis and the STF basis are simply two different choices of basis for the same irreducible representation, with
\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}
denoting the STF product of
\ell
unit vectors.
The scalar spherical harmonics form a complete orthonormal basis on
S^{d-2}
, so

\displaystyle\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{{\bf\it m}}Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})\,Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})=\delta^{(d-2)}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}),

(S19)

where
\delta^{(d-2)}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})
is the delta function on the unit sphere, normalized by

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Omega'_{d-2}}\,\delta^{(d-2)}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})\,f(\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})=f(\hat{{\bf\it r}}).

(S20)

If instead one keeps
\ell
fixed, then the sum over
{\bf\it m}
gives the projector onto the
\ell
-th harmonic subspace,

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})\equiv\sum_{{\bf\it m}}Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})\,Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}),

(S21)

which is a smooth rotationally invariant function of
\hat{{\bf\it r}}\cdot\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}
rather than a delta function. To derive its explicit form, note that for fixed
\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}
the function
K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})
lies in the
\ell
-th harmonic subspace as a function of
\hat{{\bf\it r}}
. Moreover,

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(R\hat{{\bf\it r}},R\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})=K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})\qquad\forall R\in SO(d-1),

(S22)

so it can only depend on the invariant

\displaystyle y\equiv\hat{{\bf\it r}}\cdot\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}.

(S23)

Defining
K_{\ell}(y)\equiv K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})
and using

\displaystyle\Delta_{S^{d-2}}K_{\ell}(y)=(1-y^{2})K_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(y)-(d-1)xK_{\ell}^{\prime}(y),

(S24)

the harmonic eigenvalue equation becomes

\displaystyle(1-x^{2})K_{\ell}^{\prime\prime}(x)-(d-1)xK_{\ell}^{\prime}(x)+\ell(\ell+d-3)K_{\ell}(x)=0.

(S25)

This is the Gegenbauer differential equation with
\lambda=(d-3)/2
, so regularity on the sphere implies

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})=A_{\ell}\,C_{\ell}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(x).

(S26)

The coefficient
A_{\ell}
is fixed by setting
\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}=\hat{{\bf\it r}}
and integrating over the sphere:

\displaystyle S_{d-2}\,A_{\ell}\,C_{\ell}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}(1)=\sum_{{\bf\it m}}\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\big|Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})\big|^{2}=\Tr 1_{\ell}.

(S27)

Using
C_{\ell}^{\lambda}(1)=\Gamma(\ell+2\lambda)/[\Gamma(2\lambda)\Gamma(\ell+1)]
together with the expression for
\Tr 1_{\ell}
, one finds

\displaystyle A_{\ell}=\frac{2\ell+d-3}{(d-3)S_{d-2}}.

(S28)

Therefore the addition theorem reads

\displaystyle K_{\ell}(\hat{{\bf\it r}},\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime})=\frac{2\ell+d-3}{(d-3)S_{d-2}}\,C_{\ell}^{\frac{d-3}{2}}\!\left(\hat{{\bf\it r}}\cdot\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}\right),

(S29)

where
C_{\ell}^{\lambda}(x)
is the Gegenbauer polynomial. Setting
\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}=\hat{{\bf\it r}}
and using

\displaystyle C_{\ell}^{\lambda}(1)=\frac{\Gamma(\ell+2\lambda)}{\Gamma(2\lambda)\Gamma(\ell+1)},

(S30)

we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf\it m}}Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})\,Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(\hat{{\bf\it r}})=\frac{\Tr 1_{\ell}}{S_{d-2}}.

(S31)

We now review the explicit map between the spherical harmonic in the

basis and the STF basis. The spherical harmonics in the STF basis used in the main text are simply

\displaystyle Y_{L}\equiv\hat{r}_{L}\,.

(S32)

We start from the decomposition of the spherical harmonics,

\displaystyle Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}=\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}\hat{r}_{L}

(S33)

where
\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}
is a constant rank-
\ell
STF tensor. Here
\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}
denotes the identity operator on the rank-
\ell

\displaystyle\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}\,T_{L^{\prime}}=T^{L},\qquad\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}=\delta^{\langle i_{1}}_{\langle j_{1}}\cdots\delta^{\,i_{\ell}\rangle}_{j_{\ell}\rangle},

(S34)

where the brackets denote STF projection on the
i
- and
j
-indices separately. Writing
\lambda=(d-3)/2
, one may also express this projector explicitly as

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{STF}}^{i_{1}\cdots i_{\ell},\;j_{1}\cdots j_{\ell}}=\sum_{k=0}^{\lfloor\ell/2\rfloor}\frac{(-1)^{k}\,\ell!\,\Gamma(\ell-k+\lambda)}{4^{k}\,k!\,(\ell-2k)!\,\Gamma(\ell+\lambda)}\,\mathcal{S}_{i}\mathcal{S}_{j}\Big[\delta^{i_{1}i_{2}}\cdots\delta^{i_{2k-1}i_{2k}}\delta^{j_{1}j_{2}}\cdots\delta^{j_{2k-1}j_{2k}}\delta^{i_{2k+1}}_{j_{2k+1}}\cdots\delta^{i_{\ell}}_{j_{\ell}}\Big],

(S35)

where
\mathcal{S}_{i}
and
\mathcal{S}_{j}
denote symmetrization with unit weight over the
i
- and
j
-indices separately, with all terms absent when their upper index is smaller than the lower one. For example, for
\ell=2
this reduces to

\displaystyle\delta_{\text{STF}}^{ij,kl}=\frac{1}{2}\left(\delta^{ik}\delta^{jl}+\delta^{il}\delta^{jk}\right)-\frac{1}{d-1}\delta^{ij}\delta^{kl}.

(S36)

The bilinear form

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\hat{r}_{L}\hat{r}_{L^{\prime}}

(S37)

is
SO(d-1)
invariant on the irreducible rank-
\ell
STF representation, so Schur’s lemma implies

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\hat{r}_{L}\hat{r}_{L^{\prime}}=A_{\ell}\,\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}

(S38)

for some constant
A_{\ell}
. Contracting with
\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}
gives

\displaystyle A_{\ell}\,\Tr 1_{\ell}=\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\hat{r}_{L}\hat{r}_{L^{\prime}}=\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\hat{r}^{L}\hat{r}_{L}=S_{d-2}\,\hat{r}^{L}\hat{r}_{L}.

(S39)

Using

\displaystyle\hat{r}^{L}\hat{r}_{L}=\frac{\sqrt{\pi}\,\Gamma(d+\ell-3)}{2^{d+\ell-4}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d}{2}-1\right)\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d-3}{2}+\ell\right)},

(S40)

together with the expression for
\Tr 1_{\ell}
, one finds

\displaystyle A_{\ell}=\frac{\ell!}{c_{\ell}}\qquad\text{with}\qquad c_{\ell}=\frac{2^{\ell}}{2\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}\Gamma\!\left(\frac{d-1}{2}+\ell\right).

(S41)

Therefore the STF basis obeys

\displaystyle\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,\hat{r}_{L}\hat{r}_{L^{\prime}}=\frac{\ell!}{c_{\ell}}\,\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}.

(S42)

Multiplying (
S33
) by
Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}
, integrating over the sphere, and using the previous identity gives

\displaystyle\delta_{{\bf\it m}{\bf\it m}^{\prime}}=\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\,Y^{*(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}^{\prime}}=\frac{\ell!}{c_{\ell}}\,\mathcal{Y}^{*L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}^{\prime}},

(S43)

which is the orthonormality relation for the coefficients
\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}
. Since
\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}
is the change-of-basis matrix between two bases of the same irreducible representation, the inverse relation is

\displaystyle\sum_{{\bf\it m}}\mathcal{Y}^{*L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}\mathcal{Y}^{L^{\prime}}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}=\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}\,\delta^{LL^{\prime}}_{\text{STF}}.

(S44)

Using this in (
S33
), we obtain the inverse map

\displaystyle\hat{r}_{L}=\frac{\ell!}{c_{\ell}}\sum_{{\bf\it m}}\mathcal{Y}^{*L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}

(S45)

Basis change for components.

With these relations, any function may be expanded in either basis,

\displaystyle f({\bf\it r})=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\sum_{{\bf\it m}}f_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(r)Y^{(d)}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}f_{L}(r)\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{L}.

(S46)

Using the orthogonality relations, we have

\displaystyle f_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(r)=\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}Y^{(d)*}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}f({\bf\it r})\quad,\quad f_{L}(r)=\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}\int\differential{\Omega_{d-2}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}f({\bf\it r}).

(S47)

The transition relation between the two bases is therefore

\displaystyle f_{L}(r)=\sum_{\mathbf{m}}f_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(r)\mathcal{Y}^{L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}\quad,\quad f_{\ell{\bf\it m}}(r)=\frac{\ell!}{c_{\ell}}f_{L}(r)\mathcal{Y}^{*L}_{\ell{\bf\it m}}.

(S48)

Derivative identities.

We now show the derivative identities that are used in the derivation of the localized tidal Love and source terms in the earlier sections. We first derive

\displaystyle\partial_{L}\frac{1}{r^{d-3}}=(-2)^{\ell}\left(\frac{d-3}{2}\right)_{\ell}\frac{\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}}{r^{d+\ell-3}}

(S49)

where
(z)_{n}=\Gamma(z+n)/\Gamma(z)
is the Pochhammer symbol. This formula is useful for the derivation of the boundary condition (
17
). To prove it, we use
Blanchet and Damour 1986

\displaystyle\partial_{L}f(r)=\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}r^{\ell}\left(\frac{1}{r}\derivative{r}\right)^{\ell}f(r).

(S50)

Setting
f(r)=r^{3-d}
yields (
S49
). Moreover,

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\frac{1}{r^{d-3}}=-(d-3)S_{d-2}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})=-\frac{4\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-3}{2}\right)}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r}).

(S51)

Combining this with (
S49
), we conclude

\displaystyle\nabla^{2}\frac{\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}}{r^{d+\ell-3}}=-\frac{1}{(-2)^{\ell}\left(\frac{d-3}{2}\right)_{\ell}}\frac{4\pi^{\frac{d-1}{2}}}{\Gamma\left(\frac{d-3}{2}\right)}\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})

(S52)

which is the identity used in the derivation of the near-zone boundary condition.

III
Determinant of the Connection Matrix

In this appendix, we are going to prove that the determinant of the connection matrix defined in (
20
) is:

\displaystyle\det{\bf\it W}=\frac{1}{2i\omega}

(S53)

The connection matrix can be written as the usual Wronskian, defined as

\displaystyle W[f,g](r)=f(r)g^{\prime}(r)-f^{\prime}(r)g(r)\,.

(S54)

Suppose both
f,g
in the Wronskian
W[f,g]
satisfy the differential equation,
y^{\prime\prime}+q(r)y^{\prime}+p(r)y=0
, then the Wronskian as a function of
r
satisfies the Abel’s identity

\displaystyle W^{\prime}[f,g](r)=-p(r)W[f,g](r)\,.

(S55)

Through out the paper, we use bold font for the connection matrix and its matrix element as
W_{ij}
to distinguish them from the Wronskian. We also drop the argument
r
of the Wronkskian unless it is fixed to a particular value.
Using this definition and Eq. (
20
), we find

\displaystyle W_{11}=\frac{W[u^{-},u^{\text{reg}}]}{W[u^{-},u^{+}]}\quad,\quad W_{12}=\frac{W[u^{-},u^{\text{irr}}]}{W[u^{-},u^{+}]}\quad,\quad W_{21}=\frac{W[u^{+},u^{\text{reg}}]}{W[u^{+},u^{-}]}\quad,\quad W_{22}=\frac{W[u^{+},u^{\text{irr}}]}{W[u^{+},u^{-}]}

(S56)

By direct calculation, we can see that

\displaystyle\det{\bf\it W}=\frac{W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}]}{W[u^{-},u^{+}]}\,.

(S57)

Let us first show that the ratio of two Wronskians is a constant in
r
. Considering two Wronskians,
W_{a}
and
W_{b}
, whose arguments satisfying the same ODE,
y^{\prime\prime}+q(r)y^{\prime}+p(r)y=0
, we can check that

\displaystyle\derivative{r}\frac{W_{a}}{W_{b}}=\frac{W^{\prime}_{a}W_{b}-W_{a}W^{\prime}_{b}}{W_{b}^{2}}=0

(S58)

by Abel’s identity. Since the ratio is a constant, we can evaluate it at
r=\infty
for the sake of convenience. From the (
18
), we find
W[u^{-},u^{+}](\infty)=2i\omega
. In addition, we also have

\displaystyle W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](\infty)=

\displaystyle W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](0)\times\exp( \int_0^\infty\dd{r'}\sum_{n=1}^\infty\left( \frac{2GM\mu^{2\epsilon}(2\epsilon-1)}{r'^{1-2\epsilon}} \right)^n ),

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](0)\,,

(S59)

where we use that the exponential is unity under dimensional regularization. We can then evaluate the Wronskian from the near zone

\begin{split}W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](\infty)=&W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](0)\\
=&W\left[\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{2\ell-2\epsilon+1}r^{-\ell+\epsilon},\mu^{-\epsilon}r^{\ell+1-\epsilon}\right]\bigg|_{r=0}+\text{gravitational corrections}\\
=&1+\text{gravitational corrections}\end{split}

(S60)

However, by inspecting the form of
V_{\rm grav}
, we find that the gravitational correction always contains
r^{b\epsilon}
, with some positive integers
b
, which is set to
0
at
r=0
by dimensional regularization.
Thus, we find
W[u^{\text{irr}},u^{\text{reg}}](\infty)=1
and
W[u^{-},u^{+}](\infty)=2i\omega
. Using Eq. (
S57
), we complete the proof of Eq. (
S53
).

IV
Derivation of the Sommerfeld Factor from Boundary Conditions

In this appendix, we complement the detailed derivation of the waveform and the Sommerfeld factor from the boundary conditions, including the proof of Eq. (
17
) and the Green’s function method.

Boundary Condition Method.

We first derive the boundary condition (
17
). Afterward, we show how to solve for the waveform
A^{+}_{L}
explicitly. As mentioned in the main text, the boundary condition originates from the presence of the Dirac delta function. Consequently, it suffices to focus on this localized contribution. Moreover, gravitational effects enter universally in the form
1/r^{1-2\epsilon}
, which vanishes as we set
r=0
due to dimensional regularization. Therefore, in deriving the boundary condition, we are able to ignore gravitational potential and consider the following equation instead

\displaystyle\left(\nabla_{\text{Flat}}^{2}+\mu^{2\epsilon}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}F_{\ell}(\omega)\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\partial_{L}\right)\phi({\bf\it r})=\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L}\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r}),

(S61)

which is obtained from (
S2
) by setting
G=0
and performing a Fourier transform. We also drop the
\omega^{2}
term since it doesn’t affect the boundary condition. We start with the first term and show that the Laplacian acting on
u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
produces a Dirac delta function. As explained above, since gravitational effects do not influence the boundary condition, we can also set
G=0
in the wave function. Let
u^{\text{0,reg}}_{\ell}=u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}|_{G=0}
and
u^{\text{0,irr}}_{\ell}=u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}|_{G=0}
. Then, we have

\begin{split}\nabla_{\text{Flat}}^{2}\phi({\bf\it r})&=\nabla_{\text{Flat}}^{2}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\left(B_{L}^{\text{reg}}u^{\text{0,reg}}_{\ell}(r)+B_{L}^{\text{irr}}u^{\text{0,irr}}_{\ell}(r)\right)\frac{\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}}{r^{(d-2)/2}}\\
&=B_{L}^{\text{irr}}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\nabla_{\text{Flat}}^{2}\left(\frac{\mu^{\epsilon}}{2\ell-2\epsilon+1}\frac{\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L}}{r^{\ell+1-2\epsilon}}\right)=\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell+1}}{c_{\ell}}\mu^{\epsilon}B^{\text{irr}}_{L}\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\,,\end{split}

(S62)

where we have used Eq. (
S52
). In the second equality, we drop
u^{\text{0,reg}}_{\ell}
since it satisfies the Laplacian equation and does not contain any singularity. Next, we compute the Dirac delta function produced by the tidal potential

\begin{split}&\mu^{2\epsilon}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}F_{\ell}(\omega)\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\partial_{L}\phi({\bf\it r})\\
=&\mu^{2\epsilon}\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-1)^{\ell}}{\ell!}F_{\ell}(\omega)\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\partial_{L}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}=0}^{\infty}\left(B_{L^{\prime}}^{\text{reg}}u^{\text{0,reg}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(r)+B_{L^{\prime}}^{\text{irr}}u^{\text{0,irr}}_{\ell^{\prime}}(r)\right)\frac{\hat{{\bf\it r}}_{L^{\prime}}}{r^{(d-2)/2}}\\
=&\sum_{\ell=0}^{\infty}(-1)^{\ell}F_{\ell}(\omega)\mu^{\epsilon}B^{\text{reg}}_{L}\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})\,,\end{split}

(S63)

where
u^{\text{0,irr}}_{\ell}
doesn’t contribute since it is evaluated at
r=0
due to Dirac delta function and vanishes thanks to dimensional regularization. Finally, combining first, second and the sources terms and requiring
\partial_{L}\delta^{d-1}({\bf\it r})
to be canceled, we then finish the proof on the boundary condition (
17
).

We can solve
B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}
and
B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}
from the near-zone and far-zone boundary conditions

\begin{cases}&c_{\ell}^{-1}B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}-F_{\ell}(\omega)B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}=Q_{L}\\[3.0pt]
&W_{21}B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}+W_{22}B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}=A^{-}_{L}=0\,.\end{cases}

(S64)

Furthermore, since
A^{+}_{L}
can be related to
B^{\textrm{reg}}_{L}
and
B^{\textrm{irr}}_{L}
through the connection matrix, this leads to

\displaystyle A^{+}_{L}=-\det{\bf\it W}\frac{c_{\ell}Q_{L}}{W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}(\omega)W_{22}}=\frac{i}{2\omega}\,\frac{c_{\ell}Q_{L}}{W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}(\omega)W_{22}}

(S65)

where we have used
\det{\bf\it W}=1/(2i\omega)
. Combining the Eqs.(
12
), (
18
), and (
19
), we complete the derivation of Eq. (
21
).

Green’s function method.

We first derive the Green’s function and the associated boundary conditions, and then calculate the waveform. The Green’s function is defined by the following equation

\displaystyle\left(\derivative[2]{r}+\omega^{2}-\frac{(\ell-\epsilon)(\ell-\epsilon+1)}{r^{2}}-V(r)\right)G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})=\delta(r-r^{\prime})\,.

(S66)

We can construct the Green’s function from two homogeneous solutions
u^{>}_{\ell}
and
u^{<}_{\ell}
of the differential equations as

\displaystyle G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})=\frac{u^{<}_{\ell}(r_{<})u^{>}_{\ell}(r_{>})}{W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})}\,,

(S67)

where we define
r_{>}=\max\{r,r^{\prime}\}
and
r_{<}=\min\{r,r^{\prime}\}
.
We can impose the boundary conditions on
G_{\ell}
by demanding the correct boundary behaviors of
u^{>}_{\ell}
and
u^{<}_{\ell}
.
Since we only consider the emission process,
u^{>}_{\ell}
should only be outgoing for large
r
. Consequently, we should pick
u^{>}_{\ell}=u^{+}_{\ell}
in Eq. (
19
).
On the other hand,
u^{<}_{\ell}
should obey the boundary condition that encodes the correct boundary behavior at
r=0
.
Since
u^{<}_{\ell}
satisfies the homogeneous differential equation, we should use the source-free near-zone boundary condition
B^{\text{irr}}_{\ell}/B^{\text{reg}}_{\ell}=c_{\ell}F_{\ell}
from Eq. (
17
) when we write
u^{<}_{\ell}
in the near-zone basis,
u^{<}_{\ell}=B^{\text{reg}}_{\ell}u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}+B^{\text{irr}}_{\ell}u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
.
For convenience, we can choose to normalize
u^{<}_{\ell}
such that
u^{<}_{\ell}=A^{+}_{\ell}u^{+}_{\ell}+u^{-}_{\ell}
when it is written in the far-zone basis.
Using the connection matrix and the source-free near-zone boundary condition, we can fix
u^{<}_{\ell}
entirely. In summary, our Green’s function in Eq. (
S67
) is built from

\displaystyle u^{>}_{\ell}(r)

\displaystyle=u^{+}_{\ell}(r)

(S68)

\displaystyle u^{<}_{\ell}(r)

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{W_{11}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{12}}{W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22}}\right)u^{+}_{\ell}(r)+u^{-}_{\ell}(r)=\frac{1}{W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22}}\left(u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}(r)+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}(r)\right)\,.

To calculate the Wronskian
W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})
, we can use the above decomposition and the Abel’s identity and
V_{\rm grav}
to show that

\displaystyle W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=W[u^{-}_{\ell},u^{+}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})

\displaystyle=W[u^{-}_{\ell},u^{+}_{\ell}](\infty)\,\exp( \sum_{n=1}^\infty\left( 2GMn_d \mu^{2\epsilon} \right)^n (1-2\epsilon) \int_{r'}^\infty\frac{\dd{x}}{x^{1+n(1-2\epsilon)}} ).

(S69)

where in the first equality, we plug in Eq. (
S68
) and use the antisymmetry of the Wronskian.
The Green’s function simplifies drastically since our source is localized at the origin, such that we only need to evaluate the Wronskian at
r^{\prime}=0
.
In this case, all the integrals in the exponent of above equation become scaleless, so we have

\displaystyle W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](0)=

\displaystyle W[u^{-}_{\ell},u^{+}_{\ell}](\infty)=2i\omega

(S70)

where the final equality follows from the asymptotic behavior (
19
). Using Eqs. (
S68
) and (
S70
), we have the explit form of the Green’s function in Eq. (
S67
).

After obtaining the Green’s function, the waveform follows straightforwardly from

\displaystyle R_{L}(r)=\frac{1}{r^{1-\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{r'}G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})\rho_{L}(r^{\prime})=\frac{1}{r^{1-\epsilon}}\int_{0}^{\infty}\differential{r'}G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})\left[\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}(-1)^{\ell+1}\mu^{\epsilon}r^{\prime 1-\epsilon}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}Q^{L^{\prime}}\int_{\Omega^{\prime}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}_{L}\partial_{L^{\prime}}\delta({\bf\it r}^{\prime})\right]\,,

(S71)

where we use the explicit form of
\rho_{L}
in Eq. (
14
). We can combine the radial and angular integration into a bulk integration

\displaystyle R_{L}(r)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{1-\epsilon}}\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}(-1)^{\ell+1}Q^{L^{\prime}}\int\differential[d-1]{\vb*{r}'}\frac{G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})}{r^{\prime 1-\epsilon}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}_{L}\partial_{L^{\prime}}\delta({\bf\it r}^{\prime})

(S72)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{r^{1-\epsilon}}\frac{c_{\ell}}{\ell!}\mu^{\epsilon}\sum_{\ell^{\prime}}(-1)^{\ell+\ell^{\prime}+1}Q^{L^{\prime}}\int\differential[d-1]{\vb*{r}'}\delta({\bf\it r}^{\prime})\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left[\frac{G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})}{r^{\prime 1-\epsilon}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}_{L}\right]\,,

in which we perform the integration by part at the second line. To use the delta function, we have to evaluate the integrand at the origin
r^{\prime}=0
. Using Eqs. (
S68
) and (
S70
), the integrand becomes

\displaystyle\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left[\frac{G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})}{r^{\prime 1-\epsilon}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}_{L}\right]=

\displaystyle u^{+}_{\ell}(r)\,\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left[\frac{u^{<}_{\ell}(r^{\prime})}{W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})}\frac{{\bf\it r}^{\prime}_{L}}{r^{\prime\ell+1-\epsilon}}\right]

(S73)

To find the
r^{\prime}=0
limit of the integrand, we need to identify the part that contains no integer power of
r^{\prime\epsilon}
. First, the inverse Wronskian
1/W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](0)=1/2i\omega
starts as a constant at the origin. At finite
r^{\prime}
, we can see from Eq. (
S69
) that the inverse Wronskian is corrected perturbatively by powers of
\left(GM/r^{\prime 1-2\epsilon}\right)^{n}
. This correction only adds more positive powers of
r^{\prime\epsilon}
which vanish at
r^{\prime}=0
.
Second, we can expand
u^{<}_{\ell}
in Eq. (
S68
) in the near-zone basis. The function
u^{\textrm{irr}}_{\ell}
always contains positive powers of
r^{\epsilon}
even when the gravitational correction is included. On the other hand, the free part of
u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}
, shown in Eq. (
15
), is the only term with
r^{-\epsilon}
to cancel the
r^{\prime\ell+1-\epsilon}
in the denominator in Eq. (
S73
).
This implies that we should only take
1/W[u^{<}_{\ell},u^{>}_{\ell}](r^{\prime})=1/2i\omega
and project
u^{<}_{\ell}
to the
u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}
sector for the
r^{\prime}=0
limit which leads to

\displaystyle\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left[\frac{G_{\ell}(r,r^{\prime})}{r^{\prime 1-\epsilon}}\hat{{\bf\it r}}^{\prime}_{L}\right]\bigg|_{r^{\prime}=0}=

\displaystyle\frac{u^{+}_{\ell}(r)}{2i\omega(W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22})}\,\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left[\frac{u^{\textrm{reg}}_{\ell}}{r^{\prime\ell+1-\epsilon}}{\bf\it r}^{\prime}_{L}\right]\bigg|_{r^{\prime}=0}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{u^{+}_{\ell}(r)\mu^{-\epsilon}\ell!}{2i\omega(W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}W_{22})}\,\delta_{LL^{\prime}}

(S74)

where we use Eqs (
15
) and (
S68
), and
\partial_{L^{\prime}}\left({\bf\it r}^{\prime}_{L}\right)=\ell!\delta_{LL^{\prime}}
in the second line.
Plugging Eq. (
S73
) to Eq. (
S74
), we find

\displaystyle R_{L}(r)=\frac{ic_{\ell}}{2\omega(W_{21}+c_{\ell}F_{\ell}(\omega)W_{22})}\frac{u^{+}_{\ell}(r)}{r^{1-\epsilon}}Q_{L}\,.

(S75)

Combining the above with Eqs. (
12
) and (
18
), we prove Eq. (
21
) using the Green’s function method.

V
EFT Near-Zone Data

In the appendix, we discuss the renormalization of the near zone wavefunction. Once the near zone wavefunction has the UV divergence, we would need to introduce the contact term to cancel the divergence the get the renormalized wavefunction. We mainly follow the procedure in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
by writing the bare boundary condition
B_{\rm reg},B_{\rm irr}
as a linear combination of the renormalized boundary condition
\bar{B}_{\rm reg},\bar{B}_{\rm irr}

\begin{pmatrix}B_{\rm reg}\\
B_{\rm irr}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}Z_{11}&Z_{12}\\
Z_{21}&Z_{22}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{B}_{\rm reg}\\
\bar{B}_{\rm irr}\end{pmatrix}~.

(S76)

From the RG equation

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\begin{pmatrix}\mu^{-\epsilon}B_{\rm reg}\\
\mu^{\epsilon}B_{\rm irr}\end{pmatrix}=0~,

(S77)

we get that

\mu\frac{d}{d\mu}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{B}_{\rm reg}\\
\bar{B}_{\rm irr}\end{pmatrix}=-\begin{pmatrix}\gamma_{11}^{(\ell)}&\gamma_{12}^{(\ell)}\\
\gamma_{21}^{(\ell)}&\gamma_{22}^{(\ell)}\end{pmatrix}\begin{pmatrix}\bar{B}_{\rm reg}\\
\bar{B}_{\rm irr}\end{pmatrix}~,\quad\gamma^{(\ell)}=Z^{-1}\frac{d}{d\log\mu}Z+Z^{-1}\begin{pmatrix}-\epsilon&0\\
0&\epsilon\end{pmatrix}Z~.

(S78)

In the situation where there is no source, we have

F_{\ell}=c_{\ell}^{-1}\frac{B_{\rm irr}}{B_{\rm reg}}=\frac{c_{\ell}^{-1}Z_{21}+Z_{22}\bar{F}_{\ell}}{Z_{11}+Z_{12}c_{\ell}\bar{F}_{\ell}}~,\quad\bar{F}_{\ell}\equiv\frac{\bar{B}_{\rm irr}}{\bar{B}_{\rm reg}}~.

(S79)

In this paper, we choose the physical minimal subtraction scheme on
F_{\ell}
where
c_{\ell}^{-1}Z_{21},Z_{22},Z_{11},Z_{12}c_{\ell}
only contain singular in
\epsilon
terms, i.e.
1/\epsilon,1/\epsilon^{2}\cdots
. It worths noting that this scheme is different than the
B_{\rm reg},B_{\rm irr}
minimal subtraction scheme used in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
where they do not keep track
c_{\ell}
. Also, we choose to write everything in
G
instead of
\bar{G}\equiv GMn_{d}
as in
Caron-Huot et al. 2025
. As an example, for
\ell=1
case until
\mathcal{O}(G^{7})

In this appendix, we discuss the details of the renormalization group equations of tides and radiative multiples. We will first derive the equation and then give the solution and interpretations.

First of all, we note that the near zone wavefunction can be written as

Resummation

In this appendix, we give a imposed resummation proposal based on the new RG equation and the perturbative Sommerfeld data. Let us first focus on the amplitude part. We define

\displaystyle x\equiv GM\omega,\qquad z_{1}\equiv\frac{2\omega}{\bar{\mu}}e^{-\gamma_{E}}=e^{L/2-\gamma_{E}}~,\quad z_{2}\equiv\frac{2\omega}{\bar{\mu}}~.

(S134)

Our proposal separates the amplitude into three parts:

|\mathcal{S}|=|\mathcal{S}|_{\rm IR}\times|\mathcal{S}|_{\rm run}\times|\mathcal{S}|_{\rm rem},

(S135)

where the first part captures the IR resummation, the second part captures the RG running, and the last part captures the remaining corrections which can be order by order computed by comparing with the perturbative data. The

(\omega r_{s})^{-i\omega r_{s}}

S84
