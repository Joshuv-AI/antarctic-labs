Wave breaking for perturbed Burgers equations
Ethan Botelho, Khai T. Nguyen, Madhumita Roy

Abstract
We establish a simple and explicit criterion for wave breaking for a general class of perturbed Burgers equations that cover several Burgers-type models, including the Fractional KdV equation, the Whitham equation, and the Fornberg-Whitham equation. The proof is both rigorous and straightforward. Keywords. Whitham equation, fractional KdV equation, Fornberg-Whitham equation, perturbed Burgers equations, wave breaking AMS Mathematics Subject Classification. 76B15, 76B03, 35S30, 35A20

Introduction

We study a class of perturbed Burgers-type equations of the form

u_{t}(t,x)+\left(\frac{u^{2}(t,x)}{2}\right)_{x}={\bf N}[u(t,\cdot)](x),\qquad u(0,\cdot)=\bar{u},

(1.1)

where the source term
{\bf N}:H^{1}(\mathbb{R})\to{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})
is a bounded linear operator satisfying:

(A1)

For every
g\in\mathcal{C}^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
,

{\bf N}[g]^{\prime}(x)={\bf N}[g^{\prime}](x),\qquad\int_{\mathbb{R}}{\bf N}[g](x)g(x)\,dx=0.

(A2)

There exist constants
\eta_{0}\in(0,\infty]
,
\alpha_{i}>0
, and
\lambda_{i}\geq 0
for
i=1,2,3
such that

\|{\bf N}[g]\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\leq\lambda_{1}\eta^{-\alpha_{1}}\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{2}\eta^{\alpha_{2}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+\lambda_{3}\eta^{\alpha_{3}}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}

for all
\eta\in(0,\eta_{0})
and
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{R})
.

These assumptions cover a broad family of operators, ranging from singular to weakly singular kernels. With suitable choices of

While the classical Burgers equation forms steep gradients in finite time, the presence of a nonlocal source may slow down, speed up, or prevent this process.

Definition 1.1

The Cauchy problem (
1.1
) exhibits
wave breaking
(or
shock formation
) at time
T^{*}>0
if it admits a unique classical solution
u
on
[0,T^{*})\times\mathbb{R}
such that

\limsup_{t\to T^{*-}}\left(\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{x}(t,x)\right)~=~-\infty.

In this paper, our aim is to provide simple, verifiable conditions under which the nonlocal term
{\bf N}
allows solutions to form shocks.
In Theorem
2.1
, for every sufficiently small parameter
\theta>0

\bar{u}
and the
{\bf L}^{2}
-norms of its derivatives. These criteria yield bounds on the lifespan of classical solutions and identify regimes in which nonlinear steepening dominates the nonlocal effects. More precisely, we obtain that the wave breaking time
T^{*}
is bounded by

\frac{1}{(1+\theta)}\cdot\frac{1}{-\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{u}^{\prime}(x)}\;\leq\;T^{*}\;\leq\;\frac{1}{(1-\theta)}\cdot\frac{1}{-\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}\bar{u}^{\prime}(x)}.

The proof of Theorem
2.1

m(t)~\doteq~-\displaystyle\inf_{x\in\mathbb{R}}u_{x}(t,x).

By tracking

This system captures the mechanism of gradient amplification and allows us to determine an interval in which wave breaking occurs, linking it to the initial slope and to the influence of the nonlocal term.

In the remaining part of the paper, we apply our main result, Theorem

(1,\infty)

Lemma 3.2

s>0

N
:
H^{2}(\mathbb{R})\to H^{1}(\mathbb{R})
defined by

{\bf N}[g](x)~\doteq~(G_{s}*g^{\prime})(x)~=~\int_{\mathbb{R}}G_{s}(y)g^{\prime}(x-y)dy

satisfies the assumption
(A1)
. We verify the assumption
(A2)
in four cases:

1.
If
s\in(2/5,1)
, then following the same estimate as in (
3.9
) together with (
3.15
), for every
\eta>0
and
x>0
, we estimate

\begin{split}|{\bf N}[g](x)|&~\leq~\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta}+\int_{|y|>\eta}\right)G_{s}(y)g^{\prime}(x-y)dy~\leq~\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{|y|\leq\eta}|G_{s}(y)|dy\\[5.69054pt]
&\qquad\qquad+\left|G_{s}(\eta)g(x-\eta)-G_{s}(-\eta)g(x+\eta)\right|+\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\int_{|y|\geq\eta}|\partial_{y}G_{s}(y)|dy\\[5.69054pt]
&~\leq~\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta}|G_{s}(y)|dy+2\eta|G_{s}(\eta)|\right)+2\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot|G_{s}(\eta)|\\[5.69054pt]
&~\leq~{2^{1-s}(1+s)\gamma(s)\over s}\cdot\eta^{s}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+2^{1-s}\gamma(s)\cdot\eta^{s-1}\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}.\end{split}

In particular,
{\bf N}
satisfies assumption
(A2)
with
\lambda_{2}=0
and parameters

\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{3}\right)~=~2^{1-s}\left(\gamma(s),{(1+s)\gamma(s)\over s}\right),\qquad(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{3})~=~(1-s,s)\qquad\mathrm{and}\qquad\eta_{0}~=+\infty.

(3.21)

Since
s\in(2/5,1)
, it holds

0~<~\alpha_{1}~=~1-s~<~\displaystyle{3s\over 2}~=~\displaystyle{3\alpha_{3}\over 2},

and the hypotheses of Theorem
2.1
are satisfied. Hence, applying Theorem
2.1
with

\begin{cases}\theta_{0}~=~\displaystyle{3\alpha_{3}-2\alpha_{1}\over 5\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{3}}~=~\frac{5s-2}{5-2s},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{3}~=~{2\alpha_{1}\over 5\alpha_{1}+3\alpha_{3}}~=~\frac{2-2s}{5-2s},\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle C_{2}~=~0,\qquad C_{3}~=~3^{1-\bar{\alpha}_{3}}\lambda_{1}^{{2-5\bar{\alpha}_{3}\over 2}}\big(C_{GN}\lambda_{3}\big)^{{}^{{3\bar{\alpha}_{3}\over 2}}}~=~\left[3C_{GN}^{1-s}\left(\frac{2+2s}{s}\right)^{1-s}\gamma(s)\right]^{\frac{3}{5-2s}},\end{cases}

and recalling (
3.16
), we achieve the wave breaking criterion as in (
3.17
).

2.
If
s\in(2/3,1)
then for every
\eta>0
and
x>0
, we estimate

\begin{split}|{\bf N}[g](x)|&~\leq~\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta}+\int_{|y|>\eta}\right)G_{s}(y)g^{\prime}(x-y)dy~\leq~\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta}|G_{s}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}\\[5.69054pt]
&\qquad+\left|G_{s}(\eta)g(x-\eta)\right|+\left|G_{s}(-\eta)g(x+\eta)\right|+\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot\int_{|y|\geq\eta}|\partial_{y}G_{s}(y)|dy\\[5.69054pt]
&~\leq~{2^{-s+(1/2)}\gamma(s)\over\sqrt{2s-1}}\cdot\eta^{s-(1/2)}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}+2^{2-s}\gamma(s)\cdot\eta^{s-1}\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})},\end{split}

which implies that
{\bf N}
satisfies assumption
(A2)
with
\lambda_{3}=0
and parameters

\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)~=~\left(2^{2-s}\gamma(s),{2^{-s+(1/2)}\gamma(s)\over\sqrt{2s-1}}\right),\quad(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})~=~(1-s,s-(1/2))\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\eta_{0}~=+\infty.

(3.22)

Since
s\in(2/3,1)
, it holds

0~<~\alpha_{1}~=~1-s~<~2s-1~=~2\alpha_{2},

and the hypothesis of Theorem
2.1
is fulfilled in this case. Hence, applying Theorem
2.1
for

\begin{cases}\theta_{0}~=~\displaystyle{2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}\over 4\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}}~=~{3s-2\over 3-2s},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{2}~=~{\alpha_{1}\over 2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}~=~{2-2s\over 3-2s},\\[11.38109pt]
C_{2}~=~3^{1-\bar{\alpha}_{2}}\lambda^{1-2\bar{\alpha}_{2}}_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\bar{\alpha}_{2}}~=~\displaystyle 3^{\frac{1}{3-2s}}\,2^{\frac{2s-1}{3-2s}}\,\gamma(s)^{\frac{1}{3-2s}}\,(2s-1)^{-\frac{1-s}{3-2s}},\qquad C_{3}~=~0,\end{cases}

and recalling (
3.16
), we achieve the wave breaking criterion as in (
3.18
).

3.
If
s=1
, then with the same estimate as in Case 2, for every
\eta\in(0,1]
we have

\big\|{\bf N}[g]\big\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~4\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}|G_{1}(\eta)|+\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\left(\int_{|y|\leq\eta}|G_{1}(y)|^{2}dy\right)^{1/2}.

By the second bound in (
3.15
) and
\displaystyle G_{1}(1)<\frac{1}{\pi}
, we directly compute that

\begin{split}\big\|{\bf N}[g]\big\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}&~\leq~{4(1+|\ln\eta|)\over\pi}\cdot\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+{4(1+|\ln\eta|)\over\pi}\eta^{1/2}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})}\\[5.69054pt]
\end{split}

In particular, for every fixed
\tau\in(2/3,1)
, using the fact that

1+|\ln\eta|~\leq~{\eta^{\tau-1}\over 1-\tau},\qquad\eta\in(0,1],

we derive

\big\|{\bf N}[g]\big\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~{4\over\pi(1-\tau)}\eta^{\tau-1}\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}+{4\over\pi(1-\tau)}\eta^{(\tau-1/2)}\|g^{\prime}\|_{{\bf L}^{2}(\mathbb{R})},\qquad\eta\in(0,1].

Hence,
{\bf N}
satisfies assumption
(A2)
with
\lambda_{3}=0
and parameters

\left(\lambda_{1},\lambda_{2}\right)~=~\left({4\over\pi(1-\tau)},{4\over\pi(1-\tau)}\right),\quad(\alpha_{1},\alpha_{2})~=~(1-\tau,\tau-1/2)\quad\mathrm{and}\quad\eta_{0}~=1.

(3.23)

Since
\tau\in(2/3,1)
, it holds

0~<~\alpha_{1}~=~1-\tau~<~2\tau-1~=~2\alpha_{2},

and the hypothesis of Theorem
2.1
is fulfilled. Hence, applying Theorem
2.1
for

\begin{cases}\displaystyle\theta_{0}~=~\displaystyle{2\alpha_{2}-\alpha_{1}\over 4\alpha_{1}+2\alpha_{2}}~=~{3\tau-2\over 3-2\tau},\qquad\bar{\alpha}_{2}~=~{\alpha_{1}\over 2\alpha_{1}+\alpha_{2}}~=~{2-2\tau\over 3-2\tau},\qquad\eta_{0}~=~1,\\[11.38109pt]
\displaystyle C_{2}~=~3^{1-\bar{\alpha}_{2}}\lambda^{1-2\bar{\alpha}_{2}}_{1}\lambda_{2}^{\bar{\alpha}_{2}}~=~\left[{12\over\pi(1-\tau)}\right]^{1\over 3-2\tau},\qquad C_{3}~=~0,\end{cases}

we get (
3.19
).

4.
Finally, if
s>1
, then by the first inequality in (
3.15
) we have

\begin{split}\big\|{\bf N}[g]\big\|_{{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}&~=~\big\|\partial_{x}G_{s}*g\big\|_{{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}~\leq~\|\partial_{x}G_{s}\|_{{\bf L}^{1}(\mathbb{R})}\cdot\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\\[5.69054pt]
&~\leq~{\gamma(s)}\cdot\|g\|_{{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R})}\quad\hbox{for all}~g\in{\bf L}^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}).\end{split}

Hence, by Proposition
2.1
, we achieve (
3.20
).

MM

Remark 3.3

From (
3.14
) and (
3.16
), it holds

\lim_{s\to 1^{-}}\Big(\sqrt{1-s}\cdot\beta_{2}(s)\Big)~=~{\sqrt{12\over\pi}}.

This reveals a sharp connection between the wave-breaking criterion for the subcritical case
s\in(2/3,1)
in (
3.18
) and the critical case
s=1
in (
3.19
). On the other hand, a different approximation procedure for (
3.19
) could be used to yield a similar connection relating the supercritical case
s\in(1,\infty)
in (
3.20
) to the critical case
s=1
.
