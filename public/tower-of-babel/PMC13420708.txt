History of Presentation

A 76-year-old woman was referred for a preprocedural work-up for left atrial appendage occlusion because of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation. She was asymptomatic at the time of the evaluation. The physical examination was unremarkable, with hemodynamic parameters remaining stable.

Past Medical History

The patient had a history of paroxysmal atrial fibrillation, hypertension, chronic kidney disease (stage III), and dyslipidemia. She reported no known family history of neuroendocrine tumors.

Differential Diagnosis

The differential diagnosis included ascending aortic pseudoaneurysm, cardiac hemangioma, intrapericardial paraganglioma, coronary artery aneurysm, cardiac lymphoma, and metastasis.

Investigations

Cardiac computed tomography angiography revealed a well-defined, heterogeneously enhancing mass (4.2 × 3.3 cm) near the ascending aorta (Figure 1A). Cardiac magnetic resonance imaging showed a hyperintense lesion on T2-weighted imaging, with significant gadolinium enhancement suggesting a highly vascular tumor (Figures 1B and 1C). Plasma metanephrines and normetanephrines were within normal limits, suggesting a nonfunctional tumor. Postoperative positron emission tomography with 68 Ga dotatate (PET-dotatate) demonstrated mild radiotracer uptake, indicating residual disease (Figure 1D), and demonstrated an area of avid uptake in the small bowel suspicious for primary versus metastatic disease (Figure 1E). Further evaluation with video capsule endoscopy, double-balloon enteroscopy, and magnetic resonance enterography to examine the entirety of the small bowel did not reveal any suspicious lesions. Histopathology revealed a Zellballen architecture with positive chromogranin and synaptophysin staining (Figure 1F), which confirmed the diagnosis of paraganglioma. Genetic testing detected a pathogenic mutation in the SDHC (succinate dehydrogenase subunit C) gene, consistent with hereditary paraganglioma syndrome.

Management

Given the tumor's vascularity and location, a multidisciplinary team comprising specialists in cardiology, cardiothoracic surgery, radiology, and genetics coordinated the patient's care. Given the encasement of the right coronary artery by the tumor and the need for left atrial appendage exclusion, the decision was made to pursue surgery. The patient underwent resection of the tumor, right coronary artery bypass grafting, and exclusion of the left atrial appendage using an AtriClip device (AtriCure). The intraoperative course was uneventful, with adequate hemostasis and gross total resection. The patient's postoperative recovery was uncomplicated. A surveillance PET-dotatate scan revealed minimal uptake at the surgical bed, indicating residual disease. Follow-up imaging at regular intervals was planned.

Outcome and Follow-Up

The patient was discharged in stable condition. She is currently undergoing surveillance with periodic functional imaging (PET-dotatate) to monitor for recurrence. Genetic counseling and testing completed in the outpatient setting for family risk assessment detected the SDHC gene variant in the patient's sister.

Discussion

Cardiac paragangliomas are rare, making up <1% of all cardiac tumors; fewer than 200 cases have been reported in the literature. These tumors originate from the autonomic ganglia and may secrete catecholamines, causing systemic symptoms such as hypertension, palpitations, and diaphoresis. However, some of these tumors pose diagnostic and therapeutic challenges because of nonspecific symptoms, incidental discovery, and the complexity of the tumor location.1,2

Multimodality imaging (computed tomography and magnetic resonance imaging) is pivotal in identifying the vascular nature and precise anatomical location of the tumor. Magnetic resonance imaging provides superior tissue characterization, while the commonly used radiotracer scan (PET-dotatate) enables detection of residual disease after resection given the affinity of the radiotracer to the somatostatin receptors that play an integral role in the majority of neuroendocrine tumors.3

Surgical resection is the gold standard of treatment, especially in symptomatic cardiac paragangliomas, but it is often pursued if the tumor is locally affecting vital cardiac structures.4 Given the tumor's vascularity, bleeding is a known risk, and involvement of the coronary arteries or other critical structures often necessitates additional procedures (eg, coronary artery bypass grafting).5,6

Genetic testing is essential for all patients with paraganglioma, regardless of functionality, because of the high prevalence of germline mutations including RET, NF1, VHL, and SDH, particularly in SDH gene subunits (SDHB, SDHC, SDHD). SDHC mutations are associated with familial paraganglioma and increased risk of multifocal disease, requiring lifelong surveillance.7

Conclusions

This case highlights the importance of early recognition and evaluation of cardiac masses with advanced cardiac imaging. Genetic testing plays a critical role in guiding management and surveillance in patients with cardiac paraganglioma. A collaborative, multidisciplinary approach improves outcomes and ensures appropriate follow-up.6, 7, 8
