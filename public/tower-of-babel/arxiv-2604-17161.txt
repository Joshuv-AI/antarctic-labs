On the isotropy of differential Ore extensions
Rene Baltazar, Leonardo Duarte Silva, Grasiela Martini

Abstract
Let A h = 𝕜 ⁡ [ x ] ​ [ t ; d ] A_{h}=\Bbbk[x][t;d] be the differential Ore extension. We study the action of the automorphism group Aut ⁡ ( A h ) \Aut(A_{h}) on the derivations Der ⁡ ( A h ) {\rm Der}(A_{h}) and explicitly describe, using Nowicki’s decomposition of the derivations of A h A_{h} , the isotropy groups of this action. More precisely, we first obtain an explicit description of Aut ⁡ ( A h ) \Aut(A_{h}) for deg ⁡ ( h ) ≥ 1 \deg(h)\geq 1 ; then, we determine the isotropy groups of the derivations of the form D = a ​ d w + Δ s ⁡ ( x ) D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)} , which are all the derivations in the square-free case, that is, when gcd ⁡ ( h , h ′ ) = 1 \gcd(h,h^{\prime})=1 . After, in the singular case gcd ⁡ ( h , h ′ ) ≠ 1 \gcd(h,h^{\prime})\neq 1 , where special derivations of type E H E_{H} appear, we show that the isotropy problem is ruled by a suitable localization and by the element w ∗ = w + ψ − 1 ​ H w^{\ast}=w+\psi^{-1}H with ψ = gcd ⁡ ( h , h ′ ) \psi=\gcd(h,h^{\prime}) . This allows us to obtain a general criterion for the isotropy of a derivation D = a ​ d w + E H + Δ s ⁡ ( x ) D=ad_{w}+E_{H}+\Delta_{s(x)} . Finally, explicit results are provided to illustrate the new phenomena that arise in this setting.



1
Introduction

Let
\Bbbk
be an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero.
For a
\Bbbk
-algebra
R
, we denote by
\Aut(R)
and
{\rm Der}(R)
the groups of
\Bbbk
-automorphisms and
\Bbbk
-derivations of
R
, respectively.
Note that
\Aut(R)
acts by conjugation over
{\rm Der}(R)
: given
\delta\in{\rm Der}(R)
and
\rho\in\Aut(R)
, then
\rho\delta\rho^{-1}\in{\rm Der}(R)
.
The
isotropy subgroup of
\delta
, with respect to this group action, is defined
as

\Aut_{\delta}(R):=\{\rho\in\Aut(R)\ |\ \rho\delta=\delta\rho\}.

The structure and behavior of an isotropy subgroup remain largely unexplored.
However, aspects of symmetry and connections with several areas are being investigated: isotropy groups play a fundamental role across various areas of mathematics, appearing naturally in the study of symmetries of foliations, the structure of Hochschild cohomology, and the invariants under group actions in Algebra and Geometry
[
2
,
8
]
.

The differential Ore extensions arise from an interesting classical result: an Ore extension over the polynomial algebra
\Bbbk[x]
is either a quantum plane, a quantum Weyl algebra, or an infinite-dimensional associative algebra
A_{h}
which is generated by the elements
x
and
t
satisfying
tx-xt=h
, where
h\in\Bbbk[x]
(for more details, see
[
11
,
12
]
).
Since the isotropy groups of a quantum plane and a quantum Weyl algebra have already been investigated in
[
11
]
and
[
12
]
, the present work focuses on the isotropy groups of
A_{h}
.

In the Section
2
, we explore some basic concepts and important preliminary results needed for this work. This section gives an overview of the algebraic structures and main definitions used in our study, as well as key lemmas and propositions that will be important in the later sections.

In Section
3
we provide an explicit description of the automorphism group

\Aut(A_{h})
for differential Ore extensions
A_{h}
with
\deg(h)\geq 1
.
Using a convenient change of variable, we reduce the study to the case where the
defining polynomial
h\in\Bbbk[x]
is monic and has a suitable coefficient zero (the one that accompanies the term of degree

\deg(h)-1
), which allows us to apply and extend well-known classification results.

Section
4
is devoted to the study of the action of
\Aut(A_{h})
on the space of
derivations and, in particular, to the explicit description of isotropy
subgroups in the square-free case, that is, under the assumption
\gcd(h,h^{\prime})=1
.
Using the canonical decomposition of derivations due to Nowicki
[
9
, Thm. 10.1]
, we analyze how
each component behaves under conjugation and show that the spaces of the inner
derivations and the differential derivations of type
\Delta_{s(x)}
are two independent

\Aut(A_{h})
-submodules of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
It allows us to compute the isotropy group of a sum of two derivations as the intersection of the isotropy groups of each term in the sum, providing a structural framework that will be used throughout the paper.

Section
5
is dedicated to the singular case, that is, when
\gcd(h,h^{\prime})\neq 1
.
In this setting, Nowicki’s decomposition
[
9
, Thm. 11.2]
shows that a derivation of
A_{h}
may contain, in addition to the inner and the differential parts, an additional special component
E_{H}
, which substantially changes the isotropy problem.
In contrast to the case treated previously, the derivations of the type
E_{H}
are not stable under conjugation: indeed, it may produce simultaneously inner and differential terms.
To handle this phenomenon, we pass to a suitable Ore localization and combine the inner and special parts into the localized element
w^{\ast}=w+\psi^{-1}H
. This leads to a general criterion for the isotropy of derivations of the form
D=ad_{w}+E_{H}+\Delta_{s(x)}
.
In the end, we provide explicit results that illustrate the new phenomena that arise in the singular case.

For a nonzero polynomial

It is also convenient to define the degree of the zero polynomial as

Let

\Bbbk

2.1
The differential algebra

The study of automorphisms of the algebra

For example, in both Kaygorodov, Lopes and Mashurov’s work

Note that given

It is easy to see that

Furthermore, locally nilpotent derivations play an important role to obtain the automorphisms of an algebra.
Indeed, for a

A_{h}

Moreover, the set of these automorphisms generates a normal subgroup of all automorphisms of the algebra.
For the algebra
A_{h}
, both

and

are determined in the following results:

Proposition 1
.

[
7
, Prop. 2]

Let

.
Then

.

Theorem 2
.

[
3
, Thm. 8.3]
Consider

, where

, and
\mathbb{P}=\{(a,b)\in\Bbbk^{*}\times\Bbbk\ |\ h(ax+b)=a^{N}h(x)\}

Then,

The following result establishes necessary and sufficient conditions for an isomorphism between algebras of this work:

Theorem 3
.

[

Let

The following result, used in subsequent chapters, explains the group of automorphisms according to the characteristics of

Theorem 4

[

Assume that

2.2
Properties of
A_{h}

We consider some formulas for the product of elements in
A_{h}
.
The preliminary results developed here also hold in the case
\deg(h(x))=0

For
f(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
, we denote by

the usual derivative, that is, if
f(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n}a_{i}x^{i}

f^{\prime}(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{n}ia_{i}x^{i-1}

Lemma 6
.

In
A_{h}
, for any
n\geq 0
, it holds

tx^{n}=x^{n}t+(x^{n})^{\prime}h(x)=x^{n}t+nx^{n-1}h(x).

(2.2)

Proof.

We prove this by induction on
n
.
For
n=0
and
n=1
is clear.
Assume valid for
n\geq 0
, then

\displaystyle tx^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle tx^{n}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle(x^{n}t+nx^{n-1}h(x))x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{n}tx+nx^{n-1}h(x)x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{n}(xt+h(x))+nx^{n}h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{n+1}t+x^{n}h(x)+nx^{n}h(x)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle x^{n+1}t+(n+1)x^{n}h(x).

Therefore,
tx^{n}=x^{n}t+nx^{n-1}h(x)
, for all
n\geq 0
.
∎

Thus, it is possible to compute the product of
t
with any polynomial of
\Bbbk[x]
:

tf(x)=f(x)t+f^{\prime}(x)h(x).

(2.3)

In general, the product of
t
with any element of
A_{h}
is more complicated.
But sometimes it is sufficient to know the behavior of this product with respect to degree in

.
Recall that for
g\in A_{h},g\neq 0
, there is a unique decomposition
g=\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i}
, where
g_{i}\in\Bbbk[x]
and
g_{n}\neq 0
.
In that case, we set
\deg_{t}(g):=n
.
Note that
\deg_{t}(g)=0
if and only if

.
It is also convenient to define
\deg_{t}(0)=-\infty

Corollary 7
.

Let
g\in A_{h}
. Then
tg=gt+h(x)\tilde{g}
, where
\deg_{t}(\tilde{g})\leq\deg_{t}(g)
.
In particular,
\deg_{t}(tg)=\deg_{t}(g)+1
, and
\deg_{t}(\tilde{g})=\deg_{t}(g)
if and only if
g_{n}(x)\in\Bbbk[x]\backslash\Bbbk
, where
g=\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i}
.

Proof.

Write
g=\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i}
.
Then,

\displaystyle tg

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle t\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}tg_{i}(x)t^{i}

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\eqref{comm_t_fx_eq}}}{{=}}

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}(g_{i}(x)t+g_{i}^{\prime}(x)h(x))t^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i+1}+\sum_{i=0}^{n}h(x)g_{i}^{\prime}(x)t^{i}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}(x)t^{i}\right)t+h(x)\left(\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}^{\prime}(x)t^{i}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle gt+h(x)\tilde{g},

where
\tilde{g}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}g_{i}^{\prime}(x)t^{i}
.
∎

For every

The group

In this section, we use a convenient change of variable to obtain an explicit characterization of the group of automorphisms of

To achieve this goal, the following lemma plays a fundamental role.

Lemma 10
.

[
4

Let

Lemma 11
.

Let

Lemma 14

Let

\displaystyle a^{\ell}f(x)=f(ax)

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{N}a^{\ell}c_{i}x^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{N}a^{i}c_{i}x^{i}

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle a^{\ell}c_{i}=a^{i}c_{i},\ \forall i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,N}

\displaystyle a^{\ell}=a^{i},\ \forall i\in\mathcal{S}_{f}

\displaystyle a^{|i-\ell|}=1,\ \forall i\in\mathcal{S}_{f}

∎

We will use the above lemma for
\deg(h)=N\geq\ell\geq 0
.
In that case, for
h(x)=\sum\limits_{i=0}^{N}c_{i}x^{i}
,
N\geq 1
, we have
\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\tau_{\mathbb{P}},
where

\tau_{\mathbb{P}}:=\ \left\{\tau_{a}\ |\ a\in\cap_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}\mathbb{G}_{N-i}\right\}\subseteq\Bbbk^{\ast}.

(3.2)

Thus, we can precisely determine
\tau_{\mathbb{P}}
: it is finite if and only if
\cap_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}\mathbb{G}_{N-i}
is finite if and only if there is
c_{i}\neq 0
,
i\in\mathbb{I}_{0,N-1}
,
i.e.
, there exists
i\in\mathcal{S}_{h}\backslash\{N\}
.
In that case,
\cap_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}\mathbb{G}_{N-i}=\mathbb{G}_{gcd(N-i_{1},N-i_{2},...,N-i_{k})},
where
\mathcal{S}_{h}=\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k},N\}.

Otherwise,
\tau_{\mathbb{P}}=\Bbbk^{\ast}
, and so it is infinity.

Therefore, we obtain a more explicit characterization for
\Aut(A_{h})
than Theorem
4
, and conclude this section by summarizing the above discussion as follows.

Corollary 15
.

Let
h=\sum_{i=0}^{N}c_{i}x^{i}\in\Bbbk[x]
,
\deg(h)=N\geq 1
.
Then,

i)

\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\Bbbk^{\ast}
, if
h(x)=x^{N}
;

ii)

\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\left(\cap_{i\in\mathcal{S}_{h}}\mathbb{G}_{N-i}\right)=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{gcd(N-i_{1},N-i_{2},...,N-i_{k})},
where
\mathcal{S}_{h}=\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{k},N\}
, otherwise.

By (
3.1
), we know that if
\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})
,
\deg(h)\geq 1
, then
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}
, for some
r(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
a\in\Bbbk^{\ast}
.
However, in what follows, sometimes we need to consider the composition in the inverse order
\tau_{a}\circ\sigma_{r(x)}
, also
\rho^{-1}
and the powers of
\rho
.
Note that
\tau_{a}\circ\sigma_{r(x)}=\sigma_{a^{1-N}r(ax)}\circ\tau_{a}
and so
\rho^{-1}=\tau_{a^{-1}}\circ\sigma_{-r(x)}=\sigma_{-a^{N-1}r(a^{-1}x)}\circ\tau_{a^{-1}}
.
For the powers of
\rho
, we have the following lemma.

Lemma 16
.

Let
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
,
\deg(h)\geq 1
.
Then

\rho^{n}=\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n}}
, for all
n\geq 1
, where
R_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{i(1-N)}r(a^{i}x)
.

Proof.

We prove it by induction on
n
.
For
n=1
is clear.
Assume that it holds for
n\geq 1
.
Since
\tau_{a}\circ\sigma_{r(x)}=\sigma_{a^{1-N}r(ax)}\circ\tau_{a}
, then

\displaystyle\rho^{n+1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\rho\circ\rho^{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\circ\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\sigma_{a^{1-N}R_{n}(ax)}\circ\tau_{a}\circ\tau_{a^{n}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{r(x)+\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{(i+1)(1-N)}r(a^{i+1}x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{\sum_{i=0}^{n}a^{i(1-N)}r(a^{i}x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n+1}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sigma_{R_{n+1}(x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n+1}},

and, therefore, the result follows.
∎

4
The square-free case

Consider a square-free polynomial
h\in\Bbbk[x]
, where
\deg(h)\geq 1
.
In this case, it is known by
[
9
]
(see Thm.
5
) that every derivation
D
in
A_{h}
has a unique decomposition
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
, where
ad_{w}
is the inner derivation of
A_{h}
determined by
w\in A_{h}
, and
\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
is given by
\Delta_{s(x)}(x)=0
and
\Delta_{s(x)}(t)=s(x)
, where
s(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
with
\deg(s)<\deg(h)
. In this section, we characterize the isotropy group
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, and determine it explicitly in some interesting cases.

In order to understand
\Aut_{ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})
, we start with more general considerations.

Lemma 17
.

Let
R
be a
\Bbbk
-algebra,
ad_{w}
an inner derivation of
R
, and
\varphi\in\Aut(R)
. Then
\varphi\in\Aut_{ad_{w}}(R)
if and only if
\varphi(w)-w\in Z(R)
.

Proof.

Since
\varphi
is an automorphism, it holds
\varphi([w,u])=[\varphi(w),\varphi(u)]
, for all
u\in R
.
Moreover,
\varphi\in\Aut_{ad_{w}}(R)
if and only if
\varphi([w,u])=[w,\varphi(u)]
.
At last,
[\varphi(w),\varphi(u)]=[w,\varphi(u)]
if and only if
\varphi(w)-w\in Z(R)
.
∎

However, it is already known that the center of the algebra
A_{h}
is the field
\Bbbk
(see
[
3
, Sec. 5]
), thus we directly have the following result:

Corollary 18
.

Let
h\in\Bbbk[x]
with
\deg(h)\geq 0
. Consider
w\in A_{h}
and the inner derivation
ad_{w}
of
A_{h}
.
Then,

\Aut_{ad_{w}}(A_{h})=\{\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})\ |\ \rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk\}.

If
\deg(h)=0
, all derivations are inner since
A_{h}\simeq A_{1}
is the (first) Weyl algebra (see, for instance,
[
9
, Thm. 9.1]
), and so the above result characterizes their isotropy groups.
Moreover,
if
w\in\Bbbk
, then
\Aut_{ad_{w}}(A_{h})=\Aut(A_{h})
since
ad_{w}=0
and
\rho(w)=w
.

Recall that
{\rm Der}(R)
is an
\Aut(R)
-module via
\rho\cdot d=\rho d\rho^{-1}
, for
\rho\in\Aut(R)
and
d\in{\rm Der}(R)
.
Since
\rho\,ad_{w}\,\rho^{-1}=ad_{\rho(w)}
, the set of inner derivations
\Inn(R)=\{ad_{w}\ |\ w\in R\}
is an
\Aut(R)
-submodule.

Clearly
\Aut_{d_{1}}(R)\cap\Aut_{d_{2}}(R)\subseteq\Aut_{d_{1}+d_{2}}(R)
, for any
d_{1},d_{2}\in{\rm Der}(R)
.
The next proposition gives a condition when the equality holds.

Lemma 19
.

Let
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
be two independent
\Aut(R)
-submodules of
{\rm Der}(R)
.
For any
d_{1}\in\mathcal{D}_{1}
,
d_{2}\in\mathcal{D}_{2}
, it holds
\Aut_{d_{1}+d_{2}}(R)=\Aut_{d_{1}}(R)\cap\Aut_{d_{2}}(R)
.

Proof.

Consider
\rho\in\Aut_{d_{1}+d_{2}}(R)
.
Then

d_{1}+d_{2}=\rho(d_{1}+d_{2})\rho^{-1}=\rho d_{1}\rho^{-1}+\rho d_{2}\rho^{-1}.

Since
\mathcal{D}_{1}
and
\mathcal{D}_{2}
are two independent
\Aut(R)
-submodules, it holds that
\rho\,d_{1}\,\rho^{-1}=d_{1}
and
\rho\,d_{2}\,\rho^{-1}=d_{2}
.
∎

For the next results, we assume that
\deg(h)=N\geq 1
,
w\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
and recall (
3.1
):
\Aut(A_{h})=\{\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\ |\ h(ax)=a^{N}h(x)\}
, where
r(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
a\in\Bbbk^{*}
,
\sigma_{r(x)},\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
are defined as
\sigma_{r(x)}(x)=x
,
\sigma_{r(x)}(t)=t+r(x)
,
\tau_{a}(x)=ax
and
\tau_{a}(t)=a^{N-1}t
.

Lemma 20
.

Consider the subset

\Delta(A_{h})=\{\Delta_{s(x)}\ |\ s(x)\in\Bbbk[x]\textrm{ and }\deg(s(x))<\deg(h(x))\}

of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
Then,
\Delta
is an
\Aut(A_{h})
-submodule of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
Moreover,
\Inn(A_{h})
and
\Delta(A_{h})
are independent
\Aut(A_{h})
-submodules of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.

Proof.

Let
\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})
.
Then, there exist
a\in\Bbbk^{*}
and
r(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
such that
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}
, and so
\rho^{-1}=\sigma_{-a^{N-1}r(a^{-1}x)}\circ\tau_{a^{-1}}
.
Note that,
\rho\,\Delta_{s(x)}\,\rho^{-1}(x)=0
and
\rho\,\Delta_{s(x)}\,\rho^{-1}(t)=a^{1-N}s(ax)
.
If one writes
s(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{k}b_{i}x^{i}
, where
k<N
, we get
a^{1-N}s(ax)=a^{1-N}\sum_{i=0}^{k}b_{i}a^{i}x^{i}
,
i.e.
,
\rho\Delta_{s(x)}\rho^{-1}=\Delta_{S(x)}
, where
S(x)=a^{1-N}\sum_{i=0}^{k}b_{i}a^{i}x^{i}
.

Now we prove that
\Inn(A_{h})
and
\Delta(A_{h})
are independent submodules of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
Indeed, let
d\in\Inn(A_{h})\cap\Delta(A_{h})
.
Then, there exist
w\in A_{h}
and
s(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
such that
d=ad_{w}
and
d=\Delta_{s(x)}
.
Thus,

0=\Delta_{s(x)}(x)=d(x)=ad_{w}(x)=wx-xw,

that is
w\in\Bbbk[x]
.
On the other hand,

s(x)=\Delta_{s(x)}(t)=d(t)=ad_{w}(t)=wt-tw.

Since
w=w(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
, it means
w(x)t=tw(x)+s(x)
and by (
2.3
),
w(x)t=w(x)t+w^{\prime}(x)h(x)+s(x)
,
i.e.
,
s(x)=-w^{\prime}(x)h(x)
.
As
\deg(h(x))\geq 1
and
\deg(s(x))<\deg(h(x))
, we conclude that
w\in\Bbbk
and
s(x)=0
, and therefore
d=0
.
∎

The proof of Lemma
20
also ensure that
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}
if and only if
s(x)=S(x)
, that is,
s(x)=a^{1-N}s(ax)
.
Then, it holds

\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})=\left\{\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})\ |\ s(ax)=a^{N-1}s(x)\right\}.

(4.1)

The previous lemmas lead to the following consequence.

Corollary 21
.

Let
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
and
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
Then,

\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\{\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})\ |\ \rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk,\ s(ax)=a^{N-1}s(x)\}.

Therefore, the above result characterizes the isotropy groups for any derivation of
A_{h}
, where
h(x)
is square free (see
[
9
, Thm. 10.1]
or Thm.
5
).

Now, we are going to describe explicitly the group
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, where
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
.
Since
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Aut(A_{h})
, we already know by Corollary
15
that if
h(x)\neq x^{N}
,
N\geq 1
, then

\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\tau_{\mathbb{P}}
,
where
\tau_{\mathbb{P}}
is a finite subgroup of
\Bbbk^{\ast}
(in particular, it is a finite cyclic group).
On the other hand, Corollary
15
also ensures that if
h(x)=x^{N},N\geq 1
, then
\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\Bbbk^{\ast}
.
Thus, in this latter case, the condition
s(ax)=a^{N-1}s(x)
in (
4.1
) gives rise to two cases:

i)

if
s(x)=cx^{N-1}
,
c\in\Bbbk
, then
\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})=\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\Bbbk^{\ast};

ii)

if
s(x)\neq cx^{N-1}
, then
\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}\subsetneq\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\Bbbk^{\ast}
, for a determined
n\geq 1
, as in Lemma
14
.

By the above,
\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
, in most cases.
In fact, this does not hold only if
h(x)=x^{N}
and
s(x)=cx^{N-1}
,
N\geq 1
.

Now, we shall see that, if
N\geq 2
, then for
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
with
w\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
, it holds that
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
, even though
h(x)=x^{N}
(and so
\tau_{\mathbb{P}}=\Bbbk^{\ast}
).
In particular, in this case,
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subsetneq\Aut(A_{h})
.
We prove this below and the case
h(x)=x
will be considered separately later (see Proposition
23
).

Lemma 22
.

Let
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
be a derivation of
A_{h}
, where
\deg(h)\geq 2
and
w\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
.
Then,
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By Corollary
15
, it is sufficient to analyze the case
h(x)=x^{N}
,
N\geq 2
.
Considerer
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})

and write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}
.
By Corollary
21
, it holds
\rho(w)=w+c
, for some
c\in\Bbbk
.

To prove the result, we need to consider two cases.
First, if
\ell\geq 1
, then

\displaystyle\rho(w)=w+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\rho\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\rho(w_{i}(x))\left(\rho(t)\right)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(ax)a^{i(N-1)}(t+r(x))^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lem.\ref{lema_rhotnai}}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a^{i(N-1)}w_{i}(ax)(t^{i}+ir(x)t^{i-1}+g_{i})\right)+w_{0}(ax)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+w_{0}(x)+c.

Thus, comparing the coefficients of the term of degree in
t
equal to
\ell
(the greatest one), we obtain

a^{\ell(N-1)}w_{\ell}(ax)=w_{\ell}(x)
.
Since
N\geq 2
and
\ell\geq 1
, by Lemma
14
we get
a\in\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
.

We consider now the case
\ell=0
.
Thus
w=w_{0}(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
.
Since
w\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
, write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}x^{i}
,
k\geq 1
and
c_{k}\neq 0
, and then

\displaystyle\rho(w)=w+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}(a^{i}-1)x^{i}=c.

Thus, in particular
a^{k}-1=0
, that is,
a\in\mathbb{G}_{k}
. Therefore,
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
.
∎

For
h(x)=x
, we have the next result.

Proposition 23
.

Consider
h(x)=x
and
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
If
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
, where
\deg(w_{\ell}(x))\geq 1
, then
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
.

Proof.

By the proof of Lemma
22
, if
\ell=0
, then we directly obtain
w=w(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
a\in\mathbb{G}_{\deg(w(x))}
; otherwise, if
\ell\geq 1
, we have that
w_{\ell}
must satisfy
w_{\ell}(ax)=w_{\ell}(x)
, and so by Lemma
14
we conclude that
a\in\mathbb{G}_{\deg(w_{\ell}(x))}
.
∎

We would like to note that, by the two results above, we always have
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, except perhaps if
h(x)=x
and
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\in A_{h}\backslash\Bbbk
with
w_{\ell}(x)\in\Bbbk
.
In fact, in this case, this can happen or not. For instance, see the next example.

Example 24
.

Let
h(x)=x
,
w=\gamma t^{2}+(\alpha x+\beta)t+p(x)
, with
\gamma\in\Bbbk^{*}
,
\alpha,\beta\in\Bbbk
and
p(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
,
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
and
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
.
Applying Corollary
21
and Lemma
9
we obtain necessary and sufficient
conditions for
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
:

r(x)=\dfrac{\alpha(1-a)}{2\gamma}\,x

(4.2)

and

\gamma(r^{\prime}(x)x+r(x)^{2})+(\alpha ax+\beta)r(x)+p(ax)=p(x)+c,\ \ \ \ c\in\Bbbk.

(4.3)

Let

p(x)=\frac{\alpha^{2}x^{2}+2\alpha(\beta+\gamma)x+\nu}{4\gamma},\qquad\nu\in\Bbbk.

Thus, substituting (
4.2
) into (
4.3
), the terms in

x^{2}
and
x
cancel identically and we obtain
p(ax)-p(x)=0
, so that
(
4.2
) holds with
c=0
for any
a\in\Bbbk^{*}
.
Hence, for this choice of
p(x)
, we have
\sigma_{r(x)}
satisfies
\sigma_{r(x)}(w)=w
, and so
\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, for any
a\in\Bbbk^{*}
.

Let
\alpha=0
and
p(x)=x^{n}
with
n\geq 1
. Then (
4.2
) gives

r(x)=0
and (
4.3
) reduces to
p(ax)-p(x)=(a^{n}-1)x^{n}=c\in k
;
i.e.
,
a^{n}=1
.
Thus, the admissible parameters are precisely the elements of the finite cyclic group
\mathbb{G}_{n}\subsetneq\Bbbk^{*}
.

If
\alpha\neq 0
but
p(x)
is constant, comparing coefficients in
(
4.3
) forces
1-a^{2}=0
,
i.e.
,
a=\pm 1
. The value
a=-1
is admissible
only if the linear component is also compatible (for instance,
\beta=-\gamma
), and when
n
is even in the case
p(x)=x^{n}
, then
a=-1

may be allowed.

In contrast to the case
\deg(h)\geq 2
, here the multiplicative component of the isotropy group depends crucially on the choice of
(\alpha,\beta,p(x))
, and it can range from the trivial subgroup
\{1\}
, to any finite cyclic group
\mathbb{G}_{n}
and even
\Bbbk^{*}
.

Actually, by the proof of Lemma
22
, even if
\deg(h(x))=1
, we have the following result.

Proposition 25
.

Consider the derivation
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
of
A_{h}
, where
\deg(h)\geq 1
and
w=\sum_{i=0}^{k}c_{i}x^{i}\in\Bbbk[x]
.
Then,
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
if and only if
\rho(w)=w
.
Moreover, if
w\in\Bbbk[x]\backslash\Bbbk
, then
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
.

Remark 26
.

The above proposition has two interesting facts.
The first is that, if
w\in\Bbbk[x]
, then the automorphisms that are in
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
are those whose
w
is a fixed point.
The second is that, for
w\in\Bbbk[x]\backslash\Bbbk
, even if
h(x)=x^{N}
and
s(x)=cx^{N-1},c\in\Bbbk
, we obtain
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}\subsetneq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\Bbbk^{\ast}=\Aut(A_{h})
, where
n=gcd(i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{j})
being
\mathcal{S}_{w}=\{i_{1},i_{2},...,i_{j}\}
.

Next, we work to get a better description for the isotropy groups given in Corolary
21
: in fact, we shall prove that, in some cases,
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
if and only if
w
is a fixed point for
\rho
, more than
\rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk
, as in the above proposition.
The fact that there is a fixed point plays an interesting role in the case of polynomial algebras: for a simple derivation
d\in{\rm Der}(\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
, if
\rho\in\Aut_{d}(\Bbbk[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}])
has a fixed point, and so an invariant maximal ideal, then
\rho=\rm{id}
(see
[
1
, Prop. 7]
).

First, we prove that such a condition is equivalent for the automorphism
\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
.

Proposition 27
.

Consider
w\in A_{h}
,
\deg(h)\geq 1
, and
\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
, for some
a\in\Bbbk^{\ast}
.
Then
\tau_{a}(w)-w\in\Bbbk\Longleftrightarrow\tau_{a}(w)=w
.

Proof.

Let
c\in\Bbbk
and write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}
.
Then

\displaystyle\tau_{a}(w)=w+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\tau_{a}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}\tau_{a}(w_{i}(x))\left(\tau_{a}(t)\right)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}a^{i(N-1)}w_{i}(ax)t^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c.

Thus, comparing the coefficients of the term of degree in
t
equal to zero, we obtain

w_{0}(ax)=w_{0}(x)+c
.
Write
w_{0}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}x^{i}
.
Thus
w_{0}(ax)=w_{0}(x)+c
if and only if
\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}a^{i}x^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{m}c_{i}x^{i}+c
, and again, analyzing the coefficient of degree zero, we obtain
c_{0}=c_{0}+c
, and so
c=0
.
∎

Now, we investigate the situation for a general
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
.

Proposition 28
.

Let
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
be a derivation of
A_{h}
,
\deg(h)\geq 1
, where
\deg_{t}(w)\geq 2
, and
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
.
If
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
,
n\geq 1
, then
\rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk
if and only if
\rho(w)=w
.

Proof.

Consider the exact sequence of groups

\Bbbk[x]\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\iota}}{{\longrightarrow}}\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\pi}}{{\longrightarrow}}\mathbb{G}_{n}

where
\iota(r(x))=\sigma_{r(x)}
and
\pi(\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a})=\tau_{a}
.
In particular, for
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, it holds
\rho^{n}=\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}\circ\tau_{a^{n}}=\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}
by Lemma
16
, where
R_{n}(x)=\sum_{i=0}^{n-1}a^{i(1-N)}r(a^{i}x)
.
If
\rho(w)=w+c
, for some
c\in\Bbbk
, then
\rho^{i}(w)=w+ic
, and so
\rho^{n}(w)=w+nc
if and only if
\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}(w)=w+nc.

Write
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}
,
\ell\geq 2
.
Then

\displaystyle\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}(w)=w+nc

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}\left(\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\right)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+nc

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)\left(\sigma_{R_{n}(x)}(t)\right)^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+nc

\displaystyle\Longleftrightarrow

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)(t+R_{n}(x))^{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+nc

\displaystyle\stackrel{{\scriptstyle Lem.\ref{lema_rhotnai}}}{{\Longleftrightarrow}}

\displaystyle\left(\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)\left(t^{i}+iR_{n}(x)t^{i-1}+g_{i}\right)\right)+w_{0}(x)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+w_{0}(x)+nc.

The coefficient of the term of degree in
t
equal to
\ell-1
gives us the following relation:

w_{\ell-1}(x)+\ell R_{n}(x)=w_{\ell-1}(x)
.
Thus,
R_{n}(x)=0
and so
\rho^{n}=id
.
Consequently
c=0
, and so
\rho(w)=w
.
∎

In light of Lemma
22
and Proposition
28
, we obtain the following results.

Corollary 29
.

Let
\deg(h)\geq 2
and
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, where
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
.
Then
\rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk
if and only if
\rho(w)=w
.

Corollary 30
.

Let
h(x)=x
and
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
If
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})\subseteq\Bbbk[x]\rtimes\mathbb{G}_{n}
, for some
n\geq 1
, then
\rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk
if and only if
\rho(w)=w
.

Note that, in the results above, we always have that
\rho(w)-w\in\Bbbk
is equivalent to condition
\rho(w)=w
, except perhaps if
h(x)=x
and
\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, for all

.
In this case, this can happen or not, as we see in the next example.

Example 31
.

Consider
h(x)=x
,

, where

Afterward, as a consequence, in this case, the condition

Proposition 32
.

Let
\deg(h)\geq 1
,
w=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}\in A_{h}
, where
\ell\geq 2
,
w_{\ell}(x)\neq 0
and
w_{\ell-1}(x)=0

D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
.
If
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
, then
r(x)=0
.

Proof.

By Proposition
21
, we have
\rho(w)-w=c\in\Bbbk
. Then, analogously to the proof in Lemma
22
, we have

\rho(w)=w+c
if and only if

\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}a^{i(N-1)}w_{i}(ax)(t^{i}+ir(x)t^{i-1}+g_{i})+w_{0}(ax)=\sum_{i=0}^{\ell}w_{i}(x)t^{i}+c.

Thus, comparing the coefficients of the terms of degree in
t
equal to
\ell-1
and
\ell
, we obtain

\ell a^{\ell(N-1)}w_{\ell}(ax)r(x)+a^{(\ell-1)(N-1)}w_{\ell-1}(ax)=w_{\ell-1}(x)

Since

.
∎

In the conditions of the above proposition, if

then

, for some

Hence,
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\{\tau_{a}\in\tau_{\mathbb{P}}\ |\ \tau_{a}(w)=w\}.

In particular,

), or

In particular, let

However, if

On the other hand, by Proposition

.
Below, we will analyze the case in which

Lemma 33
.

Let
w=f(x)t+g(x),\ f(x),g(x)\in\Bbbk[x]

If
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})

,
\rho(w)-w=c\in\Bbbk

\displaystyle\rho(w)-w=c

\displaystyle a^{N-1}f(ax)(t+r(x))-f(x)t-g(t)=c

\displaystyle a^{N-1}f(ax)=f(x)\ \mbox{and}\ f(x)r(x)=g(x)-g(ax)+c.

∎

Remark 34
.

Let
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
, where

, and
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})

Example 35
.

1.

.

is a root of unity and the polynomial

is determined, except maybe for translation by constants.
In the next example, the situation is totally different.

Example 36
.

Let
h(x)=x^{3}+\alpha\,x+\beta
, where
0\neq\alpha\,\beta\in\Bbbk
,
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
with
w,s(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
,
\deg(s(x))<3
.
Then,
\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]
.
Indeed, by Corollary
15
ii) it follows that
\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]

D(t)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
D(x)=0

\sigma_{r(x)}\circ D=D\circ\sigma_{r(x)}

\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\Aut(A_{h})=\Bbbk[x]
.

It is known that any locally nilpotent derivation of
A_{h}
is, up to automorphism, of the form:
D_{p(x)}
, where
D_{p(x)}(x)=0
and
D_{p(x)}(t)=p(x)
(see
[
7

). For this family of derivations we have the following result.

Remark 37
.

Let
D_{p(x)}\in LND(A_{h})
,
p(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
.
Then

\Aut_{D_{p(x)}}(A_{h})=\{\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}|\ p(ax)=a^{N-1}p(x)\}.

In this case, we can uniquely decompose the derivation

as

which will depend on the degree of the polynomial
p(x)
in relation to the degree of
h(x)
.

If
\deg(p(x))<\deg(h(x))

. Otherwise, by the division algorithm, there exists

, where

.

, and so

.

5
The singular case
\gcd(h,h^{\prime})\neq 1

In the previous section, we dealt with a derivation
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}
of
A_{h}
.
If
\gcd(h,h^{\prime})=1
, any
D\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
has such decomposition
[
9
, Thm. 10.1]
(see Thm.
5
).
When
h
has multiple roots, new derivations appear, encoded by
special polynomials
, forcing a refinement of the isotropy analysis.

Let
\deg(h)=N\geq 2
and let
r
be the number of all pairwise different roots of the polynomial
h(x)
(recall that we assume
\Bbbk
an algebraically closed field of characteristic zero). Denote by
\psi
the greatest common divisor of
h(x)
and
h^{\prime}(x)
, and in this case
\deg(\psi)=N-r>0
.

We say that a polynomial
H\in A_{h}
is
special
if it is of the form

H=h_{n}t^{n}+\ldots+h_{1}t^{1},

where
n\geq 1
,
h_{i}\in\Bbbk[x]
and
\deg(h_{i})<N-r
, for all
i=\mathbb{I}_{1,n}
.
By convention, the zero polynomial is also special.
Given a special polynomial
H
, the linear map
E_{H}:A_{h}\to A_{h}
defined as

E_{H}(f)=\left[\tfrac{1}{\psi}H,f\right],\quad f\in A_{h},

is a well-defined derivation of
A_{h}
(see
[
9
, Prop. 2.1 and 8.2]
).
Moreover,
E_{H}
is an inner derivation of
A_{h}
if and only if
H=0
.

Theorem 38

(
[
9
, Theorem 11.1]
)
.

Let
h\in\Bbbk[x]
,
\deg(h)\geq 2
and
\psi=\gcd(h,h^{\prime})
.
Then every derivation
D\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
can be uniquely expressed as

D=ad_{w}+E_{H}+\Delta_{s(x)},

where
w\in A_{h}
,
s(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
with
\deg(s)<\deg(h)
,
and
H
is a special polynomial as defined above. Moreover,
D
is inner if and only if
H=0
and
s(x)=0
.

Recall that
A_{h}=\Bbbk[x][t;d]
where
d=h(x)\,\partial_{x}
.
Let
S=\{\psi^{n}\mid n\geq 0\}\subseteq\Bbbk[x]
. Since
d(\psi)=\psi^{\prime}\,h(x)\in\psi\,\Bbbk[x]
, the set
S
is an Ore set in
A_{h}
(see
[
3
, Lemma 4.2]
).
We denote by

R_{S}:=\Bbbk[x][S^{-1}]\quad\text{and}\quad\mathcal{B}:=A_{h}[S^{-1}]\cong R_{S}[t;d_{S}],

where
d_{S}:R_{S}\to R_{S}
is the unique extension of
d
.
Moreover, for any
\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})
, we can also consider its extension to
\mathcal{B}
.
Indeed, if
\rho=\sigma_{r(x)}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
, then
h(x)
satisfies
h(ax)=a^{N}h(x)
, and consequently
h^{\prime}(ax)=a^{N-1}h^{\prime}(x)
.
As
\psi(x)=\gcd(h,h^{\prime})
, then
\psi(ax)
divides both
h(x)
and
h^{\prime}(x)
, and so
\psi(ax)\,|\,\psi(x)
,
i.e.
,
\psi(ax)=a_{\psi}\,\psi(x)
, for some
a_{\psi}\in\Bbbk
.
Thus,
\rho(\psi)=\psi(ax)=a_{\psi}\,\psi(x)
and, therefore, there exists a unique extension of
\rho
to an automorphism of
\mathcal{B}
(which we will also denote by
\rho
).
Furthermore, for an element
u\in A_{h}
, by
\frac{u}{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}
we will mean
\frac{1}{\psi}u\in\mathcal{B}
.

Consider the
\Bbbk
-subspaces
\Inn(A_{h})
and
\Delta(A_{h})
as in the previous section.
If
H_{1}
and
H_{2}
are two special polynomials, and
c\in\Bbbk
, then
H_{1}+cH_{2}
is a special polynomial and
E_{H_{1}}+cE_{H_{2}}=E_{H_{1}+cH_{2}}
.
Thus, one can consider the
\Bbbk
-subspace
\mathcal{E}(A_{h})=\{E_{H}\ |\ \textrm{$H$ is a special polynomial of $A_{h}$}\}
, and the above theorem ensure that
{\rm Der}(A_{h})=\Inn(A_{h})\oplus\Delta(A_{h})\oplus\mathcal{E}(A_{h})
as
\Bbbk
-spaces.

We would like to conclude that
\Inn(A_{h}),\Delta(A_{h})
and
\mathcal{E}(A_{h})
are independent
\Aut(A_{h})
-submodules of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
, and, if that were the case, then
\Aut_{D}(A_{h}),

D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}+E_{H}
, could be calculated using Lemma
19
by computing each of the groups
\Aut_{ad_{w}}(A_{h}),

\Aut_{\Delta_{s(x)}}(A_{h})
and
\Aut_{E_{H}}(A_{h})
separately.
But that is not the case.
In general, the
\Bbbk
-space
\mathcal{E}(A_{h})
is not even an
\Aut(A_{h})
-submodule of
{\rm Der}(A_{h})
.
Indeed, let
\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})
.
Since
\rho\circ E_{H}\circ\rho^{-1}(f)=\left[\tfrac{\rho(H)}{a_{\psi}\psi},f\right]
for all
f\in A_{h}
, if
\rho(H)
is a special polynomial, then
\rho\circ E_{H}\circ\rho^{-1}=E_{(a_{\psi})^{-1}\rho(H)}
;
but it is not always the case that
\rho(H)
is a special polynomial.
More precisely, conjugating a derivation of type
E_{H}
may not produce a derivation
E_{J}\in\mathcal{E}(A_{h})
.
Consider the following example.

Example 39
.

Let
h(x)=x^{2}
and
\rho=\sigma_{x\,p(x)+c}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
,
p(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
a,c\in\Bbbk
, and
H=t\in A_{h}
.
Note that
\psi=x
and
E_{t}=[x^{-1}t,-]
.
Since
\rho(t)=a(t+xp(x)+c)
and
\rho(x)=ax
, we obtain

\rho(x^{-1}t)=\rho(x)^{-1}\rho(t)=x^{-1}t+p(x)+c\,x^{-1}.

Since
[cx^{-1},x]=0
and
[cx^{-1},t]=c
, it means that
[cx^{-1},-]=\Delta_{c}
.
Then

\rho\circ E_{t}\circ\rho^{-1}=[x^{-1}t+p(x)+c\,x^{-1},-]=E_{t}+ad_{p(x)}+\Delta_{c}.

The above discussion ensures that for a derivation
D=ad_{w}+\Delta_{s(x)}+E_{H}
, one cannot consider the derivation
E_{H}
separately.
In this context, we denote
w^{\ast}:=w+\tfrac{H}{\psi}\in\mathcal{B}
,
so that we can write
ad_{w}+E_{H}=[w^{\ast},-]
.

Lemma 40
.

If
u\in\mathcal{B}
satisfies
[u,x]=0
, then
u\in R_{S}
.

Proof.

Let
u=\sum_{i=0}^{m}f_{i}t^{i}
where
f_{i}\in R_{S}
and
f_{m}\neq 0
.
If
m\geq 1
, then

[u,x]=\sum_{i=1}^{m}f_{i}[t^{i},x].

By Lemma
8
, the highest power of
t
of
[t^{i},x]
is
ih(x)t^{i-1}
, hence the highest power of
t
of
[u,x]
is
mf_{m}h(x)t^{m-1}
. Since
R_{S}
is a domain and
h(x)\neq 0
, this term is nonzero, contradicting
[u,x]=0
. Therefore,
m=0
,
i.e.
,
u\in R_{S}
.
∎

Lemma 41
.

Let
d_{S}:R_{S}\to R_{S}
be the unique extension of the derivation
d=h(x)\partial_{x}
, where
h(x)\in\Bbbk[x]\setminus\{0\}
. Then,
\ker(d_{S})=\Bbbk
.

Proof.

Let
u\in R_{S}
and assume that
d_{S}(u)=0
.
Since
d_{S}(u)=h(x)u^{\prime}
, we obtain
u^{\prime}=0
.
Write
u=\frac{f}{g}\in R_{S}
, that is,
f\in\Bbbk[x]
and
g=\psi^{n}\in S\subseteq\Bbbk[x]
.
If
f=0
, then
u=0
,
i.e.,

u\in\Bbbk
.
Assume
f\neq 0
.
Note that
u=\frac{f}{g}=\frac{F\Psi}{G\Psi}=\frac{F}{G}\in\Bbbk(x)
, where
F,G\in\Bbbk[x]
,
\gcd(f,g)=\Psi
,
\gcd(F,G)=1
, and
\Bbbk(x)
denotes the field of fractions of
\Bbbk[x]
, which coincides with the field of fractions of
R_{S}
.
In particular, for
F\neq 0
,
\deg(F^{\prime})<\deg(F)
.
Since
0=u^{\prime}=\frac{GF^{\prime}-FG^{\prime}}{G^{2}}
, it follows that
GF^{\prime}=FG^{\prime}
.
Hence,
F\mid GF^{\prime}
, and so
F\mid F^{\prime}
since
\gcd(F,G)=1
.
If
F^{\prime}\neq 0
, then
\deg(F)\leq\deg(F^{\prime})
, but it is a contradiction.
Therefore,
F^{\prime}=0
,
i.e.
.
F\in\Bbbk
.
Then,
FG^{\prime}=GF^{\prime}=0
, that is
G^{\prime}=0
, and so
G\in\Bbbk
, and consequently
u=\frac{F}{G}\in\Bbbk
.
Thus,
\ker(d_{S})\subseteq\Bbbk
, and since the reverse inclusion is clear, it follows that
\ker(d_{S})=\Bbbk
.
∎

Proposition 42
.

Let
D=ad_{w}+E_{H}+\Delta_{s(x)}=[w^{\ast},-]+\Delta_{s(x)}\in{\rm Der}(A_{h})
, where
w^{\ast}:=w+\psi^{-1}H\in\mathcal{B}
,
H
is a special polynomial,
w\in A_{h}
,
\psi=\gcd(h,h^{\prime})
and
\deg(s(x))<\deg(h(x))
.
Then,
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
if and only if
d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)
and
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
.

Proof.

Consider the extension of
\rho
to an automorphism of
\mathcal{B}
.
Then, it holds
\rho\circ[w^{\ast},-]\circ\rho^{-1}=[\rho(w^{\ast}),-]
.
Moreover,
\rho\circ\Delta_{s(x)}\circ\rho^{-1}=\Delta_{a^{1-N}s(ax)}
, since
(\rho\circ\Delta_{s(x)}\circ\rho^{-1})(x)=0
and
(\rho\circ\Delta_{s(x)}\circ\rho^{-1})(t)=a^{1-N}s(ax)
. Therefore,

\rho\circ D\circ\rho^{-1}=[\rho(w^{\ast}),-]+\Delta_{a^{1-N}s(ax)}.

Thus,
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
if and only if

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},-]+\Delta_{a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)}=0.

(5.1)

Now, we prove that (
5.1
) holds if and only if
d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)
and
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
.
First, assume that (
5.1
) holds.
Then, evaluating it at
x
and
t
, we obtain

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},x]=0\quad\text{and}\quad[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},t]=s(x)-a^{1-N}s(ax).

By Lemma
40
, it follows that
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
, and so the Ore relation in
\mathcal{B}
gives

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},t]=-d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}).

Therefore,
d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)
.

For the converse, assume that
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
and
d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)
.
Since
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
, then

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},t]=-d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=s(x)-a^{1-N}s(ax),

and so

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},t]+\Delta_{a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)}(t)=0,

and clearly

[\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast},x]+\Delta_{a^{1-N}s(ax)-s(x)}(x)=0,

that is, (
5.1
) holds.
∎

Remark 43
.

If
\gcd(h,h^{\prime})=1
,
i.e.

\psi=1
, then
R_{S}=\Bbbk[x]
,
\mathcal{B}=A_{h}
,
w^{\ast}=w
and
H=0
.
In this case, Proposition
42
coincides with the square-free criterion obtained in the previous section (see Corollary
21
).

Corollary 44
.

Let
H
be a special polynomial and
w\in A_{h}
. If
D=ad_{w}+E_{H}=[w^{\ast},-]
, then

\Aut_{D}(A_{h})=\big\{\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})\ \big|\ \rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in\Bbbk\big\}.

Proof.

Proposition
42
, considering
s(x)=0
, states that
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
if and only if
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in R_{S}
and
d_{S}(\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast})=0
;
by Lemma
41
, the last condition is equivalent to
\rho(w^{\ast})-w^{\ast}\in\Bbbk
.
∎

Consider
D=E_{H}
in the above result,
i.e.,

w=0
.
Then, one immediately gets the following:

Corollary 45
.

Let
H
be a special polynomial. Then

\Aut_{E_{H}}(A_{h})=\bigl\{\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})\ \big|\ \rho(\psi^{-1}H)-\psi^{-1}H\in\Bbbk\bigr\}.

Consider
D=ad_{w}+E_{H}
, for some
w,H\in A_{h}
, where
H
is a special polynomial, and
\rho\in\Aut(A_{h})
.
It is clear that if
\rho(w)-w,\rho\left(\frac{H}{\psi}\right)-\frac{H}{\psi}\in\Bbbk
, then
\rho(w^{*})-w^{*}\in\Bbbk
, where
w^{*}=w+\frac{H}{\psi}
.
Therefore,
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
.
The next example illustrates that
\rho(w^{*})-w^{*}\in\Bbbk
can happen even if
\rho(w)-w\notin\Bbbk
and
\rho(\frac{H}{\psi})-\frac{H}{\psi}\notin\Bbbk
.
That is, to
\rho\in\Aut_{D}(A_{h})
it is necessary to verify
\rho\left(w+\frac{H}{\psi}\right)-\left(w+\frac{H}{\psi}\right)\in\Bbbk
, and not just
\rho(w)-w,\rho\left(\frac{H}{\psi}\right)-\frac{H}{\psi}\in\Bbbk
separately.

Example 46
.

Consider
A_{x^{2}}
,
w=\frac{1}{1-a}x+c
, where
1\neq a\in\Bbbk
, and the special polynomial
H=t
.
Thus,
\psi=x
and
w^{*}=w+\frac{t}{x}
.
For
\rho=\sigma_{x^{2}}\circ\tau_{a}\in\Aut(A_{h})
, we get
\rho(w)-w=-x
and
\rho\left(\frac{t}{x}\right)-\frac{t}{x}=x
, and so
\rho(w^{*})-w^{*}=0\in\Bbbk
.
Therefore,
\rho\in\Aut_{ad_{w}+E_{H}}(A_{h})
, but
\rho\notin\Aut_{ad_{w}}(A_{h})
and
\rho\notin\Aut_{E_{H}}(A_{h})
.

Remark 47
.

Let
u\in R_{S}
and assume
d_{S}(u)\in\Bbbk[x]
.
Then the commutator
[u,-]
is a well-defined derivation of
A_{h}
and
[u,-]=D_{-d_{S}(u)}
, where
D_{-d_{S}(u)}
is the derivation of
A_{h}
determined by
D_{-d_{S}(u)}(x)=0
and
D_{-d_{S}(u)}(t)=-d_{S}(u)
.
In particular, if
\deg(d_{S}(u))<\deg(h(x))
, then
[u,-]=\Delta_{-d_{S}(u)}
.
In general, we write
d_{S}(u)=q(x)h(x)+r(x)
, with
q(x),r(x)\in\Bbbk[x]
and
\deg(r(x))<\deg(h(x))
.
Then

[u,-]=ad_{f}+\Delta_{-r(x)},

where
f\in\Bbbk[x]
is any polynomial that satisfies
f^{\prime}(x)=q(x)
.
