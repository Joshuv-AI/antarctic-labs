Robust synchronization for multi-agent systems governed by PDEs with observable and unobservable disturbances
Yongchun Bi 1,2, Jun Zheng 2, Guchuan Zhu 3, Jiye Zhang 1

Abstract
This paper investigates robust synchronization for multi-agent systems (MASs) governed by parabolic partial differential equations in the presence of both observable and unobservable disturbances. Using only boundary output measurements, a disturbance observer is designed to estimate observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances while ensuring robustness of the observer error system with unobservable disturbances occurring in the domain. Using only the reference signal and local output information, distributed synchronization controllers are then constructed to enable all agents to track the reference trajectory. In particular, exponential tracking is achieved in the absence of unobservable disturbances, while robustness is preserved when additional unobservable disturbances occur during controller implementation. We further analyze the impact of unobservable Dirichlet-Robin boundary disturbances on synchronization performance by proving the boundedness of solutions to the synchronization error system. Moreover, to characterize the influence of all disturbances, input-to-state stability (ISS) is established for the closed-loop system. For the involved systems, the generalized Lyapunov method and the recursion technique are extensively employed in the stability analysis, and the lifting technique and semigroup theory are used to prove the well-posedness. Simulation results validate the proposed control scheme, demonstrating effective disturbance estimation and rejection, robust synchronization, and the ISS properties under various scenarios.

A key objective for addressing such problems is to design synchronizing

In the absence of disturbances, synchronization problem of PDEs has been extensively
studied, see, e.g.,

The inevitable presence of various disturbances in practical systems further complicates the synchronizing controller design and robustness analysis for MASs governed by PDEs.
It is worth noting that on one hand, existing results in the literature are typically developed under relatively ideal conditions where the disturbances are either observable

However, in practical control scenarios, input deviations caused by actuator saturation, communication delays, or unmodeled dynamics often manifest as unobservable disturbances without any prior information on their magnitude, dynamics, structure, etc. and hence, do not satisfy the commonly required harsh conditions, rendering the aforementioned results difficult to apply directly. Consequently, in the presence of such unobservable disturbances, the synchronization problem of MASs governed by PDEs remains largely unexplored. A notable exception is
[
1
]
, where the authors considered unobservable in-domain and Robin boundary disturbances and established the input-to-state stability (ISS) to characterize the effect of these disturbances on the overall closed-loop multi-agent system (MAS) governed by wave equations.

On the other hand, the most of the existing works primarily focus on simple scenarios that involve only in-domain disturbances or those acting at Neumann or Robin boundaries, e.g.,

for Neumann boundary disturbances and
[

for Robin boundary disturbances.
To the best of our knowledge, for the synchronization problem of MASs governed by PDEs, neither the active estimation and rejection of observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances nor the case involving unobservable Dirichlet boundary disturbances has yet been considered. Moreover, when observable and unobservable disturbances (whether in-domain or boundary) are present simultaneously, the problem becomes significantly more complex and challenging, thereby deserving further investigation.

For the synchronization problem of MASs governed by PDEs in such scenarios, two fundamental challenges exist.

•

From a control design perspective, it remains an open problem how to achieve robust synchronization in the presence of unobservable in-domain or boundary disturbances that lack any prior information, while simultaneously reducing inter-agent communication and enabling effective estimation and rejection of observable boundary disturbances, especially Dirichlet boundary disturbances.

•

From a stability analysis standpoint, Dirichlet boundary disturbances introduce nonhomogeneous Dirichlet boundary terms into the tracking or synchronization error system, which are inherently difficult to handle within the framework of the classical Lyapunov stability theory without involving the derivative of disturbances
[
22
]
.

To address these issues, in this paper, we investigate the synchronization problem of MASs governed by PDEs under a complex scenario characterized by three types of disturbances:

(i)

unobservable in-domain disturbances;

(ii)

unobservable implementation disturbances at the Dirichlet and Robin boundaries; and

(iii)

observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances.

To ensure that all agents achieve common objectives under such disturbances, we propose a distributed control scheme that relies solely on local interactions and neighbor-to-neighbor communication, and assess the stability within the framework of a generalized Lyapunov stability theory (see
[
45
,
46
]
), thereby enhancing the robustness, extensibility, and flexibility for MASs as indicated in
[
19
,
23
]
. More specifically,
the present work are fourfold:

(i)

For observable disturbances acting at the Dirichlet
boundary, a disturbance observer is designed for each agent. The proposed observer is capable of estimating these boundary disturbances even in the presence of other unobservable in-domain and boundary disturbances.

(ii)

Despite the presence of unobservable in-domain and Dirichlet-Robin boundary disturbances, we design distributed synchronization controllers that rely only on the reference signal and the output of a single neighboring agent. This design scheme not only reduces the communication burden among the agents but also ensures the robust synchronization.
In particular, it guarantees the exponential stability for both the tracking error and the synchronization error of the MASs with only observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances.

(iii)

For the purpose of characterizing the influence of disturbances on synchronization performance, we establish the ISS for the closed-loop system, thereby providing a framework to quantify its robustness.

(iv)

For stability analysis, a generalized Lyapunov method (GLM,
[
45
,
46
]
), combined with the recursion technique, is extensively employed to overcome the difficulties posed by Dirichlet boundary disturbances in the synchronization control of the
MAS. This approach enables not only the derivation of robustness estimates for the tracking and synchronization error systems but also the establishment of the ISS of the closed-loop system, despite the presence of various observable or unobservable disturbances. Furthermore, robustness estimates for the disturbance observer system are derived via a classical Lyapunov method when unobservable in-domain disturbances are present.

\displaystyle u_{t}^{(i)}(x,t)=

\displaystyle u^{(i)}(0,t)=

\displaystyle q^{(i)}(t)+{U^{(i)}_{0}(t)},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

\displaystyle u_{x}^{(i)}(1,t)=

\displaystyle-lu^{(i)}(1,t)+{U^{(i)}_{1}(t)},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(1c)

\displaystyle u^{(i)}(x,0)=

\displaystyle u_{0}^{(i)}(x),x\in(0,1),

(1d)

\displaystyle y^{(i)}(t)=

\displaystyle u^{(i)}_{x}(0,t),z^{(i)}(t)=u^{(i)}(1,t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

\displaystyle u^{\text{ref}}(0,t)=

\displaystyle u_{x}^{\text{ref}}(1,t)=

3
Controller Design and Main Results

In this section, we propose a distributed control design scheme for the MAS to estimate and reject observable disturbances while achieving robust synchronization in the presence of unobservable disturbances, and then present the main results.

3.1
Controller Design

Following the active disturbance rejection control scheme, which offers a framework for real-time disturbance estimation and rejection via an extended state observer
[
12
,
11
]
, we design an observer to estimate the Dirichlet boundary disturbance
q^{(i)}(t)
and then design distributed controllers for ensuring the robust synchronization of the MAS with various disturbances.

First, we construct the following
v^{(i)}
-system to decouple
q^{(i)}(t)
from the control action:

\displaystyle v_{t}^{(i)}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\alpha v_{xx}^{(i)}(x,t)-\lambda v^{(i)}(x,t),(x,t)\in Q_{\infty},

(3a)

\displaystyle v^{(i)}(0,t)=

\displaystyle{U^{(i)}_{0}(t)},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(3b)

\displaystyle v_{x}^{(i)}(1,t)=

\displaystyle-lv^{(i)}(1,t)+{U^{(i)}_{1}(t)},t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(3c)

\displaystyle v^{(i)}(x,0)=

\displaystyle v_{0}^{(i)}(x),x\in(0,1),

(3d)

\displaystyle y_{v}^{(i)}(t)=

\displaystyle v_{x}^{(i)}(0,t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(3e)

where
y_{v}^{(i)}(t)
is the measurable output and

denotes the initial data.

Using the measured outputs

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{t}^{(i)}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\alpha\hat{q}_{xx}^{(i)}(x,t)-\lambda\hat{q}^{(i)}(x,t),(x,t)\in Q_{\infty},

(4a)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{x}^{(i)}(0,t)=

\displaystyle y^{(i)}(t)-y_{v}^{(i)}(t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(4b)

\displaystyle\hat{q}_{x}^{(i)}(1,t)=

\displaystyle-l\hat{q}^{(i)}(1,t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(4c)

\displaystyle\hat{q}^{(i)}(x,0)=

Now, based on

\displaystyle U^{(i)}_{0}(t)=

\displaystyle r(t)-\hat{q}^{(i)}(0,t),i=1,\dots,N,

(5a)

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle k_{1}\left(u^{(N)}(1,t)-u^{\text{ref}}(1,t)\right),

(5b)

\displaystyle U^{(i)}_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle k_{i}\left(u^{(i-1)}(1,t)-u^{\text{ref}}(1,t)\right),i=2,\dots,N,

where

Remark 1
.

Under the control laws given by (

Notably, in practical MASs, protocols with directed information flow incur lower communication and sensing costs compared to their undirected counterparts. Under such topologies, each agent is required to sense information from only one other agent. This minimizes the total number of communication links to
N

N

Owing to this structural simplicity and high efficiency, the directed cycle topology has been successfully deployed in a wide range of practical problems within the framework of ODEs such as vehicle formations
[
18
,
14
,
32
]
, military applications
[
15
]
, and target circumnavigation
[

. In this paper, we extend the application of directed cycle topology to the MASs described by PDEs.

Note that
U^{(i)}_{0}(t)
enables the MAS to track a prescribed reference trajectory, while

coordinates synchronization among agents. However, in practical implementations, the control inputs applied at the boundary often deviate from the ideally designed

U^{(i)}_{0}(t)
and
U^{(i)}_{1}(t)
take the form

\displaystyle U^{(i)}_{0}(t)=

\displaystyle r(t)-\hat{q}^{(i)}(0,t)+d_{0}^{(i)}(t),i=1,\dots,N,

(6a)

\displaystyle U^{(1)}_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle k_{1}\left(u^{(N)}(1,t)-u^{\text{ref}}(1,t)\right)+{d_{1}^{(1)}(t)},

(6b)

\displaystyle U^{(i)}_{1}(t)=

\displaystyle k_{i}\left(u^{(i-1)}(1,t)-u^{\text{ref}}(1,t)\right){+d_{1}^{(i)}(t)},i=2,\dots,N.

(6c)

\displaystyle\mathcal{N}:=

\displaystyle\frac{\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}}{{|l|^{N}}},

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}_{i}:=

\displaystyle 1+\sum_{m=1}^{i}\frac{\prod_{r=0}^{m-1}k_{i-r}}{l^{m}}+\sum_{m=i+1}^{N-1}\frac{\left(\prod_{r=0}^{i-1}k_{i-r}\right)\left(\prod_{s=0}^{m-i-1}k_{N-s}\right)}{l^{m}},

and
\sigma\in\left(0,\lambda\right)
is an arbitrary constant.

The third main result shows the robustness of the synchronization error
\widetilde{u}^{(i,j)}
-system (

) under the control laws given by (

Theorem 3.3
.

Under the control laws given by (

),
the synchronization error
\widetilde{u}^{(i,j)}
-system (

:

\displaystyle\left\|\widetilde{u}^{(i,j)}(\cdot,t)\right\|_{C([0,1])}\leq

where

\displaystyle\left(\prod_{r=0}^{j-1}k_{\,j-r}\right)\left(\prod_{s=1}^{\,m-j}k_{\,N+1-s}\right),

\displaystyle\widetilde{\mathcal{H}}_{i,j}:=

\displaystyle\frac{k_{j}\mathcal{H}_{i-1,j-1}}{l(1-\mathcal{N})}+\frac{|k_{i}-k_{j}|}{l},

and
\sigma,\mathcal{N}
,

, and

are the same as in Theorem

4.1
Well-Posedness Analysis for the
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}
-System (
9
)

In this subsection, we employ the lifting technique and semigroup theory of linear operators to establish the well-posedness of the
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}
-system (
9
), which is stated as the following theorem.

Theorem 4.1
.

The
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}
-system (
9
) admits a unique solution
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};H^{2}(0,1))\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};L^{2}(0,1))
.

Before proving this theorem, we rewrite the equations of all
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}
-systems (
9
) into a unified form. Specifically, let

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}:=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{u}^{(1)},\dots,\widetilde{u}^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}_{0}:=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{u}_{0}^{(1)},\dots,\widetilde{u}_{0}^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},

\displaystyle{F}:=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}f^{(1)},\dots,f^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},

\displaystyle{D}:=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}d^{(1)}_{1},\dots,d^{(N)}_{1}\end{pmatrix}^{\top},

\displaystyle Q:=

\displaystyle\begin{pmatrix}\!q^{(1)}+d^{(1)}_{0}\!-\hat{q}^{(1)}(0,t),\dots,q^{(N)}+d^{(N)}_{0}\!-\hat{q}^{(N)}(0,t)\!\end{pmatrix}^{\!\top}\!\!\!,

and define the matrix

\displaystyle B:=[b_{ij}]_{N\times N}:=\begin{pmatrix}-l&0&0&\cdots&0&k_{1}\\
k_{2}&-l&0&\cdots&0&0\\
0&k_{3}&-l&\cdots&0&0\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots&\vdots&\vdots\\
0&0&\cdots&k_{N-1}&-l&0\\
0&0&\cdots&0&k_{N}&-l\end{pmatrix}.

Then, we get

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\widetilde{\nu}_{t}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\alpha\widetilde{\nu}_{xx}(x,t)-\lambda\widetilde{\nu}(x,t)+F(x,t),(x,t)\in Q_{\infty},

(18a)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\widetilde{\nu}(0,t)=

\displaystyle Q(t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(18b)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\widetilde{\nu}_{x}(1,t)=

\displaystyle B\widetilde{\nu}(1,t)+D(t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(18c)

\displaystyle\!\!\!\!\!\widetilde{\nu}(x,0)=

\displaystyle\widetilde{\nu}_{0}(x),x\in(0,1).

(18d)

Next, we
employ a lifting technique to convert (
18
) with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions into a system with homogeneous ones and rewrite it in an abstract form. Indeed, letting

\displaystyle H:=\begin{pmatrix}h^{(1)},\dots,h^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}:=Q+(x^{2}-x)\left(BQ+D\right)

and

\displaystyle\Phi:=\begin{pmatrix}\phi^{(1)},\dots,\phi^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}:=\widetilde{\nu}-H,

system (
18
) can be written as

\displaystyle\Phi_{t}(x,t)=

\displaystyle\alpha\Phi_{xx}(x,t)+F(t,\Phi),(x,t)\in Q_{\infty},

(19a)

\displaystyle\Phi(0,t)=

\displaystyle 0,t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(19b)

\displaystyle\Phi_{x}(1,t)=

\displaystyle B\Phi(1,t),t\in\mathbb{R}_{>0},

(19c)

\displaystyle\Phi(x,0)=

\displaystyle\Phi_{0}(x):=\widetilde{\nu}_{0}(x)-H(x,0),x\in(0,1).

(19d)

Let
\Psi:=\begin{pmatrix}\psi^{(1)},\dots,\psi^{(N)}\end{pmatrix}^{\top}
. Define the operator
\mathcal{A}:{D}(\mathcal{A})\subset X\rightarrow X:=(L^{2}(0,1))^{N}
by

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}{\Psi}:=\operatorname{diag}\left({\alpha}(\psi^{(1)})^{\prime\prime},\dots,{\alpha}(\psi^{(N)})^{\prime\prime}\right)

with

\displaystyle{D}(\mathcal{A}):=\Big\{\Psi\in(H^{2}(0,1))^{N}~\big|~\Psi(0)=0,\Psi^{\prime}(1)=B\Psi(1)\Big\}.

Then, system (
19
), and thus system (
18
), can be written as

\displaystyle\dot{\Phi}={\mathcal{A}}\Phi+\mathcal{F}(t,\Phi),\ \ \Phi(0)=\Phi_{0},

(20)

where
\mathcal{F}(t,\Phi):=-\lambda\Phi+F-H_{t}+\alpha H_{xx}-\lambda H
. Hence, it suffices to prove the existence of a solution to system (
20
).

Proposition 4.2
.

System (
20
) admits a unique (classical) solution
\Phi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};{D(\mathcal{A}))}\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{>0};X)
.

Proof:

By virtue of the Lumer-Phillips theorem
[
25
, p. 14, Theorem 4.3]
, we proceed with the proof in two steps.

Step 1:
Show that the operator
\mathcal{A}
is dissipative. Indeed, by the definition of
\mathcal{A}
and the inner product, together with integration by parts, (
19b
), and (
19c
), we obtain

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{X}=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\int_{0}^{1}\alpha(\phi^{(i)})^{\prime\prime}\phi^{(i)}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha\phi^{(i)}(1,t)(\phi^{(i)}(1,t))^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha\phi^{(i)}(0,t)(\phi^{(i)}(0,t))^{\prime}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(i)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha b_{ij}(\phi^{(j)}(1,t))^{2}-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(i)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x.

(21)

Substituting a variation of Agmon’s inequality (see
[
44
, Lemma 1]
) of the form

\displaystyle(\phi^{(i)}(1,t))^{2}\leq 2\int_{0}^{1}(\phi^{(i)})^{2}\text{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(i)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x

into (
4.1
) yields

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{A}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{X}\leq

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha b_{ij}\left(2\int_{0}^{1}(\phi^{(j)})^{2}\text{d}x+\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(j)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x\right)-\sum_{i=1}^{N}\alpha\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(i)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{N}\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{ij}\int_{0}^{1}(\phi^{(j)})^{2}\text{d}x+\alpha\sum_{j=1}^{N}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{N}b_{ij}-1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(j)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 2\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(-l+k_{i+1}\right)\int_{0}^{1}(\phi^{(i)})^{2}\text{d}x+2\alpha\left(-l+k_{1}\right)\int_{0}^{1}(\phi^{(N)})^{2}\text{d}x

\displaystyle+\alpha\sum_{i=1}^{N-1}\left(-l+k_{i+1}-1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(i)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x+\alpha\left(-l+k_{1}-1\right)\int_{0}^{1}\left((\phi^{(N)})^{\prime}\right)^{2}\text{d}x.

Since
k_{i}\in(0,l)
for all
i
, we have
-l+k_{i+1}<0
and
-l+k_{1}<0
. Combining this with
\alpha>0
, we obtain

\langle\mathcal{A}\Phi,\Phi\rangle_{X}\leq 0
.
Thus,
\mathcal{A}
is dissipative.

Step 2:
Prove that the operator
{\mathcal{I}}-{\mathcal{A}}
is surjective. That is, for any
\Gamma\in X
, there exists
\Phi\in{D}(\mathcal{A})
such that

\left(\mathcal{I}-\mathcal{A}\right)\Phi=\Gamma
.
Indeed, from the definition of
\mathcal{A}
, it follows that

\displaystyle-\alpha{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}+{\Phi}=

\displaystyle\Gamma,

(22a)

\displaystyle{\Phi}(0)=

\displaystyle 0,

(22b)

\displaystyle{\Phi}^{\prime}(1)=

\displaystyle B{\Phi}(1).

(22c)

We now turn to consider the solution to the ODE (
22
). We first construct a particular solution that satisfies the left boundary condition. Specifically, we look for a solution of the form

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{p}(x):=-\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sinh(\gamma(x-s))}{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma(s)\text{d}s,

(23)

where
\gamma:=\sqrt{\frac{1}{\alpha}}
. It can be directly verified that
{\Phi}_{p}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\alpha{\Phi}^{\prime\prime}_{p}+{\Phi}_{p}=\Gamma,\ \ {\Phi}_{p}(0)=0.

Therefore, the general solution to (
22
) is given by

\displaystyle{\Phi}(x)={\Phi}_{p}(x)+{\Phi}_{h}(x),

(24)

where the homogeneous term
{\Phi}_{h}
satisfies

\displaystyle-\alpha{\Phi}_{h}^{\prime\prime}+{\Phi}_{h}=0,\ \ \Phi_{h}(0)=0.

It then follows that

\displaystyle{\Phi}_{h}(x)=\sinh(\gamma x)A,

(25)

where
A\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is an unknown constant vector to be determined by the right boundary condition in (
22
). Specifically, at
x=1
, we have

\displaystyle\Phi(1)

\displaystyle=\Phi_{p}(1)+\sinh(\gamma)A,

(26a)

\displaystyle\Phi^{\prime}(1)

\displaystyle=\Phi^{\prime}_{p}(1)+\gamma\cosh(\gamma)A.

(26b)

By the definition of

\Phi_{p}
(see (
23
)), it follows that

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}(1)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\sinh(\gamma(1-s)}{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma(s)\text{d}s,

(27a)

\displaystyle\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(1)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{1}\frac{\cosh(\gamma(1-s))}{\alpha}\Gamma(s)\text{d}s.

(27b)

Substituting (
26
) and (
27
) into (
22c
) yields

\displaystyle(\gamma\cosh(\gamma)I-\sinh(\gamma)B)A=B\Phi_{p}(1)-\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(1).

Let
M:=\gamma\cosh(\gamma)I-\sinh(\gamma)B
. A direct computation yields

\displaystyle\det(M)=\sinh^{N}(\gamma)\left(\left(\gamma\coth(\gamma)+l\right)^{N}-\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}\right).

Since
k_{i}\in(0,l)
, we have
\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}<l^{N}
. Moreover, noting that
\gamma\coth(\gamma)>0
and
\sinh^{N}(\gamma)>0
for
\gamma>0
, it follows that
\left(\gamma\coth(\gamma)+l\right)^{N}>\prod_{i=1}^{N}k_{i}
. Therefore,
\det(M)>0
, which guarantees that the matrix
M
is invertible. It then follows that

\displaystyle A=M^{-1}\left(B\Phi_{p}(1)-\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(1)\right).

(28)

Hence, substituting (
23
), (
25
), and (
28
) into (
24
), we obtain

\displaystyle\Phi(x)=

\displaystyle-\int_{0}^{x}\frac{\sinh(\gamma(x-s))}{\alpha\gamma}\Gamma(s)\text{d}s+\sinh(\gamma x)M^{-1}\left(B\Phi_{p}(1)-\Phi_{p}^{\prime}(1)\right).

Therefore,
I-\mathcal{A}
is surjective.
Together with the dissipativity of
\mathcal{A}
, it follows from the semigroup theory
[
25
, pp. 184, 187, Theorem 1.2 and 1.5]
that
\mathcal{A}
generates a
C_{0}
-semigroup on
X
. Consequently, for any
\Phi_{0}\in D(\mathcal{A})
, equation (
20
) admits a unique solution
\Phi\in C(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0};{D(\mathcal{A}))}\cap C^{1}(\mathbb{R}_{>0};X)
.

\blacksquare

Based on Proposition
4.2
, we now prove Theorem
4.1
.

Proof of Theorem
4.1
:

By virtue of the equivalence between (
18
) and (
20
), we further obtain that the
\widetilde{\nu}
-system admits a solution, thereby guaranteeing that
the
\widetilde{u}^{(i)}
-system admits a solution.

\blacksquare

Step 3:
Establish the well-posedness of the

-system (

) into (

- and

7
Conclusion

For the synchronization of MASs governed by parabolic PDEs with multiple types of disturbances simultaneously, robust synchronization controllers were designed by using the reference
signal and local output information of the agents, enabling all agents to track a prescribed reference trajectory while rejecting observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances and ensuring robustness against the remaining unobservable disturbances without any prior information on their magnitude, dynamics, and structure.
While most existing studies have focused on a single disturbance type or are limited to cases where disturbances are either fully observable or partially known, the coexistence of observable Dirichlet boundary disturbances and unobservable disturbances—including in-domain disturbances and Dirichlet-Robin boundary disturbances without prior information—poses significant challenges for stability assessment. This work addressed these challenges by using the GLM, which allows
systematically handling both observable and unobservable
disturbances. Using this approach, we established robustness properties for the estimation, tracking, and synchronization error systems, along with the ISS of the closed-loop system, thus quantifying the influence of disturbances on synchronization performance.
The control and stability analysis framework developed in this paper will be extended in future work to address more challenging multi-agent control problems, including multi-agent formation control and systems described by complex PDEs under general forms, all in
the presence of various types of disturbances.
