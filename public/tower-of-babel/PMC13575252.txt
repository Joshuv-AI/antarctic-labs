Green finance and technological innovation interact to shape economic growth in China
Authors: Li He, Nurhafiza Abdul Kader Malim, Du Xuyang, Xu Zaijuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the direct effects and the interaction between green finance and technological innovation on China’s economic growth. The empirical analysis draws on a balanced panel of 30 Chinese provinces spanning 2013–2023 and estimates two-way fixed-effects models with heteroskedasticity-robust standard errors to absorb time-invariant provincial characteristics and common macroeconomic shocks. Green finance is measured with a composite indicator combining green bond and green credit, while technological innovation is measured with R&D investment. The estimates point to a positive and statistically significant complementarity: the interaction item equals 0.0737% in the robust fixed-effects model, indicating that technological innovation amplifies the growth returns associated with green financial deepening. Potential endogeneity is mitigated through IV-2SLS, system-GMM and machine-learning-IV, and the interaction effect remains positive across approaches. Heterogeneity tests further suggest that the interaction is most pronounced in eastern region and weaker in western region, consistent with regional disparities in absorptive capacity and financial–innovation infrastructure. Collectively, these findings underscore the value of policy designs that align green financial instruments with technological innovation support to promote high-quality, sustainable growth.Keywords: Green finance, Technological innovation, Economic growth, Panel data, China, interaction effectsSubject terms: Environmental economics, Sustainability

Introduction

The COVID-19 pandemic, coupled with the global economic downturn, led to a significant reduction in demand for renewable energy and green initiatives during 2020–20211. During the pandemic and the subsequent financial recession, fossil fuel prices, particularly for natural gas, declined considerably2. As a result, lower fossil fuel prices impeded the growth of renewable energy sources like solar and wind, whose levelized costs remain higher than those of conventional energy3. A decline in investor interest has hampered the efforts of the Intergovernmental Panel on Climate Change (IPCC) and the progress toward achieving the Sustainable Development Goals (SDGs). Green finance has emerged as a central topic in contemporary economic discourse4. Given that many enterprises require substantial capital investment for infrastructure and operations, substantial bank loans are often required5. Green initiatives are frequently associated with high initial investment and limited short-term returns during the early stages of R&D6.

Over the past decade, China has experienced robust industrial growth, with an average annual GDP growth rate of approximately 6.5% between 2013 and 20237. However, this growth has been accompanied by escalating environmental degradation, emphasizing the imperative for a transition to a more sustainable economy. As part of China’s commitment to sustainability under global frameworks such as the Paris Agreement, green finance, through instruments such as green bonds and green credit, has played a significant role in financing low-carbon projects and renewable energy investments8. However, this shift presents substantial challenges, as China’s traditional industrial practices have long been associated with high emissions, natural resource depletion, and ecological damage9. Despite the rapid expansion of China’s green finance market, with green bond issuance reaching a record high of USD 131.25 billion in 2023, its long-term impact on fostering sustainable growth remains a subject of ongoing evaluation10. This market expansion is closely linked to the institutionalisation of China’s green finance architecture since the 2016 Guidelines for Establishing the Green Financial System, which provided a national framework for mobilising private capital towards green sectors and constraining investment in polluting activities11. Recent regulatory coordination has further strengthened taxonomy alignment across green financial products, improving comparability and market confidence4,12,13.

Technological innovation, especially through R&D investment, is a critical driver of this transformation14. Addressing this challenge requires a thorough examination of the roles that green finance and technological innovation play in promoting economic growth and sustainability. This study aims to fill a gap in the existing literature through an empirical analysis of the influence of green finance on China’s economic growth, while also examining the interaction effects of technological innovation, measured by R&D investment.

Green finance, through instruments like green bonds and green credit, is widely acknowledged as a key mechanism for promoting sustainable development by funding environmentally conscious projects15,16. This study aims to evaluate the impact of green finance on China’s economic growth and examine how technological innovation, specifically R&D investment, interacts with green finance in shaping economic outcomes. By addressing the research gap regarding the interaction effects of green finance and technological innovation, this study contributes to a deeper understanding of China’s sustainable development dynamics.

Technological innovation serves as a fundamental catalyst for economic growth, especially when integrated with green finance17. Despite growing attention, the interaction between green finance and technological innovation is still insufficiently explored in the context of China’s economic development. While existing research highlights the positive role of green finance in fostering sustainable initiatives, limited empirical evidence exists on how its interaction with technological innovation influences long-term economic growth in China18. This highlights the necessity of examining whether technological innovation amplifies the positive effects of green finance or if other complex factors are at play13. This study aims to explore how technological innovation, measured by R&D investment, moderates the relationship between green finance and economic growth.

This study offers three key contributions to the field. First, it conducts the first statistical analysis of the interconnected effects of China’s GDP, the advancement of green finance, and technological innovation quantified through R&D investment which providing novel insights into their combined impact on economic growth. Second, by leveraging panel data from 30 Chinese provinces over the 2013–2023 period, this research captures regional disparities in economic and social development, providing a comprehensive assessment of the differential impacts of green finance and innovation across China. Employing fixed effects models with robust standard errors, the study ensures robustness and reliability in identifying relationships among key variables. Furthermore, it provides empirically grounded policy recommendations to facilitate China’s economic growth through a strategic alignment of green finance and technological innovation. These policy suggestions are crucial in light of China’s constrained energy resources and significant environmental challenges resulting from intensive resource consumption, and supports China’s progress toward achieving the SDGs. By addressing the interactive dynamics between green finance and technological innovation, this study fills a critical gap in the literature while also providing a framework for fostering sustainable economic development in China.

Literature review

Green finance, through mechanisms such as green bonds and green credit, provides essential funding for environmentally friendly projects, overcoming financial barriers and encouraging investments in green technologies5,19. In turn, this facilitates economic growth by promoting sustainable development initiatives that contribute to long-term economic stability20. Green finance’s ability to address both economic and environmental objectives simultaneously has been well-documented in the literature, emphasizing its critical role in facilitating the transition to a low-carbon economy21.

Endogenous growth theory, originally developed by scholars such as Helpman22 and later expanded by Greene23, emphasizes that economic growth is primarily driven by internal factors such as innovation, knowledge accumulation, and capital investment elements that are crucial for leveraging green finance to foster sustainable economic development. This theoretical framework provides a relevant lens for analysing the impact of green finance on China’s economic trajectory, as endogenous growth theory underscores how sustainable finance can catalyse growth through innovation and resource efficiency24. The intersection between green finance and economic growth has gained increasing attention, particularly as China strives to align its economic expansion with environmental sustainability. Although studies such as Ngo et al. have identified a positive association between green finance and economic development across ASEAN nations that suggesting similar potential in China with a comprehensive empirical analysis specific to China’s unique regional and structural economic factors is still lacking25. Similarly, Yang & Masron26 underscore China’s pivotal role in Asia’s green finance landscape and its influence on regional economies. However, a notable gap persists in understanding how China’s domestic green finance mechanisms contribute to long-term economic growth. This study aims to fill this gap by examining the dynamic interaction between green finance and sustainable economic growth in China, offering valuable insights into effective strategies for fostering sustainability within China’s unique economic context.

Moreover, technological innovation is widely recognized as a fundamental driver of economic growth27. In particular, R&D investment plays a crucial role in enhancing productivity, improving energy efficiency, and advancing green technologies, all of which are critical to maximizing the impact of green finance on sustainable growth28. Empirical evidence indicates that regions with higher levels of technological innovation tend to experience faster economic growth, as innovation facilitates the adoption of cutting-edge technologies, improving resource efficiency and reducing carbon emissions29. This relationship between technological innovation and economic growth has been extensively examined across developing and developed economies, where innovation has consistently had a positive impact on growth by driving productivity and technological advancement30.

System dynamics theory offers a robust framework for analysing complex economic systems, focusing on the dynamic interactions and feedback mechanisms that influence sustainable growth31. This study builds on system dynamics theory to examine the interaction between green finance and technological innovation as key drivers of sustainable economic growth, focusing on China’s evolving economic landscape. While existing research emphasizes the individual benefits of green finance and technological innovation, few studies have empirically quantified their combined impact within China’s unique economic context32. The interplay between these factors can serve as a catalyst for economic growth, as green finance provides critical resources for innovation-driven projects, while technological advancements enhance the effectiveness and efficiency of green initiatives. Empirical evidence indicates that their integrated impact on sustainable growth surpasses their standalone contributions, promoting environmental sustainability and fostering progress toward achieving the SDGs, particularly in economies prioritizing green development8.

Endogenous growth theory highlights that sustained growth depends on knowledge accumulation and innovation investment. In this context, green finance can affect growth through at least three channels. First, it reallocates capital toward cleaner and more productive technologies, improving resource efficiency and raising long-run output. Second, it relaxes financing constraints for green and R&D-intensive projects, lowering the cost of capital for innovation and enabling longer investment horizons. Third, system dynamics logic implies feedback loops: green finance accelerates technology deployment and learning effects; improved technological capability then increases the return to green investment, reinforcing the finance–innovation–growth linkage. These mechanisms motivate the interaction hypothesis: the marginal growth effect of green finance should rise with innovation intensity.

However, the literature presents inconclusive findings regarding the scale and consistency of these effects. While most studies report a positive link between green finance, technological innovation, and economic performance, several challenges remain, particularly concerning the adequacy of green finance flows and the pace of technological adoption33. The effectiveness of green finance is strongly contingent upon a country’s technological innovation levels. Without robust R&D investment, the full potential of green finance for economic growth could go unrealized, especially in China’s fast-paced, resource-intensive economy. This study aims to address this gap by exploring how these factors drive sustainable growth in China, with a particular emphasis on the mechanisms that enhance or constrain their combined effects within the system dynamics theory framework.

In summary, the literature indicates that green finance and technological innovation play a crucial role in promoting sustainable economic growth34,35. Although both factors independently contribute to growth, their interaction can significantly enhance their overall impact. This study aims to extend the existing literature by empirically investigating the combined effect of green finance and technological innovation on China’s economic growth. Panel data regression analysis typically employs three primary models: Pooled Ordinary Least Squares (POLS), Fixed Effects (FE), and Random Effects (RE). These models are used to capture both within-entity (intra-group) and between-entity (inter-group) variations across time and space41–43. The POLS model estimates parameters using ordinary least squares (OLS), minimizing the sum of squared residuals. The estimated coefficients reflect the average effect of the explanatory variables on the dependent variable across all cross-sectional units and time periods44. Under the assumption of parameter homogeneity and absence of omitted variable bias, POLS yields consistent, efficient, and unbiased estimates45.

In contrast, the Random Effects (RE) model provides a flexible approach to panel data analysis, particularly under the assumption that unobserved individual-specific effects are uncorrelated with the explanatory variables. By incorporating both within-entity and between-entity variations, the RE model produces efficient and consistent estimates, provided that its assumptions hold, thus making it a valuable tool in applied econometric research46. The Fixed Effects (FE) model emphasizes within-entity variation, making it particularly effective in addressing omitted variable bias stemming from time-invariant unobserved heterogeneity across entities23. Widely applied in empirical research, the FE model controls for unobserved heterogeneity by including entity-specific fixed effects, thereby removing the influence of all time-invariant factors47,48.

Detecting heteroskedasticity is a critical component of regression diagnostics, as its presence may result in inefficient estimators and unreliable statistical inference. The Breusch–Pagan test is a widely employed diagnostic procedure used to assess whether the variance of residuals from the Pooled Ordinary Least Squares (POLS) model remains constant across observations49,50. Ensuring homoskedastic residuals enhances the efficiency and reliability of POLS estimates, which is essential for accurately evaluating the effects of green finance (proxied by green bonds and green credit) and technological innovation (proxied by R&D investment) on economic growth (measured by GDP). The validity of statistical inferences drawn from the POLS model hinges on the assumption of homoskedastic residuals. The application of the Breusch–Pagan test enables researchers to assess the validity of this assumption, thereby enhancing the credibility and robustness of the empirical findings51,52.

The Hausman test, introduced by Hausman (1978), is a fundamental statistical tool for model specification in panel data analysis. It assesses whether the fixed effects (FE) or random effects (RE) model provides consistent and efficient estimates for a given panel dataset53. Specifically, the test examines whether unobserved individual-specific effects are correlated with the regressors an essential criterion for determining model consistency54. A statistically significant test result leads to the rejection of the null hypothesis, indicating that the FE model is preferable, as it accounts for potential correlation between unobserved heterogeneity and explanatory variables55.

To mitigate potential confounding factors and address endogeneity issues, this study employs a fixed-effects model with robust standard errors. These methods aim to distinguish causal relationships from spurious correlations, thereby enhancing the reliability and validity of the findings56. Across all models, control variables such as money supply, fiscal expenditure, inflation rate, fixed asset investment, and industrial structure are included in accordance with the principles of endogenous growth theory and system dynamics theory22,31.

Data sources

The dataset employed in this study comprises panel data from 30 Chinese provinces spanning the period 2013 to 2023. The primary data sources include the China Statistical Yearbook, the National Bureau of Statistics, and the China Banking and Insurance Regulatory Commission. The dependent variable, economic growth, is measured by GDP, while the primary independent variables are green finance represented by green bonds and green credit and R&D investment, which serves as the interaction term. Control variables include money supply, fiscal expenditure, inflation rate, fixed asset investment, and industrial structure.

The baseline dataset is a balanced provincial panel covering 30 mainland provincial-level regions in China from 2013 to 2023 (330 observations). Hong Kong, Macao, Taiwan, and Tibet are excluded due to non-comparability in statistical reporting or missing province-consistent series. to maintain sample consistency and homogeneity57. No additional provinces are removed. The 2013–2023 window is selected for data consistency and policy relevance: it aligns with the period in which province-level series for green credit, green bond activity, and R&D investment can be assembled under harmonized statistical standards. While economic growth is a long-run process, the panel still supports credible identification of within-province relationships using province and year fixed effects.

In conclusion, this study employs advanced panel data econometric techniques to investigate the interaction between green finance and technological innovation, as well as their combined impact on China’s economic growth. By examining both short- and long-term effects, this research provides critical insights into sustainable development goals and economic resilience.

Estimation results and robustness analysis

To enhance the robustness of the findings, diagnostic tests were performed to assess multicollinearity, heteroskedasticity, and serial correlation63. The variance inflation factor (VIF) test confirms the absence of severe multicollinearity issues, as the mean VIF values are below the commonly accepted threshold of 1064. Additionally, the Breusch-Pagan test for heteroskedasticity and the Durbin-Watson test for serial correlation were conducted to assess the presence of these econometric concerns65. While multicollinearity and serial correlation are not concerns in these models, heteroskedasticity is present and must be corrected through the application of robust standard errors to ensure accurate and reliable results.

To strengthen confidence in the estimates, we implement a battery of robustness texts covering sample sensitivity, variable definitions, specification choices, and temporal stability66–69. First, we limit the influence of outliers: we winsorise all continuous variables at the 1st and 99th percentiles. Column (1) of Table 8 indicates that the estimates are essentially unchanged: the green finance coefficient is 0.0928, and the interaction term is 0.0729, with both remaining statistically significant at conventional levels. This suggests that the core findings are not driven by a small number of extreme observations. Second, we examine sensitivity to sample composition and exclude specific province categories in turn. Excluding the four municipalities, including Beijing, Shanghai, Tianjin and Chongqing that yields a slightly larger green-finance effect of 0.0956 (Column 2), reflecting their distinct administrative and economic profiles. Endogeneity and heterogeneity checks

To address potential endogeneity, this study estimates an instrumental-variables specification using two-stage least squares (2SLS)70–73. The second-stage IV–2SLS estimates (Table 11, Column 1) exceed the OLS and fixed-effects coefficients: the green finance coefficient rises to 0.124, and the interaction term to 0.0891, with both remaining highly significant (p < 0.01). This pattern suggests that OLS estimates may be downward biased, potentially reflecting measurement error or omitted factors that attenuate the estimated effects. The IV estimates, after accounting for endogeneity, suggest a larger causal contribution of green finance to economic growth than the fixed-effects results imply. The system-GMM estimates (Table 11, Column 2) are consistent with the main results. The lagged dependent variable enters significantly (β = 0.734, p < 0.01), indicating substantial persistence in growth. Green finance remains positive (β = 0.0897, p < 0.05), and the interaction term is also positive and significant (β = 0.0682, p < 0.01), although the magnitudes are slightly smaller than in the fixed-effects specification. The smaller system-GMM coefficients may reflect its reliance on differenced moment conditions and within-province variation, which can yield more conservative estimates74,75. The consistency of signs and statistical significance across estimators supports a more credible causal interpretation. In line with recent advances in causal inference, we implement a machine-learning-assisted instrumental-variable approach as an additional robustness assessment of the endogeneity strategy76–80. Table 11, Column 3, reports the Machine Learning–assisted Instrumental Variable (ML–IV) estimates. The first-stage F-statistics (21.45/5.12) substantially exceed the Stock–Yogo threshold of 10, indicating strong instrument relevance for the machine-learning-predicted components. Across methods, the results consistently indicate that: (1) green finance exerts a positive causal effect on economic growth, (2) technological innovation amplifies this effect through a complementary interaction, and (3) the estimated effects are economically meaningful and statistically robust.

Discussion

This study finds a significant positive effect of green finance on economic growth in China, with a 1% increase in green finance being associated with a 0.0931% rise in GDP, underscoring the critical role of green finance in driving sustainable economic development. This result is consistent with previous studies highlighting the pivotal role of green financial instruments, such as green bonds and green credit, in directing resources to environmentally sustainable projects81,82. Similar studies in OECD countries have reported comparable positive effects on sustainable growth, although the magnitude of the impact varies, reflecting differences in economic structures and green finance maturity across regions. This variation likely stems from China’s rapid industrialization and the evolving stage of its green finance market, highlighting the potential of green finance to address diverse environmental and economic challenges in various regions83.

Furthermore, the study reveals that the interaction between green finance and technological innovation substantially boosts economic growth, resulting in a 0.0737% increase in GDP. System dynamics theory posits that this interaction forms a positive feedback loop, with green finance fostering innovation-driven projects and technological advancements that enhance the efficiency of green projects31, particularly in high energy-consuming sectors84. This finding suggests that green finance alone may fall short of its full potential without simultaneous technological advancements, reinforcing the importance of feedback loops in promoting sustainable growth85.

However, the literature presents mixed findings regarding the consistency and magnitude of green finance effects, with some studies noting varying levels of impact based on regional and economic factors. While most studies report a positive relationship between green finance and economic growth, challenges remain in regions with limited R&D investment or poor integration of green finance, thus limiting its effectiveness33. The variation across Chinese provinces highlights that regional disparities in innovation and financial infrastructure can significantly affect the effectiveness of green finance. Economically advanced provinces respond more strongly to green finance due to more developed R&D capacities and financial systems, whereas less developed regions often lack these essential resources. These findings suggest that region-specific policy interventions, such as targeted R&D grants, could help underdeveloped regions better leverage green finance, thereby promoting more equitable economic and environmental progress nationwide.

In summary, the study provides actionable policy recommendations to accelerate China’s economic growth through the strategic alignment of green finance with technological innovation. Targeted subsidies for green finance instruments, like green bonds, in underdeveloped regions could help reduce regional disparities and increase access to sustainable investment. Collaborative R&D grants for green technology in energy-intensive industries could further enhance synergies between green finance and innovation, thereby supporting China’s transition to sustainable growth. These policies are crucial given China’s limited energy resources and pressing environmental challenges resulting from intensive resource consumption.

Conclusion

This study empirically analyses the relationship between green finance, technological innovation, and economic growth in China, utilizing panel data from 30 provinces spanning the period 2013 to 2023. Econometric techniques, including the fixed effects (FE) model with robust standard errors, are used to assess the individual and joint effects of green finance and technological innovation on economic growth. The findings reveal how green finance and technological innovation together drive development in China’s economy.

Using panel data for 30 Chinese provinces (2013–2023) and two-way fixed effects, the estimates indicate a consistent complementarity between green finance and technological innovation. The interaction term between green finance and R&D investment is positive and statistically significant in FE specifications, implying that provinces with stronger innovation capacity translate green finance expansion into higher economic output more effectively. In contrast, the standalone coefficient of green finance is sensitive to specification and inference choices, and becomes weaker once conservative robust standard errors are applied, suggesting that the growth contribution of green finance is largely conditional on innovation intensity rather than uniform across provinces.

The empirical findings align with the theoretical frameworks of system dynamics and endogenous growth theories, confirming that green finance and technological innovation are interdependent and mutually reinforcing drivers of sustainable economic growth in China24,86. The results show that green finance serves as a catalyst for economic expansion, in line with the core principles of endogenous growth theory. Moreover, the interaction between green finance and technological innovation further promotes economic growth, consistent with the fundamental principles of system dynamics theory.

The findings offer critical policy implications for China’s sustainable growth and progress toward the SDGs. First, the positive effect of green finance on economic growth underscores the need to expand green financial instruments, such as green bonds and green credit, while also extending support to underdeveloped regions to reduce innovation disparities. Furthermore, providing tax incentives for green finance investments could speed up the nationwide adoption of sustainable finance. Establishing regional innovation hubs dedicated to green technologies would foster synergies between green finance and technological innovation, enhancing their combined impact on economic growth. Such targeted interventions are expected to improve the effectiveness of green finance across regions, facilitating China’s low-carbon transition.

This study provides robust empirical evidence that green finance and technological innovation are critical catalysts for China’s economic expansion. It is recommended that policymakers expand green financial instruments, including green bonds, and allocate targeted funding for R&D in green technologies to promote sustainable growth. Policy interventions should encourage local governments to integrate green finance mechanisms, especially in underdeveloped regions where the benefits remain underutilized. The findings emphasize that green finance, particularly green bonds and credit, plays a crucial role in promoting sustainable development. Additionally, technological innovation amplifies the effectiveness of green finance, underscoring the need to strengthen R&D investment initiatives to achieve long-term economic resilience and environmental sustainability.

In addition, several limitations warrant careful consideration. First, green finance is measured using a composite index based on green bonds and green credit, which may not fully capture policy quality, taxonomy alignment, or capital-allocation efficiency. Second, technological innovation is represented using R&D investment; alternative measures such as patents or innovation outputs may capture additional dimensions. Third, provincial panels may exhibit cross-sectional dependence driven by nationwide shocks; second-generation tests and dependence-robust inference can reduce, but cannot fully remove, this concern. Fourth, the study relies on macro-level data, which limits the identification of micro-level mechanisms; future work can test firm- or industry-level channels and implement stronger identification strategies. Although this study focuses on China, the mechanisms discussed may be relevant to other emerging economies that seek to align financial deepening with innovation-led green transformation.
