On the Extension Theorem for Packing Steiner Forests
Jinghan A Zeng

Abstract
We consider the problem of packing edge-disjoint Steiner forests in a graph. The input consists of a multi-graph G = ( V , E ) G=(V,E) and a collection of h h vertex subsets 𝒮 = { S 1 , S 2 , … , S h } \mathcal{S}=\{S_{1},S_{2},\ldots,S_{h}\} . A Steiner forest for 𝒮 \mathcal{S} , also called an 𝒮 \mathcal{S} -forest, is a forest of G G in which each S i S_{i} is connected. In the case where h = 1 h=1 , this is the Steiner Tree packing problem. Kriesell’s conjecture postulates that 2 ​ k 2k -edge-connectivity of S 1 S_{1} is sufficient to find k k edge-disjoint S 1 S_{1} -trees. Lau [ 6 ] showed that 24 ​ k 24k -edge-connectivity suffices for the Steiner Tree packing problem, which was improved to 6.5 ​ k 6.5k by West and Wu [ 9 ] and 5 ​ k + 4 5k+4 by Devos, McDonald and Pivotto [ 2 ] . In his thesis, Lau [ 6 ] asserts that for the Steiner Forest problem, if each S i S_{i} is 30 ​ k 30k -edge-connected in G G , then there exist k k edge-disjoint 𝒮 \mathcal{S} -forests. However, Lau’s proof relies on an intermediate theorem called the Extension Theorem, which in this paper we will demonstrate has a gap by providing a counterexample to Lau’s Extension Theorem. Furthermore, we will resolve this gap by correcting Lau’s proof to show that 36 ​ k 36k -edge-connectivity of each S i S_{i} suffices to pack k k 𝒮 \mathcal{S} -forests. More careful analysis yields that 35 ​ k 35k -edge-connectivity of each S i S_{i} is sufficient when k ≥ 8 k\geq 8 .

Let

[

of

S

S
(a minimal subgraph connecting

is

-edge-connected in

then there exist
k
edge-disjoint
S
-trees in
G

[
3
]
. This conjecture is still open, however much progress has been achieved starting with the important work of Lau
[

,

]
. Lau showed that it suffices for

to be
24k
-edge-connected to guarantee
k
-edge-disjoint
S
-trees. West and Wu
[

]
showed that

-edge-connectivity suffices. Devos, McDonald and Pivotto further improved this bound to
(5k+4)

[

]
.

We now consider the problem of packing Steiner forests which is a further generalization. Let

be a collection of vertex-disjoint subsets. An

-forest is a subgraph of

in which each

is connected (a minimal such subgraph will be a forest). Kriesell’s conjecture admits a natural extension to this setting: if each

-edge-disjoint

]

Furthermore, we will also remedy the gap in Lau’s proof to obtain the following result:

Theorem 1.1
.

Let

Preliminaries

We begin this section with some basic definitions. A set

on a graph is said to be

edges on

-edge-connected is equivalent to being able to find

edge-disjoint paths between any two vertices in

. An
S
-subgraph is a subgraph

of

such that

is connected in

. An

-subgraph is a subgraph

of

is connected in

-subgraph is referred to as a

, a minimal

.

To find

disjoint Steiner Forests in a graph, both Lau’s approach and our approach involve packing Steiner Trees on some

, recursively packing Steiner Forests on the rest of the graph, and merging the two together to find a packing of
k
Steiner Forests. To formalize this idea, we will need to define notions of

and an

, which we do below.

Definition 2.1
.

Given a graph

1.

For all

P_{i}\subseteq E(H_{i})
.

2.

For all

is not a cut vertex of

.

We will define an operation called the

Take a graph

Take some set

The proof for Steiner forest uses a similar idea to this lemma, where we cut the graph into two components
C_{1},C_{2}
where
C_{1}
is "small." We inductively pack
k
Steiner Forests on
C_{2}+v_{1}
, which will induce an edge subpartition of
v_{1}
, which we extend to
k
edge disjoint Steiner trees on
C_{1}
before using the edge-union operation to merge the packings together. In order to do this, we will need a theorem that allows us to pack Steiner Trees which extend a (sub)partition on a vertex. This is the so-called
Extension Theorem
. For this theorem to work, we need to assume that the partition on the vertex we extend is
balanced
, which we define below.

Definition 2.2
.

Given a vertex
v
, a partition of
E(v)=P_{1}\sqcup P_{2}...\sqcup P_{k}
into
k
parts is balanced if for all
i
,
|P_{i}|\geq 2
, where
E(v)
denotes the edges incident to
v
.

Definition 2.3
.

Given a vertex
v
, a subpartition of
P_{1}\sqcup P_{2}...\sqcup P_{k}\subseteq E(v)
into
k
parts, where a part is allowed to be the empty set, is called a balanced subpartition if there exists a balanced partition
E(v)=P_{1}^{\prime}\sqcup P_{2}^{\prime}...\sqcup P_{k}^{\prime}
such that for all
i
,
P_{i}\subseteq P_{i}^{\prime}
.

In other words, a subpartition of
v
is balanced if it can be extended to a balanced partition of
v
. If an edge
e\in E(v)
is part of
P_{i}
for some (sub)partition, we say that the (sub)partition assigns
e
a
label
of
i
.

Definition 2.4
.

Given a graph
G
, a collection of
k
edge disjoint subgraphs
H_{1},H_{2},...,H_{k}
of
G
, we say that the
H_{1},H_{2},...,H_{k}
balance
v
if the edge subpartition
E(v)\cap H_{1}
,
E(v)\cap H_{2}
,…,
E(v)\cap H_{k}
is a balanced edge subpartition of
v
. We say that
H_{1},H_{2},...,H_{k}
balance a subset of vertices

if they balance all vertices in
S
.

We now state the attempt at proving an Extension Theorem in Lau’s thesis
[
6
]
. The following theorem was used to prove the bound of
30k
for Steiner Forest Packing. Unfortunately, there is a mistake in this theorem which we elaborate on here.

Conjecture 2.1

(Lau’s Extension Theorem)
.

Let
G
be a loopless multigraph, with
S,R\subseteq V(G)
such that
S\cap R=\emptyset
,
S
is
Qk
-edge-connected and for all
r\in R
, the degree of
r
is at least
Qk
, where
Q\geq 30
. Given a vertex
v
and a subpartition
P_{k}(v)
of
E(v)
into
k
parts, there are
k
edge-disjoint
S
-subgraphs that extend
v
and balance
S\cup R
if either the following is true.

1.

v\in S
,
d(v)=Qk
, and
P_{k}(v)
is a balanced edge subpartition of
E(v)
.

2.

N(v)\subseteq S\cup R
,
d(v)\leq Qk
,
v\not\in S\cup R
, and there is no edge cut of size at most
Qk
that breaks
G
into
C_{1},C_{2}
, where
S\subseteq C_{2}
,
v\in C_{1}
, and
R\cap C_{1}\not=\emptyset
.

In simpler terms, for us to apply this Extension Theorem, either balancing at
v
is needed or the neighborhood of
v
must be contained in
S\cup R
, along with some other conditions that need to be fulfilled. Unlike the Extension Theorem used for Steiner Tree packing (see Theorem
3.1
and
4.4
for examples), balancing at
R
is needed for the Steiner Forest problem. This is because when packing Steiner Forests on
C_{2}+v_{1}
which we extend on
C_{1}+v_{2}
, we want the packing to balance
v_{1}
to ensure that the partition we extend on
v_{2}
is balanced. Lau’s proof reduces this problem down to when
v_{1}
is of degree exactly
Qk
and hence can be in
R
, allowing for the balancing of
v_{2}
, otherwise, one can ensure that the neighborhood of
v_{2}
is contained in
S\cup R
and the other parts of the second condition are fulfilled.

However, satisfying the second condition is not always sufficient for an extension of
k
edge disjoint
S
-subgraphs to exist. We will disprove the above claim with a counterexample. We note that our counterexample is essentially the same as the one given by DeVos, McDonald, and Pivotto
[
2
]
in their paper to justify why balancing is needed for the Extension Theorem.

Lemma 2.2
.

Given any integer
Q\geq 30
, there exists a loopless multigraph
G
with
S\subseteq V(G)
,
Qk
-edge-connected, all vertices in
R\subseteq V(G)
have degree at least
Qk
and
S\cap R=\emptyset
, a vertex
v
with a subpartition
P_{k}(v)
of
E(v)
that satisfies the second condition of Lau’s Extension Theorem, but does not have
k
edge disjoint
S
-subgraphs that extend
P_{k}(v)
.

Proof.

Take two cliques
A,B
, both of which are cliques on exactly
Qk+1
vertices. Add

edges arbitrarily between
A
and
B
, and set
S=A\cup B
. Select
Qk-k+1
edges arbitrarily out of the edges between
A
and
B
, and denote the set of these of edges
X
, and the set of the other edges between
A
and
B
as
Y
. Subdivide each edge in
X
, and add a vertex
v
, which has one edge going to each of the new vertices formed by the subdivision of
X
. Add
\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor
edges to
v
going to vertices in
A
(not necessarily distinct) arbitrarily, and add
\lceil(k-1)/2\rceil
edges to
v
going to vertices in
B
(not necessarily distinct) arbitrarily. Add self-loops to the vertices that subdivide
X
so that all of them have degree at least
Qk
, and subdivide the self loops themselves to ensure the graph is loopless. Designate all the vertices that subdivide
X
to be in
R
. Note that

is now

-edge-connected, as for any
a\in A
and
b\in B
,

edge disjoint paths can be formed between
a
and
b
using

, and
\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor
other edge disjoint paths can be formed from

to

by traversing through

. Furthermore, by construction, the degree of

is exactly

.

Assume, for contradiction there is a cut of

of size at most

into

such that

,
S\subseteq C_{2}
, and
R\cap C_{1}\not=\emptyset
. Let
R\cap C_{1}=R_{1}
and
R\cap C_{2}=R_{2}
. Then,

has

edges incident to a vertex in
S
or a vertex in
R_{2}
, which all must be part of the cut. Take any vertex in
r\in R_{1}
, and note that

has two neighbors in
S
, hence both edges between

and
S
must also be part of the cut. This means that the cut has at least of
k-1+|R_{2}|+2|R_{1}|=Qk+|R_{1}|>Qk
, since

must be nonempty, a contradiction. This means that this graph satisfies the second condition of Lau’s Extension Theorem.

For all edges going from
v
to

, designate them to have a label of
1
. For the edges going from

to

, designate them to have labels

. Finally, for the edges going from

to

, designate them to have labels

. Note that for any
H_{i}
where

to connect

and

, at least one edge in

must be used, because it is impossible to use the subdivided edges in

, as they must be used in

to connect some

to

or

. However, note that
|Y|=Qk-\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor-(Qk-k+1)=k-\lfloor(k-1)/2\rfloor-1<k-1
as long as

. Hence, it is impossible to find

that extend

, contradicting the theorem of Lau, as desired.
∎

Lau’s proof of the Extension Theorem is an induction proof that began by deleting vertices and edges that did not affect the connectivity of
S
. Unfortunately, one flaw with this approach is that it does not account for the fact that these deletions can make the degree of a vertex in
R
fall below
Qk
, hence removing the vertex from
R
and creating complications when recursing. To remedy this, we give and prove our own version of the Extension Theorem, which we will use to prove a bound of
36k
for Steiner Forest Packing.

In lieu of requiring the Extension Theorem to be true for a case similar to the second case in Lau’s Extension Theorem, we drop the requirement for a vertex to be in
R
from being incident to
Qk
edges to
(Q-2)k
edges. Even though self loops do not affect the connectivity of the graph, we still allow the graph to have them to make induction easier. We will prove the Extension Theorem for when
S
is
36k
-edge-connected, but more careful analysis can yield a bound of
35k
when
k\geq 8
.

, we can get a better bound of

(West-Wu’s Extension Theorem

)

and
S_{2}\subseteq G_{2}
Contract

into a vertex

and
G_{2}
into a vertex

. By the West-Wu’s Extension theorem (Theorem

Theorem 4.1

(Extension Theorem)

Suppose there is a multigraph

-edge-connected in

-subgraphs that balance

Suppose, for contradiction, that this theorem is false. Take a counterexample

where the sum of the vertices and the edges that are not self loops is minimized. We may assume without loss of generality, that each label

appears at least twice on

Definition 4.1
.

An

Definition 4.2

(West-Wu

]

A

is at least 0.

We now give our proof of Lemma

Proof.

Take a graph

with

-edge-connected on

and

of
