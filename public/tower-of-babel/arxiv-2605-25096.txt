Fractal-based variable drag model for porous-media tree representations Preprint: AIP/123-QED
T. Tokiwa, Y. Yin, R. Onishi

Abstract
Accurate representation of trees is essential for predictive urban micrometeorological simulations. However, because explicitly resolving detailed tree geometry is computationally prohibitive, trees are commonly represented as porous media. Within this framework, conventional approaches typically prescribe a constant drag coefficient, even when spatially heterogeneous area-density distributions are introduced. This limits transferability across inflow conditions and can increase sensitivity to grid resolution, particularly in the grayscale regime where a tree is represented by only a few computational cells. In this study, we propose a fractal-based variable-drag framework for porous-media tree representations, in which the drag coefficient is prescribed cell-wise as C D = C D ​ ( n eff , R ​ e eff ) C_{D}=C_{D}(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}}) . Here, n eff n_{\mathrm{eff}} denotes the cell-effective branching order, which represents unresolved local morphological complexity, and R ​ e eff Re_{\mathrm{eff}} denotes the cell-effective Reynolds number, which represents the local flow condition. The framework is assessed using steady Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes simulations of a porous fractal tree over systematic sweeps of grid resolution and inflow velocity. Model performance is evaluated primarily using aerodynamic porosity, which measures the bulk momentum attenuation induced by the tree. The proposed model produces a plausible aerodynamic response, including velocity deficit, bypass flow, and wake recovery around the porous tree region. Compared with conventional porous-tree models using constant drag coefficients, the proposed formulation improves robustness to grid resolution and captures the global inflow-velocity dependence of the bulk drag effect without empirical retuning. Notably, this whole-tree response is recovered through local cell-wise quantities, n eff n_{\mathrm{eff}} and R ​ e eff Re_{\mathrm{eff}} . These results demonstrate that incorporating morphology- and flow-dependent drag into the cell-wise drag coefficient provides a practical route to improving porous-media tree modeling. Future work includes extension of the framework to unsteady simulations such as large-eddy simulation, and ultimately to multiple-tree and district-scale urban micrometeorological applications.



I
Introduction

In recent years, accurate urban micrometeorological forecasting has become increasingly important for urban environmental assessment and planning, particularly in the context of accelerating urban population growth, intensifying heat-related risks, and the development of smart societies
32
;
12
;
26
;
31
. Meeting this demand requires physically credible representations of the processes that shape the urban atmosphere, including the effects of vegetation.

Trees play a vital role in regulating urban micrometeorology by modulating wind flow, temperature distribution, and pollutant dispersion
10
;
14
;
24
. These impacts are governed by aerodynamic interactions between the flow and the multi-scale architecture of trees, through which trees extract momentum and alter turbulence. Consequently, a reliable description of tree-induced aerodynamic resistance is a prerequisite for predictive simulations of urban ventilation, scalar transport, and related microclimatic conditions.

For practical urban-scale computations, however, resolving detailed tree geometry is rarely feasible. Tree representations in CFD can be viewed as a resolution-dependent hierarchy: (i) explicit simulations resolve branch and trunk geometry and offer high-fidelity reference solutions, albeit at high computational cost
34
;
33
; (ii) implicit approaches parameterize vegetation effects as fully subgrid forcing
3
;
16
; and (iii) an increasingly common intermediate regime represents a tree with only a few grid cells, referred to here as grayscale simulations
21
;
7
;
8
. As computational resources improve, grayscale simulations are becoming more relevant for real-world applications, where fully resolved simulations remain too expensive while fully homogenized single-cell representations are overly restrictive.

In both Reynolds-averaged Navier–Stokes (RANS) and large-eddy simulation (LES) frameworks, implicit and grayscale approaches typically model trees as porous media, i.e., distributed momentum sinks. In such models, the canopy effect is commonly expressed through a drag term proportional to a local area density and a drag coefficient, e.g.,
\mathbf{S}_{u}\propto-C_{D}\alpha|\mathbf{u}|\mathbf{u}
, where
\alpha(\mathbf{x})
denotes an area-density field such as plant or leaf area density (PAD/LAD). A common conventional practice is to prescribe spatially uniform area density over the tree cells together with a constant drag coefficient
C_{D}
, or to tune a constant
C_{D}
on a case-by-case basis, as in baseline porous-canopy implementations
18
;
7
;
8
. More recently, advanced conventional models have incorporated grid- or voxel-resolved PAD/LAD distributions derived from geometry or measurements to better represent spatial heterogeneity
21
;
36
;
25
, while still retaining a constant
C_{D}
. These developments have improved the geometric realism of porous-tree representations. However, the drag coefficient itself is still typically prescribed as a constant and therefore does not explicitly respond to local flow conditions or unresolved morphological complexity within each cell. This remaining limitation can reduce transferability across inflow conditions
17
;
1
and can introduce sensitivity to grid resolution
6
;
36
, particularly in the grayscale regime where the effective unresolved morphology represented within each cell changes with cell size.

These issues motivate a drag formulation in which the cell-wise drag coefficient adapts to both morphology and flow regime. In this study, we propose a fractal-based variable-drag framework for porous-media tree representations in which the drag coefficient is prescribed as
C_{D}=C_{D}(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}})
. Here,
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
is a cell-effective branching order that characterizes unresolved morphological complexity by exploiting self-similarity of the underlying fractal branching structure, and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}
is an cell-effective local Reynolds number constructed from the local velocity regime and a morphology-based characteristic length. The mapping from
(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}})
to
C_{D}
is obtained a priori using an analytical drag model for fractal trees (details in our previous work
29
), enabling cell-wise updates of
C_{D}
during CFD simulations without ad hoc retuning.

We validate the proposed framework using steady RANS simulations with systematic parameter sweeps in both grid resolution and inflow velocity. To isolate the bulk momentum attenuation attributable to the canopy forcing, we evaluate model performance primarily using aerodynamic porosity
11
;
2
, defined as the ratio of leeward to windward plane-averaged streamwise velocity across the tree. We compare the proposed model against (i) a general conventional baseline using uniform PAD with constant
C_{D}
and (ii) an advanced conventional baseline using voxel-resolved PAD with constant
C_{D}
.

The remainder of this paper is organized as follows. In Sec. II, we present the fractal-based variable-drag formulation, including the definitions of
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}
and the a priori construction of
C_{D}(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}})
. In Sec. III, we describe the RANS methodology and computational setup, summarize the grid-resolution and inflow-velocity sweeps, and define the aerodynamic porosity metric used for evaluation. In Sec. IV, we present the results and discussion, focusing on qualitative flow behavior, resolution robustness, and Reynolds-number dependence compared with constant-
C_{D}
baselines. Finally, Sec. V concludes the paper and outlines future work.

Fractal-based variable-drag formulation

II.1
Cell-wise descriptors:
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}

Effective branching order from fractal self-similarity.

We define the cell-effective branching order
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
to characterize the unresolved, grid-dependent morphology represented within each computational cell. As sketched in Fig.
1
, changing grid size changes the portion of the tree contained in a cell and thus the effective unresolved geometry; fractal self-similarity allows this in-cell geometry to be interpreted in terms of an equivalent branching complexity, which we quantify by
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
.

:

Schematic illustration of grid-dependent in-cell morphology and its interpretation via fractal self-similarity.

To infer
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
, we define a dimensionless cell-wise morphology index
\Omega(\mathbf{x})=\mathrm{SVR}\times R_{g}
. Here,
\mathrm{SVR}
is the surface-to-volume ratio within a cell,
\mathrm{SVR}=(\text{surface area})/(\text{tree geometry volume})

[\mathrm{m}^{-1}]
, and
R_{g}

[\mathrm{m}]
is the radius of gyration of the in-cell geometry, defined as
R_{g}=\sqrt{\left(\sum_{i}V_{i}\left|\mathbf{c}_{i}-\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{com}}\right|^{2}\right)\big/\left(\sum_{i}V_{i}\right)}
. In this expression,
V_{i}
and
\mathbf{c}_{i}
are the volume and centroid of the
i
th constituent element, respectively, and
\mathbf{R}_{\mathrm{com}}=\left(\sum_{i}V_{i}\mathbf{c}_{i}\right)/\left(\sum_{i}V_{i}\right)
is the volume-weighted center position. The radius of gyration represents the statistical spread of the constituent volume elements around this center position.

In this work, we obtain an
\Omega\!\to\!n
mapping for the present fractal-tree family by evaluating
\Omega
for trees generated at discrete branching orders and establishing a monotonic correspondence between
\Omega
and
n
. We then infer the cell-effective branching order by applying this mapping to
\Omega(\mathbf{x})
, i.e., we obtain
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})
from
\Omega(\mathbf{x})
via lookup and interpolation.

We demonstrate the proposed
\Omega

23
. For each tree type, we construct the
\Omega\!-\!n
relationship used in the inference by evaluating
\Omega
for a sequence of realizations with branching orders
n=1,\dots,8
. Figure
2
presents the
n=8
geometries and the corresponding
\Omega\!-\!n_{\mathrm{eff}}
relationships for Trees A-C.

To validate the inferred
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
across grid resolutions, we compare a scalar summary of the inferred cell-wise field with a grid-dependent theoretical prediction derived from the geometrical space-filling property of the fractal trees (Appendix
A

in each tree cell, whereas the theoretical prediction provides a single expected value as a function of grid size. We therefore compute the PAD-weighted mean effective branching order,

Re_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})=|\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})|\,L_{\mathrm{cell}}(\mathbf{x})/\nu

\mathbf{u}(\mathbf{x})

2.4

3.1

A priori construction of
C_{D}(n,Re)

To prescribe
C_{D}
as a function of
(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}})
, we construct an a priori lookup table
C_{D}(n,Re)
using our analytical drag framework for fractal trees
29
. Ref.
29
provides the detailed derivation of the underlying analytical model. In the present study, the table is constructed for Tree A, which is the target geometry used in the subsequent RANS simulations. Figure

During the CFD simulations, each tree cell is assigned

from the local velocity field. The local drag coefficient is then obtained by interpolation as
C_{D}(\mathbf{x})=C_{D}(n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x}),Re_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x}))
and used in the porous-media momentum-sink term described in Sec. III. In the present fractal-tree framework,
n=1
represents a single cylinder. Although the physically generated tree geometries are defined at discrete branching orders, the lookup table is represented over a continuous range of
n
to allow interpolation using the inferred non-integer values of
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
. For cells where the inferred value falls below the single-cylinder limit,
n_{\mathrm{eff}}<1

by linearly extending the

–

III.2
Governing equations and porous-tree source terms

We solve the steady, incompressible, isothermal Reynolds-averaged Navier-Stokes equations with the standard
k
-
\varepsilon
turbulence model. The transport equations of mass, mean momentum, turbulent kinetic energy (TKE), and its dissipation rate are given as

\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{i}}=0

(1)

\begin{split}u_{j}\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}&=-\frac{1}{\rho}\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{i}}\left(p+\frac{2}{3}\rho k\right)\\
&\quad+\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left[(\nu+\nu_{t})\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\right]+S_{u,i}\end{split}

(2)

u_{j}\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left[\left(\nu+\frac{\nu_{t}}{\sigma_{k}}\right)\frac{\partial k}{\partial x_{j}}\right]+P_{k}-\varepsilon+S_{k}

(3)

u_{j}\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial x_{j}}=\frac{\partial}{\partial x_{j}}\left[\left(\nu+\frac{\nu_{t}}{\sigma_{\varepsilon}}\right)\frac{\partial\varepsilon}{\partial x_{j}}\right]+C_{\varepsilon 1}P_{k}\frac{\varepsilon}{k}-C_{\varepsilon 2}\frac{\varepsilon^{2}}{k}+S_{\varepsilon}

(4)

Here,
u_{i}

[\mathrm{m~s^{-1}}]
is the mean velocity in the
i
th direction,
p

[\mathrm{Pa}]
is the mean pressure,
k

[\mathrm{m^{2}~s^{-2}}]
is the TKE, and
\varepsilon

[\mathrm{m^{2}~s^{-3}}]
is the TKE dissipation rate. The turbulent viscosity in the standard
k
-
\varepsilon
model is
\nu_{t}=C_{\mu}k^{2}/\varepsilon
, and
P_{k}=\nu_{t}\left(\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}+\frac{\partial u_{j}}{\partial x_{i}}\right)\frac{\partial u_{i}}{\partial x_{j}}

denotes the TKE production term. The remaining variables and coefficients are constants in this study: air density
\rho

[\mathrm{kg~m^{-3}}]
, kinematic viscosity of air
\nu

[\mathrm{m^{2}~s^{-1}}]
, and turbulence-model coefficients (
C_{\mu}=0.09
,
C_{\varepsilon 1}=1.44
,
C_{\varepsilon 2}=1.92
,
\sigma_{k}=1
,
\sigma_{\varepsilon}=1.3
).

The final terms on the right-hand side of Eqs. (
2
)-(
4
) represent the porous-tree source terms. Following Endalew et al.
4
and Oshio et al.
21
, we introduce them as

S_{u,i}=-\frac{1}{2}C_{D}\,\mathrm{PAD}\,|\mathbf{u}|u_{i}

(5)

S_{k}=\frac{1}{2}C_{D}\,\mathrm{PAD}\,|\mathbf{u}|k

(6)

S_{\varepsilon}=\frac{1}{2}C_{D}\,\mathrm{PAD}\,|\mathbf{u}|\varepsilon

(7)

Here,
|\mathbf{u}|

[\mathrm{m~s^{-1}}]
is the magnitude of the velocity vector at each cell,
\mathrm{PAD}

[\mathrm{m^{-1}}]
is the plant area density, and
C_{D}
is the drag coefficient. In the constant-
C_{D}
cases, we prescribe
C_{D}
as a uniform constant, whereas in the proposed model we evaluate it cell-wise.

We evaluate model performance primarily using aerodynamic porosity, defined as the ratio of leeward to windward plane-averaged streamwise velocity across the tree. Aerodynamic porosity therefore provides a more suitable metric for the present purpose, while a more detailed assessment of wake structure remains an important subject for future work.

In the following quantitative comparisons, we use
(1-\mathrm{AP})
rather than
\mathrm{AP}
itself, where
\mathrm{AP}
denotes aerodynamic porosity. Since
\mathrm{AP}
is generally close to unity in the present cases, expressing the drag effect as
(1-\mathrm{AP})
makes the differences among models clearer and more interpretable.

We first examine how strongly the predicted drag effect depends on grid resolution. Figure
9
presents, for each model and inflow velocity, the standard deviation of
(1-\mathrm{AP})
across the six grid resolutions. A smaller value indicates that the predicted bulk drag effect is less sensitive to grid resolution. Figure

for all constant-

8\%
in all inflow cases. Relative to the general conventional model, this corresponds to an improvement in resolution robustness of about
60\%

30\%

A similar tendency appears in the minimum-maximum range shown in Fig.
10

IV.3
Reynolds-number dependence

Figure
11
presents the values of
(1-\mathrm{AP})
, averaged over the grid-resolution cases, as a function of the tree-height-based Reynolds number
Re_{H}
, together with the DNS reference
34
. For clarity, the figure shows the advanced conventional model as the representative constant-
C_{D}
comparison. The constant-
C_{D}
model predicts nearly constant
(1-\mathrm{AP})
over all inflow cases, even when voxel-resolved PAD is used. In contrast, the DNS reference shows a clear dependence on
Re_{H}
, and the proposed model successfully reproduces this trend. This result is noteworthy because the proposed model uses local cell-wise quantities,
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})
and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})
, yet reproduces the global Reynolds-number dependence of the bulk drag effect over the whole tree.

In the present study, we represent a single tree as a porous medium in RANS and evaluate
C_{D}
from a precomputed table obtained using the analytical model of Ref.
29
. Although this table is offset by approximately
30
–
40\%
toward larger
C_{D}
values than the DNS-based reference, the resulting aerodynamic porosity changes only weakly. Thus, the naive expectation that a larger
C_{D}
should directly yield a larger
(1-\mathrm{AP})
is not observed.

This weak sensitivity can be understood from the structure of the porous-drag closure itself. In the commonly used quadratic formulation, the drag force scales with the local velocity as
f_{i}\propto-C_{D}\,a\,|\mathbf{u}|\,u_{i}
, so increasing the drag coefficient reduces the local velocity inside the porous region, which in turn weakens the drag magnitude through the same
|\mathbf{u}|u_{i}
dependence. This introduces an intrinsic nonlinear feedback and prevents a one-to-one linear mapping between
C_{D}
and AP. Such local-velocity-based drag formulations are standard in canopy-flow and porous-canopy modeling
20
;
35
, and recent analysis has shown that the conventional RANS canopy sink corresponds to a low-order approximation of a more general canopy-drag representation
15
.

In addition, the value identified as a drag coefficient depends on the choice of reference velocity. Previous studies have emphasized that canopy drag cannot in general be characterized by a unique coefficient independent of the adopted velocity scale, and that local or in-canopy velocity scales can become more appropriate than an upstream free-stream reference
20
;
22

V
Conclusions and outlook

In this study, we proposed and evaluated a fractal-based variable-drag model for porous-media tree representations. By prescribing the drag coefficient cell-wise as
C_{D}(n_{\mathrm{eff}},Re_{\mathrm{eff}})
, where
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}
denote the cell-effective branching order and the cell-effective Reynolds number, respectively, the model accounts for local morphological complexity and flow conditions. The qualitative verification showed that the proposed model produces a plausible aerodynamic response, including a velocity deficit within and behind the porous tree region, bypass flow, and wake recovery. This result supports the proposed formulation as a physically grounded alternative to ad hoc constant-
C_{D}
prescriptions conventionally used in porous-tree modeling.

The grid-sensitivity analysis showed that the proposed model improves resolution robustness compared with conventional fixed-
C_{D}
models. By incorporating the spatial variation of
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
, the model produced more consistent aerodynamic porosity across grid resolutions, indicating that local morphological information reduces the dependence of bulk drag prediction on tree discretization.

The Reynolds-number-dependence analysis showed that the proposed model reproduces the variation of the bulk drag effect across inflow conditions without empirical retuning. Importantly, the global Reynolds-number dependence of the whole-tree aerodynamic response was recovered from local cell-wise quantities, namely
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})
and
Re_{\mathrm{eff}}(\mathbf{x})
. This result demonstrates that the proposed framework improves adaptability to varying flow conditions while retaining the computational efficiency of porous-media representations.

Several directions for future work follow naturally from these results. Although the present numerical assessment was restricted to steady RANS simulations, the drag formulation itself is not limited to steady calculations. An important methodological extension is therefore to apply the present framework in unsteady CFD, particularly LES, in order to examine its ability to capture time-dependent wake dynamics and turbulence structure. Another important direction is to extend the present framework from a single isolated tree to multiple-tree configurations and eventually to urban-scale simulations, which will be essential for assessing its applicability to real urban micrometeorology, including ventilation, thermal environment, and scalar transport.

Appendix A
Theoretical prediction of
n_{\mathrm{eff}}

This appendix summarizes the theoretical prediction used to relate the grid size
\Delta
to the effective iteration
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
for a fractal tree. Let
H
denote the tree height used for normalization and define the grid factor

k\equiv\frac{H}{\Delta},\qquad k^{-1}=\frac{\Delta}{H}.

(8)

We characterize the geometric space-filling property at the resolution scale using box counting. For a box size
L
(here identified with the grid scale,
L\sim\Delta
), the number of occupied boxes
N(L)
satisfies

N(L)\propto\left(\frac{1}{L}\right)^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}},

(9)

where
D_{\mathrm{eff}}
is the (interval-wise) box-counting exponent, i.e., the local slope of
\log N
versus
\log(1/L)
over the scale range of interest. Using the normalized length
L/H
and setting
L=\Delta
, Eq. (
9
) becomes

N(\Delta)\propto\left(\frac{H}{\Delta}\right)^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}=k^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}.

(10)

Next, we introduce
n_{\mathrm{eff}}
as a coarse-grained measure of the effective iteration depth represented at grid scale
\Delta
. Since the iterative construction is multiplicative in scale (one level corresponds to a scale change by the branching ratio
b>1
), it is natural to count “levels” on a logarithmic scale with base
b
. We therefore define the within-cell effective iteration as

n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta)\equiv n_{0}-\log_{b}N(\Delta),

(11)

where
n_{0}
is the nominal iteration of the full tree. Substituting Eq. (
10
) into Eq. (
11
) yields

n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta)=n_{0}-\log_{b}\!\left(C\,k^{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}\right)=n_{0}-\log_{b}C-D_{\mathrm{eff}}\log_{b}k,

(12)

with a proportionality constant
C
. Using
\log_{b}x=\ln x/\ln b
, and imposing the normalization condition
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(\Delta=H)=n_{0}
(i.e.,
k=1
), we obtain
\log_{b}C=0
(thus
C=1
). This gives the final prediction

n_{\mathrm{eff}}(k)=n_{0}-\frac{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\ln b}\,\ln k.

(13)

Because the figures use the normalized grid size
\Delta/H=k^{-1}
on the horizontal axis, Eq. (
13
) can be written equivalently as

n_{\mathrm{eff}}\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{H}\right)=n_{0}+\frac{D_{\mathrm{eff}}}{\ln b}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{\Delta}{H}\right),

(14)

where
\ln(H/\Delta)=-\ln(\Delta/H)
has been used.

Real trees are finite and may not exhibit perfect self-similarity across all scales; consequently,
D_{\mathrm{eff}}
can be scale-dependent. In this study, we evaluate
D_{\mathrm{eff},i}
for each neighboring grid-size interval (aligned with the RANS resolution series
\Delta/H\in\{1,1/2,1/4,1/6,1/8,1/10\}
) and treat it as constant within that interval. We then apply Eq. (
13
) in a piecewise manner:

n_{\mathrm{eff}}(k)=n_{\mathrm{eff}}(k_{i})-\frac{D_{\mathrm{eff},i}}{\ln b}\,\ln\!\left(\frac{k}{k_{i}}\right),\qquad k\in[k_{i},k_{i+1}],

(15)

where we enforce continuity by updating
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(k_{i+1})
sequentially from
n_{\mathrm{eff}}(k_{i})
.
