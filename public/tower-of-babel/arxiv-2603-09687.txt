Dunford-Pettis Multilinear Operators and their variations: A revisit to the classic concepts of Operator Ideals.
Joilson Ribeiro, Fabrício Santos

Abstract
In this paper, we address broader concepts of Dunford-Pettis operators, presenting new classes and results that relate this class to others already well studied in the literature, as well as an approach outside the origin. We also investigate inclusion results and conditions for the coincidence of this class with previously studied classes and with new classes that arise throughout this work.



1
Introduction and background

The class of Dunford–Pettis linear operators has played a crucial role in the theory of Banach spaces in general, as highlighted in
[
11
]
. Several researchers have investigated this topic, mainly seeking correlations among the classes of compact linear operators, Dunford–Pettis operators, weakly Dunford–Pettis operators, and weakly* Dunford–Pettis operators, as can be seen in
[
1
,
4
,
5
]
.

A first multilinear approach to Dunford-Pettis operators between Banach spaces was proposed by Ribeiro, Santos, and Torres in
[
17
]
, where it was shown that this class is an ideal of multilinear mappings but not a hyper-ideal, a concept introduced by Botelho and Torres in
[
7
]
. In that work, Ribeiro, Santos, and Torres focused on verifying that this class does not satisfy the coherence concept introduced by Pellegrino and Ribeiro in
[
15
]
. However, they did not explore other generalizations to the multilinear setting to study inclusion and coincidence results. It is worth noting that the case we will call Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators at every point already appears in the literature and can be found in
[
3
,
9
]
.

In this paper, in addition to introducing new classes that generalize the class of Dunford-Pettis linear operators, we also revisit concepts such as multi-ideals, hyper-ideals, and coherence and compatibility to formalize these new structures into properties considered fundamental for their development, such as closure under composition with linear operators and the product of linear functionals and multilinear forms. We also present coincidence conditions for these classes and seek characterizations that make their use smoother.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. An operator
T\colon E\rightarrow F
is called a Dunford–Pettis operator, and is denoted by
\mathcal{DP}
, if
T
carries weakly convergent sequences to norm convergent sequences. In other words, if
x_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
in
\sigma(E,E^{\prime})
, then
\|T(x_{j})\|_{F}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.

Alternatively, a (bounded linear) operator
T\colon E\rightarrow F
is a Dunford–Pettis operator if and only if
T
carries relatively weakly compact sets to norm totally bounded sets. A Banach space
E
is said to have the Schur property if
Id_{E}\colon E\rightarrow E
, given by
Id_{E}(x)=x
, is a Dunford–Pettis operator. The Banach space
\ell_{1}
has the Schur property (
[
13
, Theorem 2.5.18]
).

We can also define the class of weakly Dunford–Pettis linear operators as follows: Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. An operator
T\colon E\rightarrow F
is said to be weakly Dunford–Pettis if whenever
x_{j}\to 0
in
\sigma(E,E^{\prime})
and
f_{j}\to 0
in
F^{\prime}
, then
f_{j}(T(x_{j}))\to 0
. We denote this class by
\mathcal{WDP}
(
[
2
]
).

From now on, the letters
E,E_{1},\dots,E_{m},F,G,H
will denote Banach spaces over the same scalar field
\mathbb{K}=\mathbb{R}
or
\mathbb{C}
, and
E^{\prime}
stands for the topological dual of
E
. The symbol
B_{E}
denotes the closed unit ball of
E
. We use
\mathrm{BAN}
to denote the class of all Banach spaces over
\mathbb{K}
.

By
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n};F)
we denote the Banach space of continuous
n
-linear operators from
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{n}
to
F
, endowed with the usual uniform norm
\|\cdot\|
. In the linear case we write
\mathcal{L}(E;F)
. If
E_{1}=\cdots=E_{n}=E
, we write
\mathcal{L}(^{n}E;F)
. If
F=\mathbb{K}
, we write
\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\ldots,E_{n})
and
\mathcal{L}(^{n}E)
.

A mapping
P\colon E\rightarrow F
is said to be an
m
-homogeneous polynomial
if there exists
A\in\mathcal{L}(^{m}E;F)
such that
P(x)=A(x)^{m}
for every
x\in E
, where
A(x)^{m}:=A\left(x,\overset{m}{\dots},x\right)
. In this case we write
P=\widehat{A}
. By
\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
we denote the Banach space of all continuous
m
-homogeneous polynomials from
E
to
F
, endowed with the usual supremum norm
\|\cdot\|
. By
\check{P}
we denote the unique symmetric continuous
m
-linear operator associated with
P
.

We write
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}x
in
E
and
f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\to}f
in
E^{\prime}
to mean that
x_{j}\to x
in
\sigma(E,E^{\prime})
and
f_{j}\to f
in
\sigma(E^{\prime},E)
, as
j\to\infty
.

2
Dunford-Pettis Multilinear Operators and Homogeneous Polynomials Everywhere

In this section we present the concept of Dunford–Pettis multilinear operators and Dunford–Pettis homogeneous polynomials at every point. Recall that the concept of Dunford–Pettis multilinear operators and homogeneous polynomials (at the origin) was introduced by Ribeiro, Santos, and Torres in
[
17
]
. In that paper it was shown that the coherence condition (CH1) fails, and that this class does not generate a hyper-ideal of multilinear mappings or homogeneous polynomials, concepts introduced in
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
. We begin by defining these notions.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We say that
T
is a Dunford–Pettis
m
-linear operator at
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
if, for each
i=1,\dots,m
, every sequence
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
satisfying

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}

also satisfies

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\longrightarrow T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})

in norm as
j\to\infty
.

If
T
is a Dunford–Pettis multilinear operator at every point of
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, we say that
T
is Dunford–Pettis at every point. We denote by
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
the class of all such operators. Analogously, we have the following definition.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
. We say that
P
is a Dunford–Pettis
m
-homogeneous polynomial at
a\in E
if, for every sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
with

x_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a,

we have

P(x_{j})\longrightarrow P(a)

in norm as
j\to\infty
.

If
P
is a Dunford–Pettis homogeneous polynomial at every point of
E
, we say that
P
is Dunford–Pettis at every point. We denote by
\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
the class of all such polynomials.

Proposition 2.3
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Then,
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
if, and only if, for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
and every weakly null sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, we have

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|\longrightarrow 0.

Proof.

Suppose first that
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

Let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
and let
(x_{j}^{(i)})
be weakly null sequences in
E_{i}
. Then

x_{j}^{(i)}+a_{i}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i},

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
T
is Dunford-Pettis at every point, it follows that

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|\to 0.

Conversely, suppose the condition holds. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})\subset E_{i}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}
. Define

y_{j}^{(i)}=x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i},

so that
y_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
. Then

\displaystyle\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|

\displaystyle=\|T(y_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,y_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|.

By hypothesis, the right-hand side converges to
0
, and hence

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\to T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}).

Therefore,
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Remark 2.4
.

(1)

The concepts in Definitions
2.1
and
2.2
are not new (
[
7
,
8
]
). They coincide with the classes of weakly sequentially continuous multilinear operators and weakly sequentially continuous homogeneous polynomials, respectively. We adopt this terminology for alignment with the class of Dunford–Pettis multilinear mappings.

(2)

It is immediate that
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{DP}}
and
\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{P_{DP}}
.

A natural question at this point is whether
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
is a Banach multi-ideal. To address this question, we first show that
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is a Banach space for arbitrary Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
.

Proposition 2.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is a closed subset of
\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

Proof.

Let
(T_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
with
T_{j}\to T
in norm. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}x_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
. Then
(x_{j}^{(i)})
are bounded, so

\|(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|:=\|x_{j}^{(1)}\|\cdots\|x_{j}^{(m)}\|\leq C

for some
C>0
and all
j
.

Given
\varepsilon>0
, choose
k
such that
\|T-T_{k}\|<\varepsilon
. Since
T_{k}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
,

T_{k}(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\to T_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}).

Hence,

\displaystyle\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|

\displaystyle\leq C\|T-T_{k}\|+\|T_{k}(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|+\|T-T_{k}\|\|(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|,

which tends to
0
as
j\to\infty
. Therefore,

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\to T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}),

and
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

As an immediate consequence of this proposition, and since
\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is a Banach space, we obtain the following result.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is a Banach space.

It is straightforward to verify that the pairs
(\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
and
(\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
form a Banach multi-ideal and a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials, respectively (see
[
12
,
16
]
for details). These structures generalize the Banach ideal of linear operators
(\mathcal{L_{DP}},\|\cdot\|)
. On the other hand,
(\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
is not a hyper-ideal of multilinear mappings, as shown in Examples 4.4 and 3.11 of
[
7
]
and
[
8
]
, respectively.

We next show that the sequence

\left(\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}},\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}

is coherent and compatible with
(\mathcal{L_{DP}},\|\cdot\|)
, according to
[
15
]
. However, it is not strongly coherent (see
[
17
]
). We begin by establishing the following result.

Before proving the next result, we recall a standard notion in multilinear theory. Let
S_{m}
denote the group of permutations of
\{1,\dots,m\}
. Given
T\in\mathcal{L}(^{m}E;F)
and
\sigma\in S_{m}
, define
T_{\sigma}
and
T_{S}
in
\mathcal{L}(^{m}E;F)
by

\displaystyle T_{\sigma}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=T(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(m)}),

\displaystyle T_{S}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{m!}\sum_{\sigma\in S_{m}}T_{\sigma}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m}).

A subclass
\mathcal{G}
of the class of continuous multilinear operators between Banach spaces is said to be symmetric if
T_{S}\in\mathcal{G}(^{m}E;F)
whenever
T\in\mathcal{G}(^{m}E;F)
. If
\mathcal{G}
is endowed with a function
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{G}}\colon\mathcal{G}\to[0,\infty)
, then
\mathcal{G}
is said to be strongly symmetric if
T_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{G}
and
\|T_{\sigma}\|_{\mathcal{G}}=\|T\|_{\mathcal{G}}
for all
T\in\mathcal{G}(^{m}E;F)
and
\sigma\in S_{m}
(see
[
12
]
).

Proposition 2.7
.

The subclass
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
of the class of continuous multilinear operators between Banach spaces is symmetric.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, let
\sigma\in S_{m}
, and let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be sequences such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}x_{i}\in E_{i},\quad i=1,\dots,m.

Then

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\longrightarrow T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})

in norm as
j\to\infty
.

Since
x_{j}^{(\sigma(i))}\overset{w}{\to}x_{\sigma(i)}
for
i=1,\dots,m
, it follows that

\|T_{\sigma}(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T_{\sigma}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|=\|T(x_{j}^{(\sigma(1))},\dots,x_{j}^{(\sigma(m))})-T(x_{\sigma(1)},\dots,x_{\sigma(m)})\|\to 0

as
j\to\infty
. Hence,

\|T_{S}(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T_{S}(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|\to 0

as
j\to\infty
. Therefore,
T_{S}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Theorem 2.8
.

The sequence
\left(\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}},\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}}\right)_{n=1}^{\infty}
is coherent and compatible with
\mathcal{DP}
.

Proof.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. We begin by showing that, for any
k\in\{1,\dots,m\}
,
P\in\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
if and only if
\check{P}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
(see Properties (CH5) and (CP5) in
[
15
, Definitions 2.1 and 2.2]
).

Suppose that
\check{P}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
. Let
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
be such that
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}x\in E
. Then

P(x_{j})=\check{P}(x_{j},\dots,x_{j})\longrightarrow\check{P}(x,\dots,x)=P(x)

in norm, and hence
P\in\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
.

Conversely, suppose that
P\in\mathcal{P_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}x_{i}\in E
, for all
i=1,\dots,k
. Since
\epsilon_{i}=\pm 1
, we have

\epsilon_{1}x_{j}^{(1)}+\cdots+\epsilon_{k}x_{j}^{(k)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}\epsilon_{1}x_{1}+\cdots+\epsilon_{k}x_{k}.

Hence,

\displaystyle\left\|\check{P}(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(k)})-\check{P}(x_{1},\dots,x_{k})\right\|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2^{k}k!}\left\|P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{i}x_{j}^{(i)}\right)-P\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\epsilon_{i}x_{i}\right)\right\|\longrightarrow 0

as
j\to\infty
. Thus,
\check{P}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(^{k}E;F)
.

Next, we show that for any
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
a_{i}\in E_{i}
, we have
T_{a_{i}}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{i-1},E_{i+1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and

\|T_{a_{i}}\|\leq\|T\|\|a_{i}\|,

for all
i=1,\dots,m
(Property (CH1) in
[
15
, Definition 2.1]
). Without loss of generality, consider
i=1
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}x_{i}
for
i=2,\dots,m
. Then

\|T_{a_{1}}(x_{j}^{(2)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T_{a_{1}}(x_{2},\dots,x_{m})\|=\|T(a_{1},x_{j}^{(2)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},x_{2},\dots,x_{m})\|\to 0,

since the constant sequence
(x_{j}^{(1)})_{j=1}^{\infty}=a_{1}
converges weakly to
a_{1}
. The norm inequality is immediate.

Finally, let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
\gamma\in E_{m+1}^{\prime}
. We show that
\gamma T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m+1};F)
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}x_{i}\in E_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m+1
. Then

\displaystyle\gamma T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m+1)})-\gamma T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})

\displaystyle=\gamma(x_{j}^{(m+1)}-x_{m+1})\,T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})

\displaystyle\quad+\gamma(x_{m+1})\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\big).

Since
(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))
is bounded and
x_{j}^{(m+1)}\overset{w}{\to}x_{m+1}
, it follows that

\displaystyle\|\gamma T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m+1)})-\gamma T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m+1})\|

\displaystyle\leq|\gamma(x_{j}^{(m+1)}-x_{m+1})|\,\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|

\displaystyle\quad+|\gamma(x_{m+1})|\,\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})\|\to 0

as
j\to\infty
. The norm estimate is immediate.

The conclusion follows from
[
17
, Remark 2.3(3) and Proposition 2.4]
together with Proposition
2.7
.

∎

Remark 2.9
.

We present an example showing that this class is not strongly coherent. Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, let
\varphi_{i}\in E_{i}^{\prime}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and let
y\in F
be nonzero. Choose
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
such that
a_{i}\notin\ker\varphi_{i}
for all
i
.

Define
T\colon E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}\to F
by

T(x_{1},\dots,x_{m})=\varphi_{1}(x_{1})\cdots\varphi_{m}(x_{m})\,y,

and let
Q\colon\ell_{2}\times\ell_{2}\to\mathbb{K}
be given by

Q\big((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\big)=\sum_{j=1}^{\infty}x_{j}y_{j}.

Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}a_{i}
for
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
e_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
in
\ell_{2}
, we have

\displaystyle QT(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)},e_{j},e_{j})

\displaystyle=Q(e_{j},e_{j})\,T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})

\displaystyle=\varphi_{1}(x_{j}^{(1)})\cdots\varphi_{m}(x_{j}^{(m)})\,y

\displaystyle\longrightarrow\varphi_{1}(a_{1})\cdots\varphi_{m}(a_{m})\,y\neq 0.

Thus,

QT(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)},e_{j},e_{j})\nrightarrow QT(a_{1},\dots,a_{m},0,0),

and therefore
QT\notin\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m},\ell_{2},\ell_{2};F)
, even though
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

We denote by
\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}
the class of all multilinear Dunford–Pettis operators between Banach spaces that satisfy condition (CH1) (see
[
15
, Definition 3.2]
and
[
17
, Definition 2.1]
).

Theorem 2.10
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}=\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}.

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. It follows from Remark
2.4
and Theorem
2.8
that

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}.

We now prove the reverse inclusion. Let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We show that
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}a_{i}\in E_{i},

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Then
(x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i})\overset{w}{\to}0
in
E_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

For simplicity, we consider the case
m=3
; the general case follows analogously. We have

\displaystyle T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})

\displaystyle=T(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+T_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1})+T_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2})+T_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})

\displaystyle\quad+T_{a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2})+T_{a_{2}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})+T_{a_{1}}(x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3}).

By property (CH1), we have

T_{a_{1}}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}(E_{2},E_{3};F),\quad T_{a_{2}}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}(E_{1},E_{3};F),\quad T_{a_{3}}\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}(E_{1},E_{2};F),

and hence

T_{a_{1},a_{2}}\in\mathcal{DP}(E_{3};F),\quad T_{a_{1},a_{3}}\in\mathcal{DP}(E_{2};F),\quad T_{a_{2},a_{3}}\in\mathcal{DP}(E_{1};F).

It follows that each term in the above decomposition converges to zero as
j\to\infty
, and therefore

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\|\to 0.

Thus,
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

The next result provides a condition under which every multilinear operator between Banach spaces is Dunford–Pettis at every point.

Theorem 2.11
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property for
i=1,\dots,m
, then every continuous multilinear operator from
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
into
F
is Dunford–Pettis at every point. In particular,

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, and assume that each
E_{i}
has the Schur property for
i=1,\dots,m
. Let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We show that
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
be such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}a_{i}\in E_{i},

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Then

x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}\overset{w}{\to}0,

and since each
E_{i}
has the Schur property, it follows that

x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}\longrightarrow 0

in norm, for all
i=1,\dots,m
.

For simplicity, we consider the case
m=3
; the general case follows analogously. Then

\displaystyle\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{a_{2},a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1})\|+\|T_{a_{1},a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2})\|+\|T_{a_{1},a_{2}}(x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{a_{3}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2})\|+\|T_{a_{2}}(x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{a_{1}}(x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2},x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3})\|

\displaystyle\leq\|T\|\prod_{i=1}^{3}\|x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}\|+\|T_{a_{2},a_{3}}\|\|x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1}\|+\|T_{a_{1},a_{3}}\|\|x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{a_{1},a_{2}}\|\|x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3}\|+\|T_{a_{3}}\|\|x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1}\|\|x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2}\|

\displaystyle\quad+\|T_{a_{2}}\|\|x_{j}^{(1)}-a_{1}\|\|x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3}\|+\|T_{a_{1}}\|\|x_{j}^{(2)}-a_{2}\|\|x_{j}^{(3)}-a_{3}\|.

Since
x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}\to 0
in norm for all
i
, each term on the right-hand side converges to zero as
j\to\infty
. Hence,

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\|\to 0,

and therefore
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Corollary 2.12
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property for
i=1,\dots,m
, then

\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Let us recall that, given Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
, we denote their projective tensor product by
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
(see
[
18
]
for details). As noted in
[
7
, Section 4]
, for any Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
, if
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
has the Schur property, then

\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Therefore, we readily obtain the following result.

Proposition 2.13
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property for
i=1,\dots,m
and
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
also has the Schur property, then

\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

Since each
E_{i}
has the Schur property for
i=1,\dots,m
and
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
also has the Schur property, it follows that

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

∎

We say that an
m
-linear operator
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is compact if

T(B_{E_{1}}\times\cdots\times B_{E_{m}})
is a relatively compact subset of
F
.
We denote by
\mathcal{K}
the ideal of compact linear operators and by
\mathcal{L}_{K}

the ideal of compact multilinear operators. By
[
14
]
, we have

\mathcal{L}_{K}=\mathcal{K}\circ\mathcal{L}.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Assume that each
E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
,
and the projective tensor product
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}

have the Schur property. Then, for every
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
,

\displaystyle T\in\mathcal{L}_{K}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

\displaystyle\iff T_{L}\in\mathcal{K}(E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m};F)

\displaystyle\subset\mathcal{DP}(E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m};F)

\displaystyle\iff T\in\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F),

where
T_{L}
denotes the linearization of
T
(see
[
6
]
for details). Therefore,

\mathcal{L}_{K}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

In general, this inclusion is strict, as the following example shows.

Example 2.14
.

Let
T\colon\ell_{1}\times\cdots\times\ell_{1}\longrightarrow\ell_{1}
be defined by

T\big((x_{j}^{(1)})_{j=1}^{\infty},\dots,(x_{j}^{(m)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\big)=(x_{j}^{(1)}\cdots x_{j}^{(m)})_{j=1}^{\infty}.

Note that
(e_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded in
\ell_{1}
, since
\|e_{j}\|_{1}=1
for all
j
.
Moreover,

T(e_{j},\dots,e_{j})=e_{j},

which has no convergent subsequence. Hence,
T\notin\mathcal{L}_{K}(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{1};\ell_{1})
.

On the other hand, let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be weakly convergent to
x_{i}
in
\ell_{1}
,
for
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
\ell_{1}
has the Schur property, the convergence is actually
in norm. For simplicity, consider
m=3
. Then

\displaystyle\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\|_{1}

\displaystyle\leq\|x_{j}^{(1)}-x_{1}\|_{1}\|x_{j}^{(2)}\|_{1}\|x_{j}^{(3)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\|x_{1}\|_{1}\|x_{j}^{(2)}-x_{2}\|_{1}\|x_{j}^{(3)}\|_{1}

\displaystyle\quad+\|x_{1}\|_{1}\|x_{2}\|_{1}\|x_{j}^{(3)}-x_{3}\|_{1}.

Since all sequences are bounded and converge in norm, it follows that

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},x_{j}^{(2)},x_{j}^{(3)})-T(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\|_{1}\to 0.

Therefore,
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{1};\ell_{1})
.

3
Weakly Dunford-Pettis Multilinear Operators

We begin by defining the linear case, which will be useful for showing that the class of weakly Dunford-Pettis linear operators is not a hyper-ideal (see
[
7
]
) and that the coherence and compatibility condition (CH1) fails (see
[
15
]
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E;F)
. We say that
T
is weakly Dunford-Pettis if, for every pair of sequences
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
, we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{WDP}(E;F)
the class of all weakly Dunford-Pettis linear operators from
E
into
F
. It is straightforward to verify that
(\mathcal{WDP},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach operator ideal.

Definition 3.2
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We say that
T
is a weakly Dunford-Pettis
m
-linear operator if, for every family of sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
, we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{L_{WDP}}
the class of all weakly Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Every Dunford–Pettis
m
-linear operator is weakly Dunford–Pettis.

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}0
for all
i=1,\dots,m
and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
.

Since
T
is Dunford–Pettis, we have

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|\longrightarrow 0.

Moreover, since every weakly convergent sequence is bounded, there exists
K>0
such that
\|f_{j}\|\leq K
for all
j
. Therefore,

|f_{j}(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))|\leq\|f_{j}\|\,\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|\leq K\,\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|.

Hence,

f_{j}(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))\longrightarrow 0,

and thus
T
is weakly Dunford–Pettis.
∎

Following the ideas presented by Ribeiro, Santos and Torres in
[
17
, Proposition 2.8]
, we can easily see that
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\|\cdot\|)
, endowed with the usual norm, is a Banach multi-ideal that generalizes the Banach operator ideal
\mathcal{WDP}
.

Remark 3.4
.

It is important to emphasize that, in general, the inclusion in Proposition
3.3
is strict, since there exist weakly Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators that are not Dunford-Pettis, as will be shown below.

Example 3.5
.

Consider the mapping
T\colon c_{0}\times c_{0}\to c_{0}
defined by

T\big((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\big)=(x_{j}y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}.

Since
e_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
in
c_{0}
and

\|T(e_{j},e_{j})\|_{\infty}=\|e_{j}\|_{\infty}=1,

it follows that
T
is not a Dunford–Pettis bilinear mapping.

On the other hand, we claim that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(c_{0},c_{0};c_{0})
. Indeed, let
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be sequences in
c_{0}
such that
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
and
y_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
, and let
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset\ell_{1}=c_{0}^{\prime}
be such that
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
. Since
\ell_{1}
has the Schur property, it follows that
\|f_{j}\|_{1}\to 0
. Moreover, the sequences
(x_{j})
and
(y_{j})
are bounded, hence there exists
C>0
such that
\|x_{j}\|_{\infty},\|y_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq C
for all
j
. Therefore,

|f_{j}(T(x_{j},y_{j}))|\leq\|f_{j}\|_{1}\,\|T(x_{j},y_{j})\|_{\infty}\leq\|f_{j}\|_{1}\,\|x_{j}\|_{\infty}\|y_{j}\|_{\infty}\leq C^{2}\|f_{j}\|_{1}\longrightarrow 0.

Thus,
T
is weakly Dunford–Pettis.

As observed above, the class
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach multi-ideal and therefore enjoys the usual properties of such ideals. It is natural to ask whether this class also satisfies the hyper-ideal property introduced by Botelho and Torres in
[
7
]
, that is, whether
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\|\cdot\|)
forms a hyper-ideal of multilinear mappings.

Remark 3.6
.

Although
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach multi-ideal, it is not a hyper-ideal of multilinear mappings. Indeed, consider the mapping

T\colon\ell_{2}\times\ell_{2}\to\ell_{1},\quad T\big((x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\big)=(x_{j}y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty},

as in Example 2.9 of
[
17
]
. It is straightforward to verify that
T\notin\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(\ell_{2},\ell_{2};\ell_{1})
. On the other hand, by Schur’s theorem (see
[
10
, Theorem 1.7]
) and the fact that

\mathcal{CC}(\ell_{1})=\mathcal{DP}(\ell_{1})\subset\mathcal{WDP}(\ell_{1}),

we have
Id_{\ell_{1}}\in\mathcal{WDP}(\ell_{1})
. If
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\|\cdot\|)
were a hyper-ideal, then

T=Id_{\ell_{1}}\circ T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(\ell_{2},\ell_{2};\ell_{1}),

which is a contradiction.

Definition 3.7
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. A mapping
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
is said to be a weakly Dunford-Pettis
m
-homogeneous polynomial if, for every sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
, we have

f_{j}\big(P(x_{j})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{P_{WDP}}
the class of all weakly Dunford-Pettis homogeneous polynomials.

It is possible to relate the class of weakly Dunford–Pettis multilinear mappings to that of weakly Dunford-Pettis homogeneous polynomials through the following result, whose proof follows the same ideas as the proof of condition
(CH5)
in Theorem
2.8
.

Before proving this result, we recall an important notion in multilinear theory. Given a Banach multi-ideal
(\mathcal{M},\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{M}})
, the associated Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials is defined by

\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}=\{P\in\mathcal{P}\;;\;\check{P}\in\mathcal{M}\},

endowed with the norm

\|P\|_{\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{M}}}:=\|\check{P}\|_{\mathcal{M}}.

Proposition 3.8
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then
P\in\mathcal{P_{WDP}}(^{m}E;F)
if and only if
\check{P}\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(^{m}E;F)
.

As an immediate consequence of Proposition
3.8
, we have

\mathcal{P_{WDP}}(^{m}E;F)=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{L_{WDP}}}(^{m}E;F)

for every
m\in\mathbb{N}
and all Banach spaces
E
and
F
.

Another immediate consequence of Proposition
3.8
is that the pair
(\mathcal{P_{WDP}}(^{m}E;F),\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

The result in Proposition
3.8
corresponds to condition
(CH5)
in the definitions of coherent and compatible, and strongly coherent and compatible pairs (see
[
15
,
17
]
). This naturally leads to the question of whether the sequence of pairs
\left(\mathcal{L_{WDP}},\mathcal{P_{WDP}}\right)
is strongly coherent and strongly compatible with
\mathcal{WDP}
.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m+n}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
Q\in\mathcal{L}(E_{m+1},\dots,E_{m+n})
, then

QT\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m+n};F).

Proof.

Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m+n
, and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
be such that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\to}0
for all
i=1,\dots,m+n
and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
.

Since
Q
is continuous, the sequence
\big(Q(x_{j}^{(m+1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m+n)})\big)_{j=1}^{\infty}
is bounded. Moreover, since
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

Therefore,

\big|f_{j}\big(QT(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m+n)})\big)\big|=\big|Q(x_{j}^{(m+1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m+n)})\big|\,\big|f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\big|\longrightarrow 0,

which shows that
QT\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m+n};F)
.

The norm estimate

\|QT\|\leq\|Q\|\,\|T\|

is immediate.
∎

Using similar arguments, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.10
.

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
P\in\mathcal{P_{WDP}}(^{m}E;F)
and
Q\in\mathcal{P}(^{n}E)
, then

QP\in\mathcal{P_{WDP}}(^{m+n}E;F).

Remark 3.11
.

Although the class
\mathcal{L_{WDP}}
satisfies conditions
(CH3)
and
(CH5)
, as shown in Propositions
3.8
and
3.10
, it does not satisfy condition
(CH1)
.

Indeed, let
E
be a Banach space and let
a\in E\setminus\{0\}
. By the Hahn–Banach Theorem, there exists
\varphi\in E^{\prime}
such that
|\varphi(a)|=1
. Define
T\colon E\times\ell_{2}\to\ell_{2}
by

T(x,(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty})=(\varphi(x)x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}.

It is immediate that
T
is a continuous bilinear mapping.

Let
x_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
in
E
,
y_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
in
\ell_{2}
, and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\to}0
in
(\ell_{2})^{\prime}
. Then
\varphi(x_{j})\to 0
, while the sequences
(y_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
are bounded in
\ell_{2}
and
(\ell_{2})^{\prime}
, respectively. Therefore,

|f_{j}(T(x_{j},y_{j}))|\longrightarrow 0,

which shows that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E,\ell_{2};\ell_{2})
.

However,
T_{a}\notin\mathcal{WDP}(\ell_{2};\ell_{2})
. Indeed, let
(e_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
be the canonical basis of
\ell_{2}
. Since
(\ell_{2})^{\prime}
is isometrically isomorphic to
\ell_{2}
, we may identify each
f_{j}\in(\ell_{2})^{\prime}
with
e_{j}
. Writing
e_{j}=(\delta_{n,j})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, we obtain

|f_{j}(T_{a}(e_{j}))|=|f_{j}(T(a,e_{j}))|=\left|f_{j}\big((\varphi(a)\delta_{n,j})_{n=1}^{\infty}\big)\right|=|\varphi(a)|\|e_{j}\|_{2}=|\varphi(a)|=1,

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

Thus,
(T_{a}(e_{j}))_{j=1}^{\infty}
does not converge to zero in
\ell_{2}
, and hence
T_{a}
does not satisfy condition
(CH1)
.

It is worth noting that, by Theorem
2.11
, if
E_{i}
has the Schur property for all
i=1,\dots,m
, then

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Let us now introduce the concept of weakly Dunford–Pettis multilinear operators at every point, following the same ideas as in Section
2
.

Definition 3.12
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We say that
T
is a weakly Dunford–Pettis
m
-linear operator at
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
if for every sequence
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f\in F^{\prime},

we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)

as
j\to\infty
.

When
T
is a weakly Dunford–Pettis multilinear operator at every point of
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, we say that
T
is weakly Dunford–Pettis at every point and denote the corresponding class by
\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
.

Definition 3.13
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces, and let
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
. We say that
P
is a weakly Dunford–Pettis
m
-homogeneous polynomial at
a\in E
if for every sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that

x_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f\in F^{\prime},

we have

f_{j}\big(P(x_{j})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(P(a)\big)

as
j\to\infty
.

When
P
is a weakly Dunford–Pettis homogeneous polynomial at every point of
E
, we say that
P
is weakly Dunford–Pettis at every point, and we denote the class of all such polynomials by
\mathcal{P_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
.

We now present a characterization of weakly Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators at every point, analogous to the Dunford-Pettis case.

Proposition 3.14
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Then,
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
if, and only if, for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, every family of weakly null sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
with
f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f
, we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Proof.

Suppose first that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

Let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, let
(x_{j}^{(i)})
be weakly null sequences in
E_{i}
, and let
(f_{j})\subset F^{\prime}
be such that
f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f
. Then

x_{j}^{(i)}+a_{i}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
T
is weakly Dunford-Pettis at every point, it follows that

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Conversely, suppose the condition holds. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})\subset E_{i}
and
(f_{j})\subset F^{\prime}
be such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f.

Define
y_{j}^{(i)}=x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}
, so that
y_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
. Then

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)=f_{j}\big(T(y_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,y_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big).

By hypothesis,

f_{j}\big(T(y_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,y_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Therefore,
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Following the same line of reasoning as in Proposition
2.10
, and denoting by
\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}
the class of all weakly Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators between Banach spaces that satisfy condition
(CH1)
, we obtain the following result.

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then,

\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{(CH1)}}}=\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}.

Using arguments analogous to those in Section
2.1
, the following results can be established.

Theorem 3.16
.

(a)

The class
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
is a symmetric Banach multi-ideal.

(b)

The class
(\mathcal{P_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

(c)

The sequence
(\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}},\mathcal{P_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev,n}}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is coherent and compatible with
\mathcal{WDP}
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. Then,

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

Let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, and let
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
be such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\in E_{i}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f\in F^{\prime}.

Then,

\displaystyle\big|f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)-f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\big|f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)-f_{j}\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\big|

\displaystyle\quad+\big|f_{j}\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)-f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\big|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{j}\|\,\big\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big\|+\big|(f_{j}-f)\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\big|.

Since
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}
is weakly convergent, it is bounded. Moreover, since
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}
, we have

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\longrightarrow T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})

in norm. Hence, the first term converges to zero. The second term also converges to zero because
f_{j}\to f
in the weak* topology of
F^{\prime}
.

Therefore,

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big),

which shows that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

Theorem 3.18
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
F
is reflexive, then

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\mathrm{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

We already know from Proposition
3.17
that

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

for any Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
.

To prove the reverse inclusion, let
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Suppose, by contradiction, that there exist
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
and sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}
in
E_{i}
such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,

but

T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\nrightarrow T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m}).

Then, passing to a subsequence if necessary, there exists
\varepsilon>0
such that

\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|\geq\varepsilon

for all
j\in\mathbb{N}
.

By the Hahn-Banach Theorem, for each
j
there exists
f_{j}\in F^{\prime}
with
\|f_{j}\|=1
such that

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)=\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|.

Since
(f_{j})
lies in the unit ball of
F^{\prime}
, by the Banach–Alaoglu Theorem there exist a subsequence
(f_{j_{k}})
and
f\in F^{\prime}
such that

f_{j_{k}}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f.

As
F
is reflexive, the weak* and weak topologies on
F^{\prime}
coincide, hence

f_{j_{k}}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f.

Therefore,

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\|T(x_{j_{k}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{k}}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\|

\displaystyle=f_{j_{k}}\big(T(x_{j_{k}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{k}}^{(m)})-T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)

\displaystyle=f_{j_{k}}\big(T(x_{j_{k}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{k}}^{(m)})\big)-f_{j_{k}}\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Thus,

\displaystyle\varepsilon

\displaystyle\leq\left|f_{j_{k}}\big(T(x_{j_{k}}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j_{k}}^{(m)})\big)-f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\right|

\displaystyle\quad+\left|(f-f_{j_{k}})\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)\right|.

Since
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
f_{j_{k}}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}f
, the first term converges to
0
, and the second term also converges to
0
. This is a contradiction.

Therefore,

\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F),

and the proof is complete.
∎

Another immediate consequence of Theorem
2.11
is the following result.

Proposition 3.19
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
E_{i}
has the Schur property for all
i=1,\dots,m
, then every continuous multilinear mapping from
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
into
F
is weakly Dunford–Pettis at every point. In particular,

\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

By Proposition
3.17
, we have

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)\subset\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Since each
E_{i}
has the Schur property, Theorem
2.11
ensures that

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

The conclusion follows immediately.
∎

Proposition 3.20
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property and the projective tensor product
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
also has the Schur property, then

\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

Since
E_{i}
has the Schur property for all
i=1,\dots,m
and
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
has also the Schur property, then

\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

∎

4
Weakly
∗
Dunford-Pettis Multilinear Operators

We begin by introducing the linear case. As in the previous section, this approach will be useful to show that the class of weakly
∗
Dunford–Pettis multilinear operators does not satisfy the Coherence and Compatibility condition
(CH1)
(see
[
15
]
) and also fails to be a hyper-ideal (see
[
7
]
).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(E;F)
. We say that
T
is
weakly
∗
Dunford–Pettis
if for every sequence
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that

x_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}0,

we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{WDP}^{*}(E;F)
the class of all weakly
∗
Dunford-Pettis operators from
E
into
F
. It is straightforward to verify that
(\mathcal{WDP}^{*},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach operator ideal.

Definition 4.2
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. We say that
T
is a
weakly
∗
Dunford-Pettis
m
-linear operator
if for every sequence
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{for all }i=1,\dots,m,\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}0,

we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow 0.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. A polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
is said to be
weakly* Dunford-Pettis
if for every sequences
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
such that

x_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}0,

we have

f_{j}(P(x_{j}))\longrightarrow 0.

We denote by
\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}
and
\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}}
the classes of weakly* Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators and homogeneous polynomials, respectively.

Proposition 4.4
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces and
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Then,
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
if, and only if, for every
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, every family of weakly null sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and every sequence
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
with
f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f
, we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Proof.

Suppose first that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.

Let
(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, let
(x_{j}^{(i)})
be weakly null sequences in
E_{i}
, and let
(f_{j})\subset F^{\prime}
be such that
f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f
. Then

x_{j}^{(i)}+a_{i}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}

for all
i=1,\dots,m
. Since
T
is weakly* Dunford-Pettis at every point, it follows that

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Conversely, suppose the condition holds. Let
(x_{j}^{(i)})\subset E_{i}
and
(f_{j})\subset F^{\prime}
be such that

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f.

Define
y_{j}^{(i)}=x_{j}^{(i)}-a_{i}
, so that
y_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
. Then

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)=f_{j}\big(T(y_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,y_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big).

By hypothesis,

f_{j}\big(T(y_{j}^{(1)}+a_{1},\dots,y_{j}^{(m)}+a_{m})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big).

Therefore,
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
.
∎

It is straightforward to verify that the class
\left(\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}},\|\cdot\|\right)
, endowed with the usual norm, is a Banach multi-ideal. Moreover, following the same ideas as in Proposition
3.8
, we have

\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}}(^{m}E;F)=\mathcal{P}_{\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}}(^{m}E;F),

which ensures that
\left(\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}},\|\cdot\|\right)
is an ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

We now examine the relationship between this class and the ideals of Dunford-Pettis and weakly Dunford-Pettis multilinear operators.

Since every weakly* convergent sequence is bounded, let
T\in\mathcal{L_{DP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. For sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, with
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
with
f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}0
, we have

|f_{j}(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))|\leq\|f_{j}\|\,\|T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\|.

Since
(f_{j})
is bounded and
T
is Dunford-Pettis, it follows that

f_{j}(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))\longrightarrow 0.

Thus,
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
, and therefore

\mathcal{L_{DP}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}.

Now, let
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and suppose that
x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
in
E_{i}
for all
i=1,\dots,m
, and
f_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}0
in
F^{\prime}
. Since weak convergence implies weak* convergence, we have
f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}0
. Hence,

|f_{j}(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)}))|\longrightarrow 0,

which shows that
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
. Therefore,

\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}}.

Definition 4.5
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. A mapping
T\in\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
is said to be a
weakly* Dunford-Pettis
m
-linear operator at the point

(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\in E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
if for every sequences
(x_{j}^{(i)})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E_{i}
,
i=1,\dots,m
, and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
satisfying

x_{j}^{(i)}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a_{i}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f\in F^{\prime},

we have

f_{j}\big(T(x_{j}^{(1)},\dots,x_{j}^{(m)})\big)\longrightarrow f\big(T(a_{1},\dots,a_{m})\big)

as
j\to\infty
.

When
T
is a weakly* Dunford-Pettis multilinear operator at every point of
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
, we say that
T
is
weakly* Dunford-Pettis at every point
and we denote the class of all such operators by
\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}
.

Analogously, we define:

Definition 4.6
.

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and let
E
and
F
be Banach spaces. A polynomial
P\in\mathcal{P}(^{m}E;F)
is said to be a
weakly* Dunford-Pettis
m
-homogeneous polynomial at the point

a\in E
if for every sequences
(x_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset E
and
(f_{j})_{j=1}^{\infty}\subset F^{\prime}
satisfying

x_{j}\overset{w}{\longrightarrow}a\quad\text{and}\quad f_{j}\overset{w^{*}}{\longrightarrow}f\in F^{\prime},

we have

f_{j}(P(x_{j}))\longrightarrow f(P(a))

as
j\to\infty
.

When
P
is a weakly* Dunford-Pettis homogeneous polynomial at every point of
E
, we say that
P
is
weakly* Dunford-Pettis at every point
, and we denote the class of all such polynomials by
\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}
.

It is important to note that, since
\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}}
, the class
\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}
inherits several properties from
\mathcal{L_{WDP}}
established in the previous section. However, it is worth emphasizing that, using the same operator as in Example
3.11
, one can readily verify that the class
\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}
does not satisfy condition
(CH1)
of
[
15
]
.

Following the same ideas as in the previous sections, we can obtain the following result.

Theorem 4.7
.

(a)

The class
(\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
is a symmetric Banach multi-ideal.

(b)

\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{(CH1)}}}=\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}
.

(c)

The class
(\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}},\|\cdot\|)
is a Banach ideal of homogeneous polynomials.

(d)

The sequence
(\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev,n}}},\mathcal{P_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev,n}}})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is coherent and compatible with
\mathcal{WDP^{*}}
.

(e)

Let
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,
E_{1},\dots,E_{m+n}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
T\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)
and
Q\in\mathcal{L}(E_{m+1},\dots,E_{m+n})
, then

QT\in\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m+n};F)

and
\|QT\|\leq\|Q\|\,\|T\|
.

Since weakly and weakly* convergent sequences are, in particular, bounded, and weak convergence implies weak* convergence, we have

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}.

Moreover, it follows from Theorem
3.18
that, if
F
is reflexive, then

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Following the same arguments as in the previous sections, we obtain:

Proposition 4.8
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If
E_{i}
has the Schur property for all
i=1,\dots,m
, then every continuous multilinear operator from
E_{1}\times\cdots\times E_{m}
into
F
is weakly* Dunford-Pettis at every point. In particular,

\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

For any Banach spaces
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
, we have

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}\subset\mathcal{L}.

If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property, then by Theorem
2.11
we obtain

\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F),

and the conclusion follows.
∎

As a consequence, we obtain:

Proposition 4.9
.

Let
E_{1},\dots,E_{m}
and
F
be Banach spaces. If each
E_{i}
has the Schur property and
E_{1}\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}\cdots\hat{\otimes}_{\pi}E_{m}
also has the Schur property, then

\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)=\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F).

Proof.

Under the assumptions, we have

\displaystyle\mathcal{L_{DP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L_{WDP^{*}}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L_{WDP}^{\text{ev}}}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F)

\displaystyle=\mathcal{DP}\circ\mathcal{L}(E_{1},\dots,E_{m};F),

which completes the proof.
∎
