Abstract
. The study of word maps and Waring-like problems has been widely pursued for finite simple groups, algebraic groups, and Lie groups. In this article, we study Engel word maps e m ( x , y ) = [ ⋯ [ [ x , y ] , y ] , ⋯ , y ] e_{m}(x,y)=\left[\cdots\left[[x,y],y\right],\cdots,y\right] on certain linear groups over local rings, namely, SL 2 ​ ( ℛ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{R}) and PSL 2 ​ ( ℛ ) \mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{R}) . We consider the commutative ring ℛ \mathcal{R} to be either a complete, local principal ideal ring 𝒪 \mathcal{O} , or a local principal ideal ring of finite length 𝒪 ℓ \mathcal{O}_{\ell} . Suppose the characteristic of the residue field k ≅ 𝔽 q k\cong\mathbb{F}_{q} is ≠ 2 \neq 2 . Under some mild conditions on q q , we show that there exists a constant q 0 ​ ( m ) q_{0}(m) , such that for all q ≥ q 0 ​ ( m ) q\geq q_{0}(m) , all lifts in SL 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}) of non-scalar elements of SL 2 ​ ( k ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(k) , are in the image of the m m -th Engel word over SL 2 ​ ( 𝒪 ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}) . 1.
Introduction

In 1951, Øystein Ore conjectured (see
[
30
]
) that for a non-Abelian finite simple group, every element is a single commutator. It led to several developments in group theory and related areas over the past half-century. Ore himself proved that every element of the alternating group
\mathcal{A}_{n}
can be written as a single commutator. Over the years, through the work of several mathematicians, notably by R. C. Thompson
[
40
,
41
]
; Gow and O. Bonten
[
7
]
; J. Neb
\ddot{\text{u}}
ser, H. Pahlings, and E. Cleuvers
[
29
]
; E. W. Ellers and N. L. Gordeev
[
15
]
and many more, the conjecture was partly solved. It was completely settled in 2010 by Liebeck, O’Brien, Shalev, and Tiep
[
26
]
, who relied on the character theory of finite groups of Lie type and probabilistic methods. We refer to Malle’s survey article
[
28
]
for more details.

The Ore’s conjecture led to several significant developments in the study of images of
word maps
on groups and other algebraic structures. Given a group
G
and a word
w
, a member of a free group
\mathcal{F}_{d}
with
d
many generators, one can define a map
\widetilde{w}\colon G^{d}\rightarrow G
by evaluation, which is called
word map
on
G
induced by the word
w
. One of the fundamental questions is to find the smallest positive integer
m
such that every element of
G
can be written as a product of
m
many elements of
\widetilde{w}(G):=\widetilde{w}(G^{d})
. This is also called the Waring-like problem. Over the past few decades, several foundational results have been proved for word maps over various groups, including linear algebraic groups, finite groups of Lie type, classical groups, and Lie groups (see
[
9
]
,
[
10
]
,
[
11
]
,

[
14
]
,
[
24
]
,
[
25
]
,
[
27
]
,

[
33
]
). For more details, we refer to the survey article
[
18
]
. In 1983, Borel proved that the image of any non-trivial word over a connected semisimple linear algebraic group
\mathcal{G}
is dominant, i.e., it contains a Zariski dense open subset. As a consequence, when
K
is algebraically closed field, one gets that
w(G)^{2}=G
, where
G=\mathcal{G}(K)
; see
[
8
]
. For finite simple groups, Shalev has shown that every element of such groups, when the size is large enough, is a product of two elements from the image when non-trivial (see
[
37
]
). Similar to the Waring problem, the result for matrix algebra over a finite field is obtained in
[
22
]
.

The surjectivity and, more generally, images of several specific words have been extensively studied for various groups, especially algebraic groups defined over a field, and some rings. In this article, we focus on the
Engel words
defined on the special linear group
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
where
\mathcal{O}
is a (commutative) local ring (with identity). Let
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\langle x,y\rangle
be a free group generated by two elements. The
m
-th Engel word
e_{m}(x,y)
in
\mathcal{F}_{2}
is defined recursively as follows:

e_{1}(x,y)=[x,y]=xyx^{-1}y^{-1},\ e_{2}(x,y)=[[x,y],y]\ \ \text{and}\ \ e_{m}(x,y)=[e_{m-1}(x,y),y]

for
m\geq 2
. The induced
m
-th Engel word map is
e_{m}^{\mathcal{O}}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\times\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
given by
(X,Y)\mapsto e_{m}^{\mathcal{O}}(X,Y)
, where
e_{1}^{\mathcal{O}}(X,Y)=XYX^{-1}Y^{-1}
is simply the commutator map. In
[
40
]
, Thompson proved surjectivity of the commutator map on
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
where
k
is a field. In 2007, Shalev conjectured (Conjecture 2.8 and 2.9
[
36
]
) that the
n
-th
Engel word
is almost surjective for any non-abelian finite simple group. Bandman, Garion, and Grunewald (see
[
3
]
) showed that the
n
-th
Engel word
is surjective for the groups
\mathrm{PSL}(2,q)
and almost surjective for
\mathrm{SL}(2,q)
provided
q
is large enough. Following that, in
[
4
]
and
[
6
]
the surjectivity of
Engel words
for
\mathrm{PSL}(2,\mathbb{C})
and
\mathrm{PSL}(2,K)
where
K
is algebraically closed field, were studied. In
[
19
]
, Gordeev studied Engel words on algebraic groups of small ranks. In Section
2.1
, we list some known results in one place.

In this article, we are interested in looking at the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}
and
\rm{PSL}_{2}
over certain rings. This problem has been studied for some rings, such as
p
-adic. Shalev in
[
38
]
conjectured (Conjecture 1.3) the following:

Conjecture 1

(Shalev)
.

Every element of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
where
p
is a prime and
n\geq 2
(
p>3
when
n=2
) is a commutator.

Further, Shalev proposed a similar problem (Problem 1.4) for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z})
and claimed that these would be hard problems. In response, Avni, Gelander, Kassabov, and Shalev (see
[
2
]
) proved that every lift of non-scalars is a commutator in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, for any local ring
\mathcal{O}
with residue field
k
that contains more than
n+1
elements. An approach to solve this problem would be to lift solutions of the equation from the residue field where answers are known. In
[
32
]
,
[
31
]
, the authors initiated a study to lift elements, such as regular elements and cyclic elements of
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
to
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
as images of certain maps. With this in mind, let us make our question more precise, which we try to answer in this article.

Question 1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a local principal ideal ring with residue field
k\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
of odd characteristic.

(1)

Let
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
which maps to
\bar{A}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(q)
. In
[
3
, Theorem 5.1]
, almost surjectivity (that is except
-I
) of Engel words over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
is proved for large enough
q
with an estimate of
q\geq q_{0}(m)=2\cdot 3^{2^{m+2}}
. Under the same assumption that
\bar{A}\in\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{k})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
, do we have
A\in\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
?

(2)

The surjectivity of the Engel word is known for
\rm{PSL}_{2}(q)
. Is it true that the Engel word is surjective on
\rm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
?

We answer these questions in this article. When
\mathcal{O}
is a complete local principal ideal ring, we show that for large enough
q
(odd), every non-scalar element that is in the image of
m
-th Engel word over
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
is in the image of the
m
-th Engel word in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
. This is our Theorem
10.1
. Further, when
q
is large enough, we show (see Theorem
10.4
) that the Engel word map is surjective on
\text{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
where
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is a local principal ring of length
2
. This result generalizes the same result proved by Bandman et. al. in
[
3
]
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The method for solving the lifting problem relies on dealing with several cases separately for regular semisimple elements and other cyclic elements for
\mathcal{O}_{2}
and extending the results to each finite length level. Some of the key ideas are motivated by the methods used in
[
2
]
and the properties of the trace map in
[
3
]
. We hope these ideas will evolve further and help solve Shalev’s conjecture mentioned above.

2.2.
Notation and Conventions

In this section, we recall some definitions and set the notation for the rest of the article.
A commutative ring
\mathcal{R}
with unity is said to be
complete with respect to an ideal
I
if the canonical map
\mathcal{R}\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{R}/I^{j}\mathcal{R}
is an isomorphism.
We denote by
\mathcal{O}
a local principal ideal ring that is complete with respect to its unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(\pi)
. We assume that the residue field
k
has characteristic not equal to
2
. For
\ell\geq 1
, we denote the quotient ring
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}=\mathcal{O}/\pi^{\ell}\mathcal{O}
. This is a local principal ideal ring of length
\ell
with unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\ell}=(\pi_{\ell})
, where
\pi_{\ell}=\pi+(\pi^{\ell})
. The canonical map
\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
is denoted by
\theta_{\ell}
. The kernel of the natural surjection
\theta_{j}\colon\mathcal{O}_{j+1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{j}
is
ker(\theta_{j})=\frac{\mathfrak{m}^{j}}{\mathfrak{m}^{j+1}}
. Therefore,
(ker(\theta_{j}))^{2}=0
for each
j\geq 1
. Note that
\mathcal{O}_{1}=\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}\cong k
and we simply denote
\theta_{1}=\theta
.

For a local ring
\mathcal{A}
with residue field
k
and unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}
, let
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
denotes the set of all
2\times 2
matrices with entries from
\mathcal{A}
. The general linear group
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is the set of all elements
X\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
such that
\det(X)\in\mathcal{A}^{\times}
. The special linear group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is the set of all elements
X\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
with
\det(X)=1
. The quotient map
\theta\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow k=\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}
induces a canonical map
\theta\colon\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{M}_{2}(k)
denoted by
X\mapsto\overline{X}
. Thus, we get a map
\theta\colon\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)
and
\theta\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
. Further, note that the quotient map
\theta\colon\mathcal{A}\rightarrow k
also induces a surjective map on the polynomial ring
\theta\colon\mathcal{A}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]\rightarrow k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
defined by reduction of the coefficients under
\theta
. By abuse of notation, all the above reduction maps are denoted simply by
\theta
, usually clear from the context. Similarly, all the reductions from
\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}
level to
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
level are denoted by
\theta_{\ell}
.

The commutator word map
e_{1}^{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
given by
(X,Y)\mapsto XYX^{-1}Y^{-1}=[X,Y]
induces a commutator word map at residue field level
\overline{e}_{1}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
. The induced
(m+1)
-th Engel word map
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{A}}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is defined recursively as
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)=e_{1}^{\mathcal{A}}(e_{m}^{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y),Y)
for
m\geq 1
. We denote
e_{0}^{\mathcal{A}}(X,Y)=X
. The reduced Engel word map over the field level is denoted by
\bar{e}_{m+1}\colon\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
which is defined in a similar way (thus
\bar{e}_{m+1}
is
e_{m+1}^{k}
). The notation
\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{A}})
denotes the image of the
(m+1)
-th Engel word
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{A}}
over the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
. Therefore,
\mathrm{Im}(\bar{e}_{m+1})
means the image of the
m+1
-th Engel word
\bar{e}_{m+1}
over the group
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
.

For a polynomial
F(t)=\Sigma_{i=0}^{r}a_{i}t^{i}\in\mathcal{R}[t]
, its formal derivative with respect to
t
is denoted by the notation
\frac{df}{dt}
, which is given by
\Sigma_{i=0}^{r}ia_{i}t^{i-1}\in\mathcal{R}[t]
. In the same spirit, for a polynomial
F(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{R}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
we denote the formal partial derivative of
F
with respect to
x_{i}
by the notation
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}
, which is nothing but the formal derivative of
F
with respect to
x_{i}
considering
x_{1},\ldots,x_{i-1},x_{i+1},\ldots,x_{n}
as constants. For a polynomial
f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
, the Jacobian matrix corresponding to
f
is given by
J_{f}=\left[\begin{array}[]{cccccccc}\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{1}}&\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{2}}&\cdots&\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{n}}\end{array}\right]
. Moreover,
J_{f}|_{\alpha_{0}}

denotes the Jacobian evaluated at
\alpha_{0}
for some
\alpha_{0}\in k^{n}
. A polynomial
F(t)\in\mathcal{O}_{2}[t]
is said to be
fundamental irreducible
if
\bar{F}(t)=f(t)
is irreducible in
k[t]
.

An element
X\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is said to be
regular semisimple
if
\overline{X}
is regular semisimple in
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(k)
. For a ring
\mathcal{R}
and two elements
A,B\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
, the notation
A\sim_{\mathcal{R}}B
means that there exists
C\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
such that
A=CBC^{-1}
. The
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy class of
X\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
is the set containing conjugates of
X
by the elements of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{A})
. For any
X\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
and
g\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
the notation
X^{g}
is used to denote
g^{-1}Xg
and the notation
Ad_{g}(X)
denotes
gXg^{-1}
.
For a commutative ring
\mathcal{R}
with unity and a polynomial
\mathfrak{f}(t)=t^{2}+c_{1}t+c_{0}\in\mathcal{R}[t]
, the companion matrix
C_{\mathfrak{f}}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
of degree
2
is the matrix

\begin{pmatrix}0&-c_{0}\\
1&-c_{1}\end{pmatrix}.

The zero matrix in
\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
is denoted by a boldfaced zero
\mathbf{0}
. The characteristic polynomial of
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
is denoted by the symbol
\chi(A)(t)
, which is
\det(tI-A)
. It is well known that square matrices over commutative rings with unity satisfy their characteristic equation. Moreover, for a commutative ring
\mathcal{R}
with unity,
\mathrm{tr}(AB)=\mathrm{tr}(BA)
for any two matrices
A,B\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathcal{R})
. For any
r\in\mathbb{N}
, the
r
-th Engel word on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
will be denoted by
e_{r}^{\mathcal{R}}
. Thus, the trace of the
r
-th Engel word map is denoted by
\mathrm{tr}(e_{r}(X,Y))
.

2.3.
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy classes of elements in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})

Let
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be a local principal ideal ring of length
2
. In this section, we provide a brief description of the
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy classes of the elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
by looking at their splitting into different types of conjugacy classes. A
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy class of an element
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
is the set
\{P^{-1}AP\mid P\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\}
and we denote this by
[A]_{\mathrm{GL}}
.

Example 2.1
.

Consider two matrices
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
and
B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
, where
\mathcal{O}_{2}
has the residue field
\mathbb{R}
. These two matrices are conjugate in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. More specifically, we can take
P=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
such that
P^{-1}BP=A
. But these two matrices are not conjugate in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. Because, if so, then the reduced matrices
\bar{A}
and
\bar{B}
must be conjugate by an element of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
. If possible suppose we have
P=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a&b\\
c&d\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{R})
such that
PAP^{-1}=B
. This implies
a^{2}+b^{2}=-1
, i.e.
x^{2}+y^{2}=-1
has a solution in
\mathbb{R}^{2}
; which is a contradiction.

Therefore in what follows, we consider the
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy classes of the elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
by seeing these inside
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. That would be enough for our purpose as we don’t really require exact
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
conjugacy classes in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

Let
A
be a matrix in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that there exists
\mathbf{g}_{1}
and
\mathbf{g}_{2}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that
e_{1}(\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2})=A
. Then for any
P\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
we have
e_{1}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2})=P^{-1}AP
, where
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{i}=P^{-1}\mathbf{g}_{i}P
for
i=1,2
. Note that
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}
are in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
again. Hence, an element of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
is in
\mathrm{Im}(e_{1})
if and only if the whole
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy class of that element is in
\mathrm{Im}(e_{1})
. The same would apply to the images of any Engel word
e_{m}
.

Since,
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\unlhd\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
, we consider the group action via conjugation defined by

\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\rightarrow\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})

(\textbf{g},X)\mapsto\textbf{g}\cdot X=\textbf{g}^{-1}X\textbf{g}.

Let us denote the orbit of
X
with respect to the above action by
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\centerdot X
. Therefore, we can write

\displaystyle\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})=\bigcup\limits_{X\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})}\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\cdot X=\bigcup\limits_{X\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})}\{P^{-1}XP\mid P\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\}.

In
[
5
, page 1290]
; Leigh, Cliff, and Wen have listed the similarity class information for matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
, where
\mathcal{R}
is a finite commutative local principal ideal ring with unity, with the residue field of odd characteristic. Their idea is based on constructing a subgroup chain that remains invariant under the action of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
by conjugation. The length of the chain is finite, which is critical. In fact, the same construction can be carried over to any local principal ideal ring of finite length and residue field with odd characteristic exponent. We make a note of the families of
\mathrm{GL}
-conjugacy classes of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
below:

(1)

S(\alpha):\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&\alpha\end{array}\right);\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times};\alpha^{2}=1

(2)

D(\alpha,\delta,i):\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&\delta\end{array}\right);\alpha,\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times};\alpha-\delta\in\pi_{2}^{i}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times};\alpha\delta=1;0\leq i<2

(3)

H(\alpha,\beta,i):\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\pi_{2}^{i+1}\beta\\
\pi_{2}^{i}&\alpha\end{array}\right);\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times},\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}/\pi_{2}^{1-i}\mathcal{O}_{2};\alpha^{2}-\pi_{2}^{2i+1}\beta=1;0\leq i<2

(4)

H^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,i):\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\pi_{2}^{i}\epsilon\beta\\
\pi_{2}^{i}\beta&\alpha\end{array}\right);\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{2},\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times},\alpha^{2}-\epsilon\beta^{2}\pi_{2}^{2i}=1;0\leq i<2;\epsilon
is a non square unit in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

Recall,
\mathfrak{m}_{2}=(\pi_{2})
is the unique maximal ideal of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

Remark 2.2
.

When
k
is algebraically closed, the
Corollary

3.4
implies that
any unit in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
must be a square.
Therefore a fixed unit
\epsilon
as above must be a square. In that case,
D(\alpha,\delta,0)
family and
H^{\prime}(\alpha,\beta,0)
family coincides. Therefore, any regular semisimple element in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
must be split.

We explore the
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-similarity classes of elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
in more detail and present them more clearly.

Family (1).
Take
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\\
&\alpha\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. As
\theta(A)=\bar{A}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
,we have
\bar{\alpha}=\pm 1
. Therefore
\alpha
is of the form
1+m_{1}
or
-1+m_{2}
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
, where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}
. When
\alpha=1+m_{1}
using
\alpha^{2}=1
, we obtain
m_{1}=0
. Similarly, for
\alpha=-1+m_{2}
, we have
m_{2}=0
. Hence, in this case, the only possibilities for
A
are
\pm I
.

Family (2).
Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\\
&\delta\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

(1)

\mathbf{i=0}:
In this case
\delta=\alpha^{-1}
and
\alpha-\delta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
.

(2)

\mathbf{i=1}:
In this case we have

\alpha

\delta

or

respectively, for some

. Now from the
\alpha\delta=1

\delta=1-\pi_{2}a_{1}
when
\alpha=1+\pi_{2}a_{1}
and
\delta=-1-\pi_{2}a_{2}
when
\alpha=-1+\pi_{2}a_{2}
respectively. Further,
\alpha-\delta\in\pi_{2}\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
implies that
a_{1},a_{2}
must be in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
.

Family (3).
Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\pi_{2}^{i+1}\beta\\
\pi_{2}^{i}&\alpha\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

(1)

\mathbf{i=0}:
In this case there are two possibilities. (a) If
\beta
is any non-unit then
\alpha=\pm 1
from the determinant condition.
(b) If
\beta
is a unit then a
\pi_{2}^{i+1}\beta\in\pi_{2}^{i+1}\mathcal{O}_{2}
(see the additive isomorphism
\theta
on page 1290,
[

]
). Therefore, we can write
\pi_{2}^{i+1}\beta=\pi_{2}^{i+1}v
for some unit

in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. From the determinant condition, we get
\alpha^{2}-\pi_{2}v=1
. Reducing this equation mod
\mathfrak{m}
, we get
\bar{\alpha}=\pm 1
. Therefore
\alpha
can be of the form
1+m_{1}
or
-1+m_{2}
where
m_{1},m_{2}\in\mathfrak{m}
. In these cases we obtain
m_{1}=2^{-1}\pi_{2}v
and
m_{2}=-2^{-1}\pi_{2}v
.

(2)

\mathbf{i=1}:
In this case
\pi_{2}^{i+1}\beta=0
. Therefore, the determinant condition gives
\alpha=\pm 1
.

Family (4).
Let
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\pi_{2}^{i}\epsilon\beta\\
\pi_{2}^{i}\beta&\alpha\end{array}\right)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

(1)

\mathbf{i=0}:
In this case
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&\epsilon\beta\\
\beta&\alpha\end{array}\right)
.

(2)

\mathbf{i=1}:
In this case the determinant condition gives
\alpha=\pm 1
again.

3.
Lifting a solution of multivariable polynomial over
\mathcal{O}_{2}

In this section,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
denotes a local principal ideal ring of length
2
which is
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^{2}
. When the residue field
k
is perfect, there is a complete classification of local principal ideal rings of length two with residue field
k
; see
[
34
, Theorem 2.1]
. Consider a field
k
and a monic polynomial
f(t)\in k[t]
in one variable. A root
\alpha_{0}
of
f(t)
is a
simple root
if

\frac{df}{dt}\Large|_{t=\alpha_{0}}\neq 0

where
\frac{df}{dt}
denotes the first order formal derivative of
f(t)
with respect to
t
. Similarly, one can define the notion of a simple root for multivariable polynomials over an arbitrary field
k
; see
[
35
]
, page 15.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a monic polynomial over the field
k
and
\alpha
be a root of
f
. Then,
\alpha
is said to be a
simple root
of
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
if at least one of the formal partial derivatives
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\alpha}
is nonzero for some
1\leq i\leq n
.

Now, we study when a simple root of the reduction modulo
\mathfrak{m}
of a monic polynomial in
\mathcal{O}_{2}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
lifts to a root of the original polynomial over
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

Lemma 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be a local principal ideal ring of length two with residue field
k
with characteristic
\neq 2
. Let
F(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in\mathcal{O}_{2}[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
be a monic polynomial such that the reduced polynomial
f(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})\in k[x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n}]
has a simple root
(s_{1},s_{2},\ldots,s_{n})\in k^{n}
, then this simple root can be lifted to a root
(\hat{s}_{1},\hat{s}_{2},\ldots,\hat{s}_{n})\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{n}
of the polynomial
F(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
such that
\theta(\hat{s}_{i})=s_{i}
for all
i
.

Proof.

Let
\sigma=(s_{1},\ldots,s_{n})
be a simple root of
f(x_{1},\ldots,x_{n})
in
k^{n}
. Without loss of generality, let
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\sigma}\neq 0
for some
i\leq r
where
1\leq r\leq n
. We make separate cases depending on the field characteristic
k
.

(1)
When the characteristic of
k
is zero.
Take any lift
\check{\sigma}=(\check{s}_{1},\check{s}_{2},\ldots,\check{s}_{n})
of
\sigma
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{n}
. We provide a choice of
(m_{1},m_{2},\ldots,m_{n})\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}^{n}
for
\check{\sigma}_{0}=(\check{s}_{1}+m_{1},\check{s}_{2}+m_{2},\ldots,\check{s}_{n}+m_{n})
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{n}
such that
F(\check{\sigma}_{0})=0
, where
m_{i}\in\mathfrak{m}_{2}
for all
i
. Over any commutative ring
\mathcal{R}
with unity, and for a polynomial
F(\textbf{x})
in
n
-variables over
\mathcal{R}
, one can write

F(\textbf{x}+\textbf{y})=F(\textbf{x})+\sum_{i=1}^{n}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}y_{i}+\sum_{1\leq i,j\leq n}C_{ij}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})y_{i}y_{j}

where
C_{ij}(\textbf{x},\textbf{y})\in\mathcal{R}[\textbf{x},\textbf{y}]
;
\mathbf{x}=(x_{1},x_{2},\ldots,x_{n})
and
\mathbf{y}=(y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n})
. Now
F(\check{\sigma}_{0})=0
implies
F(\check{\sigma})+\Sigma_{i=1}^{r}m_{i}\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}=0
. As the reductions of
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}
are
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\sigma}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,r
; which are units in
k
by assumption. Therefore
\frac{\partial F}{\partial X_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}
must be units in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,r
. Now choose

m_{i}=-F(\check{\sigma})\left(r\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for i=1,2, \ldots,r}

and for
i=r+1,r+2,\ldots,n
any element of
\mathfrak{m}
will work as a choice of
m_{i}
. This is because
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}
are non-units in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Therefore, we get the required choice for
m_{i}
, and we take
\hat{s}_{i}=\check{s}_{i}+m_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

(2)
When the characteristic of
k
is
p
, an odd prime.

•

Case I
(
p\nmid r
) :
Choose

m_{i}=-F(\check{\sigma})\left(r\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for i=1,2,\ldots,r}.

•

Case II
(
p|r
) :
Choose

m_{i}=-F(\check{\sigma})\left(2(r-1)\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}\hskip 5.0pt\text{for i=1,2,\ldots,r-1},\quad m_{r}=-F(\check{\sigma})\left(2\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}\right)^{-1}

and for
i=r+1,r+2,\ldots,n
any element of
\mathfrak{m}
will work as a choice of
m_{i}
. Because then
\frac{\partial F}{\partial x_{i}}|_{\check{\sigma}}
are non-units in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Therefore, we get proper choices for
m_{i}
and we take
\hat{s}_{i}=\check{s}_{i}+m_{i}
for
i=1,2,\ldots,n
.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

Since
(ker(\theta_{j}))^{2}=0
for all
j\geq 1
, this method works for lifting simple roots of polynomial equations from the residue field to any
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
of any finite length
\ell\geq 2
. Hence, for a complete local principal ideal ring with residue field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
, this lifting strategy can be applied.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be a local principal ideal ring of length two with perfect residue field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
. Then, any
L
-th power map over
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, of which reduction with respect to
\theta
is surjective over
k
, is also surjective over the unit group
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
where
gcd(L,p^{\prime})=1
and
p^{\prime}
is the characteristic exponent of
k
.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be a unit in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Consider
F(t)=t^{L}-\alpha
. Since the reduced polynomial
f(t)=t^{L}-\bar{\alpha}
under
\theta
must have a solution in
k
, let us denote a solution of it by
\beta_{0}
. As
\bar{\alpha}
must be a unit in
k
, therefore
\beta_{0}
must be a unit. Hence
f^{\prime}(\beta_{0})=L\beta_{0}^{L-1}
must be non-zero in
k
. Hence, by Lemma
3.2
we get
F(t)=0
has a solution in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
.
∎

An example of a ring
\mathcal{O}_{2}
used in Corollary
3.4
, one can consider those
\mathcal{O}_{2}
with a perfect residue field
k
, which is algebraically closed.

4.
The Trace Polynomial

The trace polynomial corresponding to the commutator word on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
plays a crucial role in various aspects when the characteristic of
k
is zero. For a given
\mu\in k
, we have
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}=\{(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in k^{3}\mid x_{1}^{2}+x_{2}^{2}+x_{3}^{2}-x_{1}x_{2}x_{3}=\mu\}
, a Markoff surface in
\mathbb{A}_{k}^{3}
. Its vanishing polynomial equation is satisfied by commutators over
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
whose trace value is
\mu-2
. In
[
17
]
, there has also been a study related to integral points and the Hasse principle over the Markoff surface
\mathcal{M}_{\mu}
when
\mu
is a given integer. We need to deal with this surface (for Engel words) over
\mathcal{O}_{2}
naturally. We would need to recover matrices in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
from the existence of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
points (under certain conditions) on these kinds of surfaces defined over
\mathcal{O}_{2}
by using given trace values of the
m+1
-th Engel word on
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. Further, it will require translating the process at each
\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
level for regular semisimple elements; we discuss this in more detail in Section
9.1
.

Let
K
be a field and
\textbf{h}_{1},\textbf{h}_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(K)
. Cossey et. al.
[
12
]
proved that
\mathrm{tr}(\textbf{h}_{1}\textbf{h}_{2})+\mathrm{tr}(\textbf{h}_{1}\textbf{h}_{2}^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}(\textbf{h}_{1})\mathrm{tr}(\textbf{h}_{2})
. In fact, this holds true for
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
, where
\mathcal{R}
is any commutative ring with unity. This can be easily seen from the proof of
[
12
, Lemma 5.2.1]
.

Now we look at the expression of trace of
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}}
where
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(R)
. We use the suffix
\mathrm{GL}
in the notation
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)
to denote that
A,B
be any arbitrary matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
and to distinguish the case from the case when
A,B
are the members of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
only.

Lemma 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative ring with unity and
A,B
be two elements of
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
. Then,

\mathrm{tr}(e_{1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})=[d_{B}s_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}+u_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}+d_{A}v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-s_{\mathrm{GL}}v_{\mathrm{GL}}u_{\mathrm{GL}}-2d_{A}d_{B}]d_{A}^{-1}d_{B}^{-1}

and

\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})=[d_{B}s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}^{2}+2v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-2d_{B}]d_{B}^{-1}\ \ \ \text{for}\ m\geq 1

where
s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}=\mathrm{tr}(e_{m}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})
. Moreover
s_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(A)
,
u_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(AB)
,
v_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(B)
,
d_{A}=\det(A),\ d_{B}=\det(B)
.

Proof.

Let
A,B\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})
. Then
\chi(A)(A)=\mathbf{0}
implies
A^{2}-\mathrm{tr}(A)A+\det(A)I=\mathbf{0}
. This implies

A-\mathrm{tr}(A)I+\det(A)A^{-1}=\mathbf{0}.

Multiplying both side by
B
from the left, we obtain:

BA-\mathrm{tr}(A)B+\det(A)BA^{-1}=\mathbf{0}.

Taking trace of both side, we get

\mathrm{tr}(BA^{-1})=[\mathrm{tr}(A)\mathrm{tr}(B)-\mathrm{tr}(AB)]\det(A)^{-1}.

Now
e_{1}(A,B)=(AB)(BA)^{-1}
. Let
X=AB
and
Y=BA
. Therefore
\mathrm{tr}(e_{1}(A,B))=\mathrm{tr}(XY^{-1})
. By the above trace expression, we get

\mathrm{tr}(e_{1}(A,B))=[\mathrm{tr}(X)\mathrm{tr}(Y)-\mathrm{tr}(XY)]det(Y)^{-1}.

Let
s_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(A)
,
u_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(AB)
,
v_{\mathrm{GL}}=\mathrm{tr}(B)
. Then
\mathrm{tr}(XY)=\mathrm{tr}(AB^{2}A)=\mathrm{tr}(A^{2}B^{2})
. Again from the respective characteristic equations of
A
and
B
we have
A^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(A)A-\det(A)I
and
B^{2}=\mathrm{tr}(B)B-\det(B)I
. Substituting these values in the expression
\mathrm{tr}(XY)=\mathrm{tr}(A^{2}B^{2})
, we get

\mathrm{tr}(XY)=s_{\mathrm{GL}}v_{\mathrm{GL}}u_{\mathrm{GL}}-d_{B}s_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-d_{A}v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}+2d_{A}d_{B};

where
d_{A}=\det(A),\ d_{B}=\det(B)
. Therefore

\mathrm{tr}(e_{1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})=[d_{B}s_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}+u_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}+d_{A}v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-s_{\mathrm{GL}}v_{\mathrm{GL}}u_{\mathrm{GL}}-2d_{A}d_{B}]d_{A}^{-1}d_{B}^{-1}.

For
m\geq 1
,
d_{e_{m}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}}}=1
and
\mathrm{tr}(e_{m}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}}B)=v_{\mathrm{GL}}
. Therefore for
m\geq 1
;

\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})=[d_{B}s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}^{2}+2v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}v_{\mathrm{GL}}^{2}-2d_{B}]d_{B}^{-1}

where
s_{m_{\mathrm{GL}}}=\mathrm{tr}(e_{m}^{\mathcal{R}}(A,B)_{\mathrm{GL}})
.
∎

Corollary 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a commutative ring with unity and
A_{1},A_{2}
be two elements of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{R})

5.
Lift of Scalars as Commutators in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})

In this section, we look at the lift of
\pm I\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
to
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
if they are commutators. Recall,
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is a local principal ideal ring of length
2
. An element
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
is said to be regular semisimple if its reduction under
\theta
, i.e.,
\bar{A}
, is regular semisimple. That is, the characteristic polynomial of
\bar{A}
is separable over
k
. If
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
is regular semisimple, then
\mathrm{tr}(\bar{A})
cannot be
\pm 2
. This is because, if it is
\pm 2
then the characteristic polynomial of
\bar{A}
will be
(x\pm 1)^{2}
, which means
\bar{A}
has a repeated eigenvalue. We note that the smallest degree monic polynomial that annihilates
A
is said to be a
minimal polynomial
for
A
. It turns out that (see
[
31
, section 3]
), for a regular semisimple
A
, its minimal polynomial is uniquely determined, which is equal to the characteristic polynomial
\chi(A)(t)
.

Lemma 5.1
.

Let
A,B
be two regular semisimple elements in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that
B=TAT^{-1}
for some
T\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. Let
x-\alpha
be a divisor of the characteristic polynomial of
A
where
\alpha\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
. Then, for each
s\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
there exists a matrix
S\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
with
det(S)=s
such that
B=SAS^{-1}
.

Proof.

As
A\sim_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}B
, both regular semisimples, by
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-conjugacy family information described in Table
4
and by Lemma 2.8
[
31
]
there exists
T_{1},T_{2}\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that
T_{1}AT_{1}^{-1}=T_{2}BT_{2}^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha&0\\
0&\beta\end{array}\right)
for some
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
. Let us take
s_{0}=s(det(T_{2}T_{1}^{-1}))
and consider
S_{0}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}s_{0}&0\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
. Therefore,
S_{0}T_{1}AT_{1}^{-1}S_{0}^{-1}=T_{1}AT_{1}^{-1}=T_{2}BT_{2}^{-1}\implies T_{2}^{-1}S_{0}T_{1}AT_{1}^{-1}S_{0}^{-1}T_{2}=B
. The element
S=T_{2}^{-1}S_{0}T_{1}
works.
∎

Remark 5.2
.

When
|k|\geq 5
of characteristic
\neq 2
there always exists some
\delta\in k
such that
\delta^{2}\neq 1,0
. Pick any element from
k^{\times}\backslash\{\pm 1\}
and denote it by
\delta
. Take a lift say,
\Delta_{0}
of
\delta
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
. Then,
\Delta_{0}\neq\Delta_{0}^{-1}
. Because otherwise
\delta^{2}
will be
1
, which is a contradiction.

Lemma 5.3
.

For
\beta\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
and
\epsilon
a non square unit in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
, the element
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\pi_{2}\epsilon\beta\\
\pi_{2}\beta&1\end{array}\right)
is a commutator, i.e., it belongs to
\mathrm{Im}(e_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{2}})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
.

Proof.

Consider
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&\pi_{2}\epsilon\beta\\
\pi_{2}\beta&1\end{array}\right)
where
\epsilon
is a non-square unit and
\beta
is an unit in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Take,
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
such that
\lambda^{2}\neq 1
and consider
D=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\Delta_{0}&\pi_{2}\\
0&\Delta_{0}^{-1}\end{array}\right)
. There exists
\Delta_{0}\in\mathcal{O}_{2}^{\times}
such that
\bar{\Delta}_{0}\neq\bar{\Delta}_{0}^{-1}
by Remark
5.2
. Now,
D
and
AD
are regular semisimples with the same characteristic polynomial, which has a factor
t-\Delta_{0}
. Therefore,
AD\sim_{\mathcal{O}_{2}}D
by the
\mathcal{O}_{2}
-conjugacy class information listed in Table
4
and Lemma 2.8
[
31
]
. Hence invoking Lemma
5.1
, there exists
S\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that
A=e_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{2}}(S^{-1},D)
.
∎

The following is a well-known result in the field case, which is true more generally.

Proposition 5.4
.

The element
-I
is a commutator, i.e., it belongs to
\mathrm{Im}(e_{1}^{\mathcal{O}_{2}})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
if and only if
-1
can be written as a sum of two squares in the residue field
k
of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
.

Proof.

Suppose we have
A_{0},B_{0}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
such that
[A_{0},B_{0}]=A_{0}B_{0}A_{0}^{-1}B_{0}^{-1}=-I
. This implies
A_{0}B_{0}A_{0}^{-1}=-B_{0}
, hence,
\mathrm{tr}(B_{0})=0
since characteristic of
k\neq 2
. Hence,
B_{0}
satisfies its characteristic polynomial
x^{2}+1
, thus,
B_{0}^{2}=-I
. Over
k
, this gives
\bar{B}_{0}^{2}=-I
and
\bar{B}_{0}=P\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)P^{-1}
for some
P\in GL_{2}(k)
. Let
P^{-1}\bar{A}_{0}P=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}a_{0}&b_{0}\\
c_{0}&d_{0}\end{array}\right)
. Then
\bar{A}_{0}\bar{B}_{0}=-\bar{B}_{0}\bar{A}_{0}
implies
(P^{-1}\bar{A}_{0}P)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)=-\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&-1\\
1&0\end{array}\right)(P^{-1}\bar{A}_{0}P)
. Solving this equation together with the condition
det(\bar{A}_{0})=1
we get
a_{0}^{2}+b_{0}^{2}=-1
in
k
.

Conversely, let there exist
a,b
in
k
such that
a^{2}+b^{2}=-1
. Consider the polynomial
F(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}+1
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}[x,y]
. Then the reduced polynomial
f
over
k
has a root
(a,b)\in k^{2}
. Since
a^{2}+b^{2}=-1
, therefore at least one of

and
b
must be nonzero. Hence, at least one of
\frac{\partial f}{\partial x}|_{(a,b)},\frac{\partial f}{\partial y}|_{(a,b)}
must be nonzero in
k
as
2
is unit in
k
. Thus,
(a,b)
is a simple root of
f(x,y)=x^{2}+y^{2}+1
in
k^{2}
. By taking
n=2
in Lemma
3.2
we get
F(x,y)=0
has a solution
(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in\mathcal{O}_{2}
such that
\bar{\alpha}_{0}=a
and
\bar{\beta}_{0}=b
. This gives,
\alpha_{0}^{2}+\beta_{0}^{2}=-1
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
. Now consider the matrices
Q_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
-1&0\end{array}\right)
and
Q_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\alpha_{0}&\beta_{0}\\
\beta_{0}&-\alpha_{0}\end{array}\right)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
, then
[Q_{1},Q_{2}]=-I
.
∎

Corollary 5.5
.

If

All possible lifts of scalars are listed in row

7.
Commutators in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})

Aner Shalev in
[
38
]
, conjectured that every element of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
for
p
a prime and
n\geq 2
is a commutator (for
n=2
one needs to assume
p>3
). Following that, Avni, Gelander, Kassabov, and Shalev (see
[
2
]
) proved that every lift of non-scalars is a commutator in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
, for any local ring
\mathcal{O}
with residue field
k
that contains more than
n+1
elements. That leaves the difficult task of dealing with the lift of scalars. Indeed, part of this was done when the residue field contains an
n
-th root of unity. This section recalls these results for commutators over
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathbb{Z}_{p})
, fixes some gaps in the proof given earlier, and looks at a few more fresh cases of lifting scalars, enough for our purpose of the case
n=2
at hand. For this, we recall and refine some of the techniques they developed.

The Lie algebra of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
is denoted by
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)=\{P\in\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)\mid\mathrm{tr}(P)=0\}
. We have a nondegenerate symmetric bilinear form given by
\langle P,Q\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(PQ)
over the
k
vector space
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
, when
char(k)
is odd and
char(k)\nmid n
. Note that for
X,Y\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
, the non-degeneracy of the Killing form
\langle X,Y\rangle_{\text{Killing}}=\mathrm{tr}(ad_{X}ad_{Y})
does not guarantee the non-degeneracy of this bilinear form when
char(k)\mid n
.
With respect to this bilinear form, the orthogonal complement of a subspace
\mathfrak{U}\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
will be denoted by
\mathfrak{U}^{\perp}
. Moreover, for an element
D\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
, we use the notation
Z(D)
to denote the centralizer of
D
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
, i.e.,
Z(D)=Z_{\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)}(D)\cap\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
. For any finite dimensional vector space
\mathcal{V}
if there is a non degenerate symmetric bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}
associated with it, then for any subspace
\mathcal{W}
of
\mathcal{V}
we have
dim(\mathcal{V})=dim(\mathcal{W})+dim(\mathcal{W}^{\perp})
, where
\mathcal{W}^{\perp}
is the orthogonal complement of
\mathcal{W}
with respect to the bilinear form
\mathfrak{B}
.
Moreover, this dimension formula and
\mathcal{W}\subset(\mathcal{W}^{\perp})^{\perp}
together implies
(\mathcal{W}^{\perp})^{\perp}=\mathcal{W}
, in this case.
For any
D,g\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
, the notation
Z(D)^{g}=Z(g^{-1}Dg)=g^{-1}Z(D)g
.

The co-adjoint action is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Ad}^{*}\colon

\displaystyle\rm{SL}_{n}(k)\times\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)

\displaystyle(h,\phi)\mapsto\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{h}\phi

where
\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{h}\phi(X)=\phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{h^{-1}}(X))
, for all
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
.
Here
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)
is the dual of
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
as a
k
-vector space. A subgroup
H
of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
has a non-trivial fixed vector in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)
if there exists a non-zero element in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)
which is fixed by every element of
H
via co-adjoint action. That is,
H
has a fixed vector
\phi
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)
means
\mathrm{Ad}_{h}^{*}\phi=\phi\ \forall h\in H
. We begin with recalling the following from
[
2
]
regarding lifting of commutators for special linear groups over local rings.

Proposition 7.1

(Avni, Gelander, Kassabov, Shalev)
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a local ring with residue field
k
of any characteristic. Let
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(K)
be elements such that the group
H=\langle\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}\rangle\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
does not have any fixed vectors in
\mathfrak{sl}_{n}^{*}(k)
. Then, for any lift
\mathbf{g}
of
[\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}]
to
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
there exist
\mathbf{g}_{1}
and
\mathbf{g}_{2}
lifts of
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1}
and
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}
respectively, such that
\mathbf{g}=[\mathbf{g}_{1},\mathbf{g}_{2}]
.

Now we review the work on lift of scalars for
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
. We remark that, in
[
2
, Corollary 3.4]
, the element
\bar{g}_{1}
is taken as
diag(1,\lambda,\lambda^{2},\ldots,\lambda^{n-1})
where
\lambda
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Note that
det(\bar{g}_{1})=\lambda^{n(n-1)/2}
which is
1
when
n
is odd, thus works in that case. Whereas when
n
is even
det(\bar{g}_{1})=\lambda^{n(n-1)/2}=-1
since
\lambda^{n/2}=-1
as
\lambda
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Hence
\bar{g}_{1}
is not an element of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
in that case. We carefully review this situation and provide an alternate proof of this case here.

Proposition 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a local ring with residue field
k
of odd characteristic
p
. Let
A=bI\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
where
n
is even and
b
is a primitive
n
-th root of unity. Then, any lift of
A
in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
is a commutator in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
.

Proof.

Existence of the primitive
n
-th root in the field ensures that
p\nmid n
. By
[
40
, Theorem 1]
we know that
A
is a commutator in
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
. Thus, there exists
y_{1},y_{2},\ldots,y_{n}\in k
such that
\det(Y_{1})=-1
where

Y_{1}=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&-y_{n}&-y_{n-1}&\cdots&-y_{2}\\
y_{2}&y_{1}&-y_{n}&\cdots&-y_{3}\\
y_{3}&y_{2}&y_{1}&\cdots&-y_{4}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
y_{n}&y_{n-1}&y_{n-2}&\cdots&y_{1}\end{pmatrix}

and
bI_{n}=PYP^{-1}Y^{-1}
where
P
is the companion matrix of
f(x)=x^{n}+1
and

Y=\begin{pmatrix}y_{1}&-by_{n}&-b^{2}y_{n-1}&\cdots&-b^{n-1}y_{2}\\
y_{2}&by_{1}&-b^{2}y_{n}&\cdots&-b^{n-1}y_{3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
y_{s}&by_{2}&b^{2}y_{1}&\cdots&-b^{n-1}y_{4}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
y_{n}&by_{n-1}&b^{2}y_{n-2}&\cdots&b^{n-1}y_{1}\end{pmatrix}

where
P,Y\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
; see
[
40
, Proof of Theorem 1]
. Let
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1}=P
and
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}=Y
and consider the subgroup
H=\langle\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}\rangle
of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
. To complete the proof, we use the Proposition
7.1
, i.e., we show that the co-adjoint action of
H
has no fixed point.

If possible let there exists
\phi\in\mathfrak{sl}^{*}_{n}(k)
such that
\phi
is a fixed vector of
\mathfrak{sl}^{*}_{n}(k)
with respect to the group
H=\langle\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}\rangle\subset\mathrm{SL}_{n}(k)
. That is, for
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
, we have
\phi(X)=\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}.\phi(X)=\mathrm{Ad}^{*}_{\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}}\phi(X)=\phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1}}(X))=\phi(\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}X\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1})
. Now, using the non-degeneracy of the bilinear form, defined as
\langle P,Q\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(PQ)
, there exists a unique
E_{\phi}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
(for the given
\phi
) such that
\phi(M)=\langle E_{\phi},M\rangle
. Thus,
\langle E_{\phi},X\rangle=\phi(X)=\phi(\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}X\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1})=\langle E_{\phi},\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}X\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1}\rangle=\langle\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1}E_{\phi}\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i},X\rangle
. Since this happens for all
X
, we get
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}E_{\phi}\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{i}^{-1}=E_{\phi}
for
i=1,2
. Therefore there exists
E_{\phi}\in\mathfrak{sl}_{n}(k)
such that
E_{\phi}\in Z_{\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)}(P)\cap Z_{\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)}(Y)
(noting
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{1}=P
and
\mathbf{\bar{g}}_{2}=Y
). Now
P
is cyclic as its minimal and characteristic polynomial both are
f(x)
, hence
Z_{\mathrm{M}_{n}(k)}(P)\cong k[P]
. Thus, we can take
E_{\phi}=\psi(P)
for some polynomial
\psi(t)\in k[t]
.
Now
YE_{\phi}Y^{-1}=E_{\phi}
implies
E_{\phi}=YE_{\phi}Y^{-1}=\psi(YPY^{-1})=\psi(b^{-1}P)
. Therefore, we obtain
\psi(P)=\psi(b^{-1}P)
. Now, going over an algebraically closed field
\bar{k}
, there exists
Q\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\bar{k})
such that
QPQ^{-1}=diag(\beta,b\beta,\ldots,b^{n-1}\beta)
. Conjugating both side of the equation
\psi(P)=\psi(b^{-1}P)
by
Q
, we obtain
\psi(\beta)=\psi(b\beta)=\ldots=\psi(b^{n-1}\beta)
. This implies
E_{\phi}=\psi(P)=cI
for some scalar
c
. Trace zero condition immediately implies
E_{\phi}=\mathbf{0}
. Thus
\phi=0
, and hence
H
doesn’t have any fixed vectors. Thus, invoking Proposition
7.1
, the result follows.
∎

Remark 7.3
.

When
H=\langle h_{1},h_{2}\rangle
the subgroup generated by

; it turns out that
H
has no nontrivial fixed vector in
\mathfrak{sl}^{*}_{2}(k)
is equivalent to say
Z(h_{1})\cap Z(h_{2})=\{\mathbf{0}\}
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
(see the proof of Proposition
7.2
).

This finally ensures the surjectivity of the commutator map over
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
as follows.

Proposition 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be a local principal ideal ring of length two with perfect residue field

of odd characteristic and

Lift of Cyclic elements as image of Engel maps

In this section, we look at cyclic elements in two parts: first, regular semisimple ones, and then non-regular semisimple ones. First, we prove that any non-trivial unipotent matrix in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
satisfies the assumptions mentioned in Proposition
8.4
.

Proposition 9.1
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
be a non-scalar matrix such that
A=[P,Q]
for some
P
and
Q
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
. Then the subgroup
H=\langle P,Q\rangle
does not have any non-trivial fixed vector under the co-adjoint action on
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{*}(k)
.

Proof.

Since
A
is non-scalar, the elements
P,Q
are both non-scalar, hence cyclic in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
. On contrary suppose
H
has a fixed vector
\phi
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{*}(k)
. Then,
\mathrm{Ad}_{P}^{*}\phi(X)=\phi(X)=\mathrm{Ad}_{Q}^{*}\phi(X)
for all
X\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
. Now, since the bilinear form
\langle,\rangle
is nondegenerate, the induced map
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)\rightarrow\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{*}(k)
defined by
E\mapsto\langle E,\ -\ \rangle
is an isomorphism of the vector spaces (see Section
7
). Under this isomorphism, for
\phi\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{*}(k)
, we choose
V\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
, that satisfies
\phi=\langle V,\ -\ \rangle
. Thus, using this and the definition
\mathrm{Ad}_{P}^{*}\phi(X)=\phi(\mathrm{Ad}_{P^{-1}}(X))=\langle V,\mathrm{Ad}_{P^{-1}}(X)\rangle
and similarly for
Q
, we get
\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{P}(V),X\rangle=\langle V,X\rangle=\langle\mathrm{Ad}_{Q}(V),X\rangle
for all
X
. This implies,
V\in Z(P)\cap Z(Q)
. We need to show that
\phi=0
, which we do by showing that
V=0
.

Now, since
Q
is cyclic and
V\in Z(Q)
, we get
V=f(Q)
for some
f(x)\in k[x]
. Then,
V\in Z(P)
gives
V=PVP^{-1}=f(PQP^{-1})=f(AQ)
. Now consider
f(x)
and the characteristic polynomial
\chi_{Q}(x)
and write
f(x)=g(x)\chi_{Q}(x)+h(x)
for some
g(x),h(x)
where
h(x)=0
or
deg(h(x))<2
. We claim here that
h(x)=0
. If not, then we write
h(x)=\alpha x+\beta
for some
\alpha,\beta\in k
. As
PQP^{-1}
and
Q
are similar in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)
, therefore
\chi_{Q}(x)=\chi_{PQP^{-1}}(x)
. Then,
V=f(Q)=f(PQP^{-1})=f(AQ)
implies
h(Q)=h(AQ)\implies\alpha Q+\beta I=\alpha AQ+\beta I
, that is,
\alpha(I-A)Q=\mathbf{0}
. Therefore,
\alpha(I-A)=\mathbf{0}
. Since
A
is non-scalar,
I-A
is nonzero, i.e., at least one of the entries of
I-A
is nonzero in
k
. This implies
\alpha=0
. Therefore
V=f(Q)=\beta I
. Now
V\in\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
implies
\beta=0
. Therefore, the only possibility is
h(x)=0
, consequently
V=\mathbf{0}
. Hence,
\phi
is the zero vector in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}^{*}(k)
.
∎

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
k
be a perfect field of characteristic
\neq 2
and
A\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
be a non-scalar matrix. Suppose
A=[P,Q]
for some
P,Q
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
and
Z(Q)\cap Z(A)=\{\mathbf{0\}}
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
. Then we have the following:

(1)

If
Q
is not a regular semisimple element then
Z(PQP^{-1})\cap Z(Q)^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
.

(2)

If
Q
is regular semisimple and either
\mathrm{tr}(A)=2
or

2\mathrm{tr}(A)\neq\mathrm{tr}(Q)^{2}
then
Z(PQP^{-1})\cap Z(Q)^{\perp}=\{\mathbf{0}\}
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
.

Proof.

Let
Y\in Z(PQP^{-1})\cap Z(Q)^{\perp}
. Then,
A=PQP^{-1}Q^{-1}
gives
Y\in Z(PQP^{-1})=Z(AQ)
.
Now,
AQ
must be a non-scalar, otherwise
AQ=PQP^{-1}
gives
Q
is a scalar and hence
Z(Q)=\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
contradicts
Z(Q)\cap Z(A)=\{\mathbf{0\}}
. Therefore,
AQ
is cyclic and
Y=aAQ+bI
for some
a,b\in k
. Since,
Y\in Z(Q)^{\perp}
we have
\langle Y,\mathfrak{X}\rangle=tr(Y\mathfrak{X})=0
for all
\mathfrak{X}\in Z(Q)
. This gives,
a\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})+b\mathrm{tr}(\mathfrak{X})=0
, which further implies
a\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})=0
since
\mathfrak{X}\in Z(Q)\subset\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
. Let us consider two separate cases:

Case I:
Q
is not regular semisimple:
We claim that
\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})\neq 0
.
Since
Q
is not regular semisimple, it is of the form
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
or
\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&1\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
up to
\mathrm{GL}_{2}
-conjugates. Let
J\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(k)
such that
JQJ^{-1}=Q^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
. Take
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=J\mathfrak{X}J^{-1}=\delta\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right)
for some
\delta\neq 0
in
k
. Write
A^{\prime}=JAJ^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{array}\right)
.
Now
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\delta\mathrm{tr}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}0&1\\
0&0\end{array}\right))=\delta u_{3}
and
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime})=u_{1}+u_{3}+u_{4}=u_{3}+\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime})=Tr(AQ)=Tr(PQP^{-1})=2
. Therefore,
u_{3}=2-\mathrm{tr}(A)
.

If
u_{3}=0
then
\mathbf{\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime})=2=\mathrm{tr}(A)}
. We have
\det(A)=u_{1}u_{4}=1
and
tr(A)=u_{1}+u_{4}=2
, this gives
u_{1}=u_{4}=1
. Since
A^{\prime}
is non-scalar,
u_{2}\neq 0
and we can write
A^{\prime}=I+u_{2}\delta^{-1}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
. Note that
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}
commutes with
A^{\prime}
. Therefore,
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}\in Z(A^{\prime})\cap Z(Q^{\prime})
, which further implies
\mathfrak{X}\in Z(A)\cap Z(Q)=0
. Thus,
Z(Q)=0
, a contradiction. Thus,
u_{3}\neq 0
when
Q^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&1\\
0&1\end{array}\right)
. A similar argument for the case
Q^{\prime}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}-1&1\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
shows that
u_{3}\neq 0
. Therefore when
Q
is cyclic but not regular semisimple then
\delta u_{3}=\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\mathrm{tr}(JAQ\mathfrak{X}J^{-1})=\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})\neq 0
.

Now,
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})\neq 0
but
a\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})=0
gives
a=0
. Hence,
Y=bI
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(k)
gives
Y=0
. This proves the statement.

Case II:
Q
is regular semisimple:
Over
\bar{k}
we can write
Q^{\prime}=JQJ^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right)
for some
J\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\bar{k})
; where
\lambda\neq\lambda^{-1}
. The elements of
Z(Q^{\prime})
in
\mathfrak{sl}_{2}(\bar{k})
are of the form
\mathfrak{X}^{\prime}=J\mathfrak{X}J^{-1}=\gamma\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right)
for some
\gamma\neq 0
in
\bar{k}
. Let
A^{\prime}=JAJ^{-1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{array}\right)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\bar{k})
. Now
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\gamma\mathrm{tr}(\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}u_{1}&u_{2}\\
u_{3}&u_{4}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}\lambda&0\\
0&\lambda^{-1}\end{array}\right)\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
0&-1\end{array}\right))=\gamma(u_{1}\lambda-u_{4}\lambda^{-1})
and
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime})=u_{1}\lambda+u_{4}\lambda^{-1}
which is equal to
\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime})=\lambda+\lambda^{-1}
because of the commutator relation
[P^{\prime},Q^{\prime}]=A^{\prime}
, where
P^{\prime}=JPJ^{-1}
. Therefore, we obtain

(xxviii)

\lambda(1-u_{1})+\lambda^{-1}(1-u_{4})=0.

Now we deal with the two subcases.

\mathbf{(i)}

When

\mathbf{\mathrm{tr}(A)=2}
: Then
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime})=2
, i.e.,
u_{1}+u_{4}=2
, which further implies
(1-u_{1})=(u_{4}-1)
. This and Equation (
xxviii
) together implies
(1-u_{1})(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})=0
. As
\lambda\neq\lambda^{-1}
therefore
u_{1}=1
which gives
u_{4}=1
. Hence
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\gamma(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})\neq 0
.

\mathbf{(ii)}

When

\mathbf{2\mathrm{tr}(A)\neq}(\mathrm{tr}(Q))^{2}\
: It gives
2\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime})\neq(\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime}))^{2}
. Let
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime})=2\sigma
for some
\sigma\in k
. Then
u_{1}-\sigma=\sigma-u_{4}
. Using this, from the Equation (
xxviii
) we obtain
(u_{1}-\sigma)(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})=(1-\sigma)\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime})
. This implies
u_{1}=(\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime})-2\sigma\lambda^{-1})(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})^{-1}
and
u_{4}=-(\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime})-2\sigma\lambda)(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})^{-1}
. Then,
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})=\gamma((\mathrm{tr}(Q^{\prime}))^{2}-2\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}))(\lambda-\lambda^{-1})^{-1}\neq 0
.

Therefore, in all the cases above
\mathrm{tr}(A^{\prime}Q^{\prime}\mathfrak{X}^{\prime})\neq 0
, hence
\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})\neq 0
. Then
a\mathrm{tr}(AQ\mathfrak{X})=0
implies
a=0
, which further implies
Y=bI
. Now
\mathrm{tr}(Y)=0
implies
b=0
hence
Y=\mathbf{0}
.
∎

Remark 9.3
.

When
Q

9.2
and either
\mathrm{tr}(A)=0

\mathrm{tr}(Q)=0

2\mathrm{tr}(A)\neq(\mathrm{tr}(Q))^{2}

\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1})

In this section, for a regular semisimple element
M\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})

(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2})\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\times\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})

\mathcal{O}_{2}

For a given
m\geq 1
, by Proposition
8.4

9.1

9.2

\bar{e}_{m+1}(h_{1},h_{2})

for all

\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}})

Example 9.4
.

In
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
, let us take
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&6\\
1&0\end{array}\right)
. Since
\mathrm{tr}(A)\neq\pm 2
, the element
A
is regular semisimple in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
. There exist elements, for example,
h_{1}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}6&2\\
4&5\end{array}\right)
and
h_{2}=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&3\\
1&2\end{array}\right)
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{7})
such that
\bar{e}_{3}(h_{1},h_{2})=A
. It is easy to observe
\bar{e}_{2}(h_{1},h_{2})=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}5&2\\
0&3\end{array}\right)
. Therefore,
\mathrm{tr}(\bar{e}_{2}(h_{1},h_{2}))=1
and
\mathrm{tr}(h_{2})=4
. This implies
2\mathrm{tr}(\bar{e}_{2}(h_{1},h_{2}))=tr(h_{2})^{2}
.

In this section, we show that for this kind of elements
A
, every lift of
A
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
must belong to
\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}})
provided
A\in\mathrm{Im}(\bar{e}_{m+1})
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(k)
.

Lemma 9.5
.

Let
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
be such that
\theta_{\ell}(A)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell})
for some
\ell\geq 1
. Then, there exists
Q_{1}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
and
Q_{2}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
such that
A=Q_{1}+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}Q_{2}
where
\pi_{\ell+1}=\pi+(\pi^{\ell+1})
is the generator of the maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}_{\ell+1}
of
\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}
.

Proof.

Since
\theta_{\ell}(A)\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell})
, we get
det(A)=1+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}v
for some
v\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}
because
\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}
is the generator of the
ker(\theta_{\ell})
. We want to find
B
such that
C=A+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}B
with
det(C)=1
. If such
B
exists then
det(A)+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}\mathrm{tr}(adj(A)B)=1
. This is because
det(C)=1
and
\det(C)=\det(A)\det(I+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}A^{-1}B)
. Now note that
A^{-1}=(\det A)^{-1}adj(A)
and
(\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell})^{2}=0
for
\ell\geq 1
, therefore the result follows. This implies
\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}(v+\mathrm{tr}(adj(A)B))=0
. Thus we can choose
B=-2^{-1}v(adj(A))^{-1}
as
adj(A)
is invertible. Consider
Q_{1}=C=A-2^{-1}\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}v(adj(A))^{-1}
and
Q_{2}=-B=2^{-1}v(adj(A))^{-1}
.
∎

The Lemma
9.5
can be generalised for
A\in\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
where
n
is coprime to the characteristic exponent of
k
.

Lemma 9.6
.

For
\ell\geq 1
, let
\widehat{A}_{0}
and
\widehat{B}_{0}
be two matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
, such that
\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{A}_{0}),\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{B}_{0})\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell})
and
\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}}(\widehat{A}_{0},\widehat{B}_{0})_{\mathrm{GL}})=\lambda
, for some
\lambda\in\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}
. Then, there exist two matrices
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}
in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
such that
\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}))=\lambda
, where
\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1})=\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{A}_{0})
and
\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2})=\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{B}_{0})
.

Proof.

From the previous Lemma
9.5
, we can write
\widehat{A}_{0}=\mathbf{g}_{0}+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}A_{1}
and
\widehat{B}_{0}=\mathbf{h}_{0}+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}B_{1}
, for some
\mathbf{g}_{0},\mathbf{h}_{0}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
and
A_{1},B_{1}\in\mathrm{M}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
. Let
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{g}_{0}^{-1}A_{1})=\alpha_{0}
and
\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{h}_{0}^{-1}B_{1})=\beta_{0}
. Consider
\tilde{A}_{1}=A_{1}-2^{-1}\alpha_{0}\mathbf{g}_{0}
and
\tilde{B}_{1}=B_{1}-2^{-1}\beta_{0}\mathbf{h}_{0}
. Now consider
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1}=\mathbf{g}_{0}+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}\tilde{A}_{1}
and
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}=\mathbf{h}_{0}+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}\tilde{B}_{1}
. Then,

det(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1})=1+\pi_{{\ell}+1}^{\ell}\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{g}_{0}^{-1}\tilde{A}_{1})=1+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}(\alpha_{0}-\alpha_{0})=1.

Similarly,
det(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2})=1
. Therefore,
\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}
are the members of
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1})
. Let
P_{0}=2^{-1}\alpha_{0}\mathbf{g}_{0}
and
Q_{0}=2^{-1}\beta_{0}\mathbf{h}_{0}
, then
\tilde{A}_{1}=A_{1}-P_{0}
and
\tilde{B}_{1}=B_{1}-Q_{0}
. Now, invoking Lemma
4.5
by taking
A_{0}=\mathbf{g}_{0}
and
B_{0}=\mathbf{h}_{0}
, we get

L_{m+1}(\tilde{A}_{1},\tilde{B}_{1})=L_{m+1}(A_{1},B_{1})-L_{m+1}(P_{0},Q_{0})=L_{m+1}(A_{1},B_{1})

as
L(P_{0},Q_{0})=0
by Corollary
4.6
. Now, using Lemma
4.5
we get,

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1},\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}}(\mathbf{g}_{0},\mathbf{h}_{0}))+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}L_{m+1}(\tilde{A}_{1},\tilde{B}_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}}(\mathbf{g}_{0},\mathbf{h}_{0}))+\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}L_{m+1}(A_{1},B_{1})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}(\widehat{A}_{0},\widehat{B}_{0})_{\mathrm{GL}})=\lambda.

Since the kernel of the natural surjection
\mathcal{O}_{\ell+1}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{\ell}
is generated by
\pi_{\ell+1}^{\ell}
, therefore
\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{1})=\theta_{\ell}(\mathbf{g}_{0})=\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{A}_{0})
. A similar argument shows that
\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{\mathbf{g}}_{2})=\theta_{\ell}(\mathbf{h}_{0})=\theta_{\ell}(\widehat{B}_{0})
.
∎

Lemma 9.7
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a local principal ideal ring, complete with respect to its unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(\pi)
, with residue field
k
of characteristic
\neq 2
. Let
M
and
P
be two cyclic matrices in
\mathrm{GL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
with the same characteristic polynomial. Then,
M\sim_{\mathcal{O}}P
.

Proof.

First, we show that in this case the null ideals of both matrices
M
and
P
are the same, that is,
\langle\chi_{M}(t)\rangle
(note
\chi_{M}(t)=\chi_{P}(t)
). First, we show that the minimal polynomials are the same too.

Let
J(t)\in N_{M}
, the null ideal of
M
. Then by
[
21
, Theorem 2.14]
there exist
U(t)
and
R(t)
in
\mathcal{O}[t]
such that
J(t)=U(t)\chi_{M}(t)+R(t)
where
R(t)=0
or
\deg(R(t))<\deg(\chi_{M}(t))
. Under the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}[t]\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\frac{\mathcal{O}}{\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{O}}[t]
induced by the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{O}
, say, the image of
R(t)
is
(R_{1}(t),R_{2}(t),\ldots)
and the image of
\chi_{M}(t)
is
(\chi_{\bar{M}}(t),\chi_{M_{2}}(t),\ldots)
. Now for each
i

R_{i}(t)\in N_{M_{i}}
. Since
\chi_{M}(t)
is monic polynomial,
\deg(\chi_{M}(t))=\deg(\chi_{M_{i}}(t))
for all

. By

32

, we obtain

must be zero polynomial for each

must be the zero polynomial in
\mathcal{O}[t]
. This also ensures that the minimal polynomial of both matrices is their characteristic polynomial.

For a matrix

, let us denote by

the

module structure on
\mathcal{O}^{2}
via the action of

by

. For cyclic

\mathrm{M}^{Q}\cong\frac{\mathcal{O}[t]}{\langle\chi_{Q}(t)\rangle}
as
\mathcal{O}[t]
module. As both
M
and

are cyclic by
[

, the factorization of

and

. Therefore
\mathrm{M}^{M}
and

are isomorphic as

∎

At this stage, let us recall the expression of

from Section

, it is obvious that

. Therefore, the existence of a solution to the equation

in
\mathcal{A}^{8}
will ensure the existence of a solution of

That is, if

Keeping this fact in mind, now we write down the main result of this section for regular semisimple elements.

Proposition 9.8

\mathcal{O}

. Therefore, by Lemma

,
(a,b,c,d,e,u,v,w)

in

\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{r+1}}(h_{1}^{(r+1)},h_{2}^{(r+1)}))=\mathrm{tr}(A_{r+1})
for
r=1,2,\ldots,\ell-1
where
h_{1}^{(1)}=h_{1},h_{2}^{(1)}=h_{2},A_{1}=\mathbf{\bar{g}}
.

Using the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{O}
, the isomorphic image of
\mathrm{tr}(A)
is
(\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\bar{g}}),\mathrm{tr}(A_{2}),\mathrm{tr}(A_{3}),\ldots)
. Now, using the canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{T^{*}}\colon\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathrm{M}_{n}(\mathcal{O}_{j})
, let
(h_{1},h_{1}^{(2)},h_{1}^{(3)},\ldots)
and
(h_{2},h_{2}^{(2)},h_{2}^{(3)},\ldots)
be the images of
{h}^{\circ}_{1}
and
h^{\circ}_{2}
respectively. Note that
\det(h^{\circ}_{1})=1=\det(h^{\circ}_{2})
. Therefore
h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})
. Via the isomorphism
\mathcal{O}\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{O}
we get the isomorphic image of
\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2}))
is exactly equal to the isomorphic image of
\mathrm{tr}(A)
which is nothing but
(\mathrm{tr}(\mathbf{\bar{g}}),\mathrm{tr}(A_{2}),\mathrm{tr}(A_{3}),\ldots)
. Therefore
\mathrm{tr}(A)=\mathrm{tr}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2}))
. Therefore,
A
and
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2})
have the same characteristic polynomial over
\mathcal{O}
. Note that
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2})
and
A
both are cyclic by definition.
Invoking Lemma
9.7
we get

A\sim_{\mathcal{O}}e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}}(h^{\circ}_{1},h^{\circ}_{2})
. This proves the required result.
∎

10.1.
Projective Special Linear group over Local Rings

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a local (commutative) ring with unity and
\mathcal{M}
be an
\mathcal{A}
module. The general linear group
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
is the group of invertible
\mathcal{A}
-linear transformations on
\mathcal{M}
. For any
a\in\mathcal{A}^{\times}
consider the linear transformation
aI_{\mathcal{M}}\in\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
defined by
x\mapsto xr
for all
x\in\mathcal{M}
. Let
\mathrm{RL}(\mathcal{M})=\{aI_{\mathcal{M}}\mid a\in\mathcal{A}^{\times}\}
. Note that
\mathrm{RL}(\mathcal{M})
forms a normal subgroup of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
. The natural surjection
\mathrm{P}\colon\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})/\mathrm{RL}(\mathcal{M})
is called the projection map of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
and its image is called the
projective general linear group of
\mathcal{M}
and is denoted by
\mathrm{PGL}(\mathcal{M})
. For any subgroup
G
of
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
,
PG\cong G/(G\cap\mathrm{RL}(\mathcal{M}))
. Now assume that
\mathcal{M}
is a free
\mathcal{A}
-module of finite rank
n
. Then there is an isomorphism (through the choice of a basis) between
\mathrm{GL}(\mathcal{M})
and
\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
which takes
\mathrm{RL}(\mathcal{M})
onto
\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})=\{aI_{n}\mid a\in\mathcal{A}^{\times}\}
; see
[
20
, section 1.2]
. The matrix analogue of the projection map is
\mathrm{P}\colon\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})\rightarrow\mathrm{GL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})/\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
. The image of
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})
under this map is called the
projective special linear group over
\mathcal{A}
and is denoted by
\mathrm{PSL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})/(\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{A})\cap\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{A}))
.

Lemma 10.3
.

Let
\mathcal{O}
be a local principal ideal ring, complete with respect to its unique maximal ideal
\mathfrak{m}=(\pi)
and has residue field
k
with characteristic
\neq 2
. Then,
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O})/\{\pm I_{2}\}
.

Proof.

It is enough to show that
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\cap\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})=\{\pm I_{2}\}
in this case. Let
\lambda I_{2}\in\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\cap\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})
. Therefore,
\bar{\lambda}
is a solution of
x^{2}-1=0
in
k
. Hence
\bar{\lambda}=\pm 1
. Denote
\mathcal{O}/\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{O}=\mathcal{O}_{j}
for
j\geq 1
. It is easy to see (check the calculations in Family (1) case in Section
2.3
) that the solutions of
x^{2}-1=0
in
\mathcal{O}_{2}
are
\pm 1
. Similarly, using the surjection
\theta_{2}\colon\mathcal{O}_{3}\rightarrow\mathcal{O}_{2}
, if
1+m
is a lift of
1
in
\mathcal{O}_{3}
that satisfies
x^{2}=1
in
\mathcal{O}_{3}
where
m\in ker(\theta_{2})
, then
m=0
as
(ker(\theta_{2}))^{2}=0
. The other solution of
x^{2}-1=0
in
\mathcal{O}_{3}
, which is a lift of
-1
, is again
-1
. Since
(ker(\theta_{j}))^{2}=0
for any
j\geq 1
, the only solutions of
x^{2}=1
over
\mathcal{O}_{j+1}
, which are the lifts of
\pm 1
, are
\pm 1
only. Hence, by Hensel’s lemma (
[
13
, theorem 7.18]
) and the canonical isomorphism
\mathcal{A}\rightarrow\varprojlim\limits_{j\geq 1}\mathcal{A}/\mathfrak{m}^{j}\mathcal{A}
, we obtain that the only solutions of
x^{2}=1
in
\mathcal{O}
, that are the lifts of
1
and
-1
are
1
and
-1
, respectively. Hence,
\lambda=\pm 1
and
\mathrm{SL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})\cap\mathrm{RL}_{n}(\mathcal{O})=\{\pm I_{2}\}
.
∎

Theorem 10.4
.

Let
\mathcal{O}_{2}
be a local principal ideal ring of length
2
, with its residue field
k\cong\mathbb{F}_{q}
of characteristic
\neq 2
. Let
e_{m+1}(x,y)
be the
(m+1)
-th Engel word in the free group
\mathcal{F}_{2}=\langle x,y\rangle
and
m\geq 1
. Then, there exists a constant
q_{0}(m)
such that for all
q\geq q_{0}(m)
, the
(m+1)
-th Engel word map
e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}_{2}}
is surjective on
\mathrm{P}\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. One can take
q_{0}(m)=2\cdot 3^{2^{m+2}}
.

Proof.

For any positive integer
m\geq 1
, let
(e_{m+1})_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})}
denotes the
(m+1)
-th Engel word on
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
. By Lemma
10.3
it follows that
\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})\cong\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})/\{\pm I_{2}\}
. Under the assumption
q\geq q_{0}(m)
, Proposition
9.8
, Lemma
9.9
, and Lemma
9.10
together ensure that all the lifts of non-scalars must be in
\mathrm{Im}((e_{m+1})_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})})
for any
m\geq 1
. This only leaves the case of lift of
I
. The lifts of
I
are in
\mathrm{Im}((e_{m+1})_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})})
by Proposition
10.2
. Consequently,
(e_{m+1})_{\mathrm{PSL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})}
is surjective.
∎

We conclude this section with a comment regarding
higher Engel words and the assumption
|k|\geq 5
in Section
5
. For higher Engel words, it may not always be possible that any lift of
-I
lies in
\mathrm{Im}(e_{m+1}^{\mathcal{O}})
for every
m\geq 1
. This follows from
[
3
, Proposition 4.7]
.

Example 10.5

(When the residue field is
\mathbb{F}_{3}
)
.

Consider
A=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}1&0\\
1&1\end{array}\right)
and
B=\left(\begin{array}[]{cc}2&0\\
1&2\end{array}\right)
be the elements in

with
o(A)=3
and

, respectively. The commutator subgroup
[\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3}),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})]

. Therefore,
A
and
B
can never be written as a single commutator or as a finite product of commutators in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{F}_{3})
. Hence,
A
and
B
can never be written as a product of a finite number of commutators in
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathcal{O}_{2})
when the residue field of
\mathcal{O}_{2}
is
\mathbb{F}_{3}
.
