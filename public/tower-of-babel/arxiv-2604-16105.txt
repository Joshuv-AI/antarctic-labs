Decoding Algorithms for Tensor Codes
Eimear Byrne, Alain Couvreur, Lucien François

Abstract




Abstract

Tensor codes are a generalisation of matrix codes. Such codes are defined as subspaces of order-
r
tensors for which the ambient space is endowed with the tensor-rank as a metric. A class of these codes was introduced by Roth, who also outlined a decoding algorithm for low tensor-rank errors that can be generalised to an algorithm with exponential complexity in the decoding radius. They may be viewed as a generalisation of the well-known Delsarte-Gabidulin-Roth maximum rank distance codes. We study a generalised class of these codes. We investigate their properties and outline decoding techniques for different metrics that leverage their tensor structure. We first consider a fibre-wise decoding approach, as each fibre of a codeword corresponds to a Gabidulin codeword. We then give a generalisation of Loidreau-Overbeck’s decoding method that corrects errors with properties constrained by the dimensions of the slice spaces and fibre spaces. The metrics we consider are bounded from above by the tensor-rank metric, and therefore these algorithms also decode tensor-rank weight errors.

1
Introduction

Error-correcting codes are critical for ensuring data reliability in modern communication systems. Rank-metric codes such as the well-known Delsarte-Gabidulin-Roth codes have attracted significant attention due to their applicability in network coding. Such codes have seen generalisations, such as the twisted Gabidulin codes
[
17
,
23
]
and the tensor codes. Tensor codes as a type of code have been introduced in
[
21
]
with a view towards criss-cross error correction, and a particular class has been studied in
[
22
]
. Roth’s tensor codes are a family of subspaces of the vector space of tensors endowed with the tensor-rank as a metric and were constructed to have a known lower bound on the minimum tensor-rank distance. For this family of codes, Roth gave decoding algorithms for tensor-rank one errors and for tensor-rank two errors reaching the decoding radius of the codes, with polynomial complexity for the first and exponential complexity in the decoding radius for the second. He also suggested generalisations to higher radius.

In this paper, we derive decoding algorithms for a generalisation of the tensor codes that were introduced in
[
22
]
. In our treatment we consider a number of new metrics, each of which provides a lower bound on the tensor rank as a weight function. We give bounded-distance decoders for these metrics which can hence be considered as bounded-distance decoders for the tensor rank as a metric with an additional range of decodable errors. The algorithms presented here all have polynomial complexity in the size of the tensors, for each decoding radius chosen. Although the presentation focuses on order-3 tensors, the generalisation of the algorithms to higher order tensors is straightforward and preserves this property. We also give a detailed comparison of the different algorithms. We base the first type of algorithm on the direct sum of codes in which the code is contained, and the second type on the Welch-Berlekamp-like algorithm introduced by Loidreau and Overbeck (see
[
28
]
,
[
1
, Section 6.8]
and
[
16
]
).

The outline of this paper is as follows.
In Section
2
, we introduce basic properties of codes and tensors. In Section
3
, we introduce relevant properties of linearised and bilinearised polynomials and describe the class of tensor codes introduced by Roth in
[
22
]
. Furthermore, we introduce a class of evaluation codes that extend Roth’s codes, describe its properties and its link with the original codes. We introduce a number of metrics relevant to the decoding and describe the decoding capabilities of the codes in respect of these distance functions. In Section
4
, we present four different decoding algorithms. The first two are based on Gabidulin decoders and exploit the fact that the family of tensor codes we study are subcodes of direct sums of Gabidulin codes. The last two algorithms use an approach similar to Loidreau-Overbeck using a left-Euclidean like factoring algorithm on solutions of a linear equation system. After studying the error correction capability of these algorithms, we consider the extended range of decodable errors provided by the application of existing probabilistic methods, namely supercode decoding methods and interleaving. In Section
5
, we compare our different algorithms in terms of their decoding capability and their asymptotic complexity. Finally, in Section
6
, we give details on the generalisation of the aforementioned codes and algorithms to higher order tensor-codes.

A complementary GitHub repository (
https://github.com/lucienfrancois/RothTensorCodes
) provides MAGMA implementations of the algorithms discussed in this paper. Computations of the examples mentioned below can be produced using this MAGMA code.

Evaluation codes of bilinearised polynomials

3.1
Linearised and bilinearised polynomials

Linearised polynomials
or
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are univariate polynomials in the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[Z]
spanned by the monomials of the form
Z^{q^{r}}
where
r
is a non-negative integer. These polynomials are endowed with the addition and the composition map, which gives them a structure of non-commutative ring denoted
\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
. It is known that such a polynomial induces an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–linear map since its monomials have exponents equal to powers of
q
. Moreover, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension of the kernel of a non-zero linearised
q
-polynomial is bounded from above by its
q
-degree, see
[
14
, Chapter 4]
. We will now generalise the notion and focus on additive vector subspaces of bivariate polynomials in the commutative algebra
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
.

Definition 3.1
.

The
bilinearised
\boldsymbol{q}
-polynomials
over

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
are the elements of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace

\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]:=\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\left\{\left.X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}\right|i\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{2}\right\}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y].

An element
F(X,Y)\in\mathcal{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
has an expression as

F(X,Y)=\sum_{i\in S}F_{i}X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}
for some finite set
S\subset{{\mathbb{N}_{0}}^{2}}
.
We then define the support of
F
to be
\Supp(F):=\{i\in S:F_{{i}}\neq 0\}
.
If
F(X,Y)
has non-empty support
S
, we define the
partial
q
-degree
of
F(X,Y)
along
X
(respectively
Y
), expressed as
q\text{-}\!\deg_{X}F(X,Y)
, (respectively
q\text{-}\!\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)
) to be
\max\{i_{1}:i\in S\}
(respectively
\max\{i_{2}:i\in S\}
).

Example 3.2
.

If

By the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linearity of the Frobenius map on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
defined by

, we have the following observation.

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
F(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
. Then the evaluation map given by
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},(x,y)\mapsto F(x,y)
is bilinear over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

The subspace of bilinearised
q
-polynomials is not a subalgebra of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
with respect to usual polynomial multiplication.
However, the
q^{th}

q
-polynomial is also a bilinearised
q
-polynomial, therefore, the space is stable upon left-composition by a (single variable) linearised
q
-polynomial. In particular, the space
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
has the structure of an
(\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z],+,\circ)
-module, as stated below.

Proposition 3.4
.

The abelian additive group
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
is a left-module over the non-commutative ring
(\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z],+,\circ)
with the external binary operation:

\circ:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]\times\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]&\longrightarrow&\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]\\
(V(Z),F(X,Y))&\longmapsto&V(F(X,Y)).\end{array}\right.

Define

U_{X,Y}:=\{f(X^{q^{n}}-X,Y)+g(X,Y^{q^{n}}-Y)\ |\ f(X,Y),g(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]\}.

(2)

This is a vector subspace of
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
, which is contained in the ideal
(X^{q^{n}}-X,Y^{q^{n}}-Y)
of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
. Indeed, for any
f(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
, since
X
divides
f(X,Y)
, then
X^{q^{n}}-X
divides
f(X^{q^{n}}-X,Y)
. For any polynomial, denote by
\overline{F(X,Y)}=F(X,Y)+U_{X,Y}
the coset containing
F(X,Y)
in the quotient vector space
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
.

Lemma 3.5
.

The set
\{\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}}:i\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\}
is a basis of
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
.

Proof.

For each
t,s\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
we have
X^{q^{t+n}}Y^{q^{s}}=X^{q^{t}}Y^{q^{s}}+(X^{q^{n}}-X)^{q^{t}}Y^{q^{s}}
, thus
\overline{X^{q^{t+n}}Y^{q^{s}}}=\overline{X^{q^{t}}Y^{q^{s}}}
, and likewise
\overline{X^{q^{t}}Y^{q^{s+n}}}=\overline{X^{q^{t}}Y^{q^{s}}}
. By induction, one can check that
\overline{X^{q^{a}}Y^{q^{b}}}=\overline{X^{q^{a\!\!\mod n}}Y^{q^{b\!\!\mod n}}}
for each
a,b\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Therefore, the set
\{\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}}:i\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\}
spans the vector space
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
.
It is straightforward to see that any non-zero polynomial in

U_{X,Y}
has partial
q
-degree along
X
or
Y
at least
n
and hence
for any non-trivial
S\subset\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
we have
\sum_{i\in S}f_{i}\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}}\notin U_{X,Y}
unless
f_{i}=0
for each
i
. Therefore, the set
\{\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}}}Y^{q^{i_{2}}}}:i\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\}
is linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
.

∎

This establishes that any bilinearised
q
-polynomial has a unique expression modulo
U_{X,Y}
whose monomials terms each have partial
q
-degrees less than
n
. Consequently, we can define the
support
of any element
\overline{f(X,Y)}
in the quotient space
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
, denoted by
\Supp(\overline{f(X,Y)})
, as the support of this unique representative. Hence, this support is necessarily a subset of
\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
.

Since
U_{X,Y}
is contained in the ideal of the ring
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}[X,Y]
generated by
\{X^{q^{n}}-X,Y^{q^{n}}-Y\}
, the evaluation of a bilinearised
q
-polynomial at any element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{2}
depends only on its corresponding coset in the quotient space.

Proposition 3.6
.

For each
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}
, denote by
E_{i}=(\mathds{1}_{\{\tilde{i}=i\}})_{\tilde{i}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}
the elementary matrix with a single non-zero entry at
i=(i_{1},i_{2})
. Recall that
\alpha
denotes an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
–basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
an
M(\alpha)
is the Moore matrix introduced in Definition
2.4
. Then the linear maps
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
uniquely defined by

\begin{array}[]{crcll}I_{1}:&\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n},&\forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}:I_{1}\left(\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}-1}}Y^{q^{i_{2}-1}}}\right)=E_{i}\\
I_{2}:&\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n},&\forall K\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}:I_{2}(K)=M(\alpha)^{\top}KM(\alpha)\end{array}

are isomorphisms of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear spaces. In particular, the evaluation map

\ev:\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n},\overline{f(X,Y)}\mapsto(f(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}}))_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}

is an isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear spaces with
\ev=I_{2}\circ I_{1}
.

Proof.

The maps
I_{1}
and
I_{2}
are
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear by construction. The map
I_{1}
is bijective as the image of a basis is also a basis by Lemma
3.5
, and the map
I_{2}
is too as the Moore matrices are invertible.
Then for each
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}
we have

(I_{2}\circ I_{1})\left(\overline{X^{q^{i_{1}-1}}Y^{q^{i_{2}-1}}}\right)=M(\alpha)^{\top}E_{i}M(\alpha)=\left(\alpha_{j_{1}}^{q^{i_{1}-1}}\alpha_{j_{2}}^{q^{i_{2}-1}}\right)_{j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}=\ev\left(X^{q^{i_{1}-1}}Y^{q^{i_{2}-1}}\right),

which concludes the proof.

∎

Let
n=3

for

satisfying

3.2
Tensor codes

In
[
22
]
, Roth introduced and studied a family of codes that can be expressed as the image of the evaluation map
\ev
defined in Proposition
3.6
of a well-chosen subspace of
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
. We recall the definition of these codes and some of their properties and then continue with a generalisation of this family. Note that in
[
22
]
, the codes were introduced with respect to three (possibly different) bases of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Since the natural action of
\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})^{3}
on
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
give rise to a tensor-rank preserving automorphism of
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
that maps a such a code to a code with the same parameters defined with the same identical bases, we will only consider here the case of a single defining basis
\alpha
. For each
\mu\in\llbracket 0,2n-1\rrbracket
, the code

\mathcal{C}(n,\mu,3;q)
is defined to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n\times n}
given by

\mathcal{C}(n,\mu,3;q):=\left\{\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}\ \left|\ \forall r\in\mathcal{S}(n,\mu,3;q):\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}}\Gamma[i](\alpha^{\bot}_{i_{1}})^{q^{r_{1}}}(\alpha^{\bot}_{i_{2}})^{q^{r_{2}}}\alpha_{i_{3}}=0\right.\right\},

where the set
\mathcal{S}(n,\mu,3;q)
is the subset of
\{r\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\ |\ r_{1}+r_{2}\leq\mu-2\}
for which there exists an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-
[\mu-1,r_{1}+1,r_{2}+1]
Hamming-metric code;
see
[
22
, Section 3.1]
. We denote by

\mathcal{S}^{(Roth)}_{\mu}=\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\setminus\mathcal{S}(n,\mu,3;q)
the complement of the set
\mathcal{S}(n,\mu,3;q)
in
\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
. Then we have

\mathfrak{s}_{\alpha}\left(\mathcal{C}(n,\mu,3;q)\right)=\ev\left(\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\left\{\overline{X^{q^{s_{1}}}Y^{q^{s_{2}}}}\mid(i_{1},i_{2})\in\mathcal{S}^{(Roth)}_{\mu}\right\}\right).

Roth showed that
\mathcal{C}(n,\mu,3;q)
has redundancy
n|\mathcal{S}(n,\mu,3;q)|\leq\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mu\\
2\end{smallmatrix}\right)n
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space, and hence
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension
n|\mathcal{S}_{\mu}^{(Roth)}|\geq n^{3}-n\left(\begin{smallmatrix}\mu\\
2\end{smallmatrix}\right)
. Moreover, he proved that that any element in
\mathcal{C}(n,\mu,3;q)
has tensor-rank at least
\mu
and
[
22
, Theorem 5]
. Along with this lower bound on the tensor-rank, in
[
22
]
two decoding algorithms for the codes
\mathcal{C}(n,3,3;q)
and
\mathcal{C}(n,5,3;q)
are given that correct errors of tensor-rank at most one and two, respectively. There is a generalisation of the second algorithm to correct errors of higher tensor-rank for suitable codes. We will discuss the complexity of these algorithms in Section
5
. We now study the properties of similar codes defined with this second characterisation as evaluation codes of
q
-polynomials with support in an arbitrary subset of
\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
.

We now consider the subspace of polynomials with a specified support in the quotient vector space, as well as its image under the isomorphism described in Proposition
3.6
, and study their properties. Throughout this section, let
\mathcal{S}
be a subset of
\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
.

Definition 3.8
.

We define the
(polynomial) Roth-tensor code
associated to
\mathcal{S}
to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector subspace of
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}

\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}):=\left\{\left.\overline{f(X,Y)}\ \right|\ \overline{f(X,Y)}\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y},\Supp(\overline{f(X,Y)})\subseteq\mathcal{S}\right\}.

We define the
(evaluation) Roth-tensor code
associated to
\mathcal{S}
and
\alpha
to be the
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear code in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
:

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}):=\left\{\left.\big(f(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}})\big)_{{i}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}\right|\begin{array}[]{l}f\in\mathcal{M}_{n,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y],\\
\Supp(f)\subseteq\mathcal{S}\end{array}\right\}.

It follows immediately from the definitions of
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
and Proposition
3.6
that
\ev(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}))=\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
.

Furthermore, we observe that every column and row of any codeword of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
is a codeword of a Gabidulin code, as stated in the following proposition.

Proposition 3.9
.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector spaces
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
are isomorphic and have
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-dimension
|\mathcal{S}|
. Moreover, the maps

\phi_{2}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})&\longrightarrow&\Big(\mathscr{G}_{\max(\pi_{2}(\mathcal{S}))+1}(\alpha)\Big)^{n}\\[5.69046pt]
C&\longmapsto&\Big(C[i_{1},:]\Big)_{i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\end{array}\right.\quad\text{and}\quad\phi_{1}:\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})&\longrightarrow&\Big(\mathscr{G}_{\max(\pi_{1}(\mathcal{S}))+1}(\alpha)\Big)^{n}\\[5.69046pt]
C&\longmapsto&\Big(C[:,i_{2}]\Big)_{i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\end{array}\right.

are
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector space monomorphisms, such that for each
j\in\{1,2\}
,
\phi_{j}
is an isomorphism if and only if
\mathcal{S}=\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\max(\pi_{j}(\mathcal{S}))\rrbracket
.

Proof.

The monomials
\{X^{q^{s_{1}}}Y^{q^{s_{2}}}:s\in\mathcal{S}\}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and generate
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
as an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector space, thus
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})=|\mathcal{S}|
. Since
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
are isomorphic, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})=|\mathcal{S}|
. We only need to prove the statement for
\phi_{2}
. For each
q
-polynomial
f(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
such that
\Supp(f(X,Y))\subseteq\mathcal{S}
and each
i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
the polynomial
f(\alpha_{i_{1}},Y)
is a linearised
q
-polynomial with
q
-degree (if non-zero) bounded from above by
\max(\pi_{2}(\mathcal{S}))
. Let
C:=\big(f(\alpha_{i_{1}},\alpha_{i_{2}})\big)_{{i}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}
be its associated codeword in
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
. Then for each
i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
,
C[i_{1},:]
is the evaluation at
\alpha
of
f(\alpha_{i_{1}},X)
and thus is an element of
\mathscr{G}_{\max(\pi_{2}(\mathcal{S}))+1}(\alpha)
. Hence, the map
\phi_{2}
is well-defined. Moreover, the map
\phi_{2}
is
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-linear and injective since it is a restriction of the isomorphism
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\to(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n})^{n}
that splits any matrix into its rows. Hence, it is an isomorphism if and only if
|\mathcal{S}|=n(\max(\pi_{2}(\mathcal{S}))+1)
, in other words, if and only
\mathcal{S}=\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\max(\pi_{2}(\mathcal{S}))\rrbracket
.
∎

Corollary 3.10
.

Let
\mu_{1},\mu_{2}\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
be integers. We have

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\llbracket 0,\mu_{1}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\mu_{2}\rrbracket)=\left\{C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\ \left|\ \forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}:C[i_{1},:]\in\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{2}+1}(\alpha)\text{ and }C[:,i_{2}]\in\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{1}+1}(\alpha)\right.\right\},

and we have the isomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-vector spaces

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\llbracket 0,\mu_{1}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\mu_{2}\rrbracket)\simeq\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{2}+1}(\alpha)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{1}+1}(\alpha).

Proof.

Let
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\left\{C\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\ \left|\ \forall i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:C[i_{1},:]\in\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{2}+1}(\alpha)\text{ and }C[:,i_{2}]\in\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{1}+1}(\alpha)\right.\right\}
. Set
\mathcal{S}=\llbracket 0,\mu_{1}\rrbracket\times\llbracket 0,\mu_{2}\rrbracket
. By definition we have
\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{2}+1}(\alpha)\otimes_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{1}+1}(\alpha)
. By Proposition
3.9
,
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\tilde{\mathcal{C}}
, and since

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\tilde{\mathcal{C}}=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{2}+1}(\alpha)\right)\cdot\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\left(\mathscr{G}_{\mu_{1}+1}(\alpha)\right)=(\mu_{2}+1)(\mu+1+1)=|\mathcal{S}|=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}),

we have the wanted result.
∎

We recall the definition of the dual of a matrix code:

Definition 3.11
.

The
dual
code of a matrix code

\mathcal{C}\subseteq\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
, is defined to be

\mathcal{C}^{\perp}:=\{X\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \forall Y\in\mathcal{C},\ \tr(XY^{\top})=0\}.

As in
[
22
, Section 3.2]
, we have an expression of the dual of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
in terms of parity check equations. That is,

\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=\left\{\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}\ \penalty\ \left|\penalty\ \ \forall r\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash\mathcal{S}\penalty\ |\penalty\ \sum_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}}\Gamma[i](\alpha^{\bot}_{i_{1}})^{q^{r_{1}}}(\alpha^{\bot}_{i_{2}})^{q^{r_{2}}}\alpha_{i_{3}}=0\right.\right\}.

This expression is the consequence of the two following facts.

1.

For each
r\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
, consider the matrix
M_{r}:=\alpha^{q^{r_{1}}}\otimes\alpha^{q^{r_{2}}}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
, i.e. the matrix whose

(i_{1},i_{2})
-component is
M_{r,(i_{1},i_{2})}=\alpha_{i_{1}}^{q^{r_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{q^{r_{2}}}
for each
i_{1},i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. Then the set
\{M_{r}:r\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\}
is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
; see
[
22
, Lemma 4]
.

2.

Let
\alpha^{\bot}
be the dual basis of
\alpha
, so that
\sum_{\ell=0}^{n-1}(\alpha_{i}\alpha^{\bot}_{j})^{q^{\ell}}=\mathds{1}_{i=j}
for each
i,j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. This is equivalent to stating that
M(\alpha)
is invertible and has
M(\alpha^{\bot})^{\top}
as its inverse. Hence, one also has
\sum_{i=1}^{n}\alpha_{i}^{q^{u}}(\alpha^{\bot}_{i})^{q^{v}}=\mathds{1}_{u=v}
for each
u,v\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
.

These two properties let us state that the dual code of an evaluated Roth-tensor code is also an evaluated Roth-tensor code evaluated at the dual basis; see
[
20
]
.

Proposition 3.12
.

We have
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\bot}=\mathcal{C}_{\alpha^{\bot}}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash S)
.

Proof.

Since
\mathcal{C}^{\perp}
is a dual relatively to the canonical dot product in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
, we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\perp}=n^{2}-|\mathcal{S}|=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha^{\bot}}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash S)
, therefore one inclusion suffices to conclude. Let
r=(r_{1},r_{2})\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash\mathcal{S}
. Then for each
s=(s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathcal{S}
, if we denote by
D=\left((\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{1}}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{2}}}\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}
and
C=\left(\alpha_{i_{1}}^{q^{s_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{q^{s_{2}}}\right)_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}
the respective codewords of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha^{\bot}}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash S)
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
associated to the monomials
X^{q^{r_{1}}}Y^{q^{r_{2}}}
and
X^{q^{s_{1}}}Y^{q^{s_{2}}}
, we have

\displaystyle\tr(CD^{\top})

\displaystyle=\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}}\alpha_{i_{1}}^{q^{s_{1}}}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{q^{s_{2}}}(\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{1}}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{2}}}

\displaystyle=\left(\sum_{i_{1}=1}^{n}\alpha_{i_{1}}^{q^{s_{1}}}(\alpha_{i_{1}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{1}}}\right)\left(\sum_{i_{2}=1}^{n}\alpha_{i_{2}}^{q^{s_{2}}}(\alpha_{i_{2}}^{\perp})^{q^{r_{2}}}\right)

\displaystyle=\mathds{1}_{r_{1}=s_{1}}\mathds{1}_{r_{2}=s_{2}}

\displaystyle=0,

since
r\not\in\mathcal{S}
by assumption . Therefore we have
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\bot}\supseteq\mathcal{C}_{\alpha^{\bot}}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash\mathcal{S})
which concludes the proof.
∎

Remark 3.13
.

In particular,
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\bot}
and
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash\mathcal{S})
are equivalent as matrix
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\text{-}[n\times n,k]
rank-metric codes,

i.e.
there exist invertible matrices
P,Q\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}})
such that
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})^{\bot}=P\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}\backslash\mathcal{S})Q
. This is a direct consequence of the form of the isomorphism
I_{2}
in Proposition
3.6
.

Given a basis
\alpha
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the families of Roth-tensor codes parametrised with the sets
\mathcal{S}
are ascending families of codes with respect to inclusion. In other words, we have the following statement.

Lemma 3.14
.

Let
\mathcal{S},\mathcal{S}^{\prime}\subseteq\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
with
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
. Then
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
and hence
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

For each
\overline{f(X,Y)}\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]/U_{X,Y}
such that
\Supp(\overline{f(X,Y)})\subseteq\mathcal{S}
, we have that
\Supp(\overline{f(X,Y)})\subseteq\mathcal{S}^{\prime}
, thus
\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})\subseteq\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.
∎

Proposition 3.15
.

Let
r_{1},r_{2}\in\mathbb{Z}
. Let
\mathcal{S}^{\prime}=\{((s_{1}+r_{1})\mod n,(s_{2}+r_{2})\mod n)\ |\ (s_{1},s_{2})\in\mathcal{S}\}
. Then there exist matrices
L,R\in\GL(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
L\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})R=\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
.

Proof.

The elements of
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
are the evaluations at
\alpha
of the polynomials with monomials of the form
X^{q^{(s_{1}+r_{1})\mod n}}Y^{q^{(s_{2}+r_{2})\mod n}}=(X^{q^{r_{1}}})^{q^{s_{1}}}(Y^{q^{r_{2}}})^{q^{s_{2}}}
. Therefore, with the notations of Proposition
3.6
, the code
\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})
is the image of
I_{1}(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}))
under the linear map
K\mapsto M(\alpha^{q^{r_{1}}})^{\top}KM(\alpha^{q^{r_{2}}})
. Since the Frobenius map is an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-automorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, then
\alpha^{q^{r_{1}}}
forms an
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and there exists a matrix
A\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
(\alpha_{1}^{q^{r_{1}}},\dots,\alpha_{n}^{q^{r_{1}}})=(\alpha_{1},\dots,\alpha_{n})A
. In particular we have
M(\alpha^{q^{r_{1}}})=M(\alpha)A
. Likewise there exists a matrix
B\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
such that
M(\alpha^{q^{r_{2}}})=M(\alpha)B
and therefore

\displaystyle\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\{(M(\alpha)A)^{\top}KM(\alpha)B\ |\ K\in I_{1}(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}))\}

\displaystyle=A^{\top}(I_{2}\circ I_{1})(\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S}))B

\displaystyle=A^{\top}\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})B.

∎

3.3
Properties of bilinearised
q
-polynomials

Lemma 3.16
.

Let
\mathcal{S}_{V}\subseteq\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
and let

\mathcal{S}_{f}\subseteq\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}
.
Let
V(Z)=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{V}}v_{\ell}Z^{q^{\ell}}\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]

and let
f(X,Y)=\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{F}}f_{s}X^{q^{s_{1}}}Y^{q^{s_{2}}}\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
.
Then
\Supp(V(f(X,Y)))\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{f}+\mathcal{S}_{V}
and for each
t\in\mathcal{S}_{f}+\mathcal{S}_{V}
, the coefficient in
V(f(X,Y))
of the monomial
X^{q^{t_{1}}}Y^{q^{t_{2}}}
is given by

\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{V}\\
t-\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\end{subarray}}v_{\ell}f_{t-\ell}^{q^{\ell}}.

Proof.

Observe that there is a bijection between the sets
\mathcal{S}_{f}\times\mathcal{S}_{V}
and
\{(t,\ell)\in(\mathcal{S}_{f}+\mathcal{S}_{V})\times\mathcal{S}_{V}\ |\ t-\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\}
. We have

V(f(X,Y))=\sum_{\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{V}}\sum_{s\in\mathcal{S}_{f}}v_{\ell}f_{s}^{q^{\ell}}X^{q^{s_{1}+\ell}}Y^{q^{s_{2}+\ell}}=\sum_{t\in\mathcal{S}_{f}+\mathcal{S}_{V}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{V}\\
t-\ell\in\mathcal{S}_{f}\end{subarray}}v_{\ell}f_{t-\ell}^{q^{\ell}}X^{q^{t_{1}}}Y^{q^{t_{2}}},

from which the statement follows.
∎

For each polynomial
F(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
, there exist unique linearized polynomials
P_{\ell}(Z),Q_{\ell}(Z)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
such that
F(X,Y)=\sum_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}P_{{\ell}}(X)Y^{q^{\ell}}=\sum_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}X^{q^{\ell}}Q_{{\ell}}(Y)
.

Proposition 3.17
.

Let
F(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
.
For each
\ell\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, let
P_{\ell}(Z),Q_{\ell}(Z)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
be such that

F(X,Y)=\sum_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}P_{{\ell}}(X)Y^{q^{\ell}}=\sum_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}X^{q^{\ell}}Q_{{\ell}}(Y).

(3)

Then
\displaystyle\bigcap_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker P_{{\ell}}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)
and we have equality if
\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)<q^{n}
. Similarly,
\displaystyle\bigcap_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker Q_{{\ell}}\subseteq\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{2}(F)
and we have equality if
\deg_{X}F(X,Y)<q^{n}
.

Proof.

Let
x\in\bigcap_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker P_{\ell}
. For each
y\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, we have
F(x,y)=0
, therefore
x\in\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)
. Conversely, let
x\in\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)
and assume that
\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)<q^{n}
. Then the polynomial
F(x,Y)
is a linearised
q
-polynomial of
q
-degree less than
n
that annihilates every element of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
, thus
F(x,Y)=0
. Hence, every coefficient of
F(x,Y)
is zero
i.e.

P_{{\ell}}(x)=0
for each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
and so
x\in\bigcap_{{\ell}\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker P_{\ell}
. A similar argument applied to the polynomials
Q_{\ell},\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.18
.

Let
F(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
be non-zero.
If
q
-
\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)<n
, then

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)\leq q\text{-}\!\deg_{X}F(X,Y)
. Similarly, if
q
-
\deg_{X}F(X,Y)<n
, then
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{2}(F)\leq q\text{-}\!\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)
.

Proof.

Assume that
q
-
\deg_{Y}F(X,Y)<n
. Let
P_{{\ell}}(X)
be the unique linearised
q
-polynomials over
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
satisfying (
3
). For each
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
, denote by
P_{{\ell}}
the corresponding
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear endomorphism of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
. Proposition
3.17
ensures that
\bigcap_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker P_{\ell}=\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)
, hence we have:

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\bigcap_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\ker P_{\ell}\leq\min_{\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}}\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\ker P_{\ell}\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}\\
P_{\ell}(X)\neq 0\end{subarray}}q\text{-}\!\deg P_{\ell}(X).

Since

is non-zero, there exists
\ell\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that

is bounded from above by

\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\mathfrak{R}\mathfrak{a}\mathfrak{d}_{1}(F)\leq q\text{-}\!\deg_{X}F(X,Y)

The proof of the second statement is analogous.
∎

Definition 3.19
.

We define the
fibre weight
of a matrix

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the entries of

\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(T)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{T_{i_{1},i_{2}}\ |\ i_{1},i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\})

Let

\llbracket 1,n^{2}\rrbracket
to
\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}
.
This yields an isomorphism
\hat{\sigma}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\rightarrow\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n^{2}},M\mapsto(M[\sigma(\ell)])_{\ell\in\llbracket 1,n^{2}\rrbracket}
for all

\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(M)=\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\hat{\sigma}(M))
for all

In particular, decoding any tensor code with respect to this metric is equivalent to decoding its isomorphic image in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n^{2}}
for the rank-metric.

For each
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
, we denote by
\mathcal{U}_{1}(T)
(
resp.

\mathcal{U}_{2}(T)
) the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-subspace of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n}
spanned by all the rows (
resp.
the columns) of
T
over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
.

Definition 3.20
.

Let
j\in\{1,2\}
. We define the
j
-slice space weight
of a tensor
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
, denoted

\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the

Proposition 3.21
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
and let
\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
be its associated tensor with respect to a basis
\omega
of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Then
\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{1}}(T)
,
\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{2}}(T)
and
\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(T)
are exactly the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimensions of
\slicesp_{1}(\Gamma)
,
\slicesp_{2}(\Gamma)
and
\fibresp_{3}(\Gamma)
respectively, which do not depend on the choice of
\omega
.

Proof.

Since
\omega
is a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the maps
\phi,\psi
defined by

\phi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n}\\
M&\longmapsto&\sum_{k=1}^{n}M[:,k]\omega_{k}\end{array}\right.\qquad\text{and}\qquad\psi:\left\{\begin{array}[]{ccc}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}&\longrightarrow&\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}\\
u&\longmapsto&\sum_{k=1}^{n}u_{k}\omega_{k}\end{array}\right.

are
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphisms. Moreover, one can verify that
\mathcal{U}_{1}(T)=\phi(\slicesp_{1}(\Gamma))
, that
\mathcal{U}_{2}(T)=\phi(\slicesp_{2}(\Gamma))

and that
\psi(\fibresp_{3}(\Gamma))
is exactly the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the entries of
T
. Since
\phi
and
\psi
are isomorphisms,
we have that the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimensions of
\slicesp_{i}(\Gamma)
and
\fibresp_{3}(\Gamma)
are preserved under these isomorphisms, respectively.
Since the weights
\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{i}}(T)
and
\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(T)
are independent of
\omega
, then so are the dimensions of
\slicesp_{i}(\Gamma)
and
\fibresp_{3}(\Gamma)
.

∎

We note the following link between these weights and the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank of the fibres of the tensors.

Lemma 3.22
.

Let
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
. We have the following properties.

1.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank of each column of
T
is bounded from above by the dimension of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the rows of
T
. In particular, we have the inequality
\max_{i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}T[:,i_{2}]\leq\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{1}}(T)
.

2.

The
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank of each row of
T
is bounded from above by the dimension of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of its columns. In particular, we have the inequality
\max_{i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}T[i_{1},:]\leq\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{2}}(T)
.

Proof.

Let
i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. Consider the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-linear epimorphism
p_{i_{2}}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}},x\mapsto x_{i_{2}}
and for each
i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
we have
p_{i_{2}}(T[i_{1},:])=T_{i_{1},i_{2}}
. Thus
\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(\{T_{i_{1},i_{2}}:i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\})=p_{i_{2}}({\mathcal{U}}_{1}(T))
. Finally, since
p_{i_{2}}
is surjective we have
\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}T[:,i_{2}]=\dim_{{\mathbb{F}}_{q}}(p_{i_{2}}({\mathcal{U}}_{1}(T)))\leq\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}({\mathcal{U}}_{1}(T))=w_{\slicesp_{1}}(T)
.

The second inequality follows similarly.
∎

These bounds are sharp.
For instance, consider
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
such that
T[i_{1},i_{2}]=0
for each
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}
with
i_{2}\neq 1
,
i.e.
only the first column of
T
is non-zero. Then, the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-span of the entries of
T
, which is
\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{T[i_{1},1]\ |\ i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\}
, has the same
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-dimension as
\mathcal{U}_{1}(T)=\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\{(T[i_{1},1],0,\dots,0)\ |\ i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\}
.

Clearly, the aforementioned weights are both subadditive and such that the zero tensor has weight zero. Therefore, for any
j\in\{1,2\}
, the maps
d_{\fibresp_{3}}:(T_{1},T_{2})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n})^{2}\mapsto\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(T_{1}-T_{2})
and
d_{\slicesp_{j}}:(T_{1},T_{2})\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n})^{2}\mapsto\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{j}}(T_{1}-T_{2})
are distance functions on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
. If
\mathcal{C}
is a non-zero tensor code, we denote by
d_{\slicesp_{j}}(\mathcal{C}):=\min\{d_{\slicesp_{j}}(C,C^{\prime}):C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},C\neq C^{\prime}\}
and
d_{\fibresp_{3}}(\mathcal{C}):=\min\{d_{\fibresp_{3}}(C,C^{\prime}):C,C^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C},C\neq C^{\prime}\}
the
minimum distances
of the code with respect to those metrics.

Likewise, since the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank yields a metric on
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n}
, the maximum
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-rank of all columns or all rows of an element in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
yields a metric on

\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
. More precisely, the maps
d_{\rk_{1}},d_{\rk_{2}}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\to\mathbb{N}_{0}
defined by

\displaystyle d_{\rk_{1}}(T_{1},T_{2})

\displaystyle:=\max\{\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T_{1}-T_{2})[:,i_{2}]\penalty\ |\penalty\ i_{2}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\}

\displaystyle\text{and}\qquad d_{\rk_{2}}(T_{1},T_{2})

\displaystyle:=\max\{\rank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T_{1}-T_{2})[i_{1},:]\penalty\ |\penalty\ i_{1}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket\},

for all
(T_{1},T_{2})\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\times\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}

define metrics on the vector space
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
.

Proposition 3.23
.

Let
\mathcal{S}
be a non-empty subset of
\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{2}
. Then the code
\mathcal{C}=\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
has the following properties.

1.

We have

.

2.

We have

,
j\in\{1,2\}
.

Proof.

Let
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
be a non-zero codeword and let

. Firstly, Proposition
3.9
states that each column (
resp.
row) of

is an element of the MRD code

(

In particular the rank of the space spanned by all the entries in

In the sequel, we will remark that the fibre and slice weights introduced above all give lower bounds on the tensor-rank. The
tensor-rank
of an element
\Gamma
in
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
, denoted by
\trank(\Gamma)
, is the least integer
R\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that there exist
vectors
a_{r},b_{r},c_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
for each
r\in\llbracket 1,R\rrbracket
such that

\Gamma=\sum_{r=1}^{R}a_{r}\otimes b_{r}\otimes c_{r}

in which case the expression above is called a
minimal tensor-rank form
of
\Gamma
.
As a coordinate tensor, we have
\Gamma[i_{1},i_{2},i_{3}]=\sum_{r=1}^{R}a_{r,i_{1}}b_{r,i_{2}}c_{r,i_{3}}
for each
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}
.
This generalises the notion of matrix rank since the rank of a matrix
M
is the smallest integer
R
such that
M
can be expressed as a sum of
R
rank-
1
matrices,
i.e.
matrices of the form
a\otimes b=a^{\top}b
. Note that the map
(\Gamma_{1},\Gamma_{2})\mapsto\trank(\Gamma_{1}-\Gamma_{2})
is a metric on the vector space
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
.

Lemma 3.24
.

Let
\omega
be a basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}/\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Let
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
and let
\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
be the tensor associated to
T
for the basis
\omega
. Then
\trank(\Gamma)
does not depend on
\omega
and is the smallest integer
R\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
such that there exists
A_{1},\dots,A_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
of rank one and
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that:

T=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\gamma_{r}A_{r}.

In particular, for each
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
and each
L,R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
we have
\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(LTR)\leq\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T)
, and this inequality is an equality if
L,R\in\GL_{n}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
.

Proof.

Let
R\in{\mathbb{N}_{0}}
. Let
(\omega_{1}^{*},\dots,\omega_{n}^{*})
be
the dual basis of
\omega
,
i.e.

\omega_{i}^{*}(\omega_{j})=\mathds{1}_{i=j}
for each
i,j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. Let
A_{1},\dots,A_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
be a collection of rank-
1
matrices and let
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
such that
T=\sum_{r=1}^{R}A_{r}\gamma_{r}
. Let
a_{r},b_{r},c_{r}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that for each
r
we have
A_{r}=a_{r}^{\top}b_{r}
and
c_{r}=(\omega_{1}^{*}(\gamma_{r}),\dots,\omega_{n}^{*}(\gamma_{r}))
. Then
\Gamma=\sum_{r=1}^{R}a_{r}\otimes b_{r}\otimes c_{r}
, thus
\trank(\Gamma)\leq R
. We have the converse inequality by definition of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-isomorphism
(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
associated to the basis
\omega
. Additionally, denote by
L,R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
two matrices over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and denote by
T\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}
a matrix with a decomposition
T=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\gamma_{r}A_{r}
with
\gamma_{1},\dots,\gamma_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
, with
A_{1},\dots,A_{R}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
matrices of rank one and with
R
a positive integer. Then we have
LTC=\sum_{r=1}^{R}\gamma_{r}(LA_{r}R)
and
LA_{r}R\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
has rank at most one for each integer
r\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
. Hence
\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(LTR)\leq R
and in particular
\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(LTR)\leq\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(T)
, and assuming that
L
and
R
are invertible, the other inequality can be obtained symmetrically.
∎

Lemma 3.30
.

Let
\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
. Then for each
j\in\llbracket 1,3\rrbracket
we have
\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\slicesp_{j}(\Gamma)=\dim_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}\fibresp_{j}(\Gamma)
.

Proof.

We will prove the property for
j=3
; the case
j=1
is similar. Let
\eta
be the least integer such that there exist
M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n\times n}
and
c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
such that

\Gamma=\sum_{r=1}^{\eta}M_{r}\otimes c_{r},\qquad\emph{i.e.}\qquad\forall(i,j,k)\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}:\Gamma[i,j,k]=\sum_{r=1}^{\eta}M_{r,(i,j)}c_{r,k}.

(4)

Let
\lambda_{1},\dots,\lambda_{\eta}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}
be scalars such that
\sum_{r=1}^{\eta}\lambda_{r}M_{r}=0
. If there exists
\ell\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket
such that
\lambda_{\ell}\neq 0
, then
\Gamma=\sum_{r\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket\backslash\{\ell\}}M_{r}\otimes(c_{r}-\lambda_{r}\lambda_{\ell}^{-1}c_{\ell})
, which contradicts the minimality of
\eta
. Therefore,
\lambda_{r}=0
for each
r\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket
proving that the
M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta}
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
. Similarly, the
c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta}
are also linearly independent. We will prove that
\{M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta}\}
and
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta}\}
generate

\slicesp_{j}(\Gamma)
and
\fibresp_{j}(\Gamma)
, respectively.

Since
\Gamma[i,j,:]=\sum_{r=1}^{\eta}M_{r,(i,j)}c_{r}
for each
i,j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
, we have the inclusion
\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta})\supseteq\fibresp_{3}(\Gamma)
. Conversely, let
\phi:\llbracket 1,n^{2}\rrbracket\to\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{2}
be a bijection. Since
(M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta})
are linearly independent over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
, the matrix of size
n^{2}\times\eta
below has
\eta
linearly independent columns over
\mathbb{F}_{q}
and thus has rank
\eta
:

\begin{pmatrix}M_{1,\phi(1)}&\ldots&M_{\eta,\phi(1)}\\
\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
M_{1,\phi(n^{2})}&\ldots&M_{\eta,\phi(n^{2})}\end{pmatrix}.

In particular, there exist distinct integers
\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\eta}\in\llbracket 1,n^{2}\rrbracket
such that
(M_{r,\phi(\ell_{\delta})})_{(\delta,r)\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket^{2}}
is a non-singular matrix in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{\eta\times\eta}
. Hence, there exists an invertible matrix
Q\in\GL_{\eta}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
with
\sum_{\delta=1}^{\eta}Q_{i,\delta}M_{r,\phi(\ell_{\delta})}=\mathds{1}_{i=r}
for each
i,r\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket
. In particular, for each
i\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket
, we can write the following:

c_{i}=\sum_{r=1}^{n}\mathds{1}_{r=i}c_{r}=\sum_{\delta=1}^{\eta}\sum_{r=1}^{\eta}Q_{i,\delta}M_{r,\phi(\ell_{\delta})}c_{r}=\sum_{\delta=1}^{\eta}Q_{i,\delta}\Gamma[\phi(\ell_{\delta}),:].

\Span_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta})\supseteq\slicesp_{3}(\Gamma)
by construction while the converse inclusion is holds by the
\mathbb{F}_{q}

c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta}

\ell_{1},\dots,\ell_{\eta}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
such that
(c_{r,\ell_{\delta}})_{(\delta,r)\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket^{2}}
is invertible with inverse
P\in\GL_{\eta}(\mathbb{F}_{q})
say, so that for each
i\in\llbracket 1,\eta\rrbracket
we have
M_{i}=\sum_{\delta=1}^{\eta}P_{i,\delta}\Gamma[:,:,\ell_{\delta}]
. Therefore,
\{c_{1},\dots,c_{\eta}\}
and
\{M_{1},\dots,M_{\eta}\}
are respective bases of the
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector spaces
\fibresp_{3}(T)
and
\slicesp_{3}(T)
, which proves the lemma.

4.2
A factorisation algorithm

We now focus on resolving the following problem, which is relevant to obtaining an extension of the Loidreau-Overbeck decoder.

Problem 4.5
.

Let
t\in\mathbb{N}
,
V(Z)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
and
N(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
such that
q\text{-}\!\deg V(Z)\leq t
,
\Supp N(X,Y)\subseteq\mathcal{S}+\llbracket 0,t\rrbracket
.
Compute
f(X,Y)\in\mathcal{C}(\mathcal{S})
satisfying
V(f(X,Y))=N(X,Y)
.

This is a generalisation of symbolic left-hand division of polynomials,
i.e.
given two
q
-polynomials
f(Z)
and
g(Z)
in
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
compute
h(Z)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
satisfying
f(h(Z))=g(Z)
; see
[
18
]
. With the observation below, we can use an existing division algorithm on the ring of linearised polynomials to solve Problem
4.5
.

Lemma 4.6
.

Let
V(Z)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[Z]
and let
N(X,Y)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}n_{s}X^{q^{s_{1}}}Y^{q^{s_{2}}}\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
be
q
-polynomials with
(n_{s})_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}
a sequence in
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
with finitely many non-zero terms.

For each integer
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, define
\displaystyle N_{\delta}(Z):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
\tau+\delta\geq 0\end{subarray}}n_{(\tau,\delta+\tau)}Z^{q^{\tau}}.

The following are equivalent.

(i)

There exists
f(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
such that
V(f(X,Y))=N(X,Y)
.

(ii)

There exists
(f_{\delta}(Z))_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}
a sequence of
q
-polynomials in
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
with finitely many non-zero terms such that all monomials in
f_{\delta}(Z)
have
q
-degree at least
n-\delta
, and
V(f_{\delta}(Z))=N_{\delta}(Z)
for each
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
.

In particular, if (ii) is satisfied for a sequence
(f_{\delta}(Z))_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}
, then
f(X,Y)=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})
satisfies (i).

Proof.

Since the sets
(\{(x,x+\delta)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{Z}\})_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}
form a partition of
\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, we have

N(X,Y)=\sum_{s\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{2}}n_{s}X^{q^{s}}Y^{q^{s}}=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
\tau+\delta\geq 0\end{subarray}}n_{(\tau,\delta+\tau)}(XY^{q^{\delta}})^{q^{\tau}}=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}}).

Note that for each
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
,
N_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})
is indeed a bilinearised
q
-polynomial, as the definition of
N_{\delta}(Z)
implies that, if not zero, its monomials have
q
-degree at least
-\delta
.

Let
f(X,Y)\in\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
be a bilinearised
q
-polynomial and let
(f_{\delta}(Z))_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}
be the sequence of
q
-polynomials satisfying

f(X,Y)=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\tau}})
.
Suppose that
V(f(X,Y))=N(X,Y)
. Since we have

\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}N_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})=N(X,Y)=V(f(X,Y))=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}V(f_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})),

and since
\Supp(V(f_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})))\subseteq\{(x,x+\delta)\ |\ x\in\mathbb{Z}\}
, we obtain that
N_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\delta}})=V(f(XY^{q^{\delta}}))
and thus
N_{\delta}(Z)=V(f_{\delta}(Z))
.

Conversely, if there exists a sequence
(f_{\delta}(Z))_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}
of
q
-polynomials in
\mathscr{M}_{q,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X,Y]
with finitely many non-zero terms such that all monomials in
f_{\delta}(Z)
have
q
-degree at least
-\delta
, and
V(f_{\delta}(Z))=N_{\delta}(Z)
for each
\delta\in\mathbb{Z}
, then setting
f(X,Y)=\sum_{\delta\in\mathbb{Z}}f_{\delta}(XY^{q^{\tau}})
, we obtain
V(f(X,Y))=N(X,Y)
.
∎

Using the existing left-Euclidean division algorithm as described in

]
, we can solve Problem

as follows: given

from

Let

(18)

In particular, assuming that

\left|\left\{E\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\ |\ \trank(E)>R_{n},\text{$\forall C\in\mathcal{C}(\llbracket 0,\mu_{n}\rrbracket^{2}):$ {Alg\penalty\ \ref{alg:RadicalDecodingFindMin}}($C+E$) $=C$ with parameter $\mu_{n}$}\right\}\right|=\Omega(q^{nR_{n}^{2}}).

Proof.

Note that

is equivalent to

\mu_{n}\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket

Consider
\mathcal{S}:=\left\{E\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\ |\ \textup{w}_{\slicesp_{1}}(E)\leq R_{n},\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(E)\leq R_{n}\right\}
. Since the transposition map

swapping the second and third component is a bijection with

, with
\dim\slicesp_{2}(t(\Gamma))=\dim\slicesp_{3}(\Gamma)
and with
\dim\slicesp_{3}(t(\Gamma))=\dim\slicesp_{2}(\Gamma)
, then the set
\mathcal{S}
as same cardinality as the set
\tilde{\mathcal{S}}:=\left\{E\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n\times n}\ |\ \textup{w}_{\slicesp_{1}}(E)\leq R_{n},\textup{w}_{\slicesp_{2}}(E)\leq R_{n}\right\}
.

Let
s_{1},s_{2}\in\llbracket 1,R_{n}\rrbracket
be integers. Consider the injective map
\phi_{s_{1},s_{2}}:\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{s_{1}\times s_{2}}\to\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{R_{n}\times R_{n}},A\mapsto\left[\begin{smallmatrix}A&0\\

-span of rows or columns, we can consider

\psi_{s_{2}}^{(2)}:\mathcal{B}_{s_{2},s_{2}}\to\mathcal{C}_{s_{1},s_{2}}
and

and by the following relations, where the minima below are defined with a fixed total order, e.g. the lexicographical order, on the set of tuples of integers.

\begin{array}[]{c}\forall E\in\mathcal{A}_{s_{1},s_{2}},\forall i\in\llbracket 1,s_{1}\rrbracket,\forall j\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:\psi_{s_{1}}^{(1)}(E)[i,j]=E[I_{i},j]\\
\phantom{x}\text{where }I:=\min\{I^{\prime}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{s_{1}}\ |\ I^{\prime}_{1}<\cdots<I^{\prime}_{s_{1}}\ ,\ (E[I^{\prime}_{1},:],\cdots,E[I^{\prime}_{s_{1}},:])\ \text{linearly independent over $\mathbb{F}_{q}$}\}.\\[5.69046pt]

corresponds to a succession of two operations preserving the slice-spaces dimension : a multiplication by a well-chosen invertible matrix along the first mode moving the considered rows up and deleting the rows below, and cropping the created zero rows, see
[

and the surjectivity of the two maps is immediate if one considers matrices of the form

Remark 5.10
.

It is non-trivial to check if the lower-bound on the tensor-rank of Corollary
3.32
is met or not. It is however the case for
\mu=n-1
as the evaluation of

has tensor-rank one, with

with an argument similar to
[

, with
[
14
, Theorem 2.24]
.

Let
\mu\in\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket
. We have seen in Section
4.5
that an interleaved decoder can be applied to
\mathcal{C}(\llbracket 0,\mu\rrbracket^{2})
. We obtain an algorithm that can correct with high probability any error

such that
\textup{w}_{\fibresp_{3}}(E)\leq\frac{2n}{2n+1}(n-\mu-1)
. With Proposition
3.31
, it is possible to consider the algorithm as a tensor-rank decoder. In other words, if

, the decoder for the interleaved code can decode any received word

with

if

is unknown too for a chosen uniformly at random

Remark 5.11
.

As expressed before, there is a significant asymptotic difference between

and

with an exponent quadratic in

, while the second is upper-bounded by a power of

Let
m\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
with
m\geq 2
. We will call
\boldsymbol{(m+1)}
-tensors
of size
n\times\cdots\times n
over the field
\mathbb{F}_{q}
any element of the

-dimensional
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-vector space
\bigotimes_{j=1}^{m+1}\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}=(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes(m+1)}
. An element
T\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes(m+1)}
will be uniquely associated to its expression as an
(m+1)
-dimensional array in the basis
(e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{m+1}})_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{m+1}}
where we denote by
(e_{\iota})_{\iota\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket}
the canonical
\mathbb{F}_{q}
-basis of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
. Namely, a unique tuple
(T[i])_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{m+1}}
is associated to
T
such that
T=\sum_{i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{m+1}}T[i]e_{i_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes e_{i_{m+1}}
.

The
tensor-rank
of an element of
\Gamma\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes(m+1)}
is the least amount
\mu
of
elementary tensors
,
i.e.
tensors of the form
u_{r,1}\otimes\cdots\otimes u_{r,m+1}
with
u_{r,1},\dots,u_{r,m+1}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
for each
r\in\llbracket 1,\mu\rrbracket
, that sum up to
\Gamma
.

Note that there is an
\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
-isomorphism

Proposition 6.1
.

Let
\mathcal{S}\subseteq\llbracket 0,n-1\rrbracket^{m}
. Let
j\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket
. Denote
\mathcal{S}^{\,\hat{\!j}}=\{(s_{1},\dots,s_{j-1},s_{j+1},\dots,s_{m})\ |\ s\in\mathcal{S}\}
. Then any

-slice of an element

is in

. Consequently, we have the following:

\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\,\hat{\!j}})\\
C\neq 0\end{subarray}}\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C)\leq\min_{\begin{subarray}{c}{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})\\
C\neq 0\end{subarray}}\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C).

Proof.

Consider the elements above. Let
C\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S})
and consider the following minimal tensor-rank form of
C
where
\tau=\trank_{\mathbb{F}_{q}}(C)
, and where
\gamma^{(r)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}
and
u_{j}^{(r)}\in\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}
for each

and each
j\in\llbracket 1,m\rrbracket
,

By construction, there exists a polynomial
f(X_{1},\dots,X_{m})\in\mathscr{M}_{n,\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}}[X_{1},\dots,X_{m}]
with
\Supp(f)\subseteq\mathcal{S}
such that
C[i]=f(\alpha_{i_{1}},\dots,\alpha_{i_{m}})
for each
i\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{m}
.

Let
I_{j}\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
. The
I_{j}^{\ th}

j
-slice of
C
is the tensor
\tilde{C}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q^{n}}^{n})^{\otimes(m-1)}
given for all
(i_{1},\dots,i_{j-1},i_{j+1},\dots,i_{m})\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{m-1}
by
\tilde{C}[i_{1},\dots,i_{j-1},i_{j+1},\dots,i_{m}]=C[i_{1},\dots,i_{j-1},I_{j},i_{j+1},\dots,i_{m}]
. Clearly,
\tilde{C}
is the evaluation tensor of the multilinearised
q
-polynomial with

which has support contained in

\tilde{C}\in\mathcal{C}_{\alpha}(\mathcal{S}^{\,\hat{\!j}})
, and we have

If

Tensor-rank combinatorics

We denote by
\left[\begin{smallmatrix}b\\
a\end{smallmatrix}\right]_{q}
the Gaussian binomial coefficient,
i.e.
the number of
a
-dimensional subspaces of
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{b}
, see
[
26
, Ch 24]
. We consider tensors in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n\times k}=\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m}\otimes\mathbb{F}_{q}^{k}
, for integers
k,m,n
. All the other definitions hold. It is known that the number of elements in
\mathbb{F}_{q}^{m\times n\times k}
with tensor-rank precisely one is exactly
\frac{(q^{m}-1)(q^{n}-1)(q^{k}-1)}{(q-1)^{2}}
. We remind the reader that a rank one matrix is uniquely defined by the choice of a one-dimensional column-space and a one dimensional row-space.

2

by

\begin{array}[]{l}\Gamma^{(0)}_{0}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}1&\text{if }i\in\{1,2\},j=k=1\\
0&\text{otherwise;}\end{array}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
\Gamma^{(1)}_{0}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-1&\text{if }i=j=k=1\\
0&\text{otherwise;}\end{array}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
\Gamma^{(2)}_{0}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-1&\text{if }i=2,j=k=1\\
0&\text{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

Therefore, we have
\Gamma=\Gamma_{0}^{(0)}+\Gamma_{0}^{(1)}+\Gamma_{1}^{(2)}+\sum_{t=1}^{r}\Gamma_{t}+\sum_{t=r+1}^{R-3}(-1)^{t}\Gamma_{0}
hence
\Gamma
has an expression as a sum of exactly
3+r+(R-3)-(r+1)+1=R
tensors of tensor-rank one.

•

Assume that
R-r=1
and that there exist
\tau\in\llbracket 1,r\rrbracket
and
(i_{0},j_{0},k_{0}),(i_{1},j_{1},k_{1})\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}
with
(i_{1},j_{1},k_{1})\neq(i_{0},j_{0},k_{0})
and
\Gamma_{\tau}[i_{0},j_{0},k_{0}]\neq 0
and
\Gamma_{\tau}[i_{1},j_{1},k_{1}]\neq 0
. Without loss of generality, assume that
i_{0}\neq i_{1}
. Consider
\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau},\Gamma_{\tau}^{(1)}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
defined by the following relations.

\forall i,j,k\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket:\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}\Gamma_{\tau}[i,j,k]&\text{if}&i=i_{0}\\
0&\text{if}&i\neq i_{0}\\
\end{array}\right.\quad and\quad\Gamma^{(1)}_{\tau}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rcl}0&\text{if}&i=i_{0}\\
\Gamma_{\tau}[i,j,k]&\text{if}&i\neq i_{0}\\
\end{array}\right.

Clearly
\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau}+\Gamma_{\tau}^{(1)}=\Gamma_{\tau}
, and notice that
\trank(\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau})=\trank(\Gamma_{\tau}^{(1)})=1
. Therefore,
\Gamma=\Gamma^{(0)}_{\tau}+\Gamma_{\tau}^{(1)}+\sum_{t\in\llbracket 1,R-1\rrbracket\backslash\{\tau\}}\Gamma_{t}
can be written as a sum of
R
elements of tensor-rank one.

•

Finally, assume that
R-r=1
and that each tensor
\Gamma_{t}
, where
t\in\llbracket 1,R-1\rrbracket
, has a single non-zero entry. Denote by
a=\Gamma_{1}[i_{1},j_{1},k_{1}]\neq 0
the single non-zero entries of
\Gamma_{1}
with a certain
(i_{1},j_{1},k_{1})\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket^{3}
. We can define
\tilde{\Gamma_{1}},\tilde{\Gamma_{1.5}}\in(\mathbb{F}_{q}^{n})^{\otimes 3}
such that for each
i,j,k\in\llbracket 1,n\rrbracket
we have

\begin{array}[]{l}\tilde{\Gamma_{1}}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}a&\text{if }(i,j,k)=(i_{1},j_{1},k_{1})\\
1&\text{if }(i,j,k)=(i_{2},j_{1},k_{1})\\
0&\text{otherwise;}\end{array}\right.\\[14.22636pt]
\tilde{\Gamma_{1.5}}[i,j,k]=\left\{\begin{array}[]{rl}-1&\text{if }(i,j,k)=(i_{2},j_{1},k_{1})\\
0&\text{otherwise;}\end{array}\right.\end{array}

with any
i_{2}\neq i_{1}
. Therefore,
\Gamma=\tilde{\Gamma_{1}}+\tilde{\Gamma_{1.5}}+\sum_{t=2}^{R-1}\Gamma_{t}
is an expression of
\Gamma
as a sum of
R
elements of tensor-rank one.
