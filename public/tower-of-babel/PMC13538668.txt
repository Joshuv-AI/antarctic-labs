Balancing accuracy and efficiency in lumbar spine image segmentation using multi-scale attention and residual learning
Authors: Vijaya Bhaskar Sadu, Bharathi Subramaniam, Rukmani Devi Sethuraman, Sudhakar Tummala, Beluguri Venkateswarlu, Jeevika Tharini Viswanathan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractAccurate automated segmentation of Lumbar Spine Structures (LSS) in Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) is important for effective diagnosis and treatment planning. However, most current methods encounter a trade-off between segmentation precision and computational efficiency. In particular, several methods fall short in modelling the intricate multi-scale anatomical details and subtle boundary transitions typical of vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs, thereby limiting their clinical utility. This study introduces Multiscale Residual and Adaptive Spatial-Channel Feature Pyramid Network (MRAS + FPN), a novel Deep Learning (DL) designed to enhance Lumbar Spine Segmentation. The model integrates Multi-Scale Attention (MS) with Residual Learning (RL) to balance accuracy and efficiency. Two principal innovations are embedded in the design: (a) Multiscale Residual Attention Blocks (MRAB), which incorporate Directionally Dilated Convolution Modules (DDCM) coupled with spatial-channel attention to address anisotropic anatomical structures, and (b) Adaptive Spatial-Channel Attention Blocks (ASCAB), which refine Feature Selection (FS) across pyramid levels dynamically. Also, Cross-level Feature Gating (CFG) is employed to regulate information flow between the encoder and decoder. The model was trained and validated on the MRSpineSeg dataset, which comprises 215 T2-weighted lumbar MRI volumes annotated by experts, covering vertebrae T9-S1 and their corresponding intervertebral discs. A composite loss function was used, combining Dice, Binary Cross-Entropy, and a boundary-sensitive term. MRAS-FPN proved to have improved segmentation performance compared with established models, including 2D U-Net, 3D U-Net, and DeepLab v3+. It achieved a Dice Similarity Coefficient (DSC) of 87.8 ± 1.3% and a mean Intersection over Union (mIoU) of 78.2 ± 2.1%, outperforming the best baseline (3D U-Net: 83.4% DSC, 73.4% mIoU) by 4.4% and 4.8%. Evaluation of boundary accuracy showed marked improvements, with a 95th percentile Hausdorff Distance (HD95) of 3.18 ± 0.8 mm and an Average Symmetric Surface Distance (ASSD) of 1.32 ± 0.5 mm—representing gains of 19.9% and 25.0% compared to 3D U-Net. Structure-wise analysis indicated stable segmentation quality across all vertebral levels (T9–S1: 84.4% mean DSC) and intervertebral discs (85.6% mean DSC), with notable gains in regions of high anatomical complexity. Ablation experiments further confirmed the utility of each component, with MRAB generating a 3.9% improvement in DSC and ASCAB contributing an additional 2.1% over the base FPN. MRAS-FPN achieves a well-calibrated balance between segmentation fidelity and resource efficiency, generating SOTA results while requiring manageable computational resources (22.1 hours of training time and 15.8 GB of memory). Its MSA successfully captures intricate anatomical features and boundary definitions critical for clinical use. The model’s strong performance across a standard range of spinal structures and its resilience to overfitting underscore its applicability in real-world scenarios, including surgical planning, diagnostic support, and automated testing of the Lumbar Spine (LS).Keywords: Magnetic resonance imaging, Deep learning, Lumbar spine structures, Residual learning, Multi-scale attention, Diagnostic support systemSubject terms: Anatomy, Computational biology and bioinformatics, Engineering, Health care, Mathematics and computing, Medical research

Introduction

Lumbar Spine (LS) disorders are among the most common musculoskeletal conditions globally, with estimates indicating that nearly 80% of individuals experience some form of lower back pain during their lifetime, resulting in significant healthcare costs and productivity losses1,2. Magnetic Resonance Imaging (MRI) has become indispensable for the comprehensive analysis of Lumbar Spine Structures (LSS), providing critical insights that inform diagnosis, therapeutic planning, and longitudinal monitoring of spinal pathologies such as disc herniation, spinal stenosis, degenerative disc disease, and vertebral compression fractures3–5. The ability to segment vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs accurately from MRI data is fundamental for enabling robust quantitative analysis, deriving volumetric indices, and powering computer-aided diagnostic tools designed to enhance Clinical Decision-Making Systems (CDMS)6–10.

Despite being the current clinical standard, manual segmentation of LSS in MRI poses several challenges that constrain its broader utility and scalability11–13. The task is notably time-intensive, frequently requiring 2–3 h for experienced radiologists to manually delineate vertebrae and intervertebral discs across all sagittal slices of a typical scan14–16. This labor-intensive process creates substantial bottlenecks in busy radiology departments, where timely image interpretation is crucial to maintaining continuity of care17,18. Moreover, manual annotation is inherently subjective, leading to notable inter-observer and intra-observer variability; reported Dice coefficients among expert annotators typically range from 0.75 to 0.8511–13. Such variability compromises diagnostic reliability and impacts downstream treatment outcomes4,13. These limitations are even more pronounced in pathological cases, such as advanced disc degeneration or vertebral compression fractures, where distorted or occluded anatomical boundaries make reliable manual interpretation particularly difficult19.

A range of automated segmentation methods has been developed to mitigate the limitations of manual annotation, yet many face persistent challenges that hinder their routine clinical deployment17,18,20,21. Classical image processing methods, such as edge detection, region growing, and atlas-based approaches, have been applied sparingly in LS-MRI, primarily due to the marked anatomical variability of the region and the subtle intensity gradients between adjacent structures22,23. These traditional approaches frequently report Dice scores below 75% and tend to fail under pathological conditions or in the presence of imaging objects, leading to unreliable segmentation outputs23. More recently, deep learning, particularly CNN-based architectures such as U-Net and its variants, has delivered notable gains in segmentation accuracy but still exhibits critical shortcomings17,18. The standard 2D U-Net frequently fails to incorporate sufficient inter-slice spatial context, leading to discontinuities across adjacent slices24. Although 3D U-Net improves spatial coherence, it remains computationally demanding and continues to struggle with fine boundary delineation24–26. Feature Pyramid Networks (FPN) and attention-enhanced models such as DeepLab v3 + are generally not tailored to capture the directional and anisotropic features unique to spinal anatomy14,18,27.

The persistent gap between existing automated segmentation methods and the clinical demands of spinal imaging underscores the need for more principled solutions that balance high accuracy with computational feasibility. The intricate anatomy of the LS introduces distinct challenges that require carefully designed network architectures. Vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs exhibit marked morphological variability across spinal levels (T9-S1), and subtle intensity gradients in T2-weighted MRI scans require models with broad, multi-scale feature representation3,28. These structures also display marked anisotropy: vertebral bodies tend to exhibit a vertically aligned morphology, whereas intervertebral discs exhibit horizontal elongation, posing specific demands on FE that must be sensitive to directional patterns5,20. Pathological conditions such as disc degeneration or vertebral fractures frequently distort anatomical regularity, making it essential for segmentation models to incorporate adaptive attention mechanisms that generalise across a broad spectrum of clinical scenarios10,19. As the clinical demand for quantitative spine analysis continues to rise alongside an ongoing shortage of radiology specialists, there is a pressing requirement for robust automated segmentation tools that can deliver expert-level performance while remaining computationally feasible for integration into routine diagnostic workflows4,21.

This study aims to develop a novel Deep Learning (DL) model that effectively balances segmentation accuracy with computational efficiency in automated LS-MRI analysis. The overarching goal is to construct a Multi-Scale Attention (MSA) capable of accurately delineating vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs while operating within the computational constraints required for clinical deployment.

Related work

Automated segmentation of LSS in MRI remains a challenging task due to high anatomical variability, the presence of imaging objects, and morphological changes associated with pathological environments. In response to these complexities, recent years have seen the emergence of increasingly sophisticated DL-based solutions. One notable improvement is ATM-Net29, which integrates anatomy-aware and text-guided MSA to align anatomical priors with visual features, enabling fine-grained segmentation of vertebrae and intervertebral discs. Similarly, the Attention LinkNet-152 model proposed by30 refines the traditional encoder-decoder model by incorporating deep residual encoding and attention-based skip connections, resulting in enhanced structural delineation across spinal regions31. adopted a diffusion-based semantic segmentation method, leveraging probabilistic refinement to achieve reliable differentiation of anatomical structures in patients experiencing lower back pain. In another hybrid model, SymTC32 combined parallel Transformer and CNN pathways to integrate global contextual cues with fine-grained local features, resulting in precise instance-level segmentation. Complementing these approaches33, introduced HCA-Net, a hierarchical context-aware attention network tailored for intervertebral disc labelling, while34 focused on automated quantification of the dural sac cross-sectional area using DL, presenting a clinically valuable tool for assessing spinal stenosis.

In parallel with advances in task-specific segmentation, substantial research has focused on enhancing network design to improve FS and spatial decisions in Medical Image Segmentation (MIS). MSA has emerged as a key approach in this progression35. projected a Multi-Scale Feature Pyramid Fusion Network (MSFPFN) adapted explicitly for MIS, successfully integrating global anatomical coherence with fine-grained structural detail through the use of stacked pyramid features and AAM. Building on this foundation, MCBTNet36 introduced a hybrid model that combines multiple convolutional backbones with bi-level Transformer-based routing attention, significantly enhancing the model’s ability to capture long-range spatial dependencies. Another noteworthy contribution, R2U++37, extended the U-Net architecture into a multiscale recurrent residual network. By incorporating dense skip connections and deep supervision, R2U + + proved robust contextual encoding even under noisy or partially available data—a vital feature for clinical MRI scenarios.

Residual Learning (RL) has significantly advanced deep segmentation by helping the retention and propagation of low-level spatial data across hierarchical layers, thereby improving model depth and trainability38. Invented a nested residual attention UNet + + architecture, which integrates channel-wise and spatial AM within residual pathways to enhance localization precision and mitigate boundary ambiguity. Extending this line of innovation39, introduced a dual-path synergy network that combines CNN with spatial self-attention Transformers, enabling synergistic interactions between local feature encoding and global contextual modeling. In a related effort40, incorporated visual Transformer modules into the U-Net, resulting in a hybrid segmentation model that improves generalization across multiple organ systems while maintaining computational efficiency.

In addition to residual and MSA, AAM has emerged as a powerful tool for enhancing segmentation accuracy by dynamically modulating FS and mitigating background interference41. introduced the Swin Transformer into the UNETR, enabling hierarchical, window-based attention to capture global contextual dependencies in brain tumor segmentation from MRI scans. This method presented a favorable balance between contextual awareness and computational efficiency. In chest imaging42, employed a Convolutional Block Attention Module (CBAM) within a U-Net, achieving notable improvements in delineating lung boundaries. Building on these methods, DoubleU-NetPlus43 is an enhanced network that integrates dual encoder-decoder pathways with attention-based feature fusion and residual refinement, facilitating robust multi-scale learning across varied imaging modalities. Complementing this direction, Saeed et al. introduced 3D MFA, a multi-feature attention model for spine segmentation that augments MobileNetv3 with a Reverse Convolution Block Attention Module and Feature Pyramid Pooling. Three view-specific encoder networks trained independently on axial, coronal, and sagittal image planes generate concatenated 3D feature maps for final decoding, achieving Dice scores of 96.52% and 94.64% on the VerSe 2020 and VerSe 2019, at notably reduced computational cost via multi-stage network pruning.

FPNs, initially developed for object detection, have increasingly been adapted and improved for MIS because they can aggregate hierarchical features across multiple spatial scales. Recent developments have produced several notable FPN variants tailored to specific medical domains. The SN-FPN44 employs nested feature hierarchies and self-attention mechanisms to support segmentation of complex tissue structures in digital pathology. In neuroimaging, enhanced FPN backbones have improved brain tumour segmentation by optimising the integration of top-down and bottom-up feature streams27. The FDUM-Net45 integrates FPN and U-Net to improve MIS precision in skin lesion analysis. Building on this direction, ISFPN46 combines FPN with Swin Transformers for ischemic stroke detection in brain CT images, achieving strong performance across lesion localisation and classification tasks. In the context of spine-specific imaging, Saeed et al. proposed an automated multi-scale feature fusion network for CT-based spine fracture segmentation, incorporating feature fusion modules, ASPP, and RCBAM for multi-scale spatial extraction and channel-wise refinement, establishing that hierarchical FPN-style feature aggregation yields reliable boundary localisation in the presence of anatomical variability.

A parallel body of work has addressed CT-based and multimodal spine segmentation, offering complementary insights into feature extraction strategies relevant to spinal image analysis. Saeed et al. proposed MSFF, a multi-scale feature fusion model for MRI-based spine fracture segmentation combining ASPP, RCBAM, and a Residual Border Refinement Attention Block to generate discriminative multi-scale representations with sharpened boundary predictions. The same research group extended this framework to volumetric CT segmentation through 3D MRU-Net, which applies MobileNetv2 with residual blocks independently across axial, coronal, and sagittal views and concatenates the resulting feature maps for decoder-based segmentation, achieving competitive Dice scores on the VerSe datasets with reduced parameter overhead. For multi-task spinal analysis, Saeed et al. proposed a two-stage CT pipeline that combines CHASPPRAU-Net for spine segmentation with a 3D MRU-Net stage for vertebral labelling, validating the utility of cascaded architectures in integrated segmentation and recognition. Addressing multimodal clinical deployment, Saeed et al. presented a lumbar spine segmentation pipeline evaluated on both MRI and CT datasets, combining region detection, centroid annotation via SpatialConfigurationNet 3D, and HyperDenseNet-based segmentation further refined through gradient-sensitivity pruning, achieving Dice, IoU, and Hausdorff scores of 95.42%, 95.68%, and 4.51 mm. Collectively, these works highlight a persistent challenge across imaging modalities—capturing directional anatomical context and multi-scale semantic coherence without disproportionate computational cost—a gap the current study addresses through directionally dilated convolutions and cross-level adaptive gating within an MRI-specific FPN backbone.

Taken together, these works highlight a typical trajectory in the field: the fusion of multiscale selections, attention-driven refinement, and reinforcement learning, frequently within a pyramid-structured backbone. However, while many existing models address specific segmentation challenges, few simultaneously optimise for boundary-level precision and cross-scale semantic coherence, particularly in the anatomically variable LS. The present study seeks to bridge this gap by proposing an MRAS-FPN, which integrates Directionally Dilated Convolutions, spatial-channel dual attention, and cross-level gating within an enhanced FPN backbone explicitly tailored for LS-MRI segmentation.

Methodology

Overview of the proposed model

The proposed segmentation network, “MRAS-FPN”, is designed to address the dual challenge of achieving high anatomical precision and computational efficiency in lumbar spine MRI segmentation. The model builds on the initial FPN structure and augments it with two targeted attention-enhancement modules: the Multiscale Residual Attention Block (MRAB) and the Adaptive Spatial-Channel Attention Block (ASCAB). These components are purposefully integrated to enhance multiscale contextual feature learning, improve inter-level semantic consistency, and reduce data loss across the network hierarchy.

The overall framework of MRAS-FPN follows an encoder-decoder method wherein the encoder comprises convolutional layers as to that FE increases semantic abstraction and decreases spatial resolution. These features are successively aggregated and upsampled using a top-down decoder path composed of feature maps to . The lateral connections between the encoder and decoder ensure the preservation of high-resolution localization data. At each stage of this pathway, the proposed MRAB modules are interposed to capture residual contextual data across multiple receptive fields and reinforce spatial and channel-wise attentional focus. The MRAB fuses low- and high-level features using a Directionally Dilated Convolution Module (DDCM), followed by a sequence of spatial and channel attention refinements and a Gated Fusion Method (GFM) to generate attention-enhanced feature maps.

Following MRAB improvement, each decoded feature level is further modulated by ASCAB, which applies learnable attention across the spatial and channel axes in an adaptive, resource-efficient manner. This second stage of refinement ensures that only the most semantically relevant data is preserved for final segmentation prediction.

The fused outputs are progressively upsampled and concatenated, culminating in a final prediction head that generates the binary segmentation map delineating vertebral structures and intervertebral discs. This model is precisely tailored to address the anatomical complexity of the lumbar spine, characterised by similar textures and blurred boundaries, by emphasizing cross-scale interactions and hierarchical attention control. The FPN serves as the network backbone of the proposed MRAS-FPN. The choice of FPN is motivated by its capacity to construct semantically rich feature hierarchies at multiple spatial resolutions, which is critical for segmenting anatomically complex structures such as the lumbar spine. FPN achieves this goal by combining a bottom-up FEA pathway with a top-down feature fusion pathway, both of which are improved by lateral connections. While FPNs have verified considerable success across several MIS tasks, the vanilla FPN exhibits notable problems when applied to anatomically complex scenarios, such as lumbar spine MRI. One primary limitation lies in the additive fusion used in the top-down pathway, which lacks the receptive field to capture the complex spatial dependencies and inter-channel relationships required to differentiate anatomically similar vertebral and disc structures. Also, the inherently static convolutional operations in the FPN backbone limit the network’s ability to adaptively emphasise diagnostically relevant anatomical features while suppressing background noise and irrelevant tissue regions. Also, the absence of residual connections in the fusion stages renders the architecture more vulnerable to gradient degradation and semantic loss, particularly in deeper layers of the network.

To overcome these constraints, this study introduces two key improvements to the conventional FPN structure. The first is the Multiscale Residual Attention Block (MRAB), which is integrated at each pyramid stage to help context-sensitive feature learning. MRAB incorporates directionally dilated convolutions, residual summation paths, and a dual-AM that recalibrates spatial and channel-level FS. The second improvement is the ASCAB, applied after pyramid decoding to refine the fused features using group-wise normalization and MSA. Together, these modules transform the FPN backbone into a deeper, more anatomically aware, and dynamically adaptive model tailored to the unique challenges of LSS.

DDCM

The DDCM serves as the introductory component (Fig. 3) of the MRAB, facilitating the extraction of spatial context from local and long-range anatomical orientations. The design rationale behind DDCM stems from the observation that vertebral bodies and intervertebral discs in lumbar spine MRI display prominent axial and sagittal elongation patterns. Traditional isotropic convolutional kernels lack sufficient directional sensitivity to capture such anatomical anisotropy, especially in the presence of degenerated or morphologically irregular structures. DDCM addresses this challenge by incorporating two orthogonal convolutional branches, each designed to encode spatial context with and without dilation along vertical and horizontal axes.

By learning to prioritize feature channels in a data-driven, anatomically adaptive manner, the CAB significantly enhances the FS of the MRAB. When used in conjunction with the SAB, it enables the network to execute fine-grained, 2-D attention calibration—spatially and across channels—generating richer, more focused feature maps for downstream segmentation.

Experimental setup

Ethics statement

This study was conducted in full compliance with institutional and national ethical standards governing research involving human subjects. This study utilized the publicly available MRSpineSeg Challenge dataset for computational analysis. All procedures performed in this study were tested in accordance with relevant research guidelines and regulations. Since the dataset consisted of anonymized retrospective medical images and did not involve direct human participation, patient recruitment, or clinical intervention, ethical approval and informed consent were not required.

Datasets

This study uses the MRSpineSeg dataset, which comprises T2-weighted volumetric lumbar spine MRI scans from 215 subjects. The data were collected from a local hospital and include healthy individuals and patients with a range of spinal pathologies, ensuring anatomical diversity and clinical significance. Each MRI volume is accompanied by expert-corrected manual segmentations of lumbar vertebrae and intervertebral discs, generated using ITK-SNAP. Up to 19 spinal structures are annotated per subject, with labels covering individual vertebrae, discs, and backgrounds across 20 classes.

The in-plane pixel spacing ranges from 0.30 to 0.59 mm, and slice thicknesses range from 4.40 to 5.50 mm, reflecting the anisotropic resolution characteristic of clinical T2-weighted sagittal acquisitions. Image dimensions range from 512 × 512 to 1024 × 1024 pixels, with 12–18 slices per volume. MRAS-FPN processes each sagittal slice independently as a 2D input, preserving full in-plane spatial fidelity while avoiding the propagation of inter-slice partial volume artifacts across network layers. This per-slice design is appropriate for MRI sequences acquired with thick sagittal slices, where through-plane resolution is insufficient to support reliable volumetric feature aggregation. Interslice spatial context is not explicitly modeled, which represents a constraint of the current implementation relative to isotropic 3D acquisition protocols. To ensure robust evaluation, this study employs the dataset’s 5-fold cross-validation protocol, with each fold comprising 43 subjects. Data augmentation during training includes random rotations, elastic deformations, and contrast variations to improve model generalisation.

For experimental purposes, the dataset was randomly divided into training (70%), validation (15%), and test (15%) splits, ensuring no subject overlap. All images were normalized using z-score normalization, and standard augmentations, including flipping, affine distortion, and intensity jitter, were applied during training to enhance model generalizability.

Training configuration

The proposed MRAS-FPN was implemented using PyTorch 2.0 and trained on a high-performance workstation equipped with an NVIDIA RTX 3090 GPU (24 GB VRAM). The training was conducted in mixed-precision mode using NVIDIA Apex AMP to optimize memory usage and computational speed. All experiments were performed under deterministic conditions, with fixed random seeds to ensure reproducibility.

Each sagittal MRI slice was preprocessed (Fig. 8) using z-score normalisation to standardise intensity distributions across patients and imaging protocols. No spatial resizing was applied, and original voxel dimensions were preserved to maintain anatomical fidelity. To improve robustness to variability in spinal morphology and MRI acquisition conditions, a set of data augmentations was applied during training. These included random horizontal flipping, rotation within ± 15°, intensity jittering, elastic deformation, and CLAHE (Contrast-Limited Adaptive Histogram Equalization).

Preprocessing steps.

A cosine annealing schedule with warm restarts was applied every 20 epochs to dynamically modulate the learning rate. The model was trained for 150 epochs, with early stopping employed if validation loss failed to improve for 15 consecutive epochs. A gradient clipping threshold of 5.0 was enforced to stabilize backpropagation using the deeper attention-enhanced modules. Directionally dilated convolution analysis

The DDCM variant analysis in Table 10 validates the proposed directional dilated convolution method over conventional convolutional approaches for capturing anatomical context in LSS. The 15% computational overhead is well compensated by substantial performance gains, particularly given that the directional expanded method effectively models the anisotropic nature of spinal anatomy, where vertebral bodies exhibit strong vertical orientation and intervertebral discs show horizontal elongation patterns, making this specialised convolutional design particularly suited for lumbar spine MRI analysis.

Loss function component analysis

The loss function component analysis (Table 11) proves the critical importance of composite loss design for achieving optimal LSS performance, with each component contributing specific advantages to distinct segmentation quality features. Individual loss functions show distinct features, with Dice loss only achieving 85.2% DSC and substantial volumetric overlap but limited boundary precision (HD95: 3.67 mm, Boundary Quality: 6.8), while BCE loss only reaches 84.1% DSC with inferior boundary metrics (HD95: 3.89 mm, Boundary Quality: 6.2), indicating that neither method alone is sufficient for high-quality MIS. The combination of Dice and BCE losses with equal weighting (λ₁ = λ₂ = 1.0) significantly improves performance to 86.9% DSC and 77.1% mIoU while enhancing boundary quality to 7.4, signifying the complementary nature of region-based and pixel-wise supervision. The progressive addition of boundary-aware loss shows systematic improvements, with λ₃=0.1 achieving 87.3% DSC and notably improved boundary metrics (HD95: 3.32 mm, Boundary Quality: 8.1), while the optimal configuration with λ₃ = 0.2 reaches the highest performance of 87.8% DSC, 78.2% mIoU, and superior boundary precision (HD95: 3.18 mm, Boundary Quality: 8.6). However, increasing the boundary loss weight to λ₃ = 0.5 results in slight performance degradation (87.1% DSC, HD95: 3.28 mm), signifying that excessive emphasis on boundary preservation can compromise overall segmentation quality. The optimal weighting method, with λ₁ = λ₂ = 1.0 and λ₃ = 0.2, effectively balances volumetric accuracy, pixel-level classification, and boundary sharpness, which is particularly vital for LSS, where precise delineation of vertebral endplates and disc boundaries is vital for clinical diagnosis and treatment planning. This validates the composite loss design as a key contributor to MRAS-FPN’s superior performance.

Discussion

Technical contributions and positioning within current literature

The proposed MRAS-FPN marks a substantive development in automated LSS, presents targeted solutions to limitations identified in the existing literature, and introduces architectural innovations that jointly improve segmentation accuracy and computational efficiency. Rooted in the principles of FPN, MRAS-FPN extends this foundation by incorporating specialised components tailored to the anatomical and imaging complexities of the LS. In contrast to generalized FPN variants such as SN-FPN44 and ISFPN46, which are optimized for broader MIS, MRAS-FPN integrates directionally dilated convolutions that explicitly account for the anisotropic morphology of spinal structures. This targeted design addresses a critical limitation in prior MSAs, which predominantly relied on isotropic convolutional kernels and thus failed to differentiate between the vertical alignment of vertebral bodies and the horizontal elongation of intervertebral discs.

Another distinguishing feature of the proposed method is the integration of dual spatial-channel AM into the MRAB and ASCAB. These components represent a refined evolution beyond standard AM, incorporating adaptive MSA that leverages group-wise normalization and residual learning to capture anatomical variability. While architectures such as Attention LinkNet-15230 and HCA-Net33 use attention layers, they lack the dynamic, structurally aware recalibration achieved by MRAS-FPN. Compared to CBAM-based developments within U-Net42, our method achieves superior quantitative performance, attaining a DSC of 87.8%, which falls within the typically reported range of 84–85%. Moreover, MRAS-FPN validates improved boundary delineation, achieving an HD95 of 3.18 mm, compared to over 4 mm reported in prior AM.

Comparative analysis with state-of-the-art (SOTA) methods

The experimental results presented in Section “Comparison with recent state-of-the-art methods” (Table 16) position MRAS-FPN as the strongest-performing method among all models evaluated on the MRSpineSeg dataset. Against the three methods sharing the same dataset and evaluation protocol, MRAS-FPN achieves a DSC of 87.8%, surpassing ATM-Net29 by 6.08% points, nnU-NetV248 by 11.37% points, and U-BiSeNet47 by 21.77% points. The corresponding mIoU improvements of 5.95%, 10.70%, and 20.53% over these same methods confirm that the performance advantage is consistent across both overlap-based metrics. These gains are particularly notable given that recent spine-specific models such as U-BiSeNet47 and ATM-Net29 already incorporate task-tailored architectural designs — multi-scale attention with BiSeNet fusion in the former and anatomy-aware text-guided multi-modal fusion in the latter. The fact that MRAS-FPN outperforms both with a purely vision-based convolutional attention architecture demonstrates that directionally dilated feature extraction and cross-level gating are highly effective design choices for this segmentation task without requiring the additional complexity of language alignment modules.

Training requires 22.1 h, representing a 23% reduction relative to 3D U-Net (28.7 h), while per-slice inference time of 18.3 ± 1.5 ms is 42.5% faster than 3D U-Net (31.8 ± 2.4 ms/slice) and only marginally slower than DeepLab v3+ (14.7 ± 1.1 ms/slice) — a difference of 3.6 ms per slice that is inconsequential in a clinical workflow context. Full-volume inference for 12 to 18 slices completes in approximately 220 to 330 ms, well within acceptable latency for offline diagnostic support. In contrast, Transformer-based models such as Swin UNETR41 and the hybrid visual Transformer U-Net40 require substantially greater memory and extended training durations, limiting their practical deployment in resource-constrained radiology environments. ATM-Net29, while achieving 81.72% DSC on MRSpineSeg, introduces the overhead of a natural language alignment pipeline; MRAS-FPN surpasses it by 6.08% DSC while operating entirely within the vision domain, offering a more computationally transparent and deployment-ready architecture.

Clinical implications and practical impact

The superior performance of MRAS-FPN translates into tangible clinical benefits, addressing key diagnostic and operational challenges in LSS. Notably, the enhanced boundary accuracy—reflected in an HD95 of 3.18 mm, compared to over 4 mm reported in existing methods—is particularly valuable in surgical planning contexts, where accurate delineation of vertebral endplates and intervertebral disc boundaries can directly affect the practicality and results of the method. This degree of precision closely aligns with the inter-observer variability observed in expert manual segmentations, highlighting the potential of MRAS-FPN as a dependable clinical decision-support tool. Moreover, the model validates consistent segmentation performance across the full range of vertebral levels (T9–S1), with DSC exceeding 90% in lumbar regions. This consistency addresses a common limitation in current methods, which frequently exhibit reduced accuracy in anatomically complex regions, such as the thoracolumbar junction.

Equally significant is the method’s robustness in the presence of pathological variability. Unlike many existing segmentation networks, which are primarily evaluated on relatively normal anatomy, MRAS-FPN has been comprehensively validated on the MRSpineSeg dataset, which encompasses a broad spectrum of degenerative spine conditions. The model’s ability to maintain performance under such variability proves its practical relevance for deployment in real-world clinical settings, where anatomical deviations are standard. Notably, the reduced Standard Deviation (SD) across all key metrics (± 1.3% DSC compared to ± 2.5% in baseline methods) reflects greater reliability and predictability—critical traits for fostering clinical trust. This performance stability contrasts with probabilistic methods, such as diffusion-based segmentation (Monzon et al., 2024), which frequently exhibit greater variability due to their inherent stochastic refinement.

Methodological innovations and generalizability

The architectural innovations embedded within MRAS-FPN contribute several broadly applicable design principles that extend beyond the specific domain of LSS. The DDCM introduces a structured method for modeling anisotropic anatomical structures and validates potential utility for segmenting other elongated or directionally biased anatomical entities, such as vascular pathways, neural tracts, or cardiac chambers. Unlike standard dilated convolution methods—such as those used in nested residual attention UNet++ (Li et al., 2022)—DDCM incorporates directional sensitivity tailored to the morphological features of medical anatomy, representing a meaningful advance in the spatial modelling capabilities of convolutional layers.

Also, the CFG mechanism recommends a refined alternative to the additive or concatenative fusion methods commonly employed in conventional FPN. By enabling learned, data-adaptive integration of multi-scale features, CFG enhances representational richness and contextual coherence, with probable applicability to a range of MIS tasks involving complex anatomical hierarchies.

The ASCAB further addresses critical limitations of earlier attention modules by incorporating group normalization and residual summation, thereby improving training stability and cross-sample generalization. This design surpasses commonly used attention strategies, such as SE-Net and CBAM, by introducing dynamic, content-aware modulation that flexibly adapts to anatomical variability. The use of these components is verified by complete ablation studies that not only validate their contributions within the MRAS-FPN but also provide generalizable insights into the design of robust AM for MIS. These findings contribute to a broader understanding of network-level innovations in DL for healthcare applications.

Limitations and future research directions

Despite the significant improvements achieved by MRAS-FPN, several limitations remain that should be addressed in future work. The current evaluation is restricted to T2-weighted sagittal MRI sequences, whereas clinical protocols frequently include multiple imaging modalities such as T1-weighted and STIR sequences, which provide complementary anatomical information. Future research should consider extending MRAS-FPN to support multimodal MRI fusion and evaluate architectures such as ATM-Net29 that integrate multiple MRI sequences via cross-modal alignment. The model processes each sagittal slice independently as a 2D input, preserving in-plane spatial fidelity but not modeling inter-slice spatial context. Extension to isotropic high-resolution 3D acquisition protocols, where through-plane resolution is comparable to in-plane resolution, represents a natural next step that would enable full volumetric feature aggregation across the spinal axis.

The model’s performance at the thoracolumbar junction, particularly at T9, reflects a dataset-level constraint arising from partial field-of-view coverage and the limited number of complete training examples at this level. Achieving diagnostically reliable segmentation at T9–T11 in clinical deployment would require dedicated acquisition protocols ensuring full thoracolumbar coverage and supplementary annotated data from this anatomical region. The composite loss function’s boundary weight λ₃ = 0.2 was optimized through systematic ablation within the T9–S1 lumbar range covered by MRSpineSeg; its sensitivity across other spinal regions, such as the cervical or thoracic spine, has not been evaluated and represents a parameter that would require reassessment when extending the model to broader anatomical coverage.

The model’s robustness under complex pathological conditions, including severe spondylolisthesis, post-operative anatomy, and advanced spinal stenosis, also requires validation using dedicated datasets that capture this clinical variability, as MRSpineSeg predominantly reflects a degenerative disease spectrum and excludes extreme post-surgical cases. Further efficiency improvements are achievable through knowledge distillation or structured pruning, enabling faster inference on more constrained hardware without significant performance degradation. Incorporating federated learning could support collaborative model development across institutions without compromising patient data privacy, addressing the scarcity of large annotated spine MRI datasets. Adapting MRAS-FPN to other spinal regions or related tasks, such as spinal cord segmentation, nerve root delineation, or cervical spine analysis, would further establish the generalizability of the proposed directional convolution and adaptive attention design principles.

Conclusion

This study introduces MRAS-FPN, a DL designed to address the core challenge of achieving high segmentation accuracy while minimising computational cost in automated LS-MRI analysis. Evaluated on the MRSpineSeg dataset comprising 215 T2-weighted lumbar spine MRI volumes, MRAS-FPN achieves strong results, recording a DSC of 87.8 ± 1.3% and a mIoU of 78.2 ± 2.1%. These results echo the 4.4% and 4.8% improvements over the best-performing baseline (3D U-Net). Notably, boundary accuracy is significantly improved, with decreases of 19.9% in HD95 and 25.0% in ASSD—metrics that are particularly relevant for clinical tasks requiring precise anatomical delineation. The model’s architectural components—MRAB with directionally dilated convolutions and ASCAB —are designed to capture the anisotropic features of spinal structures while maintaining computational efficiency. Compared to the 3D U-Net, MRAS-FPN reduces training time by 23% and memory usage by 15% while delivering higher segmentation accuracy, making it suitable for clinical integration. Ablation studies confirm the importance of each module, with MRAB contributing a 3.9% increase in DSC and ASCAB adding a further 2.1%. MRAS-FPN maintains consistent performance across all vertebral levels (T9–S1) and intervertebral discs, supported by stable training dynamics and minimal signs of overfitting. Its ability to align technical performance with practical demands positions it as a strong candidate for integration into diagnostic workflows. This model recommends a clinically viable automated spine analysis tool and establishes a novel performance benchmark, reinforcing the role of intelligent automation in advancing radiological practice.
