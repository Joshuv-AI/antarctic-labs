The Distinction Dynamics Equation

3.1
Motivation: Dissipation at the Fixed Point

The Jacobian of the fixed-point map
F_{l}(h)=h_{0}(\lambda_{l})\exp(-1-\mathcal{T}_{l}(h))

has entries:

\frac{\partial F_{l}}{\partial h(\lambda_{m})}=-h^{*}(\lambda_{l})\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{T}_{l}}{\partial h(\lambda_{m})}\biggr|_{h^{*}}.

(8)

Proposition 1

(Jacobian divergence at the fixed point)
.

For the mode-separable Gaussian mutual-information (Gaussian MI) source

\mathcal{T}_{l}[h]=\frac{\mu_{2}w_{l}}{2(\sigma^{2}+h(\lambda_{l}))}

(9)

with
\mu_{2},w_{l},\sigma^{2}>0
, the divergence of
F
at
h^{*}
is:

\mathrm{tr}(DF)|_{h^{*}}=-\sum_{l}h^{*}(\lambda_{l})\,\frac{\partial\mathcal{T}_{l}}{\partial h(\lambda_{l})}\biggr|_{h^{*}}=\sum_{l}\frac{\mu_{2}w_{l}\,h^{*}(\lambda_{l})}{2(\sigma^{2}+h^{*}(\lambda_{l}))^{2}}>0.

(10)

The MaxCal fixed-point flow is strictly volume-expanding in
\mathcal{K}_{\mathrm{graph}}

near the fixed point and dissipative in the sense that
|\det(DF)|<1

(contraction ratio
\rho<1
, consistent with convergence).

Proof.

Direct substitution of
\partial\mathcal{T}_{l}/\partial h(\lambda_{l})=-\mu_{2}w_{l}/(2(\sigma^{2}+h(\lambda_{l}))^{2})<0
into (
8
)
and summing over
l
.
∎

3.2
Definition

Definition 1

(Spectral distinction field)
.

A
spectral distinction field
on a graph
G
is a time-dependent vertex
signal
\psi_{t}\in\mathbb{R}^{|V|}
whose spectral coefficients

c_{l}(t)=\phi_{l}^{\top}\psi_{t}
are square-integrable over
[0,T]
.
The associated coefficient trajectory
\mathbf{c}(t)=(c_{l}(t))\in\mathbb{R}^{N}
is the
spectral representation
of the distinction field
— in digital-twin terms, the spectral state of the evolving scene as
represented by the twin.

Definition 2

(Distinction dynamics equation)
.

Given a spectral distinction field
\psi_{t}
and a physical dynamics operator

\mathcal{G}
, the
distinction dynamics equation
is the evolution law:

\frac{d\mathbf{c}}{dt}=\mathcal{G}[\mathbf{c},\,h_{t}],

(13)

where
h_{t}
is the current spectral kernel. We refer to (
13
) as
the distinction dynamics equation; informally, it is the
scene dynamics
equation
. The distinction dynamics equation is

coupled
to the field equation (
1
) when
\mathcal{T}_{l}

depends on
\mathbf{c}
(e.g., via
w_{l}=|c_{l}|^{2}
).

Definition 3

(Representation leakage)
.

Representation leakage
(synonymously,
representational leakage
)
is any apparent gain, loss, or redistribution of scene structure in the twin
that is not attributable to
(i)
physical scene dynamics under the
distinction dynamics equation (
13
),
(ii)
explicit
incoming observations, or
(iii)
controlled compression or erasure
under the stated update law and spectral/topology budgets.

Concretely, leakage manifests as a nonzero conservation residual: the
per-mode deficit
D_{m}=-\Delta^{\prime}
(Proposition
2
)
quantifies the rate at which each spectral mode can accumulate unpaid-for
structural change per fixed-point step. In Algorithm
1
,
the diagnostic
D_{t}
aggregates these per-mode residuals into a scalar
pre-transmission check. Establishing quantitative thresholds that
distinguish leakage from ordinary model mismatch requires controlled
injection experiments (sensor drift, registration error, calibration shift)
that are posed as Open Problem Q14 but not resolved here.

Remark 5

(Static and dynamic regimes)
.

For a geologically static planetary surface,
\mathcal{G}\approx 0
and
the distinction dynamics equation is trivial:
\mathbf{c}(t)\equiv\Phi^{\top}V
for
fixed vertex positions
V
. Dynamics arise from (i) sensor motion
(the rover reveals new portions of
V
), (ii) environment change
(rockfall, dust deposition), and (iii) compression artifacts (the
reconstructed
\hat{V}_{t}=\Phi_{k}\Phi_{k}^{\top}V
changes as
k
varies).
Representational leakage can also masquerade as dynamics: sensor motion
aliasing, registration mismatch between frames, compression-induced topology
loss below the conservation budget, or changing graph construction (e.g.
k
-NN reties) can all induce apparent scene change without true physical
evolution.

The canonical distinction dynamics equation for 3D geometry.

When the graph topology is fixed across a temporal sequence of 3D scans
(e.g., a stable terrain mesh observed from different rover positions), the
eigenbasis
\Phi
is shared across frames. The vertex positions
V_{t}

change as the rover accumulates observations, and the distinction dynamics equation reduces to:

\frac{d}{dt}\bigl(\Phi^{\top}V_{t}\bigr)=\Phi^{\top}\frac{dV_{t}}{dt}.

(14)

This is a linear, decoupled ODE for each spectral coefficient
c_{l}(t)
:

\dot{c}_{l}=\phi_{l}^{\top}\dot{V}_{t}
. The kernel field equation (
1
)
determines
h_{t}^{*}
given
\mathbf{c}(t)
; the distinction dynamics equation (
14
)
determines how
\mathbf{c}(t)
evolves given the physical scene.

Then:

(ii)

(i)
The first

independent cycles: they span the image of the Fiedler-mode cluster and
encode
\beta_{1}
topological handles of the surface Hodge complex.
Retaining
k\geq\beta_{0}+\beta_{1}

(ii)
Dropping a topologically obligate mode removes at least one

The missing mode cannot be reconstructed from the retained subspace (the

(iii)

(15)

Remark 7

For large meshes (
