Eigenbounds of symmetric positive definite tensors
Snigdhashree Nayak, Hemant Sharma, Nachiketa Mishra

Abstract
This article introduces an algebraic framework for establishing eigenvalue bounds for symmetric positive definite tensors by leveraging intrinsic invariants—specifically, the trace and determinant (resultant). We derive a hierarchy of inequalities via the Arithmetic Mean-Geometric Mean (AM-GM) inequality that yields progressively tighter upper and lower bounds for the tensor’s spectral radius and smallest eigenvalue. A comprehensive comparative analysis demonstrates that our invariant-based approach significantly outperforms classical coordinate-dependent methods, such as the Gershgorin circle theorem. We explicitly show that our bounds remain robust and informative in scenarios where Gershgorin bounds fail, particularly for tensors with negative off-diagonal entries (where algebraic cancellations occur) and higher-order tensors (where combinatorial explosion leads to loose estimates). Furthermore, we validate the practical utility of these bounds by applying them to certify the positive definiteness of Lyapunov functions for the stability analysis of nonlinear autonomous systems.

Eigenvalue bounds are essential for gaining insights into the properties and behavior of tensors in a wide range of applications. They provide a valuable tool for assessing stability, convergence, and performance in various mathematical, scientific, and engineering contexts. The positive definiteness of a homogeneous polynomial form with an even degree plays a crucial role in the examination of stability in nonlinear autonomous systems using Lyapunov’s direct method in the field of automatic control. By employing Lyapunov’s method, the analysis of stability can be simplified by establishing the existence of a positive definite function, whose time derivative along the system’s trajectories is consistently negative. Specifically, in the context of the system,

Further, in higher-order diffusion tensor imaging

Calculating eigenvalues of tensors with higher orders is an arduous task. In matrix theory, we have many eigenvalue bounds calculated using different methods, like norms, entries of matrices, etc. In this endeavor, we articulate bounds on the eigenvalues of a tensor by leveraging its intrinsic characteristics, drawing upon both the trace and determinant as instrumental elements in our analysis.

3
Bounds for the eigenvalues

Bounding the eigenvalues of tensors presents a greater challenge compared to matrices, owing to their higher dimensionality and non-linear nature. The derivation of eigenvalue bounds for tensors can be intricate, primarily due to the complex behavior exhibited by these mathematical entities.

The AM(Arithmetic Mean)-GM(Geometric Mean) inequality, a simple yet potent mathematical tool, has found extensive use in proving inequalities across various branches of mathematics and beyond. It provides a versatile means to compare means and set boundaries for expressions involving non-negative real numbers. Its broad utility renders it a valuable asset in mathematical analysis, optimization, and problem-solving endeavors.

Hence, commencing with the most elementary bounds at our disposal, we turn to the AM-GM inequalities to establish our initial bounds.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[n,m]}
be a symmetric tensor of order
m
and dimension
n
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0,
where
d=n(m-1)^{(n-1)}
and
let
1\leq k\leq d-1,
then

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\leq(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})-(d-k)\left[\left(\frac{k}{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}\right)^{k}\det(\mathcal{A})\right]^{1/(d-k)},

(1)

(\lambda_{d-k+1}\cdots\lambda_{d})\geq\bigg(\dfrac{d-k}{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}\bigg)^{d-k}\det(\mathcal{A}).

(2)

Proof.

In order to prove inequality (
1
), we start with

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{1/(d-k)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{k+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}}{d-k},

\displaystyle\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}\lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d}}{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}}\bigg)^{1/(d-k)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})}{d-k},

\displaystyle\bigg(\dfrac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}}\bigg)^{1/(d-k)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})}{d-k}.

Again,

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k})^{1/k}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}}{k}

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}}{k}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle\dfrac{1}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\dfrac{k}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}}\bigg)^{k}.

Also, we have

\dfrac{k}{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}}\geq\dfrac{k}{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}.

Since
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0,
and from Theorem
2.5
(d), we have that the sum of all the eigenvalues of
\mathcal{A}
is

(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A}).

The denominator on the right-hand side of the inequality is greater than the denominator on the left-hand side.

Therefore, we get

\bigg[\bigg(\dfrac{k}{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}\bigg)^{k}\det(\mathcal{A})\bigg]^{1/(d-k)}\leq\dfrac{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})-(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k})}{d-k},

which implies

\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\leq(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})-(d-k)\left[\left(\frac{k}{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}\right)^{k}\det(\mathcal{A})\right]^{1/(d-k)}.

For proving (
2
), we have

\displaystyle(\lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{1/(d-k)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{k+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}}{d-k},

\displaystyle\bigg(\dfrac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}}\bigg)^{1/d-k}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{k+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}}{d-k}

\displaystyle\dfrac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\bigg(\dfrac{\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{k+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}}{d-k}\bigg)^{d-k},

\displaystyle\dfrac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{\lambda_{1}\lambda_{2}\cdots\lambda_{k}}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{\lambda_{d-k+1}\lambda_{d-k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d}}\leq\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{d-k}\bigg)^{d-k},

\displaystyle\lambda_{d-k+1}\lambda_{d-k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d}

\displaystyle\geq\bigg(\dfrac{d-k}{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}\bigg)^{d-k}\det(\mathcal{A}).

∎

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[n,m]}
be a symmetric tensor of order
m
and dimension
n
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0,
where
d=n(m-1)^{(n-1)}
and
let
1\leq k\leq d,
then

(\lambda_{d-k+1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{1/k}\leq(\det(\mathcal{A}))^{1/d}\leq(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k})^{1/k}\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}}{k},

(3)

(\lambda_{d-k+1}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{1/k}\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{d-k+1}+\ldots+\lambda_{d}}{k}\leq\dfrac{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}{d}\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}}{k}.

(4)

Proof.
For (
4
), we start proving the inequality below by using the given fact that
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0
,

\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}}{k}\geq\dfrac{\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k+1}}{k+1},

(5)

\dfrac{\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d}}{k}\leq\dfrac{\lambda_{d-k}+\lambda_{d-k+1}+\ldots+\lambda_{d}}{k+1}.

(6)

So, starting with (
5
)

\displaystyle(k+1)(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle-k(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k+1})

\displaystyle=k(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k})+(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\quad\quad-k(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k})-k\lambda_{k+1},

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{1}+\lambda_{2}+\ldots+\lambda_{k})-k\lambda_{k+1}\geq 0.

Similarly (
6
) can also be proved.

\displaystyle(k+1)(\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})-k(\lambda_{d-k}+\lambda_{d-k+1}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})

\displaystyle=k(\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})+(\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})

\displaystyle~~~~~~~~~~~~~~~-k(\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})-k\lambda_{d-k}

\displaystyle=(\lambda_{d-k+1}+\lambda_{d-k+2}+\ldots+\lambda_{d})-k\lambda_{d-k}\leq 0

If we add quantities that are less than every other element then the average will be decreased. Similarly, the average will be increased if we add quantities that are greater than every other element. Hence, now by using (
5
) and (
6
), we can say that the inequality (
4

\geq
GM. For (
3

Theorem 3.4
.

Let

(7)

Dividing by

\displaystyle\frac{\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k+1}+\lambda_{k+2}+\cdots+\lambda_{d})}{d-k}

Using AM-GM inequality on left-hand side, we obtain

\displaystyle\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}(\lambda_{k+1}\lambda_{k+2}\cdots\lambda_{d})^{1/(d-k)}

\displaystyle\leq\dfrac{1}{d-k}\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{k+1}\bigg)^{k+1},

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k})^{(d-k-1)}det(\mathcal{A})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\dfrac{1}{d-k}\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{k+1}\bigg)^{k+1}\bigg]^{(d-k)},

\displaystyle(\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\bigg[\dfrac{1}{det(\mathcal{A})}\bigg[\dfrac{1}{d-k}\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{k+1}\bigg)^{k+1}\bigg]^{(d-k)}\bigg]^{1/(d-k-1)}.

\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{k}\leq\bigg\{\dfrac{1}{\det(\mathcal{A})}\bigg[\dfrac{1}{d-k}\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{(n-1)}tr(\mathcal{A})}{k+1}\bigg)^{k+1}\bigg]^{d-k}\bigg\}^{\dfrac{1}{d-k-1}}.

After rearranging the exponent on both sides we get

.

Proof.

) of the above inequality

Theorem 3.6
.

Let
\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[n,m]}
be a symmetric tensor of order
m
and dimension
n
with
\lambda_{1}\geq\lambda_{2}\geq\cdots\geq\lambda_{d}>0,
where
d=n(m-1)^{(n-1)}
and let
1\leq k\leq d.
Then

\lambda_{1}+\ldots+\lambda_{k}\leq\dfrac{(k+1)^{k+1}}{k^{k}}\dfrac{1}{det(\mathcal{A})}\bigg(\dfrac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{d+1}\bigg)^{d+1}.

(11)

Proof.

Let
0<t<1
. Then, by using AM-GM inequality, we obtain

\displaystyle\left(\frac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{d+1}\right)^{d+1}

\displaystyle=\left(\frac{t\left(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\right)+(1-t)\lambda_{1}+\cdots+(1-t)\lambda_{k}+\lambda_{k+1}+\cdots+\lambda_{d}}{d+1}\right)^{d+1},

\displaystyle\geqslant t\left(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\right)(1-t)^{k}\lambda_{1}\cdots\lambda_{d}~~,

\displaystyle=t(1-t)^{k}\left(\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\right)\operatorname{det}(\mathcal{A}).

Now,

\lambda_{1}+\cdots+\lambda_{k}\leqslant\frac{1}{t(1-t)^{k}}\frac{1}{\operatorname{det}\mathcal{A}}\left(\frac{(m-1)^{n-1}tr(\mathcal{A})}{d+1}\right)^{d+1}.

By employing differentiation to find the minimum value on the RHS, we obtain the result for
t_{0}=\frac{1}{k+1}
,

\frac{1}{t_{0}\left(1-t_{0}\right)^{k}}=\min\left\{\frac{1}{t(1-t)^{k}}\mid 0<t<1\right\}=\frac{(k+1)^{k+1}}{k^{k}}.

∎

All theorems in this section concern positive definite tensors. Since a real

4
Analyzing examples

Connection with stability.

To illustrate how the eigenvalue bounds obtained in Section
3
can be used in Lyapunov
stability analysis, we construct a positive definite even–order tensor and apply Theorem
3.1
to obtain a certified lower bound on its smallest H–eigenvalue. This bound then guarantees the positivity of a tensor–generated Lyapunov function and allows us to verify asymptotic stability of a nonlinear system.

Example 4.1
.

Consider the fourth–order, two–dimensional symmetric tensor

\mathcal{A}\in\mathbb{R}^{[2,4]}
with diagonal entries

a_{1111}=1.1,\qquad a_{2222}=1.0,

and all other entries zero.

Step 1: Constructing a positive definite tensor.
The associated homogeneous polynomial

V(x_{1},x_{2})=1.1\,x_{1}^{4}+x_{2}^{4}

is positive definite since
m=4
is even and all diagonal coefficients are positive.
The eigenvalue equation
\mathcal{A}x^{3}=\lambda x^{[3]}
yields the distinct eigenvalues

\lambda_{1}=1.1,\qquad\lambda_{2}=1.0,

each with multiplicity three.
Thus the true smallest eigenvalue is

\lambda_{\min}^{\text{true}}=1.0.

Step 2: Verifying the lower bound using Theorem
3.1
.

The trace and determinant of
\mathcal{A}
are

\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{A})=2.1,\qquad\det(\mathcal{A})=(1.1)^{3}(1.0)^{3}=1.331.

For
m=4
and
n=2
, we have
(m-1)^{n-1}=3
.
Applying Theorem
3.1
with
k=d-1
, we obtain

\lambda_{\min}\geq\frac{\det(\mathcal{A})}{(m-1)^{n-1}\operatorname{tr}(\mathcal{A})}=\frac{1.331}{3\cdot 2.1}=0.2114.

Since
0.2114\leq 1.0
, the theoretical bound is verified for this tensor.

Step 3: Using
V(x)
as a Lyapunov function.

Consider the nonlinear system
\dot{x}=g(x)
defined by the negative gradient flow

\dot{x}=g(x)=-\nabla V(x),

where

\nabla V(x)=\begin{pmatrix}4.4\,x_{1}^{3}\\
4\,x_{2}^{3}\end{pmatrix}.

Since
\mathcal{A}
is positive definite,
V(x)>0
for all
x\neq 0
.
The derivative of
V
along trajectories of the system is

\dot{V}(x)=(\nabla V(x))^{T}g(x)=-\|\nabla V(x)\|^{2}<0\qquad\text{for all }x\neq 0.

Hence
V
is a valid Lyapunov function and
\dot{x}=g(x)
is asymptotically stable.

This example demonstrates how the eigenvalue bounds in Theorem
3.1
can be used to
certify the positivity of a tensor–generated Lyapunov function and to establish the
asymptotic stability of a nonlinear system through the condition

(\nabla V(x))^{T}g(x)<0
.

6
Conclusion

In this work, we established a new algebraic framework for bounding the H-eigenvalues of symmetric positive definite tensors. By moving beyond coordinate-dependent methods and leveraging intrinsic invariants—specifically the trace and determinant—we derived a sequence of inequalities (Theorems
3.1
through
3.6
) that progressively tighten spectral estimates.

Our comparative analysis in Section
5
reveals the inherent limitations of classical magnitude-based bounds like the Gershgorin circle theorem. We demonstrated that Gershgorin bounds, which rely on the sum of absolute off-diagonal entries, struggle significantly in two common scenarios. First, when a tensor contains negative off-diagonal entries, the Gershgorin method fails to account for algebraic cancellations, leading to loose estimates. In contrast, our invariant-based approach naturally incorporates these signs, reducing the estimation error by over 50% in such cases. Second, as the tensor order increases, the combinatorial explosion of terms renders summation-based bounds practically uninformative. Our method avoids this “curse of dimensionality" by relying on fixed invariants, providing robust and stable bounds even for higher-order tensors.

Ultimately, we found that Theorem
3.6
offers the most accurate upper bound for the spectral radius among the derived inequalities. This reliability makes it a valuable tool for certifying the stability of nonlinear autonomous systems, where precise eigenvalue localization is critical. Future work will focus on extending these invariant-based techniques to non-symmetric tensors and further exploring the geometric relationship between eigenvalue clustering and bound tightness.
