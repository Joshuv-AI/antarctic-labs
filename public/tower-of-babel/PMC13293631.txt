Isolated Peripheral Nervous System Presentation in Ma/Ma2-Associated Autoimmunity
Authors: Andreu Vilaseca, Macarena Villagrán-García, Antonio Farina, Jordi Bruna, Josep O. Dalmau, Divyanshu Dubey, Jerome Honnorat, Francesc Graus
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectivesMa/Ma2-associated neurologic autoimmunity is characterized by CNS involvement. However, isolated peripheral nervous system (PNS) presentations have been rarely reported. We aimed to describe the frequency and syndromes of patients with Ma/Ma2 antibodies and isolated PNS involvement.MethodsWe performed a nested case series within multicenter cohorts of patients with Ma/Ma2-associated neurologic syndromes, confirmed by tissue-based assay and line-blot. Patients with isolated PNS presentations were included.ResultsAmong 212 patients with Ma/Ma2 antibody-associated neurologic syndromes, 7 (3%) presented with isolated PNS involvement (median age, 68 years; 4/7 female). PNS syndromes included sensory neuronopathy (3 patients), myeloradiculopathy (1), radiculoplexopathy (1), motor neuronopathy (1), and multiple mononeuropathy. All patients were anti–Ma2-positive, whereas Ma antibodies (reactive against Ma1 and Ma2 proteins) were detected in 2 (33%)/6 tested patients. An associated cancer was identified in 6 (86%)/7 patients: pleural mesothelioma; oral squamous cell; testicular, lung, and breast cancer; and B-cell lymphoma. Five patients received immunotherapy, cancer treatment, or both. After a median follow-up of 23 months, symptoms improved or stabilized in 3 patients and progressed in 4.DiscussionIsolated PNS involvement is a rare manifestation of Ma/Ma2 associated autoimmunity. Ma/Ma2 antibody testing should be considered in neuronopathies and unexplained non–length-dependent neuropathies.

Introduction

Antibodies against Ma2 were first identified in patients with limbic and brainstem encephalitis and testicular cancer.1 The presence of Ma antibodies, which recognize Ma1 and Ma2 proteins, in some patients appears to broaden the associated tumor spectrum, most notably lung cancer, while maintaining a similar pattern of CNS involvement.2-4

Subsequent cohort studies have shown that a few patients also have symptoms of the peripheral nervous system (PNS) or spinal cord concomitant with the limbic and brainstem encephalitis.3-5 Moreover, pathologic studies have demonstrated inflammatory infiltrates beyond symptomatic regions, including dorsal root ganglia.5 Although a few single cases of isolated PNS involvement or in combination with myelopathy have been published in the last decade,4,6-9 the prevalence, clinical spectrum, and tumor association of isolated PNS involvement in well-characterized Ma/Ma2 antibody-positive cohorts remain unclear.

In this study, we aim to characterize the clinical features, associated cancers, and outcomes of patients with Ma/Ma2 antibodies who present with isolated PNS symptoms.

Methods

Study Design

We conducted a nested case series within multicenter cohort of patients with Ma/Ma2 antibodies confirmed with tissue-based assay and line-blot. This international collaboration included Hospital Clinic (Barcelona-Spain), the French Reference Center for Paraneoplastic Neurologic Syndromes and Autoimmune Encephalitis (Lyon-France), and Mayo Clinic MN-). We included patients with isolated PNS clinical presentation with or without concomitant spinal cord involvement (if PNS predominated).

Data Collection and Statistical Analysis

We performed retrospective chart review. We collected detailed clinical characteristics; serum and CSF antibody results, including testing methodologies; oncologic associations; neurologic outcomes; and findings from ancillary studies such as MRI and neurophysiologic studies. Descriptive statistical analyses were conducted.

Standard Protocol Approvals, Registrations, and Patient Consents

The study was approved by IRBs of participating centers. Written informed consent was obtained for the storage and use of the samples for research purposes.

Data Availability

Anonymized data are available to qualified investigators upon reasonable request.

Results

Among 212 patients with Ma/Ma2-associated neurologic syndromes, 7 (3%) presented isolated PNS involvement (median age: 68 years, range 46–80; 4/7 (57%) female), of whom 1 had concomitant spinal cord involvement (Table). The distribution of cases across centers is shown in Figure. The onset was subacute in 5/7 (71%) patients, and at the nadir, 5 (71%) required assistance for ambulation (median mRS 4, range 2–4). Clinical phenotypes were consistent with involvement of the proximal components of the PNS in 6 (86%) patients, presenting as sensory neuronopathy (n = 3), myeloradiculopathy (n = 1), radiculoplexopathy (n = 1), and motor neuronopathy (n = 1). The remaining patient had a multiple mononeuropathy.7 This patient had an abnormal brain MRI compatible with limbic encephalitis despite the absence of seizures, behavioral changes, and a normal cognitive evaluation. Across these syndromes, sensory involvement was present in 6 patients and was asymmetric in all cases. Sensory symptoms predominated in 4 patients, including neuropathic pain (n = 4), hypoesthesia (n = 4), and sensory ataxia (n = 2). The 3 patients with predominant motor involvement showed asymmetric weakness affecting the lower limbs, upper limbs, or both (1 each, respectively). CSF examination showed pleocytosis in all but 1 patient (5/6, 83%), the latter being sampled 24 months after symptom onset. Spine MRI, performed in 6 patients, was unremarkable except in the single patient with associated myelitis.

All patients tested positive for Ma2 antibodies; Ma antibodies were detected in 2/6 (33%) patients with radiculoplexopathy and sensory neuronopathy (Table). None of the samples showed additional neuronal antibodies in tissue-based assays. Ganglioside antibodies were not tested. Six patients (86%) had associated cancer, including pleural mesothelioma and oral squamous cell carcinoma in the patients with Ma antibodies, and testicular, lung, and breast cancer, and B-cell lymphoma in the remaining patients. The only patient without a confirmed malignancy had a lung nodule that remained stable on follow-up before being resected 36 months later. The pathology examination revealed fibrotic tissue without evidence of cancer. In patients with confirmed cancer, the neurologic syndrome preceded the cancer diagnosis or relapse in 5 of 6 cases (83%), with a median delay to cancer diagnosis of 9 months (range, 0–23 months). Five patients received corticosteroids alone or in combination with other medications or IV immunoglobulins (Table) with partial improvement of symptoms in 2 patients. At the last follow-up (median 23 months; range 6–84), symptoms improved or stabilized in 3 patients and progressed in 4. Two patients died from cancer progression without improvement of the PNS symptoms. The remaining 5 patients had a median mRS of 3 (range 2–5) (Table) at the last follow-up.

Discussion

This multicenter, retrospective study confirms that Ma/Ma2 autoimmunity may be associated with isolated PNS syndromes. This association is rare as in this cohort of 212 patients with Ma/Ma2 antibodies only 7 (3%) patients were identified. The clinical syndromes were like those observed with other paraneoplastic antibodies. Four patients presented clinical features of neuronopathy (sensory 3, motor 1), that classically associate with Hu (ANNA1) antibodies, and the other 3 developed asymmetric, axonal neuropathies, with radicular involvement in 2, a common phenotype of paraneoplastic neuropathies particularly those associated with CV2 (CRMP5) antibodies.10,11

One concern is that the Ma/Ma2 antibodies merely reflected the presence of cancer rather than being associated with the paraneoplastic neurologic syndrome. However, this possibility is unlikely, as in 2 previous studies we did not detect Ma/Ma2 antibodies in 84 and 179 serum samples from patients with cancer without paraneoplastic syndromes.1,2

The commonest type of neuropathy was sensory neuronopathy, which occurred in 3 patients. Sensory neuronopathies frequently have a paraneoplastic etiology with subacute and severe course. However, 1 of our 3 patients presented only mild sensory symptoms that remained stable without treatment. This clinical course has been observed in around 10% of patients with Hu (ANNA1) antibodies and paraneoplastic sensory neuronopathies.12

None of the patients had symmetric distal or length-dependent polyneuropathy, which are more typical of other, more common causes of peripheral neuropathy. Therefore, Ma2 antibodies may be considered in patients with axonal peripheral neuropathies of unclear etiology presenting with the neuropathic syndromes described here, rather than being routinely requested in chronic, symmetric length-dependent polyneuropathy of unknown cause.

Our patients had a broader tumor spectrum beyond the predominance of testicular seminoma classically linked to Ma2 autoimmunity. This tumor heterogeneity has also been observed in a recent series of 77 patients with Ma2 antibodies with classical CNS syndromes.13 Interestingly, we also identified 1 patient with a stable lung nodule that proved to be fibrotic tissue on pathology, possibly reflecting a burned-out malignancy, an observation also noted in the examination of testicular lesions of patients with paraneoplastic syndromes and Ma2 antibodies.14

Although Ma/Ma2 antibodies are not pathogenic, the associated paraneoplastic syndrome is probably mediated by T-cell targeting Ma proteins expressed in sensory neurons of the dorsal root ganglia or the spinal motor neurons.2 Although pathologic involvement of the dorsal root ganglia has been described in Ma/Ma2-associated autoimmunity,5 there are no data on Ma protein expression in peripheral nerves or spinal nerve roots; therefore, the basis for this involvement remains to be elucidated. In a similar setting, authors who described the clinicopathologic features of neuropathies associated with amphiphysin antibodies reported RNA low level amphiphysin expression in the peripheral nerve.15

To summarize, a small subset of patients with Ma/Ma2-associated neurologic autoimmunity present with isolated PNS involvement. Accordingly, Ma2 antibody testing should be considered in patients with otherwise unexplained, neuronopathies or non–length-dependent peripheral nerve/root involvement, with or without CNS involvement, alongside other onconeural antibodies classically associated with paraneoplastic neuropathies such as Hu (ANNA-1) and CV2 (CRMP5).
