SUSY meets pseudo-Hermiticity
Cheng-Yang Lee, Gloria Cecilia de León Morales, Julio Olmos, Carlos A. Vaquera-Araujo, Siyi Zhou

Abstract
In this work, we construct the simplest pseudo-Hermitian quantum field theory that is supersymmetric. This is the pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model in the sense that it contains a pair of symplectic fermions (anti-commuting scalar fields) that satisfy the Klein-Gordon equation and a spin-half boson that satisfies the Dirac equation. The conventional spin-statistics theorem is circumvented through the use of pseudo-Hermitian conjugation to define field adjoints. To make the supersymmetry manifest, we formulate the pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model using the superfield formalism. These superfields are Grassmann-odd so it is not possible to construct non-vanishing cubic interactions using only these superfields. We show that this problem can be resolved by coupling the pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model with the Hermitian Wess-Zumino model while preserving supersymmetry.

Introduction

The spin-statistics theorem is one of the foundations in quantum field theory (QFT). Within the framework of Lorentz-invariant QFTs with Hermitian Hamiltonians, the theorem states that integer-spin fields must commute while half-integer-spin fields must anti-commute

By allowing the Hamiltonians to be pseudo-Hermitian instead of Hermitian, a new class of pseudo-Hermitian QFT where integer-spin fields are anti-commuting while half-integer spin fields are commuting, have been constructed. Within this framework, spin-zero scalar fields are fermionic (symplectic fermions

Supersymmetry, which relates bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom

(3+1)
-dimensional supersymmetric Hermitian QFT is the minimal
\mathcal{N}=1
Wess-Zumino model, which pairs a complex scalar boson with a Majorana fermion. Given the existence of models for symplectic fermions and spin-half bosons, a compelling question arises:
Can we construct a supersymmetric theory that relates symplectic fermions to spin-half bosons?

In this work, we answer this question in the affirmative by constructing the pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model. This model contains a pair of symplectic fermions and a spin-half boson. The bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom are matched by including two anti-commuting scalars to compensate the four degrees of freedom of the spin-half boson, as a Majorana-type construction is not available for commuting spinor fields. We formulate this theory using superspace formalism, and by constructing chiral and antichiral superfields whose component fields exhibit the desired statistics. The resulting free theory is manifestly pseudo-Hermitian and supersymmetric. We further extend our construction to include interactions by coupling the flipped-statistics multiplet to a Hermitian Wess-Zumino multiplet, obtaining an interacting Lagrangian that includes quartic self-interactions for the symplectic fermions as well as novel Yukawa couplings.

II
Pseudo-Hermitian QFT

In this section, we briefly review the theory of symplectic fermions and spin-half bosons that are needed to construct the pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model.

II.1
Symplectic fermions

The symplectic fermion is a theory of anti-commuting scalar fields
4
;
9
. It consists of a complex scalar field
\phi
and an adjoint
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}
whose expansions are given by

\displaystyle\phi(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\left[e^{-ip\cdot x}b(\textbf{p})+e^{ip\cdot x}b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})\right],

(1)

\displaystyle\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\left[e^{ip\cdot x}b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})-e^{-ip\cdot x}b^{c}(\textbf{p})\right].

(2)

Due to the minus sign in front of
e^{-ip\cdot x}b^{c{\dagger}}
, the fields are fermionic instead of bosonic. The field
\phi
anti-commutes with its adjoint
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}
:

\left\{\phi(t,\textbf{x}),\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}(t,\textbf{y})\right\}=0,

(3)

with the annihilation and creation operators satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations

\displaystyle\left\{b(\textbf{p}^{\prime}),b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})\right\}=\left\{b^{c}(\textbf{p}^{\prime}),b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})\right\}=(2\pi)^{3}\delta^{3}(\textbf{p}^{\prime}-\textbf{p}).

(4)

The field adjoint
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}
is the pseudo-Hermitian conjugate of
\phi

\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}(x)=\eta^{-1}_{\phi}\phi^{{\dagger}}(x)\eta_{\phi}

(5)

where
\eta_{\phi}
is a linear and Hermitian operator. This operator commutes with
b
and anti-commutes with
b^{c{\dagger}}
. Its solution is given by
9

\eta_{\phi}=\exp\left[i\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})b^{c}(\textbf{p})\right].

(6)

The symplectic fermions satisfy the Klein-Gordon equation, so their Lagrangian density is given by

\mathcal{L}_{\phi}=(\partial^{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}})(\partial_{\mu}\phi)-m^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}\phi.

(7)

The conjugate momenta for
\phi
and
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}
are
\pi=\partial_{t}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}
and
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\pi}}=-\partial_{t}\phi
, respectively. It is straightforward to show that the fields and their conjugate momenta satisfy the canonical anti-commutation relations. The free Hamiltonian is given by the Legendre transform for
\mathcal{L}_{\phi}

\displaystyle H_{0}(\phi)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}x\left[\partial_{t}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}\partial_{t}\phi+(\nabla\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}})\cdot(\nabla\phi)+m^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}\phi\right]

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}pE_{\mathbf{p}}\left[b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})b(\textbf{p})-b^{c}(\textbf{p})b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})\right].

(8)

Both the Lagrangian density and Hamiltonian are manifestly pseudo-Hermitian

\displaystyle\eta^{-1}_{\phi}\mathcal{L}^{{\dagger}}_{\phi}\eta_{\phi}=\mathcal{L}_{\phi},\quad\eta^{-1}_{\phi}H^{{\dagger}}_{0}(\phi)\eta_{\phi}=H_{0}(\phi).

(9)

From (
8
), we see that
H_{0}(\phi)
commutes with
\eta_{\phi}
, so it is simultaneously Hermitian and pseudo-Hermitian

\eta^{-1}_{\phi}H^{{\dagger}}_{0}(\phi)\eta_{\phi}=H^{{\dagger}}_{0}(\phi)=H_{0}(\phi).

(10)

II.2
Spin-half bosons

Spin-half bosons originated from the work of Ahluwalia and was later formalized within the framework of pseudo-Hermitian QFT
4
;
1
. Let
\psi
and
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}
be the massive spinor field and its adjoint for the spin-half boson. Their expansions are given by

\displaystyle\psi(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\Big[

\displaystyle e^{-ip\cdot x}u(\textbf{p},s)b(\textbf{p},s)

\displaystyle+e^{ip\cdot x}v(\textbf{p},s)b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\Big],

(11)

\displaystyle\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}(x)=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\Big[

\displaystyle e^{ip\cdot x}\bar{u}(\textbf{p},s)b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)

\displaystyle-e^{-ip\cdot x}\bar{v}(\textbf{p},s)b^{c}(\textbf{p},s)\Big],

(12)

where
u
,
v
are the Dirac spinors and
\bar{u}
,
\bar{v}
are their corresponding Dirac duals. The minus sign in front of
e^{-ip\cdot x}\bar{v}b^{c}
makes the spinor fields bosonic

\left[\psi(t,\textbf{x}),\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}(t,\textbf{y})\right]=0,

(13)

where the annihilation and creation operators satisfy

\displaystyle\left[b(\textbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}),b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\right]

\displaystyle=\left[b^{c}(\textbf{p}^{\prime},s^{\prime}),b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\right]

\displaystyle=(2\pi)^{3}\delta_{s^{\prime}s}\delta^{3}(\textbf{p}^{\prime}-\textbf{p}).

(14)

The adjoint field is defined as

\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}\equiv\eta^{-1}_{\psi}\overline{\psi}\eta_{\psi},

(15)

with

\eta_{\psi}=\exp\left[i\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum_{s=\pm\frac{1}{2}}b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)b^{c}(\textbf{p},s)\right].

(16)

The bosonic spinor fields satisfy the Dirac equation

\left(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}-m\right)\psi,

(17)

so the Lagrangian density is

\mathcal{L}_{\psi}=\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}(i\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\psi-m)\psi.

(18)

Direct computation shows that the bosonic spinor fields satisfy the canonical commutation relations and its free Hamiltonian is given by

\displaystyle H_{0}(\psi)

\displaystyle=\int d^{3}x\left[-i\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}i\gamma^{i}\partial_{i}\psi+m\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}\psi\right]

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}E_{\mathbf{p}}\sum_{s=\pm 1/2}\Big[b^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)b(\textbf{p},s)

\displaystyle\hskip 85.35826pt+b^{c}(\textbf{p},s)b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\Big].

(19)

III
Pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model

The Hermitian Wess-Zumino model consists of a Majorana fermionic field and a complex bosonic scalar field, both satisfying the conventional spin-statistics theorem. The Majorana fermion has two degrees of freedom, which is in one-to-one correspondence with the two bosonic degrees of freedom of the complex scalar field.

In accordance with the new spin-statistics theorem, the corresponding flipped-statistics Wess-Zumino model should be endowed with a global supersymmetry that relates spin-half bosons and symplectic fermions. Note however, there is no bosonic counterpart to Majorana fermions. Spin-half bosons must necessarily have four degrees of freedom on-shell. We cannot reduce its degrees of freedom by identifying the particles with anti-particles. Doing so would make the fields non-local. Our resolution to this problem is construct a Wess Zumino model consisting of
one
spin-half boson and
two
symplectic fermions. This way, the bosonic and fermionic degrees of freedom are in one-to-one correspondence.

In the scalar sector of the multiplet, we have a pair of symplectic fermion fields and their duals

\displaystyle\phi_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\textbf{p}}}}\left[e^{-ip\cdot x}b_{i}(\textbf{p})+e^{ip\cdot x}b_{i}^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})\right],

(20)

\displaystyle\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i}(x)

\displaystyle=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\textbf{p}}}}\left[e^{ip\cdot x}b_{i}^{{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})-e^{-ip\cdot x}b_{i}^{c}(\textbf{p})\right],

(21)

with
i=1,2
. These fields are anti-commuting scalars whose dynamics are described by the free massive Lagrangian density

\mathcal{L}_{S}=\sum^{2}_{i=1}\left[(\partial^{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i})(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{i})-m^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i}\phi_{i}\right]\,,

(22)

satisfying the Klein-Gordon equation

(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\phi_{i}=(\partial^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}+m^{2})\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i}=0\,.

(23)

The dual fields are defined as

\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i}=\eta^{-1}_{S}\phi^{\dagger}_{i}\eta_{S},

(24)

with the Fock-space operator

\eta_{S}=\exp\left[i\pi\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\sum^{2}_{i=1}b_{i}^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p})b^{c}_{i}(\textbf{p})\right],

(25)

that renders the scalar Lagrangian density pseudo-Hermitian

\eta^{-1}_{S}\mathcal{L}_{S}^{\dagger}\eta_{S}=\mathcal{L}_{S}.

(26)

In this work, we adopt the Van der Waerden two-component notation following the conventions of
5
. The four-component spin-half field in the chiral basis is written in terms of a pair of left- and right-handed two-component spinors according to

\psi=\begin{pmatrix}\xi_{\alpha}\\
\chi^{\dagger}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix},

(27)

with
\alpha=1,2
and
\dot{\alpha}=\dot{1},\dot{2}
. The adjoint Dirac field
\overline{\psi}
and the charge conjugate
\psi^{c}
are defined by:

\begin{split}\overline{\psi}&\equiv\psi^{\dagger}\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}\chi^{\alpha}&\xi^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix},\\
\psi^{c}&\equiv\mathcal{C}\overline{\psi}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\chi_{\alpha}\\
\xi^{\dagger}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

(28)

where the Dirac conjugation matrix
\Pi
and the charge conjugation matrix
\mathcal{C}
satisfy

\Pi\gamma^{\mu}\Pi^{-1}=\gamma^{\mu}{}^{\dagger},\qquad\mathcal{C}^{-1}\gamma^{\mu}\mathcal{C}=-\gamma^{\mu}{}^{T}.

(29)

In the chiral representation, these matrices can be written in
2\times 2
blocks as

\Pi=\begin{pmatrix}0&\delta^{\dot{\alpha}}{}_{\dot{\beta}}\\
\delta_{\alpha}{}^{\beta}&0\end{pmatrix},\qquad\mathcal{C}=\begin{pmatrix}\epsilon_{\alpha\beta}&0\\
0&\epsilon^{\dot{\alpha}\dot{\beta}}\end{pmatrix},

(30)

together with

\gamma^{\mu}=\begin{pmatrix}0&\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\\
\overline{\sigma}^{\mu\dot{\alpha}\beta}&0\end{pmatrix},\,\sigma^{\mu}\equiv(\mathbf{1},\bm{\sigma}),\,\overline{\sigma}^{\mu}\equiv(\mathbf{1},-\bm{\sigma}),

(31)

where
\bm{\sigma}
is the three-vector of Pauli matrices. In two-component notation, the relevant fields are

\begin{split}\xi_{\alpha}(x)&=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s=\pm\frac{1}{2}}\big[e^{-ip\cdot x}x_{\alpha}(\textbf{p},s)b(\textbf{p},s)\\
&\qquad\qquad+e^{ip\cdot x}y_{\alpha}(\textbf{p},s)b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\big],\\
\chi_{\alpha}(x)&=\int\frac{d^{3}p}{(2\pi)^{3}}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2E_{\mathbf{p}}}}\sum_{s=\pm\frac{1}{2}}\big[e^{-ip\cdot x}x_{\alpha}(\textbf{p},s)b^{c{\dagger}}(\textbf{p},s)\\
&\qquad\qquad+e^{ip\cdot x}y_{\alpha}(\textbf{p},s)b(\textbf{p},s)\big],\end{split}

(32)

with

(33)

and spin states
\zeta_{s}

s=\pm 1/2

\frac{1}{2}\bm{\sigma}\cdot\mathbf{\hat{s}}\zeta_{s}=s\zeta_{s}.

Dotted and undotted indices are related by Hermitian conjugation, and indices are raised and lowered by the Levi-Civita tensors according to

From these relations, one can easily form invariant bilinear forms in index-free notation

(36)

\begin{split}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}=\begin{pmatrix}\eta^{-1}\chi^{\alpha}\eta&\eta^{-1}\xi^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}\eta\end{pmatrix}\equiv\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{\chi}^{\alpha}&\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix},\end{split}

and the Dirac equation in Eq.(
17

The bosonic sector of the multiplet is composed by the fields in Eqs.(

In analogy with the Hermitian Wess-Zumino model, the simplest supersymmetric free theory involving a pair of symplectic fermions and a spin-half boson is described by the following Lagrangian density

\delta\phi_{2}&=\sqrt{2}\chi^{\dagger}\epsilon^{\dagger},\\
\delta\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}&=\sqrt{2}\epsilon\widetilde{\chi},\\
\delta\xi_{\alpha}&=\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{\alpha}f_{1}-i\sqrt{2}\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\epsilon^{\dagger\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{1},\\
\delta\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}_{\dot{\alpha}}&=\sqrt{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}\epsilon^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}+i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\epsilon^{\beta}\sigma^{\mu}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}},\\
\delta\widetilde{\chi}_{\alpha}&=\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{\alpha}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}-i\sqrt{2}\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\epsilon^{\dagger\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2},\\
\delta\chi^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}&=\sqrt{2}f_{2}\epsilon^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}+i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\phi_{2}\epsilon^{\beta}\sigma^{\mu}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}},\\
\delta f_{1}&=-i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\xi\sigma^{\mu}\epsilon^{\dagger},\\
\delta\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}&=i\sqrt{2}\epsilon\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}},\\
\delta f_{2}&=i\sqrt{2}\epsilon\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\chi^{\dagger},\\
\delta\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}&=-i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\widetilde{\chi}\sigma^{\mu}\epsilon^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(57)

In terms of the superfields, the off-shell Lagrangian of our pseudo-Hermitian Wess-Zumino model reads

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\text{off-shell}}_{0}=&\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}X+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}Y\right]_{D}-m\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}Y+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}X\right]_{F},\end{split}

(58)

where the
D
-terms of a general superfield
\mathcal{F}
are defined as

[\mathcal{F}]_{D}\equiv\int d^{2}\theta^{\dagger}d^{2}\theta\mathcal{F},

(59)

and
F
-terms of a chiral (anti-chiral) superfield
\mathcal{X}
(
\mathcal{A}
) as

[\mathcal{X}]_{F}\equiv\int d^{2}\theta\mathcal{X},\qquad[\mathcal{A}]_{F}\equiv\int d^{2}\theta^{\dagger}\mathcal{A}.

(60)

In this way,
\mathcal{L}^{\text{off-shell}}_{0}
transforms as a total derivative under Eq.(
57
).
In terms of the component fields, the off-shell free Lagrangian density is

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}^{\text{off-shell}}_{0}=&\mathcal{L}_{\psi}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[(\partial^{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i})(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{i})+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{i}f_{i}\right]\\
&-m(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}f_{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}f_{1}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}\phi_{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}\phi_{1}),\end{split}

(61)

and the equations of motion for the auxiliary fields are

f_{1}=m\phi_{2},\,f_{2}=m\phi_{1},\,\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}=m\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2},\,\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}=m\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1},

(62)

showing that the prescription for the duals of the fields
f_{i}
is consistent on-shell. Substituting (
62
) into Eqs.(
61
,
57
), we arrive at the on-shell Lagrangian density in Eq.(
42
), and the on-shell SUSY transformations in Eq.(
44
), while the transformation rules for the auxiliary fields become automatically satisfy the Dirac equation.

V
Interactions

Since the superfields
X
,
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}
,
Y
and
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}
are Grassmann-odd, it is not possible to form a non vanishing cubic superpotential out of them.
Instead, the simplest interacting theory for our spin-flipped multiplet can be obtained by coupling it with the Wess-Zumino multiplet, packaged into a commuting chiral superfield and its pseudo-Hermitian adjoint

\begin{split}\Phi&=\varphi(y)+\sqrt{2}\theta\lambda(y)+\theta\theta F(y),\\
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\Phi}}&=\eta^{-1}\Phi^{\dagger}\eta=\varphi^{\dagger}(y)+\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\dagger}(y)\theta^{\dagger}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}(y)\theta^{\dagger}\theta^{\dagger},\end{split}

(63)

where
\varphi
is a complex scalar boson,
\lambda
is a Majorana fermion and
F
is a complex scalar auxiliary boson. Notice that the physical fields of the conventional Wess-Zumino multiplet
\varphi
and
\lambda
commute with the
\eta
operator. The SUSY transformations of these fields are given by

\begin{split}\delta\varphi&=\sqrt{2}\epsilon\lambda,\\
\delta\varphi^{\dagger}&=\sqrt{2}\lambda^{\dagger}\epsilon^{\dagger},\\
\delta\lambda_{\alpha}&=\sqrt{2}\epsilon_{\alpha}F-i\sqrt{2}\sigma^{\mu}_{\alpha\dot{\beta}}\epsilon^{\dagger\dot{\beta}}\partial_{\mu}\varphi,\\
\delta\lambda^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}&=\sqrt{2}F^{\dagger}\epsilon^{\dagger}_{\dot{\alpha}}+i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\varphi^{\dagger}\epsilon^{\beta}\sigma^{\mu}_{\beta\dot{\alpha}},\\
\delta F&=i\sqrt{2}\partial_{\mu}\lambda\sigma^{\mu}\epsilon^{\dagger},\\
\delta\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}&=-i\sqrt{2}\epsilon\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\dagger}.\end{split}

(64)

The full pseudo-Hermitian and explicitly supersymmetric interacting Lagrangian is

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{\text{off-shell}}=\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}X+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}Y+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\Phi}}\Phi\right]_{D}\\
&\qquad-m\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}Y+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}X\right]_{F}+\frac{M}{2}\left[\Phi^{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\Phi}}^{2}\right]_{F}\\
&\qquad+\frac{g}{3!}\left[\Phi^{3}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\Phi}}^{3}\right]_{F}+h\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\Phi}}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{X}}Y+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{Y}}X\Phi\right]_{F},\end{split}

(65)

with
M
as the mass of the fields
\varphi
and
\lambda
, and dimensionless couplings
g
and
h
. In terms of the constituting fields, this Lagrangian reads

\begin{split}&\mathcal{L}^{\text{off-shell}}=\mathcal{L}_{\psi}+\sum_{i=1}^{2}\left[(\partial^{\mu}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{i})(\partial_{\mu}\phi_{i})+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{i}f_{i}\right]\\
&\qquad+(\partial^{\mu}\varphi^{\dagger})(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}F+M\left(\varphi F+\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}\right)\\
&\qquad+i\lambda\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\dagger}-\frac{M}{2}\left(\lambda\lambda+\lambda^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\right)\\
&\qquad-m(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}f_{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}f_{1}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}\phi_{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}\phi_{1})\\
&\qquad+\frac{g}{2}\left[\varphi^{2}F+(\varphi^{\dagger})^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}-\varphi\lambda\lambda-\varphi^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\right]\\
&\qquad+h\left[\varphi(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}f_{1}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}\phi_{1})+F\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{1}\right]\\
&\qquad+h\left[\varphi^{\dagger}(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}f_{2}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}\phi_{2})+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{2}\right]\\
&\qquad+h\left[\varphi\widetilde{\chi}\xi+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\xi\lambda+\widetilde{\chi}\lambda\phi_{1}\right]\\
&\qquad+h\left[\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}\chi^{\dagger}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\lambda^{\dagger}\chi^{\dagger}+\lambda^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}\phi_{2}\right].\end{split}

(66)

The equations of motion for the auxiliary fields are

\begin{split}F&=-M\varphi^{\dagger}-\frac{g}{2}(\varphi^{\dagger})^{2}-h\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{2}\\
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{\scalebox{1.4}[1]{$\bm{\neg}$}}}{\smash[t]{F}}&=-M\varphi-\frac{g}{2}\varphi^{2}-h\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{1}\\
f_{1}&=m\phi_{2}-h\varphi^{\dagger}\phi_{2},\\
f_{2}&=m\phi_{1}-h\varphi\phi_{1},\\
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{1}&=m\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}-h\varphi\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2},\\
\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{f}}_{2}&=m\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}-h\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1},\end{split}

(67)

Substituting these equations of motion back into
\mathcal{L}
, we obtain the on-shell Lagrangian

\mathcal{L}=\mathcal{L}_{0}+\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}++\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}+\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Y}}.

(68)

Here,
\mathcal{L}_{0}
is given by Eq.(
42
),
\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}
is the free Lagrangian of the commuting scalar

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\varphi}=&(\partial^{\mu}\varphi^{\dagger})(\partial_{\mu}\varphi)-M^{2}\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi,\end{split}

(69)

and the free Majorana spinor is described by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\lambda}=&i\lambda\sigma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\lambda^{\dagger}-\frac{M}{2}\left(\lambda\lambda+\lambda^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\right)\\
=&\frac{i}{2}\overline{\Lambda}\gamma^{\mu}\partial_{\mu}\Lambda-\frac{M}{2}\overline{\Lambda}\Lambda,\end{split}

(70)

with

\Lambda=\Lambda^{c}=\mathcal{C}\overline{\Lambda}^{T}=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{\alpha}\\
\lambda^{\dagger}{}^{\dot{\alpha}}\end{pmatrix}.

(71)

The scalar interaction Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}
is given by

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{I}}=&-\frac{g^{2}}{4}(\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi)^{2}-\frac{gM}{2}\left[\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi^{2}+(\varphi^{\dagger})^{2}\varphi\right]\\
&+h^{2}\left[\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{1}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{2}-\varphi^{\dagger}\varphi\left(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{1}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{2}\right)\right]\\
&-\frac{gh}{2}\left[(\varphi^{\dagger})^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{1}+\varphi^{2}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{2}\right]\\
&-Mh\left(\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi_{2}}}\phi_{1}+\varphi\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi_{1}}}\phi_{2}\right)\\
&+mh(\varphi^{\dagger}+\varphi)(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\phi_{1}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\phi_{2}),\end{split}

(72)

and includes the quartic self-interaction for the symplectic fermions analyzed in
10
. Finally, the Yukawa Lagrangian
\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Y}}
can be written as

\begin{split}\mathcal{L}_{\mathrm{Y}}=&-\frac{g}{2}\left[\varphi\lambda\lambda+\varphi^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\lambda^{\dagger}\right]\\
&+h\left[\varphi\widetilde{\chi}\xi+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\xi\lambda+\widetilde{\chi}\lambda\phi_{1}\right]\\
&+h\left[\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}\chi^{\dagger}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\lambda^{\dagger}\chi^{\dagger}+\lambda^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\xi}}\phi_{2}\right]\\
=&-\frac{g}{2}\left[\varphi\overline{\Lambda}_{R}\Lambda_{L}+\varphi^{\dagger}\overline{\Lambda}_{L}\Lambda_{R}\right]\\
&+h(\varphi\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}_{R}\psi_{L}+\varphi^{\dagger}\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}_{L}\psi_{R})\\
&+h(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{1}\overline{\Lambda}_{L}\psi_{R}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}_{R}\Lambda_{L}\phi_{1})\\
&-h(\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\phi}}_{2}\overline{\Lambda}_{R}\psi_{L}+\overset{\>\raisebox{4.2679pt}{$\bm{\neg}$}}{\smash[t]{\psi}}_{L}\Lambda_{R}\phi_{2}),\end{split}

(73)

with

\psi_{L,R}=\frac{1}{2}(1\mp\gamma^{5})\psi,

(74)

where
\gamma^{5}
is the chiral operator

\gamma^{5}=i\gamma^{0}\gamma^{1}\gamma^{2}\gamma^{3}.

(75)

VI
Conclusions

In this paper, we have constructed the first consistent supersymmetric theory for QFT with flipped statistics. The departure from the usual spin-statistics connection is achieved by relaxing the requirement of Hermiticity in favor of pseudo-Hermiticity. The multiplet presented here consists of two anticommuting complex scalars known as symplectic fermions, that satisfy the Klein-Gordon equation; two commuting two-component spin-half fields that satisfy the Dirac Equation and furnish Bose-Einstein statistics; and two non-dynamical anticommuting complex scalar auxiliary fields that on-shell become proportional to the symplectic fermions. The resulting model is analogous to the Wess-Zumino model but in its minimal form requires two anticommuting chiral superfields and their duals. The simplest interacting theory has been studied. This theory couples the flipped-statistics multiplet with a single regular Wess-Zumino multiplet. The resulting superpotential generates, besides the usual interactions for the Wess-Zumino fields, new interactions with the statistics-flipped fields and in particular, a quartic interaction for the symplectic fermions and Yukawa interactions.

This work is the first step towards the study of pseudo-Hermitian supersymmetric QFTs, a novel area of research. Interesting topics to explore include: super-Poincaré symmetry and its representations, along the lines studied in
16
; vector superfields and the construction of pseudo-Hermitian gauge theories mirroring
20
;
19
;
17
; the study of how some aspects of supersymmetry might be modified with the implementation of pseudo-Hermiticity, as the non-renormalization theorem, and the unitarity of the interacting theory
10
; the promotion of rigid
\mathcal{N}=1
transformations to local
\mathcal{N}=1
SUSY; the study of SUSY breaking mechanisms in this class of theories; and the possibility that a particle with flipped statistics may become the lightest supersymmetric particle and therefore play the role of a weakly interacting massive particle dark matter candidate. Regarding non-fundamental areas of research, this new class of pseudo-Hermitian supersymmetric models might find applications in areas where SUSY concepts have already been proved useful, like condensed matter physics
6
and integrated optics
12
.
