Perturbation-resilient inertial Krasnosel’skiĭ-type hybrid retractions for generalized nonexpansive mappings
Markjoe O. Uba

Abstract
. Let 𝔈 \mathfrak{E} be a uniformly smooth and uniformly convex real Banach space. We study an inertial hybrid retraction method for a countable sequence of mappings satisfying the NST-condition and an approximate ϕ \phi -Fejér inequality with vanishing errors. We prove that the generated sequence converges strongly to the sunny generalized nonexpansive retraction of the initial point onto the common fixed-point set. The theorem admits vanishing error sequences that need not be summable and therefore contains the summable-error setting as a special case. We also establish a Bregman-projection analogue and provide illustrative examples.

1.
Introduction

Inertial-type algorithms, originating in the work of Polyak
[
25
]
,
are two-step iterative procedures in which the next iterate depends not only on
the current point but also on the preceding one. Such inertial terms are widely
used because they improve the practical speed of convergence while
preserving the structure of the underlying fixed point or optimization method.
Alongside inertial methods, hybrid projection and retraction techniques have
become important tools for obtaining strong convergence, especially in settings
where standard Krasnosel’skiĭ–Mann iterations may only yield weak convergence.
Representative developments include Alber’s generalized projection and
retraction framework, the hybrid method of Takahashi, Takeuchi, and Kubota,
and the error-free inertial hybrid scheme of Chidume and Nnakwe

[
1
,
27
,
13
]
.

In smooth Banach spaces, the normalized duality mapping
J
allows one to
replace the Hilbert-space squared norm geometry by a Banach-space geometry
generated by Alber’s functional

(1.1)

\phi(x,y):=\|x\|^{2}-2\langle x,Jy\rangle+\|y\|^{2},\qquad x,y\in\mathfrak{E}.

This functional plays the role of a generalized squared distance and is central
in the study of sunny generalized nonexpansive retractions and generalized
nonexpansive mappings; see, for instance,

[
1
,
19
,
23
]
. If
T

has a nonempty fixed point set, then the generalized nonexpansive condition may
be expressed as the Fejér-type inequality

(1.2)

\phi(Tx,p)\leq\phi(x,p),\qquad x\in\mathfrak{E},\quad p\in\operatorname{Fix}(T).

Thus (
1.2
) gives a natural decrease condition relative to the
fixed point set.

Most strong convergence results for hybrid retraction schemes are formulated in
an exact setting: the operator values are assumed to be evaluated without error,
and the Fejér inequality is assumed to hold exactly. In practical
implementations, however, exact evaluation may not be available. Approximation
errors may arise from approximate operator evaluations and related computational
inaccuracies. These considerations lead naturally to the question of whether
strong convergence can be obtained under controlled inexactness.

The aim of this paper is to prove strong convergence for a scheme with a
vanishing relaxation of the exact
\phi
-Fejér decrease condition. More
precisely, we allow the Fejér estimate to hold with a nonnegative error term
\varepsilon_{k}
,

\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
, without requiring the summability condition

\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}<\infty
, and show that the inertial hybrid
retraction sequence still converges strongly to
R_{F(\Gamma)}v_{0}
.

The Bregman framework studied in this paper has a long history. Bregman’s 1967
article introduced a generalized projection and relaxation method for finding a
common point of convex sets
[
6
]
. Subsequent developments by many
authors substantially expanded this framework; see, for example,

[
11
,
12
,
4
,
8
,
22
]
and the references therein.

Against this background, the second purpose of the paper is to formulate and
prove a Bregman-projection analogue within the broader theory of Bregman
distances and projections.

Related work.

Further developments in strong convergence methods for maximal monotone
problems and hybrid fixed-point schemes include

[
5
,
14
,

,

,
21
,

,

]
.
Error-tolerant and perturbation-resilient iterative methods have also been

[
16
,
18

]
.
Recent work on inertial and perturbed Krasnosel’skiĭ–Mann-type schemes includes

[
17
]
. Additional foundations for Bregman geometry and

[
10
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be a real Banach space with dual
\mathfrak{E}^{*}
. The normalized duality mapping
J:\mathfrak{E}\to 2^{\mathfrak{E}^{*}}
is defined by

Jx=\bigl\{x^{*}\in\mathfrak{E}^{*}:\langle x,x^{*}\rangle=\|x\|^{2}=\|x^{*}\|^{2}\bigr\}.

If
\mathfrak{E}
is smooth, then
J
is single-valued. If
\mathfrak{E}
is smooth, strictly convex, and reflexive, then
J
is a bijection from
\mathfrak{E}
onto
\mathfrak{E}^{*}
; see
[
1
]
. Throughout Sections 2 and 3,
\phi:E\times E\to\mathbb{R}
is the Lyapunov functional defined by (
1.1
), and satisfies

(2.1)

(\|x\|-\|y\|)^{2}\leq\phi(x,y)\leq(\|x\|+\|y\|)^{2}.

Lemma 2.1

(
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be smooth and uniformly convex. Suppose that
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
are sequences in
\mathfrak{E}
, at least one of which is bounded. If
\phi(x_{n},y_{n})\to 0
, then
\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|\to 0
.

A retraction
R_{D}:\mathfrak{E}\to D
is
sunny
if

R_{D}\bigl(R_{D}x+t(x-R_{D}x)\bigr)=R_{D}x

whenever
x\in\mathfrak{E}
,
t\geq 0
, and the displayed argument belongs to the domain. It is
generalized nonexpansive
if

\phi(R_{D}x,R_{D}y)\leq\phi(x,y),\qquad x,y\in\mathfrak{E}.

Lemma 2.2

(
[
19
,
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be smooth and strictly convex, let
D\subset\mathfrak{E}
be a nonempty closed sunny generalized nonexpansive retract, and let
R_{D}
be its sunny generalized nonexpansive retraction. Then

(2.2)

\phi(x,R_{D}x)+\phi(R_{D}x,z)\leq\phi(x,z),\qquad x\in\mathfrak{E},\ z\in D.

Moreover,
R_{D}x
is the unique minimizer of
z\mapsto\phi(x,z)
over
D
.

Lemma 2.3

(
[
23
]
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be smooth, strictly convex, and reflexive, and let
D\subset\mathfrak{E}
be nonempty and closed. Then
D
is a sunny generalized nonexpansive retract of
\mathfrak{E}
if and only if
JD
is closed and convex in
\mathfrak{E}^{*}
.

Following Ibaraki and Takahashi
[
19
]
, a mapping
T:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}
is called
generalized nonexpansive
if
\operatorname{Fix}(T)\neq\varnothing
and

\phi(Tx,p)\leq\phi(x,p),\qquad x\in\mathfrak{E},\ p\in\operatorname{Fix}(T).

For a family
\Gamma
of mappings, write

F(\Gamma):=\bigcap_{T\in\Gamma}\operatorname{Fix}(T).

The following consequence of fixed-set results for generalized nonexpansive mappings will be useful.

Lemma 2.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be smooth, strictly convex, and reflexive. Let
\Gamma
be a family of generalized nonexpansive mappings on
\mathfrak{E}
such that
F(\Gamma)\neq\varnothing
. Then
F(\Gamma)
is a sunny generalized nonexpansive retract of
\mathfrak{E}
.

Proof.

For each
T\in\Gamma
, the set
\operatorname{Fix}(T)
is closed and
J\operatorname{Fix}(T)
is closed and convex; see Ibaraki and Takahashi
[
19
]
. Since
J
is one-to-one,

JF(\Gamma)=\bigcap_{T\in\Gamma}J\operatorname{Fix}(T).

Indeed, let
x^{*}\in\bigcap_{T\in\Gamma}J\operatorname{Fix}(T)
. For each
T\in\Gamma
, choose
p_{T}\in\operatorname{Fix}(T)
with
Jp_{T}=x^{*}
. Since
J
is one-to-one, all the points
p_{T}
coincide with a single point
p\in F(\Gamma)
, and hence
x^{*}=Jp\in JF(\Gamma)
. Thus
JF(\Gamma)
is closed and convex. The set
F(\Gamma)
is closed, and Lemma
2.3
applies.
∎

Definition 2.5

(NST-condition; Klin-eam–Suantai–Takahashi
[
21
]
)
.

Let
\{T_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
and
\Gamma
be families of mappings on
\mathfrak{E}
such that

\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{k})=F(\Gamma)\neq\varnothing.

The sequence
\{T_{k}\}
satisfies the
NST-condition with
\Gamma
if, for every bounded sequence
\{x_{k}\}\subset\mathfrak{E}
,

\|x_{k}-T_{k}x_{k}\|\to 0\quad\Longrightarrow\quad\|x_{k}-Tx_{k}\|\to 0\quad\text{for every }T\in\Gamma.

A mapping
T
is
closed
if its graph is closed in
\mathfrak{E}\times\mathfrak{E}
.

3.
Inertial hybrid retraction theorem

Definition 3.1
.

Let
F\subset\mathfrak{E}
be nonempty. A sequence
\{T_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
of mappings
T_{k}:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}
has the
vanishing-error
\phi
-Fejér property with respect to
F
if there is a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}\subset[0,\infty)
such that
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
and

(3.1)

\phi(T_{k}x,p)\leq\phi(x,p)+\varepsilon_{k},\qquad x\in\mathfrak{E},\ p\in F,\ k\geq 1.

In particular, whenever (
3.1
) holds with a nonnegative
summable error sequence, the requirement
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
is automatically
satisfied.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be a uniformly smooth and uniformly convex real Banach space. Let
\{T_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be mappings from
\mathfrak{E}
into itself, and let
\Gamma
be a family of closed generalized nonexpansive mappings on
\mathfrak{E}
such that

\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{k})=F(\Gamma)\neq\varnothing.

Assume that
\{T_{k}\}
satisfies the NST-condition with
\Gamma
and the vanishing-error
\phi
-Fejér property with respect to
F(\Gamma)
. Let
0\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\overline{\alpha}<1
for every
k
, and let
\{\beta_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
be bounded. Given
v_{0}\in\mathfrak{E}
, set
v_{1}=v_{0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\mathfrak{E}
. For
k\geq 1
, define

(3.2)

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle=v_{k}+\beta_{k}(v_{k}-v_{k-1}),

\displaystyle y_{k}

\displaystyle=\alpha_{k}w_{k}+(1-\alpha_{k})T_{k}w_{k},

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}:\phi(y_{k},v)\leq\phi(w_{k},v)+(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}\bigr\},

\displaystyle v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=R_{\mathscr{C}_{k+1}}v_{0}.

Then every
\mathscr{C}_{k}
is a nonempty closed sunny generalized nonexpansive retract of
\mathfrak{E}
, so the iteration is well defined. Moreover,

v_{k}\longrightarrow R_{F(\Gamma)}v_{0}

strongly.

Proof.

Set
F:=F(\Gamma)
. By Lemma
2.4
,
F
is a sunny generalized nonexpansive retract.

Step 1: construction of the shrinking retracts.

We prove inductively that

(3.3)

F\subset\mathscr{C}_{k},\qquad\mathscr{C}_{k}\text{ is closed},\qquad J\mathscr{C}_{k}\text{ is closed and convex}.

The assertions hold for
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\mathfrak{E}
. Indeed, uniform smoothness and uniform convexity imply that
\mathfrak{E}
is smooth, strictly convex, and reflexive. Hence
J:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}^{*}
is bijective and
J\mathscr{C}_{1}=J\mathfrak{E}=\mathfrak{E}^{*}
.

Assume (
3.3
) at index
k
. Put

\delta_{k}=(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k},\qquad c_{k}=\frac{\|w_{k}\|^{2}-\|y_{k}\|^{2}+\delta_{k}}{2}.

Expanding
\phi
shows that, for
v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
,

(3.4)

\phi(y_{k},v)\leq\phi(w_{k},v)+\delta_{k}\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\langle w_{k}-y_{k},Jv\rangle\leq c_{k}.

Thus

(3.5)

\mathscr{C}_{k+1}=\{v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}:\langle w_{k}-y_{k},Jv\rangle\leq c_{k}\}.

Uniform smoothness implies that
J
is norm-to-norm continuous on bounded subsets. To verify closedness, let
v_{n}\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
and suppose that
v_{n}\to v
. Then
v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
because
\mathscr{C}_{k}
is closed. The sequence
\{v_{n}\}
is bounded, so
Jv_{n}\to Jv
. Consequently, continuity of the duality pairing yields

\langle w_{k}-y_{k},Jv\rangle=\lim_{n\to\infty}\langle w_{k}-y_{k},Jv_{n}\rangle\leq c_{k}.

Hence
v\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
, and
\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
is closed.

Let
p\in F
. By the induction hypothesis,
p\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
. Since
x\mapsto\phi(x,p)
is convex, (
3.1
) gives

\displaystyle\phi(y_{k},p)

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}\phi(w_{k},p)+(1-\alpha_{k})\phi(T_{k}w_{k},p)

\displaystyle\leq\phi(w_{k},p)+(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}.

Hence
p\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
, and therefore
F\subset\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
.

Define the dual halfspace

H_{k}^{*}=\{x^{*}\in\mathfrak{E}^{*}:\langle w_{k}-y_{k},x^{*}\rangle\leq c_{k}\}.

Because
J
is bijective, (
3.5
) yields the exact identity

(3.6)

J\mathscr{C}_{k+1}=J\mathscr{C}_{k}\cap H_{k}^{*}.

The right-hand side is closed and convex. Lemma
2.3
therefore shows that
\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
is a sunny generalized nonexpansive retract.

Step 2: boundedness and the nested-set estimate.

Fix
p\in F
. Since
p\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
and
v_{k}=R_{\mathscr{C}_{k}}v_{0}
, Lemma
2.2
gives

(3.7)

\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\leq\phi(v_{0},p),\qquad k\geq 1.

By (
2.1
),
\{v_{k}\}
is bounded. Since
\mathscr{C}_{k+1}\subset\mathscr{C}_{k}
and
v_{k+1}\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
, applying (
2.2
) to
R_{\mathscr{C}_{k}}v_{0}=v_{k}
with
z=v_{k+1}
gives

\phi(v_{0},v_{k})+\phi(v_{k},v_{k+1})\leq\phi(v_{0},v_{k+1}).

In particular,
\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\leq\phi(v_{0},v_{k+1})
.
Thus
\{\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\}
is nondecreasing and bounded. Let

\ell=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi(v_{0},v_{k}).

If
m>n
, then
v_{m}\in\mathscr{C}_{m}\subset\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and (
2.2
) gives

(3.8)

\phi(v_{n},v_{m})\leq\phi(v_{0},v_{m})-\phi(v_{0},v_{n}).

Step 3: convergence and residual decay.

We now prove that
\{v_{k}\}
is Cauchy. If not, there would exist
\rho>0
and indices
m_{j}>n_{j}\to\infty
such that
\|v_{m_{j}}-v_{n_{j}}\|\geq\rho
for all
j
. Since
\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\to\ell
, (
3.8
) gives

0\leq\phi(v_{n_{j}},v_{m_{j}})\leq\phi(v_{0},v_{m_{j}})-\phi(v_{0},v_{n_{j}})\longrightarrow 0.

The sequence
\{v_{k}\}
is bounded, so Lemma
2.1
yields
\|v_{m_{j}}-v_{n_{j}}\|\to 0
, a contradiction. Thus
\{v_{k}\}
is Cauchy. Since
\mathfrak{E}
is complete,

(3.9)

v_{k}\to q

for some
q\in\mathfrak{E}
. For each fixed
n
, one has
v_{k}\in\mathscr{C}_{k}\subset\mathscr{C}_{n}
whenever
k\geq n
. Since
\mathscr{C}_{n}
is closed,
q\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
. Hence
q\in\bigcap_{n\geq 1}\mathscr{C}_{n}
.

Let
B=\sup_{k}|\beta_{k}|<\infty
. From (
3.9
),

(3.10)

\|w_{k}-v_{k}\|\leq B\|v_{k}-v_{k-1}\|\to 0,\qquad\text{so }w_{k}\to q.

Because
v_{k+1}\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
,

0\leq\phi(y_{k},v_{k+1})\leq\phi(w_{k},v_{k+1})+(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}.

The convergences
w_{k}\to q
and
v_{k+1}\to q
, together with norm-to-norm continuity of
J
on bounded sets, imply
\phi(w_{k},v_{k+1})\to\phi(q,q)=0
. Also,
0\leq(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}\leq\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
. Thus the right-hand side tends to zero. Lemma
2.1
gives

\|y_{k}-v_{k+1}\|\to 0.

Moreover,

\|v_{k+1}-w_{k}\|\leq\|v_{k+1}-q\|+\|w_{k}-q\|\to 0.

Hence
\|y_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0
. Since

y_{k}-w_{k}=(1-\alpha_{k})(T_{k}w_{k}-w_{k})

and
1-\alpha_{k}\geq 1-\overline{\alpha}>0
, we obtain

(3.11)

\|T_{k}w_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0.

Step 4: NST-transfer and
q\in F
.

The sequence
\{w_{k}\}
is bounded. By the NST-condition and (
3.11
),

\|Tw_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0,\qquad T\in\Gamma.

Fix
T\in\Gamma
. Since
w_{k}\to q
and
\|Tw_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0
, we have
Tw_{k}\to q
. Therefore
(w_{k},Tw_{k})\to(q,q)
, and closedness of the graph of
T
yields
Tq=q
. Hence
q\in F
.

Step 5: identification of the limit.

Let
p\in F
. Since
v_{k}\to q
and
Jv_{k}\to Jq
by the
norm-to-norm continuity of
J
on bounded sets, one has

\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\to\phi(v_{0},q).

Thus (
3.7
) implies

\phi(v_{0},q)=\lim_{k\to\infty}\phi(v_{0},v_{k})\leq\phi(v_{0},p).

Thus
q
minimizes
z\mapsto\phi(v_{0},z)
over
F
. Lemma
2.2
gives
q=R_{F}v_{0}
.
∎

4.
A Bregman-projection analogue

Let
f:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathbb{R}
be convex and Gâteaux differentiable. Its Bregman distance is

D_{f}(x,y)=f(x)-f(y)-\langle x-y,\nabla f(y)\rangle.

We use the standard projection orientation

P_{D}^{f}x=\operatorname*{argmin}_{z\in D}D_{f}(z,x).

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be reflexive. A function
f:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathbb{R}
is called an
admissible Bregman generator
if

(1)

f
is Legendre and strongly coercive;

(2)

f
is bounded and uniformly Fréchet differentiable on bounded subsets of
\mathfrak{E}
;

(3)

f
is uniformly convex on bounded subsets of
\mathfrak{E}
;

(4)

for every
x\in\mathfrak{E}
and
r>0
, the right Bregman sublevel set

\{y\in\mathfrak{E}:D_{f}(x,y)\leq r\}

is bounded.

The following lemma gives consequences of Legendre duality,
Bregman projection theory, and total convexity; see

[
2
,
4
,
8
,
9
,
10
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
f
be an admissible Bregman generator. Then:

(1)

\nabla f:\mathfrak{E}\to\mathfrak{E}^{*}
is a bijection,

\nabla f^{*}=(\nabla f)^{-1}.

Both gradients are bounded and uniformly norm-to-norm continuous on
bounded subsets of their domains; see

[
4
,
8
]
.

(2)

If
D\subset\mathfrak{E}
is nonempty, closed, and convex, then the Bregman
projection

P_{D}^{f}x=\operatorname*{argmin}_{z\in D}D_{f}(z,x)

exists uniquely and satisfies

(4.1)

D_{f}(z,P_{D}^{f}x)+D_{f}(P_{D}^{f}x,x)\leq D_{f}(z,x),\qquad z\in D;

see

[
2
,
4
,
10
]
.

(3)

For bounded sequences
\{x_{n}\}
and
\{y_{n}\}
,

(4.2)

D_{f}(x_{n},y_{n})\to 0\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\|x_{n}-y_{n}\|\to 0.

The implication from vanishing Bregman distance to norm convergence
follows from total convexity on bounded sets; see

[
9
,
10
]
.
The converse follows from the uniform Fréchet differentiability of

f
on bounded subsets of
\mathfrak{E}
.

For
x\in\mathfrak{E}
and
x^{*}\in\mathfrak{E}^{*}
, define

V_{f}(x,x^{*})=f(x)-\langle x,x^{*}\rangle+f^{*}(x^{*}).

Then

(4.3)

V_{f}(x,x^{*})=D_{f}\bigl(x,\nabla f^{*}(x^{*})\bigr),

and
x^{*}\mapsto V_{f}(x,x^{*})
is convex; see, for example,

[
4
,
8
]
.

Definition 4.3
.

Let
F\subset\mathfrak{E}
be nonempty. A sequence
\{T_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
has the
vanishing-error
D_{f}
-Fejér property with respect to
F
if there is a sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}\subset[0,\infty)
with
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
such that

(4.4)

D_{f}(p,T_{k}x)\leq D_{f}(p,x)+\varepsilon_{k},\qquad x\in\mathfrak{E},\ p\in F,\ k\geq 1.

In particular, whenever (
4.4
) holds with a nonnegative summable error sequence, the requirement
\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
is automatically satisfied.

Related shrinking-projection results for Bregman asymptotically
quasi-nonexpansive mappings in the intermediate sense were obtained in

[
28
]
, and an inertial hybrid Bregman scheme for a
variational-like system and a single asymptotic fixed-point mapping was studied
in
[
3
]
. The result below differs in that the residuals are
transferred to a countable family through the NST-condition and the additive
errors are required only to vanish.

Theorem 4.4
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}
be a reflexive real Banach space and let
f
be an admissible Bregman generator. Let
\{T_{k}\}_{k\geq 1}
be mappings from
\mathfrak{E}
into itself, and let
\Gamma
be a family of closed mappings such that

\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{k})=F(\Gamma)\neq\varnothing.

Assume that
F(\Gamma)
is closed and convex, that
\{T_{k}\}
satisfies the NST-condition with
\Gamma
, and that (
4.4
) holds with
F=F(\Gamma)
. Let
0\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\overline{\alpha}<1
for every
k
, and let
\{\beta_{k}\}\subset\mathbb{R}
be bounded. Given
v_{0}\in\mathfrak{E}
, set
v_{1}=v_{0}
and
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\mathfrak{E}
. Define

(4.5)

\displaystyle w_{k}

\displaystyle=v_{k}+\beta_{k}(v_{k}-v_{k-1}),

\displaystyle y_{k}

\displaystyle=\nabla f^{*}\!\left(\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w_{k})+(1-\alpha_{k})\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k})\right),

\displaystyle\mathscr{C}_{k+1}

\displaystyle=\bigl\{v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}:D_{f}(v,y_{k})\leq D_{f}(v,w_{k})+(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}\bigr\},

\displaystyle v_{k+1}

\displaystyle=P_{\mathscr{C}_{k+1}}^{f}v_{0}.

Then every
\mathscr{C}_{k}
is nonempty, closed, and convex, the iteration is well defined, and

v_{k}\longrightarrow P_{F(\Gamma)}^{f}v_{0}

strongly.

Proof.

Set
F:=F(\Gamma)
and
\delta_{k}=(1-\alpha_{k})\varepsilon_{k}
.

Step 1: the cuts are closed, convex, and contain
F
.

The assertion holds at
k=1
because
\mathscr{C}_{1}=\mathfrak{E}
is nonempty, closed, and convex, and
F\subset\mathscr{C}_{1}
. Suppose inductively that
\mathscr{C}_{k}
is nonempty, closed, and convex and that
F\subset\mathscr{C}_{k}
. For
v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}
, expansion of the two Bregman distances gives

\displaystyle D_{f}(v,y_{k})-D_{f}(v,w_{k})

\displaystyle=\langle v,\nabla f(w_{k})-\nabla f(y_{k})\rangle

\displaystyle\quad+f(w_{k})-f(y_{k})+\langle y_{k},\nabla f(y_{k})\rangle-\langle w_{k},\nabla f(w_{k})\rangle.

Hence

(4.6)

\mathscr{C}_{k+1}=\{v\in\mathscr{C}_{k}:\langle v,\nabla f(w_{k})-\nabla f(y_{k})\rangle\leq d_{k}\},

where

d_{k}=\delta_{k}+f(y_{k})-f(w_{k})+\langle w_{k},\nabla f(w_{k})\rangle-\langle y_{k},\nabla f(y_{k})\rangle.

Thus
\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
is closed and convex.

Since
\nabla f^{*}=(\nabla f)^{-1}
, the definition of
y_{k}
gives

\nabla f(y_{k})=\alpha_{k}\nabla f(w_{k})+(1-\alpha_{k})\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k}).

Let
p\in F
. Using (
4.3
), convexity of
V_{f}(p,\cdot)
, and (
4.4
),

\displaystyle D_{f}(p,y_{k})

\displaystyle\leq\alpha_{k}D_{f}(p,w_{k})+(1-\alpha_{k})D_{f}(p,T_{k}w_{k})

\displaystyle\leq D_{f}(p,w_{k})+\delta_{k}.

Therefore
p\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}
. Induction shows that all cuts are nonempty, closed, and convex.

Step 2: boundedness and the nested-set estimate.

Fix
p\in F
. The Bregman projection inequality gives

(4.7)

D_{f}(p,v_{k})+D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\leq D_{f}(p,v_{0}).

The map
z\mapsto D_{f}(z,v_{0})=f(z)-\langle z,\nabla f(v_{0})\rangle+f^{*}(\nabla f(v_{0}))
is coercive. Hence the boundedness of
\{D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\}
in (
4.7
) implies that
\{v_{k}\}
is bounded. Since
v_{k+1}\in\mathscr{C}_{k+1}\subset\mathscr{C}_{k}
,

D_{f}(v_{k+1},v_{k})+D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\leq D_{f}(v_{k+1},v_{0}).

Thus
\{D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\}
is nondecreasing and bounded. If
m>n
, then
v_{m}\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and

(4.8)

D_{f}(v_{m},v_{n})\leq D_{f}(v_{m},v_{0})-D_{f}(v_{n},v_{0}).

Step 3: convergence and residual decay.

Let
a_{k}=D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})
. The preceding argument shows that
a_{k}
converges. If
\{v_{k}\}
were not Cauchy, there would exist
\rho>0
and indices
m_{j}>n_{j}\to\infty
such that
\|v_{m_{j}}-v_{n_{j}}\|\geq\rho
. By (
4.8
),

0\leq D_{f}(v_{m_{j}},v_{n_{j}})\leq a_{m_{j}}-a_{n_{j}}\longrightarrow 0.

Both subsequences are bounded, so (
4.2
) gives
\|v_{m_{j}}-v_{n_{j}}\|\to 0
, a contradiction. Thus
\{v_{k}\}
is Cauchy; write
v_{k}\to q
. For each fixed
n
,
v_{k}\in\mathscr{C}_{k}\subset\mathscr{C}_{n}
for all
k\geq n
. Since
\mathscr{C}_{n}
is closed,
q\in\mathscr{C}_{n}
, and hence
q\in\bigcap_{n}\mathscr{C}_{n}
. Boundedness of
\{\beta_{k}\}
yields
w_{k}\to q
.

We next show that the auxiliary sequences are bounded. Since
\{w_{k}\}
is bounded and both
f
and
\nabla f
are bounded on bounded sets, the identity

D_{f}(p,w_{k})=f(p)-f(w_{k})-\langle p-w_{k},\nabla f(w_{k})\rangle

shows that
\{D_{f}(p,w_{k})\}
is bounded. Equation (
4.4
), together with boundedness of the convergent sequence
\{\varepsilon_{k}\}
, then shows that
\{D_{f}(p,T_{k}w_{k})\}
is bounded. The right-sublevel assumption in Definition
4.1
implies that
\{T_{k}w_{k}\}
is bounded. Consequently,
\{\nabla f(w_{k})\}
and
\{\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k})\}
are bounded in
\mathfrak{E}^{*}
. Their convex combinations are bounded, and boundedness of
\nabla f^{*}
on bounded subsets of
\mathfrak{E}^{*}
gives boundedness of
\{y_{k}\}
.

Since
v_{k+1}-w_{k}\to 0
, (
4.2
) gives
D_{f}(v_{k+1},w_{k})\to 0
. The cut inequality gives

0\leq D_{f}(v_{k+1},y_{k})\leq D_{f}(v_{k+1},w_{k})+\delta_{k}\to 0.

Hence
\|v_{k+1}-y_{k}\|\to 0
, and therefore
\|y_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0
. Uniform continuity of
\nabla f
on bounded sets yields

\|\nabla f(y_{k})-\nabla f(w_{k})\|\to 0.

By the definition of
y_{k}
,

\nabla f(y_{k})-\nabla f(w_{k})=(1-\alpha_{k})\bigl(\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k})-\nabla f(w_{k})\bigr).

Since
1-\alpha_{k}\geq 1-\overline{\alpha}>0
, it follows that

\|\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k})-\nabla f(w_{k})\|\to 0.

The two gradient sequences are bounded. Hence, by the uniform
norm-to-norm continuity of
\nabla f^{*}
on a bounded set containing them,

\displaystyle\|T_{k}w_{k}-w_{k}\|

\displaystyle=\bigl\|\nabla f^{*}(\nabla f(T_{k}w_{k}))-\nabla f^{*}(\nabla f(w_{k}))\bigr\|\longrightarrow 0.

Thus

(4.9)

\|T_{k}w_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0.

Step 4: membership in
F
.

By the NST-condition and (
4.9
),

\|Tw_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0,\qquad T\in\Gamma.

Since
w_{k}\to q
and
\|Tw_{k}-w_{k}\|\to 0
, one has
Tw_{k}\to q
. Closedness of the graph of
T
therefore gives
Tq=q
. Thus
q\in F
.

Step 5: identification of the limit.

From (
4.7
),

D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\leq D_{f}(p,v_{0}),\qquad p\in F.

Since
z\mapsto D_{f}(z,v_{0})
is continuous and
v_{k}\to q
,

D_{f}(q,v_{0})=\lim_{k\to\infty}D_{f}(v_{k},v_{0})\leq D_{f}(p,v_{0}),\qquad p\in F.

Thus
q
is the unique minimizer of
z\mapsto D_{f}(z,v_{0})
over
F
, and hence
q=P_{F}^{f}v_{0}
.
∎

Remark 4.5

(Why the two theorems have different cuts)
.

For
\psi(x)=\frac{1}{2}\|x\|^{2}
on a smooth Banach space,

D_{\psi}(x,y)=\frac{1}{2}\phi(x,y).

Nevertheless, Theorem
3.2
minimizes
D_{\psi}(v_{0},\cdot)
,
whereas the standard Bregman projection minimizes
D_{f}(\cdot,v_{0})
. The
asymmetry is structural. In particular,

D_{f}(a,v)-D_{f}(b,v)=f(a)-f(b)+\langle b-a,\nabla f(v)\rangle,

so a cut with the variable in the second argument need not be convex. The
left-oriented cut in (
4.5
) is affine in
v
, as shown
by (
4.6
).

5.
Illustrative examples

5.1.
A nonzero vanishing-error family on the real line

Proposition 5.1
.

Let
\mathfrak{E}=\mathbb{R}
, let
\eta_{k}>0
be pairwise distinct with
\eta_{k}\to 0
, and define

Sx=\frac{x}{1+|x|},\qquad T_{k}x=(1+\eta_{k})Sx.

Set
\Gamma=\{S\}
. Then the operator-family assumptions of
Theorem
3.2
are satisfied with

F(\Gamma)=\{0\},\qquad\varepsilon_{k}=2\eta_{k}+\eta_{k}^{2}.

Consequently, for every choice of parameters satisfying

0\leq\alpha_{k}\leq\alpha<1\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{k}|\beta_{k}|<\infty,

the sequence generated by (
3.2
) converges strongly to
0
.

Proof.

Here
J=\operatorname{Id}
and
\phi(x,y)=|x-y|^{2}
. The mapping
S
is continuous,

\operatorname{Fix}(S)=\{0\}
, and
|Sx|\leq|x|
, so
S
is generalized nonexpansive.
Solving
T_{k}x=x
gives

\operatorname{Fix}(T_{k})=\{0,-\eta_{k},\eta_{k}\}.

The pairwise distinctness of
\eta_{k}
therefore yields

\bigcap_{k=1}^{\infty}\operatorname{Fix}(T_{k})=\{0\}=\operatorname{Fix}(S).

Moreover,

|T_{k}x-Sx|=\eta_{k}|Sx|\leq\eta_{k}.

Thus, for every sequence
\{x_{k}\}
,

|x_{k}-Sx_{k}|\leq|x_{k}-T_{k}x_{k}|+\eta_{k},

which proves the NST-condition with
\Gamma
.

Finally,
|Sx|\leq 1
and
|Sx|\leq|x|
, so

\displaystyle|T_{k}x|^{2}

\displaystyle=(1+\eta_{k})^{2}|Sx|^{2}

\displaystyle=|Sx|^{2}+(2\eta_{k}+\eta_{k}^{2})|Sx|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq|x|^{2}+(2\eta_{k}+\eta_{k}^{2}).

This is (
3.1
) with
F=\{0\}
. Since

\varepsilon_{k}\to 0
, Theorem
3.2
applies. In particular, choosing

\eta_{k}=1/k
gives

\varepsilon_{k}=\frac{2}{k}+\frac{1}{k^{2}}\longrightarrow 0,\qquad\sum_{k=1}^{\infty}\varepsilon_{k}=\infty.

∎

5.2.
A power-type Bregman generator

Power-type Legendre generators and their Bregman projections are standard in
Bregman convex analysis and feasibility theory; see

[
4
,
8
,
9
]
. Let
d,m\in\mathbb{N}
, let
1<r<\infty
, and set

s=r/(r-1)
. Equip
\mathbb{R}^{d}
with the
\ell^{r}
norm and its standard duality
with
\ell^{s}
. Define

(5.1)

f_{r}(x)=\frac{1}{r}\sum_{j=1}^{d}|x_{j}|^{r}.

Then

(\nabla f_{r}(x))_{j}=|x_{j}|^{r-2}x_{j},\qquad(\nabla f_{r}^{*}(x^{*}))_{j}=|x_{j}^{*}|^{s-2}x_{j}^{*}.

Lemma 5.2
.

The function
f_{r}
in (
5.1
) is an admissible Bregman
generator.

Proof.

The Legendre, strong-coercivity, smoothness, and uniform-convexity properties
are standard for the finite-dimensional power function; see

[
4
,
8
,
9
]
. It remains only to give the right-sublevel
condition used in Definition
4.1
. Fenchel’s identity
gives

D_{f_{r}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{r}\|x\|_{r}^{r}+\frac{1}{s}\|y\|_{r}^{r}-\langle x,\nabla f_{r}(y)\rangle.

Since
\|\nabla f_{r}(y)\|_{s}=\|y\|_{r}^{r-1}
, Hölder’s inequality yields

D_{f_{r}}(x,y)\geq\frac{1}{s}\|y\|_{r}^{r}-\|x\|_{r}\|y\|_{r}^{r-1}+\frac{1}{r}\|x\|_{r}^{r}.

For fixed
x
, the right-hand side tends to
+\infty
as

\|y\|_{r}\to\infty
. Hence every right Bregman sublevel set

\{y:D_{f_{r}}(x,y)\leq a\}
is bounded, and
f_{r}
is admissible.
∎

5.3.
An averaged Bregman projection operator

Averaged Bregman projection constructions are classical in convex feasibility
and are closely related to iterative averaging of entropic projections and
convex combinations of Bregman nonexpansive-type operators; see

[
7
,
24
]
. Let

\mathscr{C}_{1},\ldots,\mathscr{C}_{m}\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be nonempty closed convex sets with

\mathscr{C}:=\bigcap_{i=1}^{m}\mathscr{C}_{i}\neq\varnothing.

Let
\lambda_{i}>0
and
\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}=1
. Write

P_{i}=P_{\mathscr{C}_{i}}^{f_{r}}
and define

(5.2)

Tx=\nabla f_{r}^{*}\!\left(\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}\nabla f_{r}(P_{i}x)\right).

Proposition 5.3
.

The mapping
T
is continuous,

\operatorname{Fix}(T)=\mathscr{C},

and, for every
p\in\mathscr{C}
,

(5.3)

D_{f_{r}}(p,Tx)\leq D_{f_{r}}(p,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}D_{f_{r}}(P_{i}x,x).

Consequently, Theorem
4.4
applies with
T_{k}=T
,

\Gamma=\{T\}
, and
\varepsilon_{k}=0
, and its iterates converge to

P_{\mathscr{C}}^{f_{r}}v_{0}
.

Proof.

We first verify continuity. Fix
i
, let
x_{n}\to x
, and set

z_{n}=P_{i}x_{n}
. Choose
a\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
. By the minimizing property of
P_{i}
,

D_{f_{r}}(z_{n},x_{n})\leq D_{f_{r}}(a,x_{n}).

The right-hand side is bounded. Moreover, with

M:=\sup_{n}\|\nabla f_{r}(x_{n})\|_{s}<\infty
, one has

D_{f_{r}}(z,x_{n})\geq\frac{1}{r}\|z\|_{r}^{r}-M\|z\|_{r},

so the left-hand sublevel sets are bounded uniformly in
n
. Hence

\{z_{n}\}
is bounded. If a subsequence
z_{n_{j}}\to z
, then

z\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
, and continuity of
D_{f_{r}}
gives, for every
y\in\mathscr{C}_{i}
,

D_{f_{r}}(z,x)=\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{f_{r}}(z_{n_{j}},x_{n_{j}})\leq\lim_{j\to\infty}D_{f_{r}}(y,x_{n_{j}})=D_{f_{r}}(y,x).

Uniqueness of the Bregman projection yields
z=P_{i}x
. Thus every
subsequence has a further subsequence converging to
P_{i}x
, and therefore

P_{i}x_{n}\to P_{i}x
. Hence every
P_{i}
, and consequently
T
, is continuous.

Let
p\in\mathscr{C}
. By convexity of
V_{f_{r}}(p,\cdot)
and the Bregman
projection inequality,

\displaystyle D_{f_{r}}(p,Tx)

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}D_{f_{r}}(p,P_{i}x)

\displaystyle\leq D_{f_{r}}(p,x)-\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}D_{f_{r}}(P_{i}x,x),

which proves (
5.3
). If
x\in\mathscr{C}
, then

P_{i}x=x
for every
i
, and hence
Tx=x
. Conversely, if
Tx=x
, then
(
5.3
) gives

\sum_{i=1}^{m}\lambda_{i}D_{f_{r}}(P_{i}x,x)=0.

Every term is nonnegative and every
\lambda_{i}
is positive, so

D_{f_{r}}(P_{i}x,x)=0
for all
i
. Strict convexity of
f_{r}
implies

P_{i}x=x
, and therefore
x\in\mathscr{C}
. Thus
\operatorname{Fix}(T)=\mathscr{C}
.

Since
T
is continuous, it is closed. For the constant sequence
T_{k}=T
,
the NST-condition with
\Gamma=\{T\}
is immediate, and
(
5.3
) implies the vanishing-error condition with

\varepsilon_{k}=0
. Therefore Theorem
4.4
applies.
∎
