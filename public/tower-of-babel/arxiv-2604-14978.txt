1.
Introduction

A classical theorem of Dirac
[
5
]
states that every graph
G
on
n\geq 3
vertices with minimum degree
\delta(G)\geq n/2
contains a Hamilton cycle. In fact, once this degree threshold is reached, the graph typically contains many Hamilton cycles. This observation motivated a line of research focused on counting Hamilton cycles in graphs and on extending such results to hypergraphs.

We denote the number of Hamilton cycles in a graph
G
by
\Psi(G)
and the number of perfect matchings by
\Phi(G)
. In a seminal work, Cuckler and Kahn
[
3
,
4
]
established asymptotically tight bounds on
\Psi(G)
and
\Phi(G)
in terms of a suitably defined notion of graph entropy.

To this end, for a
k
-uniform graph (
k
-graph)
G
on
n
vertices, let a
perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}
be a function
\mathbf{x}:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}(e)=1
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. The
entropy of
\mathbf{x}
is

h(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}(e)\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}(e)}.

The
entropy of the
k
-graph
G
is then
h(G)\coloneqq\sup\{h(\mathbf{x})\colon\mathbf{x}\text{ is a perfect fractional matching of }G\}
.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
3
,
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq n/2
. Then

\log\Psi(G)=2h(G)-n\log e-o(n).

If
n
is even, then

\log\Phi(G)=h(G)-\frac{n}{2}\log e-o(n).

In the same work, Cuckler and Kahn also proved a tight lower bound on
h(G)
in terms of the minimum degree. Combined with the above theorem, this yields the following result.

Theorem 1.2

(
[
4
]
)
.

Let
G
be a graph on
n
vertices with
\delta(G)\geq\delta n\geq n/2
. Then

\Psi(G)\geq\left(\delta+o(1)\right)^{n}n!.

Observe that this is asymptotically tight for the binomial random graph. These entropy-based results for graphs naturally raise the question of how hamiltonicity and its enumeration behave in the richer setting of hypergraphs. We begin by carefully defining the relevant notions of cycles in hypergraphs. Let
\ell
be an integer such that
0\leq\ell\leq k-1
. A
k
-graph
C
is an
\ell
-cycle
if there exists a cyclic ordering of its vertex set
V(C)
such that every edge of
C
consists of
k
consecutive vertices, and every pair of consecutive edges intersects in precisely
\ell
vertices. A
Hamilton
\ell
-cycle
in a
k
-graph
G
is an
\ell
-cycle
C
with
V(C)=V(G)
. A
k
-graph
G
is
hamiltonian
if it contains a Hamilton
(k-1)
-cycle, which is also referred to as a
tight Hamilton cycle
. A
perfect matching
of
G
is a Hamilton 0-cycle where the vertex ordering is ignored; equivalently, it is a set of pairwise disjoint edges that covers the vertex set of
G
.

Rödl, Ruciński and Szemerédi
[
13
,
14
]
generalized Dirac’s theorem by proving an asymptotic version for
k
-graphs, thereby establishing a threshold for the existence of Hamilton
\ell
-cycles.

Theorem 1.3

(
[
13
,
14
]
)
.

Let
k\geq 3
,
\delta>1/2
, and let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices, where
n
is sufficiently large. If the minimum
(k-1)
-degree satisfies
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
, then
G
is hamiltonian.

As in the graph setting, we use
\Psi(G)
and
\Phi(G)
to denote the number of tight Hamilton cycles and perfect matchings, respectively, in a
k
-graph
G
. Let
K_{n}^{(k)}
denote the complete
k
-graph on
n
vertices. Recently, Kwan, Safavi, and Wang
[
12
]
generalized the lower bounds on the number of perfect matchings in Theorems
1.1
and
1.2
from graphs to hypergraphs.

Theorem 1.4

(
[
12
]
)
.

Fix
k\geq 2
and
\delta>1/2
. Let
G
be an
n
-vertex
k
-graph with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
and
k
divides
n
. Then

\log\Phi(G)=h(G)-\frac{k-1}{k}n\log e+o(n)\quad\text{and}\quad\Phi(G)\geq\Phi(K^{(k)}_{n})\cdot(\delta+o(1))^{n/k}.

Let
\Psi_{\ell}(G)
denote the number of Hamilton
\ell
-cycles in a
k
-graph
G
. Observe that the expected number of Hamilton
\ell
-cycles in a random
k
-graph on
n
vertices, in which every edge appears independently with probability
p
, is
\Psi_{\ell}(K_{n}^{(k)})\cdot p^{\frac{n}{k-\ell}}
. In particular, for tight Hamilton cycles we have
\Psi(K_{n}^{(k)})=\frac{n!}{2n}
. For
0\leq\ell\leq k-2
, Ferber, Krivelevich and Sudakov
[
7
]
, as well as Ferber, Hardiman and Mond
[
6
]
, showed that this estimate using binomial random
k
-graphs in fact provides a correct lower bound once
p
is replaced by a parameter depending on the minimum
(k-1)
-degree. This extends Theorem
1.2
to hypergraphs.

Theorem 1.5

(
[
6
,
7
]
)
.

Let
0\leq\ell\leq k-2
and
\delta>1/2
. Let
n
be sufficiently large and divisible by
k-\ell
. Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
-vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
. Then the number of Hamilton
\ell
-cycles in
G
is at least
(1-o(1))^{n}\cdot\Psi_{\ell}(K_{n}^{(k)})\cdot\delta^{\frac{n}{k-\ell}}
.

Futhermore, Ferber, Hardiman and Mond
[
6
]
conjectured that this result extends to
\ell=k-1
, that is, to tight Hamilton cycles. This is a stronger form of Conjecture 7.1 by Glock, Gould, Joos, Kühn and Osthus
[
8
]
, where the authors also asked whether the entropy approach of Cuckler and Kahn can be generalized to hypergraphs.

In this paper, we answer this question affirmatively by establishing a tight lower bound for
\Psi(G)
in terms of hypergraph entropy. We prove the following two main theorems. Theorem
1.6
generalizes the lower bound on
\Psi(G)
in Theorem
1.1
to hypergraphs. Theorem
1.7
follows as a corollary to Theorem
1.6
and resolves the above-mentioned conjectures.

Theorem 1.6
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
and
\delta>1/2
. Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
. Then

\log\Psi(G)\geq kh(G)-n\log{n\choose k-1}+n\log n-n\log e-o(n).

Theorem 1.7
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
and
\delta>1/2
. Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
. Then
G
contains at least
(\delta-o(1))^{n}n!
tight Hamilton cycles.

The method presented in this paper also applies in the more general setting of counting Hamilton
\ell
-cycles in a
k
-graph

G
on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
for some
\delta>1/2
. We define

C_{n,k,\ell}\coloneqq\begin{cases}(\pi_{1}!\pi_{2}!)^{\frac{n}{k-\ell}}\quad&\text{if }\ell\in[k-1]\\
(n/k)!(k!)^{\frac{n}{k}}\quad&\text{if }\ell=0\end{cases},

where
\pi_{1}=k\bmod(k-\ell)
and
\pi_{2}=(k-\ell)-\pi_{1}
. Observe that when
(k-\ell)|n
, we have
\Psi_{\ell}(K_{n}^{(k)})=\frac{n!}{\frac{2n}{k-\ell}C_{n,k,\ell}}
for
\ell\in[k-1]
, and
\Phi(K_{n}^{(k)})=\frac{n!}{C_{n,k,0}}
. Here we state the general result in Theorem
1.8
. The proofs carry over with only minor modifications, and we therefore defer the details to the appendix. The lower bound in Theorem
1.4
and Theorem
1.5
follow as corollaries when
\ell=0
and
0\leq\ell\leq k-2
, respectively.

Theorem 1.8
.

Suppose
k\geq 2
,
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
and
\delta>1/2
. Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
(k-\ell)|n
and
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
. Then

\log\Psi_{\ell}(G)\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}h(G)-\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}-n\log e-\log C_{n,k,\ell}-o(n).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we provide an outline of our proof method. Section
3
introduces the necessary notations and definitions and proves several basic results. In Section
4
, we study how hypergraph entropy behaves under the removal of vertex subsets. In Section
5
, we study random walks in
k
-graphs and show that they are almost always well-behaved. In Section
6
, we put together the results developed earlier to count long paths and extend them to Hamilton cycles, thereby proving our main theorems.

2.
Proof overview

We provide an outline for the proof of Theorem
1.6
. Let
k\geq 2
,
\delta>1/2
and
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
.
We first reserve a set
U\subseteq V(G)
of size
n^{\alpha}
, with
\alpha
close to 1, such that every
(k-1)
-subset of
V(G)
has many neighbours in
U
. Let
G^{\prime}=G-U
and
n^{\prime}\coloneqq|V(G^{\prime})|
. Observe that
\delta_{k-1}(G^{\prime})\geq\delta^{\prime}n^{\prime}
for some
\delta^{\prime}>1/2
. We construct long tight paths in
G^{\prime}
. The strong connectivity of
U
then allows us to complete these paths into Hamilton cycles of
G
.

In
G^{\prime}
, we take a perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}
whose entropy
h(\mathbf{x})
is close to
h(G)
. We use
\mathbf{x}
as a probability distribution that guides the construction of a tight path. Moreover, we require
\mathbf{x}
to be
b
-normal, which means that the edge weights are roughly evenly distributed so that along a long path, the probability of visiting any vertex is roughly uniform.

Let
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G^{\prime})
denote the set of all
(k-1)
-tuples of vertices of
G^{\prime}
. Since extensions of a tight path are determined by its last
k-1
vertices, we study a random walk
Z
on
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, viewed as a Markov chain whose transition matrix is defined in terms of
\mathbf{x}
. Starting at some
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, we run this Markov chain for
m^{\prime}\coloneqq(n^{\prime})^{1/3}
steps. The length
m^{\prime}
is chosen large enough so that the rapid mixing property of a Markov chain applies. It is also small enough so that with high probability, the walk does not revisit vertices and thus yields a tight path
P
in
G^{\prime}
.

We show that with high probability, the random walk
Z
is well-behaved, which in particular implies the following. First,
Z
yields a tight path
P
in
G
from
\vec{S}
to some
\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G^{\prime})
. Second, the subgraph
G^{\prime\prime}=G^{\prime}-(P-T)
with
n^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq V(G^{\prime\prime})
still satisfies a minimum codegree condition
\delta_{k-1}(G^{\prime\prime})\geq\delta^{\prime\prime}n^{\prime\prime}
for some
1/2<\delta^{\prime\prime}\leq\delta^{\prime}
. Lastly,
G^{\prime\prime}
admits a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
, with
b^{\prime}
only slightly larger than
b
, such that
h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
scales appropriately with
n^{\prime\prime}
and
h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
is close to
h(G^{\prime\prime})
. Hence we can treat
G^{\prime\prime}
in the same way as
G^{\prime}
and construct random walks starting at
\vec{T}
.

We iterate this procedure. Starting from
G_{0}\coloneqq G^{\prime}
and some
\vec{S}_{0}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G_{0})
, we remove a well-behaved path
P_{i}
of length
|V(G_{i})|^{1/3}
, from
\vec{S}_{i}
to some
\vec{S}_{i+1}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G_{i})
, to obtain the next subgraph
G_{i+1}\coloneqq G_{i}-(P_{i}-S_{i+1})
. At each step, let
z_{i}
denote the number of well-behaved paths, which can be lower bounded using the entropy of the random walk. We continue this process until
Q\coloneqq P_{0}\cup...\cup P_{\kappa}
has length
|Q|\geq n^{\prime}-n^{\prime 1-\beta}
for some
\kappa\in\mathbb{N}
and small
\beta>0
.

Finally, the set
U
is used to absorb the remaining vertices and complete
Q
into a tight Hamilton cycle in
G
. The product
\prod_{i=0}^{\kappa}z_{i}
provides the desired lower bound on
\Psi(G)
.

3.
Preliminaries

In this section, we introduce some notations and definitions that will be used throughout this paper, and establish several preliminary results.

3.1.
Notation

For any integer
n\geq 1
, we define
[n]\coloneqq\{1,...,n\}
and
[n]_{0}\coloneqq\{0,...,n\}
. For real numbers
a,b
and some error term
c\geq 0
, we write
a=b\pm c
if
b-c\leq a\leq b+c
. Expressions with more error terms are defined similarly and should be read from inner to outer brackets. For
\alpha,\beta\in(0,1]
, we write that a statement holds for
\alpha\ll\beta
if there is a non-decreasing function
f:(0,1]\to(0,1]
such that the statement holds for any
\alpha\in(0,f(\beta)]
. Hierarchies with more constants are defined similarly and should be read from right to left. Note that if
1/k
and
1/n
appear in a hierarchy, this implicitly means that
k
and
n
are natural numbers, respectively, and in addition that
k\geq 2
. We ignore rounding issues when they do not affect the argument.

We use standard asymptotic notation. For functions
f=f(n)
and
g=g(n)
, we write
f=O(g)
if there exists a constant
C>0
such that
f(n)\leq Cg(n)
for all
n
,
f=o(g)
if
f/g\to 0
as
n\to\infty
, and
f=\Theta(g)
if
f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
.

We also use standard probabilistic notation. We denote the probability of an event
\mathcal{E}
by
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]
and the expected value of a random variable
X
by
\mathbb{E}[X]
.

We write
\log\coloneqq\log_{2}
and
\ln\coloneqq\log_{e}
. We use the convention
0\log 0=0
. For
x\geq 0
, we often use the inequality
\log(1+x)\leq 2x
, which follows from
1+x\leq e^{x}\leq 2^{2x}
. We also often use
\frac{n^{k}}{k^{k}}\leq{n\choose k}\leq\frac{n^{k}}{k!}
to bound binomial coefficients. Moreover, we use binomial approximation
(1+x)^{\alpha}=1+\alpha x\pm O(x^{2})=1\pm O(x)
when
|x|<1
and
|\alpha x|
is sufficiently small. We use Stirling’s approximation
\log(n!)=n\log\frac{n}{e}\pm\log n
.

3.2.
Dirac hypergraphs

We introduce the basic graph theoretic definitions and notation.

A
k
-uniform
hypergraph
(or
k
-graph
)
G
is a hypergraph in which each edge is a set of exactly
k
vertices. We denote its vertex set by
V(G)
and its edge set by
E(G)
. For any integer
d
with
d\in[k-1]_{0}
, the
neighbourhood
of a
d
-subset

S\subseteq V(G)
in
G
is defined by
N_{G}(S)\coloneqq\{T\subseteq V(G)|S\cup T\in E(G)\}
, and the
degree
of
S
in
G
is
\deg_{G}(S)\coloneqq|N_{G}(S)|
. Note that when
d=0
, we have
N_{G}(\emptyset)=E(G)
. When
d=1
or
d=k-1
, we simply write the singleton sets as vertices. That is,
N_{G}(v)\coloneqq\{T\subseteq V(G)|\{v\}\cup T\in E(G)\}
for each vertex
v\in V(G)
and
N_{G}(S)\coloneqq\{v\in V(G)|S\cup\{v\}\in E(G)\}
for each
(k-1)
-subset

S\subseteq V(G)
. The
minimum
d
-degree

\delta_{d}(G)
of
G
is the minimum degree over all
d
-subsets of
V(G)
. The minimum
d
-degree of a
k
-graph is monotone in the following sense.

Fact 3.1
.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph. Then

\frac{\delta_{0}(G)}{{n\choose k}}\geq\frac{\delta_{1}(G)}{{n-1\choose k-1}}\geq...\geq\frac{\delta_{k-1}(G)}{{n-k+1\choose 1}}.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices. Let
1/2<\delta<1
be a constant. We say that
G
is
\delta
-Dirac
if
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq\delta n
. We say that
G
is an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
if
G
is a
\delta
-Dirac
k
-graph on
n
vertices. We use
\hat{\delta}\coloneqq\delta-1/2
as a shorthand to denote the offset of
\delta
above the
1/2
threshold.

Suppose
G
is a hypergraph,
H
is a subgraph of
G
and
U\subseteq V(G)
. We write
G[U]
for the subgraph of
G
induced by
U
. We define
G-U\coloneqq G[V(G)\setminus U]
and
G-H\coloneqq G-V(H)
.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph. We say a subgraph
W
of
G
is a
(tight) walk
of length
L
if
W
admits an ordering of its vertices
V(W)=(v_{1},...v_{L+k-1})
such that
E(W)=\{\{v_{i},...v_{i+k-1}\}|i\in[L]\}
. The walk
W
is a
(tight) path
if the vertices
v_{1},...,v_{L+k-1}
are distinct. The walk
W
is a
(tight) cycle
if
(v_{1},...v_{k-1})=(v_{L+1},...,v_{L+k-1})
and the vertices
v_{1},...,v_{L}
are distinct. A
(tight) Hamilton path
in
G
is a tight path
P
such that
V(P)=V(G)
. A
(tight) Hamilton cycle
in
G
is a tight cycle
C
such that
V(C)=V(G)
.

Since we focus exclusively on tight walks, paths, and cycles in this paper, we will henceforth omit the word “tight”.

3.3.
Hypergraph entropy

We define perfect fractional matchings and hypergraph entropy.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices. An
edge weighting
of
G
is a function
\mathbf{x}:E(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
. For all
e\in E(G)
, we write
\mathbf{x}_{e}
or
\mathbf{x}[e]
for
\mathbf{x}(e)
. We call
\mathbf{x}
a
\emph{perfectfractionalmatching}
if
\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=1
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. Let
F\subseteq E(G)
, we define the
entropy of
F
induced by
\mathbf{x}
as

h_{\mathbf{x}}(F)\coloneqq\sum_{e\in F}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}.

We define the
entropy of
\mathbf{x}
by
h(\mathbf{x})\coloneqq h_{\mathbf{x}}(E(G))
. We define the
entropy
of the hypergraph
G
by
h(G)\coloneqq\sup\{h(\mathbf{x})|\mathbf{x}\text{ is a perfect fractional matching of }G\}
.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices,
\mathbf{x}
be an edge weighting of
G
and
b\geq 1
. We say
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal
if for all
e\in E(G)
, we have

\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\leq\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}.

As
h(G)
is monotone increasing with respect to taking supergraphs, it follows that
h(G)\leq h(K_{n}^{(k)})\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n\log n
, where the second inequality is an immediate consequence of the definition of graph entropy and Jensen’s inequality applied to the concave function
x\mapsto\log x
. A tight lower bound for the entropy of an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph is proved in
[
12
]
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
12
, Theorem 6.1]
)
.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph. Then,

\frac{n}{k}\log{n\choose k-1}+\frac{n}{k}\log\delta\leq h(G)\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n\log n.

It then follows that
h(G)=\Theta(n\log n)
for
(n,k,\delta)
-graphs. In fact, the same asymptotic bound holds for any
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
.

Lemma 3.3

(
[
12
, Lemma 2.6]
)
.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
with
\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq L
for every edge
e\in E(G)
. Then

h(\mathbf{x})\geq\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{n}{L^{2}k|E(G)|}.

Corollary 3.4
.

Let
G
be a
k
-graph on
n
vertices and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
for some
b\geq 1
. Then,

h(\mathbf{x})=\frac{k-1}{k}n\log n-O_{b}(n)
.

Proof.

By Theorem
3.2
,
h(\mathbf{x})\leq h(G)\leq\frac{k-1}{k}n\log n
. By Lemma
3.3
, we have

h(\mathbf{x})\geq\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{n}{(b/n^{k-1})^{2}k|E(G)|}\geq\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{n^{2k-1}}{b^{2}k{n\choose k}}\geq\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{n^{k-1}}{b^{2}}\geq\frac{k-1}{k}n\log n-O_{b}(n),

which completes the proof.
∎

The following lemma states that every
(n,k,\delta)
-graph admits a

-normal perfect fractional matching whose entropy is close to the entropy of the graph. For digraphs, this is proven as Lemma 4.6 in
[
11
]
.

Lemma 3.5

(
[
12
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Suppose
1/b\ll\varepsilon\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph with
n
sufficiently large with respect to
\hat{\delta}
and
k
. Then there is a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}
of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
.

3.4.
Information-theoretic entropy

We recall the Shannon entropy for random variables. Let
X
be a random variable with finite support. Then the
entropy
of
X
is defined as

H(X)\coloneqq\sum_{x\in{\rm supp}(X)}\mathbb{P}[X=x]\log\frac{1}{\mathbb{P}[X=x]}.

For an event
\mathcal{E}
on the same probability space as
X
, we write
H(X|\mathcal{E})
for the entropy of
X
in the conditional probability space given
\mathcal{E}
. For another random variable
Y
with finite support on the same probability space as
X
, the
conditional entropy
of
X
given
Y
is defined as

H(X|Y)\coloneqq\sum_{y\in{\rm supp}(Y)}\mathbb{P}[Y=y]H(X|{Y=y}).

We need the following facts on entropy. Fact
3.6
relates entropy to the size of the support. Fact
3.7
is the chain rule for entropy, allowing us to deal with a sequence of random variables on the same probability space. Fact
3.8
states that the entropy does not decrease significantly when conditioned on a likely event.

Fact 3.6
.

Let
X
be a random variable with finite support. Then
H(X)\leq\log|{\rm supp}(X)|
, where equality holds when
X
is a uniform distribution.

Fact 3.7
.

Let
X_{1},...,X_{m}
be random variables on the same probability space. Then

H(X_{1},...,X_{m})=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H(X_{i}|X_{1},...,X_{i-1}).

Fact 3.8

(
[
11
, Lemma 3.1]
)
.

Let
X
be a random variable with values in
\{x_{1},...,x_{N}\}
. Let
A\geq 16
be such that
\mathbb{P}[X=x_{i}]\geq\frac{1}{A}
for all
i\in[N]
. Let
J\subseteq\{x_{1},...,x_{N}\}
and
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
X\in J
. Suppose that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-a
for some
0\leq a\leq\frac{1}{2}
. Then

H(X)-H(X|\mathcal{E})\leq 2a\log A.

3.5.
Auxiliary graph

To build a path in a
k
-graph
G
, the choice of each vertex is constrained by the preceding
k-1
vertices. To facilitate many of our arguments, it is therefore useful to consider the interactions among the
(k-1)
-tuples of
V(G)
. To this end, we introduce an auxiliary graph on such tuples and study its properties in the following definitions and lemmas.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. For any
S\subseteq V(G)
with
|S|\in[k]_{0}
, we define the quantity
\mathbf{x}_{S}\coloneqq\sum_{S\subseteq e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}
, which is the total weight assigned by
\mathbf{x}
to the edges containing
S
. Define
\mathcal{S}(G)\coloneqq{V(G)\choose{k-1}}
to be the set of all
(k-1)
-subsets of
V(G)
. Let
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
denote the set of all
(k-1)
-tuples in
V(G)^{k-1}
with distinct entries. For each
\vec{S}=(s_{1},...,s_{k-1})\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, we write
S=\{s_{1},...,s_{k-1}\}
for the underlying
(k-1)
-set in
\mathcal{S}(G)
. Note that for all
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
,
\mathbf{x}_{S}\coloneqq\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]
.

We define
\mathbb{S}[G]
to be the weighted digraph with vertex set
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, where for all
\vec{S}=(s_{1},...,s_{k-1}),\vec{T}=(t_{1},...,t_{k-1})\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, there is a directed edge
\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])
if
(s_{2},...s_{k-1})=(t_{1},...,t_{k-2})
and
S\cup\{t_{k-1}\}\in E(G)
. The edge weighting
\mathbf{y}:E(\mathbb{S}[G])\to[0,1]
in
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
is defined by

\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbf{x}[S\cup T]}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}&\text{if }\mathbf{x}_{S}\neq 0\\
\frac{1}{\deg_{G}(S)}&\text{if }\mathbf{x}_{S}=0\end{cases}

for all
\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])
. As we focus on the scenario where
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal for some
b\geq 1
, we always have
\mathbf{x}_{S}\neq 0
. Note that when an edge
\vec{S}\vec{T}
exists in
\mathbb{S}[G]
, its weight is independent of the ordering of the entries in
\vec{S}
and
\vec{T}
. Therefore, for simplicity, we often write
\mathbf{y}[S\to t_{k-1}]
for
\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]
. We further observe that for each
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, we have
\sum_{\vec{T}\in N^{+}_{\mathbb{S}[G]}(\vec{S})}\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]=\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]=1
, where
N^{+}_{\mathbb{S}[G]}(\vec{S})
denotes the out-neighbourhood of
\vec{S}
in
\mathbb{S}[G]
. So we may interpret
\mathbf{y}
as a probability distribution on the outgoing edges of
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
.

For each
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, we define the
entropy of
S
induced by
\mathbf{x}
by

h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\coloneqq\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}.

Note that there is a natural bijection between the directed walks in
\mathbb{S}[G]
and the walks in
G
, by tracing the same sequence of vertices in
G
. Let
W
be a walk in
G
, and
W^{\prime}
be the corresponding directed walk in
\mathbb{S}[G]
. If
W^{\prime}
is a walk from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
, then we also say that
W
is a walk from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
.

We collect some useful properties in the following lemma.

Lemma 3.9
.

Suppose
n\geq k\geq 2
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Then the following holds.

(i)

\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=n/k,

(ii)

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}=n
, and

(iii)

\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}=k-1
for every vertex
v\in V(G)
.

If we further assume that
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal for some
b\geq 1
, then

(iv)

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)=kh(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\mathbf{x}_{S}\geq kh(\mathbf{x})-n\log{n\choose k-1}+n\log n
,

(v)

\frac{\delta}{bn^{k-2}}\leq\mathbf{x}_{S}\leq\frac{b}{n^{k-2}}
for all
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
and

(vi)

\frac{1}{b^{2}n}\leq\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]\leq\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n}

for all
\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])
.

Proof.

We have

k\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=\sum_{e\in E(G)}\sum_{v\in e}\mathbf{x}_{e}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=\sum_{v\in V(G)}1=n.

Dividing both sides by
k
yields (i). Next, (ii) and (iii) follow from the following calculations.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]=k\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=n,\text{ and}

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{u\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{u\}]=(k-1)\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=k-1

for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. Next, suppose
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal. To prove (iv), we calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\log\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]}

\displaystyle=k\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\log\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle=kh(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle\geq kh(\mathbf{x})-n\log{n\choose k-1}+n\log n,

where the last step follows from Jensen’s inequality applied to the concave function
t\mapsto\log t
, as shown below.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\right)\log\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}}{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}}=n\log\frac{{n\choose k-1}}{n}.

This proves (iv). For each
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, we have

\frac{\delta}{bn^{k-2}}\leq\delta_{k-1}(G)\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\leq\sum_{v\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\leq n\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}=\frac{b}{n^{k-2}}.

This proves (v). For each
\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])
, we exploit the previous inequality and obtain

\frac{1}{b^{2}n}=\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\frac{n^{k-2}}{b}\leq\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]\leq\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}\frac{bn^{k-2}}{\delta}=\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n},

which proves (vi).
∎

4.
Subgraph entropy

We now study how removing a subset of vertices affects the entropy of an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph. Lemma
4.1
shows that if a set interacts with the neighbourhood of each
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
roughly in the way we would heuristically expect it from a randomly chosen set, then the entropy of the graph also decreases roughly as expected after its removal. The proofs in this section follow the same general strategy as those of Lemmas 4.5 and 4.6 in
[
11
]
for digraphs.

Lemma 4.1
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
where
b\geq 4
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
M\subseteq V(G)
be of size
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Suppose the following holds for all
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
.

(i)

\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]=(1\pm n^{-\alpha})\frac{m}{n}
, and

(ii)

\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}=\frac{m}{n}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}
.

Then there is a
(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}})b
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{z}
of
G-M
that satisfies

h(\mathbf{z})\geq\frac{n-m}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}(n-m)\log\frac{n}{n-m}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Proof.

Let
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n-m
,
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-M
and
b^{\prime}\coloneqq(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}})b
. We first scale the weights on
E(G^{\prime})
to obtain an edge weighting
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
with total weight
n^{\prime}/k
, which, by Lemma
3.9(i)
, is the total weight of any perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
. We then define a procedure to slightly perturb
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
to obtain a perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{z}
. Lastly, we verify that
\mathbf{z}
is
b^{\prime}
-normal and has the desired entropy. Let

\Lambda\coloneqq\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}}

be the scaling factor. We define an edge weighting
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
by
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{e}\coloneqq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}>0
for all
e\in E(G^{\prime})
. Let
E(M,G)\coloneqq\{e\in E(G)|e\cap M\neq\emptyset\}
. Note that

\Lambda=\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}}=\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{n/k-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}}=\frac{n-m}{n-km}\frac{1}{1+\frac{k(m-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e})}{n-km}}.

We study the sum
\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}
to obtain a bound for
\Lambda
. Note that

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime})}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\leq\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}].

Recall that
\mathbf{y}:E(\mathbb{S}[G])\to[0,1]
is the edge weighting in
\mathbb{S}[G]
given by

\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]\coloneqq\frac{\mathbf{x}[S\cup T]}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}
for all
\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])
. Using Lemmas
3.9(ii)
and
3.9(v)
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\pm\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)\colon S\cap M\neq\emptyset}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\pm\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)\colon S\cap M\neq\emptyset}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]

\displaystyle=n\cdot(1\pm n^{-\alpha})\frac{m}{n}\pm n^{k-2}m\cdot\frac{b}{n^{k-2}}\cdot(1+n^{-\alpha})\frac{m}{n}

\displaystyle=\left(m\pm\frac{m}{n^{\alpha}}\right)\pm\frac{2bm^{2}}{n}=m\pm n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}.

Putting these together yields a precise estimate for
\Lambda
,

(4.1)

\Lambda=\frac{n-m}{n-km}\frac{1}{1\pm\frac{kn^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}}{n-km}}=\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\right)\left(1\pm\frac{2kn^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}}{n}\right)=1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}.

Next, we slightly redistribute the weights of
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
to obtain a perfect fractional matching. We proceed by induction on the number of vertices
v\in V(G^{\prime})
with
\mathbf{x}_{v}^{\prime}\neq 1
. If no such vertices exist, then
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
is a perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
. Otherwise, there exists a pair of vertices
v,u\in V(G^{\prime})
such that
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{v}>1>\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{u}
. Observe that
\delta_{k-1}(G^{\prime})\geq(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2})n^{\prime}
and thus by monotonicity of minimum degrees (Lemma
3.1
), the vertices
v
and
u
have at least

(4.2)

N_{G^{\prime}}(v)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(u)\geq\hat{\delta}{n^{\prime}-1\choose k-1}\eqqcolon N

common neighbours in
G^{\prime}
. We pick a set
\Gamma
of
(k-1)
-sets in
N_{G^{\prime}}(v)\cap N_{G^{\prime}}(u)
with
|\Gamma|=N
. Let
d\coloneqq\min\left\{\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{v}-1,1-\mathbf{x}^{\prime}_{u}\right\}
. For all
S\in\Gamma
, we perform a “weight shift” as follows. We decrease the weight of the edge
S\cup\{v\}
by
d/N
and increase the weight of the edge
S\cup\{u\}
by
d/N
. As a result, the total weight at
v
is decreased by
d
, the total weight at
u
is increased by
d
, and the total weight at all other vertices remains unchanged. By definition of
d
, at least one of
v
and
u
now has weight 1, as desired.

We repeat this process until the total weight at every vertex of
G^{\prime}
is 1 and denote the resulting weighting by
\mathbf{z}
. We refer to the above transformation of
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
into
\mathbf{z}
as the
normalization procedure
.

We now prove that
\mathbf{z}
is
b^{\prime}
-normal, which in particular implies that
\mathbf{z}>0
and hence that
\mathbf{z}
is a perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
. The calculation below also shows that the normalization procedure is valid, in the sense that the edge weighting at each intermediate step is
b^{\prime}
-normal, since the bounds in (
4.3
) hold throughout the procedure. Let
\Delta\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\left|\mathbf{x}_{v}^{\prime}-1\right|
. For every edge, the amount of weight redistributed due to each of its vertices is at most
\Delta/N
. Since each edge has
k
vertices, we have

(4.3)

\mathbf{z}_{e}=\mathbf{x}_{e}^{\prime}\pm k\frac{\Delta}{N}=\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}\pm k\frac{\Delta}{N}.

We proceed to find bounds for
\Delta
. Similar to our estimation of
\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}
, using Lemmas
3.9(iii)
and
3.9(v)
, for every
v\in V(G^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{v\in e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{u\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to u]\pm\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)\colon v\in S,S\cap M\neq\emptyset}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{u\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to u]

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}(k-1)\cdot(1\pm n^{-\alpha})\frac{m}{n}\pm n^{k-3}m\cdot\frac{b}{n^{k-2}}\cdot(1+n^{-\alpha})\frac{m}{n}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\left(\frac{(k-1)m}{n}\pm\frac{(k-1)m}{n^{1+\alpha}}\right)\pm\frac{2bm^{2}}{n^{2}}=\mathrel{}\frac{(k-1)m}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}.

Together with (
4.1
), this yields

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\Lambda\sum_{v\in e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}=\Lambda\left(\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}-\sum_{v\in e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}\right)\left(1-\frac{(k-1)m}{n}\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}\right)

\displaystyle=1\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}.

That is,
\Delta\leq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}
. Recall the definition of
N
in (
4.2
). Hence, we obtain

(4.4)

k\frac{\Delta}{N}\leq\frac{kn^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}}{\hat{\delta}{n-m-1\choose k-1}}\leq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-(k-1)-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}.

We further observe that

\displaystyle\left(\Lambda\pm k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}

\displaystyle=\left(\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}\right)\pm bn^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}\right)\left(1-\frac{m}{n}\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\pm 2bn^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}\right)\left(1-\frac{(k-1)m}{n}\pm\frac{k^{2}m^{2}}{n^{2}}\right)

\displaystyle=1\pm n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{7\alpha}{12}}.

Using (
4.3
) and the fact that
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal, for each edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{e}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}+k\frac{\Delta}{N}\leq\Lambda\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}+k\frac{\Delta}{N}b^{2}=\left(\Lambda+k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}\frac{b}{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}\leq\frac{\left(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{6\alpha}{12}}\right)b}{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{e}

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}-k\frac{\Delta}{N}\geq\Lambda\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}-k\frac{\Delta}{N}=\left(\Lambda-k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}\frac{1}{b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{7\alpha}{12}}\right)\frac{1}{b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}\geq\frac{1}{\left(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{6\alpha}{12}}\right)b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}.

This shows that
\mathbf{z}
is a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
.

It remains to find a lower bound on the entropy
h(\mathbf{z})
. Consider two edges
e,f\in E(G^{\prime})
with weights
w_{e}
and
w_{f}
respectively. Suppose a weight of
0<d^{\prime}\leq\Delta/N
is redistributed from
e
to
f
. That is, the new weights of the edges
e
and
f
are respectively
w_{e}-d^{\prime}
and
w_{f}+d^{\prime}
. Assume that
w_{e},w_{e}-d^{\prime},w_{f},w_{f}+d^{\prime}
are all
b^{\prime}
-normal. Then by (
4.4
),
d^{\prime}\leq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-(k-1)-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}
.
Note that
w_{e}-d^{\prime}\geq w_{e}/2
and
w_{f}+d^{\prime}\leq 2w_{f}.
So, we have

\displaystyle-\left(\log\frac{1}{w_{e}-d^{\prime}}-\log\frac{1}{w_{f}+d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq-\left(\log\frac{2}{w_{e}}-\log\frac{1}{2w_{f}}\right)=\log w_{e}-\log w_{f}-2

\displaystyle\geq\log\frac{1}{b^{\prime}n^{k-1}}-\log\frac{b^{\prime}}{n^{k-1}}-2\geq-3\log b^{\prime}.

Thus, the updated entropy of
\{e,f\}
is

\displaystyle\mathrel{}(w_{e}-d^{\prime})\log\frac{1}{w_{e}-d^{\prime}}+(w_{f}+d^{\prime})\log\frac{1}{w_{f}+d^{\prime}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}w_{e}\log\frac{1}{w_{e}-d^{\prime}}+w_{f}\log\frac{1}{w_{f}+d^{\prime}}-d^{\prime}\left(\log\frac{1}{w_{e}-d^{\prime}}-\log\frac{1}{w_{f}+d^{\prime}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}w_{e}\log\frac{1}{w_{e}}+w_{f}\log\frac{1}{w_{f}}-w_{f}\log\left(1+\frac{d^{\prime}}{w_{f}}\right)-3d^{\prime}\log b^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}w_{e}\log\frac{1}{w_{e}}+w_{f}\log\frac{1}{w_{f}}-w_{f}\cdot\frac{2d^{\prime}}{w_{f}}-3d^{\prime}\log b^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}w_{e}\log\frac{1}{w_{e}}+w_{f}\log\frac{1}{w_{f}}-4\frac{\Delta}{N}\log b^{\prime}.

Note that the normalization procedure involves weight shifts between at most
nN
pairs of edges. Moreover, the edge weighting at each intermediate step is
b^{\prime}
-normal. So we have

(4.5)

h(\mathbf{z})\geq h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})-nN\left(4\frac{\Delta}{N}\log b^{\prime}\right)=h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})-4n\Delta\log b^{\prime}\geq h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}.

We now study
h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})
. We first consider the following sum and use Lemmas
3.9(ii)
,
3.9(iv)
and
3.9(v)
.

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{v\}]}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\left(\frac{m}{n}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\right)+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}\left(\frac{m}{n}+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\frac{m}{n}\left(kh(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\mathbf{x}_{S}-\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\mathbf{x}_{S}\right)+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\left(1+\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}\right)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\frac{m}{n}kh(\mathbf{x})+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\left(1+\log\left(\frac{bn^{k-2}}{\delta}\right)\right)

(4.6)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\frac{m}{n}kh(\mathbf{x})+n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}.

Next, we bound
\log\Lambda
. Recall (
4.1
). Observe that

\displaystyle\Lambda-\left(\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}\right)^{k-1}

\displaystyle=\Lambda-\left(1+\frac{m}{n^{\prime}}\right)^{k-1}\leq 1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}-\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-m}\right)

\displaystyle=\frac{(k-1)m}{n}\left(\frac{1}{1-\frac{km}{n}}-\frac{1}{1-\frac{m}{n}}\right)+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}\leq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}.

So,
\Lambda\leq(\frac{n}{n^{\prime}})^{k-1}(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}(\frac{n^{\prime}}{n})^{k-1})
, and hence, we have

\displaystyle\log\Lambda

\displaystyle\leq\log\left[\left(\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}\right)^{k-1}\left(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}\left(\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}\right)^{k-1}\right)\right]

\displaystyle\leq(k-1)\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}+\log\left(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}\right)\leq(k-1)\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}+2n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}.

Recall that
h(\mathbf{x})=\Theta_{b}(n\log n)
by Corollary
3.4
. Therefore, using (
4.6
), we have

\displaystyle\mathrel{}h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\Lambda\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}-\Lambda\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\Lambda

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\Lambda\left(h(\mathbf{x})-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}\right)-\frac{n^{\prime}}{k}\log\Lambda

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}-n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{10\alpha}{12}}\right)\left(h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{m}{n}kh(\mathbf{x})-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{11\alpha}{12}}\right)-\frac{n^{\prime}}{k}\left((k-1)\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}+2n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{9\alpha}{12}}\right)

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\left(1+\frac{(k-1)m}{n-km}\right)\left(\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{(k-1)m}{n}h(\mathbf{x})\right)-\frac{k-1}{k}n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{8\alpha}{12}}

\displaystyle\geq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{7\alpha}{12}}.

Combining with (
4.5
) yields the desired result.
∎

The following lemma shows that when we remove a small enough subset of vertices from
G
, the entropy of the graph only decreases slightly.

Lemma 4.2
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\varepsilon\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
M\subseteq V(G)
be a vertex subset such that
|M|\leq\frac{n}{\log^{2}n}
. Then,
h(G-M)\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
.

Proof.

Let
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-M
,
m\coloneqq|M|
and
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n-m
. By Lemma
3.5
, there exists
b
with
1/n\ll 1/b\ll\varepsilon
and
b\geq 4
such that there is a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}
of
G
satisfying
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\frac{\varepsilon}{3}n
. Similar to the proof of Lemma
4.1
, we first scale the weights on
E(G^{\prime})
and then perform the normalization procedure to obtain a perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
.

Let
E(M,G)\coloneqq\{e\in E(G)|e\cap M\neq\emptyset\}
. Define the scaling factor

\Lambda\coloneqq\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}}=\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}}=\frac{n^{\prime}/k}{\frac{n}{k}-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}}=\frac{n-m}{n-k\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}}.

Define a new edge weighting
\mathbf{x}_{e}^{\prime}\coloneqq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}>0
for each
e\in E(G^{\prime})
. Note that

k\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\sum_{v\in e}\mathbf{x}_{e}\geq\sum_{v\in M}\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=m.

So, we have

\frac{m}{k}\leq\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq\sum_{v\in M}\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}=m.

Thus,
\Lambda
satisfies

1=\frac{n-m}{n-m}\leq\Lambda\leq\frac{n-m}{n-km}\leq 1+\frac{2km}{n}.

We apply the same normalization procedure as in the proof of Lemma
4.1
to redistribute the weights of
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
until the total weight at each vertex in
G^{\prime}
is 1. We call the resulting edge weighting
\mathbf{z}
. We now prove that
\mathbf{z}
is
b^{\prime}
-normal for
b^{\prime}\coloneqq(1+\frac{b^{5}m}{n})b
. It then follows that, in particular,
\mathbf{z}>0
and hence that
\mathbf{z}
is a perfect fractional matching of
G^{\prime}
. Let

\Delta\coloneqq\max_{v\in V(G^{\prime})}\left|\mathbf{x}_{v}^{\prime}-1\right|\quad\text{and}\quad N\coloneqq\hat{\delta}{n^{\prime}-1\choose k-1}\geq\varepsilon n^{k-1}.

Then, as in (
4.3
), we have

\mathbf{z}_{e}=\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}\pm k\frac{\Delta}{N}.

Observe that for all
v\in V(G^{\prime})
, we have
|\{e\in E(M,G):v\in e\}|\leq m\cdot n^{k-2}
. Hence, as
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal, this yields

0\leq\sum_{v\in e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq mn^{k-2}\cdot\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}=\frac{bm}{n}.

Thus,

\mathbf{x}_{v}^{\prime}=\sum_{v\in e\in E(G^{\prime})}\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}=\Lambda\left(\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}-\sum_{v\in e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\right)=\left(1\pm\frac{2km}{n}\right)\left(1\pm\frac{bm}{n}\right)=1\pm\frac{2bm}{n}.

Therefore, we have

\Delta\leq\frac{2bm}{n}\quad\text{and}\quad\frac{k\Delta}{N}\leq\frac{k\cdot\frac{2bm}{n}}{\varepsilon n^{k-1}}\leq\frac{b^{2}m}{n^{k}}.

Observe that

\left(\Lambda\pm k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}\leq\left(\left(1\pm\frac{2km}{n}\right)\pm\frac{b^{2}m}{n^{k}}\cdot bn^{k-1}\right)\left(1-\frac{m}{n}\right)^{k-1}\leq 1\pm\frac{b^{4}m}{n}.

We are now ready to show that
\mathbf{z}
is
b^{\prime}
-normal. For each edge
e\in E(G^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{e}

\displaystyle\leq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}+k\frac{\Delta}{N}\leq\Lambda\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}+k\frac{\Delta}{N}b^{2}=\left(\Lambda+k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}\frac{b}{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}\leq\frac{\left(1+\frac{b^{4}m}{n}\right)b}{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}_{e}

\displaystyle\geq\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}-k\frac{\Delta}{N}\geq\Lambda\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}-k\frac{\Delta}{N}=\left(\Lambda-k\frac{\Delta}{N}bn^{k-1}\right)\frac{(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}{n^{k-1}}\frac{1}{b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-\frac{b^{4}m}{n}\right)\frac{1}{b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}\geq\frac{1}{\left(1+\frac{b^{5}m}{n}\right)b(n^{\prime})^{k-1}}.

Next, we find a lower bound for
h(\mathbf{z})
. We first consider

\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}\leq mn^{k-1}\cdot\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}\log(bn^{k-1})\leq\frac{\varepsilon n}{3}

where the last step follows from the assumption that
m\leq\frac{n}{\log^{2}n}
. By the very same argument as (
4.5
), we have

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{z})

\displaystyle\geq h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})-4n\Delta\log b^{\prime}\geq\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}^{\prime}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}^{\prime}}-4n\cdot\frac{2bm}{n}\cdot\log\left(1+\frac{b^{5}m}{n}\right)b

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}}-\frac{\varepsilon n}{3}\geq\sum_{e\in E(G^{\prime})}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}-\frac{\varepsilon n}{3}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}-\sum_{e\in E(M,G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}-\frac{\varepsilon n}{3}\geq h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{2\varepsilon n}{3}\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n.

where the second line follows from that the function
t\mapsto t\log(1/t)
is increasing when
t\leq e^{-1}
and we may choose
n
large enough so that
\mathbf{x}_{e},\Lambda\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq e^{-1}
.
∎

5.
Probabilistic properties of random walks

In this section, we study random walks in an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and in its auxiliary graph defined in Section
3.5
. Results of this type can also be found in other papers on this topic; for example, for digraphs
[
11
, Section 5]
.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
\mathbb{S}[G]
be as defined in Section 2.5 and
\mathbf{y}
be the corresponding edge weighting induced by
\mathbf{x}
. A
random walk
on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
is the Markov chain
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with state space
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and a transition matrix
P=(p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}})_{\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}
, defined by

p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]&\quad\text{if }\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A
random walk
on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
is a sequence
(R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of random variables with sample space
V(G)
such that if
\vec{Z}_{t}\coloneqq(R_{t},...,R_{t+k-2})
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. We note that every random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
yields a random walk on
G
.

5.1.
Probabilistic tools

We need the following probabilistic results for the remainder of this paper. Lemma
5.1
is the rapid mixing property, which provides a lower bound on the speed of convergence of a Markov chain to the stationary distribution. A sequence
Y_{0},...Y_{\ell}
of randoms variables is a
martingale
if
Y_{0}
is a fixed real number and
\mathbb{E}[Y_{i}|Y_{0},...,Y_{i-1}]=Y_{i-1}
for all
i\in[\ell]
. Lemma
5.2
is Azuma’s inequality, see for example
[
1
]
, which yields concentration estimates of a martingale with bounded differences. Lemma
5.3
is a Chernoff-type bound that is used at the end to select a suitable subgraph for the absorption argument, see for example
[
9
]
.

Lemma 5.1

(
[
10
, Lemma 3.2]
)
.

Let
(Z_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a Markov chain with state space
\{s_{1},...,s_{n}\}
and transition matrix
P=(p_{ij})_{i,j\in[n]}
. Suppose there is a probability distribution
\sigma=(\sigma_{i})_{i\in[n]}
such that
\sigma P=\sigma
. Let

c_{1}\coloneqq\inf_{i,j,k\in[n]}\frac{p_{ij}}{\sigma_{k}}\text{ and }c_{2}\coloneqq\sup_{i,j,k\in[n]}\frac{p_{ij}}{\sigma_{k}}.

If
c_{1}>0
, then for
t\geq 2+2c_{1}^{-1}\log c_{2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[Z_{t}=s_{i}]=\left(1\pm\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\right)^{t}\right)\sigma_{i}.

Lemma 5.2
.

Let
Y_{0},...Y_{\ell}
be a martingale such that
|Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}|\leq c
for all
i\in[\ell]
. Then for all
t>0
, we have

\mathbb{P}[|Y_{\ell}-Y_{0}|\geq t]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\ell c^{2}}\right).

Lemma 5.3
.

Let
t>0
and
X
be a random variable with a binomial or hypergeometric distribution. Suppose
\mathbb{E}[X]>0
. Then

\mathbb{P}[X\leq\mathbb{E}[X]-t]\leq\exp\left(-\frac{t^{2}}{2\mathbb{E}[X]}\right).

5.2.
Convergence of random walks

We also need the following result showing that in an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
G
, any
\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
can be reached from any
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
in a fixed number of steps.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
8
, Lemma 5.3]
)
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\zeta\ll 1/L\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph. For any
\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, there are at least
\zeta n^{L-(k-1)}
walks of length
L
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
in
G
.

We now apply the rapid mixing property of Markov chains to the random walks in
\mathbb{S}[G]
.

Corollary 5.5
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Then, for any
(k-1)
-tuple
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{S}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{(k-1)!n}.

Proof.

Using the same notation as the beginning of Section
5
,
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a Markov chain with state space
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and transition matrix
P=(p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}})_{\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}
. By Lemma
3.9(vi)
, we have the following bounds on the nonzero entries
p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}
of
P
,

\frac{1}{b^{2}n}\leq p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}\leq\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n}.

By Lemma
5.4
, the hypergraph
G
, and hence
\mathbb{S}[G]
, has at least
\zeta n^{L-(k-1)}
walks of length
L
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
for any
\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, where
1/n\ll\zeta\ll 1/L\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. This allows us to define a new Markov chain
(\vec{Z}_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
with a strictly positive transition matrix
Q\coloneqq P^{L}
. We have the following bounds on the entries of
Q=(q_{\vec{S}\vec{T}})_{\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}
,

(5.1)

\frac{\zeta}{b^{2L}n^{k-1}}=\zeta n^{L-(k-1)}\left(\frac{1}{b^{2}n}\right)^{L}\leq q_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}\leq n^{L-(k-1)}\left(\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n}\right)^{L}=\frac{b^{2L}}{\delta^{L}n^{k-1}}.

We define
\sigma\coloneqq(\sigma_{\vec{S}})_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}
by

\sigma_{\vec{S}}\coloneqq\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{(k-1)!n}

for each
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
. We claim that
\sigma
is the stationary distribution of the Markov chain
(Z_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Indeed, using Lemma
3.9(ii)
, it is straightforward to verify that
\sigma
is a probability distribution. It remains to show that
\sigma
is a left eigenvector of
P
, and hence a left eigenvector of
Q
. For all
\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma P)_{\vec{T}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\sigma_{\vec{S}}p_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}=\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\sigma_{\vec{S}}\mathbf{y}[\vec{S}\vec{T}]\mathbbm{1}[\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{(k-1)!n}\cdot\frac{\mathbf{x}[S\cup\{t_{k-1}\}]}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}\cdot\mathbbm{1}[\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!n}\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\mathbf{x}[\{s_{1}\}\cup T]\mathbbm{1}[\vec{S}\vec{T}\in E(\mathbb{S}[G])]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{(k-1)!n}\sum_{v\in N_{G}(T)}\mathbf{x}[\{v\}\cup T]=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{T}}{(k-1)!n}=\sigma_{\vec{T}},

which proves our claim.

By Lemma
3.9(v)
, for each
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
, we have

\frac{\delta}{b(k-1)!n^{k-1}}\leq\sigma_{\vec{S}}\leq\frac{b}{(k-1)!n^{k-1}}.

Together with (
5.1
), this allows us to bound
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
as defined in Lemma
5.1
. We have

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\vec{S},\vec{T},\vec{U}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\frac{q_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}}{\sigma_{\vec{U}}}\geq\frac{\zeta}{b^{2L}n^{k-1}}\cdot\frac{(k-1)!n^{k-1}}{b}=\frac{\zeta(k-1)!}{b^{2L+1}}>0,\text{ and}

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\vec{S},\vec{T},\vec{U}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\frac{q_{\vec{S}\vec{T}}}{\sigma_{\vec{U}}}\leq\frac{b^{2L}}{\delta^{L}n^{k-1}}\cdot\frac{b(k-1)!n^{k-1}}{\delta}=\frac{b^{2L+1}(k-1)!}{\delta^{L+1}}.

Let

\alpha\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{\zeta(k-1)!}{2Lb^{2L+1}},\frac{\delta^{L+1}}{b^{2L+1}(k-1)!}\right\}

so that
c_{1}\geq 2L\alpha
and
c_{2}\leq\alpha^{-1}
.
Using the inequality
(1-x)^{t}\leq e^{-xt}
, Lemma
5.1
yields

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{S}]=\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t/L}^{\prime}=\vec{S}]=\left(1\pm\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{L}}\right)\sigma_{\vec{S}}=\left(1\pm\exp\left(-\frac{c_{1}t}{2L}\right)\right)\sigma_{\vec{S}}=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{S}}

for any
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and any
t/L\geq 2+2(2L\alpha)^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1})
such that
L|t
. Similarly, we conclude that under the same conditions on
t
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{i+t}=\vec{S}|\vec{Z}_{i}=\vec{T}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{S}}

for any
\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
. Therefore, we obtain

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{S}]=\sum_{\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t\bmod L}=\vec{T}]\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{S}|\vec{Z}_{t\bmod L}=\vec{T}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{S}}

for any
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
and any
t\geq L(2+2(2L\alpha)^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1}))+L-1=3L-1+\alpha^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1})
. In particular, the bound holds for all
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
.
∎

We now show that a random walk on
G
satisfies a similar rapid mixing property.

Corollary 5.6
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
(R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a random walk on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Then, for any vertex
v\in V(G)
and
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[R_{t}=v]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\frac{1}{n}.

Proof.

Let
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the corresponding random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
and choose
\alpha
as in Lemma
5.5
. Then for all
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
and
v\in V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R_{t}=v]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}\in\{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)|s_{1}=v\}]=\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)\colon s_{1}=v}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{S}]

\displaystyle=(k-2)!\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}(1\pm e^{-\alpha t})\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{(k-1)!n}=(1\pm e^{-\alpha t})\frac{1}{n}

where the last step follows from Lemma
3.9(iii)
.
∎

5.3.
Random walks and vertex subsets

The following lemma shows that with high probability, a random walk in
G
behaves as expected with regard to a subset
U\subseteq V(G)
due to the rapid mixing property (Corollary
5.6
).

Lemma 5.7
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
c\in\mathbb{R}
and let
\mathbf{w}:V(G)\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
be a vertex weighting with
\mathbf{w}_{v}\leq c
for all
v\in V(G)
. Let
U\subseteq V(G)
. Let
R=(R_{1},...,R_{m})
be a random walk on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
, where
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Then

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{v\in R\cap U}\mathbf{w}_{v}-\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|\geq cn^{\frac{1}{3}-\alpha}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

Proof.

Choose
\alpha
such that Corollary
5.6
holds. We partition
R
into
\ell
consecutive disjoint random walks
Q_{1}=(Q_{1,1},...Q_{1,m_{1}})
,
...
,
Q_{\ell}=(Q_{\ell,1},...,Q_{\ell,m_{\ell}})
such that
m^{1-\alpha}\leq|Q_{i}|\leq 2m^{1-\alpha}
for all
i\in[\ell]
. Then, we have
m^{\alpha}/2\leq\ell\leq m^{\alpha}
. For
i\in[\ell]
, let
Q_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq(Q_{i,\ln m},...,Q_{i,m_{i}})
; that is, we delete the first
\ln m
vertices from
Q_{i}
to obtain
Q_{i}^{\prime}
. Since only a few vertices are removed in this process, their contribution is negligible in the final estimate. Then Corollary
5.6
applies to every step in
Q_{i}^{\prime}
, since
\ln m>\alpha^{-2}
. We write
Q_{i}-Q_{i}^{\prime}=(Q_{i,1},...,Q_{i,\ln m-1})
, then
|Q_{i}-Q_{i}^{\prime}|\leq\ln m
. Note that

(5.2)

m-m^{\alpha}\ln m\leq|R|-\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|Q_{i}-Q_{i}^{\prime}|=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|Q_{i}^{\prime}|\leq|R|=m.

For each
i\in[\ell]
, let
J_{i}\coloneqq\{j\in[m]|R_{j}\in Q_{i}-Q_{i}^{\prime}\}
,
J_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq\{j\in[m]|R_{j}\in Q_{i}^{\prime}\}
, and

W_{i}\coloneqq\sum_{j\in J_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}\leq c\sum_{j\in J_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}\leq c|Q_{i}^{\prime}|.

Consider the exposure martingale
Y_{i}\coloneqq\mathbb{E}[\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}W_{i^{\prime}}|W_{1},...,W_{i}]
, for
i\in[\ell]_{0}
. Our plan is to use Azuma’s inequality to show that
Y_{0}
and
Y_{\ell}
are close to each other. Moreover, they are each close to one of the two sums in the statement of this lemma. Then we use triangle inequality to obtain the desired result.

Let
i\in[\ell]
and
w_{1},...,w_{i},w_{i}^{\prime}\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
be such that
w_{i^{\prime}}\leq c|Q_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|
for each
i^{\prime}\in[i]
and
w_{i}^{\prime}\leq c|Q_{i}^{\prime}|
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
W_{1}=w_{1},...,W_{i}=w_{i}
occurs, and let
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
be the event that
W_{1}=w_{1},...,W_{i-1}=w_{i-1},W_{i}=w_{i}^{\prime}
occurs. Fix
i<i^{\prime}\leq\ell
. By Corollary
5.6
, for all
j\in J_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}
and
v\in V(G)
, we have

\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v|\mathcal{E}]=(1\pm e^{-\alpha\ln m})\frac{1}{n}=(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}.

Using linearity of expectation, this implies

(5.3)

\mathbb{E}[W_{i^{\prime}}|\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{j\in J_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbb{E}[\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}=v}|\mathcal{E}]=\sum_{j\in J_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\mathbb{P}[R_{j}=v|\mathcal{E}]=|Q_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|\cdot(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}.

The same result holds when
\mathcal{E}
is replaced by
\mathcal{E}^{\prime}
. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\left|\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}W_{i^{\prime}}\right|\mathcal{E}\right]-\mathbb{E}\left[\left.\sum_{i^{\prime}=1}^{\ell}W_{i^{\prime}}\right|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}\right]\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}|w_{i}-w_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{\ell}|\mathbb{E}[W_{i^{\prime}}|\mathcal{E}]-\mathbb{E}[W_{i^{\prime}}|\mathcal{E}^{\prime}]|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}c|Q_{i}^{\prime}|+\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{\ell}\left||Q_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}-|Q_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\mathrel{}c\cdot 2m^{1-\alpha}+2m^{-\alpha}\frac{1}{n}\sum_{i^{\prime}=i+1}^{\ell}|Q_{i^{\prime}}^{\prime}|\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\leq 4cm^{1-\alpha}.

Hence,
|Y_{i}-Y_{i-1}|\leq 4cm^{1-\alpha}
for all
i\in[\ell]
. Using Azuma’s inequality and
\ell\leq m^{\alpha}
, we have

(5.4)

\mathbb{P}[|Y_{\ell}-Y_{0}|\geq cm^{1-\frac{\alpha}{3}}]\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{\left(cm^{1-\frac{\alpha}{3}}\right)^{2}}{2\ell\left(4cm^{1-\alpha}\right)^{2}}\right)\leq 2\exp\left(-\frac{m^{\alpha/3}}{32}\right)\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

Similar to (
5.3
), we obtain

Y_{0}=\mathbb{E}\left[\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}W_{i}\right]=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\mathbb{E}[W_{i}]=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}|Q_{i}^{\prime}|(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}.

Using (
5.2
), we have

\displaystyle Y_{0}

\displaystyle=(m\pm m^{\alpha}\ln m)(1\pm m^{-\alpha})\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\pm(m^{\alpha}\ln m+m^{1-\alpha}+\ln m)\frac{1}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}

(5.5)

\displaystyle=\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\pm 2cm^{1-\alpha}.

Moreover, by construction, we have

Y_{\ell}\leq\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}=\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j\in J_{i}^{\prime}}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}+\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}\sum_{j\in J_{i}}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}\leq\sum_{i=1}^{\ell}W_{i}+cm^{\alpha}\ln m\leq Y_{\ell}+cm^{1-\alpha}.

Combining this with (
5.5
), by triangle inequality, we have

\displaystyle\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}-\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}-Y_{\ell}\right|+|Y_{\ell}-Y_{0}|+\left|Y_{0}-\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|

\displaystyle\leq|Y_{\ell}-Y_{0}|+3cm^{1-\frac{\alpha}{3}}.

So using (
5.4
), we conclude

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{v\in R\cap U}\mathbf{w}_{v}-\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|\geq cn^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{10}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{j=1}^{m}\mathbf{w}_{R_{j}}\mathbbm{1}_{R_{j}\in U}-\frac{m}{n}\sum_{v\in U}\mathbf{w}_{v}\right|\geq 4cm^{1-\frac{\alpha}{3}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{P}[|Y_{\ell}-Y_{0}|\geq cm^{1-\frac{\alpha}{3}}]\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

We obtain the desired result by redefining
\alpha
to be
\alpha/10
.
∎

In particular, we apply the above lemma to obtain the following bounds on the number of vertices visited in a fixed set
U
and how the transition probabilities and the entropy scale with respect to the neighbourhood of some
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
.

Corollary 5.8
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
R=(R_{1},...,R_{m})
be a random walk on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
, where
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Then the following holds.

(i)

For every
U\subseteq V(G)
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left||R\cap U|-\frac{m}{n}|U|\right|\geq n^{\frac{1}{3}-\alpha}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

(ii)

For every
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{v\in R\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]-\frac{m}{n}\right|\geq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

(iii)

For every
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
,

\mathbb{P}\left[\left|\sum_{v\in R\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}-\frac{m}{n}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\right|\geq n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\right]\leq\frac{1}{n^{k}}.

Proof.

Let
\alpha
be such that Lemma
5.7
holds with
2\alpha
playing the role of
\alpha
. Using the same notation as in Lemma
5.7
, (i) follows by choosing
\mathbf{w}\equiv 1
and
c=1
.

Fix
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
. Using Lemma
3.9(vi)
, we obtain (ii) by choosing
\mathbf{w}_{v}=\mathbf{y}[S\to v]
for all
v\in V(G)
,
c=\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n}
and
U=N_{G}(S)
. Lastly, (iii) follows by choosing
\mathbf{w}_{v}=\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}
for all
v\in V(G)
,
c=\frac{b^{2}}{\delta n}\log(b^{2}n)
and
U=N_{G}(S)
.
∎

We now define some desirable properties of a random walk.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
0<\alpha<1/3
. We say that a set
M\subseteq V(G)
is
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved
if for each
S\in\mathcal{S}(G)
, the following holds.

(i)

\left||M\cap N_{G}(S)|-\frac{|M|}{n}|N_{G}(S)|\right|<n^{\frac{1}{3}-\alpha}
.

(ii)

\left|\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]-\frac{|M|}{n}\right|<n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}
.

(iii)

\left|\sum_{v\in M\cap N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to v]\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{y}[S\to v]}-\frac{|M|}{n}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\right|<n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}
.

Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. We say that
Z
is
self-avoiding
if
Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i-1}\not\subseteq\cup_{j=0}^{i-1}Z_{j}
for all
i\in[m]
. That is, the corresponding walk in
G
is a path. Let
R=(R_{1},...,R_{m})
be the random walk on
G
corresponding to the first
m
vertices in
Z
. We say that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved
if
Z
is self-avoiding and
R
is
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved. In this case, we also say that
R
is a
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved path
.

The following lemma shows that with high probability, a random walk is self-avoiding; to this end, a simple union bound suffices, compare
[
4
]
and
[
8
]
. We omit the short and obvious proof.

Lemma 5.9
.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
m\geq 0
and
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Suppose every edge
e\in E(G)
satisfies
\mathbf{x}_{e}\leq b/n^{k-1}
for some
b\geq 1
. Then

\mathbb{P}[Z\text{ is self-avoiding}]\geq 1-\frac{2m^{2}b^{2}}{n}.

All together, we show that with high probability, a random walk is
(\mathbb{S}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved.

Corollary 5.10
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
, where
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved. Then
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-n^{-1/4}
.

Proof.

Using Corollary
5.8
and Lemma
5.9
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-\frac{2m^{2}b^{2}}{n}-3|\mathcal{S}(G)|\frac{1}{n^{k}}\geq 1-2b^{2}n^{-\frac{1}{3}}-3{n\choose k-1}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\geq 1-n^{-\frac{1}{4}},

which completes the proof.
∎

6.
Counting Hamilton cycles

6.1.
Counting well-behaved random walks

We now find a lower bound on the entropy of a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
. This then provides a lower bound on the number of such random walks. For convenience, given an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
G
, we define

\overline{h}(G)\coloneqq h(G)-\frac{n}{k}\log{n\choose k-1}+\frac{n}{k}\log n,

since this expression appears frequently in this section. Note that
\overline{h}(G)=\Theta(n\log n)
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b,\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Fix
\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
such that
\vec{Z}_{0}=\vec{T}
and
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Then
H(Z)\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}(G)-2k\varepsilon m
.

Proof.

We use the chain rule and Corollary
5.5
to obtain the desired result. Choose
\alpha
with
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
such that Corollary
5.5
holds. Let
a\coloneqq\ln n+1>\alpha^{-2}
. Using the definition of a Markov chain, for
a\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{i-1})

\displaystyle=H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{i-1})=\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{i-1}=\vec{S}]H(\vec{Z}_{i}|{\vec{Z}_{i-1}=\vec{S}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)}\left(1-e^{-\alpha\ln n}\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}_{S}}{(k-1)!n}\cdot h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{1}{n}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S).

Using Lemma
3.9(iv)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathrel{}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\geq\mathrel{}kh(\mathbf{x})-n\log{n\choose k-1}+n\log n\geq k\overline{h}(G)-k\varepsilon n.

Using the chain rule of entropy (Fact
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle H(Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{i-1})\geq(m-a+1)\cdot\left(1-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{1}{n}\left(k\overline{h}(G)-k\varepsilon n\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-n^{-\frac{1}{3}}\ln n-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{m}{n}\left(k\overline{h}(G)-k\varepsilon n\right)\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}(G)-2k\varepsilon m,

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 6.2
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b,\varepsilon
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Fix
\vec{S}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}(G)
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
such that
\vec{Z}_{0}=\vec{S}
and
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the set of
(\mathbb{S}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved realizations of
Z
. Then
\log|\mathcal{Z}|\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}(G)-3k\varepsilon m
.

Proof.

Choose
\alpha
with
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
such that Corollary
5.10
holds. Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the event that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved. By Corollary
5.10
, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-n^{-1/4}
. Let
W
be any realization of
Z
, then Lemma
3.9(vi)
yields
\mathbb{P}[Z=W]\geq(b^{2}n)^{-m}.
By Facts
3.6
and
3.8
as well as Lemma
6.1
, we have

\log|\mathcal{Z}|\geq H(Z|\mathcal{E})\geq H(Z)-2n^{-\frac{1}{4}}\log(b^{2}n)^{m}\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}(G)-3k\varepsilon m,

as desired.
∎

6.2.
Counting long paths

The next lemma forms the basis for iterating Corollary
6.2
. It states that if we remove a
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved path from an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
G
, the resulting subgraph remains
\delta^{\prime}
-Dirac for some
\delta^{\prime}
only slightly smaller than
\delta
. This allows us to apply Corollary
6.2
again to the subgraph.

Lemma 6.3
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll 1/b\ll\varepsilon\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Suppose
P=v_{1}...v_{m}
is a
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved path, where
m\coloneqq n^{1/3}
. Let
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-P
,
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n-m
,
\delta^{\prime}\coloneqq\delta-2n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}
and
b^{\prime}\coloneqq(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}})b
. Then
G^{\prime}
is an
(n^{\prime},k,\delta^{\prime})
-graph, and there exists a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that
h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\geq h(G^{\prime})-\varepsilon n^{\prime}
and

(6.1)

h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Proof.

Choose
\varepsilon
and
b\geq 4
such that Lemma
3.5
holds for the parameters
\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}
) and
k
. Choose
\alpha
such that Lemma
4.1
holds. Since
P
is
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved, for each
S\in\mathcal{S}(G^{\prime})
, we have

\displaystyle|N_{G^{\prime}}(S)|

\displaystyle=|N_{G}(S)|-|N_{G}(S)\cap P|\geq|N_{G}(S)|-\frac{m}{n}|N_{G}(S)|-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\alpha}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}\delta n-\frac{n^{\frac{1}{3}-\alpha}}{n-m}n^{\prime}\geq\left(\delta-2n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\right)n^{\prime}>\frac{1}{2}n^{\prime}.

Thus,
G^{\prime}
is an
(n^{\prime},k,\delta^{\prime})
-graph. By Lemma
4.1
, there exists a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{z}
of
G^{\prime}
satisfying (
6.1
). By the choice of
b
and Lemma
3.5
, there exists a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{z}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that
h(\mathbf{z}^{\prime})\geq h(G^{\prime})-\varepsilon n^{\prime}
. Since
b^{\prime}>b
, taking
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
to be the one between

∎

Lemma 6.4
.

Suppose

holds for the parameters
\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}

. Choose

\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}

and
2b
(instead of

,

\delta_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
and

in the following procedure. Let

,

,

,

,
\mathbf{x}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbf{x}
and
\vec{S}_{0}\coloneqq\vec{S}

At each step

\displaystyle G_{i+1}

\displaystyle n_{i+1}

\displaystyle b_{i+1}

\displaystyle\geq\delta_{0}-2in_{0}^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\geq\delta-2\kappa n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\geq\delta-2n^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\beta}{3}}n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}\geq\delta-2n^{\frac{\alpha}{9}-\alpha}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}>\frac{1}{2},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\leq\left(1+n_{\kappa}^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)^{i}b_{0}\leq\left(1+n^{(1-\beta)(-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2})}\right)^{\kappa}b\leq\left(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{4}}\right)^{n^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\beta}{3}}}b\leq 2b.

,

is an

is immediate. For

, starting with (

) and followed by the induction hypothesis, we have

\displaystyle h(\mathbf{x}_{i})

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-1}}h(\mathbf{x}_{i-1})-\frac{k-1}{k}n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}-n_{i-1}^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i-1}}\left(\frac{n_{i-1}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n_{i-1}\log\frac{n}{n_{i-1}}-(i-1)n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)-\frac{k-1}{k}n_{i}\log\frac{n_{i-1}}{n_{i}}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n_{i}\log\frac{n}{n_{i}}-in^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

6.5

from below. By Corollary

and equation (

\displaystyle\log z_{i}

\displaystyle\geq k\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}h(G_{i})-m_{i}\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}+m_{i}\log n_{i}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}

\displaystyle\geq k\frac{m_{i}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-(k-1)m_{i}\log\frac{n}{n_{i}}-ik\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-m_{i}\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}+m_{i}\log n_{i}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}.

.
Using Stirling’s approximation, we obtain

\displaystyle\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}m_{i}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}\log n_{i}^{m_{i}}\geq\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}\log\frac{n_{i}!}{(n_{i}-m_{i})!}=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}(\log n_{i}!-\log n_{i+1}!)=\log n!-\log n_{\kappa+1}!

(6.6)

\displaystyle\geq n\log\frac{n}{e}-\log n-n_{\kappa+1}\log\frac{n_{\kappa+1}}{e}-\log n_{\kappa+1}\geq n\log n-n\log e-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n.

(6.7)

\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}i\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}i\frac{1}{n_{\kappa}^{2/3}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\frac{\kappa^{2}}{(n^{1-\beta})^{2/3}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq n^{\frac{4}{3}+\frac{2\beta}{3}}n^{-\frac{2}{3}+\frac{2\beta}{3}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n.

Note that for

(k-1)\log\frac{n}{n_{i}}+\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}\leq\mathrel{}\log\left[\frac{n}{n_{i}}\cdot\frac{n-1}{n_{i}-1}\cdot...\cdot\frac{n-k+2}{n_{i}-k+2}\cdot{n_{i}\choose k-1}\right]=\log{n\choose k-1}.

-graph

We omit the standard proof. It follows directly by choosing a set

Lemma 6.6

(

Suppose

We are now able to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.6
.

Choose
\beta
and
\varepsilon
such that Lemma
6.4

(instead of
\hat{\delta}

Theorem

7.
Conclusion

In this paper we established a tight lower bound for the number of tight Hamilton cycles in
k
-graphs with large minimum
(k-1)
-degree, thereby solving a conjecture of Ferber, Hardiman and Mond in a strong form.

Proving that our bound is in fact tight up to the error term in the exponent for
all

k
-graphs
G
with
\delta_{k-1}(G)\geq(1/2+o(1))n
is an obvious interesting open problem (this is true for graphs).
Observe that for graphs, a tight upper bound on the number of Hamilton cycles
\Psi(G)
can be easily deduced from a tight upper bound on the number of perfect matchings
\Phi(G)
by noting that
\Psi(G)\leq(\Phi(G))^{2}
.
On the exponential scale, this is tight because it is not so unlikely that two randomly chosen perfect matchings form a Hamilton cycle in graphs; whereas in
k
-graphs,
k
randomly chosen perfect matchings form a tight Hamilton cycle only with exponentially small probability.

\mathbf{y}\left[\vec{e}\vec{f}\right]\coloneqq\begin{cases}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{f}}{(k-\ell)!\mathbf{x}[e^{(\ell)+}]}&\text{if }\mathbf{x}[e^{(\ell)+}]\neq 0\\

\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
and the ordered
\ell
-walks in

.

(iv)

\frac{\delta}{k^{k}bn^{\ell-1}}\leq\mathbf{x}_{S}\leq\frac{b}{n^{\ell-1}}

for all
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)
, and

(v)

\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell}}\leq\mathbf{y}[\vec{e}\vec{f}]\leq\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta n^{k-\ell}}

for all
\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])
.

Proof.

(i) and (ii) follow from the following calculations.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\sum_{X\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup X]={k\choose\ell}\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}={k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k},\text{ and}

\displaystyle\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle=\sum_{v\in S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\sum_{X\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup X]={k-1\choose\ell-1}\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}={k-1\choose\ell-1}

for every vertex
v\in V(G)
. Next, suppose
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal. To prove (iii), we calculate

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\sum_{X\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{y}[S\to X]\log\frac{(k-\ell)!}{\mathbf{y}[S\to X]}

\displaystyle=\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\sum_{X\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup X]\log\frac{(k-\ell)!\mathbf{x}_{S}}{\mathbf{x}[S\cup X]}

\displaystyle={k\choose\ell}\sum_{e\in E(G)}\mathbf{x}_{e}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{e}}+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log(k-\ell)!+\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\mathbf{x}_{S}

\displaystyle\geq{k\choose\ell}h(\mathbf{x})+{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log(k-\ell)!-{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{{n\choose\ell}}{{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}}

\displaystyle={k\choose\ell}h(\mathbf{x})-{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{k{n\choose\ell}}{n{k\choose\ell}(k-\ell)!}

\displaystyle={k\choose\ell}h(\mathbf{x})-{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n},

where the inequality follows from Jensen’s inequality applied to the concave function
t\mapsto\log t
, as shown below.

\displaystyle\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\log\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\right)\log\frac{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}\frac{1}{\mathbf{x}_{S}}}{\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}}={k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log\frac{{n\choose\ell}}{{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}}.

This proves (iii). For each
S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)
, by Corollary
8.1
, we have

\frac{\delta}{k^{k}bn^{\ell-1}}\leq\frac{\delta}{k^{k}}n^{k-\ell}\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\leq\delta_{\ell}(G)\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\leq\sum_{X\in N_{G}(S)}\mathbf{x}[S\cup X]\leq{n\choose k-\ell}\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}\leq\frac{b}{n^{\ell-1}}.

This proves (iv). For each
\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])
, we exploit the previous inequality and obtain

\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell}}=\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!}\frac{1}{bn^{k-1}}\frac{n^{\ell-1}}{b}\leq\mathbf{y}[\vec{e}\vec{f}]\leq\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!}\frac{b}{n^{k-1}}\frac{k^{k}bn^{\ell-1}}{\delta}=\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta n^{k-\ell}},

which proves (v).
∎

We note that Lemma
4.1
still holds if
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor(k-\ell)
, for any
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
.

9.
Probabilistic properties of random
\ell
-walks

In this section, we study random
\ell
-walks in an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and in its auxiliary graph defined in Section
8.1
. As in the
\ell=k-1
case, we model these walks as Markov chains and show that after sufficiently many steps, the probability of visiting any vertex is roughly uniform.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
be as defined in Section
8.1
and
\mathbf{y}
be the corresponding edge weighting induced by
\mathbf{x}
. A
random walk
on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
is the Markov chain
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with state space
\vec{E}(G)
and a transition matrix
P=(p_{\vec{e}\vec{f}})_{\vec{e},\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)}
, defined by

p_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}=\begin{cases}\mathbf{y}[\vec{e}\vec{f}]&\quad\text{if }\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])\\
0&\quad\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

A
random (ordered)
\ell
-walk
on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
is a sequence
(R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
of random variables with sample space
V(G)
such that if
\vec{Z}_{t}\coloneqq(R_{(k-\ell)t},...,R_{(k-\ell)t+k-1})
for all
t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
, then
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. We note that every random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
yields a random
\ell
-walk on
G
.

9.1.
Convergence of random
\ell
-walks

We need the following generalized version of Lemma
5.4
. Recall that an
\ell
-walk of length
L
has
\ell
edges and
(k-\ell)L+\ell
vertices.

Corollary 9.1
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\zeta\ll 1/L\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph. For any
\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}(G)
, there are at least
\zeta n^{(k-\ell)L-\ell}

\ell
-walks of length
L
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
in
G
.

Proof.

Take
\vec{S},\vec{T}\in\vec{\mathcal{S}}_{\ell}(G)
. There are
N\coloneqq(k-1-\ell)!{n-\ell\choose k-1-\ell}
ways to extend
\vec{S}
to some
\vec{S}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(G)
such that
\vec{S}^{\prime(\ell)-}=\vec{S}
. Similarly, there are
N
ways to extend
\vec{T}
to some
\vec{T}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(G)
such that
\vec{T}^{\prime(\ell)+}=\vec{T}
. By Lemma
5.4
, we may choose
1/n\ll\zeta^{\prime}\ll 1/L^{\prime}\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
with
(k-\ell)|(L^{\prime}-1)
such that there are
\zeta^{\prime}n^{L^{\prime}-(k-1)}

(k-1)
-walks
W
of length
L^{\prime}
from each
\vec{S}^{\prime}
to
\vec{T}^{\prime}
. Let

L=\frac{L^{\prime}-1}{k-\ell}+1\quad\text{and}\quad\zeta=\left(\frac{(k-\ell-1)!}{(2(k-1-\ell))^{k-1-\ell}}\right)^{2}\zeta^{\prime},

so that
L^{\prime}+(k-1)=(k-\ell)L+\ell
. By considering a subset of edges, each
W
contains an
\ell
-walk of length
L
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
. Thus, there are at least

N^{2}\zeta^{\prime}n^{L^{\prime}-(k-1)}\geq\left(\frac{(k-\ell-1)!n^{k-\ell-1}}{(2(k-1-\ell))^{k-1-\ell}}\right)^{2}\zeta^{\prime}n^{(k-\ell)(L-1)+1-(k-1)}\geq\zeta n^{(k-\ell)L-\ell}

\ell
-walks of length
L
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
.
∎

We now apply the rapid mixing property of Markov chains to the random walks in
\mathcal{S}_{\ell}[G]
.

Corollary 9.2
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Then, for any ordered edge
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
and
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{e}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}}{(k-1)!n}.

Proof.

Using the same notation as the beginning of Section
9
,
(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
is a Markov chain with state space
\vec{E}(G)
and transition matrix
P=(p_{\vec{e}\vec{f}})_{\vec{e},\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)}
. By Lemma
8.2(v)
, we have the following bounds on the nonzero entries of
P
,

\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell}}\leq p_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}\leq\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta n^{k-\ell}}.

By Corollary
9.1
, there exist
1/n\ll\zeta\ll 1/L\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
such that for any
\vec{e},\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)
, the
k
-graph
G
has at least
\zeta n^{(k-\ell)(L-1)-\ell}

\ell
-walks of length
L-1
from
\vec{e}^{(\ell)+}
to
\vec{f}^{(\ell)-}
, and hence
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
has at least
\zeta n^{(k-\ell)(L-1)-\ell}
walks of length
L
from
\vec{e}
to
\vec{f}
. This allows us to define a new Markov chain
(\vec{Z}_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
on
\vec{E}(G)
with a strictly positive transition matrix
Q\coloneqq P^{L}
. We have the following bounds on the entries of
Q=(q_{\vec{e}\vec{f}})_{\vec{e},\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)}
,

(9.1)

\displaystyle q_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}

\displaystyle\leq n^{(k-\ell)(L-1)-\ell}\left(\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta n^{k-\ell}}\right)^{L}=\left(\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta}\right)^{L}n^{-k}\text{, and}

(9.2)

\displaystyle q_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}

\displaystyle\geq\zeta n^{(k-\ell)(L-1)-\ell}\left(\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell}}\right)^{L}=\zeta\left(\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}}\right)^{L}n^{-k}.

We define
\sigma\coloneqq(\sigma_{\vec{e}})_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}
by

\sigma_{\vec{e}}\coloneqq\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}}{(k-1)!n}

for each
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
. We claim that
\sigma
is the stationary distribution of the Markov chain
(Z_{t}^{\prime})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
.

Indeed, using Lemma
3.9(i)
, it is straightforward to verify that
\sigma
is a probability distribution. It remains to show that
\sigma
is a left eigenvector of
P
, and hence a left eigenvector of
Q
. For all
\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)
, we have

\displaystyle(\sigma P)_{\vec{f}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\sigma_{\vec{e}}p_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}=\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\sigma_{\vec{e}}\mathbf{y}[\vec{e}\vec{f}]\mathbbm{1}[\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}}{(k-1)!n}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{f}}{(k-\ell)!\mathbf{x}[e^{(\ell)+}]}\mathbbm{1}[\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])]

\displaystyle=\frac{\mathbf{x}_{f}}{(k-1)!n}\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}\mathbbm{1}[\vec{e}\vec{f}\in E(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G])]}{(k-\ell)!\mathbf{x}[f^{(\ell)-}]}

\displaystyle=\sigma_{\vec{f}}\sum_{X\in N_{G}(f^{(\ell)-})}\frac{\mathbf{x}[X\cup f^{(\ell)-}]}{\mathbf{x}[f^{(\ell)-}]}=\sigma_{\vec{f}},

which proves our claim.

Since
\mathbf{x}
is
b
-normal, for each
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
, we have

\frac{1}{b(k-1)!n^{k}}\leq\sigma_{\vec{e}}\leq\frac{b}{(k-1)!n^{k}}.

Together with (
9.1
) and (
9.2
), this allows us to bound
c_{1}
and
c_{2}
as defined in Lemma
5.1
. We have

\displaystyle c_{1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\inf_{\vec{e},\vec{f},\vec{g}\in\vec{E}(G)}\frac{q_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}}{\sigma_{\vec{g}}}\geq\zeta\left(\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}}\right)^{L}\cdot n^{-k}\cdot\frac{(k-1)!n^{k}}{b}=\zeta\left(\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}}\right)^{L}\frac{(k-1)!}{b},\text{ and}

\displaystyle c_{2}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\sup_{\vec{e},\vec{f},\vec{g}\in\vec{E}(G)}\frac{q_{\vec{e}\vec{f}}}{\sigma_{\vec{g}}}\leq\left(\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta}\right)^{L}\cdot n^{-k}\cdot b(k-1)!n^{k}=\left(\frac{k^{k}b^{2}}{(k-\ell)!\delta}\right)^{L}b(k-1)!.

Let

\alpha\coloneqq\min\left\{\frac{\zeta}{2L}\left(\frac{1}{(k-\ell)!b^{2}}\right)^{L}\frac{(k-1)!}{b},\left(\frac{(k-\ell)!\delta}{k^{k}b^{2}}\right)^{L}\frac{1}{b(k-1)!}\right\}

so that
c_{1}\geq 2L\alpha
and
c_{2}\leq\alpha^{-1}
.
Using the inequality
(1-x)^{t}\leq e^{-xt}
, Lemma
5.1
yields

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{e}]=\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t/L}^{\prime}=\vec{e}]=\left(1\pm\left(1-\frac{c_{1}}{2}\right)^{\frac{t}{L}}\right)\sigma_{\vec{e}}=\left(1\pm\exp\left(-\frac{c_{1}t}{2L}\right)\right)\sigma_{\vec{e}}=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{e}}

for any
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
and any
t/L\geq 2+2(2L\alpha)^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1})
such that
L|t
. Similarly, we conclude that under the same conditions on
t
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{i+t}=\vec{e}|\vec{Z}_{i}=\vec{f}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{e}}

for any
\vec{e},\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)
. Therefore, we obtain

\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{e}]=\sum_{\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t\bmod L}=\vec{f}]\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{t}=\vec{e}|\vec{Z}_{t\bmod L}=\vec{f}]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\sigma_{\vec{e}}

for any
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
and any
t\geq L(2+2(2L\alpha)^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1}))+L-1=3L-1+\alpha^{-1}\log(\alpha^{-1})
. In particular, the bound holds for all
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
.
∎

We now show that a random
\ell
-walk on
G
satisfies a similar rapid mixing property.

Corollary 9.3
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
(R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be a random
\ell
-walk on
G
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Then, for any vertex
v\in V(G)
and
t\geq\alpha^{-2}
, we have

\mathbb{P}[R_{t}=v]=\left(1\pm e^{-\alpha t}\right)\frac{1}{n}.

Proof.

Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
be the corresponding random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
and choose
\alpha
as in Lemma
9.2
. Fix
t\geq\alpha^{-2}(k-\ell)
. Let
\tau\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
and
\tau^{\prime}\in[k]
be indices such that
\vec{Z}_{\tau}=(s_{1},...,s_{k})
is the first step in
Z
that contains
R_{t}
and
R_{t}=s_{\tau^{\prime}}
. Note that
\tau\geq\alpha^{-2}
. So for all
v\in V(G)
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{P}[R_{t}=v]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{\tau}\in\{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)|e_{\tau^{\prime}}=v\}]=\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)\colon e_{\tau^{\prime}}=v}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{\tau}=\vec{e}]

\displaystyle=(k-1)!\sum_{v\in e\in E(G)}(1\pm e^{-\alpha t})\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}}{(k-1)!n}=(1\pm e^{-\alpha t})\frac{1}{n}

which completes the proof.
∎

9.2.
Random walks and vertex subsets

Recall the definition of well-behavedness at the end of Section
5
, we now extend it to
\ell
-walks.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
0<\alpha<1/3
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. We say that
Z
is
self-avoiding
if
Z_{i}\setminus Z_{i-1}\not\subseteq\cup_{j=0}^{i-1}Z_{j}
for all
i\in[m]
. That is, the corresponding
\ell
-walk in
G
is an
\ell
-path. Let
R=(R_{1},...,R_{m^{\prime}+\ell})
be the random
\ell
-walk on
G
corresponding to the first
m^{\prime}+\ell
vertices in
Z
, where
m_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq m(k-\ell)
. We say that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved
if
Z
is self-avoiding and
R
is
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved. In this case, we also say that
R
is a
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved
\ell
-path
.

Using a union bound, it is straightforward to show that with high probability, a random
\ell
-walk is self-avoiding.

Lemma 9.4
.

Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
for some
b\geq 1
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
,
m\geq 0
and
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
. Then

\mathbb{P}[Z\text{ is self-avoiding}]\geq 1-\frac{2m^{2}k^{k+1}b^{2}}{n}.

We note that for
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
, Corollary
5.8
generalizes directly to random
\ell
-walks
(R_{t})_{t\in\mathbb{N}_{0}}
with
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor(k-\ell)
. All together, we show that with high probability, a random
\ell
-walk is
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved.

Corollary 9.5
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
, where
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{E}
be the event that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved. Then
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-n^{-1/4}
.

Proof.

Using the generalization of Corollary
5.8
and Lemma
9.4
, we have

\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-\frac{2m^{2}k^{k+1}b^{2}}{n}-3|\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(G)|\frac{1}{n^{k}}\geq 1-2k^{k+1}b^{2}n^{-\frac{1}{3}}-3{n\choose k-1}\frac{1}{n^{k}}\geq 1-n^{-\frac{1}{4}},

which completes the proof.
∎

10.
Counting Hamilton
\ell
-cycles

10.1.
Counting well-behaved random walks

We now find a lower bound on the entropy of a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
. This then provides a lower bound on the number of such random walks. For convenience, given an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
G
and
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
, we define

\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)\coloneqq h(G)-\frac{n}{k}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}.

Note that
\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)=\Theta(n\log n)
.

Lemma 10.1
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b,\varepsilon
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Fix
\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G)
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
such that
\vec{Z}_{0}=\vec{f}
and
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
. Then
H(Z)\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-2k\varepsilon m
.

Proof.

We use the chain rule and Corollary
9.2
to obtain the desired result. Choose
\alpha
with
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
such that Corollary
9.2
holds. Let
a\coloneqq\ln n+1>\alpha^{-2}
. Using the definition of a Markov chain, for
a\leq i\leq m
, we have

\displaystyle H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{i-1})

\displaystyle=H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{i-1})=\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\mathbb{P}[\vec{Z}_{i-1}=\vec{e}]H(\vec{Z}_{i}|{\vec{Z}_{i-1}=\vec{e}})

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)}\left(1-e^{-\alpha\ln n}\right)\frac{\mathbf{x}_{e}}{(k-1)!n}h_{\mathbf{x}}(e^{(\ell)+})

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{1}{n}\frac{(k-\ell)!\ell!}{(k-1)!}\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S).

Using Lemma
8.2(iii)
, we have

\sum_{S\in\mathcal{S}_{\ell}(G)}\mathbf{x}_{S}h_{\mathbf{x}}(S)\geq{k\choose\ell}h(\mathbf{x})-{k\choose\ell}\frac{n}{k}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}\geq{k\choose\ell}\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-{k\choose\ell}\varepsilon n.

Using the chain rule of entropy (Fact
3.7
), we have

\displaystyle H(Z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=1}^{m}H(\vec{Z}_{i}|\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{i-1})\geq(m-a+1)\left(1-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{1}{n}\left(k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-k\varepsilon n\right)

\displaystyle\geq\left(1-2n^{-\frac{1}{3}}\ln n-n^{-\alpha}\right)\frac{m}{n}\left(k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-k\varepsilon n\right)\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-2k\varepsilon m,

which completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 10.2
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b,\varepsilon
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Fix
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
. Let
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m})
be a random walk on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G]
induced by
\mathbf{x}
such that
\vec{Z}_{0}=\vec{e}
and
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor
. Let
\mathcal{Z}
be the set of
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved realizations of
Z
. Then

\log|\mathcal{Z}|\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-3k\varepsilon m
.

Proof.

Choose
\alpha
with
1/n\ll\alpha\ll\hat{\delta},1/k,1/b
such that Corollary
9.5
holds. Let
\mathcal{E}
denote the event that
Z
is
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G],\alpha)
-well-behaved. By Corollary
9.5
, we have
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{E}]\geq 1-n^{-1/4}
. Let
W
be any realization of
Z
, then Lemma
8.2(v)
yields
\mathbb{P}[Z=W]\geq((k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell})^{-m}.
By Facts
3.6
and
3.8
as well as Lemma
10.1
, we have

\log|\mathcal{Z}|\geq H(Z|\mathcal{E})\geq H(Z)-2n^{-\frac{1}{4}}\log((k-\ell)!b^{2}n^{k-\ell})^{m}\geq\frac{m}{n}k\overline{h}_{\ell}(G)-3k\varepsilon m,

as desired.
∎

10.2.
Counting long paths

The next lemma generalizes Lemma
6.3
and forms the basis for iterating Corollary
10.2
. The proof is the same as the special case of
\ell=k-1
(Lemma
6.3
). Recall that Lemma
4.1
still holds when
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor(k-\ell)
, for any
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
.

Lemma 10.3
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\alpha\ll 1/b\ll\varepsilon\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph and
\mathbf{x}
be a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G
such that
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Suppose
P=v_{1}...v_{m}
is a
(G,\alpha)
-well-behaved
\ell
-path, where
m\coloneqq\lfloor n^{1/3}\rfloor(k-\ell)
. Let
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-P
,
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n-m
,
\delta^{\prime}\coloneqq\delta-2n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha}
and
b^{\prime}\coloneqq(1+n^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}})b
. Then
G^{\prime}
is an
(n^{\prime},k,\delta^{\prime})
-graph, and there exists a
b^{\prime}
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}^{\prime}
of
G^{\prime}
such that
h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\geq h(G^{\prime})-\varepsilon n^{\prime}
and

(10.1)

h(\mathbf{x}^{\prime})\geq\frac{n^{\prime}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n^{\prime}\log\frac{n}{n^{\prime}}-n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

Lemma 10.4
.

Suppose
1/n\ll\beta\ll\varepsilon\ll\hat{\delta},1/k
. Let
\ell\in[k-1]_{0}
. Let
G
be an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph. Fix
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G)
. Let
z
be the number of ordered
\ell
-paths in
G
that start at
\vec{e}
and have length at least
\frac{n-n^{1-\beta}}{k-\ell}
. Then
\log z\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}\overline{h}(G)-n\log e-\varepsilon n.

Proof.

Let
L\coloneqq\frac{n-n^{1-\beta}}{k-\ell}
. Choose
\varepsilon
and
b\geq 4
such that Lemma
3.5
holds for the parameters
\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}
) and
k
. Choose
\alpha\coloneqq 3\beta
so that Lemma
10.2
and Lemma
10.3
hold for the parameters
\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}
),
k,\varepsilon
and
2b
(instead of
b
). By Lemma
3.5
,
G
admits a
b
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}
with
h(\mathbf{x})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. We claim that
\delta_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
and
b_{i}\leq 2b
in the following procedure. Let
G_{0}\coloneqq G
,
n_{0}\coloneqq n
,
\delta_{0}\coloneqq\delta
,
b_{0}\coloneqq b

\mathbf{x}_{0}\coloneqq\mathbf{x}
and
\vec{e}_{0}\coloneqq\vec{e}
. We construct an ordered
\ell
-path in
G
as follows.

At each step
i\geq 0
, let
\mathcal{Z}_{i}
be the set of all
(\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G_{i}],\alpha)
-well-behaved realizations of the random walk
Z=(\vec{Z}_{0},...,\vec{Z}_{m_{i}})
on
\mathbb{S}_{\ell}[G_{i}]
induced by
\mathbf{x}_{i}
, where
\vec{Z}_{0}=\vec{e}_{i}
and
m_{i}\coloneqq\lfloor n_{i}^{1/3}\rfloor
. Fix some walk
Z_{i}\in\mathcal{Z}_{i}
, and let
V_{i}
be the set of the first
m_{i}^{\prime}\coloneqq m_{i}(k-\ell)
vertices in
Z_{i}
. Let
P_{i}
be the ordered
\ell
-path in
G_{i}
consisting of the first
m_{i}^{\prime}+\ell
vertices in
Z_{i}
, then
P_{i}
is a
(G_{i},\alpha)
-well-behaved
\ell
-path. Let
\vec{e}_{i+1}
be the end of
Z_{i}
. Define

\displaystyle G_{i+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq G_{i}-V_{i},

\displaystyle n_{i+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq n_{i}-m_{i}^{\prime},

\displaystyle\delta_{i+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\delta_{i}-2n_{i}^{-\frac{2}{3}-\alpha},

\displaystyle b_{i+1}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\left(1+n_{i}^{-\frac{2}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\right)b_{i},\text{ and}

\displaystyle z_{i}

\displaystyle\coloneqq\min|\mathcal{Z}_{i}|,

where the minimum is taken over all possible ordered
\ell
-paths
P_{0}\cup...\cup P_{i-1}
and starting points
\vec{e}_{i}
. By Lemma
10.3
,
G_{i+1}
is an
(n_{i+1},k,\delta_{i+1})
-graph, and there exists a
b_{i+1}
-normal perfect fractional matching
\mathbf{x}_{i+1}
of
G_{i+1}
such that
h(\mathbf{x}_{i+1})\geq h(G_{i+1})-\varepsilon n_{i+1}
and

(10.2)

h(\mathbf{x}_{i+1})\geq\frac{n_{i+1}}{n_{i}}h(\mathbf{x}_{i})-\frac{(k-1)}{k}n_{i+1}\log\frac{n_{i}}{n_{i+1}}-n_{i}^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

We terminate the procedure once
n_{i}\leq n^{1-\beta}.
Let
\kappa
be the step such that
n_{\kappa+1}\leq n^{1-\beta}<n_{\kappa}
. Note that
Q\coloneqq P_{0}\cup...\cup P_{\kappa}
is an ordered
\ell
-path of length at least
L
. This procedure is finite since at every step
i
,
G_{i+1}
is a proper subgraph of
G_{i}
. Moreover, since
m_{0}^{\prime}\geq...\geq m_{\kappa-1}^{\prime}
and
m_{0}^{\prime}+...+m_{\kappa-1}^{\prime}\leq n
, we have

\kappa\leq\frac{n}{m_{\kappa-1}^{\prime}}\leq\frac{n}{(k-\ell)n_{\kappa-1}^{1/3}}<\frac{1}{k-\ell}\cdot\frac{n}{(n^{1-\beta})^{\frac{1}{3}}}\leq\frac{1}{k-\ell}\cdot n^{\frac{2}{3}+\frac{\beta}{3}}.

By similar arguments as (
6.3
) and (
6.4
), for all
i\in[\kappa]_{0}
, we have
\delta_{i}\geq\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}>\frac{1}{2}
and
b_{i}\leq 2b
. Thus,
G_{i}
is an
(n_{i},k,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2})
-graph and
\mathbf{x}_{i}
is a
2b
-normal perfect fractional matching of
G_{i}
. Again by induction on
i\in[\kappa]_{0}
, we obtain the same inequality as (
6.5
), which is

h(G_{i})\geq h(\mathbf{x}_{i})\geq\frac{n_{i}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{k-1}{k}n_{i}\log\frac{n}{n_{i}}-in^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}.

By Corollary
10.2
, we have

\displaystyle\log z_{i}

\displaystyle\geq k\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}h(G_{i})-m_{i}\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}\frac{(n_{i}-k+1)!}{(n_{i}-\ell)!n}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}

\displaystyle\geq k\frac{m_{i}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-(k-1)m_{i}\log\frac{n}{n_{i}}-ik\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-m_{i}\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}\frac{(n_{i}-k+1)!}{(n_{i}-\ell)!n_{i}}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}

\displaystyle\geq k\frac{m_{i}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-m_{i}\log{n_{i}\choose k-1}\frac{(n_{i}-k+1)!}{(n_{i}-\ell)!}\left(\frac{n}{n_{i}}\right)^{\ell}-(k-\ell-1)m_{i}\log n+(k-\ell)m_{i}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle\quad-ik\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}.

We consider the following sums over
i\in[\kappa]_{0}
. By construction,
\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}m_{i}^{\prime}=n-n_{\kappa+1}\geq n-n^{1-\beta}
. Similar to (
6.6
) and (
6.7
), we have

\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}m_{i}^{\prime}\log n_{i}\geq n\log n-n\log e-\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n\quad\text{and}\quad\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}i\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}\leq\frac{\varepsilon}{2}n.

Note that for
i\in[\kappa]
, we have

\displaystyle{n_{i}\choose k-1}\frac{(n_{i}-k+1)!}{(n_{i}-\ell)!}\left(\frac{n}{n_{i}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle={n\choose k-1}\frac{n_{i}!(n-k+1)!}{n!(n_{i}-k+1)!}\cdot\frac{(n_{i}-k+1)!}{(n_{i}-\ell)!}\left(\frac{n}{n_{i}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle={n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!}\cdot\frac{n_{i}!(n-\ell)!}{n!(n_{i}-\ell)!}\left(\frac{n}{n_{i}}\right)^{\ell}

\displaystyle\leq{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!}.

Recall that
m_{i}^{\prime}=m_{i}(k-\ell)
for
i\in[\kappa]_{0}
. Let
z
denote the number of ordered
\ell
-paths that start at
\vec{e}
and have length at least
L
. Note that every
\ell
-path
Q
constructed above is such a path. Thus, we have

\displaystyle\log z

\displaystyle\geq\log\prod_{i=0}^{\kappa}z_{i}=\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}\log z_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\sum_{i=0}^{\kappa}\frac{k}{k-\ell}\frac{m_{i}^{\prime}}{n}h(\mathbf{x})-\frac{m_{i}^{\prime}}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!}-\frac{k-\ell-1}{k-\ell}m_{i}^{\prime}\log n+m_{i}^{\prime}\log n_{i}

\displaystyle\quad-ik\frac{m_{i}}{n_{i}}n^{\frac{1}{3}-\frac{\alpha}{2}}-3k\varepsilon m_{i}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}h(G)-\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!}-\frac{k-\ell-1}{k-\ell}n\log n+n\log\frac{n}{e}-2\varepsilon n-3k\varepsilon n

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}\overline{h}(G)-n\log e-5k\varepsilon n.

This completes the proof.
∎

10.3.
Absorbing long paths

Given an
(n,k,\delta)
-graph
G
, we again use Lemma
6.5
to pick a vertex subset
U\subseteq V(G)
that contains sufficiently many neighbours of every
(k-1)
-subset of
V(G)
. Applying Lemma
10.4
, we find many long ordered
\ell
-paths in
G-U
and use Lemma
6.6
to complete each of these paths into an ordered Hamilton
\ell
-cycle of
G
. We are now able to prove our main theorem.

Proof of Theorem
1.8
.

Choose
\beta
and
\varepsilon
such that Lemma
6.4
holds for the parameters
\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}
(instead of
\hat{\delta}
) and
k
. Let
U\subseteq V(G)
be a set of size
n^{\alpha}
guaranteed by Lemma
6.5
with
\alpha\coloneqq 1-\beta/2
. Let
G^{\prime}\coloneqq G-U
and
n^{\prime}\coloneqq n-n^{\alpha}
. Then
G^{\prime}
is an
(n^{\prime},k,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2})
-graph since

\delta_{k-1}(G^{\prime})\geq\delta_{k-1}(G)-|U|\geq\delta n-n^{\alpha}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}\right)n^{\prime}.

By Lemma
4.2
, we have
h(G^{\prime})\geq h(G)-\varepsilon n
. Fix
\vec{e}\in\vec{E}(G^{\prime})
. Let
Q
be an ordered
\ell
-path in
G^{\prime}
of length at least
\frac{n^{\prime}-(n^{\prime})^{1-\beta}}{k-\ell}
from
\vec{e}
to
\vec{f}
for some
\vec{f}\in\vec{E}(G^{\prime})
. Let
\vec{S}\coloneqq\vec{f}^{(k-1)+}
,
\vec{T}\coloneqq\vec{e}^{(k-1)-}
,
G^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq G[V(G^{\prime}-Q)\cup U\cup S\cup T]
, and
n^{\prime\prime}\coloneqq|V(G^{\prime\prime})|\leq(n^{\prime})^{1-\beta}+n^{\alpha}+2k\leq n^{\alpha}+n^{1-\beta}
. Note that
G^{\prime\prime}
is an
(n^{\prime\prime},k,\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2})
-graph since for all
S\in\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(G^{\prime\prime})\subseteq\mathcal{S}_{k-1}(G)
, we have

|N_{G^{\prime\prime}}(S)|\geq|N_{G}(S)\cap U|\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{3\hat{\delta}}{4}\right)n^{\alpha}\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}\right)(n^{\alpha}+n^{1-\beta})\geq\left(\frac{1}{2}+\frac{\hat{\delta}}{2}\right)n^{\prime\prime}.

By Lemma
6.6
, there is a tight Hamilton path
P
from
\vec{S}
to
\vec{T}
in
G^{\prime\prime}
. Observe that
P
contains an ordered Hamilton
\ell
-path from
\vec{f}^{(\ell)+}
to
\vec{e}^{(\ell)-}
. Then
P+Q
is an ordered Hamilton
\ell
-cycle of
G
. Since
Q
was arbitrary, by Lemma
6.4
, we have

\displaystyle\log\Psi_{\ell}^{\prime}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}h(G^{\prime})-\frac{n^{\prime}}{k-\ell}\log{n^{\prime}\choose k-1}\frac{(n^{\prime}-k+1)!}{(n^{\prime}-\ell)!n^{\prime}}-n^{\prime}\log e-\varepsilon n^{\prime}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}(h(G)-\varepsilon n)-\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}-n\log e-2\varepsilon n

\displaystyle\geq\frac{k}{k-\ell}h(G)-\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}-n\log e-3k\varepsilon n.

Recall that
\Psi_{\ell}^{\prime}(G)=C_{n,k,\ell}\Psi_{\ell}(G)
. This completes the proof.
∎

When
\ell=0
, we have
\log\Phi(G)\geq h(G)-\frac{k-1}{k}n\log e-o(n)
, which is the lower bound in Theorem
1.4
. Theorem
1.5
also follows as a corollary.

Proof of Theorem
1.5
.

Using the fact that
-\log(1-x)=\log(1+\frac{x}{1-x})\leq\frac{2x}{1-x}\leq 3x
for
0<x\leq 1/3
, we first calculate

\frac{1}{k-\ell}\log\frac{n(n-\ell)!}{(n-k+1)!}\geq\frac{1}{k-\ell}\log(n-k+2)^{k-\ell}\geq\log n\left(1-\frac{k-2}{n}\right)\geq\log n-\frac{3(k-2)}{n}.

We use Theorem
1.8
, together with the lower bound for
h(G)
given by Theorem
3.2
to obtain

\displaystyle\log\Psi_{\ell}^{\prime}(G)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}+\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log\delta-\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log{n\choose k-1}\frac{(n-k+1)!}{(n-\ell)!n}-n\log e-o(n)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log\delta+\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log\frac{(n-\ell)!n}{(n-k+1)!}-n\log e-o(n)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log\delta+n\log n-3(k-2)-n\log e-o(n)

\displaystyle\geq\frac{n}{k-\ell}\log\delta+\log(n!)-o(n).

Therefore, we conclude
\Psi_{\ell}(G)\geq C_{n,k,\ell}^{-1}n!(\delta-o(1))^{\frac{n}{k-\ell}}=\Psi_{\ell}(K_{n}^{(k)})(\delta-o(1))^{\frac{n}{k-\ell}}
.
∎
