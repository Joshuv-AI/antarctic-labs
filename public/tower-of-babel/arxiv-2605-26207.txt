Conserving relaxation-time approximation for electron-electron collisions
Sayak Bhattacharjee, Tatia Kiliptari, Dmitrii L. Maslov

Abstract
We develop a conserving relaxation-time approximation (cRTA) based on an explicit energy-resolved projection onto the full space of collision invariants. Our cRTA retains the energy dependence of the nonequilibrium quasiparticle distribution, allowing one to describe transport quantities sensitive to states near, but not exactly on, the Fermi surface (FS). We apply the method to several charge-transport problems in both Galilean-invariant and non-Galilean-invariant Fermi liquids. In particular, the cRTA reproduces the low- and high-temperature limits of the dc conductivity of a non-Galilean-invariant Fermi liquid with disorder, the hydrodynamic and collisionless limits of the finite-wavevector longitudinal conductivity of a clean Galilean-invariant Fermi liquid, and the asymptotic scaling forms of the optical conductivity of a clean non-Galilean-invariant Fermi liquid beyond the semiclassical limit. For several observables, the agreement with exact solutions is quantitative at the percent level. These results demonstrate that the cRTA provides a simple and accurate framework for describing transport beyond the FS projection.



Introduction.

The kinetic equation (KE) is a versatile tool for describing transport phenomena in both classical and quantum systems. However, its exact solutions are rare, even in linear response. The main difficulty arises from the non-locality of the collision integral in momentum space. Consequently, the KE is an integro-differential one. Accordingly, a number of approximate methods for solving the KE were developed in the past.
A special category of these methods are relaxation-time approximations (RTAs), which replace the non-local collision integral for binary collisions by a local one. The most common RTA with the collision integral

I_{\rm RTA}=-(f_{\mathbf{k}}-n_{\mathbf{k}})/\tau

5
, where
n_{\mathbf{k}}
is the equilibrium distribution, and
\tau
is the phenomenological relaxation time,
does not enforce conservation laws. Conserving RTAs date back to the seminal paper by Bhatnagar, Gross, and Krook on mono-atomic classical gases
7
. In condensed matter physics, partially and fully conserving RTAs for binary collision integrals were developed in Refs.
9
;
23
;
28
;
12
;
13
;
11
;
10
;
6
.

Earlier conserving RTAs were formulated in terms of local thermodynamic variables and applied mainly to density response and hydrodynamics of electron gases with a parabolic dispersion
6
. In this Letter, we formulate the conserving approximation as an explicit projection onto the hydrodynamic manifold, yielding a systematic scheme for describing transport in generic non-Galilean-invariant
Fermi liquids. Our cRTA also differs from the previous methods in that it does not rely on a Fermi-surface projection but operates with an energy-resolved projection onto the full space of collision invariants. This makes it applicable to transport quantities sensitive to the energy dependence of the nonequilibrium distribution near the Fermi surface (FS), e.g., the thermal conductivity of a generic Fermi liquid (FL) and the charge conductivity of non-Galilean–invariant FLs. We show that our cRTA reproduces several exact and asymptotically exact results.

General construction.

We consider a single-band system of electrons with arbitrary dispersion
\varepsilon=\varepsilon(\mathbf{k})
, measured from the Fermi energy. The interaction between electrons is parameterized by a phenomenological scattering time
\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}(\mathbf{k},T)
which, in general, depends on the momentum
\mathbf{k}
and temperature
T
. Beyond the semiclassical limit, i.e., for arbitrary
\Omega/T
, where
\Omega
is the frequency of the applied electric field, one also needs to include the dependence of
\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}
on
\Omega
.

Electron-electron collisions conserve particle number, momentum (in the absence of umklapp scattering), and energy, such that a vector in the
d+2
“hydrodynamic” space,

\displaystyle\vec{\psi}_{\mathbf{k}}=\left(1,\varepsilon,\mathbf{k}\right),

(1)

is conserved. Here and thereafter,
\vec{a}
denotes a vector in this
d+2
space, whereas
\mathbf{a}
denotes a vector in real space. Our goal is to construct an algebraic form of the collision integral which satisfies all the conservation laws. A deviation from equilibrium is parameterized as

\displaystyle\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f_{\mathbf{k}}-n_{\mathbf{k}}=(-n_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\chi_{\mathbf{k}},

(2)

where
n^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}}\equiv\partial_{\varepsilon}n(\varepsilon)
. Introduce another deviation projected onto the hydrodynamic space:

\displaystyle\delta f^{\mathrm{h}}_{\mathbf{k}}=(-n_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\chi^{\mathrm{h}}_{\mathbf{k}},\quad\chi^{\mathrm{h}}_{\mathbf{k}}=\vec{c}\cdot\vec{\psi}_{\mathbf{k}},

(3)

where
\vec{c}
is a
\mathbf{k}
-independent
d+2
vector.

Consider the collision integral:

\displaystyle I_{\mathrm{ee}}[f_{\mathbf{k}}]=-\frac{\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}-\delta f^{\mathrm{h}}_{\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}=-(-n_{\mathbf{k}}^{\prime})\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{k}}-\chi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{h}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}.

(4)

If the vector
\vec{c}
in Eq. (
3
) is chosen as

\displaystyle\vec{c}=\hat{M}^{-1}\cdot\left\langle\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\vec{\psi}_{\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle,

(5)

with

\displaystyle\langle X_{\mathbf{k}}\rangle\equiv\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}(-n^{\prime}_{\mathbf{k}})X_{\mathbf{k}}

(6)

and

\displaystyle M_{\alpha\beta}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle M_{\beta\alpha}=\left\langle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\psi_{\beta\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle,

(7)

then
I_{\mathrm{ee}}[f_{\mathbf{k}}]
in Eq. (
4
) satisfies all the necessary conservation laws, i.e.,

\displaystyle\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\,\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\,I_{\mathrm{ee}}[f_{\mathbf{k}}]=0,\quad\alpha=1\dots d+2.

(8)

The above statement is proven
as follows. Since
\int\frac{d^{d}k}{(2\pi)^{d}}\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}I_{\mathrm{ee}}[f_{\mathbf{k}}]=\left\langle\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}(\chi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{h}}-\chi_{\mathbf{k}})/\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}\right\rangle
, Eq. (
8
) is equivalent to

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\,\chi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{h}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\,\chi_{\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle.

(9)

Substituting Eqs. (
5
) and (
7
) into Eq. (
9
) yields

\displaystyle\left\langle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}\,\chi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\mathrm{h}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle=\left\langle\frac{\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}\psi_{\beta\mathbf{k}}\right\rangle=\sum_{\beta}c_{\beta}M_{\alpha\beta}=

(10)

\displaystyle\sum_{\beta,\gamma}(M^{-1})_{\beta\gamma}\left\langle\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\psi_{\gamma\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle M_{\alpha\beta}=\left\langle\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{k}}\psi_{\alpha\mathbf{k}}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}}\right\rangle,

as required. In what follows, we demonstrate that this method reproduces some known results.

Optical conductivity of a clean Fermi liquid beyond the semiclassical limit.

Both previous examples apply in
the semiclassical limit:
\Omega\ll T
. To extend our analysis for an arbitrary ratio
\Omega/T
, the KE needs to be modified in two ways. First, the semiclassical driving term in Eq. (
12
) is replaced by
e(\bm{v}\cdot\mathbf{E})\Delta(\Omega,\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})

\displaystyle\Delta(\Omega,\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})=\frac{1}{\Omega}\left[n\left(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\frac{\Omega}{2}\right)-n\left(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\frac{\Omega}{2}\right)\right].

(30)

This result is obtained
by Wigner-transforming the left-hand side of the Dyson equation for the lesser non-equilibrium Green’s function
4
,

\Big[i(\partial_{t_{1}}+\partial_{t_{2}})-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+e\mathbf{A}(t_{1})}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+e\mathbf{A}(t_{2})}\Big]G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(t_{1},t_{2}).

Accordingly, we re-define
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}
as
\delta f_{\mathbf{k}}=\Delta(\Omega,\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})\chi_{\mathbf{k}}
and the average in Eq. (
6

\langle X\rangle_{\Omega}\equiv\int d^{d}k\Delta(\Omega,\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})X/(2\pi)^{d}.

The second modification accounts for the effect of the electric field on the Keldysh collision integral,
I_{\rm k}
, which contains Moyal products of the self-energies
\Sigma^{>,<}_{\mathbf{k}}
and Green’s functions
G^{>,<}_{\mathbf{k}}

X_{0}
be the equilibrium part of

\delta X
its deviation, and

\displaystyle\hat{S}_{\omega,\Omega}\left[X\right]\equiv\frac{1}{2}\left[X(\omega+\Omega/2)+X(\omega-\Omega/2)\right].

(31)

Evaluating the Moyal products for a harmonic perturbation, we obtain

\displaystyle I_{\rm K}=-\int\frac{d\omega}{2\pi}

\displaystyle\{\delta\Sigma^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{S}_{\omega,\Omega}\left[G^{>}_{0\mathbf{k}}\right]+\delta G^{>}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{S}_{\omega,\Omega}\left[\Sigma^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}\right]-

(32)

\displaystyle\delta\Sigma^{>}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{S}_{\omega,\Omega}\left[G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}\right]-\delta G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}\hat{S}_{\omega,\Omega}\left[\Sigma^{>}_{0\mathbf{k}}\right]\}.

Within the cRTA, the frequency shift by
\Omega/2
can be modeled by replacing

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}^{\Omega}}=\frac{1}{2}\left[\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}+\Omega/2,T)}+\frac{1}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}-\Omega/2,T)}\right].

With these modifications, the cRTA kinetic equation becomes

\displaystyle-i\Omega\chi_{\mathbf{k}}+e(\mathbf{E}\cdot\bm{v})=-\frac{\chi_{\mathbf{k}}-\chi_{\mathbf{k}}^{\rm h}}{\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}^{\Omega}},

\left\langle\dots\right\rangle\to\left\langle\dots\right\rangle_{\Omega}

\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}\rightarrow\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}^{\Omega}

{\mathbf{c}}=0

\displaystyle\sigma_{ij}=2e^{2}\left\langle\frac{v_{i}v_{j}}{1/\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}^{\Omega}-i\Omega}\right\rangle_{\Omega}.

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ee}}=\frac{e^{2}a_{0}}{180\pi}\frac{(\Omega^{2}+4\pi^{2}T^{2})(\Omega^{2}+6\pi^{2}T^{2})}{\Omega^{2}v_{\mathrm{F}}\varepsilon_{\rm F}}.

(38)

This equation reproduces the general features of the result of a microscopic calculation in Ref.
26
, up to the numerical coefficient in the second bracket: the exact coefficient is
8\pi^{2}/3
rather than
6\pi^{2}
. The difference comes about because, in a microscopic calculation, an
\mathcal{O}(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}^{2})
term in the expansion near
\varepsilon_{\rm F}
also acquires a shift by
\pm\Omega/2
, whereas the cRTA misses this effect. At the same time, the first bracket in Eq. (
38
) ensures that the first bosonic Matsubara frequency rule is satisfied, i.e., that
\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ee}}|_{\Omega=\pm 2\pi iT}=0

22
.

In the hydrodynamic limit,
0<\Omega\ll 1/\tau_{\mathrm{ee}}(\varepsilon=0,T)
,
the conductivity saturates at the
T
-independent value

\displaystyle\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\mathrm{ee}}=\frac{1}{6\pi^{2}a_{0}}\left(1-\frac{\pi^{2}}{12}\right)e^{2}k_{\rm F}+\mathcal{O}(T^{2}).

(39)

With
a_{0}=(\pi^{3}/56)(1-\pi^{2}/12)\kappa/k_{F}
, this coincides with the exact solution of Ref.
17
, extended to the 3D case.

In conclusion, we presented a simple but versatile conserving relaxation-time approximation (cRTA), which accounts for all required conservation laws and is applicable to situations where FS projection is insufficient. We anticipate that this version of the cRTA can be applied to several other situations, including non-linear response

Pf=\langle f\rangle_{\rm FS}+d\langle\mu f\rangle_{\rm FS}\,\mu,

because

\langle 1\rangle_{\rm FS}=1,\qquad\langle\mu\rangle_{\rm FS}=0,\qquad\langle\mu^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}=\frac{1}{d}.

Hence

\langle(f-Pf)^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}=\langle f^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}-\langle f\rangle_{\rm FS}^{2}-d\langle\mu f\rangle_{\rm FS}^{2}.

and therefore

{\cal J}_{2}^{\prime}(z)+{\cal J}_{1}^{2}(z)+d{\cal J}_{2}^{2}(z)=-\langle(f-Pf)^{2}\rangle_{\rm FS}\leq 0,

so that
\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\rm L}(q,\Omega)\geq 0
.

Finally, we consider the viscoelastic limit of the collisionless regime
10
,
when
\gamma\ll v_{\mathrm{F}}q\ll\Omega
and thus
z\gg 1
.
The required large-
z
expansions are

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{1}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{dz^{2}}+\frac{3}{d(d+2)z^{4}}+O(z^{-6}),

(S58)

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}(z)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{dz}+\frac{3}{d(d+2)z^{3}}+O(z^{-5}),

(S59)

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}^{\prime}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{dz^{2}}-\frac{9}{d(d+2)z^{4}}+O(z^{-6}).

(S60)

Therefore

\displaystyle{\cal J}_{2}^{\prime}(z)+{\cal J}_{1}^{2}(z)+d{\cal J}_{2}^{2}(z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{2(d-1)}{d^{2}(d+2)}\frac{1}{z^{4}}+O(z^{-5}).

(S61)

Substituting these expansions into Eq. (
S55
)
gives

\mathrm{Re}\,\sigma_{\rm L}(q,\Omega)=\frac{ne^{2}}{m}\frac{2(d-1)}{d(d+2)}\frac{v_{\mathrm{F}}^{2}q^{2}\gamma}{\Omega^{4}},\qquad\gamma\ll v_{\mathrm{F}}q\ll\Omega,

(S62)

which is Eq. (
29
) of the MT.

S4
kinetic equation beyond the semiclassical limit

S4.1
Electric-field driving

To derive the electric-field driving term beyond the semiclassical limit, i.e., for arbitrary
\Omega/T
ratio, we temporarily omit the collision integral.
In the presence of a time-dependent and uniform electric field, the left-hand side of the Dyson equation for the lesser non-equilibrium Green’s function is

\hat{L}G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(t_{1},t_{2})\equiv\Big[i(\partial_{t_{1}}+\partial_{t_{2}})-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+e\mathbf{A}(t_{1})}+\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+e\mathbf{A}(t_{2})}\Big]G^{<}(t_{1},t_{2},\mathbf{k}).

(S63)

To linear order in the field

\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}+e\mathbf{A}(t_{1,2})}-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}=\,e\,\bm{v}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{A}(t_{1,2})=\frac{\,e\,\bm{v}_{\mathbf{k}}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{E}}{i\Omega}e^{-i\Omega t_{1,2}}.

(S64)

To the same order,
G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(t_{1},t_{2})
multiplying
\mathbf{E}
can be replaced by the equilibrium function,
G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}(t_{1}-t_{2})
, which depends only on the time difference
t_{1}-t_{2}
.

A Wigner transform of a function
X(t_{1},t_{2})
is defined as

\displaystyle X(\omega,t)\equiv\int d\tau e^{i\omega\tau}X(t+\tau/2,t-\tau/2),

(S65)

where
t=(t_{1}+t_{2})/2
and
\tau=t_{1}-t_{2}
.
Wigner transforming Eq. (
S63
) with the help of

\displaystyle\int d\tau e^{i\omega\tau}\left[e^{-i\Omega(t+\tau/2)}-e^{-i\Omega(t-\tau/2)}\right]\int\frac{d\omega^{\prime}}{2\pi}G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}(\omega^{\prime})e^{-i\omega^{\prime}\tau}=e^{-i\Omega t}\left[G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}(\omega-\Omega/2)-G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}(\omega+\Omega/2)\right],

(S66)

we obtain

\displaystyle\hat{L}G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega,t)=i\partial_{t}G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega,t)-\frac{\,e\,\bm{v}\!\cdot\!\mathbf{E}\,e^{-\Omega t}}{i\Omega}\left[G^{<}_{0}(\omega-\Omega/2,\mathbf{k})-G^{<}_{0}(\omega+\Omega/2,\mathbf{k})\right].

(S67)

In the quasiparticle approximation, the distribution function is defined as
21
;
25

\displaystyle G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}(\omega,t)=2\pi i\delta(\omega-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})f_{\mathbf{k}}(t),\;G^{<}_{0\mathbf{k}}(\omega,t)=2\pi i\delta(\omega-\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}})n(\varepsilon_{\mathbf{k}}).

(S68)

Substituting these definitions into Eq. (
S67
) and integrating over
\omega
, we obtain the driving term in Eq. (
30
) of the MT.

S4.2
Collision integral

The right-hand side of the Dyson equation for
G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}
reads

\displaystyle\tilde{I}(\mathbf{k},\omega,t)=\frac{1}{2}\left[\Sigma^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}\star G^{>}_{\mathbf{k}}-\Sigma^{>}_{\mathbf{k}}\star G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}+G^{>}\star\Sigma^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}-G^{<}_{\mathbf{k}}\star\Sigma^{>}_{\mathbf{k}}\right](\omega,t),

(S69)

where a Moyal product is defined as

\displaystyle C(\omega,t)=(A\star B)(\omega,t)=\int d\tau e^{i\omega(t_{1}-t_{2})}\int dt_{3}A(t_{1},t_{3})B(t_{3},t_{2})

(S70)

with
t=(t_{1}+t_{2})/2
and
\tau=t_{1}-t_{2}
. Shifting the integration variable as
t_{3}=t+s
,

\displaystyle C(\omega,t)=\int d\tau e^{i\omega\tau}\int dsA(t+\tau/2,t+s)B(t+s,t-\tau/2).

(S71)

In A, the “center time” and “difference time” are
(1/2)(t+\tau/2+t+s)=t+\tau/4+s/2
and
\tau/2-s
, respectively. In
B
, the center time and difference time are
t-\tau/4+s/2
and
\tau/2+s
, respectively. Expressing
A
and
B

via their Wigner transforms, we find

\displaystyle C(\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int d\tau\int ds\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi}e^{i\left[\omega-(1/2))(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})\right]\tau}e^{i(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2})s}A(\omega_{1},t+\tau/4+s/2)B(\omega_{2},t+s/2-\tau/4).

(S72)

Suppose that the dependence on the center-time is harmonic:
A(\omega,t)=e^{-i\Omega_{A}t}A_{\Omega_{A}}(\omega)
and
B(\omega,t)=e^{-i\Omega_{B}t}B_{\Omega_{B}}(\omega)
with
\Omega_{A},\Omega_{B}=0,\Omega
. Substituting these forms into (
S72
), we obtain

\displaystyle C(t,\omega)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(\Omega_{A}+\Omega_{B})t}\int d\tau e^{i\omega\tau}\int ds\int\frac{d\omega_{1}}{2\pi}\frac{d\omega_{2}}{2\pi}e^{i\left[\omega-(1/2))(\omega_{1}+\omega_{2})-\frac{\Omega_{A}-\Omega_{B}}{4}\right]\tau}e^{i\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}-\frac{\Omega_{A}+\Omega_{B}}{2}\right)s}A_{\Omega_{A}}(\omega_{1})B_{\Omega_{B}}(\omega_{2})

(S73)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(\Omega_{A}+\Omega_{B})t}\int d\omega_{1}\int d\omega_{2}\delta\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}-\frac{\Omega_{A}-\Omega_{B}}{4}\right)\delta\left(\omega_{1}-\omega_{2}-\frac{\Omega_{A}+\Omega_{B}}{2}\right)A_{\Omega_{A}}(\omega_{1})B_{\Omega_{B}}(\omega_{2})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle e^{-i(\Omega_{A}+\Omega_{B})t}A_{\Omega_{A}}(\omega+\Omega_{B}/2)B_{\Omega_{B}}(\omega-\Omega_{A}/2)

In what follows, we will be interested in linear response, when
A=\mathcal{A}_{0}+e^{-i\Omega t}\delta A
and
B=B_{0}+e^{-i\Omega t}\delta B
with time-independent
A_{0}
and
B_{0}
. To linear order in
\delta A
and
\delta B
,

\displaystyle C(\omega,t)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\left(\mathcal{A}_{0}+\delta Ae^{-i\Omega t}\right)\star\left(B_{0}+\delta Be^{-i\Omega t}\right)

(S74)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}B_{0}+\left(\delta Ae^{-i\Omega t}\right)\star B_{0}+\mathcal{A}_{0}\star\left(\delta Be^{-i\Omega t}\right)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\mathcal{A}_{0}B_{0}+\left[\delta A(\omega)B_{0}(\omega-\Omega/2)+\mathcal{A}_{0}(\omega+\Omega/2)\delta B(\omega)\right]e^{-i\Omega t}.

Linearizing the Moyal products of
\Sigma^{<,>}_{\mathbf{k}}
and
G^{<,>}_{\mathbf{k}}
in Eq. (
S69
) around their corresponding equilibrium values, applying Eq. (
S74
) to each term, and integrating over
\omega
, we obtain the collision integral (
32
) of the MT.
