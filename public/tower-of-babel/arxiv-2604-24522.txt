NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} topological structures
Pablo Andújar Guerrero

Abstract
A subset of a topological space is constructible if it is a finite Boolean combination of closed sets. We prove that every NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} expansion of ( ℝ , < , + ) (\mathbb{R},<,+) by constructible sets defines only constructible sets, and that definable functions are generically piecewise continuous. The result also holds for all NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} expansions of ( ℚ p , + , ⋅ ) (\mathbb{Q}_{p},+,\cdot) , and all NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} definably complete expansions of ordered groups. In the latter case, the structure is generically locally o-minimal, has definable choice, and carries a well-behaved notion of naive topological dimension. For NIP \mathrm{NIP} uniform topological structures, constructibility of definable sets is preserved in the Shelah expansion. We classify strong expansions of ( ℝ , < , + ) (\mathbb{R},<,+) by constructible sets, and obtain results on NTP 2 \mathrm{NTP}_{2} d-minimal structures.

This paper advances two closely related programs at the interface of topology and model theory. On one hand, it forwards the exploration of first-order structures over the real ordered group

The main axiom of neostability that we will work with is the tree property of the second kind (
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
), a setting generalizing both simple and
\mathrm{NIP}
theories. A subset of a topological space is constructible if it is a finite Boolean combination of closed sets. Our first main result, Theorem

[
37
]

iff it defines
\mathbb{Z}

We call a structure

a uniform (topological) structure if

Let

definably

-compact uniform structure. Then the open core

Any

We prove Theorem

Preliminaries

2.1
Model-theoretic conventions and terminology

Throughout we denote (first-order) structures by calligraphic letters
\mathcal{M}
,
\mathcal{R}
,
\ldots
, and their respective universes by uppercase roman letters
M
,
R
,
\ldots
. We denote tuples of variables or parameters by
x,y,z,a,b,c,\ldots
, and non-negative integers by
n,m,i,k,\ldots
. Given a structure
\mathcal{M}
, “definable” means “definable in
\mathcal{M}
possibly with parameters” unless otherwise specified. A type-definable set is an intersection of definable sets.

Given a (partitioned) formula
\varphi(x,y)
in the language of
\mathcal{M}
, and parameters
b\in M^{|y|}
, we let
\varphi(M,b)=\{a\in M^{|x|}:\mathcal{M}\models\varphi(a,b)\}
. A family
\{X_{b}:b\in B\}
of subsets of
M^{n}
is definable if
B\subseteq M^{m}
is a definable set and there exists a formula
\varphi(x,y)
, with
|x|=n
and
|y|=m
, such that
X_{b}=\varphi(M,b)
for every
b\in B
.
A collection of formulas is
k
-inconsistent if every sub-collection of size
k
is inconsistent. We adopt the standard convention of identifying formulas with the sets that they define in a given background structure and vice versa.

2.2
Neostability

We present the main notions from neostability that we will use. For other relevant definitions such as
\mathrm{NIP}
, strong dependence, and dp-rank, we direct the reader to
[
35
]
.

A structure
\mathcal{M}
has the
tree property of the second kind
, denoted
\mathrm{TP}_{2}
, if there exists a formula
\varphi(x,y)
, some
k
and, for every
n
and
m
, a collection of parameters
\{b_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq n,\,1\leq j\leq m\}
in
M^{|y|}
, such that

1.

\{\varphi(x,b_{i,j}):j\leq m\}
is
k
-inconsistent for every
i\leq n
,

2.

\{\varphi(x,b_{i,\eta(i)}):i\leq n\}
is consistent for every function
\eta:\{1,\ldots,n\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

Equivalently, in the above definition one may always assume that
k=2
.
A structure is
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
if it does not have
\mathrm{TP}_{2}
. A theory
T
is
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
if any (every) model is
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
.

For any cardinal
\kappa
, an
inp-pattern of depth
\kappa
in
\mathcal{M}
is a family of pairs
(\varphi_{\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha}),k_{\alpha})
,
for
\alpha<\kappa
, where
\varphi_{\alpha}(x,y_{\alpha})
is a formula and
k_{\alpha}
a positive integer satisfying that, for every finite subset
\{\alpha_{1},\ldots,\alpha_{n}\}<\kappa
and every
m
, there are parameters
\{b_{i,j}:1\leq i\leq n,\,1\leq j\leq m\}
, with
b_{i,j}\in M^{|y_{\alpha_{i}}|}
, such that

1.

\{\varphi_{\alpha_{i}}(x,b_{i,j}):j\leq m\}
is
k_{\alpha_{i}}
-inconsistent for every
i\leq n
,

2.

\{\varphi_{\alpha_{i}}(x,b_{i,\eta(i)}):i\leq n\}
is consistent for every function
\eta:\{1\ldots,n\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
.

We say that a set
X\subseteq M^{|x|}
admits an inp-pattern of depth
\kappa
if the conditions above are satisfied and, in condition (2), each family
\{\varphi_{\alpha_{i}}(x,b_{i,\eta(i)}):i\leq n\}
is consistent with
X
. The burden of
\mathcal{M}
is the supremum of the depths of inp-patterns admitted by
M
. We say that
\mathcal{M}
is
strong
if every inp-pattern in
\mathcal{M}
has finite depth. A theory
T
is strong if any (every) model is strong. Every strong theory is
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
.

Every strongly dependent theory is strong and
\mathrm{NIP}
, and every
\mathrm{NIP}
theory is
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
.

2.3
Topological conventions and uniform structures

Let

together with a Hausdorff

3
Constructible and
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}

Recall that a subset of a topological space is
F_{\sigma}
if it is a countable union of closed sets. We introduce a definable analogue of this property for definable sets in topological structures.

A family of sets
\mathcal{C}
is
upward
(respectively

)
directed
if, for any
C_{1},C_{2}\in\mathcal{C}
, there exists some
C_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
C_{1}\cup C_{2}\subseteq C_{3}
(respectively
C_{3}\subseteq C_{1}\cap C_{2}
). Equivalently, if, for any finite subfamily
\mathcal{F}\subseteq\mathcal{C}
, there is
C_{3}\in\mathcal{C}
such that
\bigcup\mathcal{F}\subseteq C_{3}
(respectively
C_{3}\subseteq\bigcap\mathcal{F}
).

Definition 3.1
.

Let
X
be a definable set in a topological structure
\mathcal{M}
. We say that
X
is
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
(definably
F_{\sigma}
) if

Similar but more restrictive notions than

are called

in
[

or simply

in
[

]

A subset of a topological space is
constructible
if it is a finite Boolean combination of closed (open) sets. A topological structure
\mathcal{M}
is
constructible
if every definable set in it is constructible. Robinson
[
32
]
and Pillay
[

The following characterization of constructible sets follows from the main result in

Fact 3.2

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let

1.

X

2.

\partial^{(n)}X=\overbrace{\partial\cdots\partial}^{n}X=\emptyset
for some

Fact

Fact 3.3

(
[
8
]
)
.

Let
X
be a definable set in a topological structure. Then

is constructible if and only if it is a finite Boolean combination of closed (open)

Fact
3.3
states that a definable set in a topological structure
\mathcal{M}
is constructible iff it is definable without quantifiers in the open core
\mathcal{M}^{o}

\mathcal{M}^{o}
is constructible iff it has quantifier elimination.

Remark 3.4
.

1.

If a definable set in a topological structure
\mathcal{M}
is
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}

2.

Since fibers of constructible sets (in Hausdorff spaces) are constructible, a topological structure is constructible iff all
0

3.3

B

is locally closed), then the family of sets of the form

Remark 3.6
.

Recall that the complement of an
F_{\sigma}
set is called
G_{\delta}
. Accordingly, we might call the complement of a
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
set a
G_{\delta}^{\text{def}}
set. Since constructible sets are closed under complements, by Lemma

every constructible definable set in a uniform structure is also

, in agreement with the general theory of uniform spaces.

The next two lemmas establish the crucial relationship between non-constructible sets and sets that are dense and codense in some set. We regard this straightforward connection as a central insight of this paper.

Lemma 3.7
.

Let
X
be a subset of a topological space. If there exists another subset
Z\neq\emptyset
such that
X
is dense and codense in
Z
, then
X
is not constructible.

Proof.

Let
\partial_{Z}
refer to the frontier with respect to the subspace topology on
Z
. If
X
is dense and codense in
Z
then, in particular, we have that
\partial_{Z}^{(n)}(X\cap Z)\neq\emptyset
for every
n
. Note that
\partial_{Z}^{(n)}(X\cap Z)\subseteq\partial^{(n)}X
for every
n
. Hence the lemma follows from Fact
3.2
.
∎

We show that, as long as our set
X
is definable in an
\omega_{1}
-saturated topological structure, the converse of Lemma
3.7
also holds.

Lemma 3.8
.

Let
X
be a definable set in an
\omega_{1}
-saturated topological structure. If
X
is not constructible, then
X
is dense and codense in the nonempty type-definable set
\bigcap_{n<\omega}cl(\partial^{(n)}X)
.

Proof.

Observe that the sequence of definable sets
cl(\partial^{(n)}X)
is decreasing. By Fact
3.2
, each set
cl(\partial^{(n)}X)
is nonempty. So
C=\bigcap_{n<\omega}cl(\partial^{(n)}X)
is a nonempty type-definable set. We note that
X
is dense and codense in
C
.

First observe that
X\cap C
and
X\setminus C
are both type-definable sets. In particular we have
X\cap C=\bigcap_{n<\omega}\partial^{(2n)}X
and
C\setminus X=\bigcap_{n<\omega}\partial^{(2n+1)}X
, where
(\partial^{(2n)}X)_{n}
and
(\partial^{(2n+1)}X)_{n}
are both clearly decreasing sequences of nonempty sets. So every
x\in C\setminus X
is in
cl(\partial^{(2n)}X)
for every
n
, and so, by saturation,
x\in cl(X\cap C)
. Similarly, one shows that
X\cap C\subseteq cl(C\setminus X)
.
∎

Joining Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
(and applying Remark
3.4
) we get that a definable set is not constructible iff, after passing to its definition in an
\omega_{1}
-saturated elementary extension, the set is dense and codense in a type-definable set.

A topological structure
\mathcal{M}
is
noiseless
if there does not exist a definable set that is dense and codense in a (definable) open set. Furthermore,
\mathcal{M}
is
strongly noiseless
if there does not exist a definable set that is dense and codense in another definable set. These properties are central to Miller’s program
[
29
]
. Lemmas
3.7
and
3.8
highlight the close connection between them and constructibility. In particular, the following implications are obvious:

\text{Constructible}\Rightarrow\text{Strongly Noiseless}\Rightarrow\text{Noiseless}.

We end the section with a short proposition that might be of independent interest.

Proposition 3.9
.

Let
X
be a nonempty topological space. The constructible dense subsets of

form a filter in the Boolean algebra of constructible sets.

If
X
is a nonempty set definable in a constructible structure, then the definable dense subsets of
X
form a (consistent, partial) type.

Proof.

The second paragraph follows from the first one. Let
Y
and

be two dense subsets of
X
, we show that the intersection

is dense in
X
.

Towards a contradiction, suppose that
Y\cap Z
is not dense, and let
x\in X\setminus cl(Y\cap Z)
. Let
W
be an open neighborhood of

that is disjoint from
Y\cap Z
. Then we have that
Y\cap W
and

are disjoint and dense in

. In particular

and
Y\cap W\subseteq\partial(Z\cap W)
. By Lemma

we get that

and

are both non-constructible, contradicting that
\mathcal{M}
is constructible.
∎

Remark 3.10
.

There is an obvious generalization of the notion of topological or uniform structure
\mathcal{M}
, where a definable basis for a topology or a uniformity is considered, not necessarily on

, but instead on some definable set

4
Strongly hereditarily Baire structures

We now introduce a definable hereditary form of the Baire property and prove versions of Theorem
A
and Theorem
B
where we assume only this property in place of
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
. In Section

, we show that every
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
uniform structure has this property, yielding our main theorems. The reason we isolate this condition is that it is upfront weaker than
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
. Hence, it would be interesting to know which uniform structures satisfy it (see Question

). In Remark

, we isolate a slightly simpler more technical condition that is sufficient to derive our theorems and that will be used in Section

Definition 4.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a topological structure. We say that

\mathcal{N}\equiv\mathcal{M}

has empty interior in

for every
b\in B
, then
\bigcup_{b\in B}C_{b}
is nowhere dense in
Z
.

We leave it to the reader to check that the definition of

is equivalent to asking that, for every set
Z\subseteq N^{m}
and upward directed definable covering
\{C_{b}:b\in B\}
of

by closed sets, there exists

such that
C_{b}
has nonempty interior in
Z
. This definition is strictly stronger than satisfying the Strong Baire Category Theorem proved in
[
10
]
for so-called type A expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. The notion was motivated by the analysis of
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
uniform structures laid out in Section
5
, which resembles previous work of Walsberg
[
37
]
.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
SHB
uniform structure. Then any
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
set is constructible.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
\mathcal{M}
is
\omega_{1}
-saturated.
Suppose that there exists an
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
set
X
that is not constructible. Let
Z=\bigcap_{n<\omega}cl(\partial^{(n)}X)
. By Proposition
3.8
, the set
X
is dense and codense in

. The set

and the family
\{C_{b}:b\in B\}
witnessing that

is
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
witness the failure of
SHB
.
∎

By Lemma
3.5
, the reverse implication to Lemma
4.2
is also true. So, in an

uniform structure, the constructible definable sets coincide with those that are
F_{\sigma}^{\text{def}}
. Furthermore, they also coincide with the class of

.

Recall that the graph of a continuous partial function with closed domain is closed. As a consequence, if one such function is definable in a topological structure, it is also definable in the open core. In
SHB
uniform structures, we may improve this fact as follows. We rely on the proof of the known fact that the set of points of continuity of a function between uniform spaces is

.

Proposition 4.3
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a
SHB
uniform structure, and let

be a definable function, where

is a constructible set. Let
D=\{x\in X:\text{ $f$ is continuous at $x$\lx@text@rbrace}
. Then
D
and the restriction

are constructible (the latter meaning that the graph
\Gamma(f|_{D})
is constructible). In particular,
f|_{D}
is definable in the open core
\mathcal{M}^{o}
.

Proof.

We show that
D
is constructible in the subspace topology in

. Since
X
is constructible (in
M^{n}
), it follows that
D
is also constructible. Furthermore, it is a standard exercise in continuity that

is closed in
D\times M^{m}
. So, if
D
is constructible, we have in particular that
D\times M^{m}
is constructible, and so we conclude that
\Gamma(f|_{D})
is also constructible, completing the proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}_{X}
and
\mathcal{B}_{m}
be definable bases for the uniform structures on

and
M^{m}
respectively. For every

X(U)=\{x\in X:\exists V\in\mathcal{B}_{X},\,(f(y),f(z))\in U\text{ for every }y,z\in V[x]\}
. Clearly every set

is open in

. Since

is downward directed, the definable family

is also downward directed. We sketch the details of the classical argument that
\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{B}_{m}}X(U)=D
. The inclusion
\bigcap_{U\in\mathcal{B}_{m}}X(U)\subseteq D
is obvious. Now let
x\in D
and
U\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
. Let
W\in\mathcal{B}_{m}
be such that
W\circ W\subseteq U
. Applying continuity, let
V\in\mathcal{B}_{X}
be such that

. Then, for any

, we have that
(f(y),f(x))\in W
and

, and so

.

Hence the family

witnesses that

is

in

∎

4.1

\sigma
-compact structures

We now begin exploring our compactness hypothesis. Recall that a topological space is
\sigma
-compact if it has a countable cover by compact subsets. We introduce a definable analogue of this property. Much like with the notion of

Definition 4.4
.

A definable set

We say that

is

if it has a covering given by an upward directed definable family of definably compact subsets. We say that

is definably

-compact if

Our notion of definable compactness has already been studied in various model-theoretic contexts

Theorem 4.8

Let

In

Hence, it suffices to show that the projection of a definable constructible set is constructible. We fix a definable constructible set

Let

of nonempty subsets of

.

Proof.

Let

be a definable basis for the uniformity on

For each

.

Now suppose that

\bigcap_{i=1}^{n-1}(U_{i,\eta(i)}\setminus U_{i,\eta(i)+1})[C_{i}]

, a subset

Remark 5.4

In this section we focus on the subclass of

Fact 6.1

(
[

Let

Recall that Lemma

Lemma 6.2

Let

We now observe that, in constructible uniform structures, the property of being

Let

7
Definably complete structures

In this section we restrict our analysis of uniform structures to the setting of expansions of linearly ordered groups. A linearly ordered structure
(R,<,\ldots)
is
definably complete
if every definable subset of
R
has a supremum in
R\cup\{-\infty,+\infty\}
. This definition clearly generalizes the notion of being an expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<)
. We first show that definable completeness implies definable
\sigma
-compactness, completing the proof of Theorem
A
. We then recast Theorems
A
and
B
, applied to expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
, into a descriptive set theoretic dichotomy (Theorem
7.4
). Finally, we prove some facts about constructible expansions of definably complete groups, including Theorem
D
.

Since notions such as constructibility and local o-minimality are trivial in discrete ordered groups, we often focus on densely ordered groups, where we will implicitly rely on the following fact.

Fact 7.1

(
[
28
]
, Proposition 2.2)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a definably complete expansion of a densely ordered group. Then
\mathcal{R}
is divisible and abelian.

Through the next lemma and proposition we show that, in definably complete structures, definable sets are definably compact iff they are closed and bounded, and so in particular definably complete structures are definably
\sigma
-compact. We use an approach analogous to Johnson’s
[
24
]
. It is worth noting that one could adapt the proof of Theorem
4.8
directly to the definably complete setting by using the analogue of Fact
4.5
(3) for closed and bounded sets proved by Miller
[
28
]
. Nevertheless, we consider that developing the basic theory of definable compactness in definably complete structures is of independent interest. Lemma
7.2
completes the proof of Theorem
A
.

Lemma 7.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an expansion of a linear order. If
\mathcal{R}
is definably complete, then any closed and bounded interval
I\subseteq R
is definably compact. In particular,
\mathcal{R}
is definably
\sigma
-compact.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{C}=\{C_{b}:b\in B\}
be a downward directed definable family of closed subsets of
I
. By definable completeness, note that

s=\inf_{b\in B}\sup C_{b}

exists. We observe that
s\in\cap\mathcal{C}
. Towards a contradiction, let
b_{0}\in B
be such that
s\notin C_{b_{0}}
. By definition of
s
and definable completeness there exists
s_{0}=\inf(C_{b_{0}}\cap(s,\infty))
with
s_{0}>s
. By definition of
s
there exists some
b_{1}\in B
such that
\sup C_{b_{1}}=s_{1}<s_{0}
. By downward directedness let
b_{2}\in B
be such that
C_{b_{2}}\subseteq C_{b_{0}}\cap C_{b_{1}}
. Then
\sup C_{b_{2}}=\max C_{b_{2}}<s
, which contradicts the definition of
s
.

Since the family of closed and bounded intervals in
R
is (uniformly) definable, we get that
\mathcal{R}
is definably
\sigma
-compact.
∎

Given a linearly ordered structure
\mathcal{R}
, a
box

D\subseteq R^{n}
is a product of
n
intervals in
R
. We call the box
D
open, closed or bounded whenever all the intervals have the corresponding property.

Proposition 7.3

(Definable Heine-Borel)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a definably complete structure. A definable set
X
is definably compact if and only if it is closed and bounded.

Proof.

Suppose that
X\subseteq R^{n}
is definably compact. By Fact
4.5
(2) it is closed. Let
\{D_{b}:b\in B\}
be the upward directed definable family of open boxes in
R^{n}
. Then
\{X\setminus D_{b}:b\in B\}
is a downward directed definable family of closed subsets of
X
with empty intersection. By definable compactness, there exists
b\in B
such that
X\setminus D_{b}=\emptyset
, meaning that
X\subseteq D_{b}
, and so
X
is bounded.

Now suppose that
X
is closed and bounded. Let
D
be a closed and bounded box such that
X\subseteq D
. By Lemma
7.2
and Fact
4.5
(4),
D
is definably compact. By Fact
4.5
(1), the set
X
is definably compact.
∎

A function
f:X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is called
first level Borel
if the inverse image of every
F_{\sigma}
set by
f
is
F_{\sigma}
. These functions belong to the better known collection of Baire class one functions, which are studied in the model-theoretic context in
[
10
]
. Jayne and Rogers
[
22
]
showed that, if
X
is analytic, then
f
is first level Borel iff it admits a countable partition into continuous functions with locally closed domains. One may show that every function definable in a constructible structure over
\mathbb{R}
is first level Borel. Theorem
4.10
strengthens the partition result of Jayne and Rogers in the case of
SHB
expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. We now present a version of Theorems
A
and
B
that takes the form of a dichotomy given by the definability of a certain “pathological” first level Borel function.

Theorem 7.4
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an expansion of the real field
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot)
, then exactly one holds.

1.

The open core
\mathcal{R}^{o}
is constructible and every function definable in
\mathcal{R}^{o}
is both generically and (finitely) piecewise continuous.

2.

The open core
\mathcal{R}^{o}
defines a first level Borel function that is not (finitely) piecewise continuous.

Proof.

Suppose that statement
(1)
does not hold. Let
\mathcal{N}
be an
\omega_{1}
-saturated elementary extension of
\mathcal{R}
and, for every set
A
definable in
\mathcal{R}
, let
A^{\mathcal{N}}
denote its interpretation in
\mathcal{N}
. By Remark
4.11
and
[
6
, N.B. page 1378]
, there exists a definable set
X
, a type-definable set
Z\subseteq X^{\mathcal{N}}
, and a definable covering
\{C_{b}:b\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}\}
of
X
by closed and bounded (definably compact by Proposition
7.3
) sets, such that
C_{b}\subseteq C_{b^{\prime}}
for every
b<b^{\prime}
and moreover
C^{\mathcal{N}}_{b}\cap Z
has empty interior in
Z
for every
b\in N^{>0}
.

Since (1) does not hold, then
\mathcal{R}^{o}
is not o-minimal and so, by
[
6
, Proposition 2.14]
,
\mathcal{R}^{o}
defines an infinite discrete subset
D
of
\mathbb{R}
. By
[
20
, Theorem B]
, we may assume that
D
is closed. Using the group operation we may further assume that
D
is an unbounded subset of
\mathbb{R}^{>0}
.
Let us consider the definable function
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
given by

f(x)=\min\{b\in D:x\in C_{b}\}.

This map is clearly first level Borel, since it is a countable union of constant maps on locally closed sets of the form
C_{b}\setminus C_{b^{\prime}}
, where
b
is the successor of
b^{\prime}
in
D
. Because
D
is closed and discrete and the fibers
f^{-1}(b)
, for
b\in D
, are locally closed, it is easy to see that the graph of
f
is constructible, hence
f
is definable in
\mathcal{R}^{o}
. We show that it is not piecewise continuous.

Let
X_{0}=X
and, for every
n
, let
X_{n+1}
be the points
x\in X
satisfying that, for every
r\in\mathbb{R}^{>0}
, there exists some
y\in X_{n}
with
\|x-y\|_{\infty}<r
and
f(y)>f(x)
. Clearly each set
X_{n}
is definable.

Claim 7.4.1
.

For every
n
it holds that
X_{n}\neq\emptyset
.

Proof.

We show by induction on
n
that
Z\subseteq X_{n}^{\mathcal{N}}
for every
n
. The base case is trivial. Let
x\in Z
and let
W
be an open neighborhood of
x
in
Z
. Since
C_{f(x)}^{\mathcal{N}}
is nowhere dense in
Z
, there exists some
y\in W\setminus C_{f(x)}^{\mathcal{N}}
, which implies that
f^{\mathcal{N}}(y)>f^{\mathcal{N}}(x)
. By induction hypothesis we also have that
y\in X_{n}^{\mathcal{N}}
, and so it follows that
x\in X_{n+1}^{\mathcal{N}}
.

∎

Now, towards a contradiction, let
\mathcal{Y}
be a finite partition of
X
into subsets where the restriction of
f
is continuous. Fix
n=|\mathcal{Y}|
and, by Claim
7.4.1
, some
x_{0}\in X_{n}
. Let
Y_{0}\in\mathcal{Y}
be such that
x_{0}\in Y_{0}
. By definition of
f
and continuity on
Y_{0}
, note that
f|_{Y_{0}}
is locally constant. That is, there exists a neighborhood
W_{0}
of
x_{0}
in
X
such that
f(Y_{0}\cap W_{0})=f(x_{0})
. On the other hand, since
x_{0}\in X_{n}
, there exists some
x_{1}\in W_{0}\cap X_{n-1}
with
f(x_{1})>f(x_{0})
. In particular we have that
x_{1}\notin Y_{0}
.

Continuing recursively we may find, for every
i\leq n
, some
x_{i}\in X
, a set
Y_{i}\in\mathcal{Y}
, and an open set
W_{i}
in
X
satisfying that

1.

x_{i}\in X_{n-i}\cap Y_{i}\cap\bigcap_{j\leq i}W_{j}
,

2.

f(Y_{i}\cap W_{i})=f(x_{i})
,

3.

f(x_{j})<f(x_{i})
for every
j<i
.

In particular note that, for every
i\leq n
, we have that
x_{i}\in Y_{i}\setminus\bigcup_{j<i}Y_{j}
, and so
Y_{j}\neq Y_{i}
for every
0\leq j<i\leq n
. This however contradicts that
|\mathcal{Y}|=n
.
∎

We devote the rest of the section to proving facts about constructible definably complete expansions of ordered groups.
A linearly ordered structure
(R,<,\ldots)
is
generically locally o-minimal
if, for every definable set
X\subseteq R
, there exists a definable subset
Y\subseteq X
that is open and dense in
X
and such that, for every
x\in Y
, there is an interval
I
containing
x
satisfying that
I\cap X
is either
\{x\}
or an interval. We show that every constructible definably complete structure is generically locally o-minimal. This is essentially a known consequence of the fact that constructibility implies the non-definability of a perfect nowhere dense set (see e.g. Lemma 3.26 in
[
12
]
).

Lemma 7.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a definably complete constructible structure. Then
\mathcal{R}
is generically locally o-minimal.

Proof.

Let
X\subseteq R
be a definable set. Let
Y
be the union of the interior and the isolated points in
X
. It suffices to show that
Y
is dense in
X
.

Suppose that
Y
is not dense, then, by passing to
X\setminus cl(Y)
if necessary, we may assume that
X
has no interior and no isolated points. Since
\mathcal{R}
is constructible,
cl(X)
must also have empty interior (Lemma
3.7
), and so we may assume that
X
is closed.

Let
C_{0}
be the set of points
y
in
X
such that
(y^{\prime},y)\cap X=\emptyset
for some
y^{\prime}\in R
, and
C_{1}
be the set of points
z
in
X
such that
(z,z^{\prime})\cap X=\emptyset
for some
z^{\prime}\in R
. Clearly both
C_{0}
and
C_{1}
are definable and disjoint (because
X
has no isolated points). Furthermore, since
X
has empty interior and
\mathcal{R}
is definably complete, they are both dense in
X
. Hence,
C_{0}
is both dense and codense in
X
, contradicting that
\mathcal{R}
is constructible.
∎

The next proposition is a direct consequence of Lemma

Proposition 7.6
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be an
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
definably complete expansion of an ordered group. Then

is generically locally o-minimal.

Definable choice in constructible definably complete expansions of fields was proved by Fornasiero
[
12
, Section 4]
. Miller
[
30

proved it in d-minimal expansions of groups, and Hieronymi-Nell-Walsberg
[

in noiseless expansions of the real ordered group that do not define a Cantor set (equivalently, by Theorem 8.1 in
[

, in strongly noiseless expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)

Proposition 7.7

(Definable choice)
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a definably complete expansion of an ordered group with a positive constant which we denote
1
. Let
A\subseteq R
, and let
X\subseteq R^{m+n}
be an
A
-definable set such that, for every
b\in R^{m}
, the fiber
X_{b}
is constructible. Then there exists an
A
-definable map
f:R^{m}\rightarrow R^{n}
satisfying that, for every
b\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
, if
X_{b}\neq\emptyset
, then
f(b)\in X_{b}
, and moreover, for every pair
b,b^{\prime}\in R^{m}
, if
X_{b}=X_{b^{\prime}}\neq\emptyset
, then
f(b)=f(b^{\prime})
.

As a consequence, if
\mathcal{R}
is strongly noiseless, then it has definable choice functions.

Proof.

We first explain how the second paragraph follows from the first one. Suppose that
\mathcal{R}
is strongly noiseless and let
\{Y_{b}:b\in R^{m}\}
be a definable family of nonempty sets. For each
b\in R^{m}
, let
Y^{\prime}_{b}=cl(Y_{b})\setminus cl(\partial Y_{b})=Y_{b}\setminus\partial^{(2)}Y_{b}
. By strong noiselessness, for every
b\in R^{m}
the constructible set
Y^{\prime}_{b}
is nonempty. The first paragraph of the proposition yields the existence of a definable choice function for
\{Y^{\prime}_{b}:b\in B\}
, which is also a choice function for
\{Y_{b}:b\in R^{m}\}
.

We now prove the first paragraph. Henceforth, for any
x\in R^{n}
and
r\in R^{>0}
, let
B(x,r)
denote the open ball with center
x
in the supremum
R
-valued metric, and let
\overline{B(x,r)}
be the corresponding closed ball. For any set
S\subseteq R^{n}
, let
B(S,r)=\bigcup_{x\in S}B(x,r)
. Fix
b\in B
with
X_{b}\neq\emptyset
. Suppose that
X_{b}
is closed. Let
r_{0}=\inf\{s\in R^{>0}:B(0,s)\cap X_{b}\neq\emptyset\}
. We may choose
f(b)
to be the lexicographic minimum of the closed and bounded set
X_{b}\cap\overline{B(0,r_{0}+1)}
. Observe that this covers the case where
\mathcal{R}
is discrete, so onward we may assume that it is dense. Now suppose that
X_{b}
is not closed. We prove the proposition by showing that there exists a nonempty definable closed subset of
X_{b}
, which is moreover
Ab
-definable uniformly in
b
.

Let
Z=cl(\partial X_{b})
. Since
X_{b}
is not closed, this set is nonempty. Since
X_{b}
is constructible, we have that
W=X_{b}\setminus Z\neq\emptyset
. Let
r_{1}=\sup\{s\in R^{>0}:W\setminus B(Z,s)\neq\emptyset\}
if the supremum is finite, and otherwise let
r_{1}=1
. Now set
C_{b}:=X_{b}\setminus B(Z,r_{1}/2)
. Note that
C_{b}=cl(X_{b})\setminus B(Z,r_{1}/2)
, and so
C_{b}
is closed. Finally, observe that
C_{b}
is
Ab
-definable uniformly in
b
.
∎

In his proof of Proposition

Example 7.8
.

Consider the real ordered group
\mathcal{R}=(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. It is a well-known fact that this structure has quantifier elimination and thus it is o-minimal and every definable function
f:\mathbb{R}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
is piecewise linear. We show that the interval
I=(0,\infty)
is not the projection of a closed definable set. Towards a contradiction let
C\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be a closed definable set and
\pi:C\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
be a projection, chosen without loss of generality to the first coordinate, such that
\pi(C)=I
. For each
t\in I
, let
C_{t}
be the corresponding nonempty fiber of
C
. Since
C
is closed and
\pi(C)=I
, observe that
\gamma(t)
cannot converge in
\mathbb{R}^{n}
as

. By o-minimality, there must exist a coordinate function
\gamma_{i}:I\rightarrow\mathbb{R}
of
\gamma
that converges to either
-\infty
or to
\infty
as
t\rightarrow 0
. This however contradicts the linearity of
\mathcal{R}
.

Given a nonempty set
X\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
, the
naive dimension
of
X
, which we denote henceforth by
\dim(X)
, is the maximum between zero and the largest
0<d\leq n
such that there exists some projection
\pi:\mathbb{R}^{n}\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{d}
satisfying that
\pi(X)
has nonempty interior. By convention
\dim\emptyset=-\infty
.

The dimension theory for
D_{\Sigma}
sets (see Remark
4.11
) was developed in
[
10
]
, and further analyzed in generically locally o-minimal expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
in the preprint version
[
36
]
of
[
37
]
. Through the next proposition we showcase that naive dimension is well-behaved in constructible expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. In particular, by Theorem
A
, this applies to the open core of every
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. By
[
36
, Section 10]
, the proposition holds in the more general setting of strongly noiseless expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. We nevertheless state it in the constructible setting because its proof follows mostly elementarily from
[

]
.

Proposition 7.9
.

Let
\mathcal{R}
be a constructible expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. Let
X,Y\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{n}
be definable sets. The following hold.

1.

The naive dimension
\dim(X)
of

X
is equal to its classical topological dimension
5
5

5

That is, small inductive dimension, large inductive dimension, or Lebesgue covering dimension, all of which are identical for subsets of
\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
.

2.

\dim(X\cup Y)=\max\{\dim X,\dim Y\}
.

3.

\dim X=\dim cl(X)
.

4.

If
f:X\rightarrow\mathbb{R}^{m}
is a definable function, then
\dim f(X)\leq\dim X
. In particular dimension is maintained under definable bijections.

5.

Let
0<m<n
, and let
Z\subseteq\mathbb{R}^{m}
denote the projection of
X
to some
m
coordinates. For every
a\in Z
, let
X_{a}
denote the corresponding fiber of
X
. For every
0\leq d\leq n-m
, let
S(d)=\{a\in Z:\dim X_{a}=d\}
. Then each set
S(d)
is definable and

\dim X=\max_{0\leq d\leq n-m}\dim S(d)+d.

Proof.

By
[
6
, 1.10]
every constructible definable set is
D_{\Sigma}
(see Remark
4.11
). Furthermore, by Lemma
3.7
,
\mathcal{R}
cannot define a dense
\omega
-order in the sense of
[
10
]
. It follows that statement (
1
) is given by
[

, Proposition 5.7]
, and statement (
4
) follows from
[
10
, Theorem E]
, where the results are achieved for
D_{\Sigma}
sets in expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
that do not define a dense
\omega
-order.

While statements (
2
) and (
3
) also follow from
[
10
]
, we include the proofs, which are straightforward in our context. For (
2
), note that clearly
\dim(X\cup Y)\geq\max\{\dim X,\dim Y\}
. For the other inequality, let
\pi
be a projection such that
\pi(X\cup Y)=\pi(X)\cup\pi(Y)
has nonempty interior

For statement (

8
Strong uniform structures

In this section we strengthen the
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
condition to the property of being strong. Among
\mathrm{NIP}
structures, being strong is equivalent to being strongly dependent. Following the treatment in
[
37
]
, we characterize all strong expansions of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
. More generally, we show that every strong expansion of a definably complete group has locally o-minimal open core (Theorem
E
). In the wider context of strong uniform structures, we begin the section by showing that constructibility is equivalent to strong noiselessness, and that having constructible open core is maintained under elementary equivalence.

In the proof of the next lemma, we recover the notation
(U\setminus V)[Y]=U[Y]\setminus V[Y]
used in Section
5
.

Lemma 8.1
.

Let
\mathcal{M}
be a uniform structure. Let
\kappa>1
be a cardinal and
\{X^{(\alpha)}:\alpha<\kappa\}
be a family of nonempty definable subsets of
M^{d}
satisfying that, for all
\alpha<\beta<\kappa
,

1.

X^{(\beta)}\subseteq X^{(\alpha)}
,

2.

X^{(\beta)}
is nowhere dense in
X^{(\alpha)}
(in the subspace topology).

Then
X^{(0)}
admits an inp-pattern of depth
\kappa-1
.

Proof.

Let
\mathcal{B}
be a definable basis for the product uniformity on
M^{d}
. Let
\{X^{(\alpha)}:\alpha<\kappa\}
be as in the lemma. By passing to the closures if necessary, we may assume that every set
X^{(\alpha)}
, for
0<\alpha<\kappa
, is closed in
X^{(0)}
. For each
0<\alpha<\kappa
, let
\varphi_{\alpha}
be a (partitioned) formula that defines the family of sets of the form
(U\setminus V)[X^{(\alpha)}]
, for
U,V\in\mathcal{B}
. We prove the existence of an inp-pattern of depth
\kappa-1
witnessed by the formulas
\varphi_{\alpha}
. Specifically, we describe, for every
m>1
and
0<\alpha_{1}<\cdots\alpha_{m}<\kappa
, entourages
U_{i,j}
, for
1\leq i\leq m
and
1\leq j\leq m+1
, such that the family of sets
\{(U_{i,j}\setminus U_{i,j+1})[X^{(\alpha_{i})}]:i,j\leq m\}
forms an inp-pattern of depth
m
and length
m
in
X^{(0)}
(namely conditions (1) and (2) in the definition of inp-pattern in Section
2
are satisfied, and moreover the families described in condition (2) are consistent with
X^{(0)}
). For simplicity of notation, and since there will be no room for confusion, we set
\alpha_{i}=i
for every
i\leq m
.

Let us fix
m>1
. For any
n\leq m
, suppose that we have defined entourages
U_{i,j}
for
n\leq i\leq m
and
j\leq m+1
, and consider the following two statements.

(i)_{n}

Each family
\{(U_{i,j}\setminus U_{i,j+1})[X^{(i)}]:j\leq m\}
, for
n\leq i\leq m
, is pairwise disjoint.

(ii)_{n}

For every map
\eta:\{n,\ldots,m\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
, the intersection of the family
\{(U_{i,\eta(i)}\setminus U_{i,\eta(i)+1})[X^{(i)}]:n\leq i\leq m\}
has nonempty interior which intersects
X^{(n-1)}
.

We prove the lemma by proving
(i)_{1}
and
(ii)_{1}
. We proceed by reverse induction on
n\leq m
. We will use a simple claim.

Claim 8.1.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a finite family of nonempty open sets in
M^{d}
, each intersecting
X^{(n)}
. For any entourage
U\in\mathcal{B}
, there exists another entourage
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that, for all
W\in\mathcal{F}
, the
interior
of the set
W\cap(U\setminus V)[X^{(n)}]
intersects the set
X^{(n-1)}
.

Proof.

Since
X^{(n)}
has empty interior in
X^{(n-1)}
, for each
W\in\mathcal{F}
there exists a point
x_{W}\in W\cap U[X^{(n)}]\cap X^{(n-1)}\setminus X^{(n)}
. Since
X^{(n)}
is closed, there exists
V\in\mathcal{B}
such that
\{x_{W}:W\in\mathcal{F}\}
is disjoint from the closure of
V[X^{(n)}]
. The claim follows.

∎

Let
U_{m,1}\in\mathcal{B}
be any entourage. By repeated application of Claim
8.1.1
(with
\mathcal{F}=\{M^{d}\}
) there exists a decreasing sequence of entourages
U_{m,j}
, for
j\leq m+1
, satisfying that the interior of each set
(U_{m,j}\setminus U_{m,j+1})[X^{(m)}]
intersects the set
X^{(m-1)}
. In particular condition
(ii)_{m}
is clearly satisfied. Since the sequence of entourages is decreasing, condition
(i)_{m}
is also immediate.

We now fix
n<m
, and by induction hypothesis we assume that both
(i)_{n+1}
and
(ii)_{n+1}
hold, witnessed by entourages
U_{i,j}
, for
n<i\leq m
and
j\leq m+1
. For each map
\eta:\{n+1,\ldots,m\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
, let us denote the interior of the intersection
\bigcap\{(U_{i,\eta(i)}\setminus U_{i,\eta(i)+1})[X^{(i)}]:n<i\leq m\}
by
W_{\eta}
. By condition
(ii)_{n+1}
, every such open set
W_{\eta}
intersects
X^{(n)}
. Let
U_{n,1}\in\mathcal{B}
be any entourage. By repeated application of Claim
8.1.1
, we may find a decreasing sequence of entourages
U_{n,j}
, for
j\leq m+1
, satisfying that, for any
\eta:\{n+1,\ldots,m\}\rightarrow\{1,\ldots,m\}
and
j\leq m
, the interior of the set
W_{\eta}\cap(U_{n,j}\setminus U_{n,j+1})[X^{(n)}]
intersects the set
X^{(n-1)}
. Condition
(ii)_{n}
clearly follows. Furthermore, since once again the entourages
U_{n,j}
are decreasing, condition
(i)_{n}
is immediate.
∎

Lemma

yields that strong uniform structures have a property known as the “closed chain condition”. For an exploration of the topological consequences of this property, the interested reader is directed to

Lemma 8.2
.

Let
X
be a subset of a topological space. If

has nonempty interior
Z
in

, then
X
is dense and codense in
Z
. In particular

is not constructible.

Proof.

Let

be a nonempty open set such that
W\cap cl(X)=Z\subseteq cl(\partial X)
. Note that
cl(X)=X\cup\partial X
and
cl(\partial X)=\partial X\cup\partial^{(2)}X
, where the unions are disjoint and
X\cap cl(\partial X)=\partial^{(2)}X
. Hence
W\cap X\subseteq\partial^{(2)}X
. So
W\cap X\subseteq cl(W\cap\partial X)
. Since
W\cap\partial X\subseteq cl(W\cap X)
, it follows that
X
is dense and codense in
W\cap cl(X)=Z
. The last sentence in the lemma follows from Lemma
3.7
.
∎

Let

Proof.

Statements

Let
\mathcal{M}

The equivalence between strong noiselessness and constructibility follows directly from Lemma

In general, if a topological structure does not have constructible open core, then there exists a definable constructible set

Proposition 8.5

and

9
D-minimal structures

A definably complete expansion
\mathcal{R}
of a dense linear order
(R,<)
is
d-minimal
(short for
discrete-minimal
) if, for every definable family
\mathcal{C}
of subsets of
R
, there exists some
n
such that every set in
\mathcal{C}
is the union of an open set and at most
n
discrete sets
6
6

6

In some texts this definition is called “strong d-minimality”. Meanwhile, “d-minimality” is reserved for the version of the definition where the
n
is not necessarily fixed in definable families. In this sense, recent work of Farris and Hieronymi shows that d-minimal expansions of ordered fields are strongly d-minimal.
. This notion originated from the work of Miller
[
29
]
. Known examples of
\mathrm{NIP}
d-minimal structures include
(\mathbb{R},<,+,\mathbb{Z})
and
(\mathbb{R},+,\cdot,2^{\mathbb{Z}})
.

It is an open question whether every
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
–or even just
\mathrm{NIP}
– expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
has d-minimal open core (Question
9.6
). More generally, every constructible expansion of
(\mathbb{R},<,+)
known to the author is d-minimal. The role of d-minimality in characterizing neostable structures over the reals is thus conspicuously open. In this section we approach this subject by proving Theorem
F
(through Propositions
9.4
and
9.5
).

We fix some useful terminology. Given an expansion
\mathcal{R}
of a linear order
(R,<)
and a definable set
X\subseteq R
, let
X^{[n]}
denote the
n
-th Cantor-Bendixson derivative of
X
. That is,
X^{[0]}=X
and, for every
n
, the set
X^{[n+1]}
denotes the non-isolated points in
X^{[n]}
. If
\mathcal{R}
is
\omega_{1}
-saturated, we call
Z=\bigcap_{n<\omega}X^{[n]}
the
type-definable perfect component of
X
7
7

7

In spite of the chosen terminology, observe that the set
Z
might not be closed.
. A straightforward application of saturation shows that
Z
does not contain isolated points.

Remark 9.1
.

Given a linear order
(R,<)
, it is well known that a set
X\subseteq R
is a finite union of at most
n
discrete sets iff
X^{[n]}=\emptyset
(see 1.2 and 1.3 in
[
14
]
). In particular, if
X^{[n]}=\emptyset
, then
X
is partitioned by the discrete sets of the form
X^{[i]}\setminus X^{[i+1]}
, for
i<n
. If
X
is definable in some expansion of
(R,<)
, then these sets are definable too. Furthermore, given a definable family
\{X_{b}:b\in B\}
of subsets of
R
, for every fixed
i
the sets
X_{b}^{[i]}\setminus X_{b}^{[i+1]}
are clearly definable uniformly in
b\in B
.

We prove two lemmas, the first of which establishes a connection between
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
and Cantor-Bendixson rank.

Lemma 9.2
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=(R,<,+,\ldots)
be an
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}
expansion of an ordered group and
X\subseteq R
be a definable set. Suppose that there exists a definable family
\{C_{b}:b\in B\}
of closed discrete subsets of
R
satisfying that, for every finite set
F\subseteq X
, there is some
b\in B
such that
F\subseteq C_{b}
. Then
X
is a finite union of discrete sets.

Proof.

Towards a contradiction suppose that
X
is not a union of finitely many discrete sets, equivalently
X^{[n]}\neq\emptyset
for every
n
. We may assume that
\mathcal{R}
is
\omega_{1}
-saturated. Let
Z=\bigcap_{n<\omega}X^{[n]}
be the type-definable perfect component of
X
. For every finite set
F\subseteq Z
, there exists some
b\in B
such that
F\subseteq C_{b}
. Since
Z
has no isolated points, for every
b\in B
the set
C_{b}
has empty interior in
Z
. By Lemma
5.1
we conclude that
\mathcal{R}
has
\mathrm{TP}_{2}
.
∎

Lemma 9.3
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=(R,<,+,\ldots)
be an expansion of an ordered group, and let
X\subseteq R
be a set that is the union of
n
discrete sets
X_{1},\ldots,X_{n}
. For every
\varepsilon\in R^{>0}
, let

X(\varepsilon)=\bigcup_{i=1}^{n}\{x\in X_{i}:(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)\cap X_{i}=\{x\}\}.

Then the family
\{X(\varepsilon):\varepsilon\in R^{>0}\}
is an upward directed covering of
X
by closed discrete sets.

Proof.

The case where
\mathcal{R}
is discrete is trivial so we assume otherwise.
It is immediate that the described family is an upward directed covering of
X
. Let us fix some
\varepsilon\in R^{>0}
and let
a\in R
. By density of
(R,<)
and continuity of the map
x\mapsto 2x
at
0
, there exists some
\delta\in R^{>0}
satisfying that
2\delta<\varepsilon
. It is routine to see that, for any
x\leq y
with
x,y\in(a-\delta,a+\delta)
, it holds that
y\in(x-\varepsilon,x+\varepsilon)
. Consequently, for any
i\leq n
, we have that
|(a-\delta,a+\delta)\cap X(\varepsilon)\cap X_{i}|\leq 1
, and so
|(a-\delta,a+\delta)\cap X(\varepsilon)|\leq n
. It follows that
X(\varepsilon)
is closed and discrete.
∎

.

Let

Let

[

Any
\mathrm{NTP}_{2}

\mathcal{R}
of

Proposition 9.5
.

Let
\mathcal{R}=(R,<,+,\ldots)
be an expansion of an ordered group. Let

\mathcal{R}

\mathrm{TP}_{2}
.

For every
\varepsilon\in R^{>0}
, let

is closed. By Lemma

∎
