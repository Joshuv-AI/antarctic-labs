A Lyapunov Analysis of Softmax Policy Gradient for Stochastic Bandits
Tor Lattimore

Abstract
We adapt the analysis of policy gradient for continuous time k k -armed stochastic bandits by 2 to the standard discrete time setup. As in continuous time, we prove that with learning rate η = O ⁡ ( Δ min 2 / ( Δ max ​ log ⁡ ( n ) ) ) \eta=O(\Delta_{\min}^{2}/(\Delta_{\max}\log(n))) the regret is O ⁡ ( k ​ log ⁡ ( k ) ​ log ⁡ ( n ) / η ) O(k\log(k)\log(n)/\eta) where n n is the horizon and Δ min \Delta_{\min} and Δ max \Delta_{\max} are the minimum and maximum gaps.

Related work and discussion

5
prove logarithmic regret in the deterministic setting (
7
did the same in continuous time).
In the stochastic setting
6
claimed logarithmic regret when the learning rate is
O(\Delta_{\min}^{2}/k^{3/2})
.
1

identified a small issue with their argument, which after correction probably leads to polylogarithmic regret.
Remarkably,
4
proved that when the optimal arm is unique, then
\pi_{t}
converges almost surely to a Dirac on the optimal action for
any
learning rate.
Policy gradient is often studied with a time-varying learning rate (and for MDPs) with sample complexity as the usual metric
(
8
;
3
)
.
These results are hard to compare directly.

Logarithmic factors

The main question is whether or not the logarithmic factor in the denominator of
\eta
in the condition in
Theorem

1
can be relaxed.
One interesting data point is by
1
, who show that if
\Delta_{\min}=\Delta_{\max}
, then policy gradient enjoys
O(k\log(n)/\eta)
regret when
\eta=O(\Delta_{\min}/k)
.
This bound is worse than ours when
k
is large relative to
\log(n)
but better as
n\to\infty
. Most importantly, it shows that at least in this special
case there is no need for the logarithmic factor in the denominator.
On the other hand, the lower bound by
2
(in continuous time) shows that if
\eta=C\Delta_{\min}^{2}
for suitably large
C
, then on the instance with Gaussian noise and

\mu=(1,1-\Delta_{\min},0,\ldots,0)
, the regret is
\Omega(n\Delta_{\min})
even for large
n
.

Improved asymptotics

A potential way to improve the algorithm is to use a learning rate that
increases
with time.
The miniscule learning rate was needed in the proof of
Lemma

4
, but in an asymptotic sense the argument there is weak in two places:

(1)
We naively bounded
R_{t}\leq\Delta_{\max}
; and
(2)
in the last display we used the bound
Z_{s}\geq 0
. But in reality, with high probability this bound is loose except
when
s=1
. What actually happens is
(Z_{s})
slowly grows and once sufficient growth has occurred, the learning rate can be increased dramatically without risking that it becomes negative.
The caveat is that this growth is not observed (because the set of optimal actions is unknown). Hence the identifying the correct learning rate depends on understanding the expected sample path
of
(\theta_{t})
.
Besides
Lemma

4
, we otherwise only used that
\eta\leq\Delta_{\min}/4
in the proof of
Theorem

1
.

Remark 6
.

A specialisation of
1
shows that if
\eta>\frac{3\log(3)}{k-1}
, then there exists an instance with
\Delta_{\min}\geq 1/2
where the regret is
\Omega(n^{2/3})
.
When
k
is large, this seems to contradict our bound, which on these instances shows that
\eta=\tilde{O}(1)
is sufficient for polylogarithmic regret.
The resolution is that the lower bound of
1
is asymptotic only and our proposed learning rate depends on the horizon.
