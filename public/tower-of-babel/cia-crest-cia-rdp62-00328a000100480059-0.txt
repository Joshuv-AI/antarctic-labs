A. To maximise intelligence support to the security list reviews
undertaken in connection with C0CCM negotiations, a new procedure was
adopted in July 1953 to supersede the previous arrangement under which
the Office of International Trade (later Bureau of Foreign Commerce)
had assumed responsibility for combining intelligence contributions
from various agencies and writing a single paper containing technical
definitions, intelligence and strategic rating recommendations . 'Hie
new procedure provided for the preparation within the IWG of a paper
incorporating intelligence on Soviet bloc uees, production capabilities
and shortages, with a second paper containing technical information
only being prepared In BFC. This procedure resulted in a marked
improvement in the intelligence product and was utilised until the
accelerated security list review in the spring of 1954 made IWCJ processing
of commodity paper impracticable.

B« During the recent Trilateral and CG/CGCGM review of the security
lists, the following criteria were used in placing items on embargo*
Materials and equipment (1) designed specially or used principally for
development, production or utilisation of arms, ammunition, implements
of war and atomic energy material sj (2) which incorporate advanced
technology or unique know-how, the acquisition of which may reasonably
b© expected to permit a significant advance in Soviet bloc technology
in military or atomic energy production over the level of development
already achieved or expected to be achieved within a short period! and,

(3) in which the Soviet bloc has a deficiency which is critical in
relation to its military capabilities and which it could not overcome
within a short period. An item could be placed on international embargo
only if one of the above criteria were met* It was essential, therefore,
that information pointing up the direct, or close indirect, military
application of particular commodities be made available to the 0.3.
Delegation. Discussions were held between representatives of the military
services and the Chairman, IWG, and immediate steps were agreed upon
to elfccit additional information of this nature.

C. A related problem in the production of coasnodity analyses has
been that of obtaining approval for release of information for use in

intelligence falling within the category of "classified military infor-

Because of this reluctance an effort was made to acquaint

classified military information is for the purpose of obtaining a
quid pro quo in the form of restrictions of strategic exports, and
(2) inability to use such information would impede attainment of the
objectives of U.S. economic defense policy.
