Abstract
. Let L L be a second-order, homogeneous, constant (complex) coefficient elliptic system in ℝ n {\mathbb{R}}^{n} . The goal of this article is provide a qualitative and quantitative study of the nature of the Green function associated with the system L L in the upper-half space. Starting with a definition of the Green function which brings forth the minimal features which identify this object uniquely, we establish optimal nontangential maximal function estimates and regularity results up to the boundary for the said Green function. The main tools employed in the proof include the Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg construction of a Poisson kernel for the system L L , the Agmon-Douglis-Nirenberg a priori regularity estimates near the boundary, and the brand of Divergence Theorem from [ 20 ] in which the boundary trace of the corresponding vector field is taken in nontangential pointwise sense.

1.
Introduction

Fix a space dimension
n\in{\mathbb{N}}
and, unless otherwise stated, assume
n\geq 2
.
Pick an integer
M\in{\mathbb{N}}
and consider the second-order, homogeneous,
M\times M
system,
with constant complex coefficients, written (with the usual convention of summation over
repeated indices in place) as

L:=\Big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{rs}\partial_{r}\partial_{s}\Big)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M},

(1.1)

naturally acting on vector-valued (or matrix-valued) distributions defined in (open subsets of)
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
.
Examples to keep in mind are the Laplacian and the Lamé system. Typically, two types of ellipticity conditions
are considered. First, we shall say that
L
expressed as in (
1.1
) is
strongly

elliptic
provided
there exists a real number
c>0
such that the following Legendre-Hadamard condition is satisfied:

\begin{array}[]{c}{\rm Re}\,\big[a^{\alpha\beta}_{rs}\xi_{r}\xi_{s}\overline{\eta_{\alpha}}\eta_{\beta}\,\big]\geq c|\xi|^{2}|\eta|^{2}\,\,\text{ for every}\\[8.0pt]
\xi=(\xi_{r})_{1\leq r\leq n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\,\,\text{ and }\,\,\eta=(\eta_{\alpha})_{1\leq\alpha\leq M}\in{\mathbb{C}}^{M}.\end{array}

(1.2)

Second, we shall say that
L
is
weakly

elliptic
if

{\rm det}\Big[\big(a^{\alpha\beta}_{rs}\xi_{r}\xi_{s}\big)_{1\leq\alpha,\beta\leq M}\Big]\not=0\,\,\text{ for every }\,\,\xi=(\xi_{r})_{1\leq r\leq n}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}\setminus\{0\}.

(1.3)

Obviously, any strongly elliptic system is weakly elliptic. For example, considering the one-parameter family of
scalar differential operators in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
given by

L_{\lambda}:=\partial_{1}^{2}+\cdots+\partial_{n-1}^{2}+\lambda\partial_{n}^{2},\qquad\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}},

(1.4)

then
L_{\lambda}
satisfies the Legendre-Hadamard ellipticity condition (
1.2
) if and only if
{\rm Re}\,\lambda>0
,
whereas
L_{\lambda}
is weakly elliptic if and only if
\lambda\in{\mathbb{C}}\setminus(-\infty,0]
. For more on this and related
topics the reader is referred to
[
22
]
.

As is known from the classical work of S. Agmon, A. Douglis, and L. Nirenberg in
[
1
]
-
[
2
]

(cf. also
[
14
]
,
[
27
]
,
[
29
]
,
[
30
]
,
[
12
, §10.3]
, and the discussion in
[
11
, p. 24]
),
every operator
L
as in (
1.1
)-(
1.2
) has a Poisson kernel, denoted by
P^{L}

(an object whose properties mirror the most basic characteristics of the classical harmonic Poisson kernel).
For details, see Theorem
2.6
below.

Our main result is Theorem
1.2
elaborating on the nature of the Green function associated
with a given elliptic system. Prior to formulating this result, some comments on the notation used
are in order. Throughout,
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}:=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\times{\mathbb{R}}:\,x_{n}>0\}
will
denote the upper-half space. Given a set
K\subseteq{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
along with a function
u
defined in

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus K
, by
{\mathcal{N}}^{\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus K}_{\kappa}u
we shall
denote the nontangential maximal function of

with aperture
\kappa
; see (
2.4
) for a precise
definition. Next, by
u\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}
we denote the
(
\kappa
-)nontangential limit of the given function
u
on the boundary of the upper half-space
(canonically identified with
{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}
), as defined in (
2.3
). Given any

n\in{\mathbb{N}}
, the Lebesgue measure in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}
will be denoted by
{\mathscr{L}}^{n}
.
Also, we shall denote by
\mathcal{D}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})
the space of distributions in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.
Finally, we agree to abbreviate
{\rm diag}:=\{(x,x):\,x\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\}
for the diagonal in
the Cartesian product
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
.

To set the stage, we make the following definition.

Definition 1.1
.

Fix
n,M\in{\mathbb{N}}
with
n\geq 2
.
Let
L
be an
M\times M
system with constant complex coefficients as in (
1.1
) and (
1.3
).
Call
G^{L}(\cdot,\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus{\rm diag}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{M\times M}

a
Green

function
for
L
in
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
provided for each
y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

the following properties hold
(
for some aperture parameter
\kappa>0
)
:

\displaystyle G^{L}(\cdot\,,y)\in\big[L^{1}_{\rm loc}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\big]^{M\times M},

(1.5)

\displaystyle G^{L}(\cdot\,,y)\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}=0\,\,\text{ at ${\mathscr{L}}^{n-1}$-a.e. point in }\,\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}\equiv\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+},

(1.6)

\displaystyle\int_{{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}}\Big({\mathcal{N}}^{\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus\overline{B(y,y_{n}/2)}}_{\kappa}\,G^{L}(\cdot\,,y)\Big)(x^{\prime})\frac{dx^{\prime}}{1+|x^{\prime}|^{n-1}}<+\infty,

(1.7)

\displaystyle L\big[G^{L}(\cdot\,,y)\big]=\delta_{y}\,I_{M\times M}\,\,\text{ in }\,\,\big[{\mathcal{D}}^{\prime}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+})\big]^{M\times M},

(1.8)

where the
M\times M
system
L
acts in the “dot” variable on the columns of
G
,
and
\delta_{y}
denotes Dirac’s distribution with mass at
y
.

A few comments pertaining to the nature of Definition

are appropriate.
We remark that (
1.5
) and (
1.8
) simply amount to saying that the
matrix-valued function
G(\cdot,y)
is a fundamental solution for the system
L
the upper-half space
which is adapted to this domain, in the sense that it has a vanishing boundary trace
(considered in nontangential sense, in the “dot” variable; cf. (

)).
As a byproduct, elliptic regularity guarantees that

G(\cdot\,,y)\in\big[{\mathscr{C}}^{\infty}({\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus\{y\})\big]^{M\times M}\,\,\text{ for each }\,\,y\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}.

(1.9)

This being said,
G(\cdot\,,y)
has a singularity at the pole
y
which, in particular,
explains why the nontangential maximal function in (
1.7
) is restricted over

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus\overline{B(y,y_{n}/2)}
, a region which avoids the singular point
y
.
Of course, instead of this region we could have considered (with the same effect)

{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus K
for any compact subset
K
of
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
whose interior,
denoted by
\mathring{K}
, contains the pole
y
.

Since for each
\varepsilon>0
there exists
C=C(n,\kappa,\varepsilon,y)\in(0,\infty)
such that (cf. (
3.97
))

\Big({\mathcal{N}}^{\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus\overline{B(y,y_{n}/2)}}_{\kappa}(1+|\cdot|)^{-\varepsilon}\Big)(x^{\prime})\leq C(1+|x^{\prime}|)^{-\varepsilon}\,\,\text{ for all }\,\,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},

(1.10)

it follows that the finiteness condition in (
1.7
) is automatically satisfied if,
for each fixed pole
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
,

G^{L}(\cdot\,,y)
is bounded away from the pole and there exists
\varepsilon>0
so that

G^{L}(x,y)=O\big(|x|^{-\varepsilon}\big)
as
|x|\to\infty
.

(1.11)

Properties (
1.5
)-(
1.6
) and (
1.8
) are staple features of any notion
of Green function, but these alone do not identify said Green function uniquely. For example, adding any
constant multiple of
x_{n}
to such a Green function does not affect properties (
1.5
)-(
1.6
)
and (
1.8
). We have imposed the finiteness condition in (
1.7
) in order to (hopefully!) ensure uniqueness.
That the conditions stipulated in Definition
1.1
indeed identify the Green function uniquely
makes the object of our main result, stated in Theorem
1.2
below. In addition to existence
and uniqueness, this elaborates on a wealth of other basic properties of our brand of Green function.
Among other things, it is shown that our integral finiteness condition formulated in (
1.7
)
self-improves to the pointwise properties stated in (
1.11
), at least if
n\geq 3
; see (
1.17
).

Before stating the aforementioned theorem, we make two conventions regarding notation. First, given a generic function
G(\cdot,\cdot)

of two vector variables,
(x,y)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus{\rm diag}
, for each
k\in\{1,\dots,n\}

we agree to write
\partial_{X_{k}}G
and
\partial_{Y_{k}}G
, respectively, for the partial derivative
of
G
with respect to
x_{k}
, and
y_{k}
. This convention may be iterated, lending a natural meaning to

\partial^{\alpha}_{X}\partial^{\beta}_{Y}G
, for each pair of multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in{\mathbb{N}}_{0}^{n}
.
Second, we shall interpret
\nabla_{X}G
and
\nabla_{Y}G
as the gradients of
G(x,y)
with respect to

x
and
y
, respectively. Lastly, as usual, we set
\log_{+}t:=\max\big\{0\,,\,\ln t\big\}
for each
t\in(0,\infty)
.

Theorem 1.2
.

Fix
n,M\in{\mathbb{N}}
with

(1)

(1.12)

Moreover, for any multi-indices
\alpha,\beta\in\mathbb{N}_{0}^{n}

(2)

(1.15)

(1.16)

(1.32)

As noted in (

. Bearing this in mind,
from Corollary

does not possess a Green function of the sort described in Theorem

There is a version of Theorem

, stated in Theorem

Before presenting a formal statement, we need some definitions.
Given a second-order, homogeneous, complex constant coefficient,

We shall say that

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{for each $\alpha,\beta\in\{1,\dots,M\}$ and $j,k\in\{1,\dots,n\}$}\\[0.0pt]

In particular,

the system

We wish to remark that if the system

Finally, as noted in (

Theorem 1.4

Fix

(1.38)

Moreover, one has the following partial improvement of (

)-(

(1.39)

for every

Finally, if

Our next result in this section describes the rather precise relationship between the

Corollary 1.5
.

Assume that
L
is an

system with constant complex coefficients as in

)-(

for

In addition, if the fundamental solution

(1.42)

Theorem 1.6
.

Let

where

Corollary 1.7

.
We shall work in the upper-half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}

{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}

\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime}):=\big\{y=(y^{\prime},t)\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}:\,|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|<\kappa\,t\big\}.

(2.1)

Given a vector-valued function
u:{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{M}

the nontangential maximal function of

\big({\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}u\big)(x^{\prime}):=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime}))},\qquad\forall\,x^{\prime}\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\equiv{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1},

(2.2)

{\mathscr{L}}^{n}
.
Whenever meaningful, we also define the nontangential trace of a function

\big(u\big|^{{}^{\kappa-{\rm n.t.}}}_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\big)(x^{\prime}):=\lim_{\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\ni y\to(x^{\prime},0)}u(y)\quad\text{for }\,x^{\prime}\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\equiv{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}.

(2.3)

In the sequel, we shall need to consider a localized version of the
nontangential maximal operator. Specifically, given any

E\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
, for each

\big({\mathcal{N}}^{E}_{\kappa}u\big)(x^{\prime}):=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\cap E)},\qquad\forall\,x^{\prime}\in\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\equiv{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}.

(2.4)

Hence,
{\mathcal{N}}^{E}_{\kappa}u={\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}\widetilde{u}

where
\widetilde{u}
is the extension of
u
to
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}

by zero outside
E
. In the scenario when

the entire upper-half space
{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}

therefore write

{\mathcal{N}}^{E}_{\kappa}u={\mathcal{N}}_{\kappa}({\bf 1}_{E}u).

(2.5)

Proof.

(2.7)

Proof of Theorem
1.2

Recall from Theorem
2.6

(in the sense of Definition

(ii)

In particular (cf. item

(3.1)

Throughout the proof, given an arbitrary point
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{n})\in\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

we shall denote by
\overline{y}
its reflection across
\partial\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}
, i.e.,

will denote the fundamental solution for

from Theorem
2.8

The strategy is to use the Poisson kernel

1.1

this special Green function will be employed to show that there is

1.1
.
This is accomplished in a series of steps, starting with:

Step 1:

For each
y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and each

define

G^{L}(x,y):=E^{L}(x-y)-E^{L}(x-\overline{y}\,)-P^{L}_{t}\ast\Big(\big[E^{L}(\cdot-y)-E^{L}(\cdot-\overline{y}\,)\big]\big|_{\partial{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\Big)(x^{\prime}),

(3.2)

where the convolution with
P^{L}_{t}
is applied to each column of the matrix inside the round parentheses.
Then
G^{L}(\cdot,\cdot):{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\times{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus{\rm diag}\to{\mathbb{C}}^{M\times M}

is a Green function for
L
in
\mathbb{R}^{n}_{+}

(
in the sense of Definition
1.1
)

which also satisfies

(3.3)

and

\begin{array}[]{c}\text{for each point $y\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}$, each compact $K\subset{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}$
with $y\in\mathring{K}$},\\[6.0pt]
\text{and each $\kappa>0$, there exists a constant $C_{y,K}\in(0,\infty)$ such that}\\[10.0pt]
\displaystyle\big(\mathcal{N}^{{}^{\,{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}\setminus K}}_{\kappa}G^{L}(\cdot,y)\big)(x^{\prime})\leq C_{y,K}\Big(\frac{1+\log_{+}|x^{\prime}|}{1+|x^{\prime}|^{n-1}}\Big)\,\,\text{ at each }\,\,x^{\prime}\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n-1}.\end{array}

(3.4)

To get started, fix some
\kappa>0
along with a point

, and consider the compact set

then

\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\setminus K}|y-z|\geq{\rm dist}\,(z,\partial K)\geq|x^{\prime}|/C_{\ast}

(3.102)

so (
3.100
) holds in this situation. Second, if
|x^{\prime}|>C_{\ast}\cdot{\rm dist}\,(z,\partial K)
then
(
3.101

|x^{\prime}|>\frac{2R_{K}}{\cos\alpha}
. Hence
{\rm dist}\,\big(0,\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\big)=|x^{\prime}|\cos\alpha>R_{K}
,
which forces

to be disjoint from
\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})
. In particular,
K
is disjoint from
\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})
.
Let us also observe that since
z\in K\subseteq B(0,R_{K})
we have
|z|<R_{K}
. Bearing these two properties in mind, we may then estimate

\displaystyle|x^{\prime}|\cos\alpha

\displaystyle={\rm dist}\,\big(0,\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\big)\leq{\rm dist}\,\big(z,\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\big)+|z|

\displaystyle<\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})}|y-z|+\Big(\frac{R_{K}}{{\rm dist}\,(z,\partial K)}\Big){\rm dist}\,(z,\partial K)

\displaystyle<\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\setminus K}|y-z|+\Big(\frac{R_{K}}{{\rm dist}\,(z,\partial K)}\Big)\frac{|x^{\prime}|}{C_{\ast}}

\displaystyle=\inf_{y\in\Gamma_{\kappa}(x^{\prime})\setminus K}|y-z|+\frac{|x^{\prime}|\cos\alpha}{2},

(3.103)

which shows that (
3.100
) also holds in this case. At this stage, the proof of (
3.100
) is complete,
so (
3.97
) has been justified. As noted earlier, having justified (
3.97
) concludes the proof of
(
1.31
) when
n\geq 3
.

A proof of (
1.31
) which works for
n\geq 2
goes as follows. Fix an exponent
\eta\in(0,1)
along with
a point
y=(y^{\prime},y_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
and note that for each
x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}
we may use (
2.27
)
to estimate

\displaystyle x_{n}^{1-\eta}|(\nabla E^{L})(x-\overline{y})|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}^{1-\eta}}{\big[|x^{\prime}-y^{\prime}|^{2}+(x_{n}+y_{n})^{2}\big]^{(n-1)/2}}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{Cx_{n}^{1-\eta}}{(x_{n}+y_{n})^{n-1}}\leq\frac{C}{y_{n}^{n-2+\eta}}.

(3.104)

As such,

\sup_{x=(x^{\prime},x_{n})\in{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\Big[x_{n}^{1-\eta}|(\nabla E^{L})(x-\overline{y})|\Big]\leq\frac{C}{y_{n}^{n-2+\eta}}<+\infty.

(3.105)

In addition,

(3.106)

From (
3.106

(3.107)

where
\dot{\mathscr{C}}^{\eta}\big(\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}\big)
denotes
the homogeneous Hölder space of order
\eta
in
\overline{{\mathbb{R}}^{n}_{+}}
.
In particular, if

is as in (
3.92

(3.108)

which readily implies that, with the supremum taken over all cubes

\sup_{Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{n-1}}\ell(Q)^{-\eta}\Big(\fint_{Q}\big|f(x^{\prime})-f_{Q}\big|^{2}\,dx^{\prime}\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}<+\infty,

(3.109)

where

From (

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C\ell(Q)^{-\eta}}{y_{n}^{n-2+\eta}}\Big(\int_{0}^{\ell(Q)}\frac{1}{t^{2(1-\eta)}}t\,dt\Big)^{\frac{1}{2}}=\frac{C}{y_{n}^{n-2+\eta}},

Granted (

3.92

3.109
), and (

we conclude from the well-posedness result established in

that the functions

and

. In view of this and (

The proof of Theorem
