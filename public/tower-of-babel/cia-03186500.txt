*Vietnam:

South Vietnam: Hostilities have resumed through-
out the country following the Christmas cease-fire
period.

A day-long battle took place in Phu Yen Province
on 27 December. The fighting followed an airborne
assault by US forces on suspected Communist posi-
tions north of the provincial capital at Tuy Hoa. The
enemy troops, estimated to total two companies, were
in well dug-in positions but were forced to withdraw
after nine hours of extensive air and artillery bombard-
ments.

Two other clashes occurred in Binh Duong Prov-
ince on 27 December. Elements of the North Vietnam-
ese 7th Division launched a mortar attack on a US:base
camp at Dau Tieng. A barrage of some 180 rounds of
mortar fire was directed at the camp and was accom-
panied by small-arms fire. A two-hour skirmish also
took place in the central part of the province when US
troops engaged an undetermined-size Communist force
while searching an enemy bunker complex.

North Vietnam: Hanoi continues to construct al-
ternate facilities for crossing the Red River near the
recently bombed Paul Doumer Bridge. Recent photog-
raphy shows that two causeway-pontoon bridges are
operational and a third is under construction. These
bridges are believed capable of supporting a moderate
level of truck traffic. They should be serviceable for
several months, until they are washed out when the

Red River returns to a higher level in about June. There 3.5(c)
are 12 other alternate crossings within five miles of —___

southern Laos, but there is still no indication that a
major Communist offensive is under way. Govern-
ment units recaptured Muong Phalane on 27 Decem-
ber, but the Communists are continuing to maintain
pressure on government forces in the Bolovens Pla-
teau area. Enemy forces have harassed a govern-
ment position defending an access road into Saravane,
but there does not appear to be any immediate threat
against the town. So far the Communists appear to 3.5(c)
