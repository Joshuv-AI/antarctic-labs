Observation of centrality-dependent dijet transverse momentum imbalance in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions at s NN \sqrt{s_{{}_{\text{NN}}}} = 5.36 TeV with the ATLAS detector


Abstract
The ATLAS experiment presents an observation of a centrality-dependent dijet transverse momentum imbalance in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions at a nucleon–nucleon center-of-mass energy of 5.36 TeV at the Large Hadron Collider. The measurement uses 8.0 nb -1 of O+O and 1.0 nb -1 of Ne+Ne data collected in 2025, together with 386 pb -1 of pp data at the same energy used as a reference. The dijet momentum balance is quantified using the ratio of the sub-leading jet transverse momentum to that of the leading jet, x J x_{J} . For dijets produced azimuthally back-to-back, the self-normalized x J x_{J} distributions exhibit increasingly large deviations from the pp reference as collisions become more central, corresponding to an increasing overlap of the colliding nuclei. The observed centrality dependence is consistent with medium-induced partonic energy loss in O+O and Ne+Ne collisions, demonstrating that such effects persist in collision systems considerably smaller than Pb+Pb and Xe+Xe. These results establish a new regime for investigating the path-length dependence of jet quenching and constrain the onset of quark–gluon plasma effects in small nuclear collision systems.

[

[

,

,

,

,

Evidence for path-length-dependent energy loss has been obtained in non-central Pb+Pb collisions through azimuthal modulations of high-

[

[

Collisions of light ions provide a new opportunity to investigate the path-length dependence of jet quenching in systems with a substantially smaller transverse extent than Pb
+

[

Recent measurements

Complementary measurements of hard probes have also been used to search for signatures of medium-induced effects in light-ion collisions.
The CMS Collaboration measured the charged-hadron nuclear modification factor in centrality-integrated O

In Quantum Chromodynamics (QCD), jet production proceeds through the fragmentation of quarks and gluons from a short-distance scattering process.
At leading order, pairs of jets (dijets) are produced approximately balanced in the plane transverse to the beam (back-to-back).
Dijet observables are sensitive to path-length-dependent energy loss, since the two jets may traverse different distances through the medium

Additionally, they are less sensitive than jet yields to centrality-bias effects
[

[
34

[

The recorded O

Because the event activity from multiple collisions is superimposed, the centrality of such events cannot be determined reliably. These events are therefore removed from the analysis.

Additional beam-induced background is suppressed by removing events with track multiplicities significantly smaller than expected relative to

Collisions affected by pileup are not rejected in

The measured
(\mbox{$p_{\text{T}_{\text{1}}}$},\mbox{$p_{\text{T}_{\text{2}}}$})

A symmetrized

The presence of uncorrelated jets also introduces an inefficiency for genuine dijets when one jet in the pair is replaced by an uncorrelated third jet.
This effect is corrected using the measured inclusive jet spectrum in minimum-bias events following the procedure of Ref.
[

The combination of the sideband subtraction and the correction for the uncorrelated jet inefficiency results in a net correction of at most 3.2% to the final

Contributions from three-jet topologies to the sideband region are found to be less than 0.5% in simulation and neglected.
Potential contributions from the azimuthal anisotropy of particle production (”harmonic flow”), which can induce a modulation of the uncorrelated dijet background as a function of

Systematic uncertainties in the measured
x_{\text{J}}
distributions arise from the JES, JER, combinatorial background subtraction, and unfolding procedure.
The JES uncertainty includes contributions from
in situ
calibration, cross-calibration, flavor response, flavor composition, and, for O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions, possible modifications of the calorimetric response due to medium-modified parton showers
[
39
,
62
]

in situ

pp

[
73

The use of Monte Carlo samples with data overlay avoids the need to assign an additional centrality-dependent uncertainty to account for residual differences between underlying-event fluctuations in data and simulation. The resulting JER variations are symmetrized. Their impact is evaluated by repeating the full analysis with modified response matrices and comparing the resulting self-normalized
x_{\text{J}}
distributions with the nominal result. An uncertainty in the combinatorial background subtraction is evaluated by varying the
\Delta\phi
window that defines the sideband region. The nominal window is varied (
1.1<\Delta\phi<1.4
) and the full analysis is repeated. The resulting difference is symmetrized and taken as a systematic uncertainty.

Additional systematic uncertainties arise from the unfolding procedure. The sensitivity to the Bayesian prior is evaluated by reweighting the prior to approximately reproduce the reconstructed distributions in data and repeating the unfolding. The difference from the nominal unfolded result is symmetrized and assigned as a systematic uncertainty. An additional systematic uncertainty is assigned to account for the closure after experimental effects were re-applied to the unfolded result by means of the response matrix. The closure is consistent with unity within statistical uncertainties, which are mostly below 3% in
pp
collisions and increase in O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions, particularly at low
x_{\text{J}}
where they can reach 15–20%. Any deviations from unity are conservatively taken as an additional systematic uncertainty.

Generally, the JES and the JER dominate the total systematic uncertainties. For most bins of

the total systematic uncertainty is below 10–15%, only reaching higher values in the lowest bins of
x_{\text{J}}
. At low

the measurement is dominated by systematic uncertainties, while at high

This Letter presented measurements of the dijet momentum balance,
x_{\text{J}}
, in O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions at
\sqrt{s_{\mathrm{NN}}}=5.36

TeV
for five centrality intervals, compared with
pp
collisions at the same energy.
The measurement is performed for leading jets between 63 and 251
GeV
,
with dijet pairs selected with
\Delta\phi>7\pi/8
,
and both jets having
|y|<2.1
.
The
x_{\text{J}}
distributions are unfolded for detector effects, and the distributions
are normalized by the yield of dijets in each leading jet selection.
An increase in the relative contribution of lower
x_{\text{J}}
dijets in 0–10% central O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions compared with both
pp
and peripheral collisions for each light ion system is observed over the range of leading jet
p_{\text{T}}
reported in this measurement.
The significance of the modification is quantified by comparing the ratios of the
x_{\text{J}}
distributions in O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions to those in
pp
collisions with the null hypothesis unity, corresponding to no modification. The modification is largest in the most central collisions and remains statistically significant in O
+
O (Ne
+
Ne) collisions up to the 20–40% (10–20%) centrality interval. In 0–10% central O
+
O (Ne
+
Ne) collisions, significant deviations from the no-modification hypothesis are observed up to
p_{\text{T}_{\text{1}}}
141 (126) GeV.

The observed modifications of the
x_{\text{J}}
distributions are qualitatively similar to those previously measured in nucleus–nucleus collisions
[
14
,
40
]
, while occurring in a regime where projectile-configuration effects
[
32
,
31
]
and event-activity selection biases
[
28
]
are explicitly constrained.
These measurements provide new constraints on the onset and path-length dependence of jet quenching in small collision systems, extending the study of partonic energy loss toward the smallest quark–gluon plasma droplets accessible at the LHC.

Appendix B: Significance of the modification of the

distributions

The significance of the modification of the
x_{\text{J}}
distributions in O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne collisions relative to
pp

\chi^{2}
test of the ratio to the null hypothesis of no modification (unity). The test statistic is defined as

\chi^{2}=\left(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}\right)^{\mathrm{T}}\mathbf{V}^{-1}\left(\mathbf{R}-\mathbf{I}\right),

(3)

where
\mathbf{R}
is the ratio of the
x_{\text{J}}
distributions,
\mathbf{I}
is the unity vector, and
\mathbf{V}
is the total covariance matrix including statistical and systematic bin-to-bin correlations after self-normalization. For asymmetric uncertainties, the covariance matrix corresponding to the uncertainty variation that yields the smallest deviation from unity is used.

The p-value obtained from the
\chi^{2}
test is converted to a Z-score significance. The significance values for all O
+
O and Ne
+
Ne centrality intervals are reported in Tables
1
and
2
. In both systems, significances above
5\sigma
are observed in central collisions for leading-jet
p_{\text{T}}
below 100
GeV
. Significant modifications persist up to the 20–40% centrality interval in O+O and the 10–20% centrality interval in Ne
+
Ne, while the significance decreases toward more peripheral collisions and higher leading-jet
p_{\text{T}}
.
