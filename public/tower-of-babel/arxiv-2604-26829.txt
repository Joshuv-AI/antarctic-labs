Full Definability in a Profunctorial Model
Takeshi Tsukada, Kazuyuki Asada, Kengo Hirata

Abstract
. A semantic model enjoys full definability if every semantic element in the model is a denotation of some proof or program. Full definability indicates that the model captures programs and proofs in a highly detailed manner. This paper studies full definability in a model based on the (bi)category of profunctors on groupoids, which is a proof-relevant variant of the relational model. Despite the fact that a profunctor is far more complicated than a relation, we show that a rather straightforward application of the ideas for the relational model, together with the notion of stability in profunctors, provides a complete characterisation of definable profunctors. More precisely, all logical families of stable and total profunctors are definable by proof-nets of multiplicative linear logic with MIX. As a part of the full definability proof, we show that the stability serves as a correctness criterion, which we think is of independent interest.



1.
Introduction

The relational model based on the category of sets and relations is one of the most fundamental models of linear logic
(
19
)
.
It is both concise and conceptually rich, and it connects naturally to a wide range of other notions, including intersection types
(
8
)
and a game model
(
22
)
.
The relational model itself forms a compact closed category, so the tensor product
\otimes
and par
\invamp
collapse in the relational model.
However, by equipping sets with additional structure and restricting to relations that respect that structure, one can obtain many important models that are not necessarily compact closed.
Examples of such models include coherence spaces
(
19
)
, hypercoherences
(
13
)
, finiteness spaces
(
12
)
, and totality spaces
(
25
)
.

The (bi)category of profunctors can be seen as a categorification of the category of relations.
While a relation records only whether two elements are related or not, a profunctor carries richer information: it can encode how many ways two objects may be related, and even some relationships among those ways.
Owing to this additional structure, profunctor-based models are deeply connected to several important themes, including the Taylor expansion
(
32
;
33
)
(in the sense of Danos and Ehrhard
(
6
)
), type derivations in intersection type systems
(
27
)
, and game semantics
(
4
;
5
)
.
For this reason, the (bi)category of profunctors has attracted considerable attention in recent years
(
14
;
32
;
33
;
27
;
4
;
17
)
.

It is known that profunctors arising as interpretations of linear-logic proofs and of
\lambda
-terms enjoy special properties.
For instance, one can associate a matrix to a profunctor; although this passage is not functorial for arbitrary profunctors, it behaves as a functor when restricted to profunctors that are denotations of
\lambda
-terms
(
33
;
4
)
.
It is therefore natural to ask what intrinsic properties characterize profunctors that are representable by proofs or
\lambda
-terms.

This paper addresses this question and, as an answer, presents a fully definable model based on profunctors.

A model is said to be
fully definable
(or
fully complete
) if every element of the model arises as the denotation of some proof or
\lambda
-term.
A number of fully definable models have been constructed.
For example, Loader
(
25
)
proposed the
totality space model
, which is based on the relational model and fully definable for
\mathsf{MLL}+\mathsf{Mix}
;
Devarajan et al.
(
9
)
shows that a model based on Chu spaces is fully definable for
\mathsf{MLL}
(without
\mathsf{Mix}
);
and fully definable game models have been developed for a wide range of programming languages and proof systems (see, e.g.,
(
2
;
1
;
21
;
26
)
).

Our construction is based on two key ideas in the literature.
The first is Loader’s
totality

(
25
)
, which was employed to obtain a fully definable model built on the relational model.
Since profunctors generalize relations, totality also plays a central role in our study of profunctors.
The second idea is the concept of
stable profunctor
, introduced by Taylor
(
31
)
and further developed by Fiore et al.
(
16
;
17
;
15
)
.
The concept of stable profunctor was motivated by Berry’s stable domain theory
(
3
)
and Girard’s normal functor
(
20
)
and proposed as a categorification of the stable domain model.
Fortunately, these two ideas can both be understood as instances of
orthogonality
in the sense of Hyland and Schalk
(
23
)
.
We propose
stably total orthogonality
, which combines these two notions, and construct a fully definable model by using this orthogonality.
Given that profunctors are significantly more intricate than relations, it is perhaps surprising that such a simple construction yields a fully definable model.

In contrast to the construction of the model, the proof of full definability is not straightforward.
Furthermore, we obtain some observations that may be of independent interest.

First, we prove that the stability of profunctors yields a correctness criterion (
theorem

11
).
This result can be understood as the profunctorial analogue of Retoré’s result
(
29
)
that coherence of the relational interpretation provides a correctness criterion.
Retoré’s proof and ours share the same high level idea of the proof, which suggests a close relationship between coherence and stability; however, at present we do not have a precise correspondence between them in any formal sense.

Second, we observe that strict factorization systems naturally arise in the stably total model (
proposition

4
).
Strict factorization systems on groupoids have previously been used for combinatorial analyses of profunctor models and related semantics
(
33
;
27
;
4
;
5
)
.
The appearance of strict factorization systems in this paper seems to have the same origin.
However, the way strict factorization systems arise here differs from earlier work in two respects.
First, in this paper, strict factorization systems emerge from the notions of stability and totality, which at first sight appear unrelated to strict factorization systems.
This provides a new perspective that further justifies the role of strict factorization systems in the study of profunctors.
Second, whereas existing approaches typically equip each object with a single strict factorization system, in this paper we associate multiple strict factorization systems with each object.
Moreover, part of the information of
\mathsf{MLL}
proofs is also encoded in the choice of which strict factorization system to adopt among these alternatives.

The technical contributions of this paper can be summarized as follows.

•

We construct a new model based on profunctors that is fully definable with respect to
\mathsf{MLL}+\mathsf{Mix}
. Our result shows that definable profunctors are characterized by using
totality
and
stability
.

•

We show that stability provides a new semantic correctness criterion for
\mathsf{MLL}+\mathsf{Mix}
.

•

We show that strict factorization systems arise from stability and totality. This provides a new perspective on the relevance of strict factorization systems in the analysis of profunctors.

Preliminaries

This section briefly reviews multiplicative linear logic (MLL) and profunctors on groupoids.
We also discuss the interpretation of MLL proof structure in the (bi)category of profunctors.

2.1.
Profunctors on Groupoids

A
(small) groupoid

\mathbb{A}
is a (small) category in which every morphism is invertible.
Then
\mathbb{A}(a,a)
is a group for every object
a\in\mathbb{A}
.
We write
\mathbb{A},\mathbb{B},\mathbb{C}
for groupoids,
a,b,c
for their objects, and
\alpha,\beta,\gamma
for morphisms.
Let
\mathbf{Grpd}
be the category of small groupoids and functors.

Notation 1
.

The composition of morphisms is written in diagrammatic order: the composite of
\alpha\colon a\longrightarrow b
and
\beta\colon b\longrightarrow c
is
(\alpha;\beta)\colon a\longrightarrow c
, which we sometimes simply write as
\alpha\beta
.
We write
I
for the trivial category, which has one object
\star
and one morphism
\mathrm{id}_{\star}
.
For groups
G
and
H
, we write
G\leq H
to mean that
G
is a subgroup of
H
.

For groupoids
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
, a
profunctor

\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
is a functor
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{B}\longrightarrow\mathbf{Set}
.
This definition works for general categories
\mathbb{A}
and
\mathbb{B}
, but this paper focuses on profunctors between groupoids.
For
x\in\mathcal{P}(a,b)
,
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(a^{\prime},a)
and
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b,b^{\prime})
, we write
\alpha\cdot x\cdot\beta
for
\mathcal{P}(\alpha,\beta)(x)\in\mathcal{P}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})
.
When
\alpha=\mathrm{id}
, we simply write
\mathrm{id}\cdot x\cdot\beta
as
x\cdot\beta
, and similarly for
\alpha\cdot x:=\alpha\cdot x\cdot\mathrm{id}
.

Profunctors
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
and
\mathcal{Q}\colon\mathbb{B}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{C}
compose in a manner similar to relations:

(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{Q})(a,c)\quad:=\quad\int^{b\in\mathbb{B}}\mathcal{P}(a,b)\times\mathcal{Q}(b,c).

Here
\int^{b\in\mathbb{B}}
is an operation called a
coend
, which can intuitively be thought of as a kind of existential quantification.
Concretely, the composition is

\textstyle(\mathcal{P};\mathcal{Q})(a,c)\quad:=\quad\left(\coprod_{b\in\mathbb{B}}\mathcal{P}(a,b)\times\mathcal{Q}(b,c)\right)/{\sim},

where
\sim
is an equivalence relation defined by

(x,y)\sim(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})\>:\Longleftrightarrow\>(x^{\prime},y^{\prime})=(x\cdot\beta^{-1},\beta\cdot y)\mbox{ for some $\beta$.}

This relation
\sim
is also given by
(x,\beta\cdot y)\sim(x\cdot\beta,y)
.

Unlike relations, the composition of profunctors is usually not strictly associative, but associative up to isomorphisms.
For this reason, the categorical structure in which morphisms are profunctors is often treated as a
bicategory
.
However, within the scope of this paper, no issues arise from taking quotients of profunctors by isomorphisms.
Thus, we define
\mathbf{Prof}
as the category where objects are groupoids and morphisms are isomorphism classes of profunctors.

This construction has previously been pointed out as raising concerns about size issue. A simple way to justify the quotient here is to assume a Grothendieck universe
U

\mathbf{Prof}_{U}
of the bicategory of

\mathbf{Set}_{U}
-valued profunctors.

This allows us to directly use results in the
1

By abuse of notation, we shall write as if a morphism is a profunctor (instead of an equivalence class of profunctors).

The category of profunctors is a model of linear logic.
The interpretations of logical connectives are given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\otimes\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle\mathbb{A}\invamp\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle:=\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{B}.

The negation is the opposite category
\mathbb{A}^{*}:=\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
.
Since
\otimes
and
\invamp
coincide on
\mathbf{Prof}
, it is a compact closed category.
The unit
\eta_{\mathbb{A}}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}\times\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
is
\eta_{\mathbb{A}}(\star,(a,a^{\prime}))=\mathbb{A}(a^{\prime},a)
and the counit
\epsilon_{\mathbb{A}}\colon\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}I
is
\epsilon_{\mathbb{A}}((a^{\prime},a),\star)=\mathbb{A}(a,a^{\prime})
.

A profunctor induces a relation.
For a groupoid
\mathbb{A}
, let
|{\mathbb{A}}|
be the set of the isomorphism classes of
\mathbb{A}
.
Then a profunctor
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
induces a relation
|{\mathcal{P}}|\subseteq|{\mathbb{A}}|\times|{\mathbb{B}}|
defined by
([a]_{\cong},[b]_{\cong})\in|{\mathcal{P}}|:\Longleftrightarrow\mathcal{P}(a,b)\neq\emptyset

It is easy to see that

Presheaves and profunctors on groupoids have a useful representation as a sum of atomic ones, as discussed in
(
17

We shall heavily use this representation, so we give a concrete definition.

For profunctors
\mathcal{P}_{i}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
,
the profunctor
\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{i}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
is defined by
(\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{i})(a,b):=\coprod_{i\in I}(\mathcal{P}_{i}(a,b))
on objects and
(\coprod_{i\in I}\mathcal{P}_{i})(\alpha,\beta)(\mathsf{in}_{j}(x)):=\mathsf{in}_{j}(\mathcal{P}_{j}(\alpha,\beta)(x))

When

An “atom” with respect to

For a subgroup

\widetilde{G}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(a^{\prime},b^{\prime})

\displaystyle\quad:=\quad\{\,\alpha\cdot G\cdot\beta\mid\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(a^{\prime},a),\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b,b^{\prime})\,\}

\displaystyle\widetilde{G}(\alpha,\beta)

\displaystyle\quad:=\quad X\mapsto(\alpha\cdot X\cdot\beta)

where for

,
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}(a^{\prime},a^{\prime\prime})
and
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b^{\prime},b^{\prime\prime})

(\alpha\cdot X\cdot\beta)\subseteq\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}(a,a^{\prime\prime})\times\mathbb{B}(b,b^{\prime\prime})

(\alpha\cdot X\cdot\beta):=\{(\alpha\alpha_{0},\beta_{0}\beta)\mid(\alpha_{0},\beta_{0})\in X\}
(where the composition
\alpha\alpha_{0}
is taken in
\mathbb{A}
).
This profunctor cannot be decomposed by

Every profunctor can be written as a sum of profunctors of the form
\widetilde{G}
.
Given a profunctor
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}

(x_{i}\in\mathcal{P}(a_{i},b_{i}))_{i\in I}
be a family such that (1) for every

for some

and
\beta
, and (2)
x_{j}=\alpha\cdot x_{i}\cdot\beta
implies

.
Then

\textstyle\mathcal{P}\quad\cong\quad\coprod_{i\in I}\widetilde{\mathsf{fix}(x_{i})}

where
\mathsf{fix}(x)\leq\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}(a,a)\times\mathbb{B}(b,b)
for
x\in\mathcal{P}(a,b)
is the
stabiliser subgroup
defined by

\mathsf{fix}(x)\>:=\>\{\,(\alpha,\beta)\in\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}(a,a)\times\mathbb{B}(b,b)\mid\alpha\cdot x\cdot\beta=x\,\}.

Proposition 2
.

A profunctor
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
can be expressed as

\textstyle\mathcal{P}\>\cong\>\coprod_{i\in I}\widetilde{G_{i}},\qquad\forall i\in I.~G_{i}\leq\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}(a_{i},a_{i})\times\mathbb{B}(b_{i},b_{i})

for some family

.
∎

There is some flexibility in the choice of the family
(x_{i})_{i\in I}
,
and correspondingly, there is also flexibility in the choice of
(G_{i})_{i\in I}
.
For subgroups

to

H=\{\,(\alpha^{-1}g\alpha,\,\beta h\beta^{-1})\mid(g,h)\in G\,\}

and

We write

Proposition 3
.

Let

Then
\widetilde{G}\cong\widetilde{H}
if and only if

is conjugate to

∎

2.3.
Interpretation of Proof Structures

It is well-known that an
\mathsf{MLL}
proof can be interpreted in any
*
-autonomous category, so a proof-net has an interpretation in any
*
-autonomous category.
However, interpreting a possibly incorrect proof structure needs an additional structure.
A sufficient structure to interpret a proof structure is a compact closed category, which is a
*
-autonomous category with
A\invamp B\cong A\otimes B
, so it admits

\dfrac{\vdash\Gamma,A,B}{\vdash\Gamma,A\otimes B}(\otimes{=}\invamp).

In the (wrong) proof system
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}+(\otimes{=}\invamp)
, every proof structure has a corresponding (possibly wrong) proof: Given a proof structure, prepare an instance of the axiom rule for each axiom link, take their MIX, and introduce logical connectives by using
(\invamp)
,
(\otimes{=}\invamp)
and
(\mathsf{Ex})
.
By the coherence of compact closed categories, the interpretation of a proof in
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}+(\otimes{=}\invamp)
is determined by its proof structure.
Since
\mathbf{Prof}
is compact closed, a proof structure can be interpreted in
\mathbf{Prof}
.

The interpretation of a formula is straightforward: it is obtained by replacing
\otimes
and
\invamp
with
\times
and
({-})^{\bot}
with
({-})^{\mathrm{op}}
.
For example, the interpretation of
((X_{1}\invamp X_{2})\otimes X_{2}^{\bot})\invamp((X_{2}\invamp X_{2}^{\bot})\invamp X_{1}^{\bot})
is

((\mathbb{A}_{1}\times\mathbb{A}_{2})\times\mathbb{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\times((\mathbb{A}_{2}\times\mathbb{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\times\mathbb{A}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}})

when the interpretation of
X_{i}
is
\mathbb{A}_{i}
.
The interpretation of a sequent is similar.

The interpretation of a proof structure is slightly more involved but still we have two convenient ways to describe it.

The first one comes from the above description of the “proof” corresponding to a proof structure: it is the mix of axiom rules followed by exchange rules and introductions of logical connectives.
The axiom
\vdash X^{\bot},X
is interpreted as the unit of the compact structure, which is
\mathbb{A}({-},{+})\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{A}
in the case of
\mathbf{Prof}
(where
\mathbb{A}
is the interpretation of the propositional variable
\alpha
).
So the mix
\vdash X_{\xi_{1}}^{\bot},X_{\xi_{1}},\dots,X_{\xi_{n}}^{\bot},X_{\xi_{n}}
of axiom rules gives a profunctor
\mathcal{P}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{1}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{1}}\times\dots\times\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{n}}^{\mathrm{op}}\times\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{n}}

\mathcal{P}(\star,(c_{1},b_{1},\dots,c_{n},b_{n})):=\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{1}}(c_{1},b_{1})\times\dots\times\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{n}}(c_{n},b_{n})
.
The interpretations of the exchange rule and the rules for logical connectives are just the symmetry and associativity laws of the tensor product
\times
.
Hence, the interpretation of a proof structure is
\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{1}}({-},{+})\times\dots\times\mathbb{A}_{\xi_{n}}({-},{+})
followed by the symmetry and associativity.

Consider the proof structure in

, and we have the canonical isomorphisms

A more detailed description of the interpretation of the proof structure is obtained by analysing the isomorphism

((\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{Y})\times\mathbb{Z})\times((\mathbb{U}\times\mathbb{V})\times\mathbb{W})\quad\cong\quad\mathbb{W}\times\mathbb{X}\times\mathbb{V}\times\mathbb{Y}\times\mathbb{Z}\times\mathbb{U}

This isomorphism in

\mathbf{Grpd}

In general, a functor

\mathcal{F}_{*}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}

and
\mathcal{F}^{*}(b,a):=\mathbb{B}(b,F(a))
.
The symmetry and associativity laws in
\mathbf{Prof}
come from functors, so does the above isomorphism.
So, the interpretation
\chi\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}((\mathbb{A}_{1}\times\mathbb{A}_{2})\times\mathbb{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\times((\mathbb{A}_{2}\times\mathbb{A}_{2}^{\mathrm{op}})\times\mathbb{A}_{1}^{\mathrm{op}})

\chi(\star,(((x,y),z),((u,v),w)))\cong\mathbb{A}_{1}(w,x)\times\mathbb{A}_{2}(v,y)\times\mathbb{A}_{2}(z,u)
.

The second one is based on annotations on proof structures in the spirit of
experiments

(

.

3.1.
Creed and Kit

Just as morphisms from a singleton set play a crucial role in the category
\mathbf{Set}
, profunctors from
I
(or to
I
) play an important role in
\mathbf{Prof}
.
A profunctor
\mathcal{P}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}
can be expressed as a sum

\mathcal{P}\cong\coprod_{i}\widetilde{G_{i}}
of (profunctors induced by) subgroups
G_{i}\leq\mathbb{A}(a_{i},a_{i})
.

Creed
proposed by Taylor
(
31
)
and
kit
proposed by Fiore et al.
(
16
;
17
)
are both designed to appropriately constrain these subgroups

.

Definition 1 (Creed
(
31
)
and Kit
(
16
;
17
)
).

Let
\mathcal{P}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}
.
The
creed of
\mathcal{P}
, written
\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P}
, is a family of subsets
(\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P})_{a}\subseteq\mathbb{A}(a,a)
parameterized by objects
a\in\mathbb{A}
defined by

\displaystyle(\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P})_{a}

\displaystyle\quad:=\quad\{\,\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid\exists x\in\mathcal{P}(\star,a).\>x\cdot\alpha=x\,\}

\displaystyle\quad=\quad\textstyle\bigcup_{x\in\mathcal{P}(a)}\mathsf{fix}(x).

The
kit of
\mathcal{P}
, written
\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P}
, is a family of subsets
(\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P})_{a}\subseteq\{G\mid G\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\}
of subgroups of
\mathbb{A}(a,a)
parameterized by objects
a\in\mathbb{A}
defined by

(\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P})_{a}\quad:=\quad\{\,\mathsf{fix}(x)\mid x\in\mathcal{P}(\star,a)\,\}.

Obviously,
(\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P})_{a}=\bigcup(\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P})_{a}
.
The creed and kit of
\mathcal{Q}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}I
is defined similarly.
A family
C=(C_{a})_{a}
of subsets
C_{a}\subseteq\mathbb{A}(a,a)
is a
creed
if
C=(\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P})
for some
\mathcal{P}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}
.
Similarly, a
kit
is a family obtained as
(\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P})
.
∎

Note that
\mathfrak{C}\mathcal{P}
and
\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P}
are not defined (at least at this moment) for general profunctors
\mathcal{P}\colon\mathbb{A}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}
(with
\mathbb{A}\neq I\neq\mathbb{B}
).
The definitions given above differ from the original one, but ours are equivalent to theirs as expected (
proposition

2
).

Proposition 2
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a groupoid.

(1)

A family
C=(C_{a}\subseteq\mathbb{A}(a,a))_{a}
is a creed if and only if

(a)

\alpha\in C_{a}
implies
\alpha^{k}\in C_{a}
for every
k\in\mathbb{Z}
, and

(b)

\alpha\in C_{a}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{A}(a,b)
implies
\beta^{-1};\alpha;\beta\in C_{b}
.

(2)

A family
K=(K_{a}\subseteq\{G\mid G\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\})_{a}
is a kit if and only if
K
is closed under the conjugation in the sense that, if
G\in K_{a}
and
\beta\in\mathbb{A}(a,b)
, then
\beta^{-1}\cdot G\cdot\beta\in K_{b}
, where
\beta^{-1}\cdot G\cdot\beta=\{\beta^{-1};\alpha;\beta\mid\alpha\in G\}
.
∎

Proposition 3
.

Let
\mathbb{A}
be a groupoid,
a\in\mathbb{A}
and
G\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)
.
Then
(\mathfrak{C}{\widetilde{G}})_{b}=\{\,\beta^{-1}\cdot\alpha\cdot\beta\mid\alpha\in G,\beta\in\mathbb{A}(a,b)\,\}
and
(\mathfrak{K}{\widetilde{G}})_{b}=\{\,\beta^{-1}\cdot G\cdot\beta\mid\beta\in\mathbb{A}(a,b)\,\}
.

Definition 4.

For a profunctor

on

\mathcal{P}\models\mathcal{A}
if
(\mathfrak{K}\mathcal{P})_{a}\subseteq\mathcal{A}_{a}
for every

.
∎

Proposition 5
.

Let
\mathcal{P}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{A}^{\mathrm{op}}
and assume
\mathcal{P}\cong\coprod_{i}\widetilde{G_{i}}

Let

be a creed and

\mathbb{A}

•

for every

.

•

\mathcal{P}\models K

for every

.

Definition 6.

Let

\displaystyle(C^{\bot})_{a}

\displaystyle=\{\alpha\in\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid\forall k\in\mathbb{Z}.\alpha^{k}\notin(C_{a}\setminus\{\mathrm{id}\})\}

\displaystyle(K^{\bot})_{a}

\displaystyle=\{G^{\prime}\leq\mathbb{A}(a,a)\mid\forall G\in K_{a}.G\bot G^{\prime}\}.

Let

3.2.
Creed-Kit Criterion

We characterize the correctness of a proof structure
\pi\vdash\Gamma
in terms of creed and kit.
The interpretation of a proof structure
\pi
is a profunctor
\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\llbracket{\Gamma}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
parameterized by
\vec{\mathbb{A}}=(\mathbb{A}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{A}_{N})
.
For each
\mathbb{A}_{i}
, let
\mathcal{A}_{i}
be a Boolean creed on
\mathbb{A}_{i}
and
\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{N})
.
Then
\llbracket{\Gamma}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathcal{A}}}
is a creed on
\llbracket{\Gamma}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
, so the stability of
\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{A}}
with respect to
\llbracket{\Gamma}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathcal{A}}}
makes sense.

Definition 10 (Creed-Kit criterion).

A cut-free proof structure
\pi
satisfies the
creed-kit criterion
if

\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\mathcal{U}(\vec{\mathcal{A}})}\models\llbracket{\Gamma}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathcal{A}}}
for every assignment
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
of Boolean creeds to propositional variables.
∎

The main result of this section is as follows.

Theorem 11
.

A cut-free proof structure is correct with respect to
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}
if and only if it satisfies the creed-kit criterion.
∎

The left-to-right direction is trivial since
\mathbf{SProf}
is a model of
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}

(
17
)
.
The remainder of this section is devoted to proving the converse direction.

Remark 12
.

The cut-freeness assumption is essential to
theorem

11
.
There exists an incorrect proof structure with
(\mathsf{Cut})
of which all sources of its incorrectness are eliminated by the cut elimination procedure.
An example of such a proof structure (written as an
\mathsf{MLL}+\mathsf{Mix}+(\otimes{=}\invamp)
proof) is

\pi_{0}:=\quad\vdash X,X^{\bot}\lx@proof@logical@and\vdash X,X^{\bot},X^{\bot}\invamp X\vdash X,X^{\bot}\invamp X,X^{\bot}\vdash X,X^{\bot},X,X^{\bot}\lx@proof@logical@and\vdash X,X^{\bot}\vdash X,X^{\bot}\vdash X\otimes X^{\bot}\vdash X,X^{\bot}

to which the cut-elimination procedure gives
\pi_{1}:=\>\vdash X,X^{\bot}
.
Since the semantic interpretation is an invariant of the cut-elimination procedure, any semantic criterion cannot distinguish between the correct proof
\pi_{1}
from the incorrect proof
\pi_{0}
.
∎

.
For each representative

with
\mathcal{P}(\star,a)\neq\emptyset

(x_{a},y_{a})\in\mathcal{P}(\star,a)\times\mathcal{Q}(\star,a)
such that
\mathsf{fix}(x_{a})=\mathsf{fix}(y_{a})
as follows.
Let
a^{\prime}
,
x
and

be those in the third condition.
By the second condition, there is a cototal profunctor

such that

\mathcal{P}\Bot C
.
We also have
\mathcal{Q}\Bot C

\mathcal{Q}
is stably total.
Take any

.
Since
\mathcal{P}\Bot C
, it must be the case that
\mathsf{fix}(x)\Bot\mathsf{fix}(z)
, and similarly
\mathsf{fix}(y)\Bot\mathsf{fix}(z)
.
Hence, the assumption
\mathsf{fix}(x)\subseteq\mathsf{fix}(y)
implies
\mathsf{fix}(x)=\mathsf{fix}(y)
by

6

Take an arbitrary morphism
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b^{\prime},b)
and let
x_{b}:=x\cdot\beta
and
y_{b}:=y\cdot\beta

We give a natural transformation
\psi\colon\mathcal{P}\Rightarrow\mathcal{Q}
as follows.
Let

and

be the representative of the isomorphism class of
b^{\prime}

By

10
, there exists
\beta\in\mathbb{B}(b,b^{\prime})

Then we define
\psi_{\star,b^{\prime}}(x^{\prime}):=(y_{b}\cdot\beta)

The choice of

\mathsf{fix}(x_{b})\subseteq\mathsf{fix}(y_{b})
.
This is natural by construction and injective since

.

We prove that
\psi_{\star,b^{\prime}}
is surjective.
Note that the condition implies the containment

between the associated relations.
By the maximality property for totality spaces (

|{\mathcal{P}}|=|{\mathcal{Q}}|
(note that
|{\mathcal{P}}|
is total by the first condition).
Assume

.
The above argument shows that
\mathcal{P}(\star,b^{\prime})\neq\emptyset

and

are defined where

is the representative of the isomorphism class of

By the single orbit property (

So

∎

5.
Full Definability

Every proof structure induces a family of profunctors parameterized by a groupoid assignment to propositional variables.
This section provides a complete characterization of families definable by
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}
proofs based on the category
\mathbf{STProf}
of stably total profunctors.

5.1.
Logical Characterization of Definability

The interpretation
\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
of a proof
\pi\vdash A
is parameterized by the assignment
\vec{\mathbb{A}}
of groupoids to propositional variables.
The family
(\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}})_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
is expected to exhibit similar structures across different components, and the technical challenge lies in how to capture the relationships between components.
A particular obstacle is the presence of negative occurrences of propositional variables in
A
.
Our approach is to use functors to describe the relationships between components, leveraging the fact that the opposition
({-})^{\mathrm{op}}
, the negation in
\mathbf{Prof}
, is a
covariant
functor.

To accommodate both profunctors and functors within a single framework, we use the
double category of profunctors
.
This paper does not rely on any deep results from double category theory; we merely introduce and use the following notation.
For profunctors
\mathcal{P}_{1}\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}_{1}
and
\mathcal{P}_{2}\colon\mathbb{A}_{2}\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}\mathbb{B}_{2}
and functors
\mathcal{F}\colon\mathbb{A}_{1}\longrightarrow\mathbb{A}_{2}
and
\mathcal{G}\colon\mathbb{B}\longrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{2}
, we write

\textstyle{\mathbb{A}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{F}}
\scriptstyle{|}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{P}_{1}}
\scriptstyle{\Downarrow}
\textstyle{\mathbb{B}_{1}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{G}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{A}_{2}\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|}
\scriptstyle{\mathcal{P}_{2}}
\textstyle{\mathbb{B}_{2}}

to mean that there exists a natural transformation
\mathcal{P}_{1}({-},{+})\Longrightarrow\mathcal{P}_{2}(\mathcal{F}({-}),\mathcal{G}({+}))
.
(Note that the existence of a natural transformation is well-defined on isomorphisms classes of profunctors.)

An
\mathsf{MLL}
formula

with free variables
X_{1},\dots,X_{N}
defines a mapping from sequences
\vec{\mathbb{A}}=(\mathbb{A}_{1},\dots,\mathbb{A}_{n})
to groupoids
\llbracket{A}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
, which we shall simply write as

.
Similarly, it determines a totality groupoid

for each

\vec{\mathcal{A}}=(\mathcal{A}_{1},\dots,\mathcal{A}_{n})
of totality groupoids, and we have
\mathcal{U}(A(\vec{\mathcal{A}}))=A(\mathcal{U}(\vec{\mathcal{A}}))
.

Importantly, it also determines a functor

for each
n
-tuple
\Phi=(\varphi_{1},\dots,\varphi_{n})
of functors
\varphi_{i}\colon\mathbb{A}_{i}\longrightarrow\mathbb{B}_{i}
.
It is simply defined by
(A\otimes B)(\Phi)=(A\invamp B)(\Phi)=A(\Phi)\times B(\Phi)
and
A^{\bot}(\Phi)=(A(\Phi))^{\mathrm{op}}
.

Definition 1 (Logical family of profunctors).

A family
\varpi=(\varpi_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}})_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
of profunctors
\varpi_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}A(\vec{\mathbb{A}})
is

if,
for every

,

\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|}
\scriptstyle{\varpi_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}}
\scriptstyle{\Downarrow}
\textstyle{A(\vec{\mathbb{A}})\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{A(\Phi)}
\textstyle{I\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces\ignorespaces}
\scriptstyle{|}
\scriptstyle{\varpi_{\vec{\mathbb{B}}}}
\textstyle{A(\vec{\mathbb{B}})}

holds.
It is

if every component is stably total, i.e.
\varpi_{\vec{\mathcal{A}}}:=\varpi_{\mathcal{U}(\vec{\mathcal{A}})}\in\mathbf{STProf}(I,A(\vec{\mathcal{A}}))
for every tuple
\vec{\mathcal{A}}
of totality groupoids.

In some cases, we consider a family in which the parameter
\vec{\mathbb{A}}
takes values in (tuples of) a subclass of groupoids, such as groups (i.e. single-object groupoids) or sets (i.e. groupoids in which all morphisms are identities).
The logicality in these cases is defined similarly, considering only
\Phi\colon\vec{\mathbb{A}}\longrightarrow\vec{\mathbb{B}}
between the subclass.
∎

The next theorem is the main result of this paper.
The proof will be presented in the following subsection.

Theorem 2
.

Let

be an MLL formula and
\varpi=(\varpi_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}A(\vec{\mathbb{A}}))_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}
be a family of profunctors.
It is definable (up to natural isomorphism) by an
\mathsf{MLL}+\mathsf{Mix}
proof if and only if it is logical and stably total.
∎

5.2.
Proof of Full Definability

This subsection proves

.
One direction is easy.

Lemma 3

For an
\mathsf{MLL+Mix}

(\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}A(\vec{\mathbb{A}}))_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}

\llbracket{\pi}\rrbracket_{\vec{\mathbb{A}}}

is a model of

The logicality of the family can be easily proved by induction on proofs.

Remark 4

The logicality of the interpretation in the above proof corresponds to the fact that each logical connective is a (double) functor on the double category.
In this sense, the structure of the double category is used indirectly in the paper.
Such a use of double categories subsumes the classical approach based on embedding-projection, but we will not go further into the details here.

We prove the converse.
Let

The latter has already been addressed: since the interpretation

(1b)

We first consider a simple case where

Even this simple case needs a non-trivial result from group theory.
A group isomorphism

We use the following characterization of inner automorphisms.

Theorem 7

(Schupp

)

Let

be a group and
\varphi\colon G\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}G
is a group automorphism.
Then
\varphi
is an inner automorphism if and only if
\varphi
can be extended to an automorphism via any group injection, i.e.,

for every injective group homomorphism
\iota\colon G\hookrightarrow H
, there exists an automorphism
\psi\colon H\stackrel{{\scriptstyle\cong}}{{\longrightarrow}}H
such that
\psi(\iota(g))=\iota(\varphi(g))
for every

.

Lemma 8

Let
\chi=(\chi_{G}\colon I\mathrel{\relbar\joinrel\mapstochar\rightarrow}G^{\mathrm{op}}\times G)_{G}

\displaystyle\chi_{G}\Bot\big(\widetilde{\{\mathrm{id}\}}\times\widetilde{G}\big)\quad\mbox{and}\quad\chi_{G}\Bot\big(\widetilde{G^{\mathrm{op}}}\times\widetilde{\{\mathrm{id}\}}\big)
