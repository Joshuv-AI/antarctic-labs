Artificial intelligence driven multiphysics analysis of Casson bioconvective ternary nanofluid flow over an inclined needle
Authors: Amir Abbas, Soubhagya Kumar Sahoo, Muhammad Farman, Soumyarani Mishra, Aboulbaba Eladeb, Nermeen Abdullah, Walid Hassen, Lioua Kolsi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThis study investigates the bio-convective flow of ternary Casson nanofluid across inclined moving thin needle embedded in a porous medium, considering Arrhenius activation energy and non-uniform heat generation/absorption. Such models are important in biomedical engineering, thermal energy systems, and advanced cooling technologies, where enhanced heat and mass transfer characteristics are required. The governing equations are solved numerically using the bvp4c technique, while Artificial Neural Network (ANN) analysis is employed to evaluate the accuracy and stability of the computational results. The findings reflect that increasing Casson parameter, activation energy parameter, needle thickness, bioconvection Lewis number, Peclet number, and chemical reaction parameter led to reduction in velocity distribution and reverse trend for increasing inclination angle of surface. Results show that rising chemical reaction parameter, activation energy parameter led to rise temperature distribution and opposite behavior for needle thickness. It has been reflected that increasing activation energy parameter caused to increase concentration. Graphical outcomes show that microorganism density rises with the rise of activation energy parameter, bioconvection Lewis number, Peclet number, and inclination angle. Furthermore, the ANN model demonstrates high predictive accuracy with small and uniformly distributed errors, confirming the reliability of the numerical analysis. For the better visibility, three-dimensional graphs of the velocity, temperature and concentration and microorganisms’ density profiles are shown. For compact visualization of the behavior of velocity and temperature, the graphical representation of contours plots are also presented Current results are validated with published results in the literature.Keywords: Ternary nanofluid, Arrhenius, Casson fluid, Inclined needle, Non-uniform heat rise/fall

Introduction

The collective, frequently random movement of motile microorganisms that produces fluid motion and instability due to their asymmetric mass distribution and their responses to factors such as gravity, chemical gradients, or magnetic fields is known as bioconvection. Microorganisms that move against gravity, such as gyrotactic bacteria or algae, can cause hydrodynamic instability and convection patterns in bio-convective systems. Because of these characteristics, bioconvection is a key component of bio microsystems and has potential applications in biotechnology, fuel cells, photobioreactors, enzyme biosensors, and ecological energy systems. Bioconvection has been extensively investigated through theoretical, numerical, and experimental approaches. The phenomenon was illustrated using a floating algae suspension [1], followed by the pioneering theoretical model of Plesset and Winet [2], which incorporated a wide range of motile microorganisms. Subsequently, Kuznetsov [3] developed a computational model demonstrating that cell deposition plays a crucial role in enhancing bioconvection growth. With increasing interest in nanofluid systems, several studies have examined the interaction between motile microorganisms and nanoparticles under various physical conditions. Waqas et al. [4] analyzed bioconvection through electrically conducting nanofluid flow across porous stretching disk, while Khan and Shehzad [5] explored bioconvection in Carreau nanofluid along stretching surface. Effects of radiation and porous media on oxytactic bacterial bioconvection were reported by Balla et al. [6]. Basir with his research fellows [7] considered Peclet number’s influence on nanofluid flow across stretching cylinder. Beyond Newtonian nanofluids, bioconvection studies have been extended to non-Newtonian and complex fluid models. Kuznetsov [8] reported oscillatory bioconvection in shallow nanoparticle layers containing oxytactic microorganisms. Uddin et al. [9] examined bioconvection in non-Newtonian fluids within saturated porous space and later investigated effects of slip and blowing over horizontal surfaces [10]. Rashad and Nabwey [11] analyzed mixed convection nanofluid flow with motile microorganisms under convective boundary conditions. Additional studies addressed variable viscosity effects in rotating bioconvective systems [12], bioconvection in horizontal channels using modified least-squares techniques [13], slip mechanisms induced by gyrotactic microorganisms [14], and the rheological behavior of Oldroyd-B nanofluids over oscillatory stretching surfaces [15]. More recently, Waqas et al. [16] quantitatively examined bioconvection in rate-type (Maxwell) fluid flows using the shooting method.

Recent investigations have incorporated chemical reactions and thermodynamic irreversibility into bioconvection models. Khan et al. [17] analytically considered effect of chemical reactions on the peristaltic bioconvection flow of a third-grade nanofluid. In [18], transport characteristics of Casson hybrid blood flow containing oxytactic bacteria in a stenosed artery were analyzed using Arrhenius activation energy. Furthermore, Mondal et al. [19] investigated hydromagnetic bioconvective nanofluid flow incorporating solar radiation, viscous and Joule dissipation, activation energy, Brownian motion, Hall current, and thermophoresis, and reported that buoyancy enhances velocity while entropy generation increases due to radiative and dissipative effects.

Casson fluid is shear-thinning non-Newtonian fluid, in which shear stress causes its viscosity to drop. This model has established a lot of interest recently in field of Nanofluid research, especially when it comes to modelling biological fluid dynamics like blood flow. Because of its significance in these fields, mathematicians and researchers are focusing on it to expand knowledge across the theoretical and practical sciences.

Nanofluids are formed by combining nanoparticles into base fluids, are the result of the evolution of nanotechnology. Because of their improved thermal properties, these nanofluids have outperformed conventional fluids. Due to their exceptional thermal performance and several potential uses in engineering systems, hybridized ternary nanofluids, made of three kinds of nanoparticles, have caught a lot of attention lately. By utilizing the combined effects of nanoparticles, ternary nanofluids provide important developments in overall thermal transfer and performance. The potential for this technology to transform heat regulation for industrial applications makes it a promising idea in thermal engineering.
