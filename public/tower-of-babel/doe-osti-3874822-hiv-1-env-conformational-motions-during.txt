HIV-1 Env Conformational Motions during Membrane Fusion in complex membrane

Ilona C. Unarta, S. Gnanakaran

HIV-1 Envelope glycoprotein (Env, trimeric gp120/gp41) is the membrane protein that is
responsible for viral entry via membrane fusion. The Env binds to the receptor and coreceptor
proteins, CD4 and CCR5, which induces opening of Env and gp120 shedding leading to
conformational changes of gp41 from the pre-fusion to post-fusion conformations. Currently, we
have modelled the fully open Env (CH505 strain) in complex with CD4 and CCR5 (Fig. 1). To
represent a biologically relevant complex, the transmembrane domain (TM) of gp41 was
embedded in HIV-1 complex membrane and CCR5 and CD4 TM domain was embedded in T-cell
complex membrane. We then performed four independent fully atomistic molecular dynamics
simulations of Env in complex with CCR5 and CD4 for 120 ns each. It has been suggested that
binding to both CD4 and CCR5 opens the Env more compared to binding with CD4 alone. We
compared the tilting angle of gp120 and gp41 in our simulations with available Env structures in
different states. Our simulations show that all three protomers of gp120 and gp41 shifted largely
from the closed structures. Indeed, when in complex with both CD4 and CCR5, gp120 and gp41
shifted more than the structures of Env in complex with just CD4.

(inset) The interaction between V3 loop of gp120 and CCR5 is highlighted and the V1/V2 loops

It has been suggested that once gp120 sheds from gp41, gp41 extends towards the T-cell
membrane and its fusion peptide (FP) form beta-sheet conformations. To confirm the stability of
these conformations, we modelled the beta-sheet conformation of FP alongside HR1 of gp41 in

a complex membrane containing 30% cholesterol (Fig. 2A). We then performed four independent
2 𝜇s MD simulations (Fig. 2B). The beta-sheet conformations are maintained throughout our
simulations. Interestingly, the depth of FP is correlated with its angle with respect to the
membrane. FP inserts deeper into the membrane when it is more perpendicular to the membrane
plane (Fig. 2C).

Figure 2. A) Model of beta-sheet FP and HR1 embedded in complex membrane. B)
conformations of FP in the simulations. C) The position of FP in the membrane is plotted against
its angle with respect to the membrane.

Future Works

In the future, we will perform extensive structure-based models simulations starting from CH505
Env with CD4 and CCR5 bound to the post-fusion conformations to see the effect of gp120 in the
extension and refolding of gp41. Lastly, based on conformations our structure-based models we
will embed TM domain and FP in vesicles to observe the membrane fusion events.
