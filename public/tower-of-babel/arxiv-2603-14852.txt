Path Planning of Surgical Robot for Minimally Invasive Surgery on Riemannian Manifold
Yoshiki Yamamoto, Maina Sogabe, Shunichi Hirahara, Toshiki Kaisaki, Tetsuro Miyazaki, Kenji Kawashima

Abstract
Robotic surgery for minimally invasive surgery can reduce the surgeon’s workload by autonomously guiding robotic forceps. Movement of the robot is restricted around a fixed insertion port. The robot often encounters angle limitations during operation. Also, the surface of the abdominal cavity is non-concave, making it computationally expensive to find the desired path. In this work, to solve these problems, we propose a method for path planning in joint space by transforming the position into a Riemannian manifold. An edge cost function is defined to search for a desired path in the joint space and reduce the range of motion of the joints. We found that the organ is mostly non-concave, making it easy to find the optimal path using gradient descent method. Experimental results demonstrated that the proposed method reduces the range of joint angle movement compared to calculations in position space.

Joint space obstacle mapping

Riemann metric

Surgical robot arms with five independent joints constrained by a fixed trocar port, whose configuration is represented by the vector
q_{5d}\in Q\subset\mathbb{R}^{5}
. Through intraoperative imaging and segmentation, we obtain a forbidden workspace region
X_{a}\subset\mathbb{R}^{3}
that the tool tip must avoid. Our goal is to find a continuous path

\gamma:[0,1]\to Q

such that the path minimizes the Riemannian geodesic length

L(\gamma)=\int_{0}^{1}\sqrt{\dot{\gamma}(t)^{\mathsf{T}}\,G\bigl(\gamma(t)\bigr)\,\dot{\gamma}(t)}\,\mathrm{d}t

(1)

where
G(\gamma(t))
is a composite Riemman metric, while ensuring that at all times the forward-kinematics map
K(q_{5D}(t))
remains outside the forbidden region, i.e.

\forall t\in[0,1],\quad K\bigl(\gamma(t)\bigr)\notin X_{a}.

Here,
K:Q\to\mathbb{R}^{3}
computes the end-effector position from joint angles.

Collision region in joint space

4

The forbidden joint configurations lie on the boundary of the collision region in joint space, denoted
\partial Q_{a}\subset Q
. We characterize this boundary by the set of all
\bm{q}
for which there exists a direction
(\theta,\phi)
in the half-sphere
[\pi/2,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)
such that the
end-effector position satisfies

x(\bm{q};p)=p+r(\theta,\phi)\,e_{\theta,\phi},

where
p
is the trocar port location,
r(\theta,\phi)
is the segmented radial distance to the first collision along the unit vector. Here,

e_{\theta,\phi}=\bigl(\sin\theta\cos\phi,\,\sin\theta\sin\phi,\,\cos\theta).

Then, the collision region in joint space is given as

\partial Q_{a}=\Bigl\{\bm{q}\in Q\mid\exists\,(\theta,\phi):x(\bm{q};p)=p+r(\theta,\phi)e_{\theta,\phi}\Bigr\}.

We approximate the continuous boundary
\partial Q_{a}
by sampling a grid of directions
(\theta_{i},\phi_{i})
uniformly over
[\pi/2,\pi]\times[0,2\pi)
and, for each sample, computing the corresponding Cartesian point
x_{i}=p+r(\theta_{i},\phi_{i})e_{\theta_{i},\phi_{i}}
. We then solve the inverse kinematics
q_{i}=K^{-1}(x_{i})
to obtain discrete boundary configurations in
Q
. Finally, applying Delaunay triangulation to the point set
\{q_{i}\}
yields a piecewise-linear mesh that approximates
\partial Q_{a}
without explicit enumeration environments.

Joint Space Path Planning

We define the total metric

G(\bm{q})=G_{\mathrm{q}}(\bm{q})+G_{\mathrm{obs}}(\bm{q}),

where the kinematic term
G_{\mathrm{q}}(\bm{q})
is given in Eq.(
3
).
The obstacle term

G_{\mathrm{obs}}(q)=\frac{\sigma}{\|\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{a}\|}\,I,

with
\bm{q_{a}}=\arg\min_{q^{\prime}\in Q_{a}}\|\bm{q}-\bm{q}^{\prime}\|
, enforces an infinite cost barrier as the configuration approaches the forbidden boundary
\partial Q_{a}
.
It is known that the shortest path
\bm{q}(t)
in space containing the metric
G(\bm{q})
is generally a solution of Lagrange’s equation of motion for the following Lagrangian.

L(\bm{q},\bm{\dot{q}})=\frac{1}{2}\bm{\dot{q}}^{T}G(\bm{q})\bm{\dot{q}},\hskip 8.5359pt\frac{\partial L}{\partial\bm{q}}-\frac{d}{dt}\frac{\partial L}{\partial\bm{\dot{q}}}=0

Finally, the following equation is derived (see Appendix A).

G(\bm{q})\ddot{\bm{q}}+\left(\nabla f\dot{\bm{q}}^{T}\nabla^{2}f+\nabla g\dot{\bm{q}}^{T}\nabla^{2}g\right)\dot{\bm{q}}-\frac{\sigma_{q}}{\left\|\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{a}\right\|^{3}}\left(\dot{\bm{q}}\dot{\bm{q}}^{T}-\frac{1}{2}\left\|\dot{\bm{q}}\right\|^{2}I\right)\left(I-\frac{\partial\bm{q}_{a}}{\partial\bm{q}}\right)^{T}(\bm{q}-\bm{q}_{a})=0

(4)

The shortest path in the join space from
\bm{q}_{s}
to
\bm{q}_{t}
at time
T
is given by solving Eq.(
Appendix A
) with the boundary conditions
\bm{q}(0)=\bm{q}_{s}
and

\bm{q}(T)=\bm{q}_{t}
.

Proposed edge cost function

If a convex representation in the joint space is guaranteed, gradient methods achieve fast convergence to the optimal solution.
Gaussian curvature
K
and mean curvature
H
of a surface implicitly defined by
F(x,y,z)=0
in joint space are given as follows:

K=-\frac{\rm{det}\bigg(\begin{matrix}J^{T}H(F)J+H_{o}&J^{T}(\nabla F)\\
(\nabla F)^{T}J&0\\
\end{matrix}\bigg)}{J^{T}|\nabla F|^{4}},H=\frac{1}{2|J^{T}\nabla F|^{3}}|\nabla F|^{T}J[\rm{trace}\it(J^{T}H(F)J-\nabla F+H_{o})I)^{T}-(J^{T}H(f)J+H_{o})]J^{T}\nabla F.

(5)

J=\frac{\partial(x,y,z)}{\partial(q_{1},q_{2},q_{3})},(H_{o})_{i,j}=\frac{\partial F}{\partial x}\frac{\partial^{2}x}{\partial q_{i}\partial q_{j}}+\frac{\partial F}{\partial F}\frac{\partial^{2}y}{\partial q_{i}\partial q_{j}}\frac{\partial F}{\partial F}\frac{\partial^{2}z}{\partial q_{i}\partial q_{j}}

The surface is locally non-concave at that point when
K\geq
0 and
H\geq
0.
Referring to MRI images, the surfaces of organs can be written as a combination of convex surfaces, while
the walls of body cavities are concave
[
25
]
.

First, we identify areas where the intracavity shape is represented by a combination of non-concave surfaces within the joint space (
K\geq 0
and
H\geq 0
).
Next, we search the path on non-concave surfaces.
Then, we adopt a sampling-based approximation,
since the analytic solution of the boundary-value geodesic problem shown in Eq.(
Appendix A
) is intractable under our highly nonlinear metric.
We begin by randomly sampling a finite set of collision-free configurations
V_{q}\subset Q
, always including the start
\bm{q}_{s}
and the goal
\bm{q}_{t}
. Constructing the Delaunay triangulation gives a sparse graph
(V_{q},E_{q})
whose edges connect nearby samples.
Each edge
(\bm{q}_{\alpha},\bm{q}_{\beta})\in E_{q}
is weighted by an approximation of the path length under
G(\bm{q})
plus the barrier penalty. Denoting the midpoint
\bm{\bar{q}}=(\bm{q}_{\alpha}+\bm{q}_{\beta})/2
and the closest forbidden configuration
\bm{q}_{a}(\bar{q})
, the edge cost function is proposed and given by

{c(\bm{q}_{\alpha},\bm{q}_{\beta})=}\sqrt{(\bm{q}_{\beta}-\bm{q}_{\alpha})^{\mathsf{T}}G(\bm{\bar{q}})(\bm{q}_{\beta}-\bm{q}_{\alpha})}\,\left[1+\frac{\sigma_{q}}{\|\ \bm{\bar{q}}-\bm{q}_{a}(\bm{\bar{q}})\|_{2}}\right].

(6)

Here,
\sigma_{q}
is a setting parameter.
The first term in equation (
6

The second term of Eq. (
6
) ensures that edges near obstacles incur a large penalty, discouraging collisions.

With all edge costs defined, we run Dijkstra’s algorithm on

to find the minimum-cost discrete path between the nodes nearest to
\bm{q}_{s}
and
\bm{q}_{t}

\bm{q}_{s}
and
\bm{q}_{t}
and apply cubic spline interpolation through the resulting node sequence to generate a smooth continuous trajectory

For the spline interpolation, the path is generated using cubic spline interpolation for a sequence of points (

Evaluation of the proposed method

We compare the path planning method in position space and the proposed method in joint space.

The first scenario in which the proposed method is used is to guide the forceps from near the insertion point to near the target organ.
In this scenario, a 5.0 mm margin to the organ allows for adequate guidance.

\partial X_{a}

Applying inverse kinematics to this

and determine

We evaluated the path generation of the proposed method in the joint space with the following indices.
One is the insertion angle of the forceps.

Fig.
6
shows the mimic bladder transferred from position space to joint space.
It can be seen that the shape that is concave in position space becomes convex in joint space.

For surfaces composed of multiple convex surfaces, the shortest distance in joint space can be obtained by applying the gradient descent method to each convex surface independently and taking the minimum of the output values, which is more efficient than a full search.
Thus, the fact that
\partial Q_{a}
consists of a combination of convex surfaces is a desirable condition for path planning, and this problem setting, where
\partial X_{a}
includes concave regions, has the advantage of path finding in the joint space.

Conclusion

In this paper, we proposed a path finding method on Riemannian manifolds in joint space for a surgical robot for MIS.
We confirmed that the mimic organs were represented by a combination of non-concave surfaces in the joint space of the robot arm.
The proposed method achieved faster computation by using the greedy method in the joint angle space to calculate the path avoiding collision to the organs.
We validated the proposed approach through experiments in anatomically informed abdominal environments and demonstrated that our planner yields trajectories with reduced joint motion and port stress.
These results highlight the potential of our framework as a core component in semi-autonomous surgical systems that can be implemented using existing clinical robotic platforms.

Future works are as follows.
For more complex obstacles, the necessary conditions for having a convex representation in joint space need to be clarified.
For the generated trajectories, we plan to consider collision avoidance methods other than spline interpolation used in this study.
Consideration of avoiding interference between robot arms and application to actual intracorporeal environments with more complexed shapes.
