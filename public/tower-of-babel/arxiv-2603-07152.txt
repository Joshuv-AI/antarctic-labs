On linear α p \alpha_{p} -quotients
Quentin Posva and Takehiko Yasuda, with an appendix by Linus Rösler

Abstract




Abstract.
We study “linear”
\alpha_{p}
-actions on affine spaces and the associated quotient singularities, using explicit stacky resolutions. We describe when the quotient singularities are log canonical, canonical or terminal, and we compute their stringy motivic invariants. The second author and Fabio Tonini conjectured that these invariants coincide with those of linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-quotients: our approach reduces this conjecture to an equality of explicit multi-sets, which we check for a large number of primes using a computer software.
A general proof of the equality of multi-sets is given in the appendix written by Linus Rösler.

1.
Introduction

Let
k
be an algebraically closed field of positive characteristic
p>0
. In this article, we study “linear”
\alpha_{p}
-quotient singularities from the points of view of the MMP and invariants steaming from motivic integration, and compare their properties to those of linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-quotients.

Quotients by
\alpha_{p}
-actions tend to exhibit a surprising behaviour that is sensitive to the ratio between their dimension and the characteristic
p
: their study is instructive by itself and in order to draw the line between characteristic
0
theorems and positive characteristic phenomenons (see for example
[
Pos25a
]
).
The study of the linear
\alpha_{p}
-quotient singularities was initiated in
[
TY20
]
, which was the initial motivation of this paper. The approach taken there is that of motivic integration, inspired by the study of linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-quotients singularities in
[
Yas14
,
Yas19
]
. Our approach in this paper is different, but has applications to the questions raised in
[
TY20
]
.

To define these singularities, fix a sequence of non-negative integers
\mathbf{d}=\{d_{\lambda}\}_{\lambda=1,\dots,l}
such that
0\leq d_{\lambda}\leq p-1
and let
d=\sum_{\lambda}(1+d_{\lambda})
. For a function
\varphi\in H^{0}(\mathbb{A}^{d},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{d}})
, let us write

J_{\varphi,\mathbf{d}}=\begin{pmatrix}J_{\varphi,d_{1}}\\
&J_{\varphi,d_{2}}\\
&&\ddots\\
&&&J_{\varphi,d_{l}}\end{pmatrix}

where
J_{\varphi,d_{\lambda}}
is the
(d_{\lambda}+1)\times(d_{\lambda}+1)
lower triangular Jordan block with eigenvalue (i.e. diagonal entries)
\varphi
. Consider the
k
-linear derivation
\partial_{\mathbf{d}}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d}_{k}
defined through its action on coordinate functions
x_{1},\dots,x_{d}
as follows:

\partial_{\mathbf{d}}\colon\big(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\big)^{\intercal}\mapsto J_{0,\mathbf{d}}\cdot\big(x_{1},\dots,x_{d}\big)^{\intercal}.

Concretely,
\partial_{\mathbf{d}}
acts as a shift of indices in each block defined by the partition
\{d_{\lambda}\}
. This derivation defines an
\alpha_{p}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{d}
. Let us write

\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})=\mathbb{A}^{d}_{k}/\langle\partial_{\mathbf{d}}\rangle.

Such quotients (and actions) will be called
linear

\alpha_{p}
-quotients (and actions).

In this article, we study
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
using an explicit partial resolution. In
Section 4
we construct a weighted blow-up
b\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{A}^{d}

where
\mathcal{Y}
is a regular tame Deligne–Mumford stack such that the
1
-foliation
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}}
on
\mathcal{Y}
induced by the
\alpha_{p}
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{d}
, is regular. This implies that
\mathcal{W}=\mathcal{Y}/\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}}
is a regular tame DM stack that resolves
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
. The coarse moduli space of
\mathcal{W}
has only toroidal singularities, and thus is a schematic partial resolution of the linear
\alpha_{p}
-quotient. A nice feature is that it is possible to compute the discrepancies of
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
from the discrepancies of
\mathcal{Y}
and
\mathcal{F}_{\mathcal{Y}}
. This is similar to the analysis done in
[
Pos25a
, §3]
, with some extra twists due to the presence of stacks: the necessary tools are developed in
Section 2.2
and
Section 2.3
. In this way we obtain a description of the MMP singularities of linear
\alpha_{p}
-quotients:

Theorem 1.0.1

(
Theorem 4.3.3
)
.

Suppose that
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{d}}=\sum_{\lambda}\frac{d_{\lambda}(d_{\lambda}+1)}{2}\geq 2
.
Then
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
is lc if and only if
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{d}}\geq p-1
, is canonical if and only if
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{d}}\geq p
, and is terminal if and only if
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{d}}\geq p+1
.

The singularity
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
is usually not Cohen–Macaulay (see
Section 4.3
), so the above theorem gives new examples of canonical and terminal singularities in positive characteristic which are not Cohen–Macaulay, unlike what would happen in characteristic
0
. This adds to a growing list of examples

[
Yas14
,
GNT19
,
Tot19
,
Yas19
,
Tot24
,
Pos25a
]
.

Our construction also gives a way to compute the motivic stringy invariant

2.4.
Stringy motivic invariant

We provide a short introduction to the stringy motivic invariants, and refer to
[
Yas14
]
for more details.
The Grothendieck ring of varieties, denoted by
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{k})
, is defined as the quotient of the free abelian group generated by isomorphism classes of varieties modulo the scissor relation. We consider the following two variants of this ring. Firstly, we define
K^{\mathrm{uh}}_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{k})
to be the quotient of
K_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{k})
by the extra relation that for each universal homeomorphism
Y\to X
,
[Y]=[X]
(see
[
CLNS18
, p.115]
). Let
\mathbb{L}=[\mathbb{A}^{1}]
be the class of an affine line. We then define a version of the complete Grothendieck ring of varieties by
localizing
K^{\mathrm{uh}}_{0}(\mathrm{Var}_{k})
with
\mathbb{L}
and then taking the dimensional completion; we denote this complete ring by
\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{uh}}_{k}
. We need this variant of the complete Grothedieck ring not for establishing a general theory of motivic integration in positive characteristic, but for giving a combinatorial formula for stringy motivic invariants of quotients
\mathbb{A}^{d}/\alpha_{p}
.

Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair such that
K_{X}+B
is Cartier. The divisor
B
can have only non-positive coefficients. Since
\omega_{X}(B)
is invertible, we can define an ideal sheaf
I
by

Im\left(\bigwedge^{d}\Omega_{X}\to\omega_{X}(B)\right)=I\cdot\omega_{X}(B).

The stringy motivic invariant
\mst(X,B)
is defined as

\mst(X,B)=\int_{J_{\infty}X}\mathbb{L}^{\ord I}\,d\mu_{X}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{uh}}_{k}.

More generally, for a locally closed subset
C
, we define the stringy motivic invariant along
C
by

\mst(X,B)_{C}=\int_{(J_{\infty}X)_{C}}\mathbb{L}^{\ord I}\,d\mu_{X}\in\widehat{\mathcal{M}}^{\mathrm{uh}}_{k}.

Here
(J_{\infty}X)_{C}
denotes the space of arcs that intersect with
C
.

In fact, the invariant
\mst(X,B)_{C}
depends only on singularities of the pair
(X,B)
along
C
. More precisely, if
C
is a closed subset in an open subset
U\subset X
then
\mst(X,B)_{C}
depends only on the isomorphism class of the pair
(\widehat{U},B|_{\widehat{U}})
obtained by taking the formal completion of
U
along
C
and restricting
B
to
\widehat{U}
. This is because the space
(J_{\infty}X)_{C}
together with the motivic measure and the function
\ord I
depends only on the isomorphism class of
(\widehat{U},B|_{\widehat{U}})
.
In particular, when
C=\{x\}
with
x
a closed point, then
\mst(X,B)_{x}
is determined by
(\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x},B|_{\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}})
. Thus, we sometimes write this as
\mst(\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x},B|_{\Spec\widehat{\mathcal{O}}_{X,x}})_{x}
.
The following are some more basic properties of stringy motivic invariants:

Proposition 2.4.1
.

Let
(X,B)
be a klt pair as above.

(a)

If a locally closed subset
C\subset X
is the disjoint union of finitely many locally closed subsets
C_{1},\dots,C_{l}
, then
\mst(X,B)_{C}=\sum_{i=1}^{l}\mst(X,B)_{C_{i}}
.

(b)

If
f\colon(Y,B^{\prime})\to(X,B)
is a proper birational crepant morphism of klt pairs (so that
K_{Y}+B^{\prime}
is also Cartier) and if
C\subset X
is a locally closed subset, then
\mst(Y,B^{\prime})_{f^{-1}(C)}=\mst(X,B)_{C}
.

Remark 2.4.2
.

When
K_{X}+B
is only
\mathbb{Q}
-Cartier, then the stringy motivic invariant is defined as an element of a version of the complete Grothendieck ring with some fractional power of
\mathbb{L}
adjoined.

3.
Degeneration of linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-actions into
\alpha_{p}
-actions

We keep the notations of the Introduction. In this section we explain how the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action on
\mathbb{A}^{d}
given by
J_{1,\mathbf{d}}
can be degenerated over the affine line
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
into the
\alpha_{p}
-action given by
J_{0,\mathbf{d}}
. Informally, if we replace the upper diagonal of
J_{1,\mathbf{d}}
by the parameter
s
, then for
s\neq 0
we obtain a family of
\mathbb{Z}/p
-actions whose first-order deviation from the identity is
s\cdot J_{0,\mathbf{d}}
.

To make this precise, we start by introducing a version of the
Tate–Oort group scheme

[
Rei19
]
. The base of the group scheme will be
B=\{st=0\}\subset\mathbb{A}^{2}_{st}
. The group is given by

\mathcal{G}=\Spec_{B}\left(\frac{\mathcal{O}_{B}[x,(1+tx)^{-1}]}{(x^{p}-s^{p-1}x)}\right)

with group law over
B
given by the co-multiplication

\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G})\to\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G})\otimes\mathcal{O}(\mathcal{G}),\quad x\mapsto x\otimes 1+1\otimes x+tx\otimes x.

Proposition 3.0.1

(cf.
[
Rei19
, Proposition 3.1]
)
.

With the notations as above,

(a)

\mathcal{G}
is a finite flat group scheme of length
p
over
B
,

(b)

\mathcal{G}\otimes_{B}\{s\neq 0\}
is isomorphic to the constant group
\mathbb{Z}/p
;

(c)

\mathcal{G}\otimes_{B}\{t\neq 0\}\cong\mu_{p,\{t\neq 0\}}
;

(d)

\mathcal{G}\otimes_{B}\{s=t=0\}\cong\alpha_{p}
.

Let
\mathcal{G}_{a}=\mathcal{G}\otimes_{B}\{t=0\}
: it is a finite flat group scheme over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
that is the constant group
\mathbb{Z}/p
over the open set
\{s\neq 0\}
, and whose fiber above
s=0
is
\alpha_{p}
. (Here
a
stands for “additive”, as
\mathcal{G}_{a}
embeds inside the additive group over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
. In
[
MI21
, Chapter 7]
,
\mathcal{G}_{a}
is called the
unified
p
-group scheme
.)

Consider the following matrices: we let

\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{\lambda}}=\begin{pmatrix}1&\\
s&1&\\
&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&s&1&\\
&&&s&1\end{pmatrix}\in M_{(d_{\lambda}+1)\times(d_{\lambda}+1)}(k[s])

and

\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{1}}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{l}}\end{pmatrix}.

Observe that for
n\in\mathbb{Z}
,

(\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{\lambda}})^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}1\\
\binom{n}{1}s&1\\
\binom{n}{2}s^{2}&\binom{n}{1}s&1\\
\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\ddots\\
&&&\binom{n}{1}s&1\end{pmatrix}\in M_{(d_{\lambda}+1)\times(d_{\lambda}+1)}(k[s])

and that

\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}^{n}=\begin{pmatrix}\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{1}}^{n}\\
&\ddots\\
&&\widetilde{J}_{s,d_{l}}^{n}\end{pmatrix},

and that all these matrices have order
p
.
If
f\colon T\to\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
is any
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
-scheme, we have the matrices

f^{*}(\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}^{n})\in\GL_{T}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times T)

and it is easy to check that

f^{*}(\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}^{n})=(f^{*}\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}})^{n}
.
Therefore we can define a morphism of group functors

\mathfrak{J}\colon\underline{\mathbb{Z}/p}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}\longrightarrow\underline{\GL}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s})

as follows: if
T
is connected we let

\mathfrak{J}_{T}(n)=f^{*}\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}^{n}\quad\text{for }n\in\underline{\mathbb{Z}/p}_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}(T)=\mathbb{Z}/p,

and the value on disconnected schemes is given by the product over the values of connected components. So
\mathfrak{J}
is a morphism of group schemes, whose image
\mathbf{G}
is a closed sub-group scheme of
\GL_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s})
. We think of
\mathbf{G}
as the sub-group generated by
\widetilde{J}_{s,\mathbf{d}}
inside the matrix group
\GL(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s})
. It is clear that
\mathbf{G}
is reduced, and finite flat of length
p
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
.

Proposition 3.0.2
.

We have an isomorphism of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
-group schemes
\mathbf{G}\cong\mathcal{G}_{a}
. The fiber of the action of
\mathbf{G}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
over
s=1
is the
\mathbb{Z}/p
-action given by the matrix
J_{1,\mathbf{d}}
, and the fiber over
s=0
is the
\alpha_{p}
-action given by the derivation
\partial_{\mathbf{d}}
.

Proof.

Let
z\in\Gamma(\mathbf{G},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{G}})
be the function that corresponds to the entry on the lower diagonal of the Jordan blocks: generically over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
it assumes the values
0,s,\dots,(p-1)s
and thus verifies the equation
z^{p}-s^{p-1}z=0
globally. Since any
M\in\mathbf{G}(\Spec(A))
is uniquely determined by the value
z(M)\in A
, and since
\mathbf{G}
has length
p
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
, we deduce that

\mathbf{G}\cong\Spec_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}\frac{k[s,z]}{(z^{p}-s^{p-1}z)}\quad\text{as }\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}\text{-schemes}.

If
M,M^{\prime}\in\mathbf{G}(\Spec(A))
are two
A
-valued points, then
z(M\cdot M^{\prime})=z(M)+z(M^{\prime})
. So the co-multiplication of
\Gamma(\mathbf{G},\mathcal{O}_{\mathbf{G}})
under the above isomorphism is given by
z\mapsto z\otimes 1+1\otimes z
. Hence
\mathbf{G}\cong\mathcal{G}_{a}
as group schemes over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
.

The description of the fiber of the action of
\mathcal{G}_{a}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
above
s=1
is immediate. For
s=0
, we know that we have an
\alpha_{p}
-action. To find out the corresponding derivation
\partial
, we look at
k[\varepsilon]
-points of
\mathbf{G}
above
s=0
, where
k[\varepsilon]
is the ring of dual numbers: by letting
z=\varepsilon
we find the infinitesimal automorphism

(x_{1},\dots,x_{d})^{\intercal}\mapsto\widetilde{J}_{\varepsilon,\mathbf{d}}\cdot(x_{1},\dots,x_{n})^{\intercal}=((\id+\varepsilon\partial)(x_{1}),\dots,(\id+\varepsilon\partial)(x_{n}))^{\intercal}.

This shows that
\partial=\partial_{\mathbf{d}}
, as desired.
∎

Consider the geometric quotient
\mathcal{X}=(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s})/\mathbf{G}
: it is an affine normal flat
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
-scheme of finite type. For each
\alpha\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}(k)
we have a finite comparison morphism

\varphi_{\alpha}\colon\mathbb{A}^{d}/\mathbf{G}_{\alpha}\longrightarrow\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}

which is an isomorphism on the locus where
\mathbf{G}_{\alpha}
acts freely. If
\mathbb{D}_{\mathbf{d}}\geq 2
then the ramification locus of the
\mathbf{G}
-action has relative codimension
\geq 2
over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
, and it follows that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism if and only if
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}
is
S_{2}
. This is the case for a general value of
\alpha
: for the set of points
x
of
\mathcal{X}
such that
x
is an
S_{2}
point of its fiber over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
, is open
[
Gro66
, 12.1.6]
and contains the generic fiber. The image in
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
of its complement is constructible and does not contain the generic point: so it must be a finite union of closed points. Furthermore, we have the
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action by scaling on
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
: it lifts to an action on
\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
(acting trivially on the first factor) that commutes with the
\mathbf{G}
-action. So it descends to a
\mathbb{G}_{m}
-action on
\mathcal{X}
that is transitive on the set of fibers
\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}
with
\alpha\neq 0
. So all these fibers are
S_{2}
, and it follows that
\varphi_{\alpha}
is an isomorphism when
\alpha\neq 0
. Hence the family
\mathcal{X}\otimes_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}}\{s\neq 0\}\to\{s\neq 0\}
is fiberwise trivial. Indeed, we have

\mathcal{X}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{A}^{d}/\mathbf{G}_{\alpha}\cong\mathbb{A}/(\mathbb{Z}/p,\mathbf{d})

when
\alpha\neq 0
: for linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-actions on
\mathbb{A}^{d}
are uniquely determined, up to linear isomorphisms, by the dimensions and the multiplicities of the irreducible sub-spaces.

The morphism
\varphi_{0}
is an isomorphism if and only if the
\mathbf{G}
-cohomology group
H^{1}(\mathbf{G};\mathcal{O}_{\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}})
, seen as a
k[s]
-module, has only trivial
s
-torsion (see
[
DGA+11
, Exposé I, §5]
for group-scheme cohomology). If one knows explicit generators of
H^{0}(\mathbb{A}^{d}/\mathbf{G}_{0},\mathcal{O})
, this can also be addressed by explicit computations: see
[
LMM21
, §9.2]
for concrete examples in a similar set-up. We will not study this question in more details here; nonetheless, the results that we will obtain in
Section 6
suggest that
\varphi_{0}
is an isomorphism.

In any case, as
\mathbf{G}
is geometrically reductive over
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{s}
, it follows that
\varphi_{0}
is a finite universal homeomorphism. So
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha,\mathbf{d})
is a one-parameter deformation of
\mathbb{A}/(\mathbb{Z}/p,\mathbf{d})
up to universal homeomorphism.

Remark 3.0.3
.

One can ask whether the linear
\alpha_{p}
-action can be obtained as a degeneration in the other direction, i.e. as the limit of
\mu_{p}
-actions inside an action of

\mathcal{H}=\mathcal{G}\otimes_{B}\{s=0\}=\Spec_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}\frac{k[t,x,(1+tx)^{-1}]}{(x^{p})}.

We indicate one way to do so.

The global section
x\in\Gamma(\mathcal{H},\mathcal{O}_{\mathcal{H}})
induces a group morphism
\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{G}_{a}
, and the global section
1+tx
induces a group morphism
\mathcal{H}\to\mathbb{G}_{m}
. These two morphisms yield a closed embedding of
\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
-group schemes

\mathcal{H}\cong\{(y,1+ty)\mid y^{p}=0\}\hookrightarrow\mathbb{G}_{a}\times_{\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}}\mathbb{G}_{m}.

Using this description, we can write down the following action of
\mathcal{H}
on
\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}
. Let
\mathfrak{S}=\{d_{1}+1,d_{1}+d_{2}+2,\dots,d\}
and define:

(y,1+ty)\colon\quad x_{i}\mapsto\begin{cases}x_{i}&\text{if }i\in\mathfrak{S},\\
(1+ty)x_{i}+yx_{i+1}&\text{if }i\notin\mathfrak{S}.\end{cases}

When
t=0
and
y=\varepsilon
this is the infinitesimal automorphism
\id+\varepsilon\partial_{\mathbf{d}}
, as desired.

Let
\mathcal{Y}=(\mathbb{A}^{d}\times\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t})/\mathcal{H}
. As before, we have finite comparison morphisms

\psi_{\beta}\colon\mathbb{A}^{d}/\mathcal{H}_{\beta}\longrightarrow\mathcal{Y}_{\beta},\quad\beta\in\mathbb{A}^{1}_{t}.

Since
\mathcal{H}\otimes\{t\neq 0\}\cong\mu_{p}
is linearly reductive,
\psi_{\beta}
is an isomorphism for
\beta\neq 0
. The morphism
\psi_{0}
is a finite universal homeomorphism, but in general we do not know whether it is an isomorphism.

Unlike the singularities of
\mathbb{A}/(\mathbb{Z}/p,\mathbf{d})
and
\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d})
, those of
\mathbb{A}^{d}/\mathcal{H}_{\beta}
for
\beta\neq 0
are well-behaved for any
\mathbf{d}
: there are always toroidal, so in particular Cohen–Macaulay, rational and klt.

In this section we describe partial resolutions of linear
\alpha_{p}

4.1.
Notations

We start by fixing our notations. Let

associated to the eigenvalue

∎

In the course of the proof, we have shown the following:

The coarse moduli morphism

is an isomorphism above

∎

Remark 4.4.6

(Weighted blow-up of linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-actions)
.

We have seen that well-chosen weighted blow-ups of
\mathbb{A}^{d}
resolve linear
\alpha_{p}
-actions (in the sense of

). One might ask if a similar phenomenon happens for linear
\mathbb{Z}/p
-actions, especially in view of

. If we select the weights in the same way, then the

-action lifts to the

. We prefer to work with equivariant charts, since

is singular. The action does not lift, however, to the charts

. This can be arranged, at least in a neighbourhood of the exceptional divisor, by introducing

-th roots of

-action is the exceptional divisor.

We hoped that the “

-
\mu_{p}
switch” phenomenon described by Totaro in
[

]
would apply on the locus where the ramification ideal is not principal. This is unfortunately not quite the case: on some charts we are lead to consider the non-linear action

which does not lend itself to Totaro’s method.

Let us also notice that in characteristic

to the prove a version of the McKay correspondence. We do not know if the characteristic

5.1.
Batyrev’s formula

The following formula, which is a special case of a result of Batyrev, will be our main tool in conjunction with the construction of a partial resolution in the previous section.

Proposition 5.1.1
.

Let
(\Spec(\mathcal{O}),\sum a_{i}B_{i})
be a cyclic quotient singularity of type
\frac{1}{n}(w_{1},\dots,w_{t})
(cf.
Section 2.1
). Assume that
a_{i}<1
for every

\varphi\colon(\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0})^{t}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\quad\text{be the linear function defined by}\quad\varphi(e_{i})=1-a_{i}\ \forall i

where
e_{i}
is the
i

N
be the sub-group of
\mathbb{R}^{t}
generated by the vector
(w_{1}/n,\dots,w_{t}/n)^{\intercal}
together with
e_{1},\dots,e_{d}

\mathbf{0}
is the closed point of

\mst\left(\Spec(\mathcal{O}),\sum_{i}a_{i}B_{i}\right)_{\mathbf{0}}=(\mathbb{L}-1)^{t}\cdot\prod_{i=1}^{t}\frac{1}{1-\mathbb{L}^{a_{i}-1}}\cdot\sum_{\mathfrak{p}\in N\cap(0;1]^{t}}\mathbb{L}^{-\varphi(\mathfrak{p})}.

Proof.

We first consider an affine
\mathbb{Q}
-factorial toric pair
(R,\sum a_{i}B_{i}^{\prime})
that has the same singularity type at a unique torus-invariant closed point denoted again by
\mathbf{0}
. Then, we claim that
\mst\left(R,\sum_{i}a_{i}B_{i}^{\prime}\right)_{\mathbf{0}}
is expressed by the same formula as in the statement; this will be sufficient to conclude, as the
\mst
at a closed point is a formal-local invariant. This is a slight variation of
[
Bat99
, Theorem 4.5]
and the proof is basically the same. We explain the outline of the proof. To show the claim, we first note that
\mst\left(R,\sum_{i}a_{i}B_{i}^{\prime}\right)_{\mathbf{0}}
is expressed as the motivic volume of the space of arcs that maps the closed point to
\mathbf{0}
. Since
R
is a toric variety, the space of arcs of
R
that maps the generic point into the open torus decomposes into countably many pieces indexed by
N\cap\sigma
. The arcs that does not map the generic point into the open torus has motivic measure zero and we can safely ignore them, when computing motivic volumes. Among such arcs, those that sends the closed point to
\mathbf{0}
corresponds to the pieces indexed by
N\cap\sigma^{\circ}
. We can explicitly compute the contribution of each piece to the motivic volume, by applying the change of variables formula to a toric resolution and by a direct calculation on a simple normal crossing pair. The contribution is
(\mathbb{L}-1)^{t}\mathbb{L}^{-\varphi(\mathfrak{p})}
. Summing up them over
\mathfrak{p}\in N\cap\sigma^{\circ}

∎

In

An approach to this conjecture using motivic integration was proposed in

]

In

Proposition 6.0.1
.

We have

\mst(\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d}))=\mst(\mathbb{A}/(\mathbb{Z}/p,\mathbf{d}))\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\mst(\mathbb{A}/(\alpha_{p},\mathbf{d}))_{\mathbf{0}}=\mst(\mathbb{A}/(\mathbb{Z}/p,\mathbf{d}))_{\mathbf{0}}.

More precisely, we claim that for

either

or

\mst(\mathbb{A}/(G,\mathbf{d}))=\left[\mathbb{A}^{d}\setminus V\left(x_{i}^{(\sigma)}\mid(\sigma,i)\in\mathbf{I}^{*}\right)\right]+[\mathbb{A}^{l}]\cdot\mst(\mathbb{A}/(G,\mathbf{d}))_{\mathbf{0}}.

The equivalence of the statement follows immediately from these expressions. Let us give a geometric argument that applies to both cases at the same time.

Let
\mathbf{R}
be the image of
\mathfrak{R}=V(x_{i}^{(\sigma)}\mid(\sigma,i)\in\mathbf{I}^{*})
in

\mst(X)=\left[\mathbb{A}^{d}\setminus\mathfrak{R}\right]+\mst(X)_{\mathbf{R}}.

We let the additive group
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}
act by translation on the coordinates

\mathbb{A}^{d}
that commutes with the

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}
on the arcs of

\mathbf{R}
.

Claim 6.0.2
.

The
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}
-action on
\mathbf{R}

Proof.

The morphism
\mathfrak{R}\to\mathbf{R}
is bijective and
\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}

\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}

It follows from the claim that the action map

\displaystyle\mst(X)_{\mathbf{R}}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{J_{\infty}(X)_{\mathbf{R}}}\mathbb{L}^{\ord I}\ d\mu_{X}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}\times J_{\infty}(X)_{\mathbf{0}}}\mathbb{L}^{\ord I}\ d\mu_{X}

\displaystyle[\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}]\cdot\int_{J_{\infty}(X)_{\mathbf{0}}}\mathbb{L}^{\ord I}\ d\mu_{X}

\displaystyle[\mathbb{G}_{a}^{l}]\cdot\mst(X)_{\mathbf{0}}

Lemma A.2.6
.

Let

\displaystyle\biguplus_{0\leq m\leq N}\llbracket B_{m},A_{m}\rrbracket=\biguplus_{0\leq m\leq N}\llbracket B_{m},A_{m+1}\rrbracket

\displaystyle f\coloneqq\sum_{0\leq m\leq N}\mathbbm{1}_{\llbracket B_{m},A_{m}\rrbracket}-\mathbbm{1}_{\llbracket B_{m},A_{m+1}\rrbracket}\equiv 0.

\displaystyle\mathbbm{1}_{\llbracket B_{m},A_{m}\rrbracket}-\mathbbm{1}_{\llbracket B_{m},A_{m+1}\rrbracket}=\mathbbm{1}_{\rrbracket-\infty,A_{m}\rrbracket}-\mathbbm{1}_{\rrbracket-\infty,A_{m+1}\rrbracket},
