The n n -adjacency graph for knots
Marion Campisi, Brandy Doleshal, Eric Staron

Abstract
. A knot K K is called n n -adjacent to a knot K ′ K^{\prime} if there is a set of n n crossing circles 𝒞 \mathcal{C} in K K so that a generalized crossing change at any nonempty subset of crossings in 𝒞 \mathcal{C} yields K ′ K^{\prime} . In this paper, the authors define a new graph Γ n \Gamma_{n} to represent n n -adjacency relationships between knots. We prove several results about this new object.



1.
Introduction

A generalized crossing change is a Dehn surgery along a so called
crossing circle
, i.e. a circle which bounds a
crossing disk
which intersects a knot in two points, with algebraic intersection number zero.
A knot
K
is called
n
-adjacent to a knot
K^{\prime}
if there is a set of
n
crossing circles
\mathcal{C}
in
K
so that a generalized crossing change at any nonempty subset of crossings in
\mathcal{C}
yields
K^{\prime}
.

Many researchers have studied
n
-adjacency in various contexts. Howards and Luecke
[
4
]
provide an upper bound for
n
when a knot
K
is
n
-adjacent to the unknot, in terms of the Seifert genus of
K
. Kalfagianni and Lin
[
7
]

[
6
]
generalize these results when
g(K)>g(K^{\prime})
and use knot adjacency to obstruct fiberings of knots. Torisu
[
15
]

[
14
]
provides results about which knots are 2-adjacent to 2-bridge knots. Askitas and Kalfagianni
[
1
]
use the Alexander polynomial to obstruct fibered or alternating knots from being
n
-adjacent to the unknot for
n\geq 3
. Tao
[
12
]
considers the relationship between 2-adjacency and knot polynomials, giving a condition under which the 2-adjacency relation is not symmetric. Most recently, Carney and Meike
[
CM2AdjacentToUnknot
]
show that only 20 knots of 12 or fewer crossings are 2-adjacent to the unknot.

We contribute to this effort by defining a new object, called the
n
-adjacency graph, to represent the
n
-adjacency relation between knots. We prove several results about this object.

In Section
2
, we provide the necessary background and definitions. In Section
3
, we define the
n
-adjacency graph for knots and prove several results about this new object, providing some connections to pre-existing literature. In Section
4
, we focus specifically on 2-adjacency of 2-bridge knots, and discuss a family of examples that prove the following:

Theorem 1
.

For every 2-bridge knot
K
, there are infinitely many 2-bridge knots
K^{\prime}
such that
K^{\prime}\xrightarrow{\ 2\ }K
.

2.
Background and definitions

Definition 1
.

The knot
K
is
n
-adjacent to the knot
K^{\prime}
, denoted
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
if there exist
n
crossing circles,
C_{1},C_{2},\ldots C_{n}
, for
K
such that a generalized crossing change on any nonempty subset of
\{C_{1},C_{2},\ldots C_{n}\}
yields
K^{\prime}
.

We say that two knots are
n
-adjacent if they admit diagrams which are
n
-adjacent.

We denote an order
d
generalized crossing change along crossing circle
C
for
K
by
K_{[C^{d}]}
. Using this notation we see that a knot diagram
K
is 2-adjacent to a knot diagram
K^{\prime}
if there exist crossing circles
C_{1}
and
C_{2}
, with generalized crossing changes of orders
d_{1}
and
d_{2}
respectively such that the following hold:

(1)

K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}}]}\cong K^{\prime}
,

(2)

K_{[C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}\cong K^{\prime}
, and

(3)

K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}},C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}\cong K^{\prime}
.

Definition 2
.

A crossing circle for
K
that bounds a disk in the complement of
K
is called a nugatory crossing circle.

Definition 3
.

A (generalized) crossing change on a knot
K
and its corresponding crossing circle are called cosmetic if the crossing change yields a knot isotopic to
K
and the crossing change is performed on a non-nugatory crossing.

The question is open as to whether there are knots that admit cosmetic crossing changes (Problem 1.58 of Kirby’s problem list
[
10
]
).

The Generalized Cosmetic Crossing Conjecture
.

For any knot
K\subset S^{3}
, the only way to obtain
K
from a generalized crossing change on a crossing
c
is if
c
is a nugatory crossing.

It follows from work of Scharlemann and Thompson
[
11
]
that the unknot admits no cosmetic generalized crossing changes. Torisu
[
13
]
shows that the conjecture holds for 2-bridge knots, and Kalfagianni proves the same for fibered knots
[
8
]
. Ito
[
5
]
shows the conjecture is true for genus one pretzel knots. Balm and Kalfagianni
[
3
]
prove the result for satellites, and Balm, Friedl, Kalfagianni and Powell
[
2
]
obstruct cosmetic crossing changes in genus 1 knots. Lidman and Moore
[
9
]
prove the cosmetic crossing conjecture for other large collections of knots.

Here, we relate this conjecture to 2-adjacency.

In a 2-adjacency, we require that
K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}}]}\cong K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}},C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}
and
K_{[C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}\cong K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}},C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}
. This implies that in
K_{[C_{1}^{d_{1}}]}
,
C_{2}
either encompasses a nugatory crossing or is a cosmetic circle. Likewise the crossing that intersects
C_{1}
in
K_{[C_{2}^{d_{2}}]}
is either nugatory or cosmetic. Similarly, an
n
-adjacency requires
K_{[C_{i}^{d_{i}}]}\cong K_{[C_{i}^{d_{i}},C_{j}^{d_{j}}]}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
i\neq j
. We note that, in general, if
K
is in any class of knots for which the generalized cosmetic crossing conjecture is true,
C_{i}
must be nugatory in
K_{[C_{j}^{d_{j}}]}
for all
1\leq i,j\leq n
and
i\neq j
.

For this reason, we call a crossing circle
C_{i}\in\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\}

trivializable
if the crossing circle is not nugatory in
K
but becomes nugatory in
K^{\prime}
after a crossing change on some nonempty subset
S
of
\{C_{1},\ldots,C_{n}\}
so that
C_{i}\notin S
. If a crossing circle is not nugatory in
K
and is not trivializable for any such
S
, then the crossing circle is a cosmetic circle.

3.
The
n
-adjacency graph for knots

In this section, we introduce a graph to represent the relationships obtained from
n
-adjacency on knots.

Let
\Gamma_{n}
, for
n\geq 2
, be the graph defined in the following way. Each knot is represented by a vertex of
\Gamma_{n}
. A directed edge exists between two vertices
K
and
K^{\prime}
if and only if
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
. The edge is directed toward
K^{\prime}
.

If a knot
K
is
n
-adjacent to itself, there is a bi-directed loop beginning and ending at
K
. We call
K
a
neighbor
of
K^{\prime}
when a directed edge exists pointing from
K
to
K^{\prime}
and
K
and
K^{\prime}
are not isotopic.

Note that we can define
\Gamma_{1}
similarly, where a 1-adjacency is a generalized crossing change.

We further define
\Gamma_{n}^{c}
as the subgraph of
\Gamma_{n}
so that a directed edge exists between two vertices
K
and
K^{\prime}
if and only if
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
and some crossing circle in the
n
-adjacency is a cosmetic circle.

Lemma 1
.

The unknot is an isolated vertex in
\Gamma_{n}^{c}
for
n\geq 2
.

Proof.

Work of Scharlemann and Thompson
[
11
]
indicates that the unknot has no cosmetic generalized crossing changes. This means that for any knot
K
that is
n
-adjacent to the unknot, every crossing circle used in the
n
-adjacency must be trivializable, so we have no edges directed from
K
to the unknot in
\Gamma_{n}^{c}
.
∎

In fact, for the same reason, Lemma
1
is true of any knot that satisfies the Generalized Cosmetic Crossing Conjecture.

Theorem 2
.

If the Generalized Cosmetic Crossing Conjecture holds for all knots, then
\Gamma_{n}^{c}
is totally disconnected and has no loops.

Proof.

If
K^{\prime}
is a knot that satisfies the Generalized Cosmetic Crossing Conjecture, there is no cosmetic circle for
K^{\prime}
, and so there can be no knots
K
such that
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
with some cosmetic circle. Further
K
has no cosmetic generalized crossing changes, so there can be no loops in
\Gamma_{n}^{c}
.
∎

Lemma 2
.

For
n\geq 1
,
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
.

It is easy to see from the definition of
n
-adjacency if
K\xrightarrow{n+1}K^{\prime}
, then
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
for
n\geq 1
. Then if
K
is a neighbor of
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{n+1}
, then
K
is a neighbor of
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{n}
.

Theorem 3
.

For
n\geq 2
,
\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
.

Proof.

We induct on
n
, noting that the statement is tautologically true for
n=2
.

Suppose
\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
. By Lemma
2
,
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}
, so we have
\Gamma_{n+1}\subseteq\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
.
∎

Because every adjacency graph is contained in
\Gamma_{2}
, studying
\Gamma_{2}
can give us valuable insight into adjacencies of higher order. On the other end of the spectrum, we define
\Gamma_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n=2}^{\infty}\Gamma_{n}
.

We begin to connect the graph to the literature with the following theorem of
Howards and Luecke
[
4
]
.

Theorem 4
.

Let
K
be a non-trivial knot of genus
g
. Then
K
fails to be
n
-adjacent to the unknot for all
n
with
n\geq 3g-1
.

Further, Howards and Luecke
[
4
]
construct, for each
n
, a non-trivial knot that is
n
-adjacent to the unknot. This provides two results about
\Gamma_{n}
that contrast with Lemma
1
.

Corollary 1
.

For every
n\geq 2
, the unknot has a non-trivial neighbor in
\Gamma_{n}
.

Proposition 1
.

In
\Gamma_{2}
, the unknot has infinite valance.

Proof.

By Corollary
2
, the unknot has a non-trivial neighbor,
K_{n}
, in
\Gamma_{n}
for every integer
n\geq 2
. Theorem
3
tells us that
\Gamma_{n}\subseteq\Gamma_{2}
, so each
K_{n}
is also a neighbor of the unknot in
\Gamma_{2}
. Let
\mathcal{K}
be the collection of neighbors of the unknot in
\Gamma_{2}
.

Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that the unknot,
\mathcal{U}
, has finitely many neighbors in
\Gamma_{2}
. Call the collection
\mathcal{K}=\{K_{1},\ldots,K_{m}\}
for some integer
m\geq 1
. Theorem
4
tells us that for each element
K_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
,
K_{i}
is
n_{i}
-adjacent to
\mathcal{U}
for some
n_{i}\geq 2
. Call
g_{i}
the genus of
K_{i}
for each
K_{i}\in\mathcal{K}
. Let
K_{j}\in\mathcal{K}
be the knot in
\mathcal{K}
with the largest genus,
g_{j}
. By Theorem
4
,
n_{i}<3g_{i}-1
, and in particular,
n_{j}<3g_{j}-1
. Since
g_{j}\geq g_{i}
for
1\leq i\leq m
, it follows that
n_{i}<3g_{j}-1
. That is, every
n_{i}
is bounded above by the same finite number, and we have a contradiction to Corollary
2
.
∎

Askitas and Kalfagiani
[
1
]
show that if
K
is
n
-adjacent to the unknot for some
n\geq 3
, then
K
has trivial Alexander polynomial, and as a result, conclude that the only fibered or alternating knot that is
n
-adjacent to the unknot for
n\geq 3
is the unknot itself. This gives us more information about the non-trivial neighbors of the unknot in
\Gamma_{n}
.

Corollary 2
.

For every
n\geq 3
, the unknot’s non-trivial neighbors in
\Gamma_{n}
are neither fibered nor alternating.

Kalfagianni and Lin
[
6
]
generalize the work in
[
4
]
with the following results.

Theorem 5
.

Suppose
K,K^{\prime}
are knots with
g(K)>g(K^{\prime})
. If
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
, then
n\leq 6g(K)-3
.

Corollary 3
.

If
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, then
K
and
K^{\prime}
are isotopic.

Then we can prove the following about the adjacency graph.

Proposition 2
.

Every knot is isolated in
\Gamma_{\infty}
.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that
K
is a neighbor of
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{\infty}=\bigcap_{n=2}^{\infty}\Gamma_{n}
. Then
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
for all
n\geq 2
. By Lemma
2
, we have that
\Gamma_{2}\subseteq\Gamma_{1}
, so we have
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
. Then by Corollary
3
,
K
and
K^{\prime}
are isotopic, a contradiction.
∎

Additionally, Kalfagianni
[
8
]
proves the following theorem:

Theorem 6
.

If
K^{\prime}
is a fibered knot and
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
for some
n\geq 2
, then either
K
and
K^{\prime}
are isotopic or

.

Then we have the following result.

Proposition 3
.

If
\mathcal{U}
is the neighbor of a non-trivial knot
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
, then
K^{\prime}
is not a fibered knot.

Proof.

Suppose on the contrary that the unknot,
\mathcal{U}
is the neighbor of a fibered knot
K^{\prime}
in
\Gamma_{n}
for some
n\geq 2
. By Theorem
6
, either
K^{\prime}
is trivial or
g(\mathcal{U})>g(K^{\prime})
. Both options produce a contradiction.
∎

4.
2-adjacency of 2-bridge knots

In this section, we consider 2-adjacencies of 2-bridge knots in dialogue with questions of Torisu
[
14
]
and to contrast
\Gamma_{2}
with
\Gamma_{2}^{c}
.

The usual way to consider a 2-bridge knot
S(p,q)
is formed by twisting alternating strands by the list of numbers
a_{1},-a_{2},a_{3},-a_{4},\ldots,-a_{n-1},a_{n}
. These numbers correspond to the numbers in a continued fraction which reduces to
\frac{p}{q}
. Here, we choose to think of a 2-bridge knot as a 3-string braid with an unusual closure, which we call the
2-bridge closure
.

Definition 4
.

Let
\beta
be a braid whose word
w_{\beta}
is expressed in the generators
\sigma_{1}
and
\sigma_{2}
of the braid group
B_{3}
so that
\sigma_{1}
is the first generator to appear in
w_{\beta}
. Further, let there be a zero-th vertical strand positioned to the left of the three strands in
\beta
so that this zero-th strand does not interact with
\beta
. The
2-bridge closure of
\beta
has the following properties:

(1)

Strands 0 and 1 are connected at the top.

(2)

Strands 2 and 3 are connected at the top.

(3)

If the final generator in
w_{\beta}
is
\sigma_{1}
, strands 0 and 1 are connected at the bottom, and strands 2 and 3 are connected at the bottom.

(4)

If the final generator in
w_{\beta}
is
\sigma_{2}
, strands 0 and 3 are connected at the bottom, and strands 1 and 2 are connected at the bottom.

In the braid group
B_{3}
, we write the braided part of the knot as the braid word
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{a_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{a_{2}}\sigma_{1}^{a_{3}}\cdots\sigma_{2}^{a_{n-1}}\sigma_{1}^{a_{n}}
. Because of this convention, we lose some of the conveniences of working in
B_{n}
, such as the ability to cyclically permute the generators. If the knot
K
is the result of this closure on
\beta
we say that
K

corresponds
to
\beta
. Note that it is possible for several distinct braids to correspond to the same knot type.

The reader will notice that we have taken
n
to be odd. When
n
is even, the structure of a 2-bridge knot allows us to pull one crossing from the penultimate crossing to end on
\sigma_{1}
as long as
|a_{n-1}|
is at least 2. In the case that
a_{n-1}
is equal to
\pm 1
, the structure of a 2-bridge knot allows us to add it to the penultimate crossing, increasing the number of crossings by 1.

Definition 5
.

We say the braid
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{a_{1}}\sigma_{2}^{a_{2}}\sigma_{1}^{a_{3}}\cdots\sigma_{2}^{a_{n-1}}\sigma_{1}^{a_{n}}
has
length

n
.

Definition 6
.

A knot type
\mathcal{K}
is said to be
length
n
2-bridge
if there is a 2-bridge diagram
K
of
\mathcal{K}
with length
n
. We also say
\mathcal{K}
is of minimal length
n
.

As an example, the twist knot
T_{k}
can be written as
\beta=\sigma_{1}^{2}\sigma_{2}^{-k}
, so the twist knots are length-2 2-bridge knots.

[
13
]
showed that the generalized crossing conjecture is true for two bridge knots, so we know that every 2-bridge knot is isolated in
\Gamma_{2}^{c}

5.
Questions

We end our mathematical content with two questions about the
n
-adjacency graph.

In Proposition
1
and Corollary
4
, we find that the unknot and all of the 2-bridge knots are infinite valance in
\Gamma_{2}
. One then could naturally ask what other knots have infinite valance in
\Gamma_{2}
.

On the other hand, Proposition
2
tells us that all knots are isolated in
\Gamma_{\infty}
. Kalfagianni and Lin
[
6
]
prove the following theorem

Theorem 7
.

If
K
and
K^{\prime}
are non-isotopic knots, then there is a constant
C(K,K^{\prime})
so that
K\xrightarrow{\ n\ }K^{\prime}
implies
n\leq C(K,K^{\prime})
.

As
n
increases, the number of neighbors of
K^{\prime}
changes, and Lemma
2
tells us that this change is non-increasing. One could explore, for different classes of knots, at what rate the change in the number of neighbors occurs.
