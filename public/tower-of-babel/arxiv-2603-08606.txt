Comment: Exact vacuum solution with Hopf structure in general relativity
M. A. H. MacCallum

Abstract
Harada’s recently announced Kerr-Schild solution of Einstein’s equations in general relativity [Phys. Rev. D 112 , 024020 (2025)] is identified as isometric with one of the well-known NUT solutions.



In a recent paper
Har25
, Harada derived a Kerr-Schild vacuum solution of
Einstein’s equations, based on a Hopf fibering, in the form

\displaystyle{\mbox{\rm d}}s^{2}=-2{\mbox{\rm d}}u\,{\mbox{\rm d}}v+{\mbox{\rm d}}x^{2}+{\mbox{\rm d}}y^{2}

(1)

\displaystyle+H[{\mbox{\rm d}}v-\frac{ux+by}{u^{2}+b^{2}}{\mbox{\rm d}}x-\frac{uy-bx}{u^{2}+b^{2}}{\mbox{\rm d}}y+\frac{x^{2}+y^{2}}{2(u^{2}+b^{2})}{\mbox{\rm d}}u]^{2}.

where
H=Nu/(u^{2}+b^{2})
.
Harada showed that this metric is of Petrov type D. All Petrov type D
vacuum solutions are known
Kin69
. The solutions formed one of
the first applications
KarAma81
of the Cartan-Karlhede
procedure for characterization of spacetimes described in

SteKraMac03
, chapter 9. There is a useful table summarizing the
results in
Ama84
. The files for the CLASSI software (see

MacSke94
;
Mac18
) used in
creating that table are freely available with the software. Here I
use those files to identify which of the known solutions (
1
) is.

To apply the classification methods, the first step is choice of a suitable canonical tetrad.
Debever
Deb74
showed that Kerr-Schild spacetimes with a geodesic,
diverging, and shearfree null vector
k_{\mu}
can be written (using the
conventions of
SteKraMac03
and interchanging the names
u
and
v
) as

{{\mbox{\rm d}}}s^{2}=2({{\mbox{\rm d}}}\zeta\,{{\mbox{\rm d}}}\bar{\zeta}-{{\mbox{\rm d}}}u\,{{\mbox{\rm d}}}v)+2S({{\mbox{\rm d}}}v+\overline{Y}{{\mbox{\rm d}}}\zeta+Y{{\mbox{\rm d}}}\bar{\zeta}+Y\overline{Y}{{\mbox{\rm d}}}u)^{2}

(2)

with
\zeta=(x+iy)/\sqrt{2}
. This is of the same form as (
1
) if

a=u+ib
,
Y=-\zeta/a
and
S=H/2
. Following
SteKraMac03
, a suitable
tetrad for (
2
) is

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{1}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\rm d}}\zeta+Y{\mbox{\rm d}}u,\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{2}={\mbox{\rm d}}\bar{\zeta}+\overline{Y}{\mbox{\rm d}}u,

(3)

\displaystyle\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{3}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\rm d}}v+\overline{Y}{\mbox{\rm d}}\zeta+Y{\mbox{\rm d}}\bar{\zeta}+Y\overline{Y}{\mbox{\rm d}}u,\quad\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{4}={\mbox{\rm d}}u-S\mbox{\boldmath$\omega$}^{3}.

Using (
3
), CLASSI readily confirmed that (
1
)
is a Petrov type D vacuum solution. In the coordinates of
(
1
), Harada noted the Killing vectors
\partial_{v}
and

y\partial_{x}-x\partial y
, but CLASSI reports the metric has 4 Killing
vectors. This could have easily been foreseen since the invariants
contain only one essential coordinate.

Harada’s solution has (in Newman-Penrose
NewPen62
notation)

\Psi_{2}=-N/2(u+ib)^{3}
as the only nonzero component of the Riemann
tensor, and an invariant from the derivative of the Weyl tensor

C^{abcd;e}C_{abcd;e}=45N^{3}u/2(u^{2}+b^{2})(u+ib)^{8}.

(4)

Using Åman’s files and coordinates, Kinnersley type I metrics with
C=0
have the
invariants
\Psi_{2}=-m/(r+i\ell)^{3}
and
C^{abcd;e}C_{abcd;e}=180m^{3}r/(r^{2}+\ell^{2})(r+i\ell)^{8}
. Clearly these agree with the Harada
solution if we identify
r=u,b=\ell,N=2m
.

The other cases in Åman’s table which have 4 Killing vectors have
different invariants, not matching those of Harada’s
solution. Specifically, Kinnersley Type IIA with
a=0
has

\Psi_{2}=-(m+i\ell)/(r+i\ell)^{3}
,
Kinnersley Type IV A with nonzero
a
has extra terms in
C^{abcd;e}C_{abcd;e}
and Kinnersley Type IV B has a real
\Psi_{2}
.
There is a error in the table for Type II.BCD with
b=0

which has only 2, not 4, Killing vectors.

Hence from these comparisons one finds that Harada’s solution is
locally isometric to one of the well-known Taub-NUT solutions, the one
with a zero two-space curvature parameter. (This parameter is denoted

\mu_{0}
in
NewTamUnt63
and
\epsilon
in
GriPod09
.)

Starting from the Debever form (
2
) of the metric enables
a concise way to present coordinate transformations from
(
1
) to standard forms of the NUT metric. In
(
2
) substitute
\zeta=az,{\mbox{\rm d}}\zeta=a{\mbox{\rm d}}z+z{\mbox{\rm d}}u
. The
metric then becomes

{\mbox{\rm d}}s^{2}=-2{\mbox{\rm d}}u\,\sigma+2(u^{2}+b^{2}){\mbox{\rm d}}z{\mbox{\rm d}}{\bar{z}}+H\sigma^{2}

(5)

where

\sigma=({\mbox{\rm d}}v-a\bar{z}{\mbox{\rm d}}z-\bar{a}z{\mbox{\rm d}}{\bar{z}}-z\bar{z}{\mbox{\rm d}}u)

(6)

Substituting
a=u+ib
and
z=(P+iQ)/\sqrt{2}
(so that
P=(ux+by)/(u^{2}+b^{2}),Q=(uy-bx)/(u^{2}+b^{2})
) into
\sigma
one finds

\displaystyle\sigma

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\rm d}}v-u(P{\mbox{\rm d}}P+Q{\mbox{\rm d}}Q)-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}(P^{2}+Q^{2}){\mbox{\rm d}}u

(7)

\displaystyle+bP{\mbox{\rm d}}Q-bQ{\mbox{\rm d}}P

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle{\mbox{\rm d}}v-{\textstyle{1\over 2}}{\mbox{\rm d}}((P^{2}+Q^{2})u)+bP{\mbox{\rm d}}Q-bQ{\mbox{\rm d}}P.

Making the variable transformation
v=w+{\textstyle{1\over 2}}((P^{2}+Q^{2})u)
the
metric becomes

{\mbox{\rm d}}s^{2}=-2{\mbox{\rm d}}u\ \sigma+(u^{2}+b^{2})({\mbox{\rm d}}P^{2}+{\mbox{\rm d}}Q^{2})+H\sigma^{2},

(8)

with
\sigma={\mbox{\rm d}}w+b(P{\mbox{\rm d}}Q-Q{\mbox{\rm d}}P)
. This is the original NUT form for
the case
\mu_{0}=0
, with renamed parameters
and coordinates.
NewTamUnt63
gives the Killing vectors Harada
did not find as
\partial_{P}-bQ\partial_{w}
and
\partial_{Q}+bP\partial_{w}
.

If one substitutes
w=V+\int{\mbox{\rm d}}u/H
and
introduces polar coordinates
(\rho,\,\phi)
in the
(P,\,Q)
plane the metric becomes

-{\mbox{\rm d}}u^{2}/H+(u^{2}+b^{2})({\mbox{\rm d}}\rho^{2}+\rho^{2}{\mbox{\rm d}}\phi^{2})+H({\mbox{\rm d}}V+b\rho^{2}{\mbox{\rm d}}\phi)^{2},

(9)

which (with
V=t
and
H=-f
there) is the form used in

GriPod09
, where §12.3.2 analyses properties such as the
conformal structure. In the coordinates of (
9
) the Killing
vectors that Harada did not find are

\cos\phi\,\partial_{\rho}-\sin\phi(1/\rho\,\partial_{\phi}+b\rho\partial_{V})
and

\sin\phi\,\partial_{\rho}+\cos\phi(1/\rho\,\partial_{\phi}+b\rho\partial_{V})
.
