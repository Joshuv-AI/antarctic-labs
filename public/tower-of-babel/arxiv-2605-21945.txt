A Characterization of Level- k k Realizability for Clustering Systems
Shilong Dai, Yangjing Long

Abstract
Rooted phylogenetic networks model evolutionary histories in which tree-like descent is supplemented by reticulate events. A basic invariant of such a network is its hardwired clustering system, consisting of the descendant leaf sets of all vertices. We give a complete Hasse-diagram characterization of when a clustering system 𝒞 \mathcal{C} on a finite taxa set X X is the hardwired clustering system of a rooted level- k k network. For each non-trivial block B B of the Hasse diagram ℋ ⁡ [ 𝒞 ] \mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}] , we introduce a parameter μ ⁡ ( B ) \mu(B) , defined as the minimum size of a generating set for all overlap-intersections in B B . Our main theorem states that a level- k k realization exists if and only if μ ⁡ ( B ) ≤ k \mu(B)\leq k for every non-trivial block B B . The necessity direction shows that overlap-intersections force proper hybrid vertices in any realizing block. The sufficiency direction is constructive: starting from the Hasse diagram, we iteratively split redundant hybrid vertices, preserving the hardwired clustering system while reducing each block to the required level.

Introduction

Rooted phylogenetic networks provide a way to represent evolutionary
histories in which tree-like descent is supplemented by reticulate events
such as hybridization, recombination, or horizontal transfer; see, for
example,
HusonRuppScornavacca2011
;
HusonScornavacca2011
. One basic
object associated with a rooted network
N
is its hardwired clustering
system
C_{N}
, the family of all sets of descendant leaves of vertices of

N
. For rooted phylogenetic trees, the corresponding cluster hierarchy
determines the tree uniquely
SempleSteel2003
. For rooted networks,
this uniqueness fails in general
NakhlehWang2005
, but the hardwired
cluster set still records substantial structural information.

Bounded-level networks are a standard way to control the complexity of
reticulation: the level bounds, block by block, how many hybrid vertices may
occur in a single non-tree-like region. This gives a useful middle ground
between trees and unrestricted networks. In this setting, a level bound
formalizes the biological assumption that reticulate events are locally
limited, even when the whole evolutionary history is not tree-like. Related
algorithmic work controls reticulation complexity through decompositions or
explicit constructions

GusfieldBansalBafnaSong2007
;
WangGuoLiuEtAl2013
, and many bounded-level
results start from explicit network encodings such as triplets, trees, or
clusters
ToHabib2009
;
vanIerselKelkRuppHuson2010
;
GambetteHuber2012
.
Hardwired cluster realizability is subtler than the corresponding tree case,
and also less direct than many triplet- or split-based settings: a hardwired
cluster set records descendant leaf sets, but not the local ancestry by which
those sets are produced. In particular, Hasse-hybrid vertices may be
redundant, while overlap-intersections of clusters can force hybrid vertices
in every realization.

Several nearby lines of work address different aspects of this landscape.
Cluster networks give a canonical way to summarize a set of clusters

HusonRupp2008
, but they are not intended as a criterion for whether the
same cluster set has a realization of prescribed level. The constructions of
van Iersel et al. focus on representing conflicting clusters with fewer
reticulations
vanIerselKelkRuppHuson2010
, while Gambette and Huber
study encodings of bounded-level networks
GambetteHuber2012
. These
results show how bounded reticulation complexity can be represented or
achieved by explicit constructions. They do not give a necessary-and-sufficient
level-
k
cluster-realization test expressed solely in the Hasse diagram of an
arbitrary clustering system. Results on regular networks and related network
classes give uniqueness or reconstruction statements within restricted classes

BaroniSempleSteel2005
;
Willson2010
;
Zhang2019
; they likewise do not
provide the block-wise hardwired level criterion considered here.

This leads to the realizability problem considered in this paper: given a
clustering system
\mathcal{C}
on a finite taxa set
X
, when does there exist
a rooted level-
k
network
N
on
X
with
C_{N}=\mathcal{C}
? Our goal is to
answer this question directly from the clustering system itself, without
first constructing or guessing a realizing network.

The Hasse diagram
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
of a clustering system is a natural
starting point for this problem. The closest predecessor of our work is the
study of clustering systems of phylogenetic networks by Hellmuth, Schaller, and
Stadler
HellmuthSchallerStadler2023
. Their results show how structural
properties of networks are reflected in the induced clustering systems, compare
several well-studied classes of phylogenetic networks through their clusters,
and provide block and overlap tools that we use. However, their results do not
give a blockwise numerical invariant whose value is exactly the minimum level
required for realizing a given clustering system. The parameter
\mu(B)

introduced here fills this gap.

More recently, Shanavas, Changat, Hellmuth, and Stadler studied the connections
between clustering systems and unique least common ancestors in directed acyclic
graphs, relating several classes of set systems to LCA-type properties

ShanavasChangatHellmuthStadler2024
. Related developments in the

I
-LCA framework further investigate how DAGs can be transformed while
preserving relevant cluster/LCA structure
HellmuthLindeberg2026
. These
works are orthogonal to the present paper: they focus on LCA properties and
induced set-system axioms, whereas we focus on exact hardwired realization under
a level bound.

Thus, although existing results provide important structural insight, they do
not give, in the general setting considered here, a directly checkable
block-wise criterion for when
\mathcal{C}
is realizable by a rooted level-
k

network. In other words, one would like to replace the network-side condition
“each non-trivial block has at most
k
proper hybrid vertices” by a condition
that can be read directly from
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
.

The main contribution of this paper is a purely Hasse-theoretic criterion. For
every non-trivial block
B
of
H=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, we define a parameter

\mu(B)
using generating sets for intersections of overlapping clusters inside

B
. Intuitively,
\mu(B)
measures the minimum number of independent
overlap-intersection regions that any realizing network block must encode. Our
main theorem states that
\mathcal{C}
is realizable by a rooted level-
k
network
if and only if
\mu(B)\leq k
for every non-trivial block
B
of

\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
. Thus the obstruction to level-
k
realizability is
purely Hasse-theoretic. The necessity direction shows that these
overlap-intersection pieces force at least
\mu(B)
proper hybrid vertices in any
corresponding realizing block. The sufficiency direction is constructive: in
contrast to the existence and correspondence results above, it gives an explicit
procedure that transforms the Hasse diagram into a level-
k
realization without
changing the hardwired clustering system. Figure
1
summarizes
this route from the input clustering system to the level criterion.

Proof strategy.

The proof separates the two directions of the characterization. For necessity,
we show that every overlap-intersection inside a non-trivial Hasse block must
be represented, in any realizing network, by descendant clusters of proper
hybrid vertices in a corresponding network block. This gives the lower bound

\mu(B)\leq h(B_{N})
. For sufficiency, we start from the Hasse diagram and
iteratively split all redundant hybrid vertices. The split operation preserves
the hardwired clustering system, while a lexicographic descent argument shows
that the process terminates. In the final network, each block has at most

\mu(B)
proper hybrid vertices.

Two clusters

Definition 3

(Cluster family, overlap-intersections, and

3
Comparison with Known Parameters

We compare
\mu(B)
with standard network-side measures of reticulation
complexity. The key distinction is that
\mu(B)
is defined on a non-trivial
block
B
of the Hasse diagram
H=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, while hybrid number,
reticulation number, and level are defined only after a realizing network has
been chosen.

Let
B_{H}
be a non-trivial block of
H
, and let
N
be a rooted network with

C_{N}=\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
16
, the block
B_{H}
is
represented inside some non-trivial block
B_{N}
of
N
. The necessity argument
gives
\mu(B_{H})\leq h(B_{N})
. Thus
\mu(B_{H})
is a lower bound, depending only on
the clustering system, for the number of proper hybrid vertices required in any
realizing block. Intuitively, each overlap-intersection in
B_{H}
must be
explained in the network by descendant clusters of proper hybrid vertices; the
generating set measures how many such pieces are forced.

This inequality need not be an equality for a fixed realization. A realization
may contain redundant hybrid vertices that do not create new hardwired clusters,
and then one can have
\mu(B_{H})<h(B_{N})
. For instance, in Figure
2
, the Hasse diagram itself has

\mu(B_{1})=h(B_{1})=1
. A non-minimal realization could contain additional hybrid
vertices with the same descendant cluster, leaving the clustering system
unchanged but increasing
h(B_{N})
.

Nevertheless, the bound is sharp for level realizability.
Theorem
36
states that a level-
k
realization exists
exactly when
\mu(B)\leq k
for every non-trivial block
B
of
H
. Thus

\mu(B)
gives the correct blockwise threshold, even though arbitrary
realizations may contain more hybrid vertices than necessary.

If one uses the standard reticulation number

r(N):=\sum_{v\in V(N)}(\indeg_{N}(v)-1)
, then it measures excess indegree rather
than the number of hybrid vertices. Locally, for a non-trivial block
B_{N}
, one
may consider

r_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{N}):=\sum_{h\in H^{\times}(B_{N})}(\indeg_{N}(h)-1).

Since every proper hybrid vertex has indegree at least two, one has

h(B_{N})\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{N})
, and hence, for a realizing block associated
with
B_{H}
,
\mu(B_{H})\leq h(B_{N})\leq r_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{N})
. In binary settings the
last two quantities coincide locally, while in non-binary settings the
reticulation contribution may be larger.

The level of a network is a bound on
h(B_{N})
over all non-trivial blocks

B_{N}
of that network. Hence level is network-dependent. By contrast,
\mu(B)

is computed before constructing any network. The main theorem converts the
network-side condition
h(B_{N})\leq k
into the Hasse-diagram condition

\mu(B)\leq k
.

Finally,
\mu(B)
is a finite combinatorial optimization problem. One can
enumerate the overlapping pairs in
\mathcal{C}(B)
, compute

\mathcal{I}(B)=\{\,A\cap A^{\prime}\mid A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(B),\ A\text{ and }A^{\prime}\text{ overlap}\,\}
, and then search for a smallest subset

K\subseteq\mathcal{C}(B)
satisfying

I=\bigcup\{\,E\in K\mid E\subseteq I\,\}\qquad\text{for all }I\in\mathcal{I}(B).

We do not use any complexity classification here; the point is that
\mu(B)
is
determined entirely by the Hasse diagram.

In summary,
\mu(B)
turns a network-level requirement into a clustering-system
condition. It detects, directly from
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, how much
blockwise reticulation complexity is forced in any level-
k
realization. Thus

\mu(B)
should not be interpreted as the number of hybrid vertices already
visible in the Hasse diagram; rather, it counts the overlap-intersection
regions that cannot be avoided in any realization.

4
Necessity Tools

The lemmas in this section serve one purpose: they allow us to compare a
non-trivial block of the Hasse diagram with a non-trivial block in an arbitrary
realizing network. We first localize overlapping clusters inside common
network blocks, then show that an entire Hasse block can be represented inside
one fixed network block, and finally transfer a generating set from the network
block back to the Hasse block. This gives the lower bound

\mu(B)\leq h(B_{N})
, which is the key ingredient in the necessity proof.

4.1
Overlap and intersection tools inside non-trivial blocks

The first group of lemmas records how overlap information forces vertices
into non-trivial blocks and how overlap-intersections are represented by
proper hybrid vertices.

Lemma 4

(Common-descendant block lemma)
.

Let
N
be a rooted network. If
x,y\in V(N)
are incomparable and have a common descendant leaf, then
x
and
y
lie in a common non-trivial block of
N
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the root. Choose directed paths
R_{x}:\rho\leadsto x
and
R_{y}:\rho\leadsto y
, let
u
be their last common vertex, and let
P_{x}:u\leadsto x
and
P_{y}:u\leadsto y
be the corresponding terminal subpaths. Then
V(P_{x})\cap V(P_{y})=\{u\}
.

Let
a
be a common descendant leaf of
x
and
y
. Choose directed paths
S_{x}:x\leadsto a
and
S_{y}:y\leadsto a
, let
v
be their first common vertex, and let
Q_{x}:x\leadsto v
and
Q_{y}:y\leadsto v
be the corresponding initial subpaths. Then
V(Q_{x})\cap V(Q_{y})=\{v\}
.

Any further intersection of
P_{x}
with
Q_{x}
or of
P_{y}
with
Q_{y}
would create a directed cycle, and any intersection of
P_{x}
with
Q_{y}
or of
P_{y}
with
Q_{x}
would make
x
and
y
comparable. Hence
P_{x}\cup Q_{x}\cup Q_{y}\cup P_{y}
is a simple undirected cycle through
x
and
y
, so they lie in a common non-trivial block of
N
.
∎

Lemma 5

(Overlapping vertices lie in one block)
.

Let

be a rooted network. If

and the clusters

overlap, then

and

lie in the interior of a common non-trivial block of

.

Proof.

This is exactly Lemma 19 of
HellmuthSchallerStadler2023
.
∎

Lemma 6

(Interior vertices have overlap partners)
.

Let

, and let

be a non-trivial block of

has an overlap partner in

Lemma 7

(Block-intersection decomposition)
.

Let
N
be a rooted network, and let
B_{N}
be a non-trivial block of
N
. If
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(B_{N})
overlap, then there exists a subset

such that

A\cap A^{\prime}=\bigcup_{h\in H_{A,A^{\prime}}}\sigma(h).

Proof.

Choose
u,v\in V(B_{N})
with
\sigma(u)=A
and
\sigma(v)=A^{\prime}
. By Lemma 20 of
HellmuthSchallerStadler2023
, if

Since

and

overlap, the first two cases are impossible. Thus

. By Lemma 11 of

∎

4.2
Realizing a Hasse block inside one network block

We now relate non-trivial blocks of the Hasse diagram to non-trivial blocks of a realizing network. The next lemmas provide the technical ingredients needed to pass from an edge of a non-trivial block in the Hasse diagram to a corresponding non-trivial block in a realizing network.

Lemma 8

(Avoiding a fixed cut vertex: same-cluster representatives stay in one component)
.

Let
a\in V(N)
be a cut vertex, and write
A:=\sigma(a)
. If
C\neq A
, then all vertices
x\in V(N)
with
\sigma(x)=C
lie in the same connected component of
N-a
.

Proof.

Take
x,y\in V(N)
with
\sigma(x)=\sigma(y)=C\neq A
. If
x
and
y
are incomparable, then any leaf in
C
is a common descendant leaf of both, so Lemma
4
puts them in a common non-trivial block. Otherwise they are comparable; say

. Then every vertex on that path has cluster
C
, since clusters decrease along directed paths and agree at the endpoints. Because
C\neq A=\sigma(a)
, the path avoids
a
, so
x
and
y
remain connected in
N-a
.

If
x
and
y
lie in a common non-trivial block

, then either

, or
a\in V(B)
and
B-a
is connected because the underlying graph of

is
2
-connected. Thus

and
y
are connected in
N-a
in all cases.
∎

Lemma 9

(Connected-subgraph cycle criterion)
.

Let
G
be an undirected graph, let

, and let

be a connected subgraph of

with

. Then

contains a simple undirected cycle through

and
v
. Consequently,

and

lie in a common non-trivial block of

.

Proof.

Since

is connected and contains

and

, it contains a simple

–

path

. Because

, the edge

is not in

. Hence

is a simple cycle through

and

, so they lie in a common non-trivial block.
∎

Lemma 10

(Edge-to-block realization lemma)
.

Let
H=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, let
B_{H}
be a non-trivial block of
H
, and let
U\to V
be an edge of
B_{H}
. Let

be a rooted network with

. Then there exists a non-trivial block

of

and vertices

such that
\sigma(u)=U
and
\sigma(v)=V
.

Proof.

Choose
u_{0},v_{0}\in V(N)
with
\sigma(u_{0})=U
and
\sigma(v_{0})=V
. Since
U\to V
is a cover edge,

.

If
u_{0}
and
v_{0}
are incomparable, then every leaf in
V
is a common descendant leaf of both, so Lemma
4
implies that they lie in a common non-trivial block. Thus assume they are comparable. Since

, we must have

. Along a directed path

, every cluster lies between

and

or

by the cover property. So for some

with

and
\sigma(v_{1})=V
.

Case 1:

has an overlap partner in

.

Choose

and

, so there is an undirected path

in

from

to

.

Subcase 1a:

.

Again by Lemma

and

in

from

to

passes through

, then

; if

passes through

, then

. Otherwise

and

Subcase 1b:

does not overlap

Since

. Thus

are comparable. They cannot satisfy

.

If

and

are incomparable, then any leaf in

is a common descendant leaf of both, so Lemma

yields a non-trivial block

containing them and an undirected path

in

from

to

. If

passes through

, then

; if

passes through

, then

. Otherwise

is connected, contains

, and avoids

applies.

If
u_{1}
and

are comparable, then

is impossible because it would imply

. Hence

; let

be such a path. It cannot pass through

, since that would force

passes through

, then

; otherwise

is connected, contains

, and avoids

Case 2:

has no overlap partner in

By Lemma

. Since also

, the vertex

is terminal in

. Lemma

therefore gives another parent

in

. Then

and

Choose

with

and

lie in the interior of a common non-trivial block

, so there is an undirected path

in

to

If

The next lemmas upgrade this edgewise realization to a blockwise realization by showing that the vertices of a non-trivial block of the Hasse diagram can be realized inside one fixed non-trivial block of the network.

Lemma 11

(Block-cut tree connectivity after deleting a cut vertex)
.

Let

be a connected undirected graph, and let

be a cut vertex of

be the block-cut tree of

.

Then, for every block

of

.

be two blocks of

and

.

First,

is connected for every block

, this is just

; if

and

is connected because

For

, let

be a path in

, where each

is connected in

, and consecutive ones intersect in

, so their union is connected. Hence

and

lie in the same connected component of

.

For

, choose

and

. If

, then

; if also

, then

For each edge

be the unique block containing it. Whenever

, the common vertex

is a cut vertex different from

gives a walk in

to

or

or

contains a walk from

(Cycle edge-block uniqueness)
.

Let

be a rooted network with

Suppose not. Then

contains a cut-vertex-node corresponding to some cut vertex

Fix

and

If

Lemma 16

(Realizing block lemma)
.

4.3
Pulling network generators back to the Hasse diagram

The next lemmas complete the necessity direction. They show that a
generating set in a realizing network block can be converted into a
generating set inside the corresponding Hasse block, without increasing its
size.

Lemma 17

(Hybrid vertices bound
\mu(B_{N})
)
.

For every non-trivial block

of a rooted network

, one has

\mu(B_{N})\leq h(B_{N}).

Proof.

Let

Then

.

Let

, say

with

overlapping. By Lemma

, there exists

lies in

Hence

∎

Lemma 18

(First-exit cut vertex lemma)
.

Lemma 19

(Cut-vertex localization)
.

Let
B_{H}
be a non-trivial block of

, and let

be a chosen non-trivial block of
N
furnished by Lemma
16
. Let

be a minimum generating set of

, so

. Define

Then there exists a map

such that for every

and every

with

Fix

. If

, set

; then

for every

with

.

Assume now that

. Since

, there exists
I_{0}=A_{0}\cap A_{0}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}(B_{H})
with
A_{0},A_{0}^{\prime}\in V(B_{H})
overlapping and
E\subseteq I_{0}\subseteq A_{0}
. As
E\in K_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(B_{N})
, the set

is a cluster in

, hence a vertex of

. Therefore there is a directed downward path in

from

to

, so

lies in the same connected component of the underlying graph of

as

.

Let
\Xi(E)
be the unique node of the block-cut tree of

containing
E
: if
E
is a cut vertex,
\Xi(E)
is the corresponding cut-vertex-node; otherwise it is the unique block-node containing

. Since

. Define
\tau(E)
to be the first cut vertex after the block-node

on the unique path from

to

Let

and

in

5
Splitting Construction

The sufficiency direction is constructive. The Hasse diagram itself realizes
the given clustering system, but its non-trivial blocks may contain too many
proper hybrid vertices. The construction removes redundant hybrid vertices by
splitting them into several copies. Since all copies have the same descendant
leaf set as the original vertex, the hardwired clustering system is unchanged.
The main difficulty is to ensure that such local modifications do not create
uncontrolled reticulation in other blocks. To control this process, we
distinguish the hybrid vertices that are allowed to remain, namely those whose
original labels lie in the chosen generating set
K_{B}
, from the bad hybrid
vertices that must be split.

5.1
Setting up the splitting construction

Running convention.

Every vertex in a current network carries an
original Hasse-label

\lambda(v)\in V(H)
. When a vertex
u
is split, all new copies inherit the
same label:
\lambda(u_{i})=\lambda(u)
. A vertex with original label
x
is
called a
current copy
of
x
.

Whenever the construction starts from
N_{0}=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, we identify each inclusion-minimal vertex
\{x\}
with the taxon
x
; equivalently, we relabel each leaf
\{x\}
by
x
while keeping its original Hasse-label
\{x\}
. This does not change descendant clusters, so the resulting rooted network still has clustering system
\mathcal{C}
, and we continue to denote it by
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
.

Let
N
be a rooted network, and let
u
be a non-root, non-leaf vertex of
N
with parents
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
and children
w_{1},\dots,w_{t}
. The
split
of
u
is the directed graph obtained from
N
by deleting
u
, adding new vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
, and inserting the edges
p_{i}\to u_{i}
and
u_{i}\to w_{j}
for all
i,j
. Starting from the initial network
N_{0}
above, any digraph obtained after finitely many such splits is called a
current network
; Lemma
23
shows that every current network is again a rooted network with the same clustering system. For an original non-trivial block
B
of
H=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
and a vertex
x\in V(B)
, let
d_{B}(x)
be the length of a longest directed path in
B
from
\max B
to
x
; this is the
depth
of
x
in the original block
B
.

Definition 20

(Owner block of an original label)
.

Let
x\in V(\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}])
. If there exists an original non-trivial block
B
of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
such that
x\in V(B)\setminus\{\max B\}
, then
B
is the
owner block
of
x
, denoted by
\operatorname{own}(x)
.

The next statements fix the basic bookkeeping for the splitting construction, including owner blocks, generating sets, and the behavior of leaves and bad hybrid vertices.

Observation

(Owner block is unique)
.

If an original vertex
x
of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
is a proper vertex of some original non-trivial block, then
\operatorname{own}(x)
is well defined and unique.

Proof.

If
x
is not a cut vertex of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, then it lies in a unique block, so the claim is immediate.

Assume that
x
is a cut vertex, and let
T
be the rooted block-cut tree of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
. Let
B
be a non-trivial block with
x\in V(B)\setminus\{\max B\}
. If
B
were a child block of the cut-vertex-node
x
in
T
, then every path from the root of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
to a vertex of
B
would enter

through
x
, so every vertex of
B
would lie below
x
, forcing
x=\max B
, a contradiction. Thus
B
is the parent block of
x
in
T
, and such a block is unique.
∎

Definition 21

(Bad hybrid vertices)
.

Fix, for every non-trivial block
B
of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, a minimum generating set
K_{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(B)
with
|K_{B}|=\mu(B)
. Let
N
be a current network, and let
\lambda(v)
denote the original Hasse-label of a vertex
v\in V(N)
. For a fixed original non-trivial block
B
, define

H_{B}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N):=\{\,v\in V(N)\mid\lambda(v)\in\mathcal{C}(B)\setminus(K_{B}\cup\{\max B\}),\ \indeg(v)\geq 2\,\}.

Vertices in
H_{B}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
are called bad hybrid vertices relative to
B
.

Every vertex
v\in H_{B}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
satisfies
\lambda(v)\in V(B)\setminus\{\max B\}
, so
the owner-block observation
gives
\operatorname{own}(\lambda(v))=B
. In particular, a current vertex belongs to at most one set

, and for such a vertex the depth
d_{B}(\lambda(v))
is well defined.

Lemma 22

(Singletons in a non-trivial block of the Hasse diagram lie in every generating set)
.

Let
B
be a non-trivial block of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, and let
\{x\}\in V(B)
. Then every generating set
K_{B}
of
B
satisfies
\{x\}\in K_{B}
.

Proof.

Since
\{x\}
is a singleton, it cannot equal
\max B
, and since it has no proper subset in
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, it is terminal in
B
. By Lemma
2
, there exist distinct parents
U,W\in V(B)
with
U\to\{x\}
and
W\to\{x\}
. They cannot be comparable, or one of these edges would fail to be a cover edge, so
U
and
W
overlap. As
\{x\}\subseteq U\cap W
and
\{x\}
is a singleton, we have
U\cap W=\{x\}
, hence
\{x\}\in\mathcal{I}(B)
.

If
K_{B}
is any generating set of
B
, then

\{x\}=\bigcup\{\,E\in K_{B}\mid E\subseteq\{x\}\,\}.

Every nonempty subset of
\{x\}
equals
\{x\}
, so
\{x\}\in K_{B}
.
∎

Observation

(Current leaves are exactly copies of singleton clusters)
.

In every current network obtained during the construction, a vertex
v
is a leaf if and only if its original label
\lambda(v)
is a singleton cluster.

Proof.

We argue by induction on the number of performed splits. Initially, the current network is
N_{0}=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
, and by the above convention its leaves are exactly the singleton clusters.

Now assume the claim holds for a current network
N
, and let
N^{\prime}
be obtained by splitting a bad hybrid vertex
u\in H_{B}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
for some original non-trivial block
B
. Since
\lambda(u)\in\mathcal{C}(B)\setminus(K_{B}\cup\{\max B\})
, Lemma
22
shows that
\lambda(u)
is not a singleton; by the induction hypothesis,
u
is therefore not a leaf.

Every old vertex distinct from
u
keeps the same leaf/non-leaf status after the split: only edges incident with
u
are changed, and each parent of
u
loses the edge to
u
but gains an edge to one copy of
u
. The old vertex
u
is replaced by copies
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
, each having the same children as
u
, so none of them is a leaf. Since each
u_{i}
still carries the non-singleton label
\lambda(u)
, the stated equivalence is preserved. Hence it holds for every current network.
∎

Consequently, every bad hybrid vertex can be split. Indeed, if

u\in H_{B}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
, then
\lambda(u)\in\mathcal{C}(B)\setminus(K_{B}\cup\{\max B\})
, so Lemma
22
implies that

\lambda(u)
is not a singleton. By
the leaf-label observation
,
u
is
not a leaf; since it is hybrid, it is not the root.

The next statements record the structural invariants of the splitting operation and the resulting control on how newly created bad hybrid vertices can occur.

Lemma 23

(Split preserves rooted networks and clusters)
.

Let
N
be a rooted network, and let
u
be a non-root, non-leaf vertex of
N
with parents
p_{1},\dots,p_{r}
and children
w_{1},\dots,w_{t}
, where
r\geq 1
and
t\geq 1
. Construct
N^{\prime}
from
N
by deleting
u
, adding new vertices
u_{1},\dots,u_{r}
, and adding edges

p_{i}\to u_{i}\qquad(1\leq i\leq r),

u_{i}\to w_{j}\qquad(1\leq i\leq r,\ 1\leq j\leq t).

Then:

(i)

N^{\prime}
is again a rooted network.

(ii)

The root of
N^{\prime}
is the same as the root of
N
.

(iii)

The leaf set of
N^{\prime}
is the same as the leaf set of
N
.

(iv)

C_{N^{\prime}}=C_{N}
.

Proof.

Let
\rho
be the root of
N
.

First,
N^{\prime}
is acyclic. Indeed, any directed cycle in
N^{\prime}
must contain some new vertex
u_{i}
, since
N
is acyclic. Along such a cycle, each occurrence of
u_{i}
appears in a segment
p_{i}\to u_{i}\to w_{j}
. Replacing every such segment by
p_{i}\to u\to w_{j}
produces a closed directed walk in
N
, hence a directed cycle in
N
, a contradiction.

Since
u
is not the root, the root
\rho
is not deleted. Each new vertex
u_{i}
has indegree
1
, and every old vertex except possibly the children of
u
keeps positive indegree if it had it before. Thus no new root is created, and
\rho
remains the unique indegree-
0
vertex.

Every old vertex distinct from
u
remains reachable from
\rho
: if a path from
\rho
to that vertex used
u
, replace each segment
p_{i}\to u\to w_{j}
by
p_{i}\to u_{i}\to w_{j}
. Each new vertex
u_{i}
is reachable from
\rho
through
p_{i}
. Hence
N^{\prime}
is a rooted network with root
\rho
.

The leaf set is unchanged. Since
u
is not a leaf, deleting it removes no leaf. Each new vertex
u_{i}
has outdegree

, so none is a leaf. No old leaf gains an outgoing edge. Thus
N
and
N^{\prime}
have the same leaf set.

Finally, the clustering system is unchanged. For each new vertex
u_{i}
, the descendants below
u_{i}
are exactly those below
u
, so
\sigma_{N^{\prime}}(u_{i})=\sigma_{N}(u)
. For an old vertex
x\neq u
, any directed path from
x
to a leaf in
N
can be converted to one in
N^{\prime}
by replacing segments
p_{i}\to u\to w_{j}
with
p_{i}\to u_{i}\to w_{j}
, and conversely any path in
N^{\prime}
can be converted back by replacing
p_{i}\to u_{i}\to w_{j}
with
p_{i}\to u\to w_{j}
. Hence
\sigma_{N^{\prime}}(x)=\sigma_{N}(x)
for every old vertex
x\neq u
. Therefore every cluster of
N^{\prime}
is already a cluster of
N
, and every cluster of
N
is realized in
N^{\prime}
, so
C_{N^{\prime}}=C_{N}
.
∎

Observation

(Current-edge projection)
.

Let
N
be a current network obtained during the construction. If

is an edge of

, then
\lambda(a)\to\lambda(b)

.

We argue by induction on the number of performed splits. The claim is immediate for the initial network

.

Assume it holds for a current network

, and let

be obtained by splitting a vertex

with parents

and children

. Let

be an edge of

. If it already occurs in

, the induction hypothesis applies. Otherwise it is either

or

. In the first case,

was an edge of

, so

∎

(Parent-label propagation)
.

Let

be any original vertex of

, and let

5.2
Local behavior of bad hybrid vertices under splitting

of

Lemma 25

(New bad hybrids stay in descendant blocks)
.

Definition 26

(Current support of an original non-trivial block)
.

Let

Observation

5.3
Cut vertices are never split

The next lemma isolates a key constraint on hybrid cut vertices in the original Hasse diagram, which will later ensure that such vertices are never split in the construction.

Lemma 27

(Hybrid cut vertices lie in every generating set)
.

Let

be a hybrid cut vertex of the original Hasse diagram

. Let

be the unique original block such that

Proof.

By Lemmas 9–11 of

, a hybrid vertex is a proper vertex of exactly one non-trivial block, so

is uniquely determined.

Choose two distinct direct parents

of

in
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
. Since

is the unique parent block of the cut-vertex-node

in the rooted block-cut tree, so every direct parent of

lies in
B_{H}^{\uparrow}

.

. Since

containing

. Because

is the unique non-trivial block with

is a child block of the cut-vertex-node

in the rooted block-cut tree. Thus

contains a vertex below

; choose a child

below

P_{1}:\ Z\to a^{\prime}\rightsquigarrow a.

Since

and

is generated by

, there exists

with

. As

.

We claim that

is impossible. Indeed, if

and

and

are covering edges. Hence either

or

overlaps

that avoids

lies above

; if

Next we show that

does not re-enter

after leaving

. Otherwise let

be the first later vertex of

that lies in

. Then the segment

has both endpoints in

and all internal vertices outside

, while containing

, contradicting Lemma

. So

does not re-enter
B_{H}^{\uparrow}
.

Let

be the last vertex of

lying in

; such a vertex exists because

. Let

be the first common vertex of

Hence the union of

and the reverse of

stays outside

Moreover,

The next statements show that the splitting process remains localized within the original block structure and preserves the constraints needed for the subsequent analysis of cut vertices.

Lemma 28

(Hybrid cut vertices are never split)
.

Let

.

Observation

(Unique parents persist under splitting)
.

Let

be an original non-hybrid vertex of

, and let

be its unique

The next statements analyze the behavior of original cut vertices under the splitting process and show that the separations induced by such vertices persist throughout the construction.

6
Termination and Block Control

It remains to prove that the splitting process stops and that it does not
destroy the block-wise control needed for the level bound. The key observation
is that new bad hybrid vertices can only appear deeper in the same original
block or in descendant blocks of the original block-cut tree. Therefore, if
bad hybrid vertices are always split in lexicographic order, the process admits
a finite descent measure. Once no bad hybrid vertices remain, every proper
hybrid vertex in a current block has a label belonging to the prescribed
generating set
K_{B}
, giving the desired level bound.

6.1
Block refinement and termination of the construction

Lemma 33

(No proper copy of

, every Hasse
path from
V(B)
to
\lambda(y)
passes through
Z
. Since
\lambda(u)
is not a cut vertex,

\lambda(u_{i})=\lambda(u)
and
\lambda(y)
lie in different components of

\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]-Z
.

The cut vertex
Z
is never split, and Lemma
32
preserves this
separation in
N^{\prime}
. Thus
u_{i}
and
y
lie in different components of
N^{\prime}-Z
. But a non-trivial
block containing both vertices cannot be separated by deleting a single vertex. This contradiction
shows
D^{\prime}\subseteq S
, proving (2).

For (3), affected non-trivial blocks are handled by (2). Any other non-trivial block of
N^{\prime}

uses only old vertices and old edges with unchanged incidence, hence is contained in a non-trivial
block of
N
and therefore, by the induction hypothesis in (3), in some old support

N\langle D\rangle\subseteq N^{\prime}\langle D\rangle
.
∎

The final statements establish the counting control needed for the splitting process and deduce that the construction terminates after finitely many steps.

Observation

(A current vertex has at most one child of each original label)
.

Let
N
be a current network obtained during the construction, let
q\in V(N)
, and let

x\in V(\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}])
. Then
q
has at most one child
v\in V(N)
with

\lambda(v)=x
.

Proof.

Induct on the number of splits. Initially each original label occurs once. When a vertex
u
is
split, an old parent of
u
replaces its single edge to
u
by a single edge to the corresponding
copy of
u
, and each new copy of
u
receives exactly the old child set of
u
. All other
outgoing edge sets are unchanged. The induction hypothesis therefore preserves the bound for
every original label.
∎

Lemma 35

(Global termination)
.

Let
B_{1},\dots,B_{s}
be the original non-trivial blocks of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
,
enumerated in a topological order of the original block-tree, that is, whenever

B_{j}
is a descendant of
B_{i}
in the block-tree, one has
i<j
.

For every original vertex
x\in V(\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}])
, let
\nu(x)
denote the number of
directed paths from the root
X
of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
to
x
.

Since no original cut vertex is ever split (Lemma
31
), every bad
hybrid vertex has an original label that is a proper vertex of a unique original non-trivial block. For
every bad hybrid vertex
v
in a current network, define its key by

\kappa(v):=\bigl(i,d_{B_{i}}(\lambda(v))\bigr),\qquad\text{where }\operatorname{own}(\lambda(v))=B_{i}.

For each pair
(i,\ell)
with
1\leq i\leq s
and
0\leq\ell\leq h_{i}
, where

h_{i}:=\max\{\,d_{B_{i}}(x):x\in V(B_{i})\,\},

define

a_{i,\ell}(N):=\bigl|\{\,v\in H_{B_{i}}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)\mid d_{B_{i}}(\lambda(v))=\ell\,\}\bigr|.

Let
\Theta(N)
be the vector obtained by listing all coordinates
a_{i,\ell}(N)
in
lexicographic order of the keys
(i,\ell)
.

If at each step one chooses a bad hybrid vertex of lexicographically minimal key and splits it,
then the process terminates after finitely many steps.

Proof.

Let
N^{\prime}
be obtained from
N
by one step of the procedure, so we split a bad hybrid vertex

u\in H_{B_{i}}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
of lexicographically minimal key

\kappa(u)=(i,d).

By Lemma
25
, new bad hybrid vertices can occur only in
B_{i}
or in
descendant blocks of
B_{i}
; and within
B_{i}
, Lemma
24
puts them at depth
strictly larger than
d
. Hence no coordinate of
\Theta
before
(i,d)
changes, and no new bad
hybrid vertex of key
(i,d)
is created. The split removes
u
, so

a_{i,d}(N^{\prime})=a_{i,d}(N)-1
, while only later coordinates may change. Thus

\Theta(N^{\prime})<_{\mathrm{lex}}\Theta(N)
.

It remains to show that the coordinates of
\Theta(N)
are uniformly bounded. For a current network

N
and an original vertex
x\in V(\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}])
, write

m_{N}(x):=\bigl|\{\,v\in V(N)\mid\lambda(v)=x\,\}\bigr|.

We claim that
m_{N}(x)\leq\nu(x)
for every original vertex
x
. Induct over the Hasse diagram.
The root has one current copy. For
x\neq X
, choose one parent for each current copy of
x
.
The parent-label observation puts these chosen parents over original parents of
x
, and the
fixed-label child observation makes the choice map injective. Hence

m_{N}(x)\leq\sum_{p\in\operatorname{Par}_{\mathcal{H}}(x)}m_{N}(p)
. By induction and by the
corresponding decomposition of directed paths ending with
p\to x
, this is at most

\sum_{p}\nu(p)=\nu(x)
.

Consequently, for every
i
and every
\ell
,

0\leq a_{i,\ell}(N)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V(B_{i})\\
d_{B_{i}}(x)=\ell\end{subarray}}m_{N}(x)\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}x\in V(B_{i})\\
d_{B_{i}}(x)=\ell\end{subarray}}\nu(x).

Thus each coordinate of
\Theta(N)
ranges over a fixed finite set. A strictly descending
lexicographic sequence of such finite vectors is finite, so the process terminates.
∎

7
Proof of the Main Theorem

We now combine the preceding ingredients. The necessity direction uses the
block-realization lemmas to show that overlap-intersections force proper hybrid
vertices in every level-
k
realization. The sufficiency direction uses the
splitting construction to transform the Hasse diagram into a realization in
which the only proper hybrid vertices that remain in each block are labelled by
members of the prescribed generating set.

Theorem 36

(Characterization)
.

Let
X
be a finite nonempty set, let
\mathcal{C}
be a clustering system on
X
, and let

H:=\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}].

Then there exists a rooted level-
k
network
N
such that

C_{N}=\mathcal{C}

if and only if every non-trivial block
B
of
H
satisfies

\mu(B)\leq k.

Proof.

Necessity.

Assume that there exists a rooted level-
k
network
N
with
C_{N}=\mathcal{C}
, and let
B_{H}
be a non-trivial block of
H
.

If
\mathcal{I}(B_{H})=\varnothing
, then
\mu(B_{H})=0\leq k
by definition. So assume
\mathcal{I}(B_{H})\neq\varnothing
.

By Lemma
16
, there exists a non-trivial block
B_{N}
of
N
such that every cluster in
V(B_{H})
is realized by some vertex of
B_{N}
. Fix such a block
B_{N}
. Let
K_{N}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(B_{N})
be a minimum generating set of
B_{N}
, so
|K_{N}|=\mu(B_{N})
. By Lemma
17
,
\mu(B_{N})\leq h(B_{N})
, and since
N
is level-
k
, we have
h(B_{N})\leq k
. Hence

|K_{N}|=\mu(B_{N})\leq h(B_{N})\leq k.

Define

S_{B_{H}}:=\{\,E\in K_{N}\mid\exists I\in\mathcal{I}(B_{H})\text{ with }E\subseteq I\,\}.

By Lemma
19
, there exists a map

\tau:S_{B_{H}}\to V(B_{H})

such that for every
E\in S_{B_{H}}
and every
I\in\mathcal{I}(B_{H})
with
E\subseteq I
, one has

E\subseteq\tau(E)\subseteq I.

Set
K_{B_{H}}:=\{\tau(E)\mid E\in S_{B_{H}}\}\subseteq V(B_{H})
. Then

|K_{B_{H}}|\leq|S_{B_{H}}|\leq|K_{N}|\leq k.

We show that
K_{B_{H}}
is a generating set of
B_{H}
. Let
I=A\cap A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{I}(B_{H})
, where
A,A^{\prime}\in V(B_{H})
overlap. Since
A
and
A^{\prime}
are both realized in
B_{N}
, we have
A,A^{\prime}\in\mathcal{C}(B_{N})
, and therefore
I\in\mathcal{I}(B_{N})
. Because
K_{N}
generates
B_{N}
,

I=\bigcup\{\,E\in K_{N}\mid E\subseteq I\,\}.

For every
E\in K_{N}
with
E\subseteq I
, localization gives

E\subseteq\tau(E)\subseteq I
and
\tau(E)\in K_{B_{H}}
. Hence the union on the right is contained
in
\bigcup\{\,D\in K_{B_{H}}\mid D\subseteq I\,\}
, while the latter union is plainly contained in

I
. Therefore

I=\bigcup\{\,D\in K_{B_{H}}\mid D\subseteq I\,\},

and
K_{B_{H}}
is a generating set of
B_{H}
. Thus
\mu(B_{H})\leq|K_{B_{H}}|\leq k
. Since
B_{H}
was arbitrary, every non-trivial block of
H
satisfies
\mu(B_{H})\leq k
.

Sufficiency.

Assume conversely that every non-trivial block
B
of
H
satisfies
\mu(B)\leq k
. For each such block, choose a minimum generating set
K_{B}\subseteq\mathcal{C}(B)
with
|K_{B}|=\mu(B)\leq k
.

Start with the relabeled Hasse diagram described in the initial-network convention and set
N_{0}:=H
. Then
N_{0}
is a rooted network on leaf set
X
, and
C_{N_{0}}=\mathcal{C}
.

Iteratively do the following: if some original block
B_{i}
satisfies
H_{B_{i}}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)\neq\varnothing
, choose a bad hybrid vertex of lexicographically minimal key as in Lemma
35
, equivalently choose the smallest index
i
with
H_{B_{i}}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)\neq\varnothing
and then a vertex
u\in H_{B_{i}}^{\mathrm{bad}}(N)
of minimum depth in
B_{i}
, and split
u
.

By Lemma
31
, whenever a vertex
u
is split, its original label
\lambda(u)
is not a cut vertex of the original Hasse diagram. Hence Lemma
34
applies at every step. By repeated application of Lemma
23
, every intermediate graph is a rooted network with leaf set
X
and clustering system
\mathcal{C}
. By Lemma
35
, the process stops after finitely many steps. Let
N^{\ast}
be the resulting network. Then

C_{N^{\ast}}=C_{N_{0}}=\mathcal{C}.

It remains to show that
N^{\ast}
is level-
k
. First, every non-trivial block of
N^{\ast}
is contained in
N^{\ast}\langle B\rangle
for some original non-trivial block
B
of
H
. This is true for
N_{0}=H
, since its non-trivial blocks are exactly the original ones, and the induction step is Lemma
34
(3).

Now let
D
be any non-trivial block of
N^{\ast}
, and choose
B
with

D\subseteq N^{\ast}\langle B\rangle
. If
w
is a proper hybrid vertex of
D
, then

\lambda(w)\in V(B)
and, because the construction has stopped,

\lambda(w)\in K_{B}\cup\{\max B\}
. Lemma
33
excludes

\lambda(w)=\max B
, so
\lambda(w)\in K_{B}
.

No label in
K_{B}
is ever split: cut vertices are excluded by
Lemma
31
, and a non-cut label in
K_{B}
belongs only to the block

B
, where it is not bad. Since labels initially have one copy and new copies arise only by
splitting that label, each element of
K_{B}
has a unique current copy in
N^{\ast}
. Thus distinct
proper hybrid vertices of
D
have distinct labels in
K_{B}
, and their number is at most

|K_{B}|=\mu(B)\leq k.

After termination, the only proper hybrid vertices that may remain in a block
are those labelled by elements of the chosen generating set
K_{B}
. Since
labels in
K_{B}
are never split, each such label contributes at most one
proper hybrid vertex. Since
D
was arbitrary, every non-trivial block of

N^{\ast}
has at most
k
proper hybrid vertices. Hence
N^{\ast}
is a
rooted level-
k
network with
C_{N^{\ast}}=\mathcal{C}
.
∎

Corollary 37

(Minimum level)
.

Let
\ell(\mathcal{C})
denote the minimum level among all rooted networks

N
with
C_{N}=\mathcal{C}
. Then

\ell(\mathcal{C})=\max\{\mu(B):B\text{ is a non-trivial block of }\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]\}.

Proof.

This is immediate from Theorem
36
: a level-
k

realization exists exactly for those integers
k
that dominate all values

\mu(B)
over the non-trivial blocks of
\mathcal{H}[\mathcal{C}]
.
∎
