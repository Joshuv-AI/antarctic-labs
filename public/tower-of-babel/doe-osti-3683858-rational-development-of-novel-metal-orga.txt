2. EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

The objective of this project was to rationally develop solubility-selective mixed matrix
membranes comprising highly polar rubbery polymers and soluble metal-organic polyhedra
(MOPs) to achieve high CO2 permeance (3000 GPU), high CO2/N2 selectivity (50) and CO2/O2
selectivity (20). This project comprised two budget periods (BPs).

During BP 1, we identified mixed matrix materials (MMMs) with excellent CO2 permeability and
CO2/N2 and CO2/O2 selectivity.
1. We developed MMMs containing polyPDXLA8, PEGDME240, and nanocages with
 separation properties showing CO2 permeability of >1000 Barrer, CO2/N2 selectivity of >75,
   and CO2/O2 selectivity of 23-28 at 21 – 25 ℃.
2. We designed and synthesized amorphous polyethers with high O:C ratios containing branches
 with various chain-end groups (such as OH, OC2H5, -OCOCH3, and –OSO2C7H7). The effect
 of polymer compositions and temperature on CO2/N2 and CO2/O2 separation properties was
 determined.
3. We scaled up the synthesis of polymers up to 10 - 50 g/batch and MOPs up to 5 g/batch.
4. We developed a computational model to simulate gas solubility, diffusivity, and permeability
 in functional polymers. This model was used for hypothetical polymers with various
 functional groups.
5. We developed thin-film composite (TFC) membranes with CO2 permeance of 640 – 2500
 GPU and CO2/N2 selectivity of 43 – 30 at 35 ℃, and the membranes demonstrated stability in
 the presence of water.

During BP2, we focused on converting the MMMs identified in BP1 into transformational TFC
membranes and highlighted the achievement as below.
1. We successfully prepared TFC membranes meeting the target (CO2 permeance of 3000 GPU
 and CO2/N2 selectivity of 50), though the reproducibility needs to be improved. The
 membranes exhibit the best combination of CO2 permeance and CO2/N2 selectivity for non-
 facilitated transport membranes reported in the literature.
2. We successfully scaled up the synthesis of the polymers to 10-20 g/batch. One polymer
 (pPDXLAc12) exhibits a CO2 permeability of 930 Barrer and a CO2/N2 selectivity of 53,
 which is promising for preparing membranes that meet the objectives.
3. We successfully scaled up the synthesis of the MOPs to 10 – 20 g/batch.
4. We successfully built models and tools to simulate gas sorption and diffusion in polymers
 containing various polar groups (including ether oxygen) and chain-end groups.

Despite the significant progress made in polymer synthesis and membrane separation
performance, we did not have sufficient time to optimize roll-to-roll fabrication of TFC
membranes.

Task 4. Develop Simulation Models and Simulate Gas Permeation in Polyethers and MOPs

Simulation of gas solubility. We have successfully developed modeling capacity to describe gas
solubility, diffusivity, and permeability in polymers with various chemical functional groups. To
calculate the gas solubility, we use the group contribution (GC) method based on the perturbed-
chain statistical associating fluid theory equation of state (PC-SAFT EOS). The PC-SAFT EOS
is commonly used for polymers with high molecular weights because it can easily model
industrially relevant bulk properties under different conditions. By applying simple combination
rules for chemical functional groups, the GC method allows calculation of the properties of any
molten polymer once the chemical structure is given, without adjustable parameters.

Table 8 shows the calculated CO2/N2 and CO2/O2 solubility selectivity (SS) for five different
polymer materials we have studied (PE: polyethylene, PTMO: polytetramethylene oxide, PEO:
polyethylene oxide, PDXLA: poly(1,3-dioxolane) acrylate, and POM: polyoxymethylene). Our
results generally agree with experimental data for PE, PEO, and PTMO, and predict higher SS
for the two new candidate polymers, PDXLA and POM. The calculated as well the
experimentally measured CO2/N2 SS appears to be a linearly increasing function of the O:C ratio
on the semi-log. This suggests a linear relationship in the solvation energy difference between
CO2 and N2 with the O:C ratio. This behavior is not observed with CO2/O2 SS as shown in Table
8 and Fig. 4.2a. Surprisingly, the calculated CO2/O2 SS for all polymers are very similar and
noticeably lower than the CO2/N2 SS.

Task 9. Simulate Gas Transport Properties in Polymers Containing Various Functional
Groups

We have studied five polymer materials (PE: polyethylene (O:C = 0), PTMO:
polytetramethylene oxide (O:C = 0.25), PEO: poly(ethylene oxide) (O:C = 0.5), PDXLA:
poly(1,3-dioxolane) acrylate (O:C = 0.67), and POM: polyoxymethylene (O:C = 1.0)) to
investigate the effect of O:C ratio on CO2 separation from N2 and O2. We calculated solubility,
diffusivity, and permeability selectivity for five polymers in our library. Fig. 27a shows a clear
difference in solubility selectivity for the two gas pairs. Increasing O:C ratio greatly enhances the
CO2/N2 solubility selectivity, but only slightly increases the CO2/O2 solubility selectivity. As
noted in Fig. 27b, CO2/N2 diffusivity selectivity slightly increases with the O:C ratio. The trend
in diffusivity selectivity suggests that, except for POM, the polymers in our library show lower
CO2/O2 diffusivity selectivity compared to CO2/N2 diffusivity selectivity. Lastly, we showed the
permeability selectivity (product of diffusivity and solubility selectivity) in Fig. 27c. POM has
higher permeability selectivity for CO2/N2 due to its high solubility selectivity; likewise, it has
higher permeability selectivity for CO2/O2 due to its high diffusivity selectivity.

Next, we investigated the role of temperature on gas diffusion in the POM polymer by gradually
increasing the temperature. We calculated the CO2, N2, and O2 diffusivity in POM at
temperatures ranging from 300 to 400 K. Fig. 28a shows the CO2 diffusivity in POM at
temperatures from 300 to 400 K. CO2 diffusion increases with increasing temperature. The
change in gas diffusivity with increasing temperature fits an Arrhenius expression with R2 values
above 0.99. An increase in gas diffusion is not surprising, since increasing temperature increases
the mobility of the polymer chain segments as well as the gas molecules. This holds for all three
gases (CO2, N2, and O2). On the other hand, both CO2/N2 and CO2/O2 diffusivity selectivity
decreased with increasing temperature. At higher temperatures, all three gases diffuse faster, and
the POM loses its selective interactions with CO2, thus resulting in lower diffusivity selectivity.

To understand the gas diffusion mechanism, we calculated the radial distribution function of CO2
and N2 in the POM polymer. Fig. 29a shows that the probability of finding a CO2 molecule at the

end of the chain is the same as that of the rest of the polymer chain. To increase the gas diffusion

Task 10. Scale-up of Optimized Membrane for Roll-to-Roll Production at Bench-Scale

UB synthesized six batches of pPDXLAc8 and pPDXLAc12 (with a total of ~50 grams) for
coating trials at MTR. Each coating solution was then coated onto a substrate comprising a gutter
layer and porous support on nonwoven paper, using MTR’s R&D-scale roll-to-roll coating
equipment. The R&D coater can coat membrane rolls up to ~0.30 m wide and allows
optimization of primary development variables, including coating line speed, tension, solution
concentration (viscosity), and drying temperature. The optimized parameters developed on the
