On generalized canonical bundle formula and boundedness of complements in complex analytic setting
Kenta Hashizume

Abstract
. We establish the generalized canonical bundle formula for generalized lc-trivial fibrations with irrational coefficients over non-compact bases in the complex analytic setting, and we show that the discriminant b-divisor and moduli b-divisor are compatible with restriction to arbitrary open subsets. We also discuss the boundedness of complements in this setting.

Primary: 14D06, Secondary: 14J40, 14C20, 32C15

1.
Introduction

In this paper, we establish the generalized canonical bundle formula for generalized lc-trivial fibrations over non-compact bases and prove the boundedness of complements in the complex analytic setting.

We work with the notion of generalized pairs, introduced by Birkar–Zhang
[
BiZh16
]
.
Generalized pairs have played a crucial role in Birkar’s work on the boundedness of complements
[
Bir19
]
and the boundedness of Fano varieties of fixed dimension with mild singularities
[
Bir21
]
.
These developments have led to significant progress in birational geometry, and the language of generalized pairs is now standard in this area.
In the complex analytic setting, there are two extensions of this notion depending on the framework adopted for the nef part.
One is the notion of generalized pairs whose nef part is given by b-nef
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisors.
Another is the notion of generalized pairs whose nef part is given by nef closed
(1,1)
-currents.
The former is very natural in the context of projective morphisms between complex analytic spaces.
The latter, on the other hand, is useful in the context of compact but non-projective Kähler varieties because of the lack of ample divisors.

We adopt the former definition in this paper.
The canonical bundle formula plays a central role in birational geometry, especially in inductive arguments on the dimension of varieties.
The canonical bundle formula for klt-trivial fibrations was studied by Kawamata
[
Kaw98
]
and Ambro
[
Am04
]
,
[
Am05
]
, and
Fujino–Gongyo
[
FG14
]
established the canonical bundle formula for lc-trivial fibrations.
Their theorems were extended to the framework of generalized pairs by Filipazzi
[
Fi20
]
.
For recent developments on the b-semiampleness conjecture, see Bakker–Filipazzi–Mauri–Tsimerman
[
BFMT25
]
.
For the canonical bundle formula in the Kähler context, see Hacon–Păun
[
HaPa24
]
.

We extend Filipazzi’s theorem to generalized lc-trivial fibrations in Fujino’s setting of complex analytic varieties
[
Fu22
]
.

We explain the differences between Theorem
1.1
and
[
HaPa24
, Theorem 2.3]
.
The main difference lies in the compactness assumptions in generalized lc-trivial fibrations.
Theorem
1.1
applies to generalized lc-trivial fibrations over non-compact bases.
Hence, the nef part of the generalized sub-pair is not necessarily globally nef.
In particular, if
S=Z
in Theorem
1.1
, then the nef part is only fiberwise semi-positive.
Moreover, Theorem
1.1
allows divisors with irrational coefficients.

We remark that Theorem
1.1
is new even in the case of lc-trivial fibrations and the theorem slightly improves
[
Fu22
, Theorem 21.4]
in the case of klt-trivial fibrations.
However, the starting point of the proof of Theorem
1.1
is
[
Fu22
, Theorem 21.4]
.
Using Filipazzi’s idea
[
Fi20
]
, we first prove Theorem
1.1
for generalized klt-trivial fibrations.
We then extend the framework to generalized lc-trivial fibrations.
In the proof, weak semistable reduction and the minimal model program (MMP, for short) in the complex analytic setting play important roles.
We refer to
[
EH25
]
and
[
EH24
]
for those key ingredients, respectively.
We note that we do not use the variations of mixed Hodge structures in the proof (cf.
[
FG14
, Theorem 3.6]
).
To the best of our knowledge, the strategy of the proof does not appear in the algebraic setting.

The first and most fundamental difficulty goes back to the definition of b-divisors.
In the complex analytic setting, it is not clear whether there is a reasonable definition of the restriction of b-divisors to open subsets.
This is because the number of bimeromorphic morphisms increases under restriction, and we do not have a compactification of open analytic varieties.
Hence, it is not clear whether the discriminant
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisor and the moduli
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisor associated to generalized lc-trivial fibrations are compatible with restriction over the base.
However, to run a relative MMP or take a weak semistable reduction, restriction over the base is unavoidable.
Therefore, the first step toward Theorem
1.1
is to prove a suitable compatibility of the discriminant
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisor and the moduli
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisor with respect to restriction.
This is achieved in Lemma
3.3
.
The second difficulty is the
\mathbb{R}
-b-Cartier property of the moduli
\mathbb{R}
-b-divisor.
Note that
[
Fu22
, Theorem 21.4]
does not explicitly assert the
\mathbb{Q}
-b-Cartier property of the moduli
\mathbb{Q}
-b-divisor.
This is treated in Remark
3.4
and Theorem
3.5
, which is Theorem
1.1
in the case of klt-trivial fibrations.
After extending Theorem
3.5
to the case of generalized klt-trivial fibrations using Filipazzi’s idea
[
Fi20
]
, we encounter the final difficulty: the extension to the generalized lc case.
We overcome this difficulty by using the techniques of the MMP
[
EH24
]
together with Theorem
1.1
in the case of generalized klt-trivial fibrations.
For details, see the proof of Proposition
3.9
.

Once we establish the canonical bundle formula and the MMP
[
EH26
]
, we expect that many results for projective varieties can be extended to projective morphisms between complex analytic varieties. Although we do not explore such applications in depth in this paper, we prove the boundedness of complements in the complex analytic setting.
Roughly speaking, complements provide a way to measure singularities of generalized pairs.
The boundedness of complements was originally conjectured by Shokurov
[
Sho93
]
for the existence of threefold log flips.
In addition to Birkar’s breakthrough mentioned before, the boundedness of complements is applied to the ascending chain condition for minimal log discrepancies (
[
HLS25
]
,
[
HLL25
]
), the moduli problem of Calabi–Yau pairs (
[
ABB+23
]
), and the Yau–Tian–Donaldson conjecture and the projectivity of the K-moduli of log Fano pairs (
[
LXZ22
]
).
In this paper, we show the complex analytic analogs of results by Filipazzi–Moraga
[
FiMo20
]
and Chen–Han–He–Xie
[
GHHX25
]
.

Step 1
.

In this step we fix notations.

Let
\boldsymbol{\rm N}
be the nef part of

We take a log resolution

and

We set

, and

.
We denote the induced morphism

by

.

Let

be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
defined by
K_{X^{\prime}}+B^{\prime}+M^{\prime}=\phi^{*}(K_{X}+B+M)
.
We set

(1)

T^{\prime}:=B^{\prime=1}\qquad{\rm and}\qquad\Delta^{\prime}=B^{\prime}-T^{\prime}.

We also set

(2)

Let
\Gamma^{\prime}
be an
\mathbb{R}
-divisor on
X^{\prime}
defined by

Shrinking

around

and replacing

(resp.

(resp.

) for some

sufficiently close to one, we may assume that every coefficient of
B^{\prime}-\Gamma^{\prime}
has sufficiently small absolute value.
Note that the fourth conditions of

in Lemma
4.5
is preserved after this replacement because

\begin{split}(1-a)\Gamma^{<1}+aB^{<1}\geq&(1-a)\bigl(B^{<1}-\{(n+1)B^{<1}\}\bigr)+aB^{<1}\\
=&B^{<1}-(1-a)\{(n+1)B^{<1}\}\geq B^{<1}-\{(n+1)B^{<1}\}\end{split}

for any

.

Step 2
.

In this step we define a Weil divisor
\Psi^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
and its properties.

We define a Weil divisor
\Psi^{\prime}
on
X^{\prime}
by

(3)

\Psi^{\prime}:=S^{\prime}-\lfloor(\Gamma^{\prime}+n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor)\rfloor=S^{\prime}-\lfloor(\Gamma^{\prime}-\Delta^{\prime}+\{(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\})\rfloor.

We also set

(4)

\Lambda^{\prime}:=\Gamma^{\prime}+n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor+\Psi^{\prime}=S^{\prime}+\{(\Gamma^{\prime}+n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor)\}.

We check that

is
\phi
-exceptional and all the coefficients of

.
Because the latter part follows from

, we only show that

is
\phi
-exceptional.
Since
\lfloor\Gamma\rfloor=S
and
\phi_{*}\Delta^{\prime}=\{B\}=B^{<1}
, we have

\phi_{*}\Psi^{\prime}=S-\lfloor(\Gamma-B^{<1}+\{(n+1)B^{<1}\})\rfloor=-\lfloor(\Gamma^{<1}-B^{<1}+\{(n+1)B^{<1}\})\rfloor.

By recalling that

, which is our hypothesis of Lemma

, we have
\Gamma^{<1}-B^{<1}+\{(n+1)B^{<1}\}\geq 0
.
On the other hand, since every coefficient of

has sufficiently small absolute value, all the coefficient of
\Gamma^{<1}-B^{<1}+\{(n+1)B^{<1}\}
are contained in
(0,1)
.
This implies
\phi_{*}\Psi^{\prime}=0
, and therefore
\Psi^{\prime}
is
\phi
-exceptional.
We also note that to show the latter part, we use the fact that every coefficient of
B^{\prime}-\Gamma^{\prime}
has sufficiently small absolute value.

Step 3
.

By (
1
) and (
2
), we have

(5)

\begin{split}L^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime}=&n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor+nH^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime},\end{split}

and (
4
) shows

\begin{split}L^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime}=&n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor+nH^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime}\\
=&K_{X^{\prime}}+\Gamma^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{\rm N}_{X^{\prime}}-\phi^{*}(K_{X}+\Gamma+N)+(\Lambda^{\prime}-\Gamma^{\prime})+nH^{\prime}\\
=&K_{X^{\prime}}+\Lambda^{\prime}+\boldsymbol{\rm N}_{X^{\prime}}-\phi^{*}(K_{X}+\Gamma+N)+nH^{\prime}.\end{split}

We set

.
Since

is
\pi^{\prime}
-nef and
\pi^{\prime}
-big and

, we can apply

, and

, and we have

.
By considering a long cohomology exact sequence, we see that the morphism

\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{X^{\prime}}(L^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime})\longrightarrow\pi^{\prime}_{*}\mathcal{O}_{S^{\prime}}((L^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}})

is surjective.

Step 4
.

In this step we define some
\mathbb{R}
-divisors.

Recall the hypothesis of Lemma
4.5
that
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})\to Z
has an
n
-complement
(S,B^{+}_{S}+M_{S})\to Z
over
z\in Z
such that

, where
(S,B_{S}+M_{S})\to Z
is defined by using the generalized adjunction

.
We define

By shrinking

around

, we may assume

.
Then we have

-n(K_{S}+B_{S}+M_{S})=-n(K_{X}+B^{+}_{S}+M_{S}+B_{S}-B^{+}_{S})\sim nR_{S}\geq 0.

Put

.
Then

By (
5
), we have

(6)

\begin{split}(L^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}=&(n\Delta^{\prime}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor+nH^{\prime}+\Psi^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}\\
\sim&G_{S^{\prime}}:=nR_{S^{\prime}}+n\Delta_{S^{\prime}}-\lfloor(n+1)\Delta_{S^{\prime}}\rfloor+\Psi_{S^{\prime}},\end{split}

where
\Delta_{S^{\prime}}:=\Delta^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
and
\Psi_{S^{\prime}}:=\Psi^{\prime}|_{S^{\prime}}
.
We note that

and

-divisors on

by (

) and (

We also note that

and

are both Weil divisors on

Step 5
.

In this step we prove

and lift this to an effective Weil divisor

on

.

The fact

follows from

(see Step

) and

(see (

)).
To lift

, we apply the surjectivity of the morphism

.
Hence, we can find an effective Weil divisor

on

such that

.

Step 6
.

In this step we define an effective
\mathbb{Q}
-divisor

on
X^{\prime}
.
From this step, for any
\mathbb{R}
-divisor

on

by

Since

Step 7
.

With this step we complete the proof.

We have

by construction.
By (
2
), we see that

Hence, we have

0\sim n(K_{X}+B^{+}+M)=nK_{X}+n\lfloor B\rfloor+\lfloor(n+1)\{B\}\rfloor+G+nM,

and the negativity lemma implies

K_{X^{\prime}}+T^{\prime}+\frac{1}{n}\lfloor(n+1)\Delta^{\prime}\rfloor+\frac{1}{n}G^{\prime}+M^{\prime}-\frac{1}{n}\Psi^{\prime}=\phi^{*}(K_{X}+B^{+}+M).

By restricting the equality to

and using (

\begin{split}0\sim&\phi^{*}_{S}(K_{X}+B^{+}+M)|_{S}\\
=&K_{S^{\prime}}+(T^{\prime}-S^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}+\frac{1}{n}\lfloor(n+1)\Delta_{S^{\prime}}\rfloor+\frac{1}{n}G_{S^{\prime}}-\frac{1}{n}\Psi_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}}\\
=&K_{S^{\prime}}+(T^{\prime}-S^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}+\Delta_{S^{\prime}}+R_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}}\\
=&K_{S^{\prime}}+(B^{\prime}-S^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}+R_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}}.\end{split}

This relation and

\begin{split}(K_{X}+B^{+}+M)|_{S}=&\phi_{S*}(K_{S^{\prime}}+(B^{\prime}-S^{\prime})|_{S^{\prime}}+R_{S^{\prime}}+M_{S^{\prime}})\\
=&K_{S}+B_{S}+M_{S}+R_{S}=K_{S}+B^{+}_{S}+M_{S}.\end{split}

By the above arguments, we have proved

•

B^{+}\geq\lfloor B\rfloor+\frac{1}{n}\lfloor(n+1)\{B\}\rfloor
,

•

n(K_{X}+B^{+}+M)\sim 0
, and

•

(K_{X}+B^{+}+M)|_{S}=K_{S}+B^{+}_{S}+M_{S}

By the generalized inversion of adjunction (Lemma
2.17
), we see that
(X,B^{+}+M)\to Z
is generalized lc on a neighborhood of
S\cap\pi^{-1}(z)
.
Finally, by using the connectedness principle (Lemma
2.15
), we see that
(X,B^{+}+M)
is generalized lc on a neighborhood of
\pi^{-1}(z)
.
For details, see
[
Bir19
, Step 9 in the proof of Proposition 8.1]
.
Therefore, after shrinking
Z
around
z
, we get an
n
-complement
(X,B^{+}+M)\to Z
of
(X,B+M)\to Z
over
z\in Z
.

We finish the proof.
