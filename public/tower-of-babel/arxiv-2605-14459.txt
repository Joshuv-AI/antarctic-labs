Neural Networks for Singular Perturbations – Finite Regularity
F. Rohner, Ch. Schwab, C. Xenophontos

Abstract
We study finite-element and deep feedforward neural network (DNN for short) expressivity rate bounds for solution sets of a model linear, second order singularly perturbed, elliptic two-point boundary value problem, in Sobolev norms on a bounded interval ( − 1 , 1 ) (-1,1) , with explicit dependence on the singular perturbation parameter ε ∈ ( 0 , 1 ] \varepsilon\in(0,1] . Emphasis is on low Sobolev regularity of the data, i.e., source term f f and reaction coefficient b b . A proof of ε \varepsilon -explicit solution regularity based on exponentially weighted energy-norm bounds is developed, and ε \varepsilon -robust, algebraic expression rate bounds in Sobolev norms for ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}_{1} Finite-Elements on exponential and Shishkin type meshes is proved. Expression rates for shallow (fixed depth) ReLU \ReLU -NNs are shown which are robust w.r. to ε \varepsilon and explicit in terms of the NN size. Robust NN expression rate bounds are further studied for deep feedforward DNNs with ReLU and tanh-activations. As in 15 , tanh- and sigmoid-activated sub-NNs allow to include exponential boundary layer functions exactly into the NN feature space, leading to reduced NN sizes. Recent bitstring encoding techniques for deep NNs with ReLU activations afford, still under low data regularity f , b ∈ H 1 ​ ( I ) f,b\in H^{1}(I) twice the (robust) convergence rate of ℙ 1 \mathbb{P}_{1} Finite-Elements achievable with “eXp” or Shishkin meshes.

Elliptic and parabolic PDEs which depend on a small,
non-dimensional physical parameter
\varepsilon>0

8

In all these applications, PDEs depend on a small

\varepsilon\in(0,1]

Standard numerical approximation methods (Finite Volume-,

\varepsilon

in general, a so-called

\varepsilon

in order to exhibit theoretical approximation rates.
In this paper,
we continue and complement our study
15

\varepsilon

As in

reaction-diffusion boundary value problem (BVP):

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle f(x)\;,\;x\in I=(-1,1)\;,

\displaystyle u_{\varepsilon}(\pm 1)

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle 0,

\|u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\|_{L^{\infty}([x_{i_{0}},x_{i_{1}}])}^{2}\leq\frac{C}{(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x_{i_{0}}))^{2}}\leq CN^{-4}.

Combining the two estimates yields with
|K_{2}|\leq 1
,

\|u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}-\mathcal{I}(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-})\|_{1,\varepsilon,K_{2}}^{2}\leq C(N^{-4}+|K_{2}|N^{-4})\leq CN^{-4}.

(3.17)

On
K_{3}
, we proceed as for
K_{1}
,
but use the bound on the coarse interval lengths (
3.13
).
Hence, for
\ell=0,1
,

\displaystyle\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}-\mathcal{I}(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL})\right)^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}(K_{3})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=i_{1}}^{N}\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}-\mathcal{I}(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL})\right)^{(\ell)}\|_{L^{2}(I_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=i_{1}}^{N}h_{i}^{2(2-\ell)}\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2(2-\ell)}\sum_{i=i_{1}}^{N}\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}(I_{i})}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq CN^{-2(2-\ell)}\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}([x_{i_{1}},x_{N}])}^{2}.

To bound
\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}([x_{i_{1}},x_{N}])}^{2}
,
we use that
[x_{i_{1}},x_{N}]\subset[-1+\lambda_{\varepsilon},1]
and the a priori bound (
2.11
).
Proceeding similarly as on
K_{2}
yields

\displaystyle\|\left(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L^{2}([-1+\lambda_{\varepsilon},1])}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{x\in[-1+\lambda_{\varepsilon},1]}(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x))^{-2}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|_{L_{w_{\varepsilon}}^{2}([-1+\lambda_{\varepsilon},1])}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{-4}(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(-1+\lambda_{\varepsilon}))^{-2}\leq C,

and so

\|u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}-\mathcal{I}(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL})\|_{1,\varepsilon,K_{3}}^{2}\leq C(\varepsilon^{2}N^{-2}+N^{-4}).

(3.18)

Combining the estimates
(
3.16
), (
3.17
),
and (
3.18
) yields

\displaystyle\|u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}-\mathcal{I}(u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL})\|_{1,\varepsilon,I}\leq C(N^{-1}\ln(N)+\varepsilon N^{-1}+N^{-2})\leq CN^{-1}\ln(N).

Letting
u^{BL,\Delta_{S}}_{\varepsilon,\pm}:=\mathcal{I}(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,\pm})
,
we finally observe that the CpwL approximant

u_{0}^{\Delta_{S}}+u^{BL,\Delta_{S}}_{\varepsilon,-}+u^{BL,\Delta_{S}}_{\varepsilon,+}\in S^{1}(I,\Delta_{S})

satisfies the zero BCs at
\pm 1
, by construction, as well as the bound (
3.14
).
Combining the above, the proof is complete.
∎

3.2.2
Exponential mesh
\Delta_{eXp}

In
23
a class of meshes

was derived for the robust, asymptotically optimal, approximation of the

,
appearing in (

The mesh points of
\Delta_{eXp}

are “exponentially graded” towards the endpoints, in an
\varepsilon

In

the exponential mesh
\Delta_{eXp}
may be
regarded as a generalized S-type mesh with
\alpha=1/2
,
and

\phi(t)=-\ln\left(1-2t(1-2N^{-1})\right)\;,\;\psi(t)=1-2t(1-2N^{-1}),

(3.19)

with
\max|\psi^{\prime}|\leq 2
.
For
\theta>0
fixed, the
N+1
nodal points are given by

x_{i}=\begin{cases}-1+\varepsilon\theta\phi\left(\frac{2i}{N}\right),&i=0,\dots,N/4\;,\\
\frac{4i}{N}(1-\theta\varepsilon\ln(N/2))-2+2\theta\varepsilon\ln(N/2)&i=N/4,\dots,3N/4\;,\\
1-\varepsilon\theta\phi\left(2-\frac{2i}{N}\right)&i=3N/4,\dots,N\;.\end{cases}

Here, the mesh size
h_{i}=|I_{i}|=x_{i}-x_{i-1}
of
I_{i}\subset I_{{\text{layer}}}^{-}
, for
i=1,\dots,N/4
, satisfies

\displaystyle h_{i}=x_{i}-x_{i-1}

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\theta\left[\phi\left(\frac{2i}{N}\right)-\phi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\theta\left[-\ln\left(1-2\left(\frac{2i}{N}\right)(1-2N^{-1})\right)+\ln\left(1-2\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)(1-2N^{-1})\right)\right]

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\theta\ln\left(\frac{1-2\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)(1-2N^{-1})}{1-2\left(\frac{2i}{N}\right)(1-2N^{-1})}\right)

\displaystyle=\varepsilon\theta\ln\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)-\delta}\right)\;,

(3.20)

where
\delta=\frac{4}{N}(1-2N^{-1})
.

We next adapt the arguments of
23
;
7

to the approximation of the
boundary layers
u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,\pm}
obtained in Prop.
2.1
,
using the weighted
H^{2}
estimates (
2.11
)
rather than the explicit, pointwise representation (
2.25
)
(which are valid for constant reaction coefficient)
or pointwise bounds for the second derivatives as e.g.
7
.

Proposition 3.3
.

For
0<\varepsilon\leq 1
and
b,f\in H^{1}(I)
,
let
u_{\varepsilon}
be the solution to (
1.1
)–(
1.2

Let

be its
CpwL FE approximation on the eXp mesh
\Delta_{eXp}

at length scale
\varepsilon\in(0,1]
with
1\leq N\leq 1/\varepsilon

Then there exists a positive constant
C
, independent of
\varepsilon
,
such that for every
1\leq N\leq 1/\varepsilon
holds

\|u_{\varepsilon}-u_{\varepsilon}^{\Delta_{eXp}}\|_{1,\varepsilon,I}\leq CN^{-1}.

(3.21)

Proof.

As in the proof of Proposition
3.1
,
we show the statement by constructing a CpwL interpolant of
u_{\varepsilon}
and
use the quasioptimality of the Finite Element projection in the energy norm to conclude.
We use the decomposition (
2.9
) and write the approximation as

u_{\varepsilon}^{\Delta_{eXp}}=u_{0}^{\Delta_{eXp}}+u^{BL,\Delta_{eXp}}_{\varepsilon,-}+u^{BL,\Delta_{eXp}}_{\varepsilon,+}+u_{\varepsilon}^{R,\Delta_{eXp}}\;.

Boundary layer terms
u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,\pm}
.

By symmetry, we only analyze the error for the layer at the left endpoint.
As in the case of Proposition
3.1
, we choose
\theta=2\beta_{*}
.
For each
i=1,\dots,N/4
, it holds that

\displaystyle\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\left(\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}(x)\right)^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\min_{x\in I_{i}}\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x)\right)^{2}}\int_{x_{i-1}}^{x_{i}}\left(\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}(x)\right)^{\prime\prime}\right)^{2}\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x)\right)^{2}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\frac{1}{\left(w_{\varepsilon}^{-}(x_{i-1})\right)^{2}}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w_{\varepsilon}}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)^{4}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w_{\varepsilon}}(I_{i})}\;,

where we have used the monotonicity of
w_{\varepsilon}^{-}
.

In the following we denote by
\mathcal{I}(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-})
the CpwL nodal interpolant of
u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}
on the eXp mesh.
Using classical interpolation error estimates
and (
3.20
), we obtain, for
\ell=0,1
,

\displaystyle\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{(\ell)}-\left(\mathcal{I}(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-})\right)^{(\ell)}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{{\text{layer}}}^{-})}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N/4}h_{i}^{2(2-\ell)}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N/4}\left(\varepsilon\theta\ln\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)-\delta}\right)\right)^{2(2-\ell)}\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)^{4}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w_{\varepsilon}}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2(2-\ell)}\max_{i=1,\dots,N/4}\left(\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)^{4}\ln\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)-\delta}\right)^{2(2-\ell)}\right)\sum_{i=1}^{N/4}\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w_{\varepsilon}}(I_{i})}

\displaystyle\leq C\varepsilon^{2(2-\ell)}\max_{i=1,\dots,N/4}\left(\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)^{4}\ln\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)-\delta}\right)^{2(2-\ell)}\right)\|\left(u^{BL}_{\varepsilon,-}\right)^{\prime\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}_{w_{\varepsilon}}(I)}\;.

We now claim that there exists
C>0
independent of
N,\varepsilon
such that

\max_{i=1,\dots,N/4}\left(\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)^{4}\ln\left(\frac{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)}{\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)-\delta}\right)^{2(2-\ell)}\right)\leq CN^{-2(2-\ell)}.

(3.22)

Indeed, for
i=1,\dots,N/4
, we have that

\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}}\leq\psi\left(\frac{2(i-1)}{N}\right)\leq 1\;.

Consider the map

g_{\ell}\colon y\mapsto y^{4}\ln\left(\frac{y}{y-\delta}\right)^{2(2-\ell)},\quad y\in\left[\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}},1\right]\;.

Then, for
\ell=0
,
we note that finding the maximum of
g_{0}
in
\left[\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}},1\right]

is equivalent to finding the maximum of

.
We have that

h^{\prime}(y)=\ln\left(1+\frac{\delta}{y-\delta}\right)-\frac{\delta}{y-\delta}<0,\quad\text{for }y\in\left[\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}},1\right]\;,

where we used that for
t>-1
,
\ln(1+t)<t
.
Therefore,
g_{0}
attains its maximum at the left endpoint.
Moreover,
\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}}-\delta=\frac{2}{N}
, and hence

\frac{6N-8}{N^{2}}\ln\left(3-\frac{4}{N}\right)\leq\frac{6}{N}\ln(3)\;.

This shows the claim in the case
\ell=0
.

For
\ell=1
, we have that

g_{1}^{\prime}(y)=y^{3}\ln\left(\frac{y}{y-\delta}\right)\left[4\ln\left(\frac{y}{y-\delta}\right)-\frac{2\delta}{y-\delta}\right]\;.

The sign of

is determined by

\Phi(y)=2\ln\left(\frac{y}{y-\delta}\right)-\frac{\delta}{y-\delta}=2\ln(1+u)-u\;,

for

, for all

It follows that

is monotonically increasing and attains its maximum at

. Finally, using that

for

and

\frac{4}{N}-\frac{8}{N^{2}}\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

, we find

-\ln\left(1-\frac{4}{N}+\frac{8}{N^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{\frac{4}{N}-\frac{8}{N^{2}}}{1-\frac{4}{N}+\frac{8}{N^{2}}}\leq 2\left(\frac{4}{N}-\frac{8}{N^{2}}\right)\leq\frac{8}{N}\;,

and the claim follows.
This shows the result for
u_{\varepsilon,-}^{BL}
on
I_{\text{layer}}^{-}
.

On
\tilde{I}=I\setminus I_{\text{layer}}^{-}
, the

3.1

Smooth part
u_{0}
and remainder
u_{\varepsilon}^{R}
.

3.1
, we choose the approximation of the remainder,

u_{\varepsilon}^{R,\Delta_{eXp}}
, to be zero.

We next consider the smooth part
u_{0}
.
Let
u_{0}^{\Delta_{eXp}}
denote the CpwL interpolant of
u_{0}
on the eXp mesh.
On
I_{\mathrm{coarse}}

3.1
and using in
particular (
3.15

\|u_{0}-u_{0}^{\Delta_{{eXp}}}\|_{1,\varepsilon,I_{\text{coarse}}}\leq CN^{-1}.

For the layer region, by symmetry it suffices to estimate the error on
I_{\mathrm{layer}}^{-}
.
Using the classical
L^{2}
interpolation error estimate
together with the stability of CpwL interpolation in the

we obtain

\|u_{0}-u_{0}^{\Delta_{{eXp}}}\|^{2}_{1,\varepsilon,I_{\text{layer}}^{-}}\leq C\sum_{i=1}^{N/4}\left(\varepsilon^{2}\|u^{\prime}_{0}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}+h_{i}^{2}\|u_{0}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}\right).

Since by assumption

and, by (

\displaystyle\|u_{0}-u_{0}^{\Delta_{{eXp}}}\|^{2}_{1,\varepsilon,I_{\text{layer}}^{-}}\leq CN^{-2}\sum_{i=1}^{N/4}\|u_{0}^{\prime}\|^{2}_{L^{2}(I_{i})}\leq CN^{-2},

where (

3.2.3
Bakhvalov-Shishkin mesh

In the original Bakhvalov mesh
2
the mesh points are chosen by solving a non-linear problem.
This results in a layer-adapted mesh with more nodal points in the layer regions, and usually far fewer points in the coarse region. A combination of the above two meshes, referred to as the Bakhvalov-Shishkin (B-S) mesh, includes a uniform subdivision in the coarse region and the (original) Bakhvalov nodal points in the layer regions (see, e.g.,
10
). For the B-S mesh, we have

\phi(t)=-\ln\left(1-2t(1-N^{-1})\right)\;,\;\psi(t)=1-2t(1-N^{-1}),

and there holds
\max|\psi^{\prime}|=2
.
We denote this mesh as
\Delta_{BS}
.

In numerical experiments (see, e.g.,
22
) the eXp mesh
outperforms both the Shishkin and B-S meshes in the practical range of
N
and
\varepsilon
.
An analog of Proposition
3.3
holds for the B-S mesh (with an almost identical proof).

4
Robust Neural Network Approximation

In this section, we consider the approximation of univariate functions on bounded intervals
by NNs with
\ReLU^{k}
activation function

\varrho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}:x\mapsto\max\{0,x\}^{k}
,
k=1,2
.
The case
k=1
corresponds to the classical ReLU activation.
It allows to exactly represent continuous, piecewise affine
functions on arbitrary partitions in
\mathbb{R}
.
Owing to
\ReLU
NNs not allowing for exact multiplication
of real numbers, higher degree polynomials and splines can
not be exactly represented by
\ReLU
NNs, motivating to
consider
k\geq 2
.

4.1
Neural Networks

As usual (e.g.
14
;
16
;
17
),
we define a neural network (NN) in terms of its
weight matrices
A_{\ell}
and bias vectors
b_{\ell}
.

All NNs considered are feedforward NNs without skip connections,
and we shall not indicate this at each occurrence.

We distinguish between a
NN configuration
\Phi
of a feedforward NN
and the function it realizes,
called
realization
of the NN
\Phi
,
and denoted as
\mathrm{R}(\Phi)
being
the composition of parameter-dependent affine transformations
and nonlinear activations.
We recall some NN formalism in the notation of

(
17
, Section 2)
.

Definition 4.1

(
(
17
, Definition 2.1)
)
.

[Neural Network Definitions]

1.
For
d,L\in\mathbb{N}
, a
neural network configuration
\Phi

with input dimension
d\geq 1
and number of layers
L\geq 1
,
comprises
a finite sequence of matrix-vector pairs, i.e.

\displaystyle\Phi=((A_{1},b_{1}),(A_{2},b_{2}),\ldots,(A_{L},b_{L})).

2.
For
N_{0}:=d
and
numbers of neurons
N_{1},\ldots,N_{L}\in\mathbb{N}
per layer
,
for all
\ell=1,\ldots,L
it holds that

A_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}\times N_{\ell-1}}
and

b_{\ell}\in\mathbb{R}^{N_{\ell}}
.

3. [Realization]
For a NN
\Phi
and an activation function
\varrho:\mathbb{R}\to\mathbb{R}
,
we define the associated

realization of feedforward NN
\Phi

as the function

\displaystyle\mathrm{R}(\Phi):\mathbb{R}^{d}\to\mathbb{R}^{N_{L}}:x\to x_{L},

where, for
L\geq 2
,

\displaystyle x_{0}

\displaystyle:=x,

\displaystyle x_{\ell}

\displaystyle:=\varrho(A_{\ell}x_{\ell-1}+b_{\ell}),\qquad\text{ for }\ell=1,\ldots,L-1,

\displaystyle x_{L}

\displaystyle:=A_{L}x_{L-1}+b_{L}.

4.[Activation]
The
activation function

\varrho

is assumed to act componentwise on vector-valued inputs,

\varrho(y)=(\varrho(y_{1}),\dots,\varrho(y_{m}))

for all
y=(y_{1},\dots,y_{m})\in\mathbb{R}^{m}
.

5.
The layers indexed by
\ell=1,\ldots,L-1

are
hidden layers
.
In these layers the activation function is applied.
No activation is applied in the last
layer
L
of the NN.

6. [Width, Depth, Size]
We refer to

0pt(\Phi):=L
as the
depth
of
\Phi

and call

M(\Phi):=\sum_{\ell=1}^{L}\|A_{\ell}\|_{0}+\|b_{\ell}\|_{0}

the
size
of
\Phi
,
which is the number of nonzero components
in the weight matrices
A_{\ell}
and the bias vectors
b_{\ell}
.
The
number of neurons
in the NN
\Phi
is denoted by
\#(\Phi)
,
and the
width
of the NN
\Phi
of depth
L(\Phi)

is
W(\Phi)=\max_{\ell=1,...,L(\Phi)}N_{\ell}
.
Without additional assumptions on the sparsity of
b_{\ell}
and
A_{\ell}
,

M(\Phi)\lesssim(\#(\Phi))^{2}
.

7. [Input / Output Dimension]
The parameters

d,N_{L}\in\mathbb{N}
are the
input dimension
and the
output dimension
,
respectively.

8.
The set of all such NNs is denoted by

\mathcal{NN}^{\varrho}_{L,W,d,N_{L}}
.

Remark 4.2
.

Some related works, e.g.
4
,
use the width as a measure for the complexity of a NN.
In each layer of a fully connected NN
the number of nonzero weights can be width squared.

Remark 4.3
.

We refer to NNs with only activation function
\varrho

as
strict
\varrho
-NNs
, or simply as
\varrho
-NNs.
This includes NNs of depth

,
which do not have hidden layers and
which exactly realize affine transformations.

Remark 4.4
.

[Multiple Activations]
NNs
\Phi
of depth
L=0pt(\Phi)\geq 2

may admit a different activation
\rho_{\ell}
,
\ell=1,...,L-1

at each hidden layer. We will leverage this to
include exponential boundary layers via
\tanh()
and
sigmoidal activations into the feature space.
In the case of heterogeneous activations,
we write

{\bm{\varrho}}:=\{\varrho_{\ell}:\ell=1,...,L-1\}
,
and denote the NN as
(\Phi,{\bm{\varrho}})
,
with realization
\mathrm{R}(\Phi,{\bm{\varrho}})
.
In emulation of boundary layers via
\tanh

only parts of NN layers are activated with
\tanh
,
while

\ReLU

We then collect these activations also in

and denote with

as will be clear from the context.

4.2
ReLU NN Emulation of continuous, piecewise polynomials

A first (well-known) observation is that
on arbitrary, finite partitions
\Delta

(a,b)

the space
{\mathcal{S}}^{1}(\Delta)
of continuous, piecewise linears can be
exactly represented by shallow ReLU NNs.
We refer to
(
1
, Prop. 3.4)
for a proof.

Proposition 4.5
.

Let
N\in\mathbb{N}
, and

\Delta=\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N}
a partition of
I
into
N

open, disjoint, connected subintervals

I_{j}=(x_{j-1},x_{j})
of length
h_{j}=x_{j}-x_{j-1}
,
j=1,\ldots,N
,
with mesh width
h=\max_{j=1}^{N}h_{j}
.

For each
\phi_{N}\in\mathcal{S}^{1}(\Delta)

there is a ReLU neural network

\Phi_{N}\in\mathcal{NN}_{2,{N+1},1,1}

such that

\phi_{N}(t)=\mathrm{R}(\Phi_{N})(t)
for all
t\in I
.
Its weights are
bounded in absolute value by

\max\left\{1+h_{1},\|\phi_{N}\|_{L^{\infty}(I)},2\displaystyle\max_{j=0,\dots,N}\frac{1}{h_{j}}\right\}
.

4.3
Robust Shallow
\ReLU
-NN Solution Approximation

With Prop.

5
Conclusions and Generalizations

For model, linear singular perturbation boundary value problems
(
1.1
), (
1.2
)
in a bounded interval
I=(-1,1)
,
with data belonging to finite order Sobolev or Besov spaces,
we
established robust expression rate bounds of
the parametric solutions by ReLU NNs, whose realizations
are CpwL functions of
x
.
The presently proved,

robust algebraic expression rate bounds

under, in a sense minimal, regularity on the data

and

of
the problem (
1.1
), (
1.2
),
complement the
robust exponential expression rate bounds

that were shown in

under stronger, analytic regularity hypotheses on the data.

In Sec.
2.2
, we established the solution decomposition
(
2.9
), under the regularity hypotheses

.
While this is weaker than, e.g., the
C^{2}(\overline{I})
regularity
often imposed in arguments relying on the maximum principle and/or
barrier functions,
the present approach could be extended to
data regularity below
H^{1}(I)=W^{1,2}(I)
.
To this end, one would need to adopt
in the proof of Theorem
2.1

a non-hilbertian setting,
in (exponentially weighted versions of) spaces
W^{k,p}(I)
,

,
with
1\leq p<2
,
requiring, in a sense minimally
2
2

2

Discontinuous
b(x)
can give rise to interior layers
18
.

in (
1.1
) that

.

Similar algebraic expression rate bounds (which are known)
were shown to hold for so-called
h
-version
Finite Element approximations of fixed polynomial degree
p\geq 1
,
on either
so-called Shishkin-meshes or on exponentially graded partitions of

.
We refer to

.
These results were shown here to imply corresponding
robust, algebraic expression rate bounds for shallow ReLU NNs.
Corresponding robust expression rate bounds for spiking NNs are

.

Using the tools developed in

deep ReLU NNs furnish (essentially, up to logarithmic terms)
