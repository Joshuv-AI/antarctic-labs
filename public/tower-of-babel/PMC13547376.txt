A novel fermatean fuzzy Z-number FRANK-WASPAS model for evaluating AI-powered narrative intelligence systems
Authors: Muhammad Shazib Hameed, Barira Saba, Sobia Zulfiqar, Muhammad Kamran, John T Mendy, Bander Almutairi
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractThe rise in importance of narrative intelligence systems that are inspired by artificial intelligence (AI) is increasing in terms of their ability to transmit intergenerational legacies, psychological continuity, and cultural identities. This leads to even more interest in such systems. The StoryWeaver Lab is an AI-driven app that replicates an interactive living tradition in a family. The assessment environment is rather complex and logical but ambiguous, and opposition can occur. It is difficult to model such uncertainty in an accurate way. The proposed study solves this problem with the help of comparative analysis and the weighted aggregated sum product evaluation (WASPAS) methodology. It uses a new decision-making (DM) model, which relies on the Frank norm and Fermatean fuzzy Z-numbers (FFZNs). The effectiveness of the suggested methodology is measured using a multi-attribute group DM (MAGDM) approach concerning the interdependent criteria. These criteria are narrative coherence, personal connection, ethical trustworthiness, flexibility, and long-term sustainability. The conventional MAGDM methods tend to deteriorate in case of inaccurate, partial, or reliability-sensitive data. These limitations are resolved with the use of FFZNs to describe the membership and non-membership levels and the reliability that goes with them simultaneously. The Frank norm increases the aggregate behavior by letting the criteria interact freely. This includes both reinforcing and suppressing effects. The DM model is developed based on the WASPAS method in the context of the Frank norm and FFZNs. It is evaluated in terms of consistency and strength. Mathematical case analysis of a number of AI storytelling system choices demonstrates that all methods find the same best answer. Nevertheless, the suggested FFZNs-Frank model is more interpretable. It is more reliably aware of uncertainty. Less information is lost. The results prove the legitimacy and reliability of the suggested framework. They show their benefits in comparison with traditional WASPAS. This research contributes methodologically. It offers a useful decision-support system to assess the emotional intelligence of AI systems, facilitate ethical narrative telling, sustainable cultural maintenance in unpredictable DM situations, and trustworthy human-AI relationships.Subject terms: Mathematics and computing, Psychology, Psychology

Introduction

Now, digital systems based on AI1 are more common. The question of the long-term retention of social identities, emotional stability, and cross-generational knowledge is getting an increasingly large amount of attention in the international community. Narrative intelligence technologies that are AI-driven2 are now an option to assist communities and families in documenting, shaping, and maintaining their evolving narratives over time.

In these cases, StoryWeaver Lab is a creative project that attempts to apply adaptive AI technology in combination with human memories, values, and emotions to co-create a living mythology of a family3. To promote responsible AI use, online welfare, and cultural sustainability, these systems focus on building emotional connections and supporting cultural survival. They aim to enhance human-AI interactions, and the AI storytelling network plays a key role in this, especially regarding sustainability and emotional enrichment. However, there are challenges when applying these systems. For instance, while the outputs from the AI storytelling network4 can be beneficial, they also come with limitations. It’s crucial that these systems help to strengthen connections and support cultural survival, as they are vital for effective human-AI interactions. These problems concern not only technical but also psychological perspectives. Some of them are narrative continuity, emotional authenticity, moral trust, adaptability to evolving family principles, availability of artificially created information, and long-term reliability of the information stored. The evaluation process is quite complicated because these elements are intricately linked and frequently have an impact on one another.

The evaluation of these characteristics by humans is subjective by nature, ambiguous, and susceptible to personal experiences and cultural and shifting social norms. Consequently, the views on such traits can vary significantly across time and user groups. This causes much ambiguity when it comes to DM5. As an example, the consumers can voice doubt in the credibility of the facts or the moral limits between what is true and what is fictional. They can simultaneously admire the emotional depth and creativity of the AI-generated storylines6. Similarly, the means of transparency can contribute to the development of trust. It can also diminish the immersion of the story in some individuals.

This vagueness demonstrates that AI storytelling systems cannot be measured using single-dimensional or purely quantitative metrics. Instead, they require a comprehensive framework of DM that could simultaneously consider the uncertainty, reluctance, and human appraisals’ validity. Due to this, the process of selecting or improving AI narrative platforms like StoryWeaver Lab involves evaluating their overall value in numerous ways. This includes short-term usefulness and long-term cultural and emotional impact.

Because of this, the issue is intrinsically complex and multifaceted. It involves the viewpoints of several stakeholders, including families, designers, ethical scholars, and legislators. To address these complications, multi-attribute group DM (MAGDM) techniques have played an important role in solving real-life problems that have interdependent criteria and subjective evaluations. The approaches of MAGDM provide a systematic analysis by providing criteria that express different priorities of stakeholders in varying degrees.

A group might value more creativity and emotional involvement in the context of AI-based fictional intelligence. Another group might value more ethical transparency, reliability, or sustainability. Nevertheless, conventional DM models are often not able to synthesize such biased and reliability-dependent information. This is particularly true during uncertain and hesitant situations. The ability of intuitionistic fuzzy and classical fuzzy to approximate the confidence levels of expert judgment is limited. This ability is paramount in the development of trust-sensitive AI systems.

Since decision-support systems of human-oriented applications of AI7 require the ability to be effective in simulating real-world ambiguity and information reliability, such a limitation attracts the focus on an important research gap. The present study will use a FFZN framework with the Frank norm to close this gap. This framework will allow for flexibility in the way assessment criteria interact while also implementing reliability metrics. Furthermore, the comprehensive comparison between the proposed framework and the WASPAS method confirms the consistency of ranking, soundness, and information maintenance of the proposed framework.

A WASPAS-based decision model8 is developed on the same FFZN-Frank setup in order to provide comparative insights on the stability and interpretability features. The findings demonstrate that while WASPAS can provide balanced additive-multiplicative aggregation9, the FFZN-Frank framework can provide less information distortion and a better representation of reliability-conscious uncertainty.

Review of literatures

The way uncertainty is addressed in mathematical modeling has completely changed since Zadeh10 proposed the idea of fuzzy sets (FSs). This invention has benefited all the control systems, and the FSs have allowed more intricate representations of ambiguity because the items could belong to a set with levels of membership that ranged between 0 and 1. DM pattern recognition. With the further elaboration on the FSs, the Intuitionistic FSs (IFSs) by Atanassov11, which add the additional degree of hesitation to the dimensions of membership and non-membership, were created to better reflect the uncertainty. IFSs have been quite useful in offering more flexible interpretations of subjective and ambiguous assessments on complex DM issues. Membership and non-membership magnitude limits were later relaxed to allow larger scale levels of uncertainty and extended IFSs to Pythagorean FS12 (PFSs) featuring q-rung orthopair fuzzy sets13. These extensions made the more sophisticated aggregation operators, scoring functions, and similarity measures available and played a role in the higher capability of decision-makers to give consistent ratings of the options in multi-criteria, complex problems. Researchers such as Yager14 and Garg have demonstrated the effectiveness of PFS-based MAGDM15 in the analysis of risks, the allocation of resources, and the engineering applications, showing that these sets are more indicative of subtle preferences than fuzzy model frameworks. By introducing truth, indeterminacy, and more particularly falsity, the Neutrosophic Sets (NSs)16 by Smarandache extended the prior fuzzy models and offered a powerful model of handling three-dimensional uncertainty. By encoding membership functions as periods of time, interval-valued NSs (IVNSs)17 additionally improved flexibility by enabling more accurate modeling of ambiguity and missing data. IVNS-based aggregation and ordering techniques have been effectively used in energy investment decisions, fault detection, and sustainable supplier selection, demonstrating their adaptability in complex and multifaceted decision contexts.

Senapati and Yager introduced Fermatean FSs (FFSs)18, which are an extension of PFSs that provide greater flexibility and discrimination by allowing the total of cubes whose membership and non-membership grades are bounded by one. FFZNs offer a strong foundation for simultaneously modeling uncertainty, reluctance, and confidence when paired with Z-numbers19, which take into consideration both fuzzy knowledge and its related reliability20. In MAGDM situations with interdependence criteria, where traditional fuzzy and intuitionistic approaches are frequently inadequate, FFZNs have proven to perform better.

AOs are important to take into account when assessing criteria in uncertain scenarios. Whereas, with a Frank norm-based operator, the information can be blended in a flexible manner, considering the possible support or antagonism between different attributes. The traditional linear operators are sometimes difficult to use to reflect the interconnections among criteria. The application of FFZNs21 under Frank norms has proved to be very beneficial in several areas such as DM in AI systems, healthcare management, and energy systems. These operators have been shown to be more interpretable, less lossy, and better suited to deal with uncertainty. The FFZN Frank norm frameworks show improved explanation and calculation accuracy when compared with the well-known MAGDM methods such as WASPAS with a high degree of ranking consistency. According to the growing body of research, FFZN Frank norm22 approaches provide a long-term framework for dependable, consistent, and understandable DM in a variety of fields, such as ethical technology assessment, green energy planning, and AI-driven narrative intelligence systems.

To conclude, a formal approach for dealing with uncertainty in multi-criteria DM is provided by the transition from FSs to IFSs, PFSs, NSs, and FFSs and dependability analysis of uncertainties based on Z-numbers and Frank norm aggregation. As a cutting-edge method that can handle complicated, interdependent requirements with unreliable and reliability-weighted inputs, the suggested FFZN Frank norm framework is extremely pertinent for both current applications and the DM challenges of the upcoming years. This is consistent with recent research that emphasizes the importance of managing heterogeneous and uncertain information from decision-makers in design evaluation processes using Z-number-based frameworks23,24. Ashraf et al.25 developed a decision aid for kidney transplants. Ashraf et al.26 focused on early infectious disease identification. Hameed et al.27 proposed a hybrid approach for air quality monitoring. Hameed et al.28 introduced neutrosophic similarity measures. Zulqarnain et al.29 presented a federated edge-AI framework for leak detection. In the last three years (2023-2025), some progress was achieved on FFZN-based MAGDM. Sari30 came up with a concept known as the Fermatean fuzzy Z-analytic hierarchy process for selecting third-party logistics providers. This is actually an illustration of the practical value of the reliability-oriented fuzzy frameworks. In order to optimize land development in urban areas, Xia et al.31 introduced disc Fermatean fuzzy Z-numbers and validated them through CoCoSo. Mishra et al.32 developed some new score functions for CRITIC-GLDS methods for FFNs, and Aydogan et al.33 contrasted the FFS, IFS, and classical fuzzy for ranking problems. Chen et al.34 introduced a novel framework for the Murielhead mean operator by using FFZN. These modern works as a whole confirm that the FFZN frameworks are becoming increasingly important, which creates a solid basis for our proposed FFZN-Frank-WASPAS framework.

Reliability Awareness via Z-Numbers: In contrast to IFSs, PFSs, or standard q-ROFSs, FFZNs contain reliability measures (, ), besides membership and non-membership grades. It is important for narrative intelligence systems because the ranking of the emotion and cultural characteristics is a subjective estimation, and the level of confidence is different among experts. One expert might have a strong opinion that a narrative is coherent () but not feel confident that it is ethically trustworthy (). Another expert may not be as confident. The weighted sum with weighted product aggregation operators is developed to take advantage of the dependability between membership and non-membership degrees and also to consider the dependence between the criteria. The functionality of the proposal is improved by the proposed operators. Discussion

The FFZN-based aggregation operators under the Frank norm were applied in this study to evaluate the viability of different strategic alternatives proposed for the StoryWeaver Lab. StoryWeaver Lab is an AI-enabled platform for personal mythology co-creation and intergenerational storytelling under conditions of uncertainty. The fuzzy DM framework developed from FFZNs with Frank aggregation ensured that four core alternatives were systematically assessed. These are the Living Mythology Knowledge Graph (), the Intergenerational Co-Creation Engine (), the Theme and Value Miner (), and the Human-Relationship-First Design approach ().

These options were assessed based on several attributes. These include the Narrative Healing Mode (), the Myth-Object Place connectivity readiness (), the Cross-Cultural Myth connectivity (), and the Transparency Layer differentiating the fact, fiction, and uncertainty (). The framework of FFZN was effective in capturing the complexity, subjectivity, and interpretive uncertainty that AI-motivated storytelling systems have. Conventional decision models cannot adequately characterize these aspects. The skill is particularly noteworthy in the fields of narrative and culture, emotional appeal, credibility of recollection, and symbolic significance. These elements are present and interact in a dynamic way.

The merits of using FFZN aggregation operators according to the Frank norm can be observed in this sense. In particular, the Frank norm provides the opportunity to fine-tune the relationship between evaluation attributes. This makes the decision model capture different levels of dependency among the factors of emotional healing, intergenerational engagement, and cultural continuity. This is essential because the ecosystems of storytelling in the real world rely on such interrelated and contextual narrative components. These components differ across families and cultures.

Moreover, FFZNs are used to standardize the information during the DM process since aggregation is reduced. Having preserved the independence of the elements of truth, indeterminacy, and falsity, and at the same time added the information on reliability in the form of Z-numbers, every alternative is expressed more expressively and richly with all its qualities. Such a description is especially applicable in the next two decades. AI-driven cultural preservation will be associated with the problem of narrative distortion, memory loss, and moral responsibility.

The effectiveness of the proposed FFZN-Frank aggregation framework was checked using a comparison study based on the WASPAS approach that is easily applicable in MAGDM issues. In order to rank the identical alternatives having the identical sets of qualities, a few circumstances were analyzed through a numerical and graphical analysis. The findings proved the consistency and steadiness of the proposed framework because the most appropriate narrative style was always selected.

The results also reveal that the FFZN model on the Frank norm is more adept at modeling the unpredictability, indecisiveness, familiarity of reliability, and interdependence of attributes. The WASPAS method offers a ranking that is founded on compromise through its weighted sum and weighted product machinery. This enhanced the modeling abilities, and hence it was the most appropriate to use in long-term strategic planning in AI cultural heritage and narrative systems. Also, the efficiency of this is shown by the results of the comparison which can be seen in Table 21 and Figures 3, 4, 5, and 6.

Interpretation of correlation results

It is worth noting the properties of each aggregation operator by the correlation analysis carried out between the proposed FFZN-Frank operators and the WASPAS method. The high values of the correlation obtained for , , , and , that are obtained for these operators, indicate that the results of these operators are always very similar to the classical compromise solution of the WASPAS. This is not surprising, as these operators are based on weighted averaging, and the weighting methods are geometric, and these two methods are conceptually consistent with the two methods WASPAS uses in its weighted sum and weighted product foundation. The low (−0.0317) and (0.2469) correlations, however, are of special interest. These operators contain an ordering step that moves the evaluations of the criteria around before aggregating. This ordering alters the aggregation behavior; it is using criteria with a higher number of points and not in the order of the points assigned. A correlation of almost zero for the operator means that this operator gives an almost independent ranking compared to the WASPAS ranking. This is indicative of the fact reordering is the most significant step in aggregation. This behavior is appropriate where decision makers believe that the relative performance of the alternatives along individual criteria should be given more weight than the set weights, particularly when available experts’ confidence is low in the set weights or when the significance of the criteria is highly dependent on the context.

Conclusion

This study proposed a complex decision framework using FFZNs and Frank aggregation operators. This framework tackles the issue of evaluating AI-based personal mythology and storytelling systems amid cultural, personal, and narrative uncertainty. The FF environment has the potential for higher-order concepts, and it also better represents uncertainty, hesitation, and reliability in perception, moral interpretation, and intergenerational sense-making in narratives. This is particularly useful in human-centered narrative ecosystems where the emotional response, retention, and symbolic value are in dynamic and reinforcing conversation with one another. The four strategic options for the StoryWeaver Lab case study were the Living Mythology Knowledge Graph , the Intergenerational Co-Creation Engine , the Theme and Value Miner , and the Human-Relationship-First Design . They were assessed on four dimensions: Narrative Healing Mode , Myth-Object Place Linking , Cross-Cultural Myth Connectivity , and Transparency between Fact, Fiction, and Uncertainty . The fine-grained interdependencies among these criteria were captured in the Frank-based aggregation that extended the DM process beyond the academic rigor criteria to include these other criteria, namely, emotion, perpetuation, ethics, and immersion. The results of the rankings were validated by comparing with the results obtained from the WASPAS approach, which demonstrates the consistency and stability of the model proposed for FFZN-Frank. The aggregation based on FFZN resulted in greater interpretability as it directly addresses uncertainty, non-linearity, and reliability of the expert judgments. This proves the model’s applicability to practical uses in AI storytelling, cultural heritage, and emotionally intelligent systems, extending beyond research.

Practical and policy implications

From this analysis, the following strategic points are brought up for future storytelling platforms: 1) Improving intergenerational engagement, 2) Using the art of storytelling to heal emotional wounds, 3) Cultivating cultural ties by integrating mythology; and 4) Ensuring transparency of stories and their contents, creating stories with the help of AI. Limitations and future research directions

Some of the drawbacks of the present study must be recognized. The case study presented a hypothetical dataset that was used to illustrate the functioning of the framework; further research and studies with real family storytelling data and various cultural contexts are required to validate the framework in the real world. Second, the weights of the criteria were given a priori, which should be determined using a participative approach (such as AHP or Delphi) in future work. Thirdly, the value of the Frank parameter λ was fixed; sensitivity analyses to different values of λ should be carried out and information provided about how to choose the appropriate value of λ in practice. Future work could include: (a) the validation of the framework in the longitudinal sense, with the real AI narrative systems over a longer period of time; (b) extending the framework to include the dynamic updating of the weights as user preferences change; (c) building software tools to implement the operators proposed in the framework for use by practitioners; and (d) comparative studies across different cultural contexts to evaluate the generalizability of the superiority finding. The proposed framework may be a first step on the way to the next generation of culturally responsive, emotionally attuned, ethically considerate, and storytelling platforms. It can be applied to other applications like digitally preserving heritage, emotionally expressive narrative generation systems, or morally oriented AI narrative generation.
