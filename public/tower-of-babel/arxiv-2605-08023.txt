Asymptotics of small eigenvalues on degenerations of Kähler manifolds
Junyu Cao

Abstract
. We derive the exact asymptotic rates of the small eigenvalues of the Laplacian on one-parameter degenerations of compact Kähler manifolds equipped with induced background metrics. This generalizes a recent result of Dai and Yoshikawa to higher dimensions. To achieve this, we combine Li’s uniform Skoda inequality with the method of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations, introduced by Guo–Phong–Song–Sturm–Tong and adapted by Guedj–Tô. As an application, we establish estimates for degenerations of compact Kähler manifolds with reducible singular fibers.



Introduction

Let
\pi\colon X\to S\simeq{\mathbb{D}}
be a proper surjective holomorphic map from a
complex manifold
X
to a Riemann surface biholomorphic to the unit disk.
We assume that
\pi
has connected fibers of complex dimension
n
, that

X_{0}\coloneqq\pi^{-1}(0)
is the unique singular fiber, and that
X
is
equipped with a
Kähler
metric
\omega_{X}
. We refer to this datum as a
one-parameter degeneration of compact
Kähler
manifolds, or

Kähler
degeneration for short.

In this paper, we study the asymptotics of the
small eigenvalues of the Laplacian on the smooth fibers

(\pi^{-1}(s)\eqqcolon X_{s},\omega_{X}\mid_{X_{s}}\eqqcolon\omega_{s})

for
s\neq 0
near the singular fiber.

In
[
47
]
, Yoshikawa proved that the spectrum of the
Laplacian on

(X_{s},\omega_{s})
varies continuously and converges, after a suitable base change and normalization, to the spectrum of the limiting central fiber as
s\to 0
. Precisely, write

X_{0}=\sum_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}D_{\alpha}
as the
sum of its irreducible components
(D_{\alpha})_{1\leq\alpha\leq a}
, and let
m=\prod_{\alpha}m_{\alpha}
.
Consider the following commutative diagram,

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{F^{-1}X}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F^{-1}X\coloneqq X\times_{{\mathbb{D}}_{s}}{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{t}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathbb{D}}_{s}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\iota}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\widehat{\Pi}}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces F}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\Pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\pi}
\scriptstyle{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces t\longmapsto t^{m}}

where
F^{-1}X
is the base-change of the degeneration and

\iota\colon\widehat{F^{-1}X}\to F^{-1}X
is the normalization
of
F^{-1}X
. We define
Z=\widehat{\Pi}^{-1}(0)
, which is a
reduced divisor. Then Yoshikawa’s continuity theorem takes the following form.

Theorem 0.1

(
[
47
]
*Main theorem)
.

As
s\to 0
,
the spectrum of the Laplacian of
(X_{s},\omega_{s})

converges to the spectrum of the Laplacian of

(Z_{\reg},g_{Z})
, where
g_{Z}
is the restriction of the background
metric
\iota^{\ast}F^{\ast}\omega_{X}
. We
remark that
F\circ\iota
is an immersion
on the smooth part of
Z
, so
\iota^{\ast}F^{\ast}\omega_{X}
is a well-defined metric
on
Z_{\reg}
.

Although
[
47
]
is stated for projective degenerations, the same
argument applies to
Kähler
degenerations once one uses a uniform Sobolev
inequality for
Kähler
families; see
[
45
]
*Lemma 3.2.

Let
N
be the number of irreducible components of
Z
. Because
Z_{\reg}
has
N
connected components of finite volume, there are exactly
N
zero eigenvalues in the spectrum of the Laplacian of
(Z_{\reg},g_{Z})
.
On the other hand, for
s\neq 0
, the fiber
X_{s}
is connected, meaning the Laplacian of
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
has exactly one zero eigenvalue, which we denote as
\lambda_{0}(s)=0
.
Let

0=\lambda_{0}(s)<\lambda_{1}(s)\leq\lambda_{2}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N}(s)

be the first
N+1
eigenvalues of the Laplacian of
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
.
By Yoshikawa’s continuity theorem (
Theorem

0.1
), exactly
N
eigenvalues of
X_{s}
must converge to
0
as
s\to 0
.
Therefore, we have

\lambda_{1}(s),\cdots,\lambda_{N-1}(s)\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pt\lambda_{N}(s)\to\lambda_{N,Z}\hskip 10.00002pt\text{as }s\to 0,

where
\lambda_{N,Z}>0
is the first strictly positive eigenvalue of
(Z_{\reg},g_{Z})
.

We call
\lambda_{1}(s),\cdots,\lambda_{N-1}(s)
the
small eigenvalues

of the Laplacian on
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
. Thus small eigenvalues exist if and only
if
N\geq 2
. When
X_{0}
is reduced, we have
Z=X_{0}
, so
N
is the number
of irreducible components of
X_{0}
. In particular, if
\supp(X_{0})
is
reducible, then small eigenvalues occur.

Our first main result gives a logarithmic lower bound for the first positive eigenvalue.

Theorem 0.2

(=
Theorem

1.13
)
.

Let
\lambda_{1}(s)>0
denote the first eigenvalue of the Laplacian
on
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
for
s\neq 0
. Then, there exists
a uniform constant
C>0
, independent of
s
, such that

C\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{1}(s)

for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
.

When
N\geq 2
, so that
\lambda_{1}(s)
is a small eigenvalue, this

\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert
lower bound is optimal.

Theorem 0.3

(=
Theorem

2.3
)
.

Suppose that
N\geq 2
. Then, there exists a uniform constant

C^{\prime}>0
such that

\lambda_{1}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N-1}(s)\leq C^{\prime}\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert

for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
.

Combining
Theorem

0.2
and

Theorem

0.3
yields a complete
characterization of the asymptotic behavior of all small eigenvalues.

Theorem 0.4

(Main theorem)
.

Suppose a small eigenvalue exists, i.e.,
N\geq 2
. Then there exist constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that
for all
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
,

C_{1}\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\leq\lambda_{1}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N-1}(s)\leq C_{2}\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert.

Remark 0.5
.

Due to the continuity and strict positivity of the eigenvalues
away from the singular fiber, the domain of validity can be naturally
extended from
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
to
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Remark 0.6
.

The main theorem generalizes the central result of Dai and Yoshikawa

[
14
]
*Theorem 0.2 for degenerations of Riemann
surfaces, where the singular fiber
X_{0}
is assumed to be reduced. In
contrast,
Theorem

0.4
allows non-reduced and more general
singular fibers, thereby resolving a question raised in

[
14
]
*Problem 9.1.

Remark 0.7
.

In
[
18
]
, Gromov established a polynomial spectral gap,

\lambda_{1}(s)\geq c\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{\alpha}
, for degenerations of
semi-algebraic submanifolds of the standard sphere
{\mathbb{S}}^{N-1}
. Our
logarithmic rate
\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert
is sharper than such a
polynomial bound. Moreover,
Theorem

0.4
shows that this
logarithmic rate is optimal whenever small eigenvalues exist.

We briefly sketch the proof of
Theorem

0.4
. For the upper
bound in
Theorem

0.3
, we adapt the test-function
construction of Dai and Yoshikawa
[
14
]
*Section 6 and
extend it to higher dimensions in
Section

2
. The key
point is that we carry out the relevant estimates directly on a semistable
model of the degeneration, which allows us to remove the reducedness
assumption imposed in
[
14
]
.

The lower bound in
Theorem

0.2
is subtler. As observed in

[
14
]
*Appendix, the curvature of
(X_{s},\omega_{s})

blows up as
s\to 0
, so classical Riemannian eigenvalue estimates based on
uniform lower curvature bounds are not available in our setting. We therefore
take a purely complex-analytic route. More precisely, our method combines
Li’s uniform Skoda inequality for plurisubharmonic functions
[
33
]

with the method of auxiliary Monge–Ampère equations for estimating Green’s
functions
[
24
,
20
]
. By contrast, Dai and
Yoshikawa used analytic torsion
[
4
]
to bypass the
curvature blow-up, but that argument is specific to degenerations of Riemann
surfaces. This is why a new complex-analytic approach is needed in higher
dimensions.

This article is organized as follows. In
Section

1
and

Section

2
, we establish the lower and upper bounds
following the strategy outlined above. In

Section

3.A
, we derive estimates for degenerating
families of plurisubharmonic functions and for solutions to Poisson equations,
valid for general singular fibers. In

Section

3.B
, we present examples of the
small eigenvalue phenomenon for degenerating families equipped with

Kähler
–Einstein metrics. Finally, in
Section

3.C
, we propose a
conjectural non-Archimedean interpretation of this behavior.

Notation and conventions

On a complex manifold
X
, we define

{\mathrm{d}}^{c}=\frac{\sqrt{-1}}{2}(\overline{\partial}-\partial)
,
so
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}=\sqrt{-1}\partial\overline{\partial}
.

On a
Kähler
manifold
(X^{n},\omega)
with
\omega=\sqrt{-1}\sum g_{j\overline{k}}dz_{j}\wedge d\overline{z}_{k}
, we use the positive definite analyst’s Laplacian
\Delta_{\omega}f=-\sum g^{j\overline{k}}\frac{\partial^{2}f}{\partial z_{j}\partial\overline{z}_{k}}
. Under our normalization of
\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}
, we have

\displaystyle-\Delta_{\omega}f=\operatorname{tr}_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}f)=\frac{n\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}f\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}},

\displaystyle\left\lvert\mathrm{d}f\right\rvert^{2}_{\omega}=\operatorname{tr}_{\omega}(\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}f)=\frac{n\mathrm{d}f\wedge\mathrm{d}^{c}f\wedge\omega^{n-1}}{\omega^{n}},

for every smooth function
f
.

For a closed smooth differential form
\alpha
on a compact differential manifold,
we use
[\alpha]
to denote its de Rham cohomology class.

We use
{\mathbb{D}}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\ \left\lvert z\right\rvert<1\}
to denote the unit disk in the complex plane, and
{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}\coloneqq{\mathbb{D}}\setminus\{0\}

the punctured unit disk. For a positive number
r>0
, we also
define
{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\coloneqq\{z\in\mathbb{C}\colon\ \left\lvert z\right\rvert<r\}
and

{\mathbb{D}}_{r}^{\circ}\coloneqq{\mathbb{D}}_{r}\setminus\{0\}
.

We use the following asymptotic analysis notation.
Let
(X,d)
be a metric space, fix a point
s_{0}\in X
,
and let
f
and
g
be functions taking values in a partially
ordered
{\mathbb{R}}
-vector space.
Assuming
f
and
g
are defined on a punctured neighborhood of

s_{0}
, we say that:

•

f=O(g)
(or
f\lesssim g
) as
s\to s_{0}
if there
exists a constant
C>0
, independent of
s
, such that

f\leq Cg
on a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of
s_{0}
.

•

f=\Theta(g)
(or
f\asymp g
) as
s\to s_{0}
if both

f=O(g)
and
g=O(f)
. Equivalently, this holds if there
exist constants
0<C_{1}\leq C_{2}
such that
C_{1}g\leq f\leq C_{2}g
on a sufficiently small punctured neighborhood of
s_{0}
.

Throughout this paper, we use without quoting that every

1
-parameter
Kähler
degeneration admits a semistable reduction
after a proper base change; we refer to
[
28
]
for this result.
We mention that any semistable reduction of a
Kähler
degeneration
is a
Kähler
manifold.

1.
Lower bound

In this section, we prove the lower bound of small eigenvalues
in
Theorem

0.2
. We prove it using
rescaled uniform Skoda estimates in
Section

1.A

together with the method of auxiliary Monge-Ampère equations
in
Section

1.B
. Finally, the lower bound
is proved in
Section

1.C
.

1.A.
A rescaled uniform Skoda estimate

In this section, we prove the following rescaled uniform Skoda
inequality.

Proposition 1.1
.

Let
\pi\colon(X,\omega_{X})\to{\mathbb{D}}_{s}
be a degeneration of

Kähler
manifolds of complex dimension
n
. Assume
X_{0}
is the
unique singular fiber (we allow
X_{0}
to be a general singular fiber).

Let
\beta
be a closed semi-positive form on
X
. Assume that the restriction
\beta_{s}=\beta|_{X_{s}}
is
Kähler
for
s\neq 0
, and denote its volume by
V_{\beta_{s}}=\int_{X_{s}}\beta_{s}^{n}
. Let
\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}=\beta_{s}^{n}/V_{\beta_{s}}
be the normalized volume form on
X_{s}
for
s\neq 0
.

Consider the rescaled relative
Kähler
form
\widetilde{\beta}_{s}=\frac{\beta_{s}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}
on
X\setminus X_{0}
. Then the following Skoda-type estimate holds: there exist constants
\alpha,C>0
independent of
s
, such that for all
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
and for any
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\widetilde{\beta}_{s})
with
\sup_{X_{s}}\varphi=0
, we have

\int_{X_{s}}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}\leq C.

Remark 1.2
.

In
[
33
]
*Theorem 1.3, Li proved a similar rescaled Skoda estimate for normalized Calabi-Yau measures. Although the volume forms
\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}
differ significantly from the Calabi-Yau measures near the singular fiber, the underlying proof strategies are analogous.

\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\widetilde{\beta}_{s})
with
\sup_{X_{s}}\varphi=0
, its pullback satisfies
d(\mu^{*}\varphi)\in\mathrm{PSH}(Y_{t},\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{t})
, where
\widetilde{\beta}^{\prime}_{t}=\frac{\beta^{\prime}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert t\right\rvert\right\rvert}
is the rescaled form
on
Y\setminus Y_{0}
. Assuming the estimate holds in the semistable setting (applied to
(Y,\beta^{\prime})
and
d(\mu^{*}\varphi)
), there exist uniform constants
\alpha,C>0
such that

\int_{Y_{t}}\exp(-(\alpha d)\mu^{*}\varphi)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}\leq C.

Since the normalized volume forms satisfy
\mathrm{d}V_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}=\mu^{*}\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}
, it immediately follows that

\int_{X_{s}}\exp(-(\alpha d)\varphi)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}=\int_{Y_{t}}\exp(-(\alpha d)\mu^{*}\varphi)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta^{\prime}_{t}}\leq C,

which validates the reduction.
∎

•

Reduction to a globally
Kähler
form:
We can assume that
\beta
is a
Kähler
form on the total space

, rather than merely a semi-positive form.

Proof of Reduction.

Suppose the result holds for any globally
Kähler
form
\gamma
on
X
. Let
\beta
be a closed semi-positive form on
X
. Since
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}_{s}
is proper and
\gamma
is strictly positive, there exists a constant
C_{0}>0
such that
0\leq\beta\leq C_{0}\gamma
restricted to a smaller disk, say
X|_{{\mathbb{D}}_{1/2}}
.

Consequently, on each fiber
X_{s}
with
\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
, the volume forms satisfy:

\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}\leq\frac{V_{\gamma_{s}}C_{0}^{n}}{V_{\beta_{s}}}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}\eqqcolon C_{1}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}},

where
C_{1}>0
depends only on
C_{0}
and the cohomology classes of
\beta
and
\gamma
.

Furthermore, if
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\frac{\beta_{s}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert})
, then
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\frac{C_{0}\gamma_{s}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert})
, which implies
\frac{\varphi}{C_{0}}\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\frac{\gamma_{s}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert})
.

Applying our assumption for the
Kähler
form
\gamma
to the function
\frac{\varphi}{C_{0}}
(which satisfies
\sup_{X_{s}}\frac{\varphi}{C_{0}}=0
), there exist constants
\alpha,C>0
independent of
s
such that for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\ll 1
,

\int_{X_{s}}\exp\left(-\alpha\frac{\varphi}{C_{0}}\right)\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}\leq C.

Using the volume bound
\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}\leq C_{1}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}
, we deduce:

\int_{X_{s}}\exp\left(-\frac{\alpha}{C_{0}}\varphi\right)\mathrm{d}V_{\beta_{s}}\leq C\cdot C_{1}.

By setting
\alpha^{\prime}=\alpha/C_{0}
and
C^{\prime}=C\cdot C_{1}
, we recover the Skoda estimate for the semi-positive form
\beta
.
∎

Proof of
Proposition

1.3
.

We choose the charts so that each point on

[

By the local Skoda estimate in
[
33
]

\alpha,A>0
such that on each log scale, we have

(1.2)

\int_{\rm loc}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log}\leq A\int_{\rm loc}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log}.

On each log scale, define the weight function

W(z)\coloneqq\frac{\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}}{\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log}}.

From
Equation

1.1
,

\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}\asymp\underbrace{\sum_{j=0}^{p}\left(\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}i\neq j\\
0\leq i\leq p\end{subarray}}\left\lvert z_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\right)}_{\asymp W(z)}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log},

we obtain
W(z)\asymp\sum_{j=0}^{p}\left(\prod_{i\neq j,\,0\leq i\leq p}\left\lvert z_{i}\right\rvert^{2}\right)=\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{2}\sum_{j=0}^{p}\left\lvert z_{j}\right\rvert^{-2}
. (Here,
\asymp
denotes uniform equivalence independent of

).

By definition of log scales, we have

1\leq\frac{\max_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{i}\right\rvert}{\min_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{i}\right\rvert}\lesssim 4,\hskip 10.00002pt1\leq i\leq p.

Since
\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert=\frac{\left\lvert s\right\rvert}{\prod_{i=1}^{p}\left\lvert z_{i}\right\rvert}
, it follows that

1\leq\frac{\max_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert}{\min_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{0}\right\rvert}\lesssim 4^{p}.

Thus, for the weight function on each log scale, we have

(1.3)

1\leq\frac{\max_{\rm loc}W(z)}{\min_{\rm loc}W(z)}\leq\frac{\sum_{j=0}^{p}(\min_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{j}\right\rvert)^{-2}}{\sum_{j=0}^{p}(\max_{\rm loc}\left\lvert z_{j}\right\rvert)^{-2}}\lesssim 4^{2p}\lesssim 4^{2n}.

(We use
\lesssim
to indicate a uniform upper bound independent of
s
).

Therefore, we deduce a new local Skoda estimate with respect to the measure
\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}
:

\displaystyle\int_{\rm loc}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}

\displaystyle\leq\max_{\rm loc}W(z)\int_{\rm loc}\exp(-\alpha\varphi)\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log}

\displaystyle\leq A\left(\max_{\rm loc}W(z)\right)\int_{\rm loc}\mathrm{d}\nu_{\log}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{Using }\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eqn:log-skoda})

\displaystyle=A\left(\max_{\rm loc}W(z)\right)\int_{\rm loc}\frac{\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}}{W(z)}

\displaystyle\leq A\frac{\max_{\rm loc}W(z)}{\min_{\rm loc}W(z)}\int_{\rm loc}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}

\displaystyle\lesssim 4^{2n}A\int_{\rm loc}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}}\hskip 10.00002pt(\text{Using }\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eqn:volume-over-log-weight-bound})

\displaystyle\leq A^{\prime}\int_{\rm loc}\mathrm{d}V_{\gamma_{s}},

where

is another uniform constant.

[

Let
\Phi_{s}
be the normalized eigenfunction with the first non-zero
eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
, i.e.,

\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\Phi_{s}=\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})\Phi_{s},\hskip 10.00002pt\left\lVert\Phi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s},\omega_{s}^{n})}=1.

Since
\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})
is continuous
in
s\in{\mathbb{D}}
, we have
0\leq\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})\leq\lambda
for some
0<\lambda<\infty
,
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

Recall that
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
satisfies a uniform Sobolev
inequality as it constitutes a family of minimal submanifolds (see
[
45
]
*Lemma 3.2,
[
16
]
*Proposition 3.8). For all
s\neq 0
and
u\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})
, there
is a constant
C_{\rm Sob}>0
independent of
s
and
u
such that

(1.4)

\displaystyle\begin{split}\left(\int_{X_{s}}|u|^{2\nu}\omega_{s}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{\nu}}&\leq C_{\rm Sob}^{2}\left(\int_{X_{s}}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega_{s}}\omega_{s}^{n}+\int_{X_{s}}|u|^{2}\omega_{s}^{n}\right),\\
&\left(\nu=\frac{n}{n-1}\quad\text{when }n\geq 2\right);\\
\left(\int_{X_{s}}|u|^{4}\omega_{s}^{n}\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}&\leq C_{\rm Sob}^{2}\left(\int_{X_{s}}|\nabla u|^{2}_{\omega_{s}}\omega_{s}^{n}+\int_{X_{s}}|u|^{2}\omega_{s}^{n}\right),\quad\text{when }n=1.\end{split}

From a standard Moser iteration (see

[
39
]
*Theorem 9.2.7), we have

V_{\omega_{s}}^{-1/2}=V_{\omega_{s}}^{-1/2}\left\lVert\Phi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s},\omega_{s}^{n})}\leq\left\lVert\Phi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C^{\prime}

for a uniform constant
C^{\prime}=C^{\prime}(C_{\rm Sob},n,\lambda)>0
. Indeed, we have

(1.5)

\left\lVert\Phi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq\exp\left(C_{\rm Sob}\frac{\sqrt{\nu\lambda}}{\sqrt{\nu}-1}\right)\left\lVert\Phi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s},\omega_{s}^{n})}.

Set
\widetilde{\beta}_{s}=\frac{\omega_{s}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}
. Then
\widetilde{\beta}\in{\mathcal{K}}_{\rm Skoda}((X,\omega_{X}),p,1,C_{\rm Skoda},\alpha_{\rm Skoda},\delta)

for any
p>1
, for some
0<\delta<1
and for positive
constants
C_{\rm Skoda},\alpha_{\rm Skoda}
in the uniform Skoda inequality by
Proposition

1.6
.

Note that

\Delta_{\widetilde{\beta}_{s}}\Phi_{s}=\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\Phi_{s}=\left(\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\cdot\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})\right)\Phi_{s},

and

\left\lvert\Delta_{\widetilde{\beta}_{s}}\frac{\Phi_{s}}{C^{\prime}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\cdot\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})}\right\rvert=\left\lvert\frac{\Phi_{s}}{C^{\prime}}\right\rvert\leq 1.

Applying
Proposition

1.11
, note that

\int_{X_{s}}\Phi_{s}\widetilde{\beta}_{s}^{n}=\left\lvert\log^{-n}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\int_{X_{s}}\Phi_{s}\omega_{s}^{n}=0
,
we have

\left\lVert\frac{\Phi_{s}}{C^{\prime}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\cdot\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C^{\prime\prime}

for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert<\delta
and a uniform
C^{\prime\prime}>0
.

Therefore, we obtain

\lambda_{1}(X_{s},\Delta_{\omega_{s}})\geq\left\lVert\frac{\Phi_{s}}{C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\geq C\left\lvert\log^{-1}\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert

for a uniform
C=(C^{\prime}C^{\prime\prime}V_{\omega_{s}}^{1/2})^{-1}>0
.
∎

2.
Upper bound

The main theorem of this section is the following.

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to{\mathbb{D}}_{s}
be a degeneration
of compact
Kähler
manifolds of complex dimension
n
. Suppose
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
is the unique
singular fiber and
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
is a reduced divisor with
simple normal crossings, i.e., the degeneration is
semistable.

Let
\beta
be a closed smooth semi-positive
form on
\mathcal{Y}
such that its restriction on each regular
fiber
\mathcal{Y}_{s}\eqqcolon\pi^{-1}(s)

(s\neq 0)
is
Kähler
. Let

We write
N\coloneqq N(\beta,\mathcal{Y})
and
assume

.

Then for

the first

non-zero eigenvalues of the Laplacian of
(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\beta_{s}\eqqcolon\beta\mid_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}})
, there
exists a constant
C>0
such that

\lambda_{1}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N-1}(s)\leq\frac{C}{\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert^{-1}}

for
0<\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{2}
.

In the first subsection, we show that
Theorem

2.1
implies the upper bound
of small eigenvalues of general degenerations in
Theorem

0.3
.

In the remaining part of this section,
we prove
Theorem

2.1
following
the ideas in
[
47
]
and

[
14
]
*Section 6.
The method is to construct suitable test functions on
\mathcal{Y}_{s}
and apply the min-max
principle to get the upper bound.

2.A.

Assuming

2.1

0.3
.

in

For any semistable reduction

\displaystyle N(X)

Since the induced maps

\mathcal{Y}_{1}
and

.

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces{\mathcal{W}}}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathcal{Y}_{1}}

2.B.
A quantitative retraction à la Dai–Yoshikawa

We work under the geometric assumptions of
Theorem

2.1
. Thus

\pi\colon\mathcal{Y}\to\mathbb{D}
is a semistable degeneration of compact Kähler manifolds of
complex dimension
n
, the central fiber
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
is a reduced simple normal crossings divisor,
and
\gamma
is a fixed Kähler metric on
\mathcal{Y}
. We write

\mathcal{Y}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{a}D_{i},\hskip 20.00003pt\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0})=\bigcup_{1\leq i<j\leq a}(D_{i}\cap D_{j}),

and

\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}:=\mathcal{Y}_{0}\setminus\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),

and we denote by
d_{\gamma}
the distance induced by
\gamma
. For a subset
A\subset\mathcal{Y}
and

r>0
, we set

B_{\gamma}(A,r):=\{y\in\mathcal{Y}\mid d_{\gamma}(y,A)<r\},\hskip 10.00002pt\overline{B}_{\gamma}(A,r):=\{y\in\mathcal{Y}\mid d_{\gamma}(y,A)\leq r\}.

Proposition 2.4

(Quantitative retraction)
.

Fix an integer
\nu\geq n
, and set

\epsilon(s):=2|s|^{\frac{1}{4\nu}}
.
Then, after shrinking
\mathbb{D}
if necessary, there exists a family of diffeomorphisms

F_{s}\colon\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\gamma}(\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),\epsilon(s))\longrightarrow F_{s}\bigl(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\gamma}(\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),\epsilon(s))\bigr)\subset\mathcal{Y}_{s}

with the following properties.

(1)

F_{0}=\mathrm{id}_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}}
.

(2)

For every
z
in the domain of
F_{s}
,

d_{\gamma}(F_{s}(z),z)\leq K_{1}|s|^{\frac{3}{4}}.

(3)

If
\beta
is a smooth differential form on
\mathcal{Y}
, then

\left\lVert F_{s}^{*}\beta_{s}-\beta_{0}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\gamma}(\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),\epsilon(s)))}\leq K_{2,\beta}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}.

(4)

Let
\gamma_{s}:=\gamma|_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}
, and let
(F_{s})_{*}\chi
denote the push-forward of a
test function
\chi
by
F_{s}
, extended by
0
outside the image of
F_{s}
. Then for all

\chi,\chi^{\prime}\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\gamma}(\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),\epsilon(s)))
,

\left|\bigl((F_{s})_{*}\chi,(F_{s})_{*}\chi^{\prime}\bigr)_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\gamma_{s})}-(\chi,\chi^{\prime})_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}\right|\leq K_{3}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}\left\lVert\chi^{\prime}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})},

and

\left|\left\lVert d\bigl((F_{s})_{*}\chi\bigr)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\gamma_{s})}^{2}-\left\lVert d\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}^{2}\right|\leq K_{3}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert d\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}^{2}.

In particular,

\left|\left\lVert d\bigl((F_{s})_{*}\chi\bigr)\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{s},\gamma_{s})}-\left\lVert d\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}\right|\leq K_{3}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert d\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}.

(5)

Let
\alpha
be a smooth
(1,1)
-form on
\mathcal{Y}
. Then for every

\chi\in C_{0}^{\infty}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}}\setminus\overline{B}_{\gamma}(\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}),\epsilon(s)))
,

\left|\int_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}(F_{s})_{*}\chi\,\alpha_{s}^{n}-\int_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}\chi\,\alpha_{0}^{n}\right|\leq K_{4,\alpha}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert\chi\right\rVert_{L^{1}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})},

and

\left|\int_{\mathcal{Y}_{s}}d\bigl((F_{s})_{*}\chi\bigr)\wedge d^{c}\bigl((F_{s})_{*}\chi\bigr)\wedge\alpha_{s}^{n-1}-\int_{\mathcal{Y}_{0}}d\chi\wedge d^{c}\chi\wedge\alpha_{0}^{n-1}\right|\leq K_{4,\alpha}|s|^{\frac{1}{2}}\left\lVert d\chi\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0}^{\mathrm{reg}},\gamma_{0})}^{2}.

Remark 2.5
.

The construction of the retraction using a vector flow is classic,
as seen in Clemens’ retraction
[
12
,
13
]
and in
[
29
]
*Proof of Th. 2.3.
To the author’s knowledge, a quantitative version of this retraction first appeared
in
[
14
]
*Section 6
for the degeneration of Riemann surfaces.
This flow construction was also utilized recently
in
[
6
]
*Section 2
to study complex Monge-Ampère equations.

We construct a family of diffeomorphisms which sends
the smooth part of the singular fiber to nearby smooth fibers.
The idea is to pick a
C^{\infty}
complex vector field
v
on
\mathcal{Y}\setminus\mathrm{Crit}(\pi)=\mathcal{Y}\setminus\Sing\mathcal{Y}_{0}
satisfying
\pi_{*}v=\partial/\partial s
, and use this vector field to flow points on the singular fiber to nearby smooth fibers.

We begin with a direct Łojasiewicz-type
estimate, imitating the same estimate in

[
14
]
*Lemma 6.2.

Lemma 2.6
.

There exists a constant
c_{0}>0
such that

\left\lVert d\pi(z)\right\rVert_{\gamma}^{2}\geq c_{0}\,d_{\gamma}\bigl(z,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\bigr)^{2n}

for all
z\in\pi^{-1}(\mathbb{D}_{\rho})
, after shrinking
\rho>0
if necessary. In particular,
for every integer
\nu\geq n
there exists
c_{\nu}>0
such that

\left\lVert d\pi(z)\right\rVert_{\gamma}^{2}\geq c_{\nu}\,d_{\gamma}\bigl(z,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\bigr)^{2\nu}.

Proof.

Choose finitely many adapted coordinate charts
U_{\lambda}\Subset V_{\lambda}
covering a neighborhood
of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
, where
V_{\lambda}
carries coordinates

(z_{0},\dots,z_{n})
such that

\pi=z_{0}\cdots z_{p}

for some
0\leq p\leq n
, and the components of
\mathcal{Y}_{0}
meeting
V_{\lambda}
are given by

\{z_{0}=0\},\dots,\{z_{p}=0\}
. On each
U_{\lambda}
the metric
\gamma
is uniformly
equivalent to the Euclidean metric
g_{\rm Euc}
, so it suffices to work in these coordinates.

In
V_{\lambda}
we have

d\pi=\sum_{j=0}^{p}z_{0}\cdots\widehat{z_{j}}\cdots z_{p}\,dz_{j},

hence

\left\lVert d\pi(z)\right\rVert_{g_{\rm Euc}}^{2}=\sum_{j=0}^{p}\bigl|z_{0}\cdots\widehat{z_{j}}\cdots z_{p}\bigr|^{2}.

After reordering the coordinates, we may assume

|z_{0}|\leq|z_{1}|\leq\cdots\leq|z_{p}|.

Since
\{z_{0}=z_{1}=0\}\subset\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0})
, we have

d_{\gamma}\bigl(z,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0})\bigr)^{2}\lesssim|z_{0}|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2}.

On the other hand,

\left\lVert d\pi(z)\right\rVert_{g_{\rm Euc}}^{2}\geq|z_{1}\cdots z_{p}|^{2}+|z_{0}z_{2}\cdots z_{p}|^{2}\geq|z_{1}|^{2p}+|z_{0}|^{2p}\geq 2^{1-p}\bigl(|z_{0}|^{2}+|z_{1}|^{2}\bigr)^{p}.

Therefore

We verify that

In

This verifies (2).

By construction, every local factor lies between

and

We now determine the constants for the distance property. Let

with

Case 3
. Moreover,

(2.7)

\displaystyle L_{1}\min_{1\leq i\leq p}\left\lvert y_{i}\right\rvert\leq d_{\gamma}(y,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}))\hskip 10.00002pt\text{if }d_{\gamma}(y,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}))<r_{0},

\displaystyle d_{\gamma}(y,\Sing(\mathcal{Y}_{0}))\leq L_{2}\min_{1\leq i\leq p}\left\lvert y_{i}\right\rvert.

We take
\epsilon_{0}=(r_{0}/c_{2})^{2}

We verify the distance property in (4).

If

Conversely, if

\displaystyle\left\lVert\mathrm{d}\chi^{(1)}_{\epsilon}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(\mathcal{Y}_{0},\gamma)}^{2}

\displaystyle\leq C\int_{A_{\epsilon}}\left\lvert\sum_{\alpha}P_{\epsilon,\alpha}\mathrm{d}\eta_{\alpha}\right\rvert_{\gamma}^{2}\,dV_{\gamma}

Lastly, we prove the following lemma used above.

Lemma 2.13
.

With the finite adapted covering fixed above, there exists

0<r_{0}<e^{-1}
such that the following holds. If

Proof.

Since charts of

If

Case 2
can contain

with

to

3.
Applications and Discussions

3.A.
Applications in geometric analysis

Let
\pi\colon(X,\omega_{X})\to{\mathbb{D}}_{s}
be a degeneration of
Kähler
manifolds of complex dimension
n
. Assume
X_{0}
is the unique singular fiber. Let
\omega_{s}\eqqcolon\omega_{X}|_{X_{s}}
denote the restricted metric on the smooth fibers
X_{s}
.

When
X_{0}
is reduced and irreducible,
the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian
on
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
is uniformly bounded away from
0
as
s\to 0
.
Consequently, there exists a uniform constant
C>0
such that the
following
L^{2}
-Poincaré inequality holds for all

s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
and all
f\in C^{\infty}(X_{s})
:

(3.1)

\left\lVert f-\frac{\int_{X_{s}}f\omega_{s}^{n}}{\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}^{n}}\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s},\omega_{s})}\leq C\left\lVert\mathrm{d}f\right\rVert_{L^{2}(X_{s},\omega_{s})}.

This uniform inequality is widely utilized in geometric settings
involving the degeneration of complex manifolds (see, for example,

[
42
]
*Proposition 3.2,

[
6
]
*Appendix,
[
16
]
*
Proposition 3.10,
[
38
]
*Proposition 2.2
and
[
17
]
*Theorem 3.2.4).

When
X_{0}
is not irreducible, it is well known that the uniform Poincaré
constant in
Equation

3.1
blows up as
s\to 0
.
However,
Theorem

0.4
ensures that this rate of
blow-up is strictly controlled. This control ultimately yields the
following lower bound for the Green function
G_{s}(x,y)
on the
fibers
(X_{s},\omega_{s})
, which remains valid even for general
singular fibers:

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
G_{s}(x,y)
be the Green function of the
Kähler
manifold

(X_{s},\omega_{s})
for
s\neq 0
. Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

G_{s}(x,y)\geq-C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert

for all

s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

Let
\{\Phi_{i}(s,z)\}_{i\geq 1}
be eigenfunctions
of
\Delta_{\omega_{s}}
with non-zero eigenvalues
and normalized
L^{2}
-norms.
Let
k_{0}
be the number of small eigenvalues.
Then we have

G_{s}(x,y)=\underbrace{\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(s)}\Phi_{k}(s,x)\Phi_{k}(s,y)}_{\eqqcolon G_{s,\mathrm{low}}(x,y)}+\underbrace{\sum_{k=k_{0}+1}^{\infty}\frac{1}{\lambda_{k}(s)}\Phi_{k}(s,x)\Phi_{k}(s,y)}_{\eqqcolon G_{s,\mathrm{high}}(x,y)}.

Since
\lambda_{k_{0}+1}(s)
is not a small eigenvalue, it
is uniformly bounded away from
0
. Consequently, a classical
argument by Cheng and Li
[
10
]
ensures that

G_{s,\mathrm{high}}(x,y)\geq-C_{1}
for some uniform constant

C_{1}>0
. (See also

[
7
]
*Proposition 3.6, noting that Theorem 3.7 used therein can be replaced by the higher-dimensional version in
[
8
]
*Theorem 2.1.)

For the remaining terms,
Theorem

0.4
provides control over the
small eigenvalues. Furthermore, uniform Sobolev
inequalities combined with Moser iteration bound
the
L^{\infty}
-norms of the corresponding small
eigenfunctions. Indeed, from
Equation

1.5
, we have

\left\lVert\Phi_{k}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq\exp\left(C_{\rm Sob}\frac{\sqrt{\nu\lambda}}{\sqrt{\nu}-1}\right).

Here
\lambda>0
is a uniform bound
for small eigenvalues, i.e.,

0<\lambda_{1}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{k_{0}}(s)\leq\lambda<\infty
for
s\in{\mathbb{D}}_{1/2}
, and
\nu=\nu(n)
is the exponent in
the Sobolev inequality.

Together, these bounds yield

G_{s,\mathrm{low}}(x,y)\geq-C_{2}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert

for a uniform constant
C_{2}>0
.

Summing these estimates yields the desired bound
G_{s}(x,y)\geq-C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert
.
∎

The estimate of the fiberwise Green functions leads to the
following two applications.

3.A.1.
Estimates of families of plurisubharmonic functions

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
\theta
be a smooth
(1,1)
-form on
X
. Then there is a
uniform constant
C_{1}>0
such that for
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

C_{1}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})}\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\omega_{s}^{n}-\sup_{X_{s}}\varphi\leq 0

for all
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\theta_{s})
. Here
\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})=\int_{X_{s}}\omega_{s}^{n}
is a positive constant
determined by the cohomology class of
\omega_{X}
.

Proof.

Since
\pi^{-1}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}_{3/4}})
is compact, we take
a large constant
L>0
such that
\theta<L\omega_{X}

on
\pi^{-1}(\overline{{\mathbb{D}}_{3/4}})
. Then for
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{1/2}
, we have
\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\theta_{s})\subset\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},L\omega_{s})
. So without loss of generality,
we assume
\theta=\omega_{s}
.

Since
\omega_{s}+\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi\geq 0
for
\varphi\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\omega_{s})
, we have

(3.2)

-\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi=\mathrm{tr}_{\omega_{s}}(\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi)=\frac{n\mathrm{d}\mathrm{d}^{c}\varphi\wedge\omega_{s}^{n-1}}{\omega_{s}^{n}}\geq-n.

Note that we use the positive definite Laplacian. So

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})}\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\omega_{s}^{n}-\varphi(z)=\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})(-\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi)(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime}).

By
Proposition

3.1
, we have

\displaystyle\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}(G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert)(-\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi)(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}(G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert)(-n)\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle(\text{Using }G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\geq 0\text{ and }\lx@cref{creftype\lx@tilde refnum}{eqn:Laplacian-of-psh})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert(-n)\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})=-nC\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert

\displaystyle(\text{Using }\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})=0).

Since
\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert(-\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi)\omega_{s}^{n}=0
, we have

\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})}\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\omega_{s}^{n}-\varphi(z)=\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}(G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert)(-\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi)(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})\geq C_{1}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert.

Here
C_{1}=n\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})C
.

So we have

C_{1}\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\leq\frac{1}{\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})}\int_{X_{s}}\varphi\omega_{s}^{n}-\sup_{X_{s}}\varphi\leq 0.

∎

Remark 3.3
.

When the singular fiber
X_{0}
is reduced and irreducible,
the
\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert
factor can be removed due to

[
16
]
*Conjecture 3.1 and
[
37
]
*Corollary 4.8.

Remark 3.4
.

The
\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert
factor is optimal. Indeed, in
[
16
]
*Example 3.5, they construct a family of plurisubharmonic
functions
\varphi_{s}\in\mathrm{PSH}(X_{s},\omega_{s})
so that
\sup_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}=0
and

\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}\omega_{s}^{n}\leq C\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert

for a constant
C>0
.

3.A.2.
Estimates of families of Poisson equations

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
g
be a continuous function on
X

and suppose it satisfies the integrability condition on all smooth fibers
X_{s}
(
s\neq 0
),

\int_{X_{s}}g\omega_{s}^{n}=0.

Then for
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, let
\varphi_{s}
be the unique
solution to the Poisson equation on
X_{s}
, i.e.,

\Delta_{\omega_{s}}\varphi_{s}=g\mid_{X_{s}},\hskip 10.00002pt\int_{X_{s}}\varphi_{s}\omega_{s}^{n}=0.

We have a positive constant
C_{2}>0
independent of

g
such that

\left\lVert\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}\leq C_{2}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}

for all
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
.

Proof.

For
s\in{\mathbb{D}}^{\circ}_{\frac{1}{2}}
, we have

\varphi_{s}(z)=\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}G_{s}(z,z^{\prime})g(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime}).

Using
G_{\omega_{s}}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\geq 0
in
Proposition

3.1
and

g+\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\geq 0
, we obtain that

\displaystyle\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}G_{s}(z,z^{\prime})g(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}(G_{s}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert)g(z^{\prime})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle\geq\int_{z^{\prime}\in X_{s}}(G_{s}(z,z^{\prime})+C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert)(-\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}})\omega_{s}^{n}(z^{\prime})

\displaystyle=-C\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\mathrm{vol}(\omega_{s})\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert.

Here
\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}}\coloneqq\left\lVert g\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}
. For the reverse direction, we consider
-g
. This completes the
proof.
∎

Remark 3.6
.

In the study of holomorphic dynamics, we need to solve fiberwise
Poisson equations for degenerating families of

Kähler
manifolds, e.g. in
[
17
]
*Theorem 3.2.4
and in
[
7
]
.

Remark 3.7
.

When we assume the degeneration
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}

to be semistable and the continuous function
g
also satisfies
the integrability condition on central fibers,

\int_{D}g\omega^{n}=0,

where
D
runs over all irreducible components of the singular
fiber, then we have better estimates on
\varphi_{s}
, the solution
to the fiberwise Poisson equations in
Proposition

3.5
. Indeed, following the same argument
in
[
7
]
*Theorem 3.5, we have

\frac{\left\lVert\varphi_{s}\right\rVert_{L^{\infty}(X_{s})}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{1/2}}\to 0,\hskip 10.00002pts\to 0.

3.B.
Small eigenvalues of degenerating
Kähler
-Einstein manifolds

In this subsection, we demonstrate that small eigenvalues also
appear for degenerating families of compact
Kähler
manifolds with
canonical metrics.

Example 3.8
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a degenerating family of Calabi–Yau manifolds,
polarized by a relatively ample line bundle
L
. We equip each smooth fiber
X_{s}

(
s\neq 0
) with the unique Ricci-flat Calabi–Yau metric
\omega^{\rm CY}_{s}

representing the Kähler class
c_{1}(L)|_{X_{s}}
. Let
\lambda_{k}^{\rm CY}(s)

denote the
k
-th non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})
.

Since
(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})
is a compact Riemannian manifold with non-negative Ricci curvature, classical results of Cheng

[
11
]
*Corollary 2.2 and Wu–Yang–Zhong
[
46
]
*Theorem 14.2 yield the bounds:

\frac{1}{\mathrm{diam}(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})^{2}}\leq\lambda_{k}^{\rm CY}(s)\leq\frac{8k^{2}n(n+2)}{\mathrm{diam}(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})^{2}},

where
n
is the complex dimension of
X_{s}
and
\mathrm{diam}(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})
is the diameter of
(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})
.

In light of recent developments in the geometry of degenerating Calabi–Yau manifolds,
the asymptotic behavior of
\lambda_{k}^{\rm CY}(s)
depends entirely on the dimension
of the essential skeleton
\mathrm{Sk}(X)
associated to the degeneration
(see
[
30
,
36
]
for the definition).
Specifically, we have two cases:

•

If
\dim\mathrm{Sk}(X)=0
, a result of Rong–Zhang
[
41
]
*Theorem 1.4
ensures that the diameter is uniformly bounded from above and below; that is,
there exists a constant
C>0
independent of
s
such that

C^{-1}\leq\mathrm{diam}(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})\leq C
. Consequently, there exist
uniform constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that for all
k\geq 1
,

C_{1}\leq\lambda_{k}^{\rm CY}(s)\leq C_{2}k^{2}.

In this case, there are no small eigenvalues.

•

If
\dim\mathrm{Sk}(X)\geq 1
, a recent theorem of Li–Tosatti

[
32
]
*Theorem 1.1 establishes that the diameter grows
on the order of
\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}
. That is, for a uniform constant

C>0
, we have
C^{-1}\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}\leq\mathrm{diam}(X_{s},\omega^{\rm CY}_{s})\leq C\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{\frac{1}{2}}
.
It then follows that

\frac{C_{1}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}\leq\lambda_{k}^{\rm CY}(s)\leq\frac{C_{2}k^{2}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}

for uniform constants
C_{1},C_{2}>0
and all
k\geq 1
. In this case,
each fixed non-zero eigenvalue tends to
0
.

Example 3.9
.

Let
\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a degenerating family
of hyperbolic curves. We equip each smooth fiber
X_{s}

(
s\neq 0
) with its unique Kähler–Einstein
(hyperbolic) metric

\omega^{\rm hyp}_{s}\in c_{1}(K_{X_{s}})
. Let
\lambda_{k}^{\rm hyp}(s)

denote the
k
-th non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian on
(X_{s},\omega^{\rm hyp}_{s})
.

Assume first that the degeneration is stable. By the results of
Schoen–Wolpert–Yau
[
43
]
and Masur
[
35
]
(see
also
[
19
]
), there are exactly

N-1
small eigenvalues satisfying the following asymptotic bounds:

\frac{C_{1}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert}\leq\lambda_{1}^{\rm hyp}(s)\leq\cdots\leq\lambda_{N-1}^{\rm hyp}(s)\leq\frac{C_{2}}{\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert},

where
N
is the number of irreducible components
of the central fiber
X_{0}
, and
C_{1},C_{2}>0
are constants independent of
s
.

For a general degeneration of hyperbolic curves, similar asymptotics
can be deduced by passing to its stable model via the Deligne–Mumford
stable reduction theorem
[
15
]
. We remark that the base change involved in stable
reduction induces isometries on the regular fibers, as the hyperbolic
metric is canonically determined by the complex structure.

Remark 3.10
.

We expect similar eigenvalue asymptotics to hold for
degenerations of compact Kähler manifolds of general type
equipped with canonical Kähler–Einstein metrics.
This would generalize the behavior observed in

Example

3.9
to higher dimensions.

Remark 3.11
.

For a degenerating family of Fano manifolds equipped with canonical Kähler–Einstein metrics, there are no small eigenvalues. This follows immediately from the classical theorem of Lichnerowicz, which guarantees a uniform positive lower bound for the first non-zero eigenvalue of the Laplacian.

3.C.
Non-Archimedean picture for
small eigenvalues

We now explain how the appearance of small eigenvalues is
reflected in the non-Archimedean limit of a degeneration, in the sense
of hybrid convergence of Boucksom–Jonsson.

We first recall the relevant convergence results for complex
Monge–Ampère measures. Let

\pi\colon X\to{\mathbb{D}}
be a projective degeneration of compact complex
manifolds, with central fiber
X_{0}
.

•

Suppose that
X
is a Calabi–Yau degeneration polarized by a
relatively ample line bundle
L
. We equip each smooth fiber
X_{s}

(
s\neq 0
) with the unique Ricci-flat Calabi–Yau metric

\omega_{\mathrm{CY},s}
representing

c_{1}(L)|_{X_{s}}
. Boucksom–Jonsson

[
5
]
proved that the Monge–Ampère
measures
\omega_{\mathrm{CY},s}^{n}
converge in
X^{\rm hyb}
to a
Lebesgue-type measure on the top-dimensional part of the essential
skeleton
\mathrm{Sk}(X)
.

•

Suppose that
X
is a degeneration of compact complex manifolds of
general type. We equip each smooth fiber
X_{s}
(
s\neq 0
) with
its unique Kähler–Einstein metric

\omega_{\mathrm{KE},s}\in c_{1}(K_{X_{s}})
. Pille-Schneider

[
40
]
*Theorem A proved that the
Monge–Ampère measures
\omega_{\mathrm{KE},s}^{n}
converge in

X^{\rm hyb}
to a Dirac-type measure supported on the divisorial
valuations corresponding to the irreducible components of the
central
fiber of the canonical model
\mathcal{X}_{c}
of
X
. More precisely,

\mu=\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Irr}((\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0})}((K_{\mathcal{X}_{c}})^{n}\cdot D)\delta_{v_{D}}.

•

Let
\omega_{\mathrm{bg},s}\coloneqq\omega_{X}|_{X_{s}}
be the
restriction of a background Kähler metric. Pille-Schneider

[
40
]
*Theorem B proved that the
Monge–Ampère measures
\omega_{\mathrm{bg},s}^{n}
converge in

X^{\rm hyb}
to a Dirac-type measure supported on the divisorial
valuations corresponding to the irreducible components of
the central
fiber of a normal model
\mathcal{X}
of
X
with reduced central
fiber. More precisely,

\mu=\sum_{D\in\mathrm{Irr}(\mathcal{X}_{0})}\left(\int_{D}(\nu^{*}\omega_{X})^{n}\right)\delta_{v_{D}},

where
\nu\colon\mathcal{X}\to X
is the induced map.

We next compare these non-Archimedean limits with the spectral behavior
of degenerating curves. We use two elementary spectral models for graphs. First, for a
finite connected graph with positive vertex masses, we choose positive
edge conductances
c_{e}
(for instance
c_{e}=1
) and use the weighted graph
Laplacian on
\ell^{2}(V,\mu)
,

(\Delta_{\Gamma,\mu}f)(v)=\frac{1}{\mu(v)}\sum_{w\sim v}c_{vw}\bigl(f(v)-f(w)\bigr).

This is the usual weighted graph Laplacian with vertex measure; see

[
31
]
*Section 2, especially (2.3)–(2.7)
with trivial signature. Its Dirichlet energy is

\sum_{\{v,w\}\in E}c_{vw}\left\lvert f(v)-f(w)\right\rvert^{2},

so, since the graph is connected, the kernel consists exactly of the
constant functions. Hence, if the graph has
N
vertices, the Laplacian
has one zero eigenvalue and
N-1
positive eigenvalues.

Second, a compact metric graph is a finite graph whose edges are assigned
positive lengths, so that each edge is identified with a compact interval.
On such a graph, we use the standard Kirchhoff Laplacian: it is

-d^{2}/dx^{2}
on each edge, with continuity of functions and the Kirchhoff
condition at every vertex. Its spectrum is discrete and satisfies Weyl’s
law, so it has infinitely many positive eigenvalues; see

[
3
]
*Sections 2 and 4.1, especially Lemma 4.4.

•

For degenerations of hyperbolic curves with Kähler–Einstein
metrics
(X_{s},\omega_{\mathrm{KE},s})
, the complex-analytic theory
gives exactly
N-1
small eigenvalues, each of order

\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{-1}
, where
N
is the number of irreducible
components of the central fiber of the canonical model

\mathcal{X}_{c}
; see
Example

3.9
.

On the non-Archimedean side, the measures

\omega_{\mathrm{KE},s}
converge to a Dirac-type measure supported
on the divisorial points corresponding to these irreducible
components. Since we are working with a stable model, each component
carries strictly positive mass. Thus the dual graph of

(\mathcal{X}_{c})_{0}
, together with the vertex masses

v\mapsto\mu(v)
, becomes a weighted graph. Its weighted graph
Laplacian has exactly
N-1
positive eigenvalues. When
N=2
, Ji

[
27
]
proved that the rescaled eigenvalue

\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\,\lambda_{1}(s)
converges to the corresponding
graph-theoretic eigenvalue; see also

[
19
]
*Conjecture 9. We expect
the same spectral convergence for arbitrary
N
.

•

For degenerations of complex curves with induced background metrics

(X_{s},\omega_{\mathrm{bg},s})
, we take as normal model the space

\widehat{F^{-1}X}
introduced in
Equation

2.1
, and
denote its central fiber by
Z
.

The complex-analytic results give exactly
N-1
small eigenvalues,
each of order
\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{-1}
, where
N
is the number of
irreducible components of
Z
; see
Theorem

0.4
.
Meanwhile, the non-Archimedean convergence theorem gives a
Dirac-type limiting measure supported on the components of
Z
.
Each of these components has strictly positive mass by

Lemma

2.2
. Hence the dual graph of
Z
,
weighted by
v\mapsto\mu(v)
, again has a weighted graph
Laplacian with exactly
N-1
positive eigenvalues.

In the case
N=2
, Dai–Yoshikawa proved that
the
rescaled limit

\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert\,\lambda_{1}(s)
exists (see

[
14
]
*Problem 9.2). We expect that, for general

N
, the rescaled small eigenvalues converge to the positive
eigenvalues of the weighted graph Laplacian of the corresponding
dual graph.

•

For degenerations of elliptic curves polarized by a relatively
ample line bundle
L
, we equip each smooth fiber
X_{s}
(
s\neq 0
)
with the unique Ricci-flat Calabi–Yau metric

\omega_{\mathrm{CY},s}
representing
c_{1}(L)|_{X_{s}}
.

The complex geometry gives two sharply different possibilities:
there are either no small eigenvalues or infinitely many, depending
on the dimension of the essential skeleton
\mathrm{Sk}(X)
; see

Example

3.8
. The non-Archimedean picture gives the
same dichotomy. If
\dim\mathrm{Sk}(X)=0
, then

\mathrm{Sk}(X)
is a point by connectedness, and the limiting
measure is a Dirac measure. The corresponding graph has one vertex,
so its weighted graph Laplacian has no positive eigenvalues.

If
\dim\mathrm{Sk}(X)=1
, then after passing to a semistable
model, the essential skeleton is a metric graph homeomorphic to

{\mathbb{S}}^{1}
. The limiting measure is Lebesgue-type on the edges, with
positive density. The resulting metric graph Laplacian behaves like
the Laplacian on a circle, and therefore has infinitely many
positive eigenvalues.

Remark 3.12
.

When
X
is a degeneration of compact hyperbolic curves equipped
with the Arakelov–Bergman metrics, the corresponding
non-Archimedean spectral convergence picture is established in the
work of Amini–Nicolussi
[
1
]
. We also recall
that the hybrid limit of the Arakelov–Bergman measures
is the Zhang measure, as proved by Shivaprasad

[
44
]
and by Amini–Nicolussi

[
2
]
independently.

In summary, these examples suggest a spectral convergence principle for
small eigenvalues under hybrid convergence. On the complex side,
the scale
\left\lvert\log\left\lvert s\right\rvert\right\rvert^{-1}
appears for induced metrics in

Theorem

0.4
, for Calabi–Yau metrics in

Example

3.8
, and for hyperbolic metrics in

Example

3.9
. On the non-Archimedean side, the
convergence of the Monge–Ampère measures
\omega_{s}^{n}
is known from
work of Boucksom–Jonsson in the Calabi–Yau setting, and from work of
Pille-Schneider for induced metrics and for general type degenerations.

In higher dimensions, several substantial pieces of this picture are
already available, including non-Archimedean pluripotential theory,
hybrid convergence of Monge–Ampère measures, and metric convergence
results for special classes of Calabi–Yau degenerations. However, to
the best of the author’s knowledge, a general canonical spectral object
on
X^{\mathrm{an}}
, or on
\mathrm{Sk}(X)
, together with a
convergence theorem for the rescaled Dirichlet forms and spectra, is not
yet available with the same level of generality as in the
one-dimensional theory of Amini–Nicolussi.
