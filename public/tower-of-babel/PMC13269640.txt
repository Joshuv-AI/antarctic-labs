Highly cited original research in microneedle science from 2015 to 2025 a bibliometric and altmetric analysis
Authors: Xuanrui Chen, Qian Zhang, Changbin Zhao, Guangbin Wang, Hailing Ren, Mingjing Yang, Shihui Liu, Tao Liu, Bin Guo
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
This study comprehensively analyzes the top 100 highly cited publications in microneedle (MN) research to reveal convergences and divergences in academic and public interest, assess the current research landscape and its impact, and offer data-driven support for clarifying future research directions.We identified highly cited microneedle articles published from 2015 to July 15, 2025 using the Web of Science database, validated the records through Scopus and Google Scholar, and gathered Altmetric attention scores from Altmetric.Extracted data encompassed citation counts, publication years, journals, authors, affiliations, and countries, which we summarized using descriptive statistics and visualized with CiteSpace, VOSviewer, and R for correlation analysis.From 8502 documents, the top 100 most-cited original research articles were selected. These articles appeared in 43 English-language journals and accrued 23,838 citations. The highest annual output occurred in 2020, with 18 publications and 4172 citations, while the top 10 journals together accounted for more than 60% of the highly cited articles. Zhen Gu authored the most articles (18), and the University of North Carolina at Chapel Hill led all institutions with 17 contributions.Our analysis suggests that microneedle research has expanded beyond conventional transdermal delivery into a diverse technological ecosystem encompassing clinical therapy, immunomodulation, smart diagnostics and therapeutics, and material sustainability. Among the most-cited microneedle articles in this analysis, Altmetric Attention Scores and citation metrics captured distinct dimensions of influence, with the former correlating more strongly with news and social media dissemination than with traditional citation counts. This divergence indicates a partial disconnect between public visibility and scholarly impact in the field.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s11671-026-04709-0.

Introduction

Microneedle technology, an innovative platform for drug delivery and biosensing [1], employs micrometer-scale projections to penetrate the stratum corneum minimally invasively, thereby facilitating effective drug permeation and precise biological signal detection [2, 3]. In contrast to conventional transdermal methods that depend on passive diffusion and are limited by the stratum corneum, microneedles substantially lower the skin's barrier resistance to hydrophilic drugs, biomolecules, and vaccines. This reduction significantly broadens the scope of drugs and therapeutic scenarios suitable for transdermal administration. Additionally, by enabling transdermal delivery, microneedles can partly circumvent first-pass metabolism associated with oral dosing while also diminishing the pain, needle-stick risks, and potential infections linked to hypodermic injections [4–6].

The field of microneedle technology continues to advance through the multidimensional convergence of structural innovation, manufacturing upgrades, and application expansion. Structurally, microneedles have progressed from early solid-core designs[7] to diverse forms such as hollow-core [8], coated [9], dissolving [10], layered [11, 12], and biomimetic structures [13, 14]. Recent platforms integrate controlled-release interfaces, responsive elements, and multifunctional modules to achieve intelligent, system-integrated designs [15–18]. Manufacturing processes have evolved from traditional microelectromechanical systems (MEMS) lithography and etching [19, 20] to micro-molding techniques combined with centrifugation and vacuum forming [21–23], and now to high-freedom models centered on 3D printing, including two-photon polymerization (2PP) [24], digital light processing (DLP) [25], and stereolithography (SLA) [26]. The introduction of AI-assisted parameter optimization [27] and 4D printing [28] has further enhanced capabilities in structural complexity, functional tunability, and flexible integration. Concurrently, novel materials like biodegradable polymers and conductive hydrogels [29–31] are enabling the development of flexible wearable microneedle systems [32, 33], fostering interdisciplinary convergence. In application, microneedle systems have expanded from transdermal drug delivery and vaccination into advanced biomedical scenarios such as disease monitoring [34], cosmetic care [35], point-of-care diagnostics, and biosensing [36, 37]. These developments signal an evolution from conventional transdermal systems toward multi-pathway precision therapy, demonstrating broader interdisciplinary potential and clinical translation prospects.

Despite sustained growth in microneedle research driven by interdisciplinary convergence and rapid iteration, the field's rapid expansion complicates its systematic organization and evaluation. This research spans materials, manufacturing, pharmaceuticals, immunology, and sensing, and its rapid evolution makes it difficult to identify key knowledge nodes and the pathways of hotspot development. Consequently, researchers struggle to efficiently pinpoint research priorities and evolutionary trends within the extensive literature, which impedes the field's overall progress. To delineate the field's core trajectory within this expanding body of work, this analysis uses highly cited articles as entry points for identifying pivotal knowledge nodes. Such articles typically exert strong academic influence through theoretical or methodological innovation, enabling the mapping of the field's knowledge structure and evolutionary pathways [38]. Relying solely on citations, however, fails to capture a study's dissemination in external dimensions like media coverage and public attention. We therefore introduce alternative metrics to complement the portrayal of high-impact outcomes' social dissemination, forming a more comprehensive evaluation framework.

As a classic tool for assessing research outputs, bibliometrics has been widely applied to analyze disciplinary evolution and collaboration patterns [39]. Alternative metrics track non-traditional sources such as social media, policy documents, and news reports, supplementing the gaps in social attention left by traditional citation analysis [40]. These complementary approaches enable an in-depth analysis of the multifaceted value and diffusion pathways of highly cited microneedle research across both scientific and societal dimensions. To our knowledge, existing studies using bibliometric and patent analysis have separately revealed microneedle technology pathways and intellectual property landscapes in drug delivery [41], biomedical applications [42], and transdermal delivery [43]. Structured quantitative research focused specifically on highly cited literature remains scarce, however, and few studies incorporate alternative metrics to evaluate performance in external dimensions like media dissemination, policy impact, and public attention.

This article therefore integrates the top 100 highly cited microneedle-related publications from 2015 to 2025 in the Web of Science Citation Database (WOSCC). Using a combined bibliometric and alternative metrics approach, it systematically analyzes their scientific structure and societal impact. The key research foci include annual publication trends, the evolution of research hotspots, collaborative network characteristics, and the extra-academic dissemination of high-impact outcomes. This study aims to reveal the knowledge contributions and external impacts of highly cited research over the past decade, outline its research priorities and dissemination patterns, and provide data support for constructing knowledge maps and predicting future research directions in microneedles.

Materials and methods

Data collection

A comprehensive search of the Web of Science Core Collection (WOSCC) was conducted on July 15, 2025. The topic search string was (TS = microneedl* OR TS = micro-needl* OR TS = "microneedle patch*" OR TS = "microneedle array*" OR TS = "microneedle system*" OR TS = "microneedle device*" OR TS = "microneedle fabrication*" OR TS = "derma roller*"). Publication dates were limited to January 1, 2015 through July 15, 2025, and only original research articles were included. Retrieved records were ranked in descending order based on their raw citation counts in WOSCC. The top 100 most-cited original research articles were then selected following independent screening by two researchers. Citation density was calculated as a supplementary descriptive metric and was not used as the ranking criterion for inclusion. Altmetric Attention Scores and related online attention metrics were retrieved from Altmetric Explorer on July 15, 2025. Because altmetric data are dynamic and may change over time, the retrieval date was recorded to enhance reproducibility.

Data cleaning, analysis and visualization

To ensure accurate and consistent data analysis, keywords from the original literature were standardized using VOSviewer. This process merged singular and plural forms, synonyms, and hyphenated terms, with subsequent manual verification by two researchers. Multidimensional data analysis was then conducted on the standardized dataset. Spearman correlation analyses of citation metrics—including total citations, average citations per year, and AAS—were performed using R 4.5.1 [44]. Network maps and co-occurrence analyses were generated with VOSviewer 1.6.20 [45] and CiteSpace 6.4.1 [46], encompassing high-frequency keyword co-occurrence maps, keyword clustering maps, and collaboration maps for authors, institutions, and countries. Supplementary figures were created using Scimago Graphica 1.043 and Excel. For transparency and reproducibility, the keyword normalization rules, synonym glossary, and co-occurrence map parameters are detailed in online Appendix 1. Annual publication trends and journal distribution

Analysis of annual publication and citation trends (Fig. 2) shows a marked increase in both the number of publications and citations for the top 100 highly cited articles in microneedle research from 2015 to 2020, with a peak around 2020 (18 publications and over 4000 citations). Annual publications rose steadily from 4 in 2015 to 19 in 2020, while annual citations grew from 1190 to 4172, indicating a parallel increase in research activity and academic impact. The period from 2017 to 2020 was particularly notable, with consistently high annual publication numbers and citations exceeding 3600 each year, representing a phase of concentrated, high-impact output. Since 2021, however, annual citations have declined noticeably despite the continued publication of high-quality work. This decline may be attributed to several factors, including citation lag, shifts in research focus, and database indexing cycles. Institutionally, efficient transregional networks have formed around key nodes including the Georgia Institute of Technology, the Chinese Academy of Sciences, MIT, and Nanjing University. Future efforts should strengthen cross-disciplinary integration of national strengths, activate synergy among key institutions, and improve the practical implementation and real-world impact of microneedle technology across diverse applications.

Analysis of highly co-cited references

To trace the theoretical origins and citation consensus within high-impact microneedle research, we performed a co-citation network analysis of the top 100 cited articles using VOSviewer, which identified their core knowledge foundations. The analysis yielded 3665 co-cited references, 15 of which exceeded 10 co-citations, indicating their high consensus and foundational value in the field. The most frequently co-cited work is by Jicheng Yu et al. [62], who developed a hyaluronic acid vesicle-based "smart insulin patch." This microneedle patch rapidly depolymerizes under hyperglycemic conditions by exploiting glucose oxidase-induced local hypoxia to release encapsulated insulin, enabling rapid hypoglycemic intervention in type 1 diabetic mice. Compared to traditional pH-responsive systems, this approach demonstrates superior response speed and biocompatibility, combining non-invasiveness with high efficacy. This work has been widely cited as a key example of closed-loop, personalized diabetes treatment and has guided later research on responsive drug delivery.

The work of Hao Chang et al. [63] developed a UV-crosslinked methacrylated hyaluronic acid (MeHA) hydrogel microneedle patch for the rapid, minimally invasive collection of interstitial skin fluid (ISF). This patch efficiently extracts metabolites such as glucose and cholesterol, with detection trends consistent with blood levels. It exhibits excellent mechanical properties, rapid swelling capacity, and structural integrity, leaving no residue after use and allowing for rapid skin recovery. This study provides materials and strategies for microneedle-based minimally invasive diagnostics and may support future translational research on metabolite monitoring. Future applications may include continuous health indicator monitoring in home-based, personalized healthcare, with potential expansion to multi-biomarker detection and integrated real-time sensing systems.

An earlier review by Prausnitz et al. [64] systematically outlined key technical approaches, material selection, and early empirical findings for microneedles in transdermal drug delivery. That work compared the performance of various microneedle types, detailed their application in macromolecular transdermal delivery, vaccine delivery, and protein transfer, and summarized the transition of microneedle research from engineering manufacturing to early biomedical translation. It has long served as a frequently cited reference for theoretical introductions and technical comparisons in the field. In summary, these highly co-cited publications mark the technological breakthroughs and theoretical foundations of early microneedle research while establishing methodological pathways and practical benchmarks for current directions in smart delivery and disease management.

Correlation analysis and characteristics of high-AAS articles

To comprehensively assess the impact of highly cited microneedle research, we analyzed correlations between citation metrics and alternative metrics for the top 100 cited articles. We then examined high-AAS articles to characterize how microneedle research is disseminated to the public. The correlation analysis (online Appendix 3) revealed high consistency among citation sources. Scopus and Google Scholar exhibited the strongest correlation, while WOSCC and Dimensions also showed strong associations with these metrics, confirming the relative consistency of citation-based impact assessment across databases.

Alternative metrics followed a different pattern. AAS correlated strongly with News and X mentions, indicating that media coverage and social media dissemination primarily drove AAS. In contrast, Patent mentions correlated weakly with media-related metrics and AAS, suggesting that patent attention may reflect technological application or intellectual property diffusion rather than public visibility. Overall, AAS showed weak correlations with WOSCC, Scopus, and Google Scholar citations, and a moderate correlation with Dimensions. These results suggest that citation counts and AAS capture different dimensions of research influence: citations mainly reflect cumulative academic recognition, whereas AAS is more sensitive to public visibility and online discussion.

Mendeley readers showed a bridging effect, with correlations across citation metrics, AAS, and Patent mentions, suggesting that readership may reflect early scholarly engagement while retaining some connection to broader dissemination. Analysis of the ten most disseminated AAS articles (online Appendix 2) further revealed a strong topical focus on public health and accessible biomedical technologies, including COVID-19 vaccines, infectious disease control, wearable health monitoring, and smart microneedle diagnostics. These topics likely attract greater public and media attention because their potential benefits are more easily communicated to non-specialist audiences and are closely tied to public health needs.

Discussion

Building on the results presented above, the Discussion is structured around the main patterns identified from the bibliometric and altmetric analyses. Annual publication and citation trends show that highly cited microneedle studies clustered during a period of intensive interdisciplinary growth, particularly between 2017 and 2020 (Fig. 2). The disciplinary distribution further indicates that these studies are closely linked to nanoscience, materials science, chemistry, biomedical engineering, and pharmacology (Fig. 3). Journal and collaboration networks point to concentrated knowledge production in leading interdisciplinary journals and several national, institutional, and author hubs (Figs. 4, 5, 6, and 7). More importantly, keyword co-occurrence and clustering analyses highlight major thematic nodes, such as transdermal drug delivery, hydrogel materials, insulin delivery, biosensing, interstitial fluid sampling, immunotherapy, and clinical studies (Figs. 8 and 9). Together with the co-citation analysis, these findings provide a basis for organizing the subsequent discussion around material and structural innovation, smart diagnostic and therapeutic platforms, clinical applications, translational barriers, and the divergence between scholarly impact and social attention.

Materials and structural design

The importance of materials and structural design is supported by the keyword and co-citation results. In the keyword co-occurrence and clustering analyses, terms such as “hydrogel,” “hyaluronic acid,” “chitosan,” “controlled release,” and “transdermal drug delivery” appeared as important nodes or were embedded in major thematic clusters (Figs. 8 and 9). These results suggest that material composition and structural architecture remain foundational issues in highly cited microneedle research. The co-citation analysis further highlights representative studies on smart insulin patches, interstitial fluid extraction, and transdermal delivery mechanisms, indicating that material and structural innovation has provided a core knowledge base for subsequent development in this field.

The performance of microneedle systems is fundamentally governed by their material composition and structural architecture, making innovation in these areas a central focus of research. Several influential studies exemplify this trend by introducing pivotal advances in microneedle design, functionality, and fabrication. For instance, one team developed a solvent-free crosslinking method using hyaluronic acid and Gantrez S97 to produce functional hydrogel microneedles that are biocompatible, capable of drug loading, and antimicrobial, thereby aligning material safety with sustained drug release [61]. Yamamoto et al. further extended material functionality by creating conductive, microporous polymer microneedles; these enhance macromolecule delivery via electroosmosis and integrate biobatteries to form self-powered systems, presenting new possibilities for wearable platforms [65]. Structurally, Han et al. drew inspiration from natural barbs to design a microneedle array with backward-curved hooks using 4D printing and a crosslinking density gradient, achieving an 18-fold improvement in tissue adhesion for more stable drug delivery and biosensing [66]. Krieger et al. addressed geometric precision and fabrication accessibility through a combined print-fill strategy, utilizing desktop SLA 3D printing to produce high-aspect-ratio microneedle molds that enhance sharpness while lowering production barriers [67]. Crucially, such material and structural innovations are enabled by parallel advances in manufacturing, such as the 4D printing employed by Han et al., the print-fill method of Krieger et al., and the mold-free, rapid fabrication made possible by Continuous Liquid Interface Production (CLIP) technology [68]. These highly cited works collectively reflect a deep and active knowledge base that underpins the progression of microneedle technology.

The highly cited literature points to several recurring themes in materials, structures, and manufacturing. One, many studies involve functional composites that are conductive, provide controlled release, or respond to stimuli, with smart hydrogels receiving particular attention for multimodal delivery and environmental adaptation [69–71]. Two, structural design is increasingly moving away from rigid, uniform arrays toward more flexible, multiscale, and biomimetic configurations, often supported by modeling and multi-parameter optimization to enhance mechanical performance and tissue compatibility [72–74].Three, advanced manufacturing methods such as 3D/4D printing, CLIP, and photothermal processing are frequently discussed as ways to improve precision, customization, and application-specific fabrication [75–78]. Together, these studies indicate a research trend toward more integrated microneedle systems that combine sensing, feedback, and therapeutic delivery, though their broader translational value still requires further validation [79–81].

Smart diagnostic and therapeutic platforms

The discussion of smart diagnostic and therapeutic platforms is also directly supported by the keyword results. Keywords such as “sensor,” “biosensor,” “interstitial fluid,” “wearable sensor,” and “glucose-responsive” appeared in the co-occurrence network and thematic clusters, indicating that real-time monitoring, feedback regulation, and integrated diagnosis-treatment systems have become important directions in highly cited microneedle research (Figs. 8 and 9). This pattern is also reflected in the top-cited articles, where representative studies on sweat-based glucose monitoring, wearable biosensing, and closed-loop insulin delivery received both high citation counts and substantial attention.A representative example from the TOP100 highly cited articles is the integrated patterned microneedle system by Jae Hyun Lee et al. [47], which ranks first in citations (1419 in WoS, 654 in AAS). This system employs a gold-doped graphene and flexible metal mesh to create a highly electroactive interface, combining multi-parameter electrochemical sensors for glucose, pH, and temperature with a temperature control unit. It allows real-time monitoring of sweat biomarkers and thermally triggered drug release from microneedles. In diabetic models, the platform demonstrated closed-loop sensing-decision-intervention therapy, showcasing advances in multimodal integration, feedback regulation, and biocompatibility for smart microneedle systems.With its high functional integration and platform-based design, this work exemplifies the broader shift from single-function microneedle devices toward more integrated.

Analysis of the past decade’s top 100 highly cited articles on intelligent therapeutic platforms shows microneedles progressing from devices to systems via multiple closed-loop technical routes, with intelligent functionality being especially notable. Beyond thermal drug release, studies have combined mesoporous microneedles with reverse electroosmosis to create closed-loop systems that sense, analyze, and electrochemically deliver drugs, enabling real-time response to blood glucose and precise insulin control [82]. Closed-loop sensing has since expanded to multi-parameter monitoring. Certain platforms can concurrently analyze glucose, lactate, and ketone bodies, offering richer data for identifying complex conditions like diabetic metabolic disorders [83]. The digital sophistication of these systems has also increased significantly. Existing platforms incorporate wearable electronics, wireless modules, and mobile apps to acquire, analyze, and remotely control physiological signals, reflecting the embedded role of smart microneedle platforms within IoT-based health management [84]. Moreover, work integrating synthetic signal amplifiers and pancreatic cell units has achieved in vivo biological transduction of glucose signals and intelligent responsive release, exploring deeper convergence between bionics and closed-loop microneedle diagnostics and therapy [85]. Together, these highly cited studies indicate a shift in research focus from localized functional integration toward more systemic smart microneedle platforms, a direction that may support future work on personalized and continuous disease intervention.

Clinical applications in therapy

The clinical orientation of highly cited microneedle research is evident in both the article content and the thematic clustering results. Keyword clustering analysis identified clinically relevant clusters, such as clinical study, microneedle-based biosensor, photothermal therapy, interstitial fluid sampling, and obesity treatment, along with other disease- or application-focused themes (Fig. 9). These results suggest that highly cited microneedle research is no longer confined to device fabrication or material design, but increasingly extends into disease management, vaccination, biosensing, and localized therapy.Over the past decade, more than 60% of the top 100 highly cited microneedle publications have focused on disease treatment, underscoring a major shift toward clinical translation. Research has concentrated primarily on four areas: diabetes management, with an emphasis on smart insulin delivery and closed-loop systems; tumor therapy and immune intervention, employing strategies such as immune checkpoint inhibition, photothermal therapy, and cell therapy; skin diseases and wound repair, which exploit microneedles for localized drug delivery and tissue regeneration; and vaccination against infectious diseases like COVID-19 and influenza, capitalizing on advantages including painless application, self-administration, and cold-chain independence. Applications in contraception, ophthalmology, cardiology, neurological disorders, and analgesia further demonstrate the expanding clinical scope. Consequently, clinical therapeutics emerge as a central theme in highly cited microneedle studies, indicating strong translational interest and a possible progression toward intelligent, personalized treatments.

Analysis of this literature suggests that microneedle systems are increasingly explored as cross-disease platform technologies. This trend is reflected not only in the versatility of carrier materials and response mechanisms, but also in design strategies that appear to be tailored to specific clinical needs. For example, the feedback logic of glucose-responsive closed-loop insulin systems has also been investigated in tumor therapy, where drug release can be designed to respond to hypoxic or acidic microenvironments [86]. Such transfer of design logic from metabolic disease to oncology points to the potential adaptability of microneedle platforms rather than confirming their generalizability across diseases. Similarly, soluble polymer microneedle arrays used in vaccines, psoriasis, and contraception can be adjusted by modifying needle length, drug-loading sites, and release kinetics [71]. Although this design flexibility may support cross-scenario adaptation, its relevance to scale-up and industrial translation still depends on manufacturability, quality control, and indication-specific clinical evidence. More broadly, the highly cited literature indicates that microneedle design is increasingly aligned with disease-specific requirements. Even under a conservative platform framework, system configurations require substantial re-optimization for different clinical scenarios: psoriasis treatment may emphasize skin retention and penetration, whereas ophthalmic applications may prioritize minimal invasion and sustained release [87, 88]. At the mechanism level, enzyme-mediated feedback in diabetes and hypoxia-activated strategies in tumor immunity both illustrate how pathological signals can be incorporated into microneedle design [89–92]. These examples point to a movement from passive delivery toward more responsive therapeutic systems, but their future role in precision and personalized medicine remains contingent on further experimental, clinical, and translational validation.

Translational challenges and future perspectives for microneedle technologies

Despite continuous advancements in microneedle materials, microstructures, and smart integration, the clinical and commercial translation of microneedle technologies remains constrained by several practical barriers.This issue is important for interpreting highly cited microneedle studies, because citation impact mainly reflects scholarly influence and does not, by itself, establish product readiness, regulatory feasibility, or patient benefit. For microneedle technologies, translational potential depends not only on scientific novelty, but also on whether a system can be manufactured reproducibly, evaluated under appropriate regulatory standards, applied consistently by patients or healthcare providers, and shown to offer clinically meaningful advantages over existing delivery methods. Accordingly, the challenges facing microneedle technologies primarily concern four areas: first, balancing material properties and structure to reliably achieve sufficient skin penetration, biocompatibility, drug loading, and controlled release; second, ensuring long-term stability of integrated systems in real skin environments; third, establishing scalable manufacturing and robust quality-control methods; and fourth, clarifying clinical evidence and regulatory pathways for relevant drug-device or vaccine-device products [93, 94].

Recent research has proposed translation-oriented strategies that address each of these four aspects.For material-structure balance, studies increasingly employ structural division and functional zoning, assigning distinct roles to different layers to achieve controllable overall performance through parameter coupling. Common strategies include core–shell structures, layered microneedles, gradient architectures, or composite systems, which specialize components for load-bearing, drug delivery, and trigger-release functions to enhance design flexibility and reproducibility. For instance, Wang et al. used a compartmentalized structure with a solid insulin powder core and a glucose-responsive shell, functionally separating high-density drug loading from stimulus-responsive release to enable on-demand delivery while maintaining structural stability, which demonstrated more sustained glucose control in animal models [95]. Another study designed multilayer glucose-responsive microneedles to achieve higher drug loading, more complete release, and improved system stability, further indicating that structural division of labor is a key strategy for balancing high drug loading with controlled release [96]. However, such approaches still require more systematic validation regarding puncture consistency, local skin reactions, and long-term safety. Concerning long-term stability in system integration, recent work increasingly emphasizes stable operation in real skin environments, progressively prioritizing manufacturability—scalable production with consistent performance—as a core design objective. For example, the wearable microneedle system designed by Liu et al. highlights rapid manufacturing and stable operation, demonstrating manufacturability and long-term functionality as primary goals [97]. Concurrently, other studies have integrated microneedle patches into closed-loop physiological modeling and control frameworks, validating closed-loop strategy efficacy through quantifiable metrics, thereby advancing system design beyond conceptual demonstrations [98]. Furthermore, subsequent research has incorporated edge AI algorithms and online adaptive update strategies for wearable closed-loop systems, reflecting a shift in focus from device integration alone to the robustness of closed-loop control and adaptation to individual variations [99, 100]. Regarding mass manufacturing and quality control, recent studies have systematically evaluated critical quality attributes and testing methodologies to support consistency control and product release assessment. Regulatory science white papers have systematically cataloged and prioritized microneedle CQAs, noting that critical metrics like delivery dose, puncture performance, and dissolution often require specialized or modified testing methods, establishing a foundation for quality control systems [101]. A 2025 review on additive manufacturing of microneedles integrated Quality by Design, risk management tools, and AI-assisted process prediction into a scalable framework, emphasizing the shift from laboratory-level feasibility to an industrial logic of process control and quality traceability [102]. For clinical evidence and regulatory pathways, more systematic clinical trial data with higher evidence grades have emerged, while regulatory science collaboration and consensus have become clearer. A Phase I/II trial of a measles-rubella vaccine microarray patch in West Africa systematically provided key clinical evidence on tolerability, safety, and immunogenicity, adding important clinical evidence for MAP vaccines [103]. That same year, a Phase I human study of the slow-dissolving microneedle array patch (MIMIX MAP) delivering influenza vaccine comprehensively reported safety, reactogenicity, and immunogenicity results, providing further evidence relevant to the clinical feasibility of MAP vaccines [104]. Regulatory science for microneedle formulations is also rapidly establishing common ground. For example, the Microneedle Array Patch Regulatory Working Group (MAP-RWG) continues to advance alignment on critical topics such as CMC and testing methods. Concurrently, white papers championed by organizations like PATH provide a more unified framework for defining critical quality attributes, addressing methodological gaps, and clarifying regulatory terminology [101, 105].

Higher-level clinical evidence for microneedles in vaccine delivery is beginning to emerge, offering a critical reference for their clinical feasibility. Consequently, their indications are expected to expand into areas with clearer validation pathways, such as dermatological therapy, metabolic management via drug delivery and monitoring, and local administration at mucosal or ocular sites [106–108]. Although current research on microneedles for systemic conditions like cardiac repair, liver injury, and bone disorders remains largely confined to animal and mechanistic studies, their potential for localized precision and reduced systemic exposure merits continued investigation [109, 110]. As application scenarios diversify, material and structural designs will likely prioritize adaptive optimization for specific requirements. This includes enhancing patch adhesion stability and minimizing slippage through bioinspired interfaces [111, 112], or employing compartmentalized and composite material strategies to integrate distinct functions—such as load-bearing, drug loading, and controlled release—within a single device [113–115]. Concurrently, simulations of mechanical-skin interactions are progressing beyond simple penetration validation toward quantitative assessment under multi-parameter conditions like insertion angle, skin curvature, and population variability. These studies aim to preemptively identify variations in insertion force and potential failure modes, thereby guiding structural optimization [116, 117]. Future microneedle simulation work may also incorporate more data-driven approaches, such as machine learning, to rapidly screen and rank candidates by bridging simulation and experiment. While this could narrow the feasible parameter space and reduce experimental iterations, its success depends on high-quality data and comparable evaluation frameworks [118, 119]. Furthermore, as microneedles evolve from simple delivery patches toward complex health management systems, the need for long-term operational stability becomes paramount. Although interdisciplinary integration with soft electronics, wireless communication, and algorithms continues to advance, the research emphasis may shift from adding functional modules to ensuring quantifiable, stable performance under real‑world conditions. This requires systematically addressing challenges like signal drift, individual variability, dose‑control safety margins, and usability evidence [120, 121].

In summary, microneedle technologies retain considerable potential for expanded application, particularly when indications and risk–benefit boundaries are clearly defined. At the same time, the interpretation of highly cited research should remain measured. High citation counts can identify influential scientific concepts and active research trajectories, but they do not alone demonstrate manufacturability, regulatory acceptability, commercial readiness, or patient-level benefit. Recent vaccine-related clinical studies provide encouraging evidence for selected indications, yet broader implementation will still require scalable production, standardized quality testing, reliable application procedures, clear regulatory pathways, and comparative clinical evidence [101, 103–105]. Future microneedle research should therefore move beyond proof-of-concept innovation toward translation-oriented design, with greater emphasis on manufacturable structures, validated quality-control methods, patient usability, and clinically meaningful outcomes. Such efforts may support more verifiable applications of microneedles across selected disease management scenarios.

Scholarly impact and social attention in top-cited microneedle research

The interpretation of scholarly impact and social attention relies on the correlation matrix and the analysis of high-AAS articles presented in Sect. 3.6 and online Appendix 3. The observed correlation patterns suggest that highly cited microneedle research generates impact through two partially overlapping yet distinct pathways. The first pathway, scholarly impact, is evidenced by the strong concordance among citation counts from WOSCC, Scopus, Google Scholar, and Dimensions. This pathway reflects cumulative academic recognition and knowledge diffusion within specialist communities. The second pathway, social attention, is primarily captured by AAS, News mentions, and X mentions. This pathway is more closely tied to public visibility, media narratives, and the accessibility of research topics to audiences beyond the specialist domain.

This divergence is important for interpreting high-impact microneedle research. Highly cited studies on materials, fabrication methods, delivery mechanisms, or experimental models may provide foundational academic contributions but receive limited public attention. Conversely, studies related to vaccine patches, infectious disease control, wearable monitoring, closed-loop health management, or self-administered technologies are more likely to attract media and social attention, as their potential patient-facing benefits are easier to grasp. Thus, public visibility in microneedle research appears partly topic-driven and should not be regarded as equivalent to academic impact.

Mendeley readership may serve as an intermediate signal between scholarly impact and broader dissemination, whereas Patent mentions appear to reflect a different dimension related to technological application. The weak associations among citations, media attention, and patent indicators also suggest that high citation counts or high public visibility do not necessarily indicate industrial readiness or commercial potential. For a translation-oriented field such as microneedle research, integrating bibliometric and alternative metrics can therefore provide a more balanced view of academic contribution, public dissemination, and possible translational relevance.

Limitations

While bibliometrics and altmetrics are now widely used in research evaluation, each approach carries notable limitations. Citation frequency, a common metric, suffers from a pronounced lag effect that often excludes recent high-quality studies from timely assessment. This limitation is especially relevant to the “top 100” design adopted here, because ranking articles by raw citation counts tends to favor older publications with more time to accrue citations. Consequently, recent studies with substantial methodological innovation, translational potential, or rapidly emerging scholarly influence may be overlooked. Moreover, selection of the top-cited articles relied on WOSCC records and is thus contingent on the database’s coverage, indexing practices, and citation records. Although citation data from Scopus and Google Scholar were collected for comparison, they were not used as the primary ranking criterion for inclusion. Citations are also shaped by research trends, author reputation, and journal prestige, which may not reflect an article’s genuine scholarly contribution.

Altmetrics offer a complementary perspective by capturing social attention through data from social media and public platforms. However, their dependence on fragmented data sources and platform-specific algorithms makes them susceptible to episodic, event-driven spikes in attention, which can exaggerate the perceived impact of individual publications. This sensitivity is particularly pronounced in highly specialized fields such as microneedle research, where baseline public interest is typically low; as a result, a single news cycle or social media surge can disproportionately elevate altmetric scores. Conversely, technically important studies may receive low altmetric scores when they do not align with media narratives or publicly engaging topics, even if they hold considerable applied or translational value. In essence, both traditional and alternative metrics focus on overt indicators of impact, providing limited insight into a study’s theoretical depth or translational potential. Therefore, the findings of this study should be interpreted as a structured mapping of highly cited microneedle literature rather than a comprehensive evaluation of all influential, emerging, or clinically promising work in the field. A more thorough assessment of microneedle research will thus require integrating additional multidimensional perspectives, including qualitative analysis and expert judgment. Such an integrated framework can support a more scientific, dynamic, and context-sensitive evaluation system.

Conclusions

This study systematically analyzes the top 100 highly cited original microneedle research articles from the past decade using bibliometric and alternative metrics. At the national and institutional level, the University of North Carolina (USA), Nanjing University (China), and Queen's University Belfast (UK) are prominent contributors to this highly cited literature. Core author Zhen Gu leads with 18 articles and 5135 total citations. These highly cited articles are concentrated in a select group of top-tier field-specific journals. For the collected literature, alternative metrics demonstrate a moderate correlation with traditional citation counts. Both public discourse and the academic community show sustained interest in cutting-edge technologies, such as integrated microneedle-based diagnostic and therapeutic platforms. Societal attention, however, is more focused on highly accessible and application-oriented areas, including vaccine patches, wearable monitors, and self-administered solutions for women's health. In contrast, microneedle research involving deep technical innovation, while academically significant, has not attracted a comparable level of public engagement. This disparity reveals a misalignment between societal focus and academic value. Future efforts should therefore guide and balance the dissemination of research to ensure that the most promising microneedle studies gain appropriate societal recognition and practical support.
