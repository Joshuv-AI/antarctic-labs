Abstract
. We establish a priori regularity estimates for viscosity solutions of degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equations with integrable right-hand sides. When the nonhomogeneous term belongs to L p L^{p} with p > n p>n , we prove optimal interior C 1 , α C^{1,\alpha} estimates. In the critical case, we obtain a log-Lipschitz modulus of continuity under the Lorentz condition f ∈ L n , 1 f\in L^{n,1} . We utilize sliding paraboloid or cusp methods to develop uniform Hölder estimates for equations that are elliptic only in suitable gradient regimes. Finally, we establish an approximation lemma for integrable right-hand sides via a corrector argument, which allows us to deduce the corresponding Schauder-type estimates.

1.
Introduction

This paper is concerned with the regularity of viscosity solutions
u
of the following degenerate fully nonlinear elliptic equation

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u)=f\quad\text{in $B_{1}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}$,}

where
\gamma>0
and
F
is a uniformly elliptic fully nonlinear operator. In the classical uniformly elliptic case
\gamma=0
and
f=0
, the celebrated Krylov–Safonov theory
[
26
,
27
]
yields that
u\in C^{1,\alpha_{0}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1})
for some
\alpha_{0}=\alpha_{0}(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\in(0,1)
which will be fixed throughout the paper; see Theorem
2.4
for details.
Moreover, by the classical Evans–Krylov theory
[
21
,
28
,
29
]
, if
F
is convex, then
u\in C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1})
(in fact,
C^{2,\alpha}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1})
); thus, we can take
\alpha_{0}=1
in the convex setting.
In the degenerate case
\gamma>0
, since the coefficient
|Du|^{\gamma}
may vanish at critical points of
u
, the equation is no longer uniformly elliptic. Nevertheless, it is by now well understood that viscosity solutions
u
still enjoy interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimates in terms of the
L^{\infty}
-norm of
f
. Imbert–Silvestre
[
24
]
established the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity result for some universal exponent
\alpha\in(0,1)
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(\overline{B_{1/2}})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}).

Later, Araújo–Ricarte–Teixeira
[
3
]
captured the sharp range of gradient Hölder exponents, namely,

\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\cap\left(0,\frac{1}{1+\gamma}\right].

We also refer to
[
1
,
6
,
12
,
10
,
19
,
22
,
34
,
15
]
for interior regularity results for equations with different degenerate structures,
[
4
,
7
,
9
,
11
,
8
]
for boundary regularity results under Dirichlet, Neumann, or oblique conditions,
[
5
,
30
,
31
]
for the parabolic counterparts, and
[
2
,
23
,
17
,
16
]
for the associated obstacle-type problems.

The purpose of this paper is to develop
a priori
estimates for solutions in terms of the nonhomogeneous term
f
only through its integrability norm. Let us begin with a simple observation that suggests a natural guess for the interior regularity results in the case of
f\in L^{p}
with
p\in[n,\infty)
. Consider the model equation

|Du|^{\gamma}\Delta u=f

and let
u(x)=|x|^{1+\alpha}
. Then, up to a multiplicative constant, we have

f(x)\simeq|x|^{\alpha(1+\gamma)-1}.

Thus, it is immediate to check that
f\in L^{p}
if and only if

\alpha>\frac{1-n/p}{1+\gamma}.

This computation shows that one cannot expect
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of solutions for an exponent larger than

(1-n/p)/(1+\gamma)
under the sole assumption that
f\in L^{p}
. Indeed, this exponent recovers the optimal value
1/(1+\gamma)
obtained in
[
3
]
when
p\to\infty
, while the borderline case
p=n
corresponds to the critical regime where one cannot expect a positive Hölder exponent for the gradient in general. We also note that a solution
u
of
F(D^{2}u)=f\in L^{n}
need not be Lipschitz continuous, while
u
is
C^{\alpha}
for any
\alpha\in(0,1)
; see
[
36
, Remark 5]
for details.

On the other hand, it would also be interesting to understand the subcritical integrability range

p<n
. Although one cannot expect any gradient Hölder regularity in this regime, it is natural to ask whether solutions still satisfy a uniform Hölder
estimate under the assumption
f\in L^{p}
, possibly for a suitable range of
p<n
. We leave this question for future work and refer to the related works of Escauriaza
[
20
]
and Teixeira
[
36
]
in the uniformly elliptic case
\gamma=0
.

Before we state our main theorems, let us specify our setting more precisely. We consider viscosity solutions
u\in C(B_{1})
of

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f(x)\quad\text{in $B_{1}$,}

where
F(\cdot,x)
is uniformly elliptic with
F(0,x)\equiv 0
, and
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
for some
p>n
or
f\in L^{n,1}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
; see Definition
2.6
for the definition of Lorentz spaces
L^{p,q}(\Omega)
. Since we allow the operator
F
to depend on the spatial variable
x
, we impose a small oscillation condition in the spatial variable. Following the perturbative framework of Caffarelli
[
14
]
, we measure the oscillation of
F
near
x_{0}\in B_{1}
by

(1.1)

\beta_{F}(x,x_{0})=\beta(x,x_{0})\coloneqq\sup_{M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}\setminus\{0\}}\frac{|F(M,x)-F(M,x_{0})|}{\|M\|},

where
\mathcal{S}^{n}
denotes the set of symmetric
n\times n
matrices.

Throughout the paper, we work in an
a priori
setting: we assume that
f
and
\beta_{F}
are Hölder continuous for some exponent in
(0,1)
. Let us clarify the role of this auxiliary regularity assumption used in the proof of Lemma
4.1
. Indeed, this assumption is used only to justify the construction of the correctors and the use of classical test functions, as in the a priori framework of
[
13
, Section 8.2]
. The constants in the estimates below are independent of these auxiliary Hölder norms. Hence, the final estimates depend only on the relevant
L^{p}
- or
L^{n,1}
-quantities appearing in the hypotheses.

Our first main theorem gives the optimal interior
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate in the supercritical regime
p>n
. The admissible range of
\alpha
is consistent with the heuristic obstruction suggested by the model example discussed above.

Theorem 1.1

(
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity)
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
with
p>n
. Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{ in }B_{1}.

For

\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\cap\left(0,\frac{1-n/p}{1+\gamma}\right],

there exists a constant
\eta>0
depending only on
n
,
p
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
such that if

\sup_{y\in B_{1/2}}\left(\fint_{B_{r}(y)}\beta^{p}(x,y)\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\eta\quad\text{ for any $r\in(0,1/2)$,}

then
u\in C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{1,\alpha}(B_{1})
with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}}),

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
p
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
.

We next move our attention to the critical case
p=n
, where we cannot expect
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of viscosity solutions due to the earlier observation. Instead, we describe a borderline modulus of continuity with the aid of the Lorentz space
L^{n,1}
, which is slightly stronger than
L^{n}
but still contains
L^{p}
for every
p>n
on bounded domains.

Theorem 1.2

(Log-Lipschitz regularity)
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{n,1}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
. Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{ in }B_{1}.

Then there exists
\eta>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\gamma
such that if

\sup_{y\in B_{1/2}}\frac{1}{r}\|\beta(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{r}(y))}\leq\eta\quad\text{ for any $r\in(0,1/2)$,}

then
u
is locally log-Lipschitz, i.e., for any
x,y\in B_{1/2}
,

|u(x)-u(y)|\leq C(\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}+\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}^{\frac{1}{1+\gamma}})\cdot\omega(|x-y|),

where
\omega(t)=-t\log t
and
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\gamma
.

Let us briefly summarize the strategy of the proof. A key step is to obtain a uniform Hölder estimate for a class of equations (Theorem
3.2
) that arise in the iteration argument. To be precise, after subtracting an affine function from
u
and scaling, one is led to investigate equations of the form

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f(x),

or, in view of Pucci extremal operators (see Definition
2.3
),

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)&\leq|f|\\
\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}u)&\geq-|f|\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ in }\{|Du-q|>1\}\cap B_{1}.

Then Proposition
3.3
shows that
u
satisfies one of two alternatives, corresponding to the regimes of “small gradients” (
|Du|<A_{0}
) and “large gradients” (
|Du|>A_{0}+2
). Thus, it suffices to prove a uniform
L^{\varepsilon}
estimate, which is independent of
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by combining the measure-type estimate and the doubling property in each regime. At this stage, we use the sliding paraboloid or cusp method to exploit the information on the size of gradients. Such sliding methods can be found in Savin
[
35
]
, Mooney
[
32
,
33
]
, Imbert–Silvestre
[
25
]
and Byun–Kim–Oh
[
11
]
. We also note that all results in Section
3
remain valid under the weaker assumption
f\in L^{n}
.

For
f\in L^{\infty}
, Imbert–Silvestre
[
24
]
obtained a uniform modulus of continuity result independent of
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, by proving Lipschitz estimates (for large
|q|
’s) via the Ishii–Lions method. Nevertheless, the pointwise nature of Ishii–Lions method prevents its direct application of to merely integrable right-hand sides. Therefore, we replace this approach with a measure-theoretic one based on sliding paraboloids and cusps, which is robust under the assumption
f\in L^{n}
.

Once we develop the compactness result, we show the improvement of flatness or approximation lemma (Lemma
4.1
) to guarantee that the solution
u
is close to a solution of a frozen, homogeneous uniformly elliptic equation. It is noteworthy that, at this step, another difficulty arises from the lack of pointwise control on
f
. To be precise, when we try to verify the stability issue in the proof of approximation lemma, it is necessary to construct appropriate corrector functions
\psi
that converge to
0
in the Lipschitz sense. In order to ensure the convergence, we apply the borderline potential estimate for the Lorentz space
L^{n,1}
developed by Daskalopoulos–Kuusi–Mingione
[
18
]
, and so the current proof for the approximation lemma seems not valid under a weaker condition
f\in L^{n}
. Then the remaining iteration arguments are rather standard; the approximation lemma together with the smallness assumption on
\beta
leads to the desired
C^{1,\alpha}
estimate and log-Lipschitz estimate depending on
f
.

The paper is organized as follows. In Section
2
, we collect the basic definitions and preliminary results concerning viscosity solutions, uniformly elliptic operators, Pucci extremal operators and Lorentz spaces. In Section
3
, we establish uniform Hölder estimates for elliptic equations that hold only where
|Du-q|>1
. Section
4
is devoted to the improvement of flatness via a compactness argument and perturbation from frozen homogeneous equations. In Section
5
, we prove the
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity result in the case
p>n
. Finally, in Section
6
, we deal with the critical case by proving the log-Lipschitz estimate under the Lorentz assumption
f\in L^{n,1}
.

2.
Preliminaries

Let
G:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}\times\mathbb{R}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a continuous map. We are concerned with viscosity solutions
u
of uniformly elliptic fully nonlinear equations

(2.1)

G(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)=0,

and, in particular, we may set

G(D^{2}u,Du,u,x)=|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)-f(x).

Definition 2.1

(Viscosity solutions)
.

A continuous function
u
is called a
viscosity supersolution
of (
2.1
) if for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
u-\varphi
has a local minimum at
x_{0}
, then

G(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0}),u(x_{0}),x_{0})\leq 0.

A continuous function
u
is called a
viscosity subsolution
of (
2.1
) if for all
x_{0}\in\Omega
and
\varphi\in C^{2}(\Omega)
such that
u-\varphi
has a local maximum at
x_{0}
, there holds

G(D^{2}\varphi(x_{0}),D\varphi(x_{0}),u(x_{0}),x_{0})\geq 0.

We say that
u\in C(\Omega)
is a
viscosity solution
of (
2.1
) if
u
is a viscosity supersolution and a subsolution simultaneously.

Definition 2.2

(Uniformly elliptic operator)
.

We say that
F
is
uniformly elliptic
if there exist two positive constants
\lambda\leq\Lambda
such that for any
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
and
x\in\Omega
,

\lambda\mathrm{tr}\,N\leq F(M+N,x)-F(M,x)\leq\Lambda\mathrm{tr}\,N\quad\text{for any $N\geq 0$}.

Here,
N\geq 0
means that
N
is a nonnegative definite symmetric matrix.

Definition 2.3

(Pucci’s extremal operators)
.

For given ellipticity constants
0<\lambda\leq\Lambda
, we define
Pucci’s extremal operators
by

\mathcal{M}^{+}(M)=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{+}(M)=\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}\geq 0}e_{i}+\lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i}

and

\mathcal{M}^{-}(M)=\mathcal{M}_{\lambda,\Lambda}^{-}(M)=\lambda\sum_{e_{i}\geq 0}e_{i}+\Lambda\sum_{e_{i}<0}e_{i},

where
e_{i}=e_{i}(M)
are the eigenvalues of
M\in\mathcal{S}^{n}
.

Let us recall the interior
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity result for uniformly elliptic fully nonlinear equations, which follows from the Krylov–Safonov theory
[
26
,
27
]
.

Theorem 2.4

(
[
13
, Corollary 5.7]
)
.

Let
F:\mathcal{S}^{n}\to\mathbb{R}
be a
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic operator. If
u\in C(B_{1})
is a viscosity solution of

F(D^{2}u)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

then there exists a constant
\alpha_{0}\in(0,1)
, depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, such that
u\in C^{1,\alpha_{0}}(\overline{B_{1/2}})
and

\|u\|_{C^{1,\alpha_{0}}(\overline{B_{1/2}})}\leq C\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},

where
C>0
is a constant depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
.

Remark 2.5
.

If we additionally assume that
F
is convex (or concave), then the Evans–Krylov theory (see
[
13
, Section 6]
for instance) says that a viscosity solution
u\in C(B_{1})
of
F(D^{2}u)=0
in
B_{1}
belongs to
C^{1,1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1})
(in fact,
C_{\mathrm{loc}}^{2,\tilde{\alpha}_{0}}(B_{1})
for some
\tilde{\alpha}_{0}\in(0,1)
), so we can take
\alpha_{0}=1
when
F
is convex.

Let us end this section with the definition of Lorentz spaces, which is closely related to several borderline regularity results.

Definition 2.6

(Lorentz space)
.

Let
1\leq p<\infty
,
0<q<\infty
, and let
\Omega\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an open set. For a measurable function
g:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
, we say that
g\in L^{p,q}(\Omega)
if

\|g\|_{L^{p,q}(\Omega)}\coloneqq\left(\int_{0}^{\infty}\left(t^{p}\left|\{x\in\Omega:|g(x)|>t\}\right|\right)^{q/p}\frac{dt}{t}\right)^{1/q}.

We recall that

L^{p,p}(\Omega)=L^{p}(\Omega)

for every
1\leq p<\infty
, with equivalent norms. Moreover, if
|\Omega|<\infty
, then

L^{p}(\Omega)\subset L^{n,1}(\Omega)\subset L^{n}(\Omega)\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{n}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(\Omega)}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(\Omega)}

for every
p>n
.

3.
Uniform Hölder estimates of elliptic equations that hold only where
|Du-q|>1

In this section, we seek uniform Hölder estimates for viscosity solutions of

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

where
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in L^{n}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
. Our goal is to derive the uniform estimate, which is an
L^{n}
analogue of
[
24
, Lemma 3]
, by employing the fact that this equation is uniformly elliptic when
|Du-q|>1
.

Theorem 3.1

(Modulus of continuity independent of
q
)
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

where
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in L^{n}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
.

If
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
and
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{1})}\leq 1
, then
u
satisfies a uniform
C^{\beta}
estimate for some universal exponent
\beta\in(0,1)
, i.e.,

[u]_{C^{\beta}(\overline{B_{1/2}})}\leq C,

where
C
is independent of
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

Here we observe that if
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfies

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

then
u
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)&\leq|f|\\
\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}u)&\geq-|f|\end{aligned}\right.\quad\text{ in }\{|Du-q|>1\}\cap B_{1}

in the viscosity sense. Thus, we are going to prove the uniform estimates for a larger class of solutions, and Theorem
3.1
is a direct corollary of Theorem
3.2
below.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfy the following inequalities:

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(D^{2}u)+\Lambda|Du|\quad\text{ in }\{|Du-q|>1\}\cap B_{1}

for some
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
and
f\in L^{n}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
.
Then
u\in C^{\beta}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1})
for some
\beta>0
, depending only on
n
,
\lambda
and
\Lambda
, with the uniform estimate

\|u\|_{C^{\beta}(B_{1/2})}\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})},\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{1})}),

which is independent of
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

The proof follows an argument similar to that in
[
11
]
, adapted here for the case of
L^{n}
data. We begin by observing that the scaled function of the solution
u
satisfies one of two alternatives (see
[
11
, Proposition 3.1]
for details).

Proposition 3.3
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfy

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f\ \text{ in }\{|Du-q|>1\}\cap B_{1},

for some
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.
Then for any
A_{0}>0
, the scaled function
v(x)=u(rx)/M
with
M\geq 1
and
r\in(0,1]
satisfies either

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}v)-\Lambda|Dv|\leq\tilde{f}\ \text{ in }\{|Dv|\leq A_{0}\}\cap B_{1/r}

or

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}v)-\Lambda|Dv|\leq\tilde{f}\ \text{ in }\{|Dv|\geq A_{0}+2\}\cap B_{1/r},

where
\tilde{f}(x)=r^{2}f(rx)/M
. Moreover,
\|\tilde{f}\|_{L^{n}(B_{1/r})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{1})}
.

In view of Proposition
3.3
, we investigate the regularity in each of the two regimes: the small-gradient regime and the large-gradient regime.

Let us first prove a measure estimate for the equation, which is uniformly elliptic in the regime of small gradients, by applying the sliding paraboloid method.

Lemma 3.4
.

There exist small
\delta_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and large
K>1
,
A_{0}>1
such that if
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\geq 0&&\text{in }B_{1},\\
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|&\leq f&&\text{in }\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}\cap B_{1},\\
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{1})}&\leq\delta_{0},\\
|\{u>K\}\cap B_{1}|&\geq(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|,\end{aligned}\right.

then
u>1
in
B_{1/4}
.

Proof.

By a standard approximation argument using inf-convolutions (see
[
25
, Section 3]
for instance), we may assume that
u\in C^{2}(B_{1})\cap C(\overline{B_{1}})
.
Suppose, toward a contradiction, that the conditions of the Lemma hold but
u(x_{0})\leq 1
for some
x_{0}\in B_{1/4}
.

Consider the vertex set
V=B_{1/4}
and we slide a paraboloid
\phi(z-y)=-10|z-y|^{2}
with vertex
y\in V
from below, until it touches the graph of
u
for the first time at a point
x\in\overline{B_{1}}
.
Let
T\subset\overline{B_{1}}
be the set of these contact points.
For
y\in V
, we observe that

u(x_{0})+10|x_{0}-y|^{2}<1+10\left(\frac{1}{2}\right)^{2}=\frac{7}{2},\quad\text{since $x_{0}\in B_{1/4}$}.

If
x\in\partial B_{1}
, then since
u\geq 0
and
y\in B_{1/4}
, we find that

u(x)+10|x-y|^{2}>10\left(1-\frac{1}{4}\right)^{2}>\frac{7}{2}.

Therefore, we conclude that the minimum of
u-\phi(\cdot-y)
cannot be attained on the boundary
\partial B_{1}
and
T\subset\{u<K\}\cap B_{1}
for
K=7/2
.
Note that for any
x\in T
, we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle Du(x)

\displaystyle=D\phi(x-y)=20(y-x),

(3.2)

\displaystyle D^{2}u(x)

\displaystyle\geq D^{2}\phi(x-y)=-20I.

Choosing
A_{0}=40\geq\sup_{B_{2}}|D\phi|
, we ensure
T\subset\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}
, and so the inequality

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f

is satisfied in
T
. By differentiating (
3.1
) with respect to
x
, we obtain

D_{x}y=I+\frac{1}{20}D^{2}u(x)\geq 0,

where we used (
3.2
). Using the properties of
\mathcal{M}^{-}
, we have for
x\in T
,

\displaystyle\lambda\mathrm{tr}\,D_{x}y

\displaystyle=\mathcal{M}^{-}(D_{x}y)\leq\mathcal{M}^{+}(I)+\frac{1}{20}\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)

\displaystyle\leq n\Lambda+\frac{1}{20}(f(x)+\Lambda|Du|)

\displaystyle\leq C(1+|f(x)|)

for some
C=C(n,\Lambda)>0
. Moreover, by the inequality
\det M\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,M}{n}\right)^{n}
for any nonnegative matrix
M\geq 0
, we obtain

\det D_{x}y\leq\left(\frac{\mathrm{tr}\,{D_{x}y}}{n}\right)^{n}\leq C(1+|f(x)|)^{n}.

Applying the area formula and recalling that
|T|\leq|\{u<K\}\cap B_{1}|\leq\varepsilon_{0}|B_{1}|
,

|B_{1/4}|=|V|=\int_{T}|\det D_{x}y|\,dx\leq C(|T|+\|f\|_{L^{n}(T)}^{n})\leq C(\varepsilon_{0}|B_{1}|+\delta_{0}^{n}),

which leads to a contradiction if we choose
\delta_{0}
and
\varepsilon_{0}
small enough.
∎

We next prove the doubling property for lower bounds of supersolutions by constructing an appropriate barrier function.

Lemma 3.5
.

There exist small
\delta_{0}>0
and large
K>1
,
A_{0}>1
such that if
u\in C(B_{3})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\geq 0&&\text{in }B_{3},\\
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|&\leq f&&\text{in }\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}\cap B_{3},\\
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{3})}&\leq\delta_{0},\\
u&>K&&\text{in }B_{1/4},\end{aligned}\right.

then
u>1
in
B_{1}
.

Proof.

By standard approximation again, we may assume that
u\in C^{2}(B_{3})\cap C(\overline{B_{3}})
. We use the contradiction by assuming that
u(x_{0})\leq 1
for some
x_{0}\in B_{1}
.
We define the following barrier function

\Phi(z)=\frac{M}{p}(|z|^{-p}-2^{-p})\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1/8},

for some
p>1
and
M>1
to be determined later, and extend
\Phi(>0)
smoothly inside
B_{1/8}
. For
z\in B_{4}\setminus B_{1/8}
, a direct calculation shows

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}\Phi)-\Lambda|D\Phi|

\displaystyle=M|z|^{-p-2}(\lambda(p+1)-\Lambda(n-1)-\Lambda|z|)

\displaystyle\geq 1,

provided that
p=p(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>1
and
M=M(n,\lambda,\Lambda)>1
are sufficiently large.

We choose
M
further larger to ensure that
\Phi>2
in
B_{3/2}
.
Consider the vertex set
V=B_{1/8}
and we slide
\Phi(\cdot-y)
with vertex
y\in V
from below, until it touches the graph of
u
for the first time at a point
x\in\overline{B_{3}}
. Let
T
denote the set of corresponding contact points.

For
y\in V
, we observe that

u(x_{0})-\Phi(x_{0}-y)<1-2=-1,\quad\text{since $x_{0}\in B_{1}$}.

If
x\in\partial B_{3}
, then since
u\geq 0
and
\partial B_{3}\subset\{z:\Phi(z-y)\leq 0\}
, we find that

u(x)-\Phi(x-y)\geq 0.

Therefore, we conclude that the minimum of
u-\Phi(\cdot-y)
cannot be attained on the boundary
\partial B_{3}
and
T\subset\{u<K\}\cap B_{3}
for
K=\|\Phi\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{R}^{n})}
.
Recalling that
u>K
in
B_{1/4}
, we have
T\subset\{u<K\}\cap(B_{3}\setminus B_{1/4})
.
Moreover, we find
1/8<|x-y|<4
for
x\in T
and
y\in V
.
Note that for any
x\in T
, we get

(3.4)

\displaystyle Du(x)

\displaystyle=D\Phi(x-y),

(3.5)

\displaystyle D^{2}u(x)

\displaystyle\geq D^{2}\Phi(x-y).

Choosing
A_{0}=\sup_{B_{4}\setminus B_{1/8}}|D\Phi|
, we obtain
T\subset\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}
, and so the inequality

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f

is satisfied in
T
. Therefore, for
x\in T
, we have

(3.6)

1\leq\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}\Phi)-\Lambda|D\Phi|\leq\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f(x).

By combining (
3.5
) and (
3.6
), we find

|D^{2}u(x)|\leq C(|D^{2}\Phi(x-y)|+|Du(x)|+f(x))\leq Cf(x),

where we used
|D^{2}\Phi(x-y)|\leq C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda)
.
By differentiating (
3.4
) with respect to
x
together with the fact that
D^{2}\Phi(\cdot)
is (uniformly) invertible in
B_{4}\setminus B_{1/8}
, we get

D_{x}y=I-(D^{2}\Phi)^{-1}(x-y)\cdot D^{2}u(x),

which implies that

|\det D_{x}y|\leq C|f(x)|^{n}.

Applying the area formula, we arrive at

|B_{1/8}|=|V|=\int_{T}|\det D_{x}y|\,dx\leq C\|f\|_{L^{n}(T)}^{n}\leq C\delta_{0}^{n}.

Choosing
\delta_{0}
sufficiently small yields a contradiction.
∎

Combining Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 3.6
.

There exist small
\delta_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and large
K>1
,
A_{0}>1
such that if
u\in C(B_{3})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\geq 0&&\text{in }B_{3},\\
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|&\leq f&&\text{in }\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}\cap B_{3},\\
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{3})}&\leq\delta_{0},\\
|\{u>K\}\cap B_{1}|&\geq(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|,\end{aligned}\right.

then
u>1
in
B_{1}
.

Proof.

Let
(\delta_{1},K_{1},A_{1})
and
(\delta_{2},K_{2},A_{2})
be the constants from Lemmas
3.4
and
3.5
, respectively.
We set
K=K_{1}K_{2}
,
A_{0}=\max\{A_{1}K_{2},A_{2}\}
and
\delta_{0}=\min\{\delta_{1},\delta_{2}\}
.
Then the scaled function
v(x)=u(x)/K_{2}
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.4
, implying
v>1
in
B_{1/4}
.
Thus,
u
satisfies the assumptions of Lemma
3.5
, and we conclude that
u>1
in
B_{1}
.
∎

We next consider the equation which is uniformly elliptic when gradient is large and prove the interior measure estimate lemma similar to Lemma
3.4
but using a cusp instead of a paraboloid, as in
[
25
]
.

Lemma 3.7
.

For any
A_{1}>1
, there exist small
\delta_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon_{0}>0
and large
K>1
such that if
u\in C(B_{1})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\geq 0&&\text{in }B_{1},\\
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|&\leq f&&\text{in }\{|Du|\geq A_{1}\}\cap B_{1},\\
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{1})}&\leq\delta_{0},\\
|\{u>K\}\cap B_{1}|&\geq(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|,\end{aligned}\right.

then
u>1
in
B_{1/4}
.

Proof.

Without loss of generality, we may assume that
u\in C^{2}(B_{1})\cap C(\overline{B_{1}})
via inf-convolution. Suppose, toward a contradiction, that
u(x_{0})\leq 1
for some
x_{0}\in B_{1/4}
.

Let
V=\{u>K\}\cap B_{1/4}
.
For a constant
C>1
to be determined later, we define the cusp
\phi(z)=-C|z|^{1/2}
.
For each
y\in V
, we slide the cusp
\phi(\cdot-y)
from below, until it touches the graph of
u
for the first time at
x\in\overline{B_{1}}
.
Let
T
be the set of these contact points.

By choosing
C>1
large enough, we ensure that
T\subset\{u<K\}\cap B_{1}
for some
K=K(C)>1
by using a similar argument in Lemma
3.4
.
We choose
C=C(n,A_{1})>1
further larger so that
A_{1}\leq\inf_{B_{2}}|D\phi|
.
Then we have
T\subset\{|Du|\geq A_{1}\}
, and the inequality

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f

holds in
T
. Note that for
x\in T
and
y\in V
, we have
x\neq y
since
T\cap V=\varnothing
.
Moreover, we have

(3.7)

\displaystyle Du(x)

\displaystyle=D\phi(x-y)=\frac{C}{2}|y-x|^{-1/2}\frac{y-x}{|y-x|},

(3.8)

\displaystyle D^{2}u(x)

\displaystyle\geq D^{2}\phi(x-y)=\frac{C}{4}|y-x|^{-3/2}\left(3\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}\otimes\frac{y-x}{|y-x|}-2I\right).

Therefore, using (
3.8
) and
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f
, we obtain

|D^{2}u(x)|\leq C(|f(x)|+|D^{2}\phi(x-y)|+|D\phi(x-y)|).

By differentiating (
3.7
) with respect to
x
, we get

D_{x}y=I-(D^{2}\phi)^{-1}(x-y)\cdot D^{2}u(x),

which implies

|D_{x}y|\leq C\left(1+\frac{|f(x)|+|D^{2}\phi(x-y)|+|D\phi(x-y)|}{|D^{2}\phi(x-y)|}\right)\leq C(1+|f(x)|).

Applying the area formula, we obtain

|B_{1/4}|-\varepsilon_{0}|B_{1}|\leq|V|=\int_{T}|\det D_{x}y|\,dx\leq C(|T|+\|f\|_{L^{n}(T)}^{n})\leq C(\varepsilon_{0}|B_{1}|+\delta_{0}^{n}),

which provides a contradiction for sufficiently small
\varepsilon_{0}
and
\delta_{0}
.
∎

We again use the barrier function in Lemma
3.5
to prove the doubling property.

.

For any

, there exist small

\Phi(z)=\frac{M}{p}(|z|^{-p}-2^{-p})\quad\text{ in }\mathbb{R}^{n}\setminus B_{1/8}.

∎

Combining Lemmas

and

, such that if

We summarize the results obtained so far by combining the preceding lemmas above.

.

There exist

,

,

We are now ready to prove
L^{\varepsilon}
estimate by utilizing the growing ink-spots lemma.

Theorem 3.11
.

There exist
\delta_{0}>0
,
\varepsilon>0
and
C>1
such that for any
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
, if
u\in C(B_{3})
satisfies

\left\{\begin{aligned} u&\geq 0&&\text{in }B_{3},\\
\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|&\leq f&&\text{in }\{|Du-q|>1\}\cap B_{3},\\
\|f\|_{L^{n}(B_{3})}&\leq\delta_{0},\\
\inf_{B_{1}}u&\leq 1,\end{aligned}\right.

then

|\{u>t\}\cap B_{1}|\leq Ct^{-\varepsilon}\quad\text{for any }t>0.

Proof.

We let
(\delta_{0},\varepsilon_{0},K,A_{0})
be the constants from Corollary
3.10
. Then it suffices to show that there exists a constant
c=c(n)\in(0,1)
such that

|\{u>K^{m}\}\cap B_{1}|\leq(1-c\varepsilon_{0})^{m}|B_{1}|\quad\text{for any $m\in\mathbb{N}$}.

In fact, in view of Proposition
3.3
(with
r=1
and
M=1
),
u
satisfies either

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f\ \text{ in }\{|Du|\leq A_{0}\}\cap B_{3}

or

\mathcal{M}^{-}(D^{2}u)-\Lambda|Du|\leq f\ \text{ in }\{|Du|\geq A_{0}+2\}\cap B_{3}.

Therefore, it follows from Corollary
3.10
that

|\{u>K\}\cap B_{1}|\leq(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|.

Setting
E_{m}=\{u>K^{m}\}\cap B_{1}
, it is immediate that
|E_{m+1}|\leq|E_{1}|\leq(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|
for any
m\in\mathbb{N}
. We now suppose that
B=B_{r}(x_{0})\subset B_{1}
satisfies
|B\cap E_{m+1}|>(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B|
. Then for the scaled function

v(x)=\frac{u(rx+x_{0})}{K^{m}},

we observe that

|\{v>K\}\cap B_{1}|=\frac{|B\cap E_{m+1}|}{|B|}|B_{1}|>(1-\varepsilon_{0})|B_{1}|.

In particular,
v
satisfies all the assumptions of Corollary
3.10
(with the aid of Proposition
3.3
again), and so we obtain that

v>1\quad\text{in $B_{1}$, \quad or equivalently, \quad$B\subset E_{m}$}.

We now apply the growing ink-spots lemma (see
[
25
, Lemma 2.1]
for details) to conclude that

|E_{m+1}|\leq(1-c\varepsilon_{0})|E_{m}|,

which finishes the proof.
∎

Finally, we can prove the uniform Hölder regularity, Theorem
3.2
(and so Theorem

), by repeating the standard arguments provided in
[
25
, Section 6]
, so we omit the proof.

4.
Improvement of flatness

In this section, we prove the approximation lemma by using the compactness method from the uniform Hölder estimates (Theorem
3.1
). For simplicity, we write
\beta(x)=\beta(x,0)
when
x_{0}=0
.

Lemma 4.1

(Approximation lemma)
.

Suppose
f\in L^{n,1}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
.
Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary vector and let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f(x)\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

satisfying
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
. Given any
\delta>0
, there exists
\eta>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
,
\delta
such that if

\|\beta\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\leq\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\leq\eta,

then there exists a function
h\in C(B_{1/2})
, solution to a constant coefficient, homogeneous,
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic equation

(4.1)

\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}h)=0\quad\text{in $B_{1/2}$}

such that

\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq\delta.

Proof.

We proceed by contradiction. Suppose that there exists
\delta_{0}>0
such that the statement fails.
Then there exist a sequence of continuous functions
\{u_{j}\}
, a sequence of
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic operators
F_{j}:\mathcal{S}^{n}\times B_{1}\to\mathbb{R}
, a sequence of vectors
\{q_{j}\}\subset\mathbb{R}^{n}
, and a sequence of functions
\{f_{j}\}\subset L^{n,1}(B_{1})
such that

(i)

\|u_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
;

(ii)

|Du_{j}-q_{j}|^{\gamma}F_{j}(D^{2}u_{j},x)=f_{j}
in
B_{1}
;

(iii)

\|\beta_{j}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}+\|f_{j}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\to 0\quad\text{as $j\to\infty$}
;

(iv)

however,

\sup_{B_{1/2}}|u_{j}-h|\geq\delta_{0}

for any
h
satisfying (
4.1
).

Here,
\beta_{j}
denotes the modulus of oscillation of the coefficients of the operator
F_{j}
as in (
1.1
).
Then by applying Theorem
3.1
, the sequence
\{u_{j}\}
is pre-compact in
C(B_{2/3})
-topology.
Thus,
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}
locally uniformly (up to a subsequence) in
B_{2/3}
for some
u_{\infty}\in C(B_{2/3})
.

It only remains to prove that the limit function
u_{\infty}
is a solution of a constant coefficient, homogeneous,
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic equation (
4.1
). For this purpose, we divide our analysis into two cases.

Case 1:
If
|q_{j}|
is bounded, then we can extract a subsequence of
\{q_{j}\}
that converges to some
q_{\infty}\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
. Moreover, it follows from the uniform ellipticity that
F_{j}(\cdot,0)\to\mathfrak{F}(\cdot)
for some
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic operator
\mathfrak{F}
up to a subsequence. We now claim that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution of

(4.2)

|Du_{\infty}-q_{\infty}|^{\gamma}\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}u_{\infty})=0.

Once (
4.2
) is established, the cutting lemma (
[
24
, Lemma 6]
) yields that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity solution of
\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}u_{\infty})=0
, which contradicts (iv) for sufficiently large
j
.

In order to prove (
4.2
), we show that
u_{\infty}
is a viscosity subsolution (the supersolution case is analogous).
Let
P
be a paraboloid that (strictly) touches
u_{\infty}
by above in
B_{r}(x_{0})
at
x_{0}
, where
B_{2r}(x_{0})\subset B_{1/2}
. Suppose, for the sake of contradiction, that

|DP(x_{0})-q_{\infty}|^{\gamma}\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)=-\eta<0.

By the continuity and the uniform convergence, and by choosing smaller
r
if necessary, there exist constants
a>0
and
A>0
such that

a\leq|DP(x)-q_{\infty}|\leq A\quad\text{for any $x\in B_{r}(x_{0})$}

and

a\leq|DP(x)-q_{j}|\leq A\quad\text{for any $x\in B_{r}(x_{0})$}

for all
j
large enough.
For
\varepsilon_{j}\coloneqq|\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)-F_{j}(D^{2}P,0)|
, which vanishes as
j\to\infty
, we construct a corrector
\psi_{j}
by solving the Dirichlet problem:

\left\{\begin{aligned} \mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda^{\ast},1}(D^{2}\psi_{j})&=-|f_{j}(x)|-|\beta_{j}(x)|-\varepsilon_{j}\eqqcolon g_{j}(x)&&\text{in $B_{2r}(x_{0})$},\\
\psi_{j}&=0&&\text{on $\partial B_{2r}(x_{0})$}.\end{aligned}\right.

Since
g_{j}
is continuous, the standard Perron’s method guarantees the existence of a solution
\psi_{j}
. Since
\mathcal{M}^{+}
is convex and
g_{j}
is is assumed to be Hölder continuous
a priori
, we have
\psi_{j}\in C^{2}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))
and it satisfies

\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{-}\|\geq\frac{1}{n\lambda^{\ast}}\left(\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{+}\|+|f_{j}|+|\beta_{j}|+\varepsilon_{j}\right).

Moreover, the borderline estimate (
[
18
, Theorem 1.2 and Theorem 2.1]
) gives that
\psi_{j}
satisfies the uniform estimate

\|\psi_{j}\|_{C^{1}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq C(\|\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))}+\|g_{j}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))})\leq C\|g_{j}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{2r}(x_{0}))},

where the second inequality follows from the ABP estimate (see
[
13
, Theorem 3.2]
for example). Then it follows from the condition (iii) that

\|\psi_{j}\|_{C^{1}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\to 0\quad\text{as $j\to\infty$}.

In particular, we have that for sufficiently large
j
,

\|D\psi_{j}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{r}(x_{0}))}\leq a/2,

which implies

|D(P+\psi_{j})(x)-q_{j}|\geq|DP(x)-q_{j}|-|D\psi_{j}(x)|\geq a/2\quad\text{in $B_{r}(x_{0})$}.

Therefore, we arrive at

\displaystyle F_{j}(D^{2}(P+\psi_{j}),x)

\displaystyle\leq F_{j}(D^{2}P,x)+\mathcal{M}^{+}_{\lambda,\Lambda}(D^{2}\psi_{j})

\displaystyle\leq F_{j}(D^{2}P,0)+\|D^{2}P\|\beta_{j}(x)+\Lambda\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{+}\|-\lambda\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{-}\|

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)+\varepsilon_{j}+\|D^{2}P\|\beta_{j}(x)+\Lambda\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{+}\|

\displaystyle-\frac{\lambda}{n\lambda^{\ast}}\left(\|(D^{2}\psi_{j})^{+}\|+|f_{j}(x)|+|\beta_{j}(x)|+\varepsilon_{j}\right)

\displaystyle\leq\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)-(2/a)^{\gamma}|f_{j}(x)|\,(<0)\quad\text{in $B_{r}(x_{0})$}

if we take
\lambda^{\ast}<1
small enough. We next multiply
|D(P+\psi_{j})(x)-q_{j}|^{\gamma}(\geq(a/2)^{\gamma})
on both sides to obtain that

(4.3)

\displaystyle|D(P+\psi_{j})(x)-q_{j}|^{\gamma}F_{j}(D^{2}(P+\psi_{j}),x)

\displaystyle\leq(a/2)^{\gamma}\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)-|f_{j}(x)|

\displaystyle\leq(a/2A)^{\gamma}|DP(x_{0})-q_{\infty}|^{\gamma}\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}P)-|f_{j}(x)|

\displaystyle=-(a/2A)^{\gamma}\eta-|f_{j}(x)|<f_{j}(x).

On the other hand, since
u_{j}\to u_{\infty}
and
\psi_{j}\to 0
uniformly in
B_{r}(x_{0})
, and
P
strictly touches
u_{\infty}
by above at
x_{0}
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
, there exists a constant
C_{j}>0
such that
P+\psi_{j}+C_{j}
touches
u_{j}
by above at some
x_{j}\in B_{r}(x_{0})
in
B_{r}(x_{0})
. Thus, we have

|D(P+\psi_{j})(x_{j})-q_{j}|^{\gamma}F_{j}(D^{2}(P+\psi_{j}),x_{j})\geq f_{j}(x_{j}),

which directly contradicts (
4.3
) at the point
x_{j}
.

Case 2:
If
|q_{j}|
is unbounded, then taking a subsequence, if necessary, we have
|q_{j}|\to\infty
. In this case, we define
e_{j}=q_{j}/|q_{j}|
so that
u_{j}
satisfies

\left|\frac{Du_{j}}{|q_{j}|}-e_{j}\right|^{\gamma}F_{j}(D^{2}u_{j},x)=\frac{f_{j}}{|q_{j}|^{\gamma}}.

By letting
j\to\infty
and taking further subsequence, if necessary, we use the same
argument with the previous case to find a limit function
u_{\infty}
satisfying
\mathfrak{F}(D^{2}u_{\infty})=0
for some
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic operator
\mathfrak{F}
. It again contradicts the condition (iv) for sufficiently large
j
, which finishes the proof.
∎

Remark 4.2
.

For the proof of Lemma
4.1
, the Lorentz-type conditions
\beta\in L^{n,1}
and
f\in L^{n,1}
were used for guaranteeing the
C^{1}
estimate of the corrector
\psi_{j}
, so that
D\psi_{j}\to 0
as
j\to\infty
.

On the other hand, there exists a function
f\in L^{n}(B_{1})
such that the Poisson equation
\Delta u=f
admits a
non-Lipschitz
solution
u
. In particular, one cannot expect a similar construction for the corrector
\psi_{j}
to remain valid for the critical case
\beta\in L^{n}(B_{1})
and
f\in L^{n}(B_{1})
.

5.
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity

In this section, we prove the optimal
C^{1,\alpha}
regularity of viscosity solutions when
f\in L^{p}
with
p>n
. We begin with the first step of the iteration argument.

Lemma 5.1
.

Suppose that
f\in L^{p}(B_{1})\cap C(B_{1})
with
p>n
.
Let
q\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
be an arbitrary vector and let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f(x)\quad\text{in $B_{1}$},

satisfying
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
. Given

\alpha\in(0,\alpha_{0})\cap\left(0,\frac{1-n/p}{1+\gamma}\right],

there exist constants
\rho\in(0,1/4)
and
\eta>0
depending only on
n
,
p
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
,
\gamma
and
\alpha
such that if

\left(\int_{B_{1}}\beta^{p}(x)\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\left(\int_{B_{1}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\right)^{1/p}\leq\eta,

then there exists an affine function
l(x)\coloneqq a+{b}\cdot x
satisfying

\sup_{B_{\rho}}|u(x)-l(x)|\leq\rho^{1+\alpha}

and

|a|+|{b}|\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda).

Proof.

For
\delta>0
to be determined later, let
h
be a solution of a constant coefficient, homogeneous,
(\lambda,\Lambda)
-uniformly elliptic equation such that
\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq\delta
. The existence of such a function is guaranteed by Lemma
4.1
(since
\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\lesssim\|f\|_{L^{p}(B_{1})}
), provided that
\eta
is chosen small enough depending on
\delta
and universal parameters. Since our choice for
\delta
will depend only on universal parameters, the choice of
\eta
will be universal too.

From the normalization of
u
, we have
\|h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq 2
. Consequently, by the
C^{1,\alpha_{0}}
regularity of
h
(Theorem
2.4
), we obtain that

\sup_{B_{r}}|h(x)-(h(0)+Dh(0)\cdot x)|\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\cdot r^{1+\alpha_{0}}\quad\text{for any $r\in(0,1/4)$}

and

|h(0)|+|Dh(0)|\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda).

Let us define

l(x)=h(0)+Dh(0)\cdot x.

It immediately follows that

\sup_{B_{\rho}}|u(x)-l(x)|\leq\delta+C(n,\lambda,\Lambda)\cdot\rho^{1+\alpha_{0}}.

Now, for a fixed exponent
\alpha<\alpha_{0}
, we choose
\rho
and
\delta
as

\rho\coloneqq\left(\frac{1}{2C(n,\lambda,\Lambda)}\right)^{\frac{1}{\alpha_{0}-\alpha}}\quad\text{and}\quad\delta\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\rho^{1+\alpha}.

Therefore, substituting these choices into the previous estimate yields

\sup_{B_{\rho}}|u(x)-l(x)|\leq\rho^{1+\alpha}

as desired.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

By scaling, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}}\leq 1
and
\|f\|_{L^{p}}\leq\eta
, where
\eta
is the constant from Lemma
5.1
.
We claim that there exists a sequence of affine functions
l_{k}(x)=a_{k}+b_{k}\cdot x
satisfying

(5.1)

\sup_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|u(x)-l_{k}(x)|\leq\rho^{k(1+\alpha)},

where
\rho\in(0,1/4)
is the radius from Lemma
5.1
.

We proceed by induction on
k
.
The base case
k=1
is verified by Lemma
5.1
.
Assuming that the claim holds for some
k
, we define

v(x)=\frac{(u-l_{k})(\rho^{k}x)}{\rho^{k(1+\alpha)}}.

Then
|v|\leq 1
in
B_{1}
and
v
satisfies

|Dv-q_{k}|^{\gamma}F_{k}(D^{2}v,x)=f_{k},

where

F_{k}(M,x)=\rho^{k(1-\alpha)}F({\rho^{-k(1-\alpha)}}M,\rho^{k}x),\quad q_{k}=-\rho^{-k\alpha}b_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad f_{k}(x)=\rho^{k(1-\alpha(1+\gamma))}f(\rho^{k}x).

Note that
F_{k}
inherits the ellipticity of
F
and
\beta_{F_{k}}(x)=\beta(\rho^{k}x)
.
Therefore, we obtain

\displaystyle\fint_{B_{1}}\beta_{F_{k}}^{p}(x)\,dx

\displaystyle=\fint_{B_{\rho^{k}}}\beta^{p}(x)\,dx\,\leq\eta^{p},

\displaystyle\int_{B_{1}}|f_{k}(x)|^{p}\,dx

\displaystyle=\rho^{(p(1-\alpha(1+\gamma))-n)k}\int_{B_{\rho^{k}}}|f(x)|^{p}\,dx\leq\eta^{p},

where we used the fact that
p(1-\alpha(1+\gamma))-n\geq 0
.
Applying Lemma
5.1
to
v
, there exists an affine function
\tilde{l}_{k}(x)=\tilde{a}_{k}+\tilde{b}_{k}\cdot x
such that

\|v-\tilde{l}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\leq\rho^{1+\alpha}\quad\text{ and}\quad|\tilde{a}_{k}|+|\tilde{b}_{k}|\leq C.

Setting
l_{k+1}(x)=l_{k}(x)+\rho^{k(1+\alpha)}\tilde{l}_{k}(\rho^{-k}x)
, we see that
l_{k+1}
satisfies (
5.1
) for
k+1
, completing the induction.
Furthermore, the coefficients satisfy

|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|\leq C\rho^{k(1+\alpha)}\quad\text{and}\quad|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|\leq C\rho^{k\alpha},

which implies that
a_{k}
and
b_{k}
converge and
l_{\infty}=\lim_{k\rightarrow\infty}l_{k}

becomes the affine approximation of
u
at 0.
By the standard Schauder-type argument and covering argument, we conclude that
u
is
C^{1,\alpha}
in
B_{1/2}
with the desired uniform estimate.
∎

6.
Log-Lipschitz regularity

In this section, we prove the log-Lipschitz regularity of viscosity solutions
u
, when the nonhomogeneous term
f
belongs to the Lorentz space, i.e.,
f\in L^{n,1}(B_{1})
.

Lemma 6.1
.

Let
u\in C(B_{1})
be a viscosity solution of

|Du-q|^{\gamma}F(D^{2}u,x)=f\quad\text{ in }B_{1},

satisfying
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
.
Then there exist
\rho\in(0,1/4)
and
\eta>0
depending only on
n
,
\lambda
,
\Lambda
and
\gamma
such that if

\|\beta\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\leq\eta\quad\text{and}\quad\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\leq\eta,

then there exists an affine function
l(x)=a+b\cdot x
such that

\|u-l\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\leq\rho

and

|a|+|b|\leq C(n,\lambda,\Lambda).

Proof.

For given
\delta>0
, an application of Lemma
4.1
guarantees the existence of a function
h\in C^{1,\alpha_{0}}_{\mathrm{loc}}(B_{1/2})
which is a solution of the homogeneous equation and
\|u-h\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1/2})}\leq\delta
.

Letting
l(x)=h(0)+Dh(0)\cdot x
,
C^{1,\alpha_{0}}
estimates of
h
(Theorem
2.4
) yield that

\sup_{B_{\rho}}|h(x)-l(x)|\leq C\rho^{1+\alpha_{0}}\quad\text{and}\quad|h(0)|+|Dh(0)|\leq C.

Choosing
\rho\in(0,1/4)
small enough so that
C\rho^{1+\alpha_{0}}\leq\rho/2
and
\delta=\rho/2
, we conclude the proof.
∎

Proof of Theorem
1.2
.

By scaling, we may assume that
\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{1})}\leq 1
and
\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}\leq\eta
, where
\eta
is the constant from Lemma
6.1
.
Fix
y\in B_{1/2}
.
We claim that there exists a sequence of affine functions
l_{k}(x)=a_{k}+b_{k}\cdot(x-y)
satisfying

(6.1)

\sup_{B_{\rho^{k}}(y)}|u(x)-l_{k}(x)|\leq\rho^{k},

where
\rho\in(0,1/4)
is the radius from Lemma
6.1
.
We proceed by induction on
k
.
The base case
k=1
follows directly from Lemma
6.1
.
Assuming that the claim holds for some
k\geq 1
, we consider

v(x)=\frac{(u-l_{k})(y+\rho^{k}x)}{\rho^{k}}.

Then
|v|\leq 1
in
B_{1}
and
v
satisfies

|Dv-q_{k}|^{\gamma}F_{k}(D^{2}v,x)=f_{k},

where

F_{k}(M,x)=\rho^{k}F(\rho^{-k}M,y+\rho^{k}x),\quad q_{k}=-b_{k},\quad\text{and}\quad f_{k}(x)=\rho^{k}f(y+\rho^{k}x).

Note that
F_{k}
inherits the ellipticity of
F
and
\beta_{F_{k}}(x,0)=\beta(y+\rho^{k}x,y)
.
Therefore, we have

\displaystyle\|\beta_{F_{k}}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle=\rho^{-k}\|\beta(\cdot,y)\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{\rho^{k}}(y))}\leq\eta,

\displaystyle\|f_{k}\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{1})}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{n,1}(B_{\rho^{k}}(y))}\leq\eta.

Applying Lemma
6.1
to
v
, there exists an affine function
\tilde{l}_{k}(x)=\tilde{a}_{k}+\tilde{b}_{k}\cdot x
such that

\|v-\tilde{l}_{k}\|_{L^{\infty}(B_{\rho})}\leq\rho\quad\text{ and}\quad|\tilde{a}_{k}|+|\tilde{b}_{k}|\leq C.

We define
a_{k+1}=a_{k}+\rho^{k}\tilde{a}_{k}
and
b_{k+1}=b_{k}+\tilde{b}_{k}
. Then
l_{k+1}(x)=a_{k+1}+b_{k+1}\cdot(x-y)
satisfies (
6.1
) for
k+1
, completing the induction.
Moreover, we have

(6.2)

|a_{k+1}-a_{k}|\leq C\rho^{k},\quad\text{and}\quad|b_{k+1}-b_{k}|\leq C.

By (
6.1
) and (
6.2
),
a_{k}
converges to
u(y)
with

|u(y)-a_{k}|\leq\frac{C}{1-\rho}\rho^{k}.

On the other hand, even though
b_{k}
may not converge, we have

|b_{k}|\leq\sum_{j=0}^{k}|b_{j}-b_{j-1}|\leq Ck.

For
0<r<\rho
, let
k
be the integer satisfying
\rho^{k+1}<r\leq\rho^{k}
.
Then

\displaystyle\sup_{x\in B_{r}(y)}|u(x)-u(y)|

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{x\in B_{\rho^{k}}(y)}|u(x)-l_{k}|+|u(y)-a_{k}|+|b_{k}|\rho^{k}

\displaystyle\leq C(\rho^{k}+k\rho^{k})

\displaystyle\leq\frac{C}{\rho}\left(r+\frac{\log r}{\log\rho}r\right)

\displaystyle\leq-Cr\log r,

where
C=C(n,\lambda,\Lambda,\gamma)
. This completes the proof.
∎
