GSK3B variations influence renoprotective responses to dapagliflozin in diabetic kidney disease
Authors: Jinfang Song, Yi Xu, Yongru Zhuang, Tingting Yang, Ya Chen, Changjiang Ying, Qian Lu, Tao Wang, Xiaoxing Yin
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
SummaryDapagliflozin shows variable renoprotective efficacy in patients with type 2 diabetic kidney disease (T2DKD). A retrospective clinical analysis confirmed marked interindividual variability in its urinary protein-lowering effects. To investigate the mechanism, drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) combined with quantitative proteomics was applied for target identification. Subsequent in vivo and in vitro validation suggested that GSK3β acts as a mediator of dapagliflozin-associated nephroprotection. Dapagliflozin directly bound and partially inhibited GSK3β, and GSK3β activity influenced podocyte protection. To translate findings into clinical relevance, a prospective trial was conducted. The GSK3B rs60393216 polymorphism was associated with urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) reduction after dapagliflozin therapy. These findings suggest that GSK3β contributes to the renoprotective effects of dapagliflozin and that GSK3B polymorphisms may influence therapeutic responses in T2DKD.

Introduction

Type 2 diabetic kidney disease (T2DKD) is one of the most common chronic complications of diabetes mellitus, characterized by a high incidence and insidious onset. International epidemiological surveys have reported that about 20% of patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus (T2DM) eventually develop T2DKD1,2 dapagliflozin (DAPA), a sodium-glucose cotransporter 2 (SGLT2) inhibitor, has demonstrated glycemic control and cardiorenal protective effects in patients with type 2 diabetes mellitus.3,4,5 Moreover, cardiorenal impairment caused by diabetes mellitus is the major reason for increased treatment costs and decreased quality of life in T2DM, which demonstrates that the cardiorenal protective effect is the core indicator for evaluating hypoglycemic drugs. Our clinical practice and foreign research data suggest that there may be significant individual differences in the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin, but the discrepancy cannot be effectively predicted by common clinical indicators.6,7

Among many factors such as heredity, environment, and disease, differences in genetic backgrounds that exist most consistently in individuals are often important causes of individual differences in drug effects.8,9,10,11 Therefore, it is of great significance for promoting the precise administration of dapagliflozin to search for the influencing factors of the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin based on the polymorphisms of the key genes, to explore its molecular mechanism in the prevention and treatment of T2DKD, and to analyze the impact on the drug therapy. In terms of nephroprotective effect, clinical data indicated that patients treated with dapagliflozin still showed improvement in proteinuria and renal histomorphology after correcting for the effects of changes in metabolic indexes such as blood glucose and blood pressure, suggesting that dapagliflozin not only indirectly produces nephroprotective effect by inhibiting SGLT2 to improve blood glucose, ionic homeostasis, and blood pressure but also has a direct nephroprotective effect which is independent of the improvement in blood glucose and blood pressure, however, the mechanism for this effect is still unknown.12 Therefore, it is necessary to further explore the key factors of the direct nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin and its related mechanisms, so as to lay a pharmacological foundation for the individualized medication study of dapagliflozin.

Drug affinity responsive target stability (DARTS) is an important technology developed based on the basic principle of enhanced stability of target proteins to protease hydrolysis after binding to drugs, and combined with proteomics analysis technology can more accurately and efficiently discover and identify the target proteins.13 In order to explore the key factors of the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin independent of the hypoglycemic effect and to reveal the mechanism of its protection of renal function, we firstly used DARTS combined with quantitative proteomics techniques to discover the new target, GSK3β, for the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin independent of the hypoglycemic effect, which was evaluated and validated in vivo and in vitro. Based on the basic theory of pharmacogenomics, we further explored the role of GSK3B gene polymorphisms in the individual differences in the efficacy of dapagliflozin in reducing urinary protein to provide clinical evidence and a theoretical basis for efficacy prediction.

Results

Significant individual differences in the efficacy of dapagliflozin on reducing UACR

The retrospective study included 382 patients with T2DKD treated with dapagliflozin as the primary urinary protein-lowering regimen, of which 125 patients met the data inclusion criteria (Figure 1A). According to the predetermined criteria for evaluating response to drug therapy, urinary albumin-to-creatinine ratio (UACR) was used as the main pharmacodynamic index, and the patients were divided into a nephroprotective responder group (71 patients) and a non-responder group (54 patients), with the proportion of patients in the non-responder group amounting to 43.20% (Figure 1B). The correlation between baseline clinical features and UACR change

After 12 weeks of dapagliflozin treatment, blood glucose, blood pressure, and renal function improved in the nephroprotective responder group, as evidenced by a statistically significant decrease in body mass index (BMI), glycated hemoglobin (HbA1c), fasting plasma glucose (FPG), systolic blood pressure (SBP), diastolic blood pressure (DBP), and UACR compared with baseline (all p < 0.05), while there was no statistically significant change in estimated glomerular filtration rate (eGFR) (p > 0.05) (Table S2). For patients in the nephroprotective non-responder group, BMI, HbA1c, FPG, SBP, DBP, and eGFR all decreased significantly compared with baseline, and the differences were statistically significant (all p < 0.05). In contrast, there was no statistically significant change in UACR after 12 weeks of treatment in the nephroprotective non-responder group (p > 0.05) (Table S3).

Further comparing the differences in clinical indicators before and after dapagliflozin treatment, after 12 weeks of dapagliflozin treatment, there was no statistically significant difference between the differences before and after treatment in BMI, HbA1c, FPG, SBP, and DBP of patients in the nephroprotective responder group and the non-responder group (all p > 0.05); compared with the non-responder group, the responder group had a larger decrease in UACR and a smaller decrease in eGFR, and the difference was statistically significant (all p < 0.05) (Figure S1).

With dapagliflozin treatment response as the dependent variable and gender, age, diabetes duration, baseline HbA1c, baseline FPG, baseline eGFR, baseline UACR, and combined medication as the independent variables, respectively, binary logistic regression analyses was used to find that baseline eGFR and duration of diabetes were identified as the influencing factors for the improvement of UACR in patients with T2DKD treated with dapagliflozin (p < 0.05), with OR values were 0.091 and 0.077, respectively (Table 1). According to the calculation results of our constructed model, the OR values were small, and in further linear regression analysis, no significant correlation was found between the improvement of UACR and baseline eGFR and duration of diabetes mellitus (p > 0.05). Dapagliflozin targets GSK3β of podocytes

The incubation group refers to lysates pre-incubated with dapagliflozin prior to limited proteolysis in the DARTS assay. The results of the DARTS showed that the gray level of the bands around 50 kDa in the dapagliflozin incubation group was higher than that of the control group (p < 0.05) in a dose-dependent manner, suggesting that there may exist protein molecules with increased hydrolysis resistance and stability due to dapagliflozin incubation (Figure 2A). Quantitative proteomic analysis revealed that among the top 10 proteins with differential multiplicity in the dapagliflozin incubation group, GSK3β, SGLT2, RPL22, DYNC1I2, and ENO3 showed statistically significant differences (p < 0.05) under the same protease hydrolysis conditions (Table 2; Figures 2B and 2C). Therefore, dapagliflozin incubation effectively increased the resistance of GSK3β, SGLT2, RPL22, DYNC1I2, and ENO3 to the hydrolytic action of non-specific proteases, which may be the binding targets of dapagliflozin. Among these differential targets, the abundance value of GSK3β had the largest difference of 1.8-folds (Table 2). Among the proteins identified by DARTS-based quantitative proteomics, candidate target selection was based on both statistical enrichment and biological relevance. In addition to fold-change ranking, proteins were prioritized according to their reported involvement in renal pathophysiology and diabetic kidney disease. GSK3β was selected as a primary candidate due to its significant enrichment and its known functional roles in podocyte injury, cytoskeletal regulation, and autophagy in diabetic kidney disease. The results of a western blot to detect the protein level of GSK3β in the DARTS experimental solution showed that the protein amount of GSK3β in the dapagliflozin incubation group was higher than that in the hydrolase treatment group, with a statistically significant difference (Figure 2D). DARTS study using recombinant GSK3β protein as a carrier revealed that the protein amount of recombinant GSK3β in the dapagliflozin incubation group was higher than that in the hydrolase treatment group, suggesting that the recombinant GSK3β protein was more resistant to the hydrolysis of streptavidin after incubation with dapagliflozin (Figure 2E). Therefore, validation results based on the DARTS suggest that GSK3β may be a direct binding target for dapagliflozin. In order to evaluate the effect of dapagliflozin on GSK3β kinase activity, molecular-level pharmacodynamic screening was performed. The results showed that dapagliflozin inhibited GSK3β in a dose-dependent manner with an IC50 value of 308 nmol/L (Table S4; Figure 2F). In addition, a full-flex dynamic simulation study of the interaction between GSK3β and dapagliflozin was carried out, and a GSK3β-dapagliflozin binding mode map was constructed (Figure 3). SPR analysis of dapagliflozin-GSK3β interaction

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis demonstrated a direct interaction between dapagliflozin and GSK3β. A concentration-dependent increase in response signal was observed, although signals at low concentrations were close to baseline and not suitable for reliable fitting (Table S5; Figure 4). To ensure robustness, fitting was restricted to concentration ranges with clearly detectable responses. Using a kinetic fitting approach (local fitting for Rmax and RI, global fitting for drift), the equilibrium dissociation constant (KD) was determined to be 6.79 μM. Dapagliflozin and GSK3β inhibitor (BIO) protect the renal function of diabetic mice independent of glycemic improvement

To study the effects of dapagliflozin on renal function in T2DKD mice, mice who are 8-weeks-old were divided into control group (db/m group), diabetic mice group (db/db group), voglibose treatment group (db/db+VO group), GSK3β inhibitor group (db/db+BIO group), and dapagliflozin treatment group (db/db+DAPA group), and the corresponding interventions were given respectively for 4 weeks, and biochemical indexes such as blood glucose, urea nitrogen, urine protein and UACR were detected. The results showed that glycemic control in the dapagliflozin treatment group was at a similar level compared to the voglibose treatment group, and the difference was not statistically significant (p > 0.05) (Figure 5A). The blood glucose, urea nitrogen, urinary protein, and UACR were significantly higher in the db/db group than in the db/m group (p < 0.05), indicating higher blood glucose and impaired renal function in the db/db group (Figures 5B–5D). Compared with the voglibose treatment group, the dapagliflozin treatment group had lower urea nitrogen, urinary protein, and UACR, and the difference was statistically significant (p < 0.05) (Figures 5B–5D). Periodic Acid-Schiff (PAS) staining, Masson staining, Sirius red staining, Periodic Acid-Schiff Methenamine (PASM) staining, and transmission electron microscopy scanning of renal tissues from mice were performed to study the effect of dapagliflozin on the morphology of kidneys from db/db mice. The results showed that db/db mice 7had severe renal morphological damage, and dapagliflozin had an effect of slowing down glomerular injury manifestations induced by diabetes, such as basement membrane thickening, podocyte fusion, and thylakoid stromal hyperplasia, independently of hypoglycemic effects (Figures 5E–5J). Dapagliflozin ameliorated podocyte injury in vivo and in vitro

The results showed that the expression levels of Nephrin and Synaptopodin in the db/db group were significantly lower than those in the db/m group (p < 0.05), whereas those in the voglibose treatment group, the GSK3β inhibitor group, and the dapagliflozin treatment group were significantly higher than those in the db/db group (p < 0.05) (Figures 6A–6F). The expression levels of Nephrin and Synaptopodin were higher in the dapagliflozin treatment group compared to the voglibose treatment group (p < 0.05) (Figures 6A–6F). Discussion

Dapagliflozin has a favorable nephroprotective effect and is widely used in the treatment of patients with T2DM, but clinical practice has revealed large individual differences in its nephroprotective efficacy (about 35%–45.8% non-responders in the European population).6,7 Firstly, the study conducted a retrospective clinical study to confirm the clinical problem that there are significant individual differences in the effect of dapagliflozin in reducing urinary protein in patients with Chinese Han T2DKD. Further screening using DARTS combined with quantitative proteomics techniques discovered a new target for dapagliflozin binding (GSK3β), and this finding was further supported by SPR analysis demonstrating a direct interaction between dapagliflozin and GSK3β, thereby providing complementary biophysical evidence beyond protease susceptibility-based approaches and further supporting the role of GSK3β as a key mediator of the podocyte-protective effect of dapagliflozin. Finally, returning to the clinic to conduct a prospective study found a strong correlation between GSK3B rs60393216 and improvement in UACR after dapagliflozin treatment.

The retrospective clinical study found that 43.20% of patients with Chinese Han T2DKD treated with dapagliflozin were non-responders, using UACR as the primary pharmacodynamic index. There was no significant difference in gender, age, family history of diabetes mellitus, and treatment regimen between patients in the nephroprotective responder group and the non-responder group (all p > 0.05), suggesting that all of the conventional baseline clinical indicators are difficult to use for the prediction of the efficacy of the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin. In addition, baseline UACR levels were essentially the same between the two groups, suggesting that the better outcome in the responder group after dapagliflozin treatment is not attributed to a regression to the mean effect, and it is necessary to further explore biomarkers that can predict the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin. By analyzing the changes in clinical indicators after dapagliflozin treatment, it was found that the improvement in UACR was smaller in the non-responder group, while the decrease in eGFR was larger (p < 0.05), but the differences in the changes in BMI, HbA1c, FPG, SBP, and DBP were not statistically significant (all p > 0.05), which was consistent with previous studies. In the diabetic state, the expression and activity of SGLT2 in the proximal tubule are increased, and sodium-glucose synergistic reabsorption is enhanced, which reduces the sodium ion concentration in the macula densa area and activates tubuloglomerular feedback, leading to an increase in intraglomerular pressure and ultrafiltration. Dapagliflozin can reverse these pathological changes in diabetes by inhibiting SGLT2, reducing the increase in intraglobular pressure and ultrafiltration effects induced by tubuloglomerular feedback, which is a direct mechanism for decreasing urinary protein excretion, similar to the mechanism of the urinary protein-lowering effect of renin-angiotensin system (RAS) system inhibitors.14,15 The difference between SGLT2 inhibitors and RAS system inhibitors is that RAS system inhibitors reduce intraglomerular pressure mainly by dilating the outgoing glomerular vessels, whereas SGLT2 inhibitors reduce intraglomerular pressure by constricting the afferent glomerular arteriole.14 Nonetheless, these hemodynamic mechanisms alone do not fully explain the observed heterogeneity in UACR response, suggesting that additional intracellular or target-mediated pathways may be involved. However, the present study found a more significant decrease in eGFR in patients in the non-responder group, and it is more difficult to apply the classical mechanisms described above to explain the clinical phenomenon of poorer improvement in UACR. No linear correlation was observed between improvement in UACR and routine clinical indicators through further regression analysis. Therefore, the routine clinical indicators cannot predict the urinary protein-lowering efficacy of dapagliflozin effectively, and the reasons for the individual differences need to be further explored.

Dapagliflozin has a nephroprotective effect independent of metabolic improvement, but the target and mechanism have not been clarified. Therefore, exploring the targets of the nephroprotective effects of dapagliflozin independent of metabolic improvement is important to clarify the key factors that contribute to individual differences in the nephroprotective effects of dapagliflozin. Given that dapagliflozin is a small-molecule drug with a relatively simple structure, we selected the DARTS combined with the proteomic techniques as the method to discover targets, which relies on the direct binding of the prototype drug to the protein target, with higher reliability and stability, and effectively avoids the shortcomings of active proteomics techniques, which require probe binding or modification of drug molecules.13,16,17 The results showed that GSK3β might be a binding target of dapagliflozin. In order to further verify that dapagliflozin can bind GSK3β, we carried out a series of validations such as target prediction based on the PharmMapper target prediction database, molecular docking, molecular dynamics simulation, DARTS assay combined with western blot analysis, and DARTS assay of GSK3β recombinant protein solution, which confirmed the direct binding relationship between dapagliflozin and GSK3β from multiple perspectives. To further validate target engagement, we performed SPR analysis, which demonstrated a direct interaction between dapagliflozin and GSK3β with an estimated affinity in the low micromolar range. Although this affinity is lower than that of classical ATP-competitive GSK3β inhibitors, it is consistent with a moderate binding interaction that may be sufficient for functional modulation rather than complete enzymatic blockade. This observation aligns with our kinase activity data, showing partial inhibition of GSK3β, and supports the notion that dapagliflozin may regulate GSK3β through a non-canonical mechanism. Such moderate-affinity interactions are increasingly recognized in pharmacology, particularly for drugs with pleiotropic effects. On the other hand, in order to explore the effect of dapagliflozin on GSK3β kinase activity, we applied the Kinase-Glo kinase luminescence assay to determine the changes in GSK3β activity under the action of dapagliflozin, and completed the evaluation of pharmacodynamic effect at the molecular level.18 The results showed that dapagliflozin had an inhibitory effect on the activity of GSK3β, with an IC50 of 308 nmol/L, which was a favorable inhibitory efficiency. The inhibitory potency of dapagliflozin against GSK3β was further evaluated in relation to its reported pharmacokinetic exposure. Clinical pharmacokinetic studies have shown that dapagliflozin achieves steady-state plasma concentrations in the low nanomolar to submicromolar range after standard dosing, depending on dose and renal function.6,14,15 This suggests partial overlap with the observed IC50, indicating potential pharmacological relevance of GSK3β modulation under therapeutic conditions. Although GSK3β has been shown to play a critical role in the development of T2DKD, we present here for the first time evidence supporting that GSK3β is a potential target of dapagliflozin. Although GSK3α shares high structural similarity with GSK3β, particularly in the catalytic domain, emerging evidence suggests that these two isoforms differ in subcellular localization, regulatory regions, and protein-protein interactions, which may influence ligand accessibility and functional engagement. In the present study, GSK3α was not identified in the DARTS-based screening, and all subsequent validation experiments consistently supported GSK3β as the primary interacting target. Nevertheless, we cannot exclude the possibility that dapagliflozin may also interact with GSK3α under certain conditions, which warrants further investigation. Given that the downstream signaling pathways of GSK3β have been extensively characterized, the present study does not further dissect these mechanisms but interprets the findings within this established framework. We stimulated glomerular podocytes, mesangial cells, and tubular epithelial cells with high glucose, respectively, to detect the kinase activity of GSK3β. The results revealed that the kinase activity of GSK3β is most significantly upregulated in podocytes. Podocytes, also known as the visceral epithelial cells, are an important part of the filtration barrier and are terminally differentiated cells with very limited repair and regeneration, which are closely associated with proteinuria.19,20 Therefore, based on the results of the previous study and the relevant literature from clinical and basic research, our subsequent study focused on podocytes.

In this study, db/db mice given dapagliflozin were controlled at the same level of blood glucose as db/db mice treated with voglibose, and the dapagliflozin group was found to be able to effectively improve renal function indices, such as urea nitrogen, urinary protein, and UACR, suggesting that dapagliflozin had a nephroprotective effect independently of the improvement in blood glucose. Similar strategies have been adopted in previous studies, where α-glucosidase inhibitors were used as glycemic controls to isolate the glucose-independent effects of SGLT2 inhibitors. Similarly, the results of transmission electron microscopy and histopathological staining suggested that dapagliflozin had a preventive effect on renal morphological changes in db/db mice independently of the improvement in blood glucose, and our findings were consistent with the clinical phenomenon reported in the literature.12 The expression levels of Nephrine and Synaptopodin are closely associated with podocyte morphology and proteinuria levels and play an important role in the occurrence and development of T2DKD.21,22,23,24 In this study, we found that the dapagliflozin group was able to increase the expression of Nephrin and Synaptopodin in glomerular podocytes and decrease the level of apoptosis in renal parenchymal cells more effectively in db/db mice compared to the voglibose group. However, we acknowledge that additional molecular markers of apoptosis (e.g., cleaved caspase-3, Bax/Bcl-2) would further strengthen these findings, which warrants future investigation. This observation further supports that the renoprotective effect of dapagliflozin is not solely attributable to glycemic control, but likely involves additional intracellular mechanisms. The results of the evaluation of filtration barrier function using the Transwell model as a carrier also confirmed that dapagliflozin could effectively alleviate the impairment of podocyte filtration barrier function under high glucose conditions. Therefore, dapagliflozin has a podocyte protective effect independently of glycemic improvement.

GSK3β is a subtype of glycogen synthase kinase that is distributed in the liver, lungs, brain, and kidneys and has an important role in the development of T2DKD proteinuria.25,26 In this study, we found that the administration of voglibose, GSK3β inhibitors or dapagliflozin, respectively, could effectively alleviate the abnormally elevated activity of GSK3β in the renal tissues of db/db mice, and the dapagliflozin group had a more significant improvement compared with the voglibose group that controlled blood glucose alone, suggesting that dapagliflozin had the effect of inhibiting the activity of GSK3β independently of the improvement of blood glucose. Notably, although the GSK3β inhibitor 6-bromoindirubin-3'-oxime (BIO) also reduced GSK3beta activity, its renoprotective effect was less pronounced than that of dapagliflozin, indicating that GSK3β inhibition alone may not fully account for the observed therapeutic benefit. Dapagliflozin likely exerts a multi-mechanistic effect, combining partial GSK3β inhibition with systemic metabolic regulation, which together contribute to improved renal outcomes. This may explain why dapagliflozin demonstrates superior renoprotective efficacy compared with selective GSK3β inhibition alone. Therefore, dapagliflozin may inhibit podocyte apoptosis and protect podocyte function by affecting the kinase activity of GSK3β. In this context, GSK3β is more likely to represent a complementary pathway rather than a simple downstream effector of SGLT2 inhibition. So far, it has provided a theoretical basis and a new entry point for further exploration of GSK3B gene polymorphisms affecting the efficacy of dapagliflozin. Although dapagliflozin is a representative SGLT2 inhibitor, accumulating evidence suggests that renal benefits are largely considered a class effect mediated by shared hemodynamic and metabolic mechanisms. In the present study, we did not observe clear evidence supporting a unique drug-specific mechanism beyond the SGLT2 inhibition class. Instead, our findings, together with previous reports, support that dapagliflozin exerts renoprotective effects consistent with the established class mechanisms, while additional intracellular targets such as GSK3β may further contribute to its pharmacological profile. Overall, these findings suggest that SGLT2 inhibition and GSK3β modulation may represent parallel and cell-type-specific mechanisms contributing to the overall renoprotective effects of dapagliflozin. Given the structural similarity among SGLT2 inhibitors, it is possible that related agents may share similar off-target interactions; however, this remains to be experimentally validated.

GSK3B, located on chromosome 3q13.3, is the encoding gene of the new target of action of dapagliflozin (GSK3β) identified in this study.27 Mutations in genes encoding the targets of drug action are important factors contributing to individual differences in drug efficacy by affecting the expression and function of the encoded proteins.8,9,10 Therefore, we further explored the key gene mutations leading to individual differences in the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin to provide new ideas for the rational application of dapagliflozin. The results showed that the polymorphism of GSK3B was related to the efficacy of dapagliflozin. Although direct functional validation of these variants was not performed, available annotation suggests that they may potentially influence gene regulation or protein activity, thereby modulating drug response. Further in silico functional annotation showed that rs60393216 is located in the 3′-UTR of GSK3B and exhibits limited predicted regulatory features. However, as a tagSNP, it is likely to capture the genetic effect of multiple linked variants within the same locus. Consistent with this, additional SNPs within the GSK3B region displayed higher functional scores, particularly in promoter, enhancer, and transcription factor binding site annotations. These findings suggest that the observed association may reflect combined regulatory effects at the haplotype level rather than a single functional variant. After treatment with dapagliflozin, in patients with T2DKD carrying a higher number of the T allele of rs60393216, the improvement in TC and UACR were less obvious, while the transient decrease in eGFR was more pronounced; the improvement of SBP and UACR was less significant in patients with T2DKD carrying a higher number of rs1488766 T allele; the improvement of TC and UACR is less pronounced in patients with T2DKD carrying a higher number of rs1807075 C allele. The above findings indicated that GSK3B gene polymorphisms could affect the therapeutic effect of dapagliflozin in terms of TC, SBP, eGFR, and UACR, and also confirmed that the GSK3B gene was closely related to the level of UACR at the clinical level in the Chinese Han population. The baseline UACR refers to the initial UACR level of a patient before any intervention. By measuring the baseline UACR, kidney damage can be detected at an early stage, especially when renal function is not yet significantly impaired (e.g., normal eGFR but elevated UACR), providing a basis for early intervention. Our study further indicates that baseline UACR plays a crucial role in predicting the long-term efficacy of drug treatment in patients.

To evaluate the combined effect of multiple SNP sites on the efficacy of dapagliflozin, 152 patients with T2DKD were categorized into 0–3 score group, 4–6 score group, and 7–9 score group based on the results of genetic risk scores, and the correlation analysis between the GRS scores and the clinical indexes was performed. The results showed that patients with T2DKD with higher risk gene scores had poorer urinary protein-lowering effects of dapagliflozin, suggesting that the combined effect of the SNPs of the GSK3B was an important factor contributing to individual differences in the nephroprotective effect of dapagliflozin. Previous pharmacogenomic studies on the GSK3B gene focused on therapeutic drugs for psychiatric disorders, and related evaluations were often centered on independent analyses of the association of a single or a few SNP sites with drug efficacy. In this study, we investigated the combined effect of five tagged SNPs in the GSK3B gene on the nephroprotective effect of the glucose-lowering drug dapagliflozin by using the GRS method for the first time. These findings should be interpreted as exploratory and hypothesis-generating, and further functional studies are needed to establish causal mechanisms.

Limitations of the study

This study has several limitations. First, although direct binding between dapagliflozin and GSK3β is supported by SPR, detailed structural characterization was not performed. Second, functional validation of GSK3B genetic variants was beyond the scope of this study. Third, additional cellular systems and animal models may further strengthen the generalizability of the findings. These limitations warrant further investigation.

Experimental model and study participant details

Human participants

Cohort of the retrospective clinical research

The data for the retrospective clinical study were collected from patients with T2DKD who attended the outpatient departments of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University, and the Lianyungang First People’s Hospital from June 1, 2019, to June 30, 2022. A total of 125 adult Han Chinese patients with T2DKD (73 men and 52 women; age range: 35–75 years) who met the predefined inclusion and exclusion criteria were enrolled in the retrospective clinical cohort. All patients received dapagliflozin (10 mg/day) for more than 12 weeks as the primary albuminuria-lowering therapy. Patients were allocated into responder and non-responder groups according to the predefined renal response criteria based on changes in UACR after 12 weeks of dapagliflozin treatment.

Cohort of the prospective clinical research

The prospective clinical study on the polymorphism of the GSK3B gene was conducted at the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, and the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University, with 152 adult Han Chinese patients (20–70 years of age) suffering from T2DKD participating in the research (including 83 men and 69 women).

Animal models

Male db/db mice and age-matched male db/m littermates (8 weeks old) were purchased from GemPharmatech Co., Ltd. (Nanjing, China). Animals were housed under specific pathogen-free conditions at 22 ± 2°C with a 12-h light/dark cycle and had free access to standard chow and water. Eight-week-old db/m mice served as the normal control group. Db/db mice were randomly assigned into four groups (n = 6 per group for final analyses): db/db model group, db/db + voglibose group, db/db + BIO group, and db/db + dapagliflozin group. All treatments were administered once daily by oral gavage for 4 weeks. One mouse per group was excluded from the final analysis due to technical errors during sample collection, with a starting sample size of 7 per group. Each animal was considered an independent biological replicate. Each animal was considered an independent biological replicate. All animals were housed in the same cages before and during the experiment, allowing them free access to water and food.

Cell lines

Human renal tubular epithelial cells (Cat# CL-0109) were obtained from Wuhan Procell Life Science & Technology Co., Ltd. Mouse mesangial cells (SV40, Cat# GNM21) were obtained from the Cell Bank of the Chinese Academy of Sciences. Mouse podocytes (Cat# BFN60700330) were obtained from Shanghai Qingqi Biotechnology Co., Ltd. Mouse renal tubular epithelial cells (Cat# LM858) were obtained from Shanghai Lianmai Bioengineering Co., Ltd. The conditions and specific procedures for cell culture can be referred to our previous studies.28,29,30 All in vitro experiments were independently repeated at least three times (biological replicates), and each condition was performed in triplicate (technical replicates).The identity of all cell lines was authenticated by the respective commercial suppliers before distribution. No additional authentication or mycoplasma testing was performed after receipt of the cell lines.

Ethical approval

Human studies were approved by the medical ethics committees of the Affiliated Hospital of Xuzhou Medical University, the Affiliated Hospital of Jiangnan University, and Lianyungang First People’s Hospital (Approval Numbers: XYFY2021-KL094-01, LS2021091, and KY-20210414001-01). All procedures involving human participants were conducted in accordance with the Declaration of Helsinki. The prospective clinical study was registered in the Chinese Clinical Trial Register (ChiCTR2200066653; registered December 13, 2022). No sex specific analysis was performed because no significant sex imbalance was observed among study groups. A portion of the baseline characteristics of the T2DKD cohort has been previously reported in a study based on the same registered clinical trial investigating the association between GSK3B polymorphisms and T2DKD susceptibility.31 However, no treatment outcomes, follow-up data, or analyses related to dapagliflozin response were included in that report.

All animal experiments were approved by the Animal Ethics Committee of Xuzhou Medical University (Approval Number: 20210226359) and conducted in accordance with institutional guidelines for the care and use of laboratory animals. Male mice were used in this study. No sex-specific analysis was performed because no significant sex imbalance was observed in the clinical cohorts. Only male mice were used because male db/db mice develop a more consistent DKD phenotype; therefore, potential sex-dependent differences in animal experiments could not be evaluated and should be considered a limitation of this study.

Sex as a biological variable

Both male and female participants were included in the clinical cohort, and sex was recorded as a demographic variable. Sex was included as a covariate in the multivariable analyses where appropriate. Only male mice were used in the animal experiments to minimize hormonal variability and because male db/db mice develop a more consistent diabetic kidney disease phenotype. Therefore, potential sex-dependent differences in the animal experiments could not be evaluated and should be considered a limitation of this study.

Inclusion and exclusion criteria of the retrospective clinical research

Inclusion criteria: (1) Met the diagnostic criteria for T2DM; (2) Met the diagnostic standards for DKD as outlined in the “Expert Consensus on the Prevention and Treatment of Diabetic Kidney Disease,” the American Diabetes Association standards, “Chinese Journal of Nephrology,” and other guidelines; UACR of ≥30 mg/g or 3 mg/mmol; clinically diagnosed with DKD; (3) Treated with dapagliflozin at a dosage of 10 mg/day for a continuous period of over 12 weeks; (4) No use of ACEi/ARBs or stable treatment dosage for at least 2 months within 4 weeks of initiating dapagliflozin; (5) No use of medications affecting urinary protein other than dapagliflozin within 4 weeks; (6) Well-controlled or non-hypertensive; (7) Age between 35 and 75 years.

Exclusion Criteria: (1) Patients with other diseases causing increased urinary protein; (2) Patients with endocrine or metabolic diseases other than diabetes; (3) Patients who experienced heart failure or stroke within the last 12 weeks; (4) Patients with abnormal liver function; (5) Creatinine clearance rate <45 mL/min; (6) Patients with a history of malignant tumors; (7) Patients with refractory hypertension.

Grouping

Clinical research evidence indicates that increased urinary protein is a significant marker of DKD (Diabetic Kidney Disease), and elevated urinary protein levels can triple the risk of kidney events and increase the risk of cardiovascular events by 2.5 times.9,10 In this project, we use UACR as the primary observation indicator of the renal protective effect of dapagliflozin. To analyze the individual differences in the effect of dapagliflozin in reducing urinary protein in Chinese Han population with DKD, we set a baseline UACR of 30% as the threshold. After 12 weeks of treatment, a decrease in UACR of >30% or a negative urinary protein test is considered the renal protective response group, while a decrease in UACR of ≤30% and a positive test is considered the renal non-responsive group.

Drug affinity responsive target stabilization assay and proteomics analysis

DARTS assay was performed using cellular protein lysates as follows (Figure 12). Briefly, cells at 80–85% confluence were harvested and lysed in ice-cold lysis buffer for 10 min. Cell lysates were centrifuged at 18,000 × g for 10 min at 4°C, and the supernatant was collected. Protein concentration was determined using a BCA assay, and equal amounts of protein were used for subsequent experiments. For ligand incubation, lysates were divided into a dapagliflozin incubation group and a vehicle control group (DMSO). The incubation group was treated with dapagliflozin at the indicated concentrations (or a defined concentration) and incubated for 30 min at room temperature to allow protein-ligand interaction. Limited proteolysis was then performed by adding Pronase (Roche, Switzerland) at a defined enzyme-to-protein ratio (add if known; otherwise “optimized concentration”) and incubating at room temperature for 15 min. The reaction was terminated by adding protease and phosphatase inhibitor cocktails followed by SDS-PAGE loading buffer. Proteins were then separated by SDS-PAGE and visualized using Coomassie Brilliant Blue staining. Differential band intensities between dapagliflozin-treated and control groups were quantified using densitometric analysis. Molecular docking and MD simulation

The Surflex molecular docking module, a component of the Sybyl-X 2.1 software suite (Tripos Associates Inc., USA), was employed to carry out molecular docking studies.The crystal structure of GSK3β was obtained from the Protein DataBank (PDB ID: 2O5K) and used as the receptor structure for docking. The preparation for protein docking using the Biopolymer module includes specific steps such as adding hydrogen atoms to the protein molecule, removing all water molecules, and repairing any missing amino acid side chains. The three-dimensional structure of dapagliflozin was constructed using the Sketch module and assigned Gasteiger-Huckel charges, followed by energy minimization using the Tripos force field. The “ligand” method from the Protomol module was employed to generate binding pockets, and the scoring function total score was utilized to assess the binding affinity between the target protein and the ligand molecules.

The anticipated binding interactions of the compounds, as deduced from molecular docking analyses, may not accurately reflect their true behavior, given that the Surflex docking approach considers the receptor protein to be an inflexible entity.Using the target protein-dapagliflozin complex obtained from molecular docking as a starting point, the system was placed at the center of a cubic box composed of TIP3P water molecules. The standard Amber03 force field (ff03) and the commonly used General Amber force field (GAFF) were used for parameter generation of the target protein and dapagliflozin, respectively. Structural optimization and electrostatic potential calculation were first conducted using the HF/6-31G∗ method, followed by the antechamber program to calculate the net charge of dapagliflozin and the required force field parameters. The total number of water molecules added to the entire simulation system was 227, and the number of chloride ions was 7. Prior to molecular dynamics simulation, energy minimization was performed using the sander module in Amber 12, optimizing the structure of the complex by first restricting all atoms of the target protein and ligand, optimizing only the water molecules and chloride ions using a 2,500-step steepest descent method followed by 2,500 steps of conjugate gradient. Then, with all main chains of the target protein’s amino acids restrained, only the side chains of the amino acids, ligand, water molecules, and ions were optimized, also using the aforementioned 5,000-step method. Finally, without any restrictions on the entire system, a 5,000-step steepest descent was followed by 5,000 steps of conjugate gradient. This three-step process completed the reasonable energy minimization. Subsequently, a gradual heating method was employed, with an equilibration partition of 60 ps raising the temperature from 0 K to 310 K (physiological temperature). Finally, a 100 ns molecular dynamics simulation was conducted at a constant temperature of 310 K with a step size of 2.0 fs. Trajectories were saved every 1 ns for subsequent MM/GBSA free energy calculations and free energy decomposition analysis.

The binding affinity calculation formula for the target protein-dapagliflozin complex is as follows:ΔGbind=Gcomplex−Gprotein−Gligand=ΔEMM+ΔGGB+ΔGSA−TΔS=ΔEvdw+ΔEele+ΔGGB+ΔGSA−TΔSWhere, ΔEMM represents the interaction energy between the target protein and ligand in the gas phase (i.e., vacuum), which includes van der Waals ΔEvdw and electrostatic interactions ΔEele; ΔGGB and ΔGSA represent the polar and nonpolar parts of the desolvation free energy, respectively; -TΔS represents the entropy change at temperature T.

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis

Surface plasmon resonance (SPR) analysis was performed to evaluate the interaction between dapagliflozin and GSK3β using a Biacore T200 instrument (Cytiva, Uppsala, Sweden). Recombinant human GSK3β protein (purity >90%, expressed in E. coli; Sino Biological Inc., Beijing, China) was immobilized on a CM5 sensor chip (Cytiva, 29149603) via standard amine-coupling chemistry. Briefly, the chip surface was activated with a mixture of 0.2 M N-ethyl-N′-(3-dimethylaminopropyl) carbodiimide (EDC) and 0.05 M N-hydroxysuccinimide (NHS), followed by injection of GSK3β diluted in 10 mM sodium acetate buffer (pH 4.5). Residual active groups were blocked with 1 M ethanolamine (pH 8.5). A reference flow cell without protein immobilization was prepared under identical conditions. Dapagliflozin was dissolved in DMSO and diluted into running buffer (PBS containing 0.05% Tween 20) to generate a concentration series. All samples were matched for DMSO content. Multi-cycle kinetic experiments were conducted at 25°C with a constant flow rate of 30 μL/min. Each cycle consisted of an association phase and a dissociation phase, followed by regeneration of the sensor surface using glycine-HCl (pH 2.5).Sensorgrams were processed by double-referencing (subtraction of the reference flow cell and blank injections). Due to low signal-to-noise ratios at lower concentrations, data analysis was restricted to concentration ranges exhibiting clear response signals. Curve fitting was performed using a kinetic model, with Rmax and RI fitted locally and drift fitted globally.

DNA isolation and genotyping

The Minor Allele Frequency (MAF) typically refers to a less prevalent allele within a specific population. A higher MAF enhances the statistical power in genetic association studies.36 We analyzed single nucleotide polymorphisms (SNPs) within the GSK3B gene with an MAF exceeding 0.2 for their linkage disequilibrium, characterized by an r2 value above 0.8, and categorized them into five distinct regions. Giving priority to SNPs located in functionally annotated regions, we identified five tag SNPs (rs60393216, rs334558, rs1488766, rs59669360, and rs1807075) for further investigation into their potential role in the individual variability of the renal protective effects of dapagliflozin. Genomic DNA extraction from peripheral blood leukocytes was accomplished using the SiMax Genome DNA Kit from Sbsbio (Shanghai, China). Quantification and statistical analysis

Data analysis was conducted using GraphPad Prism (version 8.0, GraphPad Software, San Diego, CA, USA). Categorical data and genotype distributions for Hardy–Weinberg equilibrium were assessed using the Chi-square (χ2) test. Quantitative data are presented as mean ± standard deviation (mean ± SD), and sample sizes (n) represent independent biological replicates unless otherwise indicated. Normality of data distribution was assessed using the Shapiro-Wilk test, and homogeneity of variance was evaluated using Levene’s test prior to parametric analyses. Parameters with a normal distribution were compared using unpaired two-tailed Student’s t tests, while comparisons among multiple groups were performed using one-way analysis of variance (ANOVA) followed by Tukey’s multiple-comparisons post hoc test. If assumptions of normality or equal variance were not met, non-parametric tests were applied as appropriate. Multiple linear regression analysis was employed to investigate the association between the renoprotective effects of dapagliflozin and GSK3B gene loci. Linkage disequilibrium among GSK3B loci was assessed using Haploview 3.2. Multivariate regression analyses were performed adjusting for baseline clinical variables to evaluate whether GSK3B polymorphisms independently predicted the response to dapagliflozin.
