Asymptotic e-processes
Pierre-François Massiani, Sebastian Schulze, Mattes Mollenhauer

Abstract
We introduce the concept of an asymptotic e-process , which is a doubly indexed stochastic process ( E m , n ) m , n ∈ ℕ (E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}} that approximates an e-process with monitoring time n n in terms of a suitable limiting behavior for an approximation parameter m → ∞ m\to\infty . This theory is motivated by practical applications in sequential hypothesis testing, in which e-variables can only be constructed approximately from observations due to model misspecification or estimation errors. We derive an asymptotic version of Ville’s inequality, which bounds excursion probabilities of ( E m , n ) m , n ∈ ℕ (E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}} over some threshold uniformly over n n up to a time horizon r m r_{m} that is determined by the quality of process approximation over m m . We investigate properties of asymptotic e-processes, their connections to asymptotic supermartingales, and provide examples of how they can be constructed from asymptotic e-variables.

Introduction

Sequential testing
addresses the problem of statistical decision-making in settings where observations are collected continuously over time, requiring repeated assessment of whether accumulated evidence warrants rejection of the null hypothesis.
Such tests arise naturally in a wide range of settings where data are collected sequentially, from monitoring patients in clinical trials and online A/B user studies to scientific experimentation.
Starting with the work of
21
, sequential tests have been characterized through
stopping times
, that is, data-dependent (possibly infinite) times at which the null is rejected.
A recurring source of criticism, however, is that guarantees are tied to a stopping rule that must be fully specified before any data are observed.
In practice, this separation is routinely violated, invalidating the guarantees.
As
13
illustrate,
even well-meaning practitioners can fall into this trap.
This phenomenon is arguably a central cause of irregularities in the reporting of statistical evidence
(
17
)
.

SAVI and e-processes.

The recent framework of
safe anytime-valid inference (SAVI)
addresses this issue by introducing sequential statistical objects (such as measures of evidence
and corresponding confidence sequences) that provide appropriate guarantees at any point in time, regardless of the stopping rule applied.
A central class of such objects is that of
e-processes

(
6
,
12
,
11
,
5
)
, which can be constructed based on
e-variables

(
20
)
.
Intuitively, e-processes accumulate evidence against the null hypothesis; they should remain small uniformly across time if the null holds, and grow if it does not.
Their close connection to supermartingales makes them theoretically tractable, and Ville’s inequality, which bounds their excursion probability past any fixed threshold, provides the central instrument for translating them into practical tests.
The game-theoretic view established in the
testing-by-betting
framework
(
16
,
18
,
19
)
provides another tool for interpretation and analysis of e-processes as an adversarial game.
E-processes, SAVI, and the testing-by-betting framework have seen rapid development in recent years; we refer to
13
and the references therein (particularly, Section 1.8) for a comprehensive review.

Asymptotic SAVI.

In practice, however, the construction of e-processes often requires precise knowledge of quantities that are unavailable and have to be estimated from data instead.
Establishing finite-sample guarantees based on these estimates is challenging and often requires additional constraints such as restricting the null hypothesis
(
10
)
.
This mirrors classical hypothesis testing, where finite-sample tests, particularly under large composite nulls or in nonparametric settings, are often intractable, and one often resorts instead to
asymptotic tests
. Such tests are obtained by substituting consistent estimators for unknown quantities and recover validity in the limit of infinite data.
Yet, while asymptotic testing has a rich and well-developed theory in the classical setting, the corresponding framework for SAVIis less mature and has only been partially investigated in recent works.
In particular, an asymptotic counterpart to e-processes remains unavailable to our knowledge.

7
introduce approximate and asymptotic e- and p-variables. While this facilitates an approximate treatment of the building blocks of SAVI, it stops short of addressing asymptotic anytime-validity directly.

22
propose distribution-uniform anytime p-values satisfying anytime-validity asymptotically with respect to an approximation index, providing the closest existing analogue to our proposed framework.

3
propose an implementation that achieves a similar practical goal but without uniformity over stopping rules, making it less directly comparable to the SAVIframework.
In the next paragraph, we show that a naive construction of asymptotic e-processes directly mirroring that of
22
yields an overly strong condition and is therefore impractical to work with in truly asymptotic settings.

Infinite monitoring horizons are too restrictive for finite approximation indices.

To motivate our approach, we briefly consider the most direct generalization of e-processes to the asymptotic setting.
Following
7
and
22
, we consider a doubly-indexed nonnegative random process
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
with
monitoring time index

{n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
approximation index

{m\in\mathbb{N}}
.
For a fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
, suppose that the process
E_{\bullet,n}:=(E_{m,n})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
constitutes a strongly asymptotic e-variable
(
7
, Definition 3.5)
; that is,
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]\leq 1
, where
P
is a distribution constituting the null hypothesis (later sections allow collections of such distributions for the null).
Naively fusing this with the definition of e-processes (
Definition

2.4
), one would impose the condition that
(E_{m,\tau})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is also a strongly asymptotic e-variable; that is,

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]\leq 1.

(1)

for
every
sequence of stopping times
\tau=(\tau_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
.
This is indeed the natural technical requirement in order to obtain asymptotic analogues for Ville’s inequality uniformly over
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
However, the condition (
1
) is too strong to be useful in practice, as it excludes important, natural constructions of asymptotic e-processes based on sufficiently regular asymptotic e-values.

In particular, we consider the cumulative product construction, which is the cornerstone of e-processes that are also supermartingales in the non-asymptotic case.
Let us assume that we have a collection of independent asymptotic e-variables
(e_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m,n}]=1+\delta_{m}
for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, with vanishing approximation error
\delta_{m}>0
and
\delta_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
We define the cumulative product

E_{m,n}:=\prod_{i=0}^{n}e_{m,i}.

We see that, for every
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]=(1+\delta_{m})^{n},

and hence for any almost surely finite but unbounded stopping time
\tau

that is independent of
E
, we get

\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}P[\tau=n]\,(1+\delta_{m})^{n},

(2)

where the right-hand side (RHS)diverges for every fixed
m
if the tail
P[\tau=n]
does not decay fast enough as
n\to\infty
,
clearly conflicting with (
1
).

The cause for this is clear: the excess
\delta_{m}
in the expectation of each factor compounds over time, and for any fixed
m
this accumulation eventually dominates. Condition
Equation

1
offers no way to account for this compounding effect, making it incompatible with the cumulative product construction for any finite
m
.
The key insight motivating our definition is that this compounding can be controlled by introducing a monitoring horizon
r_{m}\in\mathbb{N}
that grows with
m
but slowly enough that the accumulated error remains negligible. Specifically, we show that if
r_{m}\cdot\delta_{m}\rightarrow 0
as
m\rightarrow\infty
, the diverging series is effectively truncated before the error accumulates, and an asymptotic analogue of Ville’s inequality can be recovered up to time
r_{m}
under appropriate conditional assumptions (
Theorem

6.1
). As
m\rightarrow\infty
and the approximation improves,
r_{m}\rightarrow\infty
as well, so the guarantees become truly anytime-valid in the limit. This is the central idea behind our definition of an asymptotic e-process, which we develop formally in Section 3.

This work: asymptotic e-processes.

We propose the concept of
r
-asymptotic e-processes
, often abbreviated “asymptotic e-processes” when
r
is clear from context, and define them as doubly-indexed nonnegative processes
(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
,
as introduced in the previous section.
Intuitively, we require
(E_{m,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

to approximate an e-process in a suitable limiting
sense whenever
m\to\infty
.
The definition needs to satisfy two constraints:

(i)

compatibility with the SAVIframework, in the sense that SAVIshould be possible asymptotically; and

(ii)

to be easily constructed from sufficiently regular asymptotic e-variables, as this is arguably one of the key strengths of (non-asymptotic) e-processes.

This second requirement is critical to enable practical relevance.

As motivated in the previous section, the definition incorporates a monitoring horizon
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
that grows with the approximation index
m\in\mathbb{N}
, capturing the time horizon up to which the process enjoys anytime-valid properties asymptotically.
In particular, our definition implies the following asymptotic version of Ville’s inequality
(
Theorem

5.1
):

\limsup_{m\to\infty}P\left[\sup_{n\in\{1,\dots,r_{m}\}}E_{m,n}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha.

(3)

When
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
, the guarantees are truly anytime-valid in the limit; this is certainly the most interesting and motivating case, but we allow general choices.
The inequality
Equation

3
confirms that for asymptotic e-processes,
Ville’s inequality holds for increasingly large time horizons as the approximation quality improves.
We visualize this phenomenon in
Figure

1
.

of an asymptotic e-process
(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}

for a fixed approximation level

.
The blue trajectories cross the threshold
1/\alpha

at some monitoring time
n\leq r_{m}
, where
the time horizon
r_{m}
depends on
the quality of approximation determined by
m
.
Our asymptotic version of Ville’s
inequality in (

)
shows that asymptotically in
m
, the probability
of this event is at most
\alpha
.
In contrast to notions that impose
r_{m}=\infty
in their version
of Ville’s inequality, the red trajectories
crossing the threshold for
n>r_{m}

cannot be accounted for in general due to
a compounding approximation error effect.

Section

for a detailed construction of the visualized process
and the experimental setup.

As a consequence of
Equation

3
, calibrating
r
-asymptotic e-processes with
r\equiv\infty
automatically yields anytime p-values in the sense of
22
, and the converse also holds under an additional mild technical condition identified in
7
.
However, asymptotic e-processes are
more permissive than anytime p-values,
since the condition
r\equiv\infty
is very strong.
More generally, allowing arbitrary sequences
r

enables the definition of more or less restrictive notions of
r
-asymptotic e- and p-processes, based on the corresponding notions of e- and p-values introduced in
7
.
We focus on the strongest such notion for e-processes with general choices of
r
, and the anytime p-values of
22
relate to a weaker notion, but impose
r\equiv\infty
.
In any case, the duality between e-processes and p-processes via calibration
(
12
,
13
)
carries to the asymptotic versions of the notions.
A practical benefit of asymptotic e-processes is that they are easy to construct from sequences of (sufficiently regular) asymptotic e-variables in the sense of
7
, carrying over the standard constructions of non-asymptotic e-processes
(
13
, Chapter 7)
.

1.1
Summary of contributions

For the reader’s convenience, we give a more detailed
high-level overview of the results in this paper.
In
Definition

3.1
, we introduce

r
-asymptotic e-processes
as doubly-indexed nonnegative processes
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
satisfying an asymptotic e-variable condition for all stopping times up to a horizon
r_{m}\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
.
Here, the extended integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a parameter of the notion quantifying the maximum monitoring time at approximation index
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Our first main result,
Theorem

3.3
, shows via a diagonal argument that the monitoring horizon
r
can generally be assumed to diverge to
\infty
under mild conditions.

Theorem

3.4
provides several equivalent characterizations identifying
r
-asymptotic e-processes as those that are upper bounded for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
by a process that is a supermartingale up to time
r_{m}
and satisfies an asymptotic calibration property.
This result can be seen as a direct generalization of
12
to the asymptotic case, and enables a clear interpretation of the sequence
r
.
It also formally justifies the interest of introducing the sequence
r
in the definition, since it shows that
r
-asymptotic e-processes with
r\equiv\infty
can be renormalized to be e-processes.
Finally, we establish in
Theorem

3.7
that, for processes that converge in
L_{1}
along the
m
-axis, being an
r
-asymptotic e-process for some diverging sequence
r
is equivalent to the limit itself being an e-process.
This further grounds the intuition that asymptotic e-processes are approximations of e-processes that are only valid asymptotically, and that no asymptotic properties are lost by allowing finite values of
r_{m}
as long as
r
diverges.

We then investigate the connection between
r
-asymptotic e-processes and supermartingales. We
introduce in
Definition

4.1

asymptotic supermartingales (ASMs)
as processes
converging in
L_{1}
to a supermartingale, and in
Definition

4.2
the
asymptotic supermartingale property (ASP)
as a weaker condition pointwise in
n\in\mathbb{N}
on excess expectations capturing the idea of behaving similarly to a supermartingale asymptotically without requiring
L_{1}
convergence.

Theorem

4.6
establishes that calibrated processes with the ASP are
r
-asymptotic e-processes for any sequence
r
satisfying an explicit condition that
formalizes the intuition of truncating the series
Equation

2
.
This provides a simple and practical sufficient criterion for verifying that a process is an asymptotic e-process, and yields an explicit method for constructing the corresponding sequence
r
.
We leverage this in
Section

6.2
to show that the cumulative product construction yields an asymptotic e-process.

Section

4.2
shows that not all asymptotic e-processes satisfy the ASP, mirroring the fact that not all e-processes are supermartingales.
We construct such examples in
Section

6.3
by adapting the time-mixture construction of
13
.

Theorem

5.1
is an asymptotic version of Ville’s inequality for asymptotic e-processes. This result ties asymptotic e-processes directly to asymptotic hypothesis testing and establishes
r
as the time horizon up to which the process can be monitored with asymptotic guarantees.

In
Section

6
we present various simple ways to construct asymptotic e-processes, supporting our claims on practical constructability.
We focus in particular on the cumulative product already introduced, as well as an analogue of the time-mixture construction for e-processes
(
13
, Section 7.9)
, thereby exhibiting asymptotic e-processes that do not have the ASP.
The cumulative product is illustrated on simple numerical experiments in
Section

7
.
We also examine the calibration
(
13
, Section 2.3)
of the anytime p-values introduced in
22
.
We find that this yields an
r
-asymptotic e-process with
r\equiv\infty
under a slight strengthening of the definition of anytime p-values.
The necessity for the strengthening originates from the fact that we define asymptotic e-processes by relying on the strongest notion of asymptotic e-values exposed in
7
.
This completely characterizes the effect of calibration, mirroring the results available in the non-asymptotic case and paving the way for fruitful further investigations based on the weaker notions of asymptotic e-variables.

We conclude this section by clarifying the relations of the present work to the previous studies of
7
and
22
.
Both are central to our contributions.
In particular, both introduce indexing by the quality of approximations, leading to our central object being a bi-indexed process, and both provide a clear framework for handling distribution-uniform guarantees.
This uniformity is essential for ensuring that the sequence
r
does not depend on the choice of distribution
P
in the null.
Furthermore,
7
provide the foundational definitions on which we build asymptotic e-processes, just as e-processes are defined in terms of e-values.
Nevertheless, an idea central to us and not explored in those prior studies is that time uniformity need not be imposed before the asymptotic regime is attained.
It is precisely this insight that enables constructing asymptotic e-processes under relatively weak assumptions while retaining strong guarantees in the limit.

\mathbb{R}\cup\{-\infty,\infty\}

\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}\cup\{\infty\}

Definition 2.3

(Stopping time)
.

Let

\{\tau\leq n\}:=\{\omega\in\Omega\mid\tau(\omega)\leq n\}\in\mathcal{F}_{n},

For all

\mathcal{T}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})

-stopping times

such that

\forall P\in\mathcal{P},~P[\tau\leq\rho]=1,

with the convention that
\mathcal{T}(\infty,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
is the set of all
\mathcal{F}
-stopping times (possibly taking the value
\infty
).
We also introduce
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
the subset of
\mathcal{T}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
consisting of finite stopping times.

It follows trivially that
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
and
\mathcal{T}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
only differ when
\rho=\infty
.
We often omit the filtration
\mathcal{F}
in the terminology of stopping times, as it is always clear from context.
Finally, we emphasize that our results are unchanged when one defines
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
as the subset of
\mathcal{T}(\rho,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
consisting of stopping times that are only
P
-a.s.finite for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
(as opposed to finite pointwise in
\Omega
); we choose to state all results for finite stopping times only, the
P
-a.s.finite case being entirely analogous.

Definition 2.4

(e-process)
.

An
e-process
(for
\mathcal{P}
,
with respect to a filtration

\mathcal{F}
)
is a nonnegative process
E=(E_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}

adapted to
\mathcal{F}
such that, for any
\mathcal{F}
-stopping time
\tau
,
E_{\tau}
is an e-variable for
\mathcal{P}
; that is,
\forall P\in\mathcal{P},\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{\tau}]\leq 1
.

As mentioned, the definition is unaffected by whether the stopping times are enforced to be finite or not
(
12
, Lemma 6)
.

Definition 2.5

(Supermartingale)
.

A
supermartingale
(for
\mathcal{P}
, with respect to a filtration

\mathcal{F}
)
is a process
S=(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
adapted to
\mathcal{F}
such that, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
S_{n}
is
P
-integrable and

\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]\leq S_{n},\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}}

It is said to be a
test supermartingale
(for
\mathcal{P}
, with respect to a filtration
\mathcal{F}
) if, additionally, it is nonnegative and satisfies
\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{0}]\leq 1
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
.

A test supermartingale
S
is always an e-process.
This follows from the optional sampling theorem for nonnegative supermartingales
(see e.g.
9
, Theorem 10.11)
.
Finally, we recall the notion of asymptotic e-variables as introduced in
7
.
An asymptotic e-variable is a nonnegative process whose index
m\in\mathbb{N}
represents the quality of approximation of a true e-variable, in some sense.
There are many different strengths in which this approximation can occur; we focus on uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variables as they give the definition easiest to handle for the rest of our investigations.

Definition 2.6

(Asymptotic e-variable,
7
)
.

A nonnegative process
e=(e_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is said to be a
strongly asymptotic e-variable for
\mathcal{P}
if

\forall P\in\mathcal{P},~\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m}]\leq 1.

(4)

Furthermore, it is said to be a
uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variable for
\mathcal{P}
if

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m}]\leq 1.

(5)

We emphasize that, in the original work of
7
, they are called “sequences of asymptotic e-variables”.
We instead favor the terminology “asymptotic e-variable” for the full sequence.

We base our definition of asymptotic e-process on uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variables.
A possible extension of our work involves taking as a basis for asymptotic e-processes the weaker notions of asymptotic e-variables introduced in
7
such as uniformly asymptotic e-variable; we leave this for future investigations.

Convergence of bi-indexed processes in
L_{1}
.

We briefly address the convergence of a bi-indexed real process
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
to another real process
F=(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The convergence we require is pointwise in
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and should occur in
L_{1}
, uniformly over
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
We formalize it in the following definitions to facilitate the statement of later results.

Definition 2.7
.

Let
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
and
F=(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be nonnegative processes.
We say that
E
converges to
F
in
L_{1}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
(along the first index)
if the following conditions hold:

(i)

for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
E_{m,n}
and
F_{n}
are
P
-integrable;

(ii)

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
E_{m,n}\to F_{n}
as
m\to\infty
, where convergence is in
L_{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{A},P)
uniformly in
P\in\mathcal{P}
; that is,

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\lvert E_{m,n}-F_{n}\rvert\right]=0.

Finally, some of our results prompt us to take sums and differences between processes taking values in the extended real line.
This is problematic when they take the value
\pm\infty
, as this leads to expressions of the form
\infty-\infty
, which are undefined.
We address this preemptively by saying that such sums are only taken in the context of processes that converge in
L_{1}
in the sense of
Definition

2.7
.
In particular, the variables involved are integrable, and thus a.s.finite, which enables understanding these sums a.s.with respect to the measure at hand.
Such expressions should then always be understood in this a.s.sense.

We also point out that the integrability condition
Definition

2.7
(i)
may be imposed only for all
m\geq m_{0}
, with
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
independent of
P\in\mathcal{P}
, without affecting our results involving such convergence in
L_{1}
.
Nevertheless, this comes with a notational burden since the difference between processes is then only unambiguously defined for
m\geq m_{0}
as per the remark right above.
For this reason, we stick to the simpler condition in
Definition

2.7
(i)
.

3
Asymptotic e-processes

We now formally define the concept of an asymptotic
e-process.
To that end, we overload the symbols
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}
introduced in
Definition

2.3
to accept as their first two arguments sequences
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
and filtration sequences
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
, and define
\mathcal{T}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
(resp.
\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
) as the set of sequences
\tau=(\tau_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,
\tau_{m}\in\mathcal{T}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})
(resp.
\tau_{m}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})
).
As announced, we first define asymptotic e-processes using
\mathcal{T}
.

Definition 3.1

(Uniformly strongly asymptotic e-process)
.

Let
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to a filtration sequence
\mathcal{F}
and
r
be a sequence of extended integers.
We say that
E
is a
uniformly strongly
r
-asymptotic e-process
(for
\mathcal{P}
and for
\mathcal{F}
) if for all
\tau=(\tau_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{T}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
, the process
(E_{m,\tau_{m}})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variable for
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
; that is,

\forall\tau=(\tau_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{T}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P}),~\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]\leq 1.

We begin with some remarks on terminology.
First, the qualifier “uniformly strongly” directly refers to the fact that the definition leverages uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variables.
In this work, we only consider uniformly strongly asymptotic e-processes, and leave the qualifier implicit for conciseness.
Second, we often say in the discussion “asymptotic e-process”, omitting the sequence
r
if it is not relevant for the argument at hand.

Crucially, the uniformity required in
Definition

3.1
is over
\mathcal{P}
: the sequence
(\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}])_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
should
not exceed
1
in limit superior at a speed that may depend on the sequence
\tau
, but is uniform in
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
This mirrors the condition imposed in the nonasymptotic case, which requires the quantity to bounded by
1
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
We emphasize that the uniformity is
not
over the
n
index. This is precisely
the technical content of
Definition

3.1
.
Rather, the maximal horizon over the second indexing dimension of
E
involved in
\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]
is given by
r_{m}
.
Any (
\mathcal{F}
-predictable) decision rule of the statistician within that horizon achieves expectation at most
1
, asymptotically.
A consequence of the fact that
Definition

3.1
involves sequences of stopping times instead of a fixed one for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
is the following useful characterization.

Proposition 3.2
.

Let
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to a filtration sequence
\mathcal{F}
and
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of extended integers.
Then,
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process if, and only if,

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau}]\leq 1.

Proof.

The converse implication is clear, and the direct implication follows from the fact that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
\tau_{m}\in\mathcal{T}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})
such that

\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]+\frac{1}{m+1}\geq\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau}].

Taking the limit superior over
m
concludes the proof.
∎

An immediate consequence is that, if
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process and
s
is a sequence such that
s_{m}\leq r_{m}
, then
E
is also an
s
-asymptotic e-process.
It is thus of particular interest that
r
becomes large; preferably, even that
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.
The following result gives a condition under which this can be assumed.

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to a filtration sequence
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

For any bounded integer sequence
r
,
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

(ii)

There exists an integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
and
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
.

Proof.

The implication
(ii)

\Rightarrow

(i)

follows immediately from
Proposition

3.2
.
We focus on the
implication
(i)

\Rightarrow

(ii)
.
For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, define

x_{m,n}=\sup_{\tau\in\mathcal{T}(n,\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau}].

It follows from
(i)
and the converse implication of
Proposition

3.2
that
\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{m,n}\leq 1
for all fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Consequently,
Lemma

A.1
guarantees the existence of an
integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that

r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
and

\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{m,r_{m}}=\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{\tau_{m}\in\mathcal{T}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m},\mathcal{P})}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]\leq 1.

The result follows from the direct implication of
Proposition

3.2
.
∎

3.1
Characterizations with finite stopping times or nonnegative partial supermartingales

Two important theoretical results in the classical theory of e-processes are that their definition is stated equivalently by allowing or not that stopping times take the value

A first consequence is that, by the monotone convergence theorem and the tower property,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[L^{P}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n}}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{\sigma\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r_{m},\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet},\mathcal{P})}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\sigma}].

It follows from
Lemma

3.5
that this last bound is finite, since
m\geq m_{0}
, and thus
L_{m,n}^{P}
is indeed integrable, showing
(iii)b
.
Furthermore, this inequality holds for all
m\geq m_{0}
,
n\in\llbracket r_{m}\rrbracket
, and
P\in\mathcal{P}
; in particular, it holds with
n=0
for all
m\geq m_{0}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
Taking the supremum over
P\in\mathcal{P}
and the limit superior in
m
and leveraging
Lemma

3.5
together with the assumption
(ii)
shows that
(iii)d
holds.
A second consequence follows in the case
n+1\leq r_{m}
; then,
L_{m,n+1}^{P}
is
P
-integrable, and the monotone convergence theorem for conditional expectations and the tower property thus yield

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[L^{P}_{m,n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n+1}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n+1}]\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n+1}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n+1}]\,\middle|\,\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\lim_{k\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{k,n+1}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle\leq L^{P}_{m,n},\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}},

where the last step relies on the fact that
L^{P}_{m,n}
is defined via the essential supremum (since
n<n+1\leq r_{m}
) and that
\tau_{k,n+1}\in\mathcal{S}_{m,n+1}\subset\mathcal{S}_{m,n}
for all
k\in\mathbb{N}
.
This holds for all
m\geq m_{0}
and
n\in\llbracket r_{m}-1\rrbracket
, showing that
(iii)c
holds and concluding the proof.
∎

Remark 3.6

(
\infty
-asymptotic e-processes)
.

An interesting consequence of
Theorem

3.4
is that
r
-asymptotic e-processes with
r\equiv\infty
are precisely those processes that are upper-bounded by nonnegative supermartingales that satisfy the asymptotic calibration condition
Theorem

3.4
(iii)d
.
In other words, leveraging
12
, such processes

can be re-normalized to be true e-processes for all

3.2
Limiting behavior of asymptotic e-processes

We conclude this section by showing that asymptotic e-processes generalize the idea of “converging to an e-process”, in the following sense:
for bi-indexed processes that converge in
L_{1}
along the first index, being an
r
-asymptotic e-process with
r\to\infty
is equivalent to the limit being an e-process.
This further justifies the relevance of allowing for general sequences
r
other than
r\equiv\infty
in
Definition

3.1
.

Theorem 3.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filtration array,
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to
\mathcal{F}
, and
F=(F_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a process adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}
.
Assume that
E
converges to
F
in
L_{1}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
along the first index.
The following statements are equivalent:

(i)

there exists an extended integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
such that
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{P}
;

(ii)

F
is an e-process for
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}
and
\mathcal{P}
.

If one of these statements holds (and thus, both hold), then
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for any extended integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}\cup\{\infty\}
that satisfies

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert E_{m,n}-S_{n}\rvert]=0,

(6)

and there exists at least one such sequence with
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.

Proof.

We begin with the direct implication, and assume the existence of an extended integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
with
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
such that
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for
\mathcal{F}
and
\mathcal{P}
.
First, we can assume without loss of generality that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(r_{m}+1)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sup_{n\leq r_{m}}\lvert E_{m,n}-F_{n}\rvert\right]=0.

(7)

Indeed, define for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
the quantity

x_{m,n}=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(n+1)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sup_{k\leq n}\lvert E_{m,k}-F_{k}\rvert\right].

It holds that

\displaystyle x_{m,n}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(n+1)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sum_{k=0}^{n}\lvert E_{m,k}-F_{k}\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{k=0}^{n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}(n+1)\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\lvert E_{m,k}-F_{k}\rvert\right]=:y_{m,n}.

By assumption,
y_{m,n}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for all fixed
n\in\mathbb{N}
(as the sum of finitely many vanishing terms).
As a result, there exists by
Lemma

A.1
an integer sequence
s=(s_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
y_{m,s_{m}}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
and
s_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.
The above bound then guarantees
x_{m,s_{m}}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
Furthermore, defining
t_{m}=r_{m}\wedge s_{m}
, one straightforwardly verifies that
x_{m,t_{m}}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, as
x_{m,\bullet}
is nondecreasing, and this last condition is precisely
Equation

7
where
r
is formally replaced by
t
.
Crucially,
t\leq r
, and thus
E
is a
t
-asymptotic e-process.
Also,
t_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.
In other words, the sequence
t
satisfies the assumption, and is such that
Equation

7
is satisfied, which guarantees that the condition can indeed be assumed without loss of generality.

Let now
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(\infty,\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet},\mathcal{P})
.
We construct a sequence
\theta=(\theta_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
that converges to
\tau
, a.s.For this, define for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}

\displaystyle Z_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}],

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\bar{\theta}_{m}

\displaystyle=\inf\left\{k\in\mathbb{N}\mid Z_{m,k}>\frac{1}{2}\right\}.

The specific choice of
1/2
for the threshold is unimportant here; the construction works with any threshold in
(0,1)
.
It is clear that
\bar{\theta}_{m}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet}
-stopping time for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
.
Furthermore, by Lévy’s upward theorem
(
23
, Theorem 14.2)
, it follows that for all
n\in\mathbb{N}

\lim_{m\to\infty}Z_{m,n}=\lim_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]=\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}},\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}}

As a result, we have a set
A\in\mathcal{A}
with
P[A]=1
and such that the above convergence holds pointwise on
A
.
Let
n_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
, and take
\omega\in A\cap\{\tau=n_{0}\}
.
For any
n\geq n_{0}
, it holds that
Z_{m,n}(\omega)\to\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}}(\omega)=1
as
m\to\infty
.
In particular,
Z_{m,n}>1/2
for
m
sufficiently large, and thus
\bar{\theta}_{m}(\omega)\leq n
eventually.
Furthermore, for all
n<n_{0}
, it holds that
Z_{m,n}(\omega)\to\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\leq n\}}(\omega)=0
as
m\to\infty
, and thus
Z_{m,n}(\omega)<1/2
for
m
sufficiently large, showing
\bar{\theta}_{m}(\omega)>n
eventually.
Combining these two results shows that
\bar{\theta}_{m}(\omega)=n_{0}
for
m
sufficiently large.
Summarizing, we have shown that for all
\omega\in A
,
\bar{\theta}_{m}(\omega)\to\tau(\omega)
as
m\to\infty
, which implies in turn that
\bar{\theta}_{m}\to\tau
as
m\to\infty
,
P
-a.s.We now define
\theta_{m}=\bar{\theta}_{m}\wedge r_{m}
.
Since
r_{m}\to\infty
, one easily concludes that
\theta_{m}\to\tau
as
m\to\infty
,
P
-a.s.Furthermore,
\theta\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
by construction.

Now, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
, and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}-E_{m,\theta_{m}}]+\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\theta_{m}}].

We bound the first term as

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}-E_{m,\theta_{m}}]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert F_{\theta_{m}}-E_{m,\theta_{m}}\rvert]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\theta_{m}=n\}}\lvert F_{n}-E_{m,n}\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sup_{n\leq r_{m}}\lvert F_{n}-E_{m,n}\rvert\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{m}=n\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq(r_{m}+1)\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sup_{n\leq r_{m}}\lvert F_{n}-E_{m,n}\rvert\right].

By
Equation

7
, this quantity vanishes as
m\to\infty
.
Consequently, leveraging the assumption on
E
and the fact that
\theta\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
,

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}]\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}-E_{m,\theta_{m}}]+\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\theta_{m}}]\leq 1.

Next, it follows from
\theta_{m}\to\tau
as
m\to\infty
,
P
-a.s., that
F_{\theta_{m}}\to F_{\tau}
as
m\to\infty
,
P
-a.s.(since
\tau
is finite), and thus by Fatou’s lemma

\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\tau}]\leq\liminf_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}]\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[F_{\theta_{m}}]\leq 1.

We can now apply
12
to show that the property extends to arbitrary stopping times (not necessarily a.s.finite), showing that
F
is indeed an e-process.

Conversely, assume that
F
is an e-process.
For all extended integer sequences
r
, all
P\in\mathcal{P}
, all
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
, and all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, we have

\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}-S_{\tau_{m}}]+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{\tau_{m}}]\leq 1+\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}-S_{\tau_{m}}].

As a result, it suffices to choose

such that
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}-S_{\tau_{m}}]=0
for

to be an
r
-asymptotic e-process, implicitly leveraging

3.4
to conclude based on finite stopping times.
Yet, for any extended integer sequence
r
,

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}-S_{\tau_{m}}]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert E_{m,\tau_{m}}-S_{\tau_{m}}\rvert]

\displaystyle=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\left\lvert\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}}(E_{m,n}-S_{n})\cdot\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau_{m}=n\}}\right\rvert\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert E_{m,n}-S_{n}\rvert],

where the last step leverages the triangle inequality for the various operations involved.
This shows that any extended integer sequence satisfying the condition of the theorem makes
E
an
r
-asymptotic e-process.
Furthermore, we can show that such a diverging integer sequence necessarily exists by applying
Lemma

A.1
to the sequence
x_{m,n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert E_{m,n}-S_{n}\rvert]
,
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, as it satisfies
x_{m,n}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for all fixed

by assumption, concluding the proof.
∎

4
Asymptotic supermartingales

We now proceed to generalize the
theory of test supermartingales in an asymptotic
context and relate it to our definition of
asymptotic e-processes from the previous section.
The general goal is to find sufficient criteria for e-processes that can be verified from simple assumptions, similarly to the fact that, in the nonasymptotic case, test supermartingales are e-processes.
In particular, we are after conditions that are easier to verify than
Theorem

3.4
(iii)
.

Definition 4.1

(Asymptotic supermartingale in
L_{1}
)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a filtration array and
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to
\mathcal{F}
.
We say that
E
is an
asymptotic supermartingale (ASM)
(in
L_{1}
, for
\mathcal{F}
, uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
) if there exists a nonnegative supermartingale
(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\mathcal{P}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}
such that
E
converges to
S
in
L_{1}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
.

We briefly discuss the relation between
S
,
E_{\infty,\bullet}
, and the question of whether this last process is a supermartingale.
To simplify, we reason for a fixed
P\in\mathcal{P}
in the discussion.

We highlight that the convergence required in
Definition

4.1
is in
L_{1}
, and not a.s., and there is no relation in general between
S
and
E_{\infty,\bullet}
, which we defined as

E_{\infty,n}(\omega)=\limsup_{m\to\infty}E_{m,n}(\omega),

pointwise for
\omega\in\Omega
.
We may have in general
E_{\infty,n}\neq S_{n}
on events of positive measure.
The additional assumption that
E_{\infty,n}
is an actual limit suffices to enforce
E_{\infty,n}=S_{n}
a.s., however.
Indeed, this follows from the fact that both a.s.and
L_{1}
convergences imply convergence in probability to their respective limits, and thus the limits must coincide a.s.In what follows, we do not make the assumption that
E_{\infty,n}
is a true
a.s.limit, and thus stick to the distinct notation

S
to denote the limit in
L_{1}
.

Conversely, it does not suffice that
E_{\infty,\bullet}
is a true limit and is a supermartingale for
E
to be anASMwith
S=E_{\infty,\bullet}
, as the convergence may fail in
L_{1}
.
In fact, convergence in
L_{1}
of
E_{\bullet,n}
,
n\in\mathbb{N}
fixed, is equivalent to
uniform integrability
of this family
(
4
, Theorem 4.6.3)
.
In other words, convergence in
L_{1}
is a strong condition that may be tedious to verify.
Fortunately, it is also not necessary for our later results; all that is required is that
E
behaves “like” a supermartingale as
m\to\infty
.
This is handled in the next definition.

Definition 4.2

(Asymptotic supermartingale property)
.

Let
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a filtration sequence and
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process adapted to
\mathcal{F}
.
For all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, define

\delta_{m,n}=\begin{cases}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-E_{m,n},&\text{if $E_{m,n+1}$ is $P$-integrable},\\
\infty,&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where the dependency of
\delta_{m,n}
on
P
is omitted to
simplify notation.
We say that
E
has the
asymptotic supermartingale property (ASP)
(in
L_{1}
, for
\mathcal{F}
, uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
) if

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},~\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\delta_{m,n}^{+}]=0.

In the discussion, we omit for conciseness the qualifiers “in
L_{1}
”, “for
\mathcal{F}
”, and “uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
” when talking about ASMsor the ASP.
Naturally, ASMshave the ASP.

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
E=(E_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process and
\mathcal{F}=(\mathcal{F}_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a filtration array.
Assume that
E
is anASMin
L_{1}
for
\mathcal{F}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
.
Then,
E
has the ASPin
L_{1}
for
\mathcal{F}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
.

Proof.

Let
S:=(S_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a supermartingale for
\mathcal{P}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}
such that
E
converges to
S
in
L_{1}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
.
Fix
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
We decompose
\delta_{m,n}
as follows, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, recalling that
E_{m,n+1}
is
P
-integrable by
Definition

2.7
:

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}-S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-S_{n}

\displaystyle+S_{n}-E_{m,n},\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}}

Define each of these terms as
A_{m}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]
,
B_{m}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}-S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]
,
C_{m}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-S_{n}
, and
D_{m}=S_{n}-E_{m,n}
.
We handle
B_{m}
immediately as follows:

\displaystyle B_{m}

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}-S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]~\middle|~\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]-S_{n}~\middle|~\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[0\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle\leq 0,\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}}

The first three steps come respectively from the facts that
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}\subset\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}
, that
S_{n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}
-measurable, and that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]-S_{n}\leq 0
,
P
-a.s., since
S
is an
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}
-supermartingale.
As a result,
\delta_{m,n}\leq A_{m}+C_{m}+D_{m}
,
P
-a.s., and thus

\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\delta_{m,n}^{+}]\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert A_{m}\rVert_{P}+\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert C_{m}\rVert_{P}+\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert D_{m}\rVert_{P}.

We handle each term separately.
First, it follows from the tower property and the contraction property of conditional expectations that, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
,

\displaystyle\lVert A_{m}\rVert_{P}=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\left\lvert\,\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]\right\rvert\right]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\rvert],

which goes to
0
uniformly in
P\in\mathcal{P}
as
m\to\infty
by
Definition

2.7
.
The term
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert D_{m}\rVert_{P}
also vanishes asymptotically for the same reason.
For the term
C_{m}
, we have, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert C_{m}\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-S_{n}\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle\quad+\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]\rVert_{P}

\displaystyle\leq 2\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert S_{n}-E_{m,n}\rVert_{P}+\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]\rVert_{P}.

Indeed, the above relations follow successively from the definition of
C_{m}
, the
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}
-measurability of
S_{n}
, the triangle inequality for
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\lVert\cdot\rVert_{P}
, and the contraction property of conditional expectations on
L_{1}(\Omega,\mathcal{A},P)
for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
.
Now, crucially,
E_{m,n}
is
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}
-measurable, and thus
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}
-measurable as well. Therefore,
\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]=E_{m,n}
,
P
-a.s., and the second term is
0

4.2
Asymptotic e-processes without the ASP

The ASP, in combination with calibration, is only a sufficient condition for asymptotic e-processes;
it is not necessary.
This mirrors the fact that, in the nonasymptotic regime, there are e-processes that are not supermartingales.
This observation gives a general recipe to construct asymptotic e-processes that do not have the ASP: it suffices that they converge in
L_{1}
to an e-process that is not a supermartingale.
This is based on the following result, which can be seen as a converse result to
Theorem

4.3
and guarantees that the limit in
L_{1}
of a process that has the ASPmust be a supermartingale when it exists.

Theorem 4.7
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filtration array,

\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}

has the ASPin
L_{1}
for
\mathcal{F}

\mathcal{P}

\mathcal{P}
with respect to
\mathcal{F}_{\infty,\bullet}

Proof.

Let

and

\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]\leq S_{n}
,

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}=~

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]-S_{n}

Introducing

\displaystyle\delta_{m,n}-\eta_{n}=~

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n+1}-S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]

\displaystyle+\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]-\mathbb{E}_{P}[S_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{\infty,n}]

.

5
Asymptotic Ville’s inequality

We now present an essential result of this work that ties asymptotic e-processes and SAVIto asymptotic testing:
an asymptotic analogue of Ville’s inequality for asymptotic e-processes.
It reflects the core idea of our definition of an
r
-asymptotic e-process by providing an asymptotic bound for the excursion probabilities of
E_{m,\bullet}
.
Crucially, the bound only concerns a horizon up to
r_{m}
, establishing

as the time horizon that evolves with the approximation index

and until which

can be monitored with asymptotic guarantees.
Intuitively, this ties

to how close

is to being a true e-process for testing purposes.

Theorem 5.1
.

Let
\mathcal{F}
be a filtration sequence and

and

is a sequence of extended integers.
Then, it holds for all

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P\left[\sup_{n\in\llbracket r_{m}\rrbracket}E_{m,n}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right]\leq\alpha.

Proof.

For all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
, define

\tau_{m}:=\inf\left\{n\in\llbracket r_{m}\rrbracket~\middle|~E_{m,n}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\},

with the convention that
\tau_{m}=r_{m}
if the set is empty.
Since
(E_{m,n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
is adapted to
\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet}
,
\tau_{m}
is an
\mathcal{F}_{m,\bullet}
-stopping time.
Furthermore, it is certainly less than
r_{m}
,
P
-a.s.for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
, and thus
\tau\in\mathcal{T}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
.
By assumption on
E
, it follows that
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau_{m}}]\leq 1
.
Yet, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\left\{\sup_{n\in\llbracket r_{m}\rrbracket}E_{m,n}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\}=\left\{E_{m,\tau_{m}}\geq\frac{1}{\alpha}\right\},

by definition of
\tau_{m}
.
By Markov’s inequality applied to

, it follows that

and the limit superior over

In this section, we support our initial claim that asymptotic e-processes can be easily constructed.

We also discuss how the time-mixture construction of

Finally, we show how asymptotic e-processes generalize the anytime p-values introduced in

In the discussion in

6.1

such that

is an asymptotic e-variable for

.
We also define

Each section may impose additional assumptions, and formal statements redefine all symbols as appropriate.

6.1
Counterexample: diagonal cumulative product

The first example we investigate is the one that arises naturally when trying the usual construction of e-processes from sequential e-variables, which are e-variables
(\bar{e}_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
such that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[\bar{e}_{n+1}\mid\bar{e}_{0},\dots,\bar{e}_{n}]\leq 1

and

(
13

.

Given such a sequence, the cumulative product

\bar{E}_{n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}\bar{e}_{i}

is a test supermartingale and, thus, is an e-process.

In the case where only the asymptotic e-variables
e
are available, there are many options to account for the second indexing dimension.
We consider first the following option:

E_{m,n}=\prod_{i=0}^{m\vee n}e_{i,i}.

For an example illustrating the relevance of this choice, one may consider the sequence
e
as resulting from an underlying data set being revealed sequentially with
i+1
currently observed samples.
The most recently revealed sample is then used to compute the value
e_{i,i}
, which involves an approximate model trained on the other samples.
This is for instance one of the examples in
2
, though in a context without e-processes.

In general, the process
E
is not an asymptotic e-process without strong assumptions on the variables
e
.
For instance, consider the case where for all
i\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{i+1,i+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{i,i}]\geq 1+d_{i}
, with

a non-random sequence (possibly vanishing asymptotically).
Then, for all

,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{0,0}]\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{m\vee n-1}(1+d_{m})\geq\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{0,0}]\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{m-1}(1+d_{m}).

Assuming
\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{0,0}]=1
, this shows that
\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]
is certainly not less than
1
in limit superior, from which it follows that
E
is not an asymptotic e-process for any sequence

.

An intuitive explanation for this negative result is the observation that the expectation
\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]
is heavily influenced by the properties of variables

for all values of

m
.
These variables may be poor approximations of true e-variables and their (conditional) expectations may exceed

This hints that the issue lies in the cumulation of the approximation error

d_{m}
over the first index,
and that a possible fix is to allow a form of forgetting along that index.
There are many options for such forgetting; the next section explores a simple one.

6.2
Cumulative product

We consider the process given by

is that
\delta_{m,n}^{+}\to 0
in
L_{1}
as
m\to\infty
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
This can be established under uniform boundedness of the conditional expectations.
Specifically, assume that for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, there exists
d_{m}^{P}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
such that
\eta_{m,n}\leq d_{m}^{P}
,
P
-a.s.and for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[\delta_{m,n}^{+}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\cdot\eta_{m,n}^{+}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}\cdot d_{m}^{P}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]d_{m}^{P}.

Furthermore, for all
n\geq 1
,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[E_{m,n-1}\cdot e_{m,n}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n-1}\cdot e_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n-1}]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n-1}\cdot\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m,n}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n-1}]]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n-1}\cdot(1+\eta_{m,n})]

\displaystyle\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n-1}]\cdot(1+d_{m}^{P}),

and thus by induction

\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,n}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,0}]\cdot(1+d_{m}^{P})^{n},\quad\forall n\in\mathbb{N}.

Putting it all together, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[\delta_{m,n}^{+}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,0}]\cdot d_{m}^{P}\cdot(1+d_{m}^{P})^{n}.

Therefore, it suffices that
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{0,m}]
is bounded and that
d_{m}:=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}d_{m}^{P}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for
E
to have the ASP.
In particular, this implies that
(e_{m,n})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is an asymptotic e-variable for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and imposes the stronger condition that the conditional expectation of
e_{m,n+1}
conditionally on
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}
should go to
1
uniformly over
\mathcal{P}
, in addition to the expectation itself.
We now find sequences
r
that make it an
r
-asymptotic e-process by leveraging
Theorem

4.6
.
For any sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
and
m\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}-1}\delta_{m,n}^{+}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{0,m}]\cdot\sum_{n=0}^{r_{m}-1}d_{m}\cdot(1+d_{m})^{n}

\displaystyle=\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{0,m}]\cdot\left[(1+d_{m})^{r_{m}}-1\right]

It follows that a sufficient condition for
E
to be an
r
-asymptotic e-process is that
(1+d_{m})^{r_{m}}\to 1
as
m\to\infty
.
Simple manipulations show that this is equivalent to
r_{m}\cdot d_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.
In other words,
r
needs to increase slowly enough relatively to
d
.
We summarize this discussion in the following result.

Theorem 6.1
.

Let
(e_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a sequence of asymptotic e-variables for
\mathcal{P}
; that is,
e_{\bullet,n}
is an asymptotic e-variable for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
\mathcal{F}_{m,n}=\sigma(\{e_{m,i}\mid i\in\llbracket n\rrbracket\})
and

\quad E_{m,n}=\prod_{i=0}^{n}e_{m,i}.

Assume that, for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists
d_{m}\in\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
with

\forall P\in\mathcal{P},\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m,n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{m,n}]\leq 1+d_{m},\quad P\text{-{{}}{a.s.}}

If
d_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
, then
E
has the ASPfor
\mathcal{F}
uniformly in
\mathcal{P}
.
In this case,
E
is an
r
-asymptotic e-process for
\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{F}
for any sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}
such that
r_{m}d_{m}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
.

Discussion on other forms of forgetting.

The example presented in this section can be seen as an “extreme” form of forgetting: the value of the candidate e-process at index
(m,n)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}
only involves the variables
(e_{m,i})_{i\in\mathbb{N}}
.
One may consider milder options, such as

\displaystyle E_{m+1,n}=E_{m,n}^{\lambda_{m}}\cdot\prod_{i=0}^{n}e_{m,i},\quad\text{or}\quad E_{m+1,n}=C_{m}\left[\lambda_{m}E_{m,n}+\prod_{i=0}^{n}e_{m,i}\right],

where
\lambda=(\lambda_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset[0,1)
is a sequence of forgetting parameters, and
C=(C_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is a sequence of normalization constants that depends only on
\lambda
(ensuring that
E_{m,\bullet}
is indeed an e-process for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
if
e
consists of mutually independent, exact e-variables).
We expect that introducing such a recurrence leads to more constraining requirements on
e
for such processes to have the ASPcompared to the condition that
d_{m}\to 0
in
Theorem

6.1
; indeed, the conditions should probably impose that
d
decays faster than a speed parameterized by
\lambda
.
In turn, such asymptotic e-processes may have desirable properties in the specific application at hand (such as power in statistical testing, or improved stability upon increasing the index
m
).
In this work, we limit ourselves to illustrating simple ways of constructing asymptotic e-processes, and leave such explorations for future work.

6.3
Weighted average

Assume that the collection
e
of asymptotic
e-variables satisfies, for all
P\in\mathcal{P}
and
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m,n}]=1+\varepsilon_{m,n}^{P}
, with
\varepsilon_{m,n}^{P}>0
and

\varepsilon^{P}_{m,n}\to 0
as
m\to\infty
for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
For a collection of deterministic
nonnegative weights
(w_{m,n})_{m,n\in\mathbb{N}}

such that
\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}w_{m,i}\leq 1,

we consider the weighted average process
(also called
time mixture process
,

13
) given by

E_{m,n}:=\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{m,i}\,e_{i,m}.

For every integer sequence
r=(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
and stopping time
\tau\in\mathcal{T}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}_{P}[E_{m,\tau}]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\sum_{t=0}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau=t\}}\sum_{i=0}^{t}w_{m,i}e_{m,i}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}w_{m,i}e_{m,i}\sum_{t=i}^{\infty}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau=t\}}\right]

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}_{P}\!\left[\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}w_{m,i}e_{m,i}\mathbf{1}_{\{\tau\geq i\}}\right]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{r_{m}}w_{m,i}\,\mathbb{E}_{P}[e_{m,i}]

\displaystyle\leq\sum_{i=0}^{r_{m}}w_{m,i}(1+\varepsilon_{m,i}^{P})

\displaystyle\leq 1+\sum_{i=0}^{r_{m}}w_{m,i}\varepsilon_{m,i}^{P}.

Hence,
this process is an
r
-asymptotic e-process
whenever

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sum_{i=0}^{r_{m}}w_{m,i}\varepsilon^{P}_{m,i}=0.

Additionally we may assume that, for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
,
each asymptotic e-variable
(e_{m,n})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
converges in
L_{1}
to an e-variable
e_{n}
uniformly over
\mathcal{P}
,
in the sense that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\mathbb{E}_{P}[\lvert e_{m,n}-e_{n}\rvert]=0.

Also assume that
w
converges to a probability mass function on
\mathbb{N}
, in the sense that
w_{n}=\lim_{m\to\infty}w_{m,n}
exists for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, and
\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}w_{n}=1
.
Then, one can define the limit process

S_{n}=\sum_{i=0}^{n}w_{i}e_{i},\forall n\in\mathbb{N},

which is an e-process by the same reasoning as above

(see also
13
, Proposition 7.23)
.
Yet, it is also increasing, and is thus not a supermartingale without additional assumptions.
In this case, this makes
E
an asymptotic e-process that does not have the ASP, as per the discussion in
Section

4.2
.

6.4
Calibrating anytime p-values into asymptotic e-processes

An alternative approach to the validity of asymptotic sequential tests was recently proposed in
22
.
In short, the authors introduce
\mathcal{P}
-uniform anytime p-values, which are triangular arrays of variables that satisfy the condition defining anytime-valid p-values over one index,
asymptotically in the other index.
In this section, we show how anytime p-values can be calibrated into asymptotic e-processes by leveraging ideas from
7
.
This requires recalling some definitions.

Remark 6.2

(A note regarding terminology)
.

The definitions in this section rely on the work by
7
and
22
, resulting in terminology that is sometimes inconsistent.
We adhere to the terminology used in both papers,
hence we adopt the terms

(uniformly, strongly) asymptotic p-variables
, as well as
\mathcal{P}
-uniform anytime p-values
, which we abbreviate as anytime p-values.
However, we outline below
that such anytime p-values are equivalently defined by relying on a corresponding notion of asymptotic p-variables, similarly as uniformly strongly
r
-asymptotic e-processes (
Definition

3.1
) rely on uniformly strongly asymptotic e-variables.
An alternative terminology for anytime p-values consistent with ours and that of
7
would be “uniformly
r
-asymptotic p-processes” (with
r\equiv\infty
).

Definition 6.3

(p-variable, p-to-e calibrator)
.

A
p
-variable for
\mathcal{P}
is a nonnegative, finite
random variable
p
such that, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
,
P[p\leq\alpha]\leq\alpha
.
A
p-to-e calibrator
is a nonincreasing function
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
satisfying
f=0
on
(1,\infty)
and such that
f(p)
is an e-variable for any p-variable
p
.

There exist many p-to-e calibrators, and they are well-studied; we refer to
13
for an extensive discussion.

We now recall the formal definition of
\mathcal{P}
-uniform anytime p-values.
We use the notation
I=\{(m,k)\in\mathbb{N}^{2}\mid k\geq m\}\subset\mathbb{N}^{2}
for indexing upper-triangular arrays.

Definition 6.4

(
\mathcal{P}
-uniform anytime p-values,
22
)
.

Let
p=(p_{m,k})_{m,n\in I}
be a stochastic process taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
.
We say that
p
is a

\mathcal{P}
-uniform anytime p-value
if

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}P[\exists k\geq m,p_{m,k}\leq\alpha]\leq\alpha.

In what follows, we simply say “anytime p-value” in the discussion and leave the qualifier “
\mathcal{P}
-uniform” implicit.
Given an anytime p-value
p
, the candidate asymptotic e-process we consider is

\forall m,n\in\mathbb{N},~E_{m,n}=f(p_{m,m+n}),

(11)

where
f
is a p-to-e calibrator.
We show later that such calibration yields an
r
-asymptotic e-process with

as long as

is bounded.
Before showing this, however, we examine, through an example, what breaks in the case where
f
is unbounded as it is highly instructive.
Consider the case where, for all
m,n\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}

.
This is allowed by

as

.
Nevertheless, since

with positive probability and is thus not integrable for any

, showing that

is not an asymptotic e-process.

The notions introduced in

enable fully addressing this difficulty.
Specifically, it pertains to the distinction between a
uniformly asymptotic p-variable
and a
uniformly strongly asymptotic p-variable
, in the terminology of the reference.
In short,

6.4
provides the former, and Proposition 3.12 in the reference shows that calibrating it yields a uniformly asymptotic e-variable.
In contrast,

We now detail this idea, and show how a slight strengthening of

We begin by formally introducing uniformly asymptotic p-variables and their strong counterpart.
Since the original definition

Theorem 6.7

(Calibration of asymptotic p-values)
.

Let
p=(p_{m,k})_{m,n\in I}
be a stochastic process taking values in
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
, let
f:[0,\infty)\to[0,\infty]
be a p-to-e calibrator, and define

\forall m,n\in\mathbb{N},~E_{m,n}=f(p_{m,m+n}),

(15)

as well as
\mathcal{F}
the filtration array generated by

(i)

\mathcal{P}

(ii)

and

Proof.

Let
\tau\in\mathcal{T}_{\mathrm{fin}}(r,\mathcal{F},\mathcal{P})

For all

\displaystyle\{p_{m,m+\tau_{m}}\leq\alpha\}

8
Conclusion

We propose a definition of asymptotic e-processes
with respect to a fixed set of probability measures
\mathcal{P}

that naturally allows for constructions based on asymptotic e-variables.
We focus on properties and characterization of those processes
that relate them to their non-asymptotic counterparts,
as well as an explicit criterion based on asymptotic supermartingales.
In the context of hypothesis testing, our
version of Ville’s inequality for asymptotic e-processes allows for
an asymptotic type I error control when the set
\mathcal{P}

specifies the null hypothesis.

Our work naturally opens up a range of related questions.
Most importantly, in order to obtain guarantees for sequential
testing with asymptotic e-processes,
their behavior under alternative hypotheses
and their corresponding type II errors need to be investigated.
Furthermore, we build our theory around the notion
of
uniformly strongly
asymptotic e-variables
and do not consider weaker notions of asymptotic e-variables
as discussed by
7
.
Finally, from an application perspective, asymptotic
SAVIis still a recent framework and remains to be explored empirically.

A
Technical results

We collect in this section technical results that are useful throughout this paper.

Rate of uniform boundedness.

The first lemma is a diagonal argument on double-indexed sequences that are bounded asymptotically along one axis.

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(x_{n,m})_{n,m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a doubly indexed family of real numbers such that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{n,m}\leq 1\text{ for every }n\in\mathbb{N}.

Then there exists a nondecreasing sequence

(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathbb{N}

with
r_{m}\to\infty
such that

\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{r_{m},m}\leq 1.

Proof.

For each
n\in\mathbb{N}
, the assumption

\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{n,m}\leq 1

means that there exists
N_{n}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

x_{n,m}\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}\text{ for every }m\geq N_{n}.

By increasing the numbers
N_{n}
recursively if necessary, we may additionally assume that the sequence
(N_{n})
is strictly increasing and
N_{n}\to\infty
.

Now define
(r_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
by setting
r_{m}=1
for
m<N_{1}
, and, for
m\geq N_{1}
, letting

r_{m}=n\qquad\text{whenever }N_{n}\leq m<N_{n+1}.

Since
(N_{n})
is strictly increasing, the sequence
(r_{m})
is well defined and nondecreasing. Moreover, because
N_{n}\to\infty
, we also have
r_{m}\to\infty
as
m\to\infty
.

With this construction, we have

x_{r_{m},m}=x_{n,m}\leq 1+\frac{1}{n}=1+\frac{1}{r_{m}}.

Since
r_{m}\to\infty
, we have
1+\frac{1}{r_{m}}\to 1
. Therefore

\limsup_{m\to\infty}x_{r_{m},m}\leq 1.

∎

Optional sampling theorem for nonnegative supermartingales.

The second result is a strengthening of the optional sampling theorem in the case of nonnegative supermartingales.
The classical result requires bounded stopping times, and is commonly extended to a.s.finite ones; see for instance
9
.
We require allowing infinite values.
It is known that the result also extends to that case under an additional uniform integrability requirement; see for instance the discussion after (6) in
12
.
A formal statement can be adapted from
8
, which involves submartingales on
\mathbb{R}^{+}
.
In the interest of simplicity, however, we now provide a self-contained statement and proof of the special case that we use in this work.

Theorem A.2
.

Let
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative
\mathcal{F}
-supermartingale on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},P)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a filtration.
Let
\tau
be an
\mathcal{F}
-stopping time, possibly infinite (meaning that
P[\tau=\infty]
may be positive).
Then,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{\tau}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{0}],

where we recall the convention
X_{\infty}:=\limsup_{n\to\infty}X_{n}
.

Proof.

First, by Doob’s supermartingale convergence theorem
(see e.g.
9
, Theorem 11.4)
, the limit superior in the definition of
X_{\infty}
is actually a true limit and convergence holds a.s.As a result, it holds a.s.that

\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{\tau\wedge n}=X_{\tau}.

Next,
\tau_{n}:=\tau\wedge n
is a bounded stopping time.
It follows from the optional sampling theorem
(
9
, Theorem 10.11)
that

\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{\tau_{n}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{0}].

We can thus conclude by Fatou’s lemma:

\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{\tau}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}\left[\lim_{n\to\infty}X_{\tau_{n}}\right]\leq\liminf_{n\to\infty}\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{\tau_{n}}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{0}].

∎

Corollary A.3
.

Let
X=(X_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
be an
\mathcal{F}
-supermartingale on
(\Omega,\mathcal{A},P)
, where
\mathcal{F}
is a filtration.
Let
\tau
be an
\mathcal{F}
-stopping time, possibly infinite.
Let
Y
be a
P
-integrable real variable such that
X_{n}\geq Y
,
P
-a.s., for all
n\in\mathbb{N}
.
Then,

\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{\tau}]\leq\mathbb{E}_{P}[X_{0}].

Proof.

For all
n\in\mathbb{N}
, define
Y_{n}=\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]
; it constitutes a martingale.

Indeed,

\forall n\in\mathbb{N},\quad\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y_{n+1}\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{n+1}]\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]=\mathbb{E}_{P}[Y\mid\mathcal{F}_{n}]=Y_{n}.

Consequently,
Z_{n}:=X_{n}-Y_{n}
defines a supermartingale, and is nonnegative.
The result follows from
Theorem

A.2
.
∎

B
Equivalent characterization of uniformly asymptotic p-variables

The goal of this section is to prove that
Definition

6.5
is indeed an equivalent characterization of uniformly asymptotic p-variables and of uniformly strongly asymptotic p-variables as they are defined in the original work of
7
.
We begin by recalling the definition in this reference, while sticking to our convention of using the index
m\in\mathbb{N}
for the approximation index.

Definition B.1

(Approximate p-variable)
.

Let
\epsilon:\mathcal{P}\to\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and
\delta:\mathcal{P}\to[0,1]
be functions.
A nonnegative, finite random variable
P
is an
(\epsilon,\delta)
-approximate p-variable
for
\mathcal{P}
if

\forall P\in\mathcal{P},\forall\alpha\in(0,1),\quad P[P\leq\alpha]\leq(1+\epsilon(P))\alpha+\delta(P).

We introduce the notations
\mathcal{E}
and
\mathcal{D}
to denote the sets of functions from
\mathcal{P}
to
\mathbb{R}_{\geq 0}
and from
\mathcal{P}
to
[0,1]
, respectively.

Theorem B.2
.

A nonnegative, finite process
p=(p_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
is

(i)

a
uniformly asymptotic p-variable
if and only if for
\mathcal{P}
there exists
((\epsilon_{m},\delta_{m}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{D}
such that
p_{m}
is
(\epsilon_{m},\delta_{m})
-approximate for
\mathcal{P}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)=\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\delta_{m}(P)=0;

(16)

(ii)

a
uniformly strongly asymptotic p-variable
if
and only if condition
Equation

16
is replaced by

\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)=0\quad\text{and}\quad\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\delta_{m}(P)=0\quad\text{for $m$ large enough}.

(17)

Proof.

The fact that
Equation

12
is equivalent to
Theorem

B.2
(i)
is shown in
7
.
We thus focus on showing that
Equation

13
is equivalent to
Theorem

B.2
(ii)
, and begin with the converse implication.
Let then
p=(p_{m})_{m\in\mathbb{N}}
be a nonnegative process satisfying
Theorem

B.2
(ii)
; we show that it also satisfies

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\alpha^{-1}P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]\leq 1.

(18)

Let
((\epsilon_{m},\delta_{m}))_{m\in\mathbb{N}}\subset\mathcal{E}\times\mathcal{D}
be such that
p_{m}
is an
(\epsilon_{m},\delta_{m})
-approximate p-variable for
\mathcal{P}
for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
, and
Equation

17
holds.
There exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that for all
m\geq m_{0}
,
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\delta_{m}(P)=0
.
Consequently, by definition, for all
m\geq m_{0}
,
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and
P\in\mathcal{P}
,

\displaystyle P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]\leq(1+\epsilon_{m}(P))\alpha+\delta_{m}(P)=(1+\epsilon_{m}(P))\alpha,

showing that

\alpha^{-1}P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]\leq 1+\epsilon_{m}(P).

Taking first the suprema over
\alpha\in(0,1)
and
P\in\mathcal{P}
and then the limit superior over
m\geq m_{0}
shows that
Equation

18
holds, where we leverage the fact that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)=0
.

We now consider the direct implication, and assume that
Equation

18
holds.
It follows immediately that there exists
m_{0}\in\mathbb{N}
such that

\forall m\geq m_{0},\quad\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\alpha^{-1}P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]<\infty

Define then for all
m\in\mathbb{N}
and
P\in\mathcal{P}

\displaystyle\delta_{m}(P)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }m<m_{0},\\
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad\epsilon_{m}(P)

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}0,&\text{if }m<m_{0},\\
\max\{0,\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\alpha^{-1}P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]-1\},&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

One immediately verifies that
P[p_{m}\leq\alpha]\leq(1+\epsilon_{m}(P))\alpha+\delta_{m}(P)
, for all
\alpha\in(0,1)
, and that
\epsilon_{m}(P)
is finite for all

and
P\in\mathcal{P}
, by construction.
Furthermore,
\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\delta_{m}(P)=0
for all
m\geq m_{0}

All that is left is to verify that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)=0
.
But this follows immediately from

18

\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\alpha^{-1}P[P_{m}\leq\alpha]-1\leq 0,

and therefore

0\leq\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)=\max\{0,\limsup_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\sup_{\alpha\in(0,1)}\alpha^{-1}P[P_{m}\leq\alpha]-1\}=0,

showing that
\lim_{m\to\infty}\sup_{P\in\mathcal{P}}\epsilon_{m}(P)
exists and is indeed

, concluding the proof.
∎
