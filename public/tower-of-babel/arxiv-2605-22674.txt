Quasicontinuity of N 1 , ∞ N^{1,\infty} functions and the Vitali–Carathéodory property on general metric spaces
Anders Björn

Abstract




Abstract.

This note is a follow up on our recent paper with L. Malý
(to appear in
Rev. Mat. Complut.
).
We provide a simple
example of a compact metric space
\mathcal{P}
for which

L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
has the Vitali–Carathéodory property,
the Sobolev
{C_{\infty}}
-capacity is an outer capacity, but
the Newtonian space
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
contains
functions which are not weakly quasicontinuous.
The novelty here is that the Vitali–Carathéodory property is satified.
We also obtain some related results about quasicontinuous functions
in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
and
a characterization of when
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
has the Vitali–Carathéodory property.

Key words and phrases
:
metric space,
Newtonian space,
outer capacity,
quasicontinuity,
Sobolev capacity,
upper gradient,
Vitali–Carathéodory property,
weak quasicontinuity.

(2020):
Primary:

30L99, 31C15, 31E05.

1
Introduction

This note is a follow up on our paper
[
1
]
(joint with L. Malý).
We refer to that paper for further discussion of the concepts in this note.
We will provide some
rather simple but illuminating observations
concerning capacity and quasicontinuous functions in the Newtonian Sobolev space
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.

We assume throughout the note that
\mathcal{P}=(\mathcal{P},d,\mu)

is a metric measure space equipped with a
positive complete Borel measure

\mu
such that
0<\mu(B)<\infty
for every ball
B\subset\mathcal{P}
.
It follows that
\mathcal{P}
is separable and Lindelöf.

The following statements
for the metric measure space
\mathcal{P}
were studied in
[
1
]

in the setting of Newtonian spaces based on general
quasi-Banach function lattices:

(A).

Every
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is quasicontinuous.

(B).

{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity.

(F).

Every
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
has a quasicontinuous representative
v
, i.e.

{C_{\infty}}(\{x:v(x)\neq u(x)\})=0.

(G).

Every
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is weakly quasicontinuous.

See Section
2
for the definitions.
Labels
(A)
–
(G)
are as in
[
1
]
,
but we have omitted some statements
that for
L^{\infty}

are equivalent to
(B)
, by
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
.
Moreover,
[
1
, Proposition 6.1]

(see Proposition
2.3
) shows that
every quasicontinuous function in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})

is in fact continuous.
This is, however, not the case for weakly quasicontinuous functions.

In particular, it was shown in
[
1
, Theorems 1.2 and 1.4]
that

(A)
implies the other properties, but not vice versa.
The following
is a special case of results in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 1.1
.

(
[
1
, Theorems 1.4 and 1.5]
)

If

\mathcal{P}
is locally compact and
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
has the Vitali–Carathéodory
property, then

{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity, i.e.
(B)
holds.

We can now improve upon this result in the following way,
cf. Theorem
2.2
for
implications not requiring
the Vitali–Carathéodory property.

Theorem 1.2
.

Assume that

L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
has the Vitali–Carathéodory property.
Then

{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity, i.e.
(B)
holds.

Moreover,
(A)
\mathchoice{\ \Longleftrightarrow\ }{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}
(F)
\mathchoice{\ \Longleftrightarrow\ }{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}
(G)
,
and there are examples when these properties all fail, and other
examples when they all hold.

In particular, we will see in Example
3.3
that
the quasicontinuity properties
(A)
,
(F)
and
(G)

are not implied by the Vitali–Carathéodory property.
Thus, in some sense,
Theorem
1.1
(and
[
1
, Theorem 1.5]
)
is sharp.
Unlike all the counterexamples in
[
1
]
,
this example has the Vitali–Carathéodory property.

The Vitali–Carathéodory property for
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})

can be characterized in the following way.

Proposition 1.3
.

The Vitali–Carathéodory property for
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
holds if and only if

\mu(\{x\})>0
for every
x\in\mathcal{P}
.

In particular,
\mathcal{P}
has to be finite or countable.

When there are (
L^{\infty}
-almost) no nonconstant rectifiable curves
in
\mathcal{P}
,
the following result gives some further equivalent statements
to the Vitali–Carathéodory property.
Some statements about (weak) quasicontinuity of functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})

were for such spaces
\mathcal{P}
given in
[
1
, Theorem 6.2]
.

Proposition 1.4
.

Assume that
there are

L^{\infty}
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable curves
in
\mathcal{P}
.
Then the following are equivalent:

1.

The Vitali–Carathéodory property holds for
X=L^{\infty}
.

2.

{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity.

3.

{C_{\infty}}(E)=1
for every nonempty set
E
.

4.

\mu(\{x\})>0
for every
x\in\mathcal{P}
.

Another property important for quasicontinuity is the
density of continuous functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.
This property will, however, not be considered in this note.
It is easily shown that if
\mathcal{P}
has
L^{\infty}
-almost no curves, then
continuous functions are dense in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})=L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
if
and only if all points in
\mathcal{P}
are isolated,
which in turn implies that
all functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
are continuous.

For locally complete connected metric spaces
\mathcal{P}
,
equipped with a doubling measure,
Durand-Cartagena–Jaramillo–Shanmugalingam
[
2
, Theorem 3.1]

showed that
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})=\Lip^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
, with comparable seminorms,
if and only if
\mathcal{P}
supports an
\infty
-Poincaré inequality,
which in turn is equivalent
to
\mathcal{P}
being very thick quasiconvex.
See also García-Bravo–Ikonen–Zhu
[
3
]
for similar results with infinitesimally doubling measures.
However, Lipschitz continuity is a stronger requirement than
continuity (=quasicontinuity), as seen for example by the slit disc.
Note that on nontrivial connected spaces, the Vitali–Carathéodory property
fails, by Proposition
1.3
.

2
Definitions and some results from
[
1
]

In
[
1
]
we considered Newtonian spaces based
on general quasi-Banach function lattices.
In this note, we limit ourselves to Newtonian spaces
based on

L^{\infty}
, which is a Banach function space.

A Borel function
g:\mathcal{P}\to[0,\infty]

is an
upper gradient
of
u:\mathcal{P}\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}:=[-\infty,\infty]
if

|u(\gamma(0))-u(\gamma(l_{\gamma}))|\leq\int_{\gamma}g\,ds

for every nonconstant rectifiable curve
\gamma:[0,l_{\gamma}]\to\mathcal{P}
.
Here we use the convention that

|(\pm\infty)-(\pm\infty)|=\infty
.

The
Newtonian space

N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
is the space

N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})=\Bigl\{u\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P}):\|u\|_{N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})}:=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}+\inf_{g}\|g\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}<\infty\Bigr\},

where the infimum is taken over all upper gradients
g
of
u
.
In this note, it is convenient to
assume that functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})

are defined everywhere (with values in
{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
),
not just up to an equivalence class.
This is essential e.g. for the definition of
upper gradients to make sense.

The
(
Sobolev
)
capacity
of a set

E\subset\mathcal{P}
is defined as

{C_{\infty}}(E)=\inf\{\|u\|_{N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})}:u\geq 1\mbox{ on }E\}.

The capacity
{C_{\infty}}
is an
outer capacity
if

{C_{\infty}}(E)=\inf_{\begin{subarray}{c}G\text{ open}\\
G\supset E\end{subarray}}{C_{\infty}}(G)\quad\text{for every set }E\subset\mathcal{P}.

Several equivalent characterizations
of the outer capacity property were given in
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
.

Definition 2.1
.

A function
u:\mathcal{P}\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
is
weakly quasicontinuous

if for every
\varepsilon>0
there is a set
E\subset\mathcal{P}
with

{C_{\infty}}(E)<\varepsilon
such that the restriction
u|_{\mathcal{P}\setminus E}
is
continuous.
If the set

E
can be chosen open for every
\varepsilon>0
, then
u
is

quasicontinuous
.

\mathcal{P}
has
L^{\infty}
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable curves

if there is a Borel function
\rho\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})

such that
\int_{\gamma}\rho\,ds=\infty
for every
nonconstant rectifiable curve
\gamma\in\Gamma
.

The following result was one of the main results in
[
1
]
.

Theorem 2.2
.

(
[
1
, Theorem 1.4]
)

The following implications hold under our general assumptions
:

(A)
\scriptstyle{{\textstyle\kern 5.0pt\not\kern-5.0pt\Longleftarrow}}
(B)
\scriptstyle{\textstyle\text{\hbox to0.0pt{\kern-6.64433pt$\not$\hss}}{\textstyle\kern 2.79999pt\not\kern-2.79999pt\Big\Downarrow}}
(F)
\scriptstyle{{\textstyle\kern 5.0pt\not\kern-5.0pt\Longleftarrow}}
(G)
.

Moreover, there are examples when all of these properties fail.

Proposition 2.3
.

(
[
1
, Proposition 6.1]
)

The following are true for any metric measure space
\mathcal{P}

satisfying the standing assumptions
:

(i).

{C_{\infty}}(G)=1
for every nonempty open set
G
.

(ii).

Every quasicontinuous function is continuous.

(iii).

For each
E\subset\mathcal{P}
either

{C_{\infty}}(E)=0
or
{C_{\infty}}(E)=1
.

3
The Vitali–Carathéodory property

Definition 3.1
.

L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})

has the
Vitali–Carathéodory property

if

\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=\inf\{\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}:v\geq|u|\mbox{ and $v$ is lower semicontinuous on $\mathcal{P}$}\}

for every measurable function
u:\mathcal{P}\to{\overline{\mathbf{R}}}
.

If
\mu(\{x\})=0
, then
\|\chi_{\{x\}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=0
.
Let
v\geq\chi_{\{x\}}
be a lower semicontinuous function.
Then
v>\tfrac{1}{2}
in an open set
G\ni x
(which has positive measure) and hence

\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}>\tfrac{1}{2}
.
This shows that the Vitali–Carathéodory property fails.

Conversely, assume that
\mu(\{x\})>0
for every
x\in\mathcal{P}
and
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.
Then, for every
u\in L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})
, the constant function

v\equiv\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=\sup_{x\in\mathcal{P}}|u(x)|

is a lower semicontinuous
majorant of
|u|
with
\|v\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}=\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}
.
∎

As there are

L^{\infty}
-almost no nonconstant rectifiable curves,

{C_{\infty}}(E)=\|\chi_{E}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathcal{P})}
.
That
2
–
4
are equivalent
then follows from Proposition
2.3
.

The equivalence
1
\mathchoice{\ \Longleftrightarrow\ }{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}{\Leftrightarrow}
4

follows directly from Proposition
1.3
.
∎

By Proposition
1.3
,
\mathcal{P}
is at most countable
and thus contains no nonconstant curves.
It then follows from Proposition
1.4

that
{C_{\infty}}
is an outer capacity,
and moreover that

{C_{\infty}}(E)=1
for every nonempty set
E
.
Hence any weakly quasicontinuous continuous function is continuous
and thus quasicontinuous, i.e.
(G)
\mathchoice{\ \Longrightarrow\ }{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}
(A)
.
The implications

(A)
\mathchoice{\ \Longrightarrow\ }{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}
(F)
\mathchoice{\ \Longrightarrow\ }{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}{\Rightarrow}
(G)
follow
from Theorem

.

Example

shows that

(A)
,
(F)
and
(G)
can fail.
On the other hand,
(A)
,
(F)
and
(G)

trivially hold when
\mathcal{P}
is a finite or discrete set.
∎

All counterexamples in

.

(This example is a slight modification of

The difference is that

Let

4
Local connectedness

We recall the classical definition.

Definition 4.1
.

\mathcal{P}
is
locally connected at
x\in\mathcal{P}

if for every
r>0
there is a connected neighbourhood
(not necessarily open)
G\subset B(x,r)
of
x
.

\mathcal{P}
is
locally connected
if it is locally connected at
every point
x\in\mathcal{P}
.

(Connected) components are always closed.
If
\mathcal{P}
is locally connected then every
component is open.
This is never the case when
\mathcal{P}
is not locally connected.

Proposition 4.2
.

Assume that
\mathcal{P}
is not locally connected.

Then there is a non-continuous function
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
,
and moreover
u
is not even quasicontinuous.

We can find
x_{0}
and
r
such that
B:=B(x_{0},r)
does not
contain any connected (not necessarily open) neighbourhood of
x_{0}
.
Let
E
be the component of
B
containing
x_{0}
.
Then
E
is relatively closed in
B
and thus measurable.
By the choice of
r
, we see that

x_{0}\in\partial(B\setminus E)
.
Let

u(x)=\begin{cases}1-d(x,x_{0})/r,&\text{if }x\in E,\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

As there are no curves between
E
and
B\setminus E
,
we see that
g\equiv 1/r
is an
upper gradient of
u
in
\mathcal{P}
, and thus
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
.
Clearly,
u
is not continuous at
x_{0}
.
By Proposition
2.3
(ii)
,
u
is not even
quasicontinuous either.
∎

Remark 4.3
.

If
\mathcal{P}
is disconnected,
then there are two cases:

1.

The components of
\mathcal{P}
are “well separated” in the following sense:
If

x\in\mathcal{P}
and
\mathcal{P}_{x}
is the component containing
x
,
then
x\notin\overline{\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{x}}
.
In this case, the components are open
and (quasi)continuity
for functions in
N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})
can be studied in each component separately.

2.

There is

x\in\mathcal{P}
such that
x\in\overline{\mathcal{P}\setminus\mathcal{P}_{x}}
, where

\mathcal{P}_{x}
is the component of
x
.
In this case,

\mathcal{P}
is not locally connected at
x
.
Hence, by Proposition
4.2
,
there is a non-continuous function
u\in N^{1,\infty}(\mathcal{P})

which is not even quasicontinuous.
