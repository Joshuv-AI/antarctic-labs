 Abstract
 This report documents the release of evaluated neutron-induced 238,240−242 Pu prompt fission
 neutron multiplicities, ν p , for ENDF/B-IX.0. For each isotope, one evaluation out of four is
 chosen. Each chosen evaluation is based on experimental ν p and model values from the CGMF
 code. The chosen evaluations were those that agree best to differential experimental ν p and show
 the expected smoothness in the evaluated data. These evaluated data are then validated with
 respect to values of the effective neutron multiplication factor, keff , of ICSBEP Pu assemblies
 spanning the thermal to the fast energy ranges. The impact of the new Pu ν p is well within the
 Monte Carlo statistics uncertainties for most assemblies except for MCI-005, PST-012, 18 and 22
 assemblies, where the new 241 Pu ν p reduces the bias of calculated to experimental keff by 40–100
 pcm compared to ENDF/B-VIII.1. This new 241 Pu ν p at thermal also agrees better with the
 Neutron Data Standards value than ENDF/B-VIII.1. The bias in Dirty Jezebel (PU-MET-FAST-
 002) keff increases by 40 pcm but other fast assembly keff values change negligibly.

1 Introduction
Here, we document which evaluation of minor Pu neutron-induced average prompt fission neutron
multiplicities, ν p will be released to the “Cross Section Evaluation Working Group” (CSEWG) for the
next U.S. nuclear data library, ENDF/B-IX.0, and the reasoning behind this choice.
 This work started from a consistent evaluation of 238−242 Pu ν p that was a Nuclear Criticality Safety
Program (NCSP) milestone for FY23 [1] and validation of this evaluation as part of a FY24 NCSP
milestone [2]. This evaluation included consistent modeling with the fission event generator CGMF [3]
and a detailed uncertainty quantification of all accepted experimental data. This evaluation is shown
in Section 2 to differ systematically from part of the experimental ν p . Therefore, evaluations were
undertaken as part of the ASC-PEM-NP (Advanced Simulation and Computing Program-Physics and
Engineering Program-Nuclear Physics) project based on only experimental ν p as well as on experi-
mental ν p and model values for each isotope separately as we suspected that the too strong model
constraints across the chain of isotope and/or incorrect model systematics were pulling the evaluated
data away from experimental ν p .
 In Section 2, we select from these four evaluations (experimental data only, model-systematics
across isotopes, and two model-based evaluations per isotopes) the evaluations that will be released
for ENDF/B-IX.0 for each isotope. We selected the evaluated data based on agreement to differential
data, physics behavior of the curve, and validation with integral experiments. The evaluated data
are given in ENDF-6 format in appendices A–D. Each chosen evaluation is based on a combination
of model values and experimental ν p . Thus, we can compare in Section 3 the average total kinetic
energy, TKE, calculated from the evaluated parameters fitted to ν p to experimental data to further
gauge whether our chosen evaluations are realistic. The chosen evaluated ν p are also validated with
respect to the effective neutron multiplication factor of several ICSBEP [4] critical assemblies in 4. A
summary and conclusions are given in Section 5.

2 Comparison to differential data and physics behavior of curves
A total of four evaluations are studied:

 1. An evaluation obtained via generalized least squares that uses only experimental data (“Eval.
 exp. only” in Figs. 1–4).
 2. An evaluation obtained via Kalman filter fits of ARIADNE [5] using a CGMF prior (includes even-
 odd staggering for the average total kinetic energy and updates to spin parameterization) [1] and
 the same experimental data as in bullet 1. This fit was undertaken such that model parameters
 were fit simultaneously to 238−242 Pu experimental ν p at once. The resulting evaluated model
 parameters were then used as input for CGMF to give evaluated ν p . The corresponding evaluated
 data are called in Figs. 1–4 “Eval. CGMF across isotope”). The same evaluated data were used
 for previous validation studies [2] that are copied over here in Table 1. This fit leverages model
 correlations and experimental data across isotopes to inform the evaluation. However, due to
 model stiffness, it is more difficult for the Kalman filter in ARIADNE to get close to experimental
 ν p of specific isotopes.
 3. The same prior as bullet 2 was fit except that one isotope was fit at a time. So, we do not inform
 the fit for 238 Pu ν p by experimental 239 Pu ν p . The corresponding evaluated data are called in
 Figs. 1–4 “Eval. Kalman”). Contrary to bullet 2, these are evaluated ν p directly coming out of
 Kalman which are often not quite as smooth as when we use Kalman-evaluated parameters as
 input for CGMF. By fitting experimental ν p of one isotope at a time, one has more flexibility
 to get evaluated data closer to the individual experimental ν p . However, one loses the model
 consistency across the chain of isotopes with one set of parameters for all ν p .
 4. The same as bullet 3 except that the model parameters coming out of the Kalman fit of bullet 3
 were used as input to the code CGMF [3] to produce evaluated ν p values that are termed “Eval.
 CGMF” in Figs. 1–4.
For the purpose of clarity, the evaluations are named according to the legend entries in Figs. 1–4
henceforth.

2.1 Pu
Only two experimental data points at thermal were accepted for the evaluations shown in Fig. 1.
ENDF/B-VIII.1 [6] favors the data of Kroshkin et al., 1970 and is based on Los Alamos model sys-
tematics by Tudora et al. and was introduced in ENDF/B-VII.1.
 Choice: We adopt the evaluation “Eval. Kalman” as it agrees well with the two
experimental data points at thermal and shows a physical curve at higher energies. The
“Eval. CGMF across isotope” fit pulls away from experimental data and ENDF/B-VIII.1 at thermal
where the latter is also based on a global fit. Given that the curve “Eval. CGMF across isotope” also
does not agree well to experimental data of other isotopes (especially 240,242 Pu(n,f) ν p ), we reject it.
The evaluation with experimental data only (“Eval. exp. only”) is not recommended as experimental
data are too sparse to guide the whole evaluation, while “Eval. CGMF” leads to an unphysical
structure from 8–14 MeV. One could question the validity of parameters related to “Eval. Kalman” if
they result in such an unphysical structure in “Eval. CGMF”.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: The reasoning to replace ENDF/B-VIII.1 is
that we agree better to experimental ν p at thermal when factoring in the weights obtained
from a detailed uncertainty estimate. This argument is weak as we are replacing one evaluation
based on parameter systematics by another one that is mostly based on parameter systematics. We
still release it for testing for ENDF/B-IX.0 for the sake of completeness.

 241
2.3 Pu
The 241 Pu(n,f) average total prompt fission neutron multiplicity, ν t at thermal is a quantity evaluated
as part of the thermal neutron constants of the Neutron Data Standards [7]. This value should be
adopted from the Standards and is currently 2.940 n/f (neutrons per fission). If one reduces this value
by the evaluated average delayed neutron multiplicity of ENDF/B-VIII.1, one would obtain a ν p of
2.910034. In general, the experimental data in the fast range are trustworthy; Conde et al., 1968 data
suffer from poor statistics. ENDF/B-VIII.1 241 Pu(n,f) is only provided at 5 energy points and does
not fully capture experimental data due to that and overfits to the Conde data point at 0.5 MeV.
 Choice: The evaluation “Eval. CGMF” was selected as it agrees well to Frehaut et
al., 1974 data and exhibits the expected smoothness of the curve. The “Eval. CGMF
across isotope” is too high compared to Frehaut experimental data and ENDF/B-VIII.1 below 8 MeV
and too low above 12 MeV indicating a shape issue in fitting the data that was already observed for
240
 Pu(n,f) ν p . The evaluation with experimental data only (“Eval. exp. only”) suffers distinctly from
an overfitting issue and is not an adequate representation of the smoothness expected from physics’
considerations. “Eval. Kalman” and “Eval. CGMF” are nearly equal, where the latter is a bit
smoother due to the model’s stiffness. Also, at thermal, “Eval. CGMF” has a value of 2.917 n/f
closest to the value that could be derived from the Standards value.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: The new evaluation removes overfitting (and
unphysical) structures in ENDF/B-VIII.1 at 0.5 MeV and provides an adequately fine
grid. It is also closer to the current Neutron Data Standard at thermal.

 242
The Pu(n,f) ν t in ENDF/B-VIII.1 is a straight-line fit (i.e., two energy points in MF=1, MT=452)
to the data of Khoklov et al., 1994 which would not capture second-chance fission effects that CGMF
captures.
 Choice: “Eval. CGMF” is selected as it agrees well to Khoklov et al., 1994 data and
exhibits the expected smoothness of the curve. The “Eval. CGMF across isotope” is exhibiting
a major shape issue in fitting the data that was already observed for 240,241 Pu(n,f) ν p and is too
high above 2 MeV compared to the experimental data. The evaluation with experimental data only
(“Eval. exp. only”) suffers distinctly from overfitting issue and is not an adequate representation of
the smoothness expected from physics’ considerations. “Eval. Kalman” and “Eval. CGMF” are nearly
equal, where the latter is a bit smoother due to the model stiffness.
 Reasoning for replacing ENDF/B-VIII.1: ENDF/B-VIII.1 is a simple linear fit (with
two energy points), but ν p are not truly linear. Hence, this new evaluation can catch
subtle and not so subtle (second-chance fission structure) deviations from a linear fit.

2.5 General comments
We are dealing here with evaluations with scarce experimental coverage. Fitting to only experimental
data (“Eval. exp. only”) can lead to structures stemming from overfitting to few data points which
are healed by the model. The model systematics across isotopes (“Eval. CGMF across isotope”) have
clear shape deviations from differential experimental data that were trusted for evaluation purposes.
Hence, we did not select those values. Also, the cross-isotope correlations we gain through using a
cross-isotope fit were shown to be negligible for uncertainty quantification purposes when forward-
propagated correlated ν p covariances across isotopes to keff uncertainties of selected assemblies [2]. So,
there was no advantage in using “Eval. CGMF across isotope” compared to the fits of CGMF priors
one isotope at a time for the purpose of ENDF/B-IX.0.

 As we are changing the 238,240−242 Pu ν p from thermal to above 5 MeV, it was necessary to counter-
check if these changes have any impact on critical assemblies from the thermal to the fast range in
a second, more detailed, investigation. To this end, the change in 238,240−242 Pu ν p from ENDF/B-
VIII.1 was forward-propagated to keff values by using sensitivities of keff to 238,240−242 Pu ν p . These
sensitivities were calculated with ENDF/B-VIII.1 for the PARADIGM LDRD-DR project and re-used
here. The change in keff for several of those assemblies is shown in Tables 3–5.
 Most keff values change less than 10 pcm from the ENDF/B-VIII.1 value when one introduces
the new 238,240−242 Pu ν p . Such changes are below the Monte Carlo statistic uncertainties of the
original simulated value and the sensitivities uncertainties. Thus, we see a negligible impact of our
new 238,240−242 Pu ν p for most assemblies. The exceptions are Dirty Jezebel (PU-MET-FAST-002) and
the Pu thermal solution series PU-SOL-THERM-012, PU-SOL-THERM-018 and PU-SOL-THERM-
022 where decreases of 35–115 pcm can be osberved in Table 4 due to 241 Pu ν p . When one compares
to experimental values (all 1.0) in Fig. 6, these decreases in keff lead overall to an improvement in keff
values where the mean bias across all shown data decreases from 366 to 310 pcm. It is noteworthy,
that these three assembly series have a non-negligible sensitivity to the thermal 241 Pu ν p . Our selected
evaluation is closer to the Neutron Data Standards at the thermal point than ENDF/B-VIII.1. Hence,
increasing agreement to differential experimental data and the Standard with the current evaluation,
also led to a noticeable improvement of some PU-SOL-THERM keff values.
 Three more assemblies were studied: MIX-COMP-INTER-005-001 [4], and preliminary PARADIGM
low and high energy assemblies [12] were added to Table 2. The PARADIGM (and above the EUCLID)
critical assemblies are not accepted by the ICSBEP as of yet. We selected MIX-COMP-INTER-005-
001 in particular to test if there is any impact of changes in 238 Pu ν p on MOX fuels below the fast
range, while the PARADIGM assemblies provided more data in the intermediate energy range. It
can be seen from the last line in Table 2 that changes in keff due to the updated 238 Pu ν p are well
within the Monte Carlo statistics uncertainties. However, the thermal change in 241 Pu ν p has a similar
effect for MIX-COMP-INTER-005-001 as seen for PST-012, PST-018 and PST-022; it lowers the keff
by approximately 60 pcm and brings it thus closer to the experimental value of 1.0. Changes in the
intermediate energy range in ν p affect the simulation of the PARADIGM integral experiments only
within Monte Carlo statistics.

5 Summary and conclusions
This report documents the release of evaluated 238,240−242 Pu prompt fission neutron multiplicities,
ν p , for ENDF/B-IX.0. The chosen evaluations were based on experimental ν p and model values from
the fission event generator CGMF. These evaluated data were validated with respect to values of the
effective neutron multiplication factor, keff , of ICSBEP Pu assemblies spanning the thermal to the fast
energy ranges. The impact of the new Pu ν p is well within the Monte Carlo statistics uncertainties for
most assemblies except for MCI-005, PST-012, 18 and 22 assemblies, where the new 241 Pu ν p reduces
the bias of calculated to experimental keff by 40–100 pcm compared to ENDF/B-VIII.1. This new
241
 Pu ν p at thermal agrees also better with the Neutron Data Standards value than ENDF/B-VIII.1.
The bias in Dirty Jezebel keff increases by 40 pcm but other fast assembly keff values change negligibly.
 The evaluated 238,241,242 Pu ν p will be directly submitted to the CSEWG collaboration, while 240 Pu
ν p will be submitted as part of the new 240 Pu LANL file. Once these data have been tested and ap-
proved by the committee, we will provide a second version of this report including evaluated covariances
in ENDF-6 format.
