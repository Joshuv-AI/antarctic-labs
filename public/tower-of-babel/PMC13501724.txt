AI assisted photographic posture evaluation: a comparative study of ChatGPT and expert physiotherapists
Authors: Ugur Sozlu, Selim Mahmut Gunay, Gul Ozkazanc
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundTraditional visual methods used in posture analysis are limited by subjectivity and variability, whereas computer vision systems provide precise geometric measurements but require technical expertise and specialized resources. Large language models like ChatGPT offer an accessible, low-cost alternative with human-like interpretive abilities and potential educational value. This study aimed to examine the agreement between ChatGPT and expert physiotherapists in standardized photographic posture assessments.MethodsThis cross-sectional comparative study included 39 young adults with at least one visible postural deviation. The sampling frame consisted of individuals preliminarily evaluated during a posture assessment assignment by physiotherapy students. Standardized anterior, posterior, and lateral photographs were taken under controlled conditions. Two physiotherapists independently assessed posture using a seven-region Posture Analysis Form, and the same anonymized photographs were evaluated by ChatGPT-5.1 (multimodal version, web interface, 15–16 November 2025) using a standardized prompt.ResultsInter-rater agreement between physiotherapists was excellent (κ = 0.84–0.94). Agreement between physiotherapists and ChatGPT ranged from moderate to excellent. Trunk (κ = 0.85) and knee alignment (κ = 0.83) showed almost perfect agreement, while head–neck position showed substantial agreement (κ = 0.67). In contrast, scapular (κ = 0.51), shoulder girdle (κ = 0.47), pelvic (κ = 0.47), and foot alignment (κ = 0.43) exhibited moderate agreement. Percentage agreement ranged from 64.10% to 92.31%.ConclusionThis study suggested that ChatGPT shows high agreement with physiotherapists in regions with clearly observable postural deviations, while its reliability decreases in areas requiring finer angular discrimination. The findings further indicate that the model may serve as a supportive tool, particularly for assessing trunk, knee, and head–neck alignment.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s13102-026-01812-8.Keywords: Posture, ChatGPT, Artificial intelligence, Large language models

Introduction

Posture is defined as the biomechanical alignment of body segments in static or dynamic positions and plays a central role in efficient movement, pain prevention, and quality of life [1]. Prolonged sitting and sustained use of screen-based digital devices (e.g., computers and tablets) among university-aged individuals and desk-based workers are commonly associated with forward head posture, shoulder protraction, and related musculoskeletal symptoms [2, 3]. Poor postural habits have also been associated with more severe and persistent spinal pain, spinal dysfunction, and reduced quality of life [4]. Posture assessment is therefore critical for maintaining musculoskeletal health and for the early identification of potential functional impairments [2]. Traditional posture assessment methods (e.g., visual inspection, plumb-line evaluation) are widely used in clinical practice; however, these methods present several important limitations, including inter-rater variability, time-consuming procedures, and insufficient standardization [5, 6]. In response to these limitations, computer vision (CV) and pose estimation systems have emerged as alternative technological approaches for posture analysis [7]. Tools such as OpenPose, MoveNet, and MediaPipe provide objective and reproducible measurements based on joint coordinates and angle-based calculations, functioning as indirect posture analysis systems derived from pose estimation [8, 9]. Despite these advantages, such systems often require specialized software, high computational power, technical expertise, and, in some cases, paid access [10]. Therefore, these systems may not always be readily accessible in practical applications.

In recent years, Large Language Models (LLMs) have emerged as user-friendly tools due to their broad accessibility, low cost, ease of use, and ability to generate explanatory feedback [11]. Beyond free-text generation, LLMs have expanded into clinical reasoning, summarization, and decision-support applications [12]. Additionally, they can transform image-based inputs into verbal explanations, enabling interactions resembling clinician–patient communication and improving accessibility for both healthcare professionals and the general public [13, 14]. Although these systems are not designed or validated for posture analysis, their rapid integration into healthcare contexts necessitates systematic evaluation of their clinical agreement and reliability in comparison with experienced assessors [15, 16]. Importantly, LLMs are not proposed as replacements for validated computer vision based or instrumented systems. Rather, this study examines whether a widely accessible and low-threshold AI model can show meaningful agreement with expert assessment in simplified photographic screening scenarios.

From this perspective, the present study aims to investigate the extent to which LLMs can produce observational postural classifications comparable to those of physiotherapists and to explore their potential role as a rapid, supportive screening tool. This approach may offer an accessible alternative for posture evaluation. Accordingly, the primary aim of this study was to compare postural evaluations performed by physiotherapists and ChatGPT. A secondary aim was to determine the level of agreement between the two physiotherapists’ posture assessments.

Materials and methods

Study design and participants

This study was designed as a cross-sectional comparative observational study. Standardized posture photographs from the same participants were independently evaluated by two expert physiotherapists and ChatGPT, and agreement between the resulting assessments was analyzed.

Potential participants selected through purposive sampling were included to ensure representation of individuals with diverse postural characteristics. During the preliminary phase, physiotherapy students received theoretical and practical training on posture assessment as part of the “Fundamental Measurement and Evaluation in Physiotherapy” course. Following this training, a total of 101 physiotherapy students each collected data from one volunteer, resulting in demographic information and posture assessments from 101 individuals. After screening these data, 59 individuals with at least one visible postural deviation were invited to participate, and 39 consented and were included in the final sample.

Inclusion criteria were the ability to maintain an upright standing position and to comply with the photographic protocol. Exclusion criteria included acute musculoskeletal injury, recent major surgery (≤ 3 months), acute trauma affecting posture, or clothing that obscured essential anatomical landmarks.

Assessment and data collection procedure

Two experienced physiotherapists (US, SMG), each with over ten years of clinical practice, participated as expert assessors in the study. Posture training was conducted by the first physiotherapist (US). Participant photographs were taken by an independent individual (SS) who was not involved in the study procedures. Anonymization of the photographs and execution of the ChatGPT analysis using the standardized prompt were carried out by an information technology specialist (GÖ) with no background in health sciences. In the final stage, all photographs were independently evaluated by the two physiotherapists (US, SMG). The physiotherapists were fully blinded to all AI outputs during their independent evaluations. In cases of any disagreement between the two physiotherapists during their independent evaluations, both assessors met to discuss the findings and reached a consensus on the final classification. Inter-rater agreement (Cohen’s kappa) between the two physiotherapists was calculated based on their initial independent assessments prior to the consensus meeting.

Demographic data

Participants’ demographic characteristics, including age, sex, height, and weight, were collected using a brief demographic data form developed specifically for this study (see Supplement Material 1).

Posture photography protocol

Standardized posture photographs were obtained in four planes, anterior, posterior, and right and left lateral views, for use in the posture assessment (Fig. 1). All images were captured in the same room under consistent lighting conditions to ensure standardization. A high resolution digital camera was mounted on a tripod to maintain a fixed height and angle across all participants. The camera was positioned approximately 2.5 m from the participant and 1.1 m above the ground, with the field of view kept constant. Although the camera had a digital zoom function, no zoom was applied during image capture, and the focal settings were kept constant. The floor surface and background (including visible tile lines) were consistent across all photographs to ensure standardization within a natural clinical setting and were not used as reference markers during posture evaluation. Participants were instructed to wear comfortable clothing and stand barefoot in a natural, relaxed posture.

Physiotherapist-assessed posture evaluation

Before data collection, the two physiotherapists met to review the form and harmonize their operational definitions to ensure consistency in evaluations. Posture was evaluated using a Posture Analysis Form developed by the research team specifically for this study and not previously published; an English-language version is provided as Supplement Material 1. The form was structured around systematic observational criteria and student assignment feedback. The form assessed body alignment across seven anatomical regions: head and neck position, shoulder girdle, scapular position, trunk alignment, pelvic position, knee alignment, and foot alignment. This form served as the standardized assessment tool for both the student assignments and the prompt used for ChatGPT. When clinically appropriate, more than one deformity per anatomical region was allowed (with a maximum of two deviations observed per region in this dataset). In cases where multiple deviations occurred, they were combined using a plus (‘+’) delimiter to form a single composite category. Coding of physiotherapist ratings and ChatGPT outputs was performed by the first physiotherapist (US) and independently checked by the second physiotherapist (SMG).

Posture assessment using ChatGPT

Posture photographs anonymized by an information technology specialist to conceal facial and identifiable features were uploaded to the ChatGPT platform. The anonymization mask did not cover key landmarks such as the chin or tragus, ensuring that head–neck assessment was not affected. The anonymized images were used solely for this study and were not stored after processing. To prevent any influence from previous inputs, each participant’s assessment was conducted in a newly initiated session with all memory, session history, and prior-interaction features fully disabled. The analyses were performed using ChatGPT-5.1 (multimodal version), accessed via the subscription-based web interface (ChatGPT Plus) on 15–16 November 2025, using the default system configuration available at that time. The model was instructed to perform posture assessment using a predefined prompt. This approach ensured that the model performed each analysis independently and maintained methodological consistency.

Sample size

As no prior data were available regarding the prevalence or distribution of postural deviations in this population, an a priori kappa-based sample size calculation was not feasible. Therefore, the adequacy of the final sample (n = 39) was assessed using a post-hoc power analysis performed after data collection. Power was estimated using the physiotherapist–ChatGPT rating matrices. The noncentrality parameter (λ) was calculated using the formula λ = (κ_obs − κ₀) / SE, where κ_obs represents the observed Cohen’s kappa coefficient, κ₀ denotes the null hypothesis kappa value, and SE refers to the bootstrap-derived standard error of κ estimated from 2,000 resamples.

The analyses indicated > 0.99 power in regions with high observed Cohen’s kappa values (trunk and knee), moderate power for the head–neck region, and low to moderate power for the shoulder girdle, scapular, pelvic, and foot alignment regions. These findings suggest that the study had adequate sensitivity to detect large effect sizes, but limited ability to identify small to moderate effects.

Statistical analysis

Data were analyzed using IBM SPSS Statistics (Version 27, IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA). Descriptive statistics were reported as mean ± standard deviation for continuous variables and as frequencies and percentages for categorical variables. Cohen’s kappa coefficients (κ) with their 95% confidence intervals (CI) were calculated to assess agreement both between the two expert physiotherapists and between ChatGPT and the physiotherapist consensus evaluation for each postural deviation. In cases where more than one deviation was identified, these were combined into a single composite category, ensuring that each participant received only one definitive classification per region. Accordingly, Cohen’s κ was calculated using mutually exclusive participant-level categories, thereby satisfying the methodological assumptions of the statistic. Interpretation of κ values followed the classification proposed by Landis and Koch (1977): <0.00 poor, 0.00–0.20 slight, 0.21–0.40 fair, 0.41–0.60 moderate, 0.61–0.80 substantial, and 0.81–1.00 excellent agreement [17].

Cases classified as “Not understood / Not assessable” were treated as nominal categories and included in the agreement analysis. Additionally, percentage agreement between physiotherapists and ChatGPT was calculated as the proportion of exact matches at the participant level (Percentage agreement = [Number of exact matches / Total number of cases] × 100). The error rate was defined as the proportion of categorical disagreements between the two classifications (Error rate = [Number of mismatched cases / Total number of cases] × 100). Error counts were determined through direct one-to-one comparisons between the physiotherapist consensus classification and the corresponding ChatGPT classification for each anatomical region, rather than based on marginal category totals. A significance level of p < 0.05 was adopted for all statistical tests.

Results

A total of 39 participants were included in the study. The mean age was 25.9 ± 3.9 years (range: 19–33). Of the participants, 12.8% were female and 87.2% were male. Mean height was 170.5 ± 8.9 cm, mean weight was 71.8 ± 12.2 kg, and mean BMI was 24.7 ± 3.9 kg/m² (Table 1). Cohen’s kappa values for inter-rater agreement were as follows: head & neck position (κ = 0.94), shoulder girdle (κ = 0.91), scapular position (κ = 0.90), trunk alignment (κ = 0.93), pelvic position (κ = 0.84), knee alignment (κ = 0.93), and foot alignment (κ = 0.85) (p < 0.001 for all regions).

Discussion

This study examined the agreement between ChatGPT and expert physiotherapists in photograph based posture assessment and evaluated the performance of the AI model. Overall, ChatGPT showed high concordance with physiotherapists in regions characterized by more pronounced biomechanical deviations, such as the trunk and knee. However, its performance declined in anatomical areas requiring finer visual discrimination, particularly the scapular region and foot alignment. These findings should be interpreted in light of the sampling strategy, as participants were preselected based on the presence of at least one visible postural deviation. Accordingly, the agreement estimates reflect performance within a deviation-enriched sample rather than a general population context.

In our study, trunk alignment indicated an excellent level of agreement (κ = 0.85) with a high percentage agreement (89.74%). These results indicate that ChatGPT effectively identified pronounced deviations such as increased lordosis–kyphosis or scoliosis. Similarly, the high agreement observed in knee alignment (κ = 0.83; 92.31% agreement) suggests that the model accurately evaluated frontal-plane deviations such as genu valgum or genu varum. These findings are consistent with previous literature emphasizing that model performance tends to be higher in tasks involving clearer structural boundaries and more visually salient anatomical features [18, 19]. Nevertheless, given the high accuracy standards required in healthcare-related applications, such results should be interpreted cautiously and supported by expert oversight to ensure clinical reliability.

In contrast, the model’s performance declined in anatomical regions requiring finer or angle-dependent visual interpretation. Pelvic position showed moderate agreement (κ = 0.47) and a high error rate (33.33%). Considering that pelvic tilt assessment is known to show substantial variability even among trained clinicians, this finding is not unexpected. Factors such as clothing, body composition, and camera angle may also have limited the ability of both ChatGPT and physiotherapists to accurately evaluate pelvic posture from photographs. Conditions such as pes planus, pronation, and supination, as well as small angular variations in the subtalar and midfoot joints, make foot posture assessment particularly challenging. For this reason, it is not surprising that foot alignment yielded the moderate agreement (κ = 0.43) and the highest error rate (35.90%). Additionally, lighting, viewing angle, and the visibility of foot–ground contact may have further affected performance. Similar findings have been reported in AI-based foot and gait analysis studies, where accuracy remains limited without dedicated computer-vision training or advanced algorithmic support [20, 21].

Moderate agreement was observed for the shoulder girdle (κ = 0.47) and scapular region (κ = 0.51). The AI model frequently overclassified minimal scapular asymmetries as “elevated scapula,” while identifying anterior shoulder protraction less often than physiotherapists. We believe these discrepancies stem from the inherent difficulty of accurately evaluating scapular mechanics using two-dimensional images [22]. Head–neck position showed a substantial agreement (κ = 0.67) with a moderate error rate (15.38%). The model accurately identified major postural deviations but tended to overinterpret subtle asymmetries, such as minimal lateral tilt or rotation. Our findings are consistent with previous evidence suggesting that LLMs may overinterpret borderline findings when categorical boundaries are not clearly defined [23].

Overall, ChatGPT demonstrated relatively high accuracy in identifying clearly observable postural deviations, whereas reliability was lower in regions requiring more subtle angular discrimination. Taken together, these findings indicate that, within a standardized visual assessment framework, ChatGPT can generate postural classifications that show similarity to the observational categorizations made by clinicians. Importantly, this agreement reflects categorical visual assessment only and does not indicate biomechanical accuracy or diagnostic validity. However, this observed agreement should not be interpreted as validation of ChatGPT as a diagnostic or screening tool. Rather, it should be considered preliminary evidence of its potential role within the context of posture assessment.

Limitations

This study has several important limitations. First, despite using standardized, literature-based definitions in the prompt, ChatGPT’s internal reference framework cannot be fully verified due to its black-box nature. Second, although memory and session history were disabled, some variability may persist due to the probabilistic nature of large language models. A standardized prompt was used to minimize this variability; however, the model’s test–retest reliability was not assessed and should be examined in future work. Third, the regions with moderate or low post hoc power and the wide confidence intervals for several Cohen’s kappa values indicate that these findings should be interpreted cautiously. Moreover, the very low prevalence of deviations such as scoliosis further limits the generalizability of these agreement estimates. Future studies with larger samples are needed to obtain more stable and generalizable estimates. Fourth, the predominantly male sample limits generalizability to more gender-balanced populations, as differences in body composition may affect anatomical landmark visibility and posture assessment; therefore, the findings should be interpreted with caution. Fifth, the absence of a validated gold standard for posture assessment, along with the preselection of participants exhibiting postural deviations, may have artificially inflated agreement levels and limited the generalizability of the findings. In this study, physiotherapist evaluations were considered a clinical reference standard; however, they do not represent a biomechanical ground truth based on objective instrumented measurements. Sixth, the findings pertain to a single LLM (ChatGPT) and cannot be generalized to other AI systems; future studies should include multiple models. In addition, the posture assessment form used in the study was internally developed and has not undergone external validation. Finally, as the analyses were exploratory and no correction for multiple comparisons was applied, the increased risk of Type I error requires cautious interpretation of the findings.

Conclusion

ChatGPT’s performance in posture analysis based on standardized photographs varied across anatomical regions. While the model showed relatively higher agreement with physiotherapists in the trunk, knee, and head–neck regions, its accuracy was noticeably lower in areas requiring finer discrimination, such as the pelvis, foot, and shoulder girdle. These findings indicate varying levels of agreement between ChatGPT and physiotherapist assessments within a standardized visual posture evaluation framework. They suggest that the model may have exploratory and educational value in illustrating general postural tendencies. It should be emphasized that these outcomes reflect the model’s capacity for visual categorization rather than any form of clinical or biomechanical precision. In the future, integrating multimodal artificial intelligence models, advanced computer-vision algorithms, or multi-angle imaging techniques may enhance model accuracy.
