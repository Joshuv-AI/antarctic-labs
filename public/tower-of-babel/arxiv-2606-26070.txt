Polynomiality of the Generalized Verschiebung Degree
Siqing Zhang

Abstract
. For a general curve in positive characteristic, taking the Frobenius pullback induces a generically finite rational map V V on the moduli space of rank 2 vector bundles with trivial determinant. Recently, Kondo–Wakabayashi show that the generic degree of V V , considered as a function on the characteristic of the base field, is a quasi-polynomial. In this paper, we show that this quasi-polynomial is indeed a polynomial, and we write out this polynomial explicitly.



1.
Introduction

Let
X
be a smooth curve in characteristic
p>2
.
Let
F_{X}:X\to X^{\prime}
be the relative Frobenius morphism over
k
.
Let
M_{2}(X)
and
M_{2}(X^{\prime})
be the moduli space of stable rank 2 vector bundles of trivial determinant on
X
and
X^{\prime}
respectively.
Taking the Frobenius pullback
E\mapsto F_{X}^{*}E
defines a generically finite rational map
V:M_{2}(X^{\prime})\dashrightarrow M_{2}(X)
, called the
generalized Verschiebung

[
13
, Theorem A.6]
.
The behavior of this rational map has been studied extensively; see, e.g.

[
2
,
8
,
9
,
13
,
6
,
7
,
5
,
4
]
.

When
X
is a general curve of genus
g=2
, the generic degree of
V
is
\deg(V)=\frac{p^{3}+2p}{3}
, as shown by Osserman
[
13
, Theorem 1.3]
and Lange–Pauly

[
7
, Corollary]
.
For higher genus, Kondo–Wakabayashi recently expressed the generic degree in
terms of certain edge-numberings on a graph
[
3
, Theorem 5.3]
.
They also show that

\deg(V)
, as a function of the characteristic
p
, is given by a
quasi-polynomial in
p
of degree
3g-3

[
3
, Theorem 5.5]
.
They then use direct computation to show that this quasi-polynomial is a polynomial for
g=3

[
3
, Theorem C]
.

The first result of this paper shows that the quasi-polynomial
\deg(V)
is always a polynomial in
p
for arbitrary genus
g\geq 2
.

Theorem 1

(Polynomiality)
.

Let
X
be a general curve of genus
g\geq 2
over an algebraically closed field of characteristic
p>2
.
Let
R_{g}(t)\in\mathbb{Q}[t]
be the following polynomial:

R_{g}(t)=1-\frac{1}{2^{g}}+\frac{1}{2^{g}}\sum_{r=0}^{g-1}\frac{(-1)^{r-1}2^{4r}B_{2r}}{(2r)!}\left[z^{-2r}\right]\csc^{2g-2}(z)\,t^{r},

where
B_{2r}
denotes the
2r
-th Bernoulli number, and
\left[z^{-2r}\right]\csc^{2g-2}(z)
denotes the coefficient of
z^{-2r}
in the Laurent expansion of
\csc^{2g-2}(z)
at
z=0
.
Then

\deg(V)=p^{g-1}R_{g}(p^{2}).

The proof relies on a new combinatorial result in Wakabayashi’s
enumerative geometry of
dormant opers
, see
[
17
]
and

[
18
, §1]
for an illuminating introduction of the related ideas.

Namely, given a finite connected trivalent graph
G
of genus
g
, and two natural numbers
P\geq 3
and
N\geq 1
, Kondo–Wakabayashi define a finite set
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}
of certain edge-numberings on
G
and show that

(1)

\deg(V)=\frac{|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,2,G}|}{|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,1,G}|}.

The set
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}
is recalled in Definition
8
.
When
N=1
, the set
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}
, implicit already in works of S. Mochizuki, is given by the lattice points in a dilation of a polytope defined by Liu-Osserman in
[
10
, Definition 2.3]
.
Our combinatorial result reduces the level-
N
edge-numbering sets to the level-1 counterparts.

Theorem 2

(Level-reduction)
.

Let
P\geq 3
be an odd integer and
N\geq 1
be an integer, and let
G
be a finite trivalent graph.
Let
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}
be the set of
(P,N)
-edge-numberings of
G
as in Definition
8
.
Then, there is a natural bijection

\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}\cong\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}\times\operatorname{Ed}_{2P,1,G}^{N-1}.

Combinatorial background

In his work on
p
-adic Teichmüller Theory
[
11
]
, S. Mochizuki linked the nowadays so-called
dormant opers
to the uniformization problem of
p
-adic curves, and showed that the number of dormant opers in characteristic
p
is given by
|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,1,G}|
, see Liu-Osserman’s summary in
[
10
, Theorem 3.9]
.

By Ehrhart Theory
[
15
, Theorem 4.6.8]
, the number
|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|
of elements of
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}
, considered as a function of
P
, is a priori a
quasi-polynomial
.
That is, a function
f:\mathbb{Z}\to\mathbb{R}
of the form
f(n)=\sum_{i=0}^{M}c_{i}(n)n^{i}
, where each
c_{i}(n)
is a periodic function for
n
.
Liu-Osserman show the polynomiality of
|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|
for odd
P
in
[
10
, Corollary 3.6]
.
In
[
1
, Theorem 5]
, the polynomiality of
|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|
for even
P
is shown by Fernandes–de Pina–Ramírez Alfonsín–Robins.

The derivation of the explicit formulas for the odd and even polynomials have a different flavor.
The polynomial for
|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|
when
P
is odd is given by Wakabayashi in
[
16
, Theorem A]
as

|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|=\frac{P^{g-1}}{2^{2g-1}}\sum_{\theta=1}^{P-1}\csc^{2g-2}\!\left(\frac{\pi\theta}{P}\right)\hskip 20.00003pt(P\ \mathrm{odd}).

His proof passes through Quot schemes and Gromov-Witten Theory.
In the proof of Theorem
1
(see (
13
)), we give an elementary derivation of the corresponding formula for even
P
:

|\operatorname{Ed}_{P,1,G}|=\left(\frac{P}{2}\right)^{g-1}+\left(\frac{P}{4}\right)^{g-1}\sum_{\theta=1}^{P/2-1}\csc^{2g-2}\!\left(\frac{\pi\theta}{P}\right)\hskip 20.00003pt(P\ \mathrm{even}).

Remark 3
.

Theorem
2
and (
1
) give us
\deg(V)=|\operatorname{Ed}_{2p,1,G}|
.
Therefore, we have the following heuristic observation, which we find amusing:
The generic degree
\deg(V)
in characteristic
p
agrees with the number of dormant opers over a nonexistent “field of characteristic
2p
”.

2.1.
The set
{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
as in
[
3
, §5.1]

For integers
P\geq 2
and
N\geq 1
, let
{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
denote the set of triples
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{3}
such that

(2)

a_{1}+a_{2}+a_{3}\leq P^{N}-2,\hskip 10.00002pt|a_{2}-a_{3}|\leq a_{1}\leq a_{2}+a_{3},

and such that, for every
1\leq M\leq N
, if we let
[a_{i}]_{M}
be the remainder of
a_{i}
modulo
P^{M}
, then there are choices

(3)

a_{i,M}\in\{[a_{i}]_{M},\,P^{M}-1-[a_{i}]_{M}\}

such that

(4)

a_{1,M}+a_{2,M}+a_{3,M}\leq P^{M}-2,\hskip 10.00002pt|a_{2,M}-a_{3,M}|\leq a_{1,M}\leq a_{2,M}+a_{3,M}.

When
N=1
or when
M=N
, we can choose
a_{i,N}=a_{i}
and the condition (
4
) is redundant.

Note that the definition of
{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
is independent of the ordering of the triple
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.

We record a lemma that will be useful later on. The proof is straightforward.

Lemma 4
.

The condition (
2
) is equivalent to the following four inequalities

\displaystyle 2P^{N}-(2a_{1}+1)-(2a_{2}+1)-(2a_{3}+1)

\displaystyle>0,

\displaystyle-(2a_{i}+1)+\sum_{j\neq i}(2a_{j}+1)

\displaystyle>0,\hskip 10.00002pti=1,2,3.

Example 5
.

{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(3)
is the singleton
\{(0,0,0)\}
.

{}^{\dagger}C_{2}(3)
is

\{(0,0,0)\}\cup S_{3}\cdot(2,2,0)\cup\{(2,2,2)\}\cup S_{3}\cdot(3,2,2)\cup S_{3}\cdot(3,3,0),

where
S_{3}\cdot x
means the orbit of
x
under the action of
S_{3}
by permuting the coordinates.

{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(6)
is

\{(0,0,0)\}\cup S_{3}\cdot(1,1,0)\cup\{(1,1,1)\}\cup S_{3}\cdot(2,1,1)\cup S_{3}\cdot(2,2,0).

Note that, in this example, we have a bijection
{}^{\dagger}C_{2}(3)\cong{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(3)\times{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(6)
.
More explicitly, this bijection sends every instance of 2 in
{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(6)
to
3\times\frac{2}{2}+0
in
{}^{\dagger}C_{2}(3)
and every instance of 1 in
{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(6)
to
3\times\frac{1+1}{2}-1-0
.
Below we show that this bijection generalizes.

Definition 6
.

For each
(n,b)\in\mathbb{Z}^{2}
, let
\Phi(n,b)\in\mathbb{Z}
be

(5)

\Phi(n,b)=\begin{cases}\frac{n}{2}P^{N-1}+b,&n\ \text{even},\\
\frac{n+1}{2}P^{N-1}-1-b,&n\ \text{odd}.\end{cases}

Let
\Phi^{(3)}:\mathbb{Z}^{3}\times\mathbb{Z}^{3}\to\mathbb{Z}^{3}
be the function that sends
((n_{i})_{i=1,2,3},(b_{i})_{i=1,2,3})
to
(\Phi(n_{i},b_{i}))_{i=1,2,3}
.

Lemma 7

(The local factorization lemma)
.

Assume that
P\geq 3
is odd and
N\geq 2
, then
\Phi^{(3)}
restricts to a bijection

(6)

\Phi^{(3)}:{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)\times{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)\xrightarrow{\sim}{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P).

Proof.

Let us first show that the map is well-defined.
Given
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)
and
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
, we need to show that
(\Phi(n_{i},b_{i}))_{i=1,2,3}\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
.

Let us check that (
4
) is satisfied for
M<N
.
In this case, combining (
3
) and (
5
), we see that
\Phi(n_{i},b_{i})_{M}
is either
[b_{i}]_{M}
or
P^{M}-1-[b_{i}]_{M}
.
Therefore, (
4
) is satisfied for
(\Phi(n_{i},b_{i}))_{i=1,2,3}
since it is satisfied for
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
.

We now check that (
2
) is also satisfied for
(\Phi(n_{i},b_{i}))_{i=1,2,3}
.
Note that
2\Phi(n_{i},b_{i})+1=P^{N-1}n_{i}+w_{i}
, where
w_{i}=2b_{i}+1
if
n_{i}
is even, and
w_{i}=P^{N-1}-(2b_{i}+1)
if
n_{i}
is odd.
By Lemma
4
, the condition (
2
) for
(\Phi(n_{i},b_{i}))_{i=1,2,3}
is satisfied if and only if we have

(7)

\displaystyle\begin{split}P^{N-1}(2P-2-\sum_{i=1}^{3}n_{i})+\Big(2P^{N-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}w_{i}\Big)&>0,\\
P^{N-1}(-n_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}n_{j})+\Big(-w_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}w_{j}\Big)&>0,\quad i=1,2,3.\end{split}

To continue the argument, we set up the following notation.
Let
H_{0}=2P^{N-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}(2b_{i}+1)
, and let
H_{i}=-(2b_{i}+1)+\sum_{j\neq i}(2b_{j}+1)
.
By Lemma
4
, the condition that
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
is equivalent to the positivity of the
H_{i}
’s.
We first observe that
H_{i}<2P^{N-1}
for
i=0,1,2,3
. Indeed,
H_{0}=2P^{N-1}-\sum_{i=1}^{3}(2b_{i}+1)<2P^{N-1}
.
For
i=1,2,3
, we have that

H_{i}=2(-b_{i}+\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j})+1\leq 1+2\sum_{j\neq i}b_{j}\leq 1+2(P^{N-1}-2)<2P^{N-1}.

Now consider the four inequalities in (
7
).
All the terms in the first brackets are non-negative, since

(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)
.
Moreover, all four first-bracket terms
have the same parity as
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
.

The following table records the four second-bracket terms in
(
7
). In the rows involving
i,j,k
, the indices

i,j,k
are distinct elements of
\{1,2,3\}
, and the four entries are listed
in the respective order.

\begin{array}[]{c|c}\text{parity pattern}&\text{four second-bracket terms}\\
\hline\cr n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\text{ all even}&(H_{0},H_{1},H_{2},H_{3})\\
n_{i},n_{j}\text{ odd and }n_{k}\text{ even}&(H_{k},H_{j},H_{i},H_{0})\\
n_{i}\text{ odd and }n_{j},n_{k}\text{ even}&(P^{N-1}-H_{i},\ P^{N-1}-H_{0},\ P^{N-1}-H_{k},\ P^{N-1}-H_{j})\\
n_{1},n_{2},n_{3}\text{ all odd}&(P^{N-1}-H_{0},\ P^{N-1}-H_{1},\ P^{N-1}-H_{2},\ P^{N-1}-H_{3}).\end{array}

Thus, when
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is even, the second-bracket terms are a permutation
of
(H_{i})_{i=0,1,2,3}
; when
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is odd, they are a permutation of

(P^{N-1}-H_{i})_{i=0,1,2,3}
.

If
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is even, then every second-bracket term is one of the positive
numbers
H_{i}
, so all four inequalities in (
7
) hold.
If
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is odd, then every first-bracket term is at least
1
, while
every second-bracket term is of the form
P^{N-1}-H_{i}>-P^{N-1}
, since

H_{i}<2P^{N-1}
. Hence the corresponding left hand side is strictly positive
in this case as well.

Above shows that
\Phi^{(3)}
sends
{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)\times{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
to
{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
. We now construct its inverse.

Take an arbitrary element
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
.
By (
3
) and (
4
) for
M=N-1
, we can
choose

b_{i}\in\{[a_{i}]_{N-1},\,P^{N-1}-1-[a_{i}]_{N-1}\}

such that
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
satisfies (
4
) with
M=N-1
.

We claim that
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
. Indeed, it remains
only to check (
3
) and (
4
) for
M<N-1
.
For such
M
, we have
\{[b_{i}]_{M},\,P^{M}-1-[b_{i}]_{M}\}=\{[a_{i}]_{M},\,P^{M}-1-[a_{i}]_{M}\}
.
Therefore the choices that work for
(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
also work for

(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})
.

Moreover, the choice of each
b_{i}
is unique. To see this, note that
(
2
) implies that every coordinate of an element of
{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
is at most
\frac{P^{N-1}-3}{2}
.
Indeed, if
(x_{1},x_{2},x_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
, then

x_{i}\leq\sum_{j\neq i}x_{j}
and
\sum_{i}x_{i}\leq P^{N-1}-2
, hence
2x_{i}\leq P^{N-1}-2
. Since
P^{N-1}
is odd, this gives the desired
bound.
Therefore, among the two numbers
[a_{i}]_{N-1}
and
P^{N-1}-1-[a_{i}]_{N-1}
,
at most one can be a coordinate of an element of

{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
.

We can now define
n_{i}
so that
a_{i}=\Phi(n_{i},b_{i})
.
Namely, let

n_{i}=\begin{cases}2\frac{a_{i}-[a_{i}]_{N-1}}{P^{N-1}},&b_{i}=[a_{i}]_{N-1},\\[6.0pt]
2\frac{a_{i}-[a_{i}]_{N-1}}{P^{N-1}}+1,&b_{i}=P^{N-1}-1-[a_{i}]_{N-1}.\end{cases}

It remains to show that
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)
, i.e., the first brackets in the four inequalities in (
7
) are all non-negative.
The argument is the reverse of the argument for well-definedness.

Since

(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
, Lemma
4
gives the positivity of the four inequalities in (
7
). Suppose,
for contradiction, that one of the terms in the first brackets in
(
7
) is negative.

If
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is even, then every term in the first brackets is even, so
the negative first-bracket terms are
\leq-2
. We have already shown that
H_{i}<2P^{N-1}
.
Thus the corresponding left hand side in (
7
) is
strictly negative, a contradiction.

If
n_{1}+n_{2}+n_{3}
is odd, then every term in the first brackets is odd, so the
negative first-bracket terms are
\leq-1
. In this case, the corresponding second bracket
is
P^{N-1}-H_{i}<P^{N-1}
. Again the corresponding left hand side in
(
7
) is strictly negative, a contradiction.

Therefore, we have found the unique
(n_{1},n_{2},n_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2P)
and
(b_{1},b_{2},b_{3})\in{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
, which defines
\Phi^{(3),-1}(a_{1},a_{2},a_{3})
.
∎

2.2.
The set
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}

For us, a finite graph
G
consists of finite sets
V(G)
,
E(G)
, and
H(G)
of
vertices, edges, and half-edges, together with maps

\nu:H(G)\to V(G)
and
\epsilon:H(G)\to E(G)
,
such that every fiber of
\epsilon
has cardinality
2
.
Multiple edges and loops are allowed.
If the two half-edges over an edge
e
have the same image under
\nu
, then

e
is a loop.
For a vertex
v
, the set
H_{v}=\nu^{-1}(v)

is the set of branches incident to
v
, counted with multiplicity.
A graph is trivalent if
|H_{v}|=3
for every vertex
v
.
In particular, a loop at
v
contributes two of the three incident branches.
Let
G
be a finite trivalent graph with edge set
E(G)
.

Definition 8
.

[
3
, §5.1]

Let
\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}
be the set of functions

\ell:E(G)\to\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}

such that, at every vertex
v
of
G
, the three incident branch labels form a
triple in
{}^{\dagger}C_{N}(P)
.

Example 9
.

Let
G=K_{4}
be the complete graph with 4 vertices.
Then
\operatorname{Ed}_{3,1,G}
is the singleton where every edge is numbered 0.
Direct calculation shows that
|\operatorname{Ed}_{3,2,G}|=|\operatorname{Ed}_{6,1,G}|=49
.
Moreover, every edge is numbered
0,2
, or
3
by
\operatorname{Ed}_{3,2,G}
and every edge is numbered
0,1,
or
2
by
\operatorname{Ed}_{6,1,G}
. Changing each
2
to
3=\Phi(2,0)
and
1
to
2=\Phi(1,0)
defines a bijection
\operatorname{Ed}_{6,1,G}\times\operatorname{Ed}_{3,1,G}\cong\operatorname{Ed}_{3,2,G}
.
The same idea proves Theorem
2
as below.

Proof of Theorem
2
.

By iteration, it suffices to prove the one-step version

\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N,G}\cong\operatorname{Ed}_{2P,1,G}\times\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N-1,G}.

Indeed, this follows by applying Lemma
7
edge-wise on
G
.
The inverse construction in Lemma
7
is
coordinatewise. More explicitly, for an edge
e
, let
b(e)
be the unique element of

\{[\ell(e)]_{N-1},\ P^{N-1}-1-[\ell(e)]_{N-1}\}

which can occur as a coordinate of an element of
{}^{\dagger}C_{N-1}(P)
;
equivalently, the unique element of this set which is at most
(P^{N-1}-3)/2
.
Then define

n(e)=\begin{cases}2\frac{\ell(e)-[\ell(e)]_{N-1}}{P^{N-1}},&b(e)=[\ell(e)]_{N-1},\\[6.0pt]
2\frac{\ell(e)-[\ell(e)]_{N-1}}{P^{N-1}}+1,&b(e)=P^{N-1}-1-[\ell(e)]_{N-1}.\end{cases}

Applying Lemma
7
at every vertex shows that

(e\mapsto n(e))\in\operatorname{Ed}_{2P,1,G}
and

(e\mapsto b(e))\in\operatorname{Ed}_{P,N-1,G}
.
∎

3.
Polynomiality

By
[
3
, Theorem 5.3]
, the generic degree
\deg(V)
of the generalized Verschiebung map is given by
\frac{|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,2,G}|}{|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,1,G}|}
, where
G
is any genus
g
connected finite trivalent graph.
By Theorem
2
, we have that

(8)

\deg(V)=|\operatorname{Ed}_{2p,1,G}|.

The goal of this section is to write out an explicit polynomial of
p
for
|\operatorname{Ed}_{2p,1,G}|
.
The idea is closely related to Gromov-Witten Theory, and is probably known to the experts.
For example, in
[
16
, Theorem A]
, Wakabayashi counts dormant opers using the Vafa-Intriligator formula and gives explicit expression for
|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,1,G}|
in terms of trigonometric sums.
However, we decide to give a streamlined derivation of the formula, assuming only some basic enumerative combinatorics.

We start by taking the graph
G
to be built up from blocks of the following form, where the edges will be numbered by
a,x,y,b\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
respectively:

a
b
x
y

For example, when
g=5
, we take
G
to be the following graph:
a_{2}
a_{3}
a_{4}
a_{1}

For each block, and fixed numberings
0\leq a,b\leq p-1
, let

T_{ab}=|\{(x,y):\;(a,x,y),(b,x,y)\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2p)\}|

be the number of ways to choose
x
and
y
so that this block obeys the conditions of
\operatorname{Ed}_{2p,1,G}
.
Let
T=(T_{ab})_{0\leq a,b\leq p-1}

be the corresponding
p\times p
matrix. Then

(9)

|\operatorname{Ed}_{2p,1,G}|=\sum_{a_{1},\ldots,a_{g-1}=0}^{p-1}T_{a_{1}a_{2}}T_{a_{2}a_{3}}\cdots T_{a_{g-1}a_{1}}=\operatorname{Tr}(T^{g-1}).

The last equality follows from some facts of transfer matrices in enumerative combinatorics as in
[
15
, §4.7.1]
. Indeed, consider
the auxiliary weighted directed graph with vertex set
\{0,\ldots,p-1\}
, in
which the edge
a\to b
has weight
T_{ab}
. Then the above summation is
the total weight of closed walks of length
g-1
, which is

\operatorname{Tr}(T^{g-1})
by
[
15
, first line of the proof of Corollary 4.7.3]

We now find
\operatorname{Tr}(T^{g-1})
by diagonalizing
T
.
For this purpose, we introduce the following notation:

N_{ab}^{c}=\begin{cases}1,&(a,b,c)\in{}^{\dagger}C_{1}(2p),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Let us remark that this looks very similar to the even part of the fusion ring as in
[
14
, (1.1)]
.

Observe that

(10)

T_{ab}=\sum_{x,y=0}^{p-1}N_{ax}^{y}N_{bx}^{y}.

The next lemma turns
N_{ab}^{c}
into a trigonometric sum.

Lemma 10
.

For
1\leq j\leq p
, set

S_{aj}=\begin{cases}\sqrt{\frac{2}{p}}\,\sin\!\left(\frac{(2a+1)j\pi}{2p}\right),&1\leq j\leq p-1,\\[6.0pt]
\frac{(-1)^{a}}{\sqrt{p}},&j=p.\end{cases}

Then,

(a)

The matrix
S=(S_{aj})_{0\leq a\leq p-1,\ 1\leq j\leq p}

is orthogonal.

(b)

(11)

N_{ab}^{c}=\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{S_{aj}S_{bj}S_{cj}}{S_{0j}}.

Proof.

Set
\theta_{j}=\frac{j\pi}{2p}

(a)
Since
2\sin X\sin Y=\cos(X-Y)-\cos(X+Y)
, we have that, for
1\leq j,k\leq p-1
,

\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}S_{aj}S_{ak}=\frac{1}{p}\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\left[\cos((2a+1)(\theta_{j}-\theta_{k}))-\cos((2a+1)(\theta_{j}+\theta_{k}))\right].

Now use the arithmetic progression cosine formula

\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}\cos((2a+1)u)=\frac{\sin(2pu)}{2\sin u}
,
whenever
\sin u\neq 0
. In our case,
u=(j-k)\pi/(2p)
, or
u=(j+k)\pi/(2p)
. Therefore, the sum vanishes unless
j=k
.
If
j=k
, the first cosine sum is
p
, and the second still vanishes. Therefore,
\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}S_{aj}S_{ak}=\delta_{jk}
.
For the last column, we have that
S_{a,p}=\frac{(-1)^{a}}{\sqrt{p}}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{p}}\sin((2a+1)\pi/2)
.
The same product-to-sum argument gives
\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}S_{a,p}S_{a,k}=0\hskip 20.00003pt(1\leq k\leq p-1)
,
and clearly
\sum_{a=0}^{p-1}S_{a,p}^{2}=1
.

(b).
We claim that for every
1\leq j\leq p
,

(12)

\sum_{c=0}^{p-1}N_{ab}^{c}\sin((2c+1)\theta_{j})=\frac{\sin((2a+1)\theta_{j})\sin((2b+1)\theta_{j})}{\sin\theta_{j}}.

Because the condition is symmetric in
a,b
, assume
a\geq b
.

First suppose
a+b\leq p-1
. Then the possible values for
c
are
a-b,\ a-b+1,\ldots,a+b
, so that

\sum_{c}\sin((2c+1)\theta_{j})=\sum_{r=0}^{2b}\sin((2(a-b+r)+1)\theta_{j})=\frac{\sin((2b+1)\theta_{j})\sin((2a+1)\theta_{j})}{\sin\theta_{j}},

where we have used the arithmetic progression sine formula for the last equality.

If
a+b\geq p
, then the possible values for
c
are
a-b,\ a-b+1,\ldots,2p-2-a-b
, and we can conclude using the same idea as above.

Now (
12
) give us

\sum_{c=0}^{p-1}N_{ab}^{c}S_{cj}=\frac{S_{aj}S_{bj}}{S_{0j}}
for every
1\leq j\leq p
.
When
j=p
, the same formula becomes
\sum_{c}N_{ab}^{c}(-1)^{c}=(-1)^{a+b}
, which is also true.
Indeed, from above we see that the admissible
values of
c
form an interval of odd length starting with
a-b
, hence
\sum_{c}(-1)^{c}=(-1)^{a-b}=(-1)^{a+b}
.

Finally, we can apply the orthogonality of
S
to conclude (b).
∎

Combining (

\displaystyle T_{ab}

\displaystyle=\sum_{x,y=0}^{p-1}N_{ax}^{y}N_{bx}^{y}=\sum_{x,y=0}^{p-1}\left(\sum_{j=1}^{p}\frac{S_{aj}S_{xj}S_{yj}}{S_{0j}}\right)\left(\sum_{k=1}^{p}\frac{S_{bk}S_{xk}S_{yk}}{S_{0k}}\right)

Therefore, if we set
D=\operatorname{diag}\left(S_{01}^{-2},\ldots,S_{0p}^{-2}\right)

T=SDS^{t}

\operatorname{Tr}(T^{g-1})=\sum_{j=1}^{p}S_{0j}^{-2(g-1)}

By symmetry about

4.
Dormant opers

Opers

Let
X
be a smooth projective curve over an algebraically closed field
k
of genus
g>1
.
Let
\omega_{X}
be the canonical sheaf of
X
.

In this paper, by opers, we mean what are usually called
\operatorname{PGL}_{2}
-opers.
Namely, an oper over
X
is a
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle
\pi:P\to X
together with a connection
\nabla
, i.e., a splitting of the tangent exact sequence

0\longrightarrow T_{P/X}\longrightarrow T_{P}\mathrel{\begin{subarray}{c}\xrightarrow{\ d\pi\ }\\[-2.58334pt]
\xleftarrow[\ \nabla\ ]{}\end{subarray}}\pi^{*}T_{X}\longrightarrow 0,

such that there is a section
\sigma:X\to P
so that we have an induced isomorphism
d\sigma-\sigma^{*}\nabla:T_{X}\to\sigma^{*}T_{P/X}
.

Note that the choice of
\sigma
is unique up to isomorphism if it exists.
Indeed, if
\sigma^{\prime}
is another such section, then
\sigma^{\prime}
determines a nonzero section of the bundle
\sigma^{*}T_{P/X}
, contradicting the negativity of the degree of
\sigma^{*}T_{P/X}\cong T_{X}
.

Uniformization

As explained in
[
12
, §1]
, one
motivation for the introduction of opers is that, over
\mathbb{C}
, every
X
has a canonical oper over it. Indeed, let
\mathbb{H}\subset\mathbb{C}
be the upper half plane and
\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{H})\subseteq\operatorname{Aut}(\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}})
be the representation associated to the universal cover of
X
.
Then the
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle on
X
given by the quotient of
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
by the diagonal action of
\pi_{1}(X)
carries a canonical oper structure: the connection is given by descending the trivial connection on
\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
and the section
\sigma
is given by descending the tautological section
\mathbb{H}\to\mathbb{H}\times\mathbb{P}^{1}_{\mathbb{C}}
.

One is interested in studying how special the canonical oper is. For example, the associated representation
\pi_{1}(X)\to\operatorname{PGL}_{2}(\mathbb{C})
is real-valued.
A natural way to imitate this property in the
p
-adic setting is to require the oper to admit some kind of Frobenius structure.
In turn, in characteristic
p
, this requirement boils down to the nilpotency of the
p
-curvature.
Among the opers with nilpotent
p
-curvatures, the ones with
zero

p
-curvatures have been particularly studied.

Dormant opers

An oper
(P,\nabla)
on
X
is called dormant if its
p
-curvature
\psi(\nabla)
vanishes.
Equivalently, by Cartier descent and the étale local triviality of
\operatorname{PGL}
-torsors, this means that the pair
(P,\nabla)
is étale locally isomorphic to the constant
\mathbb{P}^{1}
-bundle together with the canonical connection.

Over
W_{N}(k)

Let
W_{N}(k)
be the
N
-th ring of Witt vectors.
The definition of opers as above also makes sense for a lift
\widetilde{X}
over
W_{N}(k)
of
X
.
The dormancy of an oper over
\widetilde{X}
is treated by Wakabayashi in
[
18
]
.
The definition is somewhat involved, but it is shown in
[
18
, Corollary 3.12]
to be equivalent to the étale-local constancy condition
(P,\nabla_{P})\cong(\mathbb{P}^{1}\times\widetilde{X},\nabla^{\mathrm{can}})
as above.

Counting dormant opers

It is shown by Wakabayashi in
[
18
, Theorem D, Theorem E]
that, for a general
X
with a lift
\widetilde{X}
over
W_{N}(k)
, the number
|\operatorname{Ed}_{p,N,G}|
is the same as the number of dormant opers over
\widetilde{X}
.
Therefore, the oper-theoretic corollary of our Theorem
2
is:

Corollary 11
.

Let
X
be a general curve of genus
g>1
over an algebraically closed field
k
together with a lift
\widetilde{X}
over
W_{N}(k)
.
Let
\operatorname{Op}^{Z}_{X}
and
\operatorname{Op}_{\widetilde{X}}^{Z}
be the set of isomorphism classes of dormant opers over
X
and
\widetilde{X}
respectively.
Then, we have the following equality:

|\operatorname{Op}_{\widetilde{X}}^{Z}|=|\operatorname{Op}^{Z}_{X}|\cdot\deg(V)^{N-1}.
