Electric Teichmüller spaces and k k -multicurve graphs
Kento Sakai

Abstract
. Masur and Minsky showed that the curve graph is quasi-isometric to the Teichmüller space electrified along its thin part, and hence the Teichmüller space is weakly relatively hyperbolic with respect to the thin part. In this paper, we extend this result to the k k -multicurve graph by electrifying the Teichmüller space along the thin part where the extremal length of k k curves is sufficiently small. A key ingredient is a bound on the k k -multicurve graph distance in terms of the intersection number, which is obtained by adapting the upper bound for the pants graph due to Lackenby and Yazdi.



1.
Introduction

The
Teichmüller space
of a surface is the space of conformal (equivalently, hyperbolic) structures on the surface, up to isotopy.
In low-dimensional topology, the mapping class group is a fundamental object, and it is often studied in close connection with Teichmüller space.
To describe the large-scale and asymptotic geometry of Teichmüller space, a number of quasi-isometric combinatorial models have been proposed.
The
curve graph
, introduced by Harvey
[
9
]
, has been a topic of interest in low-dimensional topology.
The vertices of the curve graph are isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves on the surface, and two vertices are joined by an edge if the corresponding curves can be realized disjointly.
Masur and Minsky
[
18
]
showed that the curve graph is a hyperbolic metric space.

Masur and Minsky also proved that the curve graph is quasi-isometric to the metric space obtained by electrifying (or coning off) Teichmüller space along its thin part
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
.
Here the thin part consists of those Riemann surfaces on which the extremal length of some essential simple closed curve is at most a fixed constant
\epsilon_{0}>0
.
Each component of the thin part is quasi-isometric to a product of metric spaces
[
21
]
, and hence provides an obstruction to the Teichmüller space being hyperbolic.
In particular, Teichmüller space is weakly relatively hyperbolic with respect to the thin part.

In this paper, we extend
[
18
, Theorem 1.2]
to the
k
-multicurve graph
, introduced in
[
6
]
.
Let
\Sigma
be a surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures.
The vertices of the
k
-multicurve graph
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
are
k
-multicurves on
\Sigma
; that is, multicurves consisting of exactly
k
pairwise disjoint curves, and two distinct vertices are joined by an edge if the corresponding multicurves contain the same
(k-1)
-multicurve and the remaining two complementary curves intersect minimally (see
definition

2.1
for details).
For a
k
-multicurve
\alpha
, let
\mathrm{Thin}_{\alpha}
be the subset of the Teichmüller space
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
consisting of Riemann surfaces on which each component of
\alpha
has the extremal length at most
\epsilon_{0}
.
Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma)
denote the metric space obtained by electrifying
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
along the collection
\{\mathrm{Thin}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)}
.
The main purpose of this paper is to show the following.

Theorem A
.

The electrified Teichmüller space
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma)
is quasi-isometric to the
k
-multicurve graph
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
.

Vokes introduced the notion of
twist-freeness
for graphs of multicurves, and proved that any twist-free graph of multicurves is a hierarchically hyperbolic space
[
24
]
.
Consequently, the general theory of hierarchically hyperbolic spaces
[
1
,
2
]
yields many large-scale geometric properties of twist-free graphs of multicurves
[
24
,
23
]
.
In particular, hyperbolicity and quasi-flat rank are determined by the number of pairwise disjoint
witnesses
for the graph of multicurves, where a witness is a connected subsurface that essentially intersects every vertex of the graph of multicurves.
In
[
13
]
, an explicit formula is given for the maximal number
m(g,n,k)
of pairwise disjoint witnesses for
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma_{g,n})
:

m(g,n,k)=\begin{cases}\min\left\{\left\lfloor\frac{-\chi(\Sigma)}{a(\xi(\Sigma)+1-k)}\right\rfloor,\left\lfloor\frac{\xi(\Sigma)+1}{\xi(\Sigma)+2-k}\right\rfloor\right\}&(n,k)\neq(0,1)\\
1&(n,k)=(0,1),\end{cases}

where
a(x)=\lceil(2x+1)/3\rceil
,
\chi(\Sigma)=2-2g-n
and
\xi(\Sigma)=3g-3+n
.
As an immediate consequence, we obtain the following.

Corollary B
.

If
m(g,n,k)=1
, then
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
is weakly relatively hyperbolic with respect to the collection
\{\mathrm{Thin}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)}
.

Corollary C
.

Let
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma_{g,n})
denote the electrified Teichmüller space along the collection
\{\mathrm{Thin}_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)}
.

(1)

Hyperbolic case.

\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma_{g,n})
is hyperbolic if and only if
m(g,n,k)=1
.

(2)

Relatively hyperbolic case.

\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma_{g,n})
is relatively hyperbolic if and only if

•

g
is even,
n
is even and at least
2
, and
k=(3g+n)/2
,

•

g
is even,
n=0
, and
k\in\{3g/2,(3g+2)/2\}
,

•

g
is odd,
n\in\{0,2\}
, and
k=(3g+3)/2
, or

•

g
is odd,
n
is odd and at least
3
, and
k=(3g+n)/2
.

(3)

Thick case.

\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma_{g,n})
is thick if and only if neither (1) nor (2) holds.

Corollary D
.

The quasi-flat rank of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}^{k}(\Sigma_{g,n})
is equal to
m(g,n,k)
.

To prove
A
, we establish an upper bound on the distance between two vertices of
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
in terms of the intersection number of the corresponding multicurves.

Theorem E
.

For any
k
-multicurves
\alpha,\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
, we have

d_{\mathcal{C}^{[k]}}(\alpha,\beta)\leq 6\cdot 4^{6g-6+2n-2k}i(\alpha,\beta)^{2}+f(k),

where
f(k)=\min\{k,\xi(\Sigma)-k\}
.

Our proof of
E
uses the quadratic upper bound on pants graph distance in terms of intersection number due to Lackenby and Yazdi
[
14
]
.
In the latter part of
[
14
]
, they also obtain a sharper bound that grows only logarithmically with the intersection number.
By using this, one can also derive an upper bound of the
k
-multicurve graph distance in terms of a logarithmic function of the intersection number; however, the quadratic upper bound suffices to show
A
, and so we adopt it for simplicity.

2.
Preliminaries

2.1.
Metric geometry

Let
(X,d_{X})
and
(Y,d_{Y})
be metric spaces.
A map
f\colon X\to Y
is called a
quasi-isometry
if the following conditions hold.

(1)

There exist constants
K\geq 1,L>0
such that, for any
x_{1},x_{2}\in X
,

\frac{1}{K}d_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})-L\leq d_{Y}(f(x_{1}),f(x_{2}))\leq Kd_{X}(x_{1},x_{2})+L.

(2)

There exists
C>0
such that every point of
Y
lies within distance
C
of
f(X)
.

If a map
f\colon X\to Y
satisfies the condition (1), it is called a
quasi-isometric embedding
.
The
quasi-flat rank
of a metric space
X
is the largest integer
n
for which there exists a quasi-isometric embedding
\mathbb{Z}^{n}\to X
.

We recall the definition of an electrified metric space, following
[
7
]
.
Let
X
be a geodesic metric space, and let
\mathcal{H}=\{H_{\alpha}\}_{\alpha\in A}
be a family of connected subsets of
X
.
For each
\alpha\in A
, we introduce a new point
v_{\alpha}
and join every point of
H_{\alpha}
to
v_{\alpha}
with a segment of length
1/2
.
We denote the resulting space by
\hat{X}
, and equip
\hat{X}
with the path metric
d_{e}
.
We refer to the construction of the metric space
(\hat{X},d_{e})
as
electrifying
(or
coning off
)
X
along
\mathcal{H}
.

2.2.
Multicurve graphs

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,n}
be a surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures.
A simple closed curve on
\Sigma
is said to be
essential
if it is not homotopic to a point or to a puncture.
A
k
-multicurve
on
\Sigma
is a set of isotopy classes of pairwise disjoint essential simple closed curves on
\Sigma
whose cardinality is
k
, where
1\leq k\leq 3g-3+n
.
In particular, a
(3g-3+n)
-multicurve on
\Sigma
is called a
pants decomposition
of
\Sigma
.

We define the
k
-multicurve graph
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
, which was introduced in
[
6
]
.

Definition 2.1
.

The
k
-multicurve graph

\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
is the graph whose vertices are
k
-multicurves on
\Sigma
.
Two
k
-multicurves
\alpha
and
\beta
are joined by an edge if there exists a common
(k-1)
-multicurve
\nu\subset\alpha\cap\beta
such that the two isotopy classes
\alpha\setminus\nu
and
\beta\setminus\nu
intersect minimally on
\Sigma\setminus\nu
.
More precisely,

•

if
k<3g-3+n
, then
i(\alpha\setminus\nu,\beta\setminus\nu)=0
;

•

if
k=3g-3+n
and one of the connected components of

\Sigma\setminus\nu
is a once-holed torus, then

i(\alpha\setminus\nu,\beta\setminus\nu)=1
;

•

if
k=3g-3+n
and one of the connected components of

\Sigma\setminus\nu
is a four-holed sphere, then

i(\alpha\setminus\nu,\beta\setminus\nu)=2
.

We endow the
k
-multicurve graph
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
with the graph metric in which each edge has length
1
.
We denote the distance function of
\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
by
d_{\mathcal{C}^{[k]}}
.
Since the
(3g-3+n)
-multicurve graph is the same as the pants graph
\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
,
the distance function of the
(3g-3+n)
-multicurve graph is often denoted by
d_{\mathcal{P}}
.

2.3.
Quadratic differentials and measured foliations

We recall the construction of the measured foliation associated to a holomorphic quadratic differential (see, for example
[
8
]
).
Let
X
be a Riemann surface homeomorphic to
\Sigma
, and
let
q
be a holomorphic quadratic differential on
X
with finite
L^{1}
-norm.
Away from zeros of
q
, there is a holomorphic coordinate
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
in which
q=d\zeta^{2}
.
Such a coordinate is uniquely determined up to
\zeta\mapsto\pm\zeta+c
, and hence the horizontal lines
\{\eta=\text{constant}\}
define a foliation on
X
.
A zero of
q
of order
m
gives an
(m+2)
-pronged singularity of this foliation.
Moreover, the density
|d\eta|
defines a transverse measure on the singular foliation of
q
, and the resulting measured foliation is called the
horizontal measured foliation
of
q
and denoted by
\mathcal{F}_{h}(q)
.
It is known that the map
q\mapsto\mathcal{F}_{h}(q)
is a homeomorphism
[
11
]
.
Therefore, for each
k
-multicurve
\alpha
, we can choose a holomorphic quadratic differential
q_{\alpha}
whose horizontal measured foliation corresponds to
\alpha
.

2.4.
Extremal length and Teichmüller space

Let
\Sigma=\Sigma_{g,n}
be a surface of genus
g
with
n
punctures.
We suppose that
2-2g-n<0
.
Let
X
be a Riemann surface homeomorphic to
\Sigma
.
We define the
extremal length

\Ext_{X}(\alpha)
of a multicurve
\alpha
with respect to
X
by

\Ext_{X}(\alpha)=\sup_{\rho}\frac{\ell_{\rho}(\alpha)^{2}}{\Area(\rho)},

where the supremum is taken over all conformal metrics
\rho
on
X
.
It is well known that extremal length extends continuously to a function on the space of measured foliations on
\Sigma
.

The
Teichmüller space

\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
of
\Sigma
is the space of isotopy classes of punctured Riemann surface structures on
\Sigma
.
Let
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
be Riemann surfaces.
For each quasiconformal map
f\colon X\to Y
, the
maximal dilatation
K(f)
is defined by

K(f)=\frac{1+\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}}{1-\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}},

where
\|\mu_{f}\|_{\infty}
is the
L^{\infty}
-norm of the Beltrami differential
\mu_{f}
of
f
.
Then we define a distance function
d_{\mathcal{T}}
on
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
as

d_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)\coloneqq\frac{1}{2}\inf_{f\sim\id}\log K(f).

The distance
d_{\mathcal{T}}
is called the
Teichmüller distance
of
\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
.
For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, there exists a unique extremal map for maximal dilatation in the isotopy class of the identity map on
\Sigma
, which is called the
Teichmüller map
.
Let
f\colon X\to Y
be the Teichmüller map with dilatation
K
.
Then there exists a unique holomorphic quadratic differential
q
on
X
such that
f
is represented by the map
\xi+i\eta\mapsto K\xi+i\eta
in the canonical coordinates
\zeta=\xi+i\eta
associated with
q
.
Then we denote
Y
by
X_{q,K}
.
The one-parameter family
\{X_{q,e^{2t}}\}_{t\geq 0}
of Riemann surfaces is called the
Teichmüller geodesic
in the direction of
q
, which is parametrized by arc length with respect to the Teichmüller distance.

We now recall a formula for the Teichmüller distance, known as Kerckhoff’s formula.

Theorem 2.2

(Kerckhoff’s formula
[
12
]
)
.

For any
X,Y\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, we have

e^{-2d_{\mathcal{T}}(X,Y)}=\min_{\gamma\in\mathcal{MF}(\Sigma)}\frac{\Ext_{Y}(\gamma)}{\Ext_{X}(\gamma)},

where the minimum is attained by the vertical measured foliation of the holomorphic
quadratic differential associated with the Teichmüller map between
X
and
Y
.

2.5.
Length of curves

For any
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
and any
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
, let
\ell_{X}(\gamma)
denote the length of the geodesic representative of
\gamma
with respect to the hyperbolic metric in the conformal class determined by
X
.
Bers proved the following theorem.

Theorem 2.3

(
[
3
]
)
.

For every
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, there exists a pants decomposition
P\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
whose curves all have length at most
B_{g,n}
in the hyperbolic metric associated with
X
, where
B_{g,n}
depends only on
g
and
n
.

The constant
B_{g,n}
is called the
Bers’ constant
.
From
theorem

2.3
, we immediately have the following corollary.

Corollary 2.4
.

Let
k
be an integer with
1\leq k\leq 3g-3+n
.
Then, for every
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, there exists a
k
-multicurve
\alpha=\{\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{k}\}\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
such that
\ell_{X}(\alpha^{i})\leq B_{g,n}
for each
\alpha^{i}\in\alpha
.

As for a relation between extremal length and hyperbolic length, Maskit’s inequality is well known.

Theorem 2.5

(
[
16
]
)
.

For any
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
and any
\gamma\in\mathcal{C}(\Sigma)
, the following inequality holds:

\Ext_{X}(\gamma)\leq\ell_{X}(\gamma)e^{\ell_{X}(\gamma)/2}.

From
corollaries

2.4
and
2.5
, we obtain the following corollary.

Corollary 2.6
.

Let
k
be an integer with
1\leq k\leq 3g-3+n
.
For every
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, there exists a
k
-multicurve
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
with
\Ext_{X}(\alpha)\leq\delta_{0}
,
where
\delta_{0}
is a constant depending only on
g
and
n
.

Proof.

For every
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
, using
corollary

2.4
, we take a
k
-multicurve
\alpha=\{\alpha^{1},\ldots,\alpha^{k}\}\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
with
\ell_{X}(\alpha^{i})\leq B_{g,n}
for each
\alpha^{i}\in\alpha
.
By definition of extremal length, we can take a conformal metric
\rho
with

\Ext_{X}(\alpha)\leq\frac{\ell_{\rho}(\alpha)^{2}}{\Area(\rho)}+1.

For this
\rho
, we have

\displaystyle\frac{\ell_{\rho}(\alpha)^{2}}{\Area(\rho)}

\displaystyle=\frac{\left(\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{\rho}(\alpha^{i})\right)^{2}}{\Area(\rho)}

\displaystyle\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\frac{\ell_{\rho}(\alpha^{i})^{2}}{\Area(\rho)}

\displaystyle(\text{Cauchy-Schwartz})

\displaystyle\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\Ext_{X}(\alpha^{i})

\displaystyle\leq k\sum_{i=1}^{k}\ell_{X}(\alpha^{i})e^{\ell_{X}(\alpha^{i})/2}

\displaystyle(\text{\lx@cref{creftypecap\lx@tilde refnum}{theorem:Maskit'sLemma}})

\displaystyle\leq(3g-3+n)^{2}B_{g,n}e^{B_{g,n}/2}.

∎

We will use the following inequality later.

Lemma 2.7

(Minsky’s inequality
[
20
]
)
.

Let
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}\in\mathcal{MF}(\Sigma)
be measured foliations on
\Sigma
, and
X\in\mathcal{T}(\Sigma)
. Then,

i(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})^{2}\leq\Ext_{X}(\gamma_{1})\Ext_{X}(\gamma_{2}).

3.
Inequality between intersection numbers and distance

3.1.
Inequality for pants graph distance

Hempel
[
10
]
showed that the distance
d_{\mathcal{C}}(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
between any two curves
\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2}
in the curve graph is bounded above by a logarithmic function of their geometric intersection number
i(\gamma_{1},\gamma_{2})
.
This result is often referred to as Hempel’s lemma.
To prove Hempel’s lemma, he used a surgery argument for curves.
However, this surgery argument does not apply to the analogous problem in the
k
-multicurve graph, in particular, in the pants graph.
On the other hand, Lackenby and Yazdi
[
14
]
established an inequality showing that the distance
d_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{1},P_{2})
between two pants decompositions
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
is bounded above by a logarithmic function of the intersection number
i(P_{1},P_{2})
.

Theorem 3.1

(
[
14
, Theorem 1.1]
)
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}\in\mathcal{P}(\Sigma)
be pants decompositions on
\Sigma
.
Then there exists a constant
C>0
such that

d_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{1},P_{2})\leq C(1+\log i(P_{1},P_{2})),

where
C
depends only on
\chi(\Sigma)
.
Moreover
C=O(|\chi(\Sigma)|^{2})
as
|\chi(\Sigma)|\to\infty
.

Furthermore, as an intermediate step in the proof of
theorem

3.1
, Lackenby and Yazdi derived the following estimate, whose upper bound is a quadratic function of the intersection number, but which is more explicit and simpler than the bound in
Theorem

3.1
.

Theorem 3.2

(
[
14
, Corollary 3.16]
)
.

Let
P_{1},P_{2}
be pants decompositions of
\Sigma
.
Then

(3.1)

d_{\mathcal{P}}(P_{1},P_{2})\leq 6i(P_{1},P_{2})^{2}.

Remark 3.3
.

The right-hand side of
eq.

3.1
appears implicitly in the arguments in Section 3 of
[
14
]
.
In
Appendix

A
, we discuss the explicit upper bound.

3.2.
Inequality of
k
-multicurve graph distance

Lemma 3.4
.

Let
k
and
l
be integers with
1\leq k,l\leq 3g-4+n
.
For any
k
-multicurve
\alpha\in\mathcal{C}^{[k]}(\Sigma)
and any
l
-multicurve
\beta\in\mathcal{C}^{[l]}(\Sigma)
, there exists a
(k+1)
-multicurve
\tilde{\alpha}\in\mathcal{C}^{[k+1]}(\Sigma)
such that
\alpha\subset\tilde{\alpha}
and

i(\tilde{\alpha},\beta)\leq 2\,i(\alpha,\beta).

Proof.

Choose representatives
a
and
b
of the multicurves
\alpha
and
\beta
that realize minimal intersection number in their homotopy classes, and regard them as subsets of
\Sigma
.
Since
k\leq 3g-4+b
, there exists a connected component
X
of
\Sigma\setminus a
that is not a pair of pants.
Consequently, each connected component of
X\cap b
is a subarc of the curve
b
or a closed curve which is a component of
b
.
Let
c_{1},\ldots,c_{m}\subset b
be all connected components of
X\cap b
.
Then the number
m
of connected components of
X\cap b
is at most the geometric intersection number

.
Finally, capping off every disk component of
\Sigma\setminus N_{j}
, we obtain a subsurface
F_{j}
.
For each
j
, let
\gamma_{j}
denote the multicurve (namely, a set of isotopy classes of essential simple closed curves) consisting of all boundary components of
F_{j}
that are essential in
\Sigma
, where parallel simple closed curves are identified
[
14
, Construction 3.10]
.
Then, the union
\bigcup_{j=1}^{i}\gamma_{j}
gives a pants decomposition of
\Sigma

[
14
, Lemma 3.13]
.
We denote the pants decomposition by
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O})
.

If
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
is obtained from an ordering
\mathrm{O}
of the edges of
\mathcal{T}
by swapping two consecutive indices, then the distance between
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O})
and
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O}^{\prime})
is bounded above by a uniform constant
C

[
14
, Lemma 3.18]
.
Now let
\mathrm{O}
and
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
be arbitrary orderings of the edges of
\mathcal{T}
.
Since any permutation of
i
elements can be written as a product of at most

1+\cdots+(i-1)=i(i-1)/2
adjacent transpositions,
\mathrm{O}
can be transformed into
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
by at most
i(i-1)/2
adjacent transpositions, where
i
is the number of edges of
\mathcal{T}
.
Therefore, the distance between
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O})
and
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O}^{\prime})
is at most
Ci(i-1)/2

[
14
, Corollary 3.19]
.

Let
P
and
P^{\prime}
be pants decompositions of
\Sigma
, and assume that they are in minimal position.
If
P
and
P^{\prime}
share some components, then by cutting
\Sigma
along the common curves and restricting to the resulting subsurfaces, we may assume without loss of generality that
P
and
P^{\prime}
have no common component.
Let
\mathcal{T}_{P\cup P^{\prime}}
be the pre-triangulation obtained by superimposing
P
and
P^{\prime}
.
Then the number of edges of
\mathcal{T}_{P\cup P^{\prime}}
is at most
2\,i(P,P^{\prime})
.
Moreover, there exist orderings
\mathrm{O}
and
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
of the edges of
\mathcal{T}_{P\cup P^{\prime}}
such that
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}_{P\cup P^{\prime}},\mathrm{O})=P
and
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T}_{P\cup P^{\prime}},\mathrm{O}^{\prime})=P^{\prime}
.
Therefore,

(A.1)

\begin{split}d_{\mathcal{P}}(P,P^{\prime})&\leq C\cdot\frac{2\,i(P,P^{\prime})(2\,i(P,P^{\prime})-1)}{2}\\
&=C\bigl(2\,i(P,P^{\prime})^{2}-i(P,P^{\prime})\bigr).\end{split}

Thus, finding the constant
C
explicitly, we obtain an inequality between the pants graph distance and the intersection number.
By the following proposition, we see that the best possible constant
C
is
3
.

Proposition A.2
.

Let
\mathcal{T}
be a pre-triangulation of
\Sigma
.
Let
\mathrm{O}
and
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
be orderings of edges of
\mathcal{T}
.
Suppose that
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
is obtained by swapping two consecutive indices of
\mathrm{O}
.
Then,

d_{\mathcal{P}}(\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O}),\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O}^{\prime}))\leq 3.

Proof.

Following the argument in the proof of
[
14
, Lemma 3.18]
, we verify this proposition.
Let
\mathrm{O}
be an ordering of edges of
\mathcal{T}
and let
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
be the ordering obtained by swapping
j
-th and
(j+1)
-th edges.
We write

\mathrm{O}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{j},e_{j+1},\ldots,e_{i})\text{ and }\mathrm{O}^{\prime}=(e_{1},\ldots,e_{j+1},e_{j},\ldots,e_{i}).

Let
\{N_{k}\}_{k=1}^{i}
and
\{F_{k}\}_{k=1}^{i}
be the sequences of subsurfaces associated with
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathrm{O}
, and let
\{N^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{i}
and
\{F^{\prime}_{k}\}_{k=1}^{i}
be those associated with
\mathcal{T}
and
\mathrm{O}^{\prime}
.
For simplicity, we denote
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O})
and
\mathbf{P}(\mathcal{T},\mathrm{O}^{\prime})
by
P
and
P^{\prime}
, respectively.
By the construction of subsurfaces, we find that
F_{k}=F_{k}^{\prime}
if
|k-j|\geq 1
.
Therefore, every curve in the symmetric difference
P\triangle P^{\prime}
is contained in the subsurface

F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}=F^{\prime}_{j+1}\setminus F^{\prime}_{j-1}.

Since
\chi(F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1})\geq-2

[
14
, Lemma 3.12]
, the number of connected components of
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
that are neither a disk, an annulus, or a pair of pants is at most one.
After discarding disks and annuli in connected components of
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
, the subsurface
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
is either

(1)

an essential four-holed sphere, an essential once-holed torus, an essential twice-holed torus or

(2)

a disjoint union of at most two essential pairs of pants.

If
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
is of type (2), the pants decompositions
P
and
P^{\prime}
coincide.
Thus, we may assume that
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
is of type (1).
Let
N
denote the subsurface
N_{j-1}=N^{\prime}_{j-1}
.
If at least one of
e_{j}
and
e_{j+1}
is a simple closed curve, the subsurface
F_{j+1}\setminus F_{j-1}
is of type (2).
If at least one endpoint of
e_{j}
or
e_{j+1}
is disjoint from
N
, then
\partial N_{j}
and
\partial N^{\prime}_{j}
can be isotoped to be disjoint.
Therefore, we may assume that every endpoint of
e_{j}
and
e_{j+1}
lies in
N
.
In this case, the ways in which
e_{j}
and
e_{j+1}
attach to
N
are shown in

.

d_{\mathcal{P}}(P,P^{\prime})

Theorem A.4

For any two pants decompositions

d_{\mathcal{P}}(P,P^{\prime})\leq 6i(P,P^{\prime})^{2}-3i(P,P^{\prime}).
