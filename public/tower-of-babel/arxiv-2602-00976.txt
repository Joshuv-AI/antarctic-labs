Knots with large character varieties
Philip Choi, Joan Porti, Seokbeom Yoon

Abstract
. We study knots whose SL 2 ​ ( ℂ ) \mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbb{C}) -character varieties have a component of dimension greater than one. We call such knots 𝒳 \mathcal{X} -large and introduce two diagrammatic constructions that produce 𝒳 \mathcal{X} -large knots. The first construction uses split link diagrams and rational tangle replacements, providing a topological explanation for most 𝒳 \mathcal{X} -large knots observed in knot tables. The second construction is based on braids and orientation-reversing involutions, and is motivated by a detailed analysis of the knot 10 123 10_{123} , also known as the Turk’s head knot T ​ h ​ ( 3 , 5 ) Th(3,5) . In particular, this approach applies to Turk’s head knots T ​ h ​ ( p , q ) Th(p,q) with p p and q q odd, leading us to conjecture that all such knots are 𝒳 \mathcal{X} -large. In doing so, we also present a non-orientable analogue of Thurston’s theorem giving a lower bound on the dimension of character varieties of non-orientable 3-manifolds.



1.
Introduction

Let
K
be a knot in
S^{3}
. Here and throughout the paper, we distinguish knots from links: a knot has a single component, whereas a link has more than one component. We denote by
\mathcal{G}(K):=\pi_{1}(S^{3}\setminus K)
the knot group of
K
, and by
\mathcal{X}(K)
the
\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})
-character variety of
\mathcal{G}(K)
,

\mathcal{X}(K):=\mathrm{Hom}(\mathcal{G}(K),\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C}))/\!/_{\mathrm{SL}_{2}(\mathbbm{C})}.

Character varieties of knot groups have been extensively studied in the literature. However, most existing results focus on the case in which
\mathcal{X}(K)
has dimension one. This is partly because, when
K
is hyperbolic, the geometric representation lies in a one-dimensional component of
\mathcal{X}(K)
, and partly because many knots (particularly those with few crossings) appear to have one-dimensional character varieties.

In this paper, we study knots
K
for which
\dim\mathcal{X}(K)>1
. Here,
\dim
denotes the (complex) dimension of a component of maximal dimension. We call such knots
\mathcal{X}
-large
. It is well known that if
K
is
\mathcal{X}
-large, then
K
is large, meaning that its complement has a closed essential surface
[
4
]
. However, the converse does not hold: there exist large knots that are not
\mathcal{X}
-large, such as
8_{16}
and
8_{17}

[
12
]
. According to computational data in
[
9
]
, obtained in joint work of the first author with Seonhwa Kim on parabolic representations, there are only three candidates in Rolfsen’s table with at most 10 crossings that can be
\mathcal{X}
-large:
10_{98}
,
10_{99}
, and
10_{123}
(see Appendix
A
for details). One motivation of this paper is to determine whether these knots are indeed
\mathcal{X}
-large and, if so, to provide a topological explanation for this phenomenon, rather than relying on brute-force computations of character varieties. To the author’s knowledge, existing related results (for instance,
[
5
,
13
,
3
]
) do not apply to these knots.

A main goal of the paper is to introduce two new diagrammatic constructions that produce
{\mathcal{X}}
-large knots.

The first construction uses split link diagrams and 2-tangle replacements. We present this construction explicitly in Section
2.2
(Theorem
2.1
), after explaining the key idea using the knot
10_{98}
as a toy example in Section
2.1
. This construction is simple yet powerful: it produces infinitely many
\mathcal{X}
-large knots from a fixed link diagram. In fact, most
\mathcal{X}
-large knots observed in our computation arise from this construction. For example, it provides a diagrammatic explanation of why
10_{98}
and
10_{99}
are
\mathcal{X}
-large.
Unfortunately, at present we do not know of a similar explanation for
10_{123}
; however, our second construction applies to
10_{123}
.

The second construction uses braids and orientation-reversing involutions. It was motivated by the observation that the knot
10_{123}
admits a braid presentation that decomposes into two parts, one of which is conjugate to the other by an orientation-reversing involution (see Figure
6
). We present a detailed analysis of
10_{123}
in Section
3.1
and then generalize this construction in Section
3.2
(Theorem
3.5
). This construction is applied in particular to Turk’s head knots
Th(p,q)
with
p
and
q
odd, which leads us to conjecture that all such Turk’s head knots are
\mathcal{X}
-large (Conjecture
3.6
).
We also present a variant of Theorem
3.5
that applies to strongly positive amphichiral knots (Theorem
3.7
).

The proof of Theorem
3.5
requires a non-orientable analogue of Thurston’s theorem giving a lower bound on the dimension of character varieties
[
16
, Theorem 5.6]
. Roughly speaking, Thurston’s theorem asserts that under mild assumptions, a (connected) orientable 3-manifold with
k
boundary tori has a character variety of dimension at least
k
. For Theorem
3.5
, we require a corresponding statement for non-orientable 3-manifolds with Klein bottle boundary.
To the best of the authors’ knowledge, such a non-orientable version does not appear elsewhere in the literature. We therefore postpone the proof of Theorem
3.5
to Section
4
, where we formulate and prove the dimension estimation in the non-orientable setting (Theorem
4.4
).

Using our constructions, we are able to account for all
\mathcal{X}
-large knots observed in our computations. In particular, as mentioned above, we confirm that
10_{98}
,
10_{99}
, and
10_{123}
are
\mathcal{X}
-large. Consequently, we obtain the following statement:

Up to 10 crossings, the only
\mathcal{X}
-large knots are
10_{98}
,
10_{99}
, and
10_{123}
.

This naturally leads to the question of whether our constructions capture all
\mathcal{X}
-large knots up to a certain crossing number. However, we do not attempt to address this question in this paper, as determining the list of
\mathcal{X}
-large knots requires computing full character varieties and their dimensions, which is extremely difficult even for knots with 11 crossings.

Rational tangle replacements

2.2.
General construction

In this section, we generalize the tangle replacement discussed in the previous section by using an arbitrary rational 2-tangle diagram instead of the specific one shown in Figure
2

Given a diagram of a knot or link,
replacing a crossing
c
with a 2-tangle diagram

means removing a small disc containing
c
and inserting
R
into it, while keeping the exterior of the disc unchanged; see Figure

(resp.,

) if they are connected by the over-pass (resp., under-pass) of

-closure
of

as the knot or link obtained from

by joining the two

-points, creating one crossing outside the disc in which the
o
-points (resp.,

-points) are connected by the over-pass (resp., under-pass); see Figure

-closure of the 2-tangle diagram in Figure

(right) is
3_{1}

.

Let

Let

We now consider the given diagram of the split link

Braids and orientation-reversing involutions

Lemma 3.1
.

There exists a subvariety
U\subset\mathcal{X}^{\mathrm{irr}}(F_{3})
whose dimension is at least 2 and such that each point
[G_{1},G_{2},G_{3}]\in U

\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}=\mathrm{tr}\,G_{2}=\mathrm{tr}\,G_{3},\quad\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{2}G_{3}\neq\pm 2,\quad\text{and}\quad[H_{1},H_{2},H_{3}]=[G_{3}^{-1},G_{2}^{-1},G_{1}^{-1}].

(5)

Proof.

Here the variables

and

are related to

\displaystyle(x,y,z)=(\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{2},\ \mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{3},\ \mathrm{tr}\,G_{2}G_{3}),

\displaystyle b=\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}(\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}+\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{2}G_{3}),\quad c=4-3(\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1})^{2}-(\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{2}G_{3})^{2}-(\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1})^{3}\mathrm{tr}\,G_{1}G_{2}G_{3}.
