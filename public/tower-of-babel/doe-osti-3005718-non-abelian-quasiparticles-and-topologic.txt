 3. Executive summary

 The research performed on this proposal lead to significant progress in understanding
 spectra of charge carrier holes in spin 3/2 valence bands in two-dimensional systems and
 topological phenomena in these systems; resulted in enhanced knowledge of topological
 excitations, such as Majorana fermions and parafermions in electron and hole systems;
 lead to new breakthroughs in understanding quantum Hall systems, which is an important
 breeding ground for topological excitations; contributed to understanding of several
 aspects of theory of spin-orbit interactions; and understanding of hybrid
 superconductor/semiconductor structures. Methods and techniques used or developed in
 the research were effective in achieving its results. Some of the developed methods have
 been used by the research community. The achieved results are beneficial for both
 theorists and experimentalists in condensed matter and related areas of research,
 developed knowledge is important for progress in quantum science and technology, and
 therefore is of benefit to the public.

 4. What are the major goals of the project?

The project involved three stages, in 2013-2016, 2016 -2019 and 2019-2024 funding periods.
The first two stages of 2013-2019 included the effort to investigate topological and non-abelian
phenomena in systems with angular momentum (spin) 3/2. These two stages allowed to
formulate several general problems of the field of non-abelian phenomena in condensed matter
systems. On the third stage, several of these problems were addressed.

The principal objectives of the first two stages included:

 1. Advance theory of spectra, interactions and correlations of charge carrier holes in
 nanostructures, including the effects of magnetic field and strain. These features are
 absent in electronic systems but are specific to holes and can have profound impact
 on non-Abelian physics.
 2. Theoretical investigation of paired and parafermion Fractional Quantum Hall effect
 states in 2D hole liquid. By numerically simulating two dimensional holes (2DH) in
 001 grown GaAs via exact diagonalization, we expect to find the optimal setting for
 the non-Abelian fractional quantum Hall states.
 3. Theoretical investigation of novel correlated Quantum Hall states in the vicinity of
 crossings in 2D holes energy spectrum. Investigation of hole charge density wave states
 in high Landau levels, and their competition with other quantum Hall states at the
 crossing points.
 4. Theoretical study of Majorana Fermion excitations in hole nanowires proximity
 coupled to s-wave superconductors.
 5. Establishing the non-Abelian or Abelian nature of several quantum Hall hole states by
 extending the investigation of the fractional quantum Hall effect to a larger number of

 particles in exact diagonalization studies, using the density matrix renormalization
 group method, and by evaluating entanglement entropy.
 6. Investigating charge carrier hole settings leading to generation and control of Fibonacci
 anyon quasiparticles.
 7. Developing theory and methods of simulation of edge states in hole systems.
 Investigating how electric field, strain and in-plane magnetic fields affect edge states,
 by using exact diagonalization studies in disc geometry at various filling factors.
 Examine tunneling between edge states
 8. Investigating Majorana modes in hybrid superconductor/epitaxial or core-shell
 nanowire hole systems.
 9. Uncovering new materials and nanostructures with chiral spectra and unusual
 correlations in the charge carrier hole and related systems

The principal objective of the second stage of the proposal continued to be uncovering new non‐
Abelian physics settings aimed at suggesting experimentally realistic paths to generate and
control non‐Abelian excitations. The specific objectives were:

10. Investigating high-degeneracy superconducting order parameters for realization of high-order
non-abelian excitations
11. Investigating proximity superconducting order parameter for contacts of an s-superconductor
with topological insulators, charge carrier hole spin-3/2 systems.
12. Investigating the effects of in-plane magnetic field in quantum wells in superconducting
proximity systems
13. Calculation of interference effects in transport in superconducting proximity systems
14. Developing theory and methods of simulation of proximity superconductivity to fractional
quantum Hall edge states
15. Developing theory of domain walls of fractional quantum Hall effect spin
ferromagnets.

 5. What was accomplished under these goals?

 1. G. E. Simion and Y. B. Lyanda-Geller, Theory of magnetic field spectral crossings of
 Luttinger holes in quantum wells

Over several decades, understanding spectra of holes in spin 3/2 degenerate valence band in a
magnetic field has been a challenging problem. In quantum wells, the problem becomes even
more non-trivial due to a strong coupling of spin, in-plane motion of holes and their motion in
the direction of spatial quantization. We have achieved significant progress in understanding
quantization of the magnetic field spectrum of quantum well holes described by Luttinger
Hamiltonian. The principal result is finding numerous crossings between various hole levels,
including those in the ground state of holes. Our results account for coupling of cyclotron motion

with spatial quantization and spin, which represents the crucial effect of mutual transformation
between heavy and light holes upon reflection from heteroboundaries. These effects were largely
omitted by researchers earlier. We found an analytical method to include these effects non-
perturbatively, which significantly affects the spectrum compared to inclusion of perturbative
admixture of the hole states.
The following results are important: determination of g-factors of holes in a high magnetic field,
Determination and understanding of the cyclotron mass, in particular, the proof that for high
Landau indices the cyclotron mass coincides with in-plane hole mass in the absence of magnetic
field; theory of the Dresselhaus spin-orbit interactions in a high magnetic field, including non-
trivial $k_z^3$ term, full account of the effects of anisotropy (warping) of hole spectra. Finally,
we determined the origin of beats in Shubnikov-De Haas oscillations, and show that
experimentally observed features can arise from Luttinger terms alone, in the absence of the
Dresselhaus and Rashba interactions.

2. G. E. Simion and Y. B. Lyanda-Geller Theory of the fractional quantum Hall effect in hole
systems

One of the principal goals of the proposed research has been the prediction and
understanding of new states of matter for charge carrier holes, particularly new Fractional
Quantum Hall effect states. As a very important step in this direction, we proposed a new method
of mapping states of holes confined to a finite width quantum well in a perpendicular magnetic
field to states in a spherical shell geometry. Unusual hole spectra in magnetic field arise from
strong coupling between the in-plane and spatial quantization $z-$direction motion in a quantum
well, caused by strong spin-orbit interactions. Holes wavefunctions are four-spinors and their
in-plane and $z-$direction degrees of freedom are inseparable, making the treatment of
Coulomb interactions very challenging. For electrons, the in-plane and z-direction motion are
independent, so it is possible to use the Haldane technique of homogeneous states with
translationally invariant wavefunctions for a finite number of electrons on a sphere in a
monopole magnetic field. This method cannot be applied to holes, hence we developed the
method of spherical shell geometry.

Using the spherical shell geometry, we calculated two-hole pseudopotentials and computed
corrections to the two-hole pseudopotentials due to Landau
level mixing (more precisely, due to mixing of confined hole levels in a magnetic field).
Furthermore, we calculated three-hole pseudopotentials
and their irreducible components. These pseudopotentials are then used in exact diagonalization
of finite size hole systems. Evaluating Coulomb interactions matrix elements for holes is much
more complex than for electrons. While the electron wavefunction on the sphere in the absence
of spin-orbit coupling has a single component, the hole wavefunction has 16 components

(4 angular momenta and 4 spin states). Interaction matrix elements contain summation over all
angular momenta and spin states of two pairs of states. As a result, there are 65536 "electron-
like" matrix elements for holes instead of a single matrix element
for electrons. For the hole wavefunction in a spherical shell, angular and transverse radial part of
the wavefunction
are coupled due to spin-orbit interactions, and therefore these 65536 matrix elements contain
double integral over radial components of the two interacting holes. Such integration is absent in
the electron case. These features characterizing charge carrier hole interactions make the
evaluation of pseudopotentials for holes and account of Landau level mixing a computationally
intensive task. We were able to overcome these challenges.

Compared to electrons, holes have smaller cyclotron energy and stronger Landau level mixing by
Coulomb interactions. Single-hole magnetic spectra exhibit multiple level crossings, particularly
in the ground state. Near spectral crossings, a single quantum well hole system
mimics the physics of electron bilayers. Around crossings, ratio of interaction and cyclotron
energies can change significantly with a magnetic field, and interaction pseudopotentials can be
easily tuned. Therefore, hole systems are much more controllable than electron systems. We
have demonstrated that these features make hole system promising for
observation of non-Abelian excitations and transition between non-Abelian and Abelian states.
In particular, we applied our spherical shell method for investigation of the quantum Hall effect
at half filling of the hole ground state in a magnetic field.

3. G. E. Simion and Y. B. Lyanda-Geller, Non-Abelian matter in the fractional quantum Hall
effect of holes at ν =1/2.

Shayegan's group observed a unique fractional quantum Hall effect of holes at half-filling of the
ground state of holes in magnetic field. Using calculated pseudopotentials, we considered a
system of 8, 10 and $12 holes at 1/2 filling factor.
Exact diagonalization shows an angular momentum J=0 ground state separated by a gap
from the continuum of states, a clear indication of an incompressible state. We have
demonstrated that in the presence of mixing of hole levels in a magnetic field the overlap of the
wavefunction of the state of holes with the Moore-Read Pfaffian state is up to 0.7, raising the
possibility that charge carrier hole excitations at filling factor 1/2 present an
example of non-Abelian matter.

We have computed the spectrum of pair excitations of this 1/2 filling factor state. They are
obtained either by adding or removing a flux quantum, leading to states containing a pair of
quasiholes or a pair of quasielectrons. The appearance of excitations in pairs is yet another
indication that the FQHE hole state is the state of paired composite Fermions, such as the Moore-

Read state. The overlap of the wavefunctions of the quasihole hole excitations with Moore-Read
quasiholes is ~ 0.65, also in favor of non-Abelian character of the
hole ν=1/2 quantum Hall state.

We also tested whether it is possible that the ν=1/2 state represents the Halperin 331 state. The
overlap of the calculated hole wavefunctions with the wavefunction of the 331 state found by
Haldane and Rezayi is ~ 0.17 in the broad range of fields around the crossing in the hole spectra,
making the331 state highly unlikely.

4. Jingcheng Liang and Yuli Lyanda-Geller, Charge carrier holes and Majorana fermions

In search of Majorana zero modes in hybrid superconductor-semiconductor structures, the key
problem is achieving significant strength of p-pairing due to spin-orbit interactions. We have
demonstrated that hole GaAs and InSb systems exhibit stronger p-pairing compared to electron
systems. While InSb electrons have strong spin-orbit coupling constants γ
the characteristic spin-orbit energy 𝛾 2 𝑚 is also defined by the electron mass, which is very small
in InSb.In hole-type InSb and GaAs systems, spin-orbit constants are comparable, but masses are
bigger. We found optimal conditions for realization of Majorana fermions in quantum well-based
nanowires. The combination of values of g-factor, chemical potential, and strength of spin-orbit
interactions, as well as favorable conditions for superconducting proximity effect due to surface
defect levels inside the semiconductor bandgap, makes these systems a promising experimental
setting.

We advanced an understanding of the criterion for realizing Majorana modes and topological
superconductivity in hybrid settings with spin-orbit interactions. Spin-orbit effects in hole wires
lead to momentum-dependent Zeemann fields in all three spatial directions. This situation can
also take place in electron settings. We have demonstrated that zero energy Majorana mode in
this case exists despite the Bogoliubov-De Gennes equation cannot have real solutions, and no
choice of the phase of the superconducting order parameter can decouple real and imaginary
parts of the BdG wavefunctions.

5,6,7 G.E. Simion, Y.B. Lyanda-Geller, Study of Entanglement entropy of the quantum Hall
states, DMRG studies of the quantum Hall states. Investigating charge carrier hole settings
leading to generation and control of Fibonacci anyon quasiparticles. Manipulation of fractional
quantum Hall states of charge carrier holes.

The universal aspects of the FQHE are efficiently revealed by investigating entanglement
properties of ground states. Entanglement entropy gives a measure of correlations in FQHE.
We computed the entanglement entropy for the fractional Quantum Hall State of holes near the
filling factor ½. The result indicates that entanglement entropy depends on whether only the
ground state of holes or both states at the crossing are occupied. When only the ground state is
occupied, we applied exact diagonalization and obtained entanglement entropy consistent with
the Moore-Read state. In the case of two states occupied, we developed DMRG approach for 16
particles on a spherical shell, and also obtained entanglement entropy consistent with non-
Abelian state. Changing population of states near degeneracies and manipulation of these
fractional Hall states can be achieved via in-plane magnetic field, and potentially electric fields
and strain.

When an s-type superconductor is proximity coupled to charge carrier hole system, one may
expect higher order degeneracies to emerge, such as parafermions or, if a two-dimensional array
of parafermions is achieved, the Fibonacci anyons. However, modeling of superconducting
proximity effects to quantum Hall systems, and particularly to quantum Hall hole system
demonstrated that the order parameter rapidly decays into fractional quantum Hall system,
despite there is no fundamental reason why proximity effect is impossible. Experimental
attempts to achieve proximity superconductivity in GaAs also failed so far.

In order to advance understanding of non-Abelian fractional Hall states, we undertook several

Finite size and Landau Level Mixing effects in 5/2 Fractional Hall Effect in disc
configuration

To further develop theory of the fractional quantum Hall effect in valence band hole liquid and
understand non-Abelian hole matter, it is important to understand which of the non-Abelian
states, the Moore-Read Pfaffian or the anti-Pfaffian are realized in hole systems. However, even
in electron systems, this problem is not fully resolved.
In order to develop needed codes and procedures for undertaking such study for hole systems, we
conducted exact diagonalization for electrons at the filling factor ν=5/2 taking into account
Landau level mixing on a disc and considering finite thickness of the quantum well.

Our principal result is that due to neutralizing background in the disc configuration, there is a
phase transition from the anti-Pfaffian to the Pfaffian as Landau level mixing is turned on, in
stark contrast to what is observed in a spherical geometry. This behavior is in agreement with
existing experiments in electron system, showing the appearance of the Pfaffian state at strong

Landau level mixing before the system enters a compressible phase. The inclusion of Landau
level mixing leads to an increased charge e/4 quasihole size. Landau level mixing interactions
are also shown to overcome the effects of edge reconstruction. Due to finite thickness effects,
these properties are enhanced dramatically. We also find that only the Pfaffian and anti-Pfaffian
states continue to possess energy gaps at a finite width, while gaps for compressible stripe states
close, which is in agreement with available experimental data.

We have also simulated the $\nu=5/2$ electron system on the disc in the presence of radial
magnetic field and demonstrated transition between Pfaffian, anti-Pfaffian and stripe states. This
setting can be realized in quantum Hall studies in the Corbino disc geometry.

New topological excitations in bubble phases of the quantum Hall systems

In the field of charge density wave states in high Landau levels, we succeeded in proposing an
explanation of experimental data on strain effects on resistivity of bubble phases and putting
forward the theory of new topological excitations and melting transitions in quantum Hall
systems. This work signifies importance of topology and symmetry in quantum Hall physics.

Topology and symmetry define states of matter and their responses to external forces. How
solids melt and become fluids, or how insulators become conductors is often controlled by
excitations rather than by the ground state of systems. Non-trivial topology of excitations can
alter the properties of the system. Topological excitations are notoriously difficult to predict
since they cannot be obtained as a perturbation of the ground state. We investigated Landau level
filling factors between integer and half-integer, which exhibit the re-entrant integer quantum Hall
effect (RIQHE) with vanishing longitudinal resistance and the Hall
resistance quantized to a nearest integer at lowest temperatures. Experiments by Rokhinson-
Csathy groups on samples developed by Manfra demonstrated the temperature dependent width
of RIQHE with the insulating state bounded by a sharp resistance peak separating insulating
and conducting phases in the B-T plane. The most striking feature is strain dependence of
resistivity: it changes sign across the RIQHE, i.e decreases or increases on the two sides of the
minima.

We demonstrated that the experimentally observed sign change is incompatible with the
symmetry of the bubble crystal ground state of the RIQHE. We showed that defects needed to
make the system conducting should change symmetry across RIQHE, and observed that
hedgehog and vortex-like distributions of charge density can be responsible for experimental
observations. We conducted numerical simulations of defects in a bubble crystal on a partially
filled third Landau level. We also developed analytical theory based on existence of a small
parameter λ/w, where λ is the magnetic length, and w is the lattice constant of the bubble crystal.
We explain the data, both on insulator to metal transition and on strain dependence of resistivity,

by establishing that charge excitations in the RIQHE regime are topologically non-trivial finite
size textures of electron density with charge-dependent symmetry. We find that a charge defect
(an extra electron or lack of an electron on a bubble) leads to textured deformation of electron
bubbles in its vicinity, thereby lowering its activation energy. These textures are vortices when
electron is lacking and hedgehogs for extra electron on defects. Calculations show that strain
dependence of resistivity has opposite signs for vortex and hedgehog textures that dominate two
sides of the RIQHE minima. Our calculations indicate that transition from the insulating to
conducting state is as follows. The two-electron bubble crystal at low temperatures is essentially
a Wigner crystal with defects, such as 2D hedgehogs in the vicinity of
1-electron bubble defects and vortices in the vicinity of 3-electron bubble defects, with the
predominant type of defects defined by a filling factor. At low defect densities controlled by
temperature and magnetic field, topological defects do not overlap. They attract each other at
large distances, similarly to vacancies in a Wigner crystal and repel at small distances due to
topology of charge distribution around bubble defects.
Thus, extended topological charge defects loosely form an insulating crystal. At high defect
density topological defects start to overlap, and their interactions are described by the XY-model.
Such high densities are achieved at temperatures well above the Berezinski-Kosterlitz-Thouless
transition, and the crystal of topological defects melts resulting in a sharp insulator-metal
transition. The new phase transition resembles asymptotic freedom of quarks requiring them to
be "squeezed" in order to be freed.

Investigation of a quantum phase transition from topological paired Pfaffian phase to
striped-nematic phase in the two-dimensional electron liquid

On the second 3-year stage of the project we further explored the half filling states in the
quantum Hall regime.

In collaboration with Csathy experimental group at Purdue, we investigated possible topological
paired Moore-Read states in the fractional quantum Hall systems in the second partially filled
Landau level at filling factors ν = 5/2 and 7/2. We studied ed the transition between the
topological states at ν = 5/2 and 7/2 to nematic (stripe) states and into the Fermi liquid state. The
experiments have shown that the pressure induced paired-to-nematic transition develops both at ν
= 5/2 and at ν = 7/2, demonstrating the universal nature of this transition in the second orbital
Landau level. However, it was found that pressure is not the only parameter controlling this
transition. The data suggests that the electron-electron interactions play a critical role in driving
the paired-to-nematic transition. Our numerical results indicate that the transition between
Pfaffian and nematic phases depends on the width of the quantum well w and the Landau level
mixing parameter 𝜅 = 𝑒 2 𝜆𝑚/(𝜀ℏ2 ) where e is the electron charge, 𝜆 is the magnetic length, m is
the effective mass and 𝜀 is the dielectric constant. The bigger 𝜅 favor the nematic phase; the

bigger the w, the bigger the 𝜅 needed for the transition to the nematic phase. Electron-electron
interaction in realistic 2D samples depends on these parameters as well, but at fixed m, 𝜀 and w,
the interaction depends only on the magnetic field. Then the experimental observation that the
critical point for the transition from nematic to paired state for both 5/2 and 7/2 is observed at the
same critical magnetic field, suggests that the transition is defined by essentially by the
characteristic electron-electron interaction. Then the role of the pressure can be considered as its
effect on interactions via mass, dielectric constant and density.

Investigation of the effect of the in-plane electric field on the ν=5/2 fractional quantum Hall
effect in disk geometry

It is important to understand how electric fields can shift the balance between different many-
body states, particularly non-abelian states, in various configurations in which the effects of
electric field can be important. Electric fields emerge naturally in quantum Hall systems in the
process of measurement, which requires an application of voltage. This small voltage difference
across a sample applied in experiments to probe the system has been often ignored in theoretical
studies due to the Galilean invariance in the thermodynamic limit.
No experimental sample, however, is Galilean invariant.

To understand this phenomenon, we explored the effects of the probe electric fields in a disk
geometry in electron systems with finite thickness at a filling factor ν=5/2. At this filling factor,
both non-Abelian Moore-Read state and stripe states are possible. We have found that weak
probe fields enhance the Moore-Read Pfaffian state but sufficiently strong electric fields destroy
the incompressible state. We observe that in a disk geometry, the behavior of the system depends
on the polarity of the applied radial field, which can potentially be observed in experiments using
in a Corbino disc configuration. Our simulation demonstrated that the application of such a field
enhances the coherence length of the quasi-holes propagating through the edge channels.

To explore the effects of these electric fields, we performed exact diagonalization calculations in
a disk geometry with a finite sample thickness for n=8, 10 and 12 particles. We have explored
the phase diagram of the system as the strength of the applied electric field, the Landau level
mixing strength and interactions with the neutralizing background are varied. We have found
regions where the total angular momentum makes the Pfaffian state plausible, and confirmed its
existence using overlap integrals with the known Pfaffian wavefunction. Our conclusion is that
the in-plane fields can be used to fine-tune the ground state and improve the edge properties of
the non-Abelian states.

Charge and valley currents in electron-hole system due to Berry's phases in van der Waals
materials

Spin-orbit coupling is an important ingredient of topological matter in crystalline systems. In this
regard, I explored systems with strong spin-orbit coupling beyond hole systems in III-V
materials. One such system that attracted a lot of attention is transition metal di-chalcogenides
and van der Waals heterosructures developed from those two-dimensional materials.
This system, similarly to holes in III-V semiconductors, is characterized by intrinsic magnetic
moments in spectra, related to the Berry's connection. I have developed theory of non-linear
transport in these materials, based on interference of the matrix elements of transitions between
levels, and particularly developed understanding of transverse and valley currents due to Berry's
phase effects.

Investigation of generation and relaxation of spin currents in systems with synthetic spin-
orbit interactions

Yet another direction that we explored in the context of non-abelian properties of matter was the
spin phenomena in the presence of synthetic spin-orbit coupling. Understanding the effects of
spin-orbit coupling and many-body interactions on spin transport is important in condensed
matter physics and spintronics. Creating synthetic gauge fields promises novel non-abelian gauge
field phenomena in atomic gases. In most of experimental settings interactions play key role in
physics of Bose-Einstein Condensates. They define their transport properties and dynamics of
their evolution when parameters of the system are changed. In collaboration with
experimentalists, we investigated spin-orbit Bose-Einstein Condensates were probed using both
adiabatic and abrupt switching of laser fields. These laser fields induce effective spin-orbit band
structure and their variation changes ground and excited states of the system. We explored the
effects of synthetic spin-orbit coupling on the spin transport in an atomic Bose-Einstein
condensate, where the spin-dipole mode of two interacting spin components constitutes an
alternating spin current. We have found that spin-orbit interactions significantly enhances the
damping of the spin current and reduces the thermalization and the reduction of the condensate
fraction. Theory revealed that the spin-orbit coupling modifies interference crucial role of
interaction between the spin components in spin transport. In order to model the generation of
spin current and its evolution in a condensate trap, the mean field approach via Gross-Pitaevski
equation was primarily used. Modeling of this equation describes qualitatively well main
experimental features. However analysis also shows that full quantitative microscopic
description of thermalization and relaxation of spin current requires consideration of the quantum

Boltzmann equation for the density matrix that includes scattering of particles and quasiparticles
via collision integral, beyond the force term arising from the mean field Gross-Pitaevskii
treatment of collisions.

8, 9. Investigation of a chiral spectrum in hole epitaxial and core-shell nanowires and
emergence of Majorana fermions in this system

We extended our studies of charge carrier hole wires from quantum well-based wires, such as
lithographically- or gate-defined and cleaved edge overgrowth wires, to the epitaxial growth and
core-shell nanowires. The difference between these two classes is the character of quantization of
holes. In quantum-well based wires, the in-plane width of the wire is much bigger than the
height, i.e., the quantum well width. In core-shell and epitaxially grown wires, the spatial extent
of the hole wavefunction in two directions of quantization is similar. For these wires, a mutual
transformation of the hole branches at the heteroboundaries remains important.

We have identified the regime, in which the longitudinal mass describing kinetic energy of the
hole motion along the wire in the ground state becomes infinite, so that the spectrum of the wire
is chiral, linear in momentum and spin similarly to the spectrum of topological insulators.
However, we realized that such spectrum contradicts Nielsen-Ninomiya no-go theorem, and
implies that a smaller, quartic in momentum term exists in this case in core-shell nanowires. It
remains of significant interest to explore transport and optical properties of these systems. The
change in spectrum is also expected to lead to a change of criterion for emergence of topological
superconductivity.

 10, 11, 12 ,14 and 15. J. Liang, G. Simion and Y. Lyanda‐Geller. Induced edge states, domain
walls and parafermions in the fractional quantum Hall effect spin transitions
Using numerical and field‐theoretical methods, we have studied the domain walls and the edge
states formed in spin transitions in the fractional quantum Hall effect. Exact diagonalization of
the Hamiltonian in a disk and torus geometries proves the existence of the counter‐propagating
edge modes with different spin polarizations on the boundaries between areas of the electron
liquid in polarized and unpolarized phase states at a filling factor ν=2/3. We succeeded in

numerical simulation of proximity superconductivity in an non‐uniform quantum Hall system
with finite number of particles.
Simulating superconducting coupling in the quantum Hall effect is a challenging problem. It
requires both electron and hole excitations to be present in the system, in which the number of
particles is not fixed. Applying two creation or annihilation operators to a given state would
transforms two electrons into two holes and vice versa. For simulation of parafermions we
use the Bogoliubov‐deGennes Hamiltonian that includes single particle energies (including the
Zeeman effect) for both electrons and holes, as well as electron‐electron and hole‐hole
interactions. These are the block‐diagonal, each containing a fixed number of electrons or holes.
Superconductor coupling provides matrix elements that make the Hamiltonian non‐block
diagonal. As a result, we have the block with N electrons and N holes. N‐2 electrons and N+2

Y. B. Lyanda-Geller, V. Ponomarenko, Y. Wang, and L. P. Rokhinson "Engineering

Majorana fermions are solutions of a mean-field single-particle problem, and there is a number
of physical platforms proposed to support them. Emergence of higher order non-abelian
excitations, such as parafermions or Fibonacci anyons that can realize universal quantum
computing, require strongly interacting systems. A leading contender for their realization is the
fractional quantum Hall effect, where helical channels can potentially emerge from counter-
propagating chiral modes with fractionalized charge excitations. Current theoretical approaches
assume these channels to be weakly interacting. However experimental data on transport in
helical channels in the fractional quantum Hall effect at a 2/3 filling factor shows resistance
nearly an order of magnitude smaller than expected in a weakly interacting model. We developed
a microscopic theory of strongly interacting helical states and show that a helical Luttinger liquid
emerging due to the boundary between polarized and unpolarized fractional quantum Hall phases
manifests itself as unequally populated charge, spin and neutral modes. We demonstrated that in
strong coupling picture counter-propagating modes of opposite spin polarization emerge at the
sample edges, providing a viable path for generating proximity topological superconductivity
and parafermions. Current, calculated in strongly interacting picture is in agreement with the
experiments data.

Domain walls in fractional quantum Hall ferromagnets are gapless helical one-dimensional
channels formed at the boundaries of topologically distinct polarized and un-polarized quantum
Hall liquids. Naïvely, these helical domain walls constitute two counter-propagating chiral states
with opposite spins. Coupled to an s-wave superconductor, such helical channels are expected to
lead to topological superconductivity with high order non-Abelian excitations. In this paper we
presented the results of experimental and theoretical studies of transport properties of helical
domain walls in the ν = 2/3 fractional QH regime. Experimentally measured current carried by
the helical domain wall is substantially smaller than the prediction of the naïve model. Luttinger
liquid theory of the system reveals redistribution of currents between
quasiparticle charge, spin and neutral modes, and predicts the reduction of the helical domain
wall current. Tunneling current is studied using strong coupling boundary conditions, and the
domain wall has been modeled as a single quantum point contact, two quantum point contacts on
the opposite edges, two point contacts with scattering between them and a finite length domain
wall. Remarkably, all these models yielded a universal result, predicting that tunneling of 2/3
filled state between polarized and un-polarized quantum Hall liquids leads to G=e^2/2h plateau
in the tunneling conductance. This conductance magnitude was smaller than experimentally
observed values. Inclusion of spin-non-conserving tunneling processes due to nuclei spin flip
reconciled theory with experiment. Furthermore, passing a strong electric current that resulted in
saturation of nuclear spin polarization due to Overhauser effect, which lead to suppression of
nuclei spin flips, resulted in increase of the conductance magnitude making it close to universal
theoretical prediction. Analysis of charge neutral and spin currents with chiral Luttinger liquid
model confirmed emergence of spin modes required for the formation of fractional topological
superconductivity.

A.C. Keser, Y. Lyanda-Geller, O. P. Sushkov, Nonlinear Quantum Electrodynamics in Dirac
Materials
Topological Insulators, Dirac and Weyl materials are important breeding grounds for topological
phenomena, and understanding their properties is one of the most important current research
directions. In this work, we investigated electrodynamics and magnetic and electric properties of
Dirac and Weyl materials. Classical electromagnetism is linear. However, fields can polarize the
vacuum Dirac sea, causing quantum nonlinear electromagnetic phenomena, e.g., scattering and
splitting of photons that occur only in very strong fields found in neutron stars or heavy ion
colliders. We have shown that strong nonlinearity arises in Dirac materials at
much lower magnetic fields of order of 1 Tesla and electric fields of the order of 1kV, allowing
us to explore the non-perturbative, extremely high field limit of quantum electrodynamics in
solids. We were able to explain recent experiments in a unified framework and to predict novel
nonlinear magneto-electric responses, including a magnetic enhancement of dielectric constant
of insulators and a strong electric modulation of magnetization. We proposed experiments and
discuss the applications in novel materials. We have included nonlinear contributions to all
orders by non-perturbatively analysing the Heisenberg-Euler action, going both beyond known
results of QED and the general framework in condensed matter physics.

13. T. de Picoli, Z. Blood, Y. Lyanda-Geller and J. Vayrynen, Superconducting diode effect in
quasi-one-dimensional systems
The recent observations of the superconducting diode effect pose the challenge to fully
understand the necessary ingredients for nonreciprocal phenomena in superconductors. In this
theoretical work, we focused on the nonreciprocity of the critical current in a quasi-one-
dimensional superconductor. We define the critical current as the value of the supercurrent at
which the quasiparticle excitation gap closes (so that depairing occurs). Once the critical current
is exceeded, the quasiparticles can exchange energy with the superconducting condensate, giving
rise to dissipation. Our minimal model can be microscopically derived as a low-energy limit of a
Rashba spin-orbit coupled superconductor in a Zeeman field. Within the proposed model, we
explore the nature of the nonreciprocal effects of the critical current both analytically and
numerically. Our results quantify how system parameters such as spin-orbit coupling and
quantum confinement affect the strength of the superconducting diode effect. We demonstrate
that at weak spin-orbit coupling the effect linear in Rashba constant vanishes, and only cubic in
Rashba constant effect can take place. Our theory provides a complementary description to
Ginzburg-Landau theories of the effect.

Y Lyanda-Geller, Evolution of Bose–Einstein condensate systems beyond the Gross–Pitaevskii
equation

Topological phenomena emerge not only in Condensed matter systems, but also in atomic BEC
condensates and superfluids such as 3-HeA. While many phenomena in cold atoms and other
Bose–Einstein condensate (BEC) systems are often described using the mean-field approaches,

dynamics of the BEC, such as the nucleation of the condensate or the decay of the persistent
current, requires, in the presence of factors making a symmetry breaking possible, considering
collisions with thermal atoms via the collision integral. In spinor Bose-Einstein condensates,

persistent current, due to corresponding terms in system Hamiltonians. We have discussed the
kinetics of spin–orbit-coupled BEC and derived the kinetic equation for the particle spin density
matrix. Numerical simulations demonstrate significant effects of the collision integral on the
dynamics of the spin–orbit-coupled BEC upon quenching of the Raman coupling that generates

15. V. Ponomarenko, Y Lyanda-Geller, Unusual Quasiparticles and Tunneling Conductance in
Quantum Point Contacts in 𝜈=2/3 Fractional Quantum Hall Systems.
The Fractional Quantum Hall effect remains by far the best breeding ground for topological
phenomena and is the most important experimental setting, which may manifest non-Abelian
statistics, eventually leading to topologically protected qubit systems. Crucial role in experiments
on non-Abelian physics is played by quantum Hall interferometers based on tunneling of edge
states through quantum point contacts. Therefore understanding of tunneling of edge states,
which are chiral Luttinger liquid modes, through quantum point contacts is of significant
importance. Furthermore, understanding topological matter in the fractional quantum Hall effect
requires identifying the nature of edge state quasiparticles. In this work we have studied
fractional quantum Hall edge states at the filling factor ν = 2/3 in the spin-polarized and un-
polarized phases is represented by the two modes of composite fermions the parallel or opposite
spins. Tunneling through a quantum point contact in such systems between different or similar
spin phases is solved exactly. With the increase of the applied voltage, the quantum point contact

that takes into account interference of different chiral Luttinger modes, as opposed to alternative
approach, in which equilibration length is short, and no interference takes place. We propose
experiments on the applied voltage and temperature dependence of the QPC conductance and
noise that can distinguish between these two models and shed light on the nature of edge states
and FQH transport. Our results explain recent experimental findings by Manfra group on
tunneling between two polarized quantum Hall liquids and results by Rokhinson group on
tunneling between polarized and un-polarized quantum Hall liquids.
