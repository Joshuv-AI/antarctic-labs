 Second, we learned that carbon bond hybridization can be manipulated by conditions in the Formatted: Font: (Default) +Headings (Times New

 These discoveries build the case that carbon bond hybridization in carbon nanoparticles can be
selectively tuned by the parameters of low-temperature plasmas. As selective bond hybridization
allows tuning of the carbon nanoparticle properties such as bandgap, thermal conductivity,
electrical conductivity, and others, this increased knowledge of how selection can occur and its
physical foundations is an important step towards on-demand nanoparticle manufacturing for
energy, biomedical, and optoelectronic applications.

 Key Findings: Via experiments conducted at MSU and at Princeton Collaborative Low-
 Temperature Plasma Research Facility (PCRF), the team identified the
 The promise of carbon nanoparticles for luminescence, biosensing, catalysis – among Formatted: Font: (Default) +Headings (Times New
 others - is immense. Low-temperature plasmas (LTPs) could open up a route that allows Roman), 12 pt
 the full potential of carbon nanoparticle production to be reached – including selective
 bond hybridization via reactor parameters. LTPs, while they can produce
 extraordinarily high-quality semiconductor nanocrystals, are generally classic “black
 box” reactors: the user determines the input parameters to the reactor and the reactor
 produces outputs that might be linked to inputs, but the actual reaction mechanisms are
 unknown or only just sketched out. Revealing the true processes underlying the
 nucleation and growth of nanoparticles in LTPs has been identified by the community as
 a pressing need, underscored by the creation of DOE-supported user facilities such as the
 Princeton Collaborative Low-Temperature Plasma Research Facility (PCRF) to allow
 researchers access to specialized diagnostic equipment and highly trained research staff.
