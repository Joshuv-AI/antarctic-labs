 EXECUTIVE SUMMARY

Speciation of mercury during the pretreatment steps of tank waste processing is critical to successful
mercury removal prior to vitrification during Liquid Waste Operations (LWO) at SRS. OLI software has
been used to predict mercury speciation and activity throughout LWO. The OLI software operates based
on a thermodynamic framework called the Mixed Solvent Electrolyte (MSE) framework [1]. The MSE
framework allows prediction in theoretically infinitely dilute to concentrated mixtures (e.g., purely solute
solutions). Before modification to the MSE framework databanks, certain critical mercury species were
missing in the MSE databank, and some thermodynamic data needed to be updated for the OLI software to
accurately predict mercury chemical species in SRS waste tanks. To better reflect streams across LWO,
new mercury species were integrated into the MSE database.

To evaluate the changes to the OLI MSE framework per the Technical Task Request (TTR) [2] and the
Task Technical and Quality Assurance Plan (TTQAP) [3], waste stream compositions from Tanks 38, 43,
and Tank 50 decontaminated salt solution (DSS) were used as model inputs. Models were developed and
executed using both the old and new databases. Compositional analyses from caustic Tank 50 DSS and
caustic Tanks 38 and 43 were used as the input streams. These streams represent the most comprehensive
chemical data sets where both mercury and tank constituents were measured together. Results for Tank 50
DSS predict HgO as the predominant species in both databases. Both methyl and dimethyl Hg species are
present when the new database is ‘on’ and are not predicted with the new database turned ‘off’. The new
database predicts a greater amount of HgO and a greater fraction of it in the solid phase. Pourbaix diagrams
(potential vs. pH) generated for each Tank 50 DSS were identical regardless of which database was used.
Elemental Hg and HgO were predicted in the water stable region under basic conditions.

Tanks 38 and 43 follow similar trends as the Tank 50 DSS models. Unlike Tanks 38 and 50 DSS, the Tank
43 Pourbaix plot shows a region of stability for an aqueous HgOHCO3- species between approximately pH
7-11. In all streams, when MeHg+ is included in the inputs, the new database predicts aqueous MeHgOH
as the dominant species. If elemental or dimethyl mercury is in the waste stream, the new database model
predicts they are unchanged and remain in those states and quantities. Additionally, the total mercury values
are reported for both the measured input data and the OLI output data for all considered tanks. The summary
indicates that the percentage error between the measured and calculated values is less than 1% in all cases

The reconciliations and generation of the Pourbaix diagrams for Tank 50 DSS took approximately ten times
longer with the new database ‘on’. In addition, over the course of that time, models with the new database
‘on’ were more likely to crash or display an error. Some modest performance improvements were noted
when modeling with an i7 processor versus an i5. An example error is found in Appendix A. Furthermore,
Appendix B provides V&V for two chemical systems analyzed with the OLI software, results were
satisfactory.

It is recommended to utilize the new databases (i.e., HCO.ddb and SR-Hg.ddb) in future Savannah River
Mission Completion applications of OLI to represent pseudo steady-state. Furthermore, the integration and
utilization of the new databases (i.e., HCO.ddb and SR-Hg.ddb) in modeling applications (e.g., Aspen) is
also recommended.

Suggested future work includes:
 • Replicate these data as a function of temperature
 • Simplify models to decrease run time and iterate changes in concentration to determine critical
 influences from other chemical species
 • Repeat this exercise with a well characterized acidic stream
 • Understand the differences in Tank 38 and Tank 43 Pourbaix diagrams

• Model bench scale simulant experiments with known Hg species

1.0 Introduction

OLI software was launched about 50 years ago using the Aqueous (AQ) thermodynamic framework, where
acid-base equilibria are expressed in terms of proton or H+ ion. In 2000, OLI began to transition to a new
thermodynamic framework called Mixed Solvent Electrolyte (MSE), where acid-base equilibria are
expressed in terms of hydrated proton or H3O+ ion; one of its major advantages over AQ is that it has no
limitations on the electrolyte concentration, thus enabling simulation from infinite dilution to pure solute
limits. A previous survey showed the existing database, prior to upgrade, contained a total of 18 mercury
species: Hg2Cl2, HgCl2, Hg2F2, HgF2, HgH2EDTA, Hg2(NO3)2, Hg2(NO3)2⋅2H2O, Hg2(OH)2, HgO,
HgCO3, Hg(OH)2, Hg(OH)F, HgS, HgS⋅H2S, HgCO3⋅HgO, HgI2, HgBr2, Hg(C2H3O2)2. Several key
mercury species were either missing or contained outdated thermodynamic parameters. The following new
mercury species were integrated into the MSE database:

• Hg(C2H3O3)2 – Mercuric glycolate
• Hg(NO3)2 – Mercuric nitrate
• HgC2O4 – Mercuric oxalate
• Hg(HCO2)2 –Mercuric formate
• Hg(CH3)2 – Dimethyl mercury
• CH3HgX – Methyl mercury salts: X = Cl-, NO3-, and OH-
• Hg2(C2H3O3)2 – Mercurous glycolate
• Hg2C2O4 – Mercurous oxalate
• Hg2(HCO2)2 – Mercurous formate

In addition to the species listed above, equilibrium data were added for the reduction of mercuric oxide
(HgO):

HgO + HCOOH → Hg + CO2 + H2O

The thermodynamic parameters for all the reactants, products, and potential intermediates (such as
Hg(HCO2)2 and Hg2(HCO2)2) were added. Also, the parameters for the redox equilibria involving mercury
(reduced from 2+ to zero oxidation state) and carbon, from formate to CO2 (oxidized from 2+ to 4+
oxidation state) were incorporated.

To evaluate the new additions per the Technical Task Request (TTR) [2] and the Task Technical and Quality
Assurance Plan (TTQAP) [3], waste stream compositions from Tanks 38, 43, and Tank 50 decontaminated
salt solution (DSS) were used as model inputs.

2.0 Experimental Procedure

Thermodynamic calculations for this verification study were executed using OLI Systems’ Stream Analyzer
computation module within the OLI Studio software platform (Ver. 12.0, Rev. 12.0.0.11). Data compiled
in the referenced reports were used as inputs. Tank 50 semiannual sampling from January 2022 to October
2024 include mercury analyses as well as elemental and ion chromatography data [4-9]. Subsurface sample
data from Tanks 38 and 43 were used. Mercury data from subsurface sample data for Tanks 38 and 43
were derived from Bannochie et al., 2016 [10]. It should be noted that all mercury data from [4-9] are
reported in units of mg species/L, whereas all mercury data from [10] are reported in units of mg Hg/L.
Since OLI uses the mg species/L input and output, all mercury data from [10] was converted to mg species/L
before inputting into OLI.

Anionic species were input as reported. When reported as elemental, species were input as anions or cations
based on their most common oxidation and chemical state in a basic media. For example, Na was
represented as Na+ and Mo as MoO42-. Data for species listed below the limit of detection (LOD) were
added at the reported LOD. Arsenic, methanol, and NORPAR 13 were excluded because these data were
unavailable, or the species were not found in the database. Radionuclides were also excluded from the
models. Methyl mercury values were input as mg CH3Hg+/L and dimethyl mercury values were input as
mg Me2Hg/L. Elemental mercury values were input as such. Ionic mercury (Hg2+) was input according to
the following:

OLD Database
Hg2+ = Total Hg - Hg0

with “Total Hg” equal to the measured amount of total Hg (mg Hg/L)

NEW Database
Hg2+ = Total Hg - Hg0 - 0.9302·MeHg+ - 0.8696·Me2Hg

with “Total Hg” equal to the measured amount of total Hg (mg/L) and both MeHg+ and Me2Hg converted
from mg species/L to mg Hg/L through the use of the equation above to allow them to be subtracted from
the total Hg value.

Once species values were input, the charge balance was reconciled, with the redox function disabled, by
adding or removing Na+. In most cases, the charge imbalance was less than 10-3 mg/L. The balanced stream
was added as a new model to generate the Pourbaix diagrams, which does include the redox function in
OLI. Nitric acid was used as the titrant and Hg as the contact surface. For the “old” database, corrosion,
geochemical, and surface complexation double layer MSE models were used. For runs with the new
database these MSE models were left ‘on’ and the new database simply toggled ‘on’.

The summaries of all the mercury species used as input to OLI are listed in Tables 2-1 and 2-2 for all three
tanks discussed earlier. All mercury data for Table 2-1 were used as reported in [4-9] with units of mg
species/L. All mercury data for Table 2-2 were converted from their original reported units of mg Hg/L in
[10] to the OLI input units of mg species/L.

3.0 Results and Discussion

Predicted mercury speciation for six different Tank 50 DSS concentration inputs are shown below in Table
3-1 through Table 3-6. With both old (New Database OFF) and new (New Database ON) databases, the
only predicted precipitate is HgO and if elemental Hg is present it remains in its standard state. The new
 1-
database predicts more HgO in the solid phase. The HHgO2 ion is proposed by both databases with the
older database predicting it in slightly greater concentrations. It is suspected that this is a result of a change
in the equilibrium constant from 5.22E-32 to 1.61E-31for the following reaction:

                                        HgO(s) + H2O ↔ Hg2+ + 2 OH-

Hydroxy carbonate complexes also exist in both models but are in order of 10-3 mg/L or less.

The updated database predicts CH3HgOH as the major organomercury species if the CH3Hg+ cation is
present. Other forms of CH3Hg+ are said to exist but are at de minimis concentrations. The next most
abundant methylated mercury species is CH3HgCl reported at approximately 10-6 mg/L. If dimethyl
mercury is present, it is reported to be chemically inert in the aqueous phase.

Pourbaix diagrams plot electrochemical stability for different oxidation states of an element as a function
of pH. These diagrams are essentially phase diagrams that map the conditions of potential and pH where
different oxidation states and species are stable. Lines ‘a’ and ‘b’ in the diagrams below represent water
reduction and water oxidation. Below line ‘a’ water will decompose to form hydrogen and above line ‘b’
decompose to oxygen. The governing equations are as follows:

 Acidic: Basic:

        O2 + 4H+ + 4e- → 2H2O                             O2 + 2H2O + 4e- → 4OH-

        2H+ + 2e- → H2                                    2H2O + 2e- → H2 + 2OH-

Generally, vertical lines represent pure acid/base equilibria, while horizontal lines represent pure redox
reactions. Sloped lines are a combination of both.

Seen in Figure 3-2 and 3-3, the Pourbaix diagrams for all Tank 50 DSS models are visually identical with
both database configurations. In the acidic regime, HgCl2, Hg2Cl2, and Hg have equilibria in an oxidizing
environment. The calomel (Hg2Cl2) species exists in a narrow redox band owing to Hg+ disproportionation.
The acid base equilibrium between HgCl2 and HgO does shift ~0.1 pH units across different DSS samples.
It is unclear why the aqueous and solid-state labels do not align with the reported species, but they are the
same in both database configurations.

4.0 Recommendations and Conclusions

Actual waste streams containing detailed elemental analysis and mercury speciation have been modeled
with the new and old OLI MSE databases. The new database predicts an increase in the solid fraction of
HgO indicating changes made to the thermodynamic parameters of the database have a tangible impact.
The ability to include the methyl mercury cation suggests that, when present, CH3HgOH is the next most
concentrated mercury species. Both databases predict inert chemical behavior from elemental mercury and
the new database suggests the same for dimethyl mercury. Pourbaix diagrams appear to be unaffected by
the database improvements.

The reconciliations and generation of the Pourbaix diagrams for Tank 50 DSS took approximately ten times
longer with the new database ‘on’. In addition, over the course of that time, models with the new database
‘on’ were more likely to crash or display an error. Some modest performance improvements were noted
when modeling with an i7 processor versus an i5. An example error message is found in Appendix A.
Furthermore, Appendix B documents satisfactory testing of the OLI software against the two new
databases. The purpose of Validation and Verification (V&V) is to document that the OLI software testing
was satisfactory prior to the acceptance of the databases from the vendor during the procurement process.

It is recommended to utilize the new databases (i.e., HCO.ddb and SR-Hg.ddb) in future Savannah River
Mission Completion applications of OLI to represent pseudo steady-state conditions. Furthermore, the
integration and utilization of the new databases (i.e., HCO.ddb and SR-Hg.ddb) in modeling applications
(e.g., Aspen) is recommended.

Suggested future work includes:
 • Replicate these data as a function of temperature
 • Simplify models to decrease processing time and iterate changes in concentration to determine
 critical influences from other chemical species
 • Repeat this exercise with a well characterized acidic stream
 • Understand the differences in Tank 38 and Tank 43 Pourbaix diagrams
 • Model bench scale simulant experiments with known Hg species
