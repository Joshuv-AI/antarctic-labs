Decoupled iterative schemes for solving stationary MHD problems Journal: Communications in Nonlinear Science and Numerical Simulation
Aziz Takhirov, Driss Yakoubi

Abstract
We develop a novel iterative approach for solving the incompressible magnetohydrodynamics problem. The main idea is to split the velocity-momentum and magnetic induction equations with respect to the diffusive terms, as in [ 26 ] . As a result, we get a smaller system that is iteration-level-dependent, along with two Stokes systems that need to be assembled only once. We also extended the scheme to the Elsässer variables reformulation of the equations. For both schemes, we established boundedness and convergence. Several numerical experiments are presented to show the effectiveness of the schemes.

Introduction

Magnetohydrodynamics (MHD) describes the interaction between electrically conducting fluids and the electromagnetic fields. In an MHD system, the coupling between the flow and the electromagnetic field occurs through two mechanisms. Firstly, the electric current and the magnetic field generate the Lorentz force that acts on the flow. Secondly, the flow of conducting fluid itself induces a magnetic field, thereby altering the applied field. The MHD system has many practical applications, such as industrial liquid metals
[
9
]
, metallurgy
[
6
]
, dynamo
[
17
]
, etc.

Mathematically, the stationary MHD system can be written as follows:

\displaystyle-\nu\Delta\mathbf{u}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}-\kappa\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{B}-\gamma\nabla\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{u}+\nabla p

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f},

(1)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=0,

(2)

\displaystyle-\mu\Delta\mathbf{B}+\mathbf{u}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{B}-\mathbf{B}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{u}-\gamma\nabla\mathrm{\nabla\cdot}\mathbf{B}+\nabla\lambda

\displaystyle=\mathbf{g},

(3)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=0,

(4)

where
\mathbf{u}
is the velocity of the fluid,
\mathbf{B}
is the magnetic field,
p:=\hat{p}+\kappa\frac{|\mathbf{B}|^{2}}{2}
is the modified pressure with
\hat{p}
being the pressure,
\lambda
is a variable acting as a Lagrange multiplier corresponding to the solenoidal constraint on the magnetic field,
\mathbf{f}
is the body forcing,
\mathbf{g}
is the divergence-free forcing on the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
,
\kappa
is a coupling number,
\nu
is the kinematic viscosity, and
\mu
is the magnetic diffusivity.
For simplicity, we consider homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for both
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{B}

\mathbf{u}=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{and }\quad\mathbf{B}=\mathbf{0}\quad\text{on }\;\partial\Omega.

(5)

The

terms in (
1
)-(
4

[
15
,
10
,
16
,
18
]
.

With appropriate changes, our analysis can also be extended for no-slip velocity conditions together with
\mathbf{B}\cdot\mathbf{n}=0
and
(\nabla\times\mathbf{B})\times\mathbf{n}=0
(in this case, the Maxwell equation uses the curl-curl form of the dissipation term).

The steady MHD system has been the subject of many studies. Among the earliest ones, in
[
13
]
, Gunzburger et. al. studied the well-posedness for the continuous and discrete problem with nonhomogeneous boundary conditions. Moreover, they analyzed three linearization methods: the fully explicit method, Newton’s method, and the semi-implicit Oseen’s method. In
[
25
]
, the authors considered two-grid Newton’s method with nonconforming finite element spaces.
[
12
]
studied a DG-based mixed method, where the linearization was performed via the Picard iterations.
[
24
]
developed an algebraic splitting method, in the spirit of
[
19
]
, where the Schur complement matrix is SPD and independent of iteration level. Yang et. al.
[
27
]
extended the Arrow-Hurwicz method of
[
5
]
to stationary MHD equations. Due to the lagging of some dissipative terms, approaches of
[
27
,
5
]
usually entail complicated assumptions on the scheme parameters. On the other hand, an improved version of the Arrow-Hurwicz method was developed in
[
20
,
21
]
. Finally, we mention the article
[
8
]
, where, similar to the work of
[
13
]
, three iterative methods of Stokes-, Newton-, and Oseen-type were investigated.

The goal of this paper is to present two iterative schemes inspired by the Incremental Viscosity Splitting scheme for unsteady Navier-Stokes
[
26
]
flows. The idea has already been successfully applied to steady Navier-Stokes equations
[
22
]
. In this approach, the equations are split with respect to the viscous and resistive terms, respectively. This results in a PDE system where the intermediate velocity and magnetic fields must be solved for simultaneously, and then the end-of-step variables are obtained by solving two linear Stokes systems. These Stokes systems result in SPD Schur complement matrices that are assembled and preconditioned only once. Unlike the Arrow-Hurwicz scheme
[
20
]
, IVS schemes still require a solution of a mixed problem. However, a gain is that the end-of-step fields are (discretely) divergence-free.

Let

6
SIVS - Elsässer formulation

Even though the Steady Incremental Viscosity Splitting Algorithm
5.1
requires a solution of simpler linear systems compared to the classical Picard Algorithm
4.1
, the first step (
32
) couples the intermediate velocity and magnetic fields
(\mathbf{\tilde{u}}_{k},\mathbf{\tilde{B}}_{k})
, which requires a solution of a large linear system. In this section, we will construct a novel IVS scheme based on the Elsässer reformulation of the MHD system (
1
)-(
4
) that replaces (
32
) with two decoupled linear systems of a smaller size.

To this end, we introduce the following Elsässer variables
[
2
]
:

\displaystyle\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{u}+\sqrt{\kappa}\mathbf{B},\,p^{+}:=p+\sqrt{\kappa}\lambda,

(56)

\displaystyle\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{u}-\sqrt{\kappa}\mathbf{B},\,p^{-}:=p-\sqrt{\kappa}\lambda,

\displaystyle\mathbf{f}^{+}

\displaystyle:=\mathbf{f}+\sqrt{\kappa}\mathbf{g}\,,\mathbf{f}^{-}:=\mathbf{f}-\sqrt{\kappa}\mathbf{g},

\displaystyle\nu^{+}

\displaystyle:=\frac{\nu+\mu}{2},\,\nu^{-}:=\frac{\nu-\mu}{2}.

To obtain a system for
(\mathbf{w},p^{+},\mathbf{z},p^{-})
, we first scale the equation (
3
) by
\sqrt{\kappa}
, then add it to and subtract it from (
1
), resulting in the following system:

\displaystyle-\nu^{+}\Delta\mathbf{w}+\mathbf{z}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{w}+\nabla p^{+}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}^{+}+\nu^{-}\Delta\mathbf{z},

(57)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{w}

\displaystyle=0,

(58)

\displaystyle-\nu^{+}\Delta\mathbf{z}+\mathbf{w}\cdot\nabla\mathbf{z}+\nabla p^{-}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{f}^{-}+\nu^{-}\Delta\mathbf{w},

(59)

\displaystyle\nabla\cdot\mathbf{z}

\displaystyle=0.

(60)

First, we prove an a priori bound for the solution of the system (
57
)-(
60
):

Lemma 8
.

Any solution
(\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z})
of (
57
)-(
60
) satisfies

\|(\nabla\mathbf{w},\nabla\mathbf{z})\|:=\sqrt{\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\mathbf{z}\|^{2}}\leq\,\frac{2\nu^{+}}{\nu\,\mu}\sqrt{\|\mathbf{f}^{+}\|^{2}_{-1}+\|\mathbf{f}^{-}\|^{2}_{-1}}:=\;\mathcal{N}_{1}.

(61)

Additionally, if

\Lambda_{2}:=\frac{\mathcal{M}\mathcal{N}_{1}+|\nu^{-}|}{\nu^{+}}<1,

(62)

then the solution of (
57
)-(
60
) is unique.

Proof.

Multiply (
57
) by
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{w}
and integrate it over
\Omega
, yielding

\displaystyle\nu^{+}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle\mathbf{f}^{+},\mathbf{w}\rangle-\nu^{-}(\nabla\mathbf{z},\nabla\mathbf{w})

(63)

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu^{+}}{\,\nu\,\mu}\|\mathbf{f}^{+}\|^{2}_{-1}+\frac{\nu\mu}{4\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\frac{\nu^{+}}{2}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\frac{(\nu^{-})^{2}}{2\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{z}\|^{2}\implies

\displaystyle\frac{2(\nu^{-})^{2}+\mu\nu}{4\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\nu^{+}}{\,\nu\,\mu}\|\mathbf{f}^{+}\|^{2}_{-1}+\frac{(\nu^{-})^{2}}{2\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{z}\|^{2}.

Similarly, multiplying (
59
) by
\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{z}
and integrating gives

\frac{2(\nu^{-})^{2}+\mu\nu}{4\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{z}\|^{2}\leq\frac{\nu^{+}}{\,\nu\,\mu}\|\mathbf{f}^{-}\|^{2}_{-1}+\frac{(\nu^{-})^{2}}{2\nu^{+}}\|\nabla\mathbf{w}\|^{2}.

(64)

Combining (
63
) and (
64
) yields the bound (
61
).

To show the uniqueness of the solution, assume
(\mathbf{w}_{1},\mathbf{z}_{1})
and
(\mathbf{w}_{2},\mathbf{z}_{2})
solve (
57
)-(
60
). Then their differences
\delta\mathbf{w}:=\mathbf{w}_{1}-\mathbf{w}_{2}
and
\delta\mathbf{z}:=\mathbf{z}_{1}-\mathbf{z}_{2}
satisfy:

\displaystyle\nu^{+}(\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{z}\|^{2})

\displaystyle=-c^{*}(\delta\mathbf{z},\mathbf{w}_{1},\delta\mathbf{w})-c^{*}(\delta\mathbf{w},\mathbf{z}_{1},\delta\mathbf{z})-2\nu^{-}(\nabla\delta\mathbf{z},\nabla\delta\mathbf{w})

(65)

\displaystyle\leq 2(\mathcal{N}_{1}\mathcal{M}+|\nu^{-}|)\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{w}\|\,\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{z}\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\mathcal{N}_{1}\mathcal{M}+|\nu^{-}|}{\nu^{+}}(\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{w}\|^{2}+\|\nabla\delta\mathbf{z}\|^{2}).

Owing to the assumption (
62
), we obtain the uniqueness of the solution.
∎

Algorithm 6.1
.

(\mathbf{w}_{0},p^{+}_{0},\mathbf{z}_{0},p^{-}_{0})=(\mathbf{0},0,\mathbf{0},0)
and for

Step 2:

\displaystyle b(q,\mathbf{w}_{k})

(68)

Step 4:

(\mathbf{z}_{k},p^{-}_{k})\in\mathbf{X}\times Q

(69)

\displaystyle b(q,\mathbf{z}_{k})

\displaystyle=0,\qquad\forall\,q\in Q.

.

\displaystyle a_{1}(\mathbf{e}_{k}-\mathbf{\tilde{e}}_{k},\mathbf{v})+b(\delta_{k}-\delta_{k-1},\mathbf{v})

Remark 1
.

In the classical formulation of the MHD system (
1
)-(
4
), the velocity
\mathbf{u}
and the magnetic field
\mathbf{B}
are coupled via two nonlinear terms in each equation. On the other hand, in the Elsässer reformulation (
57
)-(
60
), the coupling between
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{z}
is through one nonlinear and one linear term. In theory, this allows for the construction of iterative methods with improved properties, assuming homogeneous Dirichlet boundary conditions for both
\mathbf{u}
and
\mathbf{B}
. However, when the boundary conditions for these fields are not of the same type, determining the physically correct expressions for
\mathbf{w}
and
\mathbf{z}
becomes challenging.

7.3
3D Hartmann flow

In this section, we test Algorithm
6.1
on a 3D Hartmann flow. The exact analytical solution for the in a rectangular duct
\Omega=[0,L]\times[-y_{0},y_{0}]\times[-z_{0},z_{0}]
,
y_{0},z_{0}\ll L
, subjected to a constant transverse magnetic field
B_{d}=(0,1,0)
, is given as follows:

\displaystyle\mathbf{u}

\displaystyle=(u(y,z),0,0),

\displaystyle\mathbf{B}

\displaystyle=(B(y,z),1,0),

\displaystyle p

\displaystyle=-Gx-S_{c}\frac{B^{2}(y,z)}{2}+p_{0}.

The velocity profile
u(y,z)
and the induced magnetic field profile
B(y,z)
are expressed using infinite Fourier series in the
z
-direction:

\displaystyle u(y,z)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}GR_{e}(z^{2}-z_{0}^{2})+\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}u_{i}(y)\cos(\lambda_{i}z),

\displaystyle B(y,z)

\displaystyle=\sum_{i=0}^{\infty}b_{i}(y)\cos(\lambda_{i}z).

The Fourier coefficients
u_{i}(y)
and
b_{i}(y)
depend on the
y
-coordinate and are defined as:

\displaystyle u_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=A_{i}\cosh(p_{1}y)+B_{i}\cosh(p_{2}y)

\displaystyle b_{i}(y)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{R_{e}S_{c}}\left(A_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{2}}{p_{1}}\sinh(p_{1}y)+B_{i}\frac{\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{2}^{2}}{p_{2}}\sinh(p_{2}y)\right)

The eigenvalues
\lambda_{i}
and the roots
p_{1}
and
p_{2}
governing the boundary layers are:

\displaystyle\lambda_{i}=\frac{(2i+1)\pi}{2z_{0}}\text{ and }p_{1,2}^{2}=\lambda_{i}^{2}+\frac{Ha^{2}}{2}\pm Ha\sqrt{\lambda_{i}^{2}+\frac{Ha^{2}}{4}}.

and
B_{i}
are determined using the boundary value
u_{i}(y_{0})
and the denominator
\gamma_{i}
:

\displaystyle u_{i}(y_{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{-2GR_{e}}{\lambda_{i}^{3}z_{0}}\sin(\lambda_{i}z_{0}),

\displaystyle\gamma_{i}

\displaystyle=p_{2}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})\sinh(p_{1}y_{0})\cosh(p_{2}y_{0})-p_{1}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{2}^{2})\sinh(p_{2}y_{0})\cosh(p_{1}y_{0}),

\displaystyle A_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{-p_{1}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{2}^{2})}{\gamma_{i}}u_{i}(y_{0})\sinh(p_{2}y_{0}),

\displaystyle B_{i}

\displaystyle=\frac{p_{2}(\lambda_{i}^{2}-p_{1}^{2})}{\gamma_{i}}u_{i}(y_{0})\sinh(p_{1}y_{0}).

8
Conclusion

In this work, we first established the existence and uniqueness of the solution to the stationary MHD problem under suitable assumptions. We then analyzed the classical Picard iterative scheme and proved its convergence in the three-dimensional setting under the same conditions ensuring uniqueness of the continuous problem.

To overcome some implementation difficulties related to the strong coupling of the unknowns, we introduced a new iterative algorithm inspired by the IVS strategy. This approach allows for a decoupling of the main variables, leading to simpler and more efficient linear subproblems. We proved that this new iterative method converges toward the unique solution of the original MHD problem.

For each proposed algorithm, we explicitly derived and detailed the associated linear systems, which provides a practical framework for numerical implementation.

Furthermore, we extended our analysis to the Elsässer formulation of the MHD equations, showing that the proposed methodology can also be adapted to this alternative formulation.

Finally, several two-dimensional and three-dimensional numerical experiments were performed to validate the theoretical results and illustrate the efficiency and robustness of the proposed methods.

A natural continuation of this work concerns the unsteady MHD problem. Future investigations will focus on the extension of the proposed strategies to the time-dependent setting, with particular attention devoted to the analysis of temporal and spatial stability, as well as convergence properties of the fully discrete schemes.
