Extrapolation of solvability of the parabolic L p L^{p} Neumann problem on bounded Lipschitz cylinders
Martin Dindoš, YingYi Liu

Abstract
. A recent result of the first author with Li and Pipher has established the extrapolation of solvability of the L p L^{p} parabolic Neumann problem on unbounded graph domains of the form Ω = { ( x ′ , x n ) : x n > φ ⁡ ( x ′ ) } × ℝ \Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):\,x_{n}>\varphi(x^{\prime})\}\times\mathbb{R} , where φ : ℝ n − 1 → ℝ \varphi:\mathbb{R}^{n-1}\to\mathbb{R} is a Lipschitz function. The result shows that under the assumptions that the L p L^{p} parabolic Neumann problem for the equation L u = − ∂ t u + div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 Lu=-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0 in Ω \Omega and also the L p ′ L^{p^{\prime}} parabolic Dirichlet problem for the adjoint equation L ∗ u = ∂ t u + div ( A ∇ u ) = 0 L^{*}u=\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0 in Ω \Omega are solvable, then also the L q L^{q} parabolic Neumann problem for the equation L ​ u = 0 Lu=0 in Ω \Omega is solvable for all 1 < q < p 1<q<p . However the mentioned paper does not answer the question whether the same claim is also true for domains of the form 𝒪 × ℝ \mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R} , where 𝒪 \mathcal{O} is a bounded Lipschitz domain (in spatial variables) since this case does not follow from our argument for the unbounded case. Indeed, the bounded Lipschitz cylinder case requires a significantly different approach which we present in this article and establish an analogous result when 𝒪 \mathcal{O} is a bounded Lipschitz domain.

1.
Introduction

The main objective of this paper is to establish extrapolation of solvability for the
L^{p}
Neumann problem for the parabolic PDE

Lu=-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0,\qquad\mbox{in}\Omega,

where the domain
\Omega
is of the form
\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is a bounded Lipschitz domain (in spatial variables), i.e., a bounded (in space) Lipschitz cylinder that is infinite in time variable.

The coefficients of the elliptic matrix
A
are assumed to be real, bounded, measurable and are allowed to be time varying. No other assumption of the coefficients is made. More precisely, we assume that
A=[a_{ij}(X,t)]
is a
n\times n
bounded matrix satisfying the uniform ellipticity condition for a.e.
X\in\mathcal{O}
,
t\in\mathbb{R}
, there exist positive constants
\lambda
and
\Lambda
such that

(1.1)

\lambda|\xi|^{2}\leq\sum_{i,j}a_{ij}(X,t)\xi_{i}\xi_{j},\qquad|A(X,t)|\leq\Lambda,

for almost every
(X,t)\in\Omega
and all
\xi\in\mathbb{R}^{n}
.

We prove that if for some
p\in(1,\infty)
the
L^{p}
Neumann problem for the PDE
Lu=0
in
\Omega

is solvable (and also the
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem for the adjoint PDE
L^{*}u=0
in
\Omega
is solvable), then the
L^{q}
Neumann problem for the PDE
Lu=0
in
\Omega
is solvable for all
1<q<p
.

The importance of this result is that it is “clean” and does not impose any additional assumptions on the coefficients beyond the natural ones (ellipticity and boundedness). Results of this nature are known for the parabolic Dirichlet problems (either using known properties of
B_{p}
weights or alternatively by interpolation against the
L^{\infty}
end-point which is a consequence of the maximum principle) and also recently for the Regularity problem
[
5
]
using an interpolation against a parabolic Hardy-Sobolev endpoint
p=1
. An analogous elliptic result for the Neumann problem is known and has been proven first in
[
9
]
under stronger assumptions (where related Regularity problem must also be solvable). This was later relaxed in
[
8
]
to the solvability of the
L^{p^{\prime}}
Dirichlet problem for the adjoint PDE. Our claim only uses this weaker assumption. Hence our result completes the gap in our understanding of parabolic boundary value problems.

The paper
[
7
]
has proven this result for unbounded graph domains of the form

(1.2)

\Omega=\{(x^{\prime},x_{n}):\,x_{n}>\varphi(x^{\prime})\}\times\mathbb{R}

however the case of bounded Lipschitz cylinder we consider here is significantly different and requires substantially new ideas. As we shall see, at one point we introduce an unbounded domain of the type (
1.2
) and solve an auxiliary Neumann problem associated to it. We will have to overcome a significant obstacle there since we do not assume Neumann solvability on the domain (
1.2
) but only on the original bounded
domain.

We note that for specific operators (such as the heat equation on Lipschitz cylinders, or even more general domains), this result is known (cf.
[
3
]
). We also note that in the paper in preparation
[
6
]
, the solvability of the
L^{p}
Neumann problem
in the range
1<p<p_{0}
for some
p_{0}>1
has been established on bounded Lipschitz cylinders, i.e., domains we consider here, but with additional assumptions, namely the coefficients
A
satisfying small Carleson measure condition and also smallness of the Lipschitz norm of the domain. Hence, the presented result here is of different nature, as we make no smallness assumptions on the domain and no further assumptions on the coefficients.

This is the case considered in

and therefore from now on we shall always assume that

2.1.
Energy solutions

We recall the papers
[
2
,
4
]
where these concepts are discussed is greater details. With
\Omega=\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}
, where
\mathcal{O}
is either bounded or unbounded Lipschitz domain,
we say that
v:\Omega\to\mathbb{R}
belongs to the
energy class

\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
if

\displaystyle\|v\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}:=\bigg(\|\nabla v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}+\|H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}v\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})}^{2}\bigg)^{1/2}<\infty.

Consequently, these are called
energy solutions
. When considered modulo constants,
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
is a Hilbert space and it is in fact the closure of
\mathcal{C}_{0}^{\infty}\!\big(\,\overline{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\,\big)
for the homogeneous norm
\|\cdot\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}

and it coincides with the space
\dot{L}^{2}_{1,1/2}(\Omega)
.

As shown in
[
2
]
(with a small generalization), functions from
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
have well defined Dirichlet traces with values in
the
homogeneous parabolic Sobolev space

\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
. Here,
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{s}_{\pm\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\mathbb{R}^{n})
is defined as the closure of Schwartz functions
v\in{\mathcal{S}}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
with Fourier support away from the origin in the norm
\|\mathcal{F}^{-1}((|\xi|^{2}\pm i\tau)^{s}\mathcal{F}v)\|_{2}
. This yields a space of tempered distributions modulo constants in
L_{\operatorname{loc}}^{2}({\mathbb{R}^{n}})
if
0<s\leq 1/2
.
Conversely, any
g\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
can be extended to a function
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
with trace
v\big|_{\partial\mathbb{R}^{n+1}_{+}}=g
. For this via partition of unity we can define

\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R})
for
\mathcal{O}
Lipschitz.

Hence, by the energy solution to
-\partial_{t}u+\divg(A\nabla u)=0
with Dirichlet boundary datum
u\big|_{\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}=f\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
(understood in the trace sense) we mean
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
such that

\displaystyle a(u,v):=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

holds for all
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
, the subspace of
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
with zero boundary trace.

Moving onto the Neumann problem, given any
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}(\Omega)
, the co-normal derivative
\partial^{A}_{\nu}u\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=:\langle A\nabla u,\nu\rangle\Big|_{\partial\Omega}=g
is defined via the formula

(2.9)

\displaystyle\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t-\int_{\partial\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}gv\,\mathrm{d}x\,\mathrm{d}t=0,

for all
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
. Here, since the traces of
v
belong to
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
and all elements of the space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
are realized by some
v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
,
the Neumann boundary data must by duality naturally belong to the space
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
.

By
[
2
]
, the key to solving these problems is the introduction of the modified sesquilinear form (introduced earlier in
[
10
]
):

(2.10)

a_{\delta}(u,v):=\iint_{\mathcal{O}\times\mathbb{R}}\left[A\nabla u\cdot{\nabla(1-\delta H_{t})v}+H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\cdot{D_{t}^{1/2}(1-\delta H_{t})v}\right]\,\mathrm{d}X\,\mathrm{d}t,

where
\delta
is a real number yet to be chosen. The Hilbert transform
H_{t}
is a skew-symmetric isometric operator with inverse
-H_{t}
on both
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
and
\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}
. Hence,
1-\delta H_{t}
is invertible on these spaces for any
\delta\in\mathbb{R}
. Hence for a fixed
\delta>0
small enough,
a_{\delta}
is coercive on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}
since

(2.11)

a_{\delta}(u,u)\geq(\lambda-\Lambda\delta)\|\nabla u\|_{2}^{2}+\delta\|H_{t}D_{t}^{1/2}u\|_{2}^{2}.

In particular
\delta=\lambda/(\Lambda+1)
would work.
To solve the Dirichlet problem we take an extension
w\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}
of the data
f
and apply the Lax-Milgram lemma to
a_{\delta}
on
\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
to obtain some
u\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}
such that

\displaystyle a_{\delta}(u,v)=-a_{\delta}(w,v)\qquad(v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}_{0}).

Hence,
u+w
is an energy solution with data
f
. Should there exist another solution
v
, then
a_{\delta}(u+w-v,u+w-v)=0
and hence by coercivity
\|u+w-v\|_{\dot{\mathsf{E}}}=0
. Thus the two solutions only differ by a constant. It means that Dirichlet problem associated with our parabolic PDE is
well-posed
in the energy class. Similar arguments allow us to solve the Neumann problem by considering for the datum
g\in\dot{H}{\vphantom{H}}^{-1/4}_{\partial_{t}-\Delta_{x}}(\partial\Omega)
the solution
u\in\dot{E}
such that

\displaystyle a_{\delta}(u,v)=\langle g,\mbox{Tr }(1-\delta H_{t})v\rangle\qquad(v\in\dot{\mathsf{E}}).

applying the Sobolev inequality on

and hence by interior Hölder regularity of
\tilde{u}

(4.11)

\sup_{J_{1/16}(X,t)}|\tilde{w}|\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))},

To see (

) consider points with

and

. For these clearly

(4.12)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{J_{1/4}(X,t)}|\nabla\tilde{w}|\lesssim\fint_{Q_{1/4}(x,r^{-1}\phi(rx))\times(t-1/16,t)\cap\partial\Omega}\tilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})d\sigma\\
\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))}.

Then by interior Poincaré’s inequality for

(Lemma

)

\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{J_{1/8}(X,t)}|\tilde{w}-c_{\tilde{w}}|\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))},

where

denotes the average of
\tilde{w}
in

. Clearly, if we can take

is zero there.

The rest of the argument goes as in
[

over neighbouring backward parabolic cubes
are again controlled by the righthand side of (

[
7
]
. Hence we have (

) for all points
(X,t)
outside of

with
\delta(X,t)\leq 5/4
and
t\leq 2/K
.

Boundedness for the remaining points (i.e. those where

) follows by the maximum principle (which must hold even though

variable, since the fact that
\tilde{w}
is an energy solution implies a weak decay of
\tilde{w}
when

). Hence,
\tilde{w}
is indeed bounded on any complement of an enlargement of

with

.

Consider now a smooth cutoff function
\eta
which vanishes on

and is equal to

Q_{2}(0,0)
. It follows that
\eta\tilde{w}
is bounded on
\mathbb{R}^{n}\times(-\infty,2/K)
and hence for any

and any bounded time interval
(a,b)
,
b\leq 2/K

\int_{a}^{b}\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}|(\eta\tilde{w})(X,t)|^{2}e^{-c|X|^{2}}dX\,dt<\infty,

which then implies the integrability also on the interval
(-\infty,2/K)
as
\tilde{w}
vanishes when
t<-1/K
.
Recall Aronson’s bound for the fundamental
solution (
[
1
]
), denoted by

(4.13)

|E(X,t,Y,s)|\leq C\chi_{(0,\infty)}(t-s)(t-s)^{-n/2}e^{-c|X-Y|^{2}/(t-s)},

for some

. Also
\int_{\mathbb{R}^{n}}E(X,t,Y,s)dY=1
for all

and
(X,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}
.

It follows that
(\eta\tilde{w})
can be written as

\eta\tilde{w}(X,t)=\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}}E(X,t,Y,s)\left[\tilde{w}(Y,s)\partial_{s}\eta(Y,s)-\langle\tilde{A}(Y,s)\nabla\tilde{w}(Y,s),\nabla\eta(Y,s)\rangle\right]dYds\\
+\iint_{\mathbb{R}^{n}\times\mathbb{R}}\tilde{w}(Y,s)\langle\tilde{A}(Y,s)\nabla\eta(Y,s),\nabla_{Y}E(X,t,Y,s)\rangle dYds.

Hence using (
4.13
) and Caccioppoli’s inequality for the term involving
\nabla E
, it follows that for some small
c>0

(4.14)

|\tilde{w}(X,t)|\leq Ce^{-c|X|^{2}},\qquad\mbox{for all $(X,t)$ with }\|(X,t)\|\geq 9/8\mbox{ and }t<2/K.

Here the constant
C
in the above estimate is a multiple of
\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))}
. The decay we have picked up in (

It enables us to estimate
\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}(S_{k})}
on a sequence of

partitioning the complement of the set
Q_{1}(0,0)\cap\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})
.

S_{k}=\{(X,t)\in\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}):2^{k}<\|(X,t)\|\leq 2^{k+1}\},\qquad\mbox{for }k=1,2,\dots.

We refer the reader for details of this calculation to section 5 of
[
7
]
(as it is identical).
The only difference is that we stop at the scale when

since as we recall the rescaled PDEs for
w

and
u
coincide up to the scale
[4r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\cap r^{-1}\mathcal{O}]\times\mathbb{R}
. It then follows:

(4.15)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}\\
\leq\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))}+\sum_{k=1}^{k_{0}}\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}(S_{k})}\\
\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{B_{3/2}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K)})\|_{L^{1}(\partial(r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega}))}\lesssim C_{K}+\iint_{(B_{3}(0)\times(-1/K,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|dXdt,

where the last inequality follows from (
4.9
). The implied constants in the estimate above do not depend on the scale
r>0
, and only depend on the ellipticity constants, the Lipschitz constant of the graph and the constants in the estimates for the

\rm{(}\rm{N}_{p}\rm{)}
L
and
\rm{(}\rm{D}_{p^{\prime}}\rm{)}
{}^{L^{*}}
problems. With the exception of the constant
C_{K}
remaining constants do not depend on
K
(which is not yet chosen).

Our next objective is to establish estimates for the last term on the righthand side of (
4.15
).
For an ease of notation, let

\displaystyle S_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=B_{3}(0)\times(2/K-9,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon},

\displaystyle\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=B_{4}(0)\times(2/K-16,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon/2}

\displaystyle\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=B_{4}(0)\times(2/K-16,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon/4}

\displaystyle\accentset{\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{1.73315pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}}{S}_{\varepsilon}

\displaystyle=B_{5}(0)\times(2/K-25,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon/5}.

Clearly as
w=0
for
t<1/K
, the integral of
\nabla w
over
S_{\varepsilon}
equal to the last term on the righthand side of (
4.15
). We can think about this term as an interior parabolic “cube”
whose distance to the boundary is comparable to
\varepsilon
. Hence by Lemma
3.1
and Hölder
it follows that

(4.16)

\iint_{(B_{3}(0)\times(-1/K,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|dXdt\lesssim C\varepsilon^{-1}\left(\iint_{\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}\right)^{1/2}.

Recall that since
w=0
for
t<-1/K
by fundamental theorem of calculus (by integrating in the

we have that for a fixed

\int_{-\infty}^{2/K}w^{2}(X,s)ds\leq C/K\int_{-\infty}^{2/K}\partial_{t}w(X,s)w(X,s)ds,

where the term

appears thanks to the fact that the length of the time interval where
w
does not vanish is proportional to
1/K
.
Let
\varphi
be a smooth nonnegative cutoff function in
X
variables only (constant in

) such that
\varphi=1
on

\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}
and
\varphi
is vanishes outside of
\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}
and
|\nabla\varphi|\lesssim\varepsilon^{-1}
. Then using the equation above we have that

(4.17)

\iint_{\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}dXdt\leq\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}(X,t)\varphi^{2}(X)dXdt\\
\leq C/K\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}\partial_{t}w(X,t)w(X,t)\varphi^{2}(X)dXdt=C/K\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}\divg(A\nabla w)(X,t)w(X,t)\varphi(X)dXdt\\
=-C/K\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}\tilde{A}\nabla w(X,t)\cdot\nabla w(X,t)\varphi^{2}(X)dXdt-C/K\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}A\nabla w(X,t)\cdot\nabla(\varphi^{2}(X))w(X,t)dXdt\\
\leq C/K\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w||\nabla\varphi||w||\varphi|dXdt\leq 1/2\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}\varphi^{2}dXdt+C/(\varepsilon K)^{2}\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|^{2}dXdt.

Hence after hiding the penultimate term we get that

\iint_{\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}dXdt\leq\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}\varphi^{2}dXdt\leq 2C/(\varepsilon K)^{2}\iint_{\accentset{\approx}{S}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|^{2}dXdt.

We note that to derive this inequality we worked with
\partial_{t}w
which for a general parabolic PDE is only a distribution, but the calculation above can be made rigorous by approximating the coefficients by smooth
PDEs and a limiting argument. Finally, by using reverse Hölder inequality for the gradient of a solution (which is a consequence of Lemma
3.1
) we get that

\left(\iint_{\widetilde{S}_{\varepsilon}}w^{2}dXdt\right)^{1/2}\leq C_{\varepsilon}/K\iint_{\,\,\accentset{\mbox{\makebox[0.0pt][l]{\raisebox{1.73315pt}{$\approx$}}$\approx$}}{S}_{\varepsilon}}|\nabla w|dXdt.

By combining the estimate above with (

(4.18)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}\\
\leq C_{k}+C_{\varepsilon}K^{-1}\iint_{(B_{5}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon/5}}|\nabla w|dXdt.

The final observation is that since

\iint_{(B_{5}(0)\times(-\infty,2/K))\cap\widetilde{r^{-1}\Omega}_{\varepsilon/5}}|\nabla w|dXdt\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})},

we see that

(4.19)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}\\
\leq C_{k}+\tilde{C}_{\varepsilon}K^{-1}\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}.

It follows that for sufficiently small

(4.20)

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}\leq 2C_{K}.

This is a “near final” version of our estimate for
w
. We still need to take care of estimates for
\nabla w
for

to obtain an analogue of the estimate (
4.20
) without this restriction on the time interval. With (
4.20
) in hand we revisit our construction we gave for

.

Recall, that we have constructed
\tilde{w}
by reflection (excluding the parabolic ball

).
Our objective previously was to establish bounds that do not depend on
K
and then choose
K
sufficiently small so that a key term (as seen above) can be absorbed by the lefthand side. With this done and

fixed permanently we now can observe that the Neumann datum
g
is in fact supported in
(Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K))\cap\partial\tilde{\Omega}

and obtain a function defined
on the set
\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K)}
. We shall still call this function

as it is an extension to a larger set of the previously defined

We now claim that our function
\tilde{w}
is also bounded for

(4.21)

\sup_{J_{1/(16K)}(X,t)}|\tilde{w}|\lesssim\mathchoice{{\vbox{\hbox{$\textstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.675pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-4.02336pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-2.53589pt}}{{\vbox{\hbox{$\scriptscriptstyle\mkern-1.5mu{-}\mkern-7.5mu{-}$}}\kern-1.7709pt}}\!\int\!\!\!\!\!\int_{J_{1/(8K)}(X,t)}|\tilde{w}|\leq C\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))},

for all points
(X,t)
such that
J_{1/(4K)}(X,t)\cap(Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K)))=\emptyset
.
The proof is analogous to the one given above, where once again we first prove the claim for
the points such that
\delta(X,t)\leq O(1)
and

, use the maximum principle to get it for all points

(X,t)\in\mathbb{R}^{n+1}\setminus\overline{Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K)}

and then since the set

\overline{Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K)}
does not meet the hyperplane

\tilde{w}
on this hyperplane. Hence, can use the maximum principle again for further times (

). We obtain that outside of any open enlargement of the set

\overline{Q_{1}(0)\times(-1/K,1/K)}
there holds

|\tilde{w}(X,t)|\leq C_{K}\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w{\mathds{1}}_{t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))}\leq\tilde{C}_{K}.

Note the dependence on

which explains the two-step process. Modifying now the cutoff function
\eta

and again using (

) and representation of

)

(4.22)

|\tilde{w}(X,t)|\leq Ce^{-c|X|^{2}},\qquad\mbox{for all $(X,t)$ with }\|(X,t)\|\geq 2\mbox{ and }t<20,

. The final step is identical to the argument in

|\tilde{w}(X,t)|\leq C\|(X,t)\|^{-n-\eta},\qquad\mbox{for all $(X,t)$ with }\|(X,t)\|\geq 2.

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla w)\|_{L^{1}((2r^{-1}{\mathbb{Z}}_{1}\times(-2r^{-2},2r^{-2}))\cap r^{-1}\tilde{\Omega})}\leq C_{K}.

4.5.
Small atoms, part 2

\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u{\mathds{1}}_{1/K<t<1/K+1})\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)}\lesssim_{K}\|\widetilde{N}(\nabla u{\mathds{1}}_{1/K<t<2/K})\|_{L^{1}(\partial\Omega)},
