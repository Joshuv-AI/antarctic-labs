Abstract
. We study the central limit theorem (CLT) for linear eigenvalue statistics of several types of matrix models, whose entries are having exploding moments, i.e., moments of the entries are increasing with the size of the matrix. In particular, we study elliptic, centrosymmetric, circulant, and inter-correlated block matrices. The CLTs are established using asymptotic Wick formula. Keywords: exploding moment, central limit theorem, elliptic, circulant, centrosymmetric, inter-correlated block MSC2020 Subject Classification: 15B52; 60BXX; 60FXX



1.
Introduction

We consider different types of random matrix models with exploding moments. We study the asymptotic behavior of normalized traces and the fluctuations of the associated linear eigenvalue statistic for monomial test functions. In particular, we prove a central limit theorem (CLT) for these generalized moments under suitable growth conditions. It is observed that although Gaussian fluctuations may still occur, the normalization and the variance depend on the rate at which the moments increase. The CLT is proved by establishing an asymptotic Wick formula for the product of linear eigenvalue statistics.

We carry out this analysis for several structured random matrix models, including elliptic, centrosymmetric, circulant, inter-correlated block matrices, whose CLTs were established in
[
9
,
7
,
2
,
6
]
when the entries of the matrices were light-tailed. We believe that the techniques used for circulant matrices with exploding moments can be utilized to prove CLT results for some other types of structured random matrices with exploding moments, such as reverse circulant, symmetric circulant, Toeplitz, and Hankel matrices.

Fluctuation results for heavy-tailed random matrices have been studied in various frameworks; see, for example,
[
3
,
8
,
5
]
. Our proof relies on the techniques demonstrated by Male et al.
[
8
,
4
]
, where they established CLT for exploding moment and heavy tailed Wigner type random matrices. However, when the matrix model is changed from Wigner to other types such as Elliptic, Centrosymmetric, the internal combinatorics to establish Wick’s formula — the central part of the CLT proof — changes significantly. Moreover, it also impacts the final covariance kernel. Besides, we use a slightly generalized definition of exploding moment, where the rate of explosion is controlled by an additional parameter
\alpha>0.
Additionally, we also consider a strongly structured matrix model, namely the circulant random matrix. In this latter case, the techniques used for proving the CLT are different from the techniques used in the former cases. The stringent structure of the circulant matrices, which was analyzed in the context of CLT by Adhikari et al.
[
2
]
in the light tail case, makes it stand out from the Wigner or Elliptic types of matrices. But the techniques used in
[
1
,
4
]
are not directly applicable here.

This article is split into several sections, where each section is dedicated to a particular type of matrix model. Also, for the convenience of the reader, the sections are mostly made self-contained.

2.
Elliptic matrices

We consider a sequence of non-Hermitian random matrices

A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}X=[a_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N},

whose entries satisfy the correlation and exploding moment conditions stated in the Condition
2.1
. Here
X=[x_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
is a real-valued non-Hermitian random matrix.

Condition 2.1

(Elliptic random matrices with exploding moments)
.

Let
A=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}X=[a_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N},
where
X=[x_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

For all
1\leq i,j\leq N
,

\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}]=0,\qquad\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}^{2}]=1.

(2)

The diagonal entries of
X
are independent among themselves and of off-diagonal entries as well. The off-diagonal entries’ only dependence occurs between pairs
(x_{ij},x_{ji}),
while
(x_{ij},x_{ji})
is independent of
(x_{kl},x_{lk})
if
\{i,j\}\neq\{k,l\}.

(3)

The entries of
X
have exploding moments in the sense that for all
k,l\geq 0
with
k+l\geq 2
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}^{k}x_{ji}^{l}]}{N^{(k+l)/2-\alpha}}\longrightarrow C_{k,l},

for
i<j
. For all
k\geq 1
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[|x_{ii}|^{k}]}{N^{k/2-\alpha}}=O(1),

for some
\alpha>0
, where
(C_{k,l})_{k,l\geq 0}
are finite constants.

Remark 2.2
.

The matrix ensemble considered here belongs to the class of random matrices with exploding moments, which is quantified by the factor
N^{k/2-\alpha}
. In particular, the factor

N^{-\alpha}
shows the moment growth does not depend on
k
, and therefore this is not just a rescaled version of a light-tailed model.

The parameter
\alpha
controls how fast the moments grow with
N
. When
\alpha=0
, the model reduces to the classical light-tailed setting with bounded moments. For
\alpha>0
, the higher moments increase with
N
, which is characteristic of heavy-tailed behavior. Moreover, larger values of
\alpha
mean the heavy-tailed effect becomes stronger.

2.1.
Convergence of the normalized traces

Let
k\geq 1
be an integer. The normalized trace of
A^{k}
can be written as

(2.1)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(A^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{N}a_{i_{1}i_{2}}a_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots a_{i_{k}i_{1}}.

To study the asymptotic behavior of this trace, we consider
\mathcal{P}(k)
, which denotes the set of partitions of
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
.
For each
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)
, define
S_{\pi}\subset[N]^{k}
as the set of all multi indices
(i_{1},i_{2}\dots,i_{k})
satisfying
i_{m}=i_{n}
iff
m\sim_{\pi}n.

Thus, the expansion (
2.1
) can be rewritten as

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(A^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\mathbf{i}\in S_{\pi}}a_{i_{1}i_{2}}a_{i_{2}i_{3}}\cdots a_{i_{k}i_{1}}

(2.2)

\displaystyle=:\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}\tau_{N}^{e}[\pi].

Note that, because of the partition
\pi
, the same random variables get merged together in
\tau^{e}_{N}[\pi],
and their multiplicity appear in their exponent. For instance, suppose
k=5,N=10,
and
\pi=\{\{1,3\},\{5\},\{2,4\}\}.
Then
i_{1}=i_{2},i_{2}=i_{4}
and
i_{5}
is neither of
i_{1},i_{2}
, which yields a sequence of the form
\mathbb{E}[a_{19}a_{91}a_{19}a_{97}a_{71}].
This sequence will reduce to
\mathbb{E}[a_{19}^{2}a_{91}]\mathbb{E}[a_{97}]\mathbb{E}[a_{71}].
This enables us to analyze the limiting behavior of
\operatorname{Tr}(A^{k})/N
in terms of
C_{k,l}s
— the limiting mixed moments.

Now, for each partition
\pi
of
\{1,2,\dots,k\},
we consider a directed graph

T_{\pi}=(V,E)
, where
V=\{V_{1},V_{2},\dots\}
is the set of blocks of
\pi
and
E
is the multiset of directed edges defined as follows. There is an edge from block the
V_{i}
to the block
V_{j}
if there exists
m\in\{1,2,\dots,k\}
such that
m\in V_{i}
and
m+1\in V_{j},
where
k+1
is identified with
1.

For such a graph
T_{\pi}
, we can write

(2.3)

\displaystyle\tau_{N}^{e}[\pi]=\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi:V\to[N]\\
\text{injective}\end{subarray}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}.

Definition 2.3

(Thick tree)
.

A directed graph
T=(V,E)
is called a thick tree if it is
a connected graph without loops and does not have any vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected
only
by a single directed edge. Additionally, the underlying simple graph
\mathcal{T}=(V,\mathcal{E}),
obtained by ignoring orientation and multiplicity satisfies
|V|=|\mathcal{E}|+1
.

Theorem 2.5

(Convergence of normalized trace)
.

Let
A
be the matrix same as in Condition
2.1
.
For any partition

\pi\in\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}(k)
, let
\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]
be as defined in (
2.3
). Then, as
N\to\infty
, the following hold.

(a)

For
\alpha>1
we have

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]]\longrightarrow 0.

(b)

For
\alpha=1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow\tau^{e}[T_{\pi}]:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}C_{k,l}^{\,q_{k,l}},&\text{if $T_{\pi}$ is a thick fat},\\[8.00003pt]
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
q_{k,l}
denote the number of ordered vertex pairs
(i,j)
with
i<j
such that there are exactly
k
edges from vertex
i
to vertex
j
and
l
edges from vertex
j
to vertex
i
in thick tree
T_{\pi}
.

(c)

For
\alpha<1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\sim\begin{cases}0,&\parbox[t]{207.0021pt}{\raggedright$T_{\pi}$ has either a vertex pair $\{u,v\}$ connected by a single directed edge, or a vertex with a single loop (or both)\@add@raggedright},\\[6.00006pt]
O(N^{|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}),&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
|V|
is the number of vertices of
T_{\pi}
, and
\mathfrak{p}
is the number of edges of the graph, which is obtained by forgetting the multiplicity and orientation of the edges of the graph
T_{\pi}
.

Proof.

For a fixed partition
\pi
in
\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}(k),
let
\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]
be as defined in (
2.3
). Then

(2.4)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi:V\to[N]\\
\text{injective}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg].

Since the distribution of the entries
a_{ij}
is invariant under relabeling of the
indices,

\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg]

does not depend on the particular choice of the injective map
\phi
. Therefore,

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\frac{N!}{(N-|V|)!}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg]

(2.5)

\displaystyle=:\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-|V|)!}\delta_{N}^{0}(T(a_{N})),

where
\delta_{N}^{0}(T(a_{N}))=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg].
Since
\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}]=0
for
1\leq i,j\leq N
, we have

(2.6)

\delta_{N}^{0}(T(\mathbf{a}_{N}))=0

whenever there exists a vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected only by a single edge in
T_{\pi}
, or there is a vertex having a loop of multiplicity one. Therefore, to obtain a non-zero contribution, from this point onward, we assume that
T_{\pi}
does not have any vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is only connected by a single edge, and there is no vertex having a loop of multiplicity one. We denote such graphs by
\hat{T}_{\pi}
. The reduced simple graph will be denoted by
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\pi},
which is obtained after ignoring the direction and multiplicity of the edges of
\hat{T}_{\pi}.

By Condition
2.1
, the diagonal entries are independent among themselves and of off-diagonal entries as well. The off-diagonal entries’ dependence occurs only within the pairs
(x_{ij},x_{ji}),
while
(x_{ij},x_{ji})
is independent of
(x_{kl},x_{lk})
if
(i,j)\neq(k,l).

For
k\geq 2
, let
p_{k}
denote the number of vertices carrying exactly
k
loops. For
k,l\geq 0
with
k+l\geq 2,
let
q_{k,l}
denote the number of ordered vertex pairs
(i,j)
with
i<j
such that there are exactly
k
edges from vertex
i
to vertex
j
and
l
edges from vertex
j
to vertex
i
.
Then

\displaystyle\delta_{N}^{0}(T(a_{N}))

\displaystyle=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 2}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{11}^{k}]\big)^{p_{k}}\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{12}^{k}a_{21}^{l}]\big)^{q_{k,l}}.

Recall that
a_{ij}=x_{ij}/\sqrt{N}
. By Condition
2.1
, we have

N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]=O(1),\qquad N^{-\frac{k+l}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]\longrightarrow C_{k,l}.

Therefore, each loop contributes a factor of order
N^{-\alpha}
, and each ordered vertex
pair
(i,j),
with
i<j
also contributes a factor of order
N^{-\alpha}
.
Therefore,

(2.7)

\displaystyle\delta_{N}^{0}(T(a_{N}))=N^{-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}\left(\prod_{k\geq 2}\left(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{11}^{k}]\right)^{p_{k}}\right)\left(\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\Big(N^{-\frac{k+l}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]\Big)^{q_{k,l}}\right),

where

\mathfrak{p}:=\sum_{k\geq 2}p_{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}q_{k,l}.

Here
\mathfrak{p}
is the number of edges of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\pi}
. Now, by using (
2.7
) in (
2.1
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[\hat{T}_{\pi}]\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-|V|)!}\frac{1}{N^{\alpha\mathfrak{p}}}\left(\prod_{k\geq 2}\left(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{11}^{k}]\right)^{p_{k}}\right)\left(\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\Big(N^{-\frac{k+l}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]\Big)^{q_{k,l}}\right)

(2.8)

\displaystyle=N^{|V|-(\alpha\mathfrak{p}+1)}\left(\prod_{k\geq 2}\left(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{11}^{k}]\right)^{p_{k}}\right)\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\Big(N^{-\frac{k+l}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]\Big)^{q_{k,l}}+o(1).

Since
for all
k,l\geq 0
with
k+l\geq 2

N^{-\frac{k+l}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]\longrightarrow C_{k,l},

and for all
k\geq 2

N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]=O(1),

by Condition
2.1
, the asymptotic behavior of (
2.1
)
depends on the power of
N
that is on
N^{|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}.
We now discuss the following cases for different values of
\alpha
as follows.

Case (a)
(\alpha>1)
.
For any connected graph, we have
|V|\leq\mathfrak{p}+1
. Since
\hat{T}_{\pi}
is connected by definition, it follows that
|V|\leq\mathfrak{p}+1
. Since
\alpha>1
, we have

|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}-\alpha\mathfrak{p}=\big(1-\alpha\big)\mathfrak{p}<0,

which implies

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[\hat{T}_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow 0.

If
T_{\pi}
has a vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected only by a single edge, or a vertex with a single loop (or both), then from (
2.6
) we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]=0.

Therefore, for any graph
T_{\pi}
associated to
\pi
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow 0.

Case (b)
(\alpha=1)
.
In this case, we have

|V|-\alpha\mathfrak{p}-1=|V|-\mathfrak{p}-1,

where
\mathfrak{p}
is the number of edges of
\hat{\mathcal{T}_{\pi}}
. From
[
11
, Theorem 2.1.4]
,
|V|=\mathfrak{p}+1
if and only if
T_{\pi}
is thick tree.
Thus, for thick tree
T_{\pi}
, we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 1\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}C_{k,l}^{\,q_{k,l}}.

Now, suppose that
T_{\pi}
is not thick tree; that is, there exists a vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected by a single directed edge only, or there is a vertex having a loop of multiplicity one, or
|V|\neq\mathfrak{p}+1
. In the first two cases, we have
\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]=0.
In the last case, since
|V|\leq\mathfrak{p}+1
for any connected graph, it follows that
|V|-\mathfrak{p}-1<0,
which implies

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow 0.

Case (c)
(\alpha<1)
.

If
T_{\pi}
has a vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected by a single directed edge only, or there exists a vertex with a single loop, then from (
2.6
) we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]=0

Since
\alpha<1
, for the graph
\hat{T}_{\pi}
, we have

|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}\leq\mathfrak{p}-\alpha\mathfrak{p}=(1-\alpha)\mathfrak{p}>0.

This inequality does not give the sign of
|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}
in general, it may be positive, zero, or negative.
Thus

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]\big]\sim O(N^{|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}).

∎

Going ahead, we now discuss the CLT for the normalized trace in the following subsection.

2.2.
CLT for the normalized traces

We consider the centered and normalized random variables

(2.9)

\displaystyle Z_{N}(T_{\pi}):=\sqrt{N}\,\big(\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]-\mathbb{E}[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{\pi}]]\big).

It is sufficient to prove the convergence of
\{Z_{N}(T_{\pi})\}_{\pi\in\bigcup_{k\geq 1}\mathcal{P}(k)}
to Gaussian process in order to establish the CLT for
\{Z_{N}(k)\}_{k\geq 1}
,
where

Z_{N}(k)=\sqrt{N}\left(\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(A^{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(A^{k})\right]\right).

Theorem 2.6

(CLT for the normalized traces)
.

Let
A
be the matrix same as in Condition
2.1
with
\alpha=1
. For any partition
\pi\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{P}(k)
, let
Z_{N}(T_{\pi})
be defined as in (
2.9
).
Then the family of random variables

\big(Z_{N}(T_{\pi})\big)_{\pi\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{P}(k)}

converges to a centered
Gaussian process

\big(z(T_{\pi})\big)_{\pi\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{P}(k)}
as
N\to\infty.

Moreover, for any two partitions
\pi_{1},\pi_{2}
,
the covariance of the limiting process is given by

\mathrm{Cov}\left(z(T_{\pi_{1}}),z(T_{\pi_{2}})\right)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{\pi_{1}},T_{\pi_{2}})}\tau^{e}[T],

where
\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{\pi_{1}},T_{\pi_{2}})
denotes the collection of graphs obtained by disjoint copies of the graphs
T_{\pi_{1}}
and
T_{\pi_{2}}
and gluing them with at least one common edge, and
\tau^{e}[T]
is same as defined in the Theorem
2.5
(b).

Proof.

Let
\pi_{1},\pi_{2},\dots,\pi_{r}\in\bigcup_{k}\mathcal{P}(k)
.
Let
T_{1},T_{2},\dots,T_{r}
be directed graphs, each corresponding to a partition
\pi_{j}
, with
T_{j}=(V_{j},E_{j})
. These graphs are defined in the same way as in Section
2.1
. Recall from (
2.9
)

Z_{N}(T_{i})=\sqrt{N}\Big(\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{i}]-\mathbb{E}[\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{i}]]\Big),

where

\tau_{N}^{e}[T_{i}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\phi:V_{i}\to[N]\atop\text{injective}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{i}}a_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}.

To prove the convergence of
\{Z_{N}(T_{j})\}
to a Gaussian process,
it is sufficient to show that the joint moments

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

satisfy Wick’s formula. We now express

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=N^{-r/2}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r}\\
\phi_{j}:V_{j}\rightarrow[N]\text{ injective}\end{subarray}}\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Bigg(\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\bigg]\Bigg)\right].

Let
\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})
be the set of partitions of disjoint union of
V_{1}\cup V_{2}\cdots\cup V_{r}

such that each block contains at most one vertex from each
V_{j}
. For
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})
,
let

S_{\sigma}=\left\{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r}):\phi_{i}(v)=\phi_{j}(v^{\prime})\iff v\sim_{\sigma}v^{\prime}\right\}.

Then

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})}N^{-r/2}\sum_{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})\in S_{\sigma}}\omega_{N}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r}),

where

\omega_{N}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})=\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}\Bigg(\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}-\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\bigg]\Bigg)\right].

Since
\omega_{N}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})
depends only on
\sigma
so we denote

\omega_{N}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})=\omega_{N}(\sigma).

Thus,

(2.10)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})}N^{-r/2}\sum_{(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})\in S_{\sigma}}\omega_{N}(\phi_{1},\phi_{2},\dots,\phi_{r})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})}N^{-r/2}\frac{N!}{(N-|\sigma|)!}\omega_{N}(\sigma).

Now, if any graph
T_{i}
does not share an edge with any of the other graphs under
\sigma
, then by centering and independence of the entries of
A
, we have
\omega_{N}(\sigma)=0.

Only partitions
\sigma
that identify at least one edge between two graphs contribute. Therefore, we define

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})

\displaystyle=\Big\{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})\ \Big|\ \parbox{207.0021pt}{each graph $T_{i}$ shares at least one edge with another graph $T_{j}$ for $j\neq i$
}\Big\}.

Hence, from (
2.10
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})}N^{-r/2}\frac{N!}{(N-|\sigma|)!}\omega_{N}(\sigma)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})}N^{-r/2+|\sigma|}\,\omega_{N}(\sigma)\,(1+O(N^{-1})).

We now expand
\omega_{N}(\sigma)

(2.11)

\displaystyle\omega_{N}(\sigma)=\sum_{B\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,r\}}(-1)^{r-|B|}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{j\in B}\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\bigg]\prod_{j\notin B}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\bigg].

Since
\mathbb{E}[a_{ij}]=0
for
1\leq i,j\leq N

(2.12)

\omega_{N}(\sigma)=0

whenever there exists a vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected by a single directed edge only, or there is a vertex having a loop of multiplicity one (or both), in
T_{\pi}
. Therefore, to obtain a non-zero contribution, from this point onward, we assume that
T_{\pi}
does not have any vertex pair
\{u,v\}
, which is connected by a single directed edge, and there is no vertex having a loop of multiplicity one. We denote such graphs by
\hat{T}_{\pi}
. The reduced simple graph is denoted by
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\pi},
which is obtained after ignoring the direction and multiplicity of the edges of
\hat{T}_{\pi}
.

For a fixed

, let
T_{B}
be the directed graph obtained by merging
the vertices of
T_{j}
,
j\in B
, that belong to the same block of
\sigma
. Let

\hat{T}_{B}
with
k

and
q_{k,l}

with
u<v

such that there are
k

u
to
v
and
l

v
to
u
. By independence and the correlation assumptions on
A
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j\in B}\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\right]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 2}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{11}^{k}]\big)^{p_{k}}\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{12}^{k}a_{21}^{l}]\big)^{q_{k,l}}

\displaystyle=N^{-\mathfrak{p}_{B}}\prod_{k\geq 2}\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]}{N^{k/2-1}}\bigg)^{p_{k}}\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]}{N^{(k+l)/2-1}}\bigg)^{q_{k,l}}

(2.13)

\displaystyle=:N^{-\mathfrak{p}_{B}}\,\delta_{N}(B),

where

\mathfrak{p}_{B}:=\sum_{k\geq 2}p_{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}q_{k,l}.

Here
\mathfrak{p}_{B}
is the number of edges of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{B}
.

Let
T_{B^{\complement}}
be the graph obtained by merging the vertices of the graphs

,

,
that belong to the same block of
\sigma

Let

\displaystyle\prod_{j\notin B}\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E_{j}}a_{\phi_{j(u)}\phi_{j(v)}}\bigg]

\displaystyle=\prod_{k\geq 2}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{11}^{k}]\big)^{p_{k}{{}^{\prime}}}\displaystyle\prod_{\begin{subarray}{c}k,l\geq 0\\
k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\big(\mathbb{E}[a_{12}^{k}a_{21}^{l}]\big)^{q_{k,l}^{\prime}}

k+l\geq 2\end{subarray}}\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}x_{21}^{l}]}{N^{(k+l)/2-1}}\bigg)^{q_{k,l}^{\prime}}

\sigma

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

Here the quantities

and

We first consider
\sum_{B\subseteq\{1,2,\dots,r\}}N^{\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma}-\mathfrak{p}_{B}-\mathfrak{p}_{B^{\complement}}}.
Here

Then,

\displaystyle N^{\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma}-\mathfrak{p}_{B}-\mathfrak{p}_{B^{\complement}}}=\mathbf{1}_{\{B=\{1,2,\dots,r\}\}}+O(N^{-1}).

We now consider the remaining factors

Since

N^{c_{\sigma}-r/2}=O(N^{-\epsilon})\text{ if }c_{\sigma}<r/2,

where

Now, from

\displaystyle N^{|\sigma|-c_{\sigma}-\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma}}=O(N^{-1})\text{ if }|\sigma|-c_{\sigma}-\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma}<0

Consequently, if either

or

is not a forest, then

Thus, to obtain a non zero contribution, we need

, and graph

to be a forest, which is a collection of disjoint trees. Hence

(2.16)

\displaystyle N^{|\sigma|-c_{\sigma}-\mathfrak{p}_{\sigma}}=\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\text{ is a forest}\}}+O(N^{-1}),

and

(2.17)

\displaystyle N^{c_{\sigma}-r/2}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }c_{\sigma}=r/2\\[4.0pt]
O(N^{-1}),&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Each partition
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},V_{2},\dots,V_{r})
induces another partition
\bar{\sigma}
of
\{1,2,\dots,r\}
, defined by

i\sim_{\bar{\sigma}}j\quad\text{if and only if}\quad T_{i}\text{ and }T_{j}\text{ belong to the same connected component of }\hat{T}_{\sigma}.

Then we can write (
2.17
) as follows

(2.18)

\displaystyle N^{c_{\sigma}-r/2}=\begin{cases}1,&\text{if }\bar{\sigma}\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r),\\[4.0pt]
O(N^{-1}),&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

where
\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)
denotes the set of pair partition of
\{1,2,\dots,r\}.

Thus from equations (
2.14
),(
2.15
), (
2.16
), and (
2.18
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},\dots,V_{r})\\
\bar{\sigma}=\pi\end{subarray}}\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\text{ is a forest of }r/2\text{ trees}\}}\,\delta_{N}(\sigma)+O(N^{-1})

(2.19)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\pi}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{i},V_{j})}\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\text{ is a tree}\}}\,\delta_{N}(\sigma)\right)+O(N^{-1}),

where in the last equality we have used the independence of the entries of the matrix
A
to split
\delta_{N}(\sigma)
, for
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},\dots,V_{r}),
as a product of
\delta_{N}(\sigma)
, where
\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{i},V_{j})
.
We now evaluate the covariance kernel, let
r=2

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(T_{1})Z_{N}(T_{2})]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{1},V_{2})}\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\text{ is a tree}\}}\,\delta(\sigma)+o(1)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{1},T_{2})}\tau_{e}[\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}]+o(1),

where
\delta(\sigma)=\lim_{N\to\infty}\delta_{N}(\sigma)=\tau^{e}[\hat{T}_{\sigma}].
Here
\tau^{e}[\hat{T}_{\sigma}]
is same as
\tau^{e}[T]
defined in the Theorem
2.5
(b).

Thus, from equation (
2.2
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{j=1}^{r}Z_{N}(T_{j})\right]

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\pi}\left(\sum_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(V_{i},V_{j})}\mathbf{1}_{\{\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\sigma}\text{ is a tree}\}}\,\delta_{N}(\sigma)\right)+o(1)

(2.20)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)}\prod_{\{i,j\}\in\pi}\sum_{T\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{i},T_{j})}\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(T_{i})Z_{N}(T_{j})]+o(1),

which is precisely Wick’s formula. Hence, the proof is complete.
∎

3.
Non-Hermitian matrices

In this section, we consider a non-Hermitian matrix model with
independent entries and exploding moments.
Unlike the previous Section
2.1
, where correlations between matrix entries
were allowed, here all entries are independent.

Let
M
be a sequence of non-Hermitian random matrices of order
N\times N
satisfying some conditions. In particular, we assume

Condition 3.1

(Matrix assumptions)
.

Let
M=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}X=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}[x_{ij}]_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
sequence of non-Hermitian real valued random matrices. Assume that the matrix
X
satisfies the following conditions.

(1)

\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}]=0,\quad\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}^{2}]=1
for all
1\leq i,j\leq N.

(2)

All entries
\{x_{ij}\}_{1\leq i,j\leq N}
are independent.

(3)

The entries of
X
have exploding moments in the sense that for all
k\geq 2
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}^{k}]}{N^{k/2-\alpha}}\longrightarrow C_{k},\;\;\;i\neq j,

and for all
k\geq 1
,

\frac{\mathbb{E}[|x_{ii}|^{k}]}{N^{k/2-\alpha}}=O(1),

for some
\alpha>0
, where
(C_{k})_{k\geq 0}
are finite constants.

As in the previous Section
2.1
, we expand

\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(M_{N}^{k})=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}],

where
T_{\pi}=(V,E)
is the directed graph associated with the partition
\pi
defined as similar to as in Section
2.1
.

Theorem 3.2

(Convergence of normalized trace)
.

Let
M
be the matrix same as in Condition
3.1
.
For any partition
\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)
, let

\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\phi:V\to[N]\atop\mathrm{injective}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E}m_{\phi(u)\phi(v)},

be defined for
M
same as in (
2.3
).
Then, as
N\to\infty
, the following hold.

(1)

For
\alpha>1
and for every
T_{\pi}
we have

\mathbb{E}[\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}]]\longrightarrow 0.

(2)

For
\alpha=1
we have

\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}]\big]\longrightarrow\tau^{nh}[T_{\pi}]:=\begin{cases}\displaystyle\prod_{k\geq 2}C_{k}^{\,q_{k}},&\text{if $T_{\pi}$ is a fat tree},\\[8.00003pt]
0,&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
q_{k}
denote the number of unordered vertex pairs
\{i,j\}
such that there are exactly
k
edges between these vertices.

(3)

For
\alpha<1
we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}]\big]\sim\begin{cases}0,&\parbox[t]{207.0021pt}{\raggedright$T_{\pi}$ contains any edge of multiplicity one (in any direction) or any vertex with a single loop (or both),\@add@raggedright}\\[6.00006pt]
O(N^{|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}),&\text{otherwise,}\end{cases}

where
|V|
is the number of vertices of
T_{\pi}
, and
\mathfrak{p}
is the number of edges of the graph obtained by forgetting the multiplicity and orientation of the edges of the graph
T_{\pi}
.

Proof.

All the notations used in this section are borrowed from the Section
2.1
. For a fixed partition
\pi
, let
T_{\pi}=(V,E)
be the associated directed graph. Then we have

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{nh}[T_{\pi}]\big]

\displaystyle=\frac{(N-1)!}{(N-|V|)!}\delta_{N}^{0}(T(m_{N})),

where
\delta_{N}^{0}(T(m_{N}))=\mathbb{E}\bigg[\prod_{(u,v)\in E}m_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}\bigg].

Since
\mathbb{E}[x_{ij}]=0
, we have
\delta_{N}^{0}(T(m_{N}))=0
whenever there exists an ordered pair of vertices
(u,v)
such that they are connected by a directed edge of multiplicity one (in any direction), or there exists a vertex having a loop of multiplicity one. Therefore, to obtain a non zero contribution, we henceforth assume that the graphs
T_{\pi}
do not contain any ordered pair
(u,v)
connected by a directed edge of multiplicity one (in any direction), and no vertex has a loop of multiplicity one. We denote such graphs by
\hat{T}_{\pi}
. The reduced simple graph is denoted by
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\pi},
which is obtained after ignoring the direction and multiplicity of the edges of
\hat{T}_{\pi}.

By Condition
3.1
, all the entries of the matrix are independent, all diagonal entries are identically distributed, and all off-diagonal entries are identically distributed. Let, for
k\geq 2
, let
p_{k}
denote the number of vertices carrying exactly
k
loops. For
k\geq 2
let
q_{k}
denote the number of unordered vertex pairs
\{i,j\}
such that there are exactly
k
edges between these vertices. Then

(3.2)

\displaystyle\delta_{N}^{0}(T(m_{N}))=N^{-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}\left(\prod_{k\geq 2}\left(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]\right)^{p_{k}}\right)\left(\displaystyle\prod_{k\geq 2}\Big(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}]\Big)^{q_{k}}\right),

where

\mathfrak{p}:=\sum_{k\geq 2}p_{k}+\displaystyle\sum_{k\geq 2}q_{k}

is the number of edges of
\hat{\mathcal{T}}_{\pi}.
Now, by using (
3.2
) in (
3.1
) we have

(3.3)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\tau_{N}^{nh}[\hat{T}_{\pi}]\big]

\displaystyle=N^{|V|-(\alpha\mathfrak{p}+1)}\left(\prod_{k\geq 2}\left(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]\right)^{p_{k}}\right)\left(\displaystyle\prod_{k\geq 2}\Big(N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}]\Big)^{q_{k}}\right)+o(1).

Since
for all
k\geq 2

N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[x_{12}^{k}]\longrightarrow C_{k},

and for all
k\geq 2

N^{-\frac{k}{2}+\alpha}\mathbb{E}[|x_{11}|^{k}]=O(1),

by Condition
3.1
, the asymptotic behavior of (
3.3
)
depends the power of
N
that is on
N^{|V|-1-\alpha\mathfrak{p}}.
Now, by following the same set of arguments as outlined in Theorem
2.5
we conclude the Theorem
3.2
. However, one should keep in mind that the underlying graph here is slightly different from the one used in
2.5
. Here, for any pair of vertices
\{u,v\},
the edges
u\to v
and
v\to u
are independent, whereas those were correlated in the former case. This is reflected in the part (b) of the Theorems
2.5
and
3.2
. In the first case, it was thick tree, which is changed to fat tree in the current theorem.
∎

We now state CLT for the normalized traces.

Theorem 3.3

(CLT for the normalized traces)
.

Let
M
be the matrix same as in Condition
3.1
with
\alpha=1
. For any partition

.

\mathrm{Cov}\left(z(T_{\pi_{1}}),z(T_{\pi_{2}})\right)=\sum_{T\in\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{\pi_{1}},T_{\pi_{2}})}\tau^{nh}[T],

where
\mathcal{P}^{\#}(T_{\pi_{1}},T_{\pi_{2}})
denotes the collection of graphs obtained by disjoint copies of the graphs
T_{\pi_{1}}
and

and gluing them with at least one common edge, and where

is same as defined in the Theorem
3.2
.

Proof.

This follows by a similar argument to that in the proof of Theorem
2.6
.

.

4.
Correlated block diagonal matrices with exploding moments

We consider a block diagonal matrix

\displaystyle M=\left(\begin{array}[]{ll}M^{(1)}&0\\

where, for

We assume the following.

.

(1)

For

, let

The normalized trace of

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(M^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}((M^{(1)})^{k})+\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}((M^{(2)})^{k})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{i_{1},\dots,i_{k}=1}^{N}(m_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{(1)}m_{i_{2}i_{3}}^{(1)}\cdots m_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(1)}+m_{i_{1}i_{2}}^{(2)}m_{i_{2}i_{3}}^{(2)}\cdots m_{i_{k}i_{1}}^{(2)}).

Let
\mathcal{P}(k)
denote the set of partitions of
\{1,2,\dots,k\}
. For each partition

Then from (

(4.4)

\displaystyle\frac{1}{N}\operatorname{Tr}(M^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}(k)}\left(\tau_{N}^{(1)}[T_{\pi}]+\tau_{N}^{(2)}[T_{\pi}]\right),

where

\tau_{N}^{(t)}[T_{\pi}]=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\phi:V\to[N]\\
\text{injective}\end{subarray}}\prod_{(u,v)\in E}m^{(t)}_{\phi(u)\phi(v)}.

Here

, constructed exactly as in Section

.

(Colored fat tree)
.

A connected directed graph

\mathcal{T}=(V,\mathcal{E}),
obtained by ignoring orientation and multiplicity satisfies
|V|=|\mathcal{E}|+1

To study fluctuations of the matrix

5.
Structured ensembles: Centrosymmetric and Circulant matrices

In this section, we extend the analysis of correlated non-Hermitian random matrices with exploding moments to several structured random matrix ensembles. Structured matrices arise naturally in applications, including signal processing, time series analysis, wireless communications, and statistical physics. Unlike fully independent ensembles, these matrices exhibit deterministic algebraic symmetries that introduce additional dependencies among entries. It is therefore natural to ask whether the results established in the previous sections, particularly the central limit theorem for linear eigenvalue statistic, continue to hold under such structural constraints.

5.2.
Circulant Matrices

In this section, we establish the central limit theorem for linear eigenvalue
statistic of random circulant matrices in the exploding moment regime.

Let

C=[c_{ij}]_{0\leq i,j\leq N-1}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Circ}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})

be an
N\times N
random circulant matrix generated by independent
real valued random variables
\{x_{j}\}_{j=0}^{N-1}
. The entries of
C
satisfy

c_{ij}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\,x_{(i-j)\bmod N},\qquad 0\leq i,j\leq N-1.

Equivalently, the matrix can be written in the form

C=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\begin{bmatrix}x_{0}&x_{1}&x_{2}&\dots&x_{N-1}\\
x_{N-1}&x_{0}&x_{1}&\dots&x_{N-2}\\
x_{N-2}&x_{N-1}&x_{0}&\dots&x_{N-3}\\
\vdots&\vdots&\vdots&\ddots&\vdots\\
x_{1}&x_{2}&x_{3}&\dots&x_{0}\end{bmatrix}.

Each row of
C
is obtained from the previous row by a cyclic
right shift.

Condition 5.5

(Circulant Ensemble with Exploding Moments)
.

Let

C=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Circ}(x_{0},x_{1},\dots,x_{N-1})

be an
N\times N
random circulant matrix generated by independent real valued random variables
\{x_{i}\}_{i=0}^{N-1}
. We assume the following.

(1)

\mathbb{E}[x_{i}]=0
,
\mathbb{E}[x_{i}^{2}]=1
.

(2)

\{x_{i}\}_{0\leq i\leq N-1}
are independent.

(3)

For all
k\geq 2
, and
0\leq i\leq N-1

\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{i}^{k}]}{N^{k/2-\alpha}}\longrightarrow C_{k},

where
\alpha\geq 0
and
(C_{k})_{k\geq 2}
are finite constants.

For circulant matrices, spectral analysis simplifies significantly. The eigenvalues of
C
are given by

\lambda_{k}=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_{j}\omega_{N}^{jk},\qquad\omega_{N}=e^{2\pi i/N},\qquad k=0,1,\dots,N-1.

We now consider

\displaystyle\operatorname{Tr}(C^{k})

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\lambda_{m}^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\sum_{j=0}^{N-1}x_{j}\omega_{N}^{jm}\bigg)^{k}

\displaystyle=\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0}^{N-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}\omega_{N}^{m(j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2}}\sum_{j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0}^{N-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\omega_{N}^{m(j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k})}

(5.1)

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=0(\text{mod }N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}x_{j_{1}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}

where in the last equality we used the following identity

\sum_{m=0}^{N-1}\omega_{N}^{mr}=\begin{cases}N,&r=0\;(\text{mod }N),\\
0,&\text{otherwise}.\end{cases}

Thus from (
5.2
), we have

(5.2)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Tr}(C^{k})]=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=0(\text{mod }N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}]

Since
\mathbb{E}[x_{i}]=0
and the variables are independent, therefore

\mathbb{E}[x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}]
is non zero only when each variable
appears at least twice. Let us consider

i_{1},i_{2},\dots,i_{r}
be the distinct indices with multiplicities

m_{1},m_{2},\dots,m_{r}
such that

m_{1}+m_{2}+\cdots+m_{r}=k,\;m_{i}\geq 2.

Thus, from Condition
5.5
, we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\prod_{a=1}^{r}\mathbb{E}[x_{i_{a}}^{m_{a}}]

\displaystyle=N^{k/2-r\alpha}\prod_{a=1}^{r}\bigg(\frac{\mathbb{E}[x_{i_{a}}^{m_{a}}]}{N^{{m_{a}}/2-\alpha}}\bigg)

\displaystyle\sim N^{k/2-r\alpha}\prod_{a=1}^{r}C_{m_{a}}.

We now estimate the number of index tuples satisfying the constraint

j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=0(\text{mod }N).
Without the constraint, the number of ways to choose
r
distinct indices is
O(N^{r})
. Now, from (
5.2
) we have

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Tr}(C^{k})]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=0(\text{mod }N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}[x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=0\\
i_{1},\dots i_{r}\text{ are distinct}\\
m_{a}\geq 2,\,\sum m_{a}=k\\
m_{1}i_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}i_{r}=0(\text{mod }N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r}!}\mathbb{E}[x_{i_{i}}^{m_{1}}\cdots x_{i_{r}}^{m_{r}}]

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{r}=0\\
i_{1},\dots i_{r}\text{ are distinct}\\
m_{a}\geq 2,\,\sum m_{a}=k\\
m_{1}i_{1}+\cdots+m_{r}i_{r}=0(\text{mod }N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r}!}\mathbb{E}[x_{i_{i}}^{m_{1}}]\cdots\mathbb{E}[x_{i_{r}}^{m_{r}}]

\displaystyle\sim\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\\
m_{a}\geq 2,\,\sum m_{a}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r}!}N^{r-1}N^{k/2-r\alpha}\prod_{a=1}^{r}C_{m_{a}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\\
m_{a}\geq 2,\,\sum m_{a}=k\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r}!}N^{r(1-\alpha)}\prod_{a=1}^{r}C_{m_{a}}.

Therefore,

\lim_{N\to\infty}\mathbb{E}[\operatorname{Tr}(C^{k})]=\begin{cases}\infty,&\alpha<1,\\[6.0pt]
\displaystyle\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}(m_{1},\dots,m_{r})\\
m_{a}\geq 2,\,\sum_{a=1}^{r}m_{a}=k\\
1\leq r\leq\left\lfloor\frac{k}{2}\right\rfloor\end{subarray}}\frac{k!}{m_{1}!\cdots m_{r}!}\prod_{a=1}^{r}C_{m_{a}},&\alpha=1,\\[12.0pt]
0,&\alpha>1.\end{cases}

We now move to the CLT for the normalized traces.
Recall from (
5.2
) that for every integer
k\geq 1
,

(5.3)

\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k})=\frac{1}{N^{k/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}\cdots x_{j_{k}}.

We now define the centered and normalized traces by

(5.4)

Z_{N}(k):=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k})\right].

We now state the CLT for the normalized traces.

Theorem 5.6

(CLT for the normalized traces)
.

Let
C_{N}
be the random circulant matrix satisfying
Condition
5.5

with
\alpha=1
. Then for
k\geq 1
,

Z_{N}(k)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k})-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k})\right]

converges in distribution to a centered Gaussian random variable
z(k)
. Moreover, the Covariance kernel is given as follows.

(5.5)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Cov}(z(k),z(l))=\begin{cases}k!,&\text{if }k=l,\\
0,&\text{if }k\neq l.\end{cases}+o(1),

Proof.

We prove the theorem using the Wick’s formula. For fixed integers
k_{1},\dots,k_{r}\geq 1
, consider the joint moment, we have

\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r}Z_{N}(k_{i})\right].

From (
5.4
) and (
5.3
), we have

\displaystyle\prod_{i=1}^{r}Z_{N}(k_{i})

\displaystyle=\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k_{i}})-\mathbb{E}\left[\frac{1}{\sqrt{N}}\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k_{i}})\right]\Bigg)

\displaystyle=N^{-r/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\bigg(\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k_{i}})-\mathbb{E}\Big[\operatorname{Tr}(C_{N}^{k_{i}})\Big]\bigg)

\displaystyle=N^{-r/2}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\frac{1}{N^{k_{i}/2-1}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\Big(x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}-\mathbb{E}[x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}]\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=N^{-r/2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})-r}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\Big(x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}-\mathbb{E}[x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}]\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=N^{r/2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})}}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Bigg(\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\Big(x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}-\mathbb{E}[x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}]\Big)\Bigg)

\displaystyle=N^{r/2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\\
1\leq i\leq r\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}\Big(x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}-\mathbb{E}[x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}]\Big)

\displaystyle=:N^{r/2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\\
1\leq i\leq r\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\prod_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\big(j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\big),

where

A_{i}\big(j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\big)=x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}-\mathbb{E}[x_{j^{(i)}_{1}}\cdots x_{j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}}]

This implies

(5.6)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{r}Z_{N}(k_{i})\right]

\displaystyle=N^{r/2}\frac{1}{N^{\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}=0\\
j^{(i)}_{1}+\cdots+j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\\
1\leq i\leq r\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Bigg[\prod_{i=1}^{r}A_{i}\big(j^{(i)}_{1},\dots,j^{(i)}_{k_{i}}\big)\Bigg].

This product contains
r
centered trace blocks i.e.,
Z_{N}(k_{i})
. Since
\mathbb{E}[x_{j}]=0
, only those configurations, where every random variable appears at least twice, have nontrivial contributions. If each block is independent of the rest of the blocks, then after fixing a configuration, we obtain
r
disjoint blocks. In that case, because of the self centering of the blocks themselves, the contribution is zero. In fact, even if a single block is independent of all the others, its expectation vanishes due to centering, and hence the entire contribution is zero. Therefore, to obtain a non-zero contribution, each block must be linked, that is sharing a common random variable, to at least one other block.

It is evident that the sum in the right hand side of (
5.6
) can be re-expressed as a sum of different types of linked-block configurations. In particular, the sum contains all possible terms from
\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(r)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\pi}\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(k_{i})Z_{N}(k_{j})].
Additionally, it also contains the terms where more than two blocks are linked. However, as discussed below, the latter case does not contribute anything asymptotically. We now analyze all the possible cases as follows.

Case 1 (Exactly two blocks are linked).

In this case, we assume that each block is linked to exactly one other block. Consequently,
r
must be even. Consider the following example with four chains.

(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}x_{i_{4}})(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}x_{j_{3}}x_{j_{4}})(x_{t_{1}}x_{t_{2}}x_{t_{3}}x_{t_{4}})(x_{s_{1}}x_{s_{2}}x_{s_{3}}x_{s_{4}}).

Case 1(a) (Complete overlap of the chains).

We link the first chain with the second chain as follows.

x_{i_{1}}
x_{i_{2}}
x_{i_{3}}
x_{i_{4}}
x_{j_{1}}
x_{j_{2}}
x_{j_{3}}
x_{j_{4}}
Complete overlap

That means we have

i_{1}=j_{1},\qquad i_{2}=j_{2},\qquad i_{3}=j_{3},\qquad i_{4}=j_{4}.

From the modular constraints,

i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}+i_{4}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N),\qquad j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N),

we obtain

i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}+i_{4}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N).

Thus, we have 3 free indices under this type of linking. If every element in the first chain is paired with an element in the second chain, then the number of free indices is

\text{(length of the chains)}-1

degree of freedom.

Case 1(b) (Partial chain linking).

Suppose we link the chains only partially (i.e., not a complete linking), for example, as follows.

x_{i_{1}}
x_{i_{2}}
x_{i_{3}}
x_{i_{4}}
x_{j_{1}}
x_{j_{2}}
x_{j_{3}}
x_{j_{4}}
Partial chain linking

That means we have

i_{1}=j_{1},\qquad i_{2}=j_{2},\qquad i_{3}=i_{4},\qquad j_{3}=j_{4}.

From the modular constraints

i_{1}+i_{2}+i_{3}+i_{4}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N),\qquad j_{1}+j_{2}+j_{3}+j_{4}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N),

we have

i_{1}+i_{2}+2i_{3}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N),\qquad i_{1}+i_{2}+2j_{3}=0(\mathrm{mod}\ N).

In this case, we obtain only 2 degrees of freedom, which is strictly less than in the complete linking case. This is because partial linking introduces additional dependencies, thereby reducing the number of free variables.

Case 1(c) (More than two random variables are equal).

In the previous case 1(a) and case 1(b), we considered the case where exactly two random variables are equal. We now consider the case where more than two random variables are equal. For this, consider the following type of linking.

x_{i_{1}}
x_{i_{2}}
x_{i_{3}}
x_{i_{4}}
x_{j_{1}}
x_{j_{2}}
x_{j_{3}}
x_{j_{4}}
Not competently linked

That means

i_{1}=j_{1},i_{2}=j_{2},i_{3}=i_{4}=j_{3}=j_{4}

In this case, we again obtain only
2
degrees of freedom. The reason is that equating more variables creates additional dependencies among the indicesand consequently decreases the degrees of freedom. However, it should be noted that if a random variable appears with multiplicity more than two, then due to the nature of exploding moment, as mentioned in Condition
5.5
, one gets an exploding contribution of
N^{k/2-1}C_{k}.
Nevertheless, this contribution can not compensate for the lost degree of freedom. Because every additional power of a random variable amounts to loss of one additional degree of freedom, which is a multiplication by
N^{-1}
. By contrast, from the exploding moment nature of the random variable, only
N^{1/2}
is gained per additional power. Therefore overall, per additional constraint, it is a loss of
1/2
degrees of freedom.

From this, we conclude that if two chains are linked, they must be linked completely to get the leading order contribution. Any other type of partial linking yields a strictly smaller contribution.

Therefore, in the following chains

(x_{i_{1}}x_{i_{2}}x_{i_{3}}x_{i_{4}})(x_{j_{1}}x_{j_{2}}x_{j_{3}}x_{j_{4}})(x_{t_{1}}x_{t_{2}}x_{t_{3}}x_{t_{4}})(x_{s_{1}}x_{s_{2}}x_{s_{3}}x_{s_{4}}),

if we overlap the first chain completely with any of the remaining chains, and the other two chains are overlapped completely, then each pair of chains contributes
3
degrees of freedom, and hence the total number of degrees of freedom is
6.

Therefore, from Case 1 and Case 2, we conclude that if more than two chains are connected through the pairing. For instance, consider a configuration in which three chains are linked together. In such a case, the indices from these chains become coupled through the pairing, which creates additional dependencies among them. As a result, several indices are forced to be equal, and the corresponding modular constraints are no longer independent. This reduces the number of free variables compared to the case where chains are paired disjointly. In such cases, the total number of free indices is strictly less than in the case where exactly two chains are linked completely. Consequently, the contribution from such configurations is of lower order. Therefore, these configurations contribute strictly less compared to the pairing case (i.e., when chains are connected in disjoint pairs).

Exact degree of freedom in complete overlapping

We now determine the exact degree of freedom in the case where exactly two chains are completely linked. Consider
r
chains, each of the same length, given by

(x_{i_{1}^{(1)}}x_{i_{2}^{(1)}}x_{i_{3}^{(1)}}\dots x_{i_{k_{1}}^{(1)}})(x_{i_{1}^{(2)}}x_{i_{2}^{(2)}}x_{i_{3}^{(2)}}\dots x_{i_{k_{1}}^{(2)}})\dots(x_{i_{1}^{(r)}}x_{i_{2}^{(r)}}x_{i_{3}^{(r)}}\dots x_{i_{k_{1}}^{(r)}}).

Suppose that the chains are paired and each pair is completely linked. Then the reduced modular constraints become

\displaystyle i_{1}^{(1)}+i_{2}^{(1)}+\cdots+i_{k_{1}}^{(1)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N),

\displaystyle i_{1}^{(2)}+i_{2}^{(2)}+\cdots+i_{k_{1}}^{(2)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N),

\displaystyle i_{1}^{(r/2)}+i_{2}^{(r/2)}+\cdots+i_{k_{1}}^{(r/2)}\equiv 0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N).

Thus, each pair contributes
(k_{1}-1)
degrees of freedom. Therefore, the total number of degrees of freedom is

(k_{1}-1)+(k_{2}-1)+\dots(k_{r/2}-1).

From (
5.6
), the corresponding exponent of
N
is

N^{r/2}N^{-\frac{1}{2}(\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i})}N^{(k_{1}-1)+(k_{2}-1)+\dots(k_{r/2}-1)}

which is of order
O(1),
since all chains have the same length
k_{1}.
Therefore, when exactly two chains are completely linked (i.e., chains are paired), the contribution is of constant order.

On the other hand, as shown in Case 1 and Case 2, any configuration in which more than two chains are linked leads to fewer degrees of freedom. In such cases, the total number of free indices is strictly less than

\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i=1}^{r}k_{i}-\frac{r}{2}.

Consequently, all configurations other than pairwise linking contribute at most

O(N^{-\epsilon}),\epsilon>0

Thus

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\left[\prod_{i=1}^{2m}Z_{N}(k_{i})\right]=\sum_{\pi\in\mathcal{P}_{2}(2m)}\prod_{(i,j)\in\pi}\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(k_{i})Z_{N}(k_{j})]+o(1).

Calculation of the covariance kernel

From (
5.6
), taking
r=2
, we obtain

(5.7)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(k)Z_{N}(l)]

(5.8)

\displaystyle=N\frac{1}{N^{(k+l)/2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}j_{1},\dots,j_{k}=0\\
j_{1}+\cdots+j_{k}=0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}i_{1},\dots,i_{l}=0\\
i_{1}+\cdots+i_{l}=0\ (\mathrm{mod}\ N)\end{subarray}}^{N-1}\mathbb{E}\Big[A_{1}(j_{1},\dots,j_{k})\,A_{2}(i_{1},\dots,i_{l})\Big].

Now the expectation is nonzero only when each index among

\{j_{1},\dots,j_{k},i_{1},\dots,i_{l}\}

appears exactly twice.

Case 1:
k\neq l
.
In this case, it is not possible to pair all indices between the two blocks so that each index appears exactly twice. Hence,

\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(k)Z_{N}(l)]=0.

Case 2:
k=l
.
In this case, the only contributing configurations are those in which each index from the first block is paired with exactly one index from the second block. Such configurations correspond to bijections between
\{1,\dots,k\}
and
\{1,\dots,k\}
, and their number is
k!
. Therefore, we have

(5.9)

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}[Z_{N}(k)Z_{N}(l)]=\begin{cases}k!\,(\mathbb{E}[x_{0}^{2}])^{k}+o(1),&\text{if }k=l,\\
0,&\text{if }k\neq l.\end{cases}
