Low-Rank and Sparse Drift Estimation for High-Dimensional Lévy-Driven Ornstein–Uhlenbeck Processes
Marina Palaisti

Abstract
. We study high-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck processes driven by Lévy noise and consider drift matrices that decompose into a low-rank plus sparse component, capturing a few latent factors together with a sparse network of direct interactions. For discrete-time observations under the localized, truncated contrast of Dexheimer and Jeszka, we analyze a convex estimator that minimizes this contrast with a combined nuclear-norm and ℓ 1 \ell_{1} -penalty on the low-rank and sparse parts, respectively. Under a restricted strong convexity condition, a rank–sparsity incoherence assumption, and regime-specific choices of truncation level, horizon, and sampling mesh for the background driving Lévy process, we derive a non-asymptotic oracle inequality for the Frobenius risk of the estimator. The bound separates a discretization bias term of order d 2 ​ Δ n 2 d^{2}\Delta_{n}^{2} from a stochastic term of order γ ⁡ ( Δ n ) ​ T − 1 ​ ( r ​ log ⁡ d + s ​ log ⁡ d ) \gamma(\Delta_{n})T^{-1}(r\log d+s\log d) , thereby showing that the low-rank-plus-sparse structure improves the dependence on the ambient dimension relative to purely sparse estimators while retaining the same discretization and truncation behavior across the four Lévy regimes.

1.
Introduction

High-dimensional Ornstein–Uhlenbeck (OU) models driven by Lévy processes provide a flexible framework for multivariate time series with both continuous fluctuations and jumps, and arise in applications ranging from finance to network models and neuroscience. In such settings, the drift matrix
A_{0}
encodes both the global mean-reversion structure and the network of linear interactions between components. In many applications,
A_{0}
is believed to exhibit a simultaneous low-rank and sparsity structure, reflecting a small number of dominant latent factors together with a sparse network of direct pairwise interactions. This type of structure has been extensively exploited in the low-rank plus sparse literature for i.i.d. matrices and multivariate regression via convex estimators that combine a nuclear norm and an
\ell_{1}
-penalty.

The goal of this paper is to develop non-asymptotic Frobenius-risk bounds for low-rank-plus-sparse drift estimation in Lévy-driven Ornstein–Uhlenbeck models under high-dimensional scaling. While recent work by Dexheimer and Jeszka establishes sharp results for purely sparse drift matrices under localized and truncated contrasts, the present paper studies a structurally different estimation problem in which the drift matrix decomposes into a low-rank and a sparse component. This requires a different geometric and statistical analysis, combining nuclear-norm and
\ell_{1}
-regularization, and leads to oracle inequalities depending on
(r+s)
rather than purely on
s
.

More precisely, we consider the Lévy-driven OU process

dX_{t}=-A_{0}X_{t}\,dt+dZ_{t},\qquad t>0,

where
A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the unknown drift matrix and
Z
is a
d
-dimensional Lévy process. We assume that
X_{t}
is observed at discrete times
t_{k}=k\Delta_{n}
,
k=0,\dots,n
, over an observation horizon
T=n\Delta_{n}
, and that the stationary invariant distribution is used to initialize the process. Following Dexheimer and Jeszka, we construct a localized and truncated quadratic contrast
\ell_{n}(A)
by restricting to times at which
X_{t_{k-1}}
lies in a bounded ball
B
of radius of order
\sqrt{d}
and increments
\Delta X_{k}
have Euclidean norm at most
\eta
. We then estimate
A_{0}
by decomposing it as
A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}
, with
L_{0}
low-rank and
S_{0}
sparse, and minimizing
\ell_{n}
with a combined nuclear and
\ell_{1}
penalty on
(L,S)
.

Our analysis relies on two main ingredients. The first is an abstract oracle inequality for low-rank-plus-sparse matrix estimation with a general convex loss and decomposable penalties, in the spirit of the framework developed by Negahban and Wainwright and by Agarwal, Negahban and Wainwright for noisy matrix decomposition. This abstract result applies under (i) a second-order lower bound for the loss, (ii) dual norm bounds for the gradient at the true parameter, and (iii) a restricted strong convexity (RSC) condition on an appropriate low-rank-plus-sparse error cone. The second ingredient is a specialization of these conditions to the localized, truncated OU/Lévy contrast, using the concentration and covariance bounds established by Dexheimer and Jeszka for the four BDLP regimes (continuous, bounded-jump, sub-Weibull, and polynomial-moment).

Under a rank–sparsity incoherence assumption on the decomposition
A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}
, we show that the nuclear and
\ell_{1}
norms are compatible on the low-rank-plus-sparse cone, and that the localized contrast satisfies a cone-restricted RSC property with high probability when
T
exceeds a regime-dependent sample-complexity threshold. Combining these ingredients, we obtain an oracle inequality for the Frobenius risk of the nuclear-plus-
\ell_{1}
-penalized estimator
\hat{A}
that separates the discretization/truncation bias from the stochastic term. In particular, our main result (Theorem
5.1
) yields, with high probability,

\|\hat{A}-A_{0}\|_{F}^{2}\lesssim d^{2}\Delta_{n}^{2}+\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})}{T}\,\bigl(r\log d+s\log d\bigr),

where
\gamma(\Delta_{n})
captures the dependence on the BDLP regime and the truncation level, and the constants depend only on the ambient model parameters. The first term reflects the discretization bias incurred by observing the OU process on the mesh
\Delta_{n}
, while the second term is a variance term with a low-rank-plus-sparse complexity factor
(r+s)
up to logarithmic factors.

1.1.
Contributions

Our contributions can be summarized as follows.

•

We introduce and analyze a nuclear-plus-
\ell_{1}
-penalized estimator for the drift matrix of a high-dimensional Lévy-driven OU process, under a simultaneous low-rank and sparsity structure. The estimator is based on the localized and truncated quadratic contrast of Dexheimer and Jeszka, but extends their purely sparse framework to the low-rank-plus-sparse setting.

•

We develop an abstract oracle inequality for low-rank-plus-sparse matrix estimation with decomposable penalties and a general convex loss, and we verify the required second-order, dual norm, and RSC conditions for the OU/Lévy contrast under standard structural and mixing assumptions. This yields non-asymptotic Frobenius-risk bounds that scale with the effective complexity
r\log d+s\log d
.

•

We specialize our main oracle inequality to the four BDLP regimes considered by Dexheimer and Jeszka (continuous, bounded jumps, sub-Weibull tails, and polynomial
p
-th moments). In each regime we obtain explicit choices of
(\eta,T,\Delta_{n})
that retain the same discretization and truncation behavior as in the purely sparse case, while improving the stochastic term from a purely sparse dependence to the low-rank-plus-sparse dependence
(r+s)
.

These results show that the low-rank-plus-sparse structure of the drift coefficient can be fully exploited in the Lévy-driven OU setting, yielding improved high-dimensional rates while remaining compatible with the localization and truncation techniques needed to handle jumps and heavy tails.

1.2.
Relation to Dexheimer–Jeszka

Our work is motivated by the recent analysis of Dexheimer and Jeszka,
who study
\ell_{1}
-penalized estimation for sparse drift matrices in
Lévy-driven Ornstein–Uhlenbeck models under localized and truncated
contrast functions.

While their framework provides a detailed analysis of sparse estimators
under different Lévy regimes, the present paper develops a

structurally distinct estimation framework
based on a
low-rank-plus-sparse decomposition of the drift matrix. This extension
is not merely notational: it requires a different geometric analysis,
involving decomposable regularizers, compatibility of nuclear and

\ell_{1}
norms, and control of error on a joint low-rank-plus-sparse cone.

In particular, our results:

•

introduce a new estimator combining nuclear and
\ell_{1}
penalties,

•

establish oracle inequalities that depend on
(r+s)
rather than purely on
s
,

•

verify restricted strong convexity on a nontrivial intersection cone,

•

and show that the resulting rates retain the same discretization and truncation structure across Lévy regimes.

Thus, although we build on the probabilistic bounds of Dexheimer and Jeszka,
the statistical and geometric analysis developed here is different and
self-contained.

2.
Model and Estimator

We consider a
d
-dimensional Lévy-driven Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process

(1)

dX_{t}=-A_{0}X_{t}\,dt+dZ_{t},\qquad t>0,

where
A_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
is the unknown drift matrix and

Z=(Z_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is a
d
-dimensional Lévy process defined on a filtered
probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},(\mathcal{F}_{t})_{t\geq 0},\mathbb{P})
.
We assume that
X_{0}\sim\pi
is independent of
Z
, where
\pi
denotes the
invariant distribution of
X
.

Assumption A0 (OU/Lévy setup).:

We assume:

(1):

A_{0}\in\mathcal{M}_{+}(\mathbb{R}^{d})
, that is, all eigenvalues of
A_{0}
have strictly positive real parts, so that (
1
) admits a unique stationary solution.

(2):

The background driving Lévy process
Z
is a square-integrable martingale with Lévy measure admitting a
p
-th moment for some
p>2
.

(3):

The initial distribution coincides with the invariant distribution,
X_{0}\sim\pi
, so that
(X_{t})_{t\geq 0}
is strictly stationary.

(4):

For a fixed bounded Borel set
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
with radius of order
\sqrt{d}
, the truncated invariant covariance

C_{\infty}(B):=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}[XX^{\top}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X\in B\}}]

is positive definite.

We observe
X
at equidistant times
t_{k}=k\Delta_{n}
,
k=0,\dots,n
, with

\Delta_{n}>0
and observation horizon
T=n\Delta_{n}
. We denote increments by

\Delta X_{k}:=X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}},\qquad k=1,\dots,n.

Following
[
1
]
, we localize and truncate the contrast
function. Let
B\subset\mathbb{R}^{d}
be a bounded Borel set with radius

\mathrm{rad}(B)
of order
\sqrt{d}
and let
\eta>0
be a truncation level.
Define the localized and truncated contrast

(2)

\ell_{n}(A):=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t_{k-1}}\in B,\ \|\Delta X_{k}\|\leq\eta\}}\,\bigl\|\Delta X_{k}+AX_{t_{k-1}}\Delta_{n}\bigr\|_{2}^{2},\qquad A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}.

This is the same localized/truncated loss used in
[
1
]
for sparse drift estimation, except that we will equip it with a different regularizer.

We focus on drift matrices of the form

A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0},

where
L_{0}
is low-rank and
S_{0}
is sparse. As in the noisy matrix decomposition literature
[
2
,
3
]
, we require a structural identifiability condition that rules out matrices that are simultaneously too low-rank and too sparse.

Definition 2.1

(Low-rank-plus-sparse drift class)
.

For integers
r\in\{0,\dots,d\}
and
s\in\{0,\dots,d^{2}\}
, define

\mathcal{A}(r,s):=\bigl\{A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\ \rank(L_{0})\leq r,\ \|S_{0}\|_{0}\leq s\bigr\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{0}
denotes the number of non-zero entries.

Assumption A1 (Rank–sparsity incoherence).:

Let
L_{0}
have SVD
L_{0}=U_{0}\Sigma_{0}V_{0}^{\top}
of rank
r
, and write

U_{0},V_{0}\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}
with orthonormal columns. Define

T_{L}:=\{UV_{0}^{\top}+U_{0}V^{\top}:U,V\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times r}\},\qquad T_{S}:=\{M\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\operatorname{supp}(M)\subseteq\operatorname{supp}(S_{0})\},

with orthogonal complements
T_{L}^{\perp},T_{S}^{\perp}
. We assume there exist constants
\xi_{\mathrm{L}},\xi_{\mathrm{S}}\in(0,1)
such that

(3)

\|P_{T_{L}^{\perp}}(M)\|_{*}\leq\xi_{\mathrm{L}}\|M\|_{*}\quad\text{for all }M\in T_{S},

and

(4)

\|P_{T_{S}^{\perp}}(N)\|_{1}\leq\xi_{\mathrm{S}}\|N\|_{1}\quad\text{for all }N\in T_{L},

with
\xi_{\mathrm{L}}+\xi_{\mathrm{S}}<1
.

Remark 2.2

(Relation to standard incoherence conditions)
.

Assumption A1 is a rank–sparsity incoherence condition closely aligned with
those used in
[
3
,
2
]
. In particular, the bounds (
3
) and
(
4
) imply that the tangent spaces
T_{L}
and
T_{S}

have sufficiently small overlap, so that
T_{L}\cap T_{S}=\{0\}
and the
decomposition
A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}
is identifiable. Moreover, as shown in

[
2
,
3
]
, such
rank–sparsity incoherence guarantees that the nuclear and
\ell_{1}
norms are
compatible on the cone
\mathcal{C}
in the sense that the decomposability
arguments used in our oracle proof apply with constants depending only on

(\xi_{\mathrm{L}},\xi_{\mathrm{S}})
. Rather than re-deriving these geometric
facts here, we adopt A1 as a standing structural assumption and refer to the
above references for detailed sufficient conditions and examples.

We estimate
A_{0}
by decomposing it into a low-rank and a sparse component
and minimizing the localized contrast with a nuclear-plus-
\ell_{1}
penalty:

(5)

(\hat{L},\hat{S})\in\arg\min_{L,S\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}}\Bigl\{\ell_{n}(L+S)+\lambda_{*}\|L\|_{*}+\lambda_{1}\|S\|_{1}\Bigr\},

where
\|\cdot\|_{*}
denotes the nuclear norm and
\|\cdot\|_{1}
the
entry-wise
\ell_{1}
norm, and
\lambda_{*},\lambda_{1}>0
are tuning parameters.
We then define the drift estimator

\hat{A}:=\hat{L}+\hat{S}.

We equip
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
with the Frobenius norm

\|A\|_{F}:=(\sum_{i,j}A_{ij}^{2})^{1/2}
and define the empirical norm

\|A\|_{n,B,\eta}^{2}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t_{k-1}}\in B,\ \|\Delta X_{k}\|\leq\eta\}}\,\|AX_{t_{k-1}}\|_{2}^{2}.

3.
Abstract Decomposable-Penalty Framework

In this section we state an abstract oracle inequality for low-rank-plus-sparse
matrix estimation with a general convex loss and decomposable penalties, in the
spirit of
[
4
,
2
]
. Our OU/Lévy
drift estimator will then be obtained as a direct corollary by plugging in the
localized/truncated contrast
\ell_{n}
.

Let
\mathcal{L}_{n}(A)
be a convex, differentiable loss function defined on

\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, with gradient
G_{n}(A):=\nabla\mathcal{L}_{n}(A)
, and
consider the penalized estimator

(6)

(\hat{L},\hat{S})\in\arg\min_{L,S}\Bigl\{\mathcal{L}_{n}(L+S)+\lambda_{*}\|L\|_{*}+\lambda_{1}\|S\|_{1}\Bigr\},\qquad\hat{A}=\hat{L}+\hat{S}.

We assume that the true parameter
A_{0}
admits a decomposition
A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}

with
L_{0}
low-rank and
S_{0}
sparse, and that the identifiability condition
A1 holds. Let
T_{L},T_{S}
and their orthogonal complements
T_{L}^{\perp},T_{S}^{\perp}

be as in Assumption A1, and for any matrix
\Delta
write

\Delta_{L,T}:=P_{T_{L}}(\Delta),\quad\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}:=P_{T_{L}^{\perp}}(\Delta),\qquad\Delta_{S,T}:=P_{T_{S}}(\Delta),\quad\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}:=P_{T_{S}^{\perp}}(\Delta).

Definition 3.1

(Low-rank-plus-sparse error cone)
.

The low-rank-plus-sparse error cone associated with
(L_{0},S_{0})
is

\mathcal{C}:=\Bigl\{\Delta\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}:\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}\leq 4\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*},\ \|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}\leq 4\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}\Bigr\}.

We next formulate the abstract assumptions in terms of a seminorm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}}
and a bias term.

Assumption B1 (Second-order lower bound).:

There exists a seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}

and a nonnegative bias term
\mathrm{Bias}_{n}
such that, for all
A
,

(7)

\mathcal{L}_{n}(A)-\mathcal{L}_{n}(A_{0})-\langle G_{n}(A_{0}),A-A_{0}\rangle\geq\|A-A_{0}\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}-\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Assumption B2 (Dual norm bounds).:

The gradient at
A_{0}
is controlled in the dual norms of the penalties:

(8)

\|G_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq\lambda_{*}/2,\qquad\|G_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq\lambda_{1}/2.

Assumption B3 (Restricted strong convexity).:

There exists
\kappa>0
such that for all
\Delta\in\mathcal{C}
,

(9)

\|\Delta\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\geq\kappa\|\Delta\|_{F}^{2}.

Theorem 3.2

(Abstract low-rank-plus-sparse oracle inequality)
.

Suppose:

(1)

A_{0}=L_{0}+S_{0}
with
\rank(L_{0})\leq r
,
\|S_{0}\|_{0}\leq s
, and Assumption A1 holds.

(2)

Assumptions B1–B3 hold with seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathcal{L}}
, bias
\mathrm{Bias}_{n}
, and cone
\mathcal{C}
as above.

Let
(\hat{L},\hat{S})
be any solution of (
6
) and

\hat{A}=\hat{L}+\hat{S}
. Then there exist universal constants

C_{1},C_{2}>0
such that

(10)

\|\hat{A}-A_{0}\|_{F}^{2}\leq C_{1}\,\mathrm{Bias}_{n}+C_{2}\bigl(r\lambda_{*}^{2}+s\lambda_{1}^{2}\bigr).

Proof.

Let
\Delta:=\hat{A}-A_{0}
. By optimality of
(\hat{L},\hat{S})
we have

\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{A})+\lambda_{*}\|\hat{L}\|_{*}+\lambda_{1}\|\hat{S}\|_{1}\leq\mathcal{L}_{n}(A_{0})+\lambda_{*}\|L_{0}\|_{*}+\lambda_{1}\|S_{0}\|_{1}.

Writing
\hat{L}=L_{0}+\Delta_{L}
,
\hat{S}=S_{0}+\Delta_{S}
and simplifying gives

\mathcal{L}_{n}(\hat{A})-\mathcal{L}_{n}(A_{0})\leq-\lambda_{*}(\|L_{0}+\Delta_{L}\|_{*}-\|L_{0}\|_{*})-\lambda_{1}(\|S_{0}+\Delta_{S}\|_{1}-\|S_{0}\|_{1}).

By Assumption B1,

\|\Delta\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\leq-\langle G_{n}(A_{0}),\Delta\rangle+\lambda_{*}(\|L_{0}+\Delta_{L}\|_{*}-\|L_{0}\|_{*})+\lambda_{1}(\|S_{0}+\Delta_{S}\|_{1}-\|S_{0}\|_{1})+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Using decomposability of the nuclear and
\ell_{1}
norms on

T_{L},T_{L}^{\perp},T_{S},T_{S}^{\perp}

(cf.
[
4
,
2
]
) yields

\|L_{0}+\Delta_{L}\|_{*}-\|L_{0}\|_{*}\geq\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}-\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*},

\|S_{0}+\Delta_{S}\|_{1}-\|S_{0}\|_{1}\geq\|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}-\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}.

Assumption B2 and duality give

-\langle G_{n}(A_{0}),\Delta_{L}\rangle\leq\|G_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\|\Delta_{L}\|_{*}\leq\frac{\lambda_{*}}{2}\bigl(\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}+\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}\bigr),

-\langle G_{n}(A_{0}),\Delta_{S}\rangle\leq\|G_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\infty}\|\Delta_{S}\|_{1}\leq\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\bigl(\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}+\|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}\bigr).

Combining these inequalities and rearranging as in the standard proofs
(e.g.
[
2
, Lemma 1]
) gives

\|\Delta\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\leq-\frac{\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}+\frac{3\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}-\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}+\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Since the left-hand side is nonnegative, this implies

\frac{\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}+\frac{\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}\leq\frac{3\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}+\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

For sample sizes such that
\mathrm{Bias}_{n}
is dominated by the stochastic
terms, we can absorb it into constants and deduce the cone constraints

\|\Delta_{L,T^{\perp}}\|_{*}\leq 3\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*},\qquad\|\Delta_{S,T^{\perp}}\|_{1}\leq 3\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1},

so that
\Delta\in\mathcal{C}
. Dropping the negative terms then yields the
cone-restricted inequality

\|\Delta\|_{\mathcal{L}}^{2}\leq\frac{3\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}+\frac{3}{2}\lambda_{1}\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Assumption B3 implies

\kappa\|\Delta\|_{F}^{2}\leq\frac{3\lambda_{*}}{2}\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}+\frac{3\lambda_{1}}{2}\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Using
\operatorname{rank}(\Delta_{L,T})\leq 2r
and
\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{0}\leq s

gives

\|\Delta_{L,T}\|_{*}\leq\sqrt{2r}\,\|\Delta\|_{F},\qquad\|\Delta_{S,T}\|_{1}\leq\sqrt{s}\,\|\Delta\|_{F},

so

\kappa\|\Delta\|_{F}^{2}\leq C\bigl(\sqrt{r}\lambda_{*}+\sqrt{s}\lambda_{1}\bigr)\|\Delta\|_{F}+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

For
\|\Delta\|_{F}>0
, divide both sides by
\|\Delta\|_{F}
and apply

ab\leq\frac{\kappa}{4}a^{2}+\frac{1}{\kappa}b^{2}
with
a=\|\Delta\|_{F}
and

b^{2}\asymp r\lambda_{*}^{2}+s\lambda_{1}^{2}
to obtain

\frac{\kappa}{2}\|\Delta\|_{F}^{2}\leq C_{2}(r\lambda_{*}^{2}+s\lambda_{1}^{2})+\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Renaming constants yields (
10
).
∎

4.
Verification of Assumptions B1–B3 for the OU/Lévy Contrast

The proof of our main oracle inequality follows the abstract decomposable-penalty
framework of
[
4
,
2
]
, combined
with OU/Lévy-specific localization, truncation, and covariance concentration
from
[
1
]
. In this section we collect the two key
probabilistic ingredients: gradient control in the dual norms of the penalties,
and restricted strong convexity of the empirical quadratic form on the
low-rank-plus-sparse cone.

The quantity
\gamma(\Delta_{n})
denotes a regime-dependent scaling factor
arising in the concentration bounds for the localized empirical process.
More precisely, for each Lévy regime considered in

[
1
]
, there exists a function

\gamma:(0,1]\to\mathbb{R}_{+}
such that stochastic fluctuations of the
contrast and its gradient are controlled at rate

\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})\log d}{T}}.

For example:

•

in the continuous and bounded-jump regimes,
\gamma(\Delta_{n})
is of constant order;

•

in sub-Weibull regimes,
\gamma(\Delta_{n})
grows poly-logarithmically;

•

in polynomial-moment regimes,
\gamma(\Delta_{n})
exhibits polynomial growth.

The precise expressions are given in Table 1 of

[
1
]
, but for our analysis it suffices to treat

\gamma(\Delta_{n})
as a known scaling function satisfying these bounds.

We now show that the localized contrast
\ell_{n}
satisfies Assumptions B1–B3 with a suitable choice
of seminorm, bias term, and tuning parameters. Throughout this section we work under Assumption A0
and under the localization and truncation scheme described in
Section
2
.

Lemma 4.1

(Second-order lower bound for
\ell_{n}
)
.

There exists a nonnegative bias term
\mathrm{Bias}_{n}
and a seminorm

\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{L}}
on
\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
such that, for all
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
,

(11)

\ell_{n}(A)-\ell_{n}(A_{0})-\langle\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0}),A-A_{0}\rangle\geq\|A-A_{0}\|_{\mathsf{L}}^{2}-\mathrm{Bias}_{n}.

Moreover, we may take
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{L}}=\|\cdot\|_{n,B,\eta}
and

\mathrm{Bias}_{n}=\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})+\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)
, where

\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})
and
\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)
represent,
respectively, discretization and truncation errors arising from:

•

approximation of the continuous-time dynamics by discrete increments,

•

removal of large jumps via the truncation threshold
\eta
.

More explicitly:

\displaystyle\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\sup_{A}\big|\mathbb{E}[\ell_{n}(A)]-\ell(A)\big|,

\displaystyle\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)

\displaystyle:=

\displaystyle\mathbb{E}\big[\|\Delta X_{k}\|^{2}\mathbf{1}_{\{\|\Delta X_{k}\|>\eta\}}\big],\quad\Delta X_{k}=X_{t_{k}}-X_{t_{k-1}}.

Under Assumption A0 and standard moment conditions,

\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})\lesssim d^{2}\Delta_{n}^{2},

while
\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)
can be made sufficiently small under appropriate moment or tail conditions on the Lévy measure and suitable choices of
\eta
.

Proof.

We expand the contrast function explicitly. Writing

\Delta X_{k}=-A_{0}X_{t_{k-1}}\Delta_{n}+\varepsilon_{k},

where
\varepsilon_{k}
collects martingale increments and discretization errors,
we obtain

\Delta X_{k}+AX_{t_{k-1}}\Delta_{n}=(A-A_{0})X_{t_{k-1}}\Delta_{n}+\varepsilon_{k}.

Substituting into
\ell_{n}(A)
yields

\ell_{n}(A)=\ell_{n}(A_{0})+\langle\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0}),A-A_{0}\rangle+\|A-A_{0}\|_{n,B,\eta}^{2}+R_{n}(A),

where the remainder term
R_{n}(A)
collects higher-order discretization
and truncation contributions.

The key point is that, under localization and truncation, these remainder
terms are uniformly controlled, and satisfy

|R_{n}(A)|\leq\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})+\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta).

Rearranging gives the desired lower bound.
∎

Lemma 4.2

(Dual norm bounds for the gradient)
.

Let
\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0})
denote the gradient of the localized contrast at the true drift
A_{0}
.
There exist constants
c_{\mathrm{op}},c_{1}>0
, depending only on the OU/Lévy triplet, the invariant
covariance, and the mixing and truncation parameters, such that for all

large enough and all

(12)

\|\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\mathrm{op}}\leq c_{\mathrm{op}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})\,\log d}{T}},\qquad\|\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0})\|_{\infty}\leq c_{1}\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})\,\log(d^{2})}{T}},

with probability at least
1-2\delta
for any fixed

. In particular, if the

\lambda_{*}\geq 2c_{\mathrm{op}}\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})\,\log d}{T}},\qquad\lambda_{1}\geq 2c_{1}\,\sqrt{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})\,\log(d^{2})}{T}},

then Assumption B2 holds for
\ell_{n}
with high probability.

Proof.

Under Assumption A0 and the localization/truncation scheme, the gradient
\nabla\ell_{n}(A_{0})
can be
expressed as an average of matrix-valued martingale differences plus a deterministic remainder term
controlled by discretization and truncation. Using the martingale concentration inequalities and
regime-dependent scaling developed by Dexheimer and Jeszka for the filtered Lévy increments, one
obtains operator- and entrywise-infinity norm bounds of the form (

), where the factor

\gamma(\Delta_{n})
encodes the dependence on the BDLP tail regime and the mesh
\Delta_{n}
.
The remainder term is of smaller order and can be absorbed into the constants

c_{\mathrm{op}},c_{1}
for

large enough. The high-probability statement and the sufficient
conditions (
13
) for Assumption B2 follow directly.
∎

Lemma 4.3

(Restricted strong convexity on the low-rank-plus-sparse cone)
.

Let
\mathcal{C}
denote the low-rank-plus-sparse error cone associated with
(L_{0},S_{0})

as in Definition
3.1
. Under Assumption A0, Assumption A1, and the
localization/truncation scheme, there exists a constant
\kappa>0
and a sample-complexity
threshold
T_{0}=T_{0}(d,r,s,\cdot)
such that, for all
T\geq T_{0}
,

(14)

\|A\|_{n,B,\eta}^{2}\geq\kappa\,\|A\|_{F}^{2},\qquad\text{for all }A\in\mathcal{C},

with probability at least
1-2\delta
for any fixed
\delta\in(0,1)
. In particular,
Assumption B3 holds for
\ell_{n}
with seminorm
\|\cdot\|_{\mathsf{L}}=\|\cdot\|_{n,B,\eta}
.

Proof.

Define the truncated empirical covariance matrix

C_{n,B,\eta}:=\frac{1}{n}\sum_{k=1}^{n}\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{t_{k-1}}\in B,\ \|\Delta X_{k}\|\leq\eta\}}\,X_{t_{k-1}}X_{t_{k-1}}^{\top},

and its population counterpart

C_{\infty,B,\eta}:=\mathbb{E}_{\pi}\bigl[X_{0}X_{0}^{\top}\,\mathbbm{1}_{\{X_{0}\in B\}}\bigr].

Then
\|A\|_{n,B,\eta}^{2}=\tr(A^{\top}C_{n,B,\eta}A)
, and by Assumption A0 the truncated
population covariance satisfies
C_{\infty,B,\eta}\succeq c_{B}I_{d}
for some
c_{B}>0
independent of

d
. The covariance concentration results for the localized and truncated OU/Lévy process imply
that, for
T\geq T_{0}(d,r,s,\cdot)
, the operator norm deviation

\|C_{n,B,\eta}-C_{\infty,B,\eta}\|_{\mathrm{op}}
is bounded by
\frac{1}{2}c_{B}
with high
probability. Consequently,

\|A\|_{n,B,\eta}^{2}=\tr\bigl(A^{\top}C_{\infty,B,\eta}A\bigr)+\tr\bigl(A^{\top}(C_{n,B,\eta}-C_{\infty,B,\eta})A\bigr)\geq\bigl(c_{B}-\|C_{n,B,\eta}-C_{\infty,B,\eta}\|_{\mathrm{op}}\bigr)\,\|A\|_{F}^{2}\geq\frac{c_{B}}{2}\,\|A\|_{F}^{2}

for all
A\in\mathbb{R}^{d\times d}
, and hence for all
A\in\mathcal{C}
.
Taking
\kappa=c_{B}/2
yields (
14
).
∎

Combining Lemmas

Theorem 5.1

(Oracle inequality for low-rank-plus-sparse drift)
.

Suppose Assumptions A0 and A1 hold and let

\operatorname{rank}(L_{0})\leq r
and

, truncation level

Remark 5.3

(Dominance of discretization and truncation bias)

The step where we absorb

Proof.

By Lemma

By Lemma

7.
Discussion

7.1.
Interpretation of the oracle bound

The oracle inequality in Theorem
5.1
separates the estimation error into two components:

\|\hat{A}-A_{0}\|_{F}^{2}\;\lesssim\;\underbrace{d^{2}\Delta_{n}^{2}}_{\text{discretization bias}}\;+\;\underbrace{\frac{\gamma(\Delta_{n})}{T}(r\log d+s\log d)}_{\text{stochastic error}}.

The first term reflects the intrinsic bias introduced by observing the continuous-time
Ornstein–Uhlenbeck process on a discrete grid. This term is independent of the
low-rank-plus-sparse structure and coincides with the discretization behavior
already identified in the purely sparse setting.

The second term captures statistical variability and depends on the structural
complexity of the drift matrix. In contrast to purely sparse estimators, where
the complexity scales with
s\log d
, the present bound depends on

(r+s)\log d
, reflecting the combined low-rank and sparse structure.

7.2.
When does low-rank plus sparse help?

The advantage of the low-rank-plus-sparse formulation becomes apparent in regimes
where the drift matrix admits a decomposition with
r\ll d
and
s\ll d^{2}
.
In such cases, the effective dimension of the estimation problem is reduced from

d^{2}
to
rd+s
, leading to improved statistical rates.

In particular:

•

If the drift matrix is approximately low-rank with only a few dominant
factors, the nuclear norm penalty captures global structure efficiently.

•

If, in addition, there are sparse local interactions, the
\ell_{1}
penalty
isolates these without inflating the overall complexity.

This combination is especially natural in applications such as financial
networks or high-dimensional biological systems, where both latent factors and
localized interactions are present.

7.3.
Role of truncation and discretization

The quantities
\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})
and
\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)
play a
central role in controlling approximation errors.

•

The discretization term
\mathrm{Disc}(T,\Delta_{n})
arises from replacing
the continuous-time dynamics with discrete increments. Its scaling

d^{2}\Delta_{n}^{2}
reflects the cumulative effect of approximation errors across
coordinates.

•

The truncation term
\mathrm{Trunc}(T,\eta)
controls the contribution of
large jumps. Its magnitude depends on the tail behavior of the Lévy measure
and can be made small by appropriate choice of
\eta
in each regime.

Importantly, these two terms are not artifacts of the estimation procedure but
are intrinsic to high-frequency observation of jump processes. The results show
that the low-rank-plus-sparse structure improves only the stochastic component,
while leaving discretization and truncation behavior unchanged.

7.4.
Relation to existing work

While the present analysis builds on the localization and truncation framework
introduced by Dexheimer and Jeszka, the statistical problem considered here is
substantially different. In particular, the introduction of a low-rank-plus-sparse
structure requires a joint geometric analysis of nuclear and
\ell_{1}
norms,
together with control of the estimation error on a composite cone.

The resulting oracle inequality therefore extends beyond the purely sparse case
and demonstrates that the same probabilistic tools can be combined with more
structured regularization schemes.

7.5.
Limitations and future directions

Several limitations of the present work suggest directions for further research.

•

The analysis assumes a known decomposition structure characterized by
rank
r
and sparsity level
s
. Developing adaptive procedures that estimate
these quantities remains an open problem.

•

The truncation level
\eta
is chosen based on regime-specific arguments.
A data-driven selection of
\eta
would improve practical applicability.

•

The results focus on Frobenius risk bounds. Extensions to operator norm
bounds or inference for individual entries may require additional techniques.

•

Finally, it would be of interest to study whether similar improvements can
be obtained for other stochastic processes with jumps beyond the OU setting.

Overall, the results highlight that structural assumptions beyond sparsity can
be effectively leveraged in high-dimensional stochastic systems, even in the
presence of jumps and heavy tails.
