Quasimonophobic graphs and degree spectral sequences in discrete cubical homology
Samira Sahar Jamil, Mark Behrens

Abstract
. We introduce the degree filtration on the discrete cubical chain complex of a graph, defined in terms of the maximal injective dimension of the facets of singular n n -cubes, and study the degree spectral sequence which arises from this filtration. This spectral sequence interpolates between the discrete cubical homology of a graph H n ​ ( G ) H_{n}(G) and the injective homology H n i ​ n ​ j ​ ( G ) H_{n}^{inj}(G) , a variant of the discrete cubical homology based on injective singular cubes. Building on the work of Babson et al. we introduce the combinatorial condition of quasimonophobicity on graphs, and show quasimonophobicity implies both the vanishing of the degree spectral sequence in certain bidegrees, and implies H n i ​ n ​ j ​ ( G ) H_{n}^{inj}(G) is isomorphic to the homology of the CW complex obtained by “filling in” subcubes of the graph. These results are applied to compute H 2 ​ ( G n s ​ p ​ h ) H_{2}(G_{n}^{sph}) for the Greene sphere graphs G n s ​ p ​ h G^{sph}_{n} .

A

The

We shall write

For

In this section, we show that for a certain class of graphs, which we call

-quasimonophobic graphs
, the homology group

admits a
particularly simple description. We begin by introducing the notion of

n
-quasimonophobic graphs.

The concept of
monophobic cube neighborhoods
was introduced by Babson, Greene, and Jamil
in
[
1
]
, a sufficient condition for nontriviality of certain elements in the discrete homotopy
groups and in the discrete cubical homology groups of a graph
G
is established.
In the present section, we give the definition of
n
-quasimonophobic graphs
and develop several results building on those in
[
1
]
,
to facilitate
the computation of
H_{n}^{[n]}
for
n
-quasimonophobic graphs.

A
rigid
n
-cube
in a graph is an induced subgraph isomorphic to

I^{n}
, the standard discrete
n
-cube. A singular
n
-cube
\sigma
is said
to be
supported by
a rigid
n
-cube if the image of
\sigma
is equal
to that cube.

A rigid
n
-cube

is
monophobic
in a graph
G
if,
whenever a singular
(n+1)
-cube contains a face supported by
Q
, it also
contains at least one additional face supported by
Q
. This relates to the framework of
[
1
]
in the sense that a rigid
n
-cube
Q\subseteq G
is monophobic if and only if it posesses a monophobic neighborhood.

We shall say that a rigid
n
-cube
Q
is
quasimonophobic
in a graph

if,
whenever a
noninjective
singular
(n+1)
-cube contains a face supported by
Q
, it also
contains at least one additional face supported by
Q
.

Definition 5.1

(
n
-monophobic graph)
.

Define a graph

to be

-monophobic
if every
n
-cube

in

is rigid and monophobic.

Definition 5.2

(
n
-quasimonophobic graph)
.

Define a graph

to be
n
-quasimonophobic
if every
n
-cube

in

is rigid and quasimonophobic. We shall say it is
quasimonophobic
if it is
n
-quasimonophobic for every
n
.

Note the following:

•

If a graph is
n
-monophobic, then it is
n
-quasimonophobic.

•

Every graph is
0
-quasimonophobic, but the only graphs which are
0
-monophobic are those without edges.

•

A graph is
1
-monophobic if and only if it is triangle-free and square-free, and it is
1
-quasimonophobic if and only if it is triangle-free.

•

If
G
has cubical dimension

, it is tautologically
(>d)
-monophobic (and therefore
(>d)
-quasimonophobic).

In this section, we focus on the degree-

associated with an

-quasimonophobic graph

and are primarily concerned with its homology in dimension

All boundary computations pertaining to the boundary operator

are understood to be taken modulo singular cubes of degree less than

, and only injective singular

.

Now, we proceed to show that

of

modulo the equivalence relation defined in the following theorem.

Theorem 5.3
.

Let
Q
be an
n
-cube in any graph

, and let
\sigma
and
\gamma
be singular
n
-cubes in

with

\mathrm{Im}(\sigma)=\mathrm{Im}(\gamma)=Q.

Then

is homologous to

up to a sign in

We will prove Theorem

through a sequence of lemmas. These lemmas were originally proven in
[
11
]
in the context of graphs which are digital images; we include the proofs here for completeness.

Note that
\sigma
and
\gamma
are related by a finite sequence of compositions of cube automorphisms

T_{i},\ T_{i,j}\colon I^{n}\to I^{n},\quad i,j\in\{1,\ldots,n\},\ i<j,

where

reflects the
i
-th coordinate via

, and

permutes the
i
-th and
j
-th coordinates.
To prove the desired result, we show that
\sigma
,
\sigma\circ T_{i}
, and
\sigma\circ T_{i,j}
are homologous to each other, up to a sign, where the homology consists solely of singular
(n+1)
-cubes of degree

.

Lemma 5.4

(
[
11
]
)
.

For a singular
n
-cube
\sigma
in

C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
,
the chain
\sigma+\sigma\circ T_{i}
lies in
\partial_{n+1}\!\bigl(C_{n+1}^{[n]}(G)\bigr).

Proof.

We will show that
\sigma+\sigma\circ T_{i}=(-1)^{i}\partial\rho
in
C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
, where

\rho\in C_{n+1}^{[n]}(G)
is the singular
(n+1)
-cube given by:

\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})=\begin{cases}\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n+1})&\text{ if }t_{i}=0\\
\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n+1})&\text{ if }t_{i}=1.\end{cases}

We must compute the various faces of
\rho
.

First we compute

and
f^{+}_{i}\rho
:

\displaystyle f_{i}^{-}\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i},\dots,t_{n})=\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},t_{i},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle f^{+}_{i}\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},1,t_{i},\dots,t_{n})=\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,\dots,t_{n})

Here we see that
f_{i}^{-}\rho=\sigma
, and
f^{+}_{i}\rho
is degenerate since it does not depend on coordinate
t_{i}
.

Next we compute
f^{-}_{i+1}\rho
and
f^{+}_{i+1}\rho
:

\displaystyle f^{-}_{i+1}\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{i},0,t_{i+1},\dots,t_{n})=\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n+1})

\displaystyle f^{+}_{i+1}\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{i},1,t_{i+1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},1,t_{i+1},t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n})&\text{ if }t_{i}=0\\
\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+1},\dots,t_{n})&\text{ if }t_{i}=1\end{cases}

\displaystyle=\sigma\circ T_{i}(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

Thus

is degenerate, and
f^{+}_{i+1}\rho=\sigma\circ T_{i}
.

Now we compute
f^{-}_{j}\rho
and
f^{+}_{j}\rho
for

. For simplicity in the notation below, we will assume that

:

\displaystyle f^{-}_{j}\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\rho(t_{1},\dots,0,t_{j},\dots,t_{n})

\displaystyle=\begin{cases}\sigma(t_{1},\dots,0,t_{j},\dots,t_{i-1},t_{i+1},t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n})&\text{ if }t_{i}=0\\
\sigma(t_{1},\dots,0,t_{j},\dots,t_{i-1},0,t_{i+2},\dots,t_{n})&\text{ if }t_{i}=1\end{cases}

From the two cases above, we see that

can take on at most

values, so

is not injective, and thus
f^{-}_{j}\rho=0\in C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
. A similar computation shows that
f^{+}_{j}\rho=0\in C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
.

Summing the faces of
\rho
in
C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
gives only the two nonzero terms
f_{i}^{-}\rho=\sigma
and
f^{+}_{i+1}\rho=\sigma\circ T_{i}
, and we have

\partial\rho=\sum_{j}(-1)^{j}(f^{-}_{j}\rho-f^{+}_{j}\rho)=(-1)^{i}\sigma-(-1)^{i+1}\sigma\circ T_{i}=(-1)^{i}(\sigma+\sigma\circ T_{i})

as desired.
∎

The following two lemmas show that the chain
\sigma+\sigma\circ T_{i,j}
is a boundary in

.
To this end, we first define the cube automorphism

which acts on the cube

as a quarter-turn rotation in the anticlockwise direction. Then

.

Lemma 5.5

For a singular

-cube
\sigma
in

Proof.

We show that

, where
\rho\in C_{n+1}^{[n]}(G)
is the singular

-cube whose front and back

-faces are

and

, respectively. That is,

\rho(t_{1},\dots,t_{n+1})=\begin{cases}\sigma(t_{1},\dots,t_{n}),&\text{if }t_{n+1}=0,\\[2.0pt]

Thus, it suffices to show that all remaining faces of

Observe that for

Lemma 5.6
.

For a singular
n
-cube
\sigma
in

C_{n}^{[n]}(G)
,
