Maternal age differences among women with histologically diagnosed molar pregnancy
Authors: Yael Yagur, Hanoch Schreiber, Lior Heresco, Ofer Markovitch, Omer Weitzner, Ron Schonman, Zvi Klein, Yair Daykan, Nissim Arbib
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
ObjectiveTo determine the occurrence of molar pregnancies across maternal age groups and to examine βhCG levels at admission and follow-up.MethodsA retrospective cohort study conducted between the years 2014–2021. Included women with a pathology-diagnosed partial or complete mole. Demographic, clinical and laboratory data of women with a molar pregnancy, ≤ 40 or > 40 years were compared. Long-term follow-up data after surgical evacuation and βhCG levels were collected.ResultsA total of 115 cases of complete (n = 15) or partial moles (n = 100) during the study period were included. Women’s ages ranged from 16 to 55 years (mean 33.3 ± 8.2). Most participants, 73 (63.5%) were 20–35 years. Compared to women > 40 years (n = 23), women ≤ 40 (n = 92) partial moles were more common in the ≤ 40 group (85 [92.4%] vs. 15 [65.2%], p = 0.001), while complete moles were more frequent in the > 40 group (8 [34.8%] vs. 7 [7.6%], p = 0.001), even after controlling for age, gravidity, and parity (p = 0.005, OR 6.5). βhCG levels over time were followed with follow-up ranged from 30 to 203 days. βhCG levels normalized within 3 months in 90% of cases, with the largest decrease occurred after the first month. Gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN) occurred in 7 (6.1%) cases, with no age group differences.ConclusionsThis study emphasis that molar pregnancies affect a wide age range. Furthermore, it confirms a positive long-term prognosis.Supplementary InformationThe online version contains supplementary material available at 10.1186/s12884-026-09398-2.

Introduction

Molar pregnancy, also known as hydatidiform mole, is part of a range of rare, pregnancy-related disorders that arise from gestational trophoblastic tissue after a defect in gametogenesis or fertilization [1]. Molar pregnancies are classified into partial or complete. Partial moles are characterized by trophoblastic hyperplasia and edema of the villi. They are triploid with an excess of androgenetic chromosomes. Complete molar pregnancy is characterized by the absence of a fetus, and the entire uterine contents is composed of abnormal placental tissue [2, 3].

The prevalence of molar pregnancies varies across different geographical regions and populations [4]. In areas with higher frequency, the incidence of complete mole is 1 case in 1000 pregnancies and 4 per 1000 for partial moles. However, accurate data on prevalence may differ due to variations in reporting and diagnostic practices [5]. Age also influences prevalence. Women who are extremely young or of advanced maternal age are at increased risk [6, 7].

Timely diagnosis and appropriate management of molar pregnancies are crucial for patient outcomes. Despite the generally favourable prognosis, molar pregnancies can have long-term sequelae. Some patients have an increased risk of developing gestational trophoblastic neoplasia [8]. The treatment of choice typically involves uterine evacuation through suction curettage to remove the abnormal tissue. Pathological diagnosis is crucial [9] as well as follow-up instructions [10]. Regular monitoring of human chorionic gonadotropin (hCG) levels is essential to detect any potential gestational trophoblastic neoplasia or recurrence [11].

The literature suggests a notable association between maternal age and the occurrence of partial or complete molar pregnancies. Studies have reported that both types tend to occur more frequently among women at the extreme ends of the reproductive years [12, 13]. Maternal age, typically defined as 35 years and older, has been identified as a risk factor for both partial and complete moles [14] but complete moles are more common above the age of 45 [7]. Other epidemiological data have shown an increase in the risk of complete mole among women over 40 years [4]. A higher incidence of molar pregnancies has also been observed among younger women, especially those under the age of 15 or 20 years [2, 5]. These findings indicate a U-shaped relationship between maternal age and molar pregnancies a pattern that likely reflects both the distribution of pregnancies across these age groups and the increased risk observed among women over 40 years. The underlying mechanisms for this age-related association warrant further investigation, as they may provide valuable insights into the aetiology of molar pregnancies.

Despite this established association, there is limited evidence comparing the clinical characteristics of molar pregnancies between age groups. In particular, there is a lack of data describing the distribution of partial versus complete moles across maternal age categories as well as detailed, age-stratified βhCG values at admission and throughout follow-up.

Healthcare providers should be vigilant in diagnosing and managing molar pregnancies in women of all reproductive ages. The objective of this study was to increase our knowledge regarding the incidence and characteristics of molar pregnancies across the different age groups and further evaluate the βhCG levels during admission and follow-up.

Materials and methods

Patients

This retrospective study included cases of partial and complete mole diagnosed by pathology from 2014 to 2021. Data were obtained from the electronic medical records of the Obstetrics and Gynecology Department at a tertiary medical centre.

The inclusion criteria were women with a pathological diagnosis of partial or complete mole who underwent surgical evacuation and had long-term follow-up. All patients with a confirmed molar pregnancy were included in the cohort, regardless of whether βhCG levels at admission were recorded. Patients were excluded only if both admission and follow-up βhCG values were entirely missing, which did not occur in our dataset.

For descriptive analysis, we obtained data on women with a pathological diagnosis of partial and complete mole who underwent surgical evacuation. Subsequently, we performed a second analysis comparing women younger than 40 years old with those older than 40 years old.

Data

Collected data consisted of patient demographics, medical history, and obstetrical data, such as maternal age, background diseases, smoking status, body mass index (BMI), gravidity, parity, history of caesarean section, and gestational age. Data on previous molar pregnancies, relevant clinical information, including symptoms and laboratory data (TSH and βhCG levels at admission). Long-term follow-up data, including βhCG levels and the need for further surgical or chemotherapy treatment, were also retrieved.

Outcomes

The primary outcome was the distribution of partial versus complete molar pregnancies among women with histologically confirmed molar pregnancy, stratified by maternal age (≤ 40 vs. > 40 years). The secondary outcomes aimed to explore (1) The age distribution of our cohort. (2) The percentage of gestational trophoblastic neoplasm between the age groups. (3) To establish the βhCG levels during the follow-up period of all the cohort and between the age groups.

Current retrospective study protocol

Patients admitted to our medical centre with a diagnosis of missed abortion or suspected sonographic molar pregnancy, or those requiring urgent evacuation due to incomplete abortion, underwent surgical evacuation. Only patients with a confirmed histologic diagnosis of molar pregnancy (partial or complete mole) were included.

The surgical evacuation consisted of dilation and curettage (D&C), following standard practice and included oxytocin infusion at the time of induction, mechanical dilation of the cervix, ultrasound-guided suction aspiration, and sharp curettage to ensure complete evacuation of molar tissue [15].

βhCG monitoring was initiated at admission and repeated after the uterine evacuation. In cases of partial mole, weekly βhCG measurements were conducted until a single normal value was confirmed. This was followed by monthly βhCG testing for 3 months. For a complete mole, follow-up was conducted for up to 6 months [10]. Throughout the follow-up period, patients were advised to avoid pregnancy by using contraception of any kind.

A sonographic evaluation was performed four weeks after the procedure at the outpatient gynaecology clinic to inspect the uterus for retained products of conception or any parameters suggesting invasion. Results were documented in the patients’ medical records.

Patients who were diagnosed with gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN) were advised to complete a computerized tomography (CT) scan to evaluate distant organ involvement. Patients with GTN were treated with methotrexate (MTX) and actinomycin D based on known protocols [10].

Ethics

This study was conducted according to good clinical practice guidelines and was approved by the Meir Medical Center Institutional Review Board MMC#0087 − 16. Due to the retrospective nature of the study, informed consent for participation was not required.

Statistical analysis

Data are presented as numbers and percentages for categorical variables, and as means and standard deviations for continuous variables. Patient characteristics were compared between the groups, using Student’s t-test for continuous variables, and chi-square or Fisher Exact Test for categorical variables. Results were considered significant when the p-value was ≤ 0.05. Multivariable regression was used to study the association between the pathology s and woman’s age. All statistical analyses were performed using SPSS Statistics for Windows (IBM Corp., Armonk, NY, USA).

Results

A total of 115 cases were included, 100 (87%) women diagnosed with partial mole and 15 (13%) diagnosed with complete mole. Ninety-two (80%) were ≤ 40 years of age and 23 (20%) were older than 40 years. The mean follow-up period was 53.4 days for partial mole and 134.5 days for complete mole (range 30 to 203 days).

Among the participants, 11 women (9.5%) were older than 45 years, 23 women (20%) were 40 or above, and 38 (33%) were older than 35 years. Four women (3.5%) in the cohort were age 20 or younger. The mean gestational age was 9 + 5 ± 2.6 weeks, with a range of 4 to 18 weeks. Patients with a history of previous molar pregnancy accounted for 1.7% of the cohort.

Among the participants, 50 patients (43.5%) presented with vaginal bleeding at the time of admission of those 38 (76%) in the partial mole group and 12 (24%) on the complete mole group. One patient with diagnosis of partial mole (0.9%) experienced hyperemesis during this period. Additionally, TSH levels were within the normal range for all patients.

The mean βhCG level at admission of the cohort was 139,996 mIU/ml ± 185,849. On the day after the D&C, βhCG declined to 24,095 mIU/ml (an 84% reduction). βhCG values normalized within one month for 72.5% of the patients, within two months for 86%, and within three months for 90% of the entire cohort. The largest decrease occurred after the first month. No significant differences were observed in the pattern or rate of βhCG decline between the two age groups, as βhCG values at all follow-up time points were comparable for women ≤ 40 and > 40 years.

During the follow-up period, 7 patients (6.1%) of the cohort had a gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN). Among the partial mole group, 4 patients (4%) were diagnosed with GTN, and of these, 3 (75%) were found to have involved lymph nodes in the lung based on CT scans. All patients with GTN were treated with MTX, while 2 (50%) also received actinomycin D, resulting in full recovery as of 2021. In the complete mole group, 3 patients (20%) were diagnosed with GTN during follow-up, and among them, 2 (66.6%) were found to have involved lymph nodes in the lung on CT scans. All patients with GTN in this group were treated with MTX, while 1 (33.3%) also received actinomycin D, leading to full recovery as of 2021.

In the univariate analysis comparing patients ages ≤ 40 years to those > 40 years, no significant differences were observed in baseline characteristics, except for gravidity (2.8 ± 1.6 in the age ≤ 40 group and 4.9 ± 2.1 in the age > 40 group, p < 0.001), parity (1.4 ± 1.3 in the age ≤ 40 group and 2.5 ± 1.6 in the age > 40 group, p < 0.001), and history of abortion (0.6 ± 0.8 in the age ≤ 40 group and 1.5 ± 1.6 in the age > 40 group, p < 0.001; Table 1).

Significant differences in the pathology results were observed between the two age groups. Partial mole was more prevalent in the age ≤ 40 group (85 cases, 92.4%) compared to the age > 40 group (15 cases, 65.2%), with a p = 0.001. Conversely, complete mole was observed more frequently in the age > 40 group (8 cases, 34.8%) compared to the age ≤ 40 group (7 cases, 7.6%), also p = 0.001. This finding was significant after controlling age, gravidity, and parity (p = 0.005, aOR 6.5). Gestational trophoblastic neoplasia (GTN) occurred in 7 (6.1%) cases, with no age group differences. Discussion

The present study aimed to assess the prevalence of molar pregnancy in relation to maternal age within our population and to investigate the βhCG levels during the admission and follow-up periods. The study participants ranged in age from 16 to 55 years, with a mean age of 33.3 ± 8.2 years. A substantial proportion of the cohort, comprising 73 women (63.5%), fell within the age range of 20 to 35 years; highlighting the significance of this age group in the context of molar pregnancies.

The age distribution of molar pregnancy is a crucial aspect to explore, as it can provide insights into potential risk factors or predispositions associated with different age groups [13]. Previous studies reported higher occurrences of molar pregnancies in younger women, particularly those younger than 20 years, and in older women above the age of 40 [7]. Our data, however, emphasize the significance of ages of 20 to 35, which constituted the majority of cases in our population. These results align with some existing literature, which indicates a broad range of maternal ages in molar pregnancy cases [13]. It should be noted, however, that this predominance likely reflects the underlying distribution of pregnancies in our population, rather than indicating a higher age-specific risk within this group.

Moreover, our study demonstrated that patients older than 40 years were significantly more likely to present with complete molar pregnancy in contrast to patients ≤ 40 who were more likely to present with a partial molar pregnancy. Previous studies have also reported an association between maternal age and the type of molar pregnancy. Seckl et al. found that complete moles were more commonly associated with advanced maternal age. This is thought to be due to a higher rate of genetic abnormalities in the fertilized egg. Similarly, partial moles have been observed to occur more frequently in younger women. This could be related to the more commonly observed triploid (69 chromosomes) karyotype in partial moles [16].

In terms of long-term follow-up and outcomes, effective follow-up is paramount in managing molar pregnancies, and this study reaffirms the importance of adhering to established guidelines [4, 17]. Regular monitoring of βhCG levels allows for the prompt detection of GTN or recurrences, enabling timely intervention [15, 17–19]. In our study we tracked behaviour of βhCG levels from admission throughout the follow-up period. The initial mean βhCG level at admission of our cohort was 126,484.3 mIU/ml with a substantial decline to 26,914 mIU/ml after D&C, representing a 79% decrease. This decline is consistent with the expected response following the evacuation of molar tissue and is crucial for monitoring treatment response and identifying cases of GTN. According to the literature, βhCG levels can normalize after 3–4 weeks [20]. The follow-up period revealed that significant proportions of patients attained normal βhCG levels within specific timeframes: 72.5% of the patients achieved βhCG normalization within one month, 86% within two months, and 90% within three months for the entire cohort. This information is valuable for assessing the efficacy of management strategies and predicting the resolution of molar pregnancies.

Another key finding was the favourable long-term outcomes associated with molar pregnancies. Historically, molar pregnancies have been associated with concerns about malignancy and complications. However, in line with recent research, our study emphasizes that the prognosis for most molar pregnancies is optimistic [21]. The resolution of βhCG levels following surgical evacuation indicates successful management, with a high likelihood of complete recovery. This information can reassure both clinicians and patients about the potential for positive outcomes even in the face of a molar pregnancy diagnosis.

This study utilized an extensive dataset obtained from the electronic medical records of a tertiary medical centre, ensuring comprehensive and reliable information for analysis, with long-term follow-up of βhCG levels and treatment outcomes. The diverse age distribution of the study participants, ranging from 16 to 55 years, allowed for a comprehensive examination of molar pregnancies across different age groups; enhancing the generalizability of the findings.

This study was limited, most notably by the small sample size and that it was conducted in one tertiary centre. Therefore, the findings may not fully represent the broader population, and variations in management practices at other healthcare facilities could impact the results. The retrospective nature of the study may be subject to inherent biases and limitations, as it relied on existing medical records and might not capture all relevant information. In addition, some of the data might be missing due to incomplete medical records, potentially affecting the analysis and interpretation of treatment outcomes. Future prospective studies with larger, multi-centre cohorts and carefully designed control groups can further enhance our understanding of molar pregnancies and their management.

Conclusion

This study highlights the diverse age distribution of molar pregnancies within our population, with a notable proportion occurring in women within the normal reproductive age range. Additionally, we have provided insights into the dynamics of βhCG levels and long-term outcomes, which emphasize the likelihood of a favourable prognosis. These findings contribute to a better understanding of molar pregnancies and can add information for management strategies based on age and trends in βhCG levels.
