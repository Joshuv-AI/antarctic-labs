On Galois categories and condensed contractible schemes
Catrin Mair

Abstract
We extend the study of the condensed Galois category of a scheme introduced by Barwick, Glasman and Haine in their work on Exodromy [ Exodromy ] . We elaborate its connection to Lurie’s work on Ultracategories [ 22 ] and provide a description in terms of w w -contractible rings . We give a classification of schemes whose Galois category has an initial, respectively, a terminal object. This implies the condensed homotopy type of the scheme, which was studied in more detail in [ 10 ] , to be trivial. Furthermore, we compute a formula for the (underlying group of the) condensed fundamental group of a general Dedekind domain and show that it is non-trivial for Spec ⁡ ( 𝐙 ) \mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}) . This means that Spec ⁡ ( 𝐙 ) \mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z}) is not condensed contractible .

Introduction

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme. In their work on Exodromy
[

This condensed category is one variant of a condensed version of the

Its name is justified by the fact that it is, somewhat, a globalization of the absolute Galois groups of the residue fields of points of

The

Based on the work on Exodromy, together with Haine, Holzschuh, Lara, Martini and Wolf, we study in

of

\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(X,\bar{x})

This object constitutes a joint refinement of both the étale homotopy type of Friedlander-Artin-Mazur
[
7

1

and the proétale fundamental group of Bhatt-Scholze

4

In the joint project, it is shown to be of homotopy theoretical interest beyond that:
On the one hand, it behaves similarly to the étale homotopy type and, for example, satisfies a fundamental fiber sequence

On the other hand, opposite to the classical case, the condensed fundamental group of the affine line over the complex numbers as well as its abelianization are non-trivial

\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1},\bar{x})^{(ab)}\neq 1\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

The non-triviality of the fundamental group can be fixed by passing to the

Motivation

The result on the condensed fundamental group
\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1},\bar{x})
of the affine line to be non-trivial shows that the condensed homotopy type can store additional information on a scheme compared to its étale predecessor: While
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}
is étale contractible it is not
condensed contractible
. This can be stated in terms of local systems. Namely, in
[
14
, Proposition A.1]
Hemo-Richarz-Scholbach show that the condensed homotopy type
\Pi_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cond}}(X)
classifies local systems on
X
with coefficients in any condensed ring.
From this perspective, the fact that
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}
is not condensed contractible means that there exists a condensed ring with a non-trivial local system on
\mathbf{A}_{\mathbf{C}}^{1}
. It can be constructed as in
[
10
, Example 7.10]
.
An analogous statement does not hold for the étale homotopy type
[
4
, Example 7.4.9]
. The étale homotopy type is only known to classify local systems with values in profinite rings such as
\mathbf{Z}_{\ell}
.

Motivated by this, in this article we focus on the question of when a scheme is
condensed contractible
and give an overview on (non-)examples.
In particular, we compute the group
\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(\mathrm{Spec}(A),\bar{x})({\mathbin{*}})
for
A
a general Dedekind domain (
Theorem

4.25
). This provides us with

as a second example of an étale but not condensed contractible scheme,
Example

4.29
.

We follow this strategy to examine cases of schemes whose condensed homotopy type is contractible.

1.5 Theorem

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme and
\Pt(X_{\et})
the category of points of its étale topos and

its condensed Galois category. Then we have equivalences:

a)

X

\iff

\mathrm{Gal}(X)

\iff

\mathrm{Gal}(X)

\iff

b)

\mathrm{Gal}(X)

In particular, in both cases, a) and b), the schemes are condensed contractible.

Beyond this, we provide further (non-)examples of

.

(

Let

Condensed Sets and condensed anima

2.2

We recall the presentation of the

2.23 Recollection

Let

[

2.24 Definition
.

Let

\displaystyle\textbf{{Extr}}^{\op}

\displaystyle\to\textbf{{Cat}}_{\infty},\ S\mapsto\mathrm{Fun}^{*,\coh}(X_{\et},\Sh(S))\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

2.25 Remark
.

Originally, the Galois category is defined as the stratified homotopy type of the étale topos stratified over the Zariski space of the scheme

Our description appears roughly in

There pushforward functors are considered and the coherent assumption is missing. This is corrected in

2.26 Remark

Let

As the

Referring to the previous remark, we can view

The underlying category of

Let

2.30 Recollection

Points of the étale topos

Let

The category of points

\infty

–

Objects are given by geometric points:

A geometric point

\kappa(x)
is a separable closure of the residue field
\kappa(x_{0})

–

Morphisms are given by lifts of geometric points to strict localizations:

For

\bar{y}\rightarrow X

{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Spec}(\kappa(y))}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces\mathrm{Spec}(\mathcal{O}_{X,x}^{\mathrm{sh}})}
{\lx@inpgf@ignorespaces X}

Here, the prime spectrum of the strict henselization

of

2.31 Recollection
.

Morphisms of points

Let
\bar{x}\rightarrow X

\bar{y}\rightarrow X

and
y_{0}
in

1.

The set of morphisms

x_{0}
is in the closure of

2.

The morphism set

\kappa(x)

The category

[

We prove a generalization of this remark for

2.33 Recollection
.

The underlying Zariski space

of a scheme

carries the structure of a poset via the partial order induced by specializations.
For two points

x

2.34 Example

For every qcqs scheme, there is a natural functor

3
An alternative description of the Galois category

The purpose of this section is to provide an alternative and more extensive description of the Galois category
\mathrm{Gal}(X)
of a qcqs scheme

that supports the idea of
\mathrm{Gal}(X)
being a condensed and topos theoretic enrichment of the concept of (geometric) points of a scheme.
In particular, in
Theorem

, we will show for every
affine
scheme

and

the identification

(3.1)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Gal}(X)(S)

\displaystyle\simeq\{\text{$S$-connected $w$-contractible rings over $X$ + $S$-connected maps}\}\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Here,
S
-connected means that we only consider
w
-contractible rings with
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{Spec}(A))=S
and morphisms of rings

such that
\mathrm{Spec}(B)\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(A)
induces the identity on
\pi_{0}
. This extends
Remark

2.32
as a

-contractible ring is connected iff it is a strictly henselian local ring.

3.1

We need the following selection of definitions, mainly taken from
[

3.2 Definition
.

[
4
, Definition 2.1.1, Lemma 2.1.4]

Let
X
be a spectral space. We call
X

w
-local
if it satisfies:

(1)

The subspace
X_{\mathrm{cl}}\subset X
of closed points is closed.

(2)

Every connected component has a unique closed point.

3.3 Remark
.

Let
X
be a
w
-local spectral space.
The composition

, where
\pi_{0}(X)
carries its profinite topology, is a homeomorphism, see
[
4
, Lemma 2.1.4]
.

3.4 Definition
.

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme.

(1)

We call
X

w
-local
if its underlying spectral space is
w
-local.

(2)

We call
X

w
-strictly local
if it is
w
-local and every étale surjection

3.5 Remark

The connected components of a
w
-local qcqs scheme

correspond to local rings at closed points: By the assumption on every connected component to have a unique closed point, every other point in the connected component needs to specialize to this closed point. The local ring at the closed point is exactly the subscheme of its generalizations
[

,
Tag 01J7
]
.

3.6 Remark
.

As observed in
[

, every

-strictly local qcqs scheme is affine as it is a retract of an affine scheme.

3.7 Remark
.

A

w
-local qcqs scheme such that all local rings at closed points are strictly henselian
[
4
, Lemma 2.2.9]

3.8 Definition
.

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme. An affine scheme

if every pro-étale covering morphism, i.e., every faithfully flat weakly étale map

3.9 Definition
.

A ring

is

-contractible
if every faithfully flat ind-etale map
A\rightarrow B
, i.e.,
B
is a filtered colimit of étale

.

Weakly contractible affine schemes identify with

-contractible rings. Moreover for

-contractible,

is

-strictly local,

We give a simple example on how to create from a local ring

Let

For every profinite set

4
On condensed contractible schemes

We first recap how the notions of connected and contractible categories naturally translate to the condensed setting. Then we collect examples of schemes which are known to be
condensed contractible
, i.e., the condensed homotopy type is contractible.

4.1 Recollection
.

An
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is
connected
, respectively,
contractible,
if the classifying anima
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{C}\smallin\textbf{{Ani}}
is a connected, respectively, (weakly) contractible Kan complex.

4.2 Definition
.

We say that a condensed
\infty
-category
\mathcal{C}
is
connected
, if for all
K\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
the
\infty
-categories
\mathcal{C}(K)\smallin\textbf{{Cat}}_{\infty}
are connected.
Equivalently, if the classifying condensed anima
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{C}\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Ani}})
is
connected
, i.e., its condensed connected components are trivial
\pi_{0}(\mathrm{B}\mathcal{C})={\mathbin{*}}
.
Similarly, a condensed category
\mathcal{C}\smallin\textbf{{Cat}}_{\infty}
is
contractible
if the evaluation
\mathcal{C}(K)
at all
S\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
is contractible, i.e., the anima
\mathrm{B}\mathcal{C}(K)={\mathbin{*}}
are contractible.

4.3 Definition
.

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme.

(1)

We call the scheme
X

condensed connected
if the condensed connected components are trivial, i.e., we have
\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{0}(X)={\mathbin{*}}
.

(2)

We call the scheme
X

condensed contractible
if the condensed homotopy type is contractible, i.e., it is
\Pi_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cond}}(X)={\mathbin{*}}.

4.4 Remark
.

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme. By definition,
X
is condensed connected, respectively, condensed contractible if and only if the condensed category
\mathrm{Gal}(X)\smallin\Cond(\textbf{{Cat}})
is connected, respectively, contractible. Whenever
\Pi_{\infty}^{\mathrm{cond}}(X)
, respectively
\mathrm{Gal}(X)
, is contractible it is connected. The reverse direction is not true.

4.5 Remark
.

Under notice of
Remark

2.42
, a scheme is condensed contractible if and only if it is condensed connected an all its condensed fundamental groups (which then are independent of the base point) are trivial.

, Theorem 1]
, Holschbach-Schmidt-Stix prove that there does not exist a non-trivial smooth variety in positive characteristic which is étale contractible.
By the previous remark, this observation likewise translates to condensed contractibility.

Studying the previous examples, one might wonder whether the condensed homotopy type adds anything new to the picture of homotopy theory for schemes. The next example on a scheme that is étale but not condensed contractible might give an answer.

4.21 Example
.

In
[
10
, Section 7.1]
, it is proven that the condensed fundamental group of the affine line over the complex numbers
\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}},\bar{\eta})
is non-trivial. Hence,
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
is not condensed contractible although it is étale contractible: By the profiniteness theorem
[
1
, Theorem 11.1]
, the étale homotopy type of
\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}}
is profinite and coincides by the generalized Riemann existence theorem
[
1
, Theorem 12.9, Corollary 12.10]
with the profinite completion of the classical homotopy type
\Pi_{\infty}(\mathbf{C})
of the complex numbers which is contractible.

The following section deals with an extension of this example and especially a generalization of the computation of
\mathrm{\pi}^{\mathrm{cond}}_{1}(\mathbf{A}^{1}_{\mathbf{C}},\bar{\eta})
given in
[
10
, Section 7.1]
to general Dedekind domains.

4.2
Condensed fundamental groups of Dedekind domains

In the following, we give a formula for the computation of the underlying group of the condensed fundamental group of a general Dedekind domain. Following an argument by Jakob Stix, we show that for
\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})

\mathrm{Spec}(\mathbf{Z})
provides another example of an étale contractible scheme, compare
[
Exodromy
, Example 11.2.2]
, that is not condensed contractible.

5.5 Lemma

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme.
Then the following is always true:

a)

\Pt(X_{\et})
has a terminal object
\iff

\Pt(X_{\et})

b)

\Pt(X_{\et})

Note that for posets, being (co)filtered is equivalent to being
(co)directed
and terminal, respectively, initial objects correspond to

, respectively,

5.6 Lemma
.

Let
X
be a qcqs scheme.
The following assertions are equivalent.

a)

The Zariski poset
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
has a maximal element (respectively, a minimal element).

b)

The Zariski poset
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
is (co)directed.

c)

The scheme
X
is irreducible (respectively, local, i.e., corresponds to a local ring).

Proof.

The equivalences of a), b) and c) rely on the definition of the order by specializations.
For a)
\implies b)
note that a poset with a maximal or minimal object is, in particular, (co)directed. For b)
\implies
c), we have to argue why there is a unique generic (closed) point:
The poset
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
to be (co)directed means that any two points are specializations (generalizations) of a common point.
We handle the two cases separately.

1.

The directed case:

To satisfy the condition on specializations, the sober topological space
|X|
cannot have more than one irreducible component since two maximal points, i.e., the unique generic points of irreducible components, do not specialize further.
This implies irreducibility.

2.

The codirected case:

Every closed point of
|X|
does not generalize further as there is no other point lying in its closure. Hence, a scheme with more than one closed point contradicts the condition on
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
to be codirected.
A scheme that is quasi-compact and contains exactly one closed point is known to be equivalent to the spectrum of a local ring.
This follows from the fact that every quasi-compact scheme, and thus also every closed subset of a quasi-compact scheme, has a closed point.
Then the complement of an affine open containing the unique closed point has to be empty.
This already implies that the scheme itself is affine with a unique closed point corresponding to a unique maximal ideal of the underlying ring.

From c) to a), it is clear that the unique generic point or, respectively, the unique closed point of the scheme constitutes a maximal or, respectively, a minimal element of
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
.
∎

5.7 Remark
.

If the category of points
\mathrm{Gal}(X)({\mathbin{*}})=\Pt(X_{\et})
of a qcqs scheme

is (co)filtered, the underlying Zariski poset
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
is (co)directed.
By the previous lemma, the scheme already needs to be irreducible, respectively, local.

We call an object in an

This definition differs from the one of initial, respectively, terminal objects only by the fact that no uniqueness of morphisms (up to a contractible choice) is required.

5.9 Lemma

Let

Proof.

Assume that

By the description of

Before stating a complete proof of the theorem, we give an idea of the arguments and outsource different results needed.
Justification for the claim comes from the following:

5.18 Example

Let
X
be an absolutely integrally closed scheme, i.e., an integral normal scheme whose function field is algebraically closed.
In other words,

is the strict normalization

of a scheme

In

Particularly, the Galois category

As any absolute integrally closed scheme is everywhere strictly local

For a general everywhere strictly local scheme, we can fall back to the Zariski space.

5.19 Proposition
.

Let
X
be an everywhere strictly local qcqs scheme.
Then for every
S\smallin\textbf{{Extr}}
we have an identification of categories

\mathrm{Gal}(X)(S)=\Hom_{qc}(S,|X|)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {,}\hss}

where
\Hom_{qc}(S,|X|)
is the set of quasicompact continuous maps
S\rightarrow|X|
from
S
to the underlying Zariski space of

which inherits the structure of a category through the poset structure of
|X|
.

More precisely, the poset of quasicompact maps
f\colon S\to|X|
is ordered by pointwise specialization: it is
f\leq g
if and only if for all
s\smallin S
, we have
f(s)\smallin\overline{\{g(s)\}}
.
In particlular,
\mathrm{Gal}(X)({\mathbin{*}})
recovers the specialization poset
{X}_{\zar}^{\leq}
of
|X|

Proof.

For
X
an everywhere strictly local scheme, the étale and the Zariski topoi of

agree
[
30

.
Using this, we are reduced to algebraic morphisms between sheaf topoi of spectral topological spaces as

\mathrm{Gal}(X)(S)\simeq\mathrm{Fun}^{*,\coh}(\Sh(|X|,\textbf{{Set}}),\Sh(S,\textbf{{Set}}))\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

Geometric morphisms of sheaf topoi on topological spaces are the same as morphisms of the underlying locales of opens of these spaces.
As proven in
[
18
, Proposition C.1.4.5]
, this refines to an equivalence of categories

(5.20)

\displaystyle\mathrm{Fun}_{*}(\Sh(S,\textbf{{Set}}),\Sh(|X|,\textbf{{Set}}))\simeq\Hom_{\mathrm{Loc}}(\mathrm{Ouv}(S),\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|))\hbox to0.0pt{\ {,}\hss}

where a morphism
f\colon\mathrm{Ouv}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|)
of the locales of open subsets corresponds to a morphism

f^{-1}\colon\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ouv}(S)

of frames. Then the set of morphisms on the right hand side in (
5.20
) obtains the structure of a poset by the partial order of inclusions on the frames.
Indeed, we say that there exists a morphism
f\rightarrow g
between two maps
f,g\colon\mathrm{Ouv}(S)\rightarrow\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|)
if and only if for all
U\smallin\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|)
open subset of
|X|
we have
f^{-1}(U)\subset g^{-1}(U)
.
Furthermore, it is known by
[
24
, Chapter IX, Section 3, Theorem 1]
that the functor

\mathrm{Ouv}\colon\textbf{{Top}}\rightarrow\mathrm{Loc},\ X\mapsto\mathrm{Ouv}(X)

sending a topological space to its locale of opens has a right adjoint
\Pt\colon\mathrm{Loc}\rightarrow\textbf{{Top}}
and that the unit of the adjunction is a homeomorphism exactly on sober topological spaces
[
24
, Chapter IX, Section 3, Proposition 2]
.
As the underlying space of a scheme is sober, the adjunction gives bijective correspondences

\displaystyle\Hom_{\mathrm{Loc}}(\mathrm{Ouv}(S),\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|))

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(S,\Pt(\mathrm{Ouv}(|X|)))

(5.21)

\displaystyle\simeq\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(S,|X|)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {.}\hss}

These extend to equivalences of categories by taking over the poset structure from the left hand side.
In more detail, there exists a morphism between two continuous maps
f,g\colon S\rightarrow|X|

if and only if for all open subsets
U\subset|X|
it is
f^{-1}(U)\subset g^{-1}(U)
. If we now take the opposite of this poset, we obtain a poset structure on
\Hom_{\textbf{{Top}}}(S,|X|)
given as follows: We have a morphism
f\rightarrow g
or an order
f\leq g
between two continuous maps
f,g\colon S\rightarrow|X|
if and only if for all
s\smallin S
it is
f(s)\smallin\overline{\{g(s)\}}
. Indeed, the condition
f(s)\smallin\overline{\{g(s)\}}
for all
s\smallin S
is equivalent to the condition
g^{-1}(U)\subset f^{-1}(U)
for all opens
U\subset|X|
.
Comparing the results of (
5.20
) and (
5.2
) with the claim in the proposition, it is only left to relate coherent geometric morphisms with quasi-compact maps of topological spaces.
That these two can in our situation be identified with each other is explained in
[

\mathrm{Gal}(X)(S)=\Hom_{qc}(S,|X|)\hbox to0.0pt{\ {,}\hss}

where the poset structure on the right is inherited by the poset structure of

The equivalence

Assuming (i), we know that the residue fields at all local rings are separably closed.

Let

\eta\smallin Y
its generic point and

Then

maps every maximal point of
X

Proof.

We use
[
AG1
, Lemma 14.42]
, which tells us that taking the inverse image of
\{\eta\}
under
f
is compatible with taking closures, i.e., we have

f^{-1}(\bar{\eta})=\overline{f^{-1}(\eta)}
.
Note that the Lemma can be applied to our prerequisites as flat morphisms of schemes are generizing
[
AG1
, Lemma 14.9]
and the canonical map
\mathrm{Spec}(\kappa(\eta))\rightarrow X
mapping to
\eta
exhibits the one point set
\{\eta\}
as a pro-constructible set.
As
\eta
is the generic point, the closure of the preimage of
\eta
has to be the whole space
X
.
Thus, all maximal points of
X
, which are the unique generic points of the irreducible components, have to be contained in the preimage, i.e., they are mapped to
\eta
under
f
.
∎

Proof of

.

The canonical map

As there is only one point in the preimage assumed, we can deduce that the spectrum of the strict henselization has a unique maximal point which means nothing else than that it its irreducible.

5.29 Remark

Theorem

Classification of Gal(X) having an initial object

We have explained in great detail what it means for a scheme to have a Galois category with trivial object.

The scheme

Let

Proof.

Again by

As the property to be faithfully flat quasi-compact is stable under base change, also every base change of

.
Since moreover a bijective quotient map is a homeomorphism and surjectivity is stable under base change, to show (

is universally injective.
Referring to
[

1.

We show that the map of schemes
\mathrm{Spec}(A^{\mathrm{sh}})\rightarrow\mathrm{Spec}(A)

For a point

, every object in the fiber of

under

determines at least one morphism

.
As there is assumed to exist only one such morphism, the fiber has to consist of exactly one point.
As the argument works for every

2.

For a point

5.37 Remark

Invariance under universal homeomorphisms of the (pro-)étale topos of a scheme
