Abstract
Using 10 087 ± ± 44 × 10 6 10\,087\pm\pm 44\text{\times}{10}^{6} J / ψ J/\psi events collected with the BESIII detector, we perform the first amplitude analysis of the process J / ψ → γ ​ η ​ π 0 J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0} . The decay is dominated by the intermediate processes J / ψ → π 0 b 1 ( 1235 ) 0 ( → γ η ) J/\psi\to\pi^{0}b_{1}(1235)^{0}\left(\to\gamma\eta\right) , J / ψ → π 0 ρ ( 1450 ) 0 ( → γ η ) J/\psi\to\pi^{0}\rho(1450)^{0}\left(\to\gamma\eta\right) and J / ψ → η h 1 ( 1170 ) ( → γ π 0 ) J/\psi\to\eta h_{1}(1170)\left(\to\gamma\pi^{0}\right) . I
Introduction

The radiative decay
J/\psi\to\gamma X
provides a clean environment in which to search for glueballs
Reinders et al. 1985
;
*Morningstar:1999rf
;
*Gross:2022hyw

Among isospin-multiplet mesons, the

Similar questions motivate studies of the

The decay

BESIII Experiment and Data Sets

The BESIII detector

The solenoid is supported by an octagonal flux-return yoke with resistive plate counter muon identification modules interleaved with steel.
The charged-particle momentum resolution at

is

0.5\%
, and the
{\rm d}E/{\rm d}x

6\%
for electrons
from Bhabha scattering events.
The EMC measures photon energies with a resolution of
2.5\%
(
5\%

1\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}

The time resolution in the TOF barrel region is
68\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}

until its upgrade in
2015. The upgraded end cap TOF system uses multi-gap resistive plate chamber technology

;
*Guo2017
;
*Cao:2020ibk
, providing a time resolution of
60\text{\,}\mathrm{p}\mathrm{s}
, which benefits
87\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the data used in this analysis.

Simulated data samples produced with a
geant4

*1610988

*JADACH2000260

III
Event Selection

The
\eta
and
\pi^{0}
mesons are reconstructed via their dominant decay modes
\eta\to\gamma\gamma
and
\pi^{0}\to\gamma\gamma
, which offer high branching fractions and clean signatures. Signal events are required to contain at least five photon candidates and no charged tracks.
Photon candidates are identified as isolated EMC showers with energies above 25 MeV in the barrel (
|\cos\theta|<0.80
) or 50 MeV in the end cap (
0.86<|\cos\theta|<0.92
).
A four-constraint (4C) kinematic fit imposing overall energy-momentum conservation is performed on all five photon combinations, and the one with the smallest

\chi^{2}
(
<25
) is selected as the signal candidate. The resulting four-momenta are used for subsequent background suppression. The
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
candidates are identified by minimizing

\Delta^{2}=\frac{(m_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}-m_{\pi^{0}}^{\rm{PDG}})^{2}}{\sigma_{\pi^{0}}^{2}}+\frac{(m_{\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}}-m_{\eta}^{\rm{PDG}})^{2}}{\sigma_{\eta}^{2}},

(1)

where
m_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}
(
m_{\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}}
) is the invariant mass of two of five photons,
m_{\pi^{0}}^{\rm{PDG}}
(
m_{\eta}^{\rm{PDG}}
) is the known

\pi^{0}
(
\eta
) mass cited from the PDG
Navas et al. 2024
, while
\sigma_{\pi^{0}}=$5\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
(
\sigma_{\eta}=$9\text{\,}\mathrm{M}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
) represents the mass resolution of
\pi^{0}
(
\eta
).
A six-constraint (6C) kinematic fit that additionally constraining the
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
invariant masses to their known values
Navas et al. 2024
is then applied to improve the resolution, and the resulting four-momenta are used as input to the amplitude analysis.

Potential background contributions are investigated using inclusive MC samples and their event topology characteristics
Zhou et al. 2021
. The dominant backgrounds stem from incorrect photon pair associations in
\pi^{0}
and
\eta
reconstruction.
To mitigate these backgrounds, a discriminating variable
\Delta^{2}_{\pi^{0}}
is introduced:

\Delta^{2}_{\pi^{0}}=(m_{\gamma_{1}\gamma_{2}}-m_{\pi^{0}}^{\mathrm{PDG}})^{2}+(m_{\gamma_{3}\gamma_{4}}-m_{\pi^{0}}^{\mathrm{PDG}})^{2}.

(2)

After evaluating all possible four-photon combinations, the requirement
\min\left(\Delta^{2}_{\pi^{0}}\right)>$0.05\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}^{2}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{4}$
is
imposed
Ablikim et al. 2016
, which removes
99.5\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the combinatorial backgrounds while maintaining
68\text{\,}\mathrm{\%}
of the signal events.
In addition, several other combinatorial backgrounds can be further identified by examining intermediate resonances in the corresponding mass distributions. To
be specific, the following mass-window requirements are imposed to suppress background contribution from
\omega\to\gamma\pi^{0}
in the decays

J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime}(\to\gamma\omega)
and
J/\psi\to\pi^{0}b_{1}(1235)^{0}(\to\omega\pi^{0})

•

\left|m_{\pi^{0}\gamma_{\eta}}-m_{\omega}^{\mathrm{PDG}}\right|>65
MeV/
c^{2}
,

•

\left|m_{\gamma\gamma_{\eta}}-m_{\omega}^{\mathrm{PDG}}\right|>65
MeV/
c^{2}
,

and the following one for
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\eta
decay:

•

\left|m_{\gamma\gamma_{\pi^{0}}}-m_{\eta}^{\mathrm{PDG}}\right|>39
MeV/
c^{2}
,

where
\gamma_{\eta}
(
\gamma_{\pi^{0}}
) is one of the photons from the selected
\eta
(
\pi^{0}
) candidate,
and
m_{\omega}^{\mathrm{PDG}}
is the known mass of the
\omega
meson
Navas et al. 2024

After these criteria, the MC studies indicate that the remaining background is dominated by
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta^{\prime}
with subsequent decay
\eta^{\prime}\to\gamma\gamma\pi^{0}
or
\pi^{0}\pi^{0}\eta
and
J/\psi\to\eta\omega(\phi)
with
\omega(\phi)\to\gamma\pi^{0}

Therefore, the events fulfilling

Notably, these two requirements will suppress over

of the

\eta\pi^{0}

Background Studies and Signal Extraction

Background contributions are classified into two categories:
(I) those in which the
\eta
and
\pi^{0}
are correctly reconstructed, and
(II) those in which at least one of them is mis-reconstructed.

Since the decay
J/\psi\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
is forbidden by the charge conjugation symmetry, the category I background is dominated by the process

J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
with high
\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
mass, or from
\eta_{c}
decays, where the radiative photon from
J/\psi
or one of
the photons from a
\pi^{0}
is too soft to be detected.
The category I background yield is estimated to be
225\pm\pm 15
with a data driven method described below:

1.

Select
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
candidates in the
\eta_{c}
mass region (
m_{\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}}>$2.89\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
) with the same event selection as the nominal event selection criteria, but requiring the final state to be
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
.

2.

Generate PHSP MC samples. The lineshape of the
\eta_{c}
is observed to deviate from a standard relativistic Breit-Wigner distribution
Mitchell et al. 2009
. Therefore, a correction term
E_{\gamma_{J/\psi}}^{3}\cdot e^{-E_{\gamma_{J/\psi}}^{2}/\beta^{2}}
is introduced to modify the Breit-Wigner function, where
E_{\gamma_{J/\psi}}
is the energy of the radiative photon from the
J/\psi
decay in the rest frame of
J/\psi
, and
\beta
is the damping factor.

3.

Define a reweighting factor
w_{\eta_{c}}
as the ratio of the Dalitz PHSP plot of the decay
\eta_{c}\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
between data and MC samples.

4.

Apply the reweighting factor
w_{\eta_{c}}
to PHSP MC sample and estimate the background contribution by performing the nominal selection criteria of the signal process.

Category-II backgrounds, arising mainly from mis-reconstructed
\eta
and/or
\pi^{0}
candidates, are separated from the signal with a
Q
-weight method

*CrystalBarrel:2014tvm
;
*BESIII:2019hek
;

(3)

where

\pi^{0}\eta
,

\gamma\eta

\gamma

\pi^{0}

The normalization factors

[0,1]

2.1\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}^{2}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{4}

and

\displaystyle=N_{1}\cdot(s_{\pi^{0}}\times s_{\eta})+N_{2}\cdot(s_{\pi^{0}}\times b_{\eta})

(4)

\displaystyle+N_{3}\cdot(b_{\pi^{0}}\times s_{\eta})+N_{4}\cdot(b_{\pi^{0}}\times b_{\eta}),

V
Amplitude Analysis

To determine the branching fractions of intermediate states and to search for possible new resonances, we perform the first amplitude analysis of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}
.
The analysis is performed by using the
GPUPWA

Berger et al. 2010

J/\psi\to\gamma\ X(\to\eta\pi^{0})
,
J/\psi\to\pi^{0}\ Y(\to\gamma\eta)
, and
J/\psi\to\eta\ Z(\to\gamma\pi^{0})
.
Charge-conjugation and parity conservation allow
0^{++}
and
2^{++}
for
X
, and
1^{+-}
and
1^{--}
for
Y
and
Z
.
Isospin conservation further restricts
X
and
Y
to isospin triplets and
Z
to an isospin singlet.

The decay amplitude for
N_{\mathrm{wave}}
partial waves is constructed with the covariant-tensor formalism
Zou and Bugg 2003
:

\mathcal{A}=-\frac{1}{2}\sum_{i}^{N_{\mathrm{wave}}}\sum_{j}^{N_{\mathrm{wave}}}\Lambda_{i}\Lambda_{j}^{*}\sum_{\mu=1}^{2}U_{i}^{\mu\nu}g_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}^{(\bot\bot)}U_{j}^{*\mu\nu^{\prime}},

(6)

where

•

U^{\mu\nu}
represents the covariant tensor combining partial waves and intermediate state lineshapes;

•

\Lambda_{i}=m_{i}e^{i\phi_{i}}
is the complex magnitude parameter floated in the fit;

•

g_{\nu\nu^{\prime}}^{(\bot\bot)}
is the dressed metric tensor for radiative decays
Zou and Bugg 2003
.

Intermediate states are described by relativistic Breit-Wigner (RBW) function
Breit and Wigner 1936
with corresponding PDG masses and widths, except for the
a_{0}(980)^{0}
(Flatté
Wu and Zou 2008
;
Ablikim et al. 2021
) and the
\rho(770)^{0}
and
\rho(1450)^{0}
(Gounaris–Sakurai
Gounaris and Sakurai 1968
).
No non-resonant contribution is included in the fit.

The fraction of each quasi-two-body process
X
is evaluated at both generator and reconstruction level:

f_{X,\mathrm{gen}}=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{gen}}}\mathcal{A}_{X}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{gen}}}\mathcal{A}},\quad f_{X,\mathrm{rec}}=\frac{\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{rec}}}\mathcal{A}_{X}}{\sum_{n=1}^{N_{\mathrm{rec}}}\mathcal{A}},

(7)

where
N_{\mathrm{gen}}
and
N_{\mathrm{rec}}
are the numbers of generated and reconstructed events in the PHSP MC sample (in the full
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}
region),
respectively, and
\mathcal{A}_{X}
sums over partial waves for the process
X
only. The efficiency for process
X
is then calculated as

\left(f_{X,\mathrm{rec}}N_{\mathrm{rec}}\right)/\left(f_{X,\mathrm{gen}}N_{\mathrm{gen}}\right)
.

An unbinned maximum likelihood fit is performed on the data.
To account for the signal events in the region
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}<$1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
, the object function for likelihood minimization is revised as,

\displaystyle s=

\displaystyle-\sum_{i}^{N_{\mathrm{data}}}Q_{i}\ln{\frac{\mathcal{A}_{i}}{C^{+}}}+\sum_{i}^{N_{\eta_{c}}}w_{\eta_{c},i}\ln{\mathcal{A}_{i}}

\displaystyle-\frac{\left(\frac{C^{-}}{C^{+}}\sum_{i}^{N_{\mathrm{data}}}Q_{i}-N_{0}\right)^{2}}{\sigma_{0}^{2}},

(8)

where

•

the first term is for the signal candidates above
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}>$1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
,
N_{\mathrm{data}}
is the number of data events,
Q_{i}
is the signal weight obtained with the
Q
-weight method as described in Sec.
IV
,
C^{+}
is the normalization factor in the
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}>$1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
region;

•

the second term is for the category I background obtained with the MC simulated sample of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}(\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0}
).
N_{\eta_{c}}
is the number of
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta_{c}(\to\eta\pi^{0}\pi^{0})
simulated sample,
\omega_{\eta_{c},j}
is the corresponding event weight to ensure the correct background model and the background yield
225\pm\pm 15
, obtained with the data-driven method as described in Sec.
IV
;

•

the third term imposes a Gaussian constraint on the signal yield
N_{0}=$676\pm\pm 80$
in the region
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}<$1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
, which is estimated in Sec.
IV
.
C^{-}
is the corresponding normalization factor in the region
m_{\eta\pi^{0}}<$1.0\text{\,}\mathrm{G}\mathrm{e}\mathrm{V}\mathrm{/}\mathrm{\text{$c$}}^{2}$
obtained from the amplitude model.

The amplitude model is optimized by sequentially testing all known intermediate states from the PDG compilation
Navas et al. 2024
.
Each resonance is added to the baseline model individually, and only those processes exhibiting statistical significance larger than

5
\sigma
(evaluated from the change in likelihood and the number of additional free parameters
Fraser 1991
) are retained in the nominal amplitude model.
For the nominal amplitude mode, the yield of process
X
is calculated as
N_{X}=f_{X,\mathrm{rec}}\left(\sum_{i}^{N_{\mathrm{data}}}Q_{i}+N_{0}\right)
, and the corresponding statistical uncertainty of the yield is evaluated using the bootstrap method
Efron 1979
;
Ablikim et al. 2024c
.
100 bootstrap samples are generated by randomly sampling the data events with replacement, and the variation of the fitted yields across these samples is taken as the statistical uncertainty.

Summary

The decay
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}
is studied with a data sample of
10\,087\pm\pm 44\text{\times}{10}^{6}

From a broader perspective, this study offers valuable experimental inputs for ongoing efforts to understand light meson structures and interactions. While current theoretical models are consistent with several key observations, further predictions, especially those involving alternative meson configurations and decay dynamics, would increase the significance and scope of these observations.
Experimentally, the limited statistics hinder the identification of additional intermediate states in the decay
J/\psi\to\gamma\eta\pi^{0}
.
The possible existence of exotic radiative decay channels such as
J/\psi\to\gamma a_{0}(1710)^{0}(\to\eta\pi^{0})

Ablikim et al. 2022c
and
J/\psi\to\gamma\pi_{1}(1600)^{0}(\to\eta\pi^{0})

Thompson et al. 1997
, along with the hypotheses involving two-pole structures
Clymton and Kim 2023
;
Clymton and Kim 2024
or unobserved members of the
b_{1}
and
h_{1}
meson families, remain open and compelling questions. To obtain more definitive and comprehensive results, larger
J/\psi
data samples will be essential in future studies
Achasov et al. 2024
;
*Charm-TauFactory:2013cnj
.
