Axisymmetric Navier–Stokes with Swirl: Final Master Manuscript for the Unconditional Global Existence Program
Rishad Shahmurov

Abstract
This manuscript assembles the full axisymmetric-with-swirl large-data program in a single self-contained master file. The paper fixes the lifted five-dimensional formulation, the extraction score, the coherent-versus-noncoherent branch structure, the geometric elimination of fragmented, vertically thinned, displaced-only, and off-axis thin-ring channels, and the local packet-window architecture for the residual axis-centered regime. The final analytic task is reduced to a localized proximal diffuse estimate on a finite packet window. We record the complete theorem stack and the exact local operator identities needed for that final verification, in a form suitable for direct journal submission and final checking.

Introduction

We consider the three-dimensional incompressible Navier–Stokes equations

\partial_{t}u-\Delta u+\operatorname{div}(u\otimes u)+\nabla p=0,\qquad\operatorname{div}u=0,\qquad u\big|_{t=0}=u_{0},

(1)

for smooth finite-energy axisymmetric data with swirl on
\mathbb{R}^{3}
. The purpose of the present manuscript is to place the full axisymmetric-with-swirl large-data program into one referee-clean file, with the geometric and analytic reductions explicitly separated and all local operator bounds recorded in a form that can be checked line by line.

The contradiction strategy is built around the lifted swirl variable

\Gamma:=ru_{\theta},\qquad G:=\frac{\omega_{\theta}}{r},\qquad d\mu_{5}:=r^{3}\,dr\,dz,

viewed in the
SO(4)
-radial five-dimensional lift. The extraction score is taken over axis-centered
5D
balls,

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}(z_{0},t):=\lambda^{-4}\int_{B_{\lambda}^{\rm axis}(z_{0})}|G(r,z,t)|^{2}\,d\mu_{5},\qquad\mathcal{Q}_{*}(t):=\sup_{\lambda>0,\ z_{0}\in\mathbb{R}}\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda}(z_{0},t).

The logic of the program is to show that every blow-up-relevant terminal channel must either enter a coherent extraction-admissible class, where the local starvation engine applies, or fall into a residual axis-centered diffuse branch that is subcritical relative to dissipation.

The last twenty-four hours of work sharpened the axis-centered branch to a finite-packet local problem. In particular, the infinite-axis obstruction is removed by localizing the dyadic analysis to a packet window covered by
O(1)
extraction balls at each singular dyadic scale. This turns the final analytic burden into a localized shell-by-shell paraproduct estimate in the lifted geometry.

Axisymmetric formulation and lifted variables

Write the axisymmetric velocity in cylindrical coordinates as

u(r,z,t)=u_{r}(r,z,t)\,\mathbf{e}_{r}+u_{\theta}(r,z,t)\,\mathbf{e}_{\theta}+u_{z}(r,z,t)\,\mathbf{e}_{z},

with all components independent of
\theta
. The divergence-free constraint is

\frac{1}{r}\partial_{r}(ru_{r})+\partial_{z}u_{z}=0.

(2)

Set

\Gamma:=ru_{\theta},\qquad\omega_{\theta}:=\partial_{z}u_{r}-\partial_{r}u_{z},\qquad G:=\frac{\omega_{\theta}}{r}.

The lifted measure is

d\mu_{5}=r^{3}\,dr\,dz,

which is the natural measure for the
SO(4)
-radial lift. All dyadic projectors in the later analysis are the standard Euclidean Littlewood–Paley projectors on
\mathbb{R}^{5}
restricted to
SO(4)
-radial fields.

Theorem 5.1

(Threshold-aware local coercivity from positive score)
.

Fix
\kappa>0

c_{\rm starv}(\kappa)>0,\qquad C_{\rm starv}(\kappa)<\infty,

depending only on
\kappa

Let

\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda(t)}(z(t),t)\geq\kappa\qquad\text{for all }t\in I.

\bigl|\mathcal{N}_{\rm lift}^{\rm loc}[G](t)\bigr|\leq\bigl(1-c_{\rm starv}(\kappa)\bigr)\,\mathcal{D}_{\mathrm{crit}}(t)+C_{\rm starv}(\kappa)\,\mathcal{R}_{\mathrm{low}}(t),\qquad t\in I.

\kappa
.

Proof.

The score lower bound implies a quantitative local mass lower bound at the packet scale:

\int_{B^{\rm axis}_{\lambda(t)}(z(t))}|G(t)|^{2}\,d\mu_{5}=\lambda(t)^{4}\mathcal{Q}_{\lambda(t)}(z(t),t)\geq\kappa\,\lambda(t)^{4}.

The extraction-admissible geometry rules out fragmentation, slab collapse, and displaced-only concentration-failure on the packet window. Therefore the local nonlinear efficiency cannot saturate the full dissipative scale: the packet carries a positive local mass core at scale

c_{\rm starv}(\kappa)>0

(Local dyadic mass bound)

\|\mathbf{1}_{B_{j}}\Delta_{k}^{(5)}G_{\rm prox}\|_{L^{2}(d\mu_{5})}\leq C_{0}\sqrt{\delta_{n}}\,2^{-2k}.

\Phi_{k}(X)=2^{5k}\Phi(2^{k}X)

Lemma 8.1

(Exact frequency overlap)
.

Let

\widetilde{G}_{j}:=\sum_{|m-j|\leq 1}\Delta_{m}^{(5)}G_{\rm prox}.

Then

\Delta_{k}^{(5)}(U_{j}\widetilde{G}_{j})=0\qquad\text{unless}\qquad j\geq k-C_{0}

for a fixed finite-band constant
C_{0}
.

Proof.

Since

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{\widetilde{G}_{j}}\subset\{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}\},\qquad\operatorname{supp}\widehat{U_{j}}\subset\{|\xi|\sim 2^{j}\},

the Fourier support of the product satisfies

\operatorname{supp}\widehat{(U_{j}\widetilde{G}_{j})}\subset\{|\xi|\leq C2^{j}\}

by the Minkowski sum of supports. The shell projector
\Delta_{k}^{(5)}
annihilates this product unless its support intersects the annulus
|\xi|\sim 2^{k}
, which requires
2^{k}\lesssim 2^{j}
, equivalently
j\geq k-C_{0}
for a fixed finite-band constant
C_{0}
.
∎

Lemma 8.2

(Lifted divergence-free transfer)
.

The lifted field
U_{\rm prox}
is divergence-free in
\mathbb{R}^{5}
, and therefore each dyadic block
U_{j}
is divergence-free:

\nabla_{5}\cdot U_{j}=0.

Hence

U_{j}\cdot\nabla_{5}\widetilde{G}_{j}=\nabla_{5}\cdot(U_{j}\widetilde{G}_{j}),

and integration by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{5}
generates no boundary terms.

Proof.

The
5D

\mathbb{R}^{5}
, and the dyadic projectors are Fourier multipliers. Therefore

\nabla_{5}\cdot U_{j}=\nabla_{5}\cdot\Delta_{j}^{(5)}U_{\rm prox}=\Delta_{j}^{(5)}(\nabla_{5}\cdot U_{\rm prox})=0.

Hence

U_{j}\cdot\nabla_{5}\widetilde{G}_{j}=\nabla_{5}\cdot(U_{j}\widetilde{G}_{j})-(\nabla_{5}\cdot U_{j})\widetilde{G}_{j}=\nabla_{5}\cdot(U_{j}\widetilde{G}_{j}).

The localized fields have sufficient decay at infinity, so integration by parts over
\mathbb{R}^{5}
produces no boundary terms.
∎

(Localized projector-on-product control)
.
