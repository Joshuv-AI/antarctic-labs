Stability Analysis of Higher Order Fractional Difference Equations
Janardhan Chevala, Sachin Bhalekar

Abstract
Fractional difference equations provide a flexible mathematical framework for modeling complex systems with memory, hereditary, and non-local effects. In this work, we study the stability of higher-order two-term fractional linear difference equations Δ α ​ x ​ ( t ) + a ​ Δ β ​ x ​ ( t + α − β − 1 ) = ( b − 1 ) ​ x ​ ( t + α − 2 ) \Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta-1)=(b-1)x(t+\alpha-2) . The stability results are derived, and we discuss the bifurcations for 0 < β ≤ 1 < α ≤ 2 0<\beta\leq 1<\alpha\leq 2 , a > 0 a>0 , b ∈ ℂ b\in\mathbb{C} or b ∈ ℝ b\in\mathbb{R} with examples. We extend this to the stability of an equilibrium point of a nonlinear higher-order fractional difference equation. Moreover, we study the stability of higher-order one-term linear fractional difference equations Δ α ​ x ​ ( t ) = ( c − 1 ) ​ x ​ ( t + α − N ) \Delta^{\alpha}x(t)=(c-1)x(t+\alpha-N) with N − 1 < α ≤ N N-1<\alpha\leq N , where N ∈ ℕ N\in\mathbb{N} .

[

For a function

(\Delta^{-\beta}x)(t)=\frac{1}{\Gamma(\beta)}\sum_{s=0}^{n}\frac{\Gamma(n-s+\beta)}{\Gamma(n-s+1)}\,x(s),

where

,

, and

.

Definition 2.2

[
77
]

Let
\mu>0

m-1<\mu<m
, where
m\in\mathbb{N},m=\lceil\mu\rceil.

The
\mu

\Delta^{\mu}x(t)=\Delta^{-(m-\mu)}(\Delta^{m}x(t)),

where

and

\Delta^{m}x(t)=\sum_{k=0}^{m}\left(\begin{array}[]{c}m\\
k\end{array}\right)(-1)^{m-k}x(t+k).

Definition 2.3

[
41
,
77
]

The Z-transform of a sequence
\{y(n)\}_{n=0}^{\infty}
is a complex function given by

Y(z)=Z[y](z)=\sum_{k=0}^{\infty}y(k)z^{-k},

where
z
is a complex number for which the series converges absolutely.

Property 2.1

[
41
,
77
]

The convolution
\phi\ast x
of the functions
\phi
and
x
defined on
\mathbb{N}_{0}
is defined as

(\phi\ast x)(n)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\phi(n-s)x(s)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\phi(s)x(n-s).

Then, the Z-transform of this convolution is
Z(\phi\ast x)(n)=(Z[\phi(n)])\,(Z[x(n)])

\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta-1)=f(x(t+\alpha-2))-x(t+\alpha-2),

(1)

This representation can be used to solve IVP (

\displaystyle\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{2-\alpha}[(z^{2}-2z+1)X(z)-(z^{2}+2z)x(0)-zx(1)]

General Case (Higher Order):

Consider the IVP

\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)=(c-1)x(t+\alpha-N),

(12)

x(i)=x_{i},\quad 0\leq i\leq N-1,

where
c\in\mathbb{C}
,
t\in\mathbb{N}_{N-\alpha}
,
N-1<\alpha\leq N
and
N\in\mathbb{N}
.

By the definition of the Caputo difference operator, Eq. (
12
) can be written as

\left(\Delta^{-(N-\alpha)}(\Delta^{N}x)\right)(t)=(c-1)x(t+\alpha-N),

(13)

where
t=N-\alpha+n,n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}
.

From the definition of a fractional sum, we have

\left(\Delta^{-(N-\alpha)}y\right)(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\binom{n-s+N-\alpha-1}{n-s}y(s).

(14)

Let
\tilde{\phi}_{\mu}(n)=\begin{cases}\binom{n+\mu-1}{n}&\text{if }n\in\mathbb{N}_{0}\\
0&\text{if }n<0\end{cases}

be the family of binomial functions defined on
\mathbb{Z}
, parameterized by
\mu\in\mathbb{R}
.

So, the expression (
14
) can be written as

\left(\Delta^{-(N-\alpha)}y\right)(t)=\sum_{s=0}^{n}\tilde{\phi}_{N-\alpha}(n-s)y(s).

(15)

Therefore, the eq. (
13
) can be written as

\sum_{s=0}^{n}\tilde{\phi}_{N-\alpha}(n-s)\Delta^{N}x(s)=(c-1)x(n),

(16)

where
n=t+\alpha-N
.

We can express eq (
16
) in the form of convolution of
\tilde{\phi}_{N-\alpha}
and
\Delta^{N}x
as

(\tilde{\phi}_{N-\alpha}*\Delta^{N}x)(n)=(c-1)x(n),

(17)

where
*
is a convolution operator.

We have
[
77
]
,

\mathcal{Z}[\tilde{\phi}_{\alpha}](z)=\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{\alpha},

(18)

and
[
41
]
,

\mathcal{Z}[\Delta^{k}x(n)]=(z-1)^{k}X(z)-z\sum_{j=0}^{k-1}(z-1)^{k-j-1}\Delta^{j}x(0).

(19)

Applying the Z-transform to the eq. (
17
), and using the formulas (
18
) & (
19
), we get

\left(\frac{z}{z-1}\right)^{N-\alpha}\left((z-1)^{N}X(z)-\text{terms involving initial conditions}\right)=(c-1)X(z),

(20)

where
X(z)
is z-transform of
x
.

The Characteristic equation of the system (
12
) can be obtained by equating the coefficients of the term
X(z)
in eq. (
20
) to zero as

{z^{N}(1-z^{-1})^{\alpha}+1=c}.

(21)

Substituting
z=e^{i\theta}
in (
21
) for the boundary of the stable region. Simplifying and then separating the real and imaginary parts of the expression
c
.

We get,

\displaystyle\Gamma(\theta)=\Bigl(2^{\alpha}\bigl(\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\bigr)^{\alpha}\Bigl(\cos\Bigl(\tfrac{\alpha\pi}{2}+\theta\bigl(N-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)\Bigr)+1\Bigr),\;2^{\alpha}\bigl(\sin\tfrac{\theta}{2}\bigr)^{\alpha}\sin\Bigl(\tfrac{\alpha\pi}{2}+\theta\bigl(N-\tfrac{\alpha}{2}\bigr)\Bigr)\Bigr).

(22)

This is the parametric form of the boundary curve of the system (
12
).

5
Conclusion

We considered the fractional order difference equation
\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)+a\,\Delta^{\beta}x(t+\alpha-\beta-1)=(b-1)x(t+\alpha-2)
with two difference operators
0<\beta<1
and
1<\alpha\leq 2
. The
Z
-transform is used to find the characteristic equation. The boundary of the stable region obtained by substituting
z=e^{i\theta}
in the characteristic equation. It is observed that the boundary curve is complex as compared with that in the difference equation with only one fractional order. Various bifurcation behaviors are discussed in detail. We found that the stable regions disappear after hitting the second bifurcation value
a_{2}^{*}
, and shrink as the parameter
a>0
grows. Applying this theory to the nonlinear system, we found that the trivial equilibrium satisfies all the local stability results. However, the nontrivial equilibrium satisfies the local stability results only for the smaller values of the parameter
a
. For the equation
\Delta^{\alpha}x(t)=(c-1)x(t+\alpha-N)
,
N-1<\alpha\leq N
, the system doesn’t show any stable behavior for
N\geq 3
, for any
c\in\mathbb{C}
. Numerical experiments are done to support the theoretical results.
