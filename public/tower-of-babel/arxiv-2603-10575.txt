1.
Introduction

The study of linear dynamics, especially in the last decades, has undergone great development and many connections with other areas of mathematics have been established, such as ergodic theory, number theory and geometry of Banach spaces
[
2
,
6
]
. For instance, studies have been developed on the concepts of chaos, chain recurrence, structural stability, and the shadowing property, in the series of papers
[
3
,
Bernardes_Alfred_Shadowing2024
,
Bernardes-Cirilo
,
Nilson_Bonilla_Muller_Peris_LiYorkechaos
,
Favaro
]
.

A fundamental notion on dynamical systems is the
shadowing property
, which comes from the works Sinaĭ
[
Sinai
]
and Bowen
[
Bowen
]
. The shadowing property means that near a pseudo-orbit there exists an exact orbit. In the context of linear dynamics, it is well known that every invertible
hyperbolic
continuous linear map has the shadowing property
[
Ombach
,
Morimoto
]
. Another important idea in dynamical systems is the concept of
expansivity
, which was introduced by Utz
[
Utz
]
, and studied on Banach spaces in
[
3
,
Bernardes-Cirilo
]
. Several authors have become interested in these topics, either by looking for examples of maps that are hyperbolic, expansive or possess the shadowing property; or by establishing general criteria that allow one to determine when a map has such properties. For instance, complete characterizations of expansive and uniformly expansive weighted shifts on classical Banach sequence spaces were obtained in
[
Bernardes-Cirilo
]
, while the characterization those that satisfy the shadowing property was obtained in
[
3
]
. Recently, Bernardes and Messaoudi established the following relation between these three concepts.
Let
T
be a invertible continuous linear map on a Banach space, then
T
is hyperbolic if and only if
T
is expansive and has the shadowing property

[
3
, Theorem 1]
. In the same work, and allowing non invertible operators, they established the following.
Let
T
be a continuous linear map on a Banach space, then
T
is hyperbolic with spectrum contained in
\mathbb{C}\backslash\overline{\mathbb{D}}
if and only
T
is positively expansive and has the positive shadowing property

[
3
, Proposition 5]
.

Inspired by this results, it is natural to ask which is the situation for more concrete operators; this is where composition operators arise. Let
X
be a space of complex-valued functions on the set
\Omega
and
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega
be a function. The
composition operator

C_{\phi}
with
symbol

\phi
is defined by
C_{\phi}f=f\circ\phi,
for any
f\in X.
This type of operators have been studied on a variety of spaces
[
Cowen
,
5
]
, and as is well known, there are several non-trivial questions in the study of composition operators, such as: boundedness, cyclicity, compactness, hypercyclicity, spectra, spectral radius and more recently notions as expansivity and shadowing property. For example, if
X
is the
L^{p}
space, then recent contributions on linear dynamics can be found in
[
Bernardes-Pires
,
Martina_Darji_Daniello_shadowingandghinL^p
,
Darji_Pires_Benito_compo
]
.

Phenomena concerning the orbit behavior of bounded linear fractional composition operators (that is, when its inducing symbol is a linear fractional transformation) has been studied on several holomorphic function spaces, with special interest on the Hardy spaces of the open unit disk
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and of the open right half-plane
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}),
and on the weighted Dirichlet space
\mathcal{S}_{\nu}.
In this context: Bourdon and Shapiro
[
4
]
completely characterized those that are cyclic and hypercyclic
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),
Gallardo-Gutiérrez and Montes-Rodríguez
[
Eva-Afonso
]
completely characterized those that are cyclic and hypercyclic on each
\mathcal{S}_{\nu},
Noor and Severiano
[
Noor-Severiano
]
completely characterized those that are cyclic and hypercyclic on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).

Recently, the (positive) shadowing property for linear factional composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
was studied in
[
1
]
. One of the advantages of the half-plane setting is that some composition operators are hyperbolic (in the linear dynamics sense), expansive or normal. These three concepts provides nice ways to approach the shadowing property using a general theory. The situation is more complicated on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
since all composition operators fix the reproducing kernel function
k_{0}
, so neither hyperbolicity nor expansivity occurs for these operators. This suggests that a new approach is necessary.

The goal of this paper is to add the positive shadowing property to the list of properties which are completely understood for linear fractional composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).
This work can be seen as a contribution to the composition operator program (see the introduction of
[
4
]
).

The paper is organized as follows. In Section

2.
notations and background

Throughout the paper,
\mathbb{N}
and
\mathbb{C}
denote the set of positive integers and the set of complex numbers, respectively. Moreover,
\mathbb{T}
denotes the unit circle
\{z\in\mathbb{C}:|z|=1\}.
Let
\phi:\Omega\to\Omega
be a function, the
n
-th iterate of
\phi
is denoted by
\phi^{[n]}
(where
n\in\mathbb{N}
). For convenience, we write
\phi^{[0]}
for the identity function. If
\Omega
is an open subset of
\mathbb{C},
we denote the set of all complex-valued holomorphic functions on
\Omega
by
\mathrm{Hol}(\Omega).

Let
(X,\|\cdot\|_{X})
be a complex Banach space and
T:X\to X
be a linear continuous map. We will call
T
an operator (instead of bounded operator) and denote by
\mathcal{B}(X)
the set of all operators on
X.
Let
T\in\mathcal{B}(X).
We write
T^{n}
instead of
T^{[n]}.
The norm of
T
is
\|T\|=\sup\{\|Tx\|_{X}:x\in X\ \text{and}\ \|x\|_{X}\leq 1\}
(when the context is clear, we write
\|x\|
instead of
\|x\|_{X}
). The
spectral radius
of
T,
denoted by
r(T),
is determined by the spectral radius formula
r(T)=\lim\|T^{n}\|^{1/n}.
The
spectrum
and the
right spectrum
of
T
are given by
\sigma(T)=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:T-\lambda I\ \text{is not invertible}\}
and
\sigma_{r}(T):=\{\lambda\in\mathbb{C}:T-\lambda I\ \text{is not right invertible}\},
respectively. We say that
T_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{1})
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{2})
are similar if there exists an invertible operator
U:X_{1}\rightarrow X_{2}
such that
T_{1}=U^{-1}T_{2}U.

2.1.
Shadowing in linear dynamics

Our goal in this subsection is to establish the definitions of positive shadowing property and generalized hyperbolicity. We also present some known facts about these topics.

Definition 2.1
.

Let
\delta>0.
By a
\delta
-
pseudo-orbit
of an operator
T\in\mathcal{B}(X),
we mean a sequence
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
in
X
such that

\displaystyle\|Tx_{n}-x_{n+1}\|\leq\delta,\quad\ \text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

For
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
and
\delta>0,
we can construct a natural
\delta
-pseudo-orbit from a non-zero vector
x\in X,
as follows. Define
x_{1}=\delta\|x\|^{-1}x
and

\displaystyle x_{n}=\delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}T^{j}x,\quad n\geq 2.

Then, for each
n\in\mathbb{N},
we have
Tx_{n}=x_{n+1}-x_{1}
and hence
\|Tx_{n}-x_{n+1}\|=\|x_{1}\|=\delta.
This construction will be used frequently in the next section.

Definition 2.2
.

We say that
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
has the
positive shadowing
property if for every
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
of
T
is
\varepsilon
-shadowed by a real orbit of
T,
that is, there exists
x\in X
such that

\displaystyle\|T^{n}x-x_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon,\quad\ \text{for all}\ n\in\mathbb{N}.

As usual, for an invertible operator, we define the notion of
shadowing
simply
by replacing the set
\mathbb{N}
by the set
\mathbb{Z}
in the definition of positive shadowing. In
[
Bernardes_Alfred_Shadowing2024
, Theorem 1]
, Bernardes and Peris showed that these notions coincide for invertible operators on Banach spaces.

The following result is folklore; we include a proof for the sake of completeness.

Proposition 2.3
.

If
T_{1}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{1})
and
T_{2}\in\mathcal{B}(X_{2})
are similar, then
T_{1}
has positive shadowing property if and only if so does
T_{2}.

Proof.

Let
T_{1}=U^{-1}T_{2}U
for some invertible bounded operator
U:X_{1}\to X_{2}.
Suppose that
T_{1}
has the positive shadowing property. Then given
\varepsilon>0
there exists
\delta>0
such that every
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
T_{1}
is
\varepsilon/\|U\|
-shadowed by a real orbit of
T_{1}.
Let
(y_{n})_{n\geq 1}
be an arbitrary
\delta/\|U^{-1}\|
-pseudo-orbit of
T_{2}
and define
x_{n}=U^{-1}y_{n}
for
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Hence

\displaystyle\|T_{1}x_{n}-x_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle=\|T_{1}U^{-1}y_{n}-U^{-1}y_{n+1}\|=\|U^{-1}T_{2}y_{n}-U^{-1}y_{n+1}\|

\displaystyle\leq\|U^{-1}\|\|T_{2}y_{n}-y_{n+1}\|\leq\delta,

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Therefore,
(x_{n})_{n\geq 1}
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
T_{1}.
By the choice of
\delta,
there exists
x\in X_{1}
such that
\|T_{1}^{n}x-x_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon/\|U\|
for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Let
y=Ux,
then

\displaystyle\|T_{2}^{n}y-y_{n}\|=\|T_{2}^{n}Ux-y_{n}\|=\|UT_{1}^{n}x-Ux_{n}\|\leq\|U\|\|T_{1}^{n}x-x_{n}\|\leq\varepsilon

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
The proof of the other implication is similar.
∎

Definition 2.4
.

We say that
T\in\mathcal{B}(X)
is generalized hyperbolic if there exists a direct sum decomposition
X=M\oplus N
where
M
and
N
are closed subspaces of
X
with the following properties

(1)

T(M)\subset M
and
r(T_{|M})<1;

(2)

T|_{N}:N\to T(N)
is bijective,
T(N)
is closed,
N\subset T(N)
and
r((T{{}_{|N}})^{-1})_{|N})<1.

Rewriting
[
3
, Proposition 19]
in terms of the last definition, we have that each generalized hyperbolic operator has the positive shadowing property. We refer to
[
3
,
Bernardes_Alfred_Shadowing2024
,
Bernardes-Cirilo
]
and
[
Cirilo-Gollobit
]
for more details on shadowing in the context of linear dynamics and on generalized hyperbolic operators, respectively.

2.2.
Linear fractional composition operators and canonical forms

The Hardy space of the open unit disk, denoted by
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),
consists of all functions
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
such that

\displaystyle\|f\|_{2}:=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{2}d\theta\right)^{1/2}<\infty.

It is well known that
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a Hilbert space when endowed with the inner product

\displaystyle\langle f,g\rangle=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\widehat{f}(n)\overline{\widehat{g}(n)}

where
(\widehat{f}(n))_{n\geq 0}
and
(\widehat{g}(n))_{n\geq 0}
are the Maclaurin coefficients for
f
and
g,
respectively. Moreover,
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space of holomorphic functions. Indeed, if
w\in\mathbb{D}
and
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),
then
f(w)=\langle f,k_{w}\rangle
where the
reproducing kernel function

k_{w}
is given by

\displaystyle k_{w}(z)=\frac{1}{1-\overline{w}z}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\overline{w}^{n}z^{n},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

A simple computation gives
\|k_{w}\|_{2}=(1-|w|^{2})^{-1/2}
for each
w\in\mathbb{D}.
For each
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}),
the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality gives the following pointwise growth

\|f\|_{2}\|k_{w}\|_{2}\geq|f(w)|.

If
\phi
is a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D},
then
C_{\phi}
is bounded on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
due to the Littlewood subordination theorem. The
n
-th iterate of
C_{\phi}
is given by
C_{\phi}^{n}=C_{\phi^{[n]}}.
Moreover, since
k_{0}
is a fixed point for
C_{\phi},
it follows that
C_{\phi}
is neither hyperbolic nor positively expansive. Therefore, results as
[
3
, Theorem 1 and Proposition 5]
, which relate hyperbolicity, (positive) expansivity and (positive) shadowing, cannot be used directly to characterize composition operators that have the shadowing on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
.

Recall that a
linear fractional self-map
of
\Omega\subset\mathbb{C}
is a symbol of the form

(1)

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{az+b}{cz+d},

whose coefficients
a,b,c,d\in\mathbb{C}
satisfy
ad-bc\neq 0
and the condition
\phi(\Omega)\subset\Omega
holds. If
\Omega=\mathbb{D},
we denote the set of such symbols by
\mathrm{LFT}(\mathbb{D}),
and if
\phi\in\mathrm{LFT}(\mathbb{D}),
we call
C_{\phi}
a linear fractional composition operator. Every
\phi\in\mathrm{LFT}(\mathbb{D})
, which is not the identity function, has either one or two fixed points, and according to the location of the fixed points of
\phi,
we classified it as follows.
Parabolic
if
\phi
has just one fixed point, which must lie in
\mathbb{T}.
In this case, we distinguish between two different situations for
\phi
, parabolic automorphism (
PA
) and parabolic non-automorphism (
PNA
). The other possible situations is when
\phi
has two fixed points, one of which must be in
\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
If it lies on
\mathbb{T},
we say that
\phi
is
hyperbolic
. In this case, we distinguish between three different situations. If the other fixed point of
\phi
lies in
\mathbb{T},
we call
\phi
a
hyperbolic automorphism
(
HA
). If the other fixed point of
\phi
lies outside of
\overline{\mathbb{D}}
(with the convention that
\infty
can be the other fixed point), we call
\phi
a
hyperbolic non-automorphism of type
I (
HNA I
).
Otherwise, we call
\phi
a
hyperbolic non-automorphism of type
II (
HNA II
). Finally, if
\phi
has no fixed point in
\mathbb{T}
then
\phi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
and another fixed outside
\overline{\mathbb{D}}.
We distinguish between two different situations: if
\phi
is an automorphism,
then
\phi
is said to be
elliptic
(
EA
). Otherwise,
\phi
is said to be
loxodromic
(
LOX
). We refer to
[
4
,
Shapiro_livro_compopandfunctiontheory
]
for more details on this classification.

It is well-known that each linear fractional composition operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
is similar to a composition operator in its
canonical form

[
4
]
. For instance, Bourdon and Shapiro
[
4
, page 28]
showed that if
\phi
is a parabolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
then
C_{\phi}
is similar to some
C_{\varphi},
where

\displaystyle\varphi(z)=\frac{(2-a)z+a}{-az+(2+a)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

with
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0.
On the other hand, if
\phi
is a hyperbolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
of type I, then they show that
C_{\phi}
is similar to some
C_{\psi},
where

\displaystyle\psi(z)=az+1-a,\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

with
a\in(0,1).

By Proposition
2.3
is sufficient to study the canonical forms of the linear fractional self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
to completely characterize the linear fractional composition operators on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
that have the positive shadowing property.

Remark 2.5
.

Note that the term hyperbolic is used in distinct contexts here. There is a notion of a (generalized) hyperbolic operator and the notion of a hyperbolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
introduced above. We caution the reader not to confuse these definitions.

We finish this subsection by enunciating the main result of this paper.

Theorem 2.6
.

Let
C_{\phi}
be a linear fractional composition operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).
Then
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property if and only if
\phi
is a hyperbolic automorphism or a hyperbolic non-automorphism of type I.

3.
Shadowing property on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})

In this section we present the proof of Theorem
2.6
. We do it by studying four cases separately. In the hyperbolic non-automorphism of type I and parabolic cases, we reduce our study to its canonical forms, respectively.

3.1.
EA, HNA II and LOX cases

The following general result suffices to show that linear fractional composition operators induced by elliptic, hyperbolic non-automorphism of type II and loxodromic self-maps of
\mathbb{D}
do not have the positive shadowing property.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
\phi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
If
\phi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

Suppose that
\alpha\in\mathbb{D}
is the fixed point of
\phi.
We construct a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
C_{\phi}
which cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed by any element of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
\varepsilon>0
in the following way: given
\delta>0,
we choose
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
with
f(\alpha)\neq 0
and we consider the natural
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
C_{\phi}
constructed from
h=\frac{f}{\|f\|_{2}}
, i.e,
f_{1}=\delta h
and

\displaystyle f_{n}=\delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}C_{\phi}^{j}h,\quad\text{for}\ n\geq 2.

Since
h(\phi^{[j]}(\alpha))=f(\alpha)/\|f\|_{2}
for each
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},
it follows that
f_{1}(\alpha)=\delta f(\alpha)/\|f\|_{2}
and

\displaystyle f_{n}(\alpha)=\left(\delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}C_{\phi}^{j}h\right)(\alpha)=\frac{\delta f(\alpha)}{\|f\|_{2}}n,\quad\text{for}\ n\geq 2.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\|k_{\alpha}\|_{2}

\displaystyle\geq|g(\alpha)-f_{n}(\alpha)|=\left|\frac{\delta f(\alpha)}{\|f\|_{2}}n-g(\alpha)\right|

\displaystyle\geq\frac{\delta|f(\alpha)|}{\|f\|_{2}}n-|g(\alpha)|

which implies

(2)

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\geq(1-|\alpha|^{2})^{1/2}\left[\frac{\delta|f(\alpha)|}{\|f\|_{2}}n-|g(\alpha)|\right]

for every
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).
Taking
n\to\infty
in (
2
), we see that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\to\infty.
This shows that
(f_{n})
cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed for any
\varepsilon>0.

∎

Corollary 3.2
.

Let
\phi
be a elliptic, hyperbolic non-automorphism of type II or a loxodromic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
Then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

3.2.
PA and PNA cases

In this subsection, it is enough to consider the case that
\phi
has the form

(3)

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{(2-a)z+a}{-az+(2+a)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0,
since each composition operator on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
induced by a parabolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}
is similar to a composition operator induced by a symbol as in (
3
) .

We can inductively show that the iterates of (
3
) are given by

(4)

\displaystyle\phi^{[n]}(z)=\frac{(2-na)z+na}{-naz+(2+na)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D}.

Before proceeding further, we need an auxiliary result.

Lemma 3.3
.

For any
s\in(0,1)
and
a\in\mathbb{C}
with
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0
there exists a constant
c>0
depending on
s
and
a
such that

(5)

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}(2+ja)^{s}\right|\geq cn^{s+1}

for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
.

Proof.

Given
j\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
and
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0
there is
\theta_{j}\in(-\frac{\pi}{2},\frac{\pi}{2})
such that
2+ja=|2+ja|e^{i\theta_{j}}.
In particular, for
s\in(0,1)
we have
(2+ja)^{s}=|2+ja|^{s}e^{is\theta_{j}}
and
s\theta_{j}\in(-\frac{s\pi}{2},\frac{s\pi}{2}).
Since
\cos(\cdot)
is a non-increasing function in
(0,\frac{\pi}{2}]
and
s\theta_{j}<\frac{s\pi}{2}<\frac{\pi}{2},
it follows that
\cos(s\theta_{j})\geq\cos(\frac{s\pi}{2}):=c_{1}>0.
This allows us to obtain the following inequality

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}(2+ja)^{s}\right|

\displaystyle\geq\left|\mathrm{Re}(\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}(2+ja)^{s})\right|=\left|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}\mathrm{Re}((2+ja)^{s})\right|

\displaystyle=\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}|2+ja|^{s}\cos(s\theta_{j})\geq\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}|ja|^{s}\cos(s\theta_{j})

(6)

\displaystyle\geq c_{1}|a|^{s}\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}j^{s}.

On the other hand, since
j^{s}\geq\displaystyle\int_{j-1}^{j}x^{s}dx,
it follows that

(7)

\displaystyle\sum_{j=1}^{n}j^{s}\geq\sum_{j=1}^{n}\left(\int_{j-1}^{j}x^{s}dx\right)=\int_{0}^{n}x^{s}dx=\frac{n^{s+1}}{s+1}.

Therefore by (
3.2
) and (
7
), we obtain the estimate

\displaystyle\left|\sum\limits_{j=0}^{n}(2+ja)^{s}\right|\geq\frac{c_{1}|a|^{s}}{s+1}n^{s+1},

which completes the proof.
∎

We are now ready for the main result of this subsection.

Theorem 3.4
.

Let
\phi
be a parabolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
Then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

Suppose that
\phi
has the form

\displaystyle\phi(z)=\frac{(2-a)z+a}{-az+(2+a)},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

where
\mathrm{Re}(a)\geq 0.
We will construct a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
C_{\phi}
which cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed by any element of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and
\varepsilon>0
. Fixed
s\in(0,\frac{1}{2}),
the function
f(z)=(1-z)^{-s}
is in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
(see
[
7
, Example 1.1.14]
). Given
\delta>0,
we consider the natural
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
C_{\phi}
constructed from
h:=f/\|f\|_{2}
, i.e,
f_{1}=\delta h
and

\displaystyle f_{n}=\delta\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}C_{\phi}^{j}h,\quad\text{for}\ n\geq 2.

By using (
4
), we can compute
f_{n}(0)
precisely. Indeed,
h(0)=1/\|f\|_{2}
and for
j\in\mathbb{N},
we obtain

\displaystyle h(\phi^{[j]}(0))=\frac{1}{2^{s}\|f\|_{2}}(2+ja)^{s},

which gives
f_{1}(0)=\delta/\|f\|_{2}
and

\displaystyle f_{n}(0)

\displaystyle=\delta\sum_{j=1}^{n-1}h(\phi^{[j]}(0))+\delta h(0)=\frac{\delta}{2^{s}\|f\|_{2}}\sum_{j=0}^{n-1}(2+ja)^{s}\quad\text{for}\ n\geq 2.

By Cauchy-Schwarz inequality, we get

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\|k_{0}\|_{2}+\|g\|_{2}\|k_{\phi^{[n]}(0)}\|_{2}\geq|g(\phi^{[n]}(0))-f_{n}(0)|+|g(\phi^{[n]}(0))|\geq|f_{n}(0)|.

Thus,

\displaystyle 2\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\geq|f_{n}(0)|(1-|\phi^{[n]}(0)|^{2})^{1/2}-\|g\|_{2}

holds for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
By Lemma
3.3
and the following inequality

\displaystyle 1-|\phi^{[n]}(0)|^{2}

\displaystyle=1-\frac{n^{2}|a|^{2}}{(2+n\mathrm{Re}(a))^{2}+(n\mathrm{Im}(a))^{2}}=\frac{4+4n\mathrm{Re}(a)}{4+4n\mathrm{Re}(a)+n^{2}|a|^{2}}

\displaystyle\geq\frac{4}{n^{2}(2+|a|)^{2}}\quad(n\in\mathbb{N})

we conclude that there exists a constant
c>0
(depending on
s
and
a
) such that

(8)

\displaystyle\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\geq\frac{\delta c}{2^{s}\|f\|_{2}(2+|a|)}\frac{(n-1)^{s+1}}{n}-\frac{\|g\|_{2}}{2},

for every
g\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
and all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Taking
n\to\infty
in (
8
), we see that
\|C_{\phi}^{n}g-f_{n}\|_{2}\to\infty.
This shows that
(f_{n})_{n\geq 1}
cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed for any
\varepsilon>0.

∎

HA case

Pituk

\sigma_{r}(T)\cap\mathbb{T}=\emptyset.

Recall that if
\phi

then
\phi

).

Proposition 3.5
.

Let
\phi
be a hyperbolic automorphism of
\mathbb{D}.
Then
C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

Since each
\lambda\in\mathbb{T}
belongs to the interior of

[
MR899654

In this subsection, it is enough to consider

(9)

C_{\phi}
has the positive shadowing property. We introduce now some auxiliary operators and Hilbert spaces, which play a key role in our study.

Let
\mathbb{C}_{+}
and

\displaystyle\|f\|_{H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})}:=\left(\sup_{0<x<\infty}\int_{-\infty}^{\infty}|f(x+iy)|^{2}\right)^{1/2}

\displaystyle\psi(w)=aw+b,\quad w\in\mathbb{C}_{+},

where
a>0
and
\mathrm{Re}(b)\geq 0.
In this case, the norm of
C_{\psi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is given by
\|C_{\psi}\|=a^{-1/2}.
Henceforth, for a fixed
a>0,
let us denote by
\psi_{a}
the self-map of
\mathbb{C}_{+}
given by
\psi_{a}(w)=a^{-1}w+(a^{-1}-1).

Recall that
L^{2}:=L^{2}(\mathbb{R}_{+},dt)
is the Hilbert space of all square integrable measurable complex-valued functions on
\mathbb{R}_{+}.
By the
Paley-Wiener Theorem
the transformation defined by

\displaystyle(PF)(w)=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}F(t)e^{-tw}dt

is an invertible operator of
L^{2}
onto
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+}).
In
[
Carmo-Noor
, Lemma 9]
, Carmo and Noor showed that if
\psi
is as (
10
), then
a^{-1}C_{\psi}:H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})\to H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is similar, via the operator
P,
to the operator
W:L^{2}\to L^{2}
given by
(WF)(t)=e^{-b/t}F(t/a).
In particular, this shows us that if
a>0,
then
a^{-1}C_{\psi_{a}}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is similar to the operator
W_{a}:L^{2}\to L^{2}
defined by

\displaystyle(W_{a}F)(t)=e^{-t(1-a)}F(at).

On the other hand, it is known that each bounded composition operator
C_{\psi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{C}_{+})
is similar, via an invertible operator
\mathcal{U},
to the
weighted composition operator

W_{\Phi}
on
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
defined by

(11)

\displaystyle(W_{\Phi}f)(z)=\frac{1-\Phi(z)}{1-z}f(\Phi(z))

where
\Phi:=\gamma^{-1}\circ\psi\circ\gamma
and
\gamma
is the
Cayley transform
from
\mathbb{D}
onto
\mathbb{C}_{+}
defined by
\gamma(z)=\frac{1+z}{1-z}.
Let
\phi

9

and
\frac{1-\Phi(z)}{1-z}f(\Phi(z))=a.
Therefore, we conclude that
C_{\psi_{a}}
is similar to
aC_{\phi}
or equivalently

similar to
C_{\phi}
(for instance, see
[
Carmo-Noor
, Lemma 14]
).

We have summarized these observations in the following commutative diagram

Theorem 3.6
.

If
a\in(0,1)
then
W_{a}
is a generalized hyperbolic operator.

Proof.

Observe that
L^{2}
is the direct sum of the following closed subspaces
M=\{f\in L^{2}:f\ \text{vanishes a.e on}\ (0,a)\}
and
N=\{f\in L^{2}:f\ \text{vanishes a.e on}\ [a,\infty)\}.
We will show that
M
and
N
satisfy the conditions of the definition of generalized hyperbolicity.

Claim 1
.

W_{a}(M)\subset M
and
r({W_{a}}_{|M})<1.

It is easy to see that
W_{a}(M)\subset M.
Inductively, we see that the
n
-th iterate of
W_{a}
is given by

\displaystyle(W_{a}^{n}F)(t)=e^{-t(1-a^{n})}F(a^{n}t),

and by the change of variable
s=a^{n}t
we obtain the following estimate

\displaystyle\|W_{a}^{n}F\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|(W_{a}^{n}F)(t)|^{2}dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|e^{-t(1-\frac{1}{a^{n}})}F(a^{n}t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{a^{n}}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{-2s(\frac{1}{a^{n}}-1)}|F(s)|^{2}ds=\frac{1}{a^{n}}\int_{a}^{\infty}e^{-2s(\frac{1}{a^{n}}-1)}|F(s)|^{2}ds

\displaystyle\leq\frac{e^{-2a(\frac{1}{a^{n}}-1)}}{a^{n}}\|F\|_{L^{2}}^{2}

for all
F\in M.
Hence

\displaystyle\|(W_{a})^{n}_{|M}\|^{\frac{1}{n}}\leq\frac{e^{-\frac{a}{n}(\frac{1}{a^{n}}-1)}}{a^{2}}

for all
n\in\mathbb{N}.
Since
a\in(0,1),
it follows that
\frac{a}{n}(\frac{1}{a^{n}}-1)=\frac{1-a^{n}}{na^{n-1}}\to\infty
as
n\to\infty.
Therefore,
r({W_{a}}_{|M})=0
.

Claim 2
.

{W_{a}}|_{N}:N\to W_{a}(N)
is a bijection,
W_{a}(N)
is closed,
N\subset W_{a}(N)
and
r((W_{a}{{}_{|N}})^{-1})_{|N})<1.

Since
W_{a}
is injective, it follows that
{W_{a}}_{|N}
is injective and hence
{W_{a}}|_{N}:N\to W_{a}(N)
is a bijection. Now suppose that
W_{a}F_{n}\to G
on
L^{2}
for some sequence
(F_{n})_{n\geq 1}\subseteq N
. Then some subsequence
(F_{n_{k}})_{k\geq 1}
satisfies
W_{a}F_{n_{k}}\to G
pointwise almost everywhere
\mathbb{R}_{+}.
This implies that
F_{n_{k}}\to H\cdot G
almost everywhere
\mathbb{R}_{+},
where
H(t)=e^{t(1-a)}.

On the other hand, since
H
has no zeros in
\mathbb{R}_{+}
and
F_{n_{k}}
vanishes almost everywhere on
[a,\infty),
we conclude that
G
vanishes almost everywhere on
[a,\infty).
This shows that
W_{a}(N)
is closed. For each
F\in N,
we define the function

\displaystyle(V_{a}F)(t)=e^{\frac{t}{a}(1-a)}F(t/a).

Then
V_{a}F
vanishes almost everywhere on
[a,\infty)
and by the change of variable
s=t/a
we get

\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|(V_{a}F)(t)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{\frac{2t}{a}(1-a)}|F(t/a)|^{2}dt=a\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{2s}|F(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=a\int_{0}^{a}e^{2s(1-a)}|F(s)|^{2}dt\leq ae^{2a(1-a)}\|F\|^{2}_{L^{2}}.

This shows us that
V_{a}
is a bounded operator on
N,
which satisfies
W_{a}(V_{a}F)=F
for all
F\in N.
In particular, we have
W_{a}(N)\subset N
and
({W_{a}}_{|N})^{-1}_{|N}=V_{a}.
Inductively, we obtain

\displaystyle(V_{a}^{n}F)(t)=e^{\frac{t}{a^{n}}(1-a^{n})}F(t/a^{n}).

We now estimate
\|V_{a}^{n}\|.
By using the change of variable
s=t/a^{n}
, we obtain

\displaystyle\|V_{a}^{n}F\|^{2}_{L^{2}}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}|(V_{a}^{n}F)(t)|^{2}dt=\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{\frac{2t}{a^{n}}(1-a^{n})}|F(t/a^{n})|^{2}dt

\displaystyle=a^{n}\int_{\mathbb{R}_{+}}e^{2s(1-a^{n})}|F(s)|^{2}dt=a^{n}\int_{0}^{a}e^{2s(1-a^{n})}|F(s)|^{2}dt

\displaystyle\leq a^{n}e^{2a(1-a^{n})}\|F\|_{L^{2}}

for all
F\in N,
which implies
\|V_{a}^{n}\|\leq a^{\frac{n}{2}}e^{a(1-a^{n})}.
Therefore,
r(V_{a})=\sqrt{a}<1.
This completes the proof.
∎

Corollary 3.7
.

If
a\in(0,1)
then
W_{a}
has the positive shadowing property.

This allows us to resolve the remaining case.

Corollary 3.8
.

Let
\phi
be a hyperbolic non-automorphism of
\mathbb{D}
of type I. Then

4.
Comments on the shadowing property on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D}),
for
1\leq p\leq\infty

In this section, we briefly study the shadowing phenomenon for composition operators on the Banach space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D}),
where
1\leq p\leq\infty.
First, let us recall that given
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D}),

\|f\|_{p}
is defined by

\displaystyle\|f\|_{p}:=\left(\sup_{0<r<1}\frac{1}{2\pi}\int_{0}^{2\pi}|f(re^{i\theta})|^{p}d\theta\right)^{1/p},\quad\text{for}\ 1\leq p<\infty

and
\|f\|_{\infty}=\sup\{|f(z)|:z\in\mathbb{D}\}.
For
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the Hardy space
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
is the Banach space of all functions
f\in\mathrm{Hol}(\mathbb{D})
for which the norm
\|f\|_{p}
is finite.

The following result shows that
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
does not support composition operators with the shadowing property.

Proposition 4.1
.

Let
\phi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
Then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).

Proof.

For
\delta>0
and
n\in\mathbb{N},
let
f_{n}
be the constant function
f_{n}(z)=n\delta/2,
for
z\in\mathbb{D}.
Then
(f_{n})
is a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit of
C_{\phi}.
By using the
H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D})
-norm, we obtain

\displaystyle\|f_{n}-C_{\phi}^{n}f\|_{\infty}\geq|f_{n}(z)-f(\phi^{[n]}(z))|\geq\frac{n\delta}{2}-|f(\phi^{[n]}(z))|\geq\frac{n\delta}{2}-\|f\|_{\infty}

for every
f\in H^{\infty}(\mathbb{D}).
This implies that
(f_{n})_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
cannot be
\varepsilon
-shadowed for any
\varepsilon>0
.
∎

When
1\leq p<\infty
, our arguments can be adapted for
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
except for hyperbolic automorphisms and hyperbolic non-automorphisms of type I.
The changes are essentially related with the absence of the inner product structure.
When
\phi
is
LOX
,
EP
or
HNA II
, the shadowing of
C_{\phi}
in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
was solved in Proposition
3.1
, which has an identical version for
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
(the next theorem). In the proof for
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
we consider an element
f\in H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
such that
f(\alpha)\neq 0
where
\alpha
is a fixed point of
\phi

and we construct a
\delta
-pseudo-orbit which can not be
\varepsilon
-shadowed by any
\varepsilon>0
. The same proof can be adapted by replacing the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality by the pointwise estimate

(12)

\displaystyle|f(z)|\leq\frac{\|f\|_{H^{p}(\mathbb{D})}}{(1-|z|^{2})^{\frac{1}{p}}},\quad z\in\mathbb{D},

and starting with a function
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
such that
f(\alpha)\neq 0
. This show how to construct many
\delta
-pseudo-orbits which can not be
\varepsilon
-shadowed for any
\varepsilon>0
. In particular, the existence of one function
f\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
satisfying
f(\alpha)\neq 0
(for example some constant function) is enough. The following theorem is a consequence of this discussion.

Theorem 4.2
.

Let
\phi
be a holomorphic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
If
\phi
has a fixed point in
\mathbb{D}
then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D}).

The parabolic case (
PA
or
PNA
) in
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
was solved using Lemma
3.3
and Theorem
3.4
. The lemma is a purely algebraic statement and does not involves any property of
H^{2}(\mathbb{D})
. About the theorem, we need to change the test functions a bit; we consider now
f_{s}
where

f_{s}(z)=(1-z)^{-s}

and
s
is a real number; for the new test function in the proof we choose any
f_{s}
where
0<s<\frac{1}{p}.
It is well known that
f_{s}\in H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
if, and only if,
s<\frac{1}{p}
. As in the case of fixed points, arguments using the Cauchy-Schwarz inequality are replaced by the pointwise estimate (
12
). Therefore, we obtain:

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\phi
be a parabolic self-map of
\mathbb{D}.
Then
C_{\phi}
does not have the positive shadowing property on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D}).

When
\phi
is
HA
or
HNA I
, our method is not adaptable. For
HA
we used a chain of results which are valid (at least for now) only for Hilbert spaces. For
HNA I
we used the map
\mathcal{U}
to move our problem to the Hardy space of the right half plane and also the Paley-Wiener theorem to move it to
L^{2}
. Therefore, if
p\neq 2
and
\phi
is
HA
or
HNA I
, to decide if the composition operator
C_{\phi}
on
H^{p}(\mathbb{D})
has the shadowing property remains an open problem.
