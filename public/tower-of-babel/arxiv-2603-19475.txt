Disordered Ground States of Ergodic Quantum Spin Systems
Eric B. Roon & Jeffrey H. Schenker Department of Mathematics Michigan State University East Lansing, MI., U.S.A

Abstract
In this letter, we fill a hole in the existing literature about disordered quantum spin systems generated by a random local interaction { 𝔥 ⁡ ( Z ) } Z ⋐ ℤ ν \{\mathfrak{h}(Z)\}_{Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}} satisfying a statistical version of translation invariance. We show such systems always have disordered ground states in the thermodynamic limit with the same symmetry. A key tool we use is a disordered version of the Lieb-Robinson bounds, which hold almost surely under mild conditions on 𝔥 \mathfrak{h} . Along the way, we formalize the notion of a random state on a C ∗ C^{*} -algebra and prove a weak- ∗ \ast version of the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem, which seems not to have been recorded in the vector measures literature. As a consequence of the existence of the aforementioned disordered ground states, we show that the spectrum of the GNS Hamiltonain associated to the bulk dynamics is deterministic with respect to the disorder.

\tau_{x}h^{\Lambda}(\omega)=h^{\Lambda+x}(\vartheta_{x}\omega),\,\quad\mathbb{P}-\text{a.e.}\,\omega\,,\text{ all }x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}.

(1.1)

Theorem A

(Theorem
4.6
Informal)
.

If
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
is equipped with a translation-invariant metric, and

is an ergodic interaction as in (

\psi_{\omega}
satisfying

\psi_{\omega}\circ\tau_{x}=\psi_{\vartheta_{x}\omega},\,\mathbb{P}-a.e.\,\omega,\qquad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\,.

(1.2)

We note that by bulk ground state, we mean that
\psi_{\omega}
is weakly-
\ast
measurable and satisfies the
C^{*}
-algebraic ground state condition
\omega
-point-wise (cf inequality (
4.7

[

,
57
]

\vartheta_{x}
acting on the probability space; and, two, the property (
1.1
). It will be clear from context which definition we mean.

A common example of an interaction which satisfies (
1.1
) is given by a nearest-neighbor Hamiltonian interaction
h_{j,j+1}
with a random transverse field

H^{[0,L]}=\sum_{j=0}^{L-1}h_{j,j+1}+\sum_{j=0}^{L}\lambda_{j}\sigma^{z}_{j}\,,

,

h_{j,j+1}=\mu_{j}((1+\gamma_{j})\sigma_{j}^{x}\sigma_{j+1}^{x}+(1-\gamma_{j})\sigma_{j}^{y}\sigma_{j+1}^{y})
(where
\mu,\gamma

[
1
]
. Random spin chains has been mathematically studied by various authors in the context of many-body-localization (see the nonexhaustive list
[

,

,

,

,

,

,

]

the topic of this letter. Rather, we are concerned with providing a mathematical framework that includes these kinds of models as well as the disordered deformation of the AKLT model we study in
[

]
.

One commonality between these seemingly disparate models is that the Hamiltonians here are known to have ergodic (in the disorder) ground states, but the methods used to prove such results are highly model-specific. The import of Theorem
A
is that such ergodicity of the disordered local interactions implies there are ergodic bulk ground state sectors.

The usual proof to show that there is a translation-invariant ground state is to examine the weak-
\ast
limit points of spatial averages of any ground state
[
10
]
. However in our case, the subsequence along which those limits exist may depend on the disorder, and may change after applying translation. Therefore it is necessary to argue from a functional analytic perspective. In particular, we need to assume that the probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is countably generated in order for the weak-
\ast
topology in an appropriate dual space to be sequentially compact in the unit ball. However, this assumption encompasses the so-called standard probability spaces, and models of IID and quasiperiodic disorder. A key tool is a kind of
C^{*}
-version of the Riesz-Markov-Kakutani theorem (see Proposition
A.10
below).

For our second main result, we show that a version of the Pastur-Kirsch-Martinelli theorem holds for the spectrum of the GNS Hamiltonian with respect to an ergodic ground state.

Theorem B

(Corollary
4.9
Informal)
.

Let
\{h^{\Lambda}_{\omega}\colon\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\}
be a local ergodic interaction with sufficient spatial decay. Suppose
\gamma_{\omega}
is a translation-covariant ground state, and let
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\Psi_{\omega})
be the associated GNS representations. Then the thermodynamic limit
(\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega})_{t\in\mathbb{R}}
passes to a strongly continuous unitary group on
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
generated by a self-adjoint operator
H_{\omega}
. Furthermore, there are unitaries
\{U_{x}\colon x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\}
so that

H_{\vartheta_{x}\omega}=U^{*}_{x}H_{\omega}U_{x}\,.

Whence, the spectrum of the GNS generator for
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}
in a covariant ground state is deterministic almost surely.

Interestingly, the condition of local ergodicity as in (
1.1
) appeared in the quantum spin system context as early as 1978 in the work of Pastur and Figotin
[
51
]
. Pastur
[
53
]
goes on to analyze thermodynamic limits of the classical random Ising model. Both Pastur and Figotin
[
51
]
and Pastur
[
53
]
show that certain quantities (e.g. the free energy) associated to a local ergodic interaction are deterministic in the thermodynamic limit. Something similar is true in our context for the Lieb-Robinson velocity (see Corollary
4.3
), namely that the so-called F-norm of an ergodic local Hamiltonian is deterministic. Thus when finite, this provides an essential upper bound to the Lieb-Robinson velocity.

2
Preliminaries

Below we set notation and state some important background information that the reader may find convenient. The goal of this section is to point out facts which may be already known by experts in the community, but whose statements will make this letter more understandable. We provide references and proofs when necessary.

2.1
Quantum Spin Systems

Our setting is the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
with dimension
\nu\in\mathbb{N}
. To each site
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
we associate a replica of the
k\times k
matrices
\mathcal{A}_{x}\cong\mathbb{M}_{k}
. By
\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})
we mean the set of finite subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. When it is convenient, we will write
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
to indicate
Z\in\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})
. Given
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
one forms the
local algebra (of observables)
by taking the tensor product
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}\mathcal{A}_{x}\,.
Whenever
\Lambda_{0}\subset\Lambda
are finite, there is a canonical
*
-homomorphism
\iota_{\Lambda_{0},\Lambda}:\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
given by taking tensor products with the identity:

\iota_{\Lambda_{0},\Lambda}(a_{\Lambda_{0}})=a_{\Lambda_{0}}\otimes\mathbbm{1}_{\Lambda\setminus\Lambda_{0}}\,,

and extending linearly. Importantly,
\iota_{\Lambda_{0},\Lambda}
is an isometry, thus one may identify
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}}
with the
C^{*}
-sub-algebra
\iota_{\Lambda_{0},\Lambda}(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{0}})
of
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
. In this way the family
\{\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}\colon\Lambda\in\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})\}
forms a net. One may further define the
quasi-local algebra (of observables)
as the norm completion of the union of the local algebras,

\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}:=\overline{\bigcup_{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}}^{\|\cdot\|}\,.

Note that there are then canonical
*
-homomorphism inclusions
\iota_{\Lambda}:\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
and so one may regard the local algebras once again as subalgebras of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
. For convenience we write
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
for the dense
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
corresponding to the union of the local algebras. Following the convention of
[
45
,
46
,
47
]
, we say that a sequence of finite volumes
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
is an
increasing and adsorbing sequence (IAS)
if
\Lambda_{n}\subset\Lambda_{n+1}
for all
n\geq 1
and
\bigcup\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. We summarize some key properties below.

Proposition 2.1
.

Suppose
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
is the quasilocal algebra associated to one-site algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\{x\}}\cong\mathbb{M}_{k}
. The following hold:

1.

Let
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be any IAS. Then,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
is an increasing union of the increasing sequence of algebras
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
.

2.

In particular,
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
is norm separable and contains a unit. Furthermore, there is a unique state
\rho\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
so that
\rho(ab)=\rho(ba)
for all
a,b\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
. We call
\rho
the
tracial state
.

3.

For any IAS
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exist
\rho
-invariant unital completely positive maps
\mathcal{E}_{n}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
so that for any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
, the limit
\|a-\mathcal{E}_{n}(a)\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}}\to 0
as
n\to\infty
.

The last item is a consequence of being able to take partial traces in finite volumes (cf.
[
44
,
45
]
). In the operator algebras literature, the third property is referred to as
nuclearity
, which holds for a much larger class of
C^{*}
-algebras than we consider here (see Proposition
A.6
below for our humble application of this more general concept).

Recall that
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
is canonically an abelian group under point-wise addition, and it acts on itself via translation isomorphisms:

\tau_{k}(x_{1},\dots,x_{\nu}):=(x_{1}+k_{1},\dots,x_{\nu}+k_{\nu})\,.

One clearly has
\tau_{k}\circ\tau_{j}=\tau_{k+j}
. These translations induce automorphisms of the quasilocal algebra by translating the indices of pure tensors via

\tau_{k}(a_{x_{1}}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{x_{n}}):=a_{x_{1}+k}\otimes\cdots\otimes a_{x_{n}+k}\,.

These
\tau_{k}
are norm-preserving and therefore extend to
\ast
-automorphisms of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
.

2.2
Random states on Quasilocal Algebras.

We will briefly recall two technical results which will be useful for addressing one of the central questions of this letter which is measurability of weak-
\ast
limits of states in the quasilocal algebra. For us, a
random state
is a function
\phi:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
which satisfies the following:

1.

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
, the numerical mapping
\omega\mapsto\phi_{\omega}(a)
is Borel measurable;

2.

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
the probability
\mathbb{P}[\phi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)\geq 0]=1
;

3.

one has
\phi_{\omega}(\mathbbm{1})=1
almost surely.

Recall that when
\mathcal{A}
is a separable
C^{*}
-algebra, the weak-
\ast
topology on the unit ball in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is metrizable
[
58
, Theorem 3.16]
and compact
[
14
, Theorem V.3.1]
. Since compact metric spaces are complete, this makes
(\mathcal{S}(A),w*)
a compact Polish space which is also sequentially compact. We emphasize that the unit ball of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
is not generally sequentially compact: this occurs because of the existence of a metric. The following lemma tells us how to lift sequential compactness to weak-
\ast
“disordered” sequential compactness in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Our first lemma allows us to guarantee that weak-
\ast
limits of random finite volume states are measurable which is a crucial ingredient in the proof that disordered ground states exist in Lemma
4.4
below.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
and let
\mathcal{A}
be a separable unital
C^{*}
algebra. Let
\mathscr{E}
be the closed unit ball of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
equipped with the weak-
\ast
topology, which we induce via a choice of metric
d
. If
(\Phi_{n}:\Omega\to\mathscr{E})_{n=1}^{\infty}
is a sequence of weak-
\ast
measurable random variables with the property that the norm function
\|\,\|\Phi_{n}(\omega)\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\leq 1
for all
n
. Then, there exists a sequence of
\mathbb{N}
-valued random variables
(\sigma_{n}:\Omega\to\mathbb{N})_{n=1}^{\infty}
which are finite almost surely, and a weakly-
\ast
-measurable function
\Psi:\Omega\to\mathscr{E}
so that
\lim_{n\to\infty}\Phi_{\sigma_{n}}=\Psi
almost surely.

Proof.

Consider the closed cover
\mathcal{U}_{1}:=\{b_{\psi}(2^{-1})\colon\psi\in\mathscr{E}\}
of
\mathscr{E}
by metric balls of radius
1/2
. By compactness, there is a finite sub-cover
\{b_{\psi_{1}}(2^{-1}),\dots,b_{\psi_{k}}(2^{-1})\}
of
\mathcal{U}
. Then, there is some
k_{1}(\omega)
which is finite almost surely so that
B_{\psi_{k_{1}(\omega)}}(2^{-1})
contains infinitely many members of the sequence
\Phi_{n}(\omega)
.

Let
\sigma_{1}(\omega)=\inf\{n\colon\Phi_{n}(\omega)\in B_{\psi_{k_{1}(\omega)}}(2^{-1})\}
. Now, consider the closed cover
\mathcal{U}_{2}=\{b_{\psi^{\prime}}(2^{-2})\}
of
b_{\psi_{k_{1}(\omega)}}(2^{-1})
by closed balls of radius
2^{-2}
. By the same argument, there is some
k_{2}(\omega)
so that infinitely many members of the sequence
\Phi_{n}(\omega)
belong to
b_{k_{2}(\omega)}(2^{-2})
when
n\geq\sigma_{n}(\omega)
. Let
\sigma_{2}(\omega):=\inf\{n>\sigma_{1}(\omega)\colon\varphi_{n}(\omega)\in b_{\psi_{2}^{\prime}(\omega)}(2^{-2})\}
.

Proceed inductively to produce a measurable sequence of hitting times
\{\sigma_{1},\sigma_{2},\dots\}
which is finite almost surely (once again by compactness). For each realization of the disorder, we have produced a sequence of nested closed sets in a compact metric space
b_{\psi_{k_{1}}(\omega)}(2^{-1})\supset b_{\psi_{k_{2}}^{\prime}(\omega)}(2^{-2})\supset\cdots
. By basic topology, this tells us that there is a single point,
\Phi(\omega)
in their common intersection and furthermore that
\lim_{k\to\infty}d(\Phi_{\sigma_{k}(\omega)}(\omega),\Phi(\omega))\leq\lim_{k\to\infty}2^{-k+1}=0
. Whence
\Phi(\omega)
is weakly-
\ast
-measurable.
∎

Turning to the next result, we remark that in order to prove that
ergodic
bulk ground states exist, we found it necessary to argue at the level of vector measures. This is because the weak-
\ast
limits of a shifted sequence of ground states depend on a random time
\sigma_{n}
which need not vary ergodically. Proposition
A.10
allows us to get around this in the proof of Theorem
A
below. We want to enlarge the the class of linear functionals we consider to encompass all linear functionals on the Bochner space
L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})
of measurable
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
-valued functions with integrable operator norm functions. Given such an
A:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
with
\int_{\Omega}\|A_{\omega}\|d\mathbb{P}<\infty
, one can define a linear functional
\Phi
by integrating the composition with
A
as follows.

\Phi(A):=\int_{\Omega}\phi_{\omega}(A_{\omega})d\mathbb{P}(\omega)\,.

This defines a vector measure into
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
which is absolutely continuous with respect to
d\mathbb{P}
. The following proposition allows us to find a measurable Radon-Nikodym derivative with respect to the weak-
\ast
topology in
L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})^{*}
. The proof is technical, so we relegate it to the Appendix, where we also have a slightly more general statement of the result.

Proposition 2.3
.

Suppose
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
is the quasi-local algebra. Suppose
\Phi
is a positive normalized linear functional on
L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})
. Then there is a weakly-
\ast
-measurable RND
G_{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
which is positive and essentially bounded so that

\Phi(A)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\omega}(A(\omega))d\mathbb{P}\,\quad\forall A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})\,.

(2.1)

We emphasize that this weak-
\ast
RND is not the same as a Bochner-measurable RND which turns out to rely on the properties of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
as a Banach space (cf. remarks
A.11
and
A.12
in the appendix below).

3
Quasilocality Estimates for Disordered Interactions

In this section we will discuss the set up for a quantum spin system on the integer lattice
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
equipped with the
\|\cdot\|_{\infty}
metric

d(x,y):=\max_{1\leq j\leq\nu}|x_{j}-y_{j}|\,.

We aim to model local interactions which are disordered but co-vary with respect to a group action and a family of ergodic maps on an underlying probability space.

The closed ball of radius
r\in\mathbb{N}
about a point
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
is the set
b_{x}(r)=\{y\colon d(x,y)\leq r\}
. A simple combinatorial argument shows that
|b_{0}(r)|=(2r+1)^{\nu}\leq 2^{\nu}(1+r)^{\nu}
. Since
d
is translation invariant and integer valued, we have calculated the size of any closed ball. The upper bound is the so-called
\nu
-regularity property. In fact,
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
is
\nu
-hyper-regular
in the sense that there exists a constant
\kappa>0
so that

\kappa:=\sup_{r>1}|b_{0}(r)\setminus b_{0}(r-1)|(1+r)^{-(\nu-1)}<\infty\,.

(3.1)

Recall that an
interaction
is a function from the finite volumes
\mathfrak{h}:\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
into the local algebra, so that
\mathfrak{h}(Z)=\mathfrak{h}(Z)^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}
for all finite subsets
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}

[
10
,
42
]
. We say a function
\mathfrak{h}:\Omega\times\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
is a
disordered interaction
whenever the following are satisfied:

i.)

For all
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, and almost every
\omega\in\Omega
,
\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)=\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)^{*}\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}
.

ii.)

The mapping
\omega\mapsto\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)
is
weakly locally measurable
in the sense that
\omega\mapsto\varphi(\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z))
is Borel measurable with respect to
\mathbb{C}
for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
.

Remark 3.1
.

Recall that Takeda’s Theorem
[
63
]
says that any state
\varphi
(hence any continuous linear functional) on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
is consistent with respect to volumetric inclusions and also uniquely determined by its finite dimensional restrictions. It is not difficult to show that in the case of a disordered interaction weakly local measurability is equivalent to requiring that for every
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, the numerical function
\omega\mapsto\varphi(\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z))
to be Borel measurable for every
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{Z}^{*}
.

In this setup, a
(local) disordered Hamiltonian
in a finite voluem
\Lambda
is the sum-function

\Omega\ni\omega\longmapsto H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}:=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}.

Observe that the operator norm may be expressed as a supremum over states

\|H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}\|=\sup_{\phi\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda})}|\phi(H^{\Lambda}_{\omega})|\,,

and the right hand side of the quantity is measurable since
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda}
is finite dimensional. Given a disordered Hamiltonian with
\mathbb{P}[\|H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}\|<\infty]=1
, one can measurably generate a one-parameter unitary group via
H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}
in the usual way. Indeed, let

U^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t)=e^{-itH^{\Lambda}_{\omega}}=\sum_{n=0}^{\infty}\frac{(-itH^{\Lambda}_{\omega})^{n}}{n!}\,,

which is clearly uniformly convergent for
t
in compacts
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
since
\|H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}\|
is finite almost surely, and hence
U^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t)
is measurable for all
t\in\mathbb{R}
. The Heisenberg dynamics associated to
H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}
are then given by

\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}(a)=U^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(-t)\,a\,U^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t)\,.

(3.2)

The resulting images are all weakly measurable since the product of measurable linear operators is still measurable
[
8
, Theorem 2.14]
.

Recall the
F
-function formalism introduced in
[
42
]
. Let
F:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
be a non-increasing function and suppose the following hold:

1.

The function
F
is uniformly summable over
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
in the sense that

\|F\|:=\sup_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}F(d(x,y))<\infty\,,

2.

There is a constant
C_{F}>0
so that for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, one has

\sum_{z\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}F(d(x,z))F(d(z,y))\leq C_{F}\,F(d(x,y))\,.

In this case
F
is called an
F
-function
. A standard set of examples of
F
-functions is given by the power-law decay
F(x)=(1+x)^{-\nu-(1+\epsilon)}
for a small
\epsilon>0
on a
\nu
-regular discrete metric space (see
[
42
]
). Given a disordered interaction
\mathfrak{h}
, one can define a
[0,\infty]
-valued random variable

\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)=\sup_{x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}:\\
Z\ni x,y\end{subarray}}\,\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\|F(d(x,y))^{-1}\,,

(3.3)

where we allow the possibility of infinite values for now (later we will restrict to the case that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}
is finite almost surely). Note that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}
is just the
F
-norm of
\mathfrak{h}
on at a given realization of the disorder
\omega
— if the interaction
h
is ergodic, as in (
1.1
) or (
4.1
) below, it follows that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}
is deterministic. Whenever
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)
is finite, equation (
3.3
) implies that for every finite
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, the norm-function
\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\|
is bounded:

\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\|\leq\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)\sum_{x,y\in Z}F(d(x,y))\ .

Thus the following rough estimate holds almost surely for any disordered interaction

\|H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}\|\leq\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda\colon\\
x,y\in Z\end{subarray}}F(d(x,y))=2^{|\Lambda|-2}\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)\sum_{x,y\in\Lambda}F(d(x,y))\,.

(3.4)

Proposition 3.2

(Lieb-Robinson Bound)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a disordered interaction with
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]=1
. Let
X,Y\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
be disjoint sets and let
a\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
and
b\in\mathcal{A}_{Y}
. Then, whenever
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
satisfies
X\cup Y\subset\Lambda
one has

\|[b,\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}(a)]\|\leq\frac{\|a\|\|b\|}{C_{F}}(e^{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}C_{F}|t|}-1)\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Y}F(d(x,y))\,,

(3.5)

on a set with probability one.

The proof of this result is a simple ‘insert-
\omega
’ corollary to the well-established proofs of quasilocality estimates in (e.g.)
[
42
, Theorem 1]
or
[
49
, Theorem 2]
, etc., so we will not repeat it here. For state-of the art estimates see Theorem 3.1 in
[
45
]
. We now turn to the existence of the thermodynamic limit. One arrives at the following corollary of the almost-sure Lieb-Robinson bound.

Corollary 3.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be a disordered interaction with
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty
almost surely. Let
H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}
be the disordered local Hamiltonian and let also
\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}
be the associated one-parameter group of
*
-automorphisms. Let
\Lambda_{n}
be an IAS. Then for all
\omega\in[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]
the following limit exists

\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})=:\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})\,,

(3.6)

for all local observables
a_{X}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
. The propagator dynamics
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t,\omega}
is weakly-
\ast
measurable in the sense that for all
\varphi\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
and all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, the function

\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\varphi(\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X}))\in\mathbb{C}\,,

is measurable. Moreover,
\alpha_{t;\omega}
is independent of the IAS
\{\Lambda_{n}\}_{n=1}^{\infty}
.

Proof.

Following the methods of
[
42
,
45
]
, we find that for any
a_{X}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, if
n>m\geq N
where
N\gg 0
is large enough so that
\Lambda_{N}\supset X
, the following estimate holds
\omega
-point-wise by Duhamel’s principle.

\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})-\alpha^{\Lambda_{m}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})\|\leq\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda_{n}\colon\\
Z\not\subset\Lambda_{m}\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{|t|}\|[\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z),\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{s;\omega}(a_{X})]\|ds\leq\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda_{n}\colon\\
p\in Z\end{subarray}}\int_{0}^{|t|}\|[\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z),\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{s;\omega}(a_{X})]\|ds\,.

Applying Proposition
3.2

\omega
-pointwise, we obtain an upper bound of the form

\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})-\alpha^{\Lambda_{m}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})\|\leq\frac{\|a_{X}\|}{C_{F}}\left(\int_{0}^{|t|}e^{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}|s|}ds\right)\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{m}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\subset\Lambda_{n}\colon\\
p\in Z\end{subarray}}\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\|\cdot\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{y\in Z}F(d(x,y))

for all
\omega
in the event
[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]
. From this, use Fubini’s theorem to rearrange the sums to obtain

\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})-\alpha^{\Lambda_{m}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})\|\leq\|a_{X}\|\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)\left(\int_{0}^{|t|}e^{\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}|s|}ds\right)\sum_{x\in X}\sum_{p\in\Lambda_{n}\setminus\Lambda_{m}}F(d(x,p))\,,

which tends to zero as
n,m\to\infty
almost surely because
[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]
with probability one. Furthermore, we see that
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})
is the limit of a sequence of weakly measurable functions, namely
\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})
, so it is measurable for all
a_{X}
. But this is just a re-phrasing of the weakly-
\ast
measurable requirement.
∎

Notation 3.4
.

To keep the exposition tidier, we will often write
\alpha_{t;\omega}
instead of
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}
, reserving the latter only when we need to emphasize the thermodynamics over finite-volume dynamics.

4
Main Results

This section is divided into three main parts. The first subsection introduces our notion of Ergodic Local Hamiltonian. As noted in the introduction, a similar definition appears as far back as
[
51
]
. Our focus however is significantly more general and we establish ergodic co-variance equations for the propagator dynamics in the thermodynamic limit. Then, using Lemma
2.2
, we establish the existence of measurable infinite-volume ground states for the thermodynamics. In the second subsection, we show that among the measurable bulk ground state sectors, one can construct a sector that co-varies along with the translation automorphisms. The key tool in that proof is Proposition
A.6
. We conclude by proving our second main result, Theorem
B
that in the GNS representation with respect to the ergodic ground state, the spectrum of the bulk Hamiltonian is constant.

4.1
Ergodic Local Hamiltonians

The key theorem to our approach is the existence of an ergodic ground state for the family of Hamiltonians
\{H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}:\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\}
in the thermodynamic limit. Once this is established, the proof that the bulk excitation spectrum is deterministic is straightforward. Let us begin by restricting our attention to a class of disordered interactions.

Definition 4.1
.

Given a disordered interaction
\mathfrak{h}:\Omega\times\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, we say that
\mathfrak{h}
is a
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
-
ergodic interaction
if there exists a group action of
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
on
\Omega
by measure-preserving, ergodic maps
\{\vartheta_{x}\colon\Omega\to\Omega\}_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
so that

\tau_{x}(\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z))=\mathfrak{h}(\vartheta_{x}\omega,\tau_{x}(Z))\,,

(4.1)

where
\tau_{x}(Z)=\{z+x\colon z\in Z\}
. By a slight abuse of terminology, we will drop the
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
adjective and just write “
ergodic interaction
”. The next lemma is a result of a straightforward computation.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}
be the local Hamiltonian associated to an ergodic interaction
\mathfrak{h}
. Then, the following relations hold for any
\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
:

H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}=\tau_{-x}(H^{\tau_{x}(\Lambda)}_{\vartheta_{x}\omega})\,,

(4.2)

and moreover,

\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}=\tau_{-x}\circ\alpha^{\tau_{x}(\Lambda)}_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}.

(4.3)

Corollary 4.3
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}:\Omega\times\mathscr{P}_{0}(\mathbb{Z}^{\nu})
be an ergodic interaction and consider the family of local dynamics
\{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}\}_{\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
associated to
\mathfrak{h}
. Suppose that
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]>0
. Then the following hold.

1.

The quantity
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}
is almost surely constant.

2.

The thermodynamic limit exists and satisfies the following ergodicity condition with probability one.

\alpha_{t;\omega}=\tau_{-x}\circ\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}\,\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}.

(4.4)

Proof.

To prove 1., our aim is to show that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)=\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\vartheta\omega)
. Observe that for any pair of points
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, the quantities

N_{x,y,n}(\omega):=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\ni x,y\\
\diam(Z)\leq n\end{subarray}}\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z)\|F(d(x,y))^{-1}

(4.5)

form an increasing sequence of positive random variables which satisfy
\sup_{x,y}\lim_{n\to\infty}\mathcal{N}_{x,y,n}=\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}
.
For any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, we have

\displaystyle N_{x,y,n}(\vartheta_{z}\omega)

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\ni x,y\\
\diam(Z)\leq n\end{subarray}}\|\mathfrak{h}(\vartheta_{z}\omega,Z)\|F(d(x,y))^{-1}=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}Z\ni x,y\\
\diam(Z)\leq n\end{subarray}}\|\tau_{z}\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z-z)\|F(d(x-z,y-z))^{-1}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\ni x-z,y-z\\
\diam(W)\leq n\end{subarray}}\|\mathfrak{h}(\omega,W)\|F(d(x-z,y-z))^{-1}=N_{x-z,y-z,n}(\omega)\,.

Note we have crucially used translation invariance of the
\ell^{\infty}
-metric
d
on
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
to obtain the second equality. Now, taking the supremum over
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
and
n\in\mathbb{N}
, we obtain the equation
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)=\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\vartheta_{z}\omega)
. Whence
\vartheta_{z}^{-1}[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]\subset[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]
.

For 2., the estimate (
3.4
) shows that the local dynamics
\{\alpha^{\Lambda}_{t;\omega}:\Lambda\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\}
are essentially bounded almost surely. Furthermore, by combining part 1 and Proposition
3.3
, the strong limit

s-\lim_{n\to\infty}\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}=\alpha_{t;\omega}\,,

exists with probability one for every IAS of finite volumes
\Lambda_{1}\subset\Lambda_{2}\subset\cdots
with
\bigcup\Lambda_{n}=\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. Now, suppose
a\in\mathcal{A}_{X}
where
X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. For any
\epsilon>0
, on a set of probability one, there is some
n
large enough so that

\max\{\|\alpha_{t;\omega}(a)-\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}\|,\|\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)-\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}^{\Lambda_{n}-x}\circ\tau_{x}(a)\|\}<\epsilon/2\,,

since
\Lambda_{n}-x
is still an IAS. By Lemma
4.2
, we now have

\displaystyle\|\alpha_{t;\omega}(a)-\tau_{x}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)\|

\displaystyle\leq\|\alpha_{t;\omega}(a)-\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a)\|+\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}(a)-\tau_{x}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)\|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{\epsilon}{2}+\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}+x}_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)-\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)\|<\epsilon\,.

Therefore
\alpha_{t;\omega}(a)=\tau_{x}^{-1}\circ\alpha_{t;\vartheta_{x}\omega}\circ\tau_{x}(a)
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, which is a norm-dense
*
-subalgebra of
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
.
∎

4.2
Covariant Disordered Ground States

The next step in our analysis is to establish the existence of a weakly-
\ast
-measurable state
\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\gamma_{\omega}\in\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})
that satisfies both the ground state property for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, and the translation co-variance property
\gamma_{\omega}\circ\tau_{x}=\gamma_{\vartheta_{x}\omega}
.
Suppose we have some ergodic local interaction
\mathfrak{h}
. Define the unbounded disordered derivation
\delta_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}}^{\nu}
via

\delta_{\omega}(a_{X})=\sum_{Z\cap X\neq\varnothing}[i\mathfrak{h}(\omega,Z),a_{X}]\,,

(4.6)

for all
a_{X}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
. Note that
\dom(\delta_{\omega})\supset\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
for every
\omega\in\Omega
, so
\delta_{\omega}
is densely defined. We say that a weakly-
\ast
-measurable function
\Gamma:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})
is a
disordered ground state
if the ground state property holds almost surely:

-i\Gamma_{\omega}(a^{*}\delta_{\omega}(a))\geq 0,\quad\forall a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc},\,\mathbb{P}-\mathrm{a.e.}\omega\,.

(4.7)

Lemma 4.4
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
be the quasilocal algebra. Suppose
\mathfrak{h}
is an ergodic interaction and
\omega\in\Omega
such that
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}(\omega)<\infty
. Let
(\Lambda_{n}\colon n\in\mathbb{N})
be an IAS. If for each
n
,
\lambda^{(n)}_{\omega}
is a ground state for the local dynamics
\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t;\omega}
generated by
\mathfrak{h}
, then any weak-
\ast
limit of the sequence
\lambda^{(n)}_{\omega}
is a ground state of
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}
.

Proof.

An application of Lemma
2.2
finds a random increasing sequence
\sigma_{n}\to\infty
and a measurable weak-
\ast
limit point
\Gamma_{\omega}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
. It is straightforward to check that
\Gamma_{\omega}
is a state
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
. We need to verify that
\Gamma_{\omega}
satisfies the relevant positivity condition.

Write
\delta_{n}=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda_{n}}[i\mathfrak{h}(Z,\omega),\,\cdot\,].
By Corollary
3.3
, the strong limit
\alpha^{\Lambda_{n}}\to\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
exists with probability one. Let
\delta_{\omega}
denote the unbounded derivation defined as in (
4.6
).
For each
\omega\in[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]
, the derivation
\delta_{\omega}
is closable with dense domain containing
\mathcal{A}^{\loc}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
as a core, and
\alpha_{t;\omega}=\exp\{t\overline{\delta_{\omega}}\}
(see Bratteli and Robinson
[
9
, Proposition 3.2.22, Example 3.2.25]
). Therefore, for any
a\in\dom(\overline{\delta_{\omega}})
, we can find a sequence
a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
(which may depend on
\omega
but need not be measurable in
\omega
) for which
a_{n}\to a
and
\delta_{n;\omega}(a_{n})\to\delta_{\omega}(a)
. We observe that along the evaluations of the random sequence
\sigma_{n}(\omega)
the corresponding disordered ground state condition holds for all
\omega
in a probability one set:

-i\lambda^{(\sigma_{n})}_{\omega}(a^{*}_{\sigma_{n}}\delta_{\sigma_{n}}(a_{\sigma_{n}}))\geq 0\,.

(4.8)

Therefore, taking the limit
n\to\infty
, we arrive at

-i\Gamma_{\omega}(a^{*}\delta_{\omega}(a))\geq 0\text{ almost surely}.

Hence
\Gamma
is a disordered ground state.
∎

Remark 4.5
.

The argument we give can be easily generalized to show that weak-
\ast
limits of local Gibbs states satisfy a disordered version of the
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS condition in the thermodynamic limit. This is essentially a disordered version of Propositions 3.5.25 and 6.2.15 of
[
10
]
if one replaces (
4.8
) with the relevant Araki-Roepsdorff-Sewell conditions (see
[
10
, Theorem 3.5.15]
).

Now that we have established that disordered ground states exist generally, we want to show that there are disordered ground states satisfying the same ergodicity condition as (
4.1
).

Theorem 4.6

(Theorem
A
)
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a countably generated probability space. Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an ergodic interaction with
\mathbb{P}[\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty]>0
for some deterministic
F
-function,
F
. There exists a weak-
\ast
measurable function
\Gamma
that is a disordered ground state of
\mathfrak{h}
satisfying

\Gamma_{\omega}\circ\tau_{x}=\Gamma_{\vartheta_{x}\omega}\quad\forall x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}\text{ almost surely.}

(4.9)

Proof.

Let
\gamma_{\omega}
be a disordered ground state for
\mathfrak{h}_{\omega}
which exists by Lemma
4.4
. Notice that
\gamma_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}
is a ground state for
\mathfrak{h}_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}
for any
p\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, but by the covariance of the thermodynamic limit, this means
\gamma_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}
is a ground state for
\tau_{p}(\mathfrak{h}_{\omega})
. With this in mind, we define the following sequence of ‘averaged’ ground states of
\mathfrak{h}_{\omega}

\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}=\frac{1}{|b_{0}(n)|}\sum_{p\in b_{0}(n)}\gamma_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}\circ\tau_{-p}\,.

(4.10)

Notice that
\|\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}}\equiv 1
by the Russo-Dye theorem. In view of Lemma
A.8
we can regard the
\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}
as elements of the dual space
[L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})]^{*}
. But, since
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is countably generated,
L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})
is separable by
[
25
, Proposition 1.2.29]
. Thus by general functional analysis the unit ball
[L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})]^{*}
is weakly-
\ast
sequentially compact. Thus, we lose no generality by assuming
\gamma^{(n)}
converges to, say,
\Phi
in the
\sigma(L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}),L^{1}(\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}})^{*})
topology. By Proposition
A.10
, we conclude there is some
\Gamma:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
which is weakly-
\ast
measurable representing
\Phi
whose norm function is essentially bounded. Finally, by the continuity of scalar-valued Radon-Nikodym derivatives
[
7
]
for the associated complex measures
\nu_{\Gamma}(\,\cdot;a)
and
\nu_{\gamma^{(n)}}(\,\cdot;a)
as in (
A.6
), we conclude
\Gamma_{\omega}(a)=\lim_{n\to\infty}\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}(a)
for almost every
\omega
and every
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
, hence
\Gamma_{\omega}
is a state on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
almost surely.

Let
z
be a unit length generator for
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. By
\nu
-hyperregularity, the symmetric difference of
b_{0}(n)
and
b_{z}(n)
obeys
|b_{0}(n)\Delta b_{z}(n)|\lesssim n^{\nu-1}
and
|b_{0}(n)|\sim n^{\nu}
. Now,

\displaystyle\left|\gamma^{(n)}_{\vartheta_{z}\omega}(a)-\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}\circ\tau_{z}\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|b_{0}(n)|}\left|\sum_{p\in b_{0}(n)}\gamma_{\vartheta_{p+z}\omega}\circ\tau_{-p}(a)-\sum_{p^{\prime}\in b_{0}(n)}\gamma_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}\circ\tau_{z-p}(a)\right|

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{|b_{0}(n)|}\left|\left(\sum_{p\in b_{0}(n)\setminus b_{z}(n)}+\sum_{p\in b_{z}(n)\setminus b_{0}(n)}\right)\gamma_{\vartheta_{p}\omega}\circ\tau_{-p}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\|a\||\gamma_{\omega}(\mathbbm{1})|\frac{|b_{0}(n)\Delta b_{z}(n)|}{|b_{0}(n)|}=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})\text{ as }n\to\infty\,,

where the second equality follows by re-indexing
p^{\prime}=z-p
and canceling the differences. In particular, this shows
\esssup_{\omega\in\Omega}\left|\gamma^{(n)}_{\vartheta_{z}\omega}(a)-\gamma^{(n)}_{\omega}\circ\tau_{z}\right|=\mathcal{O}(n^{-1})
as
n\to\infty
.

Therefore, if
\Gamma
is the weak-
\ast
limit (with respect to the weak-
\ast
topology on the Bochner space
(L^{1}(\mathcal{A}))^{*}
) of the
\gamma^{(n)}
’s, an
\epsilon/3
-argument combined with the estimate above shows that with probability one,

\Gamma_{\vartheta_{z}\omega}(a)=\Gamma_{\omega}\circ\tau_{z}(a)\,,

(4.11)

for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
, almost surely. But
z
was an arbitrary generator of
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
and
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}
is norm-dense in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
, so we conclude that
\Gamma
is ergodic.
∎

Remark 4.7
.

Similar to Remark
4.5
above, the conclusions of Theorem
4.6
can be restated in terms of w*-limits of disordered local Gibbs states, to show there is a covariant
(\alpha,\beta)
-KMS state.

4.3
The Spectrum of a Bulk Hamiltonian in an Ergodic Ground State

Since we have established Theorem
4.6
, we may now prove our second main result, Theorem
B
. We shall state this as Corollary
4.9
to a much more general assertion Theorem
4.8
which can be proved without reference to a specific physical model.

Theorem 4.8
.

Let
\mathcal{A}
be a separable unital
C^{*}
-algebra, and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Suppose
G
is a discrete group acting on
\mathcal{A}
via
\tau
, and suppose
G
also acts on
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
via family of measure preserving ergodic maps
\{\vartheta_{g}:g\in G\}
. Suppose
\{S_{t;\omega}\colon\mathcal{A}\to\mathcal{A}\}_{\omega\in\Omega}
is a family of strongly continuous one-parameter groups of
*
-automorphisms which are weakly-
\ast
measurable in
\omega
. Further, suppose that
S_{t;\omega}
obey the following co-variance condition

S_{t;\omega}=\tau_{g}^{-1}\circ S_{t;\vartheta_{g}\omega}\circ\tau_{g};\quad\forall g\in G,\,\mathbb{P}-a.e.\omega\,.

(4.12)

Assume
S_{t;\omega}
admits a ground state
\psi_{\omega}
so that
\psi_{\omega}\circ S_{t;\omega}=\psi_{\omega}
which also satisfies the following covariance condition

\psi_{\omega}=\psi_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}\circ\tau_{g}\quad\text{$\mathbb{P}$ almost surely}\,.

(4.13)

If
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\Psi_{\omega})
is the GNS triple associated to a covariant state
\psi_{\omega}
on
\mathcal{A}
, then the following hold

1.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
, there is a densely defined self-adjoint operator
H_{\omega}
in the GNS space
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\Psi_{\omega})
which generates the implemented dynamics such that
\inf\sigma(H_{\omega})=0
given by

S_{t;\omega}(\pi_{\omega}(a)\Psi_{\omega}):=\pi_{\omega}(S_{t;\omega}(a))\Psi_{\omega}\quad\forall t\in\mathbb{R},a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\,.

2.

For
\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
, there exist unitaries
U_{g}:\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{H}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}
which intertwine
H_{\omega}
in the following way

H_{\omega}=U_{g}^{*}H_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}U_{g}\,.

(4.14)

3.

There is an event
\Omega_{0}
which has full probability for which

\spec_{*}(H_{\omega})\text{ is deterministic for }*\in\{\mathrm{ess},\mathrm{dis},\mathrm{c},\mathrm{ac},\mathrm{pp},\mathrm{sc}\}\,.

(4.15)

Proof.

Recall that the GNS Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
is formed by completion of the quotient space
A/J_{\omega}
with respect to the inner product
\langle\,\cdot\,|\,\cdot\,\rangle_{\omega}
induced by
\psi_{\omega}
, where
J_{\omega}:=\{a\in A\colon\psi_{\omega}(a^{*}a)=0\}
. We denote by
[a,\omega]
the equivalence class of
a\in A
modulo
J_{\omega}
. In particular, the cyclic vector
\Psi_{\omega}=[\mathbbm{1},\omega]
. Since
\mathcal{A}
is separable, the resulting Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
are also separable. In particular, for every
\omega
, we can pick on orthonormal basis of
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
consisting of vectors of the form
\{[a,\omega]\colon a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}\}
, by the Gram-Schmidt process.

We define the
U_{g}
in the following way. Let
U_{g}:\pi_{\omega}(A)\Psi_{\omega}\to\mathcal{H}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}
via

U_{g}[a,\omega]=[\tau_{g}(a),\vartheta_{g}\omega]\,.

(4.16)

We note that

\langle U_{g}[a,\omega]|U_{g}[b,\omega]\rangle_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}=\psi_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}(\tau_{g}(a^{*})\tau_{g}(b))=\psi_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}\circ\tau_{g}(a^{*}b)=\langle[a,\omega]|[b,\omega]\rangle_{\omega}\,,

by the co-variance property. Whence
U_{g}
is well defined for all
g\in G
and moreover the
U_{g}
are densely defined linear operators which preserve the inner product, so they extend uniquely to unitaries
U_{g}:\mathcal{H}_{\omega}\to\mathcal{H}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}
.

By standard theory we know that
S_{t;\omega}
can be implemented by a strongly continuous one parameter group (of endomorphisms) on
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. By abuse of notation, let us identify
S_{t;\omega}
with
S_{t;\omega}[a,\omega]:=[S_{t;\omega}(a),\omega]
. Then,
S_{t;\omega}
is generated by a densely defined self-adjoint operator
H_{\omega}
. By the covariance condition, we see that

\displaystyle U_{g}^{*}H_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}U_{g}[a,\omega]

\displaystyle=[\tau_{g}\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{S_{t;\vartheta_{g}\omega}(\tau_{g}(a))-\tau_{g}(a)}{t},\omega]=[\lim_{t\downarrow 0}\frac{S_{t;\omega}a-a}{t},\omega]=H_{\omega}[a,\omega]\,,

as required.

By the intertwining relation, any spectral projection
P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\omega}:=\chi_{(\lambda,\mu)}(H_{\omega})
where
\lambda<\mu
are rational, satisfies
U_{g}^{*}P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\omega}U_{g}=P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}
. Furthermore,
P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\omega}
has a measurable trace-function. Indeed, given any orthonormal basis
\{[a_{n},\omega]\colon a_{n}\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{\loc}\}_{n\in\mathbb{N}}
for
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
, we find

\text{Tr}[P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\omega}]=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle[a_{n},\omega]|P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\omega}|[a_{n},\omega]\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\omega}}=\sum_{n=1}^{\infty}\langle[\tau_{g}(a_{n}),\vartheta_{g}\omega]|P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}|[\tau_{g}(a_{n}),\vartheta_{g}\omega]\rangle_{\mathcal{H}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}}=\text{Tr}[P^{(\lambda,\mu)}_{\vartheta_{g}\omega}]\,.

In particular, since
\vartheta_{g}
are ergodic, this means that the trace of all the spectral projections of
H_{\omega}
are constant for fixed
\lambda,\mu
. The rest of the proof now follows the same argument as
[
32
]
. Namely, the spectral family of
H_{\omega}
has dimension jumps which are independent of
\omega
. Since this characterizes points in the spectrum (cf.
[
64
, Theorems 7.22 and 7.24]
) we are done.
∎

One immediately has the following.

Corollary 4.9

(Theorem
B
)
.

Let
\mathfrak{h}
be an ergodic interaction with
\mathcal{N}^{\mathfrak{h}}_{F}<\infty
almost surely. Suppose
\varphi_{\omega}
is a covariant ground state of the thermodynamics
\alpha_{t;\omega}
on
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
. Let
(\mathcal{H}_{\omega},\pi_{\omega},\Phi_{\omega})
denote the GNS triple associated to
\varphi_{\omega}
and let
H_{\omega}
denote the GNS Hamiltonian associated to the strongly continuous group of unitaries
U_{t;\omega}
which implement
\alpha^{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}_{t;\omega}
in
\mathcal{H}_{\omega}
. Then,
\spec(H_{\omega})
is deterministic.

Remark 4.10
.

This pair of results is strongly reminiscent of the classic theorem of Pastur
[
52
]
later generalized by Kirsch and Martinelli
[
32
, Theorem 1]
, but is distinct in several key ways. Besides being a result for quantum spin systems, one key difference is that in the proof of Theorem
4.8
the underlying Hilbert space that the unitaries map onto is
\omega
-dependent. We are able to circumvent this by using separability of the fibers, and independence of the canonical trace from a choice of orthonormal basis. A version of the Pastur-Kirsh-Martinelli theorem with
\omega
-dependent fibers is given in
[
38
]
in the context of measurable groupoids.

4.4
An Application

In this section we make some contact with the disordered spin chain models we mentioned in the Introduction by considering a random perturbation of an interaction in
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
. Proposition
4.11
below shows that for interactions of a specific form resembling the disordered XY and XXZ models has a deterministic spectrum in its ground state. The key innovation is that our proof works for arbitrary dimension
\nu
, whereas the results for the XY and XXZ results depend on the Jordan-Wigner theorem which only applies if
\nu=1
.

Proposition 4.11
.

Let
\Phi
be a deterministic, translation invariant interaction on
\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
with
\|\Phi\|_{F}<\infty
for some
F
-function
F:[0,\infty)\to(0,\infty)
. Suppose
(\lambda_{x})_{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
is an array of IID random variables on a common probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
which are almost surely finite. Suppose
v\in\mathcal{A}_{\{0\}}
and define

\Psi(X,\omega)=\Phi(X)+\delta_{1}(|X|)\sum_{x\in X}\lambda_{x}(\omega)\tau_{x}(v)\quad\forall X\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}

(4.17)

where
\delta
is the Kronecker-delta. Notice that the total Hamiltonian in a finite volume is then given by

H^{\Lambda}_{\omega}=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\Psi(Z,\omega)=\sum_{Z\subset\Lambda}\Phi(Z)+\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\lambda_{x}(\omega)v_{x}\,.

Then,
\Psi
defines an ergodic interaction. In particular, the following hold:

1.

There is an ergodic ground state
\psi_{\omega}
for
\Psi
.

2.

With respect to any ergodic ground state, the spectrum of the GNS Hamiltonian
H
is deterministic.

Furhtermore, in the case that the
\lambda_{x}
are essentially bounded, we can give a quantitative estimate on the
F
-norm of the perturbation via

\|\Psi\|_{F}=\|\Phi\|_{F}+F(0)^{-1}\|v\|\,\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\,.

Proof.

It is a standard application of Kolmogorov’s Extension Theorem to see that we can replace the original probability space in such a way that the IID
\lambda_{x}
are recovered as ergodic shifts of of a single continuous random variable with the same law as
\lambda_{0}
.

Let
\alpha^{\Lambda,\Psi}_{t;\omega}
denote the Heisenberg dynamics generated by
\Psi_{\omega}
and let
\alpha^{\Lambda,\Phi}_{t}
denote the unperturbed Heisenberg dynamics. If the
\lambda_{x}
are essentially bounded, it is a simple calculation to show that

\|\Psi\|_{F}\leq\|\Phi\|_{F}+F(0)^{-1}\|v\|\|\lambda\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})},

so we may apply Proposition
3.3
to conclude that the thermodynamic limit of the
\alpha^{\Lambda,\Psi}_{t;\omega}
exists almost surely.

We claim the thermodynamic limit still holds if the
\lambda_{x}
are only finite almost surely. Postponing the proof of this claim for now, we can obtain Proposition
4.11
by arguing in the same way as the proof of Lemma
4.4
to obtain a global ground state, and then as in the proof of Theorem
4.8
to obtain an ergodic disordered ground state. Now the result follows by straightforward application of Corollary
4.9
.

To prove the claim, consider the family of random local unitaries
u_{x}(t):=e^{it\lambda_{x}v_{x}}
which are mutually commuting for any
x\neq y
. Defining
T^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t)=\bigotimes_{x\in\Lambda}u_{x}(t)
we observe that
T^{\Lambda}(t)=\exp\{it\sum_{x\in\Lambda}\lambda_{x}v_{x}\}
. Consider the transformation on
\alpha^{\Lambda,\Psi}_{t;\omega}
given by

\tilde{\alpha}_{t}^{\Lambda}(\,\cdot\,):=(T^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t))^{*}\alpha^{\Lambda,\Psi}_{t;\omega}(\,\cdot\,)T^{\Lambda}_{\omega}(t)\,.

Let
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
be an IAS. By the product structure of the unitary transformations
T^{\Lambda}_{\omega}
, we observe that for any
n\geq m
, and any finite volume
B\supset\Lambda_{n}
, one has

\displaystyle\|\alpha^{\Lambda_{n},\Psi}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})-\alpha^{\Lambda_{m},\Psi}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})\|

\displaystyle=\|T^{B}(t)^{*}(\alpha^{\Lambda_{n},\Psi}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})-\alpha^{\Lambda_{m}}_{t;\omega}(a_{X}))T^{B}(t)\|

\displaystyle=\|(T^{\Lambda_{n}}(t))^{*}\alpha^{\Lambda_{n},\Psi}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})T^{\Lambda_{n}}(t)-T^{\Lambda_{m}}(t)\alpha^{\Lambda_{m},\Psi}_{t;\omega}(a_{X})T^{\Lambda_{m}}(t)\|

\displaystyle=\|\tilde{\alpha}^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t}(a_{X})-\tilde{\alpha}^{\Lambda_{m}}(a_{X})\|\,.

So it is sufficient to show that the thermodynamic limit of
(\tilde{\alpha}^{\Lambda_{n}}_{t})_{n=1}^{\infty}
exists. It is easy to check that the
\tilde{\alpha}_{t}
are generated by the local interaction

\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}(Z,t)=(T^{Z}(t))^{*}\Phi(Z)(T^{Z}(t))\,,

which we note obeys
\|\tilde{\Phi}_{\omega}(Z,t)\|=\|\Phi(Z)\|
for any
t\in\mathbb{R}
, any
Z\Subset\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}
, and all
\omega\in\Omega
. Hence
\sup_{t\in\mathbb{R}}\|\tilde{\Phi}(t)\|_{F}=\|\Phi\|_{F}<\infty
. Therefore, we can conclude the thermodynamic limit exists because of the time-inhomogeneous Lieb-Robinson bounds as in Theorem 3.5
[
45
]
.
∎

Remark 4.12
.

As a corollary, we obtain a different proof that models such as the disordered XY model studied in
[
22
]
or the XXZ chain
[
62
]
have deterministic bulk spectra (the usual proof is obtained by applying the Jordan-Wigner transformation and then the Pastur-Kirsch-Martinelli theorem). Simplicity of the eigenvalues for Anderson and Anderson-like models have been investigated in (e.g.)
[
33
,
41
]
. Interestingly, since both the XY and XXZ models have unique ground states, we have also shown that they are both ergodic.

Appendix A
Measurability Concerns

In this section, we will recall some basic facts about random variables and measures which take values in infinite-dimensional Banach spaces. For the convenience of the reader we present more details in this section as they will play a role in our applications of the material below.

We will take a few lines to recall some basics about random variables in Banach space. Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separable Banach space and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Famously, there are two distinct notions of measurable functions from
\Omega
into
\mathcal{X}
. One says that a function
s:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}
is
simple
if there exist measurable sets
E_{1},E_{2},\dots,E_{k}\in\mathcal{F}
and elements
x_{1},\dots,x_{k}\in\mathcal{A}
so that

s_{\omega}=\sum_{j=1}^{k}x_{j}\cdot\chi_{E_{j}}(\omega)\,.

A function
X:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}
is
strongly (
\mathbb{P}
-)measurable
if there exists a sequence of simple functions
s^{(n)}
so that
\|X-s^{(n)}\|_{\mathcal{X}}\to 0

\mathbb{P}
-a.e.
\omega
. On the other hand, a function
Y:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}
is said to be
weakly measurable
if for every linear functional
f\in\mathcal{X}^{*}
, the
\mathbb{C}
-valued function
\Omega\ni\omega\longmapsto f(Y(\omega))
is Borel measurable. If
\mathcal{X}
is separable, these notions agree due to a general theorem of Pettis
[
55
]
:

Theorem A.1

(Pettis Measurability Theorem)
.

Let
\mathcal{X}
be a separable Banach space, and let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. Then a function
X:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}
is strongly measurable if and only if it is weakly measurable.

For a proof see any of
[
8
,
15
,
25
]
. With the Pettis Theorem in mind, we will refer to a measurable function into
\mathcal{X}
as a(
n
\mathcal{X}
-valued
)
random variable
.

Remark A.2
.

If
\mathcal{X}
is a separable Banach space and
X:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}
is a random variable, then the
norm function
given by
\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\|X_{\omega}\|\in[0,\infty]
is Borel measurable. Indeed since
\mathcal{X}
is separable, there is a countable subset
D\subset\mathcal{X}^{*}
so that
\|y\|=\sup_{f\in D}|f(y)|
for any
y\in\mathcal{X}
by standard functional analysis.

It is standard theory that linear combinations of random variables in a Banach space are once again random variables. Furthermore, one can define
L^{p}
spaces based on integrability of the norm function. These so-called
Bochner spaces
are defined for
1\leq p<\infty
as follows:

L^{p}(\mathcal{X}):=\{G:\Omega\to\mathcal{X}\text{ $\mathbb{P}$-measurable with }\|\,\|G\|_{\mathcal{X}}\|_{L^{p}(\mathbb{P})}<\infty\}\,,

where functions are identified modulo equality on set of positive measure. Each Bochner
L^{p}
space is a Banach space in and of itself
[
24
, Theorem 3.7.8]
. Recall that a given probability space
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is
countably generated
if there is a sequence of measurable sets
F_{n}\in\mathcal{F}
which generates
\mathcal{F}
as a
\sigma
-algebra (cf.
[
25
, Definition 1.2.27]
). If
\mathcal{F}
is countably generated, and
\mathcal{X}
is separable then the Bochner spaces
L^{p}(\mathcal{A})
are separable Banach spaces by Proposition 1.2.29 of
[
25
]
.

A.1
Weak-
\ast
Measurability and Duality

Now, let
\mathcal{A}
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra with a unit. The
state space
of
\mathcal{A}
is the set
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A}^{*}
of linear functionals
\phi:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{C}
which are positive
\phi(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
, and unit-normalized
\phi(\mathbbm{1})=1
. In this letter, we will be concerned with state-valued random variables
\Phi:\Omega\to\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})\subset\mathcal{A}^{*}
into the state space of
\mathcal{A}
. A natural topology on
\mathcal{S}(\mathcal{A})
with respect to which we should define measurability is the weak-
\ast
-topology (that is, the
\sigma(\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{*})
topology in the notation of
[
56
]
). There does not seem to be much in the literature about weakly-
\ast
measurable functions in non-reflexive Banach spaces, so we will prove several basic properties here. Many of the following lemmas can be generalized to a separable Banach space with the appropriate properties, but we state the
C^{*}
-algebraic formulations for convenience.

Definition A.3
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and let
\mathcal{A}
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra. A mapping
\Phi:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}^{*}
is
random linear functional
if for every
a\in\mathcal{A}
, one has that the function

\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}(a)\in\mathbb{C}\,,

(A.1)

is Borel measurable in
\mathbb{C}
, and furthermore
\Phi
is linear almost surely.

Remark A.4
.

There is a bit of a clash in terminology in the literature about analysis in Banach spaces which we need to point out. In the language of
[
8
,
25
,
26
,
27
]
a mapping
\Phi
as in (

) would be called a

\mathbb{P}
-measurable
random linear operator when viewed as taking values in

. In particular, such a

takes random variables in
\mathcal{A}
to random variables in
\mathbb{C}
, see
[

.

Remark A.5
.

Note that if
\Phi_{\omega}
is weak-
\ast
measurable, then the operator-norm function
\omega\mapsto\|\Phi_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}
is also measurable. Indeed, pick some countable dense subset of
\mathcal{A}
, in which case
\|\Phi_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}=\sup_{n}\frac{|\Phi_{\omega}(a_{n})|}{\|a_{n}\|}
with equality holding point-wise if we allow for
+\infty
. Cf. Remark

above.

The next lemma is not strictly necessary, but it may be of independent interest since it characterizes weak-
\ast
measurability. In words, we show that weakly-
\ast
measurable functions into the dual of a quasilocal algebra can be approximated by simple functions in a suitable topology.

Proposition A.6
.

Suppose the one-site algebra
\mathcal{A}_{\{x\}}\cong\mathbb{M}_{k}
for a fixed integer
k
and let
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
be the associated quasilocal algebra. Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space. A function
f:\Omega\to\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
is a random linear functional if and only if there is a set
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}\Omega_{0}=1
, and a sequence of simple functions
\ell^{(n)}_{\omega}
so that for all
a\in\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}
, and all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
, the limit
\lim_{n\to\infty}\ell^{(n)}_{\omega}(a)=f_{\omega}(a)
.

Before beginning the proof, we take a few lines to discuss the main idea of the proof. The key ingredient of Proposition
A.6
is that for any IAS
(\Lambda_{n})_{n=1}^{\infty}
, there exist unital completely positive maps
\mathcal{E}_{n}:\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}\to\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
(namely, taking partial traces), such that
\|a-\mathcal{E}_{n}(a)\|\to 0
as
n\to\infty
for any
a\in\mathcal{A}

[
44
,
45
]
. By taking a weakly-
\ast
-measurable
f
, and restricting to
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
, one can regard
f
as a limit of weakly measurable functionals in the dual of the finite dimensional subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}
. Thus, at each level
f|_{\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}}
is a limit of simple functions in
\mathcal{A}_{\Lambda_{n}}^{*}
. Then, by composing these restrictions with the
\mathcal{E}_{n}
and taking a diagonal argument, one finds a sequence of simple functions in
\mathcal{A}_{\mathbb{Z}^{\nu}}^{*}
which weakly-
\ast
-approximates
f
.

In the
C^{*}
-algebras literature, this approximation property is an instance of the

property. In words, a
C^{*}
-algebra
\mathcal{A}
is

if the identity mapping
\id_{\mathcal{A}}
approximately factors through finite dimensional matrix algebras strongly. More precisely, (see
[
12
, Proposition 2.2.6]
)
\mathcal{A}
is nuclear if for every
n\in\mathbb{N}
, there is
k(n)\in\mathbb{N}
, and a pair of unital completely positive mappings
\pi_{n}:A\to\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}
and
\iota_{n}:\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}\to A
so that for any

,

\lim_{n\to\infty}\|\iota_{n}\circ\pi_{n}(a)-a\|=0\,.

(A.2)

Since this approximation property holds for a larger class of algebras and is relatively straightforward to state, we will prove Proposition
A.6
in this level of generality.

Proof of Proposition
A.6
.

The backward implication has nothing to do with nuclearity. Instead we note that the hypothesis implies
\lim_{n\to\infty}|\phi^{(n)}_{\omega}(a)|=|f_{\omega}(a)|
, whence the seminorms defining the weak-
\ast
topology pass through
f
to become measurable functions in
\mathbb{R}
since
|\phi^{(n)}_{\omega}(a)|
is
\mathbb{P}
-measurable.

For the forward implication, let us first assume that the norm-function
\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\|f_{\omega}\|\in[0,\infty)
is essentially bounded (the general case will follow by truncation arguments). Write
F:=\esssup\|f_{\omega}\|<\infty
.

Now, find the sequence of matrix algebras
\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}
, and ucp maps
\pi_{n}:\mathcal{A}\to\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}
and
\iota_{n}:\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}\to\mathcal{A}
which satisfy the approximation property (
A.2
). Let
f^{(n)}_{\omega}:=f_{\omega}\circ\iota_{n}
. This
f^{(n)}_{\omega}
can be viewed as a linear functional on the finite-dimensional subspace
R_{n}:=\iota_{n}(\mathbb{M}_{k(n)})\cong\mathbb{M}_{k(n)}
, where the isomorphism is that of Banach spaces. Furthermore,
f^{(n)}_{\omega}(\pi_{n}(a))
is measurable for all

by hypothesis. Therefore, we may view
f^{(n)}_{\omega}
as a weakly-
\ast
measurable random variable in the dual space
R_{n}^{*}
viewed as a subspace of
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Since
R_{n}
is finite dimensional, this means that there is a sequence of simple functions
\{s^{(n,m)}_{\omega}\}_{m=1}^{\infty}\subset(R_{n})^{*}
which approximate

in the weak-
\ast
topology of

almost surely. Again using the fact that

is finite dimensional, this is equivalent to convergence in the norm topology by basic functional analysis. Thus, there is a set
\Omega_{n}\subset\Omega
with
\mathbb{P}[\Omega_{n}]=1
so that

\lim_{m\to\infty}\|f^{(n)}_{\omega}-s^{(n,m)}_{\omega}\|_{R_{n}^{*}}=0\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega_{n}\,.

(A.3)

Notice that any simple function in
R_{n}^{*}
can be canonically extended to a simple function in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
since any finite-dimensional subspace of a Banach space is complemented
[

(in particular, one need only compose with

). Therefore, for any

there is

so that

\|f^{(n)}_{\omega}-s^{(n,m_{n})}_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}<2^{-n}\quad\forall\omega\in\Omega_{n}\,.

Put
\Omega_{0}=\{\omega:\esssup\|f_{\omega}\|<\infty\}\cap\bigcap_{n=1}^{\infty}\Omega_{n},
and let
\ell^{(n)}_{\omega}:=\chi_{\Omega_{0}}(\omega)s^{(n,m_{n})}_{\omega}\circ\pi_{n}
. Notice
\mathbb{P}(\Omega_{0})=1
, and each
\ell^{(n)}_{\omega}
is a simple function in
\mathcal{A}^{*}
.

Now, let
\epsilon>0
,

, and
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
. By nuclearity, there exists

so that for all

, we have

. Furthermore, the following inequalities hold point-wise in

\displaystyle 0\leq|f_{\omega}(a)-\ell^{(n)}_{\omega}(a)|

\displaystyle\leq\|f_{\omega}\|\epsilon+|f_{\omega}\circ\iota_{n}\circ\pi_{n}(a)-\chi_{\Omega_{0}}(\omega)s^{(n,m_{n})}_{\omega}(\pi_{n}(a))|

\displaystyle\leq\epsilon\,F+\|f^{(n)}_{\omega}-s^{(n,m_{n})}_{\omega}\|_{R_{n}^{*}}\,\|\pi_{n}(a)\|\leq\epsilon\,F+2^{-n}\|a\|\,,

where the last inequality follows from the definition of

and the fact that ucp maps are completely contractive.

Now, suppose that
\Omega\ni\omega\mapsto\|f_{\omega}\|\in[0,\infty)
is not essentially bounded. Let
B_{k}=\{\omega\colon\|f_{\omega}\|\leq k\}
which are measurable. By applying the above argument to the random variable

, we find a sequence of simple functions

which almost surely approximate the truncation of

. Applying a diagonal argument once more, we can construct a sequence which approximates

almost surely in the weak-
\ast
topology.
∎

We restrict our focus to those weakly-
\ast

Notation A.7

\mathcal{A}

\mathcal{A}^{*}
is
positive
if
\varphi(a^{*}a)\geq 0
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
. Furthermore, the Russo-Dye theorem states that any positive linear functional attains its operator norm at the unit, namely
\|\varphi\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}=|\varphi(\mathbbm{1})|

[
54

.

A result originally due to Hanš
[
8
, Theorem 2.14]
ensures that if
\Phi_{\omega}
is a weakly-
\ast
-measurable random variable and
A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
then the composition
\omega\mapsto\Phi_{\omega}\circ A_{\omega}\in\mathbb{C}
is Borel measurable. Therefore, if
\Phi_{\omega}
is weakly-
\ast
measurable and takes values in
(\mathcal{A}^{*})_{+}
, it is essentially bounded in norm if and only if
0\leq\Phi(\mathbbm{1})\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})
. We will say that a function
A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
is positive if
A(\omega)\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
almost surely. Then an element
\Phi
of
[L^{1}(\mathcal{A})]^{*}
is
positive
if whenever
A\in(L^{1}(\mathcal{A}))_{+}
one has
\Phi\circ A\geq 0
with probability one.

In fact by definition, on a set with full probability
|\Phi_{\omega}(A_{\omega})|\leq\|\Phi_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\|A_{\omega}\|_{\mathcal{A}}
for arbitrary
\Phi
and
A
. A quick application of Hölder’s inequality yields the following lemma.

Lemma A.8
.

Let
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
be a probability space and
\mathcal{A}
be a separable
C^{*}
-algebra with a unit. If
\Phi\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{w^{*}}(\mathcal{A})
and
A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, then
\Phi\circ A\in L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
and the following estimate holds:

\left|\int_{\Omega}\Phi_{\omega}(A_{\omega})d\mathbb{P}\right|\leq\|\,\|\Phi\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\,\|A\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{A})}\,.

(A.4)

Therefore, the pairing
(\Phi,A):=\int_{\Omega}\Phi\circ Ad\mathbb{P}
defines a bounded bilinear pairing which is faithful on positive entries.

Recall the following elementary lemma from measure theory.

Lemma A.9
.

Suppose
(\Omega,\mathcal{F})
is a measurable space,
\nu
is a complex measure in
\Omega
and
\mu
is a finite positive measure on
\Omega
. The following are equivalent:

1.

The total variation
|\nu|\ll\mu
and the Radon-Nikodym derivative
\frac{d\mu}{d\lambda}\in L^{\infty}(\mu)
,

2.

there is a constant
C>0
so that
|\nu|(E)\leq C\mu(E)
for all
E\in\mathcal{F}
, and in particular
\|\frac{d\nu}{d\mu}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mu)}\leq C
.

Proposition A.10
.

Suppose
(\Omega,\mathcal{F},\mathbb{P})
is a probability space and
\mathcal{A}
is a separable
C^{*}
-algebra with a unit. Consider the Bochner space
L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
. Suppose
\Phi\in[L^{1}(\mathcal{A})]^{*}
is positive. Then there is a weak-
\ast
measurable function
G\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{w^{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
so that
\mathbb{P}[G_{\omega}(a^{*}a)\geq 0]=1
for all
a\in\mathcal{A}
and for any
A\in L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
, one has

\Phi(A)=\int_{\Omega}G_{\omega}(A(\omega))d\mathbb{P}\,.

(A.5)

In particular,
\|\,\|G\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\,\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\|\Phi\|_{L^{1}(\mathcal{A})^{*}}
.

Before we prove this result, a few remarks are in order.

Remark A.11
.

We emphasize that we have only identified
L^{1}(\mathcal{A})^{*}
with
\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{w^{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
in a weak-topological sense. To wit, there are examples of Banach spaces
\mathcal{X}
where the dual
L^{1}(\mathcal{X})^{*}
is strictly larger than essentially bounded functions, and hence is
not
isometric to
L^{\infty}(\mathcal{X})
. This is related to the question of whether or not a vector measure possesses a Radon-Nikodym derivative is a property of both the measure itself
and
the target Banach space. Banach spaces with the property that every vector measure admits an appropriate Radon-Nikodym derivative are said to possess the Radon Nikodym Property (RNP), and there are known Banach spaces for which this fails
[
59
]
.

Remark A.12
.

One might hope that the dual of a quasilocal algebra might have the RNP, but this is false. Work in the 1970’s by Jensen
[
30
,
31
]
, and later by Chu
[
13
, Theorem 3]
shows that the dual
\mathcal{B}^{*}
of a
C^{*}
algebra,
\mathcal{B}
, has the RNP if and only if
\mathcal{B}
is a so-called
scattered
C^{*}
-algebra
. Work by Kusada
[
35
, Theorem 2.2]
shows that a necessary condition for
\mathcal{B}
to be scattered is that it must be a type I
C^{*}
-algebra: meaning every nonzero irreducible representation of
\mathcal{B}
contains the compact operators
[
40
, p. 169]
. By contrast, most examples of quasi-local algebras in the mathematical physics literature are Uniformly Hyperfinite (UHF) algebras which are known to admit irreducible type
\mathrm{II}_{1}
representations (namely, the GNS-representation with respect to the unique tracial state, cf.
[
4
, Theorem 11.2.2]
). Thus, for many cases of interest, the dual of the quasilocal algebra fails to have the RNP, and one must check the individual probability measure for this property instead.

Proof of Proposition
A.10
.

Fix a positive element
a\in\mathcal{A}_{+}
and define the set function

\mathcal{F}\ni E\longmapsto\nu_{\Phi}(E;a):=\Phi(\chi_{E}\,a)\in[0,\infty)\,.

(A.6)

Then,
\nu_{\Phi}
is clearly finitely additive, and linear in the operator-entry. To see that
\nu_{\Phi}(\,\cdot\,;a)
is countably additive, recall that the countable additivity of
\mathbb{P}
can be rephrased as the
L^{1}(\mathbb{P})
convergence of characteristic functions: whenever
E=\bigsqcup E_{n}
, the limit
\|\chi_{E}-\sum_{1}^{N}\chi_{E_{n}}\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}\to 0
as
N\to\infty
. Therefore, the sequence of simple functions
\sum_{1}^{N}\chi_{E_{n}}a\to\chi_{E}a
in
L^{1}(\mathcal{A})
. In fact,

\left|\sum_{n=1}^{N}\nu_{\Phi}(E_{n};a)-\nu_{\Phi}(E;a)\right|\leq\|a\|\|\Phi\|\left\|\chi_{E}-\sum_{1}^{N}\chi_{E_{n}}\right\|_{L^{1}(\mathbb{P})}\,.

Now, notice that by construction

0\leq\nu_{\Phi}(E;a)\leq\|a\|\|\Phi\|\,\mathbb{P}[E],

whence
\nu_{\Phi}(\,\cdot\,;a)\ll\mathbb{P}
and by Lemma
A.9
, there is a positive scalar function
g_{a}=\frac{d\nu_{\Phi}(\cdot;a)}{d\mathbb{P}}\in L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})
so that

\nu_{\Phi}(E;a)=\int_{E}g_{a}(\omega)d\mathbb{P}\,.

Since the scalar Radon-Nikodym derivative is
\mathbb{P}
-essentially unique, the mapping
a\mapsto g_{a}(\omega)
may be extended to a linear mapping almost everywhere. Note we are crucially using separability of
\mathcal{A}
in order to identify the full probability event in
\Omega
where linearity holds. By Lemma
A.9
, there is a set
\Omega_{0}\subset\Omega
with full probability so that
|g_{a}(\omega)|\leq\|a\|\|\Phi\|
for all
\omega\in\Omega_{0}
since
\|g_{a}\|_{L^{\infty}(\mathbb{P})}\leq\|\Phi\|\|a\|
. By the Uniform Boundedness Principle, we conclude
\sup_{\omega\in\Omega_{0}}\|g_{\bullet}(\omega)\|_{\mathcal{A}^{*}}\leq\|\Phi\|
whence
g\in\mathcal{L}^{\infty}_{w^{*}}(\mathcal{A}^{*})
as required. Equation (
A.5
) is a straightforward application of approximation by simple functions.
