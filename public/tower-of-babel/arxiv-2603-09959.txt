A complete classification of 2d symmetry protected states with symmetric entanglers
Alex Bols, Wojciech De Roeck, Michiel De Wilde, Bruno de O. Carvalho

Abstract
We consider symmetry protected topological states of 2 ​ d 2d quantum spin systems, with a finite symmetry group G G . It has been conjectured that such states are classified by the cohomology group H 3 ​ ( G , U ​ ( 1 ) ) H^{3}(G,U(1)) , but the completeness of this classification is an open problem. We restrict ourselves to symmetry protected topological states that can be prepared from a product state by a symmetric entangler. For this class of states, we prove that the classification by H 3 ​ ( G , U ​ ( 1 ) ) H^{3}(G,U(1)) is complete.

The topology of gapped ground states of quantum lattice systems has grown into a well-developed subject. The present paper focuses on states that are intrinsically trivial, i.e. deformable to product states, but that can fail to be deformable to product states if an on-site global symmetry is required to hold. In Section

Setup and Main Result

2.1
Spin systems

A
d
-dimensional spin system assigns to all sites
j\in\Z^{d}
an on-site
d_{j}
-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}_{j}
with associated matrix algebra
\mathcal{A}_{j}\simeq\End(\C^{d_{j}})
. We assume that there is a
d_{\max}
such that
d_{j}\leq d_{\max}
for all
j\in\Z^{d}
. For finite
S\subset_{f}\Z^{d}
we can form the algebra
\mathcal{A}_{S}=\otimes_{j\in S}\mathcal{A}_{j}
of observables supported on
S
. If
S_{1}\subset S_{2}
are finite subsets of
\Z^{d}
then there is a natural inclusion
\mathcal{A}_{S_{1}}\hookrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{S_{1}}
given by tensoring with the identity of
\mathcal{A}_{S_{2}\setminus S_{1}}
. This makes the
\{\mathcal{A}_{S}\}_{S\subset_{f}\Z^{d}}
into a directed system of
\rm C^{*}
-algebras whose limit
\mathcal{A}
is the quasi-local algebra of the spin system. For any
X\subset\Z^{2}
(possibly infinite), the subalgebra
\mathcal{A}_{X}\subset\mathcal{A}
of observables supported on
X
is the direct limit of
\{\mathcal{A}_{S}\}_{S\subset_{f}X}
. We refer to standard references
[
34
,
35
,
36
,
37
,
38
]
for more background and details.

We emphasize that the assignment
\mathcal{A}_{j}
of on-site algebras is part of the data of what we mean by ‘spin system’. Later, we will encounter cases where a quasi-local algebra
\mathcal{A}
is cast as a spin system in two different ways, formally
\mathcal{A}=\otimes_{j}\mathcal{A}_{j}=\otimes_{j}\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{j}
where the subalgebras
\mathcal{A}_{j},\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\prime}
are not identical. Nevertheless, we will often denote a spin system by its quasi-local algebra when the assignment of on-site algebras is clear from context. If
d=1
, we will usually refer to spin systems as
spin chains
.

For any
X\subset\Z^{d}
we write
X^{(r)}:=\{j\in\Z^{d}\,:\,\dist(j,X)\leq r\}

for the
r
-fattening
of
X
.
For simplicity, we consistently use the terminology
automorphism, isomorphism, etc.
for
∗
-automorphisms,
∗
-isomorphisms, etc. between
C^{*}
-algebras. We denote by
\textrm{Aut}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{A}}}\right)
the automorphism group of
\mathcal{A}
.

2.1.1
Stacking of spin systems

Given a pair of spin systems
\mathcal{A},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
, we consider the
stacked
spin system

\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\prime},

whose on-site algebras are
\mathcal{A}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\prime}
, corresponding to the on-site Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{H}^{\prime}_{j}
.

2.2
Quantum cellular automata and finite depth quantum circuits

A
quantum cellular automaton
(QCA) on a spin system
\mathcal{A}
is an automorphism
\alpha\in\textrm{Aut}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{A}}}\right)
for which there exists
r\geq 0
such that
\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{X})\subset\mathcal{A}_{X^{(r)}}
for any
X\subset\Z^{d}
. The
range
of a QCA is the smallest
r
for which this holds. The inverse of a QCA of range
r
is also a QCA of range
r
(
[
39
, Lemma 3.1]
), a fact which we will use without comment throughout the paper. The quantum cellular automata on
\mathcal{A}
therefore form a subgroup of
\textrm{Aut}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{A}}}\right)
which we denote by
\QCA(\mathcal{A})
. The notation
(\alpha,\mathcal{A})
is used to denote a QCA
\alpha
acting on the spin system
\mathcal{A}
. We denote by
(\mathrm{id},\mathcal{A})
the identity automorphism on
\mathcal{A}
.

A
block
is a subset
I=[m_{1},n_{1}]\times\dots\times[m_{d},n_{d}]\subset\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, for integers
m_{i}\leq n_{i}
,
i=1,\ldots,d
. We write
\absolutevalue{I}
for the cardinality of
I
. Let
\{I_{a}\}_{a\in\Z^{d}}
be a partition of
\Z^{d}
into blocks
I_{a}\subset\Z^{d}
with
\max_{a}|I_{a}|<\infty
. Suppose we have for each
a\in\Z^{d}
a unitary
V_{a}\in\mathcal{A}_{I_{a}}
. Then we can define a QCA
(\gamma,\mathcal{A})
by the infinite product

\gamma=\bigotimes_{a\in\Z^{d}}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V_{a}}}\right).

(4)

This yields a well-defined automorphism, as one can first define its action on
\mathcal{A}_{X}
for any finite
X\subset\Z^{d}
and then extend by density.
Any QCA of this form is called a
block partitioned
QCA. The unitaries
V_{a}
are called the
gates
of the block partitioned QCA. A
depth
n
quantum circuit
is a composition
\gamma=\gamma_{n}\circ\gamma_{n-1}\circ\dots\circ\gamma_{1}
of
n
block partitioned QCAs
\{\gamma_{i}\}_{i=1}^{n}
. The block partitioned QCA
\gamma_{i}
is called the
i^{\rm{th}}

layer
of the circuit. A
finite depth quantum circuit
(FDQC)
(\gamma,\mathcal{A})
is a QCA that can be cast as a depth
n
quantum circuit on
\mathcal{A}
, for some
n<\infty
.

2.3
Symmetries

Let
G
be a finite group that is fixed throughout the paper, and let
e\in G
denote the identity element. A
locality preserving
G
-symmetry
(LPS)
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
on
\mathcal{A}
is a group homomorphism
\beta:G\rightarrow\QCA(\mathcal{A})
. That is, for each
g\in G
we have a quantum cellular automaton
\beta_{g}
such that
\beta_{e}=\mathrm{id}
and
\beta_{g}\beta_{h}=\beta_{gh}
for all
g,h\in G
. The range of a locality preserving symmetry is the largest range of its component QCAs. A particular case of LPS is an
on-site symmetry
, which is an LPS of the form

\beta_{g}=\otimes_{j}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{j}(g)}}\right)

with
g\mapsto U_{j}(g)
a unitary representation of
G
on
\mathcal{H}_{j}
for each
j\in\Z^{d}
.

We denote by
\Sym_{G,d}
the set of LPSs on spin systems of dimension
d
.

2.3.1
Stable equivalence of LPSs

Two LPSs
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A})
are
equivalent
if there is an FDQC
(\gamma,\mathcal{A})
such that

\beta^{\prime}_{g}=\gamma^{-1}\circ\beta_{g}\circ\gamma

(5)

holds for all
g\in G
.
Locality preserving symmetries
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
can be
stacked
into an LPS (recall Subsection
2.1.1
)
(\beta\otimes\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
. Two LPSs
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
are
stably equivalent
, denoted as

(\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}),

if there exist on-site symmetries
(\delta,\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\delta^{\prime},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
such that
(\beta\otimes\delta,\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})\text{ and }(\beta^{\prime}\otimes\delta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})

are equivalent. In particular, this requires that the local algebras
\mathcal{A}_{j}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}_{j}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}_{j}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}_{j}
are isomorphic.

Stable equivalence is an equivalence relation on
\Sym_{G,d}
. The quotient
(\Sym_{G,d}/\sim)
is an abelian monoid with multiplication given by stacking. The identity element is the equivalence class of on-site symmetries.

2.4
Symmetric entanglers

A
symmetric entangler

(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
is an FDQC
(\alpha,\mathcal{A})
that is
G
-equivariant
with respect to an on-site symmetry
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
, i.e.

\alpha\circ\beta_{g}=\beta_{g}\circ\alpha,\qquad\forall g\in G.

We denote by
\sEnt_{G,d}
the set of all symmetric entanglers on
d
-dimensional spin systems. A more restricted class of symmetric entanglers are
FDQCs with symmetric gates
. More precisely,
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
is an FDQC with symmetric gates if
\alpha
can be represented by a circuit each of whose layers has gates
\{U_{a}\}
that satisfy
\beta_{g}(U_{a})=U_{a}
for all
g\in G
.

2.5
Classification of symmetric entanglers

We say two symmetric entanglers
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A}),\ (\alpha^{\prime},\,\beta,\mathcal{A})
are equivalent if there exists an FDQC
(\gamma,\beta,\mathcal{A})
with symmetric gates such that

\alpha\circ\gamma=\alpha^{\prime}.

(6)

This equivalence relation is symmetric, because it implies that
\alpha=\alpha^{\prime}\circ\gamma^{-1}
and
\gamma^{-1}
is also an FDQC with symmetric gates.

Two symmetric entanglers
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
can be
stacked
into a new symmetric entangler (recall Subsection
2.1.1
)

(\alpha\otimes\alpha^{\prime},\beta\otimes\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\prime}).

We define also the notion of
stable equivalence
: two symmetric entanglers
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
are stably equivalent:

(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})

if there are on-site symmetries
(\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
such that
(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\alpha^{\prime}\otimes\mathrm{id},\beta^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
are equivalent.

The stacking operation makes the sets
\sEnt_{G,d}
and
(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)

into monoids. One of the main results of the present paper is the identification of
(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)
.
We write
H^{n}(G,U(1))
for the
n^{\mathrm{th}}
cohomology group of
G
with coefficients in
U(1)
, see Appendix
A
. Then

Proposition 2.1

(Classification of symmetric entanglers)
.

For
d=0,1,2
, the monoid
(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)
is a group and there is a group isomorphism

\mathrm{ind}:(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)\,\simeq\,H^{d+1}(G,U(1)).

(7)

The case
d=0
is Lemma
C.2
in Appendix
C
. The case
d=1
been proven by
[
28
,
29
]
, and we review the proof in Section
3
. The proof of the Proposition for
d=2
is the subject of Section
4
and it constitutes the bulk of the present paper.
The construction of a group homomorphism
(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)\,\to\,H^{d+1}(G,U(1))
can be found in the literature, and its surjectivity was at least suggested. The injectivity is the novel part.

Remark 2.2
.

The index
\mathrm{ind}:(\sEnt_{G,d}/\sim)\rightarrow H^{d+1}(G,U(1))
will be defined in each case
d=0,1,2
as the quotient of an index map
\mathrm{ind}:\sEnt_{G,d}\rightarrow H^{d+1}(G,U(1))
defined on individual symmetric entanglers. Lemma

together with Proposition

, shows that for

the index

is a group homomorphism, where
\sEnt_{G,d}(\mathcal{A},\beta)
is the group of symmetric entanglers on a fixed spin system
\mathcal{A}
with fixed on-site symmetry
\beta
, with group operation given by composition.

Lemma 2.3
.

Let
(\alpha_{1},\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\alpha_{2},\beta,\mathcal{A})
be symmetric entanglers on a
d
-dimensional spin system
\mathcal{A}
. Then

(\alpha_{2}\circ\alpha_{1},\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2},\beta\otimes\beta,\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}).

Proof.

For each site
j\in\Z^{d}
, let
\Omega_{j}\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H}_{j}\otimes\mathcal{H}_{j})
be the swap unitary, uniquely determined up to phase by
the property that
\Omega_{j}(A\otimes B)\Omega_{j}^{*}=B\otimes A
for all
A,B\in\mathcal{A}_{j}
.

We have
(\beta_{g}\otimes\beta_{g})(\Omega_{j})=\Omega_{j}
for all
j\in\Z^{d}
and all
g\in G
so

\big(\gamma:=\otimes_{j\in\Z^{d}}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\Omega_{j}}}\right),\beta\otimes\beta,\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}\big)

is a FDQC with symmetric gates. Since
\alpha_{2}\otimes\mathrm{id}=\gamma\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\alpha_{2})
we find

\alpha_{2}\circ\alpha_{1}\sim\alpha_{2}\circ\alpha_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id}=\gamma\circ(\mathrm{id}\otimes\alpha_{2})\circ(\alpha_{1}\otimes\mathrm{id})=\gamma\circ(\alpha_{1}\otimes\alpha_{2})

as required.

2.6
Symmetry protected states

A
symmetry protected topological (SPT) phase
is a stable equivalence class of symmetric short-range entangled states, which we define now.

We say a state
\psi
on
\mathcal{A}
is
G
-invariant
with respect to an on-site symmetry
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
if
\psi\circ\beta_{g}=\psi
for all
g\in G
. For simplicitly, when this condition is met for some fixed on-site symmetry that is clear from the context, we will say that
\psi
is
G
-invariant.

2.6.1
Symmetric short-range entangled states

A
product state

\phi
on a spin system
\mathcal{A}
is a pure state that satisfies

\phi(AB)=\phi(A)\phi(B)

for any
A,B\in\mathcal{A}
supported on disjoint subsets of
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Any product state
\phi
on
\mathcal{A}
restricts to pure states on on-site algebras
\mathcal{A}_{j}
represented by unit vectors
\ket{\phi_j}\in\mathcal{H}_{j}
, so that
\phi(\cdot)=\otimes_{j}\langle\phi_{j}|\cdot|\phi_{j}\rangle
. If
\phi
is moreover
G
-invariant under an on-site symmetry
g\mapsto\otimes_{j\in\Z^{d}}U_{j}(g)
then
U_{j}(g)\ket{\phi_j}=q_{j}(g)\ket{\phi_j}
, for on-site
G
-charges

q_{j}\in\hom(G,U(1))
. A
special

G
-invariant product state is a
G
-invariant product state satisfying
q_{j}\equiv 1
for every
j\in\Z^{d}
.

We restrict ourselves to short-range entangled symmetric states that can be prepared by a symmetric entangler from an arbitrary
G
-invariant product state
\phi
on
\mathcal{A}
.
That is,

\psi_{\alpha}:=\phi\circ\alpha

for a symmetric entangler
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
. Any triple
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})
with
\psi_{\alpha}
of this form is called a
G
-state
. If
\psi_{\alpha}
is moreover a (special) product state then we call
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})
a (special) product
G
-state. We denote by
\sSta_{G,d}
the set of
G
-states in dimension
d
.

2.6.2
Stable equivalence of
G
-states

Two
G
-states
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta,\mathcal{A})
are
equivalent
if there exists an FDQC
(\gamma,\beta,\mathcal{A})
with symmetric gates such that

\psi_{\alpha}=\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\circ\gamma.

Similarly, two
G
-states
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
and
(\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta,\mathcal{A})
are
stably equivalent
,

(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}),

if there exist special product
G
-states
(\phi,\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\phi^{\prime},\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
such that
(\psi_{\alpha}\otimes\phi,\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\psi_{\alpha^{\prime}}\otimes\phi^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
are equivalent.
The following lemma shows how to leverage knowledge on the symmetric entanglers to classify the corresponding
G
-states.

Lemma 2.4
.

If
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\mathrm{id},\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
, then the
G
-state
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})
is stably equivalent to a special product
G
-state.

Proof.

By assumption, there are on-site symmetries
(\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
such that

(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
and
(\mathrm{id},\beta^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\beta}^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime})
are equivalent. Let
\tilde{\beta}_{g}
be given as a tensor product
\tilde{\beta}_{g}:=\otimes_{i}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{i}(g)}}\right)
, and define an on-site symmetry
(\overline{\beta},\overline{\mathcal{A}})
as follows :
\overline{\mathcal{A}}
is a copy of
\tilde{\mathcal{A}}
equipped with the on-site symmetry
\overline{\beta}_{g}=\otimes_{i}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left({\overline{U}_{i}({g})}}}\right)
, the
complex conjugate representation
associated to
U_{i}
. A standard result asserts that
U_{i}\otimes\bar{U}_{i}
contains a trivial one-dimensional subrepresentation. This means that there exists a special product
G
-state
(\tilde{\phi},\tilde{\beta}\otimes\overline{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{A}})
. Moreover,

(\psi_{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\phi},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta}\otimes\overline{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{A}})\sim(\phi\otimes\tilde{\phi},\beta^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\beta}^{\prime}\otimes\overline{\beta},\mathcal{A}^{\prime}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}^{\prime}\otimes\overline{\mathcal{A}}).

Finally, since all product
G
-states are stably equivalent (see Lemma
C.5
), the claim is proven.
∎

2.6.3
Classification of
G
-states

A
symmetry protected topological (SPT) phase with symmetric entanglers
in dimension
d
is a stable equivalence class of
G
-states. We denote the set of such phases by

\sSPT_{G,d}:=(\sSta_{G,d}/\sim).

It has the structure of a commutative monoid with product operation given by stacking. It is a submonoid of
\mathsf{SPT_{G,d}}
, the monoid of symmetry protected topological phases, consisting of stable equivalence classes of symmetric short-range entangled states in
d
dimensions. In dimension
d=1
, the monoid
\mathsf{SPT}_{G,1}
has been completely classified
[
25
,
5
,
26
,
27
]
.

Theorem 2.5

(Classification of 2d SPTs with symmetric entanglers)
.

There is a group isomorphism

\sSPT_{G,2}\simeq H^{3}(G,U(1)).

(8)

Proof.

Let
(\psi_{\alpha},\beta,\mathcal{A})
be a 2d
G
-state. We define
\mathrm{ind}(\psi_{\alpha}):=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)
. By Lemma
2.4
and Proposition
2.1
, it is a well-defined bijective homomorphism.
∎

It remains an open question whether all 2d symmetric short-range entangled states can be prepared from a product state by a symmetric entangler.

3
Classification of 1d symmetric entanglers

It is well known that equivariant QCAs on spin chains with global symmetry group
G
are completely classified up to stable equivalence by their GNVW index
[
40
]
and a
H^{2}(G,U(1))
-valued invariant
[
29
,
28
]
. In our application, we run into the problem of disentangling some equivariant FDQCs, which by definition have trivial GNVW index. Thus, their classification is entirely cohomological:

Lemma 3.1
.

The monoid
(\sEnt_{G,1}/\sim)
is a group, and there is a group isomorphism

(\sEnt_{G,1}/\sim)\simeq H^{2}(G,U(1)).

(9)

This Lemma is the
1
-dimensional case of Proposition
2.1
. The proof of Lemma
3.1
follows from the combination of Lemmas
3.4
,
3.5
, and
3.7
, which are to be proven in the following subsections.

3.1
An index for 1d symmetric entanglers

Let
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
be a symmetric entangler in
d=1
, with range
R(\alpha)
.

3.1.1
The boundary algebra

Choose an FDQC representation of
\alpha
and let
r\geq nq
where
n
is the depth of the circuit and
q
is the maximum (over blocks and over layers of the quantum circuit) diameter of the blocks.

We define the half-lines
L_{x}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ i<x\}
, and boundary regions

\partial_{r}L_{x}:=\{i\in\mathbb{Z}\ |\ x-r\leq i<x\}.

(10)

The corresponding
boundary algebra

\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
associated to
\alpha
is defined as (c.f.
[
39
,
41
]
)

\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}:=\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}^{\prime}

(11)

where the prime denotes the commutant in
\mathcal{A}
. This object clearly depends on
\alpha
, but we choose to not indicate this dependence in the notation.

We now give an alternative construction of the boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
in terms of the FDQC representation. Recall from Subsection
2.2
that, for an FDQC representation
\alpha=\alpha_{n}\circ\ldots\circ\alpha_{1}
, each block partitioned QCA
\alpha_{i}
is called its
i^{\mathrm{th}}
layer. Define the FDQC
(\eta_{x},\mathcal{A})
to be composed of gates in
\alpha
as follows:

\eta_{x}
L_{x-r}
\partial_{r}L_{x}
L_{x}^{\text{c}}
L_{x}

Figure 2
:
An illustration of a depth 2 FDQC representation of
\alpha
. The gates comprising
\eta_{x}
are shown in blue.

•

In the first layer of
\alpha
, we retain gates whose support intersects both
L_{x}
and
{L_{x}}^{c}
. These gates compose the first layer of
\eta_{x}
. Let
B_{1}
be the union of the supports of all those gates.

•

For
i=2,\ldots,n
, the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
layer of
\eta_{x}
consists of all gates of the FDQC representation of
\alpha
whose support intersects
B_{i-1}
, and we call the union of
B_{i}
with the supports of these new gates
B_{i}
.

This construction is illustrated by Figure
2
and yields

\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})=\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})=\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{r}L_{x}})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}=\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}},

(12)

where first and second equalities follow by construction of
\eta_{x}
and the last equality follows from the definition (
11
).

Lemma 3.2
.

The boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
is
G
-invariant, i.e.
\beta_{g}(\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x})=\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
for all
g\in G
.

Proof.

Since the action
\beta
is on-site,
\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}^{\prime}=\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}^{c}}
is
G
-invariant. Moreover,
[\alpha,\beta_{g}]=0
, and hence
\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})
is also
G
-invariant.
∎

The boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
is therefore isomorphic to a symmetric finite-dimensional full matrix algebra
\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{r}L_{x}})
contained in the finite-dimensional algebra
\mathcal{A}_{[-r,r]}
. Every automorphism of a full matrix algebra is inner, so there is a collection of unitaries
\{W_{x}^{[r]}(g)\}_{g\in G}\subset\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}
such that

\beta_{g}(\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x})=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(W_{x}^{[r]}(g)}}\right)(\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}),\qquad\qquad W_{x}^{[r]}(g)W_{x}^{[r]}(h)=\mu_{x}^{[r]}(g,h)W_{x}^{[r]}(gh),

(13)

for a 2-cocycle
\mu_{x}^{[r]}\in Z^{2}(G,U(1))
(see Appendix
A.1
for a discussion on projective representations). The unitaries
W_{x}^{[r]}(g)
are unique up to a choice of
U(1)
phases. We define the
index
of
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
as

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=[\mu_{x}^{[r]}]\in H^{2}(G,U(1)).

(14)

Lemma 3.3
.

The index map (
14
) is well defined. That is, the class
[\mu_{x}^{[r]}]

1.

does not depend on the choice of
r\geq nq
made when defining the boundary (
10
);

2.

does not depend on the FDQC representation of
\alpha
;

3.

does not depend on
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Proof.

1.

Let
r_{0}
be a positive integer. Since
\eta_{x}|_{\mathcal{A}_{[-r-r_{0},-r-1]}}=\mathrm{id}
, it follows that

\mathcal{P}^{[r+r_{0}]}_{x}=\mathcal{P}^{[r]}_{x}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{[-(r+r_{0}),-r-1]}.

Consequently,

W_{x}^{[r+r_{0}]}(g)=c(g)W_{x}^{[r]}(g)\otimes U_{[-r-r_{0},-r-1]}(g)

for phases
c(g)\in U(1)
. Since
g\to U_{i}(g)
are linear representations, we get
[\mu_{x}^{[r+r_{0}]}]=[\mu_{x}^{[r]}]
.

2.

The unitaries
W_{x}^{[r]}
are, up to
U(1)
phases, uniquely determined by the symmetry action on the boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
. Moreover, the boundary algebra, by definition, depends on the FDQC representation of
\alpha
only through the constraint
r\geq nq
. By item 1, the index does not depend on this choice.

3.

Let
x_{0},r_{0}
be positive integers such that
r_{0}>2r
and
x_{0}<r
. We compute

\displaystyle\mathcal{P}_{x+x_{0}}^{[r_{0}]}

\displaystyle=\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}-r_{0}}}^{\prime}

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})\otimes\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}})}}\right]\cap\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}-r_{0}}}^{\prime}

by simply using the definition. We proceed by using the commutativity between algebras
\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}})
and
\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}-r_{0}}}
, which follows from the assumptions on
x_{0}
and
r_{0}
. Thus we can continue:

\displaystyle=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left[\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}-r_{0}}}^{\prime}}}\right]\otimes\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}})

\displaystyle=\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r_{0}-x_{0}]}\otimes\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}}).

Since
\beta_{g}\circ\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}})=\alpha\circ\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U_{[x,x+x_{0})}(g)}}\right)(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x+x_{0}}\setminus L_{x}})
and
g\to U_{[x,x+x_{0})}(g)
is a linear representation, we obtain

[\mu_{x+x_{0}}^{[r_{0}]}]=[\mu_{x}^{[r_{0}-x_{0}]}].

The claim then follows from item 1 above.

∎

Lemma 3.4
.

The index (
14
) descends to a homomorphism of monoids

\mathrm{ind}:(\sEnt_{G,1}/\sim)\to H^{2}(G,U(1))

which we call ‘the index map’.

Proof.

Fix a symmetric entangler
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
.

1.

The index map is invariant w.r.t. composition with FDQCs with symmetric gates:
Firstly, notice that the index is manifestly locally computable: it depends on the projective action
g\to W_{x}^{[r]}(g)
on
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
, which depends only on the local action of
\alpha
on
\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{r}L_{x}}
. Let
(\gamma,\beta,\mathcal{A})
be an FDQC with symmetric gates, and denote by
(\gamma_{R{x}},\beta,\mathcal{A})
an FDQC with symmetric gates obtained from
\gamma
by dropping gates intersecting
L_{x}
. By local computability and independence of half-lines we obtain

\displaystyle\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha\circ\gamma_{R_{x}})=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha\circ\gamma).

2.

The index map is a homomorphism :
If
\alpha=\mathrm{id}
, then
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}=\mathcal{A}_{[x-r,x-1]}
and
W_{x}^{[r]}(g)=U_{[x-r,x-1]}(g)
. Triviality of its index follows from the fact that
\beta_{g}
is an on-site action, i.e., each
g\to U_{i}(g)
is a linear representation of
G
. Moreover, if two given symmetric entanglers
\alpha
and
\tilde{\alpha}
yield boundary algebras
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
and
\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{x}^{[r]}
with symmetry implemented by unitaries
\{W_{x}^{[r]}(g)\}\subset\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
and
\{\tilde{W}_{x}^{[r]}(g)\}\subset\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{x}^{[r]}
, then the stacked symmetric entangler yields boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{P}}_{x}^{[r]}
with symmetry implemented by
\{W_{x}^{[r]}(g)\otimes\tilde{W}_{x}^{[r]}(g)\}
. It follows that the index is additive with respect to stacking.

∎

3.2
Surjectivity of the index map in 1d

Lemma 3.5

(Surjectivity of the index map in 1d)
.

Given a class
[\mu]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
, there exists a 1d symmetric entangler
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
such that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=[\mu]
.

Proof.

For any 2-cocycle
[\mu]
, let
(V_{\mu},\mathcal{K}^{\mu})
be a projective unitary representation
g\mapsto V_{\mu}(g)
of
G
on a finite dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}^{\mu}
with
V_{\mu}(g)V_{\mu}(h)=\mu(g,h)V_{\mu}(g,h)
.

Let
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
be the spin chain with on-site degrees of freedom
\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\mu}\simeq\End(\mathcal{K}^{\mu})
. For each
j\in\Z
we then have a projective representation
V_{j,\mu}:G\rightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{A}_{j}^{\mu})
whose simultaneous action defines
\beta^{\mu}_{g}:=\otimes_{j}\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V_{j,\mu}(g)}}\right)
for each
g\in G
.

Consider the stacked chain

\mathcal{A}:=\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\bar{\mu}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
with on-site symmetry
\beta=\beta^{\mu}\otimes\beta^{\bar{\mu}}\otimes\mathrm{id}
. This is indeed an on-site symmetry because the on-site actions are given by conjugation with
U_{i}=V_{i,\mu}\otimes V_{i,\bar{\mu}}\otimes\I_{i}
, which is a unitary representation of
G
. Let
U_{[x,y]}
be the product
\prod_{x\leq i\leq y}U_{i}
.

Let
\alpha=S\otimes\mathrm{id}\otimes S^{*}
where
S
is the right shift on the spin chain
\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
. One easily verifies that
\alpha\circ\beta_{g}=\beta_{g}\circ\alpha
for all
g\in G
, and since the GNVW index of
\alpha
is trivial
[
40
]
, it is an FDQC of range
R(\alpha)=1
, and
nq=4
. Therefore,
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
is a symmetric entangler.

Let us now compute its index. Let us write
\mathcal{A}_{i}=\mathcal{A}_{i,1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{i,2}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{i,3}
for the decomposition of each on-site degree of freedom of
\mathcal{A}=\mathcal{A}^{\mu}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\bar{\mu}}\otimes\mathcal{A}^{\mu}
. With
L_{x}=\{i\in\Z\,:\,i<x\}
, as introduced before, we have

\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{0}})=\mathcal{A}_{L_{-1}}\otimes\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathcal{A}_{-1,1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{-1,2}}}\right)\otimes\mathcal{A}_{0,1}.

With the choice
r=4
the boundary algebra is
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{[r]}=\mathcal{A}_{[-4,-2]}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{-1,1}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{-1,2}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{0,1}
. The symmetry
\beta
restricts to
\mathcal{P}_{0}^{[r]}
as the projective representation
U_{[-4,-2]}\otimes V_{\bar{\mu}}\otimes V_{\mu}\otimes V_{\mu}
. We conclude that
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha)=[\mu]
.
∎

Symmetric blending in 1d

Auxiliary spin chain

3.3.2
Symmetric blending

Consider the symmetric entangler

(16)

3.4
Injectivity of the index map in 1d

Lemma 3.7
.

The index map
\mathrm{ind}:(\sEnt_{G,1}/\sim)\to H^{2}(G,U(1))

Proof.

The crux of the proof is to show that 1d symmetric entanglers with trivial index are stably equivalent to

Let

be a symmetric entangler in 1d such that
\mathrm{ind}{(\alpha)}=[1]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
, as in the previous section.
Lemma
3.6
gives us symmetric entanglers
(\theta_{x},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
satisfying (
17

We define symmetric entanglers

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha_{k},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}}}\right),\qquad k\in\mathbb{Z},

with
\alpha_{k}:=\theta_{3rk}^{-1}\circ\theta_{3r(k+1)}

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}|_{L_{3rk-r}}=\mathrm{id},\quad

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}|_{L_{3r(k+1)}^{c}}=\mathrm{id},

\displaystyle\alpha_{k}|_{\mathcal{A}_{[3rk+r,3r(k+1)-r]}}

\displaystyle=\alpha|_{\mathcal{A}_{[3rk+r,3r(k+1)-r]}}\otimes\mathrm{id}.

(18)

The formal infinite products

\displaystyle\bigg(\alpha_{\text{even}}=\prod_{k\in 2\mathbb{Z}}\alpha_{k},\,\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\,\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\bigg),

and

\displaystyle\bigg(\alpha_{\text{odd}}=(\alpha\otimes\mathrm{id})\circ\alpha_{\text{even}}^{-1},\,\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta},\,\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\bigg)

are symmetric block partitioned FDQCs of ranges bounded by

We now have

(19)

where each factor

is stably equivalent to
\mathrm{id}
by Lemma

. This means that
\alpha_{\rm{even}}\otimes\mathrm{id}
and

4
Classification of 2d symmetric entanglers

4.1
An index for 2d symmetric entanglers

Throughout this section we consider a symmetric entangler
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
of range
R(\alpha)
on a 2-dimensional spin system
\mathcal{A}=\otimes_{j\in\Z^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{j}
.

4.1.1
The boundary algebra

We construct boundary algebras associated to
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
which generalize the boundary algebras constructed in Section
3.1.1
for the one-dimensional case.

We choose an FDQC representation of
\alpha
and take
r\geq nq
where
n
is the depth of the circuit and
q
is the maximum (over blocks and over layers of the quantum circuit) of diameters of blocks.

For
x\in\Z
we let
L_{x}
be the half-plane

L_{x}=\{(i_{1},i_{2})\ |\ i_{1}<x\}\subset\Z^{2},

and we define define the boundary region

\partial_{r}L_{x}:=\{(i_{1},i_{2})\ |\ x-r\leq i_{1}<x\}.

(20)

The
boundary algebra
is defined as

\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}:=\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})\cap\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}^{\prime}.

(21)

Let
(\eta_{x},\mathcal{A})
be the FDQC build out of gates of the FDQC decomposition of
\alpha
as follows:

•

From the first layer of
\alpha
, we retain gates whose support intersects both
L_{x}
and
L_{x}^{c}
. These gates form the first layer of
\eta_{x}
. Let
B_{1}
be the union of the supports of all those gates.

•

For
i=2,\ldots,n
, the
i^{\mathrm{th}}
layer of
\eta_{x}
consists of all gates of the FDQC representation of
\alpha
whose support intersects
B_{i-1}
, and we call the union of
B_{i}
with the supports of these new gates
B_{i}
.

This construction is illustrated by Figure
2
. With this definition of
\eta_{x}
we get

\alpha(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})=\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}})=\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{\partial_{r}L_{x}})\otimes\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}=\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}\otimes\mathcal{A}_{L_{x-r}}.

(22)

By analogous reasoning as in Lemma
3.2
, the boundary algebras
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
are
\beta_{g}
-invariant.

For future use, we note that

\alpha|_{\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}}}=\eta_{x}|_{\mathcal{A}_{L_{x}}}\circ\alpha^{\prime}_{[x]}

(23)

where
\alpha^{\prime}_{[x]}
is the FDQC comprised of those gates of
\alpha
whose support overlaps with
L_{x}
and that were not chosen in
\eta_{x}
(the red gates in Figure
2
).

4.1.2
A locality preserving symmetry on the boundary algebra

We will consider spin chains oriented along the boundary
\partial_{r}L_{x}
. To that end, note that
\partial_{r}L_{x}
as defined in (
20
) is a strip. For the sake of this subsection, it will be advantageous to view this strip as having unit “width”, and so we put, for
y\in\mathbb{Z}
:

\mathcal{A}_{x,y}^{[r]}=\mathcal{A}_{\{(x-r,y),\ldots,(x-1,y)\}}

and define
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]}=\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{x,y}^{[r]})
(the algebras
\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]}
still depend on
\alpha
, which we omit for clarity of notation). Then

\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}=\otimes_{y}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]}

is a spin chain. Even though
\beta_{g}
was an on-site action on
\mathcal{A}
, it is not necessarily on-site when restricted to the spin chain
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
, since the notion of “sites” is different. Namely, in general,

\beta_{g}(\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]})=\beta_{g}\circ\eta_{x}(\mathcal{A}_{x,y}^{[r]})\not\subset\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]},

since
\eta_{x}
is not necessarily equivariant. However, each
\beta_{g}
acts as a QCA on
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}
, from which we obtain the following lemma:

Lemma 4.1
.

The family
\{\beta_{g}|_{\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}}\}_{g\in G}
is an LPS on the spin chain
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}=\otimes_{y}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]}
. The range of
(\beta,\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]})
is bounded above by
r
.

Recall that we write
\Sym_{G,1}
for the set of locality preserving symmetries on spin chains. In
[
33
]
an index
\Omega:\Sym_{G,1}\to H^{3}(G,U(1))
was defined which completely classifies LPSs on spin chains up to stable equivalence, see Proposition
B.3
in Appendix
B
. We will now show that
\Omega((\beta,\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}))
is a well-defined index for our symmetric entangler
\alpha
. We will often simply write
\beta
instead of
(\beta,\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]})
when it is clear from context that
\beta
is to be interpreted as an LPS on a boundary algebra.

4.1.3
An index for 2d symmetric entanglers

We define the index

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha):=\Omega(\beta)\in H^{3}(G,U(1))

(24)

of the symmetric entangler
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
as the index of the corresponding locality preserving symmetry
(\beta,\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]})
from Lemma
4.1
.

Lemma 4.2
.

The index is well-defined. That is, the class
\Omega(\beta)

1.

does not depend on the choice of
r\geq nq
, for fixed FDQC representation of
\alpha

2.

does not depend on the choice of FDQC representation for
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
;

3.

does not depend on
x\in\mathbb{Z}
.

Items 1 and 3 follow from analogous arguments as in the proof of Lemma
3.3

For item 2, let
\tilde{\eta}_{x}
be obtained from another FDQC representation of
\alpha
of depth
\tilde{n}
and maximal block diameter

4.3.1
Disentangling

on the spin chain

Recall that, by Lemma

, the boundary algebra
\mathcal{P}_{x}^{[r]}=\otimes_{y}\mathcal{P}_{x,y}^{[r]}
is a spin chain that carries an action of

.

The index map

Proof.

. Lemma
4.5
gives symmetric blendings

Define symmetric entanglers

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\alpha_{k}=\theta_{{3rk}}^{-1}\circ\theta_{3r(k+1)},\beta\otimes\delta\otimes\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}}}\right).

Appendix A
Group cohomology

For a comprehensive discussion on finite group cohomology, see e.g.
[
43
]
. Let
G
be a finite group. For any left G-module
M
, we denote by
C^{n}(G,M)
the set of functions from
G\times\dots\times G\to M
, where the product runs over
n
copies of
G
. Elements of
C^{n}(G,M)
are called
n-
cochains. The pair
(C^{n}(G,M),\cdot)
is a group for every
n
, so that co-boundary group homomorphisms can be defined as

\displaystyle d^{n+1}:\ C^{n}(G,M)

\displaystyle\to C^{n+1}(M,G)

\displaystyle\mu

\displaystyle\mapsto(d^{n+1}\mu),

given explicitly by

\displaystyle(d^{n+1}\mu)(g_{1},\dots,g_{n+1}):=

\displaystyle\mu(g_{2},\dots,g_{n+1})

\displaystyle+\sum\limits_{i=1}^{n}(-1)^{i}\mu(g_{1},\dots,g_{i}g_{i+1},\dots,g_{n+1})+(-1)^{n+1}\mu(g_{1},\dots,g_{n}),

(30)

where we are using additive notation for the abelian module operation. The map

d^{n+1}\circ d^{n}:\ C^{n}(G,M)\to C^{n+2}(G,M)

(31)

is identically zero, from which we conclude there is a cochain complex

\dots\leftarrow C^{n}\leftarrow C^{n-1}\leftarrow\dots,

with
n
-th cohomology group defined as

H^{n}(G,M):=\dfrac{Z^{n}(G,M)}{B^{n}(G,M)},

(32)

with

\displaystyle Z^{n}(G,M):=\ker(d^{n+1}),

\displaystyle B^{n}:=\begin{cases}0,\ &\text{if }n=0,\\
\text{im}(d^{n}),\ &\text{if }n\geq 1.\end{cases}

That is, the n-th cohomology group
H^{n}(G,M)
is the group of equivalence classes of n-cocycles (elements of
Z^{n}(G,M)
) with respect to equivalence modulo n-coboundaries (elements of
B^{n}(G,M)
).

A.1
Projective representations

We define a projective representation (
V,\mathcal{K}
) with 2-cocycle
\mu\in Z^{2}(G,U(1))
as a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{K}
, together with a map

V:\ G\longrightarrow\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{K})

such that
V(g)V(h)=\mu(g,h)V(gh)
. Two projective representations
(V,\mathcal{K})
,
(V^{\prime},\mathcal{K}^{\prime})
are isomorphic if there exists a unitary intertwiner
\mathfrak{U}:\mathcal{K}\to\mathcal{K}^{\prime}
such that

\mathfrak{U}\circ V(g)=V^{\prime}(g)\circ\mathfrak{U},

for all
g\in G
. By
[
44
, Corollary 3.8]
, two projective representations with the same 2-cocycle
\mu
and the same character are isomorphic. This implies

Lemma A.1
.

Let
(V,\mathcal{H})
,
(V^{\prime},\mathcal{H}^{\prime})
be two projective representations of the same dimension with the same 2-cocycle
\mu
. Then

(V\otimes\rho,\mathcal{H}\otimes\mathbb{C}[G])
is isomorphic to
(V^{\prime}\otimes\rho,\mathcal{H}^{\prime}\otimes\mathbb{C}[G]),

where
(\rho,\mathbb{C}[G])
is the regular representation of
G
.

Proof.

Let
\chi_{V},\chi_{V^{\prime}},\chi_{\rho}
denote the character functions of projective representations
V
,
V^{\prime}
, and
\rho
, respectively. Since
\chi_{\rho}(g)=\delta_{e,g}
, the result follows by
\chi_{V\otimes\rho}(g)=\chi_{V}(g)\cdot\chi_{\rho}(g)=\dim(V)\delta_{e,g}=\chi_{V^{\prime}\otimes\rho}(g).

∎

Lemma A.2

(Classification of 0d LPS)
.

There is a group isomorphism

\displaystyle\Omega:(\Sym_{G,0}/\sim)\to H^{2}(G,U(1)).

Proof.

A 0-dimensional locality preserving symmetry is a pair
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
, where the algebra
\mathcal{A}=\End{(\mathcal{H})}
is the full matrix algebra on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, and the symmetry action
\beta_{g}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U(g)}}\right)
for a unitary projective representation
g\to U(g)\in\mathcal{A}
of
G
.

The anomaly index
\Omega(\beta)
of
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
is defined as the projective class of
g\to U(g)
, and takes value in
H^{2}(G,U(1))
. For any 2-cocycle
\mu
, consider
\mathcal{A}=\End(\C[G])
and
\beta
given by
g\mapsto U(g)
with
U(g)\ket{h}={\mu(g,h)}\ket{gh}
. Then
\Omega(\beta)=[\mu]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
. Thus the index map is surjective.

Suppose now
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
and
(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
are 0-dimensional LPS with
\Omega(\beta)=\Omega(\beta^{\prime})=[\mu]
. By stacking with trivial representations, we may assume w.l.o.g. that
\mathcal{A}
and
\mathcal{A}^{\prime}
have the same dimension. Moreover, possibly by redefining phases, one can pick the projective representations so that they yield the same 2-cocycles (not just up to coboundaries). The stable equivalence
(\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\beta^{\prime},\mathcal{A}^{\prime})
now follows from Lemma
A.1
, showing injectivity.
∎

Appendix B
Locality preserving symmetries

We have defined (see
2.3.1
) equivalence of LPSs with respect to conjugation with FDQCs. By stabilization, this equivalence can be lifted to accommodate also QCAs, as stated in the following Lemma:

Lemma B.1
.

Let
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
be a LPS on a spin chain
\mathcal{A}
, and
(\gamma,\mathcal{A})
a QCA on
\mathcal{A}
. Then
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
is stably equivalent to
(\gamma\circ\beta\circ\gamma^{-1},\mathcal{A})
.

Proof.

By definition,

(\gamma\circ\beta\circ\gamma^{-1},\mathcal{A})\sim\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma\circ\beta\circ\gamma^{-1}}}\right)\otimes\mathrm{id},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma\otimes\gamma^{-1}}}\right)\circ\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta\otimes\mathrm{id}}}\right)\circ\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma^{-1}\otimes\gamma}}\right),\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A}}}\right).

But
(\gamma\otimes\gamma^{-1},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})
is an FDQC
[
40
]
, so

(\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma\otimes\gamma^{-1}}}\right)\circ\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\beta\otimes\mathrm{id}}}\right)\circ\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma^{-1}\otimes\gamma}}\right),\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})\sim(\beta\otimes\mathrm{id},\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{A})\sim(\beta,\mathcal{A}).

∎

B.1
Disentangling anomaly-free LPSs

LPSs on spin chains have been fully classified in terms of their anomaly index, taking values in the third group cohomology
H^{3}(G,U(1))
.

Recall that stable equivalence of LPSs was defined (see Section
2.3.1
) by allowing to stack with on-site symmetries. That is, range 0 symmetries with on-site actions that are linear representations of
G
. The following Lemma connects this definition with the completeness results in
[
33
,
42
]
.

Lemma B.2
.

If an LPS
(\beta,\mathcal{A})
of range
r
on a spin chain
\mathcal{A}
has a trivial anomaly index
\Omega(\beta)=[1]
, then there exists an on-site symmetry
(\delta,\mathcal{D})
, and an FDQC
(\gamma,\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D})
of range
\mathcal{O}(r)
so that

\gamma\circ(\beta\otimes\delta)\circ\gamma^{-1}

is an on-site symmetry.

Proof.

By
[
33
, Proposition 6.2]
, the statement holds without mention of linearity of the on-site actions. That is, there is a range 0 LPS
(\delta,\mathcal{D})
and a disentangling FDQC
(\gamma,\mathcal{A}\otimes\mathcal{D})
whose range is bounded by
\mathcal{O}(r)
so that
\gamma\circ(\beta\otimes\delta)\circ\gamma^{-1}

is a range 0 LPS acting projectively on each site.

Let
\overline{\delta}
denote the symmetry with conjugate on-site projective actions. Then
(\delta\otimes\overline{\delta},\mathcal{D}\otimes\mathcal{D})
is an on-site symmetry action, and

\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma\otimes\mathrm{id}}}\right)\circ(\beta\otimes\delta\otimes\overline{\delta})\circ\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma^{-1}\otimes\mathrm{id}}}\right)=\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(\gamma\circ(\beta\otimes\delta)\circ\gamma^{-1}}}\right)\otimes\overline{\delta}

is still on-site. Moreover, by Lemma
C.4
(any two 1d range 0 LPS are stably equivalent), there are on-site symmetry
\delta^{\prime}
and an equivariant FDQC
(\Delta,\mathcal{D}^{\prime})
such that

\Delta\circ(\gamma\circ(\beta\otimes\delta\otimes\overline{\delta})\circ\gamma^{-1}\otimes\delta^{\prime})\circ\Delta^{-1}=\Delta\circ\gamma\circ(\beta\otimes\delta\otimes\overline{\delta}\otimes\delta^{\prime})\circ\gamma^{-1}\circ\Delta^{-1}

is an on-site symmetry.
∎

By Lemma
B.2
, stable equivalence with respect to stacking with on-site symmetries is equivalent to stable equivalence with respect to stacking with range 0 symmetries. This result together with
[
33
, Theorem 2.1]
yields

Proposition B.3

(Classification of 1d LPSs)
.

There is a group isomorphism

\displaystyle\Omega:(\Sym_{G,1}/\sim)\to H^{3}(G,U(1)).

Appendix C
Eilenberg–Mazur swindle

C.1
H^{2}(G,U(1))
charges in 2d

Let
\mathcal{A}=\otimes_{j\in\Z^{2}}\mathcal{A}_{j}
be a two-dimensional spin system. Let
\{I_{i}\}_{i\in\Z}
be a disjoint family of discrete intervals of uniformly bounded size. Let
\alpha=\otimes_{i}\alpha_{I_{i}}
where the
\alpha_{I_{j}}
are FDQCs on
\mathcal{A}
of uniformly bounded range, whose gates are supported on the vertical strips
v_{j}=I_{j}\times\Z
. Any FDQC of this form is called a
striped FDQC
of
\mathcal{A}
. If the striped FDQC is moreover equivariant w.r.t. a global on-site symmetry, we call it a
striped symmetric entangler
.

Lemma C.1
.

Any two striped symmetric entanglers in 2d are stably equivalent.

Proof.

We prove that a striped symmetric entangler
\alpha=\otimes_{i}\alpha_{I_{i}}
is stably equivalent to the identity. Since the stripes
I_{i}
are disjoint, each
\alpha_{I_{i}}
is
G
-equivariant. By regrouping sites, we may assume that each
I_{i}
is a vertical line
I_{i}=\{i\}\times\mathbb{Z}
and write
\alpha_{I_{i}}=\alpha_{i}
.

By Lemma
3.1
there is a well-defined complete invariant
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i})\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
for each
\alpha_{i}
, seen as a 1-dimensional symmetric entangler on its support. Lemma
3.5
provides for each class
[\omega]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
a 1-dimensional symmetric entangler
(\alpha^{[\omega]},\beta^{[\omega]},\mathcal{A}^{[\omega]})
with
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{[\omega]})=[\omega]
. Since
H^{2}(G,U(1))
is finite, this set of entanglers is finite, and therefore has uniformly bounded local Hilbert space dimensions and uniformly bounded ranges.

We define an auxiliary striped symmetric entangler by

\displaystyle(\tilde{\alpha},\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}):=(\otimes_{i\geq 1}\alpha^{[\omega_{i}]},\otimes_{i\geq 1}\beta^{[\omega_{i}]},\otimes_{i\geq 1}\mathcal{A}^{[\omega_{i}]}),

(33)

where

1.

for odd
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle[\omega_{i}]=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i})+\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i-1})+\dots+\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{1})

(34)

2.

for even
i\in\mathbb{N}
,

\displaystyle[\omega_{i}]=-[\omega_{i-1}]=-\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i-1})-\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i-2})-\dots-\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{1}).

(35)

We have for any 2-cocycle
\omega
that
\alpha^{[\omega]}\otimes(\alpha^{-[\omega]})
is stably equivalent to
\mathrm{id}
as a 1-dimensional symmetric entangler. Define another auxiliary symmetric entangler
\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
by

(\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime},\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}):=(\otimes_{i\geq 1}\alpha^{-[\omega_{i}]},\otimes_{i\geq 1}\beta^{[\omega_{i}]},\otimes_{i\geq 1}\mathcal{A}^{[\omega_{i}]}),

then the symmetric entangler
(\tilde{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime},\tilde{\beta}\otimes\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
is stably equivalent to
(\mathrm{id}\otimes\mathrm{id},\tilde{\beta}\otimes\tilde{\beta},\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}})
. We therefore find

\displaystyle(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})\sim(\alpha\otimes\tilde{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime},\beta\otimes\tilde{\beta}\otimes\tilde{\beta},\mathcal{A}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}\otimes\tilde{\mathcal{A}}).

The right-hand side is still a striped symmetric entangler, and each strip
i\geq 1
now comes with index
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i})+[\omega_{i}]-[\omega_{i}]\in H^{2}(G,U(1))
. We represent the obtained symmetric entangler on the right half-plane by

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-[\omega_{1}]\\
[\omega_{1}]\\
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{1})\end{bmatrix}\qquad\otimes\qquad\begin{bmatrix}-[\omega_{2}]\\
[\omega_{2}]\\
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{2})\end{bmatrix}\qquad\otimes\qquad\begin{bmatrix}-[\omega_{3}]\\
[\omega_{3}]\\
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{3})\end{bmatrix}\qquad\dots

where each tensor factor represents a strip, with the corresponding stacked structure as described above.

Let
i\in 2\mathbb{N}
be even. The strips
i,i+1
carry indices

\displaystyle\begin{bmatrix}-[\omega_{i}]\\
[\omega_{i}]\\
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i})\end{bmatrix}\qquad\otimes\qquad\begin{bmatrix}-[\omega_{i+1}]\\
[\omega_{i+1}]\\
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i+1})\end{bmatrix}.

From definitions (
35
) and (
34
) we get

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i})+\mathrm{ind}(\alpha_{i+1})=[\omega_{i}]+[\omega_{i+1}],

so by the complete classification of 1-dimensional symmetric entanglers (Lemma
3.1
), the joint strip
\{i,i+1\}
is stably equivalent to a pair of neighbouring strips
\alpha^{[\omega_{i}]}\otimes\alpha^{[\omega_{i+1}]}
with indices

[\omega_{i}]\qquad\otimes\qquad[\omega_{i+1}].

This procedure can be carried out for each even-odd pair of strips. Moreover, the stacked spin chains necessary to realize the stable equivalences can be chosen to have uniformly bounded on-site dimensions, and the FDQCs with symmetric gates realizing the equivalences with
\mathrm{id}
have uniformly bounded ranges. This can be seen by inspecting the completeness proof of Lemma
3.1
(see in particular the constructions in Lemmas
3.6
and
3.7
). We therefore find that
\alpha\otimes\tilde{\alpha}\otimes\tilde{\alpha}^{\prime}
is stably equivalent to a striped 2-dimensional symmetric entangler with a strip charge configuration on the right half-plane as follows:

[\omega_{1}]\qquad\otimes\qquad[\omega_{2}]\qquad\otimes\qquad[\omega_{3}]\qquad\dots

For each odd
i\in\mathbb{N}

[\omega_{i}]\qquad\otimes\qquad[\omega_{i+1}].

By definition (Eqs. (
34
), (
35
)) we have
[\omega_{i}]+[\omega_{i+1}]=0
, so the entangler restricted to each joint strip

is stably equivalent to an identity entangler by Lemma
3.1
.

Again, the required stacking has uniformly bounded on-site Hilbert spaces dimensions, and the FDQCs with symmetric gates realizing the equivalence with
\mathrm{id}
have uniformly bounded ranges. From this, we conclude that
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
is stably equivalent to its own restriction to the left half-plane, all the striped symmetric entanglers on the right half-plane having been removed by the above construction.

The same construction can now be repeated to remove all the remaining striped symmetric entanglers on the left half-plane. We conclude that
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
is stably equivalent to the identity.
∎

C.2
Triviality of block partitioned symmetric entanglers

Let
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
be a block partitioned 1d symmetric entangler. Without loss of generality, assume that
\alpha
is factorized. That is,
\alpha
leaves each on-site algebra
\mathcal{A}_{i}
invariant and restricts there to an equivariant automorphism
\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{i}}}\right):\mathcal{A}_{i}\to\mathcal{A}_{i}
, for some unitary
v_{i}\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
. Equivariance of
\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v_{i}}}\right)
means that

\beta_{g}(v_{i})\beta_{g}(x)\beta_{g}(v_{i}^{*})=v_{i}\beta_{g}(x)v_{i}^{*}

for all
x\in\mathcal{A}_{i}
. This implies that there are phases
\lambda_{i}(g)\in U(1)
such that
\beta_{g}(v_{i})=\lambda_{i}(g)v_{i}
. The fact that
g\mapsto\beta_{g}
is a group homomorphism implies that
g\mapsto\lambda_{i}(g)
is a 1-cocycle. That is,
v_{i}
transforms as a one-dimensional representation
\lambda_{i}
of
G
. One might think that these on-site “charges” are obstructions to deforming
\alpha
to
\mathrm{id}
via an FDQC with symmetric gates. We argue in Lemma
C.3
below that this is not the case by showing that these charges can be pumped to infinity using a swindle argument.

The proof is completely analogous to the one of Lemma
C.1
, except that the role of the 1-dimensional symmetric entanglers with
H^{2}(G,U(1))
-valued index will now be played by 0-dimensional symmetric entanglers with
H^{1}(G,U(1))
-valued index. Let us prove the required complete classification of such symmetric entanglers (the case
d=0
of Proposition
2.1
):

Lemma C.2
.

The monoid
(\sEnt_{G,0}/\sim)
is a group and there is a group isomorphism

\mathrm{ind}:(\sEnt_{G,0})\simeq H^{1}(G,U(1)).

Proof.

A 0-dimensional symmetric entangler is a triple
(\alpha,\beta,\mathcal{A})
where the algebra
\mathcal{A}=\End{(\mathcal{H})}
is the full matrix algebra on a finite-dimensional Hilbert space
\mathcal{H}
, the on-site symmetry
\beta
is of the form
\beta_{g}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U(g)}}\right)
for a unitary representation
g\mapsto U(g)\in\mathcal{A}
of
G
, and
\alpha=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v}}\right)
for a unitary
v\in\mathcal{A}
, unique up to phase, such that
\beta_{g}\circ\alpha=\alpha\circ\beta_{g}
for all
g\in G
.

The latter condition implies that
\beta_{g}(v)=\lambda(g)v
for a 1-cocycle
\lambda:G\rightarrow U(1)
. The index is defined by

\mathrm{ind}(\alpha):=[\lambda]\in H^{1}(G,U(1)).

Note that in
H^{1}(G,U(1))
, the equivalence relation defining classes is trivial, i.e. if
[\lambda]=[\mu]\in H^{1}(G,U(1))
then
\lambda=\mu
are equal 1-cocycles.

Let us first argue that this index map is surjective. Given a 1-cocycle
\lambda
, let
\mathcal{H}=\C[G]=\rm{span}\{\ket{g}\,:g\in G\}
and let
\beta
be the regular representation. Let
v\in\mathcal{U}(\mathcal{H})
be defined by
v\ket{h}=\lambda(h)^{-1}\ket{h}
and
\alpha=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v}}\right)
. Then one easily checks that
\beta_{g}(v)=\lambda(g)v
. This proves surjectivity.

Suppose now
(\alpha^{(i)}=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(v^{(i)}}}\right),\beta^{(i)}=\{\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(U^{(i)}(g)}}\right)\}_{g\in G},\mathcal{A}^{(i)}=\End(\mathcal{H}^{(i)}))_{i=1,2}
are two 0-dimensional symmetric entanglers with the same index
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{(1)})=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{(2)})=[\lambda]
. That is,
\beta^{(i)}_{g}(v^{(i)})=\lambda(g)v^{(i)}
for all
g\in G
and
i=1,2
.

By stacking with trivial representations of
G
of appropriate dimensions and extending
v\rightarrow v\otimes\I
we can assume without loss of generality that
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}
and
\mathcal{H}^{(2)}
have the same dimension. Further stacking with the regular representation of
G
and extending
v\rightarrow v\otimes\I
we get from Lemma
A.1
that there is a unitary isomorphism
\Phi:\mathcal{H}^{(1)}\otimes\C[G]\rightarrow\mathcal{H}^{(2)}\otimes\C[G]
which intertwines the symmetries
\beta^{(1)}\otimes\beta^{\rm{reg}}
and
\beta^{(2)}\otimes\beta^{\rm{reg}}
. This means that, after stacking, we may assume without loss of generality that our 0-dimensional symmetric entanglers act on the same Hilbert spaces
\mathcal{H}^{(1)}=\mathcal{H}^{(2)}=\mathcal{H}
with the same
G
-actions
\beta^{(1)}=\beta^{(2)}=\beta
.

In that case, note that the unitary
V:=v^{(2)}(v^{(1)})^{*}
is symmetric :
\beta_{g}(V)=V
for all
g\in G
. Moreover,
Vv^{(1)}=v^{(2)}
, in other words,
\alpha^{(2)}=\gamma\circ\alpha^{(1)}
where
\gamma=\textrm{Ad}\mathopen{}\mathclose{{\left(V}}\right)
is a FDQC with symmetric gate
V
. That is,
\alpha^{(1)}
and
\alpha^{(2)}
are equivalent as 0-dimensional symmetric entanglers. Since we used stacking to reduce to the case where both entanglers act on the same Hilbert space with the same
G
-action, we conclude that any
\alpha^{(1)}
and
\alpha^{(2)}
with
\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{(1)})=\mathrm{ind}(\alpha^{(2)})
are stably equivalent. This proves the Lemma.
∎
