Project Overview and Objectives

The overarching goal of this project was to develop a data-driven materials discovery framework that
explicitly incorporates structure motifs and crystal and orbital symmetries into machine-learning models for
functional materials. The project aimed to move beyond conventional crystal graph descriptions by
embedding physically meaningful motif and symmetry information into graph-based learning architectures,
thereby improving both predictive accuracy and physical interpretability for electronic structures and related
materials properties. These methodological advances were intended to establish new hypotheses and design
rules for materials discovery and, when combined with high-throughput density functional theory
calculations, to accelerate the identification of light absorbers, transparent conductors, and two-dimensional
light-emitting materials and heterojunctions for optoelectronic applications.

Major Accomplishments and Scientific Highlights

Along the proposed research directions, we have made the following scientific achievements: (1)
Incorporation of structure motifs in machine learning frameworks for solid-state systems through
hypergraphs and material-motif networks. (2) Development of equivariant neural networks for the accurate
prediction of tensors and tensorial spectra. (3) Development of deep learning models for electronic structures
of materials based on an end-to-end approach as well as Hamiltonian learning. (4) Development of
computational frameworks for predicting complex properties in disordered systems based on graph neural
networks and Monte Carlo simulation. (5) Incorporation of physical constraints in machine learning-based
density functional design. (6) Collaborative studies of excited-state properties in two-dimensional in-plane
heterojunctions and magnetic structures for energy conversion and related applications. Eleven articles
supported by the grant have been published in the award period (including Nature Communications, PNAS,
ACS Nano, Nano Letters, npj Computational Materials) and three articles are under review. Following are
the detailed achievements and scientific highlights.

1. Structure motif centric learning through hypergraphs and material-motif networks

A major accomplishment of this award was the further development of motif-centric learning as a framework
to incorporate higher-tier materials information in the graph learning process. Building on the first part of the
Early Career work (09/2019-08/2022) supported by award #DE-SC0020310, which established that local
structural motifs serve as informative building blocks in graph learning process for understanding materials
behavior (Science Advances (2022)), this project significantly advanced motif-based learning from a graph-
level representation into a more powerful design infrastructure. First, the introduction of hypergraph-based
crystal learning model further expanded this capability by capturing higher-order structural relationships that
are missed in conventional pair-wise crystal graphs and represented as hyperedges in hypergraphs (npj
Computational Materials (2025)). These crystal hypergraph convolutional networks are trained based on

various property prediction tasks for a vast set of solid-state materials. The improved performance of models
based on both pair-wise edges and local environment hyperedges demonstrates that hypergraphs are an
effective and efficient method for incorporating geometrical information in material representations. In
another work (submitted to Advanced Intelligent Discovery), by introducing material-motif network and
heterogeneous graph formalisms, materials are connected through shared structural units rather than only by
composition or atom-wise geometry, making it possible to identify hidden relationships across seemingly
distinct materials families and learn effective embeddings for solid-state materials just based on their motif
connections. Together, these developments improved both the expressiveness and interpretability of
materials representations, opening new routes for motif-guided screening, structure-property analysis, and
the generation of chemically meaningful design hypotheses. More broadly, this line of work helped establish
a foundation for moving materials informatics beyond black-box prediction toward models that can reveal
transferable structural principles for functional materials discovery.

2. Equivariant neural networks for the prediction of tensorial properties and tensorial spectra

A central scientific contribution of the award was the development of symmetry-aware machine-learning
methods for complex tensorial materials properties, together with the data infrastructure required to support
the development of these models. By using equivariant neural networks, this set of work enabled the
prediction of tensor quantities that transform consistently under rotations and crystal symmetry operations,
addressing a fundamental limitation of conventional machine-learning approaches that treat tensor
components as unrelated outputs. This made it possible to learn not only static tensor properties (AI for
Science, submitted (2026)), but also more challenging tensorial spectra that vary with photon frequency and
encode rich anisotropic optical behavior. The impact of this direction is twofold. Scientifically, it establishes
a physically principled and data-driven route for learning properties that are directly relevant to optics,
transport, electromechanical response, and broader functional behavior in crystals (Nature Communications,
in press (2026)). Infrastructurally, the generation of a large-scale dataset of tensorial optical and transport
properties created an important public resource for training, benchmarking, and extending future models in
this area (Scientific Data (2025)). Together, these efforts produced a coherent pipeline that links first-
principles data generation, open data infrastructure, and symmetry-respecting model development,
significantly advancing the ability of AI methods to address realistic anisotropic materials properties at scale.

3. Learning electronic structures of materials: end-to-end approach vs Hamiltonian learning

Another key achievement of the project was the development of new machine-learning approaches for
predicting the electronic structures of complex materials, which are central to understanding electronic,
optical, transport properties of material systems. One direction focused on end-to-end learning of band
structures directly from crystal structure (Nature Communications, under review (2026)), providing a route

to predict rich electronic information without relying on handcrafted intermediate descriptors or simplified
scalar targets such as band gaps alone. By combining graph Transformer architecture with sequential learning
and Fourier transformation, the model can predict the electronic band structures of unknown systems with an
average error of just 0.3 eV. This represents an important step toward ML models that can learn full electronic
structure rather than scalar quantities. In a complementary direction, we developed a physics-informed
framework for learning Hamiltonian-level information and linking deep-learning force fields with electronic
structure prediction (npj Computational Materials (2025)). The work is impactful in that it unifies structural
relaxation, atomistic simulation, and electronic-property prediction within a single computational framework,
reducing the traditional separation between atomistic modeling and electronic-structure theory. Together,
these efforts greatly expanded the project’s ability to connect atomic structure with electronic and optical
functionality, laying the groundwork for more complete AI-based workflows for realistic materials design,
especially in systems where direct first-principles calculations are costly.

4. Frameworks for disordered systems based on graph neural network and Monte Carlo simulation

The project also made substantial progress in one of the most challenging areas of computational materials
science: predicting properties in atomically disordered systems. Unlike ideal crystals, disordered materials
exhibit behavior that emerges from large ensembles of local atomic configurations, making direct first-
principles treatment computationally expensive and often impractical. Our work demonstrated that graph
neural networks can be combined effectively with Monte Carlo simulations to learn configurational
energetics and predict disorder-related properties such as configurational entropy and heat capacity with high
efficiency and strong accuracy (npj Computational Materials (2024)). The impact of this advance is
significant because it extends machine-learning-based materials prediction beyond perfectly ordered crystals
to more realistic materials in which disorder strongly influences optical response, charge transport,
thermodynamics, and phase behavior. The later development of a more general framework for ensemble-
property prediction further strengthened this direction by enabling the modeling of complex spectral
properties that depend on configurational statistics rather than single structures (ACS Nano (2025)).
Collectively, these advances established a scalable strategy for integrating data-driven learning with
statistical physics, thereby opening new opportunities for the study and design of alloys, disordered functional
materials, and other complex systems where configurational variability is essential to performance.

5. Incorporation of physical constraints in density functional design

This project also contributed to the broader effort of integrating physical constraints directly into machine-
learning models for electronic-structure theory. Rather than using machine learning only as a flexible fitting
tool from electron density to exchange/correlation energy, this work showed that exact theoretical constraints
can be embedded into learned representations during model development (Digital Discovery (2023)). The

specific incorporation of density scaling behavior into density functional design through contrastive learning
demonstrates how data-driven approaches can be made more physically faithful, more robust outside the
training set, and less dependent on large amounts of labeled data. It provides a conceptual framework for
how machine learning and electronic-structure theory can reinforce one another, with modern representation
learning used to preserve exact physics rather than replacing it.

6. Collaborative studies of 2D heterojunctions and magnetic material/molecular systems

In addition to methodological advances, the project contributed to several experimental collaborations that
broadened its scientific reach and demonstrated relevance to real materials platforms. One collaborative
direction advanced the understanding of excited-state confinement and optical behavior in in-plane two-
dimensional heterostructures, helping clarify how low-dimensional architectures can be used to engineer
optoelectronic functionality and enable the design of emitting devices (Nature Communications (2024)).
Another set of collaborations focused on magnetic and molecular systems with coupled optical, ferroic, and
chiral responses, including photo-responsive multiferroic behavior and light-controlled magnetic
superstructures. The impact of these efforts lies in connecting computational and theory advances with
experimentally accessible systems that exhibit complex emergent phenomena (PNAS (2024) and Nano
Letters (2025)). As a result, the work helped strengthen the role of computational approaches in guiding the
understanding of excited states, photo-induced control, and multifunctional behavior in next-generation
functional materials for energy conversion and beyond.
