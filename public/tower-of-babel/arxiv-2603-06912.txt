The Stockwell transform on Gelfand pairs and localization operators
Claude G. Dosseh 1 , Mawoussi Todjro 2 and Yaogan Mensah 1

Abstract
. This paper addresses the extension of the Stockwell transform to Gelfand pairs. Some majors properties of this transform are examined. The localization operators related to the Stockwell transform in this framework are studied.



Keywords and phrases: Gelfand pair, Stockwell transform, localization operator.

2020 Mathematics Subject Classification: 43A30, 43A85, 47B10.

1.
Introduction

Signal processing appeals integral transforms, the oldest among them being the Fourier transform. However, the latter is more suitable for stationary signals (these are signals whose statistical characteristics do not vary over time). To handle non-stationnary signals, efficient time-frequency analysis tools have been bult (Gabor analysis, wavelet transfrom, shearlet transform, Stockwell transform, etc). The Stockwell transform, also known as the S-transform, was introduced by R.G. Stockwell et al. in 1996
[
11
]
. It has been proved to be a powerful analysis tool for non-stationary signals. Mainly, it provides a dynamic view of the frequency components. The S-transform preserves the absolute phase of each component of the signal, what is crucial for some applications. This feature makes it particularly valuable in applied sciences such as seismology, electroencephalography, medical imaging, mechanical vibration analysis and image processing, where phase information is highly sensitive.
Many researchers have studied the mathematical aspects of the S-transform and its applications in various fields of sciences. Readers interested in the Stockwell transforms (standard and modifed forms) and their associated localization operators may consult for example

\mathbb{R}^{d}
. However, one can explore a more general framework by investigating the Stockwell transform on Gelfand pairs since Gelfand pairs generalize locally compact abelian groups.

The introduction of Gelfand pairs occurred from the necessity to generalize harmonic analysis beyond locally compact abelian groups. It resulted a suitable theory applicable to some locally compact groups

2.
Harmonic analysis on Gelfand pairs

This section collects some necessary ingredients which may be useful in the rest of the paper.

For this subsection, we refer to
[
15
]
.
Let
G
be a locally compact group (assumed to be Hausdorff once for all) and let
K
be a compact subgroup of
G
. A complex-valued function
f
on
G
is said to be
K
-bi-invariant if
\forall x\in G,\forall k,k^{\prime}\in K
,

(1)

f(kxk^{\prime})=f(x).

Let
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G//K)
denote the space of complex-valued continuous compactly supported
K
-bi-invariant functions on
G
and let
L^{p}(G//K),\,1\leq p\leq\infty
, denote the space of elements of the Lebesgue space
L^{p}(G)
which are
K
-bi-invariant. The space
L^{p}(G//K)
inherits its norm from
L^{p}(G)
.
The convolution product is defined by

(2)

(f\ast g)(x)=\int_{G}f(y)g(y^{-1}x)dy,\ f,g\in L^{1}(G).

The pair
(G,K)
is called a Gelfand pair if
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G//K)
is a commutative convolution algebra. Since
\mathcal{C}_{c}(G//K)
is dense in
L^{1}(G//K)
, then it is equivalent to say that
(G,K)
is a Gelfand pair if
L^{1}(G//K)
is commutative under the convolution product
[
15
, page 154]
. Let us insist on the fact that if
(G,K)
is a Gelfand pair, then
G
is a unimodular group
[
15
, page 154]
.
Let us provide examples of Gelfand pairs.

1.

(G,\{e\})
where
G
is a locally compact abelian group with neutral element
e
,

2.

(E(n),SO(n))
where
E(n)
and
SO(n)
are respectively the group of Euclidean motions and the special orthogonal group on
\mathbb{R}^{n}
,

3.

(GL(n,\mathbb{C}),U(n))
where
GL(n,\mathbb{C})
is the general linear group and
U(n)
is the unitary group on
\mathbb{C}^{n}
.

A
K
-bi-invariant function
\varphi
is called a spherical function for the Gelfand pair
(G,K)
if the map
\chi_{\varphi}:\mathcal{C}_{c}(G//K)\to\mathbb{C},f\mapsto\displaystyle\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x)dx
is a nontrivial character of the convolution algebra
L^{1}(G//K)
. Let
\mathcal{S}_{b}(G,K)
denote the set of bounded spherical functions for the Gelfand pair
(G,K)
.

The spherical Fourier transform of
f\in L^{1}(G//K)
is defined by

(3)

\widehat{f}(\varphi)=\int_{G}f(x)\varphi(x^{-1})dx,\,\varphi\in S_{b}(G,K).

A function
\varphi:G\to\mathbb{C}
is said to be positive-definite if
\forall N\in\mathbb{N},\forall x_{1},\cdots,x_{N}\in G,\forall z_{1},\cdots,z_{N}\in\mathbb{C}
, we have

\displaystyle\sum_{n=1}^{N}\sum_{n=1}^{N}\varphi(x^{-1}_{n}x_{m})\bar{z_{n}}z_{m}\geq 0.

Positive-definite functions have the following properties.

Theorem 2.1
.

[
15
, Proposition 8.4.2]

Let
\varphi
be a positive-definite function. Then,

\forall x\in G,|\varphi(x)|\leq\varphi(e)
, where
e
is the neutral element of
G
.

\forall x\in G,\varphi(x^{-1})=\overline{\varphi(x)}
.

Let
\mathcal{S}^{+}
and
B(G//K)
denote respectively the set of positive definite spherical functions for the Gelfand pair
(G,K)
and the set of linear combinations of positive definite and
K
-bi-invariant functions on
G
. Then, there exists a positive Radon measure
\mu
on
\mathcal{S}^{+}
such that if
f\in B(G//K)\cap L^{1}(G//K)
, then
\widehat{f}\in L^{1}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
and the following spherical Fourier inversion formula holds
[
15
, page 191]
:

(4)

f(x)=\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\widehat{f}(\varphi)\varphi(x)d\mu(\varphi),\,x\in G.

In the sequel, we will write
d\varphi
for
d\mu(\varphi)
.

There exists a Plancherel type theorem for the spherical Fourier transform which reads as follows.

Theorem 2.2
.

[
15
, Theorem 9.5.1]

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair. If
f\in L^{1}(G//K)\cap L^{2}(G//K)
, then
\widehat{f}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
and

\|\widehat{f}\|_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Furthermore, the spherical Fourier transform
\mathcal{F}:L^{1}(G//K)\cap L^{2}(G//K)\to L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
extends to an isometry from
L^{2}(G//K)
onto
L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
.

Corollary 2.3
.

[
15
, Corollary 9.5.2]

If
f,g\in L^{2}(G//K)
, then
\widehat{f},\widehat{g}\in L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})
and

\langle\widehat{f},\widehat{g}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})}=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

To finish this subsection, we recall the following interpolation theorems.

Theorem 2.4
.

[
12
, Theorem 21.2]

Let
1\leq p_{0},p_{1},q_{0},q_{1}\leq\infty
. If
T:L^{p_{0}}(\nu)\to L^{q_{0}}(\mu)
is a bounded linear operator with norm
M_{0}
and
T:L^{p_{1}}(\nu)\to L^{q_{1}}(\mu)
is a bounded linear operator with norm
M_{1}
, then for
0<\theta<1,T:L^{p}(\nu)\to L^{q}(\mu)
is a bounded linear operator with norm
M\leq M_{0}^{1-\theta}M_{1}^{\theta}
, where
\dfrac{1}{p}=\dfrac{1-\theta}{p_{0}}+\dfrac{\theta}{p_{1}}
and
\dfrac{1}{q}=\dfrac{1-\theta}{q_{0}}+\dfrac{\theta}{q_{1}}
.

3.
Stockwell transform on Gelfand pairs

We begin by recalling the expressions of the classical Stockwell transformation and its formulation on locally compact abelian groups.

•

Let
\theta\in L^{1}(\mathbb{R})\cap L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
be such that
\displaystyle\int_{\mathbb{R}}\theta(x)dx=1
.
The Stockwell transform of
f\in L^{2}(\mathbb{R})
with respect to
\theta
is defined by
[
6
]
:

(5)

(S_{\theta}f)(b,\xi)=\displaystyle\frac{|\xi|}{\sqrt{2\pi}}\int_{\mathbb{R}}f(x)\overline{\theta(\xi(x-b))e^{-ix\xi}}dx,\,b\in\mathbb{R},\xi\in\mathbb{R}\setminus\{0\}.

•

Let
G
be a locally compact abelian group with character group
\widehat{G}
. Let
\alpha
be a topological automorphism of
G
. The measure
d(\alpha(x))
is also a Haar measure of
G
, so there exists a positive constant
\delta_{\alpha}
such that
d(\alpha(x))=\delta_{\alpha}dx
. Let
\theta\in L^{1}(G)\cap L^{2}(G)
.
The Stockwell transform of
f\in L^{2}(G)
with respect to
\theta
and
\alpha
is given by
[
3
]
:

(6)

(S_{\theta,\alpha}f)(t,\xi)=\sqrt{\delta_{\alpha}}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\xi(x)\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}dx,\,t\in G,\xi\in\widehat{G}.

Let us now move forward the case studied in this article. Let
G
be a locally compact group with a compact subgroup
K
such that
(G,K)
is a Gelfand pair. Let
\alpha
be a topological automorphism of
G
. As above, the measure with density
\alpha(x)
with respect to the Haar measure
dx
of
G
is also a Haar measure of
G
. Therefore, there exists a positive constant
\delta_{\alpha}
such that
d(\alpha(x))=\delta_{\alpha}dx
.
Hereafter is the main definition of the article.

Definition 3.1
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair.
Let
\alpha
be a topological automorphism of
G
and let
\theta
be in
L^{1}(G//K)\cap L^{2}(G//K)
. The Stockwell transform of
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
with respect to
\theta
and
\alpha
is defined by

(7)

S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)=\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\varphi(x)\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}dx,\quad t\in G,\varphi\in S_{b}(G,K).

Let us notice that the formula (
7
) can be rewritten as

(8)

S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)=\langle f,M_{\varphi}T_{t}D_{\alpha}\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)},\quad t\in G,\varphi\in S_{b}(G,K),

where the modulation operator
M_{\varphi}
, the translation operator
T_{t}
and the dilatation operator
D_{\alpha}
are defined respectively for
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
by

1)

M_{\varphi}f(x)=\varphi(x)f(x)
,

2)

T_{t}f(x)=f(t^{-1}x)
,

3)

D_{\alpha}f(x)=\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}f(\alpha(x))
.

Let us consider the set

L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})=\left\{f:G\times\mathcal{S}^{+}\to\mathbb{C}\text{ such that }\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|f(x,\varphi)|^{2}dxd\varphi<\infty\right\}

equipped with the norm

\|f\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}=\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|f(x,\varphi)|^{2}dxd\varphi\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}.

We are now in a position to prove the following result.

Theorem 3.2
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair. If
\theta,\phi,f,g\in L^{2}(G//K)
, then

\displaystyle\langle S_{\theta,\alpha}f,S_{\phi,\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}\langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Moreover, if
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
, then

\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}
.

Proof.

Set
A=\langle S_{\theta,\alpha}f,S_{\phi,\alpha}g\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}
. We have

\displaystyle A

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)\overline{S_{\phi,\alpha}g(t,\varphi)}dtd\varphi

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\varphi(x)\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}dx\right)\left(\int_{G}\overline{g(y)}\varphi(y)\phi(\alpha(t^{-1}y))dy\right)dtd\varphi

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\left(\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)\overline{\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}\phi(\alpha(t^{-1}y))dxdy\right)dtd\varphi

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)\overline{\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}\phi(\alpha(t^{-1}y))dtd\varphi\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)d\varphi\right)\left(\int_{G}\overline{\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}\phi(\alpha(t^{-1}y))dt\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)d\varphi\right)\left(\int_{G}\overline{\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}))}\phi(\alpha(t^{-1}))dt\right)dxdy

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)d\varphi\right)dxdy\left(\int_{G}\overline{\theta(s)}\phi(s)ds\right)

\displaystyle\text{(by setting }\alpha(t^{-1})=s)

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{g(y)}\overline{\varphi(x)}\varphi(y)d\varphi dxdy\ \langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\left(\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\varphi(x)}dx\overline{\int_{G}g(y)\overline{\varphi(y)}dy}\right)d\varphi\ \langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\hat{f}(\varphi)\overline{\hat{g}(\varphi)}d\varphi\ \langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=\langle\hat{f},\hat{g}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})}\ \langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=\langle f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}\ \langle\phi,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Now, we assume that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
. Then,

\displaystyle\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\langle S_{\theta,\alpha}f,S_{\theta,\alpha}f\rangle_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}

\displaystyle=\langle f,f\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}\langle\theta,\theta\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}^{2}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}^{2}.

∎

Although we will not need it in this article, we would like to inform you that the inversion formula corresponding to (
7
) reads as

(9)

f(x)=\dfrac{\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}}{\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}^{2}}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)\varphi(x)\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))dtd\varphi,\,f\in L^{2}(G//K).

Theorem 3.3
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair. Let the function
\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
be such that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
. Then, the range
\mathrm{Range}(S_{\theta,\alpha})
of the Stockwell transform
S_{\theta,\alpha}
is a closed subspace of
L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
.

Proof.

Let
\{F_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
be a sequence of functions in
\mathrm{Range}(S_{\theta,\alpha})
which converges to
F
in
L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
. For each
i
, there exists
f_{i}\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
S_{\theta,\alpha}f_{i}=F_{i}
. Thanks to Theorem
3.2
, we have

\|f_{i}-f_{j}\|=\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f_{i}-S_{\theta,\alpha}f_{j}\|=\|F_{i}-F_{j}\|
converges to 0 as
i
tends to
\infty
. Therefore,
\{f_{i}\}_{i=1}^{\infty}
is a Cauchy sequence in
L^{2}(G//K)
. Since the latter is complete, there exists a function
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
f_{i}
converges to
f
.
Thus,
F_{i}=S_{\theta,\alpha}f_{i}
converges to
S_{\theta,\alpha}f
because
S_{\theta,\alpha}
is bounded as an isometry. Therefore,
S_{\theta,\alpha}f=F
. ∎

Let us recall the definition of a reproducing kernel Hilbert space.

Definition 3.4
.

[
1
, Definition 1]

Let
H
be a Hilbert space of complex-valued functions defined on a measure space
X
. A function
k:X\times X\to\mathbb{C}
is called a reproducing kernel of
H
if the following conditions are satisfied:

(1)

\forall y\in X,k(.,y)\in H
,

(2)

\forall x\in X,\forall f\in H,f(x)=\displaystyle\int_{X}f(y)k(x,y)dy.

A reproducing kernel Hilbert space is a Hilbert space which possesses a reproducing kernel.

We are going to prove that the range of the Stockwell transform on a Gelfand pair is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space. For

\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
, we set

\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}=M_{\varphi}T_{t}D_{\alpha}\theta.

Theorem 3.5
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair. Let
\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
. The range of the Stockwell transform
S_{\alpha,\theta}
is a reproducing kernel Hilbert space with the reproducing kernel

(10)

k(t,\varphi,\tau,\psi)=\overline{S_{\theta,\alpha}\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(\tau,\psi)}=\overline{\langle\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t},\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}}\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)},

where
\langle\cdot,\cdot\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}
is the scalar product in
L^{2}(G//K)
.

Proof.

By construction
k(t,\varphi,.,.)\in Rang(S_{\alpha,\theta})
.
Let
F\in Rang(S_{\theta,\alpha})
. Theorem
3.3
assures that there exists a function
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
F=S_{\theta,\alpha}f
. Thus,

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}k(t,\varphi,\tau,\psi)F(\tau,\psi)d\tau d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\overline{\langle\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t},\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}\rangle}_{L^{2}(G//K)}S_{\alpha,\theta}f(\tau,\psi)d\tau d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}S_{\alpha,\theta}f(\tau,\psi)\langle\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau},\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}d\tau d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\left(\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(x)}dx\right)\left(\int_{G}\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(y)\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(y)}dy\right)d\tau d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}f(x)\left(\int_{G}\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(x)}\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(y)d\tau\right)\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(y)}dxdyd\psi.

Let us notice that

\displaystyle\int_{G}\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(x)}\theta_{\alpha,\psi,\tau}(y)d\tau

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{G}\overline{\psi(x)\theta(\alpha(\tau^{-1}x))}\psi(y)\theta(\alpha(\tau^{-1}y))d\tau

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha}\int_{G}\overline{\psi(x)}\psi(y)\overline{\theta(\alpha(\tau^{-1}))}\theta(\alpha(\tau^{-1}))d\tau

\displaystyle=\overline{\psi(x)}\psi(y).

Therefore,

\displaystyle\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}k(t,\varphi,\tau,\psi)F(\tau,\psi)d\tau d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\psi(x)}\psi(y)\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(y)}dxdyd\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\left(\int_{G}f(x)\overline{\psi(x)}dx\right)\overline{\left(\int_{G}\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(y)\overline{\psi(y)}dy\right)}d\psi

\displaystyle=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\widehat{f}(\psi)\overline{\widehat{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}}(\psi)}d\psi

\displaystyle=\langle\widehat{f},\widehat{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}}\rangle_{L^{2}(\mathcal{S}^{+})}

\displaystyle=\langle f,\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)

\displaystyle=F(t,\varphi).

Thus,
k
is the reproducing kernel of the range of the Stockwell transform.
∎

4.
Localization operators related to the Stockwell transform on Gelfand pairs

In this section, we introduced localization operators related to the Stockwell transform on Gelfand pairs. We discuss their boundedness under some given conditions.

Let
u:G\times\mathcal{S}^{+}\rightarrow\mathbb{C}
be a measurable function. Let
\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
. The localization operator
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}
related to the Stockwell transform
S_{\theta,\alpha}
is the linear operator defined for
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
by:

(11)

\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f(x)=\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(x)dtd\varphi,\,x\in G.

Theorem 4.1
.

Let
\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
. If
u\in L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}:L^{2}(G//K)\rightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is bounded and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}
.

Proof.

Let
u\in L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
and
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
. We have

\displaystyle\int_{G}|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f(x)|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f(x)\overline{\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\int_{G}\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(x)dtd\varphi\right)

\displaystyle\times\overline{\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(s,\psi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(s,\psi)\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(x)dsd\psi\right)}dx

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)dtd\varphi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\overline{u(s,\psi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(s,\psi)}dsd\psi\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{G}\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(x)\overline{\theta_{\alpha,\varphi,t}(x)}dx

\displaystyle=\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)dtd\varphi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\overline{u(s,\psi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(s,\psi)}dsd\psi\right)

\displaystyle\times\int_{G}|\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))|^{2}dx

\displaystyle=\left|\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)dtd\varphi\right|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|u(t,\varphi)||S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)|dtd\varphi\right)^{2}

\displaystyle\leq\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|u(t,\varphi)|^{2}dtd\varphi\right)\left(\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)|^{2}dtd\varphi\right)

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}^{2}\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}^{2}

\displaystyle=\|u\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}^{2}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}^{2}.

Therefore,

\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}
is bounded and its operator norm does not exceed
\|u\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}
.
∎

We need the following lemma.

Lemma 4.2
.

Let
(G,K)
be a Gelfand pair,
\alpha
be a topological automorphism of
G
and let
\theta\in L^{1}(G//K)\cap L^{2}(G//K)
. If
f\in L^{2}(G//K)
, then
S_{\theta,\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
and

\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Proof.

We have

\displaystyle|S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)|

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}\int_{G}|f(x)|\overline{\varphi(x)\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))}|dx

\displaystyle\leq\delta_{\alpha}^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{G}|f(x)|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}\left(\int_{G}|\theta(\alpha(t^{-1}x))|^{2}dx\right)^{\frac{1}{2}}

\displaystyle\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle<\infty.

It follows that
S_{\theta,\alpha}f\in L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
and

\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

∎

Theorem 4.3
.

Let
\theta\in L^{2}(G//K)
such that
\|\theta\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}=1
. If
u\in L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}:L^{2}(G//K)\rightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is bounded and
\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(G//K)
. We have

\displaystyle\left|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}\right|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|u(t,\varphi)||S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)|\overline{S_{\theta,\alpha}g(t,\varphi)}|dtd\varphi

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|S_{\theta,\alpha}g\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle\mbox{(by Lemma \ref{Lemma-bound})}.

Hence,
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}
is bounded and

\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}.

∎

Theorem 4.4
.

If
u\in L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}:L^{2}(G//K)\rightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is bounded and

\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(G//K)
, we have from Hölder’s inequality,

\displaystyle|\langle\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}|

\displaystyle\leq\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}|u(t,\varphi)||S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)|\overline{S_{\theta,\alpha}g(t,\varphi)}|dtd\varphi

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|S_{\theta,\alpha}f\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|S_{\theta,\alpha}g\|_{L^{2}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}

\displaystyle\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\|g\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Thus,
\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}.

∎

Theorem 4.5
.

If
u\in L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+}),1\leq p\leq\infty
, then
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}:L^{2}(G//K)\rightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is bounded and

\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}.

Proof.

Let us fix
f\in L^{1}(G//K)\cap L^{2}(G//K)
. We consider the operator

T_{f}:L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})\cap L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})\to L^{2}(G//K)

defined by

T_{f}(u):=\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f.

Using respectively Theorem
4.3
and Theorem
4.4
, we have

(12)

\|T_{f}(u)\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{1}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}

and

(13)

\|T_{f}(u)\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{\infty}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Therefore, by the interpolation theorem (Theorem
2.4
), whenever
1\leq p\leq\infty
, the map
T_{f}:L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})\rightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is a bounded linear operator and

\|T_{f}\|\leq\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

That is, if for
u\in L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})
, then

\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}\|f\|_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Thus,
\|\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}\|\leq\|u\|_{L^{p}(G\times\mathcal{S}^{+})}.

∎

Theorem 4.6
.

The adjoint operator
(\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u})^{\ast}
of the localization operator
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}:L^{2}(G//K)\longrightarrow L^{2}(G//K)
is the localization operator
\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{\bar{u}}
, where
\bar{u}
is the complex conjugate of
u
.

Proof.

Let
f,g\in L^{2}(G//K)
. Then,

\displaystyle\langle\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f,g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{G}\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u}f(x)\overline{g(x)}dx

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}u(t,\varphi)S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)\overline{S_{\theta,\alpha}g(t,\varphi)}dtd\varphi

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\int_{\mathcal{S}^{+}}\int_{G}\overline{u(t,\varphi)}S_{\theta,\alpha}g(t,\varphi)\overline{S_{\theta,\alpha}f(t,\varphi)}dtd\varphi}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\overline{\langle\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{\overline{u}}g,f\rangle}_{L^{2}(G//K)}

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle\langle f,\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{\bar{u}}g\rangle_{L^{2}(G//K)}.

Thus
(\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{u})^{\ast}=\mathcal{L}_{\theta,\alpha}^{\bar{u}}
.
∎
