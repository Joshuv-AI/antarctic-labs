Introduction

Let
R=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
denote the polynomial ring in
n
variables over a field
K
,
graded by
\deg x_{i}=1
.
Let
I\subset R
be a homogeneous ideal with
\dim(R/I)=d
.
When the minimal graded free resolution of
R/I
takes the form

\mathbb{F}_{R/I}:0\to\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}R(-j)^{\beta_{p,j}}\to\cdots\to\bigoplus_{j\geq 0}R(-j)^{\beta_{1,j}}\to R\to R/I\to 0,

the
(Castelnuovo–Mumford) regularity

\reg(R/I)
of
R/I
is defined by

\reg(R/I):=\max\{j-i\mid\beta_{i,j}\neq 0\}.

Let
G=(V(G),E(G))
be a finite simple undirected graph with vertex set

V(G)=\{x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}\}
and edge set
E(G)
.
Throughout this paper, all graphs are assumed to be finite, simple, and undirected.
Set
R=K[V(G)]=K[x_{1},\ldots,x_{n}]
, and define the
edge ideal
of
G
by

I(G)=\left(x_{i}x_{j}:\{x_{i},x_{j}\}\in E(G)\right)\subset R.

Edge ideals occupy a central position in commutative algebra and combinatorics,
and have been studied extensively.
Of particular interest is the relationship between algebraic invariants of edge ideals
and combinatorial invariants of graphs.
Among such invariants, the Castelnuovo–Mumford regularity of an edge ideal is one of
the most fundamental—alongside dimension and depth—and has attracted a great deal
of attention. (see, e.g.,
[
1
]
,
[
7
]
and
[
14
]
). Representative results are as follows.

Theorem 0.1

(
[
15
]
Lemma 7,
[
8
]
, Theorem 6.7, and
[
11
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Let
\operatorname{m}(G)
denote the matching number of
G
, and
\operatorname{im}(G)
the induced matching number
of
G
. Then the following inequalities hold:

\operatorname{im}(G)\leq\reg\!\left(R/I(G)\right)\leq\operatorname{m}(G).

Theorem 0.2

(
[
5
]
)
.

For a graph
G
,
\reg(R/I(G))=1
if and only if the complement
G^{c}
is chordal.

Theorem 0.3

(
[
5
]
)
.

For a graph
G
,
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 2
if and only if
G^{c}
contains a cycle of length
at least
4
.

While Theorems

and

are established,
the case
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
was only partially understood:
for bipartite graphs, the following result was known.

(
[

Let

.

In this paper, we extend this result to arbitrary graphs.

Let

(1)

Over any field

\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
if and only if

2

(2)

Over a field

\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3

Graph theory

We begin with a brief review of the graph-theoretic terminology used in this paper.

A graph
G
is called
bipartite
if there exists a partition

V(G)=U\cup W
with
U\cap W=\emptyset
such that every edge

e\in E(G)
satisfies
e\cap U\neq\emptyset
and
e\cap W\neq\emptyset
.
More generally,
G
is called an
n
-partite graph
if there is a partition

V(G)=U_{1}\cup U_{2}\cup\cdots\cup U_{n}
into pairwise disjoint sets such that
for every edge
e\in E(G)
there exist distinct indices
i\neq j
with

e\cap U_{i}\neq\emptyset
and
e\cap U_{j}\neq\emptyset
.
An
n
-partite graph
G
is called
complete
n
-partite
if

E(G)=\bigl\{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}:x_{i}\in U_{i},\,x_{j}\in U_{j},\,i\neq j\bigr\}
,
and is denoted
K_{|U_{1}|,|U_{2}|,\ldots,|U_{n}|}
.
In particular, the complete tripartite graph
K_{2,2,2}
is called the

octahedral graph
.

The
complement
of a graph
G
is the graph
G^{c}
defined by

V(G^{c})=V(G)
and

E(G^{c})=\bigl\{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}:x_{i},x_{j}\in V(G),\,i\neq j\bigr\}\setminus E(G)
.

For a bipartite graph
G
with bipartition
V(G)=U\cup W
, the

bipartite complement

G^{bc}
is the graph with
V(G^{bc})=V(G)
and

E(G^{bc})=\bigl\{\{x_{i},x_{j}\}:x_{i}\in U,\,x_{j}\in W\bigr\}\setminus E(G)
.

For a graph
G
and a subset
W\subset V(G)
, the
induced subgraph

G_{|W}
is defined by
V(G_{|W})=W
and

E(G_{|W})=\bigl\{e=\{x,y\}\in E(G):x,y\in W\bigr\}
.
When
W=V(G)\setminus\{x\}
, we write
G-x:=G_{|W}
.

A
walk
from
x_{s}
to
x_{g}
in
G
is a sequence

x_{s}x_{p_{1}}x_{p_{2}}\cdots x_{p_{m}}x_{g}
such that

\{x_{s},x_{p_{1}}\},\{x_{p_{1}},x_{p_{2}}\},\ldots,\{x_{p_{m}},x_{g}\}\in E(G)
.
A graph
G
is
connected
if for any
x,y\in V(G)
there exists a walk
from
x
to
y
in
G
.

A graph
G
is
2
-connected
if
G-x
is connected for every vertex
x
.
More generally, for an integer
n\geq 2
,
G
is
n
-connected
if

G-x
is
(n-1)
-connected for every vertex
x
.

A walk
x_{s}x_{p_{1}}x_{p_{2}}\cdots x_{p_{m}}x_{g}
with
x_{s}=x_{g}
and all other
vertices distinct is called a
cycle
of length
m+1
.
A cycle
C
in
G
is an
induced cycle
if
G_{|V(C)}=C
.
A subset
U\subset V(G)
is called an
anticycle
of
G
if

(G_{|U})^{c}
is an induced cycle of length at least
4
.

For vertices
x
and
y
, we say
x
and
y
are
adjacent
if

\{x,y\}\in E(G)
.
The
open neighborhood
of
x
is

N_{G}(x)=\{y\in V(G):\{x,y\}\in E(G)\}
,
and the
closed neighborhood
is
N_{G}[x]=N_{G}(x)\cup\{x\}
.
The
degree
of
x
is
d_{G}(x)=|N_{G}(x)|
.
And we denote
G_{x}:=G_{|V(G)\setminus N_{G}[x]}
.

A set
M=\{e_{1},\ldots,e_{s}\}\subset E(G)
is a
matching
in
G
if

e_{i}\cap e_{j}=\emptyset
for all distinct
e_{i},e_{j}\in M
.
A matching
M
is an
induced matching
if
G_{|V(M)}=M
(i.e.,
M
forms
the entire edge set of the induced subgraph on
V(M)=\bigcup_{e\in M}e
).
The maximum size of a matching is the
matching number

\operatorname{m}(G)
, and the
maximum size of an induced matching is the
induced matching number

\operatorname{im}(G)
.

For an edge
e=\{a,b\}\in E(G)
, the
contraction

G/e
is the graph with

V(G/e)=(V(G)\setminus\{a,b\})\cup\{v\},

E(G/e)=E\!\left(G_{|V(G)\setminus\{a,b\}}\right)\cup\bigl\{\{v,w\}:w\in(N_{G}(a)\cup N_{G}(b))\setminus\{a,b\}\bigr\}.

Regularity of edge ideals and monomial ideals

We next recall several useful results on the regularity of edge ideals and,
more generally, of monomial ideals.

The following bounds on regularity are well known.

Theorem 1.1

(
[
8
]
, Theorem 6.7, and
[
11
]
, Lemma 2.2)
.

Let
\operatorname{m}(G)
be the matching number and
\operatorname{im}(G)
the induced matching number of
G
.
Then

\operatorname{im}(G)\leq\reg\!\left(R/I(G)\right)\leq\operatorname{m}(G).

In particular, if
G^{c}=K_{2,2,2}
then
\operatorname{m}(G)=\operatorname{im}(G)=3
, so

\reg(R/I(G))=3
.

The following lemma on the regularity of short exact sequences is standard.

Lemma 1.2

(
[
13
]
, Proposition 18.6)
.

Let

0\rightarrow U\rightarrow U^{\prime}\rightarrow U^{\prime\prime}\rightarrow 0

be a short exact sequence of graded finitely generated
R
-modules with
degree-zero homomorphisms. Then

(1)

If
\reg(U^{\prime})>\reg(U^{\prime\prime})
, then
\reg(U)=\reg(U^{\prime})
.

(2)

If
\reg(U^{\prime})<\reg(U^{\prime\prime})
, then
\reg(U)=\reg(U^{\prime\prime})+1
.

(3)

If
\reg(U^{\prime})=\reg(U^{\prime\prime})
, then
\reg(U)\leq\reg(U^{\prime\prime})+1
.

Consider the following short exact sequence, where
I
is an ideal in
R
and
x
is a
variable:

0\rightarrow\frac{R}{(I:x)}(-1)\rightarrow\frac{R}{I}\rightarrow\frac{R}{(I,x)}\rightarrow 0.

Applying Lemma
1.2
to this sequence gives the following bound.

Theorem 1.3
.

For an ideal
I\subset R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and a variable
x
,

\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)\leq\max\!\left\{\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{(I,x)}\right),\,\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{(I:x)}\right)+1\right\}.

For monomial ideals, equality holds:

Theorem 1.4

(
[
3
]
, Lemma 2.10)
.

For a monomial ideal
I\subset R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
and a variable
x
,

\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{(I,x)}\right)\quad\text{or}\quad\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{I}\right)=\reg\!\left(\frac{R}{(I:x)}\right)+1.

In the case of edge ideals, this specializes as follows.

Lemma 1.5
.

Let
G
be a graph with edge ideal
I(G)
. For any vertex
x\in V(G)
, exactly one
of the following equalities holds:

(1)

\reg\!\left(\dfrac{R}{I(G)}\right)=\reg\!\left(\dfrac{R}{I(G-x)}\right).

(2)

\reg\!\left(\dfrac{R}{I(G)}\right)=\reg\!\left(\dfrac{R}{I(G_{|V(G)\setminus N_{G}[x]})}\right)+1.

Proof.

Since
I(G)
is generated by quadratic monomials, we have

(I(G):x)=I(G_{|V(G)\setminus N_{G}[x]})
and
(I(G),x)=I(G-x)
,
from which the result follows by Theorem
1.4
.
∎

The following lemma will also be needed.

Lemma 1.6

(
[
10
]
, Lemma 3.2)
.

Let
R_{1}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m}]
and
R_{2}=K[y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
be polynomial rings over a field
K
. Let
I_{1}
be a nonzero homogeneous ideal of
R_{1}
and
I_{2}
that of
R_{2}
.
Let
R=R_{1}\otimes_{K}R_{2}=K[x_{1},\dots,x_{m},y_{1},\dots,y_{n}]
and regard
I_{1}+I_{2}
as a homogeneous ideal of
R
. Then

\reg(R/(I_{1}+I_{2}))=\reg(R_{1}/I_{1})+\reg(R_{2}/I_{2})
.

We now recall the notion of a Betti splitting.
Let
\Gamma(I)
denote the minimal system of monomial generators of
I
.

Definition 1.7

(
[
2
]
, Definition 1.1)
.

Let
I
,
I^{\prime}
, and
I^{\prime\prime}
be monomial ideals such that
\Gamma(I)
is the disjoint union
of
\Gamma(I^{\prime})
and
\Gamma(I^{\prime\prime})
.
Then
I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
is a
Betti splitting
if

\beta_{i,j}(I)=\beta_{i,j}(I^{\prime})+\beta_{i,j}(I^{\prime\prime})+\beta_{i-1,j}(I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime})\quad\text{for all }i\in{\mathbb{N}}\text{ and all degrees }j,

where
\beta_{i,j}(I)
denotes the
(i,j)
-th graded Betti number of
I
.

Theorem 1.8

(
[
2
]
, Corollary 2.2)
.

Let
I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
be a Betti splitting. Then

(a)

\reg(I)=\max\{\reg(I^{\prime}),\,\reg(I^{\prime\prime}),\,\reg(I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime})-1\}
, and

(b)

\operatorname{pd}(I)=\max\{\operatorname{pd}(I^{\prime}),\,\operatorname{pd}(I^{\prime\prime}),\,\operatorname{pd}(I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime})+1\}
.

Definition 1.9

(
[
2
]
, Definition 2.6)
.

Let
I
be a monomial ideal in
R=k[x_{1},\dots,x_{n}]
.
Let
I^{\prime}
be the ideal generated by all elements of
\Gamma(I)
divisible by
x_{i}
,
and let
I^{\prime\prime}
be the ideal generated by all remaining elements of
\Gamma(I)
.
We call
I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
an
x_{i}
-partition
of
I
.
If this partition is also a Betti splitting, we call it an
x_{i}
-splitting
.

Lemma 1.10

(
[
2
]
, Corollary 2.7)
.

Let
I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
be an
x_{i}
-partition of
I
in which every element of
\Gamma(I^{\prime})

is divisible by
x_{i}
. If
\beta_{i,j}(I^{\prime}\cap I^{\prime\prime})>0
implies
\beta_{i,j}(I^{\prime})=0

for all
i
and all multidegrees
j
, then
I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
is a Betti splitting.
In particular, if the minimal graded free resolution of
I^{\prime}
is linear, then

I=I^{\prime}+I^{\prime\prime}
is a Betti splitting.

The following formula, due to Hochster, computes the Betti numbers of a
Stanley–Reisner ring in terms of the simplicial homology of the complex.

Lemma 1.11

(Hochster’s formula, cf.
[
12
]
)
.

Let
k[\Delta]=R/I(\Delta)
be the Stanley–Reisner ring of a simplicial complex

\Delta
. Then

\beta_{i,j}(k[\Delta])=\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}W\subset V(\Delta)\\
|W|=j\end{subarray}}\dim_{k}\widetilde{H}_{j-i-1}(\Delta_{W};\,k),

where
\Delta_{W}
denotes the restriction of
\Delta
to
W
.

Closed
2
-manifolds

Finally, we recall two standard facts about closed
2
-manifolds (surfaces) and their
triangulations.

Fact 1.12

(cf.
[
6
]
)
.

A graph
\Gamma
is the
1
-skeleton of a triangulation of a closed
2
-manifold if and
only if the link of every vertex of
\Gamma
(i.e., the neighborhood subgraph induced by

N_{\Gamma}(v)
) is a cycle.

Fact 1.13

(cf.
[
9
]
)
.

Let
\Sigma
be a triangulation of a closed surface, and let
k
be a field.
Here
S^{2}
denotes the
2
-sphere,
\Sigma_{g}
the orientable surface of genus
g
,
and
N_{g}
the non-orientable surface of genus
g
. Then

(1)

\widetilde{H}_{2}(\Sigma;\,k)\cong\begin{cases}k&\text{if }\Sigma=S^{2}\text{ or }\Sigma_{g}\text{ with }\ch(k)\neq 2,\\
0&\text{if }\Sigma=N_{g}\text{ with }\ch(k)\neq 2,\end{cases}

(2)

\widetilde{H}_{2}(\Sigma;\,k)\cong k\quad\text{if }\ch(k)=2\text{ (for any closed surface }\Sigma\text{)}.

2.
Main Result

Theorem 2.1
.

Let
G
be a graph and
k
a field.

(1)

Over any field
k
,
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
if and only if
G^{c}
contains a
triangulation of an orientable closed
2
-manifold as a subgraph.

(2)

If
\ch(k)=2
, then
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
if and only if
G^{c}
contains a
triangulation of a closed
2
-manifold (orientable or not) as a subgraph.

The proof of Theorem
2.1
proceeds by showing that if
G
satisfies

\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
and is vertex-minimal with respect to this property, then the
combinatorial structure of
G
is severely constrained.

Proof of Theorem
2.1
.

Let
G
be vertex-minimal among graphs satisfying
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
.
That is,
\reg(R/I(G_{|W}))\leq 2
for every proper subset
W\subsetneq V(G)
.

For a vertex
x\in V(G)
,
we claim that
\reg(R/I(G_{x}))=2
for every vertex
x
, and in particular

G_{x}
contains an anticycle as an induced subgraph.

Indeed, if
G_{x}
contains no anticycle, then
\reg(R/I(G_{x}))=1
by
Theorem
0.3
, and Lemma
1.5
gives

\reg(R/I(G))=\reg(R/I(G-x))
, contradicting vertex-minimality.

We now prove by induction on
|V(G)|
that
G_{x}
is itself an anticycle
for every vertex
x
.

Since
\reg(R/I(G))\geq 3
implies
\operatorname{im}(G)\geq 3
by Theorem
1.1
,

G
contains at least
6
vertices.
If
|V(G)|=6
, then
G
must be the complement of the octahedral graph.
If
G
is a complement of the octahedral, then
G_{x}
consists of two disjoint edges, which from the complement of
4
-cycle for any vertex
x\in V(G)
.
So we may assume
|V(G)|\geq 7
.

Fix a pair
a,b\in V(G)
with
\{a,b\}\notin E(G)
.
Define a graph
G^{\prime}
by

V(G^{\prime})=V(G)\cup\{v_{1},v_{2}\},

E(G^{\prime})=E(G)\cup\bigl\{\{v_{i},w\}:i\in\{1,2\},\,w\in N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b)\bigr\}\cup\bigl\{\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\bigr\}.

Step 1.
We show that
a,b
can always be chosen so that

N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b)\neq\emptyset
.

Suppose for contradiction that
N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b)=\emptyset
for every
non-edge
\{a,b\}\notin E(G)
.
Then for any edge
\{a,b\}\in E(G^{c})
, we have

N_{G^{c}}(a)\cup N_{G^{c}}(b)=V(G)\setminus\{a,b\}
.

By the argument above, the induced subgraph
G^{c}_{|N_{G^{c}}(a)}
,
which coincides with
(G_{a})^{c}
, contains an induced cycle
C=b_{1}b_{2}b_{3}\cdots b_{l}b_{1}
.

Since
b_{1}\in N_{G^{c}}(a)
, we have
\{a,b_{1}\}\notin E(G)
,
so by assumption
N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b_{1})=\emptyset
.
Combined with
N_{G^{c}}(a)\cup N_{G^{c}}(b_{1})=V(G)\setminus\{a,b_{1}\}
,
every vertex of
N_{G}(a)
lies in
N_{G^{c}}(b_{1})
; pick any
a^{\prime}\in N_{G}(a)
.

Applying the same argument to each adjacent pair
\{b_{i},b_{i+1}\}\in E(C)\subset E(G^{c})
,
we find that
a^{\prime}\in N_{G^{c}}(b_{i})
for all
b_{i}\in C
.
Since
C
is an induced cycle in
G^{c}
and
a^{\prime}
is adjacent in
G^{c}
to all vertices of
C
,
the cycle
C
has length exactly
4
.

It follows that
a,a^{\prime},b_{1},b_{2},b_{3},b_{4}
span an induced octahedral graph in
G^{c}
,
so vertex-minimality forces
|V(G)|=6
, a contradiction.

Step 2.
We show
\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime}))=3
, where
R^{\prime}=R[v_{1},v_{2}]
.

Consider the two short exact sequences

0\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}):v_{2})}(-1)\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(G^{\prime})}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}),v_{2})}\to 0,

0\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{((I(G^{\prime}),v_{2}):v_{1})}(-1)\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}),v_{2})}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}),v_{1},v_{2})}\to 0.

Since
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}\in E(G^{\prime})
, we have

G^{\prime}_{v_{2}}=G_{a}\cup G_{b}\subsetneq G
, so

\reg(R^{\prime}/(I(G^{\prime}):v_{2}))+1=3
.
Similarly,
\reg(R^{\prime}/((I(G^{\prime}),v_{2}):v_{1}))+1=3
.
Moreover,
(I(G^{\prime}),v_{1},v_{2})=I(G)
because
(I(G^{\prime}),v_{1},v_{2})=I(G^{\prime}-v_{1}-v_{2})=I(G)
, so by Lemma
1.5
,

\reg(R^{\prime}/(I(G^{\prime}),v_{2}))=3
, and applying the first sequence gives

\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime}))=3
.

Step 3.
Set
G^{\prime\prime}=G^{\prime}-b
. We show
\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime}))=3
.

Consider the sequence

0\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}):b)}(-1)\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(G^{\prime})}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime}),b)}\to 0.

Since
\{b,v_{i}\}\notin E(G^{\prime})
and
N_{G^{\prime}}(v_{i})=\{v_{j}\}\cup(N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b))
,
we get
G^{\prime}_{b}=G_{b}\cup\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
.
By Lemma
1.6
,

\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime}_{b}))=3
, and then

\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime}))=\reg(R^{\prime}/(I(G^{\prime}),b))=\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime}))=3
.

Step 4.
Set
G^{\prime\prime\prime}=G^{\prime\prime}-a
. We show
\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime\prime}))=3
.

We split into two cases.

Case 4a:
G_{a}
contains an anticycle not involving
b
.

Let
A
be such an anticycle. Since both
A
and
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
are contained in

G^{\prime\prime}_{a}
and are disjoint,
\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime}_{a}))+1=4
.
Hence
\reg(G^{\prime\prime\prime})=\reg(G^{\prime\prime})=3
.

Case 4b: Every anticycle in
G_{a}
involves
b
.

Let
A
be an anticycle in
G_{a}
containing
b
.
There exist two vertices
u_{1},u_{2}
on
A
not adjacent to
b
.
Define
\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}
by

V(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}})=V(G^{\prime\prime})\cup\{v_{3}\},

E(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}})=E(G^{\prime\prime})\cup\bigl\{\{v_{3},w\}:w\in V(G)\setminus\{u_{1},u_{2}\}\bigr\}.

Setting
R^{\prime\prime}=R^{\prime}[x_{v_{3}}]
and considering the sequence

0\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{(I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}):v_{3})}(-1)\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}})}\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{(I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}),v_{3})}\to 0,

since
\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}-v_{3}=G^{\prime\prime}
and
\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}_{v_{3}}
consists of the four
vertices
u_{1},u_{2},v_{1},v_{2}
, we obtain

\reg(R^{\prime\prime}/I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}))=\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime}))=3.

Next, consider

0\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{(I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}):a)}(-1)\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}})}\to\frac{R^{\prime\prime}}{(I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}),a)}\to 0.

Since
\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}_{a}
contains the induced cycle
(A-b)\cup\{v_{3}\}

together with the edge
\{v_{1},v_{2}\}
, the left-hand term has regularity
4
.
Therefore
3=\reg(R^{\prime\prime}/I(\overline{G^{\prime\prime}}-a))
.
Removing the open neighborhood of
v_{3}
then gives

3=\reg(R^{\prime\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime}-a))=\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime\prime}))
.

Step 5.
Set
H=G^{\prime\prime\prime}-v_{2}
. We show
\reg(R^{\prime}/I(H))=3
.

Consider the two Mayer–Vietoris-type sequences

0\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{T_{1}}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(G^{\prime\prime\prime}),v_{2})}\oplus\frac{R^{\prime}}{(v_{2}w:w\in N_{G^{\prime\prime\prime}}(v_{2}))}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(G^{\prime\prime\prime})}\to 0,

0\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{T_{2}}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{(I(H),v_{1})}\oplus\frac{R^{\prime}}{(v_{1}w:w\in N_{H}(v_{1}))}\to\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(H)}\to 0,

where
T_{1}=(I(G^{\prime\prime\prime}),v_{2})\cap(v_{2}w:w\in N_{G^{\prime\prime\prime}}(v_{2}))

and
T_{2}=(I(H),v_{1})\cap(v_{1}w:w\in N_{H}(v_{1}))
.

Since
(v_{2}w:w\in N_{G^{\prime\prime\prime}}(v_{2}))
and
(v_{1}w:w\in N_{H}(v_{1}))
have linear
resolutions, Lemma
1.10
implies both sequences are Betti splittings.
Therefore

\reg(G^{\prime\prime\prime})=\max\!\left\{\reg\!\left(\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(H)}\right),\,\reg\!\left(\frac{R^{\prime}}{T_{1}}\right)-1\right\},

\reg(H)=\max\!\left\{\reg\!\left(\frac{R^{\prime}}{I(H-v_{1})}\right),\,\reg\!\left(\frac{R^{\prime}}{T_{2}}\right)-1\right\}.

The minimal monomial generators of
T_{1}
and
T_{2}
include
v_{2}e
and
v_{1}e

respectively for
e\subset N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b)
, so their regularities are at least
2
.
Moreover,
v_{1}\in N_{G^{\prime\prime\prime}}(v_{2})
and

(v_{2}w:w\in N_{G}(a)\cap N_{G}(b))=(T_{1}:v_{1})
, giving

\reg(R^{\prime}/T_{1})=\reg(R^{\prime}/(T_{1},v_{1}))=\reg(R^{\prime}/T_{2})

from the exact sequence
(T_{1}:v_{1})(-1)\to(T_{1})\to(T_{1},v_{1})
and

(T_{1},v_{1})\cong(T_{2})+(f;f\in\Gamma(T_{1})\text{ with }v_{1}\text{ divide f})+(v_{1})=(T_{2})
.
Therefore we obtain
3=\reg(R^{\prime}/I(G^{\prime\prime\prime}))=\reg(R^{\prime}/I(H))
.

Step 6.
We show
H
is vertex-minimal with respect to
\reg=3
.

Let
S=R[v_{1}]=R^{\prime}/v_{2}
. Since
H-v_{1}\subsetneq G
, clearly

\reg(S/I(H-v_{1}))\leq 2
.
Suppose there exists
w\in V(H)\setminus\{v_{1}\}
with
\reg(S/I(H-w))=3
.
Reversing the argument of Steps 2–5 with
H
replaced by
H-w
yields

\reg(R/I(G-w))=3
, contradicting the vertex-minimality of
G
.
Hence
H
is vertex-minimal.

Step 7.
We verify that
G
satisfies the neighborhood condition.

Since
H^{c}=G^{c}/\{a,b\}
(the contraction of the edge
\{a,b\}
in
G^{c}
)
and
|V(H)|=|V(G)|-1
, the induction hypothesis applies to
H
.
By hypothesis, the neighborhood
H_{w}
of every vertex
w
of
H
is an anticycle.
We now deduce the same for
G
.

Since
H^{c}
is the
1
-skeleton of a triangulation of a closed
2
-manifold
(a pseudomanifold), every edge of
H^{c}
is contained in exactly two triangles.
In particular, there exists
w\in N_{H^{c}}(v_{1})
.
Since
H_{w}
is an anticycle, there exist vertices
s,t
(other than
v_{1}
) with

\{s,t\}\notin E(H)
.
At least one of
s,t
— say
s
— is not contained in
N_{G^{c}}(a)\cup N_{G^{c}}(b)
.

Since
H_{s}=G_{s}
is an anticycle and
G_{s}
contains no anticycle avoiding
t
,
a parallel argument applies with
s
and
t
replacing
a
and
b
, producing a
graph
H^{\prime}
.
Both
(H_{v})^{c}
and
(H^{\prime}_{a})^{c}
,
(H^{\prime}_{b})^{c}
are induced cycles.
Hence
(G_{a})^{c}
and
(G_{b})^{c}
are subgraphs of induced cycles that themselves contain
induced cycles, so they must be induced cycles.

Repeating this argument for every vertex in
N_{G^{c}}(a)\cup N_{G^{c}}(b)
and using the
neighborhoods in
H^{c}
and
(H^{\prime})^{c}
, we conclude that the neighborhood of every vertex
of
G^{c}
is an induced cycle.

Conclusion.
By Fact
1.12
,
G^{c}
is the
1
-skeleton of a triangulation
of a closed
2
-manifold.

Let
\Delta(G)
be the independent complex of
G
.
From the vertex-minimality of
G
, we obtain

\widetilde{H}_{2}(\Delta(G)_{|W};k)=0
for any
W\subset V(G)
,
and from
\reg(R/I(G))=3
,

\widetilde{H}_{2}(\Delta(G);k)\neq 0
by Lemma
1.11
.
Hence, when
\ch(k)\neq 2
,
\Delta(G)
is a triangulation of an orientable surface
(Fact
1.13
); when
\ch(k)=2
,
\Delta(G)
is a triangulation of any closed
surface.

Conversely, suppose
G^{c}
contains a triangulation
\Sigma
of an orientable closed surface as a subgraph. Let
W=V(\Sigma)
and let
\Delta_{W}
denote the restriction of the independence complex of
G
to
W
.
Since
\Sigma\subset G^{c}_{|W}
, the triangles of
\Sigma

are faces of the clique complex of
G^{c}_{|W}
, which equals the independence complex
(\Delta(G))_{W}
.
Therefore
\widetilde{H}_{2}((\Delta(G))_{W};\,k)\supseteq\widetilde{H}_{2}(\Sigma;\,k)\cong k\neq 0
by Fact
1.13
.

Applying Hochster’s formula (Lemma
1.11
) with
j=|W|
and
i=|W|-3
,
we obtain
\beta_{|W|-3,\,|W|}(R/I(G))\geq 1
,
and hence
\reg(R/I(G))\geq|W|-(|W|-3)=3
.

∎
