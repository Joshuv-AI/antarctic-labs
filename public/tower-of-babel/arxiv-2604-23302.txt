1.
Introduction

In this paper, we obtain the following weighted trigonometric summation formula.

Theorem 1.1
.

Let
A
be a nonsingular
d\times d
integer matrix and
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. Then,

(1.1)

\begin{split}\sum_{\xi\in T_{A}^{*}}{\left\langle w,\cos(2\pi\xi)\right\rangle}^{n}=\frac{|\det A|}{2^{n}}\sum_{x\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{y\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|x|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(x)+y)!y!}w^{{\rm abs}(x)+2y}\end{split}

for any
n\geq 1
. Here,
T_{A}^{*}=(A^{T})^{-1}\mathbb{Z}^{d}/\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and for any
\xi\in T_{A}^{*}
,

\cos(2\pi\xi):=\left(\cos(2\pi\xi_{1}),\cos(2\pi\xi_{2}),\cdots,\cos(2\pi\xi_{d})\right)\in\mathbb{R}^{d},

{\rm abs}(x)=(|x_{1}|,|x_{2}|,\cdots,|x_{d}|)\mbox{ and }|x|=\sum_{i=1}^{d}|x_{i}|

for
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and

x!=x_{1}!x_{2}!\cdots x_{d}!\mbox{ and }w^{x}=w_{1}^{x_{1}}w_{2}^{x_{2}}\cdots w_{d}^{x_{d}}

for
x\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
. Moreover, for any
k\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}
,

\mathscr{P}(k,d)=\{x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}\ |\ |x|=k\}.

When
k<0
or
k\not\in\mathbb{Z}
,
\mathscr{P}(k,d)=\emptyset.

In Theorem
1.1
, when
w_{1}=w_{2}=\cdots=w_{d}=1
, the identity (
1.1
) becomes the trigonometric summation formula obtained by Grygor’yan-Lin-Yau
[
7
]
. When
\det A=\pm 1
,
T_{A}^{*}=\{0\}
and the identity (
1.1
) becomes the multinomial theorem:

(w_{1}+w_{2}+\cdots+w_{d})^{n}=\sum_{m\in\mathscr{P}(n,d)}\frac{n!}{m!}w^{m}.

By applying the multinomial theorem to the LHS of (
1.1
), and comparing the coefficients of the polynomials on
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d}
of the both sides of (
1.1
), we have the following corollary.

Corollary 1.1
.

Let
A
be a nonsingular
d\times d
integer matrix and
m\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}
. Then,

(1.2)

\sum_{\xi\in T_{A}^{*}}[\cos(2\pi\xi)]^{m}=\frac{|\det A|}{2^{|m|}}\sum_{\overset{x\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d},y\in\mathbb{Z}_{\geq 0}^{d}}{{\rm abs}(x)+2y=m}}\frac{m!}{({\rm abs}(x)+y)!y!}.

The proof of Theorem
1.1
is following the arguments in
[
7
]
and
[
4
]
. It can be viewed as a discrete version of Poisson summation formula or a combinatorial trace formula.

Let’s recall the classical trace formula and Poisson summation formula (see
[
2
]
). Let
(M^{n},g)
be a closed Riemannian manifold, and

0=\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{k}\leq\cdots

be the Laplacian eigenvalues of
M
counting multiplicities. Let

\varphi_{1},\varphi_{2},\cdots,\varphi_{k},\cdots

be an orthonormal system of real-valued eigenfunctions with

\Delta\varphi_{i}=-\mu_{i}\varphi_{i}.

Then, the heat kernel
H(x,y,t)
of
M
can be written as

H(x,y,t)=\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{i}t}\varphi_{i}(x)\varphi_{i}(y).

In particular, we have

(1.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{\infty}e^{-\mu_{i}t}=\int_{M}H(x,x,t)dV_{g}(x).

This is the classical trace formula. When considering some special Riemannian manifolds where the spectrum data and heat kernel are explicitly known, we will get interesting summation formulas. For example, consider the unit circle
\mathbb{S}^{1}=\mathbb{R}/2\pi\mathbb{Z}
. The Laplacian eigenvalues of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
are

(1.4)

\mu_{1}=0,\mu_{2}=\mu_{3}=1,\cdots,\mu_{2k}=\mu_{2k+1}=k^{2},\cdots.

The heat kernel of
\mathbb{S}^{1}
is

H([x],[y],t)=\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}p(x+2\pi k,y,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{|x+2\pi k-y|^{2}}{4t}\right)

where

p(x,y,t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}e^{-\frac{|x-y|^{2}}{4t}}

is the heat kernel of
\mathbb{R}
. So

H([x],[x],t)=\frac{1}{\sqrt{4\pi t}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}k^{2}}{t}\right)

and

(1.5)

\int_{\mathbb{S}^{1}}H([x],[x],t)ds=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}k^{2}}{t}\right).

So, by substituting (
1.4
) and (
1.5
) into (
1.3
), we get the classical Poisson summation formula:

\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}e^{-k^{2}t}=\sqrt{\frac{\pi}{t}}\sum_{k\in\mathbb{Z}}\exp\left(-\frac{\pi^{2}k^{2}}{t}\right).

The proof of Theorem
1.1
goes in the same way as the above. Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted finite graph,

0=\mu_{1}\leq\mu_{2}\leq\cdots\leq\mu_{|V(G)|}

be its Laplacian eigenvalues, and
q_{n}(x,y)
be the discrete-time heat kernel of
G
. Then, by a similar argument as the above, one has the discrete trace formula

(1.6)

\sum_{i=1}^{|V(G)|}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}=\sum_{x\in V(G)}q_{n}(x,x)m_{x}.

For details, see Section 2. Then, by applying (
1.6
) to the discrete tori
\mathbb{Z}^{d}/A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
equipped with a symmetric quotient weight where the both sides of (
1.6
) can be computed explicitly, one obtains (
1.1
). For details, see Section 3. Some interesting analysis on discrete tori can be found in
[
3
,
5
,
8
,
9
]
.

2.
Preliminaries

We first recall some preliminaries on analysis of graphs and derive the discrete trace formula.

Definition 2.1
.

A triple
(G,m,w)
is called a weighted graph if

(1)

G
is a simple graph,

(2)

m:V(G)\to(0,+\infty)
and

(3)

w:E(G)\to(0,+\infty)
.

The function
m
is called the vertex measure and the function
w
is called the edge weight.

For convenience, we also view
w
as a function on
V(G)\times V(G)
which is also denoted as
w
with

w_{xy}=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}w(e)&e=\{x,y\}\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

For two functions
f,g:V(G)\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support, define their inner product as

{\left\langle f,g\right\rangle}=\sum_{x\in V}f(x)\overline{g(x)}m_{x}.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph. Then, the Laplacian operator
\Delta
on
G
is defined as

\Delta f(x)=\frac{1}{m_{x}}\sum_{y\in V(G)}(f(y)-f(x))w_{xy},\ \forall\ x\in V(G),

where
f\in\mathbb{C}^{V(G)}
. The Laplacian operator is formally self-adjoint:

{\left\langle\Delta f,g\right\rangle}={\left\langle f,\Delta g\right\rangle}

for any
f,g:V(G)\to\mathbb{C}
with finite support.

A sequence of functions
q_{n}:V(G)\times V(G)\to\mathbb{R}
with
n\geq 0
such that

(2.1)

\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}q_{n+1}(x,y)-q_{n}(x,y)=\Delta_{x}q_{n}(x,y)&n\geq 0\\
q_{0}(x,y)=\delta_{y}(x),\end{array}\right.

is called the discrete-time heat kernel of
G
. Here

\delta_{y}(x)=\frac{1}{m_{y}}\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)

where

\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)=\left\{\begin{array}[]{ll}1&x=y\\
0&\mbox{otherwise.}\end{array}\right.

When
(G,m,w)
is a weighted finite graph, we have the following expression of the discrete-time heat kernel.

Proposition 2.1
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted finite graph on
N
vertices, and
f_{1},f_{2},\cdots,f_{N}
be a unitary system of Laplacian eigenfunctions with

\Delta f_{i}=-\mu_{i}f_{i}.

Then,

(2.2)

q_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}f_{i}(x)\overline{f_{i}(y)},\ \forall\ n=0,1,2,\cdots.

In particular,

(2.3)

\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}=\sum_{x\in V(G)}q_{n}(x,x)m_{x},\ \forall\ n=0,1,2,\cdots.

Proof.

Let
Q_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}f_{i}(x)\overline{f_{i}(y)}
. Then,

\begin{split}Q_{n+1}(x,y)-Q_{n}(x,y)=&-\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}\mu_{i}f_{i}(x)\overline{f_{i}(y)}\\
=&\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}\Delta_{x}f_{i}(x)\overline{f_{i}(y)}\\
=&\Delta_{x}Q_{n}(x,y).\end{split}

Moreover, for any
f:V(G)\to\mathbb{C}
, suppose that

f=\sum_{i=1}^{N}c_{i}f_{i}.

Then,

{\left\langle Q_{0}(\cdot,y),f\right\rangle}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}{\left\langle f_{i},f\right\rangle}\overline{f_{i}(y)}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}\overline{c_{i}}\cdot\overline{\varphi_{i}(y)}=\overline{f(y)}={\left\langle\delta_{y},f\right\rangle}.

So,
Q_{0}(x,y)=\delta_{y}(x).
Thus, the sequence of functions
Q_{n}(x,y)
with
n\geq 0
also satisfy (
2.1
) and hence

q_{n}(x,y)=Q_{n}(x,y),\forall\ x,y\in V(G).

This gives us (
2.2
).

Furthermore, by (
2.2
), we have

\sum_{x\in V(G)}q_{n}(x,x)m_{x}=\sum_{x\in V(G)}\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}f_{i}(x)\overline{f_{i}(x)}m_{x}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}(1-\mu_{i})^{n}.

This completes the proof of the proposition.
∎

Next, we introduce a way to compute the discrete-time heat kernel
q_{n}(x,y)
which is similar to that in
[
6
,
1
]
. Here, we present the method for arbitrary weighted locally finite graphs.

By (
2.1
), we have

(2.4)

\begin{split}q_{n+1}(x,y)=&q_{n}(x,y)+\Delta_{x}q_{n}(x,y)\\
=&q_{n}(x,y)+\frac{1}{m_{x}}\sum_{z\in V}(q_{n}(z,y)-q_{n}(x,y))w_{xz}\\
=&(1-{\rm Deg}(x))q_{n}(x,y)+\sum_{z\in V}\frac{w_{xz}}{m_{x}}q_{n}(z,y),\end{split}

where

{\rm Deg}(x)=\frac{1}{m_{x}}\sum_{y\in V}\frac{w_{xy}}{m_{x}}.

In particular,

q_{1}(x,y)=(1-{\rm Deg}(x))q_{0}(x,y)+\frac{w_{xy}}{m_{x}m_{y}}=p_{1}(x,y)/m_{y},

where

(2.5)

p_{1}(x,y)=(1-{\rm Deg}(x))\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)+\frac{w_{xy}}{m_{x}}.

Moreover, by (
2.4
),

(2.6)

\begin{split}q_{n+1}(x,y)=&(1-{\rm Deg}(x))q_{n}(x,y)+\sum_{z\in V}\frac{w_{xz}}{m_{x}}q_{n}(z,y)\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}\left((1-{\rm Deg}(x))\mathbf{1}_{x}(z)+\frac{w_{xz}}{m_{x}}\right)q_{n}(z,y)\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}\left((1-{\rm Deg}(x))\frac{\mathbf{1}_{z}(x)}{m_{z}}+\frac{w_{xz}}{m_{x}m_{z}}\right)q_{n}(z,y)m_{z}\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}q_{1}(x,z)q_{n}(z,y)m_{z}.\end{split}

Let
P:\mathbb{C}^{V(G)}\to\mathbb{C}^{V(G)}
be defined as

(Pf)(x)=\sum_{y\in V(G)}p_{1}(x,y)f(y)

which is analogue of the Markov operator in
[
6
]
. Suppose

(P^{n}f)(x)=\sum_{y\in V(G)}p_{n}(x,y)f(y).

By this definition, it is natural to take
p_{0}(x,y)=\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)
. Then, by that

P^{m+n}f=P^{m}(P^{n}f),

we have

(2.7)

p_{m+n}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in V(G)}p_{m}(x,z)p_{n}(z,y)

for any nonnegative integers
m
and
n
. Here
p_{n}(x,y)
can be viewed as an analogue of the
n
-step transition probability from
x
to
y
in
[
6
,
1
]
.

Similar to that in
[
6
,
1
]
, we have the following properties for
q_{n}
and its relation to
p_{n}
.

Lemma 2.1
.

Let the notations be the same as in the above. Then,

(1)

for any
x,y\in V(G)
and nonnegative integer
n
,

q_{n}(x,y)=p_{n}(x,y)/m_{y};

(2)

for any
x,y\in V(G)
and nonnegative integers
m
and
n
,

q_{m+n}(x,y)=\sum_{z\in V}q_{m}(x,z)q_{n}(z,y)m_{z};

(3)

for any
x,y\in V(G)
and nonnegative integers
n
,

q_{n}(x,y)=q_{n}(y,x).

Proof.

(1) We prove this by induction on
n
. It is true for
n=0,1
by definition. Suppose the conclusion is true for
n=k
. When
n=k+1
, by (
2.6
) and (
2.7
), we have

(2.8)

\begin{split}q_{k+1}(x,y)=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}q_{1}(x,z)q_{k}(z,y)m_{z}\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}p_{1}(x,z)p_{k}(z,y)/m_{y}\\
=&p_{k+1}(x,y)/m_{y}.\end{split}

So, we have the conclusion (1).

(2) By (1) and (
2.7
), we have

(2.9)

\begin{split}q_{m+n}(x,y)=&p_{m+n}(x,y)/m_{y}\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}(p_{m}(x,z)/m_{z})(p_{n}(z,y)/m_{y})m_{z}\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}q_{m}(x,z)q_{n}(z,y)m_{z}.\end{split}

(3) We also prove it by induction. The conclusion is clearly true for
n=0,1
by definition. Suppose the conclusion is true for
n=k
. When
n=k+1
, by (
2.6
) and (2), we have

\begin{split}q_{k+1}(x,y)=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}q_{1}(x,z)q_{k}(z,y)m_{z}\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}q_{k}(y,z)q_{1}(z,x)m_{z}\\
=&q_{k+1}(y,x).\end{split}

∎

By (1) of Lemma
2.1
, to compute the discrete-time heat kernel
q_{n}
, we only need to compute the
n
-step transition probability
p_{n}
. Note that, by (
2.5
),
p_{1}(x,y)=0
when
x\neq y
and
x\not\sim y
, and when
{\rm Deg}(x)=1
for any
x\in V(G)
,
p_{1}(x,y)=0
for
x\not\sim y
. Combining these with (
2.7
), we have the following expression for
p_{n}(x,y)
.

Corollary 2.1
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph. Then, for any
x,y\in V(G)
and positive integer
n
,

(2.10)

p_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{\omega\in\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}(x,y)}p_{1}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})p_{1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\cdots p_{1}(\omega_{n-1},\omega_{n}),

where
\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}(x,y)
is the collection of
n
-step generalized walks from
x
to
y
. That is,

\widetilde{\Omega}_{n}(x,y)=\{\omega:x=\omega_{0}\overset{=}{\sim}\omega_{1}\overset{=}{\sim}\cdots\overset{=}{\sim}\omega_{n}=y|\omega_{i}\in V(G),\ \forall i=0,1,\cdots,n\}.

Here
u\overset{=}{\sim}v
means that
u\sim v
or
u=v
. In particular, if
(G,m,w)
is normalized weighted, that is
{\rm Deg}(v)=1
for any
v\in V(G)
, then for any
x,y\in V(G)
and positive integer
n
,

(2.11)

p_{n}(x,y)=\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}(x,y)}p_{1}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})p_{1}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\cdots p_{1}(\omega_{n-1},\omega_{n}),

where
\Omega_{n}(x,y)
is the collection of
n
-step walks from
x
to
y
. That is,

\Omega_{n}(x,y)=\{\omega:x=\omega_{0}\sim\omega_{1}\sim\cdots\sim\omega_{n}=y|\omega_{i}\in V(G),\ \forall i=0,1,\cdots,n\}.

Finally, we recall something about eigenfunctions and discrete-time heat kernel on quotient graphs which is an analogue of that in
[
7
]
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph. A bijection
\varphi:V(G)\to V(G)
is called an automorphism of
(G,m,w)
if

m\circ\varphi=m\mbox{ and }w\circ(\varphi\times\varphi)=w\circ(\varphi\times\varphi).

It is clear that an automorphism of the weighted graph must be at least an graph automorphism. The collection of automorphisms for
(G,m,w)
is denoted as
{\rm Aut}(G,m,w)
which is called the automorphism group of
(G,m,w)
.

By definition, it is not hard to see that
{\rm Deg}
,
p_{n}
and
q_{n}
are invariant under automorphisms of
(G,m,w)
.

Lemma 2.2
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph and
\varphi\in{\rm Aut}(G,m,w)
. Then, for any
x,y\in V
,
{\rm Deg}(\varphi(x))={\rm Deg}(x)
,

p_{n}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=p_{n}(x,y)

and

q_{n}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=q_{n}(x,y)

for any nonnegative integer
n
.

Proof.

First,

\begin{split}{\rm Deg}(\varphi(x))=&\frac{1}{m(\varphi(x))}\sum_{y\in V(G)}w(\varphi(x),y)\\
=&\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in V(G)}w(x,\varphi^{-1}(y))\\
=&{\rm Deg}(x).\end{split}

Moreover, it is clear that

p_{0}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=\mathbf{1}_{\varphi(y)}(\varphi(x))=\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)=p_{0}(x,y)

and

\begin{split}p_{1}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=&(1-{\rm Deg}(\varphi(x)))\mathbf{1}_{\varphi(y)}(\varphi(x))+\frac{w(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))}{m(\varphi(x))}\\
=&(1-{\rm Deg}(x))\mathbf{1}_{y}(x)+\frac{w(x,y)}{m(x)}\\
=&p_{1}(x,y).\\
\end{split}

So,
p_{0}
and
p_{1}
are invariant under
\varphi
. Suppose
p_{n}
is invariant under
\varphi
for
n=k
. Then, when
n=k+1
, by (
2.7
)

(2.12)

\begin{split}p_{k+1}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}p_{1}(\varphi(x),z)p_{k}(z,\varphi(y))\\
=&\sum_{z\in V(G)}p_{1}(x,\varphi^{-1}(z))p_{k}(\varphi^{-1}(z),y)\\
=&p_{k+1}(x,y).\end{split}

Finally, by (1) of Lemma
2.1
,

q_{n}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))=p_{n}(\varphi(x),\varphi(y))/m(\varphi(y))=p_{n}(x,y)/m(y)=q_{n}(x,y).

∎

Next, we recall the definition of weighted quotient graph in
[
7
]
.

Definition 2.2
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph and

\Gamma
be a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G,m,\mu)
. Then, the weighted quotient graph

Q=G/\Gamma
is defined as follows:

(1)

V(Q):=V(G)/\Gamma
;

(2)

E(Q):=\{\{[x],[y]\}\ |\ [x]\neq[y]\ \mbox{ and }E_{G}([x],[y])\neq\emptyset\}
;

(3)

for any
[x]\in V(Q)
,

m_{Q}([x]):=m_{G}(x);

(4)

for any
\{[x],[y]\}\in E(Q)
,

w_{Q}([x],[y]):=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}w_{G}(x,g_{.}y).

It is not hard to see that the quotient weighted graph
(Q,m_{Q},w_{Q})
is well-defined:

(1)

The vertex-measure
m_{Q}
and edge-weight
w_{Q}
are well-defined since
m_{G}
and
w_{G}
are invariant under
\Gamma
;

(2)

For any
[x],[y]\in V(Q)
,
[x]\sim_{Q}[y]
if and only if
w_{Q}([x],[y])>0
.

The following properties of weighted quotient graphs come directly from definition.

Proposition 2.2
.

Let
(G,m,w)
be a weighted locally finite graph,
\Gamma
be a subgroup of
{\rm Aut}(G,m,w)
and
(Q,m_{Q},w_{Q})
be the weighted quotient graph of
G
over
\Gamma
. Then,

(1)

for any
[x]\in V(Q)
,

{\rm Deg}_{Q}([x])={\rm Deg}_{G}(x);

(2)

for any
f\in\mathbb{C}^{V(Q)}
,

\pi^{*}(\Delta_{Q}f)=\Delta_{G}(\pi^{*}f)

where
\pi:V(G)\to V(Q)
is the quotient map;

(3)

if
G
is a normalized weighted graph or
\Gamma
acts on
G
freely, then for any
[x],[y]\in V(Q)
and positive integer
n
,

p_{n}^{Q}([x],[y])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p^{G}_{n}(x,g_{.}y);

(4)

if
G
is a normalized weighted graph or
\Gamma
acts on
G
freely, then for any
[x],[y]\in V(Q)
and positive integer
n
,

q_{n}^{Q}([x],[y])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}q_{n}^{G}(x,g_{.}y).

Proof.

(1) For any
[x]\in V(Q)
,

\begin{split}{\rm Deg}_{Q}([x])=&\frac{1}{m_{Q}([x])}\sum_{[y]\in V(Q)}w_{Q}([x],[y])\\
=&\frac{1}{m_{G}(x)}\sum_{[y]\in V(Q)}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}w_{G}(x,g_{.}y)\\
=&{\rm Deg}_{G}(x).\end{split}

(2) For any
x\in V(G)
,

(2.13)

\begin{split}\pi^{*}(\Delta_{Q}f)(x)=&\Delta_{Q}f([x])\\
=&\frac{1}{m_{Q}([x])}\sum_{[y]\in V(Q)}(f([y])-f([x]))w_{Q}([x],[y])\\
=&\frac{1}{m_{G}(x)}\sum_{[y]\in V(Q)}(\pi^{*}f(y)-\pi^{*}f(x))\sum_{g\in\Gamma}w_{G}(x,g_{.}y)\\
=&\frac{1}{m_{G}(x)}\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\sum_{[y]\in V(Q)}(\pi^{*}f(g_{.}y)-\pi^{*}f(x))w_{G}(x,g_{.}y)\\
=&(\Delta_{G}\pi^{*}f)(x).\end{split}

(3) For any
[x],[y]\in V(Q)
, by (
2.5
),

(2.14)

\begin{split}p_{1}^{Q}([x],[y])=&(1-{\rm Deg}_{Q}([x]))\mathbf{1}_{[y]}([x])+\frac{w_{Q}([x],[y])}{m_{Q}([x])}\\
=&(1-{\rm Deg}_{G}(x))\mathbf{1}_{[y]}([x])+\frac{\sum_{g\in\Gamma}w_{G}(x,g_{.}y)}{m_{G}(x)}\\
\end{split}

If
{\rm Deg}_{G}(x)=1
for any
x\in V(G)
, then

p_{1}^{Q}([x],[y])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\frac{w_{G}(x,g_{.}y)}{m_{x}}=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,g_{.}y).

If
\Gamma
acts on
G
freely, then

\mathbf{1}_{[y]}([x])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}\mathbf{1}_{g_{.}y}(x)

since if
[y]=[x]
, then there is a unique
g\in\Gamma
such that
x=g_{.}y
. So, the same as before, we have

p_{1}^{Q}([x],[y])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,g_{.}y).

Suppose that the conclusion is true for
n=k
. Then, when
n=k+1
, by (
2.7
) and Lemma
2.2
,

(2.15)

\begin{split}p_{k+1}^{Q}([x],[y])=&\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}p_{1}^{Q}([x],[z])p_{k}^{Q}([z],[y])\\
=&\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}\left(\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,g_{.}z)\right)\left(\sum_{h\in\Gamma}p_{k}^{G}(z,h_{.}y)\right)\\
=&\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}\sum_{g,h\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,g_{.}z)p_{k}^{G}(z,h_{.}y)\\
=&\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}\sum_{g,h\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,g_{.}z)p_{k}^{G}(g_{.}z,gh_{.}y)\\
=&\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}\sum_{h,k\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,kh^{-1}_{.}z)p_{k}^{G}(kh^{-1}_{.}z,k_{.}y)\\
=&\sum_{k\in\Gamma}\sum_{[z]\in V(Q)}\sum_{h\in\Gamma}p_{1}^{G}(x,kh^{-1}_{.}z)p_{k}^{G}(kh^{-1}_{.}z,k_{.}y)\\
=&\sum_{k\in\Gamma}p^{G}_{k+1}(x,k_{.}y).\end{split}

This completes the proof of (3).

(4) By (1) of Lemma
2.1
and (3), we have

q_{n}^{Q}([x],[y])=p^{Q}_{n}([x],[y])/m_{Q}([y])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p^{G}_{n}(x,g_{.}y)/m_{G}(g_{.}y)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}q_{n}^{G}(x,g_{.}y).

∎

Remark 2.1
.

Note that

p_{0}^{Q}([x],[y])=1_{[y]}([x])=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}1_{g_{.}y}(x)=\sum_{g\in\Gamma}p_{0}^{G}(x,g_{.}y)

is only true when
\Gamma
acts on
G
freely.

3.
Weighted tori and proof of Theorem
1.1

In this section, we compute the discrete-time heat kernel and the Laplacian spectrum of the weighted tori
T_{A}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
(see the definition below), and apply (
2.3
) to prove Theorem
1.1
.

Let
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the integer lattice graph and
w=(w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d})\in\mathbb{R}^{d}_{>0}
.
Let

w_{\{x,x+e_{i}\}}=w_{i}>0

for
i=1,2,\cdots,d
and
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, and

m_{x}=\sigma:=2\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}

for any
x\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Then,
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},m,w)
is a normalized weighted graph.

For
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{n}
, denote the translation map
x\mapsto x+z
as
\tau_{z}
. It is clear that

\tau_{z},{\rm diag}(\pm 1,\cdots,\pm 1)\in{\rm Aut}(\mathbb{Z}^{d},m,w)

for any
z\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.
So, by Lemma
2.2
,

(3.1)

p_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(x,y)=p_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(0,y-x)=p_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(0,{\rm abs}(y-x))

for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
.

Theorem 3.1
.

Let
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},m,w)
be the weighted integer lattice graph defined above. Then, for any
x,y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}
and positive integer
n
,

p_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{n}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y-x|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(y-x)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(y-x)+2z}

and

q_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(x,y)=\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+1}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y-x|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(y-x)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(y-x)+2z}.

Proof.

By (
3.1
) and (1) of Lemma
2.1
, we only need to prove the first formula for
x=0
and
y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}_{\geq 0}
. Let

\omega:0=\omega_{0}\sim\omega_{1}\sim\cdots\sim\omega_{n}=y

be a walk joining
0
to
y
and
u_{i}=\omega_{i}-\omega_{i-1}
. Then
u_{i}=\pm e_{k_{i}}
for some
k_{i}=1,2,\cdots,d
, and

u_{1}+u_{2}+\cdots+u_{n}=y.

Let
a_{k}
be the number of
e_{k}
in
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n}
and
z_{k}
be the number of
-e_{k}
in
u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n}
. Then,

\sum_{k=1}^{d}a_{k}+\sum_{k=1}^{d}z_{k}=n

and

a_{k}-z_{k}=y_{k}

for
k=1,2,\cdots,d
. It is then clear that

z\in\mathscr{P}\left(\frac{n-|y|}{2},d\right).

Conversely, for any
z\in\mathscr{P}\left(\frac{n-|y|}{2},d\right)
, let
a=y+z
. Then, each element in

\mathscr{U}(y,z):=\left\{u:=(u_{1},u_{2},\cdots,u_{n})\bigg|\begin{array}[]{l}\mbox{$e_{k}$ and $-e_{k}$ appears $a_{k}$ and $z_{k}$ times in $u$}\\
\mbox{respectively, for $k=1,2,\cdots,d$.}\end{array}\right\}

corresponds to a walk

\omega:0=\omega_{0}\sim\omega_{1}\sim\cdots\sim\omega_{n}=y

with
u_{i}=\omega_{i}-\omega_{i-1}
. Therefore, by (
2.11
) and (
3.1
),

\begin{split}p_{n}^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(0,y)=&\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}(0,y)}p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(\omega_{0},\omega_{1})\partial_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\omega_{1},\omega_{2})\cdot p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(\omega_{n-1},\omega_{n})\\
=&\sum_{\omega\in\Omega_{n}(0,y)}p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(0,\omega_{1}-\omega_{0})p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(0,\omega_{2}-\omega_{1})\cdots p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(0,\omega_{n}-\omega_{n-1})\\
=&\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y|}{2},d)}\sum_{u\in\mathscr{U}(y,z)}p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}_{n}}(0,u_{1})p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(0,u_{2})\cdots p_{1}^{\mathbb{Z}^{n}}(0,u_{n})\\
=&\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y|}{2},d)}|\mathscr{U}(y,z)|\frac{w^{y+2z}}{\sigma^{n}}\\
=&\frac{1}{\sigma^{n}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{(y+z)!z!}w^{y+2z}.\end{split}

This completes the proof of the theorem.
∎

For any
d\times d
nonsingular integer matrix
A
,
A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
is a lattice in
\mathbb{Z}^{d}
. Note that the weighted graph
(\mathbb{Z}^{d},m,w)
is invariant under translations. It induces a weighted quotient graph on
T_{A}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
as in Definition
2.2
. We call
T_{A}
the weighted discrete tori induced by
A
. By (4) of Proposition
2.2
, we have the following conclusion.

Corollary 3.1
.

Let
A
be a
d\times d
nonsingular integer matrix and
T_{A}=\mathbb{Z}^{d}/A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
be the weighted discrete tori induced by
A
defined above. Then, for any
[x],[y]\in T_{A}
and
n\geq 1
,

(3.2)

q_{n}^{T_{A}}([x],[y])=\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+1}}\sum_{g\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|y+g-x|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(y+g-x)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(y+g-x)+2z}.

In particular,

(3.3)

q_{n}^{T_{A}}([x],[x])=\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+1}}\sum_{g\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|g|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(g)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(g)+2z}

for any
[x]\in T_{A}
and
n\geq 1
.

Next, we imitate the method in
[
7
]
to compute the Laplacian spectrum of
T_{A}
.

Proposition 3.1
.

Let
A
be a
d\times d
nonsingular integer matrix and
T_{A}
be the weighted discrete torus induced by
A
. Then,

(1)

for any
\xi\in T^{*}_{A}
,
f_{\xi}(x)=e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}{\left\langle\xi,x\right\rangle}}
is a Laplacian eigenfunction of
T_{A}
with eigenvalue
\left(1-\frac{2}{\sigma}{\left\langle w,\cos(2\pi\xi)\right\rangle}\right)
;

(2)

the family of functions
f_{\xi}
with
\xi\in T_{A}^{*}
are orthogonal of each other;

(3)

the Laplacian spectrum of
T_{A}
consists of
\left(1-\frac{2}{\sigma}{\left\langle w,\cos(2\pi\xi)\right\rangle}\right)
with
\xi\in T_{A}^{*}
.

Proof.

(1) The same as the argument in the proof of
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
, we know that
f_{\xi}
is invariant under
A\mathbb{Z}^{d}
, So
f_{\xi}
can be viewed as a function on
T_{A}
. Moreover,
note that

\begin{split}\Delta_{\mathbb{Z}^{d}}f_{\xi}(x)=&\frac{1}{m(x)}\sum_{y\in\mathbb{Z}^{d}}(f_{\xi}(y)-f_{\xi}(x))w_{xy}\\
=&\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{d}(f_{\xi}(x+e_{i})+f_{\xi}(x-e_{i})-2f_{\xi}(x))w_{i}\\
=&\frac{1}{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{d}\left(e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}{\left\langle\xi,(x+e_{i})\right\rangle}}+e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}{\left\langle\xi,(x-e_{i})\right\rangle}}-2e^{2\pi\sqrt{-1}{\left\langle\xi,x\right\rangle}}\right)w_{i}\\
=&-\left(1-\frac{2}{\sigma}\sum_{i=1}^{d}w_{i}\cos(2\pi\xi_{i})\right)f_{\xi}(x).\end{split}

Then, by (2) of Proposition
2.2
, we complete the proof of (1).

(2) The conclusion is proved in
[
7
, Lemma 3.1]
.

(3) Note that
|T_{A}|=|T_{A}^{*}|=|\det A|
. Combining this with (1) and (2), we get the conclusion.
∎

We are now ready to prove Theorem
1.1
by using (
2.3
).

Proof of Theorem
1.1
.

Applying (
2.3
) to the weighted discrete tori
T_{A}
, and using Corollary
3.1
and Proposition
3.1
, we have

\begin{split}&\sum_{\xi\in T_{A}^{*}}\left(\frac{2}{\sigma}{\left\langle w,\cos(2\pi\xi)\right\rangle}\right)^{n}\\
=&\sum_{[x]\in T_{A}}q_{n}^{T_{A}}([x],[x])m_{T_{A}}([x])\\
=&|\det A|\sigma\times\frac{1}{\sigma^{n+1}}\sum_{g\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|g|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(g)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(g)+2z}\\
=&\frac{|\det A|}{\sigma^{n}}\sum_{g\in A\mathbb{Z}^{d}}\sum_{z\in\mathscr{P}(\frac{n-|g|}{2},d)}\frac{n!}{({\rm abs}(g)+z)!z!}w^{{\rm abs}(g)+2z}\\
\end{split}

for
n\geq 1
. This gives us (
1.1
) for
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d}>0
. Note that the both sides of (
1.1
) are polynomials on
w_{1},w_{2},\cdots,w_{d}
. So, the polynomials of the both sides must be the same and this gives us (
1.1
) for any
w\in\mathbb{R}^{d}
. This completes the proof of Theorem
1.1
.
∎
