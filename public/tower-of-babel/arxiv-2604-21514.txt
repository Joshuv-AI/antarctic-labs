Pohozaev identities and bubbling obstruction for Yang-Mills fields in conformal dimension
Mario Gauvrit

Abstract
. We study bubbling for sequences of Yang–Mills connections on closed four-manifolds and we derive a compatibility condition of Pohozaev-type between the weak limit connection and the bubble formed at a concentration point, involving the Weyl curvature of the background metric. This yields obstructions to bubbling extending earlier results of Yin [ 22 ] beyond the locally conformally flat case. As an application, we rule out certain bubbling configurations on 𝐂𝐏 2 \mathbf{CP}^{2} .



Introduction

The study of compactness properties of Yang–Mills connections originates in the foundational work of Uhlenbeck
[
19
]
. This framework plays a central role in the analysis of the moduli space of (anti-)self-dual connections, in particular in four-dimensional geometry where they arise as absolute minimizers of the Yang–Mills functional.

A major development in this direction was achieved by Donaldson
[
2
]
, who used these compactness results to construct a compactification of the moduli space of instantons with topological charge
1
. In this setting, bubbling phenomena are completely understood: sequences of instantons may degenerate by energy concentration at isolated points, leading to the formation of bubbles modelled on the standard 1-instanton on
\mathbf{S}^{4}
, as described via Taubes’ gluing theory
[
16
]
.

For higher topological charge, however, the structure of the resulting bubble-tree compactification remains only partially understood. In particular, a precise description of how energy decomposes between the weak limit connection and the emerging bubbles is still not fully understood. A natural question is whether there exist compatibility constraints between the limiting connection and the bubbles that appear in the blow-up process.

The main result of this paper shows that, in a bubbling configuration, the weak limit connection and the bubble are not independent. Instead, they satisfy a nontrivial compatibility condition at the concentration point, involving both the curvature of the limit connections and the conformal geometry of the underlying manifold and such constraints can rule out certain bubbling configurations.

This type of interaction is expected from analogy with the theory of harmonic maps from
\mathbf{S}^{2}
to
\mathbf{S}^{2}
, which are either holomorphic or anti-holomorphic, preventing an anti-holomorphic bubble from developing on a holomorphic weak limit. In the Yang–Mills setting, related phenomena appear in the work of Yin
[
22
]
on the blow-up analysis of Yang–Mills fields in dimension four under a local conformal flatness condition: in the case of one bubble, algebraic constraints on curvature at concentration points are obtained. As a consequence no
1
-instanton can blow up on a
-1
-instanton.

The aim of this paper is to extend such obstruction results beyond the locally conformally flat case. In particular, we investigate conditions under which certain bubbling configurations are ruled out, with applications to compact four-manifolds such as
\mathbf{CP}^{2}
.

Let

This constraint is a Pohozaev identity, analogous to the balancing law between the radial part and the angular part of the energy of harmonic maps. It is obtain in the same manner, by integrating the PDE against a suitable solution of its linearisation. The origin of such infinitesimal balancing conditions is the conformal invariance of the problem which implies, by Noether’s theorem, the existence of conservation laws involving the stress-energy tensor.
Theorem

Organisation of the paper.
We first recall the definition and relevant properties of the stress-energy tensor in the Yang-Mills setting and formulate the corresponding Pohozaev identities. We then establish a pointwise asymptotic expansion of the curvature in the neck regions, relying on Rivière’s gauge. In
section

3
, these ingredients are combined to prove the main theorem: we treat separately the constraints coming from infinitesimal conformal symmetry and from infinitesimal gauge symmetry, and extend the result to the general bubble-tree setting.
Section

4
is devoted to applications: rigidity statements along the branches of a bubble-tree and the exclusion of certain bubbling configurations on
\mathbf{CP}^{2}
. Technical results are collected in the appendix.

The stress-energy tensor

1.1.
Definition and first properties

In this section, we recall general properties of the stress-energy tensor, see
[
21
]
.

Definition 1.1
.

Let
F\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
. The stress-energy tensor of
F
is the symmetric
2
-tensor denoted by
S_{F,h}
and defined as

S_{F,h}=\frac{1}{4}|F|^{2}_{h}h-F{\circ}_{h}F.

Remark 1.2
.

Up to a sign,
S_{F,h}
is the traceless part of
F{\circ}_{h}F
.

Remark 1.3
.

Consider
F\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
and
g:M\mapsto G
. Then
S_{F^{g},h}=S_{F,h}
where
F^{g}\coloneq g^{-1}Fg
.

We now state an integration by parts formula for the stress-energy tensor which will be crucial in the following.

Proposition 1.4

((2.14) in
[
21
]
)
.

Consider a domain
D\subset M
with smooth boundary. Denote by
\nu
its unit outward normal vector field. For every
X\in\Gamma(TM)
and
F\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
,

\int_{D}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle S_{F,h},\mathcal{L}_{X}h\rangle_{h}+\mathrm{div}_{h}S_{F,h}(X)\right)\mathrm{vol}_{h}=\int_{\partial D}S_{F,h}(X,\nu){\mathrm{vol}_{h}}_{|\partial D}.

In the context of Yang-Mills fields, this formula can be simplified using the following property:

Proposition 1.5

((2.10) in
[
21
]
)
.

The stress-energy tensor associated to the curvature of a Yang-Mills connection is divergence-free.

Recall that the Hodge operator
\star_{h}:\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\to\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M
is an isometry satisfying
\star_{h}^{2}=\mathrm{id}
hence induce an orthogonal splitting

\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M=\Lambda^{+}T^{*}M\oplus\Lambda^{-}T^{*}M

where
\Lambda^{\pm}T^{*}M
is the
\pm 1
eigenspace of
\star_{h}
. Sections of
\Lambda^{+}T^{*}M
(resp.
\Lambda^{-}T^{*}M
) are called self-dual forms (resp. anti-self-dual forms) and if
\omega
is a section of
\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M
, we write
\omega=\omega^{+,h}+\omega^{-,h}
its decomposition according to this splitting.

Proposition 1.6
.

For every
F\in\Gamma(\Lambda^{2}T^{*}M\otimes\mathfrak{g})
,

S_{F,h}=-2F^{+,h}{\circ}_{h}F^{-,h}.

In particular, if either
F^{+,h}=0
or
F^{-,h}=0
then
S_{F,h}=0
.

Proof.

From
lemma

A.1
,
S_{F,h}=-S_{\star_{h}F,h}
and since
\star_{h}
is an isometry and
\circ_{h}
is bilinear

\displaystyle S_{F,h}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{2}(S_{F,h}-S_{\star_{h}F,h})

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}(F\circ_{h}F-\star_{h}F\circ_{h}\star_{h}F)

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}((F^{+,h}+F^{-,h})\circ_{h}(F^{+,h}+F^{-,h})-(F^{+,h}-F^{-,h})\circ_{h}(F^{+,h}-F^{-,h}))

\displaystyle=-\left(F^{+,h}\circ_{h}F^{-,h}+F^{-,h}\circ_{h}F^{+,h}\right)

Finally, use
lemma

A.1
again to check that
F^{+,h}\circ_{h}F^{-,h}=F^{-,h}\circ_{h}F^{+,h}
.
∎

\displaystyle\mathrm{P}

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B}\left(\iota_{\partial_{r}}S\otimes r\mathrm{d}r\right){\mathrm{vol}_{h}}_{|\partial B}

1.5

Write

\displaystyle 0

\displaystyle=\int_{\partial B}S(Tr\partial_{r},\partial_{r}){\mathrm{vol}_{h}}_{|\partial B}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\langle S,\mathcal{L}_{Tr\partial_{r}}h\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\displaystyle=T_{ij}\left(\int_{\partial B}S(x^{j}\partial_{i},\partial_{r}){\mathrm{vol}_{h}}_{|\partial B}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\langle S,\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}\right)

\Phi_{ij}=\int_{\partial B}S(x^{j}\partial_{i},\partial_{r}){\mathrm{vol}_{h}}_{|\partial B}-\frac{1}{2}\int_{B}\langle S,\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h\rangle_{h}\mathrm{vol}_{h}

(2)

On the other hand, writing

we get the following expression

\displaystyle\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h

\displaystyle=\left(\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h_{\alpha\beta}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}+h_{\alpha\beta}\left(\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\right)\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}+h_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\otimes\left(\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}\right)

\displaystyle=\left(x^{j}\partial_{i}h_{\alpha\beta}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}+h_{\alpha\beta}\delta_{i\alpha}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}+h_{\alpha\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\otimes\delta_{i\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{j}

\displaystyle=\nabla_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h+h_{i\beta}\mathrm{d}x^{j}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}+h_{\alpha i}\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{j}

\displaystyle\langle S,\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h\rangle_{h}-\langle S,\nabla_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=h_{i\beta}\langle S,\mathrm{d}x^{j}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\beta}\rangle_{h}+h_{i\alpha}\langle S,\mathrm{d}x^{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{\alpha}\rangle_{h}

\displaystyle=h_{i\beta}h^{j\mu}S_{\mu\nu}h^{\nu\beta}+h_{i\alpha}h^{j\mu}S_{\mu\nu}h^{\nu\alpha}

Finally, we obtain

(4)

\langle S,\mathcal{L}_{x^{j}\partial_{i}}h\rangle_{h}=\left(\langle h^{-1}S,h^{-1}\nabla h\rangle\otimes r\mathrm{d}r+2S\circ h^{-1}\right)_{ij}.

Therefore, if
\mathrm{P}
is the tensor defined in
eq.

1
, the combination of

2
and
4

(5)

\mathrm{P}=\Phi+\int_{B}\left(S\circ\xi-\frac{1}{4}\langle\xi-h^{-1},S\rangle\xi\right)\mathrm{vol}_{h}.

However,
S\circ\xi=S
and
\langle\xi-h^{-1},S\rangle=\mathrm{tr}(S)-\mathrm{tr}_{h}(S)=\mathrm{tr}(S)

(6)

\mathrm{P}-\Phi=\int_{B}\left(S-\frac{1}{4}\mathrm{tr}(S)\xi\right)\mathrm{vol}_{h}

\mathrm{P}-\Phi

On the one hand

and on the other hand

\displaystyle 2\delta_{i\beta}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha j}\omega+2\delta_{i\alpha}\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}_{\beta j}\omega

\displaystyle=\delta_{i\beta}\partial_{\alpha}\omega_{j}+\delta_{i\beta}\partial_{j}\omega_{\alpha}+\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{\beta}\omega_{j}+\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{j}\omega_{\beta}

\displaystyle=\partial_{\alpha}(\delta_{i\beta}\omega_{j})+\partial_{\beta}(\delta_{i\alpha}\omega_{j})+\delta_{i\beta}\partial_{j}\omega_{\alpha}+\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{j}\omega_{\beta}

\displaystyle=2\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}_{\alpha\beta}(\omega_{j}\mathrm{d}x^{i})+\delta_{i\beta}\partial_{j}\omega_{\alpha}+\delta_{i\alpha}\partial_{j}\omega_{\beta}

so we deduce

\langle S,V\rangle=\langle S,\nabla^{\mathrm{s}}\left(\nabla\omega\otimes r\mathrm{d}r\right)\rangle-\left(S_{j\beta}\partial_{i}\omega_{\beta}+S_{\alpha i}\partial_{j}\omega_{\alpha}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{j}

is divergence-free:

\Phi=-\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4}}\left(fS+\left(S_{j\beta}\partial_{i}\omega_{\beta}+S_{\alpha i}\partial_{j}\omega_{\alpha}\right)\mathrm{d}x^{i}\otimes\mathrm{d}x^{j}\right)\mathrm{vol}

which is indeed a symmetric tensor. Combining this with

\Phi

is traceless symmetric, where

\displaystyle P_{[ij]}+\mathrm{Tr(P)}\delta_{ij}

\displaystyle\quad+\frac{1}{3\pi^{2}}\int_{\mathbf{R}^{4}}\left(\frac{1}{2}\langle S,x^{[j}\partial_{i]}w-(S\circ w)_{[ij]}+\delta_{ij}\langle S,w\rangle\right)\mathrm{vol}

\omega_{\lambda}(x)=|x|+\lambda|x|^{-1}
.

Lemma A.2
.

\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
is the space of
all harmonic functions
\vartheta
in
\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\}
satisfying

\sup_{x\neq 0}\omega_{1}(x)^{-\alpha}|x\vartheta(x)|<+\infty

Moreover the map
\Phi:\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathcal{L}(\mathcal{H}_{\alpha},\mathbf{R}^{2})
defined by

\Phi(\vartheta)\varphi=\int_{\mathbf{S}^{3}}(\vartheta(x),\partial_{r}\vartheta(x))\varphi(x)\mathrm{d}x

is an isomorphism.

Proof.

The first part is a Liouville type result (see for instance
[
1
, Theorem 10.5]
. For the second part, it is enough to check that
\ker\Phi=\{0\}
since

\Phi
is a linear map between spaces of the same dimension. If
\vartheta\in\ker\Phi
, taking
\varphi=\vartheta_{|\mathbf{S}^{3}}
and
\varphi=x^{k}\partial_{k}\vartheta_{|\mathbf{S}^{3}}
we get

\int_{\mathbf{S}^{3}}|\vartheta|^{2}=\int_{\mathbf{S}^{3}}|\partial_{r}\vartheta|^{2}=0

However this would imply that
w=\vartheta\mathbf{1}_{\text{B}_{1}}
is harmonic
in
\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\}
then by analyticity that
w=0
and
finally that
\vartheta=0
which is the desired contradiction.
∎

Proposition A.3
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
, for all
v\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda})
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

|x|^{3}|\Delta v|\leq\omega_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
in

\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

(2)

|x||v|\leq 1
in
\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

(3)

\Phi\left(v_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\right)=0
,

we have for all
x\in\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

|x||v|\leq C\omega_{\lambda}^{\alpha}.

Proof.

We use a contradiction argument. Assume there exists a sequence

(\lambda_{k})_{k}\subset(0,1)
and
\lim\lambda_{k}=0
and functions

(v_{k})_{k}
with
v_{k}\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda_{k}})
satisfying
items

1
,
2
and
3
and

M_{k}\coloneq\sup_{\text{B}_{1}\backslash\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}}}\omega_{\lambda_{k}}^{-\alpha}|x||v_{k}|\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightarrow}+\infty

Consider
x_{k}\in\text{B}_{1}\backslash\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}}
realising the
supremum, that is such that
r_{k}|v_{k}(x_{k})|=M_{k}\omega_{\lambda_{k}}(x_{k})^{\alpha}
, where
r_{k}=|x_{k}|
. By
item

1
, we have for all

\delta>0

\sup_{\text{B}_{1}\backslash\text{B}_{\delta}\cup\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}/\delta}\backslash\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}}}\omega_{\lambda_{k}}^{-\alpha}|x||v_{k}|\leq\left(\inf_{\rho\in(\delta,1)\cup(\lambda_{k},\lambda_{k}/\delta)}(\rho+\lambda_{k}\rho^{-1})\right)^{-\alpha}\leq\delta^{-\alpha}

which implies that both inequalities
\limsup r_{k}>\delta
and
\liminf r_{k}/\lambda_{k}<1/\delta
are false. This in turn implies
r_{k}\rightarrow 0

and
\lambda_{k}/r_{k}\rightarrow 0
.

Define now for
y\in\text{B}_{1/r_{k}}\backslash\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}/r_{k}}
the rescaled function
\overline{v}_{k}(y)=|v_{k}(x_{k})|^{-1}v_{k}(r_{k}y)
. The definition of
M_{k}
translates as

\displaystyle|y||\overline{v}_{k}(y)|\leq

\displaystyle\frac{r_{k}|y|}{M_{k}}\frac{|v_{k}(r_{k}y)|}{(r_{k}+\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1})^{\alpha}}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\frac{(r_{k}|y|+\lambda_{k}(r_{k}|y|)^{-1})^{\alpha}}{(r_{k}+\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1})^{\alpha}}

(46)

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle\left(|y|\frac{r_{k}}{r_{k}+\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1}}+|y|^{-1}\frac{\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1}}{r_{k}+\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1}}\right)^{\alpha}

whereas assumption
\left(\text{{i}}\right)
gives

\displaystyle|y|^{3}|\Delta\overline{v}_{k}|

\displaystyle\leq|y|^{3}r_{k}^{2}|v_{k}(x_{k})|^{-1}|(\Delta v_{k})(r_{k}y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{|r_{k}y|^{3}}{M_{k}(r_{k}+\lambda_{k}r_{k}^{-1})^{\alpha}}|(\Delta v_{k})(r_{k}y)|

\displaystyle\leq\frac{1}{M_{k}}(|y|+|y|^{-1})^{\alpha}

This means

(47)

\Delta\overline{v}_{k}\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightarrow}0\text{ in }\text{L}^{\infty}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\})

and we deduce that there exists a harmonic function
\overline{v}_{\infty}

on
\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\}
such that

(48)

\overline{v}_{k}\underset{k\rightarrow+\infty}{\rightarrow}\overline{v}_{\infty}\text{ in }\mathcal{C}^{1}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\}).

Moreover,
since
\overline{v}_{k}(x_{k}/r_{k})=1
, we get in the limit

(49)

\inf_{\partial\text{B}_{1}}|\overline{v}_{\infty}|\geq 1.

Now we are in either one of the following three situations:

•

\limsup{r_{k}}/{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}=+\infty
. In this case, there is a subsequence satisfying
r_{k}^{2}/\lambda_{k}\rightarrow+\infty
. According to
eq.

46
for
y\in\text{B}_{1/r_{k}}\backslash\text{B}_{\lambda_{k}/r_{k}}

(50)

|y||\overline{v}_{k}(y)|\leq\left(|y|\frac{r_{k}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}}{r_{k}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}+1}+|y|^{-1}\frac{1}{r_{k}^{2}\lambda_{k}^{-1}+1}\right)^{\alpha}

so we deduce that for all
y\in\mathbf{R}^{4}\backslash\{0\},|y||\overline{v}_{\infty}(y)|\leq|y|^{\alpha}
that is

(51)

|\overline{v}_{\infty}(y)|\leq|y|^{\alpha-1}.

Since
\alpha
is not an integer, the only possibility is
\overline{v}_{\infty}=0
which contradicts
eq.

49
.

•

\liminf{r_{k}}/{\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}}=0
. A similar argument yields

(52)

|\overline{v}_{\infty}(y)|\leq|y|^{-\alpha-1}

for all
y\neq 0
, which is again a contradiction.

•

r_{k}/\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}
is bounded and bounded away from zero. This implies that, up to a subsequence, the ratio
r_{k}/\sqrt{\lambda_{k}}

converges to some
\rho\in(0,+\infty)
. From
eq.

46
, we deduce

(53)

|y||\overline{v}_{\infty}(y)|\leq(|y|+|y|^{-1})^{\alpha}

that is
\overline{v}_{\infty}\in\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
and assumption
\left(\text{{iii}}\right)
gives in the limit
\Phi\left({\overline{v}_{\infty}}_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\rho}}\right)=0
so

\overline{v}_{\infty}=0
since
\Phi
is one to one.

∎

Theorem A.4
.

There exists
\lambda_{0}\in(0,1)
and
C>0
such that for all
\lambda\in(0,\lambda_{0})
, for all
v\in\mathrm{W}^{1,2}_{\mathrm{loc}}(\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda})
satisfying the following conditions

(1)

|x|^{3}|\Delta v|\leq\omega_{\lambda}^{\alpha}
in

\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

(2)

|x||v|\leq\omega_{\lambda}^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}
in
\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

we have for all
x\in\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}

|x||v-\vartheta|\leq C\omega^{\alpha}

where
\vartheta
is the unique element of
\overline{\mathcal{H}}_{\alpha}
such
that
\Phi\left(\vartheta_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\right)=\Phi\left(v_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\right)
.

Proof.

We apply
proposition

A.3
to
v-\vartheta
which satisfies
items

1
and
3
trivially.

Consider the norm
\|\cdot\|
on
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}
defined by
\|\varphi\|=\sup_{\mathbf{S}^{3}}(|\varphi|+|\partial_{r}\varphi|)

and denote by
\|\cdot\|
the associated operator norm
for linear maps
\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}\rightarrow\mathbf{R}^{2}
. Since

\Phi
is an isomorphism, the map
\vartheta\mapsto\|\Phi(\vartheta)\|
defines a norm on
\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}
which is
equivalent to
\vartheta\mapsto\sup_{x\neq 0}\omega_{1}(x)^{-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}|x\vartheta(x)|
since
\overline{\mathcal{H}_{\alpha}}
is finite
dimensional. In particular for all
x\neq 0

\omega_{1}(x)^{-\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}\left|\sqrt{\lambda}x\vartheta\left(\sqrt{\lambda}x\right)\right|\leq C\|\Phi\left(\sqrt{\lambda}\vartheta_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\right)\|=C\Phi\left(\sqrt{\lambda}v_{|\partial\mathrm{B}_{\sqrt{\lambda}}}\right)\leq C\lambda^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor/2}

that is

|x\vartheta(x)|\leq C\lambda^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor/2}\left(\frac{|x|}{\sqrt{\lambda}}+\frac{\sqrt{\lambda}}{|x|}\right)^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}=C\omega_{\lambda}^{\lfloor\alpha\rfloor}

and in particular, for
x\in\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}
,

|x||v(x)-\vartheta(x)|\leq C

which means that
item

2
is satisfied (up to a multiplicative
constant).
∎

Remark A.5
.

Elliptic regularity estimates yield, under the hypothesis of
theorem

A.4
, for
x\in\mathrm{B}_{1/2}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{2\lambda}
,

|x||v-\vartheta|+|x|^{2}|\nabla v-\nabla\vartheta|\leq C\omega^{\alpha}.

Lemma A.6
.

There exists
C>0
such that, if
h
is a metric on the unit ball with

|h-\xi|+|x||\nabla h|\leq|x|^{2},

where
\xi
is the flat metric, then for any
1
-form
A
such that, in

\mathrm{B}_{1}\backslash\mathrm{B}_{\lambda}
,
\lambda\in(0,1)
,

|x|^{2}|\nabla A|+|x|^{3}|\nabla^{2}A|\leq\omega^{2}

we have

|x|^{3}|\Delta_{\xi}A-\Delta_{h}A|\leq C\omega^{4}.

Proof.

For any 2-form
\Omega
,

\displaystyle\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{h}}\Omega

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\star_{h}\mathrm{d}\star_{h}(\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j})

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\star_{h}(\partial_{k}\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j})+\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}))

\displaystyle=

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2}\partial_{k}\Omega_{ij}\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}))+\frac{1}{2}\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{h}}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j})

It is clear that

\displaystyle|\partial_{k}\Omega_{ij}\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\star_{h}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}))-\partial_{k}\Omega_{ij}\star_{\xi}(\mathrm{d}x^{k}\wedge\star_{\xi}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j}))|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\nabla\Omega||h-\xi|

\displaystyle|\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{h}}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j})-\Omega_{ij}\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{\xi}}(\mathrm{d}x^{i}\wedge\mathrm{d}x^{j})|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C|\Omega|(|h-\xi|+|\nabla h|)

therefore for
A
as in the statement of the lemma,

\displaystyle|x|^{3}|\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{h}}\mathrm{d}A-\mathrm{d}^{\ast_{\xi}}\mathrm{d}A|

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C(|\nabla^{2}A||h-\xi|+|\nabla A|(|h-\xi|+|\nabla h|))

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega^{2}|x|^{2}

\displaystyle\leq

\displaystyle C\omega^{4}.

∎
