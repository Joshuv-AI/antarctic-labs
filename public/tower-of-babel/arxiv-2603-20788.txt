Equivalence of Uniform Polyconvexity and Almgren Uniform Ellipticity for Lipschitz Q Q -Graph Test Pairs
Maciej Lesniak

Abstract
We investigate the relationship between uniform polyconvexity of anisotropic geometric integrands and Almgren’s uniform ellipticity. We first establish the converse implication for uniform ellipticity with respect to polyhedral test pairs, thereby strengthening earlier results. Our main theorem shows that uniform polyconvexity is equivalent to Almgren’s uniform ellipticity with respect to Lipschitz Q Q -graph test pairs, building on techniques developed by De Rosa, Lei, and Young. As a consequence, we show that for a classical integrand, uniform polyconvexity is equivalent to uniform quasiconvexity of the associated Q Q -integrand for every Q ∈ ℕ Q\in\mathbb{N} .



1
Introduction

In geometric measure theory one is led to the study of minimisers and critical
points of functionals defined on
k
–dimensional currents or varifolds
in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
. In particular, if
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
denotes the Grassmannian of
oriented
k
–planes in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, a continuous
geometric integrand

\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to(0,\infty)
determines an anisotropic energy
functional

E_{\Psi}(T)={\textstyle\int}\Psi(\vec{T}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\|T\|(x)\,,

where
\|T\|
denotes the variation measure of the rectifiable current
T
and

\vec{T}
is the associated orientation map; see
[
9
, 2.5.12, 4.1.5,
4.1.7]
. If
\Psi
is even,
E_{\Psi}
extends naturally to

k
–dimensional varifolds; see
[
1
]
. Based on Almgren’s
notion
[
2
, 1.2, 1.6(2)]
, given a family
\mathcal{P}
of pairs of

k
-dimensional surfaces in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
we call
\Psi

elliptic with respect to

\mathcal{P}
if

E_{\Psi}(S)>E_{\Psi}(D)\quad\text{for all $(S,D)\in\mathcal{P}$}\,.

At this stage
\mathcal{P}
may be arbitrary but one should think that
D
is
always a flat
k
-dimensional disc (or a cube) and
S\neq D
is a

k
-dimensional surface attached to the relative boundary of
D
. In case there
exists
c>0
such that

E_{\Psi}(S)-E_{\Psi}(D)\geq c\bigl(\mathscr{H}^{k}(S)-\mathscr{H}^{k}(D)\bigr)\quad\text{for all $(S,D)\in\mathcal{P}$}

we call
\Psi

uniformly elliptic with respect to
\mathcal{P}
. Uniform
ellipticity is the condition that allowed Almgren to prove full partial
regularity of minimisers of
E_{\Psi}
in classes of rectifiable sets and
rectifiable currents with coefficients in an abelian group; see
[
2
, 1.4,
1.7]
.

Parallel to this geometric setting, in the classical calculus of variations one
studies minimisers and critical points of functionals of the type

E_{\psi}(u)={\textstyle\int_{\Omega}}\psi(\mathrm{D}u(x))\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{L}^{k}(x)\,,

where
\Omega\subseteq\mathbf{R}^{k}
is a bounded open set,
u:\Omega\to\mathbf{R}^{n-k}

is a weakly differentiable map, and
\psi:\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{k},\mathbf{R}^{n-k})\to(0,\infty)

is a given
classical integrand
; cf.
4.7
.
In this context one introduces the notions of quasi-convexity, polyconvexity,
and ellipticity of
\psi
and these three notions differ; see e.g.
[
5
, Chapter 5]
.

The geometric ellipticity condition depends strongly on the choice of
\mathcal{P}
,
i.e., on the class of
admissible competitors
. This phenomenon is
described in more detail in the introduction to our previous paper;
see
[
12
]
and references therein. Here we focus on two particular
choices for
\mathcal{P}
:
polyhedral test pairs
constructed from
polyhedral currents with real coefficients and
graph test pairs
being
pairs of graphs of certain
Q
-valued functions;
cf.
3.8
and
7.1
.

When competitors in
\mathcal{P}
are rectifiable currents, uniformly convex
norms on
{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
induce elliptic integrands (we identify
\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)

with the set of unit simple
k
-vectors in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
);
cf.
[
9
, 5.1.2]
. In this setting Burago and
Ivanov
[
4
]
proved that semi–ellipticity is equivalent to
polyconvexity. De Rosa, Lei, and Young reproved that theorem
and showed also that polyconvexity of a classical integrand
\psi
and
quasiconvexity of the associated Q–integrand
\bar{\psi}_{Q}
for every

Q\in\mathbb{N}
are equivalent; cf.
[
8
]
.

In our previous paper
[
12
, Theorem 5.10]
, it was shown that
Almgren uniform ellipticity with respect to polyhedral
k
-chains implies
uniform polyconvexity of the associated geometric integrand. The first result of
the present work establishes the converse implication, yielding an equivalence
between the two conditions.

Theorem

(see
3.12
)
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to\mathbf{R}
be a Lipschitz geometric integrand and let

c>0
. Then
\Psi
is Almgren uniformly elliptic with constant
c
with
respect to polyhedral
k
-chains with real coefficients if and only if

\Psi
is uniformly polyconvex with the same constant
c
.

Our second result establishes an analogous relation for the uniform counterparts
with respect to
Q
–graph test pairs.

Theorem

(see
8.8
)
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)^{+}\to\mathbf{R}
be a Lipschitz geometric integrand and
let
c>0
. Then
\Psi
is Almgren uniformly elliptic with respect to

Q
–graph test pairs if and only if
\Psi
is uniformly polyconvex (both
with the same constant
c
).

The preceding results suggest that uniform polyconvexity is a natural condition
underlying ellipticity in both the classical and multigraph settings.
This perspective motivates the study of analogous conditions in the
multiple–valued framework introduced by Almgren for the study of
area–minimizing currents; see
[
3
]
. This leads to considering

Q
-valued maps
v:T\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(T^{\perp})
and related
Q
-energies,
where
Q
is a fixed integer and
T
is a
k
-dimensional subspace of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
;
cf.
[
3
,
7
,
11
]
. Given a geometric integrand

\Psi
and
Q\in\mathbb{N}
one can define the associated classical integrand

\psi
and also a
Q
-integrand
\bar{\psi}_{Q}
so that

E_{\Psi}(S)=E_{\psi}(u)
if
S
is the graph of
u
and

E_{\Psi}(R)=E_{\bar{\psi}_{Q}}(v)
if
R
is the
Q
-graph of
v
;
cf. §
9
.

In
[
7
]
, De Lellis and Spadaro
revisited Almgren’s theory and developed a self–contained analytic framework
for Dir–minimizing
Q
–valued functions. Further developments were obtained in

[
11
]
, where it was shown that a
Lipschitz multiple–valued map naturally defines an integer rectifiable current,
and explicit formulas for the boundary, the mass, and the first variations were
derived.

In
[
6
]
, De Lellis, Focardi, and Spadaro introduced a notion of
quasiconvexity for
Q
–integrands. This condition extends Morrey’s classical
quasiconvexity to energies defined on Sobolev spaces of multiple–valued maps
and provides a necessary and sufficient condition for weak lower semicontinuity
of the associated functional.

A natural question is whether minimizers of such functionals exhibit regularity
properties. In
[
14
]
, Spadaro asked whether partial regularity
results can be obtained for minimizers of quasiconvex functionals in spaces of

Q
–valued maps, and whether there exists a distinguished subclass of
integrands for which such regularity holds.

The present work contributes to this program by identifying a structural
condition on the classical integrand—uniform polyconvexity—that is equivalent to
uniform quasiconvexity of the associated
Q
–integrands (see
Theorem
10.3
). By classical theory, uniform quasiconvexity ensures
weak lower semicontinuity of the corresponding energy functional. Thus, the
third result of the present work provides a foundation for studying existence,
and potentially regularity, of minimizers within the class of integrands
considered in the theorem below.

Theorem

(see
10.3
for the precise statement)
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)^{+}\to\mathbf{R}
be a Lipschitz geometric integrand,
and let
\psi
be the associated classical integrand. For each
Q\in\mathbb{N}
,
let
\bar{\psi}_{Q}
denote the corresponding
Q
-integrand. Then, for any
constant
c>0
,
\psi
is uniformly polyconvex if and only if
\bar{\psi}_{Q}

is uniformly quasiconvex for every
Q\in\mathbb{N}
.

Finally, we remark that in

[
10
]
it was shown
that, for each fixed
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, there exists a
Q
–integrand
\bar{\psi}_{Q}

whose associated energy functional is lower semicontinuous in
W^{1,p}
,
although the corresponding classical integrand
\psi
is not polyconvex. This
result shows that lower semicontinuity for a fixed
Q
does not force
polyconvexity of the underlying classical integrand, highlighting the importance
of structural conditions that hold uniformly with respect to
Q
.

This paper is organised as follows. In Section
2
, we
recall the basic terminology used throughout the
paper. Section
3
introduces uniform polyconvexity
and uniform ellipticity with respect to polyhedral test pairs for geometric
integrands, and establishes the equivalence between these notions. In
Section
4
, we define uniform polyconvexity for
classical integrands. Sections
5
and

6
present the framework of multiple-valued functions and
the associated theory needed for the subsequent results. In
Section
7
, we define ellipticity with respect to

Q
–graph test pairs, and Section
8
contains the proof
of the main result. Finally, Sections
9
and

10
introduce
Q
–integrands and uniform
quasiconvexity, culminating in the proof of the third result.

3
Geometric Integrands

3.1 Definition
.

A
geometric integrand
is positively homogeneous, non-negative and
continious function
\Psi:\ray\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)\to\mathbf{R}
.

3.2 Definition
.

A geometric integrand
\Psi
is
polyconvex
if it can be extended to a
convex function on
({\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n})
.

3.3 Definition
.

Let
0<c<\infty
and let
\Psi
be a geometric integrand.
We say that
\Psi
is
uniformly polyconvex (with constant
c
)
if

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\Psi(\eta_{i})-\Psi(\eta_{0})\;\geq\;c\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\bigr)

whenever
d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}\in(0,\infty)
, and

\eta_{0},\ldots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
satisfy

\eta_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\eta_{i}
.
If
\Psi
is uniformly polyconvex with constant
c
, we write

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
.

3.4 Remark
.

(
[
12
, 3.22]
). If
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
, then
\Psi
is
polyconvex.

3.5 Lemma
.

If
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
for every
c<c_{0}
, then
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c_{0})
.

Proof.

Assume toward a contradiction that
\Psi\notin\mathrm{UPC}(c_{0})
.
Then, by definition, there exist

d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\},\qquad m_{1},\ldots,m_{d}>0,\qquad\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\ldots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)

with

\eta_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\eta_{i}

such that the inequality for
\mathrm{UPC}(c_{0})
fails, i.e.

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\Psi(\eta_{i})-\Psi(\eta_{0})\;<\;c_{0}\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\Bigr).

Since
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
for every
c<c_{0}
, we have

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\Psi(\eta_{i})-\Psi(\eta_{0})\;\geq\;c\Bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\Bigr).

Combining the two inequalities, we obtain, for every
c<c_{0}

c_{0}\Bigl(\sum m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\Bigr)\;>\;\sum m_{i}\Psi(\eta_{i})-\Psi(\eta_{0})\;\geq\;c\Bigl(\sum m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\Bigr).

Letting
c\uparrow c_{0}
the right-hand side converges to the left-hand
expression, contradicting the strict inequality. Hence

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c_{0})
.
∎

3.6 Definition

(cf.
[
12
, 4.10]
)
.

By
\mathbf{P}_{k}(U)
we denote the space of polyhedral
k
-currents in

U\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
.

3.7 Definition
.

Recalling
[
9
, 4.1.8 and 4.1.32]
for
k\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
we set

I_{k}=\underbrace{\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket\times\cdots\times\llbracket 0,1\rrbracket}_{\text{$k$ factors}}\in\mathbf{I}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{k})\,.

3.8 Definition

(
[
12
, 5.2]
)
.

We say that
(S,D)\in\mathcal{P}_{C}
is a
polyhedral test pair
if
there exists
p\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
such that

\displaystyle S\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})\,,\quad D=(p^{*})_{\#}I_{k}\,,\quad\text{and}\quad\partial S=\partial D\,.

3.9 Definition
.

Let
\Psi
be a geometric integrand.
For every
k
-rectifiable current
T
represented by
(M,\tau,\Theta)
,
we define the
anisotropic energy functional

E_{\Psi}
by

E_{\Psi}(T):=\int_{M}\Theta(x)\,\Psi\bigl(\tau(x)\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{k}(x).

3.10 Remark
.

By the definition of the push-forward measure (see
[
9
, 2.1.2]
),
the anisotropic energy functional can be expressed in terms of the weighted
Gaussian image as

E_{\Psi}(T)=\int_{M}\Theta\,\Psi\circ\tau\,\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{k}=\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}\Psi\,\,\mathrm{d}\tau_{\#}\bigl(\Theta\,\mathscr{H}^{k}\mathop{\rule[1.0pt]{0.5pt}{6.0pt}\rule[1.0pt]{4.0pt}{0.5pt}}\nolimits M\bigr)=\int_{\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)}\Psi\,\,\mathrm{d}\gamma_{T}.

3.11 Definition
.

(
[
12
, 5.3]
).
Let
\Psi
be a geometric integrand and
\mathcal{P}_{C}
be the set of
polyhedral test pairs. We say that
\Psi
is
elliptic with respect to

\mathcal{P}_{C}
if there exists
0<c<\infty
(the
ellipticity
constant
) such that

E_{\Psi}(S)-E_{\Psi}(D)\geq c\bigl(\mathbf{M}(S)-\mathbf{M}(D)\bigr)\quad\text{for $(S,D)\in\mathcal{P}_{C}$}\,.

If this holds we write
F\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P_{C}},c)
.

3.12 Theorem
.

Assume
\Psi
is a Lipschitz geometric integrand, and let
c>0
. Then

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P_{C}},c)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c).

Proof.

Suppose first that
\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P_{C}},c)
.
By Theorem
[
12
, 5.10]
and Lemma
3.5
, we have

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
. Conversely, assume
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
.
Let
(S,D)\in\mathcal{P_{C}}
be a polyhedral test pair. By the structure of
such test pairs and
[
9
, 5.1.2]

\int\vec{S}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\|S\|(x)=\int\vec{D}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\|D\|(x)=\mathbf{M}(D)\eta_{0},\quad\eta\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k),

where
\eta_{0}=\vec{D}
for
\|D\|
–almost every
x\in\spt D
. Since

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}(c)
, the function
G
defined as

G(\eta):=\Psi(\eta)-c|\eta|

satisfies the Jensen–type inequality associated with uniform polyconvexity.
Thus,

\mathbf{M}(D)G(\eta_{0})=G(\int\vec{S}(x)\,\mathrm{d}\|S\|(x))\leq\int G(\vec{S}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\|S\|(x),

which is eqivalent to

\mathbf{M}(D)\big(\Psi(\eta_{0})-c|\eta_{0}|\big)\leq\int\Psi(\vec{S}(x))\,\mathrm{d}\|S\|(x)-c\mathbf{M}(S).

This shows that
\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P_{C}},c)
.
∎

(ii)

4.6 Remark
.

Let

4.7 Definition
.

A

is a continuous, non-negative function

\psi:\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k}\to\mathbf{R}.

4.8 Definition

(
[
8
, 1.6]
)
.

For
X\in\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
, let
\mathds{1}_{k}
denote the
k\times k

identity matrix and define

M(X):=\begin{pmatrix}\mathds{1}_{k}\\
X\end{pmatrix}\in\mathbf{R}^{n\times k}.

Writing
w_{1}(X),\ldots,w_{k}(X)
for the columns of
M(X)
, we define

{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M(X):=w_{1}(X)\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{k}(X)\in\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n}).

This yields a map

{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M:\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k}\longrightarrow\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n}).

4.9 Remark

(
[
8
, 1.6]
)
.

In the standard basis of
\Lambda^{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
, the coordinates of

\bigwedge M(X)
are precisely the minors of
X
of all orders, which
coincide with the
k\times k
minors of the matrix
M(X)
.

4.10 Definition
.

A classical integrand
\psi
is
polyconvex
if there exists a gauge

\Psi:({\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n})^{+}\longrightarrow\mathbf{R}

such that
\psi=\Psi\circ{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M
.

4.11 Definition
.

A classical integrand
\psi
is
uniformly polyconvex
if it is
polyconvex and the associated geometric integrand
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}_{\xi_{0}}(c)
.
If
\psi
is uniformly polyconvex with constant
c
, we write

\psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{\text{cl}}_{\xi_{0}}(c)
.

4.12 Remark
.

By definition, if
\psi=\Psi\circ{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M
, then

\psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{\text{cl}}_{\xi_{0}}(c)
if and only if

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}_{\xi_{0}}(c)
.

Lipschitz
Q
-valued functions

5.1 Remark
.

Throughout this paper we work in the framework of multiple-valued functions
and the associated theory of currents developed in

[
11
]
,

[
7
]
,
and
[
6
]
.
Unless otherwise stated, measurability is always understood with respect to
the appropriate Hausdorff measure. Moreover,
\Sigma\subset\mathbf{R}^{n+m}

denotes a
\mathscr{C}^{1}
,
m
-dimensional oriented submanifold.

5.2 Definition
.

(
[
6
, 1.1]
)
Let
Q\in\mathbb{N}
. The metric space

(\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n}),\mathcal{G})
of unordered
Q
-tuples is defined by

\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n}):=\left\{\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket P_{i}\rrbracket\;:\;P_{i}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}\text{ for }i=1,\dots,Q\right\},

where
\llbracket P_{i}\rrbracket
denotes the Dirac mass at

P_{i}\in\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
For any two elements

T_{1}=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket P_{i}\rrbracket,\qquad T_{2}=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket S_{i}\rrbracket,

the distance is defined by

\mathcal{G}(T_{1},T_{2}):=\min_{\sigma\in\mathcal{P}_{Q}}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\lvert P_{i}-S_{\sigma(i)}\rvert^{2}\right)^{1/2},

where
\mathcal{P}_{Q}
denotes the group of permutations of
\{1,\dots,Q\}
.

5.3 Lemma
.

(
[
11
, 1.1]
)
Let
M\subset\Sigma
be measurable and let

F:M\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be Lipschitz.
Then there exist a countable partition
\{M_{i}\}_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}
of
M
into bounded
measurable subsets and Lipschitz functions

f_{i}^{j}:M_{i}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
(
j=1,\ldots,Q
) such that:

(a)

F|_{M_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\llbracket f_{i}^{j}\rrbracket
for every
i
, and

\operatorname{Lip}(f_{i}^{j})\leq\operatorname{Lip}(F)
for all
i,j
;

(b)

for every
i
and
j,j^{\prime}\in\{1,\ldots,Q\}
, either

f_{i}^{j}\equiv f_{i}^{j^{\prime}}
or

f_{i}^{j}(x)\neq f_{i}^{j^{\prime}}(x)
for all
x\in M_{i}
;

(c)

for every
i
,

DF(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\llbracket\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)\rrbracket\quad\text{for a.e.\ }x\in M_{i}.

Moreover, for
\mathscr{H}^{m}
-almost every
x
, the differential
DF(x)
is well
defined independently of the chosen decomposition.

5.4 Definition
.

(
[
11
, 1.2]
)
Let
M\subset\Sigma
be measurable and let

F\colon M\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
be a Lipschitz map.
We say that
F
is
proper
if the following holds:
for every Lipschitz decomposition given by Lemma
5.3
, namely,
for every bounded measurable set
M_{i}\subset M
and Lipschitz functions

f_{i}^{j}\colon M_{i}\to\mathbf{R}^{n}
(
j=1,\dots,Q
) such that

F|_{M_{i}}=\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\llbracket f_{i}^{j}\rrbracket,

and for every compact set
K\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
, the set

\bigcup_{j=1}^{Q}\overline{(f_{i}^{j})^{-1}(K)}

is compact in
M_{i}
.

6
Q
-graphs of Lipschitz functions

6.1 Definition
.

(
[
11
, 1.3]
)
Let
M\subset\Sigma
be measurable, and

F:M\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
a proper Lipschitz map.
The push-forward of
M
through
F
is the
m
-current

T_{F}:=\sum_{i,j}(f_{ij})_{\#}\llbracket M_{i}\rrbracket,

where
\{M_{i},f_{ij}\}
is any decomposition as in Lemma
5.3
.

6.2 Remark
.

For a smooth compactly supported differential
m
-form

\omega\in\mathcal{D}^{m}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
, the action of
T_{F}
can be
written as

T_{F}(\omega):=\sum_{i\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}}\sum_{j=1}^{Q}\int_{M_{i}}\langle\omega(f_{i}^{j}(x)),\,\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)_{\sharp}\,\vec{e}(x)\rangle\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{m}(x).

6.3 Lemma
.

(
[
11
, 1.4]
)

The action of
T_{F}
given in Remark
6.2
is independent of the
chosen partition
\{M_{i}\}
and decomposition
\{f_{i}^{j}\}
.
Hence
T_{F}
is an integer rectifiable current

T_{F}=(\operatorname{Im}(F),\,\Theta,\,\vec{\tau}),

where:

(a)

\operatorname{Im}(F)=\bigcup_{x\in M}\operatorname{spt}(F(x))=\bigcup_{i}\bigcup_{j=1}^{Q}f_{i}^{j}(M_{i})

is an
m
-rectifiable set;

(b)

\vec{\tau}
is a Borel unit
m
-vector orienting
\operatorname{Im}(F)
, and for

\mathscr{H}^{m}
-a.e.
p\in\operatorname{Im}(F)
,
if
f_{i}^{j}(x)=p
and
\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)\,\sharp\,\vec{e}(x)\neq 0
, then

\vec{\tau}(p)=\pm\frac{\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)_{\sharp}\,\vec{e}(x)}{|\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)_{\sharp}\,\vec{e}(x)|};

(c)

\Theta(p)=\sum_{i,j,\,x:f_{i}^{j}(x)=p}\left\langle\vec{\tau}(p),\,\frac{\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)_{\sharp}\,\vec{e}(x)}{|\mathrm{D}f_{i}^{j}(x)_{\sharp}\,\vec{e}(x)|}\right\rangle.

6.4 Definition
.

(
[
11
, 1.10]
)
Let
M\subset\Sigma
be measurable, and let
f\colon M\to\mathcal{A}_{Q}(\mathbf{R}^{n})

be a proper Lipschitz map, with the following decomposition

f=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket f_{i}\rrbracket.

Define the map
\tilde{F}\colon M\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{m+n})
by

\tilde{F}(x):=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket(x,f_{i}(x))\rrbracket.

The current
T_{\tilde{F}}
associated with
\tilde{F}
is called the
graph current
of

f
and will be denoted by
G_{f}
.

6.5 Remark
.

By the area formula for
Q
-graphs (
[
11
, 1.11]
),
if
f
is a proper Lipschitz map, then the associated graph current
G_{f}
has
finite mass, given by

\mathbf{M}(G_{f})=\int_{M}\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\sqrt{\det\bigl(I+(\mathrm{D}f_{i})^{T}\mathrm{D}f_{i}\bigr)}\,\mathrm{d}\mathscr{H}^{m},

where
\{f_{i}\}
are as in Lemma
5.3
.

7
Ellipticity

7.1 Definition
.

Let
\eta\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
and let
D\subset\asssp\eta\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}

be a unit cube contained in the
k
-dimensional plane
\asssp\eta
. Let

f,h\colon D\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}((\asssp\eta)^{\perp})

be proper Lipschitz
Q
-valued functions, and assume that

h(x)=\sum_{j=1}^{J}Q_{j}\llbracket a_{j}+L_{j}x\rrbracket,\qquad a_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{\,n-k},\;L_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k},\quad\sum_{j=1}^{J}Q_{j}=Q.

Denote by
G_{f}
and
G_{h}
the associated graph currents in
\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
We say that
(G_{f},G_{h})
is a
graph test pair
, and write

(G_{f},G_{h})\in\mathcal{P}_{G}
, if
f
and
h
coincide on the boundary of
D
:

f(x)=h(x)\quad\text{for all }x\in\partial D.

In particular, this implies that
\partial G_{f}=\partial G_{h}
.

7.2 Definition
.

Fix
\eta_{0}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. By
\mathcal{P}_{G}(\eta_{0})
we denote the
class of graph test pairs whose base cube lies in the plane
\asssp\eta_{0}
.

7.3 Definition
.

Let
\Psi
be a geometric integrand and let
\mathcal{P}_{G}
be the class of
graph test pairs.
We say that
\Psi
is
elliptic with respect to
\mathcal{P}_{G}
if there
exists a constant
c>0
(the
ellipticity constant
) such that

E_{\Psi}(G_{f})-E_{\Psi}(G_{h})\geq c\bigl(\mathbf{M}(G_{f})-\mathbf{M}(G_{h})\bigr)\quad\text{for all }(G_{f},G_{h})\in\mathcal{P}_{G}.

If this holds, we write
\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c)
.

8
Main Result

8.1 Definition

(cf.
[
8
, §3]
)
.

Let
e_{1},\ldots,e_{n}
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
, and let

P~\subseteq~\mathbf{R}^{n}
be a linear subspace of dimension
k
. We say that
P

has
rational slope
if there exists
f~\in~\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathbf{R}^{n-k})
such
that

P=\ker f\quad\text{and}\quad f(e_{i})\in\mathbf{Z}^{\,n-k}\text{ for all }i=1,\ldots,n.

8.2 Definition

(
[
12
, 5.6]
)
.

Let
u_{1},\ldots,u_{n-k}
be the standard basis of
\mathbf{R}^{n-k}
and

\ast:{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{n-k}\mathbf{R}^{n}\to{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
be the Hodge star defined with
respect to the standard orientation of
\mathbf{R}^{n}
; cf.
[
9
, 1.7.8]
.
Set

\displaystyle\xi_{f}=\ast\bigl(f^{*}(u_{1})\wedge\cdots\wedge f^{*}(u_{n-k})\bigr)\in{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}\quad\text{for $f\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathbf{R}^{n-k})$}

(1)

\displaystyle\text{and}\quad Q(n,k)=\bigl\{t\xi_{f}:t\in\mathbf{R},\,f\in\Hom(\mathbf{R}^{n},\mathbf{R}^{n-k}),\,f^{*}(u_{i})\in\mathbf{Z}^{n}\text{ for }i\in\{1,2,\ldots,n-k\}\bigr\}\,.

(2)

8.3 Lemma

(
[
12
, 5.8]
)
.

There holds

S_{\mathbf{Q}}=\bigl\{w_{1}\wedge\cdots\wedge w_{k}:w_{1},\ldots,w_{k}\in\mathbf{Q}^{n}\bigr\}\subseteq Q(n,k)\subseteq S_{\mathbf{R}}:=\bigl\{\eta\in{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}:\eta\text{ is simple}\bigr\},

and
S_{\mathbf{Q}}
is dense in
S_{\mathbf{R}}
.

8.4 Lemma
.

Suppose
d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,
m_{1},\dots,m_{d}>0
, and

\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
satisfy

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\eta_{i}=\eta_{0},

where
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}
are positively oriented with respect to
\eta_{0}
.
Then for every
\varepsilon>0
there exist an integer

N
with
0\leq N-d\leq\binom{n}{k}
and elements

\tilde{\eta}_{0},\dots,\tilde{\eta}_{N}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
such that

|\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\eta_{0}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},\qquad|\tilde{\eta}_{i}-\eta_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2dm_{i}}\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d,

and

\tilde{\eta}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i}+\sum_{i=d+1}^{N}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i},

where
m_{i}\geq 0
for
i=d+1,\dots,N
and each
\tilde{\eta}_{i}
is positively
oriented with respect to
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
.

Proof.

Fix
\varepsilon>0
.
For
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, we claim that there exist elements

\tilde{\eta}_{i}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
for
i=0,\dots,d
such that the stated
estimates hold and, in addition,

\tilde{\eta}_{i}\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}>0\quad\text{for }i=1,\dots,d,\qquad\text{and}\qquad\Bigl(\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\Bigr)\bullet\frac{\tilde{\eta}_{0}}{|\tilde{\eta}_{0}|}>0.

By Lemma
8.3
, we may choose

\tilde{\eta}_{0}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
such that

|\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\eta_{0}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2}\quad\text{and}\quad(\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\eta_{0})\bullet\eta_{0}>0.

Set
\zeta:=\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\eta_{0}
. Using Lemma
8.3
again, we
choose
\tilde{\eta}_{1},\dots,\tilde{\eta}_{d}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
such that

m_{i}\,|\tilde{\eta}_{i}-\eta_{i}|<\frac{1}{d}\,\zeta\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0},\qquad i=1,\dots,d.

For
\varepsilon
sufficiently small, this choice guarantees that

\tilde{\eta}_{i}\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}>0
for
i=1,\dots,d
.
Moreover,

\displaystyle\Bigl(\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i}\Bigr)\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}

\displaystyle=(\eta_{0}+\zeta)\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}(\tilde{\eta}_{i}-\eta_{i})\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\eta_{0}\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}

\displaystyle=\zeta\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}(\tilde{\eta}_{i}-\eta_{i})\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}

\displaystyle\geq\zeta\bullet\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}|\tilde{\eta}_{i}-\eta_{i}|>0,

by the choice of the approximations.

Define the residual

r:=\tilde{\eta}_{0}-\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i}.

Let
\{e_{1},\dots,e_{M}\}
be an orthonormal basis of

\bigwedge_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
, where
M=\binom{n}{k}
, with

e_{1}:=\frac{\tilde{\eta}_{0}}{|\tilde{\eta}_{0}|}.

Then there exist
\beta>0
and
w^{\prime}\in\operatorname{span}\{e_{1}\}^{\perp}
such that

r=\beta e_{1}+w^{\prime}.

Choose
\rho>0
such that
|w^{\prime}|<\beta\rho
.
Select vectors
w_{1},\dots,w_{M-1}\in\operatorname{span}\{e_{1}\}^{\perp}
satisfying

B(0,2\rho)\subset\operatorname{conv}\{\pm w_{1},\dots,\pm w_{M-1}\}.

Define

t_{j}:=|\tilde{\eta}_{0}|(e_{1}+w_{j}),\qquad j=1,\dots,M-1.

By Lemma
8.3
, each
t_{j}
admits a rational approximation
s_{j}

such that

|s_{j}-t_{j}|<\varepsilon_{1},

where
\varepsilon_{1}>0
is chosen sufficiently small.
If
\varepsilon_{1}<|\tilde{\eta}_{0}|/2
, then

s_{j}\bullet e_{1}>\frac{|\tilde{\eta}_{0}|}{2}>0,

so each
s_{j}
is positively oriented with respect to
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
.

Define

u_{j}:=\frac{s_{j}}{s_{j}\bullet e_{1}}-e_{1}.

For
\varepsilon_{1}
sufficiently small one has
|u_{j}-w_{j}|<\rho
, hence

B(0,\rho)\subset\operatorname{conv}\{\pm u_{1},\dots,\pm u_{M-1}\}.

Since
|w^{\prime}|<\beta\rho
, there exist coefficients
y_{j}\geq 0
with

\sum_{j}y_{j}=1
such that

\frac{w^{\prime}}{\beta}=\sum_{j=1}^{2M-2}y_{j}u_{j}.

Consequently,

r=\sum_{j=1}^{2M-2}\Bigl(\beta\frac{y_{j}}{s_{j}\bullet e_{1}}\Bigr)s_{j},

which expresses
r
as a positive linear combination of rational, positively
oriented
k
-vectors. By Carathéodory’s theorem
[
13
, Theorem 17.1.2]
,
at most
M
coefficients are nonzero. Setting
\tilde{\eta}_{d+j}:=s_{j}
and

m_{d+j}:=\beta y_{j}/(s_{j}\bullet e_{1})
concludes the proof.
∎

8.5 Theorem

(cf.
[
8
, 3.3]
)
.

Let
\eta_{0}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
and let
\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)

be positively oriented with respect to
\eta_{0}
, with positive weights

m_{1},\dots,m_{d}\in(0,\infty)
satisfying

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\eta_{i}=\eta_{0}.

Let
E\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(k,k)
denote the standard orientation of
\mathbf{R}^{k}
, and let
p\in\mathbf{O}^{\ast}({n},{k})
satisfy
{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}p^{*}(E)=\eta_{0}
. Define

D:=(p^{*})_{\#}I_{k}\quad\text{and}\quad\mu:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\,\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\eta_{i}}.

Then, for every
\varepsilon>0
, there exists a polyhedral
k
-current

B_{\varepsilon}\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
with rational coefficients such that

\partial B_{\varepsilon}=\partial D,\quad\|\gamma_{B_{\varepsilon}}-\mu\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\varepsilon,

and all tangent planes of
B_{\varepsilon}
are positively oriented with respect
to
\eta_{0}
.

8.6 Lemma

(cf.
[
8
, 3.4]
)
.

Let
[0,1]^{k}
be the unit cube in
\mathbf{R}^{k}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
. Suppose

B\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
is a polyhedral
k
-current with rational
coefficients such that

\partial B=\partial I_{k},

and
\mathscr{H}^{k}
-almost every tangent plane to
B
is positively oriented
with respect to
\mathbf{R}^{k}
. Then there exists an integer
Q\neq 0
and
a
Q
-valued Lipschitz function

f:[0,1]^{k}\longrightarrow\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-k})

such that

B=Q^{-1}G_{f},

where
G_{f}
denotes the graph current associated to
f
.

8.7 Theorem
.

Assume
\Psi
is a Lipschitz geometric integrand and

0<c_{2}<c_{1}<\infty
. If

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c_{1})
, then

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c_{2})
.

Proof.

We argue by contradiction. Assume that

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c_{1})
is Lipschitz with

\Lip\Psi=L<\infty
and that
\Psi\notin\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c_{2})
.
By Kirszbraun’s theorem
[
9
, 2.10.43]
,
\Psi
can be extended
to all of
{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}_{k}\mathbf{R}^{n}
preserving the Lipschitz constant; we
denote this extension again by
\Psi
.

Since
\Psi\notin\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c_{2})
, there exist
d\in\mathbb{N}\cup\{0\}
,

m_{1},\dots,m_{d}>0
, and
\eta_{0},\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
, with

\eta_{1},\dots,\eta_{d}
positively oriented with respect to
\eta_{0}
, such that

\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\eta_{i}=\eta_{0},\qquad\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\Psi(\eta_{i})-\Psi(\eta_{0})<c_{2}\left(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}|\eta_{i}|-|\eta_{0}|\right).

Set

M:=\sup\im\Psi,\qquad\varepsilon:=\frac{\frac{1}{2}(c_{1}-c_{2})\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}-1\bigr)}{\frac{3}{2}L+M+M\binom{n}{k}^{1/2}+c_{1}}.

By Lemma
8.4
, there exist

\tilde{\eta}_{0},\dots,\tilde{\eta}_{N}\in Q(n,k)\cap\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
such that

|\eta_{i}-\tilde{\eta}_{i}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2dm_{i}}\quad(i=1,\dots,d),\qquad|\eta_{0}-\tilde{\eta}_{0}|<\frac{\varepsilon}{2},

and

\tilde{\eta}_{0}=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\tilde{\eta}_{i},

with all
\tilde{\eta}_{i}
positively oriented with respect to
\tilde{\eta}_{0}
.
Define the measures

\mu:=\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\eta_{i}},\qquad\tilde{\mu}:=\sum_{i=1}^{N}m_{i}\boldsymbol{\delta}_{\tilde{\eta}_{i}}.

By Theorem
8.5
, there exist polyhedral
k
-currents

B_{\varepsilon},D\in\mathbf{P}_{k}(\mathbf{R}^{n})
with rational coefficients such
that

\vec{D}=\tilde{\eta}_{0},\qquad\spt D\text{ is a unit cube in }\asssp\tilde{\eta}_{0},\qquad\partial B_{\varepsilon}=\partial D,

and

\|\gamma_{B}-\tilde{\mu}\|_{\mathrm{TV}}<\varepsilon.

Let
R\in SO(n)
be a rotation such that
R_{\#}D=\llbracket[0,1]^{k}\rrbracket
.
Then
R_{\#}B
is a polyhedral
k
-current with rational coefficients satisfying

\partial(R_{\#}B)=\partial\llbracket[0,1]^{k}\rrbracket,

and all tangent planes of
R_{\#}B
are positively oriented with respect to

\mathbf{R}^{k}\subset\mathbf{R}^{n}
.
By Lemma
8.6
, there exists a
Q
-valued Lipschitz function

f:[0,1]^{k}\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-k})

such that

G_{f}=Q\,R_{\#}B_{\varepsilon}.

Define the trivial
Q
-valued function

h:[0,1]^{k}\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-k}),\qquad h(x):=Q\llbracket 0\rrbracket.

Rotating back, define

\tilde{f}(x):=f(Rx),\qquad\tilde{h}(x):=h(Rx),\quad x\in R^{-1}([0,1]^{k})\subset\asssp\tilde{\eta}_{0},

so that the associated graph currents satisfy

G_{\tilde{f}}=(R^{-1})_{\#}G_{f}=QB_{\varepsilon},\qquad G_{\tilde{h}}=(R^{-1})_{\#}G_{h}=QD.

Since
(G_{\tilde{f}},G_{\tilde{h}})\in\mathcal{P}_{G}
and

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c_{1})
, we obtain

E_{\Psi}(G_{\tilde{f}})-E_{\Psi}(G_{\tilde{h}})\geq c_{1}\bigl(\mathbf{M}(G_{\tilde{f}})-\mathbf{M}(G_{\tilde{h}})\bigr).

On the other hand, repeating verbatim the estimates of

[
12
, 5.10]
yields

c_{2}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}-1\bigr)+\varepsilon\bigl(\tfrac{3}{2}L+M+M\binom{n}{k}^{1/2}\bigr)>E_{\Psi}(G_{\tilde{f}})-E_{\Psi}(G_{\tilde{h}})>c_{1}\bigl(\sum_{i=1}^{d}m_{i}-1\bigr)-c_{1}\varepsilon.

(3)

This is impossible by the choice of
\varepsilon
. Hence, the assumption that

\Psi\notin\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c_{2})
is false, and therefore

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c_{2})
.
∎

8.8 Theorem
.

Assume
\Psi
is a Lipschitz geometric integrand, and let
c>0
. Then

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c).

Proof.

Suppose first that
\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G},c)
.
Combining Theorem
8.7
with Remark
4.5
,
we have

\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c).

Conversely, assume
\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{+}(c)
, and let

(G_{f},G_{h})\in\mathcal{P}_{G}
be a graph test pair. Since
(G_{f},G_{h})
is
a test pair, repeating the same argument as in
3.12
shows that

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P},c)
.
∎

8.9 Remark
.

Fix
\eta_{0}\in\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)
. Using the same arguments as in
Lemma
4.5
and Theorem
8.7
, one obtains
the analogue of Theorem
8.8
. That is, the
uniform ellipticity of
\Psi
on graphs over a single plane
\eta_{0}
is
equivalent to the plane-wise uniform polyconvexity i.e.

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G}(\eta_{0}),c)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}_{\eta_{0}}(c).

9
Q
-Energies

9.1 Definition

(
[
6
, 0.1]
)
.

A measurable map

F\colon(\mathbf{R}^{n-k})^{Q}\times(\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k})^{Q}\to\mathbf{R}

is called a
Q
-integrand
if it is invariant under permutations, i.e.,
for every permutation
\sigma
of
\{1,\ldots,Q\}
,

F(a_{1},\ldots,a_{Q},A_{1},\ldots,A_{Q})=F\bigl(a_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,a_{\sigma(Q)},A_{\sigma(1)},\ldots,A_{\sigma(Q)}\bigr).

9.2 Remark
.

Let

f\colon[0,1]^{k}\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{\,n-k})
be a proper
Lipschitz
Q
-valued function. By the decomposition lemma
5.3
,
its differential
\mathrm{D}f(x)
is an element of
\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k})
.
More precisely, if

f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket f_{i}(x)\rrbracket,

then

\mathrm{D}f(x)=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket\mathrm{D}f_{i}(x)\rrbracket.

9.3 Definition
.

Let
F
be a
Q
-integrand. For a
Q
-valued function

f\colon[0,1]^{k}\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q}}(\mathbf{R}^{\,n-k})
with decomposition

f=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\llbracket f_{i}\rrbracket
,
we define the associated
anisotropic
Q
-energy functional
by

E_{F}(f):=\int_{[0,1]^{k}}F\bigl(f_{1}(x),\ldots,f_{Q}(x),\mathrm{D}f_{1}(x),\ldots,\mathrm{D}f_{Q}(x)\bigr)\,\,\mathrm{d}x.

9.4 Remark
.

The symmetry of
F
ensures that the definition of
E_{F}(f)
is independent
of the chosen decomposition of
f
.

9.5 Remark
.

Every geometric integrand

\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)^{+}\to\mathbf{R}

induces a
Q
-integrand in a natural way. Let

\psi:=\Psi\circ{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M
, and define

\bar{\psi}_{Q}(X_{1},\ldots,X_{Q}):=\sum_{i=1}^{Q}\psi(X_{i})\quad\text{for}\quad(X_{1},\ldots,X_{Q})\in(\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k})^{Q}.

Then the associated anisotropic
Q
-energy depends only on the differential
of
f
and is given by

E_{\bar{\psi}_{Q}}(f)=\int_{[0,1]^{k}}\bar{\psi}_{Q}\bigl(\mathrm{D}f(x)\bigr)\,\mathrm{d}x\,.

10
Uniform Quasiconvexity

10.1 Definition

(cf.
[
6
, 0.1]
)
.

Let

F:(\mathbf{R}^{n-k})^{Q}\times(\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k})^{Q}\to\mathbf{R}

be a locally bounded
Q
–integrand.
We say that
F
is
uniformly quasiconvex with constant
c>0
if the
following holds: for every decomposition
Q=Q_{1}+\cdots+Q_{J}
, every
choice of vectors
a_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{n-k}
with
a_{i}\neq a_{j}
for

i\neq j
, and every
L_{j}\in\mathbf{R}^{(n-k)\times k}
, define the
affine
Q
–valued map

h(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{J}Q_{j}\llbracket a_{j}+L_{j}x\rrbracket.

For every collection of Lipschitz maps

f_{j}:[0,1]^{k}\to\mathcal{A}_{{Q_{j}}}(\mathbf{R}^{n-k}),\qquad f_{j}|_{\partial[0,1]^{k}}=Q_{j}\llbracket a_{j}+L_{j}x\rrbracket,

set

f(x):=\sum_{j=1}^{J}f_{j}(x).

Then

E_{F}(f)-E_{F}(h)\;\geq\;c\bigl(\mathbf{M}(G_{f})-\mathbf{M}(G_{h})\bigr),

where
G_{f}
and
G_{h}
denote the graphs of
f
and
h
, respectively.
In this case we write
F\in\mathrm{UQC}(c;Q)
.

10.2 Remark
.

Let
\bar{\psi}_{Q}
be as in Remark
9.5
.
By the area formula for
Q
-valued graphs
[
11
, 1.11]
,

E_{\bar{\psi}_{Q}}(f)=E_{\Psi}(G_{f})

for every Lipschitz
Q
-valued function
f
.
Accordingly, the condition
\bar{\psi}_{Q}\in\mathrm{UQC}(c;Q)
for all
Q\in\mathbb{N}

is equivalent to

\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G}(\xi_{0}),c),

with
\xi_{0}:=e_{1}\wedge e_{2}\wedge\dots\wedge e_{k}
.

10.3 Theorem
.

Let
\Psi:\mathbf{G}_{0}(n,k)^{+}\to\mathbf{R}
be a Lipschitz geometric
integrand, and let
\psi:=\Psi\circ{\textstyle\boldsymbol{\bigwedge}}M
be the associated
classical integrand. For each
Q\in\mathbb{N}
, let
\bar{\psi}_{Q}
denote
the corresponding
Q
-integrand as in Remark
9.5
.
Then, for any constant
c>0

\psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{\text{cl}}_{\xi_{0}}(c)\quad\Longleftrightarrow\quad\bar{\psi}_{Q}\in\mathrm{UQC}(c;Q)\text{ for every }Q\in\mathbb{N}.

Proof.

The equivalence follows from a chain of equivalences, each justified by the cited remarks:

\displaystyle\psi\in\mathrm{UPC}^{\mathrm{cl}}_{\xi_{0}}(c)

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{\ref{rem:class_geom_poly_equivalence}}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\;\Psi\in\mathrm{UPC}_{\xi_{0}}(c)

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{\ref{rem:plane_wise_equivalence}}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\;\Psi\in\mathrm{AUE}(\mathcal{P}_{G}(\xi_{0}),c)

\displaystyle\;\overset{\text{\ref{rem:aue_uqc}}}{\Longleftrightarrow}\;\bar{\psi}_{Q}\in\mathrm{UQC}(c;Q)\quad\text{for every }Q\in\mathbb{N}.

∎
