Risk Factors and Prognosis Analysis of Infiltrating Micropapillary Carcinoma of Breast: A Retrospective Study
Authors: Pengfei He, Yupeng Sha, Yige Lu, Tong Li, Xiaoming Li, Yixin Liu, Hui Pang, Jiguang Han, Quan Yuan
Published: 2026

ABSTRACT
AbstractBackgroundInvasive micropapillary carcinoma (IMPC) is a rare breast cancer subtype with high invasiveness and lymphovascular/lymph node metastasis. While poor prognosis is reported, long-term survival and roles of molecular subtyping/laboratory markers remain unclear.MethodsWe retrospectively analyzed 653 IMPC patients from a Chinese center (as of December 2024). Kaplan-Meier estimated progression-free survival (PFS) and overall survival (OS); Cox regression assessed prognostic factors.ResultsAmong 653 patients with IMPC, the molecular subtype distribution was as follows: HR+/HER2−, 48.2%; HR+/HER2+, 35.4%; HR−/HER2−, 11.0%; and HR−/HER2+, 5.4%. Most patients had T1–2 disease and were postmenopausal. The 3-year and 5-year OS rates were 97.42% and 93.73%, respectively, while the 3-year and 5-year PFS rates were 90.66% and 81.20%, respectively. Kaplan–Meier analysis showed significant differences in PFS among molecular subtypes, but not in OS. In multivariable analysis, the HR+/HER2+ subtype and higher total bilirubin were independently associated with better PFS, whereas higher systemic immune-inflammation index and stage IIIB were associated with worse PFS. Poorer OS was independently associated with advanced stage, no surgery, alcohol consumption history, higher systemic immune-inflammation index, menopausal status, and elevated blood glucose, while mastectomy and endocrine therapy were associated with better OS.ConclusionIMPC shows favorable OS but requires progression risk monitoring. PFS varied by molecular subtype, while OS did not. Molecular typing, clinical stage, and inflammatory/biochemical markers aid prognosis and personalized treatment.Keywords: breast cancer, invasive micropapillary carcinoma, progression-free survival, overall survival, prognostic factors

Introduction

Invasive micropapillary carcinoma (IMPC) of the breast is an uncommon histological subtype of breast cancer.1,2 Although its incidence is relatively low, IMPC has attracted considerable attention because of its distinctive histopathological morphology and aggressive clinical behavior, notably its high propensity for lymphovascular invasion and lymph node metastasis.3,4 While IMPC is generally regarded as biologically aggressive, its impact on long-term prognosis remains controversial.4–7 Some studies have reported higher recurrence rates or more advanced nodal diseases, and most research has concluded that IMPC has a poorer prognosis than conventional breast cancer types.6,8 Molecular subtype is a well-established determinant of prognosis and treatment selection in breast cancer, but its prognostic role in IMPC has not been fully clarified.8 In addition, readily accessible laboratory indicators, including inflammation-related and biochemical markers, might provide complementary prognostic information, but evidence in IMPC was limited.9,10 Therefore, this study retrospectively analyzed patients with pathologically confirmed breast IMPC who were treated at our institution to describe their clinicopathological characteristics and to evaluate associations of molecular subtype, treatment-related variables, and laboratory parameters with PFS and OS.

Materials and Methods

Study Design and Patient Selection

The study obtained ethical approval from the Institutional Review Board of Harbin Medical University Cancer Hospital (reference: YD2026 – 03) and was conducted in strict accordance with the ethical standards of the Declaration of Helsinki. Because the study was retrospective and all data were anonymized, the requirement for patient-informed consent was waived. The investigation comprised a retrospective cohort study at our institution. We screened patients with pathologically confirmed IMPC of the breast, identified in the institutional electronic medical records through December 2024, for eligibility. Inclusion criteria required pathologically confirmed breast IMPC and complete basic clinical and pathological data, follow-up information, and survival outcome data. The patient selection process was shown in Figure 1.

Flowchart of patient selection.

Data Collection

Demographic characteristics, clinicopathological variables, treatment information, and baseline laboratory parameters were extracted from the medical records. Molecular subtypes were classified according to hormone receptor and HER2 status.

Definitions and Endpoints

OS was defined as the time from date of diagnosis to death from any cause or last follow-up. PFS was defined as the time from same starting point to disease progression, recurrence, death, or last follow-up, whichever occurred first. Based on the established criteria from the American Society of Clinical Oncology (ASCO) and the College of American Pathologists (CAP), estrogen receptor and progesterone receptor positivity were characterized as ≥1% of tumor cell nuclei demonstrating positive staining.11 HER2 expression was determined in accordance with the 2018 ASCO/CAP criteria, whereby immunohistochemistry scores of 3+ are classified as positive, while scores of 2+ are deemed positive when HER2 gene amplification is confirmed through fluorescence in situ hybridization (FISH) analysis. Follow-up information was obtained from outpatient records, inpatient records, and telephone follow-up when necessary. The last follow-up date was December 2025.

Statistical Analysis

Continuous variables are presented as mean ± standard deviation or median (interquartile range), as appropriate, and categorical variables are presented as counts and percentages. Survival curves for PFS and OS were estimated using the Kaplan–Meier method and compared with the Log rank test.12 Univariate Cox proportional hazards regression was first performed to assess potential prognostic factors. Variables with P < 0.05 in univariate analysis were subsequently entered into multivariate Cox regression models. Hazard ratios (HRs) and 95% confidence intervals (CIs) were calculated. A two-sided P < 0.05 was considered statistically significant. Statistical analyses were conducted using R Studio version 4.3.3 and Jupyter Notebook 5.6.0.

Results

Patient Selection and Baseline Characteristics

As of December 2024, a total of 802 patients pathologically diagnosed with IMPC were screened from the hospital’s electronic medical record system in this study. After excluding 45 cases of non - primary breast malignancies, 13 duplicate cases, 57 cases with incomplete clinicopathological data, and 34 cases lacking follow - up or survival outcome information, a total of 653 patients were finally included in the survival analysis. The specific inclusion process is shown in Figure 1. The baseline clinicopathological characteristics of the included patients are presented in Table 1. Among the 653 patients, there were 315 cases (48.2%) of the HR+/HER2− subtype, 231 cases (35.4%) of the HR+/HER2+ subtype, 72 cases (11.0%) of the HR−/HER2− subtype, and 35 cases (5.4%) of the HR−/HER2+ subtype. Overall, the patients were predominantly female (98.6%), with ages mainly concentrated between 40 and 64 years (75.8%), and postmenopausal patients accounted for 58.3%. In terms of treatment, most patients received surgical treatment and systemic treatment. Among them, 579 cases (88.7%) received chemotherapy and/or targeted therapy, 547 cases (83.8%) received endocrine therapy, 82 cases (12.6%) received neoadjuvant therapy, and 303 cases (46.4%) received radiotherapy. Regarding the surgical approach, mastectomy or mastectomy with preservation of the nipple–areola complex was the main method (88.7%). In terms of tumor staging, most patients were in the T1–2 stage (94.8%), indicating that the primary tumor burden was mainly in the early and middle stages. However, lymph node metastasis was relatively common, with a total of 438 cases (67.1%) of patients in the N1–3 stage, suggesting that IMPC has a strong tendency for lymph node metastasis. Distant metastasis at the time of initial diagnosis was rare, with only 4 cases (0.6%) in the M1 stage. After stratified comparison by molecular subtypes, the distribution of baseline variables was relatively balanced among most groups. However, there were statistical differences in the proportion of endocrine therapy (P = 0.001) and the distribution of M staging (P = 0.021) among different subtypes, while there were no statistically significant differences in variables such as tumor side, gender, age, menopausal status, comorbidity status, surgical approach, radiotherapy, T staging, and N staging among the groups.

Survival Outcome Analysis

Follow - up analysis of the entire cohort of patients revealed that the OS of patients with IMPC was relatively good, yet the risk of disease progression could not be ignored. These results indicated that in this cohort, the short-to-medium-term OS of IMPC patients was satisfactory. However, as the follow - up time extended, the number of disease progression events gradually increased, and the decline of PFS was more significant than that of OS. Further, Kaplan–Meier survival analysis was conducted on patients according to molecular subtypes. The results demonstrated a statistical difference in PFS among patients with different molecular subtypes (P = 0.029, Figure 2A), suggesting that molecular subtypes may be associated with the risk of disease progression in IMPC patients. In contrast, there was no statistical difference in OS among patients with different molecular subtypes (P = 0.820, Figure 2B), indicating that within the follow-up scope of this study, the impact of molecular subtypes on the overall risk of death was not fully manifested.

Kaplan–Meier survival curves of patients with invasive micropapillary breast cancer of different molecular subtypes. (A) Progression-free survival (PFS) curves of patients with different molecular subtypes; (B) Overall survival (OS) curves of patients with different molecular subtypes.

Univariate Cox Regression Analysis

We performed univariate Cox proportional hazards regression with PFS and OS as the outcomes. The results appeared in Tables S1 and S2 in Supplementary Material. For PFS, univariate analysis showed that neutrophil count (neu), white blood cell count (wbc), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), neutrophil-to-lymphocyte ratio (NLR), lactate dehydrogenase (LDH), alkaline phosphatase (ALP), monocyte count (mono), platelet count (PLT), total bilirubin (BILT), stage III B, and HR+/HER2+ molecular subtype were significantly associated with PFS (all P < 0.05). Lymphocyte count (lym) was also associated with PFS (P = 0.033). For OS, univariate analysis showed that stage IV, LDH, BILT, non-surgery, stage M1, history of alcohol consumption, SII, Ki-67, stage N3, stage IIIC, menopause, hypertension, ALP, blood glucose (GLU), receiving endocrine therapy, stage IIIA, stage IIIB, stage N2, stage T2, diabetes, NLR, smoking, prothrombin time (PT), neutrophil count (neu), stage T4, and white blood cell count (wbc) were significantly associated with OS (all P < 0.05).

Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis of PFS

Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis of PFS were included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model. The model showed that the HR+/HER2+ molecular subtype, total bilirubin (BILT), systemic immune-inflammation index (SII), and stage IIIB were independently associated with PFS (Figure 3). The HR+/HER2+ subtype was associated with a lower risk of disease progression (HR = 0.424, 95% CI: 0.246–0.733, P = 0.002). An elevated total bilirubin level was associated with a lower risk of progression (HR = 0.961, 95% CI: 0.928–0.995, P = 0.027). By contrast, an elevated SII (HR = 1.002, 95% CI: 1.000–1.003, P = 0.031) and stage IIIB (HR = 4.935, 95% CI: 1.389–17.526, P = 0.014) were associated with a higher risk of progression. The remaining variables in the model, including neu, wbc, NLR, LDH, ALP, mono, PLT, HR−/HER2+, HR+/HER2−, stage IIA, stage IIB, stage IIIA, stage IIIC, stage IV, and lym, showed no statistically significant associations with PFS (all P > 0.05).

Forest plot of multivariate Cox regression analysis for PFS in patients with IMPC of the breast. The figure shows the hazard ratios (HR) and 95% confidence intervals (95% CI) corresponding to each variable included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model for PFS.

Multivariate Cox Regression Analysis of OS

Variables with P < 0.05 in the univariate analysis of OS were included in the multivariate Cox proportional hazards regression model. The results showed that stages IIIA, IIIB, IIIC, IV, total bilirubin (BILT), surgical approach, history of alcohol consumption, SII, menopause status, ALP, blood glucose (GLU), endocrine therapy, stage N2, stage N3, NLR, and stage T4 were independently associated with OS (Figure 4). Among them, stage IIIA (HR = 9.298, 95% CI: 6.053–14.283, P < 0.001), stage IIIB (HR = 1590.949, 95% CI: 364.853–6937.370, P < 0.001), stage IIIC (HR = 18.276, 95% CI: 11.383–29.344, P < 0.001), stage IV (HR = 437.918, 95% CI: 129.790–1477.562, P < 0.001), elevated total bilirubin (HR = 1.027, 95% CI: 1.012–1.043, P < 0.001), no surgery (HR = 71.107, 95% CI: 3.328–1519.420, P = 0.006), history of alcohol consumption (HR = 15.334, 95% CI: 5.064–46.438, P < 0.001), elevated SII (HR = 1.001, 95% CI: 1.000–1.001, P < 0.001), menopause (HR = 1.958, 95% CI: 1.307–2.931, P = 0.001), and elevated blood glucose (HR = 1.124, 95% CI: 1.041–1.215, P = 0.003) were associated with poorer OS. In contrast, undergoing mastectomy or nipple-areola complex-sparing mastectomy (HR = 0.430, 95% CI: 0.221–0.840, P = 0.014), elevated ALP (HR = 0.993, 95% CI: 0.988–0.998, P = 0.011), receiving endocrine therapy (HR = 0.501, 95% CI: 0.327–0.768, P = 0.002), stage N2 (HR = 0.226, 95% CI: 0.149–0.344, P < 0.001), stage N3 (HR = 0.162, 95% CI: 0.102–0.257, P < 0.001), elevated NLR (HR = 0.877, 95% CI: 0.789–0.975, P = 0.015), and stage T4 (HR = 0.019, 95% CI: 0.004–0.081, P < 0.001) were associated with a lower risk of death. The remaining variables included in the model, such as LDH, Mammoplasty, stage M1, hypertension, Ki–67, stage N1, stage T2, stage T3, diabetes, smoking, PT, neutrophil count, and white blood cell count, were not significantly associated with OS (all P > 0.05).

Discussion

This study analyzed the clinicopathological features, molecular subtype distribution, and influencing factors of PFS and OS in patients based on a single-center, large-sample cohort of IMPC. The results showed that the overall OS of IMPC patients was good, but the PFS gradually decreased with the prolongation of follow-up time. There were significant differences in PFS among different molecular subtypes, while the differences in OS were not significant. Multivariate analysis further revealed that molecular subtype, clinical stage, and some inflammatory and biochemical indicators were associated with the prognosis of patients.

Since this is a rare and special type of breast cancer, previous studies have generally suggested that IMPC is highly invasive, especially lymphatic vessel invasion and regional lymph node metastasis-prone.13 This is also the result of the baseline characteristics. Even though most of the primary tumors were T1–2 the percentage of patients were N1–3, IMPC tends to regional lymph node metastasis even when the primary lesion is not very large.14,15 This is consistent with previous studies of high lymph node metastasis rate of IMPC and suggests that the local staging assessment and regional lymph node management of patients with this subtype should be taken into account in clinical practice.16,17

In this study, the 3-year and 5-year OS rates were 97.42% and 93.73%, respectively, and the 3-year and 5-year PFS rates were 90.66% and 81.20%, respectively. Overall, patients with IMPC had a relatively good OS, but the decline in PFS was more significant. This suggests that IMPC patients are more likely to experience recurrence or progression events during follow-up, and these events do not necessarily immediately translate into an increased risk of death in the short term. On the one hand, this may be related to the improvement of the current comprehensive treatment level for breast cancer. Even if patients experience progression, they can still achieve a relatively long survival through subsequent endocrine therapy, anti-HER2 therapy, chemotherapy, and local treatment. On the other hand, it also indicates that in the long-term management of IMPC patients, simply focusing on the death outcome is not sufficient, and PFS is also an important indicator reflecting the biological behavior of the disease.

The Molecular subtype analysis showed statistically significant differences in PFS among different subtypes, but not in OS. Multivariate analysis showed that HR+/HER2+ subtype was associated with a lower risk of PFS events. This suggests that even for the same subtype, molecular biological properties may still play a role in disease progression. Patients with HER2-positive and hormone receptor-positive status usually receive more comprehensive treatment under modern treatment conditions such as endocrine therapy and anti-HER2 targeted therapy.18,19 However, no differences between molecular subtypes were observed in OS in this study, suggesting that the effect of subtypes on death risk may take longer follow-up period or some differences have been reduced by treatment.

Stage IIIB and high SII were poor prognostic factors and high BILT and HR+/HER2+ were a lesser risk of progression. Stage IIIB represents higher burden of locally advanced disease and poor PFS is consistent with clinical patterns. SII is an inflammatory marker integrating information on platelets, neutrophils and lymphocytes. It represents the host systemic inflammation status and changes in tumor microenvironment. In this study, elevated SII is associated with poor PFS. Negative correlation between total bilirubin and PFS may reflect its role in oxidative stress, inflammation regulation or metabolic status of the body. This result should be cautious and should be validated again in future studies. The multivariate analysis of OS shows that clinical stage variables still play a role in OS. Stage IIIA, IIIB, IIIC and IV were associated with increased death risk. Menopausal status, alcohol consumption history, elevated SII and high blood glucose were also associated with poor OS. Having endocrine therapy and mastectomy or nipple-areola complex-sparing mastectomy were associated with lower death risk, consistent with the clinical experience that standard comprehensive treatment can improve OS.

Notably, some variables in the OS model of this study are not in agreement with clinical intuition. Patients in N2/N3 stage, T4 stage with high NLR and high ALP have a less serious death risk. This may be due to complex collinearity of variables in retrospective study, setting of the reference group, uneven distribution of events and statistical fluctuations after adjustment in multivariate model.20,21 In particular, when the sample size of some strata is small or correlation between variables is strong, the estimated direction of the model may vary. These results should be considered to be considered as exploratory results and not over inferred.21 Future, more validation is needed through larger sample, multi center studies, and external validation.

This paper has some clinical relevance. First, we included a relatively large sample size of IMPC patients, which makes it easier to describe the clinicopathological characteristics of this rare subtype. Second, we evaluated the prognosis from multiple perspectives (molecular subtypes, treatment factors, and laboratory indicators) simultaneously, which makes it easier to build more general risk stratification framework. Third, we suggest that molecular subtypes and inflammation-related indicators may provide additional prognostic information for IMPC patients, which may be useful to develop clinical follow-up and individual treatment.22,23

This paper has some limitations. First, this was a single-center retrospective study, and selection bias and information bias were inevitable.24 Second, it covered a relatively long time period and diagnostic and treatment strategies and adjuvant regimens changed and could have influenced results. Third, although the sample size was large, there were only few patients in some subgroups which could have affected some estimates. Fourth, several laboratory indicators were only analyzed from baseline data, and information about their dynamic changes was not included. Finally, there was no external validation cohort, so results could be generalised.

Conclusion

This study showed that patients with IMPC of breast had good OS, but that they still have a risk of progression. PFS differed significantly between the molecular subtypes, but OS differences were not significant. Multivariate analysis showed that HR+/HER2+ subtype, stage IIIB, SII and total bilirubin were independent of PFS; clinical stage, surgical approach, endocrine therapy, alcohol consumption history, menopause status, SII, blood glucose and some laboratory indicators were independently associated with OS. These results suggest that prognosis of IMPC patients should include molecular subtype, clinical stage, treatment factors and laboratory indicators. Future multicenter prospective studies were still needed.
