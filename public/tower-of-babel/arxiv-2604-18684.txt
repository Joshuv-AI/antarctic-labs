Hatsugai-Kohmoto-like Models for Altermagnets and Odd-Parity Magnets
Konstantin Rickelt, Denis Sedov, Mathias S. Scheurer

Abstract
We introduce a generalized Hatsugai-Kohmoto multi-orbital model and study its phase diagram and physical properties in the additional presence of perturbations that lift any extensive ground-state degeneracies. The unperturbed, exactly solvable model already displays a rich set of spectral functions, including regimes reminiscent of unconventional magnets. We map the first-order study of additional spatially local multi-orbital Hubbard interactions to a Heisenberg model in momentum space, which leads to symmetry-breaking instabilities already at weak coupling. Interestingly, translational-symmetry breaking orders, such as antiferromagnetism, are excluded. Instead, in addition to ferromagnetism, unconventional p p -wave and d d -wave magnets occur, characterized by spin order on the bonds of the underlying square lattice. Adding another type of momentum-space interaction, which still allows to solve the model exactly, is shown to stabilize a non-degenerate singlet ground state that retains the spin splitting characteristic of unconventional magnets. We discuss its impact on the spin structure factor. Taken together, our findings show that Hatsugai-Kohmoto-like models provide a rich playground for unconventional magnetism.

In this work, we start from a two-orbital HK-like model, where—similar to what was dubbed “orbital HK models” in Ref.

However, we still obtain parameter regimes with extensive ground-state degeneracies in momentum-space regions that are shaped by the interplay of interactions and hopping processes. Besides long-range order, we consider an additional interaction term that does not spoil the exact solvability and stabilizes a unique ground state. This shows how momentum-dependent splittings of the spectral function, reminiscent of unconventional magnets, and in the absence of long-range order or extensive ground-state degeneracies can be obtained in a theoretically controlled way.

Conclusion
—We have shown how HK-inspired models can be used as a transparent starting point to capture the formation of and interaction effects in itinerant unconventional magnets and related non-magnetic states. We believe that our work can further provide the starting for many interesting follow-up works, e.g., on the interplay of pairing with altermagnetism in the strong-coupling regime.

A.2
Band basis of the checker board lattice

Up to this point, the creation and annihilation operators act on the different sublattices
l\in\{A,B\}
. They can be transformed with a unitary transformation
U
into the band basis of the Bloch Hamiltonian
h_{\boldsymbol{k}}
where they act on the two bands
\alpha\in\{\pm\}
,

U^{T}(\boldsymbol{k})h_{\boldsymbol{k}}U(\boldsymbol{k})=\begin{pmatrix}\lambda_{-}(\boldsymbol{k})&0\\
0&\lambda_{+}(\boldsymbol{k})\end{pmatrix}

(31)

with the eigenvalues
\lambda_{\pm}
of the Bloch Hamiltonian
h_{\boldsymbol{k}}
.
The eigenvalues are given as

\lambda_{\pm}=\frac{p+g}{2}\pm\sqrt{f^{2}+\left(\frac{g-p}{2}\right)^{2}}

(32)

and with this, the transformation matrix can be written as

U_{l,\alpha}=\begin{pmatrix}U_{A,-}&U_{A,+}\\
U_{B,-}&U_{B,+}\end{pmatrix}=\begin{pmatrix}\frac{f}{N_{-}}&\frac{f}{N_{+}}\\
\frac{\lambda_{-}-p}{N_{-}}&\frac{\lambda_{+}-p}{N_{-}}\end{pmatrix}

(33)

where the normalizations are given as
N_{\pm}~=~\sqrt{f^{2}+(\lambda_{\pm}-p)^{2}}
. It can be clearly seen that the transformation matrix elements depend on the momentum
\boldsymbol{k}
.
With the transformation matrix, we can then express the orbital basis operators
(c^{\dagger}/c)
in terms of the new band operators
(f^{\dagger}/f)
. With this we can write the ground state as in Eq. 2 of the main text. The mapping is given as

c^{(\dagger)}_{\boldsymbol{k},l,\sigma}=\sum_{\alpha}U_{l,\alpha}(\boldsymbol{k})f^{(\dagger)}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}.

(34)

Note that there is no need for complex conjugation as the transformation matrix
U
is real.

Appendix B
Derivation of the Heisenberg model

In this section, we show that the Coulomb-like interaction projected on the degenerate ground state of the HK model can be mapped onto the spin-1/2 quantum Heisenberg model.

B.1
One-site HK model

We first consider the one-site HK model,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{HK}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{k}},\sigma}\xi_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}.

(35)

The degenerate ground states of this model are given by

\displaystyle\ket{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}}=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}\ket{0},

(36)

where
\mathcal{S}_{1,2}
denote the singly and doubly occupied regions of the BZ, respectively.

Let us consider the following Coulomb-like interaction

\displaystyle\Delta H=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}:,

(37)

where
:\hat{A}:
denotes normal ordering. We want to project the Hamiltonian (
37
) onto the degenerate HK ground state. Let
P
be the corresponding projector,
P=\sum_{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}}\ket{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}}\bra{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}}
. Since this subspace is characterized by a fixed number of electrons at each momentum
\boldsymbol{k}
, the projected Hamiltonian conserves the number of particles
\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}
. Therefore, only the terms with
\boldsymbol{q}=0
and
\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}\neq 0
in (
37
) survive after the projection,
P\Delta HP
. Let us study these two contributions separately,

\displaystyle P\Delta HP=\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=0}+\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}},

(38)

where

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=0}=P\frac{\mathcal{V}_{0}}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}:P=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{0}}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}P:\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}:P=\mathrm{const.}P,

(39)

and

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=P\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}:P

\displaystyle=-P\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}P

(40)

We can rewrite the remaining non-trivial term in the following way,

\displaystyle-P\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}P

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}2\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}P\left[\frac{1}{4}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}+\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\right]P

\displaystyle=\mathrm{const.}P-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}2\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}P\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}P,

(41)

where
\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{2}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
. The second term is nonzero only when
\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
, where it can be directly mapped onto the spin-1/2 quantum Heisenberg model. Thus,

\displaystyle P\Delta HP\mapsto-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}+\mathrm{const}.

(42)

B.2
Multiorbital HK model

We now generalize the obtained mapping to the multiorbital HK models,

\displaystyle H_{\mathrm{HK}}=\sum_{\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{k}},\sigma,\alpha,\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}h_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\beta,\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}U^{\alpha\beta}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}},

(43)

where
h_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}
is a one-particle Hamiltonian in the orbital basis, and
U^{\alpha\beta}
is an interaction matrix. We limit our analysis to the case when the ground states have a non-degenerate doubly-occupied region
\mathcal{S}_{2}
and a spin-degenerate singly occupied region
\mathcal{S}_{1}
. The degenerate ground states are then given by

\displaystyle\ket{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}}=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},1,\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\ket{\mathcal{S}_{2}},

(44)

where
\ket{\mathcal{S}_{2}}
is the doubly-occupied part of the ground state, and
f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},1,\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
is the creation operator of the lowest band of the Bloch Hamiltonian
h_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}
. Note that we do not need to know the exact structure of the ground state wavefunction in the doubly occupied region.

We consider the multiorbital Hubbard model with the Coulomb-like interaction,

\displaystyle\Delta H=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}:,

(45)

where the summation over orbitals
\alpha,\beta
is assumed. Due to the conservation of
\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
, only two terms survive the projection onto the degenerate ground states in Eq. (
44
). Let us analyze the first term

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=0}=\frac{\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\alpha\beta}}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}P:\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:P,

(46)

where
\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}
. In the subspace of interest, different
\boldsymbol{k}
momenta lying in
\mathcal{S}_{2}
are uncorrelated; hence, for these momenta,

\displaystyle P:\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:P=\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\left[\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}}-\delta_{\alpha,\beta}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}}\right]P+(1-\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}})\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}}P,\quad\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}.

(47)

When one or both of the momenta lie in the singly occupied region, the projection requires more accurate treatment. Let
f_{\boldsymbol{k},i}
be band operators, with
f_{\boldsymbol{k},1}
corresponding to the lowest one-particle band. In the following calculations, we denote the wavefunctions of this band by
\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}
. For
\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}
,
f_{\boldsymbol{k},i}P\propto\delta_{i,1},Pf^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},i}\propto\delta_{i,1}
, therefore,

\displaystyle P:\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:P=|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}}P\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},f_{1}}P=|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}}P,\quad\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2},\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},

(48)

and

\displaystyle P:\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:P

\displaystyle=|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}|^{2}P\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},f_{1}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},f_{1}}P-\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}P\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},f_{1}}P

\displaystyle=|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}|^{2}P-\delta_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\delta_{\alpha,\beta}|\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}|^{2}P,\quad\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}.

(49)

Thus, expression (
47
) holds for an arbitrary combination of momenta. The second term that survives projection reads

\displaystyle\Delta\tilde{H}_{\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}

\displaystyle=P\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}:P.

(50)

We can rewrite it in the convenient way,

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}:

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma^{\prime}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\left[\frac{1}{2}C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}+2\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\right],

(51)

where
C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta,\sigma},\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\frac{1}{2}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\boldsymbol{\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}

Following the same argument as before, for
\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}
, we get

\displaystyle PC^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}P=\braket{C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\braket{C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}P,

(52)

and

\displaystyle P\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}P=\begin{cases}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}^{*}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}^{*}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}P\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},f_{1}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k},f_{1}}P,\quad&\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\\
0,\quad&\text{otherwise},\end{cases}

(53)

where
\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k},f_{1}}=\frac{1}{2}f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},1}\boldsymbol{\sigma}f^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},1}
.
Thus, we can again map the projected Hamiltonian onto the spin-
1/2
quantum Heisenberg model,

\displaystyle P\Delta HP\mapsto\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}-J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{\sigma}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}},\quad J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\alpha,\beta}\mathcal{V}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}^{*}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}^{*}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}.

(54)

B.3
Checkerboard model and mean-field solution

We now specifically discuss the Hubbard interaction projected onto the degenerate ground state of the checkerboard lattice model introduced earlier in Eq. (
8
). Let us consider on-site and nearest-neighbors interactions which are given by

\displaystyle\Delta H

\displaystyle=H_{V}+H_{V^{\prime}},

(55a)

\displaystyle H_{V}

\displaystyle=V\sum_{\boldsymbol{R},l}n_{\boldsymbol{R}+\boldsymbol{a}_{l},\uparrow}n_{\boldsymbol{R}+\boldsymbol{a}_{l},\downarrow}=\frac{V}{2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{R},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}:n_{\boldsymbol{R}+\boldsymbol{a}_{l},\sigma}n_{\boldsymbol{R}+\boldsymbol{a}_{l},\sigma^{\prime}}:

(55b)

\displaystyle H_{V^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\frac{V^{\prime}}{4}\sum_{\boldsymbol{R},\sigma,\sigma^{\prime}}n_{\boldsymbol{R},\sigma}^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}\sum_{j=0}^{3}n_{\boldsymbol{R}+C_{4z}^{j}\boldsymbol{a}_{B},\sigma^{\prime}}.

(55c)

Following the convention (
10
) of the Fourier transform, we can rewrite the interaction in
\boldsymbol{k}
-space in the form of (
45
) with the following interaction functions,

\displaystyle\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{AA}=\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{BB}=\frac{V}{2},\quad\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{AB}=\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{BA}=\frac{V^{\prime}}{2}\cos\left(\frac{q_{x}}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{q_{y}}{2}\right).

(56)

Recalling that the lower-band wavefunctions of the non-interacting model are real (
33

\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},l}=U_{l,-}(\boldsymbol{k})
, we obtain the coupling constants of the effective spin-

\displaystyle J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}=\frac{V}{4N}\left[\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},A}^{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},A}^{2}+\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},B}^{2}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},B}^{2}\right]+\frac{V^{\prime}}{2N}\cos\left(\frac{k_{x}-k_{x}^{\prime}}{2}\right)\cos\left(\frac{k_{y}-k_{y}^{\prime}}{2}\right)\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},A}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},A}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k},B}\phi_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},B}.

(57)

We now solve this effective spin model in the mean-field approximation. If we further focus on collinear solutions, the problem of finding the MF ground state of the effective spin-model reduces to obtaining the ground state of the classical Ising model,

\displaystyle E[\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}]=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}-J_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}},\quad\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}^{\min}=\operatorname*{argmin}_{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\pm\}}E[\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}].

(58)

From the spin-configuration
\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}^{\min}

\displaystyle\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}=\ket{\{\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}\}^{\min}}=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}f^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\min}}\ket{\mathcal{S}_{2}},

where we associate
\pm

\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}

\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}
.

Let us first isolate the

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta H=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{q}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\alpha}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\beta}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:&=\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}:\\

(\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q})\neq(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{0})\end{subarray}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{q}}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q},\alpha}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{q},\beta}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}:,\end{aligned}

(60)

where we suppressed the spin index for notations simplicity.
Imposing translational invariance, we perform the MF decomposition (Hartree-Fock) of the second term. Then the MF version of the Hamiltonian

\displaystyle\Delta H_{\mathrm{MF}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\alpha\beta}:c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}:

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{0}^{\alpha\beta}\left[\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}}+\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}-\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha}}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta}}\right]

(61a)

\displaystyle-\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\left[\braket{C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}+C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\braket{C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\braket{C^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\braket{C^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right]

(61b)

\displaystyle-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\left[\braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\cdot\braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\cdot\braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\beta\alpha}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\right].

(61c)

This Hamiltonian is consistent with our prior projection and MF calculations. Let us elaborate on what we mean by consistency. The projection and MF decomposition do not generally commute, nonetheless, the expectation value of the constructed Hamiltonian in the state
\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}
coincides with the energy of this state obtained
after
projection and MF,

\displaystyle\braket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}|\Delta H_{\mathrm{MF}}[\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}]|\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}=E_{\mathrm{P+MF}}.

(62)

An exact treatment of the
\boldsymbol{k}
-local part is required because the obtained MF state is the product state only at the
\boldsymbol{k}
-level,
\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}}\ket{\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
, while each
\ket{\Psi_{\boldsymbol{k}}}
can generally exhibit orbital mixing/entanglement in the doubly occupied region
\mathcal{S}_{2}
.

Our primary reason for constructing the MF Hamiltonian is to demonstrate the spin-splitting in the spectral function. Hence, we neglect the spin-diagonal band renormalization terms, and consider only the SU(2) breaking part of the MF Hamiltonian. In the main text, we use the following spin-splitting Hamiltonian constructed from the MF solution
\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}
,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Delta H_{\mathrm{MF}}^{\text{spin-splitting}}[\ket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}]=&-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha\beta}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\cdot\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\neq\boldsymbol{k}}\Braket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}|\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\beta\alpha}|\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}\\
&-\frac{2}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\mathcal{V}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\beta\alpha}\cdot\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\neq\boldsymbol{k}}\Braket{\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}|\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha\beta}|\Omega_{\mathrm{P+MF}}}.\end{aligned}

(63)

Appendix C
Expression for the static spin correlator

In this section, we consider the HK model with an additional interaction term
\delta H
, that still allows the exact diagonalization of the full Hamiltonian,
H=H_{\mathrm{HK}}+\delta H
. For simplicity, let us first focus on the one-site HK model. We are interested in the interactions that fully or partially remove the degeneracy of the HK ground state. One example of such an interaction is

\displaystyle\delta H_{2S}

\displaystyle=-J\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{-\boldsymbol{k}},

(64)

which fully lifts the degeneracy when
J<0
. The ground state in the singly occupied region then consists of decoupled singlets formed by electrons at
\boldsymbol{k}
and
-\boldsymbol{k}
,

\displaystyle\ket{\mathrm{GS}}=\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1},k_{x}>0}\frac{1}{\sqrt{2}}\left[c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}-c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\downarrow}c^{\dagger}_{-\boldsymbol{k},\uparrow}\right]\prod_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathcal{S}_{2}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\uparrow}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\downarrow}\ket{0}.

(65)

Note that
\delta H
modifies the singly and doubly occupied regions. The subsequent calculations are based on the assumption that
\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}=\sum_{\sigma}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}
is conserved. It means that we either treat
\delta H
as a perturbation (by diagonalizing
P\delta HP
) or we diagonalize the full Hamiltonian
H_{\mathrm{HK}}+\delta H
, and require that
[\delta H,\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}]=0
. Finally, we denote the set of momenta that are coupled by the interaction
\delta H
to a given
\boldsymbol{k}
by
C(\boldsymbol{k})
, which includes
\boldsymbol{k}
as well. For example, the interaction (
64
) couples only two momenta,
\boldsymbol{k}
and
-\boldsymbol{k}
. Momenta from different clusters
C
are uncorrelated.

C.1
Spin correlator

We want to understand how the additional interaction
\delta H
affect the spin correlations,

\displaystyle\Braket{S^{z}(\boldsymbol{0})S^{z}(\mathbf{R})}=\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}_{1},\boldsymbol{k}_{2},\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{q}}\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{1}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}_{2}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\boldsymbol{R}}.

(66)

Since
\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}
is conserved, only the terms corresponding to the following combination of momenta give non-vanishing contribution,

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{q}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{0},\quad

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{\boldsymbol{k}_{1}}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{k}_{2};

(67a)

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{k}_{2},\quad

\displaystyle\boldsymbol{k}_{1}

\displaystyle=\boldsymbol{k}.

(67b)

Therefore,

\displaystyle\Braket{S^{z}(\boldsymbol{0})S^{z}(\mathbf{R})}=\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k})\boldsymbol{R}}.

(68)

It is useful to split the summation over
\mathbf{k}^{\prime}
into correlated and uncorrelated parts. The first term then reads

\displaystyle\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{BZ}\setminus C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in\mathrm{BZ}\setminus C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{3}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}},

(69)

where we exploited SU(2) symmetry,
\braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{j}}=0
,
\braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{j}S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{j}}=1/3\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}
.

We now rewrite the expectation value in the second term of Eq. (
68
),

\displaystyle\braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle=-\sum_{\sigma}\Braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma}}+\braket{n_{\boldsymbol{k}}}+\sum_{\sigma}\braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\overline{\sigma}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\overline{\sigma}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\sigma}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\braket{n_{\boldsymbol{k}}}-2\Braket{S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{z}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+2\Braket{S^{x}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{x}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+2\Braket{S^{y}_{\boldsymbol{k}}S^{y}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{1}{2}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\braket{n_{\boldsymbol{k}}}+\frac{2}{3}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}.

(70)

Using the same splitting as in (
69
) and again exploiting SU(2), we finally obtain

\displaystyle\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{0})\cdot\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{R})}=

\displaystyle-\frac{3}{8N^{2}}\left|\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{i\mathbf{kR}}\right|^{2}+\delta_{\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{0}}\frac{3}{4N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}

\displaystyle+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\biggl\{2+\cos[(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k})\boldsymbol{R}]\biggr\}-\frac{3}{4N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}1.

(71)

Thus, we can clearly see that the all the information about the perturbation
\delta H
is encoded in the spin-spin correlations
\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}
. If
\delta H
couples finite number (independent of the system size
N
) of momenta (e.g. the interaction (
64
) couples two momenta), the interaction-induced correlation vanish in the thermodynamic limit, and spin-correlator is fully determined by the fermionic statistics,

\displaystyle\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{0})\cdot\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{R})}=-\frac{3}{8}\left|\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{k}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}e^{i\mathbf{kR}}\right|^{2}+\delta(\boldsymbol{R})\frac{3}{4}\frac{1}{(2\pi)^{d}}\int_{\mathrm{BZ}}\mathrm{d}^{d}\boldsymbol{k}\braket{\hat{n}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}.

(72)

This expression coincides with the result for the Fermi sea, which is the consequence of the fact that the HK model inherits a lot of properties from non-interacting systems.

C.2
Static spin structure factor

From Eq. (
71
), we can straightforwardly obtain the static spin-structure factor,

\displaystyle S({\boldsymbol{q}})

\displaystyle=\sum_{\boldsymbol{R}}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{0})\cdot\boldsymbol{S}(\boldsymbol{R})}e^{i\boldsymbol{qR}}

\displaystyle=-\frac{3}{8N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}n_{\boldsymbol{k}}n_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}+\frac{3}{4N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}n_{\boldsymbol{k}}+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\left[\delta_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right]-\frac{3}{4N}\delta_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{0}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}1

\displaystyle=\frac{3}{8N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}n_{\boldsymbol{k}}(2-n_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}})+\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\left[\delta_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{0}}+\frac{1}{2}\delta_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}\right]-\frac{3}{4N}\delta_{\boldsymbol{q},\boldsymbol{0}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}1.

(73)

The ferromagnetic structure factor then reads

\displaystyle S(\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{0})

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C}\braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{C}\Braket{\biggl(\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in C}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\biggr)^{2}},

(74)

where
\sum_{C}
denotes the summation over clusters of coupled momenta. We can see that depending on the perturbation we can modify the ferromagnetic correlations, even enhance them or fully suppress. For example, the four-spin perturbation,

\displaystyle\delta H=-J\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\sum_{j=0}^{3}\boldsymbol{S}_{C_{4z}^{j}\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{C_{4z}^{j+1}\boldsymbol{k}},

(75)

removes the degeneracy and results in the unique ground state when
J<0
. The ground state is now a singlet state which fully suppresses the ferromagnetic structure factor. Note that
J>0
would enhance
S(\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{0})
.

The finite momentum spin structure factor is given by

\displaystyle S(\boldsymbol{q}\neq\boldsymbol{0})=\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in\mathcal{S}_{1}}\mathbf{1}_{\mathcal{S}_{1}}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q})\left[\frac{3}{4}+\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q})\braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\right],

(76)

where
\mathbf{1}_{A}(x)
is the indicator function. Analogously to the ferromagnetic correlations, the perturbation
\delta H=-J\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{Q}}
with
\boldsymbol{Q}=(\pi,\pi)
fully suppresses the antiferromagnetic correlations
S(\boldsymbol{Q})
when
J<0
. This interaction, however, does not lift the degeneracy for an arbitrary filling.

C.3
Multiorbital case

We now generalize the calculations of the spin-spin correlations to the multi-orbital HK model of the form (
43
) with an additional perturbation that modifies spin-spin correlations of the unperturbed HK ground state while conserving SU(2) symmetry and the number of particles at each momentum
\boldsymbol{k}
. It is instructive to consider the following spin-spin perturbation

\displaystyle\delta H=-\sum_{C}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C}\sum_{\alpha,\beta,\alpha^{\prime},\beta^{\prime}}\tilde{J}_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha\beta\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}\,\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}},

(77)

where the summation
\sum_{C}
goes over non-overlapping finite-size clusters
C
of coupled momenta.

The spin-spin correlator reads

\displaystyle\Braket{S^{\alpha\beta}_{z}(\boldsymbol{0})S_{z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{R})}

\displaystyle=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k},z}^{\alpha\beta}S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}+\frac{1}{4N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k},\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime}}}e^{-i(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}-\boldsymbol{k})\boldsymbol{R}},

(78)

where we use the following convention for the Fourier transform,
c_{\boldsymbol{R},\alpha}=N^{-1/2}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}e^{i\boldsymbol{k}\boldsymbol{R}}c_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}
. The first term can be again split into correlated and uncorrelated parts. The uncorrelated part vanishes due to SU(2) symmetry, hence,

\displaystyle\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k},z}^{\alpha\beta}S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k},z}^{\alpha\beta}S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}+\left(1-\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\right)\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k},z}^{\alpha\beta}}\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\Braket{S_{\boldsymbol{k},z}^{\alpha\beta}S_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}.

(79)

The second term can be rewritten in the convenient way,

\displaystyle c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime}}

\displaystyle=\sum_{\sigma}\left[\delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime},\sigma}-c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime},\sigma}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma}+c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha,\sigma}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime},\overline{\sigma}}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime},\overline{\sigma}}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta,\sigma}\right]

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}-\frac{1}{2}C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}-2S^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k},z}S^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},z}+2S^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k},x}S^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},x}+2S^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k},y}S^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},y}.

(80)

Due to the SU(2) symmetry, we get

\displaystyle\Braket{c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k},\alpha}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\beta}c^{\dagger}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime},\alpha^{\prime}}\sigma_{z}c^{{\phantom{\dagger}}}_{\boldsymbol{k},\beta^{\prime}}}

\displaystyle=\delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\frac{1}{2}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}+\frac{2}{3}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}}

\displaystyle=\begin{aligned} \delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}&+\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\left[\frac{2}{3}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}}-\frac{1}{2}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\right]\\
&-\left(1-\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\right)\frac{1}{2}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Braket{C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}.\end{aligned}

(81)

Thus,

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} \Braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}(\boldsymbol{0})\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{R})}&=\delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\frac{3}{4N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\left[\delta_{\boldsymbol{R},\boldsymbol{0}}-\frac{1}{N}\right]-\frac{3}{8N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\not\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Braket{C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\boldsymbol{R}}\\
&+\frac{1}{N^{2}}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2N^{2}}\sum_{\begin{subarray}{c}\boldsymbol{k};\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C(\boldsymbol{k})\\
\boldsymbol{k}\neq\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\end{subarray}}\left[\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}}-\frac{3}{4}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}}\right]e^{i(\boldsymbol{k}-\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime})\boldsymbol{R}}.\end{aligned}

(82)

Taking the Fourier transform, we find the static spin structure factor,
S^{\alpha\beta\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{q})=\sum_{\boldsymbol{R}}\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha\beta}(\boldsymbol{0})\cdot\boldsymbol{S}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{R})}e^{-i\boldsymbol{q}\boldsymbol{R}}
. As in the single-orbital case, for
\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{0}
, it is again determined solely by the perturbation-induced spin-correlations,

\displaystyle S^{\alpha\beta\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{q}=\boldsymbol{0})=\frac{1}{N}\sum_{C}\Braket{\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}\in C}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta}\cdot\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}\in C}\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}^{\prime}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}}.

(83)

The finite-momentum spin-structure factor reads

\displaystyle\begin{aligned} S^{\alpha\beta\alpha^{\prime}\beta^{\prime}}(\boldsymbol{q}\neq\boldsymbol{0})&=\delta_{\alpha^{\prime},\beta}\frac{3}{4N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}-\frac{3}{8N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\left[1-\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q})\right]\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}}\Braket{C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\\
&+\frac{1}{2N}\sum_{\boldsymbol{k}}\mathbf{1}_{C(\boldsymbol{k})}(\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q})\left[\Braket{\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}}^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}\cdot\boldsymbol{S}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}}-\frac{3}{4}\Braket{C^{\alpha\beta^{\prime}}_{\boldsymbol{k}}C^{\alpha^{\prime}\beta}_{\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}}}\right].\end{aligned}

(84)

Although the multiorbital nature of this expression makes it generally difficult to analyze, we clearly see that the perturbation
\delta H
induces spin-correlations at finite momentum
\boldsymbol{q}
only if there exists
\boldsymbol{k}
such that both
\boldsymbol{k}
and
\boldsymbol{k}+\boldsymbol{q}
lie in the same cluster
C
.
